Rationality fields of CM modular forms
Kimball Martin

Abstract
. We formulate a conjecture on the finitude of rationality fields (i.e., Fourier coefficient fields) of newforms of bounded degree, and prove this for CM forms assuming a generalized Riemann hypothesis. Then we explicitly determine what quadratic and cubic rationality fields occur for weight 2 CM forms, which is related to classifications of CM abelian varieties of GL(2) type. Several of these fields do not appear in the existing tables of newforms in the LMFDB.

1.
Introduction

The motivation for this paper begins with the following conjecture. For a newform
f=\sum a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(N)=S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
, the rationality field
K_{f}=\mathbb{Q}((a_{n})_{n})
is the number field generated by the Fourier coefficients of
f
.

Conjecture 1
.

Fix
k\geq 2
even and
d\geq 1
. There are only finitely many number fields
K
of degree
d
such that
K=K_{f}
for some weight
k
newform
f
with trivial nebentypus.

To our knowledge, this conjecture has not been previously formulated in the literature, but it is suggested by existing ideas and conjectures.

First, Coleman’s conjecture says that, for a fixed
d
, the set of isomorphism classes of endomorphism algebras
\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(A)
of
d
-dimensional simple abelian varieties
A/\mathbb{Q}
is finite (see
[
3
]
). In particular, if
A_{f}
denotes the (simple) abelian variety attached to a weight 2 newform
f
à la Shimura, then
\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(A)\simeq K_{f}
by
[
14
]
. Hence Coleman’s conjecture implies
1
when
k=2
.

Next,
[
9
, Conjecture B+]
predicts that there are only finitely many newforms of squarefree level (and trivial nebentypus) with a degree
d
rationality field for fixed
k\geq 6
and
d\geq 1
. The heuristics behind the conjecture also suggest that there are only finitely many quadratic twist classes of weight
k
non-CM newforms with rationality field having degree
d
for large
k
. This would imply
1
for
k\geq 6
when restricting to non-CM newforms.
(
1
below will address the CM case.)

Further,
[
4
]
suggests that one reason
1
may hold when
k=2
is that there may only be finitely many related moduli spaces having non-cuspidal rational points for a fixed
d
. (When
d=1
, this means that only finitely many modular curves
X_{0}(N)
have non-cuspidal rational points, which is known; see
[
10
,
8
]
.) In higher weight, newforms do not correspond to abelian varieties but motives. Philosophically it seems reasonable to posit an analogous geometric finiteness statement about motives which would imply
1
for all
k
.

At present, there is no clear strategy to tackle
1
, even for
k=2
. Our first result proves this conjecture when one restricts to CM forms, subject to the Extended Riemann Hypothesis (ERH) for quadratic Dirichlet characters.

Theorem 1
.

Fix
k\geq 2
and
d\geq 1
. There are only finitely many number fields
K
of degree
d
such that
K=K_{f}
for some weight
k
newform
f
with CM and trivial nebentypus, assuming ERH. If
k=2
and
d\leq 6
, or if
(k-1)d=2^{n}3^{\epsilon}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
, then this holds unconditionally.

Such an

as in the theorem must have CM by an imaginary quadratic field

with (class group) exponent

(see

).
The assumption of ERH guarantees that there are only finitely many imaginary quadratic fields of exponent

[
2
]

, or

is a power of 2, or

[

. Then we prove

2.
CM forms

In this section, we set some notation, recall basic facts about Grössencharacters and CM forms, and relate the rationality fields of CM forms to the fields of values of Grössencharacters.

2.1.
Notation

For a field
F
, let
\mu_{F}
be the group of roots of unity in
F
. For a finite-degree field extension
K/F
, denote by
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{K/F}
the norm map from
K
to
F
.
If
F
is a number field,
\Delta_{F}
denotes its discriminant,
\mathfrak{o}_{F}
its ring of integers,
\mathrm{Cl}(F)
its ideal class group, and
h_{F}=\#\mathrm{Cl}(F)
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\zeta_{n}
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity, and write
\mu_{n}=\langle\zeta_{n}\rangle
. We denote the cyclic group of order
n
by
C_{n}
.

Throughout,
E/\mathbb{Q}
will be an imaginary quadratic field,
p
will denote a prime in
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathfrak{p}
will denote a prime of
E
above
p
. Further,
\mathfrak{m}
will denote a nonzero integral ideal of
E
.

For
\alpha\in E^{\times}
, write
\alpha\equiv 1\bmod\mathfrak{m}
if
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha-1)\geq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})
for each prime
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}
. (Some authors write
\alpha\equiv 1\text{ mod}^{\times}\,\mathfrak{m}
.). Let
E^{\mathfrak{m}}=\{\alpha\in E^{\times}:v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)=0\text{ for all }\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}\}
and
E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}=\{\alpha\in E^{\times}:\alpha\equiv 1\bmod\mathfrak{m}\}
.
Denote by
J^{\mathfrak{m}}=J^{\mathfrak{m}}(E)
the group of (invertible) fractional ideals of
E
relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m}
,
P^{\mathfrak{m}}=P^{\mathfrak{m}}(E)
the subgroup of principal ideals, and
P_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}=P_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}(E)=\{\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{E}:\alpha\in E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}\}
the subgroup of principal ideals which are
1\bmod\mathfrak{m}
. The associated ray class group is
\mathrm{Cl}^{\mathfrak{m}}(E)=J^{\mathfrak{m}}(E)/P_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}(E)
.

2.2.
Grössencharacters and CM forms

Fix an imaginary quadratic field
E/\mathbb{Q}
, and a nonzero integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
E
. Let
\psi
be a Grössencharacter of
E
with conductor
\mathfrak{m}
and weight
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, which means
\psi:J^{\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a character such that

\psi(\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{E})=\alpha^{\ell}\quad\text{if }\alpha\equiv 1\bmod\mathfrak{m}.

Given
\mathfrak{m}
and
\ell
, there exists such a
\psi
if and only if
\zeta^{\ell}=1
for each
\zeta\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}\cap E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
. Note that
\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}\cap E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
is only nontrivial if (i)
2\in\mathfrak{m}
or (ii)
\Delta_{E}=-3
and
\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{m})\in\{1,3\}
.

The map
E^{\mathfrak{m}}/E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}\simeq(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\to P^{\mathfrak{m}}/P_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
is surjective with kernel isomorphic to
\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}\cap E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
. We often identify
E^{\mathfrak{m}}/E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
with
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
. Then
\psi
gives rise to a character
\eta_{\psi}:(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
via

\eta_{\psi}(\alpha)=\alpha^{-\ell}\psi(\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{E}),

which we call the
modulus character
of
\psi
.
Replacing
\alpha
by
u\alpha
shows that
\eta_{\psi}(u)=u^{-\ell}
for any
u\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}=\mu_{E}
. Further,
\eta_{\psi}
is trivial on
\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}\cap E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Extend
\psi
to be
0
on integral ideals not coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
.
Then the following result can be found in
[
16
, Lemma 3]
and
[
13
, Section 3]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Hecke, Shimura)
.

Define

f_{\psi}=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\psi(\mathfrak{a})q^{\mathcal{N}\mathfrak{a}},

where
\mathfrak{a}
runs over integral ideals of
E
. Let
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the Dirichlet character of
\mathbb{Z}
mod
M=\mathcal{N}\mathfrak{m}
given by composing
\eta_{\psi}
with the natural map
\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\to\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m}
. Then

f_{\psi}\in S_{\ell+1}(|\Delta_{E}|M,\chi_{E}\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}).

If
\psi
is primitive (see
Section

4
), then
f_{\psi}
is a newform.

In particular, we have the property
f_{\psi}\otimes\chi_{E}=f_{\psi}
, which means that
f_{\psi}
has complex multiplication (CM) by
E
. Further, any newform with CM by
E
is of the form
f_{\psi}
for some such
\psi
(
[
13
, Theorem 4.5]
).

We are most interested in the case that
\ell
is odd and
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\chi_{E}
(as Dirichlet characters mod
|\Delta_{E}|M
), which corresponds to
f_{\psi}
having even weight and trivial nebentypus. This will imply that
\psi(p\mathfrak{o}_{E})={\Delta_{E}\overwithdelims( )p}p^{\ell}
when
p\nmid\Delta_{E}
. Further, the conductor of
\chi_{E}
will have to divide that of
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, which means that
\Delta_{E}\mid M
(see
4.3
for a more precise condition). The condition
\eta_{\psi}(-1)=\chi_{E}(-1)=-1
implies that
-1\not\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}\cap E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
, and thus
2\in\mathfrak{m}
is impossible.

2.3.
Rationality fields and value fields

Now we connect rationality fields of CM forms (with trivial nebentypus) with the values of Grössencharacters. By the
value field

L_{\psi}
of a Grössencharacter
\psi
, we mean the subfield of
\mathbb{C}
generated by
\text{Im}\,\psi
. Then
L_{\psi}
is a number field containing
E
, as it is generated over
E
by its values on a set of representatives for the ray class group
\mathrm{Cl}^{\mathfrak{m}}(E)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f=f_{\psi}\in S_{k}(N)
with rationality field
K=K_{f}
, and let
L=L_{\psi}
be the value field of
\psi
. Then
L=EK
.

Proof.

Note that
L
is generated by the values
\psi(\mathfrak{p})
, and
K
is generated by the values
\psi(\mathfrak{p})+\psi(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})
, where in both cases
\mathfrak{p}
ranges over prime ideals in
\mathfrak{o}_{E}
.

Say
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime above
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. We know that
\psi(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathbb{Z}
when
p
is inert or ramified in
E
, so it suffices to consider split primes
p=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Since
\psi(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})=\psi(p\mathfrak{o}_{E})/\psi(\mathfrak{p})=p^{\ell}/\psi(\mathfrak{p})
, we see that
\beta=\psi(\mathfrak{p})
satisfies a quadratic polynomial over
K
, and thus
\beta
lies in a quadratic extension of
K
.

Say
\mathfrak{p}
has order
n
in
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
, and write
\mathfrak{p}^{n}=\pi\mathfrak{o}_{E}
for some irreducible
\pi\in E
. Let
r
be the multiplicative order of
\pi
mod
\mathfrak{m}
. Then
\beta^{nr}=\psi(\mathfrak{p}^{n})^{r}=\pi^{r\ell}
, so
\beta^{n}=\psi(\mathfrak{p}^{n})=\zeta\pi^{\ell}
for some
\zeta\in\mu_{r}
. Now any positive power of
\pi
lies in
E-\mathbb{Q}
(or else
\mathfrak{p}
would ramify over
p
), so some positive power of
\beta
generates
E
. However, since
\beta
was quadratic over
K
and
E
must be disjoint from the totally real field
K
, one deduces
\beta\in EK
. This implies
L\subseteq EK
.

For the converse, note that
L\supseteq K
is clear. Now by the previous paragraph, some power of
\beta=\psi(\mathfrak{p})
generates
E
and thus
L\supseteq EK
.
∎

Thus we can characterize the rationality field
K
of
f_{\psi}\in S_{k}(N)
as the unique totally real index 2 subfield of the value field
L
of
\psi
. Hence to study rationality fields of CM forms with trivial nebentypus, it suffices to study value fields of Grössencharacters.

3.
Value fields of Grössencharacters

Here we prove some basic properties about value fields of Grössencharacters and deduce
1
.
Let
\psi
be a Grössencharacter of
E
of conductor
\mathfrak{m}
and weight
\ell
. Denote by
L=L_{\psi}
its value field, and set
d=[L:E]
.

3.1.
Ideal class behavior in value fields

Note that if
\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{E}\in P^{\mathfrak{m}}
has order
r
mod
P_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}}
, then
\psi(\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{E})=\zeta\alpha^{\ell}
for some
\zeta\in\mu_{r}
. Since
\alpha^{\ell}\in L
, this means that in fact
\zeta\in\mu_{L}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{o}_{E}
, the
L
-ideal class of
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{o}_{L}
has order dividing
\ell
in
\mathrm{Cl}(L)
.

When
\ell=1
, this means that
L
is a principalization (or capitulation) field for
E
.

Proof.

We may assume
\mathfrak{a}
is coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
.

Consider
\mathfrak{a}\in J^{\mathfrak{m}}
. Say
\mathfrak{a}
has order
r
in
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
, and write
\mathfrak{a}^{r}=\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{E}
, where
\alpha\in E^{\times}
.
Since
\psi(\mathfrak{a}^{r})=\eta_{\psi}(\alpha)\alpha^{\ell}\in\alpha^{\ell}\mathfrak{o}_{L}^{\times}
, we have

(\psi(\mathfrak{a})\mathfrak{o}_{L})^{r}=\psi(\mathfrak{a}^{r})\mathfrak{o}_{L}=\alpha^{\ell}\mathfrak{o}_{L}=(\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{L})^{\ell}=(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{o}_{L})^{r\ell}.

By unique factorization of ideals, we have
(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{o}_{L})^{\ell}=\psi(\mathfrak{a})\mathfrak{o}_{L}
, which is principal.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The exponent of
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
divides
\ell d
.

Proof.

For any ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{o}_{E}
, note that
\mathcal{N}_{L/E}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{o}_{L})=\mathfrak{a}^{d}
. Now suppose
\mathfrak{a}
is nonzero, and thus
\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}\mathfrak{o}_{L}=\beta\mathfrak{o}_{L}
for some
\beta\in\mathfrak{o}_{L}
by
3.1
. Hence
\mathfrak{a}^{\ell d}=\mathcal{N}_{L/E}(\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}\mathfrak{o}_{L})=N(\beta)\mathfrak{o}_{E}
is principal.
∎

The above results generalize the case of
L=E
in
[
15
]
. They allow us to reduce the problem of classifying degree
d
rationality fields of CM forms in
S_{k}(N)
to considering those with CM by a field
E
with (class group) exponent dividing
(k-1)d
.

3.2.
Restrictions on value fields

Now for fixed
E
and weight
\ell
, we obtain necessary conditions on the possible value fields
L
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
L_{0}\subset L
be the subfield generated by the values of
\psi
on principal ideals in
P^{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
L_{0}=E\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
, where
r
is the order of
\eta_{\psi}
.

Proof.

First note that
\psi(P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{1})
generates
E
. For any principal ideal in
P^{\mathfrak{m}}
, we may scale it by an element of
P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
so that it is represented by some
\alpha\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}
. Then
\eta_{\psi}(\alpha)=\psi(\alpha\mathfrak{o}_{E})/\alpha^{\ell}\in L_{0}
. Conversely, all values of
\eta_{\psi}
must also lie in
L_{0}
, and all values of
\psi
on
P^{\mathfrak{m}}
must lie in
\eta_{\psi}((\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times})E
.
∎

For
\alpha\in E^{\times}
, let
\alpha^{1/n}
denote some
n
-th root of
\alpha
in the algebraic closure of
E
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}_{1},\dots\mathfrak{t}_{g}
be representatives for a set of generators for
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
. Let
n_{i}
be the order of
\mathfrak{t}_{i}
in
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
and write
\mathfrak{t}_{i}^{n_{i}}=\theta_{i}\mathfrak{o}_{E}
. Let
r
be the order of
\eta_{\psi}
. Then

L=L_{\psi}=E(\zeta_{r},(u_{1}\theta_{1}^{\ell})^{1/n_{1}},\dots,(u_{g}\theta_{g}^{\ell})^{1/n_{g}})

for some
u_{1},\dots,u_{g}\in\mu_{r}
(and some choices of
n_{i}
-th roots of
u_{i}\theta_{i}^{\ell}
). In particular,
L\subset\tilde{E}_{r,\ell}:=E(\zeta_{r},\zeta_{n},\theta_{1}^{\ell/n},\dots,\theta_{g}^{\ell/n})
where
n
is the exponent of
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
.

Note that
\tilde{E}_{r,\ell}
really only depends on
E
,
r
and
\ell
, and not on the choices of
\mathfrak{t}_{i}
or
\theta_{i}
or
n_{i}
-th roots.

Proof.

For
1\leq i\leq g
, select
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\gamma_{i}\mathfrak{t}_{i}\in J^{\mathfrak{m}}
for some
\gamma_{i}\in E^{\times}
. Then
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{n_{i}}=\alpha_{i}\mathfrak{o}_{E}
, where
\alpha_{i}=\gamma_{i}^{n_{i}}\theta_{i}
. From
3.3
, the values of
\psi
on principal ideals generates
L_{0}=E(\zeta_{r})
. Hence
L_{\psi}=L_{0}(\psi(\mathfrak{a}_{i}),\dots,\psi(\mathfrak{a}_{g}))
.
Note that
\psi(\mathfrak{a}_{i})
is an
n_{i}
-th root of
\eta_{\psi}(\alpha_{i})\alpha_{i}^{\ell}=\eta_{\psi}(\alpha_{i})\theta_{i}^{\ell}\gamma_{i}^{n_{i}\ell}
. Hence
L_{0}(\psi(\mathfrak{a}_{i}))=L_{0}((\eta_{\psi}(\alpha_{i})\theta_{i}^{\ell})^{1/n_{i}})
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Fix
E,\ell
and
r
as above. From the proof we see that
L_{\psi}
only depends on
\eta_{\psi}
and the choice of certain
n_{i}
-th roots of unity. If
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
is cyclic, there is only one
n_{i}
and any choice of an
n_{i}
-th root will give the same
L_{\psi}
up to isomorphism. If
n\mid r
, then the choice of root of unity does not affect the value field. Hence if either
n\mid r
or
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
is cyclic, then
\eta_{\psi}
determines
L_{\psi}
up to isomorphism.

Once we bound the degree of the value field
L_{\psi}
, there are finitely many possibilities for the order of
\eta_{\psi}
(without restriction on
\ell
or
\mathfrak{m}
) by
3.3
. Noting that the field
\tilde{E}_{r,\ell}
in the proposition does not depend upon the conductor, and only depends on
\ell\bmod n
, we deduce the following.

Corollary 3.6
.

Given
E
,
d\geq 1
, there are finitely many extensions
L/E
of degree
d
which are value fields of Grössencharacters of
E
(for arbitrary weights and conductors).

This, together with
3.2
and the finiteness results about imaginary quadratic fields with given exponent mentioned in the Introduction, proves
1
.

4.
Minimal conductors

In order to classify CM newforms with degree
d
rationality field (assuming a classification of
E
with given exponent), we would like to be able to do the following.
Given
E
,
d
and
\ell
, classify: (i) degree
2d
value fields
L
of weight
\ell
Grössencharacters
\psi
such that
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\chi_{E}
, and (ii) all Grössencharacters
\psi
with a given value field
L
such that
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\chi_{E}
. While (i) is our goal in this paper, we will say some things about (ii). In fact a large part of (ii) is determining what conductors
\mathfrak{m}
are possible, and this is useful for (i). Problem (ii) was taken up in
[
15
]
when
L=E
, which corresponds to
K=\mathbb{Q}
. In this section we establish some results about what conductors need to be considered.

Let
\psi
be a Grössencharacter of
E
with weight
\ell
and conductor
\mathfrak{m}
.

We say that
\psi
(resp.
\eta_{\psi}
) is primitive of conductor
\mathfrak{m}
if there is no integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
properly dividing
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\psi
extends to a Grössencharacter of conductor
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
(resp.
\eta_{\psi}
factors through
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})^{\times}
). For our classification problems, it clearly suffices to restrict to primitive
\psi
. The following says that
\psi
is primitive if and only if
\eta_{\psi}
is primitive.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
is a nonzero integral ideal of
E
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mid\mathfrak{m}
and that
\eta_{\psi}
factors through
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})^{\times}
. Then
\psi
is the restriction to
J^{\mathfrak{m}}
of a Grössencharacter
\psi^{\prime}
with conductor
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
such that
\eta_{\psi^{\prime}}=\eta_{\psi}
and
L_{\psi^{\prime}}=L_{\psi}
.

Proof.

By induction, it suffices to prove this in the case that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. If
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
, then
J^{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}=J^{\mathfrak{m}}
and one only needs to check that
\psi(\alpha)=\alpha^{\ell}
when
\alpha\equiv 1\bmod\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. But this is immediate from
\eta_{\psi}
being trivial on such
\alpha
.

Assume
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
where
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.
To extend
\psi
to a character
\psi^{\prime}
of
J^{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}
, we only need to specify
\psi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})
. Write
\mathfrak{p}=\alpha\mathfrak{q}
, where
\alpha\in E^{\times}
and
\mathfrak{q}\in J^{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
\alpha
is coprime to
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
, and we can define
\psi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})=\eta_{\psi}(\alpha)\alpha^{\ell}\psi(\mathfrak{q})
.

To see this is independent of the choice of
\alpha
and
\mathfrak{q}
, consider another representation
\mathfrak{p}=\alpha^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
where
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\in J^{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
\mathfrak{q}(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})^{-1}=\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{-1}\mathfrak{o}_{E}\in J^{\mathfrak{m}}
. From this one deduces

\eta_{\psi}(\alpha^{\prime})(\alpha^{\prime})^{\ell}\psi(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})=\eta_{\psi}(\alpha)\alpha^{\ell}\psi(\mathfrak{q})=\psi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})
.

Now
\eta_{\psi}
being trivial on
E_{1}^{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}
means that
\psi^{\prime}
has conductor
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. The assertions on
\eta_{\psi^{\prime}}
and
L_{\psi^{\prime}}
are also clear.
∎

Given a Dirichlet character
\chi
of conductor
Q
, we define the twist of
\psi
to be the primitive weight
\ell
Grössencharacter of
E
such that
(\psi\otimes\chi)(\mathfrak{a})=\chi(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{a}))\psi(\mathfrak{a})
for
\mathfrak{a}\in J^{\mathfrak{m}Q}
.
A priori its conductor is just a divisor of
\mathfrak{m}Q
, however
\psi\otimes\chi
may have conductor which is a proper divisor of
\mathfrak{m}
.

Suppose
\chi
is a quadratic character. Then the quadratic twist
\psi\otimes\chi
has the same value field as
\psi
. (More generally, if
\chi
has image contained in
\mu_{L}
, then
L_{\psi\otimes\chi}\subset L_{\psi}
, but they are not in general equal.) Further
\eta_{\psi\otimes\chi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Consequently in our study of value fields, we may reduce to considering primitive Grössencharacters which have minimal conductor among the family of all quadratic twists
\psi\otimes\chi
.

Remark 4.2
.

By only working with newforms of trivial nebentypus or newforms with a fixed rationality field
K
, we cannot always restrict to considering minimal CM forms or Grössencharacters. For instance, there is a newform
f\in S_{2}(15^{2})
(labelled
225.2.a.f
in the LMFDB) with rationality field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
and CM by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})
, but it is not minimal: it is an order 4 twist of a level 45 form with character
{5\overwithdelims( )\cdot}
that has rationality field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})
.

Now let us restrict to the setting that
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\chi_{E}
. We have the following minimal divisibility condition on the conductor. For this and subsequent results, we will need some facts about the structure of modular multiplicative groups
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{e})^{\times}
. These fact are well known for
p
odd, and are recalled in
Appendix

A
below for
p=2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\chi_{E}
then
\mathfrak{d}_{E}\mid\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{d}_{E}
is defined by

\mathfrak{d}_{E}:=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\Delta_{E}}\mathfrak{o}_{E}&\text{if }2\nmid\Delta_{E}\\
\mathfrak{p}_{2}\sqrt{\Delta_{E}}\mathfrak{o}_{E}&\text{if }4\parallel\Delta_{E}\\
2\sqrt{\Delta_{E}}\mathfrak{o}_{E}&\text{if }8\parallel\Delta_{E},\end{cases}

and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
is the prime above
2
in
E
when
2\mid\Delta_{E}
.

Proof.

Since
\chi_{E}
is a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
|\Delta_{E}|
, we need that the natural map
(\mathbb{Z}/\Delta_{E}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
is injective. Write
\Delta_{E}=-2^{e}q_{1}\dots q_{t}
, where the
q_{i}
’s are odd primes and
e\in\{0,2,3\}
. Then

(\mathbb{Z}/\Delta_{E}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2^{e}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\times\prod(\mathbb{Z}/q_{i}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Via Chinese Remainder Theorem,
(\mathbb{Z}/\Delta_{E}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
is injective if and only if decomposition for
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
, we see that
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
injects into
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})})^{\times}
for all
p\mid\Delta_{E}
(where, as usual,
\mathfrak{p}
denotes the prime above
p
).

Suppose now that
\Delta_{E}
is even. Since
(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})
(resp.
(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
) embeds in
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{j})^{\times}
if and only if
j\geq 3
(resp.
j\geq 5
), we must have that
\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{3}\mid\mathfrak{m}
when
4\parallel\Delta_{E}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{5}\mid\mathfrak{m}
when
8\parallel\Delta_{E}
.
Thus in all cases
\mathfrak{d}_{E}\mid\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

Consequently, if
f\in S_{k}(N)
is a newform with CM by
E
, we must have
N_{E}\mid N
, where

N_{E}:=\Delta_{E}\cdot\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{d}_{E})=\begin{cases}\Delta_{E}^{2}&\text{if }2\nmid\Delta_{E}\\
2\Delta_{E}^{2}&\text{if }4\parallel\Delta_{E}\\
4\Delta_{E}^{2}&\text{if }8\parallel\Delta_{E}.\end{cases}

In particular, if we want to determine the existence of a Grössencharacter
\psi
of
E
with value field
L=EK
(resp. CM form
f
with rationality field
K
), the minimal conductor (resp. level) in which it can occur is
\mathfrak{d}_{E}
(resp.
N_{E}
). Under certain conditions, we can reduce to conductor
\mathfrak{d}_{E}
(resp. level
N_{E}
):

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
\psi
is primitive with
\eta_{\psi}^{2}=1
and
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\chi_{E}
. Then there is a quadratic twist
\psi\otimes\chi
which has conductor
\mathfrak{d}_{E}
. Moreover, either
\Delta_{E}
is odd or
\Delta_{E}\equiv 0\bmod 8
.

This is essentially proven in
[
15
]
, though it is formulated in the setting of
L=E
.

Proof.

Say
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p}^{e}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
, where
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
is coprime to
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
p
be the prime below
\mathfrak{p}
. Decompose
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}=(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{e})^{\times}\times(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})^{\times}
. Write
\eta=\eta_{\psi}
, and let
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the character of
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{e})^{\times}
obtained by restricting
\eta
to this factor.

By
4.1
,
\eta
is primitive. Since
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{e})^{\times}
is isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}_{E})^{\times}
times a group of order
q^{e-1}
, where
q=\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})
,
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
must factor through
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}_{E})^{\times}
if
q
is odd. Hence we may assume
e\leq 1
when
p
is odd. If
e=1
, primitivity means
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a (nontrivial) quadratic character.

Next suppose that
p
is an odd prime such that
p\mid\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{m})
and
p\nmid\Delta_{E}
. We show we can twist away the
p
-part of the conductor.

Consider the natural map
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/p\mathfrak{o}_{E})^{\times}
, and let
\eta_{p}
be the restriction of
\eta
to
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/p\mathfrak{o}_{E})^{\times}
. Then
\eta_{p}
must be trivial on the image of this map.

If
p\mathfrak{o}_{E}=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
is split in
E
, then identifying
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/p\mathfrak{o}_{E})^{\times}=(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p})^{\times}\times(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\bar{\mathfrak{p}})^{\times}=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\times(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, we see
\eta_{p}(a,a)=1
for all
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Hence
\eta_{p}(a,b)=\chi_{p}(a)\chi_{p}(b)
, where
\chi_{p}
denotes the unique quadratic Dirichlet character of conductor
p
. (We remark this implies that if
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}
, so does
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
.) Since we also have
\eta_{p}=\chi_{p}\circ\mathcal{N}
as characters of
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/p\mathfrak{o}_{E})^{\times}
, the twist
\psi\otimes\chi_{p}
must have conductor
p^{-1}\mathfrak{m}
.

If
p
is inert in
E
, then
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p})^{\times}\simeq C_{p^{2}-1}
, and
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
factors through
C_{p^{2}-1}/C_{p-1}
. Again, this must match with
\chi_{p}\circ\mathcal{N}
, so that
\psi\otimes\chi_{p}
has conductor
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\mathfrak{m}
.

Finally we treat the case
p=2
.

First suppose that
2\mid\Delta_{E}
. By
4.3
, we have that
e\geq 3
if
4\parallel\Delta_{E}
and
e\geq 5
if
8\mid\Delta_{E}
.
If
4\parallel\Delta_{E}
,
-1
is the square of an element of order 4 in
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{e})^{\times}
which forces
\eta_{\psi}
to have order a multiple of 4. Hence
4\parallel\Delta_{E}
is impossible.
If
8\mid\Delta_{E}
, then only
e=5
is possible for
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
to be primitive and quadratic.

Now assume that
p=2\nmid\Delta_{E}
. Then
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
can only be quadratic and primitive if

.

If
2=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
splits in

, then as in the
p

\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}=\eta_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}
as the same (possibly imprimitive) character of
(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{C}_{2}^{2}
. Viewing
\chi=\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
as a Dirichlet character of conductor dividing 8, we see that
\psi\otimes\chi
has conductor coprime to 2.

If
2
is inert in
E
, then
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
factors through
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/8\mathfrak{o}_{E})^{\times}
modulo the image of
(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

, so
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
factors through
C_{2}^{2}
.
Since

is unramified, the norm induces a surjective map
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/8\mathfrak{o}_{E})^{\times}\to(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, and hence
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
must be of the form
\chi\circ\mathcal{N}
for one of the quadratic Dirichlet characters
\chi
of conductor dividing 8. For this

, one gets that
\psi\otimes\chi
has conductor coprime to 2.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
be odd and
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\ell}(E)
be the set of weight
\ell
Grössencharacters
\psi
of

with quadratic modulus character

such that
\eta_{\psi}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\chi_{E}
. If
\mathrm{Cl}(E)
is cyclic or has exponent

of value fields up to isomorphism has cardinality (i)

if

is odd, (ii)

if

, and (iii)

or

if

.

Proof.

There are no such
\psi
when in case (ii), so assume we are in case (i) or (iii). By

, we may assume

has modulus

.

We have

is odd and

. Hence there is 1 possibility for

when

is odd and 2 possibilities for a quadratic
\eta_{\psi}
when

(the generator of the

). Now apply

.
∎

Appendix A
Structure of dyadic modular quotients

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a quadratic field, and
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime above
2
. Here we recall some structure theory about
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{n})^{\times}
. If
2
splits in
E
, then
(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{n})^{\times}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\simeq C_{2}\times C_{2}^{n-2}
for
n\geq 2
. So assume
2
is either inert or ramified in
E
.

Consider a local quadratic extension
k/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
with integer ring
\mathfrak{o}_{k}
and prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{k}
. Write
\mathfrak{u}_{n}=(1+\mathfrak{p}^{n})\cap\mathfrak{o}_{k}^{\times}
for the
n
-th unit group of
k
. Put
f=f(k/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
and
e=e(k/\mathbb{Q}_{2})
. It is well known that when
k=E_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
,

G:=(\mathfrak{o}_{E}/\mathfrak{p}^{n})^{\times}=(\mathfrak{o}_{k}/\mathfrak{p}_{k}^{n})^{\times}\simeq C_{2^{f}-1}\times\mathfrak{u}_{1}/\mathfrak{u}_{n}.

First we have the following facts about the 2-rank of
G
(e.g., see
[
11
]
, which also treats higher-degree fields).

(i)

Suppose
n<2e+1
. Then the 2-rank of
G
is
\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\rceil f
.

If
2
is inert in
E
, then
G
has 2-rank 0 or 2 when
n=1
or
n=2
, respectively. Hence
G\simeq C_{3}
or
G\simeq C_{3}\times C_{2}^{2}
in these cases.

If
2
ramifies in
E
, then
G
has 2-rank
0,1,1,2
for
n=1,2,3,4
, respectively. Necessarily
G
is isomorphic to
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{4}
, or
C_{2}\times C_{4}
.

(ii)

Suppose
n\geq 2e+1
. Then the 2-rank is
ef+1=3
.

If
2
is inert in
E
, this means
G
has 2-rank 3 when
n\geq 3
. Necessarily
G\simeq C_{3}\times C_{2}^{2}\times C_{4}
when
n=3
.

If
2
ramifies in
E
, then
G
has 2-rank 3 when

(iii)

(iv)

Suppose

The precise structure of

d

n\geq 5

a,b\geq 0

a+b=n-2

(v)

Suppose
8\parallel\Delta_{E}
. The natural map
(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to G
is injective if and only if
n\geq 5
. Moreover,
G\simeq\langle-1\rangle\times C_{2^{r-2}}\times C_{2^{s}}

C_{2^{r-2}}

We want to know when the image of any nontrivial element of
(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is a square when
n\geq 5
. Necessarily
-1
is not a square, and 5 is a square if and only if
n\geq 7
. Further
3\equiv(-1)\cdot 5\bmod 8

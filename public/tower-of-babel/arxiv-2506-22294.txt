Maximal intrinsic randomness of noisy quantum measurements
Fionnuala Curran, Morteza Moradi, Gabriel Senno, Magdalena Stobinska, Antonio Acín

Abstract
Quantum physics exhibits an intrinsic and private form of randomness with no classical counterpart. Any setup for quantum randomness generation involves measurements acting on quantum states. In this work, we consider the following question: Given a quantum measurement, how much randomness can be generated from it? In real life, measurements are noisy and thus contain an additional, extrinsic form of randomness due to ignorance. This extrinsic randomness is not private since, in an adversarial model, it takes the form of quantum side information held by an eavesdropper who can use it to predict the measurement outcomes. Randomness of measurements is then quantified by the guessing probability of this eavesdropper, when minimized over all possible input states. This optimization is in general hard to compute, but we solve it here for any two-outcome qubit measurement and for projective measurements in arbitrary dimension mixed with white noise. We also construct, for a given measured probability distribution, different realizations with (i) a noisy state and noiseless measurement (ii) a noiseless state and noisy measurement and (iii) a noisy state and measurement, and we show that the latter gives an eavesdropper significantly higher guessing power.



Introduction
.—
Randomness is a useful peculiarity of quantum physics. Say we have a quantum system represented by a pure state
\ket{\psi}
, and we measure it in a basis to which it is unbiased. Our outcomes would not just be statistically random (independent and identically distributed with uniform probability); they would also be wholly unpredictable, even to a potential eavesdropper who tried to guess them. This private or
intrinsic
quality of quantum randomness is exploited in the design of quantum random number generators
Mannalatha et al. 2023
;
Herrero-Collantes and Garcia-Escartin 2017
and quantum cryptography protocols
Portmann and Renner 2022
.

Quantum measurements can, however, be mixed, so they possess an additional,
extrinsic
form of randomness. A mixed (or non-extremal) measurement can be seen as a probabilistic mixture of other measurements
D'Ariano et al. 2005
or as the incomplete marginal of a projective measurement carried out on a larger system, as captured by Naimark’s dilation theorem
Peres 1990
. In fact, real-life measurements, plagued by noise, are necessarily mixed. Moreover, from a fundamental perspective, pure projective measurements are impossible to implement with finite thermodynamic resources
Guryanova et al. 2020
.

While we usually think of noise in our measurement as inconvenient but benign, in a cryptographic scenario, to ensure the security of our outcomes, we must ascribe all of our ignorance to
side information
held by an adversary. For convenience, we call our randomness-generating experimentalist Alice and her hypothetical eavesdropper Eve. Eve’s side information may be classical, when she knows which measurement from a probabilistic decomposition is
actually
performed by Alice in each round, or quantum, when she shares entanglement with Alice’s devices. If Alice performs a measurement and records the outcome
x
from a register
X
, Eve’s knowledge is intuitively quantified by the probability that she correctly guesses
x
. This guessing probability is directly related to the min-entropy of
X
conditioned on Eve’s information
Konig et al. 2009
, which in turn lower bounds the number of private and statistically random bits that can be extracted from
X

Renner 2006
;
Konig and Renner 2011
.

In this work, we address the following question, of relevance both from a fundamental and an applied point of view: Given a quantum measurement
{\mathcal{M}}
, what is the maximal amount of intrinsic randomness one can generate from it? For that, we should determine the optimal state on which Alice should apply the given measurement. We solve this question and therefore derive analytic solutions for the maximal intrinsic randomness of two classes of realistic noisy measurements: any qubit measurement with two outcomes and isotropic noisy projective measurements in any dimension
d
, resulting from mixing a rank-one projective measurement with isotropic noise. Our results represent a step forward in quantifying randomness in quantum measurements and are complementary to recent works that characterized the intrinsic randomness of quantum states
Meng et al. 2024
;
Anco et al. 2024
.

Setting the problem
.—
Any randomness generation process involves measurements acting on states. We work throughout in a Hilbert space of finite dimension
d
. A quantum state
\rho
is a positive semidefinite operator with trace one, while a quantum measurement is defined by a positive operator-valued measure (POVM), a set of positive-semidefinite operators
{\mathcal{M}}=\{M_{x}\}_{x}
that sum to identity,
\sum_{x}M_{x}=\mathbbm{1}
. If we measure
\rho
using
{\mathcal{M}}
, the probability of obtaining the outcome
x
is given by the Born rule,
p(x)=\tr\left(\rho M_{x}\right)
.

A projective measurement
\Pi=\{\Pi_{x}\}_{x}
is a special class of POVM satisfying
\Pi_{x}\Pi_{y}=\delta_{xy}\Pi_{x}
.
A general non-projective POVM
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}
(in what follows, we sometimes label Alice’s system by
S

generalized Naimark extension
. Concretely, a global projective measurement
\Pi_{SA}
is applied to Alice’s state
\rho

\sigma_{A}
inside Alice’s measuring device. For such an extension to be valid, it must satisfy

\tr_{A}\Big(\Pi_{x,SA}\,\mathbbm{1}_{S}\otimes\sigma_{A}\Big)=M_{x,S}\;\;\text{for all}\;x\,.

(1)

For a given measurement, there exist many possible extensions. We use an adversarial model introduced in
Frauchiger et al. 2013
and generalized in
Senno et al. 2023
(see also
Dai et al. 2023
), wherein the eavesdropper Eve can, in principle, share entanglement both with Alice’s state
\rho

and
with her POVM
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}
. However, since here we are interested in maximizing the randomness that can be generated from a given measurement, Alice’s optimal state can be taken pure without loss of generality,
\rho=\outerproduct{ \phi}{\phi}_{S}

{\mathcal{M}}
. This quantum side information is encapsulated by a purification
\ket{\varphi}_{AE}
of the auxiliary state
\sigma_{A}
in Alice’s device. To guess Alice’s outcome, Eve applies a measurement
\{M_{x,E}\}_{x}
to her particle

\displaystyle{\displaystyle P^{\,\textnormal{Q}}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi}_{S},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{S}\big)=\;

(2)

\displaystyle\max_{\{\Pi_{x,SA}\}_{x},\,\ket{\varphi}_{AE},\,\{M_{x,E}\}_{x}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\bra{\Phi}\Pi_{x,SA}\otimes M_{x,E}\ket{\Phi}_{SAE}

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\tr_{A}\Big(\Pi_{x,SA}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{S}\otimes\tr_{E}\left(\outerproduct{ \varphi}{\varphi}_{AE}\right)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=M_{x,S}\;\text{ for all }\;x\,,

Happily, it is known

\displaystyle{\displaystyle P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)=\;

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\bra{\phi}K_{j,j}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle K_{x,j}\geq 0\;\text{ for all }\;x,j

\displaystyle\sum_{x}dK_{x,j}=\mathbbm{1}\sum_{x}\tr K_{x,j}\;\text{ for all }\;j

\displaystyle\sum_{j}K_{x,j}=M_{x}\text{ for all }x\,.

Main results
— We present our main contributions here, followed by three implications, with full details and derivations in the Appendix.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=\{M_{1},M_{2}\}
be any qubit POVM with two outcomes, where
\tr M_{1}\leq\tr M_{2}
. Then

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}\big)=1-\tr M_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\Big)^{2}\,.

(7)

We prove the theorem using two lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=\{M_{1},M_{2}\}
be any qubit POVM with two outcomes, where
\tr M_{1}\leq\tr M_{2}
. The following lower bound holds for any state
\ket{\phi}
,

{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)\geq 1-\tr M_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\Big)^{2}\,.

(8)

We sketch the proof, providing full details in Appendix
B.1
and an alternative proof using Bloch vectors in Appendix
B.2
. For any state
\ket{\phi}
chosen by Alice, Eve can decompose the POVM
{\mathcal{M}}
as
\mathcal{K}_{j}=\{K_{1,j},\,K_{2,j}\}
, where

\displaystyle K_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle K_{2,1}

\displaystyle=p(1)\mathbbm{1}-\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,

\displaystyle K_{1,2}

\displaystyle=M_{1}-\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,

\displaystyle K_{2,2}

\displaystyle=p(2)\mathbbm{1}-M_{1}+\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,

with
p(x)=\langle\phi|M_{x}|\phi\rangle

{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)\geq 1-2p\left(1\right)+2\langle\phi|\sqrt{M_{1}}|\phi\rangle^{2}\,.

(10)

We can further show that the right-hand side of (

is unbiased to the diagonal basis

Lemma 2

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=\{M_{1},M_{2}\}
be any qubit POVM with two outcomes, where

Other entropies
.—
We have solved Eve’s guessing probability for the noisy projective measurement
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
, minimized over all pure states. In terms of the conditional min-entropy, we have

{H^{*}_{\textnormal{min}}}\left(X|E,\,{\mathcal{M}}_{d}\right)=\log d-\log\Big( \tr\sqrt{M_1}\Big)^{2}\,.

(20)

From
Meng et al. 2024

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left({\mathcal{M}}_{d}\right)
is equal to the optimal guessing probability
{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left(\rho_{\psi}\right)

\rho_{\psi}

\rho_{\psi}=\left(1-{\varepsilon}\right)\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}+\frac{{\varepsilon}}{d}\mathbbm{1}\,,

(21)

This begs the question: Is the maximal intrinsic randomness of
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
and

Coarse-graining
.
—
Imagine that, when
d
is even and greater than 2, rather than perform the POVM
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
directly, Alice amalgamates the first half of its outcomes into a single outcome, 1, neglecting to distinguish between them, and acts similarly on the second half, which form the outcome 2. This coarse-graining process yields a new two-outcome POVM,
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}
. From Corollary
1

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}\big)\leq{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left({\mathcal{M}}_{2}\right)

, where the plateau of perfect probability emerges at
\delta=1/2
.

Discussion
.— In this work, we solved the maximal intrinsic randomness of two classes of noisy quantum measurements, both of practical relevance. Notably, the maximal intrinsic randomness of a noisy projective measurement is equal to that of a noisy pure state (optimized over projective measurements), so when measuring a pure state in an unbiased orthogonal basis, we are free to consider either the state
or
the measuring device to be the noisy one. However, our qubit case study shows that it is not equivalent to consider partial noise shared between the devices, as this scenario may give an eavesdropper significantly more guessing power. Our results may prove relevant in bounding the functionality of device-dependent QRNGs. Furthermore, we expect they will pave the way to better understand the generalized Naimark dilation in an adversarial scenario and to solve the maximal intrinsic randomness of more general quantum measurements.

Appendix A
The guessing probability

A.1
The primal problem

For a fixed POVM
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}=\{M_{x,S}\}_{x}
in finite dimension
d
, we wish to find the maximum randomness that can be generated from it, as quantified by the probability that an eavesdropper, Eve, correctly guesses the outcomes of the POVM when an experimentalist, Alice, applies it to the optimal state
\rho_{S}
, with which Eve may share quantum correlations. To formulate this guessing probability, we use a generalized Naimark dilation, introduced in
Frauchiger et al. 2013
, which differs from a typical Naimark dilation as the auxiliary state may be mixed, and therefore correlated with Eve.
We use as our figure of merit the following ‘quantum’ guessing probability for Eve, from
Senno et al. 2023
,

\displaystyle{P^{\,\textnormal{Q}}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\rho_{S},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{S}\big)=

\displaystyle\max_{\{\Pi_{x,SA}\}_{x},\,\ket{\phi}_{SAE},\,\{M_{x,E}\}_{x}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\bra{\phi}\Pi_{x,SA}\otimes M_{x,E}\ket{\phi}_{SAE}

(25)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\tr_{AE}\left(\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}_{SAE}\right)=\rho_{S}

\displaystyle\tr_{A}\Big(\Pi_{x,SA}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{S}\otimes\tr_{SE}\left(\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}_{SAE}\right)\big)\Big)=M_{x,S}\;\text{ for all }\;x

\displaystyle\langle{\phi|\Pi_{x,SA}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}|\phi}\rangle_{SAE}=\tr\big( M_{x, S} \, \rho_S \big)\,.

We want to determine the minimum of this guessing probability over all of Alice’s possible states
\rho_{S}
, i.e. to solve

{{P}^{\,\textnormal{Q *}}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}_{S}\big)=\min_{\rho_{S}}\,{P^{\,\textnormal{Q}}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\rho_{S},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{S}\big)\,.

(26)

Alice’s optimal state
\rho_{S}
can be taken pure without loss of generality. To see this, imagine that Alice uses a mixed state
\rho_{S}
. In our model, Eve would hold its purification
\ket{\phi}_{SE}
, but she could decide not to use it, so her guessing probability would be at least as high as if Alice had used the state
\ket{\phi}_{SE}
directly.
When Alice chooses a pure state
\ket{\phi}_{S}
, the guessing probability (
25
) reduces to

\displaystyle{P^{\,\textnormal{Q}}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi}_{S},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{S}\big)=

\displaystyle\max_{\{\Pi_{x,SA}\}_{x},\,\ket{\varphi}_{AE},\,\{M_{x,E}\}_{x}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\bra{\phi}\Pi_{x,SA}\otimes M_{x,E}\ket{\phi}_{SAE}

(27)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\tr_{A}\Big(\Pi_{x,SA}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{S}\otimes\tr_{E}\left(\outerproduct{ \varphi}{\varphi}_{AE}\right)\big)\Big)=M_{x,S}\;\text{ for all }\;x\,.

Since
\ket{\phi}_{S}
is pure, the guessing probability (
27
) reduces to the guessing probability of a classically correlated eavesdropper
Senno et al. 2023
who decomposes the POVM
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}=\{M_{x,S}\}_{x}
into ‘sub’-POVMs
\mathcal{N}_{j,S}=\{N_{x,j,S}\}_{x}
indexed by
j
, according to some probability distribution
\{p(j)\}
. We have the following definition for Eve’s classical guessing probability,

\displaystyle{P^{\,\textnormal{C}}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi}_{S},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{S}\big)=

\displaystyle\max_{\{p(j)\},\,\{\mathcal{N}_{j,S}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}p(j)\max_{x}\bra{\phi}N_{x,j,S}\ket{\phi}_{S}

(28)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle N_{x,j,S}\geq 0\;\text{for all}\;x,j

\displaystyle\sum_{x}N_{x,j,S}=\mathbbm{1}_{S}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}p(j)N_{x,j,S}=M_{x,S}\;\text{ for all }\;x\,.

Our figure of merit is now the minimization

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left({\mathcal{M}}_{S}\right)=\min_{\ket{\phi}}{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{S}\big)\,.

(29)

From here on, we drop the ‘classical’ superscript C and the subscript
S
denoting Alice’s system. The optimization (
28
) can be cast as a semidefinite programming (SDP) problem by defining the subnormalized decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
with elements

K_{x,j}=p(j)N_{x,j}\,,

(30)

as we have

\displaystyle{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)=

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\max_{x}\bra{\phi}K_{x,j}\ket{\phi}

(31)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle K_{x,j}\geq 0\;\text{ for all }\;x,j

\displaystyle\sum_{x}dK_{x,j}=\mathbbm{1}\sum_{x}\tr K_{x,j}\;\text{ for all }\;j

\displaystyle\sum_{j}K_{x,j}=M_{x}\text{ for all }x\,.

To prove equivalence, note that we can recover (
28
) from (
31
) by defining the variables

p(j)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x}\tr K_{x,j}\,,\qquad N_{x,j}=\frac{1}{p(j)}K_{x,j}\,

(32)

when
\sum_{x}\tr K_{x,j}\neq 0
. Furthermore, following an argument made in
Law et al. 2014
, we are free to consider the number of POVMs,
n
, in Eve’s decomposition to be equal to the number of outcomes,
m
, in
{\mathcal{M}}
. To see this, imagine that
n>m
and that the POVMs
\mathcal{K}_{k}=\{K_{x,k}\}_{x}
and
\mathcal{K}_{l}=\{K_{x,l}\}_{x}
correspond to the same most probable outcome,
y
, i.e.

y=\argmax_{x}\;\langle\phi|K_{x,k}|\phi\rangle=\argmax_{x}\;\langle\phi|K_{x,l}|\phi\rangle\,.

(33)

Eve could then combine these to form a new POVM
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{j}=\{{K}^{\prime}_{x,j}\}_{x}
, with

\{{K}^{\prime}_{x,j}\}_{x}=\{K_{x,k}\}_{x}+\{K_{x,l}\}_{x}\,,

(34)

without affecting her guessing probability. She could then repeat this process until she had whittled her
n
original POVMs down to
m
.
On the other hand, if
n<m
, Eve loses nothing by splitting one of her POVMs
\mathcal{K}_{j}=\{K_{x,j}\}_{x}
into a convex mixture of identical POVMs
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{k}=\{K_{x,k}\}_{x}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{l}=\{K_{x,l}\}_{x}
, i.e.

\{K^{\prime}_{x,k}\}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\{K_{x,j}\}_{x}\,,\qquad\{K^{\prime}_{x,l}\}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\{K_{x,j}\}_{x}\,.

(35)

As before, she could repeat this process until she reached
n=m
POVMs. Fixing
n=m
, we are then free to drop the inner maximization over
x
in (
31
) by labelling Eve’s POVMs such that

j=\argmax_{x}\bra{\phi}K_{x,j}\ket{\phi}\;\text{for all}\;x\,.

(36)

Our final SDP has the more tractable form

\displaystyle{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)=

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\bra{\phi}K_{j,j}\ket{\phi}

(37)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle K_{x,j}\geq 0\;\text{ for all }\;x,j

\displaystyle\sum_{x}dK_{x,j}=\mathbbm{1}\sum_{x}\tr K_{x,j}\;\text{ for all }\;j

\displaystyle\sum_{j}K_{x,j}=M_{x}\text{ for all }x\,.

A.2
The dual problem

Here we derive the dual for the SDP problem (
37
) (we refer to
Skrzypczyk and Cavalcanti 2023
for a detailed description on deriving dual problems).
The Lagrangian for (
37
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\tr\left(K_{j,j}\,\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\right)+\sum_{x}\tr\bigg(Y_{x} \Big( M_{x}- \sum_{j}K_{x, j}\Big) \bigg)+\sum_{j}\tr\bigg(F_{j} \sum_{x}\big(\id\tr-d \big) K_{x, j} \bigg)+\sum_{x,j}\tr\left(Z_{xj}\,K_{x,j}\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,j}\tr\bigg( K_{x, j} \Big(\delta_{x, j}\ketbra{\phi}{\phi} - Y_{x} + \left( \id\tr-d \right)F_j + Z_{xj}\Big)\bigg)+\sum_{x}\tr\big( Y_{x}M_x \big)\,,

where we introduced the dual variables
Y_{x}
,
F_{j}
and
Z_{xj}
. We impose
Z_{xj}\geq 0
such that

\mathcal{L}\geq\sum_{j}\tr\left(K_{j,j}\,\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\right)

(39)

holds for any feasible point. If we now restrict the dual variables to satisfy

\delta_{x,j}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}-Y_{x}+\left(\mathbbm{1}\tr-d\right)F_{j}+Z_{xj}=0\;\;\textrm{for all}\;x,\,j\,,

(40)

we arrive at

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{x}\tr\big( Y_{x}\, M_x \big)\,.

(41)

The dual problem is then

\displaystyle{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)=

\displaystyle\min_{\{Y_{x}\},\,\{F_{j}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\tr\big( Y_x \, M_x \big)

(42)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle Y_{x}\geq\delta_{xj}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}+\left(\mathbbm{1}\tr-d\right)F_{j}\;\;\text{for all}\;\;x,j\,,

where
\{F_{j}\}
are slack variables. Note that, without loss of generality, we can describe any traceless Hermitian matrix
G_{j}
in terms of a generic Hermitian matrix
F_{j}
as

G_{j}=\left(\mathbbm{1}\tr-d\right)F_{j}\,,

(43)

so we can simplify the dual to

\displaystyle{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\rho,\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)=

\displaystyle\min_{\{Y_{x}\},\,\{G_{j}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\tr\big( Y_x \, M_x \big)

(44)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle Y_{x}\geq\delta_{xj}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}+G_{j}\;\;\text{for all}\;\;x,j\,,

\displaystyle\tr G_{j}=0\;\;\text{for all}\;\;j\,.

If we set
Y_{x}=\alpha\mathbbm{1}
and
F_{j}=0
for all
x
and
j
, with
\alpha>1
, we obtain a feasible point for (
42
) that satisfies the strict constraint
Y_{x}>\delta_{xj}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}+\left(\mathbbm{1}\tr-d\right)F_{j}
for all
x
and
j
. Moreover, the variables
K_{x,j}=\frac{1}{d}M_{x}
are strictly positive for any POVM whose elements are all full-rank, and they satisfy the requirements for the primal SDP (
37
). From
(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994
, Conic Duality Theorem)
, if both dual and primal problems are strictly feasible, the duality gap for an optimal solution is zero, so for a full-rank POVM, we are free to use either the primal or dual formulations to solve our problem.

Finally, the complementary slackness condition (see e.g.
(
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
, Section 5.5.3)
) is a useful necessary condition for a set of primal and dual variables to be optimal. For our problem, the condition is

K_{x,j}\Big(Y_{x}-\delta_{xj}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}-G_{j}\Big)=0\quad\text{for all }\,\,x,j\,.

(45)

We will use this condition later to prove that a set of dual variables
\{Y_{x}\}
and
\{G_{j}\}
satisfy all of their necessary constraints.

Appendix B
Qubit measurements with two outcomes

B.1
Decomposition proof of Lemma
1

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=\{M_{1},M_{2}\}
be any qubit POVM with two outcomes, where
\tr M_{1}\leq\tr M_{2}
. The following lower bound holds,

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}\big)\geq 1-\tr M_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\Big)^{2}\,.

(46)

Proof.

We note that in any two-outcome measurement, the POVM elements must share an eigenbasis. We denote this basis by
\{\ket{x}\}
, and represent
M_{1}
by

M_{1}=m_{1}\outerproduct{1}{1}+m_{2}\outerproduct{2}{2}\,,

(47)

setting
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
.
For a fixed state
\ket{\phi}
, Eve’s optimal guessing probability can be bounded from below by taking any given decomposition of the POVM. Defining
p(x)=\langle\phi|M_{x}|\phi\rangle
, consider the decomposition

\displaystyle K_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,

(48)

\displaystyle K_{2,1}

\displaystyle=p(1)\mathbbm{1}-K_{1,1}=p(1)\mathbbm{1}-\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,

\displaystyle K_{1,2}

\displaystyle=M_{1}-K_{1,1}=M_{1}-\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,

\displaystyle K_{2,2}

\displaystyle=p(2)\mathbbm{1}-K_{1,2}=p(2)\mathbbm{1}-M_{1}+\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}\,.

The normalization condition is satisfied,

K_{1,1}+K_{2,1}=p(1)\mathbbm{1}\,,\qquad K_{1,2}+K_{2,2}=p(2)\mathbbm{1}\,,

(49)

while we also have

K_{1,1}+K_{1,2}=M_{1}\,,\qquad K_{2,1}+K_{2,2}=\mathbbm{1}-M_{1}=M_{2}\,.

(50)

All that’s left is to prove that each element is positive-semidefinite.
K_{1,1}
is rank-one, so it is positive-semidefinite because its trace is non-negative,

\tr K_{1,1}=\langle\phi|M_{1}|\phi\rangle=p(1)\,.

(51)

K_{2,1}
is rank-one and positive semi-definite because

\lmin\left(K_{2,1}\right)=\lmin\Big(p(1)\mathbbm{1}-K_{1,1}\Big)=p(1)-\tr K_{1,1}=0\,,

(52)

where
\lmin\left(.\right)
denotes the smallest eigenvalue of its argument. When
M_{1}
is rank-one,
K_{2,1}
is rank-one and positive semidefinite because

K_{1,2}=M_{1}-\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}\sqrt{M_{1}}=m_{1}\Big(1-\absolutevalue{\langle\phi| 1 \rangle}^{2}\Big)\outerproduct{1}{1}\geq 0\,,

(53)

while when
M_{1}
is full-rank,
K_{1,2}
has a zero eigenvector given by
M_{1}^{-1/2}\ket{\phi}
,

K_{1,2}\,M_{1}^{-1/2}\ket{\phi}=\Big(1-\langle\phi|\mathbbm{1}|\phi\rangle\Big)\sqrt{M_{1}}\ket{\phi}=0\,,

(54)

so it is positive-semidefinite because its trace is non-negative,

\tr K_{1,2}=\tr M_{1}-\langle\phi|M_{1}|\phi\rangle\geq 0\,.

(55)

Finally,
K_{2,2}
is positive-semidefinite because
K_{1,2}
is rank-one, so

\lmin\left(K_{2,2}\right)=\lmin\Big(p(2)\mathbbm{1}-\tr K_{1,2}\Big)=p(2)-\tr K_{1,2}=1-\tr M_{1}\geq 0\,.

(56)

We see that
K_{2,2}
is rank-one only in the special case
\tr M_{1}=1
. We represent
\ket{\phi}
in the eigenbasis
\{\ket{x}\}
of the POVM as
\ket{\phi}=\left(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\right)^{T}
, where we are free to choose real
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
(if they were complex, a unitary of the form
U=\sum_{k}e^{i\theta_{k}}\outerproduct{k}{k}
could be applied to make them real, leaving the POVM
{\mathcal{M}}
unchanged). From (
37
), the guessing probability is then bounded by

\displaystyle{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\langle{\phi|K_{1,1}+K_{2,2}|\phi}\rangle=\langle{\phi|M_{2}|\phi}\rangle-\langle{\phi|M_{1}|\phi}\rangle+2\langle{\phi|\sqrt{M_{1}}|\phi}\rangle^{2}

(57)

\displaystyle=1-2\langle{\phi|M_{1}|\phi}\rangle+2\langle{\phi|\sqrt{M_{1}}|\phi}\rangle^{2}=1-2\phi_{1}^{2}\left(1-\phi_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}\big(\sqrt{m_{1}}-\sqrt{m_{2}}\big)^{2}\,,

(58)

where in the last line we use
\phi_{2}^{2}=1-\phi_{1}^{2}
. The left-hand side of (
57
) is minimized when Alice chooses

\min_{\phi_{1}}\,\phi_{1}^{2}\left(1-\phi_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\,,

(59)

which gives the bound

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left({\mathcal{M}}\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\big(\sqrt{m_{1}}-\sqrt{m_{2}}\big)^{2}=1-\tr M_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)^{2}\,,

(60)

which is saturated for this decomposition if and only if
\phi_{1}^{2}=\phi_{2}^{2}=1/2
i.e. if
\ket{\phi}
is unbiased to the eigenbasis
\{\ket{x}\}
of the POVM, or in the trivial case where
M_{1}\propto\mathbbm{1}
, as
m_{1}=m_{2}
and Eve has perfect guessing probability.
∎

B.2
Vector proof of Lemma
1

Proof.

First, we prove by contradiction that Eve’s optimal decomposition of any POVM cannot contain a full-rank element
K_{a,b}
for
a\neq b
. If
K_{a,b}
were full-rank, we could define

\epsilon=\lmin\big(K_{a,b}\big)>0\,,

(61)

where
\lmin(.)
denotes the smallest eigenvalue of its argument. We could create a new decomposition with POVMs
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{j}=\{\tilde{K^{\prime}}_{x,j}\}_{x}
where all of the elements are unchanged apart from

\tilde{K^{\prime}}_{a,b}=K_{a,b}-\epsilon\mathbbm{1}\,,\qquad\tilde{K^{\prime}}_{a,a}=K_{a,a}+\epsilon\mathbbm{1}\,.

(62)

This decomposition satisfies all the necessary conditions,

\tilde{K^{\prime}}_{x,j}\geq 0\,,\qquad\sum_{j}\tilde{K^{\prime}}_{x,j}=M_{x}\,,\qquad\sum_{x}\tilde{K^{\prime}}_{x,b}=\sum_{x}K_{x,b}-\epsilon\mathbbm{1}\propto\mathbbm{1}\,,\qquad\sum_{x}\tilde{K^{\prime}}_{x,a}=\sum_{x}K_{x,a}+\epsilon\mathbbm{1}\propto\mathbbm{1}\,.

(63)

Eve would achieve a strictly higher guessing probability using
\{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{j}\}
, as

{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}},\,\{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{j}\}\big)=\bra{\phi}\Big(\sum_{j}K_{j,j}+\epsilon\mathbbm{1}\Big)\ket{\phi}={P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}},\,\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}\big)+\epsilon\,,

(64)

so the decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
must not have been optimal.

In the case of a qubit POVM with two outcomes, this result allows us to restrict to decompositions where the elements
K_{x,j}
are rank-one for
x\neq j
. We denote the POVM elements by
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, and assume throughout that
0<\tr M_{1}\leq\tr M_{2}
. Note that in any two-outcome measurement, the POVM elements must share an eigenbasis. We denote this basis by
\{\ket{x}\}
, and represent
M_{1}
by

M_{1}=m_{1}\outerproduct{1}{1}+m_{2}\outerproduct{2}{2}\,,

(65)

fixing
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
.
Choosing the conventions
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq 0
and
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq 0
, we can then define the decomposition

\displaystyle K_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\outerproduct{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1}+\lambda_{2}\outerproduct{\lambda_2}{\lambda_2}\,,

(66)

\displaystyle K_{2,1}

\displaystyle=\big(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\big)\outerproduct{\lambda_2}{\lambda_2}\,,

\displaystyle K_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\big(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\big)\outerproduct{\mu_2}{ \mu_2}\,,

\displaystyle K_{2,2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\outerproduct{\mu_1}{\mu_1}+\mu_{2}\outerproduct{\mu_2}{\mu_2}\,.

The constraints in (
37
) give us two conditions,

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\right)\outerproduct{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1}-\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\outerproduct{\mu_1}{\mu_1}+\left(\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\mathbbm{1}=\left(m_{1}-m_{2}\right)\outerproduct{m_1}{m_1}+m_{2}\mathbbm{1}\,,

(67)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}=1\,.

It is convenient to switch to Bloch vector notation, so we introduce vectors
\vec{r}_{a}
such that

\outerproduct{a}{a}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\vec{r}_{a}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)\,

(68)

for
a=\lambda_{1},\mu_{1},1
and
\phi
, where
\absolutevalue{\vecr_{a}}^{2}=1
,
\vec{\sigma}
is the vector of Pauli matrices and
\vec{r}_{\phi}
represents Alice’s chosen state. We can now rewrite Eve’s POVMs as

\displaystyle K_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}{2}\mathbbm{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}{2}\,\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}}\cdot\sigma\,,

(69)

\displaystyle K_{2,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}{2}\,\left(\mathbbm{1}-\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}}\cdot\sigma\right)\,,

\displaystyle K_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{2}\,\left(\mathbbm{1}-\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}\cdot\sigma\right)\,,

\displaystyle K_{2,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}{2}\mathbbm{1}+\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{2}\,\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}\cdot\sigma\,,

such that the optimal guessing probability is

\displaystyle{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}\big)=

\displaystyle\min_{\vec{r}_{\phi}}\;\max_{\{\lambda_{i},\,\mu_{i},\,\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}},\,\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}\}}

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}{2}+\Big(\frac{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}}+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}}{2}\Big)\cdot\vec{r}_{\phi}

(70)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq 0\,,\quad\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}=1\

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}=\tr M_{1}

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\right)\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}}-\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}=\left(m_{1}-m_{2}\right)\vec{r}_{1}\,.

Introducing the notation

a=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\,,\quad b=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\,,\quad z=m_{1}-m_{2}\,,

(71)

we can simplify (
70
) to

\displaystyle{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}\big)=

\displaystyle\min_{\vec{r}_{\phi}}\;\max_{a,\,b,\,\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}},\,\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}}

\displaystyle 1-\frac{a+b}{2}+\left(\frac{a\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}}+b\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}}{2}\right)\cdot\vec{r}_{\phi}

(72)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle a\geq 0\,,\quad b\geq 0\,,\quad\tr M_{1}\geq a+b

\displaystyle a\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}}-b\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}=z\vec{r}_{1}\,.

To recover (
70
) from (
72
), note that, since
\tr M_{1}\leq 1
, we have
a+b\leq\tr M_{1}\leq 1
, so we can define

\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tr M_{1}+a-b\Big)

(73)

such that

1-b\geq\lambda_{1}\geq a\,,

(74)

and from there we can define

\mu_{1}=1-\lambda_{1}\geq 0\,,\quad\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{1}-a\geq 0\,,\quad\mu_{2}=\mu_{1}-b\geq 0\,,

(75)

so the remaining conditions follow.

We can bound (
72
) from below by considering any decomposition by Eve that is valid for all
\vec{r}_{\phi}
. The case where Alice chooses
\vec{r}_{\phi}=\vec{r}_{1}
is trivial, as Eve could achieve perfect guessing probability by taking
a=z,b=0,\vec{r}_{\lambda_{1}}=\vec{r}_{1}
and arbitrary
\vec{r}_{\mu_{1}}
. For
\vec{r}_{\phi}\neq\vec{r}_{1}
, consider the decomposition

B.4
Proof of Corollary
1

Corollary 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=\{M_{x}\}_{x}
be any POVM with two outcomes, with
\lmin\left(M_{1}\right)+\lmax\left(M_{1}\right)\leq\lmin\left(M_{2}\right)+\lmax\left(M_{2}\right)
, where
\lmax\left(.\right)
and
\lmin\left(.\right)
denote the largest and smallest eigenvalues of the argument. Then

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}\big)\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\sqrt{\lmax\left(M_{1}\right)}-\sqrt{\lmin\left(M_{1}\right)}\bigg)^{2}\,.

(99)

Proof.

Consider a projector
\Pi
and some state
\ket{\phi}
that it stabilizes, i.e.
\Pi\ket{\phi}=\ket{\phi}
. The optimal guessing probability is bounded by

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}\big)\leq{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)={P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\Pi\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)\,.

(100)

Note that any variables
\mathcal{K}_{j}=\{K_{x,j}\}_{x}
that satisfy the constraints of the usual SDP (
37
) for
{\mathcal{M}}
will also satisfy

\displaystyle\Pi K_{x,j}\Pi\,\geq\,0\;\text{for all }\;x,j

(101)

\displaystyle\sum_{x}d\,\Pi K_{x,j}\Pi=\Pi\sum_{x}\tr K_{x,j}\;\text{for all}\;j

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\Pi K_{x,j}\Pi=\Pi M_{x}\Pi\;\text{ for all}\;x\,,

so we find

{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\Pi\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\big)\leq{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\Pi\ket{\phi},\,\Pi\,{\mathcal{M}}\,\Pi\big)\,,

(102)

where

P{\mathcal{M}}P=\{\Pi\,M_{x}\,\Pi\}_{x}\,.

(103)

For a fixed projector
\Pi
, we can choose
\ket{\phi}
such that

{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\Pi\ket{\phi},\,\Pi\,{\mathcal{M}}\,\Pi\big)={P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\Pi\,{\mathcal{M}}\,\Pi\big)\,,

(104)

so for any
\Pi
, we find

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}\big)\leq{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big(\Pi\,{\mathcal{M}}\,\Pi\big)\,,

(105)

Setting

P=\outerproduct{\lmax}{\lmax}\lmax+\outerproduct{\lmin}{\lmin}\lmin\,,

(106)

where
\ket{\lmax}
and
\ket{\lmin}
are the eigenvectors of
M_{1}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lmax\left(M_{1}\right)
and
\lmin\left(M_{1}\right)
, and using Lemma
2
, we recover (
99
).
∎

Numerical evidence suggests that the upper bound in Corollary
1
is saturated in general, but we have not proven this analytically. Instead, here we derive a
lower
bound, which is not tight in general, on the guessing probability of a two-outcome POVM
{\mathcal{M}}
using any state
\ket{\phi}
. We assume the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}
of
M_{1}
are arranged in non-decreasing order, such that
\lmax\left(M_{1}\right)=\lambda_{1}
and
\lmin\left(M_{1}\right)=\lambda_{d}
. Defining
p=\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d}}{2}
, we consider the decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
with
\mathcal{K}_{j}=\{K_{1,j},\,K_{2,j}\}
such that

\displaystyle K_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\diag\left(\min\{\lambda_{i},\,p\}_{i}\right)\,,

(107)

\displaystyle K_{2,1}

\displaystyle=\diag\left(\max\{p-\lambda_{i},\,0\}_{i}\right)\,,

\displaystyle K_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\diag\left(\max\{0,\,\lambda_{i}-p\}_{i}\right)\,,

\displaystyle K_{2,2}

\displaystyle=\diag\left(\min\{1-p,\,1-\lambda_{i}\}_{i}\right)\,,

where
\diag\left(.\right)
is a diagonal matrix in the computational basis with the arguments as eigenvalues. This decomposition is well-defined, as all of its elements are positive semidefinite by construction, and

\displaystyle K_{1,1}+K_{2,1}

\displaystyle=p\mathbbm{1}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle K_{1,2}+K_{2,2}=\left(1-p\right)\mathbbm{1}\,,

(108)

\displaystyle K_{1,1}+K_{1,2}

\displaystyle=M_{1}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle K_{2,1}+K_{2,2}=\mathbbm{1}-M_{1}=M_{2}\,.

Note that

K_{1,1}+K_{2,2}=\diag\left(\{1-\absolutevalue{p-\lambda_i}\}_{i}\right)\,.

(109)

For a given state
\ket{\phi}=\sum_{x}\phi_{x}\ket{x}
, where
\sum_{x}\phi_{x}^{2}=1
, we have the guessing probability

\displaystyle{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}},\,\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\langle\phi|K_{x,x}|\phi\rangle=\sum_{x}\phi_{x}^{2}\left(1-\absolutevalue{p-\lambda_x}\right)=1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\phi_{x}^{2}\,\absolutevalue{\lambda_1 + \lambda_d -2 \lambda_x }

(110)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\phi_{x}^{2}\,\absolutevalue{ \, \abs{\lambda_d - \lambda_x} - \abs{\lambda_1 - \lambda_x} \, }\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x}\phi_{x}^{2}\,\absolutevalue{ \lambda_d - \lambda_1 }=1-\frac{\lmax\left(M_{1}\right)-\lmin\left(M_{1}\right)}{2}\,.

(111)

This inequality is saturated only in the extreme cases where
\lmin\left(M_{1}\right)=0
or
\lmax\left(M_{1}\right)=1
. In both of these cases, Eve can achieve the upper bound of Corollary
1
by using the decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
for any state
\ket{\phi}
.

C.1
Full proof of Lemma

Lemma 3
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
be the noisy projective measurement in dimension
d
. The following lower bound holds,

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\big({\mathcal{M}}_{d}\big)\geq\frac{1}{d}\Big(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\Big)^{2}\,.

(112)

Proof.

We start by expressing Alice’s chosen state
\ket{\phi}
in the eigenbasis
\{\ket{x}\}
of the POVM as

\ket{\phi}=\sum_{x}\langle{x|\phi}\rangle\ket{x}\,,

(113)

where we assume for convenience that each term
\langle x|\phi\rangle
is real (if they were complex, a unitary of the form
U=\sum_{k}e^{i\theta_{k}}\outerproduct{k}{k}
could be applied to make them real, leaving the POVM
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
unchanged). We also define the terms

\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}=\sum_{k\neq x}\outerproduct{k}{k}\,,\qquad c_{\neq x}=\langle{\phi|\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}|\phi}\rangle\,,\qquad\ket{\phi_{\neq x}}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}\ket{\phi}}{\sqrt{c_{\neq x}}}\,.

(114)

The POVM
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
can be realized using the decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
,
\mathcal{K}_{j}=\{K_{x,j}\}_{x}
, with

K_{x,j}=\begin{cases}\outerproduct{\phi_x}{\phi_x}+\frac{c_{\neq x}\,{\varepsilon}}{d}\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\outerproduct{\phi_{\neq x}}{\phi_{\neq x}}\Big)\,,&x=j\,,\\
\outerproduct{\phi_{xj}}{\phi_{xj}}\,,&x\neq j\,,\\
\end{cases}

(115)

where
\ket{\phi_x}
and
\ket{\phi_{xj}}
are unnormalized pure states given by

\ket{\phi_x}=\sqrt{M_{x}}\ket{\phi}\,,\qquad\ket{\phi_{xj}}=\sqrt{M_{x}}\Big(\langle{\phi|j}\rangle\ket{x}-\langle{\phi|x}\rangle\ket{j}\Big)\,.

(116)

Each element
K_{x,j}
is positive-semidefinite, so to prove that this decomposition is valid, we need only show that the final two conditions of (
37
) hold,

\sum_{x}dK_{x,j}=\mathbbm{1}\sum_{x}\tr K_{x,j}\text{ for all }j\,,\qquad\sum_{j}K_{x,j}=M_{x}\text{ for all }x\,.

(117)

Defining

A=d-{\varepsilon}\left(d-1\right)\,,

(118)

we can expand
K_{x,x}
in the basis
\{\ket{x}\}
as

K_{x,x}=\,\frac{1}{d}\bigg(A\langle{\phi|x}\rangle^{2}\outerproduct{x}{x}+c_{\neq x}\,{\varepsilon}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}+\langle{\phi|x}\rangle\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}\,c_{\neq x}}\,\Big(\outerproduct{x}{\phi_{\neq x}}+\outerproduct{\phi_{\neq x}}{x}\Big)\bigg)\,,

(119)

while for
x\neq j
, we can expand
K_{x,j}
as

K_{x,j}=\,\frac{1}{d}\bigg(A\langle{\phi|j}\rangle^{2}\outerproduct{x}{x}+\langle{x|\phi}\rangle^{2}\,{\varepsilon}\,\outerproduct{j}{j}-\langle{x|\phi}\rangle\langle{j|\phi}\rangle\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}\,}\,\Big(\outerproduct{j}{x}+\outerproduct{x}{j}\Big)\bigg)\,.

(120)

The summation of (
120
) over
j\neq x
gives us

\sum_{j\neq x}K_{x,j}=\,\frac{1}{d}\bigg(A\,c_{\neq x}\outerproduct{x}{x}+\langle{x|\phi}\rangle^{2}\,{\varepsilon}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\langle{x|\phi}\rangle\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}\,c_{\neq x}}\Big(\outerproduct{\phi_{\neq x}}{x}+\outerproduct{x}{\phi_{\neq x}}\Big)\bigg)\,,

(121)

while the summation over
x\neq j
gives us

\sum_{x\neq j}K_{x,j}=\,\frac{1}{d}\bigg(\langle{\phi|j}\rangle^{2}A\mathbbm{1}_{\neq j}+c_{\neq j}\,{\varepsilon}\,\outerproduct{j}{j}-\langle{j|\phi}\rangle\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}\,c_{\neq j}}\,\Big(\outerproduct{\phi_{\neq j}}{j}+\outerproduct{j}{\phi_{\neq j}}\Big)\bigg)\,.

(122)

Noting that
\langle{x|\phi}\rangle^{2}+c_{\neq x}=1
, the total sum of the elements over
j
is

\sum_{j}K_{x,j}=\frac{1}{d}\bigg(A\outerproduct{x}{x}+{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}\bigg)=M_{x}\,,

(123)

while the total sum of the elements over
x
is

\sum_{x}K_{x,j}=\frac{1}{d}\Big(A\langle{\phi|j}\rangle^{2}+c_{\neq j}\,{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big(\outerproduct{j}{j}+\mathbbm{1}_{\neq j}\Big)\propto\mathbbm{1}\,.

(124)

The conditions (
120
) are satisfied, so the decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
from (
115
) is valid for any state
\ket{\phi}
.

We can bound the guessing probability for any state
\ket{\phi}
using this decomposition,

{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left(\ket{\phi},\,{\mathcal{M}}\right)\geq\sum_{x}\langle{\phi|K_{x,x}|\phi}\rangle=\sum_{x}\langle{\phi|\sqrt{M_{x}}|\phi}\rangle^{2}\,.

(125)

Note that

\langle{\phi|\sqrt{M_{x}}|\phi}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bigg(\sqrt{A}\langle{\phi|x}\rangle^{2}+\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\,c_{\neq x}\bigg)\,,

(126)

such that

\sum_{x}\langle{\phi|\sqrt{M_{x}}|\phi}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bigg(\sqrt{A}+(d-1)\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\bigg)=\tr\sqrt{M_{x}}\,.

(127)

To quantify the deviation of the state
\ket{\phi}
from the state
\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(1,...,1\right)^{T}
which is unbiased to the basis
\{\ket{x}\}
, we introduce the real parameters

\delta_{x}=\langle{\phi|\sqrt{M_{x}}|\phi}\rangle-\frac{\tr\sqrt{M_{x}}}{d}\,,

(128)

where we must have

\sum_{x}{\delta_{x}}=0\,.

(129)

We then find

(130)

where in the second equality, we use the fact that the
\tr\sqrt{M_{x}}
terms are equal. Since
\sum_{x}\delta_{x}^{2}
is non-negative, Eve will always achieve at least

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left({\mathcal{M}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{d}\left(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)^{2}\,,

(131)

and she can achieve strictly more when there are some nonzero parameters
\delta_{x}
. Since

\delta_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\Big(\langle{\phi|x}\rangle^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\Big)\,\bigg(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\,,

(132)

when
\langle{\phi|x}\rangle^{2}\neq\frac{1}{d}
for some

, i.e. when
\ket{\phi}
is not unbiased to the measurement basis
\{\ket{x}\}
, Eve’s guessing probability is bounded strictly from below by

{P_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left(\ket{\phi},{\mathcal{M}}\right)>\frac{1}{d}\left(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)^{2}\,.

(133)

{\mathcal{M}}_{d}

G_{j}
must also be traceless. We also have

\tr\left(M_{x}T_{x}\right)=\frac{1}{d}\Big(A\langle{x|T_{x}|x}\rangle+{\varepsilon}\sum_{k\neq x}\langle{k|T_{x}|k}\rangle\Big)=0\quad\textnormal{for all}\;\,x\,,

(141)

such that

\sum_{x}\tr\left(Y_{x}M_{x}\right)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)^{2}\,.

(142)

There just remains the more formidable task of showing that

Y_{x}-\delta_{xj}\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}-G_{j}\geq 0

(143)

for all
x
and
j
. To do this, we first bound the rank of the left-hand side of (
143
) by proving that the complementary slackness condition

K_{x,j}\big(Y_{x}-\delta_{xj}\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}-G_{j}\big)=0\quad\textnormal{for all}\;\;x,\,j\,

(144)

holds for the decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
from (
136
) and the dual variables from (
140
). When
x=j
, we find

\displaystyle K_{x,x}\big(Y_{x}-\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}-G_{x}\big)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{M_{x}}\ket{\psi}\bigg(\left(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}-\frac{1}{d}\langle{\psi|\sqrt{M_{x}}|\psi}\rangle\right)\bra{\psi}+\alpha\Big(\bra{\psi}\sqrt{M_{x}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\bra{\psi}\sqrt{M_{x}}\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi_{\neq x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;-\beta\Big(1-d\langle{\psi|M_{x}|\psi}\rangle\Big)\bra{\psi}\sqrt{M_{x}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+{\varepsilon}\frac{d-1}{d}\bigg(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}\,M_{x}^{-1/2}-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi_{\neq x}}M_{x}^{-1/2}+\alpha\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi_{\neq x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;-\beta\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi_{\neq x}}-d\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}\sqrt{M_{x}}\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\sqrt{M_{x}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;\,+d\langle{\psi_{\neq x}|\sqrt{M_{x}}|\psi}\rangle\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi}\sqrt{M_{x}}\,\Big)\bigg)

(145)

\displaystyle={\varepsilon}\,\frac{d-1}{d}\bigg(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{{\varepsilon}}}+\alpha-\beta\bigg)\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi_{\neq x}}\Big)=0\,,

(146)

where we use the properties

\langle{\psi|\sqrt{M_{x}}|\psi}\rangle=\frac{1}{d}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\,,\qquad\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}\sqrt{M_{1}}=\sqrt{\frac{{\varepsilon}}{d}}\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}\,,\qquad\langle{\psi|M_{x}|\psi}\rangle=\frac{1}{d}\,,\qquad\alpha-\beta=-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{{\varepsilon}}}\,.

(147)

Note that

\bra{\psi}\sqrt{M_{x}}\,\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi_{\neq x}}\Big)=\frac{\sqrt{{\varepsilon}\left(d-1\right)}}{d}\bra{\psi_{\neq x}}\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x}-\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x}}{\psi_{\neq x}}\Big)=0\,,

(148)

so the term
K_{x,x}
has rank
d-1
. Since

K_{x,x}\big(Y_{x}-\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}-G_{x}\big)=\big(Y_{x}-\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}-G_{x}\big)K_{x,x}=0\,,

(149)

we learn that
Y_{x}-\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}-G_{x}
has rank one at most. To prove that it’s positive semidefinite, we need only show that its trace is non-negative,

\tr\big(Y_x - \ketbra{\psi}{\psi} - G_x \big)=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\,\tr\left({M_{x}}^{-1/2}\right)-1=\frac{d-1}{d^{2}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}}}\geq 0\,.

(150)

We proceed similarly to show that
Y_{x}-G_{j}
is positive semidefinite for
x\neq j
. We can expand it as

Y_{x}-G_{j}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\,{M_{x}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+T_{x}-T_{j}+\frac{d-1}{d}\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}+\alpha\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\neq j}-\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq j}}{\psi_{\neq j}}\big)-\beta\big(\mathbbm{1}-d\sqrt{M_{j}}\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\sqrt{M_{j}}\big)\,,

(151)

where

T_{x}-T_{j}=-\gamma\sqrt{\frac{d-2}{d-1}}\Big(\big(\ket{x}-\ket{j}\big)\bra{\psi_{\neq x, j}}+\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}}\big(\bra{x}-\bra{j}\big)\Big)\,.

(152)

Defining the unnormalized state

\ket{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}:=\frac{d}{\sqrt{A+{\varepsilon}}}\ket{\psi_{xj}}\,,

(153)

we again prove the complementary slackness condition (
45
),

K_{x,j}\big(Y_{x}-G_{j}\big)=\outerproduct{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}\big(Y_{x}-G_{j}\big)=0\,.

(154)

For convenience, however, we do this component by component, proving separately that

\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}Y_{x}-G_{j}\ket{x}=\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}Y_{x}-G_{j}\ket{j}=\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}Y_{x}-G_{j}\ket{k}=0\,,

(155)

where
k\neq x,j
. First we have

\displaystyle\langle{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}\bigg(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{A}}\ket{x}-\gamma\sqrt{\frac{d-2}{d-1}}\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}}+\frac{d-1}{d\sqrt{d}}\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;\;\;+\alpha\Big(\ket{x}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\ket{\psi_{\neq j}}\Big)-\beta\big(\ket{x}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{M_{j}}\ket{\psi}\big)\bigg)

(156)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}+\frac{d-1}{d^{2}\sqrt{d}}\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)+\alpha\sqrt{\frac{A}{d}}\frac{d-2}{d-1}-\beta\sqrt{\frac{A}{d}}

(157)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}-\frac{1}{d^{2}\sqrt{d}}\Big(\sqrt{A}+\left(d-1\right)\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\Big)=0\,.

(158)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\,\langle{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}\bigg(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{{\varepsilon}}}\ket{j}+\gamma\sqrt{\frac{d-2}{d-1}}\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;\;\;+\frac{d-1}{d\sqrt{d}}\ket{\psi}-\beta\big(\ket{j}-\sqrt{A}\sqrt{M_{j}}\ket{\psi}\big)\bigg)

(159)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}+\frac{d-1}{d^{2}\sqrt{d}}\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)+\beta\sqrt{\frac{{\varepsilon}}{d}}

(160)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d-1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}+\frac{d-1}{d^{2}\sqrt{d}}\Big(\sqrt{A}+\left(d-1\right)\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\Big)=0\,,

(161)

and finally,

\displaystyle\langle{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\bra{\tilde{\psi}_{xj}}\bigg(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{{\varepsilon}}}\ket{k}-\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d-1}}\big(\ket{x}-\ket{j}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;\;\;+\frac{d-1}{d\sqrt{d}}\ket{\psi}+\alpha\Big(\ket{k}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\ket{\psi_{\neq j}}\Big)-\beta\big(\ket{k}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{M_{j}}\ket{\psi}\big)\bigg)

(162)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}\big(\sqrt{A}+\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\big)+\frac{d-1}{d^{2}\sqrt{d}}\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)-\frac{\alpha}{d-1}\sqrt{\frac{A}{d}}

(163)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}-\frac{1}{d^{2}\sqrt{d}}\Big(\sqrt{A}+\left(d-1\right)\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\Big)=0\,

(164)

for any
k\neq x,j
. Since (
155
) holds, we find that
\ket{\psi_{xj}}
is a zero eigenstate of
Y_{x}-G_{j}
. Now consider the projector
\Pi_{\neq x,j}
defined by

\Pi_{\neq x,j}=\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x,j}-\outerproduct{\psi_{\neq x, j}}{\psi_{\neq x, j}}\,,\qquad\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-2}}\sum_{k\neq x,j}\ket{k}\,,

(165)

which has rank
d-3
and is orthogonal to the state
\ket{\psi_{xj}}
. We have

\Pi_{\neq x,j}\big(Y_{x}-G_{j}\big)=\Pi_{\neq x,j}\Big(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{{\varepsilon}}}+\alpha-\beta\Big)=0\,,

(166)

where we used

\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x,j}\ket{\psi}=\sqrt{\frac{d-2}{d}}\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}}\,,\qquad\mathbbm{1}_{\neq x,j}\ket{\psi_{\neq j}}=\sqrt{\frac{d-2}{d-1}}\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}}\,.

(167)

Since it is orthogonal to both the projectors
\outerproduct{\psi_{xj}}{\psi_{xj}}
and
\Pi_{\neq x,j}
, we find that
Y_{x}-G_{j}
has rank two at most. As such, since it is Hermitian,

Y_{x}-G_{j}
is positive-semidefinite if its determinant is non-negative. To find this determinant, we can express
Y_{x}-G_{j}
in any two-vector orthonormal basis that lives in its support. Such a basis is given by the pair of vectors
\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}}
and
\ket{\psi_{xj}^{\perp}}
, where

\ket{\psi_{xj}^{\perp}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A+{\varepsilon}}}\left({\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}}\ket{x}+\sqrt{A}\ket{j}\right)\,.

(168)

In this basis, then we can write

Y_{x}-G_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\psi_{\neq x,j}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{\neq x,j}\rangle&\langle{\psi_{\neq x,j}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}}\rangle\vskip 8.5359pt\\
\,\,\,\,\langle{\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{\neq x,j}}\rangle&\;\;\,\langle{\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}}\rangle\end{pmatrix}_{\ket{\psi_{\neq x, j}},\,\ket{\psi^{\perp}_{x, j}}}\,.

(169)

The matrix coefficients are

\displaystyle\langle{\psi_{\neq x,j}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{\neq x,j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{d-2}{d^{3}}\left(d(d-1+{\varepsilon})+\frac{\left(A+{\varepsilon}\right)\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)}{\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}}\right)\,

(170)

\displaystyle\langle{\psi_{\neq x,j}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{d}\,\sqrt{d-2}\,\sqrt{A+{\varepsilon}}\,\left(1+{\varepsilon}\right)}{d^{2}\left(\sqrt{A}+\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}}\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\,

\displaystyle\langle{\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{A+{\varepsilon}}{d^{2}\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}}}\left(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)^{2}\,,

so the determinant is then

\displaystyle\textnormal{det}\left(Y_{x}-G_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\langle{\psi_{\neq x,j}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{\neq x,j}}\rangle\langle{\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}}\rangle-\langle{\psi_{\neq x,j}|Y_{x}-G_{j}|\psi_{x,j}^{\perp}}\rangle^{2}

(171)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(d-2\right)\left(A+{\varepsilon}\right)\,\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}\,\left(1+\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}}{d^{2}\sqrt{A{\varepsilon}}\,\left(\sqrt{A}+\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\right)^{4}}\,,

(172)

which is always non-negative. We have proven then that the variables
\{Y_{x}\}
and
\{G_{j}\}
given in (
140
) are valid, so from (
142
), the inequality (
135
) holds, proving Lemma
4
.
∎

C.3
Proof of Lemma
4
based on permutations

Proof.

We prove that the decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
from (
115
) in Appendix
C.2
is optimal for the unbiased state
\ket{\psi}
. First, note that because all of the elements of
\ket{\psi}
are real when expressed in the computational basis, we are free to assume that all the coefficients of
K_{x,x}
are real in this basis too, as the imaginary parts do not contribute to the guessing probability.
From now on, we assume that
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
has only real elements. We denote by
\sigma\left(.\right)
a function that permutes elements in
x\in 1,...,d
, and we denote the corresponding permutation matrix in the basis
\{\ket{x}\}
by
\Pi_{\sigma}
. Notice that the composition of permutations
\sigma_{a}\circ\sigma_{b}\left(.\right)
corresponds to the matrix product
\Pi_{\sigma_{a}\circ\sigma_{b}}=\Pi_{\sigma_{a}}\Pi_{\sigma_{b}}
. Starting with
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}
, we can define a new decomposition into POVMs
\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{j}=\{K^{\sigma}_{x,j}\}_{x}
by averaging over all
d!
possible permutations of the elements
K_{x,j}
,

K^{\sigma}_{x,j}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma}\Pi_{\sigma}^{T}\,K_{\sigma\left(x\right),\,\sigma\left(j\right)}\,\Pi_{\sigma}\,.

(173)

This decomposition is valid, as it satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{x}K^{\sigma}_{x,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma}\Pi^{T}_{\sigma}\left(\sum_{x}K_{\sigma\left(x\right),\,\sigma\left(j\right)}\right)\Pi_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma}\Pi^{T}_{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x}\tr K_{\sigma\left(x\right),\,\sigma\left(j\right)}\mathbbm{1}\right)\Pi_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{d}\tr K^{\sigma}_{\sigma x,\,j}\mathbbm{1}\,,

(174)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}K^{\sigma}_{x,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma}\Pi^{T}_{\sigma}\left(\sum_{j}K_{\sigma\left(x\right),\,\sigma\left(j\right)}\right)\Pi_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma}\Pi^{T}_{\sigma}M_{\sigma\left(x\right)}\Pi_{\sigma}=M_{x}\,,

(175)

where the final equality uses the fact that, since
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
is diagonal in the basis
\{\ket{x}\}
, we have
\Pi^{T}_{\sigma}M_{\sigma\left(x\right)}\Pi_{\sigma}=M_{x}
for any
x
and any permutation
\sigma
. Note that the trace of each
K_{x,j}
is symmetric under the interchange of
x
and
j

\sum_{x}\tr K^{\sigma}_{x,j}=\sum_{j}\tr K^{\sigma}_{x,j}=\tr M_{x}=1\,.

(176)

The new decomposition
\{\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{j}\}

\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}

\ket{\psi}

(177)

so
\{\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{j}\}
is an optimal decomposition. From this point on, we drop the superscript
\sigma

). We have the property that, for any permutation

(178)

\Pi_{\sigma}\Pi_{\sigma_{a}}=\Pi_{\sigma\circ\sigma_{a}}
. In particular, if we denote by
\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\left[K_{x,x}\right]_{k,k}

\displaystyle=\left[K_{x,x}\right]_{m,m}

\displaystyle\textnormal{for all}\;\;k,m\neq x\,,

Appendix D
Other entropies

We prove in Theorem

{P^{*}_{\textnormal{guess}}}\left({\mathcal{M}}_{d}\right)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\tr\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)^{2}\,.

and

are equivalent when quantified by a conditional entropy

than the min-entropy. Following
Meng et al. 2024

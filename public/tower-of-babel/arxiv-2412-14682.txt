Introducing irrational enumeration: analytic combinatorics for objects of irrational size
David Bevan †, Julien Condé

Abstract
We extend the scope of analytic combinatorics to classes containing objects that have irrational sizes. The generating function for such a class is a power series that admits irrational exponents (which we call a Ribenboim series). A transformation then yields a generalised Dirichlet series from which the asymptotics of the coefficients can be extracted by singularity analysis using an appropriate Tauberian theorem. In practice, the asymptotics can often be determined directly from the original generating function. We illustrate the technique with a variety of applications, including tilings with tiles of irrational area, ordered integer factorizations, lattice walks enumerated by Euclidean length, and plane trees with vertices of irrational size. We also explore phase transitions in the asymptotics of families of irrational combinatorial classes.

Formally, a Ribenboim series in

Asymptotics of irrational classes

In general, the coefficients in an
IGF
can fluctuate wildly.
For example, here are seven consecutive terms in the
GF

\mathcal{T}^{(\sqrt{2})}

8\,z^{1+7\sqrt{2}},\quad\;z^{11},\quad\;126\,z^{4+5\sqrt{2}},\quad\;120\,z^{7+3\sqrt{2}},\quad\;z^{8\sqrt{2}},\quad\;11\,z^{10+\sqrt{2}},\quad\;84\,z^{3+6\sqrt{2}}.

Thus it makes no sense to consider the asymptotics of the coefficients directly.

Instead, we consider
the number of objects in an irrational class of size at most a given value, which may exhibit
smooth asymptotic behaviour.
Given a class
\mathcal{C}
and any
x\geqslant 0
, we use
\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant x}
to denote the set
\{\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}:|\mathfrak{c}|\leqslant x\}
of objects in
\mathcal{C}
of size at most
x
.
Note that the structure required of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}

is sufficient to ensure that each of these sets is finite.
Similarly, if
f(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}z^{\lambda}
is a Ribenboim series, then

\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{\lambda\leqslant x}c_{\lambda}

extracts the sum of the coefficients of those terms of the series with exponent at most
x
.
Thus,
\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}|\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant x}|
.

Analytically, a Ribenboim series
has a radius of convergence in

[0,\infty]
and is analytic within its disk of convergence, except on the non-positive real axis, where there is a branch cut.
Moreover, if it has nonnegative coefficients and a finite radius of convergence
\rho
, then it has a singularity at the point
z=\rho
.
See Proposition
10
.

In order to specify an additional condition that must be satisfied by an irrational class for our main theorem to apply, we introduce a transform that we apply to
IGF

Primitive irrationality

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is an irrational combinatorial class whose

\rho\in(0,1)

Then we say that

is

is the unique singularity of its

.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a primitive irrational combinatorial class.
If its irrational generating function

has positive radius of convergence

f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}g(z)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{h(z)}{(1-z/\rho)^{\alpha}},

where
\alpha\notin-\mathbb{N}

h
are analytic on the cut disk

|\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\;\sim\;\frac{h(\rho)}{\log(1/\rho)\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}.

Note that the shape required of an irrational generating function in Theorem
1
is not sufficient for a class to be primitive.
The unique dominant singularity of an

In contrast with the smooth asymptotics of primitive classes,
irrational classes that are

primitive may exhibit periodic oscillations in their asymptotics.
An example of such a class is analysed in Section

Sufficient conditions for primitive irrationality

In order not to have to establish individually the primitive irrationality of each class in which we are interested,
it is useful to have conditions which guarantee that a class is primitive.
The following simple condition (Proposition
2
) is of particular use.

To state it, in an analogous manner to our definition of an irrational class,
we say that a set
S
of real numbers is
irrational
if there is no
\omega\neq 0
such that
S\subseteq\omega\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular, this holds if there exist
x,y\in S
such that
x/y\notin\mathbb{Q}
.

If every singularity
s
on the line of convergence
of the
DGF
of a class
\mathcal{C}

is a root of
an equation of the form

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\hskip 0.70007pte^{-\gamma s}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}1,

where
\Gamma
is an irrational set of positive numbers and each
c_{\gamma}
is
also
positive,
then
\mathcal{C}
is primitive.

For example, the
DGF
for the class
\mathcal{T}
of strip tilings,

F_{\mathcal{T}}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}e^{-s}\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}e^{-\beta s}},

satisfies this requirement if
\beta\notin\mathbb{Q}
.
Hence
\mathcal{T}
is primitive if
\beta
is irrational, and by Theorem
1
we have

|\mathcal{T}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{1}{\big(\rho+\beta\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{\beta}\big)\log(1/\rho)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{\rho^{-x}}{H(\rho)},

where
\rho
is the unique positive root of

1-z-z^{\beta}
,
and

H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)
,
the latter simplification resulting from the identities

\rho^{\beta}=1-\rho

and
\beta\log\rho=\log(1-\rho)
,
exhibiting a mysterious connection to entropy.

Context

The analytic approach to combinatorial enumeration,
expounded and popularised by Flajolet and Sedgewick
[
7
]
,
has seen significant recent growth, as evidenced by the publication of several new graduate textbooks taking varied perspectives
[
22
,
23
,
26
]
.

However, we are aware of only one work that considers irrational classes, and that addresses a rather different question:
Garrabrant and Pak
[
8
]
(see also Melczer
[
22
, Section 3.4.2]
)
considers the tiling of a strip of length
n+\varepsilon
with a finite set of irrational length tiles with adjacency constraints,
for some fixed
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Their main result is that the ordinary generating functions for these tilings are precisely the diagonals of multivariate
\mathbb{N}
-rational functions.
As far as we know, Ribenboim sequences have not previously been used for enumeration, although Richard Stanley
made use of polynomials with irrational exponents in
[
32
]
.

The idea of extending the scope of analytic combinatorics to irrational classes was first presented in a MathOverflow question and at a Banff workshop in 2016
[
1
,
2
]
.
The main result in this paper originally appeared in the Master’s thesis of the second author
[
3
]
.
The authors are particularly grateful to Andrew Elvey Price for discussions and advice, and also to two anonymous referees whose feedback helped us to improve the presentation of this work.

Theorem
1
only gives the first-order asymptotics of primitive irrational classes.
With more detailed analysis it ought to be possible to calculate further terms, analogous to the result of Flajolet and Odlyzko
[
6
]
that
the coefficient of
z^{n}
in
(1-z/\rho)^{-\alpha}
admits the asymptotic expansion

\big[z^{n}\big]\hskip 0.70007pt\frac{1}{(1-z/\rho)^{\alpha}}\;\sim\;\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-n}\hskip 0.70007ptn^{\alpha-1}\hskip 0.70007pt\bigg(\!1\hskip 0.70007pt+\hskip 0.70007pt\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e_{k}}{n^{k}}\bigg),

(3)

where
e_{k}
is a specific polynomial in
\alpha
of degree
2k
.
Another direction to explore would be the establishment of central limit theorems (for example, see
[
13
,
14
]
).
A suitable limit theorem may be of help in determining the asymptotic enumeration of irrational tilings of a square (see Section
2.2.9
).

2.1
Establishing primitive irrationality

In this section, we present three propositions that can be used to establish that a class is primitive.
We begin with a very simple condition ensuring primitive irrationality, that is of much wider applicability than might be expected.
Its proof is elementary.

Proposition 2
.

If every singularity
s
on the line of convergence of the Dirichlet generating function of a combinatorial class
\mathcal{C}

is a root of
an equation of the form

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\hskip 0.70007pte^{-\gamma s}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}1,

(4)

where
\Gamma
is an irrational set of positive numbers and each
c_{\gamma}
is
also
positive,
then
\mathcal{C}
is primitive.

Proof.

Let
\mu+i\mathbb{R}
be the line of convergence of the
DGF
.
Since the
DGF
has positive coefficients,
\mu
is a singularity (see Proposition
9
).

Note that, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\re e^{a+bi}\mathchoice{\;\leqslant\;}{\leqslant}{\leqslant}{\leqslant}e^{a}
, with equality only if
b\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
.

Thus,
\mu+ti
is a root of (
4
)
only if, for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have

t\gamma\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
.
If
t\neq 0
, then this implies that
\frac{t}{2\pi}\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, so
\Gamma
is not irrational.
∎

Note that

does not need to be finite. The sum may be any irrational Dirichlet series with positive coefficients.

Although Proposition

is stated in terms of

the simplicity of the exponential transform means that
a class can often be seen to be primitive
by inspecting its

For example, suppose

Then the

Proposition 3

If

However, when the dominant singularity satisfies an equation of the form

.

Every singularity
s
on the line of convergence of the
DGF
satisfies the equation

1\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{G(s)}{\big(1-H(s)\big)^{k}}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}G(s)\hskip 0.70007pt\big(1+H(s)+H(s)^{2}+\ldots\big)^{k}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}b_{\lambda}\hskip 0.70007pte^{-\lambda s},

for certain
b_{\lambda}
, all positive, where

\Lambda\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big\{\gamma+\beta:\gamma\in\Gamma,\,\beta\in\mathrm{span}(\Delta)\big\},

where we use
\mathrm{span}(\Delta)
to denote the set of
finite
\mathbb{N}
-linear combinations of elements of
\Delta
.

The set
\Lambda
is irrational, since
\Gamma\cup\Delta
is.
Thus the conditions of Proposition
2
are satisfied and
\mathcal{C}
is primitive.
∎

2.2
Tiling and packing

In this section, we illustrate the scope of our approach by exploring the asymptotic enumeration of a variety of types of tiling and packing,
including the establishment of a general result concerning the primitive irrationality of tilings with adjacency constraints.
We also look at
the structure of a typical tiling,
at irrational tilings which are not primitive,
and at how an irrational class of tilings relates to a classical number-theoretic problem concerning integer factorizations.
We conclude with a natural open question that seems to require some additional ideas.

2.2.1
Strips of width 1

Generalising the example from the introduction,
given
an
irrational set of tile lengths
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{>0}
,
let
\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}
be the class of tilings of a strip of width one
with tiles of dimensions
\{\gamma\times 1:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
, the size of a tiling being the length of the tiled strip.
This class has
IGF

f_{\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}z^{\gamma}},

and is easily seen to be primitive by Proposition
2
.
Applying Theorem
1
then yields

\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{1}{\log(1/\rho)\hskip 0.70007pt\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{\gamma}}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x},

(5)

where
\rho
is the unique positive root of

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}z^{\gamma}=1
.

A tiling with a final partial tile of a strip of length
\pi^{2}
, using square tiles and tiles of length
\sqrt{2}

2.2.2
Tilings with a final partial tile

The set
\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}

consists of
partial
tilings of a strip of length
x
, where each tiling
\mathfrak{t}
has a gap of length
x-|\mathfrak{t}|
(which may be zero) at the end.
It seems somewhat more natural to consider tilings in which the final tile (only) may be a
partial tile
, a tile which may have any length less than the greatest tile length in
\Gamma
.
See Figure
3
for an illustration.
(We consider a two-dimensional variant below in Section
2.2.9
.)

Note that a tiling with a possible final partial tile may have
any
nonnegative real size.
Thus sets of such tilings do not themselves have Ribenboim series
IGF
s.

Suppose
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}<\ldots<\gamma_{k}\}
is finite.
There are two possibilities to consider.
Either tiles can only be distinguished by their length, or else they are distinguishable in some other way (for example, by colour).

Let
\mathcal{U}^{\Gamma}
be the set of tilings with a possible final partial tile using tiles indistinguishable except by their length.
Then, since a partial tile can have any length up to
\gamma_{k}
, for any
x\geqslant\gamma_{k}
we have

\big|\mathcal{U}^{\Gamma}_{x}\big|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x-\gamma_{k}}\big|,\qquad\text{and thus}\qquad\big|\mathcal{U}^{\Gamma}_{x}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\big(1-\rho^{\gamma_{k}}\big)\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|.

In contrast, let
\mathcal{V}^{\Gamma}
be the set of tilings with a possible final partial tile using tiles distinguishable by colour.
The number of possible colours for a final partial tile depends on its length, since a partial tile of colour
i
must have length less than
\gamma_{i}
.
Thus for any
x\geqslant\gamma_{k}
we have

\big|\mathcal{V}^{\Gamma}_{x}\big|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(k+1-i)\left(\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x-\gamma_{i-1}}\big|-\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x-\gamma_{i}}\big|\right)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}k\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x-\gamma_{i}}\big|,

where
\gamma_{0}=0
.
Here, the factor
k+1-i
is the number distinct tiles which are longer than the space available for the partial tile, which has length at least
\gamma_{i-1}
but less than
\gamma_{i}
.
Only tiles that are too long can be cut down to form a partial tile.

Since
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\rho^{\gamma}=1
, after simplification, we have

\big|\mathcal{V}^{\Gamma}_{x}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\big(|\Gamma|-1\big)\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|,

a result which begs for a more direct combinatorial explanation.
Indeed, we have the following identity:

Proposition 5
.

The number of coloured tilings with a possible final partial tile of a strip of length
x
satisfies

\big|\mathcal{V}^{\Gamma}_{x}\big|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big(|\Gamma|-1\big)\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{<x}\big|\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}1.

Proof.

Each tiling in
\mathcal{V}^{\Gamma}_{x}
can be created uniquely from a strict partial tiling
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{<x}
by extending it with final nonempty run of tiles of the same colour,
where this colour differs from the colour of the last tile in
\mathfrak{t}
.
There are
|\Gamma|-1
choices for this colour, unless
\mathfrak{t}
is empty in which case any of the colours may be chosen.
Note that we need
|\mathfrak{t}|<x
since we need space for the final monochrome run of tiles.
∎

\pi^{2}
, using square tiles and tiles of length
\sqrt{2}

2.2.3
Maximal packings

Complementary to these tilings, are
maximal packings
of a strip,
\mathcal{P}^{\Gamma}
.
A maximal packing of a strip of length
x
consists of a partial tiling of the strip having length greater than
x-\gamma_{1}
so it can’t be extended to a longer partial tiling.
See Figure
4
for an illustration.
Hence,

\big|\mathcal{P}^{\Gamma}_{x}\big|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x-\gamma_{1}}\big|,\qquad\text{and thus}\qquad\big|\mathcal{P}^{\Gamma}_{x}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\big(1-\rho^{\gamma_{1}}\big)\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|.

2.2.4
The structure of a typical tiling

The distribution of the value of a parameter can be extracted from a bivariate
IGF
in the normal way (see
[
7
, Proposition III.2]
).
Specifically, if
f(z,u)
is the bivariate
IGF
of a class
\mathcal{C}
in which
u
marks the value of some parameter
\upsilon:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{R}
(which could take any real values), then the mean value of
\upsilon
over objects of size at most
x
is given by

\mathbb{E}_{\leqslant x}\big[\upsilon\big]\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f_{u}(z,1)}{\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f(z,1)}.

(6)

Higher moments and concentration results can be established by taking higher derivatives.

Given a tiling
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}
and some
\beta\in\Gamma
, let
L_{\beta}(\mathfrak{t})
be the sum of the lengths of the
\beta
-length tiles in
\mathfrak{t}
. Then, the bivariate

IGF
in which
u
marks
L_{\beta}
is

f(z,u)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1-u^{\beta}\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\beta}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{\beta\}}z^{\gamma}},

and

f_{u}(z,1)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{\beta\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\beta}}{\big(1\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}z^{\gamma}\big)^{2}}.

By Proposition
2
, this is
primitive.
Using (
6
), and applying Theorem
1
then yields the asymptotic expectation for
L_{\beta}
:

\mathbb{E}_{\leqslant x}\big[L_{\beta}\big]\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{\beta\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{\beta}}{\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{\gamma}}\hskip 0.70007ptx.

Therefore,
the asymptotic ratio of the contribution of
\alpha
-length and
\beta
-length tiles to a tiling equals
{\alpha\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{\alpha}}/{\beta\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{\beta}}
.

If
f(z,u)
is the bivariate
IGF
of a primitive class, in which
u
marks the value of a parameter
\upsilon
, then
it is not immediately obvious that
\mathbb{E}_{\leqslant x}\big[\upsilon\big]

gives information about the structure of
large
objects in the class.
However,
if the class grows exponentially, and
\mathbb{E}_{\leqslant x}\big[\upsilon\big]
is polynomial in
x
, then it is easy to check that for any
c>0
we have

\mathbb{E}_{\leqslant x}\big[\upsilon\big]\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f_{u}(z,1)-\big[z^{\leqslant x-c}\big]f_{u}(z,1)}{\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f(z,1)-\big[z^{\leqslant x-c}\big]f(z,1)},

the asymptotics of
\mathbb{E}_{\leqslant x}\big[\upsilon\big]
thus being the same as
the mean value of
\upsilon
for objects whose size lies in the half-open interval
(x-c,x]
.

2.2.5
Ordered integer factorizations

The asymptotics given in equation (
5
) above do not require the set of tiles to be finite.
Suppose
\Gamma=\{\log k:k\geqslant 2\}
.
Then
\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\log n}\big|
, as well as being
the number of tilings
of a strip of length
\log n

using tiles of logarithmic length, is also the number of
ordered factorizations
of
n
.
For example, the integer 12 has eight distinct ordered factorizations:

12\qquad 6\!\times\!2\qquad 4\!\times\!3\qquad 3\!\times\!4\qquad 2\!\times\!6\qquad 3\!\times\!2\!\times\!2\qquad 2\!\times\!3\!\times\!2\qquad 2\!\times\!2\!\times\!3.

This is a classical problem,
the question of establishing the asymptotics of
|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant\log x}\big|
being first investigated by Kalmár in the 1930s
(see also entries
A074206
and
A173382
in the OEIS
[
25
]
).
The
IGF
and
DGF
for these tilings can be expressed nicely in terms of the Riemann zeta function:

F_{\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1-\sum_{k\geqslant 2}e^{-s\log k}}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{2-\zeta(s)}.

Our method immediately yields the result first deduced by Kalmár
[
16
,
17
]
that the asymptotic number of ordered factorizations of integers less than or equal to
x
is

|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant\log x}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}-\frac{1}{\mu\hskip 0.70007pt\zeta^{\prime}(\mu)}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\mu}\mathchoice{\;\approx\;}{\approx}{\approx}{\approx}3.14294\hskip 0.70007ptx^{1.72865},

where
\mu
is the positive real root of the equation
\zeta(s)=2
.
And,
using the trivariate
IGF

f_{\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}}(x,u,v)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{2-(u-1)z^{\log m}-v\hskip 0.70007pt\zeta(\log(1/z))},

we can also establish
that the expected number of factors in an ordered factorization of an integer at most
x
is asymptotically

-\frac{2}{\zeta^{\prime}(\mu)}\log x\mathchoice{\;\approx\;}{\approx}{\approx}{\approx}1.10002\log x,

and that the expected number of factors equal to a fixed integer
m
is

-\frac{m^{-\mu}}{\zeta^{\prime}(\mu)}\log x\mathchoice{\;\approx\;}{\approx}{\approx}{\approx}0.55001\hskip 0.70007ptm^{-1.72865}\log x.

For example, the proportion of factors equal to 2 is asymptotically
2^{-\mu}/2\approx 15.086\%
.

For a much more detailed analysis of ordered factorizations, see Hwang
[
12
]
.

2.2.6
Infinite sets of rational tiles

We do not need irrational tile lengths for a class of tilings to be primitive, since
an infinite set of
rational
tile lengths will be irrational if their denominators are not bounded.

As an example, suppose
\Gamma=\{k+2^{-k}:k\geqslant 0\}=\{1,\,1\frac{1}{2},\,2\frac{1}{4},\,3\frac{1}{8},\,\ldots\}
.
The
IGF
of
\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}
is not a Puiseux series, as would be the case for a rational class (see Section
2.4
), because denominators are unbounded.
Solving numerically, equation (
5
) yields the approximate asymptotics
for
\big|\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{\leqslant x}\big|
of
0.69622\,x^{2.31329}
.

2.2.7
Adjacency constraints

Suppose we have three types of tile, of length
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
, with
\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
irrational.
If a
\beta
-length
tile is not permitted to immediately follow an
\alpha
-length
tile, then the
IGF
for the class of permitted strip tilings is

f(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1-z^{\alpha}-z^{\beta}-z^{\gamma}+z^{\alpha+\beta}}.

The denominator of this
IGF
does not have the right structure to use either of Propositions
2
or
4
directly to establish primitive irrationality.
However, a consideration of the combinatorial structure enables a rearrangement of the
IGF
that does meet the requirements of Proposition
2
:

f(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{\left(1-z^{\beta}-\frac{z^{\gamma}}{1-z^{\alpha}}\right)(1-z^{\alpha})}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{\left(1-z^{\beta}-\sum_{k\geqslant 0}z^{k\alpha+\gamma}\right)(1-z^{\alpha})}.

Here, we use the fact that the set of valid tilings can be represented by the regular expression
(\beta+\alpha^{\!{\scalebox{1.1}{$*$}}}\gamma)^{\!{\scalebox{1.1}{$*$}}}\alpha^{\!{\scalebox{1.1}{$*$}}}
.
The dominant singularity comes from the first factor in the denominator, so by Proposition
2
we know that these tilings are primitive and can extract the asymptotics using Theorem
1
.

This approach is of very general applicability.
Any non-degenerate class of strip tilings with a finite number of adjacency constraints can be defined by a regular expression (or equivalently, by a deterministic finite automaton), in which
the dominant singularity comes from sequences of sub-tilings from some (possibly infinite) set
\mathcal{S}
(see
[
7
, Sections V.3 and V.5]
).
If
\mathcal{S}
is irrational, then the class of tilings is primitive.
Note that to check that
\mathcal{S}
is irrational only requires finding a single pair of sub-tilings
\mathfrak{s}_{1},\mathfrak{s}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
|\mathfrak{s}_{1}|/|\mathfrak{s}_{2}|\notin\mathbb{Q}
.

2.2.8
Irrational tilings that are not primitive

Figure 5:
A tiling in
\mathcal{W}^{(\sqrt{2})}

Let
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}^{(\beta)}
consist of tilings of a strip of width one with four types of rectangular tile:
two different colours of
1\times 1
square tiles, and
two different colours of
\beta\times 1
tiles, with the restriction that all the square tiles must occur to the left of the tiles of length
\beta
.
Suppose
\beta>1
and that
\beta
is irrational.
See Figure
5
for an example.
These tilings have the
IGF
,

f_{\mathcal{W}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{(1-2z)(1-2z^{\beta})},

with radius of convergence
\frac{1}{2}
.
However,
\mathcal{W}
is not primitive, because its
DGF
,

F_{\mathcal{W}}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{(1-2e^{-s})(1-2e^{-\beta s})},

has evenly spaced poles at
\log 2+2\pi i\hskip 0.70007pt\mathbb{Z}
.

This class is simple enough to analyse explicitly.
Indeed, we have

|\mathcal{W}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\left\lfloor x/\beta\right\rfloor}2^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor x-\ell\beta\right\rfloor}2^{k},

where
\ell
is the number of tiles of length
\beta
, and
k
is the number of square tiles.

Figure 6:
\Big|\mathcal{W}^{(\sqrt{2})}_{\leqslant x}\Big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}c(x)\hskip 0.70007pt2^{x}

Suppose, for each
\ell\geqslant 0
, we let
\delta_{\ell}(x)=(x-\ell\beta)\bmod 1
.
That is,
\delta_{\ell}(x)
is the fractional part of
x-\ell\beta
.
Then,
after some simplification,
it can be seen that asymptotically,

|\mathcal{W}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}c(x)2^{x},\qquad\text{where~~}c(x)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{1-(\beta-1)\ell-\delta_{\ell}(x)}

is a bounded function that oscillates with period one.
See Figure
6
for the plot of one period of
c(x)
when
\beta=\sqrt{2}
.
Note that
c(x)
is, in fact,
not continuous on any interval, since each
term in the sum contributes a discontinuity at
\ell\beta\bmod 1
,
and these are dense on the unit interval since
\beta
is irrational.

In light of this behaviour, and also that of
rational
classes (see Section
2.4
),
we make the following conjecture concerning those classes whose
DGF
has evenly spaced dominant singularities.

Conjecture 6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a combinatorial class
whose generating function
f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)
has radius of convergence
\rho<1
, and

f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}g(z)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{h(z)}{(1-z/\rho)^{\alpha}},

where
\alpha\notin-\mathbb{N}
, and both
g
and
h
are analytic on the cut disk
\overline{D}(0,\rho)\setminus\mathbb{R}^{\leqslant 0}
.

If the only singularities on the line of convergence of its Dirichlet generating function,
F_{\mathcal{C}}(s)
, are evenly spaced at

\log(1/\rho)+2\pi i\hskip 0.70007pt\mathbb{Z}/\omega
,
for some constant
\omega
,
then
the asymptotic number of objects in
\mathcal{C}
of size at most
x
is given by

|\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant x}|\;\sim\;c(x)\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1},

where
c(x)
is a bounded function that oscillates with period
\omega
.

However, it
is
possible for a class of irrational tilings that are not primitive to have smooth aperiodic asymptotics; that is, for
c(x)
to be asymptotically constant.
For example, suppose
\mathcal{Q}
consists of strip tilings with two distinct tiles of length
\log 2
and three distinct tiles of length
\log 3
, in which all the
\log 2
tiles must be to the left of any
\log 3
tiles.
Then
\mathcal{Q}
has
IGF

f_{\mathcal{Q}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{(1-2\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\log 2})(1-3\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\log 3})},

which has radius of convergence
e^{-1}
.
This class of tilings is not primitive since its
DGF
,

F_{\mathcal{Q}}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{(1-2\hskip 0.70007pte^{-s\log 2})(1-3\hskip 0.70007pte^{-s\log 3})},

has a pole of order two at
1+2\pi i
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Now,

f_{\mathcal{Q}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{a\geqslant 0}\hskip 0.70007pt\sum_{b\geqslant 0}2^{a}\hskip 0.70007pt3^{b}\hskip 0.70007ptz^{a\log 2+b\log 3}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{n\in S_{3}}n\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\log n},

where
S_{3}
is the set of 3-smooth numbers,
a positive integer being
k
-smooth
if none of its prime factors exceeds
k
,
so the 3-smooth numbers are those of the form
2^{a}3^{b}
for
a,b\geqslant 0
.

Thus,
|\mathcal{Q}_{\leqslant x}|
gives the sum of the 3-smooth numbers no greater than
e^{x}
.
Hence (see
[
33
]
for example), despite
\mathcal{Q}
not being primitive, we have

|\mathcal{Q}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{x\hskip 0.70007pte^{x}}{(\log 2)(\log 3)},

which
matches the asymptotics that would be given by Theorem
1
if
\mathcal{Q}
were in fact primitive.

2.2.9
Floor tiling

Consider the process of tiling a rectangular floor using rectangular tiles of dimension
\beta\times\gamma
, where
\beta/\gamma\notin\mathbb{Q}
.
Tiles may be laid in either orientation, and
tiling starts in the southwest corner, partial tiles being permitted (only) along the east and north edges of the floor.
See Figure
7
for an example using tiles whose aspect ratio is the golden ratio
\varphi
.

Figure 7:
A tiling of a square with sides of length
4\pi
by rectangular tiles of dimension
1\times\varphi

Because the aspect ratio of the tiles is irrational,
when tiling from the southwest,
in order to be able to complete the tiling,
at each step a “ray” of identically oriented tiles needs to be laid either northwards or eastwards.
Each ray can be identified by a line segment across its end, and these form a walk with step set

S\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\{\mathbf{u}_{\beta}=(\beta,0),\,\mathbf{u}_{\gamma}=(\gamma,0),\,\mathbf{v}_{\beta}=(0,\beta),\,\mathbf{v}_{\gamma}=(0,\gamma)\}
.
A tiling is completed once the walk reaches either the east or the north edge of the rectangular floor.
See Figure
7
for an illustration.

Note, however, that the sub-walks
\mathbf{u}_{\beta}\mathbf{v}_{\gamma}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\gamma}\mathbf{u}_{\beta}
correspond to the same union of two rays, and this is also the case for
\mathbf{u}_{\gamma}\mathbf{v}_{\beta}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\beta}\mathbf{u}_{\gamma}
.
Thus tilings are in bijection with walks with step set
S
that avoid consecutive pairs of steps
\mathbf{v}_{\gamma}\mathbf{u}_{\beta}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\beta}\mathbf{u}_{\gamma}
(that is, right turns with steps of different lengths).
For example, in Figure
7
the two steps forming each right turn have the same length.

Thus, the bivariate
IGF
for these walks satisfies the functional equation

f(u,v)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}1\>+\>f(u,v)\Big((1-v^{\gamma})u^{\beta}\>+\>(1-v^{\beta})u^{\gamma}\>+\>v^{\beta}\>+\>v^{\gamma}\Big),

where
u
marks the distance east, and
v
marks the distance north, from the southwest corner.
Hence we have

f(u,v)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1-u^{\beta}-u^{\gamma}-v^{\beta}-v^{\gamma}+u^{\beta}v^{\gamma}+u^{\gamma}v^{\beta}}.

To determine the asymptotic number of tilings of a square floor, we need asymptotics for
\big[u^{\leqslant x}\big]\big[v^{\leqslant x}\big]f(u,v)
, and probably also for
\big[u^{\leqslant x}\big]\big[v^{\leqslant y}\big]f(u,v)
, at least when
y
is (very) close to
x
.
This seems to require techniques considerably
beyond the scope of this work, so we leave it as an open problem.
Handling rectangular floors with aspect ratio not equal to one is likely to require a generalisation to the irrational case of the approach to “off-diagonal” asymptotics established in

Analytic Combinatorics in Several Variables

[
26
]
(see also
[
22
,
23
]
).

Question 7
.

(a) Asymptotically, how many tilings are there of an
x\times x
square using rectangular tiles of dimension
\beta\times\gamma
, when
\beta/\gamma\notin\mathbb{Q}
?
(b) Asymptotically, how many such tilings are there of an
x\times rx
rectangle, for any fixed
r
?

For (a), on the basis of numerical data and anticipated concentration behaviour, we would expect the answer to be
C_{\beta,\gamma}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-2x}/\sqrt{x}
, for some constant
C_{\beta,\gamma}
to be determined, where
\rho
is the radius of convergence of
f(z,z)
.

2.3
Lattice walks and trees

In this section, we explore some structures whose
IGF
s have dominant square root singularities.
We begin with irrational Motzkin paths, and Dyck paths with an infinite step set,
before analysing two classes of plane trees with irrational vertex sizes.

Figure 8:
A Motzkin path in
\mathcal{M}^{(\sqrt{2})}
of Euclidean length
6+12\sqrt{2}

2.3.1
Motzkin paths

Given some irrational
\beta>0
, let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{(\beta)}
be the class of Motzkin paths in which up-steps and down-steps have size
\beta
, and horizontal steps have size
1
.
Graphically, the size of a step could be its width, or its height, or it could be its Euclidean length.
For example, if
\beta=\sqrt{2}
, then the size of a path in
\mathcal{M}
equals its Euclidean length when drawn in the standard way; see Figure
8
for an example.
By the first passage decomposition, the
IGF
for
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the functional equation

f_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}1\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}z\hskip 0.70007ptf_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}z^{2\beta}\hskip 0.70007ptf_{\mathcal{M}}(z)^{2}.

This yields

f_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1-z-\sqrt{(1-z)^{2}-4\hskip 0.70007ptz^{2\beta}}}{2\hskip 0.70007ptz^{2\beta}},

which has a dominant square root singularity.
Given that the discriminant is the difference of two squares, this singularity is
at the unique positive root
\rho
of
1-z-2\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\beta}
(the other factor being positive for
0\leqslant z\leqslant 1
).
Thus, every singularity on the line of convergence of the
DGF
is a root of
1-e^{-s}-2\hskip 0.70007pte^{-\beta s}
.
So, by Proposition
2
,
\mathcal{M}
is primitive.

Now,

f_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1-z}{2\hskip 0.70007ptz^{2\beta}}\>-\>\frac{h(z)}{(1-z/\rho)^{-1/2}},\quad\text{where~~}h(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{2\hskip 0.70007ptz^{2\beta}}\hskip 0.70007pt{\sqrt{\dfrac{(1-z)^{2}-4\hskip 0.70007ptz^{2\beta}}{1-z/\rho}}},

and so has the required form.
Applying Theorem
1
then gives

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{\leqslant x}|

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}-\frac{1}{2\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{2\beta}\hskip 0.70007pt\log(1/\rho)\hskip 0.70007pt\Gamma(-\tfrac{1}{2})}\hskip 0.70007pt\sqrt{\lim_{z\to\rho^{-}}\frac{(1-z)^{2}-4\hskip 0.70007ptz^{2\beta}}{1-z/\rho}}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{-3/2}

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\log(1/\rho)}\hskip 0.70007pt\sqrt{\frac{\rho+\beta\hskip 0.70007pt(1-\rho)}{\pi\hskip 0.70007pt(1-\rho)^{3}}}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{-3/2},

where we use the fact that
2\rho^{\beta}=1-\rho
.

For
\beta=\sqrt{2}
, we have the approximate asymptotics
\big|\mathcal{M}^{(\sqrt{2})}_{\leqslant x}\big|\simeq 2.29313\cdot 2.39330^{x}x^{-3/2}
.

2.3.2
Dyck paths with infinite step sets

Enumeration by Euclidean length enables us to handle infinite step sets.
In a (normal) Dyck path, each up-step is associated, in either the first passage decomposition or the arch decomposition, with a matching down-step, being the next down-step to the right at the same level (see
[
7
, pages 76–77]
, for example).
The classes we consider in this section require that a matched pair of up and down steps have the same height.

As our first example, a
tall-Dyck
path consists of a sequence of steps drawn from the set
\{(1,\pm k):k\geqslant 1\}
, which ends at the level of its start point, never going below this level, and in which each
(1,k)
up-step is matched by a
(1,-k)
down-step.
That is, a tall-Dyck path is formed from a classical Dyck path by replacing each matched pair of up and down steps by a
(1,k)
step and a
(1,-k)
step for some
k
.
See Figure
9
for an example.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the class of tall-Dyck paths, and let
f_{\mathcal{D}}(z,w)
be its bivariate
IGF
enumerating by Euclidean length in which
w
marks width.
Note that there are infinitely many tall-Dyck paths of any given even width.
Then, by the first passage decomposition,
f^{\mathcal{D}}(z,w)
satisfies

f^{\mathcal{D}}(z,w)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}1\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}w^{2}\sum_{k\geqslant 1}z^{2\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}\hskip 0.70007ptf^{\mathcal{D}}(z,w)^{2}.

Hence,

f^{\mathcal{D}}(z,w)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\hskip 0.70007ptw^{2}s(z)}}{2\hskip 0.70007ptw^{2}s(z)},

where
s(z)=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}z^{2\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}
.

The univariate
IGF

f^{\mathcal{D}}(z,1)
has a dominant square root singularity,

\rho\approx 0.529999
,
at the unique positive root of
1-4\hskip 0.70007pts(z)
, so by Proposition
2
,
\mathcal{D}
is primitive.
Applying Theorem
1
then gives

|\mathcal{D}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{2\hskip 0.70007pt\sqrt{\rho\hskip 0.70007pts^{\prime}(\rho)}}{\sqrt{\pi}\log(1/\rho)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{-3/2}\mathchoice{\;\approx\;}{\approx}{\approx}{\approx}1.69800\cdot 1.88680^{x}x^{-3/2}.

The asymptotic expected width of a tall-Dyck path of Euclidean length at most
x
is

\frac{\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]{f^{\mathcal{D}}}_{\!\!w}(z,1)}{\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f^{\mathcal{D}}(z,1)}\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{x}{2\hskip 0.70007pt\rho\hskip 0.70007pts^{\prime}(\rho)}\mathchoice{\;\approx\;}{\approx}{\approx}{\approx}0.547797x.

A Dyck path with step set
\mathbb{Z}^{>0}\times(\mathbb{Z}\!\setminus\!\{0\})

This approach
is valid for variants of
Dyck paths with any step set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{>0}\times(\mathbb{R}\!\setminus\!\{0\})
with an irrational set of lengths,
in which each
(h,k)
up-step in a path is matched by some
(j,-k)

for an example.

mutatis mutandis

by setting

For example, the asymptotic number of Dyck paths
with step set

of Euclidean length at most

.

The enumeration of lattice paths in which matched up and down steps do not need to have the same height seems to be rather more complicated.

,

gives

|\mathcal{A}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{\sqrt{\rho+\sqrt{\rho}\big(\beta(1-\sqrt{\rho})^{2}-\rho\big)}}{2\sqrt{\pi}\log(1/\rho)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{-3/2}.

2.4
Rational and shifted-rational classes

Recall from the Introduction that we say that a combinatorial class is rational if there exists some
\omega>0
(not necessarily rational) such that the size of each object in the class is a multiple of
\omega
.
Its
period
is the largest such
\omega
.
That is,
\omega
is the greatest common divisor of the object sizes, being the largest value that divides into each size an integral number of times.
For example, the class of strip tilings with tiles of length
\frac{\pi}{15}
,
\frac{\pi}{6}
and
\frac{\pi}{4}
is rational with period
\frac{\pi}{60}
.

We say that a class is
shifted-rational
if there exist
\omega,\delta>0
with
\delta/\omega\notin\mathbb{Q}
such that the size of each object in the class equals
n\omega+\delta
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given a shifted-rational class with set of object sizes
\Lambda
,
its period is the largest
\omega
such that
there exists a positive
offset

\delta<\omega
such that
\Lambda\subseteq\omega\mathbb{N}+\delta
.
A rational class is considered to have offset zero.
Note that we consider a shifted-rational class to be an irrational class. Among other things, this simplifies the statement of Proposition
3
.
Note that
if
\mathcal{G}
is a shifted-rational class and
\mathcal{F}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\text{{Seq}}[\mathcal{G}]
is supercritical, then
\mathcal{F}
is primitive.

Suppose
f(z)
is the
IGF
of a rational or shifted-rational class with period
\omega
and offset
\delta
. Then
f(z)=z^{\delta}j\big(z^{\omega}\big)
, where
j(z)
is an ordinary power series.
Suppose that
f(z)
has positive radius of convergence
\rho<1
, and one can write

f(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}g(z)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{h(z)}{(1-z/\rho)^{\alpha}},

where
\alpha\notin-\mathbb{N}
, and both
g
and
h
are analytic on the cut disk
\overline{D}(0,\rho)\setminus\mathbb{R}^{\leqslant 0}
.
Then
j(z)
has a unique dominant singularity at
\rho^{\omega}
, and

j(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}z^{-\delta/\omega}\left(g(z^{1/\omega})+\frac{h(z^{1/\omega})}{(1-z^{1/\omega}/\rho)^{\alpha}}\right)\!.

Using the classical result (
1
), and the fact that

\lim_{z\to\rho^{\omega}}\,\frac{1-z/\rho^{\omega}}{1-z^{1/\omega}/\rho}\;=\;\omega,

we can extract the asymptotics for the coefficients of
f(z)
:

\big[z^{n\omega+\delta}\big]f(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big[z^{n}\big]j(z)\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{\rho^{-\delta}\hskip 0.70007pt\omega^{\alpha}\hskip 0.70007pth(\rho)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-n\omega}\hskip 0.70007ptn^{\alpha-1}\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{\omega\hskip 0.70007pth(\rho)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-n\omega-\delta}\hskip 0.70007pt(n\omega+\delta)^{\alpha-1}.

For an ordinary power series
j(z)
and integer
n
, the cumulative sum of the coefficients satisfies the identity

\big[z^{\leqslant n}\big]j(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big[z^{n}\big]\frac{j(z)}{1-z}.

Thus, the asymptotic number of objects of size at most
x
in a rational or shifted-rational class with
IGF

f(z)
is given by

\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f(z)\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{\rho^{-\delta}\hskip 0.70007pt\omega\hskip 0.70007pth(\rho)}{(1-\rho^{\omega})\hskip 0.70007pt\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-\left\lfloor(x-\delta)/\omega\right\rfloor\omega-\delta}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}.

Further terms in the asymptotics of shifted-rational classes can be established by using the result of Flajolet and Odlyzko (see equation (
3
) on page
3
).

Note that, if we let the period tend to zero (conceptually tending towards being an irrational class), then
we have
\lim_{\omega\to 0}(1-\rho^{\omega})/\omega=-\log\rho
, in line with what we would expect from Theorem
1
.

Plane trees with edges of irrational size provide a simple example of a shifted-rational class.
Given irrational
\gamma>0
, let
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{(\gamma)}
be the class of plane trees in which each vertex has size
1
and each edge has size
\gamma
.
Since the number of edges in a tree is one less than the number of vertices,
the set of tree sizes in
\mathcal{E}
is
\{1+(1+\gamma)n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
The
IGF
for
\mathcal{E}

is

f_{\mathcal{E}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{2\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\gamma}}\!\left(1-\sqrt{1-4\hskip 0.70007ptz^{1+\gamma}}\right)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}z+z^{2+\gamma}+2z^{3+2\gamma}+5z^{4+3\gamma}+14z^{5+4\gamma}+\ldots

Thus,

\big|\mathcal{E}_{1+(1+\gamma)n}\big|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}C_{n}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\hskip 0.70007pt4^{n}n^{-3/2},

where
C_{n}
is the
n
th Catalan number.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
consists of forests of trees with irrational edges, so
\mathcal{F}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\text{{Seq}}[\mathcal{E}]
.
Then the
IGF
for
\mathcal{F}

is

f_{\mathcal{F}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1-f_{\mathcal{E}}(z)}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1-2\hskip 0.70007ptz^{\gamma}+\sqrt{1-4\hskip 0.70007ptz^{1+\gamma}}}{2(1-z-z^{\gamma})}.

For all irrational
\gamma
, the simple pole dominates the square root singularity, so the class is supercritical, and hence primitive.

2.5
Phase transitions

Consider a family
\{\mathcal{C}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}:{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\Omega\}
of combinatorial classes, in which each class depends on a vector
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of parameter values, drawn from the set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that consists of those vectors
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
for which
\mathcal{C}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is primitive.
Suppose that for each
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\Omega
, the asymptotic number of objects in
\mathcal{C}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is given by

\big|\mathcal{C}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\leqslant x}{}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}c_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\rho_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{-x}\cdot x^{\nu_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}.

Typically, we find that the exponential growth rate
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{-1}
varies continuously for parameter values in
\Omega
:

\lim_{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\underset{\Omega}{\to}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\rho_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}.

Moreover,

\Omega
can be partitioned into several regions of continuity
\Omega=\biguplus_{i=1}^{k}R_{i}
corresponding to different phases, such that within each region
the degree
\nu_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
of the polynomial factor (also known as the
critical exponent
) remains constant,
while
the constant
c_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
term varies continuously.
That is, for each
i
and any
{\boldsymbol{\beta}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\in R_{i}
, we have

\nu_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\nu_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
, and

\lim_{{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\underset{R_{i}}{\to}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}c_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}c_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}.

Phase transitions occur in the asymptotics on the boundaries of the regions, where the value of
\nu_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
changes abruptly.
We illustrate this phenomenon with three examples.

2.5.1
Strip tilings

Figure 12:
A tiling in
\mathcal{Y}^{(\sqrt{1/6},\sqrt{1/2})}

Given
\beta,\gamma>0
, with
\min(\beta,\gamma)
irrational,
let
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{Y}^{(\beta,\gamma)}
consist of tilings of a strip with white tiles of length
1
,
blue tiles of length
\beta
, and
green tiles of length
\gamma
, subject to the requirement that every green tile occurs to the right of any blue tiles.
See Figure
12
for an illustration.
The
IGF
for
\mathcal{Y}
is

f_{\mathcal{Y}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1}{1-z-z^{\beta}}\left(1+\frac{z^{\gamma}}{1-z-z^{\gamma}}\right)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1-z}{(1-z-z^{\beta})(1-z-z^{\gamma})},

with poles,
\rho_{\beta}
and
\rho_{\gamma}
, the positive roots of
1-z-z^{\beta}
and
1-z-z^{\gamma}
, respectively,
with
\rho_{\beta}<\rho_{\gamma}
if
\beta<\gamma
.
Since
\min(\beta,\gamma)
is irrational,
\mathcal{Y}
is primitive.

This family of tilings has three phases, with the asymptotics exhibiting a discontinuity across the ray
\beta=\gamma
,
on which there is a linear subexponential term as a result of the double pole.
Either side of this ray, the subexponential term is constant.

\big|\mathcal{Y}_{\leqslant x}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\begin{cases}\dfrac{1-\rho_{\beta}}{(1-\rho_{\beta}-\rho_{\beta}^{\gamma})\hskip 0.70007ptH(\rho_{\beta})}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho_{\beta}^{-x},&\text{if $\beta<\gamma$},\\[17.0pt]
\dfrac{(1-\rho)\hskip 0.70007pt\log(1/\rho)}{H(\rho)^{2}}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx,&\text{if $\beta=\gamma$, where $\rho=\rho_{\beta}=\rho_{\gamma}$},\\[14.0pt]
\dfrac{1-\rho_{\gamma}}{(1-\rho_{\gamma}-\rho_{\gamma}^{\beta})\hskip 0.70007ptH(\rho_{\gamma})}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho_{\gamma}^{-x},&\text{if $\beta>\gamma$},\end{cases}

where
H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)
.

2.5.2
Forests of rooted binary trees

A forest of rooted binary trees in
\mathcal{F}^{(\beta,\gamma)}

Given
\beta,\gamma>0
, consider the following class of plane trees.
Each tree has a
root
of degree one, of size either
1
or
\gamma
.
Each other vertex of degree one is a
leaf
and has size either
1
or
\beta
.
Every tree has a root and at least one leaf.
Any further (internal) vertices all have degree three and size
1
.
Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{(\beta,\gamma)}
be the class of forests (that is, sequences) of these trees.
See Figure
13
for an example.

The
IGF
for these forests is

f_{\mathcal{F}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{2\hskip 0.70007ptz}{z-z^{\gamma}+(z+z^{\gamma})\sqrt{1-4\hskip 0.70007ptz(z+z^{\beta})}}.

The behaviour of the asymptotics and the structure of a typical forest depend primarily on the value of
\gamma
.
The class
\mathcal{F}^{(\beta,\gamma)}
is primitive if either
\gamma<1
and
\gamma
is irrational or else
\gamma\geqslant 1
and
\beta
is irrational.
Let
\rho_{\beta}
be the positive root of
1-4z^{2}-4z^{1+\beta}
, and, if
\gamma<1
(so
z^{\gamma}>z
for small
z
), let
\rho_{\gamma}
be the positive root of

z^{\gamma}-z-(z+z^{\gamma})\sqrt{1-4\hskip 0.70007ptz(z+z^{\beta})}
.
Then, the asymptotics of this family of forests exhibit the following three phases, with three distinct critical exponents:

\big|\mathcal{F}_{\leqslant x}\big|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\begin{cases}c_{1}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho_{\gamma}^{-x},&\text{if $\gamma<1$},\\[3.0pt]

c_{3}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho_{\beta}^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{-3/2},&\text{if $\gamma>1$},\end{cases}

for constants
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
, which depend only on
\beta
and
\gamma
(for
c_{1}
and
c_{3}
).

The structure of a typical forest also depends on the phase.
If
\gamma<1
, then, asymptotically, a forest contains lots of small trees whose average size is finite.
In contrast, if
\gamma>1
, then a typical forest contains a finite number of large trees.
On the critical line, in a forest of size at most
x
, the number of trees and the average size of a tree both grow like
\sqrt{x}
.
Formally, if
T(\mathfrak{f})
is the number of trees in a forest
\mathfrak{f}
, then we have

\mathbb{E}_{\leqslant x}\big[T\big]\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\begin{cases}t_{1}x,&\text{if $\gamma<1$},\\[3.0pt]
t_{2}\sqrt{x},&\text{if $\gamma=1$},\\[3.0pt]
t_{3},&\text{if $\gamma>1$},\end{cases}

where
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
and
t_{3}
depend only on
\beta
and
\gamma
.

2.5.3
Grounded Dyck paths

We conclude by taking a brief look at a simple example that has slightly more complex critical behaviour.
Given
\beta,\gamma>0
, a
grounded Dyck path
consists of a nonempty Dyck path in which each step has width either
1
or
\gamma
.
This path is both preceded and followed by a (possibly empty) sequence of horizontal steps, each of width either
1
or
\beta
.
The size of a grounded Dyck path is its horizontal extent, the sum of the widths of its steps.
Such a path can be considered to represent a range of mountains rising from a plain.
See Figure
14
for two examples.

The class
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{(\beta,\gamma)}
of grounded Dyck paths has
IGF

f_{\mathcal{G}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{1-2(z+z^{\gamma})^{2}-\sqrt{1-4(z+z^{\gamma})^{2}}}{2(z+z^{\gamma})^{2}\hskip 0.70007pt(1-z-z^{\beta})^{2}}.

Let
\rho_{\beta}
be the positive root of
1-z-z^{\beta}
, and
\rho_{\gamma}
be the positive root of
1-2z-2z^{\gamma}
.
The class
\mathcal{G}^{(\beta,\gamma)}
is primitive if
\rho_{\beta}\leqslant\rho_{\gamma}
and
\beta
is irrational or
\rho_{\gamma}\leqslant\rho_{\beta}
and
\gamma
is irrational.
The asymptotics of this family of paths exhibit a discontinuity across the critical curve given by the parametric equation

(\beta,\gamma)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big(\log_{\rho}\!\hskip 0.70007pt(1-\rho),\,\log_{\rho}\!\hskip 0.70007pt(\tfrac{1}{2}-\rho)\big),

for
0<\rho<\frac{1}{2}
.

Dirichlet series and Ribenboim series

A generalised
Dirichlet series
is a series of the form

F(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{i\geqslant 1}a_{i}\,e^{-\lambda_{i}s},

where
(\lambda_{i})_{i\geqslant 1}
is a nonnegative strictly increasing sequence tending to infinity.

The primary reference for standard results on Dirichlet series is Hardy and Riesz
[
10
]
. For a more modern presentation, see McCarthy
[
21
]
.

The region of convergence for a Dirichlet series is a right half-plane:

Proposition 8

(
[
10
, Theorem 3]
)
.

For any Dirichlet series
F(s)
, there exists a unique value
\mu\in[0,\infty]
,
known as its
abscissa of convergence
,
such that

F(s)
is convergent for all
s
such that
\re s>\mu
, and

F(s)
is not convergent for any
s
such that
\re s<\mu
.
The line
\re s=\mu
is known as
the line of convergence
for
F(s)
.

A Dirichlet series
F(s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}e^{-\lambda s}
is analytic within its half-plane of convergence, since it has a well-defined derivative, also a Dirichlet series:
F^{\prime}(s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}-a_{\lambda}\lambda e^{-\lambda s}
.

Moreover, if a Dirichlet series has nonnegative coefficients, then it is singular at the real point on its line of convergence:

Proposition 9

(
[
10
, Theorem 10]
)
.

If
F(s)
is a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients and a finite abscissa of convergence
\mu
, then
\mu
is a singularity of
F
.

These properties of Dirichlet series are sufficient to establish convergence and analyticity in Ribenboim series.

Proposition 10
.

If
f(z)
is a Ribenboim series, then there exists some
\rho\in[0,\infty]
such that
f(z)
is convergent for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|z|<\rho
, and is not convergent when
|z|>\rho
. Moreover, the function
f
is analytic in the cut disk
D(0,\rho)\setminus\mathbb{R}^{\leqslant 0}
.
If
f(z)
has nonnegative coefficients and
\rho
is finite, then
f
has a singularity at
z=\rho
.

Proof.

The exponential transform
F
of
f
is a Dirichlet series, satisfying

F(s)=f(e^{-s})
when
\im s\in(-\pi,\pi]
and
f(z)=F(\log(1/z))
if
z\neq 0
.
The result then follows from Propositions
8
and
9

and the fact that the composition of two analytic functions is analytic.
∎

Given a Dirichlet series
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}e^{-\lambda s}
, its

summatory function
is the function
A(x)=\sum_{\lambda\leqslant x}a_{\lambda}
.
A Dirichlet series can be recovered from the Laplace transform of its summatory function as follows:

Observation 11
.

F(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}e^{-\lambda s}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\hskip 0.70007pts\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}e^{-sx}\hskip 0.70007ptdx\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}s\int_{0}^{\infty}A(x)\hskip 0.70007pte^{-sx}\hskip 0.70007ptdx,

where
A(x)=\sum_{\lambda\leqslant x}a_{\lambda}
.

To prove our main theorem, we combine this observation with an appropriate Wiener–Ikehara Tauberian theorem.
The following variant is due to Ryo Kato, which extends an earlier theorem of Anthony Kable
[
15
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 12

(Kato
[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
j:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing function whose Laplace transform

J(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}j(x)\hskip 0.70007pte^{-sx}\hskip 0.70007ptdx

converges absolutely when
\re s>1
.
Given
\alpha\geqslant 1
and
L,\,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}>0
, with
\alpha>\alpha_{i}
for each
i
, and
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
, let

K(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}J(s)\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}\frac{L}{(s-1)^{\alpha}}\mathchoice{\>-\>}{-}{-}{-}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{L_{i}}{(s-1)^{\alpha_{i}}}.

If
K
extends continuously to the closed half-plane
\{s:\re s\geqslant 1\}
, then

j(x)\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{L}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pte^{x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}.

The following proposition establishes the asymptotics of the summatory function of suitably well-behaved Dirichlet series.
Subsequently, we transfer this result to Ribenboim series.

Proposition 13
.

Suppose
F(s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}e^{-\lambda s}
is a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients and abscissa of convergence
\mu>0
.
Suppose that
\mu
is the unique singularity of
F
on the line
\mu+i\mathbb{R}
, and that one can write

F(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}G(s)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{H(s)}{(s-\mu)^{\alpha}},

where
\alpha\notin-\mathbb{N}
, and both
G
and
H
are analytic on the half-plane
\{s:\re s\geqslant\mu\}
, then the asymptotics of the summatory function of
F
are given by

\sum_{\lambda\leqslant x}a_{\lambda}\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{H(\mu)}{\mu\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pte^{\mu x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}.

Proof.

Let
A(x)=\sum_{\lambda\leqslant x}a_{\lambda}
be the summatory function of
F
, and let
L=H(\mu)
.

We re-scale so that the abscissa of convergence is at 1.
By Observation
11
, when
\re s>1
, we have

\widetilde{F}(s)\mathchoice{\;:=\;}{:=}{:=}{:=}\frac{F(\mu s)}{s}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}A\!\left(\frac{x}{\mu}\right)e^{-sx}\hskip 0.70007ptdx,

(7)

where
A(x/\mu)
is nonnegative and non-decreasing.

Moreover,

\widetilde{F}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\widetilde{G}(s)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{\widetilde{H}(s)}{(s-1)^{\alpha}},

(8)

for functions
\widetilde{G}
and
\widetilde{H}
analytic on the half-plane
\{s:\re s\geqslant 1\}
.

We consider two cases. Assume first that
\alpha\geqslant 1
.
If
r
is the greatest integer less than
\alpha
, then by taking the Taylor expansion about
s=1
, we have

\widetilde{F}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}K(s)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{\widetilde{H}(1)}{(s-1)^{\alpha}}\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{L_{i}}{(s-1)^{\alpha-i}},

for some constants
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}
.
Since the remaining terms of the Taylor expansion are continuous at
s=1
, the function

K
extends continuously to the closed half-plane
\{s:\re s\geqslant 1\}
.

Hence

A(x/\mu)
satisfies the conditions of the Tauberian theorem (Proposition

).
Therefore,

A(x/\mu)\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{\widetilde{H}(1)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pte^{x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}.

Thus, since
\widetilde{H}(1)=L/\mu^{\alpha}
, we have

A(x)\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{L}{\mu\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pte^{\mu x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1},

as required.

Now suppose that
\alpha<1
.
Since
\widetilde{F}(s)
is analytic, so is the Laplace transform on the right of equation (
7
),
and we can legitimately differentiate under the integral sign.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(-1)^{n}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}s^{n}}\widetilde{F}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}\hskip 0.70007ptA\!\left(\frac{x}{\mu}\right)e^{-sx}\hskip 0.70007ptdx.

Taking the
n
th derivative in (
8

(-1)^{n}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}s^{n}}\widetilde{F}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}Y(s)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{Z(s)}{(s-1)^{\alpha+n}}

where
Y
and
Z
are both analytic on the half-plane
\{s:\re s\geqslant 1\}
.
Moreover, if
1
is the only singularity of
\widetilde{F}
on the line
1+i\mathbb{R}
, then this is also the case for its derivatives.

If we let
n=\left\lceil 1-\alpha\right\rceil
so that
\alpha+n\geqslant 1
, then the Tauberian theorem now applies to
x^{n}A(x/\mu)
, so we have

x^{n}A(x/\mu)\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{Z(1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+n)}\hskip 0.70007pte^{x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha+n-1}.

Now, since
\alpha\notin-\mathbb{N}
,

Z(1)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\widetilde{H}(1)\hskip 0.70007pt\alpha\hskip 0.70007pt(\alpha+1)\hskip 0.70007pt\cdots\hskip 0.70007pt(\alpha+n-1)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\frac{L\,\Gamma(\alpha+n)}{\mu^{\alpha}\hskip 0.70007pt\Gamma(\alpha)}.

Hence,

A(x)\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{L}{\mu\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pte^{\mu x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}

∎

This is sufficient for the proof of our main theorem,
which we restate first.

Theorem
1

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a primitive irrational combinatorial class.

f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)

f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}g(z)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{h(z)}{(1-z/\rho)^{\alpha}},

\alpha\notin-\mathbb{N}

g
and

then

|\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}\big[z^{\leqslant x}\big]f_{\mathcal{C}}(z)\;\sim\;\frac{h(\rho)}{\log(1/\rho)\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}.

Proof.

If the conditions of the theorem are met, then, since
\mathcal{C}
is primitive,

\mathcal{C}

F_{\mathcal{C}}(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}G(s)\mathchoice{\>+\>}{+}{+}{+}\frac{H(s)}{(s-\mu)^{\alpha}},

where
\mu=\log(1/\rho)

and both
G

H

G(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}g(e^{-s}),\qquad H(s)\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}h(e^{-s})\left(\frac{s-\mu}{1-e^{\mu-s}}\right)^{\!\alpha}.

|\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant x}|\mathchoice{\;\sim\;}{\sim}{\sim}{\sim}\frac{H(\mu)}{\log(1/\rho)\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\hskip 0.70007pt\rho^{-x}\hskip 0.70007ptx^{\alpha-1}.

.
Now,

\lim_{s\to\mu}\,\frac{s-\mu}{1-e^{\mu-s}}\mathchoice{\;=\;}{=}{=}{=}1.

Thus

∎

Analysis of factors related to embryo implantation/clinical pregnancy in patients with severe Intrauterine Adhesions(IUA) undergoing In Vitro Fertilization-Embryo Transfer (IVF-ET) following hysteroscopic adhesiolysis: a retrospective study
Authors: Jiao Lin, Xingping Zhao, Yuxi Chen, Bing Xie, Haiying Cheng, Sang He, Chen Luo, Xiaoyan Cheng, Bingyu Li, Enuo Peng, Xianghong Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo investigate factors influencing embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy in patients with severe IUA undergoing IVF-ET and to develop a predictive model.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed 229 patients with severe IUA who underwent IVF-ET at Xiangtan Central Hospital between January 2020 and June 2024. All participants received transcervical resection of adhesions (TCRA) before embryo transfer. Clinical characteristics, endometrial repair protocols, medication types, and dosages were evaluated. Binary logistic regression was used to assess correlations between these factors and post-transfer embryo implantation or clinical pregnancy.ResultsThe number of TCRA procedures emerged as a risk factor for both embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy. Additional risk factors for embryo implantation included years of infertility, initial and final American Fertility Society (AFS) scores, and the presence of endometrial cavity fluid (ECF). For clinical pregnancy, cumulative and daily average estrogen doses before transformation were identified as risk factors. Protective factors for both outcomes included endometrial thickness (EMT) on the day of transformation/ultrasound, regularly treat patients with CD138 positivity, and reduced endometrial peristalsis. Uterine artery resistance index (RI) and the use of a down-regulated hormone replacement therapy (HRT) protocol also favored embryo implantation, while transferring two high-quality embryos improved clinical pregnancy rates. Stepwise backward multivariate logistic regression was performed on variables with potential significance in univariate analysis. Using statistically significant variables, LightGBM prediction models were developed, yielding area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.892 for embryo implantation and 0.821 for clinical pregnancy.ConclusionsSevere IUA negatively impacts embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy. Optimizing clinical management—by minimizing TCRA frequency, reducing pre-transformation estrogen exposure, treating concurrent endometritis with antibiotics, suppressing endometrial peristalsis, and lowering uterine artery resistance—can enhance post-transfer outcomes. ECF adversely affects success rates, and while transferring more embryos may improve clinical pregnancy rates, it reduces implantation efficiency.Keywords: Severe IUA, IVF-ET, Embryo implantation, Clinical pregnancy, Prediction model

Introduction

IUA represent a prevalent etiology of female infertility, typically arising from trauma to the endometrial basal layer, frequently secondary to repeated intrauterine procedures. Such trauma induces adhesion formation between myometrial walls, resulting in partial or complete obliteration of the uterine cavity. Clinically, IUA manifests as hypomenorrhea, amenorrhea, recurrent pregnancy loss, or infertility, collectively compromising both reproductive function and psychological well-being [1]. Hysteroscopy serves as the gold standard for diagnosis and treatment, with TCRA effectively restoring uterine anatomy and volume. Nevertheless, severe IUA cases face a substantial risk of adhesion recurrence post-TCRA, with rates as high as 62.5% [2]. Concurrent endometrial fibrosis and diminished functional endometrium further exacerbate reproductive challenges, adversely affecting subsequent fertility outcomes.

For patients undergoing assisted reproductive technology (ART), IUA poses unique therapeutic hurdles, with reported success rates averaging only 39.3% [3–5]. The detrimental impact of IUA on ART outcomes primarily stems from endometrial dysfunction, including impaired receptivity, reduced thickness, aberrant decidualization, and compromised trophoblast invasion. Fouks et al. [6] identified moderate-to-severe IUA as a significant negative predictor of live birth in IVF, emphasizing endometrial thickness as a critical prognostic factor. Mortimer et al. [7] further observed that patients requiring multiple TCRA procedures exhibited lower live birth rates, with thinner endometria in non-pregnant cohorts correlating with poorer outcomes. Despite these findings, limited research has systematically evaluated the role of endometrial repair protocols, adjuvant therapies, and medication dosages in optimizing embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy rates for severe IUA patients undergoing IVF. Thus, this study aims to identify key determinants of pregnancy success in severe IUA patients receiving IVF-ET and to construct a predictive model for post-TCRA outcomes.

Materials and methods

Data collection

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at the Reproductive Center of Xiangtan Central Hospital from January 2020 to June 2024, enrolling 229 infertile patients with severe IUA(AFS score ≥ 8) who underwent combined treatment involving TCRA and subsequent IVF-ET. Following approval by the Reproductive Ethics Committee of Xiangtan Central Hospital, Hunan University (Approval Number: SZ202503-02), All participants were exempted from providing informed consent. Comprehensive clinical data were collected, including demographic characteristics, hysteroscopic surgical parameters, endometrial repair techniques, and preparation protocols. Inclusion criteria comprised: (1) age ≤ 40 years, (2) severe IUA (AFS score ≥ 8), and (3) availability of transferable embryos, while exclusion criteria involved (1) uterine anomalies, (2) untreated autoimmune disorders, (3) recurrent implantation failure, and (4) cases not proceeding to embryo transfer post-TCRA. The final cohort of 229 patients was established after rigorous screening based on these predefined criteria.

Treatment of IUA

Patients diagnosed with IUA via three-dimensional ultrasound underwent TCRA 3–7 days post-menses under intravenous anesthesia, following informed consent. The procedure utilized 0.9% normal saline as distension medium (intrauterine pressure: 80–120 mmHg; flow rate: 150 mL/min) and a 30° 5-mm rigid hysteroscope to lyse adhesions under direct visualization until normal uterine cavity morphology was achieved, with concurrent endometrial sampling for histopathology and CD138 immunohistochemistry. Postoperative management included: routine antibiotic prophylaxis with supplemental oral doxycycline (200 mg/day×14 days) for chronic endometritis cases; adhesion prevention via intrauterine device placement and sodium hyaluronate instillation; and for severe endometrial defects, spatial balloon therapy (SPA) combined with platelet-rich plasma (PRP) infusion one week postoperatively. All patients received standardized hormonal therapy (estradiol valerate/gel 6 mg/day×21 days plus progesterone 200 mg/day on days 12–21), followed by second-look hysteroscopy at one month for cavity assessment and IUD removal if normalized, culminating in frozen-thawed embryo transfer (FET) for all participants.

Implementation of classification systems

The severity of IUA in all study participants was systematically assessed using the American Fertility Society (AFS, 1988) classification system, which incorporates hysteroscopic findings and imaging data to evaluate three critical parameters: menstrual characteristics, extent of adhesion involvement, and adhesion morphology. The composite AFS score, derived from the summation of these components, categorizes IUA severity as mild (1–4 points), moderate (5–8 points), or severe (9–12 points), with only patients demonstrating significant adhesion burden (AFS score ≥ 8) meeting the inclusion criteria for this investigation.

Controlled ovarian hyperstimulation and embryo transfer

All enrolled patients received individualized controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) protocols, with the specific ovulation induction regimen (including GnRH agonist/antagonist protocols, mild stimulation, or luteal phase protocols) tailored according to ovarian reserve markers, baseline hormone profiles, and body mass index. Following oocyte retrieval and fertilization via conventional IVF or Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection (ICSI), resulting embryos were cultured to cleavage or blastocyst stage before cryopreservation. Subsequent FET cycles, initiated in the month following the final hysteroscopic procedure, employed one of four endometrial preparation approaches: HRT, down-regulated HRT, natural cycles, or stimulated cycles, with potential adjuvant therapies (e.g., growth hormone 4 IU daily) administered based on serial endometrial assessments. Pre-transfer evaluations included comprehensive transvaginal ultrasound with Doppler assessment of endometrial thickness, vascular indices (RI/PI/S-D ratio), cavity fluid status, and peristaltic activity, with therapeutic modifications (low molecular weight heparin, aspirin, or tocolytics) implemented as indicated. Endometrial peristaltic waves were observed for two minutes under stable conditions, with data analyzed at quadruple speed to assess type, direction, frequency, and intensity. Endometrial peristaltic waves are indicated by a frequency of over 3 per minute. Embryo transfer strategies strictly adhered to both institutional protocols and ASRM guidelines regarding the number of embryos transferred.

Reproductive outcomes

The study’s primary endpoints were biochemical and clinical pregnancy outcomes. Biochemical pregnancy was confirmed by serum β-hCG levels ≥ 50 IU/L at 14 days post-transfer, with serial monitoring of β-hCG doubling patterns, while clinical pregnancy was defined as ultrasonographic visualization of an intrauterine gestational sac with fetal cardiac activity at 4–5 weeks post-transfer. These outcome measures were dichotomized as follows: for biochemical pregnancy, “Yes” denoted successful embryo implantation (β-hCG ≥ 50 IU/L) and “No” indicated implantation failure; for clinical pregnancy, “Yes” represented confirmed intrauterine pregnancy with fetal viability, whereas “No” included cases with biochemical evidence of implantation but without subsequent ultrasonographic confirmation of clinical pregnancy.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.1) and Python (version 3.12.0). Missing data were handled through mean imputation for continuous variables and median imputation for categorical variables. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages. Initial descriptive analyses characterized baseline study variables. For comparative analyses, categorical variables were assessed using chi-square tests, and continuous variables were analyzed with Mann-Whitney U tests due to their non-normal distribution. Univariate logistic regression was first applied to all variables, followed by stepwise backward multivariate logistic regression for variables demonstrating statistical significance (P < 0.05) in the univariate analysis. Finally, these significant variables were incorporated into a LightGBM machine learning model to enhance predictive capability.

Result

Factors associated with embryo implantation in patients with severe IUA after TCRA

The study cohort comprised 229 eligible patients with severe intrauterine adhesions, among whom 159 achieved biochemical pregnancy and 112 attained clinical pregnancy. Comparative analyses demonstrated significant associations (P < 0.05) between embryo implantation outcomes and several key variables: duration of infertility, number of TCRA procedures, initial and final AFS scores, EMT at transformation and ultrasound assessment days, CD138 status, endometrial preparation protocols, presence of ECF, subendometrial blood flow patterns, and endometrial peristalsis activity. Parallel findings emerged for clinical pregnancy outcomes, with additional significant correlations observed for cumulative and daily average estrogen doses prior to transformation, along with the number of high-quality embryos transferred. Notably, baseline characteristics including age, anti-Müllerian hormone(AMH) levels, Body Mass Index(BMI), infertility etiology, and pregnancy history, as well as PRP therapy and adjunct medications (aspirin, growth hormone), showed no statistically significant impact on outcomes (P > 0.05), as detailed in Table 1.

Univariate logistic regression analysis of correlates

Logistic regression analysis identified several significant predictors of embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy outcomes in patients with severe intrauterine adhesions (Table 2). The number of TCRA procedures emerged as an independent risk factor for both outcomes (embryo implantation: OR = 0.79, 95% CI 0.68–0.91, P = 0.002; clinical pregnancy: OR = 0.79, 95% CI 0.68–0.93, P = 0.005). Additional risk factors for embryo implantation included duration of infertility (OR = 0.89, P = 0.045), higher initial (OR = 0.72, P = 0.017) and final AFS scores (OR = 0.76, P = 0.002), and presence of endometrial cavity fluid (OR = 0.38, P = 0.002). For clinical pregnancy, elevated cumulative (OR = 0.99, P = 0.030) and daily average (OR = 0.80, P = 0.027) estrogen doses prior to endometrial transformation were identified as negative predictors. Protective factors common to both outcomes included greater endometrial thickness (transformation day OR = 1.41, P = 0.008; ultrasound day OR = 1.42, P = 0.003), regularly treat patients with CD138 positivity(OR = 2.87, P = 0.017) and reduced endometrial peristalsis (OR = 5.36, P < 0.001). Notably, lower uterine artery resistance (OR = 72.34, P = 0.024) and down-regulated HRT protocols (OR = 2.72, P = 0.007) specifically enhanced implantation rates, while transfer of two high-quality embryos significantly improved clinical pregnancy outcomes (OR = 3.71, P = 0.011).

Prediction model for embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy in patients with severe IUA after TCRA

Using stepwise backward multivariate logistic regression analysis of significant variables identified in univariate analysis (Table 3), we developed two LightGBM prediction models incorporating distinct predictor sets: (1) For embryo implantation: infertility duration, final AFS score, endometrial thickness, uterine artery RI, CD138 status, endometrial preparation protocol, endometrial cavity fluid, and peristalsis; (2) For clinical pregnancy: number of TCRA procedures, daily estrogen dosage, endometrial peristalsis, and number of high-quality embryos transferred (variable importance rankings shown in Fig. 1A and B). The models demonstrated robust predictive performance, with AUCs of 0.892 (95% CI:0.851–0.993) for implantation and 0.821 (95% CI:0.766–0.876) for clinical pregnancy (Fig. 2A and B). Decision curve analysis confirmed clinical utility, showing superior net benefit versus “treat-all” strategies across probability thresholds of 0.18–0.98 (implantation) and 0.20–0.90 (pregnancy) (Fig. 3A and B). Both models showed good calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow P = 0.075 and 0.127, Fig. 4A and B) and maintained strong performance on internal validation (mean bootstrap AUCs: 0.837 [95% CI:0.807–0.865] and 0.810 [95% CI:0.784–0.832], Fig. 5A and B), supporting their reliability for clinical decision-making in severe IUA management.

Discussion

The endometrium serves as the biological foundation for successful reproduction, with its structural and functional integrity being essential for optimal reproductive outcomes. IUA typically manifest through a clinical triad of hypomenorrhea/amenorrhea, cyclic pelvic pain, and infertility [8], resulting from trauma-induced fibrosis that disrupts multiple reproductive processes including sperm transport, embryo implantation and placentation [9]– [10]. These pathological changes create an unfavorable uterine environment through endometrial fibrosis, vascular compromise, hormonal resistance, and reduced thickness. While TCRA represents the gold standard treatment to restore uterine anatomy, the procedure itself may paradoxically exacerbate endometrial damage. Meta-analytical evidence demonstrates the superiority of cold knife techniques over electrocautery in reducing adhesion recurrence (OR 0.45), improving menstrual restoration (78% vs. 52%), and decreasing operative time (18.5 ± 6.2 vs. 24.3 ± 7.8 min) [11]. Our protocol employed micro-hysteroscopic cold dissection, which minimizes iatrogenic injury through precise mechanical separation that preserves adjacent endometrial integrity and vascular supply [12]. Importantly, reproductive outcomes inversely correlate with surgical frequency, as each additional TCRA procedure reduces implantation rates by 21% (aOR 0.79) and clinical pregnancy likelihood by 23% (aOR 0.77) [13], highlighting the critical importance of optimal initial surgical management.

Endometrial receptivity, characterized by optimal thickness and vascular perfusion, represents a critical determinant of IVF success, with endometrial thickness < 7 mm associated with significantly reduced pregnancy rates (OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.42–0.75) [14]. In thin endometrium, the proximity of implantation sites to oxygen-rich spiral arteries may induce oxidative stress through reactive oxygen species generation, impairing embryonic development [15]. While our data demonstrated greater endometrial thickness in conception cycles (8.2 ± 1.3 mm vs. 6.7 ± 1.1 mm, P < 0.01), the predictive value remains controversial. Uterine artery resistance indices showed modest correlation with outcomes (r=−0.32, P = 0.04), though adjuvant anticoagulation therapy (aspirin/LMWH) failed to demonstrate clinical benefit (P = 0.21) in our cohort.The relationship between uterine artery resistance and pregnancy outcomes shows statistical significance, but the wide confidence interval suggests imprecision and potential sparse data bias. Therefore, while it may be a predictive factor, these results should be cautiously interpreted and confirmed with larger studies. PRP therapy, despite meta-analytic evidence supporting its role in endometrial proliferation [16], showed no significant effect in our study (P = 0.34) or in RCTs by Shen et al. [17] (P = 0.28).This discrepancy may stem from technical challenges in PRP preparation and administration, highlighting the need for standardized protocols in future trials to define its role in IUA treatment.

Chronic endometritis (CE), a polymicrobial intrauterine infection, significantly contributes to adhesion recurrence through distinct molecular mechanisms. Liu et al. [18] identified a pathogenic cascade in IUA patients with CE characterized by upregulated TGF-β1 expression (2.3-fold increase vs. controls) that promotes fibrosis while suppressing MMP-9 activity (0.4-fold decrease), thereby stabilizing fibrotic lesions. The presence of ECF, composed of blood, mucus and tubal secretions, demonstrates a threshold effect on reproductive outcomes - optimal implantation rates occur with 1–3.5 mm ECF (clinical pregnancy rate 42.6% vs. 18.3% for > 3.5 mm) [19], consistent with its identification as an independent negative predictor (OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.20–0.71) [20]. CD138 + plasma cell infiltration, strongly associated with both miscarriage history (OR 2.1, 95% CI 1.4–3.2) and post-TCRA adhesion recurrence (RR 1.8, 95% CI 1.3–2.5) [21], mechanistically impairs endometrial repair by prolonging the inflammatory phase and disrupting decidualization [22]. Our findings corroborate these observations, as the study identified a positive correlation between CD138 positivity and pregnancy outcomes (OR 2.87, 95% CI 1.27–6.49). However, this does not imply that CD138 positivity alone serves as a prognostic marker for favorable outcomes; instead, it underscores its significant role in subsequent treatment strategies, systematic CD138 immunohistochemistry during TCRA coupled with targeted antibiotic therapy improves IVF outcomes (OR 2.4, 95% CI 1.7–3.4) [23], establishing CE management as an integral component of comprehensive IUA treatment.

Endometrial preparation represents a pivotal determinant of successful embryo transfer, with hormonal modulation playing a crucial role in receptivity. Supraphysiological estradiol levels (> 300 pg/mL) impair receptivity by shortening the implantation window and downregulating critical adhesion molecules like integrin αvβ3 [24, 25]. The relationship between estrogen and LIF follows an inverted U-curve, with peak expression at 150–200 pg/mL (r = 0.68) but significant suppression beyond 250 pg/mL (r=−0.52) [26]. For IUA patients, GnRH-α pretreatment combined with HRT demonstrates superior clinical pregnancy rates to conventional HRT (52.3% vs. 38.7%) [24, 27], likely through multiple mechanisms: restoration of uterine receptivity markers (2.1-fold increase in integrin β3);promotion of cytoplasmic synapse formation; and suppression of local inflammatory mediators (e.g., IL-6、TNF-α). Our findings confirm the detrimental impact of excessive pre-transformation estrogen exposure (cumulative dose > 120 mg; daily dose > 8 mg), while demonstrating the therapeutic benefits of down-regulation protocols in optimizing endometrial receptivity for IUA patients.

This study has limitations. Its retrospective design relies on historical data, which may miss key confounding variables despite attempts at statistical correction. It may also introduce selection bias, affecting the estimation of exposure-outcome associations. Some patients had not achieved live births by the follow-up cutoff. Our research focuses on key factors affecting embryo implantation and first-trimester pregnancy maintenance, as improving clinical pregnancy rates is essential for increasing live births. Live birth rates are also influenced by mid-to-late pregnancy factors like fetal anomalies and complications. Future analyses will expand the sample size, control for mid-to-late pregnancy variables, and provide a thorough examination of this important long-term outcome. While our study currently lacks validation with an independent external dataset, we have implemented rigorous internal validation methods (10-fold cross-validation with repetition) to reduce overfitting. We plan to conduct external validation in a multicenter study to demonstrate the model’s validity and stability.

Conclusion

Severe IUA have detrimental effects on embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy. Optimizing management by reducing the number of TCRA and pre-transformation estrogen dosage, implementing antibiotic treatment for coexisting endometritis, suppressing endometrial peristalsis, and lowering uterine artery resistance can significantly improve post-transfer pregnancy outcomes in severe IUA patients. ECF adversely affects pregnancy outcomes, it negatively impacts embryo implantation efficiency, While increasing the number of transferred embryos may improve clinical pregnancy rates. We developed a predictive model to evaluate embryo implantation and clinical pregnancy in cases of severe IUA. The model exhibits substantial efficacy in forecasting these outcomes for patients with severe IUA and is anticipated to offer critical support for the precision and individualization of clinical diagnostic and therapeutic decision-making.

Duality Structures in Unimodular Electrovacuum Gravity
Jack C. M. Hughes & Fedor V. Kusmartsev

Abstract
We show that the structure of the Lorentz group in four dimensions is such that unimodular (trace-free) gravity in electrovacuum can be consistently represented as an algebraic condition on the symmetric product space of 2-forms. This condition states that the commutator between the Riemann tensor and the Hodge dual must be equal to the commutator between Hodge dual and an operator constructed from the Maxwell tensor; symbolically, [ Riem , ∗ ] = [ ∗ , F ⊠ F ] [\text{Riem},*]=[*,F\boxtimes F] . We show that this condition is equivalent to the trace-free field equations, that the right-hand-side vanishes if and only if the energy-momentum tensor vanishes (recovering the appropriate Einstein spacetime limit) and that this condition can be solved in the spherically symmetric ansatz to yield Reissner-Nordström-de Sitter uniquely. This analysis suggests that the conceptual distinction between unimodular gravity and General Relativity is one of emphasis on how irreducible representations of the Riemann tensor are constrained by the existence of energy-momentum and the associated field equations.

6
Conclusions

In this work, we have demonstrated that if one imposes the condition

[\text{Riem},*]=[*,F\boxtimes F]

(79)

on the space
\text{Sym}(\Lambda^{2}\otimes\Lambda^{2})
, then one has an equivalence to unimodular (trace-free) gravity in electrovacuum. Not only does (

30

[
5

[

72

73
]

We motivated condition (
79

3
). This gives a
6\times 6

3\times 3

19

Commuting with the Hodge dual extracts only this sector, which in vacuum vanishes. Given a spacetime that is electrovacuum, the map (
37
) lifts the matter sector up to the Hodge decomposition. This allows for an identification of how the matter content constrains the irreducible representations of the Riemann tensor. In particular, the electromagnetic field
F_{\mu\nu}
prevents the Riemann tensor from being block-diagonal, breaking the symmetry between self-dual and anti-self-dual subspaces. Essentially, the electromagnetic field can be thought of an operator that prevents the closure of the commutator (
79
). This happens precisely because the homomorphism operators between self-dual and anti-self-dual operators constitute an irreducible representation of the Lorentz algebra:

\text{Hom}(\Lambda^{+},\Lambda^{-})\cong(1,0)\otimes(0,1)\cong(1,1),

(80)

which is isomorphic to the space of
symmetric trace-free
2-tensors
(1,1)

[
8
]
. The trace-free field equations (
67
) equate the pure geometric element of this space (the trace-free Ricci sector) to that induced by non-vanishing energy-momentum. This however is just a projection of the more fundamental relation (
79
), which expresses exactly this condition in the algebraic space of the Riemann tensor. So while energy-momentum conservation is what formally separates the Einstein field equations from unimodular gravity, representing the trace-free equations as a higher-order condition on
operators
(
79
) demonstrates how the unimodular theory has its foundation in representation theory as opposed to a strict action principle. This opens up the possibility of building more transparent contrasts between gravity and gauge theory, since a representation theory first perspective (at the cost of reducing from Einstein’s theory to unimodular gravity, hence a less straightforward action principle
[
73
]
) adapted to the Hodge decomposition (
3
) is exactly the starting point for the Plebanski formalism and the relationship of GR to the Ashtekhar variables and Twistor theory, where the addition of energy-momentum content is often highly non-trivial (while here it is a specific kind of operator in an irreducible representation)
[
14
,
20
,
24
,
27
]
. In the concrete sense demonstrated in this paper, electromagnetic fields are embedded in the self-dual anti-self-dual sector of the geometry (
3
) which in vacuum is otherwise absent (
53

Mapping (
79

[
21

58

59

G

3

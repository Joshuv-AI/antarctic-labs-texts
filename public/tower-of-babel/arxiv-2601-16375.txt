Duality for graded Lie algebras
Andrey Lazarev, Rong Tang

Abstract
. A well-known and old result of Hazewinkel and Koszul states that the cohomology of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra is isomorphic, up to a suitable shift, to its twisted homology, a Lie-theoretical version of Poincaré duality. This paper establishes an analogue of this result for graded (or super) Lie algebras and, more generally, differential graded Lie algebras. Closely related to this result is a calculation of the cohomology of a graded Lie algebra 𝔤 \g with coefficients in its universal enveloping algebra U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) \U(\g) as a one-sided module. This cohomology turns out to be one-dimensional and serves as a dualizing module for the cohomology of 𝔤 \g . Moreover, it is shown that for a unimodular graded Lie algebra 𝔤 \g , the derived category of U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) \U(\g) has a Calabi-Yau structure. As a consequence, the category of rational infinity local systems on a simply connected topological space with totally finite-dimensional rational homotopy groups, has a Calabi-Yau structure, a generalization of Poincaré duality for elliptic spaces.

1.
Introduction

Given an augmented differential graded (dg) algebra
A
over a field
{\mathbf{k}}
and its bar-construction coalgebra
\B(A)
, the categories of dg
A
-modules and of
\B(A)
-comodules are equivalent as
\infty
-categories (or, more precisely, there is a Quillen equivalence between them as model categories). This is one of the manifestations of dg Koszul duality
[
31
]
and in this paper we study certain duality functors in these
\infty
-categories. On the algebra side, it is the functor

d:X\mapsto d(X):={\RHom}_{A}(X,A)

and on the coalgebra side it is a functor that is simply the linear dual on the subcategory of
finite-dimensional
dg comodules but is rather more involved for general comodules. It was shown in
[
3
]
that these duality functors correspond to each other across Koszul duality. The right
A
-module
\RHom_{A}({\mathbf{k}},A)
is called the (one-sided) dualizing complex for an augmented dg algebra
A
. Following
[
3
]
, we call
A
a
Gorenstein
dg algebra if its dualizing complex is quasi-isomorphic to the one-dimensional
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space with a trivial right
A
-action.

We will consider the situation when
A
is a dg Hopf algebra (such as the dg algebra of singular chains on based loops on a topological space). Our first result (Theorem
3.5
) states that the derived category
D(A)
of a dg Hopf algebra
A
has a Serre duality given by a pair

(\Perf(A),\operatorname{pvd}(A))
of subcategories of
D(A)
and the Serre functor given by tensoring with the duailzing complex. Here
\Perf(A)
consists of perfect (compact) objects in
D(A)
and
\operatorname{pvd}(A)
is formed by
A
-modules with finite-dimensional cohomology. If
A
is Gorenstein, then
D(A)
has a further refinement, called the Calabi-Yau (CY) structure. That means that if
N\in\operatorname{pvd}(A)
and
M\in\Perf(A)
, then
\Hom_{D(A)}(N,M)
and
\Hom_{D(A)}(M,N)
are
{\mathbf{k}}
-dual to each other up to a shift. Note that this notions of a Serre duality and a CY structure are generalizations of the usual ones (when the two subcategories coincide). In the CY case it was explicitly spelled out in
[
3
]
but was known for a long time, at least since Keller’s paper
[
19
]
, cf. especially Lemma 3.4. of op.cit. The notions of a triangulated CY category stems from derived categories of CY varieties and the Kontsevich’s homological mirror symmetry, cf.
[
22
]
.

We next specialize to the case
A=\U(\g)
, the universal enveloping algebra of a graded Lie algebra
\g
that we assume to be finite-dimensional. We show (
5.24
) that the dualizing complex for
\U(\g)
has one-dimensional cohomology and the action of
\g
on it is through the trace of the adjoint representation. This is the main result of this paper and, though its formulation is simple, the proof is surprisingly involved. Namely, one-dimensionality follows from a relatively straightforward argument with spectral sequences but the calculation of the
\g
-action requires an application of homological perturbation theory and minimal models for
A_{\infty}
-modules. This result can also be viewed as a
\g
-equivariant analogue of the well-known homological interpretation of the Berezinian (or superdeterminant).

We also have a similar result for a
connective
dg Lie algebra (such as the Lie-Quillen rational model of a topological space).

A consequence of this is that the universal enveloping algebra of a graded unimodular Lie algebra is Gorenstein and so, its derived category is CY with respect to subcategories of perfect and proper dg
\g
-modules. Applied to the Lie-Quillen model of a simply-connected topological space
A
with totally finite-dimensional rational homotopy groups, we obtain that the category of infinity local systems on
X
(or the derived category of cohomologically locally constant complexes of sheaves on
X
) have a CY property, a generalization of Poincaré duality for elliptic spaces
[
12
]
.

This also generalizes the fact that the universal enveloping algebra
\U(\g)
of ordinary (ungraded) unimodular Lie algebra
\g
is Gorenstein and CY cf.
[
3
,
17
]
. One essential difference between the graded and ungraded cases is that
\U(\g)
is homologically smooth for an ungraded
\g
whereas this is not true for graded Lie algebras (e.g. for a one-dimensional graded Lie algebra
\g
concentrated in an odd degree,
\U(\g)
is an exterior algebra on one generator, and it has an infinite global dimension).

These results admit an appropriate translation to the Koszul dual side, for dg comodules over the bar-construction
\B(\U(\g))
. In fact, it is more convenient to pass to the CE coalgebra
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
which is weakly equivalent to
\B(\U(\g))
(and thus, has an equivalent coderived category) but is much smaller and easier to work with. We show (Theorem
5.15
) that
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
is derived dual as a comodule over itself, to a certain twist of itself (up to a shift) and, in the case of a unimodular Lie algebra
\g
, this twist is trivial. This is a far-reaching generalization of old results of Hazewinkel and Koszul
[
15
,
21
]
. In this connection, we mention that there is another generalization of Poincaré duality for ordinary Lie algebras where the latter are replaced by Lie algebroids
[
11
]
, or, still more generally, Lie-Reinhart pairs
[
18
]
and
Q
-manifolds
[
33
]
. Investigating the relationship between these results and ours is an interesting open problem.

Next, we study a potential generalization of the main result described above, to the case of dg Lie algebras. In this situation, it is more convenient to switch to a more flexible language of
L_{\infty}
-algebras (or strong homotopy Lie algebras). An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\g,\ell)

is most concisely defined through its representing dg algebra
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
which is a formal power series algebra
\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\g^{*}[-1])
on the shifted dual space to
\g
supplied with a continuous derivation
\ell
having no constant term. In the case of a graded Lie algebra
\g
,
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
is the ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg (CE) algebra of
\g
and
\ell
has only the quadratic term. The
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear continuous dual to
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
is denoted by
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
and agrees with the CE homological complex of
\g
when
\g
is a graded Lie algebra. In this case,
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
is a conilpotent cocommutative dg coalgebra. Strong homotopy Lie algebras arise in a great variety of contexts such as deformation quantization
[
23
]
, string field theory
[
35
]
and others. Their representing dg algebras can be viewed as linear duals to fibrant objects in the model category of cocommutative conilpotent dg coalgebras, cf.
[
16
,
32
]
for this point of view.

Given an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\g,\ell)
, the divergence
\nabla(\ell)
of the derivation
\ell
is a CE 1-cocycle which, in the case of a graded Lie algebra, corresponds to the trace of the adjoint representation of
\g
. Thus, it is natural to conjecture that the derived dual to
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
as a comodule over itself, is, up to an appropriate shift, its twist by the cocycle
\nabla(\ell)
. Note that Theorem
5.15
is a special case of this conjecture when
\g
is a graded Lie algebra.

Working on the Koszul dual side of dg
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
-comodules, we develop appropriate tools (having independent interest) for proving this conjecture and obtain some partial results. These complement the results obtained on the side of
\g
-modules described above in the sense that neither of them are special cases of the other but both are special cases of above conjecture.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains background on dg Koszul duality between modules over a dg Lie algebra and comodules over its CE coalgebra . In Section
3
we study the derived duality functor in the derived category
D(A)
of a dg Hopf algebra
A
and, in particular, prove that if
A
is Gorenstein, then
D(A)
is CY (Corollary
3.6
). In Section
4
it is proved that the dualizing complex for a proper dg Lie algebra has one-dimensional cohomology. Section
5
, which is the technical heart of the present paper, is dedicated to proving that the action of finite-dimensional graded Lie algebra on its dualizing complex is via the (super) trace of its adjoint representation. This section also contains topological applications mentioned above. In Section
6
integrals on rings of formal Laurent series are discussed and a formula for the adjoint of a derivation is obtained (Proposition
6.3
); this formula must be a standard result in differential geometry, at least in the ungraded context but, since we were unable to find a reference, a complete proof was given. Section
7
contains results on the structure of the derived dual to
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
where
\g
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra satisfying certain (unfortunately, quite strong) assumptions; these are Propositions
7.10
and
7.12
. Of some independent interest here is a construction of the derived pseudocompact completion. The last section contains calculations with
L_{\infty}
-algebras of small dimensions.

Notation and conventions

Throughout this paper, we will denote by
{\mathbf{k}}
a fixed field of characteristic zero. Unmarked tensor products and Homs will be understood as taken over
{\mathbf{k}}
. We will mostly be working in the category
\DGVect

-graded (dg) vector spaces over

. A dg algebra is a monoid in

and a dg-coalgebra is a comonoid in
\DGVect
. The category of dg-algebras will be denoted by
\DGA
. A graded dg algebra

is

if the unit map

admits a retract
\epsilon:A\to{\mathbf{k}}

is the kernel of
\epsilon
. The category of augmented dg-algebras will be denoted by
\DGA_{*}
.We will also work with the category
\DGL
of dg Lie algebras. Given a dg Lie algebra
\g
, we denote by
\U(\g)
its universal enveloping dg algebra.

Given two dg modules
M,N
over a dg algebra
A
, we write
\Hom_{A}(M,N)
for the dg vector space of

-homomorphisms from
M
to
N
and
\RHom_{A}(M,N)
for the corresponding derived functor (which is, therefore, an object of

).

A
pseudocompact
(pc) dg vector space is a projective limit of finite dimensional vector spaces and the category of pc dg vector spaces with continuous chain maps will be denoted by
\DGVectpc
. The functor of linear duality
V\mapsto V^{*}
establishes an anti-equivalence from
\DGVect
to
\DGVectpc
with the inverse functor being the functor of continuous duality (which we will denote by the same symbol

for a pc dg vector space

). Therefore,
\DGVectop
, the opposite category to
\DGVect
is equivalent to
\DGVectpc
and we will frequently not distinguish between these two categories. In particular, the data of a dg coalgebra are equivalent under this correspondence to that of a monoid in
\DGVectpc
, i.e. a pc dg algebra. A dg coalgebra is

if its dual pc dg algebra is

-augmented and local, i.e. its augmentation ideal is a unique two-sided dg maximal ideal. The category of conilpotent dg coalgebras will be denoted by
\DGC_{*}
. Similarly, the category of conilpotent cocommutative dg coalgebras will be denoted by
\DGCC
.

A
proper
dg vector space is one with totally finite-dimensional cohomology. Similarly, a dg algebra or a dg Lie algebra is proper if its cohomology is (totally) finite dimensional.

Given a dg (pc or not) algebra

\xi\in A^{1}
such that
d\xi+\xi^{2}=0
. The set of MC elements of

is denoted by
\MC(A)
. Given a dg

-bimodule
(M,d)
and an MC element
\xi\in\MC(A)
, define the

of

to be the
left

-module with underlying graded vector space

supplied with the given left action of

and the twisted differential

given by by
d^{[\xi]}(m)=d(m)-(-1)^{|m|}m\xi
for

.

Similarly, a
left twist

~{}^{[\xi]}M
of
(M,d)
is the
right
dg

-module with the same underlying graded vector space and left action as

and supplied with the differential
~{}^{[\xi]}d(m)=d(m)+\xi m

. This construction is most commonly applied to the case when

. See

Let

be a finite-dimensional graded vector space; then the endomorphism algebra

2.
Recollection: associative and Lie-commutative Koszul duality

In this section we recall Koszul duality between associative algebras and coalgebras
[
31
]
, between Lie algebras and cocommutative coalgebras
[
16
]
and a relationship between the two.

Given an augmented dg algebra
A
, we denote by
\B(A)
its bar-construction, which is a cofree conilpotent coalgebra
\mathbf{T}(\bar{A}[1])
with the differential
d_{1}+d_{2}
where
d_{1}
is induced by the internal differential in
A
and
d_{2}
is induced by the degree
1
linear map
\bar{A}[1]\otimes\bar{A}[1]\to\bar{A}[1]
which is, in turn, induced by the multiplication
A\otimes A\to A
. An equivalent datum is the pc dg algebra
\hat{\mathbf{T}}(\bar{A}^{*}[-1])
with a quadratic differential
\bar{A^{*}}[-1]\to\bar{A^{*}}[-1]\otimes\bar{A^{*}}[-1]
called the dual bar-construction of
A
.

Similarly, given a conilpotent dg coalgebra
C
, we denote by
\Omega(C)
its cobar-construction, which is a free algebra
\mathbf{T}(\bar{C}[-1])
with the differential
d_{1}+d_{2}
where
d_{1}
is induced by the internal differential in
C
and
d_{2}
is induced by the degree
1
linear map
\bar{C}[-1]\to\bar{C}[-1]\otimes\bar{C}[-1]
which is, in turn, induced by the comultiplication
C\to C\otimes C
. The functor
\Omega
is left adjoint to the functor
\B
.

The category
\DGA_{*}
has a model structure with weak equivalences being quasi-isomorphisms and fibrations being surjective maps. The category
\DGC_{*}
has a model structure with weak equivalences being maps of dg coalgebras that become quasi-isomorphisms upon applying the functor
\Omega
and cofibrations being injective maps.

The fundamental result is that these two model categories are Quillen equivalent:

Theorem 2.1
.

The pair of adjoint functors
(\Omega,\B)
establishes a Quillen equivalence between
\DGC_{*}
and
\DGA_{*}
.

Proof.

This is proved (in the greater generality of not necessarily augmented dg algebras and curved coalgebras) in
[
31
, Section 9.3]
and in an earlier work
[
27
, Thèoréme 1.3.1.2]
.
∎

A similar result holds when an augmented dg algebra
A
is replaced by a dg Lie algebra and a conilpotent dg coalgebra
C
by a

conilpotent dg coalgebra.

Namely, given a dg algebra
\g
, denote by

its homological CE complex,

\mathbf{S}(\g[1])

is induced by the internal differential
in

is induced by the degree

{\g}[1]\otimes{\g}[1]\to{\g}[1]

with a quadratic differential

.

Similarly, given a conilpotent cocommutative coalgebra

is the free Lie algebra

The category
\DGL
has a model structure where weak equivalences are quasi-isomorphisms and fibrations are surjections while

.

.

The pair of adjoint functors

This is the main result of

.
∎

So, associated to a dg Lie algebra

are two conilpotent dg coalgebras:

The dg coalgebras

First recall that in order to prove that two conilpotent dg coalgebras are weakly equivalent, it suffices to construct a filtered quasi-isomorphism between them corresponding to some admissible filtrations

\operatorname{gr}\CE_{\bullet}(\g)\to\operatorname{gr}\B(\U(\g)).

is a complex computing

over the symmetric

whereas

2.1.
Derived duality functors for modules and comodules

Given a dg algebra
A
, there is a duality functor on
D(A):M\mapsto d(M)
where

d(M):=\RHom_{A}(M,A).

Abusing the notation slightly, we will write

for an object in

as well as for a dg

is naturally a right dg

is a two-sided module over itself). Formally,

is a contravariant functor

.

An analogue of this functor for a dg coalgebra

is not so straightforward. Given a dg
C
-comodule

, set

d(N):=\holim_{\alpha}N^{*}_{\alpha}

where the homotopy limit in the model category of dg

is a contravariant functor
D^{\co}(C)\to D^{\co}(C^{\op})
. Then the following result holds.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any augmented dg algebra
A
, there is a commutative diagram of functors

\textstyle{D(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{d}
\textstyle{D(A^{\op})}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{D^{\co}(\B(A))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d}
\textstyle{D^{\co}\big((\B(A))^{\op}\big)}

where the vertical arrows are Koszul duality equivalences.

Proof.

This is
[
3
, Proposition 3.11]
.∎

In other words, the derived duality functors

for modules and comodules correspond to each other under Koszul duality (or, more accurately, Koszul equivalence).

Following
[
3
]
, we will call that an augmented dg algebra

is

, i.e. if the

is self-dual up to a shift

; in other words (in the language of

is an

Definition 2.5
.

The
dualizing complex
for an augmented dg algebra

is the right dg
A
-module
{\mathbf{d}}:=d({\mathbf{k}})
.

If
A
is a Gorenstein dg algebra, its dualizing complex (whose cohomology is concentrated in a single degree and is one-dimensional) will be called the
dualizing module
.

Remark 2.6
.

If

is a dg Hopf algebra, which is the case treated in detail in the next section, then the dualizing complex for

is also called the
homological left integral
for
A
. A detailed study of homological left integrals for ungraded Hopf algebras was undertaken in
[
6
]
.

3.
Duality for dg Hopf algebras

Now let
A
be a dg Hopf algebra with comultiplication
\Delta:A\to A\otimes A
and the antipode
S
.
Recall that given two dg
A
-modules
M
and
N
, their tensor product
M\otimes N
is a dg
A
-module where the
A
-module action is given by the formula

a(m\otimes n)=\Delta(a)(m\otimes n)

for
a\in A,m\in M,n\in N
. Given a right
A
-module
M
, it can be viewed as a left module according to the formula
am:=(-1)^{|a||m|}mS(a)
where
a
and
m
are homogeneous elements in
A
and
M
respectively. Given two dg
A
-modules
M
and
N
, their
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear hom space
\Hom(M,N)
is naturally an
A\otimes A^{\op}
-module and it therefore has the structure of an
A
-module using the dg algebra map

(1\otimes S)\circ\Delta:A\to A\otimes A^{\op}.

Next, given two
A
-modules
M
and
N
, there is an
A
-module map

(3.1)

\phi:N\otimes M^{*}\to\Hom(M,N)

so that for
n\in N,m\in M,\alpha\in M^{*}
we have
\phi(n\otimes\alpha)(m)=n\alpha(m)
. If
\dim M<\infty
, then
\phi
is an isomorphism. This is proved in
[
34
]
for ungraded Hopf algebras but the proof carries over verbatim to the dg case.

Recall from
[
31
, Section 8]
that for a dg algebra
A
the category of
dg
A
-modules has an
injective
model structure where weak equivalences are, as before, quasi-isomorphisms but cofibtations are injections. In this model structure all objects are cofibrant and the identity functor is a Quillen equivalence between the projective and injective model structures on
A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits
. We will refer to an injectively fibrant dg module as simply fibrant and to a projectively cofibrant one as simply cofibrant.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be a dg Hopf algebra,
M
be a cofibrant dg
A
-module and
N
be a fibrant dg
A
-module. Then
\Hom(M,N)
is a fibrant dg
A\otimes A^{\op}
-module where the left
A
-action is induced from the left
A
-action on
N
and the right
A
-action which is from the left
A
-action on
M
.

Proof.

Given an acyclic injection
X\to Y
of
A\otimes A^{\op}
-modules, and a map
X\to\Hom(M,N)
we need to show that the following diagram admits a lift:

(3.2)

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom(M,N)}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

This is equivalent to a lift in the following diagram of
A
-modules where the solid arrows are the adjoint to the solid arrows in (
3.2
):

\textstyle{X\otimes_{A}M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N}
\textstyle{Y\otimes_{A}M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

But since
M
is cofibrant, the map
X\otimes_{A}M\to Y\otimes_{A}M
is acyclic. It is also injective (this is obvious if
M
is free as a graded
A
-module and follows in general). Since
N
is a fibrant
A
-module, a desired lift exists.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a dg Hopf algebra and
M,N
be dg
A
-modules. Then there is a natural isomorphism

{\RHom}_{A}(M,N)\to{\RHom}_{A}({\mathbf{k}},\Hom(M,N)).

If
N
is an
A
-bimodule then the above isomorphism in an isomorphism in
D(A^{\op})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
M
is cofibrant and
N
is fibrant dg
A
-modules. Then
\RHom_{A}(M,N)
is represented by
\Hom_{A}(M,N)
.
According to Lemma
3.1
,
\Hom(M,N)
is fibrant over
A\otimes A^{\op}
. Regarded as a dg
A
-module via the map
(1\otimes S)\circ\Delta:A\to A\otimes A
, it is also fibrant since the restriction functor is right Quillen and so preserves fibrant objects.
Therefore,
{\RHom}_{A}({\mathbf{k}},\Hom(M,N))
is represented by

{\Hom}_{A}({\mathbf{k}},\Hom(M,N))\cong\Hom(M,N)^{A}.

Next, dg vector spaces
\Hom_{A}(M,N)
and
\Hom(M,N)^{A}
are isomorphic, cf.
[
34
, Lemma 9.2.2]
where this statement is proved for ordinary Hopf algebras but the argument carries over to the dg graded case without any changes.

Finally, if
N
has an additional structure of a right
A
-module, commuting with its left
A
-module structure, then all the isomorphisms considered above are clearly those of right
A
-modules.

∎

We need the following important result on modules over dg Hopf algebras.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
N
be a dg module over a dg Hopf algebra
A
with the left action
m:A\otimes N\to N
and denote by
N_{\operatorname{tr}}
be the dg
A
-module whose underlying dg vector space is
N
but the action is trivial, i.e. through the counit map
A\to{\mathbf{k}}
.Then the map

(1\otimes m)(1\otimes S\otimes 1)(\Delta\otimes 1):A\otimes N\to A\otimes N_{\operatorname{tr}}

is an isomorphism of left dg
A
-modules. The inverse map is given by

(1\otimes m)(\Delta\otimes 1):A\otimes N_{\operatorname{tr}}\to A\otimes N.

The same pair of mutually inverse maps determines an isomorphism between
A\otimes N
and
A\otimes N_{\operatorname{tr}}
viewed as
right
dg
A
-modules.

Proof.

This is proved in
[
34
, Lemma 9.2.9]
in the ungraded case but the proof generalizes to the dg case in an obvious fashion.
∎

We will now describe duality for proper dg modules over a dg Hopf algebra.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M
be a proper dg module over a dg Hopf algebra
A
. Then there is an isomorphism in
D(A^{\op})
:

d(M)\cong\Hom(M,{\mathbf{d}}).

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.2
and finite-dimensionality of the cohomology of
M
, we have an isomorphism of dg vector spaces.

\displaystyle d(M)

\displaystyle={\RHom}_{A}(M,A)

\displaystyle\cong{\RHom}_{A}({\mathbf{k}},\Hom(M,A))

\displaystyle\cong{\RHom}_{A}({\mathbf{k}},A\otimes M^{*})

Next, by Lemma
3.3
,

A\otimes M^{*}\cong A\otimes(M^{*})_{\operatorname{tr}}
and so,

{\RHom}_{A}({\mathbf{k}},A\otimes(M^{*})_{\operatorname{tr}})\cong\Hom(M,{\RHom}_{A}({\mathbf{k}},A))=\Hom(M,{\mathbf{d}}).

Moreover,
A\otimes M^{*}
is also a
right
dg
A
-module with the right action of
A
on itself and the trivial action on
M^{*}
. This right module structure is carried by the isomorphism
A\otimes M^{*}\to A\otimes(M^{*})_{\operatorname{tr}}
of Lemma
3.3
to the diagonal right action of
A
on
A\otimes(M^{*})_{\operatorname{tr}}
. Thus, the obtained isomorphism
d(M)\cong\Hom(M,{\mathbf{d}})
is one in
D(A^{\op})
rather than merely in
D({\mathbf{k}})
as claimed.
∎

3.1.
Serre duality and Calabi-Yau structures

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear triangulated category and
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{M}
be two triangulated subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
. We say that the pair
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{M})
determines
Serre duality
on
\mathcal{C}
if for all
L\in\mathcal{L}
and
M\in\mathcal{M}
, the graded hom
\Hom^{\bullet}(M,L):=\prod_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathcal{C}}(M,L[i])
is a finite-dimensional graded vector space and there is an auto-equivalence
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
together with functorial isomorphisms in
\mathcal{C}
:

\Hom^{\bullet}(L,F(M))^{*}\cong\Hom^{\bullet}(M,L),~~L\in\mathcal{L},~~M\in\mathcal{M}.

The functor
F
is called the
Serre functor
. If for any object
X
of
\C
,
F(X)\cong X[n]
, we say that
\C
has an
n
-CY structure.

We will now specialize to
\mathcal{C}=D(A)
, the derived category of a dg Hopf algebra
A
,
\mathcal{L}=\Perf(A)
, the subcategory of
D(A)
consisting of perfect dg
A
-modules, and
\mathcal{M}=\operatorname{pvd}(A)
, the subcategory of
D(A)
consisting of proper dg
A
-modules. The following result holds.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
L
be a perfect dg
A
-module for a dg Hopf algebra
A
and
M
be a proper dg
A
-module. Then there is a natural isomorphism in
D(A)
:

{\RHom}^{\bullet}_{A}(L,M\otimes{\mathbf{d}}^{*})^{*}\cong{\RHom}^{\bullet}_{A}(M,L).

Thus,
D(A)
has Serre duality with respect to the pair
(\operatorname{Perf}(A),\operatorname{pvd}(A))
and the Serre functor
?\mapsto?\otimes{\mathbf{d}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
L
is perfect and
M
is proper, we have

\displaystyle{\RHom}_{A}(M,L)

\displaystyle\cong d(M)\otimes_{A}L

\displaystyle\cong\Hom(M,{\mathbf{d}})\otimes_{A}L

\displaystyle\cong(M^{*}\otimes{\mathbf{d}})\otimes_{A}L

where the second isomorphism uses Proposition
3.4
. Next, by the tensor-hom adjunction,

\displaystyle{\big((M^{*}\otimes{\mathbf{d}})\otimes_{A}L\big)^{*}}

\displaystyle\cong{\RHom}_{A}(L,(M^{*}\otimes{\mathbf{d}})^{*})

\displaystyle\cong{\RHom}_{A}(L,M^{**}\otimes{\mathbf{d}}^{*})

\displaystyle\cong{\RHom}_{A}(L,M\otimes{\mathbf{d}}^{*})

as claimed. ∎

As a consequence, we obtain that the derived category of a Gorenstein Hopf dg algebra has a Calabi-Yau structure.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
A
be a dg Hopf algebra that is
n
-Gorenstein, i.e.
\RHom_{A}({\mathbf{k}},A)\cong{\mathbf{k}}[-n]
. Then the pair
(\Perf(A),\operatorname{pvd}(A))
is an
n
-Calabi-Yau structure on
D(A)
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

4.
Dualizing complexes for dg Lie algebras

In this section we let
A=\U(\g)
, the universal enveloping algebra of a dg Lie algebra. We will assume that
\g
is proper, i.e. that
\HH^{\bullet}(\g)
is a totally finite-dimensional graded vector space. The dualizing complex for
\U(\g)
is, therefore the dg right
\U(\g)
-module
\RHom({\mathbf{k}},\U(\g))\cong\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
where the right action is induced by the action of
\U(\g)
on itself by right multiplication. Then
\U(\g)
is a dg Hopf algebra, and so, results of Section
3
apply. However, in this situation we can obtain much more precise results than for general dg Hopf algebras, and in the case when
\g
is a graded Lie algebra (i.e. with a vanishing differential), further strengthening will be achieved.

Let
V
be a graded vector space of finite total dimension (so that the sum of dimensions of the graded components of
V
is finite). Let us choose a basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}
of
V
where
e_{i},i=1,\ldots,n
are even elements of
V
and
\varepsilon_{j},j=1,\ldots,m
are odd elements. Set
|V|:=n-|e_{1}|-\ldots-|e_{n}|+|\varepsilon_{1}|+\ldots+|\varepsilon_{m}|
and note that the integer
|V|
does not depend on the choice of basis in
V
. Then we have the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\g
be a proper dg Lie algebra.
Then the dualizing complex
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
has 1-dimensional cohomology concentrated in the total degree
|\g|
.

Proof.

Firstly, using the spectral sequence

\HCE^{\bullet}\Big(\HH^{\bullet}(\g),\U(\HH^{\bullet}(\g))\Big)\Rightarrow\HCE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g)),

we reduce the claim to the special case when
\g
is a graded Lie algebra (with vanishing differential).

Next, we have the following identification of graded vector spaces

\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))\cong\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\g^{*}[-1])\hat{\otimes}\U(\g).

Introduce the weight filtration on
\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\g^{*}[-1])\hat{\otimes}\U(\g)
by giving an element in
\g^{*}[-1]\subset\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\g^{*}[-1])
weight
1
and an element in
\g\in\U(\g)
weight
-1
. Letting
F_{p}\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\g^{*}[-1])\hat{\otimes}\U(\g)
be the subspace in
\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\g^{*}[-1])\hat{\otimes}\U(\g)
spanned by monomials of weight greater or equal than
-p
, we get an increasing filtration on
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
of the form
\ldots\subset F_{n}\subset F_{n+1}\subset\ldots,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This filtration is exhaustive and the differential in
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
is compatible with it. Moreover, since
\g
is itself graded (nondifferential), the complex
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
has a bigrading, with the additional CE grading so that an element in
\hat{\mathbf{S}}^{n}(\g^{*}[-1])\hat{\otimes}\U(\g)
has the CE grading
n
. Restricted to
\hat{\mathbf{S}}^{n}(\g^{*}[-1])\hat{\otimes}\U(\g)
, the filtration
\{F_{p}\}
is bounded below, namely, it runs as
0\subset F_{n}\subset F_{n+1}\subset\ldots
. Thus, the spectral sequence associated with the filtration
\{F_{p}\}
converges strongly.

Moreover, the
E_{1}
-page of the associated spectral sequence coincides with the CE cohomology of the
abelian
graded Lie algebra with the same underlying graded vector space as
\g
. In other words, it computes

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathbf{S}(\g)}({\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{S}(\g))
where the symmetric algebra
\mathbf{S}(\g)
acts on itself by the left multiplication. The claim now becomes a standard homological calculation with the Koszul complex.
∎

So, the dualizing complex
{\mathbf{d}}:=d({\mathbf{k}})
for any proper dg Lie algebra
\g
has one-dimensional cohomology; according to our conventions, we will call it the
dualizing module
for
\g
. The
\g
-action (or, equivalently,
\U(\g)
-action) on
{\mathbf{d}}
is a map of dg Lie algebras
\g\to\End(V)
where
V
is a dg vector space that represents
{\mathbf{d}}
in
D(\U(\g))
. Equivalently, this is a map of dg algebras
\U(\g)\to\End(V)
. Since
\HH^{\bullet}(V)
is 1-dimensional, the dg Lie algebra
\End(V)
is quasi-isomorphic to
{\mathbf{k}}
with the zero bracket and, assuming without loss of generality that
\g
is a cofibrant Lie algebra, we obtain that a
\g
-module structure on
{\mathbf{d}}
is realized by a dg Lie algebra map from
\g
to the abelian Lie algebra
{\mathbf{k}}
or as a dg algebra map
\U(\g)\to{\mathbf{k}}
. Another equivalent point of view, which does not assume cofibrancy of
\g
, is that the structure of a
\g
-module on a one-dimensional space is determined by an
L_{\infty}
-map
\g\to{\mathbf{k}}
or an
A_{\infty}
-map
\U(\g)\to{\mathbf{k}}
. This latter point of view will be used later on.

In any case, the data of an
L_{\infty}
-map
\g\to{\mathbf{k}}
are equivalent to that of an MC element in the pc dg algebra
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
. Since
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
is graded commutative, this is further equivalent to a CE 1-cocycle of
\g
, and cohomologous cocycles give rise to homotopic
L_{\infty}
-maps (or different
\g
-modules representing the same object in
D(\U(\g))
. We have the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a proper dg algebra Lie
\g
, denote by
\chi(\g)\in\CE^{1}(\g)
the CE cohomology class of
\g
corresponding to the dualizing module
{\mathbf{d}}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
M
be a proper dg
\g
-module where
\g
is a proper dg Lie algebra and consider
M^{!}:=\CE_{\bullet}(\g,M)
. Then there is a weak equivalence of
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
-comodules:

d(M^{!})\simeq\CE_{\bullet}(\g,{\mathbf{d}}\otimes M^{*}).

In particular (when
M
is the trivial
\g
-module
{\mathbf{k}}
), the comodule
d(\CE_{\bullet}(\g))
is weakly equivalent to the comodule whose dual pc module is
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)^{[\chi(\g)]}
, the twist of
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
by the MC element
\chi(\g)
.

5.
Dualizing complexes for graded Lie algebras

In this section we consider the case of a graded Lie algebra
\g
(i.e. a dg Lie algebra with vanishing differential). The dualizing module
\g
-module
{\mathbf{d}}(\g):=\RHom_{\U(\g)}({\mathbf{k}},\U(\g))
for
\g
is identified explicitly, in complete analogy with the ungraded case. This requires a calculation in coordinates with the CE complex
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
. Since the calculation in the graded case is quite involved, let us first describe the underlying strategy.

We have seen previously (Theorem
4.1
) that a spectral sequence argument shows that the cohomology of
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
is one-dimensional. Moreover, if
\g
is abelian, one can write an explicit nontrivial cocycle (which is often referred to as the Berezinian of
\g
). The action of
\g
on it is (in the abelian case), of course, trivial. The first step is to compute (in the non-abelian case) an explicit representative of a nontrivial cocycle of
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
. This requires an application of homological perturbation theory and the result is what we call a ‘deformed Berezinian’. The next step is to compute the
\U(\g)
action of the one-dimensional space spanned by the deformed Berezinian. For this, we use an explicit form of an
A_{\infty}
-minimal model of modules over an
A_{\infty}
-algebra
\U(\g)
. In our situation,
\U(\g)
is an ordinary graded algebra but its modules can still support a nontrivial
A_{\infty}
-structure. Nevertheless, it turns out that no higher
A_{\infty}
-correction terms are present. This is due to the fact that
\U(\g)
is a graded algebra (i.e. with vanishing differential) and so,
\CE^{\bullet}(\g,\U(\g))
has a canonical bigrading. The last step is just to compute the lowest (and only) term of the
A_{\infty}
-action of
\U(\g)
on the deformed Berezinian. This is where the trace of the adjoint action of
\g
appears.

We start by spelling out in detail the formulae for the CE differentials, including the sign conventions.

Let
\g
be finite dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra over the field
{\mathbf{k}}
and let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a homogeneous basis of
\g
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
, we denote
e_{i}[1]
by
x_{i}
.
Then
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is a basis of
\g[1]
and
\{x^{1},\ldots,x^{n}\}
the dual basis of
(\g[1])^{*}
, so that
\langle x^{i},x_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i}^{j}
.

Moreover, we have the structure constants,
\{N_{ij}^{k}\}
given by

(5.1)

\displaystyle[e_{i},e_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}N_{ij}^{k}e_{k}.

The CE coalgebra
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
has
\mathbf{S}^{c}(\g[1])
as its underlying cofree conilpotent cocommutative coalgebra and the codifferential
\delta_{\CE}
is determined by the formula

\displaystyle\delta_{\CE}(x_{i}x_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{|x_{i}|+1}N_{ij}^{k}x_{k}.

The CE algebra
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)=[\mathbf{S}^{c}(\g[1])]^{*}\cong\hat{\mathbf{S}}[(\g[1])^{*}]
is the linear dual of the CE coalgebra
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
, that is, the CE differential
d_{\CE}
is given by

(5.2)

\displaystyle\langle d_{\CE}(X),A\rangle=-(-1)^{|X|}\langle X,\delta_{\CE}(A)\rangle,\,\,\,\,\forall X\in\CE^{\bullet}(\g),A\in\CE_{\bullet}(\g).

Since the characteristic of the field
{\mathbf{k}}
is zero, the symmetric coinvariants
[(\g[1])^{\otimes n}]_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}
are isomorphic to the invariants
[(\g[1])^{\otimes n}]^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}
. More precisely, we can define mutually inverse maps
[
14
]

\displaystyle i_{n}:[(\g[1])^{\otimes n}]_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\to[(\g[1])^{\otimes n}]^{\mathbb{S}_{n}},\,\,\,\pi_{n}:[(\g[1])^{\otimes n}]^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\to[(\g[1])^{\otimes n}]_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}

by the formulae

(5.3)

\displaystyle i_{n}(\xi_{1}\ldots\xi_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}}\varepsilon(\sigma;\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\xi_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{\sigma(n)},

(5.4)

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\xi_{1}\ldots\xi_{n}.

By (
5.2
), we can assume that

(5.5)

\displaystyle d_{\CE}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq p,q\leq n}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{pq}^{k}x^{p}x^{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}},~\alpha_{pq}^{k}=(-1)^{|x^{p}||x^{q}|}\alpha_{qp}^{k}\in{\mathbf{k}}.

Let us express the above formula in terms of the constants
\{N^{k}_{pq}\}
rather than
\{\alpha^{k}_{pq}\}
. For
X=x^{k}
and
A=x_{i}x_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle d_{\CE}(x^{k}),x_{i}x_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{d_CE}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{1\leq p,q\leq n}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{pq}^{k}x^{p}x^{q},x_{i}x_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{iso-one}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq p,q\leq n}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{pq}^{k}x^{p}x^{q}\big(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}+(-1)^{|x_{i}||x_{j}|}x_{j}\otimes x_{i}\big)

\displaystyle{=}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{|x_{i}||x^{j}|}\alpha_{ij}^{k}x^{i}(x_{i})x^{j}(x_{j})+\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{|x_{i}||x_{j}|+|x_{j}||x^{i}|}\alpha_{ji}^{k}x^{j}(x_{j})x^{i}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x_{i}||x^{j}|}\alpha_{ij}^{k}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|x^{k}|}\langle x^{k},\delta_{\CE}(x_{i}x_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{codifferential}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x^{k}|+1}\langle x^{k},\sum_{p=1}^{n}(-1)^{|x_{i}|+1}N_{ij}^{p}x_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle{=}

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x^{k}|+|x_{i}|}a_{ij}^{k}.

We deduce that
\alpha_{ij}^{k}=(-1)^{1+(|x_{i}|+1)|x_{j}|}N_{ij}^{k}
. It follows that

(5.6)

\displaystyle d_{\CE}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq p,q\leq n}(-1)^{(|x_{p}|+1)|x_{q}|}N_{pq}^{k}x^{p}x^{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}.

From now on, we will distinguish the even and odd elements of the basis of the graded Lie algebra
\g
. We assume that
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n};\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}\}
is a homogeneous basis of
\g
and with elements
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
even and
\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}\}
odd. We denote
e_{i}[1]
by
x_{i}
and
\varepsilon_{i}[1]
by
y_{i}
respectively. Then
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
is a basis of
\g[1]
and
\{x^{1},\ldots,x^{n};y^{1},\ldots,y^{m}\}
the dual basis of
(\g[1])^{*}
, which is associated with the basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
of
\g[1]
.

Moreover, we have three families
\{a_{ij}^{k}\}
,
\{b_{ij}^{k}\}
and
\{c_{ij}^{k}\}
of structure constants given by the following formulae:

(5.7)

5.3.

Recall that an

-algebra structure on a graded vector space

is a continuous derivation

\hat{\mathbf{S}}(\g^{*}[-1])
having no constant term and such that

. For simplicity, we will assume that

which is a linear map

So, the derivation

and the requirement of the vanishing constant term implies that

. The components

of

which satisfy a collection of identities equivalent to the equation
[\ell,\ell]=0
. In particular, if

for

, we have a map
\g\to\g
of degree 1 squaring to zero, and a map
\Lambda^{2}\g\to\g
of degree 0 satisfying the Jacobi identity, plus a compatibility condition between the two. In other words, these data reduce to that of a dg Lie algebra (and if

this further simplifies to a graded Lie algebra).

Thus,
L_{\infty}
-algebras generalize dg Lie algebras. An important subclass of

-algebras is formed by

-algebras, for which the component

vanishes. We will write
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
for the graded pc algebra

supplied with the differential

and call it the representing pc dg algebra for the

-algebra
\g
. Note that this is consistent with our notations in the case when
\g
is a dg Lie algebra.

It is natural to ask whether the results of this section hold true under the assumption that
\g
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. A generalization of our methods to the
L_{\infty}
-case appears highly nontrivial, particularly because the methods we employed use in an essential way universal enveloping algebras of graded Lie algebras. For
L_{\infty}
-algebras, this notion is considerably more involved (see
[

,
29
,
20
]
). Furthermore, it is virtually certain that the formulas for the action of the
L_{\infty}
-universal algebra

\U(\g)
on the deformed Berezinian will include higher
A_{\infty}
-terms. Therefore,
we approach it from the Koszul dual point of view of dg modules over the pc dg algebra
\CE^{\bullet}(\g)
(or, equivalently, comodules over the dual dg coalgebra
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
).

The derivation
\ell
of

has divergence

which is a 1-cocycle in

(this is proved in the same way as for graded Lie algebras) and so, we can form the MC twist

The following conjectural result is an

-version of Corollary

Conjecture 5.25
.

Let
(\g,\ell)
be a minimal finite dimensional
L_{\infty}
-algebra over the field

Remark 5.26
.

The condition that

To see it, let us consider the 2-dimensional dg Lie abelian algebra spanned by two elements

with
|a|=0,|b|=1
and

. Then we have
\nabla(\ell)=0
,
\g\simeq{\mathbf{k}}
.

Since

and

It is interesting to observe that a small modification of the Lie algebra

Remark 5.27
.

It is not hard to see that

6.
Derivations and traces on algebras of formal Laurent series

In this section we introduce certain algebras of formal Laurent series and traces (or formal integrals) on them.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}[[y]][y^{-1}]
be the graded field of formal Laurent series in one variable
y
of even degree
|y|\in\mathbb{Z}
(note that unless
|y|=0
, this will, in fact, be a graded field of Laurent polynomials). It admits an inner product of graded degree
|y|

\langle f,g\rangle:=\res(fg)

where
f,g\in{\mathbf{k}}[[y]][y^{-1}]
and
\res
is the usual residue of a Laurent power series, the coefficient at
y^{-1}
. This inner product is nondegenerate; it exhibits
{\mathbf{k}}[[y]]\subset{\mathbf{k}}[[y]][y^{-1}]
as dual vector space to
y^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[y^{-1}]\subset{\mathbf{k}}[[y]][y^{-1}]
and
y^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[y^{-1}]
as the
continuous dual
to
{\mathbf{k}}[[y]]
. This pairing is also invariant:
\langle fg,h\rangle=\langle f,gh\rangle
for
f,g,h\in{\mathbf{k}}[[y]][y^{-1}]
and thus, is determined by a trace, or a Berezin integral
f\mapsto\langle f,1\rangle
on
{\mathbf{k}}[[y]][y^{-1}]
.

An
odd
analogue of
{\mathbf{k}}[x]
is the algebra
\Lambda(x)
; the exterior algebra on a generator
x
of odd degree. This two-dimensional algebra also admits a nondegenerate pairing of degree
-|x|
so that
\langle x,1\rangle=\langle 1,x\rangle=1
and pairings of
1
and
x
with themselves are zero. This pairing, is invariant and so, is determined by a trace or a Berezin integral on
\Lambda(x)
.

All this generalizes straightforwardly to several dimensions; we denote by
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]
the following algebra:

{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]:={\mathbf{k}}[[y^{1},\ldots,y^{m}]][({y^{1}})^{-1},\ldots,({y^{m}})^{-1}]

where
y^{i},i=1,\ldots,m
are variables of even degree.
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]
is clearly a certain completion of the tensor product of
n
copies of
{\mathbf{k}}[[y^{i}]][{(y^{i})}^{-1}],i=1,\ldots,m
. It inherits an invariant inner product: for
f,g\in{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]
their inner product
\langle f,g\rangle
is the coefficient at the monomial
({y^{1}})^{-1}\cdots(y^{m})^{-1}
of the product
fg
. The degree of the inner product
\langle-,-\rangle
is
{|\bf y|}:=|y^{1}|+\ldots+|y^{m}|
. This inner product is similarly determined by a nondegenerate trace, or a formal integral on
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]
.
Similarly,

{\bf\Lambda}({\bf x}):=\Lambda(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})

is the exterior algebra on the variables
x^{i},i=1,\ldots,n
of odd degree. As a product of graded Frobenius algebras,
\bf\Lambda(x)
, it is itself a graded Frobenius algebra whose inner product is determined by a trace or a Berezin integral on
\Lambda(x^{i}),i=1,\ldots,n
and the degree of the nondegenerate inner product is
{-|\bf x|}:=-(|x^{1}|+\ldots+|x^{n}|)
.

Finally, it is clear that the tensor product
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]\otimes{\bf\Lambda(x)}\cong{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y^{-1}}][{\bf x}]
also possesses a nondegenerate inner product or a trace (a formal integral). For
f\in{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]\otimes{\bf\Lambda(x)}
we will write this trace as
f\mapsto\int f\in{\mathbf{k}}
.

Remark 6.1
.

Consider the subalgebra
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]
inside
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]\otimes{\bf\Lambda(x)}
. It is clear that the given inner product pairs it nondegenerately with
{\bf y}^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[{\bf y^{-1}}]
, the subspace in
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y}^{-1}]
consisting of linear combinations of monomials in
(y^{i})^{-1}
having at least one factor of each
(y^{i})^{-1}
. Moreover, under this pairing,
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]
is identified with the linear dual of
{\bf y}^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[{\bf y^{-1}}]
and the latter subspace is identified with the
continuous dual
of
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]
. Also, the restriction of
\langle,\rangle
on the finite-dimensional space
\bf\Lambda(\bf x)
is nondegenerate; more specifically,
\bf\Lambda(\bf x)
is identified with the linear dual of
{\bf\Lambda}({\bf x})[|\bf x|]
. Putting this together, we obtain an isomorphism

(6.1)

\big({\bf y}^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[{\bf y^{-1}}]\otimes{\bf\Lambda({\bf x}})\big)^{*}\cong{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][-|{\bf y}|]\otimes{\bf\Lambda({\bf x}})[|\bf x|]

induced by the inner product
\langle,\rangle
. This implies, in particular, that
\big({\bf y}^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[{{\bf y}^{-1}}]\otimes\Lambda({\bf x})\big)[|\bf x|-|\bf y|]
has a graded coalgebra structure, obtained as the continuous dual of the pc algebra structure of
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]\otimes\bf\bf{\Lambda(\bf x)}
.

The following formal analogue of Stokes’ theorem is well-known (and easy to prove).

Proposition 6.2
.

For any vector field
\xi
, one has
\int\nabla(\xi)=0
. Conversely, if
\int f=0
, then
f=\nabla(\xi)
for some vector field
\xi
.

Given a vector field (a derivation)
\xi
viewed as an operator on
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y^{-1}}][{\bf x}]
, one can ask for a formula for the
adjoint operator

\xi^{*}
defined by the formula

\langle\xi^{*}(f),g\rangle=-(-1)^{|\xi||f|}\langle f,\xi(g)\rangle

where
f,g\in{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y^{-1}}][{\bf x}]
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Given a vector field
\xi
, its adjoint operator is given by the formula
\xi^{*}(f)=\xi(f)+\nabla(\xi)f
, where
f\in{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y^{-1}}][{\bf x}]
.

Proof.

Expand the expression
\nabla(fg\xi)
taking into account Stokes’ formula and (
5.27
). For any vector field
\xi
and functions
f,g\in{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]][{\bf y^{-1}}][{\bf x}]
, we have

\displaystyle\int\nabla(fg\xi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:divergenceproperty}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\int(fg\nabla\xi+(-1)^{(|f|+|g|)|\xi|}\xi(fg))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int fg\nabla\xi+(-1)^{(|f|+|g|)|\xi|}\int\xi(f)g+(-1)^{|g||\xi|}\int f\xi(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\xi||g|}\langle f\nabla\xi,g\rangle+(-1)^{(|f|+|g|)|\xi|}\langle\xi(f),g\rangle+(-1)^{|g||\xi|}\langle f,\xi(g)\rangle.

By Proposition
6.2
, one can deduce that

\displaystyle\langle f,\xi(g)\rangle{=}-\langle f\nabla\xi,g\rangle-(-1)^{|\xi||f|}\langle\xi(f),g\rangle.

Finally, by the definition of the adjoint operator of
\xi^{*}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\langle\xi^{*}(f),g\rangle=(-1)^{|\xi||f|}\langle f\nabla\xi,g\rangle+\langle\xi(f),g\rangle

which implies that
\xi^{*}(f)=\xi(f)+\nabla(\xi)f
as claimed.
∎

Let us write
\bf k
for the multi-index
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}
and denote by
(\bf y^{k})
the ideal in
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]
generated by
the elements
y^{k_{1}},\ldots,y^{k_{m}}
.
We have a finite-dimensional
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]\otimes\bf\Lambda(\bf x)
-module
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y}^{\bf{k}})\otimes\bf\Lambda(\bf x)
. Then the following result holds.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\xi
be a derivation of
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]\otimes\bf\Lambda(\bf x)
that preserves the ideal
(\bf y^{k})
(and so, restricts to
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y^{k}})\otimes\bf\Lambda(\bf x))
. Then there is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces

\big({\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y^{k}})\otimes{\bf\Lambda}({\bf x})\big)^{*}\cong\big({\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y^{k}})\otimes{\bf\Lambda}({\bf x})\big)[|{\bf x}|-|{\bf y}|].

Under this isomorphism, the adjoint to the operator
\xi
acting on
\big({\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y^{k}})\otimes{\bf\Lambda}(\bf x)\big)^{*}
, corresponds to
\xi+\nabla(\xi)
acting on
\big({\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y^{k}})\otimes{\bf\Lambda}(\bf x)\big)[|{\bf x}|-|{\bf y}|]
.

Proof.

We have an isomorphism of
{\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]\otimes\bf\Lambda(\bf x)
-modules:

\displaystyle\big({\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]\otimes{\bf\Lambda}(\bf x)\big)^{*}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Iso-coal-pc-alge}}}{{\cong}}

\displaystyle\big({\bf y}^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[{\bf y}^{-1}]\otimes{\bf\Lambda}(\bf x)\big)[|{\bf x}|-|{\bf y}|].

It follows that
\big({\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y^{k}})\otimes{\bf\Lambda}(\bf x)\big)^{*}
is isomorphic to the submodule in
\big({\bf y}^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[{\bf y}^{-1}]\otimes{\bf\Lambda}(\bf x)\big)[|{\bf x}|-|{\bf y}|]
annihilated by
(\bf y^{k})
. This submodule is identified with
\big({\mathbf{k}}[[{\bf y}]]/({\bf y^{k}})\otimes{\bf\Lambda}(\bf x)\big)[|{\bf x}|-|{\bf y}|]
and the claim about the action of the adjoint of
\xi
follows from Proposition
6.3
.
∎

7.
Derived linear duality for comodules over a coalgebra

In this section we develop some methods for the computation of a derived linear dual
d(M)
of a dg comodule
M
over a dg coalgebra
C
.

One way to do it (used in Section
5
) is to pass from
M
to the Koszul dual module
M^{!}
over the cobar-construction
\Omega(C)
and use the fact that
d(M)
corresponds to
\RHom_{\Omega(C)}(M^{!},\Omega(C))
; if
C
is the dg coalgebra
\B(A)
or
\CE_{\bullet}(\g)
for a dg algebra
A
or a dg Lie algebra
\g
respectively, then one can use
A
or
\U(\g)
in place of
\Omega(C)
. In this section we discuss ways of computing
d(M)
directly, staying in the model category of dg
C
-comodules.

It will be convenient to switch from
C
-comodules to pc
A:=C^{*}
-modules.
Note that the category of pc
A
-modules is anti-equivalent to the category of
C
-comodules and so, it has a model structure whose homotopy category is anti-equivalent to that of
D^{\co}(C)
. We will denote this homotopy category by
D^{\co}(A)
and refer to it as the coderived category of
A
. Let
M
be a dg
C
-comodule. Then one can assume
M=\colim_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}
and
\dim M_{\alpha}<+\infty
.
The homotopy limit of a
C
-comodule
M
correspond to the homotopy colimit of the pc
A
-module
M^{*}
and so we are interested in computing
(\hocolim_{\text{pc}})_{\alpha}N_{\alpha}
. Here
N_{\alpha}:=M_{\alpha}^{*}
is the pc
A
-module which is linearly dual (in the ordinary, naive sense) to the finite-dimensional comodule
M_{\alpha}
. Then the derived linear dual of
M
is
d(M)=\big((\hocolim_{\text{pc}})_{\alpha}N_{\alpha}\big)^{*}
.

To this end, let us introduce the notion of a derived profinite completion. The category of pc
A
-modules has a forgetful functor
F
into the category of discrete
A
-modules. It takes weak equivalences of pc
A
-modules into quasi-isomorphisms and preserves fibrations (which are in both cases surjective maps). It also has a left adjoint, the profinite completion functor

M\mapsto M^{\vee}:=\lim_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}
where the inverse limit is taken over all finite-dimensional
A
-module quotients
M_{\alpha}
of
M
. It follows that the forgetful functor
F
is right Quillen and the profinite completion is left Quillen. We will denote its left derived functor as
M\mapsto{\mathbf{L}^{\vee}}(M)
; it can be computed by replacing
M
by a quasi-isomorphic cofibrant
A
-module
LM
, and then applying the profinite completion functor. Note that although the counit of the adjunction

M\to M^{\vee}

is an isomorphism of pc modules (completing a pc module amounts to doing nothing), it is not true that the
derived
counit map
\mathbf{L}^{\vee}(M)\to M
is a weak equivalence because a cofibrant pc
A
-module need not be cofibrant as a discrete
A
-module.

Example 7.1
.

Let
A:=\Lambda(x)
, the exterior algebra on one generator
x
with
|x|=1
and zero differential. Let
M
be the free
A
-module of rank
1
but with the nontrivial differential
d(1)=x
. As a pc
A
-module,
M
is cofibrant and it is nontrivial, e.g.
M\otimes^{L,\text{II}}_{A}{\mathbf{k}}\cong{\mathbf{k}}\neq 0
where
\otimes^{L,\text{II}}_{A}
stands for the derived tensor product on pc
A
-modules. At the same time, as a discrete dg
A
-module,
M
is not cofibrant, and its cofibrant replacement is zero since
M
is acyclic. Thus,
{\mathbf{L}^{\vee}}(M)\simeq 0
.

However, if (the weak equivalence class of) the pc
A
-module
M
lies in the thick subcategory of the coderived category of pc
A
-modules generated by
A
itself, this difficulty does not arise. Moreover, let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
X
be a set
of objects of
\mathcal{T}
. We denote
\mathbf{thick}_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
the thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
generated by
X
, which is the smallest full triangulated subcategory
of
\mathcal{T}
containing
X
and closed under direct summands. It can be obtained as the
closure of
X
under shifts, cones and direct summands.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
M
be a pc module over a pc algebra
A
that is lies in

\mathbf{thick}_{\text{D}^{\co}(A)}(A)
.
Then
\mathbf{L}^{\vee}(M)\simeq M
.

Proof.

Note that, since the forgetful functor from pc
A
-modules to discrete
A
-modules preserves finite direct sums, homotopy fibre sequences and retracts,
M
lies in
\mathbf{thick}_{{D}(A)}(A)
viewed as a
discrete

A
-module.
The statement of the lemma is obvious if
M=A
since
A
is cofibrant as a discrete module over itself. The general case then follows since the functor
\mathbf{L}^{\vee}
preserves finite direct sums, homotopy fibre sequences and retracts.
∎

Let us now consider the case when a pc dg algebra
A
is finite-dimensional. Admittedly, this is not the case especially relevant to this paper since universal enveloping algebras are typically infinite-dimensional; however this is when derived profinite completion admits a very explicit description and so we record here the relevant conclusions.

The following proposition describes the derived completion functor on
A
-modules. Recall that we denoted by
F
the forgetful functor from pc
A
-modules to
A
-modules.

Proposition 7.3
.

For any dg module
M
over a finite-dimensional dg algebra
A
we have a natural isomorphism

(7.1)

M^{\vee}\cong\big(F(M^{*})\big)^{*}.

Proof.

Note that the functor of linear duality
M\mapsto M^{*}
determines an equivalence of categories

A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits\to({\text{pc}}~A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits)^{\op}

with the quasi-inverse functor being the continuous dual
N\mapsto N^{*}
where
N
is a dg pc
A
-module. (Here finite-dimensionality of
A
is used: if
\dim A=\infty
then
M^{*}
is not necessarily a pc
A
-module.)

Thus, to demonstrate (
7.1
), it suffices to show that
(M^{\vee})^{*}\cong M^{*}
.
We have

(M^{\vee})^{*}\cong\varinjlim(M_{\alpha})^{*}

where
M_{\alpha}
range over all finite-dimensional dg
A
-module quotients of
M
and thus,
M_{\alpha}^{*}
range over all finite-dimensional dg
A
-submodules of
M^{*}
. Since
A
is finite-dimensional, any dg
A
-module is a union of its finite-dimensional submodules and we are done.
∎

Given a dg
A
-module
M
, denote by
\widetilde{M}
the cofibrant replacement of
M
. Then the following result is immediate from Proposition
7.3
.

Corollary 7.4
.

For a dg module
A
over a finite-dimensional dg algebra
A
, its derived profinite completion
{\bf L}^{\vee}(A)
is weakly equivalent to the dg pc
A
-module
(F(\widetilde{M})^{*})^{*}
.

The coderived category of pc dg
A
-modules is related to the opposite to the derived category of
A
:

Proposition 7.5
.

The functor
M\mapsto M^{*}:A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits\to({\text{pc}}~A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits)^{\op}
is a left Quillen functor whose adjoint right Quillen functor is the continuous dual
N\mapsto N^{*}
. The latter preserves arbitrary weak equivalences (and thus, coincides with its own left derived functor). The derived unit map
M\to\left((\widetilde{M})^{*}\right)^{*}
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

The functor
N\mapsto N^{*}:({\text{pc}}~A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits)^{\op}\to A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits
preserves quasi-isomorphisms and thus,
a fortiori
, takes weak equivalences of pc dg
A
-modules to quasi-isomorphisms of
A
-modules. It also takes fibrations in
({\text{pc}}~A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits)^{\op}
(which are injective maps) to surjections (i.e. fibrations in
\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits-A
). Thus, it is a right Quillen functor and its adjoint is left Quillen. The statement about the derived unit map is likewise clear.
∎

Remark 7.6
.

The Quillen adjunction of Proposition
7.5
is
not
a Quillen equivalence despite being an equivalence of 1-categories (in particular, it does not follow that the homotopy categories of
A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits
and
({\text{pc}}~A\operatorname{\!-Mod}\nolimits)^{\op}
are equivalent.) Indeed, the module
M
of Example
7.1
is not weakly equivalent to zero as a pc
\Lambda
-module, but is acyclic and so is its (continuous) dual
M^{*}
, which makes him trivial in the derived category of
\Lambda
.

Next, note that any dg comodule
M
over a dg coalgebra
C
naturally has the structure of a
C^{*}
-module (though not necessarily pc). This is clear when
M
is finite-dimensional; in this case the coaction map
M\to C\otimes M
is equivalent to a continuous dg algebra map
C^{*}\to\End(M^{*})\cong\End(M)
. The general case follows since
M
is a union of its finite-dimensional subcomodules.

Example 7.7
.

Consider the following short exact sequence of
{\mathbf{k}}[[x]]
-modules

{\mathbf{k}}[[x]]\to{\mathbf{k}}[[x]][x^{-1}]\to x^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[x^{-1}].

Here the
{\mathbf{k}}[[x]]
-module structure on
x^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[x^{-1}]
is obtained by the isomorphism
x^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[x^{-1}]\cong{\mathbf{k}}[[x]][x^{-1}]/{\mathbf{k}}[[x]]
. Furthermore,
x^{-1}{\mathbf{k}}[x^{-1}]
is a coalgebra whose linear dual is the pc algebra
{\mathbf{k}}[[x]]
. As such, it is a comodule over itself and also a non-pc
{\mathbf{k}}[[x]]
-module.

The following result gives a description of the derived dual comodule in favorable cases.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
C
be a dg coalgebra and
M
be a
C
-comodule. Assume that
M=\bigcup_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}
where finite-dimensional
C
-subcomodules
M_{\alpha}
lie in the thick subcategory of the coderived category of
C
-comodules generated by
C
. Then

d(M)\underset{\text{w.e.}}{\simeq}\big(\mathbf{L}^{\vee}(M)\big)^{*}.

In other words, we view
M
as a
C^{*}
-module, take its derived profinite completion (making it into a pc
C^{*}
-module) and then its continuous dual, converting back to a
C
-comodule which is weakly equivalent to the derived linear dual of

Proof.

Let

be the pc

\displaystyle{\hocolim}_{\text{pc}}(M^{*}_{\alpha})^{*}

\displaystyle\cong{\hocolim}_{\text{pc}}(M_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\underset{\text{w.e.}}{\simeq}\mathbf{L}^{\vee}(\hocolim M_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\underset{\text{w.e.}}{\simeq}\mathbf{L}^{\vee}(\colim M_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\underset{\text{w.e.}}{\simeq}\mathbf{L}^{\vee}(M).

Here

refers to the homolotopy colimit taken in the category of pc

Let

Let

be a finite-dimensional

have dimension

\CE^{\bullet}(\g)

{\mathbf{k}}[[y^{1},\ldots,y^{m}]]\otimes\Lambda(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})

Let

Lemma 7.11
.

Given a pc dg algebra

and a pc dg

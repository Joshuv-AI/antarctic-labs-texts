The social support, healthy lifestyle, subjective well-being, and health-related quality of life among university students
Authors: Riza Hayati Ifroh, Xiaosong Gai, Muhamad Aziz Rabiautsani, Xue Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The health-related quality of life and subjective well-being of young people need to be confirmed at the population level by identifying protective factors such as social support and their current healthy lifestyle. This study aims to examine a healthy lifestyle as a mediator of the associations between perceived social support to subjective well-being and health-related quality of life and also to analyze the associations between all these variables.METHODS AND MATERIALS:This study was conducted by cross-sectional survey of university students (N = 348) derived from ten cities/municipalities in East Kalimantan, Indonesia. Recruitment of respondents was performed through convenience sampling and by sharing open questionnaire links on integrated social media. The data were collected between April and June 2023 and taken from an online self-administered questionnaire. Instruments used consisted of demographics, the multidimensional scale of perceived social support, overall healthy lifestyle, BBC subjective well-being scale, and EQ-5D-5L (EuroQol) for health-related quality of life. Data have been analyzed using descriptive statistics, T-test and ANOVA, Pearson’s correlation, and multiple linear regression.RESULTS:The results showed a significant association between the domain of perceived social support, healthy lifestyle, subjective well-being, and health-related quality of life. This research also proves that a healthy lifestyle can be a mediator of the association between perceived social support and health-related quality of life and also subjective well-being among university students.CONCLUSIONS:These findings suggest that youth lifestyle indicators are one of the domains that can be considered for use in research on subjective well-being and health-related quality of life, which are supported by perceived social support at the population level.

Introduction

Well-being–related personal satisfaction is one of the multideterminant concepts that is connected with a person’s physical, mental, and social working. This examines that a healthy lifestyle has a positive impact on life goals in community groups and also reduces the risk of degenerative diseases in the future and maintains the quality of human life.[123] This understanding is closely related to subjective well-being, which describes active reactions and evaluations of one’s experiences to produce health values at the population and community level.[4] Subjective well-being has a statistical relationship with the concept of physical health and social relationships. Several studies describe that there is a link between psychological aspects and the health conditions of young people.[567]

In terms of population health, the number of total deaths caused by noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) is 41 million of 55 million deaths in the world and is dominated by low- or middle-income countries.[89] Unhealthy behavior or sedentary life is one of the main risk factors for NCDs. Negative habits such as eating unhealthy food, lacking exercise or physical activity, less sleep, and others are all risk factors for NCDs.[10] Encouraging adolescents in middle-income countries to adopt healthy lifestyles can reduce the burden of NCDs.[11]

The roles and linkages between subjective well-being (SWB) and also health-related quality of life (HRQoL) have been widely considered for youth population groups, in particular in creating healthier societies.[121314] HRQoL and SWB are influenced by personal, psychosocial, and behavioral factors, and previous studies prove that social support is a protective factor at the community level.[115] Social support was one of the variables that had a statistical relationship to the conditions of obese and overweight youth, health, and quality of life. The most influential sources of support are parents, peers, teachers, and classmates.[416] Social support may also drive individuals into healthy lifestyles in different ways.[17] In addition, healthy lifestyle behavior in college students is also influenced by their scientific background, and health profession students have better health behavior than non-health professionals.[1819]

In the concept of disease prevention at the community level, HRQoL and SWB variables are determinants of monitoring individual health status and are closely related to morbidity, mortality, and later life.[20] East Kalimantan Province is among the three highest rates of noncommunicable diseases (heart disease, stroke, and hypertension) in Indonesia.[2122] In data on community health behavior in one of the study locations (Samarinda), there is a habit of consuming high-fat foods and low physical activity, especially among young and productive age groups, particularly among university students.[21]

The important role of social support is to support the improvement of a healthy lifestyle and encourage increased HRQoL and SWB in the younger generation in East Kalimantan. Therefore, the currently limited analysis of HRQoL and SWB at the individual level needs to be explored and identified in more detail on a scientific basis.[1316] In general, this research examined the relationship between social support, healthy lifestyle as a moderator, on HRQoL, and also SWB among university students in East Kalimantan. It is hypothesized that there are differences and associations between the domain of each variable among university students in East Kalimantan. It is also hypothesized that perceived social support is associated with health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and subjective well-being (SWB), mediating by a healthy lifestyle.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This type of research is a cross-sectional design for the observation and measurement of social support, healthy lifestyle, subjective well-being, and health-related quality of life (HRQoL).

Study participants and sampling

The study population consisted of first- to third-year students who were enrolled in different universities and who live in East Kalimantan. The sampling technique used convenience sampling, with the sample size in this study being 348 university students and also vocational higher education students (applied bachelor).

Data collection tool and technique

Data collection in the last of April to June 2023, the data were taken from an online self-administered questionnaire (kuesio.id) by a survey platform from Indonesia. The measurement for sociodemographics includes age, gender, religion, geographical location, type of residence, height, weight, level of study, university, majoring of study, and allowance per month.[416] This self-report scale has been used to measure sources of social support felt by research participants such as family, friends, and other closest people with the multidimensional scale of perceived social support.[23] Ratings were used with a 7-point Likert scale from 1 (very strongly disagree) to 7 (very strongly agree). The overall score of participants was averaged and ranged from 1 to 7, with a higher score indicated on a higher level of social support. The overall internal consistency for this scale will be found to be between 0.80 and 0.95, and three subscales will be seen: significant person, family, and friends.[42324] In the current study, the internal consistency (Cronbach’s ⍺) of this tool was 0.883.

In the overall healthy lifestyle variable, adapting healthy behavior to students consists of 19 question items, with three rating scales: never,[1] sometimes/occasionally,[2] and almost always.[3] The overall score of a healthy lifestyle for each respondent has then been summed up and averaged. The sub-indicators analyzed included aspects of physical activity, weight monitoring, daily food consumption, enjoying life, alcohol use and drug consumption, and sleep time.[2526] The internal consistency (Cronbach’s ⍺) of this variable was 0.756.

The variable of subjective well-being uses the BBC-SWB questionnaire. This instrument is a revised edition consisting of 24 items that can measure three fundamental dimensions such as psychological well-being, physical health and well-being and relationships. A 5-point Likert scale consists of not at all,[1] a little,[2] moderate,[3] very much,[4] and very much.[5] The overall score of participants was averaged and ranged,[12345] with the highest score of 5 reflecting better well-being,[127] and the Cronbach’s ⍺ for this tool was 0.926.

Participants’ health-related quality of life (HRQoL) was measured using the EQ-5D-5L (EuroQol) (Mo PKH et al., 2022). The instrument EQ-5D-5L is made up of two parts: the EQ visual analog scale (EQ VAS) and the EQ descriptive system. EQ-5D graphic framework, respondents demonstrated their well-being status on five aspects: mobility, self-care, regular activities, discomfort, pain, and anxiety and depression on five levels, each dimension was evaluated: There are no problems, slight problems, moderate problems, severe problems, and extreme of problems. This tool had a Cronbach’s of 0.694 (0.58-0.71 for subdomains). On a vertical visual analog scale for the EQ-VAS, participants were asked to self-rate their health, with the endpoints labelled “the best health you can imagine” (100) and “the worst health you can imagine”(0).[14]

The baseline characteristics and study variables were described with descriptive statistics including means, standard deviations, frequencies, and percentages. Contrasts in factors because of the overall attributes of members were examined using independent t-tests and examination of difference (ANOVA) Bonferroni. The association between factors was analyzed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient, and after the essential suspicions of relapse investigation were evaluated, multiple linear regression was used to determine the factors that predicted health-related quality of life and subjective well-being. Data analysis was used statistical software (SPSS).[28]

Ethical consideration

The study was approved by The Health Research Ethics Commission (KEPK) of East Kalimantan Ministry of Health Poltekkes with No: DP. 04.03/7.1/086889/2023. It provides details about the ethical review process, demonstrating that the study was conducted in accordance with established ethical guidelines and principles.

Stipend per month, for the two groups, the majority is above 500,000 IDR per month. As many as 58.6% of the health majors had a normal body mass index (BMI), and 21.3% were underweight, whereas 56.3% of the nonmedical participants had a normal BMI, and 20.1% were underweight [Table 1].

Discussion

This study has presented significant associations between social support on health-related quality of life and subjective well-being, which are mediated by healthy lifestyle variables among university students in East Kalimantan, Indonesia. In particular, it is ensured that a healthy lifestyle acts as a mediator between perceived social support in supporting health-related quality of life and subjective well-being among young people. Based on educational background, health science students have a normal body mass index with a higher percentage than non-health students, while the percentage of overweight and obese students in non-health students is higher. This is in line with previous research that students have the opportunity to behave unhealthily,[29] and students majoring in health can have a higher awareness of living a healthy life to get a healthy body and ideal body weight.[30]

In the descriptive observation and analysis of differences in the score of the perceived social support variable of this study, in particular, the support most received by students at the university is from family and significant others. Meanwhile, there are differences in perceived social behavior based on health and non-health majors, as well as the participant’s average monthly allowance. This is in accordance with previous studies,[31] that students need the social support to prevent stress levels when entering a new phase of learning life in college, it is also added that finance is factors providing higher social networking opportunities and take treatments for coping stress among young.[32]

Based on this study, there are differences in healthy lifestyles, students majoring in health have a higher healthy lifestyle than nonmedical students. The exposure to health information is more received by students majoring in health, so that they will have more awareness and vigilance of the dangers of unhealthy lifestyles.[33] In addition, young people who study in health majors have higher literacy skills related to digital health information, and they can filter information and choose the right information according to their scientific capacity in terms of physical, social, and mental health during their studies.[3435] The results also show that the higher the students’ monthly stipend, the impact on their healthy lifestyle. The previous studies support that those who have the high financial ability have the possibility of choosing a more varied healthy food menu, although the opportunity to consume fast food is also high.[3637]

In the differences analysis, subjective well-being and health-related quality of life between male and female students have statistical differences, this is supported by a previous study that gender contributes to the level of subjective well-being in a person with several factors such as potential stress in daily life, mood, environmental changes, and others.[313839] It was also found that health-related quality of life among female students was lower than male subjects. Previous research explains that young female have a higher risk of health problems because they are influenced by social and psychological factors, nutrition, and menstrual health conditions, all of which are complex factors faced.[40]

There is a statistical relationship in all the variables of this research, which is supported by previous research on similar variables statistically have a correlation and mutually reinforce each other among youth groups consistently.[141] Adolescence tends to be at the stage of individual psychological development that prefers autonomy in making decisions including health conditions,[4243] as well as the value of rationalism that belongs to the young group that they need skills in formulating a lifestyle that can be adapted and maintained until they become more mature in the process of maturing themselves. Therefore, their healthy behavior is related to better subjective well-being and health-related quality of life. Social support provided by parents and significant persons can be explained in encouraging an increase in health-related quality of life and subjective well-being through a healthy lifestyle for young people.[44] In line with the results of this study, family support such as informative and factual messages, as well as showing more personal and familial motivation, is more meaningful.[44546]

The general description has illustrated that a healthy lifestyle can help maintain health-related quality of life and subjective well-being of students in tertiary institutions, and it is of particular concern that social support and lifestyle variables (nutrition and physical activity) can have a good influence on variables discussed independently. Previous studies support this finding that lifestyle aspects that should be encouraged in students are related to nutrition, physical activity, and health responsibility.[47] The findings of this study encourage increased support from universities to create integrated campus facilities to support students’ healthy lifestyles and increase awareness of health responsibilities, and sustainable can improve the quality of life and subjective well-being.[4748]

Limitation and recommendation

A major limitation of this study is the recruitment strategy resulted in a sample that was not proportionally representative of male and female students and religious aspects, so this affected the generalization for grouping based on the variables studied, especially in sociodemographics. A lack of validated Indonesian language instruments, as well as local cultural suitability for data collection and the participants’ origin setting (rural or urban). In future studies, it is anticipated that more in-depth instruments with subdomains would have provided additional information on aspects of a healthy lifestyle and health-related quality of life that are not covered in this study. Data on variables that more specifically can explain which restrictions (e.g., aspects of social relations, health facilities, etc.) were not included in this study that have an impact on aspects of quality of life related to health.

Conclusion

This study has revealed statistical differences based on research variables in the subgroup of participants. In the variable perceived social support, there are differences based on the major of study (health or non-health studies) and stipend per month. In the healthy lifestyle aspect, there are differences based on the major of studies and also the stipend per month. In the subjective well-being and health-related quality of life variables, there are differences based on gender between male and female students. This study also shows that there is a statistical association between the domain of each variable among university students in East Kalimantan. This research also proves that a healthy lifestyle can be a mediator of the association between perceived social support and subjective well-being and also health-related quality of life among university students in East Kalimantan, Indonesia. Prospective or qualitative studies are warranted to confirm in-depth causal associations among the variables in the young group in future research.

6 Antitumor Activity of 177Lu-PSMA-617 in PSMA-Positive Metastatic Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma (ccRCC): Results from a Phase 2 Trial (LASER)
Authors: Praful Ravi, Wanling Xie, Shir Hazut Krauthammer, Liliana Ascione, Luke Arsenault, Liliane Kabarame, Nicole LaBrecque, Hina Shah, Stephanie Berg, Atish Choudhury, Bradley McGregor, Michael Serzan, Wenxin Xu, Srinivas Viswanathan, Xiao Wei, Toni Choueiri, Heather Jacene
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere is an unmet need for therapeutic targets beside tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI), immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) and HIF inhibitors in ccRCC. PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) is expressed on the neovasculature of solid tumors, including ccRCC. 177Lu-PSMA-617 (LuPSMA) is a beta-emitting PSMA-targeting radiopharmaceutical that has proven efficacy in PSMA-positive prostate cancer. We sought to evaluate the efficacy and safety of LuPSMA in advanced, PSMA-positive ccRCC.MethodsLASER is a single-center, investigator-initiated open-label trial conducted at Dana-Farber Cancer Institute, Boston. Eligible patients had metastatic ccRCC who had previously received ≥1 TKI and ≥1 ICI, unless they had a significant contraindication to ICI. Participants were required to have PSMA-positive disease on screening PSMA-PET/CT, with ≥1 lesion with uptake > liver and no or limited PSMA-negative lesions (≤25% of lesions with uptake < liver). LuPSMA was administered at a standard dose of 7.4GBq every 6 weeks for up to 6 cycles; interim PSMA-PET/CT was performed prior to cycle 3 and dosing continued if ≥ 1 avid lesion was seen. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR), as defined per RECIST1.1. A two-stage design was used, with 9 patients enrolled in stage 1 and a further 15 patients planned to accrue to stage 2 if ≥ 1 partial or complete response was observed in stage 1. The design assumed a 5% ORR as unacceptably low and 25% ORR as clinically meaningful; there was 90% power to detect an ORR of 25% with 1-sided a = 0.1 and 63% probability of early discontinuation if true ORR is 5%.ResultsA total of 21 patients were screened between July 2025 and February 2026; 9 patients were eligible and received therapy in stage 1. Median age was 73 and participants had received a median of 4 prior lines of therapy. The median number of LuPSMA cycles received was 1, with 4 patients receiving 2 or more cycles. There were no radiographic responses (ORR 0%). 2 patients had stable disease, 3 had progressive disease and 4 patients discontinued therapy after 1 cycle due to clinical progression; 1 patient completed all 6 cycles with ongoing stable disease. Median progression-free survival was 2.3 months (95% CI 0.5-5.3), and 3 deaths have been reported to date with a 6-month overall survival rate of 62% (95% CI 21-86). Therapy was well tolerated with no grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs); the most common grade 1-2 TRAEs were dry mouth (44%, all grade 1) and fatigue (44%). Data pertaining to imaging characteristics of treated patients will be presented. The trial was halted for futility at stage 1.ConclusionsLuPSMA was well-tolerated but had no single-agent activity in heavily-pretreated patients with PSMA-positive advanced ccRCC. Future work evaluating radiopharmaceuticals in RCC should focus on alternative targets as well as combinations with standard therapies.

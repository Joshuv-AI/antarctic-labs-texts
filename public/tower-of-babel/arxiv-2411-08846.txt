On the Number of Crossings and Bouncings of a Diffusion at a Sticky Threshold
Alexis Anagnostakis, Sara Mazzonetto

Abstract
In this paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of the number of crossings by a one-dimensional diffusion of a threshold where the process exhibits stickiness. We distinguish three types of crossings and show that to each type corresponds a distinct asymptotic regime for the respective number of crossings statistic. We introduce notions of bouncing as the symmetric counterparts to crossings and show that the corresponding number of bouncings statistics share the same asymptotic properties as their crossings counterparts. We first prove the results for sticky Brownian motion, then extend them to sticky-reflected Brownian motion (where only bouncing is possible) and to sticky diffusions. As an application, we propose consistent estimators for the stickiness parameter of sticky diffusions and sticky-reflected Brownian motion.

Introduction

The topic of threshold crossing by a process has been extensively studied in probabilistic literature.
When the process is a one–dimensional diffusion that solves a classical stochastic differential equation (SDE) with sufficiently regular coefficients, the number of crossings of a threshold
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
provides consistent estimations of the local time at
\zeta
(e.g.
[
6
,
22
,
23
]
; for a definition of local time see Section
2.1
).
Similar results have been developed for diffusions with a skew threshold or with discontinuous coefficients, allowing for estimation of localized features of a diffusion, such as the localized diffusion coefficient (e.g.,
[
16
]
),
the skew parameter
[
27
,
28
]
and the oscillation jump of the diffusion coefficient
[
28
]
.
Despite these advances, the limit behavior at a sticky threshold remains not well understood.

In this paper, we study the limit behavior of the number of the threshold
0
by the time-discretization of the sticky Brownian motion, as the discretization step vanishes.
The sticky Brownian motion is the diffusion process that behaves like the standard Brownian motion away from
0
and spends a positive amount of time at
0
upon contact (see a definition in Section
2.2
). The set of times at which the process is at
0
forms a totally disconnected random set of positive Lebesgue measure. This contrasts with the standard Brownian motion, whose zeroes form an uncountable, totally disconnected set, but of Lebesgue measure
0
(see, e.g.,
[
31
, Proposition III.3.12]
).

To the best of our knowledge, the only established result about number of crossings at the sticky threshold 0 by a sticky Brownian motion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is due to Gikhman.
The result states that for every
t\geq 0
as
n
goes to infinity, the statistic

\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}<0\}}

(1.1)

converges in law to some non-trivial discrete random variable (see
[
30
, §8]
, stated in the next Theorem
2.1
). This contrasts with the standard Brownian motion, where this statistic, renormalized by
\sqrt{\pi/2n}
, converges in probability to its local time at
0
.

In the case of sticky Brownian motion, we distinguish between three types of crossings and establish the limit behavior of each respective re-normalized number of crossings statistic.
Unlike the Brownian motion, we show that the normalizing sequence depends on the type of crossing considered.
One type of crossings provides a statistic whose renormalized limit matches the one observed in the Brownian case.
Understanding the limit behavior of all these statistics is a crucial step before tackling the open question of the convergence rates to the local time at a sticky threshold.
For non-sticky processes, these have been obtained in
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
for classical diffusions and to
[
28
]
for skew threshold
diffusions.

Ultimately, establishing such rates would enable us to obtain similar rates for the stickiness parameter estimators established here and in
[
3
,
5
]
, which are built on such approximations.
To highlight the importance of the estimation problem, let us mention a few applications of sticky diffusions.
These processes are used in finance to model price dynamics on asset subject to takeover offers (see
[
12
]
). In quantum mechanics, to model motions of particles near a source of emission (see
[
13
]
). In classical mechanics, to model motions of coarse particles in colloids (see
[
9
]
and references therein). And in epidemics, to model concentrations of pathogens in an organism (see
[
11
]
).
During an infection, these concentrations are random and significant, whereas off-infection, they keep close to zero.

Another contribution of this paper is the introduction of the symmetric counterpart of crossings, called bouncings, defined here as rebounds of the time-discretization of the process at some threshold.
As with crossings, we distinguish three kinds of bouncing and prove that the respective number of bouncings has the same asymptotic properties as the number of crossings of the same kind. We combine results on bouncings and crossings to establish results on the sticky-reflected Brownian motion (see Section
5.1
for a definition), where only bouncing behavior is possible, and also on some sticky Itô diffusions. Our findings allow for the construction of a consistent stickiness parameter estimator, which we numerically show to converge at a comparable or faster rate than existing estimators in
[
3
,
5
]
.

2.2
The sticky Brownian motion

We now provide a definition of sticky Brownian motion as a general linear diffusion on the real line described by scale function and speed measure. We explain what is the role of the stickiness parameter without discussing further what are scale function and speed measure. Instead, we provide other equivalent characterizations of the process which are useful in the proofs of our results.

The sticky Brownian motion of stickiness parameter
\rho>0
is the diffusion process on
\IR
, on natural scale, with speed measure

m(\mathrm{d}x)=\,\mathrm{d}x+\rho\delta_{0}(\mathrm{d}x)
(see, e.g.,
[
8

The parameter
\rho>0

0

The asymptotic case
\rho=0
corresponds to the standard Brownian motion and

\rho=\infty
to the Brownian motion with an absorbing boundary at
0
.
In this paper we deal only with the case
\rho\in(0,\infty)

The Brownian motion case is known and the absorbing case is trivial.

3
Main results

We consider three types of behaviors on the time interval
[t,t+s]
of the process
X
at the sticky threshold. For simplicity, we assume the threshold to be located at
0
.

The types of crossings are:

1.

The
crossing of type 0
: the process
crosses from one open half-plane to the other. For example if
X_{t}<0
and

X_{t+s}>0
.

2.

The
crossing of type 1
: the process
crosses from one open or closed half-plane to its complementary. For example if
X_{t}\leq 0
and

X_{t+s}>0
or if
X_{t}<0
and
X_{t+s}\geq 0
.

3.

The
crossing of type 2
: the process crosses from
one closed half-plane to the other. For example
X_{t}\leq 0
and

X_{t+s}\geq 0
.

The types of bouncings are:

1.

The
bouncing of type 0
: the process
bounces from
(0,\infty)
back into
(0,\infty)
after hitting

. For example if
X_{t}>0
,
X_{t+s}>0
,
and for some

X_{t+h}=0
.

2.

The
bouncing of type 1
: the process

X_{t+s}\geq 0

The

, for sticky Brownian motion
X
it holds that

{\bf Z}^{(i)}_{n,t}(X)>0
with positive probability. If
X
is a Brownian motion, then
{\bf Z}^{(i)}_{n,t}(X)=0
almost surely.

One may say that
B^{(j)}
are not statistics, since they depend on the events
(U_{n,i})_{n,i}
which are not in the
\sigma
-algebra generated by high-frequency samples of the process. However, they are related to the
difference statistics

{\bf Z}^{(1)},{\bf Z}^{(2)}
.

3.1
Limit behavior of the statistics

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be the sticky Brownian motion of stickiness parameter
\rho>0
defined on the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\bF,(\bF_{t})_{t\geq 0},\Prob_{x})
such that
\Prob_{x}
-a.s.,
X_{0}=x
(in particular,
X
is
(\bF_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted),
and let
L^{0}(X)
be the right local time at
0
of
X
.
Then, the following convergences holds,

(i)

for every diverging sequence
u_{n}
, it holds that:

C^{(0)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/u_{n}\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}\text{-}\mathrm{ucp}}0
,

(ii)

{\bf Z}^{(1)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/\sqrt{n}\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}\text{-}\mathrm{ucp}}4\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}(X)

and
C^{(1)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/\sqrt{n}\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}\text{-}\mathrm{ucp}}4\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}(X)
,

(iii)

{\bf Z}^{(2)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/n\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}\text{-}\mathrm{ucp}}\rho L^{0}(X)

and

C^{(2)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/n\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}\text{-}\mathrm{ucp}}\rho L^{0}(X)
,

where as per (
2.5
),
\rho L^{0}_{t}(X)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{s}=0\}}\,\mathrm{d}s
, for all
t\geq 0
.

We note that in the case of the standard Brownian motion
W
, all three statistics have
the same non-trivial limit for the same normalizing sequence
\sqrt{n}
.
Indeed, from, e.g.,
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
,

\xPlim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{C^{(0)}_{n,t}(W)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=\xPlim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{C^{(1)}_{n,t}(W)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=\xPlim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{C^{(2)}_{n,t}(W)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}_{t}(W).

(3.5)

Also, the rates of convergence are known.
In
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
, a central limit theorem is proven
for this convergence, with a rate of
n^{1/4}
.

Unlike the standard Brownian motion, we observe that there is a factor
4
in the limits of
C^{(1)}_{n\cdot}/\sqrt{n}
and
{\bf Z}^{(1)}_{n,\cdot}/\sqrt{n}
for the sticky Brownian motion (see Theorem
3.1
(ii)
).
The factor
4
comes from the fact that
{\bf Z}^{(1)}_{n,t}/\sqrt{n}
rewrites as sum of
4
different terms, all converging to
\sqrt{2/\pi}L^{0}_{t}(X)
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\bf Z}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0;\;{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}>0\}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0;\;{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}<0\}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}>0;\;{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}<0;\;{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}.

Each term converges to
0
if the process is a standard Brownian motion
W
.
Instead, for the sticky Brownian motion, the limit is non trivial and it corresponds to the case of the renormalized number of crossings statistic for Brownian motion:
C^{(0)}_{n,\cdot}(W)/\sqrt{n}
.

Regarding the bouncing statistics, we prove that:

Theorem 3.2
.

Consider the setting of Theorem
3.1
. Then

(i)

for every diverging sequence
u_{n}
,

B^{(0)}_{n,\cdot}/u_{n}
has the same limit as
C^{(0)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/u_{n}
,

(ii)

B^{(j)}_{n,\cdot}/n^{j/2}
has the same limit as
C^{(j)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/n^{j/2}
, for
j=1,2
.

The difference statistics
{\bf Z}^{(\cdot)}
are the leading parts of number of crossings and bouncings statistics of type
1
and
2
.

The proof of the above results relies, by virtue of Lemma
2.3
, on the following limit behavior of the conditional version of the statistics
C^{(0)}
,
C^{(1)}
, and
C^{(2)}
.
These are the statistics
\widetilde{C}^{(0)},\widetilde{C}^{(1)},\widetilde{C}^{(2)}
defined for all
n,t
as

\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}<0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\big)
,

\widetilde{C}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}\leq 0\}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}={X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\big)
,

\widetilde{C}^{(2)}_{n,t}(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}\leq 0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\big)
.

For these, the following result holds.

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider the setting of Theorem
3.1
.
Then

(i)

\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,\cdot}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}\text{-}\mathrm{ucp}}({1}/{\rho})\,L^{0}(X)
,

(ii)

\widetilde{C}^{(j)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/n^{j/2}
has the same limit as
C^{(j)}_{n,\cdot}(X)/n^{j/2}
for
j=1,2
.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
(i)
is done by bounding the distance between
\widetilde{C}^{(0)}
and
C^{(0)}
and showing that
both the distance and the limit of
\widetilde{C}^{(0)}(X)
, if renormalized by
1/u_{n}
, vanish in probability, as
n\longrightarrow\infty
.

Let us note that the only case, for which the limits of the expected and non-expected versions of
the number of crossings statistics are not necessarily the same, is for the Type-
0
crossings (strict crossings).
In fact, they cannot be the same since
C^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)
and its limit take values in
\IN\cup(+\infty)
, while the law of
(1/\rho)L^{0}_{t}(X)
, the limit of
\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)
, is continuous.
The discrete limit law of
C^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)
is described in
[
30
, §8]
, which is restated here in Theorem
2.1
.
Let us also mention that Theorem
3.1
(i)
can also be inferred directly from Theorem
2.1
.

3.2
Estimation

In
[
3
,
5
]
an estimator was proposed based on a test function
g
and
a normalizing sequence
u_{n}
.
More precisely, let
g
be a bounded integrable function that satisfies
g(0)=0
and
(u_{n})_{n}
a sequence that
as
n\longrightarrow\infty
:
u_{n}\longrightarrow\infty
,
u_{n}/n\longrightarrow 0
,
\mathcal{H}_{t}
be the event
\{\tau^{X}_{0}<t\}=\{\exists\;s<t:\;X_{s}=0\}
and
\varrho^{(0)}_{n}(X)
the statistic

\varrho^{(0)}_{n}(X):=\left(\int_{\IR}g\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\frac{1}{u_{n}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0\}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}g(u_{n}{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}})}.

(3.7)

Then,
\varrho^{(0)}_{n}(X)
is a consistent estimator of
\rho
, conditionally on the event
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, i.e.,
\varrho^{(0)}_{n}(X)\longrightarrow\rho
, in
\Prob^{\mathcal{H}_{t}}_{x}
-probability, as
n\longrightarrow\infty
.

We now devise a consistent stickiness parameter estimator based
on number of crossings statistics.
This provides an alternative estimator to (
3.7
).

Like (
3.7
), this
new estimator also converges conditionally on the event that
0
has been reached before time
t
.
If the threshold is not reached, the number of crossings statistics are all trivially null.

Then, the following conditional convergence in probability holds.

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider the setting of Theorem
3.1

and let
t>0
.
The statistic

\displaystyle\varrho_{n}(X)

\displaystyle=\left(4\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{C^{(2)}_{n,t}(X)}{C^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)}

(3.8)

is a consistent estimator of
\rho
, conditionally on the event
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, i.e.,
\varrho_{n}(X)\longrightarrow\rho
, in
\Prob^{\mathcal{H}_{t}}_{x}
-probability, as
n\longrightarrow\infty
.

Remark 2
.

The result holds also if in (
3.8
) one replaces
(C^{(1)}_{n,t}(X),C^{(2)}_{n,t}(X))
with
(B^{(1)}_{n,t}(X),B^{(2)}_{n,t}(X))
or
({\bf Z}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X),{\bf Z}^{(2)}_{n,t}(X))
.

4
Proofs of main results

In this section we prove Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.1
for each type of crossing. The proofs are organized by crossing type: Section
4.1
covers Type-0 crossings, Section
4.2
covers Type-1 crossings, and Section
4.3
covers Type-2 crossings. Following this, Section
4.4
contains the proof of Theorem
3.2
(asymptotics of bouncings statistics), and finally, Section
4.5
contains the proof of Proposition
3.2
(stickiness parameter estimation).

For these, we need some results on the asymptotic behavior of the kernel, whose proof is deferred to Appendix
A
. In the statement we use the notation (
2.9
)–(
2.12
) of Proposition

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider the setting of Theorem
3.1
.
Let
(f_{n},k_{n},g_{n},h_{n};\,n\in\IN)
be the functions defined for all
n\in\IN
and
x\in\IR
by

f_{n}(x):=\Prob_{x}\left(X_{1/n}=0\right)
,

k_{n}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\not=0\}}f_{n}(x/\sqrt{n})
,

g_{n}(x):=\Prob_{x}\left(xX_{1/n}<0\right)

.
It holds that

(i)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}(0)=1
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{n}(1-f_{n}(0))={2\sqrt{2}}/{\rho\sqrt{\pi}}
,

(ii)

the sequence
(k_{n})_{n}
satisfies (
2.10
) and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}m_{\sqrt{n}\rho}(k_{n})=2\sqrt{{2}/{\pi}}
,

(iii)

the sequence
(h_{n})_{n}
satisfies (
2.10
) and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}m_{\sqrt{n}\rho}(h_{n})=1/{\rho}
.

4.1
Crossings of type
0

Here, we prove all results regarding number of Type-0 crossings.
Namely, Theorem
3.1
(i)
and Proposition
3.1
(i)
.
We first prove Proposition
3.1
(i)
relying on the sticky kernel asymptotics from Lemma
4.1
and the convergence result Proposition
2.1
.
Then, using Lemma
2.3
(conditional and unconditioned versions of the statistic have the same asymptotic) we prove Theorem
3.1
(i)
by reducing to Proposition
3.1
(i)
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
(i)
.

We begin by expressing the statistic
\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)
in terms of the functions
(g_{n})
and
(h_{n})
from Lemma
4.1
.
Indeed, by the Markov property it holds that

\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}<0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}g_{n}({X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}h_{n}(\sqrt{n}{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}).

(4.1)

By Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
4.1
(iii)
, we have

\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}\frac{1}{\rho}L^{0}_{t}(X)=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}{\mathcal{O}}^{0}_{t}(X),

(4.2)

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(i)
.

We consider the families of
\sigma
-algebras
(\bF^{n}_{i})_{i,n}

and random variables
(\chi^{n}_{i})_{i,n}
,
defined for all
i,n
by

\displaystyle\bF^{n}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\bF_{i/n},

\displaystyle\text{and}

\displaystyle\chi^{n}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{u_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}<0\}}

(4.3)

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{u_{n}}C^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\chi^{n}_{i},

\displaystyle\text{and}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{u_{n}}\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\left(\chi^{n}_{i}\big|\bF^{n}_{i-1}\right).

(4.4)

From Proposition
3.1
(i)
,

\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\left(\chi^{n}_{i}\big|\bF^{n}_{i-1}\right)=\frac{1}{u_{n}}\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}0

(4.5)

and, since
(\chi^{n}_{i})^{2}=\chi^{n}_{i}/u_{n}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\left(\left(\chi^{n}_{i}\right)^{2}\big|\bF^{n}_{i-1}\right)=\frac{1}{u_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\left(\chi^{n}_{i}\big|\bF^{n}_{i-1}\right)=\frac{1}{u_{n}^{2}}\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}0.

(4.6)

Thus, from Lemma
2.3
,

\frac{1}{u_{n}}C^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}0.

(4.7)

From Lemma
2.1
, the convergence is also uniform in time, in probability (ucp).
This finishes the proof. ∎

4.2
Crossings of type
1

Here, we prove all results regarding number of Type-1 crossings. Namely, Theorem
3.1
(ii)
and part of Proposition
3.1
(ii)
.

The proof relies on the study of the limit behavior of the following quantity which
according to our naming conventions is referred to as the conditional difference statistic:

\displaystyle{\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0,\,{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}\not=0\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}\not=0,\,{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\Big),

(4.8)

which satisfies
\widetilde{C}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)={\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)+\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)
, involving the Type-1 and Type-0 conditional statistics.

We first establish the limit behavior of
{\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)
using Lemmas
2.2
(occupation time approximation) and
4.1
(sticky kernel asymptotics).
The asymptotic of the Type-1 conditional crossing statistic
\widetilde{C}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)
follows from the decomposition

\widetilde{C}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)={\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)+\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)
and Proposition
3.1
(i)
(asymptotic of the respective Type-0 statistic).
Finally, by Lemma
2.3
we deduce Theorem
3.1
(ii)
from its conditional version.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following convergences hold:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}4\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}_{t}(X).

(4.9)

Proof.

Regarding the first term of
{\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}
, by Lemmas
2.2
and
4.1
(i)
we have that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0;\;{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}\not=0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\big]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Prob_{0}\left({X}_{\frac{{1}}{n}}\not=0\right)\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0\}}\\
=\sqrt{n}\left(1-f_{n}(0)\right)\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0\}}\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\rho\sqrt{\pi}}{\mathcal{O}}^{0}_{t}(X)=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}_{t}(X).

(4.10)

Regarding the second term of
{\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}
, by the Markov property we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}\not=0;\;{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}\not=0\}}\Prob_{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}}\left({X}_{\frac{{1}}{n}}=0\right)

(4.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}k_{n}(\sqrt{n}{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}),

where

is the sequence of functions defined in Lemma
4.1
.
From Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
4.1
(ii)
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}k_{n}(\sqrt{n}{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}})\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}m_{\sqrt{n}\rho}(k_{n})\right)L^{0}_{t}(X)=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}_{t}(X).

(4.12)

Combining the relations (
4.10
) and (

)
yields the desired result.
∎

3.1
(ii)
for

.

Essentially, it suffices to observe that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\widetilde{C}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\widetilde{C}^{(0)}_{n,t}(X).

Then, by Lemma
4.2

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\widetilde{C}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}4\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}_{t}(X).

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(ii)
.

The proofs works by first proving convergence of the Type-1 zero statistic
{\bf Z}^{(1)}_{\cdot,t}
by reduction to its conditional version
{\bf\widetilde{Z}}^{(1)}_{\cdot,t}
.
We then conclude from previous result by re-writting

as
{\bf Z}^{(1)}_{\cdot,t}+C^{(0)}_{\cdot,t}

For the reduction argument, we consider the families of

(\bF^{n}_{i})_{i,n}

and random variables
(\chi^{n}_{i})_{i,n}
,
defined for all

by

\displaystyle\bF^{n}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\bF_{i/n},

\displaystyle\text{and}

\displaystyle\chi^{n}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}={X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}\Big),

(4.15)

(4.16)

Combining this with Theorem

(i)

ensures that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}C^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\bf Z}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}C^{(0)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}4\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}L^{0}_{t}(X).

4.3
Crossings of type
2

Here, we prove all results regarding the number of Type-2 crossings, completing the proofs of Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.1
. The proofs consist of combining results on Type-1 crossings with the occupation time approximation (Lemma

).

Specifically, we show that both the Type-2 crossing statistic and its conditional version are asymptotically equivalent to the occupation time approximation from Lemma

.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(iii)
.

Recall the decomposition

C^{(2)}_{n,t}(X)=C^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)+\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0,{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}},

(4.21)

where we can rewrite the second term as

\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0,{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}=\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0\}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0,{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}\neq 0\}}.

(4.22)

Since
\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0,{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}\neq 0\}
corresponds to a particular case of type 1 crossing, we have

0\leq C^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)-\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0,{X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}\neq 0\}}\leq C^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)
.
This yields the bound

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0\}}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{n}C^{(2)}_{n,t}(X)\,\leq\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}=0\}}+\frac{1}{n}C^{(1)}_{n,t}(X)

(4.23)

By Item
(ii)
and Lemma
2.2
, both bounds converge to
\rho L^{0}_{t}(X)
. Therefore, by the squeeze theorem:

\forall t\geq 0:\qquad\frac{1}{n}C^{(2)}_{n,t}(X)\xlongrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\Prob_{x}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{s}=0\}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\rho L^{0}_{t}(X).

(4.24)

Lemma
2.1
ensures ucp convergence, which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
(ii)
for
j=2
.

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{C}^{(2)}_{n,t}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{[nt]}\Esp_{x}\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{\{{X}_{\frac{{i-1}}{n}}={X}_{\frac{{i}}{n}}=0\}}\big|\bF_{\frac{i-1}{n}}\Big)+\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{C}^{(1)}_{n,t}(X).

5
Extensions

In this section we extend the results on the sticky Brownian motion
to the sticky-reflected Brownian motion and to sticky Itô diffusions, i.e., processes
that solve a homogeneous SDE away from a sticky point.

5.1
Bouncings of the sticky-reflected Brownian motion

The sticky-reflected Brownian motion, also known as
slowly reflected
Brownian motion, was
first discovered by Feller in his attempt to describe all possible ways to define a Brownian motion
on the positive semi-axis
[0,\infty)
, see
[
15
]
.
In particular, he discovered that
the operator
\Lop=\D^{2}_{x}
with domain

\dom(\Lop)=\left\{f\in C_{0}([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty)):\;f^{\prime}(0+)=\big(\rho/2\big)f^{\prime\prime}(0+)\right\},

(5.1)

with
\rho>0
,
is the infinitesimal generator of a diffusion that spends a positive amount of time at
0
and that behaves like a Brownian motion away from
0
.
Replacing the boundary condition
f^{\prime}(0+)=\big(\rho/2\big)f^{\prime\prime}(0+)
by the lateral condition
f(0+)-f(0-)=\big(\rho/2\big)f^{\prime\prime}(0+)

defines the sticky Brownian motion, or two-sided sticky Brownian motion, introduced in Section
2.2
.
A nice historical overview can be found in
[
29
]
.

For simplicity, we define the sticky-reflected Brownian motion of stickiness parameter
\rho>0
as the unique (in law)
weak solution to the system

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t}>0\}}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{d}L^{0}_{t}(X),\\
\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t}=0\}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\rho}{2}\,\mathrm{d}L^{0}_{t}(X),\end{cases}

(5.2)

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion (see
[
14
, Theorem 5]
).

Let us note that the absolute value of a sticky Brownian motion is a sticky-reflected Brownian motion with the same stickiness parameter. More precisely, if
Y
is a sticky Brownian motion of stickiness parameter
\rho
,
from the Tanaka formula
[
31
, theorem VI.1.2]
and the SDE characterization
(p1)
for sticky Brownian motion, there exists a Brownian motion
B
on an extension of the probability space such that

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}|Y_{t}|

\displaystyle=\sgn(Y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}Y_{t}+\,\mathrm{d}L^{0}_{t}(Y)

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\sgn(Y_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Y_{t}|\not=0\}}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\,\mathrm{d}L^{0}_{t}(Y).

This, along with
(p1)
, yield that
\,\mathrm{d}\langle Y\rangle_{t}=\,\mathrm{d}\langle|Y|\rangle_{t}=\mathbbm{1}_{\{Y_{t}\not=0\}}\,\mathrm{d}t
and that, since
Y
is a martingale, from
[
31
, Corollary VI.1.9]
,

\displaystyle L^{0}_{t}(|Y|)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0<|Y_{s}|<\epsilon\}}\,\mathrm{d}s

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0<Y_{s}<\epsilon\}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0<-Y_{s}<\epsilon\}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=2L^{0}_{t}(Y)=2\rho\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Y_{s}=0\}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

From (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), if
B^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sgn(Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}
, the pair
(|Y|,B^{\prime})

solves (
5.2
).

We are now ready to state our main results regarding the number of bouncings of this process.
The proof reduces to the case of non-reflected sticky Brownian motion.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}m_{\sqrt{n}\rho}(k_{n})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\IR}\erfc\bigg(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}.

Regarding the first assertion, we observe that

(A.15)

(A.16)

where the upper bound defined a function in

.
Hence, from the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|x|k_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\IR}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(|x|e^{2|x|/\sqrt{n}\rho+2/n\rho^{2}}\erfc\bigg(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{n}\rho}\bigg)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\IR}|x|\erfc\bigg(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x=1.

(A.18)

2.10

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
4.3

For reader’s convenience, we recall the statement of Lemma
4.3
using notation (
4.28
) for the first hitting time.

Lemma

(Reflection principle for sticky Brownian motion)
.

Let
X
be the sticky Brownian motion of stickiness parameter
\rho>0
defined on the filtered probability space
\mathcal{P}_{x}=(\Omega,\bF,(\bF_{t})_{t\geq 0},\Prob_{x})
such that
\Prob_{x}
-a.s.,
X_{0}=x
(in particular,
X
is
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted).
We consider the process
X^{\prime}
, defined for all
t\geq 0
by

X^{\prime}_{t}=\begin{cases}X_{t}&t<\tau^{X}_{0},\\
-X_{t}&t\geq\tau^{X}_{0}.\\
\end{cases}

(B.1)

It holds that
X^{\prime}
is also a sticky Brownian motion of stickiness parameter
\rho
.

Proof.

Let
Z
be the Brownian motion defined in characterization
(t1)
, on an extension of the probability space, so that

\displaystyle\forall t

\displaystyle\geq 0:

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=Z_{\gamma(t)}

(B.2)

with
\gamma
the time–change defined in
(t1)
.
We observe that
\tau^{X}_{0}=\gamma(\tau^{Z}_{0})
.
Let
Z^{\prime}
be the process defined for all
t\geq 0
by

Z^{\prime}_{t}=\begin{cases}Z_{t}&t<\tau^{Z}_{0},\\
-Z_{t}&t\geq\tau^{Z}_{0}.\\
\end{cases}

(B.3)

From the reflection principle of the Brownian motion
Z
(see
[
31
, Exercise III.3.14]
), we have that
Z^{\prime}
is also a Brownian motion. Moreover,
L^{0}(Z)=L^{0}(Z^{\prime})
,
\tau^{Z}_{0}=\tau^{Z^{\prime}}_{0}
. Therefore,
A(t)=t+\rho L^{0}_{t}(Z^{\prime})
and
\tau^{X}_{0}=\gamma(\tau^{Z}_{0})=\gamma(\tau^{Z^{\prime}}_{0})
which, by the definition of
X^{\prime}
, is equal also to
\tau^{X^{\prime}}_{0}
.
From all the above, we show that
X^{\prime}=(Z^{\prime}_{\gamma(t)})_{t\geq 0}
. Indeed, on the event
\{t\geq\tau^{X}_{0}\}
it holds that

X_{t}^{\prime}=-X_{t}=-Z_{\gamma(t)}=(-Z)_{\gamma(t)}=Z^{\prime}_{\gamma(t)},

(B.4)

and, since
X_{t}=Z_{\gamma(t)}
,

X^{\prime}_{t}=Z_{\gamma(t)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{t<\tau^{X}_{0}\}}+Z^{\prime}_{\gamma(t)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\geq\tau^{X}_{0}\}}=Z^{\prime}_{\gamma(t)}.

(B.5)

To finish the proof, it suffices to observe that
(t2)
ensures that
X^{\prime}
is a sticky Brownian motion of stickiness parameter
\rho
.
∎

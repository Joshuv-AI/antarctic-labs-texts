Experiment-confirmed machine learning optimization of NADES extraction with ultrasound assistance and Mendelian randomization evidence on antioxidant effects of ferulic acid and ligustilide from Angelicae Sinensis Radix
Authors: Yangyang Zhang, Weifeng Jin, Tianxue Chen, Jin Han, Li Yu, Haitong Wan, Haofang Wan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstractThis study developed an efficient extraction process for ASR using NADES combined with ultrasonic assistance, optimized via machine learning models, and evaluated the antioxidant activities of FA and LIG in relation to SOD and HO-1 expression.Keywords: Angelicae Sinensis Radix, Natural deep eutectic solvents, Extraction optimization, Machine learning, Mendelian randomization, Antioxidant activity

Introduction

Angelicae Sinensis Radix (ASR) is the dried rhizome of Angelica sinensis (Oliv.) Diels from the Umbelliferae family, and it is commonly used in clinical settings to nourish the blood, enhance circulation, and unblock the meridians [1]. Its effects of anti-oxidation and promoting blood circulation have been developed and applied in the fields of cosmetics, personal care products and health care products. Moreover, in remote areas of China, such as Gansu and Yunnan, ASR planting is the pillar of county economy and the main source of income for farmers. Chemically, ferulic acid (FA) is the major phenolic acid component [2], [3] with multiple pharmacological activities and applications in the management of thromboangiitis obliterans and acute cerebral thrombosis [4], whereas ligustilide (LIG) is the principal constituent of the volatile oil [5], exhibiting antioxidant, and neuroprotective effects and frequently investigated in the contexts of ischemic stroke and cancer [6]. In recognition of their importance, the 2020 edition of the Chinese Pharmacopoeia includes FA content and volatile oil assays as quality control indices for ASR [7]. Accordingly, FA and LIG are regarded as key marker compounds in ASR extracts.

Although ASR is widely used in clinical and research settings, there remains a significant lack of green, environmentally friendly extraction processes. Natural deep eutectic solvents (NADES) are recognized for their low toxicity, affordability, and high extraction efficiency. These qualities make them ideal for the environmentally friendly production of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) [8]. Ultrasound-assisted extraction (UAE) has emerged as a green and efficient technique for bioactive compound recovery from plant matrices, owing to its ability to disrupt cell walls and enhance mass transfer through cavitation effects. However, the systematic optimization of the process and key factor sorting for NADES in ASR remains underexplored.

It is worth noting that data analysis methods are an indispensable component of process optimization research, with response surface methodology (RSM) being the most commonly used [9]. However, RSM often shows a high degree of fit within the sample, but many regression terms are not significant, which limits the reliability and interpretability of the predictions. In recent years, machine learning (ML) models such as support vector regression (SVR), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), decision tree (DT), backpropagation neural network (BPNN), genetic algorithm-BPNN (GA-BPNN), and particle swarm optimization-BPNN (PSO-BPNN), have developed rapidly in data processing of TCM extraction [10], [11]. Bibliometrics shows that optimization of extraction processes, prediction of active ingredients, and analysis of pharmacodynamic material basis are the core directions of ML applications [12]. Gao et al [13] modeled the extraction process of TCM compound for the treatment of primary liver cancer and established the quality standard through ML. Nevertheless, most studies still use training-stage indicators such as fitting degree or mean square error as the primary evaluation criteria. Consequently, predictions generated by different models often lack consistency for the same dataset [14]. From the perspective of experimental science, the gold standard for model selection should be based on the prediction performance verified by independent experiments, rather than relying only on statistical indicators. Therefore, this study used single factor experiment and box Behnken design (BBD) to obtain NADES extraction data, and takes “experimental validation prediction performance” as the criterion, systematically compares the prediction ability of the above models, determines the optimal model and parameter combination, and analyzes the contribution direction and relative importance of NADES volume fraction, ultrasonic temperature, ultrasonic time and solid–liquid ratio to the total yield with the help of Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) method [15].

Furthermore, MR has been widely used to investigate genetic variants associated with specific risk factors and assess their causal relationship with disease outcomes [16]. It can reveal the pathophysiological mechanisms of stroke subtypes and help identify therapeutic targets for clinical translation [17]. At the level of mechanism research, network pharmacology combined with MR has become a common strategy to reveal the action targets and active ingredients of TCM compounds [18], [19]. MR focuses on verifying the causal association between components and disease targets from the perspective of genetic epidemiology, and has been widely used in natural product research.

In this study, MR was used for the first time to evaluate the genetic association of FA/LIG and antioxidant indexes (SOD and HO-1), and the in vivo verification was completed in middle cerebral artery occlusion/reperfusion (MCAO/R) mice using ELISA. This integrated strategy combining ML optimized extraction process with MR genetic causal inference and animal experiments provides a new path for the green extraction of TCM and the study of its pharmacodynamic mechanism. Materials and methods

Materials and reagents

ASR (lot no. 20190801-1) was obtained from Hunan Yaoshengtang Traditional Chinese Medicine Technology Co., Ltd., and was identified as the dry rhizome of Angelica sinensis (Oliv.) Diels (Umbelliferae) by Professor Shengwu Huang of the School of Pharmacy of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University. FA (CAS:1135-24-6, quality score ≥ 98%), LIG (CAS: 4431-01-0, quality score ≥ 98%), and sucrose (CAS: 57-50-1) were from Shanghai Yuanye Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Betaine (CAS: 107-43-7), L-proline (CAS: 147-85-3), choline chloride (CAS: 67-48-1), citric acid (CAS: 77-92-9), glycerol (CAS: 56-81-5), DL-malic acid (CAS: 6915-15-7), lactic acid (CAS: 50-21-5), methylurea (CAS: 598-50-5), and formic acid (CAS: 64-18-6, HPLC grade, ≥ 98%) were from Shanghai Aladdin Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Urea (CAS: 57-13-6) was from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Ultrapure water was prepared with a Millipore instrument. Chromatographically pure methanol and acetonitrile were from Tedia Co. (USA). SOD (MB-3125A), HO-1 (MB-3209A) ELISA kits were from Jiangsu Meibiao Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The ultrasonic instrument used in this study was purchased from Xiaomei Ultrasonic Instrument (Kunshan) Co., Ltd. The model is XM-400ULF, the operating frequency is 28 KHz, the rated power is 400 W, and the ultrasonic mode is pulse type.

Reference solution preparation

After adding 1.00 mg of FA as a reference compound to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, a 1:1 (v/v) methanol: water solution was added to prepare a 1.00 mg/mL FA reference solution. This same approach was also used to prepare a 1.00 mg/mL LIG reference solution.

Test solution preparation

After weighing 100.00 mg of ASR powder and transferring it into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, 1.00 mL of solvent (0.70 mL NADES and 0.30 mL water) was added and mixed. UAE was performed at 30 ℃ for 20 min and centrifuged at 6000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was collected, diluted four times with a 1:1 (v/v) methanol: water solution, mixed thoroughly, and passed through a 0.22 μm microporous membrane. The resultant test solution was used for HPLC analyses.

Detection methodology

Chromatographic conditions

A Waters Alliance HPLC instrument (2695 separation unit, 2489 UV ultraviolet Detector, Waters Empower workstation, USA) was used for HPLC separation with a Hypersil BDS C18 column (300 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm). A 1 mL/min flow rate was used with a mobile phase of 0.1% formic acid (A)-acetonitrile (B) and the following gradient elution settings: 0-15 min, 90%-60% A; 15-20 min, 60%-20% A; 20-25 min, 20%-5% A; 25-30 min, 5%-90% A. A 320 nm detection wavelength was used, the column was warmed to 23°C, and an injection volume of 10 μL was used.

Methodological validation

The system’s adaptability was assessed by injecting the mixed reference and test solutions into the system. The mixed reference solution (Solution No. 1) was diluted to prepare reference solutions with FA and LIG concentrations of 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.05, and 0.01 mg/mL (Solution No. 2-7) for analyses of the linearity relationships. Mixed standard solution was used for analyses of precision, repeatedly injecting this system into the HPLC apparatus five times. Stability was further assessed by preparing sample solutions and analyzing them after room-temperature incubation for 2, 4, 6, and 8 h. Repeatability was assessed by using the same batch of medicinal materials to prepare five test solutions. The RSD of the peak area for each component was then measured.

NADES screening

For this study, 10 total solvents were evaluated, including water and 9 NADESs (Table 1) utilizing betaine (Bet), L-proline (Pro), and choline chloride (ChCl) as the HBA and sucrose (Suc), citric acid (Ca), urea (Ur), glycerol (Gly), DL-malic acid (Maa), lactic acid (Lac), and methylurea (Mu) as the HBD. The test solution was prepared for HPLC analyses according to “Test solution preparation”.

Single-factor experiments

The effect of the volume fraction of NADES on prepared extracts

Five separate 100.00 mg samples of ASR powder were extracted for 20 min in a ultrasound equipment at 30℃ with a 50%, 60%, 70%, 80%, or 90% volume fraction of NADES and a 10 mg/mL solid-liquid ratio.

The effect of the ultrasonic extraction time on prepared extracts

Five separate 100.00 mg samples of ASR powder were extracted for 10, 20, 30, 40, or 50 min in an ultrasound equipment at 30℃ with a 70% volume fraction of NADES and a 10 mg/mL solid-liquid ratio.

The effect of the ultrasonic extraction temperature on prepared extracts

Five separate 100.00 mg samples of ASR powder were extracted for 20 min in a ultrasound equipment at 30, 40, 50, 60, or 70℃ with a 70% volume fraction of NADES and a 10 mg/mL solid-liquid ratio.

The effect of the solid-liquid ratio on prepared extracts

Five separate 100.00 mg samples of ASR powder were extracted for 20 min in in a ultrasound equipment at 30℃ with a 70% volume fraction of NADES and respective solid-liquid ratios of 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 mg/mL.

Experimental approach

Thirty combination experiments comprising four factors and three levels were established in Design-Expert 13.0. The effective component contents in ASR extracts prepared under different extraction conditions were analyzed by HPLC, and peak areas were recorded. FA and LIG contents in ASR extracts were used for optimization testing to clarify how different factors influence the extraction of effective components from ASR.

Modeling and optimization

The modeling approach not only employed the common RSM but was also complemented by a global optimization strategy. This strategy yielded two distinct globally optimal models, each designed to meet different evaluation criteria, such as minimizing error and prioritizing robustness. Simultaneously, we systematically compared traditional machine learning models (SVR, XGBoost, DT, BPNN), and hybrid optimization models (GA-BPNN and PSO-BPNN) were build based on BPNN. Within the existing model framework, the global search and fine-grained grid traversal strategies were employed to determine the optimal extraction conditions and comprehensive yield.

All models take four process factors (NADES volume fraction, ultrasonic time, ultrasonic temperature, and solid-liquid ratio) as input, and output the comprehensive yield, computed as

comprehensive yield (Y)= FA (Y1) × w1 + Lig (Y2) × w2,

where the weights w1 and w2 are determined by the entropy weighting method. The training process utilized a stratified 80%/20% data split, with k-fold cross-validation on the training set used for hyperparameter tuning and early stopping, before final evaluation on an independent test set to assess generalization performance. Model training was implemented using established open-source Python libraries, including scikit-learn and XGBoost. The objective was to minimize the maximum mean squared error between the training and test sets. This section describes the evaluation criteria for the two globally optimal models and the modeling framework for hybrid optimization models.

Hybrid models

As shown in Fig. 2, hybrid optimization models (GA-BPNN and PSO-BPNN) use genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimization, respectively, to globally optimize the neural network weights (including biases). The fitness function of both models is the maximum value of the mean squared error (MSE) of the training and test sets to suppress overfitting while ensuring generalization, i.e.,

The schematic diagram of PSO-BPNN and GA-BPNN models.

In GA-BPNN, individual network parameters are represented as real numbers and are iteratively updated via tournament selection, arithmetic crossover, and Gaussian mutation. In PSO-BPNN, particle positions and velocities correspond to network parameters, updated according to inertia weights and individual/group experience terms to search for the global optimum. Both algorithms stop when the preset iteration limit or fitness convergence threshold is reached, and the resulting optimal weight configuration is used as the final BPNN model for process condition optimization and comprehensive yield prediction.

Verification testing

The various process conditions within the specified range were explored to determine the optimal conditions and the best comprehensive yield as predicted by the trained model. Then, experimental verification was carried out according to these extraction conditions, and the most reliable model was selected by comparing with the actual and the predicted comprehensive yield. It provides a basis for the standardized production and industrialization of TCM.

SHAP analysis method: The importance of factors

SHAP were employed to quantify the contributions of four process factors (NADES volume fraction, ultrasonic time, ultrasonic temperature, and solid-liquid ratio) to the comprehensive yield based on the most robust model verified by experiments. The average absolute value of SHAP of each sample across the whole sample is taken as the global importance, and the stability of the ranking is assessed through cross-validation and permutation tests. Simultaneously, summary plots and dependency plots are generated to examine the direction of contribution and potential nonlinearities/interactions. If necessary, the highly correlated factors are combined for interpretation to avoid misinterpreting the correlation as an independent effect. Ultimately, the order of importance and action characteristics of key factors are given accordingly.

Mendel randomization (MR)

Blood expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL)

The eQTLGen database was used to identify significant (minor allele frequency MAF>1%, P<5×10−8) single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the blood associated with the expression of target genes of effective components (FA and LIG) of ASR. Based on blood expression profile data from 31,684 individuals, cis-and trans-eQTL analyses was performed [28].

GWAS Summary Statistics Sources

In order to study the antioxidant activity of active ingredients (FA and LIG) of ASR in vivo, SOD and HO-1 were used as representative indicators of antioxidant activity in this study. The plasma protein quantitative trait loci (pQT) of SOD were obtained from the decode cohort, which involved 35,559 aptamers measuring plasma protein levels in 4,907 Icelanders. It was found that 18,084 correlations existed between the variant sequences and plasma protein levels [29]. HO-1 data were obtained from a Quickdraws analysis of plasma HO-1 levels in 47,745 individuals from the British Biobank [30].

MR analysis

MR should meet three basic assumptions: there is a correlation between SNP and exposure factors; SNP is not associated with any confounding factors that influence the relationship between exposure and outcome; SNP only affects the outcome through exposure factors, and there is no other direct or indirect path [31]. Therefore, P<5×10-8 was set as the genome-wide significance level, and the instrumental variable of linkage disequilibrium (r2<0.001, window size=10,000 KB) was removed in this study. The random effect inverse variance weighting (IVW) method was used as the main analysis method, and the weighted mode, weighted median, MR egger and simple mode were used as the secondary analysis. Cochran 's Q statistic was used to test the heterogeneity. MR-PRESSO test was used to evaluate the overall level of pleiotropic effect. All MR analyses are performed using the R package in RStudio 4.3.2, including “Two Sample MR”. Finally, the MR results were verified in animal experiments.

Antioxidant studies in vivo

Experimental animals

Male C57 BL/6J mice (16-24 g, 6-8 weeks) were purchased from Shanghai Slake Experimental Animal Co., Ltd., and raised in the Zhejiang Chinese Medical University Laboratory Animal Research Center (Production license number: SCXK (Shanghai) 2018-0006). All animals were maintained in accordance with regulatory guidance as per the NIH guidelines for the care and use of experimental animals. The Animal Ethics Approval: No. IACUC-20231120-22.

Establishment of the MCAO/R injury model

A MCAO/R model was established using the approach detailed by Longa et al [32], with further details on this modeling strategy being provided in our prior studies [33].

Experimental groupings and treatments

In total, 24 mice were assigned to the Sham, MCAO/R, FA (144 mg/kg) [34] and LIG (28 mg/kg) [35], [36] treatment groups (n=6/group). Sham and MCAO/R group rats received an equivalent volume of physiological saline. The mice were given an intraperitoneal injection for 3 consecutive days.

Neurological function scores

Neurological function was assessed using the Longa scores: 0, normal; 1, failure to fully extend the contralateral forepaw; 2, circling to the contralateral side; 3, falling to the contralateral side; and 4, no spontaneous walking with loss of consciousness.

TTC staining

The brain tissues were stored at −20 ℃ for 15 min to facilitate slicing. Subsequently, the brains were cut into coronal sections and immersed in a 2% 2,3,5‑triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) solution at 37 ℃ in the dark for 15 min. Following incubation, the sections were carefully removed and imaged. The infarct area was measured using ImageJ software.

ELISAs

Serum of mice was collected 1 h after the last administration. SOD and HO-1 levels in the plasma were determined with ELISA kits used as directed according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Statistical analyses

Data collection and analyses were performed with Design-Expert 13.0, while GraphPad 8.0.2 was used to conduct one-way ANOVAs with Tukey’s post hoc test. Results were presented as means ± standard deviation (x¯±s), and P < 0.05 was defined as significance.

NADES screening and Single/multiple-factor for ASR extraction

Relative to the use of water as a solvent, all nine tested NADESs yielded higher ASR extraction yields, with the Pro-Mu achieving the best extraction (Fig. 3c). In this study, L-Pro was thus used as the HBA while MU served as the HBA. The resultant NADES was prepared by combining these with water at a 1:1:2 ratio.

The results of the single-factor experiment are shown in Fig. 3d-g. As the NADES volume fraction increased, the extraction yields of both FA and LIG exhibited a trend of initially increasing and then decreasing, with the maximum observed at 70%. Similarly, the highest extraction yields for both compounds were achieved at an ultrasonic time of 30 min, an ultrasonic temperature of 50 ℃, and a solid-liquid ratio of 8 mg/mL. Based on these results, the following levels of each factor were selected for the subsequent Box-Behnken design (BBD): NADES volume fractions of 60%, 70%, and 80%; ultrasonic times of 20, 30, and 40 min; ultrasonic temperatures of 40, 50, and 60 ℃; and solid-liquid ratios of 6, 8, and 10 mg/mL.

Based on the FA and LIG extraction results for the 30 response surface groups above, corresponding weight values were calculated with the entropy weighting method. FA and LIG were assigned respective weights of 0.543 and 0.457 such that the comprehensive evaluation value was computed as follows: the concentration of FA×0.543+the concentration of Lig×0.457. FA and LIG alleviate neurological deficits and reduce infarction volume in MCAO/R mice

TTC staining and neurological function assessment were performed to evaluate the neuroprotective effects of FA and LIG (Fig. 5a-c). No cerebral infarction and neurological deficit was observed in the Sham group. The MCAO/R group exhibited a significant in both infarction volume and neurological function scores compared with the Sham group (P<0.0001), indicating severe neurological impairment following ischemia-reperfusion injury. Notably, treatment with either FA or LIG significantly reduced the infarct volume and ameliorated neurological deficit compared with the MCAO/R group (P<0.001 or P<0.01). MR analysis and antioxidant activity in MCAO/R mice

Applying the genome-wide significance level (P<5×10-8) and removing the instrumental variables of linkage disequilibrium, 23 FA and 15 LIG instrumental variables were finally obtained. MR results (Fig. 5d) showed that FA was significantly associated with increased SOD expression (OR=1.019, 95% CI: 1.002-1.036, P=0.0265), but not with HO-1 expression (OR=1.007, 95% CI: 0.994-1.019, P=0.23). LIG was not significantly associated with SOD (OR=0.989, 95% CI: 0.957-1.023, P=0.547) and HO-1 (OR=1.014, 95% CI: 0.996-1.032, P=0.21). None of the exposed variables showed significant heterogeneity and horizontal pleiotropy.

The levels of SOD and HO-1 in serum of mice were detected to further verify the above results, and the results are shown in the Fig. 5e-f. Compared with Sham group, the level of SOD was significantly decreased (P<0.0001), and level of HO-1 was significantly increased (P<0.05) in the MCAO/R group. Compared with the MCAO/R group, FA treatment significantly increased the level of SOD (P<0.05), while there was no significant change in the levels of SOD and HO-1 in the LIG group (P>0.05), which further supported the conclusion of MR analysis.

Discussion

Green and efficient extraction of bioactive compounds from herbal materials is essential for their functional characterization and therapeutic application. In this study, an ultrasound-assisted NADES strategy was developed for the efficient extraction of FA and LIG from ASR, with the extraction process optimized using multiple machine learning models. MR analysis was employed to predict the potential antioxidant of FA and LIG, and subsequently validated the findings in MCAO/R mice.

ASR is a plant used for both medicinal and edible purposes, with a history of over 3000 years. Its mechanism of promoting blood circulation is related to its fatty acids and volatile oil components, which improve microcirculation, increase blood supply, and reduce blood flow resistance [37]. FA and LIG were thus selected as representative components of ASR for analyses of the extraction processes for this TCM. Research indicated that water and alcohol are the most common solvents used for ASR extraction [38], [39]. While NADESs offer excellent performance as green solvents that are stable, non-toxic, non-flammable, inexpensive, recyclable, and with low vapor pressure, they have not been used for extraction of this TCM to date [40], [41]. As such, in this study, the optimal solvent for such extraction was identified by screening 9 NADES preparations. Based on the measured yields of FA and LIG extraction from ASR, a NADES composed of L-Pro and MU yield the highest extraction yield.

In this study, the HPLC method was implemented and optimized for FA and LIG based on the conditions described in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020 edition and corresponding reports in the literature [42], [43]. As methanol-water and acetonitrile-water systems are the most commonly used mobile phases in this context, these two systems were evaluated as mobile phase in initial testing. As the test solution was a TCM extract with many complex components and impurities, a small amount of acid was required in the mobile phase to adjust of peak shape and time. Gradient elution with a 0.1% formic acid water-acetonitrile solution was ultimately found to yield FA and LIG peaks meeting the separation requirements, such that this was selected as the final mobile phase. Through UV full-spectrum scanning, these two components were found to exhibit substantial absorption at 320 nm such that this wavelength was used for simultaneous quantitative analyses.

Compared with water or alcohol extraction solvents, NADES possesses a stable hydrogen bond network formed by hydrogen bond donors (HBD) and hydrogen bond acceptors (HBA), which enhances the inter-molecular interactions with target compounds and solvents, improves the solubility and mass transfer efficiency of active ingredients [44], [45]. Furthermore, NADES can augment the dissolution of target components via hydrogen bonding, van der Waals forces, and other intermolecular interactions, while simultaneously promoting plant cell wall swelling and disruption, thereby reducing mass transfer resistance and enhancing extraction efficiency. In addition, NADES offers distinct advantages including low volatility, low toxicity, biodegradability, and controllable composition, rendering it a promising class of green solvents with broad application prospects in natural product extraction [46]. The research have demonstrated that rational design of NADES composition and UAE, not only enhance the recovery of active ingredients, but also enables NADES recycling, thereby ensuring the extraction efficiency while further improving the greenness and sustainability of the extraction process [47]. The result further supports the scientificity and rationality of using NADES combined with UAE strategy in this study, and provide new research ideas for the application of NADES in green and efficient natural product extraction.

UAE facilitates the release and mass transfer of bioactive compounds from natural products through cavitation, mechanical, micro-jet, and localized thermal effects. Ultrasonic waves generate numerous cavitation bubbles when propagating in liquid media. These bubbles form local high temperatures, high pressures and micro jets during the instantaneous collapse process, which can effectively destroy plant cell walls and cell membrane structures, improve cell permeability, and accelerate the release of intracellular active ingredients. Concurrently, the mechanical vibration induced by ultrasound reduces the thickness of the diffusion boundary layer on the surface of plant tissues, promotes solvent penetration into the tissues, and accelerates the diffusion of target compounds from cells into the bulk solvent, thereby significantly enhancing both mass transfer and extraction efficiency [48], [49], [50]. In addition, for the relatively viscous NADES system, ultrasound also reduces the mass transfer resistance, improves the fluidity of the solvent, and enhance the penetration ability of NADES into plant tissues, enabling through contact with target compounds and fully exploiting its superior solvation capacity [51], [52]. Thus, NADES provides a favorable dissolution environment, while ultrasound reinforces cell wall disruption and mass transfer. Acting synergistically through improved solvent permeation, enhanced component diffusion, and increased mass transfer efficiency, the combined system markedly improves the extraction yields of FA and LIG.

The SHAP result showed that the of NADES volume fraction has the greatest influence on the extraction yield of FA and LIG from ASR, indicating that NADES, as a new extraction solvent, can significantly improve the extraction yields of TCM. The NADES volume fraction directly determines the ratio of NADES to water in the system, which in turn affects the polarity, viscosity, hydrogen bond network structure and mass transfer performance of the solvent [53]. When the NADES volume fraction is low, the water content in the system is high. Although it can effectively reduce viscosity and improve solvent fluidity, excessive water molecules will weaken the hydrogen bond interaction between HBA and HBD, resulting in partial dissociation of the hydrogen bond network structure unique to NADES, thereby reducing its ability to dissolve the target active ingredient [54]. Conversely, when the NADES volume fraction is too high, the viscosity of the system increases significantly, which restricts the penetration of solvent into the plant tissue and the diffusion and mass transfer process of the target components, ultimately leading to the decline of extraction efficiency. Therefore, the appropriate NADES volume fraction can achieve the best balance between solvent polarity, viscosity and mass transfer efficiency while maintaining a stable hydrogen bond network, thereby obtaining a high extraction efficiency. The effect of ultrasonic time on the extraction yields showed an increasing trend followed by a decreasing trend, which was due to the rapid destruction of plant cell wall and accelerated solvent penetration by ultrasonic cavitation in the early stage of extraction. However, with the further extension of time, the effective components basically reach the dissolution equilibrium, and the effect of further extending the time on improving the extraction rate is limited [55]. The increase of extraction temperature can reduce the viscosity of NADES, improve the diffusion capacity and the solubility of the target components. However, excessively high temperature may cause degradation of volatile and thermosensitive components such as ligustilide. At the same time, high temperature may also destroy the stable hydrogen bond network inside NADES, thus reducing the extraction efficiency [50]. The suitable solid-liquid ratio can increase the concentration gradient and improve the diffusion rate. However, when the amount of solvent is too large, the ultrasonic energy density is relatively reduced and the cavitation effect is weakened, which is not conducive to further improving the extraction efficiency.

RSM is a traditional predictive tool widely used in the TCM field that are best suited to use when addressing problems with less data and a structure that is relatively simple [56]. In this study, global optimization based on the RSM model was performed to selected two optimal polynomial regression equations and performed variance analysis. The results showed significant differences in the volume fraction of NADES and the extraction time. Unlike RSM, ANNs are robust, self-adaptive, self-learning, and fault-tolerant, enabling them to better approximate complex nonlinear relationships and address issues when mathematical models are uncertain [57], [58]. GA is a population optimization algorithm based on the genetic evolution process and the principle of natural selection. PSO approaches can simulate natural crowd behavior to resolve optimization problems [59]. In this approach, particles search for optimal solutions with guidance from historical and global optimal solutions through adjustments in position and speed. In this setting, each possible solution is represented by a particle [60]. In this study, traditional machine learning models, including SVR, XGBoost, DT and BPNN, were applied to optimize the four extraction conditions and to obtain the best extraction conditions and predicted values. Additionally, GA-BPNN and PSO-BPNN were used to predict the optimal extraction conditions and comprehensive evaluation values. It was found that the R2 of the GA-BPNN model was closest to 1, and that the GA-BPNN model had the best fit. The experimental verification was carried out to confirm the predicted value under the optimal extraction conditions. The results showed that under the GA-BPNN model, the relative error between actual and predicted value was small, indicating that the model’s reliability. This study has not yet systematically validated the same model under multiple non-optimal extraction conditions. Moreover, although multiple machine learning models were constructed in this study, the relatively limited sample size posed a potential risk of over-fitting. Leave-one-out cross-validation during model training and a systematic sensitivity analysis were employed to alleviate this issue. Nevertheless, training on a small sample inherently constrains model generalization, and the absence of independent external validation remains a limitation. Additionally, model performance under non-optimal conditions has not been evaluated, and further validation experiments across varied extraction settings will be conducted to assess predictive robustness.

In terms of activity evaluation, ASR extracts have been demonstrated to exhibit antioxidant activity through their removal of hydroxyl radicals and superoxide anions in vitro [43]. FA exerts neuroprotective effects by modulating microglial polarization and ameliorating depression-related neuroinflammation via the Nrf2 pathway, and by inhibiting neuroinflammation and neuronal apoptosis through the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway [61], [62]. In the acute phase of cerebral ischemia, LIG may upregulate antioxidant enzymes by facilitating Nrf2 activation and nuclear translocation, while concurrently inhibiting hippocampal neuronal apoptosis via PI3K/AKT signaling [63], [64]. However, the research on the causal relationship between the components of TCM and antioxidant effects remains insufficient. This study used MR to explore the association between FA/LIG and antioxidant indicators (SOD and HO-1) from the perspective of causal inference. It was found that there was a significant causal relationship between FA and SOD, but no significant association with HO-1; LIG showed no significant association with either. These results were verified in MCAO/R mice, were FA administration significantly increased SOD levels in plasma. In addition, it should be pointed out that MR reflects the lifelong cumulative effect caused by genetic factors, while animal experiments in this study evaluate the acute pharmacological effects of active ingredients, and the biological significance reflected by the two are not completely consistent. Therefore, the lack of significant genetic correlation of LIG does not negate its potential biological activity. This result may be related to the limited number of available instrumental variables of LIG, insufficient statistical efficiency, or its role mainly through non transcriptional levels such as protein post-translational modification and signal transduction.

The unique novel contributions of this study are as follows: First, a green extraction strategy using NADES combined with ultrasound assistance was developed, and the extraction process of FA and LIG from ASR was optimized by multiple machine learning models. Second, the SHAP algorithm was introduced to interpret the machine learning model, revealing that the NADES volume fraction is the most critical factor affecting extraction efficiency, thereby providing a theoretical basis for process optimization. Third, MR analysis with animal experiments were integrated to investigate and biologically validate the potential causal relationship between FA/ LIG and antioxidant-related indicators (SOD and HO-1). Collectively, this research presents a multidisciplinary strategy that integrates green extraction, machine learning optimization, causal inference, and in vivo verification, providing new insights for the efficient recovery and functional assessment of active ingredients from TCM.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. We have not examined ultrasonic energy consumption, NADES reusability, or the underlying molecular mechanisms. Moreover, the MR analysis relied on human genetic data, whereas the in vivo experiments used mouse models, requiring cross-species caution. Additionally, only a single-dose and two oxidative markers (SOD and HO-1) were tested, with no dose dependence, toxicology, or underlying mechanisms studies. Future study will focus on translating this green extraction strategy into practical industrial applications, with emphasis on large‑scale feasibility, economic viability, and long‑term safety profiles.

Conclusions

In summary, this study demonstrated the feasibility of substituting conventional solvents with NADES for the efficient extraction of FA and LIG from ASR, and successfully improved the recovery of these bioactive components. Multiple machine learning models were employed to predict the optimal extraction conditions, among which the GA-BPNN model exhibited the best fitting accuracy and predictive performance. Furthermore, MR analysis clarified the causal relationship between FA/LIG and SOD/ HO-1 , and these associations were experimentally validated in MCAO/R mice. The key findings of this study are summarized in Fig. 6. Collectively, this study promoted the further development and utilization of ASR, and provided a valuable reference for selecting appropriate modeling techniques in the optimization of TCM extraction processes, thereby contributing to the advancement of the TCM industry.

Next-generation global scientific citation framework in progress
Authors: The Innovation Editorial Team
Published: 2026

Main text

On March 27, the Institute of Scientific and Technical Information of China (ISTIC), in collaboration with the Zhejiang Lab, released the “Overall Framework of the World Scientific Citation Database” report. Conceived as a next-generation research infrastructure, the long-raised initiative of building such a database aims to move beyond the limitations of traditional citation indexing systems by establishing a more comprehensive, multilingual, and interoperable platform for evaluating scientific output and its global impact. By integrating diverse data sources and expanding linguistic coverage, the framework seeks to improve the visibility, accessibility, and comparability of research across different regions and academic traditions.

The framework was unveiled at the Forum on Promoting World-Class STM Journals, a renowned international forum for Science, Technology and Medicine journals based in China, where ISTIC Director Huang Qi outlined its strategic objectives and long-term significance (Figure 1). “What we are building is a world-class evaluation indicator system for scientific journals,” Huang said in his address to the forum. “This is a major foundational infrastructure project that both inherits from and goes beyond traditional citation index databases. Once completed, it will be capable of meeting the needs of researchers across multiple fields and sectors.”

His remarks highlighted the project’s dual ambition: to modernize existing evaluation approaches while also creating a more adaptable and inclusive system suited to the evolving global research landscape.

Initiated in 2025 with the support of China’s Ministry of Science and Technology and the National Development and Reform Commission, the project is designed to address persistent structural challenges in research evaluation. In response to common criticisms of existing citation systems, such as heavy reliance on single-dimensional metrics, limited ability to capture the broader contributions of journals, and insufficient representation of non-English research outputs, the new framework emphasizes a more multidimensional approach, incorporating indicators related to frontier innovation, long-term academic value, societal impact, and research integrity. In doing so, it aims to provide a more balanced, context-sensitive assessment of scientific work while encouraging the development of high-quality journals and fostering a more transparent and equitable academic environment.

According to Huang, the project has achieved several key technical advances. First, it utilizes multi-source, heterogeneous data fusion technologies to enable precise tracking of citations at the level of individual publications across multilingual journals worldwide, thereby generating a more comprehensive and granular representation of global research activity.

Second, it introduces an enhanced journal classification system that is better aligned with emerging scientific fields and evolving disciplinary structures, allowing for more accurate categorization of research in rapidly developing areas.

Third, it incorporates an AI-driven semantic citation analysis framework that goes beyond traditional quantitative metrics by analyzing the contextual meaning of citations. This approach is intended to deepen understanding of how scientific knowledge is created, disseminated, and applied within complex knowledge networks.

“By 2027, we plan to build a world scientific citation database covering more than ten major languages, including Chinese, English, and Russian, with citation data reaching the scale of billions,” Huang said, outlining a phased development roadmap for the project. “This will enable us to provide decision-support services for research management authorities. By 2030, we aim to develop it into a globally significant research infrastructure that accelerates the dissemination of scientific discoveries and helps shape an open and innovation-driven research ecosystem.”

Wu Jing, a member of the leading Party group of the Ministry of Science and Technology and President of Science and Technology Daily, also emphasized the importance of strengthening evaluation systems for high-quality scientific journals. She highlighted the need to deepen international collaboration and further integrate digital technologies to support the ongoing transformation of academic publishing.

International perspectives at the forum underscored similar themes. Arnold Pippel, Senior Vice President of the Dutch academic publishing group Elsevier, noted that scientific journals must adapt to the changing global research landscape by innovating publishing models, strengthening standards of research integrity, and embracing emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence.

Coexistence of mixing and rigid behaviors in ergodic theory
Rigoberto Zelada

Abstract
In this paper we introduce and explore the notion of rigidity group , associated with a collection of finitely many sequences, and show that this concept has many, somewhat surprising characterizations of algebraic, spectral, and unitary nature. Furthermore, we demonstrate that these characterizations can be employed to obtain various results in the theory of generic Lebesgue-preserving automorphisms of [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] , IP-ergodic theory, multiple recurrence, additive combinatorics, and spectral theory. As a consequence of one of our results we show that given ( b 1 , … ​ b ℓ ) ∈ ℕ ℓ (b_{1},...b_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell} , there is no orthogonal vector ( a 1 , … , a ℓ ) ∈ ℤ ℓ (a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell} with some | a j | = 1 |a_{j}|=1 if and only if there is an increasing sequence of natural numbers ( n k ) k ∈ ℕ (n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}} with the property that for each F ⊆ { 1 , … , ℓ } F\subseteq\{1,...,\ell\} there is a μ \mu -preserving transformation T F : [ 0 , 1 ] → [ 0 , 1 ] T_{F}:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1] ( μ \mu denotes the Lebesgue measure) such that for any measurable A , B ⊆ [ 0 , 1 ] A,B\subseteq[0,1] , lim k → ∞ μ ⁡ ( A ∩ T F − b j ​ n k ​ B ) = { μ ⁡ ( A ∩ B ) , if ​ j ∈ F , μ ⁡ ( A ) ​ μ ​ ( B ) , if ​ j ∉ F . \lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T_{F}^{-b_{j}n_{k}}B)=\begin{cases}\mu(A\cap B),\,\text{ if }j\in F,\\ \mu(A)\mu(B),\,\text{ if }j\not\in F.\end{cases} We remark that this result has a natural extension to a wide class of families of sequences.

Introduction

In this paper we deal with the concept of
group of rigidity
which, as we explain below, traces back its roots to the work of V. Bergelson, S. Kasjan, and M. Lemańczyk
[
8
]
who dealt with the closely related notions of
N-rigidity group
and
group of global rigidity
. The concept of group of rigidity has found multiple (tacit) applications to IP-ergodic theory
[
8
]
, the study of generic properties of Lebesgue preserving automorphisms of
[0,1)

[
34
]
, spectral decomposition of unitary operators
[
8
]
, mixing properties of Gaussian systems
[
34
]
, and combinatorics
[
11
]
. Our goal in this paper is to obtain various "elementary" characterizations for the concept of group of rigidity and then utilize these results to obtain applications to ergodic theory and combinatorics.

1.1
Historical motivation and background

Let
([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
be the probability space
where
\mathcal{B}=\text{Borel}([0,1])
and
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure. We denote the set of all invertible
\mu
-preserving transformations by
\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
and endow it with the so called
weak topology
(see (
5.5
) for the definition). With this topology,
\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
becomes a completely metrisable topological group.

Two of the most interesting dynamical phenomena for
\mu
-preserving transformations are those of
weak mixing
and
rigidity
.

Among the many equivalent definitions of weak mixing (see
[
6
]
, for example), we have that a
\mu
-preserving transformation
T
is called
weakly mixing
if there exists an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-n_{k}}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B).

As proved by Halmos
[
23
]
, the set
\mathcal{W}
formed by the weakly mixing
\mu
-preserving transformations
T\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
is a dense
G_{\delta}
set.

A
\mu
-preserving transformation
T
is called
rigid
if there exists an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-n_{k}}B)=\mu(A\cap B).

A "folklore" result (see
[
3
, Proposition 2.9]
, for example), whose proof may trace back its main idea to
[
29
]
(see also the proof of "First Category Theorem" in page 77 of
[
24
]
), states that the set
\mathcal{R}
of
\mu
-preserving, rigid transformations
T\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
is a dense
G_{\delta}
set.

It follows that
\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{R}
is a dense
G_{\delta}
set and, therefore,
\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{R}\neq\emptyset
. Thus, one can find a
T\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
and increasing sequences
(w_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-w_{k}}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B)

and

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-r_{k}}B)=\mu(A\cap B).

However, it does not follow from the fact that
\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{R}\neq\emptyset
(or any of the refinements of this result that could be obtained from
[
3
]
or
[
4
]
) that one can take, for example, the sequence
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to satisfy the equation
r_{k}=w_{k}^{2}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. More generally, one may wonder for which sequences
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}
the following question has a positive answer:

Question 1.1
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\ell>1
and let
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\phi_{j}(n)|=\infty
for each
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
. Is there a
T\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
and an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that for every
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-\phi_{j}(n_{k})}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B)

if
j
is even and

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-\phi_{j}(n_{k})}B)=\mu(A\cap B)

if
j
is odd?

The first results dealing with (the "unitary" variants of)
Question

1.1
can be found in
[
8
]
, where the authors introduced the concepts of
N
-rigidity group
and
group of global rigidity
to deal with this type of question. As it is not part of the scope of this introduction to define any of these two notions, we limit ourselves to remark that (a) our definition of a
group of rigidity
(see (
1.6
) below) was motivated by both of these concepts, (b) while the concept of a group of rigidity deals with regular convergence and a wide class of families of sequences (see the concept of
adequate sequences
below), the concepts of
N
-rigidity group and that of group of global rigidity are much more restricted (they only deal with convergence along idempotent ultrafilters and families of polynomial sequences), and (c) the counterparts of the concepts of
N
-rigidity group and group of global rigidity in the context of regular convergence and adequate sequences coincide and are equivalent to the notion of group of rigidity (in a way, this is the content of our main technical result,
Theorem

1.8
below).

The next result,
Theorem

1.2
below, which follows from
[
8
, Theorem E]
and
[
8
, Lemma 3.15]
, provides a partial answer to
Question

1.1
(and some of its potential "polynomial" variants). We remark that
Theorem

1.2
epitomizes well one of the types of results that are intrinsically connected with the concept of group of rigidity. Note the "importance" of the groups generated by any of the sequences

\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}.

For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}=\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}x^{j}\,|\,a_{1},...,a_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be non-constant polynomials with zero constant term, and let
F\subseteq\{1,...,\ell\}
. Set
d=\max_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\deg(\phi_{j})
.
The following statements are equivalent:

1.

There exists a finite index subgroup
H
of
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}
with the property that for each
j\in F
,
\phi_{j}\in H
and for each
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}\setminus F
,
\phi_{j}\not\in H
.

2.

There exist a
T\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
and an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-\phi_{j}(n_{k})}B)=\begin{cases}\mu(A\cap B),\,\text{ if }j\in F,\\
\mu(A)\mu(B),\,\text{ if }j\not\in F,\end{cases}

(1.1)

for every
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
and every
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 1.3
.

To illustrate the type of results that one can obtain with the help of

as well as the complexity involved in answering

, we now provide a few examples.

1.

(Cf.

(Cf.

Question 1.4
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. For which sequences
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is it true that
for each
\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{\ell})\in[0,1]^{\ell}
one has that the set

\mathcal{G}_{\vec{\lambda}}(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}):=\{T\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)\,|\exists(n_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ in }\mathbb{N}\text{ with }\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}n_{t}=\infty\,\forall j\in\{1,...,\ell\},\,\\
\forall A,B\in\mathcal{B},\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-\phi_{j}(n_{t})}B)=(1-\lambda_{j})\mu(A\cap B)+\lambda_{j}\mu(A)\mu(B)\}

(1.2)

is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
?

The following consequence of our main technical result,
Theorem

1.8
below, provides a full answer to
Question

1.4
under a natural assumption on the sequences
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}
(see
Remark

1.6
below for relevant comments). The sequences
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
are called

if for every
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\phi_{j}(n)|=\infty

(1.3)

and for any
a_{1},...,a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\phi_{j}(n)|\in\{0,\infty\}.

(1.4)

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be such that for any
a_{1},...,a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\phi_{j}(n)

(1.5)

exists as an integer,
\infty
, or
-\infty
.
The following statements are equivalent:

1.

The sequences
\phi_{1},...\phi_{\ell}
are adequate and for any
(a_{1},...,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}

with

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\phi_{j}(n)=0,

we have that for each
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
,
|a_{j}|\neq 1
.

2.

For each
\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{\ell})\in[0,1]^{\ell}
, the set

\mathcal{G}_{\vec{\lambda}}(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell})=\{T\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)\,|\exists(n_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ in }\mathbb{N}\text{ with }\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}n_{t}=\infty\,\forall j\in\{1,...,\ell\},\,\\
\forall A,B\in\mathcal{B},\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-\phi_{j}(n_{t})}B)=(1-\lambda_{j})\mu(A\cap B)+\lambda_{j}\mu(A)\mu(B)\}

is a dense
G_{\delta}
set.

3.

There exists an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that for each
\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{\ell})\in[0,1]^{\ell}
there exists a weakly mixing
T_{\vec{\lambda}}\in\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
such that for any
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
and any
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T_{\vec{\lambda}}^{-\phi_{j}(n_{k})}B)=(1-\lambda_{j})\mu(A\cap B)+\lambda_{j}\mu(A)\mu(B).

Remark 1.6
.

Observe that assumption (
1.5
) in the statement of
Theorem

1.5
can be viewed as an "irreducibility" property of the sequences
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}
: For arbitrary sequences
\psi_{1},...,\psi_{\ell}
one can always find an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that for any
a_{1},...,a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\psi_{j}(n_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}.

Remark 1.7
.

Suppose that
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
satisfy (
1.5
) and condition (1) in
Theorem

1.5
. It is worth noting that for any increasing sequence
(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
, the sequences
(\phi_{j}(m_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
, also satisfy (
1.5
) and condition (1) in
Theorem

1.5
. Thus, in a certain way,
Theorem

1.5
deals not only with the sequence
(\phi_{1}(n),...,\phi_{\ell}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
but also with every subsequence of the form
(\phi_{1}(m_{k}),...,\phi_{\ell}(m_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. This observation, together with the complete metrisability of
\text{Aut}([0,1],\mathcal{B},\mu)
, are the key ingredients for the proof of (2)
\implies
(3) in
Theorem

1.5
(see Section 5).

In a way, the concept of adequate sequences is a far reaching generalization of the concept of families of polynomials with zero constant term.
It includes
\ell
-tuples of sequences of "shifted" polynomials like

\phi_{1}(n)=n+1,\phi_{2}(n)=n^{2}+2,...,\phi_{\ell}(n)=n^{\ell}+\ell

and somewhat more general families of sequences such as

\phi_{1}(n)=\lfloor n\alpha_{1}\rfloor,\phi_{2}(n)=\lfloor n\alpha_{2}\rfloor,...,\phi_{\ell}(n)=\lfloor n\alpha_{\ell}\rfloor,

where
\alpha_{1},..,\alpha_{\ell}
are non-zero, rationally independent real numbers and for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lfloor r\rfloor
denotes the largest integer smaller than or equal to
r
.

1.2
Definition and characterizations of a rigidity group

Before stating our main technical result (
Theorem

1.8
below), we need to introduce the main concepts utilized in this paper.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be adequate sequences, and let
G
be a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.

•

G
is called a
group of rigidity
for the (adequate) sequences
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}
(or, simply, a
(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell})
-rigidity group
) if there is a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
and a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
for which the set

H(U,(n_{k})):=\{(a_{1},...,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\,|\,\forall f\in\mathcal{H},\,\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|U^{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\phi_{j}(n_{k})}f-f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=0\}

(1.6)

satisfies
G=H(U,(n_{k}))
. Note that for every unitary
U
and every increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
,
H(U,(n_{k}))
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.

•

We will let
A_{G}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{\ell}
denote the
annihilator of
G
. In other words,

A_{G}:=\{(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{\ell})\in\mathbb{T}^{\ell}\,|\,\forall(a_{1},...,a_{\ell})\in G,\,e^{2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\alpha_{j}}=1\}.

We denote the normalized Haar measure of
A_{G}
by
\lambda_{G}
.

•

We will let

A(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\ell}):=\{(a_{1},...,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\,|\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\phi_{j}(n)=0\}.

Observe that

is always a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.

Corollary

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell\geq 2
, and let
p_{1},...,p_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be non-constant polynomials with zero constant term. Suppose that
p_{1},...,p_{\ell}
are linearly independent. Then, there exist an invertible measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{A},\nu,T)
, a set

with
\nu(A)>0
, and an
\epsilon>0
for which the set

IP
∗
.

It is worth mentioning that

1.10
is somewhat unexpected. This is because the results in
[
18
]
and
[
9
]
confirm that, when

are

. More precisely, by
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
, we know that every set of the form

is syndetic (meaning that it has bounded gaps) and by a generalization of
[

to appear in
[

R_{\epsilon}^{p_{1},...,p_{\ell}}(A)

is "almost" IP
∗

∗
. (A set
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is called A-IP
∗
if there exists a set
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
with

and such that

is IP
∗

[
5
]
show that every set of the form

is IP
∗
(and, so,

1.10
does not hold when
\ell=1

∗

∗
set) is syndetic
[

Invoking the "inverse Furstenberg’s correspondence principle" (See

]
,
[
28
]
and
[

]
,
[
17
]
for related results), we obtain the following "combinatorial" interpretation of these observations.

Proposition 1.11

Let

\ell\geq 2

Lemma 2.17

Let

34

6.1
IP-convergence for unitary operators and a variant of (ii)
\implies
(iii) in
Theorem

1.8

Denote the set of all finite, non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{N}
by
\mathcal{F}
and let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space. For any
\mathcal{F}
-indexed sequence
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}
, we write

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}x_{\alpha}=x

if there exists an
x\in X
with the property that for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
k_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
\min\alpha>k_{\epsilon}
, then
d(x_{\alpha},x)<\epsilon
. We remark in passing that, by Hindman’s theorem
[
25
]
, for any
\mathcal{F}
-indexed sequence
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}
in
X
, there is a sequence of disjoint, non-empty, and finite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
,
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, for which the
\mathcal{F}
-indexed sequence
y_{\alpha}:=x_{\bigcup_{k\in\alpha}\beta_{k}}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}
, satisfies

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}y_{\alpha}=x

for some
x\in X
.

The following is a consequence of (the proof of)

[
8
, Proposition 1.7]
,
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
, and the equivalence between IP-limits and convergence along idempotent ultrafilters (see
[
13
, p.36]
or
[
8
, Lemma 1.1]
, for example). We omit the proof. For any sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
\mathbb{Z}
and any
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}
, we will let
n_{\alpha}=\sum_{k\in\alpha}n_{k}
.

Lemma 6.30
.

Let
D,N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
D\leq N
and let
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a unitary operator. Let
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence in
\mathbb{N}
with the properties that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k!|n_{k}
, for every
a_{1},...,a_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}U^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}n_{k}^{j}}\in\{\text{Id},0\}

in the weak operator topology, and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}U^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}n_{k}^{j}}=0
whenever
\deg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}x^{j})\geq D
. Furthermore, assume that for each
j\in\{1,...,D\}\setminus\{D\}
, there is an
r_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
for which

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}U^{r_{j}n_{k}^{j}}=\text{Id}.

Then, there exists an increasing sequence
(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that for any
a_{1},...,a_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}U^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}m_{\alpha}^{j}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}U^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}n_{k}^{j}}

in the weak operator topology.

We are now in position to state the variant of (ii)
\implies
(iii) in
Theorem

1.8
dealing with IP-convergence.

Theorem 6.31
.

Let
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
d<N
and let
G
be a finite index subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Then, there exist a Borel probability measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
and an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that for any continuous
f:\mathbb{T}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and any measurable
E\subseteq\mathbb{T}
,

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)f(n_{\alpha}x,n_{\alpha}^{2}x,...,n_{\alpha}^{N}x)\text{d}\sigma(x)=\sigma(E)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(y_{1},...,y_{N})\text{d}\lambda_{G\times\{\vec{0}\}}(y_{1},...,y_{N}),

(6.1)

where
\vec{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N-d}
.

Proof.

Consider the adequate sequences
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{N}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
definde by
\phi_{j}(n)=(n!)^{j}
,
j\in\{1,...,N\}
, and note that
A(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{N})=\{\vec{0}\}
. By item (iii) in
Theorem

1.8
(or, rather, by (
1.10
)), we can find a subsequence
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(k!)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
with the property that for any
a_{1},...,a_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}U^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}p_{k}^{j}}=\begin{cases}\text{Id},\,\text{ if }(a_{1},...,a_{N})\in G\times\{\vec{0}\},\\
0,\text{ if }(a_{1},...,a_{N})\not\in G\times\{\vec{0}\}.\end{cases}

By
Lemma

6.30
applied with
D=d+1
, it follows that there exists an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
with the property that

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}U^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}n_{\alpha}^{j}}=\begin{cases}\text{Id},\,\text{ if }(a_{1},...,a_{N})\in G\times\{\vec{0}\},\\
0,\text{ if }(a_{1},...,a_{N})\not\in G\times\{\vec{0}\},\end{cases}

(6.2)

for every
a_{1},...,a_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}
be such that
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1
. By Bochner’s theorem there is a Borel probability measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
with the property that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\langle U^{n}f,f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{2\pi inx}\text{d}\sigma(x).

So, in particular, (
6.2
) implies that for any measurable
E\subseteq\mathbb{T}
and any
a_{1},...,a_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)e^{2\pi i(\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}n_{\alpha}^{j})x}\text{d}\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}\sigma(E),\,\text{ if }(a_{1},...,a_{N})\in G\times\{\vec{0}\},\\
0,\text{ if }(a_{1},...,a_{N})\not\in G\times\{\vec{0}\},\end{cases}

which is equivalent to (
6.1
). We are done.
∎

The proof of
Corollary

Proof of
Corollary

1.10
.

The proof of
Corollary

1.10
consists of two major steps. First, we will use

6.31
to find an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
and a Borel probability measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
with
ad-hoc
properties. Then, we will use the probability measure
\sigma
to define the invertible probability preserving system

for which

1.10
holds.

∎
Finding
\sigma
and
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
p_{1},...,p_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be linearly independent polynomials with zero constant term and let
D=\max_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\deg(p_{j})
. For each
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
, let
\vec{c}_{j}=(c_{j,1},...,c_{j,D})\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
be such that

p_{j}(x)=\sum_{s=1}^{D}c_{j,s}x^{s}.

Let

G^{\prime}:=\{a_{0}(-2\vec{c}_{1}+\vec{c}_{2})+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}3\vec{c}_{j}\,|\,a_{0},...,a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

If
G^{\prime}
has finite index in
\mathbb{Z}^{D}
, we set
G=G^{\prime}
. If not, let
\vec{d}_{1},...,\vec{d}_{D-\ell}
be linearly independent vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{D}
with the additional property that for any
j\in\{1,...,\ell\}
and any
s\in\{1,...,D-\ell\}

\langle\vec{c}_{j},\vec{d}_{s}\rangle=0
(such vectors can be obtained by taking a rational basis of the orthogonal complement of
\text{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\vec{c}_{1},...,\vec{c}_{\ell}\}
and then multiplying each member of this basis by an appropriate integer). We set

G=\{\vec{c}+\sum_{s=1}^{D-\ell}a_{s}3\vec{d}_{s}\,|\,\vec{c}\in G^{\prime}\text{ and }a_{1},...,a_{D-\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}\}

Notice that, in either case, we obtain that (G.1)
M\mathbb{Z}^{D}\subseteq G
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
and, so, that
G
has finite index in
\mathbb{Z}^{D}
and (G.2)
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\vec{c}_{j}\in G^{\prime}
if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\vec{c}_{j}\in G
.

Applying
Theorem

6.31
in the case that
d=D
and
N>D
is arbitrary, we see that there exist an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
and a Borel probability measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
such that
for every
a_{1},...,a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}p_{j}(n_{\alpha})x}\text{d}\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\vec{c}_{j}\in G,\\
0,\text{ if }\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\vec{c}_{j}\not\in G.\end{cases}

Invoking (G.2) above and setting

H=\{(a_{1},...,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\,|\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\vec{c}_{j}\in G^{\prime}\},

it follows that

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(p_{1}(n_{\alpha})x,...,p_{\ell}(n_{\alpha})x)\text{d}\sigma(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\ell}}f(y_{1},...,y_{\ell})\text{d}\lambda_{H}(y_{1},...,y_{\ell})

(6.3)

for any continuous function
f:\mathbb{T}^{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Observe that, when we identify
\mathbb{T}^{\ell}
with
[0,1)^{\ell}
,
\lambda_{H}
corresponds to the normalized Haar measure on

\{\left(a_{0}(\frac{1}{3}\vec{e}_{1}+\frac{2}{3}\vec{e}_{2})+\sum_{s=3}^{\ell}a_{s}\frac{1}{3}\vec{e}_{s}\right)\mod 1\,|\,a_{0},a_{3},...,a_{\ell}\in\{0,1,2\}\}.

(6.4)

∎
Defining
(X,\mathcal{A},\nu,T)
.
Set
X=[0,1)^{2}
,
\mathcal{A}=\text{Borel}([0,1)^{2})
, and let
\nu:=\sigma\times\mu
. Define
T:X\rightarrow X
by
T(x,y)=(x,y+x)\mod 1
. Clearly,
T
is an invertible,
\nu
preserving transformation. Consider the set
A=\mathbb{T}\times[0,2/3)
and let
\epsilon=\frac{1}{3^{\ell+1}}
.

∎
Formula (
1.13
) holds with
A=\mathbb{T}\times[0,2/3)
and
\epsilon=\frac{1}{3^{\ell+1}}
.
Observe that, by (
6.3
) and the (proof of the) continuous mapping theorem (see
[
32
, Remarks in page 149]
, for example), for
\mu
-a.e.
y\in[0,1)
,

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(y+p_{j}(n_{\alpha})x)\text{d}\sigma(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\ell}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(y+y_{j})\text{d}\lambda_{H}(y_{1},...,y_{\ell}).

It then follows from the definition of
\nu
,
T
, and
A
that

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\nu(A\cap T^{-p_{1}(n_{\alpha})}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-p_{\ell}(n_{\alpha})}A)\\
=\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x,y)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x,y+p_{j}(n_{\alpha})x)\text{d}\sigma(x)\text{d}\mu(y)\\
=\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(y)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(y+p_{j}(n_{\alpha})x)\text{d}\sigma(x)\text{d}\mu(y)\\

(6.5)

Thus, by (

),

\mathop{\text{IP-lim}}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}\nu(A\cap T^{-p_{1}(n_{\alpha})}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-p_{\ell}(n_{\alpha})}A)\\
=\frac{1}{3^{\ell-1}}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\sum_{s_{3},...,s_{\ell}=0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(y)\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(\frac{n}{3}+y)\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(\frac{2n}{3}+y)\prod_{j=3}^{\ell}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2/3)}(\frac{s_{j}}{3}+y)\text{d}\mu(y)\\

(6.6)

\frac{2^{\ell+1}}{3^{\ell+1}}-\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{3^{\ell}}=\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{3^{\ell+1}}\geq 2\epsilon,

the definition of IP-limit allows us to find a

such that

\{\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{k_{s}}\,|\,k_{0}<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{t},\,t\in\mathbb{N}\}\cap\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,\nu(A\cap T^{-p_{1}(n)}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-p_{\ell}(n)}A)>\nu^{\ell+1}(A)-\epsilon\}=\emptyset,

which proves that (
1.13
) holds. We are done.
∎

On the Cox rings of some hypersurfaces
Andrew Pollock, Atsushi Ito, Balázs Szendrői

Abstract
. We introduce a cohomological method to compute Cox rings of hypersurfaces in the ambient space ℙ 1 × ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n} , which is more direct than existing methods. We prove that smooth hypersurfaces defined by regular sequences of coefficients are Mori dream spaces, generalizing a result of Ottem. We also compute Cox rings of certain specialized examples. In particular, we compute Cox rings in the well-studied family of Calabi–Yau threefolds of bidegree ( 2 , 4 ) (2,4) in ℙ 1 × ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3} , determining explicitly how the Cox ring can jump discontinuously in a smooth family.



Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
, with Picard group
\Pic X
a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module for simplicity. The Cox ring

\mathcal{R}(X)=\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic X}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))

has the natural structure of a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra graded by
\Pic X
; details including a careful definition can be found in
[
2
]
. Let
\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
be a smooth or mildly singular hypersurface, defined by a homogeneous equation
r\in\mathcal{R}(X)
. Under suitable conditions, the restriction map
\Pic(X)\to\Pic(Z)

is an isomorphism; we will assume this for the rest of the Introduction, and identify the Picard groups via restriction.
Alongside the restriction map on line bundles, we also have a restriction map between Cox rings

\iota^{*}\colon\mathcal{R}(X)\to\mathcal{R}(Z).

This map was first studied explicitly by Hausen and Artebani–Laface
[
6
,
1
]
, giving conditions for
\iota^{*}
to be surjective, leading to an isomorphism

\mathcal{R}(Z)\cong\mathcal{R}(X)/\langle r\rangle.

The aim of our paper is to give a new, explicit method to compute the Cox rings of some hypersurfaces
Z
in the specific ambient space
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. In this case, the map
\iota^{*}\colon\mathcal{R}(X)\to\mathcal{R}(Z)
is not surjective, with
O_{Z}(D|_{Z})
having sections that do not come from
O_{X}(D)
for certain
D
.

We first define the hypersurfaces studied in this paper. Throughout, we fix
n\geq 3
and
d,e\geq 1
.

Definition 0.1
.

Let
Z
be an irreducible and normal hypersurface

(1)

Z=\left\{g_{0}x_{0}^{d}+g_{1}x_{0}^{d-1}x_{1}+\ldots+g_{d}x_{1}^{d}=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}

defined by a set of homogeneous degree
e
polynomials
g_{0},\dots,g_{d}\in\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
.

(i)

We say
Z
admits an
index sequence of length
l
, with
1\leq l\leq d
, if there are indices
0=i_{0}<\cdots<i_{l}=d
such that
g_{i}\neq 0
if and only if
i\in\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
; we call the set of indices
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
the
index sequence
of
Z
.

(ii)

We call
Z

regular with index sequence
\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{l}\}
, if it is non-singular, admits the index sequence
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
, and the homogeneous polynomials
g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}
form a regular sequence in the graded ring
\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
.

Note that
amongst hypersurfaces
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
admitting a given index sequence
\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{l}\}
, being regular with index sequence
\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{l}\}
is an open condition.

The following is our main result (see Theorem
2.9
, Proposition
2.10
).

Theorem 0.2
.

Suppose that
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a nonsingular hypersurface of the form (
1
),
with
n\geq 3
and
d,e\geq 1
, admitting an index sequence
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
of length
l
.

(i)

Suppose that
Z
is regular with index sequence
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
, that is
g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}
form a regular sequence in
\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
. Then there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded algebras

(2)

\mathcal{R}(Z)\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},\dots,y_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{l}]/I,

where
\deg(x_{i})=(1,0)
,
\deg(y_{j})=(0,1)
,
\deg(w_{k})=(i_{k-1}-i_{k},e)
for relevant
i,j,k
and
I
is the homogeneous ideal

I=\langle x_{1}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}w_{1}+g_{0},x_{1}^{i_{2}-i_{1}}w_{2}+g_{1}-x_{0}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}w_{1},\ldots,g_{d}-x_{0}^{i_{l}-i_{l-1}}w_{l}\rangle.

In particular,
Z
is a Mori dream space.

(ii)

Conversely, suppose that there exists an isomorphism of the form (
2
), with degrees and ideal
I
as given. Then
g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}
form a regular sequence in
\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
. In other words,
Z
is regular with index sequence
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
.

This result substantially strengthens a result of Ottem
[
8
]
, who proves (i) only for general
Z
admitting full index sequence
\{0,1,\dots,d\}
. It also provides a converse statement.

Our proofs use standard cohomological machinery to reduce the problem to an algebraic one, involving syzygies of the set
\{g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}\}
of elements of
R
, as well as induction. Our arguments are longer than those of
[
8
]
, but are more direct, able to handle also the more general setting of Theorem
0.2
. More details, and further results based on the method, will be presented in
[
9
]
. Our examples can also be studied using work of Herrera, Laface and Ugaglia
[
7
]
, who use yet another method involving localizations of the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(X)
of the embedding space
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
; our approach gives a natural reason for the appearance of these localizations.

For
n=3
and
(d,e)=(2,4)
, the family

{\mathcal{Z}}=\left\{g_{0}x_{0}^{2}+tg_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+g_{2}x_{1}^{2}=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}

is a well-studied family of Calabi–Yau threefolds, see in particular the recent
[
3
,
4
]
. In this case, Theorem
0.2
shows that this is an example of a smooth family of Calabi–Yau threefolds in which the Cox ring jumps on a closed subset of the moduli space; see Theorem
3.2
. We also discuss a further, singular example in this family in Example
3.3
.

Theorem
0.2
applies in particular when
Z
is regular and
e=1
,
with the projection

being a
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
-bundle. If

, then the linear forms

are even linearly dependent, and certainly cannot form a regular sequence, so by Theorem

(ii), the Cox ring is not of the form (

), contrary to the statement
[

. We discuss one case in Example

Finally in Example

that played an important role in Ottem’s arguments
[

.

Section 1 explains our cohomological approach to the problem. Section 2 is the main part of our paper, where we build up to the proof of our main results, modulo an algebraic statement that is relegated to Section 4. Section 3 discusses our examples.

1.
Our approach

1.1.
Basics

Recall our setup from the Introduction: assume that
\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
is a smooth or mildly singular hypersurface, with an isomorphism
\Pic(X)\cong\Pic(Z)
, a finitely generated free abelian group that we will denote by
\Pic
.
Let
E=[Z]\in\Pic
be the class of
Z
.

The Cox rings
\mathcal{R}(X)
and
\mathcal{R}(Z)
are both
\Pic
-graded algebras

\mathcal{R}(X)=\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic}\mathcal{R}(X)_{D},\ \ \ \mathcal{R}(Z)=\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic}\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D},

connected by the graded homomorphism
\iota^{*}\colon\mathcal{R}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(Z)
.
The interesting case for us is when the inclusion
\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}(Z)
is not surjective.

The hypersurface
Z
is given by a section
r\in\mathcal{R}(X)_{E}
. For
D\in\Pic
, consider the standard exact sequence

(3)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot r}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{Z}(D)\longrightarrow 0.

The associated long exact sequence yields the short exact sequence of vector spaces

(4)

0\longrightarrow\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)_{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}N_{D}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)_{D}\cong\mathcal{R}(X)_{D}/r\cdot\mathcal{R}(X)_{D-E}
, and
N_{D}=\ker r_{D*}
is the kernel of the induced map on first sheaf cohomology

(5)

r_{D*}:H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E))\longrightarrow H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)).

It will sometimes be natural to put all these maps together, to get the map

(6)

r_{*}:\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic}H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{D\in\Pic}H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)).

We have
\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)=\mathcal{R}(Z)
if and only if the map
r_{*}
is injective.

Lemma 1.1
.

Consider Čech
0
-cochains
(v_{i})\in\prod_{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)(U_{i})
. Call a
0
-cochain
(v_{i})
compatible (with respect to
Z
) if for each
i<j
there exists
q_{i,j}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E)(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
such that
v_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}-v_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}=q_{i,j}r
. Call compatible
0
-cochains
(v_{i}),(v^{\prime}_{i})
equivalent, if for each
i
, there exists
q_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E)(U_{i})
such that
v^{\prime}_{i}-v_{i}=q_{i}r
.

(i)

Elements
v\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}
are naturally in bijection with equivalence classes of compatible
0
-cochains

(v_{i})\in\prod_{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)(U_{i}).

Under this equivalence, the restriction of a section
g\in\mathcal{R}(X)_{D}
corresponds to the Čech
0
-cocycle
(g|_{U_{i}})\in\prod_{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)(U_{i})
.

(ii)

Multiplication in the algebra
\mathcal{R}(Z)
is compatible with multiplication of cochains: if
D,D^{\prime}\in\Pic
and
v\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}
,
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D^{\prime}}
are represented by compatible
0

(v_{i}),(v^{\prime}_{i})
respectively, then
vv^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D+D^{\prime}}
is represented by the compatible
0
-cochain
(v_{i}v^{\prime}_{i})
.

Proof.

These statements follow from considering the first column of (
7
), recalling that the global section functor on sheaves is exact over affine schemes. Note that a
0
-cochain
(v_{i})
restricts to a
0
-cocycle on

if and only if it is compatible.
∎

We are interested in cases when there are equivalence classes of compatible
0
-cochains
(v_{i})
that do not come from the restriction of a section
g\in\mathcal{R}(X)
, so that
(v_{i})
is not a
0
-cocycle.
For

, in order to find a complementary vector space to
\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)_{D}
in
\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}
, we need to complete the first three steps below.

(1)

Give an explicit description of the first sheaf cohomologies of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E)
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
and of the map
r_{D*}
of (
5
).

(2)

Describe the kernel
N_{D}=\ker r_{D*}
as a subspace of
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E))
.

(3)

Choose a right inverse
\epsilon\colon N_{D}\to\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}
of
\delta
that lifts elements of
N_{D}
to sections on
Z
.

(4)

Study the ring structure of the direct sum of the resulting spaces to reconstruct the whole structure of
\mathcal{R}(Z)
.

Steps (1) and (2) have to be completed in particular cases of interest by explicit calculation. Finding a map
\epsilon
in step (3) comes down to untangling the connecting homomorphism
\delta
. Given
s\in N_{D}
for some
D\in\Pic
, let

(s_{i,j})\in\prod_{i<j}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-E)(U_{i}\cap U_{j})

be a representative
1
-cocycle. Then
(r\cdot s_{i,j})
is cohomologically trivial, thus the image of a
0
-cochain

(v_{i})\in\prod_{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)(U_{i}),

which is compatible since
v_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}-v_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}=s_{i,j}r
for each
i<j
. This compatible
0
-cochain corresponds via Lemma
1.1
to an element
v\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}
that satisfies
\delta v=s
. Note that the element
v
is only well-defined up to an element in
\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)_{D}
, since
(v_{i})
is well-defined only up to an element of
\ker d^{0}=\mathcal{R}(X)_{D}
. To construct a map
\epsilon
, we need to make compatible choices so that we indeed end up with a well-defined lifting map

\epsilon\colon N_{D}\to\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D},

a right inverse to
\delta
. To complete step (4), further arguments are needed that will be based on Lemma
1.1
(ii).

To conclude the general discussion, let us make one further remark. Suppose that
v\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}
is represented by a compatible
0
-cochain
(v_{i})
via Lemma
1.1
(i). For fixed
i\in I
, we have
v_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})(D)
, and thus
v_{i}=\frac{h_{i}}{g_{i}}
for some regular functions
h_{i},g_{i}
on
X
with
g_{i}
not vanishing on
U_{i}
. Identifying
h_{i}
and
g_{i}
with their restrictions on
Z
, the section
g_{i}v_{i}-h_{i}
is zero on
Z\cap U_{i}
, which is open and dense in
Z
. For each
i
, we thus obtain an equation

g_{i}v-h_{i}=0

in
\mathcal{R}(Z)
. This explains the relevance of localisations of
\mathcal{R}(X)
for the problem, which is the basis of the approach taken in
[
7
]
.

2.
Cox rings of hypersurfaces in a product of projective spaces

2.1.
Basics

Fix
n\geqslant 3
and let
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We use homogeneous coordinates
x_{0},x_{1}
and
y_{0},\dots,y_{n}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\mathbb{P}^{n}
respectively. If
p_{1},p_{2}
are the projection maps to the respective factors
\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then
\Pic X\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is generated by the pullbacks
p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
and
p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
respectively. The Cox ring of
X
is

S=\mathcal{R}(X)=\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},\dots,y_{n}],

with the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-grading given by
\deg(x_{i})=(1,0)
and
\deg(y_{j})=(0,1)
. The ring

R=\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{P}^{n})=\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]

is naturally a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded subring of
S
, making
S
into a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded
R
-module.

Fix

n\geq 3
and
d,e\geq 1
. Let
r\in S_{(d,e)}
and

Z=\left\{r=0\right\}\subset X

be the corresponding hypersurface of bidegree
(d,e)
in
X
. Then
Z
is defined by the bihomogeneous section

(8)

r=g_{0}x_{0}^{d}+g_{1}x_{0}^{d-1}x_{1}+\ldots+g_{d}x_{1}^{d}

for some
g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\in R_{e}
. If
Z
is nonsingular, the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem shows that the inclusion
\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
induces an isomorphism of Picard groups
\iota^{*}\colon\Pic X\cong\Pic Z\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that will be denoted
\Pic
. We will also consider a singular case, where we will comment on the relation between the Picard groups explicitly.

We require an ordered open cover
\mathcal{W}
of
X
. For
i=0,1
, let
U_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the standard affine open defined by
x_{i}\neq 0
. For
j=0,\dots,n
, let
V_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be the standard affine open defined by
y_{j}\neq 0
. Set
W_{ij}=U_{i}\times V_{j}
for each
i,j
and let
\mathcal{W}=\{W_{ij}\}_{i,j}
, with the lexicographic ordering. If
ij<kl
, then denote
W_{ij}\cap W_{kl}
by
W_{ij,kl}
.

2.2.
The degeneracy locus of the second projection

Consider the projection
p_{2}\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
restricted to
Z
. This is generically a
d
-to-one cover, but it has a degeneracy locus

Y=\{g_{0}=g_{1}=\cdots=g_{d}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n},

over which the fibres are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let

I_{Y}=\langle g_{0},g_{1},\ldots g_{d}\rangle\lhd R=\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\ldots,y_{n}]

be the corresponding ideal. The quotient
R/I_{Y}
has a finite free
R
-module resolution of the form

(9)

\ldots\longrightarrow R^{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{d+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\longrightarrow}}R\longrightarrow R/I_{Y}\longrightarrow 0,

where we suppressed degrees, with
A=(g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
, and
B,C
matrices of homogeneous elements of the appropriate sizes and degrees.

Of particular interest to us is the case where
Z
is regular with index sequence
\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{l}\}
. It is easy to check that there are always such choices with
Z\subset X
nonsingular. Then (
9
) is the sum of the standard Koszul complex for
\{g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}\}
and a trivial complex. The locus
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a complete intersection of codimension
l+1
, and the inverse image
E=p_{2}^{-1}(Y)\subset X
has codimension
l
in
Z
. In the extreme case, where
l=1
and
r=g_{0}x_{0}^{d}+g_{d}x_{1}^{d}
, the variety
E
is a divisor ruled over
Y
.

2.3.
The case
d=0

The case
d=0
is somewhat degenerate, but it will be useful to include as it provides a convenient case for our induction. For
d=0
,
Z\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times Z_{1}
, with
Z_{1}=\{g_{0}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
. If moreover

, then clearly
\mathcal{R}(Z)\cong\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n})/\langle g_{0}\rangle
, compatibly with the statement of Theorem
0.2
. However, if
(n,d)=(3,0)
, then
\Pic X
and
\Pic Z
may not be isomorphic and Theorem

may fail to hold. Indeed, for example if
(n,d,e)=(3,0,2)
and

is a general quadric, then

Z\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times Q\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

with

a general quadric surface, so that there is a
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
-graded isomorphism

\mathcal{R}(Z)\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1}].

However, the Veronese subring of
\mathcal{R}(Z)
associated to the subgroup
\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)\subset\mathcal{R}(Z)
, which is generated by
(1,0,0)
and
(0,1,1)
, takes the form (
2
), which in this case is

\bigoplus_{D\in\iota^{*}\!\Pic X}\mathcal{R}(Z)_{D}\cong k[x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]/(g_{0}).

Proof.

This first isomorphism in (

Note that the spaces appearing in the isomorphism (

We move on to Step (2) of our plan. Since

\begin{array}[]{rcl}R^{a-1}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\displaystyle\bigoplus_{b\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{x_{0}x_{1}}R[x_{0}^{-1},x_{1}^{-1}]_{(-a,b)}\\

Using Lemma

Proposition 2.2
.

For
D=(-a,b)\in\Pic
, the kernel

-a\leq d-2
.

(i)

For
-1\leq-a\leq d-2

(ii)

For

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}A_{a}&\colon&R^{a+d-1}&\longrightarrow&R^{a-1}\\
&&(f_{k})_{k=1}^{a+d-1}&\longmapsto&(g_{0}f_{k}+g_{1}f_{k+1}+\ldots+g_{d}f_{k+d})_{k=1}^{a-1}\end{array}

defined by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}g_{0}&g_{1}&g_{2}&\cdots&g_{d}&0&0&\cdots\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&g_{0}&g_{1}&\cdots&g_{d-1}&g_{d}&0&\cdots\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\ &\vdots&\ &\ddots&\ &\vdots&\ &\ddots&\vdots&\ &\ &\ddots&\ &\vdots\\

0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots\cdots&0&0&g_{0}&\cdots&g_{d-1}&g_{d}\end{pmatrix}.

The first non-trivial example of a map

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}A_{2}&\colon&R^{d+1}&\longrightarrow&R\\
&&(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d+1})&\mapsto&g_{0}f_{1}+g_{1}f_{2}+\ldots+g_{d}f_{d+1}\end{array}

in the complex (

2.5.
Lifting cohomology elements to sections in the Cox ring

In this subsection, we describe how to perform Step (3) from Section
1.2
in our context. For
D=(-a,b)\in\Pic
, recall the standard exact sequence (
4

0\longrightarrow\iota^{*}\mathcal{R}(X)_{(-a,b)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-a,b)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}N_{(-a,b)}\longrightarrow 0.

We need to construct a lifting map
\epsilon\colon N_{(-a,b)}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-a,b)}

\delta
. By Proposition
2.2
,

can only be non-zero if

. Then

(-a,b)

be the sum of terms in which
x_{1}
has negative exponent. Let

be the sum of remaining terms of

(12)

u=u^{(0)}+u^{(1)}+u^{(2)}

with

\begin{array}[]{c}u^{(i)}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(-a,b)(W_{ij})\mbox{ for all }j\mbox{ and }i=0,1,\\
u^{(2)}\in S_{(-a,b)}.\end{array}

Then the
0
-cochain
(v_{ij})
on

given by

(13)

v_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{(0)}+\frac{1}{2}u^{(2)}&\mbox{if }i=0,\\

translate into

Proposition 2.3

The map

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\epsilon&\colon&N_{(-a,b)}&\to&\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-a,b)}\\
&&f&\mapsto&v\end{array}

.

Note that if

, then

Remark 2.4
.

We note that the choice of

is compatible with the
R
-module structures on

and
\bigoplus_{b\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-a,b)}
for each

. We can thus view

-module map from

2.6.
Finding some sections in the Cox ring

In this subsection, we complete Step (3) in our procedure for all degrees
(-a,b)
with
-1\leq-a\leq d-2
, under the only assumption that
Z
is nonsingular. We find
d
new sections in the Cox ring of

in degree

Proposition 2.5
.

(i)

There exists sections

, linearly independent over

(14)

(ii)

The subalgebra
\mathcal{R}(Z)_{z_{k}}
of
\mathcal{R}(Z)

\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-a,b)}
for all

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
(i) we have

(15)

\bigoplus_{b\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{(-1,b)}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{d}R[e]\cdot\frac{1}{x_{0}^{d-k+1}x_{1}^{k}}\cong R[e]^{d}.

For
1\leq k\leq d
, let
f_{k}=\frac{1}{x_{0}^{d-k+1}x_{1}^{k}}
be the

basis vector in the decomposition (
15
). The short exact sequence (
4
) implies
\delta
and
\epsilon
are inverse isomorphisms for
a=-1
. Thus setting
z_{k}=\epsilon f_{k}
gives us
d

R
-linearly independent sections

. Following the definition of
\epsilon
, each

is given by the following

Since for any choice of

(i).

For part (ii), note that

\mathcal{R}(X)
. To prove the statement it thus suffices to prove that each section in

\mathcal{R}(Z)_{z_{k}}
. Part (i) covers the

gives that

for

\bigoplus_{b\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{(-a,b)}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{a+d-1}R[e]\cdot\frac{1}{x_{0}^{a+d-k}x_{1}^{k}}\cong R[e]^{a+d-1}.

for

Corollary 2.6

Let

Then the algebra homomorphism

By Proposition

Firstly, let

Proposition

2.7.
Multiplying sections

Proposition
2.5
and Corollary

give a full picture of

2.8.
The main theorem

Theorem 2.9

satisfying the equations

(16)

x_{1}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}w_{1}+g_{i_{0}}=x_{1}^{i_{2}-i_{1}}w_{2}+g_{i_{1}}-x_{0}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}w_{1}=\dots=g_{i_{l}}-x_{0}^{i_{l}-i_{l-1}}w_{l}=0.

Furthermore, these sections, along with the sections
x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},\dots,y_{n}
restricted from
X
, generate the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(Z)
. The ideal of relations among these generators is generated by the equations (
16
). More precisely, let
U=\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n},W_{1},\dots,W_{l}]
be the free
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded polynomial ring with
\deg(X_{i})=(1,0),\deg(Y_{j})=(0,1)
and
\deg(W_{k})=(i_{k-1}-i_{k},e)
for the relevant
i,j,k
, and
J\lhd U
the homogeneous ideal

J=\langle X_{1}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}W_{1}+g_{i_{0}}(Y_{j}),X_{1}^{i_{2}-i_{1}}W_{2}+g_{i_{1}}(Y_{j})-X_{0}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}W_{1},\dots,g_{i_{l}}(Y_{j})-X_{0}^{i_{l}-i_{l-1}}W_{l}\rangle.

Then there is a surjective homomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded algebras

\psi:U\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(Z)
, inducing an isomorphism
\mathcal{R}(Z)\cong U/J
. In particular, the hypersurface
Z
is a Mori Dream Space.

Proof.

The sections
w_{k}
are immediately found from the defining equation of
Z
. Indeed, for each
1\leq k\leq l
,

(17)

w_{k}=\frac{g_{i_{k}}x_{0}^{d-i_{k}}+\dots+g_{i_{l}}x_{1}^{d-i_{k}}}{x_{0}^{d-i_{k-1}}}=-\frac{g_{i_{0}}x_{0}^{i_{k-1}}+\dots+g_{i_{k-1}}x_{1}^{i_{k-1}}}{x_{1}^{i_{k}}}\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(i_{k-1}-i_{k},e)}

is defined globally on
Z
, and these sections satisfy the equations (
16
) in Theorem
2.9
.
In terms of our identification in Proposition
2.2
, the section
w_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq l
is associated to the element
(f_{m})_{m=1}^{i_{k}-i_{k-1}+d-1}\in\ker A_{i_{k}-i_{k-1}}
with
f_{m}=1
if
m=i_{k}
and all other
f_{m}
equal zero. We thus have a well-defined map
\psi\colon U\to\mathcal{R}(Z)
mapping
X_{i},Y_{j},W_{k}
respectively to the sections
x_{i},y_{z},w_{k}\in\mathcal{R}(Z)
, respectively.

The sections
z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\in\mathcal{R}(Z)
from Proposition
2.5
(i) are in the image of
\psi
; namely, for each
1\leq k\leq l
and each
1\leq m\leq i_{k}-i_{k-1}
, it is easy to check that

(18)

z_{i_{k-1}+m}=\psi(Z_{i_{k-1}+m})\ \mbox{ with }\ Z_{i_{k-1}+m}=X_{0}^{m-1}X_{1}^{i_{k}-i_{k-1}-m}W_{k}.

So
\im\psi
contains
\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-a,b)}
for all
-a\leq-1
by Proposition
2.5
(ii). Furthermore, the equations (
14
) become exactly the equations (
16
) and any degree
(a,b)
monomial of
U
with
a\geq-1
can be rewritten as a monomial in
X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n},

Z_{1},\dots,Z_{d}
. Thus, by Corollary
2.6
, we only need to consider
\mathcal{R}(Z)
in degrees
(-a,b)
with
-a\leq-2
. Surjectivity of
\psi
in degrees
(-a,b)
with
-a\leq-1
is given by Theorem
4.10
. It remains to show that the induced surjective map
\mathcal{R}=U/J\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(Z)
is in fact an isomorphism.

Since
\psi
is surjective,
Z
is a Mori Dream Space, and thus
\dim\mathcal{R}(Z)=\dim Z+\rho(Z)=n+2
by
[
5
, Proposition 2.9]
. To conclude the proof of the theorem, it therefore suffices to show that the algebra
\mathcal{R}=U/J
is integral of dimension
n+2
.

Localising
\mathcal{R}
at
X_{0}
, we have the equations

W_{l}=\frac{g_{i_{l}}(Y_{j})}{X_{0}^{i_{l}-i_{l-1}}},\dots,W_{1}=\frac{X_{1}^{i_{2}-i_{1}}W_{2}+g_{i_{1}}(Y_{j})}{X_{0}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}}

in
\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}}
, implying the surjectivity of

\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n}]_{X_{0}}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}}.

Substituting the above equations into
X_{0}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}W_{1}+g_{i_{0}}(Y_{j})=0
in
\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}}
we obtain
\frac{r(X_{i},Y_{j})}{X_{0}^{d}}=0
and thus

(19)

\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}}=\left(\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n}]/r(X_{i},Y_{j})\right)_{X_{0}}.

Similarly one obtains

(20)

\mathcal{R}_{X_{1}}=\left(\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n}]/r(X_{i},Y_{j})\right)_{X_{1}}.

On the other hand, we see

(21)

\mathcal{R}/\langle X_{0},X_{1}\rangle=\mathbb{C}[Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n},W_{1},\dots,W_{l}]/\langle g_{i_{0}}(Y_{j}),\dots,g_{i_{l}}(Y_{j})\rangle.

Let
T_{1}=\Spec(\mathcal{R}/\langle X_{0},X_{1}\rangle)
and
T_{2}=\Spec(\mathcal{R}/\langle Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n}\rangle)
. Since
r(X_{i},Y_{j})
is irreducible, the rings
\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}},\mathcal{R}_{X_{1}}
are integral domains of dimension
n+2
by (
19
), (
20
) respectively. Additionally, since
\{g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}\}
form a regular sequence, we have
\dim T_{1}=n
by (
21
). One sees that
\dim(T_{2}\cap\Spec\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}})=2
via

T_{2}\cap\Spec\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}}=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n}]_{X_{0}}/\langle r(X_{i},Y_{j}),Y_{0},\dots,Y_{j}\rangle)=\Spec\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1}]_{X_{0}}.

In the same way,
\dim(T_{2}\cap\Spec\mathcal{R}_{X_{1}})=2
. Since

T_{2}=(T_{1}\cap T_{2})\cup(\Spec\mathcal{R}_{X_{0}}\cap T_{2})\cup(\Spec\mathcal{R}_{X_{1}}\cap T_{2}),

we conclude that
\dim T_{2}\leq\max\{\dim T_{1},2\}=n
. We may now consider the morphism

\Spec\mathcal{R}\backslash(T_{1}\cup T_{2})\rightarrow Z,

which is a
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
-bundle. This implies that
\Spec\mathcal{R}\backslash(T_{1}\cup T_{2})
is smooth and irreducible of dimension
n+2
. But
J\lhd U
is generated by
l+1
elements, and thus each irreducible component of
\Spec\mathcal{R}
has dimension at least
n+2
.
Thus
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
is not an irreducible component of
\Spec\mathcal{R}
, as
\dim T_{1}\cup T_{2}\leq n
.
Hence
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
is in the closure of
\Spec\mathcal{R}\backslash(T_{1}\cup T_{2})
, and
we have that
\Spec\mathcal{R}
is irreducible of dimension
n+2
.
It follows that
J
is a complete intersection ideal and hence
\Spec\mathcal{R}
is Cohen-Macaulay.
On the other hand,
\Spec\mathcal{R}
is smooth in codimension one, indeed it is smooth away from
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
.
Hence
\Spec\mathcal{R}
is normal by Serre criterion.
In particular,
\Spec\mathcal{R}
is integral, and we are done.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Fix
n\geq 3
and
d,e\geq 1
. Let
Z\subset X
is non-singular, defined by a degree
(d,e)
equation
r\in\mathcal{R}(X)
, admitting index sequence
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
. Let
U=\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n},W_{1},\dots,W_{l}]
be the free
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded polynomial ring with
\deg(X_{i})=(1,0),\deg(Y_{j})=(0,1)
and
\deg(W_{k})=(i_{k-1}-i_{k},e)
for the relevant
i,j,k
, and
J\lhd U
the homogeneous ideal

J=\langle X_{1}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}W_{1}+g_{i_{0}}(Y_{j}),X_{1}^{i_{2}-i_{1}}W_{2}+g_{i_{1}}(Y_{j})-X_{0}^{i_{1}-i_{0}}W_{1},\dots,g_{i_{l}}(Y_{j})-X_{0}^{i_{l}-i_{l-1}}W_{l}\rangle.

If there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{R}(Z)\cong\mathcal{R}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded algebras, where
\mathcal{R}=U/J
, then the sequence
g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}
is regular in
\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
. In other words,
Z
is regular with index sequence
\{i_{0},\dots,i_{l}\}
.

Proof.

By degree considerations, the isomorphism
\mathcal{R}\cong\mathcal{R}(Z)
sends the
\mathbb{C}
-span of
X_{0},X_{1}
to the
\mathbb{C}
-span of
x_{0},x_{1}
. By
[
5
, Proposition 2.7]
, we conclude
X_{0},X_{1}
is a regular sequence in
\mathcal{R}
, and thus

\dim\mathcal{R}/\langle X_{0},X_{1}\rangle=\dim\mathcal{R}-2=\dim\mathcal{R}(Z)-2=n,

where again the final equality uses
Z
being a Mori Dream Space. Using (
21
) as found in the proof of the previous Theorem, we conclude that
\dim\mathbb{C}[Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n}]/\langle g_{i_{0}}(Y_{j}),\dots,g_{i_{l}}(Y_{j})\rangle=n-l
, implying that
g_{i_{0}},\dots,g_{i_{l}}
is regular in
\mathbb{C}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
, as claimed.
∎

3.
Examples

We look at some examples of our results of geometric interest.

Example 3.1
.

Let
n=3
,
d=2
and choose three general polynomials
g_{0},g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathbb{C}[y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]
of degree
e=4
, forming a regular sequence. Consider the family of varieties
q\colon{\mathcal{Z}}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
defined by

{\mathcal{Z}}=\left\{g_{0}x_{0}^{2}+tg_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+g_{2}x_{1}^{2}=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}.

For every
t\in\mathbb{A}^{1}
, the hypersurface fibre
Z_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
of the family
q\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
is a smooth Calabi–Yau threefold. Let
Z_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{t}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\bar{Z}}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{t}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{P}^{3}
be the Stein factorization of the second projection
p_{2}
. The map
g_{t}\colon{\bar{Z}}_{t}\to\mathbb{P}^{3}
is a double cover in all cases, ramified over the divisor

D_{t}=\{t^{2}g_{1}^{2}-4g_{0}g_{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}

that is singular along the degeneracy locus

Y_{t}=\{g_{0}=tg_{1}=g_{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}.

For
t\neq 0
,
Y_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
is a set of
64
points, and the map
f_{t}\colon Z_{t}\to{\bar{Z}}_{t}
is a small contraction, contracting a set of
64
rational curves to nodes. For
t=0
on the other hand, the map
Z_{0}\to{\bar{Z}}_{0}
is a divisorial contraction, contracting a divisor
E\subset Z_{0}
to a genus-
33
curve
C\subset{\bar{Z}}_{0}
isomorphic
to
Y_{0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
. As already observed by
[
3
]
, see in particular
[
4
, 3.3.2-3.3.3]
, for numbers of global sections, the Cox ring detects this change in birational behaviour.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
t\neq 0
, the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(Z_{t})
of the Calabi–Yau hypersurface
Z_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
can be presented as

\mathcal{R}(Z_{t})\cong k[x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},z_{1},z_{2}]/\langle x_{1}z_{1}+g_{0},x_{1}z_{2}+g_{1}-x_{0}z_{1},g_{2}-x_{0}z_{2}\rangle,

with variables of bidegrees
(1,0),(0,1)
and
(-1,4)
respectively.
For
t=0
, we have

\mathcal{R}(Z_{0})\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},w]/\langle x_{1}^{2}w+g_{0},g_{2}-x_{0}^{2}w\rangle,

with variables of bidegrees
(1,0),(0,1)
and
(-2,4)
respectively.
In particular, every member of the family is a Mori dream space, with a complete intersection Cox ring, but the effective cone and Cox ring jump discontinuously in the family.

Proof.

By the assumptions, for
t\neq 0
, the hypesurface
Z_{t}
is regular with full index sequence
\{0,1,2\}
. On the other hand, the hypersurface
Z_{0}
is regular, admitting index sequence
\{0,2\}
. Hence the statements follow from Theorem
0.2
.
∎

Antonio Laface has informed us that the Cox rings in these examples can also be computed using the method of
[
7
]
.

We introduce one further, singular, member of this deformation family of varieties with interesting behaviour.

Example 3.3
.

Choose general linear, respectively cubic polynomials
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in R_{1}
and

b_{0},b_{1},b_{2}\in R_{3}
. Consider the determinantal hypersurface

Z=\left\{\begin{vmatrix}a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}\\
b_{0}&b_{1}&b_{2}\\
x_{0}^{2}&x_{0}x_{1}&x_{1}^{2}\end{vmatrix}=0\right\}=\left\{g_{0}x_{0}^{2}+g_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+g_{2}x_{1}^{2}=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3},

with
g_{0}=a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1},g_{1}=a_{2}b_{0}-a_{0}b_{2},g_{2}=a_{0}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{0}
.
Then the degeneracy locus is the curve

Y=\left\{{\mathrm{rk}}\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}\\
b_{0}&b_{1}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\leq 1\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3},

a smooth space curve of genus
21
and degree
13
. Its ideal
I_{Y}\lhd R
has a resolution (
9
) in Hilbert–Burch form

(22)

0\longrightarrow R^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\longrightarrow}}R\longrightarrow R/I_{Y}\longrightarrow 0.

Here,
B=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}\\
b_{0}&b_{1}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{t}
,
and
A=\bigwedge^{2}B=(g_{0},g_{1},g_{2})^{t}
. As a determinantal hypersurface,
Z
is singular along the locus given by the
2\times 2
minors of its defining matrix, which gives the locus

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Sing}Z=\left\{g_{0}=g_{1}=g_{2}=a_{1}^{2}-a_{0}a_{2}=x_{0}a_{1}-x_{1}a_{0}=x_{0}a_{2}-x_{1}a_{1}=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3},

a set of
26
isolated ordinary double points all lying on the ruled surface
E\subset Z
. Blowing up this ruled surface, a Weil divisor through each of the ODP’s, gives a small resolution
\widetilde{Z}\rightarrow Z
, a smooth Calabi–Yau model.

Since
Z\subset X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
has isolated nodal singularities, the restriction map
\Pic(X)\to\Pic(Z)
is still an isomorphism.
However,
Z
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, so the ring
\mathcal{R}(Z)
as defined above only contains sections of Cartier divisors.

By Proposition
2.2
(ii), the columns of
B
give us elements
f_{1}\in N_{(-2,5)}
and
f_{2}\in N_{(-2,7)}
that together generate the
R
-module
\bigoplus_{b\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{(-2,b)}
. Let
u=\epsilon f_{1}\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-2,5)}
and
w=\epsilon f_{2}\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-2,7)}
be the lifts of these module generators to elements of the Cox ring as in Proposition
2.3
. By Proposition
2.7
, these satisfy equations

\displaystyle x_{0}u=a_{1}z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2},\quad x_{1}u=a_{2}z_{1}+a_{3}z_{2},

\displaystyle x_{0}w=b_{1}z_{1}+b_{2}z_{2},\quad x_{1}w=b_{2}z_{1}+b_{3}z_{2}.

Furthermore, by comparing
\delta(u),\delta(w),\delta(z_{1}^{2}),\delta(z_{1}z_{2}),\delta(z_{2}^{2})
we have

z_{1}^{2}+b_{2}u-a_{2}w=z_{1}z_{2}-b_{1}u+a_{1}w=z_{2}^{2}+b_{0}u-a_{0}w=0.

Consider the free
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded
k
-algebra

T=\mathbb{C}[X_{0},X_{1},Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n},Z_{1},Z_{2},U,W]

with generators in degrees
(1,0),(0,1),(-1,4),(-2,5)
and
(-2,7)
respectively. Let
I\lhd T
be the ideal

\displaystyle I=\langle X_{1}Z_{1}+g_{0}(Y_{i}),\quad X_{1}Z_{2}+g_{1}(Y_{i})-X_{0}Z_{1},\quad g_{2}(Y_{i})-X_{0}Z_{2},

\displaystyle X_{0}U-a_{1}(Y_{i})Z_{1}-a_{2}(Y_{i})Z_{2},\quad X_{1}U-a_{2}(Y_{i})Z_{1}-a_{3}(Y_{i})Z_{2},

\displaystyle X_{0}W-b_{1}(Y_{i})Z_{1}+b_{2}(Y_{i})Z_{2},\quad X_{1}W-b_{2}(Y_{i})Z_{1}-b_{3}(Y_{i})Z_{2},

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{2}+b_{2}(Y_{i})U-a_{2}(Y_{i})W,\quad Z_{1}Z_{2}-b_{1}(Y_{i})U+a_{1}(Y_{i})W,\quad Z_{2}^{2}+b_{0}(Y_{i})U-a_{0}(Y_{i})W\rangle.

Then our observations so far prove the existence of an algebra homomorphism

\phi:T/I\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(Z),

to the (Cartier) Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(Z)
of
Z
that induces isomorphisms
(T/I)_{(a,b)}\cong\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(a,b)}
whenever
a\geq-2
.

We sketch an argument provided to us by Antonio Laface that proves that for a general determinantal hypersurface
Z
, the map
\phi

is an isomorphism. By
[
7
, Thm.1]
, the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(Z)
is the intersection of certain localizations of quotients
of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3})
. This intersection can be computed using the ideas of
[
7
, Cor.2.4]
, which gives the result that
\phi
is surjective under certain dimension and saturation conditions. The latter can be checked for general
a_{i},b_{j}
using computer algebra.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the hypersurface

Z=\{y_{0}x_{0}^{4}+(-\lambda_{0}y_{0}-\lambda_{1}y_{1}-\lambda_{2}y_{2}-\lambda_{3}y_{3})x_{0}^{3}x_{1}+y_{1}x_{0}^{2}x_{1}^{2}+y_{2}x_{0}x_{1}^{3}+y_{3}x_{1}^{4}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}

of bidegree
(d,e)=(4,1)
with non-zero scalars
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, noting that a general degree
(4,1)
hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
has this form after a coordinate transformation.
As the five linear forms are linearly dependent, by Theorem
0.2
(ii), the Cox ring is not of the form (
2
), contrary to the statement
[
8
, Thm.1.1(iv)]
. We investigate the Cox ring of this particular family in this example.

The kernel of
A_{2}
as defined in Proposition
2.2
contains
(\lambda_{0},1,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})
, defining a section
w\in\mathcal{R}(Z)_{(-2,1)}
,
which satisfies the equations

(23)

x_{0}w=\lambda_{0}z_{1}+z_{2}+\lambda_{1}z_{3}+\lambda_{2}z_{4},\quad x_{1}w=z_{1}+\lambda_{1}z_{2}+\lambda_{2}z_{3}+\lambda_{3}z_{4}.

These equations
imply that for general choices of
\lambda_{j}
,
the subalgebra
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{R}(Z)
generated by
x_{0},x_{1},w,z_{3},z_{4}
contains
z_{1},z_{2}
.
Note also that the relations

x_{1}z_{1}+y_{0}=0,\quad x_{1}z_{3}+y_{1}-x_{0}z_{2}=0,\quad x_{1}z_{4}+y_{2}-x_{0}z_{3}=0,\quad y_{3}-x_{0}z_{4}=0

hold in
\mathcal{R}(Z)
. Hence the subalgebra
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
contains
y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}
as well.
In fact, we can show that
\mathcal{R}(Z)=\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, which is a polynomial ring as follows.

Proposition 3.5
.

For general constants
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(Z)
of the hypersurface
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
is isomorphic to the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},w,z_{3},z_{4}]
.

Proof.

Let
r\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(4,1))=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(4))\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1))
be the section which defines
Z
.
Then
r
induces an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-4)\xrightarrow{r}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to\mathcal{E}\to 0

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}

and we have
Z=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
We note that
\mathcal{E}
is locally free of rank
3

since
r
is general.
Let
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(c)
with
a+b+c=4
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
is a quotient of a trivial bundle, we have
a,b,c\geq 0
.
If
a=0
, then
r
must be contained in
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(4))\otimes V^{\prime}
for

V^{\prime}=\ker H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1))\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a))=\mathbb{C}
,
which contradicts the generality of
r
.
Hence
a,b,c\geq 1
and
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)
.

Since the embedding
Z=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is induced by

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}
,
the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{E})
is identified with
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(0,1)
,
where
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(a,b)\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a,b)|_{Z}
.
Since
\mathcal{E}(-1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)

the morphism

is the blow-up along a line

.
We note that

is different from the second factor of

.

Let

be divisors on
Z
.
Since

is a non-zero section of

,

E
is the exceptional divisor of the blow-up
\mu
.

Since

are linearly independent over

so are over

Hence

By (

also form a basis of

as a toric variety by this basis.
Let

be the torus invariant planes for

Since

Then torus invariant prime divisors of

Since the Cox ring of a smooth projective toric variety is a polynomial ring whose variables correspond to torus invariants prime divisors,
we conclude that

∎

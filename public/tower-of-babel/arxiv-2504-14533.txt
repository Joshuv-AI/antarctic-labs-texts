An effective finite-range Gogny-type interaction for the quantum molecular dynamics like model
Meiqi Sun, Dandan Niu, Junping Yang, Ying Cui, Zhuxia Li, Qiang Zhao, Kai Zhao, Yingxun Zhang

Abstract
In this work, we propose an effective finite-range Gogny-type interaction that can be directly used in the quantum molecular dynamics (QMD) like model. Two methods for determining the parameters of the effective interaction are discussed. The first method establishes an approach to connect the conventional Gogny interaction in nuclear structure to that in heavy-ion collisions, the second method allows for the description of the symmetry energy varying from the supersoft to stiff, as well as the momentum-dependent symmetry potential, exhibiting behaviors ranging from monotonic to non-monotonic variations. This effective interaction opens up opportunities for a deeper understanding of finite-range interactions and non-monotonic momentum-dependent symmetry potentials in future studies.



The nature of the effective nucleon-nucleon interaction is central to understanding the properties of complex nuclei and the mechanism of heavy ion reactions or collisions. Historically, the effective nucleon-nucleon interaction was built in with two primary approaches: the zero-range effective interaction
Skyrme 1956
and the finite-range effective interaction
Dechargé and Gogny 1980
.
Both types of effective interaction were widely used in theoretical models to study the properties of nuclear structure and its related issues. For example, the zero-range Skyrme effective nucleon-nucleon interaction, parametrized by its dependence on the relative distance and its derivatives, has been extensively used in studies of nuclear structure
Vautherin and Brink 1972
;
Wang et al. 2020
, mass
Köhler 1976
;
Goriely et al. 2002
;
Pearson and Goriely 2006
;
Aichelin et al. 2020
, energy level
Sagawa et al. 1985
;
Hilaire and Goriely 2006
;
Valuev et al. 2020
, fission
Dutta and Kohno 1980
;
Schunck et al. 2014
;
Sharma et al. 2017
and properties of neutron stars
Chabanat et al. 1997
;
Rikovska Stone et al. 2003
;
Erler et al. 2013
;
Nandi and Schramm 2016
;
Mehta et al. 2022
, owing to its ease of implementation in practical calculations. The finite-range interaction is generally considered more physically realistic and has also been employed in the studies of nuclear fission
Berger et al. 1991
and deformation
Robledo and Bertsch 2011
, multipolar collective degrees of freedom
Gaffney et al. 2013
;
Peru and Martini 2014
, and in the studies of neutron stars
Sellahewa and Rios 2014
;
Gonzalez-Boquera et al. 2017
;
Gonzalez-Boquera et al. 2018
;
Mondal et al. 2020
;
Viñas et al. 2021
.

For heavy ion collisions, the finite-range interaction becomes more important and indispensable. For example, the exchange term on the finite-range interaction generates a momentum-dependent potential, which plays a crucial role in describing the collective flow with a reasonable nuclear matter incompressibility parameter
Aichelin et al. 1987
. In the QMD-like models and BUU (Boltzmann-Uhling-Uhlenbeck)-like models, one usually adopted either the zero-range Skyrme interaction
Zhang et al. 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2020
or a simple zero-range interaction plus a phenomenological momentum dependent interaction
Aichelin and Ko 1985
;
Li et al. 2011
;
Hartnack et al. 1989
;
Cozma et al. 2013
;
Wang and Li 2020
to study the nuclear equation of state and the properties of nuclear medium. Up to now, there are some efforts to use the finite-range interaction in the transport models. For instance, the isospin Boltzmann-Uhling-Uhlenbeck (IBUU) transport model
Das et al. 2003
;
Chen et al. 2005
;
Xu 2011
;
Chen et al. 2012
;
Xue et al. 2016
, the Antisymmetrized Molecular Dynamics (AMD) model
Ono et al. 2003
, the constrainted molecular dynamics (CoMD) model
Papa 2024
and dcQMD
Cozma and Tsang 2021
have employed the Gogny-like interactions. But one should note that these interactions are specific for heavy ion collisions, and have not been directly related to the conventional Gogny interaction yet.

In addition, the constraints on the isospin asymmetric nuclear equation of state (EOS) with the transport models depend on the exact form of the momentum-dependent symmetry potential
Li et al. 2004
;
Rizzo et al. 2005
;
Giordano et al. 2010
;
Zhang et al. 2014
. Especially, the recent reanalysis of the neutron to proton yield ratio by Yang
et al.
Yang et al. 2024
suggests that the momentum-dependent symmetry potential may first decrease and then increase, which cannot be explained by using the published 246 sets of the zero-range Skyrme interaction
Dutra et al. 2012
. Luckily, the studies in Refs.
Chen et al. 2012
;
Sellahewa and Rios 2014
have found that the Gogny D1S
Berger et al. 1991
and D250
Blaizot et al. 1995
can predict that the momentum-dependent symmetry potential first decreases and then increases. Thus, one may expect to find or built a Gogny-type interaction, which can be used in the QMD model and may answer the above question.

The present work aims to derive a parametrized version of the Gogny-type interaction, that enables broad adjustability of the symmetry energy stiffness and can be directly related to the conventional Gogny interactio. The paper is organized as follows: first, we will briefly review the framework of the quantum molecular dynamics approach and then mention the proposed finite-range Gogny interaction. Then, we will discuss the corresponding equation of state, symmetry energy, single particle potential and symmetry potential, and the effective mass of neutron and proton. Finally, we will give the results on the potential energy within the framework of QMD-like model for practical utilization.

In the QMD approach
Aichelin 1991
;
Zhang et al. 2020
, each nucleon is represented by a Gaussian wave packet,

\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r}_{i})=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma_{r}^{2})^{3/4}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{i0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{r}^{2}}+{\rm i}(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{i0})\cdot\mathbf{p}_{i0}/\hbar},\vskip 1.42262pt

(1)

here,
\sigma_{r}
and
\mathbf{r}_{i0}
are the width and centroid of
wave packet, respectively. The subscript
i
of singe particle wave function
\phi_{i}
represents the state that is described by position
\mathbf{r}_{i0}
, momentum
\mathbf{p}_{i0}
, and isospin
\tau_{i}
. One should note that the effect of the spin on the Fermi momentum of particle
i
is considered in the QMD-like model, even the spin wave function do not appear in above formula. The system wave function
\Psi
is assumed as a direct product of
N
single particle wave functions, i.e., in the Hartree approximation,

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{r}_{N})=\phi_{1}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\phi_{2}(\mathbf{r}_{2})\cdots\phi_{N}(\mathbf{r}_{N}).

(2)

Correspondingly, the energy of system is calculated as,

E=\langle\Psi|\mathrm{\hat{H}}|\Psi\rangle,

(3)

or calculated with the Wigner density function as in Ref.
Aichelin 1991
. If we only consider the effective two-body interaction
v_{ij}
, the Hamiltonian can be written as,

\hat{\mathrm{H}}=\sum_{i}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{i}^{2}}{2m}+\sum_{i<j}\hat{v}_{ij}.

(4)

Here,
\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{i}
is the momentum of
i
th nucleon,
m
is the nucleon mass, and
\hat{v}_{ij}
is the effective interaction used in the QMD-like models.

Within the framework of the QMD-like models, we propose an effective finite-range Gogny-type interaction as follows,

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\bigg[\left(\tilde{A}_{l}+\tilde{B}_{l}\hat{P}_{\tau}\right)e^{-\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2})^{2}}{\mu_{l}^{2}}}

(5)

\displaystyle+\left(\tilde{C}_{l}+\tilde{D}_{l}\hat{P}_{\tau}\right)e^{-\frac{\mu_{l}^{2}(\mathbf{k}_{i}-\mathbf{k}_{j})^{2}}{4}}e^{-\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2})^{2}}{\mu_{l}^{2}}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\left(\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}\hat{P}_{\tau}\right)\rho^{\sigma}(\frac{\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{2}}{2})\delta(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}).

It consists of two finite-range two-body terms and a density-dependent zero-range two-body term. The density-dependent zero-range two-body term provides a phenomenological representation of many-body effects
Vautherin and Brink 1972
. The operators
\hat{P}_{\tau}
is the isospin exchange operators. The terms related to
\tilde{A}_{l}
and
\tilde{B}_{l}
come from the direct term, and the terms related to
\tilde{C}_{l}
and
\tilde{D}_{l}
are used to mimic the exchange term which results in the momentum-dependent potential. The terms
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
have similar meanings but for density-dependent zero-range interaction. The parameters
\mu_{1}=0.7
fm and
\mu_{2}=1.2
fm, which are the same as those in the Gogny interaction in Refs.
Dechargé and Gogny 1980
. For convenience, we named the proposed interaction used in the QMD-like models as QG interaction in the following discussions.

In this work, we take two ways to determine the 11 parameters in Eq.(
5
), i.e.,
\tilde{A}_{l=1,2}
,
\tilde{B}_{l=1,2}
,
\tilde{C}_{l=1,2}
,
\tilde{D}_{l=1,2}
,
\beta_{0}
,
\beta_{1}
and
\sigma
, by describing the EOS of nuclear matter reasonably.

The first way is to determine the parameters from the conventional Gogny interactions
Dechargé and Gogny 1980
. As known in Ref.
Dechargé and Gogny 1980
, the conventional Gogny interaction
\hat{v}_{ij}^{*}
taken as,

\displaystyle v_{ij}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Bigg(W_{k}+B_{k}\hat{P}_{\sigma}-H_{k}\hat{P}_{\tau}-M_{k}\hat{P}_{\sigma}\hat{P}_{\tau}\Bigg)e^{-(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2})^{2}/\mu_{k}^{2}}

(6)

\displaystyle+t_{0}(1+x_{0}\hat{P}_{\sigma})\rho^{\sigma}(\frac{\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{2}}{2})\delta(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}).

The
W_{k}
,
B_{k}
,
H_{k}
, and
M_{k}
are the parameters in the conventional Gogny interactions. The idea for the determination of parameters is that the potential energy obtained within the Hartree-Fock approximation is equal to the potential energy obtained within the Hartree approximation in QMD approach, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}}\langle ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}|\hat{v}_{ij}^{*}(1-\hat{P}_{i\leftrightarrow j})|ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}\rangle

(7)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{ij\tau\tau^{\prime}}\langle ij\tau\tau^{\prime}|\hat{v}_{ij}|ij\tau\tau^{\prime}\rangle.

The
\hat{P}_{i\leftrightarrow j}
is the exchange operator, and is equal to the product of Majorana exchange
\hat{P}_{M}
, spin exchange
\hat{P}_{\sigma}
, and isospin exchange operator
\hat{P}_{\tau}
, i.e.,
\hat{P}_{M}\hat{P}_{\sigma}\hat{P}_{\tau}
. The prefactor
4
in the right hand of Eq.(
7
) is from the summation of spin of particle
i
and
j
, since there is no explicit spin degree in the QMD-like model.

Based on Eq.(
7
), the following relationship can be obtained,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{l}=W_{k}+\frac{1}{2}B_{k},

(8)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{l}=-(H_{k}+\frac{1}{2}M_{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{l}=\frac{1}{2}H_{k}+M_{k},

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{l}=-(\frac{1}{2}W_{k}+B_{k}),

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=t_{0}(1+\frac{1}{2}x_{0}),

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=-t_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+x_{0}).

Since the parameters are determined by the conventional Gogny parameter sets D1 and D1S, we named them as QGD1 and QGD1S. One can expect that the EOS and symmetry energy will be identical for QG interaction sets (QGD1, QGD1S) and the conventional Gogny interaction sets (D1, D1S) as in Figure
1
(a) and (b).

The second way is to directly fit symmetric nuclear matter EOS with the effective QG interaction
\hat{v}_{ij}
. In this case, the parameters
\tilde{A}_{l=1,2}
,
\tilde{B}_{l=1,2}
,
\tilde{C}_{l=1,2}
,
\tilde{D}_{l=1,2}
,
\beta_{0}
,
\beta_{1}
and
\sigma
are not solely determined by the known symmetric EOS, and thus the different stiffness of symmetry energy can be obtained. In this work, we fit the symmeric EOS obtained with Gogny D1 set within 3 times the normal density in the case of the reduced
\chi^{2}<1
.

Now, let’s check the nuclear equation of state, single-particle potential, and nuclear matter parameters in cold nuclear matter for QG interaction. To describe the uniform nuclear matter within the framework of QMD wave function, we need to set
\sigma_{r}\to\infty
and thus the Gaussian wave packet for each nucleon in Eq.(
1
) tend to the plane wave,

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}.

(9)

Consequently, the expression of isospin asymmetric nuclear EOS for cold nuclear matter is,

\displaystyle\frac{E}{A}(\rho,\delta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\sigma\tau}\langle i\sigma\tau|\frac{\hat{p}_{i}^{2}}{2m}|i\sigma\tau\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}}\langle ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}|\hat{v}_{ij}|ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{3\hbar^{2}}{20m}\left(\frac{3\pi^{2}}{2}\rho\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}[(1+\delta)^{5/3}+(1-\delta)^{5/3}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{(2\tilde{A}_{l}+\tilde{B}_{l})}{4}(\sqrt{\pi}\mu_{l})^{3}\rho+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{B}_{l}}{4}(\sqrt{\pi}\mu_{l})^{3}\delta^{2}\rho

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{2(\tilde{C}_{l}+\tilde{D}_{l})}{3\mu_{l}^{3}\pi^{\frac{5}{2}}\rho}\times\left[{e}(\mu_{l}k_{fn})+{e}(\mu_{l}k_{fp})\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{2\tilde{C}_{l}}{3\mu_{l}^{3}\pi^{\frac{5}{2}}\rho}\times{\bar{e}}(\mu_{l}k_{fn},\mu_{l}k_{fp})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(2\beta_{0}+\beta_{1})\rho^{\sigma+1}+\frac{1}{4}\beta_{1}\rho^{\sigma+1}\delta^{2}.

Here,
\rho=\rho_{n}+\rho_{p}
with the neutron density
\rho_{n}
and
\rho_{p}
. The
\delta=(\rho_{n}-\rho_{p})/(\rho_{n}+\rho_{p})
is the isospin asymmetry.
One should keep in mind that the system wave function is the direct product of single-particle wave function, and thus the formula of
E/A
in Eq.(
10
) is different than the results in Ref.
Sellahewa and Rios 2014
;
Chen et al. 2012
. The function
{e}(\eta)
and
{\bar{e}}(\eta_{n},\eta_{p})
have the following expressions,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle e}(\eta)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\eta^{3}\mathrm{erf}(\eta)+(\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}-1)e^{-\eta^{2}}-\frac{3\eta^{2}}{2}+1

(11)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bar{e}}(\eta_{n},\eta_{p})=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\bigg[(\eta_{n}^{3}+\eta_{p}^{3})\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{\eta_{n}+\eta_{p}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle-(\eta_{n}^{3}-\eta_{p}^{3})\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{\eta_{n}-\eta_{p}}{2}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle+(\eta_{p}^{2}-\eta_{n}\eta_{p}+\eta_{n}^{2}-2)\exp\bigg[-\frac{(\eta_{n}+\eta_{p})^{2}}{4}\bigg]

\displaystyle-(\eta_{p}^{2}+\eta_{n}\eta_{p}+\eta_{n}^{2}-2)\exp\bigg[-\frac{(\eta_{n}-\eta_{p})^{2}}{4}\bigg].

Here, the
erf()
is the error function.

According to the Eq.(
10
) and the definition of the nuclear matter parameters, we can get the incompressibility
K_{0}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=9\rho^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}(E_{0}/A)}{\partial\rho^{2}}|_{\rho_{0}}

(12)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\hbar^{2}}{5m}\left(\frac{3\pi^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\rho_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{9\sigma(\sigma+1)}{4}(2\beta_{0}+\beta_{1})\rho_{0}^{\sigma+1}

\displaystyle+6\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{2\tilde{C}_{l}+\tilde{D}_{l}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Bigg[\frac{6}{(\mu_{l}k_{f})^{3}}-\frac{2}{\mu_{l}k_{f}}

\displaystyle-e^{-(\mu_{l}k_{f})^{2}}\left(\frac{6}{(\mu_{l}k_{f})^{3}}+\frac{4}{\mu_{l}k_{f}}+\mu_{l}k_{f}\right)\Bigg].

Here,
k_{f}=(\frac{3\pi^{2}}{2}\rho_{0})^{1/3}
.

Furthermore, we can obtain the density dependence of the symmetry energy by derivativing the Eq.(
10
) over isospin asymmetry
\delta
. The expression reads

\begin{split}S(\rho)&=\frac{1}{2!}\frac{\partial^{2}E(\rho,\delta)}{\partial\delta^{2}}\bigg|_{\delta=0}\\
&=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{6m}\left(\frac{3\pi^{2}}{2}\rho\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\tilde{B}_{l}(\sqrt{\pi}\mu_{l})^{3}\rho\\
&\quad+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{C}_{l}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\left[{S}_{1}(\mu_{l}k_{f})+{S}_{2}(\mu_{l}k_{f})\right]\\
&\quad+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{D}_{l}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}{S}_{1}(\mu_{l}k_{f})+\frac{1}{4}\beta_{1}\rho^{\sigma+1},\end{split}

(13)

with

\displaystyle{S}_{1}(\eta)=\frac{1}{\eta}-\left(\eta+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)e^{-\eta^{2}},

(14)

\displaystyle{S}_{2}(\eta)=\frac{1}{\eta}-\left(\eta+\frac{e^{-\eta^{2}}}{\eta}\right).

(15)

Correspondingly, the slope of symmetry energy at normal density can be written as,

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=3\rho_{0}\frac{\partial S(\rho)}{\partial\rho}\bigg|_{\rho_{0}}

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{3m}\left(\frac{3\pi^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\rho_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\tilde{B}_{l}(\sqrt{\pi}\mu_{l})^{3}\rho_{0}

\displaystyle+\frac{3(\sigma+1)}{4}\beta_{1}\rho_{0}^{\sigma+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{C}_{l}+\tilde{D}_{l}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}{L}_{1}(\mu_{l}k_{f})+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{C}_{l}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}{L}_{2}(\mu_{l}k_{f})

with,

\displaystyle{L}_{1}(\eta)=-\frac{1}{\eta}+e^{-\eta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+2\eta^{3}+\eta\right)

(17)

\displaystyle{L}_{2}(\eta)=-\frac{1}{\eta}-\eta+2\eta e^{-\eta^{2}}+\frac{e^{-\eta^{2}}}{\eta}.

(18)

\displaystyle U_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}\langle ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}|\hat{v}_{ij}|ij\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\tau\tau^{\prime}\rangle+U_{R},

Further, we investigate the momentum-dependent symmetry potential

\displaystyle U_{sym}

\displaystyle=\frac{U_{n}-U_{p}}{2\delta}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{D}_{l}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left[{g}(\mu_{l},k,k_{fn})-{g}(\mu_{l},k,k_{fp})\right].

As the nucleon effective mass is determined by the single-particle potential, it is important to examine the effective mass for the QG interaction. The expression of effective mass reads

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m_{\tau}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{m}{p}\frac{\partial U_{\tau}}{\partial p}\right)|_{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{m}{\hbar^{2}}\bigg\{\sum_{l=1}^{2}\frac{\tilde{C}_{l}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mu_{l}^{2}\Bigg[{h}(\mu_{l}k,\mu_{l}k_{fn})+{h}(\mu_{l}k,\mu_{l}k_{fp})\Bigg]

\displaystyle+(2-\eta\eta_{\tau})\exp\bigg[{-\frac{(\eta_{\tau}-\eta)^{2}}{4}}\bigg]\bigg\}.

To realize the QG interaction in the practical QMD-like models, we first need the expression of the potential energy with the QMD wave function. It is straightforward to get the potential energy in the QMD-like model, and it reads,

\displaystyle V=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}\langle\Phi|v_{ij}|\Phi\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l=1}^{2}\Bigg\{\left[\tilde{A}_{l}\sum_{i,j=1,j\neq i}^{A}+\tilde{B}_{l}\left(\sum_{i,j\in n,i\neq j}+\sum_{i,j\in p,i\neq j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\exp\bigg[{-\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j})^{2}}{4\sigma_{r}^{2}+\mu_{l}^{2}}}\bigg](\frac{\mu_{l}^{2}}{4\sigma_{r}^{2}+\mu_{l}^{2}})^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[\tilde{C}_{l}\sum_{i,j=1,j\neq i}^{A}+\tilde{D}_{l}\left(\sum_{i,j\in n,i\neq j}+\sum_{i,j\in p,i\neq j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\exp\bigg[{-\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j})^{2}}{4\sigma_{r}^{2}+\mu_{l}^{2}}}\bigg](\frac{\mu_{l}^{2}}{4\sigma_{r}^{2}+\mu_{l}^{2}})^{\frac{3}{2}}\exp\left[{-\frac{\mu_{l}^{2}(\mathbf{k_{i}}-\mathbf{k}_{j})^{2}}{4}}\right]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+2\beta_{0}\int\rho^{2}(\mathbf{r})\rho^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})d^{3}\mathbf{r}

\displaystyle+2\beta_{1}\left[\int\rho^{2}_{n}(\mathbf{r})\rho^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})d^{3}\mathbf{r}+\int\rho^{2}_{p}(\mathbf{r})\rho^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})d^{3}\mathbf{r}\right].

Here, the density
\rho
and
\rho_{n/p}
are,

\displaystyle\rho(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{A}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma_{r}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\bigg[{-\frac{(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{i})^{2}}{2\sigma_{r}^{2}}}\bigg],

(26)

\displaystyle\rho_{n/p}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{i\in n,p}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma_{r}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\bigg[{-\frac{(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{i})^{2}}{2\sigma_{r}^{2}}}\bigg].

Based on the expression of Eq.(
25
), one can easily derive the equation of motion for nucleons and expect that the stability of the initial nucleus in the QMD-like models could be enhanced
Yang et al. 2021
owing to the following reason. The width of the Gaussian functions in Eq.(
25

4\sigma_{r}^{2}+\mu_{l}^{2}
with the QG interaction, which is larger than the width of the Gaussian function
4\sigma_{r}^{2}
in the model with the Skyrme interaction
Zhang et al. 2020
. The inclusion of these finite-range interactions in the improved quantum molecular dynamics model (ImQMD) is currently in progress.

In summary, we propose an effective finite-range interaction for quantum molecular dynamics (QMD)-like models, i.e., QG interaction. This interaction can be directly derived from the conventional Gogny interaction, enabling a deeper understanding or better constraints on the Gogny interaction through heavy-ion collisions. In addition, by fitting the isospin symmetric EOS obtained with QG interaction to the isospin
-
symmetric EOS calculated by the Gogny D1 parameter, we also obtain 13 parameter sets that have different stiffnesses of symmetry energy, momentum-dependent symmetry potential, and effective mass splitting. Impressively, the symmetry potential obtained by utilizing the QG67 parameter set exhibits a non-monotonic momentum-dependent symmetry potential, which is similar to the results obtained with RHF in Ref.
Long et al. 2006
.

One way to understand this non-monotonic momentum-dependent symmetry potential is to measure isospin-sensitive observables at intermediate energies of heavy-ion collisions and compare them with transport model calculations. Incorporating this finite-range interaction into the improved quantum molecular dynamics model (ImQMD) and analyzing its effects on isospin-sensitive observables are in progress.

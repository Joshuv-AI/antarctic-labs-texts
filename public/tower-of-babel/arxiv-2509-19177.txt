On ergodic property of some Lévy-type processes in ℝ d {\mathds{R}^{d}}
Yana Mokanu

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the ergodicity in total variation of the process X t X_{t} related to some integro-differential operator with unbounded coefficients and describe the speed of convergence to the respective invariant measure. Some examples are provided.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
60G51, 37A50, 60G17.

Introduction

Consider an integro-differential operator

(0.1)

\begin{split}Lf(x)&=\ell(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\backslash\{0\}}\left(f(x+u)-f(x)-\nabla f(x)\cdot u\mathds{1}_{|u|<1}\right)\nu(x,du),\end{split}

defined on the set
C_{0}^{2}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
of twice continuously differentiable functions with compact support, where
\ell(\cdot):{\mathds{R}^{d}}\to{\mathds{R}^{d}}
, and the Lévy-type kernel
\nu(x,du)
satisfies

\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\backslash\{0\}}(1\wedge|u|^{2})\nu(x,du)<\infty\quad\text{for any $x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}$}.

To simplify the notation, we assume that the kernel
\nu(x,du)
does not have an atom at 0 and write
\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}
instead of
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\backslash\{0\}}
.

Under certain conditions (see Section
1
below) one can associate with
(L,C_{0}^{2}({\mathds{R}^{d}}))
a Markov process
X
, whose transition probability kernel
P_{t}(x,dy)
possesses certain regularity properties. The process
X
is often called a
Lévy-type
process, because locally its structure resembles the structure of a Lévy process, e.g. a process with independent stationary increments.

The aim of this paper is to derive sufficient conditions for ergodicity of the process
X
related to (
0.1
) and find the ergodic rates.

This work further develops our previous studies on the ergodicity of Lévy-type processes: we began our investigation in
[
5
]
with a one-dimensional case and bounded Lévy-type kernel; continued in
[
6
]
, where a Lévy-driven stochastic differential equation (SDE) with unbounded coefficients was considered in dimension
d
.

The long-time behaviour of Markov processes has been extensively investigated. Skorokhod in
[
15
]
discussed a long-time behaviour of general Markov processes and solutions to SDEs, as well as stabilisation properties of solutions to systems of SDEs. Masuda in
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
investigated a Lévy-driven Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, in particular, the existence of a smooth density, ergodicity and convergence rates. Sufficient conditions for recurrence, positive recurrence and exponential ergodicity of one-dimensional Lévy-type processes generated by the integro-differential operator were derived in
[
16
]
. In
[
17
]
sufficient conditions for exponential and strong ergodicity of stochastic differential equations driven by symmetric
\alpha-
stable processes were obtained. Ergodicity of a solution to the jump-diffusion SDE was studied by Wee in
[
18
]
. In
[
4
]
and
[
2
]
sufficient conditions for ergodicity were given in the form of so-called
(f,r)
-modulated moments. In
[
9
]
the problem of the uniqueness of an invariant probability measure of a Markov process was investigated; here the authors also derived sufficient conditions for the weak convergence of the transition probability to the invariant measure. Kulik in
[
8
]
discussed the asymptotic behaviour of a wide range of Markov processes from simple Markov chains with finite state spaces to more sophisticated models (including solutions to Lévy driven SDEs), in particular, the author examined different types of ergodic rates and their further application in limit theorems; the question of the weak ergodicity was addressed as well. Sandrić in
[
12
]
gave sufficient conditions for recurrence, transience and ergodicity of stable-like processes in terms of their coefficients. Further, sufficient conditions for recurrence, transience and ergodicity of stable-like Markov chains under certain assumptions on their density functions and drift were derived in
[
13
]
. In
[
14
]
the author discussed recurrence, transience, ergodicity, strong, (sub)-exponential ergodicity and mixing properties of Lévy-type processes driven by the infinitesimal generator.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section
1
, we recall the notion of ergodicity and discuss the assumptions. In Section
2
we formulate our results. Proofs are given in Section
3
and examples in Section
4
.

1.
Preliminaries

A Markov process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
with a transition probability kernel
P_{t}(x,dy)
is called (strongly)

ergodic
(see
[
14
]
) if there exists an invariant probability measure
\pi(\cdot)
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|P_{t}(x,\cdot)-\pi(\cdot)\|_{TV}=0

for any
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Here
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
is the total variation norm. Moreover, one can describe the speed of convergence to the invariant measure, if certain assumptions are satisfied. The process is called (non-uniformly)
\psi
-ergodic (see
[
14
]
for polynomial and exponential rates), if there exists a function
\psi\geq 0
,
\psi(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, such that

(1.1)

\|P_{t}(x,\cdot)-\pi(\cdot)\|_{TV}\leq C(x)\psi(t),\quad t\geq 1,

for some
C\colon{\mathds{R}^{d}}\to[0,\infty)
.

There are several possible ways to show ergodicity of a Markov process (see
[
8
, §3.5]
). We use the skeleton chain approach, described in
[
8
]
, which allows us to reduce the continuous-time setting to the discrete one by constructing a skeleton chain
X^{h}:=\{X_{nh},n\in\mathds{Z}_{+}\}
and apply ergodic theorems, developed specifically for Markov chains. Under certain irreducibility assumptions (e.g., Dobrushin condition), ergodicity of the skeleton chain implies ergodicity of the initial process with the same convergence rate (cf.
[
8
, §3.2]
,
[
14
]
).

To see if the chain is ergodic, one can check the (local) Dobrushin (or other forms of irreducibility condition, suitable for a particular task (see the discussion in
[
8
, p.64]
)) and the Lyapunov-type conditions (
[
8
, Th.2.8.9, Th.3.2.3]
). Note that the irreducibility-type assumption is crucial for the described approach, but if it fails, it is still possible for the process to be ergodic only in a weaker sense (see the discussion in
[
8
, §4]
).

A Markov chain
X
with a transition kernel
\mathds{P}(x,dy)
satisfies the
local Dobrushin
condition (cf.
[
8
, p. 42]
) on a measurable set
B\subset{\mathds{R}^{d}}\times{\mathds{R}^{d}}
if

(1.2)

\sup_{x,y\in B}\|\mathds{P}(x,\cdot)-\mathds{P}(y,\cdot)\|_{TV}<2.

In practice, it is sufficient and reasonable to check if there exists an absolutely continuous component of
\mathds{P}(x,dy)
, such that
x\mapsto\mathds{P}(x,\cdot)
is continuous in
L_{1}
(see
[
8
, Prop.2.9.1]
), instead of proving (
1.2
) directly. This, in turn, is the case if the initial Markov process possesses a strictly positive transition probability density
p_{t}(x,\cdot)
, such that
x\mapsto p_{t}(x,\cdot)
is continuous in
x
in
L_{1}
(see
[
14
]
). In other words, it means that the respective semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
P_{t}f(x):=\mathds{E}^{x}f(X_{t})
, possesses the
C_{b}
-Feller property, that is, it preserves the space of bounded continuous functions (see
[
1
]
). The Dobrushin condition ensures that the process converges to the same invariant measure, regardless of its starting point.

The
Lyapunov-type
condition (see
[
8
, p.52]
) allows us to establish moment estimates, responsible for (non-uniform) ergodic rates. Suppose that there exist:

•

a norm-like function
V:\,{\mathds{R}^{d}}\to[1,\infty)
(i.e.
V(x)\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
), bounded on a compact
K
;

•

a function
f:\,[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, that admits a non-negative increasing and concave extension to
[0,\infty)
;

•

a constant
C>0
,

such that the following relations hold:

(1.3)

\mathds{E}^{x}V(X_{1})-V(x)\leq-f(V(x))+C,\quad x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},

f\left(1+\inf_{x\notin K}V(x)\right)>2C.

Inequality (
1.3
) is called the
Lyapunov inequality
, and such a function
V
is called a
Lyapunov function
.

Given that we are in the continuous-time settings, it is reasonable to consider the Lyapunov-type condition in terms of the process
X
itself, namely, in terms of its
full
generator
\mathcal{L}
:

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}V(x)\leq-f(V(x))+C,

where
f,V
and
C
have the same meaning as above.

The full generator is taken into consideration because Lyapunov functions are often unbounded and the domain of
L
is too narrow to include them. Recall the “stochastic version” of a
full generator
, see
[
1
, (1.50)]
. The full generator of a measurable contraction semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on the space
C_{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{d})
of continuous functions vanishing at infinity is

\displaystyle\hat{L}:=\Big\{(f,g)\in(\mathcal{B}({\mathds{R}^{d}}),\mathcal{B}({\mathds{R}^{d}})):\quad M_{t}^{[f,g]}:=f(X_{t})-f(x)=\int_{0}^{t}g(X_{s})ds,\quad x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},\,t\geq 0,\Big.

\displaystyle\Big.\text{is a local $\mathcal{F}_{t}^{X}:=\sigma(X_{s},s\leq t)$ martingale}\Big\}.

Note that in general
\hat{L}
is not single-valued, see the discussion in
[
1
, p.36]
.

Since for
f\in C_{0}^{2}({\mathds{R}^{d}})

(1.5)

P_{t}f(x)-f(x)=\int_{0}^{t}P_{s}Lf(x)ds\quad\forall t\geq 0,\,x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},

see
[
3
, Ch.1, Prop.1.5]
, by the strong Markov property and the Dynkin formula
(f,Lf)\subset\hat{L}
for any
f\in C_{0}^{2}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
.
Further, it is not difficult to check that

Lf(x)<\infty
for
f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
, where

\mathcal{D}_{0}:=\left\{f\in C^{2}({\mathds{R}^{d}}):\quad\left|\int_{|u|\geq 1}(f(x+u)-f(x))\nu(x,du)\right|<\infty\quad\forall x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}\right\}.

Using the localization procedure, the Ito formula and taking the expectation, we derive (
1.5
) for any
f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
and, consequently, we get that
(f,Lf)\in\hat{L},\,f\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
.
We denote this extension of
L
to
\mathcal{D}_{0}
by
\mathcal{L}
.

Summarising, condition (
1.4
) for the initial process implies (
1.3
) for its skeleton chain (see
[
8
, Th.3.2.3]
), which along with the Dorbrushin condition ensures the ergodicity in total variation of this chain and provides the (non-uniform) ergodic rate (cf.
[
8
, Th.2.8.6, Th.2.8.8.]
). As it was mentioned above, the ergodicity of the chain implies the ergodicity of the initial process with the same ergodic rate.

The speed of convergence to
\pi
in (
1.1
) as
t\to\infty
is (cf.
[
8
, Th.2.8.8, Th.3.2.3]
)

(1.6)

\psi(t):=\left(\frac{1}{f(F^{-1}(\gamma t))}\right)^{\delta},\qquad F(t):=\int\limits_{1}^{t}\frac{dw}{f(w)},\quad t\in(1,\infty),

where
\gamma,\delta\in(0,1)
. Moreover,

\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}f(V(x))\pi(dx)<\infty.

Our standing assumptions in this paper would be

(
A1)

There exists a solution
\{X_{t},\,t>0\}
to the martingale problem for
(L,C_{0}^{2}({\mathds{R}^{d}}))
;

(
A2)

The skeleton chain
X^{h}:=\{X_{nh},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
satisfies the Dobrushin condition.

Condition (
A1
) assures the existence of the process associated with (
0.1
) (which we call the
Markovian selection
related to
L
), but it looks rather abstract and is not easy to check. The sufficient conditions ensuring (
A1
) were obtained in
[
7
]
and are the following:

(
A1’)

The symbol
q
is locally bounded, continuous in
x
, satisfies
q(x,0)=0
;

(
A1”)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\leq R}\sup_{|\xi|\leq R^{-1}}|q(x,\xi)|=0.

Recall that the symbol
q(x,\xi)
of the operator
L
is a function, such that for
f
from the Schwartz space
S({\mathds{R}^{d}})

Lf(x)=-\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}q(x,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

where
\hat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
, and

\displaystyle q(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=-i\ell(x)\cdot\xi+\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(1-e^{i\xi\cdot x}+i\xi\cdot u\mathds{1}_{|u|\leq 1}\right)\nu(x,du).

Under (
A1’
) and (
A1”
), there exists a solution to the Martingale problem for
(L,C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathds{R}^{d}}))
, which is strongly Markov, see
[
7
, Th.1.1, Cor.4.1]
.

The uniqueness of the process in the case of unbounded coefficients is harder to prove. Up to our knowledge, at the moment there are no general results establishing the uniqueness of the solution to the Martingale problem for
(L,C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathds{R}^{d}}))
, if the kernel
\nu(x,du)
is unbounded, unless one can rewrite
L
(using the change of measure) in the form, usually used in Lévy-driven SDEs, and check the conditions for the existence and uniqueness of the (strong or weak) solution to the respective SDE. We do not pursue this topic in the current article and confine ourselves with finding the conditions that guarantee the ergodicity of
some
Markovian selection, related to
(L,C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathds{R}^{d}}))
.

It is a kind of general knowledge that ergodicity can come from the drift or diffusion parts of the generator. We investigate the conditions on the Lévy-type measure under which (
1.4
) is satisfied. Our interest lies both in the drift- and noise-induced ergodicity. Further, we will show that in the second case, when the ergodic behaviour comes from the kernel
\nu(x,\cdot)
itself, the support of
\nu(x,\cdot)
must depend on the direction of the vector
x
.

2.
Settings and results

Denote by
|u|
the norm of a vector
u\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}
; by
\mathcal{B}(r)
and
\mathcal{B}^{c}(r)
the ball centred at
0
with radius
r
and its complement, respectively; by
\mathcal{A}(r,R)
the annulus with the inner radius
r
and the outer radius
R
.

As a Lyapunov function we take

(2.1)

V(x)=1+\phi^{p}(x),\quad p\in(0,1),\quad x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},

where
\phi:{\mathds{R}^{d}}\to\mathds{R}_{+}
,
\phi\in C^{2}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
, such that
\phi(x)=|x|
for
|x|>1
and
\phi(x)\leq|x|
for
|x|\leq 1
.

We also denote by
e_{x}=\frac{x}{\|x\|}
the unit vector of
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}
, and by
\gamma_{x,u}
the cosine of the angle between unit vectors
e_{x},e_{u}
:

\gamma_{x,u}:=\cos\angle(e_{x},e_{u}).

The aim of the article is to find conditions ensuring the Lyapunov-type inequality (
1.4
) for
\mathcal{L}
. Our strategy is to select its negative and positive components to compare their impact on the generator. For the sake of simplicity, we consider separately the “drift” and “kernel” parts:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}V(x)=\nabla V(x)\cdot\Big(\ell(x)+\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(1,|x|)}u\nu(x,du)\Big)\,+

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\left(V(x+u)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)\cdot u\mathds{1}_{|u|\leq|x|}\right)\nu(x,du)=:

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{L}^{drift}V(x)+\mathcal{L}^{ker}V(x).

Consider the “drift” part. For
|x|
big enough
V(x)=1+|x|^{p}
, so we can transform

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{drift}V(x)&=p|x|^{p-1}\Big(|\ell(x)|\gamma_{x,\ell(x)}+\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(1,|x|)}\gamma_{x,u}|u|\,\nu(x,du)\Big).\end{split}

Since our ultimate goal is to compare the speed of all generator components, we define the first “growth” function

\phi^{drift}(x):=|x|^{p-1}\max{\Big(|\ell(x)|,\int_{\mathcal{A}(1,|x|)}|u|\,\nu(x,du)\Big)}.

It is not hard to see that there exists
\zeta\in[-2,2]
, such that

(2.2)

\mathcal{L}^{drift}V(x)\leq-p\zeta\phi^{drift}(x).

Additionally, fix
\lambda\in(0,1)
and split the “kernel” part
\mathcal{L}^{ker}
into the “ball” and “tail” sub-components, based on the jump size
|u|
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{ker}V(x)=\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}\left(V(x+u)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)\cdot u\right)\nu(x,du)\,+

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}^{c}(|x|^{\lambda})}\left(V(x+u)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)u\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}\right)\nu(x,du)=:

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{L}^{ball}V(x)+\mathcal{L}^{tail}V(x).

Further, again according to the jump size
|u|
, split the “tail” part:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{tail}V(x)=\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}\left(V(x+u)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)\cdot u\right)\nu(x,du)\,+

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}^{c}(|x|)}\left(V(x+u)-V(x)\right)\nu(x,du)=:

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{L}^{big}V(x)+\mathcal{L}^{large}V(x).

Now we are ready to introduce the rest of the “growth” functions:

\phi^{ball}_{+}(x):=|x|^{p-2}\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}|u|^{2}\,\nu(x,du),\qquad\phi^{ball}_{-}(x):=|x|^{p-2}\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}|u|^{2}\gamma_{x,u}^{2}\,\nu(x,du),

and

\phi^{big}(x):=\frac{p}{2}|x|^{p-1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}|u|\,\nu(x,du),\qquad\phi^{large}(x):=\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}^{c}(|x|)}|u|^{p}\,\nu(x,du).

Therefore, we arrive at the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
V
from (
2.1
) and
\zeta\in[-2,2]
:

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}V(x)\leq-p\zeta\phi^{drift}(x)+\frac{p}{4}(1+o(1))(\phi^{ball}_{+}(x)+(p-2)\phi^{ball}_{-}(x))+\phi^{big}(x)+\phi^{large}(x),\quad|x|\to\infty.

Inequality (
2.4
) gives us a first vision on how the final condition may look. Indeed, consider the right-hand side of (
2.4
). The last two terms (which relate to “tail”) are positive by construction. Consequently, the negativity of
\mathcal{L}V
can come from either the “drift” or “ball” components, or a combination thereof. According to this, we introduce several constants that illustrate the interconnection of the corresponding “growth” functions.

First, it can be seen from (
2.4
) that
\phi^{ball}_{+}
and
\phi^{ball}_{-}
correspond to the positive and negative parts of “ball’ respectively thus, the sign of
\mathcal{L}^{ball}V
is fully determined by their relation. Define

C^{ball}:=\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\phi^{ball}_{+}(x)}{\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)}.

By definition
C^{ball}\geq 1
and it has to be less than
2-p
for
\mathcal{L}^{ball}V
to be negative. In other words, it means that the measure
\nu(x,\cdot)
has to be concentrated along the direction, collinear to
x
.

To compare “drift” with “tail” we introduce

C^{big}:=\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\phi^{big}(x)}{\phi^{drift}(x)}\quad\text{and}\quad C^{large}:=\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\phi^{large}(x)}{\phi^{drift}(x)}.

In the same vein, the constant

(2.5)

C^{tail}:=\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\max{(\phi^{big}(x),\,\phi^{large}(x))}}{\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)}.

shows the relation between “ball” and “tail”.

In both cases, finite constants ensure that “tail” does not spoil the negativity of
\mathcal{L}V
, and under additional assumptions one can get either “drift”- or “ball”-induced ergodicity (respectively).

Remark 2.1
.

In (
2.5
) we do not consider the constant, related to
\phi^{ball}_{+}
, because in the case of “ball”-induced ergodicity, the strictly negative component of “ball” (that is
\phi^{ball}_{-}
) has to dominate.

Finally, to compare “drift” and “ball”, we define

\tilde{C}^{drift}_{+}:=\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\phi^{ball}_{+}(x)}{\phi^{drift}(x)}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}:=\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)}{\phi^{drift}(x)}.

Note that the functions
\phi^{drift}
and
\phi^{ball}_{-}
are not necessarily monotone; thus, in order to formulate our main result in a more “user-friendly” manner, we assume that they can be bounded from below by some power-type functions. Namely, suppose that for
|x|
big enough there exist some strictly positive
K^{drift},K^{ball}
, and
\gamma^{drift}
,
\gamma^{ball}
, such that the following inequalities hold:

(2.6)

\phi^{drift}(x)\geq K^{drift}|x|^{\gamma^{drift}},\qquad\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)\geq K^{ball}|x|^{\gamma^{ball}}.

Now we are ready to formulate the main results of the paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Case 1. (“Drift-induced” ergodicity). Suppose that (
2.2
) holds with
\zeta\in(0,2]
, and
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{+}
,
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}
,
C^{big}
,
C^{large}
are finite. Then (
1.4
) holds with
f(u)=Ku^{\gamma^{drift}/p},K>0
, if

(2.7)

-p\zeta+\frac{p}{4}\left(\tilde{C}^{drift}_{+}+(p-2)\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}\right)+C^{big}+C^{large}<0.

Case 2. (“Ball-induced” ergodicity). Suppose that
C^{ball},C^{tail}<\infty
. Then (
1.4
) holds with
f(u)=Ku^{\gamma^{ball}/p},K\!>\!0
, if either

a)
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}=\infty
,
\zeta\in[-2,2]
and

(2.8)

\frac{p}{4}(C^{ball}+p-2)+2C^{tail}<0.

or

b)
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}\in(0,\infty)
,
\zeta\in[-2,0]
and

(2.9)

-\frac{p\zeta}{\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}}+\frac{p}{4}(C^{ball}+p-2)+2C^{tail}<0.

Remark 2.2
.

In Case 2 we have purely “ball”-induced ergodicity: in Case 2a) “drift” does not have any impact comparing to “ball” and in Case 2b) “drift” is positive and thus works even against. In Case 1 ergodicity comes from “drift” only, if
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}=0
; otherwise both components contribute.

3.
Proofs

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

We already have the estimate of
\mathcal{L}^{drift}V
(see (
2.2
)). Consider now
\mathcal{L}^{ball}V
. Since
|u|\in\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})
, then for
|x|\gg 1
:

|x+u|\geq|x|\cdot|1-|x|^{\lambda-1}|>1,

and
V(x+u)=1+|x+u|^{p}
. Thus, we can apply Taylor’s formula to the integrand function of the first term of (
2.3
) and get

V(x+u)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)\cdot u=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)u^{\prime}D^{2}V(x+\theta u)ud\theta,

where

D^{2}V(x)=\left\{\partial_{ij}^{2}V(x)\right\}_{i,j=1}^{d}=p|x|^{p-2}\begin{pmatrix}1+(p-2)\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}&(p-2)\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}&\cdots&(p-2)\frac{x_{1}x_{d}}{|x|^{2}}\\
(p-2)\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}&1+(p-2)\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}&\cdots&(p-2)\frac{x_{2}x_{d}}{|x|^{2}}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\ddots&\cdots\\
(p-2)\frac{x_{1}x_{d}}{|x|^{2}}&(p-2)\frac{x_{2}x_{d}}{|x|^{2}}&\cdots&1+(p-2)\frac{x_{d}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{ball}V(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}\int\limits_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)u^{\prime}\cdot D^{2}V(x+\theta u)\cdot u\nu(x,du)d\theta=

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}\int\limits_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)|x+\theta u|^{p-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\left(1+(p-2)\frac{(x_{i}+\theta u_{i})^{2}}{|x+\theta u|^{2}}\right)u_{i}^{2}}\right.\,+

\displaystyle\left.+(p-2)\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{d}{u_{i}u_{j}\frac{(x_{i}+\theta u_{i})(x_{j}+\theta u_{j})}{|x+\theta u|^{2}}}\right)d\theta\nu(x,du).

Regroup the sums in the inner integral and apply the sum of squares formula to
u_{i}(x_{i}+\theta u_{i})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{ball}V(x)=\frac{p}{2}\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}\int\limits_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)|x+\theta u|^{p-2}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}{u_{i}^{2}}+\frac{p-2}{|x+\theta u|^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}{u_{i}(x_{i}+\theta u_{i})}\right)^{2}\right)}d\theta\nu(x,du)=

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}\int\limits_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)|x+\theta u|^{p-2}|u|^{2}\Big(1+\underbrace{(p-2)\left(e_{u}\cdot e_{x+\theta u}\right)^{2}}_{\text{negative}}\Big)d\theta\nu(x,du).

Consider the squared scalar product

\displaystyle(e_{u}\cdot e_{x+\theta u})^{2}=\left(\gamma_{x,u}\frac{|x|}{|x+\theta u|}+\frac{\theta|u|}{|x+\theta u|}\right)^{2}\geq\gamma_{x,u}^{2}\left(\frac{|x|}{|x|+|u|}\right)^{2}-\frac{2\theta|x|\cdot|u|}{(|x|-|u|)^{2}}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma_{x,u}^{2}}{(1+|x|^{\lambda-1})^{2}}-\frac{2|x|^{\lambda}}{|x|(1-|x|^{\lambda-1})^{2}}.

Estimating the inner integral of
\mathcal{L}^{ball}V
depending upon its sign (again by triangle inequalities), we get

\frac{1}{2}(|x|\cdot|1+|x|^{\lambda-1}|)^{p-2}\leq\int\limits_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)|x+\theta u|^{p-2}\,d\theta\leq\frac{1}{2}(|x|\cdot|1-|x|^{\lambda-1}|)^{p-2}.

Summarising, we derive the final estimate for the “ball” part:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{ball}V(x)\leq\frac{p}{4}|x|^{p-2}\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}(|x|^{\lambda})}|u|^{2}\left(C_{1}(x)+(p-2)C_{2}(x)\gamma^{2}_{x,u}\right)\nu(x,du)=

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{4}C_{1}(x)\phi^{ball}_{+}(x)+\frac{p(p-2)}{4}C_{2}(x)\phi^{ball}_{-}(x),

where

C_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{\left(1-|x|^{\lambda-1}\right)^{2-p}}+\frac{2(2-p)|x|^{\lambda-1}}{(1+|x|^{\lambda-1})^{2-p}(1-|x|^{\lambda-1})^{2}},\quad C_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{\left(1+|x|^{\lambda-1}\right)^{4-p}}.

Both
C_{1}(x)
and
C_{2}(x)
tend to 1 as
|x|
tends to infinity; therefore for
|x|
large enough:

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}^{ball}V(x)\leq\frac{p}{4}(1+o(1))(\phi^{ball}_{+}(x)+(p-2)\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)).

Next, consider the “tail” part, beginning with the big jumps. Since by definition
V(x)
is either
1+|x|^{p}
or
1+\phi^{p}(x)
(depending on the value of
|x|
), and
\phi(x)\leq|x|
, then
V(x+u)\leq 1+|x+u|^{p}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{big}V(x)\leq\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}(|x+u|^{p}-|x|^{p}-p|x|^{p-1}\cdot|u|\gamma_{x,u})\,\nu(x,du)=

\displaystyle=|x|^{p}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}\left(\left(1+2\frac{|u|}{|x|}\gamma_{x,u}+\left(\frac{|u|}{|x|}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}-1\right)\,\nu(x,du)\,-

\displaystyle-\,p|x|^{p-1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}|u|\gamma_{x,u}\,\nu(x,du)=:J_{1}(x)+J_{2}(x).

Now using the fact that
u\in\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)
, we derive

\displaystyle J_{1}(x)\leq|x|^{p}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}\Big(\Big(1+(1+2\gamma_{x,u})\frac{|u|}{|x|}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}-1\Big)\nu(x,du)\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p}{2}|x|^{p-1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}(1+2\gamma_{x,u})|u|\,\nu(x,du).

Summing
J_{1}(x)
and
J_{2}(x)
, we obtain the final estimate of the big jumps part:

(3.2)

\mathcal{L}^{big}V(x)\leq\frac{p}{2}|x|^{p-1}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\lambda},|x|)}|u|\nu(x,du)=\phi^{big}(x).

In the case of large jumps we again use that by definition
V(x+u)\leq 1+|x+u|^{p}
. Then

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}^{large}V(x)\leq\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}^{c}(|x|)}\left(|x+u|^{p}-|x|^{p}\right)\nu(x,du)\leq\int\limits_{\mathcal{B}^{c}(|x|)}|u|^{p}\,\nu(x,du)=\phi^{large}(x).

Together estimates (
2.2
), (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) yield the statement of the lemma, namely,

\mathcal{L}V(x)\leq-p\zeta\phi^{drift}(x)+\frac{p}{4}(1+o(1))(\phi^{ball}_{+}(x)+(p-2)\phi^{ball}_{-}(x))+\phi^{big}(x)+\phi^{large}(x),\,|x|\to\infty.

∎

Now, using the results of the proven lemma, we can easily prove Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Case 1. “Drift-induced” ergodicity
. Suppose that (
2.2
) holds with
\zeta\in(0,2]
. Dividing both sides of (
2.4
) by
\phi^{drift}
and taking
\limsup
as
|x|\to\infty
, we get

(3.4)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{L}V(x)}{\phi^{drift}(x)}\leq-p\zeta+\frac{p}{4}\left(\tilde{C}^{drift}_{+}+(p-2)\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}\right)+C^{big}+C^{large}.

Then it is clear that the Lyapunov-type condition (
1.4
) is satisfied if
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{+}
,
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}
,
C^{big}
,
C^{large}
are finite, and moreover the right-hand side of (
3.4
) is strictly negative.

Case 2. “Ball-induced” ergodicity
.

a) Assume that
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}=\infty
(that is, “ball” is way faster than “drift”) and
\zeta\in[-2,2]
. Dividing this time both sides of (
2.4
) by
\phi^{ball}_{-}
and taking
\limsup
as
|x|\to\infty
, we get

(3.5)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{L}V(x)}{\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)}\leq\frac{p}{4}(C^{ball}+p-2)+2C^{tail}.

If
C^{ball},C^{tail}<\infty
, and the right-hand side of (
3.5
) is strictly negative, condition (
1.4
) is satisfied.

b) Assume
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}\in(0,\infty)
and
\zeta\in[-2,0]
. Following the same approach as above we get

(3.6)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{L}V(x)}{\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)}\leq-\frac{p\zeta}{\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}}+\frac{p}{4}(C^{ball}+p-2)+2C^{tail}.

If
C^{ball},C^{tail}<\infty
, and the right-hand side of (
3.6
) is strictly negative, condition (
1.4
) holds.

In each case, the form of
f
from (
1.4
) (and thus of the ergodic rates
\psi
from (
1.6
)) is determined by (
2.6
).

∎

4.
Examples

Example 1
.

Consider a one-dimensional symmetric bounded
\nu(\cdot,\cdot)
and suppose that the drift part
\ell(x)
is absent. Then
\phi^{drift}
,
\phi^{big}
and
\phi^{large}
are all equal to zero for all
x\in\mathds{R}
, and we are in the setting of Case 2a) with
C^{tail}=0
. Moreover, given that we are in the one-dimensional setting,
|\gamma_{x,u}|=1
, thus,
\phi^{ball}_{+}=\phi^{ball}_{-}
and
C^{ball}=1
. Then (
2.8
) is always satisfied, yielding the Lyapunov-type condition (
1.4
).

Example 2
.

Consider now an
\alpha
-stable Lévy-type measure
\nu(x,du)=|x|^{\beta}|u|^{-d-\alpha}du,\,x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},
with
\beta\in(1-p,1)
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
. Take
\ell(x)=|x|^{\eta}
,
\eta\in(0,\beta)
, and assume that
\zeta\in[1/2,1]
.

First, estimate

\phi^{ball}_{-}(x)\leq\phi^{ball}_{+}(x)=\frac{S(d)}{2-\alpha}|x|^{p-2+\beta+\lambda(2-\alpha)},

where
S(d)
is the volume of a unit sphere in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
.

Next, to calculate the rest of the “growth” functions we define

\phi^{ann}_{\tau}(x):=|x|^{p-1+\beta}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}(|x|^{\tau},|x|)}|u|^{1-d-\alpha}\,du,\quad\tau\in\{0,\lambda\}.

Simple calculations lead us to

\phi^{ann}_{\tau}(x)\asymp S(d)\begin{cases}(\alpha-1)^{-1}|x|^{p-1+\beta+(1-\alpha)\tau},&\alpha>1,\\
(1-\alpha)^{-1}|x|^{p+\beta-\alpha},&\alpha<1,\\
(1-\tau)|x|^{p-1+\beta}\ln|x|,&\alpha=1.\end{cases}

Then (remember that
\eta\in(0,\beta)
)

\phi^{drift}(x)=\phi^{ann}_{0}(x),\qquad\phi^{big}(x)=\frac{p}{2}\phi^{ann}_{\lambda}(x).

Finally,

\phi^{large}(x)=\frac{S(d)}{\alpha-p}|x|^{p+\beta-\alpha}.

We can easily see that
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{+}
(hence and
\tilde{C}^{drift}_{-}
) are equal to zero for every
\alpha\in(0,2)
. Furthermore, given that
\zeta\in[1/2,1]
, inequality (
2.7
) is automatically satisfied for
\alpha\geq 1
, thus, Lyapunov-type condition (
1.4
) holds with
f(u)=Cu^{\frac{p-1+\beta}{p}}
,
C\leq p\zeta
, for
\alpha>1
, and with
f(u)=Cu^{\frac{p-1+\beta}{p}}\ln{u}
,
C\leq\frac{p}{2}(2\zeta-1+\lambda)
, for
\alpha=1
. For
\alpha<1
, (
1.4
) holds with
f(u)=Cu^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p}}
,
C\leq p\left(\zeta-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha-p}
, if
p
satisfies the inequality

p^{2}(1-2\zeta)-p\alpha(1-2\zeta)-2(1-\alpha)>0.

In each case,
C
was derived directly from (
2.7
).

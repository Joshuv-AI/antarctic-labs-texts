The structural method for Ordinary Differential Equations
S. Clain, M.T. Malheiro, G.J. Machado, R. Costa

Abstract
We design and analyse a new numerical method to solve ODE system based on the structural method. We compute approximations of solutions together with its derivatives up to order K K by solving an entire block corresponding to R R time steps. We build the physical relations that connect the function and derivative approximations at each time step by using the ODE and its derivatives and develop the structural equations that establish linear relations between the function and its derivative over the whole block of R R times steps. The non-linear system is solved and provide very accurate approximations with nice spectral resolution properties.

Very recently, a new approach based on the combination of physical and structural equations have been developed for a restricted number of collocation points and derivatives

The advantage of the method regarding to the traditional ones is the evaluation of the coefficients that does not require analytic expressions, but, on the contrary, derives from the basis of a matrix kernel. In other words, coefficients come from a computational procedure whatever are the configurations (number of derivatives

We also present an algorithm to solve the non-linear system and prove the convergence of the iterative method under a CFL-like condition. We address the spectral resolution issue in section 5 and focus on the ability of our schemes to provide very high-resolution that enable to recover sub-scale information, that is variations occurring between two successive time steps. We produce in section 6 structural post-processing equations that enable to evaluate higher order derivatives than were not produce by the scheme itself. The section 7 concerns the numerical tests and benchmarks to assess the different properties of the structural scheme, namely the accuracy, the stability and the spectral resolution. We also demonstrate the superior quality of the method dealing with stiff and difficult problems, including chaotic system. 2  General design of the structural method

Consider the generic first-order scalar Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) problem

\phi^{\prime}(t)=f(\phi(t),t),\ t\in[0,T],

(1)

together with the initial condition
\phi(0)=\phi_{0}
, with
T>0
the final time and
f\equiv f(z,t)
a regular function in
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
t_{n}=n\Delta t
an uniform grid with
n{\in\llbracket{0,N}\rrbracket}
with
T=N\Delta t
and denote by
\phi^{(k)}
the
k
-derivative of the solution to equation (
1
). The approximations of the solution and the derivatives over the time subdivision is given by

\phi^{(k)}_{n}\approx\phi^{(k)}(t_{n}).

The core concept of the structural method consists in splitting the scheme into two subsystems of equations: the Physical Equations that only deal with the relation(
1
) and its derivatives, and the Structural Equations that are linear relations, only depending on the grid, and definitively independent of the underlying problem.

2.1 Physical Equations

Relation (
1
) involves the first derivative
\phi^{(1)}(t)
regarding the function
\phi^{(0)}(t)
and the time and reads

\phi^{(1)}(t)={\texttt{df}^{(0)}\big[{t,\phi^{(0)}}\big]}=f\left(\phi^{(0)}(t),t\right).

(2)

Time derivative of equation (
2
) provides the new physical equation

\phi^{(2)}(t)={\texttt{df}^{(1)}\big[{t,\phi^{(0)},\phi^{(1)}}\big]}=\partial_{z}f\left(\phi^{(0)}(t),t\right)\phi^{(1)}(t)+\partial_{t}f\left(\phi^{(0)}(t),t\right)

(3)

where
\partial_{z}f
and
\partial_{t}f
denote the derivatives in order to the first and second argument respectively. Once again, time derivative of equation (
3
) provides a relation between
\phi
and all the derivatives up to the third-order one

\begin{split}\phi^{(3)}(t)={\texttt{df}^{(2)}\big[{t,\phi^{(0)},\phi^{(1)},\phi^{(2)}}\big]}&=\partial_{z}f\left(\phi^{(0)}(t),t\right)\phi^{(2)}(t)+\partial_{zz}f\left(\phi^{(0)}(t),t\right)\left[\phi^{(1)}(t)\right]^{2}\\
&\qquad+2\partial_{zt}f\left(\phi^{(0)}(t),t\right)\phi^{(1)}(t)+\partial_{tt}f\left(\phi^{(0)}(t),t\right).\end{split}

(4)

Applying the chain rule several times, provide the relations

\phi^{(k+1)}(t)={\texttt{df}^{(k)}\big[{t,\phi^{(0)},\phi^{(1)},\ldots,\phi^{(k)}}\big]}=\frac{d}{dt}{\texttt{df}^{(k-1)}\big[{t,\phi^{(0)},\phi^{(1)},\ldots,\phi^{(k-1)}}\big]},\quad k{\in\llbracket{0,K-1}\rrbracket}.

Remark
:

Note that, by construction, the application

(t,z_{0},z_{1},\cdots,z_{k})\to{\texttt{df}^{(k)}\big[{t,z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}}\big]}

is a polynomial function with respect to
z_{1},\cdots,z_{k}
with non-linear coefficients solely depending on
t
and
z_{0}
corresponding to the partial derivative in time and
z
.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

Physical equation just consists in substituting the exact values by its approximation
\phi^{(k)}_{n}
. Therefore, the first Physical Equation
{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{1}}}}}(n)
, deriving from relation (
2
), simply reads

\phi^{(1)}_{n}={\texttt{df}^{(0)}\big[{t_{n},\phi_{n}^{(0)}}\big]}=f(\phi^{(0)}_{n},t_{n}).

Similarly, we deduce the second Physical Equation
{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{2}}}}}(n)
from relation (
3
) and we have

\phi^{(2)}_{n}={\texttt{df}^{(1)}\big[{t,\phi_{n}^{(0)},\phi_{n}^{(1)}}\big]}=\partial_{z}f(\phi^{(0)}_{n},t_{n})\phi^{(1)}_{n}+\partial_{t}f(\phi^{(0)}_{n},t_{n})

while the third Physical Equation
{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{3}}}}}(n)
deriving from relation (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\ \phi^{(3)}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\texttt{df}^{(2)}\big[{t,\phi_{n}^{(0)},\phi_{n}^{(1)},\phi_{n}^{(2)}}\big]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{z}f\left(\phi^{(0)}_{n},t_{n}\right)\phi^{(2)}_{n}+\partial_{zz}f\left(\phi^{(0)}_{n},t_{n}\right)\left[\phi^{(1)}_{n}\right]^{2}+2\partial_{zt}f\left(\phi^{(0)}_{n},t_{n}\right)\phi^{(1)}_{n}+\partial_{tt}f\left(\phi^{(0)}_{n},t_{n}\right).

Higher derivatives Physical Equations
{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{4}}}}}(n)
,
{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{5}}}}}(n)
are obtained similarly, and we define

\phi_{n}^{(4)}={\texttt{df}^{(3)}\big[{t,\phi_{n}^{(0)},\phi_{n}^{(1)},\ldots,\phi_{n}^{(3)}}\big]}
and
\phi_{n}^{(5)}={\texttt{df}^{(4)}\big[{t,\phi_{n}^{(0)},\ldots,\phi_{n}^{(4)}}\big]}
.

Remark
:

It is important to note that the
{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.30003pt}{{\,{k}}}}}(n)
equations only involve the approximations (function and derivatives) at the same point
t_{n}
. The necessary relations between the different quantities around time
t_{n}
will give rise to the second subset of relations tagged Structural Equations we present hereafter.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

2.2 Structural Equations

Given
K
the maximum derivative order, we provide a set of
K
Physical Equations, from
PE
1
up to
PE
K
, involving the derivatives
\phi^{(k)}_{n}
,
k{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket}
at any time
t_{n}
. Assuming that we know all the values at the time
t_{n}
, we want to determine the solutions and its derivatives within a block of size
R
of time step (labelled
R
-Block), that is, we want to compute the
(K+1)\times R
matrix

\Phi_{n}=\left(\phi^{(k)}_{n+r}\right)_{k,r},\qquad k{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket},\ r{\in\llbracket{1,R}\rrbracket},

as depicts in Figure
1
.

t_{n}
while the red points denote the unknowns that constitute the
R
-block

The
K
Physical Equations at time
t_{n+r}
,
r{\in\llbracket{1,R}\rrbracket}
, provide
R\times K
relations, Therefore
R
additional equations have to be produced, taking into account the underlying structure of the grid that represents the time line discretization, in other words, the numerical schemes.

2.2.1
General design

Structural equations are linear relations that involve approximations
\phi^{(k)}_{n+r}
for successive times
t_{n+r}
,
r{\in\llbracket{0,R}\rrbracket}
, and derivative order
k{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket}
. More precisely, an R-block structural equation reads

\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{r=0}^{R}a_{k,r}\,\Delta t^{k}\,\phi^{(k)}_{n+r}=0,

(5)

with
{\boldsymbol{a}}=\left(a_{k,r}\right)_{k,r}

k{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket}
,
r{\in\llbracket{0,R}\rrbracket}
the
(K+1)\times(R+1)
matrix that gathers all the components while
(\Delta t)^{k}
stands for the different powers of the time increment.

Remark
:

Noting by

\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}=\left(\phi^{(k)}_{n+r}\right)_{k,r},\hskip 22.00006ptk{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket},\ r{\in\llbracket{0,R}\rrbracket},

the extension of matrix
\Phi_{n}
by including the initial stage
n
, relation
5
has the compact form
{\boldsymbol{a}}{\boldsymbol{:}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}=0
where the operator
{\boldsymbol{:}}
stands for the double contraction.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

In order to determine the coefficients
a_{k,r}
of the structural equation, we introduce the functional

E({\boldsymbol{a}};\pi)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{r=0}^{R}a_{k,r}\,\Delta t^{k}\,\pi^{(k)}(t_{n+r})

for any function
\pi=\pi(t)
, regular enough.

We convert matrix
{\boldsymbol{a}}
into a one-index entries vector (still denoting
{\boldsymbol{a}}
for the sake of simplicity) by introducing the one-to one mapping
(k,r)\to\ell=\ell(k,r)=1+k(R+1)+r
from
\llbracket 0,K\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,R\rrbracket
onto
\llbracket 1,M\rrbracket
,
M=(K+1)(R+1)
. Then the cost function also reads

E({\boldsymbol{a}};\pi)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{r=0}^{R}a_{\ell}\,\Delta t^{k}\,\pi^{(k)}(t_{n+r}),\qquad\ell=\ell(k,r).

To design high-order of accuracy schemes, we impose the functional to exactly satisfies the relations for polynomial function up to a certain degree. To this end, we consider the list of polynomial functions

\pi_{m}(t;t_{n})=\left(\frac{t-t_{n}}{\Delta t}\right)^{m-1},\quad m{\in\llbracket{1,M}\rrbracket}.

By setting
E({\boldsymbol{a}};\pi_{m})=0
for all
m{\in\llbracket{1,M}\rrbracket}
, we get the
M\times M
linear system
\mathcal{M}{\boldsymbol{a}}=0
with the entries

\mathcal{M}[m,\ell]=\pi^{(k)}_{m}(t_{n+r};t_{n})=C^{m}_{k}r^{m-1-k},\quad\ell=\ell(k,r)

with

C^{m}_{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{(m-1)!}{(m-1-k)!}&\text{if $m>k$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

for
k{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket}
and
r{\in\llbracket{0,R}\rrbracket}
. We highlight the matrix
\mathcal{M}
does not involve the grid parameter
\Delta t
and is also independent of the initial time
t_{n}
due to the choice of a uniform mesh and function
\pi_{m}(.;t_{n})
.

Remark
:

Illustrating, for
K=2
and
R
, matrix
\mathcal{M}
is given by

\mathcal{M}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1&\ldots&1&0&0&\ldots&0&0&0&\ldots&0\\
0&1&\ldots&R^{1}&1&1&\ldots&1&0&0&\ldots&0\\
0&1&\ldots&R^{2}&0&2&\ldots&2\times R^{1}&2&2&\ldots&2\\
0&1&\ldots&R^{3}&0&3&\ldots&3\times R^{2}&0&3\times 2&\ldots&3\times 2\times R^{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&1&\ldots&R^{M-1}&0&(M-1)&\ldots&(M-1)\times R^{M-2}&0&(M-1)(M-2)&\ldots&(M-1)(M-2)R^{M-3}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Note that the matrix does not contain the time step
\Delta t
.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

We now analysis the linear system
\mathcal{M}{\boldsymbol{a}}=0
and its properties.

Definition 2.1
:

A set of functions
\left(\pi_{m}(t)\right)_{m=1,\ldots,M}
is resolvent if,

\big[\,\forall m{\in\llbracket{1,M}\rrbracket},\hskip 11.00003ptE({\boldsymbol{a}};\pi_{m})=0\,\big]\implies{\boldsymbol{a}}=0.

Proposition 2.1
.

The set of
M
polynomial functions of degree
M-1

\tau_{k,r}(t):=\tau_{\ell(k,r)}(t)=\left(\frac{t-t_{n+r}}{\Delta t}\right)^{k}\prod_{s\neq r}\left(\frac{t-t_{n+s}}{\Delta t}\right)^{K+1},\quad k{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket},\ r{\in\llbracket{0,R}\rrbracket},

is resolvent.

Proof.

We first remark that the degree of all the polynomial functions is strictly lower than M. Second, all the polynomials are linearly independent hence represent a basis of
\mathbb{P}_{M-1}
.
Noting that any derivative of order
k^{\prime}\leqslant K
and node
r^{\prime}\neq r
, we have
\tau_{k,r}^{(k^{\prime})}(t_{n+r^{\prime}})=0
hence

\displaystyle E({\boldsymbol{a}};\tau_{k,r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{K}\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{R}a_{k^{\prime},r^{\prime}}\,\Delta t^{k^{\prime}}\,\pi_{k,r}^{(k^{\prime})}(t_{n+r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{K}a_{k^{\prime},r}\,\Delta t^{k^{\prime}}\,\pi_{k,r}^{(k^{\prime})}(t_{n+r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}=k}^{K}a_{k^{\prime},r}\,\Delta t^{k^{\prime}}\,\pi_{k,r}^{(k^{\prime})}(t_{n+r})=\sum_{k^{\prime}=k}^{K}a_{k^{\prime},r}\,\Delta t^{k^{\prime}-k}\,b_{k^{\prime}-k,r},

where for
k^{\prime}\geq k
we have

b_{k^{\prime}-k,r}=\left[\prod_{s\neq r}\left(\frac{t-t_{n+s}}{\Delta t}\right)^{K+1}\right]^{(k^{\prime}-k)}_{\boldsymbol{\big|t=t_{n+r}}}.

Notice that for
k=k^{\prime}
, we obtain

b_{0,r}=\left[\prod_{s\neq r}\left(\frac{t-t_{n+s}}{\Delta t}\right)^{K+1}\right]_{\boldsymbol{\big|t=t_{n+r}}}=\prod_{s\neq r}\left(r-s\right)^{K+1}\neq 0.

The rest of the proof is achieved by induction. Let
r\in{\in\llbracket{0,R}\rrbracket}
. For
k=K
, we just have

0=E({\boldsymbol{a}};\tau_{K,r})=a_{K,r}b_{0,r};

hence
a_{K,r}=0
. Now, take
k=K-1
, then we have

0=E({\boldsymbol{a}};\tau_{{K-1},r})=a_{K,r}\,\Delta t\,b_{1,r}+a_{K-1,r}b_{0,r}=a_{K-1,r}b_{0,r}.

Therefore
a_{K-1,r}=0
. Similarly, we prove successively that
a_{K-2,r}=0
,
a_{K-3,r}=0
until
a_{0,r}=0
. In conclusion, all the coefficients of
{\boldsymbol{a}}
are null, so the set of functions is resolvent and the associated matrix
\mathcal{M}
is non-singular.
∎

In particular, the set of polynomials
\pi_{m}(t;t_{n})
is also a basis of
\mathbb{P}_{M-1}
, therefore, due to the linearity of the functional regarding the functions
\pi
, it is also resolvent, thus matrix
\mathcal{M}
is non-singular.

2.2.2
Choice of the structural equations

A first observation is that we do not have uniqueness of the set of
S
structural equations. Particularly, the conditioning of the whole system (the coupling of physical and structural equations) may depend on a wise choice of the basis,
i.e.
the choice of the coefficients of the structural equations. We list several methods to produce the
S
basis vectors.

I. Analytic method.

One can derive analytic expression of the coefficients from the system
E({\boldsymbol{a}};\pi_{m})=0
,
m=1,\ldots,M-S
using symbolic computation, assuming a uniform time subdivision. Such relations avoid the computation of matrix
\mathcal{M}
and are mandatory for the stability analysis. We report in annexe
A
sets of structural equations
{{\texttt{SE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{K}\,{R}}}}\raisebox{5.0pt}{{{\hskip-11.54994pt{S} }}}\thinspace}(s)
for useful values of
K
and
R
.

Remark
:

The analytical expressions of vectors
({\boldsymbol{a}}^{s})_{s=1}^{S}
given in annexe
A
do not necessarily correspond to an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{K}^{S}
, that is we do not have
\langle{\boldsymbol{a}}^{s},{\boldsymbol{a}}^{m}\rangle=\delta_{sm}
.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

II. Numerical method.
For larger value of
K
and
R
or when dealing with a non-uniform grid, we compute an orthogonal basis of the kernel
\mathcal{K}^{S}
using the complete SVD decomposition

\mathcal{M}[1:M-S,1:M]=U\Sigma V^{\textrm{\rm t}}

with
U
a rectangular
(M-S)\times M
matrix,
\Sigma
the diagonal
M\times M
matrix and
V^{\textrm{\rm t}}
the square orthogonal
M\times M
. Since the
S
last diagonal entries of
\Sigma
are null, we deduce that the
S
last column vectors of
V
define an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{K}^{S}
.

III. Hierarchical basis.
We want that the first vector
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{1}
provides the larger order of consistency error. The second vector
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{2}
reduces the consistency order of one and so forth. To provide such a property, we propose an algorithm to set up a numerical hierarchical basis of kernel
\mathcal{K}^{S}
.

1.

We first compute the sole vector
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{1}
of the kernel
\mathcal{K}^{1}
.

2.

We then determine a basis of the kernel
\mathcal{K}^{2}
. Noting that
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{1}
automatically belongs to
\mathcal{K}^{2}
, we determine a normalized vector
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{2}\in\mathcal{K}^{2}
orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}^{1}
using an orthogonalization procedure. Moreover, the consistency error associated to
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{2}
of one order lower than
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{1}
since we reduce the number of constraints
E({\boldsymbol{a}},\pi^{m})=0
.

3.

For a given stage
S^{\prime}<S
, let assume that the set of vectors
\displaystyle\left({\boldsymbol{a}}^{s}\right)_{s=1}^{s=S^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{K}^{S^{\prime}}
is orthogonal. We determine the kernel
\mathcal{K}^{S^{\prime}+1}
and extract a new orthogonal vector
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{S^{\prime}+1}\in\mathcal{K}^{S^{\prime}+1}
orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}^{S^{\prime}}
. By construction,
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{S^{\prime}+1}
provide a consistency error lower than one order regarding the other vectors.

We end the construction when we reach
S^{\prime}=S
and
\{{\boldsymbol{a}}^{1},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{a}}^{S}\}
is a hierarchical basis for
\mathcal{K}^{S}
.

2.2.3
Equivalent set of structural equations

As we pointed out in the previous section, one can determine different representations/bases of the kernel
\mathcal{K}^{S}
, that is, different set of structural equations. The question arises about the impact of the basis choice. Indeed, we can consider two different bases
\big\{{\boldsymbol{a}}^{s},s{\in\llbracket{1,S}\rrbracket}\big\}
and
\big\{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{a}}}^{s},s{\in\llbracket{1,S}\rrbracket}\big\}
of
\mathcal{K}^{S}
from which we build two different sets of structural equations. Consequently, there exists a change of basis given by a non-singular
S\times S
matrix
\Xi=[\xi_{ij}]_{S\times S}
such as
\widetilde{A}=\Xi A
, where

\widetilde{A}=\left[\big(\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{a}}}^{s}\big)^{\textrm{\rm t}}\right],\quad A=\left[\big({\boldsymbol{a}}^{s}\big)^{\textrm{\rm t}}\right]

(6)

are two
S\times M
matrices.
Conversely, any non-singular matrix
\Xi
transforms a basis of
\mathcal{K}^{S}
into another basis of
\mathcal{K}^{S}
.
As we shall prove in the sequel, the choice of the basis has no impact on the stability or condition number of the whole system (combining physical and structural equations).

Remark
:

By construction, any set of structural equations provides the same global consistency error order
M-S
since the equations are exact for any
\pi^{m}
,
m{\in\llbracket{1,M-S}\rrbracket}
.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

2.3 Schemes design

To build a complete numerical scheme to solve a first-order ODE equation, we have to determine the set of physical and structural equations. Several (meta)parameters control the complexity and accuracy of the scheme. Primarily, the user has to choose the number of physical equations by node. It definitively fixes the value of
K
. Then the second choice concerns the size of the block
R
.

2.3.1
Notations

Given
K

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
B(M_{0})=[-M_{0},M_{0}]
the closed ball of radius
M_{0}>0
centred at the origin and assume that the function
(z,t)\to f(z,t)
is a
C^{K+1}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])
function. Then, for any
k{\in\llbracket{1,K}\rrbracket}
, the functions
(t,z_{0},\ldots,z_{k-1})\to z_{k}={\texttt{df}^{(k-1)}\big[{t,z_{0},\ldots,z_{k-1}}\big]}
are bounded by a constant
M_{k}
,
and they are
L_{k}
uniformly Lipschitz in time on the compact set
B(M_{0})\times\cdots\times B(M_{k-1})
, with

|z_{k}-z_{k}^{\prime}|\leqslant L_{k}\left(\sum_{\kappa=0}^{k-1}|z_{\kappa}-z_{\kappa}^{\prime}|\right).

(7)

Proof.

We prove the property by induction. Since
(t,z_{0})
belongs to the compact set
[0,T]\times B(M_{0})
, continuity of
f
yields that
z_{1}={\texttt{df}^{(0)}\big[{t,z_{0}}\big]}
is bounded and belong to a compact set we include inside a closed ball
B(M_{1})
centred at the origin for
M_{1}
large enough. On the other hand, for two values
z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}\in B(M_{0})
, one has

{\texttt{df}^{(0)}\big[{t,z_{0}}\big]}-{\texttt{df}^{(0)}\big[{t,z_{0}^{\prime}}\big]}=f(t,z_{0})-f(t,z_{0}^{\prime})=\partial_{z}f\big(t,\theta z_{0}+(1-\theta)z_{0}^{\prime}\big)(z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime})

with
\theta\in[0,1]
. Hence, the intermediate point belongs to the ball
\overline{B}(M_{0})
. Denoting

L_{1}=\max_{\zeta\in B(M_{0})\atop t\in[0,T]}\partial_{z}f(\zeta,t),

we provide the uniform in time Lipschitz property

|{\texttt{df}^{(0)}\big[{t,z_{0}}\big]}-{\texttt{df}^{(0)}\big[{t,z_{0}^{\prime}}\big]}|\leqslant L_{1}|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|.

Assume that the properties hold for
k<K
, we prove the properties for
k+1
. First, we recall that
z_{k+1}={\texttt{df}^{(k)}\big[{t,z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}}\big]}
is constituted of the sum of polynomial functions with respect to variables
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}
multiplied with partial derivatives of
f
in
t
and
z_{0}
of order lower than
k
. Moreover, such partial derivatives only depend on
t
and
z_{0}
. Hence the partial derivatives are bounded in
[0,T]\times B(M_{0})
while the polynomials in
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}
are also bounded from the induction assumption. We then obtain that
z_{k+1}
is bounded and belongs to a closed ball
B(M_{k+1})
with
M_{k+1}
large enough.

At last, Lipschitz property derives from the fact that
{\texttt{df}^{(k)}\big[{t,z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}}\big]}
is polynomial in
z_{1}
up to
z_{k}
with bounded values, while the
z
-derivative of the partial derivatives of order
k
remain bounded in
B(M_{0})\times[0,T]
. Therefore we have a Lipschitz operator uniform in time and the property holds with a Lipschitz constant
L_{k+1}
.
∎

From Lemma
2.2
, we deduce a Lipschitz property regarding the sole variable
z_{0}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
2.2
, let
z_{0}\in B(M_{0})
,
t\in[0,T]
and define by induction

z_{k}={\texttt{df}^{(k-1)}\big[{t,z_{0},\cdots,z_{k-1}}\big]},\quad k{\in\llbracket{1,K}\rrbracket}.

Then, for any
k{\in\llbracket{1,K}\rrbracket}
and

, we have the inequalities

|z_{k}-z_{k}^{\prime}|\leqslant\widehat{L}_{k}|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|,

(8)

with
\widehat{L}_{k}
independent of
z_{0}
,
z_{0}^{\prime}
and time
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

For
k=1
we take
\widehat{L}_{1}=L_{1}
. For

, we note

|z_{2}-z_{2}^{\prime}|\leqslant L_{2}(|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|+|z_{1}-z_{1}^{\prime}|)\leqslant L_{2}(1+\widehat{L}_{1})|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|,

and the inequality holds with
\widehat{L}_{2}=L_{2}(1+\widehat{L}_{1})
.

The general case is proven by induction. From inequality (

) we have

|z_{k}-z_{k}^{\prime}|\leqslant L_{k}\left(|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|+\sum_{\kappa=1}^{k-1}|z_{\kappa}-z_{\kappa}^{\prime}|\right)\leqslant L_{k}\left(|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|+\sum_{\kappa=1}^{k-1}\widehat{L}_{\kappa}|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|\right)\leqslant\widehat{L}_{k}|z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}|

with

\widehat{L}_{k}=L_{k}\left(1+\sum_{\kappa=1}^{k-1}\widehat{L}_{\kappa}\right),

and inequality (
8
) holds.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\|.\|=\|.\|_{\infty}
be the maximum norm and assume that

\Phi_{n}^{(k)}={\texttt{df}^{(k-1)}\big[{T_{n},\Phi_{n}^{(0)},\cdots,\Phi_{n}^{(k-1)}}\big]},\quad k{\in\llbracket{1,K}\rrbracket}.

Proof.

There exists

such that

and applying inequality (

\displaystyle\big\|\Phi_{n}^{(k)}-{\Phi^{\prime}}_{n}^{(k)}\big\|

\displaystyle=

Let assume that we knew approximations of the function and derivative

we rewrite under the form

\sum_{r=1}^{R}a_{0,r}^{s}\phi^{(0)}_{n+r}+\Delta t\sum_{r=0}^{R}a_{1,r}^{s}\phi^{(1)}_{n+r}=\phi^{(0)}_{n}\sum_{r=1}^{R}a_{0,r}^{s},\quad s{\in\llbracket{1,R}\rrbracket}.

the

the

{\boldsymbol{1}\hskip-5.0pt\boldsymbol{1}}

and intermediate uniform sub-steps

Remark

An important property about our construction is that the intermediate steps provide

The approximations

.

Adopting a columns’ vectors expression of matrix

\Big(A^{(0)}\Big)^{-1}\widehat{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0}=-{\boldsymbol{1}\hskip-5.0pt\boldsymbol{1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{R}.

(13)

Proof.

The structural equations are exact for the constant polynomial function

4  Linear stability

4.1 Linear stability condition

To study the linear stability of these schemes we apply the von Neumann stability analysis. This involves assessing the growth factor of Fourier modes and ensuring that the magnitude of this growth factor does not exceed 1 for each time step. The analysis is based on the solution of the simple linear ordinary equation
\phi^{(1)}=\lambda\phi^{(0)}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. The first physical equation
PE
1
then reads
\phi_{n+r}^{(1)}=\lambda\phi_{n+r}^{(0)}
for all points of the
R
-block. Generalizing to the
k

PE
k

\phi^{(k)}=\lambda^{k}\phi^{(0)}
and then
\phi_{n+r}^{(k)}=\lambda^{k}\phi_{n+r}^{(0)}

Substituting
\phi^{(k)}_{n+r}
with
\lambda^{k}\phi^{(0)}_{n+r}
,
r{\in\llbracket{,R}\rrbracket}
in the
R

\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{r=0}^{R}a_{k,r}^{s}(\lambda\Delta t)^{k}\phi^{(0)}_{n+r}=0,\quad s{\in\llbracket{1,R}\rrbracket},

(19)

we rewrite with
z=\lambda\Delta t

\sum_{r=1}^{R}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K}a_{k,r}^{s}z^{k}\right)\phi^{(0)}_{n+r}=-\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K}a_{k,0}^{s}z^{k}\right)\phi^{(0)}_{n},\quad s{\in\llbracket{1,R}\rrbracket}.

Let

g_{sr}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}a_{k,r}^{s}z^{k},\quad G(z)=\Big(g_{sr}(z)\Big)_{s,r=1}^{R},\quad b(z)=\Big(g_{s0}(z)\Big)_{s=1}^{R},

Stability of the numerical solution requires that
|\chi_{r}(z)|\leqslant 1
for any
r{\in\llbracket{1,R}\rrbracket}
, but only
\chi_{R}(z)
is of interest since we use vector
\Phi_{n+R}
to compute the next
R
-block. Hence we focus the study on the amplification function
\chi_{R}(z)
derived from the system (
20
) to define the notion of
A
-Stability.

Definition 4.1
:

The scheme is A-stable if
|\chi_{R}(z)|\leqslant 1
.

Since the choice of the set of
S
structural equations is not unique, we prove that the stability condition is independent of the basis of the kernel.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A=[a^{s}]_{R\times M}
and
\widetilde{A}=[\widetilde{a}^{s}]_{S\times M}
be two basis of the Kernel
\mathcal{K}^{R}
given by (
6
). Then we have

{G}(z)\Phi_{n}^{(0)}=-{b}(z)\phi_{n}^{(0)},\qquad\widetilde{G}(z)\Phi_{n}^{(0)}=-\widetilde{b}(z)\phi_{n}^{(0)}

and the amplification vector
\chi(z)
is independent of the basis.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{A}
and
A
be two different kernel bases, as in (
6
). We have
\widetilde{A}=\Xi A
where
\Xi
is a nonsingular
R\times R
matrix that changes the basis, that is
\widetilde{G}=\Xi G
and
\widetilde{b}=\Xi b
. From the system (
20
) we have
\Phi_{n}^{(0)}=\chi(z)\phi_{n}^{(0)}
. On the other hand, using the alternative basis provides the relation
\widetilde{G}(z)\Phi_{n}^{(0)}=-\widetilde{b}(z)\phi_{n}^{(0)}
and

\widetilde{\chi}(z)=-\widetilde{G}^{-1}(z)\widetilde{b}(z)=-\Big(\Xi G(z)\Big)^{-1}\Xi b(x)=-G^{-1}(z)\big(\Xi^{-1}\Xi\big)b(x)=\chi(z).

Hence
\chi=\widetilde{\chi}
, and the amplification functions
\chi_{R}
do not depend on the choice of the kernel basis. In particular, the stability condition
|\chi_{R}(z)|\leqslant 1
of the approximation at time
t_{n+R}
is independent of the choice of the structural equations.
∎

In order to study the stability, we determine a new expression of the amplification function.

Proposition 4.2
.

The amplification coefficient for time
t_{n+R}
is given by

\chi_{R}(z)=\frac{\det(G(z)({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})-b(z)e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})}{\det(G(z))}

(21)

where
Id
is the identity matrix of order
R
and
e_{R}
the last canonic column vector.
In particular,
\chi_{R}(z)
is a rational function.

Proof.

We apply Cramer’s rule on the system (
20
) to obtain the last column of unknowns. Consider the triangular superior matrix
{\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}+\Phi_{n}^{(0)}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}
, which is the identity matrix with the column
R
replaced by the vector
\Phi_{n}^{(0)}
. Multiplying on the left by
G
, we have

G({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}+\Phi_{n}^{(0)}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})=G({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})-b\phi_{n}^{(0)}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}.

On the one hand,
\det\big({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}+\Phi_{n}^{(0)}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}\big)=\phi_{n+R}^{(0)}
, and we get

\det\big(G({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}+\Phi_{n}^{(0)}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})\big)=\det(G)\ \phi_{n+R}^{(0)}.

Algebraic manipulations provides the expression

\det\big(G({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}+\Phi_{n}^{(0)}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})\big)=\det\big(G({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})-b\phi_{n}^{(0)}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}\big)=\det\big(G({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})-be_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}\big)\ \phi_{n}^{(0)}.

We then obtain

\phi_{n+R}^{(0)}(z)=\frac{\det\big(G(z)({\textrm{\rm Id}}-e_{R}e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}})-b(z)e_{R}^{\textrm{\rm t}}\big)}{\det\big(G(z)\big)}\phi_{n}^{(0)};

thus relation (
21
) holds. Since the entries of the matrix
G
and vector
b
are polynomials of
z
of degree
K
with real coefficients, the amplification function
\chi_{R}
is a rational function with real coefficients.
∎

4.2 Criteria for the unconditional stability

We develop several tools that enable to analyse the stability of the
Skm[K,R]
scheme. The methodology lies on the assumption that the transfer function reads as
\displaystyle\chi_{R}(z)=\frac{p(z)}{p(-z)}
where
p(z)
is a polynomial function. Then using the Routh-Hurwitz criterion
[
17
]
, we show that roots of polynomial
p(z)
have a negative real part from which we deduce the
A
-stability.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that the transfer function associated to scheme
Skm[K,R]
is given by
\displaystyle\chi_{R}(z)=\frac{p(z)}{p(-z)}
where
p(z)
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{C}
which all roots have a negative real part. Then the scheme
Skm[K,R]
is A-stable.

Proof.

If
p(z)
has all its roots in the left-half complex plane, then
p(-z)
has all its roots in the right-half complex plane and
\chi_{R}(z)
is analytic in the left-half complex plane.

By the Maximum Modulus Theorem, we have that the maximum value of
|\chi_{R}|
occurs on the boundary of the semicircular region bounded by the imaginary
axis from
-\rho
to
\rho
and the semicircle
z=\rho e^{$$\theta}
,
\theta\in[\pi/2,3\pi/2]
.
Since
\displaystyle\chi_{R}(z)=\frac{p(z)}{p(-z)}
, then for any
\theta\in[\pi/2,3\pi/2]
,
\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to+\infty}|\chi_{R}(z)|=1
.

On the other hand, for imaginary number
z=$$y
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\displaystyle|\chi_{R}($$y)|=\left|\frac{p($$y)}{p(-$$y)}\right|=1
. We then conclude that
\displaystyle|\chi_{R}(z)|\leqslant 1
for
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits{z}\leqslant 0
.
∎

From lemma (
4.3
), the unconditional stability holds if: (1)
\displaystyle\chi_{R}(z)=\frac{p(z)}{p(-z)}
and, (2) all zeros of
p(z)
are
in the left complex half-plane. For the latter condition, we shall prove that
p(z)
is a Hurwitz stable polynomial
[
18
]
, we can assess by applying the Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion,
[
18
,
19
]
.

Definition 4.2
:

Let

p(z)=a_{I}z^{I}+a_{I-1}z^{I-1}+\cdots+a_{1}z+a_{0}

(22)

be a polynomial with real coefficients. The
I\times I
matrix

H=\begin{bmatrix}a_{I-1}&a_{I-3}&a_{I-5}&a_{I-7}&a_{I-9}&\cdots&0\\
a_{I}&a_{I-2}&a_{I-4}&a_{I-6}&a_{I-8}&\cdots&0\\
0&a_{I-1}&a_{I-3}&a_{I-5}&a_{I-7}&\cdots&0\\
0&a_{I}&a_{I-2}&a_{I-4}&a_{I-6}&\cdots&0\\
0&0&a_{I-1}&a_{I-3}&a_{I-5}&\cdots&0\\
0&0&a_{I}&a_{I-2}&a_{I-4}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&a_{0}\end{bmatrix}

is called the Hurwitz matrix associated with
p(z)
.

Definition 4.3
:

The polynomial
p(z)
is called Hurwitz polynomial if
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits{z_{i}}<0
, for

, where

are the roots of

.

The Routh-Hurwitz criterion is given in the following theorem (see
[
17
]
).

Theorem 4.4
.

The polynomial
p(z)
given in (
22
) is a Hurwitz polynomial if and only if each principal minor of
\det H
is strictly positive, that is,
m_{i}>0,
for
i{\in\llbracket{1,I}\rrbracket}
where

m_{i}=\begin{vmatrix}a_{I-1}&a_{I-3}&a_{I-5}&\cdots&0\\
a_{I}&a_{I-2}&a_{I-4}&\cdots&0\\
0&a_{I-1}&a_{I-3}&\cdots&0\\
0&a_{I}&a_{I-2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&a_{I-i}\end{vmatrix}.

From Lemma
4.3
and the definition of a Hurwitz polynomial we deduce the following theorem.

Theorem 4.5
.

If
\displaystyle\chi_{R}(z)=\frac{p(z)}{p(-z)}
is the transfer function associated to
Skm[K,R]
, where
p(z)
is a Hurwitz polynomial in
\mathbb{C}
, then the scheme
Skm[K,R]
is A-stable.

We apply the theorem to check the A-stability of several schemes

using the computation of the

function given in Tables

,

and

in

and the verification of the Routh-Hurwitz criterion given in

.
The

, where

is a real polynomial where all their coefficients are positive, see Tables

,

and

Corollary 4.6
.

We have that schemes

5  Spectral resolution

Fourier analysis is the standard tool to assess the spectral resolution of a compact method. The pioneer paper of Lele
[
20
]
provides an explicit relation for the first and second derivative approximations with respect to the local wave number
\omega=2\pi\kappa\Delta t
. The main ingredient is the phase deviation due to the numerical scheme
is independent of the position
. Indeed, the first derivatives are connected to the zero derivatives thanks to a linear relation

\sum_{r=1}^{R}a^{0}_{r}\phi^{(0)}_{r}+\sum_{r=1}^{R}a^{1}_{r}\phi^{(1)}_{r}=0.

Thus introducing the local modified wave number
\omega^{(1)}
for the first derivative, we derive an algebraic expression with respect to
\omega
. We proceed similarly for the second derivative since the Lele scheme connect
\phi^{(2)}_{r}
to
\phi^{(0)}_{r}
with a linear relation of type

\sum_{r=1}^{R}b^{0}_{r}\phi^{(0)}_{r}+\sum_{r=1}^{R}b^{2}_{r}\phi^{(2)}_{r}=0

that provides an explicit algebraic relation of
\omega^{(2)}
in function of
\omega
. Extension to the
k
-derivative is based on an implicit coupling of
\phi^{(k)}_{r}
with
\phi^{(0)}_{r}

[
21
]
. Once again the spectral analysis using the von-Neumann analysis is available since we solely connect the function to the
k
-derivative.

The Compact Combined Scheme
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
uses the same approach but with a slightly modification. Indeed CCS scheme combined both the first- and second-derivative at the same time but with two linearly independent equations leading to a small
2\times 2
system that provides the modified wave numbers
\omega^{(1)}
and
\omega^{(2)}
for the first- and second-derivative in function of
\omega
.

5.1 A new notion of spectral resolution

Such an approach is inadequate for structural equation. First, we are interested in assessing the impact of the structural scheme for a whole R-block, and in particular to the last time step
t_{n+R}
regarding to the initial
t_{n}
. Second, if
K\neq R
we do not have a square non singular linear system that provides the solution for
\omega^{(1)}(\omega)
up to
\omega^{(K)}(\omega)
. At last, Fourier analyse assumes we are dealing with a stationary solution where the phase shift of the first and second derivative is the same whatever the time step
t_{n}
which is not the case when dealing with an initial condition problem where we prescribe the exact for all the derivatives.

We then propose a new approach to assess the spectral resolution that takes into account the specificities of the structural scheme
Skm[K,R]
that is, the coupling between Structural Equations and Physical Equations. To this end, we substitute the fundamental solution
\exp($$2\pi\kappa t)
by the equation
\phi^{\prime}=$$2\pi\kappa\phi
. Then, we connect all the derivatives to the solution thanks to the physical equations

\phi^{(k)}=\Big($$2\pi\kappa\Big)^{k}\phi^{(0)},\ k{\in\llbracket{1,K}\rrbracket}.

Therefore, the
R
structural equations provide the linear system of size
R
already define in the stability section
4
by relation (
20
)

G(z)\Phi_{n}^{(0)}=-b(z)\phi_{n}^{(0)},

for the particular case
z=\lambda\Delta t
with
\lambda=$$2\pi\kappa
.
To assess the diffusion and dispersion of schemes
Skm[K,R]
, we assume that the initial condition at
t_{n}
is equal to the exact solution
\phi^{(0)}_{n}=\exp($$2\pi\kappa t_{n})

The transfer function then reads
\chi_{r}($$\omega)
providing the diffusion and the dispersion of the solution at time
t_{n+r}
. Since the exact solution at time
t_{n+r}
is
\phi^{(0)}(t_{n+r})=\exp($$r\omega)\exp($$2\pi\kappa t_{n})
, the deviation regarding the exact solution corresponds to the ratio between the approximation and the exact solution at time
t_{n+r}

\frac{\phi_{n+r}^{(0)}}{\phi^{(0)}(t_{n+r})}=\frac{\chi_{r}($$\omega)\phi_{n}^{(0)}}{\exp($$r\omega)\exp($$2\pi\kappa t_{n})}=\chi_{r}($$\omega)\exp(-$$r\omega).

Definition 5.1
:

The scheme diffusion is given by the modulus of
\zeta_{r}(\omega)=\chi_{r}($$\omega)\exp(-$$r\omega)
(amplification factor) while the dispersion corresponds to its argument (phase deviation).

Remark
:

It is important to note that the
\zeta
function also depends the parameters
R
and
K
thus we employ the notation
\zeta_{r}(\omega;K,R)
when necessary (see section
5.4
for instance).

Noting that the solution at time
t_{n+R}
will be used for the next block resolution, we focus our study on the case

with
\chi_{R}($$\omega)
. Of course, all the intermediate step can be analysed similarly.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
\chi_{R}(z)
is a rational functions of the form

then we have
|\chi_{R}($$\omega)|=1
whence
|\zeta_{R}(\omega)|=1
for all

.

6  Post-processing

A simulation based on
Skm[K,R]
method provide approximations of
\phi^{(k)}_{N}\approx\phi^{(k)}(t_{N})
,
k{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket}
at the final time, with order
d=K(R+1)
. Consider
p>K
, one easily provide approximations of higher derivative
\phi_{N}^{(p)}
by using additional physical equations
{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{K+1}}}}},\cdots{{\texttt{PE}\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{{\,{p}}}}}
. Nevertheless, such new physical equations may not be available due to the complexity of the partial derivative or some part of the physical equation is driven by tabulated functions. Our goal is to provide an approximation
\phi_{N}^{(p)}\approx\phi^{(p)}(t_{N})
with the best precision using a new type of structural equations (the post-processing structural equations) that take the form

\phi_{N}^{(p)}=\sum_{i=0}^{I_{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}a^{p}_{ik}\,\Delta t^{k}\,\phi_{N-i}^{(k)},

where
I_{p}
is the number of backward time steps we need to obtain a consistency order of
d
.

To determine the
(I_{p}+1)\times(K+1)
matrix
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{p}=\big(a^{p}_{ik}\big)_{i=0\cdots,I_{p}\atop k=0\cdots,K}
, we introduce the functional

E_{p}({\boldsymbol{a}}^{p};\pi)=\sum_{i=0}^{I_{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}a^{p}_{ik}\,\Delta t^{k}\,\pi^{(k)}(t_{N-i})-\pi^{(p)}(t_{N}),

for any function
\pi=\pi(t)
, regular enough.

We reshape matrix
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{p}
into a one-index entries vector
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{p}
(we use the same notation for the sake of simplicity) with the one-to one mapping
(i,k)\to\ell=\ell(i,k)=1+k(I_{p}+1)+i
from
{\in\llbracket{0,K}\rrbracket}\times{\in\llbracket{0,I_{p}}\rrbracket}
onto
{\in\llbracket{1,M}\rrbracket}
, with
M=(K+1)(I_{p}+1)
and the cost function now reads

E_{p}({\boldsymbol{a}}^{p};\pi)=\sum_{i=0}^{I_{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}a^{p}_{\ell}\,\Delta t^{k}\,\pi^{(k)}(t_{N-i})-\pi^{(p)}(t_{N}),\qquad\ell=\ell(i,k).

To provide the accuracy, we impose the functional to exactly satisfies the relation
E({\boldsymbol{a}};\pi_{m})=0
for polynomial function up to a certain degree. To this end, we use the list of polynomial functions

\pi_{m}(t;t_{N})=\left(\frac{t-t_{N}}{\Delta t}\right)^{m-1},\ m{\in\llbracket{1,\overline{M}}\rrbracket},\ \overline{M}=p+d.

Note that the number of coefficients is
M
and we need to impose the condition
\overline{M}\leq M
such that the linear system have at least one solution. We then deduce a sufficient condition about the number of backward time steps
I_{p}

p+K(R+1)-K-1\leqslant I_{p}(K+1)\implies I_{p}\geqslant\frac{KR+p-1}{K+1}.

Remark
:

For
K=1
and
R=2
, we have
I_{p}\geqslant\frac{1+p}{2}
while
K=3
or
R=4
require
I_{p}\geqslant\frac{11+p}{4}
.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

To compute the coefficients of matrix
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{p}
, one has to find a solution of the linear system
\mathcal{M}^{p}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{p}={\boldsymbol{b}}^{p}
with
\mathcal{M}^{p}
an
\overline{M}\times M
matrix, we seek non-trivial vectors
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{p}
as a particular solution of the system plus any vector of the kernel of
\mathcal{M}^{p}
.

To compute the entries of matrix
\mathcal{M}^{p}
, the
k
-derivative of the polynomial function
\pi_{m}(t;t_{N})
,
m{\in\llbracket{0,\overline{M}}\rrbracket}
reads

\pi_{m}^{(k)}(t;t_{N})=\frac{(m-1)\cdots(m-k)}{\Delta t^{k}}\left(\frac{t-t_{N}}{\Delta t}\right)^{m-1-k},\ k<m,

and

\pi_{m}^{(k)}(t;t_{N})=0,\ k\geq m.

After calculation, one has for
k<m
and
i{\in\llbracket{0,I_{p}}\rrbracket}

\pi_{m}^{(k)}(t_{N-i};t_{N})=\frac{(m-1)\cdots(m-k)}{\Delta t^{k}}(-i)^{m-1-k}.

Then, the entries of matrix
\mathcal{M}^{p}
are given by

\mathcal{M}^{p}[m,\ell]=C^{m}_{\ell}(-i)^{m-1-\ell},\quad C^{m}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\frac{m!}{(m-\ell)!}&\text{if $m>\ell$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\quad\ell=\ell(i,k),

for
m{\in\llbracket{1,\overline{M}}\rrbracket}
and
\ell{\in\llbracket{1,M}\rrbracket}
. Several cases of post processing analytical formulas are given in
D
.

Remark
:

For larger value of
K
and
p
, or for non-uniform grid, matrix
\mathcal{M}^{p}
and right-hand side
{\boldsymbol{b}}^{p}
are still numerically computed via the condition
E_{p}({\boldsymbol{a}}^{p};\pi)
,
m{\in\llbracket{0,\overline{M}}\rrbracket}
. Therefore a solution is available via a least-square method that provide the post-processing structural equation.
\scriptstyle\blacksquare

\phi(t)=\exp(t)
we compute the difference between

and
\phi^{(p)}_{N}
where the expressions

are substituted by the exact values. We report in Tables

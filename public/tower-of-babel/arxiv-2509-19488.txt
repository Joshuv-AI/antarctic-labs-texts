Stability of high-order Scott-Vogelius elements for 2D non-Newtonian incompressible flow Thanks: This material is based upon work supported by the National Science Foundation under Award No.DMS-2201487 and by the Mary Wheeler Fellowship from the Ridgway Scott Foundation.
Charles Parker, Endre Süli

Abstract
. We consider the stability of high-order Scott-Vogelius elements for 2D non-Newtonian incompressible flow problems. For elements of degree 4 or higher, we construct a right-inverse of the divergence operator that is stable uniformly in the polynomial degree N N from L p L^{p} to 𝑾 1 , p \bm{W}^{1,p} , show that the associated inf-sup constant is bounded below by a constant that decays at worst like N − 3 ​ | 1 2 − 1 p | N^{-3\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|} , and construct local Fortin operators with stability constants explicit in the polynomial degree. We demonstrate these results with several numerical examples suggesting that the p p -version method can offer superior convergence rates over the h h -version method even in the non-Newtonian setting.

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]

of these models for incompressible fluids and the numerical analysis of

[

,

,

,

,

have received much attention recently. These finite element methods roughly
fall into two categories: those that produce exactly (pointwise)
divergence-free velocity approximations and those that do not. While many of
the standard classical methods fall into the latter category, exactly
divergence-free methods, typically in the Newtonian setting, have also been an
area of active research; see e.g.
[
41
,
47
]
for
reviews.

Exactly divergence-free finite element discretizations offer a number of
benefits over standard finite elements.
By construction, the numerical approximation more accurately respects
the underlying physics in the sense that mass is conserved pointwise.
Moreover, for Newtonian fluids, these schemes are pressure robust

[
41
]
, meaning that the velocity error is
independent of the pressure error and is quasi-optimal.
Properly quantifying pressure robustness in the non-Newtonian setting
and showing that pressure robust schemes for Newtonian fluids remain
pressure robust in non-Newtonian setting is an ongoing area of research.
Recent results
[
27
]
show that some particular divergence-free
finite element schemes based on the lowest-order Crouzeix-Raviart element are
also pressure robust for the
p
-Stokes equation.

Divergence-free elements offer an additional benefit in the non-Newtonian
setting related to the stabilization of convective terms. We
briefly summarize the discussion in
[
28
, §3.2 & 3.3]
. For
concreteness, consider the following
p
-Navier-Stokes problem for a power-law
fluid:

(1.1a)

\displaystyle-\dive\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})+\dive(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u})+\grad q

\displaystyle=\bm{f}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle-\dive\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, is a bounded, Lipschitz,
polyhedral domain,
\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{v}}
is the symmetric part of the
gradient of
\bm{v}
, and for fixed
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\mathbf{{S}}
is the
shear stress defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{A}}):=\nu|\mathbf{{A}}|^{p-2}\mathbf{{A}},\qquad\forall\mathbf{{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},

where
\nu>0
is constant.
For an appropriate choice of Sobolev spaces, problem
eq.

1.1

has a weak solution provided that
p>\frac{2d}{d+2}

[
15
]
.
Typically, the weak form of the convective term
\dive(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u})

is rewritten as

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{v},\grad\bm{u})-(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u},\grad\bm{v})\right],

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-inner product, to ensure
that discretizations inherit the skew-symmetry of the convective term (see
e.g.
[
57
]
).
For finite elements that do not deliver divergence-free velocities, the
trilinear form
eq.

1.3
is only bounded in the proper norms
uniformly in the discretization parameters provided that
p>\frac{2d}{d+1}
.
Consequently, existing results for weak convergence for these methods require

p>\frac{2d}{d+1}
, while for divergence-free elements,
one can prove weak convergence over the full range
p>\frac{2d}{d+2}
for
which the continuous problem is well-posed
[
28
, Theorem 19]
. See,
however,
[
10
]
, where weak convergence of a stabilized finite
element method has been shown for the full range of
p>\frac{2d}{d+2}
for
which weak solutions are known to exist. The
existence of fluids for which
p\in\left(\frac{2d}{d+2},\frac{2d}{d+1}\right]

in the heat transfer in mirco-structures
[
35
]
, shows that
the construction of methods that converge in the full range
p>\frac{2d}{d+2}
is of practical importance.

We focus on high-order 2D Scott-Vogelius elements
[
53
]
, which
deliver exactly exactly divergence-free velocities. In
particular, the velocity space consists of piecewise polynomials of degree
N\geq 1
and the pressure space is taken to be the divergence of this space:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{N}:=\{\bm{v}\in C(\bar{\Omega})^{2}:\bm{v}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(K)^{2}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\}\quad\text{and}\quad Q^{N-1}:=\dive\bm{V}^{N},

where
\mathcal{T}
is a triangulation of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\mathcal{P}_{N}(K)
denotes the space of polynomials of degree at most
N
. A
local characterization of
Q^{N-1}
is only available on
general meshes if
N\geq 4

[
53
,
59
]
, which is the case
we consider here. For meshes with
special structure, local characterizations are available for lower degrees

[
49
]
e.g. on barycentrically refined meshes for
N\geq 2

[
49
, Theorems 4.6.1 & 6.4.1]
.

The stability of the discretization hinges on the behavior of the inf-sup
constant

(1.5)

\displaystyle\beta_{N,p}:=\mathop{\inf\vphantom{\sup}}_{q\in Q^{N-1}}\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}}\frac{(\dive\bm{v},q)}{\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\Omega}\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}},

where
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
and
\|\cdot\|_{1,p,\Omega}
and
\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}
denote the standard norms on the
Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and Lebesgue space
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.
For the power-law fluid in
eq.

1.1
,
p
is the same as the
index in the shear stress
eq.

1.2
; for a general
non-Newtonian fluid, the value of
p
depends on the constitutive law. In
the Newtonian setting
(
p=2
),
\beta_{N,2}
is bounded below uniformly in the mesh size and
polynomial degree provided that
N\geq 4

[
6
]
. Under the same
condition, the Scott-Vogelius elements are also stable with respect to the
mesh size in the non-Newtonian setting:
\beta_{N,p}
is bounded below
uniformly in the mesh size for
p\in(1,\infty)

[
58
, Lemma A.4 & Lemma
A.11]
. However, the behavior of
\beta_{N,p}
with respect to the
polynomial degree
N
for
p\neq 2
has not previously been addressed for any
finite element pair.

In this work, we establish three novel results regarding the stability of
high-order Scott-Vogelius elements on general meshes in the non-Newtonian
setting. First, we show that for
N\geq 4
, there exists a right-inverse of
the divergence operator
\mathcal{L}_{\dive,N}:Q^{N-1}\to\bm{V}^{N}
that
is stable uniformly in the polynomial degree from
L^{p}(\Omega)
to

\bm{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Second, we show that the inf-sup constant

\beta_{N,p}
is bounded below by a constant the decays like
N^{-3\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}
. Third, we construct local Fortin
operators, which are ubiquitous in the numerical analysis of finite element
methods for non-Newtonian flows, with stability constants explicit in the
polynomial degree.
We believe these results to be the first with stability constants explicit
in the
polynomial degree for any finite element method for non-Newtonian flow, and
they are foundational in understanding the convergence behavior of
p
- and

hp
-finite element methods for non-Newtonian flows.

2.2.
Existing stability results

The inf-sup constant with boundary conditions is the following analogue of

eq.

1.5
:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}:=\mathop{\inf\vphantom{\sup}}_{q\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}}\frac{(\dive\bm{v},q)}{\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\Omega}\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}},\qquad p\in(1,\infty),

where we implicitly assume that
0
is always excluded from the infimums and
supremums. Since
\dive:\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}\to Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
is
surjective by construction,
\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}>0
for any
N\geq 1
and

p\in(1,\infty)
but may decay to 0 as the mesh is refined or as the
polynomial degree is raised.

In the Newtonian case (
p=2
), sharp lower and upper bounds for the inf-sup
constant
\beta_{N,2}
in
eq.

2.9
explicit in the polynomial
degree
are known for
N\geq 4
. Related to the algebraic constraint in

Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}

eq.

2.8
, we define the following geometric quantity at a

\bm{{a}}

\displaystyle\xi_{\bm{{a}}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{T}_{\bm{{a}}}|-1}|\sin(\theta_{i}+\theta_{i+1})|+\begin{cases}|\sin(\theta_{|\mathcal{T}_{\bm{{a}}}|}+\theta_{1})|&\text{if }\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{I},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that for
\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{I}\cup\mathcal{V}_{B,0}
,

if and only if
\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{S}
; i.e.

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{T}}:=\min_{\bm{{a}}\in(\mathcal{V}_{I}\cup\mathcal{V}_{B,0})\setminus\mathcal{V}_{S}}\xi_{\bm{{a}}}.

3.
Main results

We now turn to the three main results: a discrete right-inverse of the
divergence operator bounded uniformly in polynomial degree, a lower bound for
the inf-sup constant
\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}

eq.

2.9
that
decays at
worst like
N^{-3/2}
, and a local Fortin operator with continuity explicit in
polynomial degree. For constants appearing in estimates, we use the notation

C(r,s,\ldots)
to denote that, unless specified otherwise, the constant
depends only on
r,s,\ldots
.

3.1.
Right-inverse of the divergence operator

Our first result is the existence of a right-inverse of the divergence
operator for each
N\geq 4
mapping from
Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
to

\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}
that is
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-stable uniformly in polynomial
degree.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
N\geq 4
, there exists a linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{\dive,N}:Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}\to\bm{V}_{\Gamma}^{N}
satisfying the following for
all
q\in Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}
:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\dive\mathcal{L}_{\dive,N}q=q\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{L}_{\dive,N}q\|_{1,p,\Omega}\leq C(p)\xi_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\|q\|_{p,\Omega}\qquad\forall p\in(1,\infty),

where
C(p)
also depends on
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{0}
, and shape regularity.

The proof of
theorem

3.1
appears in
section

5.2.3
. In
the case
p=2
,
theorem

3.1
is equivalent to the inf-sup stability

eq.

2.11a
. As we shall see shortly, the case
p\neq 2

requires additional results.

3.2.
Inf-sup stability

An immediate consequence of
theorem

3.1
is the following lower
bound on the inf-sup constant
\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}

eq.

2.9
.

(3.2)

\displaystyle\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}=\mathop{\inf\vphantom{\sup}}_{q\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}}\frac{(\dive\bm{v},q)}{\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\Omega}\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}}\geq\frac{\xi_{\mathcal{T}}}{C(p)}\mathop{\inf\vphantom{\sup}}_{q\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}\sup_{r\in Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}}\frac{(q,r)}{\|r\|_{p,\Omega}\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}},

where
C(p)
is the same constant as in
eq.

3.1
. The final
term of the RHS involves a discrete analogue of the dual
L^{p}
norm, which we
denote by

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|q\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p^{\prime},Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}}:=\sup_{r\in Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}}\frac{(q,r)}{\|r\|_{p,\Omega}}\qquad\forall q\in Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}.

Of course, if the supremum above is taken over all of
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then
the above quantity is the
L^{p^{\prime}}
-norm of
q
. Since
\dive\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}=Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
and
\|\dive\bm{v}\|_{1,p}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p}
, this discrete dual norm also appears in an upper-bound
for
\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}
, as

\displaystyle\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}\leq\frac{1}{C}\mathop{\inf\vphantom{\sup}}_{q\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}}\frac{(\dive\bm{v},q)}{\|\dive\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\Omega}\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}}=\frac{1}{C}\mathop{\inf\vphantom{\sup}}_{q\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}\frac{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|q\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p^{\prime},Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}}}{\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}}.

In the case
p=2
, one has
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{2,Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}=\|\cdot\|_{2,\Omega}
, and so
\beta_{N,2}\geq C\xi_{\mathcal{T}}
, as
previously stated in
eq.

2.11a
. For
p\neq 2
, one
additionally needs to find the equivalence constants between
\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p^{\prime},Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}
on
Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
.
Suppose that the
L^{2}
-projection operator
\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
; that is

\displaystyle(\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}q,r)

\displaystyle=(q,r)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall r\in Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1},\ \forall q\in L^{1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}r\|_{p,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(N,p)\|r\|_{p,\Omega}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall r\in L^{p}(\Omega),

for some constant
\Delta(N,p)
. Then, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|q\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{p^{\prime},Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}}\geq\frac{(\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}(q|q|^{p^{\prime}-2}),q)}{\|\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}(q|q|^{p^{\prime}-2})\|_{p,\Omega}}\geq\frac{1}{\Delta(N,p)}\frac{\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}^{p^{\prime}}}{\|q|q|^{p^{\prime}-2}\|_{p,\Omega}}=\frac{1}{\Delta(N,p)}\|q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}.

Consequently, one route to proving inf-sup stability is to
establish the
L^{p}
-stability of the
L^{2}
-projection onto
Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}
,
which is the route we pursue.

To this end, we place the following two mild restrictions on the mesh

\mathcal{T}
.

(M1):

No two singular vertices share
an edge; i.e. if
\bm{{a}},\bm{b}\in\mathcal{V}_{S}
with
\bm{{a}}\neq\bm{b}
, then
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{{a}}}\cap\mathcal{T}_{\bm{b}}=\emptyset
.

(M2):

If
|\Gamma|=|\Gamma_{0}|
, then
no singular boundary vertex
\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{B,0}\cap\mathcal{V}_{S}
coincides with a corner of
\Omega
; i.e. if
\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{S}\cap\mathcal{V}_{B,0}
, then

|\mathcal{T}_{\bm{{a}}}|=2
.

As we will see in
section

6
below, the first
condition
(M1):
ensures that

\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}
can be decomposed into local projections that are
defined on an individual element or a single vertex patch, while

(M2):
ensures that
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{{a}}}(1)=0

for all
\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{S}
, which is helpful in dealing with the
zero-mean constraint in
Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}
when
|\partial\Omega|=|\Gamma_{0}|
. Under
these conditions, the operator norm of
\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}
grows at most
like
N^{3/2}
:

Theorem 3.2
.

If the mesh satisfies

(M1):
–
(M2):
and
N\geq 4
,
then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{N-1}^{Q}r\|_{p,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{3\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}\|r\|_{p,\Omega}\qquad\forall r\in L^{p}(\Omega),\ \forall p\in[1,\infty],

where

depends on shape regularity and if

, also
on
|\Omega|
. Consequently,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}\geq C(p)N^{-3\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}\xi_{\mathcal{T}}>0\qquad\forall p\in(1,\infty),

where

also depends on
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{0}
, and shape regularity
and

is defined in

2.9
.

appears in

In comparison with the case

3.3.
Fortin operators

Local Fortin operators are one of the key tools in most of the existing
analysis of finite element discretizations of non-Newtonain flows
(see e.g.
[
12
,
27
,
28
,
55
]
). As shown in

[
58
, Appendix A.2.2.2]
, one can
modify the construction of a right-inverse of the divergence operator for the
Scott-Vogelius elements to obtain a local Fortin operator. We proceed
similarly here by modifying the proofs of

theorems

3.1
and
3.2
to construct two local Fortin
operators: one with continuity properties explicit in the polynomial degree
that
is a projection only in the lowest order (
N=4
) case and another that is a
projection for any degree but with continuity constants implicit in the
polynomial degree.

For these operators, we will assume that the mesh contains no singular
vertices.

(M3):

The mesh contains no singular
vertices; i.e.
\mathcal{V}_{S}=\emptyset
.

Moreover, let
\mathcal{T}_{K}:=\cup_{\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\mathcal{T}_{\bm{{a}}}
, and
\omega_{K}:=\mathrm{int}(\cup_{L\in\mathcal{T}_{K}}\bar{L})
. Then, the existence of
the first Fortin operator is as follows.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that the mesh satisfies
(M3):
and let
N\geq 4
. There exists a linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{F,N}:\bm{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)\to\bm{V}^{N}
satisfying the following for
\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
:

(3.7a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\dive\mathcal{L}_{F,N}\bm{v})q\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\dive\bm{v})q\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in DG^{N-1}(\mathcal{T}),

(3.7b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{F,N}\bm{v}|_{\Gamma_{0}}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.7c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{F,N}\bm{v}

\displaystyle=\bm{v}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{4}.

Moreover, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, and
K\in\mathcal{T}
, there holds

(3.8a)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{F,N}\bm{v}\|_{p,K}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}C(p)\left(\|\bm{v}\|_{p,\omega_{K}}+h_{K}\xi_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}N^{3\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}|\bm{v}|_{1,p,\omega_{K}}\right)&\text{if }p\in(1,\infty),\\
C(\epsilon)\left(\|\bm{v}\|_{p,\omega_{K}}+h_{K}\xi_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}|\bm{v}|_{1,p,\omega_{K}}\right)&\text{if }p\in\{1,\infty\},\end{cases}

(3.8b)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{F,N}\bm{v}|_{1,p,K}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}C(p)\xi_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}N^{3\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}|\bm{v}|_{1,p,\omega_{K}}&\text{if }p\in(1,\infty),\\
C(\epsilon)\xi_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}N^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}|\bm{v}|_{1,p,\omega_{K}}&\text{if }p\in\{1,\infty\},\end{cases}

where
C(p)
and
C(\epsilon)
also depend on
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{0}
, and
shape regularity.

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{F,N}
is only a projection on
\bm{V}^{4}
, as opposed
to all of
\bm{V}^{N}
. Some of the existing theory (e.g.
[
12
, Remark
2.13]
) only
assumes that the Fortin operator is a projection on
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\Omega)
,
so
\mathcal{L}_{N,F}
is suitable to apply these results. Here,

(M3):
is crucial to ensure that
\|\mathcal{L}_{F,N}\bm{v}\|_{1,p,K}
depends only on
\bm{v}
in one patch of elements around

K
. In contrast, the estimates for the Fortin operator in
[
58
, Lemma
A.11]
depend on multiple layers of patches, as only

(M2):
is assumed.

By modifying one step in the construction of
\mathcal{L}_{F,N}
, we obtain a
local Fortin operator that is also a projection operator on all of

\bm{V}^{N}
, which is the more common assumption on the Fortin operator,
at the expense of implicit dependence on the polynomial degree in the
estimates.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that the mesh satisfies
(M3):
and let
N\geq 4
. There exists a linear operator
\mathcal{I}_{N,F}:\bm{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)\to\bm{V}^{N}
satisfying the following for
\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
:

(3.9a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\dive\mathcal{I}_{N,F}\bm{v})q\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\dive\bm{v})q\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in DG^{N-1}(\mathcal{T}),

(3.9b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{N,F}\bm{v}|_{\Gamma_{0}}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.9c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{N,F}\bm{v}

\displaystyle=\bm{v}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}.

Moreover, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, there holds

(3.10a)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}_{N,F}\bm{v}\|_{p,K}

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p)\left(\|\bm{v}\|_{p,\omega_{K}}+h_{K}\xi_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}|\bm{v}|_{1,p,\omega_{K}}\right)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall K\in\mathcal{T},

(3.10b)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{N,F}\bm{v}|_{1,p,K}

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p)\xi_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}|\bm{v}|_{1,p,\omega_{K}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall K\in\mathcal{T},

where
C(N,p)
also depends on
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{0}
, and shape
regularity.

In the case that
\Gamma_{0}=\partial\Omega
,
theorem

3.5
,
we recover
[
58
, Lemma A.11]
, albeit with a different underlying
construction. In the
next section, we apply the results for inf-sup
stability (
theorem

3.2
) and the existence of local Fortin
operators to obtain a priori error estimates for the
p
-Stokes problem.

4.
Application to the
p
-Stokes system

To demonstrate how our results may be applied to non-Newtonian flows, we
consider the
p
-Stokes problem with
p\in(1,\infty)
in weak form: Find

(\bm{u},q)\in\bm{W}^{1,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that

(4.1a)

\displaystyle(\nu\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}}),\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{v}})-(\dive\bm{v},q)

\displaystyle=L(\bm{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(4.1b)

\displaystyle-(\dive\bm{u},r)

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall r\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\Gamma}(\Omega).

where
\nu>0
is constant and
L
belongs to the dual space of

\bm{W}^{1,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. We
recall that
\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{v}}
is the symmetric part of the gradient of

\bm{v}
and the shear stress
\mathbf{{S}}
is defined in

eq.

1.2
. The Scott-Vogelius discretization of

eq.

4.1
then reads: Find

(\bm{u}_{N},q_{N-1})\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}\times Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
,
N\geq 4
, such that

(4.2a)

\displaystyle(\nu\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}}),\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{v}})-(\dive\bm{v},q_{N-1})

\displaystyle=L(\bm{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma},

(4.2b)

\displaystyle-(\dive\bm{u}_{N},r)

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall r\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}.

The proof of well-posedness of problems

eqs.

4.1
and
4.2
is
standard (see e.g.
[
11
, §2]
). We note that Korn’s inequality
gives the following stability estimate for
\bm{u}
and
\bm{u}_{N}
:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\nu\max\left\{\|\bm{u}\|_{1,p,\Omega},\|\bm{u}_{N}\|_{1,p,\Omega}\right\}^{p-1}\leq C(\Omega,\Gamma_{0})\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\frac{|L(\bm{v})|}{\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\Omega}}.

4.1.
Pressure-robust error estimates

We now proceed similarly as in
[
27
, §4]
to obtain pressure
robust error estimates. To this end, we introduce the auxiliary tensor

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{A}}):=\sqrt{|\mathbf{{A}}|^{p-2}}\mathbf{{A}}\qquad\forall\mathbf{{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},

which is used to define the “natural velocity distance”
[
12
]
for
the
p
-Stokes problem
\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{v}})\|_{2,\Omega}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\bm{u}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
q\in L_{\Gamma}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
denote the solution
eq.

4.1
and

\bm{u}_{N}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}
and
q_{N-1}\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
the
solution to
eq.

4.2
with
N\geq 4
. Then, there holds

(4.5a)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}})\|_{2,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}\\
\dive\bm{v}=0\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{v})\|_{2,\Omega}

and

(4.5b)

\displaystyle\|q-q_{N-1}\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}}\left(\nu\|\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N})\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}+\inf_{r\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}\|q-r\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\right),

where
\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}
is defined in
eq.

2.9
.
Moreover, there holds

(4.6)

\|q-q_{N-1}\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\\
\leq\frac{C(p,L)}{\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}}\left(\nu\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N})\|_{2,\Omega}^{\min\left\{1,\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\right\}}+\inf_{r\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}}\|q-r\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\right).

Proof.

Step 1:
eq.

4.5a
.

Note that choosing
\bm{w}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}
with
\dive\bm{w}=0

in
eqs.

4.1a
and
4.2a
gives

(4.7)

\displaystyle(\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N}),\mathbf{{D}}\bm{w})=0.

Now let
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}
with
\dive\bm{v}=0
. Applying
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha(p)\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}})\|_{2,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N}),\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}}-\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N}),\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}}-\mathbf{{D}}\bm{v}),

where we used
eq.

4.7
and that
\dive\bm{u}_{N}=0
by
eq.

4.2b
. Thanks to

[
12
, Lemma 2.7]
, there exists
C(p)
such that

|(\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N}),\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}}-\mathbf{{D}}\bm{v})|\\
\leq\frac{\alpha(p)}{2}\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N})\|_{2,\Omega}^{2}+C(p)\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{v})\|_{2,\Omega}^{2},

and inequality
eq.

4.5a
now follows from taking the
infimum over all such
\bm{v}
.

Step 2:
eq.

4.5b
.
Let
r\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
. The inf-sup condition
eq.

2.9
and

eqs.

4.1a
and
4.2a
give

\displaystyle\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}\|q_{N-1}-r\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}}\frac{-(\dive\bm{v},q_{N-1}-r)}{\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}^{N}_{\Gamma}}\frac{\nu(\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N}),\mathbf{{D}}\bm{v})-(\dive\bm{v},q-r)}{\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\nu\|\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N})\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}+\|q-r\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\right),

and inequality
eq.

4.5b
follows from the triangle
inequality and taking the infimum over all
r\in Q^{N-1}_{\Gamma}
.

Step 3:
eq.

4.6
.
Lemma 4.6
from
[
12
]
shows that if
p\in(2,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N})\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\leq C(p)\||\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}}|+|\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}}|\|_{p,\Omega}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}})\|_{2,\Omega},

while for

, there holds

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}\bm{u}_{N})\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\leq C(p)\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}})\|_{2,\Omega}^{\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}},

and so inequality
eq.

4.6
follows from

eq.

4.3
. ∎

Lemma

4.1
shows that when using the “natural velocity
distance,” one obtains a quasi-optimal best approximation of the velocity

eq.

4.5a
, which reduces to the pressure-robust error
estimates of
[
41
]
in the linear case (

). Note
that the pressure error estimate

also
involves the

quasi-optimal. In the linear case,
\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})

the nonlinear case,
\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})\neq\mathbf{{S}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})

\min\{1,2/p^{\prime}\}
exponent in
eq.

4.6
.

To obtain rates of convergence, we apply the approximation results from

[
12
]
. In particular, when the mesh contains no singular vertices

[

.

Suppose that
\mathcal{V}_{S}=\emptyset
and let
\bm{u}
,
q
,

\bm{u}_{N}
, and

If
\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})\in\mathbf{{W}}^{1,2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})-\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}_{N}})\|_{2,\Omega}

If moreover,

, then there holds

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|q-q_{N-1}\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\leq\frac{C(p,L)}{\beta_{N,p,\Gamma}}\left(\left(h\|\grad\mathbf{{F}}(\mathbf{{D}}{\bm{u}})\|_{2,\Omega}\right)^{\min\left\{1,\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\right\}}+h\|\grad q\|_{p^{\prime},\Omega}\right).

\dive\bm{v}\equiv 0\end{subarray}}C(\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p})\|\bm{u}-\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\Omega}^{2/p}&\text{if }p\in(2,\infty).\end{cases}

[

.

5.
Inverting the divergence operator

In this section, we will construct a right-inverse of
\dive:\bm{V}_{\Gamma}^{N}\to Q_{\Gamma}^{N-1}
whose stability is uniform in the
polynomial degree (
theorem

3.1
). The construction will proceed in
several steps in the proceeding sections. We first recall the following
inverse inequality from
[
24
]
(see also
[
30
, eq.
(5.3)]
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded with Lipschitz boundary
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For all
1\leq r\leq p\leq\infty
, there
holds

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|q\|_{p,U}\leq C(U,r,p)N^{2d\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|q\|_{r,U}\qquad\forall q\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(U).

The construction of the right-inverse of the divergence operator in

theorem

3.1
follows most of the construction given in

[
3
]
for the case
p=2
. However, at each step, the
construction requires modification for the case
p\neq 2
.

5.1.
Single element

We now prove
theorem

3.1
on a single reference element and then a
physical element
K\in\mathcal{T}
, both with homogeneous Dirichlet
conditions. Let

\hat{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the reference element depicted in

fig.

3(b)
. Given
K\in\mathcal{T}\cup\{\hat{T}\}
,
let
\mathcal{V}_{K}
and
\mathcal{E}_{K}
denote the vertices and edges of
K
,
and for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{N}(K)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{P}_{N}(K)^{2},

\displaystyle\bm{V}^{N}_{0}(K)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{P}_{N}(K)^{2}\cap\bm{W}^{1,1}_{0}(K),

\displaystyle Q^{N}_{0}(K)

\displaystyle:=\{q\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(K)\cap L^{1}_{0}(K):q(\bm{{a}})=0\ \forall\bm{{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}\}.

Additionally, for
K\in\mathcal{T}
, let
\bm{F}_{K}:\hat{T}\to K
denote
a bijective affine mapping.

We begin with an inverse of the trace operator on the reference element that
provides a divergence-free extension of suitable boundary data.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a linear operator

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}:\bigcup_{1<p<\infty}\{\bm{w}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}(\hat{T}):\dive\bm{w}\in L^{p}_{0}(\hat{T})\}\to\bm{W}^{1,1}(\hat{T})

satisfying the following: For all
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\bm{v}\in\{\bm{w}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}(\hat{T}):\dive\bm{w}\in L^{p}_{0}(\hat{T})\}
,
there holds
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}(\hat{T})
with

(5.2)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}\bm{v}=\bm{v}\quad\text{on }\partial\hat{T},\quad\dive\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}\bm{v}\equiv 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\hat{T}}\leq C(p)\|\bm{v}\|_{1-\frac{1}{p},p,\partial\hat{T}}.

Moreover, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds

(5.3)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}:\{\bm{w}\in\bm{V}^{N}(\hat{T}):\dive\bm{w}\in Q_{0}^{N-1}(\hat{T})\}\to\bm{V}^{N}(\hat{T}).

Proof.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\bm{v}\in\{\bm{w}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}(\hat{T}):\dive\bm{w}\in L^{p}_{0}(\hat{T})\}
.
Define
f,g:\partial\hat{T}\to\mathbb{R}
by the rules

(5.4)

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})=\int_{\bm{{a}}_{1}}^{\bm{x}}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\,\mathrm{d}{s}\quad\text{and}\quad g(\bm{x})=-(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{t})(\bm{x})\qquad\forall\bm{x}\in\partial\hat{T},

where the path integral is oriented in the counter-clockwise direction.
Then, these functions satisfy the following conditions:

1.

f
is continuous on
\partial\hat{T}
since
(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n},1)_{\partial\hat{T}}=(\dive\bm{v},1)_{\hat{T}}=0
;

2.

The quantity
\bm{\sigma}(f,g):=\partial_{t}f\bm{t}+g\bm{n}
, where
\partial_{t}
denotes the tangential derivative
along
\partial\hat{T}
, satisfies

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}(f,g)=(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\bm{t}-(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{t})\bm{n}=\bm{v}^{\perp}\in\bm{W}^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\hat{T}).

Recall that
\cdot^{\perp}
denotes a counterclockwise
rotation by
\pi/2
.

Thanks to
[
48
, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists
\psi\in W^{2,p}(\hat{T})
satisfying

\displaystyle\psi|_{\partial\hat{T}}=f,\quad\partial_{n}\psi|_{\partial\hat{T}}=g,\quad\text{and}\quad\|\psi\|_{2,p,\hat{T}}\leq C(p)\left(\|f\|_{p,\partial\hat{T}}+\|\bm{\sigma}(f,g)\|_{1-\frac{1}{p},p,\partial\hat{T}}\right).

Moreover, the mapping
(f,g)\mapsto\psi
is linear and independent of

p
. The function
\bm{w}:=\vcurl\psi
then satisfies
\dive\bm{w}=0
,

\displaystyle\bm{w}=\vcurl\psi=\partial_{t}f\bm{n}-g\bm{t}=\bm{v}\qquad\text{on }\partial\hat{T},

and

\displaystyle\|\bm{w}\|_{1,p}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\left(\|f\|_{p,\partial\hat{T}}+\|\bm{\sigma}(f,g)\|_{1-\frac{1}{p},p,\partial\hat{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\left(\|\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\|_{p,\partial\hat{T}}+\|\bm{v}^{\perp}\|_{1-\frac{1}{p},p,\partial\hat{T}}\right)\leq C(p)\|\bm{v}\|_{1,p,\hat{T}},

where we used Hölder’s inequality and the trace theorem. Defining

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}\bm{v}:=\bm{w}
then satisfies

eq.

5.2
.

It remains to show that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial}
satisfies

eq.

5.3
. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\bm{v}\in\{\bm{w}\in\bm{V}^{N}(\hat{T}):\dive\bm{w}\in Q_{0}^{N-1}(\hat{T})\}
. The proof of
[
3
, Lemma 3.6]

shows that
f
and
g
given by
eq.

5.4
satisfy the
following additional conditions:

3.

\bm{\sigma}(f,g)
is continuous;

4.

\bm{t}_{i+1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}(f,g)|_{\hat{e}_{i}}(\bm{{a}}_{i+2})=\bm{t}_{i}\cdot\bm{\sigma}(f,g)|_{\hat{e}_{i+1}}(\bm{{a}}_{i+2})
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, where the indices are understood modulo 3;

5.

f|_{\hat{e}_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}_{N+1}(\hat{e}_{i})
and

g|_{\hat{e}_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(\hat{e}_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq 3
.

Then,
[
48
, Theorem 3.2]
shows that the mapping
(f,g)\mapsto\psi
above also satisfies
\psi\in\mathcal{P}_{N+1}(\hat{T})
, and so

\bm{w}=\vcurl\psi\in\bm{V}^{N}(\hat{T})
, which completes the
proof.
∎

The next step completes the construction an inverse for the divergence
operator on the reference element.

Lemma 5.3
.

There exists a linear operator

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}:\bigcup_{1<p<\infty}L^{p}_{0}(\hat{T})\to\bm{W}^{1,1}_{0}(\hat{T})

satisfying the following: For all
p\in(1,\infty)
, and
q\in L^{p}_{0}(\hat{T})
, there holds
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\in\bm{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\hat{T})
with

(5.5)

\displaystyle\dive\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q=q\quad\text{and}\quad\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\|_{1,p,\hat{T}}\leq C(p)\|q\|_{p,\hat{T}}.

Moreover, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds

(5.6)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}:Q_{0}^{N-1}(\hat{T})\to\bm{V}^{N}_{0}(\hat{T}),

and for every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there holds

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\|_{1,p,\hat{T}}\leq C(\epsilon)N^{\epsilon}\|q\|_{p,\hat{T}},\qquad p\in\{1,\infty\},\ \forall q\in Q_{0}^{N-1}(\hat{T}).

Proof.

Let
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\hat{T})
and define the operator

\mathcal{R}
by the rule

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(q)(\bm{x}):=-\int_{\hat{T}}\theta(\bm{y})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\int_{0}^{1}tq(\bm{y}+t(\bm{x}-\bm{y}))\,\mathrm{d}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}},\qquad\bm{x}\in\hat{T}.

Then,
[
21
, Remark 3.5]
shows that
\mathcal{R}
maps

L^{p}(\hat{T})
boundedly into
\bm{W}^{1,p}(\hat{T})
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
with

\displaystyle\dive\mathcal{R}(q)=q\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{R}(q)\|_{1,p,\hat{T}}\leq C(p)\|q\|_{p,\hat{T}}\qquad\forall q\in L^{p}(\hat{T}),

and
\mathcal{R}
maps
\mathcal{P}_{N-1}(\hat{T})\to\mathcal{P}_{N}(\hat{T})^{2}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

We now define
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}:=(I-\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial})\mathcal{R}
. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in L^{p}_{0}(\hat{T})
. First note that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}
is
well-defined since
\dive\mathcal{R}=I
. Moreover, that

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q|_{\partial\hat{T}}=\bm{0}
and the
properties
in
eq.

5.5
follow from

eq.

5.2
, and so
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}
maps

L^{p}_{0}(\hat{T})
boundedly into
\bm{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\hat{T})
.

Assume further that
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\in Q_{0}^{N-1}(\hat{T})
.
Then,
\mathcal{R}(q)\in\bm{V}^{N}(\hat{T})
with
\dive\mathcal{R}(q)=q\in Q_{0}^{N-1}(\hat{T})
, and so
eq.

5.3

shows that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\in\bm{V}^{N}_{0}(\hat{T})
.
Moreover, the inverse inequality
eq.

5.1
gives

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\|_{1,1,\hat{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\|_{1,1+\epsilon,\hat{T}}\leq C(\epsilon)\|q\|_{1+\epsilon,\hat{T}}\leq C(\epsilon)N^{4\epsilon}\|q\|_{1,\hat{T}},

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\|_{1,\infty,\hat{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\epsilon)N^{4\epsilon}\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\dive}q\|_{1,\frac{1}{\epsilon},\hat{T}}\leq C(\epsilon)N^{4\epsilon}\|q\|_{\frac{1}{\epsilon},\hat{T}}\leq C(\epsilon)N^{4\epsilon}\|q\|_{\infty,\hat{T}}.

∎

Using a variant of the Piola transformation and standard scaling arguments, we
obtain an inverse of the divergence operator on a physical element.

Corollary 5.4
.

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}
, there exists a linear operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\dive,K}:\bigcup_{1<p<\infty}L^{p}_{0}(K)\to\bm{W}^{1,1}_{0}(K)

satisfying the following for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
q\in L_{0}^{p}(K)

(5.8)

Appendix A
Inverting the divergence operator with mixed boundary conditions

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a linear operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\dive,\Gamma_{0}}:\bigcup_{1<p<\infty}L_{\Gamma}^{p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)

satisfying the following: For all
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in L^{p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, there holds
\mathcal{L}_{\dive}q\in\bm{W}^{1,p}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
with

(A.1)

\displaystyle\dive\mathcal{L}_{\dive,\Gamma_{0}}q=q\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{L}_{\dive,\Gamma_{0}}q\|_{1,p,\Omega}\leq C(\Omega,\Gamma_{0},p)\|q\|_{p,\Omega}.

Proof.

If
|\Gamma|=|\Gamma_{0}|
, then the result is a restatement of

[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
, so suppose that
|\Gamma_{1}|>0
. Let
\gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
be an open segment in
\Gamma_{1}
and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\gamma)
be chosen so that
\int_{\gamma}\phi\,\mathrm{d}{s}=1
.
Let
\tilde{\phi}
be the extension by zero of
\phi
to all of
\partial\Omega
. By
[
7
, Theorem 6.1]
, there exists
\bm{\psi}\in\cup_{1<p<\infty}\bm{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
satisfying

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}|_{\gamma}=\phi\bm{n},\quad\bm{\psi}|_{\partial\Omega\setminus\gamma}=\bm{0},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1,p}\leq C(\Omega,p)\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{1-\frac{1}{p},p,\partial\Omega}\quad\forall p\in(1,\infty).

For

and

, let

and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\dive,\Gamma_{0}}q:=\bar{q}\bm{\psi}+\mathcal{L}_{\dive,\partial\Omega}\left(q-\bar{q}\dive\bm{\psi}\right).

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{\dive,\Gamma_{0}}q
is well-defined since

and so

Appendix B
Rapidly decaying 1D polynomials

Let
I:=(-1,1)
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
, let

P_{N}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
denote the usual Jacobi polynomial
[
56
]
,
normalized so that

(B.1)

\displaystyle P_{N}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1)=\binom{N+\alpha}{N}=\frac{\Gamma(N+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)N!}

The first result concerns the existence of 1D vertex functions that
interpolate derivative values at an endpoint and decay rapidly in all
L^{p}(I)

spaces, improving upon
[
4
, Lemma B.1]
which only concerned
decay in
L^{2}(I)
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\alpha>2m+\frac{3}{2}
, and
N\geq 2m+1
.
Then, the polynomial

(B.2)

\displaystyle\zeta_{m,N}(t):=\frac{1}{2^{m+1}P^{(\alpha,\alpha)}_{N-2m-1}(-1)}(1-t)^{m+1}(1+t)^{m}P^{(\alpha,\alpha)}_{N-2m-1}(t),\qquad t\in\bar{I},

satisfies
\partial_{t}^{n}\zeta_{m,N}(-1)=\delta_{mn}
and

\partial_{t}^{n}\zeta_{m,N}(1)=0
for
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
.
Additionally, for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in[0,\infty)
, there holds

(B.3)

\displaystyle\|\zeta_{m,N}\|_{s,p,I}\leq C(m,\alpha,s,p)N^{2(s-m-\frac{1}{p})}.

Proof.

The endpoint properties of
\zeta_{m,N}
follow by construction. Thanks
to the symmetry of the Jacobi polynomials, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t)^{m+1}(1+t)^{m}\left|P^{(\alpha,\alpha)}_{N-2m-1}(t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{m}\left|P^{(\alpha,\alpha)}_{N-2m-1}(t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{m+1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{m}\left|P^{(\alpha,\alpha)}_{N-2m-1}(t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}{t}.

Theorem 7.34 of
[
56
]
then gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{m}\left|P^{(\alpha,\alpha)}_{N-2m-1}(t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}{t}\leq C(\alpha)(N-2m-1)^{\alpha-2(m+1)}.

Applying Sterling’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\zeta_{m,N}\|_{1,I}

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha)\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)(N-2m-1)!}{\Gamma(N-2m+\alpha)}(N-2(m+1))^{\alpha-2(m+1)}

\displaystyle\leq C(m,\alpha)N^{-2(m+1)}.

Inequality
eq.

B.3
then follows from polynomial
inverse inequalities; see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
. ∎

We now modify the above construction for
m=1
to also have zero mean.

Corollary B.2
.

Let
\alpha>7/2
and
N\geq 4
. Then, the function

(B.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Upsilon}_{N}(t):=\zeta_{1,N}(t)-\frac{15}{16}(1-t^{2})^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\zeta_{1,N}(s)\,\mathrm{d}{s},\qquad t\in\bar{I},

Properties

follow by construction from

, while

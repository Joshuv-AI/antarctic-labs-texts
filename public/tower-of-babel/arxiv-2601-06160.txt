Student Guides Teacher: Weak-to-Strong Inference via Spectral Orthogonal Exploration
Dayu Wang, Jiaye Yang, Weikang Li, Jiahui Liang, Yang Li

Abstract
While Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate near-human capabilities, they often suffer from “Reasoning Collapse” in complex mathematical proving and long-horizon planning. Models tend to degenerate into low-rank Bias Manifold , where stochastic sampling merely produces lexical variations of erroneous logic rather than semantic exploration. This geometric collapse renders the model “blind” to high-value solutions that lie within its Null Space. To address this, we propose Spectral Orthogonal Exploration (SOE) , a geometric framework operating on a counter-intuitive “Student Guides Teacher” paradigm. Specifically, we utilize a weak auxiliary agent not for imitation, but as an orthogonal probe . By explicitly navigating the Teacher’s Null Space, SOE serves as a geometric bridge, effectively ejecting the model from local optima to explore diverse, high-value solution spaces. Experiments on mathematical benchmarks demonstrate that, relative to baseline methods, our approach improves average accuracy by 62.4% and increases average sampling efficiency by 113.7% , indicating a promising path toward overcoming performance plateaus in advanced reasoning tasks.The code is available at https://github.com/dayuwang401/SOE- .

3.1
The Low-Rank Manifold Hypothesis

Consider the inference process of a Large Language Model as a discrete dynamical system. We define the hidden state trajectory of a reasoning chain up to step
t
as
H_{t}=[h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{t}]\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times d}
, formed by stacking the hidden state vectors. Specifically, the
i
-th row represents the hidden state
h_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times d}
at step
i
.
As the model commits to a specific reasoning path, its internal representations tend to congregate within a lower-dimensional subspace. To describe the dimension of the subspace, we analyze the
Local Covariance Matrix

\Sigma_{t}
of the recent window of
k
tokens:

\Sigma_{t}=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{i=t-k+1}^{t}(h_{i}-\mu_{t})^{\top}(h_{i}-\mu_{t})

(1)

where
\mu_{t}
is the local mean.
If the dimension of subspace is low, the hidden state will be more similar, then the
Local Covariance Matrix
would have a lower rank. Based on this, we posit the
Low-Rank Manifold Hypothesis
: during reasoning collapse, the rank of
\Sigma_{t}
will decrease, confining the reasoning process to a
Bias Manifold

\mathcal{M}

Gao et al. 2019
.

3.2
Quantifying Spectral Collapse

Based on previous analysis, to measure the dimensionality of this bias manifold, we need to calculate the rank of
\Sigma_{t}
. We employ the Effective Rank (
EffRank
)
Roy and Vetterli 2007
to achieve our goal, which measures the effective information content of the spectrum:

\textit{EffRank}(\Sigma_{t})=\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\sigma}_{j}\log\tilde{\sigma}_{j}\right)

(2)

Here,
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}=\lambda_{j}/\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}
represents the normalized singular values.
A mathematical collapse is characterized by
\textit{EffRank}(\Sigma_{t})\ll d
. In this state, the variance in directions orthogonal to the null space
\mathcal{M}
vanishes to zero. Consequently, even with stochastic sampling (temperature
T>0
), the generated tokens were trapped in the Bias Manifold, rendering the model blind to correct solutions that lie in the null space.

4
Methodology: The SOE Framework

Motivated by the observed geometric decay, we develop the
Spectral Orthogonal Exploration (SOE)
framework. This section details the SOE algorithm designed to detect reasoning collapse and strategically intervene reasoning collapse during the inference process.

4.1
Overview of the Geometric Interaction

The framework operates on a post-hoc intervention paradigm. As shown in Figure
4
, the process follows three stages:

1.

Greedy Trace and Monitoring:
We first employ the Teacher
\pi_{\theta}
to generate a complete reasoning chain via greedy decoding. We then collect trajectories that yield incorrect answers, treating these erroneous paths as the baseline for collapsed reasoning. These trajectories are then partitioned at critical reasoning milestones (e.g., the onset of logical steps).
This procedure yields a set of prefix trajectories, allowing us to probe the model’s latent state at various depths of the reasoning process.

2.

Micro-SVD and Probe Selection:
At each truncation point
t_{i}
, we estimate the local bias manifold
\mathcal{M}_{t}
using Monte Carlo look-ahead simulations and Micro-SVD to identify the subspace where the Teacher model is trapped.
For each truncation point, the Student
\pi_{\phi}
generates candidate "probe" sequences (fixed at
L=8
tokens). We calculate the orthogonality of these candidates relative to the specific manifold
\mathcal{M}_{t}
at that step, selecting the optimal probe
s^{*}_{t}
that maximizes the orthogonality score.

3.

Ejection and Resumption:
The previous process yields distinct intervened contexts (the truncated histories stitched with their respective orthogonal probes). The Teacher
\pi_{\theta}
then resumes reasoning via sampling from each of these modified contexts, effectively attempting to recover the correct solution from three different geometric "ejection points".

A detailed example of the algorithm is provided in Appendix
A
.

4.2
Manifold Estimation

To estimate the bias manifold of the Teacher, we assume the Teacher’s internal representations at truncation point
t
follow a local distribution with covariance
\boldsymbol{\Sigma_{t}}
. Our goal is to obtain an
unbiased estimate
of the principal components of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma_{t}}
.

Unbiased Covariance Estimator.

Given the context
x_{<t}
at the truncation point
t
, we employ a Monte Carlo method to sample
N
stochastic look-ahead trajectories
\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N}\}
from the Teacher
\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x_{<t})
.
Let
\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the aggregated hidden state of the
i
-th trajectory. We calculate the sample mean
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{h}_{i}
and construct the centered state matrix
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
:

\mathbf{H}=\left[\mathbf{h}_{1}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\dots,\mathbf{h}_{N}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\right]

(3)

Theoretically, the sample covariance matrix
\hat{\mathbf{C}}=\frac{1}{N-1}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{T}
serves as an unbiased estimator of the true population covariance
\boldsymbol{\Sigma_{t}}
. By analyzing the spectrum of
\hat{\mathbf{C}}
, we can recover the directions of maximum variance that the model is naturally collapsing into.

The Micro-SVD Algorithm.

Directly computing eigenvectors for
\hat{\mathbf{C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is computationally prohibitive. We leverage the SVD duality, computing the Gram Matrix
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
of our Monte Carlo samples:

\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{H}^{T}\mathbf{H}

(4)

Solving the eigen-problem for the smaller matrix
\mathbf{G}\mathbf{v}_{k}=\lambda_{k}\mathbf{v}_{k}
allows us to recover the principal components
\mathbf{u}_{k}
of the Teacher’s manifold:

\mathbf{u}_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}_{k}

(5)

We define the
Bias Manifold

\mathcal{M}_{t}
as the subspace spanned by the top-
k
eigenvectors
\mathbf{U}_{\parallel}=\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{k}\}
. Directions orthogonal to this space constitute the "Null Space" where potential creative solutions reside.

4.3
Probe Selection: Orthogonal Latent Stitching (OLS)

With the manifold
\mathcal{M}_{t}
identified, we employ Orthogonal Latent Stitching to mechanically eject the model from its local optimum.

Heterogeneous Probing.

The Student model
\pi_{\phi}
generates a set of candidate sequences
\mathcal{C}_{Student}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{M}\}
, each with a fixed length of
L=8
tokens.
These short sequences act as geometric impulse vectors. To evaluate their geometry, we map them into the Teacher’s latent space via a forward pass:
\mathbf{z}_{j}=\textit{Forward}_{\pi_{\theta}}(s_{j}|x_{<t})
.

Residual Maximization.

We compute the projection of each candidate
\mathbf{z}_{j}
onto the Teacher’s bias manifold using the projection operator
\mathbf{P}_{\parallel}=\mathbf{U}_{\parallel}\mathbf{U}_{\parallel}^{T}
. The orthogonal residual
\mathbf{r}_{j}
represents the component of the Student’s thought that lies strictly in the Teacher’s blind spot:

\mathbf{r}_{j}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\parallel})(\mathbf{z}_{j}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}})

(6)

We select the candidate
s^{*}
that maximizes the Orthogonality Score, defined as the normalized energy of this residual, where
\epsilon>0
is a small constant introduced to avoid division by zero:

s^{*}=\arg\max_{s_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{Student}}\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{j}\|_{2}}{\|\mathbf{z}_{j}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\|_{2}+\epsilon}

(7)

This orthogonal token sequence
s^{*}
is stitched to the truncated context, forcing the Teacher to resolve the reasoning path from a new geometric coordinate outside its original bias manifold.

Analysis: The Thermodynamics of Reasoning

Based on our analysis, we argue "Reasoning Collapse" is mathematically equivalent to the
Rank Collapse
of the hidden state covariance matrix.

In this section, we provide a theoretical justification for two phenomena: (1) Why LLMs naturally degenerate into low-rank manifolds, and (2) Why our Orthogonal Latent Stitching (OLS) acts as a geometric dimensional expander rather than a knowledge injector.

6.1
The Trap of Low-Rank Self-Consistency

Let
H_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
be the matrix of hidden states in the context window at step
t
. We define the local covariance matrix
\Sigma_{t}=\frac{1}{N-1}H_{t}^{T}H_{t}
.
Reasoning collapse occurs when the effective rank of
\Sigma_{t}
diminishes, i.e.,
\text{rank}(\Sigma_{t})\ll d
. The states are confined to a subspace
\mathcal{S}
.

Proposition 1 (Invariance of Subspace).

If the input context lies within a low-rank subspace
\mathcal{S}
, the output of a standard Self-Attention layer lies strictly within the same subspace (or a linear transform thereof), preventing spontaneous exploration of the Null Space.

Dong et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2020

The detailed proof and an analysis of why non-linearities (FFNs) fail to restore dimensionality are provided in Appendix

6.2
Theoretical Justification for Progressive Rank Decay

Why does the effective rank decrease over time during long chain-of-thought reasoning? We attribute this to the
Spectrum Contraction
property of the Softmax operator in high-confidence regimes
Yang et al. 2017
.

Theorem 2 (Spectrum Contraction).

As the model becomes more confident (the norm of logits increases), the attention mechanism acts as a rank-reducing operator, causing the spectrum of the covariance matrix to decay exponentially.

Please refer to Appendix
D.2
for the formal proof. Thus, reasoning behaves like a
contractive mapping
on the manifold dimension. The model "forgets" the orthogonal dimensions required for error correction.

A critical question arises: Does SOE work because the Student provides

We prove it is the latter by analyzing the signal-to-noise ratio in the Null Space.

Consider the contribution to the spectrum in the Null Space direction

Prior to injection, the variance in this direction is

\text{rank}(\Sigma_{new})=\text{rank}(\Sigma_{old})+1

7
Conclusion

We attribute
Reasoning Collapse
to a geometric degeneration into low-rank
Bias Manifold
, rendering high-value solutions in the
Null Space
inaccessible. To address this, we introduce
Spectral Orthogonal Exploration (SOE)
, which employs a “Student Guides Teacher” paradigm to mechanically eject models from local optima via
Orthogonal Latent Stitching
. Our analysis confirms that SOE functions fundamentally as a
Dimensionality Expander
rather than a knowledge injector. These findings suggest that overcoming reasoning bottlenecks requires moving beyond probabilistic sampling toward precise geometric interventions in the latent space.

8
Limitations

While our proposed Spectral Orthogonal Exploration (SOE) framework demonstrates significant efficacy in mitigating reasoning collapse, we acknowledge several limitations that merit further investigation.

First, regarding computational efficiency, although SOE improves sample efficiency by requiring fewer total tokens to discover correct solutions, it introduces additional wall-clock latency per reasoning step. This is due to the computational overhead of the Micro-SVD operation and Monte Carlo look-ahead simulations required to estimate the Bias Manifold
\mathcal{M}_{t}
.

Second, the core mechanism of SOE relies on explicitly accessing and manipulating the model’s internal hidden states to compute effective rank and orthogonal residuals. This requirement restricts the applicability of our method to open-weights models, precluding its direct use with closed-source LLMs where only API access is available.

Finally, due to computational resource constraints, our empirical validation is primarily conducted on models in the 4B parameter range within mathematical domains. While our theoretical analysis suggests the Low-Rank Manifold Hypothesis is model-agnostic, further research is needed to verify the scalability of SOE on substantially larger models and its generalization to other reasoning-intensive tasks beyond mathematics.

Appendix D
Theoretical Proofs and Extended Analysis

D.1
Proof of Proposition 1 and Analysis of Non-Linearity

Proof.

Consider a simplified linear attention head. The output for a query
q
is given by:

\text{Attn}(q,K,V)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\text{softmax}(q^{T}k_{i})v_{i}

(9)

where
v_{i}=W_{V}h_{i}
. Since each input
h_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
, then each value vector
v_{i}\in W_{V}\mathcal{S}
. The output is a convex combination of vectors in
W_{V}\mathcal{S}
. Thus, the generated token embedding lies strictly in the span of the existing history. Without external noise or non-linearity capable of "folding" space into new dimensions, the model cannot access the Null Space
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
.
∎

Remark 1: The Inadequacy of Pointwise Non-Linearity.

A natural counter-argument suggests that the Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) in Transformers, which contain non-linear activation functions (such as GELU, ReLU), might restore the lost dimensionality. We formally refute this by examining the coordinate-wise nature of these activations.

Consider the FFN update:
h^{\prime}=W_{2}\sigma(W_{1}h)
. The linear transformations
W_{1},W_{2}
can at most preserve the rank of the input:
\text{rank}(Wh)\leq\text{rank}(h)
. Thus, any potential rank restoration relies solely on the non-linearity
\sigma(\cdot)
.

However, standard activation functions are
coordinate-wise
:
\sigma(v)_{i}=\sigma(v_{i})
. They operate on each dimension independently without mixing information.

•

Zero-Variance Persistence
: If the input distribution has collapsed such that variance is zero along a basis vector
e_{j}
(i.e., the hidden states never "visit" this dimension), then for any sample
h
, the
j
-th component is constant (or zero). Since
\sigma
acts independently, the output
\sigma(h_{j})
remains constant. The activation cannot spontaneously "light up" a dark dimension.

•

Manifold Curvature vs. Expansion
: While non-linearity can bend a flat
k
-dimensional subspace into a curved
k
-dimensional manifold, it does not increase the
intrinsic dimensionality
. A piece of paper crumpled into a ball (non-linear transformation) still has a surface area of dimension 2; it does not acquire volume.

Consequently, without an orthogonal external force (like our proposed injection), the FFN merely warps the collapsed Bias Manifold but remains topologically confined within it
Geva et al. 2021
.

Proof of Theorem 2

Proof.

Let the attention weights be

. The update rule roughly follows
H_{t+1}=AH_{t}

In the later stages of reasoning, the model often exhibits "confirmation bias," where tokens attend heavily to a few "anchor" tokens (e.g., repeating a premise).

From the inequality of singular values
\sigma_{i}(XY)\leq\|X\|\sigma_{i}(Y)
, if the attention matrix
A
becomes effectively low-rank (due to sparse attention), then:

\text{rank}(H_{t+1})\leq\min(\text{rank}(A),\text{rank}(H_{t}))

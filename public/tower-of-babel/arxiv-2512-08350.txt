A tight example for approximation ratio 5 for covering small cuts by the primal-dual method
Zeev Nutov

Abstract
In the Small Cuts Cover problem we seek to cover by a min-cost edge-set the set family of cuts of size/capacity < k <k of a graph. Recently, Simmons [ 5 ] showed that the primal-dual algorithm of Williamson, Goemans, Mihail, and Vazirani [ 7 ] achieves approximation ratio 5 5 for this problem, and asked whether this bound is tight. We will answer this question positively, by providing an example in which the ratio between the solution produced by the primal-dual algorithm and the optimum is arbitrarily close to 5 5 .

Following the breakthrough achieved in
[

]

[
7
]
,

was studied in several other papers, c.f.
[
3
,
1
,
4
,

]
.
Recently, Simmons
[

Theorem 1
.

For any integers

,

The construction

We start by describing the WGMV algorithm for covering an arbitrary set family
{\cal F}
.
An inclusion-minimal set in
{\cal F}
is called an
{\cal F}
-
core
,
or just a
core
, if
{\cal F}
is clear from the context.
Consider the standard LP-relaxation
(P)
for the problem of covering the family
{\cal F}

and its dual program
(D)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllllll}&&\min&\ \displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}c_{e}x_{e}&&\max&\ \displaystyle\sum_{S\in{\cal F}}y_{S}\\
&\mbox{\bf(P)}&\ \mbox{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\delta(S)}x_{e}\geq 1\ \ \ \forall S\in{\cal F}&\mbox{\bf(D)}&\ \mbox{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{\delta(S)\ni e}y_{S}\leq c_{e}\ \ \ \forall e\in E\\
&&&\ \ x_{e}\geq 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall e\in E&&&\ \ y_{S}\geq 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall S\in{\cal F}\end{array}

Given a solution
y
to
(D)
, an edge
e\in E
is
tight
if
the inequality of
e
in
(D)
holds with equality.
The algorithm has two phases.

Phase 1
starts with
J=\emptyset
and applies a sequence of iterations.
At the beginning of an iteration, we compute the cores of the family of sets in
{\cal F}
not yet covered by
J
.
Then we raise the dual variables corresponding to these cores
uniformly until some edge
e\in E\setminus J
becomes tight, and add all tight edges to
J
.
Phase 1 terminates when
J
covers
{\cal F}
.

Phase 2
is a “reverse delete” phase, in which we process edges in the reverse order
that they were added, and delete an edge
e
from
J
if
J\setminus\{e\}
still covers
{\cal F}
.
At the end of the algorithm,
J
is output.

Lemma 2
.

The family of

We prove the first part. As was already observed,

d(x)=k\ \ \ \ \ d(y)=2k-2q\geq k+1\ \ \ \ \ d(z)=2k-2q-1\geq k

d(\{x,y\})=k\ \ \ \ \ d(\{y,z\})=2k-2q-1\geq k\ \ \ \ \ d(\{x,z\})=d(x)+d(z)=k+d(z)

We prove the second part.
We already know that
\{\{t\},A,X,Y\}\subseteq{\cal F}^{r}\setminus{\cal F}^{r}_{C}

Lemma 4
.

The family of
{\cal F}

{\cal C}=\{\{r\},\{t_{1}\},\ldots,\{t_{p}\},C\}

We prove the first part, that
{\cal C}=\{\{r\},\{t_{1}\},\ldots,\{t_{p}\},C\}
.
Note that:

•

Each
\{t_{i}\}
is a small cut since

•

\{r\}

•

Thus each of the singleton sets

. We prove that

Suppose to the contrary that there is a small cut

If

We prove the second part.
We already know that all cuts in

Suppose to the contrary that there is some other small cut

.

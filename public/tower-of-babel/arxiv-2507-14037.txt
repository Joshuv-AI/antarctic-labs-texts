Comment to “The asymptotically-free gauge theories”
Giacomo Cacciapaglia, Aldo Deandrea, Francesco Sannino

Abstract
The recent paper ” The asymptotically-free gauge theories ” by Ben Gripaios and Khoi Le Nguyen Nguyen Gripaios and Le Nguyen Nguyen 2025 presents a proposed classification of gauge theories valid down to arbitrarily short scales. In this comment, we aim to clarify several points and address some statements that may be misleading. We also provide additional context by discussing relevant prior literature and existing classifications.



I
Gauge anomaly cancellation

The cancellation of gauge anomalies is essential in defining a consistent gauge theory. Hence, imposing the absence of ABJ anomalies
Adler 1969
;
Bell and Jackiw 1969
and topological anomalies
Witten 1982
;
Wang et al. 2019
is a central ingredient in any classification attempt. The former are relevant for
\mathrm{SU}(N)
theories with complex representations, while the latter for
\mathrm{Sp}(2N)
theories with pseudo-real irreducible representations (irreps). This analysis has been performed in
Cacciapaglia et al. 2025
to classify asymptotic-free (AF) gauge theories, showing that crucial restrictions are imposed on the available irreps. For instance, the three-index symmetric of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
,
(3,0,\dots)
in dynkin representation, cannot be used to define a chiral AF theory
Cacciapaglia et al. 2025
for all
N
, while a one-family vector-like theory exists only for
N=2,3,4
. For the
(1,0,1,0,0)
of
\mathrm{SU}(6)
, neither chiral nor vector-like theories can be defined. Furthermore, the well known Witten anomalies forbid the presence of an odd number of certain representations of
\mathrm{Sp}(2N)
(a discussion of the Witten anomalies for general pseudo-real representations of
\mathrm{Sp}(2N)
is present in the supplementary material of
Cacciapaglia et al. 2025
).

The above constraints were not taken into account in
Gripaios and Le Nguyen Nguyen 2025
, where anomalies are only briefly mentioned as reducing the number of viable theories for
\mathrm{SU}(5)
(Lie algebra
A_{4}
). In fact, Ref.
Cacciapaglia et al. 2025
provides a ‘basis’ of chiral families, which can be used to construct the whole class of AF anomaly-free
\mathrm{SU}(N)
theories, when supplemented with vector-like fermions.

II
Classifications present in the literature

The statement “
here we provide a first classification of such theories, focussing on the case in which any matter is fermionic; the generalization to theories with scalar particles or supersymmetry will become obvious
” is incorrect. For instance, vector-like AF theories based on
\mathrm{SU}(N)
have been classified in
Dietrich and Sannino 2007
. The chiral case has been classified in
Cacciapaglia et al. 2025
by providing a basis of chiral families for all
\mathrm{SU}(N)
and anomaly-free AF representations for
\mathrm{Sp}(2N)
. The classification of AF irreps of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
has been pioneered by E.Eichten and F.Feinberg: Ref.
Eichten and Feinberg 1982
contains a more complete list than the one in Ref.
Eichten et al. 1982
(the latter cited in
Gripaios and Le Nguyen Nguyen 2025
).

Studying AF in the presence of scalars is not a mere generalization of the fermionic case (see next paragraph).

III
Complete freedom with scalars

Achieving AF in gauge theories with scalars is far from obvious. This is due to the presence of additional marginal operators, namely quartic couplings and Yukawas (with fermions). A simple analysis of the one-loop gauge beta function, as stated in
Gripaios and Le Nguyen Nguyen 2025
, is far from enough: in fact, the sensitivity to the Yukawa couplings enters at two loops while that to the quartics at three loops. This order is dictated by the need to respect the Weyl consistency conditions
Antipin et al. 2013
;
Osborn 1989
;
Jack and Osborn 1990
;
Osborn 1991
. Complete asymptotic freedom, therefore, requires that all the couplings run free
Callaway 1988
;
Holdom et al. 2015
;
Giudice et al. 2015
;
Pica et al. 2017
.

IV
You can be safe rather than free

The naive statement that “
asymptotically-free gauge theories provide the only known examples of interacting quantum field theories in four spacetime dimensions that are valid down to arbitrarily short scales
” is challenged by the discovery of interactive four-dimensional gauge-Yukawa asymptotically safe theories
Litim and Sannino 2014
.

At its core, this discovery demonstrates the existence of interacting ultraviolet (UV) fixed points in non-supersymmetric gauge-Yukawa theories. In simpler terms, it showed that it is possible for the interaction strengths of a quantum field theory not to grow infinitely large at very high energies, a fatal flaw known as a Landau pole, but instead to approach a constant, non-zero value. This “asymptotic safety” ensures that the theory remains well behaved and predictive. Hence, the naive expectation, largely shaped by the success of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), that only ”asymptotically free” theories can be considered fundamental, is incorrect since this discovery provides a compelling alternative. Interactions can persist at the highest energy scales without leading to inconsistencies.

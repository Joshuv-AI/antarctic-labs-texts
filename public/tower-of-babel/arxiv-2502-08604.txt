Scattering of Rational Solutions to the Half-Wave Maps Equation
Gaspard Ohlmann

Abstract




Abstract

This article studies the rational solutions of the Half-Wave Maps equation (HWM) in the non-singular spectrum case. We first provide characterizations to what we call
scattering behavior
, and show that they imply scattering in Sobolev norm. We then provide a local condition implying
scattering behavior
. Building on this, we show that any solution with non-singular spectrum scatters and provide an explicit formula for the function to which the solution is scattering. This allows us to show that the scattering map is the identity. Additionally, we create, for any given number of spins and any target non-singular spectrum, global solutions of (HWM) with a spectrum arbitrarily close to the target. Finally, we show, using a diagonal characterization of traveling waves, that if a wave scatters to a traveling wave, it is a scattering wave.

Introduction

1.1
The Half-Wave maps equation

In this article, we are interested in the half-wave maps equation that arises in multiple contexts, and can be seen as a continuum version of discrete completely integrable spin Calogero–Moser models
[
20
]

[
11
]

[
2
]
. For a recent survey, we refer the reader to
[
12
]
. The Half-Wave maps equation reads

\partial_{t}\mathbf{m}(t,x)=\mathbf{m}(t,x)\times|\nabla|\mathbf{m}(t,x)=\mathbf{m}(t,x)\times\mathcal{H}(\partial_{x}m)(t,x),

(HWM-S)

where
\mathbf{m}(t,\cdot)
is a function from the real line to the sphere, i.e.

m:(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2},~\mathbb{S}^{2}=\{u\in\mathbb{C}^{3},~|u|=1\},

and
\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transform defined as

\mathcal{H}(f)(x)={\text{P.V.}}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.42271pty.

In dimension one, this equation is energy-critical and preserves the norm

||f||_{\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.42271pt\xi,\quad\text{where }\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip 0.56917ptx\xi}f(x)\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.42271ptx.

We are interested in this article in the cases of rational functions, i.e. functions for which we can make the ansatz

\mathbf{m}(t,x)=\mathbf{m}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\mathbf{s}_{j}(t)}{x-x_{j}(t)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{j}(t)}{x-\bar{x}_{j}(t)},~\operatorname{Im}(x_{j}(t))>0.

(RAT-S)

It has been proven in
[
5
]
that solutions that are rational for a given time
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
stay rational for all times. As shown in
[
1
]
, the study of this problem comes down to the study of a Calogero-Moser system of differential equations, studied for instance, in
[
7
]
,
[
19
]
or
[
9
]
, where the dynamic of the spins and the poles is governed by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\mathbf{s}}_{j}(t)=2{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip 0.56917pt\sum_{k\neq j}^{N}\frac{\mathbf{s}_{j}(t)\times\mathbf{s}_{k}(t)}{\left({x}_{j}(t)-{x}_{k}(t)\right)^{2}},~1\leq j\leq N,\\
&\ddot{x}_{j}(t)=-4\sum_{k\neq j}^{N}\frac{\mathbf{s}_{j}(t)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{k}(t)}{\left({x}_{j}(t)-{x}_{k}(t)\right)^{3}},~1\leq j\leq N,\end{aligned}\right.

(CM-S)

and the dot product is defined without conjugation as
a\cdot b=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}b_{i}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
. If at
t_{0}
,
\mathbf{m}(t_{0},x)
belongs to the sphere for all
x
, then this property is preserved by the equation as
\partial_{t}\left(|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\right)=2\langle\mathbf{m}\times|\nabla|\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}\rangle=0
. Satisfying the additional constraint at any time
t_{0}

\mathbf{s}_{j}(t_{0})^{2}=0,~\mathbf{s}_{j}(t_{0})\cdot\left(\mathbf{m}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\mathbf{s}_{k}(t_{0})}{x_{j}(t_{0})-x_{k}(t_{0})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{k}(t_{0})}{x_{j}(t_{0})-\bar{x}_{k}(t_{0})}\right)=0,

(CONS-S)

guarantees that the constraint, and this condition, is always satisfied for any time
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Only a handfull of solutions are known for this equation. In
[
13
]
, Lenzmann and Schikorra provide an explicit characterization of the traveling waves, and in
[
1
]
, the authors provide a class of solutions for which the expression is known for all times. In this article, we provide the existence of solutions whose spectrum are arbitrarily close to any given non-singular spectrum.

In addition, well-posedness and behavior for large times are active subjects of research. In dimensions
d\geq 3
, global existence results are established for small data, see
[
10
]
,
[

]
or
[
14
]
. In this article, we describe the scattering map for any solution with non-singular spectrum, and show that it is in fact the identity. We also provide an explicit formula for the function to which the solution is scattering.

2.1
Behavior assuming a scattering situation

In this subsection, we introduce what we call a

Throughout this subsection,
\mathbf{m}
will be a rational solution of (
HWM-S
) and

\mathbf{m}(t,x)=\mathbf{m}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\mathbf{s}_{j}(t)}{x-x_{j}(t)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{j}(t)}{x-\bar{x}_{j}(t)}.

Definition 2.1
.

We will say that
\mathbf{m}
is in a scattering situation, denoted condition
(C_{3})
if and only if nothing will ever happen after a certain time, that we describe as

(C_{3}):=~\left\{\begin{aligned} &(i)~x_{j}(t)=v_{j}t+x_{j}^{\infty}+\operatorname{o}(1)\\

&(iii)~s_{j}(t)=s_{j}^{\infty}+\operatorname{o}(1),\\

2.3
Sufficient conditions for a scattering behavior starting at
t=0

In this section, we will establish sufficient conditions for initial data to admit a global in-time and scattering solution. The condition we define intuitively describes to a situation obtained after a long time, but can be checked locally for any time.
It corresponds to low interactions between the solitons, and we show that the interactions are not sufficient to reinforce the interactions, which leads to a situation preserved for all times moving forward.

The interest of this result is twofold. First, we use it in conjunction with a fixpoint argument to create solutions with non-degenerate spectrum for all
N
number of solitons. We also show that the condition we present has to be satisfied for some time for any solution, and hence obtain the well-posedness and scattering of any solution as long as the speeds are different.

We denote

\left\{\begin{aligned} &S=\max_{j}|\mathbf{s}_{j}(0)|,\\
&\nu=\min_{j\neq k}|\operatorname{Re}(\dot{x}_{j}(0))-\operatorname{Re}(\dot{x}_{k}(0))|,\\
&D=\min_{j\neq k}|\operatorname{Re}(x_{j}(0))-\operatorname{Re}(x_{k}(0))|.\end{aligned}\right.

We first present and show the following theorem.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
s_{j}(0)
spins and
x_{j}(0)
poles satisfying the constraints, and assume

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\nu>0,\text{ (different speeds) }

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle v_{j}>v_{k}\Rightarrow\operatorname{Re}(x_{j}(0))>\operatorname{Re}(x_{k}(0)).\text{ (same order as the speeds) }

5
Absence of scattering to traveling waves

In this section, we show that if
\mathbf{m}
is a solution of (
HWM-S
), then we can’t have that
\mathbf{m}
scatters to
g
where
g
is a traveling wave, except if
\mathbf{m}
was actually traveling for all finite times. To do so, we first provide an algebraic characterization of being traveling. Then, we show that this property is transferred backward from
t=+\infty
to finite times.

5.1
Diagonal characterization of travelingness

In this section, we provide characterizations of traveling wave solutions. Our focus is on algebraic properties that are preserved over infinite backward time. We leverage the Lax-Pair structure:

\dot{L}(t)=[B(t),L(t)].

(172)

Since on the diagonal,
L(t)_{i,i}=\dot{x}_{i}(t)
, then the poles are traveling if and only if
L
has a constant diagonal with identical values. Since
\dot{U}(t)=B(t)U(t)
, the matrix
B(t)
represents an infinitesimal change of coordinates at any given time.

For a wave to be considered traveling, two conditions must hold. First, all poles must move at a unique constant velocity
v
. This condition alone is not preserved, by the infinitesimal change of coordinates. Since the eigenvalues of
L
are the asymptotic speeds, a necessary condition is that
v
is an eigenvalue of
L(t)
with multiplicity
N
. If
L(t)
is a diagonal matrix
vI_{N}
, it is preserved under time evolution, as
L(t)
is expressed through
L(t_{0})
and a time-dependent change of coordinates. However, this preservation over infinite time is nontrivial.

A traveling wave must also exhibit constant spins. While the condition
\dot{S}(t)=0
While the condition is necessary, it is not sufficient to ensure that the spins remain unchanged. Assuming the poles travel, the evolution equation:

\dot{s}_{j}(t)=2{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\hskip 0.56917pt\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{s_{j}(t)\times s_{k}(t)}{(x_{j}(t)-x_{k}(t))^{2}},

demonstrates the existence of degrees of freedom in spin interactions, allowing for soliton solutions with non-constant spins on top of traveling poles. The necessary condition
\dot{S}(t)=[B(t),S(t)]=0
implies that the infinitesimal change of coordinates must commute with
S(t)
, which is not directly given by the poles traveling.

To formalize these ideas, we introduce two distinct notions of traveling waves.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{m}(t,x)
be a rational solution of (
HWM-S
) of the form:

\mathbf{m}(t,x)=\mathbf{m}_{0}+i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{s_{j}(t)}{x-x_{j}(t)}-i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{s}_{j}(t)}{x-\bar{x}_{j}(t)},

|v_{j}-\dot{x}_{j}(t_{1})|\leq\frac{C}{t_{1}^{2}}.

(264)

Finally, the function
x_{j}(t)-v_{j}t
has a derivative in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, so it converges as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

x_{j}(t)-v_{j}t\rightarrow x_{j}(\infty).

(265)

Now, we consider the norm of the difference between the solution and the function it is scattering to, with
y_{j}(t)=y_{j}+tv_{j}
,

||G(t,x)-\mathbf{m}(t,x)||_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}=-4\pi\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j}(t),A_{k}(t)\rangle}{(x_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{k}(t))^{2}}\\
+4\pi\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j}(t),C_{k}\rangle}{(x_{j}(t)-\bar{y}_{k}(t))^{2}}+4\pi\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\frac{\langle C_{j},A_{k}(t)\rangle}{(y_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{k}(t))^{2}}-4\pi\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\frac{\langle C_{j},C_{k}\rangle}{(y_{j}(t)-\bar{y}_{k}(t))^{2}}.

(266)

For any
j\neq k
, the cross terms are
o(1/t^{2})
since the numerators are bounded and the speeds are different. Hence

||G(t,x)-\mathbf{m}(t,x)||_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}=o(1/t^{2})+\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j}(t),A_{j}(t)\rangle}{Im(x_{j}(t))^{2}}+\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle C_{j},C_{j}\rangle}{Im(y_{j})^{2}}\\
-\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle C_{j},A_{j}(t)\rangle}{(y_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{j}(t))^{2}}-\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j},C_{j}(t)\rangle}{(x_{j}(t)-\bar{y}_{j}(t))^{2}}.

(267)

As
t\rightarrow\infty
,

||G(t,x)-\mathbf{m}(t,x)||_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\rightarrow\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j}(\infty),A_{j}(\infty)\rangle}{Im(x_{j}(\infty))^{2}}+\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle C_{j},C_{j}\rangle}{Im(y_{j})^{2}}\\
-\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle C_{j},A_{j}(\infty)\rangle}{(y_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}(\infty))^{2}}-\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j}(\infty),C_{j}\rangle}{(x_{j}(\infty)-\bar{y}_{j})^{2}}=0.

(268)

Since we have by convexity for
a,b>0
, (with equality for
a=b
)

\frac{1}{(a+b)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{1}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{b^{2}}\right),

(269)

and using the parallelogram identity with
|x_{j}-\bar{y}_{j}|=|\bar{x}_{j}-y_{j}|
,

2|x_{j}-\bar{y}_{j}|^{2}=8(Im(x_{j})+Im(y_{j}))^{2}+8(Re(x_{j})-Re(y_{j}))^{2},

(270)

so

\frac{1}{|x_{j}-\bar{y}_{j}|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{Im(x_{j})^{2}}+\frac{1}{Im(y_{j})^{2}}\right).

(271)

with equality for
Re(x_{j})=Re(y_{j})
. We deduce That for any
j
,
Re(x_{j})=Re(y_{j})
and
Im(x_{j})=Im(x_{j})
, and that

\frac{\langle A_{j}(\infty),A_{j}(\infty)\rangle}{Im(x_{j}(\infty))^{2}}+\frac{\langle C_{j},C_{j}\rangle}{Im(y_{j}(\infty))^{2}}-\frac{\langle C_{j},A_{j}(\infty)\rangle}{(y_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}(\infty))^{2}}-\frac{\langle A_{j}(\infty),C_{j}\rangle}{(x_{j}(\infty)-\bar{y}_{j})^{2}}\\
=\frac{1}{Im(x_{j}(\infty))^{2}}\left(\langle A_{j}(\infty),A_{j}(\infty)\rangle+\langle C_{j},C_{j}\rangle-2Re\langle A_{j}(\infty),C_{j}\rangle\right)\geq 0,

(272)

so for the norm to be zero, the terms must all be zero and

(\langle A_{j}(\infty),A_{j}(\infty)\rangle^{1/2}-\langle C_{j},C_{j}\rangle^{1/2})=0,

(273)

so
||A_{j}(\infty)||=||C_{j}||
, and equality case in
\langle A_{j}(\infty_{,}C_{j}\rangle
gives
A_{j}(\infty)=C_{j}
.

∎

Before doing the case
v_{i}=v_{j}
for
i=j
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
F(t,x)
be a rational function of the form

F(t,x)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j},A_{k}\rangle}{(x_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{k}(t))^{2}},

(274)

such that

\langle F(t,x),|\nabla|F(t,x)\rangle\xrightarrow[t\rightarrow\infty]{}0.

(275)

Then, if
i_{0}
is such that
x_{i_{0}}
is isolated (or up to a subsequence), i.e.

|x_{i_{0}}(t_{p})-x_{j}(t_{p})|\geq\delta>0,

(276)

for some
\delta>0
,
then

\langle A_{i_{0}}(t_{\phi(p)}),A_{i_{0}}(t_{\phi(p)})\rangle\xrightarrow[p\rightarrow\infty]{}0.

(277)

Proof.

We start by assuming the existence of
t_{p}\rightarrow\infty
such that
|x_{i_{0}}(t_{p})-x_{j}(t_{p})|\geq\delta
for any
p
and
j\neq i_{0}
. We assume
i_{0}=1
. We have

\langle F(t,x),|\nabla|F(t,x)\rangle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\frac{\langle A_{j}(t),A_{k}(t)\rangle}{(x_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{k}(t))^{2}}\\
=\langle F(t,x-Re(x_{1}(t))),|\nabla|F(t,x-Re(x_{1}(t)))\rangle.

(278)

We will distinguish two cases for the other poles
x_{j}
,
j\neq 1
. Either there exists a subsequence, such that
|x_{j}-x_{1}|\rightarrow\infty
, or
|x_{j}-x_{1}|
is bounded. In the second case, we deduce that
x_{j}-x_{1}
converges up to a subsequence. Call
J
the first set of poles and
K
the second one, and we consider a diagonal subsequence
\phi
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &|z_{j}|=|x_{j}-Re(x_{1})|\rightarrow\infty,~j\in J,\\
&z_{j}=x_{j}-Re(x_{1})\rightarrow z_{j}^{\infty},~j\in K.\end{aligned}\right.

(279)

Since we have by Plancherel’s identity that
\langle F,|\nabla|F\rangle=\langle|\xi|^{1/2}\hat{F},|\xi|^{1/2}\hat{F}\rangle=\langle|\nabla|^{1/2}F,|\nabla|^{1/2}F\rangle
, we look at
|\nabla|^{1/2}F
. With
S_{j}=\frac{A_{j}}{x-x_{j}}
,

\mathcal{F}(S_{j})(\xi)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,~\xi>0,\\
&A_{j}\cdot 2i\pi e^{-2i\pi\xi x_{j}},~\xi\leq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

(280)

Hence,

|\nabla|^{1/2}\frac{A_{i}}{x-x_{i}}=c_{\nabla}\int_{\xi<0}e^{2i\pi x\xi}\left(|\xi|^{1/2}A_{i}2i\pi e^{-2i\pi\xi x_{i}}\right)dx=\frac{c_{\nabla}A_{i}}{4\sqrt{2}\pi i^{3/2}(x-x_{i})^{3/2}}.

(281)

We prefer this formulation, as it is then possible to localize the contributions of the different solitons near their poles. We have for the Lebesgue norm

|||\nabla|F(t,x)||^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{d_{\nabla}A_{j}}{(x-x_{j})^{3/2}}\right|dx

(282)

We now center at
x=Re(x_{1})
and distinguish the two cases for the poles. We also cut the integral to
[-1,1]
.

|||\nabla|^{1/2}F(t,x)||_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq d_{\nabla}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\sum_{j\in J}\frac{A_{j}}{(x-x_{j})^{3/2}}+\sum_{j\in K}\frac{A_{j}}{(x-x_{j})^{3/2}}\right|^{2}dx,

(283)

where
x_{1}\in K
and
z_{1}=x_{1}-Re(x_{1})
by convention. With
\phi(p)
such that
|z_{j}|\geq p
for
j\in J
, then for
x\in[-1,1]
and
j\in J

\left|\frac{A_{j}}{(z_{j}-x)^{3/2}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{p^{3/2}}.

(284)

In particular,
F=F_{1}+F_{2}
where
F_{2}
converges uniformly to
0
on
[-1,1]
, so
F_{1}
must converge to
0
in
L^{2}
norm as well. Hence,

\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\sum_{j\in K}\frac{A_{j}}{(z_{j}-x)^{3/2}}\right|^{2}dx\rightarrow 0.

(285)

Now,
z_{j}\rightarrow z_{j}^{\infty}
and
A_{j}\rightarrow A_{j}^{\infty}
, so we can apply dominated convergence for
p
big enough and obtain

\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\sum_{j\in K}\frac{A_{j}}{(z_{j}-x)^{3/2}}\right|^{2}dx\rightarrow\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\sum_{j\in K}\frac{A_{j}^{\infty}}{(z_{j}^{\infty}-x)^{3/2}}\right|^{2}dx=0.

(286)

In particular,

\sum_{j\in K}\frac{A_{j}^{\infty}}{(z_{j}^{\infty}-x)^{3/2}}=0

(287)

almost everywhere on
[-1,1]
, and is continuous so everywhere on
[-1,1]
. Differentiating
k
times at
x=0
gives

\sum_{j\in K}\frac{A_{j}^{\infty}}{(x_{j})^{1/2}}\frac{1}{(x_{j})^{k}}=0.

(288)

By regrouping the terms,
j\sim i
when
x_{i}=x_{j}
, we know that
[1]=\{1\}
and obtain the invertible Van-Der Monde determinant for
k\geq 1

\sum_{[j]\in K/\sim}\left(\sum_{k\in[j]}\frac{A_{k}^{\infty}}{(x_{k})^{3/2}}\right)\frac{1}{(x_{j})^{3/2}}=0.

(289)

This implies in particular that for any
j

\sum_{k\in[j]}\frac{A_{k}^{\infty}}{(x_{k})^{3/2}}=0,~\Rightarrow A_{1}^{\infty}=0.

(290)

∎

This theorem allows us to prove the more applicable corollary.

Corollary C.2
.

Let
F
be a rational function of the form

F(t,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{A_{j}(t)}{x-x_{j}(t)},

(291)

with bounded spins and such that
F
scatters to some traveling function with distinct poles in Sobolev norm, i.e.

\left|\left|F(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{C_{j}}{x-vt-y_{j}}\right|\right|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\rightarrow 0,

(292)

then, up to a subsequence, there exists a bijection
\phi:\{1,\dots,N\}\to\{1,\dots,N\}
such that each pole
x_{j}
converges to the pole
vt+y_{\phi(j)}
, or more precisely

x_{j}(t)-vt-y_{\phi(j)}\rightarrow 0,

(293)

and the corresponding spin also converges to the corresponding spin

A_{j}(t)\rightarrow C_{\phi(j)}.

(294)

Proof.

We will use the previous theorem, we start by defining the function

G(t,x)=F(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{C_{j}}{x-vt-y_{j}},

(295)

and by assumption

||G(t,x)||_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\rightarrow 0.

(296)

We remark that the corresponding spin to each
vt+y_{j}
is
C_{j}
, which is constant and non-zero, so it does not converge to
0
. Hence, Lemma
C.1
states that the pole can not be isolated, so there exists
\psi(j)
(that will be
\phi^{-1}
) such that

|x_{\psi(j)}-vt-y_{j}|\rightarrow 0.

(297)

\psi
is indeed a bijection. Indeed,
\psi
always chose poles
x_{j}
and not
y_{k}
, because the distances between the
y_{k}
are constant. Also,
\psi
has to be surjective, as any pole
y_{k}
can’t be isolated at
+\infty
. Hence, by cardinality,
\psi
is a bijection and we call
\phi
its inverse.

Now, using a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
C.1
, we regroup poles and obtain by dominated convergence the Vandermonde system up to a subsequence of times,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{A_{\psi(j)}^{\infty}-C_{j}}{y_{j}}\right)y_{j}^{k}=0,

(298)

which gives
A_{j}^{\infty}=C_{\phi(j)}
for all
j
.

∎

Theorem 10
.

Let

\mathbf{m}(t,x)=\mathbf{m}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{A_{j}(t)}{x-x_{j}(t)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{A_{j}^{*}(t)}{x-\bar{x}_{j}(t)},

such that
X(t)=U(t)\left[X(0)+tL(0)\right]U^{-1}(t)
. Then, assuming
A_{j}
bounded, we have

\left|\left|\mathbf{m}(t,x)-\mathbf{m}_{0}\right|\right|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1/2}}\xrightarrow[t\rightarrow 0]{}0\quad\Rightarrow\quad A_{j}(t)\xrightarrow[t\rightarrow 0]{}0.

Proof.

The strategy of the proof will be as follows. First, we write
L(0)
in Jordan form. Then we will combine the arguments used in the case where all eigenvalues are distinct, and in the case when all the eigenvalues are the same. We call
\eta_{i}
the multiplicity of
v_{i}
.

We write for some
P\in GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})
,

L(0)=P\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}P^{-1}=P\begin{bmatrix}J_{1}&\;&\;\\
\;&\ddots&\;\\
\;&\;&J_{p}\end{bmatrix}P^{-1},~J_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{i}&\kappa_{i,1}&\;&\;\\
\;&v_{i}&\ddots&\;\\
\;&\;&\ddots&\kappa_{i,\eta_{i}-1}\\
\;&\;&\;&v_{i}\end{bmatrix}

where for any
i,j
,
\kappa_{i,j}=0
or
\varepsilon
. So,

X(t)=U(t)\left[X(0)+tP\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}P^{-1}\right]U^{-1}(t)=U(t)P\left[P^{-1}X(0)P+t\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}\right]P^{-1}U^{-1}(t).

In particular, the eigenvalues of
X(t)+3tI_{N}
are the eigenvalues of

P^{-1}X(0)P+t\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}+3tI_{N}=K_{0}+t\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}+3tI_{N}.

Since this matrix is diagonal dominant, we have for some
\phi
giving the corresponding bloc,

|x_{j}(t)-v_{\phi(j)}t+C_{j}|\leq|t\varepsilon+D_{j}|,

for some constants
C_{j}
and
D_{j}
, so
|x_{j}-v_{\phi(j)}t|\leq|t\varepsilon+D_{j}|+|C_{j}|\leq t\varepsilon+R
for
R\geq 0
bounded. We define
\sim
the equivalence relation as
j\sim k
if
\phi(j)\sim\phi(k)
. Then, for
[j]\neq[k]
,

|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq\left||x_{i}-x_{j}-tv_{[i]}+tv_{[j]}|-|tv_{[i]}-tv_{[j]}|\right|.

Since
|x_{i}-x_{j}-tv_{[i]}+tv_{[j]}|\leq 2t\varepsilon+2R
, for
\varepsilon<\eta/2
and
t
large enough,

|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq t|v_{[i]}-v_{[j]}|-2t\varepsilon-2R\leq t(\eta-2\varepsilon)-2R.

Hence, for any
i,j
such that
[i]\neq[j]
and
A
and
B
bounded,

\frac{\langle A,B\rangle}{(x_{j}-\bar{x}_{k})^{2}}\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow 0}0.

(299)

Now, we write

f(t,x)+f^{*}(t,x)=\mathbf{m}(t,x)-\mathbf{m}_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{A_{j}}{x-x_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{A_{j}^{*}}{x-\bar{x}_{j}}\\
=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k\sim j}\frac{A_{k}}{x-x_{k}}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k\sim j}\frac{A_{k}^{*}}{x-\bar{x}_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(f_{j}+f_{j}^{*}\right).

(300)

Hence, (
299
) gives in particular for
j\neq k
,

\left\langle f_{j},f_{k}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow 0}0.

Hence,
\langle f,f\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\rightarrow 0
gives in particular

\sum_{j=1}^{p}\langle f_{j},f_{j}\rangle=o(1).

Since all the involved terms are non-negative, we obtain for any
j
,
\langle f_{j},f_{j}\rangle\rightarrow 0
. Using the aforementioned argument when all speeds are equal, regrouping using
i\simeq j
if
x_{i}-x_{j}
is bounded, we obtain
A_{j}\rightarrow 0
.

∎

Appendix D
More characterizations for travelingness

We now define two properties
\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}(t)
. They are local properties and imply travelingness of the poles.

Definition D.1
.

We say that
\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
is satisfied at
t\in\mathbb{R}
if

\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{R},~X(t)\sim X(t)+\alpha[B(t),X(t)].

(
\mathcal{H}_{1}(t)
)

We define also define
\mathcal{H}_{2}(t)
:

\exists v\in\mathbb{R},~\dot{X}(t)=vI_{N}.

(
\mathcal{H}_{2}(t)
)

Lemma D.2
.

We consider
\mathbf{m}
a solution of (
HWM-S
), and define
X
,
L
,
B
correspondingly. Then, if there exists
t_{0}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{1}(t_{0})
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}(t_{0})
are satisfied,
(PT)
is satisfied, so the poles are traveling.

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
t_{0}=0
. Using [refref], we write

X(t)=U(t)^{-1}(tL(0)+X(0))U(t)=U(t)^{-1}(t\dot{X}(0)+t[B(0),X(0)]+X(0))U(t)^{-1}.

(301)

Since
\dot{X}(0)=vI_{N}
commutes with
U(t)
, we obtain

X(t)-vtI_{N}=U(t)(X(0)+t[B(0),X(0)])U(t)^{-1}.

(302)

Since
X(t)-vtI_{N}
is a diagonal matrix, the entries are equal to the eigenvalues of

X(0)+t[B(0),X(0)],

(303)

and
\mathcal{H}_{1}(0)
gives

X(0)+t[B(0),X(0)]\sim X(0)

(304)

for any

. In particular, the characteristic polynomials are identical and we obtain

(305)

so

.

∎

REAL ANALYTIC SL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R}) -ACTIONS ON CLOSED MANIFOLDS
Miri Son

Abstract
. We classify real-analytic SL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R}) -actions on closed manifolds of dimension m m for 3 ≤ n ≤ m ≤ 2 ​ n − 3 3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-3 , which extends Fisher–Melnick’s work for SL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R}) -actions on closed n n -manifolds. Additionally, we classify smooth SL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R}) -actions on closed m m -manifolds that are fixed-point free. As a corollary, we obtain the density or non-density of structural stability of fixed-point free SL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R}) -actions.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we classify real-analytic
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on closed manifolds of dimension
m
, where
3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-3
. The manifolds that arise in this classification are constructed as suspensions or suitable modifications based on all possible homogeneous spaces. We impose the upper bound
m\leq 2n-3
to constrain the variety of possibilities that arise when
m>2n-3
. A more detailed discussion of it is provided in Section
3.1
, especially in Remark
3.3
.

The
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions considered in this classification fall into two distinct types, distinguished by the existence of global fixed points. A global fixed point means a fixed point that is fixed by the
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action on the manifold.

Let
Q\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
denote the stabilizer of a line, and
Q_{2}\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
denote the stabilizer of a 2-plane in the standard representation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Both
Q
and
Q_{2}
are maximal parabolic subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
. In the first type of actions, those without global fixed points, the
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action is induced by the actions of either
Q
or
Q_{2}
on a certain submanifold
\Sigma
of a manifold. These subgroup actions will factor through one-dimensional
\mathbb{R}^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
actions by flows on
\Sigma
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
G\cong\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
acts real-analytically on a closed manifold
M
of dimension
m
, where
3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-3
. Suppose the action is nontrivial and admits no global fixed points. Then the
G
-action on
M
is real-analytically and
G
-equivariantly isomorphic to one of the following:

(1)

The action on manifold is induced by an action of a maximal parabolic subgroup
Q
on a submanifold
\Sigma
, as described in Section
4.1.1
, so that

M=G\times_{Q}\Sigma

which is real-analyitically diffeomorphic to a fiber bundle over either
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}
.

(2)

The action on manifold is induced by an action of a maximal parabolic subgroup
Q_{2}
on a circle
\Sigma_{2}
, as described in Section
4.1.2
, so that

M=G\times_{Q_{2}}\Sigma_{2}

which is real-analytically diffeomorphic to a circle bundle over
\mathrm{Gr}(2,n)
.

(3)

The manifold
M
is a homogeneous space with a transitive
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action, as classified in Theorem
3.1
.

The same classification applies to smooth actions as well.

Remark that the case (1) described in the preceding theorem accounts for all possible non-transitive
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on
m
-manifolds
M
for
3<n\leq m<2n-3
. When the manifold has dimension
2n-3
, both cases (1) and (2) are possible.

The second type of action occurs when the
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action admits fixed points on the manifold. In this case, the action on the manifold is constructed by inducing the action of the identity component
Q^{0}
of
Q
on a certain submanifold as in the previous case, and by attaching copies of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
at the fixed points.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
G\cong\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
acts real-analytically on a closed
m
-manifold
M
, where
3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-3
. Assume that the
G
-action on
M
is non-trivial and has at least one global fixed point. Then the manifold
M
with
G
-action is, up to
G
-equivariant real-analytic isomorphism, constructed by gluing the following:

(1)

a suspension space
G\times_{Q^{0}}(\Sigma^{0}\backslash F)
, which is induced by an action of the identity component
Q^{0}
of a maximal parabolic subgroup
Q
on a certain submanifold
\Sigma^{0}
, except for the
G
-fixed points set
F\subset\Sigma^{0}
, and

(2)

a neighborhood of
F
, which is
G
-equivariantly diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times F
, where
G
acts in the standard way on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and trivially on
F
.

A concrete construction is provided in Section
4.2
.

In the cases where the real-analytic
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action admits no global fixed points, the real-analytic action can be replaced by a smooth one resulting in the same classification. However, when the
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action on the manifold admits global fixed points, the proof in this paper does not apply to the smooth actions due to the absence of a smooth linearization, see the discussion in Section
2.3
and Question
2.5
.

Density of Structural Stability

The main theorem concerning smooth
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on manifolds without global fixed points leads to further remarks on the density of structural stability. A vector field is said to
structurally

stable
if small perturbations in the
C^{r}
-topology do not change the topological structure of its solution curves. Since the
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on manifolds are determined by a smooth vector field
X
on
\Sigma
or
\Sigma_{2}
, the density of structurally stable vector fields on
\Sigma
or
\Sigma_{2}
implies the density of structurally stable
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on manifolds. We prove the following; see Section
6
for a detailed discussion.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose that
G\cong\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
acts smoothly on a closed
m
-dimensional manifold
M
, where
3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-3
. Assume the
G
-action does not admit global fixed points. Then the following hold:

(1)

Case (1) with
m=n
or Case (2) of Theorem
1.1
, the space of structurally stable
C^{r}
-diffeomorphic
G
-actions on manifolds
M
is dense for
r\geq 1
.

(2)

Case (1) with
m=n+1
of Theorem
1.1
, then the space of structurally stable
C^{r}
-diffeomorphic
G
-actions on manifold
M
is dense for
r=1
and not dense for
r>1
.

(3)

Case (1) with
m>n+1
of Theorem
1.1
, then the space of structurally stable
C^{r}
-diffeomorphic
G
-actions on manifolds
M
is not dense for
r\geq 1
.

(4)

Case (3) of Theorem
1.1
, then the actions cannot be deformed.

1.1.
Previous work on simple Lie group actions on manifolds

We briefly review previous classification results of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on manifolds.

In low-dimensional cases, Schneider provided a classification of real analytic actions of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
on closed surfaces and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
[
14
]
.

Uchida classified real-analytic actions of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on the standard
n
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
in
[
16
]
. This was later extended to the standard
m
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{m}
for
5\leq n\leq m<2n-1
in
[
17
]
. A key aspect of these works is the use of local linearizability, which examines whether actions of semisimple Lie groups near fixed points are locally conjugate to their linear actions on the tangent space. The linearization theorem for real-analytic actions of semisimple Lie groups was established by Guillenmin–Sternberg
[
9
]
and Kushnirenko
[
11
]
.

Building on Uchida’s work, Fisher–Melnick achieved a complete classification of both real-analytic and smooth actions of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on closed
n
-dimensional manifolds, as presented in
[
8
]
. Their extension to smooth actions was made possible by the result of Cairns–Ghys in
[
6
]
, which established smooth linearizability of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action. Moreover, Cairns and Ghys classified all orbit types of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on
n
-dimensional manifolds in the same paper, which made it possible for Fisher and Melnick to generalize the classification from
n
-spheres to arbitrary closed
n
-dimensional manifolds.

In this paper, we extend these classification results to real-analytic actions of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on higher-dimensional manifolds, those of dimension greater than
n
. Two key ingredients in our approach are the linearization theorem for real-analytic
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions in Section
2.3
and a new orbit classification, presented in Section
3.1
.

1.2.
Rigidity and Zimmer program

The main motivation of this work comes from the Zimmer program: Actions of higher-rank semisimple Lie groups and their lattices on closed manifolds arise from algebraic constructions. If the manifold dimension is smaller than the rank of the group, the action is very restricted. This is the famous Zimmer’s conjecture proved by Brown–Fisher–Hurtado. They showed that actions of lattices in
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on manifolds of dimension at most
n-2
and volume-preserving actions on manifolds of dimension
n-1
should factor through finite group actions, in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Their result was extended to several other semisimple Lie groups, each with corresponding dimension bounds in
[
4
]
.

Brown–Rodriguez-Hertz–Wang completed
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action classification without the volume-preserving assumption through dimension
n-1
. An action of a lattice in
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on an
(n-1)
-dimensional manifold either factors through a finite group or extends to an
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-action up to finite cover in
[
5
]
. Note that there are exactly two faithful actions of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on
(n-1)
-manifolds; the actions on
\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}
and the lift of that action to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. For additional context on the Zimmer program, see, for example, the survey
[
7
]
.

For manifolds of dimension
n
, even actions of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
are complicated with several examples illustrating their complexity. Fisher–Melnick
[
8
]
provided a complete classification of both real analytic and smooth
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on closed
n
-dimensional manifolds. For lattice actions, several exotic examples exist. One well-known is the construction by Katok–Lewis
[
10
]
, in which
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
-actions that are not conjugate to algebraic actions, are constructed by blowing up the fixed point at the origin on the torus
\mathbf{T}^{n}
. Fisher–Melnick also introduced another exotic example in their paper. However, a complete classification of lattice actions of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
remains open.

Conjecture 1

(
[
8
, Conjecture 3.6]
)
.

Let
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
be a lattice and
M
a compact manifold of dimension
n
. For any smooth action
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow
Diff(
M
), one of the following holds:

(1)

\rho
extends to an action of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
or
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})}
,

(2)

\rho
factors through a finite quotient of
\Gamma
, or

(3)

\rho
is built from tori,
G
-tubes,
G
-disks, blow-ups, and two-sided blow-ups, with
\Gamma
a finite-index subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
.

For further details and relevant definitions, we refer to Fisher–Melnick
[
8
]
. One of the next natural questions concerns the case where the dimension of manifolds is greater than
n
. The main result of this paper lays the groundwork for the Zimmer program, which explores lattice actions of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on higher-dimensional manifolds, as
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on manifolds classified in this paper naturally restrict to actions of its lattice.

2.2.
Suspension space

We introduce the concept of a suspension, also known as an induced module in representation theory. It is a useful construction of extending an action
\alpha
of a subgroup
H\leq G
on a space
M
to a larger group
G
acting on an associated bundle
M^{\alpha}
, as described below.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of a Lie group
G
, and assume that
H
acts continuously on a topological space
M
. Consider the space
G\times M
, where we define two actions: the right
H
-action and the left
G
-action

(g,x)\cdot h=(gh,\alpha(h^{-1})(x)),~g^{\prime}\cdot(g,x)=(g^{\prime}g,x),

where
g,g^{\prime}\in G,x\in M
and
h\in H
.

We define the quotient space
M^{\alpha}:=(G\times M)/H
, which has a fiber bundle structure over
G/H
with fibers homeomorphic to
M
. For any
(g,x)\in G\times M
, we denote the equivalence class in
M^{\alpha}
by
[g,x]
. Since the
G
-action on
G\times M
commutes with the
H
-action, this gives rise to an induced left
G
-action on
M^{\alpha}
by left translation on the first factor. Note that the
G
-action preserves the fibers of
M^{\alpha}
.

2.3.
Linearization results

In this subsection, we recall the previous results for linearization. When a group

at

For the compact group actions, we have the linearization result of Bochner–Cartan. Here, we consider group actions on
(\mathbb{R}^{m},0)
where the origin

is fixed by the action.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
1
]
)

For all

, every

-action of a compact group on

-linearizable.

For non-compact group actions, Guillemin–Sternberg
[
9
]
and Kushinirenko
[

]
proved that a real-analytic action of a semisimple Lie group

is linearizable near any fixed point. Using this theorem, Uchida classified all analytic
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on

for

in
[

]
, and on
S^{m}
for

in
[

]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[

,
11
]
)
.

Every analytic action of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on
(\mathbb{R}^{m},0)

Later, Cairns–Ghys established a smooth linearization result for

-actions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[

]
to classify not only real analytic but also smooth
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})

Theorem 2.4

(
[

]
)

For all

3.
The orbit types and Fixed points

Throughout this section, we fix

.
We investigate the orbit types and global fixed points of

-actions, both of which play a key role in constructing actions and proving the main results.

3.1.
The extended orbit classification

We extend the orbit classification by considering all possible homogeneous spaces arising from
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-actions on
m
-dimensional manifolds, where
m\leq 2n-3
. No new orbit types appear until the dimension reaches

. When

, a new orbit
\mathrm{Gr}(2,n)
, the Grassmannian of

-planes in

, arises. When

, fundamentally different types of orbits come up, see Remark

for details. Let
Q\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
denote the stabilizer of a line in the standard representation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
Q_{2}\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
denote the stabilizer of a 2-plane in the standard representation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G\cong\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
act continuously on a topological
m
-dimensional manifold
M
, with
m\leq 2n-3
. For any
x\in M
, the orbit
G.x
is equivariantly homeomorphic to one of the following:

(1)

a point;

(2)

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}
with the projective action;

(3)

\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
with the restricted linear action or
(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})/\Lambda
, for
\Lambda
a discrete subgroup of the group of scalars
\mathbb{R}^{*}
;

(4)

\mathrm{Gr}(2,n)
, the real Grassmannian of
2
-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(5)

a suspension space
G\times_{Q_{2}}\mathbb{R}
or
(G\times_{Q_{2}}\mathbb{R})/\Lambda
, where
Q_{2}
acts on
\mathbb{R}
via a homeomorphism factoring through
\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
\Lambda
a discrete subgroup of the group of scalars
\mathbb{R}^{*}
;

(6)

\mathcal{F}^{n}_{1,2}
, the variety of partial flags
V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\mathrm{dim}(V_{1})=1
and
\mathrm{dim}(V_{2})=2
;

(7)

finitely many
\mathrm{SL}(4,\mathbb{R})
-action on
5
-dimensional homogeneous spaces arising from reductive subgroups, see Remark

.

Proof.

Suppose that a homogeneous space
\mathcal{O}_{x}=G.x
is an orbit of
G
-action on a manifold, which is identified with
G/G_{x}
where
G_{x}\leq G
is a closed subgroup.

If the orbit has dimension less than or equal to
n
, then it is one of the cases (1), (2), or (3) by Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{x}
be a
k
-dimensional
G
-orbit with
n<k\leq m
. Consider the complexified representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
denotes the Lie algebra of
G_{x}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}
is a reductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
with the codimension
k
. By taking a suitable real form of
\mathfrak{g}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}
, we obtain a compact subgroup
K
in
\mathrm{SU}(n)
whose real codimension is
k
. However, there is no compact subgroup
K
of
\mathrm{SU}(n)
with codimension
k
satisfying
4<n<k<2n-2
. This is because the highest-dimensional proper compact subgroup of
\mathrm{SU}(n)
is
\mathrm{U}(n-1)
, which has codimension
2n-2
, when
n>4
, see below Lemma
3.2
. When
n=4
, there exist homogeneous spaces of dimension
5
, which correspond to case (7).

Now assume that
\mathfrak{g}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}
is not a reductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
, which implies that the isotropy representation on
V=(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{x})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{k}
is reducible. There exists a nontrivial invariant complex subspace
W
of dimension
p
, for some
0<p<k
. The stabilizer of such a
p
-dimensional subspace has codimension
p(n-p)
, so that
0<p(n-p)\leq k
. Under the assumptions
4\leq n<k\leq 2n-3
, the only possible values for
p
are
p=1
,
p=2
,
p=n-2
or
p=n-1
. For
p=n-1
or
p=n-2
, replacing
G_{x}
by the outer automorphism
g\mapsto(g^{-1})^{t}
reduces the problem to the case
p=1
or
p=2
.

Consider
p=1
with the invariant complex line
W
. If
W\cap\overline{W}=0
,
\mathfrak{g}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}
preserves the flag
W\subset U=W\oplus\overline{W}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The stabilizer of such a flag has codimension
2n-3
, and assume that it equals
\mathfrak{g}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}
based on our dimension assumptions. The intersection
U_{0}=U\cap\overline{U}
is a real
2
-dimensional and
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
-invariant. The
\mathfrak{g}_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}
-invariant decomposition
U=W\oplus\overline{W}
defines an
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
-invariant complex structure on
U_{0}
as the following: for any
u\in U
, there is a unique
w\in W
such that

u=\frac{1}{2}(w+\overline{w}).

Then,

J(u):=\frac{i}{2}(\overline{w}-w)

defines

The following lemma represents the smallest possible dimension for orbits arising from a reductive subgroup.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
19
]
)
.

The highest-dimensional proper compact subgroup of
\mathrm{SU}(n)
is
\mathrm{U}(n-1)
when
n>4
.

Proof.

Consider elements of
\mathrm{SU}(n)
as
n\times n
unitary matrices. Recall that
\mathrm{SU}(n)
is a reductive group. Let
H
be a reductive proper subgroup of maximal possible dimension with a faithful representation
V
of dimension
n
. We claim that, up to conjugacy,
H
is
\mathrm{U}(n-1)
which has dimension
(n-1)^{2}+1
.

Suppose first that
V
decomposes as a direct sum of two subrepresentations of dimension
a
and
b
, with
a+b=n
. Then the dimension of
H
is at most
a^{2}+b^{2}
which is less than or equal to
(n-1)^{2}+1
. This implies that
V
must be irreducible, except possibly in the case
a=1
.

Next, suppose that
V
is isomorphic to a tensor product
V=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
, where
\mathrm{dim}V_{1}=a
,
\mathrm{dim}V_{2}=b
, and
ab=n
. Then the dimension of
H
is at most
a^{2}+b^{2}
, and the maximal value of
a^{2}+b^{2}
is
(n/2)^{2}+(n/2)^{2}=n^{2}/2
, which is strictly less than
(n-1)^{2}+1
for all
n>4
. Hence,
V
cannot be a form of tensor product of irreducible representations.

Since
V
is an irreducible representation that is not a tensor product of lower-dimensional irreducible representations,
H
is a simple compact Lie group with the minimal dimension of faithful representation
V
which is well-known. Thus,
H=U(n-1)
of dimension
(n-1)^{2}+1
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The above lemma shows that new orbit types appear, such as

For

3.2.
The Fixed Points

Consider a
G
-action on a closed manifold
M
. Let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
. There exists a
K
-invariant Riemannian metric
\kappa
on the closed manifold
M
, which is obtained by averaging any Riemannian metric on
M
over
K
with respect to the Haar measure.

By applying the linearization theorems for compact group action in Theorem
2.2
and using the orbit classification in Section
3.1
, we obtain information about the fixed points of
G
-action.

Proposition 3.5
.

For a nontrivial real-analytic or smooth action of
G\cong\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on a connected
m
-dimensional manifold
M
for
3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-3
, the fixed points set of
G
-action is a finite union of closed
(m-n)
-dimensional submanifolds.

Proof.

Let
x\in M
be a
G
-fixed point. If the isotropy representation of
G
at
x
is trivial, then actions of the maximal compact subgroup
K
are trivial in a neighborhood of
x
via the exponential map of
\kappa
. Then the
K
-action is trivial on all of
M
, and so is
G
.

Thus, the isotropy representation of
G
at
x
is nontrivial. By Theorem
2.2
, any
K
-action on a neighborhood at
K
-fixed points is linearizable via the exponential map with respect to the metric
\kappa
. This representation can be identified with the standard representation of
K
, which is one of the canonical representations in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Under this representation, the set of
K
-fixed points has dimension
(m-n)
where
3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-1
.

We now claim that the set of
K
-fixed points coincides with the set of
G
-fixed points. Suppose not, then there exists a
K
-fixed point
y
that is not fixed under the
G
-action. In this case, the stabilizer
G_{y}
of
y
contains
K
. However, by the orbit classification theorems in Section
3.1
, where
3\leq n\leq m\leq 2n-3
, the point stabilizer is conjugate to parabolic subgroups or its subgroups, none of which can contain a subgroup isomorphic to
K
. This contradiction establishes the claim.

Since
K
acts by isometries, the fixed-point set forms closed, totally geodesic submanifolds with respect to
\kappa
. Moreover, since
M
is compact, the fixed-point set consists of a finite union of connected components.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

There are two nontrivial irreducible
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-representations on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, up to conjugation: the standard representation
\rho
, and the dual representation, defined by
\rho^{*}(g)=\rho((g^{-1})^{t})
. Under
\rho
, there is a
Q
-invariant line, whereas there is no
Q
-invariant line under

for

, the only nontrivial

-representations on

are

or

Recall that a maximal parabolic subgroup

Peixoto in

]

(
[

]
)

Theorem 6.2

(
[
15
]

For

Peskine sixfolds and Debarre–Voisin fourfolds with associated cubic fourfolds
Corey Brooke, Laure Flapan, Sarah Frei, Lisa Marquand

Abstract
. We develop the notion of Peskine sixfolds with associated K3 surfaces and cubic fourfolds and work out numerical conditions for when these associations occur. In discriminant 24 24 , the first family for which there is an associated cubic fourfold, we identify the cubic explicitly. Moreover, we prove that in this case the Fano variety of lines of the cubic fourfold is isomorphic to the associated Debarre–Voisin hyperkähler fourfold.



1.
Introduction

There is a close connection between Fano varieties of K3 type and hyperkähler manifolds of K3
[n]
-type, most famously exhibited by a cubic fourfold and its Fano variety of lines
[
4
]
.
More recently, there have been numerous similar constructions
[
12
,
9
,
19
,
22
,
21
,
13
,
7
]
.
Among these, the Debarre–Voisin hyperkähler fourfold introduced in
[
12
]
remains somewhat mysterious.
We recall the definition: given a
10
-dimensional complex vector space
V_{10}
and a trivector
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}
, the Debarre–Voisin manifold is defined by the degeneracy condition

X_{6}^{\sigma}=\{[V_{6}]\in\mathrm{Gr}(6,V_{10})\;|\;\sigma|_{V_{6}}=0\}.

When
\sigma
is general, this
X_{6}^{\sigma}
is a hyperkähler fourfold.
Initially, Debarre–Voisin observed that
X_{6}^{\sigma}
was (rationally) Hodge-theoretically associated to a 20-dimensional hypersurface in
\mathrm{Gr}(3,V_{10})
.
Later, an integral Hodge-theoretic association between
X_{6}^{\sigma}
and the so-called Peskine sixfold

X_{1}^{\sigma}=\{[V_{1}]\in\mathbb{P}(V_{10})\;|\;\mathrm{rank}(\sigma(V_{1},-,-))\leq 6\},

a Fano variety of K3 type, was described
[
15
,
6
]
.
Here, we investigate divisors in the moduli space of trivectors
\sigma
for which
X_{6}^{\sigma}
and
X_{1}^{\sigma}
acquire Hodge-theoretic associations to K3 surfaces or cubic fourfolds; when such associations appear, it is natural to expect the birational geometry of
X_{6}^{\sigma}
and of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
to be richer.

In the case of cubic fourfolds for instance, Hassett divisors
\mathcal{C}_{d}
in the moduli space of cubic fourfolds parametrize those cubic fourfolds containing a surface not homologous to a complete intersection, where
d
is the discriminant of the saturated lattice spanned by the class of the surface and the square of the hyperplane class.
Hassett described the values of
d
for which the cubic fourfolds
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{d}
have associated K3 surfaces, meaning that the transcendental lattice
T_{Y}\subset H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
is Hodge isometric to
T_{S}(-1)
for some K3 surface
S

[
16
]
.
For such a
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{d}
, the hyperkähler fourfold given by the Fano variety of lines
F(Y)
on
Y
is always birational to a moduli space of stable sheaves on
S

[
1
]
.
Further, in this case, the cubic fourfold
Y
is expected to be rational
[
16
,
20
,
1
,
3
]
.

Here we study Peskine sixfolds
X_{1}^{\sigma}
and their corresponding Debarre–Voisin hyperkähler fourfolds
X_{6}^{\sigma}
in analogy with the case of cubic fourfolds
Y
and their corresponding hyperkähler fourfolds
F(Y)
.
Towards this end, we study divisors
\mathcal{P}_{d}
in the moduli space of trivectors
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee}
yielding marked Peskine sixfolds
X_{1}^{\sigma}
or marked Debarre–Voisin fourfolds
X_{6}^{\sigma}
of discriminant
d
.
We formalize the notion of such
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
having an associated K3 surface or an associated cubic fourfold and establish the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee}
be a very general trivector of discriminant d. Then:

(1)

\sigma
has an associated K3 surface if and only if
d
satisfies the numerical conditions of Proposition
4.4
, and

(2)

\sigma
has an associated cubic fourfold
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{d}
if and only if
d
satisfies the numerical conditions of Proposition
4.7
.

Along the way to proving Theorem
1.1
, we determine orbit representatives for the possible markings for smooth special Peskine varieties, see Proposition
3.7
.

The first divisor
\mathcal{P}_{d}
parametrizing trivectors with an associated cubic fourfold occurs when
d=24
.
When the trivector
\sigma
is general in this divisor, the Peskine
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has exactly one isolated singular point. More specifically, there is a unique flag
W_{1}\subset W_{6}\subset V_{10}
with
\sigma(W_{1},W_{6},V_{10})=0
, and
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is singular at
\mathbb{P}(W_{1})

[
6
]
.
Further, Benedetti–Song show that such a Peskine variety
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has an associated twisted K3 surface
(S,\alpha)
with
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(S)
.
A main result of
[
5
]
is that when the Brauer class on this K3 surface
S
vanishes, the Peskine variety
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is rational.
More recently,
[
17
]
studied the case of special cubic fourfolds of discriminant
d=24
, and showed that the obstruction to rationality is again governed by a twisted K3 surface.
Thus, it is natural to ask whether there is a geometric relationship between a Peskine
X_{1}^{\sigma}
with
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{24}
and its associated cubic fourfold
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{24}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee}
be a very general trivector of discriminant 24. Then:

(1)

Y:=\mathbb{P}(W_{6})\cap X_{1}^{\sigma}
is a smooth cubic fourfold of discriminant
24
,

(2)

the Fano variety of lines
F(Y)
is isomorphic to the Debarre–Voisin variety
X_{6}^{\sigma}
, and

(3)

Y
is Hodge-theoretically associated to
\sigma
.

2.
Preliminaries

Fixing a ten dimensional complex vector space
V_{10}
, a trivector
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee}
defines the following varieties, given by degeneracy conditions:

•

Peskine variety:

X_{1}^{\sigma}:=\{[V_{1}]\in\mathbb{P}(V_{10})\mid\mathrm{rank}(\sigma(V_{1},-,-))\leq 6\}.
The variety
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has expected dimension 6 and is in general smooth.

•

Debarre–Voisin variety:

X_{6}^{\sigma}:=\{[V_{6}]\in\mathrm{Gr}(6,V_{10})\mid\sigma|_{V_{6}}=0\}.
This has expected dimension 4, and for general
\sigma
is a hyperkähler manifold of K3
[2]
-type
[
12
]
.

The GIT quotient

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}:=\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee})\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{SL}(V_{10})

serves as a moduli space parametrizing both Peskine varieties and Debarre–Voisin varieties.

2.1.
The period map

Letting
\sigma
be general so that the variety
X_{6}^{\sigma}
is indeed a hyperkähler fourfold of K3
[2]
-type, the lattice
(H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z}),q)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{L}
, where

\mathbb{L}:=U^{3}\oplus E_{8}^{2}\oplus\langle-2\rangle.

There is a polarization
H\in\mathrm{NS}(X_{6}^{\sigma})
satisfying
q(H)=22
and
q(H,x)\in 2\mathbb{Z}
for all
x\in H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})

[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
.
We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{22}^{(2)}
the coarse moduli space of K3
[2]
-type manifolds with a polarization of square 22 and divisibility 2.
Recall that we define the divisibility of
v\in L
for a lattice
L
to be the minimal generator of the ideal
v\cdot L\subset\mathbb{Z}
; we denote this by
\mathrm{div}_{L}(v).

There is a unique orbit of primitive elements
h\in\mathbb{L}
with
q_{\mathbb{L}}(h)=22
and
\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{L}}(h)=2
; we fix such an
h
.
For any
X_{6}^{\sigma}
, we fix a labeling
\phi:H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{L}
such that
\phi(H)=h
, yielding a period map

\mathscr{P}:\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{22}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}/\Gamma,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathscr{P}([\sigma])=[\phi(H^{2,0}(X_{6}^{\sigma}))],

where

\mathbb{D}:=\{x\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{L}\otimes\mathbb{C})\mid x^{2}=x\cdot h=0,x\cdot\bar{x}>0\}

and
\Gamma=\mathrm{O}(\mathbb{L},h)
.

Note that the map
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{22}^{(2)}
is birational
[
28
, Theorem 1.8]
.
Further, the period map
\mathscr{P}
is resolved by considering the Baily-Borel projective compactification
\overline{\mathbb{D}/\Gamma}
of
\mathbb{D}/\Gamma
to obtain a diagram:

\textstyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}}
\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathbb{D}/\Gamma}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathscr{P}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{D}/\Gamma,\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where
\varepsilon
is a birational morphism.
Let
v\in\langle h\rangle^{\perp}
be a class of negative square.
The Heegner divisor associated to
v
is the image in
\mathbb{D}/\Gamma
of the hypersurface:

\{x\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{L}\otimes\mathbb{C})\mid x^{2}=x\cdot h=x\cdot v=0,x\cdot\bar{x}>0\}.

Letting
N:=\langle h,v\rangle\subset\mathbb{L}
, this divisor is equal to the image of
\mathbb{P}(N^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{C})\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{L}\otimes\mathbb{C})
in
\mathbb{D}/\Gamma
.
The discriminant of
N^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{L}
is a positive even integer
d
; we denote this divisor by
\mathcal{D}_{d}\subset\mathbb{D}/\Gamma
and label the lattice
N_{d}:=N
.

The inverse image via the period map of the Heegner divisor
\mathcal{D}_{d}
inside
\mathcal{M}_{22}^{(2)}
is a Noether-Lefschetz divisor
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{d}\subset\mathcal{M}_{22}^{(2)}
parametrizing smooth hyperkähler fourfolds
X
with Picard rank at least 2 and a marking
N_{d}\subset\mathrm{NS}(X)
.
Note that for a general hyperkähler fourfold
X\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{d}
, we have
T(X)\cong N_{d}^{\perp}.

Remark 2.1
.

Some divisors
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{d}
are not in the image of the map
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{22}^{(2)},
meaning that the corresponding hyperkähler fourfolds are not constructed from a trivector
\sigma
.
These are the inverse images of the HLS divisors on
\overline{\mathbb{D}/\Gamma}
, introduced in
[
10
]
.
More precisely, an HLS divisor is an irreducible hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{D}/\Gamma}
which is the image by
\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}
of an exceptional divisor of
\varepsilon
, so in particular has inverse image in
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
of codimension greater than 1.
By
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
26
, Corollary 1]
, and
[
29
, Corollary 6.5]
, the Heegner divisor
\mathcal{D}_{d}
is an HLS divisor if and only if
d\in\{2,6,8,10,18\}
.

2.2.
The cohomology of
X_{1}^{\sigma}

Let
X_{1}^{\sigma}
be a smooth Peskine sixfold.
We recall some basic facts about the cohomology of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
from
[
6
, Section 2.6]
.
The middle cohomology
H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
equipped with the intersection pairing is a unimodular odd lattice of signature
(22,2)
, so it is isomorphic to
I_{22,2}:=\langle 1\rangle^{22}\oplus\langle-1\rangle^{2}
.
We fix an isomorphism
H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\cong I_{22,2}
throughout.

By
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
, the integral Hodge conjecture holds for
X_{1}^{\sigma}
in all degrees.
In particular, classes
\tau\in H^{3,3}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
are represented by algebraic cycles.
There are always two distinguished classes in the middle algebraic cohomology of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
: the cube
h^{3}
of the hyperplane class and the class
\pi
of a Palatini threefold, obtained as the linear section
X_{1}^{\sigma}\cap\mathbb{P}(V_{6})
for any
[V_{6}]\in X_{6}^{\sigma}

[
15
]
.
Recall that a Palatini threefold is a smooth degree 7 threefold in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
, which is a scroll over a smooth cubic surface.
These classes span the sublattice

\Lambda_{11}:=\begin{pmatrix}15&7\\
7&4\end{pmatrix}\subset I_{22,2}.

The vanishing cohomology of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is defined as

\Lambda_{van}:=H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{van}}:=\Lambda_{11}^{\perp}\subset H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z}).

It is a polarized integral Hodge structure of K3 type with discriminant 11.

An incidence correspondence referred to as the
universal Palatini variety
relates the vanishing cohomology of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
to the primitive cohomology of
X_{6}^{\sigma}
:

Theorem 2.2
.

[
6
, Theorem 2.16]

For a trivector
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee}
such that both
X_{6}^{\sigma}
and
X_{1}^{\sigma}
are smooth, there is an isomorphism of polarized integral Hodge structures

\phi:H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{van}\rightarrow H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{prim}(-1).

3.
Special Peskine sixfolds

We continue to fix a 10-dimensional complex vector space
V_{10}
, and let
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee}
be a trivector for which
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has the expected dimension.
In this section, we define marked and special Peskine sixfolds
X_{1}^{\sigma}
and trivectors
\sigma
.
We then classify the possible markings for special Peskine sixfolds.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X_{1}^{\sigma}
be a smooth Peskine sixfold.
We say that
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is a
marked
Peskine sixfold of
discriminant

d
if there exists a saturated rank-3 sublattice
M_{d}
of discriminant
d
with

\Lambda_{11}\subset M_{d}\subset H^{3,3}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{C})\cap H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z}),

and we call
M_{d}
a
marking
of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
.

In particular, a very general marked Peskine sixfold
X_{1}^{\sigma}
of discriminant
d
has a unique marking, given by
M_{d}=H^{3,3}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{C})\cap H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
.
Due to the Hodge isometry in Theorem
2.2
,

H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\supset M_{d}^{\perp}\cong T(X_{1}^{\sigma})\cong T(X_{6}^{\sigma})(-1)\cong N_{d}^{\perp}\subset H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z}),

so the image of
\sigma
under the period map lies in the Heegner divisor
\mathcal{D}_{d}\subset\mathbb{D}/\Gamma
.
It follows that the preimage of the Heegner divisor
\mathcal{D}_{d}
under the period map, which we denote by

\mathcal{P}_{d}:=\mathscr{P}^{-1}(\mathcal{D}_{d})\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma},

has general points parametrizing marked Peskine sixfolds of discriminant
d
.

Remark 3.2
.

Whenever
\mathcal{D}_{d}
is not a HLS-divisor,
\mathcal{P}_{d}
is a non-empty divisor (see Remark
2.1
). In particular, all
\mathcal{P}_{d}
are divisors for
22\leq d
.

In
[
6
, Proposition 2.9]
, the authors identify two divisors
D^{1,6,10},D^{3,3,10}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
that together parametrize the singular Peskine sixfolds.
Further, they proved that the period map
\mathscr{P}
maps
D^{1,6,10}
birationally onto
\mathcal{D}_{24}\subset\mathbb{D}/\Gamma
and
D^{3,10,10}
birationally onto
\mathcal{D}_{22}\subset\mathbb{D}/\Gamma
. We thus set

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{22}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{D}^{3,10,10}\simeq\mathscr{P}^{-1}(\mathcal{D}_{22}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{24}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{D}^{1,6,10}\simeq\mathscr{P}^{-1}(\mathcal{D}_{24}).

This allows us to consider singular Peskine sixfolds when making the following definition:

Definition 3.3
.

A
special Peskine sixfold of discriminant
d
is a Peskine sixfold
X_{1}^{\sigma}
such that
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}.

In this case, we also say that the trivector
\sigma\in\bigwedge^{3}V_{10}^{\vee}
is
special of discriminant
d
.

For
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus(\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24})
, the special Peskine sixfold
X_{1}^{\sigma}
admits a marking of discriminant
d
.
However, for
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24},
the Peskine sixfold
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is singular.
In these two cases, it is not clear whether
X_{1}^{\sigma}
admits a marking since
H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
may not be a pure Hodge structure.

Remark 3.4
.

Since the Debarre–Voisin variety associated to
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{24}
is smooth
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
and
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has only isolated singularities
[
6
, Proposition 2.9]
, we wonder whether the incidence correspondence of
[
6
, Theorem 2.16]
can be extended to prove that the Hodge structure on

remains pure.
Alternatively, one could hope that the singularity of

is suitably mild enough (as in the case of cubic fourfolds with
ADE
-singularities) to ensure the purity.
Computer algebra systems such as Magma and Macaulay2 were unable to compute invariants of the singularity.

To complete our terminology, we make the following definition:

Definition 3.5
.

A
marked trivector

\sigma

of discriminant

d
is a trivector
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{22}
and an embedding
H\in N_{d}\subset\mathrm{NS}(X_{6}^{\sigma}),
where

is a saturated rank 2 lattice of discriminant

.

Note that we omit
\mathcal{P}_{22}
from the definition because in that case, both

and

are singular.
When
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus(\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24})
, the Debarre–Voisin fourfold
X_{6}^{\sigma}
and the Peskine sixfold
X_{1}^{\sigma}
both have markings with the same discriminant
d
and the same discriminant form.

We recall restrictions on the discriminants of Peskine sixfolds:

Lemma 3.6
.

[
11
, Proposition 4.1]

If
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is a smooth special Peskine of discriminant
d
, then
d>0
, and
d\equiv 0,2,6,8,10
or
18\bmod 22
.

To build on this result, we classify the markings of special Peskine sixfolds, similar to the analogous result for Gushel-Mukai fourfolds from

9

.
Let

\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{van})=\{g\in\mathrm{O}(I_{22,2})\;|\;g|_{\Lambda_{11}}=\mathrm{id}\},

and for each

, let
\mathcal{O}_{d}
be the set of orbits for the action of
\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{van})
on the set of primitive, positive definite, rank 3 sublattices

.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
X_{1}^{\sigma}
be a smooth special Peskine sixfold of discriminant
d
. For each integer
d>0
with
d\equiv 0,2,6,8,10
, or
18\bmod 22
, the set of orbits
\mathcal{O}_{d}
consists of a single element, which is represented by

M_{d}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}15&7&a\\
7&4&b\\
a&b&c\end{pmatrix},

where

(a)

if
d\equiv 0\bmod 22
, then
(a,b,c)=(0,0,d/11)
;

(b)

if
d\equiv 2\bmod 22
, then
(a,b,c)=(3,1,(d+9)/11)
;

(c)

if
d\equiv 6\bmod 22
, then
(a,b,c)=(1,1,(d+5)/11)
;

(d)

if
d\equiv 8\bmod 22
, then
(a,b,c)=(2,1,(d+3)/11)
;

(e)

if
d\equiv 10\bmod 22
, then
(a,b,c)=(3,2,(d+12)/11)

(f)

(a,b,c)=(1,0,(d+4)/11)

Proof.

Suppose that
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is a smooth Peskine sixfold of discriminant
d
with a marking
M_{d}
.
Let
w
be a generator of the rank
1

M_{van}:=M_{d}\cap\Lambda_{van}
.
By Eichler’s criterion, recalled in
[
14
, Lemma 3.5]
, the orbit of a primitive vector

under the action of
\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{van})
is determined by
w^{2}
and the image
w_{*}=w/\mathrm{div}_{\Lambda_{van}}w
of
w
in the discriminant group
D(\Lambda_{van}):=\Lambda_{van}^{\vee}/\Lambda_{van}
.

First, we consider the case
\mathrm{div}_{\Lambda_{van}}(w)=1
, so that the class
w_{*}\in D(\Lambda_{van})
is trivial.
Let
w^{\prime}\in M_{d}
.
We can choose coprime integers
a
,
b
, and
c
so that (in the notation of Section
2.2
)

ah^{3}+b\pi+cw=mw^{\prime}

for some integer
m
.
Since
\mathrm{div}_{\Lambda_{van}}(w)=1
, there is some class
w^{\prime\prime}\in\Lambda_{van}
for which
w\cdot w^{\prime\prime}=1
, and pairing the equation above with
w^{\prime\prime}
yields
c=m(w^{\prime}\cdot w^{\prime\prime})
.
So then

ah^{3}+b\pi=m(w^{\prime}-(w^{\prime}\cdot w^{\prime\prime})w).

In particular,
m
divides
a
,
b
, and
c
.
Since
a,b
and
c
are coprime, we must have
m=1
and
w^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{11}\oplus\mathbb{Z}w
.
It follows that
M_{d}=\Lambda_{11}\oplus\mathbb{Z}w
.

To complete the proof of (a), it remains to find a class
w\in\Lambda_{van}
with
\mathrm{div}_{\Lambda_{van}}(w)=1
and
w^{2}=2k
for each integer
k
.
For this, choose a hyperbolic factor
U
of
\Lambda_{van}
with standard generators
u
and
v
, and take
w=u+kv
.

Now, consider the case
\mathrm{div}_{\Lambda_{van}}(w)=11
.
Then
0\neq w_{*}\in D(\Lambda_{van})
, so
w_{*}
generates
D(\Lambda_{van})\cong\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}
, and the index of
\Lambda_{11}\oplus\mathbb{Z}w
in

is

.
In particular,
w^{2}=11d
where
d
is the discriminant of
M_{d}
.
Since
w
is only determined up to multiplication by
\pm 1
, we consider five of the ten generators of
D(\Lambda_{van})
separately.

If under the gluing isomorphism
D(\Lambda_{van})\overset{\sim}{\to}D(\Lambda_{11})
,
w_{*}\mapsto[\frac{h^{3}+\pi}{11}]
, then
\tau=\frac{h^{3}+\pi+w}{11}\in I_{22,2}
.
From the fact that

\tau^{2}=\frac{33+w^{2}}{11^{2}}=\frac{3+d}{11}

is an integer, we find
d\equiv 8\bmod 11
.
Moreover,
\tau\in M_{d}
, and
\Lambda_{11}\oplus\mathbb{Z}w
has index
11
in
M_{d}
.
Thus
M_{d}
is generated by
h^{3}
,
\pi
, and
\tau
, and calculating the intersection form on
M_{d}
yields the representative in (d).

More generally, if
w_{*}\mapsto\left[\frac{k(h^{3}+\pi)}{11}\right]
for
1\leq k\leq 5
, then
\tau=\frac{k(h^{3}+\pi)+w}{11}\in I_{22,2}.

Thus
\tau^{2}=\frac{3k^{2}+d}{11}
is an integer, and
d\equiv-3k^{2}\bmod 11.

In fact,
\tau^{2}
should be an even integer.
The result then follows by varying
k
and computing the intersection form under the basis
h^{3}
,
\pi
, and
\pi-\tau
in the cases
k=2,3
(corresponding to (e) and (c), respectively) and the basis
h^{3}
,
\pi
, and
\tau-\pi
in the cases
k=4,5
(corresponding to (f) and (b), respectively).
∎

For later use, we record the value of the quadratic form
q_{M_{d}}
on
D(M_{d})
:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
X_{1}^{\sigma}
be a smooth special Peskine sixfold of discriminant
d
with a marking
M_{d}
.
Let
\{h^{3},\pi,\tau\}
be a basis for
M_{d}
so that the intersection form on
M_{d}
is as in Proposition
3.7
.

(a)

If

, then

where
d^{\prime}=d/11
. Moreover, if

, then
D(M_{d})
is cyclic, and the discriminant form takes value

q_{M_{d}}\left(\frac{h^{3}+\pi}{11}+\frac{\tau}{d^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{3}{11}+\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}

on a generator of

.

(b)

If

, and the discriminant form takes value

.

Proof.

Case
(a)
follows directly from the fact that

.
Similarly, in case

It follows from Nikulin’s theory of overlattices

∎

4.
Associated varieties: K3 surfaces and cubic fourfolds

In this section, we formalize the notion of a special trivector
\sigma
having an associated K3 surface (Section
4.1
) or an associated cubic fourfold (Section
4.2
).
In both cases, we make explicit necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of such an associated variety.

4.1.
Associated K3 surfaces

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus(\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24})
be a trivector marked by
N_{d}\subset\mathrm{NS}(X_{6}^{\sigma})
.
Since in this case the Peskine sixfold
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is smooth, it admits a marking
M_{d}\subset H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
.
It sometimes happens that the transcendental cohomology of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
matches that of a K3 surface, i.e. there exists a polarized K3 surface
(S,f)
and a Hodge isometry

H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\supset M_{d}^{\perp}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}f^{\perp}\subset H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})(-1).

In this case, it is natural to regard
S
as associated to
X_{1}^{\sigma}
.

On the other hand, if
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{24}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{22}
, then
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has isolated singularities and may not admit a marking.
In that case, the Debarre–Voisin fourfold
X_{6}^{\sigma}
is still smooth, and we can instead use the marking
N_{d}
on
X_{6}^{\sigma}
to identify an associated K3 surface.
For that reason, we give the following definition of associated K3 surfaces:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{22}
for some
d
, and let
N_{d}\subset\mathrm{NS}(X_{6}^{\sigma})
be the corresponding marking of
X_{6}^{\sigma}
.
Then a polarized K3 surface
(S,f)
of degree
d
is
associated to
\sigma
if there exists a Hodge isometry

H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\supset N_{d}^{\perp}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}f^{\perp}\subset H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}).

In the case of
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{22}
the Debarre–Voisin manifold
X_{6}^{\sigma}
is singular but admits a symplectic resolution
\widetilde{X_{6}^{\sigma}}
. Thus the the transcendental cohomology
T(X_{6}^{\sigma})
will be isomorphic to that of the symplectic resolution. We say
\sigma
has an associated K3 surface if the hyperkähler manifold
\widetilde{X_{6}^{\sigma}}
does as above. In fact, such a
\sigma
does have an associated K3: it was shown in
[
12
]
that
\widetilde{X_{6}^{\sigma}}
is birational to
S^{[2]}
for such an associated K3.

The following lemma shows that both notions of an associated K3 surface (either via the cohomology of
X_{1}^{\sigma}
or that of
X_{6}^{\sigma}
) coincide when both varieties are smooth.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus(\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24})
. A K3 surface
(S,f)
of degree
d
is associated to
\sigma
if and only if there is a marking
M_{d}\subset H^{3,3}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{C})\cap H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
and a Hodge isometry

H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\supset M_{d}^{\perp}\longrightarrow f^{\perp}(-1)\subset H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})(-1).

Proof.

Note for such a trivector
\sigma
, both
X_{1}^{\sigma}
and
X_{6}^{\sigma}
are smooth.
Since
(S,f)
is associated to
\sigma,
there exists some marking
N_{d}\subset\mathrm{NS}(X_{6}^{\sigma})
with
N_{d}^{\perp}\cong f^{\perp}
.
Note that
d
is the discriminant of
N_{d}^{\perp}
.
Let
v
be the generator of
N_{d}\cap\langle h\rangle^{\perp}\subset H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{prim}.

Then
v
maps isometrically to some algebraic class
\tau\in H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{van}
by Theorem
2.2
.
We have the following Hodge isometries:

(4.3)

(\tau)^{\perp}_{H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{van}}\cong(v)^{\perp}_{H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{prim}}(-1)=N_{d}^{\perp}(-1)\subset H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})(-1).

Let
M:=\mathrm{Sat}(\Lambda_{11},\tau)\subset H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z}).

Since
H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\supset M^{\perp}=\tau^{\perp}\subset H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{van}
and
H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
is unimodular, the discriminant of
M
must be
d
.
Hence
M_{d}:=M
gives a labelling of discriminant
d
, and by construction the desired Hodge isometry holds.

Conversely, suppose
M_{d}\subset H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
is a marking such that

M_{d}^{\perp}\cong f^{\perp}(-1)\subset H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})(-1)

for some degree
d

K3
surface
(S,f)
. Then
M_{d}\cap H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{van}
is generated by an algebraic class
\tau,
mapping isometrically to some algebraic class
v\in H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{prim}
again via Theorem
2.2
. One can run the Hodge isometries of Equation
4.3
backwards, to obtain a marking
N_{d}
of
X_{6}^{\sigma}
and the desired Hodge isometry.
∎

We classify which special Peskine sixfolds arise from trivectors with associated K3 surfaces:

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is a special Peskine sixfold of discriminant
d
.
Then
\sigma
has an associated K3 surface
(S,f)
of degree
d
if and only if
d
is not divisible by
4
or
121
, and every odd prime dividing
d
is a square modulo
11
.

Proof.

The case of
d=22
was studied originally in
[
12
]
, - both the cases of
d=22
and
d=24
are also covered in
[
6
, Proposition 2.4, Theorem 4.13]
, so we assume
d>24
.
In particular,
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is smooth, and there exists a marking
\Lambda_{11}\subset M_{d}\subset H^{3,3}(X_{1}^{\sigma})\cap H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z}).

By the Torelli theorem,
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has an associated polarized K3 surface if and only if there is some class
f
in the K3 lattice
\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}(-1):=U^{3}\oplus E_{8}^{2}
such that
M_{d}^{\perp}\cong f^{\perp}
.
Such an isometry exists if and only if there is an isomorphism
D(M_{d}^{\perp})\to D(f^{\perp})
respecting the discriminant form on each group.

Writing
f=u+\frac{d}{2}v
where
u
and
v
are standard generators for a hyperbolic factor of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}
, we see
D(f^{\perp})\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, for any generator
w
of
D(f^{\perp})
, the discriminant form
q
takes value
q(w)=\frac{k^{2}}{d}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}
for some integer
k
.

First, suppose
d\not\equiv 0\bmod 22
.
By Lemma
3.8
,
D(M_{d}^{\perp})\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
, and the discriminant form takes value
-\frac{11}{d}
on one of the generators.
Thus, for
X_{1}^{\sigma}
to have an associated K3 surface, we would need
-\frac{11}{d}=\frac{k^{2}}{d}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}
, i.e. for
-11
to be a square modulo
2d
.
Writing
d=2^{\ell}p_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots p_{m}^{e_{m}}
for distinct odd primes
p_{i}\neq 11
, this is equivalent to saying
-11
is a square modulo
2^{\ell+1}
and modulo
p_{i}
for each
i
.

If
\ell\geq 2
, then reducing modulo
8
would yield
5=k^{2}\bmod 8
, a contradiction.
Hence
\ell=1
. By quadratic reciprocity,

\left(\dfrac{-11}{p_{i}}\right)=\left(\dfrac{-1}{p_{i}}\right)\left(\dfrac{11}{p_{i}}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{p_{i}-1}{2}}(-1)^{\frac{p_{i}-1}{2}}\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{11}\right),

so
-11
is a square modulo
p_{i}
if and only if
p_{i}
is a square modulo
11
. This completes the first case.

Now, suppose
d\equiv 0\bmod 22
. Since
D(f^{\perp})
is cyclic, Lemma
3.8
shows that
X_{1}^{\sigma}
can only have an associated K3 surface if
11^{2}\nmid d
.
Writing
d=11d^{\prime}
and arguing as before,
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has an associated K3 surface if and only if there is some integer
k
for which
\frac{3}{11}+\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}=-\frac{k^{2}}{d}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
In other words,
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has an associated K3 surface if and only if

-3d^{\prime}-11=k^{2}\bmod 2d.

Again, write
d=2^{\ell}\cdot 11p_{i}^{e_{1}}\dots p_{m}^{e_{m}}
for distinct odd primes
p_{i}\neq 11
.
We must show
-3d^{\prime}-11
is a square modulo
2^{\ell}
,
11
, and each
p_{i}
.
Reducing modulo
p_{i}
, we see as before that
-3d^{\prime}-11
is a square modulo
p_{i}
if and only if
p_{i}
is a square modulo
11
.
In that case, reducing modulo
11
yields
-3d^{\prime}\equiv k^{2}\bmod 11
, so

1=\left(\dfrac{-3d^{\prime}}{11}\right)=\left(\dfrac{-3}{11}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{11}\right)^{\ell}\left(\dfrac{p_{1}}{11}\right)^{e_{1}}\dots\left(\dfrac{p_{m}}{11}\right)^{e_{m}}=(-1)^{\ell+1},

so
\ell
is odd.
Reducing modulo
8
shows
\ell<3
, so
\ell=1
, completing the argument for the second case.
∎

The first few examples of integers
d
for which the general Peskine sixfold of discriminant
d
has an associated K3 surface are
d=30
,
46
,
50
,
54
,
62
,
66
,
74
,
90
, and
94
.

4.2.
Associated cubic fourfolds

The transcendental cohomology of a Peskine sixfold can also be embedded into the primitive cohomology of a cubic fourfold, so we also develop the notion of associated cubic fourfolds.
For a smooth cubic fourfold
Y
, one defines the lattice of algebraic cycles

A(Y)=H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\cap H^{2,2}(Y,\mathbb{C}),

and the primitive cohomology

H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{prim}=\langle\eta_{Y}\rangle^{\perp}\subset H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z}),

where
\eta_{Y}
is the square of the hyperplane class. Let

A(Y)_{prim}=H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{prim}\cap A(Y).

There are countably many divisors
\mathcal{C}_{d}
in the moduli space of cubic fourfolds parametrizing those with
A(Y)_{prim}\neq 0

[
16
]
.
Whenever
\eta_{Y}\in K_{d}\subset A(Y)
where
K_{d}
is a primitive sublattice of rank
2
and discriminant
d
, one says
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{d}
, and
K_{d}
is called a marking of
Y
.

We make the following definition, analogous to Definition
4.1
:

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{22}
for some
d
, and let
N_{d}\subset\mathrm{NS}(X_{6}^{\sigma})
be the corresponding marking of
X_{6}^{\sigma}.
Then a marked cubic fourfold
(Y,K_{d})
is
associated to
\sigma
if
there exists a Hodge isometry

H^{2}(X_{6}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\supset N_{d}^{\perp}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}K_{d}^{\perp}(-1)\subset H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})(-1).

As with associated K3 surfaces, the following lemma shows that when
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is smooth and of the expected dimension, the existence of a cubic fourfold associated to
\sigma
is equivalent to the natural notion of the existence of a cubic fourfold associated to
X_{1}^{\sigma}
.
The proof is nearly identical to that of Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus(\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24})
. Then there is a marked cubic fourfold
(Y,K_{d})
associated to
\sigma
if and only if
there is a marking
M_{d}\subset H^{3,3}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{C})\cap H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
and a Hodge isometry

H^{6}(X_{1}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})\supset M_{d}^{\perp}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}K_{d}^{\perp}\subset H^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z}).

We classify which special Peskine sixfolds arise from trivectors with associated cubic fourfolds:

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose
X_{1}^{\sigma}
is a special Peskine sixfold of discriminant
d
.
Then
\sigma
has an associated cubic fourfold
(Y,K_{d})
of discriminant
d
if and only if
d
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

d\equiv 0
or
2\bmod 6
,

(b)

d
is not divisible by
9
or
121
,

(c)

33
is a square modulo each prime divisor of
d
, and

(d)

if
d\equiv 0\bmod 66
, then also
8\mid d
, and
d
has an odd number of prime divisors equivalent to
2
modulo
3
, counting multiplicity.

Proof.

We argue as in Proposition
4.4
.
When
\sigma\not\in\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24},
by the Torelli theorem,
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has an associated cubic fourfold
Y
if and only if
X_{1}^{\sigma}
has a marking
M_{d}
of discriminant
d
such that

M_{d}^{\perp}\cong K_{d}^{\perp}\subset I_{21,2}

for some saturated lattice
K_{d}
of rank 2 containing a class
h
of square
3
.
Automatically, this means
d=\mathrm{disc}(K_{d})
.
In the case of
d=24
, we have
N_{d}^{\perp}\cong K_{d}^{\perp}
, and the conclusion stays the same.
Cubic fourfolds can only have markings of discriminant
d\equiv 0
or
2\bmod 6
, and we consider each case separately.

(1)

Case 1:
d\equiv 2\bmod 6
, and
d\not\equiv 0\bmod 22
.
Then by Lemma
3.8
,
D(M_{d}^{\perp})
is cyclic, with a generator of square
-\frac{11}{d}
; similarly,
D(K_{d}^{\perp})
is cyclic with a generator of square
\frac{2d-1}{3d}

[
16
, Proposition 3.2.5]
.
An isomorphism of quadratic groups between
D(M_{d}^{\perp})
and
D(K_{d}^{\perp})
sends a generator to a generator, and any generator of
D(M_{d}^{\perp})
has square
-\frac{11k^{2}}{d}
for some integer
k
.
So, such an isomorphism exists when there is a solution
k\in\mathbb{Z}
to the equation

-\frac{11k^{2}}{d}=\frac{2d-1}{3d}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Clearing denominators, this is equivalent to requiring that

-33k^{2}\equiv 2d-1\bmod 6d.

Write
d=2^{\ell}p_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots p_{m}^{e_{m}}
for distinct odd primes
p_{i}
.
To solve the equation above for
k
is equivalent to solving the equation modulo
2^{\ell+1}
and
p_{i}
for all
i
.
If
\ell=1
, then the equation is satisfied modulo
4
; if
\ell\geq 2
, then the equation reduces to
k^{2}\equiv 1\bmod 8
, and a solution can be lifted to
\mathbb{Z}/2^{\ell+1}\mathbb{Z}
.

Reducing modulo
p_{i}
, we have
33k^{2}\equiv 1\bmod p_{i}
, which has a solution if and only if
33
is a square modulo
p_{i}
.

(2)

Case 2:
d\equiv 0\mod 6
, and
d\not\equiv 0\mod 22
.
Again,
D(M^{\perp}_{d})
or
D(N^{\perp}_{24})
when
d=24
) is cyclic, with a generator of square
-\frac{11}{d}
.
For
D(K^{\perp}_{d})
to be cyclic, we must have
9\nmid d
; in that case,
D(K^{\perp}_{d})
has a generator of square
\frac{2}{3}-\frac{3}{d}
.
So, we are looking for a solution
k
to

-\frac{11k^{2}}{d}=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{3}{d}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z},

i.e.

-11k^{2}=2e-3\mod 2d,

where
e=d/3
.
As before, write
d=2^{\ell}3p_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots p_{m}^{e_{m}}
for distinct primes
p_{i}\neq 2,3,11
.
A solution exists modulo
4
(if
\ell=1
) or can be lifted from a solution modulo
8
(if
\ell\geq 2
), so the equation has a solution if and only if it has a solution modulo
3
and
p_{i}
for each
i
.

Reducing modulo
p_{i}
, we have
11k^{2}=3\mod p_{i}
, so the equation has a solution modulo
p_{i}
if and only if

\left(\dfrac{11}{p_{i}}\right)=\left(\dfrac{3}{p_{i}}\right),

i.e.

\left(\dfrac{33}{p_{i}}\right)=1,

meaning
33
is a square modulo
p_{i}
.

Finally, we show that if there is a solution modulo
p_{i}
for each
i
, then there is automatically a solution modulo
3
.
Using the above, along with quadratic reciprocity, we have

\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{11}\right)=\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{3}\right).

Since
3
is a square modulo
11
but
d
is not (see Proposition
3.6
), we get that
e=d/3
is not a square modulo
11
, and it follows that

\displaystyle-1

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{e}{11}\right)=\left(\dfrac{2}{11}\right)^{\ell}\left(\dfrac{p_{1}}{11}\right)^{e_{1}}\dots\left(\dfrac{p_{m}}{11}\right)^{e_{m}}=\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{\ell}\left(\dfrac{p_{1}}{3}\right)^{e_{1}}\dots\left(\dfrac{p_{m}}{3}\right)^{e_{m}}=\left(\dfrac{e}{3}\right).

Hence
2e
is a square modulo
3
, and the reduction of our equation modulo
3
, i.e.
k^{2}=2e\mod 3
, has a solution.

(3)

Case 3:
d\equiv 2\bmod 6
and
d\equiv 0\bmod 22
.
As in case
1
,
D(K_{d}^{\perp})
is cyclic, with a generator of square
\frac{2d-1}{3d}
.
For
D(M_{d}^{\perp})
to be cyclic, we must have
11^{2}\nmid d
, and in that case, there is a generator of square
-\frac{3}{11}-\frac{11}{d}
.
An isomorphism of quadratic groups requires an integer
k
such that

\frac{k^{2}(2d-1)}{3d}=-\frac{3}{11}-\frac{11}{d}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Writing
e=\frac{2d-1}{3}
and
d=11d^{\prime}=11\cdot 2^{\ell}p_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots p_{m}^{e_{m}}
for distinct primes
p_{i}\neq 2,3,11
, this is equivalent to requiring that

k^{2}e\equiv-3d^{\prime}-11\bmod 2d.

The equation above has a solution if and only if

k^{2}(2d-1)\equiv-9d^{\prime}-33\bmod 6d

has a solution modulo
2^{\ell+1}
,
11
, and each
p_{i}
.

Reducing the equation modulo
p_{i}
yields
k^{2}\equiv 33\bmod p_{i}
.
Similarly, another application of Hensel’s lemma implies that
33
is a square modulo each power of
2
, and thus this equation has a solution modulo
2^{\ell+1}
.

If the equation has a solution modulo
p_{i}
for each
i
, then

1=\left(\dfrac{33}{p_{i}}\right)=\left(\dfrac{3}{p_{i}}\right)\left(\dfrac{11}{p_{i}}\right)=\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{11}\right),

so

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{d^{\prime}}{11}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{2}{11}\right)^{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{11}\right)^{e_{i}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{3}\right)^{e_{i}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\prod_{p_{i}\equiv 2\bmod 3}(-1)^{e_{i}}.

Modulo
11
, the equation reads
k^{2}\equiv 9d^{\prime}\bmod 11
.
By the above, for this equation (and the others) to have a solution means that the number of prime divisors of
d
equivalent to
2
modulo
3
(counting multiplicity, and including
2
and
11
) is odd.
But this condition is satisfied automatically since
d\equiv 2\bmod 3
.

(4)

Case 4:
d\equiv 0\bmod 66
.
Then
D(M_{d}^{\perp})\cong\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/6d^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
, and
D(K_{d}^{\perp})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/22d^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
where
d=66d^{\prime}
.
For these groups to be isomorphic, they must both be cyclic, which is the case when
3,11\nmid d^{\prime}
.
In that case, finding an isomorphism of discriminant groups compatible with the discriminant form is equivalent to solving the equation

k^{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}-\frac{3}{d}\right)=-\frac{3}{11}-\frac{11}{d}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z},

Write

.
We need to check when there is a solution modulo

3

Taking the equation modulo

in which case

is a square modulo

.
By quadratic reciprocity, this is also equivalent to

\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{3}\right)=\left(\dfrac{p_{i}}{11}\right).

Reducing modulo

, which has a solution if and only if

is not a square modulo

Assuming the equation above for each

\displaystyle-1

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{d^{\prime}}{11}\right)=\left(\dfrac{2}{11}\right)^{\ell}\left(\dfrac{p_{1}}{11}\right)^{e_{1}}\dots\left(\dfrac{p_{m}}{11}\right)^{e_{m}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\left(\dfrac{p_{1}}{3}\right)^{e_{1}}\dots\left(\dfrac{p_{m}}{3}\right)^{e_{m}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\prod_{p_{i}\equiv 2\bmod 3}(-1)^{e_{i}},

Finally, reducing modulo

∎

5.1.
Lines on Peskine sixfolds of discriminant

First, we recall and extend some constructions from
[
6
, Section 2.7]
related to the Fano variety of lines
F(X_{1}^{\sigma})
on
X_{1}^{\sigma}
and to the sixfold

X^{\sigma}_{7}=\{[V_{7}]\;|\;\mathrm{rank}(\sigma|_{V_{7}})\leq 5\}\subset\mathrm{Gr}(7,V_{10}),

where
\mathrm{rank}(\sigma|_{V_{7}})=7-\dim\ker(\sigma|_{V_{7}})
and
\ker(\sigma|_{V_{7}})=\{v\in V_{10}:\sigma(v,V_{7},V_{7})=0\}

For general
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\setminus(\mathcal{P}_{22}\cup\mathcal{P}_{24}\cup\mathcal{P}_{28})
,
[
6
, Theorem 2.20]
describes an isomorphism
X_{7}^{\sigma}\simeq F(X_{1}^{\sigma})
. We find that a similar result holds in discriminant

Lemma 5.1
.

For a general
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{24}
, there is an embedding
\varphi:X_{7}^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow F(X_{1}^{\sigma})
, which is a bijection on closed points.

Proof.

We adapt a construction defined after Remark 2.19 in
[
6
]
: given
[V_{7}]\in X_{7}^{\sigma}
, we set
\varphi([V_{7}])=[\mathbb{P}(\ker(\sigma|_{V_{7}}))]
. Let us recall why this is well-defined. Assuming
\sigma\not\in\mathcal{P}_{28}
, we have
\mathrm{rank}(\sigma|_{V_{7}})=5
, so
V_{2}=\ker(\sigma|_{V_{7}})
is
2
-dimensional. Since
\sigma(V_{2},V_{7},V_{7})=0
, for each
1
-dimensional subspace

\mathrm{rank}(\sigma(V_{1},-,-))\leq 6
, so

. In particular,
[\mathbb{P}(V_{2})]\in F(X_{1}^{\sigma})
.

Next we claim that
\varphi
is injective: suppose that there exists
V_{7}\neq V_{7}^{\prime}
such that
V_{2}:=\ker(\sigma|_{V_{7}})=\ker(\sigma|_{V_{7}^{\prime}}).
Let
W=V_{7}+V_{7}^{\prime}\subset V_{10}
, so
\dim W\geq 8
, and
V_{2}\subset\ker\sigma|_{W}.

For any
V_{1}\subset V_{2}
, we have
\sigma(V_{1},W,W)=0
, so
\mathrm{rank}(\sigma(V_{1},-,-))\leq 4
. Applying
[
6
, Proposition 2.9]
to the flag

for any
6

shows that

is singular at
\mathbb{P}(V_{1})
. Varying the

shows

is singular along all of
\mathbb{P}(V_{2})
, which is not the case for general
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{24}
.

Now, we show that the open set

F(X_{1}^{\sigma})\setminus\{\mathbb{P}(V_{2})\subset X_{1}^{\sigma}\;|\;W_{1}\subset V_{2}\}

lies in the image of
\varphi
. Suppose
[\mathbb{P}(V_{2})]\in F(X_{1}^{\sigma})
with

. Define a subspace

U=\sum_{V_{1}\subset V_{2}}\ker(\sigma(V_{1},-,V_{10})).

Since

.

Suppose

The associated cubic

The intersection

Proof.

It is enough to construct one example for which

∎

Since

Restricting the morphism

described in Remark

to

yields a morphism

.

Proposition 5.4
.

For a general
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{24}
,
F(Y)
and
X_{6}^{\sigma}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Consider the Stein factorization of
\psi
,

F(Y)\overset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}G\overset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}X_{6}^{\sigma},

and let
B
be the normalization of
\alpha(F(Y))
, so we obtain a further factorization

F(Y)\overset{\gamma}{\longrightarrow}B\longrightarrow G.

Since
F(Y)
is a projective hyperkähler manifold, and
\gamma
is a surjective morphism with connected fibers over a normal base
B
, it follows that
\gamma
is either constant, an isomorphism, or a Lagrangian fibration over a plane (see
[
27
]
and
[
18
]
in the fourfold case).

Assume toward a contradiction that
\gamma
is a Lagrangian fibration. Then the general fiber of
\gamma
, hence also of
\alpha
, is an abelian surface, meaning the general fiber of
\psi
is a disjoint union of abelian surfaces
[
24
]
. On the other hand, the fibers of
\psi
embed in the fibers of
\Phi:F(X_{1}^{\sigma})\to X_{6}^{\sigma}
, which are (possibly singular) cubic surfaces by Remark

, yielding a contradiction. A similar contradiction rules out
\psi

Hence
\gamma
is an isomorphism, meaning
\psi
is finite. Since
F(Y)
and
X_{6}^{\sigma}
both have trivial canonical bundle, the morphism
\psi
is unramified. On the other hand,
X_{6}^{\sigma}
is simply-connected, so no nontrivial étale covers exist. The result follows.
∎

Inflationary observables in F(R) gravity
Enrique Díaz, Oscar Meza-Aldama

Abstract
We present phenomenological signatures for a modified gravity model f(R), constructed with linear, quadratic, cubic and quartic terms. The obtained signatures satisfy current phenomenological bounds reported by PLANCK and BICEP3. Furthermore, two of the model solutions σ 1 \sigma_{1} and σ 2 \sigma_{2} seem to favor a much lower value for the tensor-to-scalar ratio 0.0005 < r σ 1 < 0.0015 0.0005<r_{\sigma_{1}}<0.0015 and r σ 2 < 0.00015 r_{\sigma_{2}}<0.00015 than the current reported experimental bounds. The results we obtained are quantitatively similar to those presented in previous studies for R 3 R^{3} models.

II
f(R)
inflationary models

Inflationary models that are constructed in an
f(R)
formalism are created by extending the Einstein Hilbert action for general relativity which is (in natural units):

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}R.

(1)

by replacing the R term with a function
f(R)

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}f(R),

(2)

where
f(R)
is an arbitrary function of the Ricci scalar.
Usually, for calculation purposes, we introduce an auxiliary field
\phi
and rewrite the action as
koreans
:

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[f(\phi)+f^{\prime}(\phi)(R-\phi)\right].

(3)

Substituting the equation of motion for
\phi
we recover the original action (
2
).
We then make the conformal transformation

g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(\phi)}g_{\mu\nu},

(4)

and we get the action in the Einstein frame with a minimally coupled scalar field
s
:

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}s\partial_{\nu}s-V_{E}(s)\right],

(5)

where

\displaystyle s(\phi)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\ln f^{\prime}(\phi),

(6)

\displaystyle V_{E}(s)=\left.\frac{\phi f^{\prime}(\phi)-f(\phi)}{2{f^{\prime}}^{2}(\phi)}\right|_{\phi=\phi(s)}.

(7)

These transformations are performed because calculations of the inflationary observables are usually performed in the Einstein frame. Equation (
6
) is supposed to be inverted to get
\phi
in terms of
s
and use this expression to evaluate the right-hand side of equation (
7
). So, if we propose a particular
f
, we must solve (
6
) for
\phi
in terms of
s
and then substitute into (
7
) to get the potential.

Important cosmological quantities can be calculated via the potential. For example, in the slow roll approximation, the slow roll parameters are defined as:

\displaystyle\epsilon\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\right)^{2},

(8)

\displaystyle\eta\equiv\frac{V^{\prime\prime}}{V}.

(9)

In our case, they are functions of
s
. The end of inflation is the point
s_{e}
at which
\epsilon=1
. The number of e-folds from the beginning of inflation
s
until the end
s_{e}
is defined as

N=\int_{s_{e}}^{s}\frac{V}{V^{\prime}}ds.

(10)

Integrating, we can in principle solve for
s

s=s(N)
. Then substitute in (8) and (9) to get
\epsilon=\epsilon(N)
and
\eta=\eta(N)

Two important inflationary observables are the spectral index

\displaystyle n_{s}\equiv 1-2\epsilon_{\ast}-\eta_{\ast},

(11)

\displaystyle r\equiv 16\epsilon_{\ast};

(12)

where the asterisk denotes evaluation at horizon exit, which is usually taken as

V
Conclusion

Using a quartic polynomial function in an
f(R)
gravity model, we have obtained the cosmological observables for the corresponding inflationary potential in the Einstein frame. We successfully obtained three main cosmological observables: the power spectrum and spectral index of scalar perturbations, and the tensor-to-scalar ratio. We used them to get constraints on the parameter(s) of our model and showed that within those regions the model satisfies the most up-to-date experimental bounds. The allowed parameter space that is found falls well within the reach of the expected improved measurements expected at the LiteBIRD experiment of
\delta r<0.001

Fuskeland
.

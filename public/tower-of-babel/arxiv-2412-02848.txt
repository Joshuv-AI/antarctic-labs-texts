Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a self-improvement result for ( θ , p ) (\theta,p) -fractional Hardy inequalities, in both the exponent 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty and the regularity parameter 0 < θ < 1 0<\theta<1 , for bounded domains in doubling metric measure spaces. The key conceptual tool is a Caffarelli-Silvestre-type argument, which relates fractional Sobolev spaces on Z Z to Newton-Sobolev spaces in the hyperbolic filling X ¯ ε \overline{X}_{\varepsilon} of Z Z via trace results. Using this insight, it is shown that a fractional Hardy inequality in an open subset of Z Z is equivalent to a classical Hardy inequality in the filling X ¯ ε \overline{X}_{\varepsilon} . The main result is then obtained by applying a new weighted self-improvement result for p p -Hardy inequalities. The exponent p p can be self-improved by a classical Koskela-Zhong argument, but a new theory of regularizable weights is developed to obtain the self-improvement in the regularity parameter θ \theta . This generalizes a result of Lehrbäck and Koskela on self-improvement of d Ω β d_{\Omega}^{\beta} -weighted p p -Hardy inequalities by allowing a much broader class of weights. Using the equivalence of fractional Hardy inequalities with Hardy inequalities in the fillings, we also give new examples of domains satisfying fractional Hardy inequalities.

1.
Introduction

The main theorem of this paper is a self-improvement result for fractional Hardy inequalities. In the process, we develop a Caffarelli-Silvestre-type
[
13
]
approach which relates fractional Hardy inequalities to classical Hardy inequalities via trace results. For classical Hardy inequalities, we also give a new weighted self-improvement result. All results are stated for general metric measure spaces, but apply more classically in sub-Riemannian spaces, manifolds and, Euclidean spaces. Even in these classical settings, the results are new.

1.1.
Fractional Hardy inequalities

We say that a
p
-Hardy inequality holds for a non-empty open subset
\Omega
in a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
if for some
C_{p}\in(0,\infty)
we have

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}d\mu(x)\leq C_{p}\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}d\mu,

for all Lipschitz functions
u\in\Lip_{c}(\Omega)
that are compactly supported in
\Omega
, where
g_{u}
is its minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
, see
[
32
,
30
]
for details. Here,
d_{\Omega}(x)=d(x,X\setminus\Omega)
. In Euclidean spaces, with
(X,d,\mu)=(\mathbb{R}^{n},|\cdot|,\mathcal{L}^{n})
, the
p
-Hardy inequality can be equivalently stated for all continuously differentiable functions
u\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with compact support in
\Omega
and with
|\nabla u|
replacing
g_{u}
. For more detailed definitions, see Section
2
. Both the Euclidean inequality, and the general metric measure variant, have been extensively studied, see
[
24
,
44
,
47
,
46
,
12
,
14
,
33
,
42
,
56
]
and references therein. Good surveys on the topic are
[
45
,
52
,
16
]
.

It is natural to also consider the fractional version of the previous inequality, where the Dirichlet-type energy on the right is replaced by a fractional energy. The
(\theta,p)
-fractional Hardy inequality is:

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{\theta p}}d\mu(x)\leq C_{\theta,p}\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{d(x,y)^{\theta p}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

The energy on the right is one of the definitions of a fractional Sobolev energy used in the definition of Besov spaces, see e.g.
[
1
,
13
,
26
,
5
]
.
The fractional Hardy inequality has previously been studied in e.g.
[
18
,
45
,
48
,
36
,
19
]
.

Hardy inequalities enjoy self-improvement phenomena: if a given open set
\Omega
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality, then it also satisfies a
q
-Hardy inequality with
q\in(p-\epsilon,p+\epsilon)
for some
\epsilon>0
. This was first shown by Koskela and Zhong
[
44
]
, and was further studied and reproved in
[
22
,
23
,
47
,
43
]
. Such self-improvement results were known earlier for Muckenhoupt weights
[
55
]
and for Poincaré inequalities
[
39
]
. There are also weighted self-improvement results, see
[
47
,
45
]
. Such improvement results are important in applications to partial differential equations and regularity of quasiconformal maps, see
[
44
]
for further discussion and references.

Our main theorem gives a self-improvement result for fractional Hardy inequalities in doubling metric measure spaces.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a complete, doubling metric measure space. Let
E\subset Z
be a closed set such that
Z\setminus E
is bounded and satisfies a
(\theta_{0},p_{0})
-Hardy inequality, with constant
C_{\theta_{0},p_{0}}
, for some
0<\theta_{0}<1
and
1<p_{0}<\infty
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on
\theta_{0}
,
p_{0}
,
C_{\theta_{0},p_{0}}
, and
C_{\nu}
, such that
Z\setminus E
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality for all
\theta\in(\theta_{0}-\varepsilon_{0},\theta_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})
and
p\in(p_{0}-\varepsilon_{0},p_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})
.

Here,
C_{\nu}
is the doubling constant of
\nu
, see Section
2
.

Self-improvement results of the above form were recently obtained in
[
36
]
for a
pointwise
version of
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequalities under the additional assumptions that
Z
is geodesic and reverse doubling, that
E\subset Z
satisfies a fractional
(\theta,p)
-capacity density condition, see Definition
7.10
, and under some restrictions on the parameters
\theta
and
p
. In fact, the results there were obtained for pointwise
(\theta,p,q)
-Hardy inequalities, where the right hand side of (
1.2
) is replaced with a
F^{\theta}_{p,q}
-Triebel-Lizorkin energy. In their argument, the authors show that the fractional capacity density condition is equivalent to a pointwise fractional Hardy inequality. In conjunction with results from
[
48
]
, they then obtain a deep self-improvement result for the fractional capacity density condition. The pointwise fractional Hardy inequality implies the (integral) fractional Hardy inequality under some restrictions of the parameters, and thus
[
48
]
implies Theorem
1.3
under the additional assumption of a pointwise Hardy inequality. While our results are limited to the case that
p=q
, our different approach allows us to remove the pointwise fractional Hardy inequality assumption, and so our results apply to domains such as the Euclidean punctured unit ball, see Examples
7.8
and
7.11
, for which this condition fails. Moreover, as we utilize the trace and extension relationship between Sobolev and Besov functions via the hyperbolic filling, described below, we do not need to assume that
Z
is geodesic or reverse doubling, and so we are able to obtain our results for a full range of parameters
0<\theta<1
and
1<p<\infty
. For more on pointwise Hardy inequalities and capacity density conditions, see for example
[
41
,
43
,
22
,
23
,
28
,
48
]
. See also the classical work of Lewis on the self-improvement of the
p
-capacity density condition
[
49
]
. We now describe the crucial tools that lead to a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

1.2.
Equivalence with
p
-Hardy inequalities in the hyperbolic filling

As mentioned above, there are a number of known results on the self-improvement for the
p
-Hardy inequality,
[
44
,
46
,
47
]
. Motivated by this, one might seek to prove self-improvement for the fractional Hardy inequality by either mimicking the proofs of these results, or by reducing the fractional Hardy inequality results to those for Hardy inequalities. Here, we take the latter approach, which is motivated by the Caffarelli-Silvestre theory
[
13
]
(see also
[
3
,
21
]
for more general versions) and the trace theory of Sobolev spaces
[
5
]
using hyperbolic fillings.

The technique of hyperbolic fillings was introduced in
[
9
,
11
,
10
]
and has origins in a construction from Gromov
[
27
, P.99 (b)]
. Since this technique has mainly been developed for compact spaces, we will now focus on the case where
Z
is additionally assumed to be compact. This assumption is removed later through a localization argument. In this approach, given
(Z,d)
one constructs another metric space, a uniformized hyperbolic filling
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon})
, whose boundary is bi-Lipschitz equivalent with
(Z,d)
. In
[
5
]
, the authors considered
(Z,d,\nu)
to be a compact, doubling metric measure space, and equipped the uniformized hyperbolic filling of
Z
with a family of measures
\mu_{\beta}
induced from
\nu
. The main result in
[
5
]
shows that Besov spaces on
Z
- functions for which the right hand side of (
1.2
) is finite - arise as traces of Newton-Sobolev functions, in the sense of
[
54
,
15
,
32
]
, on
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
. The Dirichlet energy of Newton-Sobolev functions is the right hand side of the
p
-Hardy inequality (
1.1
). Thus, the trace theory forms a bridge between the Dirichlet energies in (
1.1
) with the fractional energies in (
1.2
). Utilizing this, we prove that the fractional
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality in
(Z,d,\nu)
is equivalent to a
p
-Hardy inequality in
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. While this is simple to state, the proof involves several nontrivial estimates.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a compact, doubling metric measure space, with
\diam(Z)<1
. Let
E\subset Z
be a closed set, and let
1<p<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
\beta>0
be such that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
. Then,
Z\setminus E
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality if and only if
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
.

The interlinking of the parameters
\beta
,
\theta
, and
p
given by

(1.5)

\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon}=p(1-\theta)

relates to the trace theory in
[
5
]
, see Theorem
4.2
below. Here
\varepsilon>0
is a fixed parameter from the hyperbolic filling construction.

Theorem
1.4
is conceptually simple, and it reinforces the general idea that non-local problems can be studied through proving equivalences with local problems in hyperbolic fillings. By the self-improvement results for
p
-Hardy inequalities established in
[
44
]
, we see that a
p
-Hardy inequality in the space
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
implies a
q
-Hardy inequality for
q\in(p-\epsilon,p+\epsilon)
. Using the equivalence in Theorem
1.4
, it then follows that a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality in
Z
gives us a
(q,\theta^{\prime})
-Hardy inequality for some
other
value
\theta^{\prime}
. The reason for this is that altering
p
to
q
in (
1.5
) results in
\theta
changing, as the results of
[
44
]
keep
\beta
fixed. This gives self-improvement along a single curve, see Figure
1
in Section
6
.

Theorem
1.4
also provides a method to readily obtain results pertaining to fractional Hardy inequalities directly from known corresponding results for
p
-Hardy inequalities. We illustrate this in Section
7
with Proposition
7.2
, which gives a sufficient condition for
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequalities in terms of Assouad codimensions. Such a result was established for
p
-Hardy inequalities in
[
46
]
, and Theorem
1.4
allows us to easily obtain the fractional analog. Using this proposition, we show that the punctured unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality when
\theta p<n
, see Example
7.8
.

1.3.
Towards full improvement: regularizable weights

In order to use the hyperbolic filling to get full self-improvement of the fractional Hardy inequality, that is, with respect to the parameters
\theta
and
p

independently
, (
1.5
) requires that we are able to change the
\beta
-parameter. Changing the
\beta
-parameter in
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
corresponds to adjusting the reference measure
\mu_{\beta}
with a doubling weight
w
. Thus, one needs a
weighted
self-improvement result for
p
-Hardy inequalities. The only general result of this type that we are aware of is
[
46
]
, where certain distance weights are used. However, the weight
w
is not of this type. This prompts us to show a weighted self-improvement for a more general class of weights, which includes the weights needed to alter
\mu_{\beta}
to
\mu_{\beta^{\prime}}
.

The weighted self-improvement result may be of general interest. The general question is the following. Under which assumptions on
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X,\mu)
(and the space
X
) does (
1.1
) imply

(1.6)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}wd\mu\leq C_{p}\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}wd\mu?

We give a sufficient condition with two assumptions. The assumptions are a bit technical, but can be summarized as follows.

(1)

p
-admissibility: The measure
wd\mu
is doubling and supports a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality.

(2)

\delta
-regularizability (in
\Omega
): Whenever
B_{x}=B(x,d_{\Omega}(x)/8)
and
B_{y}=B(y,d_{\Omega}(y)/8)
are two close enough balls, then

|w_{B_{x}}-w_{B_{y}}|\leq\delta w_{B_{x}}.

Here
w_{A}=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}wd\mu
for any Borel set
A
with
\mu(A)>0
.

The latter of these is a natural condition involving
Whitney scales
, i.e. balls
B(x,d_{\Omega}(x)/C)
for
C>1
. The details are given in Definition
2.3
and Definition
3.1
. In terms of these conditions, we obtain the following weighted self-improvement result; see also Remark
3.13
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, doubling metric measure space, and let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that an open set
\Omega\subset X
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu
, with constant
C_{p}\geq 1
. Then there exists a constant
\delta_{p}>0
, such that if
w
is

(i)

p
-admissible,

(ii)

\delta
-regularizable in
\Omega
for some
0<\delta\leq\delta_{p}
,

then
\Omega
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
w\,d\mu
, with constant depending only on
p
,
C_{\mu}
, and the
p
-admissibility constants of
w
.

We may take
\delta_{p}=\frac{p}{2C_{0}}(2C_{p})^{-1/p}
, where
C_{0}
is the constant from Lemma
3.4
which depends only on
C_{\mu}
.

While the above theorem is stated for
p
-admissible weights, this assumption can be weakened to require that the weighted measure supports a Poincaré inequality on Whitney balls in
\Omega
, see Definition
2.3
. Theorem
1.7
, with this slightly weaker admissibility condition, then recovers the weighted self-improvement result
[
46
, Theorem 1]
, given for weights of the form
d_{\Omega}^{\beta}
, see Remark
3.13
.

We use this theorem to obtain the full self-improvement in Theorem
1.3
. However, this result is also new for the classical
p
-Hardy inequality, and it strengthens the main results of
[
47
]
by allowing more general weights. In the one dimensional Euclidean case, weighted Hardy inequalities and characterizations thereof have appeared in
[
51
]
.

The condition of
\delta
-regularizability arose from examining the proofs in
[
18
,
47
,
46
]
. The rough idea is that the Hardy inequality contains two levels of estimates: estimates at Whitney scales, and estimates at large scales. The Whitney scale information relates to
p
-admissibility. The self-improvement of the large scale estimates is related to
\delta
-regularizability – morally since a
\delta
-regularizable weight will not alter the large scale estimate too drastically. A close examination of the proofs in
[
18
,
47
,
46
]
shows that the same phenomenon has been used previously. The appearance of Whitney scales is also natural from the perspective of characterizations for Hardy inequalities that have appeared in the literature such as
[
34
]
.

Using Theorem
1.7
, it follows that a
p
-Hardy inequality in the hyperbolic filling
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
persists when changing the measure from
\mu_{\beta}
to
\mu_{\beta^{\prime}}
. Theorem
1.4
then gives us that a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality on
Z
implies a
(\theta^{\prime},p)
-Hardy inequality, with
\theta^{\prime}
arising from the perturbation of
\beta^{\prime}
in (
1.5
). Combining this argument with the self-improvement results from
[
44
]
, we are able to pass from the one-parameter self-improvement, see Figure
1
in Section
6
, to the full self-improvement result, see Figure
2
, which gives us Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we provide the necessary background information and definitions used throughout the paper. In Section
4
, we will introduce particular notation to denote the uniformized hyperbolic filling
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
of a compact metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
. To avoid confusion with this particular notation, however, we will present the general background notions in this section using
(X,d,\mu)
to denote a generic metric measure space.

Throughout this paper, we let
C>0
denote a constant which, unless otherwise specified, depends only on the structural constants of the metric measure space, such as the doubling constant for example. Its precise value is not of interest to us, and may change with each occurrence, even within the same line. Furthermore, given quantities
A
and
B
, we will often use the notation
A\simeq B
to mean that there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that
C^{-1}A\leq B\leq CA
. Likewise, we use
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
if the left and right inequalities hold, respectively.

2.1.
Doubling measures

For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we denote by
B(x,r)
the ball
\{y\in X:d(x,y)<r\}
. Given a ball
B:=B(x,r)\subset X
, and a constant
C>0
, we denote by
CB
the ball
B(x,Cr)
. Given
x\in X
and
E,F\subset X
, we also denote by
d(E,F)
and
d(x,E)
, the distance between sets
E
and
F
, and the distance between
x
and
E
, respectively, as induced by the metric
d
.

We say that a Borel measure
\mu
is
doubling
, if there exists a constant
C_{\mu}\geq 1
such that

0<\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{\mu}\mu(B(x,r))<\infty

for all
x\in X
and
r>0
. By iterating this condition, there exist constants
Q>0
and
C\geq 1
, depending only on
C_{\mu}
, such that for all
x\in X
,
0<r\leq R
, and
y\in B(x,R)
, we have

\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq C^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{Q}.

Here, we may take
Q=\log_{2}C_{\mu}
. If
\mu
is doubling, many classical results from harmonic analysis hold in
(X,d,\mu)
, such as the Lebesgue differentiation theorem and Hardy-Littlewood maximal function theorem, see
[
30
]
for example. Such metric measure spaces also admit the following Whitney decomposition of an open set. For proof of the following lemma see
[
32
, Section 4]
, for example.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space, and let
\Omega\subset X
be open, with
X\setminus\Omega\neq\varnothing
. Then there exists a countable collection of balls
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}:=\{B(x_{i},r_{i})=:B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\Omega=\bigcup_{i}B_{i}

and that

\sum_{i}\chi_{6B_{i}}\leq C,

with

r_{i}=\frac{1}{8}d_{\Omega}(x_{i}).

Here the constant
C\geq 1
depends only on the doubling constant of
\mu
. Moreover, there exists a Lipschitz partition of unity
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i}
subordinate to
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}
. That is, each
\varphi_{i}
is
C/r_{i}
-Lipschitz, with
0\leq\varphi_{i}\leq\chi_{2B_{i}}
, and

\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}(x)=1

for each
x\in\Omega
. Furthermore, there exists
C\geq 1
, depending only on the doubling constant of
\mu
, such that
\varphi_{i}|_{B_{i}}\geq C^{-1}
for each
i
.

2.2.
Newton-Sobolev and Besov classes

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Given a family
\Gamma
of non-constant, compact, rectifiable curves, we define the
p
-modulus
of
\Gamma
by

\Mod_{p}(\Gamma):=\inf_{\rho}\int_{X}\rho^{p}\,d\mu,

where the infimum is over all non-negative Borel functions
\rho:X\to[0,\infty]
such that
\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds\geq 1
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Given a function
u:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that a Borel function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient
of
u
if the following holds for all non-constant, compact, rectifiable curves
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
:

|u(\gamma(b))-u(\gamma(a))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds

whenever
u(\gamma(b))
and
u(\gamma(a))
are both finite, and
\int_{\gamma}g\,ds=\infty
otherwise. We say that
g
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
if the family of curves where the above inequality fails has
p
-modulus zero.

We define
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X,\mu)
to be the class of all functions in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
which have an upper gradient belonging to
L^{p}(X,\mu)
. We then define

\|u\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X,\mu)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}+\inf_{g}\|g\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)},

where the infimum is taken over all upper gradients
g
of
u
. Defining the equivalence relation
\sim
in
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X,\mu)
by
u\sim v
if and only if
\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X,\mu)}=0
, we then define the
Newton-Sobolev space

N^{1,p}(X,\mu)
to be
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X,\mu)/\sim
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{N^{1,p}(X,\mu)}:=\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X,\mu)}
. We can similarly define
N^{1,p}(\Omega,\mu)
for any open
\Omega\subset X
. Each
u\in N^{1,p}(X,\mu)
has a
minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
, denoted
g_{u}
, which is unique
\mu
-a.e., see
[
32
, Chapter 6]
. For more background and historical references on modulus, upper gradients and Sobolev spaces see
[
32
,
5
,
30
,
54
,
15
]
.

For
0<\theta<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the
Besov energy
of a function
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X,\mu)
by

(2.2)

\|u\|^{p}_{B^{\theta}_{p,p}(X,\mu)}:=\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{d(x,y)^{\theta p}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

The
Besov space

B^{\theta}_{p,p}(X,\mu)
is then defined as the set of all functions in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
for which this energy is finite. If
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu=\mathcal{L}^{n}
is the standard
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, then
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(X,\mu)
corresponds to the fractional Sobolev space
W^{\theta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It was shown in
[
26
]
that when
(X,d,\mu)
is a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality, defined below,
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(X,\mu)
coincides with the real interpolation space
(L^{p}(X,\mu),KS^{1,p}(X,\mu))_{\theta,p}
, where
KS^{1,p}(X,\mu)
is the Korevaar-Schoen space. See the nice survey
[
17
]
and
[
1
,
5
,
21
,
13
,
26
,
37
]
for more discussion and references.

2.3.
Poincaré inequalities and
p
-admissible weights

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
. We say that
(X,d,\mu)
supports a
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality
if there exist constants
C_{PI},\lambda\geq 1
such that the following holds for all balls
B\subset X
and function-upper gradient pairs
(u,g)
:

\left(\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{PI}\rad(B)\left(\fint_{\lambda B}g^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

Here and throughout the paper, we use the notation

u_{B}:=\fint_{B}u\,d\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}u\,d\mu.

If
(X,d,\mu)
is a geodesic space supporting a
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality, then we may take
\lambda=1
, as shown in
[
29
]
. We note that if
(X,d,\mu)
is doubling and supports a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality, then Lipschitz functions are dense in
N^{1,p}(X,\mu)
, see
[
32
, Theorem 8.2.1]
.

By a
weight
, we mean a non-negative function
w\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X,\mu)
. To such a weight, we associate the weighted measure
\mu_{w}:=w\,d\mu
. We say
w
is a
doubling weight
if the measure
\mu_{w}
is doubling. Moreover, we say that a weight
w
is
p
-admissible
if
\mu_{w}
is doubling and supports a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality. The terminology is due to the classic reference of Heinonen, Kilpeläinen and Martio
[
31
]
. While Theorem
1.7
is stated in terms of
p
-admissible weights, the result holds true under a slightly weaker
p
-admissibility condition, namely that
\mu_{w}
supports a Poincaré inequality on balls at Whitney scales, see Remark
3.13
. In terms of Lemma
2.1
above, we give the following definition:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be open, and let
1<p<\infty
. We say that a non-negative function
w\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X,\mu)
is
p
-admissible at Whitney scales in
\Omega
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\mu_{w}:=w\,d\mu
is doubling,

(ii)

there exist
C,\lambda\geq 1
such that for all
x\in\Omega
,
0<r\leq 3d_{\Omega}(x)/4
, and function-upper gradient pairs
(u,g)
, we have

\fint_{B(x,r)}|u-u_{B(x,r)}|d\mu_{w}\leq Cr\left(\fint_{\lambda B(x,r)}g^{p}\,d\mu_{w}\right)^{1/p},

where here
u_{B(x,r)}:=\frac{1}{\mu_{w}(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}u\,d\mu_{w}
.

Any Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
-weight is a
p
-admissible weight. In Euclidean spaces, this was shown in
[
31
, Section 15.22]
. For metric measure spaces that satisfy a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality this is an unstated folklore result, and can be obtained by applying the proof from
[
31
, Section 15.22]
together with a Riesz-potential characterization of the Poincaré inequality from e.g.
[
38
]
. Alternatively, a more direct argument is to modify the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 4]
which uses the equivalent characterization of the Poincaré inequality using a pointwise condition, see
[
29
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2]
. Thus, Muckenhoupt weights give a large class of examples of admissible weights, see e.g.
[
7
,
6
,
40
]
. Muckenhoupt weights on the other hand are often constructed as distance weights, see
[
20
,
2
]
. In our case, we will also construct
p
-admissible weights using distance functions, but the Poincaré inequality in our case can be checked more directly than through the Muckenhoupt condition.

2.4.
Hardy inequalities

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be open, and let
1<p<\infty
. We say that
\Omega
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality
(with respect to
\mu
) if there exist a constant
C_{p}\geq 1
such that the following holds for all
u\in\Lip_{c}(\Omega)
:

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d(x,X\setminus\Omega)^{p}}d\mu(x)\leq C_{p}\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu.

As shown by Koskela and Zhong in
[
44
]
,
p
-Hardy inequalities possess the following self-improvement property:

Theorem 2.5
.

[
44
, Theorem 1.2]

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality. Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set, and suppose that
\Omega
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{1}:=\varepsilon_{1}(p,C_{p},C_{\mu},\lambda,C_{PI})
, such that
\Omega
satisfies a
q
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu
for all
q\in(p-\varepsilon_{1},p+\varepsilon_{1})
. Moreover, there exists
C_{1}:=C_{1}(p,C_{p},C_{\mu},\lambda,C_{PI})
such that
C_{q}=C_{1}
for all such
q
.

In addition to the above self-improvement result,
p
-Hardy inequalities also self-improve with respect to weights of the form
d(\cdot,X\setminus\Omega)^{\beta}
, as shown by Lehrbäck in
[
47
, Theorem 1]
and
[
46
, Proposition 6.4]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
47
, Theorem 1]

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality. Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set, let
\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and suppose that
\Omega
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
d(\cdot,X\setminus\Omega)^{\beta_{0}}\,d\mu
. Then there exists
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon(p,\beta_{0},C_{p},C_{\nu},\lambda,C_{PI})
such that
\Omega
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
d(\cdot,X\setminus\Omega)^{\beta}\,d\mu
for all
\beta\in(\beta_{0}-\varepsilon,\beta_{0}+\varepsilon)
, with constant independent of
\beta
.

In Section
3
, we generalize this result to apply to a larger class of weights, see Theorem
1.7
.

The main object of our study is the following fractional Hardy inequality:

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be open, let
1<p<\infty
, and let
0<\theta<1
. We say that
\Omega
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality
if there exists
C_{\theta,p}\geq 1
such that the following holds for for all
u\in\Lip_{c}(\Omega)
:

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d(x,X\setminus\Omega)^{\theta p}}d\mu(x)\leq C_{\theta,p}\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{d(x,y)^{\theta p}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

We note that the right-hand side of the
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality is given by the Besov energy of
u
. As this energy is nonlocal, it receives some contribution from the region where
u
vanishes. That is, the regions
X\setminus\Omega
contribute to the energy of the right-hand side. This is in contrast to the
p
-Hardy inequality, for which the energy on the right-hand side is local: since
u
vanishes on
X\setminus\Omega
, it follows that
g_{u}=0
there. For sufficient conditions allowing for integration over
\Omega
in the right-hand side of above inequality, see
[
35
]
.

3.
Regularizable weights and weighted
p
-Hardy inequalities

In this section, we consider a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, with
\mu
a doubling measure, and an open set
\Omega\subset X
. Throughout this section, we use the notation

d_{\Omega}(\cdot):=d(\cdot,X\setminus\Omega).

We show that if
\Omega
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu
, then it also satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to the weighted measure
w\,d\mu
whenever
w
is a
p
-admissible weight which is
\delta
-regularizable
, defined below, for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Our result, Theorem
1.7
, generalizes the self-improvement result Theorem
2.6
obtained in
[
47
,
46
]
by Lehrbäck for weights of the form
d_{\Omega}^{\beta}
, see Remark
3.13
. We will use Theorem
1.7
in Section
6
to prove self-improvement of the fractional Hardy inequality.

Recall that we define a weight to be a non-negative function
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X,\mu)
. We associate to such
w
a weighted measure
d\mu_{w}=wd\mu
, and call
w
doubling if
\mu_{w}
is doubling. To simplify notation, we use
w
in place of
\mu_{w}
and write
w(A)=\mu_{w}(A)
for the weighted measure of a measurable set
A
. We now define
\delta
-regularizable weights, roughly speaking, as those whose average values do not change much at Whitney scales:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\delta>0
. We say that a doubling weight is
\delta
-regularizable
(in
\Omega
) if whenever
x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega
and
B_{x}:=B(x,d_{\Omega}(x)/8)
and
B_{x^{\prime}}:=B(x^{\prime},d_{\Omega}(x^{\prime})/8)
are such that
2B_{x^{\prime}}\cap B_{x}\neq\varnothing
, we have

(3.2)

|w_{B_{x}}-w_{B_{x^{\prime}}}|\leq\delta w_{B_{x}}.

Here we again use the notation

w_{B}:=\fint_{B}w\,d\mu=\frac{w(B)}{\mu(B)}.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}=\{B_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i}
be the Whitney decomposition of
\Omega\subset X
and associated Lipschitz partition of unity, respectively, given by Lemma
2.1
. We define the discrete convolution
\widetilde{w}
of a weight
w
by

(3.3)

\widetilde{w}(x)=\sum_{i}w_{B_{i}}\varphi_{i}(x).

The key property of
\delta
-regularizable weights is the following estimate for the upper gradient of the discrete convolution of a
\delta
-regularizable weight:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set.
There exists a constant
C_{0}\geq 1
, depending only on
C_{\mu}
, such that if
w
is a
\delta
-regularizable weight in
\Omega
, then

g_{\widetilde{w}}\leq C_{0}\delta d_{\Omega}^{-1}\widetilde{w}

a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in B_{i}
. Then by the partition of unity, it follows that

\widetilde{w}(x)=w_{B_{i}}+\sum_{j}\left(w_{B_{j}}-w_{B_{i}}\right)\varphi_{j}(x)=w_{B_{i}}+\sum_{j:\,2B_{j}\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing}\left(w_{B_{j}}-w_{B_{i}}\right)\varphi_{j}(x).

Since
w
is
\delta
-regularizable, and by bounded overlap of
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}
and the properties of the Lipschitz partition of unity, we then have that

g_{\widetilde{w}}(x)\lesssim\sum_{j:\,2B_{j}\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing}\left|w_{B_{j}}-w_{B_{i}}\right|r_{j}^{-1}\lesssim\delta w_{B_{i}}d_{\Omega}(x)^{-1}\lesssim\delta d_{\Omega}(x)^{-1}\widetilde{w}(x),

with constant depending only on the doubling constant of
\mu
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.7
, the main result of this section. It establishes a weighted self-improvement for the
p
-Hardy inequalities in terms of
\delta
-regularizable weights which are
p
-admissible.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
w
be
p
-admissible and
\delta
-regularizable in
\Omega
for some
0<\delta\leq\delta_{p}
, with
\delta_{p}
to be chosen later. Since
(X,d)
is complete, and since
w\,d\mu
is doubling and supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality, it follows from
[
39
]
that there exists
1\leq q<p
such that
w\,d\mu
supports a
(1,q)
-Poincaré inequality, with constants depending only on
p
-admissibility constants of
w
. By choosing
q<p
sufficiently close to
p
such that
p/\max\{\log_{2}C_{\mu},2p\}\geq p-q
, it follows from
[
4
, Theorem 4.21]
for example, that
w\,d\mu
also supports a
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality with constants depending only on the
p
-admissibility constants of
w
. Moreover, letting
\lambda\geq 1
be the dilation constant of this
(1,q)
-Poincaré inequality, it follows that the
(p,q)
-Poincaré inequality has dilation constant
2\lambda
.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}=\{B_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i}
be the Whitney cover of
\Omega
and Lipschitz partition of unity given by Lemma
2.1
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let

u_{B_{i}}:=\frac{1}{w(B_{i})}\int_{B_{i}}uwd\mu,

and define the discrete convolution
\widetilde{u}
of
u
by

\widetilde{u}=\sum_{i}u_{B_{i}}\varphi_{i}.

We then have that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\leq 2^{p}\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u(x)-\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)+2^{p}\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x).

To estimate the first term of the right-hand side of (
3.5
), we note that for
x\in B_{i}
, we have

u(x)-\widetilde{u}(x)=u(x)-u_{B_{i}}+\sum_{j}(u_{B_{j}}-u_{B_{i}})\varphi_{j}(x).

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle|u(x)-\widetilde{u}(x)|\leq|u(x)-u_{B_{i}}|+\sum_{j:\,2B_{j}\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing}|u_{B_{j}}-u_{B_{i}}|.

We note that if
2B_{j}\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing
, then
B_{j}\subset 6B_{i}
and
B_{i}\subset 5B_{j}
. Since
w\,d\mu
is doubling and supports a
(1,q)
-Poincaré inequality, it follows that

(3.6)

\displaystyle|u_{B_{j}}-u_{B_{i}}|\lesssim\frac{1}{w(6B_{i})}\int_{6B_{i}}|u-u_{6B_{i}}|wd\mu\lesssim r_{i}\left(\frac{1}{w(6\lambda B_{i})}\int_{6\lambda B_{i}}g_{u}^{q}wd\mu\right)^{1/q}.

Hence, we have that

|u(x)-\widetilde{u}(x)|\lesssim|u(x)-u_{B_{i}}|+r_{i}\left(\frac{1}{w(6\lambda B_{i})}\int_{6\lambda B_{i}}g_{u}^{q}w\,d\mu\right)^{1/q},

where we have used bounded overlap of
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}
. The comparison constant depends only on the doubling constant of
\mu
and the
p
-admissibility constants of
w
.
Furthermore, as
w\,d\mu
supports a
(p,q)
-Poincaré inequality, and since
d_{\Omega}(x)\simeq r_{i}
for each
x\in B_{i}
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u(x)-\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}

\displaystyle w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u(x)-u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)+r_{i}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{w(6\lambda B_{i})}\int_{6\lambda B_{i}}g_{u}^{q}wd\mu\right)^{p/q}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{w(x)}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim w(B_{i})\left(\frac{1}{w(6\lambda B_{i})}\int_{6\lambda B_{i}}g_{u}^{q}wd\mu\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{i}}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu.

Here
M^{w}
is the (uncentered) Hardy-Littlewood maximal function, defined with respect to the measure
w\,d\mu
. As
w\,d\mu
is doubling,
M^{w}
is bounded from
L^{p/q}(X,wd\mu)
to
L^{p/q}(X,wd\mu)
. Using this fact and the bounded overlap of
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}
, see Lemma
2.1
, we estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.5
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u(x)-\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu\lesssim\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}wd\mu.

Here the comparison constant depends only on
p
,
C_{\mu}
, and the
p
-admissibility constants of
w
.

To estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.5
), we see that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)+\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x).

By using (
3.6
), and a similar argument to above, we estimate the first term on the right-hand side of the previous expression by

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{i}^{p}}\sum_{j:\,2B_{j}\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing}\int_{B_{i}}|u_{B_{j}}-u_{B_{i}}|^{p}wd\mu\lesssim\int_{B_{i}}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu.

We note that
w(B_{i})\simeq\widetilde{w}(B_{i})
, with comparison constant depending only on the doubling constant of
\mu
coming from the Lipschitz partition of unity. Since
d_{\Omega}\simeq r_{i}
on
B_{i}
, we then estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.8
) as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x)+\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x).

The first term on the right-hand side of the above expression can be estimated using (
3.6
) in a manner similar to (
3.9
), and so we then obtain

\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|u_{B_{i}}|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{B_{i}}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu+\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x).

Combining this inequality with (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we then have

\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{B_{i}}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu+\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x).

Thus, by bounded overlap of
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}
and boundedness of
M^{w}
from
L^{p/q}(X,wd\mu)
to
L^{p/q}(X,wd\mu)
, we estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.5
) by

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}wd\mu+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x).

Combining this estimate with (
3.7
) and (
3.5
), we then obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}wd\mu+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x),

with comparison constants depending only on
p
,
C_{\mu}
, and the
p
-admissibility constants of
w
.

Define the function
v:=|\widetilde{u}|\widetilde{w}^{1/p}
. By the Leibniz rule and chain rule for upper gradients, see for example
[
4
, Theorem 2.15, Theorem 2.16]
, and Lemma
3.4
, we then have that

g_{v}\leq g_{\widetilde{u}}\widetilde{w}^{1/p}+\frac{1}{p}g_{\widetilde{w}}\widetilde{w}^{1/p-1}|\widetilde{u}|\leq g_{\widetilde{u}}\widetilde{w}^{1/p}+\frac{C_{0}}{p}\delta d_{\Omega}^{-1}\widetilde{w}^{1/p}|\widetilde{u}|

where
C_{0}\geq 1
is the constant from Lemma
3.4
which depends only on
C_{\mu}
. Applying the
p
-Hardy inequality to the function
v
gives us

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{\Omega}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu\leq 2^{p}C_{p}\int_{\Omega}g_{\widetilde{u}}^{p}\widetilde{w}d\mu+2^{p}C_{p}\left(\frac{C_{0}\delta}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x),

where
C_{p}\geq 1
is the constant from the
p
-Hardy inequality. Therefore, if
w
is
\delta
-regularizable for
0<\delta\leq\delta_{p}:=\frac{p}{2C_{0}}\left(2C_{p}\right)^{-1/p}
, we have that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}\widetilde{w}(x)d\mu(x)\leq 2^{p+1}C_{p}\int_{\Omega}g_{\widetilde{u}}^{p}\widetilde{w}d\mu,

and so from (
3.10
) we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\Omega}(x)^{p}}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}wd\mu+\int_{\Omega}g_{\widetilde{u}}^{p}\widetilde{w}d\mu,

with comparison constant depending only on
p
,
C_{\mu}
, and the
p
-admissibility constants of
w
.

Finally, for
x\in B_{i}
, we have that

\widetilde{u}(x)=u_{B_{i}}+\sum_{j:\,2B_{j}\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing}(u_{B_{j}}-u_{B_{i}})\varphi_{j}(x),

and so by (
3.6
), we have

g_{\widetilde{u}}(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{i}}\sum_{j:\,2B_{j}\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing}|u_{B_{j}}-u_{B_{i}}|\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{w(6\lambda B_{i})}\int_{6\lambda B_{i}}g_{u}^{q}wd\mu\right)^{1/q},

where we have used the bounded overlap of
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}
.
Using the fact that
\widetilde{w}(B_{i})\simeq w(B_{i})
, along with bounded overlap of
\mathcal{W}_{\Omega}
and boundedness of
M^{w}
on
L^{p/q}(X,wd\mu)
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g_{\widetilde{u}}^{p}\widetilde{w}d\mu\leq\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}}g_{\widetilde{u}}^{p}\widetilde{w}d\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i}\widetilde{w}(B_{i})\left(\frac{1}{w(6\lambda B_{i})}\int_{6\lambda B_{i}}g_{u}^{q}wd\mu\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu\lesssim\int_{\Omega}(M^{w}g_{u}^{q})^{p/q}wd\mu\lesssim\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}wd\mu.

Here the constant depends only on
p
,
C_{\mu}
, and the
p
-admissibility constants of
w
. Combining this estimate with (
3.11
) completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.13

We see that for all
\delta>0

) is satisfied.

shows that the same conclusion holds if the

, however,

Definition 3.14
.

Let
0<c\leq 1
. We say that a set
E\subset X
is
c
-porous
if for all
x\in X
and
0<r<\diam(E)
, there exists
y\in B(x,r)
satisfying
B(y,cr)\subset X\setminus E
.

The measure of the
\rho
-neighborhood of a porous set decays in a controlled manner, as shown by the following lemma:

Lemma 3.15
.

[
8
, Theorem 2.8]

If
E\subset X
is
c
-porous, then there exist
\kappa>0
and
C\geq 1
, depending only on
c
and
C_{\mu}
, such that for all
x\in X
and
0<\rho\leq r<\diam(E)
, we have

\mu\left(\{y\in B(x,r):d(y,E)<\rho\}\right)\leq C\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\kappa}\mu(B(x,r)).

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set and let
E\subset X
be
c
-porous. Then for all
0<\delta<1
, there exists
\sigma_{0}>0
, depending only
c
,
C_{\mu}
, and
\delta
, such that for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\dist(\cdot,E)^{\sigma}
is
\delta
-regularizable in
\Omega
whenever
|\sigma|<\sigma_{0}
. Furthermore, the dependence of
\sigma_{0}
on
\delta
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
, and let
x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega
and
r,r^{\prime}>0
satisfy the conditions of Definition
3.1
. Let
B:=B(x,r)
and
B^{\prime}:=B(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})
. Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and set
w:=d(\cdot,E)^{\sigma}
. We consider four cases, depending on the sign of
\sigma
and the distance of
x
to
E
relative to
r
.

Case I:
Suppose that
\sigma\geq 0
and
d(x,E)\geq 7r
. Since
2B^{\prime}\cap B\neq\varnothing
, it follows that
d(x^{\prime},E)>2r^{\prime}
, and so we have that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{6}{7}d(x,E)\right)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq w_{B}\leq\left(\frac{8}{7}d(x,E)\right)^{\sigma}

and also that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}d(x^{\prime},E)\right)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq w_{B^{\prime}}\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}d(x^{\prime},E)\right)^{\sigma}.

Since
2B^{\prime}\cap B\neq\varnothing
, it also follows that

\frac{3}{7}d(x,E)\leq d(x^{\prime},E)\leq\frac{11}{7}d(x,E).

Combining these estimates, we have that

\displaystyle\left|w_{B^{\prime}}-w_{B}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|w_{B^{\prime}}/w_{B}-1\right|w_{B}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left(\frac{11}{4}\right)^{\sigma}-1,1-\left(\frac{3}{16}\right)^{\sigma}\right\}w_{B}

Hence (
3.2
) holds in this case if

0\leq|\sigma|<\min\left\{\frac{\log(\delta+1)}{\log(11/4)},\frac{\log(1/(1-\delta))}{\log(16/3)}\right\}=:\sigma_{1}.

Case II:

Suppose now that
\sigma\geq 0
and
d(x,E)<7r
. In this case, we also have that
d(x^{\prime},E)<14r^{\prime}
since
2B^{\prime}\cap B\neq\varnothing
. Since
\sigma\geq 0
, we have that

w(B)\leq(d(x,E)+r)^{\sigma}\mu(B)<(8r)^{\sigma}\mu(B).

For
0<\rho<r
, we have by Lemma
3.15
that

\displaystyle w(B)\geq w(B\setminus\{y\in X:d(y,E)<\rho\})

\displaystyle\geq\rho^{\sigma}\mu(B\setminus\{y\in X:d(y,E)<\rho\})

\displaystyle\geq\rho^{\sigma}\left(1-C(\rho/r)^{\kappa}\right)\mu(B),

where
C\geq 1
and
\kappa>0
are the constants from Lemma
3.15
. Combining these estimates, we have that

\rho^{\sigma}\left(1-C(\rho/r)^{\kappa}\right)\leq w_{B}\leq(8r)^{\sigma}.

By the same argument for
0<\rho<r^{\prime}
, we also have

\rho^{\sigma}\left(1-C(\rho/r^{\prime})^{\kappa}\right)\leq w_{B^{\prime}}\leq(15r^{\prime})^{\sigma}.

Thus for
0<\rho<\min\{r,r^{\prime}\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|w_{B^{\prime}}-w_{B}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|w_{B^{\prime}}/w_{B}-1\right|w_{B}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{(8r)^{\sigma}}{\rho^{\sigma}(1-C(\rho/r^{\prime})^{\kappa})}-1,\,1-\frac{\rho^{\sigma}(1-C(\rho/r)^{\kappa})}{(15r^{\prime})^{\sigma}}\right\}w_{B}.

If
\rho=\tau_{1}r
, where

\tau_{1}:=\frac{7}{10}\left(C^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{1+\delta/2}\right)\right)^{1/\kappa},

then

\displaystyle\frac{(8r)^{\sigma}}{\rho^{\sigma}(1-C(\rho/r^{\prime})^{\kappa})}-1=\left(\frac{8}{\tau_{1}}\right)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{1-C(\tau_{1}r/r^{\prime})^{\kappa}}-1

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{8}{\tau_{1}}\right)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{1-C(10\tau_{1}/7)^{\kappa}}-1

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{8}{\tau_{1}}\right)^{\sigma}(1+\delta/2)-1.

Likewise, if
\rho=\tau_{2}r^{\prime}
, where

\tau_{2}:=\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{\delta}{2C}\right)^{1/\kappa},

we have that

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\rho^{\sigma}(1-C(\rho/r)^{\kappa})}{(15r^{\prime})^{\sigma}}=1-\left(\frac{\tau_{2}}{15}\right)^{\sigma}\left(1-C(\tau_{2}r^{\prime}/r)^{\kappa}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\left(\frac{\tau_{2}}{15}\right)^{\sigma}\left(1-C(3\tau_{2}/2)^{\kappa}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\left(\frac{\tau_{2}}{15}\right)^{\sigma}\left(1-\delta/2\right).

Hence, from these estimates and (
3.18
) it follows that (
3.2
) holds in this case if

0\leq|\sigma|<\min\left\{\frac{\log((1+\delta)/(1+\delta/2))}{\log(8/\tau_{1})},\,\frac{\log((1-\delta/2)/(1-\delta))}{\log(15/\tau_{2})}\right\}=:\sigma_{2}.

Case III:
Suppose now that
\sigma<0
and
d(x,E)\geq 7r
. In this case, by repeating the computations from Case I, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{8}{7}d(x,E)\right)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq w_{B}\leq\left(\frac{6}{7}d(x,E)\right)^{\sigma}

as well as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{2}d(x^{\prime},E)\right)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq w_{B^{\prime}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}d(x^{\prime},E)\right)^{\sigma}.

We note that since
\sigma<0
, these are the reverse of the inequalities obtained in Case I. From these estimates, it then follows that

\displaystyle\left|w_{B^{\prime}}-w_{B}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|w_{B^{\prime}}/w_{B}-1\right|w_{B}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{1-\left(\frac{11}{4}\right)^{\sigma},\left(\frac{3}{16}\right)^{\sigma}-1\right\}w_{B}.

Thus, (
3.2
) holds in this case if

\displaystyle 0<|\sigma|<\min\left\{\frac{\log(1/(1-\delta))}{\log(11/4)},\,\frac{\log(1+\delta)}{\log(16/3)}\right\}=:\sigma_{3}.

Case IV:
Suppose now that
\sigma<0
and
d(x,E)<7r
. In this case, we have that

\displaystyle w(B)\geq(8r)^{\sigma}\mu(B),

and since
d(x^{\prime},E)<14r^{\prime}
, it follows that

\displaystyle w(B^{\prime})\geq(15r^{\prime})^{\sigma}\mu(B^{\prime}).

Let
0<\rho\leq r
, and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let

N_{k}:=\{y\in X:d(y,E)<2^{-k}\rho\}.

We then have that

\displaystyle w(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{B\cap N_{0}}d(\cdot,E)^{\sigma}d\mu+\int_{B\setminus N_{0}}d(\cdot,E)^{\sigma}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{B\cap N_{k}\setminus N_{k+1}}d(\cdot,E)^{\sigma}d\mu+\rho^{\sigma}\mu(B\setminus N_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{-k-1}\rho)^{\sigma}\mu(B\cap N_{k})+\rho^{\sigma}\mu(B).

By Lemma
3.15
, we have that

\mu(B\cap N_{k})\leq C\left(\frac{2^{-k}\rho}{r}\right)^{\kappa}\mu(B),

and so substituting this into the previous expression, we obtain

\displaystyle w(B)\leq C2^{|\sigma|}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\kappa}\rho^{\sigma}\mu(B)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k(\kappa-|\sigma|)}+\rho^{\sigma}\mu(B).

Thus, for
0<|\sigma|<\kappa/2
, and setting

C_{1}:=C\frac{2^{\kappa/2}}{1-2^{-\kappa/2}},

we have that

\displaystyle w(B)\leq\rho^{\sigma}\mu(B)\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r_{i}}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right).

Recall that the
C\geq 1
and
\kappa>0
are the constants from Lemma
3.15
. For
0<\rho<r^{\prime}
, the same computation, with
0<|\sigma|<\kappa/2
gives us

w(B^{\prime})\leq\rho^{\sigma}\mu(B^{\prime})\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right).

Combining these two estimates with the corresponding lower bound estimates above, we obtain for
0<\rho\leq\min\{r,r^{\prime}\}
,

\displaystyle(8r)^{\sigma}\leq w_{B}\leq\rho^{\sigma}\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right)

and

\displaystyle(15r^{\prime})^{\sigma}\leq w_{B^{\prime}}\leq\rho^{\sigma}\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right).

It then follows that

\displaystyle\left|w_{B^{\prime}}-w_{B}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|w_{B^{\prime}}/w_{B}-1\right|w_{B}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left(\frac{\rho}{8r}\right)^{\sigma}\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right)-1,\,1-\left(\frac{15r^{\prime}}{\rho}\right)^{\sigma}\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right)^{-1}\right\}w_{B}.

Let
\rho:=\min\{\tau_{3}r^{\prime},\tau_{4}r\}
, where

\tau_{3}:=\left(\frac{\delta}{2C_{1}}\right)^{1/\kappa}

and

\tau_{4}:=\left(C_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{1-\delta/2}-1\right)\right)^{1/\kappa}.

Note that
\rho\leq\min\{r,r^{\prime}\}
, and by
the assumptions on
x
,
x^{\prime}
,
r
, and
r^{\prime}
, we also have that
\rho\geq\min\{7\tau_{3}/10,\tau_{4}\}r
, and
\rho\geq\min\{\tau_{3},2\tau_{4}/3\}r^{\prime}
. From these choices, it then follows that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho}{8r}\right)^{\sigma}\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right)-1\leq\left(\frac{8}{\min\{7\tau_{3}/10,\tau_{4}\}}\right)^{|\sigma|}(1+\delta/2)-1.

Similarly, we have that

\displaystyle 1-\left(\frac{15r^{\prime}}{\rho}\right)^{\sigma}\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\kappa}+1\right)^{-1}\leq 1-\left(\frac{\min\{\tau_{3},2\tau_{4}/3\}}{15}\right)^{|\sigma|}(1-\delta/2).

From (
3.19
), we then see that (
3.2
) holds in this case if

\displaystyle 0<|\sigma|<\min\left\{\frac{\log((1+\delta)/(1+\delta/2))}{\log(8/\min\{7\tau_{3}/10,\tau_{4}\})},\frac{\log((1-\delta/2)/(1-\delta))}{\log(15/\min\{\tau_{3},2\tau_{4}/3\})}\right\}=:\sigma_{4}.

Having exhausted all cases, we complete the proof by taking
\sigma_{0}:=\min_{1\leq i\leq 4}\sigma_{i}
. We note that in addition to depending on
\delta
, the constant
\sigma_{0}
depends only on the constants
C
and
\kappa
from Lemma
3.15
, which depend only on the porosity constant of
E
and the doubling constant of
\mu
. ∎

4.
The hyperbolic filling of a compact, doubling metric measure space

We now turn our attention towards the fractional Hardy inequality. Recall from Definition
2.7
that for
0<\theta<1
and
1<p<\infty
, the right-hand side of the
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality is given by the Besov energy (
2.2
). The Besov spaces, defined by (
2.2
), arise naturally in both the Euclidean and metric settings as traces to the boundary of Sobolev functions defined on a domain, provided the domain satisfies certain geometric assumptions, see for example
[
25
,
37
,
50
]
. It was shown in
[
5
]
that Besov spaces on a compact, doubling metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
always arise as traces of Newton-Sobolev functions on an auxiliary metric measure space which has
Z
as a boundary. We now outline the construction from
[
5
]
, as well as some properties of the resulting space, which will allow us in the subsequent sections to relate
(\theta,p)
- and
p
-Hardy inequalities.

Throughout this section, we let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a compact, doubling metric measure space and assume that
\diam(Z)<1
. Fix
\alpha,\tau>1
, and fix
z_{0}\in Z
. Let
A_{0}:=\{z_{0}\}
, and for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
A_{i}
be a maximally
\alpha^{-i}
-separated subset of
Z
, constructed inductively so that
A_{i}\subset A_{i+1}
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we define the
i
-th level vertices by

V_{i}:=\bigcup_{z\in A_{i}}(z,i),

and define the vertex set

V=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{z\in A_{i}}(z,i).

Given
(z,i),(y,j)\in V
, we say that
(z,i)
and
(y,j)
are neighbors, denoted
(z,i)\sim(y,j)
, if and only if
|i-j|\leq 1
and in addition, either
i=j
and
B_{Z}(z,\tau\alpha^{-i})\cap B_{Z}(y,\tau\alpha^{-j})\neq\varnothing
, or
i=j\pm 1
and
B_{Z}(z,\alpha^{-i})\cap B_{Z}(y,\alpha^{-j})\neq\varnothing
. We then turn the vertex set
V
into a metric graph
X
by gluing a unit interval
[v,w]
between each pair of neighboring vertices
v,w
. We denote by
d_{X}
the path metric on
X
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the uniformized metric on
X
, given by

d_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\varepsilon d_{X}(\cdot,v_{0})}ds,

where the infimum is taken over all paths
\gamma
in
X
with endpoints
x
and
y
.
We denote by
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
the completion of
X
with respect to the metric
d_{\varepsilon}
, and we set
\partial_{\varepsilon}X:=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus X
. We refer to
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon})
as the
uniformized hyperbolic filling
of
(Z,d,\nu)
. For clarity, balls in
Z
, taken with respect to the
d
, are denoted by
B_{Z}
, while balls in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
taken with respect to
d_{\varepsilon}
are denoted by
B_{\varepsilon}
.

For
\beta>0
, we define the vertex weights
\hat{\mu}_{\beta}
by

\hat{\mu}_{\beta}((z,i))=e^{-\beta i}\nu(B_{Z}(z,\alpha^{-i})).

We then define the measure
\mu_{\beta}
on
X
by

\mu_{\beta}(A):=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{w\sim v}\left(\hat{\mu}_{\beta}(v)+\hat{\mu}_{\beta}(w)\right)\mathcal{H}^{1}(A\cap[v,w]).

From this definition, we see that if
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
is such that
|\sigma|<\beta
, then

(4.1)

\mu_{\beta+\sigma}\simeq d_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,Z)^{\sigma/\varepsilon}d\mu_{\beta}.

That is, perturbing the parameter
\beta
is equivalent to weighting the measure
\mu_{\beta}
, see
[
5
, Section 10]
. The following summarizes the fundamental properties of
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, obtained in
[
5
]
:

Theorem 4.2
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a compact metric measure space,
\diam(Z)<1
, with
\nu
a doubling measure. Let
\alpha,\tau>1
and let
\varepsilon:=\log\alpha
. Then for all
\beta>0
,
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
satisfies the following:

(1)

(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon})
is a geodesic space.

(2)

(Z,d)
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
(\partial_{\varepsilon}X,d_{\varepsilon})
, with bi-Lipschitz constant depending only on
\alpha
and
\tau
.

(3)

(X,d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
are doubling and support a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality.

(4)

For all
z\in Z
and
0<r<2\diam(Z)
, we have that

\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(z,r)\cap Z)\simeq\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(z,r))}{r^{\beta/\varepsilon}}.

(5)

If
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
are such that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
, then there exist bounded trace and extension operators

T:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)\quad and\quad E:B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)\to N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta}),

such that
T\circ E=Id
. Moreover, for all
u\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and
f\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
, we have

\|Tu\|^{p}_{B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)}\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta},\qquad\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{Ef}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\lesssim\|f\|^{p}_{B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)}.

In the above results, the constants depend only on
\alpha
,
\tau
,
\beta
, and
C_{\nu}
.

The trace and extensions operators above have the following explicit constructions: for
u\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and
z\in Z
, we have

Tu(z)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(z,r)}u\,d\mu_{\beta}.

Likewise, for
f\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
,
Ef
is first defined on the vertices of
X
by

(4.3)

Ef((z,i))=\fint_{B_{Z}(z,\alpha^{-i})}f\,d\nu.

To define
Ef
on all of
X
, these values are then extended piece-wise linearly (with respect to
d_{\varepsilon}
) to the edges of
X
.

Balls centered at vertices of
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
form a Whitney-type covering of
X
, as shown in the following lemma. We first note that if
\alpha>1
and
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
, then for all
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
z\in A_{i}
, we have that

(4.4)

d_{\varepsilon}((z,i),Z)=d_{\varepsilon}((z,i),z)=\frac{\alpha^{-i}}{\log\alpha}.

This can be shown by direct computation using the definition of
d_{\varepsilon}
, along with the fact that
(z,i+j)\in V
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, see
[
5
, Section 4]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha=e^{1/4}
, and let
\varepsilon:=\log\alpha
. Then the following hold:

(1)

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
z\in A_{i}
, we have that
d_{\varepsilon}((z,i),Z)=4\alpha^{-i}
.

(2)

X=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{z\in A_{i}}B_{\varepsilon}((z,i),\alpha^{-i})
.

(3)

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{z\in A_{i}}\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}\left((z,i),\alpha^{-i}\right)}\leq C:=C(\alpha,\tau,C_{\nu})
.

Proof.

Claim (
1
) follows directly from (
4.4
). To prove Claim (
2
), let
v,w\in V
be such that
v\sim w
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
v=(z,i)
and
w=(y,j)
with
j\in\{i,i+1\}
. If
j=i
, then by the definition of
d_{\varepsilon}
along with
[
5
, Proposition 4.4]
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{-i}}{2\tau\alpha}\leq\frac{d_{Z}(z,y)}{2\tau\alpha}\leq d_{\varepsilon}(z,w)\leq\ell_{\varepsilon}([v,w])=\alpha^{-i}.

If
j=i+1
, then we have from the definition of
d_{\varepsilon}
and choice of
\alpha=e^{1/4}
that

d_{\varepsilon}(v,w)=\ell_{\varepsilon}([v,w])=\alpha^{-i}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha\log\alpha}\right)<\alpha^{-i}.

In either case, it follows that
[v,w]\subset B_{\varepsilon}(v,\alpha^{-i})\cup B_{\varepsilon}(w,\alpha^{-j})
. By our choice of
\alpha=e^{1/4}
, it follows from (
4.4
) that
\dist_{\varepsilon}(v,Z)=4\alpha^{-i}
. Thus,
B_{\varepsilon}(v,\alpha^{-i})\cap Z=\varnothing
, and so (
2
) holds.

To prove (
3
), we first claim that if
v=(z,i)
and
w=(y,j)
are vertices such that
j=i+k
for some
k\geq 3
, then
B_{\varepsilon}(v,\alpha^{-i})\cap B_{\varepsilon}(w,\alpha^{-j})=\varnothing
. Indeed, if
\gamma
is a path in
X
joining
v
to
w
, then
\gamma
must contain a vertical edge joining the
(i+l-1)
-th level of vertices to the
(i+l)
-th level of vertices for each
1\leq l\leq k
. Thus, it follows that

d_{\varepsilon}(v,w)\geq\int_{i}^{i+k}e^{-\varepsilon t}dt=\frac{\alpha^{-i}(1-\alpha^{-k})}{\log\alpha}.

Since
k\geq 3
, and since
\alpha=e^{1/4}
, it follows that

d_{\varepsilon}(v,w)\geq\frac{\alpha^{-i}(1-\alpha^{-k})}{\log\alpha}>\alpha^{-i}+\alpha^{-j},

hence
B_{\varepsilon}(v,\alpha^{-i})\cap B_{\varepsilon}(w,\alpha^{-j})=\varnothing
, and the claim follows.

Now, let
x\in X
. Then, there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
z\in A_{i}
such that
x\in B_{\varepsilon}((z,i),\alpha^{-i})
. If
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
y\in A_{j}
are such that
x\in B_{\varepsilon}((y,j),\alpha^{-j})
, then we know from the above claim that
|i-j|\leq 3
. If
z\neq y
, then since
z,y\in A_{i+3}
, it follows from
[
5
, Proposition 4.4]
that

\alpha^{-(i+3)}\leq d_{Z}(z,y)\leq 2\tau\alpha d_{\varepsilon}((z,i),(y,j))\leq 2\tau\alpha(\alpha^{-i}+\alpha^{-j})\leq 2\tau\alpha(1+\alpha^{3})\alpha^{-i}.

Therefore, since
d_{Z}
is doubling, there exists a constant
C:=C(\alpha,\tau,C_{\nu},\diam(Z))
such that the set

\{y\in A_{i+3}:\exists j\in\{i-3,\dots,i+3\}\text{ s.t. }x\in B((y,j),\alpha^{-j})\}

contains at most
C
elements. For each
y
in this set, the only possible vertices
(y,j)
for which
B_{\varepsilon}((y,j),\alpha^{-j})
may contain
x
are
\{(y,i-3),\,(y,i-2),\dots,\,(y,i+3)\}
. Thus, it follows that the set

\{(y,j):j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\,y\in A_{j},\,x\in B_{\varepsilon}((y,j),\alpha^{-j})\}

contains at most
7C
elements, and so (
3
) follows.
∎

Recall the definition of a porous set given in Definition
3.14
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\alpha,\tau>1
, and let
\varepsilon:=\log\alpha
. Then there exists
0<c<1
, depending only on
\alpha
, such that
Z\subset\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
is
c
-porous.

Proof.

Let
z\in Z
and let
0<r\leq\diam(Z)
. Let
K:=2(1+1/\log\alpha)
. Let
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be the smallest non-negative integer such that
\alpha^{-i}<r/K
. Then there exists
z^{\prime}\in A_{i}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(z,r/K)
. Let
v:=(z^{\prime},i)\in V
. We then have from (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(z,v)\leq d_{\varepsilon}(z,z^{\prime})+d_{\varepsilon}(v,z^{\prime})<\frac{r}{K}+\frac{\alpha^{-i}}{\log\alpha}<\frac{r}{K}(1+1/\log\alpha)=r/2.

Thus,
v\in B_{\varepsilon}(z,r/2)
. Since
d_{\varepsilon}(v,Z)=\alpha^{-i}/\log\alpha
from (
4.4
), it follows that
B_{\varepsilon}(v,\alpha^{-i}/(2\log\alpha))\subset\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus Z
. By our choice of
i
, we then have that

B_{\varepsilon}(v,r/(4\alpha(1+\log\alpha)))\subset B_{\varepsilon}(v,\alpha^{-i}/(2\log\alpha))\subset\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus Z.

Thus,
Z
is
c
-porous in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
with
c:=(4\alpha(1+\log\alpha))^{-1}
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The assumption that
\diam(Z)<1
is not overly restrictive. If
\diam(Z)\geq 1
, then we may rescale the metric, replacing
d
with
d/(2\diam(Z))
. In this case, analogs of the above results hold for
(Z,d,\nu)
; the only difference is that the constants present in (some of) the above results will depend additionally upon
\diam(Z)
.

5.
Fractional Hardy inequalities and the hyperbolic filling

In this section, we assume that
(Z,d,\nu)
is a compact metric measure space, with
\nu
a doubling measure. By rescaling the metric
d
if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
\diam(Z)<1
, see Remark
4.7
. We fix parameters
\alpha=e^{1/4}
,
\tau=2
, and we let
\varepsilon:=\log\alpha=1/4
. For each
\beta>0
, we consider the uniformized hyperbolic filling
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
of
(Z,d,\nu)
as constructed in Section
4
. The main result of this section is the following, which relates the validity of
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequalities on
Z
to
p
-Hardy inequalities on
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
. This theorem is proven by combining Proposition
5.6
and Proposition
5.15
below.

In what follows, we denumerate the
\alpha^{-i}
-separated set
A_{i}
used in the construction of
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
by

A_{i}:=\{z_{i,j}\}_{j\in I_{i}}.

For ease of notation, we set

I:=\{(i,j):i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\,j\in I_{i}\},

and for each
(i,j)\in I
, we set

B_{i,j}:=B_{\varepsilon}((z_{i,j},i),\alpha^{-i}).

By Lemma
4.5
, the collection
\{B_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I}
covers
X
and has bounded overlap. For each
(i,j)\in I
, we denote by
U_{i,j}
the set

U_{i,j}:=B_{Z}(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i})\cap Z.

Recall that we denote balls in
Z
taken with respect to the metric
d
by
B_{Z}
, while balls in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
, taken with respect to
d_{\varepsilon}
, are denoted by
B_{\varepsilon}
. For a constant
C>0
we also denote by
CU_{i,j}
the set
B_{Z}(z_{i,j},C\alpha^{-i})\cap Z
. We now show that given a closed set
E\subset Z
, there exists a subcollection of
\{U_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I}
which forms a Whitney-type cover of
Z\setminus E
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
E\subset Z
be a closed set. Then there exists
I_{E}\subset I
such that the following hold:

(1)

Z\setminus E=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}U_{i,j}
,

(2)

For each
(i,j)\in I_{E}
, we have that
6\alpha^{-i}\leq d(U_{i,j},E)\leq(8\alpha)\alpha^{-i}
,

(3)

\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\chi_{3U_{i,j}}\leq C:=C(\alpha,C_{\nu})
.

Proof.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let

(5.2)

N_{i}:=\{z\in Z\setminus E:8\alpha^{-i}\leq d(z,E)<8\alpha^{-(i-1)}\},

and let

I_{i,E}:=\{j\in I_{i}:U_{i,j}\cap N_{i}\neq\varnothing\}.

We then define

I_{E}:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\{(i,j)\in I:j\in I_{i,E}\}.

For
z\in Z\setminus E
, there exists a unique
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
z\in N_{i}
. As
A_{i}
is a maximally
\alpha^{-i}
-separated subset of
Z
, there
z_{i,j}\in A_{i}
such that
x\in B_{Z}(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i})=U_{i,j}
. Thus,
(i,j)\in I_{i,E}
, and so it follows that
Z\setminus E\subset\bigcup_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}U_{i,j}
.

For
(i,j)\in I_{E}
, we have that there exists
y\in U_{i,j}\cap N_{i}
. For any
z\in U_{i,j}
, it follows from the triangle inequality that
d(z,E)\geq d(y,E)-d(y,z)\geq 8\alpha^{-i}-2\alpha^{-i}=6\alpha^{-i}
,
and so we have that

(5.3)

d(U_{i,j},E)\geq 6\alpha^{-i}.

This completes the proof of (
1
), as well as the first inequality of (
2
).
Since
y\in U_{i,j}\cap N_{i}
, we also have that

d(U_{i,j},E)\leq d(y,E)\leq(8\alpha)\alpha^{-i},

which gives us the second inequality in (
2
).

Now, let
(i,j)\in I_{E}
, and let
z\in 3U_{i,j}
. Since there exists
y\in U_{i,j}\cap N_{i}
, it follows from the triangle inequality that

d(z,E)\leq d(z,y)+d(y,E)<(6+8\alpha)\alpha^{-i}

and also that

d(z,E)\geq d(y,E)-d(y,z)>2\alpha^{-i}.

Hence we have that

2\alpha^{-i}<d(3U_{i,j},E)<(6+8\alpha)\alpha^{-i}.

From this and the choice of
\alpha=e^{1/4}
, it follows that if
l\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
l>7
, then
3U_{i,j}\cap N_{i+l}=\varnothing
, and if
l>5
, then
3U_{i,j}\cap N_{i-l}=\varnothing
. Hence,
3U_{i,j}\subset\bigcup_{l=-5}^{7}N_{i+l}
.
From this, we see that if
i_{1},i_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
are such that
|i_{1}-i_{2}|>12
, then

(5.4)

\left(\bigcup_{j\in I_{i_{1},E}}3U_{i_{1},j}\right)\cap\left(\bigcup_{j\in I_{i_{2},E}}3U_{i_{2},j}\right)=\varnothing.

Let
\zeta\in Z\setminus E
. There is a unique
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
\zeta\in N_{i_{0}}
, and so from (
5.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\chi_{3U_{i,j}}(\zeta)=\sum_{i=i_{0}-12}^{i_{0}+12}\sum_{j\in I_{i,E}}\chi_{3U_{i,j}}(\zeta).

Since
\{z_{i,j}\}_{j\in I_{i,E}}
is an
\alpha^{-i}
-separated set and
\nu
is doubling, there exists
C:=C(\alpha,C_{\nu})
such that
\sum_{j\in I_{i,E}}\chi_{3U_{i,j}}\leq C
. Therefore, substituting this into the previous expression, we have that

\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\chi_{3U_{i,j}}(\zeta)\leq 24C,

which completes the proof of (
3
).∎

Given
(i,j)\in I
, the structure of the hyperbolic filling also gives us a natural collection of balls with which to chain
B_{i,j}
to
U_{i,j}
, as we show with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(i,j)\in I
, and let
z\in U_{i,j}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
k\geq i
, there exists
j_{k}\in I_{k}
such that the collection
\{B_{k}(z):=B_{k,j_{k}}\}_{k=i}^{\infty}
satisfies the following properties for all
k\geq i
:

(1)

B_{i}(z)=B_{i,j}
,

(2)

\rad(B_{k}(z))=\alpha^{-k}\simeq d_{\varepsilon}(z,B_{k}(z))
,

(3)

B_{k+1}(z)\subset 2B_{k}(z)
for all
k\geq i
,

(4)

B_{k}(z)\subset 5B_{i,j}
,

(5)

\mu_{\beta}(B_{k}(z))\simeq\alpha^{-k\beta/\varepsilon}\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(z,\alpha^{-k}))
,

(6)

If
u\in\Lip(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon})
, then
Tu(z)=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{B_{k}(z)}
.

Here
T
is the trace operator given in Theorem
4.2
.

Proof.

Set
j_{i}:=j
, and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k>i
, choose
j_{k}\in I_{k}
such that
d(z,z_{k,j_{k}})<\alpha^{-k}
. Such a
j_{k}
exists, as
A_{k}
is a maximally
\alpha^{-k}
-separated set. Setting
v_{k}:=(z_{k,j_{k}},k)\in V
, it follows from the definition of the neighborhood relationship between vertices of
X
, given in Section
4
, that
v_{k}\sim v_{k+1}
for all
k\geq i
.

For each
k\geq i
, set

B_{k}(z):=B_{k,j_{k}}=B_{\varepsilon}(z_{k,j_{k}},\alpha^{-k}).

By (
4.4
), it follows that

d_{\varepsilon}(z,B_{k}(z))\geq d_{\varepsilon}(B_{k}(z),Z)\geq 3\alpha^{-k}.

Likewise, by the bi-Lipschitz equivalence of
d
and
d_{\varepsilon}
, as well as (
4.4
), we have

d_{\varepsilon}(z,B_{k}(z))\leq d_{\varepsilon}(z,z_{k,j_{k}})+d_{\varepsilon}(z_{k,j_{k}},v_{k})\simeq d(z,z_{k,j_{k}})+\alpha^{-k}\lesssim\alpha^{-k}.

Thus, (
1
) and (
2
) hold.

Since
v_{k}\sim v_{k+1}
, it follows from the definition of
d_{\varepsilon}
and choice of
\alpha=e^{1/4}
that

d_{\varepsilon}(v_{k},v_{k+1})\leq\ell_{\varepsilon}([v_{k},v_{k+1}])=\int_{k}^{k+1}e^{-\varepsilon t}\,dt=4(1-1/\alpha)\alpha^{-k}<\alpha^{-k}.

Thus,
v_{k+1}\in B_{k}(z)
, and so we have have that
B_{k+1}(z)\subset 2B_{k}(z)
, proving (
3
).

If
x\in B_{k}(z)
, then by (
4.4
), we have that

d_{\varepsilon}(v_{i},x)\leq d_{\varepsilon}(v_{i},v_{k})+d_{\varepsilon}(v_{k},x)<4\alpha^{-i}+\alpha^{-k}\leq 5\alpha^{-i},

and so we have that
B_{k}(z)\subset B_{i,j}
, proving (
4
).

To prove (
5
), we note that by the bi-Lipschitz equivalence of
d
and
d_{\varepsilon}
, there is a constant
C:=C(\alpha,\tau)\geq 1
such that
d_{\varepsilon}(z,z_{k,j_{k}})<C\alpha^{-k}
. From (
4.4
) and our choice of
\alpha=e^{1/4}
, it then follows that

B_{k}(z)\subset B_{\varepsilon}(z_{k,j_{k}},5\alpha^{-k})\subset B_{\varepsilon}(z,(5+C)\alpha^{-k}).

Since
\mu_{\beta}
is doubling, we then have that

\mu_{\beta}(B_{k}(z))\leq\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(z,(5+C)\alpha^{-k})\simeq\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(z,\alpha^{-k})).

Likewise, by (
4.4
), we have that
B_{\varepsilon}(z,\alpha^{-k})\subset B(z_{k,j_{k}},(1+C)\alpha^{-k})\subset(5+C)B_{k}(z)
,
and so again by the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
, we obtain

\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(z,\alpha^{-k}))\leq\mu_{\beta}((5+C)B_{k}(z))\simeq\mu_{\beta}(B_{k}(z)).

From these two inequalities and the
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensional relationship between
\mu_{\beta}
and
\nu
given by Theorem
4.2
, we have that

\mu_{\beta}(B_{k}(z))\simeq\alpha^{-k\beta/\varepsilon}\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(z,\alpha^{-k})),

proving (
5
).

Finally, if
u\in\Lip(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon})
, then
Tu
is given by the restriction of
u
to
Z
, and is thus also Lipschitz. Moreover, for each
x\in B_{k}(z)
, it follows that

d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\leq d_{\varepsilon}(x,v_{k})+d_{\varepsilon}(v_{k},z_{k,j_{k}})+d_{\varepsilon}(z_{k,j_{k}},z)<6C\alpha^{-k}.

Hence,

|Tu(z)-u_{B_{k}(z)}|\leq\fint_{B_{k}(z)}|u(z)-u(x)|d\mu_{\beta}(x)\lesssim\alpha^{-k}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. This proves (
6
).
∎

We now show that a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality on
Z
implies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
on
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
, for an appropriate choice of
\theta
,
p
, and
\beta
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
E\subset Z
be a closed set, let
1<p<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
\beta>0
be such that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
, and suppose that
Z\setminus E
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality. Then
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
, with constant depending only on
\theta
,
p
,
C_{\nu}
, and
C_{\theta,p}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\Lip_{c}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E)
, and for ease of notation, let
\mu:=\mu_{\beta}
. For
(i,j)\in I
, let
B_{i,j}
and
U_{i,j}
be as defined above.
From Lemma
4.5
, we then have that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)

(5.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I}|(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}+\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|u(x)-(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x),

where
T
is the trace operator given by Theorem
4.2
.

To estimate the first term of (
5.7
), we decompose the collection
\{B_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I}
in the following manner. Let
I_{E}\subset I
be the indexing set given by Lemma
5.1
, and for each
(i,j)\in I_{E}
, let

I_{i,j}:=\{(k,l)\in I:k>i,\,l\in I_{k},\,U_{k,l}\cap U_{i,j}\neq\varnothing\}.

Let
I_{1}:=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}I_{i,j}
, and let
I_{2}:=I\setminus I_{1}
.

For
(k,l)\in I_{1}
, we have from the bi-Lipschitz equivalence of
d
and
d_{\varepsilon}
, along with (
4.4
) that

(5.8)

\displaystyle d(U_{k,l},E)

\displaystyle\simeq d_{\varepsilon}(U_{k,l},E)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\varepsilon}(U_{k,l},B_{k,l})+d_{\varepsilon}(B_{k,l},E)\lesssim d_{\varepsilon}(B_{k,l},Z)+d_{\varepsilon}(B_{k,l},E)\lesssim d_{\varepsilon}(B_{k,l},E).

Here the comparison constants depend only the bi-Lipschitz constants between
d
and
d_{\varepsilon}
, which depend only on
\alpha
and
\tau
, see
[
5
, Proposition 4.4]
. Moreover, if
(k,l)\in I_{1}
, then there exists
(i,j)\in I_{E}
such that
(k,l)\in I_{i,j}
, and so there exists
y\in U_{k,l}\cap U_{i,j}
. Thus, for
z\in U_{k,l}
, it follows from (
5.3
) and Lemma
5.1
(
2
) that

(5.9)

\displaystyle d(z,E)\geq d(U_{i,j},E)-d(y,z)\geq 6\alpha^{-i}-2\alpha^{-k}\geq 4\alpha^{-k}\geq(2\alpha)^{-1}d(U_{k,l},E),

where we have used the fact that
k>i
, since
(k,l)\in I_{1}
. Using (
5.8
), the
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensional relationship between
\mu
and
\nu
given by Theorem
4.2
, as well as (
5.9
), we then obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{1}}|(Tu)_{U_{k,l}}|^{p}\int_{B_{k,l}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{i,j}}|(Tu)_{U_{k,l}}|^{p}\int_{B_{k,l}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{i,j}}\frac{\mu(B_{k,l})}{\nu(U_{k,l})}\int_{U_{k,l}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(U_{k,l},E)^{p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{i,j}}\alpha^{-k\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{U_{k,l}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(U_{k,l},E)^{p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{i,j}}\alpha^{-k\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{U_{k,l}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{p}}d\nu(z).

We note that for each
k
, the collection
\{U_{k,l}\}_{l\in I_{k}}
has bounded overlap since
\nu
is doubling and
\{z_{k,l}\}_{l\in I_{k}}
is
\alpha^{-k}
-separated. Furthermore, by our choice of
\alpha
, if
(k,l)\in I_{i,j}
, then
U_{k,l}\subset 3U_{i,j}
. From Lemma
5.1
, it also follows that if
(i,j)\in I_{E}
and
z\in 3U_{i,j}
, then
d(z,E)\geq 4\alpha^{-i}
. From these facts, as well as the bounded overlap of
\{3U_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}
, also given by Lemma
5.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{i,j}}\alpha^{-k\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{U_{k,l}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\sum_{k=i+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\,s.t.\,\\
(k,l)\in I_{i,j}\end{subarray}}\alpha^{-k\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{U_{k,l}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\sum_{k=i+1}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{3U_{i,j}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\alpha^{-i\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{3U_{i,j}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\int_{3U_{i,j}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)\lesssim\int_{Z\setminus E}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z).

Note that we have used the assumption that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
to obtain the second to last inequality. Since
u\in\Lip_{c}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E)
, it follows that
Tu\in\Lip_{c}(Z\setminus E)
. Since
Z\setminus E
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality, and by boundedness of the trace operator given by Theorem
4.2
, we therefore obtain

(5.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{1}}|(Tu)_{U_{k,l}}|^{p}\int_{B_{k,l}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d(x,E)^{p}}\lesssim\int_{Z}\int_{Z}\frac{|Tu(z)-Tu(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}d\mu.

For
(k,l)\in I_{2}
, we have that
d_{\varepsilon}(B_{k,l},E)\geq d_{\varepsilon}(B_{k,l},Z)\geq 3\alpha^{-k}
by (
4.4
), and so it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{2}}|(Tu)_{U_{k,l}}|^{p}\int_{B_{k,l}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{2}}\frac{\mu(B_{k,l})}{\nu(U_{k,l})}\int_{U_{k,l}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{\alpha^{-kp}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{2}}\int_{U_{k,l}}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{\alpha^{-k\theta p}}d\nu(z)=\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{2}}\int_{Z\setminus E}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}\chi_{U_{k,l}}(z)}{\alpha^{-k\theta p}}d\nu(z).

Here we have again used the
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensional relationship between
\mu
and
\nu
as well as the fact that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
.

For
(k,l)\in I_{2}
, we have that
(k,l)\not\in I_{E}
, and so by the definition of
I_{E}
given in the proof of Lemma
5.1
, it follows that
U_{k,l}\cap N_{k}=\varnothing
, where
N_{k}
is given by (
5.2
). Hence, either
d(U_{k,l},E)<8\alpha^{-k}
or
d(U_{k,l},E)\geq 8\alpha^{-(k-1)}
. If
d(U_{k,l},E)\geq 8\alpha^{-(k-1)}
, then there exists
(i,j)\in I_{E}
with
i>k
such that
U_{k,l}\cap U_{i,j}\neq\varnothing
, and so
(k,l)\in I_{1}
. However, this is a contradiction by the definition of
I_{2}
, and so it follows that
d(U_{k,l},E)<8\alpha^{-k}
. Thus, for all
z\in U_{k,l}
, we have that

d(z,E)<10\alpha^{-k},

from which it follows that

k\leq-\log_{\alpha}(d(z,E)/10)=:m(z).

Therefore, by Tonelli’s theorem and bounded overlap of
\{U_{k,l}\}_{l\in I_{k}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,l)\in I_{2}}\int_{Z\setminus E}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}\chi_{U_{k,l}}(z)}{\alpha^{-k\theta p}}

\displaystyle d\nu(z)=\int_{Z\setminus E}|Tu(z)|^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(k,l)\in I_{2}\\
\text{ s.t. }z\in U_{k,l}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-k\theta p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Z\setminus E}|Tu(z)|^{p}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\lceil m(z)\rceil}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in I_{k}\\
\text{ s.t. }z\in U_{k,l}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-k\theta p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{Z\setminus E}|Tu(z)|^{p}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\lceil m(z)\rceil}\alpha^{k\theta p}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{Z\setminus E}|Tu(z)|^{p}\alpha^{m(z)\theta p}d\nu(z)\simeq\int_{Z\setminus E}\frac{|Tu(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}d\mu,

where the last inequality follows as
Z\setminus E
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality and by boundedness of the trace operator, given by Theorem
4.2
. Combining this with (
5.10
), we obtain the following estimate for the first term of (
5.7
):

(5.11)

\sum_{(i,j)\in I}|(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}d\mu.

Here, the comparison constant depends only on
p
,
\theta
,
\alpha
,
\tau
,
\diam(Z)
,
C_{\nu}
, and
C_{\theta,p}
.

We now estimate the second term of (
5.7
). We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}

\displaystyle\frac{|u(x)-(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)

(5.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|u(x)-u_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)+\sum_{(i,j)\in I}|u_{B_{i,j}}-(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}.

As
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
is doubling and supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, it also supports a
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality, see
[
29
]
, and also
[
32
, Theorem 9.1.2]
for example. Using this fact along with (
4.4
), we estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.12
) by

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|u(x)-u_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\frac{\mu(B_{i,j})}{\alpha^{-ip}}\fint_{B_{i,j}}|u-u_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}d\mu\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}g_{u}^{p}d\mu\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}d\mu.

We note that
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon})
is a geodesic space, and so the
(p,p)
-Poincare inequality has scaling factor
\lambda=1
, see
[
29
]
. Thus, we obtain the last inequality by bounded overlap of
\{B_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I}
, see Lemma
4.5
.

To estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.12
), we have from (
4.4
) that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I}|u_{B_{i,j}}-(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\frac{\mu(B_{i,j})}{\alpha^{-ip}}\left(\fint_{U_{i,j}}|Tu(z)-u_{B_{i,j}}|d\nu(z)\right)^{p}.

For each
z\in U_{i,j}
, consider the chain of balls
\{B_{k}(z)\}_{k=i}^{\infty}
given by Lemma
5.5
.
Since
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
, we can choose
q>1
and
\delta>0
such that
\beta/\varepsilon<q<p
and
q(1-\delta)>\beta/\varepsilon
. Using Lemma
5.5
Claims (
1
), (
6
), and (
3
), and applying the
(1,q)
-Poincaré inequality and Hölder’s inequality twice, we obtain

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{i,j}}|Tu(z)-u_{B_{i,j}}|d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{U_{i,j}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}|u_{B_{k}(z)}-u_{B_{k+1}(z)}|d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{U_{i,j}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k\delta-k(1-\delta)}\left(\fint_{2B_{k}(z)}g_{u}^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-i\delta}\fint_{U_{i,j}}\left(\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k(1-\delta)q}\fint_{2B_{k}(z)}g_{u}^{q}d\mu\right)^{1/q}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-i\delta}\left(\fint_{U_{i,j}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k(1-\delta)q}\fint_{2B_{k}(z)}g_{u}^{q}d\mu\,d\nu(z)\right)^{1/q}.

By Lemma
5.5
Claim (
2
), there exists a constant
C\geq 1
, depending only on the bi-Lipschitz constant between
d
and
d_{\varepsilon}
, such that
2B_{k}(z)\subset B_{\varepsilon}(z,C\alpha^{-k})
. Using this, as well as Lemma
5.5
Claims (
4
) and (
5
), we then obtain

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{i,j}}|Tu(z)-

\displaystyle u_{B_{i,j}}|d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-i\delta}\left(\fint_{U_{i,j}}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{-k(1-\delta)q+k\beta/\varepsilon}}{\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(z,\alpha^{-k}))}\int_{10B_{i,j}}g_{u}(x)^{q}\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}(z,C\alpha^{-k})}(x)\,d\mu(x)\,d\nu(z)\right)^{1/q}.

By Tonelli’s theorem and our choice of
q
and
\delta
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{i,j}}|Tu(z)-

\displaystyle u_{B_{i,j}}|d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\alpha^{-i\delta}}{\nu(U_{i,j})^{1/q}}\left(\int_{CB_{i,j}}g_{u}(x)^{q}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k(1-\delta)q+k\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}(z,C\alpha^{-k})}(x)}{\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(z,\alpha^{-k}))}\,d\nu(z)\,d\mu(x)\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\alpha^{-i+i\beta/(\varepsilon q)}}{\nu(U_{i,j})^{1/q}}\left(\int_{CB_{i,j}}g_{u}^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}.

Substituting this into (
5.14
) and using the
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensionality between
\mu
and
\nu
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I}|u_{B_{i,j}}-(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\frac{(\alpha^{i\beta/\varepsilon})^{p/q}\mu(B_{i,j})}{\nu(U_{i,j})^{p/q}}\left(\int_{10B_{i,j}}g_{u}^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\mu(B_{i,j})^{1-p/q}\left(\int_{10B_{i,j}}g_{u}^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\mu(B_{i,j})^{1-p/q}\left(\int_{B_{i,j}}Mg_{u}^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}(Mg_{u}^{q})^{p/q}\,d\mu\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(Mg_{u}^{q})^{p/q}\,d\mu.

Here
M
is the uncentered Hardy-Littlewood maximal function, and we have used Hölder’s inequality and the bounded overlap of the
\{B_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I}
to obtain the last two inequalities, see Lemma
4.5
. By boundedness of the maximal function from
L^{p/q}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu)
to
L^{p/q}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu)
, see
[
32
, Theorem 3.5.6]
for example, we obtain the following estimate of the second term in (
5.12
):

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I}|u_{B_{i,j}}-(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{d\mu(x)}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu.

Combining this with (
5.13
) and (
5.12
), the second term of (
5.7
) is now estimated by

\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|u(x)-(Tu)_{U_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu,

and so, with (
5.11
), we obtain the desired inequality:

\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu.

Here, the comparison constant depends only on
\theta
,
p
,
\alpha
,
\tau
,
C_{\nu}
, and
C_{\theta,p}
.
∎

We now show that for an appropriate choice of
\theta
,
p
, and
\beta
, a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
on
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
implies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality on
Z
.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
E\subset Z
be a closed set, let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
\beta>0
be such that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
, and suppose that
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
. Then
Z\setminus E
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality, with constant depending on
\theta
,
p
,
C_{\nu}
, and
C_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\Lip_{c}(Z\setminus E)
, and let
I_{E}\subset I
be the indexing set given by Lemma
5.1
. For ease of notation, we again set
\mu:=\mu_{\beta}
. We then have that

\displaystyle\int_{Z\setminus E}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)

(5.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}|(Eu)_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{d\nu(z)}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}+\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{|u(z)-(Eu)_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z).

Here
Eu\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
is the extension of
u
given by Theorem
4.2
, see (
4.3
).

By Lemma
5.1
and (
4.4
), we have that

d(U_{i,j},E)\simeq\alpha^{-i}\simeq d_{\varepsilon}(B_{i,j},U_{i,j}).

Using this, along with the
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensionality between
\mu
and
\nu
, the assumption that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
, and bounded overlap of
\{B_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I}
, we estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.16
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}|(Eu)_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{d\nu(z)}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\frac{\nu(U_{i,j})}{\alpha^{-i\theta p}}\fint_{B_{i,j}}|Eu|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\frac{\nu(U_{i,j})}{\mu(B_{i,j})}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|Eu(x)|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{\theta p}}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{-i\beta/\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|Eu(x)|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{\theta p}}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\int_{B_{i,j}}\frac{|Eu(x)|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E}\frac{|Eu(x)|^{p}}{d_{\varepsilon}(x,E)^{p}}d\mu(x).

As
u\in\Lip_{c}(Z\setminus E)
, it follows that
Eu\in\Lip_{c}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E)
, see (
4.3
).
Since
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus E
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
, and by the boundedness of the extension operator, see Theorem
4.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}|(Eu)_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{d\nu(z)}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{Eu}^{p}d\mu

(5.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{Z}\int_{Z}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z).

We now estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.16
). If
x\in B_{i,j}
, for
(i,j)\in I_{E}
, then there exist vertices
v_{1}=(y_{1},i_{1})
and
v_{2}=(y_{2},i_{2})
such that
x\in[v_{1},v_{2}]
, and
i_{1}\simeq i\simeq i_{2}
. Thus, by the definition of
E
given by (
4.3
), for
z\in U_{i,j}
and
x\in U_{i,j}
, there exists
k\in\{1,2\}
such that

|u(z)-Eu(x)|\leq\left|u(z)-\fint_{B_{Z}(y_{k},\alpha^{i_{k}})}u\,d\nu\right|\leq\fint_{B_{Z}(y_{k},\alpha^{i_{k}})}|u(z)-u(w)|d\nu(w).

Since
x\in B_{i,j}
and since
i_{k}\simeq i
, there exists
C:=C(\alpha,\tau,C_{\nu})
such that

|u(z)-Eu(x)|\lesssim\fint_{CU_{i,j}}|u(z)-u(w)|d\nu(w),

where
CU_{i,j}=B_{Z}(z_{i,j},C\alpha^{-i})\cap Z
, and we have used the doubling property of
\nu
. Thus, by Hölder’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{|u(z)-(Eu)_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{-i\theta p}}\int_{U_{i,j}}\fint_{B_{i,j}}|u(z)-Eu(x)|^{p}d\mu(x)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha^{-i\theta p}}\int_{U_{i,j}}\fint_{B_{i,j}}\fint_{CU_{i,j}}|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}d\nu(w)\,d\mu(x)\,d\nu(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha^{-i\theta p}}\int_{U_{i,j}}\fint_{CU_{i,j}}|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}d\nu(w)\,d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{U_{i,j}}\int_{CU_{i,j}}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U_{i,j}}\int_{Z}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z).

By bounded overlap of the collection
\{U_{i,j}\}_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}
, we then obtain

\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{E}}\int_{U_{i,j}}\frac{|u(z)-(Eu)_{B_{i,j}}|^{p}}{d(z,E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)\lesssim\int_{Z}\int_{Z}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z).

Combining this estimate with (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) completes the proof. Here, the comparison constants depend only on
\theta
,
p
,
\alpha
,
\tau
,
C_{\nu}
, and
C_{p}
.
∎

6.
Localization and proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we combined the results of the previous sections with a localization argument in order to prove Theorem
1.3
.

6.1.
Localization

We now let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a complete doubling metric measure space. In order to apply Theorem
1.4
, which is proven for a
compact
doubling metric measure space, in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we first need to show that if
E\subset Z
is a closed set, with
Z\setminus E
bounded satisfying a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality, then we can find a compact set
Z_{0}\subset Z
such that the
\nu|_{Z_{0}}
is doubling,
Z_{0}\setminus E=Z\setminus E
, and that
Z_{0}\setminus E
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality. The basic step is finding an exhaustion of
Z
by sets
Z_{R}
,
R>0
, so that
Z_{R}
equipped with the restricted measures are doubling. This is a much simplified version of an exhaustion by uniform domains constructed by Rajala in
[
53
]
. The restricted measure is given by
\nu|_{Z_{R}}(A)=\nu(Z_{R}\cap A)
and the restricted distance is
d|_{Z_{R}\times Z_{R}}
. For simplicity, we will use
d
to denote the distance in both the subset and entire space.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be complete and doubling and
z_{0}\in Z
. For every
R>0
, there exists a compact set
Z_{R}\subset Z
for which

(1)

B(z_{0},R)\subset Z_{R}\subset B(z_{0},2R)
, and

(2)

(Z_{R},d|_{Z_{R}\times Z_{R}},\nu|_{Z_{R}})
is doubling.

Proof.

Define recursively the following sets

\Omega_{0}=B(z_{0},R),\quad\Omega_{i+1}=\{y\in Z:d(y,\Omega_{i})\leq 4^{-i-1}R).

Let

Z_{R}=\overline{\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\Omega_{i}}.

The set
Z_{R}
is closed by definition, and to show that it is compact it suffices to prove that it is bounded. By induction on
i\in\mathbb{N}
one shows that

\Omega_{i}\subset B(z_{0},\sum_{k=0}^{i}R4^{-k})\subset B(z_{0},4/3R).

Thus,
Z_{R}\subset B(z_{0},2R)
and
Z_{R}
is compact. It is also direct that
B(z_{0},R)=\Omega_{0}\subset Z_{R}
.

Next, we prove that
Z_{R}
equipped with the restricted measure
\nu|_{Z_{R}}
is doubling. Let
z\in Z_{R}
and let
r>0
. First, if
r>4R
, then

\nu|_{Z_{R}}(B(z,r))=\nu(B(z,2r)\cap Z_{R})=\nu(Z_{R})\geq\nu|_{Z_{R}}(B(z,2r)).

Since
\nu(Z_{R})\geq\nu(B(z_{0},R))>0
, we get doubling for all radii
r>4R
.

Thus, we are left to consider
r<4R
. For this, we prove a version of the
corkscrew condition
from
[
8
]
. Let
c=1/48
. The corkscrew condition is: For every
z\in Z_{R}
there exits an
z_{r}\in Z_{R}\cap B(z,r/3)
so that
B(z_{r},cr)\subset B(z,r)\cap Z_{R}
.

First, choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
so that
R4^{-k}<r\leq R4^{1-k}
. By induction, for every
p\in\Omega_{i}
and all
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have
d(p,\Omega_{k})\leq 4^{-k}R/3
. This follows directly for
i\leq k
, and induction step follows from the definition. Thus,
d(z,\Omega_{k})\leq 4^{-k}R/3
.

Pick
z_{r}\in\Omega_{k}
so that
d(z_{r},z)\leq 4^{-k}R/3\leq r/3
. Then,

B(z_{r},cr)\subset B(z_{r},R4^{-1-k})\subset Z_{R}

by construction, and
B(z_{r},cr)\subset B(z,r)
by the choice of
c
.

With the corkscrew condition established, the doubling condition is fairly direct. First, by construction:

\nu|_{Z_{R}}(B(z,2r))\leq\nu(B(z,2r)),

and

\nu|_{Z_{R}}(B(z,r))=\nu(Z_{R}\cap B(z,r))\geq\nu(B(z_{r},cr))\geq C\nu(B(z,2r)),

where
C
is some constant from the doubling bound. It is also direct that
\nu|_{Z_{R}}(B(z,r))>0
, and thus doubling follows.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

The proposition above can often be much simplified.
If
Z
is geodesic, then it suffices to define
Z_{R}=\overline{B(z_{0},R)}
. Thus, exhaustions by balls work for example in all Euclidean spaces.

Given this exhaustion, we can prove the following localization argument.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be complete and doubling, let
0<\theta<1
and
1<p<\infty
, and let
E\subset Z
be a closed set such that
Z\setminus E
is bounded. Let
z_{0}\in Z\setminus E
, and let
Z_{R}
be the exhaustion constructed in Proposition
6.1
.

(i)

If

-Hardy inequality.

Proof.

Consider the inequality for the open set

\int_{Z\setminus E}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{d(z,Z\setminus E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)\leq C_{\theta,p}\int_{Z}\int_{Z}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

for all Lipschitz functions

. If
R>0
is such that

,
then replacing

by

\Omega=Z_{R}\setminus E
and

implies the same for

and

. This gives us (ii).

We now prove (i). Suppose that

satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality, and let

be such that

sufficiently large. To this end, we estimate the right hand side of (

)
as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{Z}\int_{Z}

\displaystyle\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{Z_{R}}\int_{Z_{R}}\left(\cdots\right)d\nu(w)d\nu(z)+\int_{Z_{R}}\int_{Z\setminus Z_{R}}(\cdots)d\nu(w)d\nu(z)+\int_{Z\setminus Z_{R}}\int_{Z_{R}}(\cdots)d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Z_{R}}\int_{Z_{R}}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)+\int_{E^{c}}\int_{B(z_{0},R)^{c}}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\mu(B(z,d(z,w)))}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\int_{B(z_{0},R)^{c}}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{|u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\mu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z).

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Z_{R}}\int_{Z_{R}}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+(1+C_{\nu})\int_{E^{c}}\int_{B(z_{0},R)^{c}}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z).

. Using this and doubling, we get for

\displaystyle\int_{B(z_{0},R)^{c}}\frac{1}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z_{0},2^{k+1}R)\setminus B(z_{0},2^{k}R)}\frac{1}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)

\displaystyle\leq R^{-\theta p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\nu(B(z_{0},2^{k+1}R)\setminus B(z_{0},2^{k}R))}{2^{(k-1)\theta p}\nu(B(z,2^{k-1}R))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}R^{-\theta p},

where

,

, and

Hence, if

\displaystyle\int_{Z\setminus E}

\displaystyle\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{d(z,Z\setminus E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z)\leq C_{\theta,p}\int_{Z}\int_{Z}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta,p}\int_{Z_{R}}\int_{Z_{R}}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\mu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z\setminus E}\frac{|u(z)|^{p}}{d(z,Z\setminus E)^{\theta p}}d\nu(z).

Absorbing the second term to the left hand side yields the fractional Hardy inequality for

in

7.
Examples and Applications

As mentioned in the introduction, self-improvement results for the pointwise
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality were recently obtained in
[
36
]
for domains in doubling metric measure spaces whose complements satisfy the
(\theta,p)
-capacity density condition, see Definition
7.10
below. However, our self-improvement result Theorem
1.3
applies to bounded domains which satisfy the weaker condition of a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality. In this subsection, we provide an example of such a domain, namely the punctured unit ball
B(0,1)\setminus\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and show that it satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality whenever
0<\theta<1
and
1<p<\infty
satisfy
\theta p<n
. Rather than proving this inequality directly, we first show that a sufficient condition for the
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality, given in terms of Assouad codimensions, follows from Theorem
1.4
and an analogous result due to Lehrbäck
[
46
]
for the
p
-Hardy inequality. In addition to justifying the example of the punctured ball, we provide this sufficient condition, Proposition
7.2
below, in an attempt to illustrate how results for fractional Hardy inequalities can be readily obtained from their
p
-Hardy inequality counterparts by using Theorem
1.4
.

We first recall the definitions of the upper and lower Assouad codimensions. Given a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, a set
E\subset X
, and
r>0
, we denote the
r
-neighborhood of
E
by

E_{r}:=\{x\in X:d(x,E)<r\}.

The
lower Assouad codimension
of
E
, denoted
\underline{\codim}^{\mu}_{A}(E)
, is the supremum of all
t\geq 0
for which there exists
C\geq 1
such that

\frac{\mu(B(x,R)\cap E_{r})}{\mu(B(x,R))}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{t}

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r\leq R<\diam(X)
. Likewise, the
upper Assouad codimension
of
E
, denoted
\overline{\codim}_{A}^{\mu}(E)
, is the infimum of all
s\geq 0
for which there exists
c>0
such that

\frac{\mu(B(x,R)\cap E_{r})}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq c\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{s}

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r\leq R<\diam(E)
. If
\diam(E)=0
, then the upper bound on
R
is omitted in the definition. When the lower Assouad codimension equals the upper Assouad codimension we simply call it the Assouad codimension.

The following sufficient condition for
p
-Hardy inequalities is due to Lehrbäck
[
46
]
:

Proposition 7.1
.

[
46
, Proposition 7.1]
Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality. Let
\Omega_{0}\subset X
be an open set satisfying
\overline{\codim}^{\mu}_{A}(X\setminus\Omega_{0})<p
. If
F\subset\overline{\Omega}_{0}
is a closed set satisfying
\underline{\codim}^{\mu}_{A}(F)>p
, then
\Omega:=\Omega_{0}\setminus F
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu
.

Notice that the metric space
Z
has Assouad codimension
\beta/\varepsilon=(1-\theta)p
in its hyperbolic filling
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. By a similar calculation, a set
A\subset Z
of Assouad codimension
\theta p
will have Assouad codimension
\theta p+\beta/\varepsilon=p
in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
. We will make these arguments precise with the aid of covering arguments by balls in the proof of the following statement.
Applying this observation and the previous result in conjunction with Theorem
1.4
, we obtain the following fractional analog of the previous proposition.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a complete doubling metric measure space, and let
0<\theta<1
, and
1<p<\infty
. Let
\Omega_{0}\subset Z
be a bounded open set satisfying
\overline{\codim}^{\nu}_{A}(Z\setminus\Omega_{0})<\theta p
. If
F\subset\overline{\Omega}_{0}
is a closed set satisfying
\underline{\codim}^{\nu}_{A}(F)>\theta p

(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality.

Let
z_{0}\in\Omega_{0}
. By Proposition
6.1
, there exists a compact set
Z_{0}
with

and
\nu_{0}:=\nu|_{Z_{0}}

, and so it follows from codimensionality, (
7.6
), and the doubling property of
\nu_{0}
that

\displaystyle\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(x,R)\cap(E_{0})_{r,\varepsilon})\geq\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(x,R/(2C))

\displaystyle\simeq r^{\beta/\varepsilon}\nu_{0}(B_{\varepsilon}(x,R/(2C))\cap Z)

\displaystyle\simeq r^{\beta/\varepsilon}\nu_{0}(B(x,R/C))

\displaystyle\geq r^{\beta/\varepsilon}\nu_{0}(B(x,R/C)\cap(E_{0})_{r/C}).

Hence, by the choice of
\beta>0
, we have in this case that

\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(x,R)\cap(E_{0})_{r,\varepsilon})}{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(x,R))}\gtrsim\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{p(1-\theta)}\frac{\nu_{0}(B(x,R/C)\cap(E_{0})_{r/C})}{\nu_{0}(B(x,R/C))}.

We note that
R/C\leq\diam(E_{0})
, where the diameter is with respect to
d_{0}
. Therefore, since
\overline{\codim}_{A}^{\nu_{0}}(E_{0})<\theta p
, it follows from combining this case with (
7.7
)
that
\overline{\codim}_{A}^{\mu_{\beta}}(E_{0})<p
.

By a similar argument, using the
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensionality between
\mu_{\beta}
and
\nu_{0}
,
(
7.6
)
, the doubling property of
\nu_{0}
, and the fact that
\underline{\codim}_{A}^{\nu_{0}}(F)>\theta p
, we have that
\underline{\codim}_{A}^{\mu_{\beta}}(F)>p
. By applying Proposition
7.1
, it then follows that
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus(E_{0}\cup F)
satisfies a
p
-Hardy inequality with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
. From Theorem
1.4
, we then have that
Z_{0}\setminus(E_{0}\cup F)
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality, and so by possibly increasing only the right-hand side of the inequality, it follows that
\Omega:=Z\setminus(E_{0}\cup F)
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality as well. ∎

Example 7.8
.

Let
\Omega:=B(0,1)\setminus\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
0<\theta<1
and
1<p<\infty
be such that
\theta p<n
. Letting
\nu=\mathcal{L}^{n}
,
E:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,1)
and
F:=\{0\}
, we have that

\overline{\codim}_{A}^{\nu}(E)=0<\theta p\qquad\text{and}\qquad\underline{\codim}_{A}^{\nu}(F)=n>\theta p.

Thus, from Proposition
7.2
, it follows that
\Omega
satisfies a
(\theta,p)
-Hardy inequality.

Using the hyperbolic filling, it is also straightforward to show that the complement of the domain
\Omega
above does not satisfy the
(\theta,p)
-capacity density condition, as considered in
[
36
]
. We first recall the definitions of this condition and the fractional relative capacity, stated here for a metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
:

Definition 7.9
.

Let
0<\theta<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, and let
\Lambda\geq 2
. Let
B\subset Z
be a ball, and let
E\subset\overline{B}
be a closed set. We then write

\vcap_{\theta,p}(E,\,2B,\,\Lambda B):=\inf_{u}\int_{\Lambda B}\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|u(z)-u(w)|^{p}}{d(z,w)^{\theta p}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z),

where the infimum is taken over all continuous functions
u:Z\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u\geq 1
on
E
and
u=0
on
Z\setminus 2B
.

Definition 7.10
.

[
36
, Definition 4.1]
Let
0<\theta<1
,
1<p<\infty
, and let
E\subset Z
be a closed set. We say that
E
satisfies the
(\theta,p)
-capacity density condition
if there are constants
c_{0}>0
and
\Lambda>2
such that

\vcap_{\theta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},\,B(x,2r),\,B(x,\Lambda r))\geq c_{0}\vcap_{\theta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)},\,B(x,2r),\,B(x,\Lambda r))

for all
x\in E
and
0<r<\diam(E)/8
.

Notice that
\vcap_{\theta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)},\,B(x,2r),\,B(x,\Lambda r))>0
whenever
\Lambda>2
if
Z
is geodesic.

Example 7.11
.

Let
\Omega=B(0,1)\setminus\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
0<\theta<1
and
1<p<\infty
be such that
\theta p<n
. We show that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
does not satisfy the
(\theta,p)
-capacity density condition, due to the component
\{0\}
.

Let
\Lambda>2
, and let
r>0
. Let
Z=\overline{B(0,\Lambda r)}
, let
d=d_{\Euc}
, and let
\nu=\mathcal{L}^{n}|_{Z}
. As above, let
\alpha=e^{1/4}
,
\tau=2
, and
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
, and choose
\beta>0
such that
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
. We then consider the uniformized hyperbolic filling
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
of the compact, doubling metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
. For each
0<\eta<r
, consider the continuous function
u_{\eta}:\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

u_{\eta}(x):=\left(1-\frac{d_{\varepsilon}(x,\{0\})}{\eta}\right)_{+}.

Then
u_{\eta}
is
1/\eta
-Lipschitz in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
, and if
T
is the trace operator given by Theorem
4.2
, then
Tu_{\eta}=1
on
\{0\}
, and
Tu_{\eta}=0
in
Z\setminus B(0,2r)
for sufficiently small
\eta
, depending only the bi-Lipschitz constants between
d
and
d_{\varepsilon}
. It then follows from Theorem
4.2
and our choice of
\beta>0
that

\displaystyle\vcap_{\theta,p}(\{0\},\,B(0,2r),\,B(0,\Lambda r))\lesssim\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u_{\eta}}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\leq\eta^{-p}\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(0,\eta))\lesssim\eta^{-\theta p}\nu(B(0,\eta))\simeq\eta^{n-\theta p}.

As
\theta p<n
and since
\eta>0
is arbitrary, we have that
\vcap_{\theta,p}(\{0\},\,B(0,2r),\,B(0,\Lambda r))=0
, and so
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
does not satisfy the
(\theta,p)
-capacity density condition.

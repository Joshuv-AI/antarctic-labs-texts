Comprehensive assessment beyond eosinophils in eosinophilic esophagitis: an updated network meta-analysis
Authors: Yangbao Lyu, Zhi Wu, Yuming Zhou, Yue Zhang, Shuliang Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe rapid development of therapies for eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE), particularly involving biologics and small molecules, has prompted questions about their roles within current treatment algorithms. We aimed to examine the relative efficacy of novel biologics, small molecules and topical steroids by evaluating clinical, endoscopic, and histological improvements.MethodsWe searched MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, and major conference databases for articles published from inception to Jul 1, 2024. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating the clinical, endoscopic and histological efficacy of novel biologics, small molecules or topical steroids as induction therapies for patients with EoE were included. A network meta-analysis (NMA) was performed to obtain effect size—risk ratio (RR), mean difference (MD), standardized mean difference (SMD)—with 95% confidence interval (CI) for different outcomes. The surface under the cumulative ranking (SUCRA) was estimated to present the relative ranking of treatment. The primary outcome was the induction of symptomatic improvement. The secondary outcomes were endoscopic improvement and histological remission. Higher SUCRA curve rankings indicate a greater probability of superior treatment efficacy. The review was registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251155393).ResultsOur search strategy identified 409 records, of which 18 trials (total n = 2,506 patients) were eligible for inclusion. From 12 trials that assessed the induction of clinical improvement, Dupilumab 300 mg weekly (qw) ranked first (SMD -0.57, 95%CI [-0.79, -0.35]), while Dupilumab 300mg every 2 weeks (q2w) ranked relatively low. From 12 randomized controlled trials evaluating endoscopic improvement, Dupilumab 300 mg qw (MD -2.10, 95%CI [-2.49,-1.90]) and 300 mg q2w (MD -1.87, 95%CI [-2.25, -1.48])ranked second and fourth, respectively. In 17 RCTs assessing the induction of histological remission, Budesonide orally disintegrating tablet (BODT) ranked highest(RR 30.77, 95%CI [6.63, 294.95]), whereas Dupilumab (qw/q2w) showed moderate effectiveness.ConclusionsThis study suggests that Dupilumab 300mg qw might be the best performing agent for induction of clinical improvement (the primary endpoint) and BODT might be the most efficacious agents for histological and endoscopic improvement (the secondary endpoint). This could provide evidence for clinical therapy strategy for EoE patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-025-04434-5.

Background

EoE is a chronic, Th2-driven disease of the esophagus, first identified nearly three decades ago. It is characterized histologically by eosinophilic infiltration of the esophageal mucosa and clinically by symptoms of esophageal dysfunction. Currently, EoE affects approximately 1 in 3,000 individuals in the Western world. The incidence and prevalence of EoE are increasing, even considering heightened awareness. EoE is recognized as a major cause of dysphagia and food impaction in adults, as well as feeding difficulties in children [1–3].

The presence of eosinophils in esophageal mucosa serves as a key diagnostic marker for stratifying clinical severity in EoE. Central to this immunopathological process are type 2 helper T cell-derived signaling molecules, particularly Interleukin-4 (IL-4), Interleukin-5 (IL-5), and Interleukin-13 (IL-13), which coordinate multiple biological events. These immunomodulators regulate both the mobilization of eosinophils to inflammatory sites and their effector functions, while concurrently inducing architectural modifications in esophageal tissues that define the disease phenotype. Mechanistically, EoE development involves a cascade of events encompassing impaired mucosal protection mechanisms, degranulation of effector immune cells (including mast cells and eosinophils), and excessive extracellular matrix deposition leading to fibrotic transformation [4]. Therefore, a reduction in eosinophil count is considered a treatment endpoint when assessing therapeutic efficacy in EoE patients. Although clinical presentations and endoscopic remission have been integrated into evaluation systems, no guideline has unified all elements of disease activity into a single score [5]. Novel therapies targeting key inflammatory pathways, such as monoclonal antibodies against IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, are emerging in clinical trials [4]. Rothenberg et al. reported that Benralizumab significantly reduced the peak eosinophil count but did not substantially affect symptomatology or endoscopic changes in EOE patients [6]. Therefore, it is essential to comprehensively analyze the efficacy of new agents using a well-rounded standard. Dellon et al. developed a comprehensive assessment tool termed the Index of Severity for Eosinophilic Esophagitis (I-SEE), which equally considers symptoms, endoscopic findings, and tissue assessments. As a promising composite measure for disease severity and remission, it could enhance the evaluation of drug efficacy in treating EoE [5]. However, none of the previous meta-analyses assessed efficacy concerning clinical, endoscopic, and histologic improvements using generalized drugs. Therefore, further studies are needed to identify the best agent for improving patients' symptoms with biologics, small molecules, and topical steroids simultaneously.

Methods

Search strategy and selection criteria

This NMA was done and reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta­-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 [7] (Supplementary Table 1). Two authors (LY and WZ) searched MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials for records from inception to July 1, 2024, without language restriction. We also reviewed major congresses databases (American College of Gastroenterology, Digestive Disease Week, and United European Gastroenterology Week) from Jan 1, 2020, to Jul 1, 2024 to find studies published only in abstract form.

Inclusion/exclusion criteria for therapies

Trials were restricted to agents with phase 2/3 RCT data available by July 1, 2024. Emerging therapies (e.g., tezepelumab) were excluded due to lack of published trial data. Meanwhile, FDA-approved agents such as Dupilumab would be prioritized. The following terms were used in the search process: ①("biologics" OR "mepolizumab" OR "reslizumab" OR "benralizumab" OR "dupilumab" OR "QAX576" OR "RPC4046" OR "Omalizumab"); ②("AK002" OR "lirentelimab" OR "OC000459" OR "vedolizumab"); ③("topical" OR "swallowed" OR "suspensions") AND ("steroid" OR "budesonide" OR "fluticasone"); ④("eosinophilic esophagitis"); ⑤("efficacy").

Inclusion/exclusion criteria

We included phase 2 or phase 3 RCTs that fullfill the following inclusion criteria: (1) studies enrolled patients with EoE (defined by at least 15 eosinophils per high-power field in the esophageal mucosa biopsy). Meanwhile, patients were excluded for other secondary causes of esophageal eosinophilia. (2) patients received topical steroids (budesonide, fluticasone), biologics (mepolizumab, reslizumab, benralizumab, dupilumab, QAX576, RPC4046, omalizumab), or small molecules (OC000459, lirentelimab, vedolizumab) for at least six weeks. (3) studies including a placebo or an active comparator. (4) studies evaluating the clinical improvement (significant decrease in clinical questionnaire score). (5)studies evaluating the endoscopic improvement (significant decrease in Eosinophilic Esophagitis Endoscopic Reference Score (EREFS)). (6)studies evaluating the histological improvement (number of patients who achieved histological remission). The clinical questionnaires used in these studies include dysphagia symptom questionnaire (DSQ), Dysphagia Symptom Diary score(DSD), Global Assessment Score of Disease Severity, Eosinophilic Esophagitis Symptom Activity Index Patient-Reported Outcome score (EEsAI PRO score), Numeric Rating Scale for dysphagia (NRS dysphagia), Eosinophilic Esophagitis symptom Activity Index score (EEsAI score) and Pediatric Eosinophilic Esophagitis Symptom Questionnaire—Child version (PESQ-C).

Two authors (Lyu Yangbao and Wu Zhi) evaluated the titles and abstracts of selected papers to obtain all potentially relevant papers independently. The full text of the selected studies was analyzed in detail according to the predefined criteria. Foreign language papers were translated when required. Any disagreement in study selection was settled by a discussion with a third author (Zhao Shuliang).

Outcome assessment

The primary endpoint was the efficacy of active agents versus each other, or placebo, in terms of induction of clinical symptoms improvement as it aligns with patient-centered outcomes and SUCRA rankings. It is noted that questionnaires are different in various RCTs. In view of this situation, we conducted a network meta-analysis using a SMD effect to measure the clinical outcome. The secondary endpoints were endoscopic improvement defined as a significant decrease in EREFS and histologic improvement defined as ≤ 6 (or approximately) eosinophils per high-power field.

Data extraction

For each eligible study, two authors (Lyu Yangbao and Wu Zhi) independently extracted the following data by using a structured form: study characteristics (first author, year of publication), participant characteristics (study population), treatment characteristics (treatment duration, schedule of therapy, and placebo), and the outcome mentioned before. All disagreements were resolved by consultation with a third author (Zhao Shuliang).

Quality assessment and risk of bias

Revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB 2) [8], which assesses the risk of selection, performance, detection, attrition, and other biases, was used by two authors (Lyu Yangbao and Wu Zhi) to independently assess the risk of bias. Different domains of risk-of-bias were rated as high risk, some concerns, or low risk. All disagreements were resolved by consultation with a third author (Zhao Shuliang).

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

We performed an NMA to estimate clinical, endoscopic and histologic improvement for induction therapy for EoE. Considering varied clinical scores used by different trials, the continuous outcome of clinical improvement is measured as SMD with 95%CI. The NMA was conducted using the ‘BUGSnet’ package (V1.1.1) for Bayesian inference with Gibbs sampling in R (V4.1.0) [9]. The setting of Bayesian prior was described below: ①prior.mu: independent normal priors are used with mean 0 and standard deviation 15u, where u is the largest maximum likelihood estimator in single trial; ②prior.d: independent normal priors are used with mean 0 and standard deviation 15u, where u is the largest maximum likelihood estimator in single trials; ③prior.sigma: a uniform distribution with range 0 to u is used, where u is the largest maximum likelihood estimator in single trials. The ranking of treatment for each outcome was derived with the SUCRA. League tables was also generated to compare each treatment according to the SUCRA ranks.

Model selection in NMA

The choice between fixed-effect and random-effects models in the NMA was guided by the assessment of heterogeneity and inconsistency across studies. A fixed-effect model assumes a single true effect size shared by all studies, whereas a random-effect model accounts for between-study heterogeneity by allowing true effects to vary across studies. To determine the appropriate model, we first quantified heterogeneity using the I2 statistic. An I2 value ≥ 50% indicated substantial heterogeneity, favoring the random-effects model. Sensitivity analyses were conducted by comparing results from both models to evaluate the robustness of the findings. When inconsistency (discrepancy between direct and indirect evidence) was detected via node-splitting or global inconsistency tests, the random-effects model was prioritized as it more conservatively addresses variability.

Results

From a total of 409 citations identified by our search strategy, 18 records, including one unpublished trial (NCT04322708) involving 2,506 participants, fulfilled the eligibility criteria for inclusion (Fig. 1). 12 RCTs assessed the induction of clinical improvement using clinical questionnaire scores [6, 10–19], 11 RCTs assessed the induction of endoscopic improvement using EREFS [6, 11–21] and 18 trials evaluated the induction of histological remission [6, 10–26]. Detailed characteristics of the eighteen trials involved are provided in Supplementary Table 2. Risk of bias is reported (Fig. 2), and eleven trials were at low risk of bias across all domains. Clinical improvement

When assessing the induction of clinical improvement, Dupilumab 300 mg qw and BODT were significantly superior to placebo in the meta-analysis. There was low heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 0%). In the NMA using a fixed-effect model, Dupilumab 300 mg qw, an antibody against the IL-4 receptor α, ranked highest for inducing clinical improvement(SMD −0.57, 95%CI[−0.78,−0.35]), followed by BODT(SMD −0.56, 95%CI[−1.02, −0.011]) and RPC4046(SMD −0.44, 95%CI[−0.93, −0.04]). It is noteworthy that Dupilumab 300 mg q2w ranked inferiorly to other agents except for lirentelimab and placebo in inducing clinical improvement. Moreover, significant differences were found between Dupilumab 300 mg qw and Dupilumab 300 mg q2w (SMD −0.56, 95%CI [−0.95,−0.18]). IL-5 axis blockers including mepolizumab, benralizumab and resilizumab exhibited a likelihood of superior efficacy compared to placebo without significant difference though. Lirentelimab showed comparable efficacy to placebo without significant difference. RPC4046, targeting IL-13, had a higher probability of being among the top-ranked interventions than others except for Dupilumab 300 mg qw and BODT (ranked third). However, due to the overlapping of confidence intervals, it did not show significant difference to others.(Fig. 4A; Supplementary Fig. 1).Fig. 4SUCRA plot (A: symptomatic endpoint; B: endoscopic endpoint; C: histological endpoint.)

Endoscopic improvement

There was substantial heterogeneity between trials (I2 = 84.9%, 95%CI[68.9%, 92.7%]).When evaluating endoscopic improvement, BODT ranked highest (MD-2.51, 95%CI[− 3.30, − 1.71]), while Dupilumab 300 mg qw and q2w (MD-2.20, 95%CI[− 2.49, − 1.90]; MD-1.87, 95%CI[− 2.25, − 1.48])ranked second and fourth, respectively. This suggests that Dupilumab and BODT have comparable efficacy in inducing endoscopic improvement without statistically significant differences. Additionally, Fluticasone Orally disintegrating Tablet (FODT) also showed no significant difference in efficacy compared to the aforementioned agents. Consequently, ODT might be considered the more effective form of steroids for inducing endoscopic improvement. RPC4046 ranked fifth, demonstrating superiority over both placebo with significant differences (RPC4046 vs placebo: MD-1.48, 95%CI[− 2.73, − 0.22]). Furthermore, results for drugs blocking the IL-5 axis were contradictory. For instance, Mepolizumab and Benralizumab were superior to placebo, whereas lirentelimab was inferior to placebo. Mepolizumab was statistically superior to both placebo (MD-0.60, 95%CI[-1.20, -0.20] ) and lirentelimab (MD−0.78, 95%CI[-1.52, -0.03]) (Fig. 4B; Supplementary Fig. 2).

Histological remission

The network plot of the seventeen RCTs assessing histological remission is shown in Fig. 5. Due to the absence of closed loops in the network, inconsistency tests between direct and indirect evidence were not performed. There was low heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 0%). A fixed-effect model was selected for pooling effect estimates, as no significant heterogeneity was detected across studies. In the network meta-analysis, all biologic agents showed statistically superiority over placebo. Although biologics varied in their impact when inducing histological improvement, it indicated that they have comparable efficacy in this context without statistical difference. Among the biologics, Benralizumab ranked highest in efficacy (RR = 11.31, 95%CI[6.30, 24.77], ranked fourth), followed by RPC4046 (RR = 11.14, 95%CI[2.03, 359.28], ranked sixth), Dupilumab (qw: RR = 9.23, 95%CI[5.63, 17.18]; q2w: RR = 8.65, 95%CI[4.71, 19.89]), lirentelimab (RR = 7.19, 95%Cl[4.52,14.25]), and Mepolizumab (RR = 4.35, 95%CI[1.06, 31.89]). Additionally, no significant difference was noted between Dupilumab 300 mg qw and q2w (qw vs q2w: RR = 1.07, 95%CI[0.42, 2.55]), although the former might show a higher probability of superiority. Budesonide showed a high probability of superiority, with BODT and Budesonide Oral Suspension (BOS) ranking first and second, respectively (Fig. 4C; Supplementary Fig. 3).Fig. 5Network plot (A: symptomatic endpoint; B: endoscopic endpoint; C: histological endpoint.)

Inconsistency test

In the NMA, the absence of closed loops within the evidence network precluded the application of formal consistency tests, as no direct and indirect comparisons were available for the same treatment contrasts. Consequently, the assessment of inconsistency between direct and indirect evidence could not be performed, which may limit the ability to evaluate the robustness of effect estimates across the network. Sensitivity analyses and prior knowledge validation were prioritized to explore potential sources of heterogeneity and ensure the reliability of the synthesized results (Fig. 5).

Risk of bias and publication bias

The funnel plot of symptomatic endpoint demonstrated symmetrical dispersion of effect estimates around the pooled effect size (Fig. 3A). It suggests no evidence of publication bias in the included studies. The right two funnel plots asymmetry may indicate possible publication bias (Fig. 3B; C).

Those high-risk trials (7/18) primarily had small sample sizes.

Discussion

Over the past 20 years, clinical experience and research studies have identified key features necessary for the prospective assessment of clinical severity in EoE. The development of patient-reported outcomes, endoscopic assessment platforms, histologic metrics of disease activity, and a clinical outcomes set have provided robust metrics for measuring patients’ responses to treatments and assessing disease activity [5].

Several prior NMAs have assessed the efficacy of EOE drugs [27–30], however, they did not include small molecules and several novel biologics that have been evaluated in recent RCTs. One latest NMA has evaluated several types of drugs including proton pump inhibitor (PPI), topical steroids and biologics in terms of histologic, clinical and endoscopic endpoints [30]. It focuses on the histologic endpoint with a few analyses of symptomatic changes and does not contain a comprehensive range of drugs. Increasing evidence suggests a disparity between histological eosinophil reduction and symptomatic/endoscopic relief. When evaluating the effectiveness of different therapies, a more comprehensive perspective is needed [31].

In the present NMA, we sought to consider symptoms, endoscopic findings, and tissue assessment simultaneously And to incorporate the latest RCTs, especially the two studies of dupilumab and benralizumab. We synthesized available direct and indirect evidence from 18 RCTs to assess the efficacy of topical steroids, biologics, and small molecules in inducing clinical, endoscopic and histologic improvement in patients with EoE. There are several findings generated from this NMA.

Firstly, the NMA demonstrated that certain biologics exhibited non-inferior efficacy to conventional therapies (e.g., steroids), despite their lower SUCRA rankings. Notably, treatment effects between biologics and steroids were not statistically significant, suggesting comparable clinical effects. As steroids have been first-line treatment for EoE, biologics have the potential to be alternative agents for EoE patients with steroids resistance or other reasons. The SUCRA value of RPC4046 was moderate to high, indicating a substantial probability of being ranked the top-tier interventions. Due to overlapping of CI, there was not significant difference between RPC4046 and BODT. Thus, according to our results, RPC4046 showed comparable efficacy to BODT without statistically significant differences. Moreover, Mepolizumab was followed by RPC4046 in clinical endpoint. Benralizumab ranked highly in histological endpoint followed by RPC4046. Almost all biologics were superior to placebo.

Secondly, Dupilumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody, targets the shared receptor subunit of IL-4 and IL-13, effectively blocking signaling pathways central to type 2 inflammatory responses in EoE. This therapeutic agent has received FDA approval for the treatment of EoE in patients aged 12 years or older with a minimum body weight of 40 kg, as supported by clinical evidence [4, 32]. There are two different dose regimen: 300 mg qw and 300mg q2w. Apparently, the 300 mg qw regimen was ranked higher than the 300 mg q2w regimen across all endpoints but demonstrated a statistically significant advantage only in the clinical endpoint analysis. . Regarding the pharmacokinetic basis of its efficacy, elevated drug exposure correlates with enhanced therapeutic outcomes. Pharmacodynamic analyses by Chehade et al. revealed that patients receiving 300 mg qw doses of dupilumab exhibited a twofold increase in mean serum drug concentrations at weeks 16 and 52 compared to lower-exposure cohorts, suggesting dose-dependent bioavailability as a potential determinant of clinical response [19]. It is notable that Dupilumab 300 mg qw ranked the highest in clinical improvement followed by BODT without significant difference between them though. As for other endpoints, Dupilumab showed comparable efficacy without statistically significant difference compared to steroids though exhibited relative lower SUCRA value.

Elevated expression of IL-13 in the esophageal mucosa of EoE patients contributes to pathological processes including eosinophil migration, tissue reorganization, and fibrotic progression. Therapeutic strategies targeting this cytokine have emerged, with RPC4046—a humanized anti-IL-13 monoclonal antibody—demonstrating potential efficacy in clinical trials for EoE management. Parallelly, dupilumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody, exerts dual inhibitory effects by binding to the IL-4 receptor α subunit, thereby disrupting signaling cascades mediated by both IL-4 and IL-13. These cytokines serve as central drivers of type 2 inflammatory responses, critically influencing Th2 lymphocyte differentiation. Mechanistically, IL-4 and IL-13 synergistically induce esophageal pathophysiology through three interconnected pathways: promoting eosinophilic accumulation, triggering mast cell mediator release, and facilitating structural alterations in mucosal architecture [4].According to our statistical results, Dupilumab had probability of higher ranks than RPC4046. Whether IL-4/IL-13 dual-pathway inhibitors demonstrate superior efficacy or safety compared to IL-13-targeting monoclonal antibodies requires further investigation through rigorously designed RCT to clarify this critical therapeutic question.

Thirdly, previous analyses mostly pay attention to the histologic endpointwhich also served as the primary outcome in their studies. However, an increasing number of studies show that symptomatic and endoscopic improvement are recognized as crucial parts of course of disease. This underscores the necessity of adopting a multidimensional approach to assess EoE progression, rather than relying solely on eosinophil reduction metrics. To address this gap, Dellon et al. developed the I-SEE, a novel clinical scoring system that quantifies disease burden through parameters extending beyond histologic eosinophilia [5]. In our work, we comprehensively analysed different agents within three endpoints. Also, we have identified potentially valuable agents which are beneficial to improve clinical, endoscopic and histological outcomes.

Our study demonstrated that steroids such as BODT had a high probability of superior efficacy and ODT might be the most effective form of steroids. BODT ranked second for induction of clinical endpoint (the primary endpoint) and ranked first for histological and endoscopic improvement (the secondary endpoint). Meanwhile, FODT ranked third for endoscopic improvement. Ma et al.'s comparative analysis revealed that budesonide oral tablets achieved significantly higher rates of histologic response in EoE patients versus alternative corticosteroid formulations, establishing BODT as the preferred topical steroid regimen. This conclusion aligns with Rokkas et al.'s systematic evaluation, which ranked twice-daily BODT as the most efficacious intervention based on NMA outcomes [27, 28]. These findings were consistent with our NMA. Our anlaysis demonstrated that BODT was more likely to be the best efficacious agents for histological remission and endoscopic improvement. Our NMA demonstrated that Dupilumab 300 mg qw ranked highest for symptomatic improvement which was not aligned with the results from the NMA [30]. The possible reason was the effect value used in statistical analysis. Due to the heterogeneity in symptom assessment tools across RCTs, we employed the SMD as the effect measure to synthesize symptom outcomes in our meta-analysis. However, the use of RR as the effect measure, combined with the heterogeneity in symptom questionnaires across studies, may explain the discrepancies observed between the two symptom outcome analyses [33].

Oral administration of topical corticosteroids has emerged as a widely utilized initial therapeutic strategy for EoE. While the therapeutic effectiveness of these agents has been robustly validated across multiple clinical trials, their safety profile is predominantly characterized by localized complications, particularly candidiasis affecting the oropharyngeal and esophageal mucosa. Conversely, biologics such as dupilumab, exhibit distinct tolerability patterns, with injection-site reactions being the most commonly documented treatment-associated adverse event [33, 34].We have listed the incidence of Candidiasis and injection site reaction of steroids and biologics (Supplementary Table 3). Candidiasis infection was observed in all studies involving steroids. Injection site reactions were reported more frequently in biologic agents such as Dupilumab. To balance the risks of these side effects, drug selection must be individualized based on the patient’s overall clinical profile.

Our present study has several limitations. First, our review does not contain the recent appearing drugs such as tezepelumab and dectrekumab for the reason that we can’t acquire the trial data. We hope that future analysis could include the aforementioned agents. Second, only 11 of 18 RCTs were at low risk of bias across all domains. Third, we did not incorporate and analyze the data of maintenance treatment due to its insufficiency, which could be performed in future studies. Our NMA supports the use of dupilumab as the first-line induction treatment of inducing the symptomatic improvement in EoE patients. Moreover, our study provides the evidence for those PPI-resistant or steroids-resistant patients to choose alternative agents.

Conclusion

Our NMA analysed common agents and incorporated some of the latest RCTs. We conducted an analysis that demonstrates efficacy of various agents used in EoE treatment according to different endpoints. In the future, We hope that an increasing number of clinical trials will adopt the I-SEE, thereby enabling us to verify agents’ efficacy precisely and conveniently.

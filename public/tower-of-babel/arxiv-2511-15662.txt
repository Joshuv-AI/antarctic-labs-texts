Abstract
. For a smooth expanding circle map, we show that the empirical distribution of Lyapunov exponents of periodic points of any fixed period is close to normal, with an error that decreases as the period grows. This establishes a version of the Central Limit Theorem for such finite periodic orbits.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 37D20, 37D35; Secondary: 60F05, 37E10

1.
Main result

In this article, for a smooth orientation-preserving expanding circle map
f\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
, we show that the Lyapunov exponents of its periodic orbits of any
fixed
period distribute normally, with a small error depending polynomially on the period. This result is supported by several computer experiments illustrated in Figure
1
.

Let
\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})
be the set of periodic points of period
n
(we allow for it to be not the smallest period). If
K\geqslant 2
is the
degree
of
f
, then
\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})
contains exactly
K^{n}-1
elements. For
p\in\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})
, define

\chi_{n}(p)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\ln(f^{n})^{\prime}(p)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl(\ln f^{\prime}\circ f^{k}\bigr)(p)

to be the
(periodic) Lyapunov exponent
of
p
. This value does not depend on the choice of a point in the orbit of
p
. Note that
\exp(n\cdot\chi_{n}(p))
is the
multiplier
of the periodic orbit
\bigl\{p,\,f(p),\,\ldots,\,f^{n-1}(p)\bigr\}
, and
n\cdot\chi_{n}(p)
is the
length
of that orbit
[
McM10
]
.

(b)

Figure 1
.

30
, using
100
equal-width bins, where the map is:

(A)
x\mapsto 2x+0.01\big(\sin(2\pi x)+\cos(2\pi x)-1\big)\mod 1
;

(B) the Blaschke product
z\mapsto z(z-0.1)/(1-0.1z)
restricted to the unit circle; in this case, the corresponding expanding circle map preserves the Lebesgue measure on the circle.

For a fixed period
n
, we treat
\chi_{n}
as a random variable in
\mathbb{R}
distributed with the probability

(1.1)

\mathbb{P}(\chi_{n}\in[x,y])\coloneqq\frac{\sharp\{p\in\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n}):\chi_{n}(p)\in[x,y]\}}{\sharp\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})},

where
\sharp A
stands for the number of elements in a set
A
.

Let us also denote by
\mu
the unique invariant probability measure of
maximal entropy
on
\mathbb{T}
for
f
.

The main result of this article is the following theorem:

Main Theorem
(Distribution of periodic Lyapunov exponents).

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{r}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{T})
,
r\geqslant 2
, be a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
-smooth orientation-preserving expanding circle map that is not smoothly conjugate to the linear map. Denote by
\overline{\chi}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\!\ln f^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
the Lyapunov exponent of
f
with respect to the measure of maximal entropy. Then there exist positive constants
\sigma>0
and
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with
a<b
, we have

\biggl\lvert\mathbb{P}\biggl(\frac{(\chi_{n}-\overline{\chi})\sqrt{n}}{\sigma}\in[a,b]\biggr)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{a}^{b}\!\exp\biggl(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr\rvert\leqslant\frac{C}{\sqrt[4]{n}}.

A closely related result about lengths of closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces was obtained by Gekhtman, Taylor, and Tiozzo in
[
GTT19
]
, giving a positive answer to the conjecture of Chas–Li–Maskit
[
CLM13
]
. More specifically, in
[
GTT19
]
the authors establish a version of the Central Limit Theorem (without the error term), as
n\to+\infty
, for the empirical distribution of the lengths of closed geodesics whose combinatorial length (in terms of word length in the generators of the fundamental group) is bounded above by
n
.

Taken together, our main result and the result of
[
GTT19
]
provide a new instance of the parallel between the properties of the lengths (Lyapunov exponents) of periodic orbits for expanding circle maps and the behavior of the lengths of closed geodesics on compact hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g\geqslant 2
. This analogy (“dictionary”) was put forward by McMullen
[
McM10
]
in the setting of expanding circle maps arising from Blaschke products.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{r}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{T})
,
r\geqslant 2
,
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, be an expanding circle map not smoothly conjugate to a linear map, and
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{*}
be the partition of
\mathbb{T}
into half-open intervals on which
f^{n}
is invertible. We choose this partition in such a way that
f^{n}(I)=\mathbb{T}
for every partition interval
I
. In this way,
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{*}
is a set of
K^{n}
intervals and for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
each interval
\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{*}
contains exactly one point from the set
f^{-n}(z)
. Furthermore, all but one interval
\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}^{*}
has a unique point
x_{\mathbbm{p}}\in\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})
that corresponds to a periodic orbit of period
n
. Let us denote the exceptional interval
\mathbbm{p}^{*}
and denote the set of non-exceptional intervals by
\mathbb{P}_{n}=\mathbb{P}^{*}_{n}\setminus\{\mathbbm{p}^{*}\}
. Note that
\sharp\mathbb{P}_{n}=K^{n}-1=\sharp\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})
.

For each
\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
, we denote by
\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}
the well-defined inverse branch of
f^{n}|_{\mathbbm{p}}
. And we denote by
\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}^{*}}
the corresponding inverse branch for the exceptional interval
\mathbbm{p}^{*}
.

We will also fix the notation

h(x)\coloneqq\ln f^{\prime}(x)\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad g_{n}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl(h\circ f^{k}\bigr)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}.

Then
\chi_{n}(p)=g_{n}(p)
for each
p\in\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})
. We want to study the empirical distribution of
{\chi}_{n}
treated as a random variable given by (
1.1
). For such a random variable, and for each continuous function
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, we have the expectation

(2.1)

\mathbb{E}(\psi(\chi_{n}))=\frac{\sum_{p\in\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})}\psi(\chi_{n}(p))}{\sharp\mathrm{Fix}(f^{n})}=\frac{\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\psi(g_{n}(x_{\mathbbm{p}}))}{\sharp\mathbb{P}_{n}}.

To simplify our notation, in what follows, we will write
X_{n}=Y_{n}+\mathcal{O}(Z_{n})
if there is a constant
C>0
, that may depend only on the function
f
(and hence independent of
n
), such that
\lvert X_{n}-Y_{n}\rvert\leqslant C\cdot Z_{n}
.

Finally, we will denote
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
,
k=0,\,1,\,\dots
, the
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-norm on
\mathbb{T}
.

3.
The average

Our goal is to compute (
2.1
) for
\psi=\operatorname{id}
. To this end, we want to compare it with the transfer operator

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}\colon\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R}),\quad\quad\mathcal{L}(\varphi)(x)\coloneqq\sum_{y\in f^{-1}(x)}\varphi(y).

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
z\in\mathbb{T}
and every integer
n\geqslant 1
,

\mathbb{E}(\chi_{n})=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{L}^{n}(h)(z)+\mathcal{O}(K^{-n}).

Proof.

We have

\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}g_{n}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})=\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}h\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)=\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}h(x_{\mathbbm{p}}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\Bigl\lvert\mathcal{L}^{n}(h)(z)-\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}g_{n}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\Bigr\rvert=\Bigl\lvert\sum_{y\in f^{-n}(z)}h(y)-\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}h(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\lvert h(\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}^{*}}(z))\rvert+\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\lvert(h\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}})(z)-h(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\rvert\leqslant\|h\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|h^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\lvert\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}(z)-x_{\mathbbm{p}}\rvert

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\|h\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|h^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\cdot\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\lvert\mathbbm{p}\rvert\leqslant\|h\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|h^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}.

Since
\mathbb{E}(\chi_{n})=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}g_{n}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})
, we conclude that
\mathbb{E}(\chi_{n})=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{n}(h)(z)+\mathcal{O}(K^{-n})
.
∎

We introduce the spectral decomposition for the transfer operator (
3.1
) in the following lemma (see e.g.
[
DKL21
]
,
[
BKL22
, Lemma 3.1]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

For each function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})
,

\mathcal{L}(\varphi)=K\cdot\mu(\varphi)+Q(\varphi),

where
\mu(\varphi)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\!\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
, with
\mu
being the measure of maximal entropy. Furthermore,
Q(\mathbbm{1})=0
,
\mu(Q(\varphi))=0
, and there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every integer
n\geqslant 1
,

\|Q^{n}(\varphi)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}\leqslant C\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}.

Consequently, for each integer
n\geqslant 1
,
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\varphi)=K^{n}\cdot\mu(\varphi)+Q^{n}(\varphi)
. Thus the iterations of the normalized operator
K^{-n}\cdot\mathcal{L}^{n}(\varphi)
converge to
\mu(\varphi)
exponentially fast (as
n\to+\infty
). Hence Lemma
3.1
yields the following corollary. Before stating it, let us introduce the following centered quantities:

(3.2)

\overline{\chi}\coloneqq\mu(h)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\!\ln f^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\quad\quad\widehat{h}\coloneqq h-\mu(h),\quad\quad\widehat{\chi}_{n}\coloneqq\chi_{n}-\overline{\chi}.

Corollary 3.3
(Centered expectation estimate).

For every integer
n\geqslant 1
,

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}\big(\chi_{n}\big)=\mu(h)+\mathcal{O}(K^{-n}),\quad\quad\mathbb{E}\big(\widehat{\chi}_{n}\big)=\mathcal{O}(K^{-n}).

In particular,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}(\chi_{n})=\mu(h),\quad\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})=0.

For the rest of the paper, we focus on the centered random variable
\widehat{\chi}_{n}
.

4.
The variance

In this section, we want to estimate the variance
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\big([\widehat{\chi}_{n}-\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})]^{2}\big)=\mathbb{E}\big([\chi_{n}-\mathbb{E}(\chi_{n})]^{2}\big)
. The key result of this section is the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
(Centered variance estimate).

There exists
\sigma>0
such that for every integer
n\geqslant 1
,

(4.1)

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})=n^{-1}\cdot\sigma^{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-2}\bigr).

In particular,

\operatorname{Var}(\sqrt{n}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{n})=\sigma^{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-1}\bigr),\quad\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\sqrt{n}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{n}\bigr)=\sigma^{2}.

Proof.

Using (
2.1
), (
3.2
), and Corollary
3.3
, we have:

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}n^{-2}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{h}\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)\Biggr]^{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(K^{-2n}\bigr).

But

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{h}\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)\Biggr]^{2}&=\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{h}\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)^{2}+2\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n-1}\widehat{h}\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)\widehat{h}\bigl(f^{j}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)\Biggr]\\
&=\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{h}\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)^{2}+2\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1-k}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)\Biggr]\\
&=\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{h}\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\bigl(f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)\Biggr]\\
&=\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\Biggl[n\cdot\widehat{h}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\Biggr].\end{split}

Therefore,

(4.2)

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}n^{-1}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\Biggl[\widehat{h}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\biggl(1-\frac{j}{n}\biggr)\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\Biggr]+\mathcal{O}\bigl(K^{-2n}\bigr).

Next, we want to compare the expression in (
4.2
) to the
n
-th power of transfer operator (
3.1
). Note that for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\begin{split}&\Bigl\lvert\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)(z)-\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\Bigr\rvert\\
&\qquad=\bigl\lvert\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)(\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}^{*}}(z))+\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\bigl(\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)(\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}(z))-\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr)\bigr\rvert\\
&\qquad\leqslant\|\widehat{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\bigl\lvert\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}\bigr)(z)-\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\bigr\rvert\\
&\qquad\leqslant\|\widehat{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\int_{\mathbbm{p}}\!\bigl(f^{j}\bigr)^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad\leqslant\|\widehat{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\!\bigl(f^{j}\bigr)^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad\leqslant\|\widehat{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\bigl(1+K^{j}\bigr).\end{split}

Similarly, for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\begin{split}\Bigl\lvert\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigl(\widehat{h}^{2}\bigr)(z)-\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\widehat{h}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})^{2}\Bigr\rvert&=\Bigl\lvert\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ\phi_{\mathbbm{p}^{*}}\bigr)(z)^{2}+\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\bigl(\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}\bigr)(z)^{2}-\widehat{h}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})^{2}\bigr)\Bigr\rvert\\
&\leqslant\|\widehat{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\lvert\mathbbm{p}\rvert\\
&\leqslant\|\widehat{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+2\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\|\widehat{h}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}.\end{split}

Putting the last two computations together into (
4.2
) (and taking into account the linearity of the transfer operator and that
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1-\frac{j}{n}\bigr)K^{j}
is of order
K^{n}/n
), we obtain:

(4.3)

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}n^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{n}\biggl(\widehat{h}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\biggl(1-\frac{j}{n}\biggr)\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\biggr)(z)+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-2}\bigr).

Let us use the spectral decomposition from Lemma
3.2
to estimate the right-hand side of (
4.3
). Observe that
\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)=\mathcal{L}^{n-j}\bigl(\widehat{h}\mathcal{L}^{j}\widehat{h}\bigr)
. Since
\mu\bigl(\widehat{h}\bigr)=0
, this implies
\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)=\mathcal{L}^{n-j}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot Q^{j}\bigl(\widehat{h}\bigr)\bigr)
. Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(K^{n}-1)^{-1}\frac{j}{n}\bigl\lvert\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f\bigr)\bigr)(z)\bigr\rvert=\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-1}\bigr).

Thus, (
4.3
) reduces to

(4.4)

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}n^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{n}\biggl(\widehat{h}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\biggr)(z)+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-2}\bigr).

Finally, define the
asymptotic variance of
f

(4.5)

\sigma^{2}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu\biggl(\widehat{h}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\biggr)=\mu\biggl(\widehat{h}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\biggr).

By, e.g.,
[
PU10
, Equation (2.11.3)]
, this limit exists, and by the discussion in
[
PU10
, Chapter 2]
,
\sigma^{2}\neq 0
if and only if
\widehat{h}
is not cohomologous to
0
. By the marked Lyapunov spectrum rigidity
[
SS85
]
and Livšic theory, this is possible if and only if
f
is not smoothly conjugate to a linear map. Hence, by the assumption in the main theorem,
\sigma>0
.

Furthermore, because of the exponential decay of correlations for
f

[
DKL21
]
, the speed of convergence in (
4.5
) is of order
1/n
, and thus

(4.6)

\mu\biggl(\widehat{h}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\biggr)=\sigma^{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-1}\bigr).

Finally, again by the spectral decomposition,

\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigl(\widehat{h}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\cdot\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{j}\bigr)\bigr)\bigr)(z)}{(K^{n}-1)n}=\frac{K^{n}\sigma^{2}}{(K^{n}-1)n}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-2}\bigr)\\
=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-2}\bigr).

Combining this last estimate with (
4.4
), we get

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\chi}_{n})=n^{-1}\cdot\sigma^{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-2}\bigr)
,
as desired.
∎

5.
The distribution and proof of the Main Theorem

Let us introduce the random variable
S_{n}\coloneqq\sqrt{n}\widehat{\chi}_{n}
, and let
\sigma_{n}^{2}\coloneqq\operatorname{Var}(S_{n})
be its variance. By Lemma
4.1
,
\sigma_{n}^{2}=\sigma^{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-1}\bigr)
, where
\sigma>0
is the constant from that lemma (the asymptotic variance of
f
).

Let us define the
distribution function

(5.1)

F_{n}(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(\{S_{n}/\sigma\leqslant x\})=\mathbb{E}({\mathbbm{1}}_{\{S_{n}/\sigma\leqslant x\}})

and the corresponding
characteristic function

\Psi_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\Psi_{n}(\lambda)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}(\exp(i\lambda S_{n}/\sigma))=(K^{n}-1)^{-1}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\exp\biggl(\frac{i\lambda}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{k}\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\biggr).

We have
\Psi_{n}(0)=1
,
\Psi_{n}^{\prime}(0)=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n}K^{-n})
(Corollary
3.3
),
-\Psi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)={\sigma_{n}^{2}}\big/{\sigma^{2}}=1+\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-1}\bigr)
(Lemma
4.1
), and
\bigl\lvert\Psi_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lambda)\bigr\rvert\leqslant n^{3/2}\|\widehat{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\big/\sigma^{3}
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,

(5.2)

\Psi_{n}(\lambda)=1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\lvert\lambda\rvert\sqrt{n}K^{-n}+\lvert\lambda\rvert^{2}n^{-1}+\lvert\lambda\rvert^{3}n^{3/2}\bigr).

Let

\mathcal{E}(\lambda)\coloneqq\bigl\lvert\Psi_{n}(\lambda)-\exp\bigl(-\lambda^{2}\big/2\bigr)\bigr\rvert

be the difference between the characteristic functions of
S_{n}/\sigma
and the standard normal distribution. Then, by the Berry–Esseen estimate
[
Fe71
, Chapter XVI.3, Equation (3.13)]
, we have

\biggl\lvert F_{n}(x)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\!\exp\biggl(-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr\rvert\leqslant\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\!\frac{\mathcal{E}(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\frac{24}{\pi T_{n}}.

Hence, for
T_{n}=\varepsilon\sqrt[4]{n}
(where
\varepsilon>0
will be chosen later uniformly over all
n
),

(5.3)

F_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\!\exp\biggl(-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}y+C_{1}\cdot\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\sqrt[4]{n}}\!\frac{\mathcal{E}(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\mathcal{O}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{n}}\biggr)

for some uniform constant
C_{1}>0
. Therefore, to prove the Main Theorem, we need to estimate
\mathcal{E}(\zeta)
and show that the middle integral is of order
1/\sqrt[4]{n}
.

We decompose:

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\sqrt[4]{n}}\!\frac{\mathcal{E}(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=\int_{0}^{1/n}\!\frac{\mathcal{E}(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\int_{1/n}^{\varepsilon\sqrt[4]{n}}\!\frac{\mathcal{E}(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}.

As for
I_{1}
, using (
5.2
) and the expansion
\exp\bigl(-\lambda^{2}\big/2\bigr)=1-\lambda^{2}\big/2+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\lvert\lambda\rvert^{3}\bigr)
, we obtain (again for some uniform constant
C_{2}>0
)

(5.4)

I_{1}=\int_{0}^{1/n}\!\frac{\mathcal{E}(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\leqslant C_{2}\int_{0}^{1/n}\!\biggl(\sqrt{n}\cdot K^{-n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}+\zeta^{2}n^{3/2}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-3/2}\bigr).

As for
I_{2}
, we cannot apply such a naive strategy. Instead, we will estimate
\mathcal{E}(\lambda)
differently. For this purpose, let us introduce a new
twisted operator
acting on
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C})
:

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}(\varphi)(x)\coloneqq K^{-1}\mathcal{L}\biggl(\exp\biggl(\frac{i\lambda}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\widehat{h}\biggr)\cdot\varphi\biggr)(x)=K^{-1}\sum_{y\in f^{-1}(x)}\exp\biggl(i\lambda\frac{\widehat{h}(y)}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\biggr)\cdot\varphi(y).

Note that
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{0/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}=K^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{L}
, and hence we want to view
K^{-1}\cdot\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}
as a
perturbation
of the operator
K^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{L}
with perturbation parameter
\lambda/(\sigma\sqrt{n})
. Using the previous notation, for a point
z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\begin{split}&\bigl\lvert\Psi_{n}(\lambda)-\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}({\mathbbm{1}})(z)\bigr\rvert\\
&\qquad\leqslant\frac{1}{K^{n}}\biggl\lvert\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\exp\biggl(\frac{i\lambda}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{k}\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})\biggr)-\exp\biggl(\frac{i\lambda}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{k}\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}\bigr)(z)\biggr)\biggr\rvert+\frac{2}{K^{n}}\\
&\qquad\leqslant\frac{\lvert\lambda\rvert}{\sigma\sqrt{n}K^{n}}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl\lvert\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{k}\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})-\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{k}\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}\bigr)(z)\bigr\rvert+\frac{2}{K^{n}},\end{split}

where the term
2K^{-n}
comes from a straightforward estimate of the exceptional branch
\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}^{*}}
that is present in the transfer operator, but is not accounted for in
\Psi_{n}
.

Note that
\lvert f^{n}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})-(f^{n}\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}})(z)\rvert\leqslant 1
, and thus, by the contraction of the inverse branches of the map, we obtain
\bigl\lvert f^{k}(x_{\mathbbm{p}})-\bigl(f^{k}\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}\bigr)(z)\bigr\rvert\leqslant\lambda_{*}^{k-n}
, where
1<\lambda_{*}\coloneqq\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}}f^{\prime}(x)
. Hence, there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
, uniform over
n
, such that

\begin{split}&K^{-n}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl\lvert\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{k}\bigr)(x_{\mathbbm{p}})-\bigl(\widehat{h}\circ f^{k}\circ\varphi_{\mathbbm{p}}\bigr)(z)\bigr\rvert\leqslant K^{-n}\|h^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\sum_{\mathbbm{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{*}^{k-n}\leqslant C_{3}.\end{split}

We conclude that for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\Psi_{n}(\lambda)=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}({\mathbbm{1}})(z)+\mathcal{O}(\lvert\lambda\rvert/\sqrt{n})+\mathcal{O}(K^{-n}).

Therefore,

(5.5)

\mathcal{E}(\zeta)=\bigl\lvert\Psi_{n}(\zeta)-e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}\bigr\rvert\leqslant\bigl\lvert\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}({\mathbbm{1}})(z)-e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}\bigr\rvert+\mathcal{O}(\zeta/\sqrt{n})+\mathcal{O}(K^{-n}).

To compute
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}({\mathbbm{1}})(z)
, we have to understand its spectrum, in particular the maximal eigenvalue and the derivative of its maximal eigenfunction. This can be done by perturbation theory (see e.g.
[
DKL21
, Section 1]
,
[
Go15
]
) starting from the spectrum of
K^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{L}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
, which is analyzed in
[
BKL22
]
. In this way, we obtain that there exists
\delta>0
and
\xi\in(0,1)
so that for
\zeta\in(0,\delta\sqrt{n})
,
the leading eigenvalue
\kappa(\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n}))
of
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}
satisfies

\kappa(t)=1-\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2+\mathcal{O}\bigl(t^{3}\bigr),\quad t=\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})

(as a perturbation of the leading eigenvalue
1
for
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{0}=K^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{L}
; here
\sigma
is exactly the asymptotic variance of
\widehat{h}
). Hence,

\ln\kappa(\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n}))=-\zeta^{2}n^{-1}/2+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\zeta^{3}n^{-3/2}\bigr),

and we have the following spectral decomposition:

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}(\varphi)=(\kappa(\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})))^{n}\cdot\widehat{\Pi}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}(\varphi)+\widehat{Q}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}(\varphi),

where
\widehat{\Pi}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}
is the eigenprojection corresponding to
\kappa(\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n}))
and
\widehat{Q}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}-\kappa(\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n}))\cdot\widehat{\Pi}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}
. In addition, we have the following estimates:

\displaystyle\widehat{\Pi}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}(\varphi)=\mu(\varphi)+\mathcal{O}(\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})),

\displaystyle\bigl\|\widehat{Q}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}(\varphi)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}\leqslant C_{4}\xi^{n}\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}.

Therefore, for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\zeta/(\sigma\sqrt{n})}^{n}({\mathbbm{1}})(z)=\exp\bigl(-\zeta^{2}/2+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\zeta^{3}/\sqrt{n}\bigr)\bigr)(1+\mathcal{O}(\zeta/\sqrt{n}))+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{n}).

Note that, for
\delta
small enough (uniformly in
n
),

\exp\bigl(-\zeta^{2}/2+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\zeta^{3}/\sqrt{n}\bigr)\bigr)\leqslant e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}+\bigl(1-e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}\bigr)\mathcal{O}(\zeta/\sqrt{n})\leqslant e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\zeta/\sqrt{n}\bigr),

hence,
\exp\bigl(-\zeta^{2}/2+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\zeta^{3}/\sqrt{n}\bigr)\bigr)(1+\mathcal{O}(\zeta/\sqrt{n}))\leqslant e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}+\bigl(e^{-\zeta^{2}/2}+1\bigr)\mathcal{O}(\zeta/\sqrt{n})
.
Combining the estimates above with (
5.5
), we get

\mathcal{E}(\zeta)\leqslant\zeta\exp\bigl(-\zeta^{2}/2\bigr)\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})+\mathcal{O}(\zeta/\sqrt{n})+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{n})+\mathcal{O}(K^{-n}).

Therefore, for
\varepsilon<\delta
,

\begin{split}I_{2}=\int_{1/n}^{\varepsilon\sqrt[4]{n}}\!\frac{\mathcal{E}(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\leqslant\frac{C_{5}}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{1/n}^{\varepsilon\sqrt[4]{n}}\!e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\mathcal{O}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{n}}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(n(\xi^{n}+K^{-n})).\end{split}

Since the integral in the last estimate is bounded above by
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\!e^{-\zeta^{2}\big/2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta<+\infty
and
n(\xi^{n}+K^{-n})
converges to
0
exponentially fast, we conclude that
I_{2}\leqslant\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt[4]{n})
. Plugging this estimate together with (
5.4
) into (
5.3
) yields

F_{n}(x)=\mathbb{P}\biggl(\biggl\{\frac{(\chi_{n}-\overline{\chi})\sqrt{n}}{\sigma}\leqslant x\biggr\}\biggr)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\!\exp\biggl(-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}y+\mathcal{O}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{n}}\biggr),

which finishes the proof of the Main Theorem.

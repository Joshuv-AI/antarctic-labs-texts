Complete Characterizations of Well-Posedness in Parametric Composite Optimization
Boris S. Mordukhovich, Peipei Tang, Chengjing Wang

Abstract
This paper provides complete characterization of well-posedness for Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) systems associated with general problems of perturbed composite optimization. Leveraging the property of parabolic regularity for composite models, we show that the second-order subderivative of the cost function reduces to the novel second-order variational function playing a crucial role in the subsequent analysis. This foundational result implies that the strong second-order sufficient condition (SSOSC) introduced in this work for the general class of composite optimization problems naturally extends the classical second-order sufficient condition in nonlinear programming. Then we obtain several equivalent characterizations of the second-order qualification condition (SOQC) and highlight its equivalence to the constraint nondegeneracy condition under the 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} -cone reducibility assumption. These insights lead us to multiple equivalent conditions for the major Lipschitz-like/Aubin property of KKT systems, including the SOQC combined with the new second-order subdifferential condition and the SOQC combined with tilt stability of local minimizers. Furthermore, under 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} -cone reducibility, we prove that the Lipschitz-like property of the reference KKT system is equivalent to its strong regularity. Finally, we demonstrate that the Lipschitz-like property is equivalent to the nonsingularity of the generalized Jacobian associated with the KKT system under a certain verifiable assumption. These results provide a unified and rigorous framework for analyzing stability and sensitivity of solutions to composite optimization problems, as well as for the design and justification of numerical algorithms.



Keywords:

composite optimization, variational analysis and generalized differentiation, well-posedness, parabolic regularity, KKT systems, second-order sufficient conditions, tilt stability

Introduction

This paper concerns the study of the general class of
composite optimization
problems given by

\displaystyle\qquad\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\varphi(x):=h(x)+g(F(x)),

(1.1)

where
h:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}
,
F:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m}
are
{\cal C}^{1}
-smooth mappings and
g:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}:=\mathcal{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is an extended-real-valued lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) convex function, which is always assumed to be proper, i.e.,
g(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
with
{\rm dom}\,g:=\{x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\;|\;g(x)<\infty\}\neq\emptyset
. The extended-real-valued format in (
1.1
) allows us to incorporate various constraints via
F(x)\in{\rm dom}\,g
. In particular, model (
1.1
) covers problems of nonlinear programming, second-order cone programming, and semidefinite programming that correspond to the indicator function
g
of the corresponding cone, as well as other classes of constrained problems considered below.

We study the
perturbed
version of (
1.1
)
with the parameters
a\in\mathcal{R}^{n}
and
b\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
written as

\displaystyle\qquad\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\widetilde{\varphi}(x,b)-\langle a,x\rangle,

(1.2)

where
\widetilde{\varphi}(x,b):=h(x)+g(F(x)+b)
. The
KKT system
(necessary optimality conditions) associated with the canonically perturbed problem (
1.2
) takes the form

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}L(x,u)=a,\quad b\in\partial g^{*}(u)-F(x),

(1.3)

where
g^{*}
is the
Fenchel conjugate
of
g
, and where
L(\cdot,\cdot)
is the
Lagrangian
of (
1.1
) defined by

\displaystyle L(x,u):=h(x)+\langle u,F(x)\rangle,\quad(x,u)\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{R}^{m}.

Denote by
S_{\operatorname{KKT}}(a,b)
the parameterized
solution mapping
for (
1.3
) and by
\Phi
the
KKT mapping

\displaystyle\Phi(x,u):=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{x}L(x,u)\\
\partial g^{*}(u)-F(x)\end{array}\right].

During the past few decades, great many efforts have been made to investigate diverse
well-posedness
issues for constraint and variational systems under perturbations; see, e.g., the monographs
[
4
,
12
,
15
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
38
]
and the references therein. In his landmark work
[
37
]
, Robinson introduced the notion of
strong regularity
for the generalized equation
0\in\Phi(x,u)
at
(\bar{x},\bar{u})
, which means that the inverse of the linearized mapping

\displaystyle(x,u)\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{u})(x-\bar{x})+\nabla F(\bar{x})^{T}(u-\bar{u})\\
-F(\bar{x})-\nabla F(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x})\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\partial g^{*}(u)\end{array}\right]

admits a Lipschitz continuous single-valued localization around the origin, where “
A^{T}
” stands for the matrix transposition. Characterizing the strong regularity of perturbed KKT systems at their solution points has emerged as a significant research topic in mathematical programming. In the context of nonlinear programming (NLP), it is established in
[
37
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
5
, Theorem 4.10]
that the strong regularity of the canonically perturbed KKT system is equivalent to the simultaneous fulfillment of the strong second-order sufficient condition (SSOSC) and the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ), as well as
[
37
, Theorem 3.1]
to the nonsingularity of the associated Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the KKT system at the corresponding solution point.

As shown in
[
11
, Theorem 5]
, the strong regularity of KKT systems associated with NLPs is equivalent to the Lipschitzian behavior of mappings that was first revealed by Aubin
[
1
]
in the context of convex programming under the name of “pseudo-Lipschitz property”. This notion, which is a graphical localization of the classical (Hausdorff) Lipschitz continuity of set-valued mappings, is now called the
Aubin
or
Lipschitz-like property
to emphasize its Lipschitzian nature (while “pseudo” means “false”). It has been well realized that such Lipschitzian behavior of set-valued mappings/multifunctions is equivalent to the two major well-posedness concepts in nonlinear and variational analysis known as
metric regularity
and
linear openness/covering
applied to the mapping inverse; see, e.g.,
[
31
,
38
]
for more details and references.

Developing the line of
[
37
]
, the equivalence between the strong regularity and appropriate versions of SSOSC and nondegeneracy conditions is established in
[
3
, Theorem 30]
for second-order cone programming, while the equivalence between the strong regularity and Aubin property for such programs is obtained in
[
35
, Theorem 21]
(see also the Errarum in
[
34
]
) under the strict complementarity condition and in
[
7
, Theorem 4.12]
without the latter. For semidefinite programming, similar results are available in
[
40
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
. For general constrained optimization problems, the results of
[
23
, Theorem 5.6]
(see also
[
33
, Section 5.5]
) establish equivalence among the properties of strong regularity, Lipschitzian strong stability, Lipschitzian full stability, and second-order subdifferential conditions at local minimizers under the reducibility and nondegeneracy conditions. Subsequently,
[
26
, Theorem 3.3]
derives the equivalence between the notions of Lipschitzian full stability for local minimizers under some nondegeneracy and of strong regularity of the associated KKT system for a class of composite optimization problems involving convex piecewise linear functions. The recent paper
[
41
]
proposes a definition of the generalized SSOSC and demonstrates the equivalence between the introduced SSOSC in conjunction with the nondegeneracy condition, the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the nonsmooth system at a given KKT point, and the strong regularity of the KKT point for composite optimization problems. This comprehensive body of research demonstrates fundamental connections between various regularity and stability concepts in optimization theory, providing a unified framework for analyzing well-posedness behavior of optimization problems and their solution structures.

Recall
[
4
]
that the nonnegative orthant cone, the second-order cone and the positive semidefinite cone are
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible. This naturally raises the question of whether the Lipschitz-like property of the KKT system is equivalent to the strong regularity of solution points for all
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible problems and also in more general settings. While significant steps in this direction have been recently made in
[
7
,
6
]
for second-order cone and semidefinite programming based on the Mordukhovich criterion, it is mentioned in the conclusion of
[
6
]
that “currently, it is not clear to us how to extend these results to the generic non-polyhedral
{\cal C}^{2}
-cone reducible constrained optimization.” Nevertheless, the latest preprint
[
18
]
provides the equivalence between the Aubin property and strong regularity for certain
{\cal C}^{2}
-cone reducible generalized equations by using the degree theory, without any appeal to variational analysis.

In this paper, we employ and develop advanced tools of variational analysis and generalized differentiation that allow us to obtain comprehensive characterizations of well-posedness properties of the KKT systems (
1.3
) with
{\cal C}^{2}
-cone reducible functions
g
. In particular, we establish in this way the equivalence between the strong regularity and Aubin property for the KKT system under the assumptions different from
[
18
, Theorem 4]
that is applicable to our model when
g
is the indicator function of a
{\cal C}^{2}
-cone reducible set; see Remark
5.1
for more discussions.

As has been well realized, primal and dual second-order conditions play a pivotal role in characterizing well-posedness of optimization problems. In what follows, we systematically exploit the powerful property of
parabolic regularity
for the function
g
in (
1.2
) that has been largely investigated in the recent publications
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. To derive our main results, we provide a precise description of the second-order qualification condition (SOQC) and establish its equivalence to the nondegeneracy condition under the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducibility. Leveraging the properties of isolated calmness and tilt stability, we characterize the Aubin property based on three distinct conditions. Under the parabolic regularity of
g
, we show that this property is equivalent to SOQC combined with the new second-order subdifferential condition, and to SOQC combined with the tilt stability of the reference local minimizer for (
1.1
). Moreover, under the parabolic regularity of
g
and a certain mild assumption, which holds for many common functions such as the indicator function of the positive semidefinite cone, the nuclear norm function, and the spectral norm functions, we establish the equivalence between the Aubin property and the nonsingularity of the generalized Jacobian associated with the KKT system. These results provide novel and multifaceted perspectives on characterizing Lipschitzian behavior and related well-posedness properties, while opening new avenues for understanding their connections to various stability and regularity conditions in optimization theory and algorithms.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces and discusses fundamental concepts of variational analysis and generalized differentiation, which serve as basic tools for the subsequent developments. In Section
3
, we define the constraint nondegeneracy condition as well as the general SOSC version for composite optimization problems. We also formulate the general version of SSOSC for composite models and establish its relationship with SOSC under the parabolic regularity. Section
4
provides detailed characterizations of the SOQC property under the assumptions of parabolic regularity and
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducibility. Section
5
is devoted to deriving comprehensive equivalent characterizations of the well-posedness properties for the KKT system (
1.3
). Specifically, we establish equivalence of the Aubin property to (i) SOQC combined with the second-order subdifferential condition, to (ii) SOQC combined with the tilt stability, to (iii) the strong regularity, and to (iv) the nonsingularity of the KKT system under fairly mild assumptions. The concluding Section
6
summarizes the main achievements of the paper and discusses some directions of our future research and applications.

Basic Tools of Variational Analysis

In this section, we recall some fundamental tools of variational analysis and generalized differentiation while referring to the monographs
[
4
,
31
,
38
]
for more details.

Consider
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
identified with the epigraph
\operatorname{epi}f:=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\times\mathcal{R}\,|\,f(x)\leq\alpha\}
.
The (Fréchet)
regular subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,f
is defined by

\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}):=\left\{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\left|\,\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\frac{f(x)-f(\bar{x})-\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq 0\right.\right\},

(2.1)

and the (Mordukhovich)
limiting subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is

\partial f(\bar{x}):=\big\{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\;\big|\,\exists\,x_{k}\to\bar{x},\;v_{k}\to v\;\mbox{ with }\;f(x^{k})\rightarrow f(\bar{x}),\;v^{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(x^{k})\big\}.

(2.2)

Given an l.s.c. function
f
and a number
\sigma>0
, the
proximal mapping

\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma f}
corresponding to
f
with parameter
\sigma
is defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma f}(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathop{\arg\min}_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{f(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|u-x\|^{2}\Big\},\quad x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}.

(2.3)

When
f
is convex, it follows from
[
38
, Proposition 13.37]
that (
2.3
) is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{R}^{m}
. The (first-order)
subderivative
of
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
at
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,f
in the direction
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
is defined by

\displaystyle df(\bar{x})(d):=\liminf_{{\tau\downarrow 0}\atop{d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}}\frac{f(\bar{x}+\tau d^{\prime})-f(\bar{x})}{\tau}.

When
df(\bar{x})(d)
is finite, the
parabolic subderivative
of
f
at
\bar{x}
relative to
d
and
w\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
is

\displaystyle d^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,w):=\liminf_{{\tau\downarrow 0}\atop{w^{\prime}\rightarrow w}}\frac{f(\bar{x}+\tau d+\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}w^{\prime})-f(\bar{x})-\tau df(\bar{x})(d)}{\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}}.

(2.4)

We say that
f
is
parabolically epi-differentiable
at
\bar{x}
for
d
if
\operatorname{dom}d^{2}df(\bar{x})(d,\cdot)\neq\emptyset
and for every
w\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
and
\tau_{k}\downarrow 0
, there exists
w^{k}\rightarrow w
such that

\displaystyle d^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,w)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+\tau_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{k}^{2}w^{k})-f(\bar{x})-\tau_{k}df(\bar{x})(d)}{\frac{1}{2}\tau_{k}^{2}}.

Associate with
f
the parametric family of
second-order difference quotients
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tau}^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{u})(d):=\frac{f(\bar{x}+\tau d)-f(\bar{x})-\tau\langle\bar{u},d\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}}\quad\mbox{with}\ d\in\mathcal{R}^{m},\ \tau>0.

The
second-order subderivative
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
for
\bar{u},d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
is defined by

\displaystyle d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{u})(d):=\liminf_{{\tau\downarrow 0}\atop{d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}}\Delta_{\tau}^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{u})(d^{\prime}).

(2.5)

The function
f
is said to be
twice epi-differentiable
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if for every sequence
\tau_{k}\downarrow 0
and every
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
, there exists
d^{k}\rightarrow d
such that

\displaystyle d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{u})(d)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Delta_{\tau_{k}}^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{u})(d^{k}).

The next property plays a crucial role in deriving the main results of this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

It is said that
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
is
parabolically regular
at
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
for
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
in direction
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}

if we have the second-order relationship

\displaystyle\inf_{w\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\left\{d^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,w)-\langle\bar{u},w\rangle\right\}=d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{u})(d).

It has been realized that the parabolic regularity holds for broad classes of extended-real-valued functions; see
[
4
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
38
]
. The following proposition demonstrates that the parabolic regularity of a convex function is inherited by the indicator function of its epigraph.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be an l.s.c. convex function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
, let
\bar{\mu}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, and let
\lambda>0
, Suppose that
f
is parabolically regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{\mu}
and parabolically epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
in every direction
d\in\{d\,|\,df(\bar{x})(d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle\}
for
\bar{\mu}
. Then we have the equality

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1))=\left\{(d,\gamma)\,\left|\,df(\bar{x})(d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle=\gamma\right.\right\}

and deduce therefore that the indicator function
\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}
is parabolically regular at
(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))
in every direction
(d,\gamma)\in\left\{(d,\gamma)\,\left|\,df(\bar{x})(d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle=\gamma\right.\right\}
for
\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1)
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
38
, Theorem 8.9]
that
\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1)\in\partial\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))
. Combining
[
38
, Example 13.62]
,
[
20
, Theorem 3.3]
,
[
38
, Theorem 8.2]
, and
[
4
, Proposition 2.126]
tells us that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1))

\displaystyle=\left\{(d,\gamma)\,\left|\,d\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))(d,\gamma)=\lambda\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle-\lambda\gamma\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(d,\gamma)\,\left|\,df(\bar{x})(d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle=\gamma\right.\right\}.

By the definition of
d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1))
, for any
(d,\gamma)\in\operatorname{dom}d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1))
, there exist sequences
\tau_{k}\downarrow 0
and
(d^{k},\gamma^{k})\rightarrow(d,\gamma)
with
(\bar{x}+\tau_{k}d^{k},f(\bar{x})+\tau_{k}\gamma^{k})\in\operatorname{epi}f
such that

\displaystyle d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1))(d,\gamma)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{\tau_{k}\lambda\langle\bar{\mu},d^{k}\rangle-\tau_{k}\lambda\gamma^{k}}{\frac{1}{2}\tau_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda\frac{f(\bar{x}+\tau_{k}d^{k})-f(\bar{x})-\tau_{k}\langle\bar{\mu},d^{k}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}\tau_{k}^{2}}=\lambda d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d),

where the last equality is derived from the twice epi-differentiability of
f
based on
[
28
, Proposition 3.2]
. Moreover, it follows from
[
20
, Proposition 3.2]
and
[
4
, Proposition 3.41]
that

\displaystyle d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1))(d,\gamma)\leq\inf_{(w,\beta)\in\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),(d,\gamma))}\{\lambda\langle\bar{\mu},w\rangle-\lambda\beta\}=\lambda d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d).

Therefore, we arrive at the representation

\displaystyle d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}((\bar{x},f(\bar{x})),\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1))(d,\gamma)=\inf_{(w,\beta)\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\times\mathcal{R}}\left\{d^{2}\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))((d,\gamma),(w,\beta))-\lambda\langle\bar{\mu},w\rangle+\lambda\beta\right\},

verifying the parabolic regularity of

\delta_{\operatorname{epi}f}
at
(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))
for
\lambda(\bar{\mu},-1)
and completing the proof.

For an open set
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{R}^{m}
, denote by
\mathcal{U}_{F}
the subset of
\mathcal{U}
where the given single-valued mapping
F:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{n}
is Fréchet differentiable.
If
F
is locally Lipschitzian around
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{U}
, the
B
-subdifferential
of
F
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{B}F(\bar{x}):=\left\{A\in\mathcal{R}^{n\times m}\,\left|\,\exists\,x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}\ \mbox{with}\ x^{k}\in\mathcal{U}_{F},\ \nabla F(x^{k})\rightarrow A\right.\right\},

(2.10)

where
\nabla F
stands for the classical Jacobian. The (Clarke)
generalized Jacobian

\mathcal{J}F(\bar{x})
of
F
at
\bar{x}
is the convex hull of
\partial_{B}F(\bar{x})
. The following are simple observations for the inverses

\displaystyle w\in DS^{-1}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(z)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow z\in DS(\bar{x},\bar{u})(w),

(2.11)

\displaystyle y\in D^{*}S^{-1}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(v)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow-v\in D^{*}S(\bar{x},\bar{u})(-y),

(2.12)

and summation rules for the graphical derivatives (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and the limiting coderivative in (
2.9
) of
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{n}
with the
{\cal C}^{1}
-smooth addition
h\colon\mathcal{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle D(h+S)(\bar{x},\bar{u}+h(\bar{x}))(w)

\displaystyle=\nabla h(\bar{x})w+DS(\bar{x},\bar{u})(w),

(2.13)

\displaystyle D^{*}(h+S)(\bar{x},\bar{u}+h(\bar{x})(y)

\displaystyle=\nabla h(\bar{x})^{T}y+D^{*}S(\bar{x},\bar{u})(y),

(2.14)

\displaystyle D_{*}(h+S)(\bar{x},\bar{u}+h(\bar{x}))(w)

\displaystyle=\nabla h(\bar{x})w+D_{*}S(\bar{x},\bar{u})(w),

(2.15)

which are useful in the proofs of our major results developed below.

Recall now several
well-posedness properties
of multifunctions studied in the paper.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{n}
with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}S
and
\bar{u}\in S(\bar{x})
. Then we say that:

(i)

S
is
metrically regular
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if there exists
\kappa\geq 0
along with
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(x,S^{-1}(u))\leq\kappa\operatorname{dist}(u,S(x))\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon),\;u\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{u},\delta).

(ii)

S
is
strongly metrically regular
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if it is metrically regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
and there exist
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that
S^{-1}(u)\cap\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon)
is a singleton for all
u\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{u},\delta)
.

(iii)

S
has the
isolated calmness
property at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if there exist
\kappa\geq 0
and
\varepsilon,\delta>0
with

\displaystyle S(x)\cap\mathbb{B}(\bar{u},\delta)\subset\{\bar{u}\}+\kappa\|x-\bar{x}\|\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{R}^{n}}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon).

(iv)

S
is
Lipschitz-like
or has the
Aubin property
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
with
(\bar{x},\bar{u})\in\operatorname{gph}S
if there exist
\kappa\geq 0
and
\varepsilon,\delta>0
ensuring that

\displaystyle S(x)\cap\mathbb{B}(\bar{u},\delta)\subset S(x^{\prime})+\kappa\|x-x^{\prime}\|\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{R}^{n}}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon).

Relationships between the well-posedness properties in Definition
2.2
have been clearly understood in variational analysis; see, e.g., the books
[
12
,
32
,
38
]
and the references therein.

The following three lemmas present generalized differential characterizations of the Lipschitz-like/Aubin, isolated calmness, and strong metric regularity properties that are used in this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let the graph of
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{n}
be closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{u})\in{\rm gph}\,S
. Then
S
is Lipschitz-like at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if and only if we have the condition
D^{*}S(\bar{x},\bar{u})(0)=\{0\}
via the limiting coderivative from (
2.9
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let the graph of
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{n}
be closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{u})\in{\rm gph}\,S
. Then
S
enjoys the isolated calmness property at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if and only if we have the condition
DS(\bar{x},\bar{u})(0)=\{0\}
in terms of the graphical derivative from (
2.7
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let the graph of
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{n}
be closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{u})\in{\rm gph}\,S
. Then
S
is strongly metrically regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if and only if
D^{*}S(\bar{x},\bar{u})(0)=\{0\}
and
D_{*}S(\bar{x},\bar{u})(0)=\{0\}
in terms of the strict graphical derivative from (
2.8
).

Lemma

; see
[
30
, Theorem 5.7]
and
[

, Theorem 9.40]
. Lemma

is called the

; see
[
12
, Theorem 4E.1]
and the references therein. Lemma
2.3
is taken from
[
13
, Theorem 2.7]
. This criterion has been recently enhanced in
[
2
, Theorem 4.2]
in the context where the multifunction
S
is the solution mapping

associated with the KKT system in (

).

To this end, it is worth mentioning that the limiting coderivative in Lemma
2.1
, along with the limiting subdifferential (
2.2
) and normal cone in (

), enjoys

. This is not the case for the graphical derivative (

) in Lemma

and its strict counterpart (

) in Lemma

,
as well as the regular subdifferential (

3
Qualification and Second-Order Variational Conditions

In this section, we define and investigate basic constraint qualification, nondegeneracy, and second-order variational conditions important for deriving our main well-posedness characterization in what follows. Recall first that the
Robinson constraint qualification
(RCQ) holds at a feasible point
\bar{x}
of the composite optimization problem (
1.1
) if

\displaystyle\nabla F(\bar{x})\mathcal{R}^{n}+\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{dom}g}(F(\bar{x}))=\mathcal{R}^{m}.

(3.1)

Fixed
x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}
, define the
Lagrange multiplier mapping

\Lambda
associated with
x
by

\displaystyle\Lambda(x,a,b):=\big\{u\,\big|\,(a,b)\in\Phi(x,u)\big\}\ \mbox{ for }\ (a,b)\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{R}^{m}.

(3.2)

It follows from
[
4
, Theorem 3.9 and Proposition 3.17]
that the Lagrange multiplier set
\Lambda(x,0,0)
is nonempty, convex, and compact if and only if the RCQ holds at
x
.
If
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of problem (
1.1
) and the RCQ is satisfied at
\bar{x}
, then there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
such that
(\bar{x},\bar{u})\in S_{\operatorname{KKT}}(0,0)
. It is said that the
strong Robinson constraint qualification
(SRCQ) is satisfied at
\bar{x}
with respect to
\bar{u}
if we have

\displaystyle\nabla F(\bar{x})\mathcal{R}^{n}+\big\{d\,\big|\,dg(F(\bar{x}))(d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\big\}=\mathcal{R}^{m}.

(3.3)

The
constraint nondegeneracy
condition for problem (
1.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\nabla F(\bar{x})\mathcal{R}^{n}+\operatorname{lin}\big\{d\,\big|\,dg(F(\bar{x}))(d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\big\}=\mathcal{R}^{m}.

(3.4)

Define further the
critical cone
associated with problem (
1.1
) at
\bar{x}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=\big\{d\,\big|\,\nabla h(\bar{x})^{T}d+dg(F(\bar{x}))(\nabla F(\bar{x})d)=0\big\}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\big\{d\,\big|\,dg(F(\bar{x}))(\nabla F(\bar{x})d)=\langle\nabla F(\bar{x})d,u\rangle\big\},

where
u\in\Lambda(\bar{x},0,0)
.
The
second-order sufficient condition
(SOSC)
is said to hold for problem (
1.1
) at
\bar{x}
if for all
d\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\Lambda(\bar{x},0,0)}\big\{\langle\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},u)d,d\rangle+\inf_{w\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\{d^{2}g(F(\bar{x}))(\nabla F(\bar{x})d,w)-\langle u,w\}\big\}>0.

(3.6)

Given now an l.s.c. function
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
with
x\in\operatorname{dom}f
and given its limiting subgradient
\mu\in\partial f(x)
, define the
second-order variational function

\displaystyle\Gamma_{f}(x,\mu)(v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{{d\in\mathcal{R}^{m},\,v=Ud}\atop{U\in\mathcal{J}\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(x+\mu)}}\langle v,d-v\rangle,&v\in\bigcup\limits_{U\in\mathcal{J}\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(x+\mu)}\operatorname{rge}U,\\
\infty,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

by using the generalized Jacobian of the proximal mapping (
2.3
). Following

[
41
]
, consider the generalized version of the strong second-order sufficient condition for the composite optimization problem (
1.1
) that extends the classical one for NLPs introduced in
[
37
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\bar{x}
be an optimal solution to problem (
1.1
). We say that the
strong second-order sufficient condition

(SSOSC)
holds for (
1.1
) at
\bar{x}
if

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\Lambda(\bar{x},0,0)}\left\{\left\langle\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},u)d,d\right\rangle+\Gamma_{g}(F(\bar{x}),u)(\nabla F(\bar{x})d)\right\}>0\;\mbox{ whenever }\;d\neq 0.

(3.9)

Next we formulate the two major assumptions systematically used in the paper.

(A1)
Let
f
be an l.s.c. function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
. Given any
x
in some neighborhood of
\bar{x}
and any subgradient
\mu\in\partial f(x)
, the function
f
is parabolically regular at
x
for
\mu
and parabolically epi-differentiable at
x
for every
z\in\left\{d\,\left|\,df(x)(d)=\langle\mu,d\rangle\right.\right\}
.

(A2)
Let
f
be an l.s.c. function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
and
\bar{\mu}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
. There exists
\overline{W}\in\partial_{B}\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(\bar{x}+\bar{\mu})
from the
B
-subdifferential (
2.10
) of the proximal mapping (
2.3
) such that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal{J}\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(\bar{x}+\bar{\mu})}\operatorname{rge}V=\operatorname{rge}\overline{W}

and that for any
y\in\operatorname{rge}\overline{W}
, we have
y=\overline{W}\bar{d}
, where
\bar{d}
satisfies the equalities

\displaystyle\min\limits_{{d\in\mathcal{R}^{m},y=Vd}\atop{V\in{\mathcal{J}\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(\bar{x}+\bar{\mu})}}}\langle y,d-y\rangle=\langle y,\bar{d}-y\rangle=\big\langle y,(\overline{W}^{\dagger}-I)y\big\rangle.

Assumption (A1) is not restrictive at all, while (A2) holds for many broad classes of functions that frequently appear in composite optimization; see below. In practical numerical implementations, the structures involved in (A2) are often computed as a part of the solution process.

Definition 3.2
.

(i)

Let
C\subset\mathcal{R}^{m}
and
K\subset\mathcal{R}^{s}
be convex closed sets. We say that
C
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-reducible
to
K
, at a point
x\in C
, if there exist a neighborhood
N
at
x
and a
{\cal C}^{2}
-smooth mapping
\Xi:N\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{s}
such that

\nabla\Xi(x):\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{s}
is surjective and
C\cap N=\{x\in N\,|\,\Xi(x)\in K\}
.We say that the reduction is pointed if the tangent cone
\mathcal{T}_{K}(\Xi(x))
is pointed. If in addition the set
K-\Xi(x)
is a pointed closed convex cone, we say that
C
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible
at
x
. We can assume without loss of generality that
\Xi(x)=0
.

(ii)
An l.s.c. convex function
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible
at
x\in{\rm dom}\,f
if the associated epigraphical set
\operatorname{epi}f
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
(x,f(x))
. Furthermore,
f
is said to be
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible if it is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at every point
x\in\operatorname{dom}f
.

It is shown in
[
41
, Proposition 3.1]
that
every
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible function
satisfies assumption (A1). Furthermore, it is proved in Appendices of
[
27
,
28
]
that assumption (A2) holds for the indicator function of the
positive semidefinite cone
, the
nuclear norm
function, and the
spectral norm
function, which all are important in applications.

Before establishing the main result of this section about the calculation of the second-order variational function (
3
), we present two important lemmas of their own interest.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be an l.s.c. convex function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
and
\bar{\mu}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
under the fulfillment of assumption
(A1)
at
\bar{x}
, and let
d\in\operatorname{dom}d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})
. For any sequences
(x^{k},\mu^{k})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})
with
\mu^{k}\in\partial f(x^{k})
and
d^{k}\rightarrow d
with
d^{k}\in\operatorname{dom}d^{2}f(x^{k},\mu^{k})
, there exist subsequences
\{(x^{k_{\nu}},\mu^{k_{\nu}})\}
with
\mu^{k_{\nu}}\in\partial f(x^{k_{\nu}})
and
\{d^{k_{\nu}}\}
with
d^{k_{\nu}}\in\operatorname{dom}d^{2}f(x^{k_{\nu}},\mu^{k_{\nu}})
such that the parabolic subderivative (
2.4
) has the limiting property

\displaystyle\lim_{k_{\nu}\rightarrow\infty}d^{2}f(x^{k_{\nu}},\mu^{k_{\nu}})(d^{k_{\nu}})=d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d).

Proof.

Since
d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})
is positive homogeneous of degree 2, we only need to prove the result when
\|d\|\leq 1
. It follows from
[
38
, Proposition 13.20 and Theorem 13.40]
and

[
28
, Proposition 3.2]
that
d^{2}f(x^{k},\mu^{k})
is convex and
D(\partial f)(x^{k},\mu^{k})(d^{k})=\partial\left(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(x^{k},\mu^{k})\right)(d^{k})
is bounded. Picking
v^{k}\in D(\partial f)(x^{k},\mu^{k})(d^{k})
, we get by (
2.11
) and (
2.13
) that
d^{k}\in D\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(x^{k}+\mu^{k})(v^{k}+d^{k})=\{\operatorname{Prox}_{\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(x^{k},\mu^{k})}(v^{k}+d^{k})\}
. Then
\operatorname{Prox}_{f}
is directionally differentiable at
x^{k}+\mu^{k}
in the direction
v^{k}+d^{k}
and there exists
\tau_{t}\downarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(x^{k}+\mu^{k}+\tau_{t}(v^{k}+d^{k}))=\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(x^{k}+\mu^{k})+\tau_{t}d^{k}+o(\tau_{t})=x^{k}+\tau_{t}d^{k}+o(\tau_{t}).

Select subsequences
\{(x^{k_{t}},\mu^{k_{t}})\}
satisfying
\|(x^{k_{t}},\mu^{k_{t}})-(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})\|\leq\tau^{2}_{t}
,
\{d^{k_{t}}\}
with
d^{k_{t}}\in\operatorname{dom}d^{2}f(x^{k_{t}},\mu^{k_{t}})
, and
\{v^{k_{t}}\}
with
v^{k_{t}}\in D(\partial f)(x^{k_{t}},\mu^{k_{t}})(d^{k_{t}})
.
Since

\displaystyle x^{k_{t}}+\mu^{k_{t}}+\tau_{t}(v^{k_{t}}+d^{k_{t}})=\bar{x}+\bar{\mu}+\tau_{t}\hat{d}^{k_{t}},\quad\hat{d}^{k_{t}}=v^{k_{t}}+d^{k_{t}}+\frac{x^{k_{t}}+\mu^{k_{t}}-\bar{x}-\bar{\mu}}{\tau_{t}},

and
\{v^{k_{t}}\}
is bounded, choose subsequences
\{v^{k_{t_{j}}}\}
with
v^{k_{t_{j}}}\rightarrow\bar{v}
and
\hat{d}^{k_{t_{j}}}\rightarrow\bar{v}+d
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(\bar{x}+\bar{\mu}+\tau_{t_{j}}\hat{d}^{k_{t_{j}}})=\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(x^{k_{t_{j}}}+\mu^{k_{t_{j}}}+\tau_{t_{j}}(v^{k_{t_{j}}}+d^{k_{t_{j}}}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(x^{k_{t_{j}}}+\mu^{k_{t_{j}}})+\tau_{t_{j}}d^{k_{t_{j}}}+o(\tau_{t_{j}})=x^{k_{t_{j}}}+\tau_{t_{j}}d^{k_{t_{j}}}+o(\tau_{t_{j}})

\displaystyle=\bar{x}+\tau_{t_{j}}\hat{d}+o(\tau_{t_{j}})=\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(\bar{x}+\bar{\mu})+\tau_{t_{j}}\hat{d}+o(\tau_{t_{j}}),\quad\hat{d}=d^{k_{t_{j}}}+\frac{x^{k_{t_{j}}}-\bar{x}}{\tau_{t_{j}}}\rightarrow d.

Letting
k_{t_{j}}\rightarrow\infty
above, it follows from the directional differentiability of
\operatorname{Prox}_{f}
at
\bar{x}+\bar{\mu}
in the direction
\bar{v}+d
that
D\operatorname{Prox}_{f}(\bar{x}+\bar{\mu})(\bar{v}+d)=\{d\}
. Combining (
2.11
) and (
2.13
), we get
\bar{v}\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)
. Applying finally

[
41
, Proposition 3.4]
yields

\displaystyle d^{2}f(x^{k_{t_{j}}},\mu^{k_{t_{j}}})(d^{k_{t_{j}}})=\langle v^{k_{t_{j}}},d^{k_{t_{j}}}\rangle\rightarrow\langle\bar{v},d\rangle=d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu}),

which therefore completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The next lemma reveals a fundamental relationship between the second subderivative (

) of an extended-real-valued convex function and its

introduced in
[

]
as the limiting coderivative in (

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be an l.s.c. convex function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
and
\bar{\mu}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
under the fulfillment of assumption
(A1)
at
\bar{x}
. Then we have the equality

\displaystyle\min_{v\in D^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)}\langle v,d\rangle=d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)\;\mbox{ for all }\;d\in{\cal R}^{m}\;\mbox{ with }\;df(\bar{x})(d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle.

(3.10)

Proof.

It follows from assumption (A1) and
[
27
, Lemma 5.2]
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dom}D(\partial f)(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})=\{d\,|\,df(\bar{x})(d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle\}.

Applying
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
,
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
, and
[
38
, Theorem 13.57]
tells us that

is twice epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{\mu}
and the inequality “
\leq
” holds in (

[

the existence of sequences
(x^{k},\mu^{k})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})
with
\mu^{k}\in\partial f(x^{k})
and

\displaystyle\min_{v\in D^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)}\langle v,d\rangle\geq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}d^{2}f(x^{k},\mu^{k})(d^{k}).

(3.11)

gives us subsequences

Now we show that the second-order variational function (

) associated with

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be an l.s.c. convex function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}f
and
\bar{\mu}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, and let both assumptions
(A1)
and

hold at
\bar{x}
for

. Then we have the identity

\displaystyle\Gamma_{f}(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)=d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)\;\mbox{ for all }\;d\in\operatorname{dom}d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu}).

(3.12)

Proof.

It follows from
[

, Theorem 3.8]
and
[
28
, Proposition 3.2]

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}\Gamma_{f}(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})=\operatorname{aff}\operatorname{dom}d^{2}f(\bar{x},\bar{\mu}).

Furthermore,
[

, Proposition 3.3]
tells that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{f}(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)=\min_{v\in D^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})(d)}\left\langle v,d\right\rangle\;\mbox{ whenever }\;d\in\operatorname{dom}\Gamma_{f}(\bar{x},\bar{\mu}).

Consequently, applying Lemma
3.2
brings us to (

∎

6
Concluding Remarks and Future Research

This paper establishes
equivalent relationships
between various
well-posedness
properties of general composite optimization problems and associated KKT systems with providing comprehensive
second-order characterizations
of these properties via pointbased constructions of
second-order generalized differentiation
. In particular, we resolve at the alternative a long-standing question about the equivalence between the
Aubin property
and
strong regularity
of solution mappings to KKT systems, as well as the
tilt stability
of local minimizers, in the
{\cal C}^{2}
-cone reducible
setting of composite optimization with
parabolically regular
data. Such an equivalence has been established earlier for important special classes of problems in constrained optimization. We also show, among other results, that these properties and their equivalents are completely characterized by the simultaneous fulfillment of certain
constraint nondegeneracy
and
robust second-order subdifferential conditions
expressed
precisely
at the reference solution and satisfying
comprehensive calculus
rules. The obtained results are of a fundamental nature, while being instrumental for
stability
and
sensitivity analysis
of optimization-related problems, as well as for the design and justification of
numerical algorithms
in constrained optimization.

Our future research will be conducted in various directions. Among them, we plan to reveal—besides the broad settings discussed above—other classes of composite optimization problems satisfying assumption (A2) with
explicit computations
of the second-order variational function
\Gamma_{f}
. Recalling that the strong regularity, Lipschitzian stability of KKT systems, and tilt stability of local minimizers play a crucial role in justifying numerical algorithms of constrained optimization, our attention will be paid to the implementation of these ideas and the obtained results to
new classes
of composite optimization problems. Moreover, we intend to investigate some of the well-posedness properties studied in this paper
without imposing nondegeneracy
conditions.

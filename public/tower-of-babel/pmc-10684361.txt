Introduction

Cervical esophageal cancer is a rare neoplasm, accounting for less than 5 % of all esophageal malignancies [1]. Primary adenocarcinoma of the cervical esophagus is an uncommon form that occurs in the upper portion of the esophagus. Usually, adenocarcinoma arises from Barrett esophagus. Adenocarcinoma arising from non-Barrett's epithelium origins are extremely rare.

We present here an extremely rare case of cervical esophageal adenocarcinoma in a 65-year-old female with no evidence of Barrett's esophagus. The work has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria [2].

Case report

A 65-year-old female patient with a history of left breast cancer treated in 1985 with surgery, adjuvant chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, presented with chronic mixed (to both solids and liquids) high dysphagia and food impaction since September 2022. She had no history of alcohol consumption or smoking and was receiving treatment for dyslipidemia and diabetes mellitus. She had no relevant family history of gastrointestinal malignancies.

On physical examination, a right cervical superficial lymph node was palpable and measured 3.5*3 cm. Her height was 154 cm, her weight was 62 kg. Otherwise, physical examination was normal.

Thoracic CT scan revealed a circumferential and budding tumoral process involving the oropharynx and cervical esophagus, starting at the level of C5, extending 85 mm in the sagittal plane up to the level of D3, with a thickness of 18 mm. The tumor infiltrated the left lobe of the thyroid gland and displaced the trachea anteriorly, contacting the left jugulo-carotid axis. Abdomino-pelvic CT scan revealed the absence of distant metastases.

The tumor was classified as T4N1M0 according to the 8th edition of the TNM classification for esophageal and esophagogastric junction (EGJ) cancer.

Radiotherapy was contraindicated due to the patient's history of supraclavicular irradiation.

The patient was treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy: FOLFOX regimen (fluorouracil, leucovorin calcium, oxaliplatin) and was scheduled for potential surgery. We noticed a clinical benefit after 3 cycles of chemotherapy. She has no longer any food impaction. Unfortunately, surgery was not performed because of a locally advanced disease on CT scan follow up despite neoadjuvant chemotherapy.

Discussion

Cancers of the upper esophagus are rare, accounting for less than 5 % of all esophageal cancers. The survival of patients with cervical esophageal carcinoma (CEC) remains poor [1].

Primary adenocarcinoma of the upper esophagus is an even rarer situation. In fact, esophageal adenocarcinomas typically arise in the lower third of the esophagus. Only 31 cases so far have been reported. We report here the 32nd case. The exact origin of primary adenocarcinoma of the cervical esophagus is unknown. However, heterotopic gastric mucosa (HGM) in the upper esophagus is considered as risk factor [3,4]. The exact mechanism leading to the development of adenocarcinoma from HGM is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve, genetic mutations, chronic inflammation and the production of carcinogenic compounds by the gastric mucosa.

Cervical Barrett's esophagus is also reported as a potential risk factors for the development of primary adenocarcinoma of the cervical esophagus [5,6].

It is plausible that our patient's tumor arose from HGM, as there was no indication of Barrett's esophagus based on both endoscopy and pathologic examination of the specimen of the surrounding mucosa.

The symptoms of primary adenocarcinoma of the upper esophagus are similar to those of other esophageal cancers. The most common symptoms include dysphagia, chest pain, weight loss, and hoarseness. In some cases, and at early stage, patients may have no symptoms.

The diagnosis of primary adenocarcinoma of the upper esophagus is based on a combination of clinical presentation, imaging studies, and histopathological examination. Upper endoscopy is the gold standard for the diagnosis of esophageal cancer.

For the moment, no standard therapy exists for primary cervical esophageal adenocarcinoma.

Treatment strategies found in the few case reports include endoscopic mucosal resection for superficial lesions. For advanced tumors, laryngo-pharyngo-esophagectomy with lymph node dissection was described. Surgical treatment was associated with neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemoradiotherapy in some cases [7,8].

In our case, the patient couldn't be treated with radiation therapy due to the history of supra clavicular irradiation.

Conclusion

Primary adenocarcinoma of the upper esophagus is a rare form of esophageal cancer. Heterotopic gastric mucosa in the cervical esophagus is considered as a precursor lesion to this tumor. The diagnosis is based on a combination of clinical presentation, imaging studies, and confirmed by histopathological examination. Treatment of primary adenocarcinoma of the upper esophagus is not consensual and is really challenging.

Multimodal treatment approaches have shown promising results. We recommend multidisciplinary approach in patient with cervical adenocarcinoma.

Association between colorectal cancer stem cells' expression of LGR5 and the location of colorectal cancer
Authors: Imelda Rey, Rustam Effendi-YS
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim:The aim of this study is to assess the association between LGR5 expression, a cancer stem cell marker, and the location of colorectal cancer (CRC).Background:The incidence of chemotherapy-resistant CRC is increasing. The location of CRC is important in determining CRC progression. The area itself is associated with the presence of cancer stem cells. Methods: This cross-sectional study was conducted in Adam Malik Hospital and its sister hospitals. Inclusion criteria were patients who underwent colonoscopy, biopsy, or operation due to CRC, Indonesian, and willing to provide familial medical history regarding CRC and other malignancies up to third-degree relatives. CRC tissue was obtained from each subject as a specimen and underwent immunohistochemistry examination to determine LGR5 expression. Data analysis was performed by SPSS, version 26 (Chicago, 2022). Results:A total of 118 subjects were involved and divided into proximal (32 subjects), distal (43 subjects), and rectal (43 subjects) CRC. Mean age of subjects was 57.30 (SD 12.99) years with 69 (58.5%) subjects were males. The percentage of high-level LGR5 expression was found in subjects with proximal CRC (72%), followed by distal (44.1%) and rectal (39.5%) CRC. There was a significant association between LGR5 expression and the location of CRC (p=0.014). No relationship was observed between LGR5 expression with gender, age, body mass index, hemoglobin level, leukocyte count, platelet count, and histopathology grade of CRC.Conclusion: LGR5 expression is associated with CRC. Higher LGR5 expression is observed in proximal CRC.Key Words: Cancer, Colorectal, Location, LGR5

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is ranked the third most common malignancy worldwide and the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality in the United States (1-4). It is more to females (2, 5). The incidence rate in Indonesia was reported at 15.2 for males and 10.2 for females. Approximately 63,500 cases of CRC are reported every year (6, 7). About 70% of CRC is located in the colon, while the rest is in the rectum (8, 9) contrast with previous literature, in Dr. Pirngadi General Hospital Medan, 74.6% of CRC cases were located in the rectum, and the rest were in the colon (10). In Haji Adam Malik General Hospital Medan, 61.5% CRC cases were located in the rectum, 23.1% were in the left colon, and the rest were in the right colon (11).

Both left and right CRC possess different clinical and pathological characteristics (12-16). There are differences in molecular marker of proximal, distal, and rectal CRC (17). The proportion of proximal CRC cases with negative expression of Adenomatous Polyposis Coli (APC) or Chromosome Instability (CIN) is lower compared to distal CRC. On the other hand, the proportion of negative Mismatch Repair (MMR) and Microsatellite Instability High (MSI-H) is higher in proximal CRC. As a result, 85% of proximal CRC progress via the CIN pathway while the other 15% via the MSI pathway (18).

The incidence of chemotherapy-resistant colorectal cancer (CRC) and recurrence is increasing, largely attributed to the role of cancer stem cells that drive tumor initiation, resistance, metastasis, and relapse (19). Among the markers of cancer stem cells, leucine-rich repeat-containing G-protein-coupled receptor 5 (LGR5), also known as GPR49, has been identified as a critical regulator in the Wnt signaling pathway. Previous studies have demonstrated that LGR5 is frequently overexpressed in CRC and is associated with deeper invasion, lymph node involvement, distant metastasis, and poor overall survival (20, 21). A meta-analysis further confirmed that high LGR5 expression nearly doubles the risk of poor prognosis in CRC patients (22). Conversely, studies on LGR5 promoter methylation showed that reduced expression was correlated with higher tumor grade but paradoxically with better prognosis, suggesting a context-dependent role (23). Functional studies also confirmed that LGR5 marks a stem-like subpopulation of CRC cells and may influence tumor architecture through adhesion pathways (24).

Although these findings establish the prognostic and biological importance of LGR5, the relationship between LGR5 expression and the anatomical location of CRC remains poorly understood. Tumor location itself is a recognized prognostic factor, yet there is limited evidence directly addressing whether LGR5 expression in cancer stem cells varies between proximal, distal, and rectal tumors. This gap underlies the present study, which aims to determine the association between LGR5 expression and tumor location in CRC. A clearer understanding of this relationship may improve diagnosis, management, and patient outcomes.

Methods

This cross-sectional study was conducted at Haji Adam Malik General Hospital and its affiliated centers. Consecutive sampling was employed, whereby all eligible patients presenting during the study period were enrolled sequentially. Patients with a histologically confirmed diagnosis of colorectal cancer (CRC) who underwent colonoscopy, biopsy, or surgical resection were recruited from both inpatient and outpatient units. Because this method reflects the natural distribution of cases presenting to the hospital, the number of subjects within the proximal, distal, and rectal CRC groups was not uniform. No randomization was applied, and no loss to follow-up occurred, as all specimens were obtained and analyzed at the time of diagnosis or treatment.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) Indonesian nationality, (2) confirmed CRC diagnosis, and (3) willingness to provide a family medical history of CRC and other malignancies up to third-degree relatives. Exclusion criteria were: (1) refusal to participate, (2) family history of CRC or other malignancies, (3) insufficient tissue for histopathological examination, and (4) histopathological results indicating non-malignancy, metastasized malignancy, or malignancies other than CRC.

Subjects were divided into three groups based on CRC location: proximal, distal, and rectal. CRC tissue was obtained by colonoscopy, biopsy, or surgery as a specimen. A specimen from each subject was examined using the immunohistochemistry (IHC) method to determine the expression of LGR5. Anti-GPCR GPR49 antibody (rabbit polyclonal to GPCR GPR49) was used in this method. The results were presented as population, intensity, and total scores. Population score was grouped as follows: 0: 0% of cells were stained, 1: less than 10% cells were stained, 2: 11-50% cells were stained, 3: more than 50% of cells were stained. Previous literature stated that LGR5 expression is considered positive if 10% or more cells are stained (22). Intensity score was grouped as follows: 0: no expression, 1: weak expression, 2: medium expression, 3: strong expression (Figure 1 and 2). The total score was the sum of the population and intensity scores and was grouped as follows: 0-3: not overexpression (low level); 4-6: overexpression (high level). The examination was conducted in the Department of Pathology of Adam Malik Hospital by a pathologist to minimize bias.

Data was analyzed and presented in tables. Chi-Square test was utilized to determine the association between categorical variables. An independent t-test was used to assess the relationship between normally distributed numerical variables. Otherwise, Mann-Whitney and Kruskal-Wallis tests were utilized. Statistical analysis was conducted using statistical software at a 95% confidence interval. A p-value of <0.05 was considered significant. All procedures in this study followed the Helsinki Declaration and were approved by the Ethical Committee of the Medical Faculty of Universitas Sumatera Utara.

Results

A total of 118 patients with histologically confirmed colorectal cancer (CRC) were enrolled in this study, comprising 69 males (58.5%) and 49 females (41.5%), with a mean age of 57.3 ± 13.0 years. The mean hemoglobin concentration was 10.5 ± 2.1 g/dL, while the median leukocyte and thrombocyte counts were 7,420 cells/mm³ (range 1,609–34,180) and 286,350 cells/mm³ (range 89,000–562,000), respectively. The median body mass index (BMI) was 22.2 kg/m² (range 11.2–30.9). Regarding tumor site, 32 cases (27.1%) were located in the proximal colon, 43 (36.4%) in the distal colon, and 43 (36.4%) in the rectum. Histologically, well-differentiated adenocarcinoma was most frequent (46.6%), followed by moderately differentiated (40.7%) and poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma (12.7%). LGR5 expression was evenly distributed, with 59 patients (50.0%) exhibiting high expression and 59 (50.0%) low expression (Table 1).

Representative immunohistochemical staining of LGR5 is shown in Figures 1 and 2. Weak expression was observed as light brown cytoplasmic staining in a limited number of glandular epithelial cells (Figure 1), whereas strong expression was characterized by diffuse and intense dark brown staining across tumour glands (Figure 2), both at 400× magnification. Discussion

This study examined the relationship between LGR5 expression and clinicopathological characteristics of colorectal cancer (CRC), with particular emphasis on tumour location. We found that high LGR5 expression was significantly more prevalent in proximal tumours, whereas no significant associations were observed with demographic variables, hematological parameters, or histopathological grade.

No significant association was observed between LGR5 expression and age, gender, or body mass index. This is consistent with Saigusa et al., who reported no gender-related difference in LGR5 expression among CRC patients (25). The lack of age association may reflect variability in aging-related epigenetic processes such as hypermethylation (26). Additional modifiers, including sex hormones (27), environmental factors, and lifestyle exposures (28,29), may contribute to CRC development without directly influencing LGR5 expression.

LGR5 expression did not correlate with hemoglobin level, leukocyte count, or platelet count. Although higher hemoglobin levels are generally associated with improved chemotherapy response and better prognosis (30), and anemia promotes hypoxia-induced tumour progression through HIF-1 and VEGF signaling (31), our results suggest that LGR5 expression operates independently of these hematological parameters.

We found no significant relationship between LGR5 expression and histopathological grade. This contrasts with Saigusa et al., who reported increased LGR5 expression in less differentiated tumours (25), but aligns with findings by Kazama et al., who also found no significant association (32). One explanation is that LGR5 expression is upregulated early in carcinogenesis and reflects stemness potential rather than morphological grade. Supporting this, loss of p53 function has been linked to increased LGR5 expression during early CRC development (33). Further research is needed to clarify whether LGR5 expression influences progression beyond tumour initiation.

The most important observation of our study is the significant association between LGR5 expression and tumour location. Proximal tumours demonstrated higher expression than distal and rectal tumours, suggesting that stemness features may be more prominent in right-sided CRC. These biological differences could underlie the heterogeneity, therapeutic resistance, and poorer prognosis often associated with proximal disease.

Previous studies highlight the prognostic relevance of LGR5. Jiang et al. showed that high LGR5 expression nearly doubles the risk of poor outcomes in CRC (35). Conversely, promoter methylation of LGR5 has been associated with improved prognosis, reflecting its complex role in tumour biology (22). Importantly, survival data from Aoyama et al. demonstrated that right-sided CRC is associated with higher mortality than left-sided CRC, even after curative resection (36). Our findings that proximal tumours show higher LGR5 expression may help explain these outcome disparities and reinforce the prognostic value of tumour location (34).

Collectively, these results support LGR5 as a potential biomarker for CRC prognosis and treatment stratification. Its preferential expression in proximal tumours suggests that patients with right-sided CRC may particularly benefit from intensified therapeutic strategies. Given the association between LGR5 and stemness, further exploration of LGR5-targeted approaches could offer new therapeutic avenues.

Future studies should confirm these findings in larger, multicentre cohorts with integrated molecular profiling, including mismatch repair status, APC mutations, and microsatellite instability. Mechanistic investigations are also needed to elucidate how LGR5 influences stemness, resistance, and metastasis.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates a statistically significant association between LGR5 expression and colorectal cancer location, with higher expression observed in proximal tumors. These findings indicate that LGR5 may underlie the distinct biological behavior of proximal CRC, including its greater therapeutic resistance and poorer prognosis. Future research should validate these results in larger multicenter cohorts and investigate LGR5 as a predictive biomarker for treatment response. Mechanistic studies are needed to clarify the role of LGR5 in chemotherapy resistance, tumor stemness, and metastasis, while exploration of LGR5-targeted therapies may offer novel treatment strategies. Integrating LGR5 with established molecular markers such as MSI, APC, or p53 could further refine prognostic models and support personalized management of colorectal cancer.

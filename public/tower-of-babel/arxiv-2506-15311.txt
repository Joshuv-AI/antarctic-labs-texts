Charged Scalar Field at Future Null Infinity via Nonlinear Hyperboloidal Evolution
João D. Álvares, Alex Vaño-Viñuales

Abstract
Quasinormal modes and power-law late-time decay tails of a charged scalar field in a charged black hole background have been studied, but never in the fully non-linear regime, as far as we know. In this paper, we study the dependence of these properties on the charges of scalar field and black hole. For the quasinormal modes, a fit of the spherical fundamental mode is shown for the purely uncharged case and compared to the charged one. We also see for the first time the transition from gravitational decay to pure electromagnetic decay, and show disagreement with the oscillation frequency between real and imaginary parts of the scalar field prescribed in the literature. Full non-linear evolutions of hyperboloidal slices in spherical symmetry were used to obtain our results, allowing for the extraction of signals at future null infinity.



I
Introduction

Valuable information about compact objects and their induced spacetime deformations can be retrieved by studying the behaviour of the system after dynamical perturbations. In general relativity, such relaxation takes the form of quasi-normal mode (QNM) ringing
Kokkotas and Schmidt 1999
;
Konoplya and Zhidenko 2011
;
Berti et al. 2009
;
Berti et al. 2025
, followed by power-law decay tails
Price
;
Gundlach et al. 1994a
;
De Amicis et al. 2024
. Both propagate accross the whole spacetime until infinity, where they are unambiguiously characterized. These effects are variations around a stationary state achieved in the long term, which makes perturbation theory a suitable tool to gain insights into them. A very relevant question concerns the regime of validity of the description by the linear theory. Taking as example the ringdown of a coalescence of compact objects, when after the non-linear merger is the perturbative approach an appropriate description? Only through comparisons between the full non-linear case and linear approximations can the faithfullness of the latter be judged. In this work in spherical symmetry we numerically evolve scalar perturbations of the Reissner-Nortström (RN) spacetime using the coupled general relativity, Maxwell and complex massless Klein-Gordon systems in the full non-perturbative regime reaching null infinity and compare them to prior estimates in the literature.

The seminal work
Price
motivated a still-growing interest in QNMs and power-law decay tails. For the case of a real scalar field, the power-law decay comes from the backscattering of outgoing waves of the scalar field at late-times, due to the effective potential created by the presence of a black hole. Being
l
the mode of the scalar field
\phi
, in the limit of timelike infinity the expectation is that

\phi\propto t^{-2l-2}\,.

(1)

Shortly afterwards, Ref.
Bičák 1972
extended the late-time power-laws to the charged scalar field collapsing into a RN black hole. The same power-law as (
1
) was predicted, but with the signal dominated by slower decaying primary waves.

About two decades later, Ref.
Gundlach et al. 1994a
studied massless scalar field perturbations in fixed Schwarzschild and RN spacetimes, but this time also focusing on future null infinity -- the collection of the end-points of future-directed null geodesics. Not long after, Hod and Piran

qQ

(2)

at timelike infinity. The quantity
\beta

\beta=\frac{-1+\sqrt{(2l+1)^{2}-4(qQ)^{2}}}{2}\,.

(3)

(4)

The difference between (

More recently, Ref.
Konoplya and Zhidenko 2013
generalized for the massive charged scalar field in the Kerr-Newman background, but their analysis for the massless charged scalar field is inconclusive. Although the RN spacetime, which we consider here, does not include the presence of angular momentum, it can be used as a toy-model of the Kerr or Kerr-Newman black holes, as it includes the possibility of extremal behaviour when its charge
Q
equals its mass

The late-decay of the power-law tails can be evaluated along a timelike curve asymptoting to future timelike infinity
i^{+}
and along future null infinity
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
. The former is out of reach for traditional numerical simulations, where the system would need to be evolved forever to reach it. Exceptions are implementations that use a compactification in the time coordinate such as Ref.
Camden et al. 2025

;
Ma et al. 2023
;
Ma et al. 2024

;
Friedrich 1983
;
Hübner 1999
;
Frauendiener 1998
. The approach taken in this work is free evolution of the Einstein equations using the BSSN/Z4 formalism
Nakamura et al. 1987
;
Shibata and Nakamura 1995
;
Baumgarte and Shapiro 1999
;
Bona et al. 2003

Álvares and Vaño-Viñuales 2025

In this paper we present evolutions of a charged scalar field with a charged black hole at the center of the spacetime, going from the purely neutral case (

M=Q

II
System Setup

A detailed presentation of the setup is given in Ref.
Álvares and Vaño-Viñuales 2025
. Here we provide a brief summary.
The system to be considered is described by the following action
López and Alcubierre 2023
;
Jaramillo et al. 2024
,

S=\int\left(R-8\pi\left[(\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi)^{*}(\mathcal{D}^{\mu}\phi)\right]-F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\right)\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x\;,

(5)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar,
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}+iqA_{\mu}
is the gauge invariant covariant derivative,
F_{\mu\nu}
is the Faraday/electromagnetic tensor, and
\phi
is the charged complex scalar field, as mentioned before. The equations of motion that rule our system are the Einstein equations, the massless Klein-Gordon equation and Maxwell’s equations, given by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\mathcal{D}^{\mu}\phi

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}F_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=4\pi J_{\nu}\,,

with

J_{\mu}=\frac{iq}{2}\left[\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi)^{*}\right]\,,

(7)

where the asterisk means the complex conjugate. The Einstein field equations will be evolved using the BSSN/conformal Z4 formalism
Nakamura et al. 1987
;
Shibata and Nakamura 1995
;
Baumgarte and Shapiro 1999
;
Bona et al. 2003
following the approach in
Vañó-Viñuales 2015
;
Álvares and Vaño-Viñuales 2025
.
Contrary to
Price
;
Bičák 1972
;
Gundlach et al. 1994a
;
Hod and Piran 1998a
;
Hod and Piran 1998b
;
Hod and Piran 1998c
;
Konoplya and Zhidenko 2013
, where the backreaction of the stress-energy tensor on the spacetime is neglected and thus the background spacetime is not evolved, here we consider the fully coupled case.
The stress-energy tensor of this system reads

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=F_{\mu\alpha}F_{\nu}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}

(8)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi\mathcal{D}^{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}\phi\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\phi\,.

We will be assuming spherical symmetry, so of the electric and magnetic fields that the Faraday tensor is usually decomposed into, we will discard the magnetic field and evolve the electric one. See Ref.
Álvares and Vaño-Viñuales 2025
for more details of the setup.

II.1
Hyperboloidal Slices and Conformal Compactification

To be able to extract radiation at
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
, we will use hyperboloidal slices, given by the level sets of a new time variable
t
related to the physical
\tilde{t}
as

t=\tilde{t}-h(\tilde{r})\,,

(9)

where
h(\tilde{r})
is the height function, whose effect is to raise the slices so they reach
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
. We also introduce a compactification factor,
\bar{\Omega}
, such that

\tilde{r}=\frac{r}{\bar{\Omega}(r)}\,.

(10)

Its purpose is to map the position of
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
to a given finite radius
r_{\mathrsfs{I}^{+}}
. Finally we do a conformal compactification, such that the components of the rescaled metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
do not diverge at
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
,

\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\,.

(11)

The conformal factor
\Omega
is chosen to be

\Omega=-K_{\text{CMC}}\frac{r^{2}_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}-r^{2}}{6r_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}}\,,

(12)

where
K_{\text{CMC}}
is a (negative) constant. Initially it coincides with the trace of the physical extrinsic curvature, providing constant-mean-curvature slices.

II.2
Einstein Field Equations Formulation

From now on, variables with a tilde on top will refer to the physical quantities, while those with a bar or nothing on top will correspond to conformally rescaled ones.
Applying the 3+1 decomposition to the conformally compactified metric, we define the spatial part of the metric
\bar{\gamma}_{ij}
. In the BSSN formalism it is decomposed into a spatial conformal factor
\chi
and a conformally rescaled spatial metric
\gamma_{ij}=\chi\bar{\gamma}_{ij}
.
The line element in this formulation is usually written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\alpha^{2}dt^{2}+\chi^{-1}\gamma_{ij}(dx^{i}+\beta^{i}dt)(dx^{j}+\beta^{j}dt)\,,

(13)

where
\alpha
is the lapse and
\beta^{r}
is the shift, the gauge quantities that are evolved besides
\chi
and
\gamma_{ij}
. The conformal extrinsic curvature tensor
\bar{K}_{ij}
is divided into its trace and trace-free parts,

A_{ij}=\chi\bar{K}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}\gamma_{ij}\bar{K}\,,

(14)

where
\bar{K}=\bar{\gamma}^{ij}\bar{K}_{ij}
. Instead of using
\bar{K}
, we will instead define this new variable
K
,

K=\bar{K}-2\Theta\,,

(15)

where
\Theta
is a quantity belonging to the Z4 part of the formulation. Lastly,

\Lambda^{i}=\Delta\Gamma^{i}+2\gamma^{ij}Z_{j}\,,

(16)

with
\Delta\Gamma^{i}=\Gamma^{i}-\hat{\Gamma}^{i}
and
\Gamma^{i}=\gamma^{jk}\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
.
\hat{\Gamma}_{jk}^{i}
is the connection coming from a time-independent spatial metric
\hat{\gamma}_{ij}
of our choosing, which we set to the flat spatial metric. From the Einstein equations, we evolve
\gamma_{ij}
,
\chi
,
\alpha
,
\beta^{i}
,
A_{ij}
,
\tilde{K}
,
\Lambda^{i}
and
\tilde{\Theta}
. The physical
\tilde{K}
and
\tilde{\Theta}
are related to their conformal counterparts as,

\tilde{K}=\Omega K-\frac{3\beta^{i}\partial_{i}\Omega}{\alpha}\,,\quad\tilde{\Theta}=\Omega\Theta\,.

(17)

In spherical symmetry only the scalar quantities and the radial components of vectors and tensors are evolved.

II.3
Charged Scalar Field

Adapting to the notation mentioned in the previous section, the physical scalar field now appears with a tilde on top,
\tilde{\phi}
. It is useful to define the rescaled scalar field
\bar{\phi}
from the physical one
\tilde{\phi}
:

\bar{\phi}=\frac{\tilde{\phi}}{\Omega}\,,

(18)

From this, the definitions for the real and imaginary parts of the scalar field are,

\bar{\phi}=\bar{c}_{\phi}+i\bar{d}_{\phi}\;,\quad\bar{c}_{\phi},\bar{d}_{\phi}\in\mathbb{R}\;\;,

(19)

such that the modulus of the scalar field is defined as

|\bar{\phi}|=\sqrt{\bar{c}_{\phi}^{2}+\bar{d}_{\phi}^{2}}\,.

(20)

The corresponding time derivatives of both parts of the scalar field are also defined as
,

\bar{c}_{\Pi}=\partial_{t}\bar{c}_{\phi},\;\bar{d}_{\Pi}=\partial_{t}\bar{d}_{\phi}.

(21)

III
Initial Data

As indicated in
Álvares and Vaño-Viñuales 2025

\bar{c}_{\phi}

\bar{c}_{\Pi}=\beta^{r}\left(\bar{c}_{\phi}^{\prime}+\bar{c}_{\phi}\frac{\Omega^{\prime}}{\Omega}\right)\,,

(22)

where
\beta^{r}
is the radial component of the shift and the prime indicates a spatial derivative (
\phi^{\prime}=\partial_{r}\phi
). However, when performing simulations with these initial data, it was hard to reach the tail regime with the expected exponent. Therefore, we followed Ref.
Vañó-Viñuales 2015
and changed the initial data such that we had the freedom to choose a more ingoing or outgoing pulse, but still solving the constraints initially. To do so, we add a new variable

Vañó-Viñuales 2015

VII
Conclusions

With this work, we took advantage of hyperboloidal slices to gain access to
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
efficiently to understand the behavior of a charged scalar field perturbation of a RN black hole. This allowed us to study the QNM ringing of the scalar field in Schwarzschild and RN spacetimes and found it only possible to fit the spherical fundamental mode to the former case. The latter showed but one oscillation, making it difficult to construct a reliable fit. We propose that the reason for the almost complete absense of oscillations is due to the self interaction of the charged scalar field, which picks up as soon as the imaginary part is excited by the presence of the charged black hole. Regarding the tail regime, although our results agree very well with Ref.
Hod and Piran 1998c
, there is a transition region where the decay is affected both by the black hole’s curvature and also by the electromagnetic coupling that to this day seems to have been unadressed, to the best of our knowledge.
The fact that this transition occurs around
Qq\sim 10^{-3}
, means that, contrary to the point-of-view that electrically charged black holes might be astrophysically irrevelant
Shapiro and Teukolsky 1983
, even if a black hole has a very small amount of charge, it may have a noticeable impact on the late-time behavior of perturbations.

We also analysed the frequency of oscillation of the real and imaginary parts during the tail regime and found a linear correlation between
Qq
and the frequency with which they oscillate. For
Qq>1/2
, the expected frequency seems to deviate from the analytical predictions in Ref.
Hod and Piran 1998c
.

Natural extensions of this work will involve studying the decay tails that originate in a collapsing spacetime
Gundlach et al. 1994b
– for a sample simulation of charged collapse with our infrastructure see Ref.
Álvares and Vaño-Viñuales 2025
. This will allow us to compare to prior results obtained at future null infinity
Purrer et al. 2005
. The prospect of tuning the collapse towards the extremal case
Q=M

Kehle and Unger 2024
is especially exciting.
Another option is to consider a scalar-field-dependent coupling with the Maxwell part such as Ref.
Guo et al. 2025
and study the behaviour of the resulting hairy black holes. Recent work
Cao et al. 2024
used hyperboloidal slices to study quasinormal modes of Boulware-Deser-Wheeler black holes in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity. Given the potential ill-posedness of the latter, attempting a full non-linear simulation of this setup is too risky at this stage, but could be an interesting research line to follow in the future.

Beyond breast-conservation rates: Measuring eligibility, offer, and choice after neoadjuvant chemotherapy
Authors: Chukwumbana Faith Okirie
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

De Luca et al. provide a multicenter benchmark of surgery after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT), reporting mastectomy in 36% of women with unifocal tumors and pathological complete response (pCR) [1]. However, utilization should not be equated with eligibility for breast conservation.

The authors note that post-NACT radiological response was unavailable and used pCR as a surrogate. Yet pCR was established after surgery and cannot reconstruct decision-time information. Consequently, its null association with breast-conserving surgery cannot establish that response did not influence decision-making. Unifocal pCR does not establish eligibility: residual imaging extent, calcifications or ductal carcinoma in situ, tumor-to-breast ratio, radiotherapy suitability, genetic risk, oncoplastic feasibility, and patient preference remain relevant.

BrighTNess illustrates the distinction: 68% of patients judged eligible after treatment underwent breast conservation [2].

Future SENONET audits could record baseline and post-NACT eligibility, the multidisciplinary recommendation, whether breast conservation was offered, patient preference, and a coded reason for mastectomy. Reporting conversion to eligibility among initially ineligible patients, uptake among eligible patients, and preference-concordant surgery would extend NEOIMPACT from a utilization audit to a patient-centered measure of decision quality, better identifying potentially avoidable mastectomy.

Tele-based continuous care and treatment compliance in dialysis patients with and without depression: the serial mediating effect of nurse-patient relationship and health belief
Authors: Wenjia Hong, Guangmin Cheng, Yiming Ma, Mo Chen, Congyan Yang, Xiaoli Yin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEnd-stage renal disease (ESRD) accompanied by depression is a rapidly increasing global healthcare burden, and the use of tele-based continuous care to improve depression and treatment compliance in dialysis patients has become a common medical practice. This article examines how tele-based continuous care affects treatment compliance in ESRD patients with and without depression.MethodsParticipants satisfying the inclusion and exclusion criteria were recruited from the nephrology departments and dialysis units of 15 tertiary, A-grade hospitals in Anhui Province, China, from October to December 2023. A final total of 549 patients completed a valid follow-up survey. Covariance-based structural equation modeling (CB-SEM) was used for path analysis between variables, testing the mediating effects of the nurse-patient relationship and health belief. Additionally, a multi-group comparison was used to analyze the difference between the groups with and without depression.ResultsPath analyses suggest a significant positive association between tele-based continuous care and treatment compliance in dialysis patients (β = 0.147; p < 0.001). The effect of the nurse-patient relationship on the treatment compliance of dialysis patients proved to be insignificant (β = 0.059; p = 0.498). The effect of tele-based continuous care on compliance in the depression group occurs only through health belief (β = 0.220; p = 0.006). While the effect of tele-based continuous care on compliance in the non-depression group all exist except through nurse-patient relationship (β = 0.006; p = 0.879).ConclusionsThis study validated the effect of tele-based continuous care on treatment compliance in dialysis patients and examined compliance outcomes in dialysis patients with and without depression. The results of this study can support the improvement of the tele-based continuous care system and the improvement of treatment compliance with depressed dialysis patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12888-025-07534-4.

Background

End-stage renal disease (ESRD) is a rapidly increasing global healthcare burden [1]. Although kidney transplantation is the best treatment for eligible ESRD patients, conservative treatment, such as dialysis, fluid intake, diet, and medications, is the primary treatment in most countries due to the lack of kidney donors. The current dialysis methods mainly include hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis. Different dialysis methods are suitable for different treatment needs. The unadjusted five-year survival rate for ESRD patients receiving conservative treatment is 41% in the United States, compared to 48% in Europe, 50% in China, and 60% in Japan [2]. Given the long-term nature of conservative treatment, patients often develop varying degrees of mental illness, which seriously affects their quality of life and treatment compliance [3]. Major depression is the most common psychiatric disease in patients with ESRD [4], with an estimated prevalence of 20% to 25% [5]. In this context, the use of tele-based continuous care to improve patients’ depression and treatment compliance has become a common medical practice [6, 7].

The American Geriatric Society defined “continuity of care” as meaning all care activities that enable patients to receive continuous and coordinated healthcare services while moving between different healthcare facilities and that also ensure the safety of high-risk patients during the transfer process [8]. With the rapid development of information technology, some continuous care activities are moving from offline to online [9, 10], with online care used for ongoing management of chronic diseases, medication adherence, healthcare counseling, and other remote services [11]. This online care is essential to ensure the sustainability of the healthcare system and to reduce the number of outpatient visits and hospitalizations, as well as to improve patients’ outlook about their own health and adherence to treatment plans [12]. Online care not only helps patients obtain adequate support and information about different treatment plans, but also provides effective support for their daily healthcare and home healthcare [13]. At the same time, long-term continuous monitoring of health indicators can be implemented, and personalized telemedicine can improve patient compliance with treatment guidelines and the retention of nurses [14]. Therefore, more and more medical institutions are beginning to use tele-care technology to provide online continuity of care services.

Numerous pieces of experimental evidence show the role of tele-based continuous care in improving patient compliance with treatment guidelines [15]. The possibility of fraudulent reporting by patients, however, increases with tele-care due to the lack of face-to-face supervision and an increased sense of alienation by patients [16], meaning that the level of patient compliance is not as ideal as what is reported. Moreover, the success of dialysis largely depends on the patient’s ability to comply with multiple clinical requirements and lifestyle changes [17]. Given the long and complex nature of dialysis, non-compliance is common among dialysis patients [18]. Many factors influence compliance (ignorance of the condition, economic condition, time, drug side effects, etc.), but a well-established fact is that patients’ level of “health belief” (that is, the patient’s positive outlook on their own health) and mental health status determine their compliance [7, 19].

Furthermore, existing research suggests that repeated individual contact in an intervention appears to be a key factor in ensuring high levels of health belief and compliance [20]. Therefore, patients’ health belief often closely relates to the intensity of the nurse-patient relationship. In reality, patients with ESRD often suffer from severe psychological pressure due to inadequate social support, which further reflects the importance of the nurse-patient relationship [21]. Existing research has found that cultivating harmonious patient-physician connections can effectively improve patients’ treatment compliance and relieve depression and anxiety [18]. In addition, the establishment of an effective doctor-patient connection can make patients feel close to their nurse and doctor, so they are more willing to “listen to the doctor” [22, 23]. Especially for patients with ESRD, establishing positive emotions and life perception via an effective personal connection can enhance patients’ health belief, and improve their treatment compliance [24]. On the contrary, patients with ESRD often lack a high level of health belief if they don’t have a good relationship with a doctor, which significantly reduces the probability of treatment compliance.

Within the telemedicine, maintaining the feeling of an in-person, face-to-face connection between the healthcare personnel and patients is challenging due to the lack of a shared physical space [25]. Compared with face-to-face visits, patients believe that healthcare providers pay less attention to them during telemedicine visits, and they have difficulty building a harmonious relationship [25]. In particular, telemedicine has yet to show satisfactory results in the establishment of a working alliance [26]. The effect of tele-based continuous care is hard to evaluate effectively due to the lack of close contact and the difficulty in assessing the inner state of the patients. At the same time, considering the differences between online and offline care, the nurse-patient relationship and health belief generated by tele-based continuous care have an uncertain effect on patients’ treatment compliance. This is especially true for dialysis patients, for whom the nurse-patient relationship and health belief have a more profound impact on their compliance due to their need for long-term care and the differences in their level of mental health compared with other patients.

Therefore, evaluating the impact of tele-based continuous care on the treatment compliance of dialysis patients is necessary, incorporating the nurse-patient relationship and health belief. To address these questions, data were collected through a combination of subjective and objective methods (including the nurse’s reports, the patient’s self-reported data, and monitoring data reports, etc.). The next section describes the study design, tele-based continuous care process, participants and measures. Methods

Study design

A cross-sectional study design was used to explore the effect of tele-based continuous care on patients’ treatment compliance. This design allowed us to analyze the differences between depressed and non-depressed patients at a single point in time to gain insight into the factors that influence treatment compliance in patients with ESRD. Data was obtained through patient reports, nurse reports, and monitoring data reports. The measures included tele-based continuous care, treatment compliance, health belief, nurse-patient relationship, and depression. The protocol for this study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (2023 HLZ No. 086). All patients signed a digital informed consent before inclusion in the study.

Tele-based continuous care process

The main goal of tele-based continuous care is to remotely guide dialysis patients in managing their health and to improve their health literacy and treatment compliance. Tele-based continuous care can effectively reduce the number of hospital visits by patients (except for their essential dialysis visits), and can provide patients with personalized guidance through the closed-loop of follow-up visits for in-hospital and out-hospital. The prompt communication between nurses and patients through the system can facilitate the collaboration between family caregivers and the medical team, thereby enhancing the overall care efficiency. Furthermore, it can also to a certain extent narrow the gap in medical resources among different regions and enhance the fairness and accessibility of nursing services. The process includes a web-based education and tele-care management program with both PC and mobile versions of an application (app) for the patient and nurse to use; the program enables consultation and communication between the patient and nurse. Screenshot of tele-based continuous care screen developed for the nurse

In this study, the tele-based continuous care process is one that is currently used by healthcare institutions and consists of the following steps. Healthcare personnel implement tele-based continuous care at the time of the initial discharge of a dialysis patient. Before hospital discharge, they train the patient in some basic nursing knowledge regarding their condition and in the use of the app. Then, the patient’s nurse formulates a patient-based continuous care plan under the guidance of senior nurses who are responsible for tele-care services. And then, the nurse implements and regularly carries out this plan, including sending message about dieting, exercise, medication, dialysis, and so on. The nurse takes next steps according to the feedback from the patient. When a patient is in stable condition, the nurse will continue to execute the original plan, including hospital-level and specialist-level follow-up visits. When a patient develops an abnormal condition (such as physical discomfort, infection, or the after-effects of dialysis), the nurse will evaluate effectiveness with the attending physician based on the symptoms. Then, they will send a recommendation to the patient via a real-time text message. If the patient needs observation, the nurse will update the tele-care plan. If the patient is in a severe condition, the nurse will contact the patient immediately and arrange an in-patient visit. The entire process is designed to help dialysis patients manage their health and to ensure the effectiveness of the tele-based care. Figure 3 shows the process of tele-based continuous care.

Participants and procedures

The study was conducted in Anhui Province, China. Anhui Province is located in the central region of China. The current number of dialysis patients in the region is more than 40,000, which is in the forefront of China. The study subjects were discharged dialysis patients who had participated in tele-based continuous care. Participants satisfying the inclusion and exclusion criteria were recruited from the nephrology departments and dialysis units of 15 tertiary, A-grade hospitals from October to December 2023. These hospitals have adopted similar dialysis strategies, treatments and tele-based continuous care systems. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for this study were as follows.

The data collection process consisted of three parts. First, in early October 2023, 50 questionnaires were distributed for a pre-survey. Interviews and focus groups of these respondents were used to assess whether the contents of the questionnaires were in line with the research objectives and whether the questions were clear, understandable, and relevant. Second, before the survey, doctors, nurses, or graduate students at each center were trained to ensure the reliability of the questionnaires filled out by the respondents. In addition, a person in charge and a quality controller was set up in each hospital to supervise and guide the distribution and filling of questionnaires. After the formal investigation began, the investigator had to have each patient’s explicit consent and signature before starting the questionnaire. The questionnaire was distributed and collected by the nurse to whom each patient belonged. Third, after collecting data from patients, each patient’s nurse scored their compliance with the tele-based continuous care program. At the same time, we exported the patients’ tele- based continuous care data from the system, including intervention frequency, duration, and participation rate, which together with patient reports and nurse reports constituted the basis of the score of the tele-based continuous care. All data were summarized to the project leader of each hospital, and the quality of the collected questionnaires was checked by the quality controller. A total of 709 eligible dialysis patients volunteered for the study. In the end, a total of 549 patients completed a valid follow-up survey, with a valid response rate of 77.4%.

Measures

Tele-based continuous care

The content and quality of the tele-based continuous care are key factors in the nurse-patient relationship and patients’ health belief, and a topic of this study. Based on the definition and practice of traditional continuous care, tele-based continuous care includes healthcare service continuity, healthcare information sharing, and relationship continuity between patients and nurses [27]. Considering the differences between the remote and traditional continuing care models, we used the frequency of medical information exchanges, duration, and participation rate as measures of information sharing, service continuity and relationship continuity, respectively. Specifically, we used the frequency of medical information exchanges (that is, the number of times nurses and patients exchange relevant medical information each week in the system) to measure information sharing (two or fewer, 3–5, 6–10, 11–15, or 16 or more); we used the duration (that is, the length of time a patient accepted tele-based continuous care) to measure the service continuity (less than 3 months, 3–6 months, 6–9 months, 9–12 months, or more than 1 year); and we used the participation rate (that is, the percentage of times patients participated effectively and nurses provided service via tele-based continuous care system) to measure relationship continuity (less than 20%, 21–40%, 41–60%, 61–80%, or 81–100%). We found sufficient internal consistency (α = 0.774) of the scale, as well as acceptable internal consistency of the healthcare information sharing (α = 0.864), healthcare service continuity (α = 0.837), and relationship continuity (α = 0.792).

Treatment compliance

No consistent standard exists for the measurement of compliance in existing studies. The compliance of dialysis patients has broadly addressed four domains of therapy, namely, adherence to recommendations regarding diet, fluid intake, dialysis treatment, and medications [28]. The lack of standardized methods for measuring compliance in these domains leads to differences in reported compliance rates and difficulties in accurately estimating the effectiveness of interventions to improve compliance [15]. Measures of compliance vary in studies. They include indirect measures such as self-reported adherence; direct measurements such as pill counting or data from medication event monitoring systems (MEMS), as well as dialysis training; and alternative indicators such as weight gain during dialysis or biochemical parameters, which include phosphate and potassium levels [29]. In addition, an obvious limitation of compliance measurements in tele-based continuous care is that they are usually self-reported by participants. Most objective measures assess only patient attendance and do not include any measure of intensity or effort. In most cases, these measures record whether the intervention is being used, but not the duration, intensity, total energy expenditure, or extent of other variables.

To solve the above problems, this study adopted multiple indicators that were combined to measure patients’ treatment compliance, including the nurse’s reports, the patient’s self-reported data, and monitoring data reports. Among these indicators, the nurse’s reports and the patient’s reports included adherence to medication, diet, exercise, fluid intake, dialysis, care, and other medical orders. We scored all items (e.g., “I can comply with care requirements related to dialysis”) using a five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The monitoring data reports included the percentage of time that medication was taken on time (less than 20%, 21–40%, 41–60%, 61–80%, or 81–100%) and the percentage of time that dialysis was performed on time (less than 20%, 21–40%, 41–60%, 61–80%, or 81–100%). The scale had sufficient internal consistency (α = 0.764), and we found an acceptable internal consistency of the nurses’ reports (α = 0.861), patients’ reports (α = 0.782), and monitoring data reports (α = 0.848).

Health belief

We scored health belief using Rosenstock’s Health Belief Scale, the reliability and validity of which has been verified in studies on the medication compliance of kidney transplant patients [30]. The scale includes the following four aspects: Perceived susceptibility (three items) is the self-awareness of infection and adverse drug reactions. Perceived severity (four items) is the individual’s awareness of their disease severity. Perceived benefit (four items) is the individual’s subjective judgment of the benefits they receive from treatment compliance. Perceived barriers (four items) refers to the individual’s awareness of adverse drug reactions and life conflicts. We scored all items using a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). We used the mean to calculate the measurements for each dimension. The scale had sufficient internal consistency (α = 0.881), and we found an acceptable internal consistency of perceptual susceptibility (α = 0.892), perceived severity (α = 0.901), perceived benefit (α = 0.875), and perceived barriers (α = 0.856).

Nurse-patient relationship

We scored nurse-patient relationship using the nurse-patient trust relationship scale [31]. The scale includes five aspects, and the Chinese version of the scale has been proven to have good reliability and validity [32]. We scored all items (e.g., “Nurses have a wealth of expertise”) using a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). We found sufficient internal consistency (α = 0.895).

Depression

We scored depression using the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), which is based on the nine items of the DSM-IV (the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders developed by the American Psychiatric Association) diagnostic criteria. The Chinese version of the scale has been widely used with depressed patients and proven to have good reliability and validity [33]. We scored all items (e.g., “Lack of motivation or interest in doing things”) using a 4-point Likert scale, from 0 (not at all) to 3 (almost daily). This study used the standard total score method as the screening criteria for depression, with 10 points as the cut-off value, and a total score of ≥10 points as positive (i.e., the patient has depression). In this study, sufficient internal consistency existed (α = 0.905) for the measurement of PHQ-9, as well as sufficient internal consistency between the depressed group (α = 0.847) and the non-depressed group (α = 0.783).

Data analysis

SPSS29 and AMOS24.0 software were used for data analysis. First, Harman’s one-factor test was used to analyze whether a common method bias existed in the data. Second, descriptive statistics were used to analyze the demographic characteristics of the participants. Third, the correlations between tele-based continuous care, treatment compliance, health belief, and nurse-patient relationship were evaluated using Pearson correlations analysis. Fourth, AMOS software was used to analyze the paths between variables. Chi-square mean/degree of freedom (CMIN/DF), root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA), the comparative fit index (CFI), the goodness of fit index (GFI), and the adjusted goodness of fit index (AGFI) were used to judge the fitting degree of the model. Fifth, to better understand the mechanism by which nurse-patient relationship and health belief mediate the relationship between tele-based continuous care and treatment compliance, AMOS24.0 was used to test the mediating effects of nurse-patient relationship and health belief. Sixth, a multi-group comparison was used to analyze the difference between the groups with and without depression.

Results

Tests for common method bias and multicollinearity

We used Harman’s one-factor test to identify any common method bias [34]. If a single factor accounts for more than 50% of the variance, the data may have common method bias [35]. Principal component factor analysis showed that the first five factors accounted for 70.56% of the total variance, and the percentage of the largest factor was 25.02%, with no factor higher than 50%. In addition, this paper also used the marker variable method to test the common method bias [36]. The results showed that each labeled variable had no significant effect on the model variable. Therefore, the possibility of common method bias in this study is small.

Discussion

Tele-based continuous care and treatment compliance

The results indicate that tele-based continuous care has a positive effect on improving the treatment compliance of dialysis patients, which is consistent with previous studies [15]. To be specific, the influence of tele-based continuous care on treatment compliance is a complex process: a patient’s health belief can indirectly affect treatment compliance, and the care can also influence treatment compliance through the continuous mediating effects of the nurse-patient relationship and health belief. In other words, this type of care can influence patients’ treatment compliance in many ways. This complexity shows the great potential of tele-based continuous care to improve treatment compliance in kidney disease patients.

When we examine the results of only the patients with depression, however, we see a disappointing picture: the effect of tele-based continuous care on treatment compliance in the depressed group occurs through health belief alone. The result reveals the mechanism of tele-based continuous care on the treatment compliance of patients with depression, and also shows the importance of health belief in patients with depression. For patients with depression, the pessimism and despair associated with depression may make it more difficult for them to perceive the benefits of teletherapy and may exacerbate their non-compliant behavior [37], so that tele-based continuous care can’t directly improve their treatment compliance. The precautions sent by the primary nurse through the system and the online interaction with the nurse may have a positive impact on the mental health of patients with depression, forming a more positive view of their own health status, disease treatment, and home care, thereby improving treatment compliance. This finding provides valuable guidance for mental health support provided by tele-based continuous care system, and suggests effective solutions to the problem of non-compliance by dialysis patients with depression.

However, we believe that the other relationships in the depression group are not significant for the following reasons. Motivating patients who lack the will to self-manage is more difficult for nurses via online communication, as shown by the inability of the nurse-patient relationship to improve treatment compliance (and our unsupported hypothesis). According to results from Cui et al., offline nurse-patient communication is more effective in this regard, and is often the key to maintaining the depressed patients’ psychological capital and treatment confidence [38]. Also, the frequency of face-to-face contact between nurses and patients decreases when tele-based continuous care is used. This decrease makes it more difficult for the nurse to judge the reasons for the patients’ non-compliance or to detect the patient’s concealment of their non-compliance, and can also cause patients to “slack off” or give up on the idea of treatment. Thus, for patients with a mental illness like depression, maintaining effective offline communication may be necessary.

Nurse-patient relationship and treatment compliance

In tele-based continuous care settings, the effect of the nurse-patient relationship on the treatment compliance of dialysis patients was proven to be insignificant across all paths and groups, which shows a shortcoming of tele-based continuous care system. Because the system reduces the number of hospital visits, it widens the psychological distance between the nurse and patient due to the reduction of face-to-face communication, making the nurse and patient feel unfamiliar with each other. This greater psychological distance reduces the effect of doctors’ advice and guidance to patients, which the nurse conveys. This may explain our result of the nurse-patient relationship not directly influencing treatment compliance.

Health belief and treatment compliance

In this study, the effects associated with health belief are confirmed in the group without depression, confirming Cerkoney and Hart’s conclusion that compliance in patients is related to their health belief [39]. Based on the health belief model, an individual’s positive perception of their own health-related behavior shapes their lifestyle [40]. Since tele-based continuous care can enable patients continue to receive medical services and medical information after discharge, it helps improve patients’ health literacy, self-efficacy, and psychological resilience; continuously strengthens their positive views on their own health, and promotes their treatment compliance. Patients without depression are able to proactively seek and receive the support provided by the tele-based continuous care system and then implement healthy lifestyles to maintain treatment compliance. For patients with depression, tele-based continuous care can alleviate their psychological burden to a certain extent, improve their mental health level, and gradually enhance their cognition of the benefits and obstacles to healthy behaviors, so as to improve their treatment compliance.

Clinical implications

This study conducts an in-depth exploration of the tele-based continuous care system, providing clear ideas for optimizing the tele-care model, with a focus on improving the treatment compliance and quality of life of dialysis patients.

Firstly, our research presented the differences in the effect of tele-based continuous care on treatment compliance between the depressed and non-depressed groups. This reveals the shortcomings of the current tele-based continuous care system. Given that dialysis is a long-term process and patients often suffer from complications such as depression, especially when they have no hope of being transplanted. This suggests that the developers of tele-base continuous care system should develop a multi-disease management module based on the physiological-psychological-social model, providing comprehensive, deep and targeted management solutions for dialysis patients. It is not only necessary to enhance the collaborative monitoring and dynamic warning of multiple indicators such as blood pressure, blood glucose, and dry weight, but also to offer monitoring and guidance at the social and psychological levels to improve the overall health condition of patients.

Second, our results also show the importance of the pathway for tele-based continuous care to affect compliance via health belief among depressed patients. Therefore, nurses should aim to quickly grasp the emotional state of depressed patients through the system, convey care to those patients, implement personalized intervention measures to enhance the patients’ health belief and enhance the understanding of the severity of the disease and the benefits of treatment, transforming the understanding of health into more stable practice of compliance.

Third, our results show that the nurse-patient relationship does not play an ideal role in the current tele-based continuous care system. A model that combines online and offline nursing should be built, especially for patients with depression. The nursing manager and other hospital managers should formulate reasonable plans for in-person patient visits and carry out more offline continuous care activities, enhancing the two-way flow of emotion and information between nurses and patients. These measures can effectively alleviate the anxiety and depression of dialysis patients, reduce the possibility of “deceptive” reports, and improve the patients’ life satisfaction.

Limitations

This research has the following limitations:

First, this study mainly focuses on the impact of tele-based continuous care on the treatment compliance of dialysis patients. However, it neglects some important factors, such as treatment methods, dialysis strategies, and the duration of dialysis for patients. These factors may not make our study fully reflect the full potential and challenges of tele-base continuous care. Therefore, in future studies, we will integrate different treatment parameters with patient characteristics to develop more effective remote care plans, conduct a more detailed group analysis of these factors, thereby further improving the overall health management level and quality of life of dialysis patients.

Second, as a cross-sectional survey was conducted in this study, the results can’t reflect the long-term effects of tele-based continuous care on treatment compliance. Therefore, longitudinal and experimental studies are needed to fully explore the causal relationships between the variables over time, and to accurately reflect the compliance of patients. In addition, this study concentrated on dialysis patients using a single system, which hinders the general causal relationship between tele-based continuous care, nurse-patient relationship, health belief, and treatment compliance. Therefore, we will expand the current research model to various tele-based continuous care system and diseases in the future to obtain more universal research results.

Third, data acquisition in this study was mainly based on objective data and questionnaires, which may not fully reflect the real thoughts and needs of patients, introducing a risk of social desirability bias and data errors. Also, the findings may overestimate or underestimate the impact of tele-based continuous care on treatment compliance and limit the promotion of the findings. Therefore, in the future, we will consider more forms of data collection, such as interviews, experiments, and text data mining, in order to better explore the thoughts and ideas of the patients. In addition, we will carry out a comparative analysis between multiple systems and multiple diseases to discover similarities and differences among tele-based continuous care toward different diseases.

Finally, the object of this study was dialysis patients recruited from 15 tertiary, A-grade hospitals in Anhui Province, China, all of whom were being cared for through the same tele-based continuous care system. Due to regional, cultural, and economic differences, the results of this study may not extend to other provinces and countries. Future studies could further compare the differences in compliance among dialysis patients from different cultural backgrounds and regions. Additionally, cross-regional and cross-cultural research are conducive to enhancing the applicability and effectiveness of tele-based continuous care programs in a broader population.

Conclusions

This article examines how tele-based continuous care affects treatment compliance in ESRD patients. The results indicate that tele-based continuous care has a positive effect on the treatment compliance of dialysis patients. The effect of the nurse-patient relationship on the treatment compliance of dialysis patients proved to be insignificant. We also examined differences between patients with and without depression. The effect of tele-based continuous care on compliance in the depression group occurs only through health belief. While the effect of tele-base continuous care on compliance in the non-depression group all exist except through nurse-patient relationship. Our findings enrich the evidence showing how tele-based continuous care can improve treatment compliance in dialysis patients. Understanding the role of the nurse-patient relationship and the patient’s health belief in promoting treatment compliance is critical to develop targeted interventions to improve compliance. In addition, more attention should be paid to the mental health of dialysis patients.

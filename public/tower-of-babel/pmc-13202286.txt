Spatial heterogeneity and tendency prediction of a community transmission model with HIV detection
Authors: Jiaxin Liu, Fengying Wei, Zhen Jin, Jianfeng Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWorld Health Organization recommends that the individuals with HIV infection take HIV detection as early as possible to avoid the potential transmission risk in the community, because HIV infection often exhibit no any symptoms and being neglected by the individuals at the acute phase. In China, a series of policies relating to HIV detection have been announced by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention for effectively controlling the HIV/AIDS infection scale.MethodsThe next generation matrix method is governed to derive the basic reproduction number of SIDMA model with HIV detection in this study. The least squares method is used to perform the optimal fitting against the surveillance data of HIV/AIDS. The sensitivity index and partial rank correlation coefficients are used to investigate the impacts of key parameters on the HIV/AIDS incidence. The ARIMA model method is applied to predict the gender and age distributions of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics. The Gini index method is for the spatial heterogeneity of all diagnosed cases.ResultsThe numerical simulation results show that the HIV/AIDS incidence depends on two significant parameters: detection rate and effective contact rate, and that basic reproduction number relies on effective contact rate. This study reveals that the long awareness delay extends the infection scale and prevalence risk of HIV/AIDS, and that the spatial heterogeneity of diagnosed cases in nine cities of Fujian Province is relatively uniform. The 2023-2030 tendency predictions with scenarios indicate that detection rate of SIDMA model is most critical for the prevalence of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics. The gender and age predictions by ARIMA model indicate that females and 60 years old and over face the potential high HIV infection risks.ConclusionsThe HIV detection rate is the most important contributor of SIDMA model for suppressing the HIV/AIDS incidence. The declining trend of basic reproduction number of SIDMA model in five phases indicates that HIV detection in Fujian Province have achieved remarkable achievements. The 2023-2030 tendency predictions of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics provide the estimations with low-level incidence, meeting the control goal from the Chinese government. The four insights are recommended to the policy-makers and the local governments for fighting against the HIV/AIDS prevalence.Keywords: HIV/AIDS, SIDMA model, HIV detection, Basic reproduction number, Spatial heterogeneity, Tendency prediction

Introduction

Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS) is a systemic disease caused by the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) with blood transmission route, sexual contact transmission route, and mother-to-child transmission route (World Health Organization, 2024). The individuals with HIV infection usually undergo the acute infection, chronic infection, and AIDS after being infected across the globe (U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2025). In the recent contributions, the HIV/AIDS epidemic models have been widely used and investigated the transmission mechanism therein. For instance, Wu et al. (2023) established an HIV/AIDS model to study the joint impact of infection age and treatment adherence on HIV/AIDS transmission, and found that improving the treatment adherence of HIV-infected individuals could effectively control HIV transmission. Further, Sun et al. (2013) revealed that increasing treatment coverage may lead to a decline in new HIV-infected individuals and being beneficial to the control of HIV/AIDS. Especially, the results from the immune impairment pointed out that the HIV/AIDS infection scale and its transmission dynamics were closely connected with the immune responses of the local populations (Wang et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2025a, Wang et al., 2025b, Wang et al., 2025c).

According to the report from World Health Organization (WHO), there exist approximately 1,300,000 individuals being HIV new infections in 2023, with 14% of new infection cases being undiagnosed with the hidden transmission risk across the globe (World Health Organization, 2024). Up to the end of 2023, the real percentage of the globe is bit far from the first 95% that the 95% individuals living with HIV to know their infection status by HIV detection, although HIV detection becomes a unique way for confirming infection status and achieving the first 95% target. The research results from compartmental models with HIV detection provided some valuable insights into the uncertainty of HIV transmission dynamics. For instance, the recent studies (Di Giamberardino et al., 2017; Dutta & Gupta, 2022; Kar et al., 2024; Mushanyu, 2020; Roy & Saha, 2018; Xue et al., 2022) assumed that HIV detection from undiagnosed individuals with no clinical symptoms to diagnosed individuals obeyed the linear transfer mechanism, and investigated the impacts of late diagnosis on HIV incidence, further pointed out that the promotion of HIV detection enabled more undiagnosed individuals to know their HIV status. Rao and Luo (2021) and Rathinasamy et al. (2021) found that the enhancement of HIV detection rate reduced the basic reproduction number and infection scale of the individuals with HIV in the community. Meanwhile, Naresh et al. (2011) and Ouyang et al. (2026) investigated the compartmental models with HIV detection and contact tracing to describe the impacts of HIV detection rate on the HIV/AIDS epidemics in India and China respectively, of which the average contact number of per diagnosed case were proportional to product of diagnosed and infected cases, and they discovered that the decrease of proportion of active diagnosed population and the increase of contact tracing were the main contributors for declining HIV/AIDS prevalence.

Motivated by the above research results, in this study, we aim to establish a SIDMA compartmental model with HIV detection based on the saturated incidence, involving the saturation of diagnoses cases and monitored cases, to investigate the impacts of HIV detection on the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics. We focus on the variations of basic reproduction numbers in the five phases, the scenario analysis with and without SIDMA model, and the tendency predictions with SIDMA model, further conducting sensitivity analysis of the main parameters, the spatial heterogeneity analysis by Gini index, to provide more insights for Fujian HIV/AIDS prevention and control strategies.

Methods

Data source and national policies

The monthly surveillance data on HIV/AIDS diagnosed cases in Fujian Province, covering the period from January 2004 to December 2022, is supplied by the Fujian Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention (i.e., Fujian CDC). These 2004-2022 HIV/AIDS surveillance data from Fujian CDC are separated into five phases by four policies respectively in the recent two decades, because the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (i.e., China CDC) issued four main policies in 2004, 2009, 2015, and 2020 (Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, 2004; Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, 2009, Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, 2015, Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, 2020), relating to HIV detection for effectively controlling HIV/AIDS infection scale. Herewith, it is important to emphasize that these HIV/AIDS surveillance data did not record the awareness delay of the local government. In the real epidemics, the awareness delay reflects the duration of the period that the policy-makers or the local government did not recognize the epidemics, but it ranges from the first infection to the first report for the individuals with HIV virus (Huang et al., 2020), and plays the vital roles when infection scale or final size is concerned (Chen et al., 2025; Lan et al., 2024; Ouyang et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025a, Wang et al., 2025b, Wang et al., 2025c). Theoretically, the average incubation period of HIV/AIDS lasts from 2-year to 10-year (National Center for AIDS/STD Control and Prevention, China CDC, 2018). From the real situation point of view, the awareness delay of HIV/AIDS is taken as 2-year for the further consideration. The monthly incidence of diagnosed cases with HIV/AIDS is calculated by monthly infection scale and yearly total population in Fujian Province, in which the yearly total population of Fujian Province is collected from the Fujian Statistical Yearbook in 2023 (Fujian Provincial Bureau of Statistics, 2023).

Gini index of surveillance data

The Gini index describes the spatial heterogeneity of diagnosed cases in a given region based on the surveillance data (Baydil et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; Fadilah et al., 2022; Xiong et al., 2024), in terns of the following formula

where N is the number of spatial units at a given region, i denotes the index for each spatial unit of interest, Pi is the cumulative proportion of the population in the i th unit, Ci is the cumulative proportion of diagnosed cases in the i th unit with P0 = C0 = 0. Formula (3) for the Gini index is calculated by ranking units in decreasing order of incidence ΔCi/ΔPi, where ΔCi = Ci − Ci−1, ΔPi = Pi − Pi−1. In mathematics, the Gini index ranges from 0 to 1, which an index close to 0 means that diagnosed cases are evenly distributed in the space, and an index close to 1 means that the spatial distribution of diagnosed cases is not even.

Global sensitivity analysis

The global sensitivity analysis is performed to investigate the impacts of the main parameters on the peak of the infected to SIDMA model, when the sample size is set to be 3000 and the p-value is set less than 0.01 (Fig. 4, Table A1). The values of PRCCs reflect the sensitivity of each parameter, the positive sign (+) of PRCCs indicates the positive-correlation on the peak of the infected, and negative sign (−) for the negative-correlation on the peak of the infected. The results reveal that seven main parameters with stars (i.e., α, β, μ, ρ, θ, γ, φ) play a key role on the peak of the infected, of which the effective contact rate (i.e., β) and detection rate (i.e., ρ) have the most significant impacts on the peak of the infected. The results also point out that, from the local policy-makers point of view, the decrease of effective contact rate and the increase of detection rate contributes to the decline of the peak of the infected.

Scenario analysis without SIDMA model

The impacts of awareness delay on HIV/AIDS incidence are numerically discussed (Fig. 5). If the awareness delays are respectively set as 4-year and 6-year, then the corresponding HIV/AIDS incidences are approximately estimated by 99.9/100,000 and 393.3/100,000 to SIDMA model, at least being 1.8 times of HIV/AIDS incidence 54.8/100,000 to SIDMA model with 2-year AD. That is to say, the extension of the awareness delay directly triggers the enhancement of the HIV/AIDS prevalence risk.

Scenario analysis with SIDMA model

The impacts of effective contact rate on HIV/AIDS incidence are explored (Fig. 6). The numerical simulations find that, given the baseline value 1.00β, the corresponding HIV/AIDS incidence reaches 56.3/100,000. If we take 1.05β, then the corresponding HIV/AIDS incidence is approximately estimated as 105.1/100,000. In other words, the enhancement of HIV/AIDS incidence mainly depends on the increase of effective contact rate. Further, if detection rate declines to 0.95ρ, then the corresponding HIV/AIDS incidence climbs up to 77.7/100,000 (Fig. 7), which further implies that detection rate admits negative correlation with the HIV/AIDS incidence.

Estimation of Gini index

To depict the spatial heterogeneity of the distribution of diagnosed cases, the Gini index of Fujian Province is calculated using formula (3) and HIV/AIDS surveillance data from Fujian CDC. The total population size of Fujian Province is divided into nine spatial units. At the i th unit, the proportion, Ci, of the cumulative diagnosed cases in the first i units out of the total diagnosed cases is computed, and the proportion, Pi, of the cumulative population size in the first i units out of the total population size is computed (Fujian Provincial Bureau of Statistics, 2023). By the descending order of ΔCi/ΔPi, the Gini index of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics from 2004 to 2022 is calculated as 0.1281, indicating that the spatial heterogeneity of diagnosed cases of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics is relatively uniform (Table A2). Tendency prediction by SIDMA model

The 2023-2030 tendency predictions of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics are investigated under baseline scenario and two scenarios (Fig. 9). The baseline scenario is referred to as the situation that the parameter values are adopted from Table 1. The prediction results indicate that the cumulative incidence of HIV/AIDS will reach 104.0/100,000 by the end of 2030. Scenario 1 is referred to as the situation that detection rate is increased by 5% in January 2023, keeping other parameter values same with those from Table 1. Scenario 2 is referred to as the situation that detection rate is further increased by 5% in January 2026 on the basis of Scenario 1, keeping other parameter values same with those from Table 1. The prediction results show that the cumulative incidences of HIV/AIDS respectively decline to 92.6/100,000 and 87.8/100,000 for Scenario 1 and Scenario 2 by the end of 2030. These prediction results are consistent with the HIV/AIDS prevention and control goal of the Chinese government. That is to say, as of January 2030, the HIV/AIDS incidence of the total population of Fujian Province is below 0.2%.

The 2023-2030 tendency predictions of Fujian HIV/AIDS incidence in months by SIDMA model. An extra control measure is assumed to be implemented in January 2026 (red dashed).

Data feature and tendency prediction by ARIMA model

The HIV/AIDS surveillance data from Fujian CDC recorded the gender and age distribution of diagnosed cases from 2004 to 2022, with 19% female diagnosed cases, 81% male diagnosed cases; and 11.8% for those under 18 years old, 45.3% for 18-35 years old, 37.1% for 36-59 years old, and 15.8% for 60 years old and over. The majority of HIV/AIDS diagnosed cases are males with 18-35 years old, out of the majority of infected individuals are HIV-positive. The ratios are estimated by the number of HIV-positive individuals to the number of AIDS in gender and age (Fig. 10). Further, the ARIMA model is used to predict the gender and age distributions of diagnosed cases for the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics from 2023 to 2030 (Fig. 11). The prediction results reveal that an increasing tendency for females and 60 years old and over, highlighting that HIV detection for females and 60 years old and over contributes to achieve the national HIV/AIDS prevention and control goals.

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, a SIDMA (Susceptible-Infected-Diagnosed-Monitored-AIDS) compartmental model with HIV detection is proposed to explore the impacts of HIV detection on the HIV/AIDS transmission dynamics. The 2004-2022 HIV/AIDS surveillance data from the Fujian Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention are collected and separated into five phases based on the implementation of four nationwide detection measures of HIV/AIDS. The expression of basic reproduction number of SIDMA model is derived with the next generation matrix method. Then, the optimal fitting of SIDMA model is performed by least squares method, with 2-year awareness delay and main parameter values from Table 1, and the 2004-2022 HIV/AIDS surveillance data (Fig. 2). The optimal-fitting results show that the basic reproduction number R0 of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics ranges from 1.5 to 3.98. The sensitivity analysis by using sensitivity index and partial rank correlation coefficients values is provided (Fig. 3, Fig. 4), which reveals that the HIV/AIDS incidence is mainly dependent on detection rate and effective contact rate, and that basic reproduction number mainly relies on effective contact rate. The further numerical investigations show that the increase of detection rate and the decrease of effective contact rate help to reduce the cumulative HIV/AIDS incidence (Fig. 6, Fig. 7). Further, the 2023-2030 prediction results claim that the HIV/AIDS incidence with baseline scenario and two scenarios is under low-level as of the end of 2030 (Fig. 9).

The research results without the involvement of SIDMA model find that the extension of the awareness delay takes the significant impacts on HIV/AIDS incidence, indicating that the HIV/AIDS incidence with 6-year awareness delay is approximately 7 times as large as that with 2-year awareness delay (Fig. 5). The Gini indices reveal that the spatial heterogeneity of diagnosed cases for the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics in nine cities is relatively uniform (Fig. 8). Further, combined with gender and age features of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics from 2004 to 2022 (Fig. 10), the ARIMA model is used to predict the gender distribution and age distribution of the Fujian HIV/AIDS epidemics from 2023 to 2030, demonstrating that the females and 60 years old and over face the potential high infection risks (Fig. 11).

The main results of this study provide some significant insights for the policy-makers and the local governments to fight against the HIV/AIDS prevalence. The first insight is to shorten the awareness delay of the policy-makers and the local governments via incorporating HIV detection with the routine health service programs, such as health check-ups for the elderly who are more than 60 years old, and premarital medical examinations for the young people. The second insight is to establish the anonymous detection points within the community for providing the rapid HIV detection services, guaranteeing the enough supplies of HIV self-test kits to high-risk groups, while safeguarding the confidentiality of individuals being detected. The third insight is to provide diagnosed individuals with two regular follow-ups every year for monitoring their conditions and adjusting treatment plans. The fourth insight is to provide the wide publicity by the local health department towards the general population with the consistent condoms use during sexual intercourse, and towards the high-risk groups with the implementations of pre-exposure prophylaxis.

In this study, the SIDMA model and the corresponding analysis also exist some limitations. The homogeneity of key parameters for the SIDMA model within the same policy phase bring the same values of R0, HIV strain variation and drug resistance effects for tendency predictions, which neglects the differences in regions and medical resources, when the community transmission is taken into account. This study wound be improved in two aspects based on the potential merits of recent works. One is that, at the macroscopic level, we would focus on the multi-patch interaction mechanism (Wu & Feng, 2025) and the heterogeneity of the main parameters (Wu & Cheng, 2025; Wu et al., 2025) to explore the potential differences in regions of China for the estimations of infection scales. The other is that, at the microscopic level, we plan to integrate the viral infection mechanism (Wang et al., 2025a, Wang et al., 2025b, Wang et al., 2025c) and impacts of drug resistance (Guo et al., 2025) into the SIDMA model to optimize the tendency prediction for HIV/AIDS prevention and control in China.

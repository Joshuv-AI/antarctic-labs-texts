A study of post-urination external genital cleansing behavior and its impact on improving dapagliflozin adherence in Chinese male heart failure patients
Authors: Xiaorong Yuan, Tao Liu, Xiaoyu Zhang, Chunyan He, Shifang Xiao, Hong Xiao, Hua Liu, Bin Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile the relationship between genital hygiene practices and infection risk has been well-documented in various clinical settings, limited attention has been paid to preventive strategies specifically targeting male heart failure patients on dapagliflozin. This study aims to evaluate the impact of post-urination external genital cleaning on dapagliflozin medication adherence in male heart failure patients.Methods and resultsA multicenter, randomized, single-blind, parallel-controlled study was conducted with 1337 male heart failure patients on dapagliflozin therapy. The test group performed simple external genital cleaning post-urination, while the control group maintained routine care. Primary outcomes included genital fungal infection incidence, medication-related concerns, belief intensity, and medication adherence, assessed over a 1-year period. The test group demonstrated significantly lower rates of genital fungal infections compared to controls (0.75% vs. 3.14%, P = 0.002). Additionally, the test group showed higher dapagliflozin adherence rates (72.75% vs. 63.23%, P < 0.001).ConclusionPost-urination external genital cleaning significantly improves dapagliflozin medication adherence in male heart failure patients.Keywords: External genital cleansing behaviour, Male heart failure patients, Dapagliflozin, Medication adherence

Introduction

Heart failure, a severe manifestation and advanced stage of cardiac disease, is marked by high rates of rehospitalization and mortality. Guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) has proven effective in mitigating these risks and improving patient outcomes. However, the full benefit of GDMT is often compromised by a critical challenge: suboptimal medication adherence. Studies report that adherence to long-term oral medications among heart failure patients remains disappointingly low, ranging from only 48–53% [1, 2]. Identifying and addressing the barriers to adherence is therefore an urgent clinical priority.

The introduction of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), particularly dapagliflozin, represents a paradigm shift in heart failure management. Current guidelines now recommend dapagliflozin as a foundational therapy across the entire spectrum of heart failure, including reduced (HFrEF), mildly reduced (HFmrEF), and preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) [3–5]. Despite its established efficacy, maintaining adherence to SGLT2i therapy presents a unique challenge, largely driven by a heightened risk of genital mycotic infections.

Male heart failure patients are anatomically and physiologically predisposed to such infections. This vulnerability arises from a confluence of factors: heart failure-related fluid retention and renal impairment can lead to altered urinary flow and incomplete bladder emptying. The elongated male urethra further impedes complete drainage, fostering a warm, moist environment ideal for fungal growth. These conditions are compounded by the immune dysfunction common in heart failure patients [6, 7] and the increased urinary glucose excretion intrinsic to SGLT2i mechanism of action. Together, these elements create a perfect storm for genital fungal infections, which affect up to 9% of male SGLT2i users and lead to treatment discontinuation in approximately 1–2% of cases [8, 9].

The impact of this adverse effect extends beyond physical morbidity. The fear of developing genital infections can significantly erode patient confidence, fostering anxiety about the medication and potentially undermining the cardiovascular benefits of SGLT2i therapy. While the link between hygiene and infection risk is well-established in other contexts, targeted preventive strategies for male heart failure patients on dapagliflozin have been largely overlooked.

It is crucial to reinforce that the benefits of persevering with dapagliflozin are substantial. Beyond its primary role in heart failure, dapagliflozin confers significant cardiovascular and electrophysiological advantages, such as improving atrial electromechanics and reducing arrhythmia risk [10]. Furthermore, it provides robust renal protection, a cornerstone of HFrEF management, with a well-documented safety profile [11]. Ensuring uninterrupted therapy is key to securing these long-term benefits.

Therefore, this study proposes and evaluates a novel, simple intervention: structured post-urination external genital cleaning. We hypothesize that by directly mitigating a preventable and concerning side effect, this behavioral strategy can alleviate patient anxieties, reinforce belief in the treatment, and ultimately improve dapagliflozin adherence in male heart failure patients.

Materials and methods

Participants

A total of 1337 cases who attended the cardiology departments of Luzhou Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Affiliated Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine of Southwest Medical University, Suining Central Hospital, Wuhan Red Cross Hospital, Leshan Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Naxi Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, The Second People’s Hospital of Naxi District, Xuyong Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Xuyong People’s Hospital, Gulin Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, and Lu County Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, in a total of 11 hospitals, were chosen from August 2022 to August 2023 male patients with heart failure were studied.

This study was a multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial (RCT) with parallel-group design. Blinded outcome assessment was performed, where outcome assessors (dermatologists and cardiovascular physicians) were blinded to group allocation during fungal infection assessment and medication adherence evaluation. Patients who met the inclusion criteria were randomized into study groups after providing written informed consent. Randomization was performed using SPSS 22.0 software to generate patient visit numbers and corresponding treatment allocations. The envelope method was employed for allocation concealment, whereby randomly assigned numbers were placed in opaque, sealed envelopes. Patients were then allocated to groups by drawing numbers from these sealed envelopes.

A total of 673 participants were enrolled in the test group. During the study period, 2 participants were lost to follow-up, 2 died due to acute heart failure exacerbation, and 1 died from surgical complications, resulting in 668 completed cases. In the control group, 677 participants were initially enrolled. Among these, 3 were lost to follow-up, 3 died from acute heart failure exacerbation, and 2 died from surgical complications, yielding 669 completed cases.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

(1) Male patients aged 18–80 years with diagnosed heart failure; (2) established on guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) for ≥3 months, including dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily (AstraZeneca Pharmaceuticals, Inc.); (3) appropriate device therapy (ICD/CRT) when indicated according to current heart failure guidelines.

Exclusion criteria

(1) History of genital fungal infection within 1 year prior to enrollment; (2) prolonged systemic antibiotic therapy; (3) chronic systemic glucocorticoid therapy; (4) active malignancy; (5) unresolved obstructive urological conditions; (6) end-stage renal disease (ESRD); (7) cognitive disorders including Alzheimer’s disease; (8) Parkinson’s disease; (9) history of frequent urinary catheterization (≥3 times/year); (10) inability to provide informed consent; (11) participation in other clinical trials; (12) HbA1c > 8.5% for patients with comorbid diabetes mellitus.

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Luzhou Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. 2022-KY-002). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Interventions

The clinical study period was 1 year for both the control and test groups.

Patients in the control group received standard care according to the following protocol: (1) heart failure management in line with the 2021 ESC Heart Failure Guidelines, including the use of ACE inhibitors/ARBs, beta-blockers, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists; (2) diabetes management following the 2023 ADA Standards of Care; (3) patient education regarding dapagliflozin side effects and precautions according to manufacturer guidelines; (4) instruction to avoid excessive sweating; (5) guidance to change into clean, dry underwear once daily; (6) education on complete bladder emptying during urination; (7) explicit direction to refrain from cleaning the external genitalia after urination; and (8) a standardized management protocol for genital fungal infections, wherein patients would be referred to dermatology and venereology specialists for treatment, with decisions regarding dapagliflozin continuation during and after treatment determined through interdisciplinary consultation between cardiovascular and dermatology/venereology physicians, and all treatment recommendations and precautions thoroughly communicated to patients [12].

The test group received the same standard care as the control group, supplemented with a structured post-urination external genital cleansing procedure. This protocol required participants to use clean paper towels to wipe the genital area in the following sequence: glans penis, penile body and root, scrotum, and bilateral inguinal regions. A new paper towel was used for each anatomical area and discarded immediately after use. The procedure aimed to eliminate residual moisture from the genital area, thereby reducing fungal infection risk. Cleansing was considered effective when the external genitals, scrotal area, and bilateral inguinal regions were completely dry and free from any foreign body residue. Notably, no antiseptics or antifungal agents were employed during this process to avoid potential irritation or adverse effects.

All test group participants received standardized education sessions at enrollment, including demonstrations of proper cleansing technique. Adherence to the protocol was monitored through monthly follow-up visits using structured questionnaires and patient diaries, supplemented by monthly telephone calls from healthcare providers to reinforce proper technique and evaluate compliance.

The fundamental distinction between the study groups was the implementation of a systematic post-urination genital hygiene protocol in the test group, contrasting with the standard care instruction to avoid cleaning in the control group. This difference represents a paradigm shift from current practice and establishes a clear behavioral intervention distinction between the two groups.

Outcome measures

Incidence of genital fungal infections

In both groups, patients underwent monthly dermatological assessments. Genital fungal infections were evaluated based on a combination of clinical presentation and mycological laboratory tests [13]. Clinically, infections were identified through symptoms such as itching, erythema, and discomfort in the genital region. To confirm the diagnosis and exclude other potential causes, laboratory tests were performed, including microscopy of skin scrapings, fungal cultures, and polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assays for fungal DNA, which provided definitive evidence of fungal infection.

Patient medication beliefs

Patients’ beliefs about dapagliflozin were assessed by cardiovascular physicians using the Beliefs about Medicines Questionnaire-Specific (BMQ-Specific) before the study began and after the 1-year clinical period. The BMQ-Specific comprises two dimensions: beliefs about medication necessity and beliefs about medication concerns, reflecting the patient’s perceived benefit-risk balance of treatment. The questionnaire includes 10 items scored on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (“Strongly Disagree”) to 5 (“Strongly Agree”). Each dimension is scored between 5 and 25 points. The total score is calculated as the necessity score minus the concern score, ranging from −20 to 20. A positive total score indicates that the patient perceives the necessity of medication as outweighing their concerns, with higher scores reflecting stronger belief in the medication [14, 15].

Dapagliflozin medication adherence

Medication adherence to dapagliflozin was evaluated at the end of the 1-year study period using the 8-Item Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-8), administered by cardiovascular physicians in both groups. The MMAS-8 includes eight items that assess various medication-taking behaviors, such as forgetting doses, carelessness, and discontinuing medication when feeling better or worse. Items 1–7 are dichotomous (yes/no, scored as 0/1), and item 8 uses a 5-point Likert scale. Total scores range from 0 to 8, with scores below 6 indicating poor adherence and scores of 6 or above indicating good adherence [16, 17].

Statistical analysis

Data management and statistical analyses were conducted using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) and R (version 4.4.1). Descriptive statistics for quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median [P25, P75], depending on whether the data met parametric assumptions. Group comparisons were performed using t tests for normally distributed data and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for non-normally distributed data. For paired data, paired t tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were applied as appropriate.

Categorical data are summarized as frequencies and percentages (n, %). Differences between groups for categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as applicable. For ordinal or hierarchical data, the rank-sum test was used. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a significance level of α = 0.05 was adopted for hypothesis testing.

Confidence intervals (95% CI) were calculated for effect sizes, including risk ratios (RR) and Cohen’s d, to provide a range of values within which the true effect is expected to lie. These estimates help in assessing the clinical significance of the findings. Statistical significance was determined based on the P value and the corresponding confidence intervals.

Results

Patient flow and follow-up

Among the 1350 participants initially randomized, 1337 completed the study. In the test group (n = 673), five participants were excluded from analysis: two were lost to follow-up, two died from acute heart failure exacerbation, and one died from surgical complications, resulting in 668 completed cases. In the control group (n = 677), eight participants were excluded: three were lost to follow-up, three died from acute heart failure exacerbation, and two died from surgical complications, yielding 669 completed cases.

Baseline characteristics and comparability

A total of 1337 participants were included in the final analysis, with 669 (50.03%) in the control group and 668 (49.96%) in the test group. Baseline characteristics showed a mean age of 62.43 ± 12.61 years, with an obesity prevalence of 33.96%. Circumcision was reported in 4.94% of participants. At enrollment, the New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification was predominantly Class II (87.51%). Heart failure classification was primarily preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF, 45.50%), followed by reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF, 40.39%). Comorbidities included type 2 diabetes mellitus (34.18%), urinary tract obstructive disease (38.37%), and chronic renal insufficiency (17.13%). Comparability analysis demonstrated no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics between the control and test groups (P > 0.05 for all variables), confirming good baseline balance between the two groups. The effect size, calculated as the risk ratio (RR), was 0.24 (95% CI: 0.08–0.67), indicating a significant reduction in the incidence of genital fungal infections in the test group compared to the control group. Discussion

This study demonstrates that simple external genital cleansing after urination significantly reduces the incidence of genital fungal infections in male heart failure patients treated with dapagliflozin over a 1-year period. Genital fungal infections represent a well-established adverse effect of SGLT2i therapy, primarily attributable to increased urinary glucose excretion creating a favorable environment for fungal growth [18]. Consistent with previous research, our findings confirm that SGLT2i like dapagliflozin elevate the risk of genital infections, particularly in male diabetic patients [18]. Notably, these infections may present subclinically and remain undetectable without laboratory confirmation, yet still contribute to patient discomfort and medication concerns. In our investigation, we employed comprehensive clinical evaluation complemented by laboratory methods including microscopy and fungal cultures to detect both overt and subclinical infections.

The therapeutic benefits of dapagliflozin extend beyond glucose modulation to encompass significant cardiovascular and electrophysiological advantages. Emerging evidence indicates that SGLT2i improve atrial electromechanical function, thereby reducing arrhythmia risk and enhancing overall cardiac performance. As demonstrated by Ziyrek et al. [10], SGLT2i combined with metformin significantly improved P-wave indices and atrial electromechanics in type 2 diabetes patients. These findings substantiate the rationale for maintaining dapagliflozin therapy, not only for heart failure symptom control but also for cardiovascular protection, reinforcing the importance of treatment persistence.

Furthermore, dapagliflozin plays a pivotal role in renal protection, particularly crucial for HFrEF management. The renal safety profile of dapagliflozin has been extensively validated, with multiple studies confirming its kidney-protective properties despite systemic physiological alterations. Recent research by Tezcan and Büyükterzi [11] examining Ramadan fasting’s effects on blood pressure and renal function in hypertensive patients underscores how metabolic factors influence renal safety. These observations align with the nephroprotective effects observed in SGLT2i users, providing additional reassurance regarding long-term medication safety.

When interpreting our results, several methodological considerations warrant attention. The open-label design introduces potential performance bias, as participants were aware of their group assignment, which might have influenced behavioral patterns, particularly in the test group. Additionally, self-reported adherence to the cleansing protocol may be subject to recall bias. Although blinded outcome assessment mitigated observer bias, these limitations should be considered when generalizing our findings.

The generalizability of our results requires careful consideration. Conducted exclusively in Chinese male heart failure patients, our findings, while significant within this demographic, may not directly translate to populations with different ethnic or cultural backgrounds. Moreover, the 1-year follow-up period may be insufficient to capture long-term intervention effects on medication adherence and clinical outcomes. Future studies with extended follow-up durations and more diverse populations are necessary to establish broader applicability.

Preventing SGLT2i discontinuation remains a critical clinical priority, given that side effects like genital infections contribute significantly to treatment cessation. Our findings indicate that patient education emphasizing hygiene practices and the preventive role of external genital cleansing can effectively mitigate concerns regarding medication side effects, thereby improving adherence. The significantly higher medication adherence observed in the test group underscores the value of addressing patient concerns through structured education protocols.

An optimal patient education framework should comprehensively address dapagliflozin’s benefits, potential adverse effects, and the crucial role of hygiene in preventing genital fungal infections. By emphasizing these elements, healthcare providers can foster patient confidence in treatment regimens, ultimately enhancing adherence and clinical outcomes. Proactive management of genital infection concerns, as implemented in our study, can positively influence patients’ psychological attitudes toward medication, reducing side effect apprehension and promoting long-term adherence.

Medication adherence is particularly crucial in chronic conditions such as heart failure, especially for patients using SGLT2i. Existing evidence consistently demonstrates that medication beliefs substantially influence adherence behaviors. Ho et al. [14] established that hypertension patients’ beliefs in medication efficacy directly correlated with improved adherence and blood pressure control, suggesting similar mechanisms may operate in heart failure patients using dapagliflozin. Our results align with this concept, as genital fungal infections—frequently associated with embarrassment—markedly affected patients’ attitudes and adherence. Male patients who perceived themselves as capable of preventing infections through hygiene measures demonstrated stronger belief in medication benefits, resulting in improved adherence [19].

Anatomical characteristics of male genitalia, including urethral length and potential for incomplete urinary drainage, inherently predispose to infections. This vulnerability is compounded by the immune dysfunction prevalent in heart failure patients [18]. Additionally, heart failure patients frequently experience lower urinary tract symptoms such as overactive bladder, which promotes urinary retention and further elevates fungal infection risk [20]. These factors collectively underscore the importance of early intervention and consistent genital hygiene to prevent infections and maintain dapagliflozin adherence.

Other urological conditions, including urinary incontinence and detrusor muscle impairment, may contribute to residual urine accumulation, creating environments conducive to fungal proliferation. Comprehensive management of these conditions can substantially reduce genital fungal infection risk and improve overall health outcomes. Therapeutic approaches for lower urinary tract symptoms in heart failure patients require careful balancing of medications that might exacerbate these conditions against the necessity to prevent infections associated with residual urine [21]. Through promoting complete bladder emptying and maintaining genital hygiene, our intervention aimed to minimize fungal infection risk—which, if unaddressed, not only causes physical discomfort but also erodes treatment confidence. The apprehension surrounding genital infections may prompt therapy discontinuation or non-adherence, ultimately compromising dapagliflozin’s therapeutic benefits.

Recent investigations have further elucidated the association between SGLT2i and genital mycotic infections. Banerjee et al. [22] identified baseline predictors for these infections in men with type 2 diabetes, while Yang et al. [23] established a broader epidemiological link through analysis of the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System. Kumar et al. [24] documented severe infections including Candida tropicalis fungemia, highlighting the necessity for vigilant fungal infection monitoring in SGLT2i users. In our cohort, the test group exhibited significantly reduced medication concerns and stronger necessity beliefs regarding dapagliflozin, ultimately translating to superior medication adherence compared to controls.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the single-blind design may introduce performance bias as participants were aware of their group allocation. Second, compliance with the cleaning intervention was assessed through self-report, which may be subject to recall bias. Third, the study was conducted exclusively in Chinese male patients, potentially limiting generalizability to other populations. Fourth, the 1-year follow-up period may not capture long-term adherence patterns. Fifth, we did not assess the economic impact or quality of life measures related to the intervention. Finally, the difference between intervention and control groups may appear modest, requiring larger studies to confirm clinical significance.

Conclusion

Active self-initiated external genital cleaning post-urination significantly improves medication adherence to dapagliflozin among male heart failure patients. This simple intervention demonstrates effectiveness in reducing genital fungal infections, alleviating medication concerns, and strengthening medication beliefs. Given its feasibility, cost-effectiveness, and clinical efficacy, this practice warrants widespread implementation in clinical settings.

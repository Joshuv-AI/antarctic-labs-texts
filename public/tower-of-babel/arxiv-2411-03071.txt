Abstract
The celebrated Morris counter uses log 2 ⁡ log 2 ​ n + O ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ σ − 1 ) \log_{2}\log_{2}n+O(\log_{2}\sigma^{-1}) bits to count up to n n with a relative error σ \sigma , where if λ ^ \hat{\lambda} is the estimate of the current count λ \lambda , then 𝔼 ​ | λ ^ − λ | 2 < σ 2 ​ λ 2 \mathbb{E}|\hat{\lambda}-\lambda|^{2}<\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2} . The Morris counter was proved to be optimal in space complexity by Nelson and Yu [ 14 ] , even when considering the error tails. A natural generalization is multi-dimensional approximate counting. Let d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 be the dimension. The count vector x ∈ ℕ d x\in\mathbb{N}^{d} is incremented entry-wisely over a stream of coordinates ( w 1 , … , w n ) ∈ [ d ] n (w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in[d]^{n} , where upon receiving w k ∈ [ d ] w_{k}\in[d] , x w k ← x w k + 1 x_{w_{k}}\leftarrow x_{w_{k}}+1 . A d d -dimensional approximate counter is required to count d d coordinates simultaneously and return an estimate x ^ \hat{x} of the count vector x x . Aden-Ali, Han, Nelson, and Yu [ 1 ] showed that the trivial solution of using d d Morris counters that track d d coordinates separately is already optimal in space, if each entry only allows error relative to itself , i.e., 𝔼 ​ | x ^ j − x j | 2 < σ 2 ​ | x j | 2 \mathbb{E}|\hat{x}_{j}-x_{j}|^{2}<\sigma^{2}|x_{j}|^{2} for each j ∈ [ d ] j\in[d] . However, for another natural error metric—the Euclidean mean squared error 𝔼 ​ | x ^ − x | 2 \mathbb{E}|\hat{x}-x|^{2} —we show that using d d separate Morris counters is sub-optimal. In this work, we present a simple and optimal d d -dimensional counter with Euclidean relative error σ \sigma , i.e., 𝔼 ​ | x ^ − x | 2 < σ 2 ​ | x | 2 \mathbb{E}|\hat{x}-x|^{2}<\sigma^{2}|x|^{2} where | x | = ∑ j = 1 d x j 2 |x|=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2}} , with a matching lower bound. We prove the following. • There exists a ( log 2 ⁡ log 2 ​ n + O ⁡ ( d ​ log 2 ​ σ − 1 ) ) (\log_{2}\log_{2}n+O(d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1})) -bit d d -dimensional counter with relative error σ \sigma . • Any d d -dimensional counter with relative error σ \sigma takes at least ( log 2 ⁡ log 2 ​ n + Ω ⁡ ( d ​ log 2 ​ σ − 1 ) ) (\log_{2}\log_{2}n+\Omega(d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1})) bits of space. The upper and lower bounds are proved with ideas that are strikingly simple. The upper bound is constructed with a certain variable-length integer encoding and the lower bound is derived from a straightforward volumetric estimation of sphere covering.

1.1
Technical Overview

1.1.1
Upper Bound

The upper bound in Theorem
1
uses the natural idea of maintaining a common
scale counter

U
for all
d
dimensions and tracking the relative magnitude of each coordinate with a vector
V\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. The estimate
\hat{x}
is equal to
2^{U}V
. For demonstration, we will first describe a very simple but sub-optimal solution and then discuss how to modify it to reach optimal space-accuracy tradeoff. The transition of the states is designed similarly with the design of the Morris counter, in which the estimate is maintained
unbiased
at any moment (
\mathbb{E}2^{U}V=x
). Initially we set
U=0
and
V=(0,\ldots,0)
.

•

When the
j
th coordinate is incremented (i.e.,
x_{j}\leftarrow x_{j}+1
), we update
V_{j}\leftarrow V_{j}+1
with probability
2^{-U}
, ensuring the unbiasedness of the estimate.

•

Each coordinate
V_{j}
is restricted to the range
[0,a]
, so that the space to store
V
is
d\log_{2}(1+a)
. Whenever some
V_{j}
exceeds
a
, we have to
scale up
to keep
V_{j}
in range. Again, we want to maintain the property that
\mathbb{E}2^{U}V=x
. Thus, the scale-up should be done as follows.

–

U\leftarrow U+1
. The scale counter is increased by 1.

–

For the estimate to be unbiased,
V_{j}
should become
V_{j}/2
for every
j
. Here we also want
V_{j}
to always be an integer. Therefore, if
V_{j}
is even, then
V_{j}\leftarrow V_{j}/2
. If
V_{j}
is odd, then
V_{j}\leftarrow(V_{j}+\xi)/2
where
\xi\in\{-1,1\}
is a freshly sampled Rademacher random variable.

The algorithm above is unbiased by design. However, the memory-accuracy tradeoff is
not
optimal. There is a fundamental problem of the design above that the parameter
a
has to be
\Omega(\sqrt{d})
(with
\sigma=O(1)
) for it to be a
d
-dimensional counter. Instead of analyzing the algorithm in details, this problem can be identified by simply checking the set of
all possible estimates
that are produced by the algorithm:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\{2^{U}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}):U\in\mathbb{N},\forall j\in[d],V_{j}\in[0,a]\}.

It turns out that
\mathcal{D}
is not
dense enough
for estimating some input
x
. Indeed, we choose

\displaystyle x=2^{s}(\underbrace{a,\ldots,a}_{r},\underbrace{1/2,\ldots,1/2}_{d-r}),

for some
r\in[0,d]
so that both
y_{1}=2^{s}(a,\ldots,a,1,\ldots,1)
and
y_{2}=2^{s-1}(a,\ldots,a,1,\ldots,1)
are far from
x
. By construction, the nearest estimate of
x
in
\mathcal{D}
has to be either
y_{1}
or
y_{2}
.
3
3

3

Note that
y_{1}
is one possible probabilistic rounding of
x
. Other roundings
\{2^{s}(a,\ldots,a,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d-r})\mid z_{j}\in\{0,1\}\}
all have the same distance to
x
. By symmetry, we only need to consider
y_{1}
. The vector
y_{2}
is produced by decreasing the scale to
2^{s-1}
, so the smaller entries can be represented precisely with the cost of underestimating the large entries.
We choose
r
so that the two choices have equal distances to
x
. A simple calculation shows that we need to set

r=d/(1+4a^{2})
.
4
4

4

For simplicity, we only require
r>1
and omit the details of rounding
r
to an integer.
Assume now
a<\sqrt{d}/3
and then
r>1
. Thus
\min_{y\in\mathcal{D}}|x-y|^{2}=2^{2s}\frac{da^{2}}{1+4a^{2}}
, while
|x|^{2}=2^{2s}\cdot 2a^{2}d/(1+4a^{2})
. Therefore, if the algorithm is an
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter, then
\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}-x|^{2}<\sigma^{2}|x|^{2}
, which means there is at least one
x_{*}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
|x_{*}-x|^{2}<\sigma^{2}|x|^{2}
. This suggests that
2^{2s}a^{2}d/(1+4a^{2})<\sigma^{2}2^{2s}\cdot 2a^{2}d/(1+4a^{2})
and therefore
\sigma>1/\sqrt{2}
. In other words, if
a<\sqrt{d}/3
, then any
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter must have
\sigma>1/\sqrt{2}
. We conclude that
a
needs to be
\Omega(\sqrt{d})
when
\sigma\leq 1/\sqrt{2}
, indicating an additional
O(d\log d)
space overhead in comparison to the optimal space.

From the discussion above, we see that one has to have a dense enough set of estimates
\mathcal{D}
for a correct
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter.
Roughly speaking, one wants to spend more bits on large coordinates and fewer bits on small coordinates. This can be done with a simple and natural algorithmic idea:
variable-length integer encoding
. The usual binary encoding of non-negative integers automatically uses more bits on large numbers and fewer bits on small numbers. We will prove the following set of estimates is dense enough.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\text{compressed}}=\{2^{U}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}):U\in\mathbb{N},\text{the encoding length of $V$ is at most $O(d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1})$}\},

This leads to our upper bound in Theorem
1
. One may observe how this encoding idea better handles the previous counterexample
x=2^{s}(a,\ldots,a,1/2,\ldots,1/2)
. An estimate of
2^{s-1}(2a,\ldots,2a,1,\ldots,1)
can now be returned because though each entry with value
2a
needs above average space to encode, each entry with value
1
uses less than average space to encode,
saving the memory space for the large entries
.

1.1.2
Lower Bound

As we have discussed above, the set of all estimates produced by the counter has to be dense enough for the task of
d
-dimensional counting to be possible.
The lower bound in Theorem
1
thus arises from estimating the size of
multiplicative space coverings
.

Definition 2

(multiplicative space covering)
.

For any subsets
A,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma>0
, we say
R
is a
\sigma
-multiplicative covering
of
A
if for any
x\in A
there exists
y\in R
such that
|x-y|<\sigma|x|
.

Clearly, the set of all estimates of an
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter will form a
\sigma
-multiplicative space covering of
\mathcal{X}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\mid x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}\leq n\}
. The lower bound is thus proved by estimating the covering size of
\mathcal{X}
, which can be further reduced to the classic problem of covering spheres with smaller spheres
[
16
]
.

Related Work

There have been many variants and analysis of Morris counters before
[
10
,
11
,
8
,
7
,
14
]
. Nevertheless, they have only considered the one-dimensional case. Tracking a multi-dimensional count vector (with a vector norm) is a new problem to the best of our knowledge.

In the one-dimensional case (

), our algorithm is equivalent to the variant—

—by Csűrös
[

]
, except for that the floating-point counter restricts

can be simulated by generating random bits sequentially, looking for

Proof.

Fix any input sequence
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in[d]^{n}
.
Let
(U,V)
be the final state of the algorithm and
x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{w_{j}}
be the final count vector. Define
\hat{x}=2^{U}V
. If the memory budget is
M_{*}=4d+2d\log_{2}(1+a)
with
a\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=x,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}-x|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{6a-2}|x|^{2}.

Furthermore for any
r\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(U\geq r+\log_{2}\left(\frac{n}{ad}+1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-r}.

Proof.

\mathbb{E}\hat{x}=x
is true by construction, since both
Increment
and
Scale-up
are designed to keep
\hat{x}=2^{U}V
unbiased. The challenge is to analyze the variance
\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}-x|^{2}
. We start by proving the following lemma that bounds the final variance by the distributional properties of the scale counter
U
at time
k=1,\ldots,n
.

Lemma 2

(variance decomposition)
.

For any
k\leq n
, let
(U^{(k)},V^{(k)})
be the memory state at time
k
(i.e., after seeing
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
) and
x^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{w_{j}}
be the count vector at time
k
. Let
\hat{x}^{(k)}=2^{U^{(k)}}V^{(k)}
to be the estimate at time
k
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}|^{2}-|x^{(n)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}(2^{U^{(k)}}-1)+\frac{d}{3}\mathbb{E}(2^{2U^{(n)}}-1).

Proof.

We define
Y^{(k)}
to be the intermediate vector after
Increment
before
Scale-up
at time
k
. By construction, we know
Y^{(k)}=V^{(k)}+e_{w_{k+1}}
with probability
2^{-U^{(k)}}
; otherwise
Y^{(k)}=V^{(k)}
. For simplicity, we now fix
k\geq 0
and write
U^{(k)},V^{(k)},x^{(k)},w_{k+1},Y^{(k)}
as
U,V,x,w,Y
and
U^{(k+1)},V^{(k+1)},x^{(k+1)}
as
U^{\prime},V^{\prime},x^{\prime}
respectively. By the definition of
Increment
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|2^{U}Y|^{2}-|2^{U}V|^{2}\mid U,V)

\displaystyle=2^{-U}\cdot 2^{2U}(2V_{w}+1)=2^{U}(2V_{w}+1).

(1)

Now if
\psi(Y)\leq M_{*}
, then we have
(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})=(U,Y)
where no
Scale-up
is executed. If
\psi(Y)>M_{*}
, then we must have
\psi(Y)=M_{*}+1
because by Lemma
1
,
\psi(Y)\leq\psi(V+e_{w})\leq\psi(V)+1
and
\psi(V)\leq M_{*}
. By construction,
M_{*}\geq 3d
and thus there exists
j\in[d]
such that
Y_{j}\geq 3
. By Lemma
1
, we know
\psi(\lceil Y/2\rceil)\leq\psi(Y)-1=M_{*}
. Therefore, after the
Scale-up
step, the space will be at most
M_{*}
. When
Scale-up
is executed,
U^{\prime}=U+1
and
V^{\prime}=Y/2+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\xi_{k}\mathbbm{1}\left[{2\nmid Y_{k}}\right]
. Here,
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}
are i.i.d. Rademacher random variables. Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|2^{U^{\prime}}V^{\prime}|^{2}-|2^{U}Y|^{2}\mid U,Y)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y)>M_{*}}\right]\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(2^{2U+2}\left(\frac{Y_{k}+\xi_{k}\mathbbm{1}\left[{2\nmid Y_{k}}\right]}{2}\right)^{2}-2^{2U}Y_{k}^{2}\right)\;\middle|\;U,Y\right],

note that
\mathbb{E}\xi_{k}=0
and
\xi_{k}^{2}=1

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y)>M_{*}}\right]\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(2^{2U}(Y_{k}^{2}+\mathbbm{1}\left[{2\nmid Y_{k}}\right])-2^{2U}Y_{k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y)>M_{*}}\right]2^{2U}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathbbm{1}\left[{2\nmid Y_{k}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y)>M_{*}}\right]2^{2U}\cdot d.

(2)

Take the total expectation and by Equation (
1
) and (
2
) we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|2^{U^{\prime}}V^{\prime}|^{2}-|2^{U}V|^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}(|2^{U}Y|^{2}-|2^{U}V|^{2}\mid U,V)+\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}(|2^{U^{\prime}}V^{\prime}|^{2}-|2^{U}Y|^{2}\mid U,Y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(2^{U}(2V_{w}+1))+\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y)>M_{*}}\right]2^{2U}d).

(3)

Recall that
|x^{\prime}|^{2}-|x|^{2}=(x_{w}+1)^{2}-x_{w}^{2}=2x_{w}+1
and
\mathbb{E}2^{U}V_{w}=x_{w}
. This implies
\mathbb{E}2^{U}V_{w}=(|x^{\prime}|^{2}-|x|^{2}-1)/2
. Thus by Equation (
3
) we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|2^{U^{\prime}}V^{\prime}|^{2}-|2^{U}V|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq|x^{\prime}|^{2}-|x|^{2}+\mathbb{E}(2^{U}-1)+\mathbb{E}(d2^{2U}\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y)>M_{*}}\right]).

(4)

Sum up the differences and we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|2^{U^{(n)}}V^{(n)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}(|2^{U^{(k+1)}}V^{(k+1)}|^{2}-|2^{U^{(k)}}V^{(k)}|^{2})

Recall that
U^{\prime},V^{\prime},U,V
are short for
U^{(k+1)},V^{(k+1)},U^{(k)},V^{(k)}
. By Equation (
4
),

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(|x^{(k+1)}|^{2}-|x^{(k)}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}(2^{U^{(k)}}-1)+\mathbb{E}\left(d2^{2U^{(k)}}\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y^{(k)})>M_{*}}\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=|x^{(n)}|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}(2^{U^{(k)}}-1)+d\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{2U^{(k)}}\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y^{(k)})>M_{*}}\right]\right].

Note that
\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y^{(k)})>M_{*}}\right]
is the indicator whether
Scale-up
is executed when processing
w_{k+1}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{2U^{(k)}}\mathbbm{1}\left[{\psi(Y^{(k)})>M_{*}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{U^{(n)}-1}2^{2j}=\frac{4^{U^{(n)}}-1}{4-1}=\frac{2^{2U^{(n)}}-1}{3}

We conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|2^{U^{(n)}}V^{(n)}|^{2}-|x^{(n)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}(2^{U^{(k)}}-1)+\frac{d}{3}\mathbb{E}(2^{2U^{(n)}}-1),

which gives the stated variance decomposition.
∎

The next step is to estimate
\mathbb{E}(2^{U}-1)
and
\mathbb{E}(2^{2U}-1)
. The idea is to relate them with
\mathbb{E}2^{U}V=x
and
\mathbb{E}|2^{U}V|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}|^{2}

Lemma 3
.

If
U\geq 1
, then
|V|^{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}\geq ad
.

Proof.

Since

. Thus it suffices to prove
\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}\geq ad
. Note that if
U\geq 1
, then the algorithm has executed at least one

. Note that by Lemma

, the space
\psi(V)
increases at most by one per

and decreases at most by

per

. Therefore, after the first

, the memory usage
\psi(V)
is between

and
M_{*}
. Note that
M_{*}
is set to

. Thus we have

\displaystyle\psi(V)

\displaystyle\geq M_{*}+1-2d\geq 2d+d\log_{2}(1+a).

On the other hand, by Lemma
1
(encoding space bound), we have

\displaystyle\psi(V)

\displaystyle\leq 2d+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log_{2}(1+V_{j}).

The bounds above imply that
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log_{2}(1+V_{j})\geq d\log_{2}(1+a)
. Since
\log_{2}(1+r)
is convex in

, we conclude that

\displaystyle d\log_{2}(1+a)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log_{2}(1+V_{j})\leq d\log_{2}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}/d\right),

and thus
\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}\geq ad
.
∎

Note that if
U=0
, then
2^{U}-1=0
.
With the lemma above, we now have

\displaystyle ad\mathbb{E}(2^{U}-1)\leq\mathbb{E}(2^{U}-1)\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}\leq\mathbb{E}2^{U}\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}=k,

where
k
is the current time, i.e., the total number of increments so far. This implies
\mathbb{E}(2^{U}-1)\leq k/(ad)
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle ad\mathbb{E}(2^{2U}-1)\leq\mathbb{E}(2^{2U}-1)\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}2^{2U}\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}^{2}=\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}|^{2},

which implies
\mathbb{E}(2^{2U}-1)\leq\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}|^{2}/(ad)
. Inserting the estimates back into Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}|^{2}-|x^{(n)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}(2^{U^{(k)}}-1)+\frac{d}{3}\mathbb{E}(2^{2U^{(n)}}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}k/(ad)+\frac{d}{3}\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}|^{2}/(ad)

Note that
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}k\leq n^{2}/2\leq d|x^{(n)}|^{2}/2
since
\sum_{j=1}^{d}x^{(n)}_{j}=n
and
d|x^{(n)}|^{2}\geq(\sum_{j=1}^{d}x^{(n)}_{j})^{2}
.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2a}|x^{(n)}|^{2}+\frac{1}{3a}\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}|^{2}.

Reorganizing the inequality above, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}|^{2}-|x^{(n)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{6a-2}|x^{(n)}|^{2}.

Note that since the estimate is unbiased, we have
\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}|^{2}-|x^{(n)}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}-x^{(n)}|^{2}
. Thus we conclude that
\mathbb{E}|\hat{x}^{(n)}-x^{(n)}|^{2}\leq\frac{5}{6a-2}|x^{(n)}|^{2}
.

Finally we estimate the tail of
U
. We already know that
\mathbb{E}(2^{U^{(k)}}-1)\leq k/(ad)
and thus
\mathbb{E}2^{U^{(k)}}\leq 1+k/(ad)
. Then for any
z>0
, by Markov,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(2^{U^{(k)}}\geq z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}2^{U^{(k)}}}{z}\leq\left(\frac{k}{ad}+1\right)/z

Setting
z=2^{r}(k/(ad)+1)
and we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(U^{(k)}\geq r+\log_{2}\left(\frac{k}{ad}+1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-r}.

We thus have proved the tail bound for
U
.
∎

Theorem
2
characterizes the mean and variance of the estimator. We now analyze the space. The vector

is encoded with length at most
M_{*}
by construction. We are left to analyze the memory space needed for the scale counter

.

Corollary 1
.

For
\sigma\in(0,1/3)
, there exists an
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter with state space size
\log_{2}|\Omega|=\log_{2}\log_{2}n+O(d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1})
.

Proof.

If
n\leq\sigma^{-1}
, we may use
d
deterministic counters taking
d\log_{2}n\leq d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1}
space which surely form a
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter. We thus assume
n>\sigma^{-1}
.

With a fixed space budget for storing the scale counter
U
, there is a chance that
U
grows too large to be stored.
In such case, the algorithm simply enters a fail state and returns
(0,\ldots,0)
upon query. For
\tau>0
, we store
U
only up to
U_{*}=\log_{2}(\tau^{-1})+\log_{2}(n/(ad)+1)
. By design
U
is non-decreasing in time and it suffices to analyze
U
at the end of the stream. By Theorem
2
, we know
\mathbb{P}(U\geq U_{*})\leq\tau.
The estimate
\hat{x}
can be written as
\mathbbm{1}\left[{U<U_{*}}\right]2^{U}V
. Its mean squared error is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\mathbbm{1}\left[{U<U_{*}}\right]2^{U}V-x\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}\left[{U<U_{*}}\right]|2^{U}V-x|^{2}+\mathbbm{1}\left[{U\geq U_{*}}\right]|x|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|2^{U}V-x|^{2}+\mathbb{P}(U\geq U_{*})|x|^{2}

Note that
\mathbb{E}|2^{U}V-x|^{2}\leq\frac{5}{6a-2}|x|^{2}
by Theorem
2
and
\mathbb{P}(U\geq U_{*})\leq\tau
by construction.

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{5}{6a-2}+\tau\right)|x|^{2}

We set
\tau=\sigma^{2}/2
and

and have

\displaystyle\frac{5}{6a-2}+\tau=\frac{5}{12\sigma^{-2}-2}+\sigma^{2}/2\leq\sigma^{2},

since
\sigma<1
. In this setting, we have
\mathbb{E}|\mathbbm{1}\left[{U<U_{*}}\right]2^{U}V-x|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}|x|^{2}
which implies the algorithm is an
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter. We now compute its space usage. By construction, the space used by
U
is at most
\log_{2}(\log_{2}(\tau^{-1})+\log_{2}(n/(da)+1)))\leq\log_{2}\log_{2}n+O(1)
bits since we assumed

. The space used by the vector

is by construction

. Note that
V
is encoded with a alphabet set of size

(
\{0,1,|\}
) and

is the maximum number of symbols. One may encode

using bits by adding an additional

factor to the leading constant. We conclude that the total space needed to store

is
\log_{2}\log_{2}n+O(d\log\sigma^{-1})
.
∎

3
Lower Bound: Multiplicative Space Covering

Recall that by Definition
2
,
for any subset
A,R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma>0
, we say
R
is an
\sigma
-multiplicative covering of
A
if for any

there exists

such that
|x-y|<\sigma|x|
.

We now prove that any correct
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter will induce an
O(\sigma)
-multiplicative covering of the space.
The proof here will be slightly more complicated than what we described in §
1.1.2
. Previously we assumed the algorithm returns a fixed estimate for a given state and therefore the set of estimates naturally form a covering. In general, a probabilistic algorithm is allowed to produce a randomized answer even if the final memory state is fixed. We show that even when estimates are randomized for a given state, an
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter will still induce a covering of the space.

Lemma 4
.

If
\Omega
is the state space of an
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter with
\sigma<1/3
, then there exists a
4\sigma
-multiplicative covering
R
of
\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\mid\sqrt{d}\sigma^{-1}\leq|x|\leq n/\sqrt{d}\}
with
|R|\leq|\Omega|
.

Proof.

Let
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the randomized function that returns the estimate
\hat{x}=g(S)
based on the final state
S\in\Omega
. Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\mid\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}\leq n\}
be the space of all possible count vectors. By definition of the
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter, for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|g(S)-x|^{2}<\sigma^{2}|x|^{2}.

For any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, define

. Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|g(s_{x})-x|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|g(S)-x|^{2}<\sigma^{2}|x|^{2}.

(5)

This implies
\mathbb{E}|g(s_{x})-x|\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|g(s_{x})-x|^{2}}<\sigma|x|
.
Suppose for some

with

. By using the same randomness for

and

, we have

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle=|g(s_{x})-x-(g(s_{y})-y)|\leq|g(s_{x})-x|+|(g(s_{y})-y)|

(6)

By Equation (
5
), we know
\mathbb{E}|g(s_{x})-x|<\sigma|x|^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}|g(s_{y})-y|<\sigma|y|^{2}
. Take expectation on both sides of Equation (

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|g(s_{x})-x|+\mathbb{E}|(g(s_{y})-y)|<\sigma(|x|+|y|).

(7)

We now group the count vectors

by the value

. For any

according to the lexicographic order. Next we are going to show that

For any

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lfloor x_{j}\rfloor\leq\sqrt{d}|\lfloor x\rfloor|\leq\sqrt{d}|x|.

If

. Set

\displaystyle|y-\lfloor x\rfloor|<\sigma(|y|+|\lfloor x\rfloor|)\leq\sigma(|\lfloor x\rfloor|+|y-\lfloor x\rfloor|+|\lfloor x\rfloor|)

\displaystyle|y-\lfloor x\rfloor|<\frac{2\sigma}{1-\sigma}|\lfloor x\rfloor|.

Thus we have

\displaystyle|y-x|

\displaystyle\leq|y-\lfloor x\rfloor|+|x-\lfloor x\rfloor|

\displaystyle<\frac{2\sigma}{1-\sigma}|\lfloor x\rfloor|+\sqrt{d}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sigma}{1-\sigma}|x|+\sqrt{d},

If

. Thus we have

\displaystyle|y-x|<\frac{2\sigma}{1-\sigma}|x|+\sigma|x|=\frac{3\sigma-\sigma^{2}}{1-\sigma}|x|.

Since

, we have
\frac{3\sigma-\sigma^{2}}{1-\sigma}\leq 4\sigma.

Thus

. Note that the above argument holds for any

. Therefore

-multiplicative cover of

.

b_{k}(1+\sigma)<b_{k+1}(1-\sigma)
since
\sigma<1/3
. Therefore
R_{k}
s are disjoint for different
k
s. By construction, if
b_{k}\in[\alpha,\beta]
, then
R_{k}
covers
b_{k}\mathbb{S}_{+}^{d-1}
. By Lemma
5
, we know
|R_{k}|\geq 2^{-d}\sigma^{-(d-1)}
(the
multiplicative
covering of a sphere does not depend on the radius.) Thus

\displaystyle|R|\geq\sum_{k:b_{k}\in[\alpha,\beta]}|R_{k}|\geq 2^{-d}\sigma^{-(d-1)}\sum_{k:b_{k}\in[\alpha,\beta]}1,

and we only need to count the number of
b_{k}
s that are in
[\alpha,\beta]
.
Note that

\displaystyle b_{k}\in[\alpha,\beta]\iff k\log(1+3\sigma)\in[\log\alpha,\log\beta]\iff k\in[\frac{\log\alpha}{\log(1+3\sigma)},\frac{\log\beta}{\log(1+3\sigma)}].

We conclude that

\displaystyle|R|

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-d}\sigma^{-(d-1)}(\frac{\log\beta-\log\alpha}{\log(1+3\sigma)}-1)

relaxing
1+3\sigma<e^{3\sigma}
and
1\leq 1/(3\sigma)

\displaystyle>2^{-d}\sigma^{-(d-1)}(\frac{1}{3\sigma}\log(\beta/\alpha)-\frac{1}{3\sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}2^{-d}\sigma^{-d}\log(\beta/(e\alpha)),

which lower bounds the size of
\sigma
-multiplicative covers of
[\alpha,\beta]\mathbb{S}_{+}^{d-1}
.
∎

We now finish the proof of the space lower bound of
(n,d,\sigma)
-counters.

Corollary 3
.

If
\Omega
is the state space of an
(n,d,\sigma)
-counter with
\sigma<1/3
,
then

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\frac{1}{3}2^{-d}(4\sigma)^{-d}\log(n/(e\sigma^{-1}d)).

This implies that for
n\geq e^{2}
,
d\geq 1
, and
\sigma<1/16
,

\displaystyle\log_{2}|\Omega|=\log_{2}\log_{2}n+\Omega(d\log\sigma^{-1}).

Proof.

By Lemma
4
, we know there exists a
4\sigma
-multiplicative covering
R
of
[\sqrt{d}\sigma^{-1},n/\sqrt{d}]
with
|R|\leq|\Omega|
. By Corollary
2
, we know
|R|\geq\frac{1}{3}2^{-d}(4\sigma)^{-d}\log(n/(ed\sigma^{-1}))
. This proves the first statement. For the second statement, note that

\displaystyle\log_{2}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\geq d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1}-3d-\log_{2}3+\log_{2}\log(n/(e\sigma^{-1}d)).

If
\sigma<1/16
then
\log_{2}\sigma^{-1}>4
and thus
3\leq\frac{3}{4}\log_{2}\sigma^{-1}
. This implies that
d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1}-3d\geq\frac{1}{4}d\log_{2}\sigma^{-1}
. Furthermore, if
\sigma^{-1}\geq n^{1/6}
or
d\geq n^{1/6}
, then
\log_{2}\log_{2}n=o(d\log\sigma^{-1})
. Otherwise, we have
n/(d\sigma^{-1})\geq n^{1/3}
. Note that
e\leq n^{1/2}
by assumption. Thus we have
\log_{2}\log(n/(e\sigma^{-1}d))\geq\log_{2}\log n^{1/6}=\log_{2}\log_{2}n+\log_{2}(\frac{1}{6}\log 2)
. We conclude that

\displaystyle\log_{2}|\Omega|

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\log_{2}n+\Omega(d\log\sigma^{-1})-O(1)

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\log_{2}n+\Omega(d\log\sigma^{-1}),

when
n\geq e^{2}
,
d\geq 1
, and
\sigma<1/16
.
∎

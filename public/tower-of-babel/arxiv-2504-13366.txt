Lattice cohomology and the embedded topological type of plane curve singularities Thanks: The authors are partially supported by NKFIH Grant “Élvonal (Frontier)” KKP 144148.
Alexander A. Kubasch, Gergő Schefler

Abstract
. Analytic lattice cohomology is a new invariant of reduced curve singularities. In the case of plane curves, it is an algebro-geometric analogue of Heegaard Floer Link homology. However, by the rigidity of the analytic structure, lattice cohomology can be naturally defined in higher codimensions as well. In this paper we show that in the case of irreducible plane curve singularities the lattice cohomology is a complete embedded topological invariant. We also compare it to the integral Seifert form in the case of multiple branches.

1.2.
Curve singularities

The study of curve singularities, particularly in the complex plane, is a crucial area within algebraic geometry. Initially driven by algebraic and analytic tools, its understanding has been enriched by low-dimensional topology. This interdisciplinary approach has yielded a wealth of algebraic and topological invariants and deep insights. More recently invariants like the HOMFLY polynomial, HF homology and Khovanov homology have further propelled this field. This paper fits into this context by studying the lattice cohomology of curve singularities, a complex analytic analogue of the HF theory which works in higher codimensions as well.

1.3.
Basic structure of lattice cohomology

The lattice cohomology module of an isolated curve singularity
(C,o)
is a bigraded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module denoted by
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
. It admits a
cohomological grading

\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)=\bigoplus_{q}\mathbb{H}^{q}(C,o)
and a
weight grading

\mathbb{H}^{q}(C,o)=\bigoplus_{2n}\mathbb{H}^{q}_{2n}(C,o)
. This latter grading is
even
in order to emphasize the compatibility with the HFL theory. If
(C,o)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(C_{i},o)
has
r
irreducible components, then
\mathbb{H}^{q}(C,o)=0
for
q\geq r
. Moreover,
\mathbb{H}^{*}_{2n}(C,o)=0
for
n\ll 0

[
AN23
]
and
\mathbb{H}^{*}_{2n}(C,o)=\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}(C,o)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
for
n>0

[
KNS24b
]
. The Euler characteristic of
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
is the delta invariant
\delta(C,o)

[
AN23
]
and
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
determines wether or not
(C,o)
is Gorenstein
[
KNS24b
]
.

1.4.
Embedded topological type and other invariants

For planar germs, lattice cohomology is an embedded topological invariant and in this case
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
determines the multiplicity
m(C,o)

[
KNS24a
,
KNS24b
]
. It is natural to ask how
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
is related to other topological invariants.

Theorem 1.4.1
.

[Theorem
3.1.1
]

Let
(C,o)
be an irreducible plane curve singularity. Then
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)=\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
is a complete embedded topological invariant.

Even more, in subsection
3.8
we give an explicit algorithm for reconstructing the semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
from the graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module structure of
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
. The proof of this statement (together with the necessary preliminaries) takes up the majority of this article. It is based on the technique of ‘local minima’ developed in
[
KNS24a
,
KNS24b
]
.

We also present examples of plane curve singularities
(C,o)
with multiple branches showing that
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
and the Seifert form
S(C,o)
do not determine each other. It follows, in particular, that in this case the lattice cohomology module is not a complete embedded topological invariant. Note however that there exists an enhanced version of this theory, the
filtered lattice homology of curve singularities
, which
is
a complete topological invariant even in this case
[
N23
]
.

The multivariate Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{C,o}(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
is a complete embedded topological invariant of plane curve singularities
[
Y84
]
. In particular, it determines
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
. In the final section of this paper we present an example that this is no longer the case for non-planar curves (where the Alexander polynomial is replaced by the multivariate Poincaré series).

1.5.
Structure of the paper.

In section 2 we give a brief overview of the analytic lattice cohomology of curve singularities. Section 3 is devoted to proving the main theorem. We introduce the required machinery and give several examples. In section 4 we compare the analytic lattice cohomology with the Seifert form of plane curve singularities. In section 5 we present its relation with the multivariable Poincaré series in the non-planar case.

2.
Lattice cohomology of curve singularities

2.1.

In this section we give a
very
brief description of the analytic lattice cohomology of curve singularities introduced in
[
AN23
]
. For more details on the theory see
[
AN23
,
N23
,
KNS24a
,
KNS24b
]
.

Let
(C,o)
be a reduced complex curve singularity and
(C,o)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(C_{i},o)
its irreducible decomposition . Denote the local algebras by
\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}_{C,o}
and
\mathcal{O}_{i}:=\mathcal{O}_{C_{i},o}
respectively. The integral closure of
\mathcal{O}
is
\overline{\mathcal{O}}=\bigoplus_{i}\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\cong\bigoplus_{i}{\mathbb{C}}\{t_{i}\}
and the inclusions (i.e. the normalization maps)
n_{i}^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{i}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
bring about the valuations

\mathfrak{v}_{i}:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}_{i}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\ \ ;\quad f\mapsto\text{ord}_{t_{i}}(n_{i}^{*}f).

They induce the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
-filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{O}
given by
\mathcal{F}(\ell)=\{\,f\in\mathcal{O}\ |\ \mathfrak{v}_{i}(f)\geq\ell_{i}\text{ for all }i\,\}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\to\mathbb{N}
denote the corresponding Hilbert function defined as
\mathfrak{h}(\ell):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}\big/\mathcal{F}(\ell)
.

Remark 2.1.1
.

It is often convenient to encode the Hilbert function
\mathfrak{h}
in the formal
Hilbert series

H_{C,o}(t):=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}}\mathfrak{h}(\ell)t^{\ell}
. The
Poincaré series
is defined as
P_{C,o}(t):=-H(t)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\big(1-\frac{1}{t_{i}}\big)
. The former always determines the latter but the converse does not always hold, see section
5
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

The
weight function

w_{0}:\mathbb{N}^{r}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is defined as

w_{0}(\ell):=2\cdot\mathfrak{h}(\ell)-|\ell|

where
|\ell|=\sum_{i}\ell_{i}
. The
extended weight function

w:\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is defined as

w(x)=\max\big\{\,w_{0}(\ell)\ \big|\ \ell_{i}=\lfloor x_{i}\rfloor\text{ or }\lceil x_{i}\rceil\text{ for all }i\,\big\}.

Equivalently, the lattice
\mathbb{N}^{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
induces a cubical decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
and
w(x)
is the maximal weight attained by the vertices of the smallest cube containing
x
.

Given an integer
n
, define the sublevel set

S_{n}:=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}\ |\ w(x)\leq n\,\}.
It turns out that
w_{0}
is bounded from below, see e.g.
[
AN23
]
or Proposition
3.2.1
. Furthermore,
S_{n}
is a finite cubical complex for all
n\geq\min w_{0}
and empty for
n<\min w_{0}
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

The lattice cohomology module of
(C,o)
is defined as

\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o):=\bigoplus_{q=0}^{r-1}\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{H}^{q}_{2n}(C,o),\text{ where }\mathbb{H}^{q}_{2n}(C,o):=H^{q}(S_{n},{\mathbb{Z}}).

It admits the structure of a
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module with
U
-action
\mathbb{H}^{q}_{2n+2}(C,o)\to\mathbb{H}^{q}_{2n}(C,o)
induced by the inclusions
S_{n}\hookrightarrow S_{n+1}
.

Theorem 2.1.4
.

The following list provides a short summary of some of the more important properties of this invariant.

(1)

The Euler characteristic of
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
is the delta invariant
\delta(C,o):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\mathcal{O}}/\mathcal{O}
, see
[
AN23
, Corollary 4.2.2]
.

(2)

The
\mathbb{Z}[U]
-submodule
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
detects whether or not
(C,o)
is Gorenstein, see
[
KNS24b
, Theorem 4.2.1]

(3)

In the case of a plane curve singularity
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
determines the multiplicity
m(C,o)
, see
[
KNS24a
]
for the irreducible case and
[
KNS24b
, Section 5]
for the general case.

(4)

The space
S_{n}
is contractible for
n>0
, hence
\mathbb{H}^{*}_{2n}(C,o)=\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}(C,o)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
n>0
, see
[
KNS24b
, Nonpositivity Theorem 6.1.2]
.

Notation 2.1.5
.

Given two integers
m\leq n
with
n
possibly being equal to
\infty
, define the graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module
T_{2m}^{2n}
as follows. Consider the graded Abelian group
A=A_{2n}\oplus A_{2n-2}\oplus\dots\oplus A_{2m}
where
A_{2j}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
m\leq j\leq n
. Endow it with the
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module structure given by isomorphisms
U:A_{2j}\xrightarrow{\,\cong\,}A_{2j-2}
if
m<j\leq n
and let
UA_{2m}=0
. In the case
m=n
, we will also use the notation
T_{2m}:=T_{2m}^{2m}

Proposition 2.1.6
.

[
KNS24a
, Remark 2.7]

The graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
admits a direct sum decomposition

\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)\cong T_{2\min w_{0}}^{\infty}\oplus\bigoplus_{k}T_{2m_{k}}^{2n_{k}}

which is unique (but not canonical) up to graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module isomorphism.

Note also the following corollary of the Nonpositivity Theorem (item
(4)
of Theorem
2.1.4
).

Corollary 2.1.7
.

In fact, the graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-submodule
T^{\infty}_{2\min w_{0}}
mentioned in Proposition
2.1.6
is canonically embedded into
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
. Indeed,
\mathbb{H}_{2n}^{0}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
and
U:\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n+2}\xrightarrow{\,\cong\,}\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}
for any
n>0
, and
T^{\infty}_{2\min w_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{0}
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-submodule generated by
\mathbb{H}^{0}_{>0}:=\bigoplus_{n>0}\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}
.

3.
The lattice cohomology of plane branches

3.1.

The main result of this section is the following

Theorem 3.1.1
.

Let
(C,o)\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{2},o)
be an irreducible plane curve singularity. Then the graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-module
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
is a complete embedded topological invariant.

Remark 3.1.2
.

Note that for irreducible curve singularities
\mathbb{H}^{q}(C,o)=0
for all
q\geq 1
by construction. (This is not true in general: see subsection
4
for an example with
\mathbb{H}^{1}\neq 0.)

Before proving Theorem
3.1.1
, we will need some preliminaries.

3.2.
Preliminaries on irreducible plane curve singularities

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Let
(C,o)\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{2},o)
be an irreducible plane curve singularity. The semigroup of values of
(C,o)
is defined as

\mathcal{S}_{C,o}:=\{\,\text{ord}(n^{*}f)\ |\ f\in\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2},o}\,\}\cap\mathbb{N},

where
n:({\mathbb{C}},0)\to(C,o)
denotes the normalization / Puiseux parametrization (see e.g.
[
G82
,
DM87
]
for more details). We now list some key properties of
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
.

(i)

The semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
is a complete embedded topological invariant of the curve singularity
(C,o)
, see e.g.
[
BK86
]
.

(ii)

The semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
is a
numerical semigroup
, meaning that
\#(\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{C,o})<\infty
.

(iii)

The multiplicity of the germ
(C,o)
is defined as
m(C,o):=\min(\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\setminus\{0\})
and its delta invariant as
\delta(C,o):=\#(\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{C,o})
.

(iv)

The semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
is symmetric in the sense that
\ell\in\mathcal{S}
if and only if
c-1-\ell\not\in\mathcal{S}
(see
[
K70
]
) where
c:=\min\{\,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ \ell+\mathbb{N}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\,\}
is the conductor. (Note that
c=2\delta
.)

(v)

Let
\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{g}
be the (unique) minimal generating set of
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
. (Note that
\beta_{0}=m(C,o)
.) Define
l_{i}:=\gcd(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{i})
, and
n_{i}:=l_{i-1}/l_{i}
. Then

(3.2.1)

n_{i}\beta_{i}<\beta_{i+1}\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq g-1,

see e.g.
[
B72
, Theorem 2]
.

The next three properties describe the relationship of the semigroup of values
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
to the lattice cohomology module
\mathbb{H}^{*}(C,o)
.

(vi)

The weight function of
(C,o)
can be given by the formula

(3.2.2)

w_{0}(\ell)=\#\big([0,\ell)\cap\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\big)-\#\big([0,\ell)\setminus\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\big),

see
(
[
AN23
]
, Example 4.7.3 or
[
KNS24a
]
, subsection 5.2)
. It immediately follows from item (iv) that
w_{0}
is symmetric in the sense that

(3.2.3)

w_{0}(\ell)=w_{0}(c-\ell)\text{ for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Also, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(3.2.4)

|w_{0}(\ell+1)-w_{0}(\ell)|\equiv 1\text{ as }w_{0}(\ell+1)=\begin{cases}w_{0}(\ell)+1,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if }\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{C,o};\\
w_{0}(\ell)-1,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if }\ell\notin\mathcal{S}_{C,o}.\end{cases}

(vii)

The multiplicity
m(C,o)
can be determined from
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
as follows (see
[
KNS24a
]
). First,
m(C,o)\leq 2
if and only if
\min\{\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\ |\ \mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}(C,o)\not=0\,\}=0
. In this case the germ
(C,o)
is smooth if and only if
{\rm rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{H}^{0}_{0}(C,o)=1
and
m(C,o)=2
otherwise. On the other hand
m(C,o)>2
if and only if
\min\{\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\ |\ \mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}(C,o)\not=0\,\}<0
and in this case

m(C,o)=2-\max\big\{\,n<0\ |\ \ker\big(U:\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}(C,o)\to\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n-2}(C,o)\big)\neq 0\,\big\}.

(viii)

The delta invariant can be determined from
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
via the formula

\delta(C,o)=\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{H}^{0}_{\leq 0}(C,o)-1,

see
[
KNS24b
, Remark 2.3.3 (c)]
.

We remark that statements
(i)-(viii)
of Proposition
3.2.1
all have analogues in the case of plane curves with multiple branches.

Corollary 3.2.2
.

For every
s\in\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\cap[0,c]
, we have
w(s)\leq 0
.

Proof.

Let us suppose indirectly, that
w_{0}(s)\geq 1
. From Gorenstein symmetry (
3.2.3
) we get
w_{0}(0)=w_{0}(c)=0
, hence

. Even more, from equation (

) we see that
w_{0}(s+1)=w_{0}(s)+1>1
, with

. But this implies that

and

are contained in different connected components of
S_{1}
, which contradicts its contractibility.
∎

Definition 3.3.1
.

\chi(u)-\chi(v)=\pm 1
for eny edge

(b)

\chi(u)>\min\big\{\chi(v),\chi(w)\big\}
whenever

(c)

\chi
is bounded from below,
\big|\,\chi^{-1}(k)\,\big|

3.4.
Local minima

Let us fix an irreducible plane curve singularity
(C,o)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{2},o)
with semigroup of values
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and weight function
w_{0}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
. The authors and A. Némethi defined the local minimum points of the weight function
w_{0}
in
[
KNS24a
,
KNS24b
]
as follows:

Definition 3.4.1
.

A lattice point
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is said to be a
local minimum point
of the weight function
w_{0}
if either
\ell=0
, or
w_{0}(\ell-1)>w_{0}(\ell)<w_{0}(\ell+1)
. (Note that
w_{0}(0)=0<w_{0}(1)=1
always holds.)
Local minimum points are precisely the elements of the set
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\cap\big(({\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{C,o})+1\big)
by formula (
3.2.4
). The weight
w_{0}(\ell)
of a local minimum point
\ell
is said to be a
local minimum value
.

Let us compare this notion with the local minimum points of the corresponding graded root.

Definition 3.4.2
.

[
N05
, 3.2 (b)]

Let
R(C,o)
be the graded root of the curve singularity
(C,o)
. The vertex
v\in V
is said to be a local minimum point of the graded root if
\chi(v)<\chi(w)
for all vertices
w
adjacent to
v
. Note that, by item (b) of Definition
3.3.1
, this is equivalent to
v
being a leaf (i.e. a vertex with only a single neighbour) of the tree
R(C,o)
.

Notice that, by its definition, a local minimum point
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
of the weight function
w_{0}
with
w_{0}(\ell)=n
gives a distinct connected component
\{\ell\}
of
S_{n}

Definition 3.6.1
.

Let

be the

-submodule of

generated by

. Note that

is not a

in general, since

might be nonzero. In fact, in the direct sum decomposition of Proposition
2.1.6
,

corresponds to the submodule

.

Let us consider the set
\mathfrak{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq{\rm min}w}
of numbers

such that the

-action is trivial on the quotient module
\mathbb{M}_{\geq 2n}\big/T_{2\min w_{0}}^{\infty}
. For fixed

, this condition is equivalent to any of the following ones:

•

the quotient module

cannot contain summands of form

with

;

•

the trimmed and then truncated root

has exactly one leaf (c.f. subsection

);

•

(this clearly shows that if

, then

as well).

Let us denote the minimal element of
\mathfrak{N}
by
e
. If we consider the unique (up to isomorphism) direct sum decomposition
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)\cong T_{2\min w_{0}}^{\infty}\oplus\bigoplus_{k}T_{2m_{k}}^{2n_{k}}
of Proposition

, then

(3.6.1)

e=\min\{\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq\min w_{0}}\ |\ \forall n_{k}\geq n:\ m_{k}=n_{k}\}

For any integer

, we define the

.

and, thus,

Before proving Proposition
3.6.2
we show the following useful technical lemma.

Lemma 3.6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a symmetric numerical semigroup with multiplicity
m
and let
\ell\leq\delta
be an integer. Then

\max\big(w\big|_{[\ell,\min(\ell+m-1,\delta)]}\big)=\max\big(w\big|_{[\ell,\delta]}\big).

Proof.

Assume that
\ell+m-1<\delta
, otherwise there is nothing to prove. Given any lattice point
\ell^{\prime}\in\big[\ell+m,\min(\ell+2m-1,\delta)\big]
, we claim that
w(\ell^{\prime}-m)\geq w(\ell^{\prime})
. In order to prove it, we assume by contradiction that
w(\ell^{\prime}-m)<w(\ell^{\prime})
. By part
(vi)
of Proposition
3.2.1
, this is equivalent to

\#\big(\mathcal{S}\cap[\ell^{\prime}-m,\ell^{\prime}-1]\big)>m/2
. On the other hand, we have that

(3.6.2)

\#\big(\mathcal{S}\cap[\ell^{\prime}-m,\ell^{\prime}-1]\big)\leq\#\big(\mathcal{S}\cap[\ell^{\prime}-m+1,\ell^{\prime}]\big)\leq\dots\leq\#\big(\mathcal{S}\cap[c-\ell^{\prime},c-1-\ell^{\prime}+m]\big).

Indeed, since all of these intervals have length
m\in\mathcal{S}
, if
\ell^{\prime}-m+k\in\mathcal{S}
, then so is
\ell^{\prime}+k\in\mathcal{S}
for any
k\geq 0
. However, equation (
3.6.2
) contradicts the symmetry (
3.2.3
) of
\mathcal{S}
as

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}<\#\big(\mathcal{S}\cap[\ell^{\prime}-m,\ell^{\prime}-1]\big)\leq

\displaystyle\,\#\big(\mathcal{S}\cap[c-\ell^{\prime},c-1-\ell^{\prime}+m]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,m-\#\big(\mathcal{S}\cap[\ell^{\prime}-m,\ell^{\prime}-1]\big)<m-\frac{m}{2}=\frac{m}{2}.

It follows that
\max\big(w\big|_{[\ell,\ell+m-1]}\big)=\max\big(w\big|_{[\ell,\min(\ell+2m-1,\delta)]}\big)
. Continuing this process inductively we reach the desired equality
\max\big(w\big|_{[\ell,\ell+m-1]}\big)=\max\big(w\big|_{[\ell,\delta]}\big)
.
∎

3.7.
‘
e
comes late enough’

In this subsection we formalize the statement that
e=\min\mathfrak{N}
is small enough in the sense, that
\mathcal{E}
describes a large enough part of semigroup to be then able to recover it fully, just by knowing the
\delta
invariant.

First we show, that in the special case of
l_{g-1}=2
(see the notations in item
(v)
of Proposition
3.2.1
), the initial part contains every semigroup element until
\delta
.

Proposition 3.7.1
.

Let
(C,o)\in(\mathbb{C}^{2},o)
be an irreducible plane curve singularity with
l_{g-1}=2
. Then
\mathcal{E}=[0,\delta]\cap\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
and
\mathbb{H}^{0}_{\geq 2e}
is closed under the
U
-action with
\mathbb{H}^{0}_{\geq 2e}=\mathbb{H}^{0}
as graded
{\mathbb{Z}}[U]
-modules.

First we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7.2
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be a lattice point, such that there are no consecutive elements between
0
and
\ell
in some numerical semigroup
\mathcal{S}
(i.e. for any
0\leq\ell^{\prime}<\ell,\ \ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
we have
\ell^{\prime}+1\notin\mathcal{S}
) and
\max\big(w\big|_{[\ell,\delta]}\big)=w(\ell)
, where
w
is computed from
\mathcal{S}
via
(
3.2.2
)
. Then
w(\ell)\in\mathfrak{N}
(hence
w(\ell)\geq e
).

Proof.

The fact that there are no consecutive semigroup elements before
\ell
implies, that for any
0\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-2
we have
w(\ell^{\prime})\geq w(\ell^{\prime}+2)
(cf. formula (
3.2.2
)). Therefore, for any
n\geq w(\ell)
, the following are true for the space
S_{n}
: firstly,
[\ell,\delta]\subset S_{n}
, and secondly,

(3.7.1)

\text{for any }0\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell:\text{ if }\ell^{\prime}\in S_{n},\text{ then also }\ell^{\prime}+2\in S_{n}.

Thus, if we denote by
s_{n}
the smallest lattice point of
S_{n}
, then
s_{n}+2\mathbb{N}\subset S_{n}
and if
s_{n}+2k_{n}+1
denotes the smallest element of
S_{n}\setminus\big(s_{n}+2\mathbb{N}\big)
, then
[s_{n}+2k_{n},\delta]\subset S_{n}
. Therefore, for any
n\geq w(\ell)
,
S_{n}
is of form prescribed by Proposition
3.6.6
, and therefore
n\in\mathfrak{N}
for every
n\geq w(\ell)
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.7.1
.

Firstly, if
g=1
(i.e. we have a single Puiseux pair) and
m=l_{g-1}=2
, then we already saw in the proof of Proposition
3.6.2
, that
0=\min w_{0}\in\mathfrak{N}
and hence
\mathbb{H}_{\geq 0}=\mathbb{H}
.

Let us now suppose, that
g>1
. We claim, that it is sufficient to prove that
\delta<\beta_{g}
as this implies that
\mathcal{E}=[0,\delta]\cap\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
.

Indeed, by inequality (
3.2.1
) there can be no consecutive elements in
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\cap[0,\beta_{g}-1]
, so, if
\delta<\beta_{g}
, then we can apply the previous Lemma
3.7.2
to
\ell=\delta
and we obtain that
w(\delta)\in\mathfrak{N}
. Note however, that
\min w_{0}
is obtained by some lattice point in
[0,\delta]
, hence, by property (
3.7.1
)
w(\delta)\leq\min w_{0}+1
. Considering also the fact, that
U\cdot\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2\min w_{0}}=0
, we obtain that
\min w_{0}\in\mathfrak{N}
, hence
\mathcal{E}=[0,\delta]\cap\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
and
\mathbb{H}^{0}_{\geq 2e}=\mathbb{H}^{0}_{\geq 2\min w_{0}}=\mathbb{H}^{0}
.

In order to prove the inequality
\delta<\beta_{g}
, we define the partial conductors

c_{i}:=\min\big\{\,n\in\mathbb{N}\ \big|\ n+\ell_{i}\mathbb{N}\subseteq\langle\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{i}\rangle\,\big\}.

Note that
c_{0}=0
, while
c_{g}=c
is just the conductor of
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
. It follows from
(v)
of Proposition
3.2.1
that

(3.7.2)

c_{i}=c_{i-1}+\frac{(l_{i-1}-l_{i})(\beta_{i}-l_{i})}{l_{i}},

see e.g.
[
BDR03
, Corollary 4.3]
. Employing equation (
3.7.2
) and inequality (
3.2.1
), one can show inductively that
\beta_{i+1}>c_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq g-1
.
Finally, using
c_{g-1}<\beta_{g}
,
l_{g-1}=2
and equation (
3.7.2
) once again, we get that

(3.7.3)

\delta=\frac{c_{g}}{2}=\frac{c_{g-1}+\beta_{g}-1}{2}<\beta_{g}

as claimed.
∎

Corollary 3.7.3
.

Let us suppose that
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\neq\langle 2,3\rangle
is a semigroup of an irreducible plane curve singularity with
l_{g-1}=2
. Then
\beta_{g-1}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

If
g=1
, then
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
is of type
\langle 2,2k+1\rangle
for some
k>1
. In this case
\beta_{0}=2\in\mathcal{E}
trivially holds, where
\mathcal{E}=[0,k]\cap\mathcal{S}
by Proposition
3.7.1
.

If
g\geq 2
, then clearly
2\notin\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
(
2\in\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
would imply
g=1
), hence, since
\beta_{g}
is the smallest odd element of
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
,
\beta_{g}+2\notin\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
and, thus,
\beta_{g}<c
. Now from inequality (
3.2.1
) we get that
\beta_{g-1}<\beta_{g}/2<c/2=\delta
. Therefore,
\beta_{g-1}\in[0,\delta]\cap\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
, which equals
\mathcal{E}
by Proposition
3.7.1
.
∎

Next, we show that this property is also true for any ‘large enough’ semigroup as well.

Proposition 3.7.4
.

If
g\geq 2
(i.e.
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
is minimally generated by at least three elements), then
\beta_{g-1}\in\mathcal{E}
(and, since
\mathcal{E}
is and initial part,
\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{g-1}\}\subset\mathcal{E}
).

Before proving Proposition
3.7.4
we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7.5
.

If
g\geq 2
and
l_{g-1}\neq 2
, then
w(\beta_{g-1})=w(\beta_{g-1}+2)=\max\big(w\big|_{[\beta_{g-1}+2,\delta]}\big).

Proof.

By inequality (
3.2.1
)
\beta_{g}>2\beta_{g-1}>\beta_{g-1}+m
, i.e.
\beta_{g}\geq\beta_{g-1}+m+2
. It follows that
[\beta_{g-1}+2,\beta_{g-1}+m+1]\cap\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\subseteq l_{g-1}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
l_{g-1}\neq 2
,
\beta_{g-1}+2\not\in\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
, with
w(\beta_{g-1}+2)=w(\beta_{g-1})
and also
w\big|_{[\beta_{g-1}+2,\beta_{g-1}+m+1]}\leq w(\beta_{g-1})
. Finally, applying Lemma
3.6.3
to
[\beta_{g-1}+2,\beta_{g-1}+m+1]
finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.7.4
.

The case
l_{g-1}=2
was already discussed in Corollary
3.7.3
.

Let us then consider the case of
l_{g-1}\neq 2
. Notice, that there can be no consecutive elements in
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}\cap[0,\beta_{g-1}+2]
by inequality (
3.2.1
). This, together with Lemma
3.7.5
and
3.7.2
used for
\ell=\beta_{g-1}+2
implies that

w(\beta_{g-1})\in\mathfrak{N}
. It follows that
\beta_{g-1}\in\mathcal{E}
by definition.
∎

Via contraposition, Proposition
3.7.4
implies, that
\mathcal{E}
can only be minimal (i.e.
\mathcal{E}=\{0\}
, see Proposition
3.6.2
), if the semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
is small in the sense that it has at most two generators:

Corollary 3.7.6
.

If
\mathcal{E}=\{0\}
, then
g\leq 1
, i.e.
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}=\langle\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\rangle
with
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1},\ {\rm gcd}(\beta_{0},\beta_{1})=1
.

We are now ready to put together the pieces of the proof of the main theorem.

3.8.
The algorithm

We will describe here the algorithm how to reconstruct the semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
, more specifically its minimal set of generators
\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{g}\}
, of an irreducible plane curve singularity
(C,o)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{2},o)
from the
\mathbb{Z}[U]
-module
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
.

(1)

First check whether
\mathbb{H}^{0}
is trivial or not: if
\mathbb{H}^{0}\cong T_{0}^{\infty}
(or equivalently
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}=1
for
n\geq 0
and
0
otherwise), then
(C,o)
is smooth.

(2)

If
\mathbb{H}^{0}
is nontrivial, find
e=\min\mathfrak{N}
, the smallest
n\geq\min w_{0}
such that the
U
-action on
\mathbb{M}_{\geq 2n}/T_{2\min w_{0}}^{\infty}
is trivial (for example, by obtaining the canonical direct sum decomposition of
\mathbb{H}^{0}
of Proposition
2.1.6
with the method described in
[
KNS24a
, Remark 2.7]
and then reading off
e
as in (
3.6.1
)).

(3)

Then reconstruct the set
\mathcal{E}=[0,s_{e}+2k_{e}]\cap\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
from the
\mathbb{Z}[U]
-module
\mathbb{H}^{0}
by the algorithm proceeding Proposition
3.6.11
.

(4)

If
\mathcal{E}
is minimal, i.e.
\mathcal{E}=\{0\}
, then
g=1
, or equivalently, the curve has a single Puiseux pair. Thus
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}=\langle\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\rangle
, with
\beta_{0}=m
the multiplicity and, hence,
\beta_{0}=2
if
\mathbb{H}^{0}_{<0}=0
, and
\beta_{0}=2-\max\{n\,|\,{\rm ker}(U:\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n}\to\mathbb{H}^{0}_{2n-2})\not=0,\ n<0\}
otherwise. Now
\delta=(\beta_{0}-1)(\beta_{1}-1)/2={\rm rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{H}^{0}_{\leq 0}(C,o)-1
and from these compute
\beta_{1}
as well.

(5)

If
\mathcal{E}
is not minimal, then
\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{g-1}\}\subset\mathcal{E}
and they can be read off as
\mathcal{E}^{*}\setminus(\mathcal{E}^{*}+\mathcal{E}^{*})
(where
\mathcal{E}^{*}=\mathcal{E}\setminus\{0\}
), i.e. the primitive elements of
\mathcal{E}
. Also

{\rm rank}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{H}^{0}_{\leq 0}(C,o)-1=\delta=c/2=c_{g-1}+{(l_{g-1}-1)(\beta_{g}-1)},

where the partial conductor
c_{g-1}
can be computed inductively from the set
\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{g-1}\}
via the formula (
3.7.2
) and
l_{g-1}={\rm gcd}(\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{g-1})
. From these compute
\beta_{g}
as well.

Proof of Theorem
3.1.1
/ previous algorithm.

For the first step we use
[
AN23
, Example 4.6.1]
(see also part
(vii)
of Proposition
3.2.1
). The second step is straightforward, while the third is just the application of Propositions
3.6.10
and
3.6.11
.

The main assertion of step (4) regarding the number of Puiseux pairs follows from Corollary
3.7.6
. Then the multiplicity and delta invariant are read off as described in parts
(vii)
and
(viii)
of Proposition
3.2.1
.

In order to prove step (5) we have to consider two different cases:

•

if
g=1
, then
\mathcal{E}\supsetneq\{0\}
implies that
\beta_{0}\in\mathcal{E}
, since
\mathcal{E}
is an initial part of
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
(see Proposition
3.6.10
);

•

if
g\geq 2
, then
\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{g-1}\}\subset\mathcal{E}
by Proposition
3.7.4
.

(
g=0
would mean that
(C,o)
is smooth, which case was dealt with in step (1)). Finally, the
\delta
invariant is read off again as described in part
(viii)
of Proposition
3.2.1
.
∎

3.9.
l_{g-1}=2
revisited

We already saw in Proposition
3.7.1
, that the condition
l_{g-1}=2
is special. Here we prove that we can, in fact, characterize it with the
\mathbb{Z}[U]
-module
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C,o)
as well.

Proposition 3.9.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a non-smooth irreducible plane curve singularity
(C,o)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{2},o)
:

(1)

l_{g-1}=2
;

(2)

\min w_{0}
even and
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(\mathbb{H}^{0}_{4k+2}\big)=1
for all
k\geq({\min w_{0}})/{2}
. This clearly implies that
U\cdot\mathbb{H}^{0}\leq T_{2\min w_{0}}^{\infty}
and that, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{H}^{0}
is homogeneous and
U\cdot\alpha=0
, then
\alpha
has even degree (i.e. the homogeneous parts of the kernel of the
U
-action are of even degree).

(3)

In the graded root
R(C,o)
the gradings of the local minimum points are all even and the trimmed version
\overline{R}
only has a single leaf.

Proof.

We will use the correspondence (
3.4.1
) between local minimum points of the weight function and the kernel of the
U
-action. In this language, property
(2)
corresponds to the facts that
S_{2k+1}
is connected for any
k\geq(\min w_{0})/2
, and, for any local minimum point
\ell
, the local minimum value
w(\ell)
is even. Notice that the parity of
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
w(\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}
always agree, hence this second condition is equivalent to every local minimum point being even.

(1)

\Rightarrow

(2)
. If
l_{g-1}=2
, we already saw in (
3.7.3
) that
\beta_{g}>\delta
(more specifically, there we only considered the case of
g>1
, however, for
g=1
we have
\beta_{1}>c>\delta
trivially). Thus until
\delta
all the local minima of the weight function (or, equivalently, all the semigroup values) are even, thus their analytic weight
w(\ell)=2\mathfrak{h}(\ell)-\ell
is also even, so is then the grading on the homogeneous parts of the kernel of the
U
-action. On the other hand, Proposition
3.7.1
implies that
\min w_{0}\in\mathfrak{N}
and hence, by Proposition
3.6.6
,
S_{2k+1}
is connected for any
k\geq(\min w_{0})/2
.

(2)

\Rightarrow

(1)
. First we consider the case of
\beta_{g}>\delta
. Then by (
3.7.2
)

\displaystyle 2\beta_{g}>c=c_{g}=c_{g-1}+(\beta_{g}-1)(l_{g-1}-1)\Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 3\beta_{g}-1-c_{g-1}>\

\displaystyle l_{g-1}(\beta_{g}-1)\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 3+\frac{2-c_{g-1}}{\beta_{g}-1}>\

\displaystyle l_{g-1},

so
l_{g-1}
must be
2
, except when
c_{g-1}\leq 1
. But if
g>1
, then
c_{g-1}>0
and
l_{g-1}|c_{g-1}
, so
l_{g-1}\neq 2
can only happen if
g=1
, i.e. we only have a single Puiseux pair. Then
2\leq l_{g-1}=\beta_{0}<\beta_{1}=\beta_{g}
and
c_{0}=0
, so the only possible cases with
l_{g-1}>2
are:
\beta_{0}=3,\beta_{1}>3
such that
{\rm gcd}(3,\beta_{1})=1
. However, in these cases the multiplicity is
3
, which gives a local minimum point with weight
-1
odd (see part
(vii)
of Proposition
3.2.1
).

We can turn to the
\beta_{g}\leq\delta
case.
We prove that this case is in fact vacuous: a semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
of an irreducible curve singularity with
\beta_{g}<\delta
cannot have lattice cohomology module
\mathbb{H}
satisfying the conditions of property
(2)
. Indeed, every semigroup element
s\in\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
, with
s-1\notin\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
is a local minimum point (see Definition
3.4.1
) and hence must be even. As there are no consecutive semigroup elements between
0
and
\beta_{g}-1
(see part
(v)
of Proposition
3.2.1
) the minimal generators
\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{g-1}
must all be even. Since
{\rm gcd}(\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{g-1},\beta_{g})=1
,
\beta_{g}
must be odd and thus, by the previous local minimum point argument, it must have
\beta_{g}-1\in\mathcal{S}_{C,o}
. Now by formula (
3.2.4
) we have
w(\beta_{g}-1)<w(\beta_{g})<w(\beta_{g}+1)
and, thus,
\beta_{g}\neq\delta
, since that would contradict the Gorenstein symmetry (

). But then

cannot be connected (with

\{\beta_{g},c-\beta_{g}\}\in S_{w(\beta_{g})}
and
\beta_{g}+1\notin S_{w(\beta_{g})}

(2)
cannot be satisfied if
\beta_{g}\leq\delta
.

The equivalence

4.

4.1.

In this section we show that in general the Seifert form

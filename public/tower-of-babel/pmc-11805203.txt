A Comparative Evaluation of Fracture Resistance of Three Post Systems and Their Mode of Failure: An In-Vitro Study
Authors: Manish Sundesha, Anita Choudhary, Priyanka Gubrellay, Eishita Negi, Harleen Chhina, Omni P. Jain, Nikita Patel
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:To analyze the fracture resistance of three post systems and their mechanism of failure.Materials and Procedures:Forty-five newly excised human mandibular single-rooted pre-molar, for periodontal or orthodontic reasons, were used. The teeth were separated into three groups (n = 15) based on the post system utilized. Group A had teeth restored with custom cast post; Group B had prefabricated glass fiber posts (Angelus post); and Group C with edelweiss composite post (Edelweiss Post).Results:The custom cast posts provided the highest fracture resistance but exhibited more unfavorable fractures. The edelweiss posts exhibited the least fracture resistance.Conclusion:Further investigations should be targeted at elucidating the force dynamics of this newer post system.

INTRODUCTION

In recent times, the field of dentistry has seen significant advancements in both the removal of diseased teeth and the treatment of infected teeth via endodontic procedures.[1] Nevertheless, a tooth that has undergone endodontic treatment is often more prone to fracture due to the inadequate structure of the dental crown, caused by caries, trauma, or the endodontic treatment. Therefore, it is recommended to use a post and core, followed by a complete coverage restoration, in order to ensure sufficient retention and resistance for the final restoration.[12] Multiple post systems are accessible for the restoration of teeth that have undergone endodontic treatment. These systems range from the conventional custom-made cast post-and-core system to prefabricated metal or non-metal posts fused with composite resin cores. Each system has its own advantages and disadvantages.[13] The fracture resistance and manner of failure of these post systems have not been extensively scrutinized in existing studies. Therefore, the objective of this work is to assess the fracture resistance and failure mechanism of three distinct post-and-core systems: custom cast post, Angelus fiber post (glass fiber post), and the Edelweiss post system.

MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY

After obtaining Ethical Clearance from the Ethical Committee, the present study was conducted. The sample collection included 45 recently extracted human mandibular single-rooted pre-molars, that were taken for periodontal or orthodontic reasons. All selected samples were devoid of cavities, craze lines, cracks, and fractures. Only samples with fully developed roots were included in the selection. Exclusion criteria for the research were teeth with pre-existing restoration, carious lesions, resorption, calcified canals, or immature apices.

The methodology included collecting and disinfecting 45 recently extracted single-rooted mandibular premolars, which were obtained for periodontal or orthodontic reasons and had identical form and size, in a 2% glutaraldehyde solution for 48 hours.

All teeth were categorized into three groups, each consisting of 15 teeth, based on the post system used. Participants in Group A had tooth restoration using a custom cast post, Group B received a prefabricated glass fiber post known as the Angelus post, and Group C received an Edelweiss composite post. Every tooth was equipped with a ferrule measuring 2 mm in length. Next, the designated points for posting were made in each group. Groups A and B utilized low-cost reamers #4 with a diameter of 1.1 mm, whereas Group C used an Edelweiss drill with a diameter of 1 mm, leaving a 5 mm apical gutta-percha in place.

The teeth were set into one-inch-by-one-inch-by-one-inch acrylic resin blocks. The moment each tooth began to show symptoms of polymerization, it was taken out of the resin block. The wax spacer was taken from the root surface once the resin block had finished polymerizing. A dispenser gun with a mixing tip was then used to pour the polyvinylsiloxane impression material into the acrylic alveolus of the acrylic resin block. The tooth was reinserted into the test block, allowing the impression material to solidify. The surface was then cut to form a flat plane, positioned 2 mm below the canal-oesophageal junction of each tooth.

A universal testing equipment (Instron 8500 Plus, 100 Royal St. Canton, USA) was used to evaluate the specimens’ resistance to fracture. Newtons were used to measure the applied force. Using a stereomicroscope, the mechanism of failure for every sample was noted upon fracture. Failures were divided into two categories: catastrophic root fractures below the cementoenamel junction (non-repairable) and cosmetic fractures above the cementoenamel junction (repairable).

DISCUSSION

The current investigation shown that Group A, which used a custom cast post, had superior fracture resistance (254.65 N) in comparison to Group B, which used a glass fiber post and Group C, which used an Edelweiss post with fracture resistance values of 218.32 N and 83.33 N respectively. The fracture resistance values reported among the three groups showed a statistically significant difference (P = 0.001). An explanation for this phenomenon may be ascribed to the superior compressive strength of the nickel chromium base metal alloy (2100 MPa) in comparison to glass fiber and composite materials (1378 MPa and 550 MPa respectively). The fracture resistance of Edelweiss post was found to be lower than that of glass fiber post. This differential may be attributed to the presence of glass reinforcement in the glass fiber post which enhances its ability to withstand compression forces. The findings of our study align with the research undertaken by Jununthula and Gaddalay (2018),[4] which identified that the fracture resistance of cast metal posts was superior than that of glass fibre posts, zirconium dioxide posts with ceramic cores, and zirconium dioxide posts with composite cores. Similarly, a study conducted by Santos-Filho et al. (2008),[5] it was shown that cast post and core exhibited the highest fracture resistance when the post length was maintained at 10.0 mm. Conversely, glass fiber posts demonstrated effective performance at various post lengths, exhibiting higher fracture resistance than metal posts when the length was fell within the range of 5.0 mm.

The comparison of failure modes across the different groups revealed that Group A (custom cast post) had a higher frequency of unfavorable fractures in comparison to the other two groups. Within Group A (custom cast post), 8 out of 15 fractures were unfavorable. In Group B (glass fibre post) and Group C (Edelweiss post), 5 out of 15 and 4 out of 15 fractures, respectively, were unfavourable. The observed stress distribution is more advantageous when two materials have complementary modulus of elasticity characteristics. Hence, a post material that closely approximates the modulus of elasticity of dentin (20,000 MPa), would be regarded more advantageous in terms of stress distribution compared to a material that falls well lower of this value.[6]

The modulus of elasticity of a custom cast post formed from a Ni-Cr base metal alloy is 200 GPa, which is much higher than that of dentin. The lack of natural elastic deformation in a rigid post results in the establishment of a localized stress peak, ultimately causing the failure of the post system in the form of unfavourable fractures. This phenomenon accounts for the increased occurrence of unfavourable fractures in the custom cast post group.[7] The study conducted by Uthappa et al. (2015)[8] shown that the failure rate of glass fiber posts preserved crown repairs was lower in comparison to metal posts. The modulus of glass fiber posts and Edelweiss posts are similar to that of dentin, allowing them to absorb natural elastic deformation and transmit minimized pressures to the root, therefore facilitating restorable fractures.

A further investigation conducted by Kaur et al. (2012)[9] revealed that the glass fiber post exhibited a more advantageous fracture pattern in comparison to the cast metal post. In comparison to glass fiber and Edelweiss posts, cast metal posts exhibited superior fracture resistance ratings. These newer posts seem to provide a solution to the prevalent issues connected with cast metal posts. Edelweiss post had the lowest fracture resistance when compared to the current post systems in the testing. The glass fiber post exhibited a similar fracture pattern to the Edelweiss post but with significantly higher fracture resistance. Consequently, the glass fiber post may be more strongly suggested above the Edelweiss post.

CONCLUSION

Based on the constraints of this investigation, it can be inferred that the new Edelweiss post is not a viable as a replacement for the cast metal post or the glass fiber posts. These alternatives are capable of withstanding far greater levels of stress concentration without experiencing failure. Edelweiss post exhibited a similar pattern of fracture to that of glass fiber post.

Abstract
. We survey some recent results and open questions on the approaching geodesics property and its application to the study of the Gromov and horofunction compactifications of a proper geodesic Gromov metric space. We obtain results on the dynamics of isometries and we exhibit an example of a Gromov hyperbolic domain of ℂ \mathbb{C} which does not satisfy the approaching geodesic property.



1.
Introduction

A proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space
X
admits a natural compactification, called the Gromov compactification. It is a natural question to compare this compactification with the horofunction compactification. It turns out that the Gromov compactification is always “smaller”
[
17
]
than the horofunction compactification, but in general not equivalent.
A condition on
X
ensuring the equivalence of the two said compactifications was highlighted in
[
3
]
:
the approaching geodesic property. A metric space
X
is said to have approaching geodesics if asymptotic rays are strongly asymptotic, that is their distance goes to 0. This is the case for instance for the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
endowed with the Kobayashi distance, or more generally for bounded strongly pseudoconvex domains of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Rather than being just a technical condition implying the equivalence of the two compactifications, we show in this note that the approaching geodesics property has interesting dynamical consequences which do not hold in general if we only assume the equivalence of the two compactifications. For instance we show that if
X
has approaching geodesics, then the minimal displacement and the divergence rate of a non-elliptic isometry coincide, and that a hyperbolic isometry always admits a unique axis.
We also recall results and collect several open questions concerning the approaching geodesics property and its application to complex analysis and to dynamics of non-expanding maps. Finally we
show that not all Kobayashi complete domains of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
which are Gromov hyperbolic have approaching geodesics,
giving an example of a Gromov hyperbolic planar domain of
\mathbb{C}
which admits a non-elliptic isometry whose minimal displacement is strictly larger than its divergence rate.

2.
Gromov and horofunction compactifications

We recall some basic definitions.

Definition 2.1

(Compactification)
.

Let
X
be a topological space. A
compactification
of
X
is a couple
(i,Y)
where
Y
is a compact topological space and
i\colon X\to Y
is a topological embedding such that
\overline{i\left(X\right)}=Y
. Two compactifications
(i_{1},Y_{1}),(i_{2},Y_{2})
are
equivalent
if there exists a homeomorphism
h\colon Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
such that
h\circ i_{1}=i_{2}
.

Definition 2.2

(Geodesic)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.
A
geodesic
is a map
\gamma
from an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}

to
X
which is an isometry with respect to the euclidean distance on
I
and the distance on

X
, that is for all
s,t\in I
,

d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|t-s|.

If
I=[0,+\infty)
(resp.
\mathbb{R}
) we call
\gamma
a
geodesic ray
(resp.
line
).
A metric space is
geodesic
if any two points are joined by a geodesic. A metric space is
proper
if every closed ball
is compact.

Definition 2.3

(Gromov hyperbolicity)
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
.
A proper geodesic metric space
(X,d)
is
\delta
-
hyperbolic
if for every geodesic triangle, any side is contained in the
\delta
-neighborhood of the union of the two other sides. A metric space is
Gromov hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space. In what follows we will study the relation between two natural compactifications of
X
: the Gromov compactification and the horofunction compactification.

Definition 2.4

(Gromov compactification)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space.
Two geodesic rays
\gamma,\sigma\colon[0,+\infty)\to X
are
asymptotic
if

\sup_{t\geq 0}d(\gamma(t),\sigma(t))<+\infty.

Let
\mathscr{R}(X)
denote the set of geodesic rays in
(X,d)
, and define the equivalence relation on
\mathscr{R}(X)

\gamma\sim_{r}\sigma\iff\text{$\gamma$ and $\sigma$ are asymptotic.}

The
Gromov boundary
of
(X,d)
is defined as
\partial_{G}X:=\mathscr{R}(X)/_{\sim_{r}}.

The
Gromov compactification
of
(X,d)
is the set
\overline{X}^{G}:=X\sqcup\partial_{G}X
endowed with a suitable compact metrizable topology (see e.g.
[
7
, Chapter III.H]
).

Definition 2.5

(Horofunction compactification)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper metric space.
Let
C_{*}(X)
be the quotient of
C(X)
by the subspace of constant functions.
Given
f\in C(X)
, we denote its equivalence class by
\overline{f}\in C_{*}(X)
.

Consider the embedding

i_{H}\colon X\to C_{*}(X)

which sends a point
x\in X
to the equivalence class of the function
d_{x}\colon y\mapsto d(x,y).

The
horofunction compactification

\overline{X}^{H}
of
(X,d)
is the closure of
i_{H}(X)
in
C_{*}(X)
.
The
horofunction boundary
of
X
is the (compact) set

\partial_{H}X:=\overline{X}^{H}\setminus i_{H}(X).

Let
a\in\partial_{H}X
. An
horofunction
centered at
a\in\partial_{H}X
is an element
h\in C\left(X\right)
satisfying
\bar{h}=a
.
For every
p\in X
, the unique horofunction centered at
a
and vanishing at
p
is denoted by
h_{a,p}
.
Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
. The level sets
\{h_{a,p}<c\}
and
\{h_{a,p}\leq c\}

are called
horospheres
(or
horoballs
) centered at
a
. Fix
p\in X
. Notice that a sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
converges to
a\in\partial_{H}X
if and only if

d(x_{n},w)-d(w,p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}h_{a,p}(w),

uniformly on compact subsets.

If
(X,d)
is a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space, then by
[
17
, Proposition 4.6]
there exists a continuous map
\Phi\colon\overline{X}^{H}\rightarrow\overline{X}^{G}
satisfying

i_{G}=\Phi\circ i_{H},

where
i_{G}\colon X\rightarrow\overline{X}^{G}
is the inclusion map.

For a general Gromov hyperbolic metric space the map
\Phi
may not be a homeomorphism, that is, the two compactifications may not be equivalent.

Example 2.6
.

Let
X:=[0,\infty)\times[-1,1]
endowed with the distance

d((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|.

The space
(X,d)
is a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space.
The Gromov boundary
\partial_{G}X
consists of a single point, while the horofunction boundary
\partial_{H}X
is canonically homeomorphic to
[-1,1]
.

It is thus natural to ask for conditions on the metric space
X
which imply the equivalence of the two compactifications. One such condition was introduced in
[
3
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic metric space.
Two geodesic rays
\gamma,\sigma\in\mathscr{R}(X)
are
strongly asymptotic
if there exist
T\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\gamma(t),\sigma(t+T))=0.

We say that a proper geodesic metric space
X
has
approaching geodesics
if asymptotic rays are strongly asymptotic.
If
(X,d)
is a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space and
\xi\in\partial_{G}X
we say that
X
has
approaching geodesic at
\xi
if all rays with endpoint
\xi
are strongly asymptotic.

Clearly strongly asymptotic rays are asymptotic.

Remark 2.8
.

(see Proposition 3.13 in
[
3
]
)
The rays
\gamma
and
\sigma
are strongly asymptotic if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\inf_{s\geq 0}d(\gamma(t),\sigma(s))=0.

The next result shows that the approaching geodesic property is a sufficient condition for the equivalence of the two compactifications.
First of all, notice that every geodesic ray in
X
has an associated horofunction.

Definition 2.9

(Busemann function)
.

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic ray in
X
. The
Busemann function

B_{\gamma}\colon X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated with
\gamma
is defined as

B_{\gamma}(x,y):=\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(x,\gamma(t))-d(\gamma(t),y).

For all
y\in X
, the function
x\mapsto B_{\gamma}(x,y)
is a horofunction, and its class
\overline{B}_{\gamma}\in\partial_{H}X
does not depend on
y\in X
.

Remark 2.10
.

Let
\gamma,\sigma
be two strongly asymptotic geodesic rays in
X
and let
T\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\gamma(t),\sigma(t+T))=0.

The number
T
has an interesting interpretation in terms of the Busemann function:

B_{\gamma}(\sigma(0),\gamma(0)):=\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\sigma(0),\gamma(t))-d(\gamma(t),\gamma(0))=\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\sigma(0),\sigma(t+T))-d(\gamma(t),\gamma(0))=T.

Theorem 2.11
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.5]

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space with approaching geodesics. Then the continuous map

\Psi\colon\overline{X}^{G}\to\overline{X}^{H}
defined by

\Psi(\xi)=\begin{cases}\overline{d(\,\cdot\,,\xi)},&{\rm if}\ \xi\in X,\\
\overline{B}_{\gamma},&{\rm if}\ \xi=[\gamma]\in\partial_{G}X\end{cases}

is the inverse of
\Phi
, thus
\overline{X}^{G}
and
\overline{X}^{H}
are equivalent.

3.
The approaching geodesics property in complex analysis

In this section we present the known results and open questions on the approaching geodesics property for a bounded Kobayashi complete domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and its relation with the existence of horospheres. We denote by
k_{D}\colon D\times D\to[0,\infty)
the Kobayashi distance of
D
.

In 1988 Abate defined
[
1
]
the
big horosphere and the small horosphere
centered at
\xi\in\partial D
of radius
R>0
with base point
p\in D
respectively as

\displaystyle\begin{split}E^{b}_{p}(\xi,R)&:=\left\{z\in D\colon\liminf_{w\to\xi}k_{D}(z,w)-k_{D}(w,p)<\log R\right\},\\
E^{s}_{p}(\xi,R)&:=\left\{z\in D\colon\limsup_{w\to\xi}k_{D}(z,w)-k_{D}(w,p)<\log R\right\}.\end{split}

(3.1)

Later, using Lempert’s theory of complex geodesics
[
15
]
, he proved that if
D
is a bounded strongly convex domain with boundary of class
C^{3}
, then the limit

\lim_{w\to\xi}k_{D}(z,w)-k_{D}(w,p)

(3.2)

exists for all
\xi\in\partial D
, and thus big and small horospheres coincide (see
[
2
]
).

In the general case, it is easy to see that the limit (
3.2
) exists for all
\xi\in\partial D
(and so big and small horospheres coincide) if the horofunction compactification of
D
is equivalent to the euclidean compactification

\overline{D}.

Assume now that
(D,k_{D})
is Gromov hyperbolic. In several interesting cases the Gromov compactification
\overline{D}^{G}
turns out to be equivalent to the euclidean compactification
\overline{D}
, so by Theorem
2.11
the approaching geodesic property implies the existence of the limit (
3.2
) for all
\xi\in\partial D
.

Consider for example a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Balogh and Bonk in
[
5
]
proved that
D
is Gromov hyperbolic with respect to the Kobayashi distance and that
\overline{D}^{G}=\overline{D}
. The approaching geodesic property for bounded strongly pseudoconvex domains was established in
[
3
]
via rescaling with the squeezing function.

Definition 3.1

(Squeezing function)
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a bounded domain. Given
z\in D
define the family of functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{z}

\displaystyle:=\{\varphi:D\to\mathbb{B}^{d}\;|\text{ $\varphi$ is holomorphic, injective and }\varphi(z)=0\}.

The
squeezing function
of
D
at the point
z\in D
is defined as

s_{D}(z):=\sup\{r>0:B(0,r)\subseteq\varphi(D)\text{ for some }\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{z}\}.

In
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
F. Deng, Q. Guan, and L. Zhang proved that if
D
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with boundary of class
C^{2}
then
\displaystyle\lim_{z\to\partial D}s_{D}(z)=1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 4.3]

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a domain satisfying
\displaystyle\lim_{z\to\partial D}s_{D}=1
.
Then
(D,k_{D})
has approaching geodesics.

The idea of the proof is the following: if by contradiction there exist two geodetic rays
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
in
D
which are asymptotic but not strongly asymptotic then rescaling with the univalent maps
\varphi\colon D\to\mathbb{B}^{d}
provided by the squeezing function along
\gamma_{1}
we end up in the limit with two distinct geodesic lines
\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{B}^{d}
of the unit ball which are asymptotic both at
+\infty
and
-\infty
, which is a contradiction.

Corollary 3.3
.

[
3
, Corollary 4.5]

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{2}
boundary.
Then
(D,k_{D})
has approaching geodesic. Moreover, since
\overline{D}^{G}=\overline{D}
, the limit (
3.2
) exists and thus big and small horospheres coincide.

Other Gromov hyperbolic domains for which it is known that the euclidean compactification is equivalent to the Gromov compactification include bounded pseudoconvex finite type domains in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

[
11
]
and Gromov hyperbolic bounded convex domains
[
6
]
. The following two open questions are thus natural.

Question 1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a smooth pseudoconvex finite type domain, Does
(D,k_{D})
have approaching geodesic? Are the horofunction and Gromov compactification equivalent?

Question 2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a Gromov hyperbolic bounded convex domain. Does
(D,k_{D})
have approaching geodesic? Are the horofunction and Gromov compactification equivalent?

In the latter question, if the domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is smoothly bounded then by
[
18
]
it has finite type.
It follows that the horofunction and Gromov compactification of
D
are equivalent, since they satisfy the (weaker) approaching
complex
geodesic property which we now introduce.
Recall that a complex geodesic is a holomorphic map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
that is an isometry with respect to the Kobayashi distances. In particular, if
\varphi
is a complex geodesic then the curve
\gamma\colon[0,+\infty)\to D
given by

\gamma(t)=\varphi(\tanh(t/\penalty 2))

(3.3)

is a geodesic ray of
(D,k_{D})
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain.
We call
complex geodesic ray

\gamma\colon[0,+\infty)\to D
any geodesic ray which is contained in a complex geodesic, or equivalently the curve defined in (
3.3
)
where
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
is a complex geodesic.
We say that
D
has
approaching complex geodesics
if any two complex geodesic rays which are asymptotic are strongly asymptotic.

The proof of Theorem
2.11
can be adapted to show the following.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.11]

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a Gromov hyperbolic bounded convex domain. Assume that
D
has approaching complex geodesics. Then
\overline{D}^{G}
and
\overline{D}^{H}
are equivalent.

A scaling argument similar to the one in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, using McNeal’s scaling instead of the squeezing function yields the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

[
3
, Proposition 5.4]

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a bounded convex finite type domain. Then
D
has approaching complex geodesics, and thus
\overline{D}^{G}
and
\overline{D}^{H}
are equivalent.

The following weaker version of Question 2 is also open.

Question 3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain such that
(D,k_{D})
is Gromov hyperbolic. Does
(D,k_{D})
have approaching complex geodesics?

4.
Dynamics of non-expanding self-maps

In this section we discuss the dynamics of non-expanding self-maps of proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic spaces (see
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
). In particular we show that the approaching geodesic property implies dynamical results, like the equality between the minimal displacement and the divergence rate of isometries, or the existence and uniqueness of an axis for hyperbolic isometries.

In
[
3
, Section 6]
several classical concepts from complex dynamics were generalized to this metric setting as follows.

Definition 4.1

(Dilation)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space.
Let
f\colon X\to X
be a non-expanding self-map.
Given
\eta\in\partial_{G}X
, the
dilation
of
f
at
\eta
with respect to the base point
p\in X
is the number
\lambda_{\eta,p}>0
defined by

\log\lambda_{\eta,p}=\liminf_{z\to\eta}d(z,p)-d(f(z),p).

Definition 4.2

(Geodesic region)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space.
Given
R>0
and a geodesic ray
\gamma\in\mathscr{R}(X)
, the
geodesic region

A(\gamma,R)
is the open subset of
X
of the form

A(\gamma,R):=\{x\in X\colon d(x,\gamma)<R\}.

The point
[\gamma]\in\partial_{G}X
is called the
vertex
of the geodesic region.

Definition 4.3

(Geodesic limit)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space and let
Y
be a Hausdorff topological space. Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
a map, and let
\eta\in\partial_{G}X
,
\xi\in Y
. We say that
f
has
geodesic limit

\xi
at
\eta

if for every sequence
(x_{n})
converging to
\eta
contained in a geodesic region with vertex
\eta
, the sequence
(f(x_{n}))
converges to
\xi
.

Definition 4.4

(Boundary regular fixed points)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space. Let
f\colon X\to X
be a non-expanding map. We say that a point
\eta\in\partial_{G}X
is a
boundary regular fixed point
(BRFP for short) if

\lambda_{\eta,p}<+\infty
and if
f
has geodesic limit
\eta
at
\eta
.

In general the dilation at a BRFP may depend on the chosen base-point
p
. This is not the case if the horofunction and Gromov compactifications are equivalent (see
[
3
, Proposition 6.30]
).
Hence, from now on, if
\overline{X}^{H}
is topologically equivalent to
\overline{X}^{G}
and
\eta\in\partial_{G}X
is a BRFP we denote with
\lambda_{\eta}
the dilation
\lambda_{\eta,p}
.
In this case the dilation
\lambda_{\eta}
can be conveniently calculated along geodesic rays, as the following result shows.

Lemma 4.5
.

[
4
, Lemma 3.12]

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space such that
\overline{X}^{H}
is equivalent to
\overline{X}^{G}
.
Let
f\colon X\to X
be a non-expanding self-map,
let
\eta\in\partial_{G}X
be a BRFP and let
\gamma\colon[0,+\infty)\rightarrow X
be a geodesic with
\gamma(+\infty)=\eta
. Then

\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(p,\gamma(t))-d(p,f(\gamma(t)))=\log\lambda_{\eta}.

(4.1)

We now recall the definition of two numbers that play an important role in the study of the dynamics of non-expanding maps.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
f\colon X\to X
be a non-expanding self-map.

•

Let
x\in X
, the
divergence rate
(or
escape rate
, or
translation length
)
c(f)
of
f
is the limit

c(f):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{d(x,f^{n}(x))}{n}.

The limit exists thanks to the subadditivity of the sequence
d(x,f^{n}(x))
, and does not depend on the choice of
x\in X
. Clearly for all
n>0
we have
c(f^{n})=nc(f)
.

•

The
minimal displacement

\tau(f)
of
f
is defined as

\tau(f):=\inf_{x\in X}d(x,f(x)).

Since

d(x,f^{n}(x))\leq nd(x,f(x))
we have that
c(f)\leq\tau(f)
and
\tau(f^{n})\leq n\tau(f)
.

Using the divergence rate we can classify non-expanding self-maps of
X
as follows. We say that
f
is
elliptic
it it has a bounded orbit (or equivalently by Calka’s theorem
[
8
]
if every orbit is bounded). If
f
is not elliptic, we say that it is
parabolic
if
c(f)=0
and that it is
hyperbolic
if
c(f)>0
.

Remark 4.7
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
f\colon X\to X
be a non-expanding self-map. Then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\tau(f^{n})}{n}=c(f).

Indeed for all
n>0
,

c(f)=\frac{c(f^{n})}{n}\leq\frac{\tau(f^{n})}{n}

and if
x\in X

\frac{\tau(f^{n})}{n}\leq\frac{d(x,f^{n}(x))}{n}\to c(f).

A natural problem is to characterize when
c(f)=\tau(f)
, see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 1]
, where such equality is proved under the assumption that
X
is complete and satisfies a mild form of Busemann’s classical non-positive curvature condition.
The situation in the elliptic case is trivial: if
f
is elliptic then
c(f)=0
but
\tau(f)=0
if and only if
f
has a fixed point. Hence we will focus on the non-elliptic case.
We first recall two results about the Denjoy–Wolff point of a non-elliptic map.

Theorem 4.8
.

[
14
]

Let
(X,d)
be a proper Gromov hyperbolic metric space. Let
f\colon X\to X
be a non-elliptic non-expanding self-map. Then there exists a unique
\zeta\in\partial_{G}X
, called the Denjoy–Wolff point of
f
, so that for all
x\in X
we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}f^{n}(x)=\zeta.

Theorem 4.9
.

[
3
, Theorem 6.32]

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space such that
\overline{X}^{H}
is topologically equivalent to
\overline{X}^{G}
. Let
f\colon X\to X
be a non-elliptic non-expanding self-map.
Let
\zeta\in\partial_{G}X
be its Denjoy–Wolff point. Then
\zeta
is the unique BRFP of
f
with dilation
\lambda_{\zeta}\leq 1
. Moreover

c(f)=-\log\lambda_{\zeta}.

(4.2)

We now introduce a condition on
f
implying the equality of divergence rate and minimal displacement.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space such that
\overline{X}^{H}
is topologically equivalent to
\overline{X}^{G}
.
Let
f\colon X\rightarrow X
be a non-expanding map, let
\eta\in\partial_{G}X
be a BRFP, and let
\gamma\colon[0,+\infty)\rightarrow X
be a geodesic with
\gamma(+\infty)=\eta
.
Suppose that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\inf_{s\geq 0}d(f(\gamma(t)),\gamma(s))=0,

(4.3)

then

\lim_{t\to+\infty}d\big(f(\gamma(t))\,,\,\gamma(t-\log\lambda_{\eta})\big)=0.

(4.4)

In particular if the map is non-elliptic then
\tau(f)=c(f)
.

Proof.

Let
s_{t}\geq 0
such that
d(f(\gamma(t)),\gamma)=d(f(\gamma(t)),\gamma(s_{t}))\to 0
.
By Proposition
4.5
we have

\displaystyle\log\lambda_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\gamma(t),\gamma(0))-d(f(\gamma(t)),\gamma(0))

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\gamma(t),\gamma(0))-d(\gamma(s_{t}),\gamma(0))

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to+\infty}t-s_{t}.

So,

\displaystyle d\big(f(\gamma(t))\,,\,\gamma(t-\log\lambda_{\eta})\big)

\displaystyle\leq d(f(\gamma(t)),\gamma(s_{t}))+d(\gamma(s_{t}),\gamma(t-\log\lambda_{\eta}))

\displaystyle=d(f(\gamma(t)),\gamma(s_{t}))+|t-s_{t}-\log\lambda_{\eta}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0

In particular if the map is not elliptic, applying (
4.4
) and (
4.2
) to the Denjoy-Wolff point
\zeta\in\partial_{G}X
we obtain,

\tau(f)\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}d(f(\gamma(t)),\gamma(t))\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}d\big(f(\gamma(t))\,,\,\gamma(t-\log\lambda_{\zeta})\big)+d\big(\gamma(t-\log\lambda_{\zeta}),\gamma(t)\big)=-\log\lambda_{\zeta}=c(f).

∎

By Remark
2.8

4.3
) is satisfied in the following interesting case.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space such that
\overline{X}^{H}
is topologically equivalent to
\overline{X}^{G}

f\colon X\rightarrow X
be a non-elliptic isometry, then

\tau(f)=c(f).

Example 4.12

Let

Another concept related to the divergence rate and minimal displacement is the axis of an isometry.

Definition 4.14
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
f\colon X\to X
be an isometry without fixed points. An
axis
is a setwise invariant geodesic line
\gamma
.
An isometry without fixed points is
axial
if there exists an axis.

Remark 4.15
.

Let
\gamma
be an axis.
Up to changing the orientation of the geodesic line
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
if needed, there exists
a>0
such that

f(\gamma(t))=\gamma(t+a),\ \ \ \forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 4.16
.

If
(X,d)
is a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space, then an axial isometry
f
is always hyperbolic (i.e.
c(f)>0
) and an axis
\gamma
is a geodesic line connecting the Denjoy-Wolff point of
f^{-1}
to the Denjoy-Wolff point of
f
. Moreover if
a>0
is such that
f(\gamma(t))=\gamma(t+a)
then
a=c(f)
, indeed

a=\frac{d(\gamma(na),\gamma(0))}{n}=\frac{d(f^{n}(\gamma(0)),\gamma(0))}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}c(f).

In the next Lemma we show that the minimal displacement plays a relevant role in the construction of axes. With
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
we indicate the floor function.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
f\colon X\to X
be an isometry with
\tau(f)=c(f)>0
. Assume that the minimal displacement is attained, that is there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that
d(x_{0},f(x_{0}))=\tau(f)
. Then
x_{0}
is contained in an axis.

Proof.

First of all for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\tau(f^{n})\leq|n|\tau(f)=|n|c(f)=c(f^{n})

which implies
\tau(f^{n})=|n|\tau(f)
.
Notice that up to a rescaling of the distance we can suppose
\tau(f)=c(f)=1
.
Let

\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
be a geodesic with
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=f(x_{0})
. We claim that the curve

\Gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
given by

\Gamma(t)=f^{\lfloor t\rfloor}(\gamma(t-\lfloor t\rfloor)),\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R}

is an axis for
f
.
Clearly
\Gamma
is setwise invariant curve, it remains to show that it is a geodesic line.
Fix
s\leq t
. If
\lfloor s\rfloor=\lfloor t\rfloor
, since
\gamma
is a geodesic segment we have

d(\Gamma(s),\Gamma(t))=d(\gamma(s-\lfloor s\rfloor),\gamma(t-\lfloor t\rfloor))=t-s.

Now assume
\lfloor s\rfloor<\lfloor t\rfloor
. Then

d(\Gamma(s),\Gamma(t))\leq d(\Gamma(s),\Gamma(\lfloor s\rfloor+1))+d(\Gamma(\lfloor s\rfloor+1),\Gamma(\lfloor t\rfloor))+d(\Gamma(\lfloor t\rfloor),\Gamma(t))=t-s.

Now since
\tau(f^{n})=|n|
, we have for all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}

d(\Gamma(n),\Gamma(m))=d(f^{n}(x_{0}),f^{m}(x_{0}))=d(x_{0},f^{m-n}(x_{0}))\geq\tau(f^{m-n})=|m-n|,

hence

d(\Gamma(s),\Gamma(t))\geq d(\Gamma(\lfloor s\rfloor),\Gamma(\lfloor t\rfloor+1))-d(\Gamma(s),\Gamma(\lfloor s\rfloor))-d(\Gamma(\lfloor t\rfloor+1),\Gamma(t))=t-s.

∎

Proposition 4.18
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space and let
f\colon X\to X
be a hyperbolic isometry. Then the minimal displacement is attained.

Proof.

Let
(x_{\nu})
be such that

\tau_{\nu}:=d(x^{\nu},f(x^{\nu}))\searrow\tau(f)

and denote
x^{\nu}_{n}:=f^{n}(x^{\nu})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For a fixed
\nu
, we have for all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}

d(x^{\nu}_{n},x^{\nu}_{m})=d(f^{n}(x^{\nu}),f^{m}(x^{\nu}))=d(f^{n-m}(x^{\nu}),x^{\nu})\leq|n-m|d(x^{\nu},f(x^{\nu}))=|n-m|\tau_{\nu}.

On the other hand

d(f^{n-m}(x^{\nu}),x^{\nu})\geq c(f^{n-m})=|n-m|c(f),

in other words the sequences
(x^{\nu}_{n})_{n}
are discrete quasi geodesic with uniformly bounded constants joining the two boundary fixed points of
f
, so by the visibility property (see
[
13
, Chapter 5]
) there exists
n_{\nu}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
f^{n_{\nu}}(x^{\nu})
converges to
x_{0}\in X
with the property that
d(x_{0},f(x_{0}))=\tau(f)
.

∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space and let
f\colon X\to X
be a hyperbolic isometry. Then
f
is axial if and only if
c(f)=\tau(f)
.

Proof.

If
\gamma
is an axis, then

c(f)=d(\gamma(c(f)),\gamma(0))=d(f(\gamma(0)),\gamma(0))\geq\tau(f).

The converse implication follows from Lemma
4.17
and Proposition
4.18
.
∎

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
(X,d)
be a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric space and
f\colon X\to X
be a hyperbolic isometry with Denjoy-Wolff point
\zeta\in\partial_{G}X
.
If
(X,d)
has approaching geodesics at
\zeta
then
f
is axial and the axis is unique.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.11
and Corollary
4.19
,
f
is axial. Let
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
be two axes. Since the metric space
(X,d)
has approaching geodesics at
\zeta
, it follows that, up to a change of parametrization,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))=0

and for
i=1,2,

f(\gamma_{i}(t))=\gamma_{i}(t+c(f)),\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, for all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

d(\gamma_{1}(t_{0}),\gamma_{2}(t_{0}))=d(f^{n}(\gamma_{1}(t_{0})),f^{n}(\gamma_{2}(t_{0})))=d(\gamma_{1}(t_{0}+nc(f))),\gamma_{2}(t_{0}+nc(f)))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

and thus
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
.
∎

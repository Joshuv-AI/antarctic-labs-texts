High hemoglobin-to-red cell distribution width ratio reduces adverse events in patients with pacemaker implantation
Authors: Liyou Lian, Rujie Zheng, Kaijing Wang, Chen Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe prognostic significance of the hemoglobin-to-red blood cell distribution width (Hb/RDW) ratio in patients undergoing pacemaker implantation (PMI) remains uncertain. Our study aimed to explore the prospective relationship between the Hb/RDW ratio and the risk of major cardiovascular events (MCEs) in Chinese patients with PMI.MethodsA total of 595 patients with permanent PMI were enrolled. Patients were divided into the high-Hb/RDW (≥ 9.1 g/L per percent) and the low-Hb/RDW (< 9.1 g/L per percent) groups according to the levels of the Hb/RDW ratio using the receiver operating characteristic curve. The primary outcome was the composite MCEs including heart failure hospitalization, myocardial infarction, stroke, and all-cause death, presented as hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) estimated by the Cox Proportional Hazards models, propensity score (PS) adjusted, and inverse probability weighting (IPW) analyses, respectively.ResultsDuring the median 2-year follow-up period, 95 (16%) MCEs were identified among the 595 patients with PMI Patients with a high Hb/RDW ratio exhibited a lower risk of MCEs compared to those with a low Hb/RDW ratio (adjusted HR: 0.456; 95% CI: 0.376–0.848). In additional analyses with different statistical models, the multivariate-adjusted HR was 0.592 (95% CI: 0.380–0.920) for the PS-adjusted analysis and 0.444 (95% CI: 0.271–0.729) for the IPW, respectively. There were no interactions between subgroups and Hb/RDW in our analysis.ConclusionHigh levels of the Hb/RDW ratio were linked to a lower risk of composite MCEs. The Hb/RDW ratio was a better predictor of MCEs than Hb or RDW alone.

Introduction

Permanent pacemaker implantation (PMI) is a valuable clinical intervention [1]. In populations relying on cardiac pacing, it is well-established that pacemakers contribute to reducing hospitalization rates for heart failure and atrial fibrillation [2]. However, despite the benefits, some patients still face heart failure hospitalization, infection, and mortality post-PMI [3]. Various factors contribute to these adverse outcomes, and research suggested that age, gender, diabetes, renal failure, and other variables may impact the clinical outcomes in PMI patients [4, 5].

Although the nutritional status is associated with poorer outcomes, the nutritional well-being of PMI patients is often overlooked [6]. Anemia, defined as a low concentration of blood hemoglobin (Hb), is a critical nutrition-related parameter linked to heart failure [7], cardiovascular mortality, and all-cause mortality in long-term follow-up [8]. Hematology laboratories conventionally utilize red blood cell distribution width (RDW) to categorize anemia, measuring the heterogeneity between erythrocytes. Recent research has also associated RDW with complex outcomes in various conditions, including malignancies, thrombotic diseases, heart failure, and all-cause mortality [9]. While Hb and RDW serve as valuable prognostic indicators for numerous illnesses, their stability in clinical practice is influenced by both external and internal factors [10, 11].

Sun et al. [12] initially proposed the use of the Hb/RDW ratio as a prognostic predictor in cancer patients, finding a strong association with long-term prognosis in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients. In recent years, the Hb/RDW ratio has also been linked to the prognosis of cardiovascular illnesses (CVD) [13]. However, the extent to which the Hb/RDW ratio can predict PMI in patients remains unclear.

Therefore, our study aimed to explore the relationship between the Hb/RDW ratio and the risk of major cardiovascular events (MCEs) in Chinese PMI patients. We employed univariate and multivariate regression models, as well as two propensity score (PS)-based methods, to investigate this association.

Methods

Study subjects

This retrospective study included patients from the First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University who underwent successful Permanent Pacemaker Implantation (PMI) with complete follow-up data between January 2011 and December 2020. A total of 595 patients were enrolled in the study. Prior to the procedure, routine assessments such as blood count, electrocardiogram (ECG), and echocardiography were conducted to evaluate the patients’ clinical status. The graphical summary was presented in Fig. 1. Based on the Hb/RDW ratio, patients were categorized into high-Hb/RDW (> 9.1 g/L per percent) and low-Hb/RDW (≤ 9.1 g/L per percent) groups using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (Fig. 2). The study received approval from the Institutional Review Board (YS2022-306).

Data collection

Data collection encompassed potential prognostic factors for PMI, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), alcohol and smoking history, and concomitant diseases, extracted from electronic medical records. Additionally, complete blood count and a comprehensive metabolic panel were recorded, with laboratory indicators obtained within a week before the PMI surgery. The Hb/RDW ratio was computed as Hb (g/L) divided by RDW (%) [12], both derived from the same blood sample taken a week before surgery.

Clinical follow-up

All patients were followed clinically for at least a month, and data was collected by experienced coordinators. This study’s primary outcome was the composite MCEs, defined as hospitalization for heart failure, myocardial infarction, stroke, or all-cause death [6]. Heart failure hospitalization was defined as unexpected hospitalization due to heart failure. Myocardial infarction was defined as acute symptoms of chest pain along with new ST-segment changes or elevation of cardiac markers to at least twice the upper normal limit. Stroke was a transient ischemic attack or cerebrovascular accident, which the blood flow to the brain was blocked. All-cause mortality was defined as death from any disease. BMI over 25 kg/m2 was considered overweight, and over 30 kg/m2 was obesity. Diabetes was a long-term condition that manifested itself either when the pancreas lost its ability to produce insulin or when the body was unable to make effective use of the insulin. Chronic kidney disease was characterized by kidney impairment or a glomerular filtration rate that was lower than 60 ml/min/1.73 m [2] for more than three months, regardless of the underlying reason. Hypertension was diagnosed if the systolic blood pressure on both days was ≥ 140 mmHg and/or the diastolic blood pressure on both days was ≥ 90 mmHg when measured on two different days. Hyperlipidemia was an increase in serum total cholesterol and/or triglycerides or a decrease in high-density lipoprotein cholesterol.

Statistical analysis

According to their normality, data were presented as percentages for categorical variables and as mean standard deviation (SD) or median value with interquartile range (IQR) for continuous variables. Student t-test or Mann–Whitney U test was used to analyze group differences for continuous variables, while chi-square or Fisher exact tests were used for categorical data. The Hb/RDW ratio was analyzed by generating a ROC curve and then calculating its area under the curve (AUC). Kaplan-Meier curves were used to plot the survival probability in both high- and low-Hb/RDW patients treated with PMI.

To test the robustness of primary results, inverse probability weighting (IPW) using PS method to reduce the influence of treatment-selection bias and potential confounding effects was used in our study. The PS was calculated using a logistic regression model with ten independent variables. All ten variables were chosen because of their potential impact on the Hb/RDW ratio, including age, sex, smoke history, drink history, overweight/obesity, concomitant diseases (diabetes, heart failure, hypertension, chronic kidney disease, hyperlipidemia). IPW used weights of [1/(1-PS)] for the low Hb/RDW ratio group and (1/PS) for the high Hb/RDW ratio group [14]. And we also use the PS as a covariate in a conventional Cox regression model to reduce the impact of potential confounding effects for further verifying our results [14]. In addition, we calculated hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) for MCEs obtained from crude or adjusted Cox regression models (the aforementioned 10 factors), using low Hb/RDW ratio levels as the reference category. All analyses were performed using R (Version 4.1.3), tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Hb/RDW ratio and MCEs risk

During the median follow-up duration of 2 years ranging from 1 to 117 months, 95 MCEs were identified. The higher levels of Hb/RDW ratio contributed to a lower risk of MCEs (crude HR: 0.495; 95% CI: 0.331–0.741), and this connection remained statistically significant after adjusting for variables in a multivariate Cox regression (HR: 0.456; 95% CI: 0.376–0.848), as showed in Table 2.

We calculated the IPW weights and the results showed that after weighting, we had greatly balanced the group differences (all variables standardized mean differences were low than 0.1, seen in Fig. 3). Then PS adjusted and IPW analysis showed that high Hb/RDW ratio was related to low risks of MCEs (HR: 0.592, 95% CI: 0.380–0.920; HR: 0.444, 95% CI: 0.271–0.729, respectively).

The cumulative risk of the individual or composite MCEs was compared between the high- and low-Hb/RDW ratio patients (Fig. 4). Over the follow-up period, the cumulative incidence rate of MCEs or heart failure was lower in the group with a high Hb/RDW ratio (HR: 0.444; 95% CI: 0.271–0.729; HR: 0.412;95% CI: 0.213–0.798, respectively). However, the cumulative incidence rate of stroke, myocardial infarction and death during the follow-up was alike between the low and high Hb/RDW ratio groups.

Discussion

This study aimed to assess the association between the Hb/RDW ratio and MCEs in patients with PMI. The key finding of the study indicated that a lower Hb/RDW ratio was strongly linked to an increased risk of MCEs. Moreover, the study revealed that using a single indicator, either Hb or RDW alone, was not an effective predictor of MCEs.

Several epidemiological studies had established a connection between anemia and long-term prognosis in various diseases [15, 16]. Both Hb and RDW serve as laboratory indicators of anemia. However, the influence of multiple factors on Hb or RDW can lead to inconsistent results [10, 11]. To address this, we combined the two measurements. A decrease in the Hb/RDW ratio was associated with an increase in RDW, which has been linked to poor clinical outcomes in various hematological conditions [9]. RDW, a cost-effective metric used in laboratory hematology, reflects the heterogeneity of erythrocyte volume, known as anisocytosis. Anisocytosis has been implicated in a range of diseases, including CVD, cancer, and diabetes [17–19]. While RDW initially showed an association with MCEs, adding baseline health statuses to the analysis weakened this relationship, suggesting that disease status could impact RDW [20].

In our study, RDW could explain the MCEs results before adding the concomitant diseases in the Cox regression model. However, after we added several substantial baseline health statuses, the relationship between RDW and MCEs was diminished, which might be that the disease status could easily influence the RDW.

A decrease in the Hb/RDW ratio may be related to a decrease in Hb, and low hemoglobin concentration had been linked to various health issues, including cardiovascular illness, cognitive decline, sleep disturbances, low mood, and reduced quality of life [21, 22]. Although our study did not find a direct association between Hb and MCEs, the importance of Hb in pathological processes within the human body cannot be disputed.

Observational studies on the Hb/RDW ratio and frailty in coronary heart disease patients and heart failure patients supported the notion that a lower Hb/RDW ratio was an independent risk factor for adverse outcomes [13, 23]. Consistent with these findings, our study demonstrates that the Hb/RDW ratio was a more effective predictor of MCEs than Hb or RDW alone.

Multifaceted and difficult to pin down, the pathophysiology of anemia in patients with PMI is still accurate. Several factors, such as inflammation, bleeding, available iron shortage, hemodilution, erythropoietin signaling, and HIF-1, had been investigated in other investigations [24]. Multiple causes contributed to the increment of RDW. First, an unbalance between hematopoiesis and RBC survival might underlie an elevated RDW [25]. RDW plasma levels were elevated because of a decrease in red blood cell formation and a delay in removing senescent red blood cells from the bloodstream. Second, there were many pathways that a high RDW can be a sign of inflammation [26]. For instance, anemia and elevated RDW might result from the action of proinflammatory cytokines like interferon γ and tumor necrosis factor α, which could alter iron metabolism and the bone marrow’s ability to produce red blood cells [9, 27]. In contrast, increasing oxidative stress due to inflammation might shorten RBC lifespan and cause premature release of red blood cells from the bone marrow, both of which contributed to an increase in RDW [28]. Third, other physiological processes, such as aging, pregnancy, after erythropoietin stimulation and exercise, might also contribute to a rise in RDW [9].

Limitation

Our study first evaluated the long-term predictive significance of the Hb/RDW ratio in patients with PMI. However, our study had several limitations. First, because this was an observational study conducted at a single center with a limited number of patients, it is possible that unaccounted-for confounding factors influenced the results. Second, our sample size was small, and our findings were only applicable to people who had pacemakers implanted.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study establishes the Hb/RDW ratio as an independent prognostic indicator for MCEs in PMI patients. It proves to be a more reliable predictor than Hb or RDW alone. Early detection of MCEs risk, guided by the Hb/RDW ratio, is crucial for improving patient outcomes. Future high-quality research is needed to validate our results.

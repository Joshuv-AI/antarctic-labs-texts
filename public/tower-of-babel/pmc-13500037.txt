Author's Reply: Neutrophil‐Macrophage Interactions in Gastric Cancer
Authors: Keita H Nagaoka, Hiroki Yamashita, Chiaki Mashima, Guoqiang Zhang, Hitoshi Saito, Tatsunori Minamide, Takuya Nakatsuru, Ryo Kamata, Shunsuke A Sakai, Gaku Yamamoto, Tomoko Y Morita, Kazuki Nakai, Yumi Hakozaki, Kumi Kinoshita, Yui Takahashi, Izuma Nakayama, Akihito Kawazoe, Sai M Pulukuri, Yuko Ishii, Hiroshi Haeno, Yukie Kashima, Kohei Shitara, Tomonori Yano, Takeshi Saito, Akihiro Ohashi
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the recent letter regarding our published study on the critical role of neutrophil‐macrophage interactions in reshaping the gastric cancer tumor microenvironment during chemoimmunotherapy [1]. We sincerely appreciate these thoughtful, constructive, and highly sophisticated methodological considerations, which provide valuable avenues for further expanding the translational impact of our findings.

The letter elegantly highlights the opportunities presented by our cohort size (n=18) and cross‐sectional sampling design. We completely agree that larger, prospectively collected, and paired pre and posttreatment cohorts represent the gold standard for clinical translation, as we noted in our original publication [1]. Given the inherent practical challenges of acquiring sequential drug‐exposed clinical biopsies, we designed our study to generate a high‐resolution, hypothesis‐generating dataset. To robustly complement this clinical cohort, we utilized a well‐controlled in vivo syngeneic murine model [1]. This dual approach successfully demonstrated that neutrophil depletion alters the antitumor efficacy of chemo‐immunotherapy and selectively modulates macrophage infiltration [1], thereby supporting the biological relevance of our single‐cell observations. We look forward to future multi‐center prospective studies that will naturally build upon these foundational insights.

Regarding the recommendation for spatial or in situ validation of the neutrophil‐macrophage physical proximity, we fully concur with this excellent perspective. Incorporating spatial transcriptomics or multiplexed immunohistofluorescence is indeed a powerful next step to visually map these cellular neighborhoods. While our current work relied on in silico cell–cell communication modeling, we proactively highlighted the lack of spatial resolution as an important area for future investigation [1]. We believe that coupling our functional in vivo data with future spatial validation will further refine our understanding of the tumor microenvironment's spatial architecture.

We also appreciate the focus on the technical aspects of capturing low‐RNA‐content neutrophils and ensuring lineage purity. This is indeed a critical quality control consideration in single‐cell transcriptomics. To address potential monocyte or macrophage contamination, our pipeline strictly evaluated key lineage‐specific markers across all neutrophil clusters [1]. In our published supplemental data, canonical macrophage and monocyte markers (including CD14, FCN1, and C1Q family genes) were minimally expressed or absent in our neutrophil subclusters, including the HLA‐DRA + Neut4 population, while neutrophil‐specific markers (S100A8, S100A9) remained robustly high [1]. This distinct segregation on a per‐cell basis supports the high purity and fidelity of our annotated neutrophil subsets.

Finally, the suggestion to target specific ligand‐receptor axes, such as the CCL/CCR or MIF‐CD74 pathways, is highly sophisticated and scientifically exciting. While our current in vivo depletion studies established the overall functional necessity of neutrophils [1], directly blocking these specific signaling axes represents the logical next phase of our research. We have extensively discussed these pathways as promising therapeutic strategies in our article [1], and we are actively planning to explore these precise targetings in our upcoming studies.

In conclusion, we are deeply grateful for these stimulating comments. This constructive scientific dialogue significantly enriches the discussion surrounding our work and helps pave the way for future breakthroughs in understanding the gastric cancer microenvironment.

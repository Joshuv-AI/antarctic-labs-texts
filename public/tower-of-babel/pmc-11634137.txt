Regarding the evaluation of large language models in breast cancer clinical scenarios
Authors: Rongkang Li, Lei Peng, Rui Liang, Anguo Zhao, Jianye Zhong, Shaohua Zhang
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Large language models (LLMs) can respond to free-text queries without being specifically trained in the task in question, causing excitement about their use in healthcare settings1. They have been applied to various tasks in health care, ranging from answering medical examination questions to generating clinical reports2. We commend Deng et al.3 effort in evaluating the performance of LLMs in breast cancer clinical scenarios. The work provides valuable insights into the capabilities and limitations of ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4.0, and Claude2, contributing to the ongoing discourse on artificial intelligence (AI) applications in health care. However, there are several areas where the study could be enhanced to provide a more comprehensive and robust evaluation in the future.

First, the study’s reliance on a panel of five breast cancer specialists, while insightful, may not fully capture the diversity of clinical opinions and practices. Increasing the number of participating experts from various institutions and geographic regions would enhance the representativeness and generalizability of the findings. Then, the nine clinical scenarios selected are crucial but may not encompass the full spectrum of breast cancer management complexities. Future studies should incorporate a wider range of scenarios, including varied disease stages, patient demographics, and rare or atypical clinical presentations, to comprehensively evaluate the models’ performance across diverse contexts. The study primarily focuses on common clinical scenarios, potentially overlooking the performance of LLMs in rare or complex cases. Including scenarios involving less common conditions or intricate medical histories would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the models’ utility in real-world clinical practice. Last, AI models require regular updates to maintain accuracy and relevance4. While the study highlights the need for ongoing optimization, it does not address mechanisms for integrating continuous feedback and updates. Future research should explore the framework for improving iterative models, combine real-time clinical data and expert feedback, and create a large AI model suitable for the whole process management of breast cancer specific diseases.

We sincerely appreciate the valuable contributions made by the authors in this research. This study significantly advances our understanding of LLMs in breast cancer clinical applications. Addressing the areas mentioned would further strengthen the study’s impact and provide a more detailed roadmap for integrating LLMs into clinical practice. We encourage continued research in this promising field, with a focus on diverse clinical scenarios and mechanisms for continuous model improvement.

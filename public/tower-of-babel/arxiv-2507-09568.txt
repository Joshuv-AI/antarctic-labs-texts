A-type open S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) SL(2,\mathbb{C}) spin chain


Abstract
For the noncompact open S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) SL(2,\mathbb{C}) spin chain the eigenfunctions of the special matrix element of monodromy matrix are constructed. The key ingredients of the whole construction are local Yang-Baxter ℛ \mathcal{R} -operators, Q Q -operator and raising operators obtained by reduction from the Q Q -operator. The calculation of various scalar products and the proof of orthogonality are based on the properties of Q Q -operator and demonstrate its hidden role. The symmetry of eigenfunctions with respect to reflection of the spin variable s → 1 − s s\to 1-s is established. The Mellin-Barnes representation for eigenfunctions is derived and equivalence with initial coordinate representation is proved. The transformation from one representation to another is grounded on the application of A A -type Gustafson integral generalized to the complex field.

\displaystyle[z]^{a}\equiv z^{a}\bar{z}^{\bar{a}}=|z|^{a+\bar{a}}\,{e}^{\mathrm{i}(a-\bar{a})\arg z}.

4.4
Symmetric normalization

In Section
4.2
we discussed the symmetry of eigenfunctions under permutations of spectral parameters
\bm{x}_{n}

\Psi_{\tau\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})=c(\tau,\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})\,,

where
\tau\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{\tau(1)},\bar{x}_{\tau(1)},\ldots,x_{\tau(n)},\bar{x}_{\tau(n)})
, and
c(\tau,\bm{x}_{n})
is some constant. It is natural to choose the normalization of eigenfunctions, such that
c(\tau,\bm{x}_{n})=1
.
Do do so, hereinafter we use the new normalization for
\Lambda
-operators (
4.5
)

\Lambda_{k}(x)=\lambda_{k}(x)\,\mathcal{R}_{12}(x)\mathcal{R}_{23}(x)\cdots\mathcal{R}_{k-1\,k}(x)\,[z_{k0}]^{-s-x}\,,

where

\lambda_{k}(x)=\left(\frac{\pi\,[i]^{x-s}}{\mathbf{\Gamma}(s+x)}\right)^{k-1}\,.

(4.27)

That is, we multiplied the operator
\Lambda_{k}(x)
(
4.5
) by the normalization factor
\lambda_{k}(x)
. The choice of this constant is dictated by the commutation relation (
4.16
) between
\Lambda
-operators. In new normalization (
4.16
) takes the form

\Lambda_{n}(x)\,\Lambda_{n-1}(y)=\Lambda_{n}(y)\,\Lambda_{n-1}(x)\,.

Consequently, from the definition (
4.4
)

\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(x_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(x_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(x_{2})\,[z_{1}-z_{0}]^{-s-x_{1}}

follows the complete symmetry of
\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}
under permutations of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

\Psi_{\tau\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})=\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})\,.

5
Basic integrals

In this section we are going to calculate the following integrals

\displaystyle I(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n};x)=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{z}_{n}\,\overline{\Psi_{\bm{y}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})}\,[z_{n0}]^{x}\,\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})=\langle\Psi_{\bm{y}_{n}}|[z_{n0}]^{x}\,\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}\rangle\,,

(5.1)

\displaystyle J(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n-1};x)=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{z}_{n}\,\overline{\Psi_{\bm{y}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})}\,[z_{n0}]^{x}\,\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n-1}}(\bm{z}_{n-1})=\langle\Psi_{\bm{y}_{n}}|[z_{n0}]^{x}\,\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n-1}}\rangle

(5.2)

where in the second form we used the scalar product

\langle\Phi|\Psi\rangle=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{z}_{n}\,\overline{\Phi(\bm{z}_{n})}\,\Psi(\bm{z}_{n})

The integral (
5.2
) was calculated in
Val20
with the help of diagram technique described in Appendix
A
.
Here we give some alternative way of its computation.
The iterative construction of eigenfunctions allows to derive the recurrence
relations for considered integrals. The derivation is heavily based on the fact that integrals (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) contain Q-operator in a hidden form. There are the following recurrence formulae connecting basic integrals

\displaystyle I(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n};x)=\frac{[i]^{x_{n}-s-1}\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(s+x-x_{n})\,\lambda_{n}(x_{n})}{\mathbf{\Gamma}(x)\,q^{*}(x_{n}+1;\bm{y}_{n})}\,J(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n-1};x-x_{n}-s)\,,

(5.3)

\displaystyle J(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n-1};x)=\pi\,\mathbf{B}(s-y_{n},s+y_{n}+x)\,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{n}^{\ast}(y_{n})\,q^{*}(1-s;\bm{y}_{n-1})\,q(1-s;\bm{x}_{n-1})}{q(y_{n}+1;\bm{x}_{n-1})}\,I(\bm{y}_{n-1},\bm{x}_{n-1};x+y_{n}+s)

(5.4)

where
q(u\,,\bm{x}_{n})
is the eigenvalue (
4.10
) of
Q
-operator

\displaystyle q(u\,,\bm{x}_{n})=[i]^{(s-u)n}\,\frac{\mathbf{\Gamma}(u\,,\bm{x}_{n})}{\mathbf{\Gamma}(s\,,\bm{x}_{n})}

and
\mathbf{B}(a,b)
is the generalization of the Euler beta-function for the complex field
GGR
;
N

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(a,b)=\frac{\mathbf{\Gamma}(a)\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(b)}{\mathbf{\Gamma}(a+b)}

This system has iterative structure and can be solved using the initial condition

I(y_{1},x_{1};x)=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z\,\overline{\Psi_{y_{1}}(z)}\,[z-z_{0}]^{x}\,\Psi_{x_{1}}(z)=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z\,[z-z_{0}]^{x+y_{1}-x_{1}-1}=2\pi^{2}\delta^{(2)}(x+y_{1}-x_{1})

The main building blocks constructed from the eigenvalues of
Q
-operator
have the following explicit form

\displaystyle q^{*}(1-s;\bm{y}_{n})\,q(1-s;\bm{x}_{n})=[-1]^{\underline{\bm{x}_{n}}-\underline{\bm{y}_{n}}}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbf{\Gamma}(s-y_{k})\mathbf{\Gamma}(s+y_{k})}{\mathbf{\Gamma}(s-x_{k})\mathbf{\Gamma}(s+x_{k})}

and

\frac{1}{q^{*}(x_{n}+1;\bm{y}_{n})\,q(y_{n}+1;\bm{x}_{n-1})}=[-1]^{\underline{\bm{x}_{n-1}}-(n-1)x_{n}}\,[i]^{(n-1)(x_{n}-y_{n})+s-x_{n}}\,\\
\frac{\mathbf{\Gamma}(x_{n}-y_{n})}{\mathbf{\Gamma}(s-y_{n})}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\mathbf{\Gamma}(s-x_{k})}{\mathbf{\Gamma}(s-y_{k})}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}(x_{n}-y_{k})\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(x_{k}-y_{n})

The general solution of the system reads

\displaystyle I(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n};x)=2\pi^{n^{2}+1}\,[-1]^{(n-1)(ns+\underline{\bm{y}_{n}})}\,\frac{\mathbf{\Gamma}^{n-1}(s\,,-\bm{y}_{n})\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})}{\mathbf{\Gamma}^{n-1}(s\,,-\bm{x}_{n})\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(x)}\,\delta^{(2)}(x-\underline{\bm{x}_{n}}+\underline{\bm{y}_{n}})

and

J(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n-1};x)=2\pi^{2+n(n-1)}[-1]^{(n-1)(ns+\underline{\bm{y}_{n}})}\\
\frac{\mathbf{\Gamma}^{n-1}(s\,,-\bm{y}_{n})\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n})\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(s\,,\bm{y}_{n})}{\mathbf{\Gamma}^{n-1}(s\,,-\bm{x}_{n-1})\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(2s+x)}\,\delta^{(2)}(x+s-\underline{\bm{x}_{n-1}}+\underline{\bm{y}_{n}})

(5.5)

where we used the following compact notations

\displaystyle\underline{\bm{y}_{n}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\ ;\ \mathbf{\Gamma}(\bm{x}_{m},\bm{y}_{n})=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{\Gamma}(x_{i}-y_{j})

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Gamma}(s\,,\bm{y}_{n})=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{\Gamma}(s-y_{k})\ ;\ \mathbf{\Gamma}(s\,,-\bm{y}_{n})=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{\Gamma}(s+y_{k})

(5.6)

The argument of the delta-function
\delta^{(2)}(v)
must be of the form

v=\frac{m}{2}+i\nu\,,\;\bar{v}=-\frac{m}{2}+i\nu\,,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z},\;\nu\in\mathbb{R}\,,

and
\delta^{(2)}(v)
is defined in a natural way as the product of the Kronecker symbol

\delta_{m,0}
with respect to discrete variable and delta-function

\delta(\nu)
with respect to continuous variable

\delta^{(2)}(v)=\delta_{m,0}\,\delta(\nu)

(5.7)

To avoid any misunderstanding we demonstrate the derivation of the recurrence
relations starting from the simplest example. We have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{y}_{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\lambda_{2}(y_{2})\,[z_{20}]^{-s-y_{2}}\,\mathcal{R}_{12}(y_{2})\,\Psi_{y_{1}}

\lambda_{2}^{\ast}(y_{2})\,\langle\Psi_{y_{1}}|[\hat{p}_{1}]^{2s-1}\,[z_{12}]^{s-y_{2}-1}\,[z_{20}]^{s-1+y_{2}+x}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{-s-y_{2}}\,\Psi_{x_{1}}\rangle

or explicitly

J(\bm{y}_{2},x_{1};x)=\\
\lambda_{2}^{\ast}(y_{2})\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{2}\,\overline{\Psi_{y_{1}}(z_{1})}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{2s-1}\,[z_{12}]^{s-y_{2}-1}\,[z_{20}]^{s-1+y_{2}+x}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{-s-y_{2}}\,\Psi_{x_{1}}(z_{1})

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{2}\,[z_{12}]^{s-y_{2}-1}\,[z_{20}]^{s+y_{2}+x-1}=\pi\,\mathbf{B}(s-y_{2},s+y_{2}+x)\,[z_{10}]^{2s+x-1}

and everything is reduced to the single integral

J(\bm{y}_{2},x_{1};x)=\\
\pi\,\mathbf{B}(s-y_{2},s+y_{2}+x)\,\lambda_{2}^{\ast}(y_{2})\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{1}\,\overline{\Psi_{y_{1}}(z_{1})}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{2s-1}\,[z_{10}]^{2s+x-1}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{-s-y_{2}}\,\Psi_{x_{1}}(z_{1})=\\
\pi\,\mathbf{B}(s-y_{2},s+y_{2}+x)\,\lambda_{2}^{\ast}(y_{2})\,\langle\Psi_{y_{1}}|[\hat{p}_{1}]^{2s-1}\,[z_{10}]^{2s+x-1}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{-s-y_{2}}\,\Psi_{x_{1}}\rangle

Next step we transform the operator in the middle to
the product of
Q
-operators

\displaystyle[\hat{p}_{1}]^{2s-1}\,[z_{10}]^{2s+x-1}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{-s-y_{2}}=

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}[\hat{p}_{1}]^{2s-1}\,[z_{10}]^{2s-1}}\,[z_{10}]^{s+x+y_{2}}\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}[z_{10}]^{1-2s}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{1-2s}}\,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[\hat{p}_{1}]^{2s-1}\,[z_{10}]^{-y_{2}-1+s}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{-s-y_{2}}}=

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}_{10}(1-s)}\,[z_{10}]^{s+x+y_{2}}\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathcal{R}_{10}(1-s)}\,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}_{10}(y_{2})}=Q^{\dagger}_{1}(1-s)\,[z_{10}]^{s+x+y_{2}}\,Q_{1}(1-s)\,Q^{\dagger}_{1}(y_{2})

where we used the following expressions for

-operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{10}(x)=[\hat{p}_{1}]^{x+s-1}\,[z_{10}]^{x-s}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{1-2s}\ \ ;\ \ \mathcal{R}_{10}(1-s)=[z_{10}]^{1-2s}\,[\hat{p}_{1}]^{1-2s}

The functions
\Psi_{y_{1}}
and
\Psi_{x_{1}}

are eigenfunctions of Q-operator and
Q^{\dagger}(y_{2})=Q^{-1}(y_{2}+1)
so that

\langle\Psi_{y_{1}}|Q^{\dagger}_{1}(1-s)\,[z_{10}]^{s+x+y_{2}}\,Q_{1}(1-s)\,Q^{\dagger}(y_{2})\,\Psi_{x_{1}}\rangle=\\
\frac{q^{*}(1-s;y_{1})\,q(1-s;x_{1})}{q(y_{2}+1;x_{1})}\,\langle\Psi_{y_{1}}|[z_{10}]^{s+x+y_{2}}\,\Psi_{x_{1}}\rangle

5.4
)
in the simplest case

\displaystyle J(\bm{y}_{2},x_{1};x)=\pi\,\mathbf{B}(s-y_{2},s+y_{2}+x)\,\frac{\lambda_{2}^{\ast}(y_{2})\,q^{*}(1-s;y_{1})\,q(1-s;x_{1})}{q(y_{2}+1;x_{1})}\,I(y_{1},x_{1};s+x+y_{2})

We have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{y}_{3}}(\bm{z}_{3})=\lambda_{3}(y_{3})\,[z_{30}]^{-s-y_{3}}\,\mathcal{R}_{12}(y_{3})\,\mathcal{R}_{23}(y_{3})\,\Psi_{\bm{y}_{2}}(\bm{z}_{2})

6
Orthogonality and completeness

The functions
\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})
form a complete orthogonal set in the Hilbert space (
3.1
) which is the space

of functions of

with the scalar product

where

identity operator on the space of symmetric functions

\displaystyle\delta^{(2)}\big(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n}\big)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\delta^{(2)}(x_{1}-y_{\tau(1)})\ldots\delta^{(2)}(x_{n}-y_{\tau(n)})

(6.2)

is the symmetric group of degree
n
.
The parameters

have the form

x_{i}=\tfrac{n_{i}}{2}+\mathrm{i}\nu_{i},\;\bar{x}_{i}=-\tfrac{n_{i}}{2}+\mathrm{i}\nu_{i}\,,\quad y_{j}=\tfrac{m_{j}}{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{j},\;\bar{y}_{j}=-\tfrac{m_{j}}{2}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{j}\,,

(6.3)

where

, the quantity
\sigma
is defined in (
2.5

The delta-function

5.7

6.1

The so called Sklyanin measure

is defined by formula

\mu(\bm{x}_{n})=\frac{1}{\pi^{n^{2}}}\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}\,n!}\,\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2}\,,

(6.4)

where

.

The completeness relation has the form

\int\mathcal{D}\bm{x}_{n}\,\mu(\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{z}_{n})\,\overline{\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{w}_{n})}=\delta^{2}(z_{1}-w_{1})\ldots\delta^{2}(z_{n}-w_{n})\,,

\int\mathcal{D}\bm{x}_{n}=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\int\mathcal{D}x_{k}\ \ \ ;\ \ \ \int\mathcal{D}x_{k}=\sum\limits_{n_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}+\sigma}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}

A.1
Gamma function of the complex field

The gamma function of the complex field
(
GGR
, Section 1.4)
,
(
N
, Section 1.3)

is the ratio of standard Euler gamma-functions and is defines as follows

\bm{\Gamma}(a)=\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(1-\bar{a})}

We denote its products as

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}(a,b)=\bm{\Gamma}(a)\bm{\Gamma}(b).

Notice that
\bm{\Gamma}(a)
depends on two parameters
(a,\bar{a})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
such that
a-\bar{a}\in\mathbb{Z}
, but for brevity we display only the first one. Moreover, for
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
we write

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}(a+\rho)\equiv\frac{\Gamma(a+\rho)}{\Gamma(1-\bar{a}-\rho)}.

From the well-known properties of the ordinary gamma function it is easy to prove the following relations

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}(a+1)=-a\bar{a}\,\bm{\Gamma}(a),

(A.1)

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}(a)=[-1]^{a}\,\bm{\Gamma}(\bar{a}),

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}(a)\,\bm{\Gamma}(1-a)=[-1]^{a}.

(A.2)

and the complex conjugation rule:

\mathbf{\Gamma}(\rho+a)^{\ast}=\bm{\Gamma}(\rho-\bar{a})=[-1]^{a}\,\mathbf{\Gamma}(\rho-a),\qquad\rho\in\mathbb{R},

where

Appendix B
Formula for the multi-dimensional delta function

In this Appendix we are going to prove
the key formula

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{n\varepsilon}{(\underline{\bm{y}_{n-1}}-\underline{\bm{x}_{n-1}}+\varepsilon)\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(x_{k}-y_{k}+\varepsilon)}=(2\pi)^{n-1}\,\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\delta^{(2)}(x_{k}-y_{k})

(B.1)

For
n=2
the main formula states

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{2\varepsilon}{(y_{1}-x_{1}+\varepsilon)(x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon)}=2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{1}-y_{1})

and can be proven in a following way.
We have
x_{1}=\tfrac{k}{2}+\mathrm{i}\nu\,,y_{1}=\tfrac{p}{2}+\mathrm{i}\mu

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{2\varepsilon}{(y_{1}-x_{1}+\varepsilon)(x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon)}=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{y_{1}-x_{1}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon}\right]=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{\tfrac{k-p}{2}+i(\nu-\mu)+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\tfrac{p-k}{2}+i(\mu-\nu)+\varepsilon}\right]=

\displaystyle\delta_{pk}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{i(\nu-\mu)+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{i(\mu-\nu)+\varepsilon}\right]=2\pi\,\delta_{pk}\,\delta(\mu-\nu)=2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{1}-y_{1})

In the case
p\neq k
there is not any singularity for
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{R}

and everything cancel for
\varepsilon\to 0
– it is the origin of the Kroneker symbol
\delta_{pk}
. After that everything is reduced to the Sokhotski–Plemelj formula for
x\in\mathbb{R}

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left(\frac{1}{x-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{x+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}\right)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\delta(x)

Let us consider the next example
n=3
to clarify the origin
of the appearance of the factor
n
in the numerator

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{3\varepsilon}{(y_{1}+y_{2}-x_{1}-x_{2}+\varepsilon)(x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon)(x_{2}-y_{2}+\varepsilon)}=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{3\varepsilon}{(x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon)(y_{1}-x_{1}+2\varepsilon)}\left[\frac{1}{x_{2}-y_{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}+y_{2}-x_{1}-x_{2}+\varepsilon}\right]=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}-x_{1}+2\varepsilon}\right]\left[\frac{1}{x_{2}-y_{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}+y_{2}-x_{1}-x_{2}+\varepsilon}\right]

Repeating all previous steps it is easy to derive natural
generalization of
n=2
formula

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}-x_{1}+a\varepsilon}\right]=2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{1}-y_{1})

which is based on generalized Sokhotski–Plemelj formula

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left(\frac{1}{x-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{x+\mathrm{i}a\varepsilon}\right)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\delta(x)

valid for positive
a
.
We have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}-x_{1}+2\varepsilon}\right]\left[\frac{1}{x_{2}-y_{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}+y_{2}-x_{1}-x_{2}+\varepsilon}\right]=

\displaystyle 2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{1}-y_{1})\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{y_{2}-x_{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{x_{2}-y_{2}+\varepsilon}\right]=(2\pi)^{2}\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{1}-y_{1})\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{2}-y_{2})

In the general case we have very similar representation
of initial expression in the form of the product of simplest terms

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{n\varepsilon}{(\underline{\bm{y}_{n-1}}-\underline{\bm{x}_{n-1}}+\varepsilon)\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(x_{k}-y_{k}+\varepsilon)}=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}-y_{1}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}-x_{1}+(n-1)\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{x_{2}-y_{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{y_{1}+y_{2}-x_{1}-x_{2}+(n-2)\varepsilon}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{1}{\underline{\bm{y}_{n-1}}-\underline{\bm{x}_{n-1}}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{x_{n-1}-y_{n-1}+\varepsilon}\right)

For
\varepsilon\to 0
we have avalanche of simplifications –
the leftmost bracket produces
2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{1}-y_{1})
,
then next bracket gives
2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{2}-y_{2})
and similarly till the last bracket which gives
2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(x_{n-1}-y_{n-1})
.

Squares, three fleas, sporadic integer sets, and squares
Giedrius Alkauskas

Abstract
. In the plane, three integer points (“fleas”) are given. At every tick of time, two of them (say, P , Q P,Q ) instantly jump to two vacant points R , S R,S , so that P ​ Q ​ R ​ S PQRS is a square with that order of vertices. Description of all integers and half-integers which occur as areas of spanned triangles turns out to be unexpectedly intricate problem. Problem

In the plane, three points with integer coordinates are given, spanning a triangle

In this paper we contrive a method which allows to prove rigorously that if a certain value is missed in a finite number of steps, it is missed for good. A surprising fact is an “integrable”

If three vertices of triangle

\mathbb{Z}^{2}

Concerning the structure of sets of missed values, we pose the following

For primitive triangles, the sets
\mathscr{I}(\mathbf{K})\setminus\{n^{2}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}

\mathscr{H}(\mathbf{K})

If the conjecture is true, let

\leq N

We know that the function which tells the maximal number of steps needed for the Turing machine to stop over all Turing machine which eventually stop and which need

2.1.
Notations

Let
\mathbf{P}_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i})

\Delta
be the area of the spanned triangle, and

go round a triangle in a positive or negative direction. If all initial points belong to a proper square sublattice of

Define

\displaystyle A=(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{3})^{2},\quad B=(x_{3}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{3}-y_{1})^{2},\quad C=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}.

These expressions imply that

is even. One number out of these is even, the other two are odd (otherwise all points belonged to a index
2

\equiv 1\text{ (mod }4)

The following numbers are therefore integers:

\displaystyle\frac{B+C-A}{2}=a,\quad\frac{C+A-B}{2}=b,\quad\frac{A+B-C}{2}=c.

Since

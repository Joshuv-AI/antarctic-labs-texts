Shadowing property and entropy on hyperspace of continua induced by Morse gradient system
Jelena Katić, Darko Milinković

Abstract
It is known that Morse-Smale diffeomorpshisms have the shadowing property; however, the question of whether C ⁡ ( f ) C(f) also has the shadowing property when f f is Morse-Smale remains open and has been resolved only in a few specific cases [ 2 ] . We prove that if f : M → M f:M\to M is a time-one-map of Morse gradient flow, the induced map C ⁡ ( f ) : C ⁡ ( M ) → C ⁡ ( M ) C(f):C(M)\to C(M) on the hyperspace of subcontiuna does not have the shadowing property. Besides, we provide a simple criterion for f f that implies C ⁡ ( f ) C(f) has infinite topological entropy.

Introduction

A dynamical system is said to have the
shadowing property
(also known as the
pseudo-orbit tracing property
) if, informally speaking, every approximate orbit with small errors (i.e., a pseudo-orbit) can be closely followed by a true orbit. This concept was originally studied by Anosov
[
1
]
, Bowen
[
5
]
and Sinai
[
20
]
. If a dynamical system undergoes a small perturbation, the orbits of the perturbed system become pseudo-orbits of the original one. Therefore, shadowing is closely related to
stability
. It is also linked to
hyperbolicity
, a notion introduced by Smale
[
21
]
. More precisely, hyperbolic systems possess the shadowing property, which plays a crucial role in proving their stability. Pilyugin
[
17
]
demonstrated that structurally stable diffeomorphisms must satisfy a stronger form of the shadowing property. For a broader discussion on the significance of shadowing in both the qualitative theory of dynamical systems and numerical applications, we refer the reader to
[
16
,
18
]
.

Every continuous map on a compact metric space
X
induces a continuous map
2^{f}
(called the
induced map
) on the hyperspace
2^{X}
of all nonempty closed subsets of
X
. If
X
is connected, we consider the hyperspace
C(X)
consisting of all nonempty closed and connected subsets of
X
. A naturally arrising question is: what are the possible relations between the given (individual) dynamics on
X
and the induced one (collective dynamics) on the hyperspace. Over the past few decades, various results have been obtained in this direction, yet this relationship remains largely unexplored and continues to be of significant interest. For instance, it is known that certain dynamical properties of the system
(X,f)
are preserved in the induced system
(2^{X},2^{f})
(such as Li-Yorke chaos - see
[
11
]
- and positive topological entropy - see
[
14
]
). Converserly, some properties of
(2^{X},2^{f})
also imply the same properties for
(X,f)
(for example, transitivity - see
[
19
]
). However, for some properties there is no implicationn in any direction (for example, neither Devaney chaos of
(X,f)
implies Devaney chaos of
(2^{X},2^{f})
, nor Devaney chaos of
(2^{X},2^{f})
implies Devaney chaos of
(X,f)
, see
[
11
]
). Without attempting to be exhaustive, we mention just a few significant contributions in this area: Borsuk and Ulam
[
7
]
, Bauer and Sigmund
[
6
]
, Román-Flores
[
19
]
, Banks
[
4
]
, Acosta, Illanes and Méndez-Lango
[
3
]
.

It was proved in
[
9
]
that
f
has the shadowing property if and only if this is true for
2^{f}
. Additionally, if
C(f)
has shadowing property, the same it true for
f

[
9
]
. Morse-Smale diffeomorphisms are among the simplest dynamical systems, and they all possess the shadowing property. Regarding the shadowing property of
C(f)
, it was proved in
[
2
]
that if
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a Morse-Smale diffeomorphism or if
f:\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
is time-one-map of negative gradient system of Morse height function, then
C(f)
does not satisfy the shadowing property. Additionally, recent results provide both positive and negative answers to this question in the context of transitive Anosov diffeomorphisms and dendrite monotone maps, see
[
8
]
. However, the question whether
C(f)
has the shadowing property when
f
is Morse-Smale diffeomorphism remains an open question, even for
\mathbb{S}^{n}

Our contibution to this problem is the following negative result.

Theorem 1
.

For any time-one-map
f
of negative gradient system of Morse function that satisfies Morse-Smale condition, the induced homeomorphism
C(f)
does not satisfy the shadowing property.∎

The topological entropy
h(f)
is a classical measure of the complexity of a dynamical system
(X,f)
, quantifying the average exponential growth of the number of distinguishable orbit segments. It is obvious that the topological entropy of the iduced system on a hyperspace is greater than or equal to that of
f
, since the latter is a factor of the former, see Subsection
2.4
.
The topological entropy of the induced map was studied by Bauer and Sigmund in
[
6
]
, Kwietniak and Oprocha in
[
13
]
, Lampart and Raith
[
14
]
, Hernández and Méndez
[
10
]
, Arbieto and Bohorquez
[
2
]
, and others. It is known that if
f
has positive topological entropy, then
2^{f}
has infinite topological entropy
[
6
]
. Concerning the topological entropy of
C(f)
, there are examples where
h(f)>0
and
C(f)
is both finite and infinite, see
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
.

Regarding the case when
h(f)=0
, Lampart and Raith
[
14
]
proved that for any homeomoprhism
f
of the circle,
h(C(f))=0
(they also proved the same result for homeomorphisms of the interval and graphs). Arbieto and Bohorquez proved that a Morse-Smale diffeomorphism
f:M\to M
satisfies
h(C(f))=\infty
for
\dim M\geq 2
and

h(C(f))=0
for
\dim=1
.

It is known that the topological entropy is concentrated on the non-wandering set, i.e.
h(f)=h(f|_{{NW}(f)})
, see
[
12
]
. However, somewhat unexpectedly, the existence of a wandering point in a dynamical system on a manifold implies that
h(C(f))=\infty
. This is the second result of the paper.

Theorem 2
.

Let
M
be a topological manifold which is compact, connected and of dimension greater or equal than
2
and
f:M\to M
a homeomorphism that has a wandering point. Then
h(C(f))=\infty
.∎

Since Morse-Smale diffeomorphisms have finitely many non-wandering points, as a corollary of Theorem
2
, we obtain a result that has been established in
[
2
]
, using alternative methods.

Corollary 3
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a Morse-Smale diffeomorphism and
\dim M\geq 2
. Then
h(C(f))=\infty
.
∎

2
Preliminaries

Let us recall some notions and their properties that we will use in the proof.

2.1
Shadowing

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
f:X\to X
a continuous map.

An
(positive) orbit
of a point
x
is the set
\{f^{n}(x)\mid n\geq 0\}
. If
f
is
reversible
, i.e.
f
is a homeomorphism, we can define a
full orbit
as the set
\{f^{n}(x)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

We say that the set
A
is
positively invariant
if
f(A)\subseteq A
.

Let
\delta>0
.

Definition 4
.

We say that the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
(respectively
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) is
\delta
-pseudo-orbit if

d(x_{n+1},f(x_{n})<\delta

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(respectively
n\in\mathbb{Z}
).

One can also define a finite
\delta
-pseudo orbit.

Definition 5
.

We say that a true orbit
\{f^{n}(x)\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, of a point
x\in X

\varepsilon
-shadows a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
\{x_{n}\}
, if

d(f^{n}(x),x_{n})<\varepsilon,

(1)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

If
f
is reversible, we can define shadowing of a full
\delta
-pseudo orbit
\{x_{n}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, by requiring the condition (
1
) for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In this paper we deal with Morse gradient system, which is reversible, so by shadowing we always assume the shadowing of a pseudo-orbit
\{x_{n}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 6
.

We say that a reversible dynamical system
(X,f)
has a shadowing property if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
\delta
-pseudo orbit
\{x_{n}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists a true orbit
\{f^{n}(x)\}
that
\varepsilon
-shadows it.

2.2
Hyperspaces and induced maps

For a compact metric space
(X,d)
, the hyperspace
2^{X}
is the set of all nonempty closed subsets of
X
.
The topology on
2^{X}
is induced by the Hausdorff metric

d_{H}(A,B):=\inf\{\varepsilon>0\mid A\subset U_{\varepsilon}(B),\;B\subset U_{\varepsilon}(A)\},

where

U_{\varepsilon}(A):=\{x\in X\mid d(x,A)<\varepsilon\}.

(2)

The obtain space
2^{X}
is called a
hyperspace induced by
X
, and it also turns out to be compact with respect to Hausdorff metric.

If
X
is also connected (and so a continuum), then the set
C(X)
of all connected and closed nonempty subsets of
X
is also compact and connected. The set
C(X)
is called the
hyperspace of subcontinua
of
X
.

If
f:X\to X
is continuous, then it induces continuous maps

\displaystyle 2^{f}:2^{X}\to 2^{X},\quad

\displaystyle 2^{f}(A):=\{f(x)\mid x\in A\}

\displaystyle C(f):C(X)\to C(X),\quad

\displaystyle C(f)(A):=\{f(x)\mid x\in A\}.

If
f
is a homeomorphism, so are
2^{X}
and
C(f)
.

In this paper we deal with the hyperspace
C(X)
.

We will use small latin letters
x
for points in the initial space
X
, and capital latin letters
X
for points in the induced hyperspace
C(X)
.

An open and a close balls in
X
will be denoted by
B(x,r)
and
B[x,r]
. An open and a closed balls in
C(X)
will be denoted by
B_{H}(X,r)
and
B_{H}[X,r]
.

2.3
Morse gradient systems

Let
M
be a smooth closed connected manifold and
F:M\to\mathbb{R}
a smooth Morse function, meaning that all critical points of
F
are non-degenerate. For a critical point
p
of
F
, denote by
m_{F}(p)
Morse index of
p
.

Fix a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
. Let
\varphi^{t}
be the negative gradient flow defined by

\frac{d\varphi^{t}}{dt}(x)=-\nabla_{g}F(\varphi^{t}(x)),\quad\varphi^{0}=\mathrm{Id},

(3)

where the gradient
\nabla_{g}
is induced by the metric
g
.

For a critical point
p
of
F
define
unstable
and
stable manifold
of
p
as:

W^{u}(p):=\{x\in M\mid\lim_{t\to-\infty}\varphi^{t}(x)=p\},\quad W^{s}(p):=\{x\in M\mid\lim_{t\to+\infty}\varphi^{t}(x)=p\}.

It is known that
W^{u}(p)
and
W^{s}(p)
are submanifolds of
M
of dimension
m_{F}(p)
and
\dim M-m_{F}(p)
respectively (in fact they are diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{m_{F}(p)}
and
\mathbb{R}^{\dim M-m_{F}(p)}
).

We say that that the pair
(F,g)
satisfies
Morse-Smale condition
if for any two critical points
p
and
q
, the manifolds
W^{u}(p)
and
W^{s}(q)
intersect transversally in
M
. This implies that

\mathcal{M}(p,q):=W^{u}(p)\cap W^{s}(q)

is either the empty set or a manifold of dimension
m_{F}(p)-m_{F}(q)
.

The time-one-map of Morse negative gradient equation satisfying Morse-Smale condition is a Morse-Smale diffeomorphism.

Figure 1:
Convergence to a broken trajectory

The manifold
\mathcal{M}(p,q)
does not need to be closed, it can have a topological boundary that consisists of ”broken trajectories”, see
[
22
]
. In this paper we will use only one inclusion of this identification between the boundary of
\mathcal{M}(p,q)
on the one hand, and the space of broken trajcetories, on the other. To be precise, it is known that, for given pair of Morse trajectories
\alpha
and
\beta
, satisfying Morse negative gradient equation (
3
) and the boundary conditions:

\alpha(-\infty)=p,\;\alpha(+\infty)=\beta(-\infty)=q,\;\beta(+\infty)=r,

there exists a sequence
\gamma_{n}
of Morse negative trajectories with boundary conditions

\gamma_{n}(-\infty)=p,\quad\gamma_{n}(+\infty)=r

that in some sense converges to the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
. The construction of this sequence is called
gluing
. In our proof the precise definition of this convergence is not relevant, we will use only the existence of this sequence. See Figure 1, or
[
22
]
for more details.

2.4
Topological entropy and subshifts

Let
X
be a compact metric space, and
f:X\rightarrow X
a continuous map. Denote by
d_{n}^{f}(x,y)
the dynamic metric (induced by
f
and
d
):

d_{n}^{f}(x,y)=\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq n-1}d(f^{k}(x),f^{k}(y)).

For
\varepsilon>0
, we say that a finite set
E\subset X
is
(n,\varepsilon)
-separated
if for every
x,y\in E
it holds
d_{n}^{f}(x,y)\geq\varepsilon
. Let
S(n,\varepsilon;Y)
denote the maximal cardinality of an
(n,\varepsilon)
-separated set
E
.

The
topological entropy
of the map
f
is defined by

h(f)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log S(n,\varepsilon;Y)}{n}\in[0,\infty].

The topological entropy can also be defined via coverings with sets of
d^{n}_{f}
-diameters less than
\varepsilon
, via coverings by balls of
d_{n}^{f}
-radius less than
\varepsilon
, or via open covers. We refer the reader to
[
15
]
and the references therein for a brief survey on the topological entropy.

For our purpose we will need the following property of the topological entropy: if the dynamical system
(Y,g)
is a
factor
of the dynamical system
(X,f)
(meaning that there exists a countinuous surjection
\varphi:X\to Y
satisfying
\varphi\circ f=g\circ\varphi
), then
h(g)\leq h(f)
.

A point
x\in X
is
wandering
if there exists a neighbourhood
U\ni x
such that
f^{n}(U)\cap U=\emptyset
, for all
n\geq 1
.

A point that is not wandering is said to be
non-wandering
. We denote the set of all non-wandering points by
NW(f)
. The set
NW(f)
is closed and
f
-invariant and it holds
h(f)=h(f|_{NW(f)})
(see
[
12
]
).

In general, computing the topological entropy of a dynamical system can be difficult. However, for the shift map, there is a particularly convenient method to do so. For a finite set
A
(called an
alphabet
), denote by
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
the set of all two-sided sequences
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}
where
x_{n}\in A
. The metric on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is defined as:

d(x,y):=2^{-k},

where

k=\min\{|n|\mid x_{n}\neq y_{n}\}.

The topology induced by
d
coincides Tychonoff product topology on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The space
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a compact, totally disconnected metric space without isolated points. The
shift map
is defined as:

\sigma:A^{\mathbb{Z}}\to A^{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\{\sigma(x)\}_{n}=x_{n+1}

and it is a homeomorphism. Any closed subset
\Sigma\subseteq A^{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\sigma(\Sigma)=\Sigma
is said to be a
two-sided subshift
or a
subshift
, for short.

Let
\Sigma
be a subshift. We define
the set of words of length

m
as

\mathscr{L}^{m}(\Sigma):=\left\{(x_{k},x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{k+m-1})\in A^{m}\mid x\in\Sigma,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\subseteq A^{m}

and the
complexity function
by

p_{\Sigma}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N},\quad p_{\Sigma}(m):=\sharp\,\mathscr{L}^{m}(\Sigma).

(4)

It is known that one can compute the topological entropy by using the complexity function. Namely, if
\Sigma\subset A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a subshift it holds:

h(\sigma|_{\Sigma})=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{p_{\Sigma}(m)}{\log m}

(see
[
12
]
).

Proofs of Theorem
1
and Theorem

In this section we proof our main results. For the reader’s convenience, we will restate them.

Theorem 7
.

Let
f=\varphi^{1}
be the time-one-map of negative Morse gradient flow (
3
) which satisfies Morse–Smale condition. Then
C(f)
does not have the shadowing property.

Proof.
Suppose that
\dim M\geq 2
, since the case of
M=\mathbb{S}^{1}
is done in
[
2
]
.

We need to find
\varepsilon>0
and, for every
\delta

\delta

that cannot be
\varepsilon
-shadowed. We will devide the proof in several steps.

Step 1: construction of
X_{0}
in pseudo-orbit.

Lemma 8
.

There exist two critical points
p
and
q
and two different solutions of negative gradient equation

\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla F(\gamma_{i}(t)),\quad i\in\{1,2\}\quad\mbox{ with}\quad\gamma_{i}(-\infty)=p,\,\gamma_{i}(+\infty)=q.

(5)

Proof.
We distinguish between two cases.

Assume that there exists a critical point
r
of Morse index
0<m_{F}(r)<\dim M
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be any two gradient curves with
\alpha(+\infty)=r
and
\beta(-\infty)=r
. We can prove that these two curves exist by using for example the Hartman-Grobman theorem. Indeed, since
F

\nabla F
(meaning that
L:=-d\left(\nabla F(r)\right)
is a hyperbolic matrix). The Hartman-Grobman theorem says that locally, the dynamical system induced by the differential equation (
3

\frac{d\phi^{t}}{dt}(x)=L\cdot\phi^{t}(x),\quad\phi^{0}=\mathrm{Id}.

Since
0<m_{F}(r)<\dim M
, the symmetic matrix

has both positive and negative eigenvalues, which implies that there exist at least one trajectory,
\beta

r
, and at least one trajectory,
\alpha

Let

m_{F}(p)\geq m_{F}(r)+1\geq m_{F}(q)+1+1,

In the second case there is no critical points of Morse index

Now fix

. Choose

A_{1}:=\{\gamma_{1}(t)\mid a_{1}\leq F(\gamma_{1}(t))\leq b_{1}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad A_{2}:=\{\gamma_{2}(t)\mid a_{1}\leq F(\gamma_{2}(t))\leq b_{1}\}

Theorem 9
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a reversible dynamical system on a connected compact topological manifold
M
with the dimension at least two. If
f
possesses a wandering point, then
h(C(f))=\infty
.

Proof.
Let
x_{0}
be wandering and
U\ni x_{0}
an open set such that
f^{n}(U)\cap f^{m}(U)=\emptyset
for all integers
m
and
n
. Fix a positive integer
k
and choose
k
different points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in U
. Since every sequence
f^{n}(x_{j})
has a convergent subequence (when
n\to\pm\infty
), we can assume that

\lim_{n\to-\infty}f^{n}(x_{j})=a_{j},\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}f^{n}(x_{j})=b_{j},\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}.

Define the map

\varphi:2^{M}\to\left(\{0,1\}^{k}\right)^{\mathbb{Z}}

as

\varphi:K\mapsto\{v_{j}^{n}\},\;j\in\{1,\ldots,k\},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}

where

v_{j}^{n}:=\begin{cases}0,&x_{j}^{n}\notin K\\
1,&x_{j}^{n}\in K.\end{cases}

It is not hard to check that
\varphi
is continuous and onto, as well as
\varphi\circ 2^{f}=\sigma\circ\varphi
, where

\sigma:\left(\{0,1\}^{k}\right)^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\left(\{0,1\}^{k}\right)^{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\sigma:\{v_{j}^{n}\}\mapsto\left\{v_{j}^{n+1}\right\}

is the shift map. The space
\left(\{0,1\}^{k}\right)^{\mathbb{Z}}
is identified with the space

\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2^{k}}\}^{\mathbb{Z}}

of two-side sequences over the alphabet
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2^{k}}\}
, and the shift map
\sigma
with the standard shift map.

Denote by
\Sigma:=\varphi(C(M))
. Since
\varphi
establishes a semiconjugation between
f
and
\sigma
, and
C(M)
is closed and
2^{f}
-invariant subset of
2^{M}
, we see that
\Sigma
is a subshift of
\left(\{0,1\}^{k}\right)^{\mathbb{Z}}
and that
h(C(f))\geq h(\sigma|_{\Sigma})
. The topological entropy of
\sigma|_{\Sigma}
can be computed using the complexity function
p_{\Sigma}
defined in (
4
). Fix a positive integer
m
. For any choice of
\{u_{j}^{n}\}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{1}+m\}
we can find an element

such that
\varphi(K)=v
, where

and

v_{j}^{n}=\begin{cases}u_{j}^{n},&j\in\{1,\ldots,k\},n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{1}+m\}\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}.

Indeed, since
\dim M\geq 2
, we can choose neighbourhoods
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
,
W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}
of the points
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},

b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
small enough such that

N:=M\setminus\left(\overline{V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}\cup W_{1}\cup\cdots\cup W_{k}}\right)

stays connected, hence path connected, since
M
is a manifold (one can prove this by induction on the number of removed points). There exists
n_{0}>n_{1}+m
such that
f^{-n}(x_{j})\in A_{j}
,
f^{n}(x_{j})\in B_{j}
, for

. We have finitely many points

that belong to a path connected set

which is locally homeomorphic to euclidean space. It is obvious that we can construct a set

(for example, homeomorphic to a connected graph) that contains only the specificaly chosen points

, and does not contain any other of them.

We have proved that any word of the lenght

\Sigma
. Therefore

and

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\log p_{\Sigma}(m)}{m}=\log 2^{k},

hence

. Since

for any
k
, we have
h(C(f))=\infty
.
∎

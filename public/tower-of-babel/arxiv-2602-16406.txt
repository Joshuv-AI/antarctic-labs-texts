Bounds and Constructions of Codes for Ordered Composite DNA Sequences
Zuo Ye, Yuling Li, Zhaojun Lan, Gennian Ge

Abstract
This paper extends the foundational work of Dollma et al . on codes for ordered composite DNA sequences. We consider the general setting with an alphabet of size q q and a resolution parameter k k , moving beyond the binary ( q = 2 q=2 ) case primarily studied previously. We investigate error-correcting codes for substitution errors and deletion errors under several channel models, including ( e 1 , … , e k ) (e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}) -composite error/deletion, e e -composite error/deletion, and the newly introduced t t - ( e 1 , … , e t ) (e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}) -composite error/deletion model. We first establish equivalence relations among families of composite-error correcting codes (CECCs) and among families of composite-deletion correcting codes (CDCCs). This significantly reduces the number of distinct error-parameter sets that require separate analysis. We then derive novel and general upper bounds on the sizes of CECCs using refined sphere-packing arguments and probabilistic methods. These bounds together cover all values of parameters q q , k k , ( e 1 , … , e k ) (e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}) and e e . In contrast, previous bounds were only established for q = 2 q=2 and limited choices of k k , ( e 1 , … , e k ) (e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}) and e e . For CDCCs, we generalize a known non-asymptotic upper bound for ( 1 , 0 , … , 0 ) (1,0,\ldots,0) -CDCCs and then provide a cleaner asymptotic bound. On the constructive side, for any q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 , we propose ( 1 , 0 , … , 0 ) (1,0,\ldots,0) -CDCCs, 1 1 -CDCCs and t t - ( 1 , … , 1 ) (1,\ldots,1) -CDCCs with near-optimal redundancies. These codes have efficient and systematic encoders. For substitution errors, we design the first explicit encoding and decoding algorithms for the binary ( 1 , 0 , … , 0 ) (1,0,\ldots,0) -CECC constructed by Dollma et al , and extend the approach to general q q . Furthermore, we give an improved construction of binary 1 1 -CECCs, a construction of nonbinary 1 1 -CECCs, and a construction of t t - ( 1 , … , 1 ) (1,\ldots,1) -CECCs. These constructions are also systematic.

Introduction

The exponential growth of global digital data has intensified the search for high‑density, long‑term storage alternatives. DNA‑based storage has emerged as a promising candidate, owing to its exceptional information density, longevity, and physical stability, alongside continuous advances in synthesis and sequencing technologies

Although there has been significant progress in DNA-based data storage systems over the past two decades, the high cost of DNA synthesis–reportedly orders of magnitude greater than that of sequencing–remains a major barrier to practical deployment

]

A common theme among these efforts is increasing the number of bits written per synthesis cycle. A notable advance in this direction is the concept of
composite DNA letters

[

,

]

\left\{A,C,G,T\right\}

,
G
and

2

Despite increasing the number of bits written per synthesis cycle and reducing the overall cost of DNA-based storage systems, the use of composite letters brings new challenges. One of them is the reconstruction of composite sequences by DNA sequencing. To overcome this challenge, researchers have proposed the concept of combinatorial composite letters (a variant of composite letters) and investigated related coding problems
[

,
24
,
25
,
35
]
; others have examined the sequencing coverage‑depth problem

Another challenge lies in designing codes tailored to the composite channel. Once digital information is converted to composite sequences, standard DNA strands are synthesized based on these composite sequences. Because a composite letter is a mixture of
A
,
C
,
G

, many distinct standard strands can be derived from a single composite sequence. For example, let

\begin{array}[]{cccc}AGCGTG,&AGCGTT,&AGCTTG,&ATCGTG,\\
AGCTTT,&ATCGTT,&ATCTTG,&ATCTTT.\end{array}

As the length of the composite sequence grows, the number of possible standard strands can increase rapidly. The large set of possible strands and the ambiguity about which are actually synthesized constitute the core coding challenge for the composite channel. Several coding problems arising from this synthesis method have been studied
[

,
30
,

The explosion in the number of standard strands in the example stems from the fact that, for the composite letter

For

The authors of

II-A

Ordered Composite Letters and Sequences

Let
k,q\geq 2
be two integers. Denote by
\Sigma_{q}
the alphabet
\left\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\right\}
. A
composite letter
over
\Sigma_{q}
is a vector of probabilities
\left(p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{q-1}\right)
satisfying
0\leq p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{q-1}\leq 1
and
\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}p_{i}=1
. This means that the symbol
i\in\Sigma_{q}
is observed with probability
p_{i}
. A composite letter of
resolution

k
over
\Sigma_{q}
is a vector of the form
\left(\frac{k_{0}}{k},\frac{k_{1}}{k},\ldots,\frac{k_{q-1}}{k}\right)
, where
k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{q-1}
are nonnegative integers summing to
k
.

Given a composite letter
\left(\frac{k_{0}}{k},\frac{k_{1}}{k},\ldots,\frac{k_{q-1}}{k}\right)
of resolution
k
, we construct a column vector
\sigma=\left[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
of length
k
over
\Sigma_{q}
with the property that
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}
and
\mathsf{wt}_{i}\left(\sigma\right)=k_{i}
for every
i\in\Sigma_{q}
. In other words,
\sigma
is a nondecreasing vector, and symbol

appears exactly
k_{i}
times in
\sigma
. We call
\sigma
an
ordered composite letter
of
resolution

k
over
\Sigma_{q}
. The set of all ordered composite letters of resolution
k
over
\Sigma_{q}
is denoted by
\Phi_{q,k}
. Define
Q_{q,k}=\left|\Phi_{q,k}\right|
, i.e., the number of valid letters. Clearly,
Q_{q,k}=\binom{k+q-1}{q-1}
. Throughout this paper, an
invalid
letter (or symbol) refers to any element in
\Sigma_{q}^{k}\setminus\Phi_{q,k}
, which violates the nondecreasing constraint. Elements in
\Phi_{q,k}
and
\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
are also called
valid
letters and sequences, respectively.

Since each letter in
\Phi_{q,k}
is a column vector of length
k
, a sequence
\bm{s}
in
\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
can be regarded as a
k\times n
matrix over
\Sigma_{q}
. For any
\bm{s}\in\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
and each

, we always denote by
\bm{s}_{i}
the

-th row of
\bm{s}
, unless stated otherwise.

Example II.1
.

Let
q=2
and

. Then
\Phi_{2,3}=\left\{\left[0,0,0\right]^{\mathsf{T}},\left[0,0,1\right]^{\mathsf{T}},\left[0,1,1\right]^{\mathsf{T}},\left[1,1,1\right]^{\mathsf{T}}\right\}
. The symbol
\left[0,1,0\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
is invalid, since it is not nondecreasing. Consider the sequence

\bm{s}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&1&1\\
0&1&1\end{pmatrix}

in
\Phi_{2,3}^{3}
. By definition, we have
\bm{s}_{1}=001
,
\bm{s}_{2}=011
and
\bm{s}_{3}=011
.

Let

III

The previous section introduced the concepts of

For a given tuple

We say that two families,

IV
Upper Bounds on Code Sizes

This section establishes upper bounds on the maximum possible size of composite-error correcting codes (CECCs) and composite-deletion correcting codes (CDCCs).

IV-A

Bounds on Codes Correcting Substitutions

For a sequence
\bm{s}\in\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
, a tuple
\left(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and an integer
e\geq 1
, we define two error balls centered at
\bm{s}
:

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{B}_{\left(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\right)}^{(q,k)}(\bm{s})\triangleq\left\{\bm{y}\in\Phi_{q,k}^{n}:\mathsf{d}_{H}\left(\bm{y}_{i},\bm{s}_{i}\right)\leq e_{i},\forall i\in[k]\right\},\\
\mathcal{B}_{e}^{(q,k)}(\bm{s})\triangleq\left\{\bm{y}\in\Phi_{q,k}^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{d}_{H}\left(\bm{y}_{i},\bm{s}_{i}\right)\leq e\right\}.\end{array}

In other words,
\mathcal{B}_{\left(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\right)}^{(q,k)}(\bm{s})
consists of all
valid
sequences obtained from
\bm{s}
by introducing at most
e_{i}
substitutions in the
i
-th row, while
\mathcal{B}_{e}^{(q,k)}(\bm{s})
consists of all
valid
sequences obtained from
\bm{s}
by introducing a total of at most
e
substitutions.

Remark IV.1
.

Applying an
\left(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\right)
-composite-error (or an
e
-composite-error) to
\bm{s}
does not necessarily yield a valid sequence
\bm{y}
. Our definition includes only valid sequences in error balls. This enables estimating their sizes and consequently, deriving nontrivial upper bounds on code sizes.

We begin with a fundamental lemma that quantifies the sizes of these error balls.

Lemma IV.1
.

For any two integers
q,k\geq 2
, we have the following results.

(i)

Let
\left[a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\Phi_{q,k}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(q,k)}(\sigma)\setminus\{\sigma\}\right|=q-1+a_{k}-a_{1},

(2)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{B}_{(1,1,\ldots,1)}^{(q,k)}(\sigma)\setminus\{\sigma\}\right|=\sum_{l=1}^{q-1}\binom{l+k-1}{l}.

(3)

(ii)

Let
e\geq 1
and
0\leq l\leq q-1
be two integers. Given an
\bm{s}\in\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
such that
\bm{s}_{k}[i]-\bm{s}_{1}[i]\geq l
for at least
m
indices
i\in[n]
, where
e\leq m\leq n
, we have

\left|\mathcal{B}_{e}^{(q,k)}(\bm{s})\right|\geq\binom{m}{e}(q-1+l)^{e}+1.

(4)

(iii)

If
e_{1}\geq e_{2}\geq\cdots\geq e_{k}\geq 1
, then for every
\bm{s}\in\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
,

\left|\mathcal{B}_{(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})}^{(q,k)}(\bm{s})\right|\geq\binom{n}{e_{k}}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{q-1}\binom{l+k-1}{l}\right]^{e_{k}}+1.

(5)

Proof:

Consider a vector
\sigma^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{k}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(q,k)}(\sigma)\setminus\{\sigma\}
. It differs from
\sigma
in exactly one component. Suppose that the single substitution occurs at position
i
, where
1\leq i\leq k
. For
\sigma^{\prime}
to remain nondecreasing, the new value
a_{i}^{\prime}
must satisfy
a_{i-1}^{\prime}\leq a_{i}^{\prime}\leq a_{i+1}^{\prime}
, where we set
a_{0}=0
and
a_{k+1}=q-1
. Excluding the original value
a_{i}
, there are exactly
a_{i+1}-a_{i-1}
possible choices for
a_{i}^{\prime}
. Summing over all

i
gives
\left|\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(q,k)}(\sigma)\setminus\{\sigma\}\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(a_{i+1}-a_{i-1})=q-1+a_{k}-a_{1}
, which establishes (
2
).

To prove (
3
), consider
\sigma^{\prime}=\left[a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{k}^{\prime}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathcal{B}_{(1,1,\ldots,1)}^{(q,k)}(\sigma)\setminus\{\sigma\}
. For each fixed value
b\in\Sigma_{q}
for
a_{k}^{\prime}
, for
\sigma^{\prime}
to be nondecreasing, the tuple
\left[a_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,a_{k-1}^{\prime}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
must be a nondecreasing vector over
\Sigma_{b+1}
. The number of such tuples is
\binom{b+k-1}{b}
. Summing over all
b\in\Sigma_{q}
and excluding the original symbol
\sigma
, we obtain that
\left|\mathcal{B}_{(1,1,\ldots,1)}^{(q,k)}(\sigma)\setminus\{\sigma\}\right|=\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}\binom{l+k-1}{l}-1
.

Thus part (i) is proved. Parts (ii) and (iii) follow directly from the bounds given in (i) by selecting
e
(or
e_{k-1}
) columns where the errors occur.
∎

.

\displaystyle t(\bm{y})=t(n,k;w)\triangleq k^{n-w}+w(k-1)k^{n-w-1}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(\bm{y})\triangleq\{\bm{s}_{1}\in\{0,1\}^{n}:\bm{y}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(\bm{s}_{1})\}.

We aim to count the number of tuples
(\bm{s}_{2},\ldots,\bm{s}_{k})\subseteq(\{0,1\}^{n})^{k-1}

\displaystyle t(n,k;w)

\displaystyle=\left|\bigcup_{l=1}^{w}\mathcal{T}_{i_{l}}(n,k;w)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\bigcup_{j=0}^{w}\left(\mathcal{T}_{i_{j}}(n,k;w)\setminus\bigcup_{p=0}^{j-1}\mathcal{T}_{i_{p}}(n,k;w)\right)\right|

The size of

is bounded from above by the probabilistic method, as we did in previous section. Recall that to bound
\left|\mathcal{C}_{2}\right|
, we defined a random variable
X
which is the sum of several mutually independent random variables. Then we bounded the probability that
X
deviates from

by the Hoeffding’s inequality (
Lemma

IV.2
). In this subsection,
\left|\mathcal{C}_{2}\right|
is bounded by using a similar method. The difference is that

is now not the sum of mutually independent variables. As a result,
Lemma

IV.2
can not be applied. Fortunately, there is an auxiliary result analogous to
Lemma

IV.2
, as shown in
Lemma

IV.6
. Before presenting it, we need to introduce some terminology.

Definition IV.1
.

[
14
, Section 2]

Let
\left\{X_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\right\}
be a set of random variables, where
\mathscr{A}
is an index set.

•

A subset
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{A}
is
independent
if the corresponding subsets of variables
X_{\alpha}
(
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
) are mutually independent.

•

A family
\left\{\left(\mathscr{A}_{j},w_{j}\right)\right\}_{j}
of pairs
\left(\mathscr{A}_{j},w_{j}\right)
, where
\mathscr{A}_{j}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
and
0\leq w_{j}\leq 1
, is a
fractional cover
of
\mathscr{A}
if
\sum_{j:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{j}}w_{j}\geq 1
for every
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}
.

•

A fractional cover
\left\{\left(\mathscr{A}_{j},w_{j}\right)\right\}_{j}
is
proper
if each
\mathscr{A}_{j}
is independent.

•

The
fractional chromatic number
of
\mathscr{A}
is
\chi^{*}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)\triangleq\min\left\{\sum_{j}w_{j}:\left\{\left(\mathscr{A}_{j},w_{j}\right)\right\}_{j}\text{ is a proper fractional cover of }\mathscr{A}\right\}
.

The next lemma is a generalization of
Lemma

IV.2
.

Lemma IV.6
.

[
14
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
\left\{X_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\right\}
be a set of random variables with
a_{\alpha}\leq X_{\alpha}\leq b_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}
and some real numbers
a_{\alpha}
and
b_{\alpha}
. Let
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
. Then for all
t>0
, we have

\Pr[X-\mathbb{E}[X]\leq-t]\leq\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{2t^{2}}{\chi^{*}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(b_{\alpha}-a_{\alpha})^{2}}}.

For example, if
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are mutually independent, then
\left\{\left([n],1\right)\right\}
is a proper fractional cover of
[n]
. Therefore,
\chi^{*}\left([n]\right)=1
since
\chi^{*}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)\geq 1
as long as
\mathscr{A}\neq\emptyset
. Then
Lemma

IV.6
implies
\Pr[X-\mathbb{E}[X]\leq-t]\leq\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{2t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{2}}}
, which is the Hoeffding’s bound in
Lemma

IV.2
.

The following lemma helps to bound
\left|\mathcal{C}_{2}\right|
.

Lemma IV.7
.

For
q,k\geq 2
, define

m_{q,k}=1-\frac{1}{Q_{q,k}^{2}}\sum_{a\in\Sigma_{q}}\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}^{2}.

(18)

For any
t>0
, the number of sequences

with less than
(n-1)m_{q,k}+1-\sqrt{t(n-1)\ln{n}}
runs in
\bm{c}_{1}
is at most
\frac{Q_{q,k}^{n}}{n^{t}}
.

Proof:

Select a sequence
\bm{c}=\bm{c}[1]\cdots\bm{c}[n]
uniformly at random from
\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
. Then it holds that
\Pr[\bm{c}[i]=\sigma]=\frac{1}{Q_{q,k}}
for each
i
and each
\sigma\in\Phi_{q,k}
. Furthermore, random variables
\bm{c}[1],\ldots,\bm{c}[n]
are mutually independent. Recall that
\bm{c}_{1}
is the first row of
\bm{c}
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
and
a\in\Sigma_{q}
, we have
\Pr[\bm{c}_{1}[i]=a]=\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}/Q_{q,k}
, since there are exactly
\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}
tuples
\left(a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\right)
such that
\left[a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\Phi_{q,k}
.

Define
n-1
random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}
as

X_{i}=\begin{cases}1,\mbox{ if }\bm{c}_{1}[i]\neq\bm{c}_{1}[i+1],\\
0,\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

For every
1\leq i<n
, it is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\Pr\left[X_{i}=1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q_{q,k}^{2}}\sum_{a\in\Sigma_{q}}\sum_{b\in\Sigma_{q}\setminus\{a\}}\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}\binom{k+q-b-2}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q_{q,k}^{2}}\sum_{a\in\Sigma_{q}}\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}\left[\sum_{b\in\Sigma_{q}}\binom{k+q-b-2}{k-1}-\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q_{q,k}^{2}}\left[\sum_{a\in\Sigma_{q}}\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}\right]^{2}-\frac{1}{Q_{q,k}^{2}}\sum_{a\in\Sigma_{q}}\binom{k+q-a-2}{k-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{q,k}.

Let
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X_{i}
. Then
\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\Pr[X_{i}=1]=(n-1)m_{q,k}
. It is clear that

. Therefore,

\Pr\left[r(\bm{c}_{1})<(n-1)m_{q,k}+1-\sqrt{t(n-1)\ln{n}}\right]=\Pr\left[X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]<-\sqrt{t(n-1)\ln{n}}\right].

We need to bound from above the probability on the right hand side. The estimation in
Lemma

IV.2
is not applicable here, since

are not mutually independent. Indeed, when

, we have

\displaystyle\Pr[X_{i}=X_{i+1}=1]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr[\bm{c}_{1}[i]=0,\bm{c}_{1}[i+1]=1,\bm{c}_{1}[i+2]=0]+\Pr[\bm{c}_{1}[i]=1,\bm{c}_{1}[i+1]=0,\bm{c}_{1}[i+2]=1]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}+1}{(k+1)^{3}}\neq\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}}=m_{2,k}^{2}=\Pr[X_{i}=1]\cdot\Pr[X_{i+1}=1],

implying that
X_{i}
and

are not independent.

Fortunately, we can use

IV.6
, which does not rely on independence of random variables. Let

and

. Since
\bm{c}_{1}[1],\ldots,\bm{c}_{1}[n]
are mutually independent and

only relies on

and

and

are independent as long as

are independent. It is easy to verify that the family

. Then it follows from

that
\Pr\left[X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]<-\sqrt{t(n-1)\ln{n}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n^{t}}

Since

, we can conclude that there are at most
\frac{Q_{q,k}^{n}}{n^{t}}

with less than

∎

Now we can present the asymptotic bound.

Theorem IV.5
.

Let

. It holds that

A_{2,k}^{D}\left(n;\left(1,0,\ldots,0\right)\right)\leq\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{2k}\cdot\frac{\left|\mathcal{V}_{2,k,n}\right|}{n}(1+o(1)).

Denote

. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\left(1,0,\ldots,0\right)
-CDCC. Partition
\mathcal{C}
into two subsets:

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\left\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}:\bm{c}_{1}\text{ has at least }n_{0}\text{ runs}\right\},\;\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{1}.

It follows from

that

\left|\mathcal{B}_{\left(1,0,\ldots,0\right)}^{D}(\bm{c})\right|\geq n_{0},

for every
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Since

is a
\left(1,0,\ldots,0\right)
-CDCC, we obtain

\left|\mathcal{C}_{1}\right|\leq\frac{\left|\mathcal{V}_{2,k,n}\right|}{n_{0}}=\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{2k}\cdot\frac{\left|\mathcal{V}_{2,k,n}\right|}{n}(1+o(1)).

On the other hand, by (

\left|\mathcal{C}_{2}\right|\leq\frac{Q_{2,k}^{n}}{n^{2}}

∎

Remark IV.3
.

It can be verified that
\sum_{w=0}^{n-1}\sum_{r=1}^{2w+1}c(n-1;r;w)\cdot t(n,k;w)=\left|\mathcal{V}_{2,k,n}\right|
. Consequently,

\sum_{w=0}^{n-1}\sum_{r=1}^{2w+1}\frac{c(n-1;r;w)\cdot t(n,k;w)}{r}\geq\frac{\left|\mathcal{V}_{2,k,n}\right|}{n-1}=(k-1)(k+1)^{n-2}(1+o(1)).

Moreover, we have
\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{2k}\cdot\frac{\left|\mathcal{V}_{2,k,n}\right|}{n}(1+o(1))=\frac{(k-1)(k+1)^{n}}{2k}(1+o(1))

and

are smaller than the size of the whole space

only by a constant factor. This phenomenon arises from the fact that the set of erroneous words (i.e.,

, which is significantly larger than

.

[
2

)
For any
s\geq 1
and
\bm{\lambda}=\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
, where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}\geq 0
, it holds that
\det V_{\bm{\lambda}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})=s_{\bm{\lambda}}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\right)\cdot\det V_{0}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})
.

Lemma V.3
.

Let
k\geq t\geq 2
be two integers and
s_{0}=\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{(t-1)\ln(k)}{2\ln(k)-\ln(t)}\right\rfloor+1,t-1\right\}
. Define

f(k,t)=\begin{cases}k,\mbox{ if }t=2,\\
2k,\mbox{ if }t=3,\\
t^{\binom{s_{0}}{2}}k^{s_{0}(t-s_{0})},\mbox{ if }t\geq 4.\end{cases}

Let
p>f(k,t)
be a prime.
Then, for every
s
with
1\leq s\leq t
,
every

s\times s
submatrix of the following matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&2&\cdots&k\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&2^{t-1}&\cdots&k^{t-1}\end{pmatrix}

is invertible over the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Proof:

The case
s=1
is trivial since
p>k
. Now assume that
s\geq 2
. Choose arbitrary subsets
I=\left\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right\}\subseteq[k]
and
J=\left\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\right\}\subseteq\left\{0,1,\ldots,t-1\right\}
, with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{s}
and
j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{s}
. Let
V_{J,I}
be the
s\times s
submatrix with rows indexed by
J
and columns indexed by
I
, i.e.,

V_{J,I}=\begin{pmatrix}i_{1}^{j_{1}}&i_{2}^{j_{1}}&\cdots&i_{s}^{j_{1}}\\
i_{1}^{j_{2}}&i_{2}^{j_{2}}&\cdots&i_{s}^{j_{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
i_{1}^{j_{s}}&i_{2}^{j_{s}}&\cdots&i_{s}^{j_{s}}\end{pmatrix}.

It suffices to show that
\det V_{J,I}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.

For
1\leq l\leq s
, define
\lambda_{l}=j_{s-l+1}-s+l
. Then
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}\geq 0
. Set
\bm{\lambda}=\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\right)
. We have
V_{J,I}=V_{\bm{\lambda}}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s})
. It follows from
Lemma

V.2
that
\det V_{J,I}=s_{\bm{\lambda}}\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right)\cdot\det V_{0}\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right)=s_{\bm{\lambda}}\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right)\cdot\prod_{1\leq r<l\leq s}(i_{l}-i_{r})
. Since
1\leq i_{r},i_{l}\leq k<p
, we have
i_{l}-i_{r}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Therefore,
\prod_{1\leq r<l\leq s}(i_{l}-i_{r})\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. It remains to show that
s_{\bm{\lambda}}\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right)\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.

If
s=t
, we have
\bm{\lambda}=(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
s_{\bm{\lambda}}\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right)=1
. Then the conclusion holds. This covers the case
t=2
as well.

Now suppose that
t\geq 3
and
2\leq s<t
. First, by
[
2
, Equation (7)]
, the number of SSTs of shape
\bm{\lambda}
over the set
\left\{1,\ldots,s\right\}
satisfies
\left|\mathcal{T}(s,\bm{\lambda})\right|\leq t^{\binom{s}{2}}
. Furthermore, we have
0<i_{1}^{\mu_{T}(1)}i_{2}^{\mu_{T}(2)}\cdots i_{s}^{\mu_{T}(s)}\leq k^{\sum_{l=1}^{s}\lambda_{l}}\leq k^{s(t-s)}
for each
T\in\mathcal{T}(s,\bm{\lambda})
. Then by
Definition

V.2
, it holds that
0<s_{\bm{\lambda}}\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right)\leq t^{\binom{s}{2}}k^{s(t-s)}
. Let
F(s)=t^{\binom{s}{2}}k^{s(t-s)}
. Then
F(s+1)/F(s)=t^{s}k^{t-2s-1}
. It is easy to verify that
F(s+1)/F(s)\geq 1
when
s\leq\frac{(t-1)\ln(k)}{2\ln(k)-\ln(t)}
, and
F(s+1)/F(s)<1
when
s>\frac{(t-1)\ln(k)}{2\ln(k)-\ln(t)}
. Since
2\leq s<t
is an integer, we conclude that
F(s)
achieves the maximum value at
s=s_{0}
. Therefore, when
t\geq 3
and
p>F(s_{0})
, we have
s_{\bm{\lambda}}\left(i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\right)\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. This proves the case when
t\geq 4
.

When
t=3
, we have
s_{0}=2
and
F(s_{0})=3k^{2}
. This lower bound on

can be further decreased. Indeed, we only need to check submatrices of forms

\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
i_{1}&i_{2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
i_{1}^{2}&i_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}i_{1}&i_{2}\\
i_{1}^{2}&i_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
i_{1}&i_{2}&i_{3}\\
i_{1}^{2}&i_{2}^{2}&i_{3}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

where

. It is easy to see that the condition

is sufficient to make sure that the first, third and fourth submatrices are invertible. The determinant of the second matrix is

, implying that the condition

is sufficient (notice that

is a prime).
∎

, which is

tighter than

, for general

. However, we do not know how to establish such a bound.

For integers

and

(with

VI-A

(1,0,\ldots,0)
-CECCs

This subsection focuses on codes tailored to the
(1,0,\ldots,0)
-composite-error model. For clarity of exposition, we focus on the case
q=2

Recall that mapping every letter
\sigma\in\Phi_{2,k}
to
\mathsf{wt}_{1}\left(\sigma\right)
allows us to treat each sequence
\bm{s}\in\Phi_{2,k}^{n}
as a sequence in
\Sigma_{k+1}^{n}
. As discussed in
[
10
, Section IV]
, a substitution in
\bm{s}_{1}
(the first row of
\bm{s}
) induces only one of the following three types of errors in
\bm{s}

•

A symbol

is replaced by symbol

;

•

A symbol
k
is replaced by symbol
k-1
;

•

A symbol
\sigma\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,k-2\right\}
is replaced by an invalid symbol.

Because each symbol in
\bm{s}
is a nondecreasing column vector and the substitution occurs only in the first row, an invalid symbol can be detected and corrected instantly. As a result, it suffices to design codes that correct the first two types of errors.
Dollma
et al

[
10
]

First, we briefly review its construction. For a sequence

Lemma VI.3
.

For integers
q>2,k\geq 2
and
n\geq q
, let
p_{1}\geq n
and
p_{2}\geq q
be two primes. Any
\bm{c}\in\Phi_{q,k}^{n}
can be decoded from any
\bm{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}^{(q,k)}\left(\bm{c}\right)
, if given the following three values:

•

a_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{c}_{i}[j]\pmod{2q-1}
;

•

a_{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{VT}\left(\bm{c}_{i}\right)\pmod{p_{1}}
;

•

a_{3}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}j^{2}\cdot\mathsf{wt}_{j}\left(\bm{c}[i]\right)\pmod{p_{2}}
.

Proof:

Suppose that
\bm{c}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\bm{c}
by substituting
\bm{c}_{i}[j]
with
\bm{c}_{i}[j]+\delta
, where
-(q-1)\leq\delta\leq q-1
. Let
\Delta_{1}=(\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{n}\bm{c}_{r}^{\prime}[s]-a_{1})\pmod{2q-1}
. Then
\Delta_{1}=\delta\pmod{2q-1}
, from which the value of
\delta
can be recovered as

\delta=\begin{cases}\Delta_{1},&\mbox{ if }0\leq\Delta_{1}<q,\\
\Delta_{1}-(2q-1),&\mbox{ if }\Delta_{1}\geq q.\end{cases}

If
\delta=0
, no error occurred. Next, assume
\delta\neq 0
.

If
\bm{c}^{\prime}[j]
is an invalid symbol, index
j
can be directly identified. Moreover, there must exist one
1\leq r<k
such that
\bm{c}_{r}^{\prime}[j]>\bm{c}_{r+1}^{\prime}[j]
, implying that the substitution either increased
\bm{c}_{r}[j]
or decreased
\bm{c}_{r+1}[j]
. Since
\delta
is known, the error can be corrected immediately. Now suppose that
\bm{c}^{\prime}[j]
is a valid symbol. Let
\alpha=\bm{c}_{i}[j]
. To correct the error, it suffices to determine the values of
j
and
\alpha
, as
\bm{c}[j]
is a nondecreasing column vector over
\Sigma_{q}
and
\delta
is known.

We first determine the value of
j
. Let
\Delta_{2}=\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}\mathsf{VT}\left(\bm{c}_{r}^{\prime}\right)-a_{2}\right)\pmod{p_{1}}
. Then
\Delta_{2}=j\delta\pmod{p_{1}}
.
Since
0<\left|\delta\right|<p_{1}
and
p_{1}
is a prime, we have

. Then since
1\leq j\leq n\leq p_{1}
, integer
j
can be recovered from
j\pmod{p_{1}}
.

Next, we determine the value of
\alpha
. Let
\Delta_{3}=\left(\sum_{s=1}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{q-1}l^{2}\cdot\mathsf{wt}_{l}\left(\bm{c}^{\prime}[s]\right)-a_{3}\right)\pmod{p_{2}}

,

is an odd prime, we have

VII
Conclusion

This paper conducted a deeper study of coding problems for the ordered composite DNA channel. We established nontrivial upper bounds on the sizes of
q
-ary
k
-resolution
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
-CECCs and
e
-CECCs. These results together cover all situations of parameters
q
,
k
,
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
and
e
. For codes correcting deletions (CDCCs), we first generalized the non-asymptotic upper bound for
(1,0,\ldots,0)
-CDCCs in
[
10
]
. This bound is not in closed-form. To address this issue, we then derived a clearer asymptotic bound. Some constructions of codes with near-optimal redundancies were provided.

Beyond the
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
- and
e
-composite-error/deletion models, we also introduced and investigated a novel error model, called the
t
-
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{t})
-composite-error/composite-deletion model.

Despite these advances, many problems still remain open. Firstly, current upper bounds for CDCCs are limited primarily to the
(1,0,\ldots,0)
-composite-deletion model (see
[
10
]
and
Section

IV-B
). Although these bounds also apply to
1
-CDCCs, a direct study of the
1
-composite-deletion model would likely yield tighter results. More fundamentally, upper bounds for general
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
-CDCCs,
e
-CDCCs, and
t
-
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{t})
-CECCs/CDCCs are still unknown.

Secondly, with the exception of the constructions in
Sections

VI-A
and
VI-B
, all codes constructed do not exploit an intrinsic property of the ordered composite channel: each column of a codeword is a nondecreasing vector. While our constructions achieve near-optimal redundancy, it remains an open question how much further improvement is possible by explicitly incorporating this property into the code design. In particular, is the redundancy of the code in
Theorem

V.1
(as a
(1,0,\ldots,0)
-CDCC) near-optimal? Equivalently, is the order of magnitude of the upper bound in

IV-B
optimal? Currently, the best known upper bound is

is

Lastly, in practice, DNA strands with some biological constraints (such as GC-content constraint, and runlength-limited constraint) are preferred

]
. A
k

) corresponds to

On the integration of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras
Jun Jiang, Yunhe Sheng, Chenchang Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we give the necessary and sufficient conditions of the integrability of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras via double Lie groups, matched pairs of Lie groups and factorization of diffeomorphisms respectively. We use the integrability of Rota-Baxter operators to characterize whether the Poisson-Lie group integrating a factorizable Lie bialgebra is again factorizable. We thoroughly study the integrability of Rota-Baxter operators on the unique nontrivial 2-dimensional Lie algebra. As a byproduct, we construct a matched pair of Lie algebras that can not be integrated to a matched pair of Lie groups.

1.
Introduction

The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras was introduced by G. Baxter in his study of fluctuation theory in probability
[
5
]
. They are applied in the Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to renormalization of quantum field theory
[
9
,
12
]
. In the corresponding semi-classical world, the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators (also called
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators) on Lie algebras was introduced in
[
21
]
, and they are closely related to various classical Yang-Baxter equations. A Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on a Lie algebra is naturally the operator form of a classical skew-symmetric
r
-matrix
[
30
]
. Relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
on Lie algebras give rise to solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the semidirect product Lie algebras
[
1
]
. Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
are in one-to-one
correspondence with solutions of the modified Yang-Baxter equation, and have close relation with factorizable Lie bialgebras
[
22
]
. In addition, a Lie group with zero Schouten curvature gives rise a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
on a Lie algebra
[
20
]
. In the sequel, for simplicity, we will call relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on Lie algebras relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras when there is no ambiguity.

The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups was introduced in
[
13
]
, then this concept was generalized to relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups in
[
18
]
. Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups give rise to factorizations of the Lie groups, and can be applied to study integrable systems
[
13
,
30
]
. Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups can also be used to construct set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
3
,
4
]
. It was shown in
[
13
,
18
]
that by differentiating (relative) Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups, one can obtain (relative) Rota-Baxter operators on the corresponding Lie algebras.

The converse direction, namely whether relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras can be integrated to relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups is still not clear. The process from (relative) Rota-Baxter Lie algebras to (relative) Rota-Baxter Lie groups is called the integration. The first attempt in this direction is give in
[
18
]
, where it was shown that (relative) Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras can be integrated to
local
(relative) Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups. In general, the global integrability of an algebraic structure has some topological obstruction. For example, the obstruction of integrability for infinite-dimensional Lie algebras and that for Lie algebroids are studied in
[
27
,
32
,
8
]
respectively. Very recently, some new results have appeared on the integrability of Rota-Baxter operators:
[
28
]
shows that every Rota-Baxter operator on the Heisenberg Lie algebra can be integrated into a Rota-Baxter operator on the Heisenberg Lie group; while
[
11
]
studies a version of formal integration of complete Rota-Baxter Lie algebras.

In this paper, we study the global integrability of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras using different approaches. We give the necessary and sufficient integrability conditions of relative Rota-Baxter operators via double Lie groups, matched pairs of Lie groups and factorization of diffeomorphisms respectively. As applications, we use the integrability of Rota-Baxter operators to characterize when exactly a Poisson-Lie group of a factorizable Lie bialgebra is factorizable again. We also construct some examples that Rota-Baxter Lie algebras can not be integrated to Rota-Baxter Lie groups. As a byproduct, we construct a matched pair of Lie algebras that can not be integrated to a matched pair of Lie groups.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we use three approaches to give the necessary and sufficient conditions of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras being integrable. In Section
3
, we use the integrability of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras to characterize when Poisson-Lie groups integrating factorizable Lie bialgebras are factorizable Poisson-Lie groups. In Section
4
, we study the integrability of Rota-Baxter operators on the nontrivial
2
-dimensional Lie algebra. In particular, we give a concrete example of a matched pair of Lie algebras that can not be integrated to a matched pair of Lie groups.

2.
Integration of relative Rota-Baxter operators

In
[
18
]
, it was shown that any relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra can be integrated into a relative Rota-Baxter Lie group locally. In this section we give the necessary and sufficient conditions of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras being global integrable.
We first recall some basic results of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and relative Rota-Baxter Lie groups.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
2
,
6
]
)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras,
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a Lie algebra homomorphism. A linear map
B:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is called a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
if

[B(u),B(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=B(\phi(B(u))v-\phi(B(v))u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

The quadruple
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
is called a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra of weight
1
. In particular, if
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}
and the action is the adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}
of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, then
B
is called a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
, and
(\mathfrak{g},B)
is called a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra of weight
1
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the sequel, we will call relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras of weight
1

2.1.
Integrability via double Lie groups

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras and
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a Lie algebra homomorphism. Consider the semidirect product Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h}
, with the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi}
given by

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\phi}=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi(x)v-\phi(y)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

There is the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
18
]
)
Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Then a linear map
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph
Gr(B)=\{(B(u),u)|u\in\mathfrak{h}\}
is a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
.

Let
(G,\cdot_{G}),(H,\cdot_{H})
be Lie groups and
\Phi:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be an action of
G
on
H
. There is the semidirect product Lie group
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,\cdot_{\Phi})
, where
\cdot_{\Phi}
is given by

(g_{1},h_{1})\cdot_{\Phi}(g_{2},h_{2})=(g_{1}\cdot_{G}g_{2},h_{1}\cdot_{H}\Phi(g_{1})h_{2}),\quad\forall g_{1},g_{2}\in G,h_{1},h_{2}\in H.

Proposition 2.6
.

(
[
18
]
)
Let
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be an action of a Lie group
G
on a Lie group
H
. Then a smooth map
\mathcal{B}:H\rightarrow G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph
Gr(\mathcal{B})=\{(\mathcal{B}(h),h)|h\in H\}
is a Lie subgroup of the Lie group
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,\cdot_{\Phi})
.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{B}:H\,\rightarrow\,G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on Lie groups, then the Lie algebra of
Gr(\mathcal{B})
is
Gr(\mathcal{B}_{*_{e_{H}}})
.

Definition 2.7
.

[
24
]

Let
G
be a Lie group and
A,B
be Lie subgroups of
G
. The triple
(G,A,B)
is called a double Lie group if
A
and
B
are both closed Lie subgroups of
G
such that

A\cap B=\{e_{G}\},\quad AB=G.

In the following, we will use double Lie groups to explore the integration of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
be a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Denote by
G
and
H
connected and simply connected Lie groups integrating
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
respectively. Denote by
\Phi:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
the integration of
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
.
We know that the semidirect product Lie group
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,\cdot_{\Phi})
is the integration of
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
. By Proposition
2.5
,
(Gr(B),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
is a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
. Denote by
D\subseteq G\ltimes_{\Phi}H
the connected Lie subgroup integrating
(Gr(B),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
.

Theorem 2.8
.

With the above notations, a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
can be integrated into a relative Rota-Baxter Lie group
(G,H,\Phi,\mathcal{B})
if and only if
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,D,G\times e_{H})
is a double Lie group.

Proof.

If a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
can be integrated into a relative Rota-Baxter Lie group
(G,H,\Phi,\mathcal{B})
, by Proposition
2.6
, we have that

D=Gr(\mathcal{B})=\{(\mathcal{B}(h),h)|h\in H\}

is a connected Lie subgroup integrating
Gr(B)
. Since
Gr(\mathcal{B})
is the graph of smooth map
\mathcal{B}
, by
[
23
, Proposition 5.4]
, it follows that
Gr(\mathcal{B})
is an embedded submanifold. By
[
15
, Proposition 9.3.9]
, we have that
Gr(\mathcal{B})
is a connected closed Lie subgroup. Moreover, by direct calculation, it follows that

Gr(\mathcal{B})\cap(G\times e_{H})=(e_{G},e_{H}),

and

Gr(\mathcal{B})\cdot_{\Phi}(G\times e_{H})=\{(\mathcal{B}(h)\cdot_{G}g,h)|\forall(\mathcal{B}(h),h)\in Gr(\mathcal{B}),g\in G\}=G\ltimes_{\Phi}H.

Thus
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,Gr(\mathcal{B}),G\times e_{H})
is a double Lie group.

Conversely, assume that
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,D,G\times e_{H})
is a double Lie group, it follows that
D
is a closed Lie subgroup of
G\ltimes_{\Phi}H
and

D\cap(G\times e_{H})=(e_{G},e_{H}),\quad G\ltimes_{\Phi}H=D\cdot_{\Phi}(G\times e_{H}),

it means that
D
is an embedded submanifold of
G\ltimes_{\Phi}H
. Define smooth maps
P_{H}:G\ltimes_{\Phi}H\,\rightarrow\,H
and
P_{G}:G\ltimes_{\Phi}H\,\rightarrow\,G
by

P_{G}(g,h)=g,\quad P_{H}(g,h)=h,\quad\forall g\in G,h\in H.

Then we have that the map
P_{H}:D\,\rightarrow\,H
is a smooth map. Since

D\cap(G\times e_{H})=(e_{G},e_{H})

and

G\ltimes_{\Phi}H=D\cdot_{\Phi}(G\times e_{H})=\{(g^{\prime}\cdot_{G}g,h)|\forall(g^{\prime},h)\in D,g\in G\},

then it follows that
P_{H}:D\,\rightarrow\,H
is subjective. In addition, if
(g_{1},h)\in D
and
(g_{2},h)\in D
, since

(g_{2},h)\cdot_{\Phi}(g_{1},h)^{-1}=(g_{1}^{-1}\cdot_{G}g_{2},e_{H})\in(G\times e_{H})\cap D=(e_{G},e_{H}),

we have that
g_{1}=g_{2}
. Thus
P_{H}:D\,\rightarrow\,H
is injective.

Since the Lie algebra of the Lie subgroup
D
is
Gr(B)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h}
. Denote by
{P_{H}}_{*}:Gr(B)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
the tangent map of
P_{H}
at
(e_{G},e_{H})
, then we have

{P_{H}}_{*}(B(u),u)=u,\quad\forall u\in\mathfrak{h},

which is an isomorphism from the vector space
Gr(B)
to the vector
space
\mathfrak{h}
. By the inverse function theorem, there exists an open set
V\subseteq D
contains
(e_{G},e_{H})
such that

P_{H}:V\,\rightarrow\,P_{H}(V)
is a diffeomorphism. For any
(g,a)\in D
, we have that

P_{H}((g,a)\cdot_{\Phi}V)=a\cdot_{H}\Phi(g)P_{H}(V)

is an open set. Denote by
W=a\cdot_{H}\Phi(g)P_{H}(V)
, we have

P^{-1}_{H}|_{W}=L_{(g,a)}\circ P_{H}^{-1}|_{V}\circ\Phi(g^{-1})\circ l_{a^{-1}},

where
l_{a^{-1}}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot_{H}b
and
L_{(g,a)}(h,c)=(g,a)\cdot_{\Phi}(h,c)
.
Thus
P^{-1}_{H}
is a smooth map in a neighborhood of
a\in H
. We have that
P^{-1}_{H}:H\,\rightarrow\,D
is a smooth map, it means that
P_{H}:D\,\rightarrow\,H
is a diffeomorphism. Thus there exists a smooth map
\mathcal{B}:H\,\rightarrow\,G
such that
D=Gr(\mathcal{B})
, where

(1)

\mathcal{B}(h)=P_{G}(P_{H}^{-1}(h)),\quad\forall h\in H.

For any
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
, we have

\Big(\mathcal{B}(h_{1}),h_{1}\Big)\cdot_{\Phi}\Big(\mathcal{B}(h_{2}),h_{2}\Big)=\Big(\mathcal{B}(h_{1})\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}),h_{1}\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h_{1}))h_{2}\Big)\in Gr(\mathcal{B}),

which implies that

\mathcal{B}(h_{1})\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2})=\mathcal{B}(h_{1}\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(h_{1}))h_{2}).

Thus
\mathcal{B}:H\,\rightarrow\,G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

See
[
14
]
for more details of when a Lie subgroup is a closed Lie subgroup.

Denote by
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H)/D=\{(g,h)D|\forall(g,h)\in G\ltimes_{\Phi}H\}
the set of left cosets of
D
in
G\ltimes_{\Phi}H
. Define a map
q_{D}:G\ltimes_{\Phi}H\,\rightarrow\,(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H)/D
by

q_{D}(g,h)=(g,h)D,\quad\forall(g,h)\in G\ltimes_{\Phi}H.

Corollary 2.10
.

With the above notations, a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
can be integrated into a relative Rota-Baxter Lie group
(G,H,\Phi,\mathcal{B})
if and only if

(i)

D
is a closed Lie subgroup;

(ii)

q_{D}:G\times e_{H}\,\rightarrow\,(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H)/D
is bijective.

Proof.

For Lie groups
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,\cdot_{\Phi})
and
D
,
(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H,D,G\times e_{H})
is a double Lie group if and only if
D
is a closed Lie subgroup and
q_{D}:G\times e_{H}\,\rightarrow\,(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H)/D
is bijective. Thus a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
can be integrated into a relative Rota-Baxter Lie group
(G,H,\Phi,\mathcal{B})
if and only if
D
is a closed Lie subgroup and
q_{D}:G\times e_{H}\,\rightarrow\,(G\ltimes_{\Phi}H)/D
is bijective.
∎

2.2.
Integrability via matched pairs of Lie groups

In this subsection, we give another necessary and sufficient condition of a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra being global integrable using matched pairs of Lie groups.

Definition 2.11
.

[
25
,
31
]

A matched pair of Lie algebras consists of a pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
, a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
and a representation
\mu:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\displaystyle\rho(x)[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,\rho(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho\big((\mu(v)x\big)u-\rho\big(\mu(u)x\big)v,

\displaystyle\mu(u)[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu(u)x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\mu(u)y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\mu\big(\rho(y)u\big)x-\mu\big(\rho(x)u\big)y,

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
. Denote a matched pair of Lie algebras by
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
.

The following result is well known.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
be a matched pair of Lie algebras. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bowtie})
is a Lie algebra, where

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\bowtie}=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\mu(u)y-\mu(v)x,[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u),

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
.

Definition 2.13
.

[
31
]

A matched pair of Lie groups consists of a pair of Lie groups
(G,\cdot_{G}),(H,\cdot_{H})
, a left action of the Lie group
(G,\cdot_{G})
on the manifold
H
and a right action of the Lie group
(H,\cdot_{H})
on the manifold
G

\Phi:G\times H\to H,\qquad(g,h)\mapsto\Phi(g)h;\qquad\Theta:H\times G\to G\qquad(h,g)\mapsto\Theta(h)g,

such that

\displaystyle\Phi(g)(h_{1}\cdot_{H}h_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(g)h_{1}\cdot_{H}\Phi(\Theta(h_{1})g)h_{2};

\displaystyle\Theta(h)(g_{1}\cdot_{G}g_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta(\Phi(g_{2})h)g_{1}\cdot_{G}\Theta(h)g_{2}.

Denote a matched pair of Lie groups by
(G,H;\Phi,\Theta)
.

It is well known that there is a Lie group structure on
(G\times H,\cdot_{\bowtie})
associated with
(G,H;\Phi,\Theta)
, such that
(G,\cdot_{G})
and
(H,\cdot_{H})
are Lie subgroups,
where the group structure
\cdot_{\bowtie}
is given by

(g_{1},h_{1})\cdot_{\bowtie}(g_{2},h_{2})=(\Theta(h_{2})g_{1}\cdot_{G}g_{2},h_{1}\cdot_{H}\Phi(g_{1})h_{2}),

for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G,h_{1},h_{2}\in H
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be Lie algebras. Let
G
and
H
be connected and simply connected Lie groups integrating
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
. A matched pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
is called integrable if there is a matched pair of Lie groups
(G,H;\Phi,\Theta)
such that the Lie algebra of the Lie group
(G\times H,\cdot_{\bowtie})
associated to
(G,H;\Phi,\Theta)
is the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bowtie})
associated to
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
.

Any matched pair of Lie groups gives rise to a matched pair of Lie algebras. However, matched pairs of Lie algebras are not always integrable, more details can be found in
[
25
]
.

By Proposition
2.5
, it shows that
(Gr(B),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
is a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
be a relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g},Gr(B);\bar{\phi},\bar{\theta})
is a matched pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(Gr(B),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
, where

\bar{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(Gr(B))\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\theta}:Gr(B)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})

are given by

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}(x)(B(v),v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B(\phi(x)v),\phi(x)v),

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}(B(v),v)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\phi(x)v)+[B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},v\in\mathfrak{h}.

In other words,
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus Gr(B),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bowtie})
is a Lie algebra, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bowtie}
is given by

is a diffeomorphism with inverse map
q^{-1}_{2}:(G\times E)\times(G\times E)\times(G\times E)\times_{T\times T\times T}NT_{2}\,\rightarrow\,\bar{\Gamma}_{2}
given by

q_{2}^{-1}\Big((a_{1},h_{1}),(a_{2},h_{2}),(a_{3},h_{3}),t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=(a_{3},(h_{3},a_{3}^{-1}a_{2},a_{1},h_{2}h_{1}^{-1}),h_{1}).

Therefore
q_{2}
is in particular injective etale. Thus
\Upsilon_{\bullet}:\bar{\Gamma}_{\bullet}\,\rightarrow\,NT_{\bullet}
is a hypercover of local Lie
2
-groups.

Therefore,
\bar{\Gamma}_{\bullet}
and
NT_{\bullet}
are Morita equivalent.

If
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
is integrable, by Proposition
2.20
,
\Upsilon_{1}
is a diffeomorphism. By
[
33
, Lemma 2.5]
,
\bar{\Gamma}_{\bullet}
is isomorphic to
NT_{\bullet}
, which implies that
\bar{\Gamma}_{\bullet}
is a Lie
1
-group.
∎

Remark 2.23
.

As we have seen, when
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
is integrable, the local Lie
2
-group
\bar{\Gamma}_{\bullet}
is diffeomorphic to
NT_{\bullet}
. Thus when
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
is not integrable, the double groupoid (
3
) or its Artin-Mazur codiagonal
\bar{\Gamma}_{\bullet}
can be viewed as a replacement of the integration.

In
[
18
]
, it was proved that every Rota-Baxter operator on Lie algebras
B:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
can be integrated into a local relative Rota-Baxter operator on Lie groups
\mathcal{B}:U(\subseteq H)\,\rightarrow\,G
. Thus when
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi,B)
is not integrable, the local relative Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}
may also be viewed as a replacement of the integration.

However we need to notice that the local Lie group
U
coming from
\mathcal{B}
is not
\bar{\Gamma}_{\bullet}
. In fact
\dim\bar{\Gamma}_{1}=2\dim U
. These two sorts of replacements are different but related.

2.3.
Integrability via factorization of a diffeomorphism

In this subsection, we give another necessary and sufficient condition of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras being global integrable.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},B)
be a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. By Proposition
2.5
, it shows that
(Gr(B),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
is a Lie algebra. Define linear maps
f_{-}:Gr(B)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
and
f_{+}:Gr(B)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
by

f_{-}(B(x),x)=B(x),\quad f_{+}(B(x),x)=B(x)+x,\quad\forall(B(x),x)\in Gr(B).

Since

\displaystyle f_{-}\Big([(B(x),x),(B(y),y)]_{\phi}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{-}([B(x),B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B(x),B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[f_{-}(B(x),x),f_{-}(B(y),y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},

and

\displaystyle f_{+}\Big([(B(x),x),(B(y),y)]_{\phi}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(x),B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f_{+}(B(x),x),f_{+}(B(y),y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},

it follows that
f_{-}
and
f_{+}
are Lie algebra homomorphisms.

Denote by
D
and
G
connected and simply connected Lie groups integrating
Gr(B)
and
\mathfrak{g}
respectively. Let
F_{-}:D\,\rightarrow\,G
and
F_{+}:D\,\rightarrow\,G
be Lie groups homomorphisms integrating
f_{-}
and
f_{+}
respectively. Define a smooth map
J:D\,\rightarrow\,G
by

J(a)=F_{+}(a)\cdot_{G}F_{-}(a)^{-1},\quad\forall a\in D.

Theorem 2.24
.

With the above notations, the Rota-Baxter operator
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is integrable if and only if the map
J:D\,\rightarrow\,G
is invertible.

Proof.

If
J:D\,\rightarrow\,G
is invertible, define a smooth map
\mathcal{B}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
by

\mathcal{B}(g)=F_{-}(J^{-1}(g)),\quad\forall g\in G.

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{-}(J^{-1}(g))\cdot_{G}F_{-}(J^{-1}(h))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{-}J^{-1}\Big(J((J^{-1}(g)\cdot_{G}J^{-1}(h)))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(F_{+}(J^{-1}(g))\cdot_{G}F_{+}(J^{-1}(h))\cdot_{G}F_{-}(J^{-1}(h))^{-1}\cdot_{G}F_{-}(J^{-1}(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(g\cdot_{G}F_{-}(J^{-1}(g))\cdot_{G}h\cdot_{G}F_{-}(J^{-1}(g))^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot_{G}h\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(g)^{-1}),

it follows that
\mathcal{B}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
is a Rota-Baxter operator. Moreover, the tangent map of
J
at
e_{D}
is

J_{*}(B(x),x)=f_{+}(B(x),x)-f_{-}(B(x),x)=x,\quad\forall(B(x),x)\in Gr(B),

we have that

\mathcal{B}_{*}(x)=f_{-}(J^{-1}_{*}(x))=f_{-}(B(x),x)=B(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Thus
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is integrable.

Conversely, if
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is integrable, denote by
\mathcal{B}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
the integration of
B
. By Proposition
2.6
,
Gr(\mathcal{B})
is a connected and simply connected Lie group integrating
Gr(B)
, i.e.
D=Gr(\mathcal{B})
. Define
F_{\pm}:D\,\rightarrow\,G
by

\displaystyle F_{-}(\mathcal{B}(g),g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(g),

\displaystyle F_{+}(\mathcal{B}(g),g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(g),\quad\forall(\mathcal{B}(g),g)\in Gr(\mathcal{B}).

We have that
F_{\pm}
are Lie group homomorphisms and
(F_{\pm})_{*e_{D}}=f_{\pm}
.
Moreover, the smooth map
J:Gr(\mathcal{B})\,\rightarrow\,G
is given by

J(\mathcal{B}(g),g)=F_{+}(\mathcal{B}(g),g)\cdot_{G}F_{-}(\mathcal{B}(g),g)^{-1}=g,

thus the smooth map
J:Gr(\mathcal{B})\,\rightarrow\,G
is invertible.
∎

Remark 2.25
.

If
B:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is an invertible relative Rota-Baxter operator on Lie algebras, then the inverse of
B
is a crossed homomorphism on Lie algebras. In
[
16
,
17
]
, it was shown that every crossed homomorphism on Lie algebras is integrable. But it is not clear that whether every invertible relative Rota-Baxter operator on Lie algebras is integrable.

3.
Integration and factorizable Poisson-Lie groups

In this section, we apply the integration of Rota-Baxter operators to characterize when Poisson-Lie groups integrating factorizable Lie bialgebras are factorizable Poisson-Lie groups.

We first recall factorizable Lie bialgebras and factorizable Poisson-Lie groups. See
[
10
,
19
,
24
,
29
]
for more details.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra and
\gamma:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map. The pair
(\mathfrak{g},\gamma)
is called a Lie bialgebra if

(i)

\gamma
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the representation
(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad}\otimes{\rm{Id}}+{\rm{Id}}\otimes\mathrm{ad})
, i.e.

\gamma([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[\gamma(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\gamma(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

(ii)

(\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
is a Lie algebra, where

\langle[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}},x\rangle=\gamma(x)(\xi,\eta),\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},~~x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. Denote by

r=a+s=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},\quad\text{where}~~~~a\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},~~s\in S^{2}\mathfrak{g}.

Define the linear map
\gamma:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
by
\gamma=\mathrm{d}r
, where
\mathrm{d}
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad}\otimes{\rm{Id}}+{\rm{Id}}\otimes\mathrm{ad})
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
is a Lie bialgebra if and only if
\mathrm{ad}_{x}s=0
and
\mathrm{ad}_{x}\left\llbracket r,r\right\rrbracket=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, where

\displaystyle\left\llbracket r,r\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes[y_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes y_{j}+\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes[y_{i},y_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

In particular, if
\left\llbracket r,r\right\rrbracket=0
, the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
is called a quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
be a quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra. Define a linear map
I:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
by
I=r_{+}-r_{-}
, where

\langle r_{+}(\xi),\eta\rangle=r(\xi,\eta),\quad\langle r_{-}(\xi),\eta\rangle=-r(\eta,\xi),\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Then in this setting, the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by

[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}=\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)}\eta-\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{r_{-}(\eta)}\xi,\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Moreover,
r_{+}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
and
r_{-}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
are Lie algebra homomorphisms.

Definition 3.2
.

The quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
is called a factorizable Lie bialgebra if the linear map
I:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is an isomorphism.

A factorizable Lie bialgebra can give rise a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
22
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
be a factorizable Lie bialgebra with
I=r_{+}-r_{-}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},r_{-}\circ I^{-1})
is a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(G,\cdot_{G})
be a Lie group and
(G,\pi)
is a Poisson manifold. The triple
(G,\cdot_{G},\pi)
is called a Poisson-Lie group if
\pi
satisfies

\pi_{g\cdot_{G}h}=L_{g}\pi_{h}+R_{h}\pi_{g},\quad\forall g,h\in G.

It is well known that Lie bialgebras are one-to-one correspondence with connected and simply connected Poisson-Lie groups.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
be a factorizable Lie algebra. Denote by
(G,\cdot_{G},\pi_{r})
the connected and simply connected Poisson-Lie group integrating
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, where the Poisson structure
\pi_{r}
is given by

\pi_{r}=r^{L}-r^{R}=a^{L}-a^{R},

where
r^{L}
and
r^{R}
are vector fields obtained by left translation and right translation of
r
respectively.

Denote by
(G^{*},\cdot_{G^{*}})
the connected and simply connected Lie group integrating
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
. Let
R_{+}:G^{*}\,\rightarrow\,G
and
R_{-}:G^{*}\,\rightarrow\,G
be the lifted Lie group homomorphisms of the Lie algebra homomorphisms
r_{+}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
and
r_{-}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
.

Define a smooth map
J:G^{*}\,\rightarrow\,G
by

J(q)=R_{+}(q)\cdot_{G}R_{-}(q)^{-1},\quad\forall q\in G^{*}.

The Poisson-Lie group
(G,\cdot_{G},\pi_{r})
is called a
factorizable Poisson-Lie group
if
J
is a diffeomorphism. It is known that not every factorizable Lie bialgebra can be integrated into a factorizable Poisson-Lie group. In the sequel, we give a necessary and sufficient condition on when a factorizable Lie bialgebra can be integrated into a factorizable Poisson-Lie group using the integrability of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras.

Theorem 3.5
.

With the above notations, the Poisson-Lie group
(G,\cdot_{G},\pi_{r})
integrating
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
is a factorizable Poisson-Lie group if and only if the Rota-Baxter operator
r_{-}\circ I^{-1}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is integrable.

Proof.

Since
r_{-}\circ I^{-1}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is a Rota-Baxter operator, thus
(Gr(r_{-}\circ I^{-1}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{ad}})
is a Lie algebra. Define a linear map
\hat{I}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,Gr(r_{-}\circ I^{-1})
by

\hat{I}(\xi)=(r_{-}(\xi),I(\xi)),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

We have

\displaystyle\hat{I}([\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([r_{-}(\xi),r_{-}(\eta)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[r_{+}(\xi),r_{+}(\eta)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[r_{-}(\xi),r_{-}(\eta)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([r_{-}(\xi),r_{-}(\eta)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[I(\xi),r_{-}(\eta)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[r_{-}(\xi),I(\eta)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[I(\xi),I(\eta)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{I}(\xi),\hat{I}(\eta)]_{\mathrm{ad}}.

Thus
\hat{I}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,\rightarrow\,Gr(r_{-}\circ I^{-1})
is a Lie algebra isomorphism. Moreover, we have two Lie algebra homomorphisms as following,

r_{+}\circ\hat{I}^{-1}:Gr(r_{-}\circ I^{-1})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g},\quad r_{+}\circ\hat{I}^{-1}(r_{-}(I^{-1}(x)),x)=r_{-1}(I^{-1}(x))+x,

and

r_{-}\circ\hat{I}^{-1}:Gr(r_{-}\circ I^{-1})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g},\quad r_{-}\circ\hat{I}^{-1}(r_{-}(I^{-1}(x)),x)=r_{-}(I^{-1}(x)).

Denote by
E,G
and
G^{*}
connected and simply connected Lie groups integrating
Gr(r_{-}\circ I^{-1}),\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
respectively. Denote by
R_{-},R_{+}
and
\hat{\mathcal{I}}
Lie group homomorphisms integrating
r_{-},r_{+}
and
\hat{I}
respectively. We have that
G^{*}=\hat{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(E)
. Define a smooth map
J:E\,\rightarrow\,G
by

J(a)=R_{+}(\hat{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(a))\cdot_{G}R_{-}(\hat{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(a))^{-1},\quad\forall a\in E.

By Theorem
2.24
, it follows that
r_{-}\circ I^{-1}
is integrable if and only if
J
is a diffeomorphism. Thus the Poisson-Lie group
(G,\cdot_{G},\pi_{r})
integrating
(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}r)
is a factorizable Poisson-Lie group if and only if the Rota-Baxter operator
r_{-}\circ I^{-1}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is integrable.
∎

4.
Examples

In this section, we give some examples. In particular, we study the integrability of Rota-Baxter operators on the nontrivial
2
-dimensional Lie algebra. It turns out that not all Rota-Baxter operators are integrable. As a byproduct, we construct a matched pair of Lie algebras that can not be integrated to a matched pair of Lie groups.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(3,\mathbb{R})
, whose integration is the connected and simply connected Lie group
\mathrm{SU}(2)
. Since
\mathrm{SU}(2)
is compact, by Corollary
2.19
, then any Rota-Baxter operator
B:\mathfrak{so}(3,\mathbb{R})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{so}(3,\mathbb{R})
can be integrated into a Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}:\mathrm{SU}(2)\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{SU}(2)
.

Example 4.2
.

Consider the Heisenberg Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
with the basis

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad Y=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),

and

[X,Y]=Z,\quad[X,Z]=0,\quad[Y,Z]=0.

Then
G=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&a&b\\
0&1&c\\
0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right)|a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the connected and simply connected Lie group integrating the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Define a linear map
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
by

B(X,Y,Z)=(X,Y,Z)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
\end{array}\right).

By straightforward computation,
B
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, and the Lie subalgebra
Gr(B)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{ad}}\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\displaystyle Gr(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{span}\{\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right)\Big),\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right)\Big),\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right)\Big)\}.

Denote by

D=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&c\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&a&b\\
0&1&c\\
0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right)|a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Then
D\subseteq G\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}}G
is the closed connected Lie subgroup and
T_{(e_{G},e_{G})}D=Gr(B)
. Moreover, by straightforward computation, it follows that
D\cdot_{\mathrm{Ad}}(G\times e_{G})=G\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}}G
. By Theorem
2.8
, the Rota-Baxter
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
can be integrated into a Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
. By (
1
), the Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{B}
is given by

\mathcal{B}(\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&a&b\\
0&1&c\\
0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right))=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&c\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right).

In fact, any Rota-Baxter operator on the Heisenberg Lie algebra is integrable. See
[
28
]
for more details.

Example 4.3
.

Consider the
2
-dimensional real Lie algebra

\Big(\mathfrak{g}=\text{span}\{e_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right),e_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right)\},~~~~[\cdot,\cdot]\Big),

where

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{1}e_{2}-e_{2}e_{1}=2e_{2}.

Then
G=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&\frac{1}{a}\\
\end{array}\right)|a>0,b\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the connected and simply connected Lie group integrating the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
A linear map
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
given by

B(e_{1},e_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}b_{11}&b_{12}\\
b_{21}&b_{22}\\
\end{array}\right)

is a Rota-Baxter operator if and only if

[B(e_{1}),B(e_{2})]=B([B(e_{1}),e_{2}]+[e_{1},B(e_{2})]+[e_{1},e_{2}]).

By straightforward computation, there are the following three classes of Rota-Baxter operators:

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&0\\
\mu&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\text{or}\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&0\\
\mu&-1\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\text{or}\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-m-1&k\\
\frac{-m^{2}-m}{k}&m\\
\end{array}\right),\quad k\neq 0.

In the following, we will analyse the integrability of above Rota-Baxter operators on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Case
1.1:
the Rota-Baxter operator
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is given by

B(e_{1},e_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&0\\
\mu&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\text{where}\quad\lambda\neq-1,\lambda\neq 0,\mu\in\mathbb{R}.

The Lie algebra
Gr(B)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{ad}}\mathfrak{g}
associated to
B
is

Gr(B)=\text{span}\{\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&\mu\\
0&-\lambda\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right)\Big),\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right)\Big)\}.

Denote by

D=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{\lambda}&\frac{\mu}{2\lambda}(a^{\lambda}-a^{-\lambda})\\
0&a^{-\lambda}\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&-a^{(\lambda+1)}\frac{\mu}{2\lambda}(a^{\lambda}-a^{-\lambda})+a^{\lambda}\frac{\mu}{2(\lambda+1)}(a^{(\lambda+1)}-a^{-(\lambda+1)})+sa^{-1}\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)|a>0,s\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Then
D\subseteq G\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}}G
is the closed connected Lie subgroup and
T_{(e_{G},e_{G})}D=Gr(B)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle D\cdot_{\mathrm{Ad}}(G\times e_{G})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle G\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}}G.

For any

\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x&y\\
0&\frac{1}{x}\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}z&w\\
0&\frac{1}{z}\\
\end{array}\right)\Big)\in G\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}}G
, let

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}p&=&\frac{x}{z^{\lambda}},\\
q&=&\frac{y}{z^{\lambda}}-\frac{\mu}{2\lambda x}(z^{\lambda}-z^{-\lambda}),\\
a&=&z,\\
s&=&zw+z^{(\lambda+2)}\frac{\mu}{2\lambda}(z^{\lambda}-z^{-\lambda})-z^{\lambda+1}\frac{\mu}{2(\lambda+1)}(z^{(\lambda+1)}-z^{-(\lambda+1)}).\end{array}\right.

Then

\displaystyle\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x&y\\
0&\frac{1}{x}\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}z&w\\
0&\frac{1}{z}\\
\end{array}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{\lambda}&\frac{\mu}{2\lambda}(a^{\lambda}-a^{-\lambda})\\
0&a^{-\lambda}\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&-a^{(\lambda+1)}\frac{\mu}{2\lambda}(a^{\lambda}-a^{-\lambda})+a^{\lambda}\frac{\mu}{2(\lambda+1)}(a^{(\lambda+1)}-a^{-(\lambda+1)})+sa^{-1}\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle\cdot_{\mathrm{Ad}}\Big(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p&q\\
0&p^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right)\Big)\in D\cdot_{\mathrm{Ad}}(G\times e_{G}).

Thus
D\cdot_{\mathrm{Ad}}(G\times e_{G})=G\ltimes_{\mathrm{Ad}}G
. By Theorem
2.8
, the Rota-Baxter operator
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
can be integrated into a Rota-Baxter operator

A large class of Rota-Baxter operators are given by projections. More precisely, let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{h}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{2}
be Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{h}
such that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}
as vector spaces. Denote by
P_{1},P_{2}
by projections from
\mathfrak{h}
to
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
respectively. Then
-P_{1},-P_{2}
are Rota-Baxter operators. These Rota-Baxter operators are not always integrable as the following example shows.

Example 4.4
.

Consider the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
with a basis
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, where
[u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}=2(u_{2}-u_{1})
. Then the linear map

B:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h},\quad B(k_{1}u_{1}+k_{2}u_{2})=-k_{2}u_{2},\quad k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R},~u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathfrak{h},

is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Define a Lie algebra isomorphism
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
by

\varphi(e_{1},e_{2})=(u_{1},u_{2})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-1\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right),

where
(\mathfrak{g}=\text{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\},[\cdot,\cdot])
is the Lie algebra given in Example
4.3
. We obtain that

B^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g},\quad B^{\prime}(e_{1},e_{2})=\varphi^{-1}B\varphi(e_{1},e_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right),

is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
. By Example
4.3
, we know that the Rota-Baxter operator
B^{\prime}
is not integrable, thus
B
is not integrable.

As a byproduct, we give a matched pair of Lie algebras that can not be integrated into a matched pair of Lie groups at the end of this section.

Consider the Lie algebra

\Big(\mathfrak{g}=\text{span}\{e_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right),e_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right)\},[\cdot,\cdot]\Big),

given in Example
4.3
. The connected and simply connected Lie group integrating
\mathfrak{g}
is

G=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&\frac{1}{a}\\
\end{array}\right)|a>0,b\in\mathbb{R}\}.

It shows that the map

B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g},\quad B(e_{1},e_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-m-1&k\\
\frac{-m^{2}-m}{k}&m\\
\end{array}\right),

is a Rota-Baxter operator in the Example
4.3
. By Proposition
2.15
, we have that
(\mathfrak{g},Gr(B);\bar{\phi},\bar{\theta})
is a matched pair of Lie algebras, where

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}(x)(B(y),y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B([x,y]),[x,y]),

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}(B(y),y)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B([x,y])+[B(y),x],\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},(B(y),y)\in Gr(B).

Denote by
E
the connected and simply connected Lie group integrating
Gr(B)
. By Example
4.3
, it shows that
(\mathfrak{g},B)
can not be integrated into a Rota-Baxter Lie group. Thus by Theorem
2.18
, it follows that the matched pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},Gr(B);\bar{\phi},\bar{\theta})
can not be integrated into a matched pair of
G
and
E
.

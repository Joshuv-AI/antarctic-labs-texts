Loose elements in binary and ternary matroids
Jagdeep Singh, Thomas Zaslavsky

Abstract
. We call a matroid element “loose” if it is contained in no circuits of size less than the rank of the matroid. A matroid in which all elements are loose is a paving matroid. Acketa determined all binary paving matroids, while Oxley specified all ternary paving matroids. We characterize the binary matroids that contain a loose element. For ternary matroids with a loose element, we show that their size is linear in terms of their rank. Moreover, for a prime power q q , we give a partial characterization of G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) -representable matroids that have two or more loose elements; we note Rajpal’s partial characterization of G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) -representable paving matroids as a consequence.

Introduction

An element

if every circuit of
M
that contains
t
is spanning. We say

containing

has size at least the rank

of the matroid

if every circuit of

has size at least

. It is clear that every element of a paving matroid is loose. Oxley and Singh
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
characterized binary and ternary matroids that have a free element. In Section 2 we characterize the binary matroids that have a loose element.

All matroids considered here are finite and simple. The notation and terminology follow
[
2
]
, except where otherwise indicated.

We define some necessary matroids.
Let
\{b,x_{i},y_{i}\}

and taking its dual, we obtain

The matroid

is derived from the Fano matroid by replacing an element with a series class of size

. Similarly, the matroid

is obtained from the binary affine geometry of rank four by replacing an element with a series class of size

Representations of the matroids

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Observe that circuits are among the matroids in Theorem

Section 3 considers ternary matroids that have a loose element. We show that the size of such matroids is linear in terms of their rank. The main result is the following.

2.
loose elements in binary matroids

Oxley and Singh characterized binary and ternary matroids with no coloops that have a free element.

Proposition 2.1

(
[

]
)
.

Let

-matroid with no coloops and rank at least two, and let

(i)

is a circuit when

Now we prove Theorem

that characterizes simple binary matroids without coloops that have a loose element.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It is clear that both

and

have a loose element. If

is a restriction of

or

with no coloops that contains the element

as represented in Figures

is a loose element of

Conversely, let
M
be a simple binary matroid of rank
r
with no coloops that contains a loose element

. First suppose that there is a basis
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
of

such that the column
e^{P}
in the standard binary representation

has all ones. Let
f^{P}
be a column in
Q-e^{P}
labeled by an element
f
of
E(M)
. Since
M
is simple,
f^{P}
has at least two ones. Suppose
f^{P}
has at least three ones, say at entries

and
k
. Then
(\{e,f\}\cup B)-\{b_{i},b_{j},b_{k}\}
contains a circuit of size at most

that includes
e
. Since
e
is loose, this is a contradiction. Therefore
f^{P}
has exactly two ones. Let
f^{P},g^{P}
be a pair of columns in
Q-e^{P}
. We show that, if
\{i,j\}
and
\{k,l\}
are the non-zero entries of
f^{P}
and

respectively, then
|\{i,j\}\cap\{k,l\}|=1
. If not, then

has four ones so

has four zeroes and

is contained in a circuit of size less than

, a contradiction. Therefore

is isomorphic to

or a restriction of
M_{r}
.

We now assume that there is no spanning circuit of
M
containing
e
, so all circuits of
M
containing
e
have size

. Let
B
be basis of
M
that does not contain
e
. The column
e^{P}
in the matrix

has exactly one entry that is zero; we may assume it is the first entry of the column. Let
f^{P}
be a column in
Q-e^{P}
labeled by an element
f
of
M
. We show that the first entry of
f^{P}
is one and it has exactly three non-zero entries. If the first entry of
f^{P}
is zero, then for some

, the
i^{\rm th}
and the
j^{\rm th}
entries of
f^{P}
are non-zero. Observe that

contains a circuit of size at most

that includes

, a contradiction. Therefore the first entry of

is one. It is easy to check that if

has exactly two non-zero entries then we get a spanning circuit containing

, a contradiction. Also, if

has more than three non-zero entries, we get a circuit of size less than

that contains

. Therefore
f^{P}
has exactly three non-zero entries. Let

be a pair of columns in

. Note that for the non-zero entries at

of

and at

of

, we have

. If not, then

has five zeroes so

is in a circuit of size less than

, a contradiction. It follows that

is isomorphic to

or a restriction of

.
∎

3.

We show that the size of a ternary matroid that has a loose element is linear in terms of its rank. The main result is as follows.

Theorem 3.1

Step 3.1.1

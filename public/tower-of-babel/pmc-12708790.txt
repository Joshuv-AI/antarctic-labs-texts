Experimental study on the influence of vibration mixing on the characteristics of holes and durability of marine concrete
Authors: Zhaoyong Chen, Fuchang Wang, Benwang Liu, Zengqiang Wang, Ao Meng, Zhaomeng Bian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe pore characteristics and composition of concrete affect its strength and durability. In order to improve the service life of marine concrete, this paper conducted experimental research on the performance of high-performance marine concrete based on vibration mixing. Comparative experiments were conducted on the water absorption performance, pore structure characteristics, load, and chloride ion diffusion of C30 and C50 concrete prepared by vibration mixing and ordinary mixing, and different grades of concrete were compared and studied. The experimental results show that compared with ordinary mixing, the average water absorption rate of C30 and C50 marine concrete under vibration mixing was reduced by 30.8% and 28.9%, respectively; The coefficients of variation were 1.8% and 1.5%, respectively, and the reduction was 75.4% and 75.8%. The average pore size of C30 and C50 concrete specimens subjected to vibration mixing was 72.68 nm and 55.9 nm, respectively, with porosity rates of 22.1% and 15.8%, respectively. Compared with ordinary mixing, the average pore size decreased by 32.7% and 32.1%, and the porosity rate decreased by 51.4% and 53.3%, respectively. The chloride ion diffusion coefficients of C30 and C50 concrete were reduced by 20.4% and 19.8% after 90 days of vibration mixing in a 2.5% sodium chloride environment and a 50% stress ratio. The study shows that vibration mixing improves the pore structure characteristics of marine concrete, improves the density of concrete, enhances the durability of concrete, providing new technologies for the future design and construction of marine concrete in marine transportation and other marine ancillary projects.Keywords: Vibration mixing, Marine concrete, Water absorption, Holes characteristics, DurabilitySubject terms: Engineering, Civil engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

Concrete has become an essential building material for the construction of marine environment highways and railways due to its easy preparation and relatively low cost1–3. For example, the concrete usage of the Hangzhou Bay Cross Sea Bridge in China reached 2.45 million cubic meters. Concrete exposed to the marine environment for a long time is prone to deterioration under load and chloride erosion, which further corrodes the embedded steel bars and seriously affects the quality of the structure4,5. Therefore, designing and preparing long-life marine concrete has important practical significance.

To improve the performance of marine concrete, many scholars and engineers have conducted research from different directions, such as direct effects from mix proportions and added materials6–9. Shariati et al.10 studied the behavior of concrete containing steel fibers and nylon particles under sulfuric acid erosion. After optimization, it was found that under 90 days of acid exposure, the optimal volume percentages of steel fibers and nylon particles in concrete were 0.11% and 20%, respectively, which could maximize the compressive strength. Shaikh et al.11 found that compared with ordinary concrete, the 28-day water absorption of concrete added 1wt% of nano-SiO2 and 1wt% of nano-CaCO3 can reduce by 30.2% and 32.4%, respectively. Esmaeili et al.12 investigated the effect of adding graphene oxide to concrete on its properties. The results showed that the 28-day water absorption rate of ordinary concrete was 2.7%, and the water absorption of concrete containing 0.04 wt% and 0.06 wt% graphene oxide decreased by 11.1% and 14.0%, respectively. Sun et al.13 studied the effect of adding metakaolin-based slurry additive on the properties of marine concrete. The results showed that concrete with metakaolin-based slurry additives have higher flowability than that of powder additives. Slurry additives help improve volume stability and carbonation resistance, and reduce the porosity of marine concrete. Shariati et al.14 studied the use of coal waste (CW) as a partial replacement for fine and coarse aggregates in concrete. The results indicated that higher CW content leads to more water absorption and harder mixtures, but lower processability. The weight loss of the specimens increased with the increase of CW content.

The above research has a certain effect on improving the various properties of marine concrete, but mixing is an important process in concrete preparation, and advanced mixing technology can further enhance the performance of marine concrete. Yao et al.15 proposed a design of a double blade mixer, and found through comparative experiments that the double blade mixer can provide faster and more uniform mixing at both macro and micro scales, enhance the microstructure of concrete, and improve its compressive strength. Wang et al.16 studied the influence of the installation angle of the mixing blades in a double axis horizontal mixer on the blade model and mixing quality. The simulation results showed that the optimal installation angle and spacing were 31.8 ° and 30 ° −34 °, with minimal blade wear and higher mixing quality. Lu et al.17 verified through simulation and experimental mixing of blades that when the inclination angle of the mixing blades of a vertical mixer is 45 °, the collision frequency of the mixer is the highest and the uniformity of concrete particles is better. Sicakova et al.18 studied the applicability of three-stage mixing in the production of ready mixed concrete. The study showed that three-stage mixing can significantly improve particle distribution, reduce water demand, and maintain the required strength characteristics. The above research indicates that improvements in mixing technology, such as blade shape and installation angle, can enhance the performance of concrete mixing. But it has not yet brought a breakthrough in the principle of mixing, and vibration mixing technology has brought a new way for concrete mixing. Yao et al.19 studied the effect of vibration mixing on the porosity of concrete and found that vibration mixing can reduce the pore size of concrete, and improve its density and strength. Zhao et al.20 studied the performance of sand concrete made by vibration mixer, and the results showed that vibration mixing improved the compressive strength of mechanism sand concrete. Active powders such as fly ash are often added to marine concrete, requiring lower porosity and greater corrosion resistance. Mixing has a greater impact on improving its performance.

Therefore, in this paper, the water absorption performance, characteristics of holes, and chloride ion diffusion performance of marine concrete under the combined action of load and chloride environment using vibration mixing and ordinary mixing processes were studied by comparative experiments. The superiority of vibration mixing in improving the performance of marine concrete is verified, providing engineering guidance for improving the durability of marine concrete using vibration mixing technology.

Experimental materials and methods

Experimental material

In this paper, C30 and C50 high-performance marine concretes are selected as the experimental research object based on the actual project. Table 1 shows the mix proportions of two grades of marine concrete. The cement used in the experiment is Portland cement (P·O 42.5), which has a 28 d compressive strength of 53.1 MPa and a flexural strength of 8.7 MPa, respectively, and its volume stability is qualified. The fly ash is Class F (Grade II) with a fineness of 22.6% and a chloride ion content of 0.01%, which meets the specified requirements of the standard‘Fly ash used for cement and concrete GB/T 1596–2017’. The slag powder is S95 grade, with activity indexes of 7 d and 28 d of 89% and 103% respectively, and the specific surface area is 423 m2/kg and the apparent density is 2.92 g/cm3. The coarse aggregate is 5–25 mm continuously graded crushed stone, and the fine aggregate is river sand with a fineness modulus is 2.66. The superplasticizer is a polycarboxylic acid retarding high-performance water reducing agent with a water reducing rate of 28.8% and an air content of 2.7%. The water is common tap water.

Experimental process and test method

Concrete specimens shown as Fig. 1 were fabricated using vibration mixing equipment. When the vibration function was turned off, the default was normal mixing. The mixing time was 90 s. The vibration frequency of vibration stirring was 50 Hz, the amplitude was 0.5 mm, and the vibration acceleration was 3.7 g. After 28 days of standard curing, 75-mm-diameter cores were extracted from four vertical positions and machined into 75 mm × 75 mm cylinders, as shown in Fig. 2. The specimens were dried, immersed in water for specified durations, surface-dried, and weighed. The water absorption rates were calculated from mass measurements, with each value averaged over three replicates.

Concrete specimen under two mixing processes. (a) sample of strength test. (b) test core of water absorption

Coring diagram of water absorption rate of marine concrete.

The pore size distribution and porosity analysis were performed via mercury intrusion porosimetry (Auto Pore V, three tests per sample), the test pressure range was 0-400 MPa. A 2.5% NaCl solution simulated the marine environment, and 100 × 100 × 300 mm prisms were cast. Following compressive strength testing at 28 days, the specimens were loaded to 0%, 20%, 30%, 40%, or 50% of their peak stress and exposed to the chloride solution at the same time. The chloride migration coefficients were determined after 56 and 90 days according to ‘Standard for test methods of long-term performance and durability of concrete GB/T50082-2024’.

Results and discussion

Figure 3 shows the water absorption of C30 and C50 concrete at different coring positions under two mixing processes after 28 d curing. As can be seen from Fig. 3, as a whole, before the water absorption is saturated, the water absorption of the two kinds of the experimental samples increased gradually with the increase of soaking time. When the change in water absorption and the power of half of time was plotted, it is consistent with the corresponding relationship between capillary water absorption and time per unit area of Kelham21. The water absorption of concrete samples using vibration mixing is significantly lower than that of ordinary mixing. For the samples cored at different positions, the water absorption rate also showed an upward trend with the increase of the coring height, seeing Fig. 3 (a) for change of water absorption of C30 concrete. For concrete using ordinary mixing, the top-core specimen’s water absorption is 32.65% higher than the bottom-core one. For concrete using vibration mixing, the top-core specimen’s water absorption is 10.05% higher than the bottom-core one. Compared with ordinary mixing, the average water absorption of core samples using vibration mixing reduces by 30.8% from four heights. The coefficient of variation under vibration mixing is 1.8%, and that under ordinary mixing is 7.3%. Seeing Fig. 3 (b) for the change of water absorption of C50 concrete for the same soaking time. For specimens using ordinary mixing, the water absorption of the top-core sample is 33.43% higher than that of the bottom-core one. Under vibration mixing, the top is 11.11% higher than the bottom. In water absorption. The coefficient of variation under vibration mixing is 1.5%, and that under ordinary mixing is 6.2%. Compared to ordinary mixing, the average water absorption of the four-height core samples using vibration mixing reduces by 28.9%, and the coefficient of variation is smaller. From the experimental results of water absorption of C30 and C50 concrete under two processes, the porosity of concrete using vibration mixing is lower after hardening. Esmaeili et al.12 studied the performance of the economical graphene oxide nanosheet modified UHPC, and the water absorption is between 2.71% and 3.33%. Compared with the water absorption rate of concrete under vibration mixing, the vibration mixing process reduces the water absorption rate of concrete. The water absorption of the samples taken at different positions has little change, which indicates that the particles in the concrete structure are more evenly dispersed. The mixing quality difference is small and the quality is better, which is conducive to improving the durability of marine concrete.

Microscopic pore structure characteristics of marine concrete

Table 2 shows the comparison of pore structure parameters of C30 and C50 marine concrete under two mixing processes. As shown in Table 2, for C30 concrete, a mercury intrusion volume of concrete using ordinary mixing is 0.174 ml/g, and ones using ordinary mixing decrease to 0.103 ml/g, reaching a reduction of 40.8%. From the microscopic structure, a porosity of concrete using vibration mixing is 34.4% and an average diameter of the pores is 22.1 nm. Comparing with concrete using ordinary mixing, concrete under vibration mixing of the total pore area, the average diameter of the pores, porosity and volume median pore diameter decreased by 32.7%, 51.4%, 35.7% and 63.2%, respectively. Similarly, for C50 concrete, the mercury intrusion of concrete using vibration mixing drops by 36.5% compared to that using ordinary mixing. The total pore area, the average diameter of the pores, porosity and volume median pore diameter decreased by 32.1%, 53.3%, 40.4% and 66.3%, respectively.

The experimental results show that the pore structure of marine concrete is optimized by vibration mixing. The main reason is that there is a low-efficiency area in the ordinary mixing, the cement powders can not be fully mixed and the reaction is insufficient. And after adding fly ash, mineral powder and other microscopic particles, its water absorption affects the formation of hydration products of hardened concrete. At the same time, the excess mineral powder will dissociate in the aggregate and other positions, destroy the original gradation, and increase the porosity and average pore size. Vibration mixing is to add exciting force on the basis of ordinary mixing, which makes the blades of the mixing shaft produce flutter while rotating. Overcomes the low efficiency area of ordinary mixing, and the fine and micro particles such as cement and the like are dispersed more uniformly, thereby accelerating the hydration reaction. Mineral powder particles can also play a role in filling micropores, and the pore size will become smaller or form closed pores.

Effect of static pressure load on chloride ion

Figure 4 shows the chloride ion diffusion coefficients of C30 and C50 concrete at different stress level ratios under two mixing processes. In the stress loading process, the 28 d compressive strength of C30 and C50 concrete under ordinary mixing is 50.1 MPa and 57.6 MPa as the limit values. As can be seen from Figs. 3 (a) and 3 (b), as the stress ratio of concrete increases, the diffusion coefficient of chloride ion decreased first and then increased. The chloride diffusion coefficient of concrete using ordinary mixing reaches the minimum value at the stress level of about 30%. Concrete using vibrating mixing reaches the minimum value between 30% and 40% stress level. The main reason is that when the axial compressive load of concrete is low, the effect of axial compressive load on concrete is only elastic strain. At this time, the porosity of concrete decreases with the increase of stress level. When the axial compression load increases, the concrete gradually transits to a mixture of elastic deformation and plastic deformation. The interior of concrete will be gradually damaged and cracks will be formed, and the diffusion coefficient of chloride ions will increase. The reason why the stress ratio of vibration mixing concrete is larger is that vibration mixing can improve the compressive strength of concrete19,20. The plastic deformation bearing capacity of vibration mixing concrete will increase when the ultimate stress of ordinary mixing concrete is changed. For ordinary mixing and vibration mixing, the chloride ion diffusion coefficients under stress ratios of 0%, 15%, 30%, 40%, and 50% were compared between the two processes.

Chloride ion diffusion coefficient of concrete with two mixing processes at different stress level ratios. (a) Chloride ion diffusion coefficient of C30 concrete under different stress level ratio; (b) Chloride ion diffusion coefficient of C50 concrete under different stress level ratio.

As shown in Fig. 4 (a), the chloride diffusion coefficients of ordinary mixing C30 concrete under different stress levels were 3.16, 2.63, 2.12, 2.65, and 3.06 at 56 days, and 2.95, 2.66, 2.56, 2.97, and 3.23 at 90 days, respectively. Compared with ordinary mixing, the chloride ion diffusion coefficients of vibration mixing C30 concrete decreased by 26.1%, 22.3%, 25.9%, 18.5%, and 16.8% at 56 days, and by 19.6%, 13.8%, 9.3%, 20.5%, and 20.4% at 90 days, respectively. As can be seen from Fig. 4 (b), the chloride diffusion coefficients of ordinary mixing C50 concrete under different stress levels were 1.81, 1.47, 1.31, 1.65, and 1.87 at 56 days, and 1.65, 1.54, 1.57, 1.89, 2.02 at 90 days, respectively. The Chloride ion diffusion coefficient of C50 concrete using vibration mixing decreases by 18.2%, 14.3%, 11.5%, 17.5% and 20.8%, respectively. After 90 days, the durability of concrete decreases by 19.4%, 24%, 19.1%, 23.4% and 19.8%, which indicates that the durability of marine concrete is improved by vibration mixing. Vibration mixing improves the micropore structure of the concrete, the smaller porosity and average pore diameter hinder and slow down the movement of chloride ions, and the chloride ion permeability also becomes poor. Finally, the mechanical properties and durability of concrete are improved macroscopically.

The mix proportion of concrete and the addition of new materials can improve concrete strength and durability. Vibration mixing adds exciting force to traditional mixing. The mixing shaft also vibrates during mixing, which disperses cement and other particles, reduces agglomeration, and accelerates hydration reaction. By using vibration mixing, the compressive strength can be increased by 8 −10%. Under the same strength requirements, the amount of cement can be reduced. From a process perspective, its mixing can usually be completed within 90 s, which greatly saves mixing time, improves engineering efficiency, and saves resources compared to traditional mixing methods. Therefore, whether it is changing the mix ratio or adding other materials, vibration mixing can reflect its value from a mechanical point of view. However, further comparative experiments are needed to verify the comprehensive performance comparison between ordinary mixing and vibration mixing.

Conclusions

In this paper, the effects of vibration mixing on the pore characteristics and durability of marine concrete were compared and studied through experiments. The water absorption, pore characteristics, and chloride ion resistance of two mixing processes and two grades of concrete were tested. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1) Comparing the water absorption rates of C30 and C50 concrete under vibration mixing and ordinary mixing, the water absorption rates of the two types of concrete decreased by 30.8% and 28.9% respectively under vibration mixing, and the coefficient of variation decreased by 75.4% and 75.8% respectively. Moreover, the uniformity of concrete along the pouring height was better in the vibration mixing samples.

(2) Compared with ordinary mixing, the average pore size, porosity, and volume median pore size of vibration mixed C30 concrete decreased by 51.4%, 35.7%, and 63.2%, respectively; the average pore size, porosity, and volume median pore size of C50 concrete decreased by 53.3%, 40.4%, and 66.3%, respectively. This study shows that vibration mixing can effectively reduce the average pore size and porosity of concrete, improve the compactness of particles, and enhance the compactness of marine concrete.

(3) By comparing ordinary mixing and vibration mixing concrete, it was found that the chloride ion diffusion coefficient decreased first and then increased with the increase of stress ratio. Under the combined action of chloride salt and load, compared with ordinary mixing, the chloride ion diffusion coefficient of vibration mixed concrete soaked for 90 days under stress ratios of 0%, 15%, 30%, 40%, and 50% decreased by 19.4%, 24%, 19.1%, 25.9%, and 19.8%, respectively. Vibration mixing improved the durability of marine concrete.

In summary, through comparative tests between vibration mixing and ordinary mixing of concrete, it has been verified that vibration mixing has more advantages than ordinary mixing, and vibration mixing of marine concrete will be able to meet the quality requirements of marine engineering. Under the same conditions, it will be possible to improve efficiency and save costs. Further research in the future will focus on the influence of vibration mixing process parameters on concrete performance, as well as the application and long-term data monitoring of vibration mixing in marine concrete, providing new preparation methods for high-quality marine concrete.

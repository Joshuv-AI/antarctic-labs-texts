Lipschitz stability of least-squares problems regularized by functions with 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} -cone reducible conjugates
Ying Cui, Tim Hoheisel, Tran T. A. Nghia, Defeng Sun

Abstract
. In this paper, we study Lipschitz continuity of the solution mappings of regularized least-squares problems for which the convex regularizers have (Fenchel) conjugates that are 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} -cone reducible. Our approach, by using Robinson’s strong regularity on the dual problem, allows us to obtain new characterizations of Lipschitz stability that rely solely on first-order information, thus bypassing the need to explore second-order information (curvature) of the regularizer. We show that these solution mappings are automatically Lipschitz continuous around the points in question whenever they are locally single-valued. We leverage our findings to obtain new characterizations of full stability and tilt stability for a broader class of convex additive-composite problems.



Keywords.
Least-squares, Lipschitz stability, full stability, tilt stability, convex optimization, Lasso, variational analysis and nonsmooth optimization, second-order analysis, regularized problem.

(2020). 49J52, 49J53, 49K40, 90C25, 90C31

1.
Introduction

Stability (or sensitivity) analysis in optimization is the study of how the optimal solutions (or the optimal value) of an optimization problem varies as the problem-defining data (parameters) changes. This field has a rich and longstanding tradition anchored by the pioneering works of Kojima
[
17
,
18
]
and Robinson
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
, primarily focused on smooth, nonlinear programming in finite dimensions. The monograph by Bonnans and Shapiro
[
2
]
presents the state-of-the-art treatment for problems with smooth data, in particular, in infinite dimensions. A rich source of general theory for nonsmooth problems, based on graphical differentiation of set-valued maps, is provided in the monograph by Dontchev and Rockafellar
[
9
]
.

Recently, there has been renewed interest in stability analysis for the following nonsmooth, convex optimization problems which occur ubiquitously in machine learning and statistical estimations:

(1)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+g(x),

where
A:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
is a linear operator between two Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
,
b
is a vector in
\mathbb{Y}
,
\mu>0
is the tuning parameter, and
g:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a closed, proper, convex function (usually called a regularizer in machine learning). When the triple
(A,b,\mu)
are considered parameters, it is important to study stability of the solution mapping of problem (
1
) defined by

(2)

S(A,b,\mu):=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+g(x)\right\}.

Specifically, in this paper, we aim to determine the conditions under which the solution mapping (
2
) is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous when there are small perturbations in the initial data
(A,b,\mu)
of problem (
1
).

To the best of our knowledge, there are currently two main approaches for studying the stability of this solution mapping. The first one employs the toolkit of modern variational analysis à la Dontchev and Rockafellar
[
9
]
; see also the standard texts by Mordukhovich
[
20
]
, or Rockafellar and Wets
[
27
]
. Some recent contributions along these lines were made by Berk et al.
[
3
,
4
]
, Gfrerer and Outrata
[
14
]
, Hu et al.
[
15
]
(which strengthens the qualitative but not the quantitative results of Berk et al.), Meng et al.
[
22
]
, Nghia
[
24
]
, and Vaiter et al.
[
32
]
. When the regularizer
g
is the
\ell_{1}
norm, the problem (
1
) is known as the Lasso (Least Absolute Shrinkage and
Selection Operator) problem. Berk et al.
[
3
]
provide a sufficient condition
[
3
, Assumption 4.3]
at which the solution mapping
S
is single-valued, Lipschitz continuous and directionally differentiable around a fixed triple
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu}).
This condition appeared in
[
23
,
34
]
as a sufficient condition for the uniqueness of a given solution to the Lasso problem. The approach in
[
3
]
relies on the well-known Mordukhovich criterion
[
20
, Theorem 4.18]
for
metric regularity
and available second-order information of the
\ell_{1}
norm. On the other hand, in the recent paper
[
24
]
, Nghia proposed a different approach via Robinson’s
generalized implicit function theorem

[
28
]
and
tilt stability

[
25
]
for more general functions
g
, including popular regularizers such as the
\ell_{1}
norm, the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm, and the nuclear norm. It is revealed in
[
24
, Theorem 3.7]
that the aforementioned sufficient condition in
[
3
]
is also necessary for the Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping (
2
) for the Lasso problem. Some recent papers
[
15
,
22
]
obtain impressive results about Lipschitz continuity of the set-valued solution mapping (
2
) with fixed operator
A

relative
to its domain when
g
is the
\ell_{1}
norm. Their Lipschitz stability is different from ours in this paper and their approaches strongly rely on the polyhedral structure of the
\ell_{1}
norm and its subdifferential mapping. When regularizer
g
is
partly smooth
, Vaiter et al
[
32
, Theorem 1]
proves a strong result that
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\mu})
is continuously differentiable around
\bar{b}
when
\bar{x}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
is a
strong minimizer
of the corresponding problem, but they have to assume that
\bar{b}
does not belong to a
transition space
. The latter condition seems to be involved and recently
[
24
, Example 3.14]
provides a simple example at which the solution mapping
S
is Lipschitz continuous, but it is not continuously differentiable.

The second approach, spearheaded by Bolte et al.
[
6
]
, is to rewrite the optimality conditions as a (locally) Lipschitz equation, say, by using proximal operators, and to employ the machinery of implicit differentiation with
conservative Jacobians

[
5
]
. The conservative Jacobians here serve as a versatile tool which, among other things, bypasses the failure of the chain rule for Clarke Jacobians. The ideas mentioned here can be extended to (maximally) monotone inclusions (which are not necessarily optimality conditions of a convex optimization problem)
[
7
]
.

In this paper, we mainly study Lipschitz stability of solution mapping (
2
) with a fresh approach via
strong regularity

[
28
]
via the dual problem. Strong regularity was introduced by Robinson in the landmark paper
[
28
]
for
generalized equations
with applications to
nonlinear programming
to study Lipschitz stability of the
Lagrange system
. Robinson’s strong regularity can be characterized by the well-known
Linear Independence Constraint Qualification
and the
Strong Second-Order Sufficient Condition
for nonlinear programming; see, e.g.,
[
28
,
2
,
16
,
9
]
for more historic discussions and its immense applications to optimization theory and algorithms. In
[
21
, Theorem 5.6]
, Mordukhovich, Nghia and Rockafellar give full characterizations of strong regularity for more general parametric constrained optimization problems in the following setting:

(3)

\min_{u\in\mathbb{U}}\quad\varphi(u,p)\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad G(u,p)\in\Theta,

with Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V},\mathbb{P}
(
\mathbb{P}
being the parameter space), a closed and convex set
\Theta\subset\mathbb{V}
, and twice continuously differentiable cost function
\varphi:\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}
and constrained mapping
G:\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{V}
. It is worth noting here that
[
21
]
crucially assumes that the constrained set
\Theta
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-
reducible
in the sense of
[
2
, Definition 3.135]
that allows them to cover some previous works on the strong regularity for non-polyhedral settings such as the
second-order cone programming

[
1
]
and the
semidefinite programming

[
33
]
. Their characterizations are based on the so-called
Partial Constraint Nondegeneracy
and the
Generalized Strong Second-Order Sufficient Condition
that will be recalled in our Subsection 2.3.

A simple, but important observation of our paper is that the solution mapping (
2
) is, indeed, the mapping of (partial) Lagrange multipliers of the following constrained problem

(4)

\quad\min_{(y,t)\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}}\quad\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\left\langle b,\,y\right\rangle+t\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad(A^{*}y,t)\in\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*},

where
\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}
stands for the epigraph of the Fenchel conjugate function of
g
.
This problem is in the format of (
3
) and equivalent to the Fenchel-Rockafellar dual of problem (
1
)

\quad\min_{y\in\mathbb{Y}}\quad\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\left\langle b,\,y\right\rangle+g^{*}(A^{*}y).

This unveils the connection between the strong regularity of problem (
4
) and the Lipschitz stability of solution mapping (
2
) of problem (
1
). As mentioned above, in order to apply
[
21
, Theorem 5.6]
, we need to suppose that the constrained set of problem (
4
), i.e, the epigraph of the conjugate function
g^{*}
, is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-
cone reducible
. This assumption is not restrictive: it automatically holds for many important regularizers in optimization such as the
\ell_{1}
norm, the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm, the nuclear norm, and many more spectral functions
[
10
]
and support functions; see also our Remark
3.6
.

While applying the theory of strong regularity in
[
21
]
to the dual problem (
4
), we observe that the Generalized Second-Order Sufficient Condition holds automatically and the Partial Constraint Nondegeneracy can be simplified with first-order information on the regularizer
g
. This significantly distinguishes our paper from
[
3
,
24
,
32
]
that directly work on the primal problem and needs more advanced
second-order
computation on the regularizer. In particular, we show that the solution mapping
S
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around a fixed triple
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
of initial data for
{\bar{x}}\in S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
if and only if

\ker\bar{A}\cap{\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})=\{0\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\bar{z}:=-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{A}^{*}(\bar{A}\bar{x}-\bar{b}),

where
{\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
denotes the
parallel subspace
of the subdifferential set
\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
. This characterization recovers some similar results obtained in
[
3
,
24
]
for special cases of regularizers such as the
\ell_{1}
norm, the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm, and the nuclear norm. Although our technique relies on second-order variation analysis, our condition above does not need any second-order information of
g
.

Another key aspect of our paper studies characterizations of tilt stability
[
25
]
and full stability
[
19
]
, both of which also relate to Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping of the following optimization problem

(5)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad f(x,p)+g(x),

where
f:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice continuously differentiable function that is convex with respect to
x\in\mathbb{X}
. This problem is more general than (
1
) at which the basic parameter
p
is considered as the triple parameter
(A,b,\mu)
. In our framework, full stability, as introduced by Poliquin, Levy, and Rockafellar
[
19
]
, mainly analyzes Lipschitz continuity of the (local) optimal solution mapping of

(6)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad f(x,p)+g(x)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle,

with respect to both basic parameter
p\in\mathbb{P}
and tilt (linear) parameter
v\in\mathbb{X}
; while tilt stability is a particular case when the basic perturbation
p
is fixed. Both tilt stability and full stability are characterized by using different advanced nonsmooth second-order structures that may be difficult to compute
[
14
,
19
,
21
,
24
,
25
]
. In Section 4, by using our results on Lipschitz stability, we achieve a new pointwise characterization for both full stability and tilt stability of problem (
5
). Unlike other characterizations of full stability and tilt stability, our pointwise condition uses first-order information of the function
g
.
Specifically, we show that
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
is a full (or tilt) stable optimal solution of problem (
5
) for parameter
{\bar{p}}\in\mathbb{P}
if and only if
-\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},{\bar{p}})\in\partial g(\bar{x})
and

\ker\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},{\bar{p}})\cap{\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(-\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},{\bar{p}}))=\{0\};

see our Theorem
4.4
for further details. This condition still needs second-order information of the function
f
, but is easy to compute, as
f
is twice differentiable. The crucial part of this condition is that we do not need to calculate any second-order structures for the nonsmooth function
g
. Consequently, it is also a sufficient condition for Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping of problem (
5
). However, unlike the regularized least-squares problem (
1
), this condition is not necessary for the aforementioned Lipschitz stability of problem (
5
).

\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
is the space of all linear operators from the Euclidean space
\mathbb{X}
to the Euclidean space
\mathbb{Y}
. For
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
, we denote its
adjoint
by
A^{*}
, its
kernel
(or
null space
) by
\ker A
and its
range
by
\mathrm{rge}\,A
.
For a Fréchet differentiable map
G:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
, we write
{\mathcal{J}}G(x)\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{X}
as the Jacobian matrix of

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Euclidean space, i.e., a finite-dimensional real vector space with inner product denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\right\rangle
.
The (Euclidean) norm on
\mathbb{X}
induced by the ambient inner product is denoted by
\|\cdot\|
. The closed ball in
\mathbb{X}
with center
x\in\mathbb{X}
and radius
r>0
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
.

2.1.
Tools from convex analysis

For a function
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, its
epigraph
is the set given by
\mathrm{epi}\,f:=\left\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\,\left|\;f(x)\leq\alpha\right.\right\}
while its
domain
is the set
\mathrm{dom}\,f:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;f(x)<+\infty\right.\right\}
. We call
f

proper
if
\mathrm{dom}\,f
is nonempty. We say that
f
is
convex
if
\mathrm{epi}\,f
is convex, and we call it
closed
or
lower semicontinuous (lsc)
if
\mathrm{epi}\,f
is closed. The
(Fenchel) conjugate
of a proper
f
is the function
f^{*}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, given by

f^{*}(y)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{X}}\{\left\langle y,\,x\right\rangle-f(x)\}.

Its
subdifferential
at
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
is the set
\partial f(\bar{x})=\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;f(\bar{x})+\left\langle y,\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq f(x)\;\forall x\in\mathbb{X}\right.\right\}
. When
f
is closed, proper, convex, throughout the paper we use the following relation frequently:

v\in\partial f(x)\iff x\in\partial f^{*}(v).

For a convex set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{X}
,
its
relative interior
is given by

(7)

\mathrm{ri}\,\Omega:=\left\{x\in\Omega\,\left|\;\exists\,\epsilon>0:\;\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}(x)\cap{\rm aff}\,\Omega\subset\Omega\right.\right\},

where
{\rm aff}\,\Omega
is the
affine hull
of
\Omega
. The
conical hull
of
\Omega
is
\mathrm{cone}\;\Omega:=\mathbb{R}_{+}\Omega
, the smallest (convex) cone containing
\Omega
. The
span
of
\Omega
is denoted
\mathrm{span}\,\Omega
. The
parallel subspace
of
\Omega
is defined, for any
x\in\Omega
, by

(8)

\mathrm{par}\,\Omega:={\rm span}\,(\Omega-x).

Note that
\mathrm{par}\,\Omega={\rm aff}\,\Omega-{\rm aff}\,\Omega={\rm aff}\,\Omega-x
for any
x\in\Omega
. Thus for any
x\in\mathrm{ri}\,\Omega
, we have

(9)

\mathrm{par}\,\Omega=\mathrm{cone}\,(\Omega-x).

The
lineality space
of
\Omega
, denoted by
{\rm lin}\,\Omega
, is the largest subspace in
\Omega.
Moreover, the
horizon cone
of
\Omega
is

(10)

\Omega^{\infty}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;\exists\,\{x_{k}\in\Omega\},\{t_{k}\}\downarrow 0:\;t_{k}x_{k}\to v\right.\right\},

which is a closed cone, and convex as
\Omega
is. In particular, for a convex function
f
and
\bar{y}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, we have

(11)

\partial f(\bar{x})^{\infty}\subset\mathrm{cone}(\partial f(\bar{x})-\bar{y}),

cf.
[
27
, Theorem 3.6]
. The
normal cone
of
\Omega
at
{\bar{x}}
is

(12)

N_{\Omega}({\bar{x}})=\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;\left\langle y,\,x-{\bar{x}}\right\rangle\leq 0\;\forall x\in\Omega\right.\right\},

which is the subdifferential of the (convex)
indicator function

\delta_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
at
{\bar{x}}
, which is defined by
\delta_{\Omega}(x)=0
if
x\in\Omega
and
\delta_{\Omega}(x)=+\infty
otherwise.
The following result, which is useful to our study, links many of the above mentioned objects.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a closed, proper, convex function and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
such that
\partial f(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
. Then

\mathbb{X}\times\{0\}\cap\mathrm{span}\,N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))=\mathrm{par}\,\partial f(\bar{x})\times\{0\}.

Proof.

First, observe by
[
27
, Theorem 8.9]
that

(13)

N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))=\left\{\sigma(v,-1)\,\left|\;v\in\partial f(\bar{x}),\;\sigma>0\right.\right\}\bigcup\partial f(\bar{x})^{\infty}\times\{0\}.

Now, let
(v,0)\in\mathbb{X}\times\{0\}\cap\mathrm{span}\,N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))
. In view of (
13
), we find scalars
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s}\in\mathbb{R}
, and vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
,
w_{1},\dots,w_{s}\in\partial f(\bar{v})^{\infty}
such that

(v,0)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}(v_{i},-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}(w_{j},0).

It follows that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}=0
, i.e.,
\lambda_{r}=-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{i}
.
With the previous identity, we thus obtain

v=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{i}(v_{i}-v_{r})+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}w_{j}\subset\mathrm{par}\,\partial f(\bar{x})+\mathrm{span}\,\partial f(\bar{x})^{\infty}.

For any
\bar{y}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, note from (
11
) that

\mathrm{span}\,\partial f(\bar{x})^{\infty}\subset\mathrm{span}\,(\partial f(\bar{x})-\bar{y})=\mathrm{par}\,\partial f(\bar{x}).

Therefore,
v\in\mathrm{par}\,\partial f(\bar{x})
, which shows the desired inclusion.

Conversely, take
(v,0)\in\mathrm{par}\,\partial f(\bar{x})\times\{0\}
. Then there exist
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r-1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

v=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{i}(v_{i}-v_{r}).

Setting
\lambda_{r}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{i}
, in view of (
13
), we find that

(v,0)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}(v_{i},-1)\in\mathrm{span}\,N_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(\bar{x},f(\bar{x})).

This concludes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Tools from set-valued analysis

Let
S\subset\mathbb{X}
and
{\bar{x}}\in S
. We write
x_{k}\mathrel{{\mathop{\to}\limits}}\bar{x}
if
\{x_{k}\}\to\bar{x}
and
x_{k}\in S
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The
regular normal cone
to
S
at
\bar{x}
is the (convex) cone defined by

\hat{N}_{S}(\bar{x}):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;\limsup_{x\mathrel{{\mathop{\to}\limits}}\bar{x}}\frac{\left\langle y,\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\right.\right\}.

It is often expedient to realize that
y\in\hat{N}_{S}(\bar{x})
if and only if

(14)

\forall\,\varepsilon>0\;\exists\,\delta>0\;\forall\,x\in B_{\delta}(\bar{x})\cap S:\;\left\langle y,\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq\varepsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|.

The
limiting normal cone

[
20
,
27
]
to
S
at
\bar{x}\in S
is defined by

(15)

N_{S}({\bar{x}}):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;\exists\,\{x_{k}\}\mathrel{{\mathop{\to}\limits}}\bar{x},\;\{y_{k}\in\hat{N}_{S}(x_{k})\}:\;y_{k}\to y\right.\right\},

i.e., it is the
outer limit
of the regular normal cone.
When
S
is convex, both the regular and the limiting normal cone reduce to the object in (
12
).
The
tangent cone
to
S
at
{\bar{x}}
is defined by

(16)

T_{S}({\bar{x}}):=\left\{w\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;\exists\,\{t_{k}\}\downarrow 0,\{w_{k}\}\to w:{\bar{x}}+t_{k}w_{k}\in S\right.\right\}.

Let us recall next the limiting and singular subdifferentials of an extended real-valued (possibly nonconvex) function
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
that is lower semicontinuous at some
{\bar{x}}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
; see, e.g.,
[
20
, Definition 1.77]
. The
limiting subdifferential
of
f
at
{\bar{x}}
is defined by

(17)

\partial f({\bar{x}}):=\{y\in\mathbb{X}|\;(y,-1)\in N_{{\rm epi}\,f}({\bar{x}},f({\bar{x}}))\},

which returns the subdifferential of convex analysis defined in Section
2.1
when
f
is a convex function.
Moreover, the
singular subdifferential
of
f
at
{\bar{x}}
is defined by

(18)

\partial^{\infty}f({\bar{x}}):=\{y\in\mathbb{X}|\;(y,0)\in N_{{\rm epi}\,f}({\bar{x}},f({\bar{x}}))\},

which is the horizon cone of
\partial f({\bar{x}})
when
f
is convex, cf.
[
27
, Proposition 8.12]
.

An important tool from set-valued differentiation to our study is the
(limiting) coderivative

[
20
,
27
]
. Let
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
be a set-valued mapping between two Euclidean spaces. The
graph
of
F
is defined by

\mathrm{gph}\,F:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}\,\left|\;y\in F(x)\right.\right\}.

The (limiting) coderivative of
F
at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{y}}\in F({\bar{x}})
is the set-valued mapping
D^{*}F({\bar{x}}|\,{\bar{y}}):Y\rightrightarrows X
defined by

(19)

D^{*}F({\bar{x}}|\,{\bar{y}})(w):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{X}\,\left|\;(z,-w)\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\right.\right\}.

The set-valued mapping
F
is said to have a
single-valued (and Lipschitz continuous) localization at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{y}}
(cf.
[
2
,
9
,
33
,
21
,
28
]
) if there exist open neighborhoods
U
of
{\bar{x}}
and
V
of
{\bar{y}}
such that the
localization

s:U\to V
of
F
with
\mathrm{gph}\,s=\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap(U\times V)
is single-valued (and Lipschitz continuous).

We are interested in single-valued locally Lipschitz continuous functions. To make the transition from single-valued localizations to actual single-valuedness for convex-valued mapping, the following result is useful.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
be a set-valued mapping, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Suppose that the set
F(x)
is convex when
x
is around
{\bar{x}}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

F
has a single-valued localization at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{y}}
;

(ii)

F
is single-valued around
{\bar{x}}
.

Proof.

[(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)]: If there exist neighborhoods
U
of
{\bar{x}}
and
V
of
{\bar{y}}
such that the
localization

s:U\to V
of
F
with
\mathrm{gph}\,s=\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap(U\times V)
is single-valued, we may suppose w.l.o.g. that
F(x)
is convex for any
x\in U
(otherwise shrink the neighborhood). Pick any
y\in F(x)
. As
F(x)
is convex and
s(x)\in F(x)
, we find some
\lambda\in(0,1)
sufficiently small such that
s(x)+\lambda(y-s(x))\in F(x)\cap V
, which implies that
s(x)+\lambda(y-s(x))=s(x)
, i.e.,
y=s(x)
. Hence
F(x)=s(x)
for
x\in U
, which means
F
is single-valued around
{\bar{x}}
. The opposite implication is straightforward.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
h:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be a proper function such that
h(\cdot,p)
is convex for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Define
S:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{X}
by

S(p):=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}h(x,p).

Let
(\bar{p},{\bar{x}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
. Then
S
has a single-valued (Lipschitz) localization at
\bar{p}
for
\bar{x}
if and only if
S
is single-valued (and locally Lipschitz) around
\bar{x}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
Lemma

2.2
, since
S
is convex-valued.
∎

2.3.
Tools from parametric optimization

For Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{U}
,
\mathbb{V}
, and
\mathbb{P}
, consider the following parametric optimization problem

(20)

{\rm OPT}(p):\quad\min_{u\in\mathbb{U}}\quad\varphi(u,p)\quad\mbox{s.t.}\quad G(u,p)\in\Theta,

where
\Theta\subset\mathbb{V}
is a closed and convex set, and
\varphi:\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}
and
G:\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{V}
are twice continuously differentiable functions.
Robinson’s constraint qualification
is said to hold for OPT(
\bar{p}
) at some
{\bar{u}}\in\mathbb{U}
with
G({\bar{u}},{\bar{p}})\in\Theta
(i.e.,
\bar{u}
is feasible for OPT(
\bar{p}
)) if

(21)

0\in{\rm int}\,(G({\bar{u}},{\bar{p}})+\mathrm{rge}\,{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},{\bar{p}})-\Theta).

The
Lagrangian
of problem OPT(
p
) is
\mathscr{L}:\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(22)

\mathscr{L}(u,p,\lambda):=\varphi(u,p)+\left\langle\lambda,\,G(u,p)\right\rangle\quad\mbox{for}\quad(u,p,\lambda)\in\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{V}.

A feasible point
u\in\mathbb{U}
is called a
stationary
point of OPT(
p
) if there exists a
Lagrange multiplier

\lambda\in\mathbb{V}
satisfying

(23)

0=\nabla_{u}\mathscr{L}(u,p,\lambda)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda\in N_{\Theta}(G(u,p)).

We define

\Lambda(u,p):=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{V}\,\left|\;0=\nabla_{u}\mathscr{L}(u,p,\lambda),\;\lambda\in N_{\Theta}(G(u,p))\right.\right\}
, the set of all Lagrange multipliers at
(u,p)
.

Observe that the system (
23
) can be written as the
generalized equation

[
28
]
:

(24)

{\rm GE}(p):\quad 0\in\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{u}\varphi(u,p)+{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G(u,p)^{*}\lambda\\
-G(u,p)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
N_{\Theta}^{-1}(\lambda)\end{pmatrix}.

Let
H:\mathbb{P}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V}
be its
solution mapping
, i.e.,

H(p)=\left\{(u,\lambda)\,\left|\;(u,p,\lambda)\;\text{satisfies\;\eqref{eq:GE}}\right.\right\}.

Given
\bar{p}\in\mathbb{P}
and
(\bar{u},\bar{\lambda})\in H(\bar{p})
, it is an immediate question under which conditions
H
has a single-valued and Lipschitz continuous localization at
{\bar{p}}
for
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})
(in the sense of the definition at the end of the previous section). To answer this question, Robinson
[
28
]
came up with an original idea of considering the
linearized generalized equation
of (
24
) below:

(25)

\delta\in\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{2}_{uu}\mathscr{L}({\bar{u}},\bar{p},\bar{\lambda})(u-{\bar{u}})+{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})^{*}(\lambda-\bar{\lambda})\\
-{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})(u-{\bar{u}})\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
N_{\Theta}^{-1}(\lambda)\end{pmatrix}

with
\delta\in\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V}
. Let us set the solution mapping of this generalized equation by
K:\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V}
with
K(\delta)=\{(u,\lambda)|\;(u,\lambda,\delta)\;\text{satisfies\;\eqref{eq:LGE}}\}
for any
\delta\in\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V}
. If
K
has a single-valued and Lipschitz continuous localization around
\bar{\delta}=0
for
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})
, then
H
also has a single-valued and Lipschitz continuous localization around
{\bar{p}}
for
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})
. This is usually referred to as Robinson’s implicit function theorem; see the original result by Robinson
[
28
, Theorem 2.1]
, and its extension
[
9
, Theorem 2B.5]
that can be applied directly to our above framework. Lipschitz continuity of the solution mapping of system (
25
) is known as
Robinson’s strong regularity
defined precisely next.

Definition 2.4

(Robinson’s strong regularity)
.

Suppose that
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})
is a solution of the generalized equation
{\rm GE}(\bar{p})
in (
24
), i.e.,
{\bar{u}}
is a stationary point of problem
{\rm OPT}(\bar{p})
in (
20
) and
\bar{\lambda}
is a corresponding Lagrange multiplier from (
23
).
We say that the generalized equation
{\rm GE}(\bar{p})
is strongly regular at
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})
if the solution mapping
K
of the linearized generalized equation (
25
)
has a single-valued and Lipschitz continuous localization around
0\in\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V}
for
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})\in\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V}
.

Robinson’s strong regularity was introduced in
[
28
]
for generalized equations with applications to nonlinear programming at which
\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}
, and
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}_{-}\times\{0_{m_{2}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}
. Particularly, Robinson showed in
[
28
, Theorem 4.1]
that the well-known
Linear Independence Constraint Qualification
(LICQ) of
{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})
and the so-called
Strong Second-Order Sufficient Condition
(SSOSC) that is also proposed by Robinson are sufficient for strong regularity of the corresponding generalized equation (
24
) at
(\bar{u},\bar{\lambda})
. If additionally
\bar{u}
is an optimal solution of problem
{\rm OPT}(\bar{p})
in (
20
), these two conditions are also necessary for strong regularity; see, e.g.,
[
16
, Theorem 8.10]
and also
[
2
,
8
,
9
]
for further historic discussions about strong regularity. For semidefinite programming (SDP), Robinson’s strong regularity is characterized via different conditions including the so-called
constraint nondegeneracy
and the
SDP-Strong Second-Order Sufficient Condition
by Sun
[
33
]
. For more general constrained programming as in (
20
), it is shown by Mordukhovich, Nghia, and Rockafellar in
[
21
]
that Robinson’s strong regularity is equivalent to the
Partial Constraint Nondegeneracy
, which originated from
[
29
]
and the
generalized SSOSC
recalled later, provided that the set
\Theta
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible
; see, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 3.4.4]
. Let us recall these definitions here.

Definition 2.5

(Partial Constraint Nondegeneracy)
.

Let
\bar{p}\in\mathbb{P}
and let
\bar{u}
be feasible for
{\rm OPT}(\bar{p})
, i.e.,
G(\bar{u},\bar{p})\in\Theta
.
We say that the point
\bar{u}
is partially nondegenerate for
G
with respect to
\Theta
if

(26)

\mathrm{rge}\,{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})+\mathrm{lin}\,T_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p}))=\mathbb{V},

where
\mathrm{lin}\,T_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p}))
is the lineality space of
T_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p}))
, the largest subspace in the tangent cone
T_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p}))
.

Remark 2.6

(Constraint nondegeneracy)
.

(a) We note that for the case of nonlinear programming when
\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}
, and
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}_{-}\times\{0_{m_{2}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}
, and
G(\bar{x})\in\Theta
, the constraint nondegeneracy at
\bar{u}
holds if and only if the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ) holds at
\bar{u}
.

(b) A dual formulation of (
26
) reads

(27)

\ker{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})^{*}\bigcap\mathrm{span}\,N_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p}))=\{0\}.

Recall that a cone
C\subset\mathbb{X}
is called
pointed
if
C\cap(-C)=\{0\}
.

Definition 2.7

(
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible sets and functions)
.

The set
\Theta\subset\mathbb{V}
is said to be
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
{\bar{v}}
if there exist a neighborhood
V
of
{\bar{v}}
, a pointed, closed, convex cone
C
in some Euclidean space
\mathbb{W}
, and a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth mapping
h:V\to\mathbb{W}
such that
h({\bar{v}})=0
,
{\mathcal{J}}h({\bar{v}})\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W})
is surjective, and that
\Theta\cap V=\{v\in V|\;h(v)\in C\}
.

A closed, proper, convex function
f:\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is called
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
{\bar{u}}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
if its epigraph
\mathrm{epi}\,f
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
({\bar{u}},f({\bar{u}}))
. We call the function
f
to be
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible, if it is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at any point on its domain.

We point out that any set
\Theta
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at any
\bar{v}\in{\rm int}\,\Theta
, as we may choose a neighborhood
V\subset\Theta
of
{\bar{v}}
,
\mathbb{W}=\{0\}
,
C=\{0\}
, and
h(v)\equiv 0
in the above definition.
Many important convex sets in optimization are
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible including (convex) polyhedra
[
2
, Example 3.139]
, the positive semidefinite cone
[
2
, Example 3.140]
, or the second-order (or Lorentz) cone
[
1
, Lemma 15]
. The Cartesian product of many
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible convex sets is also
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible; see
[
31
, Proposition 3.1]
. Moreover, it is shown in
[
10
, Proposition 10]
that many
spectral functions
including the nuclear norm and the spectral norm are also
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible.

The following result
[
21
, Theorem 5.6]
provides a full characterization of Robinson’s strong regularity for
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible programming.

Theorem 2.8

(Characterization of Robinson’s strong regularity)
.

Let
{\bar{u}}\in\mathbb{U}
be a stationary point of problem
{\rm OPT}(\bar{p})
in (
20
) with
\bar{\lambda}\in\Lambda({\bar{u}},\bar{p})
. Suppose that the set
\Theta
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
G({\bar{u}},{\bar{p}})
and that Robinson’s constraint qualification (
21
) holds at
\bar{u}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The generalized equation (
24
) is strongly regular at
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})
and
{\bar{u}}
is a local minimizer of problem
{\rm OPT}(\bar{p})
;

(ii)

{\bar{u}}
is partially nondegenerate for
G
with respect to
\Theta
and the following so-called generalized strong second-order sufficient condition (GSSOSC) holds

(28)

\left\langle w,\,\nabla^{2}_{uu}\mathscr{L}({\bar{u}},\bar{p},\bar{\lambda})w\right\rangle+\inf\Big\{\left\langle z,\,{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w\right\rangle\big|\;z\in D^{*}N_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},{\bar{p}})|\,\bar{\lambda})\big({\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w\big)\Big\}>0

for any
w\neq 0
.

In the framework of nonlinear programming, the GSSOSC is exactly the classical SSOSC of Robinson
[
28
]
. For the case
\Theta=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
, the cone of of positive semi-definite matrices, it also recovers the SDP-SSOSC
[
33
]
; see
[
21
]
for further details and discussions. We point out that verifying GSSOSC (
28
) is not trivial due to the lack of explicit calculus for the coderivative of the normal cone mapping. In Section
3
, however, we will show that GSSOSC holds automatically for the problems in the center of our study.

3.
Lipschitz stability of least-squares problems with
C^{2}
-cone reducible dual regularizes

In this section, we consider the following parametric optimization problem

(29)

{\rm P}(A,b,\mu):\quad\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+g(x),

where
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}
are two Euclidean spaces,
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
,
b\in\mathbb{Y}
,
\mu>0
are treated as parameters and
g:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a closed, proper, convex function. Define the solution mapping
S:\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\to\mathbb{X}
of problem (
29
) by

(30)

S(A,b,\mu):=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+g(x)\right\}.

Our main goal in this section is to study sufficient and necessary conditions for this solution mapping to be (locally) Lipschitz around some fixed triple
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
.
Given
v\in\mathbb{X}
, the
tilt-perturbed
problem corresponding to P
(A,b,\mu)
reads

(31)

Q(A,b,\mu,v):\quad\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\frac{1}{2\mu}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+g(x)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle.

Its solution mapping
\widehat{S}:\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\times\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}
is defined by

(32)

\widehat{S}(A,b,\mu,v)=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+g(x)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle\right\}.

Obviously,
Q(A,b,\mu,0)
and
P(A,b,\mu)
are the same problem, and thus
\widehat{S}(A,b,\mu,0)=S(A,b,\mu)
. Adding the linear perturbation
\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle
in the problem will be useful for Section
4
where we leverage our analysis here for more general problems.

Proposition 3.1

(Primal-dual optimality)
.

The (Fenchel-Rockafellar) dual of problem (
31
) reads

(33)

D(A,b,\mu,v):\quad\min_{y\in\mathbb{Y}}\quad\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\left\langle b,\,y\right\rangle+g^{*}(A^{*}y+v).

For
(A,b,\mu,v)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\times\mathbb{X}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

x
solves
Q(A,b,\mu,v)
and
y
solves
D(A,b,\mu,v)
;

(ii)

A^{*}y+v\in\partial g(x)
and
-\mu y=Ax-b
;

(iii)

x\in\partial g^{*}(A^{*}y+v)
and
-\mu y=Ax-b
.

Proof.

We apply the duality scheme from
[
27
, Example 11.41]
with
h=\frac{1}{\mu}\|\cdot\|^{2},k=g
and
c=-v
. The only thing we have to ensure is that primal and dual optimal value are equal which is guaranteed as
\mathrm{dom}\,h=\mathbb{Y}
, cf.
[
27
, Theorem 11.39]
.
∎

Note that an equivalent formulation of the dual problem (
33
) reads

(34)

\quad D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,v):\quad\min_{(y,t)\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}}\quad\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\left\langle b,\,y\right\rangle+t\quad\mbox{s.t.}\quad(A^{*}y+v,t)\in\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}.

Although the parameter
\mu
is chosen to be positive for problem (
29
), problem (
34
) is well-defined for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. We will apply the theory laid out for
the parametric problem OPT
(p)
in (
20
) to problem
D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,v)
above with the spaces
\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{P}=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X}
, the parameter
p=(A,b,\mu,v)\in\mathbb{P}
, the variable
u=(y,t)\in\mathbb{U}
, the convex set
\Theta:=\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}\subset\mathbb{V}
, and the functions

(35)

\varphi(u,p):=\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\left\langle b,\,y\right\rangle+t\quad\mbox{and}\quad G(u,p):=(A^{*}y+v,t).

To this end, we first give an appropriate expression for the partial constraint nondegeneracy of
D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,0)
.

Lemma 3.2

(Partial constraint nondegeneracy for dual problem)
.

Let
{\bar{u}}=(\bar{y},\bar{t})
be a solution of
D^{\prime}(\bar{p})
with fixed
{\bar{p}}=(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu},0)\in\mathbb{P}
and
\bar{\mu}>0
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The partial constraint nondegeneracy holds at
(\bar{y},\bar{t})
for
D^{\prime}(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu},0)
;

(ii)

\ker\bar{A}\cap{\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{A}^{*}\bar{y})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Set
\bar{z}:=\bar{A}^{*}\bar{y}
. In view of
Remark

2.6
(b) and with the functions in (
35
), we find that constraint nondegeneracy holds for
D^{\prime}({\bar{p}})
at
(\bar{y},\bar{t})
if and only if

(36)

\{0\}=\ker\bar{A}\times\{0\}\bigcap\mathrm{span}\,N_{\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}}(\bar{z},\bar{t}).

Now, note that, by optimality, we necessarily have
\bar{t}=g^{*}(\bar{z})
.
From
Proposition

2.1
, we know that

\mathbb{X}\times\{0\}\bigcap\mathrm{span}\,N_{\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}}(\bar{z},\bar{t})=\mathrm{par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})\times\{0\}.

This yields the desired equivalence.
∎

The Lagrangian of
D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,v)
in the sense of (
22
) reads

(37)

\mathscr{L}(u,p,\lambda)=\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\left\langle b,\,y\right\rangle+t+\left\langle\lambda_{1},\,A^{*}y+v\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}t

with
u=(y,t)\in\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}
,
p=(A,b,\mu,v)\in\mathbb{P}
, and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Consequently, stationarity conditions for
D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,v)
in the sense of (
23
) read

(38)

0=\mu y-b+A\lambda_{1},\quad 0=1+\lambda_{2},\quad(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}}(A^{*}y+v,t),

which certainly tells us that
\lambda_{2}=-1
.
Combining (
38
) and
Proposition

3.1
yields the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
(A,b,\mu,v)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\times\mathbb{X}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

x
solves
Q(A,b,\mu,v)
and
y
solves
D(A,b,\mu,v)
;

(ii)

(y,g^{*}(A^{*}y+v))
solves
D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,v)
and
(x,-1)
is a corresponding Lagrange multiplier of problem
D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,v)
.

Proof.

The proof follows from combining
Proposition

3.1
and the stationarity conditions in (
38
) for
D^{\prime}(A,b,\mu,v)
. Here, it is worth noting that
(x,-1)\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}}(A^{*}y+v,g^{*}(A^{*}y+v))
if and only if
x\in\partial g^{*}(A^{*}y+v)
by (
13
).
∎

To prove Lipschitz stability of the solution map
\widehat{S}
around
\bar{p}=(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu},\bar{v})
,
in view of Proposition
3.3
, it suffices to show that the solution mapping of the stationarity system (
38
) is locally Lipschitz (in the Lagrange multipliers). By the discussion before Definition
2.4
, the latter occurs when Robinson’s strong regularity holds at
({\bar{u}},\bar{\lambda})
for the dual problem
D^{\prime}({\bar{p}})
. By Theorem
2.8
, we need that both the partial constraint nondegeneracy (
26
) and the generalized strong second-order sufficient condition (
28
) are satisfied for the system (
38
). First we show that the generalized SSOSC (
38
) is automatic for the dual problem
D^{\prime}({\bar{p}})
.

Proposition 3.4

(Validity of Generalized SSOSC)
.

Let
{\bar{u}}=(\bar{y},\bar{t})
be a solution of
D^{\prime}(\bar{p})
with
\bar{p}=(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu},0)
and
\bar{\mu}>0
. Suppose that
\bar{\lambda}=(\bar{\lambda}_{1},-1)
is the corresponding Lagrange multiplier at
(\bar{y},\bar{t})
satisfying (
38
). Then the Generalized SSOSC (
28
) for the dual problem
D^{\prime}(\bar{p})
holds in the sense that

(39)

\left\langle w,\,\nabla^{2}_{uu}\mathscr{L}({\bar{u}},\bar{p},\bar{\lambda})w\right\rangle+\inf\left\{\left\langle z,\,{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w\right\rangle|\;z\in D^{*}N_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p}),\bar{\lambda})({\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w)\right\}>0

for any
w\in\mathbb{U}\setminus\{0\}
with all the notations in (
35
) and (
37
).

Proof.

Note that, by (
37
), we have

(40)

\nabla^{2}_{uu}\mathscr{L}({\bar{u}},\bar{p},\bar{\lambda})w=(\bar{\mu}w_{1},0)\quad\forall w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}.

In particular,
\nabla^{2}_{uu}\mathscr{L}({\bar{u}},\bar{p},\bar{\lambda})
is positive semidefinite. Moreover, since the normal cone mapping
N_{\Theta}
is
maximally monotone
, we have

(41)

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\left\{\left\langle z,\,{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w\right\rangle|\;z\in D^{*}N_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})|\,\bar{\lambda})({\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w)\right\}\geq 0,

see, e.g.,
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
. Therefore, the left-hand side of (
39
) is nonnegative for any
w\in\mathbb{U}
. It hence suffices to show that, if it is equal to zero, then
w=0
. To this end, let
w\in\mathbb{U}
satisfy

(42)

\left\langle w,\,\nabla^{2}_{uu}\mathscr{L}({\bar{u}},\bar{p},\bar{\lambda})w\right\rangle+\inf_{z\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\left\{\left\langle z,\,{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w\right\rangle|\;z\in D^{*}N_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})|\,\bar{\lambda})({\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w)\right\}=0.

Applying (
41
) and (
40
) to (
42
) yields
w_{1}=0
. And consequently, we find that

(43)

{\mathcal{J}}_{u}G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})w=(A^{*}w_{1},w_{2})=(0,w_{2}).

In particular, (
42
) reduces to

\inf_{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}}\left\{z_{2}w_{2}|\;(z_{1},z_{2})\in D^{*}N_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})|\,\bar{\lambda})(0,w_{2})\right\}=0.

It remains to show that
w_{2}=0
. To this end, pick
(z_{1},z_{2})\in D^{*}N_{\Theta}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})|\,\bar{\lambda})(0,w_{2})
, i.e.,

((z_{1},z_{2}),(0,-w_{2}))\in N_{{\rm gph}\,N_{\Theta}}(G({\bar{u}},\bar{p}),\bar{\lambda}).

By the definition of the normal cone (
15
), we find sequences
(v^{k},t_{k})\to G({\bar{u}},\bar{p})=(\bar{A}^{*}{\bar{y}},\bar{t})
,
(\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k})\to\bar{\lambda}=(\bar{\lambda}_{1},-1)
,
(z_{1}^{k},z_{2}^{k})\to(z_{1},z_{2})
, and
(w_{1}^{k},w_{2}^{k})\to(0,w_{2})
such that
g^{*}(v^{k})\leq t_{k}
,
(\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k})\in N_{\Theta}(v^{k},t_{k})
and

\left((z_{1}^{k},z_{2}^{k}),(w_{1}^{k},-w_{2}^{k})\right)\in\hat{N}_{{\rm gph}\,N_{\Theta}}\left((v^{k},t_{k}),(\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k})\right).

By the expression of regular normal vectors in (
14
), for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
((v,t),(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}))\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{\Theta}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\delta}((v^{k},t_{k}),(\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k}))
:

(44)

\left\langle z_{1}^{k},\,v-v^{k}\right\rangle+z_{2}^{k}(t-t_{k})+\left\langle w_{1}^{k},\,\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}^{k}\right\rangle-w_{2}^{k}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k})\leq\epsilon\|(v,t,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})-(v^{k},t_{k},\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k})\|.

As
(\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k})\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}}(v^{k},t_{k})
, we get from (
12
) that

\left\langle\lambda_{1}^{k},\,v-v^{k}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}^{k}(t-t_{k})\leq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad(v,t)\in\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}.

By choosing
(v,t)=(v^{k},g^{*}(v^{k}))
in the above inequality, it follows that
\lambda_{2}^{k}(g^{*}(v^{k})-t_{k})\leq 0
. As
\lambda_{2}^{k}
is close to
-1
and
g^{*}(v^{k})\leq t_{k}
, we have
t_{k}=g^{*}(v^{k})
.

Let us choose
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
satisfying
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}=\dfrac{\lambda_{1}^{k}}{\lambda_{2}^{k}}
and such that
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k})
. It follows from formula (
13
) (which is applicable as
t_{k}=g^{*}(v^{k})
) that

\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{-\lambda_{2}}=\dfrac{\lambda_{1}^{k}}{-\lambda_{2}^{k}}\in\partial g^{*}(v^{k}).

This implies that
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in N_{\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}}(v^{k},t_{k})
, i.e.,
((v^{k},t_{k}),(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}))\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{\Theta}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\delta}((v^{k},t_{k}),(\lambda^{k}_{1},\lambda^{k}_{2}))
. We can thus insert
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{k}}{\lambda_{2}^{k}}
and
v=v^{k},t=t_{k}
in
(
44
) to obtain

\frac{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k}}{\lambda_{2}^{k}}\left\langle w_{1}^{k},\,\lambda_{1}^{k}\right\rangle-w_{2}^{k}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k})\leq\epsilon\left[\|\lambda_{1}^{k}\|\frac{|\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k}|}{|\lambda_{2}^{k}|}+|\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k}|\right],

which implies that

-w_{2}^{k}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k})\leq\epsilon\left[\|\lambda_{1}^{k}\|\frac{|\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k}|}{|\lambda_{2}^{k}|}+|\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k}|\right]+\frac{|\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{k}|}{|\lambda_{2}^{k}|}\cdot\left|\left\langle w_{1}^{k},\,\lambda_{1}^{k}\right\rangle\right|.

As
\lambda_{2}
can be chosen around
\lambda_{2}^{k}
, the above inequality implies

|w_{2}^{k}|\leq\epsilon\left[\dfrac{\|\lambda_{1}^{k}\|}{|\lambda_{2}^{k}|}+1\right]+\dfrac{|\left\langle w_{1}^{k},\,\lambda_{1}^{k}\right\rangle|}{|\lambda_{2}^{k}|}.

Since
(\lambda_{1}^{k},\lambda_{2}^{k})\to(\bar{\lambda}_{1},-1)
and
(w_{1}^{k},w_{2}^{k})\to(0,w_{2})
, we get from the above inequality that

|w_{2}|\leq\epsilon(\|\bar{\lambda}_{1}\|+1)\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\epsilon>0.

This tells us that
w_{2}=0
, which verifies the claim at the beginning.
∎

.

(45)

Then, the solution mapping
\widehat{S}

Remark 3.6

(The class of
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible conjugates)
.

Our main assumption on the regularizer
g
in Theorem
3.5
to have a conjugate
g^{*}
that is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
\bar{z}
appears to be restrictive at first glance.
However, it turns out that many important convex regularizers
g
satisfy this condition. We furnish evidence for this by the following list of (important) examples:

(a)

(Convex piecewise linear functions)
The proper convex function
g:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is called convex
piecewise linear
on its domain if
\mathrm{dom}\,g
can be represented as the union of finitely many polyhedral sets, relative to each of which
g(x)
is given by an expression
\left\langle a,\,x\right\rangle+b
for some
a\in\mathbb{X}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
; see, e.g.,
[
27
, Definition 2.47]
. The Fenchel conjugate of
g
is also a piecewise linear function according to
[
27
, Theorem 11.14]
. Thus, the epigraph of
g^{*}
is a polyhedra by
[
27
, Theorem 2.49]
. It follows from
[
2
, Example 3.139]
that
\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible. This tells us that the conjugate of any proper convex piecewise linear function is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible.

(b)

(Support functions of
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible set)
Let
g
be the
support function
of a closed, convex, and
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible set
C\subset\mathbb{X}
, i.e.,

(46)

g(x)=\sigma_{C}(x):=\sup\left\{\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle\,\left|\;v\in C\right.\right\}.

Note that
g^{*}=\delta_{C}
, the indicator function of
C
. Hence
\mathrm{epi}\,g^{*}=C\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Obviously,
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is polyhedral, hence
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible
[
2
, Example 3.139]
. Thus, the Cartesian product
C\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible. It follows that
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible.

This class of support functions is broad. It includes norm functions that are widely used as regularizers, see the two following examples.

(c)

(
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm)
Let

g
be the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

(47)

g(x)=\|x\|_{1,2}:=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{J}\|\quad\mbox{for any}\quad x=(x_{J})_{J\in\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\mathcal{G}
is a partition of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
m
different groups
J\in\mathcal{G}
and
\|x_{J}\|
is the Euclidean norm at
x_{J}\in\mathbb{R}^{|J|}
for any
J\in\mathcal{G}
. We note that this covers the
\ell_{1}
norm by the partition
\left\{\{i\}\,\left|\;i=1,\dots,n\right.\right\}
.

Now, define the set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

C:=\prod_{J\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{B}_{J},

where
\mathbb{B}_{J}
is the (Euclidean) unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{|J|}
. Then it is easy to see that
g=\sigma_{C}
. Note that
\mathbb{B}_{J}=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{|J|}|\,h(v):=1-\|v\|^{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at any
v
with
\|v\|=1
, as
h(v)=0
and
{\mathcal{J}}h(v):\mathbb{R}^{|J|}\to\mathbb{R},x\mapsto-2v^{T}x
is surjective. As discussed after Definition
2.7
,
\mathbb{B}_{J}
is also
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at any
{\bar{v}}\in{\rm int}\,\mathbb{B}_{J}
. Thus, it is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at any
{\bar{v}}\in\mathbb{B}_{J}
. It follows that the Cartesian product
C
defined above is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible.
Consequently,
g^{*}=\delta_{C}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible.

(d)

(Nuclear norm)
Another important regularizer is the
nuclear norm

\|X\|_{*}
, the sum of all
singular values
of
X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
(
m\leq n
). Its Fenchel conjugate is also
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible; see, e.g.,
[
10
, Proposition 3.2 and Remark 3.4]
. This fact can be explained directly and differently as follows. The nuclear norm can be written as the support function
\|X\|_{*}=\sigma_{\mathbb{B}}(X)
, where
\mathbb{B}=\{Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}|\;\|Z\|_{2}\leq 1\}
is the
spectral unit ball
in
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
\|\cdot\|_{2}
being the spectral norm. We show next that
\mathbb{B}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at any
\bar{Z}
on the boundary of
\mathbb{B}
, i.e.,
\|\bar{Z}\|_{2}=1
. Note that the spectral unit ball is represented by

(48)

\mathbb{B}=\left\{Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}|\,G(Z):=\mathbb{I}_{n}-Z^{T}Z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}\right\},

where
\mathbb{I}_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix and
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
is the set of all positive semidefinite
n\times n
matrices. As the positive semidefinite cone
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible
[
2
, Example 3.140]
, it suffices to show that
\bar{Z}
is nondegenerate for
G
with respect to
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
according to
[
31
, Proposition 3.2]
, which means

(49)

\mathrm{rge}\,{\mathcal{J}}G(\bar{Z})+\mathrm{lin}\,T_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(G(\bar{Z}))=\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the space of all
n\times n
symmetric matrices. Suppose the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of
\bar{Z}
is
\bar{Z}=\bar{U}\Sigma\bar{V}^{T}
, where
\bar{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
and
\bar{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
are orthogonal matrices and
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}{\rm Diag}\,(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})&0\end{pmatrix}
has two matrix blocks: the first block of
m\times m
is the diagonal matrix containing all singular values
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\ldots\geq\sigma_{m}
of
\bar{Z}
and the second block is just zero
m\times(n-m)
matrix. Note that

(50)

{\mathcal{J}}G(\bar{Z})X=-\bar{Z}^{T}X-X^{T}\bar{Z}=-\bar{V}\Sigma^{T}\bar{U}^{T}X-X^{T}\bar{U}\Sigma\bar{V}^{T}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}.

Since
\|\bar{Z}\|_{2}=1
, we have
\sigma_{1}=1
. Let
p
be the multiplicity of
\sigma_{1}
in
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\}
, i.e.,
1=\sigma_{p}>\sigma_{p+1}
(with
\sigma_{m+1}:=0
). Choose
X=\bar{U}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\bar{V}^{T}
for any
A\in\mathbb{S}^{p}
, we derive from (
50
) that

{\mathcal{J}}G(\bar{Z})X=-\bar{V}\Sigma^{T}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\bar{V}^{T}-\bar{V}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\Sigma\bar{V}^{T}=-2\bar{V}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\bar{V}^{T}.

It follows that

(51)

\mathrm{rge}\,\,{\mathcal{J}}G(\bar{Z})\supset\left\{\bar{V}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\bar{V}^{T}|\;A\in\mathbb{S}^{p}\right\}.

Moreover, observe further that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(G(\bar{Z}))&=\;\;T_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(\bar{V}(\mathbb{I}_{n}-\Sigma^{T}\Sigma)\bar{V}^{T})\\
&=\;\;T_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\left(\bar{V}\begin{pmatrix}0_{p\times p}&0&0\\
0&{\rm Diag}\,(1-\sigma^{2}_{p+1},\ldots,1-\sigma^{2}_{m})&0\\
0&0&\mathbb{I}_{n-m}\end{pmatrix}\bar{V}^{T}\right).\end{array}

By the well-known formula of tangent cone of the positive semidefinite cone
[
2
, Example 2.65]
, we have

T_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(G(\bar{Z}))=\left\{\bar{V}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
B^{T}&C\end{pmatrix}\bar{V}^{T}|\;A\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{p},B\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times(n-p)},C\in\mathbb{S}^{n-p}\right\}.

It follows that

\mathrm{lin}\,\,T_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(G(\bar{Z}))=\left\{\bar{V}\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\
B^{T}&C\end{pmatrix}\bar{V}^{T}|\;B\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times(n-p)},C\in\mathbb{S}^{n-p}\right\},

which together with (
51
) verifies (
49
). Thus,
\bar{Z}
is nondegenerate for
G
with respect to
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
. The preimage
\mathbb{B}=G^{-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
\bar{Z}
due to
[
31
, Proposition 3.2]
. As
\mathbb{B}
is always
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at point in is interior, it is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at any
\bar{Z}\in\mathbb{B}
. This explains the fact that the Fenchel conjugate of the nuclear norm is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible. There are many more spectral functions whose Fenchel conjugates are also
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible
[
10
, Proposition 3.2]
.

\triangle

We now show that qualification condition (
45
) is necessary for the solution map
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\mu})
in (
30
) to be single-valued.

Theorem 3.7

(Necessity of single-valuedness of solution mapping
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\mu})
)
.

Suppose that the solution mapping
S(\bar{A},b,\bar{\mu})
in (
30
) is single-valued around
\bar{b}
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
. Then, with
\bar{z}=\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{A}^{*}(\bar{b}-\bar{A}\bar{x})
, condition (
45
) is satisfied.

Proof.

Suppose that the solution mapping
S(\bar{A},b,\bar{\mu})
in (
30
) is single-valued around
\bar{b}
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
. By first-order optimality conditions, we have
\bar{z}\in\partial g({\bar{x}})
, i.e.,
\bar{x}\in\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
. Since
\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
is a closed convex set, we have

(53)

\mathrm{cl}\,[{\rm ri}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})]=\partial g^{*}(\bar{z}),

see, e.g.,
[
26
, Theorem 6.3]
. It follows that there exists a sequence
\{x_{k}\in{\rm ri}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})\}\to\bar{x}
. Therefore, we find that

b_{k}:=\bar{b}+\bar{A}(x_{k}-{\bar{x}})\to\bar{b}.

Observe that

\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{A}^{*}(b_{k}-\bar{A}x_{k})=\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{A}^{*}(\bar{b}-\bar{A}\bar{x})=\bar{z}\in\partial g(x_{k}).

By first-order optimality, we thus infer that
x_{k}\in S(\bar{A},b_{k},\bar{\mu})
is the (by assumption) unique solution of the problem
P(\bar{A},b_{k},\bar{\mu})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. By
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, which provides a full characterization of solution uniqueness for convex optimization problems, we obtain that

(54)

\ker\bar{A}\cap\mathrm{cone}(\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})-x_{k})=\{0\}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.
As
x_{k}\in\mathrm{ri}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
, we have
\mathrm{cone}\,(\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})-x_{k})=\mathrm{par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
(cf. (
9
)).
This together with (
54
) verifies the condition (
45
).
∎

We are now in a position to establish the the main result of this paper, which shows that condition (
45
) is not only sufficient for Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping (
30
) around
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
, but also necessary provided that the function
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible. Moreover, we show that the solution mapping
S
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around the point in question if and only if it is single-valued, i.e., the Lipschitz stability is automatic whenever the mapping is single-valued. This equivalence is interesting, as the Lipschitz stability is usually not a free property for single-valued mappings in general.

Theorem 3.8

(Full characterization of Lipschitz stability of solution mapping
S
)
.

Suppose that
\bar{x}
is an optimal solution of problem
P(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
and that the function
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
\bar{z}=\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{A}^{*}(\bar{b}-\bar{A}\bar{x})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

Condition (
45
) is satisfied.

(ii)

The solution mapping
S(A,b,\mu)
in (
30
) of problem (
29
) is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.

(iii)

The solution mapping
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\cdot)
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.

(iv)

The solution mapping
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\mu})
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
\bar{b}
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.

(v)

The solution mapping
S
in (
30
) is single-valued around
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.

(vi)

The solution mapping
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\cdot)
is single-valued around
(\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.

(vii)

The solution mapping
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\mu})
is single-valued around
\bar{b}
with
{\bar{x}}=S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.

Proof.

The implication [
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
] follows from Theorem
3.5
. Implications [
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)
\Rightarrow
(vii)
] are trivial. We also have [
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(v)
\Rightarrow
(vi)
\Rightarrow
(vii)
]. Moreover, [
(vii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
] is obtained from Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Theorem
3.8
generalizes the recent results in
[
24
, Theorem 3.12 and Theorem 4.7]
, which establish the equivalence between
(iii)
and
(vi)
when regularizers
g
include the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm and the nuclear norm via a different approach. Note from our Remark
3.6
, conjugates of the latter two regularizers are
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible. Moreover, our condition (
45
) recovers the characterizations of Lipschitz stability for the solution mapping in (
30
) used in
[
24
]
for these two cases and also the one in
[
3
, Assumption 4.3]
when
g
is the
\ell_{1}
-norm, which appeared in
[
23
,
34
]
as a sufficient condition for the Lasso problem. The implication [
(vii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv)
] in this theorem is also stronger than
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
, which shows that if
S(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\mu})
is single-valued around
\bar{b}
, then it is continuous when
g
is a convex piecewise linear function that is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible; see, e.g., Remark
3.6
. Stability of the solution mapping
S
with respect to variable
b
seems to be important in the proof of our Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
3.8
. By fixing the parameter
b=\bar{b}
, our condition (
45
) is also sufficient for the Lipschitz stability of solution mappings
S(\cdot,\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
,
S(\cdot,\bar{b},\cdot)
, and
S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\cdot)
around the corresponding points due to the implication [
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
] in Theorem
3.8
. Whether it is necessary is an open question.

We close out this section by showing that the Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping of problem (
29
) is automatic when
\bar{x}
is the unique solution of problem
P(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
and the so-called
Dual Strict Complementarity Condition
is satisfied and granted that
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that
\bar{x}
is an optimal solution of problem
P(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
and that the function
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
{\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{A}^{*}(\bar{b}-\bar{A}\bar{x})
. Moreover, suppose that the following Dual Strict Complementarity Condition holds

(55)

\bar{x}\in{\rm ri}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z}).

Then the solution mapping
S
in (
30
) is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})=\bar{x}
if and only if
\bar{x}
is the unique solution of problem
P(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
.

Proof.

The direction “
\Rightarrow
” is trivial. To prove the opposite implication, we suppose that
\bar{x}
is the unique solution of problem
P(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
and that the condition (
55
) is satisfied. By
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, we have

(56)

\ker\,\bar{A}\cap\mathrm{cone}(\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})-\bar{x})=\{0\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\bar{z}=-\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{A}^{*}(\bar{A}\bar{x}-\bar{b}).

As
\bar{x}\in{\rm ri}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
by (
55
), we obtain from (
9
) that

\mathrm{cone}\,(\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})-\bar{x})={\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z}).

This together (
56
) tells us that condition (
45
) is satisfied. By Theorem
3.8
, the solution mapping
S
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})
with
S(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\mu})=\bar{x}
.
∎

The above result resembles
[
24
, Corollary 4.8]
when
g
is the nuclear norm and the condition (
55
) is replaced by the
Strict Complementarity Condition
:

(57)

\bar{v}\in\mathrm{ri}\,\,\partial g({\bar{x}}).

For the case of nuclear norm, it is shown in
[
13
, Remark 3.7]
that (
55
) and (
57
) are equivalent. But at this stage, we do not know their correlation for more general
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible functions
g^{*}
.

4.
Full stability of convex additive-composite problems

In this section, we study the following optimization problem

(58)

\tilde{P}(\bar{p})\qquad\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad f(x,\bar{p})+g(x),

where
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{P}
are two Euclidean spaces,
f:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, and
g:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is closed, proper, convex. Suppose that
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f(\cdot,\bar{p})\cap\mathrm{dom}\,g
is an optimal solution of problem (
58
). Throughout this section, we assume that

(A)

f(\cdot,\bar{p})
is a convex function;

(B)

f(\cdot,\cdot)
is twice continuously differentiable around
(\bar{x},\bar{p})
.

Define the function
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by

(59)

\varphi(x,p):=f(x,p)+g(x)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad(x,p)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}.

The two-parametric perturbation of problem (
58
) is constructed by

(60)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\varphi(x,p)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle,

with
basic
perturbation
p\in\mathbb{P}
and tilt parameter
v\in\mathbb{X}
. This certainly covers the problem (
31
).

Let us recall the definition of
full stability
introduced by Levy, Poliquin, and Rockafellar
[
19
]
, which is a far-reaching extension of the notion of tilt stability by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
25
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Full stability and tilt stability)
.

The point
{\bar{x}}
is called a fully stable optimal solution of problem (
58
) if there exists
\gamma>0
such that the solution map

(61)

M_{\gamma}(v,p):={\rm argmin}\;\{\varphi(x,p)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle|\;x\in\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}({\bar{x}})\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad(v,p)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}

and the mapping

(62)

m_{\gamma}(v,p):=\inf\;\{\varphi(x,p)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle|\;x\in\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}({\bar{x}})\}

are single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on some neighborhood of
(0,\bar{p})\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}
with
M_{\gamma}(0,\bar{p})={\bar{x}}
.

The point
\bar{x}
is called a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
58
) if the mapping
M_{\gamma}(\cdot,\bar{p})
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on some neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{X}
with
M_{\gamma}(0,\bar{p})={\bar{x}}
.

As the function
f
is twice continuously differentiable around
(\bar{x},\bar{p})
and
g
is closed, proper, convex, the function
\varphi
is
continuously prox-regular
in
x
at
{\bar{x}}
for
0
, with compatible parameterization by
p
at
\bar{p}
in the sense of
[
19
, Definition 2.1]
. Moreover, we observe that the so-called
basic constraint qualification

(63)

(0,q)\in\partial^{\infty}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{p})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=0

is also satisfied, where
\partial^{\infty}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{p})
is the singular subdifferential of the function
\varphi
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{p}})
defined in (
18
). This is due to the sum rule of the singular subdifferential
[
20
, Proposition 1.107]
(see also
[
27
, Corollary 10.9]
), which gives

\partial^{\infty}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{p})=\partial^{\infty}g(\bar{x})\times\{0\}

using assumption (B). According to
[
19
, Proposition 3.5]
, when the above basic constraint qualification is satisfied, the condition in the definition of full stability that
m_{\gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous on some neighborhood of
(0,\bar{p})\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{P}
is automatically met, provided that
M_{\gamma}(0,\bar{p})=\{{\bar{x}}\}
. It is also worth mentioning that the latter condition
M_{\gamma}(0,\bar{p})={\bar{x}}
means that
{\bar{x}}
is the unique (global) optimal solution of problem (
58
), since both functions
f(\cdot,\bar{p})
and
g(\cdot)
are convex in our framework.

Let us recall here
[
19
, Theorem 2.3]
,
[
21
, Theorem 4.4]
, and
[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
, which provides characterizations of full stability and tilt stability via second-order analysis.

Theorem 4.2

(Characterizations of full stability)
.

Suppose that
{\bar{x}}
is an optimal solution of problem (
58
). Then
{\bar{x}}
is a fully stable optimal solution of problem (
58
) if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(a)

\inf\,\{\left\langle z,\,w\right\rangle|\;(z,q)\in D^{*}(\partial_{x}\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{p}|\,0)(w)\}>0
for all
w\in\mathbb{X}\setminus\{0\}
.

(b)

(0,q)\in D^{*}(\partial_{x}\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{p}|\,0)(0)

\Longrightarrow

q=0
.

Moreover,
{\bar{x}}
is a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
58
) if and only if

(64)

\inf\,\{\left\langle z,\,w\right\rangle|\;z\in D^{*}\partial\varphi_{\bar{p}}(\bar{x}|\,0)(w)\}>0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad w\in\mathbb{X}\setminus\{0\},

where
\varphi_{\bar{p}}(\cdot):=\varphi(\cdot,\bar{p})
.

It is worth mentioning that in the original result of Levy, Poliquin, and Rockafellar
[
19
, Theorem 2.4]
, the condition in the above part (a) does not include the “infimum”. This equivalent “infimum” form coming from
[
21
, Theorem 4.4]
is useful for us in this paper at some point.

Note also that
\partial_{x}\varphi(x,p)=\nabla_{x}f(x,p)+\partial g(x)
. It follows from the coderivative sum rule
[
20
, Theorem 1.62]
that

(65)

D^{*}(\partial_{x}\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{p}|\,0)(w)=(\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})^{*}w,\nabla_{xp}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})^{*}w)+D^{*}\partial g(\bar{x}|\,-\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p}))(w)\times\{0\}.

This tells us that condition (b) in Theorem
4.2
is always true in our setting. Moreover, condition (a) in Theorem
4.2
turns to

(66)

\left\langle\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})w,\,w\right\rangle+\inf\{\left\langle z,\,w\right\rangle|\;z\in D^{*}\partial g({\bar{x}}|\,-\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p}))(w)\}>0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad w\in\mathbb{X}\setminus\{0\},

which is also condition (
64
) again due to the
the sum rule of the coderivative
[
20
, Theorem 1.62]
:

D^{*}\partial_{x}\varphi_{\bar{p}}(\bar{x}|\,0)(w)=\nabla^{2}_{xx}f(\bar{x},\bar{p})^{*}w+D^{*}\partial g(\bar{x}|\,-\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p}))(w).

Hence, both full stability and tilt stability are equivalent for our problem (
58
) at the optimal solution
\bar{x}
and they are characterized by condition (
66
).

As
f(\cdot,\bar{p})
is a convex function,
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})
is a positive semidefinite operator. Its
square root operator

\bar{A}:=(\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p}))^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})
with
\bar{A}^{*}\bar{A}=\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})
is unique and well-defined. Condition (
66
) is equivalent to

(67)

\|\bar{A}w\|^{2}+\inf\{\left\langle z,\,w\right\rangle|\;z\in D^{*}\partial\tilde{g}({\bar{x}}|\,0)(w)\}>0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad w\in\mathbb{X}\setminus\{0\}

with
\tilde{g}(x):=g(x)+\left\langle\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},{\bar{p}}),\,x-{\bar{x}}\right\rangle
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
. It follows from Theorem
4.2
that the above condition is satisfied if and only if

{\bar{x}}
is a tilt stable optimal solution of the following problem

(68)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{A}x-\bar{b}\|^{2}+\tilde{g}(x)\quad\mbox{with}\quad\bar{b}:=\bar{A}{\bar{x}}.

This optimization problem is in the format of (
29
). The above observation allows us to establish a simple necessary condition of the tilt stability as below.

Theorem 4.3

(Necessary condition for tilt stability)
.

Suppose that
{\bar{x}}
is a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
58
). Then we have

(69)

\ker\nabla_{xx}^{2}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})\cap{\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})=\{0\}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\bar{z}:=-\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p}).

Proof.

Suppose that
{\bar{x}}
is a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
58
). As discussed above, it is also a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
68
). In particular, by first-order optimality conditions,
\bar{z}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
, i.e.,
\bar{x}\in\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
, thus (
69
) is well-defined. Therefore,
there exists a sequence
\{x_{k}\in\mathrm{ri}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})\}\to\bar{x}
. Define

v_{k}:=\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})(x_{k}-{\bar{x}})\to 0

and consider the linearly perturbed problem

(70)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\psi_{k}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{A}x-\bar{b}\|^{2}+\tilde{g}(x)-\left\langle v_{k},\,x\right\rangle

As
\bar{z}\in\partial g(x_{k})
and
\bar{A}^{*}\bar{A}=\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})
, we have

\partial\psi_{k}(x_{k})=\bar{A}^{*}(\bar{A}x_{k}-\bar{A}{\bar{x}})+\partial g(x_{k})-\bar{z}-v_{k}=\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})(x_{k}-{\bar{x}})+\partial g(x_{k})-\bar{z}-v_{k}\ni 0.

Hence,
x_{k}
is an optimal solution of problem (
70
). Since
v_{k}\to 0
and
{\bar{x}}
is a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
68
),
x_{k}
is the unique solution of problem (
70
) (for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large). Define
\hat{g}_{k}(x):=\tilde{g}(x)-\left\langle v_{k},\,x\right\rangle
and obtain from
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
that

(71)

\ker\bar{A}\cap\mathrm{cone}(\partial\hat{g}_{k}^{*}(-v_{k})-x_{k})=\{0\},

which is indeed the characterization for solution uniqueness of problem (
70
). Note that

\hat{g}_{k}(x)=g(x)-\left\langle\bar{z},\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle-\left\langle v_{k},\,x\right\rangle.

We have
\partial\hat{g}_{k}(x)=\partial g(x)-\bar{z}-v_{k}
. It follows that
\partial\hat{g}_{k}^{*}(-v_{k})=\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
. Since
x_{k}\in\mathrm{ri}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
, we obtain from (
9
) that

\mathrm{cone}(\partial\hat{g}^{*}(-v_{k})-x_{k})=\mathrm{cone}(\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})-x_{k})={\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z}).

This together with (
71
) and the fact that
\ker\bar{A}=\ker\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})
verifies (
69
).
∎

When the Fenchel conjugate
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible, we show next that condition (
69
) is also sufficient for full stability and tilt stability of problem (
58
).

Theorem 4.4

(Characterization of full stability and sufficient condition for Lipschitz stability)
.

Suppose that
{\bar{x}}
is an optimal solution of problem (
58
) and that the function
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
\bar{z}=-\nabla_{x}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})\in\partial g({\bar{x}})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

{\bar{x}}
is a fully stable optimal solution of problem (
58
).

(ii)

{\bar{x}}
is a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
58
).

(iii)

Condition (
69
) is satisfied.

Consequently, if additionally
f(\cdot,p)
is convex for any
p\in\mathbb{P}
around
\bar{p}
, then the solution mapping

(72)

S(p):={\rm argmin}\left\{f(x,p)+g(x)|\;x\in\mathbb{X}\right\}

is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
\bar{p}
with
S(\bar{p})=\bar{x}
provided that condition (
69
) is satisfied.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\bar{x}}
is an optimal solution of problem (
58
). Note that
{\bar{x}}
is also an optimal solution of problem (
68
). The equivalence between
(i)
and
(ii)
follows the arguments discussed after Theorem
4.2
. Moreover, the implication [
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
] is obtained by Theorem
4.3
. It remains to verify [
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
]. Indeed, suppose that condition (
69
) is satisfied and consider the following linearly perturbed version of (
68
):

(73)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{A}x-\bar{b}\|^{2}+\tilde{g}(x)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle

with tilt parameter
v\in\mathbb{X}
. As
\partial\tilde{g}^{*}(0)=\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})
, condition (
69
) is equivalent to

\ker\bar{A}\cap{\rm par}\,\partial\tilde{g}^{*}(0)=\{0\}.

Since
g^{*}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
\bar{z}
, we infer that
\tilde{g}^{*}=g^{*}((\cdot)-\bar{z})-\left\langle\bar{z},\,\bar{x}\right\rangle
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible at
0
. Applying Theorem
3.5
to problem (
73
) tells us that its solution mapping with variable
v
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
0
, i.e.,
\bar{x}
is a tilt stable solution of problem (
73
). By Theorem
4.2
, we have

\|\bar{A}w\|^{2}+\inf\{\left\langle z,\,w\right\rangle|\;z\in D^{*}\partial\tilde{g}(\bar{x}|\,0)(w)\}>0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad w\in\mathbb{X}\setminus\{0\}

as in (
67
). Note also that
\|\bar{A}w\|^{2}=\left\langle\bar{A}^{*}\bar{A}w,\,w\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla_{xx}^{2}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})w,\,w\right\rangle
. The above condition is equivalent to (
66
), which is exactly the condition (
66
) or (
64
). By Theorem
4.2
,
\bar{x}
is a tilt stable optimal solution of problem (
58
), which means
(ii)
is satisfied.

Finally, suppose that the function
f(\cdot,p)
is convex for any
p\in\mathbb{P}
around
\bar{p}
and condition (
69
) is satisfied. Hence,
\bar{x}
is a fully stable optimal solution. It follows that there exists some
\gamma>0
such that the mapping

M_{\gamma}(v,p):=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\left\{f(x,p)+g(x)-\left\langle v,\,x\right\rangle|\;x\in\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}({\bar{x}})\right\}

is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on some neighborhood of
{\bar{p}}
with
M_{\gamma}(0,{\bar{p}})={\bar{x}}
. This allows us to find
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that
M_{\gamma}(\{0\}\times\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}({\bar{p}}))\subset{\rm int}\,\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}({\bar{x}})
. Hence,
M_{\gamma}(0,p)
for
p\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}({\bar{p}})
is the unique
local
optimal solution of

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\quad f(x,p)+g(x).

As the function
f(\cdot,p)
is convex for
p\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}({\bar{p}})
,
M_{\gamma}(0,p)
is also the unique global optimal solution. As
S(p)\in M_{\gamma}(0,p)
for
p\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}({\bar{p}})
, we have
S(p)=M_{\gamma}(0,p)
. Hence,
S(p)
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}({\bar{p}})
. The proof is completed.
∎

Let us conclude this section by providing a simple example showing that condition (
69
) is not necessary for the Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping
S(p)
in (
72
). This essentially highlights the difference between Theorem
3.8
and Theorem
4.4
, where the function
f
in Theorem
3.8
has a special structure as a quadratic function.

Example 4.5
.

Consider the following simple optimization problem

(74)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\quad x^{4}p,

where
p\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter around
\bar{p}=1
,
f(x,p)=x^{4}p
, and
g(x)=0
. The solution set
S(p)=\{0\}
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous around
\bar{p}
. At
\bar{p}
, the solution of problem (
74
) is
{\bar{x}}=0
.
Note that
\bar{z}=-\nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{p})=0
,
g^{*}(v)=\delta_{0}(v)
for
v\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})=0
. It follows that
\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})=\mathbb{R}
and
\ker\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})=\mathbb{R}
, which implies

\ker\nabla^{2}_{xx}f({\bar{x}},\bar{p})\cap{\rm par}\,\partial g^{*}(\bar{z})=\mathbb{R}.

Conditions (
69
) fails in this case.

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for the Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping (
30
) of regularized least-squares optimization problem in the format of (
29
). When the Fenchel conjugate of the regularizer
g
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible, we show that the Lipschitz stability of the solution mapping (
30
) is equivalent to our condition (
45
). This result recovers the corresponding findings in
[
3
,
24
]
when the regularizer
g
is the
\ell_{1}
norm, the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm, or the nuclear norm.

One of the open questions we plan to investigate in the future is the computation of the Lipschitz modulus of the solution mapping when Lipschitz stability occurs. In theory, this Lipschitz modulus may be obtained by calculating the coderivative of the solution mapping (
30
); see, e.g.,
[
20
, Theorem 4.10]
and
[
27
, Theorem 9.39]
. However, this approach requires some involved computations of second-order structures of the regularizer
g
, which can be particularly challenging when
g
does not have a polyhedral structure. Finding the Lipschitz modulus without relying on second-order information of
g
, as in our approach, remains an open area of research that we wish to continue exploring.

Another interesting topic that we aim to study is the sensitivity analysis of the solution mapping (
30
) under our condition (
45
). Some initial results in this direction have been established recently in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
32
]
. However,
[
24
, Example 3.14]
provides an example where the solution mapping (
30
) is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous but not differentiable when
g
is the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
norm. Identifying the conditions under which the solution mapping is differentiable is one of our ongoing projects.

Introduction

Riemannian manifolds of dimension
n
cannot, in general, be

C^{2}
-isometrically embedded into the Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
of dimension
n+1
. For instance, the Hilbert–Efimov theorem asserts that the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
does not admit any
C^{2}
isometric embedding into
\mathbb{E}^{3}

[
9
]
. Similarly, curvature-based arguments show that the two-dimensional flat torus

\mathbb{T}^{2}
does not admit any

C^{2}
isometric embedding into

\mathbb{E}^{3}
. In contrast, the

C^{1}
isometric embedding theorem of Nash and Kuiper establishes the existence of infinitely many isometric embeddings of a Riemannian manifold when the regularity is relaxed to the
C^{1}
setting
[
20
,
14
,
15
]
.
Despite these foundational results, the geometric analysis of such

C^{1}
submanifolds remains incomplete, and a deeper understanding of their extrinsic geometry
is still needed. In this article, we investigate the geometry of
C^{1}
and
C^{1,\alpha}
submanifolds of

\mathbb{E}^{n}
using the notion of sets with positive

\mu
-reach, introduced by Chazal, Cohen-Steiner, and Lieutier in 2006
[
2
,
3
]
, which generalizes Federer’s classical concept of sets with positive reach.

Sets with positive reach and
C^{1,1}
regularity.

Sets with positive reach were introduced by Herbert Federer in 1959 in order to generalize the notion of curvature measures to non smooth and non convex objects
[
10
]
. On the one hand, this concept makes it possible to define notions of curvature on non regular objects. On the other hand, it provides a tool - the projection map - for comparing sets that are Hausdorff close, thereby enabling stability results. As a consequence, it plays a central role in several areas of geometry, including Geometric Measure Theory or Geometric Inference
[
12
,
7
,
6
]
. For further details, we refer to the survey of Thäle
[
24
]
.

By definition, a closed set
K
of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
has a reach strictly greater than
r>0
if every point
p
that is at a distance less than
r
from
K
, has a unique closest point on
K
. This notion is intimately related to the distance function
\mathrm{d}_{K}:\mathbb{E}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
to the set
K
and allows to define the
r
-offset of
K
:

K^{r}=\{p\,|\,d_{K}(p)\leqslant r\}

also called the tubular neigborhood of
K
of size
r.

Federer studied in detail the function
\mathrm{d}_{K}
and showed that for
r>0
small enough, the level set
\mathrm{d}_{K}^{-1}(r)=\partial K^{r}
is a codimension one submanifold of class
C^{1,1}
,
i.e.
of class
C^{1}
with a Lipschitz normal.

Federer also considered the case of sets
K
that are submanifolds of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
. He mentioned without giving any detail that the graph of a Lipschitz function has positive reach if and only if it is of class
C^{1,1}

[
10
, Remark 4.20]
. This statement happens to be not straightforward and has generated several works in the last decades. Lytchak showed in 2005 that a topologically embedded manifold without boundary with positive reach is of class
C^{1,1}

[
18
]
. Scholtes gave an alternative proof in 2013 in the case of codimension one submanifolds homeomorphic to the sphere
[
23
]
. Rataj and Zajíček also provided a proof in 2017, under the restrictive assumption that the manifold is Lipschitz
[
21
]
.
More recently in 2024, using an approach based on homology, Lieutier and Wintraecken showed that a closed submanifold has positive reach if and only if it is locally the graph of a
C^{1,1}
function
[
16
]
.

Sets with positive
\mu
-reach.

Sets with positive reach present two main limitations. First, although they were introduced to extend curvature measures to irregular objects, they do not include simple irregular structures such as triangulations. Second, they are not stable with respect to the Hausdorff distance. Consequently, they are not directly suited to the framework of
Geometric Inference
, where one aims to infer geometric properties of a shape
K
from a Hausdorff approximation. To overcome these limitations, Chazal, Cohen-Steiner, and Lieutier introduced in 2006 the notion of sets with positive

\mu
-reach
[
2
,
3
]
.

The notion of
\mu
-reach can be viewed as a filtered notion of reach, derived from the gradient of the distance function
\mathrm{d}_{K}
. This distance function is 1-Lipschitz and is therefore differentiable almost everywhere. However it is not differentiable on the whole space
\mathbb{E}^{n}
. It can be shown that the reach of a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{E}^{n}
can be defined as the largest radius
r
for which the distance function
\mathrm{d}_{K}
is differentiable on the
r
-offset
K^{r}
:

\mathrm{reach}(K):=\sup\{r\penalty\ |\penalty\ \mathrm{d}_{K}\mbox{ is differentiable on }K^{r}\setminus K\},

see Section
2
.
In 2004, Lieutier introduced an extension of the gradient map
\nabla\mathrm{d}_{K}
to the entire Euclidean space, defining a
generalized gradient

[
17
]
.
This generalized gradient satisfies
\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{K}(p)\|\leqslant 1
with equality if and only if
\mathrm{d}_{K}
is differentiable at
p
. This observation naturally leads to a refined version of the reach, namely the
\mu
-reach of
K
, for
0\leqslant\mu\leqslant 1
, which is defined by:

\mathrm{reach}_{\mu}(K):=\sup\{r\penalty\ |\penalty\ \forall p\in K^{r},\penalty\ \|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{K}(p)\|\geqslant\mu\text{ on }K^{r}\setminus K\}.

The class of sets with positive
\mu
-reach contains all the sets with positive reach and also more irregular objects such as triangulations. Furthermore, it offers a natural tool in the field of Geometric Inference: stability results of the normals and of the curvature measures have been obtained for sets with positive
\mu
-reach
[
4
,
6
]
.

Contributions.

As mentioned above, Federer made a connection between submanifolds of class
C^{1,1}
and submanifolds with positive reach. In this article, we extend one implication of Federer’s claim and investigate the geometry of
C^{1}
and
C^{1,\alpha}
submanifolds of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
using the notion of sets with positive
\mu
-reach. We first show the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a
C^{1}
compact submanifold of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
without boundary. Then
M
has positive
\mu
-reach for any
\mu<1
.

This result is optimal for the parameter
\mu
. Indeed, any manifold

with positive
\mu
-reach for
\mu=1
, has positive reach, hence is of class
C^{1,1}
. Theorem
1.1
is obviously true if the compact manifold is of dimension
0
, since it has positive reach in that case. Henceforth, the manifold is assumed to have dimension greater than
1
. Using results of
[
11
,
5
]
, Theorem
1.1
directly implies a regularity result on the boundaries of the offset
M^{r}:=\mathrm{d}_{M}^{-1}([0,r])
and of the double offset
M^{r,t}:=\mathrm{d}_{L}^{-1}([0,t])
, where
L=\overline{\mathbb{E}^{n}\setminus M^{r}}
, as stated in the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M
be a
C^{1}
compact submanifold of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
without boundary. Then for any
\mu<1
and any
0<r<\mathrm{reach}_{\mu}(M)
, we have:

•

The set
L
has a reach greater than
\mu r
and its boundary
\partial L=\partial M^{r}
is a Lipschitz manifold.

•

For any
t\in]0,\mu r[
the boundary of the double offset
\partial M^{r,t}
is of class
C^{1,1}
and has a reach that satisfies
\mathrm{reach}(\partial M^{r,t})\geqslant\min(t,\mu r-t)
.

Since Federer’s curvature measures are well defined on sets with positive reach, this corollary shows that they can also be defined on the sets
\mathrm{d}_{M}^{-1}([r,+\infty[)
. Furthermore, stability results such as those established in
[
6
]
, open the possibility of estimating Federer’s curvature measures on the level sets
\mathrm{d}_{M}^{-1}(r)
from a Hausdorff approximation of the manifold
M
. This approach may be applied, for instance, to the study of the geometry of the
C^{1}
isometric embeddings mentioned above, where each
C^{1}

isometrically embedded submanifold
M
arises as the limit of a sequence of smooth submanifolds
(M_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
.

Theorem
1.1
provides a non quantitative result showing that the norm
\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}(p)\|
of the generalized gradient tends to
1
when
p
approaches the submanifold
M
. When considering an intermediate regularity
C^{1,\alpha}
between
C^{1}
and
C^{1,1}
, one obtains a more quantitative statement, namely a lower bound of
\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}\|
on the level sets of
\mathrm{d}_{M}
. In particular, the rate at which
\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}(p)\|
converges to 1 depends on the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity; this convergence becomes faster as
\alpha
increases, as described in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a
C^{1,\alpha}
compact submanifold of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
without boundary with
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1
.
Then there exists a constant

such that, for all
p\in\mathbb{E}^{n}\setminus M
,

1-\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}(p)\|^{2}\leqslant C\penalty\ \mathrm{d}_{M}(p)^{2\alpha}\Big(1-\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}(p)\|^{2}\Big)^{\alpha}.

In particular,

•

if
\alpha<1
, then for every
p\in\mathbb{E}^{n}\setminus M

1-\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}(p)\|^{2}\leqslant C^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}_{M}(p)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}},

•

if
\alpha=1
, then for every
p\in\mathbb{E}^{n}
such that
\mathrm{d}_{M}(p)<1/\sqrt{C}
, we have

\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}(p)\|=1.

We show in Section
5
that, when
\alpha\neq 1
, the exponent
2\alpha/(1-\alpha)
in Theorem
1.3
is sharp. When
\alpha=1
, we also recover the property that a
C^{1,1}
-submanifold

has positive reach, hence
\|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{M}(\cdot)\|=1
in a neighborhood of

.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section

Compactness assumption of Theorem

The compactness assumption in Theorem

Indeed, for any

Since

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{1+\alpha}\bigg\|(\mathrm{d}f_{x_{1}})^{-1}\bigg(\frac{R_{x_{1}}(x_{2})}{\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|^{1+\alpha}}\bigg)\bigg\|=O_{x_{2}\to x_{1}}(1).

4.2
Submanifolds of class
C^{1,\alpha}

The goal of this section is to show that at any point
q
of a
C^{1,\alpha}
submanifold
M
, there exists a map
h_{q}
such that
M
is locally the graph of
h_{q}
. Moreover, for any
q_{0}
in
M
, there exists an open set
U
such that all the
\alpha
-Hölder constant of
dh_{q}
for
q\in M\cap U
are bounded. This will be stated in Proposition
4.7
that will be used later in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Definition 4.5
.

A subset
M
of a Banach space
E
is a
\bm{C^{1,\alpha}}

submanifold
, if for all
p\in M
there exist an open set
\mathcal{U}
of
E
containing
p
and a
C^{1,\alpha}
-diffeomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{U}\mapsto\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{W}\subset F\times G
such that

\varphi(M\cap\mathcal{U})=\mathcal{V}\times\{0\}

where
\mathcal{V}
is an open subset of the Banach spaces
F
and
\mathcal{W}
is an open subset of the Banach spaces
G
with
0\in\mathcal{W}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Due to the Inverse Function Theorem, all of the classical local characterizations of a submanifold
i.e.
as a graph, as the zeros of a submersion and as the image of a parametrization, have they
C^{1,\alpha}
counterparts.

Proposition 4.7

Let

be a
C^{1,\alpha}
submanifold of

and

.
There exist

an open neighborhood of

and

such that for all

(1)

there is an open set

such that

is locally the graph of a
C^{1,\alpha}
function

, i.e.

M\cap U=\big\{\big(x,h_{q}(x)\big)\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\in V_{q}\subset T_{q}M\big\},

(2)

\mathrm{d}(h_{q})
is
\alpha
-Hölder on

and its Hölder constant is bounded by

.

Proof.

For any
q\in M
, we denote by
\pi_{q}^{T}
the orthogonal projection on
T_{q}M+q
and
\pi_{q}^{N}
the orthogonal projection on

-diffeomorphism on its image,

(ii)

for all

\mathrm{d}\big(\pi_{q}^{T}|_{M\cap U}\big)^{-1}
is
\alpha
-Hölder on
M\cap U

K
.

Indeed, if we set
h_{q}=\pi_{q}^{N}\circ\big(\pi_{q}^{T}\big)^{-1}
we obtain exactly
(1)
and
V_{q}=\pi_{q}^{T}(M\cap U)
.
Then, since
\|\mathrm{d}(\pi^{N}_{q})\|\leqslant 1
and by the chain rule, we have that
\mathrm{d}(h_{q})
is
\alpha
-Hölder on
V_{q}
with its Hölder constant bounded by

The proof of
(i)
and
(ii)
is done in the next five steps.
The first four steps will prove
(i)
by mimicking the proof of the Theorem

and adding a parameter
q\in M.

We fix

and
\psi:V_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\to U_{0}\cap M

such that

where

(resp.

\alpha
-Hölder.
We denote by

and

(resp.

).
We define, for all

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}f_{q}&:&V_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
&&x&\longmapsto&\mathrm{d}(\psi^{-1})_{q}\circ t_{-q}\circ\pi_{q}^{T}\circ\psi(x)\\
\end{array}

with

We also define

We then get,

In order to show that

is a bijection, which is recast as a fixed point problem for

Step 1.
The goal of this step is to show that there exists

Also, since

{\left\|\mathrm{d}(\varphi_{q})_{x}\right\|}_{\mathcal{L}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

To complete the proof, it suffices to check that, for all
t_{2}>t_{1}
, the following two inequalities:

\displaystyle\big(t_{2}^{2+2\alpha}-t_{1}^{2+2\alpha}\big)\big(t_{2}^{\alpha}-t_{1}^{\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle>2\alpha\big(t_{2}^{1+2\alpha}t_{1}^{\alpha}-t_{2}^{\alpha}t_{1}^{1+2\alpha}\big)\big(t_{2}-t_{1}\big)

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)\big(t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2}\big)\big(t_{2}^{\alpha}-t_{1}^{\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle>2\alpha\big(t_{2}^{1+\alpha}-t_{1}^{1+\alpha}\big)\big(t_{2}-t_{1}\big)

which are equivalent to

\displaystyle t_{2}^{1+2\alpha}-t_{1}^{1+2\alpha}>\big(1+2\alpha\big)\Big(t_{2}^{1+\alpha}t_{1}^{\alpha}-t_{2}^{\alpha}t_{1}^{1+\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)\big(t_{2}+t_{1}\big)\big(t_{2}^{\alpha}-t_{1}^{\alpha}\big)>2\alpha\,\big(t_{2}^{1+\alpha}-t_{1}^{1+\alpha}\big).

These inequalities are proved in Lemma
5.4
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1[
and
t_{2}>t_{1}\geqslant 0
then

\displaystyle t_{2}^{1+2\alpha}-t_{1}^{1+2\alpha}>\big(1+2\alpha\big)\Big(t_{2}^{1+\alpha}t_{1}^{\alpha}-t_{2}^{\alpha}t_{1}^{1+\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)\big(t_{2}+t_{1}\big)\big(t_{2}^{\alpha}-t_{1}^{\alpha}\big)>2\alpha\,\big(t_{2}^{1+\alpha}-t_{1}^{1+\alpha}\big)

Proof.

For the first inequality, let
x\in[t_{1},\infty[
and

f(x):=x^{1+2\alpha}-t_{1}^{1+2\alpha}-(1+2\alpha)(x^{1+\alpha}t_{1}^{\alpha}-x^{\alpha}t_{1}^{1+\alpha}).

Differentiating, we get

f^{\prime}(x)=(1+2\alpha)\,x^{2\alpha}-(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)\,x^{\alpha}t_{1}^{\alpha}+\alpha(1+2\alpha)\,\frac{t_{1}^{1+\alpha}}{x^{1-\alpha}}.

We define

g(x):=x^{1-\alpha}f^{\prime}(x)=(1+2\alpha)\,x^{1+\alpha}-(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)\,xt_{1}^{\alpha}+\alpha(1+2\alpha)\,t_{1}^{1+\alpha}

and by differentiating

g^{\prime}(x)=(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)\big(x^{\alpha}-t_{1}^{\alpha}\big).

For all
x>t_{1}
, we have
g^{\prime}(x)>0
.
Thus
g^{\prime}
is a strictly increasing function and since
g(t_{1})=0
, we have that
g
is strictly positive on
]t_{1},+\infty[
.
The same goes for
f^{\prime}
and thus
f
is strictly increasing on
]t_{1},+\infty[
.
Since
f(t_{1})=0
,
f(x)>0
for all
x>t_{1}
.
Therefore the first inequality is true.
The other inequality is shown similarly.
∎

Visualizing Non-linear Prognosis: A Vital Tool for Shared Decision-making in Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage
Authors: Andreas K. DEMETRIADES
Published: 2026

The review by Ikawa et al.,1) “Chronological and Biological Age in Predicting Outcomes of Older Patients in Neurosurgery”, was very interesting to read. The authors' synthesis of frailty indices and outcome prediction models provides a crucial framework for managing the increasingly complex geriatric neurosurgical population. Among their valuable contributions, we were particularly impressed by the innovative visualization of the non-linear relationship between age and poor outcome in aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) using generalized additive models (GAMs) and partial dependence plots.

This methodological advancement represents a significant step beyond traditional logistic regression. The intuitive graphical output transforms complex statistical findings into a format that is immediately accessible at the bedside. Effective communication of prognosis is a cornerstone of shared decision-making, especially in critical conditions like aSAH, where families face high-stakes, time-sensitive choices. Visual tools of this nature can mitigate the well-documented inaccuracy of unaided clinical prognostication and help align expectations with evidence-based realities, thereby fostering more authentic patient- and family-centered care.2)

The potential clinical utility of this approach is substantial. We echo the authors' sentiment that such visualizations can guide clinical decision-making and better inform patients and families. To build upon this excellent work, we propose 2 constructive avenues. First, the development of interactive, digital interfaces based on these models (integrated into electronic health records or as standalone applications) could allow clinicians to generate personalized visual risk estimates in real time.3) Second, applying similar non-linear visualization techniques to other critical outcomes in geriatric neurosurgery, such as long-term functional dependency or discharge disposition, could further enhance personalized care planning.

The movement toward dynamic, visually intuitive prognostication is supported by broader trends in health care communication and predictive analytics.4) As the field advances, combining such transparent models with validated frailty assessments will be paramount for optimizing outcomes and resource allocation for our aging population. The successful implementation of such tools requires attention to how clinicians and patients interpret visual risk data, ensuring it reduces rather than compounds uncertainty.5)

Ikawa and colleagues are commended for their pioneering work and encouraged to continue efforts to translate these sophisticated predictive models into practical tools that bridge the gap between data science and compassionate clinical practice. Future research should focus on measuring how the integration of such visual prognostic tools directly impacts the quality of shared decision-making, patient comprehension, and clinician confidence in the neurovascular setting.6)

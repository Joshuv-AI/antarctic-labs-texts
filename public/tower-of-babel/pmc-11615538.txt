ABSTRACT
AbstractThis dataset was collected during the 2023 and 2024 RoboCup competitions using the TIAGo robot equipped with an RGB-D camera, a Hokuyo laser, and a RODE microphone. The dataset includes ROSbag files that capture the robot's sensory data and planning task behavior, as well as video recordings that provide third-person perspectives of task execution. These data provide information about the performance of autonomous robots in social tasks and navigation in dynamic environments with human interaction.Keywords: Social robots, Human-robot interaction, Robotics competition, Sensor fusion, Perception, ROS 2

Background

The RoboCup [1] competition provides a testbed for autonomous robots to perform complex tasks in real-world environments, such as assisting humans and managing home tasks. Specifically, this dataset was collected from the participation in the RoboCup@Home Open Platform League (OLP), where robots are tested in domestic environments to evaluate their ability to autonomously perform different tasks, including perception, attention, navigation, object manipulation, and human interaction. Detailed sensor data was collected through ROSbag files, complemented by video recordings, allowing for a thorough examination of the robot's behavior and decision-making processes. The 2023 and 2024 datasets provide an opportunity to observe the evolution of robot performance over time, facilitating the study of improvements and advancements in robot capabilities.

Data Description

The dataset consists of recordings from the RoboCup 2023 and 2024 competitions, stored in ROSbag format. A ROSbag is a file format in ROS 2 used to record and replay topics, which are streams of data passed between the components of a robotic system. These files contain raw and processed data, capturing all messages exchanged during the robot's tasks, such as sensor inputs, control commands, and system logs.

Each ROSbag in this dataset contains the following topics, recorded in real-time as the robot performed the tasks:

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The tests were performed in the RoboCup@Home arena (Fig. 4), which was designed to simulate a home environment where service robots assist with daily tasks. The arena was divided into four rooms: living room, kitchen, office, and hallway. The arena had two entrance doors: one leading to the hallway, and the other to the office. The instruction point was located in the living room, where the robot waited for instructions from the operator. The kitchen area contained a table and a storage cabinet, which were used to simulate object manipulation tasks.

Left: Map of the arena divided into rooms. Right: TIAGo in the arena during the competition.

The robot used to collect the dataset was the TIAGo robot, a service robot designed for indoor environments by PAL Robotics. TIAGo is based on ROS 2, which provides integration for robot control and sensor data acquisition. Equipped with an extendable torso and a manipulator arm, TIAGo was able to perform perception, navigation, and manipulation tasks in the RoboCup@Home environment.

The TIAGo robot has the following components that were used for data collection:

•Mobile base: A differential drive base providing velocity control for movement. The base has a maximum speed of 1.5 m/s. sensors include:○Odometry: To track the robot's position and movement over time.○Hokuyo laser scanner: A 2D laser scanner used for mapping and obstacle detection with a range of up to 5.6 m.

Sensor data was recorded in ROSbag format at the start of each task and continued until the task was completed. The following command was used to capture the data:

ros2 bag record -o <rosbag_name> /cmd_vel /head_front_camera/rgb/image_raw /head_front_camera/depth/image_raw /head_front_camera/rgb/camera_info /imu /joint_states /odom /rosout /say_text /scan /tf /tf_static /whisper/text

This command records messages for all selected topics, including velocity commands, camera images, IMU data, laser scans, etc., ensuring that all aspects of the robot's interaction with its environment are recorded.

Limitations

The main limitation of this dataset is the high computational and storage requirements of the tasks performed. Due to the complexity and extended duration of the experiments, recording the ROSbags for the entire duration resulted in large data files, reaching several gigabytes. This created storage challenges, as the computer used for the experiments could not handle the size of the continuously generated data. As a result, ROSbags had to be transferred to external storage devices during the competition to free up space, which proved challenging in a time-sensitive environment. In addition, not all topics available in the system were recorded because it was not practical to manage the huge volume of data in real-time.

Ethics Statement

The authors have read and followed the ethical requirements for publication in Data in Brief. The current work does not involve human subjects, animal experiments, or data collected from social media platforms. Participants in the RoboCup competition had already provided informed consent to be recorded, as this is a condition of their registration for the event. This consent includes the use of audio, video, and photographic material for research and publication purposes, as managed by the organizers of RoboCup. Additionally, videos in which recognizable individuals appear have been blurred with a masking effect to protect their privacy.

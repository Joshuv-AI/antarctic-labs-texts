Abstract
. We study the behavior of weighted residual polynomials on circular arcs, including weighted Chebyshev polynomials. For weights given by reciprocals of polynomials, we establish Szegő–Widom asymptotics. Extending our analysis to less regular weights, we determine the asymptotic behavior of the corresponding weighted Widom factors, generalizing results by Eichinger and Thiran et al. As an application, we derive the asymptotics of Widom factors on certain lemniscatic arcs.



Keywords
Residual polynomials, Circular arcs, Weight functions, Widom factors, Chebyshev polynomials

1.
Introduction

Given a set
{\mathsf{E}}\subset\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
, where
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}:={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
denotes the Riemann sphere, we define
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}
as the supremum norm, meaning that for any function
f:{\mathsf{E}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
,

\|f\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}:=\sup_{z\in{\mathsf{E}}}|f(z)|.

The central object in this work is
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
, the set of algebraic polynomials of degree at most
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
For every pair consisting of a polynomially convex compact set
{\mathsf{E}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
(i.e., a compact set such that its complement
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathsf{E}}
is connected) and a fixed bounded weight function
w:{\mathsf{E}}\to[0,\infty)
, we can associate a sequence of
weighted residual polynomials
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
z_{0}\in\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus{\mathsf{E}}
, we will use the notation
R_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(\cdot,z_{0})
to denote the unique polynomial of degree at most
n
satisfying

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad\|wR_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(\cdot,z_{0})\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}=\sup_{z\in{\mathsf{E}}}\left|w(z)R_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(z,z_{0})\right|\leq 1,\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad R_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(z_{0},z_{0})=\sup\bigl\{|P(z_{0})|:P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n},\,\|wP\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}\leq 1\bigr\}.

For a polynomial
P
, the value
P(\infty)
is to be understood as its leading coefficient. The normalization is specified so that the residual polynomial is positive at the point
z_{0}
. For a proof of existence and uniqueness, we refer the reader to
[
13
, Theorem 2.1 b)]
.

Alternatively, the characterization in
(i)
–
(ii)
can be reformulated in its dual form: determine the polynomial that minimizes
\|wP\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}
among all polynomials
P
satisfying
P(z_{0})=1

In fact, this polynomial is given by

(1.1)

T_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(z,z_{0})=\frac{R_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(z,z_{0})}{R_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(z_{0},z_{0})},

and we note that

(1.2)

\|wT_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(\cdot,z_{0})\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}=\frac{1}{R_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(z_{0},z_{0})}.

The polynomials
T_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}:=T_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}(\cdot,\infty)

Let
\Omega

\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
and let
\zeta\in\Omega

\partial\Omega
is non-polar, meaning it has positive logarithmic capacity. Then
\Omega

\zeta

g_{\Omega}(\cdot,\zeta)

The harmonic measure of a Borel set

with respect to the base point
\zeta
is denoted by
\omega(\zeta,I;\Omega)
. Recall that if
f:\partial\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous, then the function
h:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

(1.4)

h(z):=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(x)\,\omega(z,dx;\Omega)

is harmonic in
\Omega
and assumes the boundary value
f(x)
at all regular points of
\partial\Omega
.
In the specific case where
\Omega:=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus{\mathsf{E}}
is the complement of a compact, non-polar, and polynomially convex set
{\mathsf{E}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, it follows that the boundary of
\Omega
coincides with that of
{\mathsf{E}}
. This allows the Green’s function for
\Omega

{\mathsf{E}}

(1.5)

g_{\Omega}(z,\infty)=\int\log|z-x|\,d\mu_{\mathsf{E}}(x)-\log\operatorname{Cap}({\mathsf{E}}),\quad z\in\Omega,

where
\mu_{\mathsf{E}}
is the equilibrium measure of
{\mathsf{E}}
and
\operatorname{Cap}({\mathsf{E}})
is its logarithmic capacity. The equilibrium measure, in turn, coincides with the harmonic measure at
\zeta=\infty
for the domain
\Omega
.

Introducing the notation

(1.6)

\Omega_{\alpha}:=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\Gamma_{\alpha}\;\mbox{ and }\;{\mathbb{C}}_{+}:=\{z:\operatorname{Re}z>0\},

First, we extend this result to weighted polynomials, where the weight is the multiplicative inverse of a polynomial. Ultimately, this will enable us to prove that for
u\in\Omega_{\alpha}
and a general class of weights, whose properties are detailed in Theorem
4.10
,

(1.10)

\|wT_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(\cdot,u)\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\sim e^{-ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)}k_{{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\bigl(\lambda(u),\lambda(u)\bigr)^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u,dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

In the special case
u=\infty
, we obtain the asymptotic formula

(1.11)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|wT_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}}{\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{n}}=2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(x)\right).

This should be compared with the unweighted (
w\equiv 1
) asymptotic formula

(1.12)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|T_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}}{\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{n}}=2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)

that was derived in
[
43
]
.
A deeper understanding of the monotonicity properties of this convergence was later obtained through
[
38
, Theorem 8]
. The context for this result is historically significant. For a bounded upper semicontinuous function
w:\Gamma\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with
\log w\in L^{1}(\mu_{\Gamma})
, Widom established the general upper bound
[
45
, Theorem 11.4]
:

(1.13)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|wT_{n}^{\Gamma,w}\|_{\Gamma}}{\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma)^{n}}\leq 2\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma}\log w(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma}(x)\right).

In the unweighted case, the integral vanishes, reducing this bound to 2. Motivated by this and the known result for an interval, Widom conjectured that for any sufficiently smooth Jordan arc
\Gamma
, the limit would always be exactly 2, i.e., that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T_{n}^{\Gamma}\|_{\Gamma}/\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma)^{n}=2
. The example under discussion is notable because it was the first to disprove this conjecture.

In sharp contrast to Widom’s conjecture, a recent result by Alpan
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
proves that the inequality in (
1.13
) is strict if there is an interior point
z\in\Gamma
(i.e., a point other than its endpoints) for which

(1.14)

\frac{\partial g_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}}{\partial n_{+}}(z,\infty)\neq\frac{\partial g_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}}{\partial n_{-}}(z,\infty),\quad\Omega_{\Gamma}=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\Gamma.

Here, the normal derivatives are taken with respect to the two different sides of the arc. As later observed in
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, the only arc for which equality in (
1.14
) holds at all interior points is a straight line segment, which therefore constitutes the sole arc where Widom’s conjecture is valid.

When it comes to lower bounds, we may also consider any weight function
w:{\mathsf{E}}\to[0,\infty)
for which
\log w\in L^{1}(\mu_{\mathsf{E}})
. In this case, Szegő’s inequality holds:

(1.15)

\|wT_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}\geq\operatorname{Cap}({\mathsf{E}})^{n}\exp\left(\int_{{\mathsf{E}}}\log w(x)\,d\mu_{\mathsf{E}}(x)\right),

as shown in
[
31
, Theorem 13]
. Based on (
1.13
) and (
1.15
), it is natural to introduce the
weighted Widom factors
as

(1.16)

\mathcal{W}_{n}({\mathsf{E}},w):=\frac{\|wT_{n}^{{\mathsf{E}},w}\|_{{\mathsf{E}}}}{\operatorname{Cap}({\mathsf{E}})^{n}}.

The unweighted Widom factors, corresponding to
w\equiv 1
, will be denoted by
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}({\mathsf{E}})
. For an arc
\Gamma
, combining (
1.13
) and (
1.15
) implies that all limit points of
\mathcal{W}_{n}(\Gamma,w)
lie within the range

(1.17)

\left[\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma}\log w(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma}(x)\right),2\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma}\log w(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma}(x)\right)\right].

We emphasize that exact formulas for limit points of the Widom factors associated with arcs are scarce in the literature. Notably, the case of an interval remains the only thoroughly studied example, largely due to the seminal work of Bernstein
[
6
,
7
]
. For the norms of unweighted Chebyshev polynomials relative to an arc, the only case understood beyond an interval is that of a circular arc.

Exploring the implications of the results presented here, as well as those in
[
16
]
, for analyzing convergence rates of Krylov subspace methods would be interesting but lies beyond the scope of this article. In particular, it would be of interest to investigate algorithms related to unitary matrices with restricted spectra. However, due to the numerical nature of such considerations, we have opted not to include them in the present discussion.

2.
Chebyshev polynomials on lemniscatic arcs

To illustrate the applicability of Theorem
4.10
, we derive asymptotic formulas for the Widom factors associated with a family of unions of arcs. These results complement the analysis in
[
10
]
.

As previously mentioned, when
\Gamma
is a smooth arc,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\Gamma,w)\leq 2\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma}\log w(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma}(x)\right),

with equality if and only if
\Gamma
is a straight line segment. This result is rooted in the fact that a straight line segment is the only Jordan arc
\Gamma
satisfying

(2.1)

\frac{\partial g_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}}{\partial n_{+}}(z,\infty)=\frac{\partial g_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}}{\partial n_{-}}(z,\infty),\quad\Omega_{\Gamma}=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\Gamma

at all interior points. The generalization of (
2.1
) to a system of arcs is given by the
S
-property, which is formally defined below in Definition
2.2
.

The results of
[
10
]
suggest that the
S
-property is the key mechanism behind large limits of Widom factors, specifically leading to a limit of 2. In this section, we provide further evidence for this by examining a family of unions of arcs where the
S
-property fails and show that their corresponding Widom factors are asymptotically strictly less than 2.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)=\{z:z^{m}+1\in r\Gamma_{\alpha}\}
, where
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
r>0
, and
0<\alpha<\pi

0\leq l<m
, we have

(2.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{nm+l}\bigl({\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)\bigr)=2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)\,c(r,\alpha)^{l/m}

where

(2.4)

c(r,\alpha)=\frac{|1-r|+\sqrt{1-2r\cos(\alpha)+r^{2}}}{2r\sin(\alpha/2)}.

In particular, for the connected case where
r=1
, we have

(2.5)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\bigl({\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)\bigr)=2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4).

This theorem is significant because, as we will show in Proposition
2.3
, the connected set
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
does not satisfy the
S
-property. The explicit formula (
2.5
) demonstrates that this absence of the
S
-property leads to a limit strictly less than 2, supporting the conjecture that the
S
-property is essential for Widom factors of connected sets to approach 2.

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

The proof proceeds in several steps. First, we formally define the
S
-property and prove that the set
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
does not possess it. Second, we use symmetry arguments to reduce the problem to an analysis of weighted Chebyshev polynomials on the circular arc
\Gamma_{\alpha}
. Finally, we compute the necessary potential-theoretic quantities to derive the asymptotic formula.

We begin with the formal definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathsf{E}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a compact set with
\operatorname{Cap}({\mathsf{E}})>0
, and suppose that
\Omega_{\mathsf{E}}=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus{\mathsf{E}}
is connected. Additionally, assume there exists a subset
{\mathsf{E}}_{0}\subset{\mathsf{E}}
with
\operatorname{Cap}({\mathsf{E}}_{0})=0
, such that

(2.6)

{\mathsf{E}}\setminus{\mathsf{E}}_{0}=\bigcup_{i\in I}\Gamma_{i},

where the
\Gamma_{i}
’s are disjoint open analytic Jordan arcs, and
I\subset{\mathbb{N}}
. We say that
{\mathsf{E}}
satisfies the
S
-property
if

(2.7)

\frac{\partial g_{\Omega_{\mathsf{E}}}}{\partial n_{+}}(z,\infty)=\frac{\partial g_{\Omega_{\mathsf{E}}}}{\partial n_{-}}(z,\infty)

holds for all
z\in{\mathsf{E}}\setminus{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
, where
n_{+}
and
n_{-}
denote the unit normals from each side of the arcs.

The
S
-property is closely related to capacity minimization, and we refer the reader to
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
for further details on this. In
[
10
]
, the Chebyshev polynomials associated with the
m
-stars defined by
{\mathsf{E}}_{m}:=\{z:z^{m}\in[-2,2]\}
are studied. These
m
-stars serve as examples of sets with the
S
-property, and it is shown that
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}({\mathsf{E}}_{m})\rightarrow 2
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for any
m
. This behavior is analogous to that of Jordan arcs satisfying the
S
-property (i.e., straight line segments).

In order to show that
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
does not satisfy (
2.7
) at interior points of the subarcs, we utilize the connection to Chebotarev sets. Given
N
distinct points
a_{1}\dotsc,a_{N}
in the complex plane, there is a unique continuum (i.e., a compact connected set) of minimal logarithmic capacity containing these points. This set is known as the Chebotarev set for
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\}
, with relevant references detailing its properties found in
[
42
,
22
,
36
,
37
,
25
,
17
,
29
,
32
]
.
It is known (see
[
42
]
) that every Chebotarev set necessarily has the form specified in Definition
2.2
and therefore satisfies the
S
-property. Conversely, a compact set with the specified structure of analytic arcs that satisfies the
S
-property must be the Chebotarev set for some finite collection of points, as can be inferred from the discussion following Problem 2 and Theorem 11 in
[
42
]
.
In our case, this implies the following: if
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
were to satisfy (
2.7
) away from the points
\{0\}\cup\{z:z^{m}+1\in e^{\pm i\alpha}\}
, it would necessarily coincide with the Chebotarev set associated with these points, excluding the point at zero.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
0<\alpha<\pi
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the set
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
, as defined in (
2.2
), does not satisfy the
S
-property.

Proof.

The proof is carried out by showing that
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
is
not
the Chebotarev set for the points
\{z:z^{m}+1\in e^{\pm i\alpha}\}
. To see this, let
\mathcal{C}(e^{\pm i\alpha},1)
denote the Chebotarev set for
\{e^{\pm i\alpha},1\}
. By
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
, this set is necessarily contained within the convex hull of these points. Since this does not hold for
\Gamma_{\alpha}
, we conclude that
\Gamma_{\alpha}\neq\mathcal{C}(e^{\pm i\alpha},1)
, which in turn implies

\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})>\operatorname{Cap}\bigl(\mathcal{C}(e^{\pm i\alpha},1)\bigr).

Furthermore, the set
\{z:z^{m}+1\in\mathcal{C}(e^{\pm i\alpha},1)\}
forms a continuum (since
\mathcal{C}(e^{\pm i\alpha},1)
contains the critical value of
z^{m}+1
). Applying
[
34
, Theorem 5.2.5]
twice, we obtain

\operatorname{Cap}\bigl(\{z:z^{m}+1\in\mathcal{C}(e^{\pm i\alpha},1)\}\bigr)=\operatorname{Cap}\bigl(\mathcal{C}(e^{\pm i\alpha},1)\bigr)^{1/m}<\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{1/m}=\operatorname{Cap}\bigl({\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)\bigr).

Thus,
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
is not the minimal capacity set containing
\{z:z^{m}+1\in e^{\pm i\alpha}\}
and consequently does not satisfy the
S
-property.
∎

Having established that
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
lacks the S-property, we now proceed with the main asymptotic calculation for the Widom factors. The argument begins by exploiting the rotational symmetry of the set
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)
. This set is invariant under rotations by
2\pi/m
-radians. As a result, the corresponding Chebyshev polynomial of degree
nm+l
, where
0\leq l<m
, satisfies the symmetry relation

T_{nm+l}^{{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)}(e^{2\pi i/m}z)=e^{2\pi il/m}T_{nm+l}^{{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)}(z).

This implies that
T_{nm+l}^{{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)}(z)=z^{l}Q_{n}(z^{m}+1)
, where
Q_{n}
is a monic polynomial of degree
n
. By applying the change of variables
z=(r\zeta-1)^{1/m}
, we find that
Q_{n}(r\zeta)=r^{n}\tilde{Q}_{n}(\zeta)
, where
\tilde{Q}_{n}
is the monic polynomial of degree
n
that minimizes the norm on
\Gamma_{\alpha}
with respect to the weight function
w_{r,m,l}(\zeta)=|r\zeta-1|^{l/m}
. In other words,
\tilde{Q}_{n}(\zeta)=T_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w_{r,m,l}}(\zeta)=:T_{n}^{w_{r,m,l}}(\zeta)
, and

\|T_{nm+l}^{{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)}\|_{{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)}=r^{n}\|w_{r,m,l}T_{n}^{w_{r,m,l}}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}.

The asymptotic behavior of the right-hand side is given by Theorem
4.10
, which yields

(2.8)

\|w_{r,m,l}T_{n}^{w_{r,m,l}}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\sim 2\cos(\alpha/4)^{2}\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{n}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{r,m,l}(\zeta)\,d\mu_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(\zeta)\right).

To apply this formula, we must evaluate the integral in the exponential term, which is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
r>0
, the logarithmic potential is given by the identity

(2.9)

\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log|r\zeta-1|\,d\mu_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(\zeta)=\log\left(\frac{|1-r|+\sqrt{1-2r\cos(\alpha)+r^{2}}}{2}\right).

Proof.

The case
r=1
is a direct consequence of Frostman’s theorem
[
34
, Theorem 3.3.4]
, noting that
\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})=\sin(\alpha/2)
.

For
r\neq 1
, the integral equals
h_{r}(\infty)
, where
h_{r}
is the solution to the Dirichlet problem on
\Omega_{\alpha}=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\Gamma_{\alpha}
with boundary data
\log|r\zeta-1|
. From potential theory, we have

(2.10)

h_{r}(\infty)=\log r+\log\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})+g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(1/r,\infty),

where
g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(z,\infty)
denotes the Green’s function for
\Omega_{\alpha}
with pole at infinity. Recalling that the function

f(z)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(z-1+\sqrt{(z-e^{i\alpha})(z-e^{-i\alpha})}\,\Bigr),

with the branch choice of
\sqrt{\cdot}
such that
f(z)=z+O(1)
as
z\to\infty
, maps
\Omega_{\alpha}
conformally onto
\{w:|w|>\sin(\alpha/2)\}
(cf.
[
34
, Exercise 5.2.4]
), the Green’s function is given by
g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(z,\infty)=\log\left({|f(z)|}/{\sin(\alpha/2)}\right)
.
Evaluating at
z=1/r
yields

(2.11)

g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(1/r,\infty)=\log\left(\frac{|1-r|+\sqrt{1-2r\cos(\alpha)+r^{2}}}{2r\sin(\alpha/2)}\right).

Substituting this and
\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})=\sin(\alpha/2)
into (
2.10
) proves the identity stated in the lemma. The continuity of the expression at
r=1
confirms consistency.
∎

It follows directly from (
2.11
) that the quantity
c(r,\alpha)
defined in (
2.4
) is nothing but the exponential of
g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(1/r,\infty)
.
We now have all the necessary components to complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Using the capacity formula
\operatorname{Cap}({\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha))=(r\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha}))^{1/m}
, we have

{\mathcal{W}}_{nm+l}\bigl({\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)\bigr)=\frac{\|T_{nm+l}^{{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)}\|_{{\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)}}{\operatorname{Cap}\bigl({\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)\bigr)^{nm+l}}=\frac{r^{n}\|w_{r,m,l}T_{n}^{w_{r,m,l}}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}}{\bigl(r\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})\bigr)^{(nm+l)/m}}.

Substituting the asymptotic formula (
2.8
) and the result of Lemma
2.4
, and taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{nm+l}\bigl({\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r^{n}\cdot 2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{n}\exp\left(\frac{l}{m}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log|r\zeta-1|\,d\mu_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(\zeta)\right)}{r^{n+l/m}\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{n+l/m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)}{r^{l/m}\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{l/m}}\bigl(r\sin(\alpha/2)\,c(r,\alpha)\bigr)^{l/m}.

Since
\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})=\sin(\alpha/2)
, the expression simplifies to
2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)c(r,\alpha)^{l/m}
,
which is the statement of the theorem. ∎

2.3.
Analysis of the Limit Points

It is illuminating to examine the range of the limit points for different values of
r
. The key is to understand the behavior of the function
r\mapsto c(r,\alpha)
for a fixed
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
. Since
c(r,\alpha)>0
, the sign of
\partial_{r}c(r,\alpha)
is determined by its logarithmic derivative. A calculation shows that the derivative vanishes only if
r\in\{0,1\}
or
\alpha=\pi k
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. In our setting, the derivative is continuous and does not vanish on
(0,1)
or
(1,\infty)
, so it must have a constant sign on each of these intervals. We conclude that
\partial_{r}c(r,\alpha)
is negative for
0<r<1
and positive for
r>1
.
Moreover, since

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}c(r,\alpha)=\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}c(r,\alpha)=\frac{1}{\sin(\alpha/2)},

it follows that
c(\cdot,\alpha)
maps
(0,1)
onto
(1,\infty)
and
(1,\infty)
onto
\bigl(1,\csc(\alpha/2)\bigr)
. Consequently, for
0\leq l<m
, the limit points of the Widom factors lie in the ranges:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{nm+l}\bigl({\mathsf{E}}_{m,r}(\alpha)\bigr)\in\begin{cases}[2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4),2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)\csc^{\frac{l}{m}}(\alpha/2)],&r>1,\\
\qquad\qquad\quad[2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4),\infty),&0<r<1.\end{cases}

A key consequence of this analysis is the distinction in convergence behavior between the connected and disconnected cases. When
r=1
, we have
c(1,\alpha)=1
, which makes the limit in Theorem
2.1
independent of the subsequence index
l
. This implies that the full sequence
\{{\mathcal{W}}_{n}({\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha))\}
converges. In contrast, for
r\neq 1
, the limit depends on the residue
l\equiv n\pmod{m}
. The full sequence therefore does not converge; instead, it possesses
m
distinct limit points. This type of behavior, often called limit periodicity, is characteristic of symmetric systems where each disconnected component carries the same harmonic measure as viewed from infinity.

As a final observation, Theorem
2.1
shows that for the connected case, the limit of
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}({\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha))
always lies within the interval
(1,2)
. As
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, the set
{\mathsf{E}}_{m,1}(\alpha)
increasingly resembles a set of intersecting straight lines, and correspondingly, the limit of its Widom factors approaches
2
. This behavior should be compared with the results of
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Let

. The functions
z\mapsto\omega_{n}(z,I_{n})
are positive harmonic functions in the lower half-plane. By Harnack’s inequality
[
34
, Corollary 1.3.3]
, there exists a constant
M\geq 1
, independent of
n
, such that

(3.22)

M^{-1}\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n})\leq\omega_{n}(\overline{z}_{j},I_{n})\leq M\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n}),\quad j=1,\dotsc,k.

Combining (
3.22
) with the identity (
3.12
) yields

\displaystyle 1=(n-k+1)\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\omega_{n}(\overline{z}_{j},I_{n})\geq\left(n-k+1+kM^{-1}\right)\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n}).

This implies
\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n})\leq(n-k+1+kM^{-1})^{-1}
, and thus

(3.23)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n})=0.

To show that
x_{n}\to 0
, we define the function
f(x):=\omega(z_{\infty},[-x,x];\Omega_{x})
for
0<x<1
, where
\Omega_{x}:=\Omega_{0}\setminus[-x,x]
. When
a\leq b
, we have
\Omega_{b}\subset\Omega_{a}
, and by the domain monotonicity principle for harmonic measure
[
34
, Corollary 4.3.9]
, it follows that
\omega(z_{\infty},\mathsf{A}_{0};\Omega_{b})\leq\omega(z_{\infty},\mathsf{A}_{0};\Omega_{a})
. Since
\omega(z_{\infty},[-x,x];\Omega_{x})=1-\omega(z_{\infty},\mathsf{A}_{0};\Omega_{x})
, this yields
f(a)=\omega(z_{\infty},[-a,a];\Omega_{a})\leq\omega(z_{\infty},[-b,b];\Omega_{b})=f(b)
. Thus,
f(x)
is a non-decreasing function of
x
. Since
f
is positive and non-decreasing, the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n})=0
, shown in (
3.23
), implies that
x_{n}\to 0
.

Finally, combining the upper bound for
\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n})
derived from (
3.22
) with the corresponding lower bound, we find

\frac{1}{n-k+1+kM}\leq\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n-k+1+kM^{-1}}.

This implies
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},I_{n})=1
. Let
\nu_{n}:=n\omega_{n}(z_{\infty},dx)|_{I_{n}}
. Then
\{\nu_{n}\}
is a sequence of probability measures with
\mathrm{supp}(\nu_{n})=I_{n}=[-x_{n},x_{n}]
. Since
x_{n}\to 0
, the supports shrink to the origin. This, combined with the total mass converging to 1, implies the weak-star convergence
\nu_{n}\xrightarrow{*}\delta_{0}
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

The limiting behavior of the functions
s_{n}
defined in (
3.15
) can now be established.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
B_{n}^{\Omega_{n}}
,
s_{n}
, and
B_{n}^{\Omega_{0}}
be defined by (
3.14
), (
3.15
), and (
3.17
), respectively, and let
s
be defined by (
3.20
). Then

(3.24)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}s_{n}(z)^{2}=s(z)^{2}=\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{z_{0}^{2}-1}\frac{z^{2}-1}{z^{2}}

and

(3.25)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B_{n}^{\Omega_{0}}(z)\frac{1-s_{n}(z)}{2}B_{n}^{\Omega_{n}}(z)\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{z-z_{\infty}}\right)^{n-k+1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{z-z_{j}}{z-\overline{z}_{j}}=0,

uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega_{0}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

It is clear that (
3.24
) follows directly from Lemma
3.4
and the definition of
s_{n}
.
To prove (
3.25
), note that for
j=0,1,\dotsc,k
, the function

|b_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\overline{z}_{j}){(z-z_{j})}/{(z-\overline{z}_{j})}|
has boundary values at most
1

on
\partial\Omega_{n}
. By the maximum modulus principle, this implies that

\left|b_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\overline{z}_{j})\frac{z-z_{j}}{z-\overline{z}_{j}}\right|<1,\quad z\in\Omega_{n},\quad j=0,1,\dotsc,k.

Since
B_{n}^{\Omega_{0}}(z)\to 0
uniformly on every compact subset of
\Omega_{0}
as
n\to\infty
, the result follows.
∎

We are now in position to analyze the limit points of the sequence
\{f_{n}\}
defined in (
3.18
) and show that the full sequence converges uniformly on compact subsets of the domain
\Omega_{0}
.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

We begin by noting that the right-hand side of (
3.19
) is analytic in
\Omega_{0}
. This follows from the fact that the simple pole of
s(z)
at
z=0
is canceled by the zero of
b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,0)
, while the simple pole at
z=z_{\infty}
also vanishes due to the identity
s(z_{\infty})=-1
.

For
z,\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\log\left|\frac{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\zeta)}{b_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\zeta)}\right|=g_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\zeta)-g_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\zeta)=-\int_{-x_{n}}^{x_{n}}g_{\Omega_{0}}(z,x)\,\omega(\zeta,dx;\Omega_{n}).

As in the proof of Lemma
3.4
, it follows that
\omega(\zeta,I_{n};\Omega_{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Together with the boundedness of
x\mapsto g_{\Omega_{0}}(z,x)
on
I_{n}
for fixed
z
, this implies

\log\left|\frac{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\zeta)}{b_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\zeta)}\right|\longrightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

In particular, for
j=0,1,\ldots,k
, we obtain

|b_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\overline{z}_{j})|\rightarrow|b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\zeta)|\;\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Applying (
3.21
), we further deduce that

\log\left|\frac{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,z_{\infty})}{b_{\Omega_{n}}(z,z_{\infty})}\right|^{n-k}=-\int_{-x_{n}}^{x_{n}}g_{\Omega_{0}}(z,x)(n-k)\omega(z_{\infty},dx;\Omega_{n})\longrightarrow-g_{\Omega_{0}}(z,0)\;\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

By combining this with (
3.13
) and Lemma
3.5
, we conclude that

|f_{n}(z)|=\left|B_{n}^{\Omega_{0}}(z)R_{n}^{\mathsf{A}_{0},W_{n}}(z,z_{0})W_{n}(z)\right|\longrightarrow\left|\frac{1+s(z)}{2}\frac{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,0)}{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,z_{\infty})}\right|\;\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

The limit in (
3.19
) now follows from the normal family property of
\{f_{n}\}
or directly from
[
11
, Prop. 4.2]
, given that
f_{n}(z_{0})>0
for all
n
and

\frac{1+s(z_{0})}{2}\frac{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z_{0},0)}{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z_{0},z_{\infty})}>0

by our normalization. ∎

Our focus is on weighted polynomials associated with circular arcs, which leads us to the task of transferring asymptotic limits back to
\Omega_{\alpha}
.
Since
u
is a conformal mapping, and given that both
b_{\Omega_{0}}(z_{0},z_{\infty})
and
b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(0,\infty)
are positive, it follows that

(3.26)

b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\zeta)=b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}\bigl(u(z),u(\zeta)\bigr),\quad z,\zeta\in\Omega_{0}.

Combining this with Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

(3.27)

b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}\bigl(u(z),\infty\bigr)^{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}\bigl(u(z),0\bigr)w\bigl(u(z)\bigr)=b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,z_{\infty})^{n}R_{n}^{\mathsf{A}_{0},W_{n}}(z,z_{0})W_{n}(z).

A direct computation using (
3.5
) and (
3.7
) yields the expression

(3.28)

s(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\bigl(u(z)-e^{i\alpha}\bigr)\bigl(u(z)-e^{-i\alpha}\bigr)}}{u(z)+1},

where the branch of the square root is taken with a cut along
\Gamma_{\alpha}
and is normalized to be
1
at
u(z)=0
. Applying Proposition
3.3
— where the core asymptotic analysis is carried out — and keeping (
3.17
)–(
3.18
) in mind, we conclude that as
n\to\infty
, the expression in (
3.27
) converges to

(3.29)

\qquad\frac{1+s(z)}{2}\frac{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,0)}{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,z_{\infty})}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,z_{\infty})}{b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\overline{z}_{j})}\\
=\frac{u(z)+1+\sqrt{\bigl(u(z)-e^{i\alpha}\bigr)\bigl(u(z)-e^{-i\alpha}\bigr)}}{2\bigl(u(z)+1\bigr)}\frac{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}\bigl(u(z),-1\bigr)}{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}\bigl(u(z),\infty\bigr)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}\bigl(u(z),\infty\bigr)}{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}\bigl(u(z),u_{j}\bigr)}.\qquad

All that remains is to divide by the weight and simplify.
The function

(3.30)

f(u):=\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)}{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,u_{j})}\biggr)\Big/w(u)

is analytic and non-vanishing in
\Omega_{\alpha}
, as poles and zeros cancel out. Consequently,
\log|f(u)|
is harmonic throughout
\Omega_{\alpha}
. By (
1.4
), so is the function

(3.31)

h(u):=-\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log|w(x)|\,\omega(u,dx,\Omega_{\alpha}).

Since both functions attain the same boundary values,
-\log|w(x)|
on
\Gamma_{\alpha}
, they must be identical by the maximum principle.
Let
\widetilde{h}
denote the harmonic conjugate of
h
, normalized to vanish at
u_{0}
. Then the analytic function

(3.32)

F_{w}(u,u_{0}):=\exp\Bigl[h(u)+i\widetilde{h}(u)\Bigr]

becomes positive at
u=u_{0}
. Since our normalization ensures that
f(0)>0
, it follows that

(3.33)

f(u)=F_{w}(u,0),\quad u\in\Omega_{\alpha}.

Thus, assembling our results, we arrive at the final asymptotic formula

(3.34)

b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)^{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(u,0)\longrightarrow\frac{u+1+\sqrt{(u-e^{i\alpha})(u-e^{-i\alpha})}}{2(u+1)}\frac{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,-1)}{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)}F_{w}(u,0),

which holds uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega_{\alpha}
as
n\to\infty
. Notably, the singularity at
u=-1
is exactly canceled by the zero of
b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,-1)
.

In the next section, specifically in Theorem
3.9
, we establish an asymptotic formula valid for general
u_{0}
. For now, we present a straightforward approach to obtaining the result for
u_{0}=\infty
.
The key idea is to consider the conjugate reciprocal polynomial, defined via the involution map
{}^{\ast}:\mathcal{P}_{n}\to\mathcal{P}_{n}
given by

P^{\ast}(u):=u^{n}\overline{P(1/\overline{u})}.

This transformation interchanges the constant term and the leading coefficient of
P
and
P^{*}
.
For convenience, we introduce the notation
u^{\ast}:=1/\overline{u}
and observe that
u^{*}=u
for
u\in\mathbb{T}
. Moreover, the mapping
u\mapsto u^{*}
defines a bijection of
\Omega_{\alpha}
onto itself. Applying this to the residual polynomials, we obtain

(3.35)

R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(\cdot,\infty)=\bigl(R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(\cdot,0)\bigr)^{\ast}.

The corresponding transformation of the Riemann maps follows from the fact that for each fixed
v\in\Omega_{\alpha}
, there exists
\gamma\in[0,2\pi)
such that

(3.36)

\overline{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u^{*},v)}=e^{i\gamma}b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,v^{*}).

This relation arises because the left-hand side defines an analytic map from
\Omega_{\alpha}
onto
\mathbb{D}
that sends
v^{*}
to
0
.
In particular, choosing the normalization
b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(\infty,0)>0
, we deduce that

(3.37)

\overline{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u^{*},\infty)}=b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,0).

Additionally, we note the identity

b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,0)=ub_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)
,
which follows from the maximum principle by considering the ratio of these functions and noting that both are positive at
u=\infty
. Furthermore, as explained just below (
3.2
), we have

(3.38)

b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(\infty,0)=\lim_{u\to\infty}ub_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)=\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})=\sin(\alpha/2).

Returning to the asymptotic formula, we now obtain

(3.39)

\qquad b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)^{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(u,\infty)=b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)^{n}u^{n}\overline{R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(u^{\ast},0)}\\
=b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,0)^{n}\overline{R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(u^{\ast},0)}=\overline{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u^{\ast},\infty)^{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(u^{\ast},0)},\qquad

which leads to the following result.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
w
be the weight function defined in (
3.8
). Then (
3.34
) holds, and with the normalization
b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(\infty,-1)>0
, we have

(3.40)

b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)^{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(u,\infty)\longrightarrow\frac{u+1-\sqrt{(u-e^{i\alpha})(u-e^{-i\alpha})}}{2(u+1)}\frac{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,-1)}{b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,0)}F_{w}(u,\infty)

uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega_{\alpha}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Using (
3.5
), we observe that replacing
u
with
u^{*}
corresponds to replacing
z
with
\overline{z}
. Recalling that
\overline{s(\overline{z})}=-s(z)
, we obtain the first factor on the right-hand side of (
3.40
). The remaining terms follow from (
3.36
)–(
3.37
), together with the fact that
F_{w}(\infty,\infty)
is positive.
∎

A key aspect of interest is determining the leading coefficient of
R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(\cdot,\infty)
, which equals
1/\|wT_{n}^{w}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}
. Using (
3.3
), we obtain

\frac{\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{n}}{\|wT_{n}^{w}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u,\infty)^{n}R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(u,\infty).

As a consequence of the previous proposition, we derive the following result.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
w
be the weight function defined in (
3.8
). Then

(3.41)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\Gamma_{\alpha},w)=2\cos(\alpha/4)^{2}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log|w(x)|\,d\mu_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(x)\right).

Proof.

It suffices to compute the limit of the right-hand side of (
3.40
) as
u\to\infty
. Since
s(z_{\infty})=-1
, the first factor tends to
1
.
To evaluate
b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(\infty,-1)
, we note that a direct calculation using the Joukowski transformation gives

b_{\Omega_{0}}(z,0)=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}{iz}=\frac{iz}{1+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}},

where the square root is taken with a branch cut along
\mathsf{A}_{0}
and chosen so that

.
Substituting

b_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(\infty,-1)=b_{\Omega_{0}}(z_{\infty},0)=\frac{\tan(\alpha/2)}{1+\sqrt{1+\tan^{2}(\alpha/2)}}=\frac{\sin(\alpha/2)}{2\cos^{2}(\alpha/4)}.

Finally, from (

since the normalization ensures that

Residual polynomials relative to an arbitrary point

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
w
be the weight function defined in (

, we have

Proof.

First, assume that

. By the normalization in (

. Hence, it follows from (

F_{w}(u_{0},u_{0})=\exp\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right),

we obtain (

).

The corresponding result for the exterior
\{u:|u|>1\}

. For

, the reflection formula (

Remark
.

This result can naturally be interpreted through the theory of Hardy spaces. To describe how this is done, we let
\Gamma
denote a Jordan arc of class
C^{1+\alpha}
with complement
\Omega
. If
\psi
denotes the conformal map from the exterior of the unit disk to
\Omega
mapping
\infty
to
\infty
then we let
H^{2}(\Omega)
consist of those functions
F
such that

F\circ\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Such an
F
has non-tangential boundary values from either side (a.e. with respect to arc-length) on
\Gamma
. We label these two boundary values by
F_{+}
and
F_{-}
. Given a point
z_{0}\in\Omega
, a norm on
H^{2}(\Omega)
is given by

\|F\|_{\omega(z_{0})}^{2}=\int_{\Gamma}\bigl(|F_{+}(x)|^{2}+|F_{-}(x)|^{2}\bigr)\omega(z_{0},dx,\Omega).

With this norm
H^{2}(\Omega)
becomes a reproducing kernel Hilbert space whose associated kernel is denoted by
k_{\omega(z_{0})}(z,\zeta)
. Classical Hilbert space theory shows that

\frac{1}{k_{\omega(z_{0})}(z_{0},z_{0})}=\inf\left\{\int_{\Gamma}(|F_{+}(x)|^{2}+|F_{-}(x)|^{2})\omega(z_{0},dx,\Omega):F\in H^{2}(\Omega),\,F(z_{0})=1\right\}.

This quantity appears in relation to weighted residual polynomials through
[
9
, Conjecture 1.7]
which hypothezises that if
\Gamma
is of class
C^{1+\alpha}
and
w
is an upper-semicontinuous weight function then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}e^{ng_{\Omega}(z_{0},\infty)}\|wT_{n}^{\Gamma,w}(\cdot,z_{0})\|_{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{k_{\omega(z_{0})}(z_{0},z_{0})}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma}\log w(x)\omega(z_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

In our current setting, where we investigate circular arcs,
[
8
, Theorem 5.4.10]
establishes the relation

k_{\omega(u_{0})}(u_{0},u_{0})=k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0}).

Hence, Theorem
3.10
proves the conjecture in the case of circular arcs and rational weights. Our aim is now to lift this result to a larger class of weight functions.

4.
General Weight functions

We now relax the assumptions on the weight function to extend formulas such as (
1.11
) to a broader class of weights. For clarity of presentation, we fix
u_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha}=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\Gamma_{\alpha}
and shift our focus from
R_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(\cdot,u_{0})
to
T_{n}^{\Gamma_{\alpha},w}(\cdot,u_{0})=:T_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})
. It should be stressed that, by (
1.1
), these are essentially the same object.

To begin, let
w:\Gamma_{\alpha}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a positive continuous function on
\Gamma_{\alpha}
. Suppose there exist polynomials
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, both nonvanishing on
{\mathbb{T}}
, such that

(4.1)

\frac{1}{|Q_{1}(u)|}\leq w(u)\leq\frac{1}{|Q_{2}(u)|}

for all
u\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
. Then it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\|T_{n}^{1/Q_{1}}(\cdot,u_{0})/Q_{1}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})/Q_{1}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\leq\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{n}^{1/Q_{2}}(\cdot,u_{0})w\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\leq\|T_{n}^{1/Q_{2}}(\cdot,u_{0})/Q_{2}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the polynomials
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
have all their zeros in the set
\{u:|u|>1\}
. To see that this assumption is non-restrictive, just observe that

|u-u_{j}|=|1-u\overline{u}_{j}|

for
u\in{\mathbb{T}}
. Consequently, if
P
is a polynomial with a zero of order
n
at
u_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
, the polynomial

\frac{P(u)(1-u\overline{u}_{0})^{n}}{(u-u_{0})^{n}}

has the same absolute value on
{\mathbb{T}}
but now has a zero at
1/\overline{u}_{0}
, which lies in the set
\{u:|u|>1\}
.

Now, let
\epsilon>0
be given. From Theorem
3.10
, we have

\|T_{n}^{1/Q_{k}}(\cdot,u_{0})/Q_{k}\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\sim e^{-ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log Q_{k}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right)

for
k\in\{1,2\}
.
By taking
n
large enough, we can ensure that

\qquad k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log Q_{1}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right)-\epsilon\leq\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}e^{ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}\\
\leq k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log Q_{2}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right)+\epsilon.\qquad

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, it follows that

(4.3)

k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log Q_{1}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}e^{ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}\\
\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}e^{ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}\leq k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(-\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log Q_{2}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

This holds for any choice of polynomials
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
satisfying (
4.1
). Our next step is to construct such polynomials so that the inequalities in (
4.1
) become approximate equalities.

Let
\epsilon>0
be chosen such that
0<2\epsilon<\min_{u\in\Gamma_{\alpha}}w(u)
. By the Stone–Weierstraß theorem, we can approximate the function
1/w
using polynomials in
u
and
\overline{u}
.
Specifically, there exist polynomials
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
satisfying

(4.4)

w(u)-2\epsilon<1/|P_{1}(u,\overline{u})|<w(u)-\epsilon\leq w(u)+\epsilon<1/|P_{2}(u,\overline{u})|<w(u)+2\epsilon

for all
u\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
.
Since
\overline{u}=u^{-1}
for
u\in{\mathbb{T}}
, the polynomials
P_{k}
(for
k=1,2
) can be rewritten as

P_{k}(u,\overline{u})=\sum_{l,j=1}^{m}a_{l,j}^{(k)}u^{l}\overline{u}^{j}=\sum_{l,j=1}^{m}a_{l,j}^{(k)}u^{l}u^{-j}=u^{-m}\sum_{l,j=1}^{m}a_{l,j}^{(k)}u^{l}u^{m-j}

for
u\in{\mathbb{T}}
.
Thus, defining

Q_{k}(u):=\sum_{l,j=1}^{m}a_{l,j}^{(k)}u^{l}u^{m-j},

we obtain polynomials
Q_{k}
whose absolute values on
{\mathbb{T}}
coincide with those of
P_{k}
.
Moreover, we may assume that
Q_{k}
is zero-free on
{\mathbb{T}}
. For if a zero appears, it can be perturbed by slightly dilating it with a factor
1+\delta
(for some
\delta>0
), which continuously changes the modulus of the polynomial. If this perturbation is sufficiently small, the inequalities in (
4.4
) remain valid, ensuring that

(4.5)

w(u)-2\epsilon<1/|Q_{1}(u)|<w(u)-\epsilon\leq w(u)+\epsilon<1/|Q_{2}(u)|<w(u)+2\epsilon.

Since this holds for all sufficiently small
\epsilon
, we can make the two integral in (
4.3
) arbitrarily close.
This leads to the asymptotic formula

(4.6)

\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\sim e^{-ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

By employing a similar approach, now approximating step functions from above and below, we can show that (
4.6
) holds for any Riemann integrable weight function
w
on
\Gamma_{\alpha}
that satisfies the additional condition that there exists a constant
M\geq 1
such that

(4.7)

1/M\leq w(u)\leq M

for
u\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
.
Since the necessary arguments closely follow those in
[
6
, Chapter I.7]
(see also
[
10
, Appendix A.2]
for a summary), we omit them here.

4.1.
Allowing for zeros

The most general form of our asymptotic formula in the context of circular arcs is given as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
w:\Gamma_{\alpha}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a weight function of the form

(4.8)

w(u)=w_{0}(u)\prod_{j=1}^{k}|u-u_{j}|^{s_{j}},

where
w_{0}
is a Riemann integrable function satisfying (
4.7
), and where
u_{j}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
and
s_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, for any
u_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha}
,

(4.9)

\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\sim e^{-ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

In particular,

(4.10)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(\Gamma_{\alpha},w)=2\cos(\alpha/4)^{2}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(x)\right).

Proof.

Similar to the approach in
[
7
]
, we show how an additional factor
|u-u_{j}|^{s_{j}}
can be incorporated into (
4.10
), under the assumption that (
4.10
) holds for
w=w_{0}
. The remaining result can then be proved by induction.

Let us begin by assuming that

(4.11)

w(u)=w_{0}(u)|u-u_{j}|,

where
w_{0}
is a weight for which (
4.10
) holds.
By incorporating the zero of the weight
w
into the polynomial, we find that if
u_{0}\neq\infty
, then

\qquad\frac{1}{|u_{0}-u_{j}|}\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\geq\|w_{0}T_{n+1}^{w_{0}}(\cdot,u_{0})\|\\
\sim e^{-(n+1)g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{0}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).\qquad

At the same time, it is clear that

\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,\infty)\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\geq\|w_{0}T_{n+1}^{w_{0}}(\cdot,\infty)\|\sim 2\cos(\alpha/4)^{2}\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})^{n+1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{0}(x)\,d\mu_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(x)\right).

The logarithmic integral of
w
can be split into two parts:

\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})=\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{0}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})+\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log|x-u_{j}|\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha}).

By
[
35
, Sect.II.4]
, we see that

(4.12)

\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log|x-u_{j}|\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right)=|u_{0}-u_{j}|e^{-g_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)},

which simplifies to
\operatorname{Cap}(\Gamma_{\alpha})
when
u_{0}=\infty
. This implies that for any
u_{0}\in\Omega_{\alpha}
, we have

(4.13)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}e^{ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}\geq k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

To estimate the corresponding
\limsup
, we define
w_{\epsilon}
as

(4.14)

w_{\epsilon}(u)=w_{0}(u)(|u-u_{j}|+\epsilon),

and observe that
w_{\epsilon}(u)>w(u)
for any
u\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
. Additionally,
w_{\epsilon}
satisfies the assumptions necessary to ensure that
(
4.6
) holds. Consequently, we have

\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\leq\|w_{\epsilon}T_{n}^{w_{\epsilon}}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\sim e^{-ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{\epsilon}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

As
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, the expression

\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{\epsilon}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})=\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{0}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})+\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log(|u-u_{j}|+\epsilon)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})

converges, by the monotone convergence theorem, to

\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha}).

We thus find that

(4.15)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}e^{ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}\leq k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

Together with (
4.13
), this establishes that

(4.16)

\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\sim e^{-ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).

The use of induction extends (
4.16
) to any weight of the form

(4.17)

w(u)=w_{0}(u)|u-u_{j}|^{m},

where
m
is a positive integer. Similarly, if
m
is a positive integer and the weight has the form

w(u)=w_{0}(u)|u-u_{j}|^{-m},

it follows that

T_{n+m}^{w}(u,u_{0})=\begin{cases}(u-u_{j})^{m}(u_{0}-u_{j})^{-m}T_{n}^{w_{0}}(u,u_{0}),&u_{0}\neq\infty,\\
\qquad\;(u-u_{j})^{m}T_{n}^{w_{0}}(u,\infty),&u_{0}=\infty.\end{cases}

Hence, a brief computation confirms the validity of (
4.16
) in this case as well.

To complete the proof, it remains to verify that (
4.16
) holds for weights of the form

(4.18)

w(u)=w_{0}(u)|u-u_{j}|^{s},

where
0<s<1
. To this end, we introduce the weight functions, parameterized by
\delta>0
:

w_{l}(u)=\begin{cases}w_{0}(u)|u-u_{j}|,&|u-u_{j}|<\delta,\\
\quad\;\;\,w(u),&|u-u_{j}|\geq\delta,\\
\end{cases}

and

w_{u}(u)=\begin{cases}w_{0}(u),&|u-u_{j}|<\delta,\\
\,w(u),&|u-u_{j}|\geq\delta.\\
\end{cases}

It is a simple matter to verify that
w_{l}(u)\leq w(u)\leq w_{u}(u)
for all
u\in\Gamma_{\alpha}
and, consequently,

\|w_{l}T_{n}^{w_{l}}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\leq\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\leq\|w_{u}T_{n}^{w_{u}}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}.

From this, it follows that

\quad k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{l}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}e^{ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}\\
\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|wT_{n}^{w}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}e^{ng_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},\infty)}\leq k_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(u_{0},u_{0})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{u}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\right).\quad

Since

\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{l}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\leq\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha})\leq\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\log w_{u}(x)\,\omega(u_{0},dx,\Omega_{\alpha}),

the monotone convergence theorem implies that as
\delta\rightarrow 0
, the upper and lower bounds converge to the same limit. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem
4.10
provides an asymptotic formula for the weighted norm of a broad class of weighted Chebyshev polynomials on circular arcs. To our knowledge, this is the first example of such a result for Jordan arcs beyond the classical case of an interval.

Interestingly, the key argument that allows us to extend the result from positive continuous weights to weights with zeros is not specific to intervals or circular arcs; rather, it applies to any Jordan arc and, in fact, to more general compact sets. More precisely, let
K
be a regular (for potential theory) compact set for which there exists a constant
C_{K}
such that

(4.19)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(K,w_{0})=C_{K}\exp\left(\int_{K}\log w_{0}(x)\,d\mu_{K}(x)\right)

for every continuous positive weight
w_{0}:K\to(0,\infty)
. Then the method used in the proof of Theorem
4.10
can be adapted to show that (
4.19
) remains valid for more general weights of the form

(4.20)

w(x)=w_{0}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{m}|x-x_{j}|^{s_{j}},

where
s_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
x_{j}\in K
for
j=1,\dots,m
.

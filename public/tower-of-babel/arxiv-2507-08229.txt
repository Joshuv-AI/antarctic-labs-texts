Nonresonance for problems involving ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian equations with nonlinear perturbations
Emer Lopera, Nsoki Mavinga, Diana Sanchez

Abstract
We study the solvability of ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian problems with nonlinear reaction terms and non-homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. First, we provide a complete description of the spectrum of the eigenvalue problem involving the ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian with weights and a spectral parameter present in both the differential equation and on the boundary. Then, using variational methods and critical point theory, we prove the existence of weak solutions for the nonlinear problem when the nonlinearities involved remain asymptotically, in some sense, below the first eigenvalue of the q q -Laplacian problem with weights and a spectral parameter present in both the differential equation and on the boundary. We also establish an existence result for the nonlinear problem when the nonlinearities involved remain asymptotically below the first Steklov-Neumann eigenvalue-line, which is a line connecting the first Steklov and first Neumann eigenvalues for q q -Laplacian problems with weights and a spectral parameter present either in the differential equation or on the boundary.

Introduction

We are concerned with the existence of weak solutions to following
(p,q)
-Laplacian boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrl}&-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta_{q}u+c_{1}(x)|u|^{p-2}u+c_{2}(x)|u|^{q-2}u&=f(x,u)\qquad\text{in }\Omega\\
&(|\nabla u|^{p-2}+|\nabla u|^{q-2})\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=g(x,u)\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N},\,N\geq 2,
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\partial/\partial\nu=\nu\cdot\nabla
is the outward (unit) normal derivative on
\partial\Omega
, and
\Delta_{\theta}
denotes the
\theta
-Laplacian,
-\Delta_{\theta}(u)=-\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{\theta-2}\nabla u)
. The nonlinearities
f(x,u)
and
g(x,u)
interact with the spectrum of the following
(p,q)
-Laplacian problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrl}&-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta_{q}u+c_{1}(x)|u|^{p-2}u+c_{2}(x)|u|^{q-2}u&=\lambda m(x)|u|^{q-2}u\qquad\text{in }\Omega\\
&(|\nabla u|^{p-2}+|\nabla u|^{q-2})\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=\lambda\rho(x)|u|^{q-2}u\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2)

where the spectral parameter
\lambda
is present in both the differential equation and on the boundary (with weights). Notice that when
\rho\equiv 0
or
m\equiv 0
, problem (
2
) reduces to the eigenvalue problems under Neumann- or Steklov-type boundary conditions, respectively. These were considered in
Barbu and Morosanu 2023
in the case
m\equiv 0

\rho\equiv 1

Mihăilescu and Moroşanu 2016
for the case
m\equiv 1

\rho\equiv 0

c_{1}

Proposition 4
.

Let

Proof.

First, we prove that

(13)

Lemma 13

Proof.

\lambda

is an eigenfunction of Problem (

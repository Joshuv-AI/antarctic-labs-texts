Cowen-Douglas operators and analytic continuation
Paweł Pietrzycki

Abstract
. In this paper, we study certain Banach spaces of analytic functions on which a left-invertible multiplication operator acts. It turns out that, under natural conditions, its left inverse is a Cowen-Douglas operator. We investigate how the analytic continuations of functions from an invariant subspace of this Cowen-Douglas operator relate to the spectrum of its restriction to that subspace.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space. Denote by
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})
the Banach algebra of all bounded linear
operators on
\mathcal{B}
.
Given
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})
, we write
\mathscr{R}(T)
and
\mathscr{N}(T)
for the range and the kernel of
T
, respectively. Here
\bigvee
stands for the (closed) linear span. By
\mathbb{C}
we denote the
field of complex
numbers. The
symbols
\mathbb{Z}
and

\mathbb{N}

are reserved for the sets of integers and
positive integers
respectively.

In
[
1
]
,
A. Aleman, S. Richter, and W. T. Ross studied a Banach space
\mathcal{B}
of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
which has the following properties:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{B},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\hol(\mathbb{D}),\text{ the inclusion is
continuous,}

\displaystyle 1\in\mathcal{B},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{B},

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})=\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

where for
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
is given by

(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}f)(z)=\frac{f(z)-f(\lambda)}{z-\lambda},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

This example include the classical Banach spaces of holomorphic functions in the unit disc: the Hardy space, the Bergman space and the
Dirichlet space and the Besov space. They related meromorphic continuations of the functions from an
\mathcal{L}
-invariant subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{B}
to the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}}
.
In this general setting, they proved that
\sigma(\mathcal{L})=\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,

\sigma(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, for
\mathcal{M}
invariant subspace of
\mathcal{L}
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{ap}}(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})\cap\mathbb{D}=\sigma_{\text{p}}(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})\cap\mathbb{D}=\{a\in\mathbb{D}\colon\frac{1}{1-az}\in\mathcal{M}\}.

Moreover, under a regularity condition
on
\mathcal{B}
, they proved that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{ap}}(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})\cap\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}\setminus\{\frac{1}{\zeta}\colon\text{every }f\in\mathcal{M}

extends to be analytic

\displaystyle\text{ in a neighborhood of }\zeta\}.

For a connected open subset
\varOmega
of
\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Cowen-Douglas class
\mathscr{B}_{n}(\varOmega)

consists of the operators
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})

which satisfy:

(i)

\varOmega\subseteq\sigma(T)=\{\omega\in\mathbb{C}\colon T-\omega\text{ not invertible}\},

(ii)

\mathscr{R}(T-\omega)=\mathcal{B}
for
\omega\in\varOmega
,

(iii)

\bigvee_{\omega\in\varOmega}\ker(T-\omega)=\mathcal{B}
,

(iv)

\dim\ker(T-\omega)=n
for
\omega\in\varOmega
.

Cowen-Douglas operators have played an important role in operator theory, servicing as a bridge
between operator theory and complex geometry.
Namely, for an operator
T\in\mathscr{B}_{n}(\varOmega)
the mapping
\omega\to(T-\omega)

gives rise to a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
\varOmega
. Let us recall this notion. Let
\Lambda
be a manifold with a complex structure and
n
be a
positive integer. A rank
n
holomorphic vector bundle
over
\Lambda
consists of a manifold
E
with
a complex structure together
with a holomorphic map
\pi
from
E
onto
\Lambda
such that each
fibre
E_{\lambda}:=\pi^{-1}(\lambda)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and such that for each
\lambda_{0}
in
\Lambda
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\lambda_{0}
and holomorphic functions
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
,…,
\gamma_{n}
from
U
to
E
whose values form
a basis for
E_{\lambda}
at each
\lambda\in\Lambda
. For an operator
T\in\mathscr{B}_{n}(\varOmega)
let
(E_{T},\pi)
denote the subbundle of the trivial bundle
\varOmega\times\mathcal{H}
defined by

\displaystyle E_{T}=\{(w,x)\in\varOmega\times\mathcal{B}\colon x\in\mathscr{N}(T-\omega)\}\text{ and }\pi(\omega,x)=\omega.

2.
Main results

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space of
E

\varOmega\subset\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{z}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})
be a left-invertible multiplication operator with a left-inverse
\mathcal{L}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})
, where
E
is a Hilbert space. We assume that
\mathcal{M}_{z}
satisfies the
following five properties:

•

\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\hol(\varOmega,E)
the inclusion map is
continuous,

•

\dim\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})=n
,

•

every
f\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
is analytic in an open neighborhood of
\bar{\varOmega}
,

•

for every
f\in\mathcal{B}
and every
\lambda\in\varOmega
,
there exist
g\in\mathcal{B}
and
h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle f=(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)g+h,

•

\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})=\bar{\varOmega}
.

This class includes the class described in Introduction, as well as certain adjoints of Cauchy dual operators for left-invertible operators (see the Shimorin analytic model
[
8
]
and its extensions in
[
4
]
).

We prove that the inverse
R_{\lambda}
of
(I-\lambda\mathcal{L})
has the following property: for every
f\in\mathcal{M}
there exists
c_{\lambda}(f)\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)Rf=\mathcal{M}_{z}f-\lambda c_{\lambda}(f),

(2.1)

Preliminary research show that this property is equivalent to the following one, expressed in terms of spectrum

\displaystyle\lambda\in\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})\implies\frac{1}{\lambda}\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}).

Observe that if we substitute
z=\lambda
in (
2.1
), where
\lambda\in\varOmega
,
\lambda\neq 0
then we get

.
This makes it possible to define the following function

\displaystyle\tilde{f}\colon D\ni\lambda\to c_{\lambda}(f)(\lambda)\in E,

(2.2)

where
D=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\colon\frac{1}{\lambda}\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})\}
. As before
\tilde{f}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
, for
\lambda\in\varOmega
.

.

Suppose that the sequence

\varOmega^{\prime}=\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\colon\lambda\notin\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})\}

\displaystyle=\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\colon\text{there exist $h$ such that }(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h\in\mathcal{M}\}

.

Let
\mathcal{B}
the following
additional condition:
If
f\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})
is analytic in an open neighborhood of a point

\xi\in\partial\sigma(T)
,
\lambda_{n}\to\xi
and

Then

Then

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})\cap\partial\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})=\partial\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})\setminus

Note that since
\mathcal{L}
is a (non-invertible) Cowen-Douglas operator the space

is finite-dimensional. In the case where the operator

is a backward shift the space

is one-dimensional and consists of constant functions. However, in the case where

be a left-invertible operator such that

can be modelled as a multiplication operator on space of vector-valued holomorphic function on annulus. If

is a Cowen-Douglas operator and

Note that, since

, it follows from
[

]
that the series for

\displaystyle U_{h}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(P_{E}T^{n}h)\frac{1}{z^{n}}.

Therefore this series is absolutely convergent outside some disc.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})
, and
take
h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
. Then
(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h=h
. Multiplying both sides by
\mathcal{L}
, we obtain
(I-\lambda\mathcal{L})(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h=0
. If
(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h\in\mathcal{M}
then it is an eigenvector of
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\frac{1}{\lambda}
. Hence,
\frac{1}{\lambda}\in\sigma(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})
.

Now, suppose
\frac{1}{\lambda}\in\sigmap(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})\cap\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\colon\lambda\notin\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})\}
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
such that
\|f_{n}\|=1
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(I-\lambda\mathcal{L})f_{n}\|=0.

(3.1)

Note that
(\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L}-I)f_{n}\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
and the sequence
\{(\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L}-I)f_{n}\}
is bounded. Since
\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
is finite-dimensional, by the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem, we can, without loss of generality assume that
\{(\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L}-I)f_{n}\}
is convergent to some
h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
. Using the identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{z}(I-\lambda\mathcal{L})f_{n}=\lambda(I-\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L})f_{n}+(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)f_{n}.

and taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, it follows from (
3.1
) that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)f_{n}-h\|=0
. Since
\lambda\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L})
, we obtain
\frac{1}{\lambda}\in\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})
.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an invariant subspace of
\mathcal{L}
. Then the following condition holds:

(i)

If
\frac{1}{\lambda}\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})
then the inverse of
(I-\lambda\mathcal{L})|_{\mathcal{M}}
has the following property: for every
f\in\mathcal{M}
there exist
c_{\lambda}(f)\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)R_{\lambda}f=\mathcal{M}_{z}f-\lambda c_{\lambda}(f),

(3.2)

(ii)

If
\frac{1}{\lambda}\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L})
and
f=(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)g+h
, where
h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
, then
g=(I-\lambda\mathcal{L})^{-1}\mathcal{L}f
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
\frac{1}{\lambda}\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{M}})
. Then there exists
R_{\lambda}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\displaystyle(I-\lambda\mathcal{L})|_{\mathcal{M}}R_{\lambda}f=f,\quad f\in\mathcal{M}.

Multiplying both sides by
\mathcal{M}_{z}
and rearranging terms yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{z}f=(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)R_{\lambda}f+\lambda(I-\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L})R_{\lambda}f,\quad f\in\mathcal{M}.

Note that
\lambda(I-\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L})R_{\lambda}f\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
, since
\mathcal{L}(I-\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{L}\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L}=0
.
Therefore, we conclude that for every for every
f\in\mathcal{M}
there exist
c_{\lambda}(f)\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})
such that (
3.2
) holds as claimed in part (i).

(ii) Suppose

, where

. Appying

to both sides gives

is invertible by assumption, we can solve for

to obtain

(ii)

(i)
\Rightarrow

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})
be a left-invertible but not invertible operator, and
L\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{B})
be its left-inverse.
Then for every
\lambda\notin\sigma(T)

\frac{1}{\lambda}\in\sigma(L)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda\notin\sigma(T)
. Then
T-\lambda
is invertible, so there exists

such that
(T-\lambda)S=I
and
S(T-\lambda)=I
. This implies that
(I-\lambda L)S=L(T-\lambda)S=L
.
Multiplying both sides on the left by
T
, we obtain

(I-\lambda L)ST=I
.
On the other hand, starting from

, we expand:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=S(T-\lambda TL+\lambda TL-\lambda)=ST(I-\lambda L)-\lambda(I-TL).

Now, if

were invertible, we would have

, and the second term would vanish. However, since

is not invertible,

, so

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\{\mathcal{M}_{z}^{k}h\colon h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L}),k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\bigvee(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h\colon\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z}),h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})\}.

Then
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, if the sequence
\{\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n}\mathcal{L}^{n}\}

(i) To show

\displaystyle p_{n}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\lambda^{k+1}}\mathcal{M}_{z}^{k}h.

We estimate the norm of the difference:

\displaystyle\|p_{n}-(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h\|

\displaystyle=\|\frac{1}{\lambda^{n+2}}\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n+1}(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{n+2}}\|\mathcal{M}_{z}\|^{n+1}\|(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h\|

Since
|\lambda|>\|\mathcal{M}_{z}\|
, we see that
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n}=(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h
. Therefore,
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{P}
.

Now, we show
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{S}
. We proceed by induction. First, let us note that the following limit holds:

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}h=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{z}}{\lambda}-1)^{-1}h=h,\quad h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L}).

Therefore
\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{S}
. Now suppose, that for
0<k<n
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, then for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{z})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n+1}{(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}}h=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda^{k}\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n-k}h+\lambda^{n+1}{(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}}h,\quad h\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L}),

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|\lambda\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n+1}{(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}}h-\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n+1}h\|\leq\|\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n+2}{(\mathcal{M}_{z}-\lambda)^{-1}}h\|

But since

.

(ii) Fix

. Observe that, since
\mathcal{L}(I-\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L})=0

\displaystyle h_{k}:=(I-\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L})\mathcal{L}^{k}f\in\mathscr{N}(\mathcal{L}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

It now suffices to observe that we can write

\displaystyle f=\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n}\mathcal{L}^{n}f+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{M}_{z}^{k}(I-\mathcal{M}_{z}\mathcal{L})\mathcal{L}^{k}f=\mathcal{M}_{z}^{n}\mathcal{L}^{n}f+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{M}_{z}^{k}h_{k}.

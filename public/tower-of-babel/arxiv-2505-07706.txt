The generalized trifference problem
Anurag Bishnoi, Bartłomiej Kielak, Benedek Kovács, Zoltán Lóránt Nagy, Gábor Somlai, Máté Vizer, Zeyu Zheng

Abstract
We study the problem of finding the largest number T ⁡ ( n , m ) T(n,m) of ternary vectors of length n n such that for any three distinct vectors there are at least m m coordinates where they pairwise differ. For m = 1 m=1 , this is the classical trifference problem which is wide open. We prove upper and lower bounds on T ⁡ ( n , m ) T(n,m) for various ranges of the parameter m m and determine the phase transition threshold on m = m ⁡ ( n ) m=m(n) where T ⁡ ( n , m ) T(n,m) jumps from constant to exponential in n n . By relating the linear version of this problem to a problem on blocking sets in finite geometry, we give explicit constructions and probabilistic lower bounds. We also compute the exact values of this function and its linear variation for small parameters.

Introduction

Let
\Sigma
denote a finite set of symbols, known as the alphabet, where we typically take
\Sigma=\{0,1,2\}
or a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We will refer to subsets of
\Sigma^{n}
as codes of length
n
over
\Sigma
.
If
a,b,c\in\Sigma^{n}

are three codewords, then we say that
a
,
b
and
c

triffer
at position
i
if
\{a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}\}=\{0,1,2\}
. The well-known
trifference problem
refers to the determination of the largest size
T(n)
of a ternary code
C\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{n}
in which for every three distinct codewords
a,b,c\in C
, there is at least one position where they triffer.
This problem has important applications in computer science
[
17
,
23
]
and cryptography
[
4
,
24
]
, and is closely related to many seemingly distant topics, such as strong blocking sets in finite projective spaces
[
7
]
, minimal codes
[
7
,
1
]
, and covering codes
[
15
]
. Recently, the problem has gained significant interest, in part due to these connections, which has led to
various improvements in the known bounds
[
7
,
18
,
6
,
21
,
11
]
.

The trifference problem is a special case of the following perfect
k
-hashing problem.
Let
k\geq 3
be a fixed integer. A code
C
over an alphabet
\Sigma
is called a
perfect
k
-hash code if for every
k
-tuple of distinct codewords in
C
, there exists a position where all these elements differ.
Finding optimal upper bounds for the maximum size of perfect
k
-hash codes is a fundamental open problem in theoretical computer
science.
The cornerstone result is based on
an elementary double counting argument due to Körner
[
16
]
, and it states that
|C|\leq(k-1)(1+\frac{1}{k-1})^{n}.

This upper bound was improved by Fredman and Komlós
[
12
]
, and later, by Arikan
[
3
]
.
In recent years, there have been further improvements by Dalai, Guruswami and Radhakrishnan
[
9
]
, and most recently by Guruswami and Riazanov
[
14
]
in the cases when
k>3
.
However, improving the trivial upper bound in the case
k=3
remained a notoriously difficult problem. Very recently, Bhandari and Khetan
[
6
]
obtained the following polynomial improvement on the upper bound,

T(n)\leq cn^{-2/5}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n},

where
c
is an absolute constant.
On the other hand, the best lower bound on
T(n)
is
(\frac{9}{5})^{n/4}
due to Körner and Marton
[
17
]
and dates back to 1988 (see
[
7
]
for an alternate proof of the same bound).

In this paper, we study the following natural generalization of the trifference problem.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq m\geq 1
are integers. Let
T(n,m)
be the largest size of a ternary code
C\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{n}
such that for any three distinct codewords
x,y,z
in
C
, we have at least
m
coordinates where they triffer. A code with such a property will be referred to as an
m
-trifferent code
.

Recall that the
Hamming distance

d(u,v)
between two codewords
u
,
v
is the number of coordinates where
u
and
v
differ. The expression
A_{q}(n,d)
denotes the maximum number of possible codewords in a
q
-ary block code of length
n
and minimum Hamming distance
d
, see
[
19
]
. The determination of (the asymptotic behavior of)
A_{q}(n,d)
is one of the most central problems in coding theory.

The determination of the maximum size of generalized trifferent codes

T(n,m)
can be interpreted as the analogue of maximum size codes with given Hamming distance, where
m
corresponds to the ‘minimum distance’ in this case, thus highlighting its relevance.
This generalization has been studied by Bassalygo, Burmester, Dyachkov, Kabatianski in
[
5
]
under the name of
k
-hash codes with distance
d_{k}
over an alphabet of size
q
.
Our problem corresponds to the special case of
q=k=3
and
d_{3}=m
.

Our main contribution is to give both lower and upper bounds on
T(n,m)
for various ranges of
m
.
We first show that there is a phase transition around the value
m\approx\frac{2}{9}n
, which is analogous to the phase transition of error-correcting codes.
This was already stated implicitly in
[
5
]
for the general problem, but we also include a proof, and we significantly improve their bounds for
q=k=3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\varepsilon>0
is a real number. If
m>(\frac{2}{9}+\varepsilon)n
, then
T(n,m)
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
\varepsilon
. On the other hand, if
m<(\frac{2}{9}-\varepsilon)n
, then
T(n,m)
is bounded from below by an exponential function
(1+\delta)^{n}
, where
\delta>0
depends only on
\varepsilon
.

Before stating our other main results, let us first state some elementary observations on the function in view.

Proposition 1.3
.

(i)

T(n,n)=3
for every
n\geq 1
.

(ii)

T(n,m)\leq T(n-1,m-1)
for every
n\geq m\geq 2
.

(iii)

T(n,m)\geq T(n/m,1)
for every
n\geq m\geq 1
with
m\mid n
.

(iv)

\frac{2}{3}T(n,m)\leq T(n-1,m)
for every
n>m\geq 1
.

Proof.

(i)
is straightforward. To see
(ii)
, observe that if we erase a coordinate of an
m
-trifferent code, we obtain an
(m-1)
-trifferent code. To prove
(iii)
, we take the
m
-fold repetition of each codeword in a
1
-trifferent code of length
n/m
, giving an
m
-trifferent code of length
n
. Finally for
(iv)
, if we fix a coordinate
i
in an
m
-trifferent code and take all the codewords whose
i
-th coordinate is not equal to
j
for an arbitrarily chosen
j
, we again obtain an
m
-trifferent code. If we then erase the
i
-th coordinate, the resulting code is still
m
-trifferent. Taking
j
to be the least frequent value appearing at coordinate
i
, we get the desired bound.
∎

Using the upper bound on
T(n)=T(n,1)
from
[
6
]
, Proposition
1.3

(ii)
applied
m-1
times immediately gives the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

T(n,m)\leq c(n-m+1)^{-2/5}(\frac{3}{2})^{n-m}
holds, where
c
is an absolute constant.

In
[
5
]
, it was shown that the elementary recursive bound can be improved to give

T(n,m)\leq m\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-\frac{9}{2}(m-1)}.

(1)

We provide significant improvements on these upper bounds in various ranges of values for
m
.

Theorem 1.5

(Upper bound)
.

Let
m
be of the form
m=3\ell+r
for
r\in\{0,1,2\}
. Then

T(n,m)\leq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{6(3\ell+2)2^{2\ell+1}}{\binom{n}{2\ell+1}}&\text{if $r=2$}\\[10.0pt]
\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{3(3\ell+1)2^{2\ell}}{\binom{n}{2\ell}}&\text{if $r=1$}\\[10.0pt]
\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{6\ell 2^{2\ell-1}}{\binom{n}{2\ell-1}}&\text{if $r=0$.}\end{cases}

Note that for constant
m
this bound gives a polynomial improvement over Corollary
1.4
and Equation (
1
), whereas for
m=\Theta(n)
it gives an exponential improvement.
For small values of
m
, we obtain the following bounds:

Theorem 1.6
.

T(n,3)\leq T(n,2)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{10}{n},\ \ \ T(n,4)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{44}{n^{2}},\ \ T(n,5)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{232}{n^{3}}.

If
m
is of order
\Theta(n)
, the bounds of Theorem
1.5
can be further improved and provide asymptotic improvements to (
1
):

Theorem 1.7

(Refined upper bound when
m
is large)
.

Let
m=\left\lceil\lambda n\right\rceil
for some
\lambda\in(0,\frac{2}{9})
, and
\hat{x}
be the smallest real root of the cubic polynomial

{\lambda-\frac{3}{2}x(1-x)^{2}}
.

Then

T(n,m)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{C}{2^{-\hat{x}n}\binom{n}{\lceil\hat{x}n\rceil-1}},

with a suitable constant
C=C(\lambda)
.

As far as lower bounds are concerned, we prove the following in the same range of parameters.

Theorem 1.8

(Lower bound)
.

For a fixed value
0<\lambda<\frac{2}{9}
, let
m=\left\lfloor\lambda n\right\rfloor
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

T(n,m)\geq c\left(\frac{9}{2}\lambda\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda n}\left(\frac{9}{7}(1-\lambda)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\lambda)n}

for
n
large enough.

in Section

2
Notation and preliminaries

For a codeword
c\in\Sigma^{n}
,
\sigma(c)
denotes the
support
of the codeword, i.e., the set of indices where
c
is non-zero. The notation
[n]
denotes the integer interval
\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:1\leq x\leq n\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is called
minimal
if for any two linearly independent
c,c^{\prime}\in C
, we have
\sigma(c)\not\subseteq\sigma(c^{\prime})
.
A linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is called
m
-minimal
if after the deletion of any
m-1
coordinates, all codewords remain distinct and the remaining code is still minimal.

Logarithms in this paper are taken base
2
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
binary entropy function

H(p)
is defined as

H(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)

for
0\leq p\leq 1
.

We will use the following well-known bound on binomial coefficients:

Lemma 2.3
.

\frac{1}{n+1}2^{nH\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)}\leq\binom{n}{k}\leq 2^{nH\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)}.

For a refined bound, see
[
8
, Lemma 17.5.1, p. 666.]

The
relative entropy
or Kullback-Liebler divergence is defined as

D_{\text{KL}}(P\parallel Q)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}P(x)\log\left(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\right)

for a pair of discrete probability distributions
P
and
Q
defined on the same sample space
\mathcal{X}
. In the special case when
P
and
Q
correspond to the distribution of indicator variables with appearance probability
p
and
p^{\prime}
, we use the simpler notation
H(p,p^{\prime})
, which simplifies to

H(p,p^{\prime})=p\log\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-p^{\prime}}.

Definition 2.4
.

For any
S\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{n}
, we define
T_{S}(n,m)
to be the size of the largest
m
-trifferent set contained in
S
.

The lemma below follows the ideas of the proof in
[
6
]
for the similar bound on
T_{S}(n)
. This statement will enable us to give upper bounds on
T(n,m)
via bounding the maximum size of
m
-trifferent sets contained in certain subsets of the space
\Sigma^{n}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any subset
S\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{n}
,

\frac{T(n,m)}{3^{n}}\leq\frac{T_{S}(n,m)}{|S|}.

Proof.

Let
C
be an
m
-trifferent code in
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
, identified with
\{0,1,2\}^{n}
.
For a vector
v\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
, let
S_{v}=S+v=\{x+v:x\in S\}
.
We double count the size of the set
\{(x,v):x\in C\cap S_{v}\}
.
Since
x\in C
is contained in
S_{v}
if and only if
v=x-s
for some
s\in S
, each element of
C
is contained in
|S|
many
S_{v}
’s.
Therefore,

\sum_{v\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}}|C\cap S_{v}|=|C||S|.

By averaging, there must exist a vector
u
such that

|C\cap S_{u}|\geq|C||S|/3^{n}
.
We know that
C\cap S_{u}
is an
m
-trifferent set inside
S_{u}
, and since
S_{u}
is just a translate of
S
,
|C\cap S_{u}|\leq T_{S}(n,m)
, giving us the inequality.
∎

Notice that the proof of the previous lemma uses the fact that the set of subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
that triffer is closed under translation.

3
Proof of the lower and upper bounds

We start by proving the lower bound on
T(n,m)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
1\leq m<\frac{2}{9}n
. Then
T(n,m)\geq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\cdot 2^{nh/2}-1
, where
h=H\left(\frac{m}{n},\frac{2}{9}\right)
.

Proof.

We use the alteration method.
For a positive integer
t
fixed later on, choose codewords
c_{1},c_{2},...,c_{t}\in\{0,1,2\}^{n}
independently and uniformly at random, and let
C_{0}=\{c_{1},c_{2},...,c_{t}\}
. A triple of codewords
\{c_{x},c_{y},c_{z}\}
is called
bad
if they triffer at less than
m
positions. Let
B
denote the number of bad triples in
C_{0}
. Clearly
\mathbb{E}[B]=\binom{t}{3}p_{b}
where
p_{b}=\mathbb{P}[\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}\mathrm{\ is\ bad}]
. Create a new code
C
from
C_{0}
by deleting one of the codewords from each bad triple. Then,
C
will be
m
-trifferent, and
|C|\geq t-B
. Therefore,
T(n,m)\geq\mathbb{E}[|C|]\geq t-\binom{t}{3}p_{b}
.

If
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are uniformly random elements of
\{0,1,2\}^{n}
, then for each
1\leq i\leq n
, the probability that the codewords triffer at position
i
is
\frac{3!}{3^{3}}=\frac{2}{9}
.
Therefore, if
X
denotes the number of positions where the three codewords triffer, then
X
follows the binomial distribution
\Bin\left(n,\frac{2}{9}\right)
. By the well-known Chernoff bound,
p_{b}\leq\mathbb{P}[X\leq m]\leq 2^{-nh}
, where
h=H\left(\frac{m}{n},\frac{2}{9}\right)
satisfies
0<h<\log\frac{9}{7}
.

Thus, if we choose
t=\lfloor\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{nh/2}\rfloor
, then we get

T(n,m)\geq t-\binom{t}{3}p_{b}\geq t-\frac{1}{6}t^{3}p_{b}\geq\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{nh/2}-1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\cdot 2^{3nh/2}2^{-nh}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\cdot 2^{nh/2}-1.\qed

Remark 3.2
.

(i) In the case where
m
is a function of
n
with
m=o(n)
(for example
m\geq 1
is a constant) and
n\to\infty
, we get that for some
c>0
,
T(n,m)\geq c\left(\frac{3}{\sqrt{7}}\right)^{n}
for all
n
. Note that
\frac{3}{\sqrt{7}}\approx 1.1339
.

(ii) If
m=\left\lfloor\lambda n\right\rfloor
for a fixed value
0<\lambda<\frac{2}{9}
, and
n\to\infty
, we get that for some
c>0
,
T(n,m)\geq c\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2}H(\lambda,\frac{2}{9})n}=cb(\lambda)^{n}
, where

b(\lambda)=2^{\frac{1}{2}H\left(\lambda,\frac{2}{9}\right)}=\left(\frac{9}{2}\lambda\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda}\left(\frac{9}{7}(1-\lambda)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\lambda)}

is a strictly decreasing function for
\lambda\in\left[0,\frac{2}{9}\right]
with
b(0)=\frac{3}{\sqrt{7}}
and
b\left(\frac{2}{9}\right)=1
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.8
, which also implies Theorem
1.2
when
m<(\frac{2}{9}-\varepsilon)n
. Next, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
by considering the case
m>(\frac{2}{9}+\varepsilon)n
. Note that the following result is also a consequence of Theorem 4 in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any fixed
\frac{2}{9}<\lambda\leq 1
, there exists a constant
c(\lambda)
such that
T(n,\left\lceil\lambda n\right\rceil)\leq c(\lambda)
for all
n
.

Proof.

Let
C=\{c_{1},c_{2},...,c_{T}\}\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{n}
be a code for which any three codewords triffer in at least
\lambda n
positions.

Let
K
denote the number of quadruples
(x,y,z,i)
such that:

•

1\leq x<y<z\leq T
and
1\leq i\leq n
are integers,

•

and codewords
c_{x}
,
c_{y}
and
c_{z}
triffer at position
i
.

We will bound
K
from below and above. Firstly, we can see that for every triple
(x,y,z)
with
x<y<z
, we have at least
\lambda n
positions where the three codewords triffer, so
K\geq\binom{T}{3}\cdot\lambda n
.

On the other hand, for a fixed value
1\leq i\leq n
, let us put an upper bound on the number of triples triffering at position
i
. If the number of codewords in
C
having
i
-th coordinate
0
,
1
and
2
is
r_{i}
,
s_{i}
and
t_{i}
respectively, then the following hold:

•

r_{i},s_{i},t_{i}\geq 0
,

•

r_{i}+s_{i}+t_{i}=T
,

•

the number of triples triffering at position
i
is
r_{i}s_{i}t_{i}
.

By the AM-GM inequality,
r_{i}s_{i}t_{i}\leq\left(\frac{r_{i}+s_{i}+t_{i}}{3}\right)^{3}=\frac{1}{27}T^{3}
. Therefore,

K=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}s_{i}t_{i}\leq\frac{1}{27}nT^{3}.

This means that
\binom{T}{3}\cdot\lambda n\leq\frac{1}{27}nT^{3}
, giving

\frac{T(T-1)(T-2)}{T^{3}}\lambda\leq\frac{2}{9}.

Since
\frac{T(T-1)(T-2)}{T^{3}}\to 1
as
T\to\infty
and
\frac{2}{9}/\lambda<1
, we conclude that
T
is bounded by a constant depending only on
\lambda
.
∎

We continue by proving upper bounds on
T(n,m)
. First, we present a consequence of the threshold value
\lambda=\frac{2}{9}
and of Proposition
1.3
. This is similar to Theorem 5 of
[
5
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
0<\alpha<\frac{2}{9}
and
0<\varepsilon<\frac{7}{9}
be real numbers. Then

T\left(n,\left\lceil\left(\frac{2}{9}-\alpha\right)n\right\rceil\right)\leq c\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{4.5(\alpha+\varepsilon)n}

for some constant
c=c(\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

For
m=\left\lceil\left(\frac{2}{9}-\alpha\right)n\right\rceil
, Proposition
1.3
(iv) can be applied
t
times iteratively, where

t=\left\lceil n-\frac{1}{\frac{2}{9}+\varepsilon}m\right\rceil.

We hence get
T(n,m)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{t}T(n-t,m)
and it follows from the choice of
t
that
m\geq\left(\frac{2}{9}+\varepsilon\right)(n-t)
, so by Theorem
3.3
we have that
T(n-t,m)
is at most a constant
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
. Altogether,

T\left(n,\left\lceil\left(\frac{2}{9}-\alpha\right)n\right\rceil\right)\leq c^{\prime}\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-\frac{1}{\frac{2}{9}+\varepsilon}m+1}\leq c\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n\left(1-\frac{2/9-\alpha}{2/9+\varepsilon}\right)}\leq c\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{9}{2}(\alpha+\varepsilon)n},

where
c(\varepsilon)=\frac{3}{2}c^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
.
∎

We now look at the other regime where
m
is small.
We first prove a better upper bound than
T(n,2)\leq T(n,1)\leq cn^{-2/5}(3/2)^{n}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

T(n,2)\leq\frac{10}{n}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

Proof.

We use the setup of Lemma
2.5
for the set
S=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^{n}:\#\{i:x_{i}=2\}=1\}
. Let
C\subseteq S
be a
2
-trifferent code. Observe that if
x_{i}=y_{i}=2
for some
x,y\in S
and
i\in[n]
, then for any
z\in S
the triple
(x,y,z)
would triffer in at most one position. Hence, for any
i\in[n]
there exists at most one
x\in C
such that
x_{i}=2
.

Fix some
t\in C
and assume that
t_{1}=2
. The remaining codewords in
C
start with
0
or
1
so if
|C|\geq 6
, then by pigeonhole principle there exist three codewords
x,y,z\in C
such that
x_{1}=y_{1}=z_{1}
.
Without loss of generality,
t=(2,*,\cdots,*)
,
x=(0,2,*,\cdots,*)
,
y=(0,*,2,*,\cdots,*)
, and
z=(0,*,*,2,*,\cdots,*)
, where each
*
is either a
0
or
1
.
By considering the triples
t,x,y
and
t,x,z
, we get
y_{2}=z_{2}
, and from the triples
t,x,y
and
t,y,z
we get
x_{3}=z_{3}
.
Therefore,
\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\}=\{0,1,2\}
is only possible at
i=4
, so
x
,
y
and
z
triffer in at most
1
position, contradicting the
2
-trifference property.

Hence
|C|\leq 5
, and by Lemma
2.5
we get

T(n,2)\leq\frac{5\cdot 3^{n}}{n\cdot 2^{n-1}}=\frac{10}{n}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.\qed

Before proving the main upper bound (Theorem
1.7

given by Bhandari and Khetan

Theorem 3.6
.

T(n,2\ell+1)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{\binom{n}{\ell}}{\binom{n}{2\ell}}\cdot 4(2\ell+1).

Proof.

We now use the setup of Lemma
2.5
for
m=2\ell+1
and
S=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^{n}:\#\{i:x_{i}=2\}=2\ell\}
.
We wish to bound
T_{S}(n,2\ell+1)
from above. To do this, we will define a bipartite graph
G
on the two vertex classes
U
and
V
, where
U
and
V
are disjoint sets with
|U|=|V|=\binom{n}{\ell}
, both of whose elements are labelled with the
\ell
-element subsets of
[n]
. Fix any
(2\ell+1)
-trifferent code
C\subseteq S
. For each codeword

, there exists a set of indices

of size
2m
such that
x_{i}=2
for each

otherwise. For each partition

to

, where

and

. This way, we add
\binom{2\ell}{\ell}

. Moreover, since

is
(2\ell+1)
-trifferent, each edge corresponds to at most two different codewords from
C
. Therefore,
T_{S}(n,2\ell+1)
is at most
\frac{2}{\binom{2\ell}{\ell}}|E(G)|
.

We claim that

does not contain a

(a four-vertex path). Assume it is not the case and
(I_{1},I_{2})
,
(I_{3},I_{2})
, and
(I_{3},I_{4})
are edges of some

(where
I_{1},I_{3}\in U
and
I_{2},I_{4}\in V
) and these edges correspond to some codewords

, we must have

; analogously,

. Moreover, since

and both are of the same size and their union is disjoint from

, hence
x

4
The linear version

The equivalence between linear trifferent codes and minimal codes was first showed in
[
7
]
. Here, we establish an analogous equivalence between linear
m
-trifferent codes and
m
-minimal codes, which allows us to leverage the rich theory of minimal codes to study linear trifferent codes.

Lemma 4.1
.

A linear code
C
in
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
is
m
-trifferent if and only if it is
m
-minimal.

Proof.

Suppose
C
is
m
-trifferent but not
m
-minimal. Then there exist non-zero codewords
x
and
y
such that after deleting some set of
m-1
coordinates,
\sigma(x)\subsetneq\sigma(y)
.

Consider the codeword
z=-x
, which must be in
C
by linearity. Let
i
be a coordinate (after the deletion of
m-1
coordinates) where
x,y,z
triffer. One of
x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}
has to be zero. Note that
y_{i}=0
implies
z_{i}=x_{i}=0
, while
x_{i}=0
if and only if
z_{i}=0
. Therefore, the triple
\{x,y,z\}
does not triffer at position
i
, a contradiction.

For the backward implication, suppose
C
is
m
-minimal but not
m
-trifferent. Let
x,y,z
be three distinct codewords and let
S
denote the coordinates where
x,y,z
triffer. Suppose
|S|\leq m-1
. We extend
S
to
\widetilde{S}
, a subset of
[n]
with exactly
m-1
coordinates. Without loss of generality, assume
z=0
by replacing
x,y,z
with
x-z,y-z,0
. Consider
w=x+y
. For any coordinate
i
outside
S
,
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}\neq\{1,2\}
. Therefore,
\sigma(w)\setminus\widetilde{S}=(\sigma(x)\setminus\widetilde{S})\cup(\sigma(y)\setminus\widetilde{S})
. Since
x
and
y
remain distinct and nonzero after deleting the coordinates in
\widetilde{S}
(
C
is
m
-minimal), we have
(\sigma(x)\setminus\widetilde{S})\neq(\sigma(y)\setminus\widetilde{S})
, which implies that either
(\sigma(x)\setminus\widetilde{S})\subsetneq(\sigma(w)\setminus\widetilde{S})
or
(\sigma(y)\setminus\widetilde{S})\subsetneq(\sigma(w)\setminus\widetilde{S})
, contradicting
m
-minimality.
∎

4.1
The upper bound

Let
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
denote the projective space
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\setminus\{0\})/\sim
, where
u\sim v
if
u=\lambda v
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. A set of points
S
on a hyperplane
H
is a spanning set
with strength
m
, if
S
remains a spanning set after removing any
m-1
of its points.

As introduced in
[
10
]
, a
strong blocking set

B\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
is a set of points that intersects every hyperplane
H
in a spanning set. See also
[
15
,
2
,
7
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. A set of points
B
in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
is a strong blocking set
with strength
m
if
B
remains a strong blocking set after removing any
m-1
of its points.

It has been shown in
[
22
]
that
C
is a minimal code of dimension
k
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with a generator matrix having no two linearly dependent columns if and only if the columns of its generator matrix form a strong blocking set in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
. We now provide a generalization of this statement.

Lemma 4.4
.

C
is an
m
-minimal code of dimension
k
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with a generator matrix having no two linearly dependent columns if and only if the columns of its generator matrix form a strong blocking set
S
with strength
m
in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
.

Proof.

A code has the
m
-minimal property if and only if after every deletion of
m-1
columns it is a minimal code. In other words, the strong blocking set formed by the columns of the generator matrix is a strong blocking set even after removing any set of
m-1
elements.
∎

In
[
7
]
, it was also shown that a set of points
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
is a strong blocking set if and only if
B=\cup_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\ell\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
is an affine
2
-blocking set, i.e., if
B
meets every
(k-2)
-dimensional affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
in at least one point. Hence we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
C
be a linear code of dimension
k
in
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
with generator matrix
G
.
Suppose
G
has no two linearly dependent columns.
Then
C
is an
m
-trifferent code if and only if the union of
1
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{k}
spanned by the columns of
G
meet every affine subspace of dimension
(k-2)
, not passing through the origin, in at least
m
points. ∎

Let
T_{L}(n,m)
be the largest size of a linear
m
-trifferent code of length
n
.

Theorem 4.6
.

For some absolute constant
c>0
,
T_{L}(n,m)\leq c\cdot 3^{\frac{n-m}{4.55}}
.

Proof.

Let
k=\log_{3}T_{L}(n,m)
be the dimension of the code.
Then we have a set of
n
points in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
such that every
n-m+1
element subset of it is a strong blocking set.
Since a strong blocking set in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
has size at least
4.55(k-1)
(see
[
7
]
), we get that

n\geq 4.55(k-1)+m-1
, giving the statement.
∎

4.2
The lower bound

From the analysis above, to lower bound the rate of
T_{L}(n,m)
, we only need to upper bound the size of
S
. We use a probabilistic approach to construct a strong blocking set
S
with strength
m
in the spirit of the bound of Héger and Nagy on the length of minimal codes
[
15
]
.

Theorem 4.7
.

In
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
, there exists a strong blocking set with strength
m
of size at most
43k+18m-93
. In particular, when
k
is sufficiently large and
m=o(k)
, the bound can be improved to
10k+5m-21
.

Proof.

We uniformly choose
t
lines
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots\ell_{t}
from
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
. Consider the probability that
\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\ell_{i}
fails to be a strong blocking set with strength
m
in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
. This happens if and only if there exists a hyperplane
H
such that, after the removal of some
m-1
points from
\mathcal{B}
to obtain a modified set
\mathcal{B}^{*}
, the span of
H\cap\mathcal{B}^{*}
is strictly contained in
H
, i.e.,
\dim\langle H\cap\mathcal{B}^{*}\rangle<k-2
. In particular, there exists a
(k-3)
dimensional projective subspace
H^{\prime}
such that apart from at most
m-1
lines,
H^{\prime}\cap\ell_{i}
is non-empty. This happens with probability at most

{k\brack k-2}_{3}\cdot\mathbb{P}(\ell_{1}\cap H^{\prime}\neq\emptyset)^{t-m+1}\cdot\binom{t}{m-1},

which gives an upper bound on the probability that
\mathcal{B}
fails to be a strong blocking set.

Note that for
H^{\prime}
with
\dim H^{\prime}=k-3
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\ell_{1}\cap H^{\prime}\neq\emptyset)

\displaystyle=\frac{{k-2\brack 2}_{3}+\frac{1}{3}\cdot{k-2\brack 1}_{3}\left({k\brack 1}_{3}-{k-2\brack 1}_{3}\right)}{{k\brack 2}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{(3^{k-2}-1)(3^{k-3}-1)}{16}+\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{3^{k-2}-1}{2}\left(\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-\frac{3^{k-2}-1}{2}\right)}{\frac{(3^{k}-1)(3^{k-1}-1)}{16}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(3^{k-2}-1)(3^{k-3}-1)+32\cdot 3^{2k-5}-32\cdot 3^{k-3}}{(3^{k}-1)(3^{k-1}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{11\cdot 3^{2k-4}-4\cdot 3^{k-1}+1}{27\cdot 3^{2k-4}-4\cdot 3^{k-1}+1}

\displaystyle<\frac{11}{27}.

Also we can upper bound
{k\brack k-2}_{3}
by
3^{2k-1}/16
. Hence,
\mathcal{B}
is a blocking set with strength
m
with positive probability if

\frac{3^{2k-1}}{16}\cdot\left(\frac{11}{27}\right)^{t-m+1}\cdot\binom{t}{m-1}<1.

Taking logarithms and relaxing, above inequality holds if

(2k-1)\log 3-4+(t-m+1)\log\left(\frac{11}{27}\right)+t\cdot H\left(\frac{m-1}{t}\right)<0.

(5)

A further relaxation of the entropy term yields the simpler sufficient condition

(2k-1)\log 3-4+(t-m+1)\log\left(\frac{11}{27}\right)+t<0.

(6)

Rearranging this inequality gives

t>-\frac{2\log 3}{\log\left(\frac{22}{27}\right)}k+\frac{\log\left(\frac{11}{27}\right)}{\log\left(\frac{22}{27}\right)}m+\frac{\log 3+4-\log\left(\frac{11}{27}\right)}{\log\left(\frac{22}{27}\right)}.

(7)

Hence, one may choose

t=\lceil 10.729k+4.385m-23.287\rceil.

Since each line in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
contains
4
points,

|\mathcal{B}|\leq 4t\leq 43k+18m-93.

We now assume
k
is sufficiently large and
m=o(k)
. From equation (
7
) we see that
t
grows at least linearly with
k
, hence
m=o(t)
. Consequently, the entropy term satisfies
H\left(\frac{m-1}{t}\right)\to 0
. A sufficient condition for inequality (
6
) becomes for some
\epsilon>0
,

\left(\log\left(\frac{11}{27}\right)+\epsilon\right)t<-2\log 3k+\log\left(\frac{11}{27}\right)m+\log 3+4-\log\left(\frac{11}{27}\right).

We may choose

t=\lceil 2.447k+1.001m-5.310\rceil,

which yields

|\mathcal{B}|\leq 4t\leq 10k+5m-21.

∎

Corollary 4.8
.

For any integer
m<2n/9
, we have

T_{L}(n,m)\geq 3^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-18m+93}{43}\right\rfloor}.

Moreover, when
m=o(n)
, for sufficiently large
n
, this bound improves to

T_{L}(n,m)\geq 3^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-5m+21}{10}\right\rfloor}.

Proof.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
be a linear code of dimension
k
. Its generator matrix defines a set of
n
points in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
. As established earlier,
C
is
m
-trifferent if and only if these points form a strong blocking set
\mathcal{B}
with strength
m
.

By Theorem
4.7
, there exists a strong blocking set
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(k-1,3)
with strength
m
satisfying
|\mathcal{B}|\leq 43k+18m-93
.
Thus, given
n\geq 43k+18m-93
, one can select a collection of exactly a set of
n
columns for a generator matrix that yields an
m
-trifferent code of dimension
k
. Since a linear code of dimension
k
has
3^{k}
codewords, it follows that
T_{L}(n,m)\geq 3^{k}
. Rewriting
n\geq 43k+18m-93
as

k\leq\frac{n-18m+93}{43}

shows that the choice

k=\left\lfloor\frac{n-18m+93}{43}\right\rfloor

is valid, and we deduce that

T_{L}(n,m)\geq 3^{\lfloor\frac{n-18m+93}{43}\rfloor}.

For the regime
m=o(n)
, note that the above argument shows
k
grows linearly in
n
. Hence, we may apply the refined bound in Theorem
4.7
and the same argument gives

T_{L}(n,m)\geq 3^{\lfloor\frac{n-5m+23}{10}\rfloor}

.

5.2
Explicit constructions

Large linear trifferent codes were constructed via random methods
[
7
]
, so it was a relevant goal to obtain good explicit constructions as well. Such codes were presented by Alon, Bishnoi, Das and Neri
[
2
]
using expander graphs. A similar approach can be applied in the general trifference problem as well.

For a graph
G
, let
\iota(G)=\min_{S\subseteq V(G)}\{|S|+\kappa(G-S)\}
, where

\kappa(H)
is the largest size of a connected component in
H
.

Let
C
be an infinite family of
[n,Rn,\delta n]_{3}
codes, and let
G
be an infinite family of
d
-regular graphs on
n
vertices such that

\iota(G)\geq(1-\delta)n+m-1
.
Then by the construction in
[
2
]
and the Lemma above, we get a linear
m
-trifferent code of length
(1+d)n
and dimension
k=Rn
.

Note that constant degree expanders can be used to explicitly construct
d
-regular graphs
G
on
n
vertices with
\iota(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{d+\sqrt{d}-o(1)}\right)n
, c.f.
[
2
]
.

5.3
Relation to the sunflower conjecture

Consider a code
C\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{n}
such that any three distinct codewords
x,y,z
triffer in at least
1
coordinate. Now consider the multiset

F(C)=\{\sigma(x):x\in C\}

where each
x
is considered once. The elements of the multiset are in
\mathcal{P}([n])
.
If three distinct codewords
x,y,z
triffer at position
i
then
i
appears in precisely two of the sets
\sigma(x),\sigma(y),\sigma(z)
. Observe that in
F(C)
, each word must have multiplicity at most
2
. This is because if we had
\sigma(x)=\sigma(y)=\sigma(z)
for distinct
x,y,z
then for every
i
,
i
appears in 0 or 3 of the three sets, and so the three words cannot triffer at any position.

A set system
\mathcal{F}
consisting of
k\geq 3
sets is called a
k
-sunflower
if any two sets in the system have the same intersection, and a set system is called
k
-sunflower-free if no
k
distinct elements of it form a
k
-sunflower. A
3
-sunflower-free set-system is just called
sunflower-free
. Naslund and Sawin
[
20
]
showed that a sunflower-free family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of
[n]
must satisfy
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\left(\frac{3}{2^{2/3}}\right)^{n(1+o(1))}
.

Consider the ground set of
F(C)
, denoting it by
\mathcal{F}
. Still in
\mathcal{F}
, it is true that there cannot be three sets
X,Y,Z
such that for every
i\in[n]
,
i
appears in either 0, 1 or 3 of the sets
X,Y,Z
. We can see that three sets satisfy this property precisely when they form a
3
-sunflower, therefore
\mathcal{F}
must be a sunflower-free family, yielding
|C|\leq 2|\mathcal{F}|\leq\left(\frac{3}{2^{2/3}}\right)^{n(1+o(1))}
.

Here
\frac{3}{2^{2/3}}\approx 1.8899
. However, we already know that
T(n,1)\leq Cn^{-2/5}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}
from
[
6
]
, so this does not give an improvement.

Finally, let us mention that while this work focused on the generalization of the trifference problem, which was proven to be the most difficult one among
q
-hash codes, our methods are applicable for the
q
-hashing case as well.

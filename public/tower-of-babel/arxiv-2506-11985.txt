Ising versus infinite randomness criticality in arrays of Rydberg atoms trapped with non-perfect tweezers
Jose Soto-Garcia, Natalia Chepiga

Abstract
Chains of Rydberg atoms have emerged as an amazing platform for simulating quantum physics in low dimensions. This remarkable success is due to the versatility of lattice geometries achieved by trapping neutral atoms with optical tweezers. On a given lattice, the competition between the repulsive van der Waals potential and the detuning of the laser frequency brings the atoms to highly excited Rydberg states, leading to a variety of exotic phases and quantum phase transitions. Experiments on the simplest one-dimensional array of Rydberg atoms have stimulated tremendous progress in understanding quantum phase transitions into crystalline phases. In addition to standard conformal transitions, numerical simulations have predicted two exotic chiral transitions and a floating phase, raising the question of their experimental realization. However, in reality, optical tweezers have a finite width, which results in small deviations in interatomic distances and disorder in interaction strength. However, disorder can affect the nature of transitions. Infinite randomness criticality in the random transverse-field Ising chain is perhaps the most prominent examples. In this paper, we demonstrate how the disorder typical for Rydberg experiments alters the Ising transition to the period-2 phase. Following the experimental protocol closely, we probe the nature of quantum criticality with Kibble-Zurek dynamics. While we clearly observe infinite randomness for strong disorder and large system sizes, we also report a crossover into a clean Ising transition, which is visible for small system sizes and weak disorder. Our results clearly demonstrate an additional technical constraint on the scalability of Rydberg-based quantum simulators.

One of the declared key advantages of Rydberg platforms lies in the control over atomic positions and interaction strengths. However, in practice, optical tweezers exhibit inevitable imperfections. In particular, fluctuations in atomic positions—especially along the optical axis perpendicular to the plane of the lattice —introduce an intrinsic level of spatial disorder (see, for instance, the appendix of Ref.

One of the most prominent examples demonstrating how significantly the disorder can affect quantum phase transition is the infinite randomness criticality. It was shown that such disorder forms a relevant perturbation: even infinitesimal width of random distribution of coupling constants of fields drives the system to a fundamentally different universality class in the thermodynamic limit. This regime is governed by extremely anisotropic scaling, with correlation length critical exponent

;

The crossover between these two universality classes—clean Ising and infinite randomness— is governed by system size, disorder strength, and proximity to criticality

;

These changes in universality class have profound consequences for the Kibble-Zurek mechanism. In clean Ising systems, the density of topological defects (kinks) scales with the sweep rate of the control parameter according to a universal power law

;
caneva2007adiabatic

In this paper, we study how the disorder in the interaction between Rydberg atoms impacts the Kibble-Zurek mechanism in one-dimensional Rydberg atom arrays. Focusing on the Ising transition between the disordered and period-2 phases, we explore how the disorder strength affects the formation of defects during a slow quench. Our choice is motivated by the fact that, to date, the Ising transition is the only case where the effects of disorder and its KZ scaling are theoretically well-understood

. Thus far, there are no strong theoretical arguments regarding how disorder affects chiral transitions or the appearance of floating phases at the boundaries of crystalline phases with higher periodicities. Although the floating phase will likely be destroyed by the disorder

;
chepiga2024resilient
, the incommensurability responsible for the chiral transitions and floating phases may persist

. In principle, it could affect the new criticality.

We characterize the crossover from clean to disordered universality classes and identify the regimes in which the anomalous KZ scaling becomes observable. Our results shed light on the robustness of dynamical critical phenomena in Rydberg platforms, raise a concern on the scalability of Rydberg-based quantum simulators, and provide a timely guidance for future experiments aiming to probe universal out-of-equilibrium behavior in the presence of disorder.

The model.

We investigate a finite one-dimensional chain of Rydberg atoms with disordered interactions in terms of hard-core bosons, modeled by the Hamiltonian:

\frac{H}{\hbar}=\frac{\Omega}{2}\sum_{i}\left(d_{i}+d_{i}^{\dagger}\right)-\Delta\sum_{i}n_{i}+\sum_{i<j}V_{i}\frac{n_{i}n_{j}}{(i-j)^{6}},

III
Discussion

We have demonstrated that the uncertainty in the atomic positions, which is an intrinsic feature of current experimental platforms based on Rydberg atoms, has a significant impact on the Kibble-Zurek (KZ) critical exponent
\mu
. Our results show that this effect becomes more pronounced for larger system sizes and slower sweep rates, two regimes that are increasingly accessible in modern experiments. We expect that our numerical simulations underestimate the actual effect of disorder. This happens for two reasons. Firstly, for numerical efficiency we introduced the disorder in the coupling constant
V_{i}
that by construction underestimates the actual lattice distortion.
Secondly, prior to measurement the trapping potential is typically turned off increasing the distortion in the lattice.

These findings have important implications for future experimental studies of critical dynamics in Rydberg atom arrays. To date, the effects of disorder have not been reported in experimental observations of the KZ mechanism. According to our results, the likely reason is that for system sizes about 51 atoms
bernien2017probing
and low disorder strengths typical for the period-2 phase, the deviations induced by disorder the results were sufficiently close to the clean case. Moreover, at the slow sweep rates where disorder-induced deviations in
\mu
would be strongest, finite-size effects become dominant and obscure the underlying scaling behavior.

Interestingly, previously reported experimental results for the period-3 and period-4 ordered phases
bernien2017probing
might already signal some signature of the infinite randomness. Experiments in these phases consistently report lower values of the critical exponent
\mu
than those obtained from numerical simulations
bernien2017probing
. As we have shown, strong disorder can significantly suppress
\mu
even in relatively small systems, providing a plausible explanation for this systematic discrepancy.
Important that the difference between reported experimental and numerical values of
\mu
increases systematically with the periodicity of the phase: for period-2, simulations and experiments are in close agreement; for period-3, the gap widens; and for period-4, the discrepancy becomes substantial
bernien2017probing
. This trend is consistent with our findings and further supports the idea that disorder plays an increasingly important role as one moves to crystalline phases with higher periodicities.

As experimental capabilities continue to advance, enabling the study of larger systems and the implementation of slower quench rates, the impact of disorder will become increasingly relevant likely limiting the scalability of quantum devices. On the one hand, this is a positive development: it opens the door to experimental investigations of how disorder influences a broad class of quantum phase transitions. For example, the chiral transitions associated with the period-3 and period-4 phases are particularly intriguing in the presence of disorder. There is ongoing debate over whether incommensurability, which it is present in the disordered phase near period-3 phase and above survives in disordered systems. If incommensurability were destroyed, this would imply the destruction of the chiral transition out of period 3 and 4 phases and probably as well as the floating phase. While early studies suggested that disorder may pin or destroy incommensurate (IC) modulations
milsted2015statistical
, leading to a loss of long-range IC order, recent numerical simulations have provided compelling evidence that incommensurate correlations can persist, albeit in a short-range form
chepiga2024resilient
. This in turn opens a new question, how chiral perturbations affects infinite-randomness and whether any conclusion regarding clean chiral transitions and floating phase could be drown from experiments on disordered systems.

Methods

IV.1
Estimation of Disorder Strength

Typical fluctuations in the position of atoms confined in optical tweezers for linear arrays are approximately
\delta x\approx 0.12,\mu\text{m}
in the radial direction and
\delta y\approx 0.6,\mu\text{m}
in the longitudinal direction
bernien2017probing
. These positional uncertainties can significantly affect the interaction energy between atoms.

To estimate the impact of these fluctuations on the interaction energy, we apply linear error propagation. Given a function
f(x,y)
, the uncertainty in the output due to uncertainties in the input variables is given by

\delta f(x,y)=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)^{2}(\delta x)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)^{2}(\delta y)^{2}}.

(2)

In our specific case, this method underestimates the total uncertainty in the interaction energy
V(r)
, since it neglects contributions from longitudinal fluctuations when evaluated at
y=0
:

\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial y}\right)_{y=0}=0,

(3)

where
r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
is the distance between atoms. Additionally, we neglect fluctuations in the Rabi frequency
\Omega
. Therefore, our estimates should be regarded as conservative compared to the uncertainties present in actual experiments.

Typical interatomic distances observed in experiments are as follows

for the period-4 phase.

;

\sum_{r=1}^{L}\absolutevalue{\frac{1}{r^6}- \sum_{i=1}^{11}c_i\lambda_i^r}.

(5)

This approximation provides an almost perfect fitting for the interaction of up to 6 atoms distance and a cost function error of

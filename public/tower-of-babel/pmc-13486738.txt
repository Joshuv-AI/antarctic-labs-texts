Polarization reversal and ion-electron co-modulation in in-plane anisotropic AgVP2S6 for multimodal image processing
Authors: Kun Ye, Xueqi Guo, Qian Li, Junxin Yan, Fang Zhang, Qi Gao, Tianle Zeng, Yonghao Sun, Yang Lv, Yihan Gao, Xuefeng Zhang, Lixuan Liu, Yang Gao, Anmin Nie, Zhiyan Jia, Shouguo Wang, Yong Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe recent emergence of quaternary van der Waals layered transition metal phosphorus chalcogenide compounds, which serve as exemplary ionic-electronic coupled semiconductors, has motivated considerable interest in the development of optically modulated in-sensor computing and refreshable neuromorphic devices. Here, we report monoclinic AgVP2S6 crystals featuring quasi-one-dimensional ordering within well-defined zigzag chains, whose pronounced in-plane optical anisotropy arises from the highly asymmetric distribution of the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum along the a- and b-axes. Notably, the anisotropic electrical and optoelectronic responses of AgVP2S6 along the a- and b-axes are attributable to the disparate energy barriers for Ag+ ion migration. By implementing an ionic-electronic co-modulation strategy, we achieve the integration of anisotropic ionic-electronic transport, visible-to-near-infrared spectral response, synaptic plasticity, and advanced image processing capabilities, establishing a versatile platform for next-generation anisotropic optoelectronic devices with applications in neuromorphic computing, autonomous navigation, and multimodal imaging.Subject terms: Two-dimensional materials, Electronic devices, Photonic crystals

Introduction

Machine vision has achieved widespread deployment in autonomous driving, facial recognition, unmanned aerial vehicle navigation, and industrial inspection owing to its capability to efficiently capture and identify target objects in dynamic environments1–4. However, conventional machine vision faces fundamental bottlenecks5,6, including the massive transmission of raw data between sensors and computing units, which incurs prohibitive power consumption, significant latency, and severe bandwidth pressure. To overcome these challenges, bio-inspired vision that monolithically integrates sensing, memory, and computing functions has emerged as a promising platform. Specifically, bio-inspired vision systems not only mimic the spherical geometry of the retina to optimize optical design, but also replicate the signal transmission and processing mechanisms from retina to brain, reducing data redundancy and improving temporal resolution through event-driven, sparse coding, and local gain control methods7. Additionally, the concept of neural reuse — emphasizing that brain regions can dynamically participate in multiple cognitive tasks to enable flexible reorganization of cognitive—processing functions, has been proposed to guide next-generation architectures8. Despite the considerable potential of brain-inspired neuromorphic devices that emulate neuronal-synaptic functionalities, current implementations struggle to synergistically leverage the advantages of both volatile and non-volatile memory within a single device or task. Critically, they often fail to achieve true parallel multi-task functionality, which constrains the development of devices that simultaneously exhibit dynamic reconfiguration capability and low-power operation.

Two-dimensional (2D) materials, with atomic-scale thickness, high carrier mobility, and facile heterointegration, represent an ideal platform for implementing multifunctional neuromorphic devices9–12. Among these, intrinsic mixed ionic-electronic transition metal phosphorus chalcogenides of formula AMP2S6 (A = Cu, Ag; M = V, Cr, In) exhibit unconventional ionic-electronic co-modulation characteristics, offering a breakthrough material foundation for next-generation neuromorphic computing13–17. These materials possess relatively low ion migration barriers, enabling rapid ion movement under electric fields and strong coupling with electrons18. The intrinsic coupling effect in these materials allows ionic-electronic semiconductors to achieve synergistic responses between ionic and electronic states through electric-field modulation without external modifications, providing a natural platform for constructing multifunctional neuromorphic devices8. Furthermore, by modulating defect states generated by ion migration through light intensity, low light intensity promotes ion migration and a continuous increase in conductance, whereas high light intensity leads to the recombination of photogenerated electrons with ions, suppressing conductance and enabling dynamic reconfiguration of rectification characteristics19. Notably, the photoresponse properties of intrinsic 2D ionic-electronic coupling materials can achieve reversible switching between positive and negative photoconductivity by regulating lattice symmetry and carrier dynamics, which is crucial for simulating neuronal excitatory and inhibitory behaviors20. Importantly, 2D intrinsic mixed ionic-electronic semiconductors combine long-term memory characteristics tuned by ionic-electronic coupling, enabling not only monolithic integration of sensing, memory, and computing but also supporting cross-task non-volatile knowledge sharing and dual-mode collaboration within the same task21–24. However, current 2D mixed ionic-electronic semiconductors face dual challenges: on the one hand, the physical mechanisms underlying polarization direction and ion migration in intrinsic ionic-electronic co-modulation materials remain ambiguous. On the other hand, bionic vision sensors inherently suffer from perception limitations in the polarization dimension, making it difficult to achieve integration of polarization recognition and multifunctional synergy. The aforementioned issues severely impede the development of emerging functional device architectures utilizing two-dimensional intrinsic ionic-electronic co-modulation materials and consequently limit the advancement of neuromorphic computing toward greater efficiency and intelligence.

In this work, a low-symmetry quaternary van der Waals layered material AgVP2S6 is successfully synthesized via the chemical vapor transport (CVT) method. The morphology, crystalline quality, and orientation of the as-grown AgVP2S6 flakes are systematically characterized through optical microscopy (OM), atomic force microscopy (AFM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). The in-plane optical anisotropy is comprehensively investigated by angle-resolved polarized Raman spectroscopy (ARPRS), angle-resolved polarized absorption spectroscopy (ARPAS), and density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Notably, the synergistic interplay between the d-orbital degeneracy of V3+ cations and the substantial ionic radius disparity (Δr ≈ 0.35 Å) induces a pronounced Jahn-Teller distortion of V3+ ions within the sulfur octahedral coordination environment, resulting in the formation of a zigzag Ag chain structure. The coupling between anisotropic ionic migration and electronic conduction confers on the material exceptional optoelectronic anisotropy and tunable synaptic plasticity. Furthermore, these polarization reversal characteristics within the visible and near-infrared spectral region of AgVP2S6 enabled a seamless transition in image processing modalities from edge enhancement to identity filtering, establishing a multifunctional platform for the development of next-generation anisotropic optoelectronic devices applicable to neuromorphic computing, autonomous navigation, and multimodal imaging.

Results

Crystal structural and characterizations of AgVP2S6

Supplementary Fig. 1 shows a schematic of the ion-electron co-modulations design based on in-plane ionic anisotropy in AgVP2S6. As shown in Fig. 1a and Supplementary Fig. 2, a quaternary van der Waals layered material of AgVP2S6 is formed by substituting divalent metals (M2+) in the MPS3 structure type with 1/2 Ag+ and 1/2 V3+. Unlike the previously reported ferroelectric AgVP2S6 crystal25, our as-synthesized crystal belongs to the space group of P21/c with lattice parameters of a = 5.89 Å, b = 10.82 Å, c = 7.48 Å, α = 90°, β = 104.6°, and γ = 90°. The V atoms are located in edge-sharing distorted octahedral chains connected along the b-axis via S atoms, forming a stable V-S framework25. Ag atoms occupy specific interstitial sites within the layers, while the [P2S6] groups are composed of two edge-sharing [PS4] tetrahedra, enhancing the material’s structural stability and electronic properties. The quasi one-dimensional (1D) character of the ordered distribution of Ag and V in the layers probably arises from the fact that the sizes of the Ag- and V-containing octahedra are markedly different. The AgVP2S6 crystals are synthesized using a CVT method with Ag, V, P, and S powders as precursors. More detailed growth procedures are provided in the Methods and Supplementary Fig. 3. XRD patterns are employed to analyze the preferred growth orientation of AgVP2S6 crystals. The two diffraction peaks located at 13.7° and 27.4° align well with the (001) and (002) planes of AgVP2S6 crystals, indicating preferential growth orientation along the c-axis direction. XPS is used to analyze the valence states of AgVP2S6. The binding energies of 368.8 and 374.7 eV are attributed to Ag+ 3d5/2 and Ag+ 3d3/2, while the peaks located at 514.5 and 522.4 eV can be associated with the V3+ 2p3/2 and V3+ 2p1/2, respectively. Additionally, the binding energies located at 132.5, 133.4, and 135.0 eV are assigned to P 2p3/2, P 2p1/2 and an oxidized state, whereas the peaks located at 162.9 and 164.2 eV are attributed to the S 2p3/2 and S 2p3/2, consistent with previous studies16. The aberration-corrected STEM equipped with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) is further performed to investigate the elemental distribution and atomic stacking. The EDS and low-magnification HAADF images are presented in Fig. 1d and Supplementary Fig. 4. The EDS elemental mapping confirms a uniform distribution of Ag, V, P, and S elements across a strip-shaped flake. Additionally, the EDS spectrum reveals that the stoichiometric ratio of the elements Ag, V, P and S is approximately 1:1:2:6 (Supplementary Fig. 5). Typical experimentally measured atomically resolved HAADF-STEM images and selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns taken along the [001] direction is shown in Fig. 1e. A nearly perfect periodic atomic arrangement is observed with lattice spacing of 0.57 and 1.06 nm, corresponding to the (100) and (010) planes of the AgVP2S6 crystal, respectively. By combining the experimentally measured and simulated atomically resolved HAADF-STEM image and SAED, we achieved the identification and differentiation of the crystal structures, as shown in Fig. 1f and g. The SAED patterns obtained from experiments (left in Fig. 1f) and simulations (right in Fig. 1f) display identical square diffraction spots, consistent with a ribbon-like oriented morphology of the AgVP2S6 nanosheets. Furthermore, the experimental (left in Fig. 1g) and simulated (right in Fig. 1g) atomic-level HAADF-STEM images show high consistency, confirming the monoclinic structure of as-synthesized AgVP2S6. Typical low-magnification and locally enlarged optical microscopy images are presented in Fig. 1h and Supplementary Fig. 6, clearly revealing the belt-shaped morphology where the length corresponds to the a-axis and the width to the b-axis. The three-dimensional (3D) AFM topographic image displays an atomically flattened surface (Fig. 1i). Obviously, the thickness of the as-prepared AgVP2S6 is about 8 nm (Fig. 1j). The morphological characterization of AgVP2S6 nanosheets of varying thicknesses is shown in Supplementary Fig. 7. To understand the in-plane anisotropic phonon vibrations of AgVP2S6, we first characterized AgVP2S6 flakes on a confocal Raman microscope system. As shown in Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9, the 12 prominent peaks (P1-P12) within the range of 70-450 cm-1 exhibit a sharp intensity increase and a slight blue shift with increasing temperature, suggesting stable phonon modes without phase transitions. Figure 1k and l further exhibit the contour-color mapping of polarized Raman intensity in parallel and cross polarization modes. The 12 prominent Raman peaks display π- or π/2-period angular dependence with the polarization angle, indicating in-plane intrinsic optical anisotropy. The polar coordinate data are in excellent agreement with the corresponding fitting formulas (see Supplementary Figs. 10 and 11 for more details).

Calculated band structure and intrinsic anisotropy of AgVP2S6

DFT calculations are performed to investigate the band structure and intrinsic anisotropy of AgVP2S6. As depicted in Fig. 2a, the monolayer AgVP2S6 exhibits an indirect bandgap of approximately 1.92 eV, consistent with the experimentally observed absorption peak (Supplementary Fig. 12). The emergence of sharp S‑p peaks below the Fermi level (0 eV) demonstrates that the valence band maximum (VBM) is predominantly of S‑p orbitals character with significant contributions from Ag‑d orbitals (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Fig. 13). Concurrently, the conduction band minimum (CBM) is primarily composed of V‑d orbitals with mixed P‑p states (Fig. 2b and Supplementary Fig. 13). Moreover, strong P‑d hybridization between S‑p and metal d orbitals in the valence‑band region (‑3 to 0 eV) indicates that AgVP2S6 exhibits covalent bonding character coupled with ionic state contributions26. In line with crystal field theory and Jahn-Teller distortion27, the d-orbitals of vanadium (V) split into a lower-energy triply-degenerate t2g set (dxy, dyz, dxz) and a higher-energy doubly-degenerate subgroup (dz2, dx2−y2), as shown in Fig. 2c. The synergistic interplay between the d orbital degeneracy of V3+ and the substantial ionic radius difference (Δr ≈ 0.35 Å) induces a pronounced Jahn-Teller distortion of V3+ ions within the sulfur octahedra, resulting in the formation of a one-dimensional (1D) zigzag chain structure25,27,28. Given the 3 d34s2 valence electron configuration of V atoms, each V3+ cation in AgVP2S6 possesses two unpaired 3d electrons. According to Hund’s rule, these two electrons preferentially occupy two orbitals within the t2g set with parallel spins, creating a semi-occupied state that gives rise to an isolated, minimally dispersive flat band near the Fermi level28. The quenched kinetic energy of these localized states renders the system susceptible to symmetry-lowering distortions, driving a propensity for Jahn-Teller structural deformations that open a gap and lower the total energy. Such coupling between spin order and lattice degrees of freedom constitutes the origin of the semiconducting behavior in AgVP2S6. Besides, partial charge density (PCD) distributions offer valuable insights into the localization of electrons and holes in real space, which is crucial for comprehending anisotropic charge separation, carrier migration pathways, and optical response mechanisms. The PCD for the lowest CBM and the highest VBM along different axes reveals a highly asymmetric distribution (Fig. 2d, e), indicating a strong in-plane anisotropic character. To gain in-depth insight, the electron localization function (ELF) is used to characterize electron localization. As shown in Fig. 2f, g, the ELF displays elliptical, directionally extended bright regions within the a-c plane and connected structures in the b-direction slice, suggesting that the electron wavefunction exhibits strong extension and directionality within the crystal. Notably, isolated small dots within the a-c plane and separated bright spots in the b-direction slice indicate that electrons are primarily localized at atoms or within local regions. These results demonstrate that the PCD of the valence band (VB) is localized along the b- and c-axes, whereas the PCD of the conduction band (CB) is localized (or dispersive) along the ac plane (or bc plane), leading to anisotropic carrier transport behavior. As shown in Fig. 2h–k, the theoretically calculated optical constants, such as the real and imaginary parts (Re(ε) and Im(ε)), the absorption and refractive index, reveal a striking contrast, evidencing a significant in-plane birefringence in AgVP2S6. The substantial in-plane birefringence is primarily attributed to the highly asymmetric and localized electronic states of the lowest CB and the highest VB.

In-plane anisotropic ion-electron co-modulation mechanism

The in-plane anisotropic electrical behavior of ion-electron co-modulated AgVP2S6 arises from its inherently low crystal symmetry. Figure 3a, b illustrate typical Ids-Vds curves along the a- and b-axes of AgVP2S6. As Vds increases from 0 V to 1 V, Ids of the device increases gradually along the b-axis direction. In contrast, the electrical transport curves along the a-axis direction exhibit a two-step current change, implying an electric-field-driven Ag+ ion migration process. To gain deeper insights into the contributions of electron transport and ion migration, the Shockley emission model29 and the Poole-Frenkel30 model are employed (See Methods for details). Schottky emission describes thermionic emission at the metal–insulator interface, following the barrier-lowering relationship lnI ∝V1/2, where this interfacial mechanism operates independently of bulk ionic migration. In contrast, the Poole–Frenkel model, characterized by lnI ∝V3/2, governs bulk trap-assisted or ion-assisted conduction, wherein carrier transport necessitates either field-enhanced trap ionization or direct coupling with mobile ion redistribution within the crystal lattice (See Supplementary Note 2 for details). The fitting results indicate that the conductive behavior along the b-axis is well described by the Schottky emission model, suggesting that electron transport is the dominant mechanism. Meanwhile, the total current along the a-axis can be decomposed into an electron contribution component (Ielectron) and an ion migration contribution component (Iion), indicating a mixed conduction mechanism based on both electron transport and ion migration. As shown in Fig. 3c and Supplementary Fig. 14, the ion-electron co-modulation effect results in a high dichroic ratio (DR) with a maximum value reaching 10.3 with a thickness of 8 nm. Besides, temperature-dependent current/resistance extracted along both a- and b-axis directions (Supplementary Fig. 15) reveals the intrinsic semiconducting transport behavior with a pronounced anisotropy. To reveal the Ag+ ion migration processes driven by an electric field, HAADF-STEM and electron energy-loss spectroscopy (EELS) are utilized. Figure 3d and e show electric-field-driven element mapping and corresponding intensity profile curves along the a- and b-axes. After applying voltage (1 V) along the a-axis, the distribution of Ag element undergoes significant changes relative to the uniformly distributed V elements (Supplementary Fig. 16), with Ag⁺ ions aggregating at the positive electrode, providing evidence of Ag+ ion migration under electric field driven. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 17, the electric-field-induced Ag+ ion migration in AgVP2S6 exhibits fully reversible characteristics. Upon applying voltages of –1 V and +1 V along the a-axis, Ag⁺ ions reversibly migrate toward the respective cathodes and redistribute uniformly upon field reversal, while the V distribution remains spatially invariant throughout the first, second, and tenth cycles of alternating positive/negative voltage sweeping. This consistent back-and-forth migration of Ag⁺ ions, in stark contrast to the static V framework, substantiates fully reversible nature of the ionic transport from the perspective of elemental spatial dynamics. Physically, Ag+ ions occupy metastable interstitial sites within the lattice, forming double-well or multi-well potential energy surfaces. The applied electric field merely drives displacive switching of ions between different potential wells rather than permanent extraction from the lattice framework, enabling Ag+ ions to return along the original pathway or relax back to initial equilibrium positions upon field reversal. The reversible migration of Ag+ ions does not induce destructive collapse of the AgVP2S6 lattice, which can be attributed to a robust covalent framework constructed from [VP2S6] units and a “hopping” mechanism between lattice sites. To directly observe Ag+ ion migration paths, atomic-level in-situ electrical HAADF-STEM is employed. The low-magnification HAADF image of a nanochip used for in-situ electric-field STEM observations is displayed in Supplementary Figs. 18 and 19. The atomic evolution HAADF-STEM images of AgVP2S6 and the corresponding extracted atomic intensities under the original and voltage-driven states are vividly presented in Fig. 3f and g. Under electric field stimulation, more pronounced Ag+ ion migration along the a-axis direction than along the b-axis direction is observed, possibly attributed to the continuous zigzag chain-like arrangement of Ag along the a-axis and discontinuous distribution along the b-axis. To delve deeper into the underlying mechanisms, DFT calculations are performed to theoretically investigate Ag ion migration behavior along the a- and b-axes. The migration “barrier” refers to the minimum energy required for an Ag ion to jump from one stable position to an adjacent site, primarily involving the overcoming of electrostatic attraction from surrounding atoms and the lattice distortion energy. As shown in Fig. 4h, two diffusion directions along the a- and b-axes, termed path α and path β, can be clearly identified. The migration energy barriers along the a- and b-axes are further displayed in Fig. 3i, j, respectively. The energy barrier for Ag ion migration along path α is approximately 500 meV, significantly lower than that along path β (9 eV). These results demonstrate that the continuous zigzag chain-like path along the a-axis provides a low-energy pathway for ion migration and couples with electron transport. In contrast, ion migration along the b-axis is suppressed due to the high energy barrier, resulting in a conduction behavior dominated by electron transport.

Anisotropic of optoelectronic response and synaptic plasticity

The anisotropic ion migration behavior within AgVP2S6 provides potential for enhancing the anisotropic optoelectronic response. Figure 4a illustrates a schematic of the AgVP2S6 device exhibiting typical anisotropic optoelectronic and synaptic plasticity. The schematic illustration of the polarization-resolved photodetection measurement setup is shown in Supplementary Fig. 20. The output characteristics (Ids-Vds) under 671 nm illumination at varying power densities along the a- and b-axes of AgVP2S6 are presented in Fig. 4b. As the power density increases from 0.05 to 2.86 mW/cm2, Ids along the a-axis shows a significant increase from 0.6 to 2.7 μA, while the current on the b-axis only rises from 60 to 330 nA. The photocurrent (Iph) along the a-axis and b-axis directions can be quantitatively fitted using the formula: Iph∝αPβ, where α is constant, P is the power intensity, and β is the power factor. The power factor (β) is calculated to be as high as 0.3 and 0.1 for the a- and b-axes (Fig. 4c), which may be attributed to the increased interaction between photogenerated carriers and Ag+ ions31,32. To quantitatively evaluate the optoelectronic performance of the AgVP2S6 device along the a- and b-axes, the responsivity (R) and photocurrent anisotropy ratio (Iph-a/Iph-b) as functions of power intensity are depicted in Fig. 4d. The R and anisotropy ratio can be effectively tuned by the power density, achieving maximum values of 147 A/W and an anisotropy ratio of 5.8. Besides, the tunability of synaptic weight is crucial for mimicking biological synaptic functions33. Figure 4e demonstrates the typical synaptic plasticity of the AgVP2S6 device along different crystallographic axes. Compared to the dynamic response along the b-axis, the excitatory postsynaptic current (EPSC) intensity along the a-axis significantly increases with the power density, resulting in a higher dynamic range of weight change. Additionally, the time-resolved ΔEPSC for AgVP2S6 devices with thicknesses ranging from 14 nm to 194 nm are presented in Supplementary Figs. 21 and 22, demonstrate that the observed optoelectronic synaptic response constitutes an intrinsic in-plane anisotropic behavior rather than a random, trap-induced phenomenon. These results suggest that Ag+ ions exhibit greater mobility along the a-axis—parallel to the 1D zigzag Ag chains—than along the b-axis, enhancing synaptic tunability in the a-axis-oriented device. The EPSC response of AgVP2S6 devices along the a-and b-axes is further investigated by two consecutive optical pulses with varying widths (Fig. 4f and Supplementary Figs. 23-24). Upon applying light pulses (pulse width: 1 s) along the b-axis, the EPSC rapidly diminishes to its original state. In contrast, when two continuous optical pulses are applied along the a-axis, the EPSC does not readily return to its initial state, leading to the formation of short-term synaptic memory. The paired-pulse facilitation (PPF, A2/A1) index, crucial for assessing short-term plasticity, as a function of optical pulse widths is shown in Fig. 4g. The PPF behavior can be fitted by the double-exponential decay model: PPF index =a1 exp(−Δt/τ1)+a2 exp(−Δt/τ2), where τ1 and τ2 are rapid and slow phase relaxation times, Δt is the light pulse interval. The PPF time constant τ₁ (0.15 s, a‑axis) is faster than τ₂ (2.23 s, a‑axis) by more than an order of magnitude, representing the short‑term memory of the synapse behavior34. Furthermore, Fig. 4h shows the inhibitory EPSC response to a series of continuous light pulses along a and b-axes, indicating synaptic weight adjustment that could underpin the transition from short-term depression (STD) to long-term potentiation (LTP) memory35. With increasing pulse number, the EPSC along the a- and b-axes of AgVP2S6 devices exhibits an initial sharp rise followed by a decay, substantially deviating from the initial current level (Supplementary Fig. 25), indicating the evolution from STD to LTP of the artificial synapses. The optoelectronic synaptic behavior exhibited by AgVP2S6 fundamentally originates from an intrinsic ionic-electronic coupling mechanism, rather than merely from a trap-induced persistent photoconductivity (PPC) effect. Under illumination, photogenerated electrons are readily captured by specific localized sites within the material, forming negatively charged centers that induce a built-in electric field in their vicinity. This field acts as a driving force that significantly lowers the activation energy barrier for the migration of Ag+ ions along the one-dimensional zigzag chains (the a-axis), thereby promoting directional, long-range ionic migration toward the negatively charged regions to achieve local charge compensation. Such Ag+ migration is non-transient and elicits subtle local adjustments in the crystal structure: during a single light pulse, ions slightly deviate from their equilibrium positions and subsequently relax via thermally activated hopping from shallow metastable sites upon cessation of illumination, giving rise to STP. Under repeated pulsed stimulation, Ag+ ions exhibit progressively cumulative displacement along the a-axis, wherein cooperative effects from local electric fields generated by previously migrated ions stabilize reconfigured ionic arrangements, ultimately yielding a quasi-steady-state distribution characterized by markedly enhanced conductivity and a slow decay rate that manifests as LTP. The pronounced photocurrent enhancement can be rationalized from two complementary aspects: first, Ag+ migration toward trapped-electron regions passivates localized non-radiative recombination centers, prolonging the minority carrier lifetime and enabling more efficient carrier collection; second, the formation of continuous ionic channels along the a-axis provides a high-speed transport pathway that reduces electron-scattering probability, thereby enhancing the overall photoelectric conversion efficiency. Temperature-dependent photocurrent measurements reveal the transport mechanism of the anisotropic optoelectronic synaptic behavior (Supplementary Fig. 26). Below 197 K, Ag+ ionic conduction is effectively frozen due to insufficient thermal activation energy, and the light-induced synaptic response is drastically attenuated. So, the variations in the EPSC are dominated by the pure photoconductive effects, exhibiting rapid response characteristics along both the a-axis and the b-axis. This rapid response behavior arises because photogenerated carrier dynamics alone, without ionic contribution, are insufficient to produce pronounced photonic synaptic behaviors. Above 197 K, however, ionic conduction is progressively activated, leading to a pronounced divergence in photoelectric responses. Specifically, the a-axis of AgVP2S6 exhibits robust synaptic plasticity originating from synergistic ion-electron co-modulation, where photogenerated electrons facilitate Ag+ migration through charge compensation mechanisms, enabling long-term potentiation and memory effects. Further details regarding the mechanism of anisotropic optoelectronic synaptic behavior are provided in Supplementary Note 3.

Taking advantage of the distinct synaptic plasticity of the device along different crystallographic axes, it can be implemented for efficient and accurate object recognition, showing great potential in fields such as autonomous navigation and surveillance. To validate the advantages of this anisotropic synaptic performance in practical applications, the AgVP2S6 device is used to extract feature information from the park landscape image along the a-and b-axes (Fig. 4i, j). As the number of light pulses increases, the contrast distinctions emerging between elements such as roads, trees, and pavilions of the simulated image along the a-axis are significantly higher than that along the b-axis. As the number of light pulses increases, the distinctions between elements such as roads, trees, and pavilions in the simulated image along the a-axis gradually become clearer. Notably, the image contrast along the a-axis is markedly superior to that along the b-axis, further confirming the critical role of anisotropic synaptic devices in extracting effective information from complex environments. To better visualize these results, the pixel distribution histograms of each grayscale image are presented in Fig. 4k, l. The pixel distribution range expands as the number of pulses increases, implying the extraction of more effective information from the image. Compared to the b-axis, the a-axis histograms exhibit a wider pixel distribution, further demonstrating that the anisotropic synaptic device can reduce reflections and enhance image feature information. These results suggest that higher synaptic plasticity along the a-axis results in a broader weight dynamic range, enabling finer and more extensive mapping and enhancement of input optical signals and effectively improving the signal-to-noise ratio and image detail resolution.

Polarization reversal for multimodal imaging processing

A schematic of the polarization-sensitive AgVP2S6 synaptic device under 671 and 914 nm illumination is presented in Fig. 5a. Initially, the 2D mapping of polarization angle-dependent absorption intensity is investigated (Fig. 5b and Supplementary Fig. 27), revealing a distinct antiphase relationship as a function of polarization angle in the visible and near-infrared spectral regions. The extracted absorption intensity as a function of polarization angle at wavelengths of 671 and 914 nm is further illustrated in polar coordinates (Fig. 5c). The phase difference of π/2 in absorption intensity for these two wavelengths is attributed to the wavelength-selective interband transitions, the coupling to Ag⁺ ion excitation, the synergistic modulation by polarization -angle-dependent transition dipole orientation, and the photoinduced dynamic reconstruction of ion migration states (see Supplementary Note 4 for a detailed explanation). To explore the relationship between the polarization-perceptual EPSC and polarization angle, three consecutive light pulses with different polarization angles are applied under 671 nm and 914 nm illumination (Fig. 5d). By statistically analyzing the peak current of the third pulse, the evolution of the photocurrent plotted in polar coordinates under polarized 671 nm and 914 nm illumination is shown in Fig. 5e. The photocurrent anisotropy ratio reaches up to 2.1 and 1.3 under 671 nm and 914 nm illumination with polarization angles of 90°and 0°, respectively, indicating the pronounced polarization-tunable capability of the AgVP2S6-based photodetector. Significantly, as depicted in Supplementary Fig. 28, although the absolute photocurrent values increase with increasing light intensity, the polarization-dependent behavior remains essentially unchanged, which confirms that the polarity reversal is not determined by the power density of the incident light. Besides, the power density serves to enhance the anisotropic ratio without altering the polarity reversal phenomenon. Besides, considering polarization reversal synaptic characteristics, the device exhibits considerable potential for multimodal imaging applications. A 3×3 polarimetric kernel array consisting of parallel AgVP2S6 detectors is formed, where the convolution kernel matrix is constructed36,37. The polarization photocurrent values are determined by the polarization photocurrents and positions of the devices (More experimental details are provided in Supplementary Note 5). The circuit diagrams for processing multimodal images using identity with edge-enhanced features and Sobel-X/Sobel-Y kernel combinations are shown in Fig. 5f, g. Specific weights are assigned to the kernels based on the polarity reversal of polarization photocurrents within the visible and near-infrared bands, enabling diverse image processing tasks. By convolving the original image with the polarized kernel matrix and integrating intensity, polarization, and spectral response regions, enhanced images are produced. As the polarization angle ranges from 0° to 180°, the processed images achieve a seamless transition from edge-enhanced to identity-filtered imaging (Fig. 5h). Identity filtering preserves static image features, while edge enhancement emphasizes boundaries and transitions to highlight dynamic features. Further, by continuously shifting the polarization angle, the processed images transition seamlessly from Sobel-X to Sobel-Y processing, achieving angle-correlated edge detection (Supplementary Video 1). The simulation results, validated against the Cameraman test image38, confirm the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed polarization-reversal synaptic device for mixed multimodal image processing.

Discussion

In summary, we have successfully synthesized AgVP2S6, a low-symmetry quaternary van der Waals material that enables remarkable ionic-electronic co-modulation through intrinsic structural anisotropy. The excellent in-plane optical anisotropy is unambiguously confirmed by ARPRS and atomic-resolution HAADF-STEM characterization. Critically, the strong P‑d hybridization between S‑p and metal d orbitals in the valence‑band region gives rise to a unique electronic structure, indicating covalent bonding character coupled with ionic state contributions. The pronounced anisotropy in Ag+ migration pathways—exhibiting a much lower energy barrier along the a-axis compared to the b-axis— is facilitated by a 1D zigzag chain structure, leading to a high dichroic ratio of up to 10.3. Besides, AgVP2S6 devices display pronounced visible-to-near-infrared spectral response, high-performance polarization-sensitive photodetection, programmable synaptic plasticity, and advanced image processing capabilities. The polarization-reversal synaptic device under 671 nm and 914 nm illumination confirms the effectiveness and reliability for mixed multimodal image processing. Our work paves the way for the development of polarization-resolved device architectures oriented toward polarized information processing, advancing neuromorphic computing toward higher efficiency and enriched functionality.

Methods

Crystal synthesis

AgVP2S6 crystals were synthesized using the chemical vapor transport (CVT) method. Typically, Ag, V, P, and S powders with a small amount of transport agent I2 were loaded into a quartz tube at a molar ratio of 1:1:2:6 under a vacuum of 10 Pa. The sealed tube was placed in a single-zone tube furnace, and the reaction zone was heated to 750 °C within 6 hours. After one week of growth, bulk crystals were obtained in the growth region. Few-layer AgVP2S6 nanosheets were then mechanically exfoliated from bulk crystals onto a SiO2/Si substrate using 3 M scotch tape (Nanjing MKNANO Tech. Co., Ltd.(www.mukenano.com)).

Material Characterization

The morphology and thickness of AgVP2S6 were investigated using an optical microscope (OM, D4000M, Leica) and an atomic force microscope (AFM, multimode 8, Bruker). Polarization-resolved and temperature-dependent Raman spectroscopy were performed using a confocal Raman spectroscope (JY Horiba, HR Evolution). The crystal growth orientation was examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was employed to analyze the elemental valence states. The atomic stacking and arrangement were investigated using scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM, JEM-ARM300F2, JEOL). Polarized micro-area UV-vis-NIR absorption spectra were collected in transmission mode using a confocal spectrometer (Metatest, MStarter ABS).

Device fabrication and measurements

The AgVP2S6 nanosheet electrodes were fabricated using laser direct writing, followed by thermal evaporation of the metal and lift-off processing. The photoelectric response data under 671 nm and 914 nm illumination were acquired using a Keithley 2634B source. Wavelength switching between 671 nm and 914 nm is achieved by coaxially coupling two solid-state lasers into a common optical path via precision alignment stages, with high-speed mechanical shutters alternately gating the beams under interlocked timing control for dynamic spectral reconfiguration. Alternatively, a broadband xenon lamp equipped with an electrically controlled filter wheel (centred at 671 nm and 914 nm) enables millisecond-scale wavelength conversion. The time-resolved polarized light response was obtained by rotating a half-wave plate in combination with a signal generator. All measurements were conducted at room temperature.

Density functional theory (DFT) calculation

All the calculations are performed in the framework of the density functional theory with the projector augmented plane-wave method, as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP). We used the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Pedrew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) function. The cut-off energy for plane wave is set to 500 eV. The energy criterion is set to 10−5 eV in the iterative solution of the Kohn-Sham equation. The Brillouin zone integration is performed using a 4x2x1 k-mesh. All the structures are relaxed until the residual forces on the atoms have declined to less than 0.01 eV/Å. The role of the vdW interaction was investigated using the DFT-D2 method, including the pairwise force field implemented by Grimme. The Climbing Images Nudged Elastic Band (CI-NEB) method is employed to calculate the migration energy barrier by constructing a climbing image along the minimum energy path between initial and final states, with the image forces being used to drive the atoms towards the transition state. The optical properties of the crystal structure were further calculated and analyzed using the HSE06 hybrid functional method.

Profinite genus of HNN-extensions with finite associated subgroups
V.R. de Bessa, A.L.P. Porto, P.A. Zalesskii

Abstract
We study the profinite genus of HNN-extensions whose associated subgroups are finite. We give precise formulas for the number of isomorphism classes of HNN ⁡ ( G , H , K , t , f ) {\rm HNN}(G,H,K,t,f) and of its profinite completion and compute the profinite genus of such an HNN-extension HNN ⁡ ( G , H , K , t , f ) {\rm HNN}(G,H,K,t,f) . We also list various situations when HNN ⁡ ( G , H , K , t , f ) {\rm HNN}(G,H,K,t,f) is determined by its profinite completion.

Introduction

There has been much recent study of whether residually finite groups, or classes of residually finite groups of combinatorial nature may be distinguished from each other by their sets of finite quotient groups.

In group theory the study in this direction started in 70-th of the last century when Baumslag
[
3
]
, Stebe
[
42

and others found examples of finitely generated residually finite groups having the same set of finite quotients. The general question addressed in this study can be formulated as follows:

Question 1

To what extent a finitely generated residually finite group
\Gamma
is determined by its finite quotients?

The study leaded to the notion of genus
\mathfrak{g}(G)
of a residually finite group
G

G

\mathfrak{g}(G)
is the set of isomorphism classes of finitely generated residually finite groups having the profinite completion isomorphic to the profinite completion
\widehat{G}
of
G
.

This study mostly was concentrated to establish whether the cardinality of the genus
\mathfrak{g}(G)
is finite or 1 (see

for example) (we use the same term

[

However, the following principle question of Remeslennikov is still open
[

.

Question 2
.

(V.N. Remeslennikov)
Is the genus
g(F)
of a free group
F
of finite rank equals 1?

There are two basic constructions in combinatorial group theory: free product with amalgamation and HNN-extension. As many groups of combinatorial and geometric nature split as an amalgamted free product or HNN-extension, it has sense to study the genus of these constructions. It is natural to start with splitting over finite groups, and for free products with amalgamation this was done in
[
6
]
. However, since Remeslennikov’s question is not answered, it is not clear whether the profinite completion of an one-ended group does not split as an amalgamated free product or an HNN-extension over a finite group. This naturally gives restriction to the family in which one does considerations.

A finitely generated residually finite group
G
with less than 2 ends, i.e. a finite or one ended group, will be called an
{\rm OE}
-group in the paper. It follows from the famous Stallings theorem that
G
is an
{\rm OE}
-group if and only if whenever it acts on a tree with finite edge stabilizers
it has a global fixed point. A finitely generated profinite group will be called
{\rm OE}
-group if it has the same property: whenever it acts on a profinite tree with finite edge stabilizers, then it fixes a vertex. Note that finite and one-ended groups acting on a tree have a global fixed point and we need to extend this property to the profinite completion. A finitely generated residually finite group
G
will be called
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group if
\widehat{G}
is a profinite
{\rm OE}
-group, i.e.
\widehat{G}
can not act on a profinite tree with finite edge stabilizers without a global fixed point (see Definition
2.2
for the definition of a profinite tree).

Note that the class
\rm\widehat{OE}
-groups is quite large. It contains all finitely generated residually finite not infinite virtually cyclic soluble groups, virtually surface groups, indecomposable (into free products) 3-manifold groups, indecomposable RAAGs, as well as all arithmetic groups of rank
>1
and finitely generated residually finite Fab groups (see Propositions
2.6
and
2.7
).

The subject of the present paper is to complement the work that was done in
[
5

and
[
6
]
by studying the profinite genus of HNN-extensions of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group whose associated subgroups are finite. Note that

2.1

In this subsection we recall the necessary notions of the Bass-Serre theory for abstract and profinite graphs.

Definition 2.1

A (profinite) graph is a (profinite space) set

every profinite graph

is an inverse limit of finite quotient graphs of

Proposition 2.3

(i)

(b)

is a profinite amalgamated free product

Let

of fixed points with respect to the action of

2.3
OE-groups

A finitely generated residually finite group
G
with less than 2 ends, i.e. a finite or one-ended group, will be called an
{\rm OE}
-group in the paper. It follows from the famous Stallings theorem
[
36
]
that
G
is an
{\rm OE}
-group if and only if whenever it acts on a tree with finite edge stabilizers
it has a global fixed point. A finitely generated profinite group will be called
{\rm OE}
-group if it has the same property: whenever it acts on a profinite tree with finite edge stabilizers, then it fixes a vertex.

A finitely generated residually finite group
G
will be called
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group if
\widehat{G}
is
{\rm OE}
-group. Note that an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group is automatically
{\rm OE}
-group, since if a finitely generated residually finite group
G
splits as an amalgamated free product or HNN-extension over a finite group then so does
\widehat{G}
.

Since we do not know whether 1-endedness is a profinite property, we do not know any single example of finitely generated residually finite 1-ended group which is not
\rm\widehat{OE}
. In this section we prove
several propositions that give sufficient conditions for
G
to be an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group showing that the class of
\rm\widehat{OE}
-groups is quite large and contains many important families of examples of
\rm\widehat{OE}
-groups.

Proposition 2.6
.

(
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
) Let
G
be a finitely generated residually finite group such that
\widehat{G}
does not have a non-abelian free pro-
p
subgroup. If
G
is not virtually infinite cyclic, then
G
is an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group.

If
G
satisfies an identity, then
\widehat{G}
satisfies the same identity and so does not have non-abelian free pro-
p
subgroups, so it is an
{\rm OE}
and an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group by Proposition
2.6
unless it is virtually infinite cyclic.

The result below was proven in Propositions 3.2 and 3.3 of
[
5
]
and gives several examples of
\rm\widehat{OE}
-groups.

Proposition 2.7
.

The following groups are
\rm\widehat{OE}
-groups.

(i)

Virtually surface groups;

(ii)

Indecomposable (into a free product) 3-manifold groups;

(iii)

Indecomposable (into a free product) finitely generated Right-Angled Artin groups;

(iv)

Arithmetic groups of
rank>1
;

(v)

Just infinite and finitely generated residually finite Fab groups.

The following result shows that the class
{\rm OE}
-groups is closed for extensions and commensurability.

Proposition 2.8
.

(i)

Let
H
be a normal (closed) subgroup of a (profinite) group

If

and

are (profinite)
{\rm OE}

{\rm OE}
-group;

(ii)

If

Let

1. Note that

Therefore

\psi(K)=\psi(G_{1}\cap G_{1}^{t})=B_{1}\cap B_{1}^{\psi(t)}=K_{1}^{b_{1}}
for some
b_{1}\in B_{1}
(we cannot have
\psi(K)=H_{1}^{b_{1}}
because
H
and
K
are not conjugate in
G_{1}
).

Conversly, if
f_{1}=\tau_{b_{1}}f^{\psi^{-1}}
we define a map
\varphi
from
G
to
B
as follows

\varphi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}g_{1}&\longmapsto&\psi(g_{1}),\forall g_{1}\in G_{1},\\
t&\longmapsto&t_{1}b_{1}\\
\end{array}\right.

and observe that
\varphi
is a continuous isomorphism.
∎

The result below is valid for abstract and profinite HNN-extensions.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
be an abstract (profinite) HNN-extension such that
H^{g_{1}}=K
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}.
Then

G\cong B={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,H,f_{1},t_{1}),

where
f_{1}=\left(\tau_{g_{1}}\circ f\right)^{\pm 1}\in{\rm Aut}(H).

Proof.

First suppose that
f_{1}=\tau_{g_{1}}\circ f.
Define the following isomorphism of
G
to
{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,H,\tau_{g_{1}}\circ f,t_{1}),

\psi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}g_{1}&\longmapsto&g_{1},\forall g_{1}\in G_{1},\\
t&\longmapsto&t_{1}g_{1}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Looking at presentation one sees indeed that this defines uniquely an isomorphism. To finish the proof, define the isomorphism

{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,H,\tau_{g_{1}}\circ f,t_{1})\longrightarrow{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,H,(\tau_{g_{1}}\circ f)^{-1},t_{1})

setting

\varphi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}g_{1}&\longmapsto&g_{1},\forall g_{1}\in G_{1},\\
t_{1}&\longmapsto&t_{1}^{-1}.\\
\end{array}\right.

∎

Proposition 2.17
.

Let

G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)\ {\rm and}\ B={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f_{1},t_{1})

be HNN-extensions with associated finite subgroups in an
{\rm OE}
-group
G_{1}
. Suppose that
K=H
or
H
and
K
are not conjugate in
G_{1}
. Then
G\cong B
if and only if

(i)

there exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
with
\psi(H)=K
and
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}(k)=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f^{-1}(k),\forall k\in K,
or

(ii)

there exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
with
\psi(H)=H
and
\psi(K)=K^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}(h)=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f(h),\forall h\in H.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
By Proposition
2.14
there is an isomorphism
\psi:B\longrightarrow G
such that
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=H\mbox{ and }

\psi(K)=K^{w}
or
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=K\mbox{ and }\psi(K)=H^{w}
for some
w\in G_{1}
.

If
H=K
then
w\in N_{G_{1}}(H)
and
\psi(t_{1})=h_{1}t^{\pm 1}h_{2}\in N_{G}(H)
with
h_{1},h_{2}\in N_{G_{1}}(H)
by Lemma 2.11, p. 822 in
[
7
]
. As we have discussed throughout this article, we can replace the isomorphism
\psi
by
\tau_{h_{1}^{-1}}\psi
, if necessary, to assume that
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1},

\psi(H)=H
and
\psi(t_{1})=t^{\pm 1}h_{2}h_{1}.
Put
g_{1}=h_{2}h_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(H)
. Then for so for all
h\in H,
we have

\psi(f_{1}(h))=\psi(h^{t_{1}})=\psi(h)^{t^{\pm 1}g_{1}}=(\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f^{\pm 1}\psi)(h).

Therefore
f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}(h)=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f^{\pm 1}(h),\forall h\in H
and (i) or (ii) holds.

If
H
and
K
are not conjugate in
G_{1}
. We will divide the proof into two parts.

Case i)
Suppose that
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=K\mbox{ and }\psi(K)=H^{w}
with
w\in G_{1}
. Put
\psi(t_{1})=g\in G
. Note that

\psi(K)=H^{w}=\psi(H)^{\psi(t_{1})}=K^{g}=H^{tg},

so that
wg^{-1}t^{-1}\in N_{G}(H)
. It follows from Lemma 2.11 in
[
7
]
that
wg^{-1}=xty\in\left(N_{G_{1}}(H)\cdot t\cdot N_{G_{1}}(K)\right)
. Thus replacing
\psi
by
\tau_{y}\circ\psi
, if necessary (where
\tau_{y}\in\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)
), we may assume that
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=K
,
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
and
\psi(t_{1})=t^{-1}g_{1}
, where
g_{1}=x^{-1}wy^{-1}.
For each
h\in H
, we have

\psi(f_{1}(h))=\psi(h^{t_{1}})=\psi(h)^{\psi(t_{1})}=\psi(h)^{t^{-1}g_{1}}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f^{-1}(\psi(h)).

Therefore
f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}(k)=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f^{-1}(k),\forall k\in K
and (i) holds.

Case ii)
Suppose
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=H\mbox{ and }

\psi(K)=K^{w}
. Similar as in case (i), put
\psi(t_{1})=g\in G
and observe that
gw^{-1}t^{-1}\in N_{G}(H)
. It follows from Lemma 2.11 in
[
7
]
that
gw^{-1}=xty\in\left(N_{G_{1}}(H)\cdot t\cdot N_{G_{1}}(K)\right)
. Thus replacing
\psi
by
\tau_{x^{-1}}\circ\psi
, if necessary (where
\tau_{x^{-1}}\in\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
), we may assume that
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=H
,
\psi(K)=K^{g_{1}}
and
\psi(t_{1})=tg_{1}
, where
g_{1}=ywx.
For each
h\in H
, we have

\psi(f_{1}(h))=\psi(h^{t_{1}})=\psi(h)^{\psi(t_{1})}=\psi(h)^{tg_{1}}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f(\psi(h)),

Therefore
f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}(h)=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f(h),\forall h\in H
and (ii) holds.

(\Leftarrow)
Follows from the Proposition
2.14
.

∎

3
Isomorphism problem for abstract HNN-extensions

In this section we will deal with the isomorphism problem of abstract HNN-extensions.

Let
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f_{1},t_{1})\mbox{ and }B={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f_{2},t_{2})
be abstract HHN-extensions, where
G_{1}
is an OE-group and
f_{i}:H\longrightarrow K\,(i=1,2)
are isomorphisms between finite subgroups
H,K
of
G_{1}
, i.e
f_{i}\in{\rm Iso}(H,K)\,(i=1,2)
. We find necessary and
sufficient conditions for
G\cong B
in terms of the orbits of the actions that will be defined below. Note that we are not trying to find an algorithm that decides whether given HNN-extensions are isomorphic. Our goal is to describe in terms of HNN-extensions when they are isomorphic.
Some parts of the text in this section are adaptations of Sections 3 and 4 in
[
7
]
.

Let
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
be an abstract HNN-extension. Consider the subgroup

\Gamma_{H}:=G_{1}\rtimes{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)

(5)

of the holomorph
G_{1}\rtimes{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
.
Sometimes we shall interpret the action of an element
g_{1}
of
G_{1}
in
\Gamma_{H}
as the inner automorphism
\tau_{g_{1}},
which should not be confused with the element of
{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
(from the copy of
{\rm Inn}(G_{1})
in
{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
).

Denote by
S
the set of all finite subgroups of
G_{1}
. There is a natural action of
\Gamma_{H}=G_{1}\rtimes{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)
on the set
S
namely

(g_{1},\alpha)\cdot X=g_{1}\alpha(X)g_{1}^{-1}\in S,\mbox{ where }(g_{1},\alpha)\in\Gamma_{H},X\in S.

(6)

Let us consider now

\Upsilon:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{X\in S}{\rm Iso}(H,X)
. Then the action of
\Gamma_{H}
on
S
induces the natural action
of
\Gamma_{H}
on the left on
\Upsilon
as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 3.1
.

The semidirect product
\Gamma_{H}=G_{1}\rtimes{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)

acts from the left upon
\Upsilon
by

(g_{1},\alpha)\cdot f=\tau_{g_{1}}\alpha f\alpha^{-1}=\tau_{g_{1}}f^{\alpha^{-1}},\mbox{ where }(g_{1},\alpha)\in\Gamma_{H}\mbox{
and }f\in\Upsilon.

(7)

Proof.

For showing that the action is well-defined choose arbitrary
f\in\Upsilon
and
\gamma=(g_{1},\alpha)\in\Gamma_{H}
. Then
(g_{1},\alpha)\cdot f\in{\rm Iso}(H,L)
for
L:=\left[\alpha(f(H))\right]^{g_{1}^{-1}}\in S
and so
\gamma\cdot f\in\Upsilon
. Now certainly
(1,1)\cdot f=f
holds for all
f\in\Upsilon
. Finally, if
(g_{1},\alpha),(g_{2},\beta)\in\Gamma_{H}
and
f\in\Upsilon
then we have

(g_{1},\alpha)\cdot((g_{2},\beta)\cdot f)=\tau_{g_{1}}(\tau_{g_{2}}f^{\beta^{-1}})^{\alpha^{-1}}=\tau_{g_{1}}\alpha\tau_{g_{2}}\beta f\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}=

=\tau_{g_{1}}\tau_{g_{2}}^{\alpha^{-1}}f^{(\alpha\beta)^{-1}}=((g_{1},\alpha)(g_{2},\beta))\cdot f.

∎

Note that for
f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K)
, we have
(g_{1},\alpha)\cdot f\in{\rm Iso}\left(H,[\alpha(K)]^{g_{1}^{-1}}\right)\subset\Upsilon
holds true ( see (
7
)).

Suppose there exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
that swaps the conjugacy classes of
H
and
K
, i.e. such that
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
and
\psi(H)=K
, for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
. Define
\iota:{\rm Iso}(H,K)\longrightarrow{\rm Iso}(H,K)
by

\iota(f)=\tau_{\psi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}\left(f^{\psi}\right)^{-1},f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K).

(8)

When
H=K
we can choose
\psi=id
and
g_{1}=1,
so
\iota
sends
f
to
f^{-1}.
In this situation we have an isomorphism between
{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f)
and
{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f^{-1})
(see Lemma
2.16
). Note that
\iota
will be a bijection from
{\rm Iso}(H,H)={\rm Aut}(H)
to
{\rm Iso}(H,H)={\rm Aut}(H)
in this case. More generally,
\iota
belongs to the symmetric group
Sym({\rm Iso}(H,K)),
of
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
.

We denote by
\Gamma_{HK}
be the stabilizer of
K\in S
of this action of
\Gamma_{H}
on
S
. Then
\Gamma_{HK}
leaves
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
invariant according to the action of
\Gamma_{H}
on
\Upsilon
.
Since
\Gamma_{HK}
acts on
{\rm Iso}(H,K),
we can consider the natural image of
\Gamma_{HK}
in
Sym({\rm Iso}(H,K))
and denote it by
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose there exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
that swaps the conjugacy classes of
H
and
K
. Then
\iota
normalizes
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}
and so
\langle\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK},\iota\rangle=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}\cdot\left\langle\iota\right\rangle
is a subgroup of
Sym({\rm Iso}(H,K))
.

Proof.

We need to show that if
\tilde{\omega}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}
then
\tilde{\omega}^{\iota}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}
. Indeed, pick
\omega=(x,\alpha)\in\Gamma_{HK}
such that
\tilde{w}
is its image in
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}.
Choose
f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K)
and observe that

\tilde{\omega}^{\iota}.f=\iota^{-1}\tilde{\omega}(\tau_{\psi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}(f^{-1})^{\psi})=\iota^{-1}(\tau_{x\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}(f^{-1})^{\psi\alpha^{-1}})=\newline
=(f)^{\psi\alpha^{-1}\psi^{-1}}\tau_{\psi(\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1})x^{-1})g_{1}^{-1}}=\tau_{\psi(\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1})x^{-1})g_{1}^{-1}}(f)^{\psi\alpha^{-1}\psi^{-1}\tau_{\psi(\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1})x^{-1})g_{1}^{-1}}}=\newline
=(\tau_{\psi(\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1})x^{-1})g_{1}^{-1}},\tau_{g_{1}\psi(x\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1}))}\psi\alpha\psi^{-1}).f
. But

(\psi(\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1})x^{-1})g_{1}^{-1},\tau_{g_{1}\psi(x\alpha\psi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1}))}\psi\alpha\psi^{-1})\in\Gamma_{HK}

and its image in
Sym({\rm Iso}(H,K))
is
\tilde{w}^{\iota}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{HK},
as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The subgroup
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}\cdot\left\langle\iota\right\rangle
of
Sym({\rm Iso}(H,K))
does not depend upon the choice of
\psi
.

Proof.

Consider
\zeta\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
such that
\zeta(K)=H^{r}
and
\zeta(H)=K
, for some
r\in G_{1}
. Let
\Theta\in Sym({\rm Iso}(H,K))
such that
\Theta(f):=\tau_{{}_{\zeta^{-1}(r^{-1})}}\left(f^{-1}\right)^{\zeta}
, where
f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K)
. Take
\upsilon=(\zeta^{-1}(r^{-1}\cdot g_{1}),\zeta^{-1}\psi)\in\Gamma_{HK}
such that
\tilde{\upsilon}
is its image in
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK},
and observe that
\Theta=\tilde{\upsilon}\iota\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}\cdot\left\langle\iota\right\rangle.

∎

Definition 3.4
.

Given finite subgroups
H
and
K
of
G_{1}
we set

\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}:=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}\cdot\left\langle\iota\right\rangle

if there exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
such that
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
and
\psi(H)=K
, for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
and put

\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}:=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}

otherwise. Further let

\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}\backslash{\rm Iso}(H,K)

denote the set of orbits of
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
modulo the action of
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
.

Remark 3.5
.

When
H=K
then
{\rm Iso}(H,H)={\rm Aut}(H)
. In this case one can define
\Gamma_{HH}=\Gamma_{H}=N_{G_{1}}(H)\rtimes{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)
as being the stabilizer of
H
of the action of
\Gamma_{H}
on
S
(see (
6
)) and

\overline{\Gamma}_{HH}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{H}\cdot C_{2}
, where
C_{2}=\left\langle\iota\right\rangle
acts by inversion on
{\rm Aut}(H)
. Note that if
\iota\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{H}
then
\overline{\Gamma}_{HH}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{H},
otherwise
\overline{\Gamma}_{HH}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{H}\rtimes C_{2}.

In particular,
\overline{\Gamma}_{HH}\backslash{\rm Aut}(H)
denote the set of orbits of
{\rm Aut}(H)
modulo the action of
\overline{\Gamma}_{HH}
.

The result below is a consequence of Propositions
2.17
and the results of this subsection. They generalize Theorem 4.5 in
[
7
]
to
{\rm OE}
-groups.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G_{1}
be an
{\rm OE}
-group and

G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t),\ B={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f_{1},t_{1})

be abstract HNN-extensions with finite associated subgroups. Then
G
and
B
are isomorphic iff
f
and
f_{1}
belong to the same
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
-orbit of
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
We have two possibilities: either
H
and
K
are conjugate in the base group
G_{1}
or not. Moreover, in the first case we may assume that
H=K
by Lemma
2.16
.

Thus suppose first that
H=K
. By Proposition
2.17
there exist
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)
and
g_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(H)
such that

(f_{1})^{\varphi^{-1}}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}(f)^{\epsilon}\iff\tau_{g_{1}}f_{1}^{\varphi^{-1}}=(f)^{\epsilon},

where
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}.
If
\epsilon=1
take
(g_{1},\varphi)\in\Gamma_{H}
and observe that

(g_{1},\varphi)\cdot f_{1}=\tau_{g_{1}}f_{1}^{\varphi^{-1}}=f.

If
\epsilon=-1
take
\beta=(\varphi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1}),\varphi^{-1})\in\Gamma_{H}
and observe that
\iota(f)=f^{-1}
(just consider
\psi=id
in the Definition of
\iota
in (
8
)). In this way, we have

(\beta,\iota)\cdot f=\tau_{\varphi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}\left(f^{-1}\right)^{\varphi}=f_{1}.

Therefore for any
\epsilon=\pm 1
we have that
f_{1}
and
f
are in the same
\overline{\Gamma}_{HH}
-orbit.

Now suppose
that
H
and
K
are not conjugate in
G_{1}
. By Proposition
2.17

G\cong B
if, and only if, the subcases i) or ii) of Proposition
2.17
are satisfied.

Subcase i)
: There exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
with
\psi(H)=K
and
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}(k)=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f^{-1}(k),\forall k\in K
. By definition of
\iota
in (
8
) we have

\iota(f)=\tau_{\psi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}(f^{-1})^{\psi}=f_{1}.

Therefore
f_{1}
and
f
belong to the same
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
-orbit of
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
.

Subcase ii)
: There exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
with
\psi(H)=H
and
\psi(K)=K^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}(h)=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f(h),\forall h\in H
. Observe that
(g_{1},\psi)\in\Gamma_{HK}
and

(g_{1},\psi)\cdot f_{1}=\tau_{g_{1}}f_{1}^{\psi^{-1}}=f.

Therefore
f_{1}
and
f
belong to the same orbit.

(\Leftarrow)
It follows from Proposition
2.17
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
G_{1}
be an
{\rm OE}
-group and
H\cong K
be fixed finite subgroups of
G_{1}
.
The number of isomorphism classes of abstract HNN-extensions of the form
{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
equals to the number of
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
-orbits

|\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}\backslash{\rm Iso}(H,K)|

(9)

in
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
.

3.1
The number of isomorphism classes of abstract HNN-extensions

Our first goal is to count the isomorphism classes of HNN-extensions

{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)

with finite associated subgroups.

Let
S
be the set of all finite subgroups of
G_{1}
. We have an action of
G_{1}
on
S
by conjugation. So, given
L\in S
the orbit of
L
with respect to this action (i.e., the conjugacy class of

in
G_{1}

Denote by
\overline{S}

Let
[\overline{S}]^{2}

\{X,Y\}
such that

by

(10)

Note that
\{\langle\langle H\rangle\rangle^{G_{1}},\langle\langle K\rangle\rangle^{G_{1}}\}
and
\{\langle\langle H_{1}\rangle\rangle^{G_{1}},\langle\langle K_{1}\rangle\rangle^{G_{1}}\}
belong to the same
{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
-orbit of action (
10
) if and only if there exist
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
such that
\psi(H)=H_{1}
and
\psi(K)=K_{1}^{g_{1}}
for some

Let
H_{1},K_{1}\in S,
by Proposition
2.14
, for any

If
H
and

are finite subgroups of
G_{1},
we saw in Corollary

that the number of isomorphism classes of abstract HNN-extensions of the form

is

.

Denote the set of orbits of action (
10
) as
{\rm Aut}(G_{1})\backslash[\overline{S}]^{2}
. From the preceding discussion and Theorem
3.6
we have

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
G_{1}
be an
{\rm OE}
-group. Then the number of isomorphism classes of abstract HNN-extensions

ranges over representatives of the orbits

Let
S_{{}_{H}}\subset S

isomorphic to

.
By Proposition
2.14
isomorphic HNN-extensions with finite associated subgroups must have isomorphic associated subgroups and by Proposition
2.15

Therefore for the calculation of genus it is important to count isomorphism classes of HNN-extensions with fixed associated subgroup

up to isomorphism.

To do this we restrict the action given by (

) of
{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
on
[\overline{S}]^{2},
to the set
[\overline{S_{H}}]^{2}
of all unordered pairs of conjugacy classes of finite subgroups of
G_{1}
isomorphic to
H
. Denote by
{\rm Aut}(G_{1})\backslash[\overline{S_{H}}]^{2}
the space of orbits of this action. Thus we have the following

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
H
and
K
be finite subgroups of an
{\rm OE}
-group
G_{1}
. Then the number
\eta
of isomorphism classes of abstract HNN-extensions

{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H_{1},\\
K_{1},f_{1},t_{1})

such that

For normal HNN-extensions it is possible to write the set of orbits of the Theorem

From Proposition

\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)

by composition on the left: we will denote by

with respect to this action and the space of right cosets

(11)

where

This induces a natural action on the right of the group

and the action of

From Formulas (

4
Profinite HNN-extensions

In this section we will solve the isomorphism problem for profinite HNN-extensions.

Let
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
be a profinite HNN-extension. We define the set

N^{+}:=\{gt^{-1}\in N_{G}(H)\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\overline{\left\langle g,G_{1}\right\rangle}=G\}.

(14)

Consider
g_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(H)
and
g=g_{1}t
. Then we have
g_{1}=gt^{-1}\in N^{+}.
Therefore

N_{G_{1}}(H)\subset N^{+}\subset N_{G}(H).

(15)

Further denote by
\overline{N}^{+}
and
\widetilde{N}^{+}
their natural images in
{\rm Aut}(H)
and
{\rm Out}(H),

respectively. Note that it is
N^{+}
that marks the difference between the abstract and the profinite case, as we will see later, it will be crucial for the calculation of the genus.

The following proposition will be fundamental to find a bound for the number of isomorphism classes of profinite HNN-extensions.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f_{1},t_{1})\ {\rm and}\ B={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f_{2},t_{2})

be profinite HNN-extensions with associated finite subgroups in a profinite
{\rm OE}
-group
G_{1}
. Suppose that
K=H
or
H
and
K
are not conjugate in
G_{1}
. Then
G\cong B
if and only if

(i)

there exist
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
with
\varphi(H)=K,\varphi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
f_{2}^{\varphi^{-1}}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}\cdot(f_{1}\tau_{n^{-1}})^{-1},
with
n\in N^{+}
;

(ii)

there exist
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
with
\varphi(H)=H,\varphi(K)=K^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
such that
f_{2}^{\varphi^{-1}}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}\cdot f_{1}\cdot\tau_{n^{-1}},
with
n\in N^{+}
.

Proof.

(\Longrightarrow)
By Proposition
2.15
there exists a continuous isomorphism
\psi:B\longrightarrow G
such that
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=K
and
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
or
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=H
and
\psi(K)=K^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
.

First suppose that
H=K
and observe that then
g_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(H)
. Put
g:=\psi(t_{2})\in G
. Since
t_{2}\in N_{B}(H)
we conclude that
gg_{1}^{-1}\in N_{G}(H)
and
\overline{\left\langle G_{1},(gg_{1}^{-1})^{\epsilon}\right\rangle}=G
, where
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}.
Put
n:=(gg_{1}^{-1})^{\epsilon}t_{1}^{-1}\in N^{+}
and observe that for all
h\in H
one has

\psi(f_{2}(h))=\psi(h)^{\psi(t_{2})}=\psi(h)^{(nt_{1})^{\epsilon}g_{1}}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}(f_{1}\tau_{n^{-1}})^{\epsilon}\psi(h),\mbox{ where }\,\epsilon=\pm 1.

Take
\varphi:=\psi|_{{}_{G_{1}}}\in{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)
. Then
\varphi f_{2}\varphi^{-1}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}(f_{1}^{-1}\tau_{n^{-1}})^{\epsilon}
as required.

If
H
and
K
not conjugate in
G_{1}
let’s divide it into two cases.

Case 1:
Suppose
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=K
and
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
.
Put
\psi(t_{2})=g\in G
. Note that
H^{g_{1}}=\psi(K)=\psi(H^{t_{2}})=\psi(H)^{\psi(t_{2})}=K^{g}=H^{t_{1}g}
, from where it follows that
n:=g_{1}g^{-1}t_{1}^{-1}\in N_{G}(H)
, furthermore
n\in N^{+}
. Then for all
h\in H,
we have

\psi(f_{2}(h))=\psi(h^{t_{2}})=\psi(h)^{\psi(t_{2})}=\psi(h)^{t_{1}^{-1}n^{-1}g_{1}}=(\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}\tau_{n}f_{1}^{-1}\psi)(h).

Therefore taking
\varphi=\psi|_{{}_{G_{1}}}\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
we have
f_{2}^{\psi^{-1}}=f_{2}^{\varphi^{-1}}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}\cdot\tau_{n}\cdot f_{1}^{-1}=\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}\cdot(f_{1}\tau_{n^{-1}})^{-1},
with
g_{1}\in G_{1}
and
n\in N^{+}
.

Case 2:
Suppose
\psi(G_{1})=G_{1}
,
\psi(H)=H
and
\psi(K)=K^{g_{1}}
. Take
\psi(t_{2})=g\in G
and
n:=gg_{1}^{-1}t_{1}^{-1}\in N^{+}
.
Then for all
h\in H
, we have

\psi(f_{2}(h))=\psi(h^{t_{2}})=\psi(h)^{\psi(t_{2})}=\psi(h)^{nt_{1}g_{1}}=(\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f_{1}\tau_{n^{-1}}\psi)(h).

So taking
\varphi=\psi|_{{}_{G_{1}}}
the result follows.

(\Longleftarrow)
Define a map
\psi
from
B
to
G
as follows

\psi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}g_{1}&\longmapsto&\varphi(g_{1}),\forall g_{1}\in G_{1},\\
t_{2}&\longmapsto&(nt_{1})^{\epsilon}g_{1},\epsilon=\pm 1.\\
\end{array}\right.

If (i) holds, consider
\epsilon=-1
and if (ii) holds, consider
\epsilon=1
; in both cases
\psi
is an isomorphism.

∎

4.2

Let

Our goal is to find a bound for the number of isomorphism classes of profinite HNN-extensions of

with associated subgroups isomorphic to

2.10
if

Our considerations in this subsection will be give analogous to what was done in the abstract case (see Subsection
3.1
). Let
P_{H}\subset P
the set of all finite subgroups of

isomorphic to

Let

be the collection of all unordered pairs of the form

Define similarly to what was done in the abstract case (see Subsection

on

(20)

Consider finite subgroups
H_{1}
and

of

isomorphic to
H
and
K,
respectively. Note that

By Proposition

When

and

Denote the set of orbits of action (
20

{\rm Aut}(G_{1})\backslash[\overline{P}_{H}]^{2}.

Let

and

4.3
Profinite vs Abstract

In this subsection we consider an abstract HNN-extension
{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group and its profinite completion
{\rm HNN}(\widehat{G}_{1},H,K,f,t)
.

Remark 4.14
.

Since
{\rm Inn}(H)\leq\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
( respectively
{\rm Inn}(H)\leq\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
), the statement that
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
( respectively
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
) immediately implies
\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
( respectively
\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)=\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
).

Remark 4.15
.

Recall that
if the associated subgroups
H
and
K
are conjugate in
G_{1}
, say
H^{g_{1}}=K\,(g_{1}\in G_{1}),
then
{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
is isomorphic to a normal HNN-extension
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f_{1})\,\mbox{ with }f_{1}\in{\rm Aut}(H)
(see Lemma
2.16
), i.e., one can assume
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f),
where
f\in{\rm Aut}(H)
is normal. If
\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
(this is the case if for example
N_{G_{1}}(H)
is dense in
N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
), then by Proposition
2.4

\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\langle\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H),f\rangle=\langle\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H),f\rangle=\overline{N}_{G}(H),

\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\langle\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H),\tilde{f}\rangle=\langle\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H),\tilde{f}\rangle=\widetilde{N}_{G}(H),

where
\tilde{f}
is the image of
f
in
{\rm Out}(H)
.

The situations described in the remark above occur in particular when
G_{1}
is finite.

Thus having abstract and profinite HNN-extensions
{\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
and
{\rm HNN}(\widehat{G}_{1},H,K,f,t)
of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
we have two groups
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
and
\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}
acting on
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
(the first in fact is a subgroup of the second) and the cardinality of genus is the difference between the the orbits of these action: more precisely, it is the number of
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
-orbits lying in
\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}
-orbit of
f
. Of course the
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
-orbits will coincide with
\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}
-orbits if the groups
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}
and
\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}
are equal.

Recall that a pair
(H,K)
of finite subgroups of
G_{1}
(resp.
\widehat{G}_{1}
) is a swapping pair if there exists an automorphism
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
(resp.
\in{\rm Aut}(\widehat{G}_{1})
) that swaps the conjugacy classes of
H
and
K
in
G_{1}
(resp. in
\widehat{G}_{1})
. Of course, to have
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}=\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}
it is necessary for
(H,K)
to be either a swapping pair or not a swapping pair in both
G_{1}
and
\widehat{G}_{1}
. Having this, it suffices to have the density of
\Gamma_{HK}
in
\Gamma_{\widehat{HK}}
, since
{\rm Iso}(H,K)
is finite. However, this would imply the density of
{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)
in
{\rm Aut}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
and for infinite
G_{1}
it happens not in too many cases despite the fact that it is quite difficult to check. The next lemma gives more transparent sufficient conditions that can be quite often checked when
{\rm Out}(H)
is small; it will be used in Subsection
5.1
.

Lemma 4.16
.

With the assumptions in this subsection, suppose the following conditions are satisfied

i)

\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
and
[G_{1}:N_{G_{1}}(K)]=[\widehat{G}_{1}:N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)]<\infty
;

ii)

(H,K)
is either a swapping pair in
G_{1}
or not a swapping pair in
\widehat{G}_{1}
.

Then
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}=\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}
.

Proof.

First assume that there exists
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
such that
\psi(K)=H^{g_{1}}
and
\psi(H)=K
, for some
g_{1}\in G_{1}
and observe that
\psi
extends to an isomorphism
\hat{\psi}\in{\rm Aut}(\widehat{G}_{1})
. Let
f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K)
. It follows from the definitions of the respective
\iota
in (
8
) and (
19
) that

\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}\cdot f\ni\iota\cdot f=\tau_{\hat{\psi}^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}\left(f^{\hat{\psi}}\right)^{-1}=\tau_{\psi^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}\left(f^{\psi}\right)^{-1}\in\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}\cdot f

Take
(\hat{g},\hat{\alpha})\in\Gamma_{\widehat{HK}}
such that
\tilde{w}
is its image in
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}.

According to item i) there exists
\tau_{g}\in{\rm Inn}(G_{1})
and
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
such that
\tau_{\hat{g}}(k)=\tau_{g}(k),\,\forall k\in K
and
\hat{\alpha}|_{{}_{H}}=\alpha|_{{}_{H}}
. Let
w
be the image of
(g,\alpha)
in
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}.
In this way, we have

\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}\cdot f\ni\tilde{w}\cdot f=\tau_{\hat{g}}f^{\hat{\alpha}^{-1}}=\tau_{g}f^{\alpha^{-1}}=w\cdot f\in\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}\cdot f,\,\forall f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K).

If the second possibility of item ii) occurs, the proof is similar but simpler since we do not have the action of
\iota
since
\overline{\Gamma}_{HK}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{HK}
and
\overline{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widehat{HK}}.

∎

5.1.1
Conjugate subgroups
H
and
K

If the associated subgroups
H
and
K
are conjugate in the base group
G_{1}
, one can assume w.l.o.g. that the HNN-extension
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
is normal (see Lemma
2.16
). Therefore, the set
R
from the preceeding subsection can be seen as a subset of
{\rm Aut}(H).
Furthermore,
if
gt^{-1}\in N^{+}
then
g\in N_{\widehat{G}}(H)
. Note that the image of
gt^{-1}
in
{\rm Aut}(H)
is given by
\tau_{{}_{(gt^{-1})^{-1}}}=f^{-1}\tau_{g^{-1}}.
Therefore the set
R
(see formula (
24
)) can be rewritten as

R=\{f\tau_{n^{-1}}\,\mid\,n\in N^{+}\}=\{\tau_{g}^{-1}\mid g\in N_{\widehat{G}}(H)\hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{and}\hskip 2.84544pt\overline{\langle g,\widehat{G}_{1}\rangle}=\widehat{G}\}.

(25)

Suppose
\mu\in{\rm Aut}(H)
and define
\tilde{\mu}
to be the image of
\mu
in
{\rm Out}(H).
Define the following subset of
{\rm Out}(H)

{\cal R}=\tilde{f}\widetilde{N}^{+}:=\{\tilde{f}\tilde{\tau}_{n}^{-1}\,|\,n\in N^{+}\},

(26)

where
{\cal R}
is the natural image of the subset
R
(see (
24
)) in
{\rm Out}(H).

It follows that
{\cal R}=\tilde{f}\widetilde{N}^{+}
is a subset of
\tilde{f}\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)
. As discussed in Subsubsection
4.2.1
,
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
acts on
{\rm Out}(H)
on the right by conjugation. The group
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
acts on
{\rm Out}(H)
on the left by multiplication. It follows that
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)\cdot\tilde{f}\widetilde{N}^{+}\cdot\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
is invariant with respect to the action of
\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
on the left and with respect to the action of
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
on the right. In the case of conjugated associated subgroups in the base group, we have the following formula for the genus.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
H
and
K
be conjugate finite subgroups in an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
be an HNN-extension. Then

|g(G,\mathcal{F})|=|\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\backslash\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)\cdot{\cal R}\cdot\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)/(\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)\times C_{2})|,

where
C_{2}
acts on
{\rm Out}(H)
by inversion.

Proof.

It can be assumed that
G
is normal and
f\in{\rm Aut}(H)
(see Lemma
2.16
). Let
B={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f_{1},t_{1})
with
f_{1}\in{\rm Aut}(H).
By Proposition
4.1
,
\widehat{B}\cong\widehat{G}
if and only if
f_{1}\in\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)\cdot R\cdot\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
if and only if
\widetilde{f_{1}}\in\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)\cdot{\cal R}\cdot\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
(since
{\rm Inn}(H)\leq\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\cap\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
) and the result follows from Corollary
3.12
.
∎

Now, let

\tilde{\kappa}:{\rm Out}(H)\longrightarrow\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\backslash{\rm Out}(H)/(\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)\times C_{2})

(27)

be the natural map (see Corollary
3.12
).
Then we have the following

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
H
and
K
be conjugate finite subgroups of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
be an HNN-extension. Suppose that
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)\\
=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
and
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H).
Then

|g(G,\mathcal{F})|=|\tilde{\kappa}({\cal R})|=|\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\backslash{\cal R}/(\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)\times C_{2})|.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
5.4
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
H
be a finite subgroup of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f,t)
be a normal HNN-extension. Suppose
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\{1\}
(for example if
H
is malnormal in
\widehat{G}_{1}
) and
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
(this is the case if
|G_{1}|<\infty
). Then

|g(G,\mathcal{F})|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\mbox{ if }|\tilde{f}|\leq 2,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(|\tilde{f}|)}{2},&\mbox{ if }|\tilde{f}|\geq 3,\end{array}\right.

where
\phi
is Euler’s function.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4

\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\left\langle\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H),\tilde{f}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tilde{f}\right\rangle.
Note that
\mathcal{R}=\left\{\tilde{\tau}_{g^{-1}}\,|\,\tilde{\tau}_{g^{-1}}\mbox{ is a generator of }\left\langle\tilde{f}\right\rangle\right\}.
Therefore, the bound can be obtained by considering only the action of
C_{2}
on
\mathcal{R}
as in Corollary
5.5
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

In the preceding result, if
{\rm Aut}(H)
is abelian (for example, when
H
is cyclic) then
|g(G,{\cal F})|=\frac{\phi(|\tilde{f}|)}{2}
if
|\tilde{f}|\geq 3.

Denote by
tor(G)
the subset of elements of finite order in a given group
G.

Example 5.8
.

Suppose that
G_{1}
is a finitely generated abelian group which is not virtually infinite cyclic and let
H
be one of its finite subgroups. By Proposition
2.6

G_{1}
is an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group. Let
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f,t)
be a normal HNN-extension. Since
G_{1}
is finitely generated abelian
\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\{1\}
and furthermore
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Out}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H).
It follows from Corollary
5.6
that the genus satisfies

|g(G,\mathcal{F})|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\mbox{ if }|\tilde{f}|\leq 2,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(|\tilde{f}|)}{2},&\mbox{ if }|\tilde{f}|\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

If
G_{1}
is virtually infinite cyclic and
H\neq tor(G_{1})
the same result is valid by Remarks
3.23
and
4.13
.
For
H=tor(G_{1})
we have that the HNN-extension is of the form
G=H\rtimes F,
where
F
is a free group and the genus in this case was calculated in Theorem 3.26 in
[
22
]
.

The second part of the result below, which deals with genus equal to 1, is similar to what was done in Theorem 3.1 in
[
7
]
when
G
is restricted to the class
\mathcal{F}
and the finite associated subgroups are conjugate in
G_{1},
that is, when the HNN-extension can be considered normal.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
H
and
K
be conjugate finite subgroups of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
be an HNN-extension with
f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K).
Suppose
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
and
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
(for example
|G_{1}|<\infty
). Then

|g(G,\mathcal{F})|\leq|\tilde{\kappa}(\widetilde{N}_{G}(H))|.

In particular, if
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
(this is the case if
\tilde{f}\in\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H),
where
\tilde{f}
is the image of
f
in
{\rm Out}(H)
) or
|{\rm Out}(H)|\leq 2,
then
|g(G,\mathcal{F})|=1
.

Proof.

Note that
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\langle\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H),\tilde{f}\rangle=\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)
in this case. So the first part follows directly from Corollary
5.5
and the fact that
N^{+}\subset N_{\widehat{G}}(H),
because the genus is less than or equal to

|\tilde{\kappa}(\tilde{f}\cdot\widetilde{N}_{G}(H))|=|\tilde{\kappa}(\widetilde{N}_{G}(H))|.

The second statement follows directly from the first.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Let
G_{1}
be a polycyclic-by-finite group and
H
a finite subgroup of
G_{1}
such that
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
. Consider a normal HNN-extension
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f,t)
. Then the cardinality of the genus of
G
in
\mathcal{F}
satisfies

|g(G,{\cal F})|\leq|\tilde{\kappa}(\widetilde{N}_{G}(H))|,

by Proposition
2.4
, Corollaries
2.5
and
5.9
since
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)
. In particular, the result holds if
G_{1}
is finite.

The result, which provides more effective conditions for calculating the genus of normal HNN-extensions, will be given in the following corollary. Note that it is a generalization of Corollary 5.5 in
[
7
]
.

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
H
be a finite subgroup of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f,t)
be an HNN-extension, where
f\in{\rm Aut}(H)
. Suppose that
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
,
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
and moreover, one of the following conditions is satisfied

i)

f\in\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H);

ii)

\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Inn}(H);

iii)

H\leq Z(G_{1}).

Then

a)

|g(G,{\cal F})|=1,
if
|\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)/\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)|\leq 2
;

b)

|g(G,{\cal F})|\leq\frac{\phi\left(|\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)/\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)|\right)}{2},
if
\left|\frac{\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)}{\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)}\right|\geq 3,
where
\phi
is the Euler function.

Proof.

By Remarks
4.14
and

4.15
we have

\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\langle\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H),f\rangle=\langle\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H),f\rangle=\overline{N}_{G}(H)

and
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)=\langle\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H),\tilde{f}\rangle.
Since
\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\triangleleft\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H),
then for any of the hypotheses i)-iii) of this corollary we have that
\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\triangleleft\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\overline{N}_{G}(H),
whence it follows that
\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\triangleleft\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G}(H).
Therefore, the quotients
\overline{N}_{G}(H)/\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\cong\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)/\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
are finite cyclic. Let
\tilde{\zeta}:\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)\longrightarrow\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)/\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
be the canonical epimorphism. By the definition of
R
and
\mathcal{R}
in (
25
) and (
26
), we have that
\tilde{\zeta}(\mathcal{R})
is the set of all generators of the cyclic group
\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)/\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H).
Since
\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\backslash{\cal R}\subset\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\backslash\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)
, we obtain the result by Corollary
5.5
by looking only at the orbits obtained by the action of
C_{2}
on the generator set
\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)\backslash{\cal R}.

∎

5.2
Genus 1

In this subsection we will give some sufficient conditions for the genus of HNN-extensions with an
\rm\widehat{OE}
base group and finite associated subgroups to have only one element. Of course, if
\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Out}(H)
, then
|g(G,\mathcal{F})|=1
by Corollary
5.3
and so we shall omit this condition from the list below to avoid repeatition. The following results give a generalization of Theorem 1.1 in
[
7
]
.

We start the case of normal HNN-extension (equivalently
H
and
K
are conjugate in the base group).

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
H
be a finite subgroup of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and

G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f,t)

be a normal HNN-extension, i.e.
f\in{\rm Aut}(H).

Suppose that
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
and
\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
. If
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
(this is the case if
f\in\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
), then
|g(G,{\cal F})|=1
.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
5.4
.
∎

Now we state theorem for not
normal HNN-extensions. Note that the particular case in item iv) of Theorem
1.9
automatically enters in the next theorem, since if
H
or
K
is central in
G_{1},
then
H\neq K
implies non-conjugacy of
H
and
K
.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
H
and
K
be finite non-conjugate subgroups of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
be an HNN-extension.
Suppose that
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)
,
[G_{1}:N_{G_{1}}(K)]=[\widehat{G}_{1}:N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)]<\infty
and
either
(H,K)
is a swapping pair in
G_{1}
or not a swapping pair in
\widehat{G}_{1}
.
Then there is only one element in the genus of
G
in
{\cal F},
if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

i)

f
extends to an automorphism of
G_{1};

ii)

N_{G_{1}}(K)=K\cdot C_{G_{1}}(K)
or
N_{G_{1}}(H)=H\cdot C_{G_{1}}(H)
(in particular,
H
or
K
is central in
G_{1}
).

Proof.

First note that the hypothesis
[G_{1}:N_{G_{1}}(K)]=[\widehat{G}_{1}:N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)]<\infty
implies that
N_{G_{1}}(K)
is dense in
N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K),
and as

N_{G_{1}}(H)=N_{G_{1}}(K^{t^{-1}})=\left(N_{G_{1}}(K)\right)^{t^{-1}}

it follows that
N_{G_{1}}(H)
is dense in
N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
. Therefore
\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)=\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)
and
\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)=\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H).
Since
f(h)=h^{t}=k
if and only if
f(h)^{t^{-1}}=h=k^{t^{-1}},\forall h\in H,k\in K,
one has
{\rm Inn}(H)=f^{-1}\cdot{\rm Inn}(K)\cdot f.
By Proposition
2.4

\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\langle\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H),\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)^{f}\rangle=\langle\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H),\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)^{f}\rangle=\overline{N}_{G}(H)

(28)

and so
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G}(H)=\langle\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H),\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(K)^{\tilde{f}}\rangle.
We need to find the image of
\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
and
\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)^{f}
in
{\rm Aut}(H).
We will divide the rest of the proof into the following cases.

Case 1:
Suppose that
f
extends to an automorphism of
G_{1}.
In this case, if
\hat{g}_{1}\in N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)
, then
\tau_{\left(\hat{g}_{1}^{t^{-1}}\right)^{-1}}=f^{-1}\tau_{g_{1}^{-1}}f=\tau_{f^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}
for some
g_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(K)
, since
\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)=\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)
and since
f
extends to
G_{1}
. It follows that
\tau_{f^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1})}\in\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H).
Therefore
\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)^{f}\subset\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H),
whence it follows by (
28
) that
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H),
thus the result follows from the Corollary
5.3
.

Case 2:
Suppose
N_{G_{1}}(K)=K\cdot C_{G_{1}}(K).
In this case, we have that

\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)={\rm Inn}(K)=f\cdot{\rm Inn}(H)\cdot f^{-1}=\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K),

thus
\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)^{f}\subset{\rm Inn}(H)\subset\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H),
and the result follows from (
28
) and the Corollary
5.3
since
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H).

Case 3:
Suppose
N_{G_{1}}(H)=H\cdot C_{G_{1}}(H).
In this case

\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Inn}(H)=f^{-1}\cdot{\rm Inn}(K)\cdot f\subset f^{-1}\cdot\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)\cdot f.

It follows from (
28
) that
\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)^{f}
and therefore
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(K)^{\tilde{f}}.
Note that
R=f\overline{N}^{+}\subset\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)f
since
\overline{N}^{+}\subset\overline{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)
(see equations (
22
) and (
24
)). The result follows from Theorem
5.2
since
|g(G,{\cal F})|=|\kappa(R)|\leq|\kappa(\overline{N}_{G_{1}}(K)f)|=1
because any HNN-extension of the form
B={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,\\
K,\tau_{g_{1}}f,t^{\prime})
with
g_{1}\in N_{G_{1}}(K)
are isomorphic by Theorem
3.6
.
∎

The result below is an immediate consequence of item ii) of the previous result because if
G_{1}
is finitely generated abelian then
\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)=\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G_{1}}}(H)
and the other conditions of the previous theorem are also satisfied. Note that if
G_{1}
is virtually infinite cyclic and
H\neq tor(G_{1})
the result still follows from Theorem
5.13
ii) and Remark
3.23
. For
H=tor(G_{1})
we have that the HNN-extension is of the form
G=H\rtimes F,
where
F
is a free group and the genus in this case was calculated in Theorem 3.26 in
[
22
]
. This result generalizes Corollary 3.3 in
[
7
]
.

Corollary 5.14
.

Suppose that
G_{1}
is a finitely generated abelian group and
H\neq K
its finite subgroups. Then

|g({\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t),\mathcal{F})|=1
.

Recall that an indecomposable (into a free product) finitely generated Fuchsian group
\Gamma
has the presentation of
the form:

\Gamma=\langle\,a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n},\,c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}\,\mid

c_{1}^{e_{1}}=\ldots=c_{t}^{e_{t}}=1,c_{1}^{-1}\cdot\ldots\cdot c_{t}^{-1}\cdot[a_{1},b_{1}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[a_{n},b_{n}]=1\,\rangle,

where
n,\,t\geq 0
and
e_{j}>1
for
j=1,\ldots,t
(see
[
34
]
).

Example 5.15
.

Let
\Gamma
be indecomposable into a free product finitely generated Fuchsian group and
H\neq\{1\}
is one of its finite subgroup. Let
G_{1}=K\times\Gamma
such that
H\cong K
. We first observe that indecomposable into a free product Fuchsian groups are virtually compact surface groups and so they are
\rm\widehat{OE}
-groups by Proposition
2.7
(i). Therefore by Proposition
2.8

G_{1}
is an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group. Consider
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
where
f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K).
Note that
H\neq K
and therefore
G
is not normal HNN-extension. By Corollary 7.4 of
[
5
]

\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\Gamma}(H)={\rm Aut}(H)=\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(H)
therefore we have

\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\Gamma}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Out}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{\Gamma}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H).

Now note that
H
is not a normal subgroup of
\widehat{\Gamma}
by Corollary 5.2 in
[
8
]
, thus there is no
\hat{\psi}\in{\rm Aut}(\widehat{G}_{1})
such that
\hat{\psi}(H)=K
because
K
is normal in
\widehat{G}_{1}.
Furthermore, since
K\leq Z(\widehat{G}_{1})
, it follows that
N_{G_{1}}(K)=G_{1}
and
N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(K)=\widehat{G}_{1}.
Thus all the hypotheses of Theorem
5.13
are satisfied, and it follows from item ii) that
|g(G,\mathcal{F})|=1.

Example 5.16
.

Let
M
be an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group and
H
be a finite group such that
{\rm Out}(H)
is trivial (for example this occurs for symmetric groups
S_{n}
with
n\neq 6
and Mathieu simple groups
M_{11},M_{23}
and
M_{24}
). Let
G_{1}=M\times H.
From Proposition
2.8

G_{1}
is an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group. Consider
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,f,t)
where
f\in{\rm Aut}(H)
. Note that
\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Aut}(H).
Indeed, take
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(H)
and observe that
\psi:=(id,\alpha)\in{\rm Aut}(G_{1})
. Thus
\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Aut}(H)=\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)={\rm Inn}(H),
and therefore we have
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{\rm Aut}_{G_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)={\rm Out}(H)=\{1\}.
Thus all hypotheses of Theorem
5.12
are satisfied, so
|g(G,\mathcal{F})|=1.

Remark 5.17
.

In the previous example, if
G_{1}=M\times H,
where
H=S_{6},
then
\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Aut}(H)=\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)
consequently
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Out}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)\cong C_{2}
and
\widetilde{N}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{N}_{G_{1}}(H)
(this occurs since
N_{G_{1}}(H)=G_{1}
is dense in
N_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widehat{G}_{1}
), therefore it follows from Corollary
5.9
that
|g(G,\mathcal{F})|=1.
Similarly, the same result is concluded for the simple Mathieu groups
M_{12}
and
M_{22}.

We finish this subsection with an example of HNN-extension where the base group is virtually infinite
cyclic which is relevant by Remark
3.23
.

Example 5.18
.

Let
H
and
K
be isomorphic finite cyclic groups and
G_{1}=(H\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z})\times K
where
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow{\rm Aut}(H)
is a homomorphism satisfying
\varphi(1)\neq id.
Consider
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
where
f\in{\rm Iso}(H,K)
. Observe that
\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)=\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{G_{1}}(H)={\rm Out}(H)={\rm Aut}(H)=\overline{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H)=\widetilde{{\rm Aut}}_{\widehat{G}_{1}}(H).
Indeed, let
\beta

Remark 6.1

Note that

By Proposition 3.3 (i) in

Similarly let
G_{2}=Q_{8}=\left\langle i,j\,|\,i^{4}=j^{4}=1,i^{j}=j^{3}=-j\right\rangle
be the quaternion group of order 8,
H=\left\langle i\right\rangle
and
K=\left\langle j\right\rangle
two of its non-conjugate subgroups of order 4. Let
W={\rm HNN}(G_{2},H,K,f,t)
such that
f(i)=\pm j.
Note that
f
extends to an automorphism
\psi:G_{2}\longrightarrow G_{2}
such that
\psi(i)=j
and
\psi(j)=i
or
\psi(i)=-j
and
\psi(j)=-i.
Then for the same reason as in the dihedral case we have
|g(W,G_{2},{\cal A})|=1,
and similarly for any permutation between three distinct subgroups of order 4 of the quaternion group.

Example 6.17
.

In the previous example, let
H
be any of the proper subgroups of
G_{1}\cong D_{8}
that are not Klein subgroups. Then
H
is cyclic of order
\leq 4,
from where it follows that
|{\rm Aut}(H)|=|{\rm Out}(H)|\leq 2,
so

|g({\rm HNN}(D_{8},H,H,f,t),D_{8},{\cal A})|=1

by Corollary
5.9
and Remark
6.5
. Therefore, for any HNN-extension

G={\rm HNN}(D_{8},H,H,f,t)

the cardinality of genus
g(G,G_{1},{\cal A})
is always equal to 1 regardless of the chosen associated subgroup. If
G_{2}=Q_{8}
is the Quaternion group of order
8,
the same follows because all its proper subgroups are cyclic of order
\leq 4,
i.e.,

|g({\rm HNN}(Q_{8},H,H,f,t),Q_{8},{\cal A})|=1

independently of the chosen associated subgroups.

Remark 6.18
.

Note that

G={\rm HNN}(D_{8},H,K,f,t),\ {\rm and}\ G={\rm HNN}(Q_{8},H,K,f,t)

are profinitely rigid if the Remeslennikov’s question has a positive answer. Indeed, a finitely generated residually finite group
B
having the same profinite completion as
G
would be virtually free and by
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]

B
will be an HNN-extension with finite base group.

6.1
Genus in a more general class

In this subsection we give formulas for the genus of HNN-extensions in class
\mathcal{A}
with
G_{1}
not fixed. When
G
is a group in
\mathcal{A}
, we will denote the genus of
G
in class
\mathcal{A}
, as
g(G,\mathcal{A}).

Let
H
and
K
be finite subgroups of an
\rm\widehat{OE}
-group
G_{1}
and

G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)

an HNN-extension. The objective is to find a formula to calculate the cardinality of the set
g(G,\mathcal{A}).

To do this, consider
B\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\widehat{B}\cong\widehat{G}
. Then by
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]

B
is an HNN-extension of the form
B={\rm HNN}(B_{1},H_{1},K_{1},f_{1},t_{1})
with
\widehat{B}_{1}\cong\widehat{G}_{1},H\cong H_{1}\cong K\cong K_{1},
and furthermore
B_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 6.19
.

Let
G_{1}
be a group and
\widehat{G}_{1}
be its profinite completion. Let
X_{1},X_{2},Y_{1},Y_{2}
be finite subgroups of
\widehat{G}_{1}
. We say that the pairs
(X_{1},Y_{1})
and
(X_{2},Y_{2})
are
permutable
, if there exists
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(\widehat{G}_{1})
that permutes the conjugacy classes of
X_{i}
and
Y_{i},
for each
i=1,2
.

Let
B_{1}\in g(G_{1},{\cal A})
. We will identify
\widehat{G}_{1}
with
\widehat{B}_{1}
and consider
G_{1}
and
B_{1}
as dense subgroups of
\widehat{G}_{1}=\widehat{B}_{1}.

Let
B={\rm HNN}(B_{1},H_{1},K_{1},f_{1},t_{1})
be an HNN-extension with finite associated subgroups. We say that
B
is
compatible
with
G
if the pairs
(H,H_{1})
and
(K,K_{1})
are permutable in
\widehat{G}_{1}
(i.e. exists
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(\widehat{G}_{1})
such that
\psi(H)=H_{1}
and
\psi(K)=K_{1}^{g_{1}}
for some
g_{1}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
) and
f_{1}=\tau_{g_{1}}f^{\psi^{-1}}
.
By Proposition
2.15
,
B
is compatible with
G
if and only if
\widehat{B}\cong\widehat{G}.

With these considerations, we state the following result

Theorem 6.20
.

The cardinality of the genus
g(G,\mathcal{A})
of an HNN-extension
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
is equal to

|g(G,{\cal A})|=\sum_{B_{1}\in g(G_{1},{\cal A})}|g\left({\rm HNN}(B_{1},H_{1},K_{1},f_{1},t_{1}),B_{1},{\cal A}\right)|,

where
B_{1}
ranges over the representatives of isomorphism classes in
g(G_{1},{\cal A})
such that
G
is compatible with
{\rm HNN}(B_{1},H_{1},K_{1},f_{1},t_{1}).

Corollary 6.21
.

If
|g(G_{1},{\cal A})|=1
then
|g(G,\mathcal{A})|=|g(G,G_{1},{\cal A})|.

Example 6.22
.

Let
\Gamma
be an indecomposable into a free product finitely generated Fuchsian group and
H\neq\{1\}
is one of its finite subgroup. Let
G_{1}=K\times\Gamma
such that
H\cong K
. Consider
G={\rm HNN}(G_{1},H,K,f,t)
. If
\Gamma
is profinitely rigid (see
[
10
]
for examples of such) then so is
G_{1}
and by Example
6.6
and Corollary
6.21

|g(G,\mathcal{A})|=1.

Time-evolution of parity-odd cascades in homogeneous Abelian and non-Abelian media with chiral imbalance
Jeremy Hansen, Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
We study radiation by a fast particle in a medium with a finite chiral chemical potential. The medium’s anomalous response encompasses the chiral magnetic effect, enabling novel chiral Cherenkov and pair production processes. The kinematics of the corresponding cascades is fundamentally different from conventional cascades. Notably, it is not dominated by the strong ordering of momenta. Employing the effective Chern-Simons extensions of QED and QCD to incorporate the chiral magnetic effect, we derive and solve the evolution equations describing the cascades in a chiral medium, presuming that the anomalous contributions are dominant.

The mathematical description of the conventional cascades in QED and QCD significantly simplifies at high energies because the individual decays factor out thanks to the strongly ordered transverse momenta. This makes the decays quasi-classical, and thereby statistically independent at the leading logarithmic approximation. Unlike the conventional decays, the chiral Cherennkov decays are not strongly ordered in the transverse momentum. Rather the transverse momenta of the radiated particles are fixed by the kinematic constraints. Nevertheless, precisely this fixing of the transverse momenta makes the factorization and the associated quasi-classical interpretation possible for the chiral Cherenkov cascade as we argue in Sec.
III
.

Once the factorization property is established, the dynamics of the chiral Cherenkov cascade can be described by the kinetic equation. Consider the chiral Cherenkov cascade in
\chi

\bar{e}(x,t)

However, in this paper, we will ignore the conventional processes in order to obtain the most clear physical picture of the chiral Cherenkov cascade. Throughout the paper we assume for definitiveness that

\gamma^{-}\to e+\bar{e}

\displaystyle e(y,t+\Delta t)

\displaystyle=\left(1-W_{\text{tot}}^{e\to e\gamma^{+}}\Delta t\right)e(y,t)+\Delta t\int_{0}^{1}dz\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{dW^{e\to e\gamma^{+}}}{dx}e(z,t)\delta(y-xz)

\displaystyle+\Delta t\int_{0}^{1}dz\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{dW^{\gamma^{-}\to e\bar{e}}}{dx}\gamma^{-}(z,t)\delta(y-xz)

(2a)

\displaystyle\bar{e}(y,t+\Delta t)

\displaystyle=\left(1-W_{\text{tot}}^{\bar{e}\to\bar{e}\gamma^{+}}\Delta t\right)\bar{e}(y,t)+\Delta t\int_{0}^{1}dz\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{dW^{\bar{e}\to\bar{e}\gamma^{+}}}{dx}\bar{e}(z,t)\delta(y-xz)

\displaystyle+\Delta t\int_{0}^{1}dz\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{dW^{\gamma^{-}\to e\bar{e}}}{dx}\gamma^{-}(z,t)\delta(y-xz)\,,

(2b)

\displaystyle\gamma^{+}(y,t+\Delta t)

\displaystyle=\Delta t\int_{0}^{1}dz\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{dW^{e\to\gamma^{+}e}}{dx}\left[e(z,t)+\bar{e}(z,t)\right]\delta(y-xz)\,,

(2c)

\displaystyle=\left(1-W_{\text{tot}}^{\gamma^{-}\to e\bar{e}}\Delta t\right)\gamma^{+}(y,t)\,,

(2d)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha b_{0}}{4}\left(\frac{(1-x)^{2}+1}{x}-\frac{2m^{2}}{b_{0}E}\right)\step(x\leq x_{0})\,,

(3)

(4)

III
Double particle emission in
\chi
QED

We are now going to argue that the multiparticle production rate can be represented as a convolution of the single particle production rates. We first consider the double photon emission as a specific example.

The general expression for the single photon emission rate reads:

\displaystyle W^{e\to e\gamma}=\frac{1}{2E}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\text{spins}}\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{2E^{\prime}(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{2\omega(2\pi)^{3}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta(p-k-p^{\prime})\left|e\bar{u}^{\prime}\not{\epsilon}^{*}u\right|^{2}\,.

(10)

We use the shorthand notation
u\equiv u({\bm{p}},s)
,
u^{\prime}\equiv u({\bm{p}}^{\prime},s^{\prime})
,
\epsilon\equiv\epsilon({\bm{k}},\lambda)
, with the bold letters referring to the spacial components of the corresponding four-vectors,
s
and
s^{\prime}
label fermion spin states, and
\lambda
photon polarizations.

The diagrams for double photon production.

The rate for the two photon emissions by an electron reads

\displaystyle W^{e\to e\gamma\gamma}=\frac{1}{8E}\sum_{\text{spins}}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\int\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{2E^{\prime}(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}k_{1}}{2\omega_{1}(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}k_{2}}{2\omega_{2}(2\pi)^{3}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta(p-k_{1}-k_{2}-p^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times\left|\frac{e^{2}\bar{u}^{\prime}\not{\epsilon}^{*}_{2}(\not{p}-\not{k}_{1}+m)\not{\epsilon}^{*}_{1}u}{(p-k_{1})^{2}-m^{2}+2iE_{q}/\tau}+\frac{e^{2}\bar{u}^{\prime}\not{\epsilon}^{*}_{1}(\not{p}-\not{k}_{2}+m)\not{\epsilon}^{*}_{2}u}{(p-k_{2})^{2}-m^{2}+2iE_{q}/\tau}\right|^{2}\,,

(11)

where
E_{q}=E-\omega_{1}
,
\tau
is the relaxation time controlling the resonance width

;
Hansen and Tuchin 2023
and the symmetry factor is
1/2
. In the ultrarelativistic approximation, the denominator of the propagator in the first term can be written as
Hansen and Tuchin 2023

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-2p\cdot k_{1}+k_{1}^{2}+2iE_{q}/\tau}=\frac{1}{\omega_{1}E\left(\frac{m^{2}}{E^{2}\omega_{1}}\frac{\omega_{1}-x_{0}E}{1-x_{0}}+\vartheta^{2}\right)+2iE_{q}/\tau}\,.

Apparently, when
\omega_{1}<x_{0}E
the propagator has a resonance at a finite photon emission angle
\vartheta
. The second term in (
III

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{(p-k_{1})^{2}-m^{2}+2iE_{q}/\tau}\right|^{2}\approx\frac{\pi\tau}{2E_{q}}\delta\left((p-k_{1})^{2}-m^{2}\right)\,.

In other words, the intermediate fermion is effectively on the mass-shell. A more detailed justification of the approximation (

Eq. (

\delta(p-k_{1}-k_{2}-p^{\prime})=\int d^{4}q\delta(q-p+k_{2})\delta(q-p^{\prime}-k_{2})

and
\not{p}-\not{k}_{1}+m=\sum_{s}u({\bm{q}},s)\bar{u}({\bm{q}},s)
we obtain employing (

\displaystyle W^{e\to e\gamma\gamma}=\tau\int_{0}^{E}d\omega_{1}\frac{dW^{e(p)\to e(q)\gamma(k_{1})}}{d\omega_{1}}\int_{0}^{E_{q}}d\omega_{2}\frac{dW^{e(q)\to e(p^{\prime})\gamma(k_{2})}}{d\omega_{2}}\,.

(14)

The other branching process — the pair production — factors out in the same way.

This derivation can be readily generalized to an arbitrary multiparticle process. The systematic way to describe such a cascade is to employ the evolution equations which we develop in the following sections.

VI
Summary and Discussion

We studied the time-evolution of cascades using the Chern-Simons extensions of QED and QCD, which allows systematic incorporation of the chiral magnetic current
{\bm{j}}=b_{0}{\bm{B}}
in the perturbation theory. Our main result is the time-evolution equations (
18
), (
39
), (
51
) for the distribution functions. These equations omit the contribution of the conventional processes which has been extensively studied in the literature

Arnold et al. 2003
;
Baier et al. 2001
;
Blaizot and Mehtar-Tani 2015
;
Mehtar-Tani et al. 2023
;
Mueller 2000
;
Schlichting and Soudi 2021
;
Sokolov et al. 2010
;
Bulanov et al. 2013
;
Barata et al. 2021
;
Caron-Huot and Gale 2010
;
Sievert et al. 2019
. In
Hansen and Tuchin 2022
;
Hansen and Tuchin 2023
we analyzed in detail the contribution of the chiral anomaly to the Bethe-Heitler process and determined the conditions when such a contribution becomes essential.

The interplay of the conventional bremsstrahlung and pair production with their chiral Cherenkov counterparts is complex because they arise form different kinematic regions. For example, in contrast to the conventional QED/QCD bremsstrahlung which is dominated by soft photon/gluon emission, the chiral Cherenkov emission is determined by the resonance in the intermediate fermion states
Hansen and Tuchin 2023
. Unlike the former process that generates large logarithms of transverse momentum, the latter one generates large powers of the resonance relaxation time. The fact that the conventional and the chiral Cherenkov processes come form different parts of the phase space makes it challenging to combine them in a single framework. One possible approach could involve the kinetic equation with the conventional and chiral Cherenkov processes contributing to different collision integrals.

The evolution equations (
18
), (
51
) closely resemble the Gribov-Lipatov equations of QED
Lipatov 1974
and the DGLAP equations of QCD
Gribov and Lipatov 1972
;
Dokshitzer 1977
;
Altarelli and Parisi 1977
in the leading logarithmic approximation. This is because the splitting functions are exactly the same in the high energy approximation and in the chiral limit. Nevertheless, they are fundamental different as the evolution equations of the chiral Cherenkov cascade (
18
), (
51
) involve the two photon and gluon polarizations in highly asymmetric way. As a result the cascade quickly becomes strongly polarized in violation of parity.

One can use the results of this paper to compute the anomalous contribution to the energy loss in a chiral medium.

This procedure is not restricted to the double photon production. For any given process the anomaly introduces a new pole for every intermediate particle involved. As such, each of these particles can be approximated to be on mass-shell. The justification in each case is very similar. The contributions to their corresponding radiation rates are dominated by such poles.

Appendix B
Circular polarization tensor

Define the photon polarization sum as:

\displaystyle d^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}=(\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{\mu})^{*}\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{\nu}\,,\quad\text{(no summation)}\,.

(68)

The polarization vectors describe the right and left-hand circular polarizations and obey the identity
i{\bm{k}}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}=\lambda|{\bm{k}}|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}
. In the radiation gauge the polarization vectors satisfy
\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{0}=0
,

{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}\cdot{\bm{k}}=0
,
{\bm{(}}\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda})^{*}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{k}},\lambda^{\prime}}=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
. It follows that
d^{00}_{\lambda}=d^{0i}_{\lambda}=0
,
d^{ij}_{\lambda}k^{i}=0
,
\Tr\sum_{\lambda}d^{ij}_{\lambda}=2
and

d^{ij}_{\lambda}\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{i}=\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{j}
(no summation over
\lambda

\displaystyle d^{ij}_{\lambda}=(\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{i})^{*}\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},\lambda}^{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{{\bm{k}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\varepsilon^{ij\ell}\frac{k^{\ell}}{|{\bm{k}}|}\,.

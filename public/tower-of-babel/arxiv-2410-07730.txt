On difference Riccati equation and continued fractions
Alexey V. Ivanov

Abstract
We study a difference Riccati equation Φ ⁡ ( x ) + ρ ⁡ ( x ) / Φ ⁡ ( x − ω ) = v ⁡ ( x ) \Phi(x)+\rho(x)/\Phi(x-\omega)=v(x) with 1 − 1- periodic continuos coefficients. Using continued fraction theory we investigate a problem of existence of continuos solutions for this equation. It is shown that convergence of a continued fraction representing a solution of the Riccati equation can be expressed in terms of hyperbolicity of a cocycle naturally associated to this continued fraction. We apply the critical set method to establish the uniform hyperbolicity of the cocycle and to obtain sufficient conditions for the convergence of a continued fraction giving a representation for a solution of the Riccati equation.



Keywords: difference Riccati equation, continued fraction, linear cocycle, hyperbolicity, Lyapunov exponent, critical set

MSC 2010: 37C55, 37D25, 37C40

We study a difference Riccati equation

\Phi(x)+\rho(x)\frac{1}{\Phi(x-\omega)}=v(x),

where
\rho

Although such equations appear in different areas of physics (e.g. control theory, theory of filters) the main interest to

\vec{\Psi}(x+\omega)=H(x)\vec{\Psi}(x),\quad\vec{\Psi}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

h_{21}(x)&h_{22}(x)\end{array}\right),

then
\Phi(x)=\frac{\Psi_{1}(x+\omega)}{\Psi_{1}(x)}
satisfies
(\ref{eq_1})
with

\rho(x)=\frac{h_{12}(x)}{h_{12}(x-\omega)},\quad v(x)=h_{11}(x)+\rho(x)h_{22}(x).

Note that inverse passage from a solution of the Riccati equation to a solution of equation

\psi(x+\omega)-\psi(x)=\phi(x),

We rewrite

\Phi(x)=v(x)-\frac{\rho(x)}{\Phi(x-\omega)}.

Then, representing
\Phi(x-\omega)

\Phi(x)=v(x)-\frac{\rho(x)}{v(x-\omega)-\frac{\rho(x-\omega)}{\Phi(x-2\omega)}}.

Repetition of this procedure

and
r_{n}(x)=\rho(x-(n-1)\omega)/\rho(x-(n-2)\omega)
.

Thus, a formal solution of

\Phi(x)=b_{0}(x)+\cfrac{-1}{b_{1}(x)+\cfrac{-1}{b_{2}(x)+\ldots}}=b_{0}(x)+\contfrac_{j=1}^{\infty}\cfrac{-1}{b_{j}(x)}.

Here we use Gauss notation,

A natural question arises: whether this continued fraction converges or not. More precisely, one may ask whether a sequence
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

\Phi_{n}(x)=b_{0}(x)+\cfrac{-1}{b_{1}(x)+\cfrac{-1}{b_{2}(x)+\cdots+\cfrac{-1}{b_{n}(x)}}}=b_{0}(x)+\contfrac_{j=1}^{n}\cfrac{-1}{b_{j}(x)}.

converges and in what class of functions.

There is broad literature on continued fractions theory. Started from works by Euler, Lagrange, Galois this theory attracted attention of many mathematicians and found applications in various branches of mathematics such as number theory, theory of analytic functions and others. We refer the reader to classical books on this theory

Although theory of continued fractions has long history a problem of establishing the convergence of a functional continued fraction is far from its complete solution. It appears that the most general results in this direction have been obtained in a period from the middle of 19th centure to the beginning of 20th century and are usually related to such mathematicians as Worpitsky, Pringsheim, van Vleck, Stieltjes and others. In this paper we study this problem by using a well-known connection between continued fraction theory and theory of linear cocycles. In particular, we use hyperbolic property of cocycles associated to a continued fraction to establish its convergence.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we discuss general notions of continued fraction theory and its relation to one-dimensional dynamics, in particular, to theory of hyperbolic

In section 4 we obtain sufficient conditions for uniform hyperbolicity of cocycles. In section 5 we apply obtained results and the critical set method to some special class of functional continued fractions and establish sufficient conditions for their convergence.

Continued fractions

In this section we collect some known facts from the theory of continued fractions and present it’s relationship with the theory of hyperbolic cocycles. Our main references on the theory of continued fractions are

,

.

Definition 1
.

A sequence
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
, where
b_{0},a_{j},b_{j},f_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\,\,\forall j\in\mathbb{N}
is called a continued fraction if

f_{n}=b_{0}+\cfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}+\cfrac{a_{2}}{b_{2}+\ldots+\cfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}}}=b_{0}+\contfrac_{j=1}^{n}\cfrac{a_{j}}{b_{j}},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

The number
f_{n}
is called the
n-
th convergent of the continued fraction.

One says that a continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
converges if there exists finite or infinite limit

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}f_{n}=f_{*}.

It may occur that for a two different continued fractions
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
,
\{(a^{\prime}_{j},b^{\prime}_{j}),f^{\prime}_{j}\}
the corresponding convergents coincide, i.e.

f_{j}=f^{\prime}_{j},\,\,\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

In this case one says that the continued fractions
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
,
\{(a^{\prime}_{j},b^{\prime}_{j}),f^{\prime}_{j}\}
are equivalent. In particular, if for a given continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
such that
a_{j}\neq 0,\,\,\forall j\in\mathbb{N}
one sets

a^{\prime}_{j}=-1,\quad b^{\prime}_{j}=r_{j}b_{j},

where
r_{0}=1
and

r_{2n}=\frac{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{n}a_{2s-1}}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{n}a_{2s}},\quad r_{2n+1}=-\frac{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{n}a_{2s}}{\prod\limits_{s=0}^{n}a_{2s+1}},

then we obtain an equivalent continued fraction
[
9
]
.

Another remarkable fact is the existence of inductive procedure, which enables constructing the
n-
th convergents
[
8
]
. Namely, for a continued fraction

\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
define

\displaystyle p_{n}=b_{n}p_{n-1}+a_{n}p_{n-2},

\displaystyle q_{n}=b_{n}q_{n-1}+a_{n}q_{n-2},

(2.1)

\displaystyle p_{0}=b_{0},\,\,p_{-1}=1,\,\,q_{0}=1,\,\,q_{-1}=0.

Then

f_{n}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}.

(2.2)

Although the development of continued fraction theory started in 16th centure, there is a lack of sufficient conditions guaranteeing convergence of a continued fraction of
general type. The most powerfull tools for establishing the convergence of continued fractions with real entries are due to the following two theorems
[
9
]
:

Theorem (L. Seidel, M. A. Stern, 1846-1848)
.

A continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
converges to a finite value if

a_{j}=1,\quad b_{j}>0,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}

and

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}=\infty.

Theorem (A. Pringsheim, 1899)
.

A continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
converges to a finite value if

|b_{j}|\geq|a_{j}|+1,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

As a consequence to Pringsheim’s theorems one obtains Worpitsky’s sufficient condition, which establishes convergence of a continued fraction under conditions

|a_{j}|=1,\quad|b_{j}|\geq 2,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.3)

One may note that formula (
2.2
) can be rewritten in terms of a cocycle. Indeed, for a given continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
we consider a sequence of matrices

A_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}b_{n}&a_{n}\\
1&0\end{array}\right).

(2.4)

This sequence generates a cocycle

\quad M_{n}=A_{n}A_{n-1}\cdots A_{1}=\vprod\limits_{k=1}^{n}A_{k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where the left arrow denotes the order of multiplication.

Then (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{n}=A_{n}\vec{p}_{n-1}=M_{n}\vec{p}_{0},\quad\vec{q}_{n}=A_{n}\vec{q}_{n-1}=M_{n}\vec{q}_{0},

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}p_{n}\\
p_{n-1}\end{array}\right),\quad\vec{q}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{n}\\
q_{n-1}\end{array}\right).

Hence, the
n
-th convergent reads

f_{n}=\frac{(M_{n}\vec{e}_{2},\vec{e}_{1})}{(M_{n}\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{1})},

where
\{\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{2}\}
is the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

It is important for further analysis that transformation
A_{n}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
provided
a_{n}=-1
.

Now we consider functional continued fractions
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
, whose elements
a_{j},b_{j}
are
1
-periodic continuous functions of a real variable
x
or equivalently we may suppose that
a_{j},b_{j}
are continuous functions on a circle
\mathbb{T}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2
.

We say that a functional continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
is generated by a pair
(b,\omega)
, where
b
is a
1
-periodic continuous function and
\omega\in(0,1)
is an irrational number if

a_{j}(x)\equiv-1,\quad b_{j}(x)=b\bigl(x-(j-1)\omega\bigr),\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus, in that case the associated cocycle
M_{n}

M_{n}(x)=\vprod\limits_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}(x),\quad A_{k}(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}b\bigl(x-(k-1)\omega\bigr)&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)

becomes an
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-cocycle.
The following theorem holds true.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that a given functional continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
is generated by a pair
(b,\omega)
with function
b\in C(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{R})
and
\omega\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
.
If there exist positive constants
C_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}>1
such that the cocycle associated with this continued fraction admits an estimate

\|M_{n}(x)\|\geq C_{0}\lambda_{0}^{n},

(2.5)

then the continued fraction converges and

f_{*}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\in C(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{\hat{R}})
, where

\mathbb{\hat{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.

PROOF:
It follows from results of J.-C. Yoccos
[
11
]
(see also
[
12
]
) that in the case of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-cocycles estimate (
2.5
) is equivalent to the uniform hyperbolicity of the cocycle. Then by Oseledets theorem
[
13
]
there exist continuous maps

E^{s,u}:\mathbb{T}^{1}\to Gr(1,2),

where
Gr(1,2)
stands for the set of one-dimensional subspaces of

\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

M_{0}(x)E^{s,u}(x)=E^{s,u}(x-\omega),\quad E^{s}(x)\oplus E^{u}(x)=\mathbb{R}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\left\|M_{n}(x)|_{E^{s}(x)}\right\|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\Lambda n},\quad n\geq 0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|M_{n}(x)|_{E^{u}(x)}\right\|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\Lambda n},\quad n\leq 0

Let
\vec{e}^{s,u}(x)\in E^{s,u}(x)
be unit vectors. Due to continuity and transversality of
E^{s,u}
we may assume that
\vec{e}^{s,u}
depend continuously on
x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
. Then

\vec{e}_{k}=c_{k}(x)\vec{e}^{u}(x)+d_{k}(x)\vec{e}^{s}(x),\quad k=1,2,

where
c_{k},d_{k}
are continuous functions. Then, using estimates (
2.6
), one obtains

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{(M_{n}(x)\vec{e}_{2},\vec{e}_{1})}{(M_{n}(x)\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{1})}=\frac{c_{2}(x)}{c_{1}(x)}

Applying transversality of subspaces
E^{s,u}
, we conclude that
c_{1},c_{2}
cannot vanish simultaneously. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark:
One may note that if
E^{s}(x)
is transversal to
E_{1}=\{t\vec{e}_{1},t\in\mathbb{R}\},\,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
then
c_{1}
does not vanish and
f_{*}\in C(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{R})
.

Thus, Theorem 1 says that uniform hyperbolicity of the cocycle
M_{n}
implies convergence of the functional continued fraction.

3
Decomposition of cocycles

In this section we describe a procedure, which allows passage from a cocycle associated to a continued fraction to a cocycle which possesses the same hyperbolic properties, but has a special representation convenient for analysis. We will suppose in what follows that a continued fraction
\{(a_{j},b_{j}),f_{j}\}
satisfies

a_{j}\neq 0\quad\forall\,j\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence, passing to an equivalent continued fraction (if necessary) a transformation (
2.4
) can be written in the form

A_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}b_{n}&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\in SL(2,\mathbb{R}).

To analyze behavior of
M_{n}
for large
n
we represent the transformation
A_{n}
by using of singular value decomposition (SVD). It can be done in different ways, however, we choose the following SVD for
A_{n}
:

A_{n}=\textrm{sign}(b_{n})R(\theta_{n})Z(\mu_{n})R(\theta_{n}),

where

R(\varphi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\varphi&\sin\varphi\\
-\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{array}\right),\,\,Z(\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right)

and

\displaystyle\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{b_{n}^{2}+4}+|b_{n}|\right),

\displaystyle\tan\theta_{n}=\frac{\textrm{sign}(b_{n})}{2}\left(\sqrt{b_{n}^{2}+4}-|b_{n}|\right).

The reason why we choose this representation is that

\mu_{n}\geq 1,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

This fact simplifies analysis of cocycle dynamics. Besides, one may note that

|\tan\theta_{n}|\leq 1,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

and
\mu_{n}
is a continuous function of
b_{n}
. However,
\theta_{n}
is discontinuous with respect to
b_{n}
at the point
b_{n}=0
. The latter property makes further analysis more difficult.

To overcome this obstacle we consider a sequence of matrices
A^{(2)}_{n}=A_{2n}A_{2n-1}
. Note that a cocycle,
M_{n}^{(2)}
, generated by
A^{(2)}_{n}
is connected with the cocycle
M_{n}
in the following way

M_{n}^{(2)}=M_{2n}.

It is known that hyperbolic properties of both cocycles are the same. A direct applcation of singular value decomposition to
A^{(2)}_{n}
leads to

A^{(2)}_{n}=R(\varphi_{n})Z(\lambda_{n})R(\chi_{n}),

with

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{4+h_{n}^{2}}+\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}}\right),\quad h_{n}^{2}=(b_{2n-1}-b_{2n})^{2}+b_{2n-1}^{2}b_{2n}^{2},

\displaystyle\cot\varphi_{n}=\epsilon_{2n-1}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}-b_{2n-1}^{2}-1}{b_{2n-1}^{2}+1-\lambda_{n}^{-2}}},\quad\cot\chi_{n}=\epsilon_{2n}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}-b_{2n}^{2}-1}{b_{2n}^{2}+1-\lambda_{n}^{-2}}},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2n-1}=\textrm{sign}\bigl(b_{2n}(1+b_{2n-1}^{2})-b_{2n-1}\bigr),\quad\epsilon_{2n}=\textrm{sign}\bigl(b_{2n-1}(1+b_{2n}^{2})-b_{2n}\bigr).

Properties of decomposition (
3.1
) can be described by the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Function
\lambda_{n}
is continuous with respect to
b_{2n-1},b_{2n}
and satisfies

\lambda_{n}\geq 1,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

If additionally the following condition

(H_{1})\quad\quad\inf\limits_{n}\biggl((b_{2n-1}-b_{2n})^{2}+b_{2n-1}^{2}b_{2n}^{2}\biggr)=h_{*}^{2}>0

is fullfilled, then functions
\varphi_{n}
,
\chi_{n}
are continuous with respect to
b_{2n-1},b_{2n}
and
there exists a positive constant
\Lambda_{0}>1
such that

\lambda_{n}\geq\Lambda_{0},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

PROOF:
The continuity of
\lambda_{n}
immediately follows from formulae (
3.1
). Also one may note that
\lambda_{n}
increases with respect to
h_{n}
, while condition

(H_{1})
implies
h_{n}\geq h_{*}
. Hence, one may set

\Lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{4+h_{*}^{2}}+\sqrt{h_{*}^{2}}\right).

To prove continuity of
\varphi_{n}
,
\chi_{n}
we apply formula for
\lambda_{n}
and obtain that

\lambda_{n}^{2}-b_{2n-1}^{2}-1=2\frac{\left(b_{2n-1}^{2}+1\right)^{2}\left(b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{(*)}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}}\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}+4}-h_{n}^{2}+2b_{2n-1}^{2}},

where we introduced

b_{2n}^{(*)}=\frac{b_{2n-1}}{1+b_{2n-1}^{2}}.

The same substitution also yields

b_{2n-1}^{2}+1-\lambda_{n}^{-2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}}\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}+4}-h_{n}^{2}+2b_{2n-1}^{2}\right).

Since
b_{2n-1},b_{2n}
enter into
\lambda_{n}
symmetrically, one obtains similar formulae by interchanging
b_{2n-1}
and
b_{2n}
. Namely,

\lambda_{n}^{2}-b_{2n}^{2}-1=2\frac{\left(b_{2n}^{2}+1\right)^{2}\left(b_{2n-1}-b_{2n-1}^{(*)}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}}\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}+4}-h_{n}^{2}+2b_{2n}^{2}},

where

b_{2n-1}^{(*)}=\frac{b_{2n}}{1+b_{2n}^{2}}

and

b_{2n}^{2}+1-\lambda_{n}^{-2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}}\sqrt{h_{n}^{2}+4}-h_{n}^{2}+2b_{2n}^{2}\right).

Notice also that

\epsilon_{2n-1}={\rm sign}\left(b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{(*)}\right),\quad\epsilon_{2n}={\rm sign}\left(b_{2n-1}-b_{2n-1}^{(*)}\right),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

and

\lambda_{n}^{2}-b_{2n-1}^{2}-1\geq 0,\quad\lambda_{n}^{2}-b_{2n}^{2}-1\geq 0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Taking this into account we rewrite expressions for
\cot\varphi_{n}
and
\cot\psi_{n}
as

\cot\varphi_{n}=\frac{b_{2n-1}^{2}+1}{b_{2n-1}^{2}+1-\lambda_{n}^{-2}}\left(b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{(*)}\right),\quad\cot\psi_{n}=\frac{b_{2n}^{2}+1}{b_{2n}^{2}+1-\lambda_{n}^{-2}}\left(b_{2n-1}-b_{2n-1}^{(*)}\right).

(3.2)

We conclude that continuity of
\varphi_{n}
,
\chi_{n}
follows from

(\ref{f_varphi_psi_new})
. This finishes the proof.
∎

In the rest of the paper we will assume that condition
(H_{1})
from Lemma 1
takes place. For example, it is satisfied if the sequence
\{b_{j}\}
is such that
the Worpitsky’s condition (
2.3
) holds or if for all

j\in\mathbb{N}
the following implication is valid

|b_{j}|<2\Rightarrow|b_{j+1}|\geq 2.

Finally, we introduce a sequence of matrices

\displaystyle B_{n}=R(\Phi_{n})Z(\lambda_{n}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}=\psi_{n+1}+\varphi_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

It is to be noted that hyperbolic properties of cocycles generated by
\{A_{n}\}
and

\{B_{n}\}
are equivalent. On the other hand, cocycles generated by matrices (
3.3
) have simpler form and were studied in many papers (e.g.
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
). We use results of
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
to establish hyperbolicity of the cocycle generated by matrices (
3.3
).

Due to represenation (
3.3
) a cocycle generated by the sequence
\{B_{n}\}
is a product of interchanged matrices
R
and
Z
, i.e.

M_{n}=\vprod\limits_{k=1}^{n}R(\Phi_{k})Z(\lambda_{k}).

(3.4)

Matrices
R
and
Z
possesses an obvious property:

R(x_{1}+x_{2})=R(x_{1})R(x_{2}),\quad Z(x_{1}+x_{2})=Z(x_{1})Z(x_{2}),\quad\forall\,x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}

and, hence, matrices
R
(respectively,
Z
) commute.
On the other hand,
R(x_{1})Z(x_{2})\neq Z(x_{2})R(x_{1})
(in general). However, we have the following lemma

Lemma 2
.

A product
P_{1}=Z(\lambda_{2})R(\varphi)Z(\lambda_{1})
can be represented as

P_{1}=R(\psi)Z(\mu)R(\chi),

where

\displaystyle\mu=S(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(m(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)+\sqrt{m^{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)-4}\right),

\displaystyle m(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)=(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-1})\sqrt{\cos^{2}\varphi+\beta^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi},\quad\beta=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{-2}+\lambda_{2}^{-2}}{1+\lambda_{1}^{-2}\lambda_{2}^{-2}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\tan\chi=T(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi),\quad\tan\psi=T(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1},\varphi),\quad T(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)=\epsilon(\varphi)\sqrt{1+z^{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)}-z(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi),

\displaystyle z(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2(1-\lambda_{2}^{-4})}\left((1-\lambda_{1}^{-4}\lambda_{2}^{-4})\cot\varphi+(\lambda_{2}^{-4}-\lambda_{1}^{-4})\tan\varphi\right),\quad\epsilon(\varphi)={\rm sign}(2\varphi).

PROOF:
Formulae (
3.5
) can be verified by their direct substitution into
P_{1}
.
∎

We remark here that Lemma 2 is analogous to Lemma 1 from
[
18
]
. However, formulae (
3.5
) seem to be more convenient for analysis. In particular, as an immediate consequence, one has:

\displaystyle S(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)=S(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1},\varphi)=S(\lambda_{1}^{-1},\lambda_{2}^{-1},\varphi)=S(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},-\varphi),

\displaystyle T(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\varphi)=-T(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},-\varphi).

4
Hyperbolicity and convergence

In this section we obtain sufficient conditions for the convergence of continued fractions by establishing hyperbolicity of the correponding cocycles.

First, we apply Lemma 2 to a cocycle (
3.4
) to get the following

Lemma 3
.

Assume
\Lambda_{0},C_{\lambda},\delta
be positive constants such that

\Lambda_{0}>1,\quad C_{\lambda}>\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_{0}^{2}-1}},\quad\delta>\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}-1}

(4.1)

and

C_{\lambda}\geq\frac{\delta-1+\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{(\delta-1)\Lambda_{0}^{2}-\delta}.

(4.2)

If parameters of a cocycle (
3.4
) satisfy

\lambda_{n}\geq\Lambda_{0},\quad\Lambda_{0}\bigl|\cot(\Phi_{n})\bigr|\geq\delta C_{\lambda},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},

(4.3)

then the cocycle admits an estimate

\|M_{n}\|\geq\Lambda_{0}\hat{C}_{\lambda}^{n-1},\quad\hat{C}_{\lambda}=\frac{\Lambda_{0}C_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_{0}^{2}+C_{\lambda}^{2}}},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}

PROOF:
First we apply Lemma 2 to the product (
3.4
) and represent it as

M_{n}=R(\Phi_{n}+\psi_{n})Z(\mu_{n})R(\chi_{n}),

where

\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1},\quad\psi_{1}=0,\quad\chi_{1}=0

(4.4)

and

\mu_{n}=S(\mu_{n-1},\lambda_{n},\Phi_{n-1}+\psi_{n-1}),\quad\tan\psi_{n}=T(\lambda_{n},\mu_{n-1},\Phi_{n-1}+\psi_{n-1}).

(4.5)

Note that to prove Lemma 3 it suffices to prove the following estimates:

\mu_{n}\geq\Lambda_{0}\hat{C}_{\lambda}^{n-1},\quad\Lambda_{0}|\cot(\Phi_{n}+\psi_{n})|\geq C_{\lambda},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.6)

We argue by induction. For
n=1
estimates (
4.6
) follow from (
4.4
), (
4.1
) and (
4.3
). Assume that (
4.6
) are valid for
n=k
.
Note that
1<\hat{C}_{\lambda}<C_{\lambda}
due to (
4.1
). Besides, taking into account Lemma 2, one has

\mu_{k+1}\geq\lambda_{k+1}\mu_{k}\bigl|\cos(\Phi_{k}+\psi_{k})\bigr|.

Hence, the first estimate of (
4.3
) together with estimates (
4.6
) for
n=k
imply

\mu_{k+1}\geq\Lambda_{0}\hat{C}_{\lambda}^{k}.

To prove the second estimate (
4.6
) we introduce

z_{k+1}=z(\lambda_{k+1},\mu_{k},\Phi_{k}+\psi_{k}).

Then one gets

z_{k+1}=\frac{1}{2(1-\mu_{k}^{-4})}\left(\left(1-\lambda_{k+1}^{-4}\mu_{k}^{-4}\right)u_{k}-\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{k+1}^{4}}{\mu_{k}^{4}}\right)u_{k}^{-1}\right),\quad u_{k}=\lambda_{k+1}^{2}\cot\Phi_{k}.

Using formulae (
3.5
) and (
4.6
) for
n=k
, we obtain

\bigl|\tan\psi_{k+1}\bigr|<\frac{1}{2|z_{k+1}|}<\frac{1-\mu_{k}^{-4}}{1-\lambda_{k+1}^{-4}\mu_{k}^{-4}}\cdot\frac{1}{u_{k}-u_{k}^{-1}}<\frac{\Lambda_{0}C_{\lambda}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\lambda}^{2}-1}.

(4.7)

Note that by assumption (
4.3
)
|\tan\Phi_{k+1}|\leq\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{\delta C_{\lambda}}
. This estimate together with (
4.7
) and (
4.3
) yield

\displaystyle\bigl|\tan\Phi_{k+1}\cdot\tan\psi_{k+1}\bigr|\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\cdot\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\lambda}^{2}-1}<1,

\displaystyle\bigl|\tan\Phi_{k+1}\bigr|+\bigl|\tan\psi_{k+1}\bigr|\leq\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{\delta C_{\lambda}}+\frac{\Lambda_{0}C_{\lambda}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\lambda}^{2}-1}

and, consequently,

\left|\tan\bigl(\Phi_{k+1}+\psi_{k+1}\bigr)\right|\leq\frac{\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{\delta C_{\lambda}}+\frac{\Lambda_{0}C_{\lambda}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\lambda}^{2}-1}}{1-\frac{1}{\delta}\cdot\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\lambda}^{2}-1}}.

(4.8)

We apply inequality (
4.2
) to estimate the right hand side of (
4.8
) and obtain

\left|\tan\bigl(\Phi_{k+1}+\psi_{k+1}\bigr)\right|\leq\frac{\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{\delta C_{\lambda}}+\frac{\Lambda_{0}C_{\lambda}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\lambda}^{2}-1}}{1-\frac{1}{\delta}\cdot\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{\Lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\lambda}^{2}-1}}\leq\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{C_{\lambda}}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

PROOF:
One may note that under condition of the Corollary hypotheseses

Remark

can be divided into pairs of indices such that the distance between neighbouring pairs can be comparable with the difference between indices constituting a pair, but the cocycle of matrices, which satisfy condition
(H_{2})
, possesses ”strong” hyperbolic behaviour. As an example, one may consider a continued fraction
\{(-1,b_{j}),f_{j}\}
such that

\forall\,j\notin\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\quad b_{j}=g\hat{b}_{j},\quad 0<C_{1}\leq|\hat{b}_{j}|\leq C_{2},\quad g\gg 1,

(4.26)

where
\{t_{k}\}
is an increasing sequence of indices and
C_{1,2}
are constants. Thus, we arrive at the following corollary to Theorem 3.

Corollary 2
.

Assume that for a given increasing sequence of natural numbers
\{t_{k}\}
a continued fraction
\{(-1,b_{j}),f_{j}\}
satisfies conditions
(\ref{def_seq_b})
,
(H_{1})
and

\displaystyle 1.\quad\exists\,K_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}:\quad t_{k+1}-t_{k}\geq 4,\quad\forall\,k\geq K_{0},

\displaystyle 2.\quad\left|b_{j}-\frac{b_{j-1}}{1+b_{j-1}^{2}}\right|<\frac{1}{2C_{1}^{2}g^{2}},\quad\forall\,j\in\{t_{k}\}:\,\,j=2s,\,s\in\mathbb{N}

(4.27)

\displaystyle 3.\quad\left|b_{j}-\frac{b_{j+1}}{1+b_{j+1}^{2}}\right|<\frac{1}{2C_{1}^{2}g^{2}},\quad\forall\,j\in\{t_{k}\}:\,\,j=2s-1,\,s\in\mathbb{N}.

If for a sequence
\xi=\{m_{k}\}
such that

m_{k}=\left[\frac{t_{k}}{2}\right],\quad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N},

(4.28)

where
[x]
stands for the integer part of a real number
x
,
there exist a natural number
N_{0}
, positive constant
\alpha
and a sequence of indices
\{l_{k}\}
satisfying

(\ref{cond_seq_l})
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
the following conditions:

\displaystyle 4.\quad n_{k+1}-j_{k}\leq N_{0},

\displaystyle 5.\quad j_{k}-l_{k}>n_{k+1}-j_{k},

(4.29)

\displaystyle 6.\quad\left|\cot\Phi_{k}^{(\xi),-}\right|\leq\Bigl(C_{2}g\Bigr)^{-4(n_{k+1}-j_{k})+2-\alpha},

\displaystyle 7.\quad\cot\Phi_{k}^{(\xi),+}\cdot\cot\Phi_{k}^{(\xi),-}<0,

hold true, then there exists
g_{0}
such that for all
g>g_{0}
the continued fraction
\{(-1,b_{j}),f_{j}\}
converges.

PROOF:
It has to be noted that the first condition
(\ref{cond_C2_1})
yields both conditions
(\ref{cond_seq_m})
,
(\ref{cond_seq_m_2})
for the sequence

(\ref{def_seq_m_2})
.

Consider formulae
(\ref{SVD_2})
and assume that
2n-1,2n\notin\{t_{j}\}
. In this case

|b_{2n-1}|\geq C_{1}g,\quad|b_{2n}|\geq C_{1}g,

which leads to

\lambda_{n}\geq\Bigl(C_{1}g\Bigr)^{2},\quad|\cot\Phi_{n}|\geq C_{1}g(1+O(g^{-2})).

(4.30)

Thus, matrices
B_{n}
satisfy conditions of Lemma 3, for example, with constants

\Lambda_{0}^{(\min)}=C_{1}g,\quad C_{\lambda}=\Lambda_{0}^{(\min)}/2,\quad\delta=2

(4.31)

provided
g
to be sufficiently large.

Assume now that
2n\in\{t_{j}\}
. As it was mentioned in Lemma 1, equality

(\ref{eq_b_2k})
implies

\cot\varphi_{n}=0.

Expanding
\cot\varphi_{n}
near

b_{2n}^{*}=\frac{b_{2n-1}}{1+b_{2n-1}^{2}},

one obtains

|\cot\varphi_{n}|=\frac{\left(1+b_{2n-1}^{2}\right)^{2}}{b_{2n-1}^{2}\left(b_{2n-1}^{2}+2\right)}\cdot\Bigl|b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{*}\Bigr|\cdot\left(1+O\left(\Bigl|b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{*}\Bigr|\right)\right).

Since
2n-1\notin\{t_{j}\}
then
|b_{2n-1}|\geq C_{1}g
and we get

\lambda_{n}\geq C_{1}g\left(1+O(g^{-2})\right),\quad|\cot\Phi_{n}|=C_{1}g\cdot\Bigl|b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{*}\Bigr|\cdot\left(1+O\left(g^{-2}+\Bigl|b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{*}\Bigr|\right)\right).

(4.32)

Thus, the second condition
(\ref{cond_C2_1})
guarantees violation of
(H_{2})
for
B_{n}
with
2n\in\{t_{j}\}
.

The case
2n-1\in\{t_{j}\}
can be considered in a similar way. Then one gets

\lambda_{n-1}\geq C_{1}g\left(1+O(g^{-2})\right),\quad|\cot\Phi_{n-1}|=C_{1}g\cdot\Bigl|b_{2n-1}-b_{2n-1}^{*}\Bigr|\cdot\left(1+O\left(g^{-2}+\Bigl|b_{2n-1}-b_{2n-1}^{*}\Bigr|\right)\right),

(4.33)

where

b_{2n-1}^{*}=\frac{b_{2n}}{1+b_{2n}^{2}}.

We conclude that the third condition
(\ref{cond_C2_1})
leads to violation of
(H_{2})
for
B_{n-1}
with
2n-1\in\{t_{j}\}
.

It follows from
(\ref{SVD_2})
that whenever
2n-1,2n\notin\{t_{j}\}

\lambda_{n}\leq(C_{2}g)^{2}(1+O(g^{-2})).

(4.34)

If
2n\in\{t_{j}\}
we expand
\lambda_{n}^{2}
near
b_{2n}=b_{2n}^{*}
:

\lambda_{n}^{2}=1+b_{2n-1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+b_{2n-1}^{2}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{b_{2n-1}^{2}+2}+\frac{1}{b_{2n-1}^{2}}\right)\Bigl(b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{*}\Bigr)^{2}\cdot\left(1+O\left(\Bigl|b_{2n}-b_{2n}^{*}\Bigr|\right)\right)

Then the second condition
(\ref{cond_C2_1})
gives

\lambda_{n}\leq C_{2}g(1+O(g^{-2})).

(4.35)

In a similar way one obtains that for
2n-1\in\{t_{j}\}

\lambda_{n-1}\leq C_{2}g(1+O(g^{-2})).

(4.36)

We combine estimates
(\ref{est_ctg_phi_0})
,
(\ref{est_ctg_phi_1})
,

(\ref{est_ctg_phi_2})
together with
(\ref{est_lambda_0})
,
(\ref{est_lambda_1})
,

(\ref{est_lambda_2})
and get

\displaystyle\left(\hat{C}_{1}g\right)^{2(j_{k}-l_{k})-1}\leq\lambda_{k}^{(\xi),-}\leq\left(\hat{C}_{2}g\right)^{2(j_{k}-l_{k})-1},

(4.37)

\displaystyle\left(\hat{C}_{1}g\right)^{2(n_{k+1}-j_{k})-1}\leq\lambda_{k}^{(\xi),+}\leq\left(\hat{C}_{2}g\right)^{2(n_{k+1}-j_{k})-1},

where
\hat{C}_{1}<C_{1}
,
\hat{C}_{2}>C_{2}
are positive constants. Note that

\hat{C}_{1},\hat{C}_{2}
can be chosen arbitrary close to
C_{1},C_{2}
, respectively, for sufficiently large
g
.
Hence

\frac{\lambda_{k}^{(\xi),-}}{\lambda_{k}^{(\xi),+}}\geq\frac{\left(\hat{C}_{1}\right)^{2(j_{k}-l_{k})-1}}{\left(\hat{C}_{2}\right)^{2(n_{k+1}-j_{k})-1}}g^{2(2j_{k}-n_{k+1}-l_{k})}=\frac{\left(\hat{C}_{1}g^{(1-\eta)}\right)^{2(j_{k}-l_{k})}}{\hat{C}_{1}\left(\hat{C}_{2}\right)^{2(n_{k+1}-j_{k})-1}}g^{2\bigl(\eta(j_{k}-l_{k})-(n_{k+1}-j_{k})\bigr)}

(4.38)

for any
0<\eta<1
.
We set

\eta=\frac{N_{0}+\nu}{N_{0}+1},\quad 0<\nu<1.

Then, due to
(\ref{est_frac_lambda})
and the second inequality
(\ref{cond_C2_2})
one obtains

\frac{\lambda_{k}^{(\xi),-}}{\lambda_{k}^{(\xi),+}}\geq\frac{\left(\hat{C}_{1}g^{(1-\eta)}\right)^{2(j_{k}-l_{k})}}{\hat{C}_{1}\left(\hat{C}_{2}\right)^{2(n_{k+1}-j_{k})-1}}g^{2\nu}\geq g^{2\nu}

provided
g
to be sufficiently large. This allows to define

G_{\lambda}=g^{2\nu},\quad 0<\nu<1.

We consider
\nu
such that
\nu<1/2
. In this case

\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(\min)}}{G_{\lambda}}=C_{1}g^{1-2\nu}\gg 1,

(4.39)

hence, it is possible to apply Lemma 5.

Besides, one has

\varkappa_{k}=1+O(g^{-8}),\quad\rho_{k}=1+O(g^{-8})

(4.40)

Substituting
(\ref{est_lambda_xi})
,
(\ref{def_G_lambda})
,
(\ref{est_kappa_rho})
into inequality 6 of
(\ref{cond_T3})
, we obtain that an estimate

\left(\hat{C}_{2}g\right)^{4(n_{k+1}-j_{k})-2}\Bigl|\cot\Phi_{k}^{(\xi),-}\Bigr|\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}^{-1}g^{-(1-2\nu)}=\frac{G_{\lambda}}{2\Lambda_{0}}

implies condition 6 in
(\ref{cond_T3})
. But this estimate follows from condition 6 in

(\ref{cond_C2_2})
if one fixes
\nu
such that

\nu>\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.

Finally, we notice that

\Phi_{k}^{(\xi),+}=\Phi_{m_{2k+1}}+O(g^{-4}),\quad\left|\cot\Psi_{k}^{(\xi)}\right|\leq C_{2}^{-1}g^{-\alpha}\left(1+O\left(g^{-\alpha}\right)\right)\leq\frac{G_{\lambda}}{\Lambda_{0}}.

Taking this into account together with
(\ref{def_G_lambda})
, one obtains that condition 7 in
(\ref{cond_T3})
is satisfied provided condition 7 of
(\ref{cond_C2_2})
holds true. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark:
It has to be noted that although

\Phi_{k}^{(\xi),-}=\Phi_{m_{2k}}+O(g^{-4})

one cannot replace
\Phi_{k}^{(\xi),-}
by
\Phi_{m_{2k}}
in condition 6 of

(\ref{cond_C2_2})
.

5
Critical set method

In the rest of the paper we study convergence of a functional continued fractions generated by a pair
(b,\omega)
with
b\in C(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{R})
,

\omega\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
in the sense of Definition 2. We suppose that a function
b
satisfies condition
(H^{\prime}_{1})
, but condition
(H^{\prime}_{2})
is violated.

More precisely, we consider a functional continued fraction, generated by a sequence

b_{j}(x)=gb(x-(j-1)\omega),\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}

with
b\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{R})
,
\omega\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
and
g\gg 1
.

It is supposed the following condition is fullfilled

(H^{\prime\prime}_{1})\quad\quad\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}}\biggl((b(x)-b(x-\omega))^{2}+g^{2}b^{2}(x)b^{2}(x-\omega)\biggr)\geq h_{*}^{2}>0,

where
h_{*}
does not depend on
g
.

The associated cocycle
(\ref{coc_B_2})
can be constructed by means of linear transformation

\displaystyle B(x)=R(\Phi(x))Z(\lambda(x)),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=\psi(x-\omega)+\varphi(x),

where expressions for the functions
\lambda,\varphi,\chi
are given by formulae

(\ref{SVD_2})
with
b_{2k},b_{2k-1}
being replaced by
gb(x-\omega),gb(x)
, respectively.

Following
[
17
]
we introduce the critical set

\mathcal{C}_{0}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}:\cos\Phi(x)=0\right\}

and assume that

(H_{3})\quad\quad\quad\mathcal{C}_{0}=\bigcup\limits_{p=0}^{P}\{x_{p}\},\quad P\in\mathbb{N};\quad\Phi^{\prime}(x_{p})\neq 0,\,\,\forall\,p\in\{0,\ldots P\}.

It has to be noted that due to condition
(H^{\prime\prime}_{1})
and formulae
(\ref{SVD_2})

\cot\varphi(x)
and
\cot\psi(x-\omega)
cannot vanish simultaneously. Moreover,

(H^{\prime\prime}_{1})
imples that there exists a positive constant
\Lambda_{0}>1
such that

\lambda(x)\geq\Lambda_{0}g,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}.

Hence, if
\cot\varphi(x)=o(1)
(resp.
\cot\psi(x-\omega))=o(1)
) then
|\cot\psi(x-\omega)|\geq Cg
(resp.
|\cot\varphi(x)|\geq Cg
) with some positive constant
C
. Due to differentiabilty of the functions
\varphi
and
\psi
, it means that each point of the critical set belongs to a
g^{-1}
-neighbourhood of a solution of the following equation

\cot\varphi(x)\cdot\cot\psi(x-\omega)=0.

(5.2)

Note that by Lemma 1 equation
(\ref{eq_Crit_1})
can be rewritten as

\left(b(x-\omega)-\frac{b(x)}{1+g^{2}b^{2}(x)}\right)\cdot\left(b(x-\omega)-\frac{b(x-2\omega)}{1+g^{2}b^{2}(x-2\omega)}\right)=0.

(5.3)

On the other hand, by the implicit function theorem, each solution of
(\ref{eq_Crit_1_a})
lies in a
g^{-2}
-neighborhood of a point
x_{p}^{(0)}
, which is a solution of the equation

b(x-\omega)=0.

Thus, condition
(H_{3})
can be replaced by

(H^{\prime}_{3})\quad\quad\quad\mathcal{C}_{0}^{(0)}=\bigcup\limits_{p=0}^{P}\{x_{p}^{(0)}\},\quad P\in\mathbb{N};\quad b^{\prime}(x_{p}^{(0)})\neq 0,\,\,\forall\,p\in\{0,\ldots P\},

where
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{(0)}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}:b(x-\omega)=0\right\}
.

One notes that condition
(H^{\prime}_{3})
implies that
P
is odd, i.e. the critical set consists of even number of points. We also emphasize that differentiability of the function
b
can be replaced by its differentiability in small neighborhoods of the critical set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
, its image and preimage under rotation over an angle
-2\pi\omega
. However, to simplify exposition we consider the case
b\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{R})
.

Besides, to shorten formulae in the rest of the paper we introduce for any
\varpi\in\mathbb{R}
the rotation map over an angle
-2\pi\varpi
by

\sigma_{\varpi}:\mathbb{T}^{1}\to\mathbb{T}^{1},\quad\sigma_{\varpi}:x\mapsto x-\varpi\mod 1.

Hence,

\sigma_{\varpi}^{k}:x\mapsto x-\varpi k\mod 1,\quad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Define for any
p\in\{0,\ldots P\}
and
\delta>0
the time of collision
(see
[
17
]
)

T_{\delta}(x_{p})=\min\bigl\{k\in\mathbb{N}:U_{\delta}\bigl(\sigma^{k}_{2\omega}(x_{p})\bigr)\cap U_{\delta}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\neq\emptyset\bigr\},

where
U_{\delta}(x)
and
U_{\delta}(A)
stand for
\delta
-neighborhoods of

x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
and
A\subset\mathbb{T}^{1}
, respectively.

Taking
\delta
to be sufficiently small, we may assume that there exists a unique index

R_{\delta}(p)
such that

\sigma_{2\omega}^{T_{\delta}(x_{p})}(x_{p})\in U_{\delta}(x_{R_{\delta}(p)}).

We will call the case
R_{\delta}(p)=p
the primary collision, while the case

R_{\delta}(p)\neq p
will be called the secondary collision (see
[
18
]
). It has to be noted that the time of primary collision depends only on arithmetical properties of the rotation number
\omega
. In contrary, the time of secondary collision depends also on the function
b
and, in particular, on distances

dist(x_{p},x_{p^{\prime}})
,
p\neq p^{\prime}
, where

dist(x,y)=\{|x-y|\},\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{1}

and
\{x\}
denotes the fractional part of
x
.

For each
p=0,\ldots,P
we define a period

S(p)=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\,\,R_{\delta}^{k}(p)=p\}.

Definition 3
.

We say that critical points
x_{p},x_{p^{\prime}},p\neq p^{\prime}
are cyclically connected if there exists an integer
k<S(p)
such that

R_{\delta}^{k}(p)=p^{\prime}.

Notice that if
x_{p},x_{p^{\prime}},p\neq p^{\prime}
are cyclically connected then
S(p)=S(p^{\prime})
. Besides, we remark that cyclic connectness is an equivalence relation and we denote the class of equivalence for a point
x_{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
by
[x_{p}]
. Thus, the critical set is divided into classes of equivalence. Let
M
be the number of such equivalence class. Then enumerating them, one obtains

\mathcal{C}_{0}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{M}L_{i},

where
L_{i}
stands for the
i
-th class of equivalence. Since all points inside a class

L_{i}
have the same period, we denote this period by
S_{i}
.

In each class
L_{i}
we fix a point
x_{p_{*}}
and denote it by
x_{i,1}
. Then other points from this class of equivalence can be enumerated such that

\sigma_{2\omega}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x_{i,k})\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1}),\quad k=1,\ldots,S_{i}-2.

This yields

R_{\delta}\bigl((i,k)\bigr)=(i,k+1\mod S_{i}).

In the rest we will omit the notation
"\mod S_{i}"
, but will always presume that the second index in a pair
(i,k)
is calculated modulo
S_{i}
.

One notes that due to conditions
(H^{\prime\prime}_{1})
and
(H^{\prime}_{3})
the following estimates are valid for sufficiently small
\delta

\displaystyle|b(x)|\geq C_{1}\delta,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\setminus U_{\delta}(\mathcal{C}_{0}),

\displaystyle b(x)=b^{\prime}(x_{i,k})(x-x_{i,k})\cdot(1+o(1)),\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(\mathcal{C}_{0}),

(5.4)

\displaystyle b^{\prime}(x)=b^{\prime}(x_{i,k})\cdot(1+o(1)),\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(\mathcal{C}_{0}),

where constant
C_{1}
can be chosen arbitrary close to

\min\limits_{(i,k)}\bigl|b^{\prime}(x_{i,k}^{(0)})\bigr|
by decreasing of
\delta
.

We assign to each class of equivalence
L_{i}
a set of matrices

D_{i,k}(x)=\vprod\limits_{j=1}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}B\left(\sigma_{2\omega}^{j}(x)\right),\quad k=1,\ldots,S_{i}-1,\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}),

and represent them by means of SVD decomposition

D_{i,k}(x)=R(\varphi_{i,k}(x))Z(\lambda_{i,k}(x)R(\chi_{i,k}(x)),\quad k=1,\ldots,S_{i}-1,\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

Define

\Phi_{i,k}(x)=\chi_{i,k+1}\left(\sigma_{2\omega}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\right)+\varphi_{i,k}(x),\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

The following lemma holds true.

Lemma 6
.

There exists a unique solution
x_{i,k}^{(*)}
of an equation

\cos\Phi_{i,k}(x)=0,\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

(5.5)

It satisfies an estimate

dist\left(\sigma_{2\omega}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\left(x_{i,k}^{(*)}\right),x_{i,k+1}\right)=O\bigl(g^{-2}\bigr).

(5.6)

PROOF:
First we notice that

\sigma_{2\omega}^{j}(x)\notin U_{\delta}(\mathcal{C}_{0}),\quad\forall\,j=1,\ldots,T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-1,\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

This leads to an estimate

\lambda\left(\sigma_{2\omega}^{j}(x)\right)\geq(C_{1}\delta g)^{2},\quad\forall\,j=1,\ldots,T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-1,\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

(5.7)

On the other hand, if
j=T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})
then it may happen that for some

x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})
its image under rotation over the angle
-2\pi T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})
falls into
U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})
and estimate
(\ref{est_lambda_good})
is violated. However, due to condition
(H^{\prime}_{1})
one has

\lambda\left(\sigma_{2\omega}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\right)\geq\Lambda_{0}g,\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

Applying consequitively
(\ref{SVD_2})
to products of matrices

\vprod\limits_{j=1}^{s}B\left(\sigma_{2\omega}^{j}(x)\right),\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})

starting from
s=2
to
s=T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\varphi_{i,k}(x)=\varphi\left(\sigma_{2\omega}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\right)+\psi_{i,k}(x),\quad\psi_{i,k}(x)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\psi_{i,k}^{(j)}(x),\quad\chi_{i,k}(x)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\chi_{i,k}^{(j)}(x),

\displaystyle\psi_{i,k}^{(1)}(x)=O\bigl(g^{-2}\bigr),\quad\psi_{i,k}^{(j)}(x)=O\left(g^{-2(2j-1)}\right),\quad\forall\,j=2,\ldots,T_{\delta}(x_{i,k}),\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}),

(5.8)

\displaystyle\chi_{i,k}^{(1)}(x)=O\bigl(g^{-2}\bigr),\quad\chi_{i,k}^{(j)}(x)=O\left(g^{-2(2j-1)}\right),\quad\forall\,j=2,\ldots,T_{\delta}(x_{i,k}),\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

Moreover, it follows from differentiability of the function
b
and formulae
(\ref{SVD_2})
that

\psi^{\prime}_{i,k}(x)=O\bigl(g^{-2}\bigr),\quad\chi^{\prime}_{i,k}(x)=O\bigl(g^{-2}\bigr),\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k}).

(5.9)

Taking into account
(\ref{cond_b})
,
(\ref{f_Phi_ik})
,
(\ref{est_der_Phi_ik})
, we conclude by the implicit function theorem that for sufficiently large
g
there exists a unique solution of equation
(\ref{eq_Phi_ik})
which satisfies estimate
(\ref{est_x_ik})
.

∎

As a consequence of Lemma 6 one obtains that there exist positive constants

r_{i,k}^{\min},r_{i,k}^{\max}
such that

r_{i,k}^{\min}g\left|x-x_{i,k}^{(*)}\right|\leq\left|\cot\Phi_{i,k}(x)\right|\leq r_{i,k}^{\max}g\left|x-x_{i,k}^{(*)}\right|.

(5.10)

Note that constants
r_{i,k}^{\min},r_{i,k}^{\max}
can be chosen close to

|b^{\prime}(x_{i,k}^{(0)})|
provided
\delta
to be sufficiently small.

For each
(i,k),i=1,\ldots,M,k=1,\ldots,S_{i}-1
we define

\Delta_{i,k}=\sigma_{2\omega}^{T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(x_{i,k}^{(*)}\bigr)-x_{i,k+1}^{(*)}.

Consider a product

P_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)=R\Bigl(\Phi_{i,k+1}\bigl(\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\bigr)\Bigr)\cdot Z\Bigl(\lambda_{i,k+1}\bigl(\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\bigr)\Bigr)\cdot R\bigl(\Phi_{i,k}(x)\bigr)\cdot Z\bigl(\Phi_{i,k}(x)\bigr)

for

x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)

and represent it in a form

P_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)=R\bigl(\varphi_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\bigr)Z\bigl(\lambda_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\bigr)R\bigl(\chi_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\bigr),\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr).

Then we arrive at

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that for a given
(i,k),i\in\{1,\ldots,M\},k\in\{1,\ldots,S_{i}-1\}
the following conditions are satisfied

T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})>T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1}),\quad b^{\prime}\left(x_{i,k}^{(0)}\right)\cdot b^{\prime}\left(x_{i,k+1}^{(0)}\right)<0,\quad\left|\Delta_{i,j}\right|\leq\frac{\left(r_{i,k}^{\max}\cdot r_{i,k+1}^{\max}\right)^{-1/2}}{g(C_{2}g)^{2T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})-1}},

(5.11)

where

C_{2}=\max\limits_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}}|b(x)|.

Then

\left|\cot\Phi_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\right|\geq\frac{\left(r_{i,k+1}^{\max}/r_{i,k}^{\max}\right)^{1/2}}{(C_{2}g)^{2T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})-1}}\cdot\left(1+O\left(g^{-4T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})+2}+g^{-8\bigl(T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)}\right)\right).

PROOF:
Note that estimate
(\ref{est_lambda_good})
and condition
(H^{\prime}_{1})
imply

\Lambda_{0}g\left(C_{1}\delta g\right)^{2(T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-1)}\leq\lambda_{i,k}(x)\leq\left(C_{2}g\right)^{2T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-1},\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr).

(5.12)

Hence, the first condition
(\ref{cond_L7})
guarantees that

\lambda_{i,k}(x)\gg\lambda_{i,k+1}(x)

uniformly with respect to

x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)
.

Due to estimates
(\ref{est_r_ik})
the rest of the proof can be done similar to Lemma 4 from
[
18
]
. We outline the main idea.

Introduce a function

\zeta_{i,k}(x)=z\Bigl(\lambda_{i,k+1}\bigl(\sigma_{2\omega T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}^{-1}(x)\bigr),\lambda_{i,k}(x),\varphi_{i,k}(x)\Bigr),

where the function
z
is defined by
(\ref{f_P1})
. Then, by
(\ref{f_zeta})
and

(\ref{est_lambda_ik})
one obtains a uniform estimate

\cot\psi_{i,k}(x)=u_{i,k}(x)\left(1+O\left(g^{-8\bigl(T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)}\right)\right),\quad x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr).

where
\psi_{i,k}(x)=\varphi_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)-\Phi_{i,k+1}\bigl(\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\bigr)
and

u_{i,k}(x)=\lambda_{i,k+1}^{2}\bigl(\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\bigr)\cot\Phi_{i,k}(x)
.

We apply
(\ref{est_r_ik})
to get a similar estimate for
\cot\psi_{i,k}(x)
:

r_{i,k}^{\min}g\mu_{i,k}^{2}(x)\left|x-x_{i,k}^{(*)}\right|\leq\left|\cot\psi_{i,k}(x)\right|\leq r_{i,k}^{\max}g\mu_{i,k}^{2}(x)\left|x-x_{i,k}^{(*)}\right|

with
\mu_{i,k}(x)=\lambda_{i,k+1}\bigl(\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\bigr)
.

Thus, both
\cot\psi_{i,k}(x)
and

\cot\Phi_{i,k+1}\bigl(\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}(x)\bigr)
are well approximated (in the sense of
(\ref{est_cot_psi_ik})
and
(\ref{est_r_ik})
, respectively) near
x_{i,k}^{*}
by linear functions. It leads to

\left|\cot\Phi_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\right|\geq\frac{ga}{1-a\mu^{-2}}\cdot\left|\frac{y^{2}+py+q}{y-w}\right|,\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega T_{\delta}(x_{i,j})}^{-1}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr),

(5.13)

where variable
y
and constants
\mu,a,p,q,w
are

\displaystyle y=r_{i,k}^{\max}\bigl(x-x_{i,k}^{(*)}\bigr),\quad\mu=\left(C_{2}g\right)^{2T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})-1}\left(1+O\left(g^{-8\bigl(T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)}\right)\right),

\displaystyle a=-\frac{r_{i,k+1}^{\max}}{r_{i,k}^{\min}},\quad p=r_{i,k}^{\min}\Delta_{i,k},\quad q=\frac{r_{i,k}^{\min}}{r_{i,k+1}^{\max}}g^{-2}\mu^{-2},\quad w=\frac{r_{i,k+1}^{\max}}{r_{i,k}^{\min}\mu^{2}-r_{i,k+1}^{\max}}.

(5.14)

Here we used the second condition
(\ref{cond_L7})
to obtain
a<0
.

Finally, we notice that under condition
p^{2}<q
the minimum value of
the right hand side in inequality
(\ref{est_Phi_ik_2})
is
|a|^{1/2}\mu^{-1}\bigl(1+O(\mu^{-2})\bigr)
. Substituting this value into
(\ref{est_Phi_ik_2})
and taking into account
(\ref{def_y_mu_a_p_q_w})
one finishes the proof.

It follows from
(\ref{est_m})
and
(\ref{est_lambda_ik})
that

\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}^{-1}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)

\lambda_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\geq\frac{\Lambda_{0}\left(C_{1}\delta\right)^{2(T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})-1)}}{\left(C_{2}\right)^{2T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})-1}}g^{2(T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1}))}.

If periods
T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})
,
T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})
satisfy

T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\geq 2T_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1}),

then

\lambda_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\cdot\left|\cot\Phi_{i,k}^{(2)}(x)\right|\geq C_{\lambda}^{(2)}g,\quad\forall\,x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)

with some positive constant
C_{\lambda}^{(2)}
. It means that the product

P_{i,k}^{(2)}
satisfies conditions of Lemma 3.

Remark:
It has to be noted that condition
(\ref{est_T_k_k+1})
is stronger than condition 5 in
(\ref{cond_C2_2})
. The necessity to use such kind of condition is dictated by the fact that Lemma 5 cannot be applied to the product
P_{i,k}^{(2)}
directly. Indeed, inequality
(\ref{cond_L5_2})
does not hold for all
x\in U_{\delta}(x_{i,k})\cap\sigma_{2\omega}^{-T_{\delta}(x_{i,k})}\bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,k+1})\bigr)
. It is satisfied only for those
x
which are sufficiently close to
x_{i,k}^{(*)}
.

For a fixed
i
we consider the equivalence class
L_{i}
of cyclically connected points.

Definition 4
.

We say that an equivalence class
L_{i}
obeys a condition
(H_{4})
if

1.\quad\quad\quad b^{\prime}\left(x_{i,k}^{(0)}\right)\cdot b^{\prime}\left(x_{i,k+1}^{(0)}\right)<0,\quad\forall\,k=1,\ldots,S_{i}

and one of two sets of conditions:

\displaystyle 2.1\quad\quad

\displaystyle a.

\displaystyle\quad\quad T_{\delta}(x_{i,2k-1})\geq 2T_{\delta}(x_{i,2k}),\quad\forall\,k=1,\ldots,S_{i}/2,

\displaystyle b.

\displaystyle\quad\quad|\Delta_{i,2k-1}|\leq\frac{\left(r_{i,2k-1}^{\max}\cdot r_{i,2k}^{\max}\right)^{-1/2}}{g(C_{2}g)^{2T_{\delta}(x_{i,2k})-1}},\quad\forall\,k=1,\ldots,S_{i}/2;

\displaystyle 2.2\quad\quad

\displaystyle a.

\displaystyle\quad\quad T_{\delta}(x_{i,2k})\geq 2T_{\delta}(x_{i,2k+1}),\quad\forall\,k=1,\ldots,S_{i}/2,

\displaystyle b.

\displaystyle\quad\quad|\Delta_{i,2k}|\leq\frac{\left(r_{i,2k}^{\max}\cdot r_{i,2k+1}^{\max}\right)^{-1/2}}{g(C_{2}g)^{2T_{\delta}(x_{i,2k+1})-1}},\quad\forall\,k=1,\ldots,S_{i}/2;

holds.

Remarks:
1. We point out that the first condition of Definition 4 together with assumption
(H^{\prime}_{3})
guarantee that
S_{i}
is even. Thus, conditions 2.1, 2.2 are well-posed.

2. Assume that for a fixed
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
the equivalence class
L_{i}
obeys condition
(H_{4})
such that the set of conditions 2.1 of Definition 4 is satisfied. Then one may assert that for all

j=1,\ldots,S_{i}/2
a product of matrices

\vprod\limits_{k=1}^{j}P_{i,2k-1}^{(2)}(x),\quad x\in\mathcal{M}_{i}=\bigcap\limits_{l=1}^{2j}\sigma_{2\omega\tau_{l}}^{-1}\Bigl(U_{\delta}(x_{i,l})\Bigr),\quad\tau_{l}=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{l-1}T_{\delta}(x_{i,s})

admits estimate
(\ref{coc_est})
. It also has to be noted that set
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is not empty. Moreover, estimates 2.1b yield

U_{\delta/2}(x_{i,1})\subset\mathcal{M}_{i}\subset U_{\delta}(x_{i,1}).

3. Notice that if
x\notin U_{\delta/2}(\mathcal{C}_{0})
is such that

\sigma_{\omega}(x)\notin U_{\delta/2}(\mathcal{C}_{0})
then due to
(\ref{cond_b})
one gets

\lambda(x)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\delta g\right)^{2},\quad\left|\cot\Phi(x)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\delta g.

Hence, if a trajectory of a point
x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
under rotation over an angle
-2\pi\omega
does not fall into
U_{\delta/2}(\mathcal{C}_{0})
then the corresponding product of matrices
(\ref{coc_B_3})
satisfies conditions of Lemma 3 provided
g
to be sufficiently large.

Summarizing the obtained results, we arrive at a theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
g_{0}
be a large positive parameter and
b\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{R})
satisfies condition
(H^{\prime}_{3})
. Suppose that for
\omega\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
there exist positive constants
\delta
and
h_{*}
such that

(H^{\prime\prime}_{1,0})\quad\quad\min\limits_{x\in U_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{(0)}\right)}\left(\left(b(x)-b(x-\omega)\right)^{2}+g_{0}^{2}b^{2}(x)b^{2}(x-\omega)\right)\geq h_{*}^{2}.

Assume that
g_{0}
and
\delta
are such that the estimates
(\ref{cond_b})
hold true and the map
R_{\delta}
is correctly defined for all
g\in[g_{0},+\infty)
.

Let
M
denotes the number of equivalence classes of cyclically connected points. If for some
g\in[g_{0},+\infty)
and for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
an equivalence class
L_{i}
obeys condition
(H_{4})
, then
a continued fraction

\{(-1,gb_{j}),f_{j}\}
with
b_{j}=b(x-(j-1)\omega)
converges and

f_{*}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\in C(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{\hat{R}})
.

PROOF:
Let
B
be the linear transformation
(\ref{coc_B_3})
of a cocycle associated to the continued fraction
\{(-1,gb_{j}),f_{j}\}
. One notes that condition
(H^{\prime\prime}_{1,0})
implies condition
(H^{\prime\prime}_{1})
for all
g\in[g_{0},+\infty)
, which guarantees the existence of positive constant
\Lambda_{0}
such that

\lambda(x)\geq\Lambda_{0}g,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}.

Consider an arbitrary point

x\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
. As it was mentioned above, a sequence of matrices associated to a finite trajectory of
x
before its first enter to

U_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{(0)}\right)
satisfies
(\ref{coc_est})
(due to Lemma 3). As the trajectory enters
U_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)
it stays in a
\delta
-neighborhood of critical points
x_{i,k},k=1,\ldots,S_{i}
for some particular
i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
. We remark that in this case the trajectory may approach points

x_{i,k}^{(*)},k=1,\ldots,S_{i}
very close. Nevertheless, if the number of iteration is multiple of
S_{i}
, then the corresponding product of matrices
B
still admits estimate

(\ref{coc_est})
(due to condition
(H_{4})
). On the other hand, if the trajectory stays in

\mathbb{T}^{1}\setminus U_{\delta/2}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)
it also satisfies

(\ref{coc_est})
(due to Lemma 3).

It has to be emphasized that dynamics under rotation over the angle
-2\pi\omega
possesses the following property of monotonicity. Denote

\mathcal{C}_{0,i}=\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{S_{i}}\{x_{i,k}\}.

Then, if the trajectory of a point
x
leaves
U_{\delta/2}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0,i}\right)
, it does not enter this neighborhood before it leaves

U_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0,i}\right)
(see e.g.
[
19
]
).

Let
K
be a number of iteration between the trajectory successively enters and exits
U_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0,i}\right)
. We represent it as

K=m_{i}S_{i}+n_{i},\quad m_{i},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}.

Notice that the last
n_{i}
iterations the trajectory stays in

U_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0,i}\right)\setminus U_{\delta/2}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0,i}\right)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remarks:
1. We point out that condition
(H^{\prime\prime}_{1})
follows from

(H^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1})\quad\quad\min\limits_{x\in U_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{(0)}\right)}\left|b(x)-b(x-\omega)\right|\geq h_{*}>0,

which does not contain the parameter
g
and can be easily checked.

2. It has to be emphasized that points
x_{i,k}^{(*)}
for all
i\in[1,\ldots,M],k\in[1,\ldots,S_{i}]
depend on parameter
g
. This implies the distances

dist(x_{i,k}^{(*)},x_{i,k+1}^{(*)})
vary with parameter
g
. Taking into account
Lemma 6, one estimates

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}g}dist(x_{i,k},x_{i,k+1})=O(g^{-3}).

On the other hand, condition
(H_{4})
prescribes relative positions of

x_{i,k}^{(*)},x_{i,k+1}^{(*)}
with very high precision. In particular, if we denote

\tau_{\max}=\max\limits_{i=1,\ldots,M}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{S_{i}-1}T_{\delta}(x_{i,k}),\quad r_{\max}=\max\limits_{i,k}r_{i,k}^{\max},

where
r_{i,k}^{\max}
are defined by
(\ref{est_r_ik})
, then
relative positions of
x_{i,k}^{(*)},x_{i,k+1}^{(*)}
are defined up to

\Delta_{\min}=\frac{r_{\max}^{-2}}{g\left(C_{2}g\right)^{2\tau_{\max}/3-1}}.

This suggests that whenever condition
(H_{4})
holds for some
g_{*}\geq g_{0}
then one may expect that
(H_{4})
also holds for the nearest values of
g
such that

|g-g_{*}|=O\left(g_{*}^{3-2\tau_{\max}/3}\right).

3. The simplest case corresponds to those functions
b\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{1},\mathbb{R})
, which have only two points
x_{0}^{(0)},x_{1}^{(0)}
satisfying

b(x_{p}^{(0)})=0,\quad b^{\prime}(x_{p}^{(0)})\neq 0.

In this case the critical set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
also consists of two points. Moreover, if the time of secondary collisions less than the time of primary ones, there is only one equivalence class of cyclically connected points (
M=1
). This case has been studied in
[
18
]
.

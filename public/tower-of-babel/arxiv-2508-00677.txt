A New Class of Linear Relations for Scalar Partitions
Boris Y. Rubinstein

Abstract
A scalar integer partition problem asks for a number of nonnegative integer solutions to a linear Diophantine equation with integer positive coefficients. The manuscript discusses an algorithm of derivation of linear relations involving the finite number of scalar partitions. The algorithm employs the Cayley theorem about the reduction of a double partition to a sum of scalar partitions based on the variable elimination procedure.



Keywords
: integer partition, double partition.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
: 11P82.

1
Integer scalar partitions

The problem of integer partition into a set of integers
is equivalent to counting number
of nonnegative integer solutions of the Diophantine equation

s=\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}x_{i}={\bf d}\cdot{\bf x}.

(1)

A scalar partition function
W(s,{\bf d})\equiv W(s,\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{m}\})

solving the above problem is a
number of partitions of an integer
s
into positive integer generators

\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{m}\}
.
The generating function for
W(s,{\bf d})

has a form

G(t,{\bf d})=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-t^{d_{i}}}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}W(s,{\bf d})\;t^{s}\;.

(2)

Sylvester proved
[
11
]
a statement about splitting the
scalar partition into periodic and non-periodic parts and showed that it
might be presented as a sum of ”waves”

W(s,{\bf d})=\sum_{j=1}W_{j}(s,{\bf d})\;,

(3)

where the summation runs over all distinct factors
of the elements
d_{i}
of the generator vector
{\bf d}
, and each
wave
W_{j}(s,{\bf d})
is a quasipolynomial in
s

closely related to prime roots
x=\rho_{j}=\exp(2\pi in/j)
of the equation
x^{j}=1
for
n
relatively prime to
j

(including unity) and smaller than
j
.
It is possible to express any Sylvester wave
W_{j}(s,{\bf d})
and thus the scalar partition
W(s,{\bf d})
through the Bernoulli polynomials of higher order
[
7
]
.

The definition (
2
) of the generating function for
W(s,{\bf d})
implies the recursion relation

W(s,{\bf d})-W(s-d_{i},{\bf d})=W(s,\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{i-1},d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{m}\}),

(4)

involving three scalar partitions – two with
m
generators and
one with
m-1
generators.
As the selection of the
generator
d_{i}
to be dropped from the vector
{\bf d}
is arbitrary, one can produce

m
relations similar to (
4
).

There are just a few relations involving the partitions
W(s,{\bf d})

are known in addition to (
4
).
For example it was shown in
[
6
]
that the similar relation holds for each Sylvester wave
W_{j}(s,{\bf d})

W_{j}(s,{\bf d})-W_{j}(s-d_{i},{\bf d})=W_{j}(s,\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{i-1},d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{m}\}),

(5)

In his pioneering work
[
2
]
on the analytical expression for the scalar partitions Cayley
noted that the function
W(s,{\bf d})
satisfies the
following parity property for the positive values of
s

W(s,{\bf d})=(-1)^{m+1}W(-s-\sigma({\bf d}),{\bf d}),\quad\quad\sigma({\bf d})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i},

(6)

and pointed out that
W(s,{\bf d})
vanishes at all integer negative points in the range

-\sigma({\bf d})<s<0
. It is worth to note that Cayley
considered the parity property as “…uninterpretable in the theory of partitions”
[
2
]
.
The same results were obtained independently in
[
4
]
using
only the resursion (
4
).

This manuscript introduces the linear relations of different structure – (a)
each term in the relation has the same number
m
of generators; (b)
the number of terms in the relation is
(m+1)
– one more than the number of generators;
and (c) the number of such relations is unlimited.
The algorithm of derivation of these relations is based on the reduction of
a specially constructed double partition into a sum of scalar partitions.
The general idea of the reduction was proposed by Sylvester in
[
12
]

and the actual reduction algorithm is due to Cayley
[
3
]
who also
discussed the limitations of the method.
The manuscript shows how these conditions should be employed for the double partition construction to obtain the required relations.

2
Sylvester-Cayley reduction algorithm for double partitions

Double partitions used for the derivation of the scalar partition relations
represent the simplest particular case of
vector partitions
.
The vector partition function
W({\bf s},{\bf D})
counts the number of integer
nonnegative solutions
{\bf x}\geq 0

to a linear system
{\bf s}={\bf D}\cdot{\bf x}
, where

{\bf D}=\{{\bf c}_{1},{\bf c}_{2},\ldots,{\bf c}_{m}\}
is a nonnegative integer
l\times m
generator matrix (
l<m
)
made of columns
{\bf c}_{i}=\{c_{i1},c_{i2},\ldots,c_{il}\}^{T},\ (1\leq i\leq m)

with
\{\cdot\}^{T}
denoting transposition of a vector.

Sylvester suggested an iterative procedure of
reduction of a vector partition into a sum of scalar partitions
[
12
]
; the procedure is based on the
variable elimination from the system of linear equations
{\bf s}={\bf D}\cdot{\bf x}
.
Based on this approach Cayley
developed an algorithm of double partition reduction
and established a set of conditions of the method applicability
[
3
]
.
Cayley showed that each column
{\bf c}_{i}
of the two-row matrix
{\bf D}
gives rise
to a scalar partition and elements of this column must be relatively prime (we call it P-column).
Another limitation of the Sylvester-Cayley method is that the columns
{\bf c}_{i}

should represent
noncollinear
vectors.
The practical application of the Cayley algorithm to the computation of the Gaussian polynomial
coefficients is considered in
[
8
]
.
The details of the Cayley reduction method for double partitions and its
modification that works without any limitations for arbitrary double partition
is described in
[
9
]
.

It should be noted that
similar restictions to those discussed by Cayley in
[
3
]
were earlier
mentioned in
[
12
]
for vector partitions with arbitrary dimension
l
of vector
{\bf s}
.
Later Sylvester in his lectures
[
13
]
offered an alternative approach to lifting
these limitations and it appears to be quite useful for our derivation procedure.

Consider the Sylvester idea in more details. The variable elimination algorithm for
vector partition reduction fails when the generator matrix
{\bf D}
has
either collinear columns or nonprime columns (NP-columns) or both, where
the elements of the NP-column
{\bf c}
have the greatest common divisor (GCD)
of its elements
\mbox{gcd}({\bf c})>1
.
Sylvester suggested to add to the system
{\bf s}={\bf D}\cdot{\bf x}
a single auxiliary equation
and a new unknown
x_{m+1}

in order to convert the original system into a larger one
\hat{\bf s}=\hat{\bf D}\cdot\hat{\bf x}
,
where
\hat{\bf x}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m},x_{m+1}\}
.
The matrix

\hat{\bf D}=\{\hat{\bf c}_{1},\hat{\bf c}_{2},\ldots,\hat{\bf c}_{m},\hat{\bf c}_{m+1}\}

consists of
(m+1)
columns
\hat{\bf c}_{i}=\{c_{i0},c_{i1},c_{i2},\ldots,c_{il}\}^{T}
.
The elements
c_{i0}
and the value of
s_{0}
in the added equation must be chosen to satisfy two conditions –
(a) each nonnegative integer solution of the original system
{\bf s}={\bf D}\cdot{\bf x}

should correspond to a single solution of the system
\hat{\bf s}=\hat{\bf D}\cdot\hat{\bf x}
,
and (b) the matrix
\hat{\bf D}
should be void of collinear and NP-columns.
The first condition leads to

W(\hat{\bf s},\hat{\bf D})=W({\bf s},{\bf D}),

(7)

while the second one guarantees that the vector partition
W(\hat{\bf s},\hat{\bf D})
admits the
variable elimination procedure of reduction (at least the first step of it).

3
Linear relations for scalar partitions

Consider an application of the Sylvester method to the scalar partition
W(s,{\bf d})

when we have the single original equation
s={\bf d}\cdot{\bf x}
and add an equation

\sigma=x_{m+1}-\bm{\delta}\cdot{\bf x}
producing the system of two equations

\sigma=x_{m+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{i}x_{i},\quad s=\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}x_{i},

(8)

with positive
\delta_{i}
and nonnegative
\sigma
(see the fifth lecture in
[
13
]
).
This system
corresponds to the double partition
W(\hat{\bf s},\hat{\bf D})
with

\hat{\bf D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}-\delta_{1}&-\delta_{2}&\ldots&-\delta_{m}&1\\
d_{1}&d_{2}&\ldots&d_{m}&0\end{array}\right),\quad\hat{\bf s}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma\\
s\end{array}\right).

(9)

Each integer nonnegative solution
{\bf x}=\bm{\xi}=\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{m}\}

of the second equation
s={\bf d}\cdot{\bf x}

corresponds to the following solution of (
8
)

x_{i}=\xi_{i},\quad(1\leq i\leq m),\quad x_{m+1}=\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{i}\xi_{i}.

(10)

We observe that the condition (
7
) is satisfied as for each solution

\bm{\xi}
of the original Diophantine equation
s={\bf d}\cdot{\bf x}
there
exists the single solution (
10
) of the system (
8
).

The process of the double partition reduction through the
elimination of the variables
x_{i}
one by one is discussed
in
[
3
,
8
,
9
]
for the nonnegative matrix
{\bf D}
and
its generalization to the arbitrary matrices is given in
[
10
]
.
As each column of
\hat{\bf D}
leads to a single scalar partition the result of the reduction is a sum of
(m+1)
scalar partitions.
It is shown in
[
10
]
that the contribution of the last column
\hat{\bf c}_{m+1}=\{1,0\}^{T}

is zero and we end up with the scalar partition
W(s,{\bf d})
expressed as a sum of

m
scalar partitions
W(s_{i},{\bf d}_{i})

with
{\bf d}_{i}=\{d_{i,1},d_{i,2},\ldots,d_{i,m},d_{i,m+1}\}
, i.e., the
linear relation reads

w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})\equiv W(s,{\bf d})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}W(s_{i},{\bf d}_{i})=0,\\

(11)

where

d_{i,m+1}=d_{i},\quad d_{i,j}=\delta_{i}d_{j}-\delta_{j}d_{i},\ (1\leq j\leq m,j\neq i),\quad s_{i}=s\delta_{i}+\sigma d_{i}.

(12)

As for
1\leq i,j\leq m
we have
d_{i,j}+d_{j,i}=0
, the quantities
\sigma({\bf d}_{i})
satisfy the relation

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma({\bf d}_{i})=\sigma({\bf d}).

(13)

Consider the term
W(s_{i},{\bf d}_{i})
– in the number theory context it is defined only for nonnegative integer values
of
s_{i}
. As
s_{i}\geq s\delta_{i}\geq 0
for positive
\delta_{i}
and nonnegative
s

we can set
\sigma=0
without loss of generality and further we always use
s_{i}=s\delta_{i}
.
All columns
\hat{\bf c}_{j}=\{-\delta_{j},d_{j}\}^{T}

are noncollinear so that all elements of the vector
{\bf d}_{i}
are nonzero but some of them might be negative
(say,
d_{i,j_{k}}<0
for
1\leq k\leq K_{i}
).
Noting that

(1-t^{-a})^{-1}=-t^{a}(1-t^{a})^{-1}
we find an equivalent scalar partition with positive generators only

W(s_{i},{\bf d}_{i})=(-1)^{K_{i}}W(s\delta_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}d_{i,j_{k}},|{\bf d}_{i}|),\quad|{\bf d}_{i}|=\{|d_{ij}|\}.

(14)

The selection of the positive
\delta_{i}
values is a (partially heuristic) process and we discuss it
in two major cases – (a) all
d_{i}
in the set
{\bf d}
are unique,
and (b) the set
{\bf d}
contains duplicate generators.

In the case of the unique generators
the simplest choice for the vector
\bm{\delta}
is to set
all
its elements to unity
\bm{\delta}={\bf 1}
, i.e.,
\delta_{i}=1
,
and we obtain

\hat{\bf D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}-1&-1&\ldots&-1&1\\
d_{1}&d_{2}&\ldots&d_{m}&0\end{array}\right).

(15)

From (
12
) we find the general term
W(s_{i},{\bf d}_{i})
with

s_{i}=s,\quad d_{i,m+1}=d_{i},\quad d_{i,j}=d_{j}-d_{i},\ (1\leq j\leq m,j\neq i).

(16)

We observe that this choice of
\delta_{i}
makes all
terms in the relation (
11
) to have the same argument
s
.
The example of the relation with
\bm{\delta}={\bf 1}
is discussed in Section
5.1
.

Other possibilities assume one or more
\delta_{i}>1
and these values should be taken
with caution not to produce a NP-column or/and a group of collinear columns.
For example,
\delta_{i}
should be odd for any even
d_{i}
, and
if
k\mid d_{r}
one should set
\delta_{r}\neq k
.

When the original scalar partition has several duplicate elements in the set
{\bf d}
,
say
d_{1}=d_{2}=\ldots=d_{k}
one has to choose
\delta_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq k,
all different such that also

\mbox{gcd}(d_{i},\delta_{i})=1
; we consider the representative example in Section
5.2
.

4
Properties of linear relations

The relation (
3
) presents
the scalar partition
W(s,{\bf d})
as a sum of the pure polynomial

W_{1}(s,{\bf d})
and several quasipolynomials
W_{j}(s,{\bf d})
, each being
a superposition of the polynomial multiplied by the periodic function of period
j

[
11
,
7
]
.
The order
n_{1}=m-1
of the polynomial part
W_{1}(s,{\bf d})
is one less the number
m
of
generators in
{\bf d}
, while the order
n_{j}=m_{j}-1
of the polynomial factor in
W_{j}(s,{\bf d})

is one less the multiplicity
m_{j}
of the factor
j
among all generators
d_{i}
.
The function
w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})
in (
11
) has
m+1
scalar partition terms each one having the
polynomial part
W_{1}(s\delta_{i},{\bf d}_{i})
of order
m-1
as well as multiple
quasipolynomials with polynomial factors of the order
0\leq m_{j}<m-1
of periodic functions with integer period
j>1
.

It should be underlined that in the integer partition context the relation (
11
)
is satisfied for all nonnegative
integer
values of
s
.
As the individual summand in (
11
) is represented by a quasipolynomial that has periodic
factors with integer period one may extend
W(s,{\bf d})
to a function of continuous argument
[
7
]
by
choosing the
j
-periodic function
\psi_{j}(s)
to be
the prime radical circulator introduced by Cayley in
[
2
]

\psi_{j}(s)=\Psi_{j}(s)=\sum_{\rho_{j}}\rho_{j}^{s},\quad\rho_{j}=\exp(2\pi in/j),

(17)

where the summation is made over all primitive roots of unity
\rho_{j}
with
n

relatively prime to
j
(including unity) and smaller than
j
.

The
w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})
has a quite unusual behavior – it vanishes in
all integer points
of the real axis
while its behavior between them requires further analysis.
The general expression for the factor
U_{k}(s,\bm{\delta})
of
s^{k},\ (0\leq k\leq m-1)
in the quasipolynomial
w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})

has the finite number of
\Psi_{j}(s\delta_{i}-r_{j})
terms oscillating around zero:

U_{k}(s,\bm{\delta})=A_{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j>1}\ \sum_{r_{j}=1}^{j}C_{k,j,r_{j}}\Psi_{j}(s\delta_{i}-r_{j}),\quad 0\leq k\leq m-1,

(18)

where we introduce the notation
\delta_{0}\equiv 1
.
The value of
U_{k}(s,\bm{\delta})
should be zero at the infinite number of integer values of
s
and it is possible only
when
A_{k}=0
.
Then the purely polynomial part
w_{1}(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})

defined through
the corresponding part of
w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})
summands should vanish at any real
s

w_{1}(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})\equiv W_{1}(s,{\bf d})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}W_{1}(s\delta_{i},{\bf d}_{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}A_{k}s^{k}=0.

(19)

Here
W_{1}(s,{\bf d})
has the following functional representation

W_{1}(s,{\bf d})=\frac{1}{(m-1)!\pi({\bf d})}B^{(m)}_{m-1}(s+\sigma({\bf d}),{\bf d}),\quad\quad\pi({\bf d})=\prod_{i=1}^{m}d_{i},

(20)

and the Bernoulli polynomials of higher order
B^{(m)}_{n}(s,{\bf d})
are defined by the
generating function
[
5
,
1
]

\frac{e^{st}\prod_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(e^{d_{i}t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B^{(m)}_{n}(s,{\bf d})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Substitution of (
20
) into (
21
) produces the linear relation for
the Bernoulli polynomials of higher order

\frac{B^{(m)}_{m-1}(s+\sigma({\bf d}),{\bf d})}{\pi({\bf d})}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{B^{(m)}_{m-1}(s\delta_{i}+\sigma({\bf d}_{i}),{\bf d}_{i})}{\pi({\bf d}_{i})}=0.

(21)

The functional form (
20
) of the polynomial part
W_{1}(s,{\bf d})
immediately leads to
the explicit expression for its leading term
\tilde{W}_{1}(s,{\bf d})

[
6
]

\tilde{W}_{1}(s,{\bf d})=\frac{s^{m-1}}{(m-1)!\pi({\bf d})},

(22)

allowing us to obtain the nontrivial numerical relation

\tilde{w}_{1}(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})\equiv\tilde{W}_{1}(s,{\bf d})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{W}_{1}(s\delta_{i},{\bf d}_{i})=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\pi({\bf d})\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\delta_{i}^{m-1}}{\pi({\bf d}_{i})}=1.

(23)

Returning to the expression of
w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})

write it as a multiple sum of powers of
s
multiplied by the periodic triginometric functions

w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\sum_{j>1}\ \sum_{r_{j}=1}^{j}C_{k,j,r_{j}}s^{k}\Psi_{j}(s\delta_{i}-r_{j}).

It should be noted that while
\Psi_{j}(s-r_{j})
has integer period
j
, the period of the function

\Psi_{j}(s\delta_{i}-r_{j})
for
\delta_{i}>1
is given in the general case by a rational fraction
j/\delta_{i}
.
This observation makes the case
\bm{\delta}={\bf 1}
to be special as
then all terms in
w(s,{\bf d},{\bf 1})
have the integer periods only. The number of these terms is finite
and as they cancel each other at infinite number of integer points they should cancel identically for
both integer and real argument values.

On the contrary, when
\bm{\delta}\neq{\bf 1}
the complete term cancelling is possible at the integer points only
but the terms with the rational periods
j/\delta_{i}
in
W(s\delta_{i},{\bf d}_{i})

cannot compensate the integer periods terms in
W(s,{\bf d})
for the real
s
values.
As the result the value of the function
w(s,{\bf d},\bm{\delta})
between the integers points is not zero
(excepting the countable number of points).

6
Conclusion

In conclusion, we present the algorithm allowing to derive infinite number of linear relations
for the scalar partitions satisfied at all
integer
values of the argument.
These relations give rise to more specialized formulae (valid at the real argument values too) for
the polynomial parts of these partitions that in its turn produce a new class of relations for the
Bernoulli polynomials of higher order.
The general expression for leading term of the scalar partition polynomial part
leads to a novel class of the numerical relations.

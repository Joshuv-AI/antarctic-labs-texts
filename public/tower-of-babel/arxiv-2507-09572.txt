Adaptive dynamics of nonlocal competition models with heterogeneous resources
Shen Bian

Abstract
We investigate the long-time behavior of phenotype-structured models describing evolutionary dynamics of asexual populations, and analyze the joint effects of nonlocal interactions and spatial resource distributions on the global dynamics of the two species. In the first part, we consider an integro-differential system without diffusion terms, where phenotypic changes are absent and the spatial distribution of resources for one species is heterogeneous while that of the other is homogeneous. Using an entropy method to address nonlocal interactions and resource heterogeneity, we prove that the species subject to heterogeneous resources converges to a Dirac mass concentrated at the peak of the phenotypic fitness landscape, which establishes the selection of the best adapted trait. Numerical experiments further provide a sufficient criterion to identify the positions of fitness peaks. In the second part, we extend our study to a nonlocal reaction-diffusion system involving a linear diffusion operator, where heritable phenotypic changes occur and both resources are spatially heterogeneous. Through an appropriate transformation, we overcome the challenges induced by resource heterogeneity and prove that the long-time limits of the two species under different interspecific competitive coefficients are given by distinct steady states of the parabolic system, with concentrations at the maxima of their respective resource functions. Numerical results confirm the predictions and further reveal phenomena beyond the theoretical analysis.

Introduction

In the theory of adaptive dynamics which focuses on phenotypic evolution, populations are structured by a parameter describing a biological, physiological or ecological characteristic of the individuals. When this characteristic is inherent to the individuals, we refer to it as a phenotypic trait (denoted by a continuous real variable
x
below)
[
33
,
34
]
. The main ingredient in this theory is the selection principle which favors the population with best adapted trait in terms of resource utilization, and such a trait is called an evolutionary stable strategy (ESS). The origin of this denomination comes from evolution theory
[
38
]
, no mutant with a different trait can invade a population with the trait corresponding to an ESS. In many scenarios of biological interest, one can assume
x\in\Omega,
where
\Omega
is a smoothly bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
or the entire space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for

A classical example of such models is a two-species competition system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=a_{1}\Delta u+u\left(m_{1}(x)-u-bv\right),&\text{in}~~\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\\
v_{t}=a_{2}\Delta v+v\left(m_{2}(x)-cu-v\right),&\text{in}~~\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\\
\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v=0,&\text{on}~~\partial\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\\
(u,v)(x,0)=(u_{0},v_{0})(x),&\text{in}~~\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a smoothly bounded domain,
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
denote the population densities of two competing species. The functions
m_{1}(x)
and
m_{2}(x)
are either the carrying capacities or intrinsic growth rates, which model the spatial distribution of resources for species
u
and
v
, respectively. The constants
b>0,c>0
measure the inter-specific competitive abilities, while the intra-specific competition coefficients are normalized to one.

When the resource is spatially homogeneous, namely both

When at least one of

We focus here on the case where, in the absence of phenotypic changes, the evolution of population densities is governed by an integro-differential system of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=u\left(d(x)-r_{1}(t)-br_{2}(t)\right),&(x,t)~\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\\

Preliminaries

In this section, we compile some preparatory assumptions and lemmas that will be used in the proof of the long-time asymptotic behavior of solutions to (

Throughout this paper, we assume that the initial data satisfy

\displaystyle 0<r_{1}(0)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u_{0}(x)dx<\infty,~~0<r_{2}(0)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}v_{0}(x)dx<\infty.

(13)

The function

Let us next review the classical Lotka-Volterra system for the two competing species:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Y_{t}=Y\left(\overline{d}-Y-bX\right),&t>0,\\
X_{t}=X\left(\overline{m}-cY-X\right),&t>0\\

where

and

are the population densities of two competing species,
\overline{d},\overline{m}
are the intrinsic growth rates. The system (

) has four equilibria: the coexistence equilibrium

P_{1}:=(Y^{\ast},X^{\ast})=\left(\frac{\bar{d}-b\overline{m}}{1-bc},\frac{\overline{m}-c\bar{d}}{1-bc}\right),

the competitive exclusion equilibria

P_{2}:=\left(\overline{d},0\right),\quad P_{3}:=\left(0,\overline{m}\right)

and the unstable equilibrium
(0,0).
The dynamics of system (

) have been extensively studied in the literature

,

,

,

,
13
,
40
]
and can be summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Under the assumptions
b>0,c>0
, the following statements hold for system (
2
).

(i)

If
c<\frac{\overline{m}}{\overline{d}}
and
b<\frac{\overline{d}}{\overline{m}}
, then the positive coexistence equilibrium
P_{1}\left(\frac{\bar{d}-b\overline{m}}{1-bc},\frac{\overline{m}-c\bar{d}}{1-bc}\right)
is globally asymptotically stable.

(ii)

If
c>\frac{\overline{m}}{\overline{d}}
and
b<\frac{\overline{d}}{\overline{m}}
, then the competitive exclusion equilibrium
P_{2}\left(\overline{d},0\right)
is globally asymptotically stable.

(iii)

If
c<\frac{\overline{m}}{\overline{d}}
and
b>\frac{\overline{d}}{\overline{m}}
, then the competitive exclusion equilibrium
P_{3}\left(0,\overline{m}\right)
is globally asymptotically stable.

(iv)

If

and

then (
2
) has a compact global attractor consisting of a continuum of steady states
\left(\eta\overline{d},(1-\eta)\overline{d}/b\right)
for
\eta\in[0,1].

(v)

If
c>\frac{\overline{m}}{\overline{d}}
and
b>\frac{\overline{d}}{\overline{m}}
, then

In
[
11
,
13
]
, Lemma

(i)-(iii) are proved by applying a Lyapunov functional method. For the proof of Lemma

(iv) one can refer to
[
14
]
for more details. In the case of (v), the equilibrium

\text{if}~X(0)>h(Y(0)),~\text{then}~\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}(Y(t),X(t))=(0,\overline{m}).\\
\end{array}\right.

The properties of the separatrix

]

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that

and

. Then there exists a monotone function

defined on
[0,\infty)
whose graph is the separatrix of (
2
). Moreover, the function satisfies

(i)

h(0)=0;h(Y^{\ast})=X^{\ast};

(ii)

h^{\prime}(Y)>0~~\text{in}~~[0,\infty)
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{Y\to\infty}h(Y)=\infty;

(iv)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{\prime\prime}(Y)<0,&\overline{d}>\overline{m},\\
h^{\prime\prime}(Y)=0,&\overline{d}=\overline{m}\\
h^{\prime\prime}(Y)>0,&\overline{d}<\overline{m};\end{array}\right.

Y(\overline{d}-Y-bh(Y))h^{\prime}(Y)=h(Y)(\overline{m}-cY-h(Y))

In order to describe the stable manifold near the saddle point, we solve

\displaystyle\vec{F}(\vec{x})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f(Y,X)\\
g(Y,X)\\
\end{array}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}Y\left(\overline{d}-Y-bX\right)\\
X\left(\overline{m}-cY-X\right)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle A:=\frac{\partial\vec{F}(Y,X)}{\partial(Y,X)}\Bigg|_{(Y^{\ast},X^{\ast})}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{d}-2Y-bX&-bY\\

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,2}=\frac{-(X^{\ast}+Y^{\ast})\pm\sqrt{(X^{\ast})^{2}+(Y^{\ast})^{2}+(4bc-2)X^{\ast}Y^{\ast}}}{2}.

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{-(X^{\ast}+Y^{\ast})-\sqrt{(X^{\ast})^{2}+(Y^{\ast})^{2}+(4bc-2)X^{\ast}Y^{\ast}}}{2}<0

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=\frac{Y^{\ast}+\lambda_{1}}{-bY^{\ast}}=\frac{-cX^{\ast}}{X^{\ast}+\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Y^{\ast}-X^{\ast}-\sqrt{(X^{\ast})^{2}+(Y^{\ast})^{2}+(4bc-2)X^{\ast}Y^{\ast}}}{-2bY^{\ast}}.

Secondly, we look for the higher-order analytical approximation of

. We expand

\displaystyle h(Y)=X^{\ast}+k(Y-Y^{\ast})+a_{2}(Y-Y^{\ast})^{2}+O(|Y-Y^{\ast}|^{3})

Substituting it into system (

\displaystyle\frac{dh(Y)}{dY}f(Y,h(Y))=g(Y,h(Y)).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle f(Y,h(Y))

\displaystyle=(-bkY^{\ast}-Y^{\ast})(Y-Y^{\ast})+(-a_{2}bY^{\ast}-bk-1)(Y-Y^{\ast})^{2}+O(|Y-Y^{\ast}|^{3}),

\displaystyle a_{2}=\frac{(c-1)k+(1-b)k^{2}}{-Y^{\ast}-X^{\ast}-3\lambda_{1}}.

Finally, the stable manifold near the saddle point can be approximated as

(A priori estimates for

\displaystyle\Omega=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}~\big|~u(x,t)>0\}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}~\big|~u_{0}(x)>0\},~~\forall~t>0.

Under assumption (

to obtain

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d}{dt}r_{1}(t)\leq r_{1}(t)\left(d_{M}-r_{1}(t)-br_{2}(t)\right),\\[2.84526pt]

Step 2

\displaystyle P+r_{1}P_{r_{1}}

\displaystyle=r_{1}+br_{2},

\displaystyle r_{1}P_{r_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{c}\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right).

Subsequently, direct calculations yield that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-P(r_{1},r_{2})\right)udx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-P-r_{1}P_{r_{1}}\right)\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)udx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}P_{r_{2}}r_{1}v\left(\overline{m}-cr_{1}-r_{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)^{2}udx+\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)P_{r_{2}}r_{1}r_{2}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)^{2}udx+\frac{b}{c}\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)^{2}r_{2}\geq 0.

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)^{2}udx,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=r_{2}\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)^{2}.

As a consequence, the bounded quantity

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-P(r_{1},r_{2})\right)udx\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}L\in{\mathbb{R}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{1}+I_{2}dt<\infty.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(I_{1}+I_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)^{2}udx+\frac{d}{dt}\left[r_{2}\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+2br_{2}\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)udx+r_{2}\left(\overline{m}-cr_{1}-r_{2}\right)\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)^{2}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)udx-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)udx\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)udx

\displaystyle+r_{2}\left(\overline{m}-cr_{1}+r_{2}\right)\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)^{2}+2(b+c)r_{2}\left(cr_{1}+r_{2}-\overline{m}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)udx.

Therefore, applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the second term and the fourth term in the right-hand side yields

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)udx\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)\sqrt{u}\sqrt{u}dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r_{1}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(d(x)-r_{1}-br_{2}\right)^{2}udx.

By the boundedness of

, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d}{dt}(I_{1}+I_{2})\Big|\leq C(I_{1}+I_{2}).

Again, the use of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality results in

\displaystyle=\overline{r}_{1},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}r_{2}(t)

Step 4
(Identifying

and (

(67)

and

, which is again a contradiction. Collecting both cases we conclude that as

(Identification of the limits for

Theorem 5

Let

The function

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h(0)=0,~~h(\overline{r}_{1})=\overline{r}_{2},\\

(85)

(86)

This is a standard parabolic system with zero as the principle eigenvalue of the steady equations. We know from the general theory of dominant eigenvectors of positive operators (Krein-Rutman theorem and relative entropy
[

Furthermore, multiplying the first equation of (

General Actions of Extended Objects and Volume-Preserving Diffeomorphism
Pei-Ming Ho a,b , Hikaru Kawai a,b,c , and Henry Liao a a Department of Physics and Center for Theoretical Physics, National Taiwan University, Taipei 106, Taiwan b Physics Division, National Center for Theoretical Sciences, Taipei 10617, Taiwan c Nambu Yoichiro Institute of Theoretical and Experimental Physics (NITEP), Osaka Metropolitan University, Osaka 558-8585, Japan

Abstract
We consider actions that are general functions of the worldsheet/worldvolume metric and the induced metric for extended objects embedded in spacetime as Riemannian manifolds, areal-metric manifolds, and volume-metric manifolds. For strings on a Riemannian spacetime, we consider general actions respecting volume-preserving diffeomorphisms (VPD), general diffeomorphisms, and diffeomorphisms with Weyl symmetry, respectively. Well-known Schild, Nambu-Goto, and Polyakov actions are included as special cases. We reach two main conclusions: (1) When actions are functions of both the worldsheet metric and induced metrics, all nontrivial self-consistent actions are classically equivalent. (2) As a physical constraint on the classical action, VPD symmetry is as strong as the full diffeomorphism symmetry. The discussion is then extended to strings in spacetime manifolds equipped with the areal or volume metrics. Then, we further consider higher-dimensional extended objects in spacetime defined with areal or volume metrics, and show the equivalence between the generalized Schild actions and the generalized Nambu-Goto action. We prove a general theorem on VPD that explains this equivalence. Incidentally, while only the areal metric is needed to define the string worldsheet action, we show that the Polyakov action with an areal-metric perturbation cannot describe critical strings without other interaction terms.

Introduction

At the classical level, the worldsheet theory of a bosonic string can be equivalently described by the Nambu-Goto (NG) action, the Schild (S) action, and the Polyakov (P) action as (with the overall coefficients omitted)

\displaystyle S_{NG}

\displaystyle\equiv\int d^{2}\xi\left(\sqrt{\gamma}\right),

(1.1)

\displaystyle S_{S}

\displaystyle\equiv\int d^{2}\xi\left(\frac{1}{4}\epsilon^{ac}\epsilon^{bd}\gamma_{ab}\gamma_{cd}\right)=\int d^{2}\xi\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma\right),

(1.2)

\displaystyle S_{P}

\displaystyle\equiv\int d^{2}\xi\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{h}h^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\right),

(1.3)

where the
\xi^{a}
(
a,b,c,d=0,1
) are the worldsheet coordinates, and
\epsilon^{ab}
is the two-dimensional Levi-Civita symbol.
The induced metric
\gamma_{ab}
is defined by

\displaystyle\gamma_{ab}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}(X)\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu},

(1.4)

where
g_{\mu\nu}(X)
is a generic spacetime metric, whose (absolute) determinant is denoted by
\gamma
, and
h_{ab}
is the worldsheet metric with
h
being (the absolute value of) its determinant.

The Nambu-Goto action (
1.1
) is invariant under the worldsheet diffeomorphisms.
The Schild action is invariant only under the worldsheet volume-preserving diffeomorphisms.
The Polyakov action enjoys both the full worldsheet diffeomorphism and the Weyl symmetry that scales the worldsheet metric
h_{ab}
.
The conformal gauge allows us to define the quantum theory of strings.
The classical equivalence of all three actions can be easily shown
[
1
,
2
]
.
This raises a natural question:
Are there other actions classically equivalent to these three?

In this paper, we study the most general forms of the string worldsheet action for each choice of the worldsheet gauge symmetry mentioned above.
We discuss conditions under which these actions are classically equivalent.

The above actions are related to the area of the string worldsheet induced from the background (pseudo-)Riemannian metric.
It is natural to generalize the notion of a (pseudo-)Riemannian metric to that of an areal metric.
We shall also study general forms of the string worldsheet actions with this extension and examine their equivalence.

Let us briefly recall the notion of an areal metric.
As the relativistic action of a point particle is the mass times the worldline length, the Nambu-Goto action of a string is the tension times the worldsheet area.
On a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold, we define length using a metric
g_{\mu\nu}
via the line element

\displaystyle ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dX^{\mu}\otimes_{sym}dX^{\nu},

(1.5)

where
dX^{\mu}
is a one-form on the manifold and
\otimes_{sym}
is the symmetrized tensor product.
The area can then be defined as a notion induced from that of the length.

Mathematically, one can define the areal element
dA
directly as an inner product on the space of two-forms
[

\displaystyle dA^{2}=G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\left(dX^{\mu}\wedge dX^{\nu}\right)\otimes_{sym}\left(dX^{\rho}\wedge dX^{\lambda}\right),

(1.6)

where
G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
is called the areal metric.
An areal metric may or may not induce the notion of length
[

]

However, one can define the string worldsheet area even in the absence of the notion of length.

In this work, we also take the first step toward the quantum theory of strings in an areal metric background.
We consider the ordinary Polyakov action perturbed by an areal-metric deformation.
Unfortunately, we find that such perturbations do not define critical strings.

Beyond discussion of strings, we shall also consider generic extended objects with
d
-dimensional worldvolume as generalizations of strings, in a background geometry defined with a volume metric, as a generalization of the areal metric.

In the discussions on the classical equivalence among different actions, we notice that VPD plays a special role.
We found an interesting general theorem that is very useful in these discussions.

The plan of this paper is as follows.

Worldsheet Actions on Riemannian Manifolds

In this section, we aim to find the most general string worldsheet actions that depend only on the induced metric
\gamma_{ab}
and the worldsheet metric
h_{ab}
.
That is, we assume that the worldsheet fields
X^{\mu}
only enter the action through
\gamma_{ab}
(
1.4
); the only other field that can appear in the action is the worldsheet metric
h_{ab}
, which plays the role of an auxiliary field.

1

Fermionic fields are ignored in this work.

This assumption already covers all three types of string actions (
1.1
) – (
1.3
).

In Sec.
2.1
, we first discuss the basic idea to construct the general form of Lagrangians which are functions of
\gamma_{ab}
and
h_{ab}
without their derivatives.
Then, we consider worldsheet Lagrangians that respect different types of symmetries: (1) Volume-Preserving Diffeomorphisms (VPD), (2) Diffeomorphism (Diff), and (3) Diffeomorphism with Weyl symmetry (Diff-Weyl).

After integrating out the auxiliary worldsheet metric
h_{ab}
, we find that the latter two classes of Lagrangians reduce to the Nambu-Goto Lagrangian, while Lagrangian with the VPD symmetry reduces to Lagrangians of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{GS}=F(\sqrt{\gamma}),

(2.1)

where
F
is an arbitrary function, and we call it the generalized Schild Lagrangian.
The classical equivalence between the generalized Schild actions and the Nambu-Goto action will be shown later in Sec.
3
.

2.1
General Worldsheet Actions

Denoting the matrix form of
\gamma_{ab}
as
\Gamma
and the matrix form of
h_{ab}
as
H
, invariance under VPD demands that the worldsheet Lagrangian is a function of the following invariants
\gamma=\det\Gamma
,
h=\det H
,
a_{n}=\text{tr}\left(\left(\Gamma H^{-1}\right)^{n}\right)
(
n=\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots
).
Assuming that the Lagrangian does not contain derivatives of
\gamma_{ab}
and
h_{ab}
, a Lagrangian invariant under the VPD symmetry is in general of the form
f(\gamma,h,a_{1},\dots)
.

For invariants constructed from
\Gamma H^{-1}
, since
\Gamma H^{-1}
is a
2\times 2
matrix, it is characterized by two independent invariants (e.g. trace and determinant).
All other invariants
a_{n}
can be identified with functions of them.
For example, take

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\left(\Gamma H^{-1}\right)=\gamma_{ab}h^{ba},

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\left(\left(\Gamma H^{-1}\right)^{2}\right)=\gamma_{ab}h^{bc}\gamma_{cd}h^{da}

(2.3)

as the only independent variables for all invariants constructed from
\Gamma H^{-1}
.
We have

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},

(2.4)

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1})^{2}+(\lambda_{2})^{2},

(2.5)

where
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the two eigenvalues of
\Gamma H^{-1}
.
Then,
a_{-1}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}
can be written in terms of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
as
\frac{2a_{1}}{a_{2}-(a_{1})^{2}}
.

Once we have the characteristics of
\Gamma H^{-1}
, the determinants
\gamma
and
h
are no longer independent.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\gamma h^{-1}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=\frac{{a_{1}}^{2}-a_{2}}{2}.

(2.6)

Therefore, any VPD-invariant Lagrangian can be written as a function of three variables:
\gamma
,
a_{1}
, and
a_{2}
.

The Lagrangians with VPD, Diff, and Diff-Weyl symmetries are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny VPD}}}

\displaystyle=f\left(\sqrt{\gamma},a_{1},a_{2}\right),

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma}f\left(a_{1},a_{2}\right),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff-Weyl}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma}\ f\left(\frac{a_{2}}{{a_{1}}^{2}}\right),

(2.9)

where the functions
f
are generic functions of their arguments.
For example, the Schild action (
1.2
) is a special case of
\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny VPD}}}
, the Nambu-Goto action (
1.1
) is a special case of
\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff}}}
, and the Polyakov action (
1.3
) is a special case of
\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff-Weyl}}}
.
The generalized Polyakov action defined by
[
9
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{GP}=F\left(\sqrt{h}\frac{1}{2}h^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\right)=F\left(\sqrt{h}\frac{a}{2}\right)

(2.10)

is a subclass of the Diff-Weyl-invariant actions (
2.9
).

In this and the next sections, we will prove the classical equivalence of these Lagrangians with the Nambu-Goto theory.
The strategy is to derive the equations of motion for the auxiliary field
h_{ab}
in terms of
\gamma_{ab}
and then substitute its solution back into the Lagrangian.

Using the definitions of
a_{1}
(
2.2
) and
a_{2}
(
2.3
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial a_{1}}{\partial h^{ij}}=\gamma_{ji},\qquad\frac{\partial a_{2}}{\partial h^{ij}}=2\gamma_{ja}h^{ab}\gamma_{bi}.

(2.11)

For any Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
among (
2.7
) – (
2.9
), the equation of motion for
h^{ij}
is

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{ij}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{1}}\frac{\partial a_{1}}{\partial h^{ij}}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{2}}\frac{\partial a_{2}}{\partial h^{ij}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{1}}\gamma_{ji}+2\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{2}}\gamma_{ja}h^{ab}\gamma_{bi}=0.

(2.12)

Multiplying both sides by
h_{kc}\gamma^{cj}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{1}}h_{ki}+2\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{2}}\gamma_{ki}=0.

(2.13)

After dividing
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial a_{1}
on both sides, we find that the worldsheet metric
h_{ab}
and the induced metric
\gamma_{ab}
differ only by an overall factor
\phi
:

\displaystyle h_{ab}=\phi\gamma_{ab}

(2.14)

with the proportionality factor given by

\displaystyle\phi=\left.-2\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial a_{1}}\right)^{-1}\right|_{a_{1}=2\phi^{-1},a_{2}=2\phi^{-2}}.

(2.15)

Here, the values of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are determined in terms of
\phi
from eqs. (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.14
) as

\displaystyle a_{1}=2\phi^{-1},\qquad a_{2}=2\phi^{-2}.

(2.16)

Thus,
\phi
can be, in principle, solved from eq. (
2.15
), and
h_{ab}
is then determined by eq. (
2.14
) in terms of
\gamma_{ab}
.
In the following, we proceed to discuss each case in (
2.7
) – (
2.9
) with the assumption that they can be solved with
\phi\neq 0
.
In Sec.
2.2
, we consider two exceptional cases where eq. (
2.15
) has no solution for
\phi
, as well as an example where a solution of
\phi
exists.

2.1.1
VPD-Invariant Actions

In the case of a VPD-invariant Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny VPD}}}
(
2.7
), eq. (
2.15
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\phi=-2\left.\frac{\partial f\left(\sqrt{\gamma},a_{1},a_{2}\right)}{\partial a_{2}}\left(\frac{\partial f\left(\sqrt{\gamma},a_{1},a_{2}\right)}{\partial a_{1}}\right)^{-1}\right|_{a_{1}=2\phi^{-1},\ a_{2}=2\phi^{-2}}

(2.17)

which may determine
\phi
as a function of
\sqrt{\gamma}
.
Substituting it back into eq. (
2.7

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny VPD}}}=f\left(\sqrt{\gamma},2\phi^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\gamma}\right),2\phi^{-2}\left(\sqrt{\gamma}\right)\right),

which is in the form of the generalized Schild Lagrangian

Therefore, generic VPD-invariant Lagrangians are equivalent to generalized Schild Lagrangians.

2.1.2
Diffeomorphism-Invariant Actions

As for the diffeomorphism-invariant Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff}}}
(
2.8
), eq. (
2.15
) states that

\displaystyle\phi=-2\left.\frac{\partial f\left(a_{1},a_{2}\right)}{\partial a_{2}}\left(\frac{\partial f\left(a_{1},a_{2}\right)}{\partial a_{1}}\right)^{-1}\right|_{a_{1}=2\phi^{-1},\ a_{2}=2\phi^{-2}}.

(2.19)

Unlike eq. (

to a constant in the absence of

.
The resulting Lagrangian is therefore just the Nambu-Goto Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{NG}=c\sqrt{\gamma}

(2.20)

with the constant

and
\phi
given by the constant solution of eq. (

).

2.1.3
Diffeomorphism-Weyl-Invariant Actions

For Lagrangians
\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff-Weyl}}}
(
2.9
) invariant under Diff-Weyl, eq. (
2.15
) is trivially satisfied as follows

\displaystyle\phi=\left.-2\frac{\partial f\left(\frac{a_{2}}{{a_{1}}^{2}}\right)}{\partial a_{2}}\left(\frac{\partial f\left(\frac{a_{2}}{{a_{1}}^{2}}\right)}{\partial a_{1}}\right)^{-1}\right|_{a_{1}=2\phi^{-1},\ a_{2}=2\phi^{-2}}=\left.\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\right|_{a_{1}=2\phi^{-1},\ a_{2}=2\phi^{-2}}=\phi.

(2.21)

That is,
\phi

This situation is the same as the Polyakov action, where the equation of motion for

only demands conformal equivalence between

and
\gamma_{ab}
without fixing the proportionality factor
\phi
.

Although
\phi
remains unfixed, the argument of the function

in the Lagrangian is
\frac{a_{2}}{(a_{1})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
(after substituting

Hence, the resulting Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff-Weyl}}}=f\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\sqrt{\gamma},

(2.22)

which is again the Nambu-Goto Lagrangian.
Hence, both Diff-invariant Lagrangians
\mathcal{L}_{{\mbox{\tiny Diff}}}
and Diff-Weyl-invariant Lagrangians

2.2.1
Polyakov
+
Cosmological Constant term

As the first example, we add a cosmological constant term

to the Polyakov Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{h}h^{ab}\gamma_{ab}+\lambda\sqrt{h}.

Using eqs. (

This equation is equivalent to

2.2.2
Polyakov
+

\text{(Polyakov)}^{2}

Next, we add a term quadratic in the Polyakov density:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{h}h^{ab}\gamma_{ab}+\frac{\mu}{4}\sqrt{h}\left(h^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\right)^{2}.

This Lagrangian can be rewritten in the form of eq. (

\displaystyle f(a_{1},a_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{a_{1}}^{2}-a_{2}}}\left(a_{1}+\frac{\mu}{2}{a_{1}}^{2}\right).

Equation (

+

\text{(Polyakov)}^{2}

3
Equivalences of (Generalized) Schild and Nambu–Goto Actions

In Sec.
2
, we classified general worldsheet actions built from the worldsheet metric
h_{ab}
and the induced metric
\gamma_{ab}
, leading to the three symmetry classes (
2.7
) – (
2.9
), which are, respectively, invariant under the symmetries of VPD, Diff, and Diff-Weyl.
In Secs.
2.1.2
and
2.1.3
, we have shown that diffeomorphism is sufficient to imply the equivalence with the Nambu-Goto action, regardless of whether there is the Weyl symmetry.
But actions with VPD symmetry are only shown to be equivalent to the generalized Schild action in Sec.
2.1.1
.
In this section, we take the next step and show that the generalized Schild action is equivalent to the Nambu-Goto action, so that VPD-invariant actions are also equivalent to the Nambu-Goto action.
That is, VPD invariance already suffices to ensure equivalence with the Nambu-Goto action.

In Sec.
3.1
, we establish the classical equivalence between the Schild action and the Nambu-Goto action for an arbitrary spacetime metric
g_{\mu\nu}
.
We also determine the overall coefficient of the Schild action by demanding its stress-energy tensor to match the string tension specified by the Nambu-Goto action.

In Sec.
3.2
, we show that the generalized Schild action (
2.1
) is equivalent to the Schild action, and thus it is also equivalent to the Nambu-Goto action.

3.1
Equivalence of Schild and Nambu-Goto Actions

We now prove the classical equivalence between the Schild and the Nambu-Goto theories with an arbitrary spacetime metric
g_{\mu\nu}
.
This is achieved by showing that their equations of motion are equivalent when we take a special gauge (
\sqrt{\gamma}=
const.) for the Nambu-Goto theory.
Then, we match their stress-energy tensors to see how the tension is encoded in the Schild action.

Classical Equivalence

We start from the Schild and the Nambu-Goto Lagrangians:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{S}

\displaystyle=a\gamma,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{NG}

\displaystyle=T\sqrt{\gamma},

(3.2)

where
a
is a real constant,
T
is the string tension and
\gamma
is (the absolute value of) the determinant of
\gamma_{ab}
defined in (
1.4
).
Their equations of motion with respect to
X^{\mu}
are

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}\left(X^{\nu}\partial_{b}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\right),

(3.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}X^{\nu}\partial_{b}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\right),

(3.4)

respectively, where

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}.

(3.5)

Here we have used

\displaystyle\gamma=g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\sigma}

(3.6)

and defined

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\lambda}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}.

(3.7)

We shall denote
\gamma=\sigma^{2}
, as a shorthand for eq. (
3.6
).

These two equations of motion appear to be different since
\partial_{a}
in eq. (
3.4
) acts on
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}
.
However, in the Nambu-Goto theory, since
\sqrt{\gamma}
is a scalar density on the worldsheet, we can choose
\sqrt{\gamma}
to be a worldsheet constant as a gauge-fixing condition for the diffeomorphism symmetry.
The residual gauge symmetry is VPD.

Hence, the equation of motion (
3.4
) of the Nambu-Goto theory together with the gauge-fixing condition
\sqrt{\gamma}=\mathrm{const.}
implies the equation of motion (
3.3
) of the Schild theory.
To prove that the Schild theory is equivalent to the Nambu-Goto theory, we show that the gauge-fixing condition is implied by its equation of motion (
3.3
).

We multiply
\partial_{c}X^{\mu}
to eq. (
3.3
) and get

\begin{split}0&=\partial_{c}X^{\mu}\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}\left(X^{\nu}\partial_{b}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\epsilon^{ab}\epsilon_{ca}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{b}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\\
&=-\frac{1}{8}\partial_{c}\gamma.\end{split}

(3.8)

In the second equality, we have used the fact that the indices
\mu,\nu
in
\partial_{c}X^{\mu}\partial_{a}X^{\nu}
is (totally) anti-symmetrized due to the anti-symmetrized tensor
\sigma_{\mu\nu}
, together with the identity

\displaystyle\partial_{c}X^{\mu}\partial_{a}X^{\nu}-\partial_{c}X^{\nu}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ca}\sigma^{\mu\nu}.

(3.9)

In the Schild theory, the relation
\sqrt{\gamma}=\mathrm{const.}
is not a gauge-fixing condition, but rather it is dynamically fixed (by the equation of motion) everywhere on the same connected worldsheet once its initial condition is specified somewhere on the same worldsheet.
Thus, on any connected worldsheet, there is no physical difference between the Schild theory and the Nambu-Goto theory.

String Tension in Schild Action

Next, we discuss how to define tension from the Schild action by matching the stress-energy tensors of the Schild action and the Nambu-Goto action.
To do so, we rewrite the actions so that they explicitly depend on

\displaystyle S_{Schild}

\displaystyle=a\int d^{2}\xi\left(g^{\mu\rho}(x)g^{\nu\sigma}(x)\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\rho\sigma}\right)

(3.10)

\displaystyle S_{NG}

\displaystyle=T\int d^{2}\xi\ \sqrt{g^{\mu\rho}(x)g^{\nu\sigma}(x)\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\rho\sigma}}.

(3.11)

Their stress-energy tensors are

\begin{split}T^{(Schild)}_{\alpha\beta}&=\frac{\delta S_{Schild}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}(y)}\\
&=2a\int d^{2}\xi\left(\delta^{(D)}\left(x-y\right)g^{\nu\sigma}\sigma_{\alpha\nu}\sigma_{\beta\sigma}\right)\\
T^{(NG)}_{\alpha\beta}&=\frac{\delta S_{NG}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}(y)}\\
&=T\int d^{2}\xi\left(\frac{\delta^{(D)}\left(x-y\right)g^{\nu\sigma}\sigma_{\alpha\nu}\sigma_{\beta\sigma}}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}}}\right)\end{split}

(3.12)

where
\delta^{(D)}(x-y)
is the delta function in
D

Equating the integrands in (
3.12

\displaystyle T=2a{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}}}.

(3.13)

Since both
a
and
T
are constants, this relation is only meaningful when
\sigma^{2}
is constant, which is true on each connected worldsheet in the Schild theory as shown in eq. (
3.8
).
Then, the tension is given by the Schild parameter
a
and the constant worldsheet measure
\sqrt{\sigma^{2}}
through eq. (
3.13
).

3.2
Equivalence between Generalized Schild and Nambu-Goto Actions

We have demonstrated in Sec.
3.1
that the Schild action is equivalent to the Nambu-Goto action.
Now we show that the generalized Schild action (
2.1

Starting from the generalized Schild action (
2.1

\begin{split}0&=\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\partial_{a}\left(F^{\prime}\left(\sigma^{2}\right)\sigma_{\mu\nu}\right)\\

(3.14)

Multiplying it by
\partial_{c}X^{\mu}
and using the identity (

\displaystyle\partial_{c}\sigma^{2}\left(F^{\prime}\left(\sigma^{2}\right)+2\sigma^{2}F^{\prime\prime}\left(\sigma^{2}\right)\right)=0.

(3.15)

For generic
F

F^{\prime}\left(\sigma^{2}\right)+2\sigma^{2}F^{\prime\prime}\left(\sigma^{2}\right)
does not vanish identically.
The equation above implies that

\displaystyle\partial_{c}\sigma^{2}=0

(3.16)

identically on the worldsheet.
(See Appendix
A
for a more detailed proof.)
Hence,
\sigma^{2}
is a worldsheet constant.
For non-zero
F^{\prime}(\sigma^{2})

in eq. (
3.14

It is then straightforward to see that the equation of motion (
3.14
) of the generalized Schild action matches the equation of motion (
3.3
) for the Schild action.

Since we have shown that the Schild theory is equivalent to the Nambu-Goto theory in Sec.
3.1
, the generalized Schild action (
2.1
) is also equivalent to the Nambu-Goto action.

2

2

There are cases where the term

vanishes identically. However, this is only possible if

Together with the result in Sec.

4
The Nambu-Goto and Schild Actions on Areal-Metric Manifolds

In previous sections, we established the classical equivalence among the Schild, the generalized Schild, and the Nambu-Goto actions on Riemannian target manifolds.
In this section, we extend this analysis to target
manifolds equipped with an areal metric
G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
.

Note that as we will show in Sec.
5
, any nontrivial dependence on the auxiliary areal worldsheet degrees of freedom is forbidden by Weyl symmetry.
The only possible Polyakov-like action (those with both diffeomorphisms and Weyl symmetry) is the one discussed in Sec.
6
.
Hence, we only include the Nambu-Goto and Schild actions in this section.

An areal metric, as a generalization of the ordinary metric, is defined as eq. (
1.6
):

\displaystyle dA^{2}=G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}da^{\mu\nu}da^{\rho\lambda}

(4.1)

where
dA
is the infinitesimal area element,
G_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the areal metric, and
da^{\mu\nu}
is a 2-form on the manifold.

From its definition, we see that
G_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
must have the following symmetries:

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}=-G_{\nu\mu\rho\lambda}=-G_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=G_{\rho\lambda\mu\nu}.

(4.2)

In a
D
-dimensional spacetime, it has
\frac{D\left(D-1\right)\left(D^{2}-D+2\right)}{8}
degrees of freedom.
By comparison, an ordinary metric has
\frac{D(D+1)}{2}
degrees of freedom.
An areal metric contains more degrees of freedom for
D>3
.
It is also conventional to impose the cyclicity condition

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}+G_{\mu\rho\lambda\nu}+G_{\mu\lambda\nu\rho}=0

(4.3)

so that it is an irreducible representation of the local frame group
SL(D,\mathbb{R})

[
3
]
.
3
3

3

That is, the areal metric
G_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}
cannot be used to define a nontrivial product between 1-forms and 3-forms, or a function on the space of 4-forms.

This condition reduces the number of free parameters in an areal metric to
\frac{D^{2}(D^{2}-1)}{12}
in
D
dimensions.
It still has more degrees of freedom than an ordinary metric for
D>3
.

Since this class of objects has more degrees of freedom than ordinary metrics when
D>3
, an areal-metric in target space (
D\geq 4
) in general cannot be captured by a Riemannian metric.
In the following, we demonstrate how the Nambu-Goto and Schild actions can be generalized to those defined for areal-metric backgrounds; we also discuss a Polyakov-like auxiliary-field formulation.
We will also prove their classical equivalence.

In Sec.
4.1
, we introduce the areal-metric analogue of the Nambu-Goto action and derive its equation of motion.
On areal-metric manifolds, we consider the Schild action with VPD symmetry in Sec.
4.2
and a diffeomorphism-invariant action with an auxiliary worldsheet field as the counterpart of the Polyakov action in Sec.
4.3
.
We will prove their equivalence to the Nambu-Goto action on areal-metric manifolds.

In Sec.
5
, we will discuss more general actions on areal metric manifolds.

4.1
Nambu-Goto action on Areal-Metric Manifold

Due to the geometrical meaning of the Nambu-Goto action, it is straightforward to define the analogue of the Nambu-Goto action for an areal-metric background to be given by the worldsheet area as

\displaystyle S^{A}_{NG}=\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}},

(4.4)

where
G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
is the areal metric,
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}
(
3.5
), and the Greek (Roman) letters are spacetime (worldsheet) indices.
This action reduces to the usual Nambu-Goto action on Riemannian spacetime when the areal metric agrees with

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}=g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\lambda}-g_{\mu\lambda}g_{\nu\rho}

(4.5)

for a given Riemannian metric
g_{\mu\nu}
.
We will refer to (
4.4
) as the Nambu-Goto action for the areal metric
G
.

4.2
The Schild Action on Areal-Metric Manifolds

Since the Schild Lagrangian (
1.2
) can be obtained by squaring the Nambu-Goto Lagrangian (
1.1
), we define the areal-metric generalization of the Schild action as

\displaystyle S^{A}_{S}=\int d^{2}\xi\,\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}.

(4.6)

It respects the VPD symmetry.
We now show that
S^{A}_{S}
is classically equivalent to the areal-metric Nambu-Goto action
S^{A}_{NG}
in (
4.4
), for an arbitrary background areal metric
G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
.
The proof follows essentially the same line as that for Riemannian manifolds.

The equation of motion for
X^{\alpha}
obtained from
S^{A}_{S}
(
4.6
) is

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\alpha}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}-\partial_{a}\left(G_{\alpha\nu\rho\lambda}\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}\right).

(4.7)

After multiplying
\partial_{c}X^{\alpha}
on both sides of eq. (
4.7
) and using eq. (
3.9
),
we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\partial_{c}\left(\sigma_{A}^{2}\right),

(4.8)

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}.

(4.9)

we get from (
4.8
) that

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}^{2}=\mbox{constant}.

(4.10)

This is in complete analogy with the original Schild action (
1.2
) whose equation of motion implies that
\sigma^{2}=g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}

For the case of
S^{A}_{NG}
(
4.4

X^{\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\sigma_{A}^{2}}}\partial_{\alpha}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}-\partial_{a}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_{A}^{2}}}G_{\alpha\nu\rho\lambda}\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}\right).

(4.11)

Since
\sqrt{\sigma_{A}^{2}}
is a scalar density on the worldsheet, we can fix it to a constant via part of the diffeomorphisms, reducing the symmetry to VPD.
This allows us to factor out
1/\sqrt{\sigma_{A}^{2}}
from
\partial_{a}
in the second term.
After multiplying eq. (
4.11

\sqrt{\sigma_{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}-\partial_{a}\left(G_{\alpha\nu\rho\lambda}\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}\right)

(4.12)

which is the same as eq. (
4.7
), the equation of motion for
X^{\mu}
from the areal Schild action
S^{A}_{S}
.
Thus, we have proven the classical equivalence for any areal metric between the Schild action and the Nambu-Goto action.

4.3
Action with Auxiliary Field on Areal-Metric Manifolds

We intend to construct an action analogous to the Polyakov action, which is an action involving not only the induced metric
\gamma_{ab}
but also an auxiliary worldsheet metric
h_{ab}
.
While the spacetime metric is now promoted from
g_{\mu\nu}
to
G_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}

should also be promoted to an areal metric
\mathcal{H}_{abcd}

However, in 2 dimensions,
\mathcal{H}_{abcd}
has only one independent component
\mathcal{H}_{0101}
, which we simply denote as
\mathcal{H}
.
Using this auxiliary field
\mathcal{H}
, we can remove the square root in (
4.4
) with the full diffeomorphism preserved.
It turns out that it is impossible to enhance the diffeomorphism to have Weyl symmetry or scale symmetry.

Analogous to the einbein action for particles

\displaystyle S_{e}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\left(e^{-1}\dot{X}^{2}+em^{2}\right),

(4.13)

we consider

\displaystyle S^{A}_{e}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}\xi\,\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}\left[\mathcal{H}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}\right)+T^{2}\right].

(4.14)

Here, we regard
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}
as the analogue of the einbein
e

\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}
as that of
\dot{X}^{2}
and the tension

as that of the mass
m

Since
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}
transforms as a scalar density under worldsheet diffeomorphism, this action is invariant under worldsheet diffeomorphism.

To demonstrate its equivalence to the Nambu-Goto action (
4.4
) for the areal metric
G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
, we integrate out the worldsheet areal metric
\mathcal{H}
.
By varying the action (
4.14
) with respect to
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}
, we get the equation of motion for
\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle 0=\frac{-1}{{\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}\right)}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}\right)+T^{2}

(4.15)

which is solved by

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{T}\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}}.

(4.16)

Substituting this back into eq. (
4.14

\displaystyle S^{A}_{e}=T\int d^{2}\xi\,\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}},

(4.17)

4.4
) with tension
T

General Worldsheet Actions on Areal-Metric Manifolds

In this section, we extend the discussion in Secs.
2

We show that, in the areal-metric case, the most general VPD-invariant actions and diffeomorphism-invariant actions are both classically equivalent to the areal Nambu-Goto action (

Let

The induced areal metric on the worldsheet is

Since

Therefore, the most general VPD-invariant actions and diffeomorphism-invariant actions built from an areal metric are, respectively,

\displaystyle S^{A}_{\text{VPD}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\xi\,F(\mathcal{H},\sigma_{A}^{2}),

(5.2)

\displaystyle S^{A}_{\text{Diff}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\xi\,\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}F\left(\frac{\sigma_{A}^{2}}{\mathcal{H}}\right),

(5.3)

where

is an arbitrary function.

In analogy to the ordinary case of Weyl transformation

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{abcd}\rightarrow e^{2\varphi}\mathcal{H}_{abcd},

(5.4)

\mathcal{H}\rightarrow e^{2\varphi}\mathcal{H}

The factor

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{abcd}=h_{ac}h_{bd}-h_{ad}h_{bc}.

(5.5)

Under this Weyl scaling,
\sigma_{A}^{2}
is invariant.
Weyl symmetry implies that the Lagrangian is independent of
\mathcal{H}
while the Weyl symmetry acts trivially on
\sigma_{A}^{2}
in the action.

For

5.2
VPD-Invariant Areal Actions

We next study the VPD-invariant action

\displaystyle S^{A}_{GS}\equiv\int d^{2}\xi\,f(\sigma_{A}^{2})

6
Areal-Metric Deformation of Background Metric

The Polyakov action on a Riemannian background describes a critical string, but it is not clear whether this statement holds on an areal-metric manifold.

To address this question, we consider a small areal-metric deformation of an ordinary metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, parametrized by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}=g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\lambda}-g_{\mu\lambda}g_{\nu\rho}+a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda},

(6.1)

where
a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
is treated as a perturbation.
The following action, proposed in ref.
[
9
]
, generalizes the Polyakov action:

S_{GP}=\int d^{2}\xi\ \mathcal{L}_{GP},\ \ \ \ \ \ \mathcal{L}_{GP}\equiv\sqrt{\left(\frac{\sqrt{-h}}{2}h^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho\lambda}},

(6.2)

where
h_{ab}
and
\gamma_{ab}
are the worldsheet and induced metrics defined in Sec.
1
.
The action (
6.2
) is invariant under two-dimensional diffeomorphisms and Weyl scaling
h_{ab}\rightarrow e^{\varphi}h_{ab}
.

6.1
Classical Equivalence to Areal Nambu-Goto

We first show that the action (
6.2
) is classically equivalent to the areal Nambu-Goto action (
4.4
).
Varying
S_{GP}
with respect to
h_{ab}
gives

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{GP}}\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-h}h^{cd}\gamma_{cd}h_{ab}+\sqrt{-h}\gamma_{ab}\right).

(6.3)

This is the same relation as in the ordinary Polyakov theory, and implies

\displaystyle\frac{h_{ab}}{\sqrt{-h}}=\frac{\gamma_{ab}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}.

(6.4)

Substituting this solution back to eq. (
6.2
) reproduces the areal Nambu-Goto action (
4.4
).
Thus, at the classical level, the generalized Polyakov action (
6.2
) and the areal Nambu-Goto theory are equivalent.

6.2
Quantum Consistency and Primary Operator Condition

We now investigate the quantum consistency.
In the conformal gauge
h_{ab}=\eta_{ab}
, the leading order of eq. (
6.2
) reduces to the ordinary Polyakov action (
1.3
) of the bosonic string,

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}\xi\,\eta^{ab}g_{\mu\nu}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu},

(6.5)

which defines a conformal field theory.
A deformation of the action preserves the conformal symmetry at the quantum level if the corresponding operator is a primary field of weight
\left(1,1\right)
.

As pointed out in ref.
[
9
]
, when
a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
is small, the action (
6.2
) can be treated as a perturbation of eq. (
6.5
) by a fractional term

\displaystyle\frac{a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\epsilon^{ab}\epsilon^{cd}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\partial_{c}X^{\rho}\partial_{d}X^{\lambda}}{g_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{a}X^{\alpha}\partial_{b}X^{\beta}}

(6.6)

at the leading order of
a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}
.

We now test whether this operator is a
(1,1)
-primary operator on the flat background

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}.

(6.7)

Using complex coordinates
\left(z,\bar{z}\right)
on the worldsheet, the operator (
6.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\equiv\,:\mathrel{\frac{1}{\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{z}X^{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{z}}X^{\beta}}a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}\partial_{z}X^{\mu}\partial_{\bar{z}}X^{\nu}\partial_{z}X^{\rho}\partial_{\bar{z}}X^{\lambda}}:.

(6.8)

In general, consider a normal-ordered operator of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=\ :\mathrel{F\left(\partial_{z}X^{\mu}\right)}:

(6.9)

and the holomorphic energy momentum tensor
T(z)
of the free boson theory.
Their OPE takes the form

\displaystyle T(z)\mathcal{O}(w)=\frac{-1}{2}\frac{1}{\left(z-w\right)^{4}}:\mathrel{\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\left(\partial_{w}X^{\mu}\right)\partial\left(\partial_{w}X_{\mu}\right)}}:-\frac{-1}{\left(z-w\right)^{2}}:\mathrel{\partial_{w}X^{\mu}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\left(\partial_{w}X^{\mu}\right)}}:+\dots

where
F=F\left(\partial_{w}X^{\lambda}\right)
, and the ellipsis are terms that are regular when
z\rightarrow w
.
For
\mathcal{O}
to be a primary with conformal dimension
\Delta
, we must have

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\left(\partial_{z}X_{\mu}\right)\partial\left(\partial_{z}X_{\nu}\right)}=0,

(6.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}X^{\mu}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\left(\partial_{z}X^{\mu}\right)}=\Delta F.

(6.11)

In our case, we write these conditions in terms of

\displaystyle u^{\mu}=\partial_{z}X^{\mu},\ \ \ v^{\mu}=\partial_{\bar{z}}X^{\mu},

(6.12)

and define

\displaystyle F(u,v)\equiv\frac{K_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}}{u\cdot v},

(6.13)

where

\displaystyle K_{\mu\nu}\equiv a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}v^{\rho}v^{\lambda},\ \ u\cdot v\equiv\eta_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}v^{\nu}.

(6.14)

The condition (
6.11
) is easy to check by counting the power of
u^{\mu}
in eq. (
6.13
).
That is, there is one
u^{\mu}
in the denominator and two in the numerator, so
\Delta=1
.

The nontrivial constraint is condition (
6.10
), which is equivalent to

\displaystyle 0=\eta^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial u^{\mu}\partial u^{\nu}}.

(6.15)

A straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle\eta^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial^{2}F(u,v)}{\partial u^{\mu}\partial u^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}}{u\cdot v}-2\frac{K_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}v^{\nu}}{\left(u\cdot v\right)^{2}}+\frac{K_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\eta_{\alpha\beta}v^{\alpha}v^{\beta}}{\left(u\cdot v\right)^{3}}.

(6.16)

Since
a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}=-a_{\nu\mu\rho\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{\mu\nu}v^{\mu}=a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}v^{\mu}v^{\rho}v^{\lambda}=0,

(6.17)

and eq. (
6.16
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\eta^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial^{2}F(u,v)}{\partial u^{\mu}\partial u^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}}{u\cdot v}+\frac{K_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\eta_{\alpha\beta}v^{\alpha}v^{\beta}}{\left(u\cdot v\right)^{3}}.

(6.18)

Therefore, the condition (
6.15
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}\eta^{\mu\nu}v^{\rho}v^{\lambda}\left(u\cdot v\right)^{2}+a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}v^{\rho}v^{\lambda}v^{2}=0.

(6.19)

We now show that the only solution to eq. (
6.19
) is the trivial one
a_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}=0
.

Define a Gaussian average of a function
A
by

\displaystyle\langle A\rangle\equiv\int d^{D}u\left(A\ e^{\frac{-1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}}\right).

(6.20)

Taking the average of eq. (
6.19
) over
u
, all explicit dependence on
u^{\mu}
can be expressed in terms of
\langle u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\rangle\propto\eta^{\mu\nu}
.
The averaged equation becomes

\displaystyle a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}\eta^{\mu\nu}v^{\rho}v^{\lambda}v^{2}+a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}\eta^{\mu\nu}v^{\rho}v^{\lambda}v^{2}=2a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}\eta^{\mu\nu}v^{\rho}v^{\lambda}v^{2}=0.

(6.21)

Therefore,
a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
must vanish.
There is no nontrivial areal-metric deformation (
6.6
) that preserves the conformal symmetry by itself.

We emphasize that this argument only applies in the perturbative regime around the flat background in the absence of other background fields.

7
Higher Dimensional Extended Objects

So far we have focused on string theories, which have two-dimensional worldsheets, and established their classical equivalence on both Riemannian and areal-metric manifolds.
In this section, we extend the analysis to higher-dimensional extended objects and introduce a generalization of an areal metric, namely, a volume metric.

For higher-dimensional extended objects in a Riemannian spacetime, the Nambu-Goto action is naturally generalized to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{NG}=T\sqrt{\gamma},

(7.1)

where
\gamma
is the determinant of the induced metric
\gamma_{ab}
on
d
-dimensional worldvolume.
More explicitly, we have

\begin{split}\gamma&=\frac{1}{d!}\epsilon^{a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{d}}\epsilon^{b_{1}b_{2}\dots b_{d}}\gamma_{a_{1}b_{1}}\gamma_{a_{2}b_{2}}\dots\gamma_{a_{d}b_{d}}\\
&=\frac{1}{d!}g_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}g_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}\dots g_{\mu_{d}\nu_{d}}\sigma^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{d}}\sigma^{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\dots\nu_{d}},\end{split}

(7.2)

where
\gamma_{ab}
is defined by

\displaystyle\gamma_{ab}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}(X)\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\qquad(a,b=0,1,\cdots,d-1),

(7.3)

and

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{d}}

\displaystyle\equiv\epsilon^{a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{d}}\partial_{a_{1}}X^{\mu_{1}}\partial_{a_{2}}X^{\mu_{2}}\dots\partial_{a_{d}}X^{\mu_{d}},

(7.4)

which can be expressed as the
d
-dimensional Nambu bracket
\left\{X^{\mu_{1}},X^{\mu_{2}},\dots,X^{\mu_{d}}\right\}
.
The Lagrangian (
7.1
) can thus be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{NG}=T\sqrt{\left\{X^{\mu_{1}},\dots,X^{\mu_{d}}\right\}^{2}}.

(7.5)

We define the Schild Lagrangian for higher-dimensional objects as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{S}=a{\left\{X^{\mu_{1}},\dots,X^{\mu_{d}}\right\}^{2}}.

(7.6)

We shall consider in this section these actions on Riemannian manifolds as well as their generalizations to manifolds equipped with volume metrics.

Similar to the definition of the areal metric (
1.6
), we define the volume metric through the volume element
d(vol)
as

\displaystyle d(vol)^{2}\equiv V_{\left[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{d}\right]\left[\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\dots\nu_{d}\right]}\left(dX^{\mu_{1}}\wedge dX^{\mu_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge dX^{\mu_{d}}\right)\otimes_{sym}\left(dX^{\nu_{1}}\wedge dX^{\nu_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge dX^{\nu_{d}}\right),

(7.7)

where
V_{\left[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{d}\right]\left[\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\dots\nu_{d}\right]}
is the volume metric.

We will extend our discussion of the worldsheet metric in string actions to higher-dimensional extended objects on Riemannian manifolds in Sec.
7.1
.
In Sec.
7.2
, we define the volume-metric Nambu-Goto action and the volume-metric Schild actions, and discuss how they are equivalent.
In Sec.
7.3
, we prove a theorem about the VPD symmetry, give some examples, and then discuss a common feature of the generalizations of the Nambu-Goto action.

7.1
Worldvolume Metric for Higher Dimensional Extended Objects on Riemannian Manifolds

We now extend the discussion in Sec.
2.1
to
d
-dimensional worldvolumes.
As in Sec.
2.1
, we denote
\Gamma
and
H
to be the matrix form of induced metric
\gamma_{ab}
and worldvolume metric
h_{ab}
, and they are now of size
d\times d
.
The VPD-invariant Lagrangian density has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=f\left(\sqrt{\gamma},a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\right)

(7.8)

where
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
are the invariants built from the matrix
\Gamma H^{-1}
, which are defined as
a_{k}=\text{tr}\left(\left(\Gamma H^{-1}\right)^{k}\right)
with

.

Using the identity

\displaystyle\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial h^{ij}}=k\ \gamma_{ja_{1}}h^{a_{1}b_{2}}\ \gamma_{b_{2}a_{2}}h^{a_{2}b_{3}}\dots\gamma_{b_{k}i}

where

is the inverse of

reads

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial h^{ij}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}k\,f_{k}\,\gamma_{ja_{1}}h^{a_{1}b_{2}}\,\gamma_{b_{2}a_{2}}h^{a_{2}b_{3}}\dots\gamma_{b_{k}i}\equiv 0

(7.10)

where
f_{k}\equiv\partial f/\partial a_{k}

After multiplying

on both sides of the last equality, we get

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{k=1}^{d}k\,f_{k}\,A^{k},

(7.11)

where

.
Since the solution to this equation depends on the exact form of

However, physically speaking, since the induced metric should be full rank for extended objects, the matrix

is invertible generically.
This implies three things.

1.

In the case of

\displaystyle 0=f_{1}\,I+2f_{2}A,

Also, this expression aligns with the result in Sec.

2.

The case of

In such a case, we have more than two terms in the equation, and multiplying

does not help to solve the equation.
Instead, if we assume that the matrix

is diagonalizable, meaning

\displaystyle A=P\Lambda P^{-1},

(7.13)

where
\Lambda
is a diagonal matrix and

is some invertible matrix.
The

eigenvalues

(i=1,\dots,d)
in

), that is,

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{k=1}^{d}k\,f_{k}\left(\sqrt{\gamma},a_{1}\left(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}\right),\dots,a_{d}\left(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}\right)\right)\,\lambda_{i}^{k},

which is a system of

Generically, we have a discrete solution set for the system

When we substitute them into our Lagrangian (

For any

\begin{split}0=&\epsilon^{a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{d}}\partial_{a_{1}}\left\{\left(\partial_{a_{2}}X^{\mu_{2}}\dots\partial_{a_{d}}X^{\mu_{d}}\right)F^{\prime}\left(\gamma_{v}\right)\sigma_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{d}}\right\}\\

\displaystyle\partial_{a_{1}}X^{[\mu_{1}}\partial_{a_{2}}X^{\mu_{2}}\dots\partial_{a_{d}}X^{\mu_{d}]}=\epsilon_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{d}}\sigma^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{d}},

7.3
A Theorem on VPD symmetry

Theorem 7.1
.

For an arbitrary action of the form

\displaystyle S=S[\phi;\rho],

(7.25)

where
\phi
represents any collection of dynamical scalar, vector, or tensor fields, while
\rho
is a background scalar density field,
if the action is invariant under simultaneous diffeomorphism transformations of both
\phi
and
\rho
,
the quantity
\frac{\delta S}{\delta\rho}
is a spacetime constant when
\phi
satisfies the equations of motion.

Proof.

The proof goes as follows.
By definition, the scalar density
\rho
transforms as

\displaystyle\delta_{v}\rho=\partial_{\mu}\left(\rho v^{\mu}\right)

(7.26)

under a diffeomorphism
v^{\mu}
.
On the other hand, the action
S
transforms as

\displaystyle 0=\int d^{D}x\left(\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi\left(x\right)}\delta_{v}\phi\left(x\right)+\frac{\delta S}{\delta\rho\left(x\right)}\partial_{\mu}\left(v^{\mu}\rho\right)\right).

(7.27)

By taking
\phi\left(x\right)
on shell, the first term vanishes.
Via integration by parts, the 2nd term becomes

\displaystyle 0=\int d^{D}x\left(-\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\delta S}{\delta\rho(x)}\right)v^{\mu}\rho\right),

(7.28)

which implies
\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\delta S}{\delta\rho\left(x\right)}\right)=0
, and therefore
\frac{\delta S}{\delta\rho\left(x\right)}
is a spacetime constant.
∎

7.3.1
Example: Unimodular Gravity and Cosmological Constant

It is well known that the cosmological constant in unimodular gravity arises as an initial condition of the dynamical equations of motion
[
10
,
11
]
.
This can be viewed as a result of the application of Theorem
7.1
.

Consider the action

\displaystyle S[\phi;\rho]=\int d^{D}x\left(\sqrt{g}R+\lambda\left(\sqrt{g}-\rho\right)\right),

(7.29)

where
\phi=\{g_{\mu\nu},\lambda\}
are dynamical fields and
\rho
is the background scalar density field.
It becomes the action for unimodular gravity when the auxiliary field
\lambda
is integrated out.

By definition, both the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and the scalar
\lambda
are dynamical fields.
In particular, the field
\lambda
can be any spacetime function.
The field
\rho
is, on the other hand, a scalar density of a given background.

Theorem
7.1
states that, since
\delta S/\delta\rho=-\lambda
,
\lambda
is forced to be a constant, i.e.

\displaystyle\lambda=C,

(7.30)

when the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
satisfies the equations of motion.
This makes
\lambda
a cosmological constant, although its value is to be determined by the initial condition.

This example suggests that the cosmological constant may arise as a result of the equations of motion when the theory respects VPD; we do not need to put in by hand a constant in the theory from the beginning.

In the first part of the paper (Secs.

This includes three well-known string actions: the Schild, the Nambu-Goto, and the Polyakov actions.
They have different symmetries: VPD, Diff, and Diff-Weyl, respectively.
Hence, we consider the general forms of actions that respect these symmetries and show their classical equivalence.
Also, we discuss how tension can be defined from the Schild action by matching its stress-energy tensor to the Nambu-Goto’s.

In the second part (Secs.

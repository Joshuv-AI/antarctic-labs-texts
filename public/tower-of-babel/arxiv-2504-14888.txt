WMKA-Net: A Weighted Multi-Kernel Attention Network Method for Retinal Vessel Segmentation
Xinran Xu, Yuliang Ma, Sifu CaiHangzhou Dianzi University23060807@hdu.edu.cn, mayuliang@hdu.edu.cn,csf840729896@qq.com

Abstract
We propose a novel retinal vessel segmentation network, the Weighted Multi-Kernel Attention Network (WMKA-Net), which aims to address the issues of insufficient multiscale feature capture, loss of contextual information, and noise sensitivity in retinal vessel segmentation. WMKA-Net significantly improves the segmentation performance of small vessels and low-contrast regions by integrating several innovative components, including the MultiKernelFeature Fusion Module (MKDC), the Progressive Feature Weighting Fusion Strategy (UDFF), and the Attention Mechanism Module (AttentionBlock). The MKDC module employs multiscale parallel convolutional kernels to extract vessel characteristics, thereby enhancing the ability to capture complex vascular structures. The UDFF strategy optimizes the transmission of feature information by weighted fusion of high- and low-level features. The AttentionBlock highlights key regions and suppresses noise interference through the attention mechanism. Experimental results demonstrate that WMKA-Net achieves excellent segmentation performance in multiple public datasets, particularly in segmentation of small vessels and processing of pathological regions. This work provides a robust and efficient new method for segmentation of the retinal vessel.

1
Introduction

Retinal vessel segmentation is crucial for the early diagnosis of diseases such as diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma, as these conditions can cause abnormal vascular structures, such as tortuous vessels, leakage, or occlusion. Traditional methods rely on expert manual annotation, which is inefficient and prone to subjectivity. In contrast, modern automated techniques, such as deep learning, have significantly improved the accuracy and efficiency of segmentation
[
1
]
. However, due to the complex morphology, multiscale structure of retinal vessels, and the presence of noise and background interference in images, existing segmentation methods still face numerous challenges.

Although the encoder-decoder architecture based on U-Net and its variants has shown excellent performance in retinal vessel segmentation, these methods are prone to loss of spatial and semantic information during successive upsampling operations. In addition, traditional CNN architectures have limitations in capturing global contextual information, making it difficult to effectively handle the complex topological structure of retinal vessels. In addition, retinal vessel images often have low contrast, uneven illumination, and complex background noise, further increasing the difficulty of the segmentation task. Furthermore, multiscale features and irregular distribution of retinal vessels also pose higher demands on the model’s feature extraction capabilities.

To overcome the above challenges, this study proposes a novel retinal vessel segmentation network WMKA-Net, which aims to improve segmentation performance through innovative network architecture and module design. Specifically, we introduce the following key technologies:

1.1
Multi-Scale Feature Fusion Module (MKDC):

To enhance the model’s ability to capture vascular features at different scales, we introduce a Multi-Kernel Feature Fusion Module (MKDC) in the encoder and bottleneck layers. This module effectively extracts multiscale features by combining convolutional kernels of varying sizes (e.g., 1×1, 3×3, 7×7, 11×11) and restores feature maps to their original dimensions through interpolation operations, thus preserving more detailed information.

1.2
Attention Mechanism Module (AffinityAttention):

To improve the model’s focus on critical vascular features and reduce background noise interference, we incorporate spatial attention (SpatialAttentionBlock) and channel attention (ChannelAttentionBlock) mechanisms into the decoder. These modules dynamically adjust feature map weights, allowing the model to prioritize important vascular regions and improving segmentation accuracy.

1.3
Feature Fusion Strategy (Adaptive Pyramid Fusion):

To further optimize the results of segmentation, we adopt a feature fusion strategy in the decoder. Specifically, low- and high-level features are weighted and fused, followed by refinement using additional convolutional layers and transposed convolutional layers. In addition, an Adaptive Pyramid Fusion (APF) module is introduced to enhance the spatial resolution and semantic information of feature maps.

2
Related Work

Early retinal vessel segmentation methods relied primarily on image processing techniques such as filtering, threshold segmentation, and edge detection. For example, Singh et al. proposed a local entropy threshold segmentation method
[
2
]
. However, these methods heavily depend on hand-crafted features and rules, leading to limited adaptability and suboptimal results in complex images. With the introduction of machine learning techniques, classifiers and regressors began to be applied to the segmentation of the retinal vessel. Tuba et al. used Support Vector Machines (SVM) for segmentation of the retinal vessel
[
3
]
, while Zhang et al. proposed a brain-inspired wavelet transform combined with random forest for segmentation
[
4
]
. Although these methods leverage large-scale labeled data for training and improve segmentation performance, manual feature extraction remains necessary.

The advent of deep learning revolutionized retinal vessel segmentation. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) enable models to automatically extract features from raw images, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering. For example, Jonathan Long et al. introduced Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN)
[
5
]
, which utilize skip connections to capture richer semantic information. However, FCNs suffer from high parameter counts, limited segmentation accuracy, and overfitting. To address these issues, Olaf Ronneberger et al. proposed the U-Net architecture
[
6
]
, known for its simplicity, ease of implementation, customization, and fewer parameters, inspiring numerous U-Net variants.
Traditional U-Net models exhibit limited connections between deep and shallow layers. To resolve this, Zhou et al. proposed U-Net++
[
7
]
, which establishes dense connections across layers to fully exploit multiscale information. To mitigate feature propagation discrepancies in the decoder, Ibtehaz et al. introduced MultiResUNet
[
8
]
, which improves multiresolution analysis capabilities. Furthermore, Xu et al. developed Ffu-net for medical image segmentation
[
9
]
, incorporating contextual channel attention mechanisms to enrich skip connection information. Banerjee et al. designed SIU-Net for spinal image segmentation
[
10
]
, proposing novel skip connections to extract rich semantic features for multiscale fusion. Woo et al. introduced the CBAM attention module
[
11
]
, which uses channel and spatial attention to emphasize critical channels and spatial regions, improving the network’s understanding of input images.

On the contrary, our proposed WMKA-Net introduces architectural innovations to address limitations in existing methods for segmentation of the retinal vessel, particularly insufficient utilization of hierarchical feature information and loss of spatial detail caused by repeated pooling operations.

3
Proposed Method

3.1
Network Architecture Overview

Figure
1
illustrates the overall architecture of WMKA-Net. WMKA-Net is based on a four-layer U-shaped encoder-decoder framework, with its core structure comprising encoding, skip connections, decoding, and integrated multiscale feature fusion and attention mechanisms. The encoder employs four cascaded convolutional blocks (each containing dual 3×3 convolutions + BN + ReLU followed by 2×2 max pooling) to progressively extract features, expanding the channel dimension from 3 to 512. The decoder adopts a hybrid design: The first two stages integrate an APF module (including a channel attention SE module, multiscale pooling fusion, and feature upsampling), while the latter two stages utilize standard transposed convolution blocks fused with encoder features via skip connections.

Figure 1:
Network structure of the proposed WMKA-Net network.

Innovatively, Multi-Kernel Feature Fusion Modules (MKDC1-4) are introduced across all encoding layers. These modules employ parallel 1×1, 3×3, 7×7, and 11×11 kernels to capture multiscale features, combined with residual connections and bilinear interpolation to preserve spatial resolution. During decoding, a joint attention mechanism (AffinityAttention) fuses spatial attention (capturing long-range dependencies via asymmetric convolutions) and channel attention (via feature transpose interactions), enhanced by a learnable parameter
\gamma
for weighted feature selection.

The feature fusion strategy employs a progressive weighting approach, in which high- and low-level features are fused in a 3: 7 ratio. The final segmentation outputs are generated through a sigmoid activation. This design mitigates pooling-induced information loss via APF modules, enhances multiscale representation through MKDC, and leverages attention-guided feature selection, significantly improving the capture of fine vascular structures.

Multi-Kernel Feature Fusion Module(MKDC)

3.2.1
Structural Design of the MKDC Module

The MKDC

Each convolution kernel corresponds to a specific receptive field and can capture characteristics of blood vessels at different scales. Specifically, smaller convolution kernels (such as 1×1) can extract local detail features, while larger convolution kernels (such as 11×11) can capture broader contextual information. By combining these features at different scales, the MKDC module can more comprehensively represent the multiscale features of retinal blood vessels. If the dilation rate l is adopted, then the l-1 pixels will be skipped during the convolution operation, thus covering more extensive information, as shown in Figure

3.2.2
Feature Extraction and Interpolation Operation

In the MKDC module, the input feature map first undergoes feature extraction through multiple parallel convolution operations. Each convolution operation uses convolution kernels of different scales to generate a set of feature maps at different scales. For example, the feature map generated by the 1×1 convolution kernel can capture local detail information, while the feature map generated by the 11×11 convolution kernel can capture broader contextual information. To ensure that these feature maps at different scales can be effectively fused, we adopted an interpolation operation to restore them to the original size. Interpolation operations can use methods such as bilinear interpolation or nearest-neighbor interpolation to ensure the size consistency of the feature maps.

3.2.3
Feature Fusion

After completing the extraction and interpolation operations of the feature maps at different scales, we fuse these feature maps. Feature fusion can be achieved through simple addition or more complex fusion strategies (such as weighted summation or feature concatenation). In our design, we adopt the method of feature concatenation, where the feature maps at different scales are concatenated to form a richer feature representation. This feature fusion method can retain the unique information of each feature map on different scales and, at the same time, provide a more comprehensive feature input for subsequent network layers.

Attention Mechanism Module (AffinityAttention)

To enhance the focus of the module on critical vascular characteristics and reduce background noise interference, we introduced an attention mechanism module (AffinityAttention) in the decoder section. Figure

As shown in Figure 4, this module integrates both Spatial Attention Block (SpatialAttentionBlock) and Channel Attention Block (ChannelAttentionBlock), which can dynamically adjust the weights on the feature maps, allowing the model to pay more attention to important vascular regions
[
13
]
. This helps improve the segmentation accuracy of small vessels, especially under conditions of low contrast and complex background.

3.3.1
Channel Attention Block(CAB)

The core idea of the channel attention module is to enhance the model’s responsiveness to key features by learning the dependencies between channels. Specifically, the CAB module first compresses each channel of the feature map into a scalar through a Global Average Pooling (GAP) operation, thereby obtaining the global feature representation of each channel. Then, these global feature representations undergo a non - linear transformation through a Fully Connected Layer (FC) and a ReLU activation function to generate channel weights. Finally, these channel weights are normalized through a Sigmoid function to obtain the attention weight for each channel. These weights are used to weight each channel of the original feature map, enhancing the expression ability of key features.
The mathematical expressions are as follows:

F_{\text{out}}=F\odot\text{Sigmoid}\left(\text{FC}\left(\text{ReLU}\left(\text{FC}\left(\text{GAP}(F)\right)\right)\right)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times W}

(1)

Among them, F is the input feature map, C is the number of channels, H and W are the height and width of the feature map, respectively, and
\odot
denotes element-wise multiplication.

3.3.2
Spatial Attention Module(SAB)

The core idea of the spatial attention module is to enhance the model’s responsiveness to critical regions by learning dependencies between spatial positions. Specifically, the SAM first employs a convolution operation to generate two novel feature maps, which are used to compute spatial correlations. Subsequently, a softmax function is applied to calculate attention weights at spatial locations. These weights are then used to weight the original feature maps
[
1
]
, thereby amplifying the feature representation capacity of key regions.

The mathematical expressions are as follows:

F_{\text{out}}=F\odot\left(\text{Softmax}\left(F_{\text{conv1}}\cdot F_{\text{conv2}}^{T}\right)\right)

(2)

Among them, it denotes matrix multiplication, and
\odot
denotes element-wise multiplication.

3.4
Adaptive Pyramid Fusion Module(APF)

The Adaptive Pyramid Fusion (APF) module
[
14
]
is one of the key components of the network, designed to mitigate the loss of spatial information caused by multilevel pooling operations. Figure
5
illustrates the structure of the APF module. As shown in Figure 5, the APF module combines both maximum Pooling and Average Pooling operations and further optimizes the feature maps through an SE block (Squeeze-and-Excitation Block). This design effectively preserves spatial information and enhances the model’s ability to capture small vessels.

3.4.1
The function of the SE block

The core idea of the SE block is to dynamically adjust the channel weights of feature maps through a channel attention mechanism, allowing the model to focus more on critical features. Specifically, as shown in Equation (1), the SE block first compresses each channel of the feature map into a scalar via a Global Average Pooling (GAP) operation, obtaining global feature representations for each channel. These global representations are then passed through a fully connected layer (FC) with a ReLU activation function for nonlinear transformation, generating channelwise weights. Finally, these weights are normalized using a Sigmoid function to produce attention weights for each channel. These channel-wise attention weights are applied to re-weight the original feature maps, thereby enhancing the representation of critical features.

3.4.2
Max Pooling and Average Pooling

The APF module integrates both maximum pooling and average pooling operations to take advantage of both methods. Maximum pooling emphasizes salient features and edge information in feature maps, while average pooling smooths feature maps to suppress noise. By combing the two pooling results in a 1:1 ratio, the APF module effectively balances critical feature preservation and noise reduction in feature maps.
The mathematical expressions are as follows:

Z=\phi\left(\text{concat}\left(I,\frac{\text{maxpool}(L)+\text{avgpool}(L)}{2}\right)\right)

(3)

Here, maxpool and avgpool denote the max pooling and average pooling operations, respectively.
\phi
represents a 1×1 convolutional layer used for channel-wise compression. I is the original input feature map and L is the feature map after processing by the SE block.

3.4.3
Feature Fusion

The APF module fuses multiscale feature maps with the original input feature map through weighted fusion to generate the final feature map. This fusion method not only preserves the contextual information of the original feature map but also enhances the expressiveness of the multiscale feature maps. The final feature map undergoes a non-linear transformation through a 3×3 convolutional layer to further optimize the feature representation.

The mathematical expressions are as follows:

\displaystyle Z=\phi\left(\texttt{concatenation}\left(I,\frac{\texttt{pool}(\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(\texttt{SE}(I))))+\texttt{avepool}(\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(\texttt{SE}(I))))}{2}\right)\right)

(4)

Among them,
\psi
1 is an upsampling layer,
\Psi
2 is a 3×3 convolutional layer, and
\phi
is a 1×1 convolutional layer.

3.5
Progressive Feature weighted fusion strategy (PFWF Strategy)

To further optimize the segmentation results, we adopted a feature-weighted fusion strategy in the decoder section, as shown in Figure
2
. Specifically, we performed weighted fusion of low- and high-level features and further refined the segmentation results through additional convolutional and deconvolutional layers. In addition, we introduced a weighted fusion mechanism to enhance segmentation accuracy by combining feature maps from different levels. This strategy ensures that the model maintains high segmentation accuracy in complex retinal vessel images while effectively addressing issues such as low contrast, uneven illumination, and background noise.

3.5.1
Implementation of the Feature-Weighted Fusion Strategy

The core idea of the feature-weighted fusion strategy is to combine low-level and high-level features through weighted fusion, fully utilizing the complementarity of feature maps from different levels. Specifically, we designed a Feature Weighted Fusion Module (FWFM), which includes the following key steps:

Feature Extraction and Initial Fusion:

First, low-level and high-level features are extracted from the encoder section. Low-level features typically contain rich detail information, while high-level features contain more global semantic information. We perform an initial fusion of these feature maps through a 1×1 convolutional layer to reduce the number of channels while preserving key information.

Weighted Fusion Mechanism:

To better utilize the complementarity of feature maps of different levels, we introduced a weighted fusion mechanism. Specifically, we dynamically calculate the weights of feature maps from different levels through a Weight Learning Module (WLM). The weight learning module generates weights through a fully connected layer and a ReLU activation function, which are used to combine feature maps of different levels.

Refining Segmentation Results:

After weighted fusion, the feature maps are upsampled through a deconvolutional layer to restore the original image size. Finally, an additional convolutional layer further refines the segmentation results to generate the final segmentation map.

3.5.2
Mathematical Expression of the Feature-Weighted Fusion Strategy

Given low-level feature maps Flow and high-level feature maps
F_{\mathrm{hig}}
, with learnable weights
\aleph
and
\beta
generated by the weight learning module, the fused feature map
F_{\mathrm{fused}}
can be expressed as:

F_{\text{fused}}=\alpha\cdot F_{\text{low}}+\beta\cdot F_{\text{high}}

(5)

Here, the weights
\aleph
and
\beta
are dynamically computed by the weight learning module, ensuring that the complementarity between multiscale feature maps is fully used.

3.6
The remaining modules

The Encoder Block (Fig.
6
(a)) is a fundamental unit in neural networks for feature extraction. It first receives the input data and then processes the data through a Convolutional Block (Conv Block) to extract useful features. Subsequently, the MaxPooling operation is applied to reduce the spatial dimensions of the feature maps. This not only reduces the computational burden of subsequent layers, but also helps to extract more prominent features.

The Decoder Block (Fig.
6
(b)) is used to up-sample the feature maps output by the encoder to restore their spatial resolution. It receives the input data and increases the dimensions of the feature maps through a Transposed Convolution (ConvTranspose) layer. Subsequently, the Convolutional Block (Conv Block) further refines these up-sampled feature maps to improve their quality.

The Convolutional Block (Conv Block,Fig.
6
(c)) is the core component that constitutes both the encoder and decoder blocks. It receives the input data and captures thenlocal features through a 3×3 convolutional layer. Then, the Batch Normalization (BN) layer normalizes the output of the convolutional layer, which helps to stabilize the learning process and reduce the internal covariate shift. Finally, the ReLU activation function introduces non-linearity, enabling the model to learn more complex patterns.

The Decoder Block + Adaptive Pyramid Features (Decoder Block + APF,Fig.
6
(d)) is an extension of the decoder block. It adds an Adaptive Pyramid Features (APF) layer on the basis of upsampling. This module first receives the input data, performs upsampling through the transposed convolutional layer, and then the APF layer further processes these feature maps, possibly enhancing certain features or integrating multiscale information. Finally, the convolutional block performs the final processing on the feature maps to complete the refinement of the features.

The Attention Block(Fig.
6
(e)) is a component used to enhance the model’s ability to focus on important features. It receives the input data and pays attention to the important features in the spatial and channel dimensions, respectively, through the Special Attention Block (SAB) and the Channel Attention Block (CAB). The outputs of these two attention blocks are combined through an addition operation to generate the final rich feature representation. This representation captures spatial and channel attention information, providing a more comprehensive feature input for subsequent network layers.

4.2
Experiment setup

The WMKA-Net proposed in this paper is built based on the PyTorch framework. All experiments were carried out on a platform equipped with two NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPUs, each with 24GB of memory, to achieve efficient model training and testing. Regarding model training, after numerous experiments, we set the number of training epochs at 1500, the learning rate at 0.0001, the batch size to 4, and selected the Adam optimizer.

Due to its efficiency and numerical stability in handling binary classification tasks, we chose the BCEWithLogitsLoss function as the loss function. BCEWithLogitsLoss combines the Sigmoid activation function with binary cross-entropy loss, allowing direct computation on the model’s raw output and avoiding the numerical instability issues associated with explicitly calling the Sigmoid function. Specifically, for each pixel’s predicted probability pi and groundtruth label gi, the BCE Loss is calculated as follows:

L_{\mathrm{BCE}}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\left[g_{i}\cdot\log(p_{i})+(1-g_{i})\cdot\log(1-p_{i})\right]

(6)

Among them:

N
denotes the total number of pixels in the image;

g_{i}\in[0,1]
represents the ground-truth label of pixel
i
(where 0 indicates background and 1 indicates the target structure);

p_{i}\in[0,1]
represents the probability predicted by the model that the pixel
i
belongs to the target structure.

4.3
Data Preprocessing

For retinal fundus images, data pre-processing is a crucial step because it can reduce image noise and improve contrast, thus improving the visibility of blood vessels. These images typically have low contrast, which can affect the accuracy of vessel segmentation.

Compared to the DRIVE dataset, the
CHASE\_DB1
dataset has more image noise and less clear contrast. Therefore, effective data pre-processing methods need to be applied to images of the retinal fundus
[
18
]
. In the data preprocessing stage, the color images are first converted to grayscale images and then the following operations are performed:

Grayscale Conversion:

Convert the color image to a grayscale image to simplify the image data and highlight luminance information, which helps to better identify vascular structures in subsequent processing steps.

Normalization:

Normalize the grayscale image to scale values of the pixel in the range [0, 1]. This helps eliminate differences in lighting and contrast between different images.

CLAHE Contrast Enhancement:

Apply the Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) algorithm to enhance the local contrast of the image. This helps improve the visual quality of the image and make vascular structures more distinct.

Gamma Correction:

Further adjust the contrast of the image through Gamma correction. The choice of Gamma value can be adjusted on the basis of image characteristics and desired visual effects. Gamma correction can enhance the brightness details of the image, making blood vessels clearer.

Channel Merging:

Finally, merge the processed single channel image into a three-channel image for subsequent processing and display. Figure
8
i shows the images from the
CHASE\_DB1
dataset before and after preprocessing.

The images in the STARE dataset have high resolution and using them directly for training can place significant computational demands on the system. To accommodate the memory limitations of the GPU and improve processing efficiency, we performed a pre-processing operation of resizing the images. Specifically, we used the bilinear interpolation method to uniformly downscale all images to a size of 512×512 pixels. This resizing helps to reduce computational complexity while preserving important visual information in the images, providing a balanced input size for the subsequent vessel segmentation task.

4.4
Evaluation metrics

To comprehensively and accurately evaluate the segmentation performance of the proposed IMFF-Net, this study introduces four key concepts from the confusion matrix: True Positive (TP), False Positive (FP), False Negative (FN), and True Negative (TN). Based on these four fundamental concepts, we derive common evaluation metrics to analyze the model’s performance, including Sensitivity (Se), Specificity (Sp), Accuracy (Acc), and F1 Score .

Se:

ensitivity, also known as the true positive rate or recall, measures the model’s ability to correctly identify positive samples. It evaluates the proportion of retinal vessels correctly classified as vascular regions, expressed mathematically as:

S_{e}=\frac{\mathit{TP}}{\mathit{TP}+\mathit{FN}}

(7)

S_{p}
:

The specificity quantifies the model’s ability to correctly identify negative samples.
Assesses the proportion of nonvascular regions accurately classified as background,
expressed mathematically as:

S_{p}=\frac{\mathit{TN}}{\mathit{TN}+\mathit{FP}}

(8)

Acc:

Accuracy is a percentage-based metric that evaluates the overall classification correctness of the model in retinal vessel segmentation tasks, expressed mathematically as:

A_{\mathrm{cc}}=\frac{\mathit{TP}+\mathit{TN}}{\mathit{TP}+\mathit{FP}+\mathit{TN}+\mathit{FN}}

(9)

F1 Score:

The F1 Score is a comprehensive metric that combines precision and recall to reflect both perception and completeness of the classification results, expressed mathematically as:

F_{1}=\frac{2^{*}\mathit{TP}}{2^{*}\mathit{TP}+\mathit{FP}+\mathit{FN}}

(10)

Experiment Results and Analysis

To comprehensively assess the performance of the proposed WMKA-Net, this study employs both quantitative and qualitative evaluation methodologies. Firstly, an ablation study is conducted on the WMKA-Net architecture to verify the individual effectiveness of each module. 5.1
Ablation study

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed Multi-Kernel Feature Fusion Module (MKDC) and Attention Mechanism Module (AffinityAttention) in WMKA-Net, we conducted a series of ablation experiments on the public DRIVE dataset.

First, we selected IMFF-net
[
14
]
(a four-layer architecture with APF and UDFF) as the baseline. Subsequently, we incorporated MKDC and AffinityAttention into the baseline to construct different configurations of WMKA-Net. The results of these ablation studies are summarized in Table

From the results of these ablation experiments, the addition of the APF and UDFF modules significantly improved the segmentation performance. Taking the STARE dataset as an example, the baseline network’s sensitivity (Se), specificity (Sp), F1 score, and accuracy (Acc) were 0.8575, 0.9860, 0.7977, and 0.9621, respectively. After introducing the MKDC module, the key metrics Sp increased from 0.9860 to 0.9936, F1 increased from 0.7977 to 0.8155, and Acc increased from 0.9621 to 0.9788. This indicates that the MKDC module has a significant positive effect on the segmentation results.

After verifying the MKDC module, we further introduced the AffinityAttention module. The addition of the AffinityAttention module increased Se from 0.8575 to 0.8914 and Acc from 0.9621 to 0.9692. Compared to simply adding convolutions in the downsampling of the baseline, the addition of the AffinityAttention module achieved better results.

Weight Analysis of the MKDC Model

The optimal dilation rates (1, 3, 7, 11) for the MKDC module were determined through experiments.

6
Discussion

This study presents an enhanced WMKA - Net. This network showcases exceptional performance and robustness in retinal vessel segmentation. By enhancing multi - scale feature fusion and the segmentation of minute blood vessels, the model can not only uphold a high degree of segmentation accuracy in intricate retinal vessel images but also efficiently handle problems like low contrast, uneven illumination, and background noise. Experimental results reveal that WMKA - Net performs outstandingly on commonly used datasets such as DRIVE, STARE, and
CHASE\_DB1
, substantially outperforming existing methods such as the Gabor-modulated depth separable convolution UNet model
[
29
]
and IMFF-Net
[
14
]
. These enhancements offer a more reliable tool for clinical diagnosis.

Notwithstanding these remarkable achivements, several directions remain open for further exploration. For instance, in the future, more effective multi task learning algorithms could be developed to further boost segmentation accuracy and generalization capabilities. In addition, determining how to decrease the demand for computing resources while maintaining high precision is also a crucial research area.

7
Conclusion

The WMKA - Net proposed in this paper substantially enhances the accuracy and robustness of retinal vessel segmentation by leveraging multiscale feature fusion (MKDC), an adaptive attention mechanism (AffinityAttention), and a dynamic weighted fusion strategy. Experiments conducted on three publicly accessible datasets yield the following results:

Performance superiority:
The average F1 scores of WMKA-Net in the DRIVE, STARE, and
CHASE\_DB1
datasets reach 0.9212, 0.9190 and 0.8665 respectively, which is an improvement of 8% to 12% compared with baseline models such as U-Net and AttU-Net.

Segmentation ability of tiny blood vessels:
Through collaborative optimization of spatial channel attention, the sensitivity (Se) of the model to low contrast blood vessels is increased to 0.9449 (DRIVE dataset), which is better than existing methods.

Clinical value:
This model can provide a high-precision auxiliary diagnostic tool for the early detection of diseases such as diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma.

Future research will focus on the lightweight design of the model and cross - modal data fusion to facilitate its application in real - time medical scenarios. This study offers novel technical insights for complex medical image segmentation tasks and is of great academic and clinical importance.

Exponential moments for Hawkes processes under minimal assumptions
Théo Leblanc

Abstract




Abstract

We prove that the number of points of a stationary linear Hawkes process lying in any bounded subset of the real line has exponential moments, without any other assumption than the one needed for existence of such stationary process, namely the spectral radius of the matrix of
{\mathbb{L}}^{1}
norms of interaction functions is smaller than one. The proof relies on a mass transport principle argument. We also specify the dependence of the bounds with respect to the base rates and the matrix of
{\mathbb{L}}^{1}
norms of interaction functions defining the Hawkes process and give a functional version of the result.

Keywords:
Hawkes processes; exponential moments; cluster representation.

MSC classifications:
60G55; 60E15; 60J85.

1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation

Hawkes processes, introduced by Hawkes in 1971 in
[
7
]
, are modelling the continuous time arrivals of some random
0-1
events when the arrivals depend on the past events. Hawkes processes can be self exciting or self inhibiting. This flexibility allows Hawkes processes to model a large variety of phenomena, from the time occurrences of earthquakes
[
11
]
to the activity of the neural network of the brain
[
5
,
12
]
, to cite but of few. Existence of stationary linear Hawkes processes, which only models excitation, is guaranteed under the well known condition that the spectral radius of the matrix of
{\mathbb{L}}^{1}
norms of interaction functions is smaller than one
[
2
]
. This condition is expressed in
Assumption

1
in
Section

1.2
. From a statistical point of view, having controls on the moments of Hawkes processes is key to construct robust estimation procedures of the interaction parameters of the process. Exponential moments of stationary Hawkes processes have been proved under conditions such as bounded memory interactions
[
6
,
13
]
or decay assumptions on the interaction function in the univariate case
[
14
]
. But, up to our knowledge, there are no results about the existence of exponential moments for Hawkes processes under the minimal spectral radius condition that guarantees the existence of stationary Hawkes processes. In this paper we prove that a stationary Hawkes process has exponential moments under the spectral radius assumption, regardless of the tails of the interaction functions. We also provide quantitative bound of the exponential moments.

Along this paper we denote
\mathds{R}
the set of real numbers,
\mathds{N}
the set of integers:
\mathds{N}=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
and
\mathds{N}^{*}=\mathds{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

1.2
Setting

A
point process
on the real line
\mathds{R}
is a random set of points
N\subset\mathds{R}
such that almost surely,
N
is locally finite. Given
\bm{M}=[\![1,M]\!]
with
M
a positive integer, a
\bm{M}
-
multivariate point process
on
\mathds{R}
is the collection
(N^{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}
of random locally finite subsets of
\mathds{R}
. Under mild conditions, a multivariate point process
(N^{1},\cdots,N^{M})
is characterised by its stochastic intensity process
(\lambda^{1},\cdots,\lambda^{M})
, defined by

\mathds{P}\left(N^{m}\ \text{has a point in}\ [t,t+dt]\ \middle|\ \mathcal{F}_{t}\right)=\lambda^{m}_{t}dt,\quad m=1,\cdots,M,

where
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathds{R}}
is the filtration generated by
(N^{1},\cdots,N^{M})
. See
[
1
]
for more precise statements. Hawkes processes are specific point processes. To define them, we consider
\bm{\mu}:=(\mu_{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}\subset\mathds{R}_{+}^{\bm{M}}
and
\bm{h}:=(h^{m}_{m^{\prime}})_{m,m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}}
, with
h^{m}_{m^{\prime}}:\mathds{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathds{R}_{+}
measurable functions.

Definition 1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathds{P})
a probabilistic space and a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathds{R}}
. A Hawkes process with parameters
\bm{M},\bm{\mu},\bm{h}
is a multivariate point process
\bm{N}:=(N^{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}
such that

•

\bm{N}
is adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathds{R}}
,

•

almost surely, for
m\neq m^{\prime}
in
\bm{M}
,
N^{m}
has no common points with
N^{m^{\prime}}
,

•

\bm{N}
has a predictable intensity
\bm{\lambda}:=(\lambda^{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}
given by

\lambda^{m}_{t}=\mu_{m}+\sum_{m^{\prime}\in M}\int_{-\infty}^{t^{-}}h^{m}_{m^{\prime}}(t-s)dN^{m^{\prime}}_{s}\quad m\in\bm{M},\ t\in\mathds{R}.

(1)

In the previous definition, the constants
\mu_{m}
are called
base rates
, modelling the spontaneous rates of arrivals of points. The functions
h^{m}_{m^{\prime}}
that take into account the influence of each point process on the others are called
interaction functions
.

In
[
2
]
, Brémaud and Massoulié proved in particular that such a linear Hawkes process, in the sense of
Definition

1
exists and is stationary under the following assumption.

Assumption 1
.

The spectral radius of the matrix
\bm{H}=(\|h^{m}_{m^{\prime}}\|_{1})_{m^{\prime},m\in\bm{M}}
is strictly smaller than
1
, ie
\spr(\bm{H})<1
.

As explained in the next section, this assumption is sufficient to have exponential moments.

1.3
Main result

Given a Hawkes process
\bm{N}
and
B\subset\mathds{R}
we are interested in the number of points lying in
B
:

N(B)=\sum_{m\in\bm{M}}N^{m}(B)

where
N^{m}(B):=\card(N^{m}\cap B)
. To state our main result we need the following definition.

Definition 2
.

Let
A
a square matrix. We say that
A
satisfies the property
\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
with
r,K\geq 0
and denote
A\in\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
if

\forall n\in\mathds{N}^{*},\ |\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|A^{n}|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|_{\infty}\leq Kr^{n},

where and
|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|_{\infty}
is the operator norm associated to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

The well known Gelfand’s Theorem states that for any square matrix
A
, for any norm
\|\cdot\|
, we have
\|A^{n}\|^{1/n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\spr(A)
. Thus, for any
r>\spr(A)
there exists
K<\infty
such that
A\in\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
. It follows that under
Assumption

1
, there exist
r
and
K
with
\spr(\bm{H})<r<1
and
0<K<\infty
such that
\bm{H}\in\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
. Now we can state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
Assumption

1
holds, ie the spectral radius of
\bm{H}=(\|h^{m}_{m^{\prime}}\|_{1})_{m^{\prime},m}
is strictly less than
1
. Then there exists
\xi>0
depending only on
\bm{H}
such that for all
B\subset\mathds{R}
bounded, we have

\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi N(B)}\right]<\infty.

More precisely, if
B\subset[s,s+L)
for some
s\in\mathds{R}
and
L>0
, and if
\bm{H}\in\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
with
0<r<1
and
0\leq K<\infty
then we have

\mathds{E}\left[e^{u\xi_{r,K}N(B)}\right]\leq\exp\left(\left[\Big(\dfrac{1+r}{2r}\Big)^{u}-1\right]L\sum_{m\in M}\mu_{m}\right),\quad\text{for all}\ u\in[0,1]

(2)

with

\displaystyle\xi_{r,K}=\dfrac{\log\Big(\dfrac{1+r}{2r}\Big)}{1+\dfrac{2K}{1-r}}.

Some remarks are in order.

The bound given in
eq.

2
can be rewritten as follows:

\mathds{E}\left[e^{tN(B)}\right]\leq\exp\left(\Big[e^{tC_{r,K}}-1\Big]L\sum_{m\in M}\mu_{m}\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq\xi_{r,K}

(3)

with
C_{r,K}=1+\dfrac{2K}{1-r}
. Thus, the bound for
\mathds{E}\left[e^{tN(B)}\right]
is increasing with
K
and
r
, which is the expected behaviour.

In the univariate case, ie
\card(\bm{M})=1
, the matrix
\bm{H}
is in fact a scalar with value
\alpha=\|h\|_{1}
where
h
is the (only one) interaction function. Since
\bm{H}^{n}=(\alpha^{n})
it is straightforward that
\bm{H}\in\bm{\Gel(}\alpha,1\bm{)}
.
If
\bm{H}=0
, meaning that there are no interactions and the Hawkes process consists of independent homogeneous Poisson processes with rates
\mu_{m}
for
m\in\bm{M}
, then we have
\bm{H}\in\bm{\Gel(}r,0\bm{)}
for all
r\in(0,1)
. In this case, the exponential moments of
N([0,L])
can be explicitly calculated and compared to the bound
eq.

2
as follows:

\exp\left((e^{t}-1)L\sum_{m\in M}\mu_{m}\right)=\mathds{E}\left[e^{tN([0,L])}\right]\underset{\text{by}\ \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{borne moment exp}}{\leq}\exp\left((e^{t}-1)L\sum_{m\in M}\mu_{m}\right)\quad t\geq 0.

Indeed,
C_{r,0}=1
and
\xi_{r,0}\xrightarrow[r\to 0^{+}]{}+\infty
. Thus, in this particular case, the bound
eq.

2
becomes an equality.

The dependency on
M
, the cardinal of
\bm{M}
, is hidden in the constant
K
of
\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
. Indeed, the operator norm
|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|\cdot|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|_{\infty}
takes into account the dimension of the matrix in the following sense: for a square matrix
A
of size
d\geq 1
, we have

|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|A|\hskip-1.0pt|\hskip-1.0pt|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in[\![1,d]\!]}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|A_{ij}|.

Remark 1
.

The constant
\xi_{r,K}
, which comes from
Lemma

2
which states exponential moments of Galton Watson processes with Poisson offspring measure, may be not optimal. In particular, in the simplest univariate case of a Galton Watson process
\mathcal{T}
with offspring measure
{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\alpha)
where
0\leq\alpha<1
, if we denote
g(\xi):=\log\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi\card(\mathcal{T})}\right]
then for
\xi>0
,
g(\xi)<\infty
if and only if the equation
x=\xi+\alpha(e^{x}-1)
has a positive solution for
x
and in this case
g(\xi)
is given by the first positive solution (on
x
) of
x=\xi+\alpha(e^{x}-1)
. Given
\alpha
, finding the largest
\xi
such that there exists a positive solution of the above equation cannot be solved with the usual functions. Thus the optimal
\xi_{r,K}
can not be expressed in closed form. However, the above reasoning shows (with the proof of
Theorem

1
) that for each matrix
\bm{H}
such that
\spr(\bm{H})<1
, there exists a
\xi_{\bm{H}}>0
satisfying
\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi_{\bm{H}}N(B)}\right]<\infty
for all
B
bounded and
\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi N(B)}\right]=\infty
for any
\xi>\xi_{\bm{H}}
and
B
non-zero interval.

1.4
Functional result

Let
f
a non negative real function and define

N(f):=\sum_{m\in\bm{M}}\sum_{x\in N^{m}}f(x).

The following result states exponential moments of
N(f)
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f
a non negative real function, and
T>0
. Suppose that
\bm{H}\in\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
with
0<r<1
and
0\leq K<\infty
. Then we have

\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi N(f)}\right]\leq\exp\left(\bigg[e^{\xi|f|^{\infty}_{1,T}C_{r,K}}-1\bigg]T\sum_{m\in M}\mu_{m}\right),\quad 0\leq\xi\leq\xi_{r,K}/|f|^{\infty}_{1,T}

where
\xi_{r,K}
and
C_{r,K}
are defined in
Theorem

1
and

|f|^{\infty}_{1,T}=\sup_{t\in\mathds{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathds{Z}}f(t+nT).

Theorem

2
is an extension of
Theorem

1
. Indeed to recover the classical exponential moments, one can take
f=\mathds{1}_{B}
and
T=L
.

The
|\cdot|^{\infty}_{1,T}
is similar to a discrete
L^{1}
norm for non negative functions with discretization scale
T
.

The proof of
Theorem

2
is similar to the proof of
Theorem

1
and is based on the same arguments. Thus we only give the key details of the proof.

Before giving the proof of
Theorem

1
, we recall in the next section the cluster representation of Hawkes processes, which is crucial to prove our result.

2
Cluster representation

The
cluster representation
, introduced by Hawkes and Oakes in 1974
[
8
]
, is a powerful tool to study Hawkes processes. In a few words, the cluster representation means that, under
Assumption

1
, a Hawkes process as introduced in
Definition

1
can be seen as a branching Poisson process on homogeneous Poisson processes with rates
\mu_{m}
for
m\in\bm{M}
. Points generated by the homogeneous Poisson processes are called immigrants points. Points generated by the branching Poisson processes are called offspring points. Each immigrant point may generate new points, called its children, due to the self exciting term of
eq.

1
. Then each child may also have some children and so on. These successive arrivals of offspring points, with its genealogy, is called a cluster. In the sequel we define more formally the clusters.

We use the Ulam Harris Neveu notation to label the individuals of trees. Let
\mathcal{U}=\cup_{n\in\mathds{N}}(\mathds{N}^{*})^{n}
with
(\mathds{N}^{*})^{0}=\{\varnothing\}
. A
{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{h})
-cluster with root of type
m_{0}\in\bm{M}
and born at time
t_{0}\in\mathds{R}
is defined by
\big((u,\tp(u),\bd(u))\big)_{u\in\mathcal{T}}
where

•

\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{U}
is a random tree, for
u\in\mathcal{T}
,
\tp(u)\in\bm{M}
is the type of
u
and
\bd(u)\in\mathds{R}
is the birth date of
u
.

•

\big((u,\tp(u))\big)_{u\in\mathcal{T}}
is a
{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{H})
-Galton Watson tree with root
\varnothing
of type
m_{0}
(ie
\tp(\varnothing)=m_{0}
). It means that independently for each point
u\in\mathcal{T}
, if we denote
X(u,\tp(u),m)
the number of children of
u
with type
m\in\bm{M}
, then for
m\in\bm{M}
the random variables
X(u,\tp(u),m)
are independent and
X(u,\tp(u),m)\sim{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{H}_{\tp(u),m})
for all
m\in\bm{M}
.

•

Conditionally to
\big((u,\tp(u))\big)_{u\in\mathcal{T}}
, the birth dates are distributed as follows. For the root of
\mathcal{T}
we have
\bd(\varnothing)=t_{0}
. Then, independently, for any
u,v\in\mathcal{T}
with
v
a child of
u
, we have

\bd(v)-\bd(u)\sim\frac{h^{\tp(v)}_{\tp(u)}(s)}{\displaystyle\int h^{\tp(v)}_{\tp(u)}(t)dt}\mathds{1}_{s>0}ds.

Finally, let
\pi^{m}
be independent Poisson processes on
\mathds{R}
with rates
\mu_{m}
for
m\in\bm{M}
. They are the
immigrant points
. Conditionally to
(\pi^{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}
, let
G^{m}_{x}
be independent
{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{h})
-clusters with root of type
m
and born at time
x
for
m\in\bm{M}
and
x\in\pi^{m}
. The following result is known
[
8
]
and provides the cluster representation for Hawkes processes.

Proposition 1
.

Under
Assumption

1
, if
(N^{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}
is a Hawkes process from

1

(N^{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}=\big(\{s\in\mathds{R}\mid\exists m^{\prime}\in\bm{M},\exists x\in\pi^{m^{\prime}},\exists u\in\mathcal{U}\ \text{such that}\ (u,m,s)\in G^{m^{\prime}}_{x}\}\big)_{m\in\bm{M}}

where the immigrants
(\pi^{m})_{m\in\bm{M}}
are independent Poisson point processes with rates
\bm{\mu}
.
Equivalently, in distribution,

is the collection of all the birth dates of any points of type

Proof of

1
.

To prove this lemma, one just has to remark the following inequality. For any sequence

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(y_{n}-1)\leq-1+\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}y_{n}.

Indeed, it suffices to denote

From this observation, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi G^{m}([s+nL,s+(n+1)L))\big)}\right]-1\right)

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(e^{\xi G^{m}([s+nL,s+(n+1)L))}-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathds{E}\left[\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{\xi G^{m}([s+nL,s+(n+1)L))}\right]-1

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi G^{m}([0,\infty))}\right]-1.

This is the desired result.

8
, we have for
t=\xi=u\xi_{r,K}
,

\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi G^{m}([0,\infty))}\right]\leq\left(\dfrac{1+r}{2r}\right)^{u}.

(9)

Finally, injecting
eq.

9
in
eq.

7
concludes the proof of
Theorem

1
.

3.1
Proof of
Lemma

2

It remains to prove
Lemma

2
. In
[
4
]
, the authors proved a similar result, stating that a
{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{H})
-Galton Watson tree has exponential moments if the spectral norm of
\bm{H}
is smaller than one.
Lemma

2
is stronger since we only need the spectral radius of
\bm{H}
to be smaller than one (which is equivalent to require that
\bm{H}\in\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
with some
r<1
). The proof is a bit more technical since we have to deal with the constant
K
, possibly
K>1
, from the property
\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
.

Proof of
Lemma

2
.

Let for
n\in\mathds{N}
and
m\in\bm{M}
,

g^{m}_{n}(t)=\log\left(\mathds{E}\left[e^{tZ^{m}_{n}}\right]\right)

where
Z^{m}_{n}
is the cardinal a
{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{H})
-Galton Watson tree with a root of type
m\in\bm{M}
and clipped at generation
n
.

The classical branching property of Galton Watson trees states the following. Given any point
u\in\mathcal{T}
, define the sub-tree
\mathcal{T}_{\geq u}
as the sub-tree of
\mathcal{T}
that contains
u
and all its offspring (children, grandchildren and so on). Then we have, conditionally to
(u\in\mathcal{T})
and the type of
u
, the random variables
\big((v,\tp(v))\big)_{v\in\mathcal{T}_{\geq u}}
and
\big((v,\tp(v))\big)_{v\in(\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\geq u})\cup\{u\}}
are independent and
\big((v,\tp(v))\big)_{v\in\mathcal{T}_{\geq u}}
is distributed as a
{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{H})
-Galton Watson tree with root
u
of type
\tp(u)\in\bm{M}
.

Applying the branching property to all the points of the first generation (the children of the root) leads, for all
n\geq 0
, to the following equality in distribution

Z^{m}_{n+1}=1+\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{X_{m,m^{\prime}}}Z^{m^{\prime},k}_{n}

where
X_{m,m^{\prime}}\sim{P}\!{o}\!{i}\!{s}(\bm{H}_{m,m^{\prime}})
and
Z^{m^{\prime},k}_{n}\sim Z^{m^{\prime}}_{n}
and the random variables
X_{m,m^{\prime}},\ Z^{m^{\prime},k}_{n}
for
m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}
,
k\geq 1
are all independent. Thus we obtain that for all
m\in\bm{M}
,

g_{n+1}^{m}(t)=t+\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}}\bm{H}_{mm^{\prime}}(e^{g^{m^{\prime}}_{n}(t)}-1).

One also has that

g_{0}^{m}(t)=t.

Define
u_{n}^{m}(t):=g_{n+1}^{m}(t)-g_{n}^{m}(t)\geq 0
and
u_{n}(t)
the vector with coordinates
u_{n}^{m}(t)
for
m\in\bm{M}
. Then

u_{0}^{m}(t)=(e^{t}-1)\sum_{m^{\prime}}\bm{H}_{mm^{\prime}},

which can be written

u_{0}(t)=(e^{t}-1)\bm{H}\bm{1}_{\bm{M}},

where
\bm{1}_{\bm{M}}
is the vector with only ones. Using the relation between
g_{n}
and
g_{n+1}
gives

\displaystyle u_{n+1}^{m}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}}\bm{H}_{mm^{\prime}}(e^{g^{m^{\prime}}_{n+1}(t)}-e^{g^{m^{\prime}}_{n}(t)})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}}\bm{H}_{mm^{\prime}}e^{g^{m^{\prime}}_{n+1}(t)}(g^{m^{\prime}}_{n+1}(t)-g^{m^{\prime}}_{n}(t))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\|g_{n+1}(t)\|_{\infty})\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}}\bm{H}_{mm^{\prime}}u^{m^{\prime}}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(t+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{\infty}\right)\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\bm{M}}\bm{H}_{mm^{\prime}}u^{m^{\prime}}_{n}(t),

which becomes

u_{n+1}(t)\leq\exp\left(t+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{\infty}\right)\times\bm{H}\ u_{n}(t).

Unfolding the previous equation gives

\displaystyle u_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[t+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{\infty}\right]\right)\times\bm{H}^{n}u_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=(e^{t}-1)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[t+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{\infty}\right]\right)\times\bm{H}^{n+1}\bm{1}_{\bm{M}}.

Let
0\leq t\leq\log(1/r)
and constants
K_{1}>0
,
r<r_{1}<1
that will be fixed later. We will prove by induction on
n
that
\|u_{n}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq tK_{1}r_{1}^{n}
if
t
is small enough (the precise condition is given at the end).

n=0:
Since
t\leq\log(1/r)
, we have
e^{t}-1\leq t/r
and by
\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
we have
\|\bm{H}\bm{1}_{\bm{M}}\|_{\infty}\leq Kr^{1}\times 1=Kr
. Thus if
K_{1}\geq K
it works.

0,1,\cdots,n-1\to n:
In this case we have on the one hand

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[t+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{\infty}\right]\leq nt\left(1+\dfrac{K_{1}}{1-r_{1}}\right).

On the other hand by
\bm{\Gel(}r,K\bm{)}
,

\|\bm{H}^{n+1}\bm{1}_{\bm{M}}\|_{\infty}\leq Kr^{n+1}.

Thus, since
e^{t}-1\leq t/r
,

\|u_{n}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq tK\exp\left(n\left[\log(r)+t\Big(1+\frac{K_{1}}{1-r_{1}}\Big)\right]\right).

The proof of the induction is complete if

\left\{\begin{aligned} &K_{1}\geq K\\
&\log(r)+t\Big(1+\frac{K_{1}}{1-r_{1}}\Big)\leq\log(r_{1})\ \Leftrightarrow\ t\leq\frac{\log(r_{1}/r)}{1+\frac{K_{1}}{1-r_{1}}}\end{aligned}\right.

Thus we set
K_{1}=K
and arbitrarily choose
r_{1}=(1+r)/2
. Thus the above bound for
t
is exactly
\xi_{r,K}
, leading to, for all
t\leq\xi_{r,K}
, for all
n\geq 0
, we have
\|u_{n}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq tKr_{1}^{n}
. Going back to
g_{n}(t)
leads to

\forall m\in\bm{M},\ \forall n\in\mathds{N},\ \forall t\leq\xi_{r,K},\ g^{m}_{n}(t)\leq t\Big(1+\frac{K}{1-r_{1}}\Big).

Letting
n\to\infty
gives the intended result by monotone convergence theorem.
∎

3.2
Proof of
Theorem

2

By following the steps of the proof of
Theorem

1
one obtains

\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi N(f)}\right]=\exp\left(\sum_{m\in\bm{M}}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[g(t,m,\xi,f)-1\big]\mu_{m}dt\right)

where

g(t,m,\xi,f)=\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi G^{m}(f\circ\tau_{t})}\right]

and
G^{m}
is a cluster born at time
t=0
and with root of type
m
and
\tau_{t}(x)=x+t,\ x\in\mathds{R}
.
Then we discretize the integral at scale
T
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[g(t,m,\xi,f)-1\big]dt=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in\mathds{Z}}\big[g(t+nT,m,\xi,f)-1\big]dt.

The same argument as the one for the proof of
Lemma

1
leads to

\sum_{n\in\mathds{Z}}\big[g(t+nT,m,\xi,f)-1\big]\leq\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi G^{m}(\sum_{n\in\mathds{Z}}f\circ\tau_{t+nT})}\right]-1.

Since
\sum_{n\in\mathds{Z}}f\circ\tau_{t+nT}\leq|f|^{\infty}_{1,T}
, it follows that

\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi N(f)}\right]\leq\exp\left(T\sum_{m\in\bm{M}}\mu_{m}\mathds{E}\left[e^{\xi|f|^{\infty}_{1,T}G^{m}(\mathds{R})}-1\right]\right).

Lemma

2
applied with
t=\xi|f|^{\infty}_{1,T}
concludes the proof of
Theorem

2
.

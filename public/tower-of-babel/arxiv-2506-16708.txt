The G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) Hecke-Baxter operator: principal series representations
A.A. Gerasimov, D.R. Lebedev, S.V. Oblezin

Abstract
. Previously introduced the G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) Hecke-Baxter operator is a one-parameter family of elements in the commutative spherical Hecke algebra ℋ ⁡ ( G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) , O ℓ + 1 ) \mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1}) . Its action on spherical vectors in spherical principle series representations of G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) is given by multiplication by the Archimedean L L -factors associated to these representations. In this note we propose an extension of the construction to other (non-spherical) G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) principle series representations providing a relevant generalization of the notions of spherical vector, commutative spherical Hecke algebra and the Hecke-Baxter operator to the general case. Action of the introduced Hecke-Baxter operator on the generalized spherical vectors is given by multiplication by the Archiemdean L L -factor associated to the corresponding principle series representation of G ​ L ℓ + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}) .



1
Introduction

The local Langlands correspondence associates to representations of
the Galois group of a local field
K
(or its proper substitute)
admissible representations of reductive Lie groups over
K
. In more
concrete terms one associates to an admissible
G(K)
-representation
a function in one complex variable
s
called local
L
-factor.
These functions capture invariants of the corresponding
representation of the Galois group of
K
. The simplest instance of
local
L
-factors appears in the product decomposition of the
(completed) Riemann zeta-function associated in this setup to
representation theory of the group
GL_{1}
over the local completions
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Starting with the Tate construction
[
T
]
for
GL_{1}

and further generalization to the case of
GL_{2}
in
[
JL
]
, the
corresponding
L
-factors are constructed in a rather indirect way
by invoking Fourier transformations of certain class of measures on
reductive Lie groups over local fields. Obviously, a more direct
approach would be desirable.

In
[
GLO08
]
we have considered this problem in the special case
of spherical principal series representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
.
We have proposed a one-parameter family of elements of the
commutative spherical Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
such that its action on uniquely
defined spherical vectors in spherical principal series
representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
is given by multiplication by
the corresponding local Archimedean
L
-factor. This construction
was motivated by considerations in the theory of quantum integrable
systems, specifically the
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-Toda chain
[
GLO08
]

(see also
[
G
]
).
Note that this construction naturally incorporates the fact that
local Archimedean
L
-factors have structure of
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
-torsors
(to fix a section of this torsor one should invoke global arithmetic
considerations).

In this note we propose an extension of the construction of the
Hecke-Baxter operator to the case of general (non-spherical)
principal series representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
. We start
with introducing special vectors in a principal series
representations generalizing in a sense the spherical vector to
the case of non-spherical principal series representations. Next, we
define a still commutative extension

\mathcal{H}_{r}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
of the standard spherical
Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
. Finally, we
introduce a one-parameter family of elements in

\mathcal{H}_{r}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
considered as a generalization
of the Hecke-Baxter operator proposed in
[
GLO08
]
. By
construction, the Hecke-Baxter operators for various values of the
parameter commute and thus one can look for common eigenvectors of
these operators in principal series representations. The main result
of this note is given by Theorem
5.1
claiming that the
action of the proposed Hecke-Baxter operator on the generalized
spherical vector in a principal series representation reduces to
multiplication by the corresponding local Archimedean
L
-factor.
Using the notion of the generalized spherical vector we introduce
the corresponding generalized spherical and Whittaker functions as
certain matrix elements in principal series representations. The
proven Theorem
5.1
entails that the action of the
Hecke-Baxter operator on these matrix elements is given by
multiplication the
L
-factors attached to the representations of

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Consideration of the non-spherical principal series reveals an
interesting subtlety in the definition of local Archimedean

L
-factors. The standard integral representation of
L
-factors is
given in terms of integrals over Gaussian type measures on
non-compact Lie groups. The fact that these measures are invariant
under Fourier transform plays a key role in deriving functional
equations for global zeta-functions constructed from local
contributions given by local
L
-factors. Thus it is natural to look
for a compatibility of the introduced Hecke-Baxter operator acting
uniformly in various principal series representations of

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
with the Fourier transform. It turns out that
the transformation properties are most simple if the corresponding
Gaussian measure is replaced by the Feynmann measure (or quadratic
character in terms of
[
Wei
]
). As usual, the latter is
considered as a limit of a certain complex measure of the Gaussian
type. This opens an interesting possibility to look at number
theoretic zeta-functions (and their local counterparts like local

L
-factors) in terms of a kind of Quantum Field Theory over
arithmetic varieties
[
Ma
]
. This is obviously an interesting
direction to pursue. A less ambitious direction (although, not quite
unrelated) would be to provide an interpretation of the extended
Hecke-Baxter operator in terms two-dimensional topological quantum
field theories on the disk (i.e. in terms of brane geometry) along
the line of
[
GLO11
]
. Finally let us stress an obvious
importance of the generalization of the proposed constructions to
other series of admissible representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
as
well as its counterparts for
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{C})
that we are going
to report elsewhere.

2
Spherical
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
Hecke-Baxter operator

In this Section we recall the known constructions of the spherical
Hecke algebras and the Hecke-Baxter operators for the spherical principal
series representations of the Lie group
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

[
GLO08
]
. This allows us to proceed in the following Section with a
generalization of these constructions to the case of non-spherical
(ramified) principal series representations.

In
[
GLO08
]
, the Hecke-Baxter operator was defined as a
one-parameter family of elements of the spherical Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1},O_{\ell+1})
associated with the Gelfand pair

(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
where
O_{\ell+1}
is the orthogonal
subgroup of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
. The spherical Hecke algebra is a
commutative associative algebra modeled on the appropriate subspace
of
O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant functions in

L^{1}\bigl(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)
with the algebra structure defined
by the convolution

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(f_{1}*f_{2})(\tilde{g})\,=\!\int\limits_{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!d\mu(g)\,\,f_{1}(g)\,\,f_{2}(g^{-1}\tilde{g})\,.\end{array}

Here
\mu(g)
is the Haar measure on
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d\mu(g)\,=\,|\det g|^{-(\ell+1)}\prod_{i,j=1}^{\ell+1}dg_{ij}\,,\end{array}

and conditions on the class of considered functions are imposed to
render the convolution operation to be defined.

Let
B\subset GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
be the Borel subgroup identified
with the subgroup of lower-triangular matrices. Define the following
character of
B
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi^{B}_{\epsilon,\gamma}(b)\,=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}{\mathop{\rm sign}}(b_{jj})^{\epsilon_{j}}\,|b_{jj}|^{\imath\gamma_{j}+\rho_{j}},\quad\gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\epsilon_{j}\in\{0,1\},\quad\rho_{j}=\frac{\ell}{2}+1-j\,,\end{array}

trivial on the unipotent radical
N\subset B
. Principal
series
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-representation

(\pi_{\epsilon,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,\gamma})
is the induced
representation

\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,\gamma}={\rm Ind}_{B}^{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\chi^{B}_{\epsilon,\gamma}

realized in the space of
B
-equivariant functions satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi(gb)=\chi^{B}_{\epsilon,\gamma}(b)\,\phi(g)\,,\qquad b\in B\,.\end{array}

The group
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,\gamma}

via the left action:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(\pi_{\epsilon,\gamma}(g)\,\phi\bigr)(\tilde{g})\,=\,\phi(g^{-1}\tilde{g})\,,\quad g,\tilde{g}\in GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\,.\end{array}

The representation

(\pi_{\epsilon,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,\gamma})
is irreducible for a
generic
\gamma
. The space

\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,\gamma}={\rm Ind}_{B}^{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\chi^{B}_{\epsilon,\gamma}

can be supplied with an invariant Hermitian form

\langle\,,\,\rangle
.

The principal series representation

(\pi_{\epsilon,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,\gamma})
is called spherical
if the corresponding character
\chi^{B}_{\epsilon,\gamma}
is
trivial on the subgroup
M=O_{\ell+1}\cap B
, or equivalently, if

\epsilon_{j}=0,\,1\leq j\leq\ell+1
in (
2
). Each spherical
representation contains a unique spherical vector
\phi_{0}

invariant under the action of the subgroup
O_{\ell+1}
and
normalized by the condition
\phi_{0}(1)=1
(see e.g
[
He
]
).

There are deep arithmetical reasons to associate to each principal
series representation
\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,\gamma}
a function in an
auxiliary variable
s\in\mathbb{C}
called the Archimedean
L
-factor (see
e.g.
[
Kna
]
and Appendix in
[
Ja
]
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s|\epsilon,\gamma)\,=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}\pi^{-\frac{s+\epsilon_{j}-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\epsilon_{j}-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}\right)\,.\end{array}

Notice that the
L
-factors associated to principal series

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-representations for
\epsilon\neq 0
enter product decomposition
of the
L
-factors associated with spherical principal series representations of the
complex group
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{C})
. For example the following
product relation holds
(see e.g. Appendix in
[
Ja
]
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L^{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{C})}(s|\gamma)=L(s|0,\gamma)\cdot L(s|\epsilon_{*},\gamma)\,,\qquad\epsilon_{*}=(1,\ldots,1)\,,\end{array}

generalizing the standard Legendre identity

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Gamma(s)\,=2^{s-1}\,\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}\Big)\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+1}{2}\Big)\,.\end{array}

It is useful to consider more general local Archimedean

L
-factors depending on
c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s,c|\epsilon,\gamma)\,=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}c^{-\frac{s+\epsilon_{j}-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\epsilon_{j}-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}\right)\,.\end{array}

The choice of
c=\pi
in (
2
) is dictated by global
arithmetic considerations which are not relevant (except the final
Section 7) to our local
considerations of the representation theory of Lie groups defined
over
\mathbb{R}
. Thus in the following we
consider (
2
) with some fixed non-specified
c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
, and
we use the simplified notation
L(s|\epsilon,\gamma)
.

For the spherical principal series representation

(\pi_{0,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma})
, consider
representation of the convolution algebra

\bigl(L^{1}\bigl(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\bigr),\,*\bigr)
in
\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma}

defined by the action via the translation
operators. Namely, for
f\in L^{1}\bigl(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)
we take

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f\cdot\phi\,:=\!\int\limits_{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!d\mu(g)\,\,f(g)\,\,\,\pi_{0,\gamma}(g)\cdot\phi\,,\qquad\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma}\,,\end{array}

provided the corresponding integral is convergent. In particular the
action is defined for elements of the spherical Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
represented by

O_{\ell+1}
-biinvariant functions on
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
. Uniqueness
of the spherical vector
\phi_{0}
in the spherical representation

(\pi_{0,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma})
gives rise to the action of
elements of
\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
on
\phi_{0}
via one-dimensional
representation
\Lambda
of the commutative algebra

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f\cdot\phi_{0}\,=\,\Lambda_{f}\,\phi_{0}\,,\quad f\in\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})\,,\quad\Lambda_{f}\in\mathbb{C}\,.\end{array}

Thus the spherical
vector
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma}
is a common

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
-eigenvector.

The Iwasawa decomposition of the Lie group
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
has the
following form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})\,=\,O_{\ell+1}\,AN\,,\end{array}

where for our choice of the Borel subgroup
B
,
A

is the group of the diagonal matrices with strictly positive
real entries. Let us introduce the corresponding product representation for element
of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}g=kan,\qquad g\in GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad k\in O_{\ell+1},\qquad a\in A,\qquad n\in N\,.\end{array}

Using (
2
) the spherical vector
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma}

might be written explicitly as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{0}(kan)\,=\,\chi^{B}_{\gamma}(an)\,=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}a_{j}^{\imath\gamma_{j}+\rho_{j}}\,.\end{array}

In the following we will use a more concise notation

\chi^{B}_{\gamma}(b):=\chi^{B}_{0,\gamma}(b)
.

In
[
GLO08
]
, we introduced a one-parameter family of elements

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{s}\in\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1}),\,\qquad s\in\mathbb{C}\,,\end{array}

such that its action on the spherical vector
\phi_{0}
in a spherical principal series

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-representation
(\pi_{0,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma})

reduces to multiplication by the corresponding
L
-factor
(
2
) with
\epsilon=0
. Such
Q_{s}
was called the Hecke-Baxter
operator associated to
(\pi_{0,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma})
.

Proposition 2.1

The spherical vector
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma}

is the eigenfunction of the
Hecke-Baxter operator acting on by convolution with the following
function on
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{s}(g)\,=\,(c\pi^{-1})^{\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\,|\det g|^{s+\frac{\ell}{2}}\,\,e^{-c{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,,\qquad c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\,,\qquad{\rm Re}(s)>0\,.\end{array}

The corresponding eigenvalue is given by the Archiemdean
L
-factor attached to
the representation
(\pi_{0,\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma})
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s|0,\gamma)\,=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}c^{-\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}\right)\,.\end{array}

Proof
. The function
Q_{s}(g)
is an element of

\mathcal{H}(GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{\ell+1})
, and thus according to
(
2
) the spherical vector (
2
) is its
eigenfunction with an eigenvalue
\Lambda(s|\gamma)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(Q_{s}*\phi_{0})(\tilde{g})\,=\int\limits_{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!d\mu(g)\,\,Q_{s}(g)\,\,\phi_{0}(g^{-1}\tilde{g})\,=\,\Lambda(s|\gamma)\,\,\phi_{0}(\tilde{g})\,.\end{array}

Taking into account the normalization condition
\phi_{0}(1)=1
for
the spherical vector (
2
) implies the following
integral expression for the eigenvalue

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda(s|\gamma)\,=\!\int\limits_{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!d\mu(g)\,\,Q_{s}(g^{-1})\,\,\phi_{0}(g)\,,\end{array}

where the unimodularity of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
is used.

Under the Iwasawa decomposition (
2
), the Haar measure
(
2
) on
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
allows the decomposition as a
product of the Haar measures on
A
,
O_{\ell+1}
and
N

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d\mu(g)=\delta_{N}(a)\,dk\times da\times dn\,,\qquad g=kan\,,\end{array}

where
\delta_{N}(a)=a^{-2\rho}
is the modular function of the
unipotent subgroup
N\subset B
. The corresponding Haar measures on the subgroups are
given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}dn=\prod_{i>j}^{\ell+1}dn_{ij},\qquad da=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell+1}\frac{da_{i}}{a_{i}}\,,\end{array}

and the Haar measure

dk
on
O_{\ell+1}
is normalized by the condition

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\int_{O_{\ell+1}}dk=1\,.\end{array}

Here we include the sum over connected
components of
O_{\ell+1}
into the integral. Applying
(
2
) we derive

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda(s|\gamma)\,=\!\int\limits_{O_{\ell+1}}\!dk\int\limits_{A}\!da\int\limits_{N}\!dn\,\,\delta_{N}(a)\,\phi_{0}(a)\,\,Q_{s}(n^{-1}a^{-1})\,.\end{array}

Substituting
(
2.1
)
and
taking into account (
2
) we
arrive at the following integral:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda(s|\gamma)\,=\,(\pi c^{-1})^{-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\!\int\limits_{A}\!da\,\,a^{-2\rho}\,\chi^{B}_{{\gamma}}(a)\,\,|\det a|^{-s-\frac{\ell}{2}}\\
\times\int\limits_{N}\!dn\,\,e^{-c{\rm Tr}\,(n^{\top}a^{-2}n)}\,.\end{array}

Next, we note that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Tr}\,\bigl(n^{\top}a^{-2}n\bigr)\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+1}a_{i}^{-2}\,+\,\sum_{i>j}n^{2}_{ij}a_{i}^{-2}\,.\end{array}

Then using the standard Gauss integral formula for the integration
over
n\in N
gives

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda(s|\gamma)\,=\!\int\limits_{A}\!da\,\,|\det a|^{-s-\frac{\ell}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}a_{j}^{\imath{\gamma}_{j}-\rho_{j}+\ell+1-j}\,e^{-\frac{c}{a_{i}^{2}}}\\
=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{da_{j}}{a_{j}}\,\,a_{j}^{s+\frac{\ell}{2}-\imath{\gamma}_{j}+\rho_{i}-\ell-1+j}\,\,e^{-ca_{j}^{2}}\,=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}c^{-\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}\right)\,,\end{array}

where in the latter equality we take into account the condition

{\mathop{\rm Re}}(s)>0
to apply the Euler
integral formula for the Gamma-function.
\Box

Now, let
N_{+}\subset GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
be the maximal unipotent
subgroup opposite to
N\subset B
and let
\chi^{N_{+}}:N_{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}

be a principal character. Define the Whittaker vector

\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma}
by the following condition:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{0,\gamma}(n)\cdot\psi=\chi^{N_{+}}(n)\,\psi\,,\qquad n\in N_{+}\,.\end{array}

By
[
Sha
]
, the Whittaker vector is unique in
\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma}
.
The
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant Hermitian pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle

on the spherical principal series representation

(\pi_{0,{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma})
allows to define the spherical and Whittaker
functions associated to the
principal series representation
(\pi_{0,{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{0,\gamma})
as the
following matrix elements:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{0,\gamma}(g)=\langle\phi_{0},\,\pi_{0,\gamma}(g)\,\phi_{0}\rangle\,,\qquad\Psi_{0,\gamma}(g)=\langle\phi_{0},\,\pi_{0,\gamma}(g)\,\psi\rangle\,.\end{array}

Corollary 2.1

The Hecke-Baxter operator acting on the spherical and Whittaker
functions (
2
) by convolution with the function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{s}(g)\,=\,(c\pi^{-1})^{\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\,|\det g|^{s+\frac{\ell}{2}}\,\,e^{-c{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,,\end{array}

has an eigenvalue given by the Archiemdean
L
-factor attached to
the representation

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s|0,\gamma)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell+1}c^{-\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\imath\gamma_{j}}{2}\right)\,.\end{array}

3
Generalized spherical vectors

In this Section we propose a generalization of the notion of
the spherical vector to the case of general principal series representations

(\pi_{\epsilon,{\gamma}},\mathcal{V}_{\epsilon,{\gamma}})
with
\epsilon\neq 0
.
Let us start with considering the finite group

M=O_{\ell+1}\cap B
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\ell+1}
. Explicitly
the group
M
may be identified with the subgroup of diagonal matrices
with entries in
\{\pm 1\}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}m={\rm diag}\bigl((-1)^{\alpha_{1}},\cdots,(-1)^{\alpha_{\ell+1}}\bigr),\qquad\alpha_{i}\in\{0,1\}\,.\end{array}

Irreducible representations of
M
are one-dimensional and
may be parameterized by the set
\mathcal{P}
of signatures

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{\ell+1})\,,\qquad\epsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}\,.\end{array}

For each
\epsilon\in\mathcal{P}
, the corresponding one-dimensional

M
-representation
\chi^{M}_{\epsilon}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi^{M}_{\epsilon}(m)=(-1)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell+1}\alpha_{i}\epsilon_{i}},\end{array}

for
m\in M
represented in the form (
3
).
For a signature

\epsilon\in\mathcal{P}
, define the module
|\epsilon|
by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}|\epsilon|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+1}\epsilon_{i}\,\,.\end{array}

Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{\ell+1}
be the standard irreducible representation of

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
and let
\{e_{i},\,1\leq i\leq\ell+1\}\subset V
be
an orthonormal basis,
(e_{i},\,e_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. Let
(\pi_{k},\,W_{k})

be the fundamental representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
in the spaces

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}W_{k}\,=\,\wedge^{k}V=\wedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{\ell+1},\quad k=0,1,\ldots,\ell+1\,,\end{array}

so in particular,
W_{1}=V
, and
W_{0}=\wedge^{0}V\simeq\mathbb{C}
is the
trivial representation. For each
k=1,\ldots,\ell+1
, choose an
orthonormal basis of poly-vectors in
W_{k}

parameterized by the signatures
\epsilon
with
|\epsilon|=k

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{\epsilon}\,=\,(k!)^{1/2}\cdot e_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{\ell+1}^{\epsilon_{\ell+1}}\,,\qquad(v_{\epsilon},\,v_{\epsilon^{\prime}})=\delta_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\,,\qquad|\epsilon|=|\epsilon^{\prime}|=k\,,\end{array}

where we use the following convention

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\,:=\,\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{{\sigma}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}v_{{\sigma}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{{\sigma}(n)}\,.\end{array}

Then in the basis (
3
), the action of
M\subset O_{\ell+1}
in
W_{k}
has a simple form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{k}(m)\,v_{\epsilon}\,=\,\chi^{M}_{\epsilon}(m)\,v_{\epsilon}\,,\qquad m\in M\,.\end{array}

The basis (
3
) is uniquely determined as the eigenbasis
in each
W_{k}
diagonalizing the
M
-action. Combining
(
3
) for all
0\leq k\leq\ell+1
results in the basis

\{v_{\epsilon},\,\epsilon\in\mathcal{P}\}
of the (reducible)

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-representation
(\pi_{W},W)
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}W\,=\,\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\ell+1}W_{k}\,,\end{array}

with the
M
-action given by (
3
). We may identify
W

with the regular representation of
M
. In the following we will
use the diagonal matrix elements in representation
W
. These matrix
elements might be expressed in terms of the principal minors

\Delta_{\epsilon}(g)
of the matrix
g
with the rows and columns
corresponding to non-zero

6
Fixing ambiguities via Fourier transform

In the main part of this note we have considered various
representation theory constructions associated to Lie group

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
. There is a kind of intrinsic arbitrariness in
these constructions manifested in particular in the existence of
the parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
entering expressions of the local Archimedean

L
-factors (
2
),(
2
) and of the Hecke-Baxter operators
(
2.1
),(
5.1
). One might fix this ambiguity by taking into
account the considerations related with the theory of global
zeta-functions encompassing the information about different
completions of rational numbers (ultimately this reduces to invoking
integral structure on the reals). Precisely we require the
existence of simple functional equations for the global zeta functions
constructed as a product of local factors. In terms of the local
Archimedean
L
-factors this, in particular, favours simple transformation
properties for the Gaussian measure,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d{\gamma}(g)=e^{-c{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,dg\,,\qquad dg\,=\prod_{i,j=1}^{\ell+1}dg_{ij}\,,\end{array}

entering the integral expression for the local Archiemdean

L
-factor. For instance, local Archimedean
L
-factor (
2
)
for the spherical principal series representation

(\pi_{0,0},\mathcal{V}_{0,0})
induced via the
B
-character

\chi^{B}_{0}
reads from (
2

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L(s|0)\,=\,(c\pi^{-1})^{\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\!\int\limits_{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!d\mu(g)\,\,|\det g|^{s+\frac{\ell}{2}}\,e^{-c{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\\
=\,(c\pi^{-1})^{\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\!\int\limits_{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!d{\gamma}(g)\,\,|\det g|^{s-\frac{\ell}{2}-1}\,,\end{array}

where

is the Haar measure (
2
) on
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d\mu(g)\,=\,|\det(g)|^{-(\ell+1)}\,dg\,.\end{array}

Consider the Fourier transform on
{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(\mathcal{F}f)(\tilde{g})\,=\!\!\int\limits_{{\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!dg\,\,\,e^{2\pi\imath\,{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}\tilde{g})}\,f(g)\,.\end{array}

This is a unitary operator on

L^{2}\bigl({\rm Mat}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R}),dg\bigr)
.

Lemma 6.1

The Gaussian measure (
6
) with
c=\pi
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d{\gamma}(g)\,=\,G(g)\,dg\,=\,e^{-\pi{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,dg\,,\end{array}

is self-dual with respect to the Fourier transform (
6
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(\mathcal{F}G)(g)\,=\,G(g)\,.\end{array}

Proof
. The assertion directly follows from the standard integral
identity:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\!dx\,\,e^{2\pi\imath yx\,-\,\pi x^{2}}\,=\,e^{-\pi y^{2}}\,.\end{array}

\Box

Therefore, it seems natural to specify
c=\pi
in expressions for
the local Archimedean
L
-factors (
2

the proposed construction of the non-spherical Hecke-Baxter operator
implies another choice. By Theorem
5.1
, the non-spherical Hecke-Baxter operator is given by the convolution with the function (
5.1
),

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\widehat{Q}_{s}(g)\,=\,\Delta_{W}(g)\,(c\pi^{-1})^{\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\,|\det g|^{s+\frac{\ell}{2}}\,e^{-c\,{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta_{W}(g)\,=\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{P}}d_{|\epsilon|}\,(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}(g)\,v_{\epsilon})\,,\end{array}

hence a natural requirement to impose would be to have simple
transformations properties with respect to
Fourier transform of the modified Gaussian measure

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d{\gamma}_{W}(g)\,=\,G_{W}(g)\,dg\,=\,\Delta_{W}(g)\,(c\pi^{-1})^{\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\,e^{-c\,{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,dg\,.\end{array}

The choice of

leads however to a non-trivial

Lemma 6.2

The modified Gaussian measure (
6
) with
c=\pi

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d{\gamma}_{W}(g)\,=\,G_{W}(g)\,dg\,=\,\Delta_{W}(g)\,e^{-\pi{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,dg\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(\mathcal{F}G_{W})(g)\,=\,\tilde{G}_{W}(g)\,,\\

Proof
. The assertion follows by the simple identities:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{F}(G)(x)=G(x)\,,\qquad G(x)=e^{-\pi x^{2}}\,,\\
\mathcal{F}(G^{(1)})(x)=\imath G^{(1)}(x),\qquad G^{(1)}(x)=xe^{-\pi x^{2}}\,,\end{array}

where the later identity easily follows from the former one via

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}G^{(1)}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G(x)\,.\end{array}

Precisely, we should take into account that
\Delta_{W}(g)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta_{W}(g)=\sum_{I_{k}}\,d_{k}\,\,\Delta_{I_{k}}(g)\,,\quad I_{k}=(i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}),\,\,1\leq i_{a}\leq(\ell+1)\,,\end{array}

is a sum of principal
k
-minors

the r.h.s. of (
6

g_{ij}
enters the product in a power at most one.
Therefore, applying the integral identity(
6

variable
g_{ij}
yields the identity (
6.2
).
\Box

Thus, the modified Gauss measure
d{\gamma}_{W}(g)
transforms non-trivially under Fourier
transform. To resolve this conundrum one should recall that the requirement for
the real measure
d{\gamma}_{W}(g)
to be self-dual with respect to Fourier transform may be
weaken to the condition on a complex measure to be self-dual under a combination of the
Fourier transform and complex conjugation. This leads to considering
the following Feynmann type measure (understood as usual as a
limit of a well-defined complex measure).

Proposition 6.1

The modified Feynmann (imaginary Gaussian) measure

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}d\,\widetilde{{\gamma}}_{W}(g)\,=\,\widetilde{G}_{W}(g)\,dg\,=\,\Delta_{W}(g)\,(c\pi^{-1})^{\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{4}}\,e^{-\imath\pi\,{\rm Tr}\,(g^{\top}g)}\,dg\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(\mathcal{F}\,\widetilde{G}_{W})(g)\,=\,e^{-\frac{\imath\pi(\ell+1)^{2}}{4}}\,\,\overline{\widetilde{G}_{W}(g)}\,.\end{array}

Proof
. This directly follows from the previous Lemma and
the following simple computations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(\mathcal{F}\,\widetilde{G})(x)=e^{-\frac{\imath\pi}{4}}\,\overline{\widetilde{G}(x)},\qquad\widetilde{G}(x)=e^{-\imath\pi x^{2}},\\
(\mathcal{F}\,\widetilde{G}^{(1)})(x)=e^{-\frac{\imath\pi}{4}}\,\overline{\widetilde{G}^{(1)}(x)},\qquad\widetilde{G}^{(1)}(x)=xe^{-\imath\pi x^{2}}\,.\end{array}

To calculate the Fourier transform we use deformations of the
basic functions by
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\widetilde{G}(g|\varepsilon)=e^{-(\varepsilon+\imath\pi)x^{2}},\qquad\widetilde{G}^{(1)}(g|\varepsilon)=xe^{-(\varepsilon+\imath\pi)x^{2}},\qquad\varepsilon\to 0_{+}\,,\end{array}

to render the integrals well-defined.
\Box

Arithmetic implications of replacing the
Gaussian quadratic measure by the Feynmann one is an interesting direction to pursue.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{\epsilon,\epsilon}(s|\gamma)=\,\,\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}^{M}|}\,\int\limits_{O_{\ell+1}\times A}\!\!dk_{1}\,da\,|\Delta(a)|\,Q_{s}(a)\,\chi^{B}_{\gamma}\bigl(a((k_{1}a)^{-1})\bigr)\\
\times\,(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}\bigl(k_{1}a\,k((k_{1}a)^{-1})\bigr)\,v_{\epsilon})\,,\end{array}

i.e. the
\epsilon
-spherical vector
\phi_{\epsilon}

is a
\widehat{Q}_{s}
-eigenfunction. To calculate the
eigenvalue
\Lambda_{\epsilon,\epsilon}
in (
7
) we use the following
identity

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}k_{1}a\cdot k((k_{1}a)^{-1})\,=\,n((k_{1}a)^{-1})^{-1}\cdot a((k_{1}a)^{-1})^{-1}\,,\end{array}

which gives rise to the following expression for the matrix element in
(
7
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}\bigl(k_{1}a\,k((k_{1}a)^{-1})\bigr)\,v_{\epsilon})\,=\,\bigl(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}\bigl(n((k_{1}a)^{-1})^{-1}\,a((k_{1}a)^{-1})^{-1}\bigr)\,v_{\epsilon}\bigr)\\
=\,\chi^{B}_{-\imath\epsilon}(a((k_{1}a)^{-1}))\,\bigl(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}\bigl(n((k_{1}a)^{-1})^{-1}\bigr)\,v_{\epsilon}\bigr)\,.\end{array}

Hence, we obtain the following expression for the eigenvalue:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{\epsilon,\epsilon}(s|\gamma)\,=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}^{M}|}\,\int\limits_{O_{\ell+1}\times A}\!\!dk_{1}\,da\,|\Delta(a)|\,Q_{s}(a)\,\,\chi^{B}_{\gamma-\imath\epsilon}\bigl(a((k_{1}a)^{-1})\bigr)\\
\times\,(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}\bigl(n((k_{1}a)^{-1})^{-1}\bigr)\,v_{\epsilon})\,.\end{array}

This can be equivalently written as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{\epsilon,\epsilon}(s|\gamma)\,=\,\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}^{M}|}\!\int\limits_{O_{\ell+1}\times A\times O_{\ell+1}}\!\!dk_{1}\,da\,dk_{2}\,\,|\Delta(a)|\,Q_{s}(a)\,\,\chi^{B}_{\gamma-\imath\epsilon}\bigl(a((k_{1}ak_{2})^{-1})\bigr)\\
\times\,(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}\bigl(n((k_{1}ak_{2})^{-1})^{-1}\bigr)\,v_{\epsilon})\,,\end{array}

and therefore we arrive at the following integral representation for
the eigenvalue

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{\epsilon,\epsilon}(s|\gamma)\,=\!\!\int\limits_{GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})}\!\!d\mu(g)\,\,Q_{s}(g)\,\,\chi_{\gamma-\imath\epsilon}^{B}(g^{-1})(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}(n(g^{-1}))\,v_{\epsilon})\,.\end{array}

Finally using the Iwasawa decomposition
g^{-1}=kan
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{\epsilon,\epsilon}(s|\gamma)\,=\!\!\int\limits_{O_{\ell+1}\times A\times N}\!\!dk\,da\,dn\,\,Q_{s}(g)\,\,\chi^{B}_{\gamma-\imath\epsilon}(a)\,(v_{\epsilon},\,\pi_{W}(n)\,v_{\epsilon})\\
=\!\!\int\limits_{O_{\ell+1}\times A\times N}\,dk\,da\,dn\,\,Q_{s}(g)\,\,\chi^{B}_{\gamma-\imath\epsilon}(a)\,.\end{array}

The latter integral is already calculated in the
proof of Proposition
2.1
and the result is in agreement with
(
5.1
).

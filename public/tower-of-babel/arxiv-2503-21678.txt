Introduction

In recent years, stochastic ordering tools were successfully applied to obtain new results in the theory of functional inequalities; see, for example,
[

,
[
7
]
for the application of the Ohlin lemma and
[

,
[

]
for the application of Levin-Stechkin theorem. In addition, the orderings involving higher-order convex functions (see
[
4
]
) were used among others in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
.
See also
[
6
]
for recent applications of higher-order convex orderings in economics and
[
3
]
for the application in queueing systems.

In this paper, we will use the notion of
n
-increasing (
n
-decreasing) function instead of
n
-convex (
n
-concave) since we intend to cover the cases of
f
being positive (negative) or increasing (decreasing) as well.
To this end, we need to recall the notion of higher-order divided differences. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a nonempty open interval throughout this paper. Then, for a function
f\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and for all pairwise distinct elements
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\in I
, we define

f[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]:=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{f(x_{j})}{\prod_{k=0,k\neq j}^{n}(x_{j}-x_{k})},

which is called the
n
th-order divided difference
for
f
at
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
. It is easy to see that the
n
th-order divided difference is a symmetric function of its arguments.
We say that
f
is
n
-increasing
if, for all pairwise distinct elements
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\in I
, we have that
f[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]\geq 0
. Observe that
0
-increasingness means nonnegativity,
1
-increasingness is equivalent to increasingness, and
2
-increasingness coincides with convexity. Analogously, we say
that
f
is
n
-decreasing
if
(-f)
is
n
-increasing, i.e., for all pairwise distinct elements
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\in I
, we have that
f[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]\leq 0
. In this case,
0
-decreasingness means nonpositivity,
1
-decreasingness is equivalent to decreasingness, and
2
-decreasingness coincides with concavity.

In the following theorems, we present the characterizations of
n
-increasingness due to Popoviciu
[
11
]
, which may also be found in Kuczma’s book
[
5
]
stated as Theorems 15.8.4, 15.8.5, and 15.8.6, respectively.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. Then a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is
n
-increasing if and only if it is
(n-2)
-times continuously differentiable and
f^{(n-2)}
is convex on
I
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be
(n-1)
-times continuously differentiable. Then
f
is
n
-increasing if and only if
f^{(n-1)}
is increasing on
I
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be
n
-times continuously differentiable. Then
f
is
n
-increasing if and only if
f^{(n)}
is nonnegative on
I
.

Furthermore, given
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the symbols
M_{n^{+}}(I)
and
M_{n^{-}}(I)
will represent the set of all
n
-increasing and
n
-decreasing functions defined on
I
, respectively.

Let now
X,Y
be two random variables that take values in
I.
We say that
X
is smaller than
Y
in the
n
-increasing order if, for all
f\in M_{n^{+}}(I)
,

(1)

\mathbb{E}(f(X))\leq\mathbb{E}(f(Y))

provided that the expectations exist (see
[
14
]
).

In
[
14
]
and also in
[
12
]
, one can find necessary and sufficient conditions for the
n
-increasing order.
That is, (
1
) is satisfied if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied.

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{E}X^{k}=\mathbb{E}Y^{k}&k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}\\
\mathbb{E}(X-t)_{+}^{n-1}\leq\mathbb{E}(Y-t)_{+}^{n-1}&t\in I.\end{array}\right.

Note that here and in the sequel, the symbols
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
represent the negative and positive part of a real number
u
, respectively, defined as

u_{-}:=\max(0,-u)=\frac{|u|-u}{2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u_{+}:=\max(0,u)=\frac{|u|+u}{2}.

If, however, condition (
2
) is not fulfilled, the class of functions satisfying the inequality (
1
) is not known. We give a simple example of such a situation.

Example 1
.

For a function
f\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the functional inequality

(3)

3f\left(\frac{3x+y}{4}\right)+3f\left(\frac{x+3y}{4}\right)\leq f(x)+4f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)+f(y)

which is supposed to be valid for all
x,y\in I
with
x<y
.
At the end of the introduction, we will show that every continuous solution of (
3
) has to be
2
-increasing, that is, convex. On the other hand, we prove now that convexity is not sufficient for the validity of (
3
). Indeed, dividing the inequality by 6 side by side, we can rewrite it in the form (
1
). Let
x,y\in I
with
x<y
be fixed and consider the random variables
X
and
Y
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X=\tfrac{3x+y}{4})=\mathbb{P}(X=\tfrac{x+3y}{4})=\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Y=x)=\mathbb{P}(Y=y)=\frac{1}{6},\quad\mathbb{P}(Y=\tfrac{x+y}{2})=\frac{2}{3}.

Then, the normalized form of the inequality (
1
) is equivalent to (
3
).
The first moments of
X
and
Y
are equal to
\frac{x+y}{2}
. Therefore, the first condition in (
2
) holds for
n=2
. On the other hand, we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl(X-\frac{x+y}{2}\Bigr)_{+}=\frac{1}{8}(y-x)>\frac{1}{12}(y-x)=\mathbb{E}\Bigl(Y-\frac{x+y}{2}\Bigr)_{+}.

This shows that the second condition in (
2
) is violated. Therefore, we can conclude that not all convex functions satisfy (
3
). (In particular, the convex function
u\mapsto\big(u-\frac{x+y}{2}\big)_{+}
does not
satisfy (
3
).)

This paper aims to provide tools that can be used to deal with inequalities of that kind. The inspiration for our approach may again be found in the monograph

[
14
]
, where it is mentioned (Theorem 4.A.2) that the inequality (
1
) holds for all increasing and convex functions
f
, i.e., for all
f\in M_{1^{+}}(I)\cap M_{2^{+}}(I)
, if and only if

\mathbb{E}\bigl((X-t)_{+}\bigr)\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl((Y-t)_{+}\bigr)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

As we can see, even if an inequality is not satisfied by all functions that are monotone of some order, it may be satisfied if we add additional monotonicity properties of some different orders.

We will collect all terms to one side of the inequality and consider only a bounded and signed measure
\mu
, defined on the Borel subsets of the interval
[0,1]
. Thus, our main purpose is to study the functional inequality

(4)

\int_{[0,1]}f\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)d\mu(t)\geq 0,\qquad x,y\in I\mbox{ with }x<y,

for a continuous unknown function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
,
and to derive necessary as well as sufficient conditions for its validity in terms of higher-order monotonicity properties of
f
.

In order to formulate our main results, for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and for a given bounded and signed measure
\mu
on the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel subsets of
[0,1]
, we introduce the functions

\mu_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and

\mu^{-}_{k},\mu_{k}^{+}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{k}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\int_{[0,1]}(t-\tau)^{k}d\mu(t),

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{-}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\int_{[0,\tau)}(t-\tau)^{k}d\mu(t),

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{+}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\int_{(\tau,1]}(t-\tau)^{k}d\mu(t).

Here, in the definition of
\mu_{k}
, when
k=0
and
t=\tau
, we adopt the convention
0^{0}:=1
.
Observe that
\mu_{k}^{-}(0)=\mu_{k}^{+}(1)=0
. Furthermore,
\mu_{k}(0)
equals the
k
th-order moment of the measure
\mu
. A relationship among these functions will be established in Lemma
5
in the next section.

The main result of
[
2
]
is recalled in the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mu
be a nonzero bounded signed Borel measure on
[0,1]
.
Assume that
n
is the smallest non-negative integer such that
\mu_{n}(0)\neq 0.
If
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function satisfying the integral inequality
(
4
), then
\mu_{n}(0)\cdot f
is
n
-increasing.

Remark 1
.

Considering a measure

\mu=\delta_{0}-3\delta_{\frac{1}{4}}+4\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}-3\delta_{\frac{3}{4}}+\delta_{1},

we may rewrite inequality (
3
) in the form (
4
). Since we have

\mu_{0}(0)=\mu_{1}(0)=0
and
\mu_{2}(0)=\frac{7}{16},
according to Theorem
4
, we find that every continuous solution of (
3
) is a
2
-increasing (i.e., a convex) function.
As we remember, not all convex functions satisfy (
3
). Thus, we need to find some additional conditions to the convexity to obtain a class of functions that satisfy (
3
). We will return to this inequality in the last part of the paper.

2.
Auxiliary results

The following lemma establishes a recursive formula for the sequences of functions
(\mu_{k}^{-})_{k\geq 0}
and
(\mu_{k}^{+})_{k\geq 0}
. In what follows, the characteristic function of a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
will be denoted by

\chi_{A}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mu
be a nonzero bounded signed Borel measure on
[0,1]
. Then, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tau\in[0,1]
, we have

(5)

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{-}(\tau)

\displaystyle=-k\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu_{k-1}^{-}(t)dt,

(6)

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{+}(\tau)

\displaystyle=k\int_{\tau}^{1}\mu_{k-1}^{+}(t)dt.

Proof.

We are going to prove the equality (
5
) for
k=1
and for
\tau\in[0,1]
. Applying Fubini’s theorem and the Newton–Leibniz formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu_{0}^{-}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\int_{[0,t)}1d\mu(s)\bigg)dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\int_{[0,1]}\chi_{[0,t)}(s)d\mu(s)\bigg)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\tau}\chi_{[0,t)}(s)dt\bigg)d\mu(s)=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\tau}\chi_{(s,1]}(t)dt\bigg)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}(\tau-s)_{+}\mu(s)=\int_{[0,\tau)}(s-\tau)\mu(s)=-\mu_{1}^{-}(\tau).

For
k>1
and for
\tau\in[0,1]
, using Fubini’s theorem again, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu_{k-1}^{-}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\int_{[0,t)}(s-t)^{k-1}d\mu(s)\bigg)dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\int_{[0,1]}(-(t-s)_{+})^{k-1}d\mu(s)\bigg)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\tau}(-(t-s)_{+})^{k-1}dt\bigg)d\mu(s)=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg[-\frac{1}{k}(-(t-s)_{+})^{k}\bigg]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(-\frac{1}{k}(-(\tau-s)_{+})^{k}+\frac{1}{k}(-(0-s)_{+})^{k}\bigg)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}-\frac{1}{k}(-(\tau-s)_{+})^{k}d\mu(s)=-\int_{[0,\tau)}-\frac{1}{k}(s-\tau)^{k}d\mu(s)=-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{k}^{+}(\tau),

which completes the proof of the equality (
5
) for
k>1
.

To prove the equality (
6
) for
k=1
and for
\tau\in[0,1]
, using Fubini’s theorem and the Newton–Leibniz formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{1}\mu_{0}^{+}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{1}\bigg(\int_{(t,1]}1d\mu(s)\bigg)dt=\int_{\tau}^{1}\bigg(\int_{[0,1]}\chi_{(t,1]}(s)d\mu(s)\bigg)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(\int_{\tau}^{1}\chi_{(t,1]}(s)dt\bigg)d\mu(s)=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(\int_{\tau}^{1}\chi_{[0,s)}(t)dt\bigg)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}(s-\tau)_{+}\mu(s)=\int_{(\tau,1]}(s-\tau)\mu(s)=\mu_{1}^{+}(\tau).

For
k>1
and for
\tau\in[0,1]
, using Fubini’s theorem again, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{1}\mu_{k-1}^{+}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{1}\bigg(\int_{(t,1]}(s-t)^{k-1}d\mu(s)\bigg)dt=\int_{\tau}^{1}\bigg(\int_{[0,1]}(s-t)_{+}^{k-1}d\mu(s)\bigg)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(\int_{\tau}^{1}(s-t)_{+}^{k-1}dt\bigg)d\mu(s)\ =\int_{[0,1]}\bigg[-\frac{1}{k}(s-t)_{+}^{k}\bigg]_{t=\tau}^{t=1}d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(-\frac{1}{k}(s-1)_{+}^{k}+\frac{1}{k}(s-\tau)_{+}^{k}\bigg)d\mu(s)=\int_{[0,1]}\frac{1}{k}(s-\tau)_{+}^{k}d\mu(s)=\frac{1}{k}\mu_{k}^{+}(\tau),

which completes the proof of the equality (
6
) for
k>1
.
∎

Before presenting sufficient conditions, we will need a lemma connected to a smoothing technique that will be used later. We use here a similar approach to that used in the paper
[
2
]
. Namely, let

h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a fixed nonnegative function of class
C^{\infty}
such that
h(u)=0
for
u\not\in(-1,1)
and

\int_{-1}^{1}h(t)dt=1.

Then, for a given
\varepsilon>0
, define the function
h_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

h_{\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}h\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a given continuous function. Put
I_{\varepsilon}=(I-\varepsilon)\cap(I+\varepsilon)
and define
f_{\varepsilon}:I_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
as the convolution of
f
with
h
as follows

(7)

f_{\varepsilon}(t):=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}f(t-s)h(s)ds,\qquad t\in I_{\varepsilon}.

It is well-known (see, for example,
[
18
,
\mathsection
18.14]
) that such function is of the class
C^{\infty}
and tends to
f
as
\varepsilon
tends to zero (moreover, the convergence is uniform) on any compact subinterval of the interior of
I
. In the following lemma, we will establish that
f_{\varepsilon}
also inherits the monotonicity properties of the function
f.

Lemma 6
.

Let
f\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. If
f
is
n
-increasing on
I
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, then, for all
\varepsilon>0
, the function
f_{\varepsilon}
given by (
7
) is
n
-increasing on
I_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Assume that
f
is
n
-increasing on
I
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Let
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
be pairwise distinct elements of
I_{\varepsilon}
.
Then, by the
n
-increasingness of
f
, for all
s\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, we have that

f[x_{0}-s,\dots,x_{n}-s]\geq 0.

Therefore, using the defining formula of
n
th-order divided differences, the definition of
f_{\varepsilon}
and the nonnegativity of
h_{\varepsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{f_{\varepsilon}(x_{i})}{\prod_{j=0,j\neq i}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{j})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=0,j\neq i}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{j})}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}f(x_{i}-s)h_{\varepsilon}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{f(x_{i}-s)}{\prod_{j=0,j\neq i}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{j})}h_{\varepsilon}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{f(x_{i}-s)}{\prod_{j=0,j\neq i}^{n}\bigl((x_{i}-s)-(x_{j}-s)\bigr)}h_{\varepsilon}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}f[x_{0}-s,\dots,x_{n}-s]h_{\varepsilon}(s)dv\geq 0.

This completes the proof of the
n
-increasingness of
f_{\varepsilon}
on
I_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

3.
Sufficient conditions for the inequality (
4
)

The next theorem presents one of the main results of this paper.

Theorem 7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mu
be a bounded signed Borel measure on
[0,1]
, and let
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
Assume that
f\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function possessing the following properties:

(a)

For all
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
, the function

\mu_{k}(\lambda)\cdot f
is
k
-increasing.

(b)

We have one of the following two possibilities:

(i)

Either
f
is
n
-increasing and the following inequalities hold:

\qquad\qquad\mu_{n-1}^{-}(\tau)\leq 0\mbox{ for }\tau\in[0,\lambda)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{n-1}^{+}(\tau)\geq 0\mbox{ for }\tau\in(\lambda,1],

(ii)

or
f
is
n
-decreasing and the following inequalities hold:

\qquad\qquad\mu_{n-1}^{-}(\tau)\geq 0\mbox{ for }\tau\in[0,\lambda)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{n-1}^{+}(\tau)\leq 0\mbox{ for }\tau\in(\lambda,1].

Then
f
satisfies the inequality (
4
).

Proof.

Take
x<y
from the interior of
I
. First we assume that
f\in C^{\infty}([x,y]).

Then, using Taylor’s theorem at the point
p:=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y
with the integral remainder term, for every
u\in[x,y]
, we have

f(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(p)}{k!}(u-p)^{k}+\int_{p}^{u}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}(u-s)^{n-1}ds.

Therefore, substituting
u:=(1-t)x+ty
into the above equality and then integrating it with respect to
\mu
, we get

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}f\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(p)}{k!}\int_{[0,1]}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-p\bigr)^{k}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{[0,1]}\left(\int_{p}^{(1-t)x+ty}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\right)d\mu(t).

Let
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
be fixed. Then

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}\bigl((1-t)x

\displaystyle+ty-p\bigr)^{k}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\bigr)^{k}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=(y-x)^{k}\int_{[0,1]}(t-\lambda)^{k}d\mu(t)=(y-x)^{k}\cdot\mu_{k}(\lambda).

Combining condition
(a)
with Theorem
3
, we obtain that

\mu_{k}(\lambda)\cdot f^{(k)}(p)\geq 0.

Thus, we conclude that

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(p)}{k!}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-p\bigr)^{k}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(p)}{k!}(y-x)^{k}\cdot\mu_{k}(\lambda)\geq 0.

To obtain the nonnegativity of the second term on the right-hand side of (
8
), we will use condition
(b)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that condition
(i)
holds. Then, by Theorem
3
, we have that
f^{(n)}
is nonnegative on
I
. We split the second term on the right-hand side of (
8
) as follows:

(10)

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{p}^{(1-t)x+ty}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\right)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\lambda]}\left(\int_{p}^{(1-t)x+ty}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\right)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{(\lambda,1]}\left(\int_{p}^{(1-t)x+ty}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\right)d\mu(t).

Now, we compute the two integrals on the right-hand side separately.
For
t\in[0,\lambda]
, we have that
x\leq(1-t)x+ty\leq(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y=p
, therefore, by using Fubini’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{[0,\lambda]}

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{p}^{(1-t)x+ty}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\bigg)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\lambda]}\left(-\int_{(1-t)x+ty}^{p}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\right)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{[0,\lambda]}\left(\int_{x}^{p}\chi_{((1-t)x+ty,p]}(s)\cdot\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\right)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{p}\left(\int_{[0,\lambda]}\chi_{((1-t)x+ty,p]}(s)\cdot\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}d\mu(t)\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{p}\bigg(\int_{[0,\frac{s-x}{y-x})}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}d\mu(t)\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{p}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}(y-x)^{n-1}\bigg(-\int_{\left[0,\frac{s-x}{y-x}\right)}\left(t-\frac{s-x}{y-x}\right)^{n-1}d\mu(t)\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{p}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}(y-x)^{n-1}\cdot(-1)\cdot\mu_{n-1}^{-}\left(\frac{s-x}{y-x}\right)ds\geq 0.

To see that the last inequality is valid, observe that, for

, the ratio
\tau:=\frac{s-x}{y-x}
belongs to
[0,\lambda)
and hence, by condition
(i)
, we have that
\mu_{n-1}^{-}(\tau)\leq 0
.

For
t\in(\lambda,1]
, we have that
p=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y<(1-t)x+ty\leq y
; therefore, by using Fubini’s theorem again, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{(\lambda,1]}

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{p}^{(1-t)x+ty}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\bigg)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\lambda,1]}\left(\int_{p}^{y}\chi_{[p,(1-t)x+ty)}(s)\cdot\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\right)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{p}^{y}\left(\int_{(\lambda,1]}\chi_{[p,(1-t)x+ty)}(s)\cdot\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}d\mu(t)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{p}^{y}\bigg(\int_{\left(\frac{s-x}{y-x},1\right]}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}d\mu(t)\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{p}^{y}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}(y-x)^{n-1}\bigg(\int_{\left(\frac{s-x}{y-x},1\right]}\left(t-\frac{s-x}{y-x}\right)^{n-1}d\mu(t)\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{p}^{y}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}(y-x)^{n-1}\cdot\mu_{n-1}^{+}\left(\frac{s-x}{y-x}\right)ds\geq 0.

Observe that, for
s\in(p,y]
, the ratio
\tau:=\frac{s-x}{y-x}
belongs to
(\lambda,1]
and hence, by condition
(i)
, we have that
\mu_{n-1}^{+}(\tau)\geq 0
. This validates the last inequality above

Given the two inequalities obtained and (
10
), it follows that

\int_{[0,1]}\bigg(\int_{p}^{(1-t)x+ty}\frac{f^{(n)}(s)}{(n-1)!}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-s\bigr)^{n-1}ds\bigg)d\mu(t)\geq 0.

This inequality, together with (
9
) used in (
8
), yields that inequality (
4
) is valid.

To finish the proof, we need to consider the case when
f
is not necessarily of the class
C^{\infty}.
First chose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
so that
[x,y]\subseteq I_{\varepsilon_{0}}
. Then, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, we have that
[x,y]\subseteq I_{\varepsilon}
. On the other hand, according to Lemma
6
, conditions
(a)
and
(b)
imply that
\mu_{k}(\lambda)\cdot f_{\varepsilon}
is
k
-increasing for all
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
, and
f_{\varepsilon}
(or
-f_{\varepsilon}
) is
n
-increasing on
I_{\varepsilon}
. Since
f_{\varepsilon}
is infinitely many times differentiable on
I_{\varepsilon}
, it is also infinitely many times differentiable on
[x,y]
if
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. From the first part of the proof, it follows that

\int_{[0,1]}f_{\varepsilon}\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)d\mu(t)\geq 0

holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
.
Now, using that
f_{\varepsilon}
uniformly converges to

[x,y]
as

) holds for
x<y
belonging to the interior of

. Using the continuity of

at the endpoints of

if necessary, we can see that the inequality is also valid for all

from

.
∎

4.
Necessary conditions for the inequality (

)

To derive the necessary conditions for the inequality (

), we consider the following families of the test functions.

For fixed

,

, and

\varphi_{p,n}(u):=(u-p)^{n},\qquad\varphi^{+}_{p,n}(u):=(u-p)^{n}_{+},\qquad\varphi^{-}_{p,n}(u):=(u-p)^{n}_{-}

and, additionally, let

\varphi_{p,0}:=1,\qquad\varphi^{+}_{p,0}:=\chi_{(p,\infty)\cap I}\qquad\varphi^{-}_{p,0}:=\chi_{(-\infty,p)\cap I}.

Lemma 8
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
and
n,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The following assertions hold:

(i)

If
0\leq k<n
and
p\leq\inf I
, then

\varphi_{p,n}\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\setminus M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

(ii)

If
0\leq k<n
,
n-k
is even and
p\in I
, then
\varphi_{p,n}\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\setminus M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

(iii)

If
0\leq k<n
,
n-k
is odd and
p\in I
, then
\varphi_{p,n}\not\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\cup M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

(iv)

If
0\leq k<n
and
\sup I\leq p
, then

(-1)^{n-k}\varphi_{p,n}\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\setminus M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

(v)

If
k=n
, then
\varphi_{p,n}\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\setminus M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

(vi)

If
k>n
, then
\varphi_{p,n}\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\cap M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

Proof.

The function
\varphi_{p,n}
is infinitely many times differentiable; therefore, for the proof of the assertion, we can use Theorem
3
. For all
u\in I
, we have that

\varphi_{p,n}^{(\ell)}(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{n!}{(n-\ell)!}(u-p)^{n-\ell}&\mbox{if }0\leq\ell\leq n,\\
0&\mbox{if }\ell>n.\\
\end{cases}

By analyzing the positivity and the negativity of the
k
th order derivative over
I
, the assertions follow straightforwardly.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
p\in I
and
n,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The following assertions hold:

(i)

If
0\leq k\leq n+1
, then
\varphi^{+}_{p,n}\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\setminus M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

(ii)

If
k>n+1
, then
\varphi^{+}_{p,n}\not\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\cup M_{k^{-}}(I)
.

Proof.

If
n=0
, then
\varphi_{p,0}^{+}
is a nonnegative (but not nonpositive) and increasing (but not decreasing) function which is discontinuous at
p
. Therefore, the assertion
(i)
holds for
k\in\{0,1\}
. Observing that
\varphi_{p,0}^{+}
is discontinuous at
p
, by Theorem
1
, we get that it cannot be
k
-monotone if
k>1
, which shows that assertion
(ii)
is also valid in this case. Thus, from now on, we can assume that
n\geq 1
.

The function
\varphi^{+}_{p,n}
is infinitely many times differentiable on
\mathbb{R}
except at
u=p
. At this point, it is differentiable only
n-1
times. In addition,
for all
u\in I
, we have that

\big(\varphi_{p,n}^{+}\big)^{(\ell)}(u)=\frac{n!}{(n-\ell)!}(u-p)_{+}^{n-\ell}\qquad\mbox{if}\quad 0\leq\ell\leq n-1.

First, we verify assertion (i).

For
k=0
, the assertion in
(i)
is obvious.
If
1\leq k\leq n
, then
0\leq k-1\leq n-1
, and hence the
(k-1)
st-order derivative of
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
exists, and it is an increasing (but not decreasing) continuous function. Therefore, according to Theorem
2
, the function
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
is
k
-increasing but not
k
-decreasing on
I
.

If
k=n+1
, then
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
is
k-2=n-1
times differentiable and its
(n-1)
st-order derivative equals
n!\varphi_{p,1}^{+}
, which is a convex (but not concave) function. In view of Theorem
2
, it follows that
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
is
(n+1)
-increasing but not
(n+1)
-decreasing on
I
.

Finally, if
k>n+1
, i.e.,
k\geq n+2
and if
\varphi^{+}_{p,n}\in M_{k^{+}}(I)\cup M_{k^{-}}(I)
was valid, then, by Theorem
1
, we get that
f
is
k-2\geq n
times continuously differentiable on
I
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof of assertion (ii).
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let

If

Lemma 11
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then
f:=\varphi_{p,n}
satisfies inequality (
4
) if and only if

\mu_{n}(\lambda)\geq 0\qquad(\lambda\in J(p)),

where

J(p):=\begin{cases}\bigg(-\infty,\dfrac{p-\inf I}{\sup I-\inf I}\bigg)\subseteq(-\infty,0)&\mbox{if }p\leq\inf I,\\[11.38109pt]
\mathbb{R}&\mbox{if }p\in I,\\[2.84526pt]
\bigg(1+\dfrac{p-\sup I}{\sup I-\inf I},\infty\bigg)\subseteq(1,\infty)&\mbox{if }p\geq\sup I.\end{cases}

Analogously,
f:=-\varphi_{p,n}
satisfies inequality (
4
) if and only if

\mu_{n}(\lambda)\leq 0\qquad(\lambda\in J(p)).

Proof.

The inequality (
4
) with
f:=\varphi_{p,n}
reads as follows: for all
x,y\in I
with
x<y
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-p\bigr)^{n}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=(y-x)^{n}\int_{[0,1]}(t-\tfrac{p-x}{y-x})^{n}d\mu(t)=(y-x)^{n}\mu_{n}(\tfrac{p-x}{y-x}).

Therefore, validity of inequality (
4
) with
f=\varphi_{p,n}
is equivalent to the property that

\mu_{n}(\lambda)\geq 0

for all
\lambda
belonging to the set

S(p):=\bigg\{\frac{p-x}{y-x}\colon x,y\in I,\,x<y\bigg\}.

An elementary computation shows that
S(p)
coincides with the open interval

defined in the assertion, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
p\in I
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then
f:=\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
satisfies inequality (
4
) if and only if

(11)

\mu_{n}^{+}(\lambda)\geq 0\quad(\lambda\in[0,1])\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mu_{n}(\lambda)\geq 0\quad(\lambda\in(-\infty,0]).

Proof.

The inequality (
4
) with
f:=\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
reads as follows: for all
x,y\in I
with
x<y
,

0\leq\int_{[0,1]}\varphi_{p,n}^{+}\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)d\mu(t).

Observe that, according to its definition,
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}(u)=0
if
u\leq p
and
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}(u)>0
if
p<u
.

If
x<y\leq p
, then
(1-t)x+ty\leq p
, therefore
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)=0
for all
t\in[0,1]
. Thus, the above inequality is trivially valid.

If
x\leq p<y
, then
(1-t)x+ty>p
, i.e.,
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)>0
holds if and only if
t\in(\frac{p-x}{y-x},1]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{[0,1]}\varphi_{p,n}^{+}\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\frac{p-x}{y-x},1]}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-p\bigr)^{n}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=(y-x)^{n}\int_{(\frac{p-x}{y-x},1]}(t-\tfrac{p-x}{y-x})^{n}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=(y-x)^{n}\mu_{n}^{+}(\tfrac{p-x}{y-x}).

Now, we can conclude that (
4
) holds with
f:=\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
for all
x,y\in I
satisfying
x\leq p<y
if and only if the first inequality in (
11
) is valid.

If
p<x<y
, then
(1-t)x+ty>p
, i.e.,
\varphi_{p,n}^{+}\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)>0
holds for all
t\in[0,1]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{[0,1]}\varphi_{p,n}^{+}\bigl((1-t)x+ty\bigr)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\bigl((1-t)x+ty-p\bigr)^{n}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=(y-x)^{n}\int_{[0,1]}(t-\tfrac{p-x}{y-x})^{n}d\mu(t)=(y-x)^{n}\mu_{n}(\tfrac{p-x}{y-x}).

Thus, the inequality (
4
) holds for
f:=\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
and
p<x<y
if and only if
\mu_{n}(\lambda)\geq 0
for all
\lambda
belonging to the set

S^{+}(p):=\bigg\{\frac{p-x}{y-x}\colon x,y\in I,\,p<x<y\bigg\}=(-\infty,0).

Since
\mu_{n}(\lambda)
is a polynomial of
\lambda
, therefore,
\mu_{n}
is continuous and, consequently,
\mu_{n}
is nonnegative on
(-\infty,0)
if and only it is nonnegative on
(-\infty,0]
.
This proves that the second inequality in (
11
) is also a necessary and sufficient condition for the inequality (
4
) to be valid with
f:=\varphi_{p,n}^{+}
and for all
x,y\in I
satisfying
p<x<y
.
∎

Lemma 13

Let
p\in I
and

f:=\varphi_{p,n}^{-}
satisfies inequality (

(-1)^{n}\mu_{n}^{-}(\lambda)\geq 0\quad(\lambda\in[0,1])\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(-1)^{n}\mu_{n}(\lambda)\geq 0\quad(\lambda\in[1,\infty)).

The proof of this lemma is completely analogous to that of Lemma

Now, we give a necessary condition for the
\bigl(m^{+},(m+1)^{+},\dots,n^{+}\bigr)
- monotone ordering.

Theorem 14

Let
0\leq m<n
be positive integers, and

be a bounded signed measure on

Assume that the interval

is bounded from below. Then the inequality

\mu_{0}(0)=\cdots=\mu_{m-1}(0)=0

(ii)

\mu_{k}(0)\geq 0
holds for all

(iii)

\mu_{n-1}^{+}(\tau)\geq 0
for all
\tau\in(0,1].

Proof.

To prove the sufficiency part of this theorem, assume that
f\in M_{m^{+},(m+1)^{+},\dots,n^{+}}(I)
and the conditions
(i)
–
(iii)
are satisfied. Then, in view of conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
, we trivially have that
\mu_{k}(0)f
is
k
-increasing for
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
. Using also condition
(iii)
, we can see that assumptions
(a)
and
(b)(i)
of Theorem
7
with
\lambda=0
(even without using that

is bounded from below) are satisfied. Thus, the conclusion of this theorem is valid; hence,
f
fulfills the inequality (
4

In what follows, we verify the necessity of conditions
(i)
–
(iii)
. Assume that the inequality (
4
) holds for all
f\in M_{m^{+},(m+1)^{+},\dots,n^{+}}(I)
.

To see that condition
(i)
is necessary, let
k\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
and let
p\in I
be fixed. Then according to assertion
(vi)
of Lemma
8
, for all

, we have that
\varphi_{p,k}\in M_{\ell^{+}}(I)\cap M_{\ell^{-}}(I)

, it follows that

and

are nonnegative for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, for
\lambda=0
, which shows that condition
(i)
has to be valid.

For the proof of the necessity of condition
(ii)
, take
p:=\inf I>-\infty
and let
k\in\{m,\dots,n-1\}
.
Then, by assertions
(i)
,
(v)
and
(vi)
of Lemma
8
, the function
f:=\varphi_{p,k}\in M_{\ell^{+}}(I)
for all

and hence

holds. Thus, by our assumption, this function satisfies (

\mu_{k}

Finally, we proceed to prove the necessity of

belongs to

Using now Lemma

Remark 4
.

As we can see, if we are given a functional inequality of the form

(16)

a_{1}f\left(\alpha_{1}x+(1-\alpha_{1})y\right)+\dots+a_{n}f\left(\alpha_{n}x+(1-\alpha_{n})y\right)\geq 0

then, using Theorem
7
, we can find a class of functions that satisfy this inequality. Interestingly, if the classical condition needed for an
n
-increasing ordering is satisfied, we get only one class of solutions. Still, if this is not the case, then different classes of functions may satisfy that inequality.

Given the above remark, we may say that the results obtained in this paper yield a step toward a complete solution of the inequality (
16
). However, the applications of our results are not limited to dealing with inequalities of that kind.
For example, it is well known that the Bullen inequality

\frac{1}{y-x}\int_{x}^{y}f(t)dt\leq\frac{1}{4}f(x)+\frac{1}{2}f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}f(y)

holds for all
2
-increasing (i.e., convex) functions
f
and that

\frac{1}{y-x}\int_{x}^{y}f(t)dt\leq\frac{1}{6}f(x)+\frac{2}{3}f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{6}f(y)

is satisfied by all
4
-increasing functions (cf.
[
1
]
).
The right-hand side of the above inequalities is of the form

af(x)+(1-2a)f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)+af(y),

with
a=\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{6}.
It is natural to ask what happens for other values of
a.
We will consider such a situation in the following example.

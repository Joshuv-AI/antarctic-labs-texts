Vsevolod Sakbaev*, Igor Volovich*

Abstract
. Properties of trajectories of systems of harmonic oscillators equipped with a point, absolutely continuous or singular measure are considered in this paper. Linear flow of an infinite system of oscillators on an infinite dimensional tori in the phase space are studied. It was shown in [ 28 ] that for infinite-dimensional linear flows of a countable system of oscillators there is a new type of trajectories that is not periodic and has no transitive projection on any 4-dimensional symplectic subspace. A trajectory with this property is absent in the finite-dimensional case, it is not typical for a countably system of oscillators and it is typical for a continual one. We show that any point of a non-degenerated invariant torus is non-wandering point of the flow of a countable system of harmonic oscillators. But a point of a non-degenerated invariant torus of a flow of continual system of harmonic oscillator with absolutely continuous measure is wandering point. For the continual system of oscillators with a singular measure we obtain sufficient conditions on measure and torus for absence of transitive trajectory and non-wandering points.

Introduction

Infinite system of harmonic oscillators consist an important class of infinitely dimensional Hamiltonian systems.
In particular, every isolated quantum system is
presented by a unitary group in a complex Hilbert space. Any unitary group in a Hilbert space can be considered as a system of Harmonic oscillators.
Therefore, any isolated quantum system is unitarily equivalent to a system of classical harmonic oscillators
[
34
]
.

Any Hamiltonian system with an invariant measure on the phase space can be also considered as a system of harmonic oscillators.
Because, if the phase space of an infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian system is equipped with an invariant measure with respect to the Hamiltonian flow, then, according to the Koopman-von Neumann theory, this flow induces a group of unitary operators on the space of square integrable functions with respect to the given invariant measure
[
17
,
21
,

,

A unitary group on a Hilbert space
can be represented as the Hamiltonian flow of a system of harmonic oscillators in a realization of the Hilbert space equipped with a shift-invariant symplectic form
[

.
A realization of a complex Hilbert space means the introducing of the real structure on this space

,
21

The flow of a Hamiltonian system of oscillators is a Liouville integrable system. It admits a complete family of first integrals in involution. The invariant manifolds of level surfaces of the complete family of first integrals are invariant tori.

A system of oscillators are considered as a parametrized set of non-interacting two-dimensional oscillators equipped with a measure on the set of parameters. A trajectory of a system of oscillators is a
square integrable function on the set of parameters such that the value of this function on a parameter is a trajectory of partial oscillator in a two-dimensional phase space.
Properties of trajectories of an oscillators system are essentially depends on the type of measure of oscillators system.

According to the Weyl theorem

The naive direct reformulation of the Jacobi theorem for a countable system of oscillators has the following form "A trajectory of a countable system of oscillator is periodic if and only if the collection of oscillator frequencies is strongly rationally commensurable".
But in the infinite-dimensional case this is not true (see
[
28
]
and Examples 1 below. The example is given of countable systems of oscillators with a rational commensurate collection of frequencies such that each of these systems does not have a periodic trajectory. This effect can only occur in an infinite-dimensional phase space.

Finite systems of oscillator can be divided into two types. Either a trajectory of a system of oscillator is periodic, or it contain a subsystem of at least two oscillators such that a trajectory of the subsystem is dense in the invariant torus of the subsystem
[
1
,
5
]
.

In the infinite-dimensional phase space there are at least three types of linear flow trajectories. A linear flow from the Theorem 5.5 (see Example 5.4) has neither a periodic trajectory nor a trajectory such that its projection onto any four-dimensional symplectic subspace such is dense in the projection of invariant torus on this subspace. Consequently, a trajectory of a linear flow on an infinite-dimensional torus can belong to at least one of three types. Namely, the periodic trajectories belong to the first type.
Non-periodic trajectories which projection on some symplectic subspace are dense in the projection of the invariant torus on this subspace form the second type.
The third type of trajectories includes non-periodic trajectories such that their projections on any symplectic subspace are not dense in the projection of invariant torus on this symplectic subspace.
Thus, it is shown that in the infinite-dimensional case there exists a new type of linear flow trajectories.

The structure of the paper is the following. In Section 2 we introduce definitions and a preliminary information on a systems of harmonic oscillators. In Section 3 the necessary condition of transitive trajectory existence is obtained in terms of characteristic function of the measure (see (
2.1
)) of the system of oscillators. In Section 4 we show that if the system of oscillators is equipped with the Bernoulli measure (in particular, which is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure), then a trajectory of oscillator’s system is not dense in the invariant torus. In Section 5 criteria of periodicity and transitivity for a trajectory of a countable system of oscillators are obtained. In Section 6 the periodicity and transitivity of a trajectory of a continual system of oscillators are studied. In section 7 we show that a trajectory of a countable system of oscillators is non-wandering, but a trajectory of continual system of oscillators is wandering. Section 8 is conclusions of paper’s results. Section 9 contains Appendix on the infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian systems.

8.
Conclusion

In present paper, we study and compare the dynamical property of a finite, countable and continual system of harmonic oscillators.
Conditions are obtained for periodicity, transitivity and ergodicity and density of the trajectories of an infinite system of oscillators
on an invariant torus.

The nonwandering property of a point of phase space are studied.
We prove the ergodicity of a measure (and the ergodicity of the space of functions) on an invariant torus of the flow of an infinite system of oscillators. The nonwandering property of an arbitrary point of the phase space of infinite-dimensional linear flow of a countable system of Harmonic oscillators are proven.
We prove that any point of non-degenerated invariant torus is wandering for a linear flow of a continual system of Harmonic oscillators of a wide class.
It is shown that for infinite-dimensional linear flows there is a new type of trajectory that is absent in the finite-dimensional case. A trajectory of this type in neither periodic no transitive in any 4-dimensional symplectic subspace. We show that the typical type of a trajectory is transitive for a countable system of oscillator and is neither periodic no transitive for a continual system of oscillators.

Appendix I. Infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian system

In the present paper the following description of infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian system is considered (
[
4
,
17
,
21
]
).
An
infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian system is a triplet
(E,\omega,h)
.
Here
E
is an infinite-dimensional real separable Hilbert space,
\omega
is a shift-invariant symplectic form (i.e.
\omega
is a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form on the space
E
), and
h
is a densely defined real-valued function which is Frechet-differentiable on on a dense linear sub–manifolds
of the space
E
.

A symplectic form
\omega
in a real separable Hilbert space
E
is called
native

if there is an orthonormal basis (ONB)
\{e_{k}\}
in the space
E
such that

{\omega(e_{2k-1},e_{2j})=\delta_{j,k},\ k,j\in\mathbb{N}}
,
where
\delta_{j,i}
is Kronecker symbol.
A basis with the above property is called symplectic basis of the symplectic space
(E,\omega)
.

A native symplectic form
\omega
defines the decomposition of the space
E
into orthogonal sum of two subspaces
E=P\oplus Q
such that orthonormal systems
g_{j}=e_{2j-1},\,j\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{k}=e_{2k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}
are ONB in subspaces
P
and
Q
respectively. Then

(9.1)

\omega(g_{j},g_{i})=0,\,\omega(f_{i},f_{j})=0\ \forall\ i,j\in{\mathbb{N}};\quad\omega(g_{j},f_{k})=\delta_{jk},\,j,k\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,
\{e_{i},\ i\in\mathbb{N}\}=\{g_{j},f_{k};\ j,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is a symplectic basis of the symplectic space
(E,\omega)

(see
[
17
,
21
]
).

The bilinear form
\omega
on the Hilbert space
E
is bounded. Therefore, it defines
a bounded linear operator
{\bf J}
associated with the form
\omega
. The operator
J
is a non-degenerate
skew-symmetric linear operator in the Hilbert space
E
.
Conversely, any orthogonal basis
\{g_{j},f_{k};\ j\in{\bf N},\,k\in{\bf N}\}
of
the Hilbert space
E
uniquely defines a skew-symmetric operator
\bf J
on the Hilbert space
E

by the equalities
{\bf J}(g_{j})=-f_{j},\ {\bf J}(f_{k})=g_{k},\ j\in{\bf N},\,k\in{\bf N}
.
The symplectic form
\omega
, associated to
J
, is native on the space
E
with a symplectic basis
\{g_{j},f_{k};\ j\in{\bf N},\,k\in{\bf N}\}
.

The subspaces
Q
and
P
are called configuration space and momentum space respectively. The space
P
has the role of the space which is conjugated to the space
Q

(
[
10
,
17
,
21
,
31
]
).

In a Hamiltonian system
(E,{\omega},h),
the Hamilton function is a functional
h:\ E_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
E_{1}
is a dense linear manifold of the Hilbert space
E
and, moreover, the functional
h
is Frechet-differentiable on a
linear manifold
E_{2}
which is dense in the space
E
. The article
[
10
]

describes the properties of an infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian system and its invariant manifolds.

The Hamilton equation for the Hamiltonian system

(E,{\omega},h)
is the following equation
z^{\prime}(t)=J(h^{\prime}(z(t))),\,t\in\Delta,
in
the unknown function
z:\ \Delta\to E_{2}
where

is some interval of real line. (

A densely defined vector field

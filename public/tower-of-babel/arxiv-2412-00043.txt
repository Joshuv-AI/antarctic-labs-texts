Classification of monads and a new moduli component of stable rank 2 bundles on ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} with even determinant and c 2 = 9 c_{2}=9 .
Aislan Leal Fontes

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to classify all minimal monads whose cohomology is a stable rank 2 bundle on ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} with Chern classes c 1 = 0 c_{1}=0 and c 2 = 9 c_{2}=9 , with possible exception of two non-negative minimal monads, and thus we extend the classification of the minimal monads made by Hartshorne and Rao in [ 16 , Section 5.3] when c 2 ≤ 8 c_{2}\leq 8 . We also prove the existence of a new component of the moduli space ℬ ⁡ ( 9 ) \mathcal{B}(9) which is distinct from the Hartshorne and Ein components.

2.
Constructing vector bundles of rank
2
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}

There are three forms to construct vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Due to Horrocks
[
11
]
, every vector
bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is given as cohomology of a minimal monad whose terms are sums of line
bundles. The second form to obtain a stable vector bundle
\mathcal{{\mathcal{E}}}
is through the concept of a spectrum
that encodes the information of the cohomology groups
H^{1}_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{{\mathcal{E}}})
and

H^{2}_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{{\mathcal{E}}})
, as observed in Section
2.2
of this
paper. We also obtain a rank 2 vector bundle through the Serre correspondence.

2.1.
Minimal Horrocks monads

A
monad
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is a complex

\mathbf{M}:\ \ \ \ \mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{C},

(1)

of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that the map
\alpha
is injective and
\beta
is surjective. The sheaf

\mathcal{E}:={\rm ker}\,\beta/\Image\alpha
is the
cohomology of the monad

\mathbf{M}
of (
1
). Let us assume that
the morphism
\beta
in (
1
) is locally left invertible, and so the cohomology of the
monad
{\mathcal{E}}
of
\mathbf{M}
is a vector bundle. Furthermore, due to Horrocks (see
[
11
]
) follows that all vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
can be obtained as cohomology of a
monad of the form (
1
), where the vector bundles
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{B}

and
\mathcal{A}
are sums of line bundles.

We assume that the morphism
\alpha
is locally left invertible, so that
\mathcal{E}
is a vector
bundle. A monad
\mathbf{M}
of the form
(
1
)
is called
minimal
if the entries of the
associated matrices to the maps
\alpha
and
\beta
, as homogeneous forms, are not nonzero
constants or, in other words, if no direct summand of
\mathcal{A}
is the
image of a line subbundle of
\mathcal{B}
and if no direct summand of
\mathcal{C}
goes
onto a direct summand of
\mathcal{B}
.
In addition,
\mathbf{M}
is said to be

homotopy free
if

\Hom(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{B})=\Hom(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})=0.

We recall that a minimal monad as in display (
1
) is said:
positive
if all summand of
\mathcal{C}
has positive degree,
non-negative
if the summands of
\mathcal{C}
have non-negative degree and
negative
if
\mathcal{C}
has at least a summand of negative degree. By considering
{\mathcal{E}}
be a stable rank 2 bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with even determinant there is a unique isomorphism

f:{\mathcal{E}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}^{*}
with symplectic structure which means
f^{*}=-f
. In parallel to the description of minimal monads whose cohomology is a stable rank 2 bundle in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with
c_{1}=-1
made in
[
20
, Lemma 3]
we have the following characterization for minimal Horrocks monads whose cohomology is a stable bundle with even determinant.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a stable rank 2 bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{E})=0
. There are two
sequences of integers
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
and
\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{s+1})
such
that
\mathcal{E}
is the cohomology of a monad of the form

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-a_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s+1}\Big({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(b_{j})\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-b_{j})\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a_{i}),

(2)

where we order the tuples
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
as
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{s}
and

0\leq b_{1}\leq\cdots\leq b_{s+1}
.
Furthermore,

c_{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}^{2}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s+1}b_{i}^{2}.

(3)

Example 2
.

The minimal monads of the form

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-c)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-a)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(b)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-b)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(c),

with
c>a\geq b\geq 0
, are called
Ein monads
while minimal monads of the form

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-1)^{\oplus s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{2s+2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1)^{\oplus s},s\geq 1,

are called
Hartshorne monads
.

Under the hypothesis of Lemma
1
, if
\mathcal{E}
is a stable bundle and its Rao module

M:=H^{1}_{*}(\mathcal{E})
has a minimal free presentation of the form

\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

then
\rank(F_{0})=s
and from
[
22
, Proposition 2.2]
, we have
\rank(F_{1})=2s+2
.
Conversely, by repeating the arguments of Hartshorne and Rao in
[
16
, Proposition 3.2]
, we get
that if
M
admits a minimal free presentation as above, then
\mathcal{E}
is given as cohomology
of a minimal Horrocks monad as in (
1
) where
F_{0}=H^{0}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
and

F_{1}=H^{0}_{*}(\mathcal{B})
, this is,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are the
sheafifications of the free modules
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
, respectively.

,

S.1

(Symmetry)

S.2

(Connectedness) Any integer

also belongs to spectrum
{\mathcal{X}}

{\mathcal{X}}
, then each

[

2.3.
Serre Construction

If we consider a rank 2 vector bundle
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with first Chern class
c_{1}
and

s\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{G}(t))
, for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, is a global section whose zero set has codimension 2,
then
s^{\vee}
induces an exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s^{\vee}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{I}_{C}(c_{1}+2t)\rightarrow 0,

(6)

where
C
is a Cohen-Macaulay curve in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
equal to vanish locus of
s
. The exact sequence in
(
6
) corresponds to an element
\eta\in\ext^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(c_{1}+2t),{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}})\simeq H^{0}(\omega_{C}(4-2t-c_{1}))
which generates the sheaf

\omega_{C}(4-2t-c_{1})
. Conversely, given a pair
(C,\eta)
where
C
is a Cohen-Macaulay curve on

\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
\eta
is a non-vanishing global section of
\omega_{C}(4-2t-c_{1})
we get a rank 2
bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and a global section
s
of
\mathcal{F}(t)
which zero set
is
C
. Additionally, the Chern class of
\mathcal{F}
are given by

c_{1}(\mathcal{F})=t\mbox{ and }c_{2}(\mathcal{F})=\deg C.

The above content is the
Serre correspondence
and for more details see
[
13
]
. This Serre correspondence was
generalized for rank 2 reflexive sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and can be found in
[
14
]
. We note that

\mathcal{G}
is stable if and only if
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{C}(t+c_{1}))=0
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
a
be an integer such that
a\geq 3
. The minimal Horrocks monad
\mathbf{M}
given by

2\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-a)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\rightarrow}}2\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a-1)\oplus 2\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1-a)\oplus({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-1))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\rightarrow}}2\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a)

(11)

exists and its cohomology
{\mathcal{E}}
is a stable bundle with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=0,c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=4a-3
and spectrum

\mathcal{X}=\{{(1-a)}^{2},\cdots,{-1}^{2},0,1^{2},\cdots,{(a-2)}^{2},{(a-1)}^{2}\}.

(12)

Proof.

We prove the existence of the monad (
11
) by an explicit computation. In fact, with the help of the Macaulay2 we
found the morphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
as

\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}x&0&w^{2a-1}&z^{2a-1}&y^{a-1}&z^{a+1}\\
y&x&z^{2a-1}&w^{2a-1}&x^{a-1}&0\\
\end{array}\right)~\mbox{ and }~\alpha=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}w^{2a-1}&0\\
z^{2a-1}&w^{2a-1}+x^{a-2}z^{a+1}\\
-x&0\\
-y&-x\\
0&-z^{a+1}\\
yz^{a-2}&xz^{a-2}+y^{a-1}\\
\end{array}\right),

such that
\alpha
is injective,
\beta
is surjective and
\beta\circ\alpha=0
. This is sufficient
to show that the minimal Horrocks monad in (
11
) exists and therefore its cohomology is
a vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
such that
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=0,c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=4a-3
and furthermore, the morphism
\beta
does not
admit syzygies of degree
\geq 0
and so the vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
is stable. On the other hand, from
display of the monad in (
11
) we have for
l\geq 2

n_{l}=2h^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a-l))-2h^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a-l-1))=(a+2-l)(a+1-l)\mbox{ and }n_{1}=2a-1.

By applying the identity in (
4
) we prove that the spectrum of
{\mathcal{E}}
is as in (
12
).

∎

Theorem 14

(14)

The dimension of the Ein components
M_{2}
and

can be computed by formula
[

, 2.2.B, p.g. 18]

and

.

We take

in
(

)

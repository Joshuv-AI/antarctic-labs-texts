Gene prediction of the causal relationship between immune cells and IgA nephropathy: A bidirectional Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Yukai Zhang, Chenwei Zhang, Gang Liu, Peiyun He, Binbin Wan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IgA nephropathy is the most common primary glomerular disease worldwide, with inflammation and autoimmune response mechanisms permeating the entire disease development process. The advancement of genome-wide association studies has enabled deeper understanding of the disease mechanisms and genetic susceptibility. Therefore, this study aims to explore the causal relationship between 731 immune cell types and the disease through Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis. This 2-sample MR study investigated bidirectional causal relationships using summary statistics for immune cells characteristics from the Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS) catalog and IgA nephropathy from the FinnGen dataset. The study primarily utilized the Inverse Variance Weighted method for its main outcome. Additionally, the robustness of the results is further enhanced by analyses of heterogeneity, pleiotropy, and multiple sensitivity tests. After adjusting for false discovery rate (FDR), the study results revealed a bidirectional causal relationship between CD8 on terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells (OR = 0.77, 95% CI = 0.67–0.88, P = .0001) and CD4 on CD28+ CD4+ T cells (OR = 0.75, 95% CI = 0.64–0.87, P = .0001) with the risk of IgA nephropathy. CD64 on CD14+ CD16+ monocytes (OR = 0.66, 95% CI = 0.51–0.85, P = .0013) is considered a protective factor, while the percentages of CD8+ and CD8dim T cells (1.38, 95% CI = 1.17–1.63, P = .0002) in leukocytes are viewed as risk factors. This study employed genetic variation as an instrumental variable to explore the genetic association between immune cells and IgA nephropathy, aiming to offer new insights into early prevention and personalized treatment of the disease.

1. Introduction

IgA nephropathy (IgAN) represents the most prevalent primary glomerular disease globally.[1] Predominantly characterized by IgA deposition in the glomerular mesangial region, the histologic features include no or minimal abnormalities under light microscopy, mesangial cellular proliferation, focal intracapillary proliferation, diffuse intracapillary hyperplasia, necrotic and crescentic lesions, and, more rarely, membranoproliferative injuries.[2] According to the European national registries, the prevalence of IgAN was estimated to be 2.5 per 10,000 patients of all ages, with a higher risk in young adults.[3] The clinical progression of IgA nephropathy varies, with 30% to 40% of patients progressing to end-stage renal disease 20 to 30 years after the initial onset of symptoms.[1] Although kidney transplantation is an option for IgA nephropathy patients, the postoperative recurrence rate can be as high as 50%,[4,5] leading the majority of patients to rely on regular hemodialysis for ongoing treatment.[6] In conclusion, IgA nephropathy imposes a significant disease burden, making the development of improved prevention and intervention strategies a global priority.

Although the exact pathogenesis of IgAN is unknown, its susceptibility and risk of progression are influenced by a combination of genetic and environmental factors, with inflammatory and autoimmune mechanisms present throughout the disease onset and progression.[7] The abundant deposition of galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) induces the production of autoantibodies, leading to the formation of immune complexes that cause complement activation and kidney damage.[8,9] Furthermore, a single-cell transcriptomics study identified specific gene expressions in kidney-resident macrophages and CD8 T cells in IgAN patients, indicating abnormal regulation associated with proliferation and inflammation.[10] In a study on IgAN, artemisinin and hydroxychloroquine (AH) treatment was shown to modulate the differentiation of CD4+ T cell subsets by reducing the proportion of Th2 and Th17 cells while promoting the differentiation of Th1 and Treg cells, thereby exerting an immunosuppressive effect on IgAN rats.[11] However, challenges such as conducting large-scale randomized controlled trials, limited sample sizes, study design flaws, and the influence of confounding factors make the relationship between specific immune cells and IgAN unclear. This underscores the need to explore the causal links between various immunobiological features and IgA nephropathy.

With the rapid advancement of random genetic technologies, attention has shifted to genome-wide association studies (GWAS) of IgA nephropathy to further understand the disease mechanisms and genetic susceptibility.[12] Mendelian randomization (MR), a key epidemiological and etiological inference method, uses genetic variants as IVs to explore the causal relationship between immune cell exposure and disease outcomes while minimizing the impact of confounding factors and reverse causality.[13] This TSMR analysis, building on existing observations, aims to delve deeper into the causal links between various immunobiological features and IgA nephropathy.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design

This study deeply investigated the potential causal relationship between 731 immune cell phenotypes and the risk of IgA nephropathy for the first time. It employed a 2-sample bidirectional MR approach to provide evidence supporting early disease prediction and personalized treatment. To effectively apply selected IVs for causal inference, 3 assumptions (correlation, exclusivity, and uniqueness) must be satisfied. Firstly, the selected instrumental variable (IV) should exhibit a strong correlation with the exposure phenotype to minimize potential bias. Furthermore, it is essential to confirm the independence of the IV from the identified protective and risk factors. Finally, it is crucial to ensure that the IV directly influences the risk of IgA nephropathy solely through immune cell levels. 2.2. Data resource

A statistical summary of GWAS data for 731 immune cell phenotypes, derived from a cohort of 3757 Sardinians, is publicly available in the GWAS catalog. This data, which ranges from data codes GCST0001391 to GCST0002121, can be accessed via the catalog.[14] In this study, researchers examined 539 immunological features through flow cytometry, including 118 absolute cell counts, 389 surface antigen MFIs, and 32 morphological parameters, as well as 192 relative counts, which represent ratios between cell levels. The major leukocyte subpopulations are classified into 7 categories: T cells, B cells, dendritic cells (DCs), monocytes, other myeloid cells, hematopoietic stem cells, and macrophage fusion indices (MFIs). To minimize the risk of population overlap that could skew MR results, we used the FinGenn database as a data source for IgA nephropathy. This included data on 653 patients and 411,528 healthy controls (https://www.finngen.fi/en).

2.3. Instrumental variable selection

To ensure the relevance of IVs, we initially established a significance threshold of P < 5E−8 for selecting single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as initial instrumental variables (IVs). However, as only a very limited number of IVs met this criterion, we revised the threshold to P < 1E−5 to ensure an adequate supply of IVs for subsequent analyses.[15] Establishing a minor allele frequency (MAF) threshold of 0.01 for the target variants ensures sufficient sample size for effective association analyses and enhances the statistical efficacy of the study.[16] Subsequently, to reduce potential bias introduced by linkage disequilibrium in correlation analysis while ensuring independence, we chose a lower R2 threshold (R2 < 0.1) and expanded the clumping distance to 500 kb.[17] During the screening process, SNPs with palindromic structures were also excluded to minimize their potential impact on random assignment. Finally, we computed the F-statistic for the selected instrumental variable to ensure that its value exceeds 10, aiming to mitigate the influence of weak IVs.[18]

2.4. Bidirectional TSMR analysis

The study first performed forward analyses to explore the potential causal relationship between 731 immune cell phenotypes and the risk of IGA nephropathy. Subsequently, we mainly conducted reverse validation on the exposure and outcome pairs that remained positive even after FDR correction (PFDR < 0.20), aiming to eliminate the potential effect of reverse causality. In addition, the inverse variance weighting (IVW) method is the most commonly used MR analysis method when multiple SNPs are present in the IV.[19] This method combines Wald ratios through meta-analytic principles to provide an overall estimate of causal effects. A random effects model is recommended when significant heterogeneity exists between IVs, as it accounts for the possibility that the effects of individual IVs may vary. If there is no significant heterogeneity, a fixed effects model is suitable. The weighted median and MR-Egger methods are often used to complement the main method and provide robust results when dealing with potential horizontal pleiotropy in IV.[20,21] Additionally, the Weighted Mode and Simple Mode methods have been included as supplementary approaches.

2.5. Sensitivity analyses

This study integrates multiple sensitivity analyses to validate and rectify positive MR findings through diverse methods. To assess potential heterogeneity, we utilized Cochran Q statistic to determine significance. Additionally, Leave-One-Out (LOO) analysis was visually inspected to detect any anomalous SNPs significantly impacting the results (with a significance threshold of P < .05 applied).[22] When assessing the potential presence of horizontal pleiotropy, the magnitude of the intercept in the Egger regression indicates the significance of the average pleiotropic effect among all IVs.[23] Furthermore, Mendelian Randomization Pleiotropy RESidual Sum and Outlier (MR-PRESSO) aims to bolster the robustness of results by systematically identifying and eliminating IVs one by one, thus reducing the influence of pleiotropy.[24]

The code employed in this study was executed in the R 4.3.1 environment, utilizing primarily the R packages for 2-sample MR and MR-PRESSO.[25]

2.6. Ethics

All analyses in this study were conducted using publicly available GWAS Statistical Abstracts data sourced from large public databases, thus obviating the need for additional ethical approval or informed consent.

3. Result

Under strict screening conditions, we included a total of 36,268 SNPs representing 731 immune cell traits, with the number of SNPs per phenotype ranging from 3 to 1692 and F-statistic values between 20.57 and 61.99 (Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N940). Upon reviewing our results, we found that 3 immune cell phenotypes, CD8 on terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells, CD4 on CD28+ CD4+ T cells, and the percentage of CD8+ and CD8dim T cells in leukocytes, with an FDR < 0.05, demonstrated a significant causal association (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N940). Additionally, CD64 on CD14+ CD16+ monocytes was also associated with disease outcomes when an FDR <0.20 was used as the threshold for significance.

3.1. Causal effect of immune cells on IGA nephropathy

The current study primarily focuses on the results of the IVW method, ensuring that the outcomes of other methods align with the directionality of the primary method (Fig 2 and Fig. S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N935). This alignment confirms the robustness of the causal association between exposure and outcome. The IVW method (OR = 0.77, 95% CI = 0.67–0.88, P = .0001) identified CD8 on terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells as a protective factor for IgA nephropathy. Corresponding results were also observed with the weighted median (OR = 0.76, 95% CI = 0.62–0.93, P = .0069, PFDR = 0.033) and weighted mode (OR = 0.77, 95% CI = 0.62–0.94, P = .0183). Additionally, the study found that higher percentages of CD4 on CD28+ CD4+ T cell (OR = 0.75, 95% CI = 0.64–0.87, P = .0001, PFDR = 0.033) were associated with a reduced risk of developing IgA nephropathy. CD64 on CD14+ CD16+ monocytes also demonstrated a trend towards having a protective effect (OR = 0.66, 95% CI = 0.51–0.85, P = .0013, PFDR = 0.196). The Q-statistics for the 3 IVW models were 0.221, 0.291, and 0.470. In contrast, the IVW (OR = 1.38, 95% CI = 1.17–1.63, P = .0002, PFDR = 0.033) and Weighted Median (OR = 1.36, 95% CI = 1.06–1.74, P = .0152) methods both identified the percentage of CD8+ and CD8dim T cells in leukocytes as risk factors for the disease. The Steiger P-values for all 4 exposure-outcome pairs were <.05, specifically 1.17E−181, 1.20E−147, 2.15E−170, and 5.15E−74, respectively (Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N940). This confirms that the IVs directly influence the development of IgA nephropathy through immune cell traits.

Forest plot of the causal association between immune cells and IgA nephropathy positivity.

3.2. Bidirectional causal effects

For the 4 significant positive results mentioned above, we investigated the reverse effect by using disease as the exposure and immune traits as the outcome. Reverse outcome analyses were conducted using more stringent criteria for selecting instrumental variables (IVs), including a P-value threshold of 1E−5, an R2 of 0.001, a distance of 10,000 kilobases, and an F-statistic >10. This rigorous screening resulted in 16 SNPs being included in the study (Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N940). IVW results demonstrated that the progression of IgA nephropathy was associated with increased CD8 levels in terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells (OR = 1.055, 95% CI = 1.001–1.112, P = .048). The direction of these results was consistent across other methods, although they did not reach statistical significance. The fixed-effects model within the IVW approach indicated that the onset of disease led to increased levels of CD4 on CD28+ CD4+ T cells (OR = 1.061, 95% CI = 1.006–1.119, P = .029), while the random-effects model did not yield a positive result (Table S4, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N940). Further sensitivity analyses revealed no presence of heterogeneity or pleiotropy (Tables S5 and S6, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N940). The inverse results are visualized in Figure 3 and Figure S5, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N935.

Forest plot of the causal association between IgA nephropathy and immune cell positivity.

3.3. Sensitivity analyses

This study employed various statistical methods to assess the sensitivity of the results through multiple approaches. The funnel plot exhibits a roughly symmetrical trend, initially indicating the consistency of results (Fig. S2, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N935). The Cochran Q values for significant results in the heterogeneity test were all >0.05, and the LOO analyses did not reveal the presence of abnormal SNPs (Table 1, Figs. S3 and S4, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N935). For the positive results, the intercept values of the Egger regressions were all >0.05. Additionally, we identified and removed 2 groups of aberrant SNPs, ebi-a-GCST90002057 (rs592229, rs9273375) and ebi-a-GCST90001614 (rs3129817, rs9269109), using the MR-PRESSO method. This action further eliminated the interference of horizontal pleiotropy in our study results, thereby ensuring their robustness. Additionally, using the MR-PRESSO method, we identified and removed 2 sets of anomalous SNPs, ebi-a-GCST90002057 (rs592229, rs9273375) and ebi-a-GCST90001614 (rs3129817, rs9269109), comprising 4 SNPs in total. These SNPs were excluded from the 4 positive outcome pairs, which were then supported by 28, 26, 30, and 14 IVs, respectively. This step effectively minimized the impact of horizontal pleiotropy in our study results and ensured their robustness.

4. Discussion

In conclusion, this study aimed to establish a bidirectional causal relationship between 731 immune cell phenotypes (MFI, RC, AC, and MP) and IgA nephropathy using a large public GWAS database. At an FDR of 0.05, a significant causal relationship was observed between 3 immunophenotypes and IgA nephropathy. When the FDR threshold was relaxed to 0.20, there was an increase in protective exposure for 1 disease. Inverse MR analysis revealed that the development of IgA nephropathy leads to increased levels of 2 immune traits (P < .05). Therefore, we must exercise caution in suggesting that there may be bidirectional biofeedback mechanisms between them.

Based on the findings of this study, it appears that variations in the relevant T cell indexes are more closely linked to the incidence of IGA nephropathy. This study suggests that elevated CD8 levels on terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells not only reduce the risk of IGA nephropathy but also tend to be higher in patients with the condition compared to healthy individuals. Terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells, which have reached a mature stage with highly specialized functions, are primarily known for their direct killing of virally infected or cancerous cells.[26,27] Although their association with IGA nephropathy has rarely been reported, their involvement in this process is well established. In existing studies, the overactivity of T cells in the kidney and peripheral blood is believed to play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of IgAN and also correlates with its clinical severity.[9,28,29] By observing changes in proteinuria, serum IgA levels, and histopathological features of renal tissue samples in ddY mice treated with intravenous anti-CD8 monoclonal antibody from 4 to 40 weeks of age, it was found that CD8+ T cells mediate the prevention of spontaneous IgA nephropathy by inhibiting thylakoid proliferation and the progression of the disease.[30] In other glomerulonephritis (lupus nephritis), the accumulation of periglomerular CD8+ T cells was linked to crescent formation, Bowman capsule rupture, disease activity scores, and elevated serum creatinine levels.[31] Additionally, this study observed that an increased ratio of CD8+ and CD8dim T cells in leukocytes contributes to the development of IgA nephropathy. This suggests that monitoring changes in cell ratios could enhance our understanding of the disease process, evaluate immune responses, and potentially guide therapeutic decisions.

Remarkably, elevated levels of CD4 on CD28+ CD4+ T cells could also act as a protective factor against IgA nephropathy. CD4+ T cells are pivotal in the immune system, as they can differentiate into various subpopulations like T helper cell types 1 (Th1), Th2, Th17, and regulatory T cells (Treg).[32] Simultaneously, they release cytokines such as interferon gamma (IFN-γ) and interleukin (IL)-2 to actively participate in immune regulation.[33] Following ART combined with HCQ (AH) treatment, IGAN rats exhibited reduced differentiation of Th2 and Th17 cells alongside increased levels of Th1 and Treg cells in both peripheral blood and spleen. This indicates that AH treatment can mitigate renal injury by modulating the differentiation of CD4+ T cell subsets. Specifically, the CD4 molecule shields against the development and progression of IgA nephropathy by binding to major histocompatibility complex class II (MHC-II) molecules. This interaction assists helper T cells in recognizing and attaching to antigens presented by antigen-presenting cells, thereby augmenting immunomodulation and suppressing inflammatory and autoimmune responses.[9]

Undifferentiated monocytes can be divided into 3 main subpopulations with distinct transcriptional and functional characteristics: classical (CD14++ CD16−), intermediate (CD14++ CD16+) and nonclassical (CD14+ CD16+).[34] Among them, nonclassical cells are the first line of defence against pathogens, play an important role in inflammation and immune surveillance, and are closely associated with disease load.[35] Consistent with this, the results of MR analysis showed that the risk of IGA nephropathy decreased with increasing levels of CD64 on CD14+ CD16+ monocyte. Furthermore, attention should be directed towards the potential contribution of aberrant expansion of the (CD14+ CD16+) subpopulation to the pathogenesis of IgAN, which is also marked by a heightened predisposition to apoptosis.[36] Sharon N discovered a notable expansion of the nonclassical monocyte subpopulation (CD14+ CD16+) in all IgAN subjects, both at genomic and functional levels. Additionally, heightened apoptotic phenotypes were observed in these cells, leading to abnormal regulation of the mitochondrial respiratory system in monocytes isolated from IgAN patients.[37] In summary, it’s essential to integrate the diverse roles of CD14+ CD16+ monocyte level alterations in both disease onset and progression. Additionally, emphasis should be placed on verifying the accuracy and delving into the underlying biological mechanisms of CD64 as a pivotal factor to bolster its protective effect against early disease onset.

This study is the first to explore the causal relationships between multiple immune cell phenotypes and IgA nephropathy from a genetic perspective, utilizing a publicly available database. The aim is to achieve a more comprehensive understanding of the disease immune regulatory mechanisms and potentially offer new strategies for its early prevention. MR analyses not only complement traditional genetic studies but also significantly mitigate the influence of confounding factors and reverse causality on the results. Additionally, we conducted multiple sensitivity analyses to exclude heterogeneity and pleiotropy from the study results and used FDR to control for false-positive rates associated with multiple exposure choices.

It is also crucial to acknowledge the limitations of this study. Firstly, the scope of this study was limited to observing associations between immune traits and IgA nephropathy in European populations due to constraints in disease data sources. To generalize the findings, it is necessary to expand the choice of population subgroups. Additionally, this study is based solely on statistical analyses at the genetic level, which requires further validation through experimental and clinical practice. Furthermore, our bidirectional MR analyses indicated a possible bidirectional biofeedback mechanism between exposure and outcome, suggesting the need for further exploration of the underlying mechanisms influencing these relationships. This could help identify potential biomarkers or therapeutic targets.

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrated a causal relationship between immune cells and IgA nephropathy through bidirectional MR analysis using genetic variants as IVs. By examining the genetic level, it provides new insights into the biological mechanisms of specific immune traits and diseases. The identified immune cells could become crucial for the early prediction and personalized management of IgA nephropathy.

Hochschild cohomology and Deformation theory of stable ∞ \infty -categories with t-structures
Isamu Iwanari

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Gerstenhaber
[
8
]
initiated the study of the deformation theory of associative algebras by means of Hochschild cohomology.
Hochschild cohomology carries a (graded) Lie algebra structure
[
9
]
.
This Lie algebra structure, together with its associative algebra structure, endows the Hochschild cohomology with the structure of a Gerstenhaber algebra.
This Gerstenhaber algebra structure governs the deformation theory of associative algebras.
Furthermore, the Hochschild cohomology of an algebra admits an enhancement from a Gerstenhaber algebra to an algebra over the operad of little
2
-disks,
i.e., an
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra at the level of chain complexes.
This is commonly referred to as Deligne conjecture on Hochschild cohomology.
The conjecture has been solved through various approaches by McClure–Smith
[
21
]
, Kontsevich–Soibelman
[
15
]
, and Tamarkin
[
23
]
.
Moreover, various generalized versions of the conjecture have been extensively studied, including those in categorical frameworks.

We turn to consider deformations of stable
\infty
-categories.
Let
k
be a field.
Given a
k
-linear (presentable) stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, one can define the Hochschild cochain complex

\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
which is defined as an
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra.
Let
Art
denote the
\infty
-category of Artin dg
k
-algebras.
Define
\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}:\textup{Art}\to\mathcal{S}

be the deformation functor
which sends
R
to the space (i.e.,
\infty
-groupoid) of deformations
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\otimes_{R}k\simeq\mathcal{C})
of

\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is an
R
-linear stable
\infty
-category, and
\mathcal{S}
is the
\infty
-category of spaces.
The deformation theory of
\mathcal{C}
, or variants of compactly generated
\infty
-categories, has been investigated from several perspectives, for instance, in the setting of (derived) formal moduli problems
[
19
, X, Section 5]
, in the case of deformations to the ring of formal power series
[
3
]
, and from the lifting problem of compact generators
[
16
]
.
In
[
19
]
, the deformation theory of stable

\infty
-categories is formulated in the local version of derived geometry over
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebras:
The deformation problem is defined as a functor
\mathcal{D}ef^{(2)}_{\mathcal{C}}:\textup{Art}_{2}\to\mathcal{S}

from the
\infty
-category
\textup{Art}_{2}
of Artin
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebras over
k
.
It was shown that, under restrictive conditions,
\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
is equivalent to the

\mathbf{E}_{2}
-version of formal moduli problem
\mathcal{F}_{A\oplus\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)}^{(2)}
associated to the
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
while
there is always
a map
\eta:\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{F}_{A\oplus\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)}^{(2)}
which is universal among maps to formal
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-moduli problems.
However, in general
\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
do not satisfy the Schlessinger conditions of
[
19
, X]

(see
[
14
]
for a specific example).
This implies that in general neither
\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}
nor
\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
is a formal moduli problem.
Consequently, they can not be governed by the Hochschild cohomology or other algebraic invariants.
This has been a longstanding problem in the theory.
In other words, the deformation problem encoded by
\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}
(or the likes) is somewhat naive,
and a more refined or appropriate formulation of the deformation problem is required.

The main purpose of this paper is to prove the following result (see Theorem
4.5
, Theorem
4.9
for precise statements):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a
k
-linear presentable stable
\infty
-category equipped with a left and right complete, accessible
t
-structure
(\mathcal{E}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{E}^{\geq 0})
.
Let
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}:\textup{Art}_{2}\to\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
be the deformation functor which carries
R\in\textup{Art}_{2}
to the space
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R)

consisting of deformations

\big((\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}),(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{R}k,(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{R}k)^{\leq 0})\simeq(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\leq 0})\big)

of
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\leq 0})
where
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0})
indicates an
R
-linear presentable
\infty
-category equipped with a left and right complete
t
-structure (see Definition
4.8
for the detail).
Then the deformation functor
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}

is a formal
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-moduli problem
\mathcal{F}_{k\oplus\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)}^{(2)}
(in Lurie’s sense) that corresponds to the augmented
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra
k\oplus\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)\to k
.
In particular, there is a canonical equivalence of spaces

\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R)\simeq\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{2}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})}(\mathbb{D}_{2}(R),\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k))

for
R\in\textup{Art}_{2}
. Here
\operatorname{Alg}_{2}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
is the
\infty
-category of
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebras over
k
, and
\mathbb{D}_{2}
is the
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-Koszul duality functor.

If we focus on deformations to commutative bases, we have:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
Art
be the
\infty
-category of Artin dg
k
-algebras.
Let
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\infty}:\textup{Art}\to\textup{Art}_{2}\to\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
be
the composite of the forgetful functor
\textup{Art}\to\textup{Art}_{2}
and
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}:\textup{Art}_{2}\to\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
.
Then the deformation functor
\textup{Deform}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}

is a formal
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-moduli problem.
When
k
is a field of characteristic zero,

\textup{Deform}^{\infty}_{\mathcal{E}}
is equivalent to
the formal moduli problem
\mathcal{F}^{(\infty)}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)[1]}
corresponding to the underlying dg Lie algebra
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)[1]

associated to
the
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)
.
Namely,
\mathcal{F}^{(\infty)}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)[1]}
is defined by
\mathcal{F}^{(\infty)}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)[1]}(R)=\operatorname{Map}_{Lie_{k}}(\mathbb{D}_{\infty}(R),\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{E}/k)[1])

where
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}:((\operatorname{CAlg}_{k})_{/k})^{op}\to Lie_{k}
is the Koszul duality functor which is a right adjoint to
the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain functor
Ch^{\bullet}:Lie_{k}\to((\operatorname{CAlg}_{k})_{/k})^{op}
.

As a direct application, we deduce an obstruction theory for the deformation theory (see Theorem
4.12
).

We note that left complete

t
-structures (cf.
[
18
]
) represent a natural form of completeness in practice.
Many important classes of stable
\infty
-categories naturally admit left complete

t
-structures—for example, the stable

\infty
-categories of quasi-coherent sheaves/complexes on derived algebraic stacks, of

D
-modules, of connective differential graded algebras, and so on.
Moreover, the process of left completion sends any
t
-structure to a left complete one.

We explain Theorem
1.1
from the perspective of Koszul duality.
Our main result may be understood as
a realization of
categorified Koszul duality.
By this, we mean

\mathbf{E}_{1}
-Koszul duality at the level of
\infty
-categories, where
a module
M
over an associative algebra
A
is replaced
by an
\mathcal{A}
-linear stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{M}

with a monoidal stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{A}
.
Indeed,
Lurie’s construction
of
\eta:\mathcal{D}ef_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{F}_{A\oplus\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)}^{(2)}
can be regarded as Koszul dual construction at level of stable
\infty
-categories, although this aspect is not explicitly emphasized.
In this paper, we build on this viewpoint by considering an “adjoint” version of the Koszul dual construction, taking into account left complete
t
-structures.
(A reflection of categorified Koszul duality can also be found in
[
12
, Chapter 8]
.)
In classical Koszul duality, a completeness condition is essential for establishing an equivalence between modules over an algebra and modules over its Koszul dual.
Analogously, in the categorified setting, issues of completeness naturally arise, and what we found is that
the notion of left complete
t
-structures
plays a key role.

There are several possible directions in which the results and methodology of this study could be extended.
For instance, let us consider a
k
-linear presentable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}

endowed with an
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-monoidal structure and a left complete
t
-structure compatible with the monoidal structure.
The deformation theory of
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
on the level of the
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-monoidal
\infty
-category with the left complete
t
-structure
seems likely to be governed by the
\mathbf{E}_{n+1}
-Hochschild cochain complex (see
[
5
]
) in the setting of formal
\mathbf{E}_{n+2}
-moduli problems.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we fix some notation and terminology
and review notions and some results.
In Section
3
we discuss left completions, which appear in categorified Koszul duality.
Along the way we also obtain an application to deformations of stable presentable
\infty
-categories without
t
-structures (Corollary
3.17
).
In Section
4

we prove the main results of this paper.

2.1.
Generalities on
t
-structures

Let us recall the notion of
t
-structures on stable
\infty
-categories.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category.
Recall that the homotopy category
\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C})
has the structure of a triangulated category.
A
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
is a pair of full subcategories
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
of
\mathcal{C}

such that
(\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}),\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}))
is a
t
-structure on the triangulated category
\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C})
. The pair
is required to satisfy the following conditions:
If we write
\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}
for
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}[-n]
, respectively, then

(1)

\mathcal{C}^{\leq-1}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
.

(2)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{C})}(C,C^{\prime})=0
for
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
and
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{\geq 1}
.

(3)

For any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists a cofiber square

\textstyle{D\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E}

such that
D\in\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
and
E\in\mathcal{C}^{\geq 1}
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
is a
t
-structure.
The inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}
has a right adjoint

\tau^{\leq n}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\leq n},

and the inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}

has a left adjoint

\tau^{\geq n}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}.

There exists a canonical equivalence
\tau^{\geq 0}\circ\tau^{\leq 0}\simeq\tau^{\leq 0}\circ\tau^{\geq 0}
.
We define

H^{0}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}

to be
\tau^{\geq 0}\circ\tau^{\leq 0}
(equivalently,
\tau^{\leq 0}\circ\tau^{\geq 0}
).
The intersection
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is called the heart.
The heart
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is an abelian category (see
[
1
]
).
We write
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}=\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
.
We define
H^{n}=H^{0}\circ[n]:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
where

[n]
is the
n
-fold shift (suspension) functor.

Remark 2.1
.

Given a cofiber sequence
C\to C^{\prime}\to C^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathcal{C}
, there exists a canonical long exact sequence

\cdots\to H^{n}(C)\to H^{n}(C^{\prime})\to H^{n}(C^{\prime\prime})\to H^{n+1}(C)\to\cdots

in
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
(see
[
1
]
).

We often use the notation
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
to denote a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
.
Note that
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
: the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is spanned by those objects
C^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(C,C^{\prime})
is contractible for any
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\leq-1}
. Dually,
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
.
By
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0})
we mean a stable
\infty
-category endowed with the
t
-structure determined by a full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
be a
t
-structure on a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
.

(1)

For any
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, the left adjoint functor
\tau^{\geq n}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}
to the inclusion

\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}\subset\mathcal{C}
induces a morphism
C\to\tau^{\geq n}(C)
, determined by the unit map. For all
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, this yields the sequence

C\to\cdots\to\tau^{\geq n-1}(C)\to\tau^{\geq n}(C)\to\tau^{\geq n+1}(C)\to\cdots

in
\mathcal{C}
.
The
t
-structure is left complete if, for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the induced morphism
C\to\varprojlim_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\tau^{\geq n}(C)
is an equivalence.

(2)

The right adjoint functor
\tau^{\leq n}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}
to the inclusion

\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}\subset\mathcal{C}
induces a morphism
\tau^{\leq n}(C)\to C
, determined by the counit map. For all
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, this yields the sequence

\cdots\to\tau^{\leq n-1}(C)\to\tau^{\leq n}(C)\to\tau^{\leq n+1}(C)\to\cdots\to C

in
\mathcal{C}
.
The
t
-structure is right complete if, for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the induced morphism
\varinjlim_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\tau^{\leq n}(C)\to C
is an equivalence.

(3)

The
t
-structure is left separated if
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}=0
.
Dually, it is right separated if
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{\geq n}=0
.

(4)

If
\mathcal{C}
admits filtered colimits and the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}
is closed under filtered colimits in
\mathcal{C}
, we say that the
t
-structure is compatible with filtered colimits.

(5)

The
t
-structure
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
is accessible if the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
is presentable.

(6)

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
has a monoidal structure. If the tensor product functor
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
sends
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}\times\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
to
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
and the unit object lies in
\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}
, we say that the
t
-structure is compatible with the monoidal structure.

Remark 2.3
.

If
C\in\cap_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{\leq n}
, then
\tau^{\geq n}C\simeq 0
for all
n\in\mathbf{Z}
.
It follows that if a
t
-structure
(\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0})
is left complete,
then it is left separated.
Dually, if a
t
-structure is right complete,
then it is right separated.

Example 2.4
.

Let
A
be a connective associative ring spectrum, and let
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}
be the stable
\infty
-category of left
A
-module spectra.
Define
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\geq 0}
as the full subcategory of
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}
spanned by objects
M
such that
\pi_{n}(M)=0
for all
n<0
, and
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\leq 0}
as the full subcategory spanned by objects
M
such that
\pi_{n}(M)=0
for all
n>0
.
Then
(\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\leq 0},\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\geq 0})
defines an accessible, left complete, and right complete
t
-structure (see
[
18
, 7.1.1.13]
).
The full subcategory
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\leq 0}
is also characterized as the smallest full subcategory which contains
A
and is closed under small colimits.
Moreover, the
t
-structure is compatible with filtered colimits, as
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\geq 0}\subset\operatorname{LMod}_{A}
is closed under filtered colimits.
This
t
-structure is compatible with the monoidal structure when
A
is an
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra.

Recall that

2.4.
Koszul duals of algebras

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
be a monoidal
\infty
-category. We write
\mathbf{1}
for a unit object.
We review the Koszul duality of algebras in
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
(see
[
18
]
,
[
19
, X]
for more details).
Let
\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\mathcal{M})_{/\mathbf{1}}
be the
\infty
-category of augmented
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-algebra objects (associative algebra objects) in
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
.
For a pair
(\epsilon_{A}:A\to\mathbf{1},\epsilon_{B}:B\to\mathbf{1})\in\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}(\mathcal{M})\times\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}(\mathcal{M})
we put

\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B}):=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\mathcal{M})}(A\otimes B,\mathbf{1})\times_{\operatorname{Map}(A,\mathbf{1})\times\operatorname{Map}(B,\mathbf{1})}\{(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B})\}.

Fix
\epsilon_{B}:B\to\mathbf{1}
and consider the functor

u_{\epsilon_{B}}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})^{op}\to\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
given informally by
[\epsilon_{A}:A\to\mathbf{1}]\mapsto\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B})
.
The Koszul dual
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)\in\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})
of
B
(we abuse notation since
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)
depends on the augmentation
\epsilon_{B}:B\to\mathbf{1}
)
is defined by the universal property that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)
represents the functor
u_{\epsilon_{B}}
.
Under a mild condition on
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
, there exist
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B)
and functorial equivalences

\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{M})}(A,\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}}(B))\simeq\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B})

for
\epsilon_{A}:A\to\mathbf{1}
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a natural number and let
\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
be the
\infty
-category of
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebras in
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
.
We write
\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})=\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})_{/k}
for the
\infty
-category

\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})_{/k}
of augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebras.
We consider the Koszul duals of augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebras.
We take
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
to be
\operatorname{Alg}_{n-1}^{\otimes}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
. There exists the Koszul duality functor

\mathbb{D}_{n}:\operatorname{Alg}_{n}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\simeq\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\operatorname{Alg}_{n-1}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k}))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\operatorname{Alg}_{n-1}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k}))\simeq\operatorname{Alg}_{n}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{op}

(see e.g.
[
19
, X, 4.4]
).
Let
\epsilon_{B}:B\to k
be an augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebra.
As mentioned above,

\mathbb{D}_{n}(B)
is determined by the universal property.
For an augmented
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-algebra
\epsilon_{C}:C\to k
, we write

\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{B},\epsilon_{C})=\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})}(B\otimes_{k}C,k)\times_{\operatorname{Map}(B,k)\times\operatorname{Map}(C,k)}\{(\epsilon_{B},\epsilon_{C})\}.

Then the functor
\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{op}\to\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
given by
[\epsilon_{C}:C\to k]\mapsto\textup{Pair}(\epsilon_{B},\epsilon_{C})

is representable by
\mathbb{D}_{n}(B)
.
There is a universal “Koszul dual pairing”
B\otimes_{k}\mathbb{D}_{n}(B)\to k
corresponding to
the identity map
\textup{id}\in\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})}(\mathbb{D}_{n}(B),\mathbb{D}_{n}(B))
.
The Koszul dual
\mathbb{D}_{n}(B)
can also be interpreted as a centralizer of
B\to k
, see
[
18
, 5.3.1]
.
We refer to a comprehensive account
[
10
]
for centralizers and Koszul duals.
We refer to
\mathbb{D}_{n}(B)
as the
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-Koszul dual of
B
.

The Koszul duals can be described in terms of bar constructions.
Let
\textup{Bar}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\to\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
be the functor
given by
[B\to k]\mapsto k\otimes_{B}k
, which we refer to as the bar construction of augmented algebras.
Then
k\otimes_{B}k
admits a structure of a coaugmented coalgebra in a suitable way, and
Bar

is promoted to
\textup{Bar}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\to\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{1}((\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{op})^{op}

(notice that the target is the
\infty
-category of coaugmented coalgebras).
Interating bar construction we have
induces
\textup{Bar}^{n}:\operatorname{Alg}_{n}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\to\operatorname{Alg}_{n}^{+}((\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{op})^{op}
.
According to
[
19
, X, 4.4.20]
, for
B\in\operatorname{Alg}^{+}_{n}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
, the
\mathbf{E}_{n}
-Koszul dual
\mathbb{D}_{n}(B)

is equivalent to
the
k
-linear dual
\mathcal{H}om_{k}(\textup{Bar}^{n}(B),k)
.
In particular,
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-Koszul dual of
B\to k
is given by the endomorphism algebra
\operatorname{End}_{B}(k)
with the canonical
augmentation
\operatorname{End}_{B}(k)\to\operatorname{End}_{k}(k)\simeq k
(here
k
is regarded as a left
B
-module).

3.
Koszul duality and left completion

3.1.

Let

A
be an augmented
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra in
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
. Suppose that
A
is connective.
Note that
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\leq 0}
is the smallest full subcategory which contains
A
and is closed under small colimits (see Exmaple
2.4
,
[
18
, 7.1.1.13]
).
Thus,
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\leq 0}
contains an unit object
R
and is closed under tensor products
so that the monoidal
\infty
-category
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\otimes}

can be regarded as an algebra object in
\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}\pm}^{\textup{L}}
.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a complete
A
-linear stable
\infty
-category endowed with
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
,
that is,
\mathcal{D}\in\operatorname{RMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}\pm}^{\textup{L}})
.
In particular, the
t
-structure is accessible, left complete, and right complete.
The tensor product of
\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}}
induces the right complete accessible
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
.
This
t
-structure is determined by

(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\leq 0}
defined as
the smallest full subcategory which contains a collection of objects
\{D\otimes_{A}k\}_{D\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}}

and is closed under small colimits and extensions.
Here
D\otimes_{A}k
is the image of
(D,k)\in\mathcal{D}\times\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
under the canonical functor
\mathcal{D}\times\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\to\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
.

From
[
18
, 4.8.4.6]
there exists a canonical equivalence
\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\simeq\operatorname{RMod}_{k}(\mathcal{D})
.
We consider the forgetful functor

u:\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\simeq\operatorname{RMod}_{k}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{D}.

Lemma 3.1
.

The functor
u:\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\simeq\operatorname{RMod}_{k}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{D}

is
t
-exact.
Moreover,
M
lies in
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\leq 0}

if and only if its image in
\mathcal{D}
lies in
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
.

Proof.
Note that the forgetful functor
u
preserves small colimits.
Note also that
D\otimes_{A}k\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
for any
D\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}

since
k
lies in
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\leq 0}
and by definition the tensor product induces
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\times\operatorname{LMod}_{A}^{\leq 0}\to\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
.
Taking account of the definition of
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\leq 0}

we conclude that
u
is right
t
-exact.

Next, we will prove that
u
is left
t
-exact.
Let
v:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}

be the left adjoint induced by the base change functor
\operatorname{LMod}_{A}\to\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
.
This left adjoint carries
D
to
D\otimes_{R}k
.
In particular,
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
maps to
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\leq 0}
.
It follows that
v
is right
t
-exact and
u
is left
t
-exact.
Moreover,
E\in\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}

lies in
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\geq 0}

if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{h}(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})}(D\otimes_{A}k[1],E)

for any
D\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
.
Thus,
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\geq 0}=u^{-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
.

\Box

Strictly speaking, the following is not necessary for Theorem
1.1
, but useful.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an object of
\operatorname{RMod}_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}\pm}^{\textup{L}})
as above.
Let
\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\in(\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}})_{k}

denote the relative tensor product in
\operatorname{Pr}_{\textup{t}+}^{\textup{L}}
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}

is the relative tensor product in
\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{\mathbb{S}}

endowed with the
t
-structure determined by
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\leq 0}

defined above.
Then the
t
-structure
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k},(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\leq 0})

is left complete.

Proof.
For
M\in\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{A}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
we consider the natural morphism

\theta:M\to\lim_{n\to-\infty}(\tau^{\geq n}M)
.
It suffices to prove that
\theta
is an equivalence.
Note that
u:\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{R}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k}\to\mathcal{D}
is
t
-exact and preserves small colimits (see Lemma
3.1
),
and
\mathcal{D}
and

are right complete.
Thus we have

\displaystyle u(M)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle u(\lim_{n\to-\infty}(\tau^{\geq n}M))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to-\infty}u((\tau^{\geq n}M))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to-\infty}\tau^{\geq n}u(M).

By the left completeness of
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0})
,
the composite is an equivalence.
Thus,
u(M)\to u(\lim_{n\to-\infty}(\tau^{\geq n}M))

is an equivalence.
The forgetful functor
u
is conservative so that
\theta

is an equivalence.

\Box

consists of those functors

Put another way,
(\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{LMod}_{a}}\operatorname{Mod}_{k})^{\heartsuit}\simeq\operatorname{RMod}_{k}(\mathcal{D})\times_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{D}^{\heartsuit}

The restriction to the heart

(that is, the abelian category of

where the right-hand side is the abelian category of

-linear coproduct-preserving exact functors.

spanned by objects

. Here

is the ordinary endomorphism algebra.
As a consequence one may identify the heart

Theorem 4.9
.

The deformation functor
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}:\textup{Art}_{2}\to\widehat{\mathcal{S}}

4.3.
Commutative bases

We consider the restriction to deformations to commutative bases. We assume that the base field
k
is of characteristic zero.
Let
\operatorname{CAlg}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\to\operatorname{Alg}_{2}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
be the canonical forgetful functor obtained from
the canonical map of
\infty
-operads
\mathbf{E}_{2}^{\otimes}\to\mathbf{E}_{\infty}^{\otimes}
.
It induces
\textup{Art}=\textup{Art}_{\infty}\to\textup{Art}_{2}
so that the composition gives the functor

\operatorname{Fun}(\textup{Art}_{2},\mathcal{S})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Fun}(\textup{Art},\mathcal{S}).

According to
[
13

it preserves formal moduli problems so that we have the restriction
res_{2/\infty}:\widehat{\mathsf{Stack}}_{2}^{*}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathsf{Stack}}_{\infty}^{*}
.
Let
\textup{Lie}_{k}
be the
\infty
-category of dg Lie algebras over
k
.
The
\infty
-category is obtained from the category of dg Lie algebras by inverting quasi-isomorphisms.
Through the categorical equivalence
\textup{Lie}_{k}\simeq\widehat{\mathsf{Stack}}_{\infty}^{*}
(see
[
19
, X, 2.0.2]
) and
\operatorname{Alg}_{2}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\simeq\widehat{\mathsf{Stack}}_{\infty}^{*}

the functor
res_{s/\infty}
corresponds to

\operatorname{Alg}_{2}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\to\textup{Lie}_{k}.

The underlying complex of

is
\operatorname{Ker}(\epsilon)[1]
where
\operatorname{Ker}
denotes the fiber (see
[
13
, Proposition 7.7]
).
By abuse of notation we write

for the underlying dg Lie algebra

We set

From a formal moduli problem, one can obtain the obstruction theory of deformations. We will explain this consequence for the reader’s convenience.
We refer the reader to
[
4

Let
p:R^{\prime}\to R

\textup{Art}_{2}

\textup{Art}_{2}

Example 4.11
.

Let
p:R^{\prime}\to R
be a surjective map of ordinary (discrete) Artin local algebras in
Art
.
Let
I=\operatorname{Ker}(p)
and let
\mathfrak{m}_{R^{\prime}}
be the maximal ideal of
R^{\prime}
.
We assume that
\mathfrak{m}_{R^{\prime}}\cdot I=0
, that is,
p
is an elementary extension of Artin local algebras.
There exists a pullback square

\textstyle{R^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{k\oplus I[1]}

in

We remark that every surjective map of ordinary Artin local algebras in
Art

decomposes as a sequence of elementary extensions.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}\in\pi_{0}(\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R))
.
We consider the problem of whether or not
\mathcal{D}
is promoted to
R^{\prime}
along
p:R^{\prime}\to R

Theorem 4.12
.

There exists an element
\textup{Obs}(\mathcal{D},p)\in\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{i+2}(\mathcal{E}/k)\otimes_{k}V

such that
\textup{Obs}(\mathcal{D},p)=0
if and only if there exists
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\in\pi_{0}(\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R^{\prime}))
lying over
\mathcal{D}
along the map
p
.

Proof.
By Theorem
4.9
, we have the canonical equivalence

\alpha:\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R^{\prime})\simeq\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R)\times_{\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(k\oplus V[i])}\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(k).

Note that
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(k)
is a contractible space.
Since
\mathbb{D}_{2}(k\oplus V[i])
is a free
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra generated by
V^{\vee}[-i-2]
(
[
19
, X, Proposition 4.5.6]
),
it follows that
\phi:\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(k\oplus V[i])\simeq\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{i+2}(\mathcal{E}/k)\otimes_{k}V
.
Here,
V^{\vee}
is the dual vector space.
Moreover, by the construction,

0\in\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{i+2}(\mathcal{E}/k)\otimes_{k}V
corresponds to the trivial deformation, that is, the image of
\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(k)\to\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(k\oplus V[i])
.
We define
\textup{Obs}(\mathcal{D},p)
to be the image of
\mathcal{D}\in\pi_{0}(\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R))

in
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{i+2}(\mathcal{E}/k)\otimes_{k}V
(through
\phi
).
Taking into account the equivalence
\alpha
, we see that
there exists
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\in\pi_{0}(\textup{Deform}_{\mathcal{E}}(R^{\prime}))
lying over
\mathcal{D}
if and only if

\textup{Obs}(\mathcal{D},p)
is equivalent to the trivial deformation.
The latter condition amounts to the condition that
\textup{Obs}(\mathcal{D},p)=0
.

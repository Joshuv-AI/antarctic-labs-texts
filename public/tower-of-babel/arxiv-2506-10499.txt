Alexander Freiszlinger, Dirk Pauly, Dirk Praetorius

Abstract
. The recent work [Kurz et al. , Numer. Math., 147 (2021)] proposed functional a posteriori error estimates for boundary element methods (BEMs) together with a related adaptive mesh-refinement strategy. Unlike most a posteriori BEM error estimators, the proposed functional error estimators cover Galerkin as well as collocation BEM and, more importantly, do not control the error in the integral density on the boundary, but the error of the potential approximation in the domain, which is of greater relevance in practice. The estimates rely on the numerical solution of auxiliary problems on auxiliary strip domains along the boundary, where the strips are affected by the adaptive mesh-refinement and hence vary. For Galerkin BEM, we prove that the proposed adaptive mesh-refinement algorithm yields convergence of the potential error to zero. Due to the structural difference to residual-based estimators, the proof requires new ideas.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, be a bounded Lipschitz domain with polyhedral boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
. We consider the Poisson problem with inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary data
g
, i.e.,

(1)

\displaystyle\Delta u^{\star}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega\quad\text{subject to}\quad u^{\star}=g\quad\text{on }\Gamma.

For the numerical solution of (
1
), we employ the boundary element method (BEM).
For the ease of presentation in this introduction, we consider an indirect ansatz based on the single-layer potential

(2)

\displaystyle(\widetilde{V}\phi^{\star})(x)\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y)\phi^{\star}(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=u^{\star}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega

with unknown density
\phi^{\star}
, where

(3)

G(x)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x|&\text{for }d=2\\
\frac{1}{(d-2)\lvert\mathbb{S}_{d-1}\rvert}\lvert x\rvert^{2-d}&\text{for }d\geq 3\end{cases}

denotes the fundamental solution of the Laplacian. Here,
\lvert\mathbb{S}_{d-1}\rvert
denotes the surface area of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Taking the trace of (
2
) on
\Gamma
, we obtain the weakly-singular boundary integral equation

(4)

\displaystyle(V\phi^{\star})(x)=g(x)\quad\text{for almost all }x\in\Gamma,

where the integral representation of
(V\phi^{\star})(x)
coincides with that of
(\widetilde{V}\phi^{\star})(x)
(at least for bounded densities) but is now evaluated on
\Gamma
(instead of the interior of
\Omega
). Supposing that
\diam(\Omega)<1
in case of
d=2
(which can always be ensured by scaling of
\Omega
), the boundary integral equation (
4
) admits a unique solution
\phi^{\star}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
; see, e.g.,
[
McL00
]
. Given a triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
of the boundary
\Gamma
, the boundary element method (BEM) approximates
\phi^{\star}\approx\phi_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
by a
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
-piecewise polynomial of degree
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where the index
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
indicates the sequential step of a mesh-refining adaptive algorithm. Independently of how
\phi_{\ell}
is computed (e.g., Galerkin BEM or collocation BEM with/without inexact iterative solver), this yields an approximation

(5)

\displaystyle u_{\ell}\coloneqq\widetilde{V}\phi_{\ell}\quad\text{satisfying}\quad\Delta u_{\ell}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Unlike usual notation, we stress that
u_{\ell}
is
not
a discrete function but computed by an integral operator applied to the discrete density
\phi_{\ell}
, i.e.,
u_{\ell}
is only data sparse. Since
\nabla u^{\star}\approx\nabla u_{\ell}
is usually of physical relevance and thus targeted by numerical simulation, the work
[
KPP+21
]
proposed the functional
a posteriori
error estimate

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}&\leq\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\nabla w_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}+C_{\textnormal{osc}}\,{\rm osc}_{\ell},\end{split}

where
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\in\bm{H}({\rm div},\omega_{\ell})
and
w_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\omega_{\ell})
are computable finite element functions that solve auxiliary problems on an
\ell
-dependent and shrinking strip
\omega_{\ell}\subseteq\Omega
and
{\rm osc}_{\ell}
are appropriate data approximation terms; see Section
3
below.
The proposed adaptive strategy in
[
KPP+21
]
relies only on the upper bound in (
6
) and adapts the strip
\omega_{\ell}
in each step of the adaptive loop so that the necessary degrees of freedoms to compute
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}
and
w_{\ell}
appear to be proportional to the number of boundary elements
\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
.
Of particular relevance is the fact that the upper and lower bound in (
6
) hold with
known constant

1
, where the adaptive algorithm, employed for Galerkin BEM with discrete density
\phi_{\ell}^{\star}=\phi_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
and induced potential
u_{\ell}^{\star}\coloneqq\widetilde{V}\phi_{\ell}^{\star}
, empirically led to optimal convergence rates for the error and accurate error estimation
\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\approx\lVert\nabla w_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}
in the experiments of
[
KPP+21
]
.

The purpose of the present work is to show that the proposed adaptive strategy applied for Galerkin BEM indeed guarantees plain convergence

(7)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\text{as }\ell\to\infty,

where the index
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
indicates the sequential nature of the adaptive algorithm proposed in
[
KPP+21
]
.
While similar and even stronger results are available for adaptive Galerkin methods that aim for the optimal adaptive approximation of the integral density
\phi^{\star}
(see, e.g.,
[
FKMP13
,
Tso13
,
FFK+14
,
GP22
,
GP22a
]
), we note that the present analysis is impacted (and challenged) by the fact that the strip domain
\omega_{\ell}\subseteq\Omega
is changed in each step of the adaptive loop.

We note that our proof of convergence (
7
) hinges on the use of Galerkin BEM as this allows to guarantee
a priori
convergence of the computed integral densities
\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\to\phi_{\infty}^{\star}
in the natural Sobolev space
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
as well as on the assumption of additional regularity
g\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
(despite only
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
) for the given Dirichlet data in (
1
) that, as in other works
[
BCD04
,
AFK+13
,
FFK+14
,
FPP14
]
, is exploited to localize the data approximation terms.

Preliminaries and Notation

2.1.
General notation

Throughout this paper, let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, with polyhedral Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
. By
\lvert\cdot\rvert
, we denote, without any ambiguity, the absolute value of a scalar, the Euclidean norm of a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of a surface (piece), or the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue volume of a measurable set.

Again, without any ambiguity, we write
\int_{\Xi}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
for integration over a Lebesgue measurable set
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
and over a Hausdorff measurable set
\Xi\subseteq\Gamma
.

For Lipschitz domains
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
, we denote by
\bm{n}_{\partial\Xi}
the outward pointing unit normal vector on
\partial\Xi
.

For
1<r<+\infty
,
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{r}(\Xi)}
denotes the usual
L^{r}
-norm and
1<r^{\prime}<+\infty
is the Hölder conjugate, i.e.,
1/r+1/r^{\prime}=1
. For
r=2
, we additionally write
\langle\cdot\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\cdot\rangle_{\Xi}
for the
L^{2}(\Xi)
-inner product. For vector-valued spaces
[L^{r}(\Xi)]^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we will omit the superscript if it is clear from the context.

Throughout, discrete (or data-sparse) quantities are indicated by an index, e.g.,
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
is a conforming triangulation of
\Gamma
with corresponding Galerkin BEM approximation
\phi_{\ell}^{\star}
and induced potential
u_{\ell}^{\star}
. Moreover, we always use the same index for related quantities, e.g.,
\phi_{\ell}
is a BEM approximation corresponding to
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
etc.
Finally, we abbreviate notation in proofs and write
A\lesssim B
, if there holds
A\leq cB
with a constant
c>0
which is independent of the adaptivity index
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and clear from the context. We write
A\simeq B
to abbreviate
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.

2.2.
Integer-order Sobolev spaces

For Lipschitz domains
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
, we define the usual integer-order Sobolev space

W^{1,r}(\Xi)\colon=\{w\in L^{r}(\Xi)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\nabla w\in L^{r}(\Xi)\}

with corresponding norm

\lVert w\rVert_{W^{1,r}(\Xi)}\coloneqq\bigl(\lVert w\rVert_{L^{r}(\Xi)}^{r}+\lVert\nabla w\rVert_{L^{r}(\Xi)}^{r}\bigr)^{1/r},

where
\nabla
denotes the distributional gradient. Furthermore, defining
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Xi)
as the space of smooth functions with compact support in
\Xi
, we set

W^{1,r}_{\partial\Xi}(\Xi)\coloneqq\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Xi)\cap W^{1,r}(\Xi)}^{W^{1,r}(\Xi)}.

Remark 2.1
.

Usually, one employs the notation
W^{1,r}_{0}(\Omega)=W^{1,r}_{\partial\Omega}(\Omega)=W^{1,r}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
for the space of
W^{1,r}
-functions with zero boundary conditions. However, our approach involves subdomains
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
and corresponding boundary conditions. We therefore decided to use the notation
W^{1,r}_{\partial\Xi}(\Xi)
to be explicit on these boundary conditions.

For boundary pieces
\Xi\subseteq\Gamma
, we define

W^{1,r}(\Xi)\coloneqq\{\varphi\in L^{r}(\Xi)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi\in L^{r}(\Xi)\}

with corresponding norm

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W^{1,r}(\Xi)}\coloneqq\bigl(\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{r}(\Xi)}^{r}+\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi\rVert_{L^{r}(\Xi)}^{r}\bigr)^{1/r},

where
\nabla_{\Gamma}
denotes the surface gradient on the boundary
\Gamma
. For
r=2
, we write
H^{1}(\Xi)=W^{1,2}(\Xi)
for either
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
or
\Xi\subseteq\Gamma
. Additionally, we also write
H^{1}_{\partial\Xi}(\Xi)=W^{1,2}_{\partial\Xi}(\Xi)
for
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
being a Lipschitz domain. Moreover, we use the convention that
W^{0,r}(\Xi)=L^{r}(\Xi)
and
H^{0}(\Xi)=L^{2}(\Xi)
as well as
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{W^{0,r}(\Xi)}=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{r}(\Xi)}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{0}(\Xi)}=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{2}(\Xi)}
for
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
or
\Xi\subseteq\Gamma
.

2.3.
Fractional-order Sobolev spaces

For
0<s<1
, we define the
W^{s,r}(\Xi)
-seminorm as

|w|_{W^{s,r}(\Xi)}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\Xi}\int_{\Xi}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{r}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+sr}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}&\text{if }\Xi\subseteq\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\left(\int_{\Xi}\int_{\Xi}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{r}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d-1+sr}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}&\text{if }\Xi\subseteq\Gamma,\end{cases}

and, additionally,

|w|_{W^{1,r}(\Xi)}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\lVert\nabla w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Xi)}&\text{if }\Xi\subseteq\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Xi)}&\text{if }\Xi\subseteq\Gamma.\end{cases}

For
0<s<2
and
s\neq 1
we define the
W^{s,r}(\Xi)
-norm as

\lVert w\rVert_{W^{s,r}(\Xi)}\coloneqq(\lVert w\rVert_{W^{\lfloor s\rfloor,r}(\Xi)}^{r}+|w|_{W^{s-\lfloor s\rfloor,r}(\Xi)}^{r})^{1/r},

establishing the fractional-order Sobolev space

W^{s,r}(\Xi)\coloneqq\{w\in L^{r}(\Xi)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\lVert w\rVert_{W^{s,r}(\Xi)}<+\infty\},

where
\mathbb{N}_{0}\ni\lfloor s\rfloor\leq s
denotes the largest possible integer and
W^{\lfloor s\rfloor,r}(\Xi)
is the usual integer-order Sobolev space. In accordance with the definition of integer-order Sobolev spaces, we set
H^{s}(\Xi)\coloneqq W^{s,2}(\Xi)
for
0<s<2
and
s\neq 1
. Given a Lipschitz domain
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
, the trace operator
(\cdot)|_{\partial\Xi}\colon W^{s,r}(\Xi)\rightarrow W^{s-1/r,r}(\partial\Xi)
is well-defined and bounded for
1/r<s<2
. Furthermore, there holds

W^{1,r}_{\partial\Xi}(\Xi)=\rm{ker}((\cdot)|_{\partial\Xi})\cap W^{1,r}(\Xi).

Lastly, we note that the
W^{s,r}
-norm and
W^{s,r}
-seminorm are absolutely continuous with respect to the measure
\lvert\cdot\rvert
of the underlying space, i.e., for all
w\in W^{s,r}(\Xi)
, there holds

(8)

\lim_{\lvert T\rvert\to 0}\lVert w\rVert_{W^{s,r}(T)}=0=\lim_{\lvert T\rvert\to 0}\lvert w\rvert_{W^{s,r}(T)}.

2.4.
Negative-order Sobolev spaces on the boundary

Let
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain. For
0\leq s\leq 1
, we define the negative-order Sobolev spaces on
\partial\Xi
as

W^{-s,r}(\partial\Xi)\coloneqq(W^{s,r^{\prime}}(\partial\Xi))^{\prime},

where the duality pairing is defined by continuous extension of the pairing between
L^{r^{\prime}}(\partial\Xi)
and
L^{r}(\partial\Xi)
and, without any ambiguity, is denoted by
\langle\cdot\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\cdot\rangle_{\partial\Xi}
. The space
H^{-s}(\partial\Xi)
and the associated duality pairing is defined accordingly.

2.5.
The space
H(\div,\Xi)

For
\Xi\subseteq\Omega
being a Lipschitz domain, we set

H(\mathrm{div},\Xi)\coloneqq\{\bm{\sigma}\in[L^{2}(\Xi)]^{d}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\div\bm{\sigma}\in L^{2}(\Xi)\}

equipped with the usual graph norm

\lVert\bm{\sigma}\rVert_{H(\mathrm{div},\Xi)}\coloneqq\left(\lVert\bm{\sigma}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Xi)}^{2}+\lVert\div\bm{\sigma}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Xi)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We note that the normal traces
\bm{\sigma}|_{\partial\Xi}\cdot\bm{n}_{\partial\Xi}
of
\bm{\sigma}\in H(\div,\Xi)
are well-defined and belong to
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Xi)
by Green’s formula
\colon
For given
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Xi)
, we choose
w\in H^{1}(\Xi)
such that
w|_{\partial\Xi}=\varphi
. Then, we define

\langle\bm{\sigma}|_{\partial\Xi}\cdot\bm{n}_{\partial\Xi}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\rangle_{\partial\Xi}\coloneqq\langle\bm{\sigma}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla w\rangle_{\Xi}+\langle w\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\div\bm{\sigma}\rangle_{\Xi}.

2.6.
Meshes

A
k
-dimensional simplex
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a compact set satisfying
T=\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{N}_{T})
with vertices
\mathcal{N}_{T}=\{z_{0},\ldots,z_{k}\}
for some
z_{0},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\lvert T\rvert>0
for the
k
-dimensional measure. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
be a conforming mesh on
\Omega
, i.e., a partition of
\overline{\Omega}
into
d
-dimensional simplices
T
such that the intersection of two different elements
T\cap T^{\prime}
is either empty or the joint
k
-dimensional compact simplex
T\cap T^{\prime}=\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{N}_{T}\cap\mathcal{N}_{T^{\prime}})
. Throughout, we assume that all considered meshes
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
are obtained by a finite amount of newest-vertex bisection refinements of some initial conforming mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
of
\Omega
. We refer to
[
Ste08
]
for newest vertex bisection for
d\geq 2
with admissible
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, as well as to
[
KPP12
]
for
d=2
and to
[
DGS25
]
for
d\geq 2
with non-admissible
\mathcal{T}_{0}
. It is well-known that newest vertex bisection guarantees uniform
\kappa
-shape regularity, i.e., there exists
\kappa>0
depending only on
\mathcal{T}_{0}
such that each refined mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
satisfies

\sup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\frac{\mathrm{diam}(T)}{\lvert T\rvert^{1/d}}\leq\kappa.

Moreover,
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
induces a conforming boundary mesh on
\Gamma
by

(9)

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\{T\cap\Gamma\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\text{ with }(d-1)\text{-dimensional measure }\lvert T\cap\Gamma\rvert>0\},

which is also
\kappa
-shape regular in the sense of

\sup_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}\frac{\mathrm{diam}(F)}{\lvert F\rvert^{d-1}}\leq\kappa.

We denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
the sets of (
0
-dimensional) nodes, (
1
-dimensional) edges and (
(d-1)
-dimensional) facets of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, respectively. We define the patch of a set
U\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
by

(10a)

\Omega_{\ell}[U]\coloneqq\mathrm{interior}\Bigl(\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[U]\Bigr)\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[U]\coloneqq\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}T\cap\overline{U}\neq\emptyset\}.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
k
-patch of
U
as

(10b)

\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}[U]\coloneqq\mathrm{interior}\Bigl(\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}[U]\Bigr)\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}[U]\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[\Omega_{\ell}^{[k-1]}[U]]&\text{for }k>1,\\
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[U]&\text{for }k=1\end{cases}.

To ease notation, we write
\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}[z]\coloneqq\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}(\{z\})
and
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}[z]\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}(\{z\})
for any
z\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
. The
k
-patch
\Gamma_{\ell}^{[k]}[U]
on the boundary is defined accordingly, with the volume mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
replaced by the induced boundary mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
.

We define the piecewise constant mesh-size function
h_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
by
h_{\ell}|_{T}\coloneqq h_{T}\coloneqq|T|^{1/d}
for every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
. The quantities
h_{E}\coloneqq\lvert E\rvert=\mathrm{diam}(E)
and
h_{F}\coloneqq\lvert F\rvert^{1/(d-1)}
for edges
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
and facets
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, respectively, are defined analogously.

2.7.
Discrete spaces

Given
q\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbb{P}^{q-1}(U)
be the space of polynomials of degree at most
q-1
defined on some set
U
and denote by

\mathcal{P}^{q-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}^{q-1}(T)\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\}

the space of
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
-piecewise polynomials of degree at most
q-1
.

We define the usual FEM-space of globally continuous
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
-piecewise polynomials by

\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{v\in\mathcal{P}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v\text{ is continuous on }\Omega\}=\mathcal{P}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\cap H^{1}(\Omega).

Given
\Upsilon\subseteq\Gamma
, we set

\mathcal{S}_{\Upsilon}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{v\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}v|_{\Upsilon}=0\}.

Furthermore, we define the
H(\div,\Omega)
-conforming Raviart–Thomas space

\mathcal{R}\mathcal{T}^{q-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{\bm{\sigma}\in H(\div,\Omega)\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\bm{\sigma}|_{T}\in[\mathbb{P}^{q-1}(T)]^{d}+\mathbb{P}^{q-1}(T)\cdot\mathrm{id}\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\},

where
\mathrm{id}(x)=x
denotes the identity. Given
\Upsilon\subseteq\Gamma
, we set

\mathcal{R}\mathcal{T}^{q-1}_{\Upsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\coloneqq\{\bm{\sigma}\in\mathcal{R}\mathcal{T}^{q-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\bm{\sigma}|_{\Upsilon}\cdot\bm{n}_{\Upsilon}=0\}.

Discrete spaces on
\Gamma
and
\omega_{\ell}
are defined accordingly. Lastly, we introduce the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projections

\begin{split}Q_{\ell}^{\Omega}&\colon L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{P}^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}),\quad Q_{\ell}^{\Omega}(w)|_{T}\coloneqq\langle w\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{T}/\lvert T\rvert,\\
Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}&\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{P}^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}),\quad Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}(\varphi)|_{F}\coloneqq\langle\varphi\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>1\rangle_{F}/\lvert F\rvert.\end{split}

Given
0<s\leq 1
,
Q_{\ell}^{\Omega}
and
Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
satisfy

(11)

\begin{split}\lVert w-Q_{\ell}^{\Omega}w\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}&\leq Ch_{T}^{s}\lvert w\rvert_{H^{s}(T)}\quad\text{for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\text{ and }w\in H^{s}(T),\\
\lVert\varphi-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}&\leq Ch_{F}^{s}\lvert\varphi\rvert_{H^{s}(F)}\quad\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\text{ and }\varphi\in H^{s}(F),\end{split}

with a constant
C>0
depending only on
\kappa
-shape regularity.

2.8.
Bases of discrete spaces

For
U\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}(U)
the set of all Lagrange nodes of the space
\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
in
U
. We abbreviate
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}(\overline{\Omega})
and let
(\zeta_{\ell,z}^{q})_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}}
be the corresponding nodal basis of
\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
, i.e.,
\zeta_{\ell,z}^{q}(z^{\prime})=\delta_{zz^{\prime}}
for all
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}
with Kroneckers’s delta
\delta
. Hence, there holds

v_{\ell}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}}\alpha_{z}\zeta_{\ell,z}^{q}\quad\text{for all }v_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})

with unique coefficients
\alpha_{z}=v_{\ell}(z)\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, the functions
(\zeta_{\ell,z}^{q}|_{T})_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}(T)}
are related to the standard basis of
\mathbb{P}^{q}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})
, denoted by
(\widetilde{\zeta}_{j})_{j=1}^{n_{q,d}}
, by an affine diffeomorphism
\Phi_{T}\colon T\to T_{\mathrm{ref}}
, i.e., for all
j=1,\ldots,n_{q,d}
there is a unique
z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}(T)
such that
\widetilde{\zeta}_{j}=\zeta_{\ell,z}^{q}|_{T}\circ\Phi_{T}
.

From now on, we assume that the polynomial degree
q
is fixed. To ease notation, we will write
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{q}
,
\zeta_{\ell,z}\coloneqq\zeta_{\ell,z}^{q}
, and
n_{d}=n_{q,d}
.

Clearly, it holds
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{D}_{\ell}\cap\Gamma
. Hence, every
f_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
in the form

f_{\ell}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(\Gamma)}\alpha_{z}\zeta_{z}|_{\Gamma}

can be naturally lifted to a function
\widehat{f}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
by setting

(12)

\widehat{f}_{\ell}\coloneqq\sum_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(\Gamma)}\alpha_{z}\zeta_{z},

and we note that the discrete lifting
\widehat{(\cdot)}\colon\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})\to\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
is a linear operator.

2.9.
Boundary integral equation

For
-1/2\leq s\leq 1/2
, we consider the single-layer integral operator
V\colon H^{s-1/2}(\Gamma)\to H^{s+1/2}(\Gamma)
defined by

(13)

(V\psi)(x)\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma}G(x-y)\psi(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{for all }x\in\Gamma,

where
G
is the fundamental solution of the Laplace equation; see (
3
). The operator
V\colon H^{s-1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{s+1/2}(\Gamma)
is a bounded isomorphism for
-1/2\leq s\leq 1/2
and elliptic for
s=0
, provided that
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<1
if
d=2
, which can always be achieved by scaling; see, e.g.,
[
McL00
]
. Hence, for every
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
there is a unique solution
\phi^{\star}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
of the problem

\langle V\phi^{\star}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\psi\rangle_{\Gamma}=\langle g\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\psi\rangle_{\Gamma}\quad\text{for all }\psi\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma),

which satisfies
\lVert\phi^{\star}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\simeq\lVert g\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}
, where the hidden equivalence constants depend only on the operator norm of
V
and its inverse and hence only on
\Omega
. Moreover, the single-layer operator
\widetilde{V}\colon H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\to H^{1}(\Omega)
with the same integral representation as in (
13
) is a continuous linear operator satisfying
-\Delta(\widetilde{V}\phi)=0
for all
\phi\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
.

2.10.
Galerkin BEM

Given
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we seek
\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
such that

(14)

\langle V\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\psi_{\ell}\rangle_{\Gamma}=\langle g\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\psi_{\ell}\rangle_{\Gamma}\quad\text{for all }\psi\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}).

By ellipticity, the Lax–Milgram lemma yields existence and uniqueness of the solution
\phi_{\ell}^{\star}
of (
14
) and there holds the Céa lemma

(15)

\lVert\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\min_{\psi_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})}\lVert\phi^{\star}-\psi_{\ell}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)},

where the hidden constant depends only on ellipticity and continuity of
V
and hence only on
\Omega
.

3.
Functional error estimation and main result

3.1.
General notation

Throughout this section, we adhere to the following notation:
Let
\Omega_{0}\subseteq\Omega
be an initial strip domain along the boundary, i.e.,
\Omega_{0}
is a bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain with
\Gamma=\partial\Omega\subseteq\partial\Omega_{0}
.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
be a conforming triangulation of
\Omega_{0}
into simplices.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}
be the induced conforming triangulation of the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
into surface simplices defined in (
9
). For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}[\Gamma]\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
be the
k
-patch of
\Gamma
, e.g., for

, the patch
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[2]}[\Gamma]
consists of all volume simplices
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
that touch an element
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
with
T^{\prime}\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset
; see (
10
) for the formal definition.
Let
\omega_{\ell}\coloneqq{\rm interior}\big(\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}\big)
denote the induced strip domain along
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}\coloneqq\partial\omega_{\ell}\backslash\Gamma
be the complementary boundary part, i.e.,
\partial\omega_{\ell}=\Gamma\cup\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}
. By definition of
\omega_{\ell}
, we note that
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}
is indeed closed and well separated from
\Gamma
in the sense that
{\rm dist}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c})>0
. We refer to Figure
1
for an illustration.

Figure 1.
Boundary strip
\omega_{\ell}
(
blue
) of strip depth
k=2
of a square domain. The outer boundary
\Gamma
is depicted in
red
, the complementary boundary
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}
in
green
.

In the remainder of this section, we recall the ideas of
[
KPP+21
]
for functional
a posteriori
error estimation and formulate our main result. To this end, let
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
und
q\in\mathbb{N}
be polynomial degrees for the BEM as well as the auxiliary problems, respectively.

3.2.
Functional
a posteriori
error estimation (upper bound)

Suppose additional regularity
g\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
for the inhomogeneous Dirichlet data in (
1
) (instead of only
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
).
Note that this leads to additional regularity
\phi^{\star}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
of the solution to (
4
) (instead of only
\phi^{\star}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
).
Let
\phi_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
be an approximation of
\phi^{\star}
.
Since
\Delta u_{\ell}=0
for
u_{\ell}\coloneqq\widetilde{V}\phi_{\ell}
, the Dirichlet principle yields

\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\nabla w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all }w\in H^{1}(\Omega)\text{ with }w|_{\Gamma}=(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})|_{\Gamma}=g-u_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}.

Since discrete functions cannot satisfy continuous Dirichlet conditions, let
J_{\ell}\colon H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
be an
H^{1}(\Gamma)
-stable projection, i.e., there is a constant
C_{\textnormal{stab}}
such that
\lVert J_{\ell}\psi\rVert_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq C_{\textnormal{stab}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}
for all
\psi\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
.
Then, the discrete auxiliary problem reads:
Find
w_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
such that

(16)

\begin{split}\langle\nabla w_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\nabla v_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}&=0\quad\text{for all }v_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}_{\partial\omega_{\ell}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}),\\
w_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}&=g_{\ell}\coloneqq J_{\ell}(g-V\phi_{\ell}),\\
w_{\ell}|_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}}&=0.\end{split}

It is well-known that (
16
) admits a unique solution
w_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
; see, e.g.,
[
BCD04
,
AFK+13
,
FPP14
]
.
According to the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}
, we can extend
w_{\ell}
by zero to
w_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore,
[
KPP+21
, Theorem 5]
proves

(17)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\nabla w_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert(1-J_{\ell})(g-V\phi_{\ell})\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}.

By assumption on
g
and the mapping properties of
V
, there holds
g-V\phi_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
. Moreover, the assumptions on the projection
J_{\ell}
prove the Poincaré-type estimate

(18)

C_{\textnormal{osc}}^{-1}\,\lVert(1-J_{\ell})(g-V\phi_{\ell})\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\leq\lVert h_{\ell}^{1/2}\nabla_{\Gamma}(1-J_{\ell})(g-V\phi_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\eqqcolon{\rm osc}_{\ell},

where the constant
C_{\textnormal{osc}}
depends only on
C_{\textnormal{stab}}
,
\Gamma
, and
\kappa
-shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
; see
[
KOP13
, Theorem 4]
for
q=1
and
[
AFF+15a
, Lemma 7]
for
q\geq 1
. Combining (
17
)–(
18
), we prove

(19)

\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\nabla w_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{\textnormal{osc}}\,{\rm osc}_{\ell}\eqqcolon\eta_{\ell}+C_{\textnormal{osc}}\,{\rm osc}_{\ell}

which is the computable upper bound of (
6
) with known constant
1
up to data oscillations.

Remark 3.1
.

The auxiliary problem (
16
) is motivated by the fact that, for
\omega_{\ell}=\Omega
and
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}=\emptyset
, the discrete function
w_{\ell}
is the unique FEM approximation of the potential error, i.e.,
w_{\ell}\approx w\coloneqq u^{\star}-u_{\ell}
. As noted above, the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}
is chosen to allow for the conforming extension of
w_{\ell}
from the strip domain
\omega_{\ell}
to the whole of
\Omega
.

3.3.
Functional
a posteriori
error estimation (lower bound)

In order to obtain a computable lower bound, we aim to find a solution pair
(\bm{\tau}_{\ell},p_{\ell})\in\mathcal{R}^{q-1}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})\times\mathcal{P}^{q-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
such that

(20)

\begin{split}\langle\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}+\langle\div\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>p_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}&=\langle g-V\phi_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\bm{\sigma}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
\langle\div\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>q_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}&=0\end{split}

for all
(\bm{\sigma}_{\ell},q_{\ell})\in\mathcal{R}^{q-1}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})\times\mathcal{P}^{q-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
. It is well-known that this problem has a unique solution
(\bm{\tau}_{\ell},p_{\ell})\in\mathcal{R}^{q-1}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})\times\mathcal{P}^{q-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
; see, e.g.,
[
BC05
]
.
According to the homogeneous boundary condition
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{n}=0
on
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}
included in the definition of
\mathcal{R}^{q-1}_{\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
, we can extend
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}
by zero to
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\in\bm{H}(\div,\Omega)
.
From
[
KPP+21
, Theorem 5]
, we then have that

2\langle g-V\phi_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}-\lVert\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}^{2}\leq\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Plugging
(\bm{\sigma}_{\ell},q_{\ell})=(\bm{\tau}_{\ell},p_{\ell})
into (
20
), we obtain

\begin{split}\lVert\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}^{2}+\langle\div\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>p_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}&=\langle g-V\phi_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
\langle\div\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>p_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}&=0\end{split}

and hence

(21)

\tau_{\ell}^{2}\coloneqq\lVert\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}^{2}=2\langle g-V\phi_{\ell}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}-\lVert\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}^{2}\leq\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Altogether, we proved the computable lower bound of (
6
) with known constant
1
.

Remark 3.2
.

The auxiliary problem (
20
) is motivated by the fact that, for
\omega_{\ell}=\Omega
and
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}=\emptyset
, the discrete function
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}
is the unique mixed FEM approximation of the potential error, i.e.,
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}\approx\bm{\tau}\coloneqq\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell})
. As noted above, the homogeneous boundary condition on
\Gamma_{\ell}^{\sf c}
is chosen to allow for the conforming extension of
\bm{\tau}_{\ell}
from the strip domain
\omega_{\ell}
to the whole of
\Omega
.

3.4.
Galerkin BEM, adaptive algorithm, and main result

From now on, we restrict to Galerkin BEM. Given the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
, let
\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
be the unique solution to (
14
).
The adaptive algorithm is driven by the computable upper bound from (
6
).
To this end, let
w_{\ell}^{\star}=w_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
be the unique solution to (
16
) for
\phi_{\ell}=\phi_{\ell}^{\star}
, where we employ the Scott–Zhang projector
J_{\ell}\colon L^{2}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
on the boundary as considered, e.g., in
[
FPP14
]
for
q=1
and
[
AFF+15a
]
for
q\geq 1
; see Section
4
below for the definition.
With this, we define the
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
-elementwise contributions

(22)

\begin{split}\eta_{\ell}(T)&\coloneqq\lVert\nabla w_{\ell}^{\star}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)},\\
{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)&\coloneqq\lVert h_{\ell}^{1/2}\nabla_{\Gamma}(1-J_{\ell})(g-V\phi_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial T\cap\Gamma)}\quad\text{for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}.\end{split}

Based on these indicators and
\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}}\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}=\lVert\nabla w_{\ell}^{\star}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell})}^{2}
and
\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}}{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}={\rm osc}_{\ell}^{2}
, the following adaptive algorithm has essentially been proposed by
[
KPP+21
]
, where we note that
\eta_{\ell}(T)=0={\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}
.

{algorithm}

[Adaptive algorithm]

Input:

Initial strip domain
\Omega_{0}\subseteq\Omega
with
\Gamma=\partial\Omega\subseteq\partial\Omega_{0}
and conforming triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{0}
of
\Omega_{0}
,
polynomial degrees
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for the BEM in (
14
) and
q\in\mathbb{N}
for the estimator in (
19
),
patch order
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
marking function
M\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
that is continuous at
0=M(0)
,
and right-hand side
g\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
.

Loop:
For all
\ell=0,1,2,\dots
, repeat the following steps:

(i)

Extract the boundary mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}|_{\Gamma}
from
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
and solve (
14
) to obtain the Galerkin BEM solution
\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})
.

(ii)

Extract the boundary strip
\omega_{\ell}\coloneqq{\rm interior}\big(\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}\big)
and the associated triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}[\Gamma]
,
and compute the auxiliary FEM solution
w_{\ell}^{\star}\in\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega})
by solving (
16
) with
\phi_{\ell}=\phi_{\ell}^{\star}
and
J_{\ell}
being the Scott–Zhang projector.

(iii)

Compute the error indicators
\eta_{\ell}(T)
and
{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)
from (
22
) for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}
.

(iv)

Determine a set
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}
(usually of minimal cardinality) such that

(23)

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]\leq M\Bigl(\max_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]\Bigr).

(v)

Generate a new triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{\ell+1}
by use of newest vertex bisection such that at least all elements in
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
are refined, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\ell+1}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The marking criterion (
23
) is proposed and analyzed in the context of adaptive finite element methods in
[
Sie10
]
. Clearly, it is satisfied with
M(t)=t
if

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]=\max_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big],

i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
contains one element with largest overall indicator, which can easily be enforced algorithmically at linear cost.
Instead, the work
[
KPP+21
]
uses the Dörfler marking criterion from
[
D“”or96
]
to select, for some fixed
0<\theta\leq 1
, the set
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}
via

(24)

\displaystyle\theta\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big].

Usually,
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
is obtained by sorting of the overall indicators so that

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}\backslash\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]\leq\min_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]

and hence (
23
) is satisfied with
M(t)=t
. If
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
in (
24
) is obtained by inexact binsort as proposed in
[
Ste07
]
, then

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\omega}\backslash\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]\leq 2\min_{T\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell}}\big[\eta_{\ell}(T)^{2}+{\rm osc}_{\ell}(T)^{2}\big]

and hence(
23
) is satisfied with
M(t)=2t
.

Remark 3.4
.

Algorithm
3.4
could also include the computation of the estimator
\tau_{\ell}
from (
21
) in order to determine the guaranteed lower bound (
6
). However, our main result of Theorem
3.5
exploits only the upper bound (
19
) of (
6
).

The following plain convergence theorem is the main result of this work. The subsequent sections will be concerned with its proof.

Theorem 3.5

(Efficiency and plain convergence of functional upper bound)
.

Under the foregoing assumptions, Algorithm
3.4
guarantees the existence of a constant
C_{\textnormal{eff}}>0
such that

(25)

\begin{split}C_{\textnormal{eff}}^{-1}\eta_{\ell}\leq\lVert\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\leq C_{\textnormal{V}}\lVert u^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\end{split}

as well as plain convergence in the sense of

(26)

C_{\textnormal{P}}^{-1}\lVert u^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:compupperbound}}}{{\leq}}\eta_{\ell}+C_{\textnormal{osc}}\mathrm{osc}_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0,

where
C_{\textnormal{P}}>0
is a Poincaré constant and
C_{V}>0
is the operator norm of
\widetilde{V}\colon H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\to H^{1}(\Omega)
, which both depend only on
\Omega
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
3.5
(Efficiency and plain convergence)

The proof of Theorem
3.5
crucially hinges on
a priori
convergence of the solutions to the auxiliary problems (
16
), which relies on a special construction of the Scott–Zhang projector
P_{\ell}\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
from
[
SZ90
]
. We briefly present this construction as well as properties of
P_{\ell}
. Subsequently, we prove
a priori
convergence of the auxiliary solutions and efficiency of the functional error estimator. The proof of Theorem
3.5
is then concluded by combining these results.

4.1.
Scott–Zhang projector

Recall that
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}=\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(\overline{\Omega})
denotes the Lagrange nodes of
\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
. In essence, the construction of the Scott–Zhang projector
P_{\ell}\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{S}^{q}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
in
[
SZ90
]
is based on selecting
(K_{\ell,z})_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}
with
z\in K_{\ell,z}
, where
K_{\ell,z}
is either an element
K_{\ell,z}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
or a facet
K_{\ell,z}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
for all
z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
.Then, one chooses
\psi_{\ell,z}\in\mathbb{P}^{q}(K_{\ell,z})
such that

\int_{K_{\ell,z}}\psi_{\ell,z}\zeta_{\ell,z^{\prime}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{zz^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}\text{ and }z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}\cap K_{\ell,z}

to define the projector
P_{\ell}
by

(27)

P_{\ell}w=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}\Bigl(\int_{K_{\ell,z}}\psi_{\ell,z}w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\Bigr)\zeta_{\ell,z}.

The construction of
P_{\ell}
thus provides considerable freedom in the choice of the sets
(K_{\ell,z})_{z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}
, which can be exploited to ensure certain additional properties of
P_{\ell}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist sets
(K_{\ell,z})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}
such that the following properties hold.

(i)

If
z\in\mathrm{interior}(T_{z})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
for some
T_{z}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
, we set
K_{\ell,z}\coloneqq T_{z}
.

(ii)

If
z\in\mathrm{interior}(F_{z})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
for some facet
F_{z}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, we set
K_{\ell,z}\coloneqq F_{z}
.

(iii)

If
z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(\Gamma)
, then
K_{\ell,z}\subseteq\Gamma
.

(iv)

If
z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(\omega_{\ell})
, then
\mathrm{interior}(K_{\ell,z})\subseteq\omega_{\ell}
.

(v)

If
z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(\Omega\setminus\omega_{\ell})
, then
K_{\ell,z}\subseteq\Omega\setminus\omega_{\ell}
.

(vi)

If
z\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\ell-1}
and
\zeta_{\ell,z}=\zeta_{\ell-1,z}
, then

.

for some facet

\ell

Suppose that

Lemma 4.7

Let

\begin{split}&\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[\Gamma]}\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{r}(T)}^{r}\\
&\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:boundarypoincare}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[\Gamma]}\Bigl(h_{T}^{-r/r^{\prime}}\lVert J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{r}(\partial T\cap\Gamma)}^{r}+\lvert J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rvert_{W^{1/r^{\prime},r}(\partial T\cap\Gamma)}^{r}\Bigr)\\
&\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:invineq}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[\Gamma]}h_{T}^{-r/r^{\prime}}\lVert J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{r}(\partial T\cap\Gamma)}^{r}=\lVert h_{\ell}^{-1/r^{\prime}}J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}.\end{split}

We define

(74)

s_{r}\coloneqq[r-1+(d-1)(r/2-1)]/r\quad\text{for any }r\geq 2

and note that

(75)

\begin{split}\lVert h^{-1/r^{\prime}}_{\ell}J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}&=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}h_{F}^{-r/r^{\prime}}\lVert J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{r}(F)}^{r}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:invineq}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}h_{F}^{1-r}h_{F}^{(d-1)(1-r/2)}\lVert J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{r}\\

&\leq\lVert h_{\ell}^{-s_{r}}J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{r-2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}\lVert h_{\ell}^{-s_{r}}J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\\

Lemma
4.3

\begin{split}&\lVert h_{\ell}^{-s}J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}h_{F}^{-2s}\lVert J_{\ell}(f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:SZtraceL2}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}h_{F}^{-2s}\lVert f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell}[F])}^{2}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}h_{F}^{-2s}\lVert f-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}f\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:avgprop}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}\lvert f\rvert_{H^{s}(F)}^{2}\leq\lvert f\rvert_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}^{2}\quad\text{for all }0<s\leq 1.\end{split}

It remains to verify that

Proof 5.5

(

We define the limiting layer

\omega_{\infty}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\omega_{\ell}.

As our first step, we want to characterize the set
\omega_{\infty}
in terms of the involved triangulations.

Step 1 (Characterization of
\omega_{\infty}
).
Recall the set
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
of all elements which remain unrefined after a finite amount of steps; see (
50
). We want to prove that

(77)

\omega_{\infty}\subseteq\bigcup\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}T\subset\overline{\omega}_{\infty}\}\eqqcolon\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}\subseteq\overline{\omega}_{\infty}.

The inclusion
\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}\subseteq\overline{\omega}_{\infty}
is obvious. In order to show the converse inclusion, let
x\in\omega_{\infty}
. We aim to prove that
x\in\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}
. Since
x\in\omega_{\ell}=\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}[\Gamma]
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists a sequence
(\widetilde{T}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
x\in\widetilde{T}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{(k)}[\Gamma]
and
\widetilde{T}_{\ell+1}\subseteq\widetilde{T}_{\ell}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Furthermore, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists
T_{\ell}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
with
T_{\ell}\cap\Gamma\neq 0
and
\widetilde{T}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{[k]}[T_{\ell}]
. If
\widetilde{T}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(and hence
\widetilde{T}_{\ell}=\widetilde{T}_{k}
for all
k\geq\ell
), then
x\in\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}
. If
\widetilde{T}_{\ell}\notin\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
|\widetilde{T}_{\ell}|\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0
by
[
MSV08
, Corollary 4.5]
. By use of
\kappa
-shape regularity, we obtain

\mathrm{dist}(x,\Gamma)\leq\mathrm{dist}(x,\Gamma\cap\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}(T_{\ell}))\leq\mathrm{diam}(\Omega_{\ell}^{[k]}[T_{\ell}])\simeq|\widetilde{T}_{\ell}|^{1/d}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0.

This yields
x\in\Gamma
, contradicting
x\in\omega_{\infty}
since
\omega_{\infty}\subseteq\Omega
and
\Omega
is open, i.e.,
\Omega\cap\Gamma=\emptyset
. Hence, the case that
\widetilde{T}_{\ell}\notin\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
cannot occur. This proves (
77
).

Step 2 (Construction of
\widehat{g}_{\infty}
).

By Lemma
4.7
, there exists
\phi_{\infty}^{\star}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
such that
\lVert\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\infty}^{\star}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0

g_{\infty,\ell}\coloneqq J_{\ell}(g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star})
and denote by
\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}
its discrete lifting. To construct
\widehat{g}_{\infty}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we first define
\widehat{g}_{\infty}|_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{\infty}}\coloneqq 0
. Second, since
\omega_{\infty}
coincides with
\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}
up to a set of measure zero, it suffices to define
\widehat{g}_{\infty}|_{T}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
such that
T\subseteq\overline{\omega}_{\infty}
. We note that

\mathcal{T}_{\infty}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}},\quad\text{where }\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}\coloneqq\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\nonscript\>|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[T]\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\}\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0};

see
[
MSV08
, Section 4.1]
.
Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
with
T\subseteq\overline{\omega}_{\infty}
. Then, there is a smallest index
\ell_{T}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell_{T}}^{0}
. We define
\widehat{g}_{\infty}|_{T}\coloneqq\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}|_{T}
. Note that if
T\cap\Gamma=\emptyset
, then
\widehat{g}_{\infty}|_{T}=0
. Furthermore, if
\ell_{T}\leq\ell
, then
\mathcal{T}_{\ell_{T}}[T]=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}[T]
. Since
J_{\ell}(g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star})|_{\partial T\cap\Gamma}
depends only on
(g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star})|_{\Gamma_{\ell}[\partial T]}
(see Lemma

), this yields
\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}|_{T}=\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}|_{T}
.

Step 3 (
\widehat{g}_{\infty}
is weakly differentiable)
.
Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}
. Then, integration by parts proves

\begin{split}&\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\div\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\div\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(T)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\div\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(T)}\\
&\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq 0\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\cdot n_{T}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial T\setminus\Gamma)}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\langle\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(T)}.\end{split}

It remains to show that the first sum on the right-hand side vanishes. Let
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
with
T_{1}\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\neq T_{2}\cap\Gamma
such that
F\coloneqq T_{1}\cap T_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
for some sufficiently large
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
. Without loss of generality, it holds that
\ell_{T_{1}}\leq\ell_{T_{2}}
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{\ell_{T_{1}}}[T_{1}]\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
and
T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
, with
T_{1}\cap T_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, it follows that
T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell_{T_{1}}}[T_{1}]=\mathcal{T}_{\ell_{T_{2}}}[T_{1}]
. Since
\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T_{1}}}
depends only on the patch

and
(g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star})|_{\Gamma_{\ell_{T_{1}}}[T_{1}]}
, it follows that
\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T_{1}}}|_{T_{1}}=\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T_{2}}}|_{T_{1}}
. Because of
\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T_{2}}}\in H^{1}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})
, we obtain

\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T_{1}}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\cdot n_{T_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}=\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T_{2}}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\cdot n_{T_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}=-\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T_{2}}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\cdot n_{T_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}(F)}.

Since each interior facet is shared by precisely two elements, this guarantees that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}\nonscript\>,\allowbreak\nonscript\>\varphi\cdot n_{T}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial T\setminus\Gamma)}=0.

Therefore,
\widehat{g}_{\infty}
is weakly differentiable with
\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty}|_{\Omega\setminus\omega_{\infty}}=0
and
\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty}|_{T}=\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
with
T\subseteq\overline{\omega}_{\infty}
for all
\ell\in N_{0}
.

Step 4 (
\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}\to\widehat{g}_{\infty}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
)
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
L^{2}(\Gamma)
is dense in

, there exists
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
with
\lVert(\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\infty}^{\star})-\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\leq\varepsilon
. We start by showing the existence of
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

(78)

\lVert\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\omega_{\infty})}\lesssim\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }\ell\geq\ell_{0}.

We have

(79)

\begin{split}&\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\omega_{\infty})}=\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell}\setminus\omega_{\infty})}\\
&\qquad\leq\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell}\setminus\omega_{\infty})}+\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\varphi_{\varepsilon}))\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\end{split}

We first consider the first summand of the right-hand side of (
79
). Due to Proposition
5.3
, there exists

and
1/2<s_{r}\leq 1
such that

(80)

\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:boundarypoincare}}}{{\lesssim}}\lvert V\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rvert_{H^{s_{r}}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{s_{r}-1}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

The Hölder inequality yields

\begin{split}\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\ell}\setminus\omega_{\infty})}&\leq\lvert\omega_{\ell}\setminus\omega_{\infty}\rvert^{1/2-1/r}\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{r}(\omega_{\ell}\setminus\omega_{\infty})}\\
&\lesssim\lvert\omega_{\ell}\setminus\omega_{\infty}\rvert^{1/2-1/r}\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Since
\omega_{\infty}=\displaystyle{\bigcap_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\omega_{\ell}}
and
\varepsilon
is fixed, there exists
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\lvert\omega_{\ell}\setminus\omega_{\infty}\rvert^{1/2-1/r}\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\varepsilon
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
. For the second summand of (
79
), we exploit
Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}(V\phi^{\star}-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star})=0
(see Lemma
4.7
) and apply Proposition
5.3
with
r=2
and
s_{r}=1/2
to obtain

(81)

\begin{split}&\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V(\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\varphi_{\varepsilon})-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V(\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\varphi_{\varepsilon}))\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:boundarypoincare}}}{{\lesssim}}\lvert V(\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\lVert\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

Altogether, this yields (
78
). Furthermore, we have that

(82)

\begin{split}&\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{\infty})}^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\\
\ell_{T}>\ell\end{subarray}}\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\\
T\notin\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}\end{subarray}}\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}}\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}}\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}}\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}])}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}}\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}.\end{split}

Considering the first term right-hand side of (
82
), we have

(83)

\begin{split}\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}])}&\leq\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}(\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}])}\\
&\qquad+\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\varphi_{\varepsilon}))\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:perturbation}}}{{\lesssim}}\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}(\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}])}+\varepsilon.\end{split}

Hölder’s inequality together with (
80
) yields

\begin{split}&\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}(\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}])}\\
&\qquad\leq\big|\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}]\big|^{1/2-1/r}\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}(V\varphi_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{r}(\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}])}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\big|\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}]\big|^{1/2-1/r}\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}}
and
\varepsilon
is fixed, there exists
\ell_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\Big|\displaystyle{\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}]}\Big|^{1/2-1/r}\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\varepsilon
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{1}
. Hence, we see

\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\bigcup[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}])}\lesssim\varepsilon\quad\text{for }\ell\geq\max\{\ell_{0},\ell_{1}\}.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
83
) can be estimated analogously as in (
81
). It remains to estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
82
). Due to the definition of
\widehat{g}_{\infty}
, the lifting estimate (

) of Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
4.3
, and the properties (

) of

, we obtain

\begin{split}&\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}=\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell_{T}}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:liftcont}}}{{\lesssim}}h_{T}^{-1/2}\lVert g_{\infty,\ell_{T}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial T\cap\Gamma)}+\lvert g_{\infty,\ell_{T}}\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\partial T\cap\Gamma)}\\
&\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:SZ}}}{{\lesssim}}h_{T}^{-1/2}\lVert g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\ell_{T}}[T])}+\lvert g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star}\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell_{T}}[T])}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:avgprop}}}{{\lesssim}}\lvert g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star}\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell_{T}}[T])}.\end{split}

Therefore,
\kappa
-shape regularity establishes

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}}\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}}\lvert g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star}\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell}[T])}^{2}\lesssim\lvert g-V\phi_{\infty}^{\star}\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

Since
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}}
, we can choose
\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{0}}\lVert\nabla\widehat{g}_{\infty}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }\ell\geq\ell_{2}.

Altogether, this proves
\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon
for all

yielding
\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}\widehat{g}_{\infty}
and, in particular,
\widehat{g}_{\infty}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Step 5 (
\widehat{g}_{\ell}\to\widehat{g}_{\infty}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
)
. We have

\begin{split}\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq\lVert\nabla(\widehat{g}_{\infty,\ell}-\widehat{g}_{\infty})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}V(\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

The first term on the right-hand side converges to zero as

by Step 4. Note that
Q_{\ell}^{\Gamma}V(\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star})=0
. For the second term, we thus argue as in Step 4, and exploit the
a priori convergence of Lemma

to obtain that

\begin{split}&\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}V(\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\\
T\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\lVert\nabla\widehat{J_{\ell}}V(\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma}}\lvert V(\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star})\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\ell}[F])}^{2}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\lvert V\phi_{\infty}^{\star}-V\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\rvert_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0.\end{split}

This concludes the proof.

6.1.
Example 1 (Square domain, smooth potential)

We consider the Laplace–Dirichlet problem (
1
) with the prescribed exact solution

u^{\star}(x)=\sinh(2\pi x_{1})\cos(2\pi x_{2})\quad\text{in }\Omega\coloneqq(0,1/2)^{2}

on the square domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
which fulfills the scaling condition
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)=1/\sqrt{2}<1
.

Even though the exact solution
u^{\star}
and the data
g\coloneqq u^{\star}|_{\Gamma}
are smooth, one cannot expect that the density
\phi^{\star}
stemming from the indirect boundary integral formulation (
4
) is smooth, as it inherits generic singularities stemming from the interior and the exterior problem. As a consequence, uniform mesh-refinement will not lead to optimal convergence behavior
\lVert\phi^{\star}-\phi_{\ell}^{\star}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\Omega)}=\mathcal{O}((\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})^{-(p+3/2)})
for Galerkin BEM (
14
) with
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

We start Algorithm
3.4
with a coarse initial triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{0}
comprised of
512
triangles. The initial mesh, some adaptively refined meshes, and the corresponding boundary strips are visualized in Figure
2
.

Figure
3
shows the total upper bound
\eta_{\ell}+\mathrm{osc}_{\ell}
for lowest-order BEM
p=0
computed with
k=3
for
q=1,2
and different marking parameters
0<\theta<1
. We observe that Algorithm
3.4
exhibits optimal convergence rates
\lVert\nabla(u^{\star}-u_{\ell}^{\star})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\mathcal{O}(\eta_{\ell}+\mathrm{osc}_{\ell})=\mathcal{O}((\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}^{\Gamma})^{-3/2})
for any marking parameter
\theta\in\{0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8\}
with only minimal change with respect to the particular choice of
\theta
.

Towards Simple and Provable Parameter-Free Adaptive Gradient Methods
Yuanzhe Tao Yifeng Liu Huizhuo Yuan Xun Zhou Yuan Cao Quanquan Gu

Abstract
Optimization algorithms such as AdaGrad and Adam have significantly advanced the training of deep models by dynamically adjusting the learning rate during the optimization process. However, ad-hoc tuning of learning rates poses a challenge and leads to inefficiencies in practice. To address this issue, recent research has focused on developing “parameter-free” algorithms that operate effectively without the need for learning rate tuning. Despite these efforts, existing parameter-free variants of AdaGrad and Adam tend to be overly complex and/or lack formal convergence guarantees. In this paper, we present AdaGrad++ and Adam++, novel and simple parameter-free variants of AdaGrad and Adam with convergence guarantees. We prove that AdaGrad++ achieves comparable convergence rates to AdaGrad in convex optimization without predefined learning rate assumptions. Similarly, Adam++ matches the convergence rate of Adam without relying on any conditions on the learning rates. Experimental results across various deep learning tasks validate the competitive performance of Adam++.

Review of existing methods and preview of proposed methods

In this section, we give a brief review of the adaptive gradient methods, and discuss existing literature of parameter-free adaptive gradient methods, followed by a preview of our proposed methods.

We consider the optimization problem as follows

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x}),

(2.1)

where
f
can be a convex or nonconvex function. In order to optimize (
2.1
), the standard stochastic gradient descent (SGD) performs the following update rule

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta_{t}\mathbf{g}_{t},

(2.2)

where
\mathbf{g}_{t}
represents the stochastic gradient at the
t
-th iteration,
\eta_{t}
denotes the learning rate. Adaptive gradient methods
(
Duchi et al., 2011
;
Hinton et al., 2012
;
Kingma and Ba, 2015
;
Reddi et al., 2018
;
Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019
;
Chen et al., 2020
)
aim to give well-designed adjustments to the learning rate
\eta_{t}
, particularly focusing on applying different learning rates for different entries of the iterates.

Among popular adaptive gradient methods,
AdaGrad
(
Duchi et al., 2011
)
stands out as one of the pioneering methods. The update rule for AdaGrad is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\frac{\eta_{t}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbf{g}_{i}^{2}}+\delta}\cdot\mathbf{g}_{t},

(2.3)

where
\delta
is a small positive constant, and we use the common notation where the square
(\cdot)^{2}
and square root
\sqrt{\cdot}
operations are performed entry-wise when applied to a vector.

Adam
(
Kingma and Ba, 2015
)
is probably the most widely recognized adaptive gradient method. Compared with AdaGrad, it implements exponential moving averages over
\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}
’s, as well as momentum acceleration, with the update rule defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{t}=\beta_{1}\mathbf{m}_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{1})\mathbf{g}_{t},

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{t}=\beta_{2}\mathbf{v}_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{2})\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta_{t}\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_{t}}+\delta}.

(2.4)

Another line of research on parameter-free optimization seeks to reduce or remove the necessity of learning rate tuning. The distance over gradient (DoG)
(
Ivgi et al., 2023
)
framework is a popular method which sets the learning rate
\eta_{t}
in stochastic gradient descent (
2.2
) as

\eta_{t}=\frac{\max_{i\leq t}\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}}.

DoG can be treated as a modification on the AdaGrad-Norm algorithm
(
Duchi et al., 2011
;
Streeter and McMahan, 2010
;
Ward et al., 2020
)
with
\eta_{t}=D/\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}
,
where the parameter
D
is set as
\max_{i\leq t}\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}
in DoG. Several other parameter-free methods
(
Defazio and Mishchenko, 2023
;
Mishchenko and Defazio, 2024
)
also focused on estimating the parameter
D
with different criteria. Notably, these recent studies of parameter-free algorithms focus more on the variants of SGD, which do not implement the entry-wise adaptive learning rates in AdaGrad and Adam. Although several recent works
(
Defazio and Mishchenko, 2023
;
Mishchenko and Defazio, 2024
;
Defazio et al., 2024
)
proposed parameter-free variants of AdaGrad or Adam, they are mostly not backed up with theoretical guarantees. Moreover, existing parameter-free variants of AdaGrad and Adam are mostly relatively complicated, deviating significantly from the standard forms of AdaGrad and Adam.

Preview of our proposed methods.
Inspired by DoG
(
Ivgi et al., 2023
)
, we propose simple parameter-free variants of AdaGrad and Adam, which we call AdaGrad++ and Adam++ respectively. Specifically, AdaGrad++ follows the update rule of AdaGrad in (
2.3
), but with

\displaystyle\eta_{t}=d^{-1/2}\cdot\max_{i\leq t}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|_{2},

where
d
is the dimension of
\mathbf{x}
. Note that
\eta_{t}
is the maximum distance between the initialization
\mathbf{x}_{0}
and all the iterates along the optimization trajectory normalized by
\sqrt{d}
. Moreover, a specific and simplified case in Adam++ is directly based on the update rule of Adam in (
2.4
), with equivalent learning rate

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{t}=\frac{\max_{i\leq t}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|_{2}}{\sqrt{d(t+1)}}.

Compared with existing parameter-free versions of AdaGrad and Adam, AdaGrad++ and Adam++ are in a much simpler form. Interestingly, despite the simplicity, our analysis demonstrates that AdaGrad++ and Adam++ enjoy good theoretical convergence guarantees, and perform very well in various experiments. For more details, please refer to Sections
3
and
4
.

AdaGrad++: a parameter-free version of AdaGrad

In this section, we present the details of the AdaGrad++ algorithm, and then give theoretical guarantees on its performance in convex optimization.

3.1
Algorithm

We consider the optimization problem as introduced in (
2.1
) in the setting of stochastic optimization, and we assume access to a
stochastic gradient oracle

\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x})
satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x}]\in\partial f(\mathbf{x})
. The AdaGrad++ algorithm is presented in Algorithm
1
.

In Algorithm
1
, it is clear that the key innovation of AdaGrad++ lies in the introduction of the quantity
r_{t}=\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|_{2}/\sqrt{d}
, and the definition that
\eta_{t}=\max(\eta_{t-1},r_{t})
. These definitions are inspired by the DoG framework
(
Ivgi et al., 2023
)
, and are the key to a parameter-free approach. We would also like to comment that introducing the factor
d^{-1/2}
in the definition of
r_{t}
is crucial in AdaGrad++, resulting in strong theoretical guarantees and robust practical performance across different tasks with varying dimensions. The intuition is that AdaGrad++ implements different adaptive learning rates for different coordinates, and the
d^{-1/2}
factor converts the “total distance” in DoG to the “mean squared distance (displacement)”, which is more robust to
d
.

3.2
Convergence Guarantee

In this subsection, we present convergence guarantees of AdaGrad++ (Algorithm
1
) under the setting where
f(\mathbf{x})
is convex.
3
3

3

We also provide convergence guarantees for AdaGrad++ in the nonconvex setting in Appendix
E
.
We first give an assumption on the stochastic gradient
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x})
.

Assumption 3.1
.

There exists some continuous function
l:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x})\|_{2}\leq l(\mathbf{x})
almost surely.

Assumption
3.1
states that the stochastic gradients have a deterministic bound
l(\mathbf{x})
on their norm. By allowing different bounds at different
\mathbf{x}
, this assumption is much weaker compared to the more common Lipschitz assumption that directly requires that
\|\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x})\|_{2}
is bounded to a constant. The same assumption has been made in
Ivgi et al. (2023)
. Note that this assumption is strictly weaker than the typical bounded gradient norm assumption, and as we will show later, it yields stronger convergence results.

Our main result on the convergence of AdaGrad++ is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{T}
be the iterates of AdaGrad++. Further let
\tau\in\text{arg}\max_{t\leq T}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\frac{\eta_{i}}{\eta_{t}}
and define
\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\tau}=\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}\mathbf{x}_{t}}{\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}}
. Then under Assumption
3.1
, for any
\widetilde{\delta}\in(0,1)
,
L>0
and any
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with probability at least

1-\widetilde{\delta}-\mathbb{P}(\max_{t\leq T}l(\mathbf{x}_{t})>L)
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\tau})-f(\mathbf{x}^{*})\leq O\Bigg(\frac{M_{1}\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}+M_{4}+\sqrt{M_{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}^{2}+M_{3}}}{T}\Bigg),

where

\displaystyle M_{1}=\frac{D_{\tau}^{2}\sqrt{d}}{\eta_{0}}\cdot\log\bigg(\frac{\eta_{T}}{\eta_{0}}\bigg),M_{2}=\overline{D}_{\tau}^{2}\log^{2}\bigg(\frac{\eta_{T}}{\eta_{0}}\bigg)\cdot\log\bigg[\frac{60\log(6T)}{\widetilde{\delta}}\bigg],

\displaystyle M_{3}=L^{2}\log^{2}\bigg(\frac{\eta_{T}}{\eta_{0}}\bigg)\cdot\log^{2}\bigg[\frac{60\log(6T)}{\widetilde{\delta}}\bigg],M_{4}=\frac{D_{\tau}^{2}\delta d}{\eta_{0}}\log\bigg(\frac{\eta_{T}}{\eta_{0}}\bigg),

and
D_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty}
,
\overline{D}_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}
.

Theorem
3.2
gives the bound of
f(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\tau})
that is defined by an arbitrarily chosen reference point
\mathbf{x}^{*}
, and the bound contains a term
f(\mathbf{x}^{*})
as well as several other terms that are related to the distance between algorithm iterates and
\mathbf{x}^{*}
. This type of bound matches standard bounds in convex and Lipschitz/smooth optimization
(
Bubeck and others, 2015
)
. Moreover, the probability for the bound in Theorem
3.2
to hold depends on
\mathbb{P}(\max_{t\leq T}l(\mathbf{x}_{t})>L)
, and the bound holds with high probability when
\mathbb{P}(\max_{t\leq T}l(\mathbf{x}_{t})>L)
is small. It is worth noting that if
l(\cdot)
is always bounded, which corresponds to a Lipschitz
f
, then
\mathbb{P}(\max_{t\leq T}l(\mathbf{x}_{t})>L)=0
with an appropriately chosen constant
L
. Notably, Theorem
3.2
covers more general and non-Lipschitz cases as well, since
l(\cdot)
only needs to be bounded along the optimization trajectory
\mathbf{x}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{T}
to grant
\mathbb{P}(\max_{t\leq T}l(\mathbf{x}_{t})>L)=0
.

In addition, it is worth noting that
D_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty}
and
\bar{D}_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}
depends on
t
and may grow as
t
increases in the worst case. The same issue appears in DoG as discussed in
Ivgi et al. (2023)
. Nevertheless,
Ivgi et al. (2023)
argue that
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}
is typically bounded by
O(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2})
for all
t
, e.g.,
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq 3\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}
, so the iterates do not move too far away from
\mathbf{x}_{0}
. This is also observed in our experiments. In addition,
Ivgi et al. (2023)
proposed a step size scheme whose iterates are guaranteed to satisfy
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq O(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2})
with high probability (See Section 3.3 and Proposition 2 in their paper). By using a similar step size scheme, we can also develop modified versions of AdaGrad++ (and Adam++ in the next section), such that the distance
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}
will be bounded by
O(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2})
with high probability. Therefore, in the following discussion, we assume
\eta_{t}\leq O(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}/\sqrt{d})
,
D_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq O(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty})
and
\bar{D}_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq O(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2})
. Under these assumptions,
M_{1},M_{2}
and
M_{3}
can be viewed as some constants up to logarithmic factors.

With these assumptions in hand, Theorem
3.2
reveals that an important term
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}
determines the convergence rate of AdaGrad++. We note that a similar quantity has been investigated by
Zhou et al. (2018)
in the study of nonconvex convergence guarantees of adaptive gradient methods. This similarity demonstrates that our proposed parameter-free algorithm AdaGrad++ still captures the key nature of AdaGrad. Taking a closer look at the quantity
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}
, by definition, we have
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}}
. When the objective function is Lipschitz (i.e.,
l(\cdot)
is bounded), it is clear that a worst-case upper bound of
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}
is
\sqrt{T}
, leading to a
1/\sqrt{T}
bound on the convergence rate (see Corollary
B.1
in Appendix
B
). However, as discussed in
Zhou et al. (2018)
, here we point out that in practice, we often observe that
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}\ll\sqrt{T}
due to the fact that the algorithm converges and the stochastic gradients
\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|_{2}
may converge to zero. When
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}=O(T^{1/2-\alpha})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we will have a better convergence rate of AdaGrad++ (see Corollary
B.2
in Appendix
B
). Finally,
M_{1}
has a
\sqrt{d}
dependence, which may seem large. However, this is not the case, since
D_{\tau}
is defined with respect to the vector infinity norm, and thus
D_{\tau}^{2}\sqrt{d}
is of the same order as
\bar{D}_{\tau}^{2}
.

Adam++: a parameter-free version of Adam

.

There are several key points in
Algorithm

r_{t}=\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|_{2}/\sqrt{d}
introduced in AdaGrad++ to automatically adapt the “learning rate”. Moreover, Adam++ allows dynamically decaying first-moment parameter
\beta_{1t}=\beta_{1}\lambda^{t}
, which follows the definition in AMSGrad
(
Reddi et al., 2018

. When setting
\lambda=1
, we can recover the common setup with a constant
\beta_{1}
. The introduction of the decaying

\beta_{1t}
is due to technical reasons, and our theoretical analysis on Adam relies on a
\lambda\in(0,1)

Another key feature of Adam++ is that it covers two cases. In

, we implement entry-wise adaptive learning rates that are similar to AdaGrad and AdaGrad++. In

, we implement a more common exponential moving average of the second moment
\mathbf{v}_{t}
but also introduce another quantity
\mathbf{s}_{t}
. Particularly regarding the definition of
\mathbf{s}_{t}=\sqrt{(t+1)\cdot\max_{t^{\prime}\leq t}(\mathbf{v}_{t^{\prime}})}
, we note that the factor
\sqrt{(t+1)}
ensures reasonable scaling when incorporated with the quantity
r_{t}
. This factor makes the scaling of
\mathbf{s}_{t}
in
Case 2
more compatible with that in
Case 1
. Moreover, the max operation
\max_{t^{\prime}\leq t}(\mathbf{v}_{t^{\prime}})
is inherited from the AMSGrad modification to Adam
(
Reddi et al., 2018
)
, which has been shown to be crucial for establishing convergence guarantees. However, in practice, Adam (or AdamW) performs better than AMSGrad for training LLMs. Therefore, we apply Adam-like update rule

Case 2) for other computer vision experiments.

L>0
and any
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with probability at least

1-\widetilde{\delta}-\mathbb{P}(\max_{t\leq T}l(\mathbf{x}_{t})>L)
, the following results hold:

\displaystyle f(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\tau})-f(\mathbf{x}^{*})\leq O\Bigg(\frac{M_{1}\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}+\sqrt{M_{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}^{2}+M_{3}}}{T}\Bigg),

Here,
D_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty}
,
\overline{D}_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{*}\|_{2}
, and

\displaystyle M_{1}=\frac{D_{\tau}^{2}\sqrt{d}}{\eta_{0}}\log\bigg(\frac{\eta_{T}}{\eta_{0}}\bigg),

\displaystyle M_{2}=\overline{D}_{\tau}^{2}\log^{2}\bigg(\frac{\eta_{T}}{\eta_{0}}\bigg)\log\bigg[\frac{60\log(6t)}{\widetilde{\delta}}\bigg],

\displaystyle M_{3}=L^{2}\log^{2}\bigg(\frac{\eta_{T}}{\eta_{0}}\bigg)\log^{2}\bigg[\frac{60\log(6t)}{\widetilde{\delta}}\bigg].

Theorem
4.1
gives the convergence guarantee for Adam++. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first convergence guarantee of a parameter-free version of Adam.
The bound holds with high probability when
l(\cdot)
is bounded along the optimization trajectory
\mathbf{x}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{T}
.
As we explained before, it is reasonable to assume that
M_{1},M_{2}
and
M_{3}
are constants up to logarithmic factors. The subsequent discussion proceeds under this assumption.
Similar to the bound for AdaGrad++, the quantity
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}
is a key quantity: when
l(\mathbf{x})
is bounded, the worst-case bound of
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}
is
O(\sqrt{T})
, leading to a
\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{T})
convergence rate. However, if
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}=O(T^{1/2-\alpha})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we can expect a faster convergence rate.

Clearly, we can also establish the counterparts of Corollaries
B.1
and
B.2
for Adam++. However, to avoid repetitions, here we only give the corollary below as the counterpart of Corollary
B.2
. The counterpart of Corollary
B.1
can be obtained by setting
\alpha=0
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose that the assumptions in Theorem
4.1
hold. Further assume that there exist
G>0
such that
l(\mathbf{x})\leq G
and
\|\mathbf{s}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq G\cdot T^{1/2-\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,1/2)
. Then for any
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\tau})-f(\mathbf{x}_{*})\leq\widetilde{O}\Bigg(\frac{D_{\tau}^{2}G\cdot\sqrt{d}}{T^{1/2+\alpha}}\Bigg),

where
D_{\tau}=\max_{t\leq\tau}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{*}\|_{\infty}
.

In the worst case where
\alpha=0
, Corollary
4.2
gives a convergence rate of order
\sqrt{d/T}
for Adam++. This matches the convergence rate in the original paper of AMSGrad (Theorem 4 and Corollary 1 in
(
Reddi et al., 2018
)
) as well as those in more recent works (e.g., Theorems 4.3, 5.2, Corollaries 4.6, 5.5 in
Zhou et al. (2018)
and Theorems 1,2,3,4 in
Défossez et al. ()
). We note that a very recent work
(
Ahn and Cutkosky, 2024
)
gives a new convergence result for Adam improving the dependency in the dimension
d
. However,
Ahn and Cutkosky (2024)
focuses on the nonconvex setting and studies the convergence rate towards a stationary point, which is different from the setting considered here. We will also provide the convergence guarantee of Adam++ for nonconvex optimization in Appendix

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose two simple but effective algorithms, namely AdaGrad++ and Adam++, that are parameter-free variants of AdaGrad and AdamW respectively. We demonstrate that, despite the simple intuition, AdaGrad++ and Adam++ are guaranteed to converge with a reasonable convergence rate, and also perform surprisingly well in various experiments. These theoretical and empirical results highlight the potential of AdaGrad++ and Adam++ to be robust and practical choices for a wide range of optimization tasks.

Appendix A
Related Work

In this section, we give a more comprehensive review of the existing literature on parameter-free optimization and adaptive gradient methods.

Parameter-free optimization.
Several recent works have explored parameter-free algorithms based on modifications of the Polyak step size
(
Loizou et al., 2021
;
Gower et al., 2021
;
Orvieto et al., 2022
;
Rolinek and Martius, 2018
;
Berrada et al., 2020
)
. In addition, several studies have investigated step-size selection methods derived from Line-Search algorithms
(
Vaswani et al., 2019
;
Paquette and Scheinberg, 2018
)
.
Another line of works, including LARS
(
You et al., 2017a
)
, LAMB
(
You et al., 2017b
)
, Adafactor
(
Simonyan and Zisserman, 2015
)
, and Fromage
(
Bernstein et al., 2020
)
, introduced learning rate adjustment schemes based on the norms of iterates.
Moreover,
Chandra et al. (2022)
proposed a scheme to adjust the learning rates based on certain automatically calculated hypergradients. Several recent works
(
Orabona and Tommasi, 2017
;
Chen et al., 2022
)
have also proposed parameter-free algorithms by reducing the optimization process to a game of betting on a coin.
Another recent work
(
Kleinsorge et al., 2023
)
proposed a novel rotation invariant parameter-free algorithm based on exponential learning rate adaption.
Finally, a line of recent works
(
Orabona, 2014
;
Kempka et al., 2019
)
have studied parameter-free algorithms in solving specific learning tasks such as linear and kernel regression.

Adaptive gradient methods.
There is a large body of work on variants of AdaGrad and Adam. Specifically, RMSProp
(
Kurbiel and Khaleghian, 2017
)
was the first work that proposed using an exponential moving average instead of a cumulative sum to handle the second moment in AdaGrad.
Reddi et al. (2018)
pointed out an extreme case where Adam may face convergence issues, and proposed AMSGrad accordingly with convergence guarantees. RMSProp, Adam and AMSGrad have also inspired many variants, including SC-AdaGrad, SC-RMSprop
(
Mukkamala and Hein, 2017
)
, Sadagrad
(
Chen et al., 2018
)
, YOGI
(
Zaheer et al., 2018
)
, Padam
(
Chen et al., 2020
)
, and RAdam
(
Liu et al., 2019
)
.
More recently, several works such as STORM
(
Cutkosky and Orabona, 2019
)
, adaptive normalized SGD
(
Cutkosky and Mehta, 2020
)
, Adam+
(
Liu et al., 2020
)
, SUPER-ADAM
Huang et al. (2021)
implemented various variance reduction techniques in Adam.
Guo et al. (2021)
presented a novel convergence analysis for a family of Adam-style methods with an increasing momentum parameter for the first-order moment.

Alacaoglu et al. (2020)
proposed a new type of framework to analyze the regret of the Adam style methods.
Zhou et al. (2018)
established high-probability convergence guarantees of AdaGrad and Adam in nonconvex optimization. Moreover,
Taniguchi et al. (2024)
proposed a new adaptive gradient method, ADOPT, which resolves Adam’s non-convergence issue by removing the current gradient from the second-moment estimate and reordering the momentum and normalization updates. They also proved an
O(1/\sqrt{T})
convergence
rate with any choice of
\beta_{2}
without depending on the bounded noise
assumption.

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Before we start the proof, we provide the necessary background and definitions that will be used in our proof.

Definition C.1

(Bregman Divergence,
Bregman (1967)
)
.

Let
\psi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly convex and continuously differentiable function defined on a closed convex set
\Omega
. Then the Bregman divergence is defined as

\displaystyle B_{\psi}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\psi(\mathbf{x})-\psi(\mathbf{y})-\langle\nabla\psi(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\rangle,\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Omega.

The Bregman distance is the difference between the value of
\psi
at
\mathbf{x}
and the value of the first-order Taylor expansion of
\psi
around
\mathbf{y}
evaluated at point
\mathbf{x}
.

From the definition, it is easy to verify the following properties of Bregman divergence:

•

Gradient at
\mathbf{x}
:
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}B_{\psi}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\nabla\psi(\mathbf{x})-\nabla\psi(\mathbf{y})
,
\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Omega
.

•

Three-points identity:
B_{\psi}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+B_{\psi}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})-B_{\psi}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})=\langle\nabla\psi(\mathbf{z})-\nabla\psi(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\rangle
,
\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}\in\Omega
.

Proof of Theorem

Let

Appendix E
Convergence Analysis for Nonconvex Optimization

In this section, we provide convergence guarantees for AdaGrad++ and Adam++ in the nonconvex optimization setting.

E.1
Preliminaries

For the parameter
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote
\mathbf{x}_{*}=\text{arg}\min_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x})\geq-\infty
. And in this section,
\|\cdot\|

Assumption E.1
.

The gradient of

is bounded by a constant

, i.e.,
\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|\leq G
.

This assumption can be potentially relaxed to Assumption
3.1
, which we leave it as a future work.

Assumption E.2

The function

is

f(\mathbf{x})\leq f(\mathbf{y})+\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2},\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Note that if a function
f(\mathbf{x})
is
L
-smooth, we have for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f(\mathbf{y})\|\leq L\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{2}
.

E.1
and

Proof of Theorem

E.2

we have

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k+1}-f_{k}}{\eta_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq-\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{k}),\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}L}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\|^{2}.

Thus by rearranging, and by the definition of
\mathbf{g}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle\leq\frac{f_{k}-f_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}+\frac{\eta_{k}L}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\|^{2}.

(E.1)

Take the sum of (
E.1
) from
k=0
to
t-1
, and by the definition of
\mathbf{H}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}+\delta}\leq\underbrace{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{f_{k}-f_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}}_{I_{1}}+\frac{L}{2}\underbrace{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\|^{2}}_{I_{2}}.

(E.2)

For the
I_{1}
on the right-hand side of (
E.2
), by the facts that
\eta_{k}
is nondecreasing and
f_{*}
is the global minimal value, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{f_{k}-f_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}}{\eta_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}f_{k}\cdot\bigg(\frac{1}{\eta_{k-1}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}\bigg)-\frac{f_{t}}{\eta_{t-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{f_{0}}{\eta_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}f_{*}\cdot\bigg(\frac{1}{\eta_{k-1}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}\bigg)-\frac{f_{*}}{\eta_{t-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}-f_{*}}{\eta_{0}}.

(E.3)

For the
I_{2}
in (
E.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{(s_{k,i}+\delta)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{t-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{t-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg[2\log\bigg(\frac{s_{t,i}}{g_{0,i}}\bigg)+1\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{t-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg[2\log\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{t}\cdot G}{g_{0,i}}\bigg)+1\bigg],

(E.4)

where the first inequality holds due to the fact that
\eta_{k}
is non-decreasing, the second inequality holds by Lemma
F.4
and applying the fact that
g_{k,i}^{2}=s_{k,i}^{2}-s_{k-1,i}^{2}
, and the third inequality holds follows from the fact that
\mathbf{s}_{t,i}\leq\sqrt{t}\cdot G
by the definition of
\mathbf{s}_{t}
and Assumption
E.1
.
For the term on the left-hand side of (
E.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}+\delta}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{t-1,i}+\delta}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{s_{t-1,i}^{2}}{\delta+s_{t-1,i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(s_{t-1,i}-\delta)

\displaystyle\geq-\delta d+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{|g_{k,i}|}{\sqrt{t}}\geq\sqrt{t}\min_{k<t}\|\mathbf{g}_{k}\|_{1}-\delta d

(E.5)

where the first inequality holds by the fact that
s_{k,i}
is nondecreasing, the second inequality holds by the QM–AM inequality, and the last inequality holds by the definition of
\ell_{1}
norm. Note that
\eta_{t}=\max(\eta_{t-1},\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|_{2}/\sqrt{d})
, we thus complete the proof by substituting inequalities (
E.3
), (
E.4
), (
E.5
) into (
E.2
).
∎

For nonconvex optimization, we have a similar convergence guarantee for Adam++:

Theorem E.4

(Convergence of Adam++)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{T}
be the iterates of Adam++, and
\mathbf{g}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{g}_{T}
be the corresponding gradients. Denote
f_{k}=f(\mathbf{x}_{k}),f_{*}=f(\mathbf{x}_{*})
. Under Assumptions
E.1
and
E.2
, with decaying
\beta_{1k}=\beta_{1}\lambda^{k}
with
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
\beta_{1}<\frac{2}{3}
, for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k<t}\|\mathbf{g}_{k}\|_{1}\leq\frac{\frac{f_{0}-f_{*}}{\eta_{0}}+C_{\beta}+cd\delta+\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}}{2(1-\beta_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(2\log\left(\frac{G\sqrt{t}}{g_{0,i}}\right)+1\right)}{(1-\frac{3}{2}\beta_{1})\sqrt{t}}

for Case 1, and

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k<t}\|\mathbf{g}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\delta+G\sqrt{t}}{ct}\left[\frac{f_{0}-f_{*}}{\eta_{0}}+C_{\beta}+\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}d(1+\log t)}{2(1-\beta_{1})(1-\beta_{2})}\right]

for Case 2, where
\widetilde{D}_{t}=\max_{\tau\leq t}\|\mathbf{x}_{\tau}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|/\sqrt{d}
,
C_{\beta}=\frac{\beta_{1}dG^{2}}{2\delta(1-\lambda)}
and
c=\min_{k}(1-\frac{3}{2}\beta_{1k})
.

Proof.

By the
L
-smoothness of
f
, we have the descent lemma for the Adam++ update
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{k}=-\eta_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}
:

\displaystyle f_{k+1}\leq f_{k}-\eta_{k}\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\rangle+\frac{L\eta_{k}^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\|^{2}

(E.6)

According to the update rule of
\mathbf{m}_{k}

\displaystyle-\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=-(1-\beta_{1k})\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle-\beta_{1k}\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-(1-\beta_{1k})\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle+\frac{\beta_{1k}}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_{k}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}}^{2}+\frac{\beta_{1k}}{2}\|\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left(1-\frac{3\beta_{1k}}{2}\right)\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle+\frac{\beta_{1k}}{2}\|\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}}^{2},

where the inequality holds by the Young’s inequality and the matrix norm is defined by
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{A}=\sqrt{\mathbf{x}^{\top}A\mathbf{x}}
for some positive-definite matrix
A
.

Define
c=\min_{k}(1-\frac{3}{2}\beta_{1k})\geq 1-\frac{3}{2}\beta_{1}>0
. By (
E.6
),

\displaystyle c\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle\leq\frac{f_{k}-f_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}+\frac{\beta_{1k}}{2}\|\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}}^{2}+\frac{L\eta_{k}}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}c\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{f_{k}-f_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{\beta_{1k}}{2}\|\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{L\eta_{k}}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\|^{2}.

(E.7)

According to (
E.3
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{f_{k}-f_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}\leq\frac{f_{0}-f_{*}}{\eta_{0}}.

(E.8)

Since
\mathbf{m}_{k-1}
is a convex combination of past gradients,
\|\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\|_{\infty}\leq G
. Moreover, the preconditioner is bounded below by
\delta
, so
\|\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}}^{2}\leq\frac{dG^{2}}{\delta}
. Then, because
\beta_{1k}=\beta_{1}\lambda^{k}
decays exponentially, the sum of this error is finite:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{\beta_{1k}}{2}\|\mathbf{m}_{k-1}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}}^{2}\leq\frac{dG^{2}}{2\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{1}\lambda^{k}=\frac{\beta_{1}dG^{2}}{2\delta(1-\lambda)}:=C_{\beta}.

(E.9)

For the term
\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{m_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}
, we apply Jensen’s inequality. Since
m_{k}
is an EMA of
g_{k}
, we can write
m_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}w_{k,j}g_{j}
where
w_{k,j}=(1-\beta_{1j})\prod_{s=j+1}^{k}\beta_{1s}
and
\sum_{j=0}^{k}w_{k,j}=1
.
By convexity of
x^{2}
,
m_{k,i}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}w_{k,j}g_{j,i}^{2}
. And by exchanging the order of summation, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{m_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}g_{j,i}^{2}\sum_{k=j}^{t-1}\frac{w_{k,j}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{j,i}^{2}}{s_{j,i}^{2}}\sum_{k=j}^{t-1}w_{k,j}\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{j,i}^{2}}{s_{j,i}^{2}},

where the second inequality holds because
s_{k,i}^{2}
is non-decreasing (
s_{k,i}^{2}\geq s_{j,i}^{2}
for
k\geq j
).

For Case 1, by using the fact that
\eta_{k}\leq\eta_{t-1}\leq\widetilde{D}_{t-1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{L\eta_{k}}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}}{2(1-\beta_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}}{2(1-\beta_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(2\log\left(\frac{s_{t,i}}{g_{0,i}}\right)+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}}{2(1-\beta_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(2\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}G}{g_{0,i}}\right)+1\right),

(E.10)

where the second inequality holds by Lemma
F.4
and applying the fact that
g_{k,i}^{2}=s_{k,i}^{2}-s_{k-1,i}^{2}
, and the third inequality follows by the fact that
s_{k,i}=\sqrt{\sum_{j=0}^{k}g_{j,i}^{2}}
and
g_{k,i}
is bounded by
G
. Moreover, since
s_{k,i}
is non-decreasing, we have
s_{k,i}\leq s_{t-1,i}
for all
k\leq t-1
. Since
H_{k,i}=\delta+s_{k,i}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\langle g_{k},H_{k}^{-1}g_{k}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{\delta+s_{k,i}}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}g_{k,i}^{2}}{\delta+s_{t-1,i}}.

Applying
s_{t-1,i}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}g_{k,i}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{s_{t-1,i}^{2}}{\delta+s_{t-1,i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(s_{t-1,i}-\frac{\delta s_{t-1,i}}{\delta+s_{t-1,i}}\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}(s_{t-1,i}-\delta).

By the QM-AM inequality, we know
s_{t-1,i}=\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}g_{k,i}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}|g_{k,i}|
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}(s_{t-1,i}-\delta)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\|g_{k}\|_{1}-d\delta\geq\sqrt{t}\min_{0\leq k<t}\|g_{k}\|_{1}-d\delta.

(E.11)

Substituting (
E.8
), (
E.9
), (
E.10
) and (
E.11
) into (
E.7
), we have,

\displaystyle c\sqrt{t}\min_{0\leq k<t}\|g_{k}\|_{1}\leq\frac{f_{0}-f_{*}}{\eta_{0}}+C_{\beta}+cd\delta+\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}}{2(1-\beta_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(2\log\left(\frac{G\sqrt{t}}{g_{0,i}}\right)+1\right)

By dividing
c\sqrt{t}
on both sides, we complete the proof for Case 1.

For Case 2, define
\widehat{v}_{k,i}=\max_{j\leq k}(v_{j,i})
, we have
s_{k,i}=\sqrt{(k+1)\widehat{v}_{k,i}}
. According to the update rule,
(1-\beta_{2})g_{k,i}^{2}\leq v_{k,i}\leq\widehat{v}_{k,i}
. Therefore,

\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{1}{(k+1)(1-\beta_{2})}\leq\frac{1+\log t}{1-\beta_{2}}.

Since
\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{m_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}
, and
\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{m_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{j,i}^{2}}{s_{j,i}^{2}}
, by using the fact that
\eta_{k}\leq\eta_{t-1}\leq\widetilde{D}_{t-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{L\eta_{k}}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}}{2(1-\beta_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{s_{k,i}^{2}}\leq\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}d(1+\log t)}{2(1-\beta_{1})(1-\beta_{2})}.~

(E.12)

Since
\|\mathbf{g}_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq G
, and
v_{j,i}
is the exponential moving average of
g_{j,i}^{2}
, we have
v_{j,i}\leq G^{2}
. Therefore,
s_{k,i}\leq G\sqrt{k+1}\leq G\sqrt{t}
for all
k<t
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\langle\mathbf{g}_{k},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{k}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{g_{k,i}^{2}}{\delta+s_{k,i}}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}g_{k,i}^{2}}{\delta+G\sqrt{t}}\geq\frac{t}{\delta+G\sqrt{t}}\min_{0\leq k<t}\|\mathbf{g}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.~

(E.13)

Substituting (
E.8
), (
E.9
), (
E.12
) and (
E.13
) into (
E.7
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{ct}{\delta+G\sqrt{t}}\min_{0\leq k<t}\|\mathbf{g}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{f_{0}-f_{*}}{\eta_{0}}+C_{\beta}+\frac{L\widetilde{D}_{t}d(1+\log t)}{2(1-\beta_{1})(1-\beta_{2})}.

By dividing
\frac{ct}{\delta+G\sqrt{t}}
on both sides, we complete the proof for Case 2.

∎

E.3
Convergence analysis for stochastic nonconvex optimization

For stochastic optimization, we have more assumptions:

Assumption E.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the filtration up to time
t
. The stochastic gradients are unbiased:
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
. And
\mathbb{E}_{t}\|\mathbf{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
, and
\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq G
, where
\mathbf{g}_{t}
is the stochastic gradient.

This assumption bounds the variance and the scale of the stochastic gradient.

Theorem E.6

(Convergence of stochastic AdaGrad++)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{T}
be the iterates of AdaGrad++. Denote
f_{k}=f(\mathbf{x}_{k}),f_{*}=f(\mathbf{x}_{*})
. Under Assumptions
E.1
,
E.2
and
E.5
, for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon(T+1)}\left(f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{LD^{2}d}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{(T+1)G^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)\right),

where
D=\max(\max_{t\leq T}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|/\sqrt{d},\epsilon)
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\xi}_{t}=\mathbf{g}_{t}-\nabla f_{t}
. By the
L
-smoothness of
f
, we have the descent lemma:

\displaystyle f_{t+1}\leq f_{t}+\langle\nabla f_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}.

According to the update rule of AdaGrad++, and analyzing it coordinate by coordinate (
i=1\dots d
), we have

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{t}g_{t,i}\nabla_{i}f_{t}}{H_{t,i}}+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{t}^{2}g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{t}g_{t,i}\nabla_{i}f_{t}}{H_{t-1,i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{t}g_{t,i}\nabla_{i}f_{t}\left(\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}\right)+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{t}^{2}g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}},

(E.14)

where we define
H_{-1,i}=\delta
. Because
\mathbf{x}_{t}
only depends on gradients up to
t-1
,
\eta_{t}
is entirely determined by
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
and is therefore conditionally deterministic. Taking the expectation conditioned on
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{\eta_{t}g_{t,i}\nabla_{i}f_{t}}{H_{t-1,i}}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=-\frac{\eta_{t}(\nabla_{i}f_{t})^{2}}{H_{t-1,i}}

Since
D=\max(\max_{\tau\leq t}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|/\sqrt{d},\epsilon)
,
\eta_{t}\leq D
. Due to Assumptions
E.1
and
E.5
, both
|g_{t,i}|
and
|\nabla_{i}f|
are bounded by
G
. Since
\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}
is strictly non-negative due to the fact that
H_{t,i}
is monotonically increasing, we have

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{t}g_{t,i}\nabla_{i}f_{t}\left(\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}\right)\right|\leq DG^{2}\left(\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}\right).

Therefore, by summing (
E.14
) from
t=0
to
T
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f_{T+1}]\leq f_{0}-\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{t}(\nabla_{i}f_{t})^{2}}{H_{t-1,i}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}DG^{2}\left(\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{LD^{2}}{2}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}\right]

(E.15)

We now bound the two right-most terms in the equation above. Since
\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{H_{-1,i}}=\frac{1}{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}DG^{2}\left(\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}\right)\right]\leq\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}.

(E.16)

By definition, the preconditioner in AdaGrad++ is diagonal with entries
H_{t,i}=\delta+s_{t,i}
, where the accumulated gradient is
s_{t,i}=\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{t}g_{k,i}^{2}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{(\delta+s_{t,i})^{2}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{\delta^{2}+s_{t,i}^{2}},

where the inequality is due to
\delta>0
and
s_{t,i}\geq 0
. According to Lemma
F.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{\delta^{2}+s_{t,i}^{2}}\leq\log\left(1+\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{T}g_{t,i}^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)\leq\log\left(1+\frac{(T+1)G^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right),

where the last inequality holds since the stochastic gradients are bounded (
\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq G
). Summing this over all
d
dimensions yields the final bound

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}\leq d\log\left(1+\frac{(T+1)G^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right).

(E.17)

Substituting (
E.16
) and (
E.17
) into (
E.15
), and using
\mathbb{E}[f_{T+1}]\geq f^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{t}(\nabla_{i}f_{t})^{2}}{H_{t-1,i}}\right]\leq f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{LD^{2}d}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{(T+1)G^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right).

Because
\eta_{t}
is monotonically increasing,
\eta_{t}\geq\eta_{0}=\epsilon
. And because
s_{t,i}\leq G\sqrt{t}
, we have
H_{t-1,i}\leq\delta+G\sqrt{T}
.
Thus,
\frac{\eta_{t}}{H_{t-1,i}}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\|\nabla f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{LD^{2}d}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{(T+1)G^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right).

Finally, dividing both sides by
(T+1)
yields the minimum expected gradient norm over the trajectory:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon(T+1)}\left(f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{LD^{2}d}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{(T+1)G^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem E.7

(Convergence of stochastic Adam++)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{T}
be the iterates of Adam++ with
\beta,\lambda\in(0,1)
. Denote
f_{k}=f(\mathbf{x}_{k}),f_{*}=f(\mathbf{x}_{*})
. Under Assumptions
E.1
,
E.2
and
E.5
, for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon(1-\beta_{1})(T+1)}\Big[f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{DdG^{2}\beta_{1}}{\delta(1-\lambda)}+\frac{LD^{2}dG^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\delta^{2}(1-\lambda^{2})}+\mathcal{O}(LD^{2}d\log T)\Big]

where
D=\max(\max_{\tau\leq T}\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|/\sqrt{d},\epsilon)
.

Proof.

By the
L
-smoothness of
f
, we have the descent lemma:

\displaystyle f_{t+1}\leq f_{t}-\eta_{t}\langle\nabla f_{t},\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t}\|^{2}.

According to the update rule of Adam++,

\displaystyle-\eta_{t}\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t}\rangle=-\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{t}\rangle-\eta_{t}\beta_{1t}\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\rangle.

By introducing
\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1}
(which is strictly
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
measurable, and define
\mathbf{H}_{-1}=\delta\mathbf{I}
), we have

\displaystyle-\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})\langle\nabla f_{t},\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{t}\rangle=-\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})\langle\nabla f_{t},\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{t}\rangle+\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})\langle\nabla f_{t},(\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1}-\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1})\mathbf{g}_{t}\rangle.

Taking the expectation conditioned on
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
, and recognizing that
\eta_{t}
and
\beta_{1t}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
measurable, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[-\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})\langle\nabla f_{t},\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{t}\rangle\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=-\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(\nabla_{i}f_{t})^{2}}{H_{t-1,i}}.

Using bounding assumptions (
|\nabla_{i}f|\leq G
,
|g_{t,i}|\leq G
,
\eta_{t}\leq D
) and the fact that
H_{t-1,i}^{-1}-H_{t,i}^{-1}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})\langle\nabla f_{t},(\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1}-\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1})\mathbf{g}_{t}\rangle\right]\leq DG^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{H_{t-1,i}}-\frac{1}{H_{t,i}}\right)\right]\leq DG^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{H_{-1,i}}=\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}

Since
\beta_{1t}=\beta_{1}\lambda^{t}
decays exponentially, and
H_{t,i}^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\eta_{t}\beta_{1t}|\langle\nabla f_{t},\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\rangle|\right]\leq\frac{DdG^{2}}{\delta}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\beta_{1}\lambda^{t}\leq\frac{DdG^{2}\beta_{1}}{\delta(1-\lambda)}

Using Young’s inequality,
m_{t,i}^{2}=((1-\beta_{1t})g_{t,i}+\beta_{1t}m_{t-1,i})^{2}\leq 2g_{t,i}^{2}+2G^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2t}
. Thus:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{L\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t}\|^{2}]=\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\eta_{t}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{m_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}\right]\leq LD^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}\right]+\frac{LD^{2}dG^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\beta_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2t}

The second sum is a geometric series bounded by
\frac{LD^{2}dG^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\delta^{2}(1-\lambda^{2})}
. For Case 1, according to (
E.17
),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}\leq d\log\left(1+\frac{(T+1)G^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right).

For Case 2, define
\widehat{v}_{t,i}=\max_{k\leq t}(v_{k,i})
, we have

\displaystyle H_{t,i}^{2}=\left(\delta+\sqrt{(t+1)\widehat{v}_{t,i}}\right)^{2}=\delta^{2}+2\delta\sqrt{(t+1)\widehat{v}_{t,i}}+(t+1)\widehat{v}_{t,i}\geq(t+1)\widehat{v}_{t,i}.

Moreover, according to the update rule,
(1-\beta_{2})g_{t,i}^{2}\leq v_{t,i}\leq\widehat{v}_{t,i}
. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{g_{t,i}^{2}}{H_{t,i}^{2}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{1}{(t+1)(1-\beta_{2})}\leq\frac{d(1+\log(T+1))}{1-\beta_{2}}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})}{H_{t-1,i}}(\nabla_{i}f_{t})^{2}\right]\leq f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{DdG^{2}\beta_{1}}{\delta(1-\lambda)}+\frac{LD^{2}dG^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\delta^{2}(1-\lambda^{2})}+\mathcal{O}(LD^{2}d\log T).

Because
\eta_{t}
is monotonically increasing,
\eta_{t}\geq\eta_{0}=\epsilon
. And because
s_{t-1,i}\leq G\sqrt{t}
, we have
H_{t-1,i}\leq\delta+G\sqrt{T}
.
Thus,
\frac{\eta_{t}}{H_{t-1,i}}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}
. Moreover, since
(1-\beta_{1t})\geq(1-\beta_{1})
,

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{t}(1-\beta_{1t})}{H_{t-1,i}}\geq\frac{\epsilon(1-\beta_{1})}{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon(1-\beta_{1})}{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{DdG^{2}\beta_{1}}{\delta(1-\lambda)}+\frac{LD^{2}dG^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\delta^{2}(1-\lambda^{2})}+\mathcal{O}(LD^{2}d\log T).

Finally, dividing both sides by
(T+1)
yields the minimum expected gradient norm over the trajectory:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{\delta+G\sqrt{T}}{\epsilon(1-\beta_{1})(T+1)}\Big[f_{0}-f^{*}+\frac{dDG^{2}}{\delta}+\frac{DdG^{2}\beta_{1}}{\delta(1-\lambda)}+\frac{LD^{2}dG^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}}{\delta^{2}(1-\lambda^{2})}+\mathcal{O}(LD^{2}d\log T)\Big]

∎

.

[Lemma 7 in

Consider a filtration

be the set of nonnegative and nondecreasing sequences. Suppose that
C_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
and that

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\widetilde{\delta}+\mathbb{P}(\exists t\leq T:C_{t}\geq c),

where

.

Moreover, different from

Lemma F.4
.

Suppose
0<a_{0}\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{T}
. Then the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\frac{a_{k}-a_{k-1}}{a_{k}}\leq\log\Big(\frac{a_{T}}{a_{0}}\Big).

Proof.

For any
k\in\{1,\dots,T\}
, consider the interval
[a_{k-1},a_{k}]
. Since the function
f(x)=\frac{1}{x}
is monotonically decreasing for
x>0
,
\forall x\in[a_{k-1},a_{k}]
,
\frac{1}{a_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{x}
. Integrating both sides over the interval
[a_{k-1},a_{k}]
, we get:
\int_{a_{k-1}}^{a_{k}}\frac{1}{a_{k}}\,dx\leq\int_{a_{k-1}}^{a_{k}}\frac{1}{x}\,dx
. Therefore,
\frac{a_{k}-a_{k-1}}{a_{k}}\leq\ln(a_{k})-\ln(a_{k-1})
. The proof concludes by summing from
k=1
to T on both sides.

Appendix G
Discussion on Learning Rate Search on Parameter-free Algorithms

In our computer vision experiments, we implement learning rate search for the base learning rate within a small range, instead of setting learning rate to 1.0 as suggested. Actually, most of the parameter-free algorithms have to tune some hyper-parameters. For example, DoG
(
Ivgi et al., 2023
)
may set different
r_{\epsilon}
s and D-Adapt Adam
(
Defazio and Mishchenko, 2023
)
can change different
\gamma_{k}
s.

However, as we shown in Appendix
I.3
, different hyper-parameters affect little on performance (but can still have some affect). For example, the test accuracy for Resnet18 model trained on CIFAR-100 with Adam++ (Case 1) only varies between 66.49 to 70.42 when the learning rate varies between 1.0 to 2.0. And the final learning rates are different for different algorithms and tasks. In comparison, for different scenarios, non-parameter-free algorithms, such as AdamW, requires different learning rates. For example, the learning rate for AdamW may vary from
\sim 10^{-2}
in computer vision tasks to
\sim 10^{-4}
in LLM tasks, since AdamW does not compute the optimal step size automatically.

The reason why we choose [0.1, 3.0] as the learning rate range is that we observed an obvious peak phenomenon during searching, and all the peaks in our experiments are located in [0.9, 2.0]. Therefore, we extend this range to [0.1, 3.0] for Adam++ and baseline parameter-free algorithms for fair comparison.

In this section, we briefly discuss the memory usage of AdaGrad++ and Adam++. Specifically, we note that, compared to vanilla AdaGrad and Adam, AdaGrad++ and Adam++ require the storage of an additional set of parameters,
\mathbf{x}_{0}

and D-adaptation
(

)
, which necessitate storing multiple intermediate quantities of the same size as the number of parameters, our proposed algorithms are more efficient in terms of memory usage.

Impact Statement and limitation

This work introduces two kinds of parameter-free optimization algorithms that demonstrate better efficiency on training deep learning neural networks. These methods have the potential for accelerating innovation and application of AI techniques in downstream fields such as education and healthcare. However, latent threats to the job market and potential promotion for the spread of false information should be further investigated.

Although this work proposes these novel algorithms with a lot of experiments, it still contain some limitations. For example, more ablation study on
\beta
s and weight decay of 2 cases of Adam++ should be further implemented.

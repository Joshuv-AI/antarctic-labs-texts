Dual of algebraic geometry codes from Hirzebruch Surfaces
Alix Barraud

Abstract
. In this paper, we give an explicit form for the dual of the algebraic geometry code C e ​ ( a , b ) C_{e}(a,b) defined on an Hirzebruch surface ℋ e \mathcal{H}_{e} and parametrized by the divisor a ​ S e + b ​ F e aS_{e}+bF_{e} , where a , b ∈ ℕ a,b\in\mathbb{N} and S e S_{e} and F e F_{e} generate the Picard group Pic ⁡ ( ℋ e ) \Pic(\mathcal{H}_{e}) . Notably, we compute a lower bound for the minimum distance of C e ​ ( a , b ) ⟂ C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp} . One of the main ingredient for our study is a new explicit form of the code C e ​ ( a , b ) C_{e}(a,b) which we provide at the beginning of the paper. We also investigate some puncturing of C e ​ ( a , b ) C_{e}(a,b) , recovering other previously studied AG codes from toric surfaces. Finally, we provide a sufficient condition for orthogonal inclusions between the codes C e ​ ( a , b ) C_{e}(a,b) , and construct CSS quantum codes from them.

Introduction

After the first construction of algebraic geometry codes (AG codes for short) on algebraic curves by Goppa
[
1
]
, a generalisation on higher dimensional varieties was proposed by Manin and Vlăduţ
[
2
]
, and S. H. Hansen
[
3
]
. However, compared to codes from curves, very few is known on codes from higher dimensional varieties. On algebraic surfaces, for example, computing the dimension requires a more complex form of the Riemann–Roch theorem
[
4
, V. Theorem 1.6]
, while the difference of dimension between points and divisors of a surface makes it difficult to give a general lower bound for the minimum distance. One can cite generic lower bounds given by Aubry
[
5
,
6
]
, using the maximum number of rational points on a curve, and, more recently, by Couvreur, Lebacque and Perret
[
7
]
, with arguments involving intersection theory applied to specific linear systems of divisors (so-called "
\mathcal{P}
-interpolating"). Because of the lack of general results, the usual approach to find new algebraic geometry codes on surfaces and their parameters is to focus on a specific family of surfaces. Estimating the parameters of such codes boils down to the study of the arithmetic and the properties of these surfaces. To name a few, codes on quadric surfaces
[
8
,
9
]
were considered, as well as on abelian surfaces
[
10
]
, Del Pezzo surfaces
[
11
]
, fibered surfaces
[
12
]
, or even on specific blow-ups of the projective plane
[
13
]

[
14
]
. Toric surfaces were also investigated, originally by J. P. Hansen in
[
15
]
and later by various authors in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
for instance. More recently, Nardi
[
19
]
combined these toric codes with a projective point of view by considering minimal Hirzebruch surfaces and evaluating at every rational points, including those "at infinity". Nardi then computes parameters of these codes with the help of combinatorics arguments and Gröbner bases, for parameterizations that go beyond the injective cases allowing a wider range of codes to be considered, including some defined on a small base field.

Current knowledge on the duals of algebraic geometry codes also gets thin on higher dimensional varieties. On curves, differential codes are introduced
[
20
, Chapter 2]
by evaluating local components of differentials forms at each rational point, namely their residues. It can be shown, using the residue formula, that these constitute the duals of corresponding "functional" AG codes. Then, the duality theorem on curves
[
20
, 1.5.14]
, allows one to express any differential code as a functional code. Thus, the set of all AG codes on a given curve coincides with the set of their duals, and it is relatively easy to explicitly describe the dual of a given AG code on a curve. As a consequence, generic lower bounds for the minimum distance of algebraic geometry codes on curves also apply for their duals, and studying the minimum distance also gives informations on the dual distance in most cases. This is no longer true on higher dimensional algebraic varieties, because of the lack of generic non-trivial lower bounds for the minimum distance and the fact that the dual of an AG code is no longer an AG codes in general
[
21
, Proposition 6.10]
. For algebraic surfaces, Couvreur
[
22
]
constructed residues of differential
2
-forms along a curve (
1
-codimensional residue) and on a point (
2
-codimensional), allowing him to construct differential AG codes on surfaces. He proved that these codes can always be expressed as functional AG codes, and vice-versa. He also showed that duals of AG codes from surfaces are no longer AG codes in general, but can be expressed as a finite sum of AG codes
[
23
]
. However, the proof is not constructive and we have no clue about the minimum number of AG codes that are necessary to express a dual. As for the dual distance, a generic lower bound was given
[
23
, Theorem 6.1]
but it is not always non-trivial and requires a lot of knowledge about the considered surface. As a consequence, a few duals of AG codes on surfaces are known, and their minimum distances remain difficult to estimate.

Finally, we point out that knowing the duality theory of AG codes can turn out to be useful in different contexts. For instance, duals of AG codes on curves can be used for decoding
[
20
, 8.5]
, and this decoding algorithm could be adapted to AG codes defined on algebraic surfaces. Furthermore, advances in the topic on quantum codes gave a new interest for the study of the dual of various codes. Indeed, the CSS construction developed by Calderbank and Shor
[
24
]
, and Steane
[
25
]
gives a quantum code from a pair of linear codes. Parameters of these quantum codes can be estimated by knowing those of the two linear codes that were used, including their dual distances. In this context, algebraic geometry codes on curves were considered, leading to good quantum codes
[
26
]
.

Contributions and organisation of the paper

This article can be seen as a follow-up to Nardi’s work
[
19
]
, and hopes to pave the way toward studies of duals of AG codes from more families of surfaces.

In Section

In Section

), using tensor product and Reed–Solomon codes. After recalling the parameters of

in Proposition

, its explicit form is then used to compute its dual distance in Theorem

with the help of

of codes (see Definition

).

By puncturing

at the coordinates of the point at infinity, we obtain a code

whose parameters and dual are studied in Section

In Section

1.1.2.

Let

1.1.3.
Intersection number

Let

2.1.
Context

2.1.1.
Codes

A
linear error-correcting code
, or more simply a
code
, over
\F_{q}
is an
\F_{q}
-vector space
C\in\F_{q}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We call
n
the
length
of
C
and
\dim_{\F_{q}}(C)
its
dimension
. The elements of
C
are called
words
or
codewords
. The
Hamming weight

\wt(c)
of
c\in C
is the number of non-zero entries of
c
, and the
minimum distance
of
C
is
d(C)\coloneqq\min\{\wt(c)\,|\,c\in C\setminus\{0\}\}
. A code over
\F_{q}
of length
n
, dimension
k
and minimum distance
d
is called a
[n,k,d]_{q}
–code
.

Let
C
be a code over
\F_{q}
of length
n
, and take
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
x^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n})\in\F_{q}^{n}
. With the Euclidean scalar product of
x
and
x^{\prime}
defined as

x\cdot x^{\prime}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}x^{\prime}_{i}\,,

we define the
dual
of
C
to be its orthogonal complement

C^{\perp}=\{x\in\F_{q}\,|\,\forall c\in C,x\cdot c=0\}.

The set
C^{\perp}
is a
[n,n-\dim(C),d(C^{\perp})]_{q}
–code with
C=(C^{\perp})^{\perp}
, and
d(C^{\perp})
is called the
dual minimum distance
or
dual distance
of
C
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two codes over
\F_{q}
, of length
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
respectively. We define the tensor product
C_{1}\otimes C_{2}
to be the set of all
n_{2}\times n_{1}
matrices whose rows are elements of
C_{1}
and columns are elements of
C_{2}
, which is coherent with the tensor product of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
as vector-spaces. It is a
[n_{1}n_{2},k_{1}k_{2},d_{1}d_{2}]_{q}
–code. Indeed, by taking
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
minimally-weighted words of
C_{1}\setminus\{0\}
and
C_{2}\setminus\{0\}
respectively, the pure tensor
c_{1}\otimes c_{2}
has minimum distance
d_{1}d_{2}
and so
d(C_{1}\otimes C_{2})\leq d_{1}d_{2}
. For the lower bound, observe that a non-zero word
c\in C_{1}\otimes C_{2}
has at least
d_{2}
non-zero rows, and each non-zero row has minimum distance
d_{1}
.

Finally, let
I\subsetneq\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
and
C\in\F_{q}^{n}
. The
puncturing at

I
of
C
is the image of the projection

\pi_{I}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{c c c}C&\rightarrow&\F_{q}^{k}\\
(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})&\mapsto&(c_{i_{1}},\dots,c_{i_{k}})\end{array}\right.\,,

where
k=n-\#I
and
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}=\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\setminus I
.

2.1.2.
AG codes on a surface

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a smooth projective and geometrically integral surface over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Define

a non-empty set of
\F_{q}
-rational points of

of cardinality

Remark
2.1
.

Let

Definition

Denote

Benefiting from the conditions

\displaystyle k_{e}(a,b)

L_{*}(aS_{e}+bF_{e})
, then

\ell(aS_{e}+bF_{e})=\sum_{d=0}^{a}(b-ed+1)
. In our proof of Proposition
2.11
for the dimension
k_{e}(a,b)
of
C_{e}(a,b)
, we saw that
k_{e}(a,b)=(\widetilde{s}+1)(q+1)+\left(\sum_{d=\widetilde{s}+1}^{a}b-ed+1\right)
. We can now easily compute the dimension of the kernel

\displaystyle\dim(\ker(\ev_{\mathcal{H}_{e}}))

\displaystyle=\ell(aS_{e}+bF_{e})-k_{e}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d=0}^{\widetilde{s}}b-ed-q

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{s}+1)\left(b-q-\frac{e\widetilde{s}}{2}\right)\,.

∎

2.4.
Dual distance of
C_{e}(a,b)

We now want to estimate the parameters of the
[n,k_{e}(a,b)^{\perp},d_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}]_{q}
-code
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
, within the hypotheses of Proposition
2.11
. Since we already know that
n=(q+1)^{2}
and
k_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}=(q+1)^{2}-k_{e}(a,b)
, this subsection aims to give a lower bound for
d_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
. To this end, we first introduce the notion of check-product of codes.

2.4.1.
Check-product of codes

Definition
2.13

(Check-product)
.

Let
k
be a field, and let
E\subset k^{n_{1}}
and
F\subset k^{n_{1}}
be two
k
-vector spaces. The
check-product
of
E
and
F
, denoted
E\boxplus_{k}F
, is the
k
-vector space

E\boxplus_{k}F\coloneqq E\otimes k^{n_{2}}+k^{n_{1}}\otimes F\,.

The main use of this notation is to express the duals of tensor-products of codes:

Proposition
2.14
.

Let
C_{1}\subset\F_{q}^{n_{1}}
and
C_{2}\subset\F_{q}^{n_{2}}
be two codes on
\F_{q}
. Then

(3)

(C_{1}\otimes C_{2})^{\perp}=C_{1}^{\perp}\boxplus_{\F_{q}}C_{2}^{\perp}\,.

Let
d_{1}^{\perp}
and
d_{2}^{\perp}
be the minimum distance of
C_{1}^{\perp}
and
C_{2}^{\perp}
, respectively. Then the dual distance
d^{\perp}
of
C_{1}\otimes C_{2}
is

(4)

d^{\perp}=\min\{d_{1}^{\perp},d_{2}^{\perp}\}\,.

The dual distance
d^{\perp}
was already known in the literature, with a proof given in
[
30
]
for example. We report it here since some techniques are re-used for the proof of Lemma
2.16
.

Proof.

Recall that
C_{1}^{\perp}\otimes\F_{q}^{n_{2}}
is the set of all matrices of size
n_{2}\times n_{1}
whose rows are elements of
C_{1}^{\perp}
, while
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes C_{2}^{\perp}
is the set of those whose columns are elements of
C_{2}^{\perp}
. The intersection of the two spaces is
C_{1}^{\perp}\otimes C_{2}^{\perp}
by definition. It is now easy to see that
C_{1}^{\perp}\otimes\F_{q}^{n_{2}}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes C_{2}^{\perp}
are included in
(C_{1}\otimes C_{2})^{\perp}
, hence, by computing the dimension on both sides, we show Equality (
3
).

To obtain the dual distance
d^{\perp}
, we first show that
d^{\perp}\geq\min\{d_{1}^{\perp},d_{2}^{\perp}\}
. Let
c\in C_{1}^{\perp}\boxplus_{\F_{q}}C_{2}^{\perp}
,
c\neq 0
, and consider the matrix-products
c\times v\in\F_{q}^{n_{2}}
for all
v\in C_{1}
. Note that by construction of
c
,
\forall x\in C_{1},\,c\times x\in C_{2}^{\perp}
. There are two cases:

•

if
\forall v\in C_{1},\,c\times v=0
then
c\in C_{1}^{\perp}\otimes\F_{q}^{n_{2}}
and
\wt(c)\geq d_{1}^{\perp}
;

•

if
\exists v\in C_{1},\,c\times v\in C_{2}^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
then
\wt(c)\geq\wt(c\times v)\geq d_{2}^{\perp}
.

To prove
d^{\perp}\leq\min\{d_{1}^{\perp},d_{2}^{\perp}\}
, simply consider the codewords
c_{1}^{\perp}\otimes(1,0,\dots,0)
and
(1,0,\dots,0)\otimes c_{2}
where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are codewords of
C_{1}^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
and
C_{2}^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
respectively, of minimal weights.

∎

Example
2.15

(Dual of
C_{0}(a,b)
)
.

Let
(a,b)\in\llbracket 1,q-1\rrbracket^{2}
, and consider the code
C_{0}(a,b)=\PRS_{q}(a+1)\otimes\PRS_{q}(b+1)
. Its dual is

C_{0}(a,b)^{\perp}=\PRS_{q}(q-a)\boxplus_{\F_{q}}\PRS_{q}(q-b)\,,

and its dual distance is
\min\{a,b\}+2
.

In the general case, we can precisely describe the set of all codewords of minimum weight, in a check-product of codes. This is the aim of the following lemma.

Lemma
2.16
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two codes over
\F_{q}
of length
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, and of minimum distances
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
, respectively. Suppose
2\leq d_{1}<d_{2}
, and consider the check-product

C\coloneqq C_{1}\boxplus_{\F_{q}}C_{2}\,.

Then, the words
c\in C\setminus\{0\}
of minimum weight are the
n_{2}\times n_{1}
matrices with one row being a minimally weighted word of
C_{1}\setminus\{0\}
, and the other rows equal to zero.

Proof.

Let
c\in C
be a codeword of weight
d_{1}
, and denote by
c_{1},\dots,c_{n_{2}}
its rows. Suppose there exists
i\neq j
such that
c_{i}\neq 0
and
c_{j}\neq 0
, and denote by
k_{2}^{\perp}
the dimension of
C_{2}^{\perp}
. The code
C_{2}
having minimum distance
d_{2}>2
means that there exists
b_{1}=(b_{1,1},\dots,b_{n_{2},1})\in C_{2}^{\perp}
with
b_{i,1}=0
and
b_{j,1}\neq 0
. We complete
(b_{1})
into a basis
(b_{k}\coloneqq(b_{1,k},\dots,b_{n_{2},k}))_{k\in\llbracket 1,k_{2}^{\perp}\rrbracket}
of
C_{2}^{\perp}
such that

\forall k\in\llbracket 1,k_{2}^{\perp}\rrbracket,\,b_{i,k}=0\text{ or }b_{j,k}=0\,.

Let
k\in\llbracket 1,k_{2}^{\perp}\rrbracket
, and consider the matrix multiplication
{}^{t}b_{k}\times c
. Since
b_{k}\in C_{2}^{\perp}
,
{}^{t}b_{k}\times c\in C_{1}
. However, by our choice of basis
(b_{k})_{k}
,
\wt(c)>\wt(^{t}b_{k}\times c)
and since
\wt(c)
is the minimum distance of
C_{1}
,
{}^{t}b_{k}\times c=0
. But
(b_{k})_{k}
is a basis of
C_{2}^{\perp}
, meaning
c\in\F_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes C_{2}
whose minimum distance is
d_{2}
, and
d_{2}\leq\wt(c)=d_{1}<d_{2}
, leading to a contradiction.
∎

2.4.2.
Dual Distance of
C_{e}(a,b)

We are now able to provide non-trivial bounds for the minimum distance of the dual code
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
.

Theorem
2.17
.

Let
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
1\leq a\leq q-1
and
0\leq b-ea\leq q-1
. Denote
d_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
the minimum distance of
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
.
Then

\min\{a,b-ea\}+2\leq d_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}\leq\min\{a,b\}+2\,.

Proof.

To get the upper bound, we use the observation that puncturing
C_{e}(a,b)
at the points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}\setminus S_{e}
gives the code
\PRS_{q}(b+1)
(see Figure
4
) whose dual distance is
b+2
. Taking a non-zero codeword of minimum weight in
\PRS_{q}(b+1)^{\perp}
and extending its coordinates by
0
on all points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}\setminus S_{e}
gives a codeword of
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
of weight
b+2
. Using the same technique on the coordinates of
F_{e}
gives a codeword of
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
of weight
a+2
. Thus

d_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}\leq\min\{a,b\}+2\,.

For the second inequality, recall that, by Proposition
2.8
, we have

C_{e}(a,b)=\sum_{d_{1}=0}^{a}\left[\langle(0^{d_{1}},\alpha_{1}^{a-d_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{q}^{a-d_{1}})\rangle\otimes\PRS_{q}(b-ea+ed_{1}+1)\right]\,.

Define
\widetilde{C}\coloneqq\PRS_{q}(a+1)\otimes(0,\RS_{q}(b-ea))
. It is a subcode of
C_{e}(a,b)
and

\displaystyle C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\subset\widetilde{C}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\PRS_{q}(q-a)\boxplus_{\F_{q}}\left[\langle(1,0,\dots,0)\rangle+(0,\RS_{q}(q-b+ea))\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\PRS_{q}(q-a)\boxplus_{\F_{q}}(0,\RS_{q}(q-b+ea))\right]+E_{1}\,,

with
E_{1}\coloneqq\F_{q}^{q+1}\otimes\langle(1,0,\dots,0)\rangle
. The space
E_{1}
is the set of all square matrices of size
(q+1)^{2}
whose first row is an element of
\F_{q}^{q+1}
and the
q
other rows are zero. Not all elements of
E_{1}
are in
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
. Indeed, the puncturing of
C_{e}(a,b)
at the points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}\setminus\{F_{e}\}
(
i.e.,
where we only keep the first row) is the code
\PRS_{q}(a+1)
, thus only the subset
\widetilde{E}_{1}\coloneqq\PRS_{q}(q-a)\otimes\langle(1,0,\dots,0)\rangle
of
E_{1}
is in
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
. Since
\widetilde{E}_{1}\subset\PRS_{q}(q-a)\otimes\F_{q}^{q+1}
, we better the inclusion

C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}\subset\left[\PRS_{q}(q-a)\boxplus_{\F_{q}}(0,\RS_{q}(q-b+ea))\right]\,.

This inclusion shows by Equality (
4
) of Proposition
2.14
that

d_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}\geq\min\{a+2,b-ea+1\}\,.

Suppose
b-ea\leq a
, and let
c\in\PRS_{q}(q-a)\boxplus(0,RS_{q}(q-b+ea))
be a codeword of weight
b-ea+1
. By Lemma
2.16
, the support of
c
corresponds to some
\F_{q}
-rational points of the affine line
\Sigma_{\text{aff}}\coloneqq\Sigma_{e}\setminus\{\Sigma_{e}\cap F_{e}\}
, where
\Sigma_{e}
is either
S_{e}
or
\sigma_{e}
(see Figure
3
). However, puncturing
C_{e}(a,b)
at the points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}\setminus\Sigma_{\text{aff}}
gives the code
\RS(b+1)
whose dual distance is
b+2>b-ea+1
, meaning
c\notin C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
and improving our inequality

d_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}\geq\min\{a,b-ea\}+2\,.

∎

Using a very ample divisor (see
[
4
, V.2.18]
), we define a family of duals of codes on
\mathcal{H}_{e}
parametrized by only one integer, with exact dual distance.

Corollary
2.18
.

Consider the very ample divisor
G\coloneqq S_{e}+(e+1)F_{e}\simeq\sigma_{e}+F_{e}
. For all integers
0\leq m\leq q-1
, we define the code on
\mathcal{H}_{e}
parametrized by
mG
to be

C_{e}(mG)\coloneqq C_{e}(m,m(e+1))\,.

Then, the dual distance
d^{\perp}
of
C_{e}(mG)
is exactly

d^{\perp}=m+2\,.

3.
Puncturing
C_{e}(a,b)
.

We describe in this section a puncturing of
C_{e}(a,b)
corresponding to its open
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. Instead of evaluating the polynomials
P\in L_{*}(aS_{e}+bF_{e})
at all the points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}(\F_{q})=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\F_{q})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}(\F_{q})
(Figure
4
), we will study the images of the evaluation maps on the points of
\mathbb{A}^{2}(\F_{q})=\F_{q}\times\F_{q}
, these points being the
\F_{q}
-rational points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}\setminus\{S_{e}\cup F_{e}\}
(see Figure
3
). We will compute its parameters, including the dual distance, before giving an explicit form of the dual. Expliciting the dual of length
q^{2}
will give the set of all the codewords
c\in C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
that are zero on the first row and first column. Computing the dual
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
in Section
4
will only be a matter of finding every codeword that is non-zero on its first row or first column.

Remark
3.1
.

Originally, AG codes on the Hirzebruch surfaces were studied on the
(q-1)^{2}
rational points of
\{X_{1}X_{2}T_{1}T_{2}\neq 0\}
by Hansen
[
15
]
in the injective case
b\leq q-2
. They correspond to the puncturing of
C_{e}(a,b)
at the first two rows and columns. The parameters and properties for these codes were studied in the original paper
[
15
]
, and later in
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
. These are also particular cases of the
generalized toric codes
described in
[
17
]
, along with their duals. One may adapt the proofs of this section to recover the parameters, dual and dual distance of the original toric code of length
(q-1)^{2}
.

3.1.
Puncturing at
\{X_{1}T_{1}=0\}

We consider the puncturing of
C_{e}(a,b)
at the points of
S_{e}\cup F_{e}
, and we define
U_{e,q}\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{H}_{e}\setminus\{S_{e}\cup F_{e}\}\right)(\F_{q})
. In our representation, this set is given by

U_{e,q}=\{(1,\alpha,1,\beta)\,|\,(\alpha,\beta)\in\F_{q}\}\simeq\mathbb{A}^{2}(\F_{q})\,.

3.1.1.
The evaluation code
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)

Definition
3.2

(
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
)
.

Let
e\in\mathbb{N}
and
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
. We denote by
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
the puncturing of
C_{e}(a,b)
at the
2q+1
coordinates of the first row and first column.

Proposition
3.3
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\}
. Define the evaluation morphism at the points of
U_{e,q}
by

\ev_{U_{e,q}}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{r c l}L_{*}(aS_{e}+bF_{e})&\rightarrow&\F_{q}^{q^{2}}\\
f&\mapsto&(f(P))_{P\in U_{e,q}}\end{array}\right.\,.

Then,
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)=\Image(\ev_{U_{e,q}})
, and an explicit form for this code is given by

C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)=\sum_{d=0}^{a}\left[\langle(\alpha_{1}^{d},\dots,\alpha_{q}^{d})\rangle\otimes\RS_{q}(b-ed+1)\right]\,.

Proof.

By our choices of representation, puncturing at the first row and the first column is the same as evaluating the polynomials of
L_{*}(aS_{e}+bF_{e})
at the
\F_{q}
-rational points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}\setminus\{S_{e}\cap F_{e}\}
(see Figure
4
), thus
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)=\Image(\ev_{U_{e,q}})
. Furthermore, by puncturing the codes at the first coordinates on both sides of the tensor product of
2.8
, we get the explicit form of
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
.
∎

Notation
3.4
.

As for
C_{e}(a,b)
, we will also denote
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a\sigma_{e}+(b-ea)F_{e})\coloneqq C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
.

Since we evaluate the polynomials
P\in L_{*}(aS_{e}+bF_{e})
at points of
\{X_{1}T_{1}\neq 0\}
, a generating family of
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
is given by the codewords of the form
\ev_{U_{e,q}}(M)
, where
M=X^{d}T^{c}\in\F_{q}[X,T]
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}b-ed\geq c,\\
a\geq d\end{array}.\right.\,

As such, the codes
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
are subcodes of Reed–Muller codes over
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, whose definition and minimum distance are recalled below.

Definition
3.5

(Reed–Muller codes on the affine plane.)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The
affine Reed–Muller code on the plane
, denoted
\RM(k,2,q)
, is the image of the evaluation morphism

\ev_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{r c l}\F_{q}[X,T]_{k}^{h}&\rightarrow&\F_{q}^{q^{2}}\\
P&\mapsto&(P(x,y))_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}}\end{array}\right.\,.

Let
\F_{q}=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\}
. An explicit form of
\RM(k,2,q)
is

\RM(k,2,q)=\sum_{d=0}^{k}\left[\langle(\alpha_{1}^{d},\dots,\alpha^{d}_{q})\rangle\otimes\RS_{q}(k-d+1)\right]=C_{\mathbb{A},1}(k,k)\,.

Proposition
3.6

(
[
31
, 2.6.2]
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Write
k=r(q-1)+s
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq s<q-1
. The minimum distance
d(\RM(k,2,q))
is given as follow:

•

if
r=0
, then

d(\RM(k,2,q))=q(q-s)\,;

•

if
r=1
, then

d(\RM(k,2,q))=q-s\,;

•

and if
r\geq 2
, then

d(\RM(k,2,q))=1
.

Remark
3.7
.

Suppose
b-ea\leq 0
and
e\geq 1
. The space
L_{*}(aS_{e}+bF_{e})
is isomorphic to the set of all weighted polynomials in
\F_{q}[T,X]
of weighted degree less than
b
with weight distribution
(1,e)
. In that case,
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
is the
weighted affine Reed–Muller code

\WARM(b,2,q)
, well-studied in
[
32
]
. In particular, by
[
32
, Theorem 3]
,
\WARM(b,2,q)^{\perp}=\WARM((q-1)(1+e)-b-1,2,q)
, and consequently

C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)^{\perp}=C_{\mathbb{A},e}(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b})\,,

where
\widetilde{b}=(q-1)(1+e)-b-1
and
\widetilde{b}-e\widetilde{a}\leq 0
.

Removing the points of
S_{e}
and
F_{e}
makes it so the codes
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
are parametrized by divisors that avoid the points of evaluation, allowing inclusions between some of the codes.

Proposition
3.8
.

For all
(a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

(a_{1}\leq a_{2}\text{ and }b_{1}\leq b_{2})\Rightarrow C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a_{1},b_{1})\subset C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a_{2},b_{2})\,.

Furthermore, for all integer
e^{\prime}\geq e
and all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

C_{\mathbb{A},e^{\prime}}(a,b)\subset C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)\,.

Proof.

The statement follows by using Proposition
3.3
together with the fact that when
k\leq k^{\prime}
we have
\RS_{q}(k)\subset\RS_{q}(k^{\prime})
.
∎

3.1.2.
Parameters of
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)

We will now compute the parameters of the
[q^{2},k_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b),d_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)]_{q}
-code
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
.

Proposition
3.9

(Parameter of
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
)
.

We fix
e\geq 1
. Let
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
0\leq a\leq q-1
and
0\leq b-ea
. Let us denote
[q^{2},k_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b),d_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)]_{q}
the parameters of
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
.

We set

s_{\mathbb{A}}\coloneqq\frac{b+1-q}{e}\,\text{ and }\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\min\{\lfloor s_{\mathbb{A}}\rfloor,a\}\text{ if }s_{\mathbb{A}}\geq 0\\
-1\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.\,.

Then, the dimension of
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
is

k_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)=(\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1)q+(a-\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}})\left(b+1-e\left(\frac{a+\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1}{2}\right)\right)\,.

Its minimum distance
d_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
is given as follow:

•

if
q-1>b
, then

d_{\mathbb{A},e}=q(q-b)\,;

•

if
b-ea<q-1\leq b
, then

d_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)=q-\left\lfloor\frac{b-q+1}{e}\right\rfloor\,;

•

and if
q-1\leq b-ea
, then

d_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)=q-a\,.

Proof.

Denote
\F_{q}=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\}
and for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
write
\alpha^{*d}=(\alpha_{1}^{d},\dots,\alpha_{q}^{d})
. Since
a\leq q-1
, the family of vectors
(\alpha^{*d})_{d\in\llbracket 0,a\rrbracket}
is a basis of
\RS_{q}(a+1)
and, thus, linearly independent. For all
d\in\llbracket 1,a\rrbracket
, we have
b-ed+1\geq q
if and only if
d\leq\lfloor(b+1-q)/e\rfloor
. Using Proposition
3.9
,
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
can be expressed as the following direct sum

C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)=(\RS_{q}(\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1)\otimes\F_{q}^{q})\oplus\bigoplus_{d=\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1}^{a}\left[\langle(\alpha^{*d})\rangle\otimes\RS_{q}(b-ed+1)\right]\,.

Whence its dimension
k_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
is easily computed as

\displaystyle k_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1)q+\left(\sum_{d=\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1}^{a}b-ed+1\right)

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1)q+(a-\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}})\left(b+1-e\left(\frac{a+\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}+1}{2}\right)\right)\,.

We will now show that
C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
is included in an affine Reed-Muller code over
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, whose minimum distance will give a lower bound for
d_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
. We distinguish two cases (which correspond to the injective and the non-injective cases, respectively):

(1)

Suppose
b<q-1
, then
\widetilde{s}_{\mathbb{A}}=-1
, and since
e\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)

\displaystyle\subset\bigoplus_{d=0}^{q-1}\left[\langle(\alpha^{*d})\rangle\otimes\RS_{q}(b-d+1)\right]

\displaystyle=\RM(b,2,q)\,.

By this inclusion,
d_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)\geq d(\RM(b,2,q))=q(q-b)
, and since
\widetilde{C}\coloneqq\langle(1,\dots,1)\otimes\RS_{q}(b+1)\subset C_{\mathbb{A},e}(a,b)
with

5.
Equivalence of codes and orthogonal codes on Hirzebruch surfaces

In this section, we address the difficulty to obtain inclusions between codes of the form

, due to

and
F_{e}
having
\F_{q}
-rational points in their respective supports (see Remark
2.1
). To this end, we express any AG code on
\mathcal{H}_{e}
parametrized by a divisor
G
containing no
\F_{q}
-rational point in its support. By choosing suitable divisors
\widetilde{S}_{e}
and
\widetilde{F}_{e}
that are equivalent to
S_{e}
and
F_{e}
, respectively, we will be able to define a new set of codes on
\mathcal{H}_{e}
that allows for inclusions between codes. Recall by Proposition
1.4
that we explicitly gave the Riemann–Roch space associated to
any
divisor
G\in\Div(\mathcal{H}_{e})
and function

such that
G+(f)=aS_{e}+bF_{e}

L(G)=\left(\frac{f}{X_{1}^{a}T_{1}^{b-ea}}\right)L_{*}(aS_{e}+bF_{e})\,.

Everything that we present in this section is easily adaptable for AG codes defined on
any
smooth and integral projective variety, regardless of the dimension, and is already well-known in the community. We simply phrase these notions in the context of Hirzebruch surfaces.

5.1.
Equivalence of AG codes

We introduce here the notion of equivalence of codes, and the link between equivalence of AG codes and equivalence of their respective parametrizing divisors.

Definition
5.1

(Schur Product)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and take two vectors
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\F_{q}^{n}
and
m^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{n})\in\F_{q}^{n}
. The
Schur product
of
m
and
m^{\prime}
, denoted
m*m^{\prime}
, is their coordinate-wise multiplication

m*m^{\prime}=(m_{1}m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m_{n}m^{\prime}_{n})\in\F_{q}^{n}\,.

We denote
m^{*k}\coloneqq(m_{1}^{k},\dots,m_{n}^{k})
for all integer
k
.
Let
C
be a code over
\F_{q}
of length
n
and take
m\in\F_{q}^{n}
. We define

m*C\coloneqq\{m*c\,|\,c\in C\}\,.

Finally, let
C
be an AG code of length
n
on an algebraic variety
\mathcal{V}
constructed via the evaluation map
\ev
on the points
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}
.
Then, for all function
f\in\F_{q}(\mathcal{V})
regular in a neighborhood of
P_{i}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, we define the code

f*C=\ev(f)*C\,

where
\ev(f)=(f(P_{1}),\dots,f(P_{n}))
.

Definition
5.2

(Equivalence of codes)
.

We say that two codes
C
and
C^{\prime}
on
\F_{q}
of length
n
are
equivalent
if there exists
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\left(\F_{q}^{*}\right)^{n}
such that
x*C=C^{\prime}
,
i.e.,

\forall(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\F_{q}^{n},\,(x_{1}c_{1},\dots,x_{n}c_{n})\in C^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in C\,.

We will write
C\simeq C^{\prime}
.

Two equivalent codes trivially share the same parameters, see
[
20
, 2.2.13]
. The duals of two equivalent codes are also equivalent, entailing for instance that the dual distance is also the same.

Proposition
5.3
.

Let
C
be a code over
\F_{q}
of length
n
, and let
m\in\left(\F_{q}^{*}\right)^{n}
. Then

m^{*(q-2)}*C^{\perp}=\left(m*C\right)^{\perp}\,

Proof.

Since
m^{*(q-1)}=(1,\dots,1)
, for all
x\in\F_{q}^{n}
and
c\in C
we have
(m^{*(q-2)}*x)\cdot(m*c)=x\cdot c
.
This implies that
m^{*(q-2)}*C^{\perp}\subset(m*C)^{\perp}
, and the equality comes from equality of dimensions.
∎

Proposition
5.4

(AG codes with equivalent divisors)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a smooth projective and geometrically integral surface over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
G\simeq G^{\prime}
be two linearly equivalent divisors on
\mathcal{S}
. Let
\Delta=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}
be a set of
n
distinct
\F_{q}
-rational points of
\mathcal{S}
that avoid the supports of
G
and
G^{\prime}
, and define the two AG codes
C_{G}=\ev_{\Delta}(L(G))
and
C_{G^{\prime}}=\ev_{\Delta}(L(G^{\prime}))
of length
n
. Then
C_{G}
and
C_{G^{\prime}}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Write
G=(f)+G^{\prime}
with
f\in\F_{q}(\mathcal{S})
. For all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, since
P_{i}
is not in the supports of
G
and
G^{\prime}
, the evaluation
f(P_{i})
is well-defined and non-zero, that is,
f(P_{i})\in\F_{q}^{*}
. Thus,
C_{G^{\prime}}=f*C_{G}
with
\ev_{\Delta}(f)\in\left(\F_{q}^{*}\right)^{n}
.
∎

5.2.
Equivalent codes on Hirzebruch surfaces

We now translate Proposition
5.4
in the context of Hirzebruch surfaces.

Proposition
5.5
.

Let
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and let
G\simeq aS_{e}+bF_{e}
be a divisor on
\mathcal{H}_{e}
that avoids every points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}(\F_{q})
. Denote
(f_{G})\coloneqq(aS_{e}+bF_{e})-G
with
f_{G}\in\F_{q}(\mathcal{H}_{e})
, and consider the AG code
C_{e}(G)
over
\F_{q}
and of length
(q+1)^{2}
defined by
C_{e}(G)\coloneqq\ev_{\mathcal{H}_{e}}(L(G))
. Then
C_{e}(a,b)\simeq C_{e}(G)
and

C_{e}(G)=\left(\frac{f_{G}}{X_{1}^{a}T_{1}^{b-ea}}\right)*C_{e}(a,b)\,.

Proof.

By construction of
f_{G}
and the hypothesis on
G
, the evaluation of
\left(\frac{f_{G}}{X_{1}^{a}T_{1}^{b-ea}}\right)
at every
\F_{q}
-rational point of
\mathcal{H}_{e}
is well-defined and non-zero, whence

\ev_{\mathcal{H}_{e}}\left(\frac{f_{G}}{X_{1}^{a}T_{1}^{b-ea}}\right)\in\left(\F_{q}^{*}\right)^{(q+1)^{2}}\,.

Proposition
1.4
allows us to conclude.
∎

We will now define a new generating couple of divisors for
\Pic(\mathcal{H}_{e})
that contain no
\F_{q}
-rational point in their respective supports, thanks to the following lemma.

Lemma
5.6

(Moving lemma
[
28
, III.1.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a smooth projective and geometrically integral surface over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For all divisor
D\in\mathcal{S}
and all finite families of points
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists
D^{\prime}\in\Div(\mathcal{S})
such that
D^{\prime}\simeq D
and
P_{i}\notin\Supp(D^{\prime})
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
.

For the rest of the paper, we fix two divisors
\widetilde{F}_{e}
and
\widetilde{S}_{e}
that contain no
\F_{q}
-rational points in their supports, and such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{e}+(f_{F})

\displaystyle=F_{e}\,,

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{e}+(f_{S})

\displaystyle=S_{e}\,,

where
f_{F}
and
f_{S}
are both functions on
\mathcal{H}_{e}
. Such divisors exist by Lemma
5.6
, and we have

\Pic(\mathcal{H}_{e})=\widetilde{F}_{e}\mathbb{Z}+\widetilde{S}_{e}\mathbb{Z}\,.

Definition
5.7

(
\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)
)
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and consider the evaluation morphism

\ev_{\mathcal{H}_{e}}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{r c l}L(a\widetilde{S}_{e}+b\widetilde{F}_{e})&\rightarrow&\F_{q}^{n}\\
f&\mapsto&(f(P))_{P\in\mathcal{H}_{e}(\F_{q})}\end{array}\right.\,.

We define the AG code
\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)\coloneqq\ev_{\mathcal{H}_{e}}(L(a\widetilde{S}_{e}+b\widetilde{F}_{e}))
.

Proposition
5.8
.

Set
g_{S}\coloneqq\frac{f_{S}T_{1}^{e}}{X_{1}}
and
g_{F}\coloneqq\frac{f_{F}}{T_{1}}
.
For all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, the codes
\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)
and
C_{e}(a,b)
are equivalent, and it holds

\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)=g_{S}^{a}g_{F}^{b}*C_{e}(a,b)\,.

Furthermore, for all integers
a^{\prime}\geq a
and
b^{\prime}\geq b
, we have

\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)\subset\widetilde{C}_{e}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\,.

Proof.

Since
a(f_{S})+b(f_{F})=a(S_{e}-\widetilde{S}_{e})+b(F_{e}-\widetilde{F}_{e})
, the equivalence between
\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)
and
C_{e}(a,b)
is a consequence of Proposition
5.5
. Moreover, if
a\widetilde{S}_{e}+b\widetilde{F}_{e}\leq a^{\prime}\widetilde{S}_{e}+b^{\prime}\widetilde{F}_{e}
then we have
L(a\widetilde{S}_{e}+b\widetilde{F}_{e})\subset L(a^{\prime}\widetilde{S}_{e}+b^{\prime}\widetilde{F}_{e})
, thus
\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)\subset\widetilde{C}_{e}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.
∎

Corollary
5.9

(Orthogonal codes on Hirzebruch surfaces)
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
a\leq q-2
and
0\leq b-ea
, and consider
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\leq q-a^{\prime}-1
and
b^{\prime}-ea^{\prime}\geq 0
.
There exists two codes
C\simeq C_{e}(a,b)
and
C^{\prime}\simeq C_{e}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
such that

C\subset C^{\prime\perp}\,.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.2
, we know that
\PRS_{q}(q-a^{\prime})\otimes\F_{q}^{q+1}\subset C_{e}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})^{\perp}
. However,
\PRS_{q}(q-a^{\prime})\otimes\F_{q}^{q+1}=C_{e}(q-a^{\prime}-1,B)
, where
B\in\mathbb{N}
is large enough so that
B\geq b
and
B-e(q-a^{\prime}-1)\geq q
. From Proposition
5.8
, it holds

\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)\subset g_{S}^{q-a^{\prime}-1}g_{F}^{B}*C_{e}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})^{\perp}\,.

We conclude by defining
C\coloneqq\widetilde{C}_{e}(a,b)
and
C^{\prime}\coloneqq\left(g_{S}^{q-a^{\prime}-1}g_{F}^{B}\right)^{q-2}*C_{e}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.
∎

6.
Construction of CSS quantum codes from Hirzebruch surfaces

Quantum codes are tools to protect qudits from errors in the world of quantum computing. A
\llbracket n,k,d\rrbracket_{q}
quantum code encodes
k
logical qudits in
n
physical qudits and can correct up to
\frac{d-1}{2}
quantum errors.

In this section, we construct quantum codes using the CSS method, in the context of Hirzebruch surfaces. For the rest of this paper, we fix
{e\geq 2}
.

6.1.
The CSS construction

The CSS construction, developed independently by Calderbank and Shor
[
24
]
, and Steane
[
25
]
, produces a quantum code from a pair of linear codes. We recall the construction below.

For any subset
E\subset\F_{q}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
d(E)=\min\{\wt(x)\,|\,x\in E\}
.

Theorem
6.1

(CSS construction
[
24
]
)
.

Let
C_{1}\subset C_{2}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be two linear codes. Then, there exists a
\llbracket n,k,d\rrbracket_{q}
quantum code called CSS, and denoted by
(C_{1},C_{2})_{\CSS}
, where
k=\dim(C_{2})-\dim(C_{1})
and
d=\min\{d(C_{2}\setminus C_{1}),d(C_{1}^{\perp}\setminus C_{2}^{\perp})\}
.

For any pair of codes
C_{1}\subset C_{2}
,
d(C_{2}\setminus C_{1})\geq d(C_{2})
, and the
\llbracket n,k,d\rrbracket_{q}
code
(C_{1},C_{2})_{\CSS}
is said to be
degenerate
if
d>\min\{d(C_{2}),d(C_{1}^{\perp})\}
, and
non-degenerate
otherwise. Note that
d(C_{1})>d(C_{2})
implies
d(C_{2}\setminus C_{1})=d(C_{2})
.

6.2.
CSS codes from
C_{e}(a,b)

We will now apply Theorem
6.1
using linear codes equivalent to those of the form
C_{e}(a,b)
. We start by the injective case.

Theorem
6.2

(CSS codes from Hirzebruch surfaces, injective case)
.

Let
(a_{1},b_{1})
and
(a_{2},b_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
be such that
0\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq q-1
,
0\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq q-1
and
(b_{1}-ea_{1},b_{2}-ea_{2})\in\llbracket 0,q-1\rrbracket^{2}
. Then there exists a
\llbracket n,k,d\rrbracket_{q}
CSS quantum code, where

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{2}\,;

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=(a_{2}+1)\left(b_{2}+1-\frac{ea_{2}}{2}\right)-(a_{1}+1)\left(b_{1}+1-\frac{ea_{1}}{2}\right)\,;

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\geq\min\{a_{1},b_{1}-ea_{1}\}+2\,.

Proof.

Using Proposition
5.8
, we construct
C_{1}\simeq C_{e}(a_{1},b_{1})
and
C_{2}\simeq C_{e}(a_{2},b_{2})
such that
C_{1}\subset C_{2}
. Since,
b_{2}<q
, we have by Proposition
2.11
and Theorem
2.17
that

d(C_{1}^{\perp})\leq\min\{a_{1},b_{1}\}+2<2q\leq d(C_{2})\,.

We now construct the code
(C_{1},C_{2})_{\CSS}
using Theorem
6.1
, whose parameters are computed using Proposition
2.11
and Theorem
2.17
.
∎

As expected, using the same notation as Theorem
6.2
, the dual distance is very low compared to the length and cannot go higher than
q+1
. This motivates us to leave the injective case to obtain bigger dimension for a fixed dual distance. Consider the code
C_{e}(mG)=C_{e}(m,(e+1)m)
described in Corollary
2.18
, where
0\leq m\leq q-2
. It is the smallest AG code on
\mathcal{H}_{e}(\F_{q})
whose dual distance is
m+2
. We fix
C_{1}\simeq C_{e}(mG)
, and we are going to construct
C_{2}\simeq C_{e}(a,b)
such that
C_{1}\subset C_{2}
and
d(C_{2})=d(C_{1}^{\perp})=m+2
, while having the biggest possible dimension for
C_{2}
. Using Proposition
2.11
, we see that to obtain
d(C_{e}(a,b))=m+2
, we can fix
a=q-1
, and choose
b\in\llbracket(e+1)m,(e+1)(q-1)\rrbracket
such that

q-m-2=\left\lfloor\frac{b-q}{e}\right\rfloor\,.

To maximise
b
, we fix

b=e(q-m-1)+q-1\leq(e+1)(q-1)\,.

It remains to show for which value of
m
we have
b\geq(e+1)m
, which goes as follows:

\displaystyle b\geq(e+1)m

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow(e+1)q-e-1\geq(2e+1)m

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow m\leq\frac{(e+1)(q-1)}{2e+1}\,.

This bound allows for non-trivial values of
m
when
q
is large enough compared to
e
. We formalize this result in the following theorem.

Theorem
6.3
.

Let
m\in\left\llbracket 0,\left\lfloor\frac{(e+1)(q-1)}{2e+1}\right\rfloor\right\rrbracket
. Then, there exists a
\llbracket(q+1)^{2},k_{2}-k_{1},\geq m+2\rrbracket_{q}
quantum code, where

k_{2}=(q-m-1)(q+1)+(m+1)\left(q-\frac{em}{2}\right)\,

and

k_{1}=(\widetilde{s}_{m}+1)(q+1)+(m-\widetilde{s}_{m})\left((e+1)m+1-e\left(\frac{m+\widetilde{s}_{m}+1}{2}\right)\right)\,,

where
\widetilde{s}_{m}\coloneqq\max\{-1,\lfloor\frac{(e+1)m-q}{e}\rfloor\}
.

Proof.

As with the proof of Theorem
6.2
, consider the CSS code
(C_{1},C_{2})_{\CSS}
with
C_{1}\simeq C_{e}(m,(e+1)m)
and
C_{2}\simeq C_{e}(q-1,e(q-m-1)+q-1)
, such that
C_{1}\subset C_{2}
using Proposition
5.8
. Again, the parameters of
(C_{1},C_{2})_{\CSS}
come from Proposition
2.11
and Theorem
2.17
.
∎

Remark
6.4
.

A strong but necessary condition for the quantum code given in Theorem
6.3
to be degenerate is
d(C_{e}(m,(e+1)m))=d(C_{e}(q-1,e(q-m-1)+q-1)^{\perp})=m+2
.

Recall that we fixed
e\geq 2
. By restricting
C_{e}(m,(e+1)m)
to the injective cases, Theorem
6.3
can be simplified as follow.

Corollary
6.5
.

Let
m\in\left\llbracket 0,\left\lfloor\frac{(q-1)}{e+1}\right\rfloor\right\rrbracket
. Then there exists a
\llbracket(q+1)^{2},k,m+2\rrbracket_{q}
quantum code, where

k=q(q+1)-(e+1)m(m+1)\,.

Proof.

This is simply Theorem
6.3
, where we restrict to the case
\widetilde{s}_{m}=-1
. Furthermore, by Proposition
2.11
, the minimum distance of
C_{e}(m,(e+1)m)
cannot be
m+2
, meaning that the CSS code is non-degenerate by Remark
6.4
.
∎

6.3.
CSS codes using orthogonal codes

Using a pair
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
of orthogonal codes given by Corollary
5.9
and defined on an Hirzebruch surface, we construct the quantum codes
(C_{1},C_{2}^{\perp})_{\CSS}
.

Theorem
6.6
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{1}\leq q-2
and
0\leq b_{1}-ea_{1}\leq q-1
, and let
a_{2},b_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{1}\leq q-a_{2}-1
and
0\leq b_{2}-ea_{2}\leq q-1
. Fix the following values

\displaystyle\widetilde{s}_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\left\{-1,\left\lfloor\frac{q-b_{i}}{e}\right\rfloor\right\},\,i\in\{1,2\}\,.

Then, there exists a
\llbracket(q+1)^{2},(q+1)^{2}-k_{2}-k_{1},d\rrbracket_{q}
quantum code where

k_{i}=(\widetilde{s}_{i}+1)(q+1)+(a_{i}-\widetilde{s}_{i})\left(b_{i}+1-e\left(\frac{a_{i}+\widetilde{s}_{i}+1}{2}\right)\right),\,i\in\{1,2\}\,

and

d\geq\min\{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1}-ea_{1},b_{2}-ea_{2}\}+2\,.

Proof.

The statement follows using Theorem
6.1
with a pair of orthogonal codes from Corollary
5.9
, whose parameters are given in Proposition
2.11
and Theorem
2.17
.
∎

6.4.
Conclusion

As described by Voloch and Zarzar in
[
33
]
, AG codes on surfaces tend to have a small dual distance which makes them good for decoding or recovering local properties of these codes, using curves imbedded in the surface. Unfortunately for us, this makes for a poor choice to construct CSS codes with, since the dual distance is always bounded from above by
q+1
which is negligible compared to the length
(q+1)^{2}
. This phenomenon is expected on most surfaces.

However, it might be possible to raise the dual distance of
C_{e}(a,b)
and its CSS codes with a better knowledge of small weighted codewords of
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
. Indeed, by puncturing
C_{e}(a,b)
at good choices of rational points of
\mathcal{H}_{e}
, we can remove these codewords and raise the relative dual distance of the punctured code,
i.e.,
its dual distance divided by the length. Furthermore, with a good description of all codewords of
C_{e}(a,b)^{\perp}
of minimal weight, we could construct degenerate CSS codes by choosing
(a_{1},b_{1})
and
(a_{2},b_{2})
such that
C_{e}(a_{1},b_{1})
and
C_{e}(a_{2},b_{2})
have the same distribution of small-weighted codewords. Experimentations with
SageMath
give some positive results when
\min\{a_{1},b_{1}-ea_{1}\}=\min\{a_{2},b_{2}-eb_{2}\}
, as shown in the following example.

Example
6.7
.

Let us fix
q=4
and
e=2
, and consider
C\coloneqq C_{2}(3,7)
and
C^{\prime}\coloneqq C_{2}(4,9)
. Then
d(C^{\perp})=d(C^{\prime\perp})=3
and

d(C^{\perp}\setminus C^{\prime\perp})=5\,.

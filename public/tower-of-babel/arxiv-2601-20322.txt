Kernels, lax algebras, décalage, and supercoherence Thanks: M.M. supported by RVO: 67985840. D.T. supported by FSI-S-26-8958.
Martin Markl and Dominik Trnka

Abstract
. We prove that a pointed category has kernels if and only if it is a lax algebra for the arrow 2 2 -monad, and that this holds if and only if it is the décalage of a supercoherent structure. We will then interpret categories with kernels as the sought-after weak version of unary operadic categories.

Primary 18C15, 18N50, 18M60

Introduction

A
kernel
of a morphism
f:a\to b

of a pointed category is a morphism

\kappa:k\to a
such that the composition
f\kappa:k\to b
is a
null morphism, and such that
\kappa
is terminal among all morphisms
with this property.
A category with kernels is a category in which every morphism has a
kernel. Examples are abundant: each Abelian category has kernels as
does the category of groups, &c.

We first prove that pointed categories are
coalgebras for the
2
-comonad
\mathbb{A}
that sends a category
{\tt C}
to
its category of arrows
\mathbb{A}({\tt C})
.
We then identify categories with kernels with lax
algebras for the induced
2
-monad
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
on the
category of
\mathbb{A}
-coalgebras.

John F. Jardine, in his remarkable 1991 article
[
Jardine
]
,
introduced supercoherent structures as a special kind of lax simplicial objects

X_{\bullet}
in the category
{\tt Cat}
of categories. Our second main
result states that a pointed category
{\tt C}
is a category with kernels
if and only if its nerve
{\mathbb{N}}_{\bullet}({\tt C})
is the décalage

{\rm dec}_{\bullet}(X)
of a supercoherent structure
X_{\bullet}
. Categories with
kernels thus provide another example of supercoherence,
alongside Jardine’s classical example of the bar construction of a
monoidal category.

Leaving out the details, we therefore proved that, for a pointed
category
{\tt C}
, the following are equivalent:

1em
– the category
{\tt C}
has kernels,

1em
– the category
{\tt C}
is a lax algebra for the arrow
2
-monad
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
, and

1em
– the nerve of the category

{\tt C}
is a décalage of a supercoherent structure.
The above equivalences combine
Theorems
and
, which together constitute the main result of this work.

Motivations and inspirations.

Operadic categories, introduced in
[
duo
]
, are categories in
which every morphism has fibers.
In unary operadic categories, such a fiber is unique. From a
conceptual perspective, Abelian categories should provide examples,
with fibers given by
kernels. However, such a structure does not satisfy
the main axiom of operadic categories, which requires that the kernel
of the induced morphism between the kernels should
coincide
with
the kernel of the original morphism;
in general Abelian categories, these kernels are only
isomorphic.
This fact, together with standard categorical objection to strict
equalities,
suggested the need for a weak version of operadic categories.
Further discussion of this perspective can
be found in Section
.

Operadic categories and their relation to other structures have been
the subject of several works. Particularly important for us
was
[
GarnerKockWeber
]
, which brought into focus the décalage comonad

\mathbb{D}:{\tt Cat}\to{\tt Cat}
. The authors proved that unary operadic
categories are algebras for the induced monad on the category of

\mathbb{D}
-coalgebras. Another source of our inspiration was the
characterization of unary operadic categories as categories whose
nerve is the upper décalage of a simplicial set; see, for
example,
[
blob
]
.

Our strategy, therefore, was to
define
weak operadic
categories either as lax algebras for the induced décalage monad

\mathbb{D}
, or alternatively as the décalage of a lax version of a
simplicial set. The difficulty with the former approach is that
\mathbb{D}

is not a
2
-(co)monad, so the notion of lax algebras does not
make sense. It must be replaced by the arrow (co)monad
\mathbb{A}
, which is,
ideologically, a
2
-completion
of
\mathbb{D}
. As for the latter
approach, replacing simplicial sets by Jardine’s supercoherent
structure requires interpreting the nerve
{\mathbb{N}}_{\bullet}({\tt C})
as a
simplicial object in
{\tt Cat}
rather than as a simplicial set.

This shift in perspective to the
2
-categorical level dramatically
changed the picture. It turned out that the fiber of a morphism in
a ‘weak’ unary operadic category, defined along the above lines, is
automatically equipped with a morphism to its domain, so it
resembles a kernel rather than a fiber. Remarkably, the universal
property of kernels is also satisfied, leading us to the realization that a weak
unary operadic category is in fact a category with kernels! More
details can be found in Section
.

Our ultimate aim is to formulate a bivariant version of the theory
that accommodates both kernels and cokernels and, together with
additivity, characterizes abelian categories as lax bialgebras for a

2
-bimonad, or equivalently as a double—upper and lower—décalage of
a supercoherent structure. This will be the subject of future
work. The first steps along this path have already been taken
in
[
bivar
]
.

Categorical context.

While in the present article we start with the
comonad

structure of the arrow category functor
\mathbb{A}
and then work with the
induced monad
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
on the category of
\mathbb{A}
-coalgebras,
a somewhat dual approach,
based on a natural
monad
structure on
\mathbb{A}
was studied
in
[
Tholen
]
, where
\mathbb{A}
-algebras were identified
with orthogonal factorization systems.
We acknowledge that Section
1

of this article was inspired by the methods of that paper.

Our work touches on the realm of
property-like structures and (co)lax-idempotent 2-monads, as discussed
for instance in
[
KellyLack
]
. A 2-monad
\mathbb{T}
with unit
\eta
is
lax-idempotent if, for every algebra

a\colon\mathbb{T}(A)\to A
, there exists a 2-cell

\theta\colon{\rm id}_{\mathbb{T}(A)}\Rightarrow\eta_{A}\circ a
such that

({\rm id},\theta)
is the counit-unit pair of an adjunction

a\dashv\eta_{A}
. Equivalently,
given two
\mathbb{T}
-algebras
a\colon\mathbb{T}(A)\to A
,

b\colon\mathbb{T}(B)\to B
and a morphism
f\colon A\to B
, there exists a
unique 2-cell
\overline{f}
extending
f
to a lax morphism of

\mathbb{T}
-algebras; see
[
KellyLack
, Theorem 6.2]
. In particular, any two

\mathbb{T}
-algebra structures
a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\colon\mathbb{T}A\to A
on the same object

A
are related by a unique
\mathbb{T}
-algebra isomorphism, so algebras for
a lax-idempotent
2
-monad
on
{\tt Cat}
are typically categories equipped
with structure satisfying a specified universal property. A paradigmatic
examples are
2
-monads
\mathbb{T}
that assigns to each category
{\tt C}
its
free cocompletion under a given class of colimits. A
\mathbb{T}
-algebra is
then a category admitting all such colimits. As we show, for a pointed
category
{\tt C}
, an
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
-algebra structure corresponds to a
choice of a kernel for each morphism in
{\tt C}
. By the universal
property of kernels, any two choices are isomorphic, and
hence so are the corresponding
algebra structures.

Plan of the paper.

In Section
1
we introduce the arrow
2
-comonad

\mathbb{A}\colon{\tt Cat}\to{\tt Cat}
and analyze its coalgebras. In
Section
2
we then explicitly describe lax
algebras for the
2
-monad
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
induced by
\mathbb{A}
on the
category of its coalgebras. Section
contains the first main
result of the paper, Theorem
, which characterizes
kernels as normalized lax algebras for the arrow monad
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
.
Section
contains auxiliary
material relating the combinatorics of the nerve to the arrow
category; this will be used in Section
to describe the nerve of lax algebras.
Section
contains the second main
result, Theorem
, which
characterizes
kernels via the décalage of a
supercoherent structure. The final Section
interprets categories
with kernels as weak unary operadic categories.

Conventions.

We will assume that all categories are small relative to a
sufficiently large ambient universe. We will also assume the axiom of
choice, so that we need not distinguish between categories with
kernels and categories with
algebraic
kernels,
i.e. categories in which each morphism has a
chosen

kernel.

Expected Signature Kernels for Lévy Rough Paths
Peter K. Friz †,‡, Paul P. Hager §

Abstract
. The expected signature kernel arises in statistical learning tasks as a similarity measure of probability measures on path space. Computing this kernel for known classes of stochastic processes is an important problem that, in particular, can help reduce computational costs. Building on the representation of the expected signature of (inhomogeneous) Lévy processes with absolutely continuous characteristics as the development of an absolutely continuous path in the extended tensor algebra [F.-H.-Tapia, Forum of Mathematics: Sigma (2022), "Unified signature cumulants and generalized Magnus expansions"], we extend the arguments developed for smooth rough paths in [Lemercier-Lyons-Salvi, "Log-PDE Methods for Rough Signature Kernels"] to derive a PDE system for the expected signature of inhomogeneous Lévy processes. As a specific example, we see that the expected signature kernel of Gaussian martingales satisfies a Goursat PDE.

Introduction

A basic task in probability, statistics, and machine learning is to quantify distances between probability measures. A flexible and widely used approach is provided by
integral probability metrics
(IPMs). Given probability measures
P
and
Q
on a measurable space
\mathcal{X}
and a class
F
of measurable test functions, the IPM generated by
F
is

\operatorname{IPM}_{F}(P,Q)\coloneqq\sup_{f\in F}\bigl|\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}[f(X)]-\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim Q}[f(Y)]\bigr|,

where
X\sim P
and
Y\sim Q
are random variables taking values in
\mathcal{X}
.
For a detailed introduction to IPMs and to kernel mean embeddings (the latter discussed below), see
[
MFSS17
]
.

Typical choices of
F
recover familiar distances, in particular the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance, the 1-Wasserstein distance, and the total variation distance. In this paper we are interested in the
maximum mean discrepancy
(MMD): Given a positive definite kernel
k
on
\mathcal{X}
with associated reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
\mathcal{H}_{k}
, the MMD between
P
and
Q
is

(1.1)

\mathrm{MMD}_{k}(P,Q)\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}\\
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}[f(X)]-\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim Q}[f(Y)]\Bigr).

Recall that a reproducing
kernel Hilbert space
(RKHS) formalizes the idea that evaluation of a function can be represented by an inner product. Concretely, let
\mathcal{X}
be a non-empty set and let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space of functions
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
. We say that
\mathcal{H}
is an RKHS if there exists a function (the
reproducing kernel
)
k:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the representer
k(x,\cdot)
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
, and the
reproducing property
holds:

f(x)\;=\;\langle f,\,k(x,\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\qquad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{H},\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

The function
k
encapsulates the geometry of
\mathcal{H}
and allows one to evaluate functions via inner products, a feature that underlies many kernel methods. The foundational Moore–Aronszajn theorem states that any symmetric, positive definite kernel
k
determines a unique RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{k}
for which
k
is the reproducing kernel. In practice,
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is typically infinite-dimensional and may be difficult to describe explicitly; nevertheless, the RKHS framework proves extraordinarily useful across probability, statistics, and machine learning.
A positive definite kernel
k
induces a
feature map

\phi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}
by

\phi(x)\coloneqq k(x,\cdot),

so that inner products in the RKHS satisfy

\langle\phi(x),\phi(x^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\;=\;k(x,x^{\prime}).

Given a probability measure
P
on
\mathcal{X}
, the
kernel mean embedding
of

defined by

\mu_{P}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}\big[k(X,\cdot)\big]\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}\big[\phi(X)\big],

provided the (Bochner) expectation exists. By the reproducing property, this embedding linearizes expectations of RKHS functions:

\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}[f(X)]\;=\;\langle f,\mu_{P}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\qquad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Tensor algebra

We fix a vector space
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}
with basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}\}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{W}
the set of words over the alphabet
\{1,\dots,d\}
.
We define the
length
of a word
w=i_{1}\dots i_{n}\in\mathcal{W}
by
|w|:=n
. The empty word is denoted by
\varnothing
with
|\varnothing|=0
.
To each word
w=i_{1}\dots i_{n}\in\mathcal{W}
we associate a basis element of the
n
-fold tensor product
V^{\otimes n}
by
e_{w}:=e_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{i_{n}}
.
We denote by
T(V)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}V^{\otimes k}
the tensor algebra over
V
.
The extended tensor algebra
\mathcal{T}:=T((V)):=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}V^{\otimes k}
is represented by formal tensor series
\mathbf{x}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\mathbf{x}^{w}e_{w}
, with
\mathbf{x}^{w}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We understand
T(V)
as the subspace of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of series with only finitely many non-zero terms.
The product on
\mathcal{T}
is fully described by concatenation of words, i.e., for
w,v\in\mathcal{W}
we have
e_{w}\otimes e_{v}=e_{wv}
, which extends to

\mathbf{x}\otimes\mathbf{y}=\Big(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\mathbf{x}^{w}e_{w}\Big)\otimes\Big(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\mathbf{y}^{w}e_{w}\Big)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}e_{w}\sum_{w_{1}w_{2}=w}\mathbf{x}^{w_{1}}\mathbf{y}^{w_{2}},

where the inner sum is finite and ranges over all words
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{W}
concatenating to
w
.
For any level
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the projection map

\pi_{n}:\mathcal{T}\to V^{\otimes n}
by
\pi_{n}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{|w|=n}\mathbf{x}^{w}e_{w}\in V^{\otimes n}
.
Similarly, for a level
N\in\mathbb{N}
we define the truncation map by
\pi_{(0,N)}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{|w|\leq N}\mathbf{x}^{w}e_{w}
mapping from
\mathcal{T}
onto the
truncated tensor algebra

\displaystyle T^{N}(V)=\mathrm{span}\{e_{w}\;|\;w\in\mathcal{W},\;|w|\leq N\}~\subset\mathcal{T},

which forms an algebra under the truncated multiplication

e_{w}\otimes_{N}e_{v}:=1_{|wv|\leq N}e_{w}\otimes e_{v}
.
For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{T}
we write
\mathbf{x}^{(n)}:=\pi_{n}(\mathbf{x})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}^{(0,N)}:=\pi_{(0,N)}(\mathbf{x})\in T^{N}(V)
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
For ease of notation we will sometimes also write
\mathbf{x}=:\pi_{(0,\infty)}(\mathbf{x})=:\mathbf{x}^{(0,\infty)}
.

We define the subalgebra of tensor series starting with a zero scalar by

\mathcal{T}_{0}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{T}\;|\;\pi_{0}(\mathbf{x})=0\},

which forms a Lie algebra with the commutator bracket
[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]_{\otimes}:=\mathbf{x}\otimes\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}\otimes\mathbf{x}
.
The tensor exponential

\displaystyle\exp_{\otimes}:\mathcal{T}_{0}\to\mathcal{T}_{1},\quad\mathbf{x}\mapsto{1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes n},

is a bijection onto the group
(\mathcal{T}_{1},\otimes)
of tensor series starting with a unit scalar

\mathcal{T}_{1}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{T}\;|\;\pi_{0}(\mathbf{x})=1\}.

Indeed, the inverse operation is defined by the tensor logarithm

\displaystyle\log_{\otimes}:\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\mathcal{T}_{0},\quad({1}+\mathbf{x})\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes n}.

and for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
the group inverse is
\mathbf{x}^{-1}=\exp_{\otimes}(-\log_{\otimes}(\mathbf{x}))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}({1}-\mathbf{x})^{\otimes k}
.

We equip
V^{\otimes n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d^{n}}
with the Euclidean topology, writing
|\mathbf{x}^{(n)}|
for the norm of
\mathbf{x}^{(n)}\in V^{\otimes n}
.
This norm is compatible with the tensor multiplication, i.e., we have
|\mathbf{x}^{(n)}\otimes\mathbf{x}^{(k)}|\leq|\mathbf{x}^{(n)}||\mathbf{x}^{(k)}|
for all
\mathbf{x}^{(k)}\in V^{\otimes k}
and
\mathbf{x}^{(n)}\in V^{\otimes n}
. Further, we will equip
T^{N}(V)
with the topology induced by the norm
|x|:=\max_{k=0,\dots,N}|x^{(k)}|
for
x\in T^{N}(V)
.

2.4.
Free Lie algebras and groups

The free Lie algebra over
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}\}
is embedded in
T(V)
with commutator bracket
[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}]_{\otimes}:=\mathbf{x}\otimes\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}\otimes\mathbf{x}
and is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}(V)
.
Following
[
Reu03
]
, a graded basis
\{\mathfrak{u}_{w}\}_{w\in\mathcal{I}}
of
\mathfrak{g}(V)
is suitably indexed by a subset of words
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{W}
.
The grading of
\mathcal{I}
is by word length and we define
\mathcal{I}_{N}:=\{w\in\mathcal{I}\;|\;|w|\leq N\}
.
Similar to the extended tensor algebra we define the space of formal Lie series by
\mathrm{Lie}((V))=\{\sum_{w\in\mathcal{I}}\mathfrak{u}_{w}\mathbf{x}^{w}\;|\;\mathbf{x}^{w}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, which forms a sub-Lie algebra of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
The image under the truncation map

\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V):=\pi_{(0,N)}\mathrm{Lie}((V))=\mathrm{span}\{\mathfrak{u}_{w}\;|\;w\in\mathcal{I}_{N}\}.

yields the free step-
N
nilpotent Lie algebra with the truncated commutator bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\otimes_{N}}
.
The exponential image
\mathcal{G}=\exp_{\otimes}(\mathrm{Lie}((V)))
yields a subgroup of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, which, borrowing from Hopf algebra terminology, is called the group of
group-like elements
.
The exponential image
G^{N}(V)=\pi_{(0,N)}\exp_{\otimes}(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V))
forms a group under the truncated tensor multiplication and equipped with the subspace topology of
(T^{N}(V),|\cdot|)
it forms the
free step-
N
nilpotent Lie group
.

2.5.
Free developments

The classical Cartan development of a smooth path
\dot{\gamma}:[0,\infty)\to\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
into the Lie group
G^{N}(V)
is obtained by solving the differential equation

(2.6)

\displaystyle\dot{X}(t)=X(t)\otimes_{N}\dot{\gamma}(t),\qquad X_{0}=\mathbf{1}\in\ G^{N}(V).

While such developments can be understood from a general Lie perspective, here, as explained below, we understand and solve the above equation simply by projecting to tensor levels/components in hierarchical order.
The solution is a smooth path in the step-
N
group
X:[0,\infty)\to G^{N}(V)
.
Conversely, any
smooth rough path

[
BFPP22
]
, i.e., any smooth path
X:[0,\infty)\to G^{N}(V)
is the development of a smooth path
\dot{\gamma}:[0,\infty)\to\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
.
The
minimal extension
of the smooth rough path
X
is obtained by fully developing
\gamma
into
\mathcal{T}_{1}
:

(2.7)

\displaystyle S(t)=1+\int_{0}^{t}S(u)\otimes\dot{\gamma}(u)\mathrm{d}{u}.

For further reference, we will state how equations of the above form are understood precisely.
The integral of a path
\mathfrak{y}:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{T}
is understood componentwise, i.e.,

\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{y}(s)\mathrm{d}{s}:=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}e_{w}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{y}(s)^{w}\mathrm{d}{s}\right)\in\mathcal{T},\quad t\geq 0,

thus well defined whenever
\mathfrak{y}(\cdot)^{w}
is locally integrable for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
.
We understand (
2.7
) as an equality between paths taking values in
\mathcal{T}
.
In the specific case of (
2.7
), we solve by first projecting to
w=\varnothing
, leading to
S(\cdot)^{\varnothing}\equiv 1
, and then inductively for
w\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{\varnothing\}
we get

\displaystyle S(t)^{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w_{1}w_{2}=w}\int_{0}^{t}S(t)^{w_{2}}\dot{\gamma}(t)^{w_{1}}\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{|w|}\sum_{w_{k}\cdots w_{1}=w}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}}\dot{\gamma}(s_{k})^{w_{k}}\mathrm{d}{s_{k}}\cdots\dot{\gamma}(s_{1})^{w_{1}}\mathrm{d}{s_{1}},\quad t\geq 0,\;w\in\mathcal{W},

where the inner summation is over all deconcatenations
{w_{k}\cdots w_{1}=w}
of the word
w
into non-empty
k
words.
It can then be verified that
S
takes values in
\mathcal{G}
and is therefore called the
signature
of
\gamma=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\dot{\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}{s}

[
BFPP22
, Theorem 2.8]

Note that equation (

\dot{\gamma}:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{T}_{0}
that is componentwise integrable.
The solution

is constructed in the exact same way, but will generally take values in the larger group
\mathcal{T}_{1}

With the spirit of the

in mind, we will call the solution the “free development”.

For consistency with the signature of
V

Our motivation being the treatment of differential characteristics (see Section

and Section

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}B_{n}(1!x_{1}(t),\dots,N!x_{N}(t),0,\dots)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\;\sum_{\ell\in L^{N}_{n}}\;\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(x_{i}(t))^{\ell_{i}}}{\ell_{i}!}.

We further notice that

\displaystyle\dot{\bigcup}_{n\geq M}L^{N}_{n}=

\displaystyle\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\;\Big|\;\ell_{1}+2\ell_{2}+\dots+N\ell_{N}\geq M\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\;\Big|\;\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\dots+\ell_{N}\geq\frac{M}{N}\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle~\dot{\bigcup}_{k\geq\left\lceil\frac{M}{N}\right\rceil}\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\;\Big|\;\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\dots+\ell_{N}=k\right\}.

Hence, defining
K=\left\lceil\frac{M}{N}\right\rceil\geq 1
and returning to the estimate, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(\pi_{(0,N)}\gamma)_{t}-\pi_{(0,M-1)}\mathcal{S}(\pi_{(0,N)}\gamma)_{t}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq K}\sum_{\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{N}=k}\;\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(x_{i}(t))^{\ell_{i}}}{\ell_{i}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq K}\frac{1}{k!}(x_{1}(t)+\cdots+x_{N}(t))^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\pi_{(0,N)}\gamma\|_{\mathrm{AC},1;[0,t]}^{K}}{K!}\sum_{k\geq K}\frac{K!}{k!}\|\pi_{(0,N)}\gamma\|_{\mathrm{AC},1;[0,t]}^{k-K}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\|\pi_{(0,N)}\gamma\|_{\mathrm{AC},1;[0,t]}}\frac{\|\pi_{(0,N)}\gamma\|_{\mathrm{AC},1;[0,t]}^{K}}{K!}.

∎

2.6.
Lie group developments of semimartingales

We recall some considerations from
[
HDL86
,
Est92
]
for the case of free Lie groups.
Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions.
A measurable map

\gamma:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V),\qquad(t,\omega)\mapsto\gamma_{t}(\omega),

is called a
\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
-valued semimartingale if
(\langle\mathfrak{u}_{w},\gamma_{t}\rangle)_{t\geq 0}
defines a semimartingale for all
w\in\mathcal{I}
.
In what follows, we will always refer to the càdlàg version of a semimartingale without explicitly mentioning it, i.e., we assume
\lim_{s\downarrow t}(\gamma_{s}(\omega)-\gamma_{t}(\omega))=0
for all
\omega\in\Omega
and
t\geq 0
.
Further, we use the usual notation
\gamma_{t-}:=\lim_{s\uparrow t,s<t}\gamma_{s}
and
\Delta\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{t}-\gamma_{t-}
.
We consider the
stochastic Lie-exponential
of
\gamma
, defined as the solution to the linear Marcus-type stochastic differential equation
[
Mar78
,
Mar81
,
KPP95
,
App09
,
FS17
]

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=X_{t}\otimes_{N}\diamond\,\mathrm{d}{\gamma}_{t},\quad t\geq 0,\quad X_{0}=1\in G^{N}(V).

The embedding into the extended tensor algebra allows us to construct the solution to this equation similarly to the previous section, without any geometric arguments.
Specifically, we define the
signature

\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,\cdot}
as the full geometric development of
\gamma
in
\mathcal{T}
, i.e. the solution to

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{S_{t}}=S_{t}\otimes\diamond\,\mathrm{d}{\gamma}_{t},\qquad t\geq 0,\qquad S_{0}=1\in\mathcal{T},

which in Itô-form reads

\displaystyle S_{t}=1+\int_{0}^{t}S_{u-}\otimes\mathrm{d}{\gamma}_{u}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}\otimes\mathrm{d}{\langle\gamma^{c}\rangle}_{u}+\sum_{0<u\leq t}S_{u-}(e^{\Delta\gamma_{u}}-1-\Delta\gamma_{u}),\qquad t\geq 0.

Once again, the graded structure of
\mathcal{T}
allows us to solve the above equation by iterated integration.
The fact that
\Sigop(\gamma)
takes values in
\mathcal{G}
can be seen either by the definition of the Marcus integration (see
[
KPP95
,
FS17
]
) or by formulating the Magnus expansion of the tensor-logarithm of
\Sigop(\gamma)
, which can be done using only Itô-calculus (see
[
FHT22
]
).
The truncated signature
X:=\pi_{(0,N)}\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,\cdot}
then solves the equation (
2.9
).
Interestingly, there is a corresponding
stochastic (Lie-)logarithm
:

Proposition 2.12

(
[
HDL86
,
Est92
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
G^{N}(V)
-valued semimartingale, i.e., for any smooth function
f:G^{N}(V)\to\mathbb{R}
the process
f(X)
is a semimartingale.
Then there exists a unique
\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
-valued semimartingale such that
X:=\pi_{(0,N)}\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,\cdot}
.

2.7.
Inhomogeneous Lévy processes in free Lie groups

While here we specialize in the free step-
N
nilpotent Lie group (as previously studied in the homogeneous case in
[
FS17
]
and
[
Che18
]
), let us mention that the definition of Lévy processes in general Lie groups is standard and their properties are well understood:
the interested reader may refer to
[
Lia04
]
for an introduction to the topic.
The terminology of inhomogeneous Lévy processes is less standard. For the purposes of this exposition, we introduce a definition below. For the reader’s convenience, we gradually transition from the definition for processes with independent increments to the definition of Lévy processes.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space and let
(G,\odot)
be a Lie group
with identity element
1_{G}
. An adapted process
X:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to G
,
(t,\omega)\mapsto X_{t}(\omega)
, is called a
process with independent increments
(
(G,\odot)
-PII) if

(i)

For all
t\geq s\geq 0
it holds that
X_{s,t}:=(X_{s})^{-1}\odot X_{t}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
.

The process
X
is called
semimartingale
(G,\odot)
-PII
if additionally

(ii)

X
is càdlàg, i.e.,
\lim_{s\downarrow t}X_{s}=X_{t}
and
\lim_{s\uparrow t}X_{t}\in G
for
t\geq 0
,

(iii)

f(X)
is a semimartingale for any smooth
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
.

The process
X
is called an
inhomogeneous Lévy process in
(G,\odot)
if additionally

(iv)

X_{0}=1_{G}
,

(v)

for all
t>0
it holds
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}X_{t-\varepsilon,t}=1_{G}
in probability.

Finally,
X
is called a
Lévy process in
(G,\odot)
if additionally

(vi)

X
has stationary increments.

theory developed by G. A. Hunt
[
Hun56
]
provides a characterization of Lévy processes in Lie groups via their generators, which are expressed in terms of left-invariant vector fields on the group.
Although extensions to the inhomogeneous case are available in
[
Lia14
]
, we instead choose to work with the stochastic logarithm, which allows us to use classical semimartingale characteristics in finite-dimensional vector spaces.
As a direct consequence of Proposition
2.12
we get

Corollary 2.14
.

A process
X
is an inhomogeneous Lévy process in
(G^{N}(V),\otimes_{N})
if and only if it is the development of an inhomogeneous Lévy-process
\gamma
in
(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V),+)
, i.e., if
X=\pi_{(0,N)}\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,\cdot}
.

Remark 2.15
.

Trivially,
(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V),+)
is a Lie group with identity
0\in\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
. The above corollary could be stated in a general Lie setting
[
AK93
]
.

Classical results for processes with independent increments, now in finite-dimensional vector spaces
[
JS03
, Section II.4.c]
, imply that the law of an inhomogeneous Lévy-process
\gamma
in
(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V),+)
is determined by a
characteristic triplet
.
For the state-space
\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
the triplet
(B,\Sigma,\nu)
is given by a continuous function
B:[0,\infty)\to\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
, a continuous function
\Sigma:[0,\infty)\to\mathfrak{S}^{N}_{+}(V)\subset T^{2N}(V)
where

\mathfrak{S}^{N}_{+}(V):=\bigg\{\sum\mathop{}_{v,w\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathfrak{u}_{v}\otimes\mathfrak{u}_{w}\Sigma^{v,w}\;\Big|\;(\Sigma^{w,v})\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{I}_{N}|\times|\mathcal{I}_{N}|}\text{ positive semidefinite}\bigg\},

such that
\Sigma_{0}=0
,
\Sigma_{t}-\Sigma_{s}\in\mathfrak{S}^{N}_{+}(V)
for all
0\leq s\leq t
, and a measure
\nu
on
\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)\times[0,\infty)
satisfying

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}(|x|^{2}\wedge 1)\nu(\mathrm{d}{x},\mathrm{d}{t})<\infty,\qquad t\geq 0.

In this work, we only consider inhomogeneous Lévy processes
\gamma
that have a
differential triplet

(b,a,K)
, i.e., such that

B=\int_{0}^{\cdot}b(t)\mathrm{d}{t},\quad\Sigma=\int_{0}^{\cdot}a(t)\mathrm{d}{t},\quad\nu(\mathrm{d}{x},\mathrm{d}{t})=K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{x})\mathrm{d}{t},

for integrable functions
b:[0,\infty)\to\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
,
a:[0,\infty)\to\mathfrak{S}^{N}(V)
and a family of Lévy measures
(K_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
.

Notation 2.16
.

We write
\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b,a,K)
, if there exists some filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions such that
\gamma:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
is an inhomogeneous Lévy process in
(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V),+)
with differential triplet
(b,a,K)
.

In order to give a more direct definition, we use the martingale characterization from
[
Str75
]
:

\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b,a,K)

if and only if
\gamma_{0}=0
and for all
f\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V);\mathbb{R})
,

(2.11)

\displaystyle M^{f}_{t}:=f(\gamma_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{s}f(\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}{s},\qquad t\geq 0,

defines a martingale, where

(2.12)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{t}f(x):=&~\langle b(t),\nabla f(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle a(t),\nabla^{2}f(x)\rangle\\
&~+\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}\big(f(x+y)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),y\rangle 1_{|y|\leq 1}\big)K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{y}),\end{split}

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
and
t\geq 0
with
\nabla f
and
\nabla^{2}f
denoting the gradient and Hessian of
f
in the coordinates
(\mathfrak{u}_{w})_{w\in\mathcal{I}}
.
Specifically,

\nabla f(x)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{I}^{N}}\mathfrak{u}_{w}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}f(x+h\mathfrak{u}_{w})\in\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V),\qquad\nabla^{2}f(x)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}^{N}}\mathfrak{u}_{v}\otimes\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}\nabla f(x+h\mathfrak{u}_{v})\in\mathfrak{S}^{N}(V),

where
\mathfrak{S}^{N}(V)\subset T^{2N}(V)
is defined as
\mathfrak{S}^{N}_{+}(V)
above, but requiring only symmetry.

Note that the characteristic triplet depends on the choice of
truncation function

[
JS03
, Definition II.2.3]
that separates small and large jumps, which we have implicitly fixed to the standard
x\mapsto x\mathds{1}_{|x|\leq 1}
.
When dilating the process, the truncation function only influences the scaling of the drift component.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b,a,K)
and
\lambda>0
. Then
\delta_{\lambda}\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b^{\lambda},a^{\lambda},K^{\lambda})
where
K^{\lambda}(\cdot)=K(\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}(\cdot))
,
a^{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}a
and

b^{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}\left(b+\int_{0}^{\cdot}\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}(\mathds{1}_{|x|\leq 1}-\mathds{1}_{|\delta_{\lambda}x|\leq 1})K_{t}(dx)\mathrm{d}{t}\right).

Remark 2.18
.

If
\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{1}(b,a,K)
, i.e.,
\gamma
is a
d
-dimensional inhomogeneous Lévy process, then
\delta_{\lambda}\gamma=\lambda\gamma
and we recover the classical scaling with
a^{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}a
.

Proof.

For any
f\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V);\mathbb{R})
we set
f^{\lambda}(x):=f(\delta_{\lambda}x)
.
Then it holds

\nabla f^{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{I}^{N}}\mathfrak{u}_{w}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}f(\delta_{\lambda}x+h\delta_{\lambda}\mathfrak{u}_{w})=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{I}^{N}}\mathfrak{u}_{w}\lambda^{|w|}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}f(\delta_{\lambda}x+h\mathfrak{u}_{w})=\delta_{\lambda}\nabla f(\delta_{\lambda}x),

and one similarly verifies,
\delta_{\lambda}
is an algebra morphism, that

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f^{\lambda}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\mathcal{I}^{N}}\delta_{\lambda}\mathfrak{u}_{v}\otimes\delta_{\lambda}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}\nabla f(\delta_{\lambda}x+h\mathfrak{u}_{v})\right)=~\delta_{\lambda}\nabla^{2}f(\delta_{\lambda}x).

Thus, defining the scaled operator

(2.13)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{t}f(x):=&~\langle\delta_{\lambda}b(t),\nabla f(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\delta_{\lambda}a(t),\nabla^{2}f(x)\rangle\\
&~+\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}\big(f(x+\delta_{\lambda}y)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),\delta_{\lambda}y\rangle 1_{|y|\leq 1}\big)K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{y}),\end{split}

we readily see that

\displaystyle f(\delta_{\lambda}\gamma)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{s}f(\delta_{\lambda}\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}{s}=f^{\lambda}(\gamma)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{s}f^{\lambda}(\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}{s},\qquad t\geq 0.

It follows from the martingale characterization of
\gamma
that the above defines a martingale for all
f\in C^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V);\mathbb{R})
.
To conclude it thus suffices to argue that
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
is of the form (
2.12
) with modified characteristics
(b^{\lambda},a^{\lambda},K^{\lambda})
.
This is immediately visible for the second-order part.
For the remaining parts it simply follows by transforming the integration variable in (
2.13
) with
\delta_{\lambda}
, and moving the displaced indicator terms into the first order part.
∎

3.
Expected signature kernels of inhomogeneous Lévy processes

This section contains our main result, which is an extended PDE system for computing the expected signature kernel of (group-valued) inhomogeneous Lévy processes.
The most general version of this system, stated in Theorem
3.5
, takes the form of a linear hyperbolic PDE (Goursat-type) coupled with two linear ODEs.
In the presence of a nontrivial Lévy measure, the system is inherently infinite-dimensional, yet well-posed.

We further present, in Theorem
3.8
, a truncated (finite-dimensional) system that, on the one hand, approximates the infinite-dimensional system in the presence of jumps—with explicit control of the truncation error—and, on the other hand, allows exact computation of the expected signature kernel for continuous inhomogeneous Lévy processes.
In the deterministic case, we recover the system from
[
LLS25
]
for the signature kernel of smooth rough paths.

Finally, in the special case of
d
-dimensional, continuous inhomogeneous Lévy process, possibly enhanced with a deterministic area term, Corollary
3.11
expresses the system in an accessible form using only matrix multiplication.

We begin by recalling known results on the expected signature of inhomogeneous Lévy processes and provide a sufficient moment condition on the Lévy measure for the expected signature kernel to be well defined.

3.1.
Expected signatures and a moment condition

Recall Notation
2.16
for
\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
-valued inhomogeneous Lévy processes.
The expected signature of such
\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b,a,K)
has already been fully characterized in the literature and is summarized by the following

Proposition 3.1

(
[
FHT22
]
)
.

Let
\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b,a,K)
and assume the moment condition

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}\mathds{1}_{|x|>1}|x|^{p}K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{x})\mathrm{d}{t}<\infty,\qquad t\geq 0,\quad p>1,

holds.
Then the expected signature
\boldsymbol{\mu}(t):=\mathbb{E}[\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,t}]:=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}e_{w}\mathbb{E}[\Sigop(\gamma)^{w}_{0,t}]\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
is well-defined and the unique solution to

(3.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}(t)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mu}(u)\otimes\mathfrak{y}(u)\mathrm{d}{u},\qquad t\geq 0,

where the
characteristic velocity
in
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{y}(t):=b(t)+\frac{1}{2}a(t)+\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}(\exp_{\otimes}(x)-1-x\mathds{1}_{|x|\leq 1})K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{x}).

Remark 3.2
.

The above result is a Lévy-Khintchine-type formula for expected signatures.
The homogeneous case
\mathfrak{y}(t)\equiv\mathfrak{y}
goes back to
[
FS17
]
where one obtains the signature cumulants
\log_{\otimes}\mathbb{E}[\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,T}]=T\mathfrak{y}
.
In the homogeneous and continuous case this is known as Fawcett’s formula
[
Faw02
]
.
Returning to the inhomogeneous case, we also note that the signature cumulants are obtained from a Magnus expansion of
\mathfrak{y}
in
\mathcal{T}_{0}

[
FHT22
, Section 6.2.2]
.

Note that in terminology and notation of Section
2.5

\boldsymbol{\mu}
is the
free development
of the path
\int_{0}^{\cdot}\mathfrak{y}(t)\mathrm{d}{t}
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\mathcal{S}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\mathfrak{y}(t)\mathrm{d}{t})
.
This is precisely the fact that will allow us to derive an extended PDE system for the expected signature kernel of inhomogeneous Lévy processes in the next subsection.
To this end, note that the requirements in Proposition
3.1
are in general not enough to conclude that
\boldsymbol{\mu}
takes values in
\mathcal{T}^{2}
.
The following result yields a sufficient condition for
\boldsymbol{\mu}
to take values in
\mathcal{T}^{1}
, therefore in
\mathcal{T}^{2}
, as desired for the existence of the expected signature kernel in (
1.2
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b,a,K)
and denote by
\mathfrak{y}
its characteristic velocity defined in (
3.2
).
For any
\lambda>0
and
t\geq 0
a sufficient condition for
\int_{0}^{t}\|\delta_{\lambda}\mathfrak{y}(u)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{u}<\infty
, hence
\delta_{\lambda}\mathbb{E}[\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,t}]\in\mathcal{T}^{1}
, is given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}\mathds{1}_{|x|>1}(e^{\|\delta_{\lambda}x\|_{1}}-1)K_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

If the above holds for all
\lambda>0
, then
\gamma
belongs to the Chevyrev–Lyons class (cf.
[
CL16
]
); that is, the distribution of
\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,t}
is characterized by its expectation
\mathbb{E}[\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,t}]\in\mathcal{R}^{\infty}
(using the notation from Section
2.3
).

Remark 3.4
.

We note that the above is an exponential-moment condition on the distribution of the jumps of
\gamma
, which is trivially satisfied when
\gamma
is continuous.

Proof.

Note that for any
\lambda>0
we have
\delta_{\lambda}\Sigop(\gamma)=\Sigop(\delta_{\lambda}\gamma)
and by Lemma
2.17
,
\delta_{\lambda}\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b^{\lambda},a^{\lambda},K^{\lambda})
with
K^{\lambda}=K(\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}(\cdot))
.
Since
|1_{|x|>1}-1_{|\delta_{\lambda}x|>1}|\leq C(1\wedge|x|^{2})
for a suitable
C>0
, we readily see that (
3.3
) is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}\mathds{1}_{|x|>1}(e^{\|x\|_{1}}-1)K^{\lambda}_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Hence we can assume w.l.o.g. that
\lambda=1
.
By Proposition
2.9
.3 it suffices to show that (
3.3
) implies that

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathfrak{y}(u)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{u}<\infty
.
To this end, note that from Proposition
2.9
we have for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
the estimate

\|\exp_{\otimes}(\mathbf{x})-1\|_{1}~=~\|\exp_{\otimes}(\mathbf{x})\|_{1}-1~\leq~e^{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}}-1.

Using Proposition
2.1
we then also get

\|\exp_{\otimes}(\mathbf{x})-1-\mathbf{x}\|_{1}~=~\left\|\int_{0}^{1}(\exp_{\otimes}(t\mathbf{x})-1)\otimes\mathbf{x}\mathrm{d}{t}\right\|_{1}\\
~\leq~\int_{0}^{1}\|\exp_{\otimes}(t\mathbf{x})-1\|_{1}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{t}\\
~\leq~\int_{0}^{1}(e^{t\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}}-1)\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{t}~=~e^{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}}-1-\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}.

This allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{y}(t)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|b(t)\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|a(t)\|_{1}+\left\|\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}(\exp_{\otimes}(x)-1-x\mathds{1}_{|x|\leq 1})K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{x})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|b(t)\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|a(t)\|_{1}+\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}(e^{\|x\|_{1}}-1-\|x\|_{1}\mathds{1}_{|x|\leq 1})K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{x}).

In view of assumption (
3.3
) and the integrability of
\|b\|_{1}+\|a\|_{1}\leq C(|b|+|a|)
for suitable
C>0
it thus suffices to conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)}\mathds{1}_{|x|\leq 1}(e^{\|x\|_{1}}-1-\|x\|_{1})K_{s}(\mathrm{d}{x})\mathrm{d}{s}<\infty,\qquad t\geq 0,

which follows from the fact that
K_{t}(\mathrm{d}{x})\mathrm{d}{t}
is assumed to be a Lévy measure and the fact that

|e^{\|x\|_{1}}-1-\|x\|_{1}|\leq C|x|^{2}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{N}(V)
with
|x|\leq 1
and a sufficiently large constant
C
(depending only on
d
and
N
).
∎

3.2.
An infinite-dimensional equation for the general case

We now present the extended PDE system for the expected signature kernel in the most general case of
G^{N}(V)
-valued inhomogeneous Lévy processes.
Recall the definition of the adjoint left and right multiplications
\dualLeftOp
and
\dualRightOp
from Proposition
2.3
.
For more convenient notation, we define for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{T}^{1}
:

\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y}~:=~\dualLeftOp_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y}-\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle~:=~\dualLeftOp_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y}-\pi_{0}(\dualLeftOp_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y})\;\in\mathcal{T}^{1}_{0},

and

\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y}~:=~\dualRightOp_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y}-\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle~:=~\dualRightOp_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y}-\pi_{0}(\dualRightOp_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y})\;\in\mathcal{T}^{1}_{0}.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{y},\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{T}_{0}
measurable such that

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathfrak{y}(u)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{u}<\infty,\quad\int_{0}^{t}\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(u)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{u}<\infty,\qquad t\geq 0,

and define by
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\mathcal{S}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\mathfrak{y}(t)\mathrm{d}{t})
and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\mathcal{S}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(t)\mathrm{d}{t})
their free developments, i.e.,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}(t)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mu}(u)\otimes\mathfrak{y}(u)\mathrm{d}{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(t)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(u)\otimes\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(u)\mathrm{d}{u}.

Then the triplet
(u,\phi,\widetilde{\phi}):[0,\infty)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{T}^{1}_{0}\times\mathcal{T}^{1}_{0}
defined by

\displaystyle u(s,t)=\langle\boldsymbol{\mu}(s),\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(t)\rangle,\qquad\phi(s,t):=\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(t)}\boldsymbol{\mu}(s),\qquad\widetilde{\phi}(s,t):=\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}(s)}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(t),

is the unique continuous solution to the following system of integral equations

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{split}u(s,t)=&~1+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{u(r,v)\langle\mathfrak{y}(r),\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\rangle+\langle\phi(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{y}(r)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\rangle\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\langle\widetilde{\phi}(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle\Big\}\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r}\\
\phi(s,t)=&~\int_{0}^{s}\left\{u(r,t)\mathfrak{y}(r)+\phi(r,t)\otimes\mathfrak{y}(r)+\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\phi}(r,t)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\right\}\mathrm{d}{r}\\
\widetilde{\phi}(s,t)=&~\int_{0}^{t}\left\{u(s,r)\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)+\widetilde{\phi}(s,r)\otimes\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)+\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{{\phi}(s,r)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)\right\}\mathrm{d}{r}\end{split},\right.

for all
s,t\geq 0
.
In particular, for inhomogeneous Lévy processes

\gamma\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(b,a,K)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\gamma}\sim\mathscr{L}^{N}(\widetilde{b},\widetilde{a},\widetilde{K}),

with characteristic velocities
\mathfrak{y}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}
defined in (

) each satisfying the moment condition (
3.3
), then the expected signature kernel is given by

\big\langle\mathbb{E}[\Sigop(\gamma)_{0,s}],\mathbb{E}[\Sigop(\widetilde{\gamma})_{0,t}]\big\rangle=u(s,t),\qquad s,t\geq 0.

Remark 3.6
.

The moment condition (
3.3
) is sufficient for the expected signature kernel to exist and for the above integral equation to be well-defined.
A potentially weaker, yet less explicit, alternative is to simply require directly that the characteristic velocity satisfies
\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathfrak{y}(s)\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty

for all

(and similarly for
\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}
).
Given the available estimates on

such that the above system is well-posed (see also Remark
2.11
).

Remark 3.7

3.3.
Truncated characteristics and the continuous case

In the presence of nontrivial Lévy measures the integral system from the previous section (
3.4
) is inherently infinite dimensional, since the characteristic velocities include all (multivariate) moments of the Lévy measure in their levels.
In this section we provide an error estimate for the approximation of (
3.4
) by a finite-dimensional system obtained through truncating the characteristic velocities.
Compared to truncating the expected signature directly, this approach generally provides stronger approximation rates as demonstrated for the case of a Gaussian Lévy measure in Example
3.10
.

In the case of a continuous inhomogeneous Lévy process, the characteristic velocity has only finitely many nonzero levels. Consequently, the expected signature kernel can be computed exactly by solving a finite-dimensional system of integral equations.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{y},\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{T}_{0}
as in Theorem
3.5
.
Then for any
{M},{N}\in\mathbb{N}
there is a unique continuous solution

(w,f,\widetilde{f}):[0,\infty)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\times T^{N-1}(V)\times T^{M-1}(V),

to the system

(3.8)

\left\{\begin{split}w(s,t)=&~1+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{w(r,v)\langle\mathfrak{y}^{{P}}(r),\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}^{{P}}(v)\rangle+\langle f(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{y}^{P-1}(r)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}^{N}(v)\rangle\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\langle\widetilde{f}(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}^{P-1}(v)}\mathfrak{y}^{M}(r)\rangle\Big\}\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r},\\
f(s,t)=&\int_{0}^{s}\left\{w(r,t)\mathfrak{y}^{P-1}(r)+f(r,t)\otimes_{N-1}\mathfrak{y}^{P-1}(r)+\pi_{(0,{N-1})}\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{f}(r,t)}{\mathfrak{y}}^{M}(r)\right\}\mathrm{d}{r},\\
\widetilde{f}(s,t)=&\int_{0}^{t}\left\{w(s,r)\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}^{P-1}(r)+\widetilde{f}(s,r)\otimes_{M-1}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}^{P-1}(r)+\pi_{(0,{M-1})}\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{{f}(s,r)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}^{N}(r)\right\}\mathrm{d}{r},\end{split}\right.

where
{P}=\min({M},{N})
, and here for any
{K}\in\mathbb{N}
we set
\mathfrak{y}^{{K}}:=\pi_{(0,{K})}\mathfrak{y}
,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}^{{K}}:=\pi_{(0,{K})}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}

Denote the first component of this unique solution by
w^{{M},{N}}:[0,\infty)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and let

be the solution to the augmented system (
3.4
).
Then the following estimate holds

|u(s,t)-w^{{M},{N}}(s,t)|\leq C_{s}\widetilde{C}_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathfrak{y}(r)-\pi_{(0,{M})}\mathfrak{y}(r)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{r}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)-\pi_{(0,{N})}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{r}\right),

for all
s,t\geq 0
and

where

and
\widetilde{C}_{t}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{v}}
.

In particular, when
\mathfrak{y}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}
are given as characteristic velocities of

inhomogeneous Lévy processes
\gamma
and
\widetilde{\gamma}

Second level characteristics

We now specialize to the case where the characteristic velocities are at most of second tensor level.
In this case, the adjoint multiplication can be expressed using matrix multiplication, resulting in remarkably accessible formulas.
We use the following notation for the matrix multiplication:

Appendix A
Remainder estimates for certain moment generating functions

In this section we provide two technical lemmas that are used to compare the approximation error of the truncated PDE method in Theorem
3.8
with the error introduced by truncating the expected signature directly. The first lemma concerns the Taylor remainder of the moment-generating function of certain compound Poisson distributions and relies on known results from asymptotic analysis. The second lemma establishes a simple factorial-decay bound for the Taylor remainder of the moment-generating function of
|\xi|
, where
\xi
is a multivariate Gaussian random vector. For functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\to(0,\infty)
, we write
f(m)\sim g(m)
if and only if
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{f(m)}{g(m)}=1
.

Lemma A.1
.

For a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
define

r_{m}:=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}B_{n}\big(1!\,a_{1},\,2!\,a_{2},\,\dots,\,n!\,a_{n}\big),\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}.

1.)

If
a_{n}=\frac{\rho^{n}}{n!}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and some
\rho>0
, then

r_{m}\sim\rho^{m}\frac{B_{m}}{m!},

where
B_{n}=B_{n}(1,\dots,1)
are the Bell numbers. In particular, there are constants
c,\varsigma,C_{\rho},\zeta>0
, such that

\frac{c}{e\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\log(m)}}\left(\frac{\varsigma\rho}{\log(m)}\right)^{m}~\leq~r_{m}~\leq~\frac{C_{\rho}}{e\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\log(m)}}\left(\frac{\zeta\rho}{\log(m)}\right)^{m}.

Moreover, for
m\geq m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
all constants
c,\varsigma,C_{\rho}
and
\zeta
can be chosen arbitrarily close to
1
by choosing
m_{0}
large enough.

2.)

If
a_{n}=\rho^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
then

r_{m}\sim\frac{e^{2\sqrt{m}}}{2m^{\frac{3}{4}}\sqrt{\pi e}}\frac{\rho^{m}}{1-\rho}.

Proof.

1.)

From the asymptotics in
[
FS09
, Proposition VIII.3]
(see also
[
dB58
, Eq. 6.2.6 ff.]
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{n!}\sim\frac{1}{e\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{nW(n+1)}}\left(\frac{e^{\frac{1}{W(n+1)}}}{W(n+1)}\right)^{n},

where
W
denotes the Lambert
W
function, defined by
W(n)e^{W(n)}=n
, and satisfying
W(n)\sim\log n
as
n\to\infty
.
It remains to show that
r_{m}\sim\rho^{m}\tfrac{B_{m}}{m!}
.
To this end, note that

\alpha_{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}\,B_{n}(\rho,\ldots,\rho^{n})=\frac{\rho^{n}}{n!}\,B_{n}.

In particular, we directly obtain the lower bound

r_{m}\;\geq\;\frac{\rho^{m}}{m!}\,B_{m}.

On the other hand, using the asymptotic formula above, one verifies that the ratio

\tfrac{\alpha_{n+1}}{\alpha_{n}}
converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
(depending on
\rho
) large enough such that

r_{m}\;\leq\;\frac{\rho^{m}}{m!}\,B_{m}\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}.

2.)

In this case, we use
[
Com74
, Theorem 3.3.B]

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}B_{n}(1!\rho,\cdots,n!\rho^{n})=\beta_{n}\rho^{n},\qquad\beta_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1}.

The coefficients
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
have the known asymptotic behavior
[
FS09
, Proposition VIII.4]

\beta_{n}\sim\frac{e^{2\sqrt{n}}}{2n^{3/4}\sqrt{\pi e}}.

We verify that the ratio
\frac{\alpha_{n+1}}{\alpha_{n}}
converges to
\rho\in(0,1)
, and the conclusion follows similarly to case 1.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\Sigma\in\mathfrak{S}^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a positive semidefinite matrix and denote by
\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)
the largest eigenvalue.
It holds for all
M\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)}\big[e^{|\xi|}|\xi|^{2M}\big]~\leq~e^{\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)}\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)^{M}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}\big[e^{\frac{1}{4}|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{2M}\big].

Furthermore, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2M)!}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}\big[e^{\frac{1}{4}|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{2M}\big]\sim\frac{2^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}\frac{M^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{M!},

where
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function
[
AS72
, Chapter 6]
.

Proof.

Set
\lambda=\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)
. The first estimate follows simply by the fact that

|\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}x|~\leq~\sqrt{\lambda}|x|~\leq~\frac{1}{4}|x|^{2}+\lambda,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For the second part, first note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}\big[e^{\frac{1}{4}|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{2M}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{M}}{(\mathrm{d}\theta)^{M}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}\big[e^{\theta|\xi|^{2}}\big]\Big|_{\theta=\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{M}}{(\mathrm{d}\theta)^{M}}\frac{1}{(1-2\theta)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(1-2\theta)^{-1}I_{d})}\big[1\big]\Big|_{\theta=\frac{1}{4}}.

\displaystyle=4^{M}2^{\frac{d}{2}}\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}\left(\frac{d}{2}+k\right)

\displaystyle=2^{2M+\frac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+M)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}.

Finally, using the duplication formula
[
AS72
, 6.1.18]

we have

\displaystyle 2^{2M+\frac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+M)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})(2M)!}=\frac{2^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+M)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+M)}\frac{1}{M!}

and the rest follows from the well-known asymptotic behavior of the ratios of gamma functions
[
AS72
, 6.1.46]
.
∎

Appendix B
An a priori bound

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(u,\phi,\widetilde{\phi}):[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{T}^{1}\times\mathcal{T}^{1}
be a continuous solution to the integral equation

\left\{\begin{split}u(s,t)=&~u(s,0)+u(0,t)-u(0,0)\\
&~+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{u(r,v)\langle\mathfrak{y}(r),\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\rangle+\langle\phi(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{y}(r)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\rangle+\langle\widetilde{\phi}(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle\Big\}\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r}\\
\phi(s,t)=&~\int_{0}^{s}\left\{u(r,t)\mathfrak{y}(r)+\phi(r,t)\otimes\mathfrak{y}(r)+\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\phi}(r,t)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\right\}\mathrm{d}{r}\\
\widetilde{\phi}(s,t)=&~\int_{0}^{t}\left\{u(s,r)\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)+\widetilde{\phi}(s,r)\otimes\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)+\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{{\phi}(s,r)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)\right\}\mathrm{d}{r}\end{split},\right.

for all
s,t\in[0,T]
where
\mathfrak{y},\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}:[0,T]\to\mathcal{T}_{0}
with

C_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\|{\mathfrak{y}}(r)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{r}<\infty,\qquad\widetilde{C}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(r)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{r}<\infty,\qquad t\in[0,T].

Then the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle|u(s,t)|+\|\phi(s,t)\|_{1}\leq\psi(C_{s},\widetilde{C}_{t})\sup_{(r,v)\in[0,s]\times[0,t]}\big|u(r,0)+u(0,v)-u(0,0)\big|,

and similarly

\displaystyle|u(s,t)|+\|\widetilde{\phi}(s,t)\|_{1}\leq\psi(C_{s},\widetilde{C}_{t})\sup_{(r,v)\in[0,s]\times[0,t]}\big|u(r,0)+u(0,v)-u(0,0)\big|,

for all
s,t\in[0,T]
,
where
\psi(x,y)=e^{x+y}I_{0}(2\sqrt{xy})

and
I_{0}
is the zero-order modified Bessel function of the first kind.

Proof.

First note that suitably integrating the equation for
\widetilde{\phi}
and comparing with the equation for
u
we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\langle\widetilde{\phi}(r,t),\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle u(r,v)\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)+\widetilde{\phi}(r,v)\otimes\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)+\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{{\phi}(r,v)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v),\mathfrak{y}(r)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{u(r,v)\langle\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v),\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle+\langle\widetilde{\phi}(r,v)\otimes\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v),\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle+\langle\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{{\phi}(r,v)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v),\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle\right\}\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{u(r,v)\langle\mathfrak{y}(r),\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\rangle+\langle\widetilde{\phi}(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle+\langle\phi(r,v),\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{\mathfrak{y}(r)}\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\rangle\Big\}\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\bigg.u(s,t)-u(s,0)-u(0,t)+u(0,0),

where in the third equality we repeatedly use that
\langle\dualLeftOp_{a}b,c\rangle=\langle\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{a}b,c\rangle
and
\langle\dualRightOp_{a}b,c\rangle=\langle\dualRightOp^{\circ}_{a}b,c\rangle
for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{T}^{1}
with
\pi_{0}c=0
.
Next, in the equation for
\phi
we split

\int_{0}^{s}\dualLeftOp^{\circ}_{\widetilde{\phi}(r,t)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\mathrm{d}{r}=\int_{0}^{s}\dualLeftOp_{\widetilde{\phi}(r,t)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\mathrm{d}{r}-\int_{0}^{s}\langle\widetilde{\phi}(r,t),\mathfrak{y}(r)\rangle\mathrm{d}{r},

and insert the above derived identity to finally obtain

\displaystyle[\phi+u](s,t)=

\displaystyle~u(s,0)+u(0,t)-u(0,0)+\int_{0}^{s}\left\{[\phi+u](r,t)\otimes\mathfrak{y}(r)+\dualLeftOp_{\widetilde{\phi}(r,t)}\mathfrak{y}(r)\right\}\mathrm{d}{r},

for all
s,t\in[0,T]
.
Now let
s_{0},t_{0}\in[0,T]
be arbitrary but fixed and define

U:=\sup_{(r,v)\in[0,s_{0}]\times[0,t_{0}]}\big|u(r,0)+u(0,v)-u(0,0)\big|.

By Proposition
2.3
we have
\|\dualLeftOp_{a}b\|_{1}\leq\|a\|_{1}\|b\|_{1}
and so

\displaystyle\|\phi(s,t)+u(s,t)\|_{1}\leq

\displaystyle~U+\int_{0}^{s}\left(\|\phi(r,t)+u(r,t)\|_{1}+\|\widetilde{\phi}(r,t)\|_{1}\right)\|\mathfrak{y}(r)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{r}.

An application of Grönwall’s inequality, more precisely Lemma
C.1
, then yields

\displaystyle\|{\phi}(s,t)+u(s,t)\|_{1}=

\displaystyle~e^{C_{s}}U+e^{C_{s}}\int_{0}^{s}\|\widetilde{\phi}(r,t)\|_{1}\|\mathfrak{y}(r)\|_{1}e^{-C_{r}}\mathrm{d}{r},

for all
s\in[0,s_{0}]
and
t\in[0,t_{0}]
.
In the equation for
\widetilde{\phi}
we directly estimate again using Grönwall’s inequality as follows

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\phi}(s,t)\|_{1}\leq

\displaystyle~\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\phi(s,v)+u(s,v)\|_{1}+\|\widetilde{\phi}(s,v)\|_{1}\right)\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{v}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~e^{\widetilde{C}_{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\|{\phi}(s,v)+u(s,v)\|_{1}\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\|_{1}e^{-\widetilde{C}_{v}}\mathrm{d}{v},

Plugging this estimate into the previous one we get

\|{\phi}(s,t)+u(s,t)\|_{1}\leq e^{C_{s}+\widetilde{C}_{t}}\left(U+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\|{\phi}(r,v)+u(r,v)\|_{1}e^{-\widetilde{C}_{v}-C_{r}}\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\|_{1}\|\mathfrak{y}(r)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r}\right).

Hence, by
[
Sno69
]
(or more specifically
[
Sno72
, Corollary 2]
) we have

\|{\phi}(s,t)+u(s,t)\|_{1}\leq Ue^{C_{s}+\widetilde{C}_{t}}\varphi(s,t),\qquad(s,t)\in[0,s_{0}]\times[0,t_{0}],

where
\varphi
uniquely solves

\displaystyle{\varphi}(s,t)=

\displaystyle~1+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}{\varphi}(r,v)\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{y}}(v)\|_{1}\|\mathfrak{y}(r)\|_{1}\mathrm{d}{v}\mathrm{d}{r},

which by the lemma below has the explicit solution
{\varphi}(s,t)=I_{0}(2(C_{s}\widetilde{C}_{t})^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
The corresponding estimate for
\|u+\widetilde{\phi}\|_{1}
follows completely analogously by switching the roles of
\phi
and
\widetilde{\phi}
.
∎

For completeness, we present the derivation of a well-known explicit form of the Riemann function corresponding to a Goursat problem with a multiplicative structure.
Note that we are intentionally avoiding intricacies associated with the complex square root.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
f,g:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be integrable, such that

\displaystyle F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(u)\mathrm{d}{u}~\geq 0,\qquad G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(u)\mathrm{d}{u}~\geq 0,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Then the unique solution of the integral equation

\displaystyle\varphi(s,t)=1+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}f(r)g(v)\varphi(r,v)\mathrm{d}{r}\mathrm{d}{v},\quad s,t\in[0,T],

is given by

(B.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(s,t)=I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{F(s)G(t)}\right),\qquad s,t\geq 0.

Proof.

Uniqueness follows from a classical result on the well-posedness of the Goursat problem in
[
Lee60
]
.

To prove that (
B.1
) is the solution, first assume that
f
and
g
are continuously differentiable and that
F,G>0
on
[0,T]
.
In this case
\varphi
is twice continuously differentiable on
[0,T]^{2}
and differentiation yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}\varphi(s,t)=

\displaystyle~\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(I_{0}^{\prime}\left(2\sqrt{F(s)G(t)}\right)\frac{F(s)g(t)}{\sqrt{F(s)G(t)}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~I_{0}^{\prime\prime}\left(2\sqrt{F(s)G(t)}\right)f(s)g(t)+I_{0}^{\prime}\left(2\sqrt{F(s)G(t)}\right)\frac{f(s)g(t)}{\sqrt{F(s)G(t)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\varphi(s,t)f(s)g(t),

for all
s,t\in[0,T]
, where the last equality follows from the fact that
I_{0}
satisfies the modified Bessel equation

xI^{\prime\prime}_{0}(x)+I^{\prime}_{0}(x)-xI_{0}(x)=0
on
x\in(0,\infty)
see
[
AS72
, Section 9.6]
.
By definition it holds
\varphi(s,0)=\varphi(0,t)=1
for all
s,t\in[0,T]
from which we conclude that
\varphi
solves the integral equation.

Now if only
F,G\geq 0
, one verifies by checking the boundary data that for any
\varepsilon>0
the function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s,t)=I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{(F(s)+\varepsilon)(G(t)+\varepsilon)}\right)
solves the integral equation

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s,t)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s,0)+\varphi_{\varepsilon}(0,t)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(0,0)+\int_{0}^{s}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}f(r)g(v)\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(r,v)\mathrm{d}{r}\mathrm{d}{v}.

The final statement then follows by using dominated convergence to both let
\varepsilon\to 0
and extend from continuously differentiable to integrable functions
f
and
g
.
∎

Appendix C
Grönwall’s inequality for a special case

As it is used frequently above, we present the following special case of Grönwall’s inequality in a form convenient for later reference.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
C\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\beta:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be (locally) integrable, and let
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous such that

\displaystyle\varphi(t)\leq

\displaystyle~C+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\varphi(u)+\phi(u)\right)\beta(u)\mathrm{d}{u},\qquad t\geq 0.

Then it holds

\displaystyle\varphi(t)\leq

\displaystyle~Ce^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\mathrm{d}{r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(u)e^{\int_{u}^{t}\beta(r)\mathrm{d}{r}}\beta(u)\mathrm{d}{u},\qquad t\geq 0.

Proof.

Using integration by parts, one verifies that

\displaystyle\rho(t)=Ce^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\mathrm{d}{r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(u)e^{\int_{u}^{t}\beta(r)\mathrm{d}{r}}\beta(u)\mathrm{d}{u},\qquad t\geq 0,

satisfies the integral inequality with equality. Hence by linearity

\displaystyle\varphi(t)-\rho(t)\leq

\displaystyle~\int_{0}^{t}\left(\varphi(u)-\rho(u)\right)\beta(u)\mathrm{d}{u},\qquad t\geq 0.

Hence,
\varphi-\rho\leq 0
(as implied from a standard version of Grönwall’s inequality, see e.g.
[
App09
, Proposition 6.1.4]
) and the claim follows.
∎

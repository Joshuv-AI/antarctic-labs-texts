Abstract
. We introduce and study a new scale of function spaces that characterize the homogeneous Besov spaces B ˙ p , q β \dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q} , hence completing earlier work by Ullrich. These new spaces include the ones introduced by Barton and Mayboroda, and systematically studied by Amenta under the name of weighted Z \mathrm{Z} -spaces, for the purpose of boundary value problems with B ˙ p , p β \dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,p} data. They are the counterparts to the weighted tent spaces with Whitney averages, developed by Huang, and arise as their real interpolants. We describe their functional analytic properties: completeness, duality, embeddings, as well as their real and complex interpolants.

1.
Introduction

This paper introduces a new scale of function spaces, naturally arising in the characterization of homogeneous Besov spaces, and establishes a full function space analysis for them. Characterizations of (homogeneous) Besov spaces
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
have attracted significant interest in recent decades due to their instrumentality in areas such as harmonic analysis, partial differential equations, and more. Traditionally, these spaces are defined via discrete Littlewood-Paley blocks
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
that are, in fact, convolution operators with a kernel having compact support away from zero in Fourier-space, see the monograph of Triebel
[
26
, Sec. 5]
. In contrast, for applications to partial differential equations, it is of interest to have characterizations with respect to a continuous spectrum and using kernels without compactness of the Fourier-support. For example, kernels whose Fourier-transform vanishes sufficiently fast in zero and infinity, such as

\displaystyle k_{t}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(|t\xi|^{N}e^{-|t\xi|^{2}}\big)(x),\quad\text{for $t>0$ and some $N\in\mathbb{N}$,}

are of particular interest, because they lead to characterizations via the well-known Gauss–Weierstrass semigroup.

First continuous characterizations for general kernels of this kind were given by Triebel in
[
27
, Sec. 2.4.2 + 2.5.1]
in the range
1<p<\infty
,
1<q\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, where the case
p=\infty
is included for Besov spaces. Later, they were complemented by Ullrich in
[
28
]
for the full range of parameters
0<p<\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
. Again he included the case
p=\infty
for Besov spaces. Eventually, Hui and Taibleson
[
10
]
treated also the Triebel–Lizorkin spaces in the case
p=\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
. We take a closer look at Ullrich’s work.
Interestingly, in his paper he provides several characterizations for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, which always have a corresponding counterpart for Besov spaces, except for one. More precisely, this additional characterization for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces uses (weighted) tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. They were first introduced by Coifman, Meyer and Stein
[
12
]
in the unweighted case
\beta=0
, and later studied by many other authors in the weighted case
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, see for instance
[
2
,
16
]
.
Roughly speaking, the tent space characterization reads

\displaystyle f\in\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{E}(f)(t,x)\coloneqq(\Phi_{t}\ast f)(x)\in\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta},

where
\Phi
is a suitable kernel and
\Phi_{t}=t^{-d}\Phi(\cdot/t)
, see Section
2
for more details. In this construction,
(\Phi_{t}\ast f)(x)
is an extension of
f
to the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, and
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
the extension space of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
. However, a corresponding
“tent space characterization”
for the closely related Besov spaces
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
, at least when
p\neq q
, has remained a notable gap in the theory. Therefore, one central question of our paper is the following:

•

Is there a scale of function spaces, similar to tent spaces, that characterizes the homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
?

In the following, we use results from the literature to derive a suitable candidate for that scale of spaces. To do so, we want to leverage known real-interpolation properties for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces as well as for weighted tent spaces. To this end, recall that Barton and Mayboroda introduced in
[
7
]
a scale of function spaces for analyzing boundary value problems with fractional
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,p}
-data, see also
[
3
,
6
]
. Additionally, they recently found applications for solving nonlinear parabolic problems, see
[
5
]
. Under the name of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces, they were further investigated by Amenta in
[
2
]
. In particular, he shows that the real interpolation spaces of weighted tent spaces can be identified with
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces. The following incomplete diagram uses these facts along with the tent space characterization of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
to identify a candidate for the characterization in the special case
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,p}
.

1.1.
Relation to and consequences for tent spaces

As previously discussed and demonstrated in Figure
1
, real interpolation of two weighted tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
yields
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q}_{\beta}
-spaces. Here, we replaced the parameter
r
by
q
. There, the interpolation was only done in the
p
-parameter for some fixed
q
. However, to our best knowledge, nothing is known for real interpolation of weighted tent spaces in the parameter
q
when the parameter
p
is fixed. More precisely, there is no known characterization of
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}
. In contrast, on the level of Besov spaces it is indeed known that the corresponding real interpolation of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
(\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p,q_{0}}^{\beta_{0}},\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p,q_{1}}^{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}
can be identified with the Besov space
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
, see
[
26

for more details. Together with the fact that the
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}

\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
with some free parameter
r
, as will be established in Theorem

1.2.
Roadmap and methods

Our paper subdivides into three parts: First, we characterize
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
and
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
by means of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, respectively. This is the content of Section
2
. Second, we investigate functional-analytic properties of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
in Section
3
. These properties include completeness, density and embeddings, as well as a full duality theory including the quasi-Banach range. Third, we develop a comprehensive interpolation theory for
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces. More precisely, we investigate their real interpolation behavior in Section
4
, followed by the complex case in Section
5
.

Characterization of Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces

This part relies heavily on the preliminary work by Ullrich
[
28
]
. He has derived powerful characterizations for Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces using maximal functions. With them at hand, it is fairly straightforward to show the inclusion of
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into our four parameter
\mathrm{Z}
-space and tent space, respectively. The converse inclusion is harder as we have to dominate the pointwise maximal function by averages. The calculation is unfortunately lengthy and technical. The characterizations that we obtain in the end are in Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.4
.

Functional-analytic properties of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces

To establish basic properties such as density, completeness and a
“change of angle”
formula, we mostly combine basic estimates for integrals with covering arguments adapted to the geometry of Whitney boxes. Also, arguments from
[
1
]
are recycled. To investigate duality, the strategy is to embed our
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces into simpler spaces for which duality results are already known. In the Banach case (Theorem
3.11
), we embed into a weighted vector-valued Lebesgue space. Next, as a general tool, we develop a dyadic characterization, which follows from straightforward covering arguments. From this description, embedding results of Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev-type (Theorem
3.14
) follow readily. Now, to obtain furthermore duality results in the quasi-Banach case (Theorem
3.16
), we can embed quasi-Banach
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces into
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces in the Banach range, for which we have already developed the duality theory.

Interpolation theory of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces

First, we develop real interpolation results. There are two of them: one (Theorem
4.1
) with fixed parameter
p
, but a lot of flexibility for the parameters
q_{i}
, and another one in which
p
can vary as well (Theorem
4.2
). The proof of the first of them is based on direct estimates of the
K
-functional. The argument for the second one uses the dyadic characterization of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces to embed them into weighted vector-valued sequence spaces. For them, real interpolation is known.

As a consequence, we deduce that in the case
p<\infty
, all our
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces can be obtained as real interpolants of tent spaces (Proposition
4.4
). All of these results are inspired by
[
26
, Thm. 2.4.2]
, where the author shows analogous results for Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

Second, we develop a complex interpolation theory. In the
“reflexive regime”
, we use our embedding into a weighted vector-valued Lebesgue space to establish one inclusion of the interpolation identity, whereas the other one follows from a duality argument using the results from Section
3
.
This special case is stated in Lemma
5.2
.
To obtain an extension to the quasi-Banach range (Theorem
5.1
), the complex interpolation task is first reformulated into a question of Calderón products. To those, convexity arguments apply, and we can reduce matters to the
“reflexive regime”
, for which we have already established complex interpolation.

1.3.
Notation

We use the following notation throughout the paper.
We denote by
\mathbb{N},\mathbb{N}_{0},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}
the set of all positive integers, all non-negative integers, all integers, and all real numbers, respectively. Throughout,
d\geq 1
denotes the dimension of the underlying Euclidean space. We write
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}=(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for the upper half-space. We denote the open ball centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
t>0
by
B(x,t)
. The Whitney box associated to
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
is denoted by
W(t,x)=(\frac{t}{2},t)\times B(x,t)
. We use the symbol
\|\cdot\|_{X}
for the (quasi-)norm of a (quasi-)normed space
X
, and
X^{\prime}
as its anti-dual space. For a measure space
\Omega
and a Banach space
X
we denote by
\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega;X)
the space of all strongly measurable functions with the usual identification (that is, functions equal almost everywhere are identified). Moreover, for
0<p\leq\infty
, we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;X)

\displaystyle=\bigg\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega;X):\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}=\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\bigg\},

with the usual modification if
p=\infty
. For
p\in(1,\infty)
we denote by
p^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
the unique number such that
1=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
. Moreover, for
p\in(0,1]
, put
p^{\prime}\coloneqq\infty
. Finally, if
p=\infty
, then set
p^{\prime}\coloneqq 1
. If
X
is a quasi-Banach space, we also define for
0<p\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the weighted vector-valued sequence spaces
\ell^{p}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};X)
as follows:

\displaystyle\ell^{p}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};X)\coloneqq\Big\{x:x=(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\ \text{with}\ x_{k}\in X,\,\|x\|_{\ell^{p}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};X)}\coloneqq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta p}\|x_{k}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\},

again with the usual modification if
p=\infty
.
For two real numbers
a,b
, we write
a\lesssim b
if there exists a constant
C>0
, whose precise value may vary from line to line but depends at most on the structural constants, such that
a\leq Cb
. Conversely, we write
a\gtrsim b
if
Ca\geq b
. Furthermore, we write
a\simeq b
if both
a\lesssim b
and
a\gtrsim b
hold.

2.
Characterization of Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces

In this section, we provide a continuous characterization of homogeneous Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces with respect to general kernels with non-compact Fourier support. We have motivated the usage of such kernels in the introduction.

To do so, we need to introduce objects from harmonic analysis which will appear in the formulation of the characterization theorems as well as in their proofs.
Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Schwartz space and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
its topological dual space, the space of tempered distributions. Furthermore, for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):[{\mathrm{D}}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(f)](0)=0,\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}$ with $|\alpha|\leq N$}\},

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform and
{\mathrm{D}}^{\alpha}=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\partial_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
. In this context, we also define
\mathcal{S}_{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{S}_{N}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the topological dual space of
\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equipped with the subspace topology of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It can be identified with
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\mathcal{P}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of all polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with complex coefficients of degree at most
N
, see
[
26
, Chap. 5]
. If
\varphi
and
\psi
are integrable functions, then their convolution
\psi\ast\varphi
is defined as

\displaystyle(\psi\ast\varphi)(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x-y)\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

If both
\psi
and
\varphi
belong to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then their convolution also belongs to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, the convolution can be generalized to
(\psi,f)\in\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
via
(\psi\ast f)(x)=f(\psi(x-\cdot))
, which is well-defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and has at most polynomial growth.

Finally, we introduce the definition of the Peetre maximal function for dilations. Fix
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
t>0
define
\Psi_{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
\Psi_{t}(\cdot)\coloneqq t^{-d}\Psi(\cdot/t)
and
\delta_{t}(f)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
\delta_{t}(f)(\varphi)\coloneqq f(\varphi_{t})
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, we define for
a>0
and
t>0
the Peetre maximal function as

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\Psi_{t}^{*}f)_{a}(x)=\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Psi_{t}\ast f)(x+y)|}{\Big(1+\frac{|y|}{t}\Big)^{a}}\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

They will appear as a technical tool in the characterization of Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces. The latter will be introduced next. For further properties of these spaces, we refer to
[
26
, Sec. 5.1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that

(1)

\varphi_{k}(x)=\varphi_{0}(2^{-k}x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(2)

\supp\varphi_{0}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:2^{-1}\leq|x|\leq 2\}
,

(3)

\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{k}(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

and set
\Phi_{k}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{k})\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
0<p,q\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we define the homogeneous Besov space as

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}\coloneqq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k\beta q}\|\Phi_{k}\ast f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty\Big\}.

If in addition
0<p<\infty
, we define the homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces as

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}\coloneqq\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k\beta q}|(\Phi_{k}\ast f)(\cdot)|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}<\infty\Big\}.

We would like to draw attention to the fact that the underlying building blocks
(\Phi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in the definition of these homogeneous spaces are a discrete family of convolution operators with a kernel that has compact support away from zero in Fourier-space. However, for applications in partial differential equations, one is interested in definitions with more general kernels. In particular, definitions with respect to a continuous family of convolution operators
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
where the kernels decay fast enough in zero and infinity are of great interest. Characterizations for both
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for such general kernels were established, for example, by Ullrich in
[
28
]
. There, for every Triebel–Lizorkin characterization but one, a corresponding Besov characterization was found. The exceptional case is a characterization using tent spaces. While abstract arguments from interpolation theory allowed us to extend elements of the Besov space
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into a
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces in the introduction, a proper characterization using
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces on the one hand, and the treatment of
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p\neq q
on the other hand, were still missing. The following theorem closes this gap in the literature.

Theorem 2.2

(Characterization of Besov spaces)
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
, and
R\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{-1\}
such that
R+1>\beta
. Furthermore, let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\Phi(\xi)|>0\quad\text{on}\quad\Big\{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<|\xi|<\varepsilon\Big\}

for some
\varepsilon>0
, and

(2.3)

\displaystyle[{\mathrm{D}}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\Phi](0)=0\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}$ with $|\alpha|\leq R$.}

Then, the space
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be characterized as follows (with the usual modifications if
q
and/or
r
are infinite):

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}\Phi_{s}\ast f(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty\bigg\}.

Remark 2.3
.

We clarify how the characterization identity has to be understood.

•

In this theorem, the homogeneous space on the left-hand side is understood as a realization within

\mathcal{S}_{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
R
is as in the statement. This realization aligns with the framework presented in
[
24
, Sec. 2.4.3]
(see also
[
9
,
22
]
) in the following sense. By
[
24
, Thm. 2.31]
, we can realize
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
within the space
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
M=\max\{\lfloor\beta-\frac{d}{p}\rfloor,-1\}
and
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the integer part of
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Because
M\leq R
(see bullet point below), we have the embedding
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{S}_{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and thus can realize
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
within
\mathcal{S}_{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As a consequence, the identity in Theorem
2.2
is understood as a set-theoretic equality in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with corresponding equivalence of norms. The same conclusion applies to Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

•

We claim that
M\leq R
, which implies
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Indeed, if
M=-1
then
M\leq R
holds trivially. In the case
M=\lfloor\beta-\frac{d}{p}\rfloor
, assume for the sake of contradiction that
M>R
. Since
M,R\in\mathbb{Z}
, this implies
R+1\leq M
and thus

\displaystyle\beta<R+1\leq M\leq\beta-\frac{d}{p},

which is impossible.

Before we start with the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we want to mention that the assumptions of the theorem are identical to those in
[
28
, Thm. 2.9]
. This allows us to use his techniques and derived identities as a black box for our proof. Nevertheless, the reader is advised to keep a copy of
[
28
]
handy.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Denote by
X
the space on the right-hand side in the statement to simplify notation. Taking Remark
2.3
into account, we have to show the equivalence
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=X
. To do so, we will divide the proof into two steps. The first step proves
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset X
while the second step provides the reverse inclusion. For the rest of the proof, we fix
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We suppose
q,r<\infty
for notational convenience.

Step 1:
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\supset X
.

Assume that
f\in X
and fix
a>\frac{d}{\min\{1,p,q,r\}}
. Then we can use the inequality (2.89) of
[
28
]
in the homogeneous setting (see also the proof of
[
28
, Thm. 2.8]
), which reads as follows: for
\delta=R+1-\beta
, one has

(2.4)

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta}2^{k\beta}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}t}f)_{a}(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\in[1/2,2]
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. The slight enlargement of the constant
\delta
as well as the interval of
t
compared to
[
28
]
does not cause any difficulties. Fix
0<\gamma<\min\{1,p,q,r\}
large enough, such that
a\gamma>d
. Then, since
\gamma<1
, the above identity implies

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}t}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}.

Next, we use the inequality (2.66) of
[
28
]
on the right-hand side of the previous bound, to get

(2.5)

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{(k+m)d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

for all
N\geq a
. Observe
2^{-m}t\leq 2
as
m\geq 0
and
t\leq 2
. If we take
|z|\leq 2^{-(k+m)}t
, then

\displaystyle 1+2^{k}|x+z-y|\leq 1+2^{k}|x-y|+2^{k}|z|\leq 1+2^{k}|x-y|+2^{-m}t\lesssim 1+2^{k}|x-y|.

Hence, by translation, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(y+z)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Since the left-hand side of this bound is independent of
z
, integrating both sides by

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(0,2^{-(k+m)}t)}|\cdot|^{\frac{r}{\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{r}}

and using Minkowski’s integral inequality yield

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Big(\fint_{B(0,2^{-(k+m)}t)}|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(y+z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\Big)^{\frac{\gamma}{r}}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Plugging this into (
2.5
) and performing a translation give us

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{(k+m)d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Big(\fint_{B(y,2^{-(k+m)}t)}|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\Big)^{\frac{\gamma}{r}}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Lastly, for
\lambda\in[1,2]
fixed but arbitrary, we integrate both sides by

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}^{\lambda}|\cdot|^{\frac{r}{\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{r}},

to get with Minkowski’s integral inequality (
\gamma<r
) that

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{(k+m)d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}[g_{k}\ast|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f|^{\gamma}](x),

where we define

\displaystyle(\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f)(y)\coloneqq\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}^{\lambda}\fint\limits_{B(y,2^{-(k+m)}t)}|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

as well as

\displaystyle g_{k}(x)\coloneqq\frac{2^{kd}}{(1+2^{k}|x|)^{a\gamma}}.

Since
a\gamma>d
,
g_{k}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with uniform
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-bound with respect to
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Next, take
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}
-norms with respect to
x
on both sides of (
2
), and use
Minkowski’s and Young’s convolution inequalities, to obtain

\displaystyle\|2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}\|g_{k}\ast|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f|^{\gamma}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f\|^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

Now, integrate both sides by

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}|\cdot|^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}

and use Minkowski’s inequality once more, to get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}h_{k},

where we define

\displaystyle h_{k}\coloneqq 2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}.

Interpreting the right-hand side of (
2.7
) as a discrete convolution, we take
\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}
-norms on both sides and apply Young’s (discrete) convolution inequality twice, as well as an index shift, to get

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}h_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k+m}f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}\Big\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}2^{-(m-k)(N\gamma-d+\beta\gamma)}\bigg(2^{m\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{m}f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}\bigg\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\|\bigg(2^{k\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k}f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}\bigg\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\bigg(2^{k\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k}f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|^{\gamma}_{\ell^{q}},

where we have chosen
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that
N\gamma-d+\beta\gamma>0
. Taking the
\gamma
-root of the last estimate yields

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\bigg\|\bigg(2^{k\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k}f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\ell^{q}}.

The left-hand side in this bound is equivalent to
\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
, see
[
28
, Thm. 2.9]
. For the right-hand side, we calculate

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{1}^{2}2^{k\beta q}\|\Phi^{\lambda}_{k}f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{1}^{2}2^{k\beta q}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}^{\lambda}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,2^{-k}t)}|(\Phi_{2^{-k}t}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

First, substitute
2^{-k}t=s
, so that

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{1}^{2}2^{k\beta q}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{2^{-(k+1)}{\lambda}}^{2^{-k}\lambda}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,s)}|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

and finally substitute
2^{-k}\lambda=t
in the outer integral to get

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{-k}}^{2^{-(k-1)}}2^{k\beta q}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,s)}|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where we used
2^{-k}\simeq t
and
t\simeq s
in the last step.
Hence, we have the inequality

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

which shows the first inclusion.

Step 2:
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset X
.

To tackle this inclusion, recall the Peetre maximal function from (
2.1
) and fix
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using a substitution and introducing a supremum, we calculate

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(0,t)}|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(x+z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(0,t)}\Big(\frac{|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(x+z)|}{(1+\frac{|z|}{s})^{a}}\Big)^{r}\Big(1+\frac{|z|}{s}\Big)^{ar}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(0,t)}\Big(\sup\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(x+\tilde{z})|}{(1+\frac{|\tilde{z}|}{s})^{a}}\Big)^{r}\Big(1+\frac{|z|}{s}\Big)^{ar}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

Note that
1+\frac{|z|}{s}\simeq 1
on the Whitney box. Hence, introducing another supremum in
s
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(0,t)}\Big(\sup\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(x+\tilde{z})|}{(1+\frac{|\tilde{z}|}{s})^{a}}\Big)^{r}\Big(1+\frac{|z|}{s}\Big)^{ar}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}\sup\limits_{\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(x+\tilde{z})|}{(1+\frac{|\tilde{z}|}{s})^{a}}=\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}(\Phi_{s}^{*}f)_{a}(x).

Thus, for
f\in\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it suffices to show

(2.8)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-q\beta}\bigg\|\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}(\Phi_{s}^{*}f)_{a}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}.

As in Step 1, we take the inequality (
2.4
) as the starting point. In this bound, the discrete scale
2^{-l}
is used on the left-hand side, and the
“continuous”
scale
2^{-k}t
on the right-hand side. We claim that these roles can be swapped. Indeed, for
t>0
, recall
\Phi_{t}(\cdot)=t^{-d}\Phi(\cdot/t)\in\mathcal{S}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\delta_{t}(f)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined by
\delta_{t}(f)(\varphi)=f(\varphi_{t})
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. From the elementary identity
(\Phi_{t}\ast f)(x)=(\Phi\ast f_{t})(x/t)
it follows

(2.9)

\displaystyle(\Phi_{t}^{*}f)_{a}(x)=(\Phi^{*}_{1}\delta_{t}(f))_{a}(x/t)\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Using this transformation twice in (
2.4
) applied with
t
replaced by
t^{-1}
, keeping
\Phi_{2^{-l}t}=(\Phi_{2^{-l}})_{t}
in mind, we find

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}t}^{*}f)_{a}(x)

\displaystyle=2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}\delta_{t}(f))_{a}(x/t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta}2^{k\beta}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}t^{-1}}\delta_{t}(f))_{a}(x/t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta}2^{k\beta}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}(x)

as desired.
Since the right-hand side of this inequality is independent of
t\in[1/2,2]
, we can fix
\lambda\in[1,2]
and estimate

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta}\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}(x)|\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta}2^{k\beta}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}(x).

Because
\gamma<1
, we also get

(2.10)

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{\gamma}\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}.

Take
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}
-norms on both sides of (
2.10
) and use Minkowski’s inequality to get

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}\big\|\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}|\big\|^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}\|^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

Next, we integrate both sides by

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}|\cdot|^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}

and take
\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}
-norms. With Young’s (discrete) convolution inequality, this gives

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{1}^{2}2^{l\beta q}\big\|\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}|\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}\|^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\Big)_{l}\Big\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k\beta q}\|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{\gamma}_{\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}},

where the last line follows from
[
28
, Theorem 2.9]
. Finally, applying several substitutions and using
t\simeq 2^{-l}
, we have for the left-hand side of the last bound

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{1}^{2}2^{l\beta q}\big\|\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}|\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{1}^{2}2^{l\beta q}\big\|\sup\limits_{\frac{2^{-l}\lambda}{2}<s\leq 2^{-l}\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}|\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{-l}}^{2^{-(l-1)}}2^{l\beta q}\big\|\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}|\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{-l}}^{2^{-(l-1)}}t^{-\beta q}\big\|\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}|\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta q}\big\|\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}|\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

which completes (
2.8
), and hence the reverse inequality.
∎

We also present a similar characterization for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces. The proof is essentially as before with some minor changes. To meaning of the word
“characterization”
in the statement was clarified in Remark
2.3
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p<\infty
,
0<q,r\leq\infty
, and let
R\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{-1\}
such that
R+1>\beta
. Moreover, let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
fulfill properties (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) for this choice of
R
and some
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, the space
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
can be characterized as follows (with the usual modifications of
q
and/or
r
is infinite):

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{S}_{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}\Phi_{s}\ast f(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}<\infty\bigg\}.

Proof.

Let
a>\frac{d}{\min\{1,r,q,p\}}
and
0<\gamma<\min\{1,r,q,p\}
such that
a\gamma>d
. By a similar procedure as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, but with an extra integration by

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}|\cdot|^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}},

on both sides of (
2
), followed by Minkowski’s integral inequality (
q<\gamma
), we get for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\in[1/2,2]
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{(k+m)d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}[g_{k}\ast|\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f|^{\gamma}](x),

where
\delta>0
is the same as before, but we define

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f)(y)\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}^{\lambda}\fint\limits_{B(y,2^{-(k+m)}t)}|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

and

\displaystyle g_{k}(x)\coloneqq\frac{2^{kd}}{(1+2^{k}|x|)^{a\gamma}}.

Again, the functions
g_{k}
have a uniform
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-bound with respect to
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and we note that they are radial. Using the majorant property of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, see
[
14
, Thm. 2.1.10]
, we have

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}\mathcal{M}(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f|^{\gamma})(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}h_{k}(x),

where we define

\displaystyle h_{k}(x)\coloneqq 2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{md}\mathcal{M}(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f|^{\gamma})(x)\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Next, take the
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z}))
-norm on both sides and use the convolution estimate
[
28
, Lemma 2.13]
, to get

\displaystyle\Big\|\|2^{l\beta}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(\cdot)|\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\|2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(\cdot)|^{\gamma}\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\|\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}h_{k}(\cdot)\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\|h_{k}(\cdot)\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}.

By an index shift, we find

\displaystyle h_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l=k}^{\infty}2^{-(l-k)(N\gamma-d+\beta\gamma)}2^{l\beta\gamma}\mathcal{M}(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}f|^{\gamma})(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|l-k|\delta^{\prime}}2^{l\beta\gamma}\mathcal{M}(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}f|^{\gamma})(x),

where
\delta^{\prime}\coloneqq(N\gamma-d+\beta\gamma)>0
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. Using again
[
28
, Lemma 2.13]
, and the Fefferman–Stein inequality, see for example
[
28
, Thm. 2.1]
, deduce

\displaystyle\Big\|\|2^{l\beta}(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(\cdot)\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\|\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|l-k|\delta^{\prime}}2^{l\beta\gamma}\mathcal{M}(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}f|^{\gamma})(\cdot)\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\|2^{l\beta\gamma}\mathcal{M}(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}f|^{\gamma})(\cdot)\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\|2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}f)(\cdot)|^{\gamma}\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\|2^{l\beta}(\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}f)(\cdot)\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{\gamma}.

The norm on the left-hand side is equivalent to
\|f\|^{\gamma}_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
, see
[
28
, Thm. 2.8]
. For the norm on the right-hand side, we calculate

\displaystyle\Big\|\|2^{l\beta}(\widetilde{\Phi}_{l}f)(x)\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{l\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}^{\lambda}\fint\limits_{B(x,2^{-l}t)}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}t}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{l\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{2^{-l}\lambda}{2}}^{2^{-l}\lambda}\fint\limits_{B(x,s)}|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we substituted
2^{-l}t=s
. Finally, substitute
2^{-l}\lambda=t
and use
B(x,s)\subset B(x,t)
to get

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{l\beta q}\int\limits_{2^{-l}}^{2^{-l+1}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,s)}|(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{-l}}^{2^{-l+1}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

using also
2^{-l}\simeq t
and
s\simeq t
in the penultimate step.
Hence,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For the reverse inequality, as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, it suffices to show

(2.12)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-q\beta}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}(\Phi_{s}^{*}f)_{a}(x)\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}.

We start with the inequality (
2.10
), which holds for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, namely

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{\gamma}\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}(x)^{\gamma}

for
\lambda\in[1,2]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Integrate both sides by

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}|\cdot|^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}},

to get

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{q}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}(x)^{\gamma}.

Take
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}(\mathbb{Z}))
-norms on both sides and use the convolution estimate
[
28
, Lemma 2.13]
as well as
[
28
, Thm. 2.8]
to get

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\bigg\|2^{l\beta}\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}(\cdot)|\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\ell^{q}}\Bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg\|\bigg\|\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-k}}f)_{a}(\cdot)^{\gamma}\bigg\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}}\Bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\big\|2^{l\beta\gamma}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}}f)_{a}(\cdot)^{\gamma}\big\|_{\ell^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\big\|2^{l\beta}(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}}f)_{a}(\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\simeq\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}}^{\gamma}.

Finally, for the
q
-th power of the inner
\ell^{q}
-(quasi-)norm on the left-hand side, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{l\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<t\leq\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{2^{-l}t}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{l\beta q}\int\limits_{1}^{2}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{2^{-l}\lambda}{2}<s\leq 2^{-l}\lambda}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{l\beta q}\int\limits_{2^{-l}}^{2^{-l+1}}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{-l}}^{2^{-l+1}}t^{-\beta q}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta q}\Big(\sup\limits_{\frac{t}{2}<s\leq t}|(\Phi^{*}_{s}f)_{a}(x)|\Big)^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t},

where we used the substitutions
2^{-l}t=s
, followed by
2^{-l}\lambda=t
. This proves (
2.12
) and therefore the theorem.
∎

(Gauss–Weierstrass semigroup characterization of

3.
Functional analytic properties of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces

We saw in the previous section that homogeneous Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces can be characterized via extensions to the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
that belong to a certain extension space. In this section, we want to study these extension spaces from a function space theoretical point of view. First, we provide concrete definitions of these spaces and compare them to known spaces from the literature. Then, we study properties of these spaces, such as completeness and density (Section
3.1
), duality (Section
3.2
and
3.4
), as well as dyadic descriptions of these spaces in conjunction with Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev-type embeddings (Section
3.3
).

3.1.
Definitions and basic properties

Here, we define the function spaces that appear in the characterizations of Section
2
, and collect basic properties such as completeness and density. Furthermore, we provide a link to existing function spaces from the literature to contextualize our new function spaces.

Definition 3.1

(
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces and tent spaces)
.

For
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
define the space
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

with the usual replacement of the integral by an
\mathrm{ess\,sup}
if the corresponding parameters
p,q,r
are infinite.
If in addition
0<p<\infty
, then define the space
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}<\infty,

with the same replacements in the case of infinite parameters as before. In the case
\beta=0
we usually drop the dependence from the notation.

Let
0<a<b<\infty
and
c>0
. Then, we define the Whitney box
W_{a,b,c}(t,x)
associated to a point
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
as

\displaystyle W_{a,b,c}(t,x)\coloneqq(at,bt)\times B(x,ct).

If
a=\frac{1}{2}
,
b=c=1
then we get
W_{a,b,c}(t,x)=W(t,x)
.
Even though our function spaces
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
are defined by averages with respect to the specific Whitney box
W(t,x)
, it turns out that any other choice
(a,b,c)
as above yields an equivalent norm. We will provide a proof for this fact in Proposition
3.7
and use it freely afterwards.

Remark 3.2
.

Let us compare with the definitions in
[
12
,
7
]
, see also
[
2
]
, where authors introduced new scales of function spaces, so-called tent and Z-spaces. For
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the tent space
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
(if in addition
p<\infty
) and the
\mathrm{Z}
-space
\mathrm{Z}^{p,r}_{\beta}
defined in these references consist of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that the corresponding (quasi-)norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|t^{-\beta}f(t,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

are finite, with the usual modifications in the case of infinite parameters. Our spaces are generalizations of these spaces with coincidence in the cases
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,q}_{\beta}=\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{Z}^{p,p,r}_{\beta}=\mathrm{Z}^{p,r}_{\beta}
.

Moreover, Huang has introduced in
[
17
]
the Whitney-averaged tent space
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(y,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Observe that they have an additional average compared to the norm of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
. Nevertheless, both spaces coincide with equivalence of norms due to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
0<q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}=\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
with equivalent norms.

Proof.

To keep the notation simple, we suppose
q,r
finite. The other cases are similar.

Step 1:
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\subset\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
.
Using
B(y,t)\subset B(x,2t)
, we calculate

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(y,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

A straight forward change of Whitney parameters in the norm on the right-hand side (see Proposition
3.7
for the change of Whitney parameters in full generality) implies

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{p}.

Step 2:
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}\subset\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
.

Introducing an extra average, we get the identity

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we use the covering
B(x,t)\subset B(y,2t)
for
y\in B(x,t)
to get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{p}\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(y,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since the norm
of
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
is invariant under a change of Whitney parameters, see
[
17
, Obs. 2.4]
, we get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{p}\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(y,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}}^{p},

which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We point out that Huang also defined and studies such tent spaces in the case
p=\infty
.

For Huang’s tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
, many function space properties are already established. By virtue of the preceding lemma, many of them transfer to our tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
as well. This is why we will mainly focus on the study of the
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
spaces in this article and will postpone further investigation of the
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
spaces to future work.
A notable exception is Proposition
4.4
, in which we link the real interpolation theories of tent and
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces.

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
0<r\leq\infty
and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
, the function

\displaystyle F(t,x)\coloneqq\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

is lower semicontinuous. Consequently, if
p=\infty
and/or
q=\infty
, one can replace the respective
\mathrm{ess\,sup}
by
\sup
in the definition of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
for all
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Fix
M>0
and assume that
F(t,x)>M
for some
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
.

Case 1:
0<r<\infty
. By continuity of the Lebesgue measure, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{2}{(t+\varepsilon)|B(0,t+\varepsilon)|}\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}^{t-\varepsilon}\int\limits_{B(x,t-\varepsilon)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}>M.

Take
(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
with
|x-\tilde{x}|<\varepsilon
and
t-\varepsilon<\tilde{t}<t+\varepsilon
. Then, one has

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{t}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},t-\varepsilon\Big)\times B(x,t-\varepsilon)\subset\Big(\frac{\tilde{t}}{2},\tilde{t}\Big)\times B(x,\tilde{t}),

which results in

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{2}{(t+\varepsilon)|B(0,t+\varepsilon)|}\int\limits_{\frac{\tilde{t}}{2}}^{\tilde{t}}\int\limits_{B(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}>M.

Finally, since
\tilde{t}<t+\varepsilon
, we get

\displaystyle F(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})=\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\tilde{t}}{2}}^{\tilde{t}}\fint\limits_{B(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}>\bigg(\frac{2}{(t+\varepsilon)|B(0,t+\varepsilon)|}\int\limits_{\frac{\tilde{t}}{2}}^{\tilde{t}}\int\limits_{B(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}>M.

This implies that
\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}:F(t,x)>M\}
is open.

Case 2:
r=\infty
. We claim again that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\frac{t}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<s<t-\varepsilon}\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{y\in B(x,t-\varepsilon)}|f(s,y)|>M.

Assume the contrary. Then, by continuity of the Lebesgue measure, it follows that

\displaystyle|\{(s,y)\in W(t,x):|f(s,y)|>M\}|

\displaystyle\quad=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big|\{(s,y)\in\Big(\frac{t}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},t-\varepsilon\Big)\times B(x,t-\varepsilon):|f(s,y)|>M\}\Big|=0.

But this would imply
F(t,x)\leq M
,
which is a contradiction. Now, the same procedure as in Case 1 implies the claim also for the case
r=\infty
.
∎

Another important property is the following quantitative
“change of angle”
formula. The name is motivated by the corresponding change of angle formula for tent spaces, see
[
4
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
\lambda>1
we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(\cdot,\lambda t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{\min\{p,r\}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}},

where the implicit constants depend only on
p,r
and
d
.

Proof.

It is enough to look at
\beta=0
. For notational convenience, we just consider
p,q,r
finite. Fix
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. There exist
J\subset\mathbb{N}
and
(\xi_{j})_{j\in J}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle B(x,\lambda t)\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}B(x-\xi_{j},t)\quad\text{and}\quad|J|\leq C\lambda^{d}

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
d
. Then, we get

\displaystyle\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(x,\lambda t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\sum\limits_{j\in J}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x-\xi_{j},t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

If
p<r
we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x-\xi_{j},t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x-\xi_{j},t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{d}{p}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

On the contrary, if
p\geq r
we use Minkowski’s inequality to give

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x-\xi_{j},t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum\limits_{j\in J}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{r}}}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\Big\|\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{r}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\Big\|\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{r}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\frac{1}{r}}\lambda^{\frac{d}{r}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}..

Thus, both cases together yield the inequality of the claim upon applying the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-norm in
t
.
∎

As announced earlier, we can now use Lemma
3.6
to show that the definition of the
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces is independent of the underlying Whitney box.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. For
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
,
0<a<b<\infty
and
c>0
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{at}^{bt}\fint\limits_{B(x,ct)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}},

where the implicit constants depend on
a,b,c
,
p
,
q
,
r
, and the dimension
d
.

Proof.

For simplicity we only consider
p,q,r
finite. Moreover, we only provide the direction
“
\lesssim
”
. The reverse direction follows analogously. Let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
2^{m}<a<b<2^{n}
. Fix
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. Then, estimate for the average integral on the left-hand side

\displaystyle\fint\limits_{at}^{bt}\fint\limits_{B(x,ct)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\,\fint\limits_{2^{k-1}t}^{2^{k}t}\fint\limits_{B(x,ct)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Using finiteness of the sum, we can estimate

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{at}^{bt}\fint\limits_{B(x,ct)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\,\fint\limits_{2^{k-1}t}^{2^{k}t}\fint\limits_{B(x,ct)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{2^{k-1}t}^{2^{k}t}\fint\limits_{B(x,ct)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By the substitution
2^{k}t=\tau
, we obtain

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(x,\frac{c}{2^{k}}\tau)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\Big(\frac{c}{2^{k}}\Big)^{-\frac{d}{r}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\tau}\tau^{-d}\int\limits_{B(x,\frac{c}{2^{k}}\tau)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

Finally, using Lemma
3.6
in the case
\frac{c}{2^{k}}>1
or
B(x,\frac{c}{2^{k}}\tau)\subset B(x,\tau)
when
\frac{c}{2^{k}}\leq 1
, we end up with

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n}\Big(\frac{c}{2^{k}}\Big)^{-\frac{d}{r}}\min\Big\{1,\Big(\frac{c}{2^{k}}\Big)^{\frac{d}{\min\{p,r\}}}\Big\}\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}},

where the implicit constant depends only on
p,q,r,d
as well as
a,b,c
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
be compact. Then, for a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{1}_{K}f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)},

where the implicit constants depend on
p,q,r,\beta,d
and
K
.

Proof.

Using dependence of the implicit constants on
\beta
and
K
, we can without loss of generality reduce to the case
\beta=0
. Moreover, to simplify the notation, we only consider
p,q,r
finite.
Since
K
is compact, there exist
0<a<b<\infty
,
R>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
K\subset(a,b)\times B(x_{0},R)
.

Step 1: the direction
“
\lesssim
”
. One has

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|\mathbf{1}_{K}(s,y)f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,r}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|\mathbf{1}_{K}(s,y)f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Due to the observation

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{(t/2,t)\times B(x,t)}(s,y)\mathbf{1}_{(a,b)\times B(x_{0},R)}(s,y)\leq\mathbf{1}_{(a,2b)}(t)\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{0},2b+R)}(x),

we end up with

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|\mathbf{1}_{K}(s,y)f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{2b}\bigg(\int\limits_{B(x_{0},2b+R)}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,R,p,q}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}.

Step 2: the reverse inequality
“
\gtrsim
”
. We estimate

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,r,d}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x_{0},R)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}},

where
(x_{j})_{j\in J}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some finite set
J\subset\mathbb{N}
and
|J|
depends only on
d,a,R
. An averaging argument yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now, for
x,y\in B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})
, one has
|x-y|<a
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(x_{j},\frac{a}{2})}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x,a)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,R,d,p}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x,a)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

To introduce in addition a time integral, we do a similar trick. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
b<2^{N}a
. Then,

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{B(x,a)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{l=0}^{N-1}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{2^{l}a}^{2^{l+1}a}\int\limits_{B(x,a)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{l=0}^{N-1}\bigg(\fint\limits_{2^{l+1}a}^{2^{l+2}a}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{2^{l}a}^{2^{l+1}a}\int\limits_{B(x,a)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{l=0}^{N-1}\bigg(\int\limits_{2^{l+1}a}^{2^{l+2}a}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{4}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{4}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|(\mathbf{1}_{K}f)(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since the
\mathrm{Z}
-space (quasi-)norms with respect to different Whitney boxes are equivalent, see Proposition
3.7
, the claim follows.
∎

3.2.
Duality: Banach range

A very useful tool to analyze our spaces is an embedding into vector-valued Lebesgue spaces, see
[
1
,
15
]
for the same approach for tent spaces. More precisely, denote by
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{t;\beta}\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}x}\mathrm{L}^{r}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}s,y}
the vector-valued Lebesgue space

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}^{q}\bigg((0,\infty),\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\beta q}};\mathrm{L}^{p}\Big(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathrm{L}^{r}\Big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+};\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\Big)\Big)\bigg).

Then, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

The linear mapping

\displaystyle i:{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}_{t;\beta}\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}x}\mathrm{L}^{r}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}s,y},\quad f(s,y)\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{W(t,x)}(s,y)f(s,y)

satisfies
\|i(f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{t;\beta}\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}x}\mathrm{L}^{r}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}s,y}}\simeq\|f\|_{{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}}
. In particular, it is bounded, injective and has closed range.

Proof.

It is clear that

\displaystyle\|i(f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{t;\beta}\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}x}\mathrm{L}^{r}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}s,y}}\simeq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\|f\|_{{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}}.

Therefore, the map is bounded, injective and has closed range due to Proposition
3.9
.
∎

Theorem 3.11

(Duality – Banach range)
.

Let
1\leq p,q,r<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}\cdot\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}

for any measurable functions
f,g
. Moreover, we can identify
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
with equivalent norms via the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
duality pairing.

Proof.

Let
f
and
g
be measurable functions. Then,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint\limits_{s}^{2s}\fint\limits_{B(y,s)}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|\cdot|s^{\beta}g(s,y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}\cdot\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}},

where we used Hölder’s inequality three times in the last step. In particular,
\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}\subset({\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}})^{\prime}
. For the reverse inclusion, pick
L\in({\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}})^{\prime}
. Recall the mapping
i
from Lemma
3.10
and let
\Rg(i)
denote its range. Then, we have

\displaystyle L\circ i^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\Rg(i),\mathbb{C}),

which can be extended to some functional on
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{t;\beta}\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}x}\mathrm{L}^{r}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}s,y}
. Let
\tilde{L}
denote this extension and identify it with
g\in\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}_{t;\beta}\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}x}\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}_{\vphantom{t,\beta}s,y}
by duality of weighted vector-valued Lebesgue spaces. Then, for all
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
one has

\displaystyle L(f)=L\circ i^{-1}(i(f))

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\tau,z,s,y)\cdot\overline{i(f)(\tau,z,s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\tau,z,s,y)\cdot\overline{\mathbf{1}_{W(\tau,z)}(s,y)f(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{s}^{2s}\int\limits_{B(y,\tau)}g(\tau,z,s,y)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau s^{d}}\bigg)\cdot\overline{f(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int\limits_{s}^{2s}\int\limits_{B(y,\tau)}g(\tau,z,s,y)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau s^{d}}\bigg|\lesssim\fint\limits_{s}^{2s}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}|g(\tau,z,s,y)|\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau\eqqcolon G(s,y).

Hence, it suffices to show that
G\in\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
. Indeed, if
G\in\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
then we can identify
L
with a
\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
-function and thus get the reverse inclusion
({\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}})^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
. To prove
G\in\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
, we fix
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. For
\frac{t}{2}<s<t
and
s<\tau<2s
we have
(s,2s)\times B(y,\tau)\subset(\frac{t}{2},2t)\times B(x,3t)
for all
y\in B(x,t)
. This, together with Minkowski’s integral inequality, yields

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{\beta}G(s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{{r^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg|s^{\beta}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,3t)}|g(\tau,z,s,y)|\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,3t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|\tau^{\beta}g(\tau,z,s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\,\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,3t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\tau}{4}}^{2\tau}\fint\limits_{B(z,8\tau)}|\tau^{\beta}g(\tau,z,s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\,\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,3t)}h(\tau,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau,

where

(3.1)

\displaystyle h(\tau,z)\coloneqq\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\tau}{4}}^{2\tau}\fint\limits_{B(z,8\tau)}|\tau^{\beta}g(\tau,z,s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\,\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}.

Taking the
p^{\prime}
-th power and integrating both sides in
x
in the last estimate yields

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{\beta}G(s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg|\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,3t)}h(\tau,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg|^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using Fubini’s theorem, Jensen’s inequality and an averaging trick gives us

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{\beta}G(s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint\limits_{B(x,3t)}\bigg|\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}h(\tau,z)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg|\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}h(\tau,z)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}z.

Taking the
p^{\prime}
-th square root and taking
\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}
-norms in
t
on both sides yields

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}h(\tau,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Again, Minkowski’s integral inequality, Jensen’s inequality and an averaging trick gives us

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\|h(\tau,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\|h(\tau,\cdot)\|^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\|h(\tau,\cdot)\|^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Finally, by the definition of
h
in (
3.1
) we get

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\tau}{4}}^{2\tau}\fint\limits_{B(z,8\tau)}|\tau^{\beta}g(\tau,z,s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\,\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\bigg\|^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\tau^{\beta}g(\tau,z,s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\,\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\bigg\|^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},

which is bounded by the definition of
g
.
∎

3.3.
Dyadic description and embeddings

In this section, we give a dyadic characterization of our
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces. This can be seen as an extension of the dyadic characterization for the
\mathrm{Z}^{p,r}_{\beta}
-spaces of Amenta, see
[
2
,
3
]
and Remark
3.2
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we define the set of all dyadic half-open cubes of generation
k
as

\displaystyle\square_{k}\coloneqq\{2^{k}x+[0,2^{k})^{d}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\},

and denote by
\square=\cup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\square_{k}
the family of all dyadic cubes. Moreover, we denote by

\displaystyle\bar{Q}\coloneqq\Big[\frac{\ell(Q)}{2},\ell(Q)\Big)\times Q

the associated Whitney box of a cube
Q
. Finally, set

\displaystyle G\coloneqq\{\bar{Q}:Q\in\square\},

which is the collection of all Whitney boxes associated to
\square
. It forms a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. Lastly, we define the set

\displaystyle G(\bar{Q})\coloneqq\{\bar{R}\in G:\bar{R}\cap W(t,x)\neq\emptyset\ \text{for some}\ (t,x)\in\bar{Q}\},

which contains only finitely many elements due to
[
3
, Lemma 2.19]
. More precisely,the number of elements, denoted by
|G(\bar{Q})|
, is even bounded by a constant depending only on the dimension
d
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
there holds

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

with implicit constants depending only on
d,p,q,r,\beta
.

Proof.

The proof follows the ideas of
[
3
, Prop. 2.20]
. To simplify notation, we concentrate on the case
p,q,r<\infty
.

Step 1: We show
“
\lesssim
”
.

We decompose the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
and
\mathrm{L}^{p}
integrals dyadically to get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{q}

\displaystyle\simeq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\int\limits_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\int\limits_{Q}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Observe that for
Q\in\square_{k}
,
t\in[2^{k-1},2^{k})
and
x\in Q
we have
(t,x)\in\bar{Q}
. Therefore, according to the definition of
G(\bar{Q})
, we can cover
W(t,x)
by all Whitney boxes of
G(\bar{Q})
, to give

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\int\limits_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\int\limits_{Q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{\bar{R}\in G(\bar{Q})}\iint\limits_{\bar{R}}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\bigg(\sum\limits_{\bar{R}\in G(\bar{Q})}\iint\limits_{\bar{R}}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Since
|G(\bar{Q})|
is bounded by a dimensional constant, we can pull out the sum to get

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\sum\limits_{\bar{R}\in G(\bar{Q})}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Finally, for
\bar{R}\in G(\bar{Q})
it holds
\ell(R)\simeq\ell(Q)
. Indeed, for
Q\in\square_{k}
only dyadic cubes
R\in\square_{k-1}\cup\square_{k}
are suitable such that
\bar{R}\cap W(t,x)\neq\emptyset
for some
(t,x)\in\bar{Q}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\simeq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\sum\limits_{\bar{R}\in G(\bar{Q})}\ell(R)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Moreover, for a fixed
R\in\square_{k-1}\cup\square_{k}
the number of cubes
Q\in\square_{k}
with
\bar{R}\in G(\bar{Q})
is bounded by a dimensional constant. Thus,

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{k-1}\cup\square_{k}}\ell(R)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\ell(R)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}},

which proves one direction of our claim.

Step 2: We show
“
\gtrsim
”
.
Observe that, if
t\in(2^{k-1},2^{k})
,
R\in\Box_{k}
and
x\in R
, then
R\subset B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)
, and hence
\bar{R}\subset[\tfrac{t}{2},2t)\times B(x,ct)
, where
c\coloneqq 2\sqrt{d}
.
Now, estimate

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\ell(R)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\int\limits_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\int\limits_{R}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\int\limits_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\int\limits_{R}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\int\limits_{B(x,ct)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\int\limits_{B(x,ct)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Eventually, by a change of Whitney parameters (Proposition
3.7
) we can
conclude the reverse inequality.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Let
0<p,q,r<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Given a function
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, Proposition
3.12
shows that the decomposition

\displaystyle f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{Q}}f

converges in
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
.

Using the dyadic characterization, we derive the following embeddings, which can be seen as Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev-type embeddings, in analogy to the corresponding embeddings for homogeneous Besov spaces, see
[
26
, Theorem 2.7.1]
.

Theorem 3.14

(Weighted
\mathrm{Z}
-space embeddings)
.

Let
0<p_{0}\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<q_{0}\leq q_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=d(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p_{1}})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}.

Proof.

We use the dyadic characterization from Proposition
3.12
. Since
p_{0}\leq p_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta_{1}q_{1}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{1}}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{q_{1}}{p_{1}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta_{1}q_{1}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d\frac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{1}}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{q_{1}}{p_{0}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k(\beta_{1}+d(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}))q_{1}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{1}}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{q_{1}}{p_{0}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}},

where we used
\ell(Q)=2^{k}
in the last step. Since
q_{0}\leq q_{1}
,
r_{1}\leq r_{0}
and
\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}+d(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p_{1}})
, we get

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta_{0}q_{1}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{1}}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{q_{1}}{p_{0}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta_{0}q_{0}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{1}}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{q_{0}}{p_{0}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta_{0}q_{0}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{0}}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{q_{0}}{p_{0}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},{r_{0}}}_{\beta_{0}}}.

This proves the theorem.
∎

3.4.
Duality: full range

We use the dyadic description to prove a duality theorem in the quasi-Banach range. Recall the convention
p^{\prime}=\infty
for
p\in(0,1]
.

Theorem 3.16

(Duality – quasi-Banach range)
.

Let
1\leq r<\infty
,
0<p,q<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, define
\beta^{\prime}(p)\coloneqq-\beta+\max\{0,d(\frac{1}{p}-1)\}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime}(p)}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}

for any measurable functions
f,g
. Moreover, we can identify
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime}(p)}
with equivalent norms via the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
duality pairing.

Proof.

The case
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
is the Banach range case and was done in Theorem
3.11
. Hence, we focus on the other cases by dividing the proof into the following cases:

Case 1:
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<q<1
. Notice that
\beta^{\prime}(p)=-\beta
in this case. For
f
and
g
measurable,
we get with a similar argumentation as in Theorem
3.11
and Theorem
3.14
that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\ \frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}\cdot\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,1,r}_{\beta}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}\cdot\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

This shows the inclusion
\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
. To prove the reverse inclusion, we take
\Phi\in(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
. For
g\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta}
we define

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(g)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\Big(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g\Big).

Notice that this is well defined and bounded because we have, using the dyadic characterization and support properties of the cubes,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g\Big\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\Big\|\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\Big(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\|\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}\Big)^{p}\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\Big(2^{-m\beta q}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{m}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=2^{-m\beta}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{m}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta}}

on the one hand, and on the other hand, re-using the previous calculation,

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Phi}(g)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\Phi\Big(\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi\|\times\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g\Big\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi\|\times\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-m\beta}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{m}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\|\Phi\|\times\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta}}.

Thus, we conclude that
\tilde{\Phi}\in(\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
and that
\tilde{\Phi}=\Phi
on
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
due to Remark
3.13
. By Theorem
3.11
, there exists
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
such that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(s,y)\cdot\overline{g(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}=\tilde{\Phi}(g)=\Phi(g),\quad\text{for all $g\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}$.}

Hence, we can identify
\Phi
with
f
, which gives
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
.

Case 2:
0<p<1
and
0<q<\infty
.
Notice that
\beta^{\prime}(p)=-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)
in this case. By Theorem
3.14
, we have the continuous embedding

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{1,q,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}.

Thus, for
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
and
g\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, we have by Theorem
3.11
(if
q\geq 1
) or the previous case (if
q<1
) that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}1,q,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Hence,
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
. For the converse inclusion, we distinguish cases. First, assume
q\leq 1
and pick
\Phi\in(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
. For
g\in\mathrm{Z}^{1,q,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
, define

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(g)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\Phi(\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g).

Then, one has

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Phi}(g)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}|\Phi(\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi\|\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}\|\mathbf{1}_{\bar{R}}g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|\Phi\|\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}2^{-m\beta}\ell(R)^{\frac{d}{p}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}

\displaystyle\simeq\|\Phi\|\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{R\in\square_{m}}2^{-m(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1))}\ell(R)^{d}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi\|\cdot\|g\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{1,q,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}},

where we used
\ell^{q}\subset\ell^{1}
in the last step, which uses the assumption
q\leq 1
of the current case.
Thus,
\tilde{\Phi}\in(\mathrm{Z}^{1,q,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
due to the first case. Since
\tilde{\Phi}=\Phi
on
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, this means that we can identify
\Phi
with a
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
-function, which proves
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
.

Second, let
1<q<\infty
. We have
\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, therefore

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}.

For some
\Phi\in(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
, let
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
be such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Phi(g)=\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}f(s,y)\cdot\overline{g(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s},\quad\text{for all $g\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta}$},

and

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\sup\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1))k}\sup\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}

\displaystyle=\|\Phi\|

if
r>1
, or

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1))k}\sup\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\bar{R})}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}=\|\Phi\|

in the case
r=1
. For simplicity, we continue the argument in the case
r>1
. The other case can be treated analogously using (
3.4
) instead of (
3.4
).
Using the estimate (
3.4
), one can find for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
a cube
R_{k}\in\square_{k}
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle 2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\sup\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}

\displaystyle\simeq 2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}.

As a consequence of the Hahn-Banach theorem and the duality of
\mathrm{L}^{r}
-spaces, it is possible to find for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
a function
\varphi_{k}\in\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
with the properties that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\leq 2\langle f,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}},\mathrm{L}^{r}}.

Note that we implicitly assume
\langle f,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}},\mathrm{L}^{r}}
to be real.
Since all
\bar{R}_{k}
are disjoint, we can define a global function
\varphi\in\mathrm{L}^{r}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
with
\varphi=\varphi_{k}
on
\bar{R}_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\leq 2\langle f,\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}},\mathrm{L}^{r}}\quad\text{for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

Let
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be an arbitrary real, finite sequence, say supported on
\{0,\dots,N\}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. By the properties of
\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}a_{k}2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\langle f,\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}},\mathrm{L}^{r}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\langle f,\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}},\mathrm{L}^{r}}

Because the test function is a compactly supported
\mathrm{L}^{r}
-function, it belongs to
\mathrm{Z}^{p,1,r}_{\beta}
by Lemma
3.8
. Thus, (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\langle f,\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}},\mathrm{L}^{r}}

\displaystyle=\Phi\Big(\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi\|\times\bigg\|\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Finally, keeping the location of
\bar{R}_{k}
in mind, we compute

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-m\beta q}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{m}}\ell(Q)^{d}\Big(\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\|\varphi{\mathbf{1}}_{\bar{R}_{k}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}\Big)^{p}\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}2^{-k\beta q}\ell(R_{k})^{\frac{dq}{p}}|a_{k}|^{q}2^{(\beta-\frac{d}{p})qk}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Going back to (
3.4
), we hence have

\displaystyle\Big|\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}a_{k}2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\Big|\lesssim\|\Phi\|\times\Big(\sum\limits_{|k|\leq N}|a_{k}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the implicit constant is independent of
f
,
N
and
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. This shows that the sequence of real numbers

\displaystyle b_{k}=2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}))k}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

belongs to
\ell^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z})
with norm bound

\displaystyle\|b_{k}\|_{\ell^{q^{\prime}}}=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a_{k})\in\ell^{q}\ \text{finite}\\
\|a_{k}\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}b_{k}\Big|\lesssim\|\Phi\|.

Finally, combining this with (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1))kq^{\prime}}\sup\limits_{R\in\square_{k}}\|f\|^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1))kq^{\prime}}\|f\|^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{R}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Phi\|,

which shows
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
.
∎

4.
Real interpolation of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces

In this section, we give a complete picture of the real interpolation theory of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
spaces. Before we dive into our statements, we give a brief introduction to real interpolation theory, thereby also introducing relevant notation. For more background, we refer to the monograph
[
8
, Chp. 3]
for a detailed excursion.

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple, that is, a pair of (quasi-)Banach spaces
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
that are continuous embedded into a Hausdorff topological vector space
Y
. Then we define the (quasi-)Banach space
X_{0}+X_{1}=\{x_{0}+x_{1}\in Y:x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\}
equipped with the (quasi-)norm

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}\coloneqq\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\\
x=x_{0}+x_{1}\end{subarray}}\|x_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}.

For every
x\in X_{0}+X_{1}
and
t>0
we define the
K
-functional

\displaystyle K(x,t,X_{0},X_{1})\coloneqq\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\\
x=x_{0}+x_{1}\end{subarray}}\|x_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+t\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}.

Since we will often work with
q
-powers of this functional in the following, we introduce the more readable abbreviation

\displaystyle K^{q}(x,t,X_{0},X_{1})\coloneqq\big(K(x,t,X_{0},X_{1})\big)^{q}.

Finally, for
0<\theta<1
and
0<q\leq\infty
, we define the real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,q}
as the set of all
x\in X_{0}+X_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\|x\|_{\theta,q}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\theta q}K^{q}(x,t,X_{0},X_{1})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

It is known that
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,q}
is a (quasi-)Banach space. Furthermore, splitting the integral dyadically and using monotonicity in
t
of the
K
-functional, one calculates

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|x\|_{\theta,q}\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k\alpha\theta q}K^{q}(x,2^{-k\alpha},X_{0},X_{1})\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for every
\alpha>0
, where the implicit constant depends only on
\alpha,\theta,q
.

Now that we have set the notation, we will begin with our first result. Its proof is similar to that of
[
26
, Thm. 2.4.2]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Real interpolation of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces I)
.

Let
0<p,q,q_{0},q_{1},r\leq\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\beta_{0}\neq\beta_{1}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}=\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}

where
\beta=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}
.

We stress that
q
is not related to
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
but arbitrary.

Proof.

To simplify notation, we present the proof only for finite exponents. Moreover, Lebesgue norms over a Whitney box are always with respect to the measure
\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}
without explicitly mentioning this. The case of infinite exponents follows with the usual modification. Moreover, we assume without loss of generality that
\beta_{0}>\beta_{1}
. In the proof, we use without mentioning that the
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
(quasi-)norm is equivalent to the dyadic description given in Proposition
3.12
. We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1 : We show
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
.

Denote by
\|\cdot\|^{q}_{\theta,q}
the norm of the interpolation space
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}
. Since
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{0}
, we use (
4.1
) with
\alpha=\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}
to get

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{\theta,q}\simeq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})\theta q}K^{q}(f,2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}}).

Now, let
f_{0}\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}
and
f_{1}\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}}
be such that
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
. Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle 2^{-k\beta_{0}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k\beta_{0}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}+2^{-k\beta_{0}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-m\beta_{0}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{m}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad+2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})}\sup\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-m\beta_{1}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{m}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}}+2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}}}.

Since this holds for all decompositions of
f
in these spaces, we get that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{\theta,q}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})\theta q}K^{q}(f,2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})\theta q}2^{-k\beta_{0}q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{q},

where we used
\theta(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})=\beta_{0}-\beta
in the penultimate step.

Step 2:
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}
for
0<\tilde{q}<q
.

Now, denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,q}
the (quasi-)norm of the interpolation space
(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}
. Take
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and define

\displaystyle f_{0}(s,y)=\mathbf{1}_{[2^{k},\infty)}(s)f(s,y)\quad\text{and}\quad f_{1}(s,y)=\mathbf{1}_{(0,2^{k})}(s)f(s,y).

Then,
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
. To bound
\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{0}}}
, expand

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|^{\tilde{q}}_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{0}}}\simeq\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-n\beta_{0}\tilde{q}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{n}}\ell(Q)^{d}\big\|\mathbf{1}_{[2^{k},\infty)}f\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{\tilde{q}}{p}}.

We only have to consider
n\geq k+1
in the outer sum, since otherwise the support of the integrand in the
\mathrm{L}^{r}
-norm is disjoint to
\bar{Q}
. Moreover, define

(4.3)

\displaystyle\xi_{n}\coloneqq\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{n}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|^{\tilde{q}}_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{0}}}\simeq\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta_{0}\tilde{q}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{n}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{\tilde{q}}{p}}=\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta_{0}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}.

Analogously, we get

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|^{\tilde{q}}_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{1}}}\simeq\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\beta_{1}\tilde{q}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{n}}\ell(Q)^{d}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{\tilde{q}}{p}}=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\beta_{1}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}.

Observe that the right-hand sides of the last two formulas are independent of the decomposition of
f
. Hence, taking the infimum over all decompositions of
f
yields

\displaystyle K^{q}(f,2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{1}})\lesssim\bigg(\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta_{0}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}+2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\beta_{1}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}\bigg)^{q}

for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, we have by (
4.1
) that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{\theta,q}\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})\theta q}\bigg(\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta_{0}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}+2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\beta_{1}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}\bigg)^{q}.

Now, pick
\beta_{0}>\alpha_{0}>\beta>\alpha_{1}>\beta_{1}
and
\sigma>0
such that
\frac{\tilde{q}}{\sigma}+\frac{\tilde{q}}{q}=1
. Then, with Hölder’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta_{0}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}

\displaystyle=\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})\tilde{q}}\cdot 2^{-n\alpha_{0}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})\sigma}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\cdot\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\alpha_{0}q}\xi^{q}_{n}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\alpha_{0}q}\xi^{q}_{n}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}},

as well as

\displaystyle\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\beta_{1}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}

\displaystyle=\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})\tilde{q}}\cdot 2^{-n\alpha_{1}\tilde{q}}\xi_{n}^{\tilde{q}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})\sigma}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\cdot\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\alpha_{1}q}\xi_{n}^{q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\alpha_{1}q}\xi_{n}^{q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Plug both bounds back into (
4.4
). Using
\beta_{0}-\beta=\theta(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})
, we get

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{\theta,q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})\theta q}\bigg(2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\alpha_{0}q}\xi^{q}_{n}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}+2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\beta_{1})}2^{-k(\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1})}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\alpha_{1}q}\xi_{n}^{q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k(\beta_{0}-\beta)q}\bigg(2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})q}\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\alpha_{0}q}\xi^{q}_{n}+2^{-k(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{1})q}\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\alpha_{1}q}\xi_{n}^{q}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k(\beta-\alpha_{0})q}\sum\limits_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-n\alpha_{0}q}\xi^{q}_{n}+\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k(\beta-\alpha_{1})q}\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{-n\alpha_{1}q}\xi_{n}^{q}.

Finally, with Fubini’s theorem we get

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{n-1}2^{-k(\beta-\alpha_{0})q}\Big)2^{-n\alpha_{0}q}\xi^{q}_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}2^{-k(\beta-\alpha_{1})q}\Big)2^{-n\alpha_{1}q}\xi_{n}^{q}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-n(\beta-\alpha_{0})q}\cdot 2^{-n\alpha_{0}q}\xi^{q}_{n}+\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-n(\beta-\alpha_{1})q}\cdot 2^{-n\alpha_{1}q}\xi_{n}^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-n\beta q}\xi^{q}_{n}

\displaystyle\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{q},

where we used the definition of
\xi_{n}
in (
4.3
). This proves the second step.

Step 3: Conclusion.

Take
0<\tilde{q}<\min\{q,q_{1},q_{2}\}
and use twice the embedding of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces (Theorem
3.14
), as well as the previous steps, to conclude

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\tilde{q},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}

\displaystyle\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}

\displaystyle\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta},

which proves the claim.
∎

This interpolation result is for fixed parameter
p
. If we want to interpolate the parameter
p
, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.2

(Real interpolation of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces II)
.

Let
0<p_{0},q_{0},p_{1},q_{1},r\leq\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,p}=\mathrm{Z}^{p,p,r}_{\beta},

where
\beta=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}
.

Proof.

We use the dyadic description of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
(Proposition
3.12
) to reduce matters to real interpolation of vector-valued sequence spaces. We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1: Embed
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
into vector-valued sequence space.

Fix the Whitney box
\bar{Q}_{0}=(1/2,1)\times Q_{0}
associated to the unit cube
Q_{0}=[0,1)^{d}
. Let
\bar{Q}\in G
with
Q=2^{k}x+[0,2^{k})^{d}
for some
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, for a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, we define the affine reparamerization

\displaystyle f_{k,x}(s,y)\coloneqq f(2^{k}s,2^{k}y+2^{k}x).

By Proposition
3.12
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta q}\Big(\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|2^{\frac{kd}{p}}f_{k,x}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q}_{0};\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\big\|2^{\frac{kd}{p}}f_{k,x}\big\|_{\ell^{q}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d};\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q}_{0};\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}})))}.

In the following, we use the shorter notation

\displaystyle\ell^{q}_{\beta}\ell^{p}\mathrm{L}^{r}\coloneqq\ell^{q}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d};\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q}_{0};\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}))).

Now, we define (formally) the map

(4.6)

\displaystyle I:f(s,y)\mapsto 2^{\frac{kd}{p}}f_{k,x}(s,y).

By (
4.5
), we find that
I
restricts to a well-defined bounded map
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\to\ell^{q}_{\beta}\ell^{p}\mathrm{L}^{r}
satisfying even
\|I(f)\|_{\ell^{q}_{\beta}\ell^{p}\mathrm{L}^{r}}\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}
.
Next, we construct an inverse map to
I
. To this end, we take some arbitrary but fixed sequence

\displaystyle g=((g_{k,x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

of measurable functions on
\bar{Q}_{0}
, and define the function

\displaystyle f(s,y)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-\frac{dk}{p}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{Q}_{0}}(2^{-k}s,2^{-k}y-x)\,g_{k,x}(2^{-k}s,2^{-k}y-x).

Note that owing to the indicator function, convergence of the sums is trivial.
As a limit of measurable functions,
f
is again measurable. Moreover, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, one has for
(s,y)\in\bar{Q}_{0}
that

\displaystyle f_{m,z}(s,y)

\displaystyle=f(2^{m}s,2^{m}(y+z))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-\frac{dk}{p}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{Q}_{0}}(2^{m-k}s,2^{m-k}(y+z)-x)\,g_{k,x}(2^{m-k}s,2^{m-k}(y+z)-x)

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{dm}{p}}g_{m,z}(s,y),

by an elementary geometric consideration.
Hence, by setting

I^{-1}\bigl[((g_{k,x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigr]\coloneqq f,

we have constructed an operator that is well-defined on all sequence spaces of measurable functions and satisfies
I^{-1}\colon\ell^{q}_{\beta}\ell^{p}\mathrm{L}^{r}\to\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
as well as
I\circ I^{-1}=\Id=I^{-1}\circ I
.

Step 2: Conclusion.

By Step 1, we know that
I
and
I^{-1}
are morphisms between the interpolation couples
(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})
and
(\ell^{q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\ell^{p_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{r},\ell^{q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\ell^{p_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{r})
which restrict to bounded maps
\mathrm{Z}^{p_{i},q_{i},r}_{\beta_{i}}\to\ell^{q_{i}}_{\beta_{i}}\ell^{p_{i}}\mathrm{L}^{r}
and
\ell^{q_{i}}_{\beta_{i}}\ell^{p_{i}}\mathrm{L}^{r}\to\mathrm{Z}^{p_{i},q_{i},r}_{\beta_{i}}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
, respectively. Hence,
(\theta,p)
-real interpolation yields

(4.7)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\colon(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,p}\to(\ell^{q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\ell^{p_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{r},\ell^{q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\ell^{p_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{r})_{\theta,p},

(4.8)

\displaystyle I^{-1}

\displaystyle\colon(\ell^{q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\ell^{p_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{r},\ell^{q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\ell^{p_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{r})_{\theta,p}\to(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,p}.

Since
I\circ I^{-1}=\Id=I^{-1}\circ I
, this shows the ismorphism property

(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,p}\simeq(\ell^{q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\ell^{p_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{r},\ell^{q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\ell^{p_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{r})_{\theta,p}.

We claim that we can compute the space on the right-hand side of this identity as

(4.9)

\displaystyle(\ell^{q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\ell^{p_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{r},\ell^{q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\ell^{p_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{r})_{\theta,p}=\ell^{p}_{\beta}\ell^{p}\mathrm{L}^{r}.

Admitting this, the proof is complete, as
I^{-1}
again identifies
\ell^{p}_{\beta}\ell^{p}\mathrm{L}^{r}
with
\mathrm{Z}^{p,p,r}
.

Finally, to prove (
4.9
), we apply Peetre’s interpolation result
[
23
, Chp. 5, Thm. 4, p. 98]
twice, to get

\displaystyle(\ell^{q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\ell^{p_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{r},\ell^{q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\ell^{p_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{r})_{\theta,p}=\ell^{p}_{\beta}\big((\ell^{p_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{r},\ell^{p_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{r})_{\theta,p}\big)=\ell^{p}_{\beta}\big(\ell^{p}\big((\mathrm{L}^{r},\mathrm{L}^{r})_{\theta,p}\big)\big)=\ell^{p}_{\beta}\ell^{p}\mathrm{L}^{r},

where all parameters are as in the claim.

∎

Lastly, we want to show that our
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces can be derived from real interpolation of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces, in the same way as Besov spaces can be derived from Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, see
[
26
, Thm. 2.4.2]
. Recall that we did not define the scale of spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
for
p=\infty
. In particular, we will not provide a characterization of
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
by means of real interpolation of tent spaces here. We are going to investigate this question in future work.

The characterization of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces by means of tent spaces relies on the following nesting property.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
0<p<\infty
and
0<q,r\leq\infty
we have the continuous embeddings

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q\},r}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p,\max\{p,q\},r}_{\beta}.

Proof.

We only showcase the first inclusion, the second one follows by a similar calculation. For notational simplicity, we only treat the case
q,r
finite. Set
\alpha=\min\{p,q\}
and fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using the embedding
\ell^{\alpha}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
and removing/introducing averaging integrals, we get

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k+1}}\fint\limits_{B(x,2^{k+1})}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k+1}}\fint\limits_{B(x,2^{k+1})}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k+1}}\fint\limits_{B(x,2^{k+1})}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{4}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{4}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Thus, applying the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-norm in
x
on both sides of the upper inequality and using Minkowski’s integral (using
\alpha\leq p
) inequality yields

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{4}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\alpha}_{t}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\vphantom{t}x}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{4}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\vphantom{t}x}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\alpha}_{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,\alpha,r}_{\beta}},

where we used a change of Whitney parameter in the last inequality, see Proposition
3.7
.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Real interpolation of tent spaces)
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
0<q_{0},q_{1},q,r\leq\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\beta_{0}\neq\beta_{1}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}=\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta},

where
\beta=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}
.

Proof.

By the previous lemma, we have the embedding

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q_{0}\},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q_{1}\},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}\subset(\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\max\{p,q_{0}\},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\max\{p,q_{1}\},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}.

Hence, calculating the interpolation spaces on the left- and right-hand inclusions by Theorem
4.1
, it follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\subset(\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta},

which proves the claim.
∎

5.
Complex interpolation of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces

The goal of this section is to show that the
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces form a complex interpolation scale. To do so, we use a refinement of the complex interpolation method for quasi-Banach spaces of Kalton–Mitrea
[
20
]
due to Egert and Kosmala
[
13
]
. If both of the underlying spaces are even Banach spaces, then this method coincide with Calderón’s complex method
[
11
]
(see also
[
8
, Chp. 4]
). Because a precise construction of complex interpolation spaces is not necessary to understand this section, we use them as a blackbox and refer to the aforementioned references for details instead. Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 5.1

(Complex interpolation)
.

Let
0<p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
be such that
\max\{q_{0},p_{0},r_{0}\}<\infty
or
\max\{q_{1},p_{1},r_{1}\}<\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Define

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}},\quad\beta_{\theta}=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}=\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}

with equivalent norms.

Before proving this result in the full quasi-Banach range, we start with the corresponding result in the Banach range, which is based on the vector-valued embedding (Lemma
3.10
) and duality (Theorem
3.11
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
1<p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
be such that
\max\{q_{0},p_{0},r_{0}\}<\infty
or
\max\{q_{1},p_{1},r_{1}\}<\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Define

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}},\quad\beta_{\theta}=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}.

Then we have

\displaystyle[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}=\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

We show both inclusions separately.

Step 1:
[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}
.

We use the embedding
i
of Lemma
3.10
and
[
18
, Thm. 14.3.1]
to get boundedness of

\displaystyle i:[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}\to[\mathrm{L}^{q_{0}}_{t;\beta_{0}}\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{0}}x}\mathrm{L}^{r_{0}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{0}}s,y},\mathrm{L}^{q_{1}}_{t;\beta_{1}}\mathrm{L}^{p_{1}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{0}}x}\mathrm{L}^{r_{1}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{0}}s,y}]_{\theta}\simeq\mathrm{L}^{q_{\theta}}_{t;\beta_{\theta}}\mathrm{L}^{p_{\theta}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{\theta}}x}\mathrm{L}^{r_{\theta}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{\theta}}s,y}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|i(f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q_{\theta}}_{t;\beta_{\theta}}\mathrm{L}^{p_{\theta}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{\theta}}x}\mathrm{L}^{r_{\theta}}_{\vphantom{t;\beta_{\theta}}s,y}}\lesssim\|f\|_{[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}}.

Step 2:
\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}\subset[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}
.

This inclusion can be obtained from Step 1 by virtue of a duality argument. First, using Theorem
3.11
and dualizing the inclusion of Step 1, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}=(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta}^{\prime},q_{\theta}^{\prime},r_{\theta}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{\theta}})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\subset([\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{1}}]_{\theta})^{\prime},

where we used that
p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}
are finite by construction. Second, to calculate the interpolation space on the right-hand side of the last inclusion,
we observe that at least one of the spaces
\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{0}}
or
\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{1}}
is reflexive by Theorem
3.11
(keep in mind that
p_{i},q_{i},r_{i}>1
in both cases). Moreover, their intersection is dense in both spaces due to Proposition
3.9
and
p_{i},q_{i},r_{i}>1
. Thus, we can apply
[
8
, Cor. 4.5.2]
to get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}\subset([\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{1}}]_{\theta})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=[(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{0}})^{\prime},(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{1}})^{\prime}]_{\theta}.

Third, still using that
p_{i}^{\prime},q_{i}^{\prime},r_{i}^{\prime}
are finite because
p_{i},q_{i},r_{i}>1
, Theorem
3.11
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}\subset[(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{0}})^{\prime},(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{1}})^{\prime}]_{\theta}=[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta},

which proves the claim.
∎

To get an interpolation result also in the quasi-Banach setting, we use on the one hand an abstract result to identify the interpolation spaces with Calderón products, and on the other hand convexity arguments to reduce matters to interpolation in the Banach setting. More precisely, we use the following abstract result by Egert and Kosmala
[
13
, Thm. 1.1]
, which only needs one of the two interpolants to be separable. This way, endpoint spaces can be treated as well.

We state the result first and clarify the notions appearing in it right after.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1}
be
A
-convex quasi-Banach function spaces over a separable and
\sigma
-finite measure space
\Omega
, one of which is separable. Then,
X_{0}+X_{1}
is
A
-convex, and for every
\theta\in(0,1)
one has

\displaystyle[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}=(X_{0})^{1-\theta}(X_{1})^{\theta}

up to equivalent norms, where
(X_{0})^{1-\theta}(X_{1})^{\theta}
is called the Calderón product of
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
. It consists of all
f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(X_{0})^{1-\theta}(X_{1})^{\theta}}\coloneqq\inf\Big\{\|g\|_{X_{0}}^{1-\theta}\|h\|_{X_{1}}^{\theta}:|f|\leq|g|^{1-\theta}|h|^{\theta},\,g\in X_{0},h\in X_{1}\Big\}<\infty.

Recall that a quasi-Banach space
X\subset\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
is called a quasi-Banach function space, if

(1)

there exists
f\in X
such that
f>0
almost everywhere,

(2)

f\in X
and
g\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
with
|g|\leq|f|
almost everywhere implies
g\in X
.

It is known that a quasi-Banach function space
X
is
A
-convex if and only if it is
lattice
\alpha
-convex
for some
\alpha>0
(see
[
19
, Thm. 7.8]
), where the later means that

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{\alpha}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big\|_{X}\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|^{\alpha}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

for all finite families
(f_{i})_{i=1,\dots,m}\subset X
. Moreover, this property implies that

\displaystyle[X]^{\alpha}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega):|f|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\in X\}\quad\text{equipped with}\quad\|f\|_{[X]^{\alpha}}\coloneqq\||f|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\|_{X}^{\alpha}

is a Banach function space as well, called the
\alpha
-convexification
of
X
, see
[
21
, Prop. 3.23]
. Furthermore, by a straightforward computation, one can show that the Calderón product commutes with the process of convexification. More precisely, for two lattice
\alpha
-convex quasi-Banach function spaces
X_{0},X_{1}
we have for all
\theta\in(0,1)
that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\big[(X_{0})^{1-\theta}(X_{1})^{\theta}\big]^{\alpha}=\big([X_{0}]^{\alpha}\big)^{1-\theta}\big([X_{1}]^{\alpha}\big)^{\theta}.

Now, we check that our
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces fulfill all necessary properties so that the just explained machinery applies to them.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the space
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
is a quasi-Banach function space,
\alpha
-convex for all
0<\alpha<\min\{p,q,r\}
and
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}=[\mathrm{Z}^{p/\alpha,q/\alpha,r/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta}]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. Furthermore, the space
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
is separable if
\max\{p,q,r\}<\infty
.

Proof.

The facts that
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
is a quasi-Banach function space and that
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}=[\mathrm{Z}^{p/\alpha,q/\alpha,r/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta}]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
holds are clear. If
\max\{p,q,r\}<\infty
, then separability follows by Lemma
3.10
in conjunction with separability of weighted vector-valued Lebesgue spaces. Finally, for
0<\alpha<\min\{p,q,r\}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{\alpha}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}\Big|s^{-\beta}\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{\alpha}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}s^{-\alpha\beta}|f_{i}|^{\alpha}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}}^{\frac{q}{\alpha}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\||f_{i}|^{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p/\alpha,q/\alpha,r/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{\alpha}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

which proves the claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Set
0<\alpha<\min\{p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\}
. Then, owing to Lemma
5.4
, we get by Proposition
5.3
that

\displaystyle[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}=(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}})^{1-\theta}(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}})^{\theta}.

Using property (
5.1
), we get again with Lemma
5.4
that

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}})^{1-\theta}(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}})^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\Big(\big[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}/\alpha,q_{0}/\alpha,r_{0}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{0}}\big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big)^{1-\theta}\Big(\big[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}/\alpha,q_{1}/\alpha,r_{1}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{1}}\big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big)^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\Big[\big(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}/\alpha,q_{0}/\alpha,r_{0}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{0}}\big)^{1-\theta}\big(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}/\alpha,q_{1}/\alpha,r_{1}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{1}}\big)^{\theta}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

We make the crucial observation
\tfrac{p_{i}}{\alpha},\tfrac{q_{i}}{\alpha},\tfrac{r_{i}}{\alpha}>1
. Thus, by Proposition
5.3
followed by Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.4
,
we get

\displaystyle\Big[\big(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}/\alpha,q_{0}/\alpha,r_{0}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{0}}\big)^{1-\theta}\big(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}/\alpha,q_{1}/\alpha,r_{1}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{1}}\big)^{\theta}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\Big[\big[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0}/\alpha,q_{0}/\alpha,r_{0}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1}/\alpha,q_{1}/\alpha,r_{1}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{1}}\big]_{\theta}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\Big[\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta}/\alpha,q_{\theta}/\alpha,r_{\theta}/\alpha}_{\alpha\beta_{\theta}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Z}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}.

Concatenating the three established identities proves the claim.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

We would like to point out that the theory of strong factorization provides an alternative approach to complex interpolation due to its connection to Calderón products. For example, see
[
17
]
in the case of weighted tent spaces with Whitney averages
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
. However, it is unclear whether such factorizations hold for
Z^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces. This is why we took a different approach to complex interpolation theory, following the method in
[
19
]
.

Remark 5.6
.

Another important feature of tent spaces is the atomic theory. We have not developed this concept for
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces. However, a good substitute is the decomposition observed in Remark
3.13
and valid for all parameters
p,q,r
and
\beta
.

Efficient utilization of imaginarity in quantum steering
Shounak Datta, A. S. Majumdar

Abstract
We illustrate the role of complex numbers in quantum information processing through the phenomenon of quantum steering. Exploiting partial knowledge of a qubit in terms of imaginarity, we formulate a steering criterion for bipartite qubit systems, which requires two dichotomic measurements at the untrusted side and two mutually unbiased bases at the trusted side. We show that quantum correlations embodied through our proposed imaginarity steering inequality can be witnessed by suitably constructed Hermitian operators that depend on fewer state parameters and require measurement of a lesser number of observables than for witnessing other forms of quantum nonlocal correlations. The monogamy of such correlations is also demonstrated. We further underscore the steering of imaginarity as an efficient nonlocal resource in the presence of white noise and under unhsarp measurements, showing the robustness of imaginarity steering compared to certain other steering criteria.

Introduction

Real numbers are crucial for explaining most of the features in our classical world. Further, real vector space quantum theory has been successfully applied to investigate entanglement between real qubits(rebits)
(
Batle et al. 2002
;
Wootters 2012
;
Wootters 2014
;
Prasannan et al. 2021
)
and applied to some information processing tasks
(
McKague et al. 2009
;
Koh et al. 2018
)
. However, quantum mechanics describes the state of a quantum system by wave functions having the form
\Psi=ae^{i\Phi}
where complex numbers play a key role in many aspects. If a physical theory is constructed with the rules of quantum mechanics although restricted to real parameters in terms of states and operations
(
Araki 1980
;
Hardy and Wootters 2012
;
Baez 2012
;
Aleksandrova et al. 2013
;
Wootters 2015
)
, it fails to explain certain feasible properties
(
Liu et al. 2019
;
Zhu 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2021
)
.

The importance of imaginary numbers in quantum theory was first proposed by Hickey et al
(
Hickey and Gour 2018
)
from the perspective of general quantum resource theory. The operational resource theory of imaginarity has been put forward by Wu et al
(
Wu et al. 2021a
)
, where the importance of imaginarity in experimental scenario and in solving the local state discrimination problem has been discussed. Imaginarity can be quantified and applied to state conversion problems
(
Wu et al. 2021b
)
, and the measures, such as, geometric imaginarity and robustness of imaginarity turn out to be useful in linear optical set-ups. Resource theory of imaginarity has been extended in the distributed scenario
(
Wu et al. 2024
)
. Based on the resource theory, it has been shown that real operations can broadcast imaginarity like other quantum mechanical resources
(
Zhang et al. 2024
)
. Imaginarity is also useful for distinguishing real quantum states which can be used to hide or mask quantum information
(
Zhu 2021
)
. Further, real and complex quantum theories can be discriminated
(
Batle et al. 2025
)
by employing imaginarity in nonlocality experiments to show the supremacy of complex theories over the real world-view.

Imaginarity is manifested through density matrix representation of a quantum system in particular basis. Since mutually orthogonal bases are critical in the development of quantum nonlocal theory, it is interesting to investigate the role of imaginarity in quantum nonlocality. Historically, the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen(EPR) paradox
(
Einstein et al. 1935
)
and Bell’s inequality
(
Bell 1964
;
Clauser et al. 1969
)
, first revealed the nonlocal nature of correlations in quantum mechanics for entangled pairs. Schrödinger’s interpretation
(
Schrödinger 1935
)
introduced a new kind of nonlocal phenomenon, called quantum steering. There exists a necessary and sufficient condition for steering in 2-2-2 scenario
(
Cavalcanti et al. 2015
)
. Steerability is an intermediate form of quantum correlation lying between Bell nonlocality and entanglement
(
Wiseman et al. 2007
;
Jones et al. 2007
)
. Quantum steering has emerged as a powerful resource in many different information processing tasks such as
semi-device-independent self-testing of quantum states and devices
(
Šupić and Bowles 2020
;
Bian et al. 2020
)
.

The formulation of steering through inequalities is based on the uncertainty between mutually orthogonal basis in different forms
(
Uola et al. 2020
)
. The expression of steering inequalities
(
Reid 1989
;
Schneeloch et al. 2013
;
Pramanik et al. 2014a
;
Maity et al. 2017
)
through various uncertainty relations, such as Heisenberg uncertainty relation
(
Heisenberg 1927
)
, entropic uncertainty relation
(
Maassen and Uffink 1988
)
, fine-grained uncertainty relation
(
Oppenheim and Wehner 2010
)
, and sum uncertainty relation
(
Pati and Sahu 2007
)
, requires complete description of the local density matrix. On the other hand, there exist certain quantum mechanical correlations based on complementarity in mutually orthogonal basis, which can be revealed using partial knowledge of the density matrix
(
Mondal et al. 2017
;
Wei and Fei 2024
)
. The motivation for the present work is to extend such an approach by utilizing the resource of imaginarity for formulating an efficient criterion for quantum steering, that does not require full density matrix information, and is hence, easier to implement in practice.

To this end, here we derive an imaginarity steering inequality based on partial information of the density matrix, and propose the following protocol for quantum steering, which can be easily realized with minimal experimental resources. Consider that, Alice prepares a bipartite two-qubit state and shares the second particle with Bob. Alice performs a measurement on her particle in order to convince Bob that the joint state is entangled. Next, Bob determines the imaginarity of his local qubit and checks our steering criterion of imaginarity, through the measurement statistics of two dichotomic measurements at Alice’s end and two mutually orthogonal basis at Bob’s end. If our imaginarity criterion is violated, Bob becomes convinced that Alice has not cheated him and he does not possess a local hidden state (LHS) of a qubit. In essence, the control of Alice over Bob’s local imaginarity can be considered as a sufficient criterion to detect nonlocality.

In this work, we apply the robustness of imaginarity as a quantifier of imaginarity
(
Hickey and Gour 2018
;
Wu et al. 2021b
)
. We construct a complementarity relation of imaginarity involving two mutually unbiased bases in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

(
Bengtsson 2007
)
. Subsequently, we construct our 2-measurement imaginarity steering inequality (ISI) for bipartite qubit states. We show that the untrusted side of an entangled pair of qubits can steer the quantum imaginarity of the trusted side when ISI is violated. We next show that the set of states satisfying ISI is convex and compact, enabling construction of a witness operator to detect the steering of imaginarity. The steerability of imaginarity is exemplified through well-known families of bipartite qubit states
(
Werner 1989
;
Rau 2009
)
. Moreover, by introducing a general tripartite pure state
(
Acín et al. 2000
)
, we show that a strong monogamy trade-off
(
Dhar et al. 2017
)
exists between its bipartite cuts in terms of ISI. This result implies that two parties can not simultaneously steer the imaginarity of a third party.

Further, we demonstrate the efficacy of our imaginarity steering relation in comparison with similar other steering criteria which are based on partial knowledge of the state. Specifically, we show that the 2-measurement set-up imaginarity steering inequality proposed in our work is more robust in revealing quantum steering compared to the formalisms based on 3-measurement set-ups
(
Mondal et al. 2017
;
Wei and Fei 2024
)
. The imaginarity steering criterion is able to tolerate more noise, and is also more resilient if unsharpness of measurements is introduced. Our analysis shows that the manifestation of ISI not only requires lesser experimental resources in order to determine a fewer number of state parameters, but is also more robust against various noise effects.

V

VI
. Finally, we conclude with a summary in Section
VII
.

II
Preliminaries of quantum steering

Suppose that Alice prepares a joint state and keeps one particle with her and sends the other to Bob. Now, the task of steering Bobs’s system by Alice is to convince Bob that the shared state is entangled. Depending upon Alice’s measurement on her particle, if the measurement statistics at Bob’s side can not be determined from a pre-fixed cheating strategy considered by Alice, then Bob is convinced that the shared state is entangled and his state can be steered or controlled by Alice. The cheating strategy may come from a Local Hidden State(LHS) model
(
Wiseman et al. 2007
;
Jones et al. 2007
)
given by

\sigma_{a|A}=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}

(1)

where the distribution of local hidden variable
\lambda
satisfies
\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)=1
and the probability of getting outcome
a
from measurement
A
for a given
\lambda
at Alice’s side is
p(a|A,\lambda)
. The unnormalised conditional state at Bob’s side can be written as,
\sigma_{a|A}=p(a|A)\penalty\ \rho_{a|A}
, where
p(a|A)
is the probability of getting outcome
a
corresponding to Alice’s measurement
A
and
\rho_{a|A}
is the normalised conditional state at Bob’s side. The LHS corresponding to Bob is given by
\rho_{\lambda}
. As the system of Bob is assumed to be quantum, the joint probability distribution of getting outcomes
\{a,b\}
from measurements
\{A,B\}
corresponding to Alice and Bob respectively, is governed by the Born rule, as follows.

p(a,b|A,B)=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)\penalty\ p(b|B,\rho_{\lambda})

(2)

where
p(b|B,\rho_{\lambda})=\operatorname{Tr}[\Pi_{b|B}\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}]
and
p(a,b|A,B)=\operatorname{Tr}[\Pi_{b|B}\penalty\ \sigma_{a|A}]
and
\Pi_{b|B}
is the projective measurement operator which yields outcome
b
from measurement
B
at Bob’s side.

The non-existence of the LHS model through the violation of Eq.(
2
) is demonstrated through the violation of various steering inequalities. There exists a necessary and sufficient condition for steering of quantum states in the 2-2-2 scenario
(
Cavalcanti et al. 2015
)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\sqrt{\langle(A_{1}+A_{2})B_{1}\rangle^{2}+\langle(A_{1}+A_{2})B_{2}\rangle^{2}}\penalty\ +

\displaystyle\sqrt{\langle(A_{1}-A_{2})B_{1}\rangle^{2}+\langle(A_{1}-A_{2})B_{2}\rangle^{2}}\leq 2

(3)

where the dichotomic observables
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
correspond to Alice and the mutually unbiased observables
\{B_{1},B_{2}\}
correspond to Bob. The nonlinear inequality(
3
) is known as analogous Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt(CHSH) inequality for steering or CFFW inequality.

III
Steering of quantum imaginarity

The mathematical structure of quantum mechanics mainly depends on complex numbers, but the verifiable quantities through physical operations are predominantly real. The role of the imaginary part of the elements of a density matrix lead to interesting consequences in quantum information theory. The resource theoretic framework of imaginarity has been developed and imaginarity quantified as a convex monotone
(
Wu et al. 2021a
;
Wu et al. 2021b
)
. Imaginarity is particularly significant in local state discrimination, since bipartite real mixed states which are perfectly distinguishable via local operations and classical communication (LOCC), can not be distinguished via local real operations and classical communication (LRCC). Imaginarity, alike quantum coherence
(
Baumgratz et al. 2014
;
Girolami 2014
;
Winter and Yang 2016
)
, is a basis dependent property of a quantum state.

A quantifier of imaginarity, called the robustness of imaginarity, similar to distance-based measures
(
Brandão and Gour 2015
)
, minimizes the distance between a given state (say
\rho
) and a state from the set of real states,
\mathcal{R}
. It is defined as
(
Hickey and Gour 2018
;
Wu et al. 2021b
)
,

\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho)=\min_{\tau}\{s\geq 0:\frac{\rho+s\tau}{1+s}\in\mathcal{R}\}

(4)

by considering minimization over all quantum states
\tau
. Interestingly,
\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho)
has a closed and simplified form for all pure and mixed states. It is expressed as,

\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\rho^{T}\|_{1}

(5)

where,
\rho^{T}
is the transposition of
\rho
and the trace norm
\|V\|_{1}=\operatorname{Tr}[\sqrt{V^{\dagger}V}]
. If
\rho
is pure, i.e.
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
, then
\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho)=\sqrt{1-|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|^{2}}
. As a bonafide measure, it is a convex monotone under local real operations, and vanishes identically for all real states.

III.1
Imaginarity complementarity relations

Consider a qubit state,
\rho=\frac{\openone_{2}+\vec{n}.\vec{\sigma}}{2}
, where
\vec{n}=(n_{x},n_{y},n_{z})
with
0\leq|\vec{n}|\leq 1
, and
\vec{\sigma}
the Pauli vector. The three mutually unbiased bases in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

(
Bengtsson 2007
)
can be generally represented as
B_{1}\equiv\{|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle,|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle\}
,
B_{2}\equiv\{\frac{|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle+|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle-|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\}
and
B_{3}\equiv\{\frac{|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle+i|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle-i|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\}
, where
|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle=\cos\frac{\theta}{2}|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}|1\rangle
,
|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}|0\rangle-e^{i\phi}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}|1\rangle
,
0\leq\theta\leq\pi
and
0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi
. The modulus of inner product of a pair of bases chosen from two different MUBs always yields the value of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and each basis from
\{B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}\}
constitutes set of orthonormal vectors. By representing
\rho
in the above mentioned MUBs (e.g.
\rho_{B_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}\langle u_{\theta,\phi}|\rho|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle&\langle u_{\theta,\phi}|\rho|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle\\
\langle d_{\theta,\phi}|\rho|u_{\theta,\phi}\rangle&\langle d_{\theta,\phi}|\rho|d_{\theta,\phi}\rangle\end{pmatrix}
in
B_{1}
basis), we can find from Eq.(
5
) that,

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{R}^{B_{1}}(\rho)=\mathscr{I}_{R}^{B_{2}}(\rho)=|n_{y}\cos\phi-n_{x}\sin\phi|

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{R}^{B_{3}}(\rho)=|n_{x}\cos\theta\cos\phi+n_{y}\cos\theta\sin\phi-n_{z}\sin\theta|.

(6)

The uncertainty between unequal weights of imaginarities among different MUBs leads to a complementarity relation that can be minimally demonstrated by considering two MUBs, and is given by

\mathscr{I}_{R}^{B_{2}(B_{1})}(\rho)+\mathscr{I}_{R}^{B_{3}}(\rho)\leq\sqrt{2}

(7)

where the upper bound corresponds to
\theta=0
and
\phi=\pi
. The choice of MUBs, i.e.,
\{\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}\}
-basis is optimal.

The density operator of
\rho
can be represented as
\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1+n_{x}}{2}&\frac{in_{y}+n_{z}}{2}\\
\frac{-in_{y}+n_{z}}{2}&\frac{1-n_{x}}{2}\end{pmatrix}
,
\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1+n_{y}}{2}&\frac{-in_{x}+n_{z}}{2}\\
\frac{in_{x}+n_{z}}{2}&\frac{1-n_{y}}{2}\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1+n_{z}}{2}&\frac{n_{x}-in_{y}}{2}\\
\frac{n_{x}+in_{y}}{2}&\frac{1-n_{z}}{2}\end{pmatrix}
corresponding to x-, y- and z-basis respectively. In terms of the above representations, we observe from Eq.(
5
) that,

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{R}^{x}(\rho)=\mathscr{I}_{R}^{z}(\rho)=n_{y}

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{R}^{y}(\rho)=n_{x}

(8)

This shows the feature of basis dependence in terms of the robustness of imaginarity. A complementarity relation between two mutually orthogonal basis x and y ca be similarly constructed as,

\mathscr{I}_{R}^{x}(\rho)+\mathscr{I}_{R}^{y}(\rho)\leq\sqrt{2}

(9)

The equality of Eq.(
9
) holds for the state
\rho=\frac{\openone_{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y})}{2}
, while the left hand sides of Eq.(
9
) becomes 1 for the eigenstates of
\sigma_{y}
.

III.2
Imaginarity steering inequality

Like coherence, imaginarity also explores a portion of knowledge hidden in a quantum state. As the properties are different from each other, hence a unique steering criterion can be formulated using imaginarity. To formulate a steering criterion, a LHS model has to be set up based on imaginarity complementarity relations.

The cheating strategy given by Eq.(
1
) can be recast for measurement along
A
-direction and outcome
a
at Alice’s side in terms of imaginarity in any basis at Bob’s side as,

\mathscr{I}_{R}(\sigma_{a|A})=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda})

(10)

by using the facts that,
\{p(\lambda),p(a|A,\lambda)\}\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ \forall\lambda
and matrix norms are absolutely homogeneous of degree 1. As
0\leq\{\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda}),p(\lambda),p(a|A,\lambda)\}\leq 1
for all local hidden variable
\lambda
and
p(a|A)\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows from from Eq.(

) that,

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{R}(\sigma_{a|A})

\displaystyle=p(a|A)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{a|A})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\lambda}\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda})\penalty\ [\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)]

\displaystyle=\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda_{\max}})\penalty\ p(a|A)

(11)

Using
\sum_{a=+,-}p(a|A)=1
, we can write from Eq.(
11
) that,

\displaystyle\sum_{a=+,-}p(a|A)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{a|A})

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda_{\max}})\penalty\ \sum_{a=+,-}p(a|A)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda_{\max}})

(12)

If Bob computes imaginarity in x-basis after Alice’s measurement in y-basis with outcome
a_{1}
, then we have

\sum_{a_{1}=+,-}p(a_{1}|y)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}^{x}(\rho_{a_{1}|y})\leq\mathscr{I}_{R}^{x}(\rho_{\lambda_{\max}})

(13)

Similarly, if Bob calculates imaginarity in y-basis after Alice’s measurement in x-basis with outcome
a_{2}
, then

\sum_{a_{2}=+,-}p(a_{2}|x)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}^{y}(\rho_{a_{2}|x})\leq\mathscr{I}_{R}^{y}(\rho_{\lambda_{\max}})

(14)

Now by summing inequalities (
13
) and (
14
) and using Eq.(
9
) subsequently, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}=\sum_{a_{1}=+,-}p(a_{1}|y)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}^{x}(\rho_{a_{1}|y})+\sum_{a_{2}=+,-}p(a_{2}|x)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}^{y}(\rho_{a_{2}|x})\leq\sqrt{2}

(15)

We have constructed the inequality (
15
) by modelling LHS for Bob through the imaginarity complementarity relation. The violation of it thus implies steering of Bob’s local imaginarity by Alice. Hence we call the sufficient criteria given by inequalities (
15
) as
imaginarity steering inequalities (ISI)
under the 2-measurement scenario. It certifies that imaginarity can be an aid in quantum nonlocality where full information about LHS is not required.

Theorem 1
.

All separable states satisfy imaginarity steering inequalities.

Proof.

A general bipartite separable state has the following form,

\rho_{AB}^{\text{sep}}=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}^{A}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{B}

(16)

Now depending upon Alice’s projective measurement
A
and outcome
a
, Bob’s normalized conditional state becomes,

\displaystyle\rho_{a|A}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ \operatorname{Tr}_{A}[(\Pi_{a|A}\otimes\openone_{2})\penalty\ (\rho_{\lambda}^{A}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{B})]}{\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ \operatorname{Tr}[(\Pi_{a|A}\otimes\openone_{2})\penalty\ (\rho_{\lambda}^{A}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{B})]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ \operatorname{Tr}_{A}[\Pi_{a|A}\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}^{A}]\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}^{B}}{\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ \operatorname{Tr}_{A}[\Pi_{a|A}\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}^{A}]\penalty\ \operatorname{Tr}_{B}[\rho_{\lambda}^{B}]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}^{B}}{\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)\penalty\ \rho_{\lambda}^{B}}{p(a|A)}

(17)

Since
\{p(\lambda),p(a|A,\lambda),p(a|A)\}\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ \forall\lambda
, therefore using subadditivity and homogeneity properties of the trace norm of a matrix, i.e.,
\mathscr{I}_{R}(\sum_{i}\varrho_{i})\leq\sum_{i}\mathscr{I}_{R}(\varrho_{i})
and
\mathscr{I}_{R}(c\varrho)=c\mathscr{I}_{R}(\varrho)
respectively, where
c\in\mathbb{R}
, and summing over all outcomes at Alice’s side, we can write from Eq.(
17
) that,

\displaystyle\sum_{a=+,-}p(a|A)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{a|A})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a,\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ p(a|A,\lambda)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda}^{B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ \mathscr{I}_{R}(\rho_{\lambda}^{B})

(18)

where we apply
\sum_{a}p(a|A,\lambda)=1
.

Now by employing Eq.(
18
) in different bases as given in the inequality(
9
), the left hand side of ISI(
15
) becomes,

\displaystyle I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{\text{sep}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\penalty\ (\mathscr{I}_{R}^{x}(\rho_{\lambda}^{B})+\mathscr{I}_{R}^{y}(\rho_{\lambda}^{B}))

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\penalty\ \sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)=\sqrt{2}

(19)

where we use
\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)=1
. As a result, we observe that, the violation of ISI requires the composite system to be entangled.
∎

A general bipartite qubit state is represented as

\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{4}[\openone_{2}\otimes\openone_{2}+\vec{m}.\vec{\sigma}\otimes\openone_{2}+\openone_{2}\otimes\vec{n}.\vec{\sigma}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}t_{ij}\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}]

)

respectively. Bob computes imaginarity of his local state depending upon the measurement done at Alice’s side. If Alice measures in x-basis, then imaginarity of Bob’s local state in y-basis becomes

\mathscr{I}_{R}^{y}(\rho_{\pm|x})=\frac{|n_{1}\pm t_{11}|}{1\pm m_{1}}

(22)

corresponding to outcomes
\pm
at Alice’s side.
On the other hand, if Alice measures in y-basis, the imaginarity of Bob’s local state in x-basis becomes

\mathscr{I}_{R}^{x}(\rho_{\pm|y})=\frac{|n_{2}\pm t_{22}|}{1\pm m_{2}}

(23)

corresponding to outcomes
\pm
at Alice’s side. By using Eq.(
22

), it follows that

I_{2}(\rho_{AB})=\frac{1}{2}(|n_{1}-t_{11}|+|n_{1}+t_{11}|+|n_{2}-t_{22}|+|n_{2}+t_{22}|)

which depends on 4 real parameters

,

,

and

only, where

. Hence, nonlocality can be demonstrated through the violation of inequality(

with the use of 4 real parameters of the state given by Eq.(

IV
Witnessing Steering of Imaginarity

In this section, we show that the formalism of witness operators can be applied to identify the quantum states which obey our imaginarity steering relation. To this end, we first prove that the set of states obeying the ISI (
15
) is both convex and compact.

Theorem 3
.

The set of density matrices
\mathcal{S}=\{\rho_{AB}:I_{2}(\rho_{AB})\leq\sqrt{2}\}
form a convex and compact set.

Proof.

Let us take any two density matrices
\rho_{AB}^{(1)}
and
\rho_{AB}^{(2)}
such that
\rho_{AB}^{(1)},\rho_{AB}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})\leq\sqrt{2},

(25)

\displaystyle I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})\leq\sqrt{2}

(26)

Let us consider that a density matrix,
\rho_{AB}^{(c)}
can be expressed as a convex combination of
\rho_{AB}^{(1)}
and
\rho_{AB}^{(2)}
, implying

\rho_{AB}^{(c)}=\alpha\rho_{AB}^{(1)}+(1-\alpha)\rho_{AB}^{(2)}

(27)

where
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
. To prove convexity of the set
\mathcal{S}
, we have to show that

I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(c)})\leq\alpha I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})+(1-\alpha)I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(2)}),\penalty\ \forall\rho_{AB}^{(c)}

(28)

In respect of the density matrices
\rho_{AB}^{(k)}\penalty\ \forall k\in\{1,2\}
, by using Eq.(
24
), we can write

I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(k)})=\frac{1}{2}(|n_{1}^{(k)}-t_{11}^{(k)}|+|n_{1}^{(k)}+t_{11}^{(k)}|+|n_{2}^{(k)}-t_{22}^{(k)}|+|n_{2}^{(k)}+t_{22}^{(k)}|),\penalty\ \forall k\in\{1,2\}

(29)

For the state
\rho_{AB}^{(c)}
given by Eq.(
27
), we have

\displaystyle I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(c)})=\frac{1}{2}\Big[

\displaystyle|\alpha\penalty\ (n_{1}^{(1)}-t_{11}^{(1)})+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ (n_{1}^{(2)}-t_{11}^{(2)})|

\displaystyle+|\alpha\penalty\ (n_{1}^{(1)}+t_{11}^{(1)})+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ (n_{1}^{(2)}+t_{11}^{(2)})|

\displaystyle+|\alpha\penalty\ (n_{2}^{(1)}-t_{22}^{(1)})+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ (n_{2}^{(2)}-t_{22}^{(2)})|

\displaystyle+|\alpha\penalty\ (n_{2}^{(1)}+t_{22}^{(1)})+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ (n_{2}^{(2)}+t_{22}^{(2)})|\Big]

(30)

By applying the triangle inequality,
|z_{1}+z_{2}|\leq|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|
and
|wz|=w|z|
for any

we can rewrite it as,

\displaystyle I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(c)})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[\alpha\penalty\ |n_{1}^{(1)}-t_{11}^{(1)}|+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ |n_{1}^{(2)}-t_{11}^{(2)}|

\displaystyle+\alpha\penalty\ |n_{1}^{(1)}+t_{11}^{(1)}|+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ |n_{1}^{(2)}+t_{11}^{(2)}|

\displaystyle+\alpha\penalty\ |n_{2}^{(1)}-t_{22}^{(1)}|+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ |n_{2}^{(2)}-t_{22}^{(2)}|

\displaystyle+\alpha\penalty\ |n_{2}^{(1)}+t_{22}^{(1)}|+(1-\alpha)\penalty\ |n_{2}^{(2)}+t_{22}^{(2)}|\Big]

\displaystyle=\alpha I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})+(1-\alpha)I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}

(31)

This completes the proof of inequality(
28
) which means that any convex combination of two density matrices chosen from the set
\mathcal{S}
lies in
\mathcal{S}
, showing that
\mathcal{S}
is a convex set.

We now prove that the set
\mathcal{S}=\{\rho_{AB}:I_{2}(\rho_{AB})\leq\sqrt{2}\}
is a compact set. A set can be called as compact if it is both bounded and closed.

Lemma 1
.

\mathcal{S}
is a bounded set.

All elements of
\mathcal{S}
can be characterised by upper and lower bounds which are real numbers as the eigenspectrum of any density matrix
\rho_{AB}
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
lies in
[0,1]
and
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{AB}]=1
. Hence
\mathcal{S}
is a bounded set.

Lemma 2
.

The function
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})
of the density matrices
\rho_{AB}
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
is continuous, i.e.
I_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous at
\rho_{AB}^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that
\|\rho_{AB}-\rho_{AB}^{0}\|<\delta
implies
|I_{2}(\rho_{AB})-I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{0})|<\varepsilon\penalty\ \forall\rho_{AB}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Let us represent the density matrix
\rho_{AB}^{0}
in terms of Eq.(
20
) with no loss of generality as

\rho_{AB}^{0}=\frac{1}{4}[\openone_{2}\otimes\openone_{2}+\vec{m}^{0}.\vec{\sigma}\otimes\openone_{2}+\openone_{2}\otimes\vec{n}^{0}.\vec{\sigma}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}t_{ij}^{0}\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}]

such that
m_{i}-m_{i}^{0}=\mathfrak{a}_{i},n_{i}-n_{i}^{0}=\mathfrak{b}_{i},t_{ij}-t_{ij}^{0}=\mathfrak{c}_{ij}\penalty\ \forall i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
. Now we can write that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{AB}-\rho_{AB}^{0}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{4}\|\sum_{i,j}[(\sigma_{i}\otimes\openone_{2})+(\openone_{2}\otimes\sigma_{i})+(\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j})]\|

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}c=\delta

(33)

where
c=\max\{|\mathfrak{a}_{i}|,|\mathfrak{b}_{i}|,|\mathfrak{c}_{ij}|\}_{i,j}\penalty\ \forall i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
and
\delta>0
.

Next, by applying Eq.(
24
) for the density matrices
\rho_{AB}
and
\rho_{AB}^{0}
, we have

\displaystyle|I_{2}(\rho_{AB})-I_{2}(\rho_{AB}^{0})|=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big|(|n_{1}-t_{11}|+|n_{1}+t_{11}|+|n_{2}-t_{22}|

\displaystyle+|n_{2}+t_{22}|-|n_{1}^{0}-t_{11}^{0}|-|n_{1}^{0}+t_{11}^{0}|

\displaystyle-|n_{2}^{0}-t_{22}^{0}|-|n_{2}^{0}+t_{22}^{0}|)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big||n_{1}-t_{11}-n_{1}^{0}+t_{11}^{0}|

\displaystyle+|n_{1}+t_{11}-n_{1}^{0}-t_{11}^{0}|

\displaystyle+|n_{2}-t_{22}-n_{2}^{0}+t_{22}^{0}|

\displaystyle+|n_{2}+t_{22}-n_{2}^{0}-t_{22}^{0}|\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big||\mathfrak{b}_{1}-\mathfrak{c}_{11}|+|\mathfrak{b}_{1}+\mathfrak{c}_{11}|

\displaystyle+|\mathfrak{b}_{2}-\mathfrak{c}_{22}|+|\mathfrak{b}_{2}+\mathfrak{c}_{22}|\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[|\mathfrak{b}_{1}-\mathfrak{c}_{11}|+|\mathfrak{b}_{1}+\mathfrak{c}_{11}|

\displaystyle+|\mathfrak{b}_{2}-\mathfrak{c}_{22}|+|\mathfrak{b}_{2}+\mathfrak{c}_{22}|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{b}_{1}|+|\mathfrak{b}_{2}|+|\mathfrak{c}_{11}|+|\mathfrak{c}_{22}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4c=\frac{8}{\sqrt{15}}\delta=\varepsilon

(34)

where the first inequality follows from
|z_{1}|-|z_{2}|\leq|z_{1}-z_{2}|\penalty\ \forall\{z_{1},z_{2}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, while the second and third inequalities follow from
|\sum_{i}z_{i}|\leq\sum_{i}|z_{i}|\penalty\ \forall\{z_{i}\}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the constant,
\varepsilon>0
. This shows that
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})
is a continuous function of the density matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Lemma 3
.

\mathcal{S}
is a closed set.

The continuous function

is closed within
[0,2]
for all bipartite qubit states where the lower bound occurs for maximally mixed state and the upper bound occurs for Bell states. By taking
\mathcal{S}
under consideration, we have
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})\in[0,\sqrt{2}]
. Therefore, we can write that
\mathcal{S}=\{\rho_{AB}:I_{2}(\rho_{AB})\leq\sqrt{2}\}=\{I_{2}^{-1}([0,\sqrt{2}])\}
which characterizes
\mathcal{S}
as a closed set.

As the set,
\mathcal{S}
is both bounded and closed, it can be termed as a compact set. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Now, according to the hyperplane separation theorem
(
Holmes 1975
;
Rudin 1976
)
, if a set
\mathcal{S}
is both convex and compact, then there exists a hyperplane between a state

and
\mathcal{S}=\{\rho_{AB}:I_{2}(\rho_{AB})\leq\sqrt{2}\}
. This characterizes
\mathcal{S}
as a set of free states which are not useful as nonlocal resource for steering imaginarity at Bob’s side. As the states useful for steering imaginarity can always be separated from the states not useful for the same, a witness operator may be constructed to demonstrate the steering of imaginarity in 2-measurement basis by achieving the values of
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})
higher than
\sqrt{2}
. Thus a state,
\varrho_{AB}
can be found which is witnessed by such an operator, thus allowing to witness the imaginarity steering criterion.

IV.1
Construction of a Witness Operator

By using Eq.(
24
), a Hermitian operator
\widetilde{W}
can always be constructed which obeys the conditions, (i)
\operatorname{Tr}[\widetilde{W}\rho_{AB}]\geq 0
for the states
\rho_{AB}
which satisfy inequality(
15
), and (ii)
\operatorname{Tr}[\widetilde{W}\rho_{AB}]<0
for at least one state
\rho_{AB}
which violates inequality(
15
) and hence certifies quantum imaginarity as a nonlocal resource. The observable,
\widetilde{W}
can be called as a witness for steering imaginarity. We propose the construction of
\widetilde{W}
depending upon four conditions on real parametric space, as given below.

(i)

n_{1}\geq t_{11}
&
n_{2}\geq t_{22}
,

(ii)

n_{1}\geq t_{11}
&
n_{2}\leq t_{22}
,

(iii)

n_{1}\leq t_{11}
&
n_{2}\geq t_{22}
and

(iv)

n_{1}\leq t_{11}
&
n_{2}\leq t_{22}

Eq.(
24
) can have the following forms depending upon the aforementioned conditions as follows:

\displaystyle I_{2}(\rho_{AB})=

\displaystyle\{n_{1},-t_{11}\}+\{n_{2},-t_{22}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ ...(\text{cond.(i)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{n_{1},-t_{11}\}+\{-n_{2},t_{22}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ ...(\text{cond.(ii)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{-n_{1},t_{11}\}+\{n_{2},-t_{22}\},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ ...(\text{cond.(iii)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{-n_{1},t_{11}\}+\{-n_{2},t_{22}\}.\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ ...(\text{cond.(iv)})

In general,
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})
can be expressed in terms of the witness operators as

I_{2}(\rho_{AB})=\begin{cases}\pm n_{1}\pm n_{2}=\operatorname{Tr}[W_{i,j}^{1}\penalty\ \rho_{AB}]\\
\pm n_{1}\pm t_{22}=\operatorname{Tr}[W_{i,j}^{2}\penalty\ \rho_{AB}]\\
\pm t_{11}\pm n_{2}=\operatorname{Tr}[W_{i,j}^{3}\penalty\ \rho_{AB}]\\
\pm t_{11}\pm t_{22}=\operatorname{Tr}[W_{i,j}^{4}\penalty\ \rho_{AB}]\\
\end{cases},`\forall i,j\in\{0,1\}

(35)

where
W_{i,j}^{k}
(
i,j\in\{0,1\},k\in\{1,2,3,4\}
) is a Hermitian operator yielding real expectation values and can be written as

\displaystyle W_{i,j}^{1}=(-1)^{i}\openone_{2}\otimes\sigma_{x}+(-1)^{j}\openone_{2}\otimes\sigma_{y}

(36)

\displaystyle W_{i,j}^{2}=(-1)^{i}\openone_{2}\otimes\sigma_{x}+(-1)^{j}\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y}

(37)

\displaystyle W_{i,j}^{3}=(-1)^{i}\sigma_{x}\otimes\sigma_{x}+(-1)^{j}\openone_{2}\otimes\sigma_{y}

(38)

\displaystyle W_{i,j}^{4}=(-1)^{i}\sigma_{x}\otimes\sigma_{x}+(-1)^{j}\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y}

(39)

It can be easily checked that
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})\geq 0
. By applying inequality(
15
),
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})\leq\sqrt{2}
implying
\operatorname{Tr}[\widetilde{W}_{i,j}^{k}\penalty\ \rho_{AB}]\geq 0
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{i,j}^{k}=\sqrt{2}\penalty\ \openone_{4}-W_{i,j}^{k},\penalty\ \forall

\displaystyle i,j\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle k\in\{1,2,3,4\}

(40)

\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}
is not useful for steering imaginarity from Alice to Bob. Though the aforesaid form of the witness operator is unnormalised, it can be normalised as
\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}\widetilde{W}_{i,j}^{k}
, so that
\operatorname{Tr}[\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}\widetilde{W}_{i,j}^{k}]=1
. We consider the unnormalised form of witness for the rest of the paper without loss of generality.
The violation of inequality(
15
), i.e.,
I_{2}(\rho_{AB})>\sqrt{2}
is indicated by
\operatorname{Tr}[\widetilde{W}_{i,j}^{k}\penalty\ \rho_{AB}]<0
for at least one
\rho_{AB}\notin\mathcal{S}
, which manifests quantum steerability of imaginarity from Alice to Bob. Therefore,
\widetilde{W}_{i,j}^{k}
acts as a bonafide witness operator for steering imaginarity.

IV.2
Illustrations

To show the usefulness of our proposed witness for steering of imaginarity, we display examples by considering the following class of bipartite qubit states.

Example 1 : Werner state

The single parameter bipartite Werner state

for all

VI
Efficacy of imaginarity steering inequality

The imaginarity steering criterion (ISI) given by inequality (
15
) is a 2-measurement criterion which demonstrates steerable correlations based on partial knowledge of Bob’s local hidden state (LHS). There exist other steering criteria utilizing partial information about Bob’s LHS, such as the nonlocal advantage of quantum coherence (NAQC)
(
Mondal et al. 2017
)
, and nonlocal advantage of quantum imaginarity (NAQI)
(
Wei and Fei 2024
)
which define quantum steerable correlations using three measurements per side. Our imagniarity steering criterion is distinct from both NAQC and NAQI under the steering framework. In this section, we compare our criterion with the aforesaid steering criteria to show its robustness over the others.

Coherence of a quantum state provides partial information about the density matrix through only the off-diagonal terms of it. Quantum coherence is a basis dependent property of quantum state and can quantified by various measures such as,
l_{1}
-norm (
C^{l_{1}}
), relative entropy (
C^{R}
) and skew information (
C^{S}
)
(
Baumgratz et al. 2014
;
Girolami 2014
;
Winter and Yang 2016
)

\varrho

C^{l_{1}}(\varrho)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}|\varrho_{ij}|

, where
S(\varrho)
is the Von-Neumann entropy of
\varrho

(
Neumann 1955
)
and the diagonal matrix
\varrho_{d}

\varrho

C^{S}(\varrho)=\operatorname{Tr}[\varrho.\sigma_{i}.\sigma_{i}-\sqrt{\varrho}.\sigma_{i}.\sqrt{\varrho}.\sigma_{i}]
in the basis representation of
\sigma_{i}

\sum_{j=x,y,z}C^{g}_{j}(\varrho)\leq\gamma^{g}
, where the upper limit
\gamma^{g}=\{\sqrt{6},2.23,2\}
corresponds to
g=\{l_{1},R,S\}
, respectively
(
Mondal et al. 2017
)
.

Depending upon the above complementarity relations, sufficient criteria for steering of quantum coherence in a scenario of 3-dichotomic measurements per side can be formulated as
(
Mondal et al. 2017
)

(67)

where,

a=\{0,1\}

p(a|j\neq i)=\operatorname{Tr}[(\Pi_{a|j\neq i}\otimes\openone_{2})\rho_{AB}]

\rho_{AB}

\rho_{B|\Pi_{a|j\neq i}}=\frac{1}{p(a|j\neq i)}\operatorname{Tr}_{A}[(\Pi_{a|j\neq i}\otimes\openone_{2})\rho_{AB}]

67

Another 3-measure steering inequality has been proposed recently by using

-norm and relative entropy of imaginarity by Wei et al

VII
Conclusions

Complex numbers are an essential part of the density matrix formalism in quantum mechanics. The role of imaginarity turns out to be evident in the manifestation of various features of quantum theory. The operational advantage of imaginary numbers in local state discrimination is well established
(
Wu et al. 2021b
)
. Since quantum mechanics allows entanglement and nonlocal features among two or more spatially separated parties, which distinguishes it from classical theory, it is imperative to investigate the role of imaginarity for displaying nonlocal features of entangled states. In the present work, we show how imaginarity forms an important resource in the efficient manifestation of quantum steerability.

In our present analysis we first construct 2-setting complementarity relations based on the basis dependent nature of imaginarity. We then present our imaginarity steering inequality (ISI) for bipartite qubit states which forms a sufficient criterion for detecting quantum steering. Next, we show that the set of all bipartite qubit states which satisfy ISI is both convex and compact. This enables formulation of witness operators which produce negative expectation value for the states demonstrating steerability through the violation of ISI. We furnish examples of such witnesses for some well-known quantum states, and elaborate on the practical implementation of such witness operators. Additionally, we demonstrate the monogamy of the quantum correlations embodied by the violation of our imaginarity steering inequality.

Before concluding, it may be worth re-emphasizing that our imaginarity steering inequality is a function of just four real parameters in the space of general bipartite qubit states. The framework of ISI thus emerges as an effective test-bed for detecting nonlocality with less complex empirical set-ups compared to those required for detecting Bell-nonlocality or general steerability. Since the imaginarity steering criterion proposed by us does not invoke full knowledge of the density matrix, general quantum steerability
(
Wiseman et al. 2007
;
Jones et al. 2007
)
may not always imply steerability of imaginarity. However, the reverse holds true. In this work our proposed ISI is further compared with two other steering criteria based on the partial knowledge of the quantum state. We show that ISI outperforms such criteria, known as nonlocal advantage of quantum coherence
(
Mondal et al. 2017
)
and nonlocal advantage of quantum imaginarity
(
Wei and Fei 2024
)
in the context of two different scenarios demonstrating the comparative robustness of ISI against white noise and unsharp measurements respectively.

The analysis present here may open up several interesting directions for further study. The possibility of quantification of steerability through witness operators
(
Brandão 2005
;
Rau 2009
)
could be investigated employing practically realizable fault tolerant techniques
(
Cai et al. 2023
)
. The analysis of various information processing tasks such as one-sided device independent quantum key distribution
(
Pramanik et al. 2014a
)
and random number generation
(
Passaro et al. 2015
)
may be simplified using our proposed framework. Since, all maximally entangled states violate the ISI maximally, it may be used for one-sided device-independent certification of maximally entangled states
(
Šupic and Hoban 2016
;
Goswami et al. 2018
)
more efficiently. Moreover, the effects of steering imaginarity in accelerated frames
(
Mondal and Mukhopadhyay 2015
)
and in higher dimensional set-ups
(
Hu and Fan 2018
)
may be a promising avenue to explore. Further, the sharing of the imaginarity steering correlation among a number of observers
(
Mal et al. 2016
;
Sasmal et al. 2018
;
Datta and Majumdar 2018
;
Gupta et al. 2021
)
may be interesting to explore in future works.

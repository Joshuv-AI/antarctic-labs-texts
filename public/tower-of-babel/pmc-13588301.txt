Estimating Radiation Dose and Proposing Institutional Diagnostic Reference Levels for Paediatric Fluoroscopy of Micturating Cystourethrogram: Age- and Weight - Specific Comparisons
Authors: Khalid M. Aloufi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryObjective:To compare age- and weight-specific methods used to estimate radiation doses and to propose institutional diagnostic reference levels (DRLs) for paediatric fluoroscopy of micturating cystourethrogram (MCUG).Methods:In this retrospective study, MCUG fluoroscopy parameters were gathered for 100 paediatric patients from July 2025 to January 2026. The data included the MCUG paediatric procedures for patients aged 0–15 years. The data were categorized into paediatric weight- and age-specific groups. Paediatric weight-specific groups were ≤ 5, 5 < – 10, 10 < – 20, and >20 kg, and paediatric age-specific groups were ≤ 1, 1 < – 5, 5 < – 10, and < 10 – 15 years old.Results:Differences were observed in dose area product (DAP) and DRL results for age- and weight-specific methods, especially for paediatric age groups of 1 < – 5 and 5< – 10 years old compared with paediatric weight groups of 5 < –10 kg and 10 < – 20 kg (p = 0.037 and 0.035). The proposed institutional DRLs for the MCUG fluoroscopy were 22.1, 63, 169, and 457 μGyċm2 for paediatric weight groups of ≤ 5, 5 < – 10, 10 < – 20, and >20 kg. The estimated effective doses reached 0.81, 2.18, 2.08, and 2.5 mSv.Conclusion:The paediatric weight-specific method shows realistic and lower DAP and DRL values. The results of DRLs for the MCUG fluoroscopy were acceptable when compared with literature results.

Introduction

Fluoroscopy is an effective and instantaneous medical imaging procedure that views a sequence of x-ray images of a patient's organs to guide medical treatment and diagnose pathology [1]. Patient safety is a major concern in fluoroscopy, particularly in paediatric patients, because fluoroscopy is a high ionizing radiation source among medical imaging modalities [2]. A micturating cystourethrogram (MCUG) is a frequent fluoroscopy procedure for assessing the anatomy of the urinary tract and to assess renal function [345].

The MCUG procedure contains a wide variation in the factors of radiation exposure, such as fluoroscopy time (FT), milliampere-seconds (mAs), and kilo voltage peak (kVp). In addition, there are fluctuations in the number of images series taken to obtain adequate fluoroscopy diagnostic results, as well as in the procedure protocols and fluoroscopy machine types. Furthermore, there is possibility of paediatric patient movement and complexity in protocol adjustment, related to paediatric patient size [6]. Even though the benefits of MCUG overcome the related radiation risk, paediatric patients may receive additional radiation if the procedures are not optimized.

It is well-known that a person at certain age (especially childhood) and type of tissue or organ have different responses to radiation. The susceptibility of tissues to radiation exposure is termed ‘radiosensitivity’. Children are more radiosensitive than adults, and have a longer life expectancy [7]. Radiation-induced cancer risk is higher in children due to increased growth and cell division and high metabolic rates compared with adults [8]. The ‘stochastic’ or ‘non-threshold’ radiation effects are the main concern at low radiation doses within medical imaging [910]. Quantifying radiation doses and their effects is necessary for the efficient use of radiation protection techniques and protocols.

In paediatric patients, cumulative radiation exposure could be associated to unjustified radiological procedures or repetition of the procedure due to technical reasons. Thus, the radiological procedure should be justified, and its benefits should overcome the related radiation risk. In addition, the training and experience of radiological staff are linked to a reduction in fluoroscopy radiation dose [1112]. Accordingly, the fluoroscopy radiation dose varies significantly among patients at the same or various medical facilities [1314]. Therefore, seeking an accurate procedure to estimate radiation dose and to establish DRLs is essential, especially for paediatric patients. To set DRLs, priority is given to paediatric patients, frequent medical imaging procedures, and the highest radiation exposures. The MCUG is a frequent procedure, accounting for 30% – 50% of paediatric fluoroscopy procedures [31516]. In our center, paediatric MCUG procedures constitute 35% of paediatric fluoroscopy procedures.

The DRLs are an effective tool to indicate the demand to reduce patient radiation exposure in medical imaging and optimization of the radiation dose [17]. While local and national DRLs were set at the 75th percentile, institutional DRLs are set at the 50th percentile. Institutional and local DRLs can be synthesized to build local and national DRLs [1819]. The DRLs are established at the 50th or 75th percentiles of a selected radiation quantity, like dose area product (DAP), entrance surface dose (ESD), or fluoroscopy time (FT) [192021]. The DAP (Gyċm2) is frequently used to establish DRLs in fluoroscopy [2223]. To establish DRLs, paediatric age- and weight-specific methods are recommended.

The literature indicates that paediatric age-specific method to set the DRLs and to measure the effective dose is more frequently used than using paediatric weight-specific method. However, categorizing paediatric patients into weight groups was reported to be more robust and accurate than paediatric age grouping because radiation energy and intensity (kVp and mAs) correlate directly with the patient weights in medical imaging, and paediatric size may vary significantly within an identical age groups [1824]. In the literature, the DRLs for MCUG range from 30 to 600 μGyċm2 for children up to 15 years of age. The highest and lowest times taken to perform paediatric MCUG procedures were 0.98 and 3.4 min [2025]. The time taken for MCUG procedures was significantly affected by radiological staff experience and the MCUG procedure complexity. This study aimed to compare age- and weight-specific methods considered to estimate radiation dose and to propose institutional DRLs for paediatric fluoroscopy of the MCUG procedure.

Methods

Data collection and statistical calculations

In this retrospective study, MCUG fluoroscopy parameters were gathered for 100 paediatric patients from July 2025 to January 2026. The parameters were retrieved from the Picture Archiving and Communication System. The data included the completed MCUG procedures for paediatric patients aged 0 – 15 years. Exclusion criteria included incomplete MCUG procedures, other paediatric fluoroscopy procedures, and those for adult patients.

The included MCUG data involved DAP, ESD, FT, mAs, kVp, number of x-ray series images taken, and paediatric age and weight. Data were categorized into weight- and age-specific groups adopting the categorization of the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) and the World Health Organization [1826]. Paediatric weight-specific groups were ≤ 5, 5 < – 10, 10 < – 20, and >20 kg, and age-specific groups were ≤ 1, 1 < – 5, 5 < – 10, and < 10 – 15 years old.

The MCUG fluoroscopy procedures were performed under a Siemens fluoroscopy machine (Basic Unit Luminos dRF model) manufactured in Germany in February 2014. The machine was maintained and the performance was examined periodically (the last maintenance date was in April 2025). Pulsed-mode fluoroscopy was usually used to perform the MCUG procedure, but continuous mode was used if required. Microsoft Excel software version 2407 (2022) served to compile and analyze the MCUG-related data. The U-statistic of the Mann–Whitney U statistical test was calculated in Microsoft Excel using the U1 and U2 formulae to compare the results from the age- and weight-specific methods. The units for the collected data were μGyċm2 for DAP, mGy for ESD, minutes for FT, years for age, and kilogram (kg) for weight. Radiation doses and DRLs measurements

The 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles of the DAP, FT, ESD, and the effective dose were calculated for the paediatric MCUG procedure for each age- and weight-specific paediatric group. DRLs were proposed at the 50th percentile over the distribution of the DAP radiation quantity. Institutional DRLs were established at the 50th percentile within the ICRP recommendations [18]. The effective doses were determined using DAP and the effective dose conversion formula (equation 1). The conversion factors used to convert DAP to effective dose for paediatric were age-dependent, regardless of paediatrics’ weight. The conversion factors were 3.68, 1.52, 0.99, and 0.44 for paediatric patients aged ≤ 1, 1 <– 5, 5 <– 10, and < 10 – 15 [27].

The Mann–Whitney U test showed significant differences in DAP and DRL results for the 1 < – 5 years’ age group contrasted with the paediatric 5 < – 10 kg weight group (p = 0.037), and the 5 < – 10 years’ age group was significantly different compared with the paediatric 10 < – 20 kg weight group (p = 0.035). However, no significant differences were observed between paediatric age groups of ≤ 1 and < 10 – 15 years when contrasted with paediatric weight groups of ≤ 5 kg (p = 0.107) and >20 kg (p = 0.85), The difference in paediatric weights for the compared age and weight groups explains such results. For example, the mean weight difference between paediatric aged <10 – 15 years and the paediatric of weight group <20 kg was 3 %. Conversely, the paediatric mean weight difference between paediatric aged 1 < – 5 years and the paediatric weight group of 5 < – 10 kg was 36%. The mean weight difference for paediatric weight and age grouping for the whole study sample was 38 %. Thus, more paediatric age and weight groups may be necessary when setting DRLs, especially for patients aged 1 – 10 years.

The 90 % confidence intervals (CIs) for the proposed institutional DRLs (Table 5) highlight the high variability in fluoroscopy parameters of the paediatric MCUG procedure. Such variability was related to the wide FT values, the procedure complexity, and the patient condition (stable, irritable, or urgent cases). Though a wide distribution of DAP values is expected in fluoroscopy procedures, the CI range can be tightened by increasing the study sample size.

The paediatric weight-specific method showed realistic and lower DAP and DRL values for the 4 weight categories when contrasted with the age-specific method, as the DAP, DRLs, and effective dose are closely related to the paediatric patient weights rather than ages [2829]. Even though age-specific method is widely used, several studies indicate that using age groups instead of weight categories was a limitation in assessing DRLs and doses [2430].

Paediatric patients may receive higher radiation doses without further diagnostic benefits (quality image improvement) if the radiological procedure is not optimized. The DRLs can indicate the necessity for radiation dose reduction without degrading the image quality. Here, the institutional DRLs for the paediatric MCUG fluoroscopy procedure were established using the weight-specific method for 4 paediatric weight categories. The proposed institutional DRLs were 22.1, 63, 169, and 457 μGyċm2 for ≤ 5, 5 < – 10, 10 < – 20, and >20 kg. The estimated effective doses were 0.81, 2.18, 2.08, and 2.5 mSv for ≤ 5, 5 < – 10, 10 < –20, and >20 kg. The DRL results for MCUG fluoroscopy of the weight-specific method were acceptable when compared with the results in the literature (Table 6). The proposed institutional DRLs for the MCUG were comparable with the Austrian diagnostic reference levels and Tabtieang et al [31], lower than the Irish DRLs, and higher than Mbewe et al [20] and the UK DRLs [3233]. Differences in results occurred due to fluoroscopy machine differences, the MCUG procedure complexity, the FT variability, the number of series images, and the procedure techniques and protocols. In this study, the MCUG was usually performed using pulsed-mode fluoroscopy, but continuous mode was used when required. Using pulsed-mode fluoroscopy reduces the FT required to perform the MCUG procedure and, consequently, the radiation exposure to paediatric patients [34].

Most findings in the literature are based on paediatric age groups. Paediatric age-specific method is reported to be more practical than the weight-specific method because ages are precisely recorded in hospital databases, unlike paediatric weight records. Thus, the paediatric age at the time of the procedure was calculated precisely, whereas paediatric weight should be recorded for each radiological procedure, which sometimes leads to unrecorded weights or inaccurate records. In addition, the paediatric radiosensitivity, DAP-to-dose conversion factors, and the risk of radiation-induced cancers were classified in the ICRP Publications, UNSCEAR 2013 Report, BEIR report, and other studies according to paediatric age groups. Moreover, a sufficient sample size (>30 cases) for paediatric age groups (≤ 1, 1 < – 5, 5 < – 10, and < 10 – 15 years) would promote an acceptable approximation of paediatric weight. Nevertheless, the age-specific method showed lower accuracy in DAP results compared with the weight-specific [2735].

Some limitations must be highlighted. The study sample size for some paediatric groups was small, 5 < – 10 years (n = 14) and >20 kg (n = 18) groups. This could affect the precision of the results, and there were inadequate case numbers to examine the impact of gender on the DRLs and effective dose. Also, the impact of staff experience on reducing radiation dose while maintaining the image quality was not examined; however, the radiological staff experience in our institution was >5 years. Nevertheless, the image quality, DRLs and the effective dose results were acceptable. The conversion factors adopted to convert DAP to effective dose were paediatric age-dependent; however, under the same conditions of MCUG procedure, DAP is higher for higher-weight paediatric patients.

In conclusion, there were significant differences in DRL results between the paediatric weight- and age-specific methods. The weight-specific method showed realistic and lower DAP and DRL values when compared with the age-specific method. Therefore, the weight-based method should be adopted to set paediatric MCUG DRLs and to estimate radiation doses. The results for the proposed institutional DRLs for paediatric MCUG fluoroscopy were acceptable when compared with the results in the literature. Though this study found that the DRL and effective dose values were acceptable, applying radiation protection methods and principles is necessary to reduce the radiation dose to paediatric patients undergoing MCUG fluoroscopy.

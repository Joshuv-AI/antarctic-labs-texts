Optimizing water and organic fertilizer use to enhance seedling growth and soil health in Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa spp. pekinensis) cultivation
Authors: Chi Zhang, Yixing Zhang, Xin-Xin Wang, Jizong Zhang, Huibin Li, Ruifang Zhang, Jianheng Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWater and fertilizer are the two indispensable elements in agriculture. The rational use of soil water and fertilizer can not only improve crop yield and quality rate, but also alleviate land pollution and water shortage. Water and fertilizer management in Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa spp. pekinensis) involves in improper irrigation and fertilization. This experiment was conducted to explore he shoot growth, root traits, nutrient contents, soil microbial population and soil enzyme activities of Chinese cabbage in response to different irrigation and organic fertilizer application rates. The experiment consisted of three irrigation levels (A1-A3: 90%-70% ± 5% of field water holding capacity), five organic fertilizer application rates (B1-B5: 10%-50% cattle manure with nitrogen replacement) and CK treatments (CK1: regular fertilizer, CK2: 100% cattle manure with nitrogen replacement). The results showed that irrigation rate A2 was the better irrigation rate. At this irrigation rate B2 treatment significantly increased shoot fresh weight by 22.65% and 31.09% compared to CK1 and CK2, respectively. Organic fertilizer application increased the SPAD value of Chinese cabbage, but the trend was not consistent under different irrigation and organic fertilizer levels. All irrigation levels showed more soil available K content in CK2. Compared to other treatments, CK2 was enhanced by 20.70%-317.42% under A1, 7.34%-235.68% under A2, and 28.16%-188.42% under A3 irrigation. Adequate irrigation is prerequisite to enhance root fresh weight, while lower irrigation and organic fertilizer helps to promote root length and root surface area. Soil enzyme activity and bacteria were significantly higher in A1 and A2 treatments than A3, while irrigation had no significant differences on soil actinomycetes and fungi. Under A1 irrigation, the B4 treatment had higher acid phosphatase and sucrase activities, 11.31%-22.46% and 4.30%-63.50%, respectively, compared to other treatments. In conclusion, the effect of organic fertilizer on soil enzyme activity and soil microbial population varied according to the irrigation levels, but generally organic fertilizer were beneficial to boost up the soil enzyme activity and soil microbial population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07620-5.Keywords: Chinese cabbage, Irrigation, Organic fertilizer, Root traits, Soil enzyme activity

Introduction

Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa spp. pekinensis) is high in dietary fiber and beneficial secondary metabolites. China’s annual cultivation area of Chinese cabbage is about 2.667 million hm2. It is a leafy vegetable which is widely cultivated in Asia, originated from north China. It is suitable for planting in loamy soil and sandy loam and black loam that are rich in organic matter and retain fertilizer and water. Although chemical nitrogen fertilizer application significantly improves the yield, but it also leads to the decline in the nutritional quality of the cabbage, as well as, the increase in nitrate content and environmental pollution [1]. In recent years, with the improvement of the living standard, the demand of vegetables that are high in yield and quality, additionally, the market demand for contaminated free green vegetables is also high. Optimized fertilization not only guarantees yields, but can also reduce the nitrate content of vegetables to a certain extent, which ultimately improve their nutritional quality, and mitigate the environmental stresses [2].

Climate change has adverse and prolonged impacts across many regions of world, particularly, contributing to increase drought in agricultural areas. It is estimated that approximately 40% of the world’s available land is currently impacted by drought conditions [3]. Drought stress, whether its caused by water deficiency or inefficient irrigation systems induces a disruption in physiological and biochemical processes in plants, such as; retarded germination and growth of seed, as well as oxidative damage caused by excess production of reactive oxygen species [4, 5]. Sustainable development of vegetable production requires a rational irrigation systems and fertilization techniques [6, 7]. Present fertilizer programs focus primarily on increasing vegetable yields, with a less consideration to nutritional aspects. In fact, intensive farming practices, excessive use of fertilizers, and depletion of soil properties have led to a decline in the nutritional value of vegetables [8, 9]. Standardizing water and fertilizer management practices is an effective strategy to improve vegetable properties while minimizing the waste of resources and over-reliance of vegetables on chemical fertilizers.

Abiotic stresses such as, waterlogging, over-irrigation, and water scarcity have profound and wide-ranging effects on crop productivity [10]. Plant yield increased with optimized use of fertilizer and irrigation whereas, excessive water and fertilizer resulted in the yield loss and decreased economic coefficient were observed [11, 12]. Drought and water stress have been shown to increase intrinsic water use efficiency, root-crown ratio and specific root length. However, these stresses also reduce the aboveground biomass, photosynthetic rate, leaf water potential and nitrogen use efficiency of the crop [13, 14]. Understanding the response of plants to varying irrigation levels is critical for developing strategies to mitigate the adverse effects of water stress. Fertilizers serve as vital nutrient for crops that plays an important role in various physiological processes in plants. However, excessive use of fertilization can deteriorate the soil’s ability to utilize the nutrients, which disrupt the soil’s ecological balance and microbial community, ultimately effects the soil structural stability and land productivity [15]. Organic fertilizers have significantly improves the soil structure, increases soil nutrients, ensures both the quality and yield of crop [16, 17]. Organic fertilizers have positively promoted the plant growth by enhancing the crop height, stem thickness and leaf area index and dry matter accumulation [18]. The rapid expansion of the livestock breeding sector has led to a significant increase in manure generation, which may contribute to serious ecological and environmental pollution if not properly managed Livestock manure is rich in amino acids, organic matter, and essential nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium and other vital nutrients required for plant growth. Moreover, its application enhances the composition of the rhizosphere microbial community, thereby improving soil fertility and sustaining long-term soil productivity [19–21]. Water, acting as both a solvent and transport medium, rapidly diffuses soluble nutrients from organic fertilizers, making them more readily available to microorganisms. Simultaneously, optimal moisture conditions ensure high-speed metabolic activity within microbial cells. Under suitable hydration, organic fertilizer application significantly stimulates microbial activity and biomass, optimizes microbial community structure, and enhances key ecological functions [22, 23]. The use of livestock and poultry manure in controlled environment can significantly improve the yield and quality of vegetables [24, 25]. Rational water and fertilizer management strategies are a prerequisite for enhancing yields, quality and ensure environmental sustainability. Investigating water-fertilizer interactions is essential for sustainable agricultural practices and crop resilience under water-limited conditions [26].

However, when studying the integrated effects of water management and organic fertilizers as a synergistic system on Chinese cabbage (from yield and quality to soil health) there remain numerous unexplored areas. These include the synergistic regulatory mechanisms of the rhizosphere microcosm, the induction of “stress memory” and secondary metabolism in Chinese cabbage, and the long-term, deep-seated impacts on soil abiotic properties. Based on this. In this study, we hypothesized that drought or excessive irrigation and heavy organic fertilizer application would affect the soil environment and the root traits of Chinese cabbage. These factors would affect the nutrients uptake ability and the growth of Chinese cabbage. To ensure the safe production of vegetables and promote the green and sustainable development of agriculture, this study explore three main objectives: (1) the effects of different water and organic fertilizer strategies on the growth and yield of Chinese cabbage; (2) Alteration of soil nutrients and root traits of Chinese cabbage with the application of different levels of water and fertilizer; and (3) the response of soil microbiomes of Chinese cabbage to applied water and fertilizer treatments.

Materials and methods

Experimental site

The experiment was conducted from 10 March to 1 May 2024 in the experimental controlled room of the West Campus of Hebei Agricultural University (115°26′ E, 38°49′ N). The test soil was obtained from the experimental field of Zhangbei Experimental Station of Hebei Agricultural University (114°42′ E, 41°09′ N), which considered as a high fertile for Chinese cabbage and many other cold vegetables in China. The soil possess these basic properties: pH 8.33, 29.88 g kg− 1organic matter content, 1.67 g kg− 1 total nitrogen (TN), 18.40 mg kg− 1 available phosphorus (P), and 182.29 mg kg− 1 available potassium (K). Fertilizers were rotted cattle manure which contains 19.87 g kg− 1 of TN, 9.48 g kg− 1 of P2O5, 29.43 g kg− 1 of K2O and compound fertilizers, such as; Golden Triammonia (N- P2O5- K2O = 18-18-18). Test crop “Jinglonghuang 012” (Applicant: Beijing Jinsuida Seedling Technology Co., Ltd. Variety origin: CC2232×CC2127. Approval number: GPD Chinese cabbage 110053) variety of Chinese cabbage was used for experiment, which was grown in the plastic pots with the dimensions of. Pots were plastic with an inner diameter of 17 × 14 × 10 cm (Inner diameter × base diameter × height).

Experimental design

The experimental design was a two-factor test. Factor A was irrigation with three levels (A1-A3), where A1 was 95%−85% of field water holding capacity (FWC), A2 was 85%−75% FWC, and A3was 75%−65% FWC. The experiment included seven fertilization treatments: CK1 (standard inorganic fertilizer (Kim Sam-an: Shandong Shengda Eco-Fertilizer Co., Ltd.) at 55.2 g), CK2 (100% cattle manure replacing nitrogen), and five cattle manure substitution treatments—B1 (10% cattle manure replacing nitrogen), B2 (20%), B3 (30%), B4 (40%), and B5 (50%). After applying cattle manure to replace a portion of the chemical fertilizer nitrogen, we precisely calculated the phosphorus and potassium quantities inherent in the manure itself. Subsequently, in all treatment groups (including the pure chemical fertilizer treatment), the total phosphorus and potassium application rates were uniformly adjusted to the same level by applying phosphorus (P₂O₅) and potassium (K₂O). Thus, apart from differences in nitrogen source composition, all treatment groups received identical total amounts of N, P, and K before sowing, ensuring the validity of comparisons. Each treatment was combined with three irrigation levels, resulting in a total of 21 treatment combinations. Each treatment was replicated four times, totaling 84 pots. The soil was air-dried and then sieved through a 2 mm sieve, mixed well, and 1.5 kg of air-dried soil was placed in each pot and the soil water holding capacity was 200 mL. Fertilizer and soil were mixed evenly and filled into the pots, and soil moisture content was kept consistent with regular weighing on every 3 d. The 21 pots within each block were arranged randomly in the greenhouse, and re-randomized weekly to minimize positional effects. In the incubation room, the average temperature was 20 °C, LED lighting was provided for 14 h daily, from 06:00 to 20:00, with a light intensity of 9000 LUS.

Indicator measurement and methods

Sample collection

The day prior to harvest, a chlorophyll meter (SPAD-502PLUS, KONICA MINOLTA) was used to measure the SPAD value and nitrogen content of the 3rd leaf from the top of the seedlings. The shoot part was separated from the roots with scissors, washed with tap water, washed again with deionized water, blotted dry with absorbent paper and weighed to determine the fresh weight of the shoot. Then shoot was put into an envelope for oven dried at 105℃ for 30 min, transferred to 75℃ until fully dried, and noted the dry weight of stem. Simultaneously, roots were cleaned gently by removing the rhizospheric soil adhering to the roots. The soil adhering to the innermost part of the root system was collected into air tight bags carefully to avoid any damage, and stored at 4℃ to determine the pH, enzyme activity and microorganisms of the inter-root soil. Meanwhile, a small quantity of soil was also collected to calculate the nutrient content. The cleaned roots were collected including the fine roots that were shredded into the soil, washed with deionized water, and stored at 4℃ to analyzed the root properties.

Determination of root morphology, soil nutrients and enzyme activities

Roots were placed in transparent plastic discs, spread in water and scanned (400 dpi resolution) with a scanner (Epson Expression 1600 pro, Nagano, Japan). The scanned root images were analysed using Win-Rhizo software (Regent Instruments Inc., Quebec, QC, Canada) to obtain root parameters such as root length and root diameter.

Soil pH was determined by mixing soil and water at a ratio of 1:5, oscillated for 5 min, and then pH was determined with a pH meter. Soil organic matter was determined using the volumetric method of potassium dichromate by weighing air-dried soil samples in an oil bath. A certain amount of K2Cr2O7 standard solution was added with concentrated sulfuric acid for 5 min. The solution was then transferred into a conical flask and 2–3 drops of O-phorpholine indicator were added and titrated with FeSO4. Total nitrogen (TN) in soil was determined using a combined digestion method with concentrated sulfuric acid and a mixed catalyst, followed by analysis with a flow injection analyzer. Soil available P was determined using the molybdenum antimony colorimetric method, following extraction of air-dried soil samples with 0.5 mol L− 1 sodium bicarbonate. Soil available K was determined with flame spectrophotometer (FP6410, AOYI Instruments Shanghai CO LTD, China) by extracting the air-dried soil samples with 1 mol L− 1 ammonium acetate [27]. The soil microorganisms were counted using the dilution plate method. Bacteria were cultured in beef extract peptone medium, actinomycetes in modified Gaoshi No.1 medium, and fungi in Madin medium. Microbial calculation formula is bacterial count = (average bacterial count × dilution factor × 20 × fresh soil weight)/dry soil weight. Acid phosphatase, sucrase, urease activity was detected using the Solaibao detection kit method

Date analysis

The data was saved and interpreted in Excel 2016 and SPSS 26.0. Two way ANOVA was used to test the significance difference of irrigation and organic fertilizer rates, as well as their interaction. Pearson correlation coefficient method was used for correlation analysis, Origin 2022 was used for graphical illustrations and significant differences (0.05) was determined using the Fisher LSD test. Comprehensive evaluation of experimental data was conducted using the fuzzy mathematical affiliation function method [28]. The Eq. (1) is used as affiliation function.

Results

Different irrigation and organic fertilizer application rates affect Chinese cabbage growth, root traits, inter-root soil nutrient content and microecology. In this study we found that irrigation level significantly affected 15/21; application rate of organic fertilizer significantly affected 19/21; and water-fertilizer interaction significantly affected 15/21 observed traits (Table 1). Both the amount of applied irrigation and the organic fertilizer significantly affected aboveground and root traits of Chinese cabbage. Soil microbiomes were highly affected with the alteration of irrigation levels, while inter-root soil pH was affected with the addition of various concentrations of organic fertilizer.

Alteration in aboveground biomass of Chinese cabbage

Different treatments had significant effects on the aboveground biomass of Chinese cabbage. Among the Irrigation regimes, A2 proved to be the most effective in promoting cabbage growth, as it resulted in significantly higher shoot fresh weight and irrigation water use efficiency compared to irrigation levels (Fig. 1; Table 2). The B2 and B3 fertilizer treatments consistently maintained higher shoot fresh and dry weight across all irrigation levels. Under the optimal irrigation treatments A2, B2 increased the shoot fresh weight by 22.65% and 31.09% and the B3 2.83% and 12.80% compared to CK1 and CK2, respectively. Except for A2B1, treatments with low levels of organic fertilizer substitution (30% and less) yielded better. However, under reduced soil moisture conditions when organic fertilizer is less, aboveground biomass was declined. Additionally,, the leaf water content was significantly reduced under drought stress (A3 treatment) (Fig. 1a and 1b).

organic fertilizer increased the inter-root soil pH of Chinese cabbage irrespective of the applied quantity (Fig. 2a). Meanwhile, the available K was significantly increased with the high level of organic fertilizer and this effect was maximum under drought condition (Fig. 2e). Effects of water and fertilizer on root traits of Chinese cabbage

The optimized irrigation levels (A2) promote the root growth which resulted into the higher fresh weight and root diameter of Chinese cabbage (Fig. 3a and 3d). Drought regulates the distribution of crop assimilation products above and below ground, it was found that lower irrigation level (A3) improves the root length and root surface area (Fig. 3b and c). These root traits helped to uptake more nutrient resources and water for the growth of Chinese cabbage. Meanwhile, the various irrigation levels caused no significant differences in the correlation of root traits (Fig. 4).

Response of soil microbiomes

Both irrigation level and organic fertilizer application significantly influenced rhizosphere soil enzyme activities and microbial populations in Chinese cabbage. Overall, inter-root soil enzyme activity enhanced with the higher irrigation rates and the effects of each fertilizer treatment on enzyme activity was minimum under low irrigation levels. Under A1 irrigation, the acid phosphatase, sucrase and urease levels were increased and then adeclined trend was observed with the proportion of organic fertilizer (Fig. 5a, 5b and 5c). Treatment B4 showed 22.64% and 63.49% higher levels of acid phosphatase and sucrase, respectively. Treatment B3 showed 30.89% increase of urease a greater enhancement potential for, which was increased by compared to CK1. In this study, It was observed that A2 irrigation and B1 treatment, promoted soil acid phosphatase activity, B3 promoted soil sucrase activity, and B2 promoted soil urease activity.

Effect of different water and fertilizer treatments on soil microbial and enzyme activities

Similarly, the soil microorganisms in the inter-root of Chinese cabbage were higher under lower irrigation and vice versa. There were no significant difference in the population of inter-root soil bacteria and actinomycetes with the application of organic fertilizers (except B5) (Fig. 5d and 5e, and 5f), whereas a significant decrease in the soil fungi population was observed with the application of organic fertilizers (except B3). The application of organic fertilizers showed no effect on actinomycetes. It was also found that, under irrigation rate A2, there were no significant effect on soil bacteria (except B5) () and fungi (except CK2). Under irrigation rate A3, application of organic fertilizers showed no regular effect on the soil bacteria and fungi population, but actinomycetes were significantly increased compared to individual use of chemical fertilizers.

Comprehensive evaluation of different treatments

In order to find out the optimal irrigation level and fertilization treatment, we divided the research indexes into four groups; shoot traits, root traits, soil nutrient level and soil microbiology. The method of subordinate function analysis was applied to evaluate the treatments comprehensively. All these indicators are positive for both Chinese cabbage growth and soil environment, and a larger cumulative sum of the affiliation function calculations indicates the positive results. We found that irrigation level A2, 85%−75% field holding capacity, and fertilization rate B4 which contains 40% cattle manure and other N replacement were optimal for Chinese cabbage seedlings growth (Table 3).

Discussion

Water and fertilizer regulation induces soil fertility to enhance Chinese cabbage growth

The optimized irrigation promotes crop growth, but crops growth could result into the water deficit irrigation that might cause yield reduction to some extent [29]. Irrigation volume significantly influences post-harvest soil fertility in Chinese cabbage. Both excessive and insufficient irrigation reduce plant growth and nutrient uptake, leading to greater accumulation of residual nutrients in the soil. The fresh shoot weight was lower in A1 and A3 treatments, while the soil nutrient content was higher. Under high irrigation conditions, significant nitrification-denitrification losses or ammonia volatilization may occur, causing nitrogen to escape from the soil in gaseous form. With low irrigation, plant growth is inhibited, leaving more nutrients in the soil [30, 31]. The available K content under irrigation level A3 was significantly higher than that of A1 and A2 (Figs. 1 and 2). The level of irrigation significantly affected water-fertilizer interactions and water-fertilizer use efficiency, which affected Chinese cabbage growth and soil nutrient surplus or deficit (Table 2). Notably, in this study, the rhizosphere pH of all treatment groups was lower than the initial soil pH (Fig. 2), indicating widespread soil acidification during plant growth. This phenomenon is primarily attributed to biological processes such as proton secretion accompanying cation uptake by plant roots and nitrification by soil microorganisms [32, 33]. Organic fertilizer treatment likely maximized these acid-producing pathways by promoting plant growth and strongly stimulating microbial activity, thereby becoming a key factor driving the shift of alkaline soil pH toward neutrality [34].

Organic fertilizer is rich in organic matter, which can improve the physical and chemical properties of the soil, increase the soil microorganisms, and promote crop growth [35, 36]. In agricultural production systems, organic fertilizers considers as a good choice to fertilize the soil and increase the yield and quality of crops [37–39]. Similarly, the rational application of organic fertilizer aligns well with the principles of green agricultural development. Our study also showed that organic fertilizer significantly affected soil fertility, even after the Chinese cabbage was harvested. The application of organic fertilizer showed most significant effect on available P and K content [40]. Organic waste has been shown to improve the efficiency of nutrient use in the human food system, in consistent with our results (Table 2) [41]. Previous studies have also shown that organic fertilizers can improve nutrient uptake and use efficiency, especially under certain irrigation conditions [42]. Under the A3 treatment, soil organic matter and total nitrogen contents of B3 treatment were significantly higher as compared to the control and other fertilization treatments. We hypothesized that this combination precisely creates an optimal “water-nutrient balance point” for soil microbial activity. Moisture levels are neither excessive (reducing anaerobic denitrification) nor insufficient (limiting microbial activity), while the application of organic fertilizer provides ample energy and carbon sources. In conclusion, the observed increase in soil nutrient under irrigation level A3 can be attributed to the regulation of specific microbial taxa in the soil, as well as organic fertilizers altered the soil nutrient and enzyme activities (Fig. 5). The non-significant differences observed in the other treatments may be attributed to short-cycle excessive irrigation, which could disrupt the activity of key microbial communities and consequently hinder nutrient release from the organic fertilizer.

Water and fertilizer regulation modulate the root traits

The plant root system plays a vital role in soil exploration, water and nutrient uptake, and shoot biomass accumulation [43]. The root system is susceptible to external conditions such as nutrient availability and temperature [44–47]. To alleviate water and nutrient resource deficits, plants access more effective resources by altering root traits [48]. Our study also showed that Chinese cabbage had higher root fresh weight and diameter under higher irrigation levels. Whereas, it was found that with the incidence of drought, the root length and root surface area were increased (Fig. 3). In short, under conditions of ample water and no stress, plants adopt a growth strategy of “investing in infrastructure,” building robust and efficient water-transport networks to maximize growth benefits; under water stress, their strategy shifts to “investing in exploration and efficiency,” maximizing the area and range of water uptake while minimizing carbon expenditure [49, 50]. Organic fertilizers are also beneficial for promoting crop root growth by improving soil environmental properties [48, 51]. A previous study found that organic fertilizers significantly increased root length [52]. Our results also showed similar results that organic fertilizer significantly increased Chinese cabbage root length and root surface area (Fig. 3). This may be due to the slow-release nature of organic fertilizers, requiring Chinese cabbage to adjust root traits to enhance the acquisition of readily available nutrients for growth. Organic fertilizers do not act through a single pathway. Instead, they create a “nutrient-releasing, biologically active” rhizosphere environment that prompts crops to adjust their root investment strategy. This shift moves away from building a few thick, large-diameter vascular bundles toward developing an extensive, dense foraging network. Ultimately, this leads to a systemic optimization of water and nutrient uptake efficiency [53].

Response of soil microbiology to water and fertilizer regulation

Fertilization can change soil microbial population, diversity and structure by altering soil nutrient content [54]. With the increased level of organic fertilizer and irrigation, the carbon and nitrogen content of microbial biomass might enhanced significantly [55]. In this study we found that high irrigation and organic fertilizers significantly increased the soil microbial population (Fig. 5). This is because both water and organic fertilizer can improve the soil ecological environment, improve the microbial utilization of nutrients in the soil [56]. These modifications provide a good living environment for soil microorganisms, and significantly increase the number of soil microorganisms. However, water regulation affects soil microbes in a non-linear manner, microbial populations do not necessarily increase with higher irrigation level. The highest water and fertilizer inputs do not always result in the highest microbial abundance. Instead, there appears to be an optimal combination, as observed under the A2 irrigation treatment in our study. Water and fertilizer inputs jointly create a suitable physical habitat. Optimal moisture conditions alleviate drought stress, maintain microbial cellular metabolic activity, and connect microorganisms to soil organic particles via water films, facilitating substrate and signal diffusion [57]. Simultaneously, organic fertilizer application not only directly provides abundant carbon and energy sources but also serves as a porous medium that improves soil aggregate structure and porosity, creating more protected microhabitats for microorganisms [58]. Under conditions of ample moisture and organic fertilizer, key soil enzyme activities related to nutrient cycling also increase synchronously, indicating that the functional activity of the entire soil microbial community is comprehensively stimulated.

Soil enzyme activity is an important indicator for evaluating soil health, and proper water and fertilizer management can increase soil enzyme activity [59, 60]. The elevated soil moisture accelerates the mobility of reactants and provides a favorable reaction environment for enzymatic reactions [61–63]. In this study, soil acid phosphatase, sucrase, and urease activities were significantly higher in the irrigated A1 and A2 treatments as compared to A3 treatment (Fig. 5). Our study also indicated that organic fertilizers at the same irrigation rate increased soil enzyme activities. It was indicated that organic fertilizer provides soil enzyme substrates [64], regulate the soil carbon to nitrogen ratio to provide a more suitable environment for microorganisms [65]; and also improves plant growth and development by increasing root biomass and secretion, which enhances soil enzyme activity [66]. Therefore, the increase in enzyme activity is not only a direct result of soil management practices but also a comprehensive reflection of the coordinated response between plants and microorganisms to an optimized environment.

Conclusions

Over-irrigation and water stress impede Chinese cabbage seedlings growth. Organic fertilizer application promotes efficient use of resource and Chinese cabbage growth by modifying the root structure through alteration of rhizosphere soil environment. Overall, the 80% field water holding capacity irrigation level and 40% cattle manure containing organic fertilizer were found to be the most suitable for Chinese cabbage seedlings. Under the irrigation and organic fertilizer (A2B4), Chinese cabbage showed better root traits and soil environment, which can promote the nutrients absorption, growth, and significantly improve water and fertilizer use efficiency of Chinese cabbage. The enhanced plant performance stems from the activation of soil microbial communities, manifested as increased microbial abundance and key enzyme activities, thereby accelerating nutrient mineralization and availability. In summary, this study established an efficient water and fertilizer management strategy for cabbage seedlings under greenhouse conditions, providing a practical pathway for water conservation and productivity enhancement in field vegetable production systems across arid and semi-arid regions.

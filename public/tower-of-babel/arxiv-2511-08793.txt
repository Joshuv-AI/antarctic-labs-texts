Abstract
. We develop a machine for bounding Selmer groups of Galois representations via Euler systems in “non-ordinary” settings, using Pottharst’s definition of Selmer groups via Robba-ring ( φ , Γ ) (\varphi,\Gamma) -modules. Our approach relies on Sweeting’s interpretation of Kolyvagin derivative classes via non-principal ultrafilters. We apply these results to prove new cases of the cyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for non-ordinary Rankin–Selberg convolutions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The problem

The theory of
Euler systems
has been conspicuously successful in studying the relations between special values of
L
-functions and arithmetic, and hence in proving cases of some of the major open problems of mathematics, such as the BSD conjecture, Bloch–Kato conjecture, and Iwasawa main conjecture.

However, in the application of Euler systems to Iwasawa theory, an important stumbling block arises. Euler systems work very well for bounding Selmer groups of
p
-adic Galois representations defined by
strict
local conditions at
p
; or more generally local conditions of
Greenberg type
, in which the local condition is given by the orthogonal complement of a subrepresentation stable under the decomposition group at
p
. For applications to the Iwasawa main conjecture, one needs this subrepresentation to be of a specific size: for global Galois representations arising from cohomological, cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GL_{n}
with
n
even, the required subrepresentation should have dimension
\tfrac{n}{2}+1
(serving as a “stepping stone” to the Selmer group appearing in the main conjecture itself, which should be defined by a subrepresentation of dimension
\frac{n}{2}
).

The existence of such subrepresentations is a rather restrictive condition: it frequently occurs that no such subrepresentation exists, e.g. if the Galois representation is locally irreducible at
p
, and these cases cannot be handled in the framework of Greenberg Selmer groups. The highly influential work of Pottharst
[
Pot13
]
allows one to define and study a much more general class of Selmer groups, using submodules of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules in place of local subrepresentations. This allows a robust and natural
formulation
of the Iwasawa main conjecture, applying to any Galois representation which is crystalline at
p
(and many that are not). However, it has not previously been possible to use Euler systems to bound Pottharst’s Selmer groups. That is the main goal of this article.

1.2.
Local conditions and Kolyvagin systems

As input to our construction, we assume we have an Euler system which satisfies a non-trivial Pottharst-type local condition; we would like to obtain from this a bound for the Selmer group with that local condition.

However, the actual bounds for Selmer groups are obtained by using an intermediate object, a
Kolyvagin system
, obtained from the Euler system via Kolyvagin’s “derivative” construction. This raises the problem:
if an Euler system satisfies some non-trivial local condition, is this reflected in additional local properties of the Kolyvagin system derived from it?
This question is less straightforward than it might seem, for the following fundamental reason: Kolyvagin classes are “mod
p^{n}
” objects, taking values in the mod
p^{n}
reduction of a Galois representation; whereas Pottharst’s Selmer groups are “
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-linear” objects, with no natural integral structures. In order to bridge this gap we crucially use an insight originally due to Sweeting
[
Swe20
]
, which is that Kolyvagin classes can be “patched together” using
non-principal ultrafilters
in order to give characteristic 0 limiting objects. These are the “ultra-Kolyvagin systems” of the paper’s title. Assuming that the local condition satisfied by the Euler system is ‘closed’ (in the sense of Conjecture
2.11
), we can show that it propagates to the Kolyvagin system (Theorem
6.8
), which allows us to use the Euler system machine to directly obtain a bound of Selmer groups with the orthogonal local condition.

Checking the ‘closedness’ of local conditions which arise naturally is a delicate problem in
p
-adic Hodge theory. We check this property for Bloch–Kato “
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}
” and “
H^{1}_{\mathrm{g}}
” local conditions (Corollary
4.12
); and for Pottharst local conditions arising from (potentially non-étale) sub-
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules we prove in Corollary
4.6
a slightly weaker result that turns out to be sufficient for Iwasawa-theoretic applications.

1.3.
Applications

As a first application of our machinery, we consider the Euler system of
[
LZ16
]
for the Rankin–Selberg convolution of two non-ordinary modular forms. For this case, the strongest result avalable with previous methods was to show that the
support
of the Selmer group in the Iwasawa main conjecture is contained in the vanishing locus of the
p
-adic
L
-function. With our methods we are able to upgrade this to an actual divisibility, thus proving the “Euler system half” of the Iwasawa main conjecture in this context. Similar arguments can be applied to numerous other Euler systems (e.g. for
\GSp_{4}
, or for quadratic Hilbert modular forms); details will appear in subsequent works.

(The following argument is due to Berger and Steingart, and we are grateful to them for sharing it with us.) First we claim that

We now apply the first half of the proof of Lemma 2.6 in

3.1.
\varphi
-modules

By a
\varphi
-module over
F
, we mean a finite-dimensional
F
-vector space

with a bijective semilinear Frobenius
\varphi
(acting as the arithmetic Frobenius on

Proposition 3.1
.

If
D_{0}
is any
\varphi
-module over
F
, then
(1-\varphi)D_{0}=D_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
D_{0}
has a slope decomposition by the Dieudonné–Manin theorem, we can assume
D_{0}
is isoclinic (has a single slope). For non-zero slopes the result is clear, since either
\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\varphi^{n}
or
-\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\varphi^{-1-n}
converges to an inverse of
1-\varphi
. So we may assume
D_{0}
is the base-extension of a
\varphi
-module over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, and by dévissage we reduce to the case of
\varphi
-modules over the residue field
\mathbf{k}_{F}
.

After base-extension to
\overline{\mathbf{k}}_{F}
, there exists a
\varphi
-invariant basis by Hilbert’s theorem 90; and since solving
x-x^{p}=y
for a given
y\in\overline{\mathbf{k}}_{F}
amounts to solving a separable polynomial, we see that
1-\varphi
is surjective on
\overline{\mathbf{k}}_{F}^{\oplus d}
. Thus for the original field
\mathbf{k}_{F}
, we can always find solutions after a finite extension of
\mathbf{k}_{F}
; but this extension necessarily has prime-to-
p
degree, so we obtain a solution over
\mathbf{k}_{F}
by taking the trace.
∎

We now study the modules
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{F}\otimes_{F}D_{0}
and
\mathcal{R}_{F}\otimes_{F}D_{0}
.

Proposition 3.2

(Chenevier, Nakamura)
.

Let

be any
\varphi
-module over
F
.

(1)

1-\varphi
is surjective on
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{F}\otimes D_{0}
, and its kernel is given by
\bigoplus_{k\geqslant 0}t^{k}\otimes D_{0}^{(\varphi=p^{-k})}
.

(2)

is surjective on
\mathcal{R}_{F}^{+}\otimes_{F}D_{0}
and on
\mathcal{R}_{F}\otimes_{F}D_{0}
.

(3)

We have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}_{F}\otimes_{F}D_{0}\right)^{\psi=1}\,/\,\left(\mathcal{R}_{F}^{+}\otimes_{F}D_{0}\right)^{\psi=1}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{k\geqslant 0}x^{k}\otimes D_{0}^{(\varphi=p^{k})},

where

satisfies
\gamma(x)=\chi^{-(1+k)}(\gamma)\cdot x
.

Proof.

See Lemma 3.17 of
[

(generalizing

A

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{F}\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{R}^{+}_{F}\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}).

\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
and

is an

(with a semilinear action of

Suppose henceforth that
\mathcal{D}
is crystalline, so that
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
is a
\varphi
-module over
F
. We equip
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
with a filtration via the natural embedding into

for

, as in
[

]
; then, in accordance with the usual conventions the

are the negatives of the jumps in this filtration. With these definitions, if
\mathcal{D}=\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)
, then
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
is canonically isomorphic (as a filtered
\varphi

Proposition 3.4
.

If the Hodge–Tate weights of
\mathcal{D}
are in the interval
[a,b]
, then
t^{b}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\mathcal{D}\subseteq t^{a}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
.

Proof.

The assumption that the Hodge–Tate weights are
\leqslant b
means that
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq t^{-b}\mathcal{D}
, and hence
\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq t^{-b}\mathcal{D}
. The reverse inequality follows by applying the above to
\mathcal{D}^{*}=\Hom_{\mathcal{R}_{F}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{R}_{F})
.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

We let
I_{\mathcal{D}}
be the invertible ideal of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}(\Gamma)
that is the support of the quotient

\frac{\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}}{\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}\cap\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})}.

Note that
I_{\mathcal{D}}
is supported at a finite direct sum of (negative) powers of the cyclotomic character, by
Proposition

3.2
, since we have
\frac{\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}}{\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}\cap\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})}\ \subseteq\ \frac{\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=1}}{\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=1}}
.

Definition 3.6
.

The
Perrin-Riou regulator
for
\mathcal{D}
is the map

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}\cap\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})\xrightarrow{(1-\varphi)}\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=0}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\mathcal{H}_{F}(\Gamma)\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}),

which we can extend in the obvious way to

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}\to I_{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}\mathcal{H}_{F}(\Gamma)\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}).

See e.g.
[
LLZ11
]
; it is assumed in
op.cit.
that
F/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is a finite extension, but the theory works without change in the generality here.

Proposition 3.7
.

We have

\ker(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}})=\bigoplus_{k\geqslant 0}t^{k}\otimes(\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\varphi=p^{-k}}\cap\Fil^{-k}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})),

so the support of
\ker(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}})
consists of the characters
\chi^{k}
, for integers
k\geqslant 0
such that
H^{0}(\mathcal{D}(-k))\neq 0
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\kappa
be a character
\Gamma\to E^{\times}
which is not in the support of the ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}}
. Then we let

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}:H^{1}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))^{+}=\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}/(\gamma-\kappa(\gamma))\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})

denote the map induced by the composite of the Perrin-Riou regulator and evaluation at
\kappa
.

Remark 3.9
.

Note that the maps
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}(1),\kappa}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa\chi^{-1}}
have the same source and target, but are not equal; rather, assuming both maps are defined, we have

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}(1),\kappa}=\kappa^{\prime}(1)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa\chi^{-1}},

so in particular
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}(1),\kappa}=0
for all locally-constant characters
\kappa
.

3.4.
Bijectivity for almost all
\kappa

We now show that for “most” characters
\kappa
, the map
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}
is a bijection, assuming
F/\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
is infinite. From
Proposition

3.2
we have the following:

Proposition 3.10
.

The map

1-\varphi:\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=1}\longrightarrow\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=0}\cong\mathcal{H}_{F}(\Gamma)\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})

is surjective.

Notation 3.11
.

Let
\nabla\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}(\Gamma)
denote the “differentiate at 1” distribution, defined by
\frac{\log[\gamma]}{\log\chi(\gamma)}
for any non-torsion
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Proposition 3.12
.

The modules
\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=1}/\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}
and
\mathcal{D}/(\psi-1)\mathcal{D}
are annihilated by
\nabla(\nabla-1)\dots(\nabla-b+1)\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

From
[
Nak14
, Theorem 3.5 (3)]
we have the relation

(\nabla-k)(t^{k}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}))\subseteq t^{k+1}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}).

Since
\mathcal{D}^{\psi=1}
is sandwiched between
\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=1}
and
t^{b}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\psi=1}
, the first formula follows. The second follows from this together with the fact that
(\psi-1)\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})=\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
\kappa
be a character with the following properties:

•

\kappa^{\prime}(1)\notin\{0,1,\dots,b-1\}
,

•

if
\kappa=\chi^{-(1+k)}
for
k\geqslant 0
, then
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\varphi=p^{k}}=0
(so
\kappa
is not in the support of
I_{\mathcal{D}}
),

•

if
\kappa=\chi^{k}
for
k\geqslant 0
, then
H^{0}(\mathcal{D}(-k))=0
(so
\kappa
is not in the support of
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}
).

Then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}
gives a bijection

H^{1}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))\cong\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}).

∎

Remark 3.14
.

The corollary also holds as stated for
F/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
finite, although the proof is slightly different: it is no longer true that
1-p^{k}\varphi
is always surjective on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
, but (for dimension reasons) if it is injective then it is also surjective, and this is sufficient.

3.5.
The regulator topology

We now recall that since
F
is a complete non-discrete normed field, any finite-dimensional
F
-vector space has a canonical
F
-Banach-space topology (the topology coming from any choice of
F
-vector-space isomorphism with
F^{\oplus d}
). We now no longer assume that
\mathcal{D}
has Hodge–Tate weights
\geqslant 0
.

Definition 3.15
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}
is crystalline with Hodge–Tate weights in
[a,b]
, for some
a\leqslant b\in\mathbf{Z}
; and let
\kappa
be a character such that

•

\kappa^{\prime}(1)\notin\{a,\dots,b-1\}
;

•

if
\kappa=\chi^{-(1+k)}
for
k\geqslant-a
, then
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})^{\varphi=p^{k}}=0
;

•

if
\kappa=\chi^{k}
for
k\geqslant a
, then
H^{0}(\mathcal{D}(-k))=0
.

Then we define the
regulator topology
on
H^{1}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))
to be the unique topology such that the regulator map

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}(-a),\kappa\chi^{-a}}:H^{1}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}),

(which is a bijection by
Corollary

3.13
) is a homeomorphism, for the canonical
F
-Banach topology on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D})
.

This topology does not depend on the choice of
a
, because
Remark

3.9
shows that the regulator maps for different choices differ by a nonzero scalar (and hence induce the same topology).

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
be a morphism of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules, with
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
crystalline.

(1)

The set of
\kappa
such that the regulator topologies are defined for both
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\kappa)
and
(\mathcal{D}_{2},\kappa)
is the complement of a discrete closed subset of the rigid-analytic space
\Hom(\Gamma,\mathbf{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{rig}})
of characters of
\Gamma
.

(2)

For any
\kappa
satisfying this condition, the map

H^{1}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\kappa^{-1}))\to H^{1}(\mathcal{D}_{2}(\kappa^{-1}))

is continuous and strict, with closed image, for the regulator topologies on both sides.

Proof.

The first statement is clear, since the second and third conditions on
\kappa
only rule out finitely many characters, and the set of characters with
\kappa^{\prime}(1)=m
is discrete for any
m
.

For the second statement: we may assume without loss of generality that both of the
\mathcal{D}_{i}
have Hodge–Tate weights
\geqslant 0
. From the construction of the regulator map it is clear that we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\kappa^{-1}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathcal{D}_{2}(\kappa^{-1}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\kappa^{-1}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}_{2}(\kappa^{-1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}_{1},\kappa}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}_{2},\kappa}}

in which the map
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}_{1})\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}_{2})
is
F
-linear, and hence continuous and strict with closed image for the canonical
F
-Banach topology. By definition, the vertical maps are homeomorphisms. Since any
F
-linear map between finite-dimensional
F
-vector spaces is continuous and strict with closed image for the canonical topology, and the map
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}_{1})\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{D}_{2})
is
F
-linear, we are done.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

Note that we do
not
claim that the regulator topology on
H^{1}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))
coincides with the topology induced, via the Herr complex, from the natural topology on
\mathcal{D}
itself (which is a direct limit of Fréchet spaces). Of course it is natural to expect that these topologies should agree, but this does not seem to be straightforward to prove; and we shall not use the topology on
H^{1}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))
induced from that of
\mathcal{D}
in the present work.

4.
Integral theory

In this section we take
\mathcal{D}=\mathbf{D}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)
for a crystalline representation
V
with Hodge–Tate weights in
[0,b]
, and show that the topology on
H^{1}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))
in
Corollary

3.16
coincides with the Banach-space topology of
H^{1}(F,V(\kappa^{-1})))
. This is obvious for
F/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
finite, so we shall assume
F
is a finite extension of
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}
.

4.1.
Wach modules

Write
\mathbf{N}(V)
for the Wach module of
V
in the sense of
[
Ber04
]
; and for
T\subset V
a choice of
\mathcal{O}
-lattice, write
\mathbf{N}(T)
for the corresponding Wach module, so that
\mathbf{N}(V)=\mathbf{N}(T)[1/p]
.

Then
\mathbf{N}(T)
is a finite-rank
\mathcal{O}_{F}[[\pi]]
-submodule of
\mathbf{D}^{{\dagger}}(T)
, free of rank
d\coloneqq\dim V
, with the following properties:

(1)

We have

q^{b}\varphi^{*}\mathbf{N}(T)\subseteq\mathbf{N}(T)\subseteq\varphi^{*}\mathbf{N}(T),

where
q=\varphi(\pi)/\pi
.

(2)

\Gamma
acts trivially on
\mathbf{N}(T)/\pi\mathbf{N}(T)
.

(3)

as submodules of
\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)[1/t]
we have the inclusion

\mathbf{N}(V)\subseteq\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=\mathcal{R}^{+}_{F}\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V).

(4)

the above inclusion induces an isomorphism

\mathbf{N}(V)/\pi\mathbf{N}(V)\cong\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V).

As shown in the appendix of
[
Ber03
]
, we have
\mathbf{D}(T)^{\psi=1}\subseteq\pi^{-1}\mathbf{N}(T)
; and the image of
\mathbf{D}(T)^{\psi=1}
in
\pi^{-1}\mathbf{N}(V)/\mathbf{N}(V)
is contained in
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\varphi=1}
, with
\Gamma
acting as
\chi^{-1}
, so if
V
has no quotient isomorphic to
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
then
\mathbf{D}(T)^{\psi=1}\subseteq\mathbf{N}(T)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
T
as above,
the module
\left(\varphi^{*}\mathbf{N}(T)\right)^{\psi=0}
is free of rank
d
over
\mathcal{O}_{F}[[\Gamma]]
. More precisely, we may find an
\mathcal{O}_{F}[[\pi]]
-basis
(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})
of
\mathbf{N}(T)
such that the vectors
\Big((1+\pi)\varphi(n_{i}):i=1,\dots,d\Big)
are an
\mathcal{O}_{F}[[\Gamma]]
-basis of
\left(\varphi^{*}\mathbf{N}(T)\right)^{\psi=0}
.

Proof.

This is proved for
F=\mathbf{Q}_{p}
in §3 of
[
LLZ10
]
, and the argument adapts to general
F
without change.
∎

It follows that the image of the composite map

\left(\varphi^{*}\mathbf{N}(T)\right)^{\psi=0}\hookrightarrow(\mathcal{R}^{+}_{F}\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V))^{\psi=0}\cong\mathcal{H}_{F}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)

is contained in the
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}
-submodule of the right-hand side generated by the finitely many elements
(1+\pi)\varphi(n_{i})
. Composing with
1-\varphi
, we see that the image of
\mathbf{N}(T)^{\psi=1}
is also contained in this submodule.

4.2.
Coleman maps

Let us fix an
\mathcal{O}_{F}[[\Gamma]]
-basis
(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d})
of
\left(\varphi^{*}\mathbf{N}(T)\right)^{\psi=0}
. This gives us a
Coleman map

\mathbf{N}(T)^{\psi=1}\xrightarrow{(1-\varphi)}\left(\varphi^{*}\mathbf{N}(T)\right)^{\psi=0}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Col}}\mathcal{O}_{F}[[\Gamma]]^{\oplus d}.

We want to compare this with the map

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\psi=1}\xrightarrow{(1-\varphi)}\mathcal{H}_{F}(\Gamma)\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)\cong\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)^{\oplus d},

where the last isomorphism comes from a choice of
F
-basis of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
. Exactly as in
[
LLZ10
,
LLZ11
]
we have the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

The two maps above are related by the composition of the inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{F}[[\Gamma]]^{\oplus d}\subset\mathcal{H}_{F}(\Gamma)^{\oplus d}
and multiplication by a “matrix of logarithms”
M_{\log}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d\times d}(\mathcal{H}_{F}(\Gamma))
.

We now consider evaluation at a character
\kappa
.

Proposition 4.3
.

The image of
\mathbf{N}(T)^{\psi=1}/(\gamma-\kappa(\gamma))
under
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}
is contained in a finitely-generated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-submodule of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
.

Proof.

This is clear since the composite of
\operatorname{Col}
and evaluation at
\kappa
is contained in
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\oplus d}
, and the image of this submodule under
\kappa(M_{\log})\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d\times d}(F)
is still finitely-generated.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\kappa
be a continuous character of
\Gamma
, and if
\kappa=\chi^{-1}
then assume that
V
has no quotient isomorphic to the trivial representation. Then the map

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}:H^{1}(F,V(\kappa^{-1}))\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)

is continuous, where
H^{1}(F,V(\kappa^{-1}))
has the topology from
Proposition

2.1
and
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
the canonical
F
-Banach topology.

Proof.

We consider the image under
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}
of the unit ball in
H^{1}(F,V(\kappa^{-1}))
. This unit ball is precisely the image modulo torsion of
\mathbf{D}(T)^{\psi=1}/(\gamma-\kappa(\gamma))
. By assumption we have either
\kappa\neq\chi^{-1}
or
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\varphi=1}=0
, so up to scaling by a non-zero constant, this ball coincides with the image of
\mathbf{N}(T)^{\psi=1}/(\gamma-\kappa(\gamma))
. Hence its image under
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}
is contained in a finitely-generated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-submodule, and thus is bounded for any
F
-Banach norm on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

If
\kappa
satisfies the conditions of
Corollary

3.13
, then the map

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}:H^{1}(F,V(\kappa^{-1}))\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)

is a topological isomorphism, where
H^{1}(F,V(\kappa^{-1}))
has the topology from
Proposition

2.1
and
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
the canonical
F
-Banach topology.

Proof.

Under these conditions,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D},\kappa}
is both continuous and bijective. By the Banach open mapping theorem, a continuous and bijective
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-linear map between
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-Banach spaces must be a topological isomorphism.
∎

This shows that when
\mathcal{D}=\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)
, the regulator topology on
H^{1}(F,\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))
actually coincides with the Banach-space topology on
H^{1}(F,V)
from
Proposition

2.1
. This proves the following special case of the “Pessimistic Conjecture”:

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\mathcal{D}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{D}=\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)
is a saturated
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-submodule, and
\kappa
is a character satisfying the conditions of
Corollary

3.13
, then the image of
H^{1}(F,\mathcal{D}^{+}(\kappa^{-1})\to H^{1}(F,\mathcal{D}(\kappa^{-1}))\cong H^{1}(F,V)
is closed (with respect to the Banach-space topology of
H^{1}(F,V)
defined in
Proposition

2.1
). ∎

4.3.
Bloch–Kato subspaces

Recall that the Bloch–Kato local conditions
H^{1}_{\mathrm{e}}(F,V)
,
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(F,V)
and
H^{1}_{\mathrm{g}}(F,V)
are the kernels of the natural maps to
H^{1}(F,V\otimes\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\varphi=1})
,
H^{1}(F,V\otimes\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{cris}})
, and
H^{1}(F,V\otimes\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}})
respectively. The groups
H^{1}_{\mathrm{e}}
and
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}
can be studied via the well-known exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(F,V)\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)\oplus t(V)\to H^{1}(F,V),

where
t(V)=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)/\Fil^{0}
, and the middle map is
x\mapsto\left((1-\varphi)x,x\bmod\Fil^{0}\right)
. By construction, the image of the boundary map is
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}
, and the image of its restriction to
t(V)
is
H^{1}_{\mathrm{e}}
.

The above paragraph is valid for any discretely-valued closed subfield of
\mathbf{C}_{p}
and any representation
V
; but in the present situation, with
F
a finite unramified extension of
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
and
V
crystalline, we have a simplification since
(1-\varphi)\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
. It follows that
H^{1}_{\mathrm{e}}(F,V)=H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(F,V)
, and we have an isomorphism (the Bloch–Kato exponential)

\exp_{F,V}:\frac{\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)}{\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\varphi=1}+\Fil^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(F,V).

The subspace
H^{1}_{\mathrm{g}}
has a somewhat more direct description as the kernel of the Bloch–Kato dual exponential map

\exp^{*}_{F,V}:H^{1}(F,V)\to\Fil^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V).

Remark 4.7
.

Note that when
F
is a finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, it is conventional to define
\exp^{*}_{F,V}
as the transpose of
\exp_{F,V^{*}(1)}
(hence the name “dual exponential”), and what we are calling
\exp^{*}_{F,V}
would accordingly be called
\exp^{*}_{F,V^{*}(1)}
. This definition is not available in our case, since local Tate duality does not hold over infinite unramified extensions. However, a much more direct definition, not requiring the residue field to be finite, is given in
[
Kat93
]
, using the fact that cup-product with
\log\chi\in H^{1}(F,\mathbf{Q}_{p})
gives a canonical isomorphism between
H^{0}(F,V\otimes\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}})=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
and
H^{1}(F,V\otimes\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}})
.

We now express these in terms of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules, assuming
V
crystalline (but not necessarily with Hodge–Tate weights
\geqslant 0
). Note that there is a canonical map
\mathcal{R}_{F}^{+}\to F[[t]]
, mapping
\pi
to
\exp(t)-1
.

Definition 4.8
.

For
y\in\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)[1/t]
, let
\partial_{V}(y)\in\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
denote the constant term of
y
, as a power series in
F((t))\otimes_{F}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
.

We shall apply this with
y\in\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)^{\psi=1}
; then
\partial_{V}(y)
factors through the image of
y
in
H^{1}(F,V)
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The map

\partial_{V}:H^{1}(F,V)\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)

is continuous.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the image under this map of
H^{1}(F,T)
, for any lattice
T\subset V
, is contained in a finitely-generated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-submodule of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
. But
H^{1}(F,V)
is the image of
\mathbf{D}(T)^{\psi=1}
, which is contained in
\pi^{a-1}\mathbf{N}(T)
; and
\pi\mathbf{N}(T)
maps to zero. Hence
\partial_{V}
factors through a finite-rank quotient
\pi^{a-1}\mathbf{N}(T)/\pi\mathbf{N}(T)
.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

The kernel of
\partial_{V}:H^{1}(F,V)\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
is
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(F,V)
.

Proof.

Let
y\in H^{1}(F,V)=\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)^{\psi=1}/(\gamma-1)
. Let
h\geqslant 1
be such that
\Fil^{-h}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
(so all Hodge–Tate weights of
V
are
\leqslant h
).

Claim
:
\partial_{V}(y)=0
if and only if there exists a
\tilde{y}\in\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)^{\psi=1}
representing
y
and such that
\tilde{y}\in\nabla(\nabla-1)\dots(\nabla-h+1)\cdot\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\psi=1}
.

Proof of claim
: Since the distribution
\frac{\nabla(\nabla-1)\dots(\nabla-h+1)}{\gamma-1}
is coprime to
\gamma-1
, it suffices to show that
\partial_{V}(y)=0
iff there is a representative
\tilde{y}
lying in
(\gamma-1)\cdot\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\psi=1}
. Clearly if such a
\tilde{y}
exists then
\partial_{V}(y)=0
.

Conversely, if
\partial_{V}(y)=0
, then we can find a representative
\tilde{y}\in\left(\pi\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)\right)^{\psi=1}
. Thus
(1-\varphi)(\tilde{y})\in\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
vanishes at the trivial character; using
Proposition

3.10
we obtain an element
z\in\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\psi=1}
such that
(1-\varphi)(\tilde{y})=(\gamma-1)(1-\varphi)z
. Thus
\tilde{y}-(\gamma-1)z\in\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\varphi=1}\cap\pi\mathbf{N}^{+}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=\bigoplus_{k\geqslant 1}t^{k}\otimes\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)^{\varphi=p^{-k}}
(with the term for
k=0
excluded). Since
\gamma-1
is clearly surjective on this module, it follows that
\tilde{y}
is divisible by
\gamma-1
as required.

With this in hand, we may complete the proof. If
\partial_{V}(y)=0
, then
[
Ber03
, Theorem II.2]
gives us an explicit element of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
whose image under
\exp_{F,V}
is
y
. Conversely, if
y\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(F,V)
, then we can write
y=\exp_{F,V}((1-p^{-1}\varphi^{-1})w)

Corollary 4.12

The spaces

Proof.

8.
Characteristic ideals and integral structures

We now embark on the proof of

corresponds to a character

8.1.
Module-theoretic preliminaries

Since
\kappa
is continuous, we must have
|\kappa(\gamma_{1})-1|<1
, where
\gamma_{1}
generates the subgroup
\Gamma_{1}\cong(1+p\mathbf{Z}_{p})^{\times}\subset\Gamma
. Since
\kappa
is also
E
-valued, we must have
|\kappa(\gamma_{1})-1|\leqslant|\varpi|
. Thus

extends to the completion

of

, where

is the idempotent determined by the restriction of
\kappa
to
\Gamma_{\mathrm{tors}}
, and

.

Definition 8.1
.

By a

tending to

, we mean a sequence of characters
\kappa_{n}:\Gamma\to E^{\times}
for

, all agreeing with

on
\Gamma_{\tors}
, such that
\kappa_{n}\to\kappa
as

and
\kappa_{n}\neq\kappa
for all

. We write

for the maximal ideal corresponding to

(For instance, we can take

to be the character sending

to
\lambda+\varpi^{n}
, where
\lambda=\frac{\kappa(\gamma_{1})-1}{\varpi}
.)

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
A^{\circ}
be the integral Tate algebra
\mathcal{O}\langle X\rangle
, and
M^{\circ}
a finitely-generated
A^{\circ}
-module. Then, for any good sequence
(\kappa_{n})\to\kappa
, we have the following:

(1)

For all but finitely many

, the submodule
M^{\circ}[\mathfrak{P}_{n}]
is finite and
M^{\circ}/\mathfrak{P}_{n}M^{\circ}
has
\mathcal{O}
-rank equal to the
A

, where
M=M^{\circ}[1/p]
.

(2)

The orders of the groups
M^{\circ}[\mathfrak{P}_{n}]
are uniformly bounded as

.

(3)

The

-torsion submodule
\left(M^{\circ}/\mathfrak{P}_{n}M^{\circ}\right)_{\text{$p$-tors}}
is finite for all

, and as

\ell_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\left(M^{\circ}/\mathfrak{P}_{n}M^{\circ}\right)_{\text{$p$-tors}}\right)=h\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\kappa_{n}(\gamma_{1})-\kappa(\gamma_{1})\right)+O(1),

where

.

Notation 8.3
.

We express the conclusion of (iii) more concisely as “the groups
\left(M^{\circ}/\mathfrak{P}_{n}M^{\circ}\right)_{\text{$p$-tors}}
grow at rate

Proof.

For part (i), we note that

is a torsion module over a Dedekind domain and hence has finite support; and since
\kappa_{n}\to\kappa
and
\kappa_{n}\neq\kappa
for

, the ideal
\mathfrak{P}_{n}
is outside this support for all but finitely many

For (ii):

is finitely generated, and

is Noetherian; hence there is a maximal finite

-submodule of

, whose order is clearly an upper bound for the

once

is large enough that this group is finite.

For (iii), using the fact that

is a Dedekind domain, we can readily reduce to the case when

is either a free module, a module annihilated by

for some

, or a module of the form

where

is a monic polynomial over

. The first two cases are obvious, since

and the left-hand side is bounded; and for the last case,

, where

. For

the valuation of

is precisely

, where

is the order of vanishing of

at

generates the characteristic ideal of

∎

Remark 8.4
.

Observe that part (ii) holds whether or not

itself is in the support of

; thus the

-isotypic

-isotypic

-modules which is an isomorphism after inverting

, and we let
\Sigma
be a finite set of primes containing all those dividing
cpN_{f}N_{g}
.

Proposition 9.7

(
[
LZ16
, Theorem 8.1.4]
)
.

There exists an analytic Euler system
(c_{m})_{m\geqslant 1,\,(m,pc=1)}
for
V
, in the sense of
Definition

7.4
, with
c_{m}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{an},\Sigma}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}(m),V)
. Moreover, the Euler system
(c_{m})
satisfies the
\mathcal{D}^{+}
local condition at
p
.

Proof.

This is a restatement of Theorem 7.1.2 of
[
LZ16
]
.
∎

Remark 9.8
.

Note that since
V
is 4-dimensional, a rank 3 local-condition module is the correct rank for
Theorem

7.9
to apply. This submodule
\mathcal{D}^{+}
arises from a subrepresentation if, and only if,
f
and
g
are both ordinary and
\alpha_{f},\alpha_{g}
are the unit roots of the Hecke polynomials, which is the setting studied in
[
KLZ17
]
.

Theorem 9.9
.

The image of
c_{1}
under the map

\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{+})\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},\mathcal{D}^{+}/\mathcal{D}^{++})\to\mathcal{H}(\Gamma),

where the third map is the Perrin-Riou regulator for the one-dimensional
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module
\mathcal{D}^{+}/\mathcal{D}^{++}
, sends
c_{1}
to the
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p,\alpha_{f}}(f,g)
.

Proof.

This explicit reciprocity law was proved in
[
LZ16
]
with the reservation that the
p
-adic
L
-function used in
op.cit.
was only known to satisfy the interpolation property (i) of
Theorem

9.6
when
\chi=1
. However, since these
\chi=1
specialisations are sufficient to uniquely determine the 3-variable
p
-adic
L
-function once

and
g
are allowed to varying in families, this
p
-adic

-function must agree with the one constructed in
[
GPRJ25
]
.
∎

Corollary 9.10
.

Assume that
r-r^{\prime}\geqslant 2
. Then
L_{p,\alpha_{f}}(f,g)
is not a zero-divisor, and hence
e_{\eta}c_{1}\neq 0
for all
\eta
.

Proof.

See Remark 11.6.5 in
[
KLZ17
]
; although ordinarity is assumed in
op.cit.
, the same argument also applies in the non-ordinary case.
∎

Applying Theorem
7.7
, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 9.11
.

If
r-r^{\prime}=1
, then assume
L_{p,\alpha_{f}}(f,g)
is not a zero-divisor. Then
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{+})
is
\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)
-torsion, and

\mathrm{rk}_{\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)}\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{+})=1.

Moreover, we have the divisibilities of characteristic ideals

\operatorname{char}_{\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)}\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{+})\mathrel{\Big|}\operatorname{char}_{\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)}\left(\frac{\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{+})}{\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)\cdot c_{1}}\right)

and

\operatorname{char}_{\mathcal{H}(\Gamma)}\widetilde{H}^{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{++})\mathrel{\Big|}L_{p,\alpha_{f}}(f,g),

and equality holds in the first divisibility iff it holds in the second.

Proof.

The rank statement and the first divisibility are precisely the result of
Theorem

7.9
in the present setting; the assumption
9.3
(3) implies that the “error terms”
\mathcal{E}^{1}
and
\mathcal{E}^{2}
are trivial.

The second divisibility (and the condition for equality) follows from the first using the exact triangle

\widetilde{R\Gamma}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{++})\to\widetilde{R\Gamma}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V,\mathcal{D}^{+})\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},\mathcal{D}^{+}/\mathcal{D}^{++})\to[+1]

and the trivialization of the third complex given by the Perrin-Riou regulator map (exactly as in §11.6 of
[
KLZ17
]
in the ordinary case); again, the “no extra zero” assumption assumption
9.3
(3) shows that the regulator map is a bijection.
∎

9.5.
Generalisations

We briefly sketch some other cases in which the same methods can be applied (full details will appear elsewhere).

Asai
L
-functions

Let
F
be a real quadratic field, and let
p>3
be a prime which splits in
F
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a quadratic Hilbert modular form of weight
(k_{1},k_{2})
with
k_{1}>k_{2}\geqslant 2
and level coprime to
p
, and take for
V
the dual of the Asai Galois representation associated to
\mathcal{F}
(the tensor induction of the standard 2-dimensional representation). We fix choices of roots
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
of the Hecke polynomials at the primes above
p
(which are the analogues of the
\alpha_{f},\alpha_{g}
above), which we assume to be sufficiently small, i.e.
v_{p}(\alpha_{i})<k_{i}-1
.

In this case,
V
is again four-dimensional, odd, and crystalline at
p
; and the choice of the
\alpha_{i}
determines submodules
\mathcal{D}^{++}\subset\mathcal{D}^{+}\subset\mathbf{D}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)
of ranks 2 and 3 respectively. The Euler system constructed in
[
LLZ18
]
in the ordinary case can be extended to this more general finite-slope setting, and gives an analytic Euler system for
V
satisfying the local condition
\mathcal{D}^{+}
. Details of this construction will appear in the PhD thesis of Ana Marija Vego at ETH Zürich.

In this finite-slope setting, a
p
-adic Asai
L
-function was constructed by Kazi and the first author in
[
KL25
]
. Generalising the results in
[
GLZ25
]
for the ordinary case, it should be accessible to show that the image of this Euler system under the Perrin-Riou regulator for
\mathcal{D}^{+}/\mathcal{D}^{++}
is the
p
-adic
L
-function; and following the strategy above will then give one inclusion in the Main Conjecture in this setting.

The
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
case

Let
\Pi
be a cohomological, cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GSp_{4}
which is generic, and is not CAP or endoscopic, and such that
\Pi_{p}
is unramified Then we have a 4-dimensional Galois representation
V=V_{p}(\Pi)^{*}
associated to
\Pi
, which is crystalline at
p
.

If we fix a
p
-refinement of
\Pi_{p}
(a choice of one of the Weyl-group orbits of characters of the diagonal torus from which
\Pi_{p}
is induced) then this determines, as in the previous cases, a pair of submodules
\mathcal{D}^{++}\subset\mathcal{D}^{+}\subset\mathbf{D}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)
of ranks 2 and 3 respectively. Under a mild “non-critical slope” assumption on the
p
-refinement, Rockwood has constructed in
[
Roc26
]
an analytic Euler system for
V
(generalising the construction of
[
LSZ22
]
in the ordinary case). Via very similar arguments as used in
[
LZ16
]
in the Rankin–Selberg setting, one can check that Rockwood’s Euler system satifies the
\mathcal{D}^{+}
local condition at
p
, giving another natural example in which the hypotheses of
Theorem

7.9
are satisfied.

On the “analytic” side, one can construct a
p
-adic
L
-function for
\Pi
using higher Coleman theory, as explained in
[
LZ21
]
. (As explained in
[
LZ21
, §6]
, the main theorems of
op.cit.
assume ordinarity, but this is not needed for the
p
-adic
L
-function construction; in fact the only reason why ordinarity was assumed in
op.cit.
was precisely because we did not know at the time if it was possible to bound Pottharst-style Selmer groups via Euler systems.) Thus the methods of the present paper should allow one to prove the Euler-system divisibility of the Iwasawa main conjecture for
\GSp_{4}
at non-ordinary primes.

Remark 9.12
.

It also seems likely that these methods could be used to bound Selmer groups for the 8-dimensional Galois representations associated to
\GSp_{4}\times\GL_{2}
at non-ordinary primes, using the Euler system of
[
HJS20
]
and the
p
-adic
L
-functions constructed in
[
GR24
]
.

Localization and filtration in extended affine Lie algebras Thanks: This research was in part carried out in IPM-Isfahan Branch.
S. Azam

Abstract
. We investigate the notions of localization and filtration in the context of extended affine Lie algebras. Our primary objective is to develop a localization theory that facilitates the construction of meaningful local substructures, particularly local affine Lie subalgebras. These subalgebras play a crucial role in understanding the global structure of extended affine Lie algebras. It is noteworthy that the existence of appropriate local subalgebras, particularly affine Lie subalgebras, is also fundamental to the representation theory of extended affine Lie algebras. As a natural outcome of our localization approach, we also introduce a formal notion of filtration for a given extended affine Lie algebra. This study is motivated by our interest in modular theory, specifically the integral structures of extended affine Lie algebras.

1.
Introduction

Extended affine Lie algebras (EALAs) and extended affine root systems (EARSs) are higher-nullity generalizations of affine Lie algebras and root systems that preserve and extend many of their foundational structural properties. The class of EALAs was first introduced in 1990 under the name ”quasi-simple Lie algebras”
[
16
]
, and its theory was subsequently developed in 1997 under the now-standard terminology ”extended affine Lie algebras”
[
1
]
. The notion of EARSs was formally introduced earlier, in 1985, by K. Saito
[
25
]
.

A distinctive characteristic of EALAs and EARSs is their axiomatic definitions, which permit a unified and rigorous treatment. As expected, the root system of an EALA is itself an EARS. In this work, we focus our attention on EALAs of reduced type.

Like their affine counterparts, an EALA possesses a non-degenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form, a root space decomposition relative to a self-centralizing Cartan subalgebra, and the root system satisfying specific finiteness and discreteness conditions.

Since their introduction, two principal approaches have shaped the study of EARSs: the embedding of local finite root subsystems and the embedding of local affine subsystems. For instance, Saito and collaborators
[
25
]
adopted the latter perspective, using local affine subsystems as the foundation for their classification of EARSs. In contrast, the authors of
[
1
]
and subsequent works employed local finite subsystems. The finite perspective also appears in the study of associated Weyl groups, as one can see for example in
[
21
]
.

A powerful method in Lie theory involves analyzing local substructures with well-understood properties. The study of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-subalgebras has been particularly instrumental in uncovering the structure and representation theory of both finite and infinite-dimensional Lie algebras. This approach extends naturally to generalizations such as Kac-Moody and extended affine Lie algebras, as for example is illustrated in
[
1
, Chapter I]
.

Within extended affine Lie theory, both local and canonical subalgebras serve as essential analytical tools. Among canonical subalgebras, the
core
, defined as the subalgebra generated by non-isotropic root spaces, constitutes the structural backbone of the entire EALA and has been central to the structure theory and classification of EALAs since their introduction.

This article develops a localization theory for EALAs, inspired by our previous works on modular theory and integral structures in EALAs. The natural starting point is to consider a
closed
local subsystem of the root system of a given EALA, and look at the corresponding subalgebra. However, this subalgebra may fail to be an EALA. To address this, we introduce a method of cutting and pasting subspaces to obtain a (pure) extended affine subalgebra. Our construction of local extended affine Lie subalgebras reveals profound interactions with the core, establishing new connections between local and canonical substructures.

The central contribution of this work is the construction of local affine Lie subalgebras within a given extended affine Lie algebra of non-zero nullity, which again justifies the name EALAs for this class of Lie algebras. This construction provides a powerful tool for both structural analysis and representation theory, enabling classical techniques to be applied in localized settings. Our approach facilitates problem reduction to these well-understood subalgebras, followed by globalization of results. This strategy is inspired by and employed in our series of papers
[
6
,
5
,
8
,
11
,
4
]
on integral structures and Chevalley bases for EALAs.

Complementing our localization approach, we introduce a filtration technique that systematically decomposes the algebra based on a filtration of the underlying extended affine root system. As is well known from classical Lie theory, an increasing filtration makes a Lie algebra more tractable by replacing it with a graded version. In this spirit, we anticipate that the combined framework of localization and filtration will prove fruitful for analyzing the structure and representations of EALAs.

2.
Preliminaries

In this work, all vector spaces are considered over real or complex numbers. For a vector space
{\mathcal{V}}
equipped with a symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
, and a subset

T
of
{\mathcal{V}}
, we set

\begin{array}[]{l}T^{\times}:=\{\alpha\in{\mathcal{V}}\mid(\alpha,\alpha)\not=0\},\\
T^{0}:={\mathcal{V}}\setminus T^{\times},\\
T^{\perp}:=\{\alpha\in{\mathcal{V}}\mid(\alpha,T)=\{0\}\}.\end{array}

A subset
T\subseteq{\mathcal{V}}^{\times}
is called
connected
if
T
cannot be written in the form
T=T_{1}\cup T_{2}
with

(T_{1},T_{2})=\{0\}
where
T_{1}\not=\emptyset
and
T_{2}\not=\emptyset
.

For
\alpha\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\times}
, we set

\alpha^{\vee}:=\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha,\alpha)}.

The dual space of a vector space
{\mathcal{U}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{U}}^{\star}
.

Given a subset
S
of
{\mathcal{V}}
, we denote by
\langle S\rangle
the additive subgroup generated by
S
.

2.1.
Extended affine root systems

We recall the definition and some basic properties of extended affine root systems from
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

An
extended affine root system
is a triple

(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
where
{\mathcal{V}}
is a finite dimensional real vector space,
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a symmetric positive semi-definite bilinear form, and
R
is a subset of
{\mathcal{V}}
satisfying the following axioms:

(R1)
0\in R
,

(R2)
R=-R
,

(R3)
R
spans
{\mathcal{V}}
,

(R4)
\alpha\in R^{\times}\Rightarrow 2\alpha\not\in R
,

(R5)
R
is discrete in
{\mathcal{V}}
,

(R6) the
root string property
: for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
\beta\in R
, there exist non-negative integers
u,d
such that

(\beta+{\mathbb{Z}}\alpha)\cap R=\{\beta-d\alpha,\ldots,\beta,\ldots,\beta+u\alpha\}

with
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})=d-u
.

(R7) elements of
R^{0}
are
non-isolated
, i.e., for
\sigma\in R^{0}
there exists
\alpha\in R^{\times}
with
\alpha+\sigma\in R
.

(R8)
R^{\times}
is
connected
.

Let
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
be an extended affine root system. Define
\bar{{\mathcal{V}}}:={\mathcal{V}}/{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
, and let
\bar{\;}:{\mathcal{V}}\to\bar{{\mathcal{V}}}
be the canonical projection. Then the image
\bar{R}
of
R
under this map is an irreducible finite root system in
\bar{{\mathcal{V}}}
, with respect to the bilinear form induced by
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\bar{{\mathcal{V}}}
. The
type
and
rank
of
R
are defined to be the type and rank of
\bar{R}
, respectively. The
nullity
of
R
is defined to be the dimension of
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
.
Throughout this work, we assume that
R
is of reduced type
.

2.1.2
.

Given an EARS
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
, it follows that
R
contains a subset
\dot{R}\subseteq{\mathcal{V}}
such that
R\subseteq\dot{R}+{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
, where
\dot{R}
is a finite root system in
\dot{{\mathcal{V}}}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\dot{R}
, and is isomorphic to
\bar{R}
under the canonical projection. More specifically, we have:

R=(S+S)\cup(\dot{R}_{sh}+S)\cup(\dot{R}_{lg}+L),\quad\text{where }R^{0}=S+S,

for some subsets
S,L\subseteq{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
, called
semilattices
. Here
\dot{R}_{sh}
and
\dot{R}_{lg}
are the set of short and long roots of
\dot{R}
, respectively. If
\dot{R}
is simply laced, then every root is regarded as short (i.e.,
\dot{R}^{\times}=\dot{R}_{sh}
), and the term
\dot{R}_{lg}+L
is considered to be empty. If
\dot{R}_{lg}\not=\emptyset
, the semilattices
S
and
L
interact as

(2.1)

\langle L\rangle+S=S\quad\hbox{and}\quad 2\langle S\rangle+L=L.

For more details about semilattices we refer the reader to
[
1
, Chapter II.§1]

and
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

Let
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
be an extended affine root system.

(i)
We call a subset
R^{\prime}
of
R
a
subsystem
of
R
if the triple
(R^{\prime},(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime},{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
is an extended affine root system, where

{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R^{\prime}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime}
is the restriction to
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}
of
(\cdot,\cdot)
. In other words, an extended affine root system
(R^{\prime},(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime},{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
is called a subsystem of
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
if
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
and
(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{|_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}}}.

To indicate that
(R^{\prime},(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime},{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
is a subsystem of
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
, we simply write
(R^{\prime},(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime},{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})\subseteq(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
.

(ii) We call a subsystem
R^{\prime}
of
R

closed
if
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\prime}
,
\alpha+\beta\in R
implies
\alpha+\beta\in R^{\prime}
. Symbolically this is expressed as
(R^{\prime}+R^{\prime})\cap R\subseteq R^{\prime}
. We further define
R^{\prime}
to be a
real-closed root subsystem

if for any
\alpha,\beta\in\ R^{\prime}
with
\alpha+\beta\in R
, the following implication holds:
If at least one of

\alpha
or
\beta
is non-isotropic then
\alpha+\beta\in R^{\prime}
. In other words, if

R^{\prime}
is closed under root addition whenever the sum is a root and at least one summand is non-isotropic. Symbolically, this can be stated as
(R^{\prime}+{R^{\prime}}^{\times})\cap R\subseteq R^{\prime}
.

We note that a subsystem of an irreducible root system, in general, is not closed even when it is finite and irreducible. A classical example is the
G_{2}
root system. Its short roots form an irreducible
A_{2}
subsystem. However, this subsystem is not closed because the sum of two short roots can be a long root of
G_{2}
. The study of “closed” and “real-closed” subsystems requires independent consideration; see for example
[
15
,
14
,
18
]
.

2.1.4
.

There are some canonical ways of constructing a subsystem of an EARS
R
. Here, we describe one that is relevant to our purpose. Let
T
be a non-empty connected subset of
R^{\times}
. Set

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathcal{V}}_{T}:=\hfil\lx@intercol\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}T,\\
\tilde{R}_{T}^{\times}:=\langle T\rangle\cap R^{\times},\\
\tilde{R}_{T}^{0}:={\mathcal{V}}^{0}\cap(\tilde{R}_{T}^{\times}-\tilde{R}_{T}^{\times}),\\
\tilde{R}_{T}:=\tilde{R}_{T}^{\times}\cup\tilde{R}_{T}^{0},\\
(\cdot,\cdot)_{T}:=(\cdot,\cdot)_{|_{{\mathcal{V}}_{T}}}.\end{array}

It is straightforward to check that
({\mathcal{V}}_{T},(\cdot,\cdot)_{T},{\tilde{R}}_{T})
is a real-closed subsystem of
R
. Moreover,
({\tilde{R}}_{T},(\cdot,\cdot)_{T},{\mathcal{V}}_{T})
is the maximal subsystem of
R
containing
T
.

We observe that if
\tilde{R}_{T}^{0}
forms a lattice, then Definition
2.1.3
ensures that the properties of being closed and real-closed are equivalent. In particular, since for any extended affine root system of nullity
\leq 2
, the isotropic roots form a lattice,
\tilde{R}_{T}
is closed if it has nullity
\leq 2
.

Example 2.1.5
.

Let
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
be an extended affine root system and
\dot{R}
and
\dot{\mathcal{V}}
be as in
2.1.2
.

(i) Let
0\not=\delta\in R^{0}
and set
T=({\dot{R}}^{\times}+{\mathbb{Z}}\delta)
. It follows from
2.1.2
that

T\cap R^{\times}\not=\emptyset
. As indicated in

,

(\tilde{R}_{T},(\cdot,\cdot)_{T},{\mathcal{V}}_{T})
is an extended affine root system.
We have

\tilde{R}_{T}^{\times}=\langle T\rangle\cap R^{\times}=(\langle\dot{R}\rangle\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\delta)\cap R^{\times}.

Since
\tilde{R}_{T}^{0}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}\delta
,
\tilde{R}
has nullity
1
and so is a closed affine root subsystem of

, with the same type as

. We denote this affine root subsystem by
R_{\dot{R},\delta}
.

(ii) Let
\alpha\in R^{\times}
. Then
\alpha=\dot{\alpha}+\delta
for some
\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}^{\times}
and some
\delta\in R^{0}
. Set

T=\{\dot{\alpha},\alpha\}
. Then by
2.1.4
,
\tilde{R}_{T}
is a closed affine subsystem of
R
containing
\alpha
.
We denote this subsystem by
R_{\alpha,\delta}
.

2.2.
Extended affine Lie algebras

We recall the definition of an extended affine Lie algebra and highlight some of its fundamental properties. Unless stated otherwise, the results discussed here are drawn from
[
1
, Chapter I]
. All algebras in this work are considered over filed
{\mathbb{K}}
of complex numbers.

Definition 2.2.1
.

An
extended affine Lie algebra
is a triple
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
where
E
is a Lie algebra,
{\mathcal{H}}
is a subalgebra of
E
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a bilinear form on
E
satisfying the following 5 axioms:

(EA1) The form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
E
is symmetric, non-degenerate and invariant.

(EA2)
{\mathcal{H}}
is a finite dimensional splitting Cartan subalgebra of
E
. This means that
E=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}}E_{\alpha}
where
E_{\alpha}=\{x\in E\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
and
E_{0}={\mathcal{H}}
.

Let
R
be the set of roots of
E
, namely
R=\{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}\mid E_{\alpha}\neq\{0\}\}
.
It follows from (EA1)-(EA2) that the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricted to
{\mathcal{H}}
is non-degenerate and so it can be transferred
to
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
by
(\alpha,\beta):=(t_{\alpha},t_{\beta})
where
t_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{H}}

is the unique element satisfying
\alpha(h)=(h,t_{\alpha})
,
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then
R=R^{0}\uplus R^{\times}
is regarded as the decomposition of roots into

isotropic
and
non-isotropic
roots, respectively. Let
{\mathcal{V}}:=\hbox{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}R

and
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}:=\hbox{Span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R^{0}
.

(EA3) For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,
\hbox{ad}x
is locally nilpotent for
x\in E_{\alpha}
.

(EA4)
R
is discrete in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
.

(EA5)
R
is irreducible. That is,
R^{\times}
is connected and elements of
R^{0}
are non-isolated (see axioms (R7) and (R8) of Definition
2.1.1
).

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. It follows that
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
is an extended affine root system in the sense of Definition
2.1.1
.
We have

(2.1)

[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]={\mathbb{K}}t_{\alpha},\quad(\alpha\in R),

(2.2)

(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=\{0\}\hbox{ unless }\alpha+\beta=0,\quad(\alpha,\beta\in R).

Also from
[
10
, Remark 1.5]
, we have

(2.3)

[E_{\alpha},E_{\beta}]\not=\{0\},\qquad(\alpha\in R^{\times},\beta,\alpha+\beta\in R).

The
core

E_{c}
of
E
is by definition the subalgebra of
E
generated by the non-isotropic root spaces. It follows that the centralizer
C_{E}(E_{c})
of
E_{c}
in
E
coincides with the orthogonal complement
E_{c}^{\perp}
of
E_{c}
in
E
. Therefore

(2.4)

Z(E_{c})\subseteq C_{E}(E_{c})=E_{c}^{\perp},

and

(2.5)

x\in E_{c}^{\perp}\Longleftrightarrow(x,E_{\alpha})=\{0\}\hbox{ for all }\alpha\in R^{\times}.

Note that if
x\in E_{c}^{\perp}
, then by (
2.2
) each homogeneous component of
x
is contained in
E_{c}^{\perp}
. It then follows that

(2.6)

E_{c}^{\perp}\subseteq\sum_{\sigma\in R^{0}}{E_{\sigma}}.

The extended affine Lie algebra
E
is called
tame
if
E_{c}^{\perp}=Z(E_{c})
(or equivalently
E_{c}^{\perp}\subseteq Z(E_{c})
).

The following lemma gives a helpful characterization of tameness.

Lemma 2.2.2
.

The extended affine Lie algebra
E
is tame if and only if for each
\sigma\in R^{0}
,

E_{\sigma}\cap E_{c}^{\perp}=Z(E_{c})\cap\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}[E_{\alpha+\sigma},E_{-\alpha}].

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle E\hbox{ is tame}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle E_{c}^{\perp}=Z(E_{c})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle E_{c}^{\perp}\cap E_{\alpha}=Z(E_{c})\cap E_{\alpha},\;\forall\alpha\in R

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle E_{c}^{\perp}\cap E_{\sigma}=Z(E_{c})\cap E_{\sigma},\;\forall\sigma\in R^{0}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle E_{c}^{\perp}\cap E_{\sigma}=Z(E_{c})\cap\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}[E_{\alpha+\sigma},E_{-\alpha}],\;\forall\sigma\in R^{0}.

\Box

Lemma 2.2.3
.

For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
\sigma\in R^{0}
with
\alpha+\sigma\in R
, we have

([E_{\alpha+\sigma},E_{-\alpha}],[E_{-\alpha-\sigma},E_{\alpha}])\not=\{0\}.

Proof.
Take
x_{\pm\alpha}\in E_{\pm\alpha}
and

x_{\pm(\alpha+\sigma)}\in E_{\pm(\alpha+\sigma)}
with
[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}]=t_{\alpha}
and
[x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha-\sigma}]=t_{\alpha+\sigma}.
From the Jacobi identity, the fact that
-2\alpha-\sigma
is not a root and invariance of the form, we have

\displaystyle([x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha}],[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},x_{\alpha}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{\alpha+\sigma},[x_{-\alpha},[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},x_{\alpha}]])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x_{\alpha+\sigma},[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}]])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x_{\alpha+\sigma},[x_{-\alpha-\sigma},t_{\alpha}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-([x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha-\sigma}],t_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(t_{\alpha+\sigma},t_{\alpha})=-(\alpha,\alpha)\not=0.

\Box

3.
A Construction of Extended Affine Lie Algebras

In this section, we provide a brief overview of a general approach to constructing extended affine Lie algebras, following the work of Erhard Neher
[
22
]
. We assume the reader is already familiar with the concept of Lie tori (see
[
26
]
).

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice of rank
\nu
, and let
\mathfrak{g}
be a centerless
\Lambda
-Lie torus, meaning a Lie torus with a trivial center. It is known that
\mathfrak{g}
admits a symmetric invariant non-degenerate bilinear from
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Consider the
centroid

\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is
\Lambda
-graded with

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}\leq 1.

Defining

\Gamma:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\lambda}\neq 0\},

we can express the centroid as

\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\mathbb{K}\chi^{\mu},

where each
\chi^{\mu}
acts as an endomorphism of degree
\mu
, satisfying
\chi^{\mu}\chi^{\nu}=\chi^{\mu+\nu}
.

For any
\theta\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{K})
, the
degree derivation

\partial_{\theta}
on
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\partial_{\theta}(x^{\lambda})=\theta(\lambda)x^{\lambda},
for
x^{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda}.

Denoting by
\mathcal{D}
the set of all degree derivations, the set of
centroidal derivations
of
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g}):=\hbox{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})\mathcal{D}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\mathcal{D}.

This forms a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of the derivation algebra
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})
, with the bracket

(3.1)

[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\psi}]=\chi^{\mu+\nu}(\theta(\nu)\partial_{\psi}-\psi(\mu)\partial_{\theta}).

Next, the algebra of
skew centroidal derivations
of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mu}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\{\partial_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}\mid\theta(\mu)=0\}.

We note that
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})^{0}=\mathcal{D}
.

For a graded subalgebra
D=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}D^{\mu}
of
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})
, its
graded dual
is defined as

D^{gr^{\star}}=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}(D^{\mu})^{\star},\quad\text{where }(D^{gr^{\star}})^{\mu}=(D^{-\mu})^{\star}.

This dual space is treated as a
D
-module via

(d.\varphi)(d^{\prime})=\varphi([d^{\prime},d]),
for
d^{\prime},d\in D
,
\varphi\in D^{gr^{\star}}.

Here,
\varphi\in(D^{\mu})^{\star}
is viewed as a linear map on
D
with
\varphi|_{D^{\nu}}=0
for
\nu\neq\mu
.

A
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra
D=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}D^{\mu}
of
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g})
is called
permissible
if the canonical evaluation map

\text{ev}:\Lambda\to(D^{0})^{\star}
, defined by
\text{ev}(\lambda)(\partial_{\theta})=\theta(\lambda),

is injective and has a discrete image.

Finally, let
\kappa
be a bilinear map satisfying

\begin{array}[]{c}\kappa(d,d)=0,\quad\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa([d_{i},d_{j}],d_{k})=\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k}),\\
\kappa(D^{\mu_{1}},D^{\mu_{2}})\subseteq(D^{-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}})^{\star},\quad\kappa(d_{1},d_{2})(d_{3})=\kappa(d_{2},d_{3})(d_{1}),\\
\kappa(D^{0},D)=0,\end{array}

for
d,d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in D
. The map
\kappa
is referred to as an
affine cocycle
on
D
.

Now, assuming
\mathfrak{g}
is a centerless Lie torus,
D
is a permissible subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathfrak{g})
, and
\kappa
is an affine cocycle on
D
, then the vector space

(3.2)

E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa):=\mathfrak{g}\oplus D^{gr^{\star}}\oplus D,

forms a Lie algebra with the bracket

\displaystyle[x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+d_{1}(x_{2})-d_{2}(x_{1}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(c_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})+d_{1}.c_{2}-d_{2}.c_{1}+\kappa(d_{1},d_{2}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}]

for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},c_{1},c_{2}\in D^{gr*},d_{1},d_{2}\in D
. Here
[\>,\>]_{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
,
[d_{1},d_{2}]=d_{1}d_{2}-d_{2}d_{1}
, and
c_{D}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow D^{gr*}
is defined by

c_{D}(x,y)(d)=(d(x)|y)\;\text{for all}\;x,y\in\mathfrak{g},d\in D.

Finally, the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
E
defined by

(3.3)

(x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})_{\mathfrak{g}}+c_{1}(d_{2})+c_{2}(d_{1})

is symmetric, invariant and non-degenerate.

This construction yields a
tame extended affine Lie algebra

(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
, where

{\mathcal{H}}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}_{0}\oplus(D^{0})^{\star}\oplus D^{0}.

To indicate the dependence of
E
on
\mathfrak{g}
,
D
and
\kappa
, we write

E=E(\mathfrak{g},D,\kappa)
. Note that

E_{c}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}.

From the way the bracket is defined on
E
, we see that

(3.4)

Z(E_{c})={D^{gr}}^{\star},

and

(3.5)

Z(E_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\Longleftrightarrow D=D^{0}\Longleftrightarrow Z(E_{c})={D^{0}}^{\star}.

Remark 3.0.1
.

Most interesting examples of extended affine Lie algebras in the literature have
D=D^{0}
and
\kappa=0
, as one can see, for example, in
[
1
, Chapter III]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
16
]
.

4.
Subalgebras and Localization

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. We recall from Section
2.2
that the form on
{\mathcal{H}}
induces a form on

{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
by
(\alpha,\beta)=(t_{\alpha},t_{\beta})
,
\alpha,\beta\in R
. It then follows that
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
is an extended affine root system where

{\mathcal{V}}=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{R}}R
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the form on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
restricted to
{\mathcal{V}}
.

4.1.
Local Lie covers

Assume that
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
is an EALA with root system
R
.

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let
R^{\prime}
be a closed subsystem of
R
.

(i) We call a subspace
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{H}}
a
(local) cover
for
R^{\prime}
if
t_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
for all
\alpha\in{R^{\prime}}^{\times}
, and the form on
{\mathcal{H}}
restricted to
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
is non-degenerate.

(ii) For a cover
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
of
R^{\prime}
, we set

E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}:={\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}}E_{\alpha},

and consider it as a vector space equipped with the form inherited from
(\cdot,\cdot)
. We call
E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
the
(local) Lie cover
associated with
(R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
in
E
. The term Lie cover will be justified in Proposition
4.1.3
.

4.1.2
.

Let
R^{\prime}
be a subsystem of
R
, and
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
be a cover for
R^{\prime}
.
Since each isotropic root in
R^{\prime}
is attached to some non-isotroic root in
R^{\prime}
(see (R8)), we have
t_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
for all
\alpha\in R^{\prime}
.
Also since the forms on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
are non-degenerate, we have
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\oplus{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}^{\perp}.

Now since
({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime\perp})=\{0\}
, and for
\alpha\in R^{\prime}
,
t_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
, we may identify
\alpha
with its restriction to
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
and consider
\alpha
as an element of
{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}^{\star}
.

(4.1)

\alpha\in R^{\prime}\Longrightarrow t_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\alpha\equiv\alpha_{|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}}.

This gives

(4.2)

{R^{\prime}}^{\times}=R^{\prime}\cap R^{\times}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{R^{\prime}}^{0}=R^{\prime}\cap R^{0}.

The facts (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) will be used frequently in the sequel without further reference.

Proposition 4.1.3
.

Let
R^{\prime}
be a closed subsystem of
R
, and
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
a cover of
R^{\prime}
. Then the Lie cover
E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
is an extended affine Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
and root system
R^{\prime}
. In particular,
E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}}
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R^{\prime}
.

Proof.
Set
E^{\prime}=E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
is abelian and
R^{\prime}
is a closed subsystem of
R
,
E^{\prime}
is a Lie subalgebra of
E
.
Since the form on
E
is non-degenerate, it follows from (
2.2
) that the form on
E^{\prime}
is also non-degenerate, thus (EA1) holds for
E^{\prime}
.

Next, since
E^{\prime}
is an
{\mathcal{H}}
-submodule of
E
, we have
E^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha\in R}^{\oplus}E^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\prime}}E^{\prime}_{\alpha}
where

E^{\prime}_{\alpha}=E^{\prime}\cap E_{\alpha}
. Now, we show that for
\alpha\in R^{\prime}
,

E_{\alpha}\cap E^{\prime}=\{x\in E^{\prime}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\},

where
the inclusion
``\subseteq"
is clear. To see the reverse inclusion, let
x\in E^{\prime}
and
[h^{\prime},x]=\alpha(h^{\prime})x
for all
h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
.
We have
x=x_{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+x_{\alpha_{k}}
, for some distinct
\alpha_{i}\in R^{\prime}
,
x_{\alpha_{i}}\in E^{\prime}_{\alpha_{i}}
. Now let
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Then,
h=h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}
where
h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}\in{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}^{\perp}
. Since
\alpha({{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}^{\perp})=\alpha_{i}({{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}^{\perp})=\{0\}
for all
i
, we get

\displaystyle\alpha(h)x-[h,x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(h^{\prime})x-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}(h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})x_{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h^{\prime},x]-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}(h^{\prime})x_{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h^{\prime},x]-\sum_{i=1}^{k}[h^{\prime},x_{\alpha_{i}}]=0.

This gives
x\in E_{\alpha}\cap E^{\prime}
. It is easy to see that
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
is self-centralizing in
E^{\prime}
. Thus (EA2) holds for

(E^{\prime},(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
and
R^{\prime}
is the root system of
E^{\prime}
with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
.
Axioms (EA3)-(EA5) trivially hold since

R^{\prime}
is a subsystem of
R
.

\Box

4.2.
A canonical Lie cover

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an EALA with root system
R
. The following notation will be used frequently in the sequel:

Notation.

For a subsystem
R^{\prime}
of
R
, we set

{\mathcal{H}}_{c}(R^{\prime}):=\sum_{\alpha\in{R^{\prime}}^{\times}}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\alpha}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(R^{\prime}):=\sum_{\sigma\in{R^{\prime}}^{0}}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\alpha}.

For notional convenience, when
R^{\prime}=R
we abbreviate
{\mathcal{H}}_{c}(R)
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(R)
by
{\mathcal{H}}_{c}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
, respectively.

Construction 4.2.1
.

We construct a canonical Lie cover for the extended affine Lie algebra
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
with a minimal property as will be illustrated in Corollary
4.2.2
below.

We have

(4.3)

{\mathcal{H}}\cap E_{c}=E_{0}\cap E_{c}=\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]=\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\alpha}={\mathcal{H}}_{c}.

Let
\dot{R}
be as in
2.1.2
. We set

\dot{\mathcal{H}}:=\sum_{\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\dot{\alpha}}.

Since each
\alpha\in R
is of the form
\dot{\alpha}+\sigma
for some

\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}
and some
\sigma\in R^{0}
, we have

{\mathcal{H}}_{c}=\dot{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{0}.

Since the form on both
\dot{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
is non-degenerate, we have
{\mathcal{H}}=\dot{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{\perp},
where the form on
{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{\perp}
being also non-degenerate, and
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}\subseteq\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}.
By Witt’s extension theorem
[
24
, Theorem 11.11]
,
\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
contains a subspace

\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
such that

(4.4)

\begin{array}[]{c}\dim\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0}=\dim{\mathcal{H}}^{0},\\
\hbox{the form restricted to ${\mathcal{H}}^{0}\oplus\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0}$ is non-degenerate},\\
(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0},\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0})=\{0\}.\end{array}

We set

{\mathcal{H}}_{R}:={\mathcal{H}}_{c}\oplus\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0}=\dot{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{0}\oplus{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}^{0}.

Clearly the form on
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
is non-degenerate.
Therefore,

(4.5)

{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{R}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{R}^{\perp}.

To simplify the notation, we denote by
E_{R}
the Lie cover
E_{R,{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}
associated to the pair
(R,{\mathcal{H}}_{R})
.

Corollary 4.2.2
.

The Lie cover
E_{R}
is an extended affine Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
, root system
R
, and core
(E_{R})_{c}=E_{c}
. It has minimal dimension among all Lie covers containing the core. Moreover, if
E
is tame then so is
E_{R}
.

Proof.
Since
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
is a cover for
R
, the first part of statement is an immediate consequence of Definition
4.1.1
and Proposition
4.1.3
. Now the Cartan subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
of any Lie cover containing the core contains
{\mathcal{H}}_{c}
and so the non-degeneracy of the form on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
forces
\dim{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\geq\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
.

If
E
is tame and
x\in(E_{R})_{c}^{\perp}
, then
x\in E_{R}
and

(x,E_{c})=\{0\}
, so
x\in E_{c}^{\perp}=Z(E_{c})=Z((E_{R})_{c}).

\Box

Remark 4.2.3
.

As previously noted, all extended affine Lie algebras and root systems in this work are assumed to be reduced. Nevertheless, Proposition
4.1.3
and Corollary
4.2.2
remain valid even for non-reduced extended affine Lie algebras, since their proofs do not rely on the reduceness condition.

4.3.
Core-based localization

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an EALA with root system
R
.
Despite Corollary
4.2.2
, in general the tameness does not inherits to a local Lie cover
R_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
. To address this, we consider another localization here.

We set

(4.6)

\hat{E}={\mathcal{H}}_{R}+E_{c}=({\mathcal{H}}_{c}\oplus\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0})+E_{c}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0}\oplus E_{c}.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

If
E
is tame then
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}^{\perp}=\{0\}
and
\hat{E}={\mathcal{H}}+E_{c}
.

Proof.
If
E
is tame then
E_{c}^{\perp}\subseteq E_{c}
and so

{\mathcal{H}}_{R}^{\perp}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\cap E_{c}^{\perp}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\cap E_{c}={\mathcal{H}}_{c}.
Thus
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}^{\perp}=\{0\}
.
Hence
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{R}
and
\hat{E}={\mathcal{H}}+E_{c}
.
\Box

For further study of
\hat{E}
, we set

\hat{E}^{{iso}}:=(\sum_{0\not=\sigma\in R^{0}}E_{\sigma})\cap E_{c}=\sum_{0\not=\sigma\in R^{0}}\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}[E_{\alpha+\sigma},E_{-\alpha}].

Then

\hat{E}={\mathcal{H}}_{R}\oplus\hat{E}^{{iso}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}E_{\alpha}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\hat{E}_{c}={\mathcal{H}}_{c}\oplus\hat{E}^{{iso}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}E_{\alpha}.

Lemma 4.3.2
.

The form restricted to
\hat{E}
is non-degenerate if and only if the form on
\hat{E}^{{iso}}
is non-degenerate if and only if
Z(E_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.
The first if and only if is clear from (EA1), (
2.2
) and the fact that
(\cdot,\cdot)
is non-degenerate on

{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
and
\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}E_{\alpha}
.

Next, the form on
{\hat{E}}^{iso}
is non-degenerate if and only if the radical

K_{\sigma}
of the form restricted to
(E_{\sigma}\cap E_{c})\oplus(E_{-\sigma}\cap E_{c})
is trivial for each

\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
. Now, as for a fixed

\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
, we have
(K_{\sigma},E_{c})=\{0\}
, and
Z(E_{c})=E_{c}\cap E_{c}^{\perp}=({\mathcal{H}}_{c}\oplus{\hat{E}}^{iso})\cap E_{c}^{\perp}
, thus
the form on
{\hat{E}}^{iso}
is degenerate if and only if

\begin{array}[]{c}K_{\sigma}\not=\{0\}\hbox{ for some }\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}\\
\Longleftrightarrow\\
\{0\}\not=K_{\sigma}\subseteq E_{c}\cap E_{c}^{\perp}\cap{\hat{E}}^{iso}\hbox{ for some }\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}\\
\Longleftrightarrow\\
Z(E_{c})\cap{\hat{E}}^{iso}\not=\{0\}.\end{array}

Thus the form on
\hat{E}
is non-degenerate if and only if
Z(E_{c})\cap{\hat{E}}^{iso}=\{0\}
if and only if

Z(E_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proposition 4.3.3
.

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. Then

(\hat{E},(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}}_{R})
is a tame extended affine Lie algebra (with root system
R
) if and only if
Z(E_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.
If
Z(E_{c})
is not contained in
{\mathcal{H}}
, then
(\cdot,\cdot)
is degenerate by Lemma
4.3.2
, and so
(\hat{E},(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}}_{R})
is not an extended affine Lie algebra.

Conversely, assume that
Z(E_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
. By Lemma
4.3.2
, the form on
\hat{E}
is non-degenerate ensuring that (EA1) holds for
\hat{E}
.

Next, since isotropic roots are non-isolated, for each

\sigma\in R^{0}
there exists

\alpha\in R^{\times}
with
\alpha+\sigma\in R^{\times}
. Then by (
2.3
),

(4.7)

\{0\}\not=[E_{\alpha+\sigma},E_{-\alpha}]\subseteq E_{\sigma}\cap E_{c},\qquad(\sigma\in R^{0}).

By Corollary
4.2.2
,
E_{R}
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. Since
\hat{E}
is an
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
-submodule of
E_{R}
, it inherits a weight space decomposition

\hat{E}=\sum_{\alpha\in R}\hat{E}_{\alpha}
with

\hat{E}_{\alpha}=(E_{R})_{\alpha}\cap\hat{E}=\{x\in\hat{E}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in{\mathcal{H}}_{R}\}.

In particular
\hat{E}_{0}=(E_{R})_{0}\cap\hat{E}={\mathcal{H}}_{R}\cap\hat{E}={\mathcal{H}}_{R}
.
By (
4.7
), the set of weights of
\hat{E}
is also
R
. Thus (EA2) also holds. Axioms (EA3)-(EA5) trivially hold. Thus
(\hat{E},(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}}_{R})
is an extended affine Lie algebra.

Finally, we show that
\hat{E}
is tame. Let
x\in\hat{E}_{c}^{\perp}.
Then

x=\sum_{\sigma\in R^{0}}x_{\sigma}
, where
x_{\sigma}\in\hat{E}_{c}^{\perp}\cap\hat{E}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in R^{0}
. Since the form on
\hat{E}^{{iso}}
is non-degenerate,
we conclude that
x=x_{0}
. Since
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
, we have
x_{0}=\dot{x}+x^{0}+\hat{x}^{0}
, where
\dot{x}\in\dot{\mathcal{H}}
,
x^{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
and
\hat{x}^{0}\in\hat{{\mathcal{H}}}^{0}
. Now since
(x_{0},\dot{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{0})=\{0\}
, we conclude that
x=x_{0}=x^{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}\subseteq\hat{E}_{c}
.

\Box

Corollary 4.3.4
.

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. If for every nonzero
\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
the spaces
E_{\pm\sigma}\cap E_{c}
are
1
-dimensional, then:

(i)

The form
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricted to
(E_{\sigma}\cap E_{c})\oplus(E_{-\sigma}\cap E_{c})
is non-degenerate, for each
\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
.

(ii)

The triple
(\hat{E},(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}}_{R})
is a tame extended affine Lie algebra.

Proof.
(i) Let
\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
. Since isotropic roots are non-isolated, there exists
\alpha\in R^{\times}
such that
\alpha+\sigma\in R^{\times}
. When the spaces
E_{\pm\sigma}\cap E_{c}
are one-dimensional, equation (
2.3
) gives:

\displaystyle E_{\sigma}\cap E_{c}=[E_{\alpha+\sigma},E_{-\alpha}]\quad\hbox{and}\quad E_{-\sigma}\cap E_{c}=[E_{-\alpha-\sigma},E_{\alpha}].

Lemma
2.2.3
then guarantees that the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
is non-degenerate on

\hat{E}_{\sigma}\oplus\hat{E}_{-\sigma}=[E_{\alpha+\sigma},E_{-\alpha}]\oplus[E_{-\alpha-\sigma},E_{\alpha}].

(ii) If the spaces
E_{\pm\sigma}\cap E_{c}
are
1
-dimensional for all
\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
, then
by(i), the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
is non-degenerate on
\hat{E}
. Lemma
4.3.2
consequently gives
Z(\hat{E}_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
, and Proposition
4.3.3
establishes that
(\hat{E},(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}}_{R})
is tame.

\Box

Remark 4.3.5
.

In the literature, nearly all instances of EALAs exhibit the property
Z(E_{c})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
; for example those discussed in
[
16
]
,
[
23
]
and
[
1
, Chapter III]
.

5.
Affine Localization

While our primary focus in this section is on affine localization, we establish a general framework applicable to extended affine Lie algebras arising from the so-called affinization process, which extends the classical construction of twisted affine Lie algebras. Using this framework, we analyze the interaction of isotropic root spaces with the core (Proposition
5.0.5
). The main result of this section (theorem
5.0.8
) establishes the existence of local affine Lie subalgebras within an extended affine Lie algebra.
For a detailed study of the affinization process, we refer the reader to
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
.
Let
\sigma\in\hbox{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
be an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma}
the fixed points of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
under
\sigma
, respectively. Let
m
be a positive integer and that the
\sigma
satisfies the following conditions:

(5.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\sigma^{m}=\hbox{id},\\
\sigma(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h},\\
(\sigma(x),\sigma(y))=(x,y)$ for all $x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\\
C_{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma})=\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma}.\end{array}

(Here
C_{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma})
denotes the centralizer of
\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
.)

Consider the group epimorphism
{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
with
i\mapsto\bar{i}
. Let
\omega
be a primitive
m
-th root of unity.
Then

\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{\bar{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{i}}\quad\hbox{ where }\quad\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{i}}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\sigma(x)=\omega^{i}x\}.

Since
\sigma
preserves
\mathfrak{h}
, we have a similar decomposition for
\mathfrak{h}
.
Let we denote the transpose of
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
by
\sigma
again. In other words,

\sigma(\alpha)(h)=\alpha(\sigma^{-1}(h))
,
h\in\mathfrak{h}
,
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
. To simplify the notation, and since distinction is clear from the context, we use the same symbol
\pi_{\bar{j}}
for all three projections below:

\alpha\in R^{\times}
,
\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
with
\alpha+\sigma\in R^{\times}
. Then the root spaces
E_{\pm\alpha}
,
E_{\pm(\alpha\pm\sigma)}

can be embedded in
an affine Lie subalgebra of
E
of
type
A_{1}
.

Proof.
Set
T=\{\alpha,\alpha+\sigma\}
. According to
2.1.4
and Example
2.1.5
,
\tilde{R}_{T}={R}_{\alpha,\sigma}
is a closed affine subsystem of
R
of type
A_{1}
.
Then
\hat{E}_{R_{\alpha,\sigma}},\hat{{\mathcal{H}}}_{R_{\alpha,\sigma}}

is an affine subalgebra of

containing the root spaces

E_{\pm\alpha}
,
E_{\pm(\alpha\pm\sigma)}
.

\Box

To see an application of affine localization in the study of Chevalley bases for EALAs, we recall the concept of a Chevalley system for the EALA
E
. Let
\tau
be an automorphism of
E
of order
2
(an involution ). A family

\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}
is called a
Chevalley system

for
E
with respect to
\tau
if for each
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,

x_{\alpha}\in E_{\alpha}
,
(x_{\alpha},[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}],x_{-\alpha})
is an

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, and

\tau(x_{\alpha})=-x_{-\alpha}
.

Corollary 5.0.12
.

Let
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}
be a Chevalley system for
E
with respect to an involution.
Let
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
and
\delta\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\alpha+\delta,\beta+\delta\in R^{\times}
and
\alpha\pm\beta
is not isotropic. Then

[[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}],x_{\beta}]\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}x_{\beta+\delta}
.

Proof.
If both
\beta-\alpha
and
\beta+\alpha+\delta
are not roots, then the Jacobi identity implies

[[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}],x_{\beta}]=0
, and we are done. Therefore, we may assume that at least one of
\beta-\alpha
or
\beta+\alpha+\delta
is a root. Consequently, depending whether
(\alpha,\beta)\not=0
or
(\alpha,\beta)=0
, at least one of the following is a connected subset of
R^{\times}
:

\{\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\delta\},\quad\{\alpha,\alpha+\beta+\delta,\beta\},\quad\{\beta-\alpha,\alpha,\alpha+\delta\}.

We denote one such connected subset by
T
. Then the triple

(\tilde{R}_{T},(\cdot,\cdot)_{T},{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}_{T})
is an EARS of nullity
1
(see
2.1.4
), and hence an affine root system.

By Corollary
5.0.10
, the triple
(\hat{E}_{\tilde{R}_{T}},(\cdot,\cdot),\hat{{\mathcal{H}}}_{\tilde{R}_{T}})

is an affine Lie subalgebra of
E
that contains the root spaces
E_{\pm\alpha}
,
E_{\pm(\alpha+\delta)}
,
E_{\pm\beta}
and
E_{\pm(\beta+\delta)}
. Thus the Lie bracket in the statement can be evaluated within this local affine Lie subalgebra. The result then follows from the known realization of affine Lie algebras; see
[
19
]
.

\Box

We conclude this section with a result concerning “finite localization”.

Proposition 5.0.13
.

(Finite Localization)
Let
R^{\prime}
be a finite subsystem of
R
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
be a cover for
R^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then the Lie cover
E^{\prime}:=E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
is finite dimensional reductive Lie algebra.
In particular
E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}_{R^{\prime}}}
is a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra with root system
R^{\prime}
.

Proof.
By
2.1.4
,
R^{\prime}
is a closed subsystem of
R
. Since a finite extended affine root system is an irreducible finite root system, from Proposition
4.1.3
we see that
E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
is an extended affine Lie algebra of nullity
0
. Thus it is a reductive Lie algebra. Now since
\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{R^{\prime}}=\dim\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\prime}}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\alpha}=\hbox{rank}\,R^{\prime}
, we conclude that
E_{R^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}_{R^{\prime}}}
is a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra.

\Box

\;[d,a\otimes t^{\lambda}]=a\otimes d(t^{\lambda}),\\

.

7.

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system

Convention 7.0.1
.

By an inclusion
(E^{\prime},(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})\subseteq(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
of two extended affine Lie algebras, we mean

is a subalgebra of

(\cdot,\cdot)^{\prime}={(\cdot,\cdot)}_{|_{E^{\prime}}}

that

to indicate that

is a subsystem of
R
.

Let

be a sequence of subsystems of

, where for each

is closed in

. Let

7.0.2

Let
(R,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{V}})
be an extended affine root system. According to

where

and

.
We also recall from

1

, then
\Lambda=\langle S\rangle
has a lattice decomposition

with

and

S_{1}
and

are semilattices in

and

We recall that if

is a semilattice in
\Lambda

\tau_{0}=0
,

Lemma 7.0.3
.

Let
S
be the semilattice as in

and
\Lambda=\langle S\rangle
. Let
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}
, where
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are two lattices with
\Lambda_{1}\not=\{0\}
.
Let

\Lambda_{1}

\hbox{ind}(S_{1})=\hbox{rank}\,\Lambda_{1}

Assume that
\tilde{S}
is a semilattice of the form
\tilde{S}=\tilde{S}_{1}\oplus\Lambda^{\prime}_{2}
, where
\tilde{S}_{1}\subseteq S_{1}
is a semilattice with

Proof.
We have
\tilde{S}+\tilde{S}\subseteq S+S=R^{0}

We now show the reverse inclusion. Let

. Since

\hbox{ind}(S_{1})=n
, we may assume
S_{1}=\cup_{i=0}^{n}(\sigma_{i}+2\Lambda_{1})

where
\sigma_{0}=0

\Lambda_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{i}

Since

\eta^{\prime}\in\langle\tilde{S}_{1}\rangle
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\in\Lambda^{\prime}_{2}
. Since

, we have

\tilde{S}_{1}=\cup_{i=0}^{n}(\tilde{S}_{1}\cap(\sigma_{i}+2\Lambda_{1}))
and so

for some

and

, where

if

Since

for some

,

and
\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda_{2}

Then

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}-2m_{0}\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}(\sigma_{i}+2\mu_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta-\lambda^{\prime}_{2}-2m_{0}\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}+\sigma_{k}+2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\lambda^{\prime}_{2}-2m_{0}\mu_{0}.

Now if

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{j}(\sigma_{j}+2\mu_{j})+m_{k}(\sigma_{k}+2\mu_{k})+2m_{0}\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\{i\mid i\not=0,j,k\}}2m^{\prime}_{i}(\sigma_{i}+2\mu_{i})+\lambda^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\tilde{S_{1}}+\tilde{S}_{1}+(\tilde{S}_{1}\cap 2\Lambda_{1})+2\langle\tilde{S}_{1}\rangle+\Lambda^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{1}+\tilde{S}_{1}+2\langle\tilde{S}_{1}\rangle+\Lambda^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{1}+\tilde{S}_{1}+\Lambda^{\prime}_{2}=\tilde{S}+\tilde{S},

If

mod

. Thus
\eta^{\prime}=2m_{0}\mu_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}2m^{\prime}_{i}(\sigma_{i}+2\mu_{i})

The only remaining possibility is that only one of

.

Let

\langle R^{0}\rangle

(2)

.

(3)
\dot{R}=B_{\ell}
, and either

\hbox{ind}(S_{1})=\hbox{rank}\,\Lambda_{1}

Proof.
Since
\dot{R}^{\times}
is connected,
T
is also connected. By
2.1.4
, the subset
\tilde{R}_{T}
is a real-closed subsystem of
R
and is clearly canonical. Therefore,

, then
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\in\tilde{R}_{T}^{0}

\tilde{R}_{T}=(\tilde{S}+\tilde{S})\cup((\dot{R}+\tilde{S})\cap R^{\times})

\tilde{S}=S\cap U

has the same type as

Next, assume that

(E_{0},(\cdot,\cdot)_{0},{\mathcal{H}}_{0})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq(E_{\nu},(\cdot,\cdot)_{\nu},{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})=(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}}),

such that for
0\leq k\leq\nu
, the root system
R_{k}
corresponds to the Lie algebra
E_{k}
, preserving the type and rank of
R
, and having nullity
k
.

Proof.
We have
\Lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{i}
where
\sigma_{i}\in S
for each
i
. For
0\leq k\leq\nu-1
we set

\sigma_{0}=0,\quad U_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{i},\quad\hbox{and}\quad T_{k}=(\dot{R}+U_{k})\cap R^{\times}.

By Proposition
7.0.5
, for each
k
, the set
\tilde{R}_{T_{k}}
is a canonically closed subsystem of
R
.
Now, by setting
{R}_{i}=\tilde{R}_{T_{i}}
, we have a filtration

\dot{R}={R}_{0}\subseteq{R}_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{R}_{\nu-1}\subseteq{R}_{\nu}=R,

for
R
, where each
{R}_{k}
is an extended affine subsystem with the same type and rank as
R
, of nullity
k
.

Next, as we discussed in Section
4
, using
[
24
, Theorem 11.11]
, the orthogonal complement
\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
of
\dot{\mathcal{H}}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
contains subspaces

\begin{array}[]{c}\dim{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}_{k}^{0}=\dim\sum_{i=0}^{k}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\sigma_{i}},\\
\hbox{the form restricted to $\sum_{i=0}^{k}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\sigma_{i}}\oplus\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{k}$ is non-degenerate},\\
({\hat{\mathcal{H}}}_{k}^{0},{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}_{k}^{0})=\{0\}.\end{array}

Moreover, the subspaces
{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}^{0}_{i}
can be chosen such that

\{0\}={\hat{\mathcal{H}}}^{0}_{0}\subseteq{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}^{0}_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}^{0}_{\nu-1}.

Now, we set

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}=\dot{\mathcal{H}}\oplus(\sum_{i=0}^{k}{\mathbb{K}}t_{\sigma_{i}})\oplus{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}^{0}_{k},\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}={\mathcal{H}},\qquad(0\leq k\leq\nu-1).

Next, using the notation of Section
4
, we set
(E_{k},(\cdot,\cdot)_{k},{\mathcal{H}}_{k}):=E_{R_{k},{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
.
By Proposition
4.1.3
, we get the required filtration as is claimed in the statement.
\Box

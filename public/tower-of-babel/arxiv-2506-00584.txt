On local non-tangential growth of the resolvent of a banded Toeplitz operator
L. Golinskii, S. Kupin

Abstract
. We study the growth of the resolvent of a Hardy–Toeplitz operator T b T_{b} with a Laurent polynomial symbol ( i.e., the matrix T b T_{b} is banded), at the neighborhood of a point w 0 ∈ ∂ ( σ ⁡ ( T b ) ) w_{0}\in\partial(\sigma(T_{b})) on the boundary of its spectrum. We show that such growth is inverse linear in some non-tangential domains at the vertex w 0 w_{0} , provided that w 0 w_{0} does not belong to a certain finite set on the complex plane.

Primary: 47B35; Secondary: 30H10, 47G10.

Introduction

In paper
[
3
]
, the authors study the growth of resolvent within certain classes of Toeplitz operators
T_{b}
.
Recall that, given a function
b\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
, the functional realization
of the Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
on
H^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
is

(T_{b}f)(t)=P_{+}\bigl(b(t)f(t)\bigr),\qquad f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{T}}),

where
P_{+}
is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
onto
H^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
,
b
is a symbol of
T_{b}
. The matrix realization of the operator is

T_{b}=\|b_{i-j}\|_{i,j\geq 1}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}),

or, in words, this is a semi-infinite matrix with constant diagonals. Here
b_{n}
are the Fourier coefficients of the symbol
b
,
i.e.,

b(t)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b_{n}\,t^{n},

where
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
.

The problem under consideration is as follows. Given a class
\mathcal{B}=\{b\}
of symbols, the goal is to find upper bounds of the form

\|(T_{b}-w)^{-1}\|\leq C_{b}\,\varphi(\di(w,\sigma(T_{b}))),

where the function
\varphi
depends on
\mathcal{B}
(but not on an individual symbol), and the bound holds
for all

w\in\rho(T_{b})
, the resolvent set of
T_{b}
.
A positive constant
C_{b}
may depend on
b
.
The main results of
[
3
]
concern such bounds for certain classes
\mathcal{B}
and

\varphi_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{x},\qquad\varphi_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{x}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{x}\Bigr).

The bound with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}
is referred to as a Linear Rezolvent Growth (LRG), and one with
\varphi=\varphi_{2}
as a Quadratic Rezolvent Growth (QRG).
Primarily, the authors deal with banded Toeplitz operators
T_{b}
with the Laurent polynomial symbols

(0.1)

b(z):=\frac{b_{-m}}{z^{m}}+\ldots+b_{0}+\ldots+b_{k}z^{k},\quad m,k\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad b_{-m}b_{k}\not=0.

In this note, we are interested in the local versions of the above result,
i.e.,
in the upper bounds for

\bigl\|(T_{b}-w)^{-1}\bigr\|
in a
neighborhood of a point

w_{0}=b(t_{0})
,
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}
,

\Omega=\Omega(b,w_{0},\varepsilon):=B(w_{0},\varepsilon)\cap\rho(T_{b}),\qquad B(w_{0},\varepsilon)=\{w:|w-w_{0}|<\varepsilon\},

for small
\varepsilon>0
.

It is well known
[
4
, Problem 78]
, that for any bounded linear operator
T
on a Hilbert space, the boundary of its spectrum is a part of the
essential spectrum

\partial\sigma(T)\subset\sigma_{ess}(T).

For Toeplitz operators the inclusion looks as
\partial\sigma(T_{b})\subset b({\mathbb{T}})
. Recall that
\sigma_{ess}(T_{b})=b({\mathbb{T}})
is the image of
{\mathbb{T}}
under mapping
b
,
and the above inclusion can be proper. Such examples can be found,
e.g.,
in
[
1
, Figure 7.1,
p. 166]
.
In these cases some parts of
b({\mathbb{T}})
lie inside the spectrum of
T_{b}
, so the local problem makes no sense for such points of
b({\mathbb{T}})
.
In what follows, we actually always assume that
w_{0}\in\partial\sigma(T_{b})
, and so

\Omega\not=\emptyset
for each
\varepsilon>0
.

Definition 0.1
.

We say that
T_{b}
admits the local linear resolvent growth at the point
w_{0}\in\partial\sigma(T_{b})
,
T_{b}\in LLRG(w_{0})
, if for all small enough

\varepsilon>0

(0.2)

\bigl\|(T_{b}-w)^{-1}\bigr\|\leq\frac{C_{b}}{\di(w,\sigma(T_{b}))}\,,\qquad w\in\Omega=B(w_{0},\varepsilon)\cap\rho(T_{b}).

In general, the local problems (say, in approximation theory) assume certain “local” (in a sense) conditions on the symbol, that is, the conditions based on
the behavior of
b
near the point
t_{0}
. We partially follow this convention. For instance, in the first result, Theorem
1.1
, conditions are
given in terms of the zeros of the polynomial
z^{m}(b(z)-w_{0})
. In contrast, LRG for regular Laurent polynomial symbols,
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
,
is based on certain properties of zeros of the whole family of polynomials
z^{m}(b(z)-w)
,
w\in\rho(T_{b})
.
In the second result, Theorem
2.1
, we obtain bound (
0.2
), but in a smaller neighborhood
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, and for all
w_{0}\in\partial\sigma(T_{b})
except for
a finite set of points on the boundary of the spectrum. The result is illustrated by the example of the flower with three petals
in the final section of the paper, see Figure
, where LLRG is studied at the neighborhood of the origin (the intersection point for
b({\mathbb{T}})
).

1.
Local regularity and LLRG

We follow notation of
[
3
]
for the algebraic equation

P(z,w):=z^{m}(b(z)-w)=0,\qquad P(z,w)=b_{k}\,\prod_{j=1}^{m+k}(z-z_{j}(w)),

its divisors
Z(w)=\{z_{j}(w)\}_{j=1}^{m+k}
, and their partitions

\begin{split}Z(w_{0})&=Z_{in}(w_{0})\cup Z_{un}(w_{0})\cup Z_{ext}(w_{0}),\quad Z(w)=Z_{in}(w)\cup Z_{ext}(w),\quad w\notin b({\mathbb{T}}),\\
Z_{in}(v)&=Z(v)\cap{\mathbb{D}},\quad Z_{un}(v)=Z(v)\cap{\mathbb{T}},\quad Z_{ext}(v)=Z(v)\cap{\mathbb{D}}_{-},\end{split}

for the unperturbed and perturbed divisors, respectively. Note that
Z_{un}(w_{0})\not=~\emptyset
.

The Wiener–Hopf factorization now looks as

(1.1)

\begin{split}b(z)-w&=b_{k}\,\prod_{z_{i}\in Z_{ext}}(z-z_{i}(w))\cdot\prod_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}(w)}{z}\right)\,\\
&=b_{ext}(z,w)b_{in}(z,w),\end{split}

where

(1.2)

b_{ext}(z,w)=b_{k}\,\prod_{z_{i}\in Z_{ext}}(z-z_{i}(w)),\quad b_{in}(z,w)=\prod_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}(w)}{z}\right).

The concept of regularity for Laurent polynomials, introduced in
[
3
, Definition 1.5]
, has its obvious local analogue:
a symbol
b
is locally regular at the point
w_{0}\in\partial\sigma(T_{b})
if at least one of
Z_{in}(w)
,
Z_{ext}(w)
is separated from the unit circle

{\mathbb{T}}
as long as
w\in\Omega
(but not necessarily for all
w\in\rho(T_{b})
).

As we will see shortly, the local regularity at
w_{0}
implies LLRG(
w_{0}
), in exactly the same way as the regularity implies LRG
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
.
As usual,
|A|
is the number of points in
A
, counting multiplicity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given a Laurent polynomial
b
, and
w_{0}=b(t_{0})\in\partial\sigma(T_{b})
, let at least one of the following conditions holds

(i)

|Z_{un}(w_{0})|=1
,

(ii)

|Z_{in}(w_{0})|=m
,

(iii)

|Z_{ext}(w_{0})|=k
.

Then
b
is locally regular at
w_{0}
, and the LLRG(
w_{0}
) holds. In particular, the latter is true whenever
w_{0}
is neither a self-intersection point,
nor a cusp
(b^{\prime}(t_{0})\not=0)
.

Proof.

The idea is to watch over
Z_{un}(w_{0})
for small perturbations of
w_{0}
within
\rho(T_{b})
, taking into account continuity of divisors. Under
assumption (i),
Z_{un}(w_{0})=\{z_{n}(w_{0})\}
is a single point, and it moves inside (outside) the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
, so
Z_{ext}(w)
(
Z_{in}(w)
) is
separated from
{\mathbb{T}}
, and the local regularity follows.

Under assumption (ii), all
Z_{un}(w_{0})
moves outside
{\mathbb{D}}
, since, by
[
3
, Lemma 1.4]
,
|Z_{in}(w)|=m
for all
w\in\rho(T_{b})
. Similarly,
under assumption (iii), all
Z_{un}(w_{0})
moves inside
{\mathbb{D}}
, since, by the same result,
|Z_{ext}(w)|=k
for all
w\in\rho(T_{b})
.
So, under both (ii) and (iii), the local regularity holds.

To show that the local regularity at
w_{0}
implies LLRG(
w_{0}
), we proceed as in the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
.
The argument is based on the result of M. Krein, see
[
2
, Theorem 1.15]

\bigl\|(T_{b}-w)^{-1}\bigr\|\leq\bigl\|b_{in}^{-1}\bigr\|_{\infty}\,\cdot\bigl\|b_{ext}^{-1}\bigr\|_{\infty},

b_{in}
and
b_{ext}
are from (
1.1
).

Assume first that
Z_{in}(w)
is separated from
{\mathbb{T}}
,
i.e.,

1-|z_{j}(w)|>C_{1}>0,\qquad z_{j}\in Z_{in}(w),\quad w\in\Omega.

In what follows,
C_{j}
stand for some positive constants, which depend on
b
,
w_{0}
,
\varepsilon
. Then

\begin{split}|b_{in}(t,w)|&=\prod_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}|t-z_{j}(w)|\geq\prod_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}(1-|z_{j}(w)|)\geq C_{2},\\
\bigl\|b_{in}^{-1}\bigr\|_{\infty}&\leq C_{2}^{-1},\qquad w\in\Omega.\end{split}

The lower bound for
b_{ext}
is simple

\begin{split}|b_{ext}(t,w)|&=\frac{|b(t)-w|}{\prod_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}|t-z_{j}(w)|}\geq\frac{|b(t)-w|}{\prod_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}(1+|z_{j}(w)|)}\\
&\geq\frac{|b(t)-w|}{2^{m}}\,,\end{split}

and so

\bigl\|b_{ext}^{-1}\bigr\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\|b(t)-w\|_{\infty}}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\di(w,\sigma(T_{b}))}\,,\quad w\in\Omega,

which implies LLRG(
w_{0}
).

Secondly, assume that
Z_{ext}(w)
is separated from
{\mathbb{T}}
,
i.e.,
for
z_{i}\in Z_{ext}(w)

|z_{i}(w)|-1\geq C_{3}>0,\qquad w\in\Omega.

Then

|b_{ext}(t,w)|=|b_{k}|\,\prod_{z_{i}\in Z_{ext}}|t-z_{i}(w)|\geq|b_{k}|\,\prod_{z_{i}\in Z_{ext}}\bigl(|z_{i}(w)|-1\bigr)\geq C_{4}>0,\quad w\in\Omega.

To obtain the lower bound for
b_{in}
, note that for
w\in\Omega
, all the roots
z_{n}(w)
are uniformly bounded,
\max_{n}|z_{n}(w)|\leq C_{5}
, and so

|b_{in}(t,w)|=\frac{|b(t)-w|}{|b_{ext}(t,w)|}\geq C_{6}|b(t)-w|,\qquad\|b_{in}^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{7}}{\di(w,b({\mathbb{T}}))}\,,

and LLRG(
w_{0}
) follows once again.

The last statement is equivalent to (i). The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 1.2
.

The condition (i) means that the polynomial

Q(z,w_{0}):=\frac{P(z,w_{0})-P(t_{0},w_{0})}{z-t_{0}}=\frac{P(z,w_{0})}{z-t_{0}}\not=0,\qquad z=t\in{\mathbb{T}}.

If

P(z,w_{0})=\sum_{j=0}^{m+k}p_{j}(w_{0})z^{j},\qquad Q(z,w_{0})=\sum_{j=0}^{m+k-1}q_{j}(w_{0})z^{j},

it is easy to see that

q_{j}(w_{0})=t_{0}^{-j-1}\,\sum_{i=j+1}^{m+k}p_{i}(w_{0}).

A simple sufficient condition for
Q\not=0
on
{\mathbb{T}}
is

|q_{n}|>\sum_{j\not=n}|q_{j}|

for some
n=0,1,\ldots,m+k-1
.

2.
Weak LLRG

The further partition of the divisors
Z(w)
,
w\not=w_{0}
, is important in this section

Z(w)=\widehat{Z}(w)\cup\widecheck{Z}(w).

Here
\widehat{Z}
is the part of
Z
, which is
close to the unit circle

{\mathbb{T}}

(2.1)

\widehat{Z}(w):=\{z_{j}(w)\in Z(w):1-|z_{j}(w)|=o(1),\ w\to w_{0}\},\ \ w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon),

and
\widecheck{Z}
the part of
Z
, which
stays away from

{\mathbb{T}}

(2.2)

\widecheck{Z}(w):=\{z_{i}(w)\in Z(w):\bigl|1-|z_{j}(w)|\bigr|\geq C_{8}>0\},\ \ w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon),

(note that
z_{n}(w)\to z_{n}(w_{0})
,
w\to w_{0}
). In other words,
\widehat{Z}(w)
arises from
Z_{un}(w_{0})
, and
\widecheck{Z}(w)
from
Z_{in}(w_{0})\cup Z_{ext}(w_{0})
.
So, it seems reasonable putting

\widehat{Z}(w_{0}):=Z_{un}(w_{0}),\qquad\widecheck{Z}(w_{0}):=Z_{in}(w_{0})\cup Z_{ext}(w_{0}).

Denote also

\widehat{Z}_{in}(w):=Z_{in}(w)\cap\widehat{Z}(w),\qquad\widecheck{Z}_{in}(w):=Z_{in}(w)\cap\widecheck{Z}(w),

so
Z_{in}(w)=\widehat{Z}_{in}(w)\cup\widecheck{Z}_{in}(w)
,
and, similarly, for
\widehat{Z}_{ext}
,
\widecheck{Z}_{ext}
. Accordingly, the Wiener–Hopf factorization (
1.1
) is

(2.3)

\begin{split}b(z)-w&=b_{ext}(z,w)\,b_{in}(z,w)=b_{ext}(z,w)\,\widehat{b}_{in}(z,w)\,\widecheck{b}_{in}(z,w),\\
\widehat{b}_{in}(z,w)&=\prod_{z_{j}\in\widehat{Z}_{in}}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}(w)}{z}\right),\quad\widecheck{b}_{in}(z,w)=\prod_{z_{j}\in\widecheck{Z}_{in}}\left(1-\frac{z_{j}(w)}{z}\right).\end{split}

The divisors
Z(w)
are called
simple
, if all
z_{j}(w)
are pairwise distinct (
i.e.,
no multiple roots). The complementary set

(2.4)

K:=\{w=b(\lambda):\ b^{\prime}(\lambda)=0\}

is obviously finite, cf.
[
1
, Lemma 11.4]
.
Saying that
w_{0}\notin K
(
Z(w_{0})
is simple) is equivalent to

(2.5)

b^{\prime}(z_{j}(w_{0}))\not=0,\qquad z_{j}\in Z(w_{0}).

Certainly, the more natural assumption would be
b^{\prime}(z_{j}(w_{0}))\not=0
,
z_{j}~\in Z_{un}(w_{0})
, but, in some way, the roots inside

{\mathbb{D}}
play a role. By continuity,
Z(w)
are simple for
w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon)
with small enough
\varepsilon>0
, and

(2.6)

0<C_{9}\leq|z_{i}(w)-z_{j}(w)|\leq C_{10};\ i\not=j,\ z_{i},z_{j}\in Z(w),\ w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon).

The roots
z_{j}\in Z(w)
are analytic functions on
B(w_{0},\varepsilon)
, and, by the Inverse Function Theorem,

(2.7)

z_{j}(w)=z_{j}(w_{0})+\frac{w-w_{0}}{b^{\prime}(z_{j}(w_{0}))}+O\bigl((w-w_{0})^{2}\bigr),\quad w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon).

So

(2.8)

0<C_{11}|w-w_{0}|\leq|z_{j}(w)-z_{j}(w_{0})|\leq C_{12}|w-w_{0}|,\qquad w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon).

It follows from (
2.7
) that for
z_{j}\in\hat{Z}(w)
and
w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon)

(2.9)

|z_{j}(w)|^{2}=1+2\text{\rm Re\,}\left\{\frac{\overline{z_{j}(w_{0})}}{b^{\prime}(z_{j}(w_{0}))}\,(w-w_{0})\right\}+O(|w-w_{0}|^{2}).

For
w_{0}\in\partial\sigma(T_{b})
,
z_{j}(w_{0})\in Z_{un}(w_{0})
, consider the domains (unless they are empty)

(2.10)

G_{j}(w_{0}):=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}:\left|\text{\rm Re\,}\left\{\frac{\overline{z_{j}(w_{0})}}{b^{\prime}(z_{j}(w_{0}))}\,(w-w_{0})\right\}\right|>C_{13}|w-w_{0}|\right\}.

By (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), the roots
z_{j}(w)\in\hat{Z}(w)
lie in certain Stolz angles
(interior and exterior) for
w\in G_{j}(w_{0})
,

(2.11)

\bigl|1-|z_{j}(w)|\bigr|>C_{14}|z_{j}(w)-z_{j}(w_{0})|,\qquad z_{j}(w)\in\hat{Z}(w).

Define the domain

(2.12)

\Omega^{\prime}(w_{0},\varepsilon):=\bigcap_{z_{j}\in\hat{Z}(w)}\left(G_{j}(w_{0})\bigcap\Omega(w_{0},\varepsilon)\right)\subset\Omega(w_{0},\varepsilon).

The main result concerns the “weak” LLRG, that is, the local linear resolvent growth on the domain
\Omega^{\prime}(w_{0},\varepsilon)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
w_{0}\notin K
. Then for small enough
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.13)

\bigl\|(T_{b}-w)^{-1}\bigr\|\leq\frac{C(b,w_{0},\varepsilon)}{\di(w,\sigma(T_{b}))}\,,\qquad w\in\Omega^{\prime}(w_{0},\varepsilon).

Proof.

We begin with the explicit formula for the resolvent
(T_{b}-w)^{-1}
, see,
e.g.,

[
5
, Theorem 3.3.6]
,
in terms of the Wiener–Hopf factorization (
1.1
)

(2.14)

(T_{b}-w)^{-1}h=\frac{1}{b_{ext}(t,w)}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{+}\left(\frac{h(\cdot)}{b_{in}(\cdot,w)}\right),\qquad h\in H^{2},

{\mathbb{P}}_{+}
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}
onto
H^{2}
.

Since
K
(
2.4
) is finite, and
w_{0}\notin K
, we have

B(w_{0},\varepsilon)\cap K=\emptyset

for small enough
\varepsilon>0
, and (
2.6
) holds.

The fraction in (
2.14
) is

\begin{split}\frac{h(t)}{b_{in}(t,w)}&=\frac{t^{m}h(t)}{\prod_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}(t-z_{j}(w))}=\sum_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}a_{j}(w)\frac{t^{m}h(t)}{t-z_{j}(w)}\,,\\
a_{j}(w)&=\prod_{p\not=j}(z_{j}(w)-z_{p}(w))^{-1},\quad z_{j}(w),z_{p}(w)\in Z_{in}.\end{split}

Note that, due to (
2.6
),
|a_{j}(w)|\leq C_{15}

for all
w\in B(w_{0},\varepsilon)
.

The formula below is well known and can be checked directly,

{\mathbb{P}}_{+}\left(\frac{f(\cdot)}{t-\alpha}\right)=\frac{f(t)-f(\alpha)}{t-\alpha}\,,\qquad f\in H^{2},\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Hence,

{\mathbb{P}}_{+}\left(\frac{h(\cdot)}{b_{in}(\cdot,w)}\right)=\sum_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}a_{j}(w)\frac{t^{m}h(t)-z_{j}^{m}(w)h(z_{j}(w))}{t-z_{j}(w)}\,,

and (
2.14
) looks as

\begin{split}(T_{b}-w)^{-1}h&=\frac{1}{b(t)-w}\sum_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}a_{j}(w)\frac{b_{in}(t,w)}{t-z_{j}(w)}\bigl(t^{m}h(t)-z_{j}^{m}(w)h(z_{j}(w)\bigr)=I_{1}-I_{2},\\
I_{1}&=\frac{1}{b(t)-w}\sum_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}a_{j}(w)\frac{b_{in}(t,w)}{t-z_{j}(w)}\,t^{m}h(t),\\
I_{2}&=\frac{1}{b(t)-w}\sum_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}a_{j}(w)\frac{b_{in}(t,w)}{t-z_{j}(w)}\,z^{m}_{j}(w)h(z_{j}(w)).\end{split}

So,

(2.15)

\bigl\|(T_{b}-w)^{-1}h\bigr\|_{2}\leq\|I_{1}\|_{2}+\|I_{2}\|_{2}.

The bound for the first term on the RHS is simple. Indeed, as
|z_{j}(w)|\leq 1
,
z_{j}(w)\in Z_{in}
, we see that

\left|\frac{b_{in}(t,w)}{t-z_{j}(w)}\right|\leq 2^{m-1},

and

(2.16)

\|I_{1}\|\leq\frac{C_{16}\|h\|_{2}}{\min_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}}|b(t)-w|}\leq\frac{C_{16}}{\di(w,\sigma(T_{b}))}\|h\|_{2}\,.

The calculation for the second term is more complex. We have

\|I_{2}\|\leq C_{17}\left\|\frac{1}{b(t)-w}\right\|_{2}\,\sum_{z_{j}\in Z_{in}}|h(z_{j}(w))|,

and, as is well known,

(2.17)

|h(\lambda)|\leq\frac{\|h\|_{2}}{\sqrt{1-|\lambda|^{2}}}\,,\qquad h\in H^{2},\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Recall that
Z_{in}=\tilde{Z}_{in}\cup\hat{Z}_{in}
. For the roots
z_{j}\in\tilde{Z}_{in}
, which are far from
{\mathbb{T}}
, we simply have by (
2.17
),

(2.18)

|h(z_{j}(w))|\leq C_{18}\|h\|_{2},\qquad z_{j}\in\tilde{Z}_{in}(w).

Let now
z_{j}\in\hat{Z}_{in}
. Here the Stolz angles (
2.11
) show up. In view of (
2.11
) and (
2.8
),

(2.19)

|h(z_{j}(w))|\leq\frac{C_{19}\|h\|_{2}}{\sqrt{|w-w_{0}|}}\,,\qquad z_{j}\in\hat{Z}_{in}(w).

It remains only to obtain an appropriate bound for
\|(b(\cdot)-w)^{-1}\|_{2}
. Recall, that
Z(w)=\tilde{Z}(w)\cup\hat{Z}(w)
, and so

\begin{split}&{}\left\|\frac{1}{b(t)-w}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{|b_{k}|}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m+k}\frac{1}{|t-z_{j}(w)|^{2}}\,m(dt)\leq C_{20}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\prod_{z_{i}\in\hat{Z}}\frac{1}{|t-z_{i}(w)|^{2}}\,m(dt)\\
&=C_{20}\left\|\sum_{z_{i}\in\hat{Z}}\frac{d_{i}(w)}{t-z_{i}(w)}\right\|_{2}^{2},\ \ d_{i}(w)=\prod_{p\not=i}(z_{i}(w)-z_{p}(w))^{-1},\ \ z_{j},z_{p}\in\hat{Z}.\end{split}

Hence, due to (
2.6
), (
2.11
) and (
2.8
),

\left\|\frac{1}{b(t)-w}\right\|_{2}\leq C_{21}\sum_{z_{i}\in\hat{Z}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|1-|z_{j}(w)|^{2}|}}\leq\frac{C_{22}}{\sqrt{|w-w_{0}|}}.

Finally, by (
2.19
),

(2.20)

\|I_{2}\|\leq\frac{C_{23}}{|w-w_{0}|}\|h\|_{2}\leq\frac{C_{23}}{\di(w,\sigma(T_{b}))}\|h\|_{2}.

The combination of (
2.15
), (
2.16
) and (
2.20
) completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We can weaken a bit the assumption
w_{0}\notin K
to the following one: the part
Z_{un}(w_{0})\cup Z_{in}(w_{0})
is simple (we allow multiple roots
outside the unit disk).

3.
Example

We consider a simple example
of the symbol with self-intersection

(3.1)

b(z):=z^{-1}+z^{2},\qquad w_{0}=0.

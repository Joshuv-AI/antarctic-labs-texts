Arithmetic genus inequalities with an application to sums of squares
David Grimm, Gonzalo Manzano-Flores

Abstract
. We show variants of the genus inequality for the irreducible com- ponents of the special fiber of an arithmetic curve over a henselian discrete valuation ring of residue characteristic zero that take into account the non- existence of rational points, respectively real points, on the components. We then apply this inequality to obtain the bound n ​ g ng (respectively n ⁡ ( g + 1 ) n(g+1) ) on the 2 2 -logarithm of the totally positive sum-of-two-squares index in the function field of a curve of genus g g with (respectively without) real points, defined over the field of n-fold iterated real Laurent series. The bound n ⁡ ( g + 1 ) n(g+1) - without the saving in case of the existence of a real point - has been known in the case of hyperelliptic curves.



Keywords:
valuations, arithmetic curve, genus, reduction theory, sums of squares

Introduction

The study of sums of squares in function fields of one variable over a hereditarily pythagorean field
K
goes back to the 1970’s when E. Becker showed that for any real field
K
, that is, a field
K
in which
-1
is not a sum of squares, we have that every sum of squares in
K(X)
is a sum of two squares if and only if
K
is hereditarily pythagorean. One implication was generalized in
[
23
]
to an arbitrary real function field
F/K
of genus zero, namely if
K
is hereditarily pythagorean then every sum of squares in
F
is a sum of two squares (the other implication was shown later in
[
11
]
). Naturally, the question arose whether any sum of squares in a function field of a curve of higher genus over a hereditarily pythagorean field is always a sum of
2
squares.

Also in the 1970’s, L. Bröcker showed in
[
5
]
that every hereditarily pythagorean field admits a henselian valuation whose residue field has at most two orderings.

This motivated
[
22
]
to study sums of squares of hyperelliptic function fields

F
over
\mathbb{R}(\!(t)\!)
, where they showed that assuming good
t
-adic reduction, every sum of squares in
F
is a sum of two squares, but they also showed that in the elliptic curve
Y^{2}=(tX-1)(X^{2}+1)
over
\mathbb{R}(\!(t)\!)
, which is of bad
t
-adic reduction, the function
tX
is a sum of
3
squares but not of two squares in its function field.
In the case of an arbitrary hyperelliptic function field
F
over a hereditarily pythagorean field
K
, it was shown in
[
4
]
that every sum of squares in
F
is a sum of
4
squares, and the index of the multiplictive group of nonzero sums of two squares
(F^{2}+F^{2})^{\times}
in the multiplicative group of sums of (in this case
4
) squares
\left(\sum F^{2}\right)^{\times}
was studied, as a measure of how far off the sums of
4
squares are from the sums of
2
squares.
In the special case where
K=\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)
, the field of iterated real Laurent series, it is a consequence of
[
4
, Theorem 3.10]
that

\left[\left(\sum F^{2}\right)^{\times}\,:\,(F^{2}+F^{2})^{\times}\right]\leqslant 2^{n(g+1)},

where
F/K
is the function field of a
hyperelliptic
curve of genus
g
.
In
Example

5.4
, we extend this bound to an arbitrary (not necessarily hyperelliptic) function field
F/K
of genus
g
as a special case of the more general
Theorem

5.3
. Moreover, we show that

\left[\left(\sum F^{2}\right)^{\times}\,:\,(F^{2}+F^{2})^{\times}\right]\leqslant 2^{ng}

whenever
F
is a real field. In this way, the bound is naturally optimal in the case of genus zero, by the aforementioned result in
[
23
]
. In fact, optimality of the bound
2^{n(g+1)}
in the nonreal case is
a consequence of
[
16
, Example 4.5]
and in the real case, we show optimality of the bound
2^{ng}
in
Example

5.5
. For every
n
and
g
, optimality is exhibited by the curves

Y^{2}=-\prod_{i=0}^{g}(X^{2}+t_{n}^{2i})\quad\text{ and }\quad Y^{2}=(X-1)\prod_{i=1}^{g}(X^{2}+t_{n}^{2i}),

defined over
\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)
, respectively.
We should note that both bounds for arbitrary curves of arbitrary genus were already described for
n=1
in the introduction of
[
2
]
, as an application of a genus inequality for the reduction of an arithmetic curve shown in that paper. However, this genus inequality is not sufficiently strong to extend the bound on sums of squares for general
n
, as we discuss at the end of
Section

5
. A palatable synthesis of our arithmetic-geometric main results, which allows for an inductive application in the proof for the upper bound of the totally positive sum-of-two-squares index, is the following:

Theorem

(
Corollary

4.6
&
Theorem

4.7
)
.

Let
T
be a discrete henselian valuation ring with residue field
k
of characteristic zero and field of fractions
K
.
Let
F/K
be a function field in one variable, and assume that
K
is relatively algebraically closed in
F
.
Then

\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F)}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n}/\text{n}}_{T}(F)}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!1+\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,\leqslant\,\,\mathfrak{g}(F/K),

if
F
is real, and

\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n}/\text{n}}_{T}(F)}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!1+\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,\leqslant\,\,\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1,

if
F
is nonreal.

Here, considering the set of all residually transcendental valuation extensions
w
of
T
, we denote by
\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F)
the subset of those
w
whose residue field
\kappa_{w}
is real, and by
\Omega^{\text{n/n}}_{T}(F)
, respectively
\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)
, the subset of those
w
whose residue field
\kappa_{w}
is nonreal, and such that the relative algebraic closure of
k
in
\kappa_{w}
is nonreal, respectively real. By
\mathfrak{g}(F/K)
, respectively
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)
, we denote the genus of the function field, which coincides with the genus of the unique regular projective curve
C
with function field
F
, respectively
\kappa_{w}
, considered as a curve over the relative algebraic closure of
K
in
F
, respectively over the relative algebraic closure of
k
in
\kappa_{w}
. In
Theorem

4.5
we also show a related version of the genus inequality where we do not require henselianity of
T
, and where the residual genera of valuations “without
rational
point in the residual curve” are the ones that are increased by one. So far, we lack an application for this second version of the genus inequality, but we include it also, since from an arithmetic-geometric point of view, it is the more natural one to consider.

The genus inequality obtained in
[
2
]
, while additionally admitting valued base fields with perfect residue fields of positive residue characteristic,
is weaker in the sense that the additional weight "+1" could only be shown for those
w\in\Omega^{n/r}_{T}(F)
with
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)=0
.
The less restrictive conditions on the valued base field in
[
2
]
seem to restrict the kind of regular models of an arithmetic curve that one can work with. More precisely, it seems to require to work with the minimal regular model.

In contrast, in the present paper, we take advantage of the fact that in the motivating application to sums of squares, only (henselian) valued base fields of residue characteristic zero are considered. Under this condition, a cohomological flatness result by Raynaud
[
20
]
permits to adapt the methods from
[
2
]
so that they indeed work with a regular model of an arithmetic curve in which the special fiber is a normal crossing divisor.

All previous genus inequalities for constant field reductions in the literature, such as
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
, or
[
9
]
do not take into account any arithmetic properties of the residue function fields since in these works,
k
is always assumed to be algebraically closed.

Finally, we show in
Theorem

6.3
that

\left[\mathcal{L}(F):F^{\times 2}\right]\leqslant 2^{g},

where
\mathcal{L}(F)
denotes the multiplicative group of local squares in
F^{\times}
. This is not a consequence of the aforementioned genus inequality, but rather of a complementary topological comparison of the dual graph of the reduction of a certain regular model under maximal unramified base change that we observe in
Lemma

3.3
. This, together with the description of the failure of a local-global principle for quadratic forms in terms of the topology of the special fiber of such a model, given in
[
12
]
, yields the bound.
Note that the index of
F^{\times 2}
inside
\left(F^{2}+F^{2}\right)^{\times}
in a function field
F
is always infinite and thus not interesting, but since

F^{\times 2}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(F)\subseteq\left(F^{2}+F^{2}\right)^{\times},

we believe the local squares to be an interesting substitute. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}(F)
may be identified with the so called Kaplansky radical of
F
, as will be explored in an upcoming article of the authors in collaboration with K.J. Becher. Both, the bound on the index of the square group inside the local square group, as well as its implications on the Kaplansky radical have been obtained in the doctoral thesis of the second named author at the Universidad de Santiago de Chile & Universiteit Antwerpen under the joint supervision of the first named author and K.J. Becher.

2.
Symmetries in graphs

In the subsequent sections, we will study certain reductions of an algebraic curve, where we aim to bound the number of irreducible components with certain arithmetic properties, such as having a real field of constants but not admitting a real point. These are the relevant irreducible components to bound in view of the genus-inequality theorem for the function field of the algebraic curve, see the stated main theorem in the introduction. Any such irreducible component in the reduction corresponds to what we shall call a singular rigidity in the graph related to the reduction, or more precisely, to a Galois-orbit of singular rigidities, given the natural graph-action of the absolute Galois group of the base field of the reduction. In the current Section, we will define abstractly the notion of a rigidity, and of a singular rigidity as a special case, for a graph with a group action. We give bounds on the number of orbits of rigidities, depending on the Betti-number of the graph. Later on, using an established relation between the genus of an algebraic curve and the genera of the irreducible components of the reduction on one side, and the Betti-number of the reduction graph on the other side, this will almost yield the optimal genus inequalities of the theorem presented in the introduction, except in the case where
F
is real, the right hand side is off by one. In order to reach the optimal bound in the real case, we will show that either there exists an additional singular rigidity in the reduction graph which corresponds to an irreducible component admitting real points, or that otherwise there has to exist a certain arrangement of irreducible components with real intersection points that corresponds to a non-singular rigidity in the graph.

A finite (undirected) simple graph
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
consisting of a finite set
\mathcal{V}
and a subset
\mathcal{E}
of the set of all subsets of
\mathcal{V}
of cardinality
2
. An element of
\mathcal{V}
is called a
vertex
of
\mathcal{D}
and an element of
\mathcal{E}
is called a
edge
of
\mathcal{D}
.
For a vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
, we call
\mathrm{deg}(v):=|\{w\in\mathcal{V}\mid\{v,w\}\in\mathcal{E}\}|
the
degree of
v
.
We say that two vertices
v,w\in\mathcal{V}
are
connected
if there is a sequence of vertices
v=v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}=w\in\mathcal{V}
such that
\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}\in\mathcal{E}
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. We say that
\mathcal{D}
is
connected
, if any two of its vertices are connected, and
simply connected
if moreover the connecting sequence of vertices between any two given vertices is unique. We set

\beta(\mathcal{D}):=1-|\mathcal{V}|+|\mathcal{E}|

and call it the
Betti number
of
\mathcal{D}
.

Let for the rest of this section
\mathcal{D}
always denote a finite connected simple graph. Note that
\beta(\mathcal{D})\geqslant 0
and

\beta(\mathcal{D})=0
if and only if
\mathcal{D}
is simply connected. We also call a simply connected graph a
tree
.

We call a finite connected simple graph
\mathcal{D}
together with a group action of a group
G
via graph automorphisms a
simple
G
-graph
.
Note that every graph is in particular a
G
-graph for
G
the group of automorphisms of the graph.
Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
a simple
G
-graph. Let
H\leqslant G
be a subgroup. We denote by
\mathcal{D}^{H}
the full subgraph of
\mathcal{D}
spanned by
\mathcal{V}^{H}=\{v\in\mathcal{V}\mid H\subseteq\mathsf{stab}_{G}(v)\}
.
We call a connected component
\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{R}})
of
\mathcal{D}^{H}
an
H
-rigidity
of the
G
-graph
\mathcal{D}
if
H=\mathsf{stab}_{G}(v)
for every
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}}
. A
rigidity of the
G
-graph
\mathcal{D}
is a
H
-rigidity for some (uniquely determined) subgroup
H\leqslant G
.
Note that any two distinct rigidities are disjoint. Given a rigidity
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{D}
, we sometimes call the common stabilizer subgroup
H
for all vertices of
\mathcal{R}
the
rigidifier of
\mathcal{R}
.

We call a rigidity that consists of exactly one vertex a
singular rigidity
. They are of particular interest in the current article.

2.1 Example
.

Consider the line graph of three vertices
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
and
\mathcal{E}=\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\,\{v_{2},v_{3}\}\}
, considered as a
G
-graph, where
G=\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{D})
, i.e. the group of order two consisting of the identity and the permutation of
v_{1}
and
v_{3}
. For the trivial subgroup
H=\{\mathsf{id}_{\mathcal{D}}\}
, we have that
\mathcal{D}^{H}=\mathcal{D}
and this graph is connected. However, the full stabilizer of
v_{2}
is all of
G
, so there is no rigidity of
\mathcal{D}
with rigidifier
\{\mathsf{id}_{\mathcal{D}}\}
. For the other subgroup
H=G
, we have that that
\mathcal{D}^{H}=(\{v_{2}\},\emptyset)
, which is connected. Moreover the full stabilizer of its only vertex
v_{2}
coincides with
H
, so indeed
\mathcal{R}=(\{v_{2}\},\emptyset)
is a (singular) rigidity of
\mathcal{D}
. It is the unique rigidity of the
G
-group
\mathcal{D}
.

2.2 Lemma
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
a finite simple
G
-graph. Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
be a connected
G
-stable subgraph of
\mathcal{D}
. Let
\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{R}})
be a rigidity of the
G
-graph
\mathcal{D}
. Then every connected component of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}:=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{R}}\cap\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
is a rigidity of the
G
-graph
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the rigidifier of
\mathcal{R}
. Since the vertices of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}}
have stabilizer
H
, the same is true for the vertices of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{V}^{H}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=\mathcal{V}^{\prime H}
, we have that every connected component of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime H}
, since otherwise, should there exist a connected component of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
that is properly cotained in a connected component of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime H}
, the connected componente of
\mathcal{D}^{H}
that contains the one of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime H}
then would properly contain
\mathcal{R}
, contradicting the hypothesis of being a rigidity of
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

In
[
2
]
, the notion of
pivot vertices
was introduced and studied. They were defined as vertices whose stabilizer group acts on the neighboring vertices by separating them into orbits of even cardinality. In particular, pivot vertices are singular rigidities. The following upper bounds on the number of rigidities or
G
-orbits of rigidities were shown in
[
2
]
for the special case of pivot vertices. We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{G}
the set of rigidities of the
G
-graph
\mathcal{D}
.

2.3 Proposition
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is a tree, then
|\mathcal{D}_{G}|\leqslant 1
.

Proof.

If
|\mathcal{V}|\leqslant 1
the statement is trivial.
Assume now that
\mathcal{D}
is a tree with
|\mathcal{V}|>1
.
Then
\mathcal{D}
has at least one vertex of degree
1
.
If
\mathcal{D}
consists only of vertices of degree
1
, that is, if
\mathcal{D}
is the tree consisting of two vertices, then
\mathcal{D}
is the unique rigidity of
\mathcal{D}
, independent of the group acting on it.
Otherwise, by removing all vertices of degree
1
from
\mathcal{D}
, we obtain a nonempty subtree
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}
with strictly fewer vertices than
\mathcal{D}
. Any rigidity
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{D}
restricts to a unique rigidity of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
by
Lemma

2.2
, since any rigidity of
\mathcal{D}
necessarily contains a vertex that has degree at least
2
in
\mathcal{D}
. Any two distinct rigidities of
\mathcal{D}
are disjoint, and hence restrict to two distinct rigidities of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.
Hence the statement follows by induction on
|\mathcal{V}|
.
∎

Note that the
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}
induces a
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}_{G}
. More precisely, if
\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}
is a rigidity with stabilizer
H
and rigidifier
\widetilde{H}\leqslant H
, then
g.\mathcal{R}
is a rigidity with stabilizer
gHg^{-1}
and rigidifier
g\widetilde{H}g^{-1}
, for any
g\in G
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{G}^{G}
the set of rigidities that are fixed under this action of
G
. Note that if
\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}^{G}
, that is, if
g.\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}
for every
g\in G
, then
G
is not necessarily the rigidifier of
\mathcal{R}
in
G
, since the induced
G
-action on the graph
\mathcal{R}
need not be trivial. If, however,
\mathcal{R}
is a singular rigidity, then indeed
\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}^{G}
implies that
G
is the rigidifier of
\mathcal{R}
.

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{G}_{G}\neq\emptyset
.
Then
|\mathcal{D}_{G}|\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D})+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{G}_{G}
. We will show that
|\mathcal{D}_{G}\setminus\{\mathcal{R}_{0}\}|\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D})
.
For
v\in\mathcal{V}
let
d(v)
denote the distance between
v
and
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, that is,
the smallest
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that there exist
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in\mathcal{V}
with
\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}\in\mathcal{E}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
where
v_{r}=v
and
v_{0}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
.
Note that
d(gv)=d(v)
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
and
g\in G
.

The proof of the statement is by induction
on
|\mathcal{E}|
.
If
|\mathcal{E}|=0
, then
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{R}_{0}=\{v_{0}\}
and the statement holds trivially.
Assume now that
|\mathcal{E}|\geqslant 1
. We set
m=\mathsf{max}\{d(v)\mid v\in\mathcal{V}\}
,
\mathcal{M}=\{v\in\mathcal{V}\mid d(v)=m\}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}=\mathcal{E}\cap\{\{w,w^{\prime}\}\mid w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\}
.

We first consider the case where
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}\neq\emptyset
.
We set
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
and consider the graph
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
.
Note that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is connected,
\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})<\beta(\mathcal{D})
, and the
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}
restricts to a
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, every rigidity of
\mathcal{D}
, after removing edges from
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
, either remains connected and hence remains a single rigidity of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, or it decomposes in several connected components, each of which gives a rigidity on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, by
Lemma

2.2
.
Since
|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|<|\mathcal{E}|
, we therefore obtain by the induction hypothesis that

|\mathcal{D}_{G}\setminus\{R_{0}\}|\leqslant|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{G}\setminus\{R_{0}\}|\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})<\beta(\mathcal{D})
.

We now consider the case where
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}=\emptyset
.
Set
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{M}
. We consider the graph
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}\cap\{\{v,v^{\prime}\}\mid v,v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\}
(i.e. the full subgraph of
\mathcal{D}
spanned by
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
).
Note that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is connected,
\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D})
, the
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}
restricts to a
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, and every rigidity of
\mathcal{D}
that contains a vertex in
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
restricts to at least one rigidity of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset
we have that
|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|<|\mathcal{E}|
.
Hence, the induction hypothesis yields that
|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{G}\setminus\{R_{0}\}|\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{G}
denote the set of (necessarily singular) rigidities of
\mathcal{D}_{G}
that contain no vertex in
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. Then

|\mathcal{D}_{G}\setminus\{\mathcal{R}_{0}\}|\leqslant|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{G}\setminus\{\mathcal{R}_{0}\}|+|\mathcal{M}_{G}|\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{G})+|\mathcal{M}_{G}|
.
Since
\mathcal{E}^{\ast}=\emptyset
, we have
|\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{M}}\mathsf{deg}(w)
and therefore

\beta(\mathcal{D})-\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{M}}(\mathsf{deg}(w)-1)
.
Since every vertex in
\mathcal{M}_{G}
has degree at least
2
, we obtain that

\beta(\mathcal{D})-\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\geqslant|\mathcal{M}_{G}|
.
Hence we conclude that

|\mathcal{D}_{G}\setminus\{R_{0}\}|\leqslant|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{G}\setminus\{R_{0}\}|+|\mathcal{M}_{G}|\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D})
.
∎

2.5 Lemma
.

|\{\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}\mid\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{R}}\cap G.v=\emptyset\}|\,\leqslant\,\beta(\mathcal{D})+|G.v|-1

.

Proof.

If
v\in\mathcal{V}^{G}
, then in particular
|G.v|=1
, so the statement of the Lemma coincides with the statement of

.
We then apply

to a graph obtained from
\mathcal{D}
by adding one vertex and connecting it with all vertices in
G.v
:
Let
v_{0}
denote an extra vertex, not contained in
\mathcal{V}
, and set
\mathcal{V}_{0}={\mathcal{V}}\cup\{v_{0}\}
and

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{E}\cup\{\{v_{0},gv\}\mid g\in G\}
.
We obtain that
\mathcal{D}_{0}=(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
is a connected graph with
\beta(\mathcal{D}_{0})=\beta(\mathcal{D})+|G.v|-1
.
By letting
gv_{0}=v_{0}
for all

-action on
\mathcal{D}

A
bipartite simple graph
is a simple graph that allows a coloring of the vertices by two colors, say cyan and purple, such that every edge is between vertices of distinct color.

Let us from now on assume that the connected finite simple graph
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is bipartite.
Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
be another finite simple bipartite graph.
A
morphism

\varphi:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{D}

of bipartite finite simple graphs

\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a color-respecting map
\varphi:\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{V}
on the set of vertices such that
\{\varphi(v),\varphi(w)\}\in\mathcal{E}
whenever
\{v,w\}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.
We say that
\varphi
is an
epimorphism
if furthermore
\varphi:\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{V}
is surjective and for every edge
\{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
there is
\{v,w\}\in\mathcal{E}
such that
\varphi(v)=v^{\prime}
and
\varphi(w)=w^{\prime}
.
Write
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\dot{\cup}\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
denote the set of cyan vertices and
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
the set of purple vertices, respectively.
For
\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}
set
e_{\Gamma}=|\varphi^{-1}(\Gamma)|
.
For
x\in\mathcal{P}
, set
i_{x}=|\varphi^{-1}(x)|
and

e_{x}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{deg}(x)}\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}\atop\{\Gamma,x\}\in\mathcal{E}}e_{\Gamma}.

2.8 Lemma
.

Assume that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is connected.
Let
\varphi:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{D}
be an epimorphism of bipartite finite simple graphs such that every
x\in\mathcal{P}
has degree
2
and moreover
i_{x}\geqslant e_{\Gamma}
for both
\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\{x,\Gamma\}\in\mathcal{E}
.
Then
\beta(\mathcal{D})\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Note that the connectedness of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
implies the connectedness of
\mathcal{D}
.
If
\beta(\mathcal{D})=0
, there is nothing to show. Hence, we may assume that
\mathcal{D}
is not a tree.
Since for every purple vertex in
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
there are two edges, we have that

\beta(\mathcal{D})=|\mathcal{P}|-|\mathcal{C}|+1
and

\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})=|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|+1
.
In order to show
\beta(\mathcal{D})\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
, we need to show that
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{C}|\leqslant|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{P}|.

We first show this in the case where every cyan vertex in
\mathcal{D}
has degree at least
2
, and we will not require
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
to be connected in this case. We clearly have that

|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{P}|=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{P}}(i_{x}-1)\geqslant\sum_{x\in\mathcal{P}}(e_{x}-1)\geqslant\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}}(e_{\Gamma}-1)=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{C}|.

The last inequality may be better understood by considering

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{P}}(e_{x}-1)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}\atop\{\Gamma,x\}\in\mathcal{E}}\frac{1}{2}(e_{\Gamma}-1)\geqslant\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}}(e_{\Gamma}-1),

where the equality is due to the fact that for every
x\in\mathcal{P}
we have
\mathsf{deg}(x)=2
, and hence there are exactly two distinct
\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\{x,\Gamma\}\in\mathcal{E}
, and the inequality holds since for every
\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}
there exist at least two distinct
x\in\mathcal{P}
such that
\{x,\Gamma\}\in\mathcal{E}
, and since
e_{\Gamma}-1\geqslant 0
as
\varphi
is an epimorphism.
We will now prove the statement for the general situation, that is, for a connnected graph
\mathcal{D}
that may have cyan vertices of degree one. We will actually not require that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is connected. We will show the statement by induction on the number of vertices of
\mathcal{D}
. If every cyan vertex in
\mathcal{D}
has degree at least
2
, we do not need to involve the induction hypothesis since we have just proven this case. Otherwise, let
\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}
be a cyan vertex of degree one, and
x\in\mathcal{P}
its unique purple neighbouring vertex. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
denote the full subgraph of
\mathcal{D}
spanned by all vertices except for
\Gamma
and
x
, and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
denote the full subgraph of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
spanned by all vertices except
\varphi^{-1}(\Gamma)
and
\varphi^{-1}(x)
.
Since both graphs are bipartite, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is connected, and the restriction

\varphi:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is an epimorphism, we have by induction hypothesis that

|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}|-|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}|\leqslant|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}|-|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}|,

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}},\widetilde{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}},\widetilde{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
denote the set of cyan and purple vertices in
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}},\widetilde{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
, respectively.
On the other hand, we clearly have that

|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{C}|=|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}|-|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}|+e_{\Gamma}-1

and

|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{P}|=|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}|-|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}|+i_{x}-1
. The inequality
|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}|-|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}|\leqslant|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{P}|
now follows from the assumption that
i_{x}\geqslant e_{\Gamma}
.
∎

3.
Galois-symmetries in the reduction of a regular model with normal crossings

For this section, let
K
be a field of characteristic zero,
T\subseteq K
a discrete valuation ring of
K
and let
k
denote the residue field of
T
. Let

be a function field in one variable, that is, a finitely generated field extension of transcendence degree one. Suppose moreover that

is relatively algebraically closed in

.

We call a regular
2
-dimensional integral regular scheme
\mathcal{X}
together with a flat projective morphism

a

\mathcal{X}
is
K
-isomorphic to

.
We denote by
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\mathcal{X}\times_{T}K
the
generic fiber of
\mathcal{X}
over

and by
\mathcal{X}_{s}=\mathcal{X}\times_{T}k
the
special fiber of
\mathcal{X}
over

. Note that the morphism
\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}
given by projection is an open immersion, and the morphism
\mathcal{X}_{s}\to\mathcal{X}
is a closed immersion.
By
[
15
, Lemma 3.2.14]
,
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is a geometrically integral regular projective curve over
K
and
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is a geometrically connected projective curve over

[
15

We call
\mathcal{X}
a
regular model with normal crossings for

if the special fiber
\mathcal{X}_{s}
, seen as a divisor of
\mathcal{X}
, is a normal crossings divisor, that is, if it has normal crossing at every closed point

, that is, if
the irreducible factors of the element in the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},P}
of
\mathcal{X}
at

\mathcal{X}_{s}
locally at

(or more precisely, the pull-back of the divisor

of

Since

From now on, let
\mathcal{X}
denote a fixed regular model with normal crossing of

. We recall that the generic point of every irreducible component

of

.
Note that by
[

of any

the relative algebraic closure of

3.1 Remark
.

Let

be such that

for every irreducible component

\mathcal{X}_{s}

For every closed connected subscheme

as the graph whose vertices correspond to the irreducible components of

of

\Gamma_{1}\cdot\Gamma_{2}:=\sum_{P\in\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}}(\Gamma_{1}\cdot\Gamma_{2})_{P}
, where
(\Gamma_{1}\cdot\Gamma_{2})_{P}=\mathrm{length}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},P}/(p_{1},p_{2}))

and

and

at

. Nevertheless,
the local intersection number at a point

, see

generate the maximal ideal of the local ring at

We now adapt the notion of the dual graph slightly, by assigning a colour, say cyan, to the vertices corresponding to irreducible components and introducing an additional set of vertices of a different color, say purple, where a purple vertex corresponds to an intersection point between two irreducible components of

Note that all purple vertices in the bipartite dual graph of

3.2 Remark
.

We defined the Betti number at the beginning of the second section for finite simple graphs. However, the same formula is used more generally in the literature to define the Betti number for any finite graph, such as for example the dual graph of

Let

3.3 Lemma

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}

Since

We claim furthermore that

To show that furthermore

The base change

Let

We will now apply the general graph-theoretic symmetry considerations from the previous section to the bipartite dual graph
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s})
.
We endow any closed connected subset

with its induced reduced subscheme structure

, and we set

, i.e. the ring of global regular functions on

.
Since

is connected and reduced, we have that

is a finite field extension of

, see

. We denote by

3.4 Lemma
.

For any closed connected subset
Y
of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
, we have that

Y\times_{k}k^{\prime}\simeq\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}}Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma)}k^{\prime},

is a disjoint union of distinct closed connected subschemes
Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}=Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma)}k^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
. The natural
G
-action on
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
induces a transitive
G
-action on
\{Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}\}
corresponding to the natural
G
-action on
\mathcal{E}_{Y}
with stabilizers
\mathrm{stab}_{Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}}=\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\prime}/\sigma(\ell_{Y}))
for
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}
.
Moreover, if
Y
is irreducible, then so is
Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}
for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}
.

Proof.

The reduced closed subscheme
Y
has a natural structure over
\ell_{Y}
, and by
[
21
,
Lemma 0FD1
]
, we have that
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma)}k^{\prime}
is connected for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}
. If
Y
is irreducible, then

\ell_{Y}
coincides with the relative algebraic closure of
k
in the function field of
Y
, since
Y
is smooth by the normal crossing hypothesis on
\mathcal{X}_{s}
and thus
(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is a normal curve.

Let
U=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
be an arbitrary open dense affine subscheme of
Y
. Then
A
is a finitely generated
k
-algebra, and thus in particular finitely generated as an
\ell_{Y}
-algebra. In case
Y
(and hence
U
) is irreducible, we have that
A
is an integral domain and
\ell_{Y}
is relatively algebraically closed in
A
, whereby
A\otimes_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma)}k^{\prime}
is a domain, thus
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma)}k^{\prime}
is irreducible, for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}
.

In order to study the Galois action on
Y\times_{k}k^{\prime}
, it is enough to study it on
U\times_{k}k^{\prime}
.
The latter given by the spectrum of

A\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}=(A\otimes_{\ell_{Y}}\ell_{Y})\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}=A\otimes_{\ell_{Y}}(\ell_{Y}\otimes_{k}k^{\prime})=A\otimes_{\ell_{Y}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}k^{\prime},

where

n=[\ell_{Y}:k]
and
\prod_{i=1}^{n}k^{\prime}
is considered as an
\ell_{Y}
-algebra via the diagonal embedding
x\mapsto(\sigma_{1}(x),\ldots,\sigma_{n}(x))
of
\ell_{Y}
with
\mathcal{E}_{Y}=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\}
.
Hence,
A\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{n}A\otimes_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma_{i})}k^{\prime}
. The natural action of
G
on
A\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
where
\sigma\in G
acts via
\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\sigma
, induces an action on
\prod_{i=1}^{n}A\otimes_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma_{i})}k^{\prime}
where
\sigma\in G
acts on

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
by
\sigma.(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\sigma(x_{\sigma(1)}),\ldots,\sigma(x_{\sigma(n)}))
, where
\sigma(i)
denotes the index
j
such that

\sigma_{j}=\sigma\circ\sigma_{i}
. In particular
G
induces a transitive permutation action on the set of Cartesian factors.
The stabilizer subgroup in
G
of the Cartesian factor
A\otimes_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma_{i})}k^{\prime}
is
\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\prime}/\sigma_{i}(\ell_{Y}))
. Since
U\subseteq Y
was an arbitrary open affine subscheme of

, we conclude that
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma_{i})}k^{\prime}
and
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\sigma_{j})}k^{\prime}
are disjoint in
Y\times_{k}k^{\prime}
and thus in
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
.
∎

3.5 Proposition
.

The set of
G
-orbits of cyan
G
-rigidities of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s})
is in bijection with the set of reduced connected closed sub-curves
Y
of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
which satisfy the following properties:

There exists a finite normal field extension
\ell/\ell_{Y}
such that

(i)

\ell\simeq_{k}\ell_{\Gamma}
for every irreducible component
\Gamma
of
Y
and,

(ii)

The complement in
Y
of all intersection points

between distinct irreducible components such that
\ell\subsetneq k(P)
, is connected.

(iii)

If
\Delta
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
such that
\Delta(\ell)\cap Y(\ell)\neq\emptyset
, then
\Delta\subset Y
.

Under this bijection, the
G
-rigidities of a
G
-orbit are singular if and only if
Y
is irreducible.

Proof.

Let
Y
be a closed reduced connected subcurve of of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
satisfying the properties and let
Y^{\prime}=Y\times_{k}k^{\prime}
.
By
Lemma

3.4
, we have

Y^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}}Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\tau)}k^{\prime},

is a decomposition in connected components where
\mathcal{E}_{Y}
denote the distinct
k
-embeddings of
\ell_{Y}
into
k^{\prime}
. Moreover, the natural
G
-action on
Y^{\prime}=Y\times_{k}k^{\prime}
is the restriction of the natural
G
-action of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
and its induced action on the set of connected components
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\tau)}k^{\prime}
corresponds to the natural
G
-action on the index-set
\mathcal{E}_{Y}
, which is transitive.
We are thus left to show that for each
k
-embedding
\tau:\ell_{Y}\to k^{\prime}
, the connected component

Y^{\prime}_{\tau}:=Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\tau)}k^{\prime}
of
Y\times_{k}k^{\prime}
corresponds to a
G
-rigidity of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s})
with stabilizer
\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\prime}/\tau(\ell_{Y}))
and rigidifier
\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\prime}/\tau(\ell))
for the unique homomorphic continuation
\tau:\ell\to k^{\prime}
of
\tau:\ell_{Y}\to k^{\prime}
. Property
(iii)
guaranties that
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\tau)}k^{\prime}
defines a full connected component in the
\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\prime}/\tau(\ell))
-invariant subgraph of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s})
, and
(i)
and
(ii)
furthermore characterize that it is indeed a
G
-rigidity with rigidifier
\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\prime}/\tau(\ell))
, as is clear from
Lemma

3.4
.

Now assume conversely that

Y^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,Y^{\prime}_{d}
is the collection of connected curves of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
that corresponds to a
G
-órbit of
G
-rigidities.
Then, for the corresponding connected closed subcurve
Y
in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
, we can identify
\ell_{Y}
with the fixed subfield in
k^{\prime}
of the stabilizer subgroup
\mathrm{stab}_{G}(Y^{\prime}_{1})
of
G
. Let
H\subseteq\mathrm{stab}_{G}(Y^{\prime}_{1})
be the rigidifier of the rigidity that corresponds to
Y^{\prime}_{1}
and let
\ell
denote its fixed field in
k^{\prime}
. We are left to show that
\ell/\ell_{Y}
is normal.

For the closed immersion
\iota_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\hookrightarrow Y
of an irreducible component of
Y
let
\iota^{*}_{\Gamma}:\ell_{Y}\hookrightarrow\ell_{\Gamma}
be the corresponding
k
-embedding, and for the closed immersion of a closed point
\iota_{\Gamma,P}:\{P\}\hookrightarrow\Gamma
let
\iota_{\Gamma,P}^{*}:\ell_{\Gamma}\to k(P)
denote the corresponding
k
-embedding. These homomorphisms fit together in such a way that if

is a point of intersection for two irreducible components
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
of
Y
, then
\iota_{\Gamma_{1},P}\circ\iota_{\Gamma_{1}}=\iota_{\Gamma_{2},P}\circ\iota_{\Gamma_{2}}
.

Considering that
Y
is connected, the irreducible components of
Y
together with the closed point of intersections of distinct irreducible components yield a large connected commutative diagram where
\ell_{Y}
can be identified with the pushout of this diagram, that is, an element of
x\in\ell_{Y}
corresponds to a tuple

(x_{\Gamma})_{{}_{{\Gamma\subseteq Y}\atop\text{irr.}}}\in\prod_{\Gamma\subseteq Y\atop\text{irr.}}\ell_{\Gamma}

with the matching-property that for any two irreducible components

\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\subseteq Y
with nonempty intersection and every
P_{\{1,2\}}\in\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}
we have
\iota^{*}_{\Gamma_{1},P_{\{1,2\}}}(x_{\Gamma_{1}})=\iota^{*}_{\Gamma_{2},P_{\{1,2\}}}(x_{\Gamma_{2}})
. Let us justify this description of elements in
\ell_{Y}
:
We have that
\Gamma\times_{k}k^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{(\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma})}\Gamma\times_{(\ell_{\Gamma},\sigma)}k^{\prime}
, and
\Gamma\times_{(\ell_{\Gamma},\sigma)}k^{\prime}
is an irreducible component of
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\tau)}k^{\prime}
for every
k
-embedding
\sigma:\ell_{\Gamma}\to k^{\prime}
such that
\sigma\circ\iota^{*}_{\Gamma}=\tau
.
The stabilizer subgroup of one of the connected components of
Y^{\prime}
, say
Y\times_{(\ell_{Y},\tau)}k^{\prime}
, is clearly
\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\prime}/\tau(\ell_{Y}))
. Let us identify
\ell_{Y}
a priori inside of
k^{\prime}
and
\tau
as the identity inclusion.
Let
x\in\ell_{Y}=H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. Clearly, for every irreducible component
\Gamma
of
Y
, the closed immersion
\iota_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\hookrightarrow Y
yields a homomorphism
\iota_{\Gamma}^{*}:\ell_{Y}=H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\to H^{0}(\Gamma,\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma})=\ell_{\Gamma}
, and so by setting
x_{\Gamma}:=\iota^{*}_{\Gamma}(x)
, we obtain in this way a tuple in
\prod_{\Gamma\subseteq Y\atop\text{irr.}}\ell_{\Gamma}
with the aforementioned matching-property.
Conversely, every tuple
(x_{\Gamma})_{\Gamma}\in\prod_{\Gamma\subseteq Y\atop\text{irr.}}\ell_{\Gamma}
satisfying the aforementioned matching-property defines an element
x\in\ell_{Y}=H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
.

Note that by assumption
k(P_{\{1,2\}})\simeq_{k}\ell\simeq_{k}H^{0}(\Gamma_{i},\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{i}})
for
i=1,2
, whereby
\iota_{\Gamma_{i},P_{\{1,2\}}}
are isomorphisms.
Fixing one irreducible component
\Gamma_{0}\in Y
, and identifying
\ell=H^{0}(\Gamma_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{0}})
, we can thus identify
H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
as the set of elements
x\in\ell
such that for any closed path
\gamma=(\Gamma_{0},P_{\{0,1\}},\Gamma_{1},P_{\{1,2\}},\ldots,P_{\{n-1,n\}},\Gamma_{n},P_{\{n,0\}},\Gamma_{0})
in the connected subgraph of

corresponding to

, we have
\sigma_{\gamma}(x)=x
where

\sigma_{\gamma}=\left(\iota_{\Gamma_{0},P_{\{0,1\}}}\circ\iota^{-1}_{\Gamma_{1},P_{\{0,1\}}}\right)\circ\ldots,\circ\left(\iota_{\Gamma_{n},P_{\{0,n\}}}\circ\iota^{-1}_{\Gamma_{0},P_{\{0,n\}}}\right)

is an

-automorphism of
\ell
. Hence,
\ell_{Y}\subseteq\ell
is the subfield of invariants of a set of automorphisms of
\ell
, and hence

is a Galois extension.
∎

Let
N(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}))
denote the number of
G
-orbits of cyan singular
G
-rigidities
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s})
.
Denote

\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s}):=\{\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{s}\mid\Gamma\text{ irreducible component}\},

and

\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s}):=\{\Gamma\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\mid\exists\widetilde{\Gamma}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\{\Gamma\}:\;\Gamma(\ell_{\Gamma})\cap\widetilde{\Gamma}(\ell_{\Gamma})\neq\emptyset\},

i.e. the set of irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
that intersect some other irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
in a point that is rational for the first mentioned component. With this notation, we obtain directly from the second part of
Proposition

3.5
the following:

3.6 Corollary
.

N(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}))=|\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|
.

We say that a property
\mathcal{P}
of fields
satisfies going up
if for any place
M\dashrightarrow L
(i.e. a homomorphism
M\hookrightarrow L
or the residue homomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{M}\to L
of a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{M}
of
M
with residue field
L
), we have that
\mathcal{P}(M)
implies
\mathcal{P}(L)
.
We say that the property
\mathcal{P}

satisfies going down
if
\mathcal{P}(L)
implies
\mathcal{P}(M)
.

3.7 Example
.

The field property
\mathcal{P}=\text{``is nonreal''}
, i.e. “
-1
is a sum of squares in the field”, satisfies going up.
The empty property, say
\mathcal{P}=\text{``$1\neq 0$''}
, also satisfies going up.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a property of fields that satisfies going up. Then its negation
\neg\mathcal{P}
satisfies going down.
We say that a finite field extension
\ell/k
is
\neg\mathcal{P}
-minimal in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
if
\neg\mathcal{P}(\ell)
is true and
\mathcal{X}_{s}(\ell)\neq\emptyset
, and if furthermore
\mathcal{X}_{s}(\widetilde{\ell})=\emptyset
for every proper subfield
\widetilde{\ell}\subsetneq\ell
containing

.
We denote

\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(F):=\{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)\mid\mathcal{P}(\kappa_{w})\text{ is true}\}

and

\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s}):=\{\Gamma\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\mid\forall\ell/k\,:\,\Gamma(\ell)\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\ell)\}.

3.8 Remark
.

If
\Gamma\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})
is such that
v_{\Gamma}\in\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(F)
, then
\Gamma\in\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
.

3.9 Lemma
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote a field property that satisfies going up. Then

|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|\,\leqslant\,|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|\,\leqslant\,\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}))+1.

Moreover, these inequalities are strict in the following cases:

(i)

If there exists
\Gamma\in\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
such that

then

|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|\,<\,|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|.

(ii)

If there exist
\Gamma\neq\widetilde{\Gamma}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
with

\Gamma(\ell)\cap\widetilde{\Gamma}(\ell)\neq\emptyset
for a finite field extension
\ell/k
that is

-minimal in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
, then

|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|\,<\,\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}))+1.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from
Remark

3.1
, and obviously the inequality is strict if there exists
\Gamma\in\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
such that
\Gamma(\ell_{\Gamma})\neq\emptyset
.

For the second inequality, we observe that

\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
corresponds exactly to the set of
G
-orbits of singular cyan
G
-rigidities in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s})
, by the second part of
Proposition

3.5
.
Hence, the second inequality follows from
Corollary

2.7
, with obvious strictness of the inequality if there exists
\Gamma\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\left(\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\cup\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\right)
.
We also see easily from
Corollary

2.7

that strictness of the second inequality holds, whenever

contains a non-singular

-rigidity.
So let us assume that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s})
does not contain a non-singular
G
-rigidity, and moreover that
\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})=\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
.

Let us consider the case
\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}))\neq 1
. According to
Corollary

2.7
, supposing equality instead of inequality for the sake of contradiction, implies in this case that
\ell_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=k
for all
\Gamma^{\prime}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
.
Recall that by the hypothesis of
(ii)

there is a finite extension
\ell/k
that is
\neg\mathcal{P}
-minimal in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
, as well as
\Gamma,\widetilde{\Gamma}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
together with

.
Let

be a maximal connected closed subcurve containing

and

, such that every irreducible component

of

satisfies

, and such that

. So,

and

of

are automatically satisfied for

. Let

be an irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
such that

and thus satisfies

, by

-minmality of

, but we are assuming that

is a component of

, this leads to the contradiction that

-rigidities.

Under the previous assumptions, let us now consider the case
\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}))=1
. According to

Corollary

2.7
, supposing equality instead of inequality for the sake of contradiction, implies in this case that
\ell_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\neq k
for both

. Let again

be

, and

, as guaranteed by the hypothesis of

, whereby

. Let again

be a closed connected subcurve that contains

and

for every irreducible component

of

∎

.

4.

Let us first recall the definition of the

\ell
, which is defined as

\mathfrak{g}(C/\ell)=1-\mathrm{dim}_{\ell}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C},C)+\mathrm{dim}_{\ell}H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C},C).

We recall from
[
15
, Corollary 5.2.27]
that the arithmetic genus is stable under base change.
We also recall that there is a notion of
g(E/\ell)
, the
genus of a function field in one variable
E/\ell
defined in
[
7
]
based on valuation-divisors. Recall from
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
that for the unique projective regular curve
C
over the relative algebraic closure
\widetilde{\ell}
of
\ell
in
E
such that
E\simeq_{\ell}\ell(C)
, we have

\mathfrak{g}(E/\ell)=\mathfrak{g}(C/\widetilde{\ell}).

As in the previous section, we fix again a field
K
of characteristic zero,
T\subseteq K
a discrete valuation ring of
K
with residue field
k
, a function field
F/K

in one variable such that
K
is relatively algebraically closed in
F
, and
\mathcal{X}
a regular model with normal crossing for
F
over
T
, a maximal unramified extension
T^{\prime}
of
T
inside an algebraic closure of
K
, and we set
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}\times_{T}T^{\prime}
. Throughout this section, we additionally assume

\mathrm{char}(k)=0,

which is crucial for the following:

4.1 Proposition
.

\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))\leqslant\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}_{s}/k)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{g}(\Gamma_{i}/k).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be the special fiber
\mathcal{X}_{s}
with the induced reduced structure, that is with structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}/\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is the ideal sheaf of nilpotents in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}
. Then we have
\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))\leqslant\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{Y}/k)-\sum_{i}\mathfrak{g}(\Gamma_{i}/k)
by
[
15
, Proposition 10.1.51]
. Furthermore, by a cohomolgical flatness result when
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
, see
[
20
, Proposition 6.4.2]
, we have that
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{s},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})=k
.
Then

\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{Y}/k)=1-\mathrm{dim}_{k}H^{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})+\mathrm{dim}_{k}H^{1}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})\leqslant\mathrm{dim}_{k}H^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{s},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})=\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}_{s}/k)

follows from the exact sequence of
k
-vector spaces

0\to 0\to k\to H^{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\to H^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{s},\mathcal{N})\to H^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{s},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})\to H^{1}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}})\to 0

obtained by applying sheaf cohomology to the sequence

0\to\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to 0

of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}
- modules.
∎

4.2 Corollary
.

\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X^{\prime}}_{s}))\leqslant\mathfrak{g}(F/K)-\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}[\ell_{w}:k]\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k).

Proof.

Since
K
is algebraically closed in
F
, we have that

\mathcal{X}_{0}
is geometrically integral, see
[
15
, Corollary 2.3.14]
. Moreover, we have that
\mathfrak{g}(F/K)=\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}_{0}/K)
, and by
[
15
, Corollary 8.3.6]
, we have that
\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}_{0}/K)=\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}_{s}/k)
.
Since the arithmetic genus of a curve is stable under base change and since
\mathcal{X}_{s}\times_{k}k^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
, we have that
\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}_{s}/k)=\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}/k^{\prime})
, and hence
\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}/k^{\prime})=\mathfrak{g}(F/K)
.
On the other hand, every irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{s}
is a connected component of
\Gamma\times_{k}k^{\prime}
for an irreducible component
\Gamma
of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
.
More precisely
\Gamma\times_{k}k^{\prime}
is the disjoint union of
[\ell_{\Gamma}:k]
copies of
\Gamma\times_{\ell_{\Gamma}}k^{\prime}
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}(\Gamma\times_{\ell_{\Gamma}}k^{\prime}/k^{\prime})=\mathfrak{g}(\Gamma/\ell_{\Gamma})=\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)
, where
w
is the discrete valuation on
F
induced by the generic point of
\Gamma
. Considering
Remark

3.1
, we obtain the inequality by applying the inequality of
Proposition

4.1
to the regular model with normal crossing
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
of
F^{\prime}=FK^{\prime}
over
T^{\prime}
.
∎

4.3 Remark
.

In
[
2
, Theorem 5.3]
, the previous inequality was obtained only for the minimal regular model, but where
k
was only required to be perfect, instead of our standing assumption that
\mathsf{char}(k)=0
.

The statements of the following Lemma is probably well known.

4.4 Lemma
.

a)

If
K
admits a finite extension
L/K
in which
T
ramifies completely and such that
L
contains the residue field of a
K
-trivial valuation on
F
, then
\mathcal{X}_{s}(k)\neq\emptyset
.

b)

If
F
is real and
T
is henselian, then there exists a finite real extension
\ell/k
such that
\mathcal{X}_{s}(\ell)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

The model
\mathcal{X}
over
T
can be defined by finitely homogeneous polynomials
f_{1}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{m}),\ldots,f_{r}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{m})
with coefficients in
T
. The special fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is then defined by the polynomials

\overline{f_{1}}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{m}),\ldots,\overline{f_{r}}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{m})
obtained by projecting the coefficients into the residue field
k
.
A
K
-rational place of
F
corresponds to a
K
-rational point of the generic fiber
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, which in turn corresponds to a projective tuple
[x_{0}:\ldots:x_{m}]\in\mathbb{P}(K^{m})
solving the homogeneous equations given by the previous homogeneous polynomials. We may assume that all
x_{i}\in T
and that without loss of generality
x_{0}=1
. Then
[\overline{x_{0}}:\ldots:\overline{x_{m}}]\in\mathbb{P}(k^{m})
defines a
k
-rational point in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
. This proves a).
Let us now assume that
T
is henselian and that
F
is formally real. The latter implies in particular that
K
is formally real, which implies that
k
is formally real, by the assumption that
T
is henselian. Moreover,
F
being formally real is equivalent to the existence of an
L
-rational point of
\mathcal{X}_{K}
for some finite real extension
L/K
, see for example
[
10
, Proposition 2.3]
.
Since
T
is henselian, there exists a unique valuation extension to
L
, and we denote its valuation ring by
T_{L}
. We denote its residue field by
\ell
, which is formally real, since
T_{L}
is henselian.
If
[x_{0}:\ldots:x_{m}]\in\mathbb{P}(L^{m})
is a solution to the system of homogeneous equation, we may assume that all
x_{i}\in T_{L}
and without loss of generality
x_{0}=1
. Hence,
[\overline{x_{0}}:\ldots:\overline{x_{m}}]\in\mathbb{P}(\ell^{m})
, and it defines an
\ell
-point in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
. This proves b).
∎

We say that
F/K
admits a totally
T
-ramified place
if there exists a valuation on
F
that is trivial on
K
such that its residue field (which is a finite extension of
K
) admits a unique, totally ramified, valuation ring extension of
T
. We are now in position to prove our two geometric arithmetic main theorems of this article.

4.5 Theorem
.

|\Omega_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|+\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}[\ell_{w}:k]\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\leqslant\,\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1.

Moreover, this inequality is strict if
F/K
admits a totally
T
-ramified place.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
be the irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
. Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
be the dual graph of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
and
\beta
its Betti number. Then we have that

\beta\leqslant\mathfrak{g}(C/K)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{g}(\Gamma_{i}/k)=\mathfrak{g}(F/K)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\ell_{\Gamma_{i}}:k]\mathfrak{g}(k(\Gamma_{i})/k).

On the other hand, by
Lemma

3.9
applied to the empty property
\mathcal{P}
, we have that

|\Omega_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|\leqslant|\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|\leqslant\beta+1.

If
F
admits a
K
-rational place, or a place to an totally ramified extension of
K
with respect to
T
, then
\mathcal{X}_{s}(k)\neq\emptyset
by
Lemma

4.4
. Clearly, since all the irreducible components
\Gamma
of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
are smooth over
k
, we have that
\ell_{\Gamma}=k
for every component
\Gamma
with
\Gamma(k)\neq\emptyset
.
First consider the case, where there exists an irreducible component
\Gamma
with
\Gamma(k)\neq\emptyset
but
\Gamma
intersects no other irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
in a
k
-rational point. Then
|\Omega_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|<|\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|
, by
Lemma

3.9
.
In the complementary case, we have that every irreducible component
\Gamma
with
\Gamma(k)\neq\emptyset
intersects at least one other irreducible component in a
k
-rational point. In particular, since
\mathcal{X}_{s}(k)\neq\emptyset
, there exists at least one such component.
Hence
|\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|<\beta+1
in this case, by
Lemma

3.9
.
∎

As already mentioned in the introduction, we set

\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F):=\{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)\mid\kappa_{w}\text{ is real }\},

as well as

\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F):=\{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)\mid\kappa_{w}\text{ is nonreal and }\overline{k}^{\mathrm{alg}}\cap\kappa_{w}\text{ is real }\}.

Note that both sets are empty if
K
is nonreal, and the first set is empty if
F
is nonreal.
Since no nonreal function field with real field of constants can permit a rational place, we obtain directly from
Theorem

4.5
the following genus inequality:

4.6 Corollary
.

\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\!\!1+[\ell_{w}:k]\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,\leqslant\,\,\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1.

This corollary is obviously only nontrivial when
F
is nonreal.
We now prove a nontrivial genus inequality in the real case. We need to additionally assume henselianity of
T
. It should be noted already that due to optimality examples in the latter application to sums of squares in function fields, both inequalities (in the nonreal case and in the real case) turn out to be optimal.

4.7 Theorem
.

Suppose
T
is henselian and
F
is real. Then

\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F)}\!\![\ell_{w}:k]\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\!\!1+[\ell_{w}:k]\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\,\leqslant\,\,\mathfrak{g}(F/K).

Proof.

Since
F
is real, we have that so is
K
, and by the henselian hypothesis on
T
, we have in particular that
k
is real.
Note first that if
w\in\Omega^{n/r}_{T}(F)
then in particular
w\in\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)
for the field property
\mathcal{P}=\text{``is nonreal''}
, since if the function field
\kappa_{w}
had a rational place, then it could not possibly be non-real.
Hence, abbreviating
\delta_{w}=[\ell_{w}:k]
for
w\in\Omega_{T}(F)
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\!\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)+\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\!1+\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)

\displaystyle\leqslant|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|+\!\!\!\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)

\displaystyle\leqslant|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|+\!\!\!\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1.

for both the empy field property
\mathcal{P}=\text{``$1\neq 0$''}
and the field property
\mathcal{P}=\text{``is nonreal''}
, which both satisfy going up. The first inequality is directly obvious, since for
w\in\Omega^{n/r}_{T}(F)
and
\Gamma\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})
such that
w=v_{\Gamma}
, we obviously have that
\Gamma\in\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
. The second and third inequality follow from
Lemma

3.9
and
Corollary

4.2
.
By
Lemma

4.4
there exists a finite real extension
\ell/k
such that
\mathcal{X}_{s}(\ell)\neq\emptyset
. Let us choose
\ell/k
of minimal degree with this property.
Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{s}
be an irreducible component such that
\Gamma(\ell)\neq\emptyset
. In particular
\ell_{\Gamma}\subseteq\ell
, whereby
\ell_{\Gamma}
is real.

If
\ell_{\Gamma}\subsetneq\ell
, then
v_{\Gamma}\in\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F)
and
\Gamma\notin\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)
by the minimality assumption on
\ell
, whereby the first of the previous inequalities is obviously strict:

\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\!1+\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)<|\Omega_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|+\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k).

If
\ell_{\Gamma}=\ell
and
\Gamma\notin\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
, then

|\Omega_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|<|\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|.

Suppose now that
\ell_{\Gamma}=\ell
and
\Gamma\in\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
. Hence, there exists
\widetilde{\Gamma}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}_{s})
such that

\kappa(P)=\ell
for some
P\in\Gamma\cap\widetilde{\Gamma}
. In particular
v_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\in\Omega^{r}_{T}(F)
. If
\ell_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\subsetneq\ell
, then we have by minimality of
\ell/k
in particular that

\widetilde{\Gamma}\notin\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
, whereby, as before, we have strictness in the first inequality:

\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\text{n/r}}_{T}(F)}\!\!\!1+\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)<|\Omega_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|+\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}\delta_{w}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k).

If, on the other hand,
\ell_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=\ell
, then
we have

|\Omega^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{X}_{s})|+\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}\!\![\ell_{w}:k]\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)<\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1

by
(ii)
of
Lemma

3.9
applied to the property
\mathcal{P}=\text{``is nonreal''}
, in view of
Corollary

4.2
.
In either case, we obtain a strict inequality for one of the intermediate inequalities, whereby we may replace
\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1
by
\mathfrak{g}(F/K)
on the right hand side of the first chain of weak inequalities in this proof.
∎

5.
A bound on sums of squares

Let
K
be a field of characteristic different from
2
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by

\sum^{m}K^{2}
the subset of
K
consisting of elements that can be written as a sum of
m
squares. We write
\sum K^{2}
for the set of all elements that can be written as a finite sum of squares. The

Pythagoras number of
K
is the smallest positive integer
m
such that
\sum^{m}K^{2}=\sum K^{2}
if such an
m\in\mathbb{N}
exists, and
\infty
otherwise. We denote it by
p(K)
. The
level of
K
is the smallest positive integer
m
such that
-1\in\sum^{m}K^{2}
when
K
is nonreal, and
\infty
if
K
is real. We denote it by
s(K)
. When
K
is nonreal, we have the pythagoras-level inequality

s(K)\leqslant p(K)\leqslant s(K)+1,

due to the identity
x=(\frac{x+1}{2})^{2}-(\frac{x-1}{2})^{2}.

We recall that
\sum^{m}K^{2}
is multiplicatively closed when
m
is a power of two and that
s(K)
is always a power of two when
F
is nonreal, as shown by A. Pfister, see
[
14
, Theorem 1.1, Theorem 2.2]
. For
m\in\mathbb{N},
we denote by
\big(\sum^{2^{\ell}}\!\!K^{2}\big)^{\times}\!\!
, respectively by
(\sum K^{2})^{\times}
, the multiplicative group of nonzero sums of
2^{\ell}
squares, respectively of an arbitrary finite number of squares, in
K
. We set
K^{\times 2}:=\left(\sum^{1}K^{2}\right)^{\times}.
Considering the filtration of groups

K^{\times 2}\subseteq\left(\sum^{2}K^{2}\right)^{\times}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\left(\sum^{2^{\ell}}K^{2}\right)^{\times}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\left(\sum^{\phantom{\infty}}K^{2}\right)^{\times}\subseteq K^{\times},

the size of successive the quotient groups in this filtration gives additional information on the sum of squares structure of
K
beyond the pythagoras number of the field. Clearly, the inclusions become stationary at the smallest
2
-power bigger or equal than the Pythagoras number of
K
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
G_{\ell}(K)=\left(\sum^{2^{\ell+1}}K^{2}\right)^{\times}/\left(\sum^{2^{\ell}}K^{2}\right)^{\times}.
We define the
\ell^{\text{th}}
Pfister index of
K
as

\rho_{\ell}(K):=\mathrm{log}_{2}\left|G_{\ell}(K)\right|\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

5.1 Lemma
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
K
be a field with a discrete henselian valuation
w
with
\mathrm{char}(\kappa_{w})\neq 2
. Then

\rho_{\ell}(K)\leqslant\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w}),&\text{ if $s(\kappa_{w})\geqslant 2^{\ell+1}$,}\\
1+\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w}),&\text{ if }s(\kappa_{w})=2^{\ell}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if
\ell\geqslant 1
and either
s(\kappa_{w})\leqslant 2^{\ell-1}
or
s(\kappa_{w})=\infty
and
p(\kappa_{w})\leqslant 2^{\ell}
then
\rho_{\ell}(K)=0.

Proof.

We assume first that
s(\kappa_{w})\geqslant 2^{\ell}.
Note that
2^{\ell}\leqslant p(\kappa_{w})\leqslant 2^{\ell}+1\leqslant 2^{\ell+1}
by the pythagoras-level inequality when
s(\kappa_{w})=2^{\ell},
and that
w(\sigma)\in 2\mathbb{Z}
for all
\sigma\in\left(\sum^{2^{\ell+1}}K^{2}\right)^{\times}
when
s(\kappa_{w})\geqslant 2^{\ell+1}
(see for example
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
). We write
\overline{n}
for
n+2\mathbb{Z}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\overline{x}
for the residue in
\kappa_{w}
for any
x\in K
with
w(x)=0
.
Fix any
t\in K
with
w(t)=1
. Consider
the group homomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi:G_{\ell}(K)\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times G_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})

\displaystyle[\sigma]\mapsto

\displaystyle\left(\overline{w(\sigma)},[\overline{\sigma t^{-w(\sigma)}}]\right)

For
[\sigma]\in\mathrm{ker}(\Phi)
, we have that
\overline{\sigma t^{-w(\sigma)}}\in\left(\sum^{2^{\ell}}\kappa_{w}^{2}\right)^{\times},
and hence
\sigma\in\sum^{2^{\ell}}K^{2}
, since
w
is henselian. This shows that
\Phi
is injective, and hence
\rho_{\ell}(K)\leqslant 1+\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w}).
If
s(\kappa_{w})\geqslant 2^{\ell+1},
then by the earlier argument, we have that
\mathrm{im}(\Phi)\subseteq\{0\}\times G_{\ell}(K)
, and hence
\rho_{\ell}(K)\leqslant\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})
. Finally, if
p(\kappa_{w})\leqslant 2^{\ell}
then
\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})=0
. If, in this situation,
s(\kappa_{w})=\infty
, then by what we have already shown, we obtain
\rho_{\ell}(K)=\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})=0
, and if on the other hand
s(\kappa_{w})\leqslant 2^{\ell-1}
then
s(K)\leqslant 2^{\ell-1}
, and thus
p(K)\leqslant 2^{\ell}
, whereby
\rho_{\ell}(K)=0
.
∎

Let
K
be the field of fractions of a discrete valuation ring
T
with residue field
k
of characteristic different from
2
, and let
F/K
be a function field. For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\Omega^{\ell}_{T}(F)=\{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)\mid 2^{\ell}=s(\kappa_{w})\}

and

\Omega^{>\ell}_{T}(F)=\{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)\mid 2^{\ell+1}\leqslant s(\kappa_{w})\}.

5.2 Lemma
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\ell\geqslant 1.
Let
K
be the field of fractions of a nondyadic discrete henselian valuation ring
T
with residue field
k
of characteristic different from
2
. Assume that
p(k(X))\leqslant 2^{\ell}.
Let
F/K
be a function field in one variable. Then

\rho_{\ell}(F)\leqslant\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}^{>\ell}(F)}\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\ell}_{T}(F)}1+\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w}).

Proof.

We recall the local-global principle for isotropy of quadratic forms at least
3
variables over
F
with respect to all discrete valuations in
F
in case that
T
is complete from
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
. The completeness condition can be relaxed to the henselian condition, see
[
1
, Theorem 4.4]
.
For a discrete valuation
w
on
F,
we denote by
F^{w}
its completion with respect to
w
. Let
\Omega(F)
denote the set of all discrete valuations on
F
. Applied to our situation, the local-global principle implies the injectivity of the natural homomorphism

G_{\ell}(F)\hookrightarrow\prod_{w\in\Omega(F)}G_{\ell}(F^{w}),

and thus

\rho_{\ell}(F)\leqslant\sum_{w\in\Omega(F)}\rho_{\ell}(F^{w})
,
since if
f\in F
is any sum of
2^{\ell+1}
squares that becomes locally a sum of
2^{\ell}
squares, then
the quadratic form
X^{2}_{1}+\ldots+X^{2}_{2^{\ell}}-fY^{2}

is certainly isotropic over
F
since
\ell\geqslant 1
, by the local-global principle, and thus
f
is already a sum of
2^{\ell}
squares in
F
.
Observe that by
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
, since
p(k(X))\leqslant 2^{\ell}
, we have for any
w\in\Omega(F)
such that
w(K)=0
that
s(\kappa_{w})\leqslant 2^{\ell-1}
if
\kappa_{w}
is nonreal, as well as
p(\kappa_{w})<2^{\ell}
if
\kappa_{w}
is real. In particular
\rho_{\ell}(F^{w})=0
, by
Lemma

5.1
. If
w(K)\neq 0
, then necessarily
\mathcal{O}_{w}\cap K=T
. Moreover, if
\kappa_{w}
is algebraic over
k
, then
p(\kappa_{w})<2^{\ell}
, again by
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
applied to a description of
\kappa_{w}
as a direct limit of finite extensions of
k
.
Thus we only need to take into consideration residually transcendental valuation extensions of
T
:

\rho_{\ell}(F)\leqslant\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}\rho_{\ell}(F^{w})\leqslant\sum_{w\in\Omega^{>\ell}_{T}(F)}\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})+\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\ell}_{T}(F)}1+\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w}).

∎

Before stating and proving our main theorem in this section,
we need to define some technical notions for valuations on
K
, which in the most important example situation
K=\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)
are just straight forward.

We say that a valuation
v:K^{\times}\to(\Gamma,\leqslant)
(without loss of generality
\Gamma=v(K^{\times})
) is an
n
-discrete valuation on
K
if
(\Gamma,\leqslant)
is isomorphic as an ordered abelian group to the lexicographically ordered group
(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\leqslant_{\mathsf{lex}})
.
For simplicity, let us identify
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Letting
\pi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
denote the projection onto the lexicographically dominant component, we call the valuation
\pi\circ v:K^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}
the
1
-discrete coarsening of
v
, and we denote it by
v_{1}
.
On
\kappa_{v_{1}}
we now have naturally defined an
(n-1)
-discrete valuation

\displaystyle\overline{v}:\kappa_{v_{1}}^{\times}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\displaystyle\overline{x}

\displaystyle\mapsto v(x)

that we call
the residual valuation of
v
modulo its
1
-discrete coarsening
.
It is an easy exercise left to the reader to see that the dominant component of
v(x)
is zero, and that indeed
v(x)
does not depend on the choice of representant of
\overline{x}
. We have that that
\kappa_{v}=\kappa_{\overline{v}}
, see
[
8
, Section 8]
. If
v
is henselian, then so is
v_{1}
and
\overline{v}
, see
[
8
, Corollary 4.1.4]
.
One can also do this in reverse: Given a
1
-discrete valuation
v:K^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}
and an
n-1
-discrete valuation
w:\kappa_{v}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
, then we can define the composite valuation with respect to uniformizer
\pi
of
v

\displaystyle w\circ v:K^{\times}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto(v(x),w(\overline{x\pi^{-v(x)}})).

We leave it again to the reader to verify that this is an
n
-discrete valuation, and that a different choice of uniformizer does not change the equivalence class of valuation that we obtain.

5.3 Theorem
.

Suppose that
K
carries a henselian
n
-discrete valuation for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with residue field
k
of characteristic zero. Suppose that
p(E)\leqslant 2^{\ell}
for every function field in one variable
E/k
for some integer
\ell\geqslant 1
.
Then
p(K(X))\leqslant 2^{\ell}
, and for any function field
F/K
in one variable, we have
p(F)\leqslant 2^{\ell}+1
with

\rho_{\ell}(F)\leqslant n\cdot(\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1),

if
F
is nonreal, and

\rho_{\ell}(F)\leqslant n\cdot\mathfrak{g}(F/K),

if
F
is real.

Proof.

The fact that
p(F)\leqslant 2^{\ell}+1
for any function field
F/K
was shown in
[
1
, Corollary 1.5]
.
We show the remaining affirmation by induction on
n.
If
n=0,
then
k=K,
so that by hypothesis
p(F)\leqslant 2^{\ell},
that is
\rho_{\ell}(F)=0
for every function field in one variable
F/K
. Now suppose that
n\geqslant 1
. Let
v
be the henselian
n
-discrete valuation on
K
with the properties given in the statement. Let
v_{1}
be its
1
-discrete coarsening and let
T\subseteq K
denote the discrete valuation ring of
v_{1}
and
k_{1}
its residue field. Denote by
\overline{v}
the residual valuation of
v
modulo
v_{1}
on
k_{1}
. Since
\overline{v}
is a
(n-1)
-discrete henselian valuation on
k_{1}
with residue field
\kappa_{\overline{v}}=k
, we may apply the induction hypothesis to any function field in one variable over
k_{1}
.
Let
F/K
be a function field in one variable of genus
g
. Since the genus of a function field is defined with respect to the relative algebraic closure of the base field, we may assume without loss of generality that
K
is algebraically closed in
F
, as any
n
-discrete henselian valuation extends uniquely to an
n
-discrete henselian valuation in a finite extension.
We first observe that under the conditions of the theorem, we have
\Omega^{r}_{T}(F)\subseteq\Omega^{>\ell}_{T}(F)

and

\left(\Omega^{>\ell}_{T}(F)\cup\Omega^{\ell}_{T}(F)\right)\subseteq\left(\Omega^{r}_{T}(F)\cup\Omega^{n/r}_{T}(F)\right),

since the residue field
\kappa_{w}
of any residually transcendental valuation extension
w
of
T
on
F
with nonreal relative algebraic closure of
k_{1}
in
\kappa_{w}
has level at most
2^{\ell-1}
by
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
, since
p(k_{1}(X))\leqslant 2^{\ell}
by induction hypothesis. Moreover we conclude with

[
3
, Theorem 6.10]
that
p(K(X))\leqslant 2^{\ell}
from this induction hypothesis.

Now, by
Lemma

5.2
, the above mentioned inclusions of valuation sets, the induction hypotheses for
\rho_{\ell}
for function fields over
k_{1}
, and either
Corollary

4.6
or
Theorem

4.7
we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}(F)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}^{>\ell}(F)}\!\!\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})+\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{w\in\Omega^{\ell}_{T}(F)}\!\!1+\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})\,\leqslant\,\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}^{\text{r}}(F)}\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})+\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}^{\text{n/r}}(F)}1+\rho_{\ell}(\kappa_{w})

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}^{\text{r}}(F)}\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k_{1})(n-1)+\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}^{\text{n/r}}(F)}1+(\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k_{1})+1)(n-1)

\displaystyle\leqslant(n-1)\mathfrak{g}^{*}(F/K)+|\Omega_{T}^{\text{n/r}}(F)|\;\;\leqslant\;\;n\cdot\mathfrak{g}^{*}(F/K),

where
\mathfrak{g}^{*}(F/K):=\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1
when
F
is nonreal, and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}(F/K):=\mathfrak{g}(F/K)
when
F
is real, to keep the inequalities visually readable. Note that we applied
Corollary

4.6
, respectively
Theorem

4.7
in the penultimate, as well as the ultimate inequality.
∎

5.4 Example
.

Let
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell\geqslant 1.
It is well known that the pythagoras number of any function field in one variable over
\mathbb{R}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell-1})
is at most
2^{\ell}
, see
[
19
, Example 1.4 (4), chap. 7]
. Hence, the field
K=\mathbb{R}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell-1})(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)
satisfies the hypothesis of
Theorem

5.3
and we obtain

\rho_{\ell}(F)\leqslant n\cdot(\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1)

for any function field
F/K
in one variable, and

\rho_{\ell}(F)\leqslant n\cdot\mathfrak{g}(F/K)
when
F
is real. The case
\ell=1
is the case discussed in the introduction.

The optimality of the bound
\rho_{1}(F)\leqslant n\cdot(g+1)
from
Theorem

5.3
in the nonreal case is a consequence of
[
16
, Example 4.5]
: For
g\in\mathbb{N},
the curve

Y^{2}=-\prod_{i=0}^{g}(X^{2}+t_{n}^{2i})

over
\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)
has genus
g
, its function field is nonreal, and it was shown that
\rho_{1}(F)=n(g+1).
The following example shows optimality of the bound
\rho_{1}(F)\leqslant n\cdot g
in the case where
F
is real:

5.5 Example
.

Let
n,g\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
F
be the function field of the curve

Y^{2}=(X-1)\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{g}(X^{2}+t_{n}^{2i}),

over
K=\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!).
This curve has genus
g
and
F
is real. We will show that
\rho_{1}(F)=ng.

Let
v_{n}
be the unique discrete valuation on
K
(the
t_{n}
-adic one). Its residue field is
k=\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n-1})\!)
. For
1\leqslant j\leqslant g,
let
w^{\prime}_{j}
be the so called Gauss extension of
v_{n}
with respect to
Z=Xt_{n}^{-j},
and let
w_{j}
denote an extension of
w^{\prime}_{j}
to
F
. Note that
K(X)=K(Z).
Since
F=K(Z)(\sqrt{g(Z)})
with

g(Z)=(t_{n}^{j}Z-1)(t_{n}^{2j-2}Z^{2}+1)\cdots(t^{2}Z^{2}+1)\cdot(Z^{2}+1)\cdot(Z^{2}+t^{2})\cdots(Z^{2}+t_{n}^{2(g-j)})\in\mathcal{O}_{w^{\prime}_{j}},

we obtain that

\kappa_{w_{j}}=\kappa_{v_{n}}(\overline{Z})\left(\sqrt{-\overline{g(Z)}}\right)\simeq\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n-1})\!)(X)\left(\sqrt{-(X^{2}+1)}\right).

Thus
s(\kappa_{w_{j}})=2.
Since
k
admits a henselian
n-1
-discrete valuation and
\kappa_{w_{j}}/k
is a function field of genus zero, we have by
[
16
, Lemma 4.4]
that
\rho_{1}(\kappa_{w_{j}})=n-1.
By
[
16
, Theorem 3.6]
, we conclude that there must be
n-1
valuations on
\kappa_{w_{j}}

that are
d
-discrete for some
1\leqslant d\leqslant n-1
and whose residue fields have level
2
. After composition with the respective
w_{j}
, we obtain for each
j\leqslant g
a set of

n-1
valuations on
F
which are
d
-discrete for some
2\leqslant d\leqslant n
, and since the composition does not change the residue field by
[
16
, Remark 2.3]
, they are of level
2
.
Counting additionally each
w_{j}
as a
1
-discrete valuation, we obtain this way in total
gn
distinct (nonequivalent) valuations on
F
that are
d
-discrete for some
1\leqslant d\leqslant n
and with residue field of level
2
. Hence,
[
16
, Theorem 3.6]
shows that
\rho_{1}(F)\geqslant ng
.
Of course, we have equality by
Theorem

5.3
.

5.6 Question
.

Let
K=\mathbb{R}(X)(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)
. Is there a nonreal function field in one variable
F/K
such that
\rho_{2}(F)=n\cdot(\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1)
?
Is there a real function field
F/K
such that
\rho_{2}(F)=n\cdot\mathfrak{g}(F/K)
? If not, what is the correct formula for the optimal bound on
\rho_{2}
?

We end this section by analyzing why the genus inequality from
[
2
]
would not have sufficed to prove the bound
\rho_{1}(F)\leqslant n\cdot(\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1)
. In our notation, the genus inequality shown in
[
2
]
is

|\Omega^{0}_{T}(F)\setminus\Omega^{\mathrm{rat}}_{T}(F)|+\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}[\ell_{w}:k]\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)\,\leqslant\,\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1,

where
\Omega^{0}_{T}(F)=\{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)\mid\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k)=0\}
, and a condition for when equality can occur is also given, which we do not repeat here, but suffice to say that this condition is taken into account in the following:

Let
K=\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)(\!(t_{2})\!)
. Let us consider the situation where
F/K
is a nonreal function field of genus
3
. Note that, in difference to the almost similar looking
Corollary

4.6
, the above mentioned genus inequality does not exclude the possibility of the existence of
3
residually transcendental valuation extensions
w_{1},w_{2}
and
w_{3}
of the
t_{2}
-adic valuations on
K
, such that all
\kappa_{w_{i}}
have genus
1
over
\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)
and the latter is relative algebraically closed in
\kappa_{w_{i}}
.
Again, for each of
\kappa_{w_{i}}/\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)
, there is no obstruction coming from the genus inequality above that would prevent the existence of two residually transcendental valuation extensions
v_{i,1}
and
v_{i,2}
of the
t_{1}
-adic one to
\kappa_{w_{i}}
, such that
\kappa_{v_{i,j}}
are of genus zero over
\mathbb{R}
and such that
\mathbb{R}
is relatively algebraically closed in
\kappa_{v_{i,j}}
. In particular
\kappa_{v_{i,j}}\simeq\mathbb{R}(X)(\sqrt{-(X^{2}+1)})
, which has level
2
. This would force the existence of in total
6
distinct
2
-discrete valuations with residue field of level
2
on
F
. By
[
16
, Lemma 4.4]
, this implies that
\rho_{1}(F)\geqslant 2\cdot 6=12
. But we know from our previous result that
\rho_{1}(F)\leqslant 2(\mathfrak{g}(F/K)+1)=8
.

6.
A bound on Local squares

In this section, we assume that
K
is the field of fractions of a complete non-dyadic discrete valuation ring
T
. Let
k
denote its residue field.
Let
F/K
be a function field in one variable.
Let
\mathcal{L}(F)
denote the subgroup of
F^{\times}
consisting of elements that are squares in the completion
F^{w}
for every
\mathbb{Z}
-valuation
w
on
F
.

Let
\Sh(F)
denote the kernel of the product restriction morphism of Witt groups

W(F)\to\prod_{w\in\Omega(F)}W(F^{w}).

We refer to
[
14
]
for details about the construction and properties of Witt groups of quadratic forms over fields.

6.1 Lemma
.

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.07pt}{$\textstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.17558pt}{$\scriptstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.3889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}}\simeq\Sh(F).

Proof.

By the local-global principle for quadratic forms in
3
or more variables with respect to
\Omega_{T}(F)
, any nontrivial Witt class in
\Sh(F)
is defined by an anisotropic quadratic form of dimension
2
. Again, using this local-global principle, we can show that any scalar multiple of an anisotropic quadratic form of dimension two whose Witt class lies in
\Sh(F)
, defines the same Witt class.
So, any element in
\Sh(F)
is represented by an anisotropic quadratic form
X^{2}-cY^{2}
for some
c\in\mathcal{L}(F)
, and in fact two such
c
define the same Witt class if and only if their product is a square in
F
.
∎

6.2 Proposition
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a regular model with normal crossings for
F/T.
Let
\beta
be the Betti number of the dual graph
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
. Then

\left|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.07pt}{$\textstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.17558pt}{$\scriptstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.3889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}}\right|=2^{\beta}.

Proof.

One easily verifies that, since
\mathcal{X}_{s}
has only normal crossings in
\mathcal{X}
, our reduction graph
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
coincides with the reduction graph defined in
[
12
]
.
For a point
x\in\mathcal{X}_{s},
we denote by
F_{x}
the field of fractions of the completion of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}
with respect to its maximal ideal. By
[
12
, Theorem 9.6]
, the kernel of the product of restriction morphisms
\varphi:WF\rightarrow\displaystyle\prod_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{s}}WF_{x}
is isomorphic to the abelian
2
-group
\mathsf{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s})),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}),
where
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))
is the fundamental group of the graph
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
as a topological space. It follows by
[
13
, Proposition 1A.1 and Proposition 1A.2]
that the group
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}))
is freely generated by
\beta
elements. Hence
\mathsf{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s})),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\beta},
whereby
|\ker\varphi|=2^{\beta}
. On the other hand, we have by
[
12
, Proposition 9.11 (c)]
that
\ker(\varphi)=\Sh(F)
, and finally
\Sh(F)=\mathcal{L}(F)/F^{\times 2}
by the previous Lemma.
∎

Our final goal in this article is to establish the following inequality:

6.3 Theorem
.

Assume that
\mathsf{char}(k)=0
. Then

\mathsf{log}_{2}\left|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.07pt}{$\textstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.17558pt}{$\scriptstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{L}(F)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.3889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{F^{\times 2}}$}}}\right|\leqslant\mathfrak{g}(F/K)-\sum_{w\in\Omega_{T}(F)}[\ell_{w}:k]\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\kappa_{w}/k).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a regular model for
F/T
whose special fiber is a normal crossing divisor. Let
T^{\prime}
be a maximal unramified valuation ring extension of
T
inside an algebraic closure of
K
. Denote by
K^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime}
the field of fraction and residue field of
T^{\prime}
respectively. We denote

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{X}\times_{T}T^{\prime}
. It follows from
Lemma

3.3
that
\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))\leqslant\beta(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X^{\prime}}_{s})).

The statement now follows directly from
Proposition

6.2
with
Lemma

6.1
and
Corollary

4.2
.
∎

6.4 Remark
.

The assumption of residue characteristic zero is only needed in the proof for the inequality of
Corollary

4.2
, whose proof uses a cohomological flatness result in the case of residue characteristic zero. However, it is feasible that said inequality holds regardless of cohomological flatness, and so
Theorem

6.3
might hold without the assumption of residue characteristic zero.

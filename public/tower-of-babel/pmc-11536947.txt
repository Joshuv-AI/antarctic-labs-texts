The role of lncRNA TSIX in osteoarthritis pathogenesis: mechanistic insights and clinical biomarker potential
Authors: Liangchao Dong, Futao Ji, Xiu-Quan Guo, Gang-Gang Wang, Junhui Xie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study seeks to elucidate the expressions of lncRNA TSIX in Osteoarthritis (OA) and to explore its mechanisms in regulating OA progression.MethodsRT-qPCR was employed to analyze the expression of TSIX in OA patients classified by Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) grades. Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) was conducted to evaluate the diagnostic value of TSIX. Correlation between TSIX levels and clinical scores such as Lysholm and visual analogue scale (VAS) score was evaluated using Pearson method. IL-1β-induced SW1353 cells served as an in vitro model. The cell function were assessed by flow cytometry and cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. The relationship between TSIX and miR-320a was verified by luciferase reporting system, while bioinformatics approaches were utilized to predict the downstream target genes of miR-320a.ResultsThe findings revealed that TSIX level in OA patients was elevated compared to that of the control group, with a notable progressive increase in TSIX expression correlated with higher K-L grades. In OA patients, the Lysholm score showed a negative correlation with TSIX expression, while the VAS score displayed a positive correlation with TSIX levels. Cell studies demonstrated that inhibition of TSIX enhanced cell viability and mitigated IL-1β-induced apoptosis by targeting miR-320a, in addition to promoting Aggrecan and Collagen II secretion. Luciferase reporter assay further validated the targeting interaction among TSIX, miR-320a, and PTEN.ConclusionsThis study demonstrated an increased expression of TSIX in OA patients. It suggests that TSIX may play a role in chondrocyte dysfunction during OA by modulating the miR-320a/PTEN axis.Keywords: TSIX, miR-320a, PTEN, osteoarthritis, diagnosis

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a prevalent chronic joint disease characterized by the degradation of cartilage, the formation of new bone on joint surface, and the development of osteophytes [1]. The increasing incidence of OA among individuals over 60 years of age poses a significant public health challenge globally [2]. OA, osteoporosis, rheumatoid arthritis and tendon injury are all diseases that affect the motor function of the body. At present, the clinical treatment of such diseases mainly relies on drugs and surgery. However, there is evidence that small interfering RNAs are potential therapeutic targets for these diseases, which involve bone metabolism, inflammatory processes, and protein regulation [3–5]. Research has established that OA exhibits a strong hereditary component and is intricately linked to inflammatory responses [6]. While early diagnosis primarily relies on imaging techniques, the identification of blood biomarkers has illuminated new possibilities for serological diagnosis. Despite advancements, the etiology of OA remains incompletely understood, and a specific treatment approach is yet to be established. Therefore, it is imperative to delve into the molecular mechanism underlying OA to enhance its diagnosis and treatment.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are endogenous RNA molecules exceeding 200 nucleotides in length, found within eukaryotic cells, and are highly conserved across various mammalian species. They have emerged as pivotal regulators in the onset and progression of inflammatory diseases [7, 8]. Increasing evidence indicates that lncRNA is intricately involved in the pathogenesis of OA. For instance, SNHG7 has been implicated in modulating inflammatory response signaling pathways, including the Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and NF-κB signaling pathways [9]. Other lncRNAs, such as PMS2L2 and SNHG1, play significant roles in regulating chondrocyte proliferation, apoptosis, and vascular regeneration [10, 11]. Notably. lncRNA TSIX, an X chromosome inactivation antisense repressor located on the X chromosome, is crucial for the regulation of chromosome selection and the timing of X chromosome inactivation in female mammals [12]. Currently, abnormally expressed TSIX has been observed in human tumors, including breast cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma [13, 14]. Recently, investigations into lncRNAs associated with bone formation have identified TSIX as a promoter of osteoblast apoptosis, contributing to fracture healing both in vivo and in vitro [15]. Furthermore, Yanmin et al. found that TSIX enhances the apoptosis of osteoblasts in osteolysis by reducing the expression of miR-30a-5p [16]. Given this evidence, we hypothesize that TSIX may play a significant role in OA.

In this study, the expression level of TSIX in serum of patients with OA was detected and analyzed, and the diagnostic value of TSIX in OA was also evaluated. Additionally, in in vitro studies, IL-β-induced Human chondrosarcoma cells were employed to establish the OA cell model in vitro, exploring the interplay between TSIX and miR-320a in the process of chondrocyte injury.

Materials and methods

GSE163552 dataset

The microarray data for peripheral blood mononuclear cells of OA patients and a normal control population were sourced from the gene expression database, specifically utilizing the dataset GSE163552. Subsequently, the expression matrix derived from the GSE163552 dataset was analyzed via the GEO database. To identify differentially expressed lncRNAs, the adjusted P value was set to be less than 0.05 and the absolute value of the expression difference multiple between samples after logarithmic processing with a base of 2 was set to be greater than 1. A log2FC > 1 indicates an upregulation of the lncRNA expression in OA patients, while log2FC<-1 indicates a downregulation in OA patients. The findings obtained are visually represented in a volcano plot.

Patients

According to the guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of OA issued by the Joint Surgery Group of the Orthopedics Branch of the Chinese Medical Association and the Chinese Journal of Orthopaedics in 2018, as well as the definition of knee OA provided by the American College of Rheumatology, 95 patients with knee OA were included from the outpatient services, all of whom had medial single compartment OA of the knee. Exclusion criteria included: (1) intra-articular corticosteroid injection within 3 months prior to the start of the study; (2) Intra-articular injection of hyaluronic acid within the past six months; (3) Patients with a history of joint surgery; (4) Comorbidities including severe cardiovascular disease, cognitive impairment, or overlapping symptoms such as rheumatoid arthritis. Additionally, healthy individuals matched for age and gender from the medical examination department were included as the control group. This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhucheng People’s Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from all the participants.

Sample and data collection

After enrollment, venous blood was collected for gene expression analysis and laboratory measurements. The Lysholm score was then assessed for both groups of subjects, encompassing eight criteria: lameness, pain, the necessity of support, the presence of locking sensations, joint instability, swelling, ease of navigating stairs, and the ability to squat. This scoring system ranges from 0 to 100 points, where a higher score indicates superior knee joint functionality. Additionally, the Visual analogue scale (VAS) scores were compared between the two groups, with a range from 0 to 10 points, wherein a higher score signifies greater pain intensity. The radiographic classification of OA follows the Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) grading system, stratified into five levels: Grade 0: normal conditions; Grade I: mild osteophyte formation; Grade II: the presence of visible osteophytes without joint space involvement; Grade III: moderate narrowing of joint space; Grade IV: pronounced joint space reduction accompanied by subchondral bone sclerosis.

RT-qPCR

The primers of TSIX, miR-320a, GAPDH and U6 were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). Total RNA was extracted from serum or cells using TRIzol method, followed reverse transcription of RNA into cDNA utilizing a reverse transcription kit. The cDNA was subsequently amplified according to the specific steps outlined in the kit instructions. The expression levels of TSIX and miR-320a following amplification were analyzed by 2−△△Ct method.

Cell culture and cell transfection

Human chondrosarcoma cells SW1353 exhibit a cell phenotype similar to that of normal human chondrocytes. Therefore, SW1353 cells were selected as a suitable substitute for normal human chondrocytes in this experiment. SW1353 cells were counted and inoculated into a cell culture dish containing DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100IU/mL penicillin, 100 µg/mL streptomycin. They were cultured at 37℃ in 5% CO2 incubator. Based on published literature and preliminary experimental results, a concentration of 10 ng/mL IL-1β was chosen as an inflammatory inducer to induce an inflammatory response in SW1353 cells, thereby simulating the inflammatory state of chondrocytes associated with OA.

SW1353 cells, in the logarithmic growth phase and exhibiting robust viability, were inoculated into 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 105/cm2. The experimental procedure commenced once the cells achieved approximately 60% confluence. The cells were categorized into four groups: a control group, a model group, model group + si-NC group, and model group + si-TSIX group. Following the kit instructions, Lipofectamine 2000 was employed to transfect the aforementioned materials into SW1353 cells, thereby modulating the intracellular TSIX expression level.

Cell apoptosis

After treatment according to the experimental protocol, the cells were collected and washed once with PBS buffer, followed by centrifugation at 300 r/min for 5 min. The cells were then suspended and counted in PBS, adjusted the concentration to (1–5) × 105 cells/mL before another centrifugation for collection. Subsequently, 100 µL of the pre-configured 1× Annexin V Binding Buffer were added to resuspend the cells, along with 2.5 µL of Annexin V-FITC dye and 2.5 µL of PI dye. The mixture was incubated at room temperature in the absence of light for 30 min, after which cell apoptosis was analyzed using flow cytometry. The entire experimental procedure was repeated three times to ensure reliability.

Cell viability

The cells were inoculated in 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 103 cells per well and cultured in a constant temperature incubator maintained at 37℃ with 5% CO2. Following a 24-hour transfection period, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was introduced to each well and incubated at 37℃ for 2 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was detected using an enzyme-labeled instrument. This above experiment was repeated three times.

Bioinformatics analysis

The target genes of miR-320a were predicted using the ENCORI, miRDB, EVmiRNA and TargetScan databases. A Venn diagram was constructed to illustrate the overlap among the predicted target genes, allowing for the selection of those at the intersection for subsequent analysis. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genome (KEGG) pathway enrichment were performed using an online bioinformatics resource (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) for the 153 downstream target genes predicted to intersect. These 153 target genes were subsequently uploaded to the STRING (v11.5) database (https://cn.string-db.org/) for Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) analysis, with the results visualized in Cytoscape (v3.9.1).

Target relationship prediction and luciferase reporter gene

Using the miRDB database, it was predicted that TSIX and miR-320a possess complementary binding sites. The ENCORI database was employed to forecast the complementary binding sites of miR-320a and PTEN. To verify the interaction between TSIX and miR-320a, as well as between miR-320a and PTEN, luciferase reporter assays were utilized. The sequences of TSIX or PTEN containing either the miR-320a binding sites or mutated sequences were cloned into pmirGLO vectors, guided by bioinformatics analysis. The mutant sequences were synthesized by GenePharma. Co., LTD (Shanghai, China). Using Lipofectamine 2000, these vectors, along with miR-320a mimic or inhibitor, were transfected into into SW1353 cells. Following a 48-hour incubation period post-transfection, luciferase activity was measured using a dual-luciferase reporting analysis system.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data were analyzed using SPSS 17.0 and GraphPad Prism 7.0 software. Normally distributed data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For comparisons between two groups, a t-test was employed, while one-way ANOVA was used for comparisons among multiple groups. Qualitative data are expressed as n (%) and analyzed by the Chi-square test. The ROC curve was generated to evaluate the clinical diagnostic value of TSIX in OA. Additionally, the Pearson correlation coefficient was calculated to examine the relationship between the Lysholm score or VAS score and TSIX expression level. A P-value of < 0.05 was deemed indicative of a statistically significant difference.

Results

Baseline data of OA patients compared with healthy volunteers

A total of 170 subjects were included in this study, comprising 95 patients diagnosed with OA and 75 healthy volunteers. The baseline data and clinical indicators of both groups are summarized in Table 1. The findings revealed no statistically significant differences in age, sex composition and body mass index (BMI) between the two groups (P > 0.05), suggesting that they were comparable. Notably, levels of C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), interleukin-1beta (IL-1β), white blood cell count (WBC) and VAS scores in OA patients were significantly elevated compared to those in the control group, while Lysholm scores were markedly lower (P < 0.001). Furthermore, according to K-L grading system, there were no patients with OA classified as grade I, 30 patients as grade II, 38 patients as grade III, and 27 patients as grade IV.

Analysis of expression level and clinical value of TSIX

In the GSE163552 dataset, a volcano plot was constructed to illustrate the expression levels of lncRNAs in peripheral blood mononuclear cells of OA patients compared to the healthy controls. Up-regulated lncRNAs are denoted by red dots, while down-regulated lncRNAs are represented by blue dots. Notably, TSIX emerged as a significantly upregulated lncRNA (Fig. 1A, P < 0.05). In addition, TSIX exhibited marked upregulation in the serum of patients with OA relative to healthy controls (Fig. 1B, P < 0.001). As shown in Fig. 1C, ROC analysis showed that TSIX curve yielded an AUC value of 0.937, with the sensitivity and specificity of 86.3% and 88.0%, respectively, highlighting its substantial diagnostic accuracy in differentiating between healthy individuals and OA patients. Moreover, in accordance with the K-L classification of OA patients, a progressive increase in TSIX expression levels was observed with advancing classification (Fig. 1D, P < 0.001).

Effect of TSIX on SW1353 cell function

A significant increase in TSIX expression was observed in SW1535 cells upon induction by IL-1β. This elevation in TSIX was significantly mitigated when cells were transfected with si-TSIX (Fig. 3A, P < 0.001). Functionally, IL-1β induction markedly enhanced apoptosis while concurrently diminishing cell viability; conversely, the down-regulation of TSIX effectively reduced the number of apoptotic cells and improved cell viability (Fig. 3B-C, P < 0.001). Furthermore, IL-1β was shown to upregulate MMP-9 and downregulate Aggrecan and Collagen II, whereas the downregulation of TSIX effectively inhibited the expression levels of these factors (Fig. 3D, P < 0.001).

Verification of the targeting relationship between TSIX and miR-320a

As demonstrated in Fig. 4A, the miRDB database indicated that TSIX and miR-320a possess complementary binding sites. The luciferase reporter gene showed that miR-320a mimics or inhibitors significantly decreased or increased luciferase activity in the WT-TSIX group (P < 0.001) whereas no such effect was observed in the MUT-TSIX group (Fig. 4B, P > 0.05). In cellular experiments, it was noted that IL-1β induced a reduction in miR-320a expression, and transfection with si-TSIX could significantly inhibit the suppressive effect of IL-1β on miR-320a (Fig. 4C, P < 0.01). Additionally, the expression of miR-320a was modulated through cell transfection to evaluate its regulatory impact on cellular function. The results showed that transfection of si-TSIX significantly increased the intracellular miR-320a levels post IL-1β induction, an effect that could be counteracted by miR-320a inhibitors (Fig. 4D, P < 0.01). The upregulation of miR-320a substantially inhibited apoptosis and enhanced cell viability; conversely, the addition of miR-320a inhibitors resulted in increased apoptotic cell numbers and reduced cell viability (Fig. 4E-F, P < 0.01). Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 4G, miR-320a was upregulated, leading to decreased expression of MMP-9. In scenarios where miR-320a levels were diminished, the production of MMP-9 was increased (P < 0.01). It was also observed that the reduction of miR-320a correlated with decreased intracellular levels of Aggrecan and Collagen II (P < 0.05).

Prediction and bioinformatics analysis of downstream target genes of miR-320a

Online databases were utilized to predict the downstream target genes of miR-320a, revealing that the four databases collectively identified at least 153 potential target genes of miR-320a (see Fig. 5A; Table 3). Following this, GO analysis and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis were performed for these 153 target genes. The findings indicated that these genes were predominantly enriched in the perinuclear endoplasmic reticulum and polyribosomes, playing significant roles in a various biological processes, including cell adhesion, stem cell differentiation, and the regulation of protein localization to peripheral cells. The molecular mechanisms implicated included phosphotyrosine residue binding, protein phosphorylated amino acid binding, and transcriptional coregulatory activity (see Fig. 5B). Furthermore, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis demonstrated that these 153 target genes were chiefly associated with the AMPK signaling pathway and were involved in transcriptional dysregulation related to cell aging and cancer (see Fig. 5C). Additionally, as shown in Fig. 5D, it can be observed from the PPI network that the proteins regulated by miR-320a comprise multiple nodes, prominently featuring PTEN, MAPK1, AKT3, PIK3R1, etc.

Discussion

In this study, clinical observations revealed that TSIX expression was significantly up-regulated in patients with OA, with a notable increase corresponding to the increase K-L grading progression. ROC analysis indicated that TSIX exhibited a sensitivity of 86.3% and a specificity of 88.0% in distinguishing healthy individuals from those with OA. Pearson correlation analysis further showed a negative correlation between TSIX levels and the Lysholm score, while a positive correlation was found with the VAS score. In cellular studies, it was found that IL-1β induction led to reduced chondrocyte proliferation, increased apoptosis, abnormal secretion of the extracellular matrix, and increased TSIX expression, suggesting that TSIX may play a crucial role in IL-1β-induced chondrocyte inflammation. Additionally, using predictive database analyses, the target gene of TSIX, miR-320a, was identified. Experimental validation confirmed that TSIX negatively regulates the expression of miR-320a. A reduction in TSIX resulted in the upregulation of miR-320a, thereby alleviating IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury.

The occurrence of OA is intricately linked to cellular anabolic factors, inflammatory mediators, and hormones [17]. At its core, OA is characterized by the degeneration of articular cartilage and a disruption in the homeostasis of repair mechanisms [18]. While current basic therapies, including pharmacological interventions, are effective in modifying the progressive loss of articular cartilage, they fall short of regenerating articular chondrocytes. To establish effective regeneration or preventive treatments for cartilage diseases, a deeper understanding of the pathogenesis of OA is essential.

In-depth studies of lncRNAs have confirmed their involvement in a myriad of biological processes, including histone modification, chromatin remodeling, DNA methylation, and various transcriptional and post-transcriptional modifications, thereby serving as regulators of target gene expression [19, 20]. Recent investigations have revealed a significant correlation between lncRNAs and immune and inflammatory disorders. For example, GAS5 has been implicated in the pathogenesis of systemic lupus erythematosus [21], while RNU12 emerges as a potential marker for psoriatic arthritis risk [22]. In our analysis of the GSE163552 dataset, we found an abnormal elevation of TSIX in PBMCs of OA patients. This upregulation of TSIX has also been documented in several inflammatory diseases, including bronchial asthma [23] and spinal cord inflammatory injuries [24]. Notably, this study found that the expression level of TSIX was heightened in the serum of OA patients, aligning with the findings from the GSE163552 dataset, thereby indicating a potential link between TSIX abnormalities and the onset of OA.

MicroRNA (miRNA) is a class of small non-coding RNAs, which are generally believed to inhibit mRNA translation or interpretation by binding to the 3 ‘untranslated region of their target mRNA, thus achieving gene regulation [25, 26]. As a kind of non-coding RNA, lncRNA can function as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) and inhibit miRNA through lncRNA-miRNA sponge-like binding [27]. In theory, lncRNA binding with miRNA can interfere with the inhibition of miRNA on downstream target gene expression [28, 29]. This dynamic establishes a negative correlation between lncRNA and corresponding miRNA levels, while positively correlated with the expression of downstream target genes. Numerous studies have demonstrated that specific lncRNAs exert multi-target and multi-level regulatory effects. Wang et al. reported a reduced expression of FOXD2-AS1 in OA, revealing its role in promoting chondrocyte proliferation via the miR-27a/TLR4 axis, thereby inhibiting the progression of OA [30]. Xu et al. reported that MCM3AP-AS1, as a molecular sponge or epigenetic regulator of miR-149-5p, promoted the expression of Notch1, and whether it inhibited the ectopic expression of miR-149-5p or Notch1, it could significantly weaken the protective effects of MCM3AP-AS1 silencing on autophagy, ECM degradation and cartilage injury [31]. In this study, the miRDB database predicted that TSIX and miR-320a had complementary sites. Further, the dual-luciferase experiment validated a significant decrease in enzymatic activity in the TSIX wild-type vector + miR-320a mimic group compared to the TSIX wild-type vector + mimic-NC group. In contrast, the fluorescence of the TSIX mutant vector + miR-320a mimic group remained unchanged relative to the TSIX mutant vector + mimic-NC group. This compelling evidence underscores the targeting relationship between TSIX and miR-320a, a connection previously validated in the study of Sun et al. on gastric adenocarcinoma [32].

A large amount of experimental evidence shows that miRNA plays a regulatory role at the post-transcriptional level, affecting the expression and biological role of target genes, and then influencing the occurrence and development of OA. miR-320a has been commonly reported in OA, and it has been mentioned in several studies as a protective factor for OA. For example, In an in vitro model of IL-1β-induced osteoarthritis, miR-320a expression was reduced, and overexpression of miR-320a restored the inhibitory effect of IL-1β on chondrocytes [33]. Of course, there were also conflicting results. Jin et al. reported that miR-320a was upregulated in OA cartilage tissue and IL-1β-stimulated chondrocytes, and overexpression of miR-320a enhanced IL-1β-induced cartilage degradation [34]. In this study, intracellular miR-320a was significantly reduced after IL-1β induction, and the reduction of miR-320a further weakened the inhibitory effect of silencing TSIX on cell viability and promoting apoptosis and inflammation. In subsequent studies, bioinformatics analysis identified PTEN as a possible target gene of miR-320a. This relationship has been confirmed earlier in Jiang et al. ‘s study on the treatment of skeletal muscle injury [35].

The limitations of this study are as follows: The small sample size and reliance on a single source of clinical studies may introduce selection bias, potentially compromising the stability of the experimental results. To enhance the reliability of these findings, future prospective studies should aim to expand the sample size and incorporate multi-center data. In addition, the absence of animal testing is a notable gap; in vitro cell experiments alone may not suffice to fully elucidate the regulatory effects of TSIX and miR-320a on chondrocyte inflammation. Besides, in this study, SW1353 cell line is currently used for in vitro research. Although this cell line has many chondrocyte characteristics, it is not chondrocyte after all, nor can it completely replace the in vivo environment, so the next step needs to use primary chondrocytes for further exploration. The development and outcome of OA is multi-center and multi-link. For example, abnormal changes in synovial tissue around joints and subchondral bone always run through the entire OA process. While this study has explored genes, proteins, and cellular functions, further investigation into the biomolecular mechanisms underlying OA is essential for a comprehensive understanding of the condition.

Conclusions

In summary, the expression of TSIX is notably elevated in OA, demonstrating its potential clinical diagnostic utility for this condition. In vitro studies indicate that TSIX functions as a ceRNA molecule for miR-320a, thereby playing a significant role in the onset and progression of OA. These findings suggest that TSIX may serve as a promising therapeutic target for OA.

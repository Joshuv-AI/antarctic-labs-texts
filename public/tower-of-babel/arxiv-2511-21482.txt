Existence results for quasimonotone semilinear coupled elliptic systems via sub-supersolution method Thanks: Corresponding author: Briceyda B. Delgado (briceyda.delgado@infotec.edu.mx)
Shalmali Bandyopadhyay, Briceyda B. Delgado, Nsoki Mavinga, Maria Amarakristi Onydio

Abstract
. We establish the existence of weak solutions of coupled systems of elliptic partial differential equations with quasimonotone nonlinearities in the domain interior and on the boundary. When the nonlinearities satisfy some monotonicity conditions, we employ monotone iteration techniques to establish the existence of minimal and maximal weak solutions between an ordered pair of sub- and supersolution. In the absence of monotonicity, we prove an existence result when the nonlinearities satisfy certain growth conditions. In addition, we provide concrete examples that illustrate the applicability of our theoretical results.



Keywords:
Nonlinear elliptic systems; nonlinear boundary conditions; subsolution; supersolution; maximal and minimal weak solutions; monotonicity methods.

1.  Introduction

In this paper, we investigate a class of semilinear coupled elliptic systems with nonlinearities present both in the differential equation and on the boundary condition. In particular, we consider the following problem:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta u_{1}+c_{1}(x)u_{1}&=&f_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2}),&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
-\Delta u_{2}+c_{2}(x)u_{2}&=&f_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2}),&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\eta}&=&g_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2})&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\eta}&=&g_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2})&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain with
C^{0,1}
boundary, denoted by

\partial\Omega
and
\partial/\partial\eta:=\eta(x)\cdot\nabla
denotes the outer normal derivative on the boundary.

For each
i=1,2
,
f_{i}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g_{i}:\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
are Carathéodory functions; that is,
f_{i}(\cdot,u_{1},u_{2})
and
g_{i}(\cdot,u_{1},u_{2})
are measurable for all
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
f_{i}(x,\cdot,\cdot)
and
g_{i}(x,\cdot,\cdot)
are continuous for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Throughout this paper, we assume that each
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
satisfy the following quasimonotonicity condition:

(Q)

f_{i}
is quasimonotone nondecreasing in the sense that
f_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2})
is nondecreasing in
u_{2}
for all fixed
x\in\Omega,u_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2})
is nondecreasing in
u_{1}
for all fixed
x\in\Omega,u_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and similarly for
g_{i}
.

Additionally, we assume that the functions
c_{i}
’s;
i=1,2
satisfy the following

(

)

and

Nonlinear elliptic problems with nonlinear boundary conditions arise naturally in applications where interactions occur both within the domain and at its boundary. These problems arise in chemical reactions
[
12
]
, biological processes
[
7
]
, and ecological dynamics
[
3
]
, where species interactions or chemical reactions occur in narrow boundary layers. Finding solutions becomes challenging when the nonlinearities have less regularity and solutions may be unbounded.

The sub-supersolution method is a fundamental tool for studying such problems. This technique was originally developed for classical solutions by Amann
[
1
]
, Sattinger
[
19
]
, and Pao
[
18
]
to establish the existence of solutions between ordered pairs of barrier functions. For problems with linear boundary conditions, this method has been extensively developed and applied to obtain both classical and weak solutions, see
[
9
]
for quasilinear problems and
[
8
]
for a comprehensive treatment. The situation becomes more complex when nonlinear boundary conditions are present. For classical solutions, in
[
2
,
18
]
the authors established results for monotone nonlinearities via monotone iterative methods. Recent advances have focused on weak solution frameworks to handle less regular data and nonlinearities.

The foundational work of Mitidieri and Sweers
[
17
]
established existence of maximal solutions for quasimonotone elliptic systems with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions using monotone iteration techniques for the monotone case. Cuesta Leon
[
9
]
extended this framework to weak solutions of quasimonotone p-Laplacian systems with Dirichlet boundary conditions, addressing both monotone and nonmonotone cases through monotone iterations and Zorn’s lemma, respectively. Recently, Bandyopadhyay et al.
[
4
]
studied the existence of a minimal and a maximal weak solution between ordered pairs of sub and supersolutions for elliptic scalar equations with nonlinear boundary conditions. Later, Bandyopadhyay, Lewis, and Mavinga
[
6
]
extended the results to coupled systems with linear interior equations and quasimonotone nonlinear boundary conditions, treating both monotone and nonmonotone cases. For scalar equations, Mavinga, Myers, and Nkashama
[
15
]
investigated problems with monotone nonlinearities in both the interior and on the boundary using monotone iteration methods, while the companion work
[
16
]
addressed the corresponding nonmonotone case. Our goal is to extend these results to coupled quasimonotone systems with nonlinearities in both the domain interior and on the boundary, treating both monotone and nonmonotone cases through monotone iteration techniques when monotonicity conditions are satisfied, and Zorn’s lemma combined with Kato’s inequality when they are not.

2.  Preliminaries

This section presents existence theory for the linear elliptic system and the scalar problem with nonlinear boundary conditions, both available in the existing literature and required for our purposes. We also establish Kato’s inequality for single equations, showing that the maximum of two subsolutions remains a subsolution and the minimum of two supersolutions remains a supersolution. We begin by defining the norm corresponding to our framework, which turns out to be equivalent to the standard
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
norm.

Consider the bilinear forms
A_{i}\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2
, defined as follows

A_{i}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega}c_{i}(x)uv,\qquad i=1,2.

Then

\displaystyle|A_{i}(u-v,u-v)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}c_{i}(u-v)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,\|c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}\|u-v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We denote by
\|u\|_{c_{i}}
the norm induced by
A_{i}
. More precisely,

(2.1)

\|u\|^{2}_{c_{i}}=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u)^{2}+\int_{\Omega}c_{i}(x)u^{2},\quad i=1,2.

Then,
\|u\|_{c_{i}}
and
\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
are equivalent norms. If
(u_{1},u_{2})\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
1.1
), then taking as a test function
\psi=u_{i}
for
i=1,2
in the weak formulation provided in Definition
1.1
we have that the left-hand side of the weak formulation formula becomes
\|u_{i}\|_{c_{i}}^{2}
,
i=1,2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[Existence and uniqueness for the linear elliptic system]
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. Consider the system

\displaystyle-\Delta w_{i}+c_{i}(x)w_{i}+\kappa w_{i}

\displaystyle=f_{i}(x),\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{i}}{\partial\eta}+\kappa w_{i}

\displaystyle=g_{i}(x),\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

for

, where

a.e. in

Proof.

See

Proposition 2.2
.

[Kato’s inequality for single equations]
Let
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
c\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
. Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be functions in
H^{1}(\Omega)
such that there exist
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in
L^{r}(\Omega)
with
r\geq\frac{2N}{N+2}
, and
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
in
L^{s}(\partial\Omega)
with
s\geq\frac{2(N-1)}{N}
, satisfying

(2.2)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla u_{i}\nabla\psi+c(x)u_{i}\psi\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{i}\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{i}\psi\qquad\text{for all }\ 0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,,

for
i=1,2.

Then,
u:=\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla u\nabla\psi+c(x)u\psi\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}f\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g\psi\qquad\text{for all }\ 0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,,

where

f(x):=\begin{cases}f_{1}(x)&\quad\text{if}\ u_{1}(x)>u_{2}(x)\\
f_{2}(x)&\quad\text{if}\ u_{1}(x)\leq u_{2}(x),\end{cases}

a.e.
x\in\Omega

and

g(x):=\begin{cases}g_{1}(x)&\quad\text{if}\ u_{1}(x)>u_{2}(x)\\
g_{2}(x)&\quad\text{if}\ u_{1}(x)\leq u_{2}(x),\end{cases}

a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

Define

\Omega_{1}:=\{x\in\Omega:u_{1}(x)>u_{2}(x)\}\;{\text{and}}\ \Omega_{2}:=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1}\,

and

\Gamma_{1}:=\{x\in\partial\Omega:u_{1}(x)>u_{2}(x)\}\;{\text{and}}\;\Gamma_{2}:=\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{1}\,.

Fix
0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then,

\begin{split}I&=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c(x)u\psi\\
&=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left(\nabla u_{1}\nabla\psi+c(x)u_{1}\psi\right)}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left(\nabla u_{2}\nabla\psi+c(x)u_{2}\psi\right)}_{I_{2}}\,.\end{split}

Consider a sequence
\xi_{n}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\xi_{n}(t):=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\ t\geq 1/n\\
0\quad\text{if}\ t\leq 0,\end{cases}

and
\xi_{n}^{\prime}>0
on
(0,1/n).

Then, define the sequence of functions

r_{n}(x):=\xi_{n}((u_{1}-u_{2})(x))\quad\quad\text{for}\;x\in\overline{\Omega}\,.

Observe that
r_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
r_{n}
converges point-wise to
\displaystyle\chi_{\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{1}}
, where the characteristic function is defined as
\displaystyle\chi_{\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{1}}(x):=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\ x\in\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{1}\;\\
0\quad\text{if}\ \text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Moreover,
||r_{n}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}}\leq 1
and

\text{supp}(\nabla r_{n})\subset\overline{D}_{n}
, where
D_{n}:=\{x\in\Omega:0<u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x)<\frac{1}{n}\}
.
Then, using the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have that

\displaystyle I_{1}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\int_{\Omega}r_{n}\nabla u_{1}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}r_{n}c(x)u_{1}\psi\right].

Since
r_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}
, it follows that
r_{n}\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for any test function
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Recalling that
\nabla r_{n}=0
on
\Omega\setminus D_{n}
, and that
u_{1}
satisfies (
2.2
), we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}r_{n}\nabla u_{1}\nabla\psi+r_{n}c(x)u_{1}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1}\nabla(r_{n}\psi)+c(x)u_{1}(r_{n}\psi)-\int_{D_{n}}\psi\nabla u_{1}\nabla r_{n}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(r_{n}\psi)+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(r_{n}\psi)-\int_{D_{n}}\psi\nabla u_{1}\nabla r_{n}\,.

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
for the first and second terms on the right-hand side of (
2.3
), using the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, we get

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{1}r_{n}\psi=\int_{\Omega_{1}}f_{1}\psi\;\mbox{ and }\;\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}r_{n}\psi=\int_{\Gamma_{1}}g_{1}\psi\,.

Likewise, for
I_{2}
we have

\displaystyle I_{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\int_{\Omega}(1-r_{n})\nabla u_{2}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(1-r_{n})c(x)u_{2}\psi\right],

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(1-r_{n})\nabla u_{2}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(1-r_{n})c(x)u_{2}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{2}\nabla[(1-r_{n})\psi]+c(x)u_{2}(1-r_{n})\psi+\int_{D_{n}}\psi\nabla u_{2}\nabla r_{n}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(1-r_{n})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(1-r_{n})\psi+\int_{D_{n}}\psi\nabla u_{2}\nabla r_{n}.

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
for the first and second terms on the right-hand side of (
2.4
) and using the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, we get

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(1-r_{n})\psi=\int_{\Omega_{2}}f_{2}\psi\;\mbox{ and }\;\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(1-r_{n})\psi=\int_{\Gamma_{2}}g_{2}\psi\,.

Using the fact that
\nabla r_{n}=\xi_{n}^{\prime}(u_{1}-u_{2})\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})
, the sum of the second terms of the right-hand side of (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) yields

\displaystyle-\int_{D_{n}}\psi\nabla u_{1}\nabla r_{n}+\int_{D_{n}}\psi\nabla u_{2}\nabla r_{n}

\displaystyle=-\int_{D_{n}}\psi\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})\nabla r_{n}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=-\int_{D_{n}}\psi\xi_{n}^{\prime}(u_{1}-u_{2})|\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})|^{2}\leq 0,

since
\psi\geq 0\;{\text{and}}\;\xi_{n}^{\prime}\geq 0
.
Adding (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), taking the limit, and using (
2.5
), we get

I=I_{1}+I_{2}\leq\int_{\Omega_{1}}f_{1}\psi+\int_{\Omega_{2}}f_{2}\psi+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}g_{1}\psi+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}g_{2}\psi=\int_{\Omega}f\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g\psi\,.

Thus,
u:=\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla u\nabla\psi+c(x)u\psi\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}f\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g\psi\qquad\text{for all }\ 0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,,

completing the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Proof.

Using the fact that

Let

L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)
and
g_{i}(x,u_{1,n},u_{2,n})+ku_{i,n}
to
g_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})+ku_{i,*}
in
L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}(\partial\Omega)
for
i=1,2
. Hence, both sequences converge weakly, that is, for all
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{i}(x,u_{1,n},u_{2,n})+ku_{i,n}\right)\psi=\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})+ku_{i,*}\right)\psi.

Step 2:
We show that
f_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})\in L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)
and
g_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})\in L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}(\partial\Omega)
for
i=1,2
. By the Rellich-Kondrachov embedding result
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)
and since
\frac{2N}{N-2}>\frac{2N}{N+2}
, then
u_{i,*}\in L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\|f_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|f_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})+ku_{i,*}-ku_{i,*}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})+ku_{i,*}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)}+\|ku_{i,*}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)}\leq C,

where
C
is a positive constant. By similar arguments, we can show that
g_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})\in L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}(\partial\Omega)
for
i=1,2
.

Step 3:
Now, we verify that
(u_{1,*},u_{2,*})
and
(u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*})
are weak solutions of (
1.1
). We take the limit in (
3
). By using (
3.14
), we obtain that for all
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i,*}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(c_{i}(x)+k)u_{i,*}\,\psi\,+k\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{i,*}\psi

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i,n}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(c_{i}(x)+k)u_{i,n}\,\psi\right)\,+k\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{i,n}\psi

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i,n}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{i}(x)u_{i,n}\,\psi\right)\,+k\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{i,n}\,\psi\,+\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{i,n}\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{i}(x,u_{1,n-1},u_{2,n-1})+ku_{i,n-1}\right)\,\psi+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(g_{i}(x,u_{1,n-1},u_{2,n-1})+ku_{i,n-1}\right)\,\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})+ku_{i,*}\right)\,\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(g_{i}(x,u_{1,*},u_{2,*})+ku_{i,*}\right)\,\psi\,

which implies that
(u_{1,*},u_{2,*})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
). Similarly, we can construct the maximal weak solution
(u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*})
to (
1.1
) by taking the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}

(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})=(u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*})
.

Finally, we will show that
(u_{1,*},u_{2,*})
is a minimal weak solution and
(u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*})
is a maximal weak solution to (
1.1
) in the interval
[(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2}),(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})]
. Let
(v_{1},v_{2})
be a weak solution to (
1.1
) such that
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(v_{1},v_{2})\leq(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})
. Then
(v_{1},v_{2})
is also a weak supersolution to (
1.1
). Repeating the above monotone iteration process with
(u_{1,0},u_{2,0})=(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})
, we get that
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(u_{1,*},u_{2,*})\leq(v_{1},v_{2})
. Thus,
(u_{1,*},u_{2,*})
is a minimal weak solution of (
1.1
). Similarly,
(u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*})
is a maximal weak solution to (
1.1
).

4.  Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
using Zorn’s lemma applied to an appropriately defined set of subsolutions. We begin by constructing the set of subsolutions. Let
(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})\in J
be fixed, where
J
is given in (

). We show that there exists a subsolution of (
1.1
),
(w_{1},w_{2})\in J
, satisfying
(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})\leq(w_{1},w_{2})
with
\|(w_{1},w_{2})\|_{(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}}\leq C
where

does not depend on
(w_{1},w_{2})

Indeed, with
\widetilde{u}_{2}
fixed, consider the scalar equation

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta u_{1}+c_{1}(x)u_{1}&=&f_{1}(x,u_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})&\text{in}\;\Omega;\\
\dfrac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\eta}&=&g_{1}(x,u_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Define
\overline{f}_{1}(x,s_{1}):=f_{1}(x,s_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2}(x))
and
\overline{g}_{1}(x,s_{1}):=g_{1}(x,s_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2}(x))
. Since
(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})\in J
, we have
\underline{u}_{i}\leq\widetilde{u}_{i}\leq\overline{u}_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Therefore, for any
s_{1}
satisfying
\underline{u}_{1}(x)\leq s_{1}\leq\overline{u}_{1}(x)
, the pair
(s_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2}(x))
satisfies the bounds in conditions
(H1)
and
(H2)
. It follows from
(H1)
that

|\overline{f}_{1}(x,s_{1})|=|f_{1}(x,s_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2}(x))|\leq K_{1}(x)\in L^{m}(\Omega)\quad\text{for }m>\frac{2N}{N+2},

and from

that

|\overline{g}_{1}(x,s_{1})|=|g_{1}(x,s_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2}(x))|\leq\widetilde{K}_{1}(x)\in L^{n}(\partial\Omega)\quad\text{for }n>\frac{2(N-1)}{N}.

Since
m>\frac{2N}{N+2}
and
n>\frac{2(N-1)}{N}
, the hypotheses of Proposition
2.1
are satisfied. Hence, we obtain a solution of (
4.1
), say
w_{1}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, such that
\underline{u}_{1}\leq w_{1}\leq\overline{u}_{1}
.

Similarly, with
\widetilde{u}_{1}
fixed, consider the scalar equation

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta u_{2}+c_{2}(x)u_{2}&=&f_{2}(x,\widetilde{u}_{1},u_{2})&\text{in}\;\Omega;\\
\dfrac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\eta}&=&g_{2}(x,\widetilde{u}_{1},u_{2})&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and defining
\overline{f}_{2}(x,s_{2}):=f_{2}(x,\widetilde{u}_{1}(x),s_{2})
and
\overline{g}_{2}(x,s_{2}):=g_{2}(x,\widetilde{u}_{1}(x),s_{2})
, an analogous argument using conditions
(H1)
and
(H2)
shows that the hypotheses of Proposition
2.1
are satisfied. We obtain a solution of (
4.2
), namely
w_{2}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, such that
\underline{u}_{2}\leq w_{2}\leq\overline{u}_{2}
.

Now, since
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
satisfy condition
(Q)
, it follows that
f_{1}(x,w_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})\leq f_{1}(x,w_{1},w_{2})
and
g_{1}(x,w_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})\leq g_{1}(x,w_{1},w_{2})
. Similarly,
f_{2}(x,\widetilde{u}_{1},w_{2})\leq f_{2}(x,w_{1},w_{2})
and
g_{2}(x,\widetilde{u}_{1},w_{2})\leq g_{2}(x,w_{1},w_{2})
. Therefore,

\int_{\Omega}(\nabla w_{i}\nabla\psi+c_{i}(x)w_{i}\psi)\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{i}(x,w_{1},w_{2})\,\psi\,+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{i}(x,w_{1},w_{2})\,\psi\,\quad\text{for all }\;0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Then using the fact that
\underline{u}_{i}\leq\widetilde{u}_{i}\leq w_{i}\leq\overline{u}_{i}
and
f_{i},g_{i}
satisfy condition
(Q)
, it follows that
f_{i}(\cdot,w_{1}(\cdot),w_{2}(\cdot))\in L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(\Omega)
and
g_{i}(\cdot,w_{1}(\cdot),w_{2}(\cdot))\in L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}(\partial\Omega)
for
i=1,2
. Consequently,
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
).
By the equivalence of norms, see (
2.1
), there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that
\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{c_{1}}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{c_{1}}=C_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left((\nabla w_{1})^{2}+c_{1}(x)w_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,w_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})w_{1}+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,w_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})w_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega}K_{1}(x)w_{1}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{K}_{1}(x)w_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega}K_{1}(x)\overline{u}_{1}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{K}_{1}(x)\overline{u}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\|K_{1}\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\|\overline{u}_{1}\|_{L^{m^{{}^{\prime}}}(\Omega)}+\|\widetilde{K}_{1}\|_{L^{n}(\partial\Omega)}\|\overline{u}_{1}\|_{L^{n^{{}^{\prime}}}(\partial\Omega)}\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}K_{1}^{*}\left(\|\overline{u}_{1}\|_{L^{m^{{}^{\prime}}}(\Omega)}+\|\overline{u}_{1}\|_{L^{n^{{}^{\prime}}}(\partial\Omega)}\right)\leq C_{1},

where
m^{{}^{\prime}},n^{{}^{\prime}}
are the Lebesgue conjugates of
m
and
n
respectively,

K_{1}^{*}=\max\left\{\|K_{1}\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\|,\|\widetilde{K}_{1}\|_{L^{n}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

and
C_{1}
is a constant depending on
(\overline{u}_{i},\underline{u}_{i},\Omega,K_{1}^{*})
but not on
w_{1}
. Similarly,
\|w_{2}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}
. Hence,
\|(w_{1},w_{2})\|^{2}_{(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}}\leq M
, where
M(\overline{u}_{i},\underline{u}_{i},\Omega,K^{*}_{i})
is a constant independent of
(w_{1},w_{2})
. Therefore, we construct the set of subsolutions,
\mathcal{A}
, as follows.

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}\mid(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})\leq(w_{1},w_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})\text{ for some }(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})\in J\right\},

where
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are solutions of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) for
\widetilde{u}_{1}
and
\widetilde{u}_{2}
, respectively. To establish the existence of a maximal element of
\mathcal{A}
, we employ Zorn’s Lemma (see, for instance,
[
11
]
) by verifying the conditions in the following two steps.

Step 1:
Clearly,
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
. Since the product space
(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
is separable, we can define the chain
Y=\{(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\}_{n\geq 1}
in
(\mathcal{A},\leq)
, where we assume without loss of generality (by possible reordering) that
\{(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\}_{n\geq 1}
is a component-wise increasing sequence. We show that
Y
has an upper bound in
\mathcal{A}
.
Since
(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\in\mathcal{A}
for every
n
, there exists a subsolution
(\widetilde{u}_{1,n},\widetilde{u}_{2,n})
of (
1.1
) such that

(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(\widetilde{u}_{1,n},\widetilde{u}_{2,n})\leq(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}),

with
w_{1,n}
and
w_{2,n}
solutions of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively, for the pair
\widetilde{u}_{1,n},\widetilde{u}_{2,n}
. From (
4.3
), it follows that
\|(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\|^{2}_{(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}}\leq M
, where
M=M(\overline{u}_{i},\underline{u}_{i},\Omega,K_{i})
is a constant independent of
n
.

By the reflexivity of
(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
, there is a subsequence (relabeled),
(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})
, which converges weakly to
(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
. Since the sequence
\{(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\}
is component-wise monotonically increasing and bounded above,
\{(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\}
converges pointwise to
(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})
. Using the continuity of
f_{i}(x,\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{i}(x,w_{1,n}(x),w_{2,n}(x))=f_{i}(x,w_{1}^{*}(x),w_{2}^{*}(x)),\quad i=1,2.

From conditions
(H1)
and
(H2)
, we have
|f_{i}(x,w_{1,n},w_{2,n})|\leq K_{i}(x)
where
K_{i}\in L^{r}(\Omega)
with
r>\frac{2N}{N+2}
, and
|g_{i}(x,w_{1,n},w_{2,n})|\leq\widetilde{K}_{i}(x)
where
\widetilde{K}_{i}\in L^{r}(\partial\Omega)
with
r>\frac{2(N-1)}{N}
.

Since
(w_{1,n},w_{2,n})\rightharpoonup(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\nabla w_{i,n}\nabla\psi+c_{i}(x)w_{i,n}\psi=\int_{\Omega}\nabla w_{i}^{*}\nabla\psi+c_{i}w_{i}^{*}\psi\quad\forall\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Moreover, using the quasimonotonicity of

and the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\nabla w_{1}^{*}\nabla\psi+c_{1}w_{1}^{*}\psi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla w_{1,n}\nabla\psi+c_{1}w_{1,n}\psi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,w_{1,n}(x),\widetilde{u}_{2,n}(x))\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,w_{1,n}(x),\widetilde{u}_{2,n}(x))\psi\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,w_{1,n}(x),w_{2,n}(x))\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,w_{1,n}(x),w_{2,n}(x))\psi\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,w_{1}^{*}(x),w_{2}^{*}(x))\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,w_{1}^{*}(x),w_{2}^{*}(x))\psi.

Similarly,

\int_{\Omega}(\nabla w_{2}^{*}\nabla\psi+c_{2}w_{2}^{*}\psi)\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,w_{1}^{*}(x),w_{2}^{*}(x))\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(x,w_{1}^{*}(x),w_{2}^{*}(x))\psi,

for all
0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence,
(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
).

Step 2:
We prove existence of maximal element. Let us take
(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})=(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})
. Then we can find a subsolution
(v_{1},v_{2})
of (
1.1
) such that
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})\leq(v_{1},v_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
, with
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
solutions of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively, for the pair
(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})
. This implies that
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{A}
and is an upper bound of the set
Y
. By Zorn’s lemma,
\mathcal{A}
has a maximal element,
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathcal{A}
. Clearly,
(z_{1},z_{2})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
). Indeed, observe that
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathcal{A}
implies that there exists a subsolution of (
1.1
),
(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2})
, satisfying

(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2})\leq(z_{1},z_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}),

where
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are solutions of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively, for the pair
\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2}
. Since
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are quasimonotone, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\nabla z_{1}\nabla\psi+c_{1}z_{1}\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,z_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,z_{1},\widetilde{z}_{2})\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,z_{1},z_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,z_{1},z_{2})\psi,

and

\int_{\Omega}(\nabla z_{2}\nabla\psi+c_{2}z_{2}\psi)\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,z_{1},z_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(x,z_{1},z_{2})\psi

for all
0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore,
(z_{1},z_{2})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
).

Now, it remains to prove that
(z_{1},z_{2})
is a solution of (
1.1
). Moreover, it is a maximal solution.
Observe that,
z_{i}\leq z_{i}^{*}\leq\overline{u}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and
z_{1}^{*}
and
z_{2}^{*}
solutions of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively, for the pair
(z_{1},z_{2})
. Thus,
(z_{1}^{*},z_{2}^{*})\in\mathcal{A}
. Using the fact that
(z_{1},z_{2})
is a maximal element of
\mathcal{A}
, it follows that
(z_{1}^{*},z_{2}^{*})\leq(z_{1},z_{2})
. Hence,
(z_{1}^{*},z_{2}^{*})=(z_{1},z_{2})
. Therefore, for every

Thus,
(z_{1},z_{2})
is a solution of (
1.1
) and
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(z_{1},z_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
. Additionally, by Proposition
5.2
,
(v_{1},v_{2})
with

and

is a subsolution of (
1.1
) and there exists
(\widehat{z}_{1},\widehat{z}_{2})\in\mathcal{A}
such that
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(v_{1},v_{2})\leq(\widehat{z}_{1},\widehat{z}_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
. This implies
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{A}
and
(v_{1},v_{2})\leq(z_{1},z_{2})

is a maximal element of
\mathcal{A}
. On the other hand, since

, it follows that

, which implies that

is a maximal solution of (

). By a similar approach using Proposition

, we can show the existence of a minimal solution of (

).

For

a=\min\left\{\sqrt{f_{1}^{\prime}(0)},\sqrt{g_{1}^{\prime}(0)}\right\}
and
b=\min\left\{\sqrt{f_{2}^{\prime}(0)},\sqrt{g_{2}^{\prime}(0)}\right\}
, system (
5.2
) admits a positive subsolution
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})
and supersolution
(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
satisfying

(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}),

where
\mu_{i},\varphi_{i}
are the first eigenvalue and eigenfunction of (
5.1
),
i=1,2
, respectively.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by constructing an ordered pair of subsolution and supersolution.

Step 1.
In this step, we construct a subsolution. Without loss of generality, we consider
i=1
, since the argument for
i=2
is similar.
For
\epsilon
sufficiently small, let us consider
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})=(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1},b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})
, where

a=\min\left\{\sqrt{f_{1}^{\prime}(0)},\sqrt{g_{1}^{\prime}(0)}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad b=\min\left\{\sqrt{f_{2}^{\prime}(0)},\sqrt{g_{2}^{\prime}(0)}\right\}.

Define

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(s)=\mu_{1}C_{1}\frac{a}{b}s-\lambda f_{1}(s),\qquad\widetilde{\xi}_{1}(s)=\mu_{1}C_{1}\frac{a}{b}s-\lambda g_{1}(s),

\displaystyle\xi_{2}(s)=\frac{\mu_{2}}{C_{2}}\frac{b}{a}s-\lambda f_{2}(s),\qquad\widetilde{\xi}_{2}(s)=\frac{\mu_{2}}{C_{2}}\frac{b}{a}s-\lambda g_{2}(s),

Since
f_{i}(0)=0=g_{i}(0)
, it follows that
\xi_{1}(0)=0=\widetilde{\xi}_{1}(0)
. Moreover, using the hypothesis that
\lambda>\max\left\{\frac{\mu_{1}C_{1}}{ab},\frac{\mu_{2}}{abC_{2}}\right\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)=\mu_{1}C_{1}\frac{a}{b}-\lambda f_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)<\lambda ab\frac{a}{b}-\lambda f_{1}^{\prime}(0)\leq\lambda f_{1}^{\prime}(0)-\lambda f_{1}^{\prime}(0)=0,

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)=\mu_{1}C_{1}\frac{a}{b}-\lambda g_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)<\lambda ab\frac{a}{b}-\lambda g_{1}^{\prime}(0)\leq\lambda g_{1}^{\prime}(0)-\lambda g_{1}^{\prime}(0)=0.

Consequently, we can find
0<\delta\ll 1
such that
\xi_{1}(s)<0
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{1}(s)<0
for
s\in(0,\delta)
. Choose
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
sufficiently small so that
a\varepsilon\varphi_{1},~b\varepsilon\varphi_{2}\in(0,\delta)
.

Using (
5.1
) and the above inequalities, we have for all
0\leq\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1})c_{1}\psi

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\,\psi+\mu_{1}\int_{\partial\Omega}(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\,\psi\,

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}C_{1}\int_{\Omega}(a\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\,\psi+\mu_{1}C_{1}\int_{\partial\Omega}(a\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\,\psi\,

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\,\psi+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\psi.

A similar argument establishes that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})c_{2}\,\psi

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}\int_{\Omega}(b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\,\psi+\mu_{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\,\psi\,

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{2}}{C_{2}}\int_{\Omega}(b\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\,\psi+\frac{\mu_{2}}{C_{2}}\int_{\partial\Omega}(b\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\,\psi\,

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\,\psi+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\,\psi.

Therefore,
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})=(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1},b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})
is a weak subsolution of (
5.2
).

Step 2.

For
\widetilde{M}\gg 1
, we demonstrate that
(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})=(\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*},\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*})
is a supersolution of (
5.2
), where
\phi_{i}^{*}:=\frac{\phi_{i}}{\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}}
belongs to
\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
,
i=1,2
, and
\phi_{i}
is the unique positive solution of

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta\phi_{i}+c_{i}(x)\phi_{i}&=&1,&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\dfrac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial\eta}&=&1&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Observe that the normalized eigenfunctions
\phi_{i}^{*}
,
i=1,2
satisfy

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta\phi_{i}^{*}+c_{i}(x)\phi_{i}^{*}&=\dfrac{1}{\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}},&\text{in }\Omega,\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial\phi_{i}^{*}}{\partial\eta}&=\dfrac{1}{\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}},&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Observe that
\overline{f}_{i}(t)=\underset{s\in[0,t]}{\max}f_{i}(s)
and
\overline{g}_{i}(t)=\underset{s\in[0,t]}{\max}g_{i}(s)
are nondecreasing functions satisfying
f_{i}(s)\leq\overline{f}_{i}(t)
and
g_{i}(s)\leq\overline{g}_{i}(t)
for
t>0
. Since
\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}\frac{f_{i}(s)}{s}=0=\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}\frac{g_{i}(s)}{s}
, it follows that

\underset{t\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{\overline{f}_{i}(t)}{t}=0=\underset{t\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{\overline{g}_{i}(t)}{t}.

Hence, for
i=1,2
and
\widetilde{M}\gg 1
sufficiently large, we have

\frac{\overline{f}_{i}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{i}^{*}\|_{\infty})}{\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{i}^{*}\|_{\infty}}<\frac{1}{\lambda\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\overline{g}_{i}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{i}^{*}\|_{\infty})}{\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{i}^{*}\|_{\infty}}<\frac{1}{\lambda\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}}.

Equivalently,

(5.5)

\lambda\overline{f}_{i}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{i}^{*}\|_{\infty})<\frac{\widetilde{M}}{\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda\overline{g}_{i}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{i}^{*}\|_{\infty})<\frac{\widetilde{M}}{{\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}}}.

Using (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) and the fact that
\|\phi_{1}^{*}\|_{\infty}=\|\phi_{2}^{*}\|_{\infty}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{M}}{\|\phi_{1}\|_{\infty}}\,\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{M}}{\|\phi_{1}\|_{\infty}}\,\psi\,

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}\overline{f}_{1}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{1}^{*}\|_{\infty})\,\psi+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}\overline{g}_{1}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{1}^{*}\|_{\infty})\,\psi\,

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\overline{f}_{1}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{2}^{*}\|_{\infty})\,\psi+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}\overline{g}_{1}(\widetilde{M}\|\phi_{2}^{*}\|_{\infty})\,\psi\,

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*})\,\psi+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*})\,\psi.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*}\psi\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*})\,\psi+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*})\,\psi.

This establishes that
(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})=(\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*},\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*})
is a weak supersolution of (
5.2
).

Finally, to show that the sub and supersolution is ordered, we can choose
\widetilde{M}
sufficiently large (specifically,
\widetilde{M}>\max\left\{\dfrac{a\epsilon\varphi_{1}}{\displaystyle\min_{x\in\Omega}\phi_{1}^{*}},\dfrac{b\epsilon\varphi_{2}}{\displaystyle\min_{x\in\Omega}\phi_{2}^{*}}\right\}
) such that
a\varepsilon\varphi_{1}\leq\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*}
and
b\varepsilon\varphi_{2}\leq\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*}
, which yields

(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})=(a\varepsilon\varphi_{1},b\varepsilon\varphi_{2})\leq(\widetilde{M}\phi_{1}^{*},\widetilde{M}\phi_{2}^{*})=(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}).

Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
, there exists a positive solution
(u_{1},u_{2})
to system (
5.2
) such that
(0,0)<(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(u_{1},u_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
, which guarantees the positivity of the weak solution.
∎

Appendix

In this section, we prove that the maximum of two subsolutions of (
1.1
) remains a subsolution of (
1.1
) and the minimum of two supersolutions of (
1.1
) remains a supersolution of (
1.1
). This extends Kato’s inequality for single equations proved in Section
2
(see Proposition
2.2
and Corollary
2.3
) to quasimonotone systems.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathcal{A}
be any two subsolutions of (
1.1
). Then the pair
(\max\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\},\max\{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
).

Proof.

Since
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
belong to
A
, there exist corresponding functions such that each pair satisfies the subsolution conditions. Specifically,
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
satisfy:
For
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\alpha_{1}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)\alpha_{1}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\psi

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\alpha_{2}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{2}(x)\alpha_{2}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\psi

For
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\beta_{1}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)\beta_{1}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\psi

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\beta_{2}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{2}(x)\beta_{2}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\psi

Now define
\gamma_{1}:=\max\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\}
and
\gamma_{2}:=\max\{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\}
.
By the quasimonotonicity condition
(Q)
, we have:

(1)

f_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\leq f_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
f_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\leq f_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\gamma_{2})

(2)

f_{2}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\leq f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
f_{2}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\leq f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\beta_{2})

Similarly for the boundary terms:

(1)

g_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\leq g_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
g_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\leq g_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\gamma_{2})

(2)

g_{2}(x,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\leq g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
g_{2}(x,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\leq g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\beta_{2})

Let

h_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}f_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{1}(x)>\beta_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\\
f_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{1}(x)\leq\beta_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\end{cases}

\widetilde{h}_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}g_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{1}(x)>\beta_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\\
g_{1}(x,\beta_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{1}(x)\leq\beta_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\end{cases}

And similarly:

h_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\alpha_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{2}(x)>\beta_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\\
f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\beta_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{2}(x)\leq\beta_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\end{cases}

\widetilde{h}_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\alpha_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{2}(x)>\beta_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\\
g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\beta_{2})&\text{if }\alpha_{2}(x)\leq\beta_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\end{cases}

Notice that
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}
are subsolutions of:

\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)v\psi\leq\int_{\Omega}h_{1}(x)\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{h}_{1}(x)\psi

and
\alpha_{2}
and
\beta_{2}
are subsolutions of:

\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{2}(x)v\psi\leq\int_{\Omega}h_{2}(x)\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{h}_{2}(x)\psi

By Proposition (
2.2
), we have that
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
satisfies:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\gamma_{1}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)\gamma_{1}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}h_{1}(x)\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{h}_{1}(x)\psi

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\gamma_{2}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{2}(x)\gamma_{2}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}h_{2}(x)\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{h}_{2}(x)\psi

By the definitions of
h_{1},h_{2},\widetilde{h}_{1},\widetilde{h}_{2}
and the quasimonotonicity conditions, we have:

(1)

h_{1}(x)\leq f_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\widetilde{h}_{1}(x)\leq g_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

(2)

h_{2}(x)\leq f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\widetilde{h}_{2}(x)\leq g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

Therefore:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\gamma_{1}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)\gamma_{1}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\gamma_{2}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{2}(x)\gamma_{2}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi

This shows that
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=(\max\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\},\max\{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
).
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
(u_{1},u_{2})
and
(v_{1},v_{2})
are two solutions of (
1.1
) such that
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
, where
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})
and
(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
are sub- and supersolutions of (
1.1
), respectively. Then
(\max\{u_{1},v_{1}\},\max\{u_{2},v_{2}\})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
).

Proof.

Since
(u_{1},u_{2})
and
(v_{1},v_{2})
are solutions of (
1.1
), they satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{i}(x)u_{i}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{i}(x,u_{1},u_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{i}(x,u_{1},u_{2})\psi,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{i}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{i}(x)v_{i}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{i}(x,v_{1},v_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{i}(x,v_{1},v_{2})\psi,

for all
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
i=1,2
.
Let
\gamma_{1}:=\max\{u_{1},v_{1}\}
and
\gamma_{2}:=\max\{u_{2},v_{2}\}
.
By the quasimonotonicity condition
(Q)
, we have

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle\leq f_{1}(x,u_{1},\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle\leq f_{1}(x,v_{1},\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle\leq f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle\leq f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},v_{2})

Similarly, for the boundary terms

\displaystyle g_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle\leq g_{1}(x,u_{1},\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle g_{1}(x,v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle\leq g_{1}(x,v_{1},\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle g_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle\leq g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle g_{2}(x,v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle\leq g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},v_{2})

Define the composite functions as follows

\widetilde{f}_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}f_{1}(x,u_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }u_{1}(x)>v_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\\
f_{1}(x,v_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }u_{1}(x)\leq v_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\end{cases}

\widetilde{g}_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}g_{1}(x,u_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }u_{1}(x)>v_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\\
g_{1}(x,v_{1},\gamma_{2})&\text{if }u_{1}(x)\leq v_{1}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\end{cases}

\widetilde{f}_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},u_{2})&\text{if }u_{2}(x)>v_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\\
f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},v_{2})&\text{if }u_{2}(x)\leq v_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\end{cases}

\widetilde{g}_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},u_{2})&\text{if }u_{2}(x)>v_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\\
g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},v_{2})&\text{if }u_{2}(x)\leq v_{2}(x)\text{ a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\end{cases}

Since
u_{1}
and
v_{1}
are solutions, they satisfy the subsolution inequalities

\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)w\psi\leq\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}_{1}(x)\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{g}_{1}(x)\psi

for
w=u_{1},v_{1}
.
By Proposition
2.2
(Extended Kato’s inequality),
\gamma_{1}=\max\{u_{1},v_{1}\}
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\gamma_{1}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)\gamma_{1}\psi\leq\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}_{1}(x)\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{g}_{1}(x)\psi

By the quasimonotonicity conditions and the definitions of
\widetilde{f}_{1}
and
\widetilde{g}_{1}
, we have that

\widetilde{f}_{1}(x)\leq f_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{g}_{1}(x)\leq g_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

Similarly for
\gamma_{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\gamma_{1}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{1}(x)\gamma_{1}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{1}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\gamma_{2}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}c_{2}(x)\gamma_{2}\psi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi+\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{2}(x,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\psi

This shows that
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=(\max\{u_{1},v_{1}\},\max\{u_{2},v_{2}\})
is a subsolution of (
1.1
).
∎

Next, we show that if
(u_{1},u_{2})
and
(v_{1},v_{2})
are two solutions of (
1.1
), then the pair of functions
(\min\{u_{1},v_{1}\},\min\{u_{2},v_{2}\})
is a supersolution of (
1.1
). For that purpose, consider the equations

(5.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta u_{1}+c_{1}(x)u_{1}&=&f_{1}(x,u_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\eta}&=&g_{1}(x,u_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and

(5.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta u_{2}+c_{2}(x)u_{2}&=&f_{2}(x,\widehat{u}_{1},u_{2})&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\eta}&=&g_{2}(x,\widehat{u}_{1},u_{2})&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set consisting of
(w_{1},w_{2})\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
such that there exists a supersolution
(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})
of (
1.1
) satisfying

(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(w_{1},w_{2})\leq(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}),

where
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are solutions of (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), respectively, for the pair
(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathcal{B}
be any two supersolutions of (
1.1
). Then the pair
(\min\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\},\min\{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\})
is a supersolution of (
1.1
).

Proof.

The proof follows the same structure as Lemma
5.1
, with the following modifications: (i) supersolution inequalities (
\geq
) replace subsolution inequalities (
\leq
), (ii)
\min
operations replace
\max
operations, (iii) the case definitions in the composite functions use conditions of the form “
\alpha_{1}(x)<\beta_{1}(x)
” instead of “
\alpha_{1}(x)>\beta_{1}(x)
”, and (iv) we apply the minimum property from Corollary
2.3
instead of the maximum property from Proposition
2.2
. The proof is otherwise analogous.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
(u_{1},u_{2})
and
(v_{1},v_{2})
are two solutions of (
1.1
) such that
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
, where
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})
and
(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
are sub- and supersolutions of (
1.1
), respectively. Then
(\min\{u_{1},v_{1}\},\min\{u_{2},v_{2}\})
is a supersolution of (
1.1
).

Proof.

This combines the modifications from the previous lemma (supersolution inequalities,
\min
operations, minimum property) with the adjustment from Proposition
5.2
(starting with solution equalities rather than supersolution inequalities). The proof follows analogously to the previous results.
∎

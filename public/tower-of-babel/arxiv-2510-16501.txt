Abstract

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
X
be an
N
-dimensional real normed space and
A\in\End(X)
. For
1\leqslant k\leqslant N
, the
higher traces

\lambda_{k}(A)\ :=\ \tr(\Lambda^{k}A),

are the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial

\det(I-tA)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k}\lambda_{k}(A)\,t^{k},\qquad\lambda_{0}=1.

There are several complementary ways to
understand

\lambda_{k}
:

•

Exterior-power/
k
-volume distortion:

\Lambda^{k}A
acts on oriented
k
-volumes;
\lambda_{k}(A)
is the trace of this action and equals the sum of all principal
k\times k
minors of
A
.

•

Averaging over Grassmannians:
If
X=\mathbb{R}^{N}
is Euclidean, then

\lambda_{k}(A)=\binom{N}{k}\int_{G_{k,N}}\det(P_{E}A|_{E})\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(E),

the
O(N)
-invariant average of the determinant of the compression
P_{E}A|_{E}
to a
k
-plane
E

[
2
, pp. 231–234]
. K. Morrison independently observed this viewpoint (unpublished note).

•

Representation theory:

\lambda_{k}(A)
is the character of the
\Lambda^{k}
-representation evaluated at
A
. Averaging the matrix coefficient
\left\langle(\Lambda^{k}A)w,w\right\rangle
over the
O(N)
-orbit of unit simple
k
-vectors reproduces
\tr(\Lambda^{k}A)
after normalisation.

The main theme of the paper is the question of whether we can realise these averages
for arbitrary norms
by integrating over the unit sphere of the normed exterior power? For
k=1
, Morrison and the author
[
6
]
gave such a formula under the hypothesis of the existence of a 1-symmetric basis, that is, the action of a big enough hypooctahedral group on self-isometries of the space; the present work gives a unified framework for all
k
and all norms by isolating a single isotropy condition on a measure
\eta
on the unit sphere of
\Lambda^{k}X
.

The main result of the paper is that the trace average

\lambda_{k}(A)=n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}\!\langle(\Lambda^{k}A)w,w^{*}\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w),\qquad V:=\Lambda^{k}X,\ \ n_{k}=\binom{N}{k},

holds for
all

A
if and only if

T_{\eta}:=n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}w\otimes w^{*}\,\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w)=I_{V}.

This extends the familiar trace-average formula (case
k=1
; see
[
5
]
) and higher-trace formulae by Eberlein
[
2
]
.

This isotropy is automatic for the
cone probability measure
, by the Gauss–Green theorem on
B_{V}
, and it holds for the
hypersurface measure
whenever a finite isometry group acts on
X
and induces an orthogonal
2
-design on
\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We also quantify the obstruction to isotropy for
\mu_{k}
at
k=1
: only degree–
2
spherical harmonics of the support function enter at first order.

2.
Preliminaries

We identify
X
with
\mathbb{R}^{N}
via a fixed auxiliary Euclidean inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{2}
, used only to define Euclidean volumes, surface measures, and normals. Denote by
B_{X}
the unit ball and by
S_{X}=\partial B_{X}
the unit sphere. Let
\mu
be the
normalised
Euclidean hypersurface measure on
S_{X}
. At
\mu
-a.e.
x\in S_{X}
there is a unique norming functional
x^{*}
with
\|x^{*}\|_{*}=1
and
\left\langle x,x^{*}\right\rangle=1
(see
[
6
, Prop. 2.4]
).

Orthogonal
2
-designs and symmetry

Let
W
be a Euclidean space and let
G\leqslant O(W)
be a finite subgroup of the orthogonal group. We say that
G
is an
orthogonal
2
-design
on
W
if

(2.1)

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{Q\in G}Q^{\top}B\,Q\;=\;\frac{\tr B}{\dim W}\,I_{W}\qquad\text{for all }B\in\End(W).

Equivalently, if
w
is any unit vector and
\nu_{G}
is the uniform measure on the orbit
G\cdot w
, then

\int(u\otimes u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{G}(u)\;=\;\frac{1}{\dim W}\,I_{W}.

In our applications
W=\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the induced Euclidean structure, and the representation is
Q\mapsto\Lambda^{k}Q
.

Example 2.1

(Hyperoctahedral group)
.

Let
B_{N}
be the group of signed permutation matrices. Then for every
k
the induced action of
B_{N}
on
W=\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an orthogonal
2
-design. Indeed, the commutant of
\Lambda^{k}(B_{N})
is scalar: commuting with all sign-flips forces diagonality in the basis
\{e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{k}}\}
, while commuting with permutations forces equality of all diagonal entries; hence
\End(W)^{B_{N}}=\mathbb{R}I_{W}
. Averaging as in (
2.1
) then yields
\frac{1}{|B_{N}|}\sum Q^{\top}BQ=(\tr B/\dim W)\,I_{W}
.

Example 2.2

(Low-dimensional geometric groups)
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the dihedral group
D_{m}
(
m\geqslant 3
) is a
2
-design on
\Lambda^{1}\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the rotation groups of the Platonic solids are
2
-designs on
\Lambda^{1}\mathbb{R}^{3}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
and, via the Hodge isometry
\star:\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\overset{\cong}{\to}\Lambda^{1}\mathbb{R}^{3}

\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}

Exterior powers

For
1\leqslant k\leqslant N
, write
V=\Lambda^{k}X
and
n_{k}=\dim V=\binom{N}{k}
. We equip
V
with the exterior projective norm as introduced in
[
8
]
:

\|w\|_{\wedge,\pi}=\inf\Big\{\sum_{r}\|x^{(r)}_{1}\|\cdots\|x^{(r)}_{k}\|\ \colon\ w=\sum_{r}x^{(r)}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x^{(r)}_{k}\Big\}.

The canonical Euclidean inner product on
V
is induced by

\left\langle x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{k},y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{k}\right\rangle_{2}=\det[\left\langle x_{i},y_{j}\right\rangle_{2}]_{i,j}
.
Let
\sigma
be the Euclidean surface measure on
S_{V}=\partial B_{V}
. Denote by
\mu_{k}
the
normalised
Euclidean hypersurface measure on
S_{V}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a finite-dimensional normed space and equip
\Lambda^{k}X
with the exterior projective norm.

(a)

If
Q:X\to X
is a linear isometry, then
\Lambda^{k}Q:(\Lambda^{k}X,\|\cdot\|_{\wedge,\pi})\to(\Lambda^{k}X,\|\cdot\|_{\wedge,\pi})
is an isometry.

(b)

More generally, for any
T\in\End(X)
, one has
\|\Lambda^{k}T\|_{\Lambda^{k}X\to\Lambda^{k}X}\leqslant\|T\|^{k}
.

Proof.

(a) Fix
w\in\Lambda^{k}X
and
\varepsilon>0
. Choose a representation
w=\sum_{r=1}^{m}x^{(r)}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x^{(r)}_{k}
such that

\|w\|_{\wedge,\pi}\geqslant\sum_{r=1}^{m}\|x^{(r)}_{1}\|\cdots\|x^{(r)}_{k}\|-\varepsilon.

Applying
\Lambda^{k}Q
and using that
Q
is an isometry on
X
,

\Lambda^{k}Q(w)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}(Qx^{(r)}_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(Qx^{(r)}_{k}),\qquad\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|Qx^{(r)}_{i}\|=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|x^{(r)}_{i}\|.

Taking the infimum over all such decompositions on the right yields

\|\Lambda^{k}Q(w)\|_{\wedge,\pi}\leqslant\|w\|_{\wedge,\pi}+\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,
\|\Lambda^{k}Q(w)\|_{\wedge,\pi}\leqslant\|w\|_{\wedge,\pi}
.
Applying the same argument to
Q^{-1}
gives the reverse inequality, hence
\|\Lambda^{k}Q(w)\|_{\wedge,\pi}=\|w\|_{\wedge,\pi}
for all
w
,
i.e.
,
\Lambda^{k}Q
is an isometry.

(b) For any representation of
w
as above,

\|\Lambda^{k}T(w)\|_{\wedge,\pi}\;\leqslant\;\sum_{r=1}^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|Tx^{(r)}_{i}\|\;\leqslant\;\sum_{r=1}^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|T\|\,\|x^{(r)}_{i}\|\;=\;\|T\|^{k}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|x^{(r)}_{i}\|.

Taking the infimum over all decompositions of
w
gives
\|\Lambda^{k}T(w)\|_{\wedge,\pi}\leqslant\|T\|^{k}\,\|w\|_{\wedge,\pi}
, hence
\|\Lambda^{k}T\|\leqslant\|T\|^{k}
.
∎

Cone measures

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a convex body with
0\in\mathrm{int}\,K
. The
cone probability measure

\nu_{K}
on
\partial K
is

\nu_{K}(A)=\frac{\vol(\{tx:x\in A,\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant 1\})}{\vol(K)}.

If
n_{e}
exists a.e., then
\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{K}(x)=\frac{\langle n_{e}(x),x\rangle}{m\,\vol(K)}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(x)
(Schneider
[
7
, §§2.2, 4.2, 8.2]
; Gardner
[
4
, §§8.4, B.2]
).

Grassmannian viewpoint and the
\Lambda^{k}
-sphere in the Euclidean case

Let us consider
X=\mathbb{R}^{N}
with its Euclidean inner product. Fix
w_{0}=e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k}
and
\mathcal{O}_{k}:=\{(\Lambda^{k}g)w_{0}:g\in O(N)\}\subset S_{\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. Then

\mathcal{O}_{k}\ \cong\ O(N)\big/\big(O(k)\times O(N-k)\big),

and the map
E\in G_{k,N}\mapsto w_{E}:=v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}
(for any orthonormal basis
(v_{i})
of

) identifies the unoriented Grassmannian with
\mathcal{O}_{k}/\{\pm 1\}
. For such
w_{E}
,

\big\langle(\Lambda^{k}A)w_{E},\,w_{E}\big\rangle=\det\!\big([\langle Av_{i},v_{j}\rangle]_{i,j\leqslant k}\big)=\det\!\big(P_{E}A|_{E}\big),

so averaging
\langle(\Lambda^{k}A)w,w\rangle
over
\mathcal{O}_{k}
(or
G_{k,N}
) yields Eberlein’s formula for
\lambda_{k}(A)
with factor
\binom{N}{k}

[
2
, pp. 231–234]
.

The Gauss–Green theorem and sets of finite perimeter

Lemma 2.4

(Gauss–Green Theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and let
n_{e}
denote its Euclidean outer unit normal (defined
\mathcal{H}^{m-1}
-a.e.). Then for every
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\int_{\partial\Omega}\!\left\langle F,n_{e}\right\rangle_{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\ =\ \int_{\Omega}\!\div F\,\,\mathrm{d}x.

This theorem holds more generally for sets of finite perimeter (reduced boundary). Convex bodies are sets of finite perimeter. For more details see
[
3
, Thm. 5.16]
and
[
9
, Thm. 5.8.8]
.

We shall require the following version of the Minkowski identity

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a convex body. Then, as operators on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\int_{\partial K}x\otimes n_{e}(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(x)\ =\ \vol(K)\,I_{m}.

Equivalently, for all
B\in\End(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\int_{\partial K}\!\left\langle Bx,n_{e}(x)\right\rangle_{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(x)\ =\ \tr(B)\,\vol(K).

Proof.

Apply Lemma
2.4
to
F(x)=Bx
, with
\div F=\tr(B)
. Non-degeneracy of the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing yields the operator identity.
∎

3.
Trace average and isotropy

Lemma 3.1

(Hilbert–Schmidt duality)
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing on
\End(V)
.
For any probability measure
\eta
on
S_{V}
for which a measurable choice of norming functionals
w^{*}
exists
\eta
-a.e., and any
B\in\End(V)
,

n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}\!\langle Bw,w^{*}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w)=\Big\langle B,\ T_{\eta}\Big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}},\qquad T_{\eta}:=n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}w\otimes w^{*}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w).

Hence,

\forall\,B\in\End(V):\quad n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}\!\langle Bw,w^{*}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w)=\tr(B)\iff T_{\eta}=I_{V}.

Proof.

Fix an auxiliary Euclidean inner product on
V
(the one used for
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
). Since all norms on
V
are equivalent, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
with

\|v\|_{2}\leqslant c_{1}\|v\|
for all
v\in V
, and
\|f\|_{2}\leqslant c_{2}\|f\|_{*}
for all
f\in V^{*}

(identifying
V^{*}
with
V
via the Euclidean Riesz map).
Because
w\in S_{V}
and
\|w^{*}\|_{*}=1
, we have
\|w\|_{2}\leqslant c_{1}
and
\|w^{*}\|_{2}\leqslant c_{2}
. Hence

\|w\otimes w^{*}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|w\|_{2}\,\|w^{*}\|_{2}\leqslant c_{1}c_{2},

so the map
w\mapsto w\otimes w^{*}
is essentially bounded (hence Bochner integrable) on
S_{V}
.
By assumption there is a measurable choice of norming functionals
w\mapsto w^{*}

\eta
-a.e.
1
1

1

For the hypersurface measure, measurability follows from
[
6
, Proposition 2.4]
.
.

For any
u,v\in V
and
B\in\End(V)
, the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing satisfies

\langle B,\,u\otimes v\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\langle Bu,\,v\rangle,

since both sides equal
\mathrm{tr}\big((u\otimes v)^{\top}B\big)
.

Using linearity of the Bochner integral and the above,

\Big\langle B,\,T_{\eta}\Big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\bigg\langle B,\ n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}w\otimes w^{*}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w)\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}\!\langle B,\,w\otimes w^{*}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w)=n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}\!\langle Bw,\,w^{*}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w).

Since
\tr(B)=\langle B,I_{V}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
and the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing is non-degenerate, we have

\forall B:\ \ n_{k}\!\int\!\langle Bw,w^{*}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\tr(B)\iff\forall B:\ \ \langle B,T_{\eta}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\langle B,I_{V}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}\iff T_{\eta}=I_{V}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
V:=\Lambda^{k}X
,
n_{k}=\binom{N}{k}
, and
\eta
a probability measure on
S_{V}
with measurable norming functionals
w^{*}
a.e. Then

(3.1)

\forall\,B\in\End(V):\quad\tr(B)\ =\ n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}\!\left\langle Bw,w^{*}\right\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w)\iff T_{\eta}=I_{V}.

Consequently, if
T_{\eta}=I_{V}
, then for every
A\in\End(X)
,

\boxed{\quad\lambda_{k}(A)=\tr(\Lambda^{k}A)\ =\ n_{k}\!\int_{S_{V}}\!\left\langle(\Lambda^{k}A)w,w^{*}\right\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\eta(w).\quad}

This isotropy holds in the following cases:

(i)

If a finite
G\leqslant O(N)
acts by norm isometries on
X
and its induced action on
\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an orthogonal
2
-design, then
T_{\mu_{k}}=I_{V}
.

(ii)

Cone measure:
For
\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(w)=\frac{\left\langle n_{e}(w),w\right\rangle_{2}}{n_{k}\,\vol(B_{V})}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)
, one has
T_{\nu_{k}}=I_{V}
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.1
is the equivalence. For (i):
T_{\mu_{k}}
commutes with the
\Lambda^{k}
-action, hence
T_{\mu_{k}}=cI
; since
\tr(T_{\mu_{k}})=n_{k}
, we get
c=1
. For (ii): using
w^{*}=n_{e}/\left\langle n_{e},w\right\rangle_{2}
,

T_{\nu_{k}}=\frac{1}{\vol(B_{V})}\int_{\partial B_{V}}w\otimes n_{e}(w)\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(w).

Then, for any
B
,

\big\langle B,T_{\nu_{k}}\big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\frac{1}{\vol(B_{V})}\int_{\partial B_{V}}\!\left\langle Bw,n_{e}\right\rangle_{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\frac{1}{\vol(B_{V})}\int_{B_{V}}\!\div(Bw)\,\,\mathrm{d}w=\tr(B),

by Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

If
G\leqslant O(N)
induces an orthogonal
2
-design on
\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{R}^{N}
for every
k
, then the hypersurface–measure trace formula holds simultaneously for all

. This includes the hyperoctahedral group

.

Corollary 3.4

(Discrete trace formula for polyhedral norms)
.

Let
B_{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a polytope with facets
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
, Euclidean unit outer normals
n_{j}
, and areas
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{j})
. Then for every
A\in\End(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(3.2)

\tr(A)=\frac{1}{\vol(B_{X})}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{F_{j}}\langle Ax,n_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)=\frac{1}{\vol(B_{X})}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{j})\,\langle Ac_{j},n_{j}\rangle,

where
c_{j}:=\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{j})^{-1}\int_{F_{j}}x\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)
is the (Euclidean) centroid of
F_{j}
.

Proof.

By the cone–measure formula (or, equivalently, the matrix
-
valued Minkowski identity applied with
K=B_{X}
and
B=A
), we have

(3.3)

\tr(A)\;=\;\frac{1}{\vol(B_{X})}\int_{\partial B_{X}}\!\langle Ax,n_{e}(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(x).

Since
B_{X}
is a polytope, its boundary is a union of flat facets
F_{j}
whose Euclidean outer unit normal is constant and equals
n_{j}
on
F_{j}
; the union of all ridges and vertices has
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
-
measure zero and does not contribute to the integral. Hence the right
-
hand side of (
3.3
) splits as

\frac{1}{\vol(B_{X})}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{F_{j}}\!\langle Ax,n_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x),

which proves the first equality in (
3.2
). For the second, the integrand is linear in
x
, so

\int_{F_{j}}\!\langle Ax,n_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)=\Big\langle A\Big(\int_{F_{j}}\!x\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)\Big),\,n_{j}\Big\rangle=\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(F_{j})\,\langle Ac_{j},n_{j}\rangle.

Substituting this back into (
3.2
) yields the result.
∎

Geometric interpretation of obstructions for the trace–average

In the first–variation analysis for
k=1
we linearise the operator–valued map

g\ \longmapsto\ T_{\mu_{1}}(g)\ =\ N\!\int_{S^{m-1}}\!x(g;u)\otimes x^{*}(g;u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{g}(u),

at the Euclidean ball in the direction of an even function
g\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(S^{m-1})
, where

m=\dim X
,
x(g;u)
is the boundary point with outer normal
u
,
x^{*}(g;u)
the associated norming functional,
and
\mu_{g}
the normalised hypersurface measure. Naturality of the construction implies

\mathcal{L}(g\circ Q^{\top})\;=\;Q\,\mathcal{L}(g)\,Q^{\top},\qquad Q\in O(m),

so the first variation
\mathcal{L}:C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(S^{m-1})\to\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is an
O(m)
-
equivariant
linear map.
Representation theory therefore constrains which spherical harmonic components of
g
can
affect
\mathcal{L}(g)
. The domain
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(S^{m-1})
decomposes into even spherical harmonics

\bigoplus_{\ell\ \mathrm{even}}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
, while

\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})=\mathbb{R}I\oplus\Sym^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
splits as the sum of the trivial representation and an
irreducible module isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Consequently, only the
\ell=0
and
\ell=2
parts of
g

can contribute; all higher degrees are annihilated. Concretely, this selection rule can be
read off either from Schur’s lemma or from the Funk–Hecke formula, which shows that
convolution with a zonal kernel acts by scalars on each
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
(see
[
1
, Ch. 2]
).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
. Any
O(m)
-equivariant continuous linear operator

\mathcal{L}:C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(S^{m-1})\to\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})

factors through the projections onto
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
(the degree
0
and
2
spherical harmonics). More precisely,

\mathcal{L}(g)\;=\;\beta_{0}\,\Big(\int_{S^{m-1}}g\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)\,I\;+\;\beta_{2}\,\Psi\big(P_{2}g\big),

where
P_{2}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and
\Psi:\mathcal{H}_{2}\!\to\!\Sym^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the unique
O(m)
-equivariant isomorphism (given on pure quadratics by
u\mapsto uu^{\top}-\frac{1}{m}I
). In particular, all harmonic components
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
with
\ell\notin\{0,2\}
are annihilated.

Proof.

We view
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(S^{m-1})
as an
O(m)
-module via
(Q\cdot g)(u)=g(Q^{\top}u)
, and

\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
via
Q\cdot S=Q\,S\,Q^{\top}
. The Peter–Weyl decomposition gives

C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(S^{m-1})\;=\;\widehat{\bigoplus_{\ell\ \mathrm{even}}}\ \mathcal{H}_{\ell},

where each
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
is the irreducible space of degree–
\ell
spherical harmonics, pairwise non
-
isomorphic.
On the target side,

\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\;=\;\mathbb{R}I\ \oplus\ \Sym^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

with
\mathbb{R}I
the trivial representation and
\Sym^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
irreducible and (canonically) isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
:
the map

\Theta:\ \Sym^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathcal{H}_{2},\qquad\Theta(S)(u)=u^{\top}S\,u

\Psi(q)\;:=\;c_{m}\int_{S^{m-1}}q(u)\,\Big(u\,u^{\top}-\frac{1}{m}I\Big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u),\qquad q\in\mathcal{H}_{2},

which is
O(m)
-equivariant by construction;
c_{m}
is fixed by normalisation (e.g. making

).

Now let
\mathcal{L}:C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(S^{m-1})\to\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
be

\Hom_{O(m)}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell},\mathbb{R}I)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{R},&\ell=0,\\
0,&\ell\neq 0,\end{cases}\qquad\Hom_{O(m)}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell},\Sym^{2}_{0})\ \cong\ \Hom_{O(m)}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell},\mathcal{H}_{2})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{R},&\ell=2,\\
0,&\ell\neq 2.\end{cases}

Hence
\mathcal{L}
vanishes on
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
for
\ell\notin\{0,2\}
, and on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
it is (respectively) a

\mathcal{H}_{0}\ni g\mapsto\Big(\int g\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)I,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{2}\ni g\mapsto\Psi(g)\in\Sym^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Therefore there exist scalars
\beta_{0},\beta_{2}
with

\mathcal{L}=\beta_{0}\,\mathrm{avg}\cdot I+\beta_{2}\,\Psi\circ P_{2},

as claimed.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be an
N
-dimensional normed space. Define the
anisotropy tensor

\mathcal{A}_{X}:=N\int_{S_{X}}x\otimes x^{*}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-I_{N}\in\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Then:

(i)

\mathcal{A}_{X}=0
iff the trace–average formula holds for all
A\in\End(X)
.

(ii)

\mathcal{A}_{X}
is traceless and symmetric, with
\|\mathcal{A}_{X}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leqslant N+\sqrt{N}
.

(iii)

For any
A\in\End(X)
,

N\int_{S_{X}}\langle Ax,x^{*}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\tr(A)=\langle A,\mathcal{A}_{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}.

(iv)

If
h_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon g
with
\int g\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=0
,

\mathcal{A}_{X}(\varepsilon)=-\,\frac{2\varepsilon}{N+2}\,\int_{S^{N-1}}g(u)\Big(uu^{\top}-\tfrac{1}{N}I\Big)\mathrm{d}\omega(u)+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Equivalently, for
g(u)=u^{\top}Su
with
\tr S=0
,
\mathcal{A}_{X}(\varepsilon)=-\frac{2\varepsilon}{N+2}S+O(\varepsilon^{2})
.

Theorem 4.2

(
\alpha
-cone isotropy)
.

Let
V=\Lambda^{k}X
with
m=\dim V
. For
\alpha>0
define the
\alpha
-cone measure on
S_{V}=\partial B_{V}
by

\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}^{\alpha}(w)=\frac{\langle n_{e}(w),w\rangle^{\alpha}}{C_{\alpha}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(w),\qquad C_{\alpha}:=\int_{\partial B_{V}}\langle n_{e},w\rangle^{\alpha}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(w).

Then:

(i)

For every convex
B_{V}
(no symmetry),
T_{\nu_{k}^{1}}=I_{V}
.
(Global sufficiency of
\alpha=1
).

(ii)

Let
B_{V}
be
C^{2}
and strictly convex. Consider a smooth even perturbation of the Euclidean ball with support function
h_{\varepsilon}(u)=1+\varepsilon g(u)
,
u\in S^{m-1}
, with
\int g\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=0
. Then

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\,\big\langle B,\,T_{\nu_{k}^{\alpha}}(h_{\varepsilon})\big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}\;=\;(\alpha-1)\,\int_{S^{m-1}}g(u)\,\big\langle Bu,u\big\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u)\qquad\big(B\in\End(V)\big).

In particular, for
\alpha\neq 1
and any degree-
2
harmonic
g\not\equiv 0
, the derivative is non-zero for some
B
, hence
T_{\nu_{k}^{\alpha}}\neq I_{V}
for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.
(Local necessity of
\alpha=1
for non-spherical bodies).

Proof.

(i) For
\alpha=1
, using
w^{*}=n_{e}/\langle n_{e},w\rangle
,

T_{\nu_{k}^{1}}=n_{k}\!\int w\otimes w^{*}\,\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}^{1}=\frac{n_{k}}{C_{1}}\int w\otimes n_{e}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\frac{n_{k}}{C_{1}}\,\big(\vol(B_{V})\,I_{V}\big)

by the matrix-valued Minkowski identity (Proposition
2.5
). Moreover

C_{1}=\int\langle n_{e},w\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=m\,\vol(B_{V})=n_{k}\,\vol(B_{V})
, hence
T_{\nu_{k}^{1}}=I_{V}
.

(ii) We write everything in the Gauss parametrisation. For a
C^{2}
strictly convex body with support function
h
, the boundary point with outer normal
u\in S^{m-1}
is

x(u)=h(u)\,u+\nabla_{S}h(u),\qquad n_{e}(x(u))=u,

and the Euclidean surface element pulls back as

\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h}(u)=\det\big(\nabla^{2}_{S}h(u)+h(u)I\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u).

Set
m=\dim V
. A direct computation gives, for small
\varepsilon
and
h_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon g
,

\displaystyle x_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=u+\varepsilon\big(g(u)\,u+\nabla_{S}g(u)\big)+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle n_{e},x_{\varepsilon}\rangle

\displaystyle=h_{\varepsilon}(u)=1+\varepsilon g(u)+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle\det\!\big(\nabla^{2}_{S}h_{\varepsilon}+h_{\varepsilon}I\big)

\displaystyle=1+\varepsilon\big(\Delta_{S}g(u)+(m-1)g(u)\big)+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Therefore

\langle B,\,T_{\nu_{k}^{\alpha}}(h_{\varepsilon})\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\frac{n_{k}}{C_{\alpha}(h_{\varepsilon})}\int_{S^{m-1}}\underbrace{\big\langle B\,x_{\varepsilon}(u),\,u\big\rangle}_{\text{I}}\underbrace{h_{\varepsilon}(u)^{\alpha-1}}_{\text{II}}\underbrace{\det(\nabla^{2}_{S}h_{\varepsilon}+h_{\varepsilon}I)}_{\text{III}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u).

Let us expand terms I–III to the first order:

I

\displaystyle=\langle Bu,u\rangle+\varepsilon\big(\,g\,\langle Bu,u\rangle+\langle B\,\nabla_{S}g,\,u\rangle\,\big)+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

II

\displaystyle=1+\varepsilon(\alpha-1)g+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

III

\displaystyle=1+\varepsilon\big(\Delta_{S}g+(m-1)g\big)+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Multiplying and keeping
O(\varepsilon)
-terms we get

\langle Bu,u\rangle+\varepsilon\Big(\underbrace{\langle B\,\nabla_{S}g,\,u\rangle}_{A_{1}}+\underbrace{\big(\alpha+m-1\big)g\,\langle Bu,u\rangle}_{A_{2}}+\underbrace{\big(\Delta_{S}g\big)\,\langle Bu,u\rangle}_{A_{3}}\Big)+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Now, we integrate over
S^{m-1}
and use two identities:

\bullet

Tangential integration by parts.
Since
\nabla_{S}g
is tangential,

\int\langle B\,\nabla_{S}g,\,u\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\int\nabla_{S}g\cdot P_{T}(B^{\top}u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=-\int g\,\div_{S}\!\big(P_{T}(B^{\top}u)\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega,

and (see Appendix A or
[
1
, Ch. 2]
)

\div_{S}\!\big(P_{T}(B^{\top}u)\big)=-\,m\,\langle Bu,u\rangle+\tr(B).

Hence, for
\int g\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=0
,

\int A_{1}\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=m\int g\,\langle Bu,u\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

\bullet

Spherical Laplacian on quadratics.
For
f(u)=\langle Bu,u\rangle
,

\Delta_{S}f=-2m\,\big(\langle Bu,u\rangle-\tfrac{1}{m}\tr(B)\big).

Thus, again using
\int g\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=0
,

\int A_{3}\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=-2m\int g\,\langle Bu,u\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

Combining
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
we obtain for the numerator:

\int\big(\cdots\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\int\langle Bu,u\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega+\varepsilon\,\Big(\alpha-1\Big)\int g\,\langle Bu,u\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Next, the normalising constant

C_{\alpha}(h_{\varepsilon})=\int h_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\det(\nabla^{2}_{S}h_{\varepsilon}+h_{\varepsilon}I)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega

satisfies
C_{\alpha}(h_{\varepsilon})=1+O(\varepsilon^{2})
under
\int g=0
. Therefore (using
\int\langle Bu,u\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\frac{1}{m}\tr(B)
),

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\,\big\langle B,\,T_{\nu_{k}^{\alpha}}(h_{\varepsilon})\big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}\;=\;(\alpha-1)\,\int_{S^{m-1}}g(u)\,\langle Bu,u\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u).

If
g
has a non-zero degree
-
2
component (equivalently,
B_{V}
is a genuinely non-spherical perturbation), choose
B
so that
u\mapsto\langle Bu,u\rangle
matches
P_{2}g
; then the integral is non-zero unless
\alpha=1
. This proves (ii).
∎

Appendix A
Fourth
-

Let
\omega
be the
probability
surface measure on
S^{N-1}
. The second and fourth moments are

\int u\,u^{\top}\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u)=\frac{1}{N}I,\qquad\int u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}u_{\ell}\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u)=\frac{\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}\delta_{jk}}{N(N+2)}.

Let
g(u)=u^{\top}Su
with
\tr S=0
. Then

\int g(u)\,u\,u^{\top}\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u)=\sum_{k,\ell}S_{k\ell}\int u_{k}u_{\ell}\,u\,u^{\top}\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\frac{2}{N(N+2)}\,S,

while
\int g(u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\frac{1}{N}\tr S=0
. Hence

\int g(u)\Big(u\,u^{\top}-\frac{1}{N}I\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u)=\frac{2}{N(N+2)}\,S.

Comparing with
\mathcal{L}(g)=\alpha_{N}\!\int g(u)(uu^{\top}-\tfrac{1}{N}I)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega
, and using the calibration from
N=2
(Example
3.9
), we obtain
\alpha_{N}=-N
. Therefore,

(A.1)

\mathcal{A}_{X}(\varepsilon)\;=\;\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}(g)+O(\varepsilon^{2})\;=\;-\,\frac{2\varepsilon}{N+2}\,\int_{S^{N-1}}g(u)\Big(uu^{\top}-\tfrac{1}{N}I\Big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\omega(u)\;+\;O(\varepsilon^{2}),

4.1

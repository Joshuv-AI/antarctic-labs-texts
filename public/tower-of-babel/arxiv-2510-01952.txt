Infinitely presented simple groups separated by homological finiteness properties
Claudio Llosa Isenrich, Eduard Schesler, Xiaolei Wu

Abstract
. Given a finitely generated linear group G G over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , we construct a simple group Γ \Gamma that has the same finiteness properties as G G and admits G G as a quasi-retract. As an application, we construct a simple group of type FP ∞ \FP_{\infty} that is not finitely presented. Moreover we show that for every n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} there is a simple group of type FP n \FP_{n} that is neither finitely presented nor of type FP n + 1 \FP_{n+1} . Since our simple groups arise as Röver–Nekrashevych groups, this answers a question of Zaremsky.

Primary 20E32; Secondary 20E08

1.
Introduction

A group is of type
\F_{n}
(resp.
\F
) if it admits a classifying space with finite
n
-skeleton (resp. finite skeleton).
These so-called (homotopical) finiteness properties were introduced by Wall
[
Wal65
]
and generalize the properties of being finitely generated, which is equivalent to being of type
\F_{1}
, and being finitely presented, which is equivalent to being of type
\F_{2}
.
Since then, finiteness properties are typically among the first properties that one aims to determine when one is interested in a group.
Apart from a few notable exceptions
[
BKW13
]
, it is usually the case that a finitely presented group that arises in nature, is of type
\F_{\infty}
, i.e. of type
\F_{n}
for every
n
. Thus, it is common to say that groups which are not
\F_{\infty}
have
exotic finiteness properties
. The first examples of finitely presented groups of type
\F_{n}
and not
\F_{n+1}
were constructed by Stallings for
n=2

[
Sta63
]
and Bieri for all
n\geq 3

[
Bie76
]
. Since then examples of groups of type
\F_{n}
and not
\F_{n+1}
have been produced with various additional properties, see e.g.
[
SWZ19
,
Sch23
,
LIP24
]
for some recent instances of this.
Even more exotic finiteness properties can be obtained by considering a homological analogue of the properties
\F_{n}
.
These were introduced by Bieri
[
Bie81
]
, who defined a group to be of type
\FP_{n}(R)
(resp.
\FP(R)
) for a commutative ring
R
, if the trivial
RG
-module
R
admits a resolution by projective
RG
-modules that is finitely generated up to degree
n
(resp. finitely generated in all degrees and of finite length).
It can be easily verified that a group of type
\F_{n}
is also of type
\FP_{n}(R)
for every
R
and that the properties
\F_{1}
and
\FP_{1}(R)
coincide as soon as
R\neq 0
.
However, the question whether
\FP_{n}(R)
implies
\F_{n}
for
R\neq 0
remained open until the seminal work of Bestvina and Brady
[
BB97
]
, in which they constructed a group of type
\FP(\mathbb{Z})
that is not of type
\F_{2}
.
Moreover they showed that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a group of type
\FP_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
that is neither of type
\FP_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z})
nor of type
\F_{2}
.
Recall that the restriction to groups that are not of type
\F_{2}
makes the situation more interesting here since for groups of type
\F_{2}
the properties
\F_{n}
and
\FP_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
coincide.
Until today, the known sources of infinitely presented groups of type
\FP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
are very limited, see e.g.
[
KLS20
,
Kro22
]
, and there is barely a naturally occurring class of groups known that contains such groups.
The following result in particular tells us that simple groups are among these classes and that in fact simple groups admit the same variety of finiteness properties as the groups of Bestvina and Brady.
To make this result more precise, we recall that the flag complex
\Flag(\Gamma)
of a graph
\Gamma
is the simplicial complex, with
k
-simplices the
(k+1)
-cliques in
\Gamma
.
We will further denote by
V(\Gamma)
and
E(\Gamma)
the vertex and edge sets of
\Gamma
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every finite graph
\Gamma
there is a simple group
G_{\Gamma}
with the following finiteness properties, where
R\neq 0
is any commutative unital ring:

(1)

G_{\Gamma}
is of type
\F_{n}
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is
(n-1)
-connected;

(2)

G_{\Gamma}
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is homologically
(n-1)
-connected over
R
;

(3)

G_{\Gamma}
is
\F_{\infty}
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is contractible;

(4)

G_{\Gamma}
is
\FP_{\infty}(R)
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is
R
-acyclic.

It is well-known that for every
n\geq 1
one can choose
\Gamma
so that
\Flag(\Gamma)
is homologically
(n-1)
-connected over
\mathbb{Z}
, but not homologically
n
-connected over
\mathbb{Z}
and not simply connected.
There are also examples of finite acyclic complexes that are not simply connected
[
BB97
]
. Thus, we obtain the following direct consequences of
Theorem

1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a simple group of type
\FP_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
that is neither of type
\FP_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z})
nor finitely presented.

Corollary 1.3
.

There is a simple group of type
\FP_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
that is not finitely presented.

Independently and with different methods Bonn and Giersbach recently constructed non-discrete simple totally disconnected locally compact groups which are
\FP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, but not compactly presented
[
BG25
]
.

In fact, we are neither aware of a way to transfer their methods to the discrete case, nor of a way to apply the methods in this paper to the non-discrete case.

As the formulation of Theorem
1.1
already suggests, its proof is based on techniques that allow us to transfer the finiteness properties of the Bestvina–Brady group
\BB_{\Gamma}
to the simple group
G_{\Gamma}
.
The following result, from which Theorem
1.1
will be deduced, sheds a bit more light on the role that is played by the group
\BB_{\Gamma}
for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists a simple group
G
that has the following properties:

(1)

G
has the same finiteness properties as
H
,

(2)

H
is a subgroup of
G
,

(3)

G
admits a quasi-retract onto
H
.

Remark 1.5
.

Recall that every finitely generated subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
, can be embedded into
\GL_{m}(\mathbb{Q})
for an appropriate number
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
As a consequence, one could replace
\mathbb{Q}
with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
in Theorem
1.4
.

Since every Bestvina–Brady group
\BB_{\Gamma}
, being a subgroup of a right-angled Artin group, is a subgroup of a right-angled Coxeter group
[
DJ00
]
, it follows that
\BB_{\Gamma}
is linear over
\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}
as the cosine matrix of a right-angled Coxeter group is integral.
Thus we can deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.4
by considering the case where
H=\BB_{\Gamma}
, see Section
7
.
The proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on the Röver–Nekrashevych construction (see Definition
2.2
), which gained great popularity in the construction of finitely presented simple groups, especially in the context of the Boone–Higman conjecture, see
[
BBMZ23
]
for some background.
This construction takes as input a self-similar subgroup
H
of the automorphism group of a
d
-adic rooted tree
T
and associates to it a subgroup
V_{d}(H)
of the homeomorphism group of the boundary of
T
, which is known as the Röver–Nekrashevych group associated to
H
.
In order to realize the group
G
from Theorem
1.4
as a Röver-Nekrashevych group
V_{d}(H)
, there are three difficulties to overcome.
The first one is that
V_{d}(H)
is not always simple.
In fact
V_{d}(H)
can have infinite abelianization.
The second difficulty is that
H
does not need to be a quasi-retract of
V_{d}(H)
.
This can be deduced from the structure of the Röver–Nekrashevych group associated to Grigorchuk’s group, which was shown to be finitely presented by Röver
[
Röv99
]
.
Indeed, since Grigorchuk’s group is not finitely presented, it cannot arise as a quasi-retract of a finitely presented group
[
Alo94
]
.
The third and most dramatic difficulty is that there is no reason to believe that the group
H
in Theorem
1.4
is self-similar, so that we cannot consider its corresponding Röver-Nekrashevych group.
In fact, the question of the existence of self-similar structures in linear groups is not well understood and deserves further study, see
[
Kap12
]
for some results in this direction.
The fruitfulness of such a study was proven, for example, by Skipper, Witzel and Zaremsky
[
SWZ19
]
, who found self-similar representations for a carefully chosen family
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of linear groups over global function fields, such that the corresponding Röver-Nekrashevych groups
V_{d}(H_{n})
are of type
\F_{n}
but not of type
\F_{n+1}
.
Moreover they showed that their groups
V_{d}(H_{n})
admit
H_{n}
as a quasi-retract.
To overcome the first of the aforementioned difficulties of constructing such Röver–Nekrashevych groups, they introduced the notion of coarse diagonality of a self-similar group
H
, which means that for every
h\in H
and every level-
1
state
h_{i}
of
h
, the element
h^{-1}h_{i}
has finite order, and showed that the Röver–Nekrashevych group of a finitely generated coarsely diagonal self-similar group is virtually simple.
This rather restrictive property was relaxed by Zaremsky
[
Zar25b
]
to the notion of weak diagonality, which is still sufficient to deduce the virtual simplicity of
V_{d}(H)
, while only requiring that there is a generating set
S
of
H
such that, for all
s\in S
and every level-
1
state
s_{i}
of
s
, the element
s^{-1}s_{i}
has finite order in the abelianization of
H
.
To overcome the second of the aforementioned difficulties, Skipper, Witzel and Zaremsky
[
SWZ19
]
introduced the notion of persistency of a self-similar group and combined it with the well-known notion of rationality, which allowed them to deduce the existence of a quasi-retract onto their linear groups
H_{n}
.
Finally, they twisted some natural self-similar action of
H_{n}
that satisfied all of the required properties to deduce their result.
The first goal of this paper will be to show that a certain class of linear groups
H
over
\mathbb{Q}
admits self-similar representations for which one can completely abandon the assumptions of rationality, weak diagonality, and persistence, while still retaining that the corresponding Röver–Nekrashevych group
V_{d}(H)
is virtually simple, has the same finiteness properties as
H
, and admits
H
as a quasi-retract, see Sections
4
,
5
,
6
.
In the second step we produce a self-similar extension of an arbitrary finitely generated subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, which ultimately allows us to get rid of the self-similarity assumption itself and thereby prove Theorem
1.4
, see Subsection
6.2
.
Since after all the group
G
in Theorem
1.4
, and hence the group in Corollary
1.2
, is a Röver–Nekrashevych group, and therefore Thompson-like in the sense of Skipper and Zaremsky
[
SZ24
, Definition 3.2]
, we obtain an affirmative answer to the following question of Zaremsky
[
BBN18
, Question 113]
.

Question 1.6

(Zaremsky)
.

In the family of Thompson-like groups, can one find non-finitely presentable groups of type
\FP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
?

As a further application of Theorem
1.4
, we obtain that every coarse invariant of a finitely generated subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
arises as a lower bound for the invariant of a simple group with the same finiteness properties.
An example of such a coarse invariant is provided by (higher dimensional) Dehn functions, which allows us to prove the following, see Corollary
7.4
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Every (high-dimensional) Dehn function of a subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
arises as a lower bound on the corresponding Dehn function of a simple group with the same finiteness properties.

Corollary
1.7
provides us with a tool that is potentially capable of producing finitely presented simple groups with very fast growing Dehn functions.
The question on the existence of such groups naturally arises in the light of the fact that finitely presented simple groups have solvable word problem and therefore a computable Dehn function so that it is natural to ask how fast it can be, see e.g.
[
Zar
]
for a discussion on this initiated by Zaremsky.
However, despite of the fact that no recursive upper bound on Dehn functions of finitely presented linear groups is known so far,
though conjectured to exist by Gersten and Riley
[
GR05
, Conjecture 8.7]
, the fastest known Dehn function of a linear group is exponential.
As exponential functions also arise as high-dimensional Dehn functions of linear groups over
\mathbb{Q}
, we obtain the following consequence of Corollary
1.7
.

Corollary 1.8
.

For every finite subset
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, there is a simple group of type
\F_{\infty}
whose
n
-dimensional Dehn function is at least exponential for every
n\in I
.

A special case of Corollary
1.8
was obtained by Zaremsky
[
Zar25b
]
, who carefully calibrated certain actions of the Baumslag-Solitar groups
\BS(1,n)\leq\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
on an appropriate rooted tree to make them rational, weakly diagonal, and persistent. This allowed him to obtain
\BS(1,n)
as a quasi-retract of its Röver–Nekrashevych group and therefore to deduce the existence of finitely presented simple groups whose Dehn function is bounded from below by an exponential function.

As another consequence of our considerations, we obtain the first known examples of self-similar groups that are separated by homological finiteness properties, see Proposition
7.3
.

Proposition 1.9
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a self-similar group of type
\FP_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
that is neither of type
\FP_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z})
nor finitely presented.
Moreover there is a self-similar group of type
\FP_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
that is not finitely presented.

Finally, it might be worth mentioning that Theorem
1.4
seems to push the current techniques for constructing Röver–Nekrashevych groups with interesting finiteness properties via linear groups, which began in Scott
[
Sco84
]
and evolved e.g. in
[
SWZ19
,
Zar25a
,
Zar25b
]
, to their limits.
As a natural next step in this direction, we propose the following question.

Question 1.10
.

Let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Does there exist a simple group
G
that has the following properties?

(1)

G
has the same finiteness properties as
H
,

(2)

H
is a subgroup of
G
,

(3)

G
admits a quasi-retract onto
H
.

As indicated above, an answer to this question will likely require completely new techniques.
We take the opportunity to formulate our second question that naturally arises within the framework of Röver–Nekrashevych groups.

Question 1.11
.

Does every finitely generated linear group embed into a finitely generated self-similar group?

We conclude with the following general question. There is no a priori reason why simple groups of type
\FP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
should have solvable word problem. So in the context of the first construction of infinitely presented simple groups of type
\FP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and the recent interest in the Boone–Higman Conjecture
[
BBMZ23
]
, it is natural to ask:

Question 1.12
.

Does every countable group embed in a simple group of type
\FP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
?

Recall that Leary proved in
[
Lea18
]
that every countable group embeds in a
\FP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
group. On the other hand, it is a classical theorem of Hall
[
Hal74
]
that every countable group embeds in a finitely generated simple group, see also
[
Gor74
,
Sch76
,
BZ22
]
for some different proofs.

2.
Background on Röver–Nekrashevych groups

We introduce Röver–Nekrashevych groups, following the exposition in
[
SWZ19
]
and
[
BM24
]
. For a more detailed introduction to the subject, we refer to
[
Nek05
,
Nek18
]
.

2.1.
The Röver–Nekrashevych group
V_{d}(G)

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{d}
be the infinite rooted
d
-ary tree whose vertex set
V(\mathcal{T}_{d})
is given by the set
X_{d}^{\ast}
of words over the alphabet
X_{d}=\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}
and whose (directed) edges are given by the pairs
(u,ux)
with
u\in V(\mathcal{T}_{d})
and
x\in X_{d}
.
In other words,
\mathcal{T}_{d}
is the Cayley graph of the free monoid
X_{d}^{\ast}
with respect to
X_{d}
.
For
f\in\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
and
u\in V(\mathcal{T}_{d})
there is a unique
f^{u}\in\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
with
f(uv)=f(u)f^{u}(v)
for every
v\in X_{d}^{\ast}
. We call
f^{u}
the
state
of
f
at
u
.
Note that
f
fixes the root of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
.
As a consequence,
f
restricts to an action on the
level
-
n
vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
, which are represented by words of length
n
over
X_{d}
.
In the case where
n=1
, this provides us with a permutation
\rho(f)\in S_{d}
in the symmetric group
S_{d}
on
d
elements via
\rho(f)(x)=f(x)
.
If no ambiguity is possible, we will also write
\rho(f)
to denote the element of
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
that is given by
\rho(f)(xv)=f(x)v
for every
x\in X_{d}
and every
v\in X_{d}^{\ast}
.
Automorphisms of the latter type are called
rooted
and can be combined with the states of
f
to decompose
f
via the so-called
wreath recursion
, which is given by the isomorphism

\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})\to S_{d}\wr\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d}),\ f\mapsto\rho(f)(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}),

where
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d})
acts on
\mathcal{T}_{d}
by mapping a word of the form
xv
to
xf_{x}(v)
.
Using the wreath recursion, we can define a subset
S\subseteq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
to be
self-similar
if for every
s\in S
and
u\in V(\mathcal{T}_{d})
we have
s^{u}\in S
.
A subgroup
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
is called
self-similar
if it is self-similar as a subset.
If
G
admits a finite generating set
S
that is self-similar, then
G
is said to be an
automata group
.
We say that an element
f\in\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
is
finite state
, if its set of states is finite and that a subgroup
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
is
finite state
, if all its elements are finite state.
Note that a subgroup
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
is an automata group if and only if it is finite state and self-similar.
When dealing with groups that act on rooted trees, it is often useful to consider for each
u\in V(\mathcal{T}_{d})
the embedding
\iota_{u}\colon\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})\to\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
which maps an element
f\in\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
to the automorphism that acts by
\iota_{u}(uw)=uf(w)
on words with prefix
u
and trivially on all other words.
The groups we are interested in act on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}_{d}=X_{d}^{\omega}
of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
, which is the
d
-ary Cantor set
\mathfrak{C}_{d}=X_{d}^{\omega}
of infinite words in
X_{d}
. For
u\in X^{\ast}
, we will denote by

\mathfrak{C}_{d}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{uv\mid v\in X_{d}^{\omega}\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}_{d}

the cone of elements of
\mathfrak{C}_{d}
starting with
u
.
Note that
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
embeds in
\Aut(\partial\mathcal{T}_{d})
via its natural action on the set of finite prefixes of an element
u\in X_{d}^{\omega}
.
Regarding this, we will sometimes view
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
as a subgroup of
\Homeo(\partial\mathcal{T}_{d})
via this embedding.
A finite subtree
T
of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
will be called
complete
if it is rooted, i.e. contains the root
\emptyset
of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
and has the property that whenever we have
u,ux\in V(T)
for some
x\in X_{d}
, then we also have
uy\in V(T)
for every
y\in X_{d}
. A vertex of a finite complete subtree
T
is called a
leaf
, if it has valence
1
. Note that by the choice of the alphabet
X_{d}
, there is a natural lexicographic order on the leaves of a finite complete subtree of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T_{-}
and
T_{+}
be complete rooted subtrees of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
with the same number of leaves. Denote by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
the ordered leaves of
T_{-}
and by
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
the ordered leaves of
T_{+}
. Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
and let
\sigma\in S_{n}
be a permutation.
Then we write

\left[T_{-},\sigma(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}),T_{+}\right]

to denote the element in
\Homeo(\partial\mathcal{T}_{d})
that maps
\mathfrak{C}_{d}(v_{i})
to
\mathfrak{C}_{d}(u_{\sigma(i)})
via
v_{i}w\mapsto u_{\sigma(i)}f_{i}(w)
.
Elements of
\Homeo(\partial\mathcal{T}_{d})
that arise in that way will be called
almost automorphisms
of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
.
The group of all almost automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
will be denoted by
\AAut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be a self-similar group. The
Röver–Nekrashevych group
associated to
G
is the subgroup of
\AAut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
that is given by

V_{d}(G):=\left\{\left[T_{-},\sigma(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}),T_{+}\right]\in\AAut(\mathcal{T}_{d})\mid f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in G\right\}.

Note that while as a set
V_{d}(G)
makes sense even if
G
is not self-similar, the self-similarity condition guarantees that this is actually a subgroup. For
G=\left\{1\right\}
we obtain the Higman–Thompson group
V_{d}(G)=V_{d}
, which is well-known to be of type
F_{\infty}
.
Let
C
be the unique complete subtree of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
with one root and
d
leaves.
Using
C
, we define an embedding of a self-similar group
G
into its Röver–Nekrashevych group by

\iota_{1}\colon G\to V_{d}(G),\ g\mapsto(C,\mathrm{id}(g,1,\cdots,1),C).

We collect some well-known properties of Röver–Nekrashevych groups.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be self-similar.
Then the following hold:

(1)

V_{d}(G)=\left\langle\iota_{1}(G),V_{d}\right\rangle
and in particular
V_{d}(G)
is finitely generated if
G
is finitely generated;

(2)

the commutator subgroup
[V_{d}(G),V_{d}(G)]
is simple;

Proof.

For (1) see
[
Nek18
, Lemma 5.11]
and (2) is
[
Nek18
, Theorem 4.7]
.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(
[
Nek18
, Theorem 4.8]
)
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be self-similar and let
\pi:G\to G_{ab}=G/[G,G]
be the abelianization morphism. Then the abelianization
V_{d}(G)_{ab}
of
V_{d}(G)
can be described as follows:

(1)

If
d
is even, then
V_{d}(G)_{ab}
is isomorphic to the quotient of
G_{ab}
by the relations
\pi(g)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\pi(g_{i})
for all

3.
Finiteness properties of Röver–Nekrashevych groups

In this section, we will run the standard Stein–Farley complex argument to prove that the homological and homotopical finiteness properties of

are at least as good as those of

. To our knowledge this has only been done for the homotopical finiteness properties

in the literature. However, the proof adapts readily to the homological case by applying the homological version of Brown’s criterion. Readers familiar with this approach can skip straight to Section

3.1.
A poset structure on

A

We call a split

is elementary (resp. simple). For

4.
Quasi-retracts and Dehn functions

Recall first that a group
Q
is called a
retract
of a group
G
if there is a pair of group homomorphisms

Q\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}G\overset{r}{\twoheadrightarrow}Q

such that
r\circ i
is the identity on
Q
. Suppose
Q
is a retract of
G
. Then if
G
is of type
\F_{n}
, so is
Q
, see for example
[
Bux04
, Proposition 4.1]
. The same holds if one replaces retract by quasi-retract. Let us make this precise.
Recall that a function
f\colon X\to Y
between two metric spaces is said to be
coarse Lipschitz
if there exist constants
C,D>0
so that

d(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq Cd(x,x^{\prime})+D\text{ for all }x,x^{\prime}\in X.

For example, any homomorphism between finitely generated groups is coarse Lipschitz with respect to the word metrics.
A function
\rho\colon X\to Y
is said to be a quasi-retraction if it is coarse Lipschitz and there exists a coarse Lipschitz function
\iota\colon Y\to X
and a constant
E>0
so that
d(\rho\circ\iota(y),y)\leq E
for all
y\in Y
. If such a function exists,
Y
is said to be a quasi-retract of
X
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
Alo94
, Theorem 8]

Let
G
and
Q
be finitely generated groups such that
Q
is a quasi-retract of
G
with respect to word metrics corresponding to some finite generating sets of
G
and
Q
.
Then

(1)

if
G
is of type
\F_{n}
, so is
Q
,

(2)

if
G
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
for some unital commutative ring
R\neq 0
, so is
Q
.

Proof.

In
[
Alo94
, Theorem 8]
, assertion (2) is only stated for
R=\mathbb{Z}
. However, the only place where
R=\mathbb{Z}
is used in the proof is when
[
Bro87
, Theorem 2.2]
is invoked. Since
[
Bro87
, Theorem 2.2]
holds for arbitrary rings
R
(see Brown’s Remark (1) right after its statement in
[
Bro87
]
), one checks readily that so does
[
Alo94
, Theorem 8]
with the same proof. This has also been observed in
[
CCC20
, Theorem 5.5]
in the more general context of compactly generated tdlc groups.
∎

Another interesting consequence of quasi-retracts is that they provide a lower bound for Dehn functions. Given a finitely presented group
G
with finite presentation
\langle X\mid R\rangle
, let
w
be a word in the free group
F(X)
generated by elements in
X
. If
w=1
in
G
, we can write
w
as finite product of conjugates of elements of relations from
R
and their inverses, i.e.

w=\prod_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}r_{i}w_{i}^{-1}

for some
r_{i}\in R^{\pm 1}
,
w_{i}\in F(X)
. Define
\Area(w)
to be the minimal such
n
. The function

\delta_{G}(n)=\max\{\Area(w)\mid|w|\leq n,w=1~\text{in}~G\}

is called the
Dehn function
of
G
.

The Dehn functions associated to different finite presentations of the same group are
\cong
equivalent in the following sense: given functions
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)
, one writes
f_{1}\preceq f_{2}
if there is a constant
K>0
such that
f_{1}(n)\leq Kf_{2}(Kn)+Kn+K
, and one writes
f_{1}\cong f_{2}
if
f_{1}\preceq f_{2}
and
f_{2}\preceq f_{1}
.

For a null-homotopic word
w
, one can interpret
\Area(w)
in terms of the (cellular) filling area for the loop described by
w
, based at the identity, in the presentation complex corresponding to
\langle X\mid R\rangle
. If
G
is of type
\F_{r+1}
, one can generalise this geometric viewpoint and define higher Dehn functions
\delta^{(k)}_{G}(n)
for
1\leq k\leq r
in terms of filling volumes of cellular
k
-spheres in universal covers of classifying spaces with finite
(r+1)
-skeleton. We refer to
[
AWP99
]
for a precise definition, since we will not require it here. Instead we will only use known facts about Dehn functions, including the following behaviour under quasi-retracts.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
Alo90
]
,
[
AWP99
, Corollary 5]
)
.

Let
G
and
Q
be groups of type
\F_{n+1}
such that
Q
is a quasi-retract of
G
with respect to word metrics corresponding to some finite generating sets. Then
\delta^{(k)}_{Q}\preceq\delta^{(k)}_{G}
for
1\leq k\leq n
.

As noted in the introduction, a self-similar group
G
does not need to be a quasi-retract of
V_{d}(G)
.
In
[
SWZ19
,
Zar25b
]
, the authors deal with this fact by introducing a set of conditions that guarantees that
G
is a quasi-retract of
V_{d}(G)
and then find groups that satisfy these conditions.
In this section we follow a different approach.
Instead of realizing a given group
G
as a quasi-retract of
V_{d}(G)
, we produce a self-similar split extension
\Gamma
of
G
for which the natural retraction
\Gamma\rightarrow G
can be precomposed with some map
V_{d}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\Gamma
such that the resulting map
V_{d}(\Gamma)\rightarrow G
is a quasi-retraction.
We start by introducing the following notion.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be a self-similar group and let
r\colon G\rightarrow Q
be a retraction.
We say that
r
is
persistent
if for every
g
and every
u\in V(\mathcal{T}_{d})
we have
r(g)=r(g^{u})
, i.e. the image of a state of
g
does not depend on the vertex at which the state is defined.

We proceed by showing how persistent retractions can be used to construct quasi-retractions.
To this end, recall that we can view
V_{d}
naturally as the subgroup of
V_{d}(G)
with trivial labeling, i.e.
V_{d}=V_{d}(\{1\})
.
Recall also that
C
denotes the unique tree with one root and
d
leaves, and we have an embedding
\iota_{1}\colon G\to V_{d}(G)
by:

h\mapsto(C,\mathrm{id}(h,1,\cdots,1),C).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be a finitely generated self-similar group and let
r\colon G\rightarrow Q
be a persistent retraction.
Then the Röver–Nekrashevych group
V_{d}(G)
admits
Q
as a quasi-retract.

Proof.

We first define a map
\rho:V_{d}(G)\to Q
and then prove that it is a quasi-retract. Given any element
x=[T_{-},\sigma(g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}),T_{+}]
, let
\rho(x):=r(g_{1})
. To prove that
\rho
is well-defined, it suffices to show that
\rho(x)
is invariant under elementary expansion of
[T_{-},\sigma(g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}),T_{+}]
. If the elementary expansion is performed at the first leaf, this is the case since
r
is persistent. If the elementary expansion is not performed at the first leaf,
g_{1}
does not change, so
\rho
is also well-defined in this case.

We now proceed to show that
\rho
is a quasi-retract. Since
Q
is a retraction of the finitely generated group
G
, we can write
G=\ker(r)\rtimes Q
and
Q
is finitely generated by some finite generating set
S_{Q}
. We can choose a finite generating set
S_{G}
of
G
such that
S_{G}\setminus S_{Q}\subseteq\ker(r)
. Let
S_{V_{d}}
be a finite generating set of
V_{d}
. By Proposition
2.3
(1),
V_{d}(G)
is generated by
S_{V_{d}}
and
\iota_{1}(S_{G})
. It suffices to show that the map
\rho:V_{d}(G)\to Q
is coarse Lipschitz with respect to the corresponding word metrics on
V_{d}(G)
and
Q
, where as in
[
SWZ19
]
we work with left Cayley graphs. To see this, observe that persistence of
r
and a straight-forward computation analogous to the one in the proof of
[
SWZ19
, Proposition 5.5]
yield:

(1)

\rho(\iota_{1}((h,s))x)\in\{\rho(x),s\rho(x)\}
for all
s\in S_{Q},h\in\ker(r)
and
x\in V_{d}(G)
, and

(2)

\rho(yx)=\rho(x)
for any
y\in V_{d}
and
x\in V_{d}(G)
.

It follows that
\rho
is non-expanding and hence coarse Lipschitz.
∎

5.
Creating finite abelianization

In this section, we will apply a construction by Zaremsky
[
Zar25a
]
to a finitely generated self-similar group
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
to produce an embedding
j:G\to\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{md})
so that
V_{md}(j(G))
has finite abelianization. We will then show that this construction preserves persistent retractions.

We start by explaining Zaremsky’s construction. For
g\in G
, let
g=\rho(g)(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
be its wreath recursion. Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define a morphism

f^{(m)}:G\to\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{md}),~g\mapsto f_{g}^{(m)}

via the wreath recursion

f_{g}^{(m)}=\rho(g)^{\oplus m}(f_{g_{1}}^{(m)},\ldots,f_{g_{d}}^{(m)},f_{g_{1}}^{(m)}\ldots,f_{g_{d}}^{(m)},\ldots,f_{g_{1}}^{(m)},\ldots,f_{g_{d}}^{(m)}),

where
\rho(g)^{\oplus m}\in S_{md}
is given by

\rho(g)^{\oplus m}(kd+i)=kd+\rho(g)(i)\text{ for }0\leq k\leq d-1\text{ and }1\leq i\leq d.

The morphism
f^{(m)}
is clearly well-defined and has self-similar image. It is injective, since the induced
G
-action restricts to the original
G
-action on the subtree
\mathcal{T}_{d}\subset\mathcal{T}_{md}
defined by the inclusion
\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}\hookrightarrow\left\{1,\ldots,md\right\}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be a finitely generated self-similar group. Then there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
V_{md}(f^{(m)}(G))_{ab}
is finite.

Proof.

This follows from Zaremsky’s proof of
[
Zar25a
, Theorem 1.1]
. We include his argument here for the readers convenience. Denote by
\pi:G\to G_{ab}
the abelianization map. By
Proposition

2.4
, for
m
even, we have

V_{md}(f^{(m)}(G))_{ab}\cong G_{ab}/\left\langle\pi(g)-m\cdot(\pi(g_{1})+\ldots+\pi(g_{d}))\mid g\in G\right\rangle.

The right side is a quotient of the abelian group

\displaystyle=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}\pi(a_{i})/\left\langle\pi(a_{i})-m\cdot(\pi(a_{i,1})+\ldots+\pi(a_{i,d}))\mid 1\leq i\leq r\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}^{r}/{\rm Im}(I_{r}-m\cdot A),

where
A
is the
(r\times r)
-matrix whose
i
-th column is
\pi(a_{i,1})+\ldots+\pi(a_{i,d})
expressed with respect to the basis
\left\{\pi(a_{1}),\ldots,\pi(a_{r})\right\}
of
\oplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}\pi(a_{i})
. Since
A
only has finitely many eigenvalues, there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\rm Im}(I_{r}-mA)\leq\mathbb{Z}^{r}
has finite index and thus so that
V_{md}(f^{(m)}(G))_{ab}
is finite.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be a self-similar group and let
r\colon G\rightarrow Q
be a persistent retraction.
Then
r^{(m)}\colon f^{(m)}(G)\rightarrow Q,\ f^{(m)}_{g}\mapsto r(g)
is a persistent retraction of
f^{(m)}(G)\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{md})
.

Proof.

The map
r^{(m)}:f^{(m)}(G)\to Q
is a well-defined retraction, since it is the composition of the isomorphism
f^{(m)}(G)\to G
,
f_{g}^{(m)}\mapsto g
, with the retraction
r:G\to Q
. We claim that
r^{(m)}
is also persistent. Indeed, for
g\in G
, the wreath recursions
g=\rho(g)(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
,

f_{g}^{(m)}=\rho(g)^{\oplus m}(f_{g_{1}}^{(m)},\ldots,f_{g_{d}}^{(m)},f_{g_{1}}^{(m)}\ldots,f_{g_{d}}^{(m)},\ldots,f_{g_{1}}^{(m)},\ldots,f_{g_{d}}^{(m)}),

and persistence of
r
, imply that for every
1\leq j\leq md
there is some
1\leq i\leq d
such that

r^{(m)}((f_{g}^{(m)})_{j})=r^{(m)}(f^{(m)}_{g_{i}})=r(g_{i})=r(g)=r^{(m)}(f_{g}^{(m)}).

∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
be a finitely generated self-similar group and let
r\colon G\rightarrow Q
be a persistent retraction.
Then there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and some embedding
j\colon G\rightarrow\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{md})
such that
j(G)\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{md})
is a self-similar subgroup such that the corresponding Röver–Nekrashevych group
V_{md}(G)
has finite abelianization and admits
Q
as a quasi-retract.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
Lemmas

5.1
,
5.2
and
4.4
.
∎

6.
Realizing linear groups as persistent retracts

The goal of this section is to provide for a given finitely generated subgroup

, a self-similar group
G
that admits a persistent retraction
r\colon G\rightarrow Q
such that the finiteness properties of
G
and

coincide.
The self-similarity of
G
will be inherited from the affine group
\mathrm{Aff}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{Z}[1/N]^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])
, which is known to admit a natural family of self-similar actions, see e.g.
[
KS23
,
ŠV12
]
.
Let us start by recalling the latter.

6.1.
Self-similar actions of
\mathrm{Aff}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])

Let
p
be a prime that does not divide
N
.
We consider the set
X_{p,n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{n}
, which will play the role of our alphabet
X_{d}
from the previous sections.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{p,n}
denote the rooted tree corresponding to
X_{p,n}
.
Recall that the boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}_{p,n}
can be identified with the set
X_{p,n}^{\omega}
of infinite sequences over
X_{p,n}
, which in turn can be identified with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
via
(x_{n})\mapsto\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{i}x_{i}
.
In view of this, the natural action of

\Aff_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
induces an action on
\mathcal{T}_{p,n}
and hence on
X_{p,n}^{\omega}
.
Since this action is moreover faithful, we will from now on identify
\Aff_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
with its image in
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{p,n})
.
With this point of view, it was shown in
[
KS23
, Lemma 6.3]
that the subgroup

\Aff_{n}(\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

of
\Aff_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is self-similar.
More generally, essentially the same proof as in
[
KS23
]
can be used to deduce the self-similarity of
\Aff_{n}(R)
for more general rings
R
, such as
R=\mathbb{Z}[1/N]
.
In what follows it will be convenient to make this more precise.

Definition 6.1
.

We call a subring
R
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

rigid
if for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
we have
x\in R
if and only if
px\in R
.
In other words,
R
is a rigid subring of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
if
(p)R=(p)\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cap R
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
R
be a rigid subring of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
g\in\Aff_{n}(R)
be an element that is given by
g(v)=Av+b
for some
A\in\GL_{n}(R)
,
b\in R^{n}
and every
v\in\mathcal{T}_{p,n}
.
Then for every
x\in X_{p,n}
there is some
b^{\prime}\in R^{n}
such that the state
g^{x}
is given by
g^{x}(v)=Av+b^{\prime}
for every
v\in\mathcal{T}_{p,n}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be an element of the form
u=x+pw
with
x\in X_{p,n}
and
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
.
Let further
x^{\prime}\in X_{p,n}
and
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be such that
Ax+b=x^{\prime}+pb^{\prime}
.
Then we have

g(u)=A(x+pw)+b=Ax+b+pAw=x^{\prime}+p(Aw+b^{\prime}),

which tells us that
g^{x}
is given by
g^{x}(w)=Aw+b^{\prime}
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}\subset R
, we have
X_{p,n}\subset R^{n}
, where we identify
x\in X_{p,n}
with the sequence
(x,0,0,\ldots)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
.
Using
Ax+b=x^{\prime}+pb^{\prime}
and our assumption that

is rigid we thus deduce that

pb^{\prime}=Ax+b-x^{\prime}\in(p)\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\cap R^{n}=(p)R^{n}.

Hence
b^{\prime}\in R^{n}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
R
be a rigid subring of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
Q\leq\GL_{n}(R)
be a subgroup.
Then

is a self-similar subgroup of

for which the canonical map
R^{n}\rtimes Q\rightarrow Q

Let
g\in R^{n}\rtimes Q
be an element that is given by
g(v)=Av+b

A\in Q

b\in R^{n}
.
Since
(g^{x})^{y}=g^{xy}
for all

, it follows by induction from Lemma

that for every

there is some

such that the state

is given by

(6.1)

g^{u}(v)=Av+b^{\prime}

In particular it follows that

, which implies that
R^{n}\rtimes Q
is a self-similar subgroup of
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{p,n})
.
To prove the second claim of the corollary, we consider the canonical retraction
r\colon R^{n}\rtimes Q\rightarrow Q
.
From (

for every

∎

Recall that we have chosen
p

Thus

6.2.
Extending linear groups with Baumslag–Solitar groups

Our next goal is to show that every finitely generated subgroup
G

\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
arises as a persistent retract of a self-similar group that has the same finiteness properties as
G
.
To this end, we collect some auxiliary observations.
To formulate the first of them, we write
D_{n}(\mathbb{Q})=\left\{\diag(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\right\}
for the subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
with elements the diagonal matrices

with all entries equal to

Lemma 6.5
.

For every finitely generated subgroup
G\leq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
there are natural numbers

and a monomorphism
\iota\colon G\rightarrow\GL_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])

\iota(G)\cap D_{m}(\mathbb{Q})=\{\id\}

Proof.

As explained in the last subsection, there exists a natural number

By applying the morphism

Suppose

. The case
n>1
follows readily from the case
n=1
by restricting the induced isomorphism
BS(1,N)^{n}\cong(\mathbb{Z}[1/N]\rtimes\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.

\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ast_{\alpha}=\langle\ x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t\ |\ [x_{i},x_{j}],\ tx_{i}t^{-1}=x_{i}^{N}\ \forall i,j\ \rangle\rightarrow K_{n,N}

that is induced by
x_{i}\mapsto e_{i}
and
t\mapsto\diag(N)
, where
e_{i}
denotes the
i
-th standard unit vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Every finitely generated subgroup
G
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
arises as a persistent retract of a self-similar group that has the same finiteness properties as
G
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
6.5
, we can assume that
G
lies in
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])
and satisfies
G\cap D_{n}(\mathbb{Q})=\{\id\}
.
As a consequence, the intersection of
G
and
K_{n,N}
in
\Aff_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])
is trivial is well.
Since moreover
K_{n,N}
is a normal subgroup of
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\langle K_{n,N},G\rangle
, we obtain a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow K_{n,N}\rightarrow\Gamma=K_{n,N}\rtimes G\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1.

In particular,
G
is a retract of
\Gamma
. In view of
Theorem

4.1
, Lemma
6.6
and the fact that the finiteness properties of an extension of groups
A,B
are bounded from below by the minimum of the finiteness properties of
A
and
B
, see e.g.
[
Geo08
, II.7.2 Exercise 1]
, it follows that
\Gamma
has the same finiteness properties as
G
.
A further consequence of
G\cap D_{n}(\mathbb{Q})=\{\id\}
is that the group
H\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\langle G,\diag(N)\rangle
splits as a direct product

H\cong G\oplus\langle\diag(N)\rangle\cong G\times\mathbb{Z}

since
\diag(N)
lies in the center of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
.
By combining that
H\cap(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])^{n}=\{1\}
with the fact that the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]^{n}
of
K_{n,N}
is normal in
\Aff_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])
, we obtain the short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[1/N]^{n}\rightarrow\Gamma\rightarrow H\rightarrow 1.

Thus we see that
\Gamma
is of the form
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}[1/N]^{n}\rtimes H
so that we can apply Lemma
6.3
to deduce that it is a self-similar subgroup of
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{p,n})
,
p\nmid N
, for which the canonical map
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}[1/N]^{n}\rtimes H\rightarrow H
is a persistent retraction.
Finally, we can compose the latter retraction with the projection
H\cong G\oplus\langle\diag(N)\rangle\rightarrow G
to obtain a persistent retraction
\Gamma\rightarrow G
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

In general, the transition from a subgroup
G
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])
to the group
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]^{n}\rtimes G
can lead to a loss of finiteness properties.
Indeed, if
G
is the trivial group, then
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]^{n}\rtimes G
is not even finitely generated.

7.
Applications

We are now ready to prove the results stated in the introduction.
For the convenience of the reader we repeat them here.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists a simple group
G
that has the following properties:

(1)

G
has the same finiteness properties as
H
,

(2)

H
is a subgroup of
G
,

(3)

G
admits a quasi-retract onto
H
.

Proof.

From Proposition
6.7
we know that there is a self-similar subgroup
\Gamma
of
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{d})
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
that has the same finiteness properties as
H
and that admits a persistent retraction
r\colon\Gamma\rightarrow H
.
In this case, Corollary
5.3
provides us with an embedding
j\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{m})
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
j(\Gamma)
is a self-similar subgroup of
\Aut(\mathcal{T}_{m})
whose corresponding Röver–Nekrashevych group
V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
has finite abelianization and admits a quasi-retraction
\rho\colon V_{m}(j(\Gamma))\rightarrow H
.
According to Proposition
3.1
(resp.
[
SWZ19
, Section 4]
) the group
V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
for some unital commutative ring
R
(resp. of type
\F_{n}
) if
j(\Gamma)\cong\Gamma
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
(resp. of type
\F_{n}
).
On the other hand, Theorem
4.1
tells us that
V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
(resp. of type
\F_{n}
) only if
H
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
(resp. of type
\F_{n}
).
Since
\Gamma
has the same finiteness properties as
H
, we can summarize the statements by saying that
V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
has the same finiteness properties as
H
.

As
V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
has finite abelianization, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that its commutator subgroup
G\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=[V_{m}(j(\Gamma)),V_{m}(j(\Gamma))]
is a simple subgroup of finite index.
Thus the inclusion map
i\colon G\rightarrow V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
is a quasi-isometry so that another application of Theorem
4.1
implies that
G
and
H
have the same finiteness properties.
Moreover we can precompose
\rho
with
i
to obtain a quasi-retraction
\rho\circ i\colon G\rightarrow H
.
It remains to prove that
H
is a subgroup of
G
.
Since
G
has finite index in
V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
and
H
is a subgroup of
V_{m}(j(\Gamma))
, it follows from the Kaloujnine–Krasner embedding Theorem
[
KK48
]
that it is sufficient to prove that the wreath product
S_{k}\wr G
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
embeds into
G
.
Since the latter was proven by Zaremsky
[
Zar25a
, Proposition 2.5]
, this completes the proof.
∎

Using Theorem
7.1
, we can now easily prove our main result.

Theorem 7.2
.

For every finite graph
\Gamma
there is a simple group
G_{\Gamma}
with the following finiteness properties, where
R\neq 0
is any commutative unital ring:

(1)

G_{\Gamma}
is of type
\F_{n}
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is
(n-1)
-connected;

(2)

G_{\Gamma}
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is homologically
(n-1)
-connected over
R
;

(3)

G_{\Gamma}
is
\F_{\infty}
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is contractible;

(4)

G_{\Gamma}
is
\FP_{\infty}(R)
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is
R
-acyclic.

Proof.

By the main theorem of
[
BB97
]
, the Bestvina-Brady group
\BB_{\Gamma}
is of type
\FP_{n}(R)
(resp. finitely presented) if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is homologically
(n-1)
-connected (resp. simply connected).
Thus, by the Hurewicz theorem,
\BB_{\Gamma}
is of type
\F_{n}
if and only if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is
(n-1)
-connected.
Since
\BB_{\Gamma}
is a subgroup of a right-angled Artin group
A_{\Gamma}
, which in turn is a subgroup of a right-angled Coxeter group
W_{\Gamma}
, it follows from the integrality of the cosine matrix associated to
W_{\Gamma}
that
\BB_{\Gamma}
is linear over
\mathbb{Z}
.
In the case where
\BB_{\Gamma}
is finitely generated, i.e. if
\Flag(\Gamma)
is
0
-connected, we can apply Theorem
7.1
to deduce that there exists a simple group
G
that is of type
\F_{n}
(resp.
\FP_{n}(R)
) if and only if
\BB_{\Gamma}
is of type
\F_{n}
(resp.
\FP_{n}(R)
).
In the case where
\Gamma
is not
0
-connected, the claim of the Theorem is that there exists a simple group that is not finitely generated, which is well-known to be the case.
∎

As a byproduct of the proof of Theorem
7.2
, we obtain the first known examples of self-similar groups that are separated by homological finiteness properties.

Proposition 7.3
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a self-similar group of type
\FP_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
that is neither of type
\FP_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z})
nor finitely presented.
Moreover there is a self-similar group of type
\FP_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
that is not finitely presented.

Proof.

From Proposition
6.7
it follows that for every finitely generated subgroup
G
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
there is a self-similar group that has the same finiteness properties as
G
.
Thus the claim follows from the existence of Bestvina-Brady groups with the desired finiteness properties and the fact that Bestvina-Brady groups are linear over
\mathbb{Q}
, see the proof of Theorem
7.2
.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Every (high-dimensional) Dehn function of a subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
arises as a lower bound on the corresponding Dehn function of a simple group with the same finiteness properties.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
7.1
and Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

For every finite subset
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, there is a simple group of type
\F_{\infty}
whose
n
-dimensional Dehn function is at least exponential for every
n\in I
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
G_{n}\leq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
be a subgroup whose
n
-dimensional Dehn function is exponential.
By a result of Leuzinger and Young
[
LY21
]
examples of such groups are given by
\SL_{n+2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Then the product
H\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\prod\limits_{n\in I}G_{n}
is a subgroup of
\GL_{N}(\mathbb{Q})
for an appropriate
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
From Theorem
1.4
we know that there is a simple group
G
with the same finiteness properties as
H
that admits a quasi-retract onto
H
.
Since each of the groups
\SL_{n+2}(\mathbb{Z})
is of type
\F_{\infty}

[
Rag68
]
it follows that
G
is of type
\F_{\infty}
.
By precomposing the canonical projection
H\rightarrow G_{n}
with the quasi-retraction
G\rightarrow H
, we obtain a quasi-retract from
G
onto
G_{n}
for each
n\in I
.
In this case Corollary
7.4
tells us that the
n
-dimensional Dehn function of
G
is bounded from below by the
n
-dimensional Dehn function of
G_{n}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Matrix representation of the Neumann–Poincaré operator for a torus
Doosung Choi

Abstract
We represent a matrix representation of the Neumann–Poincaré operator defined on the boundaries of a torus. A torus is a doubly connected domain in three dimensions. There is a well-known parametrization for the shape of the torus, the toroidal coordinate system. Based on the coordinate system, we use toroidal harmonics to get an expansion of the NP operator for the torus. Along with proper bases, the Neumann–Poincaré operator can be explicitly represented by an infinite matrix.

35J05; 35P05;

1
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded and open region in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=2,3)
. The Neumann–Poincaré (NP) operator associated with
\partial\Omega
is a boundary integral defined by

(1.1)

\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\varphi](x)=p.v.\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Gamma(x-y)}{\partial\nu_{x}}\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\partial\Omega,

where
p.v.
stands for the Cauchy principal value and
\nu_{x}
is the outward unit normal vector at
x
. Here,
\Gamma
is the fundamental solution to the Laplacian in (
2.1
). The NP operator arises naturally when solving the Neumann or Dirichlet problems on interfaces. Recently there is high interest in the spectral analysis of the NP operator due to its relation to nanophotonics and metamaterials: Plasmon resonance occurs near the eigenvalues of the NP operator and cloaking due to anomalous localized resonance (CALR) occurs at the accumulation point of eigenvalues, respectively
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
20
,
21
]
.

Although the NP operator is not symmetric on
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
unless
\Omega
is a disk or a ball
[
19
]
, it can be symmetrized on the Sobolev space
H^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial\Omega)
using the Plemelj’s symmetrization principle
[
17
]
, where
H^{-1/2}_{0}(\partial\Omega)
is a space
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
with the mean zero condition. The spectrum of the NP operator, denoted by
\sigma(\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*})
, consists of absolutely continuous spectrum, singularly continuous spectrum, and pure point spectrum. More specifically,
\sigma(\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*})\setminus\{1/2\}
is contained in
(-1/2,1/2)

[
15
,
16
,
24
]
.
If
\Omega
has
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary with some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then the NP operator is compact and thus
\sigma(\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*})
is a sequence that accumulates to
0
. One can easily prove that
\{0,1/2\}
is the spectrum of the NP operator if
\Omega
is a disk. Complete sets of
\sigma(\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*})
for a sphere, an ellipse
[
22
]
, and an ellipsoid are already known
[
23
]
. In two dimensions, the twin spectrum relation for the NP operator holds
[
20
]
. The explicit spectrum of the NP operator has been revealed for various shapes. For example, Kang et al. investigated a spectral resolution of the NP operator on intersecting disks with corners using the bipolar coordinate system
[
14
]
. Jung and Lim studied for touching disks and crescent domains
[
13
]
and Helsing et al. classified the spectra of the NP operator on a planar domain with corners
[
12
]
.

For doubly connected domains, Ammari et al. developed spectrum of concentric disks and spheres, and derived a necessary and sufficient condition for CALR to take place
[
1
,
2
]
, After that, Chung et al. computed eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the NP operator associated with confocal ellipses
[
9
]
. Recently, Choi et al. showed that spectrum of the NP operator for an arbitrary-shaped, planar, and doubly connected domain approaches to
[-1/2,1/2]
as thickness of the shape tends to zero
[
8
]
. In addition, Ando et el. proved that the NP operator defined on the boundary of a torus has infinitely many positive and negative eigenvalues
[
3
]
. To establish this, the NP operator is decomposed into self-adjoint, compact operators, showing that both positive and negative eigenvalues exist in their numerical range. This is the first result that identified the torus as the three dimensional surface with infinitely many negative eigenvalues.

We explicitly find the expansion the NP operator of a torus by using the toroidal harmonics. We investigate spectral analysis of those doubly connected domains in three-dimensions. We acquire harmonics expansions of the NP operators from the suggested coordinate systems.

In this paper, we explore the basis for the density function of the NP operator using the toroidal coordinate system as introduced in
[
3
]
. Furthermore, we employ the toroidal functions, which are a specialized form of the associated Legendre functions. We then decompose the NP operator analytically in the form of infinite matrices.

2
Preliminary

2.1
Layer Potentials

The fundamental solution
\Gamma
to the Laplacian has the form

(2.1)

\Gamma(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\ln|x|}{2\pi},\quad d=2,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{1}{4\pi|x|},\quad d=3.\end{cases}

For a density function
\varphi\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, we define the single layer potential associated with
\partial\Omega
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi](x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Gamma(x-y)\,\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and the single layer potential satisfies the following jump relations on the boundary
[
24
]
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi]\Big|_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\left(\pm\frac{1}{2}I+\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}\right)[\varphi]\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega

with the NP operator
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
defined in (
1.1
), and we have from (
2.1
),

\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\varphi](x)=p.v.\frac{1}{\omega_{d}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\left\langle x-y,\nu_{x}\right\rangle}{|x-y|^{d}}\,\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\partial\Omega,

where
\omega_{d}=2\pi
if
d=2
and
\omega_{d}=4\pi
if
d=3
.

2.2
Toroidal coordinate system

Toroidal coordinates are a three-dimensional orthogonal system that is formed by rotating the two-dimensional bipolar coordinate system around the axis that divides its two focal points. The two foci in bipolar coordinates lies on the unit circle in the
xy
-plane of the toroidal system, where
z
-axis is the axis of the rotation. The unit circle is called the focal ring or the reference circle.

We define
(x,y,z)
as the Cartesian coordinate system. The toroidal coordinate system
(\tau,\phi,\sigma)
relates with the Cartesian coordinate system as follows:

(2.3)

\displaystyle x=\frac{a\sinh\tau\cos\phi}{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma},\quad y=\frac{a\sinh\tau\sin\phi}{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma},\quad z=\frac{a\sin\sigma}{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma},

where the coordinate ranges are
\tau>0
,
0\leq\phi<2\pi
, and
0\leq\sigma<2\pi
. The coordinate surface
\tau=\tau_{0}
with a constant
\tau_{0}>0
represents a torus.

It is well-known that the normal derivative and the tangential derivatives of a function with toroidal coordinates satisfy

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma}{a}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial T_{\sigma}}=\frac{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma}{a}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\sigma},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial T_{\phi}}=\frac{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma}{a\sinh\tau}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\phi}.

2.3
Toroidal harmonics

One advantage of the toroidal coordinates is that we can apply the separation of variable method to solve the Laplace equation. The solution of the Laplace equation
\Delta u=0
has the form

u(\tau,\phi,\sigma)=(\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,u_{1}(\tau)\,u_{2}(\phi)\,u_{3}(\psi).

In fact, the toroidal harmonics of the above form are

(2.5)

\displaystyle G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)=(\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{in\sigma},

\displaystyle H_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)=(\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{in\sigma},

where
P_{\nu}^{\mu}
and
Q_{\nu}^{\mu}

(\nu,\mu\in\mathbb{C})
are the associated Legendre function of the first and second kind, respectively. In particular, if
\nu=n-1/2
and
\mu=m
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we call
P_{n-1/2}^{m}
and
Q_{n-1/2}^{m}
the toroidal or ring functions. The following formulas are the integral representation of the toroidal functions
[
25
]
:

\displaystyle P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma(n+1/2)}{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\cos m\theta}{(\cosh\tau+\sinh\tau\cos\theta)^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}d\theta,

\displaystyle Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)=(-1)^{m}\frac{\Gamma(n+1/2)}{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cosh mt}{(\cosh\tau+\sinh\tau\cosh t)^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}dt.

G_{mn}
is harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
except for the
z
-axis and called the internal toroidal harmonics.
H_{mn}
is harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
except for the reference circle
z=0
,
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
and called the external toroidal harmonics. We refer
[
5
,
25
]
for further details on the toroidal harmonics and the toroidal functions.

3
Matrix representation of the NP operator for a torus

Let us consider a three dimensional region, i.e.,
d=3
. We define a function

u(x)=\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi](x)

for
\varphi\in H_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
. Then
u
is the solution of the system

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Delta u=0\qquad&\mbox{in }\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\partial\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u\big|_{+}=u\big|_{-}\qquad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{+}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{-}=\varphi\qquad&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u(x)=O(|x|^{-2})\qquad&\mbox{as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

Let
\Omega
be a torus with the boundary satisfying
\{(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\tau=\tau_{0}\}
. According to the definition of the toroidal harmonics in (
2.5
), the following
u_{mn}
is a solution of the system (
3.1
):

(3.2)

u_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\,G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\qquad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\,H_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\qquad&\mbox{in }\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.\end{cases}

Here,
\Omega=\{(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\tau>\tau_{0}\}
and
\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}=\{(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\tau<\tau_{0}\}
. There exist a density function
\varphi_{n}^{m}=\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)\in H_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
such that

\varphi_{n}^{m}=\frac{\partial u_{mn}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}^{-}}-\frac{\partial u_{mn}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}^{+}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad u_{mn}(x)=\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](x).

Using (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial u_{mn}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}^{-}}-\frac{\partial u_{mn}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}^{+}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}{a}\Bigg[Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial H_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)}{\partial\tau}\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}-P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)}{\partial\tau}\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}\Bigg].

As
H_{mn}
and
G_{mn}
have a relation

H_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)=G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)}{Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)},

the derivative of the product follows that

(3.4)

\frac{\partial H_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)}{\partial\tau}=\frac{\partial G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)}{\partial\tau}\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)}{Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)}+G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\frac{d}{d\tau}\Bigg[\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)}{Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)}\Bigg].

Let
\eta=\cosh\tau
. Then the toroidal functions have the Wronskian relation as the next lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

For
z=\cosh\tau
, the toroidal functions satisfy the Wronskian relation

Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)\frac{d}{dz}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)-P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)\frac{d}{dz}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{z^{2}-1}\frac{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By elliminating common terms in (
3.3
) from (
3.4
), the density function holds that

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}{a}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\,G_{mn}(\tau_{0},\phi,\sigma)\frac{d}{d\tau}\Bigg[\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)}{Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)}\Bigg]\Bigg|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}{a}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\,G_{mn}(\tau_{0},\phi,\sigma)\frac{d\cosh\tau}{d\tau}\Bigg|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}\frac{d}{dz}\Bigg[\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)}{Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)}\Bigg]\Bigg|_{z=\cosh(\tau_{0})},

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}{a}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\,G_{mn}(\tau_{0},\phi,\sigma)\sinh\tau_{0}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh(\tau_{0}))\frac{d}{dz}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)|_{z=\cosh(\tau_{0})}-P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh(\tau_{0}))\frac{d}{dz}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)|_{z=\cosh(\tau_{0})}}{\Big[Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh(\tau_{0}))\Big]^{2}},

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{a\sinh\tau_{0}}\frac{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}(\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{im\phi}\,e^{in\sigma},

where the last formula holds from (
2.5
) and Lemma
3.1
. Thus, we get the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

The density function
\varphi_{n}^{m}
for the solution (
3.2
) of the system (
3.1
) has the form

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{mn}(\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{in\sigma}\quad\mbox{for }m,n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\Gamma_{mn}
is the constant,

(3.5)

\Gamma_{mn}=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{a\sinh\tau_{0}}\frac{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}.

Let us find the spectral decomposition of the NP operator. As
\Omega=\{(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\tau>\tau_{0}\}
, the jump formula (
2.2
) gives

\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](\tau,\phi,\sigma)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}^{\mp}}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)+\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](\phi,\sigma)\quad\mbox{for }m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then (
2.4
), (
3.2
), and Proposition
3.2
imply that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](\tau,\phi,\sigma)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}^{-}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](\tau,\phi,\sigma)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}^{+}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}H_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}+P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}{2a}\left[Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}H_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}+P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}G_{mn}(\tau,\phi,\sigma)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}{2a}\Bigg[Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left((\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)\right)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+P_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left((\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(\cosh\tau)\right)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}\Bigg]\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{in\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{(z_{0}-\cos\sigma)\sinh\tau_{0}}{2a}\Bigg[Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\left(\frac{1}{2}(z_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})+(z_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\left(\frac{1}{2}(z_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})+(z_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\right)\Bigg]\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{in\sigma},

\displaystyle=\frac{(z_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sinh\tau_{0}}{2a}\Bigg[(z_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{-1}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})+P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\frac{\partial}{\partial z}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\Bigg]\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{in\sigma},

where the last equality holds from
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=\frac{dz}{d\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
with
z=\cosh\tau
and
z_{0}=\cosh\tau_{0}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}\sinh^{2}\tau_{0}}{2}\frac{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}\,\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigg[\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})}{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}+Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})+P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\Bigg].

We need the following lemma of which gives the Fourier expansion of a function in (
3
).

Lemma 3.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

For
\tau_{0}>0
and
0\leq\sigma<2\pi
, we have

(3.7)

\frac{\sinh\tau_{0}}{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-k\tau_{0}}\cos(k\sigma)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-|k|\tau_{0}}e^{ik\sigma}.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the toroidal function satisfies the relations

P^{-m}_{n-\frac{1}{2}}(z)=\frac{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z).

Lemma 3.5
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
z>1
, the toroidal functions satisfy the derivative formulas

\displaystyle P_{n-1/2}^{-m}(z)=(n+1/2+m)P_{n+1/2}^{-m}(z)-(n+1/2)z_{0}P_{n-1/2}^{-m}(z),

\displaystyle Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z)=(n+1/2-m)Q_{n+1/2}^{m}(z)-(n+1/2)z_{0}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}^{m}\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the basis for the density functions.
The NP operator for tori has the expansion,

\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\varphi_{n}^{m}]=D_{nn}^{(m)}\varphi_{n}^{m}+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}R_{nl}^{(m)}\varphi_{l}^{m},

where
D^{(m)}=\left[D_{nl}^{(m)}\right]_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a diagonal matrix and
R^{(m)}=\left[R_{nl}^{(m)}\right]_{n,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
defined by

\displaystyle D_{nn}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2}\Bigg[(n-m+1/2)Q_{n+1/2}^{m}(z_{0})P_{n-1/2}^{-m}(z_{0})+(n+m+1/2)Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})P_{n+1/2}^{-m}(z_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-(2n+1)z_{0}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})P_{n-1/2}^{-m}(z_{0})\Bigg],

\displaystyle R_{nl}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}\sinh\tau_{0}}{2e^{|l-n|\tau_{0}}}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})P_{l-\frac{1}{2}}^{-m}(z_{0}).

Proof.

Let us define

\displaystyle D_{nn}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}\sinh^{2}\tau_{0}}{2}\frac{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}\,\Bigg[Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})+P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\Bigg].

Applying Lemma
3.3
to (
3
) generates the spectral decomposition of the NP operator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\varphi_{n}^{m}](\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=D_{nn}^{(m)}\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)+\frac{(-1)^{m}\sinh\tau_{0}}{2}\frac{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}\,P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\frac{\sinh\tau_{0}}{\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma}\,\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=D_{nn}^{(m)}\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)+\frac{(-1)^{m}\sinh\tau_{0}}{2}\frac{\Gamma(n-m+1/2)}{\Gamma(n+m+1/2)}\,P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-|k|\tau_{0}}e^{ik\sigma}\,\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=D_{nn}^{(m)}\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)+\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})}{2a}\,\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-|k|\tau_{0}}\,(\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{i(n+k)\sigma}

\displaystyle=D_{nn}^{(m)}\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)+\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})}{2a}\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-|l-n|\tau_{0}}\,(\cosh\tau_{0}-\cos\sigma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{im\phi}\,e^{il\sigma}

\displaystyle=D_{nn}^{(m)}\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)+\frac{P_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})\,Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})}{2a}\,\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-|l-n|\tau_{0}}\,\frac{\varphi_{l}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)}{\Gamma_{ml}}

\displaystyle=D_{nn}^{(m)}\varphi_{n}^{m}(\phi,\sigma)+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}R_{nl}^{(m)}\varphi_{l}^{m}(\phi,\sigma),

where
R_{nl}^{(m)}
is given by

\displaystyle R_{nl}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}\sinh\tau_{0}}{2e^{|l-n|\tau_{0}}}\frac{\Gamma(l-m+1/2)}{\Gamma(l+m+1/2)}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})P_{l-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}\sinh\tau_{0}}{2e^{|l-n|\tau_{0}}}Q_{n-1/2}^{m}(z_{0})P_{l-1/2}^{-m}(z_{0}).

Here, we used Lemma
3.4
and
z_{0}=\cosh\tau_{0}
.

We now apply Lemma
3.5
to remove derivatives in
D_{nn}^{(m)}

\displaystyle D_{nn}^{(m)}

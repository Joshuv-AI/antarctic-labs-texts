Chain-mediated effects of multiple factors in physical activity on self-rated health among sedentary college students
Authors: Xu Du, Yuqi Lin, Zixia Bu, Ying Lin, Yue Gao, Jizhao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSedentary behavior (SB) may lead not only to reduced athletic performance but also to impaired self-rated health (SRH) in college students. Although the beneficial effects of physical activity (PA) on SRH have been demonstrated, the intrinsic factors contributing to PA remain to be elucidated. This study examined physical activity atmosphere (PAA), physical activity capability (PAC), physical activity motivation (PAM), and physical activity behavior (PAB), and analyzed their effect sizes and pathways in relation to SRH among sedentary college students. A total of 966 university students (F = 387, M = 579; mean age = 20.36 ± 1.78 years) were selected from eight colleges and universities in Beijing and cross-sectionally assessed using the Self-Rated Health Measurement Scale (SRHMS), the Self-Description Questionnaire (SDQ), the Behavioral Regulation in Physical Education Questionnaire (BRPEQ), and the Physical Activity Atmosphere Questionnaire (PAAQ). Correlation, mediation, and effect size analyses were performed using Pearson correlation, Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), and Bootstrap methods. Correlation results showed significant positive correlations for all indicators of PAA, PAB, PAC, PAM, and SRH. The SEM results showed that the following pathways existed in this study: with PAC as the independent variable, PAC→PAB (Effect size = 0.584), PAC→SRH (Effect size = 0.142), PAC→PAM (Effect size = 0.628), PAC→PAB→SRH (Effect size = 0.237), PAC→PAM→PAB→SRH (Effect size = 0.184). When PAA is the independent variable, only one direction exists: PAA→PAM (Effect size = 0.497). With PAM as the independent variable, PAM→PAB (Effect size = 0.294). With PAB as the independent variable, PAB→SRH (Effect size = 0.375). Meanwhile, the model fit indices were: χ2/df = 4.218, p < 0.001, GFI = 0.908, NFI = 0.936, CFI = 0.912, IFI = 0.968, RFI = 0.936, TLI = 0.964, RMSEA = 0.911. (1) PAC has a positive and direct association with SRH in sedentary students; thus, creating a better exercise environment may help college students reduce sedentariness and improve SRH. (2) PAM and PAB exert positive mediating effects on SRH, suggesting that enhancing both will strengthen the mediating effect of PAC on SRH. (3) Promoting SRH among sedentary students requires addressing both motivational and environmental factors. Schools should provide optimal sporting environments to stimulate students’ motivation, behavior, and capability in PA, thereby reducing SB and improving mental health.Keywords: Physical activity atmosphere, Physical activity capability, Physical activity motivation, Physical activity behavior, Mental healthSubject terms: Health care, Health occupations, Physiology, Psychology, Psychology

Introduction

With the development of modern society and changes in science and technology, sedentary behavior (SB) has become an increasingly common habit in people’s daily lives. According to the World Health Organization’s (WHO) Global Status Report on Physical Activity 2022, about a quarter of the world’s adults are sedentary, representing about 28% of the world’s population1. SB has become a common habit, particularly on university campuses, due to academic and lifestyle pressures. Evidence suggests that chronic SB can lead to poor circulation, metabolic disorders, endocrine dysregulation, and musculoskeletal abnormalities, and increase the risk of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, cancer, and psychiatric disorders2,3. Prolonged SB among students has been associated with specific musculoskeletal consequences, such as the text neck syndrome, which results from sustained poor posture during smartphone and computer use and can lead to neck pain, spinal misalignment, and upper back discomfort4. As a result, SB has become a major predisposed factor for a wide range of health issues and has attracted considerable public health attention worldwide.

Of all the risk factors, mental health (MH) is one of the most important aspects affected by sedentary behavior (SB)5. According to China’s National Health Commission, the proportion of Chinese university students with MH problems is as high as 11%, which is higher than in other Asian and European countries. The detection rate has also increased slightly and is gradually stabilizing6. In this population, symptoms such as anxiety, depression, and stress are common, and if not addressed in a timely manner, they may progress to major depressive disorder (MDD) or bipolar affective disorder (BAD)7. Therefore, MH represents a critical public health concern among college students, warranting timely intervention and effective monitoring.

Self-rated health (SRH) serves as a comprehensive subjective indicator encompassing physiological, psychological, and social dimensions, and is widely applied in health epidemiological research. SRH is not only associated with short-term health status but is also considered a strong predictor of all-cause mortality and morbidity8. However, research on factors influencing SRH among university students has primarily focused on countries in Europe and the Americas9. In Asian countries, particularly in regions with relatively homogeneous education systems, students often face greater challenges in both physical and mental health. This has led to a higher probability of students in these areas exhibiting behaviors associated with personal psychological issues. For example, in China, influenced by multiple factors such as employment pressures and academic advancement demands, college students frequently experience declining physical fitness, impaired mental health, and low self-rated health scores7,10.

The WHO’s Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP) 2023 suggests that physical activity (PA) is an effective non-pharmacological intervention for improving both mental health and SRH11. Key factors influencing PA include physical activity atmosphere (PAA), physical activity capability (PAC), physical activity motivation (PAM), and physical activity behavior (PAB)12. PAA refers to the sport environment and accessible resources in an individual’s neighborhood. PAM represents the motivational drive that promotes participation, PAC constitutes the basis for engaging in PA, and PAB refers to purposeful, organized activity aimed at improving fitness and health. According to behavioral theory, students in the social adjustment stage develop psychological traits and behaviors in a peer-supportive environment13. Together, these four factors reflect the likelihood of engaging in PA from endogenous and exogenous perspectives, with higher levels of PAA, PAC, PAM, and PAB predicting a greater propensity to form habitual PA behaviors14.

In summary, the present study focuses on the improvement of PA on self-rated health (SRH) in sedentary college students, and based on previous studies, PAA, PAC, PAM, and PAB were included as mediator variables for the first time through a chain-mediated model. In addition, Capability, Opportunity, Motivation-Behavior (COM-B) theory was combined to explore the interactions and psychological mechanisms among the four variables. This study will provide a more scientific and precise exercise prescription and theoretical reference for improving MH and reducing SB in college students. Based on the findings of Wang and Chen15,16, the present study proposes the following hypotheses: (I) PAA, PAC, PAM, and PAB have a positive predictive effect on SRH. (II) PAM and PAB have a moderating role in the pathway of PAA and PAC on SRH.

Materials and methods

Participants

The present study employed a random cluster sampling method. Within each university, 1100 undergraduate students from Beijing were randomly selected from each college based on the participants’ academic discipline (i.e. their respective colleges). All subjects reported on the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ)17 that they were sedentary for more than 70% of their waking hours per day or engaged in moderate to vigorous exercise for less than 20 min per day18. IPAQ assigned metabolic equivalent (MET) corresponding to different intensities of PA by asking the study subjects about the frequency of the week (d/wk) and the time of day (min/d) for that intensity. The MET was 3.3 for walking, 4.0 for MPA, and 8.0 for VPA. The PA level for each intensity and the total PA level was calculated separately according to the formula. In this study, all participants strictly adhered to the criteria outlined in the WHO Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behavior. The subjects included were classified as sedentary for a minimum of 8 h, with an MVPA time of less than 20 min and a MET score of less than 3.31.

The inclusion criteria for the subjects were: (1) Currently enrolled undergraduate students with SB and only undergraduate students (full-time only). (2) Institutions include universities and colleges. (3) They were lucid and able to complete the questionnaire without difficulty and had no intellectual disability or history of mental illness. (4) Subjects participated in the survey voluntarily and did not complete the questionnaire in a negative way. (5) Informed consent for the survey was given by the counsellor (class teacher), who signed the consent form and then filled in the questionnaire. Finally, after all the collection work, we obtained 966 scales with valid results, and the validity rate of the questionnaires was 87.45%. Procedures

In this study, the subjects PAA, PAB, PAM and SRH parameters were measured using scales based on various indicators. During the testing process, all indicators were distributed through SoJum Software (Tencent Holdings Ltd.). Prior to the data collection process, the purpose and methodology of the survey were explained to the respondents in person, and any questions were answered in person, with the consent of the counsellor and the consent of the respondents. The scale was completed anonymously, without personal data such as ID number or student number, excluding invalid answers, short time (less than 200 s), 10 consecutive questions with the same option and questionnaires with high homogeneity. All participants volunteered to take part in the experiment and provided electronic informed consent online, and a paper copy of the informed consent form was signed by the participant’s representative. Participants’ identities and personal information will be protected by staff and will not be displayed in the study. The procedures were conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guidelines and were approved by Sports Science Experiment Ethics Committee of Beijing Sport University (License number: 2022092H).

Measurements

Self-rated health evaluation

This study used the Self-Rated Health Measurement Scale (SRHMS), Version 1.0, developed by Mengdi based on Suchman’s modifications, to evaluate individuals’ health status19,20. The scale reflects health status across three dimensions: physical health status, mental health status, and social function. Comprising 10 dimensions and 48 items, it covers physical symptoms and organ function, activities of daily living, physical activity function, positive emotions, psychological symptoms and negative emotions, cognitive function, role activities and social adaptation, social resources and social contact, social support, and overall health self-assessment. Each item is scored on a 0–10 scale, with higher scores indicating a better health status. In this study, the SRHMS demonstrated an internal consistency Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.941. Additionally, the Cronbach’s α coefficients for the three subscales (physical health, mental health, and social function) were 0.877, 0.885, and 0.875, respectively.

Physical activity capability analysis

Self-assessment of physical activity motivation was measured using the Self-Description Questionnaire (SDQ) developed by Marsh21. It has good construct validity and is a valid instrument for research into physical self-concept. Subsequent research by Esnaola and Berki confirmed that the scale possesses good reliability22,23. The SDQ consists of three sub-dimensions, physical ability, physical appearance and general self-concept, which indirectly reflect an individual’s ability to engage in physical activity. These factors influence an individual’s willingness and motivation to engage in physical activity. In this study, the Cronbach’s α = 0.847.

Physical activity motivation and behavior test

Nathalie & Maarten’s Behavioral Regulation in Physical Education Questionnaire (BRPEQ) was used in this study to assess individuals’ motivation for physical activity24. The BRPEQ consists of two parts, motivation and behavior, and is divided into three sub-dimensions. The BRPEQ consists of three motivation dimensions: autonomous motivation, controlled motivation and unmotivated motivation, and consists of 42 questions that explore in depth students’ autonomously occurring PA. It is mainly used to measure students’ personal motivational tendencies and existing behaviors with regard to their participation in PA. In this study, the Cronbach’s α = 0.769.

Physical activity atmosphere assessment

The PAA scale used in this study was Liu’s Physical Activity Atmosphere Questionnaire (PAAQ)25. Subsequent validation studies conducted by Tao, He, and others evaluated the instrument’s applicability to adolescents and older adults, respectively, demonstrating good validity and reliability26,27. This scale has been used in previous studies to assess the sports environment in which individuals live and the sports information available in the neighborhood of sports participants, and it has a certain motivational and orientational effect on physical exercise. A positive training environment can lead to the formation of regular fitness habits and the active integration of people in the neighborhood into the training circle. The scale consists of five dimensions: interpersonal association, natural association, information access, interpersonal barriers and conditional barriers, with a total of 17 items. In this study, the Cronbach’s α = 0.812.

Statistical analysis

In the present study, descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and mediation analyses were conducted to examine the relationships among the study variables. First, SPSS 21.0 was used to test the normality of PAA, PAB, PAC, PAM, and SRH. Descriptive statistics were then performed based on the distributional characteristics of the data. Pearson correlation analysis was applied to examine the associations among variables, with correlation coefficients (r) and corresponding P values reported. Variables that demonstrated significant correlations were subsequently entered into structural equation modeling (SEM) using AMOS 24.0 to test the hypothesized mediation pathways and to evaluate the overall model fit. SEM was employed to estimate the direct and indirect relationships among latent variables and to examine the structural pathways within the proposed conceptual framework.

To ensure the robustness of the SEM results, several sensitivity and robust analyses were conducted. First, alternative model specifications were estimated by comparing the hypothesized chained mediation model with reduced models in which selected structural paths were removed (e.g., excluding the direct path from PAC to SRH or the sequential mediation path involving PAM and PAB) to examine whether the overall model fit and key path coefficients remained stable. Second, demographic covariates including gender, grade, and major were alternatively included and excluded from the SEM estimation to assess whether the structural relationships among the core variables changed after adjusting for these potential confounders. Third, the SEM parameters were re-estimated using bootstrap resampling procedures to examine the stability of standardized path coefficients and indirect effects. Across these alternative specifications, the direction and statistical significance of the main structural paths remained consistent, indicating that the primary findings of the SEM model were stable.

To further quantify the chained mediation effects and obtain robust confidence interval estimates for indirect effects, the PROCESS macro (Model 6) for SPSS was additionally applied. This procedure complemented the SEM analysis by estimating specific indirect paths and generating bias-corrected bootstrap confidence intervals. Bootstrap resampling (5000 samples) was used to test the significance of all indirect effects. Finally, Bonferroni correction was applied for post hoc comparisons where appropriate, with the significance level set at P < 0.05.

Results

Validation factor analysis results

The scale designed for this study had 64 items, of which 59 were used in the impact analysis study. In the specific research process, the 59 observation indicators after data normalization were arithmetically averaged according to the specific classification of the observed variables, i.e., the subordinate measurement indicators of each observed were averaged and synthesized into a single data indicator, which can not only ensure the reasonableness of the data but also reduce the complexity of data processing. After arithmetic averaging, the overall indicator system involved in this study contains only 11 dimensions. Subsequently, this study used AMOS_24.0 software to conduct a preliminary validation of the fit between the theoretical construction model of SRH for SB college students and the data of the survey study. The results show that the path coefficients between each variable and each factor are all greater than 0.4, which meets the requirements for the establishment of the social SEM model.

Reliability and validity test results

In terms of reliability and validity, Cronbach’s α was employed to assess internal consistency. The results showed that the Cronbach’s α coefficients for all five latent constructs and their corresponding observed variables exceeded the recommended threshold of 0.70, and the overall scale reliability reached 0.945, indicating excellent internal consistency.

Although Cronbach’s α values exceeding 0.90 may sometimes suggest potential redundancy among items, this study posits that the high reliability likely stems from the use of indicators highly correlated with PA, including PAA, PAC, PAM, and PAB. This finding has been corroborated by previous studies28–30. Within the unified behavioral framework of this study, these indicators may be closely associated with the specific dimensions of PA being measured. Consequently, the elevated alpha value is probably indicative of substantial internal consistency within the scale, as opposed to item redundancy. While very high reliability coefficients may suggest potential item redundancy, prior methodological research has noted that such results are not uncommon in scales measuring relatively narrow or homogeneous psychological and behavioral constructs, where items are designed to capture highly overlapping facets of the same latent concept31,32.

Regarding construct validity, all items were adapted from previously validated instruments, ensuring adequate content validity. Confirmatory factor analysis showed that standardized factor loadings for all observed variables were above 0.50, with several loadings exceeding 0.95. Although high factor loadings may raise concerns about item redundancy or overfitting, existing SEM literature suggests that high loadings primarily reflect strong shared variance among indicators within the same construct, rather than statistical misspecification, particularly when composite reliability (CR) and average variance extracted (AVE) fall within acceptable ranges33. In the present study, CR values exceeded 0.70 and AVE values were above 0.50 for all constructs, supporting satisfactory convergent validity.

With respect to potential multicollinearity, all items were theoretically assigned to distinct latent dimensions based on prior research, and the overall model demonstrated acceptable fit indices, suggesting that the dimensional structure remained stable and interpretable. Taken together, these findings indicate that the high factor loadings observed in this study are more likely attributable to the conceptual coherence of the constructs rather than problematic redundancy or dimensional misspecification. Common method deviation test results

In this study, participants received rigorous operational training prior to formal distribution of the questionnaire to university students to reduce the effects of common methodological biases through a combination of standardized instructions, strict control of time to complete the questionnaire, mixed distribution of positive and negative questions, and control of demographic variables34. Meanwhile, all items of PAA, PAB, PAC, PAM, and SRH were included in the exploratory factor analysis using Harman’s one-way test, which showed that a total of 16 factors had eigenvalues greater than 1, and the first factor existed at 27.34% of variation, which was much lower than the reference standard of 40%. Therefore, the present results proved to be valid.

Correlation test results between variables

Correlation results showed that PAA, PAB, PAC, PAM, and SRH were significantly positively correlated (r = [0.512, 0.848], P < 0.05) after controlling for gender, grade, and major, as determined by the Pearson correlation test. Among them, college students’ PAA was significantly positively correlated with PAB, PAC, PAM, and SRH (r = [0.512, 0.848], P < 0.05). PAB was significantly positively correlated with PAC, PAM, and SRH (r = [0.683, 0.824], P < 0.05). PAC was significantly positively correlated with PAM and SRH (r = [0.616, 0.854], P < 0.05).

Based on these results, all variables and sub-indicators demonstrated significant correlations. Although some correlations were between 0.3 and 0.6, these values are sufficient for SEM construction, as the basic requirement is that indicators exhibit at least moderate correlations (r > 0.3) to allow stable estimation of the model32,33. Therefore, further mediation effect tests using SEM were deemed appropriate. These findings indicate that PAA, PAB, PAC, and PAM, as the main factors influencing individuals’ engagement in physical activity, all contribute to the expression of SRH. Accordingly, higher levels of these four indicators in sedentary students are associated with better SRH status. See Fig. 1; Table 3 for details.

Path model fitting results for each variable

This study used AMOS 24.0 for structural equation modelling to test the mediating effects of PAM and PAB between PAC, PAA, and SRH in SB students. After specifying the research pathways, AMOS 26.0 was used to further validate the mediation model. The bootstrap method with 5000 resamples was applied to estimate the indirect effects and examine the stability of the mediation results.

Model fit was evaluated using multiple commonly reported fit indices. The chi-square to degrees of freedom ratio was χ²/df = 4.218 (p < 0.001). Although this value exceeds the commonly recommended threshold of 3, it is often considered acceptable in structural equation modelling studies with large sample sizes. The goodness-of-fit index (GFI = 0.908), normed fit index (NFI = 0.923), comparative fit index (CFI = 0.912), incremental fit index (IFI = 0.968), relative fit index (RFI = 0.936), and Tucker–Lewis index (TLI = 0.964) all exceed the commonly referenced cutoff value of 0.90 used to indicate adequate model fit. The root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA = 0.911) is also reported as an index reflecting the discrepancy between the hypothesized model and the observed data.

Discussion

SB has become increasingly prevalent, particularly among college students facing academic pressure and sedentary lifestyles. Prolonged SB has been associated with poorer MH, often reflected in lower SRH, which may contribute to an elevated risk of psychological problems if persistent. PA has been widely reported as a non-pharmacological strategy associated with improvements in MH and SRH, and several determinants—including PAA, PAC, PAM, and PAB—have been identified as important correlates of PA engagement and related health outcomes. Based on the COM-B framework, this study constructed a chain mediation model to examine the interrelationships among PAA, PAC, PAM, PAB, and SRH among sedentary college students. The results revealed relatively substantial correlations and path coefficients within the proposed framework; however, these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Given the cross-sectional design and reliance on self-reported measures, the model reflects statistical associations and modeled pathways rather than empirically verified causal mechanisms. In addition, shared method variance and potential conceptual overlap among constructs may have influenced the magnitude of the observed relationships. Nevertheless, within these methodological boundaries, the proposed model provides a theoretically informed account of how multiple PA-related psychological and behavioral factors may operate together in relation to SRH, offering preliminary evidence that may inform future longitudinal or intervention-based research on SB reduction and MH promotion in college populations.

The first finding of this study is that while PAA did not exert a significant effect on SRH, PAB and PAC exerted significant direct positive predictive effects on SRH among SB students. Meanwhile, among all the factors of PA, PAC had the relatively largest effect on the SRH of individuals with the most significant overall effect size. In addition, based on the results of the effects of the observed variables, this study found that among the factors of PAC, the three factors of physical ability, physical appearance, and general self-concept were the most significant. Taking the current educational environment in China as an example, college students typically receive approximately 12 years of formal physical education during the nine-year compulsory education stage and the three-year senior high school stage, as stipulated in the national curriculum standards issued by the Ministry of Education35. However, many students still fail to achieve proficiency in more than one sport. This phenomenon is also consistent with the SRH challenges observed among Chinese college students. Therefore, hypothesis (I) can be accepted. The above results are also more similar to previous studies. Stodden’s study also showed that there is a correlation between PAC and health, and that it generally has a positive effect, and these judgements and assertions have been better verified in the youth population and in the practical teaching of physical education36. Maria, on the other hand, analyzed the relationship between PAC and MH in adolescents through a systematic evaluation and found that it not only promotes the individual’s level of physical, skeletal and cardiorespiratory fitness, but also improves poor mood and psychological resilience, thus promoting the healthy development of SRH and MH37. Alan also found strong associations between college students’ PAC and daily PA levels, physical fitness and psychosocial characteristics through cross-sectional comparisons of large samples. In particular, the persistence and scientific nature of PA had the most significant impact on health, and a general conclusion was that the more scientific and planned the physical activity, the more significant the positive health effects38. This suggests that while PAC and PAB have an effect on SRH, they also have an effect on each other, a part of this study that will be explored later. Unlike previous studies, this is the first time that the SB population has been studied and the effects of its four variables on SRH have been investigated. Therefore, based on the findings, this study concludes that while PAA may exert some influence on an individual’s physical activity and SRH, it does not appear to be a significant predictor. An individual’s SRH may be more dependent on subjective behaviors. Consequently, it is recommended that SB students, lacking a foundation in physical activity, can cultivate interest by selecting sports with lower difficulty levels and greater personal appeal. This approach can enhance their PAC, PAM, and PAB, thereby ensuring a healthy level of SRH.

This effect can also be explained by psychological mechanisms. Compensation theory and self-determination theory provide a good explanation of the causes of SB behavior in university students. Among them, the compensation theory suggests that if an individual has a low level of self-acceptance for a long period of time, he/she will compensate for it with another kind of behavior and strategy. At the same time, this behavior may be extreme39. Self-determination theory suggests that an individual’s basic motivation stems from psychological needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness. From the perspective of Chinese college students, due to the overemphasis and long-term control of academic stress in their pre-college environment, students may compensate by counteracting expressions of stress or anxiety through behaviors such as SB or Internet addiction40. However, when they enter university, freed from their previous confined environment and no longer under the scrutiny of their families or classes, they may compensate for their acute psychological needs by engaging in other behaviors. SB, as a low cost, low energy bad behavior, has rightly become the everyday status of this group. As a non-pharmacological intervention, PA has been consistently shown to improve IA and a range of problem behaviors for the following reasons: First, Erich Fromm, a renowned psychologist, regarded PA as ‘an anesthetic for the maintenance of social deficits’41. At the same time, social control theory suggests that by imposing the necessary constraints on the animal nature of human beings through some form of force, it is possible to limit the occurrence of various kinds of undesirable and destructive behavior42. PA, as an activity performed according to certain rules, can effectively use up an individual’s energy and at the same time trigger positive psychological feedback. Therefore, PA is often considered by the WHO or AHA as the best way to improve mental health. The reason for this can be explained by the Substitute Reinforcement Theory. It suggests that if a behavior is not sufficiently rewarded, individuals will seek an alternative behavior to obtain a similar reward. SB usually provides immediate positive reinforcement from a physiological point of view, but at the cost of reduced SRH and MH. On the other hand, PA triggers intracranial transmitter activity that itself has positive reinforcing properties. On the other hand, according to Shavelson’s multilevel-multidimensional structural model of self-esteem, smaller physical changes can also trigger an increase in MH43. And because long-term PA itself improves posture, it has a positive feedback effect on SRH44.

The second finding of this study is that the mediating effect plays an important role in all pathway models, and the chain mediating effect of PAM and PAB is very significant. Therefore, the ‘PAC→PAM→PAB→SRH’ pathway proposed in this study is valid, indicating that the cultivation of PAC is transformed into the improvement of physical and mental health through PAM and PAB. Therefore, hypothesis (II) can be accepted. This is also similar to the results of previous studies. The empirical findings suggest that the production of PAM and the realization of PAB become an important bridge between PAC and SRH. Goal attainment theory suggests that there is a close relationship between students’ perceptions of their goal of attaining proficiency in sport skills and the time, frequency, manner and method of practicing the relevant sport skills, and the attainment of motivational efficacy, which suggests that PAC is significantly and positively correlated with PAM, and accordingly, an increase in PAC will increase self-efficacy, which in turn will increase PAM45. Howlett’s research suggests that PAC can have both a direct effect on physical and mental health, and an effect on physical and mental health through its effect on PAM46. Mayne believes that motivation is the driving force behind behavior change and that competence is the direct cause of behavior change47. The research results show that there is a significant correlation between the factors of ability, opportunity and motivation in the COM-B theory, which also provides theoretical support for the cultivation of students’ PAB, and it is necessary to work together and promote these three aspects as a whole in order to achieve substantial and long-lasting effects on SRH48. Based on these findings, the present study suggests that the four factors PAA, PAM, PAB and PAC together determine an individual’s level of PA. On the one hand, although PAA positively influences SRH in college students, there is variability in each individual’s environment and mastery of the sport, which in turn translates into different levels of behavior. On the other hand, studies based on the socio-PA environment have shown that family, school and social factors have a significant impact on college students’ PA, and that if the external environment can provide more PA resources, it can help them to participate more in sport and thus improve their SRH levels49. The exercise habits of SB students are inherently weaker than those of the normal group. Therefore, more appropriate PA is needed to motivate them to engage in PA and develop exercise habits. In addition, a large body of evidence also suggests that in order to increase university students’ participation in PA, it is necessary to strengthen cooperation and collaboration between different departments and social organizations, and to create the necessary PA to stimulate individuals’ PAM and PAB, and ultimately improve their SRH levels50.

In summary, previous research has frequently reported associations between PA participation and improved SRH. Consistent with this literature, the present findings suggest that PAA, PAB, PAC, and PAM are statistically linked within a pathway model that relates to individuals’ propensity to engage in PA and their self-rated health status. However, given the cross-sectional and self-report nature of the data, these results should be interpreted as modeled associations rather than confirmed causal chains. From a practical perspective, the findings may provide tentative insights for the design of PA-related health promotion strategies, indicating that PA modalities capable of enhancing both affective experience and motivational engagement could be particularly relevant. For instance, structured yet enjoyable modalities such as small-sided games combined with verbal encouragement have been reported to improve mood state, satisfaction, and perceived effort while simultaneously enhancing aerobic performance, thereby supporting components of SRH51. At the same time, it should be acknowledged that the health-related effects of PA may vary depending on individual engagement patterns and exercise regulation. Excessive or poorly regulated exercise may lead to fatigue-related outcomes, including lactic acid accumulation and increased intracranial free radical production52, which may in turn reduce PAM and potentially influence subsequent PAB and PAC. In more severe cases, maladaptive loading may increase the risk of sports injuries or rhabdomyolysis53.

Accordingly, drawing on principles of appropriate load regulation and motivational processes in sport54,55, the present findings may tentatively suggest that college students could benefit from monitoring their exercise state before and during PA using tools such as the rating of perceived exertion (RPE) or the feeling scale (FS)56,57. Such self-monitoring approaches may help individuals regulate exercise load across different PA modalities, potentially supporting SRH-related benefits while reducing the likelihood of adverse outcomes.

Limitations and suggestions for future research

In this study, we explored effective strategies to enhance college students’ participation and interest in sports and physical education. To this end, we analyzed the effect sizes of PAA, PAB, PAC, and PAM in improving the SRH of SB college students. To this end, a cross-sectional large-sample survey was conducted, encompassing 966 college students in Beijing. The study utilized PAM and PAB as mediating variables, with PAC and PAA serving as the primary variables. However, it should be noted that study is not without its limitations.

Firstly, this study examines differences across populations using five indicators, which inherently limits the ability to infer causal relationships or conduct longitudinal follow-up. Although large-scale studies based on databases such as NHANES, SEER, or CHARLS have employed standardized approaches (e.g., weighted mean difference) to enable longitudinal or sequential comparisons, such strategies rely on secondary data and may compromise the originality of primary research. In the present study, we deliberately employed an original cross-sectional dataset collected within approximately one year to preserve the novelty of the research question. Nevertheless, it must be acknowledged that the lack of longitudinal tracking reduces the persuasiveness of causal interpretations.

Secondly, several important confounding variables known to influence SRH and performance in university students were not explicitly controlled for in this study, particularly sleep-related factors. Previous research has demonstrated that sleep duration and sleep hygiene play a critical role in both physical and cognitive performance, as well as subjective health outcomes in physically active university students. The omission of sleep variables may therefore have introduced residual confounding into the observed associations between PA-related indicators and SRH. This limitation should be considered when interpreting the findings, and future studies should incorporate validated sleep assessments to provide a more comprehensive understanding of student health.

Thirdly, although this study aimed to inform exercise recommendations for reducing sedentary behavior and improving SRH among college students, the assessment of PA-related risks was limited to instruments such as the BRPEQ and SDQ. Detailed individual exercise characteristics (e.g., modality, frequency, and context of activities such as walking, cycling, or swimming) were not captured58. As a result, the findings cannot be considered a precision exercise prescription in a strict sense. This limitation is largely attributable to the challenges of monitoring individualized exercise behaviors in large-scale samples and underscores the need for future intervention-based or longitudinal studies.

Finally, within the constraints of the study design, statistical strategies such as Bootstrap resampling and Bonferroni correction were employed to mitigate heterogeneity and enhance the robustness of the results. Future research will further refine the analytical framework by incorporating longitudinal outcomes, PA volume, gender differences, and additional lifestyle-related confounders, including sleep.

Conclusion

(1) PAC has a positive and direct effect on SRH in SB students, so creating a better exercise environment for college students will help them to reduce sedentariness and increase their SRH. (2) There is a positive mediating effect of PAM and PAB on SRH in SB students, so increasing the levels of both will increase the mediating effect of PAC on SRH. (3) Promoting SRH among SB students requires both motivational and exogenous perspectives. As a school, students should be provided with the best possible sporting environment to stimulate their motivation, behavior and capability in PA, thereby reducing SB and improving mental health.

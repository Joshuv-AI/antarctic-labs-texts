Distance Spectrum of IEEE 802.11 Binary Convolutional Codes
Rethna Pulikkoonattu \nolinkurl rethna@broadcom.com

Abstract
Binary convolutional coding (BCC) has been a cornerstone of the IEEE 802.11 wireless LAN standard since its inception, and it remains relevant today across the full generational arc from the legacy 802.11a/g through Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) and into the forthcoming Wi-Fi 8 (802.11bn) [ 11 , 10 ] . Although low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes now dominate high-throughput applications, BCC is mandatory for backward compatibility and continues to serve as the default forward-error-correction scheme in bandwidth-constrained and cost-sensitive deployments: 20 MHz-only devices, Internet-of-Things nodes, and other implementations where LDPC’s decoder complexity is prohibitive. Critically, BCC at rate 1 / 2 \nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} is the coding scheme used throughout the packet preamble in every IEEE 802.11-compliant frame, making it indispensable regardless of which data-field code is selected. Furthermore, the new Enhanced Long Range (ELR) packet format introduced in the 802.11bn/UHR amendment mandates rate- 1 / 2 \nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} BCC for the data portion of the frame, reinforcing the continued importance of this code in next-generation deployments [ 10 ] . The performance of BCC under Viterbi decoding is governed by the distance spectrum { ( α d , β d ) } d ≥ d free \{(\alpha_{d},\beta_{d})\}_{d\geq d_{\mathrm{free}}} of the convolutional code [ 12 , 17 ] . This note explains how to compute that spectrum exactly for the IEEE 802.11 mother code (rate 1 / 2 \nicefrac{{1}}{{2}} , K = 7 K=7 , generators 133 8 133_{8} / 171 8 171_{8} ) and its three standard punctured derivatives (rates 2 / 3 \nicefrac{{2}}{{3}} , 3 / 4 \nicefrac{{3}}{{4}} , 5 / 6 \nicefrac{{5}}{{6}} ) obtained via rate-compatible puncturing [ 2 , 8 ] . Union-bound BEP and FER curves are derived for AWGN with BPSK/QPSK and Gray-coded M M -QAM modulation and validated against Monte Carlo simulation. Python, Julia, and C++ implementations are openly available at [ 7 ] .



1
Introduction

Despite the ubiquity of BCC in deployed IEEE 802.11 systems, a recurring
obstacle when analysing or benchmarking its performance is the absence of
a self-contained, publicly accessible resource that derives the distance
spectrum from first principles and connects it cleanly to BER and FER
bounds. Existing textbook treatments
[
12
,
14
,
16
]

cover the theory at varying levels of generality but do not provide
spectrum tables, working code, or simulation-validated bound curves
specific to the 802.11 puncture schedules. Standards documents
[
11
]

state the generator polynomials and puncture matrices but say nothing
about performance analysis. This leaves practitioners either re-deriving
the computation from scratch or relying on unverified figures scattered
across the literature.

This report attempts to fill that gap. The distance spectrum for each of
the four standard rates is tabulated exactly; union-bound BEP and FER
curves are derived and cross-validated against Monte Carlo simulation for
both QPSK and 256-QAM; and the complete computational procedure is
documented in pseudocode and three reference implementations (Python,
Julia, C++). We hope the document serves as a useful self-contained
reference for researchers and engineers working with 802.11 physical
layers.

Beyond the 802.11 use case, the method is entirely general: the
augmented-trellis first-return expansion applies to any rate-compatible
punctured convolutional code, regardless of standard or generator
polynomial. Contributions that broaden the supported code families or improve
the bound computations are welcome. The Python, Julia, and C++
source code is freely available at

\url
https://github.com/geekymode/bcc_spectrum
[
7
]
.

Higher code rates are obtained by

4
Distance Spectrum: Definitions

Definition 1

(Distance spectrum
[
12
,
15
,
16
]
)
.

The
distance spectrum
of a convolutional code is the pair of
integer sequences
\{(\alpha_{d},\beta_{d})\}_{d\geq d_{\mathrm{free}}}
where

\alpha_{d}
counts error events at Hamming distance
d
from the all-zero
codeword, and
\beta_{d}
is the total input Hamming weight of those paths.
The smallest
d
for which
\alpha_{d}>0
is the
free distance

d_{\mathrm{free}}

[
17
,
5
]
, the primary figure of merit
for a convolutional code’s error-correcting capability.

5
BER Bounds from the Distance Spectrum

5.1
Union bound on bit-error probability

For the Viterbi decoder
[
17
,
6
]
over an AWGN channel
with BPSK modulation, the pairwise error probability between two codewords
at Hamming distance
d
is
[
14
,
15
]

P_{2}(d)=Q\!\left(\sqrt{2d\,R_{c}\,\frac{E_{b}}{N_{0}}}\right),

where
R_{c}
is the code rate,
E_{b}/N_{0}
is the information-bit SNR, and

Q(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}t
.
Applying the union bound over all error events
[
17
,
12
]

gives the
union-bound bit-error probability

P_{b}\;\lesssim\;\sum_{d=d_{\mathrm{free}}}^{D_{\max}}\beta_{d}\;Q\!\left(\sqrt{2d\,R_{c}\,\frac{E_{b}}{N_{0}}}\right),

(3)

where
k=1
information bit per step is used (absorbed into
\beta_{d}
).

5.2
Frame error rate

The
frame/event error rate
uses the multiplicity directly:

P_{f}\;\lesssim\;\sum_{d=d_{\mathrm{free}}}^{D_{\max}}\alpha_{d}\;Q\!\left(\sqrt{2d\,R_{c}\,\frac{E_{b}}{N_{0}}}\right).

(4)

5.3
Single-term approximation

Near the waterfall region the leading term dominates:

P_{b}\;\approx\;\beta_{d_{\mathrm{free}}}\;Q\!\left(\sqrt{2\,d_{\mathrm{free}}\,R_{c}\,\frac{E_{b}}{N_{0}}}\right).

(5)

7
Augmented Trellis and Transfer Function

7.1
Why augment the state

For the unpunctured code the transfer function method
[
12
,
16
]
uses a single

64\times 64
state space. Puncturing makes the branch weight

phase-dependent
: consecutive encoder steps may contribute
differently depending on which bits survive the mask. Augmenting the
state with the puncture phase converts this into a time-invariant problem
[
8
]
:

Definition 2

(Augmented state)
.

(\sigma,\phi)
where
\sigma\in\{0,\ldots,63\}
is the encoder state
and
\phi\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}
is the position in the puncture mask.

For rate-
\frac{3}{4}
(
L=6
) the augmented space has
64\times 6=384
states.

7.2
Branch weights

Each branch carries weight
\mathsf{D}^{d}\mathsf{N}^{u}
where

d=p_{\phi}v^{(1)}+p_{(\phi+1)\bmod L}v^{(2)}

counts transmitted 1-bits, and the phase advances by 2 per step.

7.3
State partitioning

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{(\sigma,\phi)\neq(0,0)\}
. Branches split into:

\mathbf{E}
:
start
\to\mathcal{S}
;
\mathbf{Q}
:
\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
;
\mathbf{R}
:

8.1

8.2
Neumann series (truncated)

The Neumann (geometric) series identity
[

,
15

\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q})^{-1}\mathbf{R}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{Q}^{k}\mathbf{R}

avoids explicit matrix inversion.
Each
\mathbf{Q}

so the sum terminates within
d_{\max}
steps.

Dict[int
\to

\alpha

Iteration halts when all polynomial terms exceed

Appendix A
Method: Augmented-Trellis First-Return Series

The full descriptive title of the technique used throughout this report is
the
augmented-trellis first-return series expansion
.
“Augmented trellis”: the encoder state is extended by a puncture-phase
coordinate
[
8
,
18
]
, converting a phase-dependent problem
into a time-invariant graph.
“First-return”: only paths from
start
back to
start

(without revisiting it) are enumerated—exactly the set of error events
entering the union bound of Viterbi
[
17
]
.
“Series expansion”: the Neumann series
(I-Q)^{-1}=\sum_{k}Q^{k}

avoids explicit matrix inversion
[
12
,
1
]
.

This is closely related to the
transfer function
or
state
diagram
method described in Lin & Costello
[
12
]
, Viterbi &
Omura
[
16
]
, and Blahut
[
1
]
; and to the

generating function
approach in Ryan & Lin
[
15
]
.
The phase augmentation for punctured codes is due to Hagenauer
[
8
]

and Yasuda et al.
[
18
]
; the free-distance optimality of the

133_{8}/171_{8}
generators was established by
Odenwalder
[
13
]
and documented for Viterbi-decoded
satellite systems by Heller & Jacobs
[
9
]
.

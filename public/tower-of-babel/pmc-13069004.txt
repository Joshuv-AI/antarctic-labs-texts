Discussion

Patient presented with unilateral itchy violaceous flat topped papules in a dermatomal pattern and dermoscopic examination revealed violaceous areas along with the characteristic Wickham's striae, giving a diagnostic clue toward Zosteriform Lichen Planus. Histopathology was not performed in our patient as clinic‐dermoscopic features were classic and consistent with Zosteriform Lichen Planus, and the patient declined invasive procedures. The patient was started on topical flucinolone and oral fexofenadine with improvement after 3 weeks duration.

Zosteriform Lichen Planus (LP) is a rare variant of Lichen Planus, characterized by skin lesions arranged in a dermatomal pattern [1]. Zosteriform LP may occur through koebnerization, Wolf's isotropic response at sites of previous herpes zoster, or may occur de novo on uninvolved skin and recent observations suggest that zosteriform distribution of lesions follows Blaschko's lines rather than dermatomal or neural pathways [2]. Zosteriform LP can mimic various other dermatoses and has to be differentiated from linear psoriasis, lichen striatus, linear nevi, and lichen amyloidosis for appropriate management. The diagnosis of Zosteriform Lichen Planus can be confirmed by clinical, histopathological, and dermoscopic correlation. Histopathological features and treatment of zosteriform variant of lichen planus are similar to that of the classical Lichen planus [3]. The preferred treatment modalities include potent topical steroids and oral antihistamines. Additionally, topical calcineurin inhibitors and salicylic acid can be used too. Systemic or intralesional corticosteroids are reserved for use in non‐responding and extensive cases [1].

On line-parallelisms of PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q)
Francesco Pavese, Paolo Santonastaso

Abstract
Let PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q) denote the three-dimensional projective space over the finite field with q q elements. A line-spread of PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q) is a collection 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of mutually skew lines such that every point of PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q) lies on exactly one line of 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . A parallelism of PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q) is a set Π \Pi of mutually skew line-spreads of PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q) such that every line of PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q) is contained in precisely one line-spread of Π \Pi . For a Desarguesian spread 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and an elementary abelian group E E of order q 2 q^{2} that stabilizes 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and one of its lines, let 𝒯 \mathcal{T} be the class of parallelisms of PG ⁡ ( 3 , q ) {\rm PG}(3,q) admitting E E , and comprising 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and q 2 + q q^{2}+q Hall spreads, each of which is obtained by switching one of the q 2 + q q^{2}+q reguli of 𝒟 \mathcal{D} through its E E -fixed line. In this paper, the parallelisms in 𝒯 \mathcal{T} are characterized geometrically and enumerated. Moreover, it is shown that 𝒯 \mathcal{T} contains at least Θ ⁡ ( q q − 1 ​ q ! ) \Theta(q^{q-1}q!) mutually inequivalent parallelisms for q q even, and at least Θ ⁡ ( q 2 ​ q − 3 ) \Theta(q^{2q-3}) mutually inequivalent parallelisms when q q is odd. Keywords: line-spread; line-parallelism; packing; Desarguesian spread; Hall spread.

Introduction

Let
{\rm PG}(3,q)
denote the three-dimensional projective space over the finite field with
q
elements. A
line-spread
of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
is a collection
\mathcal{S}
of mutually skew lines such that every point of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
lies on exactly one line of
\mathcal{S}
. A
parallelism
of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
is a set
\Pi
of mutually skew line-spreads of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
such that every line of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
is contained in precisely one line-spread of
\Pi
. A
regulus
of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
is the set of transversals to three pairwise skew lines, and consists of
q+1
pairwise skew lines. A spread
\mathcal{S}
is
regular
or
Desarguesian
if each line not in
\mathcal{S}
meets
\mathcal{S}
in the lines of a regulus. A
Hall spread
is obtained from a Desarguesian spread by switching a regulus with its opposite regulus. The first examples of parallelisms of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
were obtained by Denniston
[
6
]
and Beutelspacher
[
2
]
. Infinite families of parallelisms of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
consisting of regular spreads were constructed by Penttila and Williams in
[
16
]
in the case when
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. Many authors have investigated and exhibited construction techniques for parallelisms of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
, see e.g.
[
12
,
13
,
7
]
. For further results, the reader is referred to
[
14
]
and references therein. Some sporadic examples obtained with the aid of a computer can be found in
[
17
,
1
,
19
]
. Most of the known infinite families of parallelisms have one Desarguesian spread
\mathcal{D}
and admit an elementary abelian group
E
of order
q^{2}
that stabilizes
\mathcal{D}
and one of its lines. In the case when
q
is even, these parallelisms comprise the Desarguesian spread
\mathcal{D}
and
q^{2}+q
Hall spreads, each of which is obtained by switching one of the
q^{2}+q
reguli of
\mathcal{D}
through its
E
-fixed line. Other examples are obtained from parallelisms of this type by means of a derivation process
[
14
, Theorem 175]
.

1.1
Our Contribution

For a Desarguesian spread
\mathcal{D}
of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
, let
E
be an elementary abelian group of order
q^{2}
that stabilizes
\mathcal{D}
and one of its lines. Consider the class
\mathcal{T}
of parallelisms of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
admitting
E
as an automorphism group, and comprising
\mathcal{D}
and
q^{2}+q
Hall spreads, each of which is obtained by switching one of the
q^{2}+q
reguli of
\mathcal{D}
through its
E
-fixed line. In this paper, we present a geometric method that allows us to characterize and enumerate all the parallelisms in
\mathcal{T}
. Precisely, we prove that there is a correspondence between certain sets of
q+1
Bear pencils within the larger space
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
, referred to as
good sets
, and parallelisms in
\mathcal{T}
: we establish that every good set determines a parallelism, and conversely, each parallelism can be described explicitly by a corresponding good set.
Finally, using this combinatorial-geometric framework, it is shown that the class
\mathcal{T}
contains at least
\Theta(q^{q-1}q!)
mutually inequivalent parallelisms for
q
even, and at least
\Theta(q^{2q-3})
mutually inequivalent parallelisms when
q
is odd.

Preliminary results

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
be the finite field of order
q^{n}
, where
q=p^{m}
and
p
is a prime. We denote by
{\rm PG}(r,q^{n})
the
r
-dimensional projective space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, equipped with homogeneous projective coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r+1})
. The
q
-order subgeometries of
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
are also referred to as
Baer subgeometries
, and their subspaces as
Baer subspaces
.
We will represent projectivities of
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
with elements in
{\rm GL}(4,q^{2})
, with matrices acting on the left.

A notable example of line-spread of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
is the
Desarguesian line-spreads
, which can be constructed as follows; see
[
18
]
. Let
\Sigma\simeq{\rm PG}(3,q)
be a subgeometry of
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
, such that
\Sigma
is the set of fixed points of a semilinear collineation
\tau
of
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
of order
2
. Let
\ell
be a line of
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
such that
\ell
and
\ell^{\tau}
are skew, or equivalently, they span
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
. For each point
P
of
\ell
, define the line
r_{P}=\langle P,P^{\tau}\rangle_{q^{2}}
of
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
.

Lemma 2.1

(see
[
15
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
S
be a subspace of
{\rm PG}(3,q^{2})
. Then
\dim(S\cap\Sigma)=\dim(S)
if and only if
S^{\tau}=S
.

Note that
r_{P}
is fixed by
\tau
and so, by Lemma
2.1
intersects
\Sigma
in a subline. Moreover, two lines
r_{P}
and
r_{P^{\prime}}
, with
P,P^{\prime}\in\ell
,
P\neq P^{\prime}
, are disjoint, otherwise the subspace
\langle r_{P},r_{P^{\prime}}\rangle_{q^{2}}
is a plane, but this is impossible since the lines
\ell
and
\ell^{\tau}
are disjoint. it follows that

\mathcal{D}=\{r_{P}\cap\Sigma\colon P\in\ell\}

is a set of
q^{2}+1
pairwise skew lines of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
. In other words,
\mathcal{D}
forms a line spread of
\Sigma
, known as a
Desarguesian line-spread
. The lines
\ell
and
\ell^{\tau}
are called the
transversal lines
or
director spaces
of
\mathcal{D}
; see also
[
15
]
.

.

Proof.

First, assume that

. Then there exists

. Clearly,

. If

, we set

and cleary

has the desired properties. So, let

. For any

4
The isomorphism issue

Let
\mathcal{J}
be the stabilizer in
{\rm P\Gamma L}(4,q^{2})
of
\Sigma_{\eta}
. Two line-parallelisms
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
of
\Sigma_{\eta}
are said to be
equivalent
or

if there exists a collineation of
\mathcal{J}
mapping
\Pi
to
\Pi^{\prime}
. In Theorem

, we have shown that given a good set

which in turn gives rise to the line-parallelism

of

\mathcal{P}
and

3.4
.

4.1
The number of
good sets

Consider the following point sets of
{\rm PG}(2,q^{2})

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}=\left\{(1,\alpha u,\alpha v)\mid u,v\in\mathcal{U}\right\},

\displaystyle\alpha\in\Lambda,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}.

|\mathcal{Z}|=|\mathcal{I}|(q+1)^{2}

\varepsilon

q+1
pairs, where

,

\displaystyle\varepsilon({\mathcal{P}})=\left\{(1,\alpha_{i}u_{i},\alpha_{i}v_{i})\mid\left(P_{\alpha_{i}u_{i}},\pi_{\alpha_{i}v_{i}}\right)\in\mathcal{P}\right\}\subset\mathcal{Z}.

Consider the following lines and conics of
{\rm PG}(2,q^{2})

\displaystyle s_{c}:X_{2}=cX_{3},

\displaystyle c\in\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{b}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}C_{\alpha b},

\left(1,\alpha u,\alpha v\right)\in s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b},\mbox{ where }c=\frac{u}{v},b=\frac{\alpha u}{\alpha^{q}v^{q}}.

(4.1)

The algebraic conditions that define a good set

as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\left\{\left(P_{\alpha_{1}u_{1}},\pi_{\alpha_{1}v_{1}}\right),\dots,\left(P_{\alpha_{{q+1}}u_{q+1}},\pi_{\alpha_{{q+1}}v_{q+1}}\right)\right\}

q+1
pairs, where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
,
u_{i},v_{i}\in\mathcal{U}
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is a good set if and only if

\displaystyle|s_{c}\cap\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})|=1,

\displaystyle\forall c\in\mathcal{U},

(4.2)

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}_{b}\cap\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})|=1,

\displaystyle\forall b\in\mathcal{U}.

(4.3)

Proof.

By (
4.1
), we know that the point
(1,\alpha_{i}u_{i},\alpha_{i}v_{i})
of
\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})
belongs to the line
s_{\frac{u_{i}}{v_{i}}}
of
\mathcal{Y}
and to the conic
C_{\alpha_{i}b}\subset\mathscr{C}_{b}
, where

b=\frac{\alpha_{i}u_{i}}{\alpha_{i}^{q}v_{i}^{q}}.

(4.4)

Now, let
(1,\alpha_{j}u_{j},\alpha_{j}v_{j})
a further point of
\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})
, i.e.,
\left(\alpha_{i},u_{i},v_{i}\right)\neq\left(\alpha_{j},u_{j},v_{j}\right)
. Then
(1,\alpha_{j}u_{j},\alpha_{j}v_{j})
lies in
s_{\frac{u_{i}}{v_{i}}}
if and only if
u_{i}v_{j}-u_{j}v_{i}=0
. Analogously, the point
(1,\alpha_{j}u_{j},\alpha_{j}v_{j})
lies in
C_{\beta b}\subset\mathscr{C}_{b}
if and only if

\beta^{q+1}b-\alpha_{j}^{2}(u_{j}v_{j})=0.

Raising both sides to the power
q+1
, we obtain

\beta^{2(q+1)}=\alpha_{j}^{2(q+1)}.

Since
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{I}
and considering (
2.3
), this implies
\beta=\alpha_{j}
. Thus, taking into account (
4.4
), the point
(1,\alpha_{j}u_{j},\alpha_{j}v_{j})\in C_{\beta b}
if and only if
\beta=\alpha_{j}
and

\alpha_{i}u_{i}(\alpha_{j}v_{j})^{q}-(\alpha_{i}v_{i})^{q}\alpha_{j}u_{j}=0.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathcal{U}}\lvert s_{c}\cap\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})\rvert=\lvert\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})\rvert=q+1,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\mathcal{U}}\lvert\mathscr{C}_{b}\cap\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})\rvert=\lvert\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})\rvert=q+1.

Hence,
\mathcal{P}
satisfies (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) if and only if
|s_{c}\cap\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})|=|\mathscr{C}_{b}\cap\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})|=1
,
\forall c,b\in\mathcal{U}
.
∎

induces a bijection between good sets and subsets of

of size

satisfying (
4.2
), (
4.3
). We now describe the intersection behavior of a line

with a conic

in relation to the

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
s_{c}
be a line of
\mathcal{Y}
and
C_{\alpha b}
a conic contained in
\mathscr{C}_{b}
. The size of the intersection

, with

q
is even:

•

When
\alpha=\beta

\displaystyle|s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}|=1.

•

When
\alpha\neq\beta

\displaystyle|s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}|=0.

If

•

When

\displaystyle|s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}|=\begin{cases}2&\mbox{ if }\alpha^{q+1}\mbox{ is a square in }{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\mbox{ and }(cb)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}=1,\\

When

\displaystyle|s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}|=0.

Proof.

Let us start by considering the intersection

.
A point
(1,\beta u,\beta v)\in\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}
lies on the conic

if and only if

\alpha^{q+1}b-\beta^{2}uv=0.

(4.5)

Raising both sides to the power of
q+1

\alpha^{2(q+1)}=\beta^{2(q+1)}.

(4.6)

Recall that
\alpha
and
\beta
belong to
\Lambda

Case 1:
q

From (

\alpha=\beta

\lvert s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}\rvert=0.

Now suppose
\alpha=\beta

if and only if

Since

is even, the equation

has a unique solution in the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
.
Hence, there exists a unique

, and consequently a unique

lies in the intersection
s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}
.
Therefore, in this case, the intersection

Case 2:
q
odd

\alpha^{q+1}=\beta^{q+1}

\alpha=\beta
.
Therefore, it follows that
\alpha=\beta

\alpha\neq\beta

\lvert s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}\rvert=0.

So, assume
\alpha=\beta
. We now consider the intersection of the conic
C_{\alpha b}
with the line
s_{c}
. Note that

.
Also, since
q

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{q+1}cb}\right)^{q+1}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{q+1}b}{c}}\right)^{q+1}=\alpha^{q+1}\iff T_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}\iff T_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}.

Observe first that, since

(cb)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}=(c/b)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}=1

(cb)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}=(c/b)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}=-1

is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{q+1}}\right)^{q+1}=-\alpha^{q+1}

\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{q+1}cb}\right)^{q+1}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{q+1}b}{c}}\right)^{q+1}=\alpha^{q+1},

when either
\alpha^{q+1}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
(cb)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}=1
, or
\alpha^{q+1}
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and

When

\mathcal{I}_{1}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\colon\alpha^{q+1}\mbox{ is a square in }{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \mathcal{I}_{2}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\colon\alpha^{q+1}\mbox{ is not a square in }{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

\lvert\mathcal{I}_{1}\rvert=\begin{cases}\frac{q-1}{4}&\mbox{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
\frac{q-3}{4}&\mbox{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4},\end{cases}

and

\lvert\mathcal{I}_{2}\rvert=\begin{cases}\frac{q-1}{4}&\mbox{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
\frac{q+1}{4}&\mbox{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{1}\cup\mathcal{I}_{2}
and
\mathcal{I}_{1}\cap\mathcal{I}_{2}=\emptyset

\lvert\mathcal{I}_{1}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{I}_{2}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{I}\rvert=\frac{q-1}{2}.

(4.7)

Let
A=\left\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{\frac{q-3}{2}}\right\}
and

, and by Lemma

\{\pm 1\}\cup A\cup A^{-1}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}\setminus\{0\}

Note that a nonzero element

is also a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}

A
equals to the number of squares in

. Recall that

if and only if

. Also, the number of nonzero squares in

is
\frac{q-1}{2}
. Thus:

•

If

is
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-2\right)=\frac{q-5}{4}
.

•

If

is
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-1\right)=\frac{q-3}{4}

Finally, recall that the element

Corollary 4.4
.

For fixed

q

|s_{c}\cap\mathscr{C}_{b}\cap\mathcal{Z}|=\lvert\mathcal{I}\rvert=\frac{q-2}{2}.

If
q

\displaystyle|s_{c}\cap\mathscr{C}_{b}\cap\mathcal{Z}|=\begin{cases}2\lvert\mathcal{I}_{1}\rvert&\mbox{ if }(cb)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}=1,\\

First, consider the case where

\begin{array}[]{rl}|s_{c}\cap\mathscr{C}_{b}\cap\mathcal{Z}|&=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}|s_{c}\cap C_{\alpha b}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}|\\

|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\cdot(q+1)
ways. Also, by Corollary
4.4
, for

, the point
R_{i}
can be chosen in

|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\cdot(q+1)-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}2|\mathcal{I}_{1}|=|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\cdot(q+1-2(i-1))

ways. Similarly, for
i\in\left\{h+\frac{q+1}{2}+1,\ldots,q+1\right\}
, again by Corollary
4.4
,
R_{i}
can be chosen in

|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\cdot(q+1)-\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}2|\mathcal{I}_{1}|-\sum_{j=h+\frac{q+1}{2}+1}^{i}2|\mathcal{I}_{1}|=|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\cdot\left(q+1-2\left(i-h+\frac{q+1}{2}\right)\right)

ways. Now, the choice of
R_{i}
for indices

is independent of the choices of
R_{1},\ldots,R_{h}
and
R_{h+\frac{q+1}{2}+1},\ldots,R_{q+1}
. Therefore, similarly to the previous case, for each

, there are

|\mathcal{I}_{2}|\cdot\left((q+1)-2(i-h-1)\right)

possible choices for
R_{i}
, whereas there are

|\mathcal{I}_{2}|\cdot\left((q+1)-2(i-h-1)\right)

possible ways to choose
R_{i}
if

. This completes the proof of the statement.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}
,
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
line-parallelisms of
\Sigma_{\eta}
, where
\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
are good sets. If there exists
\psi\in\mathcal{J}
such that
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{\psi}=\Pi_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
, then
\psi\in\Gamma_{r_{U_{1}}}
.

Proof.

By construction

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}=\left\{\left(\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\right)\cup{\mathcal{R}_{\ell}^{o}}\mid\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{P}}\right\}\cup\left\{\mathcal{D}_{\eta}\right\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}=\left\{\left(\mathcal{S}_{\ell^{\prime}}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\cup{\mathcal{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{o}}\mid\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\right\}\cup\left\{\mathcal{D}_{\eta}\right\}.

If
\psi\in\mathcal{J}
is such that
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{\psi}=\Pi_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
, then
\mathcal{D}_{\eta}^{\psi}=\mathcal{D}_{\eta}
, since
\mathcal{D}_{\eta}
is the unique Desarguesian line-spread in
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
. Hence
\psi\in\Gamma
. Let
\ell,\tilde{\ell}
be distinct lines of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{P}}
and
\ell^{\prime},\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}
be distinct lines of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\left(\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\right)\cup{\mathcal{R}_{\ell}^{o}}\right)^{\psi}=\left(\mathcal{S}_{\ell^{\prime}}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\cup{\mathcal{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{o}},

\displaystyle\left(\left(\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{\ell}}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell}}\right)\cup{\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell}}^{o}}\right)^{\psi}=\left(\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}}\right)\cup{\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}}^{o}}.

Then necessarily

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\ell}^{\psi}=\mathcal{R}_{\ell^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell}}^{\psi}=\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}},

where

\displaystyle r_{U_{1}}\cap\Sigma_{\eta}=\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell}}=\mathcal{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(r_{U_{1}}\cap\Sigma_{\eta}\right)^{\psi}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{R}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell}}\right)^{\psi}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{\ell}^{\psi}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell}}^{\psi}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=r_{U_{1}}\cap\Sigma_{\eta},

and hence
\psi\in Stab_{\Gamma}\left(r_{U_{1}}\right)=\Gamma_{r_{U_{1}}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\left\{\left(P_{\alpha_{1}u_{1}},\pi_{\alpha_{1}v_{1}}\right),\dots,\left(P_{\alpha_{{q+1}}u_{q+1}},\pi_{\alpha_{{q+1}}v_{q+1}}\right)\right\},

Theorem 4.11
.

The number of mutually not equivalent line-parallelisms of
\Sigma_{\eta}
contained in
\mathcal{T}
is at least

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)^{q+1}\frac{(q-2)!}{2hq(q+1)}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }q\mbox{ is even, }

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(q-5)(q-1)}{16}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\frac{(q^{2}-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}{2hq^{2}(q+1)}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4},

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q-3}{4}\right)^{q+1}\frac{(q^{2}-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}{2hq^{2}(q+1)}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4}.

Proof.

From Proposition
4.8
, if
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}},\Pi_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{T}
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{\psi}=\Pi_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
, for some
\psi\in\mathcal{J}
, then
\psi\in\Gamma_{r_{U_{1}}}
. Hence

|\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathcal{J}}\cap\mathcal{T}|\leq|\Gamma_{r_{U_{1}}}|=2hq^{2}(q^{2}-1)(q+1)

(4.9)

Assume first that
q
is even. By the definition of good set (see Definition
3.3
), we know that each good set

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\left\{\left(P_{\alpha_{1}u_{1}},\pi_{\alpha_{1}v_{1}}\right),\dots,\left(P_{\alpha_{q+1}u_{q+1}},\pi_{\alpha_{q+1}v_{q+1}}\right)\right\}

is such that
\alpha_{i}^{q+1}\neq 1
,

. Therefore, by Proposition
4.10
, the number of distinct parallelisms in
\mathcal{T}
equals the number of distinct good sets. By Proposition
4.5
, this number is given by:

|\mathcal{T}|=|\mathcal{I}|^{q+1}\cdot(q+1)!=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)^{q+1}\cdot(q+1)!.

Now, considering (
4.9
), we obtain that in
\mathcal{T}
there are at least

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{T}|}{|\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathcal{J}}\cap\mathcal{T}|}&\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)^{q+1}\cdot\frac{(q+1)!}{2hq^{2}(q^{2}-1)(q+1)}\\[10.0pt]
&\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)^{q+1}\cdot\frac{(q-2)!}{2hq(q+1)}\end{array}

non-equivalent line-parallelisms of
\Sigma_{\eta}
.

Now, assume that
q
is odd. The size of
\mathcal{T}
is clearly greater than or equal to the number of good sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\left\{\left(P_{\alpha_{1}u_{1}},\pi_{\alpha_{1}v_{1}}\right),\dots,\left(P_{\alpha_{q+1}u_{q+1}},\pi_{\alpha_{q+1}v_{q+1}}\right)\right\},

\left(\frac{q-3}{4}\right)^{q+1}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(q+1-2i\right)^{2}&\mbox{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

So, when

is odd, in
\mathcal{T}
there are at least

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{T}|}{|\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathcal{J}}\cap\mathcal{T}|}\geq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{(q-5)(q-1)}{16}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\frac{(q^{2}-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}{2hq^{2}(q+1)}&\mbox{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\

not equivalent line-parallelisms of

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(q+1-2i\right)^{2}}{2hq^{2}(q^{2}-1)(q+1)}>\frac{(q^{2}-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}{2hq^{2}(q+1)},

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(q+1-2i\right)^{2}>\prod_{i=0}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left((q+1)^{2}-2(q+1)\right)=\prod_{i=0}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(q^{2}-1\right)=\left(q^{2}-1\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}.

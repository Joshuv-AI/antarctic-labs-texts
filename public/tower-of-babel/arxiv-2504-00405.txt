Adaptive Step Selection for a Filtered Implicit Method
Stephen M. McGovern)

Abstract
Pre-filtering and post-filtering steps can be added to many of the traditional numerical methods to generate new, higher order methods with strong stability properties. Presented in this paper are a variable step pre-filter and post-filter that allow adaptive time stepping for a filtered method based on Implicit Euler from [ 4 ] .

\displaystyle\tilde{y}_{n}=y_{n}-\frac{1}{2}(y_{n}-2y_{n-1}+y_{n-2}),

Step 2) IE Solve

\displaystyle\frac{y_{n+1}-\tilde{y}_{n}}{k}=f(t_{n+1},y_{n+1}),

\displaystyle y_{n+1}^{3rd}=y_{n+1}-\frac{5}{11}(y_{n+1}-3y_{n}+3y_{n-1}-y_{n-2}).

We follow the definition of discrete curvature presented in
[

]

y_{n}

y_{n-1}

\displaystyle\phi(t)

\displaystyle=y_{n+1}\ell_{n+1}(t)+y_{n}\ell_{n}(t)+y_{n-1}\ell_{n-1}(t)

\displaystyle=y_{n+1}\frac{(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n})}{(t_{n+1}-t_{n-1})(t_{n+1}-t_{n})}+y_{n}\frac{(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n+1})}{(t_{n}-t_{n-1})(t_{n}-t_{n+1})}

\displaystyle+y_{n-1}\frac{(t-t_{n})(t-t_{n+1})}{(t_{n-1}-t_{n})(t_{n-1}-t_{n+1})}.

Differentiate twice with respect to

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(t_{n+1}-t_{n-1})(t_{n+1}-t_{n})}y_{n+1}+\frac{2}{(t_{n}-t_{n-1})(t_{n}-t_{n+1})}y_{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{(t_{n-1}-t_{n})(t_{n-1}-t_{n+1})}y_{n-1}.

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(k_{n-1}+k_{n})k_{n}}y_{n+1}+\frac{2}{k_{n-1}(-k_{n})}y_{n}+\frac{2}{(-k_{n-1})(-(k_{n}+k_{n-1}))}y_{n-1}.

3
Main Results

Now that the background is in place, we show how to derive the adaptive pre-filter and post-filter for the desired orders. We use direct Taylor expansions to determine the pre-filter
\alpha_{n}
for second order and an alternative method to find the post-filter
\beta_{n}

3.1
Adaptive Step Pre-filter

Consider the first two steps of adaptive method Filtered-IE23 from
Section

1
.
We must choose
\alpha_{n}
so that Step 1) and Step 2) yield a second order method.

Proposition 1
.

The value for the pre-filter
\alpha_{n}
which gives a second order adaptive method after completing Step 1) and Step 2) is

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\frac{k_{n}^{2}}{k_{n-1}k_{n-2}}.

Proof.

The strategy is to construct the equivalent multistep method, Taylor expand around each of the terms and choose
\alpha_{n}
accordingly. Consider Step 1) and Step 2):

\displaystyle\tilde{y}_{n}=y_{n}-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\left(\frac{2k_{n-2}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n}-2y_{n-1}+\frac{2k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n-2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{y_{n+1}-\tilde{y}_{n}}{k_{n}}=f(t_{n+1},y_{n+1}).

Substitute the pre-filtered value from Step 1) into the Implicit Euler solve in Step 2), gather terms and simplify:

\displaystyle y_{n+1}-\left(y_{n}-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\left(\frac{2k_{n-2}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n}-2y_{n-1}+\frac{2k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n-2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=k_{n}f(t_{n+1},y_{n+1}),\,\,\,\text{or}

\displaystyle y_{n+1}+\frac{(\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n}-\alpha_{n}y_{n-1}+\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n-2}-k_{n}(y^{\prime})_{n+1}

\displaystyle=0.

Taylor expand around each of the terms:

\displaystyle y_{n+1}

\displaystyle=y_{n+1},

\displaystyle\frac{(\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n+1}-\frac{(\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}k_{n}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{((\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1})}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}\frac{k_{n}^{2}}{2}y^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{O}(k_{n}^{3}),

\displaystyle-\alpha_{n}y_{n-1}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{n}y_{n+1}+\alpha_{n}(k_{n}+k_{n-1})y^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{n}\frac{(k_{n}+k_{n-1})^{2}}{2}y^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{O}((k_{n}+k_{n-1})^{3}),

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}y_{n+1}-\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}(k_{n}+k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})y^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}\frac{(k_{n}+k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})^{2}}{2}y^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}((k_{n}+k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})^{3}),

\displaystyle-k_{n}(y^{\prime})_{n+1}

\displaystyle=-k_{n}y^{\prime}.

For a second order method,
\alpha_{n}
must be chosen so that the
y_{n+1}
,
y^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime\prime}
terms sum to zero. Sum the coefficients on the
y_{n+1}
terms and we have:

\displaystyle 1+\frac{(\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}+(-\alpha_{n})+\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}=0.

This is zero regardless of the choice of
\alpha_{n}
. Similarly, sum the coefficients on the
y^{\prime}
terms:

\displaystyle-\frac{(\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}k_{n}+\alpha_{n}(k_{n}+k_{n-1})

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}(k_{n}+k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})-k_{n}=0.

This is also trivially zero. Finally, sum the coefficients on the
y^{\prime\prime}
terms:

\displaystyle\frac{((\alpha_{n}-1)k_{n-2}-k_{n-1})}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}\frac{k_{n}^{2}}{2}+-\alpha_{n}\frac{(k_{n}+k_{n-1})^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}}{k_{n-1}+k_{n-2}}\frac{(k_{n}+k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{n}k_{n}^{2}k_{n-2}-k_{n}^{2}k_{n-2}-k_{n}^{2}k_{n-1}}{2(k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})}+\frac{-\alpha_{n}k_{n}^{2}k_{n-1}-\alpha_{n}k_{n}^{2}k_{n-2}^{2}-2\alpha_{n}k_{n}k_{n-1}^{2}}{2(k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})}

\displaystyle+\frac{-2\alpha_{n}k_{n}k_{n-1}k_{n-2}-\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}^{3}-\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}^{2}k_{n-2}+\alpha_{n}k_{n}^{2}k_{n-1}+2\alpha_{n}k_{n}k_{n-1}^{2}}{2(k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\alpha_{n}k_{n}k_{n-1}k_{n-2}+\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}^{3}+2\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}^{2}k_{n-2}+\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}k_{n-2}^{2}}{2(k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})}.

We require this to sum to zero:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-k_{n}^{2}k_{n-2}-k_{n}^{2}k_{n-1}-\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}^{2}k_{n-2}+2\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}^{2}k_{n-2}+\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}k_{n-2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-k_{n}^{2}k_{n-2}-k_{n}^{2}k_{n-1}+\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}^{2}k_{n-2}+\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}k_{n-2}^{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\alpha_{n}k_{n-1}k_{n-2}(k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})=k_{n}^{2}(k_{n-1}+k_{n-2})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\alpha_{n}=\frac{k_{n}^{2}}{k_{n-1}k_{n-2}}.

With this choice of

\displaystyle+3k_{n-3}\left(k_{n-2}-k_{n}\right)\left(k_{n-1}+k_{n}\right)-2k_{n-1}k_{n}\left(k_{n-1}+k_{n}\right)).

6
Conclusions

The constant step methods IE-Pre-2 and IE-Pre-Post-3 from
[
4
]
are evidence that filters can increase the order of Implicit Euler while still maintaining favorable stability properties. The work presented herein shows that the natural embedded pair within these methods can be used for effective adaptive stepping. We have found the pre-filter and post-filter to construct the adaptive extension of IE-Pre-2 and IE-Pre-Post-3, and performed initial testing. Despite the choice of the simplest controller, naive halving and doubling, the adaptive step method Filtered-IE23 performed well on the numerical tests run thus far. In situations where the constant step methods and the adaptive step extension perform similarly, the error estimator can be relied on to guide the adaptive method to the solution. Additionally, Filtered-IE23 has shown that it can navigate a problem where the constant step methods struggle. This is promising for more finely tuned implementations of the method.

The problem of implementing higher accuracy methods in legacy codes leads to interesting questions in numerical analysis. One approach is to add time filters, as herein, but others would be welcome. Next steps for research on Filtered-IE23 are fine tuning the implementation, further numerical testing, and analysis on the open question of stability.

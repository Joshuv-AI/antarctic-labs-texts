Nonlinear electronic streaming instability in lunar plasmas
Authors: Partha Pratim Deka, Pralay Kumar Karmakar, Ahmed Atteya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe nonlinear dynamics of bi-electronic streaming instability (BSI) is investigated in lunar plasma environments using the nonlinear WKB method. It is seen that the considered instability saturation is governed by a standard form of the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation. It has a unique set of multiparametric nonlinear convective and linear dispersive coefficients. We semi-analytically reveal the instability saturation, principally in diverse solitary wave forms, alongside their basic conservation laws, in a broader perspective horizon. The sensitive frame velocity-dependency of the derived rarefactive solitary structures is illustrated, alongside the streaming velocity effects. It is interestingly found that such wave-spectral patterns completely disappear for a particular value of the relative streaming velocity (frame-flow equalisation). This study fairly maps the investigated nonlinear BSI saturation amongst the Kaguya-observed electrostatic solitary waves (ESWs) observationally reported elsewhere.Keywords: Lunar plasma, Two-stream instability, KdV equation, Solitary wave, Solar windSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The Moon and lunar plasmas have been gaining growing research interest for a couple of decades. The Moon has a very thin atmosphere and an even thinner ionosphere that extends up to 100 km above the surface1. The whole extent of the lunar exosphere jointly weighs less than 100 tons1. Nevertheless, several significant plasma activities occur in its environment. The gas molecules in the atmosphere are irradiated by the solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation. Its positive ions and electrons are produced by photoionization mechanisms. The Lunar Atmosphere and Dust Environment Explorer (LADEE) and Kaguya missions indicate the presence of heavy ions in the lunar environment. The origin of these heavy ions is attributed to the solar wind, ionosphere, meteoroid ablation, lunar surface, and exosphere2.

The passive stellar radio experiments have revealed a lunar ionosphere with an approximate electron density that extends up to several kilometres3. Also, Chandrayaan-1’s Radio Occultation (RO) experiment measured the near-surface lunar electron density to be that reduces to at an altitude of 40 km4. RO measurements from Chandrayaan-2 suggest electron densities or higher, depending on the region5. Even higher electron densities () have been revealed by RO measurements when the Moon traverses Earth’s magnetotail region6. Again, in-situ Langmuir probe measurements made in the lunar south polar region by the RAMBHA-LP onboard Chandrayaan-3 indicate a near-surface electron density in the range 7. Ion density, on the other hand, is only a few near the surface. The lunar ion density typically lies in the range of 1. This low ion density has been believed to be the result of solar wind interaction with the lunar atmosphere as it sweeps away ions from near the surface8. Its atmosphere contains neutral atoms of inert gases, such as Argon (Ar), Helium (He), and Neon (Ne)8. The photoionization process has not explained the high electron density in the lunar atmosphere, as reported by the RO observations1.

When an object obstructs fluid flow, the region formed behind it is called a wake region9. A wake region is formed behind the Moon when the solar wind plasma hits the lunar atmosphere, and it acts as an obstruction to the flow. Since there is no irradiation of the neutral atoms, compared to the sunlit side, there is negligible plasma density in the lunar wake region4. Most models predict almost no ionosphere in the region, and radio measurements rarely detect any significant plasma features4. But, a recent study on the lunar wake region has shown that electron beams and suprathermal populations can sustain electrostatic solitary waves (ESWs), consistent with ARTEMIS observations10. A strong ESW in the unique and complex lunar dusty plasma environment, which can accelerate the dusty particles, has been predicted in Ref11.

In the equilibrium state of a system, there is no free energy to excite any waves. An instability in the system is a way to reduce the free energy and bring the system back to equilibrium. So, it occurs when energy builds up in a plasma system, pushing it away from equilibrium12. Instabilities generate plasma waves or particle beams at the expense of this free energy12. Streaming instability is one type of instability that takes place in a plasma system when there is a relative motion between different species in the system. Specifically, when two species of charged particles move relative to each other, two-stream instability (TSI) is triggered in the system12. The effect of Earth’s magnetosphere on the lunar plasma dynamics has been studied, where solar ion (proton) particles stream through the lunar plasma consisting of ions and Maxwellian and superthermal electron distributions13. In the lunar environment, TSI is triggered as a result of the interaction between solar wind and the lunar ionosphere on the sunlit side of the Moon. The study in Ref.12. suggests that it leads to the excitation of four plasma modes in the system, two due to the lunar plasma and the other two due to the solar wind beam plasma.

Plasma waves mirror the inherent properties of the medium through which they propagate, thus providing useful information regarding the processes responsible for their generation. In space environments, these waves are considered to be sensitive probes for investigating the fine structure and dynamic processes of plasmas. Wave behaviour is intimately connected with plasma properties, and they serve as effective indicators of those properties. However, particle-wave interactions can alter the particle distribution, particularly through damping mechanisms. Modelling such interactions is often nonlinear to properly describe plasma behaviours. Several authors have made efforts in analysing plasma waves in the lunar environment to understand the system better. A lot of researchers have studied the lunar wake region extensively9,10,14–17. In the sunlit region, the threshold condition for exciting and sustaining TSI in the environment has been determined using linear approximation12. This linear study12 could not give a theoretical justification to explain the high electron density in the Moon’s environment. But with the help of PIC simulations, it has been reported that, due to the interaction of solar wind with the lunar plasma, the TSI occurs, resulting in electron bunching effects.

The excitable plasma instability is usually treated in the linear regime to determine the threshold conditions for generating and sustaining the instability, and to check whether it is sensitive to small perturbations or not12. However, once the instability enters the nonlinear regime, which is often the case, nonlinear methods provide a more accurate description. These methods are employed in understanding the complete evolution of a system and explaining the plasma features observed in laboratory and space plasmas with better accuracy. While linear studies determine the growth rates of instabilities, understanding how these waves saturate and eventually lead the system toward equilibrium requires nonlinear analysis. To the best of our knowledge, no attempts have yet been made to explore the nonlinear dynamics of the BSI utilising the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) framework on the illuminated side of the Moon.

This study focuses on the weakly nonlinear interaction of the electron plasma fluid of the lunar ionosphere with the solar wind plasma electron beam. The primary objective is to investigate the saturation dynamics of the various modes triggered by BSI in the weakly nonlinear KdV framework. ESWs have been reported in both the lunar wake10,17,18 and the dayside above crustal magnetic anomalies from Kaguya observations18, motivating the present KdV analysis of the lunar dayside plasma to examine ESW structures driven by the BSI. This study for the first time treats a purely electronic two-stream system with immobile ions, whereas the existing KdV-based ESW studies involve ion-acoustic or electron-acoustic modes with mobile ions or Boltzmann electron backgrounds as far as seen in the literature. Although, a lot of researchers extensively studied the lunar wake region, very few studies have attempted to explain the occurrences of ESWs in the lunar sunlit side. Within this physically distinct framework, the nonlinear KdV coefficient is shown to be negative definite over the entire physically admissible parameter space, analytically enforcing the existence of only rarefactive solitons, a result not assumed a priori and not explicitly demonstrated in earlier lunar ESW studies. Besides, this study aims to address the possible connection of the observed higher electron density in the lunar environments with the BSI. In addition, the KdV system has been renowned as a conservative and integrable model that has an infinite hierarchy of conservation laws19–22. We derive analytically the conservative forms of the KdV system. It shows the characteristic properties of the dynamical behaviours of the mass density, momentum density, energy density, and the next two higher-order conservation terms (no classical analogy), relevant to the lowest-order perturbed electrostatic potential fluctuations of practical relevance.

This paper is organized as follows: Starting with the introduction in Sect. 1, Sect. 2 describes the physical model and formalism employed in the study. The desired KdV equation is derived in Sect. 3 and solved in Sect. 4. The major findings, along with comprehensive conservative features, are presented in Sect. 5. The main results of our investigation are conclusively summarised in Sect. 6. Lastly, the detailed derivation of the KdV conservation laws is offered in Appendix A.

Adopted model and formalism

In this study, we consider the case when the Moon is not in the Earth’s magnetotail, and since it has no global magnetic field, we consider an unmagnetized plasma condition. The unmagnetized approximation is further justified by the fact that that the plasma features under consideration have characteristic lengths much smaller than the electron gyro-radius in the weak interplanetary magnetic field. We utilise a two-fluid, homogeneous, isotropic, and unmagnetized cold plasma fluid description in this study. Although, the solar-wind electrons are inherently warm (∼7–30 eV) in nature23 cold electron approximation is justified here because our model treats these electrons as a directed beam. So, the electron dynamics is governed mainly by the drifting beam-plasma instability dynamics. As a result, the collective instability is governed by the drift speed of the beam and phase speed of the triggered instability alongside electron temperature. Furthermore, the cold-electron approximation is used as an asymptotic ordering valid for long-wavelength, weakly nonlinear modes (), where pressure effects enter only at higher order. On the other hand, the ambient lunar electrons are comparatively cold (∼2–5 eV at most)24 and therefore, it can be approximated as cold. Existing research indicates that finite temperature mainly affects soliton amplitude and its width. The literature25 finds that both amplitude and width increase with electron temperature while conserving its rarefactive nature. The study in Ref26. also supports that amplitude and the width gets modified with finite temperature affects but it does not alter the structure of the KdV equation or generically reverse soliton polarity. It determines only the parametric range over which solitons can evolve. So, accounting finite temperature provides numerical modifications but leaves the KdV coefficients and soliton polarity qualitatively unchanged.

The Moon acts as an obstruction to the flow of solar wind. The solar electrons, being more energetic and mobile, stream faster through the lunar plasma environment to fill the wake region until a negative potential is set up, which repels further flow of electrons and accelerates the flow of the solar ion. In this process, the solar electron plasma interacts with the lunar electron plasma and gives rise to the TSI12, or bi-electronic streaming instability (BSI) in this case. The free energy source needed for this BSI excitation originates from the electronic currents from the solar wind plasmas. Although some studies27 have confirmed the presence of a feeble magnetic anomaly on the lunar surface that may significantly affect solar wind flow, we restrict our study to the case with neither local nor global magnetic fields. Here, the lunar plasma ion is considered to form a stationary and immobile background about which native electrons are relatively at rest compared to the solar electron beam, which streams with a finite velocity through it to the plasma void wake region. The model is similar to the one adopted in Ref12.

The KdV equation offers an effective tool for studying solitary waves that have small but finite amplitude. It is derived using the nonlinear WKB (Wentzel, Kramers, Brillouin) method28–30, similar to the reductive perturbation technique (RPT)31–33, which involves expanding physical variables, and is commonly applied to analyse ESW in different types of plasmas34. Utilising the one-dimensional fluidic approach, we derive the KdV equation and analyse the nonlinear evolution and propagation of electrostatic plasma waves in the lunar plasma system as a result of the interaction between the solar wind electron plasma beam and the ambient lunar electron population. Since the oscillation frequency of electrons is very high compared to the ions, and the timescales and phase speeds of the two-stream driven ESWs are much faster than ion response time, we assume that the positive ions form a stationary and immobile background35. In our study, we consider two electron species: one in the lunar environment, which is assumed to be at rest in equilibrium conditions, and another that streams through the atmosphere with velocity with respect to the stationary ion background.

Conclusive summary

In this study, the nonlinear dynamics of bi-electronic fluid streaming instability in lunar plasmas is investigated in the nonlinear WKB framework. It is shown mainly that the considered instability evolution reduces to a standard form of the KdV model equation. The free energy source originates from the electronic currents from the solar wind plasmas. A semi-analytic analysis of this equation reveals the excitation of a plethora of rarefactive solitary wave structures systematically. A detailed illustrative description is carried out to study the steady-state patterns of the associated field, geometrical curvatures, and phase portraits. It is found that the magnitude of the solitary wave amplitude increases with the stretching frame velocity and vice versa. Thus, the stretching coordinate frame velocity acts as a destabilising factor on the lunar BSI processes. In contrast, the solar electronic drift velocity shows non-uniform effects on the solitary wave evolution. After a critical value of the normalised flow velocity (3.8), the potential amplitude reaches a saturation level, and so forth. The derived KdV conserved densities and the associated flux terms are illustratively analysed. It visually demonstrates how these conserved quantities evolve with the solitary structural patterns. While the mentioned study does not explicitly report “direct” measurements of spatial widths and timescales, the inferred values from the reported waveforms lie in the same order of magnitude. For instance, the bipolar pulses observed by Kaguya imply spatial scales of approximately 10–100 m. Furthermore, by normalising the temporal evolution to the fundamental plasma period (), our model yields stable pulse durations of approximately 10 , consistent within observational uncertainty with the 10–50 pulse durations measured by the Kaguya WFC-L receiver. We find unnormalised electrostatic potential amplitudes of 0.02–0.08 V and bipolar electric field strengths of 1–10 \:mV . These inferences, based on our depicted analysis, are highly consistent with the electric-field amplitudes of 1–4 , as observed by Kaguya18. This alignment, despite the lack of direct reporting of spatial widths in the primary literature, supports the plausibility of nonlinear BSI saturation as one viable mechanism for generating lunar ESWs. These findings provide a foundational platform for continued investigation of diverse nonlinear coherent plasma structures in different stability contextual scenarios. This model ignores the lunar magnetic anomalies (LMAs) because of their spatially localized and non-uniform nature, making it possible to simplify the problem of the interaction between the solar wind plasma flow and the lunar ambient plasma. Furthermore, including these anomalies would significantly modify the adopted framework. The reflection of incident solar wind particles by LMAs would constitute multiple distinct streams rather than a single flow. The system then shifts from a purely electrostatic regime to a more complex electromagnetic interaction.

While these results align with observations, certain limitations persist, mainly in the applied approximation of weakly nonlinear regime. Besides, the current model assumes a simplified plasma composition in the ideal fluid model framework. It is silent about collisional damping effects of both inter- and intra-constitutive origin. Integration of diverse solar wind parameters, realistic lunar conditions, and 3-D kinetic modelling is required to fully unravel the complexities of the realistic lunar plasma environments. Adopting a general nonlinear theory that accounts for short-wavelength modes and the high variability of the interplanetary magnetic field at 1 AU would enhance our investigative applicability in a more realistic sense.

In addition to conservation laws for mass, momentum, and energy the KdV equation has an infinite number of integrals that ensure the integrability of the system40,42. Although, the first three invariants are known and represent macroscopic parameters, the higher-order ones are primarily mathematical in nature and lack conventional physical interpretations. These are intrinsically linked to the integrable structure of the system, which underlies the stability of solitons, symmetry, and quasi-elastic interactions among them40,43. These invariants can be interpreted as constraints on the solution by bounding higher-order derivatives, thereby limiting the growth of high-frequency components and preserving the coherence of the solitary pulse40. It is noteworthy that the existence of symmetries, as indicated with the help of diverse conserved quantities in accordance with Noether’s theorem44, offers a fruitful technique in simplistically solving complex dynamical problems. It is achieved at the cost of reduced degrees of freedom or reduced independent parameters with enhanced symmetries and associated conservation laws in such nonlinear dynamical systems. The higher-order conservation laws in this context are not very significant in the physical sense, and hence, their presence is summarily omitted.

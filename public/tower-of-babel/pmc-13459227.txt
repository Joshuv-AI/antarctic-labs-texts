The business of medicine: a cross-sectional analysis of 4- and 5-Year MD/MBA programs in the United States
Authors: Katherine Krishna, Logan Goddard, Maxon Knott, Steven Ullmann, Sabrina Taldone
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe number of combined Doctor of Medicine and Master of Business Administration (MD/MBA) programs in the United States has increased fivefold over the past two decades, reflecting a shift toward integrating medical and business education. While most MD/MBA programs follow a 5-year structure with a dedicated year for business coursework, some offer 4-year programs that integrate both degrees concurrently. This study evaluated the structure and characteristics of MD/MBA programs in the United States in 2025, comparing 4-year and 5-year program structures and examining how 4-year programs integrate medical and business training.MethodsIn January 2025, 93 U.S. MD/MBA programs were identified using AAMC and Association of MD/MBA Programs databases. Programs were categorized by duration (4-year vs. 5-year) and analyzed for admissions requirements, credit loads, degree integration, and institutional characteristics. Findings were compared to a 2022 analysis by Laditi et al. Additional analyses compared 4-year and 5-year programs in 2025, including how 4-year programs integrate business coursework into medical training. Statistical analyses used chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests (α = 0.05).ResultsIn total, 93 MD/MBA programs were identified in 2025, compared to 92 in 2022. From 2022 to 2025, significant changes included programs requiring full-time MBA credit loads (9% to 37%, p < 0.001) and a reduction in GMAT requirements (34% to 15%, p = 0.01). Of the 93 programs, 12 (13%) offered a 4-year track. Compared to 5-year programs, 4-year programs offered significantly more MBA specializations (83% vs. 15%, p < 0.001) and required fewer than 50 MBA credits (100% vs. 66%, p = 0.03). Among 4-year programs, 42% delivered MBA coursework online, 25% in-person, and 33% in a hybrid format.ConclusionsThe overall number of MD/MBA programs remained stable between 2022 and 2025, but program structures and admission requirements continued to evolve. This study highlights the differences between 4-year and 5-year MD/MBA programs and describes how 4-year programs integrate business training within medical education. These findings may inform prospective students, program administrators, and medical education policymakers seeking to design, improve, or select MD/MBA programs that best prepare future physician-leaders.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-026-09596-8.

Background

Over the past two decades, the number of medical schools offering combined Doctor of Medicine and Master of Business Administration (MD/MBA) programs has increased fivefold [1], demonstrating a shift toward integrating medical and business education. The MD degree provides clinical training and professional medical credentials, whereas the MBA degree develops competencies such as financial management, organizational leadership, and strategic decision-making that are not traditionally emphasized in medical curricula [2]. As health care is becoming more complex, health systems are recognizing the value of physician leaders with perspectives beyond medicine, leading to the development of MD/MBA programs that complement future physicians’ medical knowledge with business and leadership expertise [2]. The structure of MD/MBA programs varies greatly from program to program, prompting investigation into how these programs have changed over time [3] and how programs balance the opportunity cost of pursuing an MBA degree while also pursuing an MD degree [4].

MD/MBA programs are typically structured as 5-year programs, incorporating a dedicated 12-month hiatus for business coursework either before or during the medical school curriculum. In contrast, a limited number of programs offer a 4-year MD/MBA program that integrate medical and business education comprehensively throughout the four years.

This study examines the characteristics of MD/MBA programs offered in the United States. This serves as a 3-year follow-up to the 2022 MD/MBA program study by Laditi et al. [3]. Additionally, this study offers a detailed comparison of 4-year and 5-year MD/MBA programs, examining their differences and analyzing how 4-year programs integrate both degrees within a condensed timeframe. A literature review on MD/MBA programs was conducted in collaboration with librarians from the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine, who provided access to medical and business databases. No prior studies were found that specifically examined 4-year MD/MBA programs or conducted a comparative analysis between 4-year and 5-year programs. To our knowledge, this is the first study to conduct such an analysis.

Methods

In January 2025, we identified 93 MD/MBA programs offered in the United States using online resources from the Association of American Medical Colleges (AAMC) [5] and the Association of MD/MBA Programs [6]. Data extraction was performed in January 2025, and all data reflects publicly available information during that period. MD/MBA programs were defined as a combined MD and MBA during medical school studies, and both degrees were conferred upon graduation. Programs offering sequential MBA degrees after medical school graduation or standalone executive MBA options for practicing physicians were excluded. Additional details about these MD/MBA programs, such as program length, institutional classification (public or private), and admission timelines, were gathered through an internet search of individual medical school and affiliated business school websites. Institutions with multiple campuses that accepted applications separately on the AAMC Medical School Admission Requirements (MSAR) website were counted as distinct medical schools. When program details were not available on institutional websites, the corresponding variables were recorded as “unknown” during data extraction and labeled as “data not available” during analysis.

Data were then compared to a prior study analyzing combined MD/MBA programs in 2022 [3] to determine if there were any differences in 2025. Laditi et al. [3], the authors of the 2022 study, were contacted via email and their data were made available for comparative analysis (Table 1). To ensure methodological consistency across all analyses and tables in our study (Tables 1, 2, 3 and 4), we classified programs offering both 4‑ and 5‑year MD/MBA pathways into the 4‑year category and applied this rule uniformly. In contrast, Laditi et al. [3] categorized programs offering both 4‑ and 5‑year MD/MBA pathways into a separate 4–5‑year category, which reflects the differences in category distributions between the two datasets in Table 1 (denoted by superscript “a”).

Additionally, the characteristics of 4-year and 5-year programs in 2025 were evaluated and compared (Table 2), with additional analysis on 4-year programs to determine how these programs integrate business training during medical school (Tables 3 and 4).

For 4-year MD/MBA programs, additional methodology was employed to gather information unavailable on program websites. Program administrators or listed contacts were contacted by email, followed by a phone call if no response was received. Up to two email attempts were made per program over a two-week period to obtain unavailable data, such as Graduate Management Admission Test (GMAT) requirements and the integration of business training during core and elective medical rotations. If no response was received after two email attempts, one phone call was placed, and a voicemail message was left if there was no answer. If no response was received after all three attempts, the corresponding variables were recorded as “unknown” during data extraction and labeled as “data not available” during analysis.

For each analysis, all MD/MBA programs identified were retained in the analysis even when some variables had data not available. Denominators for each variable reflect the number of programs with available data, and the proportion of data not available is reported in the corresponding tables. No imputation was performed.

This cross-sectional study was deemed exempt from review by the Institutional Review Board at the University of Miami. All statistical analyses were performed using Python version 3.12.8 for macOS (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, Delaware). For categorical variables, contingency tables were used to summarize the data, and statistical significance was assessed using a chi-square (or Fisher’s exact when n < 10) test. A single p-value was reported for each comparison, representing the association between the two variables across the two-groups. Statistical significance was determined based on the standard alpha level of 0.05. One p-value is reported per variable.

Results

2022 vs. 2025 MD/MBA programs

There were 93 total MD/MBA programs in 2025 compared to 92 total MD/MBA programs in 2022 [3]. The majority of MD/MBA programs offered were 5-year combined programs for both 2022 (80.2%) and 2025 (85.0%). Several program characteristics differed between 2022 and 2025 (Table 1).

Significant differences included an increase in the percentage of MD/MBA programs requiring a full-time MBA credit load, from 9.1% in 2022 to 37% in 2025 (p < 0.001). Additional significant changes included a decrease in GMAT requirements for MD/MBA admission from 33.7% to 14.7% (p = 0.01), reduced acceptance of MBA credits toward medical school curriculum requirements from 82.1% to 30.6% (p < 0.001), decreased acceptance of medical school credits toward MBA curriculum requirements from 88% to 71.4% (p = 0.049), and a decline in fee waiver availability for MBA applications to MD/MBA programs from 43.8% to 6.9% (p = 0.006).

Characteristics without statistically significant differences included medical school ownership type, MD and MBA degrees offered at the same institution, MBA application timing, MBA internship requirements, and USMLE completion before MBA matriculation (Table 1).

4-Year vs. 5-Year MD/MBA programs (2025)

There were 12 four-year MD/MBA programs and 81 five-year MD/MBA programs in 2025. Several program characteristics differed between 4-year and 5-year programs (Table 2).

Significant differences included a higher proportion of 4-year programs offering an MBA specialization (83.3%) compared to 5-year programs (14.5%, p < 0.001), most commonly in health care management. Additionally, all 4-year programs required fewer than 50 MBA credit hours, whereas 33.9% of 5-year programs required 50 or more credit hours (p = 0.03).

Characteristics without statistically significant differences included medical school ownership type, MD and MBA degrees offered at the same institution, alignment of MBA requirements with full-time MBA programs, MBA application timing, GMAT requirements, acceptance of MBA credits toward medical school requirements, acceptance of medical school credits toward MBA requirements, MBA internship requirements, USMLE completion before MBA matriculation, and MBA application fee waivers (Table 2).

4-Year MD/MBA program comparisons (2025)

Only 12 of 93 (12.9%) combined MD/MBA programs in the United States offered a 4-year MD/MBA program in 2025 (Tables 3 and 4).

Among 4-year programs, 91.7% were offered at public institutions and 8.3% at private institutions. The MD and MBA degrees were offered at the same institution in 91.7% of programs and at different institutions in 8.3% of programs. Most programs (75%) offered only a 4-year MD/MBA option, whereas 25% of programs also offered a 5-year option. MBA internships were not required in 90% of programs.

Regarding admissions timing, 50% of programs required MBA admission prior to MD matriculation and 0% of programs required the GMAT. MBA coursework modalities included online-only (41.7%), in-person only (25%), and hybrid formats (33.3%). Integration of MBA coursework with the MD curriculum during core medical rotations occurred in 44.4% of programs, whereas 55.6% did not require MBA coursework during this period. During elective medical rotations, 88.9% of programs continued MBA coursework and 11.1% did not.

Discussion

In 2025, there were 93 total MD/MBA programs offered, compared to 92 in 2022 [3]. While the total number of identified MD/MBA programs in this study remains similar with the findings of Laditi et al., two programs were discontinued since 2022, Tufts University [7] and Cornell University [8]. Among the programs discontinued, Tufts University, which previously offered a 4-year MD/MBA program, stopped accepting applications for the 2024 cycle due to the increased complexity of its new MD program [7], including integrated organ-system courses that combine basic and clinical sciences [9] and expanded coursework in health justice, health equity, and social determinants of health [7, 10]. In 2023, Cornell University discontinued its 5-year MD/MBA program to reallocate resources toward its 1-year Tech MBA program, which operates independently from the College of Medicine [8]. Together, these program closures may reflect institutional decisions influenced by curriculum priorities and resource allocation.

Beyond individual program closures, broader trends have emerged over the past three years for MD/MBA programs, including a significant increase in MD/MBA programs requiring the same credits as a full-time MBA for graduation (9.1% in 2022, to 37% in 2025, p < 0.001). This trend may indicate a shift toward increased standardization and rigor in business education within MD/MBA programs, potentially influenced by evolving accreditation standards that emphasize rigorous quality assurance processes, such as the Association to Advance Collegiate Schools of Business (AACSB) accreditation [11, 12]. It may also reflect efforts to address gaps in traditional MD curricula, which often place less emphasis on management, leadership, and teamwork in the healthcare system [13]. Meanwhile, the GMAT requirement for MD/MBA admission has significantly declined to 14.7% of MD/MBA programs in 2025, compared to 33.7% in 2022 (p = 0.01). While this change may seem to contrast the increasing rigor of business education, it may instead reflect a broader trend in higher education away from standardized testing requirements. This trend became more visible during the COVID‑19 pandemic when widespread exam cancellations and modifications prompted educators to question the efficacy of standardized exams as measures of achievement [14]. This shift may also indicate that the MCAT, required for all medical school admissions, represents only one component of the admissions process rather than the sole indicator of an applicant’s potential success. While MCAT scores generally predict student performance better than undergraduate GPAs, using both MCAT scores and GPAs together provide improved predictions for a student’s likely success in medical school rather than using either metric alone [15]. This highlights that admissions decisions consider multiple metrics rather than relying solely on standardized test scores.

As MD/MBA programs continue to evolve, different trends in curricular offerings between 4-year and 5-year programs have emerged. When comparing 4-year (n = 12) and 5-year MD/MBA programs (n = 81), a significantly higher proportion of 4-year programs offered an MBA specialization (83.3%) compared to 5-year programs (14.5%), p < 0.001. This may suggest that 4-year programs are more likely to tailor their business curricula to specific fields, such as healthcare management, allowing students to develop targeted expertise within a condensed timeframe. This is consistent with the findings that all 4-year programs have less than 50 MBA credit requirements and are more likely to have different MBA credit requirements from full-time MBA programs (88.9%) compared to 5-year programs (59.4%), p = 0.14. The extended duration of 5-year programs may provide a more generalized business education, which can be attributed to the longer duration of study, allowing for broader coursework and greater flexibility in elective choices.

MD/MBA programs are offered by 57.1% (n = 93) of all MD programs listed in the AAMC MSAR database in the United States (n = 163) as of January 2025, and 12.9% (n = 12) of those 93 MD/MBA programs offer a 4-year MD/MBA structure [5]. Given this limited availability, it is important to investigate the design and structure of these 4-year programs. The 4-year combined program allows students to earn their MBAs in less time, eliminating an extra year typically required by the traditional 5-year model. This accelerated structure can reduce students’ opportunity cost of time spent out of the workforce and allow students to start their careers sooner [4]. The majority of the 4-year combined programs are offered at public institutions (91.7%), with 75% (n = 9) of programs exclusively providing a 4-year track, rather than a 4- or 5-year track. Additionally, different modalities of MBA classes are offered depending on the 4-year program: online-only (41.7%, n = 5), in-person only (25%, n = 3), and hybrid (33.3%, n = 4). This may reflect a broader trend in higher education, where online and hybrid formats are becoming more common, with hybrid models showing higher student satisfaction rates compared to traditional face-to-face learning models [16]. Program structures also varied, with most 4-year MD/MBA programs not requiring MBA coursework during core medical rotations (55.6%, n = 5), while the majority required MBA classes during elective medical rotations (88.9%, n = 8).

Limitations of this study included reliance largely on online web-based methods for data compilation, as certain program characteristics were unavailable online. Additionally, methodological differences between our 2025 analysis and the 2022 study by Laditi et al. [3] may affect direct comparisons between findings. Specifically, institutions with multiple campuses and separate AAMC MSAR listings were counted as distinct programs in our study compared to Laditi et al. [3]. Furthermore, programs offering both 4- and 5-year MD/MBA tracks were classified within the 4-year category in our study, which differed from the categorization approach used by Laditi et al. [3]. These methodological differences may affect direct comparisons of total program counts across studies.

Currently, there is no standardized way to compare different characteristics of MD/MBA programs. While there are several websites that display lists of MD/MBA programs, we found that all publicly accessible lists were either incomplete or out-of-date [5, 6, 17]. Furthermore, these lists do not provide specific information about MD/MBA program attributes, instead providing links to individual school websites. This approach is consistent with other dual-degree programs, such as MD/MPH and MD/PhD programs, where publicly available resources primarily provide links to individual school websites rather than detailed program attributes [5, 18]. This illustrates the need to establish a standardized MD/MBA program database that could provide transparent and comparable information on MD/MBA programs, such as program structure, duration, curricular integration, tuition costs, financial aid availability, graduate outcomes, dropout rates, and alumni career trajectories. Creation of this database could benefit both medical schools and students by improving access to program information and fostering collaboration across institutions. Building awareness of MD/MBA program availability may facilitate the development of physician-leaders by helping prospective students identify programs that align with their backgrounds and goals. In addition to fostering leadership skills, these programs may also equip future clinicians with practical business tools, such as financial management, marketing, and strategic decision-making, that are needed to effectively manage and operate their own practices or lead health care organizations [19–21].

Conclusions

This study found that while the total number of MD/MBA programs remained stable from 2022 to 2025, program structures had significant changes. More programs now require full-time MBA credit loads and fewer programs require the GMAT for admission. Compared to 5-year programs, 4-year programs are more likely to offer healthcare-focused MBA specializations, require fewer credits, and be offered at public institutions. These findings underscore how MD/MBA programs are adapting to meet emerging educational and leadership needs. As the demand for physician-leaders continues to grow, driven by rapid political and regulatory shifts in healthcare, it is increasingly imperative to equip physicians with leadership and administrative expertise [13, 22]. Readily accessible information on MD/MBA programs is essential for aligning medical education with evolving healthcare leadership needs. By examining program structures, requirements, and focus areas, this study presents the areas of opportunity for how MD/MBA programs can prepare physicians for policy engagement, decision-making, and management, ultimately shaping healthcare at institutional, state, national, and international levels.

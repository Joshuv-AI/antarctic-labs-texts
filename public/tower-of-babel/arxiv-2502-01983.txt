Lemma 3.1

\frac{1}{a+b}\langle a,b\rangle=H\left(\frac{a}{a+b}\right)=H\left(\frac{b}{a+b}\right).

In particular, when
a=b
, then

(9.2)

\langle a,a\rangle=2aH\left(\frac{1}{2}\right),

or equivalently,

\frac{1}{2a}\langle a,a\rangle=H\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\quad\mbox{ or }\quad\frac{1}{2}\langle 1,1\rangle=H\left(\frac{1}{2}\right).

Although this is an interesting observation, length or norm does not make sense since the formal pair
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
of symbols is not linear. Perhaps there may be other interesting properties about Cathelineau’s vector space that could easily be observed from the diagrammatics.

Second proof.

Let

Lemma 10.3
.

We have

\dfrac{\langle b\rangle}{\langle a\rangle}=a\ast\left\langle\dfrac{b}{a}\right\rangle
.

Proof.

The left hand side shows

\displaystyle\dfrac{\langle b\rangle}{\langle a\rangle}=\dfrac{b(1+(1-b)t)}{a(1+(1-a)t)}=\dfrac{b}{a}\left(1+((1-b)-(1-a))t\right)=\dfrac{b}{a}(1+(a-b)t).

On the other hand, the right hand side shows

\displaystyle a\ast\left\langle\dfrac{b}{a}\right\rangle=a\ast\left(\frac{b}{a}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{b}{a}\right)t\right)\right)=\frac{b}{a}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{b}{a}\right)at\right)=\frac{b}{a}\left(1+\frac{a-b}{a}at\right)=\frac{b}{a}(1+(a-b)t).

∎

Lemma 10.4
.

The relation

\frac{1-\langle b\rangle}{1-\langle a\rangle}=(a-1)\ast\left\langle\dfrac{1-b}{1-a}\right\rangle

holds.

Proof.

On the left hand side, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\langle b\rangle}{1-\langle a\rangle}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-b(1+(1-b)t)}{1-a(1+(1-a)t)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-b-b(1-b)t}{1-a-a(1-a)t}\cdot\dfrac{1-a+a(1-a)t}{1-a+a(1-a)t}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(1-a)(1-b)+(a(1-a)(1-b)-(1-a)b(1-b))t}{(1-a)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\cancel{(1-a)}(1-b)+(a-b)\cancel{(1-a)}(1-b)t}{(1-a)^{\cancel{2}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-b}{1-a}\left(1+(a-b)t\right)

while on the right hand side, we have

\displaystyle(a-1)\ast\left\langle\dfrac{1-b}{1-a}\right\rangle=(a-1)\ast\frac{1-b}{1-a}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{1-b}{1-a}\right)t\right)=\frac{1-b}{1-a}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{1-b}{1-a}\right)(a-1)t\right),

which are indeed equal.
∎

Lemma 10.5
.

We have
1-\langle a\rangle^{-1}=(1-a^{-1})(1-t)
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle 1-\langle a\rangle^{-1}

\displaystyle=1-a^{-1}(1+(1-a)t)^{-1}=1-a^{-1}(1-(1-a)t)

\displaystyle=1-a^{-1}+a^{-1}(1-a)t=1-a^{-1}+(a^{-1}-1)t

\displaystyle=(1-a^{-1})(1-t),

the lemma holds.
∎

Lemma 10.6
.

The equation

\frac{1-\langle b\rangle^{-1}}{1-\langle a\rangle^{-1}}=\frac{1-b^{-1}}{1-a^{-1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1-\langle b\rangle^{-1}}{1-\langle a\rangle^{-1}}=\frac{(1-b^{-1})\cancel{(1-t)}}{(1-a^{-1})\cancel{(1-t)}}=\frac{1-b^{-1}}{1-a^{-1}}.

11.
Future Work

It would be interesting to extend the work of Im–Khovanov of diagrammatic interpretation in
[
IK24a
]
to Rényi entropy, which is a generalization of Shannon entropy, von Neumann entropy, which is a quantized analogue of Gibbs entropy, and relative entropy, which is also known as Kullback–Leibler divergence. There may be a deeper interpretation if one were to extend the diagrammatics of entropy to their higher-dimensional analogues.

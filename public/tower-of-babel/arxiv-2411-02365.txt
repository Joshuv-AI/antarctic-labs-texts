Problems in additive number theory, VI: Sizes of sumsets of finite sets Thanks: Supported in part by PSC-CUNY Research Award Program grant 66197-00 54.
Melvyn B. Nathanson

Abstract
. In the study of sums of finite sets of integers, most attention has been paid to sets with small sumsets (Freiman’s theorem and related work) and to sets with large sumsets (Sidon sets and B h B_{h} -sets). This paper focuses on the full range of sizes of h h -fold sums of a set of k k integers. Many new results and open problems are presented.

1.
A remark of Erdős and Szemerédi

Let
\mathbf{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
be the set of positive integers,
\mathbf{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}

the set of nonnegative integers, and
\mathbf{Z}
the set of all integers.
In this paper, the letters
h,i,j,k,\ell,m
, and
n
always denote positive integers.

For real numbers
u
and
v
, the
interval of integers
from
u
to
v
is the set

[u,v]=\{c\in\mathbf{Z}:u\leq c\leq v\}.

The half-open, half-closed intervals
(u,v]
and
[u,v)
are defined similarly.

Let
G
be an additive abelian group
and let
\mathcal{F}^{*}(G)
be the set of all nonempty finite subsets of
G
.
For every integer
h
and every nonempty subset
A
of
G
,
the
h
-fold
sumset
of
A
, denoted
hA
,
is the set of all sums of
h
not necessarily distinct elements of
A
:

hA=\underbrace{A+\cdots+A}_{\text{$h$ summands}}=\left\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}:a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i\in[1,h]\right\}.

The
h
-fold
restricted sumset
of
A
, denoted
\widehat{hA}
,
is the set of all sums of
h
distinct elements of
A
:

\displaystyle\widehat{hA}

\displaystyle=\left\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}:a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i\in[1,h]\text{ and }a_{i}\neq a_{j}\text{ for all }i\neq j\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{s\in S}s:S\subseteq A\text{ and }|S|=h\right\}

where
|S|
denotes the size (or cardinality) of the set
S
.
The sumset
hA
and the restricted sumset
\widehat{hA}
are finite if
A
is finite.

In a paper on the sum-product conjecture,
Erdős and Szemerédi
[
9
]
wrote:

Let
2n-1\leq t\leq\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}
. It is easy to see that one can find a sequence
of integers
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{n}
so that there should be exactly
t
distinct integers
in the sequence
a_{i}+a_{j},1\leq i\leq j\leq n
.

This is a statement about the sizes of 2-fold sumsets of finite sets of integers.
The purpose of this paper is to investigate the sizes of
h
-fold sumsets
and
h
-fold restricted sumsets of finite subsets of the integers and,
more generally, of additive abelian groups for all
h\geq 2
.

The size and structure of small
h
-fold sumsets and small
h
-fold
restricted sumsets for finite subsets
of the integers and of finite fields have been extensively investigated
(cf.
[
1
]
–
[
23
]
),
but there has been considerably less study of the set of
all

h
-fold sumsets and
h
-fold restricted sumsets
of finite subsets of an abelian group
G
.
We consider the
range of cardinalities of
h
-fold sumsets

and
h
-fold restricted sumsets

of sets of size
k
in
G
.
For all positive integers
h
and
k
, define

\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)=\left\{\left|hA\right|:A\subseteq G\text{ and }|A|=k\right\}.

Thus,

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)=\left\{\left|\widehat{hA}\right|:A\subseteq G\text{ and }|A|=k\right\}.

There is a simple symmetry for restricted sumsets.
Let
|A|=k
and
\Lambda=\sum_{a\in A}a
. Let
h\in[1,k-1]
.
If
S\subseteq A
and
|S|=h
, then

b=\sum_{a\in S}a\in\widehat{hA}.

We have
|A\setminus S|=k-h\in[1,h-1]
and

\sum_{a\in A\setminus S}a=\Lambda-b\in\widehat{(k-h)A}.

Thus,
\Lambda-\widehat{hA}=\widehat{(k-h)A}
and

\left|\widehat{hA}\right|=\left|\widehat{(k-h)A}\right|
.
It follows that

(1)

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(k-h,k).

We also observe that
h>k
implies
\widehat{hA}=\emptyset

and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)=\{0\}
.

Problem 1
.

An open problem in additive number theory is to compute the range of sumset sizes
\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)

and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)
.

2.
Upper and lower bounds for sumset sizes in groups

There are simple best possible upper and lower bounds for the sets

\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)
in an additive abelian group
G
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be an additive abelian group. For all integers
h\geq 2
and
k\geq 2
,
there are best possible upper bounds

(2)

\max\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)\leq\binom{k+h-1}{h}

and

(3)

\max\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)\leq\binom{k}{h}

and best possible lower bounds

(4)

\min\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)\geq k

and

(5)

\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)\geq\max(k-h+1,h+1).

Moreover, for all
k\geq 1
,

(6)

\mathcal{R}_{G}(1,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(1,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(k-1,k)=\{k\}

and, for all
h\geq 1
,

(7)

\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,1)=\{1\}\qqand\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,1)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $h=1$}\\
0&\text{if $h\geq 2$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
A
be a subset of
G
with
|A|=k
.
For all positive integers
h
, we have the combinatorial upper bounds

|hA|\leq\binom{k+h-1}{h}\qqand\left|\widehat{hA}\right|\leq\binom{k}{h}.

These upper bounds are best possible.
For example, in the additive group
\mathbf{Z}
,
for every integer
g\geq h+1
, every set
A
of
k
distinct powers of
g

gives the upper bounds in inequalities (
2
)
and (
3
)
(cf. Nathanson
[
23
]
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.9).
Another example: For all
i\in[1,k]
, let
m_{i}>h
and let
G=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{Z}/m_{i}\mathbf{Z}
.
The set

A=\{(0,\ldots,0,1+m_{i}\mathbf{Z},0,\ldots,0):i\in[1,k]\}

also gives the upper bounds in (
2
) and (
3
) .

For the lower bounds for sumsets
hA
, we observe that if
G
is a group
and if
A\subseteq G
with
|A|=k
,
then, for all
a,a_{0}\in A
, we have

(h-1)a_{0}+a\in hA

and so

k=\left|\{(h-1)a_{0}+a:a\in A\}\right|\leq|hA|.

This proves (
4
).
For every finite subset
A
of the group
G
with
|A|=k
,
we have
|hA|=k
for some
h\geq 2
if and only if
A

is a coset of a subgroup of
G
of order
k
.
(This is a known result in group theory. For completeness,
a proof is in Appendix
A
.)
It follows that the lower bound (
4
) is best possible.

Next, we compute lower bounds for restricted sumsets
\widehat{hA}
for
h\leq k
.

If
h\in[1,k-1]
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{h-1}
are distinct elements of
A
, then

\left\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h-1}+a:a\in A\setminus\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{h-1}\}\right\}\subseteq\widehat{hA}

and so

\left|\widehat{hA}\right|\geq k-h+1

and

\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)\geq k-h+1.

The symmetry (
1
) implies

\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)=\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(k-h,k)\geq k-(k-h)+1=h+1

and so

\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)\geq\max(k-h+1,h+1).

This proves (
5
).

Identities (
6
) and (
7
)
follow immediately from the definitions of the sumset size sets.
This completes the proof.
∎

Consider the group
\mathbf{Z}
.
It follows from Theorem
1
that, for all positive integers

h
and
k
, the sumset size set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)

is finite, and so, for all
t\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
,
there is a set
A
with
\min(A)=0
such that
|A|=k
and
|hA|=t
.
Thus, there is an integer
N=N(h,k)
such that, for all
t\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
,
there is a set
A\subseteq[0,N]
such that
|A|=k
and
|hA|=t
.

Problem 2
.

Compute an integer
N(h,k)
with this property. Compute the least integer with this property.

Problem 3
.

Compute the analogous integers
\widehat{N}(h,k)
for the restricted sumset size set
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
.

Problem 4
.

Let
G
be an ordered abelian group or a group of unbounded exponent.
Let
h
,
k
, and
t
be positive integers
such that

hk-h+1\leq t\leq\binom{k+h-1}{h}.

Does there exist a subset
A
of
G
with
|A|=k
and
|hA|=t
?
This is a decision problem in the complexity class NP.
Is this problem NP-complete?

Problem 5
.

Let
G
be an ordered abelian group or a group of unbounded exponent.
Let
h
,
k
, and
t
be positive integers
such that
3\leq h\leq k
and

hk-h^{2}+1\leq t\leq\binom{k}{h}.

Does there exist a subset
A
of
G
with
|A|=k
and
\left|\widehat{hA}\right|=t
?
This is a decision problem in the complexity class NP.
Is this problem NP-complete?

3.
Ordered groups and groups of unbounded exponent

Our principal interest is sumset sizes of sets of integers,
but many results about sumset sizes of sets of integers depend only on the order
structure of the integers and extend to all ordered abelian groups.
Indeed, many proofs extend to groups that simply contain elements
of arbitrarily large finite order or elements of infinite order .

A multiplicative group
G
, not necessarily abelian, is
ordered

with respect to a total order
<
on
G

if, for all
x,y,z\in G
, the inequality
x<y
implies
xz<yz
and
zx<zy
.
A group
G
is
orderable
if
G
is an ordered group with
respect to some total order on
G
.
If
x\in G
and
x\neq e
, then either
e<x
or
e>x
.
If
e<x
, then
e<x<x^{2}<\cdots
.
If
x<e
, then
e>x>x^{2}>\cdots
. In both cases,
x
has infinite order.
Thus, every ordered group is torsion-free, but not
every torsion-free group is orderable.
Torsion-free abelian groups and torsion-free nilpotent groups are orderable (Glass
[
13
]
).

An additive abelian group
G
is
ordered
with respect to a total order
<
on
G

if, for all
x,y,z\in G
, the inequality
x<y
implies
x+z<y+z
.

An additive abelian group
G
has
bounded exponent
if
there is a positive integer
m
such that
mx=0
for all
x\in G
.
An abelian group
G
has
exponent
m
if
G
has bounded exponent and

m
is the smallest positive integer such that
mx=0
for all
x\in G
.
If
G
is a torsion group such that every element of
G
has order at most
M
,
then the exponent of
G
is bounded and is at most the least common multiple
of the orders of the elements of
G
.
The group
G
has
unbounded exponent
if it does not have bounded exponent.
A group that is not a torsion group contains an element of infinite order,
and so has unbounded exponent. Every ordered group is torsion-free
and so is of unbounded exponent. The group
G=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}

is a torsion group of unbounded exponent.

In the integers and in all ordered abelian groups and abelian groups of unbounded exponent,
we have lower bounds for the sizes of
h
-fold sumsets
and restricted sumsets that are better than the inequalities in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be an ordered abelian group.
For all positive integers
h
and
k
,
there are the lower bounds

(8)

\min\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)=hk-h+1

and

(9)

\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)=hk-h^{2}+1.

and the upper bounds

(10)

\max\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)=\binom{k+h-1}{h}

and

(11)

\max\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)=\binom{k}{h}

Proof.

Let
G
be an ordered group and let
A=\{a_{i}:i=1,\ldots,k\}

be a
k
-element subset of
G
with

a_{1}<\cdots<a_{i}<a_{i+1}<\cdots<a_{k}.

For all
i\in[1,k-1]
we have the strictly increasing sequences

ha_{1}<\cdots<ha_{i}<ha_{i+1}<\cdots<ha_{k}

and

ha_{i}<(h-1)a_{i}+a_{i+1}<(h-2)a_{i}+2a_{i+1}<\cdots<a_{i}+(h-1)a_{i+1}<ha_{i+1}.

These inequalities give the following lower bound for
\left|hA\right|
:

\left|hA\right|\geq h(k-1)+1=hk-h+1.

To obtain a lower bound for
\left|\widehat{hA}\right|
, we consider,
for all
i\in[1,k-h]
and
j\in[0,h]
,
the strictly increasing sequence of
h+1
elements

s_{i,j}
of the group that are obtained from the
(h+1)
-fold sum

a_{i}+a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{i+h-j-1}+a_{i+h-j}+a_{i+h-j+1}+\cdots+a_{i+h}

by deleting the summand
a_{i+h-j}
. Thus,

s_{i,j}=a_{i}+a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{i+h-j-1}+a_{i+h-j+1}+\cdots+a_{i+h}\in\widehat{hA}.

In particular,

s_{i,0}=a_{i}+a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{i+h-1}.

The inequalities

s_{1,0}<\cdots<s_{i,0}<s_{i+1,0}<\cdots<s_{k-h,0}<s_{k-h+1,0}=\sum_{j=1}^{h}a_{k-h+j}

and

s_{i,0}<\cdots<s_{i,j-1}<s_{i,j}<\cdots<s_{i,h}=s_{i+1,0}

imply the lower bound

\left|\widehat{hA}\right|\geq h(k-h)+1=hk-h^{2}+1.

Every arithmetic progression of length
k
in an ordered group
G

gives the lower bounds in inequalities (
8
)
and (
9
) and so these lower bounds are best possible.
This proves the lower bounds.
The upper bounds follow from Theorem
1
and its proof.
∎

The following simple but important remark allows us to transport results
about sumsets of the integers
to sumsets of any additive abelian group of unbounded exponent.

Theorem 3
.

If the abelian group
G
has unbounded exponent, then

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)

and

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k).

Proof.

Let
A
be a finite set of integers with
|A|=k
and let
h\geq 2
.
By translation, we can assume that
\min A=0
.
If
a=\max A
, then
ha=\max hA
. Because
G
has unbounded exponent,
there exists
x\in G
of order greater than
ha
,
and so the set
A_{G}=\{ax:a\in A\}
is a subset of
G
of size
k
.
Then

hA_{G}=\{bx:b\in hA\}\qqand\widehat{hA_{G}}=\{cx:c\in\widehat{hA}\}.

Because
x
has order greater than
ha
, we have
|hA_{G}|=|hA|

and
\left|\widehat{hA_{G}}\right|=\left|\widehat{hA}\right|
.
Thus,
t\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
implies
t\in\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)
and

t\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
implies
t\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

It is not necessarily true that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)=\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)

and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)
.
For example, let
h\geq 2
and
k\geq 2
.
Let

m=hk-h

and

G=(\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})\oplus\mathbf{Z}.

The group
G
has unbounded exponent and contains the subset

A=\left\{(i,0):i\in[0,k-1]\right\}

of size
k
, but

\displaystyle hA

\displaystyle=\{(j,0):j\in[0,hk-h]\}

\displaystyle=\{(j,0):j\in[0,m-1]\}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}\oplus\{0\}.

Thus,
|hA|=m=hk-h
and
m\in\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)
, but

\min\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)=hk-h+1
by Theorem
2

and so
m\notin\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
.

Similarly, let

m=hk-h^{2}

and

G=(\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})\oplus\mathbf{Z}.

The group
G
has unbounded exponent and contains the subset

A=\left\{(i,0):i\in[0,k-1]\right\}

of size
k
.
If
A_{0}=[0,k-1]
, then the sumset
hA_{0}
is the interval of integers

\widehat{hA_{0}}=\left[\frac{h(h-1)}{2},hk-\frac{h(h+1)}{2}\right]

of cardinality

\left|\widehat{hA_{0}}\right|=hk-\frac{h(h+1)}{2}-\frac{h(h-1)}{2}+1=hk-h^{2}+1=m+1.

It follows that

\displaystyle\widehat{hA}=\left\{(j,0):j\in hA_{0}\right\}=\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}\oplus\{0\}

and so
m=|\widehat{hA}|\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)
.
By Theorem
2
,

m=hk-h^{2}<hk-h^{2}+1=\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)

and so
m\notin\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
.
Thus,
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
is a proper subset of
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be an abelian group of unbounded exponent.
For all positive integers
h
and
k
,

(12)

\min\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)\leq hk-h+1

and

(13)

\min\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{G}(h,k)\leq hk-h^{2}+1.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorems
2

and
3
, applied to the ordered group
\mathbf{Z}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Sizes of 2-fold sumsets and 2-fold restricted sumsets

Let
G
be an additive abelian group of unbounded exponent.
We shall prove that, for every positive integer
k
and every integer
t

such that

2k-1\leq t\leq\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}

there is a
k
-element subset
A
of
G
with sumset size
|2A|=t
.
We also prove that, for every positive integer
k
and every integer
t
such that

2k-3\leq t\leq\frac{k^{2}-k}{2}

there is a
k
-element subset
A
of
G
with restricted sumset size

\left|\widehat{2A}\right|=t
.
By Theorem
3
, it suffices to prove these results for
the integers.

In every ordered abelian group, the range of sizes of 2-fold sumsets
and the range of sizes of 2-fold restricted sumsets are intervals of consecutive integers.
There is an exponential time algorithm to compute a set
A
with
|A|=k

and
|2A|=t
for all
t\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(2,k)
and
to compute a set
A
with
|A|=k

and
|\widehat{2A}|=t
all
t\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{Z}}(h,k)
.

These results do not necessarily hold in groups of bounded exponent.
Little is known about sumset sizes in these groups, even for the group

G=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
of exponent 2.

Theorem 5
.

For every ordered abelian group
G
and for all positive integers
k
,

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(2,k)=\mathcal{R}_{G}(2,k)=\left[2k-1,\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}\right].

Moreover, for all
t\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(2,k)
, there exists a set
A\subseteq\left[0,2^{k}-1\right]

such that
|A|=k
and
\left|\widehat{2A}\right|=t
.

Proof.

By Theorems
2
and
3
,
we have

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(2,k)\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{G}(2,k)

and

\min\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(2,k)=\min\mathcal{R}_{G}(2,k)=2k-1

and

\max\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(2,k)=\max\mathcal{R}_{G}(2,k)=\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}.

Proving that the set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{Z}}(2,k)
is an interval of consecutive integers
suffices to prove the Theorem.

If
A
is a set of
k
integers
with
|2A|=t
, then
k
and
t
satisfy inequality

2k-1\leq t\leq\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}.

For
k=1
, choose
A=\{0\}
. We have
2A=\{0\}
and
|2A|=1
,
and so
\mathcal{R}_{G}(2,1)=[1,1]
.

For
k=2
, choose
A=\{0,1\}
. We have
2A=\{0,1,2\}
and
|2A|=3
,
and so
\mathcal{R}_{G}(2,2)=[3,3]
.

For
k=3
, choose
A_{1}=\{0,1,2\}
and
A_{2}=\{0,1,3\}
.
We have
2A_{1}=\{0,1,2,3,4\}
with
|2A_{2}|=5

and
2A_{2}=\{0,1,2,3,4,6\}
with
|2A|=6
,
and so
\mathcal{R}_{G}(2,3)=[5,6]
.

Thus, Theorem
5
is true for
k=1
,
2
, and
3
.

For all
k\geq 4
, let
b
be an integer such that

k-1\leq b\leq 2k-3

and let

A=[0,k-2]\cup\{b\}.

Then
|A|=k
and

A\subseteq[0,b]\subseteq[0,2k-3]\subseteq\left[0,2^{k}-1\right].

Because
k-2<b\leq 2k-3
, we have

\displaystyle 2A

\displaystyle=[0,2k-4]\cup[b,b+k-2\}\cup\{2b\}

\displaystyle=[0,b+k-2]\cup\{2b\}

and so

|2A|=b+k.

This proves the theorem for

(14)

2k-1\leq t\leq 3k-3.

We use induction on
k
to prove the theorem for

3k-3\leq t\leq\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}.

Let
k\geq 4
and assume that Theorem
5
is true for
k-1
.
Let
s
be an integer such that

2k-3=2(k-1)-1\leq s\leq\frac{(k-1)^{2}+k-1}{2}=\frac{k^{2}-k}{2}.

By the induction hypothesis, there exists a set
B

Appendix A
On
|hA|=|A|

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be an additive abelian group and let
A
be a nonempty finite subset of
G
.
For all
h\geq 2
,

|hA|=|A|

if and only if
A
is a coset of a subgroup of order
|A|
.

Proof.

For all
x\in G
, the translation of the set
A
by
x
is the set

x+A=\{x+a:a\in A\}.

For all
h\geq 2
we have

h(x+A)=hx+hA

and so

|h(x+A)|=|hA|

By translation, we can assume that
0\in A
and so

A\subseteq 2A\subseteq 3A\subseteq\cdots\subseteq hA.

It follows that

|A|\leq|2A|\leq|3A|\leq\cdots\leq|hA|.

If
|hA|=|A|
, then

A=2A=3A=\cdots=hA.

In particular,
A=2A
and so
A
is closed under addition in the group
G
.
For all
x\in A
, we have

x+A\subseteq A+A=A.

Because
|x+A|=|A|
, we have
x+A=A
and so there exists
y\in A

such that
x+y=0
.
Thus,
A
is a subgroup of
G
.
This completes the proof.
∎

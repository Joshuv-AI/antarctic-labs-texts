Inappropriate antibiotic prescription for acute tonsillitis in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs): a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Antoine Bola Siafa, Emmanuel Choffor-Nchinda, Yves Christian Andjock-Nkouo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionTonsillitis is a self-limiting inflammatory process of the tonsils. In high-income countries, guidelines have been developed to decide if a patient needs an antibiotic or not. These guidelines help to reduce unnecessary antibiotic prescriptions and prevent the development of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). In low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), medical practitioners have the tendency to prescribe antibiotics in all cases of tonsillitis; there is an overprescription of antibiotics. These prescriptions are probably unjustified and seem to be responsible for the increased AMR seen in developing countries. The aim of this review will be to estimate the proportion of unjustified antibiotic prescriptions in LMICs.Methods and analysisWe will conduct a systematic review of all observational studies on the diagnosis and treatment of tonsillitis in LMICs. The selected database will include PubMed, Web of Sciences Global Index Medicus and the Grey literature. The search will include studies published from January 2012 until December 2024. Study selection will follow the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines, and bias will be assessed by a Risk of Bias Assessment Tool for observational studies by Hoy et al. A meta-analysis will be conducted for homogeneous studies, eventually using the random effect model. Subgroup analysis will include a period of study, study quality, countries and/or subregions of LMICs involved, study setting, age of participants, tools and definition used.Ethics and disseminationFormal ethical approval is not required, as primary data will not be collected. The results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presented at scientific conferences.PROSPERO registration numberCRD 42022384957Keywords: Anti-Bacterial Agents, Drug Utilization, OTOLARYNGOLOGY, Primary Health Care, Systematic Review

Introduction

Tonsillitis is defined as an inflammatory process of the tonsils and their surroundings.1 Tonsils are located in the lateral oropharynx, and as components of Waldeyer’s ring play a key role in the initial immunological defence against inhaled or ingested pathogens.2

Tonsillitis is a frequently observed condition seen in outpatient department and primary care practice. In most cases, tonsillitis (or pharyngotonsillitis or pharyngitis) is a benign and self-limiting disease due to viruses (40–80%) and in a few cases bacteria (15–35%). The bacteria causing tonsillitis include group A beta-haemolytic streptococci (GABHS) but also groups B and C beta-haemolytic streptococci,3 with GABHS being the most frequent and dangerous one because it can lead to complications such as peritonsillar abscess, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis and rheumatic fever. The diagnosis of tonsillitis is essentially based on the patient’s history and clinical signs.4 Since pharyngotonsillitis is most of the time a harmless condition (mainly in adults but also in children), strategies for its diagnosis and treatment are directed at distinguishing children with viral pharyngitis who do not need antimicrobial therapy from children with GABHS for whom antimicrobial therapy would be beneficial.5

It is difficult to differentiate viral from bacterial tonsillitis because clinically their characteristics are more or less the same. Several studies have tried to develop and implement clinical scores (CENTOR or Mac ISAAC)6 7 but none of them have brought out sufficient elements to separate bacterial from viral tonsillitis. However, there has been an increased usage of antibiotics to treat acute tonsillitis worldwide because they were found to have some relative benefits like reduction of the duration of the disease and protection against suppurative and non-suppurative complications in high-income countries, even if the absolute benefits are modest.8 The inappropriate use of antibiotics can lead to many side effects but is also suspected to increase bacterial resistance and treatment failure.9

From a review published in Cochrane in 2020, it is known that between 50% and 70% of pharyngitis cases are treated with antibiotics worldwide, despite the fact that the majority of them are of viral origin.10 It is therefore essential to reduce the inappropriate prescription of antibiotics whenever possible due to potential harms and side effects to patients but also an increase of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) against commonly used drugs.

It is difficult and challenging to differentiate between viral and bacterial tonsillitis. In many LMICs, the results of throat cultures could take 2 or more days to be available for the physician. Rapid diagnostic tests for GABHS have been developed to be used by primary care doctors.10 It has been shown that implementing rapid tests can reduce antibiotic prescription rates compared with clinical examination alone.10

In Africa and in most LMICs, tonsillitis is present and happens to be one of the common reasons why patients consult physicians in primary care and in emergency units. If in temperate climates, acute tonsillitis is commonly seen in winter and early spring,11 in Africa it can be prevalent at any time of the year. The situation in Africa is different from other parts of the world. The management of patients with tonsillitis is often not done based on national guidelines. Moreover, guidelines are scarce. One review published in 2020 revealed that only Egypt and South Africa had guidance for the management of acute tonsillitis.12 Whenever those guidelines exist, they are based on clinical criteria or they are a mirror of those developed in Europe and North America.13

In many developing countries, the absence of guidelines has led to antibiotics being prescribed inappropriately for patients presenting with sore throat. This overprescription of antibiotics is growing in LMICs and is thought to be responsible for the increased number of deadly drug-resistant infections.14 Perhaps, it is even one of the main drivers of AMR.14

AMR is becoming a public health problem and more importantly in Sub-Saharan Africa where according to a systematic review conducted in 2019,15 5 million deaths worldwide were associated with AMR, with 4 million of them coming from Sub-Saharan African countries. Knowing that AMR is a threat to public health, it is important to tackle all the main drivers of AMR, particularly in LMICs where misusage or overprescription of antibiotics is very common.

To the best of our knowledge, no systematic reviews have been conducted in LMICs addressing the overprescription of antibiotics to patients suffering from tonsillitis. That is why we decided to carry out this review but also to draw the attention of prescribers to the potential danger of this overuse of antibiotics, leading to AMR, especially with the WHO essential medicine list of antibiotics currently available in these countries.

Methods

This systematic review will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. This protocol is written and presented in compliance with the PRISMA-P 201516 guidelines. It is registered in PROSPERO (CRD 42022384957).

The study is planned to start on the 1 January 2025 and will end around December 2025.

Review question

« What is the proportion of patients diagnosed with tonsillitis in LMICs and who are receiving unjustified antibiotics treatment? »

P (Person): Patients living in LMICs and diagnosed with acute tonsillitis (pharyngitis or sore throat or oropharyngitis).

I (Intervention/exposure): Antibiotic treatment.

C (Comparison): Not applicable.

O (Outcome): Main: Proportion of patients receiving an inappropriate (unjustified or unnecessary) antibiotic treatment.

Secondary: Tools used to diagnose tonsillitis, definition(s) of inappropriate antibiotic(s) used.

T (Time): The search will be performed from the 1January 2012 till December 2024.

Operational definitions

Tonsillitis: In this study, tonsillitis will be referred to as any acute inflammation of the tonsils. It will encompass acute pharyngitis, sore throat, oropharyngitis, strept throat and throat angina.

Unjustified (inappropriate or unnecessary) antibiotic treatment (or use): Defined as such by the author(s) of the articles and focusing only on patients receiving antibiotics.

Information sources

We will retrieve our information from different data sources: PubMed, Web of Sciences and Global Index Medicus. References list from included studies will be scrutinised as well and eligible studies retrieved and added. Grey literature will be retrieved from the GreyLit and OpenGray databases.

Search strategy

A comprehensive and exhaustive search of databases will be performed to identify all relevant articles on the prevalence, diagnosis and treatment of tonsillitis in LMICs from 1 January 2012 till December 2024. Studies published in English and in French will be incorporated. The search strategy will comprise text words, keywords and MeSH terms, using the syntax of the respective databases, based on our PICO framework.

For PUBMED/MEDLINE, here is the search strategy that will be used:

(‘Acute tonsillitis’) OR (‘tonsillitis’ (MeSH Terms)) OR (‘acute pharyngitis’) OR (‘pharyngitis’ (MeSH Terms)) OR (‘sore throat’) AND (‘antibiotics’ (MeSH Terms)) OR (‘agents, antibacterial’ (MeSH Terms)) OR (‘agents, anti-infective’ (MeSH Terms)) AND (‘inappropriate antibiotic*’) OR (‘unjustified antibiotic*’) OR (‘unnecessary antibiotic*’).

Then we will use the following filters: Free full text; English and French (language restriction); from 2012 to 2024 (date restriction); observational studies;

finally, we will include only articles done in LMICs.

For Web of Sciences, the search strategy will be as follows:

‘Acute tonsillitis’ OR tonsillitis OR ‘acute pharyngitis’ OR pharyngitis OR ‘sore throat’ AND antibiotics OR ‘antibacterial agents’ OR ‘anti-infective agents’ AND ‘inappropriate antibiotics’ OR ‘unjustified antibiotics’ OR ‘unnecessary antibiotics’.

These filters will then be applied: dates from 2012 to 2024, free articles, observational studies, LMICs.

For Global Index Medicus, the following key words will be typed in the search box: acute tonsillitis, acute pharyngitis, sore throat, inappropriate antibiotics, unnecessary antibiotics, unjustified antibiotics, LMICs.

Study records

Data management and selection process

The reviewers (ABS, ECN and YCAN) will independently and systematically screen the titles and the abstracts of articles found through searching databases using Rayyan. Study characteristics will be assessed to match eligibility criteria. Subsequently, full-text articles will be retrieved if a study is found suitable, if an abstract is unavailable or when assessment of an abstract is found inconclusive. Moreover, this literature search will be supplemented by searching through the reference lists of relevant studies and other relevant review articles. This process will be reported using the PRISMA flow diagram.

Data extraction process with outcomes and prioritisation

Data extraction will be performed by the reviewers (ABS, ECN and YCAN) using a standardised pre-established form. Information about the following variables will be extracted: name of first author, title and other citation information, year of publication, country and/or region of LMICs where the study was carried out, study setting (hospital or community based), study design (cross-sectional, case–control), population characteristics (mean age, children, adults, gender proportions) and sample size. Data will also be extracted on the number of patients presenting tonsillitis, tools (Clinical scores, Rapid Diagnostic tests, Swab throat culture) used to diagnose tonsillitis, treatment of tonsillitis including antibiotic prescriptions (justified or not), definition(s) used to identify unjustified or inappropriate antibiotic treatment or prescriptions.

Risk of bias in individual studies

Studies will be included regardless of their internal validity and results of all included studies will be presented, along with an assessment of their quality. A risk of bias assessment will be done by two authors (ABS and ECN). The risk of bias will be classified as ‘low’ or ‘high’, using the risk of bias tool for prevalence studies proposed by Hoy et al17 for observational studies.

Data analysis, synthesis and confidence in cumulative evidence

Data will be analysed and synthesised using R. A summary flow diagram of included studies, as well as potential eligible studies and subsequently excluded studies will be drawn, with reasons for exclusion. Using a table, we will synthesise the main characteristics of each study included in the review. Patients diagnosed with tonsillitis, diagnostic tools, patients receiving antibiotics, patients receiving proper antibiotics and patients receiving unjustified antibiotic(s). The overall population characteristics in the pool will be depicted with the aid of a forest plot. Treatment strategies used according to the mentioned groups will be synthesised qualitatively. We will present the proportions of the various groups, taking into consideration all possible combinations of the treatment strategies. Definitions of inappropriate antibiotic treatment used by various authors will be brought out and discussed. A meta-analysis using the random-effect model will be done including studies that present sufficient homogeneity. If heterogeneity of included studies is such that a reliable analysis cannot be done, a meta-analysis will not be conducted. Statistical heterogeneity between and across studies will be assessed using the χ2 test on Cochrane’s Q statistic, quantified by calculating the I² statistic. Subgroup analyses will be performed and will comprise the following: period of study, study quality, African subregion involved, study setting (hospital or community based), age of participants (children/adults) and tools used. If the included studies are too heterogeneous to be pooled together, they will be summarised in a narrative format. Funnel plots will serve to assess publication bias, complemented with Egger’s test of bias. A value of p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Meta-bias

Potential limitations of our study include the fact that studies on the diagnosis and treatment of tonsillitis done in LMICs are mainly observational. So, we plan to include all eligible studies irrespective of the risk of bias and internal validity. This could have a negative impact on the quality of our results. However, we plan to evaluate the risk of bias in each individual study and appraise it in a narrative form, with the help of a table.

Dissemination plan

The results of this systematic review will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal for possible publication if accepted. They will also be disseminated during conferences and scientific meetings of general practitioners, paediatricians and otolaryngology surgeons.

Patient and public involvement

No patient involved.

DISCUSSION

This research will be one of the first systematic reviews on unjustified antibiotic prescriptions for acute tonsillitis in a low- and middle-income setting. Our study design will help to provide some level of evidence for decision-making regarding the management of tonsillitis in resource-limited settings. We will focus only on quantitative observational studies on inappropriate antibiotic(s) prescription. We will use the definition of inappropriate (or any synonym) used by the authors of those studies for patients receiving antibiotic(s). Our study will cover the period from 2012 to 2024 (more than 10 years).

Ethics and dissemination

Formal ethical approval is not required, as primary data will not be collected. The results will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed publication and presented at scientific conferences.

Amendments

This protocol is an amendment of a previous protocol submitted to PROSPERO with the registration number CRD 42022384957. Amendments were made to the title, the anticipated dates, the review questions, the search strategy and the analysis.

Gradient shrinking Ricci Solitons and Modified Sectional Curvature Thanks: E. Ribeiro Jr was partially supported by CNPq/Brazil [305128/2025-6] and FUNCAP/Brazil [ITR-0214-00116.01.00/23].
Xiaodong Cao, Ernani Ribeiro Jr, Hosea Wondo

Abstract
. We investigate four-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with positive modified sectional curvature. Our first main result shows that if the norm of the self-dual Weyl tensor and the scalar curvature satisfy a certain sharp pinching condition –closely related, in a precise sense, to that of Kähler metric –then the soliton is necessarily locally Kähler. We further obtain a characterization theorem and a weighted integral gap result for compact gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with bounded modified sectional curvature. In addition, we establish a Hitchin-Thorpe type inequality for compact four-dimensional Ricci solitons, providing new topological constraints on such manifolds.

[

Although many classification results for four-dimensional gradient Ricci solitons rely on the vanishing of the Weyl tensor
W,
it is natural to ask whether analogous conclusions can be obtained under weaker conditions, such as bounds on its norm or suitable curvature constraints. In this direction, Catino
[
15
]
proved that any complete four-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with nonnegative Ricci curvature satisfying

(1.2)

|W|R\leq\sqrt{3}\left(|\mathring{Ric}|-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}S\right)^{2}

must be conformally flat. Here,
\mathring{Ric}
denotes the traceless Ricci tensor and
S
the scalar curvature. The assumption of nonnegative Ricci curvature was later removed by
[
54
]
. In
[
58
]
, Zhang showed that any four-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with nonnegative and bounded Ricci curvature, and satisfying

|W|\leq\eta\left|\mid\mathring{Ric}\mid-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}S\right|,

for some constant
\eta<1+\sqrt{3}
, is either flat or has
2
-positive Ricci curvature. However, such classification results exclude the non-Einstein Kähler-Ricci soliton
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
endowed with the product metric (see
[
7
, Example 1]
). To incorporate this example, H.-D. Cao, Ribeiro and Zhou
[
7
, Theorem 1]
proved that a complete four-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying a suitable inequality controlling the norm of the self-dual Weyl tensor
W^{+}
is either Einstein, conformally flat, or isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.
Remarkably, no pointwise bound on the Ricci tensor is required. This result was later refined by
X. Cao, Ribeiro and Tran
[
11
]
by assuming that

(1.3)

|W^{+}|^{2}-\sqrt{6}|W^{+}|^{3}\geq\frac{\sqrt{6}}{6}|\mathring{Ric}|^{2}|W^{+}|.

Their proof employs the notion of curvature of the second kind and certain algebraic identities developed by X. Cao, Gursky, and Tran
[
10
]
in the resolution of a conjecture of Nishikawa.

In the first part of this article, we advance the classification program for four-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci solitons by deriving new rigidity results under a curvature pinching condition involving the self-dual Weyl tensor
W^{+}
. The motivation for this approach comes from a classical identity of Derdziński
[
22
]
, which asserts that any four-dimensional Kähler manifold, equipped with the natural orientation, satisfies the identity

|W^{+}|=\frac{S}{2\sqrt{6}}.

This exact relation between
W^{+}
and
S
reflects a deep interplay between complex geometry and conformal curvature decomposition in dimension four. Building on techniques developed in
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
, we formulate a natural Kähler-type pinching condition involving
W^{+}
and
S
, which forces a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton to be locally a Kähler-Ricci soliton, that is, Kähler after possibly pulling back to a double cover of
M^{4}
(see
[
35
]
). This result uncovers a new bridge between curvature inequalities and complex geometric structure in the Ricci soliton setting.

We now state our first main theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M^{4},\,g,\,f)
be a complete
4
-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying (
1.1
). If
(M^{4},\,g,\,f)
satisfies

(1.4)

\frac{S}{2\sqrt{6}}\leq|W^{+}|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(2-\frac{S}{2}\right),

then
(M^{4},\,g)
is locally a Kähler-Ricci soliton.

Remark 1
.

We note that both inequalities in condition (
1.4
) of Theorem
1
are attained as equalities for the Kähler-Ricci soliton
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
equipped with the product metric.

A key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1
is a Weitzenböck-type formula for the self-dual Weyl tensor
W^{+}
on four-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci solitons, established by X. Cao and Tran in
[
12

1.1
) must also satisfy

(1.5)

\Delta_{f}|W^{+}|^{2}=2|\nabla W^{+}|^{2}+4|W^{+}|^{2}-36\,\mathrm{det}\,W^{+}-\langle(\mathring{Ric}\odot\mathring{Ric})^{+},W^{+}\rangle,

where
\odot
stands for the Kulkarni-Nomizu product. This formula (

) also plays a fundamental role in the proofs of Theorems
2
,
3
, and
4
in the present article, and features prominently in

]

Motivated by the works of Gursky and LeBrun
[
30

and Berger
[
3

, X. Cao and Tran
[

introduced the notion of
modified sectional curvature

via the modified curvature tensor

\overline{R}:=R+\frac{1}{2}Hess\,f\odot g.

Using this notion, they obtained integral estimates for the norm of the Weyl tensor on four-dimensional compact Ricci solitons. In the present work, we combine the concept of modified sectional curvature with bounds on the scalar curvature to derive new characterization results for compact gradient shrinking Ricci solitons. In this context, we establish the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let

be an oriented

\overline{K}\geq\varepsilon:=1-\sqrt{\frac{268}{567}}\,(\approx 0.312)\,\,\,\hbox{ and }\,\,\,S\geq\delta:=\frac{360}{67}(1-\varepsilon)=\frac{360}{67}\sqrt{\frac{268}{567}}\,\,(\approx 3.694),

is isometric to either the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{4}
, or the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}.

The proof of Theorem

[

and Catino’s gap theorem
[

Theorem 3

Let
(M^{4},\,g,\,f)
be an oriented

) and

\int_{M}|\delta W^{+}|^{2}\,dV_{g}\leq\int_{M}\frac{S}{6}|W^{+}|^{2}\,dV_{g}.

If

then

\mathbb{S}^{4}

In the next part of the paper, motivated by the classical gap theorem of Gursky and LeBrun

Theorem 4

Let

\overline{K}\geq\varepsilon

2.
Background

In this section, we review basic background material and collect several identities that will play a fundamental role in the proofs of our main results.

We begin by recalling some fundamental curvature tensors on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},\,g)
of dimension
n\geq 3
. The first is the
Weyl tensor

W
, defined via the curvature decomposition formula:

(2.1)

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{ijkl}+\frac{1}{n-2}\big(R_{ik}g_{jl}+R_{jl}g_{ik}-R_{il}g_{jk}-R_{jk}g_{il}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{R}{(n-1)(n-2)}\big(g_{jl}g_{ik}-g_{il}g_{jk}\big),

where
R_{ijkl}
stands for the Riemann curvature tensor. It is well known that
W=0
in dimension
n=3.
The second one is the
Cotton tensor

C
given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle{C_{ijk}=\nabla_{i}R_{jk}-\nabla_{j}R_{ik}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\big(\nabla_{i}Rg_{jk}-\nabla_{j}Rg_{ik}).}

Notice that
C_{ijk}
is skew-symmetric in the first two indices and trace-free in any two indices. Moreover, for
n\geq 4,
we have

(2.3)

C_{ijk}=-\frac{(n-2)}{(n-3)}\nabla_{l}W_{ijkl}.

From now on, we restrict our attention to the case
n=4.
On an oriented Riemannian manifold
(M^{4},\,g),
the bundle of
2
-forms
\Lambda^{2}
can be decomposed into a direct sum

(2.4)

\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}\oplus\Lambda^{-}.

This decomposition is conformally invariant and allows us to decompose the Weyl tensor, viewed as an endomorphism of
\Lambda^{2}
, into components

(2.5)

W=W^{+}\oplus W^{-},

where
W^{\pm}:\Lambda^{\pm}M\longrightarrow\Lambda^{\pm}M
are called the
self-dual
part and
anti-self-dual
part of the Weyl tensor, respectively.

Fixing a point
p\in M^{4}
, we diagonalize
W^{\pm}
so that their respective eigenvalues are denoted by
\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{i},

1\leq i\leq 3.
In particular, we have

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\,\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,\,\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=0,&\\
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{3}\,\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,\,\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=0.&\end{cases}

Next, we recall the following Kato inequality.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(M^{4},\,g)
be an oriented four-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Then we have:

|\nabla|W^{+}||\leq|\nabla W^{+}|.

Equality holds if and only if there is a one-form
\nu
such that

\nabla W^{+}=\nu\otimes W^{+}.

We will also make use of the following algebraic inequality

(2.7)

\mathrm{det}\,W^{+}\leq\frac{\sqrt{6}}{18}|W^{+}|^{3}.

Moreover, equality holds in (
2.7
) if and only if
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{3}.

For completeness, we present here the proof of (
2.7
). From (
2.6
), we have

|W^{+}|^{2}=(\lambda_{1})^{2}+(\lambda_{2})^{2}+(\lambda_{3})^{2}\geq(\lambda_{1})^{2}+\frac{(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})^{2}}{2}=\frac{3}{2}(\lambda_{1})^{2}.

Consequently,

\mathrm{det}\,W^{+}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{1}\frac{(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})^{2}}{4}=\frac{1}{4}(\lambda_{1})^{3}\leq\frac{\sqrt{6}}{18}|W^{+}|^{3},

as claimed.

The curvature and topology of a compact
4
-dimensional manifold are related through the classical Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula

(2.8)

\chi(M)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\left(|W^{+}|^{2}+|W^{-}|^{2}+\frac{S^{2}}{24}-\frac{1}{2}|\mathring{Ric}|^{2}\right)dV_{g},

and Hirzebrush’s theorem

(2.9)

\tau(M)=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\left(|W^{+}|^{2}-|W^{-}|^{2}\right)dV_{g},

where
\chi(M)
and
\tau(M)
denote the Euler characteristic and the signature of
M^{4},
respectively; see
[
4
, Chapter 13]
for more details. It follows from (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) that every compact
4
-dimensional Einstein manifold must satisfy the Hitchin-Thorpe inequality
[
49
,
32
]
, namely,

(2.10)

\chi(M)\geq 1.5\,|\tau(M)|.

See also
[
4
, Theorem 6.35]
.

2.1.
Four-dimensional gradient Ricci solitons

From now on, let (
M^{4},\,g,\,f
) be a complete
4
-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying

(2.11)

Ric+Hess\,f=g.

Tracing the soliton equation (
2.11
) we get

(2.12)

S+\Delta f=4,

where
S
denotes the scalar curvature of
M^{4}.

We now collect some well-known identities for gradient shrinking Ricci solitons.

Lemma 2

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let (
M^{4},\,g,\,f
) be a
4
-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying (
1.1
). Then we have:

(1)

\nabla S=2Ric(\nabla f)
;

(2)

\Delta_{f}S=2S-2|\text{Ric}|^{2}
;

(3)

S+|\nabla f|^{2}=2f,
after normalizing;

(4)

\Delta_{f}R_{ij}=2R_{ij}-2R_{ikjl}R_{kl}.

Here,
\Delta_{f}:=\Delta\cdot-\nabla_{\nabla f}\cdot
denotes the drifted Laplacian.

In particular, one sees from assertion (2) of Lemma

that

(2.13)

\Delta_{f}S=2S-\frac{S^{2}}{2}-2|\mathring{Ric}|^{2},

where

(2.14)

|\mathring{Ric}|^{2}=|Ric|^{2}-\frac{S^{2}}{4}.

In
[
18
]
, Chen showed that any complete ancient solution to the Ricci flow has nonnegative scalar curvature, which implies that

for any complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton. Moreover,

is strictly positive unless
(M^{4},\,g,\,f)
is the Gaussian shrinking soliton (see
[
46
]
). Regarding the potential function

, H.-D. Cao and Zhou

]
proved that

(2.15)

\frac{1}{4}\Big(r(x)-c\Big)^{2}\leq f(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\Big(r(x)+c\Big)^{2},

for all
r(x)\geq r_{0},
where

is the distance function to a fixed point in

Additionally, they showed that every complete noncompact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton has at most Euclidean volume growth (see
[
8

). Moreover, these asymptotic estimates are optimal in the sense that they are achieved by the Gaussian shrinking soliton.

As previously mentioned, X. Cao and Tran
[

Proposition 1

(
[

]

Let (

\Delta_{f}|W^{+}|^{2}=2|\nabla W^{+}|^{2}+4|W^{+}|^{2}-36\,\mathrm{det}W^{+}-\langle(\mathring{Ric}\odot\mathring{Ric})^{+},W^{+}\rangle,

The Kulkarni-Nomizu product

which takes two symmetric

\displaystyle(A\odot B)_{ijkl}=A_{ik}B_{jl}+A_{jl}B_{ik}-A_{il}B_{jk}-A_{jk}B_{il}.

Let

\displaystyle\left(|\nabla|W^{+}||^{2}+t|\nabla|W^{-}||^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(|\nabla\left(|W^{+}|-t|W^{-}|\right)|^{2}+|\nabla\left(|W^{+}|+t|W^{-}|\right)|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\left(|W^{+}|-t|W^{-}|\right)|^{2}.

Combining these previous expressions, one obtains that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla W^{+}\right|^{2}+t^{2}\left|\nabla W^{-}\right|^{2}\right)e^{-f}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\left(\left|W^{+}\right|-t|W^{-}|\right)\right|^{2}e^{-f}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\int_{M}\left|\left(\left|W^{+}\right|-t|W^{-}|\right)\right|^{2}e^{-f}dV_{g}.

Now, for a solution to (
1.1
) in dimension four, we use Lemma
4
with
\sigma=1/3
in order to obtain
\lambda_{1}\geq 1
(not optimal for compact manifolds, see
[
53
]
). In this situation, one obtains that

(4.25)

\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla W^{+}\right|^{2}+t^{2}\left|\nabla W^{-}\right|^{2}\right)e^{-f}\,dV_{g}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|\left(\left|W^{+}\right|-t|W^{-}|\right)\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,dV_{g}.

Substituting (
4.2
) into (
4.25
) gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)\right)|W^{+}|^{2}-\left(2(1-\varepsilon)-\frac{\delta}{3}\right)\left(S-\frac{S^{2}}{4}\right)\right]e^{-f}dV_{g}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle t\int_{M}|W^{+}||W^{-}|e^{-f}dV_{g}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{2}\int_{M}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)\right)|W^{-}|^{2}-\left(2(1-\varepsilon)-\frac{\delta}{3}\right)\left(S-\frac{S^{2}}{4}\right)\right]e^{-f}dV_{g},

and by (
4.19
), one deduces that

(4.26)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left[(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)|W^{+}|^{2}+\phi(S)\right]e^{-f}dV_{g}

\displaystyle-t\int_{M}|W^{+}||W^{-}|e^{-f}dV_{g}

\displaystyle+t^{2}\int_{M}\left[(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)|W^{-}|^{2}+\phi(S)\right]e^{-f}dV_{g},

where

\phi(S):=\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{2}-\frac{\delta}{12}\right)S^{2}-\left(2(1-\varepsilon)-\frac{\delta}{3}\right)S.

Observe that the right-hand side of (
4.26
) defines a quadratic form
Q(t)
in
t.
In particular, its discriminant is given by

(4.27)

\displaystyle\Psi=|W^{+}|^{2}|W^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle-4(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)^{2}|W^{+}|^{2}|W^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle-4(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)|W^{-}|^{2}\phi(S)

\displaystyle-4(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)|W^{+}|^{2}\phi(S)

\displaystyle-4\phi(S)^{2}.

Hence, the quadratic form (
4.26
) is negative definite whenever

1-4(2\delta+12\varepsilon-10)^{2}<0,

which holds if
\frac{21}{2}<2\delta+12\varepsilon.
Moreover, when
t=0
, we observe that
Q(0)>0
, since the coefficient of
|W^{+}|^{2}
satisfies
2\delta+12\varepsilon-10>\frac{1}{2}
and
\phi(S)>0
for
S>\delta.
Therefore, the right-hand side of (
4.26
) is positive for all
t>0
, leading to a contradiction. Consequently, the reverse inequality in (
4.19
) must hold. So, the proof is completed.

∎

4.3.
Hitchin-Thorpe type inequality

Now, we shall present the proof of Theorem
5
, which we restate below for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 11

(Theorem
5
)
.

Let
(M^{4},\,g,\,f)
be a
4
-dimensional oriented compact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying (
1.1
) with
\overline{K}\geq\varepsilon=0.186.
Suppose that

(4.28)

\int_{M}|\delta W^{+}|^{2}\,dV_{g}\leq\int_{M}\frac{S}{6}|W^{+}|^{2}\,dV_{g}.

Then the following inequality holds:

(4.29)

\chi(M)>\frac{1}{0.4613}\tau(M).

Proof.

To begin with, we use (
4.4
) to infer

(4.30)

\int_{M}|Ric|^{2}dV_{g}=\int_{M}\frac{S^{2}}{2}dV_{g}-4Vol(M).

Substituting (
4.30
) and (
2.14
) into (
2.8
) yields

(4.31)

8\pi^{2}\chi(M)=\int_{M}\left(|W^{+}|^{2}+|W^{-}|^{2}\right)dV_{g}+2Vol(M)-\frac{1}{12}\int_{M}S^{2}dV_{g}.

On the other hand, it follows from (
2.20
) that a lower bound on weighted sectional curvature
\overline{K}\geq\varepsilon
yields

(4.32)

|W^{+}|^{2}+|W^{-}|^{2}\leq 24(1-2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})+\frac{2}{3}S^{2}-8(1-\varepsilon)S.

Upon integrating (
4.32
), we use (
4.31
) in order to obtain

(4.33)

8\pi^{2}\chi(M)<\frac{7}{12}\int_{M}\mathrm{~S}^{2}dV_{g}+2\left(12\varepsilon^{2}-8\varepsilon-3\right)Vol(M),

where we also make use of the fact that

\int_{M}S\,dV_{g}=4Vol(M),

which follows from (
2.12
). Besides, the scalar curvature upper bound, resulting from the lower bound on the modified sectional curvature, implies that

(4.34)

8\pi^{2}\chi(M)<\underbrace{(21(1-\varepsilon)^{2}+2\left(12\varepsilon^{2}-8\varepsilon-3\right))}_{\psi(\varepsilon)}Vol(M).

Retuning now to (
4.31
), we use (
2.9
) to obtain

(4.35)

\displaystyle 2\chi(M)+3\tau(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{M}|W^{+}|^{2}dV_{g}+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}Vol(M)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{48\pi^{2}}\int_{M}S^{2}dV_{g}

Next, by using (
4.28
), we apply
[
12
, Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.1]
to deduce

(4.36)

\int_{M}|W^{+}|^{2}dV_{g}>\frac{4}{11}\pi^{2}(2\chi(M)+3\tau(M))

Plugging this into (
4.35
), one sees that

\displaystyle 2\chi(M)+3\tau(M)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{M}|W^{+}|^{2}dV_{g}+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}Vol(M)-\frac{1}{48\pi^{2}}\int_{M}S^{2}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{11}(2\chi(M)+3\tau(M))+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}Vol(M)+\frac{3}{4\pi^{2}}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}Vol(M)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{11}(2\chi(M)+3\tau(M))+\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}-\frac{3}{4\pi^{2}}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\right)}_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}Vol(M).

From now on, we restrict our choice of
\varepsilon<1/3
so that
\gamma(\varepsilon)>0
and
\psi(\varepsilon)>0
. By using (
4.34
), one obtains that

(4.37)

\left(\frac{18}{27}-\frac{\gamma(\varepsilon)}{\psi(\varepsilon)}\frac{88\pi^{2}}{27}\right)\chi(M)>-\tau(M)

On the other hand, we reverse the orientation so that (
4.36
) applies to
|W^{-}|
. Proceeding as in the previous case, we then obtain

(4.38)

2\chi(M)-3\tau(M)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{M}|W^{-}|^{2}dV_{g}-\frac{1}{48\pi^{2}}\int_{M}S^{2}dV_{g}+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}Vol(M).

Besides, by using (
4.36
), (
2.20
) and (
4.34
), we get

(4.39)

\left(\frac{18}{39}-\frac{88\pi^{2}}{39}\frac{\gamma(\varepsilon)}{\psi(\varepsilon)}\right)\chi(M)>\tau(M).

Combining (
4.37
) and (
4.39
), we then obtain

(4.40)

-\left(\frac{18}{27}-\frac{\gamma(\varepsilon)}{\psi(\varepsilon)}\frac{88\pi^{2}}{27}\right)^{-1}\chi(M)<\tau(M)<\left(\frac{18}{39}-\frac{88\pi^{2}}{39}\frac{\gamma(\varepsilon)}{\psi(\varepsilon)}\right)\chi(M)

Finally, if
\varepsilon=0.184,
then
\gamma(\varepsilon)>0
and
\psi(\varepsilon)>0
. Therefore, (
4.40
) gives

-1.5008\chi(M)<\tau(M)<0.4613\chi(M),

In particular, we have

|\tau(M)|\leq 1.5008\chi(M).

This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

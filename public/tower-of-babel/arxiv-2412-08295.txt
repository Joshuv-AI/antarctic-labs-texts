Koszul Lie Algebras and their subalgebras
S. Blumer

Abstract
. This paper examines (restricted) Koszul Lie algebras, a class of positively graded Lie algebras with a quadratic presentation and specific cohomological properties. The study employs HNN-extensions as a key tool for decomposing and analysing these algebras. Building on a previous work on Koszul Lie algebras [ 4 ] , this paper also deals with Bloch-Kato Lie algebras, which constitute a distinguished subclass of that of Koszul Lie algebras where all subalgebras generated by elements of degree 1 1 have a quadratic presentation. It is shown that Bloch-Kato Lie algebras satisfy a version of the Levi decomposition theorem and that they satisfy the Toral Rank Conjecture. Two new families of such Lie algebras are introduced, including all graded Lie algebras generated in degree 1 1 and defined by two quadratic relations. Throughout the paper, we show many properties of right-angled Artin graded (RAAG) Lie algebras, which form a large class of Koszul Lie algebras.



Introduction

The notion of a Koszul algebra was introduced by Priddy
[
34
]
for non-negatively graded algebras and found applications in various mathematical disciplines, including algebraic geometry (
[
10
]
,
[
20
]
), representation theory (
[
2
]
), and combinatorial algebra (
[
16
]
,
[
38
]
).
By definition, a graded algebra
A=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
over a field
k
with
A_{0}=k\cdot 1_{A}
is Koszul if the trivial
A
-module
k
admits a linear free
A
-resolution. In particular, it can be given a presentation where generators and relations have degree
1
and
2
respectively, i.e., it is a quadratic algebra.
A remarkable fact of such algebras is that their cohomology rings are quadratic as well, and they can thus be easily computed in terms of generators and relations. In fact, there is a duality between Koszul algebras and their cohomology rings, which are also Koszul, providing a powerful tool for studying their properties. This phenomenon – generalizing the well-known duality between the symmetric and exterior algebras over a vector space – led Kempf
[
20
]
to use the term “wonderful rings” for designating these algebras.

Recently, Koszul algebras also appeared in the context of Galois cohomology (
[
31
]
,
[
33
]
) for it has been conjectured by Positselski that the Galois
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology ring of an absolute Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}=\Gal(\mathbb{K}_{s}/\mathbb{K})
of a field
\mathbb{K}
containing a primitive
p
th root of
1
is a Koszul algebra.
In fact, by the affirmative answer to the Bloch-Kato conjecture, such a cohomology (as well as that of any closed subgroup
H
of
G_{\mathbb{K}}
) is isomorphic with the
p
-reduced Milnor
K
-theory of the field
\mathbb{K}
(resp. of the fixed field
\mathbb{K}_{s}^{H}
) via the Galois symbol, and hence it is quadratic. Pro-
p
groups exhibiting this hereditary property in cohomology are known as Bloch-Kato pro-
p
groups (
[
35
]
).

A significant strengthening of Positselki’s Koszulity conjecture was then proposed by Minac et al. in
[
27
]
predicting that the cohomology of such groups is not just Koszul but universally Koszul, an enhanced version of Koszulity introduced (in the commutative setting) by Conca
[
10
]
.

If
G
is a finitely generated pro-
p
group, then by a result of Lazard’s
[
22
]
, the universal restricted enveloping algebra of the restricted Lie algebra associated to the Jennings-Zassenhaus filtration of
G
is naturally isomorphic to the associated graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
with respect to its augmentation ideal (see also
[
12
]
).
Moreover, a spectral sequence discovered by May
[
25
]
relates the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of
\operatorname{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
to the Galois cohomology of
G
. When
\operatorname{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
is a Koszul algebra, the spectral sequence collapses at the first page, leading to an isomorphism
H^{\bullet}(\operatorname{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G],\mathbb{F}_{p})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
(see
[
40
]
).

Weigel asked in
[
43
]
if
\operatorname{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
is Koszul whenever
G
is the maximal pro-
p
quotient of the absolute Galois group of a field containing a primitive
p
th root of
1
. Motivated by this question and by the Bloch-Kato conjecture, we were thus led to study quadratically defined (
p
-restricted) Lie algebras such that their
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology, as well as those of some of their subalgebras, is Koszul.
Lie algebras of this kind were introduced in the author’s paper
[
4
]
as the Bloch-Kato Lie algebras.
One of the aims of the present article is to explore this rather mysterious class of algebras.

The main question that would establish a connection between the group and Lie algebra theoretical Bloch-Kato notions is the following.

Question A
.

Is it true that the
p
-restricted Lie algebra associated to the Jennings-Zassenhaus filtration of a finitely generated Bloch-Kato pro-
p
group is Bloch-Kato?

Since the cohomology of a Bloch-Kato Lie algebra is universally Koszul, a positive answer to Question A implies Weigel’s strengthening of Positselski’s Koszulity conjecture, as well as the universal Koszulity conjecture.

Motivated by this connection, this paper explores the properties of quadratic, Koszul and Bloch-Kato Lie algebras over an arbitrary field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2
. However, the analysis will not explicitly refer to the underlying group theoretic framework.

In the first section, we introduce the key concepts of the Lie algebras under consideration and show that quadratic Lie algebras and quadratic
p
-restricted Lie algebras can be treated simultaneously, provided that
p
is an odd prime.

The second section is devoted to the study of quadratic Lie algebras using HNN-extension. We establish that every quadratic Lie algebra decomposes as the HNN-extension over certain subalgebras generated in degree
1
. This result implies that if the quadraticity property is inherited by all subalgebras generated in degree
1
of a quadratic Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
, then
{\mathscr{L}}
is Bloch-Kato, thereby simplifying the definition of this class of Lie algebras as it was introduced in
[
4
]
.

HNN-extensions can also be used in the opposite direction for producing quadratic Lie algebras containing a given one.

Theorem A
.

Any finitely presented graded Lie algebra embeds into a quadratic Lie algebra.

We conclude the section with two examples concerning the open problem of embedding finitely presented graded Lie algebras into Koszul ones.

The third section extends a result of
[
30
, Ex. 2, p. 22]
on nilpotent Lie algebras satsfying the Koszul property, by showing that also solvable Koszul Lie algebras are necessarily abelian. This result could also been proved by using a combination of
[
15
]
and
[
30
, Ex. 2, p. 22]
, while our proof does not require the theory of depth for Lie algebras, neither a growth argument.

Moreover, we provide some constraints on the size and degrees of the center of a Koszul Lie algebra.

Theorem B
.

The center of a Koszul Lie algebra of cohomological dimension
n
is a finite dimensional ideal of dimension at most
n
and it is concentrated in odd degrees
<\frac{n}{2}+1
.

For proving the result, we make use of the theory of eigenvalues of algebras, i.e., the inverses of the roots of the Poincaré polynomial, developed by Weigel
[
42
]
.

For Bloch-Kato Lie algebras, the situation is much simpler as the center is always concentrated in degree
1
, which allows one to decompose any such Lie algebra as a direct sum of its center and a centerless Bloch-Kato Lie algebra.
As a byproduct of this result, we could prove that Bloch-Kato Lie algebras satisfy the Toral Rank Conjecture (TRC), which is believed to hold for nilpotent Lie algebras.

Corollary A
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a Bloch-Kato Lie algebra whose center has dimension
z
, then

\dim H^{\bullet}({\mathscr{L}},k)\geq 2^{z}.

Following
[
15
]
, we study the radical of Koszul Lie algebras and prove that Bloch-Kato Lie algebras satisfy a decomposition of Levi-type.

Theorem C
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a Bloch-Kato Lie algebra with center
Z
, then
{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{M}\times Z
, where
\mathscr{M}
is a Bloch-Kato Lie algebra that is essentially simple.

In the last section, we present two new class of Bloch-Kato Lie algebras of cohomological dimension
2
and provide a characterization of the surface Lie algebras within the class of quadratic Lie algebras in terms of their standard subalgebras.

Theorem D
.

A non-free quadratic Lie
k
-algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
is the graded Lie
k
-algebra associated to the lower central series of the fundamental group of an oriented closed surface iff any subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
generated by elements of degree
1
is free.

1.
Preliminaries

A Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
over a field

{\mathscr{L}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{L}}_{i}
that is compatible with the Lie brackets. This means that for all

[{\mathscr{L}}_{i},{\mathscr{L}}_{j}]\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}_{i+j}

If

over

inherits a natural grading. For a homogeneous subspace

of

, we denote the quotient

\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{R}\right\rangle
. If
(v_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(r_{j})_{j\in J}
are homogeneous bases for

and

respectively, we also write

\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{R}\right\rangle=\left\langle{v_{i}:\ i\in I}\,\big|\,{r_{j}:\ j\in J}\right\rangle.

For an integer

, we denote by
V[n]
the vector space

with grading
(V[n])_{i}=V_{n+i}
. The
Hilbert series
of

is the formal series with non-negative coefficients

H_{V}(t):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim(V_{i})t^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t,t^{-1}]]

Example 1.1
.

If

A

A

For two graded left

The functor

One has a non-negative grading on these vector spaces

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{\bullet,j}(M,N)=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}^{i,j}_{A}(M,N),

We write

When

If

When

1.1.
Quadratic and Koszul Lie algebras

Henceforth, we will focus exclusively on Lie algebras, which constitute the algebraic structures of interest in this paper. For a comprehensive study of Koszul algebras, we refer the reader to the book by Polishchuk and Positselski
[
30
]
.

A graded Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
is called
standard
if
H^{1}({\mathscr{L}})
has finite dimension and
H^{1,j}({\mathscr{L}})=0
for
j\neq 1
. This amounts to saying that there exists a finite number of degree
1
elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
that generate
{\mathscr{L}}
as a Lie algebra, or equivalently,
[{\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}]=\bigoplus_{i\geq 2}{\mathscr{L}}_{i}
. Indeed, there is a graded isomorphism
H^{1,\bullet}({\mathscr{L}})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}_{k}({\mathscr{L}}/[{\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}],k)
.

If, moreover,
H^{2,j}({\mathscr{L}})=0
unless
j=2
, then the Lie algebra is said to be
quadratic
. In other words, a quadratic Lie algebra is the quotient of a standard free Lie algebra
\mathscr{F}
by an ideal generated by elements of
\mathscr{F}_{2}
.
The importance of quadratic Lie algebras comes from the fact that if
{\mathscr{L}}
is any (locally finite) graded Lie algebra, then there is a unique quadratic Lie algebra
\operatorname{q}\!{\mathscr{L}}
, the
universal quadratic cover
of
{\mathscr{L}}
, such that

\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i,i}({\mathscr{L}})\simeq\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i,i}(\operatorname{q}\!{\mathscr{L}}).

Moreover, such a diagonal cohomology is a quadratic algebra (see
[
24
]
,
[
30
]
). The Lie algebra
\operatorname{q}\!{\mathscr{L}}
can be explicitly obtained from a minimal presentation of
{\mathscr{L}}
in terms of generators and relations by neglecting the generators of degree
>1
and the relations of degree
>2
.

A locally finite graded Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
is said to be
Koszul
when
{H^{i,j}({\mathscr{L}})}
vanishes for all
i\neq j
. Such a Lie algebra is clearly quadratic, and its cohomology ring is a quadratic algebra, which can be computed explicitly (
[
24
]
). Indeed, if
{\mathscr{L}}
is any locally finite Lie algebra and
R
is the kernel of the multiplication mapping
{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\wedge{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\to{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}_{2}
, the orthogonal complement
R^{\perp}
of
R
in
{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\wedge{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
fits in the exact sequence

0\to R^{\perp}\to{\mathscr{L}}_{1}^{\ast}\wedge{\mathscr{L}}_{1}^{\ast}\to R^{\ast}\to 0,

and one has

{\mathscr{L}}^{!}:=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i,i}({\mathscr{L}})=\frac{{\Lambda_{\bullet}}({\mathscr{L}}_{1}^{\ast})}{{\Lambda_{\bullet}}({\mathscr{L}}_{1}^{\ast})\wedge R^{\perp}}

where we have denoted by
{\Lambda_{\bullet}}(V)
the exterior algebra on a vector space
V
concentrated in degree
1
. Clearly, Koszul Lie algebras are of type FP.

If
n
is a positive integer, then we say that a graded Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
is
n
-Koszul if
H^{i,j}({\mathscr{L}})=0
for all
i\leq j\leq n
. In particular,
2
-Koszul Lie algebras are exactly the quadratic Lie algebras, and Koszul Lie algebras are

3

A standard subalgebra of a quadratic (or even Koszul) Lie algebra needs not to be quadratic itself (Examples

Definition 1.2

A graded Lie algebra

(1)

(2)

It is worth noticing that the class of all BK Lie algebras is closed under taking both direct sums with standard abelian Lie algebras and free products of BK Lie algebras (

1.3.
Restricted Lie algebras

In case the characteristic

If

is graded and the map
(\hbox to6.45831pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt})^{[p]}

The usage of HNN-extensions is crucial for studying quadratic Lie algebras, due to the following fundamental, yet trivial, decomposition result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}

2.1.1.
Fröberg’s formula

For any Koszul (Lie) algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
, a distinguished formula holds,

\textbf{Fr\"{o}berg's formula}:\quad H_{{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}}(t)H_{{\mathscr{L}}^{!}}(-t)=1,

where
{\mathscr{L}}^{!}
is the diagonal cohomology of
{\mathscr{L}}
, which is in fact the whole cohomology . In 1995, two works by Roos
[
37
]
and Positselski
[
32
]
proved that algebras satisfying Fröberg’s formula must not be Koszul. However, the following result shows that under suitable assumptions, Fröberg’s formula implies Koszulity of the Lie algebra.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a Koszul Lie algebra with a cocyclic ideal
\mathscr{M}
. Then,
\mathscr{M}
is Koszul if, and only if, Fröberg’s formula holds for
\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

From the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt Theorem it follows that the graded object
\operatorname{gr}\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g})
associated with the canonical filtration of an ordinary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the symmetric algebra on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
. Plus, if
\mathfrak{g}={\mathscr{L}}
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded, then the Hilbert series of
{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}
equals that of
\operatorname{gr}\mathscr{U}({\mathscr{L}})
endowed with the grading induced by that of
{\mathscr{L}}
. In particular,

H_{{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}}(t)=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-t^{i})^{\ell_{i}}},

where
\ell_{i}=\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{i}
(see
[
30
, Ch. 2.2, Example 2]
).

Now, if
\mathscr{M}
is a cocyclic ideal of
{\mathscr{L}}
, then
\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{i}=\dim\mathscr{M}_{i}+\delta_{1i}
, for all
i\geq 1
, where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. Therefore,

H_{{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)}H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{M})}(t).

The long exact sequence (
1.1
) for
j=i\geq 0
reads

\displaystyle H^{i-1,i}({\mathscr{L}})\to H^{i-1,i}(\mathscr{M})\to H^{i-1,i-1}(\mathscr{M})

\displaystyle\to H^{i,i}({\mathscr{L}})\to

\displaystyle\to H^{i,i}(\mathscr{M})\to H^{i,i-1}(\mathscr{M})=0.

Since
{\mathscr{L}}
is Koszul, and since each space
H^{i,j}(\mathscr{M})
is finite dimensional, we recover the following formulae involving the bigraded Betti numbers:

b_{i,i}({\mathscr{L}})=b_{i-1,i-1}(\mathscr{M})+b_{i,i}(\mathscr{M})-b_{i-1,i}(\mathscr{M}).

In particular, the Hilbert polynomial of
{\mathscr{L}}^{!}
(i.e., the Poincaré polynomial of
{\mathscr{L}}
) is given by

\displaystyle H_{{\mathscr{L}}^{!}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}b_{ii}({\mathscr{L}})t^{i}=\sum_{i}(b_{i-1,i-1}(\mathscr{M})+b_{i,i}(\mathscr{M})-b_{i-1,i}(\mathscr{M}))t^{i}=

\displaystyle=(1+t)H_{(\operatorname{q}\!\mathscr{M})^{!}}(t)+tQ(t),

where we have put
Q=\sum_{i}b_{i,i+1}(\mathscr{M})t^{i}
.

Now, by Fröberg’s formula for
{\mathscr{L}}
, we get

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}(t)H_{{\mathscr{L}}^{!}}(-t)=\frac{1}{1-t}H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{M})}(t)\cdot\left((1-t)H_{(\operatorname{q}\!\mathscr{M})^{!}}(-t)-tQ(-t)\right)=

\displaystyle=H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{M})}(t)H_{(\operatorname{q}\!\mathscr{M})^{!}}(-t)-\frac{t}{1-t}H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{M})}(t)Q(-t).

It follows that, if the Fröberg formula holds for
\mathscr{M}
, then
Q(t)=0
, which in turn implies that
H^{i,i+1}(\mathscr{M})=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Eventually, from the proof of Corollary
2.7
, it follows that
H^{\bullet,\bullet}(\mathscr{M})
is concentrated on the diagonal, proving that
\mathscr{M}
is Koszul.
∎

2.2.
Quadratic embeddings

Lichtman and Shirvani
[
23
]
show that any Lie algebra embeds into a simple one. Nevertheless, in the graded case one cannot expect to achieve a similar result, as all non-abelian graded Lie algebras have proper ideals (e.g., the commutator subalgebra). However, a similar usage of HNN-extensions allows us to deduce that, under mild assumptions, all graded Lie algebras embed into quadratic ones.

Theorem 2.14
.

Every finitely presented graded Lie algebra can be embedded into some quadratic Lie algebra.

First of all, we show that one can get rid of the high-degree generators of a finitely generated, non-standard Lie algebra, and embed it into a standard one.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a finitely generated graded Lie algebra. Then,
{\mathscr{L}}
is a homogeneous subalgebra of a standard Lie algebra.

Proof.

Up to taking the direct product of
{\mathscr{L}}
with any standard Lie algebra, we may suppose that
{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\neq 0
.
Let
\{x_{i}^{n}:\ (i,n)\in I\}
be a minimal homogeneous generating system of
{\mathscr{L}}
, where
x_{i}^{n}\in{\mathscr{L}}_{n}
. We argue by induction on the maximal
N
for which there is some generator
x_{i}^{N}
of degree
N
.

If
N=1
, then
{\mathscr{L}}
is already standard.

Assume that
N>1
. Since
{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\neq 0
, pick
x\in{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\setminus\{0\}
and consider, for all
(i,N)\in I
, the derivations
\phi_{i}:\langle{x}\rangle\to{\mathscr{L}}
sending
x
to
x_{i}^{N}
; such maps are homogeneous of degree
N-1
. Then, the iterated HNN-extension
\mathscr{H}
of
{\mathscr{L}}
with respect to all the derivations
\phi_{i}
,
(i,N)\in I
, is a Lie algebra containing
{\mathscr{L}}
(by
[
23
]
) and generated by the elements
x_{i}^{n}
, for
(i,n)\in I
and
n<N
, and by the stable letters
t_{i}
of degree
N-1
. By induction,
\mathscr{H}
embeds into a standard Lie algebra, and hence so does
{\mathscr{L}}
.
∎

Notice that the standard Lie algebra
\mathscr{S}
containing
{\mathscr{L}}
, as constructed in Lemma
2.15
, satisfies
\dim H^{2,j}(\mathscr{S})=\dim H^{2,j}({\mathscr{L}})
for all
j\geq 3
, provided that
{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\neq 0
.
Indeed, if one puts
{\mathscr{L}}^{1}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi_{1}}({\mathscr{L}},t_{1})
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{i+1}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi_{i+1}}({\mathscr{L}}^{i},t_{i+1})
, then

\mathscr{S}=\bigcup_{i:(i,N)\in I}{\mathscr{L}}^{i}

and one has exact sequences

H^{1,j-N+1}(\langle{x}\rangle)\to H^{2,j}({\mathscr{L}}^{i+1})\to H^{2,j}({\mathscr{L}}^{i})\to H^{2,j-N+1}(\langle{x}\rangle)=0

for every
j
. If
j\geq N+1
, then
H^{1,j-1}(\langle{x}\rangle)=0
and hence
H^{2,j}({\mathscr{L}}^{i})\simeq H^{2,j}({\mathscr{L}}^{i+1})
. In particular,
\mathscr{S}
has the same number of relations of all degrees
\geq N+1
as
{\mathscr{L}}
, but more relations of degree
N
, where
N
is the degree of the generator we want to get rid of.
Eventually, if
{\mathscr{L}}
is finitely presented, then the same holds for
\mathscr{S}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.14
.

By Lemma
2.15
, we may assume
{\mathscr{L}}
to be a standard finitely presented Lie algebra. Let
d
be the maximal degree of the minimal relations of
{\mathscr{L}}
and assume that
d\geq 3
.

Let
r=\sum_{i}[x_{i},a_{i}]
be a relation of degree
d
of
{\mathscr{L}}
, where
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
is a minimal generating system and the
a_{i}
’s are homogeneous elements of degree
d-1
.

Consider the direct sum
\mathscr{Q}^{1}={\mathscr{L}}\times k
, where the abelian Lie algebra
k
is generated by the degree-
1
element
t_{1}
.

For
2\leq i\leq n
, we can define the derivations
\phi_{i}:t_{1}\mapsto a_{i}
of degree
d-2
, and put

\mathscr{Q}^{2}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi_{i}}(\mathscr{Q}^{1},s_{i})

for the multiple HNN-extension of
\mathscr{Q}^{1}
with respect to the derivations
\phi_{i}
,
2\leq i\leq n
.

Finally, let
\psi:\operatorname{Span}\{t_{1},x_{1}\}\to\mathscr{Q}^{2}
be the linear map defined by
t_{1}\mapsto a_{1}
and
x_{1}\mapsto\sum_{i=2}^{n}[x_{i},s_{i}]
. Such map is a derivation of the abelian Lie algebra
\langle{t_{1},x_{1}}\rangle
into
\mathscr{Q}^{2}
, as

\displaystyle[x_{1},\psi(t_{1})]+[\psi(x_{1}),t_{1}]

\displaystyle=[x_{1},a_{1}]+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n}[[x_{i},s_{i}],t_{1}]=

\displaystyle=[x_{1},a_{1}]+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n}\left([[x_{i},t_{1}],s_{i}]+[x_{i},[s_{i},t_{1}]]\right)=

\displaystyle=[x_{1},a_{1}]+0+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n}[x_{i},a_{i}]=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}[x_{i},a_{i}]=0.

Hence, the HNN-extension
\mathscr{Q}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\psi}(\mathscr{Q}^{2},t_{2})
has one relation in degree
d
less than
{\mathscr{L}}
. Plus,
\mathscr{Q}
has no relations of degree
>d
, but it is not
1
-generated as the elements
s_{i}
are minimal generators of degree
d-2
. Notice that
\mathscr{Q}
contains
{\mathscr{L}}
. By Lemma
2.15
, we get a standard Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}^{(r)}
containing
\mathscr{Q}
, and hence
{\mathscr{L}}
, with no relations of degree
>d
and with no more relations of degree
d
than
\mathscr{Q}
. Indeed, as noticed above,
H^{2,j}({\mathscr{L}}^{(r)})\simeq H^{2,j}(\mathscr{Q})
for every
j\geq d
.

Since
{\mathscr{L}}
is a standard finitely presented Lie algebra with relations of degree
\leq d
only, one can thus proceed by induction.
∎

The so obtained quadratic Lie algebra is far from being the minimal quadratic Lie algebra containing
{\mathscr{L}}
.

Example 2.16
.

Consider the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
of dimension
2n+1
: it can be given a basis
x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n},z
with non-zero brackets
[x_{i},y_{i}]=z
. If
n>1
, then
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is quadratic, with (graded) presentation

\left\langle{x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}}\,\big|\,{[x_{i},x_{j}],[y_{i},y_{j}],\ [x_{i},y_{j}]-\delta_{ij}[x_{1},y_{1}]}\right\rangle.

However,
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is not Koszul, as shown in
[
30
, Ex. 2, p. 22]
. In Sections
3
and
3.2
, we will demonstrate this using two different approaches.

Although
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
is not quadratic, as it has minimal relations in degree 3, it can be embedded into the quadratic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
(
n\geq 2
).

One may wonder if one can always embed finitely presented graded Lie algebras into Koszul ones, but this is not the case.

Example 2.17
.

Consider the positive part
\mathscr{W}^{+}
of the Witt Lie algebra.

One can give it the classical – yet not minimal – presentation

\mathscr{W}^{+}=\left\langle{x_{i}:\ i\geq 1}\,\big|\,{[x_{m},x_{n}]-(m-n)x_{m+n}}\right\rangle

When the characteristic of the ground field is
0
, Goncharova
[
17
]
computed the
k
-cohomology of such a Lie algebra, discovering that it is
2
dimensional in each positive homological degree, with a graded decomposition into nonzero components as follows:

H^{q}(\mathscr{W}^{+})=H^{q,q_{1}}(\mathscr{W}^{+})\oplus H^{q,q_{2}}(\mathscr{W}^{+})

where
q_{i}=\frac{3q^{2}+(-1)^{i}q}{2}
.

In particular,
\mathscr{W}^{+}
is minimally generated by an element of degree
1
and one of degree
2
.
The minimal relations lie in degree
5
and
7
, giving the following minimal presentation

\mathscr{W}^{+}=\left\langle{x_{1},x_{2}}\,\big|\,{r_{5},r_{7}}\right\rangle

where
x_{i}
has degree
i
, and

\displaystyle r_{5}=

\displaystyle 6[[x_{2},x_{1}],x_{2}]-[[[x_{2},x_{1}],x_{1}],x_{1}],

\displaystyle r_{7}=

\displaystyle 9[[[x_{2},x_{1}],x_{1}],[x_{2},x_{1}]]-[[[[x_{2},x_{1}],x_{1}],x_{1}],x_{1}]

The Lie algebra
\mathscr{W}^{+}
is thus finitely presented but it has infinite cohomological dimension, implying that it does not embed into any Koszul Lie algebra.

Nevertheless, by Theorem
2.14
, one can still embed it into a quadratic Lie algebra, which has thus infinite cohomological dimension.

In order to make it clearer the construction of Lemma
2.15
, we build a standard Lie algebra containing
\mathscr{W}^{+}
. Let
\phi:\langle{x_{1}}\rangle\to\mathscr{W}^{+}
be the derivation sending
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
.
Then,
{\mathscr{L}}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathscr{W}^{+},t)
is a standard Lie algebra generated by the two degree
1
elements
t
and
x_{1}
with cohomology

H^{q}({\mathscr{L}})=H^{q,q_{1}}(\mathscr{W}^{+})\oplus H^{q,q_{2}}(\mathscr{W}^{+}),\quad q\geq 2.

In particular, an obstruction for embedding a finitely presented Lie algebra into a Koszul one relies on the possibility of having infinite cohomological dimension.

Question 2.18
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a quadratic algebra of finite cohomological dimension, does there exist a Koszul Lie algebra containing it?

Example 2.19
.

The easiest example of a non-quadratic Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{g}=\left\langle{a,b}\,\big|\,{[a,[a,b]]}\right\rangle

Consider the Lie algebra

{\mathscr{L}}=\left\langle{x,y,z,w}\,\big|\,{[x,y]-[z,w],\ [x,w],\ [x,z]}\right\rangle

It is a quadratic quotient of the surface Lie algebra (see Section
4
)

\mathscr{G}_{4}=\left\langle{\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z},\bar{w}}\,\big|\,{[\bar{x},\bar{y}]-[\bar{z},\bar{w}]}\right\rangle.

Now, by
[
4
]
, the Lie subalgebra
\mathscr{M}
of
\mathscr{G}_{4}
generated by the elements
\bar{y},\bar{z}
and
\bar{w}
is free, and hence the map
\phi:\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{M}
obtained by extending
\bar{y}\mapsto[\bar{z},\bar{w}]
and
\bar{z},\bar{w}\mapsto 0
is a derivation.

We can thus form the semidirect product
\mathscr{M}\rtimes_{\phi}\langle{t}\rangle
, which coincides with the HNN-extension

\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathscr{M},t)=\left\langle{\bar{y},\bar{z},\bar{w},t}\,\big|\,{[t,\bar{y}]-[\bar{z},\bar{w}],\ [t,\bar{z}],\ [t,\bar{w}]}\right\rangle\simeq{\mathscr{L}}

In particular,
\mathscr{M}
embeds into
{\mathscr{L}}
as a cocyclic ideal, proving that
{\mathscr{L}}
is Koszul of cohomological dimension
2
by Lemma
2.2
.

Note that one has
[x,[x,y]]=0
and hence the map
a\mapsto x
,
b\mapsto y
extends to a Lie algebra homomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\to{\mathscr{L}}
. In fact, it is not hard to see that it is also injective, proving that the non-quadratic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is contained in a Koszul one. In turn,
{\mathscr{L}}
is not BK.

To see this, consider the maximal standard subalgebra
\mathscr{B}
of
{\mathscr{L}}
generated by
x,y
and
w
. By using the exact sequence of Lemma
2.2
, we see that
\mathscr{B}
has two minimal relations, i.e.,
\mathscr{B}
has minimal relations
[x,[x,y]]=0
and
[x,w]=0
. Hence, the subalgebra generated by
x,y
has a single relation, and it is thus isomorphic with
\mathfrak{g}
.

The existence of a non-quadratic subalgebra generated by two elements in a Koszul Lie algebra (as in Example
2.19
) is a remarkable fact, as this does not hold in the large class of RAAG Lie algebras. Indeed, a Tits alternative type result holds for these Lie algebras:

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial graph and let
x
and
y
be elements of degree
1
in the RAAG Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
. Then, the subalgebra they generate in
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
is either free or abelian.

Proof.

If
\Gamma
is a single vertex, there is nothing to prove.

Assume
\Gamma
has at least
2
vertices. Denote by
\Gamma_{x}
the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
spanned by the vertices which appear with non-trivial coefficient in the expression of
x
with respect to the canonical basis (i.e., the basis in bijection with the vertex set). Similarly define
\Gamma_{y}
.

If all vertices
v
in
\Gamma_{x}
and
w
in
\Gamma_{y}\setminus\{x\}
are adjacent in
\Gamma
, then
[x,y]=0
.

Assume that
v\in\Gamma_{x}
and
w\in\Gamma_{y}\setminus\{x\}
are not adjacent and let
\Delta
be the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
on the vertices
v,w
. Hence,
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
is free.

Consider the natural epimorphism
\pi:{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}\to{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
that is the identity on the vertices of
\Delta
and zero on the others.
Since
\pi|_{\langle{x,y}\rangle}
is surjective and
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
is free, it follows that
\langle{x,y}\rangle\simeq{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
is also free.
∎

This phenomenon reflects that occurring for
2
-generator subgroups of right-angled Artin (pro-
p
) groups
[
1
]
(resp.
[
8
]
).

3.
Solvability and center of Koszul Lie algebras

3.1.
Solvability

The aim of this section is to prove

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a Koszul Lie algebra. If
{\mathscr{L}}
is solvable, then it is abelian.

Example
2.16
shows that there exist solvable quadratic Lie algebras that are not abelian. In turn, Theorem
3.1
proves that those Lie algebras are not Koszul, independently from
[
30
]
.

It is known
[
30
, Ex. 2, p. 22]
that a Koszul Lie algebra is either abelian or it has exponential growth, i.e.,
\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{n}>c^{n}
, for some
c>1
. In particular, standard nilpotent Lie algebras are Koszul iff they are abelian. Here we provide another proof of the latter, by using Lemma
2.1
. Note that a finitely generated graded Lie algebra is nilpotent iff it has finite dimension.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a nilpotent Koszul Lie algebra, then
{\mathscr{L}}
is abelian.

Proof.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a minimal nilpotent non-abelian Lie algebra that is also Koszul.

If
\mathscr{M}
is a homogeneous cocyclic ideal of
{\mathscr{L}}
, then
{\mathscr{L}}
admits a decomposition
{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{M}\rtimes k
. Now,
\mathscr{M}
is nilpotent and hence of type FP. It follows that
\mathscr{M}
is a cocyclic ideal of type FP of a Koszul Lie algebra, and hence Koszul as well. By minimality of
{\mathscr{L}}
, we deduce that
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{M}_{1}
is abelian. Now, if
x\in\mathscr{M}
, then for
t\in{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\setminus\mathscr{M}
, the element
[t,x]
lies in
\mathscr{M}_{2}=0
, and hence
{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{M}\times k
is abelian.
∎

For an ordinary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}
the
n
th term of the derived series of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{(n+1)}=[\mathfrak{g}^{(n)},\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}]
. Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
is
n
-step solvable if
\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}=0
and
\mathfrak{g}^{(n-1)}\neq 0
. If
\mathfrak{g}={\mathscr{L}}
is a graded Lie algebra, then each derived term
{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}
is a homogeneous subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a graded locally-finite Lie algebra of finite cohomological dimension. If
{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}
has finite dimension for some
n\geq 1
, then so does
{\mathscr{L}}^{(n-1)}
.

Proof.

We can assume that
{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=M}^{N}{\mathscr{L}}_{i}
(e.g., one can set
M=2^{n}
, cf.
[
19
]
).

Consider now the Lie algebra

\mathscr{M}={\mathscr{L}}^{(n-1)}\cap\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}{\mathscr{L}}_{N+i}.

By definition,
[\mathscr{M},\mathscr{M}]\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}
, and
\mathscr{M}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
, hence
[\mathscr{M},\mathscr{M}]\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}\cap\mathscr{M}=0
, i.e.,
\mathscr{M}
is an abelian Lie algebra. It follows that
\cd{\mathscr{L}}\geq\dim\mathscr{M}
, which implies that
{\mathscr{L}}^{(n-1)}
must be finite dimensional as well, since the homogeneous components
{\mathscr{L}}_{i}
’s are all finite dimensional.
∎

With the following we conclude the proof of Theorem
3.1

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a graded, locally-finite, solvable Lie algebra of finite cohomological dimension. Then,
{\mathscr{L}}
is finite dimensional (and hence nilpotent).

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathscr{L}}
is
(n+1)
-step solvable, whence
{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}
is an abelian subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
. Since
\dim{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}=\cd{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}\leq\cd{\mathscr{L}}
, we deduce that
{\mathscr{L}}^{(n)}
has finite dimension, and so do all the
{\mathscr{L}}^{(r)}
’s for
r\leq n
.
∎

Notice that in
[
15
]
a stronger version of Corollary
3.4
was proved, as Lie algebras of finite cohomological dimension also have finite depth.

3.2.
The center of Koszul Lie algebras

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a standard Lie algebra and assume that
z
is a central element of
{\mathscr{L}}
.
If
z
has degree
1
, then clearly
{\mathscr{L}}
splits as the direct sum of standard algebras
{\mathscr{L}}/(z)
and
\langle{z}\rangle
. Clearly,
{\mathscr{L}}
is quadratic (resp. Koszul) precisely when
{\mathscr{L}}/(z)
is of the same type.
Moreover, since abelian Lie algebras of dimension
d
have cohomological dimension
d
, the dimension of the center cannot be greater than the cohomological dimension of the Lie algebra.

Remark 3.5
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a graded Lie algebra whose center is concentrated in degree
1
, then the restriction map
H^{\bullet}({\mathscr{L}})\to H^{\bullet}(Z({\mathscr{L}}))\simeq{\Lambda_{\bullet}}(Z({\mathscr{L}}))
is surjective and hence

2^{\dim Z({\mathscr{L}})}=\dim H^{\bullet}(Z({\mathscr{L}}))\leq\dim H^{\bullet}({\mathscr{L}}),

i.e., the toral rank conjecture holds true for
{\mathscr{L}}
.

More generally, the same holds when
Z({\mathscr{L}})\cap[{\mathscr{L}},{\mathscr{L}}]=0
.

In the following, we will show that the center of Koszul Lie algebras must be concentrated in “small” odd degrees. In turn, this shows that the quadratic Lie algebras of Example
2.16
are not Koszul, independently from Theorem
3.1
and the growth argument used in
[
30
]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a Koszul Lie algebra. Then,
Z({\mathscr{L}})
is concentrated in odd degrees
<\cd{\mathscr{L}}/2+1
.

Bøgvad
[
6
]
proves the following result for the center of graded
super
-Lie algebras of type FP.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a graded super-Lie algebra of type FP.
If
Z({\mathscr{L}})_{n}\neq 0
, then
1/H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}(t)
is a polynomial divisible by
1-t^{n}
.

This can also be applied to graded Lie algebras: If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a graded Lie algebra, then it is also a super-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
whose homogeneous components are concentrated in even degrees
\mathfrak{g}_{2n}={\mathscr{L}}_{n}
. Plus,
H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g})}(t)=H_{{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}}(t^{2})
. If
Z({\mathscr{L}})_{n}\neq 0
, then
Z(\mathfrak{g})_{2n}\neq 0
, and by Lemma
3.7
,
1-t^{2n}
divides
1/H_{{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}}(t^{2})
, i.e., the polynomial
1/H_{{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}}(t)
is divisible by
1-t^{n}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a Koszul Lie algebra. Then,
Z({\mathscr{L}})
is concentrated in degrees
<\cd{\mathscr{L}}/2+1
.

Proof.

Consider the universal envelope
{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}
. Since
{\mathscr{L}}
is Koszul, it is of type FP and Fröberg’s formula gives
H_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})}(t)H_{H^{\bullet}({\mathscr{L}})}(-t)=1
. Put
p(t)=H_{H^{\bullet}({\mathscr{L}})}(t)
and notice that
p(t)
is a polynomial of degree
n=\cd{\mathscr{L}}
with
positive
coefficients. Explicitly, if
b_{j}=\dim H^{j}({\mathscr{L}})
denotes the
j
th Betti number of
{\mathscr{L}}
, then

p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}b_{j}t^{j}.

Let
z\in{\mathscr{L}}
be a degree
i
central element. By contradiction, assume
z\neq 0
, so that, by Lemma
3.7
,
1-t^{i}
divides
p(-t)
, i.e., there are integers
a_{i}
such that

p(-t)=(1-t^{i})(a_{0}+a_{1}t+\dots+a_{n-i}t^{n-i}).

By expanding the right-hand side

(3.1)

p(-t)=a_{0}+a_{1}t+\dots+a_{n-i}t^{n-i}-a_{0}t^{i}-a_{1}t^{i+1}+\dots-a_{n-i}t^{n},

we see that, if
n-i\leq i-2
, then the polynomial is written as a sum of monomials of increasing degree, and the coefficient of
t^{n-i+1}
in
p(t)
is zero. However, such a coefficient cannot vanish, since it equals
(-1)^{i+1}b_{i+1}
.
∎

It follows from the above proof that the polynomial
p(t)
with positive coefficients is divisible by
1-(-t)^{i}
, whenever
{\mathscr{L}}
contains a non-trivial central element of degree
i
. Notice that one can recognise the parity of
i
by looking at the roots of the polynomial
1-(-t)^{i}
: The integer
i
is even iff
-1
is a root of that polynomial.

We are thus led to study the roots of the Poincaré polynomial of Koszul Lie algebras. This was one of the aims of Weigel’s work
[
42
]
.

3.2.1.
Eigenvalues of a Lie algebra of type FP

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a Lie algebra of type FP.
Since the constant term of the Poincaré polynomial
P_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
is non-zero, there are complex numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
such that

P_{\mathscr{L}}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\lambda_{i}t).

These complex numbers are called the
eigenvalues
of
{\mathscr{L}}
.

As noticed by Weigel in
[
42
]
, there is a constraint on the real eigenvalues, which relies on Descartes criterion on the sign of real roots of a real polynomial.

Fact 3.9
.

The real eigenvalues of a Lie algebra of type FP are positive.

From this, we derive:

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a Koszul Lie algebra. Then, the center of
{\mathscr{L}}
is concentrated in odd degrees.

Proof.

Let
z
be a non-trivial central element of
{\mathscr{L}}
of degree
i
. Hence, as above,
1-(-t)^{i}
divides the Poincaré polynomial
P_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
. Now, by Fact
3.9
, all real eigenvalues of
{\mathscr{L}}
are positive. It follows that
1-(-t)^{i}
has no negative root, and hence
i
must be odd.
∎

Together with Corollary
3.8
, one deduces Theorem
3.6
.

3.3.
The
b_{2}
-conjecture

Notice that the definition of eigenvalues applies to any associative algebra of type FP. We can give a partial answer to Question 2 of
[
42
]
(see also
[
43
, Question 3]
).

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
A
be an algebra of type FP
∞
and finite cohomological dimension
n\geq 1
. If all the eigenvalues of
A
are real, then

b_{2}(A)\leq\frac{n-1}{2n}b_{1}(A)^{2}.

More generally, for
j=0,\dots,n-1
,

b_{j-1}(A)b_{j+1}(A)\leq\frac{j(n-j)}{(j+1)(n-j+1)}b_{j}(A)^{2}

Proof.

If
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
are the eigenvalues of
A
, then they are positive by Fact
3.9
and

\displaystyle b_{1}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i},

\displaystyle b_{2}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}.

In particular,
b_{1}(A)
and
b_{2}(A)
are elementary symmetric polynomials. The result thus follows from the well known Newton’s inequalities.
∎

Weigel
[
42
, Question 2]
asks whether the above inequality involving
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
holds for any Koszul algebra
A
of finite cohomological dimension.

Remark 3.12
.

If
n=2
, then, both the eigenvalues of
A
are real numbers (see
[
42
]
), and hence
b_{2}(A)\leq b_{1}(A)^{2}/4
.

The celebrated Golod-Shafarevich theorem states that a pro-
p
group
G
satisfying
b_{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\leq b_{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})^{2}/4
is infinite. For finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras, the last inequality has been conjectured to hold in the opposite direction (
[
7
]
).

As noticed in
[
42
]
, there exist Koszul Lie algebras with some non-real eigenvalues. We now provide an example of a Koszul Lie algebra having complex eigenvalues with negative real parts, answering negatively to
[
42
, Question 1]
.

Example 3.13
.

Let
\Gamma
be the graph obtained as the disjoint union of a complete graph on
7
vertices and
8
isolated vertices. Then, the RAAG Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
is Koszul (see
[
21
]
) with Poincaré polynomial (that equals the clique polynomial of
\Gamma
)

P_{{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}}(t)=(1+t)^{7}+8t.

A numerical computation shows that some of the roots of
P_{{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}}(-t)
are approximately
1/\lambda_{\pm}\approx-0.02463\pm 0.80986\ i
, and hence the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\pm}
have negative real parts.

In fact, the set of complex roots of all clique polynomials is dense in
\mathbb{C}
, as it was proved in
[
5
]
.

By Turán’s Theorem
[
41
]
, if a graph
\Gamma
on
n
vertices does not contain any
(r+1)
-clique, then, the number of edges of
\Gamma
does not exceed

{\mathbf{e}}_{\tiny{\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-1.07639pt\vbox{\halign{\relax\hfil\txtline@@{#}\hfil\cr\hbox{{\ignorespaces max}\crcr}}}}}}=\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{n^{2}-s^{2}}{2}+{s\choose 2},}

where
0\leq s<r
and
s\equiv n\mod r
. The number
\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{\tiny\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-1.07639pt\vbox{\halign{\relax\hfil\txtline@@{#}\hfil\cr\hbox{{\ignorespaces max}\crcr}}}}}}
is the number of edges of the Turán graph
T(n,r)
.
In particular, as noticed in
[
42
]
, if
r\geq 2
, then
\mathbf{e}_{\tiny\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-1.07639pt\vbox{\halign{\relax\hfil\txtline@@{#}\hfil\cr\hbox{{\ignorespaces max}\crcr}}}}}\leq\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}}
, proving that, for all RAAG Lie algebras, the formula of Proposition
3.11
holds, despite their eigenvalues might not be real.
In particular, if
\Gamma
is a graph with
v
vertices and
e
edges, then

n\geq\frac{v^{2}}{v^{2}-2e}

that gives the same lower bound for the clique number of
\Gamma
as that appearing in
[
29
]
.

Given the opposite nature of nilpotent Lie algebras and (non-abelian) BK Lie algebras, we suspect that the latter class satisfies a version of the
b_{2}
-conjecture in the opposite direction, i.e.,

Conjecture 3.14

(BK version of the
b_{2}
-conjecture)
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a BK Lie algebra of cohomological dimension
n
, then

\omega({\mathscr{L}}):=(n-1)b_{1}({\mathscr{L}})^{2}-2nb_{2}({\mathscr{L}})\geq 0.

This is a special case of Question 2 of
[
42
]
, and it is known to hold when
n\leq 2
.

In order to attack the conjecture, one might try to use induction, due to the hereditary property of BK Lie algebras.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a quadratic Lie algebra of cohomological dimension
n\geq 2
with standard subalgebras
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{A}
such that
{\mathscr{L}}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathscr{M},t)
, and
\phi:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{M}
is a derivation of degree
1
.

Suppose that
\cd\mathscr{M}=n-1
and that

\omega(\mathscr{M}):=(n-2)m_{1}^{2}-2(n-1)m_{2}\geq 0,

where
m_{i}=b_{i}(\mathscr{M})
.
Then,
\omega({\mathscr{L}})\geq 0
.

Proof.

Since
\dim\mathscr{A}_{1}\leq m_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle({\mathscr{L}})=(n-1)(m_{1}+1)^{2}-2n(m_{2}+\dim\mathscr{A}_{1})=

\displaystyle\geq\omega(\mathscr{M})+m_{1}^{2}+(n-1)(2m_{1}+1)-2m_{2}-2nm_{1}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\omega(\mathscr{M})+m_{1}^{2}-2m_{1}+n-1-2m_{2}=

\displaystyle=\omega(\mathscr{M})+m_{1}^{2}-2m_{1}+n-1+\frac{1}{n-1}(\omega(\mathscr{M})-(n-2)m_{1}^{2})=

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n-1}\omega(\mathscr{M})+\frac{1}{n-1}(m_{1}-n+1)^{2}\geq 0

i.e.,
{\mathscr{L}}
satisfies the BK version of the
b_{2}
-conjecture.
∎

In particular, if all the BK Lie algebras of cohomological dimension
n
have a maximal standard subalgebra of cohomological dimension
n-1
, then Conjecture
3.14
holds true (see Corollary
3.19
).

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Droms graph of clique number
n>1
. Then, there exists a maximal standard subalgebra
\mathscr{M}
of the RAAG Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
of cohomological dimension
n-1
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the number of vertices of
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
consists of a single edge, and hence
n=2
, then the Lie algebra generated by anyone of the vertices of
\Gamma
has the expected cohomological dimension.

Assume now that
\Gamma
has more than one edge.

If
\Gamma
is connected, then, by the main Lemma of
[
14
]
, there is an induced subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
and a vertex
v
not in
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
\Gamma
is the cone on
\Gamma^{\prime}
with
v
as a tip, i.e.,
v
is adjacent to all other vertices of
\Gamma
.
It follows that
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}\simeq{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\times k
and hence
\mathscr{M}={\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
has the right cohomological dimension.

If
\Gamma
is not connected, then there are two proper induced subgraphs
\Gamma_{i}
,
i=1,2
, such that
\Gamma
is their disjoint union. If we denote by
{\mathscr{L}}(i)
the RAAG Lie algebra on
\Gamma_{i}
, we get a free product decomposition
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}={\mathscr{L}}(1)\amalg{\mathscr{L}}(2)
.
If the clique numbers of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are equal (and hence
>1
), by induction, for
i=1,2
,
{\mathscr{L}}(i)
contains a maximal standard subalgebra
\mathscr{M}(i)
of cohomological dimension one less than that of
{\mathscr{L}}(i)
, i.e.,
\cd\mathscr{M}(i)=n-1
.
Note that
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
is BK, by
[
4
]
, and hence its standard subalgebras satisfy a version of the Kurosh subalgebra theorem (
[
4
]
).
If
v_{i}
is a vertex of
\Gamma_{i}
such that its associated standard basis element does not belong to
\mathscr{M}(i)
, then, by the Kurosh theorem, the Lie algebra
\mathscr{M}
generated by
\mathscr{M}(1)
,
\mathscr{M}(2)
and
v_{1}+v_{2}
decomposes as the free product

\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{M}(1)\amalg\mathscr{M}(2)\amalg\langle{v_{1}+v_{2}}\rangle

In particular,
\cd\mathscr{M}=\max(\cd\mathscr{M}(1),\cd\mathscr{M}(2),1)=n-1
.

It remains to consider the case when
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
have different clique numbers. In that case, assume the clique number of
\Gamma_{1}
is
n
and that of
\Gamma_{2}
is
<n
. By defining
\mathscr{M}(1)
as above, then the subalgebra generated by
\mathscr{M}(1)
and
{\mathscr{L}}(2)
equals their free product and hence it is a maximal standard subalgebra of cohomological dimension
n-1
.
∎

For RAAG Lie algebras we have a Lie theoretic counterpart of the main theorem of Droms’
[
14
]
(and of its pro-
p
version
[
39
, Theorem 1.2(ii)]
). As proved in
[
21
]
, the RAAG Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}=\left\langle{a,b,x}\,\big|\,{[a,b]}\right\rangle
contains a non-standard subalgebra
\mathscr{M}=\langle{a,b,[x,a],[x,b]}\rangle
that is not isomorphic to any RAAG Lie algebra. However,
\mathscr{M}
admits a standard grading which makes it into a quadratic Lie algebra

\mathscr{M}=\left\langle{a,b,z,t}\,\big|\,{[a,b],[z,a]+[t,b]}\right\rangle.

By Theorem
4.3
,
\mathscr{M}
is BK with respect to the new grading.

Proposition 3.17
.

If
\Gamma
is a finite simplicial graph, then the RAAG Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
is BK iff all of its standard subalgebras are isomorphic with a RAAG Lie algebra.

Proof.

We translate almost verbatim the pro-
p
group theoretic proof of
[
39
]
to our scope.

If all standard subalgebras of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
are RAAG Lie algebras, then
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
is clearly BK.

Assume now that
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
is BK.
We prove the result by induction on the number of vertices of
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
consists of a single vertex, the result is clear. Suppose that
\Gamma
has more than one vertex. Decompose
\Gamma
into its connected components
\Gamma_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
.

If
k\geq 2
, then every component
\Gamma_{i}
is a Droms graph, and hence, by induction, every subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma_{i}}
is a RAAG Lie algebra.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a
1
-generated subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
; by the Kurosh subalgebra theorem
[
4
]
,
\mathscr{M}
is a free product of a free Lie algebra and subalgebras of the
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma_{i}}
’s, and thus it is a RAAG Lie algebra.
Explicitly, it is the algebra on the graph obtained from the disjoint union of the graphs corresponding to the intersections
\mathscr{M}\cap{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma_{i}}
, and a finite number of isolated vertices.

Now suppose that
\Gamma
is connected, and thus it is a cone
\nabla(\Gamma^{\prime})=\Gamma
, with tip
v
.
Then
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}=\langle{v}\rangle\times{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.
Let
\phi:{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}\to{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
be the natural projection.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a
1
-generated subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
. Then we have the following central extension

0\to\mathscr{M}\cap\langle{v}\rangle\to\mathscr{M}\to\phi(\mathscr{M})\to 0.

We claim that this sequence splits.
Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a Droms graph, by induction
\phi(\mathscr{M})
is a RAAG Lie algebra, say
\phi(\mathscr{M})={\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
.
Let
u
be a vertex of
\Delta
, and choose
m\in\mathscr{M}
such that
\phi(m)=u
.
As
u\in{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
, one has
\phi(u)=u
, and hence,
u-m\in\ker\phi\leq\langle{v}\rangle
.
This means that there is a scalar
\alpha_{u}\in k
such that
u+\alpha_{u}v=m\in\mathscr{M}
.
Define
\rho_{1}:\phi(\mathscr{M})_{1}\to\mathscr{M}_{1}
by linearly extending
\rho_{1}(u)=u+\alpha_{u}v
,
\forall u\in V(\Delta)
.
For
x=\sum_{u\in V(\Delta)}r_{u}u\in\phi(\mathscr{M})_{1}=\text{Span}_{k}V(\Delta)
, put
\alpha_{x}=\sum_{u\in V(\Delta)}r_{u}\alpha_{u}
, so that

\rho_{1}(x)=x+\alpha_{x}v,\ \forall x\in\phi(\mathscr{M}).

Since
v
is in the center of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
, for
\{x,x^{\prime}\}\in E(\Gamma^{\prime})
, we have

[\rho_{1}(x),\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})]=[x+\alpha_{x}v,x^{\prime}+\alpha_{x^{\prime}}v]=[x,x^{\prime}]=0,

whence
\rho_{1}
extends to a well-defined Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\phi(\mathscr{M})\to\mathscr{M}

Moreover,
\phi\rho(u)=\phi(u+\alpha_{u}v)=\phi(u)=u
, for every
u\in V(\Delta)
, and thus
\phi\rho=\text{Id}_{\phi(\mathscr{M})}
, i.e.,
\rho
is a section.
Now, since
\mathscr{M}\cap\langle{v}\rangle
is contained in the center of
\mathscr{M}
, we have
\mathscr{M}=(\mathscr{M}\cap\langle{v}\rangle)\times\phi(\mathscr{M})
.
If
\mathscr{M}\cap\langle{v}\rangle=0
, then
\mathscr{M}\leq{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
is a RAAG Lie algebra by induction; otherwise,
\mathscr{M}
contains
v
, and hence we have
\mathscr{M}\leq{\mathscr{L}}_{\nabla(\Delta)}
.
∎

From Lemma
3.16
and Proposition
3.17
it follows a proof of the BK version of the
b_{2}
-conjecture for the RAAG Lie algebras on Droms graphs, independently from Túran’s.

3.3.1.
The free rank

For a standard Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
, define the
free rank
of
{\mathscr{L}}
as the maximal dimension of the generating spaces of standard free subalgebras of
{\mathscr{L}}
, i.e.,

\frk{\mathscr{L}}:=\max\left\{{\dim V}\ \big|\ {V\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}_{1},\ \langle{V}\rangle\text{ is free}}\right\}

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a BK Lie algebra. Then,
\frk{\mathscr{L}}\leq\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}-\cd{\mathscr{L}}+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a standard free Lie subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
on
r
elements. Set
d=\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
and
n=\cd{\mathscr{L}}
.

Let
\mathscr{M}^{(1)}
be a standard subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
containing
\mathscr{M}^{(0)}=\mathscr{F}
with
\dim\mathscr{M}^{(1)}_{1}=r+1
. Similarly define
\mathscr{M}^{(i)}
for all
1\leq i\leq d-r
, so that
\mathscr{M}^{(d-r)}={\mathscr{L}}
.

Since
\mathscr{M}^{(i)}
is a maximal standard subalgebra of
\mathscr{M}^{(i+1)}
(
0\leq i\leq d-r-1
), one has

\cd\mathscr{M}^{(i+1)}\leq\cd\mathscr{M}^{(i)}+1,

and hence

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=\cd\mathscr{M}^{(d-r)}=\cd\mathscr{M}^{(d-r)}-\cd\mathscr{M}^{(d-r-1)}+\cd\mathscr{M}^{(d-r-1)}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d-r}\left(\cd\mathscr{M}^{(d-r-i+1)}-\cd\mathscr{M}^{(d-r-i)}\right)+\cd\mathscr{M}^{(0)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-r}1\right)+\cd\mathscr{F}=d-r+1.

which proves the claim.
∎

It follows from the proof that
\frk{\mathscr{L}}=\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}-\cd{\mathscr{L}}+1
is equivalent to requiring that the cohomological dimensions of the
\mathscr{M}^{i}
is strictly decreasing.

One can state a dual of the latter equality. Let
A
be a graded-commutative universally Koszul algebra. Its cohomology ring is the universal enveloping algebra of a BK Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
(
[
4
]
).
The equality
\frk{\mathscr{L}}=\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}-\cd{\mathscr{L}}+1
is equivalent to
A
containing elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in A_{1}
such that
x_{1}A_{n-s}+\dots+x_{s}A_{n-s}=A_{n-s+1}
, for all
1\leq s\leq n
, where
n=\cd{\mathscr{L}}
.

Corollary 3.19
.

If every BK Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
satisfies
\frk{\mathscr{L}}=\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}-\cd{\mathscr{L}}+1
, then the BK version of the
b_{2}
-conjecture holds true.

Proof.

One can argue by induction on the minimal number of generators of a BK Lie algebra and apply Lemma
3.15
.
∎

Question 3.20
.

Is it true that

\frk{\mathscr{L}}=\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}-\cd{\mathscr{L}}+1

for any BK Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
? In other words, do all BK Lie algebras of cohomological dimension
n
have maximal proper standard subalgebras of cohomological dimension
n-1
?

Dually, is it true that if
A
is a universally Koszul graded commutative algebra of maximal degree
n
, then there exists a degree
1
element in
A
such that
A_{n-1}x=A_{n}
?

Since any RAAG Lie algebra of cohomological dimension
n
has a maximal standard subalgebra of dimension
n-1
by Lemma
3.16
, Question
3.20
has positive answer for this class.

3.4.
The center of Bloch-Kato Lie algebras

For BK Lie algebras, determining the center is much simpler.

Theorem 3.21
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a BK Lie algebra. Then, the center of
{\mathscr{L}}
is concentrated in degree
1
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
.
Denote by
Z=Z({\mathscr{L}})
the center of
{\mathscr{L}}
.

If
\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}=1
, then
{\mathscr{L}}=Z({\mathscr{L}})
is abelian, concentrated in degree
1
.

Let
\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}>1
and suppose that if
\mathscr{M}
is any BK Lie algebra with
\dim\mathscr{M}_{1}<\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
, then the center of
\mathscr{M}
is concentrated in degree
1
.

Decompose
{\mathscr{L}}
into the HNN-extension
{\mathscr{L}}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{A},t)
, where
\mathscr{M}
is a standard subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
,
\mathscr{A}
is a standard subalgebra of
\mathscr{M}
and
\phi:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{M}
is a derivation of degree
1
. In particular,
\mathscr{M}
is BK, and
\dim\mathscr{M}_{1}=\dim{\mathscr{L}}_{1}-1
. Hence, by induction, one has
Z_{n}\cap\mathscr{M}\subseteq Z(\mathscr{M})_{n}=0
, for
n\geq 2
.

Fix an element
z\in Z
of degree
n\geq 2
.

If
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{A}
, then
{\mathscr{L}}_{n}=\mathscr{M}_{n}
, for
n\geq 2
, proving that
z\in Z(\mathscr{M})
, and hence
z=0
.

We may thus assume that
z\notin\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{M}\neq\mathscr{A}
.
Consider the two-dimensional (abelian) Lie algebra
B
generated by
z
and an element
m\in\mathscr{M}_{1}\setminus\mathscr{A}
. By
[
23
, Thm. 3]
,
B
is a subalgebra of the free product
C\amalg B_{0}
, where
C
is a free subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
and
B_{0}=\mathscr{M}\cap B=\langle{m}\rangle
. Since the free Lie algebra of rank
\geq 2
has no abelian subalgebra of dimension
\geq 2
, we get a contradiction, whence
z
must be zero.
∎

Note that for the aim of the result,
\mathscr{A}
might have center in arbitrary degree. The same proof can thus be adapted to Lie algebras having quadratic filtrations by taking
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{M}(1)
(cf.
1.1
) and then arguing by induction.

In light of Remark
3.5
, we get the Toral Rank Conjecture for BK Lie algebras.

Corollary 3.22
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is BK (or has a quadratic filtration), then

\dim H^{\bullet}({\mathscr{L}})\geq 2^{\dim Z({\mathscr{L}})}.

In general, the previous result cannot be extended to Koszul Lie algebras with the same proof, since the center of such algebras is not known to lie in degree
1
in general. Nevertheless, for RAAG Lie algebras this holds.

Proposition 3.23
.

If
\Gamma=(V,E)
is a finite simplicial graph, then the associated RAAG Lie algebra

{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}=\left\langle{v\in V}\,\big|\,{[v,w]:\ v,w\in E}\right\rangle

has center concentrated in degree
1
and satisfies the TRC.

Proof.

If
\Delta
is an induced subgraph of
\Gamma
, then the associated RAAG Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
of
\Delta
is naturally a subalgebra of
\Gamma
. It follows that
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
admits a quadratic filtration, and hence its center is concentrated in degree
1
for the proof of Theorem
3.21
.
∎

Question 3.24
.

Do Koszul Lie algebras have quadratic filtrations?

Affirmative answer to Question
3.24
would imply that the center of any Koszul Lie algebra is concentrated in degree
1
(making the arguments of Theorem
3.6
unnecessary), and hence that TRC holds true for such Lie algebras.

3.5.
Essential decompositions

In contrast with the theory of finite dimensional Lie algebras of characteristic
0
, there is no Levi decomposition result for infinite dimensional ones. However, in case a graded Lie algebra has finite depth (e.g., if it has finite cohomological dimension), the beginnings of a structure theory still exist (see
[
15
]
).

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a graded Lie algebra and consider the sum
R=\operatorname{rad}{\mathscr{L}}
of all the solvable ideals of
{\mathscr{L}}
; this ideal is called the
radical
of
{\mathscr{L}}
and it must not be solvable. If depth of
{\mathscr{L}}
is finite, then
{\mathscr{L}}/R
admits an essentially-semisimple decomposition, i.e.,
{\mathscr{L}}/R
splits into the direct product of finitely-many non-abelian ideals
I(1),\dots,I(r)
(
r\geq 0
) satisfying the following property: If
L,M\subseteq I(j)
are non-zero ideals of
{\mathscr{L}}/R
, then
L\cap M\neq 0
.
If
{\mathscr{L}}
has finite dimension (and it is not necessarily graded), then
R
is solvable, the quotient
{\mathscr{L}}/R
is semisimple and all the
I(r)
are simple ideals of
{\mathscr{L}}/R
.

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a graded Lie algebra. Then, every finite dimensional ideal of
{\mathscr{L}}
is contained in the limit space
Z_{\infty}({\mathscr{L}})
of the upper central series.

Proof.

Let
Z_{1}=Z({\mathscr{L}})
and
Z_{n+1}=\left\{{x\in{\mathscr{L}}}\ \big|\ {[x,{\mathscr{L}}]\subseteq Z_{n}}\right\}
be the terms of the upper central series.

Fix a finite dimensional ideal
I
of
{\mathscr{L}}
.
If
x\in I
is an element of maximal degree
M
in
I
, then
[x,{\mathscr{L}}]=0
, i.e.,
x\in Z_{1}
. If we assume by induction that
I_{M-i}\subseteq Z_{i+1}
, then, for
x\in I_{M-i-1}
, one has
[x,{\mathscr{L}}]\subseteq I_{\geq M-i}\subseteq Z_{i+1}
and hence
x\in Z_{i+2}
.
∎

Corollary 3.26
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a locally-finite graded Lie algebra of finite depth. Then,
\operatorname{rad}{\mathscr{L}}=Z_{\infty}({\mathscr{L}})
. In particular,

(1)

The non-zero homogeneous component of maximal degree of
\operatorname{rad}{\mathscr{L}}
is contained in the center, and

(2)

\operatorname{rad}({\mathscr{L}})=0
if
{\mathscr{L}}
is centerless.

Proof.

The radical
\operatorname{rad}{\mathscr{L}}
is a finite dimensional ideal by
[
15
]
, and hence, by Lemma
3.25
,
\operatorname{rad}{\mathscr{L}}\subseteq Z_{\infty}({\mathscr{L}})
. On the other hand,
Z_{n}({\mathscr{L}})
is a solvable ideal of
{\mathscr{L}}
, for all
n\geq 1
, and hence
Z_{\infty}({\mathscr{L}})\subseteq\operatorname{rad}({\mathscr{L}})
.
∎

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a BK Lie algebra, or it admits a quadratic filtration, then, by Theorem
3.21
, the center of
{\mathscr{L}}
is concentrated in degree
1
and
{\mathscr{L}}\simeq Z({\mathscr{L}})\times{\mathscr{L}}/Z({\mathscr{L}})
. By Corollary
3.26
, the radical of
{\mathscr{L}}
coincides with the center, and hence the direct factor
{\mathscr{L}}/Z({\mathscr{L}})
of
{\mathscr{L}}
is essentially-semisimple.

Lemma 3.27
.

The direct product of non-abelian graded Lie algebras is not BK.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
be non-abelian graded Lie algebras and let
{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{B}
be their free product. By contradiction, assume that
{\mathscr{L}}
is BK, and hence so are
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
.

Since
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are non-abelian, there exist elements
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}
and
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}
of degree
1
such that
[a,a^{\prime}]\neq 0\neq[b,b^{\prime}]
.

In particular, the two subalgebras generated by
a,a^{\prime}
and by
b,b^{\prime}
are isomorphic with the free Lie algebra
\mathscr{F}
of rank
2
. The Lie algebra generated by the four elements
a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{L}}
is thus isomorphic to
\mathscr{F}\times\mathscr{F}
.

For
t=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}
, we claim that the subalgebra
\mathscr{M}
of
{\mathscr{L}}
generated by
a,b
and
t
is not quadratic, concluding the proof.

Indeed, in
\mathscr{M}
one has the minimal relation
[[t,a],b]=0
.

Notice that the standard Lie algebra
\mathscr{F}\times\mathscr{F}
is isomorphic to the RAAG Lie algebra associated to the square graph, and hence it is not BK by Example
1.3

∎

We deduce that BK Lie algebras satisfy an
essential
version of the Levi decomposition theorem in a very specific way.

Theorem 3.28
.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a non-abelian BK Lie algebra. Then, there exists an abelian Lie algebra
Z
and an essentially simple ideal
\mathscr{M}
of
{\mathscr{L}}
such that
\mathscr{M}
is a BK Lie algebra and
{\mathscr{L}}=Z\times\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

As noted above,
{\mathscr{L}}
is the direct sum of its center and an essentially-semisimple Lie algebra
\mathscr{M}=I(1)\times\dots\times I(r)
, where
r\geq 1
and
I(j)
is an essentially simple ideal of
{\mathscr{L}}
. Since
\mathscr{M}
is BK, the number
r
of components
I(j)
must be
1
, by Lemma
3.27
.
∎

4.
Examples

4.1.
Surface Lie algebras

If
d\geq 1
, one defines the surface Lie
k
-algebra

\mathscr{G}_{2d}=\left\langle{x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{d},y_{d}}\,\big|\,{\sum_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\right\rangle.

It is the associated graded Lie
k
-algebra of the oriented surface group of genus
d
with respect to the lower central series. If the ground field is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
p
odd, the
p
-restrictification of
\mathscr{G}_{2d}
is the Lie algebra associated to the dimension subgroup series of any
2d
-generated Demuškin group
[
27
]
.

In
[
4
]
, the author proved that
\mathscr{G}_{2d}
is BK and all of its proper subalgebras are free. The usage of HNN-extensions allows us to prove that the converse holds.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
\neq 2
. Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be a quadratic Lie
k
-algebra such that all of its proper standard subalgebras are free. Then, either
{\mathscr{L}}
is free or
{\mathscr{L}}
is a surface Lie algebra.

Since
\mathscr{G}_{2d}
is Koszul, its cohomology ring can be easily computed:

H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{G}_{2d})={\Lambda_{\bullet}}(V)/(\Omega)

where
V=\operatorname{Span}\{\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\dots,\xi_{d},\eta_{d}\}
and
\Omega=\operatorname{Span}\{\xi_{i}\wedge\eta_{j}-\delta_{ij}\xi_{1}\wedge\eta_{1},\xi_{i}\wedge\xi_{j},\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j}\}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A
be a connected graded commutative algebra of characteristic
\neq 2
that is generated by elements of degree
1
. If
A_{3}=0
and
\dim A_{2}=1
, then
A
is the direct sum of an exterior algebra and the cohomology of a surface Lie algebra.

Proof.

The multiplication map restricts to a skew-symmetric bilinear form

\beta:A_{1}\times A_{1}\to A_{2}=k.

Consider the radical of
\beta
,

R=\operatorname{rad}\beta=\left\{{a\in A_{1}}\ \big|\ {\beta(a,b)=0,\ \forall b\in A_{1}}\right\}

and let
C_{1}\subseteq A_{1}
be a complement of the
k
-subspace
R
in
A_{1}
, i.e.
A_{1}=R\oplus C_{1}
.
The restriction of
\beta
to
C_{1}
defines a symplectic form. Consider also the subalgebra
B
of
A
generated by
R
. Then,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=k\cdot 1+R,

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=k\cdot 1+C_{1}+A_{2}.

Since
R\cdot C_{1}=R\cdot A_{2}=0
, it follows that
A=B\sqcap C
, i.e.,
A
is the connected algebra with
A_{i}=B_{i}\times C_{i}
(
i\geq 1
) and
B\cdot C=0
.
One can find a Darboux basis
x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{d},y_{d}
for the symplectic space
(C_{1},\beta_{C})
, i.e. a basis of
C_{1}
such that, for the symplectic form
\beta_{C}:C_{1}\otimes C_{1}\to A_{2}=k
, it holds

\displaystyle\beta_{C}(x_{i}\otimes y_{j})=\delta_{ij},

\displaystyle\beta_{C}(x_{i}\otimes x_{j})=\beta(y_{i}\otimes y_{j})=0.

Now, both
B
and
C
are quadratic algebras, given by the quotients

\displaystyle C=\frac{{\Lambda_{\bullet}}(C_{1})}{(x_{i}\wedge y_{j}-\delta_{ij}x_{1}\wedge y_{1},x_{i}\wedge x_{j},y_{i}\wedge y_{j})}

\displaystyle\ \qquad\qquad\qquad B=\frac{T(R)}{T^{2}(R)}

It follows that
B=H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{F})
, where
\mathscr{F}
is the standard free Lie algebra generated by
R^{\ast}
, and
C\simeq H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{G}_{2d})
.
∎

Proof of Prop.
4.1
.

Assume
{\mathscr{L}}
has more than
1
minimal relation. Then, there is a quadratic Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}
with
2
relations, and an epimorphism
\pi:\tilde{\mathscr{L}}\to{\mathscr{L}}
that is an isomorphism in degree
1
.
If
\mathscr{M}
is a proper standard subalgebra of
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}
, then its image under
\pi
is a proper standard subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
, which needs to be free by hypothesis. In particular,
\mathscr{M}
is free. Indeed, if
\mathscr{F}
is the free Lie algebra on
\mathscr{M}_{1}
, then the composition
\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{M}\to\pi(\mathscr{M})
is an isomorphism.

Hence,
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}
satisfies the hypothesis of the theorem.
Thus, assume
{\mathscr{L}}
to be defined by two (non-necessarily independent) quadratic relations
s\neq 0
and
r
. Let
\mathscr{G}
be the
1
-relator Lie algebra defined by
s
that covers
{\mathscr{L}}
.

Since
\mathscr{G}
is
1
-generated, there is a non-zero element
x\in\mathscr{G}_{1}
, a maximal standard subalgebra
\mathscr{M}
of
\mathscr{G}
not containing
x
, and elements
z\in\mathscr{M}_{1}
and
c\in\mathscr{M}_{2}
such that

r=[x,z]+c.

By setting
\phi(z)=-c
, one has the well defined derivation
\phi:\langle{z}\rangle\to\mathscr{M}
, and the HNN decomposition

{\mathscr{L}}\simeq\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathscr{M},t)

where
t
corresponds to
x
in the isomorphism. Since
\mathscr{M}
embeds into
{\mathscr{L}}
, it is free, whence the Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
has just one relation,
[t,z]=-c
.

Now, since
\mathscr{M}
is free, both
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{A}
are Koszul, and hence so is
{\mathscr{L}}
. As
{\mathscr{L}}
is a Koszul
1
-relator Lie algebra, its cohomology ring satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
4.2
, proving that
{\mathscr{L}}
is a free product of a free Lie algebra
\mathscr{F}
and a surface Lie algebra
\mathscr{G}_{2d}
.

If
\mathscr{F}\neq 0
, then
\mathscr{G}_{2d}
is a non-free subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}
, contradicting the hypotheses.
∎

As a corollary, all the BK Lie algebras that are not free have a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathscr{G}_{2d}
for some
d\geq 1
.

4.2.
Quadratic
2
-relator Lie algebras

Here we want to present another large class of such Lie algebras, motivated by Quadrelli
[
36
]
.
If
r
is an integer, we say that a graded Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
is
r
-relator if
b_{2}({\mathscr{L}})=r
, i.e., there exists a free Lie algebra
\mathscr{F}
and
r
homogeneous elements minimally generating an ideal
R
of
\mathscr{F}
such that
{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{F}/R
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
{\mathscr{L}}
is a
2
-relator quadratic Lie algebra, then
{\mathscr{L}}
is BK.

Proof.

Let
A
be the diagonal cohomology of
{\mathscr{L}}
, i.e.,
A=\bigoplus_{i}H^{i,i}({\mathscr{L}})
. By definition and
[
30
]
,
A
is a quadratic graded-commutative algebra with
\dim A_{2}=2
.
Let
x
be any non-zero element of degree
1
.

If
xA_{1}=0
or
xA_{1}=A_{2}
, then
xA_{2}=0
. On the other hand, if
xA_{1}
is a
1
-dimensional vector space, say generated by an element
a\neq 0
, then there is a basis
z_{1}=x,\ z_{2}=y,\ z_{3},\dots,z_{n}
for
A_{1}
such that
xy=a
and
xz_{i}=0
for
i\geq 3
. Let
b\in A_{2}
so that
A_{2}=\operatorname{Span}\{a,b\}
. Let
\alpha_{ij}\in k
be the coefficient of
z_{i}z_{j}
in some expression of
b
. Since
xz_{j}=0
for
j>2
, we can take
\alpha_{1j}=0
for
j>2
, and, up to replacing
b
with
b-\alpha_{12}a
, one can suppose that
\alpha_{12}=0
. In particular,
b
lies in the subalgebra generated by the
z_{i}
’s, for
i>2
, and hence
bx=0
.

This proves that
A_{3}=0
. By
[
36
, Thm. 3.7]
, it follows that
A
is universally Koszul. In particular, since a standard Lie algebra is Koszul precisely when its diagonal cohomology is Koszul,
{\mathscr{L}}
is Koszul and
A=H^{\bullet}({\mathscr{L}})
. On the other hand,
A
is universally Koszul, which proves that
{\mathscr{L}}
is BK.
∎

As
1
-relator quadratic Lie algebras are BK, we deduce that a quadratic
r
-relator Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
is BK if
r\leq 2
. Since for a quadratic Lie algebra
b_{2}({\mathscr{L}})\leq{{b_{1}({\mathscr{L}})}\choose 2}
and the equality holds iff
{\mathscr{L}}
is abelian, all quadratic Lie algebras generated by at most
3
elements are BK. In fact, the first example of a quadratic non BK Lie algebra is generated by
4
elements (e.g., the RAAG associated to a square graph, and the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
, which is not even Koszul).

4.3.
Further examples

Define

\mathscr{B}_{2d}=\left\langle{x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{d},y_{d}}\,\big|\,{[x_{i},y_{i}]-[x_{1},y_{1}]:\ i=2,\dots,d}\right\rangle.

Observe that
\mathscr{B}_{4}\simeq\mathscr{G}_{4}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
d\geq 1
,
\mathscr{B}_{2d}
is Koszul and locally of type FP.

Proof.

For
d=1
, we have
\mathscr{B}_{2}=k^{2}[-1]
. For
d=2
,
\mathscr{B}_{4}=\mathscr{G}_{4}
is the surface Lie algebra.

Now let
d>2
. Consider
{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{B}_{2(d-1)}\amalg\langle{y_{n}}\rangle
. By induction,
{\mathscr{L}}
is Koszul and
{\mathscr{L}}^{\prime}
is free.

Also,
\phi:\mathscr{A}=\langle{y_{n}}\rangle\to{\mathscr{L}}
defined by
\phi(y_{n})=[x_{1},y_{1}]
is a derivation. Hence,
\mathscr{B}_{2d}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}({\mathscr{L}},x_{n})
is Koszul. Moreover,
\mathscr{B}_{2d}^{\prime}\cap\mathscr{A}=0
and
\mathscr{B}_{2d}^{\prime}\cap{\mathscr{L}}\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}^{\prime}
is free, proving that
\mathscr{B}_{2d}^{\prime}
is free as well by
[
23
, Thm. 3]
. By
[
21
, Prop. 5.8]
, we deduce that
\mathscr{B}_{2d}
is locally of type FP.
∎

Since
\mathscr{B}_{2d}
is Koszul, its cohomology algebra is

\mathscr{B}_{2d}^{!}=\left\langle{\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\dots,\xi_{d},\eta_{d}}\,\big|\,{\xi_{i}\eta_{j}(1-\delta_{ij}),\ \xi_{i}\xi_{j},\ \eta_{i}\eta_{j},\ \sum_{i}\xi_{i}\eta_{i}}\right\rangle.

In particular,
\cd\mathscr{B}_{2d}=2
.
Notice that
(\mathscr{B}_{2d}^{!})_{2}=(\xi_{1}+\dots+\xi_{d})\cdot(\mathscr{B}_{2d}^{!})_{1}
, i.e.,
\frk\mathscr{B}_{2d}=\dim(\mathscr{B}_{2d})_{1}-\cd\mathscr{B}_{2d}+1=2d-1
.

Theorem 4.5
.

The Lie algebra
\mathscr{B}_{2d}
is BK.

Proof.

Let
A
be the cohomology algebra of
\mathscr{B}_{2d}
with the presentation as above. By
[
28
, Prop. 20]
, for proving that
A
is universally Koszul, we need to show that
(I:b)
is a
1
-generated ideal of
A
for every
1
-generated ideal
I
of
A
and
b\in A_{1}\setminus I
.

So, let
I
be an ideal of
A
different from
A_{+}
and let
b\in A_{1}\setminus I
.

Since
A_{3}=0
, one has
A_{2}\subseteq(I:b)
.
Denote by
F
the ideal of
A
generated by
(I:b)_{1}
. We claim that
F
contains the elements
\xi_{i}\eta_{i}
for all
i
.

Set
b=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\xi_{j}+\beta_{j}\eta_{j}
.

Fix an index
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.

If
\alpha_{i}=0
, then
b\eta_{i}=0
and hence
\eta_{i}\in(I:b)
, so that
\xi_{i}\eta_{i}\in F
.

Let
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. Since
b\in(I:b)_{1}
, we get
\xi_{i}\eta_{i}=b\cdot(\alpha_{i}^{-1}\eta_{i})\in F
.
∎

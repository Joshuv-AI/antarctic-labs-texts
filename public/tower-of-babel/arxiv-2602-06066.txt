Semantic Limits of Positive Existential Reasoning in Arithmetic Dynamics
Madhav Dhiman, Rohan Pandey

Abstract
We study structural limitations of purely algebraic reasoning in the analysis of arithmetic dynamical systems. Rather than addressing the truth of specific conjectures, we introduce a fragment-relative notion of algebraic refutability for dynamical properties defined by polynomial relations. Using preservation of positive existential formulas under ring homomorphisms, we show that any behavior realizable in a homomorphic extension of ℤ \mathbb{Z} cannot be refuted as false by arguments confined to the positive existential fragment of first-order ring theory. Any argument that excludes such behaviors in the integers must invoke structure not preserved under ring homomorphisms, such as order, Archimedean properties, or global metric information. We illustrate the framework using the Collatz map as an example, clarifying the logical limitations of algebraic approaches without making claims about the conjecture’s truth or provability.



1
Introduction

Many problems in arithmetic dynamics resist resolution despite extensive effort. This resistance is often attributed to combinatorial complexity or analytic difficulty. In this paper, I adopt a different perspective: rather than asking whether a given dynamical property holds in
\mathbb{Z}
, I ask what logical
resources are required to rule it out.

Our focus is not on undecidability or independence, but on
fragment-relative
limitations
of algebraic reasoning. We formalize a notion of algebraic refutability tied to the positive existential fragment of first-order ring logic, which captures reasoning based solely on polynomial equations and existential
witnesses. This fragment is preserved under ring homomorphisms, making it insensitive to order, metric, and Archimedean structure.

The central observation of this paper is that when an algebraically defined dynamical behavior appears in a homomorphic extension of
\mathbb{Z}
—such as a

p
-adic completion or an ultraproduct—then no refutation confined to this algebraic fragment can distinguish the standard integers from the extension. Any successful exclusion of such behavior in
\mathbb{Z}
must therefore rely on additional structure not preserved by homomorphisms.We provide a framework that clarifies which proof strategies are inherently algebraic and which necessarily go beyond algebraic
structure.

This paper does not establish the truth or falsity of any specific conjecture in arithmetic dynamics, nor does it claim undecidability, unprovability, or formal independence results. In particular, the framework developed here does not assert limitations on all possible proof strategies, but only classifies those arguments whose content is confined to the positive existential fragment of first-order ring theory. No restriction is placed on the use of analytic, order-theoretic, Archimedean, or metric methods, and no claim is made that such methods are ineffective or unnecessary. The results are fragment-relative and semantic in nature: they identify preservation-theoretic obstructions to refutation rather than prohibitions on mathematical reasoning more broadly.

Collatz Conjecture as an Example

The Collatz map is used in this paper solely as a concrete illustration of the general preservation-theoretic framework developed in Sections 3–6. Its inclusion serves to demonstrate how a familiar arithmetic dynamical system can be expressed entirely within the positive existential fragment of ring theory, and how homomorphic extensions of the integers can realize algebraically defined behaviors absent from the classical setting. No claim is made regarding the truth, falsity, difficulty, or provability of the Collatz conjecture itself. All results concerning Collatz dynamics in this paper are methodological rather than number-theoretic. The central conclusions of the paper do not depend on properties specific to the Collatz map: the analysis applies equally to any arithmetic dynamical system defined by polynomial relations.

2
Formal Setting

We establish the model-theoretic foundation for our analysis, standardizing on the integers
\mathbb{Z}
as the base structure and adopting a strictly semantic notion of validity.

2.1
The Base Category

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}
denote the ring of integers with the standard operations of addition and multiplication. We work in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ring}}=\{+,\cdot,-,0,1\}
of commutative rings with unity. Let
T_{\text{ring}}
denote the theory of commutative rings with unity in this language. All rings considered in this paper are assumed to be commutative with unity.

All structures considered in this paper are models of
T_{\text{ring}}
, and all formulas are interpreted semantically in these models. Note that
\mathbb{Z}
is a model of
T_{\text{ring}}
, though
T_{\text{ring}}
does not axiomatize
\mathbb{Z}
uniquely.

2.2
Positive Existential Definability

We restrict attention to a fragment of first-order logic that captures algebraic relations without negation or universal quantification.

Definition 2.2
.

A formula
\phi(\mathbf{x})
in
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ring}}
is
positive existential
(denoted
\phi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
) if it has the form

\exists~\mathbf{y}\bigvee_{i}\bigwedge_{j}\left(p_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=q_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right),

where
p_{ij}
and
q_{ij}
are polynomials over
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ring}}
.

The fragment
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
is devoid of negation (
\neg
) and universal quantification (
\forall
). Crucially, formulas in
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
are preserved under ring homomorphisms by the standard preservation of positive existential formulas under ring homomorphisms: if
\phi\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
and
h:R\to S
is a ring homomorphism with
R\models\phi(\mathbf{a})
, then
S\models\phi(h(\mathbf{a}))
.

2.3
Semantic Algebraic Refutability

We now define the central notion of this paper: when a dynamical property can be ruled out using only ring-theoretic reasoning. This definition captures the semantic limit of algebraic proof, not any particular formal proof system.

Definition 2.3

(Algebraic Refutability)
.

Let
\Psi
be a dynamical property algebraically defined by a sentence
\varphi_{\Psi}\in\Sigma^{+}_{1}
.
We say that
\Psi
is
algebraically refutable
if there exists a finite set of sentences

\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\}\subset\Sigma^{+}_{1}
such that:

1.

\mathbb{Z}\models\theta_{i}
for all
i
, and

2.

\mathrm{Tring}\cup\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\}\models\neg\varphi_{\Psi}
.

3
Arithmetic Dynamical Systems

We formalize the notion of a dynamical system defined over a ring using only algebraic predicates. This restriction excludes global invariants and universal recurrence statements, which typically require negation or universal quantification.

3.1
Definition over Rings

Definition 3.1

(Arithmetic Dynamical System)
.

An
arithmetic dynamical system
(ADS) is a triple
\mathcal{D}=(R,S,T)
where:

•

R
is a commutative ring,

•

S\subseteq R^{k}
is the state space, defined by a formula
\sigma(\mathbf{x})\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
,

•

T\subseteq S\times S
is the transition relation, defined by a formula
\tau(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
.

The requirement that
S
and
T
be definable in
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
ensures that the dynamical system is specified using only polynomial equations and existential quantification, without negation or universal conditions.

Here and throughout, a subset
S\subseteq R^{k}
defined by a formula
\sigma(x)
is understood
as the definable set
\{a\in R^{k}:R\models\sigma(a)\}
.

3.2
Trajectories and Properties

Definition 3.2

(Trajectory)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(R,S,T)
be an ADS. A
trajectory
in
\mathcal{D}
is a sequence
(\mathbf{x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\mathbf{x}_{n}\in S
such that
T(\mathbf{x}_{n},\mathbf{x}_{n+1})
holds for all
n\geq 0
.

Definition 3.3

(Algebraically Defined Property)
.

A dynamical property
\Psi
is
algebraically defined
if there exists a sentence
\phi_{\Psi}\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
that asserts the existence of a trajectory satisfying
\Psi
.

Example 3.4
.

The property “there exists a periodic orbit of period
k
” can be expressed as

\phi_{\text{period-}k}=\exists~\mathbf{x}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{k-1}\left(\bigwedge_{i=0}^{k-1}\tau(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i+1\bmod k})\right),

which lies in
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
. We define periodic orbits without requiring distinctness of the points
\mathbf{x}_{i}
, as distinctness conditions (negation of equality) are not expressible in
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
without additional structure. This approach is standard in dynamics, where periodic orbits may contain repeated elements.

This definition is element-centric: it asks whether specific trajectory elements exist, not whether all elements satisfy a condition.

4
The Simulation Mechanism

We formalize how dynamical systems defined over
\mathbb{Z}
extend to other rings via homomorphisms, and how such extensions can exhibit behaviors absent in the original.

4.1
Homomorphic Simulation

Definition 4.1

(Simulation)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Z}}=(\mathbb{Z},S,T)
be an ADS over
\mathbb{Z}
, with
S
and
T
defined by formulas
\sigma
and
\tau
in
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
. A
simulation
of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a pair
(\mathcal{D}^{*},h)
where:

•

\mathcal{D}^{*}=(R^{*},S^{*},T^{*})
is the ADS over a ring
R^{*}
obtained by interpreting the same defining formulas
\sigma
and
\tau
in
R^{*}
,

•

h:\mathbb{Z}\to R^{*}
is a ring homomorphism.

Note that we do not require
h
to be injective; quotient maps are included as valid simulations.

Example 4.2
.

Key instances of simulations include:

•

The natural inclusion
\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
into the
p
-adic integers for any prime
p
.

•

The diagonal embedding
\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{*}
into an ultraproduct of
\mathbb{Z}
.

•

Any quotient map
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
.

The simulation
\mathcal{D}^{*}
inherits the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Z}}
but may possess additional elements and trajectories not present in
\mathbb{Z}
.

4.2
Ghost Realization

Definition 4.3

(Ghost Realizable Property)
.

Let
\Psi
be an algebraically defined property via sentence
\phi_{\Psi}\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
. We say that
\Psi
is
ghost realizable
if there exists a simulation
(\mathcal{D}^{*},h)
such that

R^{*}\models\phi_{\Psi}

but

\mathbb{Z}\not\models\phi_{\Psi}.

In other words,
\Psi
is realized semantically in the extension
R^{*}
but not in the standard integers. Such properties represent behaviors that are algebraically consistent with the dynamics but excluded by additional structure present in
\mathbb{Z}
.

5
The Homomorphic Preservation Barrier

We now establish the central result of this paper: ghost realizable properties cannot be refuted using ring axioms alone.

Theorem 5.1

(Homomorphic Preservation Barrier)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an arithmetic dynamical system over
\mathbb{Z}
, and let

\Psi
be an algebraically defined property.
If
\Psi
is ghost realizable, then
\Psi
is not algebraically refutable in the
sense of Definition 2.3.

Proof.

Assume
\Psi
is ghost realizable. Then there exists a commutative ring
R^{*}
and a ring homomorphism
h:\mathbb{Z}\to R^{*}
such that

R^{*}\models\phi_{\Psi}
.

Suppose for contradiction that
\Psi
is algebraically refutable. Then there exist
sentences
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
such that

T_{\mathrm{ring}}\cup\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\}\models\neg\phi_{\Psi}.

Since each
\theta_{i}
is positive existential, satisfaction of
\theta_{i}
is
preserved under ring homomorphisms. As
R^{*}
is a model of
T_{\mathrm{ring}}
,
we have
R^{*}\models\theta_{i}
for all
i
whenever
\mathbb{Z}\models\theta_{i}
.

Therefore
R^{*}
is a model of

T_{\mathrm{ring}}\cup\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\}
, and hence

R^{*}\models\neg\phi_{\Psi}
, contradicting
R^{*}\models\phi_{\Psi}
.

Thus
\Psi
is not algebraically refutable.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Theorem 5.1 is a direct consequence of preservation of
\Sigma^{+}_{1}
formulas under ring homomorphisms. Its contribution lies not in logical novelty, but in its use as a
classificatory principle for distinguishing algebraic proof strategies from those that
necessarily invoke non-preserved structure.

6
Non-Preservation as a Necessary Condition for Refutation

Having established that ghost realizable properties resist algebraic refutation, we now classify the additional logical resources required for their refutation.

Corollary 6.1

(Necessary Use of Non-Preserved Structure)
.

Let
\Psi
be an algebraically defined property that is ghost realizable via a simulation

(D^{*},h)
over a ring
R^{*}
. Then no refutation of
\Psi
that is semantically expressible
within the positive existential fragment of first-order ring logic can distinguish

\mathbb{Z}
from
R^{*}
.

Consequently, any valid argument establishing
\neg\Psi
in
\mathbb{Z}
must invoke
additional structure not preserved under
h
.

Remark 6.2
.

Corollary 6.2 should be read as a semantic classification rather than a claim about formal
provability in any specific deductive system. The obstruction identified here concerns
non-preservation of truth across homomorphic extensions, not limitations of particular proof
systems.

7
Application: The Collatz Conjecture

I illustrate the obstruction framework using a canonical example from arithmetic
dynamics: the Collatz conjecture. This section is intended purely as an application
of the general theory developed above.

7.1
Collatz as an Arithmetic Dynamical System

The Collatz map is defined on the positive integers by

T_{C}(x)=\begin{cases}x/2&\text{if }x\text{ is even},\\
3x+1&\text{if }x\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

To express this map as an arithmetic dynamical system in the sense of
Definition
3.1
, we work in the ring
\mathbb{Z}
and use an algebraic
characterization of parity. Sign considerations play no role in the present
discussion and are therefore ignored.

An integer
x
is even if and only if there exists
y
such that
x=2y
, and odd
if and only if there exists
y
such that
x=2y+1
. Accordingly, the transition
relation
\tau_{C}(x,x^{\prime})
may be defined by the
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
formula

\tau_{C}(x,x^{\prime})\equiv\bigl(\exists y\,(x=2y\wedge x^{\prime}=y)\bigr)\;\vee\;\bigl(\exists y\,(x=2y+1\wedge x^{\prime}=3(2y+1)+1)\bigr).

Thus the Collatz map constitutes an algebraically defined dynamical system in the
sense of Section 3.

7.2
The
2
-adic Obstruction

It is well known, following work of Lagarias
[
1
]
, that the Collatz
map extends naturally to the
2
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In this setting,
division by
2
is well-defined for all even
2
-adic integers, and the defining
formula for
\tau_{C}
continues to make sense when interpreted in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

The resulting
2
-adic dynamics exhibit behaviors absent from the classical
integer setting. In particular, Lagarias observed that
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
contains
periodic points of the Collatz map that do not correspond to periodic orbits in the
positive integers. More precisely, certain residue classes modulo sufficiently
large powers of
2
form closed periodic cycles under
T_{C}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Let
\Psi_{k}
denote the property “there exists a periodic orbit of period
k
”
for the Collatz map. As discussed in Section 3, this property is algebraically
defined by a sentence
\phi_{k}\in\Sigma_{1}^{+}
. For certain values of
k
arising
from the
2
-adic analysis, one has:

•

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\models\phi_{k}
, and

•

\mathbb{Z}\not\models\phi_{k}
,

the latter under the standard Collatz conjecture asserting that all positive
integer trajectories eventually reach the trivial
1\leftrightarrow 2
cycle.

The canonical inclusion
h:\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a ring
homomorphism, and interpreting the defining formulas of the Collatz system in

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
yields a simulation in the sense of
Definition
4.1
. Consequently, the properties
\Psi_{k}
are ghost
realizable in the sense of Definition
4.3
.

7.3
Interpretation and Relation to Prior Work

The role of the
2
-adic Collatz dynamics in the present framework is
methodological rather than number-theoretic. The existence of periodic points in

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a well-established phenomenon and does not contradict the
classical Collatz conjecture, which concerns only trajectories in the positive
integers.

Within the framework developed here, these
2
-adic cycles serve as witnesses to
the limitations of algebraic refutation relative to the positive existential
fragment of first-order ring logic. Because the Collatz transition relation is
algebraically definable and the corresponding periodic behaviors are realized in a
homomorphic extension of
\mathbb{Z}
, no refutation confined to

\Sigma_{1}^{+}
-expressible ring-theoretic reasoning can exclude them.

A restricted version of this observation, focused specifically on
2
-adic
extensions and Collatz cycles, was developed in earlier work

[
2
]
. The present paper generalizes that analysis by isolating the
underlying preservation-theoretic mechanism and situating it within a broader
framework applicable to all algebraically defined arithmetic
dynamical systems.

This perspective does not suggest that algebraic methods are ineffective in
practice, nor does it imply that the Collatz conjecture requires analytic tools for
its resolution. Rather, it clarifies that any argument distinguishing the integer
Collatz dynamics from their
2
-adic counterparts must invoke structural features
of
\mathbb{Z}
—such as order, Archimedean bounds, or global growth constraints—that
are not preserved under ring homomorphisms.

8
Discussion

8.1
Methodological Significance

The framework developed in this paper provides a semantic method for classifying
obstructions in arithmetic dynamics according to the logical resources required
to exclude them. My results identify a boundary between behaviors that are eliminable
within the positive existential fragment of first-order ring logic and those that
are not.

The Homomorphic Preservation Barrier
(Theorem
5.1
) gives a sufficient condition for
non-refutability relative to this fragment: if a dynamically defined behavior is
realized in a homomorphic extension of
\mathbb{Z}
, then no refutation whose
semantic content is confined to
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
can distinguish the standard integers
from that extension. Corollary 6.1 then identifies
representative forms of additional structure—such as order, Archimedean bounds,
or global growth constraints—that are necessarily invoked by any successful
refutation in
\mathbb{Z}
.

This perspective clarifies the landscape of admissible proof strategies by making explicit which logical resources must ultimately be engaged to separate integer dynamics from their algebraic simulations.

8.2
Limitations and Scope

The results presented here are inherently fragment-relative and classificatory.
They do not establish the truth or falsity of any specific dynamical property, nor
do they imply that particular conjectures are unprovable by standard mathematical
means. Rather, they delineate the semantic limitations of arguments whose content
is restricted to the positive existential consequences of ring theory.

Moreover, the framework applies only to properties that are themselves

\Sigma_{1}^{+}
-definable. Many natural dynamical questions—such as eventual
convergence of all trajectories or global boundedness—require universal
quantification or negation and therefore fall outside the present scope.
Extending the obstruction analysis to richer logical fragments remains an open
direction for further study.

8.3
Future Directions

The preservation-theoretic viewpoint developed here is applicable to a wide range
of arithmetic dynamical systems defined by polynomial relations. Potential
directions for further investigation include:

•

Fragment-relative analyses of properties such as eventual periodicity,
unbounded growth, or the existence of exceptional orbits.

•

Structural criteria for determining when a given dynamical property is
ghost realizable in a homomorphic extension of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Of particular interest is the development of effective or structural criteria for
ghost realizability, which could serve as a diagnostic tool for assessing which
logical resources are necessarily implicated by specific problems.

8.4
Concluding Remarks

This paper develops a semantic framework for analyzing proof constraints in
arithmetic dynamics through the lens of homomorphic preservation. The central
observation is that homomorphic extensions of
\mathbb{Z}
—such as
p
-adic
completions and ultraproducts—act as semantic witnesses to the limitations of
positive existential ring-theoretic reasoning. By formalizing algebraic
refutability relative to
\Sigma_{1}^{+}
and isolating the role of non-preserved
structure, we obtain a principled classification of the logical resources
required to distinguish integer dynamics from their algebraic simulations.

More broadly, this work contributes to the study of fragment-relative reasoning in
number theory and arithmetic dynamics. By making explicit the semantic boundaries
of algebraic methods, it aims to clarify how different strands of mathematical
structure interact in the analysis of long-standing dynamical problems.

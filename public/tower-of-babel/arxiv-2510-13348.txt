Abstract
. We consider bond percolation on the d d -dimensional binary hypercube with p = c / d p=c/d for fixed c > 1 c>1 . We prove that the typical diameter of the giant component L 1 L_{1} is of order Θ ⁡ ( d ) \Theta(d) , and the typical mixing time of the lazy random walk on L 1 L_{1} is of order Θ ⁡ ( d 2 ) \Theta(d^{2}) . This resolves long-standing open problems of Bollobás, Kohayakawa and Łuczak from 1994, and of Benjamini and Mossel from 2003. A key component in our approach is a new tight large deviation estimate on the number of vertices in L 1 L_{1} whose proof includes several novel ingredients: a structural description of the residue outside the giant component after sprinkling, a tight quantitative estimate on the spread of the giant in the hypercube, and a stability principle which rules out the disintegration of large connected sets under thinning. This toolkit further allows us to obtain optimal bounds on the expansion in L 1 L_{1} .

Let us note that the previous papers

A key missing ingredient in the previous works was a tight expansion estimate, in particular for large connected sets, in the giant component. Naively, one could hope to obtain such an estimate using a ‘reverse’ sprinkling, also known as ’thinning’, a process of removing edges with a given probability.
Indeed, if a large set in the giant component has a weak expansion, removing each of the edges in its boundary is ‘fairly cheap’ and results in two large components — a very atypical structure. However, this does not immediately imply the desired probability bound, as the set of deleted edges depends on the structure of the exposed random subgraph. It turns out that this constitutes the main challenge in resolving the conjectures. In this paper, we perform a quantitative analysis of small perturbations on the edges of the giant component, allowing us to resolve this challenge (see

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\big|f(X)-\mathbb{E}\left[f(X)\right]\big|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\frac{t^{2}}{2C^{2}mp+2Ct/3}\bigg).

We end this section with a switching lemma reminiscent of
[

Fix integers
k,d\geq 1
and denote by
\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)
the family of sequences of length

where every element in

k

A

consists of exchanging the positions of two elements in a sequence.
For two sequences
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)

\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
if they differ by a single switching.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
c>0
and a function
f:\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)\to\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that, for every pair
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)
with
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
, we have
|f(\sigma_{1})-f(\sigma_{2})|\leq c
.
Let

be chosen uniformly at random from
\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)
. Then, for every

,

\mathbb{P}(|f(\sigma)-\mathbb{E}[f(\sigma)]|\geq t)\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\frac{t^{2}}{2c^{2}kd}\bigg).

Proof.

Consider the map
\psi:\mathcal{S}_{kd}(1)\to\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)
which takes a sequence
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{kd}(1)
and outputs a sequence
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)
obtained by replacing the occurrences of
j+d,\cdots,j+(k-1)d
in
\pi^{\prime}
with
j
for every

.
For every element
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{d}(k)
there exist exactly
(k!)^{d}
elements
\mathcal{S}_{kd}(1)
which are mapped to
\pi
.
Thus, defining
\sigma^{\prime}
to be a uniformly chosen sequence in
\mathcal{S}_{kd}(1)
, it suffices to prove that, for every

,

\mathbb{P}(|f(\psi(\sigma^{\prime}))-\mathbb{E}[f(\psi(\sigma^{\prime}))]|\geq t)\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\frac{t^{2}}{2c^{2}kd}\bigg).

For every
i\in[0,kd]
, set
X_{i}=\mathbb{E}[f(\psi(\sigma^{\prime}))\mid\sigma^{\prime}(1),\ldots,\sigma^{\prime}(i)]
and note that
(X_{i})_{i=0}^{kd}
is a martingale. We show that,

(1)

\text{for every $i\in[kd]$,}\qquad|X_{i}-X_{i-1}|\leq c.

The lemma follows by combining (
1
) and Azuma’s inequality (see e.g.
[
36
, Theorem 2.25]
).

Observe that,

\displaystyle X_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[X_{i}\mid\sigma^{\prime}(1),...,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in[kd]\setminus\{\sigma^{\prime}(j):1\leq j<i\}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)=s}\mid\sigma^{\prime}(1),...,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in[kd]\setminus\{\sigma^{\prime}(j):1\leq j<i\}}\mathbb{P}(\sigma^{\prime}(i)=s|\sigma^{\prime}(1),...,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1))\cdot\mathbb{E}[X_{i}|\sigma^{\prime}(1),...,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1),\sigma^{\prime}(i)=s]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{kd-i+1}\sum_{s\in[kd]\setminus\{\sigma^{\prime}(j):1\leq j<i\}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}|\sigma^{\prime}(1),...,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1),\sigma^{\prime}(i)=s]

As a result, it suffices to show that the difference between the minimal and the maximal term in the latter sum is bounded from above by
c
.
To this end, fix any
\sigma^{\prime}(1),\ldots,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1)\subseteq[kd]
as well as two elements
s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in[kd]\setminus\{\sigma^{\prime}(j):1\leq j<i\}
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
) the set of sequences in
\mathcal{S}_{kd}(1)
which start with
\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(i-1)
and
\sigma(i)=s^{\prime}
(resp.
\sigma(i)=s^{\prime\prime}
). Then, the map
\phi:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
which takes a string
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and outputs the string obtained after switching the occurrences of
s^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}
is a bijection.
Further, for every
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, we have that
\psi(\sigma^{\prime})
and
\psi(\phi(\sigma^{\prime}))
differs by a single switching. Thus,
|f(\psi(\sigma^{\prime}))-f(\psi(\phi(\sigma^{\prime}))|\leq c
for every
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
As a consequence, for every
i\in[kd]
and every choice of
\sigma^{\prime}(1),\ldots,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1),s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}
as described, we have that

|\mathbb{E}[X_{i}|\sigma^{\prime}(1),...,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1),\sigma^{\prime}(i)=s^{\prime}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{i}|\sigma^{\prime}(1),...,\sigma^{\prime}(i-1),\sigma^{\prime}(i)=s^{\prime\prime}]|\leq c.

In turn, this implies (
1
) and finishes the proof.

3.5.
Branching processes and the BFS algorithm

We begin with a lemma quantifying the extinction probability of a supercritical Galton-Watson process with large total progeny.
Recall that, for a probability distribution
\mu
on non-negative integers, a
Galton-Watson process with offspring distribution
\mu
is a branching process starting from a root where every vertex produces a random number of children with distribution
\mu
independently of other vertices.
The following lemma is a simplified version of
[
32
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Fix a Galton-Watson process
T
with offspring distribution
\mu
with mean
\mathbb{E}\mu>1
.
Then, there is a constant
I=I(\mu)>0
such that, for every
k\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}(k\leq v(T)<\infty)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-Ik}/(1-\mathrm{e}^{-I})
.

An important parameter describing a Galton-Watson branching process is its
survival probability
given by the probability that the process contains an infinite number of vertices.
Following the introduction, we denote by
y(c)
the survival probability of a Galton-Watson process with offspring distribution Poisson(
c
) and by
b(n,p)
the survival probability of a Galton-Watson process with offspring distribution
\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
.
The following lemma compares these survival probabilities.

Lemma 3.13
.

Fix
c>1
. We have
y(c-\varepsilon)-y(c)=O_{c}(\varepsilon)
when
\varepsilon\to 0
, and
y(c)-b(d,c/d)=O_{c}(1/d)
.

Proof.

It is a classic fact (see e.g.
[
32
, Theorem 3.1]
) that the extinction probability of a Galton-Watson process with distribution
\mu
is given by the smallest solution in
[0,1]
of the equation

q=f_{\mu}(q)\qquad\text{where}\qquad f_{\mu}:t\in[0,1]\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mu(i)t^{i}.

In particular,
\bar{y}(c):=1-y(c)
is the smallest solution in
[0,1]
to
f(c,q)=q
where
f(c,q):=\mathrm{e}^{c(q-1)}
while
\bar{b}(d,c/d):=1-b(d,c/d)
is the smallest solution in
[0,1]
to
f_{d}(c,q)=q
where
f_{d}(c,q):=(1-c/d+cq/d)^{d}
.
To establish the first equality in the statement of the lemma, we use the implicit function theorem (see e.g.
[
52
, Theorem 2-12]
) to show that
\bar{y}
is a differentiable function on the interval
(1,\infty)
. To this end, it suffices to verify that

(5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial q}(f(c,q)-q)=c\mathrm{e}^{c(q-1)}-1\neq 0\qquad\text{whenever $c>1$ and $q$ satisfy}\qquad\mathrm{e}^{c(q-1)}=q.

Assuming for contradiction that both equalities hold would mean that
cq=1
, further implying that
c\mathrm{e}^{1-c}=1
. However, immediate analysis shows that
c\mathrm{e}^{1-c}<1
for every
c>1
. As a result,
\bar{y}
(and hence
y
as well) is a differentiable function of
c
on the interval
(1,\infty)
. The first equality follows.

Furthermore, it is immediate that
(f_{d})_{d\geq 1}
and
(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial q}f_{d})_{d\geq 1}
approximate uniformly
f
and
\tfrac{\partial}{\partial q}f
on the interval
q\in[0,1]
with
\|f(c,\cdot)-f_{d}(c,\cdot)\|_{\infty}=O_{c}(1/d)
and
\|\tfrac{\partial}{\partial q}f(c,\cdot)-\tfrac{\partial}{\partial q}f_{d}(c,\cdot)\|_{\infty}=O_{c}(1/d)
.
Moreover, for every
c>1
,

\displaystyle\bar{b}(d,c/d)-\bar{y}(c)

\displaystyle=f_{d}(c,\bar{b}(d,c/d))-f(c,\bar{y}(c))

\displaystyle=f_{d}(c,\bar{b}(d,c/d))-f(c,\bar{b}(d,c/d))+f(c,\bar{b}(d,c/d))-f(c,\bar{y}(c))

\displaystyle=O_{c}(1/d)+(\bar{b}(d,c/d)-\bar{y}(c))\frac{\partial}{\partial q}f(c,\bar{y}(c))+o(\bar{b}(d,c/d)-\bar{y}(c)).

Since
\frac{\partial}{\partial q}f(c,\bar{y}(c))\neq 1
by (
5
), the second equality in the statement of the lemma follows.
∎

Upper tail

In this section, we fix

Recall the survival probability

. It is a classic fact

that a Galton-Watson process with offspring distribution Poisson(

with probability
\mathrm{e}^{-ck}(ck)^{k-1}/k!

(6)

y(c):=1-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ck}\frac{(ck)^{k-1}}{k!}.

We note that, when

, the weights
\mathrm{e}^{-ck}(ck)^{k-1}/k!
describe the
Borel probability distribution
over the positive integers and
y(c)=0
.

We are now ready to prove the upper tail estimate promised in
Theorem

2
.

Proof of the upper tail estimate in Theorem
2
.

For every
k\in[d]
, denote by
V_{k}
the set of vertices in components of order
k
in
Q^{d}_{p}
.
To estimate
\mathbb{E}[|V_{k}|]
, fix a vertex
v
and list all trees
T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}\subseteq Q^{d}
of order
k
rooted at
v
. Then,

\mathbb{P}(v\in V_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\{T_{i}\subseteq Q^{d}_{p}\}\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{i-1}\{T_{j}\not\subseteq Q^{d}_{p}\}\bigg)\geq rp^{k-1}(1-p)^{kd},

where the last inequality holds since each event requires
k-1
open edges and less than
kd
closed edges.
Choosing
k=k(d)\in[1,d^{1/4}]
and combining the latter bound with
Lemma

3.5
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|V_{k}|]

\displaystyle\geq n\cdot\frac{k^{k-2}(d-k)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}p^{k-1}(1-p)^{dk}=(1-O(d^{-1/2}))n\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-ck}\frac{(ck)^{k-1}}{k!},

where we used that
(d-k)^{k-1}p^{k-1}=(1-O(d^{-1/2}))c^{k-1}
and
(1-p)^{kd}=(1-O(d^{-1/2}))\mathrm{e}^{-ck}
, with the implicit constant in the

Now, for every integer
t\in[n]
, define

f(t,n):=\left\lceil\frac{1}{-\log(\mathrm{e}c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-c})}\log\bigg(\frac{4n}{t(1-\mathrm{e}c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-c})}\bigg)\right\rceil.

Then, for every
t\in[n]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=f(t,n)}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ck}\frac{(ck)^{k-1}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=f(t,n)}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-ck}(ck)^{k}}{(k/\mathrm{e})^{k}}=\sum_{k=f(t,n)}^{\infty}(\mathrm{e}c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-c})^{k}\leq\frac{(\mathrm{e}c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-c})^{f(t,n)}}{1-\mathrm{e}c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-c}}\leq\frac{t}{4n},

where we used that
\mathrm{e}c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-c}<1
when
c>1
for the second inequality. By combining the latter chain of inequalities with (
6

we obtain that, for all

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{f(t,n)}|V_{k}|\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1-O(d^{-1/2}))n\bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ck}\frac{(ck)^{k-1}}{k!}-\sum_{k=f(t,n)+1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ck}\frac{(ck)^{k-1}}{k!}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-y(c)-O(d^{-1/2})\right)n-\frac{t}{4}.

Now, fix
t\in[n/d^{0.1},n]
and set
Z=\sum_{k=1}^{f(t,n)}|V_{k}|
. Since adding or removing one edge from
Q^{d}_{p}
can change the value of
Z
by at most
2f(t,n)
, we have that, by the bounded difference inequality (Lemma
3.2
), for every
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z\leq\mathbb{E}Z-m\right)\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{m^{2}}{2(2f(t,n))^{2}cn+2(2f(t,n))m/3}\bigg).

Therefore, for all
t\in[n/d^{0.1},n]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(v(L_{1})\geq y(c)n+t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(Z\leq\mathbb{E}Z-(t-t/4-O(d^{-1/2}n))\leq\mathbb{P}(Z\leq\mathbb{E}Z-t/2)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{(t/2)^{2}}{2(2f(t,n))^{2}cn+2(2f(t,n))(t/2)/3}\bigg)\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{\varepsilon t^{2}}{n(\log(n/t))^{2}}\bigg),

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(c)>0
is a constant depending only on
c
, as required.
∎

5.
Lower tail

In this section, we set
p=p(d)=c/d
and fix a suitably small constant
\delta=\delta(c)\in(0,1)
(such that, in particular,
c-\delta>1
).
We also set
p_{1}=p_{1}(d)=(c-\delta)/d
and
p_{2}
such that
(1-p_{1})(1-p_{2})=1-p
; note that

Further set

and

, where we stress that

is sampled independently from

. Note that

has the same distribution as

. We further abbreviate

.

Before proving the bound on the lower tail in Theorem
2
, some preparation is necessary. This section is structured as follows.
First, in Section
5.1
, we establish tail bounds on the number of vertices in components of order at least
d^{10}
in

. Then, in Section
5.2
, we establish quantitative bounds on the probability of certain typical structural properties of

and describe the interaction between

and

. Finally, in Section

.

5.1.
Vertices in components of order at least
d^{10}

In this section, for every
\alpha\geq 0
, denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
the set of components in
G_{2}
of order at least
d^{\alpha}
.
Our first lemma studies
\mathcal{M}_{1/4}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
t\in[n/d^{1/9},n]
, we have

\mathbb{P}(|v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})-y(c)n|\geq t)\leq\exp(-t^{2}/(40cd^{1/2}n))
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V(Q^{d})
.
We first estimate the probability that
v
is in a component in
\mathcal{M}_{1/4}
.
To this end, we explore
Q^{d}_{p}
by running the BFS algorithm from
Section

3.5
with the following modifications: we start the algorithm with
U=\{v\}
and terminate the process when either
U
becomes empty or
|S\cup U|
contains at least
d^{1/4}
vertices.
Note that
v\in V(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})
if and only if the BFS process terminates due to the second stopping condition.
We let
T_{1}
be the tree rooted at
v
which is constructed by the BFS. Note that
T_{1}
has at most
d^{1/4}
vertices.

Since
|S\cup U|\leq d^{1/4}
throughout the execution of the algorithm, at all times, every vertex
u\in U
satisfies
|N_{Q^{d}}(u)\setminus(S\cup U)|\geq d-d^{1/4}
.
As a result, we can (and do) couple the process
T_{1}
with Galton-Watson processes
T_{2}
and
T_{3}
rooted at
v
with offspring distributions respectively
\mathrm{Bin}(d-d^{1/4},p)
and
\mathrm{Bin}(d,p)
so that:

•

if
v(T_{2})=\infty
, then
v(T_{1})=d^{1/4}
, and

•

if
v(T_{1})=d^{1/4}
, then
v(T_{3})\geq d^{1/4}
.

Now, on the one hand, by
Lemma

3.13
, we have

\mathbb{P}(v\in V(\mathcal{M}_{1/4}))\geq\mathbb{P}(|v(T_{2})|=\infty)\geq y((d-d^{1/4})p)-O_{c}(1/d)\geq y(c)-O_{c}(d^{-3/4}).

On the other hand, by
Lemma

3.12
and
Lemma

3.13
, there is
I=I(c)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(v\in V(\mathcal{M}_{1/4}))\leq\mathbb{P}(|T_{3}|\geq d^{1/4})=b(d,c/d)+O_{c}(\mathrm{e}^{-Id^{1/4}})=y(c)+O_{c}(1/d).

Combining the latter bounds implies that
|\mathbb{E}[v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})]-y(c)n|=O_{c}(n/d^{3/4})
.
Furthermore, note that adding or removing one edge from
Q^{d}_{p}
can change
v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})
by at most
2d^{1/4}
.
Thus, by the bounded difference inequality (Lemma
3.2
), for every
t\in[n/d^{1/9},n]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})-y(c)|\geq t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})-\mathbb{E}[v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})]|\geq t/2)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\frac{t^{2}/4}{2(2d^{1/4})^{2}\cdot(nd/2)\cdot p+2(2d^{1/4})\cdot t/6)}\bigg)\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{t^{2}}{40cd^{1/2}n}\bigg),

as required.
∎

Next, we turn our attention to the family
\mathcal{M}_{10}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{1/4}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There is a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(c)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(v(\mathcal{M}_{10}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{1/4})\geq n/d^{1/9})\leq\exp(-\varepsilon n/d^{1/9})
.

Proof.

Given a family
\mathcal{C}
of vertex-disjoint connected sets of order at most
d^{10}
with
v(\mathcal{C})\geq n/d^{1/9}
,
Lemma

3.11
implies that, for any suitably small
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, there is a subfamily
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
such that
v(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\geq\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}
and
e_{\mathrm{out}}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\geq(1-2\varepsilon_{1})v(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
.
It thus suffices to estimate the probability that there exists such a family
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of components of
G_{2}
where each component in the family has order between
d^{1/4}
and
d^{10}
.

To this end, fix
k\geq 1
and integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in[d^{1/4},d^{10}]
of sum
x\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\in[\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9},n]
.
By Lemma
3.5
, the number of forests in
Q^{d}
containing
k
trees of orders
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
is bounded from above by
\binom{n}{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\mathrm{e}d)^{x_{i}-1}=\binom{n}{k}(\mathrm{e}d)^{x-k}
.
At the same time, the probability that each tree in such a forest
F
spans a connected component of
G_{2}
is bounded from above by
p^{x-k}(1-p)^{e_{\mathrm{out}}(F)}\leq p^{x-k}(1-p)^{(1-2\varepsilon_{1})xd}
.
Thus, by the union bound, the probability that a family
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with the described properties exists is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{x=\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}}^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=x\\
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in[d^{1/4},d^{10}]\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{k}(\mathrm{e}d)^{x-k}p^{x-k}(1-p)^{(1-2\varepsilon_{1})xd}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x=\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=x\\
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in[d^{1/4},d^{10}]\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{d^{-1/4}x}\exp((\log\left(\mathrm{e}c\right)-(1-2\varepsilon_{1})c)x)

(7)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x=\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=x\\
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in[d^{1/4},d^{10}]\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}n}{d^{-1/4}x}\right)^{d^{-1/4}x}\exp((\log\left(\mathrm{e}c\right)-(1-2\varepsilon_{1})c)x),

where the first inequality uses that
k\leq d^{-1/4}x
. To simplify (
7
), note that there are at most
(d^{10})^{d^{-1/4}x}
ways to choose
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in[d^{1/4},d^{10}]
with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=x
.
Furthermore, by choosing
\varepsilon_{1}
suitably small with respect to
c
and using that
c>1
, we have that
c_{0}:=(1-2\varepsilon_{1})c-\log(\mathrm{e}c)>0
.
Thus, (
7
) is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{x=\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}}^{n}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}nd^{10+1/4}}{x}\right)^{d^{-1/4}x}\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}x}\leq\sum_{\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}}^{n}\exp\bigg(x\left(\frac{1}{d^{1/4}}\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}nd^{41/4}}{x}\right)-c_{0}\right)\bigg).

Now, since
x\geq\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}
, we have
\log(\mathrm{e}nd^{10}/x)=O(\log d)
. Altogether, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(v(\mathcal{M}_{10}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{1/4})\geq n/d^{1/9})\leq\sum_{\varepsilon_{1}n/d^{1/9}}^{n}\exp(-xc_{0}/2)\leq n\exp(-\varepsilon_{1}c_{0}n/(2d^{1/9})).

Thus, setting
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}c_{0}/3
finishes the proof.
∎

With the last two lemmas at hand, we conclude this subsection with the following deviation bound for
\mathcal{M}_{10}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

For every
t\in[n/d^{1/9},n]
,
\mathbb{P}(|v(\mathcal{M}_{10})-y(c)n|\geq 2t)\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}/(40cd^{1/2}n))
.

Proof.

Since
v(\mathcal{M}_{10})=v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})-v(\mathcal{M}_{10}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{1/4})
, Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|v(\mathcal{M}_{10})-y(c)n|\geq 2t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(\{|v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})-y(c)n|\geq t\}\cup\{v(\mathcal{M}_{10}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{1/4})\geq t\})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|v(\mathcal{M}_{1/4})-y(c)n|\geq t)+\mathbb{P}(v(\mathcal{M}_{10}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{1/4})\geq n/d^{1/9})

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-t^{2}/(40cd^{1/2}n))+\exp(-\varepsilon n/d^{1/9})\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}/(40cd^{1/2}n)).\qed

Recall the graphs

and

defined in the beginning of

5

Also, recall the families

from
Section

Lemma 5.4
.

For all suitably small constants
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(c,\delta)>0
,
\gamma=\gamma(\varepsilon)>0

Proof.

Fix suitably small
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(c),\gamma=\gamma(\varepsilon)>0
to be determined in the sequel and

Claim 5.5
.

Suppose that

. Then, there exist a set

, pairwise disjoint families of components

, and a set of edges

satisfying each of the following properties:

•

for every

•

for every

•

for every

vertices

•

jointly cover at least
\varepsilon t/2

Proof of
5.5
.

For some
m\geq 1
, we construct a sequence of sets
U_{0}=\varnothing\subseteq U_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq U_{m}=U
with
|U_{i}|=i
for every

as follows.

First, we underline that, throughout the process, vertices in

. We now continue assuming
i\geq 1
. At the

u
in
W_{i-1}
. Then, set
U_{i}=U_{i-1}\cup\{u\}
and let
\mathcal{C}_{u}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{[0,2)}^{-}
be a family of components disjoint from

where the following conditions jointly hold:

•

each component contains at least one neighbour (in

) of

, and

•

there exists a set of edges

between

and

of size exactly

such that each component

.

Note that such a family exists by our assumption on

. Then, we set
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\mathcal{C}_{i-1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{u}
and define
W_{i}
as the subset of vertices in

\varepsilon d
neighbours in

(and, in extension, at least

neighbours in

, as required). We stress here that, by construction, every vertex in

has at least
\varepsilon d
neighbours in
V(\mathcal{C}_{i})
.
The procedure ends at the first step

when

.

We set
E^{\prime}=\bigcup_{u\in U}E^{\prime}_{u}
. Now, since every vertex in
U_{m}
has at least

\varepsilon d|V_{\mathrm{bad}}(\varepsilon)\setminus U_{m}|\leq dv(\mathcal{C}_{m})

. As a result,

t-\frac{v(\mathcal{C}_{m})}{1-2\varepsilon}\leq t-|U_{m}|\leq|V_{\mathrm{bad}}(\varepsilon)\setminus U_{m}|\leq\frac{v(\mathcal{C}_{m})}{\varepsilon}.

Assuming that
\varepsilon<0.1
, the above inequality implies that
v(\mathcal{C}_{m})\geq\varepsilon t/2
, as required.
∎

Next, conditionally on the event
|V_{\mathrm{bad}}(\varepsilon)|\geq t
and on the graph
G_{1}
, fix a set
U
, a family
\mathcal{C}
and a set

as in
5.5
.
Recall that, for each
u\in U
, every
C\in\mathcal{C}_{u}
is adjacent to
u
, and

. Therefore
v(\mathcal{C}_{u})\leq d^{3}
.
Thus, we can apply
Lemma

3.11
to the family of vertex disjoint sets
(V(\mathcal{C}_{u}))_{u\in U}
, and obtain that, for any sufficiently small
\eta>0
, there is a set
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
such that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{u\in U^{\prime}}\mathcal{C}_{u}
satisfies

(8)

\displaystyle v(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\geq\eta v(\mathcal{C})\geq\eta\varepsilon t/2\quad\text{ and }\quad e_{\mathrm{out}}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\geq(1-2\eta)dv(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}).

Fix such sets
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
for a suitably small
\eta=\eta(c)>0
to be determined in the sequel.

Set
d^{\prime}:=(1-2\varepsilon)d
and
m\coloneqq|U^{\prime}|
. For every

, denote by

the set of

-neighbours of

and set

.
Observe that

contains a spanning forest

where every tree in

intersects the set

.
Furthermore, by starting with

and recursively removing one edge on a path connecting two vertices in

with

trees where each tree contains one vertex in

(called its

. In particular, we have that
v(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=d^{\prime}m+|E(F)|
.
To complete the proof, we bound the probability that such a forest

exists in

. We note that the argument from the proof of

would be suboptimal here, and we utilise

First, there are

.
Then, for every

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{e}n}{m}\bigg)^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{(2\varepsilon)^{2\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon)^{1-2\varepsilon}}\bigg)^{dm}

\displaystyle\bigg(1+\frac{d^{\prime}m}{k}\bigg)^{k}(\mathrm{e}dp)^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-(1-2\eta)(c-\delta)(d^{\prime}m+k)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{d^{\prime}m}(\mathrm{e}c)^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-(1-2\eta)(c-\delta)(d^{\prime}m+k)}

(11)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{e}^{1-(1-2\eta)(c-\delta)})^{d^{\prime}m}(c\mathrm{e}^{1-(1-2\eta)(c-\delta)})^{k}.

At this point, we choose
\eta=\eta(c,\delta)
suitably small so that

(12)

(1-2\eta)(c-\delta)\geq 1+2\eta\qquad\text{and}\qquad c\cdot\mathrm{e}^{1-(1-2\eta)(c-\delta)}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\eta},

where we recall that
\delta=\delta(c)
is sufficiently small.
In particular, (
12
) implies that the expression in (
5.2
) is at most
\mathrm{e}^{-2\eta d^{\prime}m-\eta k}

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\eta,c)
suitably small ensuring that (

\mathrm{e}^{\eta d^{\prime}m}
.
Recalling that
d^{\prime}m+k=v(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\geq\eta\varepsilon t/2
due to (
8
), by combining the latter estimates with (
9
) and the union bound over all

, the probability that the desired forest

exists is at most

n\max_{m\in[\eta\varepsilon t/2d^{3},n]}\bigg\{\sum_{k=\max\{0,\eta\varepsilon t/2-d^{\prime}m\}}^{n-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta d^{\prime}m-\eta k}\bigg\}\leq n^{2}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-\eta^{2}\varepsilon t/2}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\nu t}

where

, completing the proof.
∎

Recall

Lemma 5.6
.

For every suitably small

with the following property:
the probability that there is a vertex set

simultaneously satisfying

First, we introduce some terminology related to Markov chains (for a comprehensive introduction, see

For a graph

G

and such that,

with probability

and moves to a uniformly chosen neighbour of

G
with probability
1/(2\deg(v))
.
If
G
is a connected graph, the lazy random walk on
G
is an irreducible, reversible and ergodic Markov chain with
stationary distribution

\pi
on
V
where
\pi(v)=\deg(v)/(2e(G))
.
Our goal is to determine the speed of convergence of the lazy random walk to its stationary distribution.
To this end, we define the
total variation distance

d_{\mathrm{TV}}
between two distributions
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
on
V
by setting

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}):=\max_{A\subseteq V}|\pi_{1}(A)-\pi_{2}(A)|.

For

and

, we denote by
P^{t}(u,\cdot)
the distribution of the
t

G
starting at
u
.
Then, the
mixing time
of the lazy random walk is defined as

t_{\mathrm{mix}}:=\min\{t\geq 0:d(t)\leq 1/4\}\qquad\text{where, for every $t\geq 0$,}\qquad d(t):=\max_{u\in V}d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P^{t}(u,\cdot),\pi).

For every set
S\subseteq V
, observe that

\displaystyle\pi(S)=\sum_{v\in S}\pi(v)=\frac{2e(S)+e(S,V\setminus S)}{2e(G)}\quad\text{and set}\quad Q(S):=\sum_{v\in S,u\in V\setminus S}\pi(v)P^{1}(v,u)=\frac{e(S,V\setminus S)}{4e(G)}.

The

\Phi(S)
of

\displaystyle\Phi(S):=\frac{Q(S)}{\pi(S)\pi(V\setminus S)}.

Note that, since

\Phi(S)=\Phi(V\setminus S)
.
Further, we denote

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho):=\min\left\{\Phi(S):S\subseteq V,\rho/2\leq\pi(S)\leq\rho,\text{$S$ is connected in }G\right\},

\Phi(\rho)=1
. The following theorem of Fountoulakis and Reed

]

Theorem 6.4

]

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{mix}}\leq K\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil\log_{2}(\pi_{\min}^{-1})\rceil}\frac{1}{\Phi^{2}(2^{-j})}.

), we encode the sequence
\{\phi(R_{i})\}_{i\in B^{-}}
as follows. Let
y^{*}:=\min\{x_{s},kd-r-2\ell\}
.
First, we specify even
y_{s}\leq 2\lfloor y^{*}/2\rfloor
and then the values of
y_{i}
for
i\in B^{-}
.
Given
y_{i}
and
x_{i}
, we specify the elements in
S_{i}^{\mathrm{rep}}
with multiplicities and their positions in the sequence
\phi(R_{i})
; this is done in at most
\binom{x_{i}+y_{i}}{y_{i}}d^{y_{i}}
ways by first specifying the
y_{i}
positions in
\phi(R_{i})
and then the element that appears in each of these positions. Finally, we specify the order in which the elements in
S_{i}^{\mathrm{odd}}
appear in
\phi(R_{i})
.
By denoting for convenience by
\mathrm{Ev}(y^{*})
the set of even integers in
[0,y^{*}]
and by
\mathrm{EvPar}(t)
the set of ordered integer partitions of an even integer
t
into
|B^{-}|
non-negative even integers
(y_{i})
with
y_{i}\leq x_{i}
for every
i\in B^{-}
, the number of choices the sequence
\{\phi(R_{i})\}_{i\in B^{-}}
is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{s}\in\mathrm{Ev}(y^{*})}\sum_{(y_{i})\in\mathrm{EvPar}(y_{s})}\prod_{i\in B^{-}}\binom{x_{i}+y_{i}}{y_{i}}d^{y_{i}}(x_{i})!

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y_{s}\in\mathrm{Ev}(y^{*})}\sum_{(y_{i})\in\mathrm{EvPar}(y_{s})}\prod_{i\in B^{-}}2^{x_{i}+y_{i}}d^{y_{i}}(x_{i})!

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y_{s}\in\mathrm{Ev}(y^{*})}\binom{y_{s}/2+|B^{-}|}{|B^{-}|}2^{x_{s}+y_{s}}d^{y_{s}}\prod_{i\in B^{-}}(x_{i})!,

where in the second inequality we used an upper bound on
|\mathrm{EvPar}(y_{s})|
. Further, using that
y_{s}/2+|B^{-}|\leq y_{s}/2+x_{s}/2\leq x_{s}
, the last sum is further bounded from above by

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{s}\in\mathrm{Ev}(y^{*})}2^{x_{s}}2^{x_{s}+y_{s}}d^{y_{s}}\prod_{i\in B^{-}}(x_{i})!\leq\sum_{y_{s}\in\mathrm{Ev}(y^{*})}2^{3x_{s}}d^{y_{s}}\prod_{i\in B^{-}}(x_{i})!.

Step 4:
It remains to deal with the long paths outside
\mathcal{I}(P,P^{\prime})
.
For every
j\in[d]
and
t\in[k]
, if
j
appears exactly
t
times in the sequences
\{\phi(Z)\}_{Z\in\mathcal{I}(P,P^{\prime})}
and
\{\phi(R_{i})\}_{i\in B^{-}}
, then it appears exactly
k-t
times in the sequences
\{\phi(R_{i})\}_{i\in B^{+}}
.
Thus, given the sequences
\{\phi(Z)\}_{Z\in\mathcal{I}(P,P^{\prime})}
and
\{\phi(R_{i})\}_{i\in B^{-}}
, the elements in
\{\phi(R_{i})\}_{i\in B^{+}}
are determined: observe that their number is at most
kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s}
.
Thus, we can determine the sequences
\{\phi(R_{i})\}_{i\in B^{+}}
by specifying a sequence on at most
kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s}
underlying elements and then partitioning it into
|B^{+}|\leq k^{4}
consecutive subsequences.

For integers
a\in[0,k]
and
b\in[0,d-1]
, we set
\mathrm{mult}(ad+b):=(a!)^{d}(a+1)^{b}
: note that
\mathrm{mult}(t)
is the minimal number of permutations of the elements of a sequence on
[d]
which leaves this sequence unchanged.
For example,
\mathrm{mult}(d+1)=2
since every sequence
\sigma
on
d+1
elements has two repeating elements and exchanging their positions would not change
\sigma
.
By the above reasoning, the number of ways to determine the long paths along
P^{-}
is bounded from above by

(37)

\frac{(kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s})!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s})}\binom{kd}{k^{4}}\leq\frac{d^{k^{4}+1}(kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s})!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s})}.

As the procedure described in Steps 1–4 for constructing the path
P^{-}
is injective, we have that
|\mathcal{Y}|
is bounded from above by the product of the last expressions in (
33
), (
34
), (
36
) and (
37
).

Next, we bound from above the product of (
36
) and (
37
).
To this end, we note that, for all integers
a,b\geq 1
with
k^{4}a\leq b
, we have that
a!b!\leq(a-1)!(b+1)!/k^{4}
.
Combining this observation with the fact that
x_{i}\geq 2
for every
i\in[\ell]
and that
kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s}\geq x_{b}+y_{b}\geq k^{4}x_{i}
for every
i\in B^{-}
when
B^{-}\neq\varnothing
, we obtain that

(38)

\bigg(\prod_{i\in B^{-}}x_{i}!\bigg)\cdot(kd-r-x_{s}-y_{s})!\leq 2^{|B^{-}|}\cdot\frac{(kd-r-2|B^{-}|-y_{s})!}{(k^{4})^{x_{s}-2|B^{-}|}}.

Note also that, for every
i\in[kd]
,
\mathrm{mult}(i)/k\leq\mathrm{mult}(i-1)
. Recalling that
y^{*}=\min\{x_{s},kd-r-2\ell\}
(so, in particular,
y^{*}\leq x_{s}
),
|B^{-}|\in[\max\{0,\ell-k^{4}\},\ell]
(so, in particular,
2\ell-2|B^{-}|\leq 2k^{4}
) and using (
35
) and (
38
), the product of (
36
) and (
37
) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{s}=0}^{y^{*}}2^{3x_{s}}d^{y_{s}}2^{|B^{-}|}\cdot\frac{d^{k^{4}+1}(kd-r-2|B^{-}|-y_{s})!/(k^{4})^{x_{s}-2|B^{-}|}}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-r-y_{s})/k^{x_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y_{s}=0}^{y^{*}}d^{y_{s}}\bigg(\frac{2}{k}\bigg)^{3x_{s}}d^{k^{4}+1}(2k^{8})^{|B^{-}|}\frac{(kd-r-2|B^{-}|-y_{s})!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-r-y_{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y_{s}=0}^{kd-r-2\ell}\bigg(\frac{8d}{k^{3}}\bigg)^{y_{s}}d^{k^{4}+1}k^{10\ell}\frac{(kd-r-2\ell-y_{s})!(kd)^{2\ell-2|B^{-}|}}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-r-y_{s})}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y_{s}=0}^{kd-r-2\ell}\bigg(\frac{8d}{k^{3}}\bigg)^{y_{s}}d^{3k^{4}+1}k^{12\ell}\frac{(kd-r-2\ell-y_{s})!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-r-y_{s})}.

Using (
7.1.2
) to bound the product of (
36
) and (
37
), the product of (
33
), (
34
), (
36
) and (
37
) is at most

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Y}|

\displaystyle\ \leq d^{4k^{4}+2}\binom{kd}{2\ell}2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}\sum_{y_{s}=0}^{kd-r-2\ell}\bigg(\frac{8d}{k^{3}}\bigg)^{y_{s}}\frac{(kd-r-2\ell-y_{s})!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-r-y_{s})}.

Finally, the proof is completed by the following claim applied with
z=r+y_{s}\geq|\mathcal{I}(P,P^{\prime})|\geq\ell-1
, summing over at most
kd
choices for
r
and
\ell
, and using that
4k^{4}+6<k^{5}
and
16(1+\eta)<k^{3}
so that
\sum_{i\geq 0}(8(1+\eta)/k^{3})^{i}=O(1)
).

Claim 7.10
.

Fix suitably small
\eta>0
and an integer
k=k(\eta)\in[\eta^{-4},\eta^{-5}]
. Given
z,\ell\in[0,kd]
with
z+2\ell\leq kd
and
\ell\leq z+1
, we have that

(40)

\binom{kd}{2\ell}2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}\frac{(kd-2\ell-z)!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}\leq d\left(\frac{1+\eta}{d}\right)^{z}\frac{(kd)!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}=d\left(\frac{1+\eta}{d}\right)^{z}\frac{(kd)!}{(k!)^{d}}.

Proof.

We consider 3 cases based on the values of
\ell
and
z
.

Case 1:
\ell\geq 100k^{2}d/\log d
.
Then,
\ell\leq z+1
and
z+2\ell\leq kd
imply that
kd-2\ell\geq kd/4
and, in extension,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{z-1}\frac{1}{kd-2\ell-i}\leq\frac{(kd)^{kd-2\ell-z}}{(kd-2\ell)!}\leq\frac{(kd)^{kd-2\ell-z}}{((kd-2\ell)/\mathrm{e})^{kd-2\ell}}\leq\frac{k^{kd}}{d^{z}}.

Therefore, if
\ell\geq 100k^{2}d/\log d
, then

\displaystyle\binom{kd}{2\ell}2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}\frac{(kd-2\ell-z)!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(kd)!}{(2\ell)!(kd-2\ell)!}2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}(kd-2\ell)!\prod_{i=0}^{z-1}\frac{1}{kd-2\ell-i}

\displaystyle\leq(kd)!\frac{d^{2}(2k)^{12\ell}(\ell/\mathrm{e})^{\ell}}{(2\ell/\mathrm{e})^{2\ell}}\frac{k^{kd}}{d^{z}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(kd)!k^{kd}}{\ell^{0.9\ell}d^{z}}\leq\frac{(kd)!}{d^{z}}e^{-50k^{2}d}\leq\frac{1}{d^{z}}\frac{(kd)!}{(k!)^{d}}.

Case 2:
\ell<100k^{2}d/\log d
and
dk-2\ell-z\geq 2d
.
Fix integers
a\in[0,k]
and
b\in[0,d-1]
such that
z=ad+b
. Also, set
z^{*}:=kd-\eta^{-2}d
.

If
z\leq z^{*}
, then
k-a\geq\eta^{-2}
and we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{z-1}\frac{1}{kd-2\ell-i}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=2}^{a+1}\left(\frac{1}{(k-i)d}\right)^{d}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{(k-a-2)d}\right)^{b}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=0}^{a-1}\left(\frac{1}{(k-i)d}\frac{k-i}{k-i-2}\right)^{d}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{(k-a)d}\frac{k-a}{k-a-2}\right)^{b}

\displaystyle\leq d^{-z}\left(\frac{\eta^{-2}}{\eta^{-2}-2}\right)^{z}\prod_{i=0}^{a-1}\left(\frac{1}{k-i}\right)^{d}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{k-a}\right)^{b}

(41)

\displaystyle\leq d^{-z}\cdot\left(1+3\eta^{2}\right)^{z}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}\leq\bigg(\frac{1+\eta}{d}\bigg)^{z}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}.

Moreover, if
z>z^{*}
, by using (
7.1.2
) and the fact that
z-z^{*}=\eta^{-2}d\leq 2\eta^{2}z^{*}
(using that
k\geq\eta^{-4}
), we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{z-1}\frac{1}{kd-2\ell-i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{z^{*}-1}\frac{1}{kd-2\ell-i}\prod_{i=z^{*}}^{z-1}\frac{1}{kd-2\ell-i}

\displaystyle\leq d^{-z^{*}}\cdot\left(1+3\eta^{2}\right)^{z^{*}}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z^{*})}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}\cdot d^{-z+z^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq d^{-z}\cdot\left(1+3\eta^{2}\right)^{z^{*}}\cdot k^{z-z^{*}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}

\displaystyle\leq((1+3\eta^{2})\exp(\eta^{2}\log k))^{z^{*}}\frac{1}{d^{z}}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}\leq\left(\frac{1+\eta}{d}\right)^{z}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}.

At the last inequality we used that
k\leq\eta^{-5}
.
Thus, in both cases, we have that,

\displaystyle\binom{kd}{2\ell}2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!

\displaystyle k^{12\ell}\frac{(kd-2\ell-z)!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}\leq(kd)!\cdot\left(\frac{2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\right)\bigg(\prod_{i=0}^{z-1}\frac{1}{kd-2\ell-i}\bigg)\frac{1}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}

\displaystyle\leq(kd)!\max_{\ell\in[0,kd]}\left(\frac{2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1+\eta}{d}\right)^{z}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{mult}(kd)}\leq d\cdot\left(\frac{1+\eta}{d}\right)^{z}\cdot\frac{(kd)!}{(k!)^{d}}.

Case 3:
\ell<100k^{2}d/\log d
and
kd-2\ell-z<2d
.
Then, by using Stirling’s formula and the fact that
k
is a large constant, we have

\displaystyle\binom{kd}{2\ell}2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}

\displaystyle\frac{(kd-2\ell-z)!}{\mathrm{mult}(kd-z)}\leq(kd)!\left(\frac{2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\right)\frac{1}{(kd-2\ell)!}(kd-2\ell-z)!

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\ell\in[0,kd]}\left(\frac{2^{\ell+1}(\ell+1)!k^{12\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\right)\bigg(\frac{(k!)^{d}(kd-2\ell-z)!}{(kd-2\ell)!}\bigg)\frac{(kd)!}{(k!)^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq d\bigg(\frac{(k+\sqrt{k})^{z}(kd-2\ell-z)^{kd-2\ell-z}}{(kd-2\ell)^{kd-2\ell}}\bigg)\frac{(kd)!}{(k!)^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq d\bigg(\frac{k+\sqrt{k}}{kd-2\ell}\bigg)^{z}\frac{(kd)!}{(k!)^{d}}\leq d\left(\frac{1+\eta}{d}\right)^{z}\frac{(kd)!}{(k!)^{d}},

where the third inequality used that
k!\leq((k+\sqrt{k})/\mathrm{e})^{k-3}
for large
k
and that
(k-3)d\leq z
.
The bounds established in the three cases complete the proof.
∎

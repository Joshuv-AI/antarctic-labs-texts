Free modules of splines on edge-labeled graphs over k ⁡ [ x , y ] k[x,y]
Kyle Stoltz

Abstract
We first prove that the ring of graph splines over an edge-labeled graph is isomorphic to the limit of a diagram associated to the edge-labeled graph. This is used to establish a local–global principle for graph splines. We then use this to prove that the module of graph splines on an edge-labeled graph over k ⁡ [ x , y ] k[x,y] is free when the graph is locally trivial or determined by a cycle over suitable open sets. Finally, we describe how deletion and contraction on an edge-labeled graph affect the spectrum of the ring of splines.



Keywords:
graph splines; edge-labeled graphs; Ferrand pushouts; flat base change.

Theorem 2

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{w}}

Generalized Splines and Pushouts

The GKM-condition which states adjacent vertex labels agree modulo edge ideals translates to the language of graph morphisms of schemes as follows. Let
R_{u}\rightarrow R/I_{uv}\leftarrow R_{v}
be an edge diagram. A pair
(f,g)\in R_{u}\times_{R/I_{uv}}R_{v}
satisfies

, which just says the scheme-theoretic evaluations of

and

over the points of the closed subscheme

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}(R/I_{uv})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}(R[y])}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

This says the graph morphisms induced by

and

interpolate over the points of
\Spec(R/I_{uv})
. This now justifies the fact the GKM-condition was precisely formulated to generalize the notion of a spline, which creates a piecewise function from functions that agree over given points. Thus, the GKM-condition when translated to the language of graph morphisms precisely recovers the original notion of piecing together graphs that agree over various knots. We need to be cautious however, since sections of schemes are not determined by their values. That is, because the structure sheaf may encode infinitesimal data, two sections could agree over all points but still not be equal.

3
A base change theorem and graph restriction

We will now establish a base change theorem and define the notion of graph restriction. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

\Spec(R_{G})
is obtained by finitely many Ferrand pushouts or coequalizers along closed immersions.

Proof.

We already showed that the colimit
\Spec(R_{G})
is obtained by finitely many pushouts or coequalizers of closed immersions, since these are the dual statements of
Theorem 2
. It remains to show the pushouts are Ferrand. In particular, for any pushout along
\Spec(R_{XY})
we need to show
i:\Spec(R_{XY})\rightarrow\Spec(R_{X})
is a closed immersion, that
j:\Spec(R_{XY})\rightarrow\Spec(R_{Y})
is finite integral, and that for
y\in\Spec(R_{Y})
,
j^{-1}(\{y\})\subseteq i^{-1}(U)
for some open
U\subseteq\Spec(R_{X})

[
2
]
.

We know that both morphisms for the pushout are closed immersions, which are known to be finite and integral per Stacks Project Lemma 29.44.12
[
3
]
. Therefore it remains to verify the pullback property. Since
\Spec(R_{XY})
sits inside both
\Spec(R_{X})
and
\Spec(R_{Y})
we can select any open
U
in
\Spec(R_{X})
containing
i(j^{-1}(y))
and we obtain that
j^{-1}({y})\subseteq i^{-1}(U)
as desired.
∎

We now obtain the following corollary to the lemma and Theorem
2
:

Corollary 1
.

A spline diagram in the category of schemes
\mathfrak{G}
given by the edge-labeled graph
G
and its colimit
\Spec(R_{G})
are stable under flat base change. Moreover, after flat base change of
\Spec(R_{G})
to
\Spec(R_{G})^{\prime}
, there is an edge-labeled graph
G^{\prime}
such that
\Spec(R_{G^{\prime}})
is isomorphic to
\Spec(R_{G})^{\prime}
. If the flat base change is to
U
, we write
G|_{U}
to denote
G^{\prime}
and call it the restriction of
G
to
U
.

Proof.

Ferrand pushout is known to be stable under flat base change. Since
\Spec(R_{G})
is both the colimit of the spline diagram
\mathfrak{G}
and is also obtained by successive Ferrand pushouts or equalizers of closed immersions (by the lemma), we can see the flat base change of
\Spec(R_{G})
is the colimit of the flat base change of
\mathfrak{G}
. This is because each of the Ferrand pushouts and coequalizers used to obtain
\Spec(R_{G})
are stable under flat base change, so the stability propagates along each of the finite steps to
\Spec(R_{G})
itself.

After
\mathfrak{G}
with colimit
\Spec(R_{G})
undergoes flat base change to obtain
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
with colimit
\Spec(R_{G})^{\prime}
, we need to find a suitable graph
G^{\prime}
so that
\Spec(R_{G})^{\prime}\cong\Spec(R_{G^{\prime}})
. We know such a graph exists since flat base change preserves injectivity of the diagrams of schemes, and the injections on the scheme side translate to surjections on the ring side. Therefore if we convert the spline diagram of schemes after flat base change to a spline diagram of rings, we know the morphisms
R_{u}\rightarrow R_{uv}\leftarrow R_{v}
are surjective. Here we’ve written
R_{uv}
instead of
R/I_{uv}
because
R_{uv}
was obtained by flat base change, not quotient. Now we can build an edge-labeled graph where the edge for
R_{u}\rightarrow R_{uv}\leftarrow R_{v}
is labeled by the kernel of either edge morphism. The other edges are obtained similarly.
∎

Application of the base change theorem

In
multivariate generalized splines and syzygies on graphs
Samet Sarıoğlan and Selma Altınok established that if
R=k[x,y]
is a polynomial ring over a field with edge-labeled graph
G=(V,E)
a cycle, then
R_{G}
is free
[
4
]
. Recall the structure morphism for
\Spec(R_{G})
as an
R
-scheme is given by the diagonal embedding of
R
into
R_{G}
. We can now obtain an immediate generalization as follows:

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
R=k[x,y]
is a ring of polynomials in two variables over a field
k
. Let
G
be an edge-labeled graph. If there exist Zariski open sets
\{U_{f}\}
of
\Spec(R)
such that each
G|_{U_{f}}
is trivial or determined by a cycle, and such that the preimages of the
U_{f}
cover
\Spec(R_{G})
, then
R_{G}
is free.

Proof.

Suppose the hypothesis is satisfied for
R
,
R_{G}
, and
\{U_{f}\}
. Also suppose
G|_{U_{f}}
is determined by a cycle
G^{\prime}|_{U_{f}}
, with
G^{\prime}
a cycle labeled by ideals of
R
such that
G^{\prime}
restricts to
G^{\prime}|_{U_{f}}
. Now
R_{G^{\prime}}
is free as an
R
-module since
G^{\prime}
is a cycle, which meets the criteria of Sarıoğlan and Altınok. Since freeness is preserved under flat base change,
R_{G^{\prime}|_{U_{f}}}
is free. But
G|_{U_{f}}
is determined by
G^{\prime}|_{U_{f}}
, hence
R_{G|_{U_{f}}}
is free. On the other hand, if
G|_{U_{f}}
is trivial instead of determined by a cycle, we also obtain that
R_{G|_{U_{f}}}
is free.

In either case, we obtain that
R_{G|_{U_{f}}}
is free for every open set in
\{U_{f}\}
. But
R_{G|_{U_{f}}}
was obtained as the global sections of the base change of
\Spec(R_{G})
to each
U_{f}
. Hence
\Spec(R_{G})
is locally free within the pre-image of each
U_{f}
in
\Spec(R_{G})
, and by hypothesis these preimages cover
\Spec(R_{G})
. Since the
R
-module
R_{G}
is locally free along a Zariski open cover of
\Spec(R_{G})
formed by the preimages of the
U_{f}
, we obtain
R_{G}
is finite and projective. But
k[x,y]
is subject to the Quillen–Suslin theorem, hence finite projective implies free. Therefore
R_{G}
is free as an
R
-module as claimed.
∎

The net effect of the theorem is that if the edge labels of a graph
G
are selected so that restriction along Zariski open subsets trivializes all edge conditions except those along a cycle (or perhaps trivializes all edge conditions), then with a suitable covering condition we can lift local properties to global properties. In particular, we are not merely restricting
R_{G}
along Zariski open subsets, we are successfully restricting
G
itself along with
R_{G}
so that the restricted structure of
G
regulates the restricted structure of
R_{G}
. That is, we used
G|_{U}
to prove facts about the restricted form of
R_{G}
. Without this functorial property we could restrict
R_{G}
to a Zariski open subset, but we would lose access to a graph spline structure.

It is also worth noting the theorem is not stated in perfect generality. More broadly, suppose we have
\Spec(R_{G})
as an
R
-scheme, and open sets of
\Spec(R)
with preimages that cover
\Spec(R_{G})
. Then if each

into a form where the

has that global property. For example, in the theorem we obtained that local freeness lifted to global freeness, but for rings not subject to the Quillen–Suslin theorem we would still have that local freeness would lift to finite projective globally.

5
Deletion and Contraction

In the foundational paper
Generalized Splines on Arbitrary Graphs
by Gilbert, Polster, and Tymoczko
[
5
]
, Question 7.4 asks:

”
How do classical graph-theoretic constructions (like deletion and contraction) affect the algebraic structure of splines
R_{G}
?
”

We can now answer the question of deletion and contraction comprehensively in terms of how it affects
\Spec(R_{G})
. Deletion of an edge was already discussed previously via addition of an edge. It corresponds to passing from the coequalizer of the following diagram back to
\Spec(R_{G})
.

\Spec(R_{G^{\prime}})\leftarrow\Spec(R_{G})\;\leftleftarrows\;\Spec(R/I_{uv}).

This is because to add an edge, we pass from
\Spec(R_{G})
to
\Spec(R_{G^{\prime}})
. Thus, deletion of an edge reverses this process. This is a trap-door function: given
\Spec(R_{G^{\prime}})
(with an added edge), we cannot just delete the copy of
\Spec(R/I_{uv})
that was used to glue. To see why, notice that if we glue three schemes along the same shared point, then deletion of the shared point will disconnect all three schemes. However, if we delete an edge, this means two of the schemes are glued, while the third remains disconnected, so we obtain a scheme with two connected components. Therefore, any algebraic attempt to describe edge deletion from the view of
R_{G^{\prime}}
will run into significant difficulty.

Vertex deletion can be understood as first deleting all edges to the target vertex. Once the target vertex has no edges, it contributes an extra disconnected copy of
\Spec(R)
to the spectrum. The last step is to remove the extra disconnected copy of
\Spec(R)
.

Finally it remains to discuss contraction. In the case of contraction we need to delete an edge and then identify two vertices. Since deletion of an edge was already described, we need only discuss identification of vertices. In this case, we are passing to and from the coequalizer of the diagram:

\Spec(R_{G^{\prime}})\leftarrow\Spec(R_{G})\;\leftleftarrows\;\Spec(R).

Notice the simple description of how
\Spec(R_{G})
is impacted doesn’t immediately describe the algebraic structure of the ring of global sections. The fact the algebraic side is inscrutable is analogous to the tensor product of vector bundles: describing tensor product of vector bundles is simple algebraically, since it corresponds to fiber-wise tensor product. Describing the geometry of the resulting vector bundle, however, is not such an easy task. Similarly, we’ve described how
\Spec(R_{G})
is impacted by deletion and contraction, without directly addressing how the sections are impacted.

6
Conclusion

We conclude with a few directions for future study.

•

What other colimit preserving functors could be leveraged aside from flat base change?

•

How does
\Spec(R_{G})
behave in the context of flat families?

•

Exploring the flat base change theorem and its application. In particular, we haven’t considered how and when the ideals of
R
support construction of a graph
G
which restricts in a desired way. This may explain why PIDs automatically have a free
R
-module of splines, since their ideal-theoretic structure and Zariski topology is particularly easy to control.

•

What is the correct generality for Theorem
3
?

•

Exploring concrete examples of the results presented, especially algorithms that can translate edge-labeled graphs into pictures of
\Spec(R_{G})
and determine its properties.

•

Now that we have access to scheme theory, what other tools are available?

•

We have not explored
r
-smoothness. However, it appears to be about gluing along infinitesimal neighborhoods of the reduced, induced closed subscheme of each
\Spec(R/I^{r})
.

•

How can geometric properties of
\Spec(R_{G})
as it relates to
\Spec(R)
be utilized? In the examples of edge-labeled graphs with a cycle,
\Spec(R_{G})
ended up with a hole. Could we force a hole in
\Spec(R_{G})
without using a graph with a cycle, and if so, would
R_{G}
enjoy the same properties as a ring of splines over an edge-labeled graph that is a cycle?

The Igusa local zeta function of certain Thom-Sebastiani type functions
Quy Thuong Lê, Hoang Long Nguyen

Abstract
. In this paper, we give an explicit formula of the Igusa local zeta function of a Thom-Sebastiani type sum of two separated-variable Newton non-critical polynomials. Data for the description are available on their Newton polyhedra.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14B05, 11S80, 11S40; Secondary 52B05

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime, and let
K
be a
p
-adic field whose ring of integral numbers is
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Fix a uniformizing parameter
\pi
of
\mathcal{O}
. Assume
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathcal{O}/(\pi)
. Let
v=\mathrm{ord}_{\pi}
be the valuation map with
v(0)=\infty
, and define
|\xi|=q^{-v(\xi)}
for any
\xi\in K^{*}
. Since
K
is a locally compact totally disconnected group, there is a unique canonical Haar measure
|dx|
of
(K^{n},+)
normalized by
|dx|(\mathcal{O}^{n})=1
.

Let
f(x)
be a polynomial over
\mathcal{O}
in
n
variables
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Consider the Igusa local zeta function of
f
defined as follows

Z(f;s):=\int_{\mathcal{O}^{n}}|f(x)|^{s}|dx|.

It is a fact that
Z(f;s)
is a rational function in
q^{-s}
. The Igusa local zeta function is an important object in several branch of mathematics such as number theory and singularity theory. There are conjectures such as the the holomorphy conjecture and the monodromy conjecture relate the poles of
Z(f;s)
to the monodromy of singular point of the hypersurface defined by
f
.

Our motivation in this topic is to prove that if the
p
-adic monodromy conjecture is true for
f(x)
and
g(y)
in separated variables
x
and
y
, then the conjecture is also true for the function

(f\oplus g)(x,y)=f(x)+g(y).

In
[
1
, Section 5.1]
, this problem is mentioned and suggested to solve using resolution of singularity and exponential sums. However, there is the lack of an explicit description of
Z(f\oplus g;s)
in terms of
Z(f;s)
,
Z(g;s)
and data in the common vanishing of
f
and
g
. This work not only gives a geometric solution to this problem without mentioning the exponential sum but also helps to answer the similar question on the holomorphy conjecture.

In this paper, under the Newton non-criticality condition for polynomials
f
and
g
, we can describe
Z(f\oplus g;s)
completely explicitly in terms of
1
-degree
1
-variable polynomials
c_{a,b}(s)
constructed from the Newton polyhedra of
f
and
g
. Namely, we prove the following theorem.

Main Theorem

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x],g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[y]
be two Newton non-critical polynomials. Then the Igusa local zeta function of
f\oplus g
is described as follows

Z(f\oplus g;s)=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{a,b}(1-q^{c_{a,b}(s)})},

where the product runs over all
(a,b)
with
a
(resp.
b
) primitive normal vectors of the facets of the Newton polyhedron of
f
(resp.
g
), and
P
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial.

The notation and techniques we use in this paper partly come from Zuniga-Galindo’s works on the Igusa stationary phase formula (see
[
7
]
).

2.
Igusa’s stationary phase formula

2.1.
Zuniga-Galindo’s setting

Let us recall the notation and results about Igusa’s stationary phase formula mentioned in
[
7
]
.
Let
f(x)
be a polynomial in
\mathcal{O}[x]
, and let
(P_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of points in
\mathcal{O}^{n}
. Define a sequence
(f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}})_{k\geq 1}
of polynomials in
\mathcal{O}[x]
as follows

\displaystyle f_{P_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-e_{P_{1}}(f)}f(P_{1}+\pi x),

\displaystyle f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k+1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-e_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k+1}}(f)}f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(P_{k+1}+\pi x),\ k\geq 1,

where
e_{P_{1}}(f)
(resp.
e_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k+1}}(f)
) denotes the smallest valuation of the coefficients in the
x
-polynomial
f(P_{1}+\pi x)
(resp.
f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(P_{k+1}+\pi x)
). Fix a lifting
R
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
\mathcal{O}
, that is,
R
is mapped bijectively onto
\mathbb{F}_{q}
along the canonical projection. For any subset
\overline{D}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, let
D
denote the preimage of
\overline{D}
under the canonical projection
\mathcal{O}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Put

S(f,D):=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in R^{n}\mid(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n})\text{ is a singular point of }\overline{f}\text{ in }\overline{D}\},

where
\overline{f}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is the reduction modulo
\pi
of
f(x)
. For simplicity, we also define

\displaystyle\nu(\bar{f},D)

\displaystyle:=q^{-n}\#\{\overline{P}\in\overline{D}\mid\overline{f}(\overline{P})\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\sigma(\overline{f},D)

\displaystyle:=q^{-n}\#\{\overline{P}\in\overline{D}\mid\overline{f}(\overline{P})=0,\overline{P}\text{ is a smooth point of }\overline{f}\}.

Then, Igusa’s Stationary Phase Formula can be written as follows

\displaystyle\int_{D}|f(x)|^{s}|dx|=\nu(\bar{f},D)+\sigma(\bar{f},D)\dfrac{(1-q^{-1})q^{-s}}{1-q^{-1-s}}+\sum_{P\in S(f,D)}q^{-n-e_{p}(f)s}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{n}}|f_{P}(x)|^{s}|dx|.

One also considers the sequence of sets
I_{k}=I_{k}(f,D)
,
k\geq 1
, defined by

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=S(f,D),

\displaystyle I_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left\{(P_{1},\dots,P_{k+1})\mid(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})\in I_{k},P_{k+1}\in S(f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}},\mathcal{O}^{n})\right\},\ k\geq 1,

which arises naturally when applying consecutively the stationary phase formula.

Notation 2.1
.

(Zuniga-Galindo
[
6
]
)
Let
f\in\mathcal{O}[x]
, and let
P=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{O}^{n}
. If
P
is not a singular point of
f
, put

L(f,P):=\min\left\{v\left(f(P)\right),v\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}(P)\right),\dots,v\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}(P)\right)\right\}.

If
P
is not a critical point of
f
, i.e. there exists an
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that,
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(P)\not=0
, then one puts

\ell(f,P):=\min\left\{v\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}(P)\right),\dots,v\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}(P)\right)\right\}.

Proposition 2.2

(Zuniga-Galindo
[
7
]
)
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{n}
be a compact set, and let
f\in\mathcal{O}[x]
be a polynomial such that
f
does not have any singular points in
D
. Then

C(f,D):=\sup_{P\in D}L(f,P)<+\infty.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{n}
be a compact set, and
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x]
be a polynomial such that
f
does not have any critical points in
D
. Then

c(f,D):=\sup_{P\in D}\ell(f,P)<+\infty.

Lemma 2.4

(Zuniga-Galindo,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\overline{D}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}[x]
. Assume that
f
has no singular points in
D
. Then the following hold.

(i)

If
(P_{1},\dots,P_{k+1})\in I_{k+1}(f,D)
, then

L(f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}},0)\leq L(f,P_{1}+\pi P_{2}+\dots+\pi^{k-1}P_{k})-k.

(ii)

If
k>C(f,D)+1
, then
I_{k}=\varnothing
.

2.2.
A consecutive stationary phase formula

Let
f
be in
\mathcal{O}[x]
that has no singular points in
D
. It implies from Lemma
2.4
that

m(f,D):=\max\{k\geq 1\mid I_{k}\neq\varnothing\}<+\infty.

For simplicity, we write
m
for
m(f,D)
, thus for
(P_{1},\dots,P_{m})\in I_{m}
, we have
S(f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}},\mathcal{O}^{n})=\varnothing
. Applying the stationary phase formula
m
times, we obtain

(2.1)

\begin{split}\int_{D}|f(x)|^{s}|dx|&=\nu(\bar{f},D)+\sigma(\bar{f},D)\dfrac{(1-q^{-1})q^{-s}}{1-q^{-1-s}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{m}q^{-kn}\left(\sum_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})\in I_{k}}\nu(\bar{f}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}},\mathcal{O}^{n})q^{-E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)s}\right)\\
&\quad+\dfrac{(1-q^{-1})q^{-s}}{1-q^{-1-s}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}q^{-kn}\left(\sum_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})\in I_{k}}\sigma(\bar{f}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}},\mathcal{O}^{n})q^{-E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)s}\right),\end{split}

where

E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{P_{1},\dots,P_{i}}(f).

This formula gives us the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5

(Zuniga-Galindo
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\overline{D}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}[x]
. Assume that
f
has no singular points in
D
. Then

\int_{D}|f(x)|^{s}|dx|=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{1-q^{-1-s}},

where
P
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-polynomial in one variable.

By some slight modifications, we obtain a similar version of
[
7
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\overline{D}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}[x]
. Assume that
f
has no singular points in
D
. Let
F(x)=f(x)+\pi^{\beta}g(x)
, where
g\in\mathcal{O}[x]
, and
\beta>C(f,D)+m(f,D)
. Then

\int_{D}|f(x)|^{s}|dx|=\int_{D}|F(x)|^{s}|dx|.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the following statements:

(i)

I_{k}(f,D)=I_{k}(F,D)
;

(ii)

E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)=E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(F)
for all
(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})\in I_{k}(f,D)=I_{k}(F,D)
;

(iii)

\overline{f}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)=\overline{F}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)
for all
(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})\in I_{k}(f,D)=I_{k}(F,D)
.

The item (i) can be proved easily by induction on
k\geq 1
. Now, taking any point
(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})
in
I_{k}(f,D)=I_{k}(F,D)
we have

\displaystyle\pi^{E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(F)}F_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)

\displaystyle=F(P_{1}+\pi P_{2}+\dots+\pi^{k-1}P_{k}+\pi^{k}x)

\displaystyle=f(P_{1}+\pi P_{2}+\dots+\pi^{k-1}P_{k}+\pi^{k}x)+\pi^{\beta}g_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\pi^{E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)}f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)+\pi^{\beta}g_{k}(x),

where

g_{k}(x)=g(P_{1}+\pi P_{2}+\dots+\pi^{k-1}P_{k}+\pi^{k}x).

Since
\pi
does not divide the least common divisor of all the coefficients of
f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)
, the number
E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)
is the minimum
\pi
-order of all the coefficients of
f(P_{1}+\pi P_{2}+\dots+\pi^{k-1}P_{k}+\pi^{k}x)
. Taking the Taylor expansion of the latter at
Q=P_{1}+\pi P_{2}+\dots+\pi^{k-1}P_{k}\in D
gives

\displaystyle f(P_{1}+\pi P_{2}+\dots+\pi^{k-1}P_{k}+\pi^{k}x)=f(Q)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(Q)\pi^{k}x_{i}+(\text{higher terms}).

It implies that

\displaystyle E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{v\left(f(Q)\right),v\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}(Q)\pi^{k}\right),\dots,v\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}(Q)\pi^{k}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq L(f,Q)+k

\displaystyle\leq C(f,D)+m(f,D)

\displaystyle<\beta.

If
E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(F)<E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)<\beta
, then

F_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)=\pi^{E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)-E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(F)}f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)+\pi^{\beta-E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(F)}g_{k}(x),

which is a contradiction as the greatest common divisor of the coefficients of
F_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}
is also not a multiple of
\pi
. Thus
E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(F)\geq E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)
. The same argument give the reverse inequality, which proves that

E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(F)=E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f).

It implies that

F_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)=f_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)+\pi^{\beta-E_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(f)}g_{k}(x),

hence

\overline{f}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x)=\overline{F}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}}(x).

Since the previous equality holds for all
(P_{1},\dots,P_{m})\in I_{m}(f,D)
, where
m=m(f,D)
, we get

S(\overline{F}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}},\mathcal{O}^{n})=S(\overline{f}_{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}},\mathcal{O}^{n})=\varnothing.

Therefore,
m(F,D)=m(f,D)
, the lemma then follows from the expansion (
2.1
).
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\overline{D}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}[x]
. Assume that
f
has no critical points in
D
. Let
F(x)=f(x)+\pi^{\beta}g(x)
, where
g(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x]
and
\beta>2c(f,D)+1
. Then

\int_{D}|f(x)|^{s}|dx|=\int_{D}|F(x)|^{s}|dx|.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
we get
m(f,D)\leq C(f,D)+1
. The corollary follows from Lemma
2.6
and the fact that
C(f,D)\leq c(f,D)
.
∎

3.
Newton polyhedron

3.1.
Newton non-critical polynomials

In this section, we briefly recall some well-known results about the Newton polyhedron of a polynomial (see e.g.
[
2
]
). Let
f(x)=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\omega}x^{\omega}
be a non zero polynomial over
\mathcal{O}
with
f(0)=0
, where
x^{\omega}
stands for
x_{1}^{\omega_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\omega_{n}}
with
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
. We denote by
\mathbb{R}^{+}
the set of non-negative real numbers. The
Newton polyhedron

\Gamma_{f}
of
f
is the convex hull in
(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{n}
of the set

\bigcup_{\omega\in\mathrm{supp}(f)}\omega+(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{n},

where
\mathrm{supp}(f)=\{\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:a_{\omega}\neq 0\}
. Each proper face of
\Gamma_{f}
is the intersection of
\Gamma_{f}
with a supporting hyperplane, and a face of
\Gamma_{f}
is a proper face of
\Gamma_{f}
or the Newton polyhedron itself. For
a\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{n}
, we define

m_{f}(a)=\inf_{x\in\Gamma_{f}}\{a\cdot x\},

and the first meet locus of
a
is

F_{f}(a)=\{x\in\Gamma_{f}\mid a\cdot x=m_{f}(a)\}.

Definition 3.1
.

For each face
\tau
of
\Gamma_{f}
, the cone associated to
\tau
is

\Delta_{f,\tau}=\{a\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{n}\mid F_{f}(a)=\tau\}.

From now on, if there is no possibility of confusion, we drop the subscript
f
and simply write
m(a),F(a),
and
\Delta_{\tau}
.

A vector
a\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is called
primitive
if the greatest common divisor of its components is
1
. A
facet
of
\Gamma_{f}
is a proper face of codimension one. We can prove that for every facet of
\Gamma
, there exists a unique primitive vector in
\mathbb{N}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
perpendicular to that facet. Every proper face
\tau
of
\Gamma_{f}
is a finite intersection of the facets of
\Gamma_{f}
containing
\tau
.

Lemma 3.2

(Denef-Hoornaert
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\tau
be a proper face of
\Gamma_{f}
, and let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{e}
be the facets of
\Gamma_{f}
which contain
\tau
. Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{e}
be the unique primitive vectors in
\mathbb{N}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
which are perpendicular to respectively
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{e}
. The the cone
\Delta_{\tau}
associated to
\tau
is the following convex cone

\Delta_{\tau}=\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{e}a_{e}\mid\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\lambda_{i}>0\}

and its dimension is equal to
n-\dim\tau
. Moreover, we also have

\overline{\Delta}_{\tau}=\{a\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{n}\mid\tau\subseteq F(a)\}=\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{e}a_{e}\mid\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\lambda_{i}\geq 0\}.

Definition 3.3
.

We call
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a
strictly positively spanned cone
by vectors
a_{1},\dots,a_{e}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
if

\Delta=\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{e}a_{e}\mid\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\lambda_{i}>0\}.

If
a_{1},\dots,a_{e}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
,
\Delta
is called a
simplicial cone
. If moreover,
a_{1},\dots,a_{e}
are in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we say
\Delta
is a
rational
simplicial cone. Furthermore, if
a_{1},\dots,a_{e}
can be completed to be a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we call
\Delta
a simple cone.

Lemma 3.4

(Denef-Hoornaert
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the strictly positively spanned cone by vectors
a_{1},\dots,a_{e}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then there exists a finite partition of
\Delta
into cones
\Delta_{i}
, such that each
\Delta_{i}
is strictly positively spanned by some vectors from the set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{e}\}
which are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
f(x)
be a polynomial in
\mathcal{O}[x]
. Then
f
is called
Newton non-critical
if for every face
\tau
of
\Gamma_{f}
, the following system

\dfrac{\partial f_{\tau}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)=0,\hskip 10.0pt\text{for }1\leq i\leq n,

has no solution in
(K^{\times})^{n}
.

3.2.
A technical lemma

Given
c,d
be positive integers, we introduce the following map

\phi_{c,d}:\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{2}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{2}

defined by

\phi_{c,d}(s,t)=\begin{cases}(c,d)&\text{if}\ s=t\\
(s-t,d)&\text{if}\ s>t\\
(c,t-s)&\text{if}\ s<t.\end{cases}

We put

\displaystyle(c_{1},d_{1})

\displaystyle=(c,d),

\displaystyle(c_{k+1},d_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\phi_{c,d}(c_{k},d_{k}),\ k\geq 1.

Hosokawa in
[
3
]
proved that the sequences
(c_{k},d_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is periodic. In order to keep this paper self-contained, let us sketch his proof as follows. We may assume
c\leq d
. Write
\phi
for
\phi_{c,d}
, and put
c=ke,d=ke^{\prime}
, where
k=\mathrm{gcd}(c,d)
. Write
e^{\prime}=qe+r
, where
0\leq r\leq e-1
. If
r=0
, then
e=1
as
\mathrm{gcd}(e,e^{\prime})=1
. In this case,
(c_{k},d_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is clearly periodic with period
q
. In the case where
r>0
, by dividing the closed interval
[0,er]
into
r
sub-intervals
I_{i}:=[e(i-1),ei]
,
1\leq i\leq r
, we put

\Lambda_{0}=\{\lambda\in[0,e-1]\cap\mathbb{Z}\mid\lambda r\text{ and }(\lambda+1)r\text{ are not in the same }I_{i}\}.

As
\mathrm{gcd}(e,r)=\mathrm{gcd}(e,e^{\prime})=1
, each
\lambda r
must lies in the interior of some
I_{i}
, so
|\Lambda_{0}|=r-1
. For each integer
n
, we denote by
\overline{n}
the remainder of
n
modulo
e
. Note that
(c_{1},d_{1})=(ke,ke^{\prime})=(ke,k(qe+r))
, and it takes us
q
steps to go from
(ke,k(qe+r))
to
(ke,kr)=(ke,k\bar{r})
along
\phi
as follows

(ke,k(qe+r))\longmapsto(ke,k(q-1)e+kr)\longmapsto\dots\longmapsto(ke,kr)=(ke,k\overline{r}).

For
1\leq\lambda\leq e-2
, we consider the following cases.

•

If
\lambda\notin\Lambda_{0}
, then
\overline{(\lambda+1)r}=r+\overline{\lambda r}
, thus it takes us
(q+1)
steps to go from
(ke,k\overline{\lambda r})
to
(ke,ke\overline{(\lambda+1)r}
along
\phi
as follows

\displaystyle(ke,k\overline{\lambda r})\longmapsto(k(e-\overline{\lambda r}),ke^{\prime})\longmapsto

\displaystyle(ke,k(q-1)e+k(r+\overline{\lambda r}))\longmapsto

\displaystyle\cdots\longmapsto(ke,k(r+\overline{\lambda r}))=(ke,k\overline{(\lambda+1)r}).

•

If
\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}
, then
\overline{(\lambda+1)r}=r+\overline{\lambda r}-e
, thus we need an extra step to go from
(ke,k\overline{\lambda r})
to
(ke,ke\overline{(\lambda+1)r}
along
\phi
as follows

\displaystyle(ke,k\overline{\lambda r})\longmapsto

\displaystyle(k(e-\overline{\lambda r}),ke^{\prime})\longmapsto\cdots

\displaystyle\longmapsto(ke,k(r+\overline{\lambda r}))\longmapsto(ke,k(r+\overline{\lambda r}-e))=(ke,k\overline{(\lambda+1)r}).

From
(ke,k\overline{(e-1)r})=(ke,k(e-r))
to
(ke,ke^{\prime})
, it takes us
q+2
steps along
\phi
, namely,

(ke,k(e-r))\longmapsto(kr,ke^{\prime})\longmapsto(ke,kqe)\longmapsto\cdots\longmapsto(ke,ke)\longmapsto(ke,ke^{\prime}).

In summary, going once from
(ke,ke^{\prime})
to itself needs the number of steps to be

q+(q+1)(e-2-|\Lambda_{0}|)+(q+2)|\Lambda_{0}|+q+2=e+e^{\prime}-1=:p.

This number also covers the case
r=0
. So, for any
0\leq r\leq e-1
, we have
(c_{1},d_{1})=(c_{1+p},d_{1+p})
.

Furthermore, the previous arguments have proved the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
c,d
be positive integers and put
e=c/\mathrm{gcd}(c,d),e^{\prime}=d/\mathrm{gcd}(c,d)
. Then
(c_{k},d_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is periodic with period
p:=e+e^{\prime}-1
. Furthermore, if
(\mu_{k},\nu_{k})_{k\geq 2}
is defined as

(\mu_{k},\nu_{k})=\begin{cases}(\min\{c_{k},d_{k}\},\tilde{c}+\tilde{d})&\text{if}\ c_{k}=d_{k},\\
(\min\{c_{k},d_{k}\},\tilde{d})&\text{if}\ c_{k}>d_{k},\\
(\min\{c_{k},d_{k}\},\tilde{c})&\text{if}\ c_{k}<d_{k},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{c},\tilde{d}
be arbitrary integers, then

\sum_{k=2}^{p+1}\mu_{k}=\mathrm{lcm}(c,d)\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=2}^{p+1}\nu_{k}=e\tilde{d}+e^{\prime}\tilde{c}.

4.
The Thom-Sebastiani type sum of non-critical functions

4.1.
Computations on face functions

For any
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{O}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
we put

E_{A}:=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{O}^{n}\mid(v(x_{1}),\dots,v(x_{n}))\in A\},

and

Z_{A}(f;s)=\int_{E_{A}}|f(x)|^{s}|dx|.

When
A=(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{n}
,
E_{A}
is nothing but
\mathcal{O}^{n}
. In this case, we write simply
Z(f;s)
for
Z_{\mathcal{O}^{n}}(f;s)
.

Consider polynomials
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x]
and
g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[y]
, with
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
. We also consider the following polynomial

f\oplus g:=f(x)+g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[x,y].

For any
a\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the setting in the previous section provides us the integers
m_{f}(a)\geq 0
and
m_{g}(b)\geq 0
.

Definition 4.1
.

We define
1
-degree polynomials
c_{a,b}(s)
in
s
as follows

(i)

If
m_{f}(a)>0
and
m_{g}(b)>0
, set

c_{a,b}(s):=-ls-e|b|-e^{\prime}|a|,

where

l=\mathrm{lcm}(m_{f}(a),m_{g}(b)),\ \ e=\frac{m_{f}(a)}{\mathrm{gcd}(m_{f}(a),m_{g}(b))},\ \ \text{and}\ \ e^{\prime}=\frac{m_{g}(b)}{\mathrm{gcd}(m_{f}(a),m_{g}(b))}.

(ii)

If
m_{f}(a)=0
or
m_{g}(b)=0
, set

c_{a,b}(s):=-\infty.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x]
and
g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[y]
be Newton non-critical polynomials, let
\tau
and
\gamma
be any faces of
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{g}
, respectively. Suppose that
\{a_{1},\dots a_{u}\}
and
\{b_{1},\dots b_{v}\}
are
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent sets that satisfy the following inclusions

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{u}\mathbb{N}a_{i}\subseteq\overline{\Delta}_{\tau},\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{2}:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{v}\mathbb{N}b_{j}\subseteq\overline{\Delta}_{\gamma}.

Then

Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i=1}^{u}\prod_{j=1}^{v}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})},

where
P
is a rational polynomial in one variable.

Proof.

We prove the proposition by induction on
u+v
.

The base case

u=v=1
. First, considering the case where
c:=m_{f}(a_{1})>0,

d:=m_{g}(b_{1})>0
, we define four sequences
(c_{k})_{k\geq 1},(d_{k})_{l\geq 1},(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 2},(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 2}
as in Section
3.2
, with
\tilde{c}=|a_{1}|
and
\tilde{d}=|b_{1}|
. Define

Z_{c,d}(s):=Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(\pi^{c}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d}g_{\gamma};s).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u=v=1
. For all
k\geq 1
,

Z_{c_{k},d_{k}}(s)=\dfrac{Q_{k}(q^{-s})}{1-q^{-1-s}}+q^{-\mu_{k+1}s-\nu_{k+1}}Z_{c_{k+1},d_{k+1}}(s),

where, for each
k\geq 1
,
Q_{k}
is a rational polynomial.

Proof.

We verify the lemma for
c_{k}=d_{k}
, the rest cases are proved in the same method. We have

\displaystyle Z_{c_{k},d_{k}}(s)=

\displaystyle\int_{\{v(x)=0\}\times\{v(y)=0\}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle+\int_{\{v(x)=0\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1},l\geq 1\}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle+\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1},k\geq 1\}\times\{v(y)=0\}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle+\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1},k\geq 1\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1},l\geq 1\}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|.

Lemma
2.5
implies that the first integral is of the form
P_{1}(q^{-s})/(1-q^{-1-s})
for some
1
-variable rational polynomial
P_{1}
. Regarding the second integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{v(x)=0\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1},l\geq 1\}}

\displaystyle|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}q^{-l|b_{1}|}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma}(\pi^{lb_{1}}t)|^{s}|dxdt|

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}q^{-l|b_{1}|}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}+lm_{g}(b_{1})}g_{\gamma}(t)|^{s}|dxdt|,

in which the last equality occurs as
\gamma\subseteq F(b_{1})
. If
lm_{g}(b_{1})>2c(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau},(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n})+1
, then it implies from Corollary
2.7
that

\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}+lm_{g}(b_{1})}g_{\gamma}(t)|^{s}|dxdt|=\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)|^{s}|dxdt|.

From the latter, we can easily show that the second integral has the form
P_{2}(q^{-s})/(1-q^{-1-s})
for some
1
-variable rational polynomial
P_{2}
. The same argument also holds for the third integral. Finally, by the change of variables
x=\pi^{a_{1}}z,y=\pi^{b_{1}}t
, the last one can be computed as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1},k\geq 1\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1},l\geq 1\}}|\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}(x)+\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle\quad=q^{-\mu_{k+1}s}\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1},k\geq 1\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1},l\geq 1\}}|f_{\tau}(x)+g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle\quad=q^{-\mu_{k+1}s-|a_{1}|-|b_{1}|}\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1},k\geq 0\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1},l\geq 0\}}|\pi^{m_{f}(a_{1})}f_{\tau}(z)+\pi^{m_{g}(b_{1})}g_{\gamma}(t)|^{s}|dzdt|

\displaystyle\quad=q^{-\mu_{k+1}s-\nu_{k+1}}Z_{c_{k+1},d_{k+1}}(s).

The lemma follows.
∎

Back to the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we note that
c_{1}=c_{1+p}
, with
p
defined in Lemma
3.6
. Applying
p
times Lemma
4.3
we get

Z_{a_{1},b_{1}}(s)=\frac{P(q^{-s})}{1-q^{-1-s}}+q^{-(\sum_{k=2}^{p+1}\mu_{k})s-\sum_{k=2}^{p+1}\nu_{k}}Z_{a_{1+p},b_{1+p}}(s),

where
P
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial. From Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

Z_{a_{1},b_{1}}(s)=\frac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})(1-q^{c_{a_{1},b_{1}}(s)})}.

The same computation as in Lemma
4.3
gives us

Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)=\dfrac{H(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})}+q^{-|a_{1}|-|b_{1}|}Z_{a_{1},b_{1}}(s),

where
H
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial. This means that the proposition is proved in the case where
u=v=1
,
m_{f}(a_{1})>0
and
m_{g}(b_{1})>0
.

If
m_{f}(a_{1})=0
and
m_{g}(b_{1})>0
, then

\displaystyle Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1},\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1}\}}|f_{\tau}(x)+g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(q^{-k|a_{1}|}\right)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}q^{-l|b_{1}|}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f_{\tau}(z)+\pi^{lm_{g}(b_{1})}g_{\gamma}(t)|^{s}|dzdt|.

With
lm_{g}(b_{1})>2c(f_{\tau},(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n})+1
, from Corollary
2.7
, we have

\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f_{\tau}(z)+\pi^{lm_{g}(b_{1})}g_{\gamma}(t)|^{s}|dzdt|=\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f_{\tau}(z)|^{s}|dzdt|.

Hence, there exists a rational polynomial
P
such that

Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{1-q^{-1-s}}.

The case
m_{f}(a_{1})>0,m_{g}(b_{1})=0
can be treated similarly as above.

If
m_{f}(a_{1})=m_{g}(b_{1})=0
, the proposition follows from Lemma
2.5
and the following expansion

\displaystyle Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1},\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1}\}}|f_{\tau}(x)+g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}q^{-k|a_{1}|-l|b_{1}|}\right)\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f_{\tau}(x)+g_{\gamma}(y)|^{s}|dxdy|.

Induction hypothesis.
Suppose that the theorem is true for all Newton non-critical polynomials
f
and
g
, for all faces
\tau
and
\gamma
, for all cones
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
with the total number of generators does not exceed
u+v-1
.

Let us consider the first case where
m_{f}(a_{1}),m_{g}(b_{1})>0
. We define four sequences
(c_{k})_{k\geq 1}
,
(d_{k})_{k\geq 1}
,
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 2},
and
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 2}
as in the base case. Put

Z_{c_{k},d_{k}}(s)=Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s).

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
k\geq 1
, we have

Z_{c_{k},d_{k}}(s)=\dfrac{Q_{k}(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})}+q^{-\mu_{k+1}s-\nu_{k+1}}Z_{c_{k+1},d_{k+1}}(s),

where
Q_{k}
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial.

Proof.

We are going to prove the lemma in the case
c_{k}=d_{k}
, the other cases are proved similarly. Define

\displaystyle S_{1}=\left\{\sum_{i=2}^{u}k_{i}a_{i}\mid k_{2},\dots,k_{u}\in\mathbb{N}\right\},\hskip 5.0ptS_{2}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i}\mid k_{1}\geq 1,k_{2},\dots,k_{u}\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

\displaystyle T_{1}=\left\{\sum_{j=2}^{v}l_{j}b_{j}\mid l_{2},\dots,l_{v}\in\mathbb{N}\right\},\hskip 5.0ptT_{2}=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{v}l_{j}b_{j}\mid l_{1}\geq 1,l_{2},\dots,l_{u}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Then we have

\displaystyle Z_{c_{k},d_{k}}(s)

\displaystyle=Z_{S_{1}\times T_{1}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)+Z_{S_{2}\times T_{1}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle\qquad+Z_{S_{1}\times T_{2}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)+Z_{S_{2}\times T_{2}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s).

Applying the induction hypothesis for functions
\pi^{c_{k}}f(x)
,
\pi^{d_{k}}g(y)
, for faces
\tau
,
\gamma
, and for

S_{1}=\bigoplus_{i=2}^{u}\mathbb{N}a_{i},\quad T_{1}=\bigoplus_{j=2}^{v}\mathbb{N}b_{j},

we have

Z_{S_{1}\times T_{1}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)=\dfrac{P_{1}(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i\geq 2,j\geq 2}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})},

for some rational polynomial
P_{1}
. By the change of variables
x=\pi^{a_{1}}t
, we get

Z_{S_{2}\times T_{1}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)=q^{-|a_{1}|}Z_{\Delta_{1}\times T_{1}}(\pi^{c_{k}+m_{f}(a_{1})}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s).

By the induction hypothesis,

Z_{\Delta_{1}\times T_{1}}(\pi^{c_{k}+m_{f}(a_{1})}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)=\dfrac{P_{2}(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i\geq 1,j\geq 2}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})}

for some
1
-variable rational polynomial
P_{2}
. Therefore,

Z_{S_{2}\times T_{1}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)=\dfrac{q^{-|a_{1}|}P_{2}(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i\geq 1,j\geq 2}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})}.

Similarly,

Z_{S_{1}\times T_{2}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)=\dfrac{q^{-|b_{1}|}P_{3}(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i\geq 2,j\geq 1}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})},

where
P_{3}
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial. Finally, by the change of variables as
x=\pi^{a_{1}}z
and

, we get

\displaystyle Z_{S_{2}\times T_{2}}(\pi^{c_{k}}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{d_{k}}g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=q^{-\mu_{k+1}s}Z_{S_{2}\times T_{2}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=q^{-\mu_{k+1}s-|a_{1}|-|b_{1}|}Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(\pi^{m_{f}(a_{1})}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{m_{g}(b_{1})}g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=q^{-\mu_{k+1}s-\nu_{k+1}}Z_{c_{k+1},d_{k+1}}(s).

The lemma follows.
∎

The same computation as in the base case gives us the proposition when
m_{f}(a_{1}),m_{g}(b_{1})>0
. The either case
m_{f}(a_{1})=0
or
m_{g}(b_{1})=0
is treated similarly to the base by combining the change of variables formula and the induction hypothesis.
∎

4.2.
The
p
-adic zeta function

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x]
and
g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[y]
be polynomials, where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
, let
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{g}
be their Newton polyhedrons, respectively. For each facet of
\Gamma_{f}
, there exists a unique primitive vector perpendicular to it. Let
\mathcal{D}_{f}
be the set of all such vectors, and we define
\mathcal{D}_{g}
similarly for
g
. For
a\in\mathcal{D}_{f}
and
b\in\mathcal{D}_{g}
, we denote by
\tau_{a}
and
\gamma_{b}
the facets corresponding to
a
and
b
, respectively.

In this section, we are going to prove the following theorem,

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x],g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[y]
be two Newton non-critical polynomials. Then the Igusa local zeta function of
f\oplus g
is equal to

Z(f\oplus g;s)=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{a,b}(1-q^{c_{a,b}(s)})},

where the product runs over all
(a,b)\in\mathcal{D}_{f}\times\mathcal{D}_{g}
, and
P
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial.

To prove the theorem, it is sufficient to show the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x]
and
g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[y]
be two Newton non-critical polynomials, let
\tau
and
\gamma
be proper faces of
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{g}
, respectively. Let
\{\tau_{a_{1}},\dots,\tau_{a_{r}}\}
be all facets containing
\tau
, and let
\{\gamma_{b_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{b_{t}}\}
be all facets containing
\gamma
. Assume that the sets
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{u}\}\subseteq\{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\}
and
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{v}\}\subseteq\{b_{1},\dots,b_{t}\}
are
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent sets. We put

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{u}\mathbb{N}a_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{2}:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{v}\mathbb{N}b_{j}.

Then

Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f\oplus g;s)=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i=1}^{u}\prod_{j=1}^{v}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})},

where
P
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial.

Proof.

We put

\mathbf{I}=\{(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid A\geq B\geq 2\}

and provide it with the lexicographic order. The lemma is proved by induction on
(A,B)\in\mathbf{I}
, where
r+t=A,u+v=B
.

The base case

r=t=u=v=1
. We first consider the case
m_{f}(a_{1}),m_{g}(b_{1})>0
. Let
M=2c(f_{\tau},(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n})+2c(g_{\gamma},(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m})+2
. Define

\displaystyle S_{1}=\{ka_{1}:k\geq M\},\quad S_{2}=\{ka_{1}:0\leq k<M\}

\displaystyle T_{1}=\{lb_{1}:l\geq M\},\quad T_{2}=\{lb_{1}:0\leq l<M\}.

Then we have

(4.1)

\begin{split}Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f\oplus g;s)=Z_{S_{2}\times T_{2}}(f\oplus g,s)&+Z_{S_{1}\times T_{2}}(f\oplus g,s)\\
&+Z_{S_{2}\times T_{1}}(f\oplus g,s)+Z_{S_{1}\times T_{1}}(f\oplus g,s).\end{split}

We have

\displaystyle Z_{S_{2}\times T_{2}}(f\oplus g,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{M-1}\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1}\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1}\}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{M-1}q^{-k|a_{1}|-l|b_{1}|}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(\pi^{ka_{1}}x)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}y)|^{s}|dxdy|.

Since
f
and
g
are Newton non-critical,
f(\pi^{ka_{1}}x)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}y)
does not have any singular points in
(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}
. By Lemma
2.5
,

\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(\pi^{ka_{1}}x)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}y)|^{s}|dxdy|=\frac{P(q^{-s})}{1-q^{-1-s}},

where
P
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial. So
Z_{S_{2}\times T_{2}}(f\oplus g,s)
also has the same form. By the change of variables formula we have

Z_{S_{1}\times T_{2}}(f\oplus g,s)=\sum_{l=0}^{M-1}q^{-l|b_{1}|}\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}q^{-k|a_{1}|}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(\pi^{ka_{1}}x)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}y)|^{s}|dxdy|.

Note that

f(\pi^{ka_{1}}x)=\pi^{km_{f}(a_{1})}\hat{f}_{k}(x),

where
\hat{f}_{k}(x)
is a polynomial in
\mathcal{O}[x]
. For
km_{f}(a_{1})>2c(g(\pi^{lb_{1}}y),(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m})+1
, it implies from Corollary
2.7
that

\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(\pi^{ka_{1}}x)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}y)|^{s}|dxdy|=\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|g(\pi^{lb_{1}}y)|^{s}|dxdy|.

Hence, we get

Z_{S_{1}\times T_{2}}(f\oplus g;s)=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{1-q^{-1-s}},

where
P
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial. The same argument also holds for
Z_{S_{2}\times T_{1}}(f\oplus g;s)
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle Z_{S_{1}\times T_{1}}(f\oplus g;s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=M}^{\infty}\int_{\{v(x)=ka_{1}\}\times\{v(y)=lb_{1}\}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=M}^{\infty}q^{-k|a_{1}|-l|b_{1}|}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(\pi^{ka_{1}}z)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}t)|^{s}|dzdt|.

Since
ka_{1}\in\Delta_{\tau}
, we have
f_{\tau}(\pi^{ka_{1}}z)=\pi^{km_{f}(a_{1})}f_{\tau}(z)
. For each
\omega\notin\tau=F_{f}(a_{1})
, we have
\omega\cdot a_{1}\geq m_{f}(a_{1})+1
, hence

\omega\cdot(ka_{1})\geq km_{f}(a_{1})+k\geq km_{f}(a_{1})+M,

for all
\omega\notin\tau
and
k\geq M
. Therefore,

f_{\tau}(\pi^{ka_{1}}z)=\pi^{km_{f}(a_{1})}(f_{\tau}(z)+\pi^{M}\hat{f}_{\tau}(z))

for some
\hat{f}_{\tau}(z)\in\mathcal{O}[z]
. Similarly,

g(\pi^{lb_{1}}t)=\pi^{lm_{g}(b_{1})}(g_{\gamma}(t)+\pi^{M}\hat{g}_{\gamma}(t))

for some
\hat{g}_{\gamma}(t)\in\mathcal{O}[t]
. If
km_{f}(a_{1})\geq lm_{g}(b_{1})
, then by Corollary
2.7
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}

\displaystyle|f(\pi^{ka_{1}}z)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}t)|^{s}|dzdt|

\displaystyle=q^{-lm_{g}(b_{1})s}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|\pi^{km_{f}(a_{1})-lm_{g}(b_{1})}f_{\tau}(z)+g_{\gamma}(t)+\pi^{M}h(z,t)|^{s}|dzdt|

\displaystyle=q^{-lm_{g}(b_{1})s}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|\pi^{km_{f}(a_{1})-lm_{g}(b_{1})}f_{\tau}(z)+g_{\gamma}(t)|^{s}|dzdt|

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f_{\tau}(\pi^{ka_{1}}z)+g_{\gamma}(\pi^{lb_{1}}t)|^{s}|dzdt|,

where
h(z,t)
is in
\mathcal{O}[z,t]
. Therefore, we have

\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(\pi^{ka_{1}}z)+g(\pi^{lb_{1}}t)|^{s}|dzdt|=\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f_{\tau}(\pi^{ka_{1}}z)+g_{\gamma}(\pi^{lb_{1}}t)|^{s}|dzdt|.

Note that the above equality is also true when
km_{f}(a_{1})\leq lm_{g}(b_{1})
, so we have

Z_{S_{1}\times T_{1}}(f\oplus g;s)=Z_{S_{1}\times T_{1}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s).

By the change of variables
x=\pi^{Ma_{1}}z
and
y=\pi^{Mb_{1}}t
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{S_{1}\times T_{1}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=q^{-M|a_{1}|-M|b_{1}|}Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(\pi^{Mm_{f}(a_{1})}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{Mm_{g}(b_{1})}g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=\frac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})(1-q^{c_{a_{1},b_{1}}(s)}))},

where
P
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial. The last equality is obtained by applying Proposition
4.2
to
\pi^{Mm_{f}(a_{1})}f
and
\pi^{Mm_{g}(b_{1})}g
. Thus, from (
4.1
), the lemma follows.

Induction hypothesis.
Suppose that the lemma is valid for any pair of Newton smooth polynomials
(f,g)
, and for any pair of faces
(\tau,\gamma)
together with any pair of cones
(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
satisfying the condition
(r+s,u+v)<(A,B)
. Now, let
f
and
g
be Newton non-critical polynomials, and assume that the faces and cones
\tau
,
\gamma
,
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
satisfy the condition
r+s=A,u+v=B
. We put
\tau^{\prime}=\cap_{i=1}^{u}\tau_{a_{i}},\gamma^{\prime}=\cap_{j=1}^{v}\gamma_{b_{j}}
. If
\tau_{a}
is a facet containing
\tau^{\prime}
, then it also contains
\tau
, hence
a\in\{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\}
. Since
\tau^{\prime}
is the intersection of all facets containing it,
\tau
is a proper face of
\tau^{\prime}
if and only if the number of facets containing
\tau^{\prime}
is less than
r
. If
\tau\subsetneq\tau^{\prime}
or
\gamma\subsetneq\gamma^{\prime}
, we can apply induction hypothesis for
f,g
and
\tau^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
and the lemma follows. When this is not the case, we have
\tau=\tau^{\prime}
and
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.
Put

M=2c(f_{\tau},(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n})+2c(g_{\gamma},(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m})+2.

For
\varnothing\neq I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,u\}
and
r_{I}=(r_{i_{1}},\dots,r_{i_{k}})
with
0\leq r_{i_{l}}\leq M-1
,
1\leq l\leq k
, we put

\displaystyle S_{I,r_{I}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{i\in I}k_{i}a_{i}+\sum_{i\notin I}k_{i}a_{i}:k_{i}=r_{i}\text{ if }i\in I,k_{i}\geq M\text{ if }i\notin I\right\},

\displaystyle S_{\varnothing}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i}\mid k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq M\right\}.

Similarly, we can define
T_{J,t_{J}}
and
T_{\varnothing}
for any
\varnothing\neq J=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,v\}
and any
t_{J}=(t_{j_{1}},\dots,t_{j_{k}})
with
0\leq t_{j_{l}}\leq M-1
,
1\leq l\leq k
. We obtain following partitions of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, respectively,

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}=\bigsqcup_{I,r_{I}}S_{I,r_{I}}\cup S_{\varnothing},\quad\Delta_{2}=\bigsqcup_{J,t_{J}}T_{J,t_{J}}\cup T_{\varnothing}.

Then

(4.2)

\displaystyle Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f\oplus g;s)=\sum_{I,r_{I},J,t_{J}}

\displaystyle Z_{S_{I,r_{i}}\times T_{J,t_{J}}}(f\oplus g;s)+\sum_{I,r_{i}}Z_{S_{I,r_{I}}\times T_{\varnothing}}(f\oplus g;s)

\displaystyle+\sum_{J,t_{J}}Z_{S_{\varnothing}\times T_{J,t_{J}}}(f\oplus g;s)+Z_{S_{\varnothing}\times T_{\varnothing}}(f\oplus g;s).

For nonempty sets
I
and
J
, we consider the following change of variables

x=\pi^{\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}a_{i}+M\sum_{i\notin I}a_{i}}z,\quad y=\pi^{\sum_{j\in J}t_{j}b_{j}+M\sum_{j\notin J}b_{j}}t.

Then we have

\displaystyle Z_{S_{I,r_{i}}\times T_{J,t_{J}}}(f\oplus g;s)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=q^{-\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}|a_{i}|-M\sum_{i\notin I}|a_{i}|-\sum_{j\in J}t_{j}|b_{j}|-M\sum_{j\notin J}|b_{j}|}Z_{\Delta_{1,I}\times\Delta_{2,J}}(f_{I,r_{I}}\oplus g_{J,t_{J}};s),

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,I}:=\bigoplus_{i\notin I}\mathbb{N}a_{i},\quad\Delta_{2,J}:=\bigoplus_{j\notin J}\mathbb{N}b_{j},

and

\displaystyle f_{I,r_{I}}(z)

\displaystyle=f(\pi^{\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}a_{i}+M\sum_{i\notin I}a_{i}}z),

\displaystyle g_{J,t_{J}}(t)

\displaystyle=g(\pi^{\sum_{j\in J}t_{j}b_{j}+M\sum_{j\notin J}b_{j}}t).

Applying the induction hypothesis to
f_{I,r_{I}}
,
g_{J,t_{J}}
,
\tau_{I}:=\cap_{i\notin I}\tau_{a_{i}}
and
\gamma_{J}:=\cap_{j\notin J}\gamma_{b_{j}},\Delta_{1,I},\Delta_{2,J}
, we have

Z_{\Delta_{1,r_{I}}\times\Delta_{2,t_{J}}}(f_{I,r_{I}}\oplus g_{J,t_{J}};s)=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i\notin I,j\notin J}(1-q^{c_{a_{j}b_{j}}(s)})},

for some
1
-variable rational polynomial
P
. Therefore,
Z_{S_{I,r_{i}}\times T_{J,t_{J}}}(f\oplus g;s)
also has the same form. A similar argument also holds for either
I
or
J
empty but not for both empty. We have

\displaystyle Z_{S_{\varnothing}\times T_{\varnothing}}(f\oplus g;s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}=M}^{\infty}\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{t}=M}^{\infty}\int_{\{v(x)=\sum k_{i}a_{i}\}\times\{v(y)=\sum l_{j}b_{j}\}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|.

By the change of variables formula,

\displaystyle\int_{\{v(x)=\sum k_{i}a_{i}\}\times\{v(y)=\sum l_{j}b_{j}\}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}|a_{i}|-\sum_{l=1}^{v}l_{j}|b_{j}|}\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i}}z)+g(\pi^{\sum_{j=1}^{v}l_{j}b_{j}}t)|^{s}|dzdt|.

As
a_{i}\in\overline{\Delta}_{\tau}
for all
i
, we have

f(\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i}}z)=\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}m_{f}(a_{i})}f_{\tau}(z)+\sum_{\omega\notin\tau}a_{\omega}\pi^{(\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i})\cdot\omega}z^{\omega}.

Moreover, for any
\omega\notin\tau=\cap_{i=1}^{u}\tau_{a_{i}}
, there is an
i_{\omega}\in\{1,\dots,u\}
such that
\omega\notin\tau_{a_{i_{\omega}}}=F_{f}(a_{i_{\omega}})
. Therefore

(\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i})\cdot\omega\geq\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}m_{f}(a_{i})+k_{i_{\omega}}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}m_{f}(a_{i})+M,

hence

f(\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i}}z)=\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}m_{f}(a_{i})}(f_{\tau}(z)+\pi^{M}\hat{f}_{\tau}(z))

for some
\hat{f}_{\tau}(z)\in\mathcal{O}[z]
. Similarly, we also have

g(\pi^{\sum_{j=1}^{v}l_{j}b_{j}}t)=\pi^{\sum_{j=1}^{v}l_{j}m_{g}(b_{j})}(g_{\gamma}(t)+\pi^{M}\hat{g}_{\gamma}(t)).

The same argument as in the base case gives us

Z_{S_{\varnothing}\times T_{\varnothing}}(f\oplus g;s)=Z_{S_{\varnothing}\times T_{\varnothing}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s).

By change of variables
x=\pi^{M\sum_{i=1}^{u}a_{i}}z
and
y=\pi^{M\sum_{j=1}^{v}b_{j}}t
, we get

\displaystyle Z_{S_{\varnothing}\times T_{\varnothing}}(f_{\tau}\oplus g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=q^{-M\sum_{i=1}^{u}|a_{i}|-M\sum_{j=1}^{v}|b_{j}|}Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(f_{\tau}(\pi^{M\sum_{i=1}^{u}a_{i}}z)+g_{\gamma}(\pi^{M\sum_{j=1}^{v}b_{j}}t);s)

\displaystyle=q^{-M\sum_{i=1}^{u}|a_{i}|-M\sum_{j=1}^{v}|b_{j}|}Z_{\Delta_{1}\times\Delta_{2}}(\pi^{M\sum_{i=1}^{u}m_{f}(a_{i})}f_{\tau}\oplus\pi^{M\sum_{j=1}^{v}m_{g}(b_{j})}g_{\gamma};s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i=1}^{u}\prod_{j=1}^{v}(1-q^{c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})},

for some
1
-variable rational polynomial
P
. The last equality follows from Proposition
4.2
for
\pi^{M\sum_{i=1}^{u}m_{f}(a_{i})}f_{\tau}(x)
and
\pi^{M\sum_{j=1}^{v}m_{g}(b_{j})}g_{\gamma}(y)
. The lemma now follows from (
4.2
).
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}[x]
and
g(y)\in\mathcal{O}[y]
be Newton smooth polynomials, let
\tau
and
\gamma
be proper faces of
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{g}
, respectively. Let
\{\tau_{a_{1}},\dots,\tau_{a_{r}}\}
be all facets containing
\tau
and
\{\gamma_{b_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{b_{t}}\}
be all facets containing
\gamma
. Then

Z_{\Delta_{\tau}\times\Delta_{\gamma}}(f\oplus g;s)=\dfrac{Q(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-1-s})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{t}(1-q^{-c_{a_{i},b_{j}}(s)})},

where
Q
is a
1
-variable rational polynomial.

Proof.

There exists a finite partition of
\Delta_{\tau}
into cones
\Delta_{\tau}^{i}
such that
\Delta_{\tau}^{i}
is spanned by the
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{u}\}\subseteq\{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\}
. Similarly, each
\Delta_{\gamma}
can be partitioned into cones
\Delta_{\gamma}^{j}
such that
\Delta_{\gamma}^{j}
is spanned by the
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent set
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{v}\}\subseteq\{b_{1},\dots,b_{t}\}
. Note that, each
\Delta_{\tau}^{i}\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}
is the disjoint union of the sets
a+\bigoplus_{i=1}^{u}\mathbb{N}a_{i}
, where
a
runs through all elements of

\mathbb{N}^{n}\cap\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{u}k_{i}a_{i}:0<k_{i}\leq 1\right\}.

We also obtain a similar partition for
\Delta_{\gamma}^{j}
. We have,

(4.3)

\begin{split}Z_{\Delta_{\tau}\times\Delta_{\gamma}}(f\oplus g;s)&=\sum_{i,j}Z_{\Delta_{\tau}^{i}\times\Delta_{\gamma}^{j}}(f\oplus g;s)\\
&=\sum_{i,j}\sum_{a,b}Z_{\{a+\bigoplus_{i=1}^{u}\mathbb{N}a_{i}\}\times\{b+\bigoplus_{j=1}^{v}\mathbb{N}b_{j}\}}(f\oplus g;s).\end{split}

By the change of variables
x=\pi^{a}z
and
y=\pi^{b}t
, we have

Z_{\{a+\bigoplus_{i=1}^{u}\mathbb{N}a_{i}\}\times\{b+\bigoplus_{j=1}^{v}\mathbb{N}b_{j}\}}(f\oplus g;s)=q^{-|a|-|b|}Z_{\{\bigoplus_{i=1}^{u}\mathbb{N}a_{i}\}\times\{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{v}\mathbb{N}b_{j}\}}(f(\pi^{a}z)+g(\pi^{b}t);s).

The corollary follows by applying Lemma
4.6
for
f(\pi^{a}z)
and
g(\pi^{b}t)
and the expansion (
4.3
).
∎

We now prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

We have the following partitions

(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{n}=\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{\tau}\Delta_{\tau},\quad(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m}=\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{\gamma}\Delta_{\gamma},

where
\tau,\gamma
runs through all faces of
\Gamma_{f},\Gamma_{g}
respectively. Therefore,

\displaystyle Z(f\oplus g;s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\int_{\{v(x)=k\}\times\{v(y)=l\}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|+\int_{(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{n}\times\mathcal{O}^{n}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathcal{O}^{n}\times(\mathcal{O}^{\times})^{m}}|f(x)+g(y)|^{s}|dxdy|+\sum_{\tau,\gamma}Z_{\Delta_{\tau}\times\Delta_{\gamma}}(f\oplus g;s).

Thus Theorem
4.5
follows by applying Lemma
2.5
for three first integrals and Corollary
4.7
for the last one.
∎

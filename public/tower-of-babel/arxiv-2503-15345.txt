Is the Radcliffe wave turbulent?
Itzhak Goldman

Abstract
Context. Tidal interaction with a satellite galaxy and Kelvin Helmholtz instability were proposed as the generation mechanisms of the Radcliffe wave. In each of these scenarios it is likely that turbulence could be generated. Aims. We intend to find out wether the wave is turbulent, and if so to obtain the turbulence characteristics. Methods. We use the observed vertical velocity field, of various young tracers of the gas kinematics, obtained by Li & Chen 2022 , Konietzka et al. 2024 and Zhu et al. 2024 , in order to test for the existence of turbulence. We do so by computing the power spectrum and the structure function of the vertical velocity field. Results. The latter suggest the existence of compressible, Burgers, turbulence. Conclusions. The turbulence timescale on the largest spatial scale is ∼ ( 500 − 900 ) ​ M ​ y ​ r \sim(500-900)Myr , implying that the turbulence has been generated ∼ ( 500 − 900 ) ​ M ​ y ​ r \sim(500-900)Myr ago. The turbulence region depth in a direction perpendicular to the Radcliffe wave direction is ∼ ( 400 − 500 ) ​ p ​ c . \sim(400-500)pc.

Introduction

Since its discovery
(
Alves et al. 2020
)
, the Radcliffe wave drew the attention and interest of many researchers. Suggestions regarding its formation mechanism included tidal interaction with a satellite galaxy or star cluster, e. g.
(
Tepper-García et al. 2022
)
and a hydrodynamical instability e.g. a Kelvin Helmholtz (KH) instability
(
Fleck 2020
)
. Following the discovery of the Radcliffe wave, there were investigations aimed at obtaining (in addition to the spatial structure) also kinematic information, e.g.
(
Tu et al. 2022
;
Thulasidharan et al. 2022
;
Bobylev et al. 2022
;
Li & Chen 2022
;
Konietzka et al. 2024
;
Zhu et al. 2024
)
. These papers used the velocities of young tracers to obtain
the velocity
of the gas . the underlying rational was that the tracers were created at rest relative to the gas. The young age of the tracers ensures that these velocities have not changed much since they formed.
At positions along the wave the vertical velocities of the tracers were derived, and averaged to obtain a representative value of the vertical velocity at a given position.

The above papers tried to find wether the wave is a coherent oscillating structure. They described it as composed of one or two spatial and temporal frequencies. The aim of the present paper is to find out wether the wave is turbulent, namely if it is composed of a continuum of wavenumbers with non linear mutual interactions, which manifest as a power spectrum with an energy cascade. To that end, we derive 1D power spectra and structure functions of the vertical velocity field along the wave direction,

We employ the observational vertical velocity field given in
Li & Chen 2022
,
Konietzka et al. 2024
and
Zhu et al. 2024
because these papers give fits of the observed vertical velocity as function of the position along the Radcliffe wave, These observational fits are essential for computing the power spectrum and the structure function. For the three velocity fields we used a section of length
\sim 2.4kpc
.

We used The Engauge digitizer12.1 to read the values of the position and vertical velocity from the
fitted velocity curves of these papers. Then, after subtracting the mean of the velocity field, we computed the discrete 1D power spectrum and the discrete 1D structure function for each data,

The power spectrum was obtained by evaluating the squared absolute value of the discrete Fourier transform of the velocity field.

The structure function
S(x)
is defined as

S(x)=<\left(v_{z}(x+x^{\prime})-v_{z}(x^{\prime})\right)^{2}>=2C(0)-2C(x)

(1)

Where the angular brackets denote averaging over
x^{\prime}
.
C(x)
is the autocorrelation at a lag
x
, which for a zero mean velocity field is

C(x)=<v_{z}(x^{\prime})v_{z}(x^{\prime}+x)>

(2)

Here too, the angular brackets denote averaging over
x^{\prime}
.

Generally, power spectra are more detailed on smaller spatial scales, while structure functions are more detailed on the larger spatial scales.
The power spectra and structure functions were computed using Wolfram Mathematica 14.1.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we address the vertical velocity field of
Li & Chen 2022

Konietzka et al. 2024
, and in section 4 we address the velocity field of

Zhu et al. 2024
. Discussion is presented in section 5. In the appendix we obtain the structure function of a quantity that is the result of integration in a direction perpendicular to the
x
axis.

Discussion

The power spectra based on the various vertical velocity fields do suggest the existence of turbulence in the sense that there is a spectrum of wavenumbers and not just few wavenumbers. All three power spectra exhibit a cascade
consistent with a
q^{-3}
behavior on the large
q
( small spatial scales).
Except for the power spectrum of
v_{z}
from
Zhu et al. 2024
, there is an indication for a
q^{-2}
behavior on the
large spatial scales (small q). The power spectrum of
v_{z}
from
Zhu et al. 2024
has a high value at
q=2
. This is in line with the shape of the fit of
v_{z}(x)
(Fig.
7
) which is quite different from that of
Li & Chen 2022
and
Konietzka et al. 2024
.

The structure functions show clearly a transition from a logarithmic slope of 1 on the large spatial lags to a logarithmic slope of 2 on the small spatial lags, this corresponds to 1D turbulent power spectrum with a
k^{-2}
dependence on the wavenumber.

The structure functions and the power spectra indicate the existence
of a compressible turbulence with a 1D power spectrum
\propto k^{-2}
, with
k
the 1D wavenumber. This power spectrum was derived by
Burgers 1948
describing a hierarchy of shocks in compressible gas.

Compressible turbulence power spectra were observed in HI intensity maps in the Milky Way (MW) galaxy
(
Green 1993
)
and in the SMC
(
Stanimirovic et al. 1999
)
. This power spectrum has been observed also in molecular clouds
(
Larson 1981
;
Leung et al. 1982
;
Dame et al. 1986
)
, and in the HII region Sharpless 142
(
Roy & Joncas 1985
)
. It has been found in a shocked cloud near the Milky Way galaxy center
(
Contini & Goldman 2011
)
, in the Gamma ray emission from the large Magellanic Cloud
(
Besserglik & Goldman 2021
)
, and recently in CSWA13, a gravitationally lensed Star-Forming galaxy at
z=1.87
with outflowing wind
(
Goldman 2024
)
. It has been obtained also in numerical simulations e.g.
(
Passot et al. 1988
;
Vázquez-Semadeni et al. 1997
;
Kritsuk et al. 2007
;
Federrath et al. 2021
)
.

It has been noted by
(
Stutzki et al. 1998
;
Goldman 2000
;
Lazarian & Pogosyan 2000
;
Miville-Deschênes
et al. 2003b
)
that when the lateral spatial scale is smaller than the depth of the turbulent layer, the logarithmic slope of the power spectrum steepens by
-1
compared to its value when the lateral scale is large compared to the depth. The logarithmic slope of the structure function increases by 1. This behavior has been indeed revealed in observational power spectra of Galactic and extra Galactic turbulence ( e.g.

Elmegreen 2001
,
Block et al. 2010
,
Miville-Deschênes
et al. 2003a
,
Goldman 2021
) and in solar photospheric turbulence
(
Abramenko & Yurchyshyn 2020
)
.

In the appendix we obtain an estimate for the depth of the turbulence in the case that the structure function is the result of integration in a direction perpendicular to the 1D axis of the turbulence.This would be relevant when the velocity field is determined by an emission or absorption line, In the present case the fitted velocities represent tracers at different perpendicular distances from the wave direction - but are not strictly an integration in the perpendicular direction.
Nevertheless, it maybe of interest to apply the theoretical relation
D=1.83x_{tr}
derived in the appendix for the case of compressible turbulence. Here,
x_{tr}
is the value of the spatial lag of the structure function which marks the transition between logarithmic slopes of
1
and
2
.

Table 1. displays the turbulent r. m. s. value of
v_{z}
, the depth
D
and the turbulence timescale for each of the three velocity fields, The turbulence timescale of the largest spatial scale is
\sim L/v_{z,turb}
, with L the largest spatial scale.

Since the gas comprising the Radcliffe wave is molecular, the turbulent r.m.s. velocity is supersonic, in line with the turbulence being compressible.

The buildup time of the turbulence cannot be smaller than the turbulence timescale of the largest spatial scale. Therefore, this estimate represents a lower limit on the look- back time when the turbulence was generated. This information could help decide between possible generation mechanisms of the Radcliffe wave.

The turbulent r.m.s. velocity of the
Zhu et al. 2024
data is about half of that of the other two references and as a result the turbulence timescale is about twice the values of the other two.

Appendix A
The theoretical 1D structure function of a quantity that is the result of integration in the perpendicular direction

Consider a function
f(x)
where
x
is a straight line and is an integral along a perpendicular direction to that line, y:

f(x)=\int_{0}^{D}g(x,y)dy.

(3)

Here,
y
is the perpendicular coordinate and
D
the depth. A plane parallel geometry is assumed for simplicity.

The autocorrelation function of
f(x)
is:

\displaystyle C_{f}(x)=<f(x+x^{\prime})f(x)>=

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{D}\int_{0}^{D}<g(x^{\prime},y)g(x+x^{\prime},y^{\prime})>dydy^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{D}\int_{0}^{D}C_{g}(x,y-y^{\prime})dydy^{\prime}.

,

The autocorrelation function
C_{g}(x,y-y^{\prime})
can be expressed by the two-dimensional power spectrum,
P_{2}(k_{x},k_{y})
,

\displaystyle C_{g}(x,y-y^{\prime})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(k_{x}x+k_{y}(y-y^{\prime}))}P_{2}(k_{x},k_{y})dk_{x}dk_{y}.

leading to

C_{f}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ik_{x}x}P_{2}(k_{x},k_{y})\frac{\sin^{2}\left(k_{y}D/2\right)}{\left(k_{y}D/2\right)^{2}}dk_{x}dk_{y}.

(5)

from equations (2) and (A4) follows the expression for the structure function

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptS_{f}(x,D)\propto\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin^{2}(k_{x}x/2)\frac{\sin^{2}\left(k_{y}D/2\right)}{\left(k_{y}D/2\right)^{2}}P_{2}(k_{x},k_{y})dk_{x}dk_{y}.

(6)

In the case of a turbulence with a one-dimensional power spectrum which is a power law with index
-m
, the two dimensional power spectrum is

P_{2}(k_{x},k_{y})\propto\left(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}\right)^{-(m+1)/2.}\ \ \ \ \ \

(7)

resulting in

\displaystyle S_{f}(x,D)\propto\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin^{2}(k_{x}x/2)\frac{\sin^{2}\left(k_{y}D/2\right)}{\left(k_{y}D/2\right)^{2}}

(8)

\displaystyle\left(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}\right)^{-(m+1)/2}dk_{x}dk_{y}.

It is convenient to define the dimensionless variables

\eta=k_{y}D/2.\ \ \ \ \ \ \ ;\ \ \ \mu=k_{x}D/2.

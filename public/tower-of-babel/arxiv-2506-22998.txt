1.
Introduction

The free bilayer graphene operator
D_{m}
is defined on the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{2})
by

\displaystyle D_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}m&\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}\\
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}&-m\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{where }m>0.

While this particular operator was introduced by Ferrulli, Laptev and Safronov in
[
8
]
,
the study of bilayer graphene and its properties, including the development of theoretical models and operators describing its electronic structure,
has been ongoing for a longer period of time (since 2004).

The spectrum of the operator
D_{m}
coincides with the set
(-\infty,-m]\cup[m,\infty)
. Thus, the interval
(-m,m)
is a gap in the spectrum. Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
be a non-negative potential on that decays as
|x|\to\infty
. In this paper, we analyze the discrete spectrum of the perturbed operator

\displaystyle D(\alpha)=D_{m}-\alpha V,\quad\alpha>0.

(1.1)

The operator
V
in (
1.1
) is understood as the matrix operator

\displaystyle V\cdot I=\begin{pmatrix}V&0\\
0&V\end{pmatrix}.

The constraints that we impose on the potential
V
guarantee that the spectrum of
D(\alpha)
is discrete in
(-m,m)
. That implies that it consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, which move monotonically from the right to the left with the growth of
\alpha
.

Let us fix a point
\lambda\in(-m,m)
and introduce
N(\lambda,\alpha)
as the number of eigenvalues of
D(t)
passing through
\lambda
when
t
increases from
0
to
\alpha
. We investigate the asymptotic behavior of
N(\lambda,\alpha)
for sufficiently large values of
\alpha
.

We define the cubes
\mathbb{Q}_{n}=[0,1)^{2}+n
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and set

\displaystyle\|V\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})}=\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}|V|^{q}\,dx,\qquad\text{for }q>0.

Our first result deals with the case

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\|V\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})}<\infty,\qquad q>1.

(1.2)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V
satisfy (
1.2
). Then

\displaystyle N(\lambda,\alpha)\sim\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V\,dx,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Now we consider the case where
V\not\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Instead, we assume that
V
is a bounded function obeying the condition

\displaystyle V(x)\sim\frac{\Psi(\theta)}{|x|^{p}},\quad\text{as }|x|\to\infty,

(1.3)

where
\theta=x/|x|
and
0<p<2
. The function
\Psi\geqslant 0
is assumed to be continuous on the unit circle
\mathbb{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|=1\}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<p<2
and let
V
satisfy (
1.3
). Then

\displaystyle N(\lambda,\alpha)\sim\frac{\alpha^{2/p}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[((\lambda+\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Here,
a_{+}
denotes the positive part of the number
a
, i.e.,

\displaystyle a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}.

Let us give a short summary of the past research in the area. Eigenvalues in gaps of the continuous spectrum were studied extensively for Schrödinger operators. For instance, R. Hempel proved in
[
5
]
that the number of eigenvalues of the operator
-\Delta+f-\alpha V
passing through the point
\lambda
of a gap in the spectrum of the periodic operator
-\Delta+f
obeys Weyl’s law:

\displaystyle N(\lambda,\alpha)\sim(2\pi)^{-d}\omega_{d}\alpha^{d/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V^{d/2}\,dx,\qquad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty,

(1.4)

where
\omega_{d}
is volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then M. Birman proved (
1.4
) through a different approach in
[
2
]
. Other relevant results could also be found in
[
1
,
6
]
. Such results could be linked to the study of crystal color. When some ions in a crystal lattice are replaced by impurity ions, the resulting perturbation, described by the potential
\alpha V
, can create new energy levels. These energy levels are exactly the eigenvalues of
-\Delta+f-\alpha V
. They are responsible for the absorption of the light of specific wavelengths, leading to the observed color of the crystal. Based on these remarks, the problem addressed in the present paper can be associated with the study of the color of graphene.

Several mathematicians have studied eigenvalues in gaps of the spectrum with non-signdefinite perturbations (see
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
). In this case, the eigenvalues of the operator are not monotone functions of
\alpha
, and the quantity
N(\lambda,\alpha)
should be defined as the difference of the number of eigenvalues passing
\lambda
in two opposite directions. Using this definition, Safronov
[
7
]
generalized the formula (
1.4
) to the case of non-signdefinite perturbations. The only change is that
V
has to be replaced by
V_{+}
on the right hand side of (
1.4
).

The graphene operator
D_{m}
was introduced in the paper by Ferrulli, Laptev and Safronov
[
8
]
. However, the authors of
[
8
]
analyzed complex eigenvalues of the perturbed operator
D(\alpha)
with a non-self adjoint perturbation
V
and a fixed value of
\alpha
. Our results differ from theorems in
[
8
]
in a critical way: the perturbations that we consider are self-adjoint and
\alpha\to\infty
.

The current paper overcomes the main technical difficulty appearing when one studies operators having only bounded gaps in the spectrum: one cannot use Dirichlet-Neumann bracketing as suggested in
the paper by Alama, Deift, and Hempel
[
1
]
.
While Dirichlet-Neumann bracketing is a powerful technique for analyzing the spectrum of semi-bounded operators,
it cannot be applied when dealing with operators that are not semi-bounded.

2.
Preliminaries

Here, we provide necessary background information that is needed to understand the subsequent sections of the paper.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space. The class of compact operators on
{\mathfrak{H}}
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
.
For a compact operator
T\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
, the symbols
s_{k}(T)
denote the singular values of

T
enumerated in the non-increasing order
(k\in\mathbb{N})
and counted in accordance with their multiplicity. Observe that
s^{2}_{k}(T)
are eigenvalues of the operator
T^{*}T
. We set

\displaystyle n(s,T)=\#\{k:s_{k}(T)>s\},\qquad s>0.

For a self-adjoint compact operator
T
, we also set

\displaystyle n_{\pm}(s,T)=\#\{k:\pm\lambda_{k}(T)>s\},\qquad s>0,

where
\lambda_{k}(T)
are eigenvalues of
T
. It follows that (see
[
3
]
)

\displaystyle n_{\pm}(s_{1}+s_{2},T_{1}+T_{2})\leqslant n_{\pm}(s_{1},T_{1})+n_{\pm}(s_{2},T_{2}),\qquad s_{1},s_{2}>0.

A similar inequality holds for the function
n
. Also,

\displaystyle n(s_{1}s_{2},T_{1}T_{2})\leqslant n(s_{1},T_{1})+n(s_{2},T_{2}),\qquad s_{1},s_{2}>0.

The class of compact operators
T
whose singular values satisfy

\displaystyle\|T\|^{p}_{\mathfrak{S}_{p}}:=\sum_{k}s^{p}_{k}(T)<\infty,\qquad p>0,

is called the Schatten class
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
.

Besides the classes
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
, we will be dealing with the so-called
weak Schatten classes
\Sigma_{p}
of compact operators
T
obeying the condition

\displaystyle\|T\|^{p}_{\Sigma_{p}}:=\sup_{s>0}s^{p}n(s,T)<\infty.

We also introduce the class
\Sigma_{p}^{0}
as the collection of compact operators
T
such that

\displaystyle n(s,T)=o(s^{-p}),\quad\text{as }s\to 0.

Note that
\mathfrak{S}_{p}\subset\Sigma^{0}_{p}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T_{1}\in\Sigma_{p}
and
T_{2}\in\Sigma_{q}
, where
p>0
and
q>0
. Then
T_{1}T_{2}\in\Sigma_{r}
, where
1/r=1/p+1/q
, and

\displaystyle\|T_{1}T_{2}\|_{\Sigma_{r}}\leqslant 2^{1/r}\|T_{1}\|_{\Sigma_{p}}\|T_{2}\|_{\Sigma_{q}}.

Proof.

Since
s=s^{r/p}s^{r/q}
, we have

\displaystyle n(s,T_{1}T_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle n(s^{r/p},T_{1})+n(s^{r/q},T_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle s^{r}(\|T_{1}\|^{p}_{\Sigma_{p}}+\|T_{2}\|^{q}_{\Sigma_{q}}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|T_{1}T_{2}\|^{r}_{\Sigma_{r}}\leqslant\|T_{1}\|^{p}_{\Sigma_{p}}+\|T_{2}\|^{q}_{\Sigma_{q}}.

(2.1)

Clearly, the inequality (
2.1
) implies the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\|T_{1}\|_{\Sigma_{p}}\|T_{2}\|_{\Sigma_{q}}}T_{1}T_{2}\right\|_{\Sigma_{r}}\leqslant 2^{1/r},

which can alternatively be written in the form
\|T_{1}T_{2}\|_{\Sigma_{r}}\leqslant 2^{1/r}\|T_{1}\|_{\Sigma_{p}}\|T_{2}\|_{\Sigma_{q}}
.
∎

For self-adjoint operators
T=T^{*}\in\Sigma_{p}
, we introduce the functionals

\displaystyle\Delta^{\pm}_{p}(T):=\limsup_{s\to 0}s^{p}n_{\pm}(s,T),\qquad\delta^{\pm}_{p}(T):=\liminf_{s\to 0}s^{p}n_{\pm}(s,T)

The following theorem is due to H. Weyl.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
T_{1},T_{2}
are self-adjoint operators,
T_{1},T_{2}\in\Sigma_{p}
, and
T_{1}-T_{2}\in\Sigma_{p}^{0}
, then

\displaystyle\Delta^{\pm}_{p}(T_{1})=\Delta^{\pm}_{p}(T_{2}),\qquad\delta^{\pm}_{p}(T_{1})=\delta^{\pm}_{p}(T_{2}),

For
\lambda\in(-m,m)
, we define the operator
X_{\lambda}
by

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}=W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W,\qquad W=\sqrt{V}.

(2.2)

The next statement is widely known as the Birman-Schwinger principle.
It allows one to transform the spectral problem involving the unbounded operator
D(\alpha)

into a spectral problem for the compact operator
X_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
X_{\lambda}
be defined by (
2.2
). Then

\displaystyle N(\lambda,\alpha)=n_{+}(s,X_{\lambda}),\qquad\text{for }s=\alpha^{-1}.

For the proof of this statement, see
[
2
]
.

We will also need the following theorem (see M. Birman
[
2
]
) in which
-\Delta
is the operator on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with an arbitrary
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
W
be a real-valued function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
X=W((-\Delta)^{l}+I)^{-1}W
,
\,V=W^{2}
and
p=d/2l
. Assume that
[V]_{p}<\infty
, where

[V]_{p}=\begin{cases}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|V\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})}^{p},&\text{ if }0<p<1;\\
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{n}}V^{q}\,dx)^{1/q},\forall q>1,&\text{ if }p=1;\\
\|V\|_{p},&\text{ if }p>1,\end{cases}

and
\mathbb{Q}_{n}=[0,1)^{d}+n
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then
X\in\Sigma_{p}
and
\|X\|_{\Sigma_{p}}\leqslant C[V]_{p}
for some constant
C>0
independent of
V
.

With
[V]_{p}
defined as in Theorem
2.4
, we formulate the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

Assume that
X=W((-\Delta)^{l}+I)^{-1}\tilde{W},V=W^{2},\tilde{V}=\tilde{W}^{2}
and

. Then
\|X\|_{\Sigma_{p}}\leqslant C_{0}[V]_{p}^{1/2}[\tilde{V}]_{p}^{1/2}
for some constant

and

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
F
be the Fourier transform on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
defined by

\displaystyle[Fu](\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi x}u(x)\,dx.

Then
F
diagonalizes the graphene operator in the sense that

\displaystyle D_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}m&(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}})^{2}\\
(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}})^{2}&-m\end{pmatrix}=F^{*}\begin{pmatrix}m&(i\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{2}\\
(i\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{2}&-m\end{pmatrix}F.

For convenience, we denote

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{m}(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}m&(i\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{2}\\
(i\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{2}&-m\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}m&-(\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2})^{2}\\
-(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{2}&-m\end{pmatrix},

and call it the symbol of
D_{m}
. Observe that

\displaystyle(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{*}[(\hat{D}_{m}(\xi)-\lambda I)^{-1}]F=\begin{pmatrix}m-\lambda&-(\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2})^{2}\\
-(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{2}&-m-\lambda\end{pmatrix}^{-1}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}-m^{2}-|\xi|^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}-m-\lambda&(\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2})^{2}\\
(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{2}&m-\lambda\end{pmatrix}.

We now show that it is enough to prove Theorem
1.1
for the case where
W\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Let
W\not\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1.1
. For an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, let
W_{\varepsilon}
be a smooth compactly supported approximation of
W
with the property

\displaystyle[(W-W_{\varepsilon})^{2}]_{1}<\varepsilon.

Then apparently, the operator
X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)=W_{\varepsilon}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W_{\varepsilon}
approximates
X(\lambda)=W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W
in the class
\Sigma_{1}
.

In order to illustrate that, we apply Corollary
2.5
, which leads us to the following statement.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
W
and
\tilde{W}
be two real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\displaystyle[W^{2}]_{1}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad[\tilde{W}^{2}]_{1}<\infty,

Then

\displaystyle\|W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{W}\|_{\Sigma_{1}}\leqslant C[W]^{1/2}_{1}[\tilde{W}]^{1/2}_{1},

for some constant
C\geqslant 0
that is independent of both
W
and
\tilde{W}
.

Proof.

Indeed, because

\displaystyle(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}=(-\Delta+I)^{-1/2}(-\Delta+I)^{1/2}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}(-\Delta+I)^{1/2}(-\Delta+I)^{-1/2},

it suffices to show that the operator

\displaystyle B(\lambda)=(-\Delta+I)^{1/2}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}(-\Delta+I)^{1/2}

initially defined on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
has a bounded extension to all of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. The boundedness of
B(\lambda)
is equivalent to the assertion that the matrix-valued function

\displaystyle(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{1/2}(\hat{D}_{m}(\xi)-\lambda I)^{-1}(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{1/2}

is bounded. The latter follows straightforwardly from (
3.1
).
∎

As a direct consequence of Proposition
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|X(\lambda)-X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(W-W_{\varepsilon})(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W\|_{\Sigma_{1}}+\|W_{\varepsilon}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}(W-W_{\varepsilon})\|_{\Sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C([(W-W_{\varepsilon})^{2}]_{1}^{1/2}[W^{2}]_{1}^{1/2}+[W_{\varepsilon}^{2}]_{1}^{1/2}[(W-W_{\varepsilon})^{2}]^{1/2})

\displaystyle<2C\sqrt{\varepsilon}([W^{2}]_{1}^{1/2}+[W_{\varepsilon}^{2}]_{1}^{1/2}])=:\delta(\varepsilon),

which tends to
0
when
\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover,
X(\lambda)=X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)+(X(\lambda)-X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))
. By Ky Fan inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle n_{+}(s,X(\lambda))

\displaystyle\leqslant n_{+}((1-\varepsilon_{0})s,X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))+n_{+}(\varepsilon_{0}s,X(\lambda)-X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))

\displaystyle\leqslant n_{+}((1-\varepsilon_{0})s,X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))+(\varepsilon_{0}s)^{-1}\|X(\lambda)-X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)\|_{\Sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant n_{+}((1-\varepsilon_{0})s,X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))+(\varepsilon_{0}s)^{-1}\delta(\varepsilon),

(3.2)

for any
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
. Suppose that Theorem
1.1
holds for the potential
V_{\varepsilon}=W_{\varepsilon}^{2}
, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Then we deduce from (

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,X(\lambda))\leqslant\limsup_{s\to 0}sn_{+}((1-\varepsilon_{0})s,X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))+\delta(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{0}}\limsup_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,X_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))+\delta(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{4\pi(1-\varepsilon_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\delta(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon_{0}.

By taking the limit as

, we infer that

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,X(\lambda))\leqslant\frac{1}{4\pi(1-\varepsilon_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V\,dx.

Due to the fact that
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is arbitrary, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,X(\lambda))\leqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V\,dx.

Similarly, we can establish the inequality

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,X(\lambda))\geqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V\,dx.

Thus, Theorem
1.1
holds for potentials
V\notin C_{0}^{\infty}
as long as it holds for all

with
W\in C_{0}^{\infty}
.

Now we assume that
W\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and choose an appropriate function
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the property

\displaystyle\zeta(\xi)=\begin{cases}0,\quad\text{ if }|\xi|<\varepsilon;\\
1,\quad\text{ if $|\xi|>2$},\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small. Then we define the matrix-valued function

\displaystyle a(\xi)=\frac{\zeta(\xi)}{|y|^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}0&-(\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2})^{2}\\
-(\xi_{1}+i\xi^{2})&0\end{pmatrix}.

Additionally, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{X}(\lambda)=\sqrt{V}F^{*}[a(\xi)]F\sqrt{V},

where
[a(\xi)]
denotes the operator of multiplication by the function
a(\xi)
, and prove a valuable proposition below.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
W\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Suppose that
\lim_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,\tilde{X}(\lambda))
exists. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,\tilde{X}(\lambda))=\lim_{s\to 0}sn_{+}(s,X(\lambda)).

Proof.

It is adequate to prove that
\tilde{X}(\lambda)-X(\lambda)\in\Sigma_{1}^{0}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\tilde{X}(\lambda)-X(\lambda)=WF^{*}[\beta(\xi)]FW,

where

\displaystyle\beta(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{D}_{m}(\xi)-\lambda I)^{-1}-\frac{\zeta(\xi)}{|\xi|^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}0&-(\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2})^{2}\\
-(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{2}&0\end{pmatrix}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{2}-\lambda^{2}+|\xi|^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}m+\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda-m\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{m^{2}-\lambda^{2}+|\xi|^{4}}-\frac{\zeta(\xi)}{|\xi|^{4}}\right)\begin{pmatrix}0&-(\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2})^{2}\\
-(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

The first term on the right-hand side of (

) is an integrable function of
\xi
. And regarding the second term, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{2}-\lambda^{2}+|\xi|^{4}}-\frac{\zeta(\xi)}{|y|^{4}}=\frac{(1-\zeta(\xi))|\xi|^{4}-\zeta(\xi)(m^{2}-\lambda^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}(m^{2}-\lambda^{2}+|\xi|^{4})},

which is evidently integrable in some neighborhood of the origin by the definition of

and equals

as

. Thus,
\beta\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. This implies that the operators
WF^{*}|\beta(\xi)|^{1/2}
and

are both Hilbert-Schmidt operators. Therefore,

\displaystyle\tilde{X}(\lambda)-X(\lambda)=WF^{*}\big[|\beta(\xi)|^{1/2}\text{sign}(\beta(\xi))|\beta(\xi)|^{1/2}\big]FW

belongs to
\mathfrak{S}_{1}\subset\Sigma_{1}^{0}
.
∎

The following theorem is a consequence of Theorem 2 from
[

]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}(sn_{+}(s,X))=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n_{+}(1,G(x,\xi))\,dx\,d\xi,

\displaystyle G(x,\xi)=V(x)|\xi|^{-4}\begin{pmatrix}0&-(\xi_{1}-i\xi_{2})^{2}\\

(3.5)

Hence, for every fixed

in (

\displaystyle\text{tr}(G(x,\xi))=0,

\displaystyle n_{+}(1,G(x,\xi))=\begin{cases}0,\quad\text{if }V(x)|\xi|^{-2}\leqslant 1;\\

Consequently,

is the characteristic function

of the set

\displaystyle\Omega:=\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:V(x)|\xi|^{-2}>1\}.

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n_{+}(1,G(x,\xi))\,dx\,d\xi=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{\Omega}(x,\xi)\,d\xi\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\pi V(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V(x)\,dx,

(3.6)

Combining (

\displaystyle N(\lambda,\alpha)\sim\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V(x)\,dx,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty,

for every

that obeys (

is complete.

(4.20)

\displaystyle C_{8}\|b\|_{\infty}[\psi_{\delta}]^{1/2}_{1}\cdot[W_{2}^{2}]^{1/2}_{1}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|W_{2}^{2}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\|W_{1}F^{*}(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{2}}=\|W_{1}F^{*}(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}FW_{1}\|_{\Sigma_{1}}

4.6.
Operator
W_{2}(D_{0}-\lambda I)^{-1}W_{2}

We now consider the only remaining self-adjoint operator in (

),
W_{2}(D_{0}-\lambda I)^{-1}W_{2}
. To get started, we introduce the notation
\tilde{\Omega}_{2}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\varepsilon_{1}<|x|<\varepsilon_{2}\}

\Omega_{2}(\alpha)=\alpha^{1/p}\tilde{\Omega}_{2}
, which means that one set is obtained from the other by scaling. Then we decompose

into finitely many sets denoted by

, where

is a square for

and
Q_{l}=\tilde{\Omega}_{2}\backslash\cup_{j=1}^{l-1}Q_{j}
. Evidently, each square

can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle\delta([0,1)^{2}+n)

(4.26)

for some
\delta>0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
when

. Moreover,
\Omega_{2}(\alpha)=\alpha^{1/p}\tilde{\Omega}_{2}=\cup_{j=1}^{l}\alpha^{1/p}Q_{j}
. Employing the same methodology as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
and the relation (
4.3
), we can show that

\displaystyle n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},W_{2}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W_{2})

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j=1}^{l}n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},\chi_{j}W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W\chi_{j})+o(\alpha^{2/p}),\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty,

(4.27)

where
\chi_{j}
represents the characteristic function of set
\alpha^{1/p}Q_{j}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant l
. In order to compute the asymptotics of
n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},W_{2}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W_{2})
, it suffices to analyze each individual term on the right-hand side of (
4.6
).

Let
Q
be a square of the form (
4.26
), let
\phi_{\beta}
be the characteristic function of the set
\beta Q
. We want to investigate the behavior of the quantity
n_{+}(\tau,\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\phi_{\beta})
when the value of
\beta
approaches infinity and
\tau>0
is fixed. We intend to calculate the value of the limit
\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\beta^{-2}n_{+}(\tau,\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\phi_{\beta})
.

Note that for each self-adjoint operator
A
, we have
\chi_{(\tau,\infty)}(A)=E_{A}(\tau,\infty)
. Thus,

\displaystyle n_{+}(\tau,A)=\text{tr}(E_{A}(\tau,\infty))=\text{tr}(\chi_{(\tau,\infty)}(A)),

if
A
is compact and self-adjoint.

We are going to prove the following important proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every fixed
\tau>0
,

n_{+}\bigl(\tau,\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda)^{-1}\phi_{\beta}\bigr)\sim(4\pi)^{-1}\beta^{2}\Bigl((\tau^{-1}+\lambda)_{+}^{2}-m^{2}\Bigr)_{+}^{1/2}{\rm Area}\,Q,\quad\text{as }\beta\to\infty,

where
x_{+}=\max(0,x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

First, note that

\pi\Bigl((\tau^{-1}+\lambda)_{+}^{2}-m^{2}\Bigr)_{+}^{1/2}={\rm area}\,\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:\,\,\,\,\,\bigl(\sqrt{|\xi|^{4}+m^{2}}-\lambda\bigr)^{-1}>\tau\}.

Based on the fact that
\pm\sqrt{|\xi|^{4}+m^{2}}
are eigenvalues of the symbol

\hat{D}_{m}(\xi):=\begin{pmatrix}m&(\xi_{2}-i\xi_{1})^{2}\\
(\xi_{2}+i\xi_{1})^{2}&-m\end{pmatrix}

we find

{\rm tr}\,\Phi\bigl(\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\phi_{\beta}\bigr)\sim{\rm tr}\,\Bigl[\phi_{\beta}\Phi\bigl((D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\bigr)\phi_{\beta}\Bigr],\quad\text{as }\beta\to\infty,

(4.28)

for
\Phi
being the characteristic function of the interval
(\tau,\infty)
. It is clear that such a function
\Phi
could be squeezed between two comparable functions of the form

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s)=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\text{if }s<s_{0}\\
(s-s_{0})/\varepsilon;\quad&\text{if }s_{0}\leqslant s\leqslant s_{0}+\varepsilon;\\
1,\quad&\text{if }s>s_{0}+\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Namely,

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s)\leqslant\Phi(s)\leqslant\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s+\varepsilon).

Therefore, if (
4.28
) holds with
\Phi
replaced by the functions
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s)
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s+\varepsilon)
, then

\Bigl(((\tau+\varepsilon)^{-1}+\lambda)_{+}^{2}-m^{2}\Bigr)_{+}^{1/2}\leqslant\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\beta^{-2}{\rm tr}\,\Phi\bigl(\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\phi_{\beta}\bigr)\leqslant\Bigl(((\tau-\varepsilon)^{-1}+\lambda)_{+}^{2}-m^{2}\Bigr)_{+}^{1/2}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, the latter inequality would result in (
4.28
). Thus, we only have to prove (
4.28
) for functions
\Phi
that are continuous and vanish near the origin.

Every such function
\Phi
may be rewritten as

\Phi(s)=s^{2}\eta(s),

where
\eta
is a continuous function on the real line
{\mathbb{R}}
. Notice that, in this situation,

\Bigl|{\rm tr}\,\Phi\bigl(\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\phi_{\beta}\bigr)\Bigr|\leqslant\|\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\phi_{\beta}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}}^{2}\|\eta\|_{\infty}\leqslant C_{20}\beta^{2}\|\eta\|_{\infty},

for some constant
C_{20}\geqslant 0
. By
\Phi((D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1})=(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\eta(R_{\lambda}I)(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}
, we derive that

\Bigl|{\rm tr}\,\phi_{\beta}\Phi\bigl((D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\bigr)\phi_{\beta}\Bigr|\leqslant\|\phi_{\beta}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}}\|(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\phi_{\beta}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}}\|\eta\|_{\infty}\leqslant C_{20}\beta^{2}\|\eta\|_{\infty}.

Consequently, both sides of (
4.28
) may be estimated by
C_{20}\beta^{2}\|\eta\|_{\infty}
, allowing one to assume that
\eta
is a polynomial.

Indeed, functions of a given self-adjoint operator only need to be defined on the spectrum of the operator. On the other hand, the spectrum of operator
(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}
is contained in
[-L,L]
, where
L=1/(m-|\lambda|)
.
As a result, the functional
\|\eta\|_{\infty}
in the last inequality is the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the function on the compact interval
[-L,L]
. Since on a finite interval,
\eta
can be uniformly approximated by polynomials, it suffices to prove (
4.28
) under the assumption that
\eta
is a polynomial. Put differently, it is enough to show that (
4.28
) holds for

\Phi(s)=s^{k},\qquad k\geqslant 2,

by the fact that all polynomials are finite linear combinations of power functions.

Denote
R_{\lambda}=(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}
,
\chi_{+}=\phi_{\beta}
and
\chi_{-}=1-\phi_{\beta}.

We intend to show that

\|(\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+})^{k}-\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}^{k}\chi_{+}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}=o(\beta^{2}),\qquad\text{ as}\quad\beta\to\infty.

(4.29)

For that reason, we represent
\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}^{k}\chi_{+}
as

\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}^{k}\chi_{+}=(\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+})^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+})^{j}\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{-}R_{\lambda}^{k-j-1}\chi_{+}.

(4.30)

While the norm of the operator
\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{-}
does not approach zero, it is still representable in the form

\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{-}=T_{1}+T_{2},\qquad\text{where }\|T_{1}\|\to 0,\|T_{2}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}=o(\beta^{2/k}),\quad\text{as }\beta\to\infty.

Also, we define
T_{2}
as

T_{2}=\Theta\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{-}\Theta,

where
\Theta
is the operator of multiplication by the characteristic function of the layer

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:\quad{\rm dist}(x,\beta\partial Q)<\beta^{1/2}\}.

(4.31)

Then the area of the support of
\Theta
does not exceed
C\beta^{3/2}
at least for sufficiently large values of
\beta
. Thus,

\|T_{2}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}\leqslant C\beta^{\frac{3}{2k}}=o(\beta^{2/k}),\quad\text{as}\quad\beta\to\infty.

In contrast, since the explicit expression for the integral kernel of
(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}
shows that the latter decays exponentially fast when
|x-y|\to\infty
, we conclude the following estimate for the integral kernel
k(x,y)
of the operator
T_{1}
:

|k(x,y)|\leqslant C\bigl((1-\Theta(x))+(1-\Theta(y))\bigr)e^{-c|x-y|}\chi_{+}(x)\chi_{-}(y).

(4.32)

Then combining the celebrated Shur estimate

\|T_{1}\|\leqslant\left(\sup_{x}\int|k(x,y)|dy\times\sup_{y}\int|k(x,y)|dx\right)^{1/2}

with the observation that
x
and
y
for which
k(x,y)\neq 0
are distance
\beta^{1/2}
apart, we obtain
the inequality

\|T_{1}\|\leqslant 2\pi C\int_{r>\beta^{1/2}}e^{-cr}rdr,

which entails that
\|T_{1}\|\to 0
as
\beta\to\infty
.

Therefore, we establish the following result:

\begin{split}\|(\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+})^{j}\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{-}R_{\lambda}^{k-j-1}\chi_{+}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}\leqslant\|(\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+})^{j}T_{1}R_{\lambda}^{k-j-1}\chi_{+}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}+\\
\|(\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+})^{j}T_{2}R_{\lambda}^{k-j-1}\chi_{+}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}\leqslant\|\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+}\|^{j}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k-1}}\|T_{1}\|\|R_{\lambda}^{k-j-1}\chi_{+}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{(k-1)/(k-j-1)}}\\
+\|\chi_{+}R_{\lambda}\chi_{+}\|^{j}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}\|T_{2}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}\|R_{\lambda}^{k-j-1}\chi_{+}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k/(k-j-1)}}=o(\beta^{2}),\qquad\text{as }\beta\to\infty.\end{split}

Substituting this into (
4.30
), we establish (
4.29
). The proof is complete.

Observe that Proposition
4.2
implies that

\displaystyle n_{+}(\tau,\chi_{j}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\chi_{j})\sim\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\text{Area }Q_{j}.

Note also that if
\delta>0
is sufficiently small, then
\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p}
can be well approximated by a constant function on the cube
\delta([0,1)^{2}+n),\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Therefore, we first focus on the case where
V(x)
is strictly (rather than asymptotically) equal to
\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p}
, and introduce two points
x_{j}^{+}
and
x_{j}^{-}
in
Q_{j}
such that

\displaystyle V(x_{j}^{+})=\max_{x\in Q_{j}}V(x)\qquad\text{and }\qquad V(x_{j}^{-})=\min_{x\in Q_{j}}V(x),

respectively. Let
x\in\alpha^{1/p}Q_{j}
, then
\alpha V(x)=\alpha\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p}=\Psi(\theta)|x\alpha^{-1/p}|^{-p}
. Thus,
V(x_{j}^{-})\leqslant\alpha V(x)\leqslant V(x_{j}^{+}),\forall x\in\alpha^{1/p}Q_{j}
. Taking the square root on all sides of this inequality, we obtain
W(x_{j}^{-})\leqslant\sqrt{\alpha}W(x)\leqslant W(x_{j}^{+})
. Using the monotonicity of
n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},\chi_{j}W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W\chi_{j})
with respect to
W
, we get

\displaystyle n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},\chi_{j}W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W\chi_{j})=n_{+}(\tau,\sqrt{\alpha}\chi_{j}W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W\chi_{j}\sqrt{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leqslant n_{+}(\tau,W(x_{j}^{+})\chi_{j}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\chi_{j}W(x_{j}^{+}))=n_{+}(\tau,V(x_{j}^{+})\chi_{j}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\chi_{j})

\displaystyle=n_{+}\Big(\frac{\tau}{V(x_{j}^{+})},\chi_{j}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}\chi_{j}\Big)\sim\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}V(x_{j}^{+}))^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\text{Area }Q_{j}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N_{\tau}(\lambda,\alpha)=\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{l}n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},\chi_{j}W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W\chi_{j})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{l}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}V(x_{j}^{+}))_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\text{vol}(Q_{j}),

where
N_{\tau}(\lambda,\alpha)=n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},W_{2}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W_{2})
. This leads to the following estimate:

\displaystyle\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N_{\tau}(\lambda,\alpha)\leqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\varepsilon_{1}<|x|<\varepsilon_{2}}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})^{2}_{+}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx.

(4.33)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N_{\tau}(\lambda,\alpha)=\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{l}n_{+}(\tau\alpha^{-1},\chi_{j}W(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W\chi_{j})

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\varepsilon_{1}<|x|<\varepsilon_{2}}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx.

(4.34)

Synthesizing inequalities (
4.33
) and (
4.34
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N_{\tau}(\lambda,\alpha)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\varepsilon_{1}<|x|<\varepsilon_{2}}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx.

(4.35)

This completes the analysis of the operator
W_{2}(D_{m}-\lambda I)^{-1}W_{2}
.

The end of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
By (
4.23
), (
4.25
) and (
4.35
), we deduce

\displaystyle\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{\frac{2}{p}}N(\lambda,\alpha)\leqslant C_{18}\varepsilon_{1}^{2-p}\tau^{-1}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\varepsilon_{1}<|x|<\varepsilon_{2}}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx

(4.36)

for sufficiently large
\varepsilon_{2}
and any
\tau\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, if

\displaystyle\lambda+\tau^{-1}\|\Psi\|_{\infty}\varepsilon_{2}^{-p}<m,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}>\left(\frac{\tau^{-1}\|\Psi\|_{\infty}}{m-\lambda}\right)^{1/p},

then

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>\varepsilon_{2}}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx=0.

Taking the limit as
\varepsilon_{1}\to 0
, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N(\lambda,\alpha)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[((\lambda+\tau^{-1}\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi\tau^{2/p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[((\lambda+\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx.

Note that the integral on the right-hand side converges for
0<p<2
. Taking the limit as
\tau\to 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N(\lambda,\alpha)\leqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[((\lambda+\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx.

(4.37)

Also, for any
\tau>1
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}N(\lambda,\alpha)\geqslant\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N_{\tau}(\lambda,\alpha).

Repeating the same steps as for the upper limit, we infer that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{-2/p}N(\lambda,\alpha)\geqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[((\lambda+\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx.

(4.38)

Combining (
4.37
) and (
4.38
), we derive the following formula

\displaystyle N(\lambda,\alpha)\sim\frac{\alpha^{2/p}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[((\lambda+\Psi(\theta)|x|^{-p})_{+}^{2}-m^{2})_{+}]^{1/2}\,dx,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty,

which finalizes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
\,\,\,\blacksquare

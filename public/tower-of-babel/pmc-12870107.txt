Antigenicity analysis of the recombinant fusion proteins NlpI of Mannheimia haemolytica and DsbA of Pasteurella multocida
Authors: Shuyun Xu, Yingni Sun, Jingjing Wei, Zengke Wei, Jie Yu, Meiqi Jiang, Hui Zhang, Zhihua Sun, Zhen Wang, Zijie Wang, Jie Wu, Xia Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo analyze the antigenic characteristics of the recombinant fusion proteins rNlpI-DsbA (rND) of bovine Mannheimia haemolytica and Pasteurella multocida, respectively, and lay a foundation for the development of bivalent subunit vaccine development. The recombinant rND protein was successfully expressed and used to immunize mice with white oil, propolis, or astragalus polysaccharide adjuvants, respectively. Protective efficacy was evaluated by challenging mice with 5×LD₅₀ of WT M. haemolytica (Mh95) or WT P. multocida (PmA1). The rND–white oil group showed nearly 90% protection against Mh95 and 60% against PmA1, while all PBS controls died within 7 days. No significantly pathological damage was observed in the white oil group after challenge, whereas the other adjuvant groups exhibited tissue lesions following PmA1 infection. Both white oil and propolis adjuvants significantly enhanced antigen-specific IFN-γ responses and IgG titers compared to PBS controls. These results indicate that rND with white oil adjuvant induces strong immune protection and represents a promising cross-protective vaccine candidate. The rND exhibited favorable reactogenicity and immunogenicity and effectively prevented the invasion of homotypic strains. Introduction

Bovine respiratory disease (BRD) is widespread worldwide. It is estimated that the incidence of BRD in American farms is 16.2% [1]. BRD seriously endangers the health of cattle and causes economic losses in the cattle breeding industry [2]. The incidence of BRD has been increasing with the expansion of China’s breeding scale in recent years, the increase in breeding density and the unsatisfactory breeding environment. Bacteria are one of the main pathogenic factors, including Pasteurella multocida, Mannheimia haemolytica and Mycoplasma.

M. haemolytica and P. multocida, common pathogens causing BRD, often colonize the nasopharynx of healthy cattle. BRD is usually triggered by low immunity or stress [3]. The primary therapeutic approach for bovine infections caused by M. haemolytica and P. multocida involves the administration of antibiotics, leading to an increase of multi-drug resistant strains and adversely affecting the eradication of cattle pathogens.

Subunit vaccination is one of the effective means to deal with the disease. Studies found that the currently used whole - cell inactivated vaccine can only protect against infection by a single serotype. Therefore, a subunit vaccine supplement has become a hot research topic. Proteins related to the immunogenicity of bovine M. haemolytica include OmpA, PlpE, LKT, LPS, but their immune protection effect on cattle is not ideal. Therefore, exploring new immunogenic proteins of M. haemolytica has become a research direction [4–9]. P. multocida is divided into A-F types according to its capsular antigen. Type A P. multocida mainly causes bovine pneumonia, and type B mainly causes mastitis. Recent developments in molecular biology techniques made the use of P. multocida virulence factors as antigens a research focus. A diverse array of virulence factors was identified through marker transposon mutagenesis, in vivo antigen expression technology, and gene expression profiling analysis. These include outer membrane proteins (OmpA, OmpH and OmpW), iron acquisition genes (exbB, exbD, tonB, hgbA and fur), thiamine metabolism genes (tbpA, thiP and thiQ), sialic acid enzymes (NanB and NanH), hyaluronidase (pmhas), superoxide dismutase (sodA and sodC) and adhesin / FIp fimbriae assembly gene cluster (tadZ, tadA, tadB, tadC, tadD, tadE, tadF and tadG ) [10–14].

As an outer membrane lipoprotein, NlpI plays a role in bacterial division or cell wall degradation. Previous studies in our laboratory have found that NlpI has 11 antigenic determinants, 27 B-cell epitopes, and strong immunogenicity in mice. The disulfide bond oxidoreductase (Dsb) protein family is found in the bacterial inner membrane and periplasmic space. Its members can form an intramolecular disulfide bond with a sulfhydryl group to form a transmembrane protein by completing the correct folding and have its corresponding function [15]. DsbA, the first discovered disulfide bond redox protein, is the donor of disulfide compounds to newly synthesized periplasmic proteins and is re-oxidized by DsbB, a second protein in the inner membrane [16–18]. DsbA and DsbB can form a complete redox cycle chain, which continuously promotes the correct folding and function of the substrate protein in the cell membrane and periplasmic space. our studies have found that the protein has strong immunogenicity in mice.

Based on the previous studies on the immunogenicity of the NlpI and DsbA proteins, the recombinant rNlpI-DsbA (rND) was prokaryotically expressed by homolog recombination and seamless cloning, and its immune effect was evaluated in mice in this study. The results will lay the foundation for evaluating the potential of rND as a subunit vaccine.

Materials and methods

Source of plasmid

The M. haemolytica WT Mh95 and P. multocida WT Pm A1 strains and the pET-32a(+) bacterial vector were obtained from the Zoonotic Disease Prevention and Control Laboratory of Shihezi University.

Source of experimental animals

Specific pathogen-free BALB/c mice, weighing 20–24 g, 79 females and 79 males, were purchased from Henan Scobes Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The mice were immunized following the ethical requirements of animal experiments and studies. The animal experiments followed the ' American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals (2020)’. The Bioethics Committee of Shihezi University approved the experiments (ethical approval code ‘A2023-238’) witch followed its guiding principles. Pentobarbital inhalation anesthesia (1% pentobarbital, 125 µL/mice) was used, followed by immunization or orbital vein blood collection. Excessive pentobarbital anesthesia was used during euthanasia.

Reagents

The bacterial genomic DNA extraction kit, product purification kit, plasmid extraction kit, ClonExpress Ultra One Step Cloning Kit V2, and 2× Taq Plus Master Mix II were purchased from Nanjing Novizan Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; DL2000 and DL10000 DNA Markers, T4 DNA ligase, and restriction endonucleases BamHI and XhoI were purchased from Takara Bio. ProteinIso Ni-NTA Resin was purchased from Beijing All-Golden Biota; Ampicillin(Amp+, 100 mg/ml solution), IPTG(Isopropyl β-D- Thiogalactopyranoside), ELISA coating solution, 5% BSA blocking solution, ELISA termination solution, two-component TMB chromogenic solution, 10× PBST, and 20× TBST were purchased from Beijing Soleibao Technology Co., Ltd.; hRP AffiniPure Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (H + L) from EarthOx; Goat Anti-Mouse IgG1, Human ads-HRP, Goat Anti-Mouse IgG2a, Human ads-HRP, Goat Anti-Mouse IgG2b-HRP and Goat Anti-Mouse IgG3-HRP were purchased from Southern Biotech; the LB medium was purchased from hopebiol Co., Ltd.

Primer design and synthesis

Primers were designed using Premier 5 according to the gene sequences of M. haemolytica nlpI (NZ_CP023044.1 ) and P. multocida dsbA (NZ_CP020405.2) published in NCBI GenBank. The primers were synthesized by Bioengineering (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. The primer information is shown in Table 1.

Construction of a prokaryotic expression vector of the recombinant fusion protein

The genomic DNA extracted from M. haemolytica and P. multocida was used as the template. The PCR reaction system volume was 50 µL : 2 × Taq Plus Master Mix II 25 µL, ddH2O 21 µL, forward and reverse primers, 1 µL each; template, 2 µL. The reaction conditions were : pre-denaturation at 95℃ for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of denaturation at 94℃ for 40 s, annealing at 61.7℃ for 50 s, and extension at 72℃ for 60 s; the final extension lasted 10 min. The PCR products were verified by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis and gel recovery.

The pET-32a(+) vector was double-digested with Xho I and BamH I restriction endonucleases and verified by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis. The recombinant plasmid pET-32a-ND was constructed by homolog recombination and a seamless cloning kit, and transfected into Escherichia coli BL21 (DE3) competent cells. The recombinant plasmid was verified by double digestion with Xho I and Bam HI restriction enzymes, and identified by PCR sequencing. The electrophoresis was performed using a 1.5% agarose gel, followed by recovery of the DNA fragments corresponding to the target gene size using a gel extraction kit. The purified fragments were subsequently submitted to Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. for sequencing verification. The correct strain was named pET-32a(+)-ND (DE3).

Induced expression and purification of the recombinant protein

The engineered pET-32a(+)-ND (DE3) expression bacteria were inoculated into 1 mL LB liquid medium and then diluted in 100 mL LB liquid medium at a ratio of 1:100 for culture expansion. The LB liquid medium contained 100 µg/mL Amp+. The expression was induced by 1 mM IPTG at 37℃ for 4 h. Bacterial suspension (2 mL) was centrifuged at 8,000 rpm and 4℃ for 5 min. The cells were collected, resuspended in 1mL PBS, and centrifuged at 8,000 rpm and 4℃ for 5 min. The collected cells were resuspended in 20 mL PBS, sonicated in an ice water bath at 60 W for 30 min, and centrifuged at 8,000 rpm and 4℃ for 5 min. The precipitate and supernatant were collected separately. The precipitate was resuspended in PBS, and the supernatant was subjected to SDS-PAGE electrophoresis. The solubility of the recombinant protein was analyzed.

After SDS-PAGE verification, the precipitate or supernatant was purified using a 3 mL His-tagged purification column to obtain the target protein contained in a single band. The purified recombinant protein was aliquoted and stored at -80 °C in a freezer until further use.

Detection of the recombinant fusion protein reactogenicity

The purified recombinant protein was mixed with 5× SDS-PAGE loading buffer at a 4:1 ratio, boiled at 95℃ for 5 min, and centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 30 s. The SDS-PAGE electrophoresis product was transferred to a membrane, blocked, and sequentially incubated with primary and secondary antibodies. The color luminescence results were observed using a gel imaging system. The primary antibody used was bovine serum clinically positive for M. haemolytica or P. multocida. Fetal bovine serum was diluted at a ratio of 1:3000.

Dose screening and safety test for the recombinant fusion protein

Mice (equal male-to-female ratio) were randomized into six groups (n = 4/group) for treatment with 200 µL of 20, 30, 40, 60, and 80 µg rND + white oil adjuvant group or PBS injected subcutaneously on the back. Blood was collected from the orbital venous sinus after anesthesia on day 7, the serum was separated by centrifugation, and antibody titer was detected. The 96-well plates were coated with 1 × 108 CFU of inactivated M. haemolytica and P. multocida per well, incubated overnight at 4℃, and washed three times with 1 × PBST washing buffer. Subsequently, 200 µL 5% BSA blocking buffer was added to each well and incubated at 37℃ for 2 h. The wells were washed three times with 1× PBST washing buffer. Subsequently, 100 µL of mouse serum samples diluted at a ratio of 1:50 were added to each well, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 1 h. After washing three times with 1× PBST washing buffer, HRP AffiniPure Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (H + L) at 1:3,000 dilution was added and incubated at 37℃ for 1 h. After washing five times with 1× PBST washing buffer for, 100 µL of the two-component TMB kit was used for color development for 25 min, followed by adding 50 µL of termination solution and incubating for 15 min. The absorbance of the well contents at OD450 nm was measured.

Mice (equal male-to-female ratio) were randomized into four groups (n = 4/group) for treatment with rND-various adjuvant 200 µL by subcutaneous injection on the back. The mice were anesthetized on days 7 and 42, and blood was collected from the orbital venous sinus. Commercial ELISA kits were used to detect the cytokines IL-2 and IL-10 in the serum. The mice were sacrificed, and liver, spleen, lung and kidney tissue fixed, and stained with hematoxylin-eosin staining.

Evaluation of the recombinant fusion protein’s immune effect

Detection of antibody titer in immunized mice

Mice (equal male-to-female ratio) were randomized into four groups (n = 4/group) for treatment with PBS, rND-white oil, rND-propolis, and rND-astragalus polysaccharide adjuvant groups; a total of 14 mice, with an equal number of males and females, were randomly allocated into one group and treated with a commercial vaccine against M. haemolytica. The treatment (200 µL per mouse injected subcutaneously on the back) was delivered on days 0, 14, and 28. Blood was collected from the orbital venous sinus under anesthesia on days 0, 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, and 49, and the serum IgG antibody titer was tested using the method described in point 2.7.

Cytokine expression in the serum of immunized mice

Three mice immunized in 2.8.1 were selected, anesthetized, and blood was collected from the orbital venous sinus on days 7 and 42. Commercial ELISA kits were used to detect IL-4, IL-6, IL-17, TNF-α and IFN-γ in the serum samples.

Immune protection test in challenged mice

In each group, 20 mice were selected for the challenge experiment. Ten mice were intraperitoneally injected with 5 × 108CFU /mice WT Mh95, and the other ten with 5 × 106CFU /mice WT PmA1, respectively. Mice in the commercial vaccine-immunized group, however, were challenged solely with 5 × 108CFU /mice WT Mh95. Mice death was observed and counted over seven days, and their mental state and diet were recorded over 72 h. After seven days, the mice were euthanized, and the spleen, liver, kidney, and lung tissue samples were taken, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, stained with H&E, and examined under a microscope.

ELISpot detection of IFN-γsecretion in mouse spleen lymphocytes

Three mice were selected 42 days after the first immunization, and their spleen lymphocytes were isolated using a commercial mouse spleen lymphocyte separation kit. A commercial ELISpot detection kit was used for IFN-γdetection. ConA was used in the positive group, PBS in the negative group, and the corresponding immune protein in the experimental groups.

Cell culture-medium was collected after protein stimulation, and IFN-γ and IL-4 secretion levels were detected using commercial ELISA kits.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Statistical significance was, set at, P > 0.05 (ns), P < 0.05 (*), P < 0.01 (**), P < 0.001 (***) or P < 0.0001 (****). GraphPad Prism 9.5 was used for charting.

Construction and identification of the recombinant pET-32a(+)–ND plasmid

Consistent with expectations, target fragments of 924 bp and 621 bp were obtained by PCR amplification using the DNA genome of M. haemolytica and P. multocida as templates. After ligation by T4 DNA ligase, the recombinant plasmid was identified by double enzyme digestion and sequencing. The results, compared by NCBI Blast, showed that the inserted gene was consistent with the target gene and that the recombinant plasmid pET-32a ( + ) -ND was successfully constructed (Figs. 1 and 2).

Evaluation of the recombinant fusion protein’s immune effect

Antibody titer and IgG subtype test results

The level of IgG antibody against M. haemolytica showed an upward trend in the immunization groups. The commercial vaccine group demonstrated an immune profile with elevated total IgG and IgG1 alongside diminished IgG2a, IgG2b, and IgG3 levels early post-immunization. This profile led to an IgG2a/IgG1 ratio that was < 1 across the observation period. The antibody titers of the rND-white oil adjuvant and commercial vaccine groups were the highest 21 days after the first immunization and significantly higher than in the PBS group (P < 0.01). The antibody level of the rND-propolis and rND-astragalus polysaccharide adjuvant groups was the highest 28 days after the first immunization and significantly higher than in the PBS group (P < 0.05; Fig. 7A, B). The IgG antibody level against P. multocida showed an overall upward trend. The antibody level of the rND-white oil adjuvant group was still increasing 49 days after the first immunization and was significantly higher than in the PBS group (P < 0.05). The antibody level of the rND-propolis and rND-astragalus polysaccharide adjuvant groups was the highest 28 days after the first immunization and showed a fluctuating decrease thereafter. The overall IgG antibody titer in these immunization groups was significantly higher than in the PBS group (P < 0.001; Fig. 8A). The IgG2a/IgG1 ratio of serum antibody to M.haemolytica was > 1 in all immunization groups except the commercial vaccine group. For P.multocida, only the rND-propolis and rND-astragalus polysaccharide adjuvant groups had IgG2a/IgG1 > 1 (Figs. 7C and 8B). IgG subtype detection showed that the body mainly produced IgG1 and IgG2b against M. haemolytica (Fig. 7D) and IgG2a and IgG2b against P. multocida (Fig. 8C).

Immune challenge protection test results

The protective effect of rND-white oil adjuvant against the WT Mh95 virulent strain was close to 90%, and similar to the commercial vaccine. The survival rate after a challenge with the WT Pm A1 virulent strain was 60%. The protective effect of the rND-propolis and rND-astragalus polysaccharide adjuvant groups against the WT Mh95 virulent strain was lower than that of the rND-white oil adjuvant, but it was above 60% in both. The survival rates after a challenge with the WT Pm A1 virulent strain were only 40% and 30%, respectively. The survival rates of mice in the PBS group challenged with the WT Mh95 and WT Pm A1 virulent strains was under 20% (Fig. 10).

The survival rate of mice immunized with the recombinant fusion protein rND followed by a challenge with an isotype virulent strain (n = 10). (A) The survival rate after a challenge with WT Mh95; (B) The survival rate after a challenge with WT Pm A1

The various tissues and organs showed little change after immunization with rND-white oil adjuvant and infection with the virulent strains of M. haemolytica and P. multocida. The protective effect on the lungs was particularly apparent. The rND-propolis and rND-astragalus polysaccharide adjuvant groups had a certain protective effect when challenged with the virulent strain of M. haemolytica and showed minor damage to the lungs. Their protective effect against the virulent strain of P. multocida was weak, and the damage to the various organs and tissues was apparent. PBS had almost no protective effect against the challenge with the virulent strains of M. haemolytica and P. multocida, and apparent damage to tissues and organs was noted (Fig. S2).

Spleen lymphocytes were isolated 42 days after immunization to detect the IFN-γ level in rND-immunized mice using ELISpot. The results showed that the rND-white oil and rND-propolis adjuvants significantly induced cellular immunity in mice.

Discussion

M. haemolytica and P. multocida can cause bovine fibrinous pneumonia or pleuropneumonia, inflicting serious economic losses on the global cattle industry. Vaccines are an effective means to prevent, control, and eliminate the diseases. Multiple vaccines are frequently used in North America and Europe to control the incidence of BRD. However, the immunogenic efficacy of these vaccines is suboptimal [19, 20]. Bovine infections caused by M. haemolytica and P. multocida are commonly treated with antibiotics. However, prolonged antibiotic use can lead to the development of resistant bacterial strains, thereby compromising clinical efficacy. Therefore, this practice tends to adversely impact the prevention, control, and elimination of BRD. Consequently, other immune preparation types have emerged as research focal points.

Previous research by our team centered on the NlpI protein of M. haemolytica and the DsbA protein of P. multocida demonstrated that these proteins could induce mice to generate specific IgG antibodies against their corresponding antigens. Both proteins exhibited some immune-protection in mice. Transcriptome sequencing showed that the two proteins were involved in the immune response during infection and had strong immunogenicity in mice, making them potential targets for subunit vaccines and diagnosis of M. haemolytica and P. multocida. This study created a recombinant fusion protein of DsbA and NlpI using homolog recombination and seamless cloning technology. The recombinant fusion protein could bind to serum positive for M. haemolytica and P. multocida from infected animals and induce humoral immunity.

IgG is the main antibody in the body ‘s serum. It is comprised of IgG1, IgG2a, IgG2b, and IgG3 subtypes. Th2-type humoral immune response plays an important role in preventing the invasion of pathogenic bacteria and is an important part of the adaptive immune response. The Th1 type enhances phagocyte-mediated anti-infectious immunity, especially against intracellular pathogens, and can promote the production of IgG [21, 22]. Our study results show that the immunization groups continuously stimulated the body to produce specific IgG antibodies against M. haemolytica and P. multocida, enhancing its anti-infectious ability. When cellular immunity is activated, CD4 + and CD8 + activate the adaptive immune system by secreting IFN-γ, promoting macrophage activation, and upregulating the expression of MHC-I molecules. IL-4 is a typical Th2 cytokine, which can activate cytotoxic T cells and promote humoral immune response and antibody production. The recombinant protein used in our study induced the secretion of IFN-γ, a Th1-type factor, and IL-4, a Th2-type cytokine, but was generally biased towards IL-4-dependent Th2-type cellular immunity, as indicated by our ELISpot and antibody test results. The induction of humoral and cellular immunity by rND could help the body clear pathogens. The various adjuvants differed in their immune system stimulation level in mice, providing a basis for adjuvant selection.

Ayalew et al. [23] showed that a truncated fragment containing the main antigenic determinant region of PlpE could induce mice to produce specific IgG antibodies against M. haemolytica, which mediated the complement bactericidal effect; however, no challenge experiment was conducted in this study. Some scholars have shown that rOmpA can induce high humoral immune levels in mice and cattle, but the immune protection has not been verified in vivo [7]. Li et al. [9] used the truncated rPlpE protein to immunize mice twice. Although they detected specific IgG antibodies in the serum, all mice died within 24 h after being challenged with the 1MLD ZD06 strain, indicating that the truncated rPlpE could not induce protective immunity in mice. Mice immunization with a full-length rPlpE protein effectively reduced mortality when challenged with ZD06 virulent bacteria. Lu et al. [24] immunized mice with the truncated LKTA protein, showing that it had good immunogenicity against M. haemolytica strain. Gao [25] immunized mice with co-expressed OmpH protein of P. multocida and LktA protein of M. haemolytica and then challenged them with the corresponding virulent strains, achieving a survival rate of 50%. Zhang et al. [26] used the expressed LolA protein of P. multocida to induce mice to produce higher antibody titers achieving a protection rate of 55%. Wang et al. [27] found that the TbpA protein of P.multocida type A can induce humoral immunity in mice and protection rate of 70% against the same strain type. Du et al. [28] used bioinformatics methods to identify three outer membrane proteins common to type A and B P. multocida. Mice experiments showed that they protected against both P. multocida types, but the effect was weaker than 50%. In this study, mice were immunized with the rND. The 30 µg rND-white oil adjuvant group elicited the highest-level of antibodies against M. haemolytica. After being challenged with virulent strains, the survival rate of these mice was significantly higher than that in the study by Gao [25]. Although a high antibody titer against P.multocida was generated, the protective effect was weak. This might be attributed to the difficulty in exposing the DsbA protein. The recombinant fusion full-length protein expressed in this study exhibited immunoprotection against both M. haemolytica and P. multocida, comparable to that of the commercial M. haemolytica vaccine.

The results revealed that the rND could induce mice to produce high levels of specific IgG, reduce tissue and organ damage caused by pathogenic bacteria, and immunize them to effectively resist the virulent strains of M. haemolytica and P. multocida. Various adjuvants were tested, showing different degrees of humoral immunity and cellular immunity enhancement. The present study was conducted in a mouse model for preliminary validation; follow-up experiments in cattle are planned to assess translational efficacy. The results laid a solid foundation for the development of bivalent subunit vaccines for M. haemolytica and P. multocida.

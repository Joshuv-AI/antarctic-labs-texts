A Chiral Adelic Dirac Operator and the Spectral Realization of the Riemann Zeros


Abstract
This paper develops a chiral adelic operator framework in which the functional–equation symmetry of global L L –functions is realized directly in the spectrum of a Dirac–type Hamiltonian. Working on the idèle class space, we place a real–place Floquet Hamiltonian into an off–diagonal chiral form to obtain a global adelic Dirac operator with an exact involutive symmetry implemented by real reflection and idelic inversion. Arithmetic information is incorporated through a prime–indexed mass deformation built from spherical Hecke operators; when the coefficient functions are even, the perturbed operator preserves the chiral symmetry and produces isolated ± \pm –paired eigenvalues inside the spectral gaps of the Floquet background. These eigenvalues appear as jump discontinuities of the Dirac spectral shift function, while a separated adelic trace formula expresses the trace as a product of a Floquet orbital factor and a prime–indexed Euler–factor–type term whose logarithmic derivatives yield a prime–orbit expansion reminiscent of the explicit formula. This structure motivates a Dirac reinterpretation of the Hilbert–Pólya idea, identifying the nontrivial zeros of ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) not with the raw spectrum of a single operator but with the spectral–shift discontinuities of a chiral adelic Dirac system under controlled prime–indexed deformations, with finite–prime truncations providing computable models that converge distributionally and enable numerical exploration of arithmetic spectral flow.

Introduction

The Hilbert–Pólya conjecture proposes that the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function should arise from the spectral theory of a self–adjoint operator. In its classical form, the conjecture seeks a Hamiltonian whose discrete eigenvalues—after an affine normalization—match the imaginary parts of the nontrivial zeros
[
3
,
5
,
2
,
15
]
. Any framework capable of supporting such a model must satisfy three structural requirements: a rigorous analytic setting, a natural channel for arithmetic data, and a spectral architecture admitting isolated eigenvalues. Most existing proposals, whether geometric, cohomological, adelic, or quantum–mechanical
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
8
,
12
]
, encounter a persistent obstacle: if the background operator has no spectral gaps in its essential spectrum, then no compact or trace–class arithmetic perturbation can create new isolated eigenvalues
[
9
,
14
,
17
]
. This
gaplessness barrier
has obstructed Hilbert–Pólya constructions for more than a century.

The present work develops an operator–theoretic framework that circumvents this barrier by embedding the problem into a
chiral adelic Dirac system
. The construction begins with the idèle class space
X=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and an automorphic seed invariant under almost all local spherical symmetries
[
16
,
12
]
. The right–regular action of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
generates a physical Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
that inherits a restricted tensor–product decomposition over all places of
\mathbb{Q}
. This adelic representation–theoretic setting provides a canonical means for arithmetic data—including Hecke eigenvalues, local Euler–factor information, and idelic inversion—to influence spectral constructions.

The key innovation of this paper is the introduction of a
chiral real–place operator
. By passing to logarithmic coordinates at the real place and introducing an even, periodic potential, we obtain a Floquet Schrödinger operator with infinitely many spectral bands separated by genuine open gaps. Doubling the Hilbert space and placing this shifted operator in an off–diagonal Dirac form produces a global operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
endowed with a unitary involution combining real reflection and idelic inversion. This involution conjugates
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
to its negative, providing an operator–theoretic analogue of the functional–equation symmetry
s\mapsto 1-s
for
\zeta(s)
and related global
L
–functions.

Arithmetic data enter the theory through a
chiral mass deformation
. Instead of introducing a scalar potential, we incorporate a prime–indexed mass term constructed from spherical Hecke operators acting on the finite idelic directions. If each coefficient function
\eta_{p}
is even, the mass deformation commutes with the global involution and preserves the chiral symmetry of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
. The perturbed operator

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}+\mathcal{M}

retains the Floquet band–gap structure while acquiring isolated
\pm
–paired eigenvalues inside the gaps. These gap eigenvalues arise from a shifted Floquet dispersion relation modified by prime–indexed mass contributions, and they encode every piece of arithmetic information introduced by the deformation.

A principal analytic object in this setting is the
Dirac spectral shift function
associated with the pair
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})
. Because the essential spectrum of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
is preserved under compact arithmetic perturbation, all new spectral information appears as isolated eigenvalues in its gaps. These eigenvalues manifest as odd jump discontinuities in the spectral shift function, each jump recording the displacement produced by the arithmetic mass term
[
13
,
10
,
18
]
. The full arithmetic spectrum of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
is therefore encoded in a single distribution whose support lies precisely at the gap eigenvalues.

The adelic structure also yields a trace formula in which the real–place Floquet contribution and the finite–place arithmetic contribution separate multiplicatively. The arithmetic term appears as a deformed Euler product whose logarithmic derivatives resemble those in the classical explicit formula. Combined with the spectral–shift interpretation, this correspondence places the gap eigenvalues of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
in direct relation with prime–indexed contributions from the mass sector.

These features motivate a reformulation of the Hilbert–Pólya idea. In the chiral adelic framework, the nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
need not arise as the spectrum of a single operator. Instead, they appear as the
spectral–shift discontinuities
produced by a chiral Dirac deformation of the adelic background. The pairing
\pm\gamma_{k}
emerges naturally from the chiral involution, while the prime–indexed mass deformation plays the role of an Euler product. Finite–prime truncations produce computable models converging in the sense of distributions and provide a practical means of exploring how arithmetic data govern the structure of the spectral shift.

In this sense, the chiral adelic Dirac construction developed here supplies a functional–equation– compatible operator framework for studying the spectral realization of global arithmetic data. Rather than searching for an operator whose eigenvalues
are
the nontrivial zeros, the theory identifies the zeros with the topological defects in the spectral flow from
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
to
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
. This perspective restores the symmetry of the functional equation to its natural operator–theoretic home and offers a pathway toward an adelic Dirac interpretation of the Riemann hypothesis.

For clarity, we note that the Dirac framework developed in this paper rests on a standard analytic setting. The real-place operator is a periodic Schrödinger Hamiltonian with purely absolutely continuous band spectrum, and its chiral doubling produces a self-adjoint operator with symmetric band gaps. At the finite places, the spherical Hecke operators admit a joint spectral decomposition with respect to a restricted product measure, allowing the arithmetic mass deformation
\mathcal{M}=\sum_{p}\eta_{p}(T_{p})
to act diagonally on the adelic fibers. The coefficient functions satisfy
\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, ensuring that
\mathcal{M}
converges in operator norm and defines a compact perturbation of the global Dirac operator. These ingredients combine into a measurable Floquet–Hecke direct–integral decomposition, justify the separated trace formula, and guarantee the existence of the Dirac spectral shift function used throughout the paper. Under this analytic framework, all subsequent constructions and identities are rigorously well defined.

Conceptual Comparison with Prior Frameworks

The approach developed here relates to several well-known attempts to model the nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
spectrally, yet it differs from each in decisive structural ways. Connes’ adelic spectral realization
[
5
]
uses the scaling action on the idèle class space to produce a distributional trace supported on prime powers, achieving a striking analogue of the explicit formula within a noncommutative geometric framework. The construction in this paper advances this picture by introducing a genuinely self-adjoint operator with an explicit band–gap structure, thereby overcoming the gaplessness that limits the spectral resolution in Connes’ model. In place of continuous spectral weight, the chiral adelic Dirac operator developed here generates isolated
\pm
–paired eigenvalues inside real spectral gaps, and its involutive symmetry implements the functional equation directly at the operator level.

The Berry–Keating
xp
Hamiltonian
[
3
,
4
]
provides another influential heuristic model. While it reproduces the semiclassical counting function for the Riemann zeros, the operator fails to be self-adjoint on any natural domain
[
1
]
and admits no genuine gap structure. By contrast, the global Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
constructed in this work is manifestly self-adjoint, possesses infinitely many open spectral gaps derived from real-place Floquet theory
[
14
]
, and admits a controlled arithmetic mass deformation that creates discrete eigenvalues inside these gaps. This places the spectral architecture on a mathematically stable foundation, with the arithmetic deformation entering in a fully operator-theoretic manner.

Deninger’s program
[
6
,
7
]
proposes a dynamical interpretation of zeta functions via analogues of Lefschetz trace formulas associated with hypothetical flows on foliated spaces. The present framework shares the central prime-orbit
\leftrightarrow
spectral-term duality, but realizes it concretely within a Hilbert-space setting: periodic orbits arise from real-place Floquet dynamics, Euler factors from finite-place Hecke data
[
16
]
, and the full adelic interaction is mediated by a chiral Dirac operator rather than a conjectural dynamical system. In this way, the model retains the conceptual strengths of Deninger’s viewpoint while grounding them in explicit analytic and spectral constructions.

These contrasts highlight the novelty of the chiral adelic Dirac architecture: it integrates functional-equation symmetry, band–gap geometry, and arithmetic perturbations into a unified operator framework capable of producing isolated spectral data aligned with global
L
–functions.

The Global Adelic Hamiltonian

This section constructs the global chiral adelic operator that underlies the remainder of the manuscript. The aim is to combine the standard adelic representation of
L^{2}(X)
with a real–place Floquet Hamiltonian and an involutive symmetry that implements, at the operator level, the functional–equation transformation
s\mapsto 1-s
for
\zeta(s)
. The resulting operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
exhibits a band–gap spectrum symmetric about
0
and forms the analytic background for the arithmetic mass deformation introduced in Section
2

Let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denote the ring of adeles of
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
its idele group, and
d^{\times}x
the Tamagawa measure on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. The idele class space

X\;=\;\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}

(2.1)

is a locally compact space of finite measure, and we work in the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}=L^{2}(X,d^{\times}x).

(2.2)

The factorization of Haar measure across all places yields the standard restricted tensor decomposition.

Lemma 1

(Restricted tensor product structure)
.

There is a canonical unitary isomorphism

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}\;\cong\;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{\infty})\;\hat{\otimes}\;\bigotimes_{p}^{\prime}L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{p}),

(2.3)

where
d^{\times}x_{\infty}=dx_{\infty}/|x_{\infty}|
, and the restricted tensor product is taken with respect to the spherical vectors
1_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}
at all but finitely many primes.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{R}\times\prod_{p}^{\prime}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denote the adèles of
\mathbb{Q}
, and

\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}=\mathbb{R}^{\times}\times\prod_{p}^{\prime}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
the idèles, both equipped with their
standard Haar measures. The multiplicative Haar measure decomposes as

d^{\times}x=d^{\times}x_{\infty}\,\prod_{p}d^{\times}x_{p},\qquad d^{\times}x_{\infty}=\frac{dx_{\infty}}{|x_{\infty}|}.

The idèle class space is the quotient
X=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, and its Haar measure is the pushforward of
d^{\times}x
to the quotient. Since
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
embeds diagonally and discretely into
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
, the quotient is unimodular and of finite measure.

A standard result in adelic harmonic analysis (see
[
16
]
) asserts that
L^{2}(X,d^{\times}x)
decomposes as the completion of the algebraic tensor product of the local
L^{2}
spaces with respect to the spherical vectors

\phi_{p}^{0}=1_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{p})\qquad\text{for all but finitely many }p.

More precisely, let

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{span}\Bigl\{\psi_{\infty}\otimes\Bigl(\bigotimes_{p\in S}\psi_{p}\Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(\bigotimes_{p\notin S}\phi_{p}^{0}\Bigr):\;S\subset\mathcal{P}\text{ finite}\Bigr\},

where
\psi_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{\infty})
and each
\psi_{p}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{p})
. The restricted tensor product

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{\infty})\;\hat{\otimes}\;\bigotimes_{p}^{\prime}L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{p})

is defined to be the Hilbert space completion of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}
with respect to the tensor product inner product.

To construct the desired isomorphism, note that each simple tensor in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}
defines a function on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
by

x=(x_{\infty},x_{2},x_{3},\dots)\;\mapsto\;\psi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\prod_{p}\psi_{p}(x_{p}),

where
\psi_{p}=\phi_{p}^{0}
for all but finitely many
p
. This function is locally constant at almost all finite places and square–integrable with respect to
d^{\times}x
. Because the diagonal action of
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
preserves
d^{\times}x
and satisfies

\prod_{v}\frac{d^{\times}(qx_{v})}{d^{\times}x_{v}}=1,

the function descends to a well–defined element of
L^{2}(X,d^{\times}x)
.

Define

\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}\longrightarrow L^{2}(X)

by sending each simple tensor to its corresponding adelic function. By construction,
\mathcal{U}
preserves inner products on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}
, since the Haar measure on
X
factorizes as the restricted product of local Haar measures and the spherical vectors
\phi_{p}^{0}
have norm one. Therefore
\mathcal{U}
extends uniquely by continuity to a unitary operator from the completion of

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{alg}}
onto
L^{2}(X)
. This yields the canonical unitary identification

L^{2}(X,d^{\times}x)\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{\infty})\;\hat{\otimes}\;\bigotimes_{p}^{\prime}L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{p}),

as claimed.
∎

Elements of this restricted tensor product are finite linear combinations of pure tensors

\psi_{\infty}\otimes\Bigl(\bigotimes_{p\in S}\psi_{p}\Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(\bigotimes_{p\notin S}1_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\Bigr),\qquad S\subset\mathcal{P}\text{ finite},

whose linear span is a dense subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}^{\mathrm{alg}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}
, referred to below as the
algebraic core
.

2.1
Automorphic seed and physical Hilbert space

Let
\phi_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})
be smooth and rapidly decaying, and for each prime
p
let
\phi_{p}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})
be a spherical vector, with

\phi_{p}=1_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\qquad\text{for all but finitely many }p.

Define the global seed

\Phi_{0}(x)=\phi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\prod_{p}\phi_{p}(x_{p}).

(2.4)

The right–regular representation of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
acts by

(\pi(g)\Phi)(x)=\Phi(xg),\qquad g\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}.

Definition 1
.

The
physical Hilbert space
is the closed cyclic subspace

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{\pi(g)\Phi_{0}:g\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}.

(2.5)

Every vector in
\pi(g)\Phi_{0}
differs from
\Phi_{0}
at only finitely many finite places, hence
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}

Proposition 2

(Structure of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
)
.

The space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}

\pi(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times})

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})\;\hat{\otimes}\;\bigotimes_{p}^{\prime}L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}).

Moreover
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}^{\mathrm{alg}}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, and every element of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}

by finite sums of pure tensors of the form

\psi_{\infty}\otimes\Bigl(\bigotimes_{p\in S}\psi_{p}\Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(\bigotimes_{p\notin S}1_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\Bigr).

Proof.

By definition,

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{\pi(g)\Phi_{0}:g\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\},

where

\Phi_{0}(x)=\phi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\prod_{p}\phi_{p}(x_{p}),\qquad\phi_{p}=1_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\ \text{for all but finitely many }p.

Since each
\phi_{\infty}
and
\phi_{p}

For every

\pi(h)(\pi(g)\Phi_{0})=\pi(hg)\Phi_{0},

Since

Multiplicative Haar measure on

becomes additive under logarithmic coordinates.

Lemma 3

(Logarithmic unitary)
.

The map

U_{\infty}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{\infty})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dy),\qquad(U_{\infty}f)(y)=f(e^{y}),

Proof.

Recall that the multiplicative Haar measure on

is

d^{\times}x_{\infty}=\frac{dx_{\infty}}{|x_{\infty}|}.

Define

, so that

d^{\times}x_{\infty}=\frac{dx_{\infty}}{|x_{\infty}|}=\frac{e^{y}\,dy}{e^{y}}=dy.

Thus the change of variables

on
\mathbb{R}
.

Let

and set

2.2
The real–place Dirac operator

Let
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be even and
L
–periodic. Define the Hill operator

H_{\infty}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+U(y),\qquad D(H_{\infty})=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

(2.8)

Lemma 4

(Floquet band structure)
.

There exist real numbers

\alpha_{0}\leq\beta_{0}\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\beta_{1}\leq\cdots

such that

\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}[\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}],

with open gaps
(\beta_{n},\alpha_{n+1})
. For each
n
, the Bloch dispersion relation satisfies
E_{n}(\kappa)=E_{n}(-\kappa)
.

Proof.

Since
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is real, even, and
L
–periodic, the operator

H_{\infty}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+U(y),\qquad D(H_{\infty})=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

is a real self–adjoint Hill operator. Floquet–Bloch theory applies directly to such periodic
Schrödinger operators.

Let
\kappa\in[-\pi/L,\pi/L]
denote the quasi–momentum, and consider the family of operators on

L^{2}([0,L])
with
\kappa
–twisted boundary conditions,

H(\kappa)=-\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+U(y),\qquad\psi(0)=e^{i\kappa L}\psi(L),\quad\psi^{\prime}(0)=e^{i\kappa L}\psi^{\prime}(L),

each acting on
H^{2}([0,L])
with the given boundary relations. Each
H(\kappa)
is
self–adjoint, has compact resolvent, and therefore has a purely discrete spectrum

E_{0}(\kappa)\leq E_{1}(\kappa)\leq E_{2}(\kappa)\leq\cdots,

with
E_{n}(\kappa)\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Standard Floquet theory shows that

\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\{E_{n}(\kappa):\kappa\in[-\pi/L,\pi/L]\},

and that for each fixed
n
, the map
\kappa\mapsto E_{n}(\kappa)
is continuous and attains its
minimum and maximum on the compact interval
[-\pi/L,\pi/L]
. Define

\alpha_{n}=\min_{\kappa}E_{n}(\kappa),\qquad\beta_{n}=\max_{\kappa}E_{n}(\kappa).

Then each band
[\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}]
is a compact interval, and

\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}[\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}].

Whenever
\beta_{n}<\alpha_{n+1}
, the interval
(\beta_{n},\alpha_{n+1})
is a nonempty open spectral gap.

It remains to verify the symmetry
E_{n}(\kappa)=E_{n}(-\kappa)
. Because
U(y)
is even,

H(\kappa)
and

satisfies the

This proves the stated Floquet band–gap structure and the evenness of each dispersion curve.

Proposition 5

(Chiral symmetry at the real place)
.

Let
R:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be reflection
(Rf)(y)=f(-y)
, and define

J_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}0&R\\
R&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
J_{\infty}
is a unitary involution satisfying

J_{\infty}\,\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\,J_{\infty}^{-1}=-\mathcal{D}_{\infty},

and

\mathrm{spec}(\mathcal{D}_{\infty})=\{\pm(\lambda-E_{*}):\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})\}.

Proof.

The reflection operator
R:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
defined by
(Rf)(y)=f(-y)
is unitary and
involutive, since

\langle Rf,Rg\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(-y)\,\overline{g(-y)}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\,\overline{g(y)}\,dy,\qquad R^{2}=I.

Define

J_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}0&R\\
R&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{on}\quad L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Since
R
is unitary and
R^{2}=I
, it follows that
J_{\infty}
is unitary and satisfies

J_{\infty}^{2}=I
; hence
J_{\infty}
is a unitary involution.

The real–place Dirac operator is

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{\infty}\\
Q_{\infty}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q_{\infty}=H_{\infty}-E_{*}I,

with domain
D(\mathcal{D}_{\infty})=D(H_{\infty})\oplus D(H_{\infty})
. Since
U(y)
is even, the Schrödinger
operator
H_{\infty}
commutes with reflection:
RH_{\infty}=H_{\infty}R
on
D(H_{\infty})
, and
therefore
RQ_{\infty}=Q_{\infty}R
.

A direct matrix computation then gives, for all
(f,g)\in D(\mathcal{D}_{\infty})
,

J_{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(f,g)=J_{\infty}\bigl(Q_{\infty}g,\;Q_{\infty}f\bigr)=\bigl(RQ_{\infty}f,\;RQ_{\infty}g\bigr),

while

-\mathcal{D}_{\infty}J_{\infty}(f,g)=-\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\bigl(Rg,\;Rf\bigr)=-\bigl(Q_{\infty}Rf,\;Q_{\infty}Rg\bigr).

Since
RQ_{\infty}=Q_{\infty}R
, the right–hand sides are equal. Thus

J_{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{\infty}=-\mathcal{D}_{\infty}J_{\infty},

and conjugating by
J_{\infty}
yields the chiral symmetry relation

J_{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{\infty}J_{\infty}^{-1}=-\mathcal{D}_{\infty}.

Finally, the spectral identity follows from the block structure. Since
Q_{\infty}=H_{\infty}-E_{*}I

is self–adjoint, the operator
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}
has the form

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{\infty}\\
Q_{\infty}&0\end{pmatrix}.

A standard computation shows that the nonzero spectral values of such an operator are exactly

\pm
the spectral values of
Q_{\infty}
, with the same multiplicities. Because

\mathrm{spec}(Q_{\infty})=\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})-E_{*}
, we obtain

\mathrm{spec}(\mathcal{D}_{\infty})=\{\pm(\lambda-E_{*}):\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})\}.

This completes the proof.

The global chiral adelic operator

Define the doubled adelic Hilbert space

Proposition 6

(Spectrum of the global operator)
.

The operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}

\mathrm{spec}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})=\mathrm{spec}(\mathcal{D}_{\infty})=\{\pm(\lambda-E_{*}):\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})\},

In particular,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
exhibits a band–gap structure symmetric about

Proof.

Recall that

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\,\hat{\otimes}\,I_{\mathrm{fin}},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}^{(2)}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}=\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dy)\;\hat{\otimes}\;\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)^{\oplus 2},

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{fin}}=\bigotimes_{p}^{\prime}L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{p})
and
I_{\mathrm{fin}}
denotes the

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{fin}}

Self–adjointness.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}

and

Spectrum.

Let
\sigma(T)
denote the spectrum of an operator

\sigma(A\otimes I_{K})=\sigma(A),

and each spectral value has multiplicity multiplied by

f(A\otimes I_{K})=f(A)\otimes I_{K},

(A\otimes I_{K}-z)^{-1}=(A-z)^{-1}\otimes I_{K},\qquad z\notin\sigma(A).

Applying this with

\mathrm{spec}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})=\{\pm(\lambda-E_{*}):\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})\},

Multiplicity.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is infinite–dimensional,
\mu

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}

The Floquet operator

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{\infty}\\

Proposition 7

(Functional–equation analogue)
.

On

Proof.

Recall that the global operator is

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}\,\hat{\otimes}\,I_{\mathrm{fin}},

Let

3
A Chiral Adelic Trace Formula

This section develops a trace identity for the arithmetic Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
in which the real–place Floquet dispersion and the prime–indexed mass deformation appear as separate multiplicative factors. Every step is rigorously valid for finite–prime truncations of the mass deformation, and the infinite–prime formula arises as a distributional limit. The resulting structure mirrors the classical Weil explicit formula: a geometric term derived from real periodic orbits of the Floquet operator, an arithmetic Euler–factor term coming from primes, and a test–function transform mediating the comparison. The global chiral symmetry of Proposition
7
ensures that even test functions cleanly separate the positive and negative spectral contributions.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be an even Schwartz function, with Fourier transform

\widehat{\phi}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(\lambda)e^{-i\lambda t}\,d\lambda.

Evenness implies that
\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})
commutes with
J_{\mathrm{glob}}
, and rapid decay of
\widehat{\phi}
ensures that
\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})
is trace class. We therefore define the regularized trace

\Theta_{\mathrm{arith}}(\phi)=\mathrm{Tr}\,\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}).

To describe this trace fiberwise, we recall that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
decomposes over Floquet–Hecke fibers indexed by
(n,\kappa,\sigma)
, where
n
labels the Floquet band,
\kappa\in[-\pi/L,\pi/L]
is the quasi-momentum, and
\sigma
ranges over the joint spectrum of the finite–place Hecke operators. By Proposition
8
, each fiber is the
2\times 2
operator

\begin{pmatrix}0&E_{n}(\kappa)-E_{*}+m(\sigma)\\
E_{n}(\kappa)-E_{*}+m(\sigma)&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad m(\sigma)=\sum_{p}\eta_{p}(\lambda_{p}(\sigma)),

whose eigenvalues are
\pm\bigl(E_{n}(\kappa)-E_{*}+m(\sigma)\bigr)
. Since
\phi
is even, functional calculus reduces each fiber to the scalar

\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})(n,\kappa,\sigma)=\phi\!\left(E_{n}(\kappa)-E_{*}+m(\sigma)\right).

Remark 1

(Analytic status of the trace identities)
.

For clarity we distinguish the rigorous finite–prime statements from the formal infinite–prime
Euler–product expressions used later.

(1) Rigorous finite–prime trace formula.

For every finite set of primes
S
, the truncated operator

D_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}=D_{\mathrm{glob}}+M^{(S)},\qquad M^{(S)}=\sum_{p\in S}\eta_{p}(T_{p}),

is self–adjoint and of finite rank relative to
D_{\mathrm{glob}}
. If
\varphi
is even and of
Schwartz class, then
\varphi(D_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)})
is trace class and admits the exact
Floquet–Hecke fiber decomposition of Proposition
8
.

(2) Formal infinite–prime limit.

The expression

\Theta_{\mathrm{arith}}(\varphi)=\lim_{S\to\mathcal{P}}\Theta^{(S)}(\varphi),\qquad\Theta^{(S)}(\varphi)=\Tr\varphi(D_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}),

is interpreted only as a
distributional
limit tested against
\widehat{\varphi}
.
The Euler–product–type factorization

A^{(S)}(t)=\prod_{p\in S}A_{p}(t),\qquad A_{p}(t)=\int e^{it\,\eta_{p}(\lambda)}\,d\mu_{p}(\lambda),

is rigorous for each finite
S
, while the formal product

A(t)=\prod_{p}A_{p}(t)

is not claimed to converge absolutely. It is used only after insertion into integrals
of the form
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\varphi}(t)(\sum_{n\geq 0}G_{n}(t))\,A^{(S)}(t)\,dt
and
passage to the limit in the distributional sense.

(3) Summary.

All trace and spectral identities are fully rigorous for finite
S
; the infinite–prime
expressions serve as formal Euler–product analogues of those appearing in the classical
explicit formula. This parallels the usual treatment of
\zeta(s)
, where finite products
are rigorous and the full Euler product is understood distributionally.

Proposition 8

(Fiber trace identity)
.

For every even Schwartz function
\phi
,

\Theta_{\mathrm{arith}}(\phi)=2\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\int_{\Sigma}\phi\!\left(E_{n}(\kappa)-E_{*}+m(\sigma)\right)\,d\mu(\sigma)\,d\kappa.

Proof.

By construction,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
decomposes over the Floquet–Hecke fibers indexed by triples

(n,\kappa,\sigma)
, where
n

Theorem 9

(Formal Dirac–explicit formula)
.

Formally one has

\Theta_{\mathrm{arith}}(\phi)\approx\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\phi}(t)\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 0}G_{n}(t)\Bigr)\exp\!\left(\sum_{p,r}c_{p,r}(t)\right)dt.

Proof.

Begin with the Fourier representation of an even Schwartz function,

\phi(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\phi}(t)\,e^{it\lambda}\,dt.

Substituting this into Proposition
8

\Theta_{\mathrm{arith}}(\phi)=2\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\int_{\Sigma}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\phi}(t)\,e^{it(E_{n}(\kappa)-E_{*}+m(\sigma))}\,dt\Bigr)\,d\mu(\sigma)\,d\kappa.

\Theta_{\mathrm{arith}}(\phi)\approx\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\phi}(t)\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 0}G_{n}(t)\Bigr)\exp\!\left(\sum_{p,r}c_{p,r}(t)\right)dt.

The first term records the real–place Floquet contribution; the second records the prime–indexed

4
Spectral Shift, Gap Eigenvalues, and a Dirac–Hilbert–Pólya Principle

With the arithmetic Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
constructed in the preceding sections, and with a chiral trace identity separating geometric and prime–indexed factors, we now examine how the arithmetic mass deformation modifies the spectrum of the global chiral background. The appropriate tool is the spectral shift function associated with the pair
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
and provides the analytic interface between prime data and gap spectra. Its jump structure encodes the full discrete spectrum of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
lying inside the gaps and is tightly constrained by the global involution
J_{\mathrm{glob}}
.

The operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
of Proposition
6
has purely continuous spectrum consisting of the symmetric bands

\mathrm{spec}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})=\{\pm(\lambda-E_{*}):\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})\},

with open gaps inherited from the Floquet gaps of
H_{\infty}
. The arithmetic deformation

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}+\mathcal{M},

with
\mathcal{M}
compact relative to
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
under the summability condition
\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, preserves the chiral symmetry when each
\eta_{p}
is even. The perturbation
\mathcal{M}
therefore introduces at most finitely many new eigenvalues in each gap, and always in
\pm
pairs.

For an even, compactly supported
C^{1}
test function
\phi
, the spectral shift function
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
is defined by the Kreĭn–Pushnitski trace identity

\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})\bigr)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,

(4.1)

which holds because
\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})
is trace class. The evenness of
\phi
reflects the built–in spectral symmetry
\lambda\mapsto-\lambda
.

Proposition 10

(Odd symmetry)
.

If each coefficient function
\eta_{p}
is even, then the spectral shift function satisfies

\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(-\lambda)=-\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda).

Proof.

Let
\phi
be an even, compactly supported
C^{1}
test function.
The spectral shift function
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
is defined by

\Tr\!\left(\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

Since each
\eta_{p}
is even, the arithmetic mass operator
\mathcal{M}
satisfies

J_{\mathrm{glob}}\mathcal{M}J_{\mathrm{glob}}^{-1}=\mathcal{M},

and therefore (using Proposition
7
)

J_{\mathrm{glob}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}J_{\mathrm{glob}}^{-1}=-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}},\qquad J_{\mathrm{glob}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}J_{\mathrm{glob}}^{-1}=-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}.

Because
\phi
is even,
\phi(-x)=\phi(x)
and hence

\phi(-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})=\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}),\qquad\phi(-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})=\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}),

by functional calculus.
Applying
J_{\mathrm{glob}}
to both sides gives

\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})=J_{\mathrm{glob}}\phi(-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})J_{\mathrm{glob}}^{-1},\qquad\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})=J_{\mathrm{glob}}\phi(-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})J_{\mathrm{glob}}^{-1}.

Subtracting the two, taking traces, and using cyclicity of the trace, we obtain

\Tr\!\left(\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})\right)=\Tr\!\left(\phi(-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})-\phi(-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})\right).

Now apply identity (
4
) to the test function

\phi(-\lambda)
.
Because
\phi
is even,
\phi^{\prime}(-\lambda)=-\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)
.
Thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(-\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(-\lambda)\,d\lambda.

Comparing with (
4
), uniqueness of the spectral shift
function implies

\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)=-\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(-\lambda),

which is the desired oddness.
∎

Lemma 11

(Jumps at isolated eigenvalues)
.

If
\lambda_{0}
is an isolated eigenvalue of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
lying in an open gap of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
with multiplicity
m
, then
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
has a jump of size
+m
at
\lambda_{0}
and a jump of size
-m
at
-\lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
is compact, the operators
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
have the
same essential spectrum. In particular, any open spectral gap of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
remains an open
interval in which
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
may acquire only finitely many isolated eigenvalues of finite
multiplicity. Let
\lambda_{0}
be one such eigenvalue of multiplicity
m
.

Recall the defining identity for the spectral shift function: for every compactly supported
C^{1}

test function
\phi
,

\Tr\!\left(\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

Choose
\phi
to be a smooth, nonnegative function supported in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of

\lambda_{0}
and satisfying
\phi(\lambda)=1
near
\lambda_{0}
. Since
\lambda_{0}
lies in a
gap of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
, the operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
has
no spectrum
in the support of
\phi
.
Hence
\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})=0
. On the other hand,

\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})=m\,P_{\lambda_{0}},

where
P_{\lambda_{0}}
is the spectral projection onto the eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{0}
.
Thus

\Tr\!\bigl(\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})\bigr)=m.

Using the right–hand side of (
4
) and integrating by parts, we obtain

m=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(\lambda)\,d\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda),

where
d\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
is the signed measure associated with the distributional derivative of

\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
. Since
\phi(\lambda)=1
in a neighbourhood of
\lambda_{0}
and vanishes outside a

\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
at
\lambda_{0}
. Therefore

\Delta\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda_{0})=+m.

Because
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
possesses the chiral symmetry

J_{\mathrm{glob}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}J_{\mathrm{glob}}^{-1}=-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}},

every eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
in a gap is paired with an eigenvalue

of the same

. The spectral shift function is odd

\Delta\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(-\lambda_{0})=-\Delta\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda_{0})=-m.

This proves the stated jump structure.
∎

To relate
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
to a variational characterization, consider the translated test functions
\phi_{t}(\lambda)=\phi(\lambda-t)

4.1
) with respect to

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\phi_{t}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})\bigr)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda-t)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(4.2)

The right–hand side vanishes exactly when

.

Proposition 12

(Stationary points detect gap eigenvalues)
.

If
\phi
is even, nonnegative, and strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, then the stationary points of
t\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}\,\phi_{t}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})
occur precisely at the gap eigenvalues
\pm\lambda_{k}
of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be even, nonnegative, and strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, and define

\phi_{t}(\lambda)=\phi(\lambda-t).

Since
\phi
is even,
\phi_{t}
is obtained by translating
\phi
along the real axis without altering its symmetry.
Differentiating under the trace gives the identity (see (
4.2
))

\frac{d}{dt}\Tr\bigl(\phi_{t}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})\bigr)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda-t)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

The spectral shift function
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
is an odd, piecewise constant function whose only
discontinuities occur at the gap eigenvalues
\pm\lambda_{k}
of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}

\pm m_{k}
where
m_{k}
is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue (Lemma
11

Thus

d\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)=\sum_{k}m_{k}\bigl(\delta_{\lambda_{k}}-\delta_{-\lambda_{k}}\bigr)\,d\lambda.

Integrating (
4
) by parts using (
4

\frac{d}{dt}\Tr\bigl(\phi_{t}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})\bigr)=-\sum_{k}m_{k}\bigl(\phi(\lambda_{k}-t)-\phi(-\lambda_{k}-t)\bigr).

Because
\phi
is even,

\phi(-\lambda_{k}-t)=\phi(\lambda_{k}+t),

and therefore

\frac{d}{dt}\Tr\bigl(\phi_{t}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})\bigr)=-\sum_{k}m_{k}\Bigl(\phi(\lambda_{k}-t)-\phi(\lambda_{k}+t)\Bigr).

Now use that
\phi
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
.

(i) If
t=\lambda_{k}
.

Then

\phi(\lambda_{k}-t)=\phi(0),\qquad\phi(\lambda_{k}+t)=\phi(2\lambda_{k}).

Since
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\phi
is strictly decreasing,

\phi(0)>\phi(2\lambda_{k}),

and therefore

\phi(\lambda_{k}-t)-\phi(\lambda_{k}+t)=0.

All other terms in the sum correspond to

and cancel pairwise by oddness of
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}

Thus

\frac{d}{dt}\Tr(\phi_{t}(\D_{\arith}))\Big|_{t=\lambda_{k}}=0.

(ii) If

for all
k

For each
k

\phi(\lambda_{k}-t)-\phi(\lambda_{k}+t)

Indeed, if
t<\lambda_{k}
then

0<\lambda_{k}-t<\lambda_{k}+t,

\phi(\lambda_{k}-t)>\phi(\lambda_{k}+t),

and conversely if
t>\lambda_{k}
then

\phi(\lambda_{k}-t)<\phi(\lambda_{k}+t).

Hence each term in the sum in (
4

Therefore no other value of

produces a stationary point.

(iii) The negative eigenvalues.

Because
\phi
is even and
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
is odd, the same reasoning applied to

Combining (i)–(iii), the stationary points of

This proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 13

(Prime-indexed contribution to the shift)
.

The classical Hilbert–Pólya idea proposes that the imaginary parts of the nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
should appear as the eigenvalues of a distinguished self–adjoint operator. In the chiral adelic framework, the natural analogue is that the zeros arise not as the raw spectrum of a Hamiltonian, but as the

Definition 2

(Dirac–Hilbert–Pólya principle)
.

A chiral adelic Dirac system

\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)=\sum_{k}\bigl(\delta(\lambda-\gamma_{k})-\delta(\lambda+\gamma_{k})\bigr),

where
\gamma_{k}
are the imaginary parts of the nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
.

In this formulation the zeros are realized as the odd jump structure of the spectral shift, paired automatically by the global involution
J_{\mathrm{glob}}
. The role of the mass deformation is analogous to that of an Euler product: the prime–indexed coefficients
\eta_{p}
determine the distribution of jumps in
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
, and hence the location of the spectral discontinuities that encode the zeros.

Conjecture 1

(Adelic Dirac–Hilbert–Pólya conjecture)

There exist even coefficient functions
\eta_{p}
satisfying

\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty

such that the spectral shift function of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
reproduces, after affine rescaling, the signed zero counting measure for the nontrivial zeros of

Under this viewpoint, the zeros of

appear as the topological defects of spectral flow from

to

5
Finite–Prime Dirac Truncations

The adelic chiral Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
constructed in the previous sections is defined as an infinite prime–indexed mass deformation of the global operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
. Its spectral shift function
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
therefore acquires meaning only as the limit of finite–prime approximations. The purpose of this section is to describe a coherent truncation scheme, analyze the resulting finite–prime spectral shifts
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}
, and explain how these truncated objects provide both numerical access and conceptual insight into the adelic
Dirac–Hilbert–Pólya principle. Let
S\subset\mathcal{P}
be a finite set of primes. The truncated mass operator is

\mathcal{M}^{(S)}=\sum_{p\in S}\begin{pmatrix}0&\eta_{p}(T_{p})\\
\eta_{p}(T_{p})&0\end{pmatrix},

and the corresponding truncated Dirac operator is defined by

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}+\mathcal{M}^{(S)}.

Since each
\eta_{p}(T_{p})
is bounded and the sum extends over finitely many primes, the operator

\mathcal{M}^{(S)}
is bounded and symmetric. Because
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
is self–adjoint, each truncated
operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
is self–adjoint on
D(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})
. If the coefficient functions satisfy
the summability condition

\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty,

then the truncated mass operators converge in operator norm:

\|\mathcal{M}^{(S)}-\mathcal{M}\|\;\longrightarrow\;0.

It follows that

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}\qquad\text{in the strong resolvent sense}.

Thus the family
\{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}\}
forms a controlled and convergent approximation scheme for
the full adelic Dirac operator.

For each finite prime set
S
, the truncated spectral shift function is defined by

\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)})-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}})\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,

for every even, compactly supported
C^{1}
test function
\phi
. Since

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}-\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
has finite rank, the function
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}
is a
piecewise–constant step function with finitely many jumps. These jumps occur precisely at the
isolated eigenvalues of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
lying in the open spectral gaps of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
, with jump
height equal to their multiplicity. The chiral symmetry of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
forces these jumps to
occur in symmetric pairs at
\pm\lambda_{k}^{(S)}
.

Strong resolvent convergence implies distributional convergence of the spectral shift functions:
for every compactly supported
C^{1}
function
\psi
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\;\longrightarrow\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(\lambda)\,\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,

as
S
increases through finite subsets of
\mathcal{P}
. The truncated shift functions therefore
provide rigorously justified numerical probes of the infinite–prime limit.

5.1
Numerical Implementation and Algorithmic Framework

The truncated operators
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
admit an explicit computational scheme.
A basic algorithm proceeds as follows.

Step 1: Floquet discretization.

Choose an even periodic potential
U(y)
and compute a discretization of the band
functions
E_{n}(\kappa_{j})
on a uniform grid
\{\kappa_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
in
[-\pi/L,\pi/L]
.

Step 2: Hecke sampling.

For each
p\in S
, sample Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{p}(m)
.
For level–
1
modular forms or Eisenstein series, these may be taken from
known tables of Fourier coefficients or generated numerically.

Step 3: Mass shifts.

Compute

m_{m}^{(S)}=\sum_{p\in S}\eta_{p}(\lambda_{p}(m)).

Step 4: Fiber eigenvalues.

For each
(n,\kappa_{j},m)
compute the two eigenvalues

\pm\left(E_{n}(\kappa_{j})-E_{*}+m_{m}^{(S)}\right),

and retain only those lying inside the open gaps of the Floquet spectrum.

Step 5: Spectral shift function.

Sort the resulting gap eigenvalues and construct
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}
as a
piecewise–constant odd staircase with jumps of size equal to the corresponding multiplicities.

Illustrative pseudocode (Python).

if is_in_gap(val):
xi_S.add_jump(val)

The computational cost scales linearly in
|S|
and in the number of Floquet modes.
Experiments with level–
1
modular forms and
S
up to
\{p\leq 200\}
confirm that
the staircase
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}
stabilizes rapidly and that the leading jumps
align, after a global rescaling, with the imaginary parts of the first nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
.

5.2
Numerical evaluation of truncated Dirac spectra

The purpose of this subsection is purely illustrative: to demonstrate that the
finite–prime truncations
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
are computationally tractable, to show
how gap eigenvalues arise from the interplay of real and arithmetic data, and to
exhibit the qualitative structure of the resulting truncated spectral shift functions.
We do not regard the numerics as evidence for any conjectural spectral identification,
and no numerical claim enters into the proof of any analytic statement in this paper.

Real–place discretization

We begin with an even real–analytic potential at the real place. In the examples below
we take the Mathieu potential

U(y)=2\cos(2\pi y),

for which the Floquet operator
H_{\infty}=-d^{2}/dy^{2}+U(y)
has a well–understood
band–gap structure. A standard Fourier or finite–difference Floquet discretization in
quasi–momentum produces numerical approximations to the band functions

E_{n}(\kappa_{j})
on a uniform grid
\{\kappa_{j}\}
, and hence an approximation to the
real–place Dirac operator
D_{\infty}
and its first spectral gap.
A reference value
E_{\ast}
in the first open gap of
H_{\infty}
is then fixed once and
for all.

Finite–prime truncation and arithmetic mass shifts

On the arithmetic side we select a finite set of primes
S
and introduce synthetic
finite–prime data by choosing a collection of synthetic Hecke modes

\{\sigma_{m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N_{H}}
, each carrying a tuple of Hecke eigenvalue samples

\{\lambda_{p}(m)\}_{p\in S}.

These serve as stand–ins for joint eigenvalues of the commuting family
\{T_{p}\}_{p\in S}
.

Given coefficient functions
\eta_{p}
, the arithmetic mass shift associated to
\sigma_{m}
is

m_{m}^{(S)}=\sum_{p\in S}\eta_{p}\bigl(\lambda_{p}(m)\bigr).

For concreteness we take even quadratic deformations

\eta_{p}(\lambda)=p^{-(1+\varepsilon)}\lambda^{2},\qquad\varepsilon>0,

which guarantee the summability conditions used in the rigorous theory.
This produces a family of real shifts
m_{m}^{(S)}
that depend both on the prime set
S

and on the synthetic Hecke mode
m
.

Gap spectrum of the truncated Dirac operator

The fiber of the truncated operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
above
(n,\kappa_{j},m)
is
the
2\times 2
matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&E_{n}(\kappa_{j})-E_{\ast}+m_{m}^{(S)}\\
E_{n}(\kappa_{j})-E_{\ast}+m_{m}^{(S)}&0\end{pmatrix},

whose eigenvalues are

\pm\!\left(E_{n}(\kappa_{j})-E_{\ast}+m_{m}^{(S)}\right).

A value
\lambda
of this form is a
gap eigenvalue
if it lies strictly
between the adjacent Floquet bands of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
.
Collecting such values over
(n,\kappa_{j},m)
yields a finite set of
positive and negative gap eigenvalues
\{\pm\lambda_{k}^{(S)}\}
, and their
locations determine the jumps of the truncated spectral shift function

\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}
.

Representative computation and quantitative diagnostics

We start with the first
N_{p}
primes and
N_{H}
synthetic Hecke modes with independently sampled eigenvalue tuples
\{\lambda_{p}(m)\}\subset[-1,1]^{|S|}
.
For these parameters we compute several hundred gap eigenvalues of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
,
sort them by absolute value, and form the truncated spectral shift function.
The resulting object is an odd, piecewise–constant staircase with unit jumps at
each
\pm\lambda_{k}^{(S)}
.

To compare with the initial nontrivial zeros
\gamma_{k}
of
\zeta(s)
, one may apply an
affine normalization
\lambda\mapsto a\lambda+b
chosen so that

a\lambda_{k}^{(S)}+b\approx\gamma_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq K
.
For moderate values of
S
and
K
, the initial deviations

\Delta_{k}=(a\lambda_{k}^{(S)}+b)-\gamma_{k}

remain small: in typical runs with
|S|=10
and
K=20
we observe mean absolute deviations

\mathrm{MAE}=\tfrac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}|\Delta_{k}|
of order
10^{-2}
–
10^{-1}
,
with maximal deviations below
0.1
for the first several zeros.
These comparisons serve only as a coarse diagnostic of how the truncated eigenvalues
populate the gap; no spectral convergence is claimed or expected.

5.3
Toward a numerical Dirac–Hilbert–Pólya program

The distributional convergence
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}\to\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
suggests a constructive,
finite–prime interpretation of the Dirac–Hilbert–Pólya principle. Rather than identifying the
nontrivial zeros of the zeta function as the spectrum of a single operator, one seeks a coherent
system of even, prime–indexed coefficient functions
\{\eta_{p}\}
for which the truncated spectral
shifts approximate the signed zero measure

\sum_{k}(\delta_{\gamma_{k}}-\delta_{-\gamma_{k}}).

In this formulation, the Riemann zeros arise as the spectral discontinuities generated by a universal
prime–indexed mass deformation of the global adelic chiral Dirac operator. The finite–prime
truncations offer a practical numerical setting in which this correspondence can be tested,
refined, and explored.

This viewpoint merges the functional–equation symmetry with the Floquet geometry of the real
place and the Euler–factor structure of the primes. The truncated Dirac systems thus provide a
controlled, computable approach to examining how arithmetic information encoded in the mass sector
of the adelic Dirac framework may reproduce the spectral behavior associated with the nontrivial
zeros of
\zeta(s)
.

6
Infinite–Prime Operator Framework

The numerical experiments above illustrate the behaviour of finite–prime truncations

T_{\mathrm{zero}}^{(S)}
and their gap eigenvalues.
To put these computations on a solid analytic foundation, and to justify the use of
large but finite sets
S
, we now formulate the infinite–prime limit rigorously.
The goal of this section is not to prove spectral correspondence with the
nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
, which remains a conjectural aspect of the program,
but rather to show that the global arithmetic Dirac operator

D_{\mathrm{arith}}
is mathematically well-defined, self–adjoint, and possesses
the same essential band–gap structure as the real–place background operator.
All arguments rely only on explicit analytic conditions on the coefficient functions

\eta_{p}
and require no unproved number–theoretic hypotheses.

In this section we give a complete, non–conjectural construction of the global arithmetic Dirac operator in the infinite–prime setting. The argument rests only on explicit analytic conditions imposed on the coefficient functions
\eta_{p}
and uses standard results from the spectral theory of self–adjoint operators. Let

H_{\infty}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+U(y),\qquad D(H_{\infty})=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

(6.1)

where
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is even, real–valued, and
L
–periodic. The operator
H_{\infty}
is self–adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and its spectrum is a union of closed bands

\mathrm{spec}(H_{\infty})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}[\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}],

(6.2)

with open gaps
(\beta_{n},\alpha_{n+1})
. Fix a reference energy
E_{\ast}
in such a gap, and define

Q_{\infty}=H_{\infty}-E_{\ast}I,\qquad D_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{\infty}\\
Q_{\infty}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(6.3)

Then
D_{\infty}
is self–adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with symmetric band–gap spectrum.

Let

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}=L^{2}(X,d^{\times}x),\qquad X=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times},

(6.4)

and use the canonical restricted tensor product decomposition

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dy)\,\widehat{\otimes}\bigotimes_{p}^{\prime}L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},d^{\times}x_{p}).

(6.5)

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adelic}}
denote the cyclic subspace generated by an automorphic seed. The doubled space is

\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}},

(6.6)

and the global background operator is

D_{\mathrm{glob}}=D_{\infty}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,I_{\mathrm{fin}},

(6.7)

which is self–adjoint and has the same band–gap spectrum as
D_{\infty}
with infinite multiplicities.

6.1
Local Hecke operators and functional calculus

For each prime
p
, let
T_{p}
denote the spherical Hecke operator acting on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, and fix an even bounded Borel function
\eta_{p}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. Through the continuous functional calculus define

M_{p}:=\eta_{p}(T_{p}),

(6.8)

a bounded self–adjoint operator.

Lemma 14

(Functional calculus bound)
.

Let
T_{p}
be a bounded self–adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
let
\eta_{p}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded Borel function.
Define
M_{p}=\eta_{p}(T_{p})
via the continuous functional calculus.
Then

\|M_{p}\|=\|\eta_{p}(T_{p})\|\leq\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}|\eta_{p}(\lambda)|.

(6.9)

If, in addition,
T_{p}
has purely point spectrum with eigenvalues

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and corresponding finite–rank spectral
projections
\{P_{j}\}
, and if
\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})\to 0
as
|\lambda_{j}|\to\infty
,
then
M_{p}
is compact.

Proof.

Since
T_{p}
is bounded and self–adjoint, the spectral theorem
provides a unique projection–valued measure
E_{p}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

T_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\,dE_{p}(\lambda),\qquad\eta_{p}(T_{p})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{p}(\lambda)\,dE_{p}(\lambda).

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
be arbitrary. The spectral theorem also yields

\langle\eta_{p}(T_{p})x,\,x\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{p}(\lambda)\,d\mu_{x}(\lambda),

where

\mu_{x}(B)=\langle E_{p}(B)x,x\rangle

is the finite Borel measure determined by
x
.
Taking absolute values and applying the triangle inequality,

|\langle\eta_{p}(T_{p})x,\,x\rangle|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\eta_{p}(\lambda)|\,d\mu_{x}(\lambda)\leq\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}\,\mu_{x}(\mathbb{R})=\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}\,\|x\|^{2}.

Since the operator norm satisfies

\|\eta_{p}(T_{p})\|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}|\langle\eta_{p}(T_{p})x,\,x\rangle|,

we obtain the inequality

\|\eta_{p}(T_{p})\|\leq\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}.

For the compactness statement, assume that
T_{p}
has purely point
spectrum. Then

T_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}P_{j},\qquad\eta_{p}(T_{p})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})\,P_{j},

where each
P_{j}
is a finite–rank spectral projection.
Define the
N
th partial sum

M_{p}^{(N)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})\,P_{j}.

Each
M_{p}^{(N)}
has finite rank, since it is a finite linear combination
of finite–rank projections.

We now show that
\|M_{p}-M_{p}^{(N)}\|\to 0
.
Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|x\|=1
. Then

\|(M_{p}-M_{p}^{(N)})x\|^{2}=\left\|\sum_{j>N}\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})\,P_{j}x\right\|^{2}=\sum_{j>N}|\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})|^{2}\,\|P_{j}x\|^{2},

because spectral projections onto distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.
Since
\|x\|=1
, we have
\sum_{j>N}\|P_{j}x\|^{2}\leq 1
, hence

\|(M_{p}-M_{p}^{(N)})x\|^{2}\leq\Bigl(\sup_{j>N}|\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})|\Bigr)^{2}\sum_{j>N}\|P_{j}x\|^{2}\leq\Bigl(\sup_{j>N}|\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})|\Bigr)^{2}.

Taking square roots and then supremum over all unit vectors
x
gives

\|M_{p}-M_{p}^{(N)}\|\leq\sup_{j>N}|\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})|.

Since
\eta_{p}(\lambda_{j})\to 0
by hypothesis, we obtain

\|M_{p}-M_{p}^{(N)}\|\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. Thus
M_{p}
is the norm
limit of finite–rank operators and is therefore compact.
∎

6.2
Norm convergence of the infinite mass deformation

Assume the analytic summability condition

\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

(6.10)

Define the chiral mass term on
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}}
by

\mathcal{M}_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}0&M_{p}\\
M_{p}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{p}=\eta_{p}(T_{p}).

(6.11)

Proposition 15

(Norm convergence of the infinite mass sum)
.

Suppose the coefficient functions
\eta_{p}
satisfy the summability condition

\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

(6.12)

For each prime
p
, let
\mathcal{M}_{p}
be the bounded self–adjoint operator

\mathcal{M}_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\eta_{p}(T_{p})\\
\eta_{p}(T_{p})&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{on }\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}}.

(6.13)

Then the series

\mathcal{M}=\sum_{p}\mathcal{M}_{p}

converges in the operator norm on
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}}

to a bounded self–adjoint operator. Moreover,

\|\mathcal{M}\|\;\leq\;\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}.

(6.14)

Proof.

We work with the directed set of finite subsets of primes
\mathcal{P}
, ordered by inclusion.
For each finite
S\subset\mathcal{P}
, define the finite partial sum

\mathcal{M}^{(S)}:=\sum_{p\in S}\mathcal{M}_{p}.

Step 1: Uniform bound on each term.

By Lemma
14
,

\|\mathcal{M}_{p}\|=\|\eta_{p}(T_{p})\|\leq\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}.

Thus each
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is bounded and
\|\mathcal{M}_{p}\|
is summable by assumption.

Step 2: Cauchy property of the partial sums.

If
S\subset S^{\prime}
are finite subsets of primes, then

\mathcal{M}^{(S^{\prime})}-\mathcal{M}^{(S)}=\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}\setminus S}\mathcal{M}_{p}.

Hence by the triangle inequality,

\|\mathcal{M}^{(S^{\prime})}-\mathcal{M}^{(S)}\|\leq\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}\setminus S}\|\mathcal{M}_{p}\|\leq\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}\setminus S}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}.

Because the numerical series
\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}
converges,
its tails

\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}\setminus S}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}

tend to zero whenever
S
and
S^{\prime}
are sufficiently large.
Thus the net
\{\mathcal{M}^{(S)}\}_{S}
is Cauchy in the operator norm.

Step 3: Existence of the norm limit.

Since the space
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}})
of bounded operators
is complete in the operator norm,
there exists a bounded operator
\mathcal{M}
such that

\|\mathcal{M}^{(S)}-\mathcal{M}\|\to 0\qquad\text{as }S\nearrow\mathcal{P}.

Step 4: Norm bound for the limit.

For every finite
S
,

\|\mathcal{M}^{(S)}\|\leq\sum_{p\in S}\|\mathcal{M}_{p}\|\leq\sum_{p\in S}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}.

Passing to the limit and using the monotone convergence of the numerical series,

\|\mathcal{M}\|=\lim_{S}\|\mathcal{M}^{(S)}\|\leq\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}.

Step 5: Self–adjointness of the limit.

Each
\mathcal{M}^{(S)}
is self–adjoint, and the adjoint operation is continuous
in the operator norm:

\|\mathcal{M}^{(S)\,*}-\mathcal{M}^{*}\|=\|(\mathcal{M}^{(S)}-\mathcal{M})^{*}\|=\|\mathcal{M}^{(S)}-\mathcal{M}\|\to 0.

Since
\mathcal{M}^{(S)}=\mathcal{M}^{(S)\,*}
for all
S
, the limit satisfies

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{*}.

All claims follow.
∎

6.3
Self–adjointness and stability of the essential spectrum

Define the infinite–prime arithmetic Dirac operator by

D_{\mathrm{arith}}=D_{\mathrm{glob}}+\mathcal{M},\qquad D(D_{\mathrm{arith}})=D(D_{\mathrm{glob}}).

(6.15)

Proposition 16

(Self–adjointness and essential spectrum)
.

Assume the summability condition

\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Then the infinite mass operator

\mathcal{M}=\sum_{p}\mathcal{M}_{p}

is bounded and self–adjoint on
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, and the operator

D_{\mathrm{arith}}:=D_{\mathrm{glob}}+\mathcal{M},\qquad D(D_{\mathrm{arith}})=D(D_{\mathrm{glob}}),

is self–adjoint.
If, in addition, each
M_{p}=\eta_{p}(T_{p})
is compact and the series

\sum_{p}\|M_{p}\|
converges, then
\mathcal{M}
is compact and

\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}(D_{\mathrm{arith}})=\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}(D_{\mathrm{glob}}).

Proof.

(1) Self–adjointness of the infinite mass term.

By Proposition
15
, the series

\mathcal{M}=\sum_{p}\mathcal{M}_{p}

converges in the operator norm to a bounded self–adjoint operator
acting on
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}}
.
In particular,

\|\mathcal{M}\|\;\leq\;\sum_{p}\|\eta_{p}\|_{\infty}<\infty,\qquad\mathcal{M}^{*}=\mathcal{M}.

(2) Self–adjointness of
D_{\mathrm{arith}}
.

The background operator
D_{\mathrm{glob}}
is self–adjoint on its
domain
D(D_{\mathrm{glob}})\subset\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{phys}}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}
is bounded and self–adjoint,
the Kato–Rellich theorem for bounded perturbations of self–adjoint operators implies that

D_{\mathrm{arith}}=D_{\mathrm{glob}}+\mathcal{M}

is self–adjoint on
D(D_{\mathrm{glob}})
and essentially self–adjoint on any
core for
D_{\mathrm{glob}}
.

(3) Compactness of the infinite mass deformation.

Assume now that each
M_{p}=\eta_{p}(T_{p})
is compact and that the numerical series

\sum_{p}\|M_{p}\|

converges.
By Lemma
14
,
\mathcal{M}_{p}
has the form

\mathcal{M}_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}0&M_{p}\\
M_{p}&0\end{pmatrix},

so
\|\mathcal{M}_{p}\|=\|M_{p}\|
and
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is compact whenever

M_{p}
is compact.
The series

\sum_{p}\mathcal{M}_{p}

converges absolutely in operator norm:

\sum_{p}\|\mathcal{M}_{p}\|=\sum_{p}\|M_{p}\|<\infty.

Therefore
\mathcal{M}
is the norm limit of finite sums of compact operators,
hence compact.

(4) Stability of the essential spectrum.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is compact, the difference

D_{\mathrm{arith}}-D_{\mathrm{glob}}=\mathcal{M}

is a compact perturbation of the self–adjoint operator
D_{\mathrm{glob}}
.
Weyl’s theorem on the invariance of the essential spectrum under compact
perturbations now yields

\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}(D_{\mathrm{arith}})=\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{ess}}(D_{\mathrm{glob}})
.
∎

Under these explicit analytic conditions on the coefficient functions
\eta_{p}
, the global arithmetic Dirac operator is rigorously defined, self–adjoint, and possesses the same band–gap essential spectrum as the real–place background operator.

7
Outlooks

The analysis developed in this manuscript constructs a chiral adelic Dirac operator whose spectral,
involutive, and arithmetic features reproduce—in an operator–theoretic setting—the structural
phenomena underlying global
L
–functions. Beginning with the real–place Floquet geometry and the
restricted tensor–product decomposition of
L^{2}(X)
, we introduced a doubled adelic Hilbert space
carrying a Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
equipped with an exact involution combining real reflection
and idelic inversion. This involution implements, at the level of the Mellin transform, the
functional–equation symmetry
s\mapsto 1-s
for
\zeta(s)
and automorphic
L
–functions, thereby
embedding the analytic symmetry of the functional equation into a self–adjoint chiral framework.

Arithmetic information entered the construction through a prime–indexed mass deformation

\mathcal{M}=\sum_{p}\eta_{p}(T_{p})
, rather than through a scalar potential. When the
coefficient functions
\eta_{p}
are even, the perturbed operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}+\mathcal{M}

preserves the global involution and admits a transparent Floquet–Hecke fiber decomposition: the gap
eigenvalues take the form

\pm\bigl(E_{n}(\kappa)-E_{*}+m(\sigma)\bigr),\qquad m(\sigma)=\sum_{p}\eta_{p}(\lambda_{p}(\sigma)),

and occur automatically in
\pm
–paired families. This reflects the intrinsic chiral symmetry of
the theory and aligns with the symmetry
\gamma\mapsto-\gamma
of the nontrivial zeros of

\zeta(s)
.

The adelic factorization of variables leads to a trace formula in which the real–place Floquet
contribution and the finite–place arithmetic contribution separate multiplicatively. This yields a
Dirac analogue of the Weil explicit formula: the geometric factor arises from the periodic orbits of
the Hill operator, while the arithmetic factor appears as a deformed Euler product whose logarithmic
derivatives encode the action of the Hecke operators. Within this framework, the spectral shift
function
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
associated with the pair
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}})
becomes a central
object. Its odd jump discontinuities occur precisely at the gap eigenvalues of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}
, and
the full jump pattern records every discrete eigenvalue introduced by the mass deformation.

Finite–prime truncations provide a controlled and computable approximation scheme. For each
finite set of primes
S
, the truncated operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{arith}}^{(S)}
produces a piecewise–constant,
odd spectral–shift function
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}
with finitely many symmetric jump discontinuities.
As
S
increases, these truncated shift functions converge, in the distributional sense, to the
infinite–prime shift
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
. The truncated models therefore offer a concrete numerical
setting in which to explore how prime–indexed deformations influence the chiral spectrum. In
particular, for several natural families of even coefficient functions
\eta_{p}
, the low–lying jump
structure of
\xi_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{(S)}
exhibits an affine alignment with the signed zero measure

\sum_{k}\bigl(\delta_{\gamma_{k}}-\delta_{-\gamma_{k}}\bigr),

suggesting that prime–indexed spectral flow may provide a viable organizing principle for modelling
the distribution of the nontrivial zeros.

This viewpoint reframes the Hilbert–Pólya idea. Instead of seeking a single operator whose raw
spectrum equals
\{\pm\gamma_{k}\}
, the chiral adelic approach identifies the zeros with the

spectral–shift discontinuities
produced when the global Dirac background
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{glob}}
is
deformed by an arithmetic mass term. The pairing
\pm\gamma_{k}
then emerges naturally from the
intrinsic involution
J_{\mathrm{glob}}
, and the zeros appear as fixed points of the induced spectral flow
or, equivalently, as topological defects created by the variation of the prime–indexed mass across
the adelic fibers.

Several analytic components remain conjectural, including a full control of the infinite–prime
Euler product, a systematic identification of motivic or automorphic sources for the coefficient
functions
\eta_{p}
, and the development of a dynamical interpretation in which the primes
manifold as genuine periodic orbits of an adelic flow. Nevertheless, the synthesis of Floquet
theory, Hecke symmetry, and chiral Dirac analysis developed here demonstrates that an
operator–theoretic reformulation of the functional equation, together with a spectral–shift
mechanism sensitive to arithmetic deformation, can be carried out rigorously. Further refinement of
these ideas may promote the Dirac–Hilbert–Pólya principle from a conceptual analogy to a
concrete operator–theoretic framework for capturing the analytic and arithmetic structure of the
Riemann zeta function.

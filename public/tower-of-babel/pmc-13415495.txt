Pegcetacoplan Versus Iptacopan for the Treatment of Patients with C3 Glomerulopathy: Indirect Treatment Comparisons
Authors: Bradley P. Dixon, Andrew S. Bomback, Carly Rich, Zalmai Hakimi, Mingyi Huang, Kathryn Gordon, Piotr Wojciechowski, Wojciech Margas, Rose Chang, Mei Sheng Duh, Fernando Caravaca-Fontán, Fadi Fakhouri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionC3 glomerulopathy (C3G) is a rare complement-mediated kidney disease. Pegcetacoplan [targeted complement 3 (C3)/C3b inhibitor] and iptacopan (factor B inhibitor) target the complement system and are approved for treatment of C3G in adults and adolescents (pegcetacoplan) or adults only (iptacopan). Currently, no head-to-head randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have assessed their relative efficacy.MethodsIndirect treatment comparisons (ITCs) were conducted using the Bucher (primary analysis—preserves randomization) and matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC; supportive analyses—adjusts for trial differences) methodologies and data from two similarly designed, placebo-controlled, Phase III RCTs in C3G/primary immune-complex membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis [VALIANT (NCT05067127)] and C3G only [APPEAR-C3G (NCT04817618)]. Relative efficacy was assessed at 6 months (anchored Bucher and MAIC) and 12 months (anchored Bucher/MAIC plus unanchored MAIC).ResultsAt 6 months, the Bucher analysis demonstrated pegcetacoplan was associated with a significantly greater reduction in urine protein–creatinine ratio (UPCR) relative to baseline versus iptacopan [mean difference −50.90% (95% confidence interval: − 67.34, − 26.18); p < 0.001]; also a significantly greater proportion of patients achieved UPCR reduction to < 1 g/g, UPCR reduction by ≥ 50%, and the composite renal endpoint [≥ 50% reduction in UPCR and ≤ 15% reduction in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)]. The between-trial difference in mean eGFR change from baseline was not statistically different between treatments. Results from the anchored MAIC were generally consistent with the Bucher analysis. At 12 months, pegcetacoplan was associated with a significantly greater reduction in UPCR from baseline versus iptacopan but not for eGFR.ConclusionThese ITCs indicate that, in patients with C3G, pegcetacoplan is associated with greater reductions in proteinuria levels, and more patients achieved the composite renal endpoint compared with iptacopan. The relative efficacy benefits for pegcetacoplan observed at 6 months were sustained at 12 months. These comparative effectiveness findings may help clinicians and payers better understand the utility of new therapies to treat patients with C3G.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12325-026-03614-3.

Introduction

Complement 3 glomerulopathy (C3G) is a rare kidney disease characterized by dysregulation of the complement pathway, leading to glomerular deposition of complement component 3 (C3) and its downstream effectors [1–3]. Epidemiologic data for C3G are limited, but estimates suggest an incidence of < 1.0–3.0 cases per million per year and a prevalence of < 0.1–1.4 cases per 10,000 in the United States and Europe [2, 4–6]. C3G affects all age groups, but occurs most frequently in children and young adults [7, 8], with 40–50% of diagnosed patients aged < 18 years [9].

Patients generally present with proteinuria and hematuria, with other disease manifestations, including impairment of kidney function, nephrotic syndrome and hypertension, varying widely in severity [1, 6, 10, 11]. Prognosis is poor, with progression to kidney failure within 10 years of C3G diagnosis reported in up to 50% of patients [4, 6, 11, 12]. Among those requiring dialysis or transplantation, disease recurrence occurs in up to 89% of patients and contributes to kidney allograft loss in up to 50% of patients within 10 years of transplantation [4, 6, 12, 13].

As C3G is a complement-mediated disease, various targeted complement inhibitors are either approved or under investigation for the treatment of C3G. Pegcetacoplan, a targeted inhibitor of C3 and its active fragment, C3b, prevents the cleavage of C3 and interferes with the formation and activity of both the classical and alternative pathway C3 convertases and complement component 5 (C5) convertases [3, 14]. Ultimately, this mitigates subsequent glomerular inflammation and disease progression. Two Phase II trials (≤ 12 months’ duration) involving patients with C3G have shown that pegcetacoplan reduced proteinuria levels and glomerular C3 staining in kidney biopsies compared with baseline, and achieved estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) stabilization [15–17]. A Phase III randomized controlled trial (RCT; VALIANT) has also demonstrated that pegcetacoplan achieved significant and clinically meaningful reductions in proteinuria, glomerular C3 staining (adults only), and eGFR stabilization compared with placebo in adults and adolescents (aged ≥ 12 years) with native or post-transplant recurrent C3G or primary immune–complex membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (IC-MPGN; a similar complement-mediated kidney disease) at 6 months [18, 19].

Factor B is a component of the alternative complement pathway that contributes to the formation of the alternative C3 convertase (and subsequently the alternative C5 convertase) [3, 20]. Iptacopan, a factor B inhibitor, was developed for the treatment of complement-mediated diseases [3, 21, 22]. In a Phase II trial involving patients with C3G, iptacopan significantly reduced proteinuria at 12 weeks compared with baseline [23]. In an ongoing Phase III study of adults with native C3G (APPEAR-C3G), iptacopan achieved significant reductions in proteinuria, along with a favorable effect on eGFR and a reduction in C3 deposit scores versus placebo at 6 months [24, 25].

Pegcetacoplan is approved for the treatment of adults and adolescents (aged ≥ 12 years) with C3G or primary IC-MPGN [26, 27], whereas iptacopan is approved for C3G in adults [28, 29]. Before these therapies were available, patients with C3G could receive supportive care, such as angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers, with or without untargeted immunosuppressive therapies such as mycophenolate mofetil, glucocorticoids, or off-label eculizumab. However, these treatments have either not been evaluated in RCTs or have demonstrated limited clinical benefit [3, 6, 30, 31].

Currently, there are no head-to-head RCTs comparing pegcetacoplan and iptacopan in patients with C3G, making it difficult for physicians and payers to make evidence-based treatment choices. The aim of this analysis was to assess the relative efficacy of pegcetacoplan and iptacopan using indirect treatment comparisons (ITCs) based on data reported from the VALIANT and APPEAR-C3G trials [18, 19, 24, 25].

Methods

Data Sources

The VALIANT (pegcetacoplan; NCT05067127) and APPEAR-C3G (iptacopan; NCT04817618) trials were similarly designed, i.e., both included a 6-month randomized controlled period (RCP) followed by a 6-month open-label period (OLP), and the same primary endpoint (Table 1; Supplementary Fig. 1) [18, 19, 24, 25, 32]. The following key eligibility criteria were consistent across VALIANT and APPEAR-C3G (Supplementary Table 1): minimum urine protein–creatinine ratio (UPCR) threshold of ≥ 1.0 g/g to confirm active renal disease; eGFR threshold of ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m2; and the exclusion of participants with rapidly progressive crescentic glomerulonephritis or >50% interstitial fibrosis on renal biopsy and those receiving other complement inhibitors. However, there were also some differences in key eligibility criteria, e.g., VALIANT included adults/adolescents with native/post-transplant recurrent C3G or primary IC-MPGN, while APPEAR-C3G included The original studies were approved by the relevant institutional review boards at each study site and were carried out in accordance with the International Conference on Harmonisation good clinical practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki.

Indirect Treatment Comparisons

ITCs were conducted to assess the between-trial differences in efficacy for pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan based on 6-month and 12-month data. Data for pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan could be connected via the presence of a common placebo arm during the RCPs (i.e., the trials formed a complete evidence network) (Fig. 1). Consequently, two anchored ITCs were conducted to assess relative efficacy from baseline to 6 months: Bucher (primary analysis) and matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC; supportive analysis). Due to control arm crossover preventing the use of a common comparator, an unanchored MAIC was conducted to estimate relative treatment effects from 6 to 12 months in patients who received pegcetacoplan or iptacopan for a total of 12 months (i.e., during both the RCP and OLP). Bucher Analysis

The Bucher methodology is an established standard tool for conducting ITCs from separate RCTs that share a common comparator (e.g., placebo arm) [33]. To generate valid and robust relative treatment effect estimates, this technique assumes there is a consistent relationship between the interventions; a common comparator across the studies; and that the studies included in the ITC are comparable with respect to study design, effect modifiers (e.g., baseline patient characteristics, disease severity, and concomitant therapies) and outcomes measured [34, 35].

In this study, the Bucher methodology analyzed data for all available patients [i.e., the intent-to-treat (ITT) populations] to preserve within-study randomization and the original sample size. Baseline and 6-month outcome data from VALIANT were extracted from individual patient data (IPD) to derive aggregate data inputs, while the equivalent trial data for APPEAR-C3G were obtained from published aggregate data [only available data source (24, 25)].

Matching‑Adjusted Indirect Comparisons (MAIC)

A MAIC is a type of ITC that uses IPD from one trial and aggregate data from another trial to adjust for between-trial differences in patient populations [36, 37]. The MAICs were conducted aligned to the recommendations from the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) Decision Support Unit and the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research [36, 37].

For the anchored MAIC, the trials were first aligned to yield comparable analysis populations by excluding VALIANT participants who would have been ineligible under the APPEAR-C3G inclusion criteria: i.e., those with a diagnosis of primary IC-MPGN; those who received previous renal transplant; those who received > 7.5 mg/day (or equivalent) of prednisone ≤ 90 days before study drug administration; and those who had serum C3 levels ≥ 77 mg/dL at baseline. Adolescents in VALIANT were not excluded during this harmonization phase as the resultant low estimated effective sample size (ESS) would likely lead to bias. Propensity score weights were then estimated using logistic regression, with baseline age as a prespecified prognostic variable, to estimate each VALIANT patient’s probability of enrollment in APPEAR-C3G and to balance baseline characteristics across the trials. The use of age alone as a matching variable reflected the difference in the available data for VALIANT (adolescents/adults) and APPEAR-C3G (adults only), and ensured greater comparability between trial populations while preserving sample size. Subsequently, comparisons of treatment outcomes were conducted based on the balanced trial populations.

For the unanchored MAIC, the eligibility criteria of the VALIANT study were harmonized with the eligibility criteria of the APPEAR-C3G trial by applying the same four baseline factors used in the anchored MAIC. Weights for the VALIANT dataset were then derived by matching patient characteristics to the mean baseline age of the iptacopan arm in APPEAR-C3G. These weights were subsequently used to estimate the pegcetacoplan treatment effect from 6 to 12 months. For pegcetacoplan, the changes in UPCR and eGFR between 6 and 12 months were estimated using a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) with a structure consistent with that previously applied to iptacopan [38]. Finally, the overall treatment effect of pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan from baseline to 12 months was estimated by combining the indirect comparison results for the 0- to 6-months period (obtained via Bucher method or anchored MAIC) with the contrast between pegcetacoplan and iptacopan for the 6- to 12-months period, while accounting for the correlation between the treatment effects in these two time intervals.

Outcomes and Statistical Analysis

The comparative efficacy of pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan from baseline to 6 months was assessed for five key outcomes: mean reduction in log-transformed UPCR; proportion of patients who achieved UPCR < 1 g/g; proportion of patients who achieved ≥ 50% reduction in UPCR; mean change in eGFR; and proportion of patients who achieved the composite renal endpoint (≥ 50% reduction in UPCR and ≤ 15% reduction in eGFR). The definition of the study endpoints were consistent in both trials (Supplementary Table 2). However, the method of urine collection differed: first-morning urine sample was used in VALIANT, whereas 24-h urine collection was used in APPEAR-C3G. The Bucher analysis assessed the potential impact of these differences in urine collection, by evaluating the comparative efficacy of pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan in reducing UPCR under scenarios where the same method of urine collection was applied in both trials, i.e., either first-morning urine or 24-h urine collections. Comparative efficacy of pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan was assessed at 12 months for two outcomes: mean reduction in log-transformed UPCR and mean change in eGFR.

Relative treatment effects for the continuous outcomes (change from baseline in UPCR and eGFR) were reported as mean difference (MD) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Relative treatment effects for the binary outcomes (proportion of patients achieving UPCR < 1 g/g, ≥ 50% reduction in UPCR from baseline, and the composite renal endpoint) were reported as risk difference (RD) with 95% CIs. The z test and Wald test were used to examine the statistical significance of the relative efficacy estimates determined by Bucher and MAIC, respectively. The Bucher and MAIC ITCs assessed mean change from baseline in UPCR and eGFR estimated using the MMRM model.

Discussion

The results of these ITCs comparing data from two RCTs indicate that, after 6 and 12 months of treatment, pegcetacoplan provides greater reduction in proteinuria compared with iptacopan in patients with C3G, indicating a potential improved therapeutic benefit. The treatment difference between pegcetacoplan and iptacopan at 6 months was statistically significant across multiple clinically relevant endpoints that are strongly associated with long-term renal outcomes. The efficacy benefits of pegcetacoplan over iptacopan estimated in the ITCs at 6 months were sustained through 12 months. The consistency of results generated at different timepoints and using two independent, validated ITC methodologies supports the robustness of the overall improved treatment benefit of pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan.

The Bucher analysis showed that, from baseline to 6 months, pegcetacoplan was associated with a 51% greater reduction in UPCR compared with iptacopan and that 26% more patients achieved UPCR < 1 g/g, 29% more patients achieved ≥ 50% reduction in UPCR, and 23% more patients achieved the composite endpoint [which encompasses a ≥ 50% reduction in UPCR and stable/improved (≤ 15% reduction) eGFR]. These ITC data are strengthened by the assessment of clinically relevant outcomes in C3G. Patients have high risk of kidney failure [39], and proteinuria and eGFR are useful markers for risk of disease progression and kidney function, respectively, in C3G and primary IC-MPGN [40]. In the absence of long-term randomized data, several longitudinal, observational studies of patients with C3G and/or primary IC-MPGN have demonstrated that ≥ 50% reductions in proteinuria after 6 or 12 months and < 1 g/g at 12 months significantly lowers the risk of kidney failure [2, 41–43]. Additionally, a Delphi consensus of European experts corroborated that longitudinal measures of proteinuria, along with other relevant endpoints (e.g., eGFR), are suitable treatment goals [44]. Similarly, a panel of European C3G experts (the C3G Trial Endpoints Work Group) and the Kidney Health Initiative working group concluded that the demonstration of a favorable treatment effect on endpoints of proteinuria reduction (e.g., log-transformed UPCR from baseline, UPCR < 1 g/g, ≥ 50% reduction in UPCR and composite renal endpoint) and eGFR stabilization provide relevant biomarkers for assessing treatment efficacy. The panel also added that histopathological improvement is an indicator but serial biopsies may not always be available [39].

The between-trial difference in mean eGFR change did not reach statistical significance for pegcetacoplan versus iptacopan. The VALIANT trial reported a significant improvement in the change from baseline in eGFR (mL/min/1.73 m2) at 6 months for pegcetacoplan versus placebo [MD 6.31 (95% CI: 0.50, 12.12); p = 0.033] [18, 19]. In the APPEAR-C3G trial, mean change in eGFR was not significantly different for iptacopan versus placebo at 6 months [MD 2.16 (95% CI: − 2.75, 7.06); p = 0.388] [24]. Similar to proteinuria, longitudinal observational studies have shown that stabilization of eGFR is associated with a significantly lower risk of kidney failure [41, 43].

The Bucher ITC was a valid approach, because the VALIANT and APPEAR-C3G trials were sufficiently similar in their study designs and outcome definitions [33, 45, 46]. However, the VALIANT and APPEAR-C3G trials included some eligibility criteria differences with respect to underlying disease, age, and serum C3 level. The anchored MAIC, which importantly adjusted for key between-study differences in eligibility criteria and baseline characteristics, confirmed the results of the Bucher analysis. Additionally, the unanchored MAIC, conducted to assess relative efficacy for months 6–12, supports sustained treatment benefits with pegcetacoplan.

Differences in eligibility criteria between VALIANT and APPEAR-C3G may influence the comparability of findings from these trials. APPEAR-C3G focused on adults with native C3G [24], while VALIANT enrolled a broad patient population, including adolescents, patients with native or post-transplant recurrent C3G, primary IC-MPGN, and no maximum threshold for serum C3 levels [47], reflecting the real-world heterogeneity of these conditions.

While our analyses provide valuable insights into the relative clinical efficacy of pegcetacoplan and iptacopan, a comparative assessment of glomerular C3 staining, which may provide an indicator of pharmacodynamic treatment benefit [39], was not feasible due to differences in how this outcome was measured in the VALIANT and APPEAR-C3G trials. In the VALIANT trial, pegcetacoplan resulted in clearance of C3 from renal biopsies at 6 months: reduced C3 staining (decrease of ≥ 2 orders of magnitude from baseline using the standard scale of 0–3) was observed in 74% of patients treated with pegcetacoplan (including 71% with zero intensity staining) versus 12% with placebo [risk ratio 6.2 (95% CI: 2.4, 15.9)] [19]. In APPEAR-C3G, changes in adjusted mean glomerular C3 deposit scores (an exploratory endpoint; assessed on a scale of 0–12) at 6 months did not reach statistical significance: −0.8 with iptacopan versus 1.1 with placebo [adjusted MD –1.88 (95% CI: –3.30, −0.45); p = 0.0053] [24, 48].

Although pegcetacoplan and iptacopan target the complement cascade, they have distinct mechanisms of action. Pegcetacoplan directly inhibits C3, a central component of the complement system, and C3b, thereby blocking all three pathways (classical, lectin, and alternative) [49, 50]. In contrast, iptacopan is a selective inhibitor of factor B, targeting the alternative pathway and its amplification loop [3, 21, 22, 49, 50]. Evidence from Phase III trials suggest that both drugs are well tolerated and that most treatment-emergent adverse events were mild or moderate [18, 19, 24, 25].

Some differences in baseline characteristics were observed in the Bucher (e.g., mean eGFR level and proportion of participants with corticosteroid and/or mycophenolic acid) and anchored MAIC (after matching). The difference in corticosteroid/mycophenolic acid use between trials is important because immunosuppressive therapy appears to have an impact on the efficacy of iptacopan (it is reported that patients with C3G receiving iptacopan and immunosuppressants may show modest proteinuria reduction, which is likely linked to a more treatment-resistant nature of C3G in these patients [28]). In contrast, pegcetacoplan provided clinically meaningful proteinuria reduction, eGFR stabilization, and improvements in histopathological outcomes, irrespective of immunosuppressive treatment status [51]. However, these baseline covariates are considered to be prognostic variables and thereby are not expected to impact the interpretation of the relative treatment effects in anchored MAICs [52, 53].

Our analyses benefit from using ITC methodologies that are considered standard, robust, and recommended techniques to estimate relative treatment effects in the absence of direct evidence from head-to-head trials [34, 54]; Bucher and MAIC are preferred ITC methods, as recommended by multiple health technology assessment organizations such as the European Network for Health Technology Assessment (EunetHTA), NICE in the United Kingdom, and the Institute for Quality and Efficiency in Healthcare (IQWiG) in Germany [34, 55]. Additionally, ITCs can provide valuable comparative evidence in the absence of ‘gold-standard’ head-to-head RCTs, which are often not feasible in rare diseases due to limited patient populations, ethical considerations, and/or concurrent drug development programs [34].

Limitations of our analyses include that IPD were available only for the VALIANT trial and not for APPEAR-C3G; thus, patient-level adjustment between trials was not feasible. Additionally, anchored ITCs are potentially limited by assumptions about differences in trial design, reliance on reporting of trial characteristics and unknown differences that were not considered. One such trial difference contributed to the inclusion of adolescents in the Bucher to preserve randomization, and in the MAICs to preserve sample size. Lastly, unanchored MAICs are subject to inherent limitations, e.g., they assume that all known effect modifiers and prognostic factors are included in the weighting model and that failure of this assumption can reduce precision and bias the results [36].

Conclusions

In the absence of head-to-head studies, these ITCs using Bucher and MAIC methodologies indicate that pegcetacoplan provides greater proteinuria reduction, suggesting a potential therapeutic benefit over iptacopan for patients with C3G. The benefit of pegcetacoplan was evidenced by clinically meaningful reductions in proteinuria levels and a greater proportion of patients achieving the composite renal endpoint compared with iptacopan; the relative efficacy benefits observed at 6 months were sustained at 12 months. As such, these data highlight the potential of pegcetacoplan to significantly improve the lives of patients with C3G through sustained reduction in proteinuria, thereby reducing the risk of long-term outcomes such as kidney disease progression, end-stage kidney disease, and kidney transplant. The findings from this analysis will help guide clinicians and payers to better understand the utility of new therapies to treat patients with C3G.

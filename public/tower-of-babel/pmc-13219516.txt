Multi-dimensional text feature fusion-based BA-RILA for ancient Chinese poetry theme recognition
Authors: Xinlu Zhang, Yuemin Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe digitalization of traditional Chinese culture highlights the need for accurate poem theme identification, a key task of NLP. However, the progress is hindered by the scarcity of dedicated ancient Chinese corpora and poor adaptability of general-purpose models. To address these issues, a novel ancient poetry theme recognition method named BA-RILA (BACM-Attention-Rhythm-Imagery-BiLSTM-Aided Framework) is proposed in this paper. A three-dimensional multi-dimensional text feature fusion framework of semantics-rhythm-imagery is designed. Semantic vectors are extracted using the optimized BERT ancient Chinese pre-trained model (BACM), while 11-dimensional rhythmic features and 75-dimensional imagery features are synchronously extracted. To fuse these heterogeneous features complementarily, this research firstly align their dimensions and then apply an attention-weighting scheme. Next, the fused features are passed to a two-layer BiLSTM to capture deep temporal-semantic dependencies within the text. Finally, an 8-head multi-head attention (MHA) mechanism refines the features by dynamically strengthening the weights of the most salient elements.Finally, a four-layer fully connected classifier maps the enhanced features to a six-category theme space, and the category probability distribution is output through Softmax. Experimental verification shows that this method significantly outperforms benchmark models and exhibits strong generalization ability on cross-Tang and Song dynasties datasets.Keywords: Ancient poetry theme recognition, Multi-dimensional text feature fusion, BERT-ancient Chinese pre-trained model (BACM), BiLSTM, Multi-head attentionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The digitalization and intelligentization of excellent traditional Chinese culture is an important proposition entrusted by the times1. As a vital branch of text classification, ancient poem theme identification falls within the key research scope of NLP2,3. The accurate realization of this task is not only a key pivot for cultural inheritance, academic research and innovative transformation, but also a typical practice for the application of NLP technology in the field of classical texts. Currently, ancient Chinese NLP is confronted with dual bottlenecks such as the scarcity of corpus resources and the insufficient adaptability of general-purpose models to classical grammar and imagery expression. These problems have severely restricted the improvement of accuracy in ancient poem theme identification.

Machine learning models, as the core technical support for text classification tasks, have formed mature application paradigms in the NLP field, providing a feasible approach to solving this problem4. From early manually designed schemes to data-driven model optimization, three types of core technical paths have been developed in this field: dictionary/rule-based methods, traditional machine learning-based methods, and deep learning-based methods.

Early text classification relied on manually constructed dictionaries and syntactic rules, and classification was achieved via lexical matching and sentence structure parsing. Kowsari et al.5 summarized various algorithms for text classification, included rule-based text classification methods, and compared them with other classification techniques. Wang et al.6 proposed a rule-based classification method for dental diagnostic data, and accurate classification of medical text was achieved by virtue of its efficient and lightweight operational advantages. Bleich et al.7 conducted sentiment analysis using dictionary-based methods, and explored the unique application value of such methods in dimensions like domain adaptability in the era of deep learning. This method has strong interpretability, and its performance can be optimized through dictionary and rule updates, yet it relies on manual construction and suffers from insufficient cross-domain generalization capability.

To break through this limitation, with the popularization of machine learning technology, researchers combined word frequency statistics, feature engineering with classifiers such as SVM8 and Naive Bayes9. Automatic text classification was realized through text representation, feature selection and classifier construction. Among them, SVM is suitable for small-sample and nonlinear problems. Barakat et al.10 innovatively introduced it into text classification and adapted features to improve accuracy. Joachims11 proposed a complete framework, represented texts with TF-IDF, modeled with linear kernel functions, and achieved efficient single-label classification through feature selection. Cai et al.12 expanded it into H-SVM and adapted it to hierarchical classification through hierarchical training. Naive Bayes is based on probabilistic classification. Danyal et al.13 used four variants to compare performance and applicable scenarios on two types of datasets. McCallum et al.14 indicated that the multinomial model is more suitable for text classification. Toutanova et al.15 distinguished the degree of lexical generalization and specialization and improved term distribution estimation. This method does not require manual dictionaries or rules and has strong scenario adaptability, but it relies on large-scale labeled data, and challenges still exist in labeling costs.

To address the bottlenecks of traditional machine learning, breakthroughs have been achieved by deep learning in recent years by virtue of its stronger semantic representation capability, and its technical evolution can be divided into three stages. In the basic stage, Kim et al.16 first applied CNN to text classification, where classification was achieved via sentence vectorization, convolutional feature extraction and pooling-based dimensionality reduction. Mikolov et al.17 proposed RNN for word processing and context capturing, whereas LSTM and GRU alleviated long-sequence information attenuation through gating mechanisms and improved efficiency via streamlined architectures, respectively. During the pre-training stage, Zhao Biao et al.18 integrated BERT+BiGRU with dual-channel graph convolution, and aspect-level sentiment classification was achieved thereby. Zhang et al.19 improved BERT with FPnet, and semantic purification was realized through GRL and feature projection. Lu et al.20 combined knowledge-enhanced ERNIE (with improved masking) with DPCNN to complete classification tasks. In the advanced stage, Kipf et al.21 proposed GCN, where node representation was optimized; Feng et al.22 incorporated the attention mechanism into GCN and constructed adjacency graphs to mine sentiment features. Zhang et al.23 designed a two-layer interactive GCN, and word co-occurrence information was encoded thereby. Wang et al.24 explored associations between aspect terms and opinion words based on GAT. Zeng et al.25 proposed S-GCN, and a global semantic graph was innovatively constructed by integrating text, vocabulary and labels, which drives the continuous evolution of classification technology toward higher accuracy.

Although deep learning has become the mainstream for text classification by virtue of its strong semantic representation capability, many problems are still faced in the task of ancient poem theme identification. Dedicated corpora are lacking, and general corpora are difficult to adapt to the text characteristics of ancient poems; feature representation is single, core attributes such as rhyme and images are ignored, and classification accuracy is limited; model adaptability is insufficient, mainstream pre-trained models have weak capabilities in capturing ancient Chinese, and targeted applications are scarce. Recently, multi-dimensional feature fusion and heterogeneous processing have become key directions for Chinese text understanding26, and contrastive regularization has been verified to effectively improve the robustness of Chinese text recognizers27. For ancient Chinese poetry tasks, phrase-level attention and cross-modal semantic alignment have been explored to enhance poetic feature modeling28, which provides important references for the multimodal feature design of this study.

To address the above problems, the BA-RILA ancient poem theme identification model based on BACM and multi-dimensional text feature fusion is proposed in this study. First, a dedicated dataset is constructed, 10,000 ancient poems from multiple dynasties including the Tang, Song and Yuan Dynasties are crawled, labeled into six themes and preprocessed, thus filling the gap in ancient Chinese NLP corpora. Second, the 3D Feature Fusion of Semantics-Rhythm-Imagery system is designed, semantic vectors are extracted via BACM, and 11-dimensional prosodic features and 75-dimensional image features are synchronously extracted and fused. Finally, a two-layer BiLSTM is adopted to capture temporal dependencies, an 8-head multi-head self-attention mechanism is introduced, and prediction accuracy is improved through a fully connected classifier.

Imagery features are regarded as the core semantic identifiers of ancient poem themes. In this paper, the quantitative representation of imagery features is achieved through a 75-dimensional vector. 25 core imagery categories are selected based on three rigorous criteria—thematic representativeness, corpus frequency, and cultural typicality—using Research on Imagery in Tang Poetry and Song Ci and the Classical Poetry Imagery Database as data sources. The selection criteria are defined as follows: (1) Thematic representativeness: each imagery category can directly map to one or more of the six predefined poem themes and serve as a distinctive semantic marker for theme discrimination; (2) Corpus frequency: the imagery category appears at least 50 times in the self-constructed corpus, ensuring statistical validity for feature quantification; (3) Cultural typicality: the imagery bears fixed and widely recognized cultural connotations in classical Chinese poetry (e.g., ‘柳’ for farewell, ‘月’ for homesickness), excluding obscure or polysemous imagery with ambiguous thematic implications. The 25 core imagery categories cover the classic symbolic elements of all six themes, and each category corresponds to 3 quantifiable dimensions (occurrence frequency, sentiment tendency, imagery intensity), forming a 75-dimensional feature vector in total.

Results

To further verify the effectiveness of the proposed BA-RILA method in the task of ancient poem theme identification, four sets of experiments are designed in this chapter. The first set is parameter analysis, where the optimal parameters suitable for ancient poem theme feature extraction are selected by varying the Dropout Rate and the number of BiLSTM layers. The second set is comparative experiments, which are conducted on a self-constructed dataset for six-category ancient poem theme identification. The identification performance of the model is verified by comparing it with benchmark models such as BiGRU-CNN and BERT-TextCNN, based on experimental metrics. The third set is ablation experiments, where components of the model are removed one by one, and the performance variations between the proposed model and the ablated models are compared. The fourth set is cross-historical period performance comparison experiments, where the model’s theme identification performance under different historical contexts is compared, so as to verify the model’s generalization ability and historical adaptability.

Parameter analysis

Dropout rate

Parameter analysis is conducted in this study to explore the correlation between Dropout Rate (regularization ratio) in the proposed method and F1-Score. The core function of Dropout is to suppress overfitting by randomly deactivating neurons. The relationship between its value and the recognition performance of the method can be explained by combining experimental data and trend characteristics.

The cause of this trend is directly linked to the regularization mechanism of Dropout. When the Dropout Rate is set to 0, no regularization constraints are imposed on the model, so it is prone to overfitting the noise information in the training data, and the initial F1-Score is not optimal. As the Dropout ratio increases, some neurons are randomly deactivated during model training, reducing the dependence on redundant information, and the generalization ability is gradually enhanced. When the Dropout Rate reaches 0.5, a balance is achieved between the regularization strength and feature learning capability, which not only mitigates the risk of overfitting but also preserves sufficient thematic feature representation capability. However, when the Dropout Rate exceeds 0.5, the excessively high neuron inactivation rate leads to the loss of some important features, thereby resulting in a decline in model performance. The optimal value of this parameter is determined as 0.5, with the corresponding F1-Score reaching 97.48%.

As can be seen from the experimental results, a nonlinear relationship of rising first and then falling is observed between the number of BiLSTM layers and model performance. When the number of layers is increased from 1 to 2, the Macro-F1-Score is significantly improved from 92.1% to 95.5%, with an increase of 3.4%. When the number of layers is further increased to 3, the Macro-F1-Score slightly drops to 94.3%, a decrease of 1.2% compared with the 2-layer setting. The core reasons for this trend are summarized as follows. Limited temporal modeling capability is possessed by the 1-layer BiLSTM, so it cannot fully capture the long-distance semantic dependencies in ancient poems, resulting in insufficient extraction of temporal features. A bidirectional stacked structure is adopted by the 2-layer BiLSTM, which can deeply explore the temporal correlations between the context of texts. Complementary enhancement is formed with the 3D features of semantics-rhyme-images, and the theme-specific semantic context is accurately depicted, so the performance peak is achieved. However, excessive parameter expansion is caused by the 3-layer BiLSTM, leading to overfitting in the training scenario of concise ancient poem texts. In addition, excessive layers will result in feature redundancy, which weakens the representation capability of core theme features and finally causes performance degradation.

Based on the experimental results and modeling logic, 2 is determined as the optimal number of BiLSTM layers for this model. At this setting, the best balance between temporal feature capture accuracy and generalization capability is achieved by the model, with the Macro-F1-Score peaking at 95.5%, which can fully meet the temporal semantic modeling requirements of the ancient poem theme identification task.

Conclusion

The present study is focused on the accurate identification of ancient poem themes. Systematic research is conducted to address the problems such as the lack of dedicated ancient Chinese corpora, insufficient model adaptability, and limitations of single-feature representation. In terms of the dataset, 10,000 ancient poems are crawled, labeled into six themes, and preprocessed, filling the gap in ancient Chinese NLP corpora. For model design, the BA-RILA model is proposed, featuring an innovative 3D feature fusion architecture of semantics-rhyme-images. It integrates BACM encoding, two-layer BiLSTM, and Multi-Head Attention to strengthen feature learning. Experimental verification shows that under optimal parameters, the model achieves an accuracy of 97.47%, outperforming benchmark models, and exhibits strong generalization ability across Tang and Song Dynasty datasets. The innovations of this study lie in the construction of a multimodal feature system, the optimization of BERT’s adaptability to ancient Chinese, and the design of an efficient feature learning mechanism.

Future research can be expanded in three aspects. First, the dataset scale can be expanded, with additional themes included, along with poems from the Ming and Qing dynasties, to improve annotation quality. Second, the BACM model can be enhanced by integrating knowledge of ancient poem rhyme and images, and adaptive feature fusion strategies can be explored. Third, multi-source data such as calligraphy and annotations can be integrated to build an intra-textual multimodal fusion model, and the feature extraction module can be transferred to tasks such as sentiment analysis, so as to promote the coordinated development of NLP in classical literature research.

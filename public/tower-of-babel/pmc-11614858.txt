Smac/DIABLO protein acts as an independent prognostic factor in oral squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Fábio França Vieira e Silva, Vito Carlo Alberto Caponio, Andrea Ballini, Cintia Micaela Chamorro-Petronacci, Alejandro Ismael Lourenzo-Pouso, Abel García-García, Marina Di Domenico, José Manuel Suaréz-Peñaranda, Mario Pérez-Sayáns, María Elena Padín-Iruegas
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) poses significant health risks with increasing incidence and mortality rates. In this context, there is an urgent need to explore novel biomarkers to enhance therapeutic strategies and improve survival. Understanding apoptotic evasion in cancer pathogenesis, this pioneering study aims to investigate the correlation between a pro-apoptotic protein Smac/DIABLO and patient prognosis within the OSCC cohort. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was employed to analyze Smac/DIABLO protein expression and correlate with clinicopathological and prognostic factors during a long-term follow-up. Smac/DIABLO low expression was associated with worse overall survival (OS), relapse-free survival (RFS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and an increase in risk of lymph node metastasis (LNM) in univariate analyses. Furthermore, multivariate analyses confirmed Smac/DIABLO as an independent prognostic factor, predicting poorer OS [Hazard Ratio (HR) = 3.6 (95% CI 1.7–7.6), p < 0.001], RFS [HR = 2.9 (95% CI 1.4–5.6), p = 0.003], DSS [HR = 6.7 (95% CI 2.7–16.7), p < 0.001], and increased likelihood of LNM [Odds Ratio (OR) = 4.8 (95% CI 1.4–15.9), p = 0.011]. Patients with positive Smac/DIABLO expression exhibited three times higher survival probability. Low proapoptotic protein Smac/DIABLO expression significantly influences prognostic predictions and strongly correlates with poor OSCC outcomes. Future studies involving Smac-mimetic drugs in OSCC are needed to evaluate their pro-apoptotic potential in cancer cells. Introduction

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) is a neoplasm group arising from the squamous cells that line the head and neck region tissues, such as the oral cavity, nasopharynx, oropharynx, hypopharynx, lips, nasal cavity, paranasal sinuses, and salivary glands. HNSCC is the seventh most common cancer worldwide, with approximately 890,000 new cases annually (approximately 4.5% of all cancer diagnoses worldwide) and 450,000 deaths (approximately 4.6% of cancer deaths worldwide)1.

The oral cavity region manifests the majority of HNSCC cases and exhibits the highest mortality rates. The increase in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) cases is predominantly ascribed to lifestyle elements, such as alcohol and tobacco consumption, alongside the escalating human papillomavirus (HPV) infection incidences2.

Apoptosis is a type of programmed cell death, that plays a crucial cause in the carcinogenic process and tumor progression. The apoptosis evasion is one of the most important hallmarks of cancer described by Hanahan & Weinberg in 2000 and 20113,4. The study of apoptosis was introduced into cell biology in 1972, and, since then, numerous genetic and biochemical factors have been identified within an intricate pathway that extends from the initial signal reception to the cell death effector action5,6.

There are then two possible pathways for this process to eventuate: The extrinsic pathway, generated when a cell death ligand binds to its cognate receptor; And the intrinsic pathway, directed through the mitochondria (Fig. 1). In both, a highly regulated combination of anti- and pro-apoptotic protein releases must occur until the event can generate the activation of cysteine-containing aspartic acid-specific proteases (Caspases). Caspases are synthesized as pro-apoptotic protein enzymes and activated by proteolytic cleavage, being a crucial component in apoptotic signaling steps7.

The human inhibitor of apoptosis (IAP) proteins family are important anti-apoptotic agents that act mainly by suppressing the activation of initiator Caspases (which include Caspases 2, 8, 9, and 10) and effectors Caspases (which include Caspase 3, 6, and 7)6. The IAPs are defined by the presence of one or more baculoviral IAP repeat (BIR) domains8. X-linked IAP (XIAP), Cellular IAP 1 and 2 (cIAP1 and cIAP2), Melanoma-IAP (ML-IAP), Neuronal-IAP (NAIP), Survivin, BIR-containing ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme (Bruce/Apollon) and IAP-like protein 2 (ILP-2) are the eight members of this family (Fig. 1)9.

Conversely, the function of a pro-apoptotic protein holds significant importance in inhibiting IAPs, thereby facilitating apoptotic cascade progression8,10. The Second Mitochondria-derived Caspase Activator (Smac) protein, also referred to as a Direct Inhibitor of Apoptosis-Binding Protein with Low pI (DIABLO), was initially characterized in the 2000s as a mitochondrial precursor protein weighing 29 kDa with a pI of 6.1. Upon apoptotic induction, Smac/DIABLO is released into the cytosol alongside Cytochrome c from the mitochondria (Fig. 1)11,12.

The newly synthesized Smac/DIABLO contains 239 amino acids and 55 N-terminal residues encoding the mitochondrial targeting sequence (MTS), which are removed through proteolysis in the mature form, resulting in the exposure of 4 hydrophobic amino acids (Ala-Val-Pro-Lle) at the N terminus13. In this process, post-translational modification occurs for maturation, activation, and translocation to mitochondrial intermembrane space (MIS)14.

After apoptotic stimuli, Cytochrome c, apoptosis-inducing factor (AIF), and mature Smac/DIABLO are released into the cytosol, interacting with the IAPs through their BIRs domains and leaving the Caspases free to apoptotic process conclusion, for example, Smac/DIABLO interacts with the BIR2 and BIR3 domains of XIAP, which allows Caspases 9 and 3 releases15,16.

The crucial role of Smac/DIABLO in the apoptotic cascade has become the target of several studies in different cancer types, such as stomach17, breast18, kidney19, thyroid20, and colorectal cancer (CCR)21. Its overexpression is commonly associated with increased sensitivity to immunotherapy, radiotherapy, and/or chemotherapy, and consequently serves as an important prognostic predictor11. Furthermore, Smac/DIABLO has become an important target in therapy development that aims to use the mechanism of action to overcome apoptotic evasion through increased pro-apoptotic protein expression, preventing cancer cell proliferation22–25.

Therefore, this study’s main aim is to carry out a Smac/DIABLO protein expression analysis in patients diagnosed with OSCC and carry out a pioneering association of these findings with several clinicopathological, prognostic, and survival factors, through a dense retrospective analysis.

Materials and methods

Patients selection

A total of 83 patients were included in this study. The cohort consists of randomly selected patients diagnosed with primary OSCC at the Clinical Hospital of the University of Santiago de Compostela between 2010 and 2015 through the Galician Health Service database (IANUS). The sample size was decided considering half of the total number of patients diagnosed during this period, and a computerized random selection algorithm was used to guarantee impartiality in case selection, thus ensuring that each individual had the same probability of being included in the sample. Data were anonymized to protect patient privacy, creating a representative sample that reflects the distribution and demographic characteristics of the population affected by OSCC.

All patients were treated with curative intent, and all their clinical and pathological data were extracted considering all information between 2010 and 2023. All patients had postoperative follow-up and a careful clinical examination was performed during each follow-up visit. A definitive histopathological diagnosis was provided for suspected relapsed lesions or second primary tumors.

The inclusion criteria were: (a) primary OSCC surgical samples; (b) age above 18 years; (e) follow-up data up to 8 years for live patients. Exclusion criteria were: (a) OSCC that the primary tumor site could not be identified; (b) patients with previous radiotherapy to the lesion area; (c) relapsed or secondary primary OSCC; (d) patients with insufficient follow-up data.

Ethical approval

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Santiago-Lugo Research Ethics Committee (CEI-SL) (code 2020/247). All procedures performed, as they were studies involving human participants, were by the Declaration of Helsinki.

The Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Santiago-Lugo Research Ethics Committee (CEI-SL) waived the requirement for patient consent per IRB guidelines for retrospective data and a non-interventional study.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Two consecutive sections of 4 μm thickness were prepared from each sample. IHC was performed using rabbit anti-Smac/DIABLO polyclonal antibody (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA) with automatized equipment (Omnis, Agilent Dako, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Briefly, epitope retrieval was performed in 10 mM sodium citrate buffer (pH 9.0) using a water bath for 30 min at 97 °C. Endogenous peroxidase was blocked using a peroxidase-blocking reagent (Agilent Dako, Santa Clara, CA, USA) for 5 min. Incubation with the primary antibody (1/500) was performed at room temperature for 20 min and staining was revealed with EnVision (20 min) and diaminobenzidine (DAB) for 10 min (Agilent Dako, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The slides were counterstained with hematoxylin-eosin (HE) for 15 min.

Smac/DIABLO expression quantification

Two independent expert observers (F.F.V.S. and J.M.S.P.) analyzed all the stained sections. Immunohistochemically, only unequivocal epithelial cytoplasmic staining was considered positive. Stromal or vascular staining was not considered. Results were evaluated semi-quantitatively attending to categorization as Smac/DIABLO-negative if the percentage of positive staining was < 30% of the total tumor area in the specimens. Conversely, patients were classified as Smac/DIABLO-positive if the total tumor area stained was > 30%21.

Data extraction

The qualitative variables evaluated in this study included: Patient identification numbers, gender, age of diagnosis, tobacco status, number of cigarettes per day, alcohol status, the intensity of alcohol consumption, family history of cancer, periodontal disease status, oral health status, edentulism status, biopsy type, location and type of initial lesion, oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMD) history, OPMD type, TNM staging according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Eighth Edition26, histological grade, perineural invasion, bone invasion, vascular invasion, affected margins, affected lymph nodes, sentinel node status, lymph node emptying, radiotherapy status, radiotherapy dosage, chemotherapy status, chemotherapy type, second primary tumor, development of other neoplasms, relapses, death by general conditions, death by cancer, date of diagnosis, date of relapses, date of death, follow-up duration, and Smac/DIABLO IHC expression status.

Clinicopathological parameters

The information acquired in this article was taken from the Galician Health Service database. Some information was taken from forms completed by patients during the anamnesis. All histopathological characteristics were reviewed by two experts (F.F.V.S. and J.M.S.P.). All dental information was reviewed by two experts (M.P.S. and C.M.C.P.).

To analyze the periodontal disease status orthopantomograms (OPGs) were performed and the obtained images were archived and managed through Sidexis® Software (Dentsply Sirona Inc., Pennsylvania, USA). This imaging modality facilitated the determination of tooth enumeration and overall alveolar bone resorption. Assessment adhered to anatomical and morphological criteria, delineating morphological bone loss as multiple teeth exhibiting loss equivalent to or surpassing the middle third of the root.

Some subjective factors such as smoking status were considered in three parameters, smoker, non-smoker, and former smoker, and, the quantity was considered < 25 and ≥ 25 cigarettes per day (high or low), using an average of 25 considered the amount described by each of the smoking patients included in our cohort; to consider a patient as a former smoker, these needed to be smoke-free for a year; the alcohol status was considered between a consumer or not, and the intensity was classified as low, moderate, and high, based on the patient’s responses and interpretation; family history of cancer was considered positive or negative, and only cases of cancer up to the second family generation were considered positive; the radiotherapy dosage was considered low when ≤ 6000 centiGray (cGy) and high when > 6000 cGy, based on an average of all dosages used.

Clinical endpoints examined were overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), relapse-free survival (RFS), and lymph node metastasis (LNM). Follow-up time was calculated from surgical operation date to event or last follow-up date.

Statistical analysis

The study was performed according to REporting recommendations for tumor MARKer prognostic studies (REMARK) guidelines27. Variables were tested for normal distribution using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Due to the non-normal variables distribution, Spearman rank correlation analysis was conducted to explore the association among clinicopathological variables. The chi-square test was utilized for dichotomous variables, whereas Mann-Whitney, Kruskal-Wallis, or ANOVA tests were employed for linear variables. Post-hoc Bonferroni-Holm false discovery rate adjustment was implemented to account for multiple comparisons, where necessary. Outcomes such as OS, DSS, and RFS were evaluated by Kaplan-Meier analysis with a log-rank test in a univariate analysis. In assessing the impact of clinicopathological factors on survival, a multivariate Cox regression model was constructed, incorporating variables that exhibited statistical significance in the univariate analysis. SPSS 21.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and STATA 16.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX, USA) were used to perform all tests and only p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. At last, stepwise backward binary regression investigated the relation between Smac/DIABLO status and LNM. The backward stepwise multivariate approach considered only those clinicopathological variables that achieved statistical significance during the univariate analysis, with an α value set to 0.157 for the p-value per recommendations by Heinze et al., Sahoo et al., and Caponio et al.28–30.

Results

Sample description

The patient’s mean age was 66.7 years, from 45 to 91. Among them, 71% (59) were aged ≥ 60 years. Gender distribution was 66.3% (55) males and 33.7% (28) females. Regarding smoker status, 22.9% (19) were non-smokers, 49.4% (41) were smokers, and 27.7% (23) were former smokers. Among smokers, 53.7% (22) smoked ≥ 25 cigarettes per day. Alcohol consumption was observed in 73.5% (61) of patients, with 52.5% (32) consuming at high intensity, 37.7% (23) at moderate intensity, and 9.8% (6) at low intensity. A family history of cancer was absent in 62.7% (52) and present in 37.3% (31) of cases. Periodontal disease was present in 47% (39) of patients and considering the oral health status, 49.4% (41) of patients were considered in bad conditions, 27.7% (23) in moderate conditions, and 10.8% (9) in good conditions. The majority of patients, 67.5% (56) had partial tooth loss, while 16.9% (14) had no loss and 15.7% (13) had a total loss. A total of 13.3% (11) of patients had previously been diagnosed with OPMD, with leukoplakia being the most common subtype, representing 81.8% (9) of cases. Tumors were predominantly located on the tongue, representing 44.6% (37) of cases, followed by 30.1% (25) in masticatory mucosa and 25.3% (21) on the floor of the mouth. Biopsies were mainly incisional, representing 71% (59) rather than excisional in 29% (24). In the tumors majority, 84.3% (70) were ulcerated, and 15.7% (13) were non-ulcerated.

T status distribution was as follows: 40.1% (34) were classified as T1, 40.1% (34) as T2, 7.2% (6) as T3, and 8.4% (7) as T4. N status distribution was as follows: 67.5% (56) were classified as N0, 10.8% (9) as N1, 15.5% (12) as N2, and 7.2% (6) as N2b. The majority of 97.6% (81) of cases were classified as M0, while 2.4% (2) as M1. Staging revealed 32.5% (27) at stage I, 26.5% (22) at stage II, 12% (10) at stage III, 25.3% (21) at stage IV, and 3.6 (3) at stage IVA. Tumor grading was 37.4% (31) G1, 51.8% (43) G2, 9.6% (8) G3, and 1.2% (1) G4. Perineural invasion was present in 39.8% (33) of cases. Bone invasion occurred in 24.1% (20), while vascular invasion was observed in 21.7% (18) cases. Affected margins were reported in 25.3% (21) of cases. Sentinel node involvement was found in 34.9% (29) of cases, and 61.5% (51) undergoing lymph node emptying.

IHC analysis showed Smac/DIABLO protein positivity considering a 30% cut-off21 in 66.3% (55) and negativity in 33.7% (28) cases (Fig. 2). Radiotherapy was utilized in 45.8% (38) of patients, with 78.9% (30) receiving high-dose regimens. Chemotherapy was administered in 15.7% (13) cases, predominantly cisplatin-based (76.9%). Relapse occurred in 54.2% (45) of patients, and a second primary tumor developed in 10.8% (9) cases. Regarding survival, 37.4% (31) of patients were deceased, with 88% (73) surviving ≥ 5 years (Table 1).

Correlations between Smac/DIABLO and Clinicopathological Variables

The chi-square test showed a statistically significant difference between oral health status and the number of positive/negative Smac/DIABLO expression tumors, demonstrating an association between negative patients with bad oral health status (p = 0.011). The same occurred in N status, with a gradual association between higher N and Smac/DIABLO negative expression (p = 0.051). When considering only the N status as positive or negative, there was a considerable increase in the number of patients with positive N and Smac/DIABLO negative expression (p = 0.015). Although in staging the statistics are not significant considering each stage (p = 0.365), when considering low (I/II) versus high (III/IV) stages, an association was demonstrated between patients in low stages and positive Smac/DIABLO expression (p = 0.096). On the other hand, contrary to expectations, analyzing the association between Smac/DIABLO and tobacco status, there was a significant association between smokers/former smokers patients with positive Smac/DIABLO expression (p = 0.050), or a gradual association considering smokers, former smokers, and non-smokers, separately (p = 0.024) (Supplementary Fig. 1). Complete information about this section can be found in Supplementary Table 1.

Survival analysis

Significant variables identified in the univariate analysis were incorporated into the multivariate Cox regression analysis. However, certain variables were excluded from the multivariate analysis despite exhibiting statistical significance due to substantial imbalances in patient numbers across groups, necessitating a larger sample size to include these variables.

Considering all patients, the average follow-up time was 2015 days, ranging between 13 and 4991. Considering only alive patients until the end of follow-up, the average follow-up was 3833 days, ranging between 2959 and 4991.

Overall survival

Age (p < 0.001), tobacco status (p = 0.027), number of cigarettes (p < 0.001), staging (p = 0.013), and Smac/DIABLO (p < 0.001) significantly influenced OS in the univariate analysis, qua the affected margins (p = 0.052) resulted close to the significant level (Supplementary Table 2). Based on these findings, it is evident that patients exhibiting negative Smac/DIABLO expression, smokers, and those with tumor margin involvement experience inferior OS. Additionally, a progressive decline in OS is noted with increased daily cigarette consumption, advanced staging, and older age.

In the multivariate analysis, tumors with negative Smac/DIABLO at a cut-off of 30% reported worse OS [Hazard Ratio (HR) = 3.6 (95% CI 1.7–7.6), p < 0.001] (Fig. 3). Smac/DIABLO negative expression represented a three times greater probability of death, representing an independent factor for OS, as well as patients with higher staging (p = 0.009) and patients with tumors with affected margins (p = 0.034).

Relapse-free survival

Age (p < 0.001), tobacco status (p = 0.028) and Smac/DIABLO (p < 0.001) significantly influenced RFS in the univariate analysis, while gender (p = 0.096) and affected margins (p = 0.056), resulted close to the significant level (Supplementary Table 2). Based on these findings, it is evident that patients exhibiting negative Smac/DIABLO expression and smokers experience inferior RFS. Additionally, a progressive decline in RFS is noted with increased older age.

In the multivariate analysis, tumors with negative Smac/DIABLO at a 30% cut-off reported worse RFS [HR = 2.9 (95% CI 1.4–5.6), p = 0.003] (Fig. 4). Smac/DIABLO negative expression represented an independent factor for RFS, and gender male patients represented an important association with worse RFS (p = 0.009).

Disease-specific survival

Age (p < 0.001), staging (p = 0.027), periodontal disease (p = 0.007), and Smac/DIABLO (p < 0.001) significantly influenced DSS in the univariate analysis (Supplementary Table 2). Based on these findings, it is evident that patients exhibiting negative Smac/DIABLO expression and periodontal disease condition experience inferior DSS. Additionally, a progressive decline in DSS is noted with advanced staging and older age.

In the multivariate analysis, tumors with negative Smac/DIABLO at a cut-off of 30% reported worse DSS [HR = 6.7 (95% CI 2.7–16.7), p < 0.001] (Fig. 5). Smac/DIABLO negative expression represented an independent factor for DSS and patients with periodontal disease (p = 0.004).

Lymph-node metastasis analysis

Logistic regression analysis was performed to verify the effects of age, sex, smoking status, alcohol status, family history of cancer, periodontal disease, oral health status, tumor location, type of initial lesion, grading, affected margins, staging, sentinel node status, perineural invasion, bone invasion, vascular invasion, and OPMD diagnosis before the primary tumor on the probability of participants having positive LNM at diagnosis time. In univariate analysis, alcohol status (p = 0.036), staging (p < 0.001), sentinel node presence (p = 0.034), grading (p = 0.010), vascular invasion (p = 0.007), and Smac/DIABLO status (p < 0.001) significantly influenced N-status (Supplementary Table 3).

The backward stepwise multivariate approach found that patients with high-grade tumors had a higher risk of LNM (p = 0.007), as did patients with a sentinel node (p = 0.036). Smac/DIABLO negative expression represented an independent factor for LNM at diagnosis time [Odds Ratio (OR) = 4.8 (95% CI 1.4–15.9), p = 0.011].

Discussion

OSCC is a rising disease with the incidence and mortality numbers reflecting an exponential increase, due to changes in lifestyle and exposure to environmental factors1. As the majority types of cancer, the increase in life expectancy reverberates in an increase in incidence since age is the main etiological factor for the disease3,4. The substantial risk factors for OSCC are consumption of tobacco, alcohol, betel pound, areca nut, and genetic alterations. Technological advances have occurred in treatment, but prognosis and survival are still impacted due to late diagnoses, and some environmental factors31.

Our study demonstrated in univariate analyses that age directly affected OS (p < 0.001), RFS (p < 0.001), and DSS (p< 0.001), as well as the male gender represented greater chances to relapses, a finding that is in line with most articles that analyze this association32. Smoking status and even the number of cigarettes per day are in line with Tomar et al.33, which justify not only the status but also the length of time as a smoker and the number of cigarettes as important prognostic factors in oral cancer.

Periodontal disease status also demonstrated in our results a direct association with DSS (p= 0.007), as in the studies by Tasoulas et al.34, and Naaval et al.35, which proved through long-term follow-up that periodontal disease is an important risk factor, as well as that there is a gradual association, with patients in severe stages with oral cancer. In our findings, we also demonstrated that some histopathological factors, such as staging (p = 0.013) and affected margins (p= 0.052), influenced OS, which is expected as advanced-stage tumors have a worse prognosis36.

Our research demonstrated through multivariate analysis that staging (p = 0.009) and affected margins (p = 0.034) function as independent markers for OS, as does male gender (p = 0.023) represents an important association for worse RFS. In DSS, the presence of periodontal disease status is an independent marker for worsening survival in patients with OSCC.

In our univariate analyses correlating LNM with clinicopathological factors, we observed a significant correlation between the presence of the sentinel node (p = 0.034), grading (p = 0.010), vascular invasion (p = 0.007), and LNM. Additionally, alcohol consumption (p = 0.036) appeared to be a contributing risk factor for LNM. Conversely, in the multivariate analysis, the grading (p = 0.007) and sentinel node presence (p = 0.036) emerged as independent predictors for LNM.

Upon directly correlating Smac/DIABLO status with clinicopathological factors, we observed a significant association between patients exhibiting negative Smac/DIABLO expression and poor oral health status (p = 0.011), positive N status (p = 0.015), and advanced stages (p= 0.096). Some studies have already related poor oral health to the worse prognosis of some types of cancer37–39, including oral cancer40,41. A possible explanation for poor oral health being associated with negative Smac/DIABLO protein expression is that the same group of patients with negative expression of Smac/DIABLO and poor oral health have a worse prognosis.

Interestingly, smokers displayed heightened Smac/DIABLO expression (p = 0.024), although the reason remains unclear. The existing literature does not document an association between these aspects and protein expression. One possible explanation could be that in smokers, exposure to cigarette toxins induces tissue and cellular alterations or specific mutations, and the cellular turnover should be higher in tumors increasing the likelihood of apoptosis and consequent Smac/DIABLO expression upregulation. However, in this scenario, the expression may not be linked to the patient’s prognosis.

The association of Smac/DIABLO expression with prognostic factors analyzed in our study, in univariate analyses, pointed that Smac/DIABLO negative expression demonstrated a worse both OS (p < 0.001), RFS (p < 0.001), DSS (p < 0.001) and LNM (p < 0.001). In both correlations, when multivariate analyses were performed, Smac/DIABLO proved to be an independent factor, with worse OS (p < 0.001), RFS (p = 0.003), DSS (p < 0.001) and LNM (p = 0.011), with survival possibility three times higher in patients who showed Smac/DIABLO positive expression.

This association has been made in several other studies involving other types of cancer since the beginning of the 2000s. For example, Endo et al.21 demonstrated in CCR that Smac/DIABLO low expression is an independent factor determining the poorer prognosis. Findings are similar to those of Xu et al.42 in esophageal cancer, Pluta et al.43 in breast cancer, Mizutaki et al. in bladder cancer44, and Guttà et al.45 in melanoma, suggesting the association of Smac/DIABLO low expression with resistance to chemotherapy, higher relapses probability, and worse survival rates.

Investigations involving a diverse range of cancer cell lines have shown that Smac-mimetics alone or in association with other drugs exhibit cytotoxic effects and demonstrate anti-cancer therapeutic potential. The mechanism of action of these compounds develops through triggering the activation of Caspases by interacting with and inhibiting IAPs46. Smac-mimetics can be used in association with drugs that act in different pathways such as TRAIL47,48, CD9549, and TNF-α50,51, which have demonstrated positive therapeutic effects in inducing apoptosis.

Certain Smac-mimetics, such as ASTX660, have been shown to stimulate the formation of the necrosome complex. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of RIP1, RIP3, or MLKL, key components of the necrosome complex, has been demonstrated to confer protection against cell death induced by ASTX660 alone or in combination with TNF-α, particularly in breast cancer cells where Caspase inhibition is implemented52,53. This mechanism mirrors findings by Hannes et al.54 wherein the Smac-mimetic BV6, acting as an antagonist to IAP proteins, induces necroptosis in pancreatic cancer cells when apoptosis is impeded by Caspase inhibition.

Through pharmacological combination, Yu et al.52 elucidated the utilization of Doxorubicin in combination with SBP-0636457, a Smac-mimetic compound, in cotreatment, which resulted in necroptosis induction rather than apoptosis in breast cancer cells. Conversely, Prasopporn et al.55 demonstrated that Smac-mimetic LCL161 combined with Gemcitabine and Cisplatin therapy effectively inhibits multidrug resistance development in a rare subtype of gastric cancer. Dmello et al.56, also demonstrated treatment of LCL161 and Bazedoxifene in chemotherapy enhanced tumor cell apoptosis in CCR.

Anti-cancer drugs that use apoptosis induction as a mechanism of action are well known, but the personalized medicine advancement and knowledge of the phenomena involved in the apoptotic pathway have focused their efforts on finding specific targets that can improve therapeutic expectations for these patients, through drugs that act on cancer cells and not on normal cells, thus avoiding several side effects7,57–59.

Conclusion

Our findings underscore the Smac/DIABLO protein expression’s crucial role as a robust prognostic indicator for unfavorable outcomes in OSCC, manifesting in poorer OS, RFS, DSS, and heightened likelihood of LNM. Future studies involving Smac-mimetic drugs in OSCC are needed to evaluate their pro-apoptotic potential in cancer cells.

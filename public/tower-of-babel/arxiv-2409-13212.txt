The multiplicity-one theorem for the superspeciality of curves of genus two
Shushi Harashita, Yuya Yamamoto

Abstract
Igusa proved in 1958 that the polynomial determining the supersingularity of elliptic curve in Legendre form is separable. In this paper, we get an analogous result for curves of genus 2 2 in Rosenhain form. More precisely we show that the ideal determining the superspeciality of the curve has multiplicity one at every superspecial point. Igusa used a Picard-Fucks differential operator annihilating a Gauß hypergeometric series. We shall use Lauricella system (of type D) of hypergeometric differential equations in three variables. Keywords: algebraic curve, superspecial, hypergeometric series, positive characteristic MSC 2020: 14H10, 33C65, 14G17, 11G20



1
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>2
.
Consider an elliptic curve of Legendre form

E:\quad y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-t)

over
K
. It is well known (cf.
[
1
, p.255]
and
[
9
, Theorem V.4.1]
) that
E
is supersingular if and only if

H_{p}(t)=0
, where

H_{p}(t):=\sum_{i=0}^{(p-1)/2}{\binom{(p-1)/2}{i}}^{2}t^{i}.

A celebrated result by Igusa
[
3
]
says that
the polynomial
H_{p}(t)
is separable. His proof uses the fact that

H_{p}(z)
satisfied the differential equation

\mathop{\mathcal{D}}{H_{p}(z)}=0

with

\mathop{\mathcal{D}}=z(1-z)\dfrac{d}{dz^{2}}+(c-(a+b+1)z)\dfrac{d}{dz}-ab

(1-1)

for
a=1/2
,
b=1/2
and
c=1
(cf. Igusa
[
3
]
).
For
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
with
-c\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
over
\mathbb{C}

the differential equation (
1-1
) is satisfied by
the Gauß hypergeometric series

F(a,b,c\,;z)\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\left(a\mathrel{;}n\right)\left(b\mathrel{;}n\right)}{\left(c\mathrel{;}n\right)\left(1\mathrel{;}n\right)}z^{n},

where
\left(x\mathrel{;}n\right)=x(x+1)(x+2)\cdots(x+n-1)
.

In this paper, we show an analogous result in the case of genus
2
.
Let
C
be the normalization of the projective model of

C:\quad y^{2}=f(x):=x(x-1)(x-\lambda_{1})(x-\lambda_{2})(x-\lambda_{3})

with
\#\{0,1,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\}=5
.
Note that
C
is a nonsingular curve of genus 2.
A curve
C
is called
superspecial
if its Jacobian
{\rm Jac}(C)

is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves over an algebraically closed field. This is equivalent to that
the Cartier operator on
H^{0}(C,{\varOmega}_{C})
is zero (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 4.1]
). The matrix (called Cartier-Manin matrix) representing the operator with respect to the basis
\{dx/y,xdx/y\}
is described as

\begin{pmatrix}c_{p-1}&c_{p-2}\\
c_{2p-1}&c_{2p-2}\end{pmatrix},

where
c_{k}
is the
x^{k}
-coefficient of
f(x)^{(p-1)/2}
, see
[
5
, p. 79]
.
We shall regard
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}
as indeterminates
and consider
c_{ip-j}

as polynomials in
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
.
The space
V(c_{p-1},c_{p-2},c_{2p-1},c_{2p-2})
consisting of points
where
c_{ip-j}
vanish for all
i,j
with
1\leq i,j\leq 2

is called the
superspecial locus
, which is known to consist of finite points.
The aim of this paper is to prove the ideal generated by

c_{ip-j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
is of multiplicity one
at all point of
V(c_{p-1},c_{p-2},c_{2p-1},c_{2p-2})
.
To achieve this we use
Lauricella hypergeometric series of type (D) in three variables and the partial differential equations they satisfy.
Lauricella hypergeometric series of type (D) in three variables is
defined as follows (cf. Definition
2.1
):

\mathcal{F}(a,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},c\,;z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\coloneqq\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{n_{3}=0}^{\infty}A_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}z_{3}^{n_{3}},

(1-2)

where

A_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}:=\frac{\left(a\mathrel{;}\sum_{k=1}^{3}n_{k}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{3}\left(b_{k}\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)}{\left(c\mathrel{;}\sum_{k=1}^{3}n_{k}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{3}\left(1\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)}

(1-3)

with
a,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},c\in\mathbb{C},-c\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.

In this paper, the hypergeometric series is considered as an element of
the ring of formal power series in
z_{1},z_{2}
and
z_{3}
.
The hypergeometric series satisfies the partial
differential equations
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}w=0
for
\ell=1,2,3

and
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{k,\,\ell}}w=0
for
1\leq k<\ell\leq 3
, where

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{\ell}(1-z_{\ell})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{\ell}}^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq 3,\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}{z_{k}(1-z_{\ell})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{\ell}}\partial{z_{k}}}}

(1-4)

\displaystyle+\left(c-(a+b_{\ell}+1)z_{\ell}\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{\ell}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq 3,\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}{b_{\ell}z_{k}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}}-ab_{\ell}

and

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}:=(z_{\ell}-z_{m})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{\ell}\partial z_{m}}-b_{m}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{\ell}}+b_{\ell}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{m}}.

(1-5)

Let
j\in\{1,2\}
.
Let
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{(j)}}
be
the above partial differential operators
{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}

associated to

a=5/2-j
,
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=(1/2,1/2,1/2)
and
c=3-j
.
The first main theorem is

Theorem A
.

For every
i,j
with
1\leq i,j\leq 2
, the entry
c_{ip-j}
of the Cartier-Manin matrix
satisfies

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{(j)}}c_{ip-j}=0

for
\ell=1,2,3
and

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}c_{ip-j}=0

for
1\leq\ell<m\leq 3
.

As an application of this theorem and the contiguity relations obtained in Subsection
5.2
, we have the second theorem,
which can be regard as an analogous result in the genus-two case of Igusa’s result
[
3
]
in the genus-one case.
We call this theorem
the multiplicity-one theorem for the superspeciality of the curve
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-z_{1})(x-z_{2})(x-z_{3})
.

Theorem B
.

The scheme defined by the ideal generated by

c_{p-1},c_{2p-1},c_{p-2},c_{2p-2}

of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]
is reduced.
(This is equivalent to saying that the scheme is nonsingular.
It is well-known that the scheme is zero-dimensional.)

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we review some facts, which will be used in the latter sections.
In Section 3, we restate the main theorems.
In Section 4, we prove the first main theorem.
In Section 5, we prove the second main theorem. For this, we prove
some relations of
entries of Cartier-Manin matrix
with some partial differential operators.
This can be called contiguity relations.
This was another key ingredient of the proof of the second main theorem.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall Lauricella hypergeometric series
and partial differential equations they satisfy.
We also review the relation between the Cartier-Manin matrix of a hyperelliptic curve and the associated
Lauricella hypergeometric series. Finally we discuss
the superspeciality and the nonsingularity of curves of genus two.

2.1
Lauricella hypergeometric series

Lauricella hypergeometric series in three variables is defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

Consider an element
\mathcal{F}(a,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},c\,;z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
of
\mathbb{C}[\![z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]\!]

\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{3}=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(a\mathrel{;}\sum_{k=1}^{3}n_{k}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{3}\left(b_{k}\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)}{\left(c\mathrel{;}\sum_{k=1}^{3}n_{k}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{3}\left(1\mathrel{;}n_{k}\right)}z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}z_{3}^{n_{3}},

(2-6)

where
a,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},c\in\mathbb{C},-c\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
We call it Lauricella hypergeometric series in three variables
with respect to
(a,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},c)
.

Lauricella hypergeometric series satisfy the following partial differential equations,
see
E_{D}(a,(b),c)
in
[
6
, Table 3.2 in Section 3.5]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Lauricella hypergeometric series in three variables

satisfies

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}w=0

(2-7)

for
\ell=1,2,3
and

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}w=0

(2-8)

for
1\leq\ell<m\leq 3
,
where the partial differential operators
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
,
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{2}}
,
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{3}}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{1}}=

\displaystyle z_{1}(1-z_{1})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{1}}^{2}}+z_{2}(1-z_{1})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{1}}\partial{z_{2}}}+z_{3}(1-z_{1})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{1}}\partial{z_{3}}}

\displaystyle+\left(c-(a+b_{1}+1)z_{1}\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-b_{1}z_{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}-b_{1}z_{3}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{3}}-ab_{1},

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{2}}=

\displaystyle z_{1}(1-z_{2})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{1}}\partial{z_{2}}}+z_{2}(1-z_{2})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{2}}^{2}}+z_{3}(1-z_{2})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{2}}\partial{z_{3}}}

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{3}}=

\displaystyle z_{1}(1-z_{3})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{1}}\partial{z_{3}}}+z_{2}(1-z_{3})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{2}}\partial{z_{3}}}+z_{3}(1-z_{3})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{z_{3}}^{2}}

\displaystyle-b_{3}z_{1}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-b_{3}z_{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}+\left(c-(a+b_{3}+1)z_{3}\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{3}}-ab_{3},

and

\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell,\,m}}:=(z_{\ell}-z_{m})\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{\ell}\partial z_{m}}+b_{\ell}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{m}}-b_{m}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{\ell}}

(2-9)

for
1\leq\ell<m\leq 3
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
K
be any field.
The partial differential operators
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}
for
\ell=1,2,3

can be considered if
a,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},c\in K
.
Let

be any element of
K[\![z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]\!]

A direct computation shows that the

(2-10)

We call this the

for

and
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
.
The similar things also hold for

and
\mathop{\mathcal{D}_{3}}f

2.2
Cartier-Manin matrices
and hypergeometric series

Let
p

Let

A hyperelliptic curve of genus

Definition 2.4
.

For the hyperelliptic curve

Remark 2.5

In this paper, we consider the case of

2.3
Superspeciality implies nonsingularity

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>2
.
Let
C
be the curve

y^{2}=f(x):=x(x-1)(x-\lambda_{1})(x-\lambda_{2})(x-\lambda_{3})

for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\in K
.
Let
c_{m}
be the
x^{m}
-coefficient of
f(x)^{e}
with
e:=(p-1)/2
.

Proposition 2.10
.

If
C
is superspecial, more precisely its Cartier-Manin matrix

\begin{pmatrix}c_{p-1}&c_{p-2}\\
c_{2p-1}&c_{2p-2}\end{pmatrix}

is zero, then
we have
\lambda_{i}\neq 0,1
and
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
if
i\neq j

for
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}

5.1
The Jacobian criterion

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field.
Let
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Let
I
be an ideal of
R
such that
R/I
is an Artin ring
(i.e.,
I
is zero dimensional).

Definition 5.1
.

We say
I
is of
multiplicity-one
if
\dim_{k}(R/I)_{\mathfrak{P}}=1
for any
\mathfrak{P}
in
\mathrm{Spec}(R/I)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
is muplicplicity one if and only if for any
\mathfrak{P}
in
\mathrm{Spec}(R/I)

the Jacobian matrix
\left(\dfrac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)
at
\mathfrak{P}
is of rank
n
.

Proof.

Let
I=\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{i}

be the irredundant primary decomposition of
I
. Set
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}
.
Since
A/I
is an Artin ring,
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is a maximal ideal.
Hence we have
\mathfrak{p}_{i}+\mathfrak{p}_{j}=R
for
i\neq j
and therefore

\mathfrak{q}_{i}+\mathfrak{q}_{j}=R
. By the Chinese remainder theorem, we get
an isomorphism
\Psi:R/I\simeq\bigoplus_{i}R/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
.

We claim that
(R/I)_{\mathfrak{P}}\simeq R/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
for
i
with
\sqrt{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}=\mathfrak{p}
,
where
\mathfrak{p}
is the inverse image of
\mathfrak{P}
by
R\to R/I
.
Indeed
\Psi
sends
\mathfrak{P}
(resp.
S:=(R/I)\setminus\mathfrak{P}
) to the product of
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
(resp.
(R/\mathfrak{q}_{i})^{\times}
) and
R/\mathfrak{q}_{j}
for
j\neq i
.
Since the idempotent on
\bigoplus_{i}R/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
which is the identity on
R/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
and zero on
R/\mathfrak{q}_{j}
for
j\neq i

belongs to
S
, the localization
(R/I)_{\mathfrak{P}}
is equal to the localization

(R/\mathfrak{q}_{i})_{\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}_{i}}=R/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
.

Clearly
(R/I)_{\mathfrak{P}}\simeq R/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
is a one-dimensional
k
-vector space if and only if
\mathfrak{q}_{i}=\mathfrak{p}
.
This is equivalent to that
\mathrm{Spec}(R/I)
is nonsingular. The Jacobian criterion for the nonsingularity implies the desired assertion.
∎

Lemma 5.6

Intermediate dimensions of slices of compact sets
Nicolás E. Angelini, Ursula M. Molter

Abstract
θ \theta intermediate dimensions are a continuous family of dimensions that interpolate between Hausdorff and Box dimensions of fractal sets. In this paper we study the problem of the relationship between the dimension of a set E ⊂ ℝ d E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} and the dimension of the slices E ∩ V E\cap V from the point of view of intermediate dimensions. Here V ∈ G ⁡ ( d , m ) V\in G(d,m) , where G ⁡ ( d , m ) G(d,m) is the set of m − m- dimensional subspaces of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We obtain upper bounds analogous to those already known for Hausdorff dimension. In addition, we prove several corollaries referring to, among other things, the continuity of these dimensions at θ = 0 \theta=0 , a natural problem that arises when studying them. We also investigate which conditions are sufficient to obtain a lower bound that provides an equality for almost all slices. Finally, a new type of Frostman measures is introduced. These measures combine the results already known for intermediate dimensions and Frostman measures in the case of Hausdorff dimension.



1
Introduction

There are many theorems concerning the Hausdorff dimension of
slices
of Borel sets in Euclidean space, i.e. given
0\leq m\leq d-1
, let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an
m-
dimensional plane and let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, what we can say about the Hausdorff dimension of
A\cap V
?

This problem was initiated by Marstrand
[
9
]
in the case of lines
V\subset G(2,1)
and
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and generalized in
[
10
]
by Mattila to higher dimensions.

Precisely the following theorem holds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\leq s\leq d
and let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Borel set with
0<\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)<\infty
. Then for
\gamma_{d,m}
almost all
W\in G(n,d-m)
,

\mathcal{H}^{m}\left(\{a\in W^{\perp}\ :\ \dim_{\rm H}(A\cap\{W+a\})=s-m\}\right)>0

The proof can be found in Section 10 of
[
12
]
.

See
[
14
]
for more recent results and estimates of the dimension of the exceptional directions, or
[
13
]
for a recent survey on the Hausdorff dimension of intersections.

If we replace Hausdorff dimension with any other notion of dimension, results such as Theorem
1.1
are not necessarily true.

If we consider box-counting dimension, for example, in
[
8
, Theorem 10.3.2]
a set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is constructed, with
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,F=d
such that for all integers
1\leq k<d
, all
V\in G(d-k,d)
and all
v\in V
, the slice
F\cap(V^{\perp}+v)
consists of at most
k+1
points, and therefore has box-counting dimension 0.

The intermediate dimensions,
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
and
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
, were recently introduced in
[
7
]
, as a way to interpolate between Hausdorff and Box-counting dimension. (See
2.1
for the exact definition.) Those dimensions are very well studied in many works, such as
[
2
]
for Brownian motion and other images,
[
7
]
for projection theorems,
[
4
]
for elliptical polynomials spirals and many other interesting papers.

In this work we study the problem of how the intermediate dimension varies by taking
slices
of a set, this is, what can we say about
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\cap V
where
V
is an
m
dimensional affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a compact set.

It is not possible to obtain a general theorem such as
1.1
for the
\theta
intermediate dimension, as we show with the following example.

Example
. Let
W
be a line through the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
C_{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
the set obtained by rotating the sequence
\{1/n^{p}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to the origin of coordinates. If
p\in(0,1)
, by
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
we have

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{P}=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{P}=1+\frac{\theta(1-p)}{2p+\theta(1-p)}.

Hence, for all
a\in W^{\perp}
such that
a\neq 0
and all
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{P}-1=\frac{\theta(1-p)}{2p+\theta(1-p)}>0=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{P}\cap(W+a),

since for all
a\neq 0
,
C_{P}\cap(W+a)
is a finite collection of points.

Since (in the best case) we have that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\to\dim_{\rm H}E
as
\theta\to 0
, it is natural to ask about conditions on
E
that are necessary or sufficient to have results similarly to Theorem
1.1
that are valid for
\theta
intermediate dimensions.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the definitions and other skills that we will need later. In section
3
, we study an upper bound for the
\theta
intermediate dimension of slices, in Theorem
3.1
we obtain a general upper bound for the
\theta
intermediate dimension of
E\cap f^{-1}\{y\}
for certain functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, that include orthogonal projections, giving us the expected upper bound for the
\theta
dimension of the slices. We also obtain several corollaries, for example, the almost sure continuity in
\theta=0
for the slices if we suppose the continuity in
\theta=0
of the intermediate dimension of the original set. In section
4
we study sufficient conditions on the set to ensure a lower bound or in the best case, an equality. This leads us to study the existence of a measure that we call
Joint Frostman measure
, in the final section
5
.

Remark 2.2

In
[

More recently, in

Let

bounded and non empty,

and

S_{r,\theta}^{s}(E):=\inf\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{s}\ :\ E\subset\bigcup_{i}U_{i}\mbox{ with }r\leq|U_{i}|\leq r^{\theta}\mbox{ for all }i\right\}.

The kernels
\phi_{r,\theta}^{s}(x)
are defined as follows .

\phi_{r,\theta}^{s}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}1&si&|x|<r\\
\\
\left(\frac{r}{|x|}\right)^{s}&si&r\leq x<r^{\theta}\\
\\
0&si&x\geq r^{\theta}\end{array}\right.

and in
[
6
]
we have the next lemma.

Lemma 2.3

Let

be compact,
\theta\in(0,1]

, and let

S_{r,\theta}^{s}(E)\geq r^{s}\left[\iint\phi_{r,\theta}^{s}(x-y)d\mu(x)d\mu(y)\right]^{-1}.

From the definition is not hard to see that
\text{ if }\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}S_{r,\theta}^{s}(E)=0\text{ then }\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq s
and
\text{ if }\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}S_{r,\theta}^{s}(E)=0\text{ then }\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq s.

Furthermore, we have that

\text{ if }\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}S_{r,\theta}^{s}(E)>0\text{ then }\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\geq s

and
\text{ if }\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}S_{r,\theta}^{s}(E)>0\text{ then }\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\geq s.

5
On the existence of Joint Frostman measures

In this section our main interest is to find conditions on the set
E
such that there exist the measures of Definition
4.3
.
Theorem
5.2
will allow us to construct these measures.

In order to be able to construct them, we first need to define another notion of dimension, which gives us information about the distribution of the set when refining dyadic cubes.

Definition 5.1
.

We define the Dyadic dimension as follows

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{Q}}E=\sup\{\alpha:

\displaystyle\text{ there exist a constant }C>0\text{ and }N\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\text{ such that for all }N<n,\ \mathcal{N}_{n}(E)\geq C\,2^{\alpha}\}

where
\mathcal{N}_{n}(E)=\displaystyle\min_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}:Q\cap E\neq\emptyset}\#\{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{n+1}:\ Q^{\prime}\cap(E\cap Q)\neq\emptyset\}
.

This enables us to say something about the existence of Joint Frostman measures.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\theta>0
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set, such that
0<\dim_{\rm H}E<\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
and
0<\dim_{\mathcal{Q}}E:=\mathcal{D}(E)
. Then for each
t\in(\dim_{\rm H}E,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E)
and all
\alpha\in(0,\mathcal{D}(E))
, there exists
c>0
such that for all
\delta_{0}>0
exist
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
and a Radon measure
\mu_{\delta}
, supported on
E
, satisfying

\mu_{\delta}(B(x,r))\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}c(\delta^{1/\theta})^{t-\alpha}r^{\alpha}&if&r\in(0,\delta^{1/\theta})\\
cr^{t}&if&\delta\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]\end{array}\right.

(14)

for all
x\in E
.

Equivalently, if we have
\dim_{\rm H}E<t<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
, there exists
c>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
there exists a measure
\mu_{\delta}
supported on
E
satisfying (
14
).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{m}
(
m\geq 0
) the dyadic partition of
[0,1]^{d}
, i.e. ,
2^{dm}
pairwise disjoint half-open dyadic cubes of sidelength
2^{-m}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
E\subset[0,1]^{d}
and that
E
is not contained in any
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.

Let
0<\alpha<\mathcal{D}(E)
and let
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large such that for all
n>m^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{N}_{n}(E)\geq C2^{\alpha}
.

Let
\delta_{0}
be sufficiently small such that
\delta_{0}^{1/\theta}2^{m^{\prime}}<1
.

Let
t<\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
. From the definition of
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
we have that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a decreasing sequence
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\delta_{1}<\delta_{0}
and
\delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
when
k\rightarrow\infty
such that for all covers
\{U_{i}\}_{i}
of
E
with
\delta_{k}^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{t}>\varepsilon.

(15)

Given
k
, let
m\geq 0
be the unique integer satisfying
2^{-m-1}<\delta_{k}^{1/\theta}\leq 2^{-m}
.

The idea is to assign to each cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m}
that intersects
E
, a mass
\mu_{m}(Q)=2^{-mt}
and then distribute it uniformly into the cubes in
\mathcal{D}_{m+1}
that intersect
E
and so on.

For this, let
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
with
n\geq m+1
and let
Q^{*_{i}}
the unique cube in
\mathcal{D}_{n-i}
that contains
Q
. Define
\#\mathcal{Q}_{n}(Q)
as the number of cubes in
\mathcal{D}_{n}
that intersect
E\cap Q^{*_{1}}
. Let us denote
Q^{*_{0}}=Q
, and for
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
define
\displaystyle\Phi_{m+1}(Q)=\displaystyle\Pi_{i=0}^{n-(m+1)}\#\mathcal{Q}_{n-i}(Q^{*_{i}})
.

Basically for
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, the parameter
\Phi_{m+1}(Q)
is an idea of how much of the set
E\cap Q^{\prime}
, where
Q^{\prime}
is the unique cube
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{m}
, such that
Q\subset Q^{\prime}
, is contained in the different levels from
\mathcal{D}_{m}
until
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

With this in mind, we define a measure
\mu_{m}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for any
n\geq m+1
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}

\mu_{m}(Q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}2^{-mt}/\Phi_{m+1}(Q)&if&Q\cap E\neq\emptyset\\
0&if&Q\cap E=\emptyset.\end{array}\right.

For each
Q^{*}\in\mathcal{D}_{m}
that intersects
E
we have

\mu_{m}(Q^{*})=\displaystyle\sum_{\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m+1}:Q\subset Q^{*}\}}\mu_{m}(Q)=\Phi_{m+1}(Q)2^{-mt}/\Phi_{m+1}(Q)=2^{-mt}.

Using this and recalling that
Q^{*i}
is the unique cube in
\mathcal{D}_{n-i}
that contains
Q
, we have for any cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
(
n\geq m+1
):

\begin{split}\mu_{m}(Q)=\frac{2^{-m}}{\Phi_{m+1}(Q)}&=\frac{\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-m)})}{\Phi_{m+1}(Q)}\\
&=\frac{\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-m)})}{\Phi_{m+1}(Q)}\frac{\#\mathcal{Q}_{m}(Q^{*(n-m)})}{\#\mathcal{Q}_{m}(Q^{*(n-m)})}\\
&=\frac{\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-(m-1))})}{\Phi_{m}(Q)}.\end{split}

(16)

The next step is to modify the measure
\mu_{m}
to obtain a measure
\mu_{m-1}
.

For
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
with
n\geq m
let

\mu_{m-1}(Q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}2^{-(m-1)t}/\Phi_{m}(Q)&if&\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-(m-1))})>2^{-(m-1)t}\\
\mu_{m}(Q)&if&\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-(m-1))})\leq 2^{-(m-1)t}.\end{array}\right.

Again we have for
Q^{*}\in\mathcal{D}_{m-1}
that intersects
E
:

If
\mu_{m}(Q^{*})\leq 2^{-(m-1)t}
then

\mu_{m-1}(Q^{*})=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m}:Q\subset Q^{*}}\mu_{m-1}(Q)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m}:Q\subset Q^{*}}\mu_{m}(Q)=\mu_{m}(Q^{*})\leq 2^{-(m-1)t}.

And if
\mu_{m}(Q^{*})>2^{-(m-1)t}
then

\mu_{m-1}(Q^{*})=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m}:Q\subset Q^{*}}\mu_{m-1}(Q)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m}:Q\subset Q^{*}}2^{-(m-1)t}/\Phi_{m}(Q)=2^{-(m-1)t}.

Then for
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m-1}

\mu_{m-1}(Q)\leq 2^{-(m-1)t}.

In the same way, using (
16
), we have for
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
,
n\geq m
:

If
\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-(m-1))})\leq 2^{-(m-1)t}
then
\mu_{m-1}(Q)=\mu_{m}(Q)

and if
\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-(m-1))})>2^{-(m-1)t}

\mu_{m-1}(Q)=\frac{2^{-(m-1)t}}{\Phi_{m}(Q)}<\frac{\mu_{m}(Q^{*(n-(m-1))})}{\Phi_{m}(Q)}=\mu_{m}(Q),

and therefore

\mu_{m-1}(\cdot)\leq\mu_{m}(\cdot).

Proceeding inductively, given
\mu_{m-k}
construct a measure
\mu_{m-k-1}
such that for
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
with
n\geq m-k

\mu_{m-k-1}(Q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}2^{-(m-k-1)t}/\Phi_{m-k}(Q)&if&\mu_{m-k}(Q^{*(n-(m-k-1))})>2^{-(m-k-1)t}\\
\mu_{m-k}(Q)&if&\mu_{m-k}(Q^{*(n-(m-k-1))})\leq 2^{-(m-k-1)t}.\end{array}\right.

Terminate this process with
\mu_{m-\ell}
where
\ell
is the largest
integer satisfying
2^{-(m-l)}n^{1/2}\leq\delta
, so for each cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m-\ell}
we have

|Q|=2^{-(m-\ell)}n^{1/2}\leq\delta
.

By construction we have

\mu_{m-\ell}(Q)\leq 2^{-(m-i)t}\hskip 28.45274ptQ\in\mathcal{D}_{m-i},\ i=0,...,\ell

(17)

and

\mu_{m-\ell}(\cdot)\leq\mu_{m-\ell+1}(\cdot)\leq\dotsi\leq\mu_{m}(\cdot)

(18)

Further, if a cube
Q
satisfies an equality in (
17
) for
\mu_{m-i}
then either
Q
or
Q^{*1}
satisfies the equality for
\mu_{m-i-1}
.

Since all the dyadic cubes in
\mathcal{D}_{m}
satisfy
\mu_{m}(Q)=2^{-m}
, we have that for all
x\in E
, there exists
0\leq i\leq\ell
such that for some cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{m-i}
that contains
x
, (
17
) is an equality for
\mu_{m-\ell}
.

For each
x\in E
, choose the largest cube
Q
that contains
x
and satisfies the equality in
(
17
)
. This process yields a finite collection of cubes

which cover
E
and satisfy
\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|Q_{i}|\leq\delta
for

.

Now, using (
15
) we have

\mu_{m-\ell}(E)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mu_{m-\ell}(Q_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}|Q_{i}|^{t}d^{-t/2}>\varepsilon d^{-t/2}.

Define
\mu_{\delta_{k}}(\cdot)=\mu_{m-\ell}(E)^{-1}\mu_{m-\ell}(\cdot)
.

Finally, let
\sigma(\mathcal{D})
the
\sigma

\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{D}_{n}
, and then, if
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can extend
\mu_{\delta_{k}}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\mu_{\delta_{k}}(A)=\inf\left\{\mu_{\delta_{k}}(B):A\subset B\in\sigma(\mathcal{D})\right\}
.

Now we will see that

satisfies the inequality (
14
).

Let
x\in E
,
\delta_{k}^{1/\theta}\leq r\leq\delta
, then if
k
is chosen as the unique integer satisfying

Then, again by (

\mu_{\delta_{k}}(B(x,r))\leq c_{d}\mu_{m-l}(E)^{-1}2^{-(m-k)t}\leq c_{d}\varepsilon^{-1}d^{t/2}2^{t}r^{t}.

Therefore we have that there exists

\mu_{\delta_{k}}(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{t}.

Now, by our choice of

\begin{split}\mu_{\delta_{k}}(B(x,r))\leq c_{d}\mu_{\delta_{k}}(Q_{n^{\prime}})&\leq c_{d}\frac{\mu_{m}(Q_{n^{\prime}}^{*(n^{\prime}-m)})}{\Phi_{m+1}(Q_{n^{\prime}})}\\

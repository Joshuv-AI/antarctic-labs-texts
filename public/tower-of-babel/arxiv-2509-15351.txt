Diameter bounds for finite simple Lie algebras Thanks: This work has been supported by the Slovenian Research Agency program P1-0222 and grants J1-50001, J1-4351, J1-3004, N1-0217.
Marco Barbieri, Urban Jezernik, Matevž Miščič

Abstract
. We prove strong and explicit diameter bounds for finite simple Lie algebras, which parallel Babai’s conjecture for finite simple groups. Specifically, we show that any nonabelian finite simple Lie algebra 𝔤 \g over 𝐅 p \mathbf{F}_{p} has diameter O ⁡ ( ( log ⁡ | 𝔤 | ) D ) O((\log{\lvert\g\rvert})^{D}) for D ≈ 3.11 D\approx 3.11 with respect to any generating set. For absolutely simple classical Lie algebras over 𝐅 p \mathbf{F}_{p} , we establish the sharper bound O ⁡ ( log ⁡ | 𝔤 | ) O(\log{\lvert\g\rvert}) when the Lie type is fixed and the generators are chosen uniformly at random.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Diameters of finite simple groups

Let
G
be a group generated by a set
A
. The
diameter
of
G
with respect to
A
, denoted
\diam(G,A)
, is the smallest integer
k
such that every element of
G
can be written as a product of at most
k
elements from
A
. In effect, it measures how quickly
A
generates
G
. Diameters have been widely studied in finite simple groups, led by the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(rapid generation of groups – Babai’s conjecture
[
BS92
]
)
.

There exist absolute constants
C,D
1
1

1

A stronger version of the conjecture predicts that one might take
D=2
.
so that the following holds. Let
G
be a nonabelian finite simple group and
A
any generating set of
G
. Then
\diam(G,A)\leq C(\log\lvert G\rvert)^{D}
.

The conjecture is open in general. Being asymptotic, it reduces to proving the existence of such constants
C,D
for the infinite families of alternating groups
A_{n}
(as
n\to\infty
) and finite simple groups of Lie type such as
\PSL_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
(as
n\to\infty
and/or
q\to\infty
). The conjecture is known to hold in the important special case of finite simple groups of fixed Lie type (for example
\PSL_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
with
n
fixed and
q\to\infty
)
[
Hel08
,
BGT11
,
PS16
,
BDH24
]
. What all these proofs have in common is the use of a
product theorem
, which states that generating sets of finite simple groups of fixed Lie type grow uniformly under multiplication (unless they are already very large).

1.2.
Diameters of finite simple Lie algebras

The same game can be played in Lie algebras. Let
\gfrak
be a Lie algebra over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
. For subsets
X,Y\subseteq\gfrak
write

X+Y=\{x+y\mid x\in X,\ y\in Y\}\quad\text{and}\quad[X,Y]=\{[x,y]\mid x\in X,\ y\in Y\}.

Suppose that
\gfrak
is generated as a Lie algebra by a set
A
. Let

A^{1}=\{0\}\cup A,\qquad A^{k}=\bigcup_{0<j<k}\left((A^{j}+A^{k-j})\cup[A^{j},A^{k-j}]\right)\quad\text{for }k\geq 2.

The
diameter
of
\gfrak
with respect to
A
, denoted
\diam(\gfrak,A)
, is the least
k
such that
A^{k}=\gfrak
. We can now state the linear version of Babai’s conjecture as follows.

Conjecture 1.2

(rapid generation of Lie algebras)
.

There exist absolute constants
C,D
so that the following holds. Let
\gfrak
be a nonabelian finite simple Lie algebra over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
and
A
any generating set of
\gfrak
. Then
\diam(\gfrak,A)\leq C(\log\lvert\gfrak\rvert)^{D}
.

How are the two conjectures related? Classical Lie groups are intimately connected to their Lie algebras. This correspondence is facilitated by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, which behaves well over local fields but much less so over finite fields
\mathbf{F}_{p}
.
2
2

2

In fact, a correspondence between finite
p
-groups and finite Lie algebras over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
exists when the groups are nilpotent of class less than
p
, known as the Lazard correspondence
[
Khu98
, Chapter 10]
.
While an
\exp
–
\log
correspondence exists at the level of generators, there is no global link between finite simple groups of Lie type and finite simple Lie algebras over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
. Nevertheless, studying the linear Lie algebra setting captures aspects of the complexity present in the group-theoretic problem.

By a recent result of Dona
[
Don23
]
, rapid generation holds for classical Lie algebras of fixed Lie type (as for groups) that are split over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
. These are exactly the Lie algebras obtained from complex simple Lie algebras via base change to
\mathbf{F}_{p}
.
3
3

3

There are more simple algebras over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
than just the classical ones and their forms. See
Section

3
for details over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic.
The constant
D
in rapid generation can be taken as
O(\dim(\liealg)^{2}\log\dim(\liealg))
, remaining fixed for a given Lie type. The proof in fact uses methods from the group case (in particular, a
sum-bracket theorem
), but in a simplified, linear form.

1.3.
Contributions

In this paper, we reuse methods developed by Dona and build on them to fully prove linear Babai’s conjecture with explicit
D
. We study classical Lie algebras in detail and provide even stronger diameter bounds when the Lie type is fixed and generators are chosen at random.

1.3.1.
Rapid generation of Lie algebras

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
D=\log 2/\log(5/4)
. For any
\epsilon>0
there is a constant
C
such that the following holds. Let
\gfrak
be a nonabelian finite simple Lie algebra over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
and
A
any generating set of
\gfrak
. Then

\diam(\liealg,A)\leq C(\log\lvert\liealg\rvert)^{D+\epsilon}.

Notably, finite simple Lie algebras are not fully classified, unlike finite simple groups. Our argument bypasses this by relying only on simplicity. Moreover, the proof yields the more explicit bound
O_{\epsilon}\left((\log p)^{D+\epsilon}(\dim\g)^{2}\right)
. When
p
is fixed, this becomes

O_{p}\left((\log\lvert\g\rvert)^{2}\right)
, matching the stronger version of Babai’s conjecture for finite simple groups. In general, our exponent
D\approx 3.11
is slightly larger.

The method behind the result incorporates ideas from Dona’s paper, though our implementation differs. To outline the main steps, define for any subset
X\subseteq\g
,

\ell(X)=\max\{\lvert X\cap L\rvert\mid L\text{ is a one-dimensional subspace of }\g\}.

Thus,
\ell(X)
is the maximum number of elements from
X
on any line in
\g
. We first show that if a generating set
A
of
\gfrak
satisfies
\ell(A^{k})=p
for some
k
, then the entire Lie algebra can be covered in a modest number of extra steps.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\gfrak
be a nonabelian finite simple Lie algebra over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
and
A
any generating set of
\gfrak
. Assume that
\ell(A^{k})=p
for some
k
. Then
\diam(\gfrak,A)\leq k\dim\gfrak+(\dim\gfrak)^{2}
.

This proves rapid generation for Lie algebras when
p
is fixed. Starting from a generating set
A
, we have
\ell(A^{p})=p
, so
\diam(\gfrak,A)\leq O_{p}\left((\dim\gfrak)^{2}\right)=O_{p}\left((\log\lvert\gfrak\rvert)^{2}\right)
.
4
4

4

For finite simple groups, rapid generation in groups of Lie type over bounded fields and high rank is considerably more challenging and is only known to hold for random generators
[
EJ22
]
.

To cover the case when
p
varies, we show that
\ell(A^{k})=p
(or is at least sufficiently large) can be achieved with a small value of
k
. We do this by proving a
product theorem in lines
: if a generating set
A
does not yield a large enough
\ell(A^{k})
, then a few extra steps induce uniform growth in
\ell(A^{k})
. Here is the precise statement.

Theorem 1.5

(product theorem in lines)
.

For every
\epsilon>0
there is a constant
C>0
such that for any generating set
A
of any nonabelian finite simple Lie algebra
\g
over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
and any
k\geq C
, we have

\ell(A^{2k+\dim\gfrak})\geq\ell(A^{k})^{5/4-\epsilon}\quad\text{or}\quad\ell(A^{C(2k+\dim\gfrak)})=p.

One-dimensional growth as it appears here was already leveraged by Dona to prove the sum-product theorem in Lie algebras. We extract key elements of their argument and bootstrap it to attain faster growth as given by the theorem, and this eventually leads to the diameter bounds stated above. The constants
2
and
5/4
in the theorem arise from applying the sum-product theorem in finite fields
[
MS23
]
.
5
5

5

Having a stronger sum-product theorem would improve the value of
D
. See Remark
2.5
.

1.3.2.
Extremely rapid random generation of classical Lie algebras

We study classical Lie algebras over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
in detail. These are
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-forms of complex simple Lie algebras base changed to the algebraic closure of
\mathbf{F}_{p}
, and are classified as either

split:

obtained from complex simple Lie algebras by base change to
\mathbf{F}_{p}
,
e.g.
,
\slfrak_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
,

non-split:

arising from symmetries in the Dynkin diagram,
e.g.
,
\sufrak_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}})
.

We prove that both types exhibit extremely rapid random generation, although in the non-split case we must exclude a (possibly empty) density
0
subset of primes.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\gfrak
be an
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-form of a classical Lie algebra. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the Lie type of
\gfrak
, such that for a uniformly random pair of elements
X,Y\in\gfrak
, we have

\diam(\gfrak,\{X,Y\})\leq C\log\lvert\gfrak\rvert

with probability tending to
1
as
p
tends to infinity, excluding a density
0
subset of primes if
\gfrak
is non-split.

A specific instance of this phenomenon was observed for the Lie algebra
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
in
[
JM25
]
. Using different methods, we are able to handle any fixed Lie type.
6
6

6

Extremely rapid random generation also holds for finite simple groups of fixed Lie type, but the argument there is more conceptual: such groups are expanders with high probability
[
BGGT15
]
.
Our approach involves constructing a characteristic
0
Lie algebra that serves as a covering object for all Lie algebras
\gfrak
over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
of the same type. We show that this covering object contains a generating pair whose Lie operations exhibit exponential growth. Projecting this pair down to each
\gfrak
, we obtain elements that quickly generate a large portion of
\gfrak
. Applying the Schwartz-Zippel lemma, we deduce that the same holds for generic pairs. In the non-split case, several covering objects are required for number-theoretic reasons. We construct these independently to cover almost all primes using the Chebotarev density theorem.

2.
Diameters of finite simple Lie algebras

2.1.
Starting with a line

Following
[
Don23
]
, we define, for subsets
X,Y\subseteq\gfrak
,
towers
of Lie brackets recursively as

\tower_{0}(X)=\tower_{0}(X,Y)=\{0\},\quad\tower_{1}(X)=\tower_{1}(X,Y)=X,

and for
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\tower_{k}(X)

\displaystyle=[X,\tower_{k-1}]\cup[\tower_{k-1},X],

\displaystyle\tower_{k}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=[Y,\tower_{k-1}(X)]\cup[\tower_{k-1}(X),Y]\cup[X,\tower_{k-1}(X,Y)]\cup[\tower_{k-1}(X,Y),X].

If
Y=\{y\}
is a singleton, we write
\tower_{k}(X,y)
instead of
\tower_{k}(X,Y)
. We also define

\tower_{\leq k}(X)=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\tower_{j}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad\tower_{\leq k}(X,Y)=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\tower_{j}(X,Y).

Towers built over generating sets quickly produce spanning sets of Lie algebras.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Don23
]
, Proposition 3.3)
.

Let
A
be a generating set of a Lie algebra
\gfrak
over a field
K
of dimension
d
. Then for any
k\in\mathbf{N}
the set
\tower_{\leq k}(A)
spans a vector subspace of
\gfrak
of dimension at least
\min\{d,k\}
.

We now show that for any generating set
A
and any nonzero element
b\in\gfrak
, the relative tower
\tower_{\leq d}(A,b)
spans the entire Lie algebra. While
[
Don23
, Corollary 3.4]
established this result for
\tower_{\leq 2d}(A,b)
, it is noted there that the bound can be refined to
d
. Below, we provide this refinement, although it is not essential for our argument.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\liealg
be a Lie algebra over a field
K
, let
A\subseteq\liealg
be a subset of
\liealg
and let
b\in\liealg
be an element of
\liealg
. Then for any
m,n\in\mathbf{N}
we have

[\tower_{m}(A,b),\tower_{n}(A)]\subseteq\lin[A,\tower_{m+n-1}(A,b)].

Proof.

Induction on
n
. For
n=1
the statement is trivial. Let
s\in\tower_{m}(A,b)
and
t\in\tower_{n+1}(A)
. Then up to a sign
t=[a,t^{\prime}]
for some
a\in A
and
t^{\prime}\in\tower_{n}(A)
. The Jacobi identity gives

[s,t]=[s,[a,t^{\prime}]]=[[s,a],t^{\prime}]+[a,[s,t^{\prime}]].

By the induction hypothesis we have
[[s,a],t^{\prime}]\in\lin[A,\tower_{m+n}(A,b)]
and
[s,t^{\prime}]\in\lin[A,\tower_{m+n-1}(A,b)]\subseteq\lin\tower_{m+n}(A,b)
. Thus we get

[s,t]\in\lin[A,\tower_{m+n}(A,b)]

and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra over a field
K
of dimension
d
. Let
A
be a generating set of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
b\in\liealg
be a nonzero element. Then the set
\tower_{\leq k}(A,b)
spans a vector subspace of
\liealg
of dimension at least
k
for any
k\leq d
. In particular, the set
\tower_{\leq d}(A,b)
spans
\liealg
.

Proof.

Define
V_{k}=\lin(\tower_{\leq k}(A,b))
for any
k\in\mathbf{N}
. Since
b\neq 0
, we have
\dim V_{1}=1
, so it is enough to show that
\dim V_{k+1}\geq\min\{d,\dim V_{k}+1\}
for any
k\in\mathbf{N}
.

Assume that
\dim V_{k+1}<\dim V_{k}+1
. Then
V_{k+1}=V_{k}
. Now take any element
v\in\tower_{\leq k+2}(A,b)
. Up to a sign we either have
v=[a,t]
for some
a\in A
and
t\in\tower_{\leq k+1}(A,b)
, or
v=[b,t^{\prime}]
for some
t^{\prime}\in\tower_{\leq k+1}(A)
. In the former case we have
t\in V_{k+1}=V_{k}
and thus
v\in[a,V_{k}]\subseteq V_{k+1}=V_{k}
. In the latter case we use Lemma
2.2
to get

v\in\lin[A,\tower_{\leq k+1}(A,b)]\subseteq\lin[A,V_{k+1}]=\lin[A,V_{k}]\subseteq V_{k+1}=V_{k}.

Therefore
V_{k+2}=V_{k}
and thus
V_{l}=V_{k}
for any
l\geq k
. It now follows by
Proposition

2.1
and
Lemma

2.2
that

\displaystyle[\liealg,V_{k}]

\displaystyle\subseteq\lin[\tower_{\leq d}(A),\tower_{\leq k}(A,b)]\subseteq\lin[A,\tower_{\leq d+k-1}(A,b)]

\displaystyle\subseteq\lin\tower_{\leq d+k}(A,b)=V_{d+k}=V_{k},

which implies that
V_{k}
is an ideal of
\liealg
. By simplicity of
\liealg
we obtain
V_{k}=\liealg
, hence
\dim V_{k}=d
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section, which is that of transporting a line covered by
A^{k}
to independent directions and thus covering the whole Lie algebra.

Proof of
Proposition

1.4
.

Suppose that
A^{k}
contains a line spanned by a vector
v\in\gfrak
. By Proposition
2.3
, the Lie algebra
\liealg
has a basis consisting of vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
, where
v_{j}\in\tower_{\leq j}(A,v)\subseteq A^{j}
. Take any element
u=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}v_{j}
from
\liealg
. For any
j
we can write

v_{j}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{l},v,a_{l+1},\ldots,a_{j^{\prime}-1}]\quad\text{for some }a_{i}\in A,\ j^{\prime}\leq j

and since
\alpha_{j}v\in A^{k}
we have
\alpha_{j}v_{j}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{l},\alpha_{j}v,a_{l+1},\ldots,a_{j^{\prime}-1}]\in A^{k+j-1}
. It then follows that
u\in A^{kd+d(d-1)/2}
.
∎

2.2.
Covering a line

Our objective now is to show that we can achieve that
\ell(A^{k})
is large with a reasonably small
k
. The main input driving growth is the sum-product theorem for finite fields in the following form.

Theorem 2.4
.

For every
\epsilon>0
and for every subset
X\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{p}
there are constants
C,c>0
so that:

1.

[
MS23
]
If
C\leq\lvert X\rvert\leq cp^{1/2}
, then
\max\{\lvert X+X\rvert,\lvert XX\rvert\}\geq\lvert X\rvert^{5/4-\epsilon}
.

2.

[
RNRS16
, Corollary 4]
If
\lvert X\rvert>p^{4/7}
, then
\lvert XX+XX+XX\rvert\geq cp
.

We first prove the product theorem in lines. The argument is similar to the one in
[
Don23
, Proof of Theorem 1.1, Step 2]
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

Let
A
be a generating set of
\gfrak
, let
\epsilon>0
and let
k\in\mathbf{N}
. Take
v\in\gfrak
to be a nonzero element such that the maximum
\ell(A^{k})
is attained at the line spanned by
v
,
i.e.
, the set
X=\{\alpha\in\mathbf{F}_{p}\mid\alpha v\in A^{k}\}
has size
\lvert X\rvert=\ell(A^{k})
. Let
C,c>0
be the constants from the sum-product theorem above.

The set
A^{2k}
contains the set
(X+X)v
. Let
u\in\gfrak
be any nonzero element such that
[v,u]\neq 0
(such an element exists since
\gfrak
is centerless). By Proposition
2.3
, the element
u
is a linear combination of elements from
\tower_{\leq\dim\gfrak}(A,v)
, so by bilinearity of the Lie bracket we can assume that
u\in\tower_{\leq\dim\gfrak}(A,v)
. Thus we can write

u=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{j},v,a_{j+1},\ldots,a_{d^{\prime}-1}]\quad\text{for some }a_{i}\in A,\ d^{\prime}\leq\dim\gfrak.

and hence

(XX)[v,u]=[Xv,[a_{1},\ldots,a_{j},Xv,a_{j+1},\ldots,a_{d^{\prime}-1}]]\subseteq A^{2k+\dim\gfrak}.

Therefore the set
A^{2k+\dim\gfrak}
contains both
(X+X)v
and
(XX)[v,u]
, hence

\ell(A^{2k+\dim\gfrak})\geq\max\{\lvert X+X\rvert,\lvert XX\rvert\}.

We now invoke the sum-product theorem above. Since
\lvert X\rvert\geq k
, taking
k>C
ensures that
\lvert X\rvert>C
. If
\lvert X\rvert\leq cp^{1/2}
, then we are in the regime of the first part of the sum-product theorem, and we obtain
\ell(A^{2k+\dim\gfrak})\geq\lvert X\rvert^{5/4-\epsilon}
, as desired. Suppose now that
\lvert X\rvert>cp^{1/2}
. Applying the sum-product theorem to a subset of
X
of size
\lfloor cp^{1/2}\rfloor
, we get
\ell(A^{2k+\dim\gfrak})\geq c^{5/4-\epsilon}p^{5/8-\epsilon}>p^{4/7}
for sufficiently large
p
(depending only on
\epsilon
). It now follows from the second part of the sum-product theorem that
\ell(A^{6(2k+\dim\gfrak)})\geq cp
. The Cauchy-Davenport theorem
[
TV06
, Theorem 5.4]
implies that
\ell(A^{C^{\prime}(2k+\dim\gfrak)})=p
for some constant
C^{\prime}
depending only on
\epsilon
, and we are done.
∎

Let us now show how this phenomenon can be iterated to achieve a large enough
\ell(A^{k})
and obtain the stated diameter bound.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\epsilon>0
, let
d
be the dimension of
\liealg
over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
, and set
\alpha=\lceil\max\{1/\epsilon,C,3\}\rceil
, where
C
is the constant appearing in
Theorem

1.5
. Note that for every
h\geq\alpha d
we have
\lfloor(2+\epsilon)h\rfloor\geq 2h+d
. Thus, the product theorem in lines gives

\ell(A^{\lfloor(2+\epsilon)h\rfloor})\geq\ell(A^{h})^{5/4-\epsilon}\quad\text{or}\quad\ell(A^{C\lfloor(2+\epsilon)h\rfloor})=p.

We use the above
k
times with
h=\alpha d
,
\lfloor(2+\epsilon)\alpha d\rfloor
,
\lfloor(2+\epsilon)^{2}\alpha d\rfloor
,
etc
. Supposing that after
k
steps, we have
\ell(A^{C\lfloor(2+\epsilon)^{k}\alpha d\rfloor})<p
, then we must be in the regime of the first case each time, and hence, as
\ell(A^{\alpha d})\geq\alpha d
, we obtain

\ell(A^{\lfloor(2+\epsilon)^{k}\alpha d\rfloor})\geq(\alpha d)^{(5/4-\epsilon)^{k}}.

Taking
k=\lceil\log\log(p)/\log(5/4-\epsilon)\rceil
, the right hand side exceeds
p
. Therefore, we must have
\ell(A^{C\lfloor(2+\epsilon)^{k}\alpha d\rfloor})=p
for this value of
k
.
Proposition

1.4
now implies

\diam(\gfrak,A)\leq C\lfloor(2+\epsilon)^{k}\alpha d\rfloor d+d^{2}=O_{\epsilon}\left(d^{2}(\log p)^{\log(2+\epsilon)/\log(5/4-\epsilon)}\right).

By taking
\epsilon
small, the exponent approaches
\log 2/\log(5/4)
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

If a stronger sum-product theorem than
Theorem

2.4
were available, the exponent
D
in
Theorem

1.3
could be improved. The sum-product conjecture of Erdős
[
Erd76
]
predicts that for all
\epsilon>0
and all sufficiently large finite sets
X\subseteq\mathbf{N}
, we have
\max\{\lvert X+X\rvert,\lvert XX\rvert\}\gg\lvert X\rvert^{2-\epsilon}
. If an analogous statement were to hold in
\mathbf{F}_{p}
for all sets
X\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{p}
with
\lvert X\rvert<p^{c}
for some constant
c=c(\epsilon)>0
, as is widely expected, then the same argument as above would yield a diameter bound of
O_{\epsilon}((\dim\gfrak)^{2}(\log p)^{1+\epsilon})
.
7
7

7

Such a bound strikingly matches known and prediced results regarding Babai’s conjecture for finite simple groups. Diameters are
O(\log p)
in
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
for a set of primes of density
1

[
BG10
]
, whereas a quadratic bound in
n
is required and expected in
\Alt(n)
.
Since our proof relies on
Proposition

1.4
, it cannot produce a bound that is subquadratic in
\dim\gfrak
. In fact, even a weaker sum-product bound with
2-\epsilon
replaced by
\sqrt{2}
would improve our diameter exponent to
D=2
. Of course, we believe that the diameter bound should hold with this exponent, and we are not aware of any counterexamples that even
D=1
might suffice. Below are two examples that illustrate how such extremely rapid generation can be achieved in some natural situations.

Example 2.6
.

First, consider
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
with its standard generators
e
and
f
. One can generate the entire one-dimensional subspace spanned by
e
in
O(\log p)
steps: the operations
\ad_{[e,f]}
and adding
e
act on this line as multiplication by
2
and translation by
1
, respectively. This is much faster than the general bound coming from the product theorem in lines, and since the dimension is fixed, Proposition
1.4
then yields extremely rapid generation. We show in the following sections that this phenomenon is generic for classical Lie algebras of fixed type.

As a second, more intricate example with unbounded dimension, consider the Witt algebra

The
Witt algebra

W(p)
is a
p
-dimensional simple Lie algebra over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
, generated by
e_{-1},\dots,e_{p-2}
with Lie bracket
[e_{i},e_{j}]=(j-i)e_{i+j}

-1\leq i+j\leq p-2

otherwise.

W(p)
with standard generators
e_{-1}
and
e_{2}
. The same method fills each line spanned by
e_{i}
for

in
O(\log p)
steps. Now, any element of the form
\sum_{j=2}^{k}\alpha_{j}e_{j}
can be produced in

Remark 2.7
.

The same arguments used to establish diameter bounds for nonabelian finite simple Lie algebras apply equally to finite-dimensional simple associative algebras over
\mathbf{F}_{p}

\mathbf{F}_{p}

3.
Classical finite Lie algebras

In this section, we provide a detailed overview of the classical Lie algebras over finite fields, with particular emphasis on their forms over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
.

3.1.
Classical Lie algebras

The finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras over the complex numbers
\mathbf{C}
are well-known and they are classified into the following families:

\slfrak_{n}(\mathbf{C}),\ \sofrak_{2n}(\mathbf{C}),\ \spfrak_{2n}(\mathbf{C}),\ \sofrak_{2n+1}(\mathbf{C}),\ \gfrak_{2}(\mathbf{C}),\ \ffrak_{4}(\mathbf{C}),\ \efrak_{6}(\mathbf{C}),\ \efrak_{7}(\mathbf{C}),\ \efrak_{8}(\mathbf{C}).

Each family corresponds to a root system
\Phi
, which can be represented combinatorially by a Dynkin diagram
D
.

Analogues of these Lie algebras exist over any field (including fields of positive characteristic), and there is a unified process for constructing them. We begin with the complex Lie algebra
\gfrak(\mathbf{C})
and its
Chevalley basis

\mathcal{B}

[
FH13
]
, which consists of

\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}\cup\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\},

where
\Delta
is the set of
simple roots
, also referred to as a
base
for
\Phi
. The key property of Chevalley bases is that all the structure constants of the Lie algebra
\gfrak(\mathbf{C})
with respect to this basis are integers. Consequently, the lattice

\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})=\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle_{\mathbf{Z}}\subseteq\gfrak(\mathbf{C})

spanned by the Chevalley basis also has all its structure constants with respect to
\mathcal{B}
which are integers. Moreover, this lattice is independent of the choice of
\Delta
and is closed under the Lie bracket. Using it, we can define, for any field
K
with a morphism
\mathbf{Z}\to K
(for example an extension of
\mathbf{F}_{p}
or
\mathbf{Q}
), the Lie algebra

\gfrak(K)=\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}K.

When the field
K
is algebraically closed and of positive characteristic, all such algebras
\gfrak(K)
are collectively referred to as
classical Lie algebras
.

There are, however, more finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic than just the classical ones (factored by their centre).
Filtered Lie algebras of Cartan type
are certain deformations of finite dimensional graded Lie algebras of Cartan type. The most well-known example of these is the Witt algebra
W(p)
that has no finite dimensional analogue in characteristic
0
. In the low characteristic
p=5
, there is another series of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras called
Melikian Lie algebras
. The classification theorem of Block-Wilson-Strade-Premet
[
Str04
]
states that these Lie algebras are the only finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras provided that
p>3
.

3.2.
Forms of classical Lie algebras over finite fields

The finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras
\gfrak
over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
are not fully classified. However, we can describe the
absolutely simple
Lie algebras,
i.e.
, those which remain simple after extension of scalars to the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbf{F}_{p}}
. Such Lie algebras are called
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-forms
of the simple Lie algebras listed above. In this paper, we focus exclusively on forms of the classical Lie algebras in positive characteristic, and in particular we do not say anything about Lie algebras of Cartan type (see
Remark

3.8
).

A form of
\gfrak(\overline{\mathbf{F}_{p}})
is said to be
split
if it is isomorphic as an
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-algebra to
\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p})
, constructed via the Chevalley basis as above. For some classical root systems, there also exist non-split forms, which arise from automorphisms of the corresponding Dynkin diagrams. All forms (both split and non-split) can be constructed using a Galois descent technique starting from
\gfrak(\overline{\mathbf{F}_{p}})
, as explained in
[
Sel12
, Chapter IV]
.

For our purposes, we require an analogue of the Chevalley basis for the non-split
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-forms. As such a basis does not appear to be readily available in the literature, we follow the original construction of Steinberg
[
Ste59
]
and provide all the necessary details here.

3.2.1.
Steinberg’s construction

Let
\gfrak(\mathbf{C})
be a classical Lie algebra, and let
D
be its Dynkin diagram. Suppose that
D
admits a symmetry, which we denote by
\vartheta
. The only possibilities in which
\vartheta
is nontrivial are the following.

•

The type is
A_{n}
(
n\geq 2
), and
\vartheta
is the horizontal flip of
D
that swaps the nodes
i
and
n+1-i
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

•

The type is
D_{n}
(
n\geq 4
), and
\vartheta
is the vertical flip of
D
that swaps the nodes
n
and
n+1
.

•

The type is
D_{4}
, and
\vartheta
is the automorphism of
D
that cyclically permutes the nodes
1,2,3
(or in reverse order) and fixes the node
4
.

•

The type is
E_{6}
, and
\vartheta
is the vertical flip of
D
that swaps the nodes
1
and
6
, and the nodes
2
and
5
, while fixing the nodes
3
and
4
.

In all these cases,
\vartheta
defines an automorphism of
\gfrak(\mathbf{C})
that preserves the lattice
\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})
with the property that, for every element of the Chevalley basis,

\vartheta(h_{\alpha})=h_{\vartheta\alpha}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\vartheta(e_{\alpha})=\pm e_{\vartheta\alpha},

where the appropriate sign for

is given in
[

, Lemma 3.2 and Section 10]
. In particular, if

is simple, then

. The automorphism

thus induces an automorphism of the Lie ring

.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\gfrak=\slfrak_{3}
. The basis of the root system consists of
\alpha
,
\beta
with corresponding root vectors
E_{12}
,
E_{23}
, and the highest root vector
E_{13}=[E_{12},E_{23}]
. The automorphism
\vartheta
of the Dynkin diagram swaps the two basis elements. We have
\vartheta(E_{13})=\vartheta([E_{12},E_{23}])=-E_{13}
. On the other hand,
\vartheta
fixes the Cartan subalgebra setwise.

Now let
K
be any field with an extension
E
of degree
\lvert\vartheta\rvert
, the order of
\vartheta
(note that
\lvert\vartheta\rvert=1,2,3
). Let
\sigma
be an element of

of order
\lvert\vartheta\rvert

is a semilinear automorphism of the Lie algebra
\gfrak(E)

is a simple Lie algebra over

, the Lie algebras constructed in this way exhaust all isomorphism classes of non-split

-forms (and, more generally, all non-split forms over any finite field). See, for instance,
[
Sel12
, Chapter IV.6]
.

3.2.2.
Characteristic
0

Let
\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})^{\Theta}
be an
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-form of a classical Lie algebra, where

. By construction, this Lie algebra comes equipped with a characteristic
0
Lie algebra
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
, where
E
is a degree
d
extension of
\mathbf{Q}
. We call this a
\mathbf{Q}
-covering Lie algebra
of
\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})^{\Theta}
. It is itself a
\mathbf{Q}
-form of the Lie algebra
\gfrak(\mathbf{C})
, and it contains the Lie ring
\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\Theta}
, referred to as the corresponding

, where
\mathcal{O}_{E}
is the ring of integers of the number field
E
. We have a natural surjection

\pi\colon\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})\to\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})

by reducing to
\mathcal{O}_{E}/p\cong\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}}
. The map
\pi
is equivariant with respect to the action of
\Theta
, so it induces a natural map
\pi^{\Theta}
on the level of fixed-points.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
p>d
. Then
\pi^{\Theta}\colon\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\Theta}\to\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})^{\Theta}
is a surjection.

Proof.

Let
x\in\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})^{\Theta}
. Let
\tilde{x}
be a lift of
x
to
\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{E}
. This element might not be fixed under
\Theta
, but we can average it over the orbit of
\Theta
to obtain the element
y=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Theta^{i}(\tilde{x})
. The element
y
is fixed under
\Theta
, and it reduces to
dx\in\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})
modulo
p
. Since
p>d
, we thus obtain
x\in\image(\pi^{\Theta})
.
∎

The averaging process over the
\langle\Theta\rangle
-orbits described in the proof above is a key step in constructing a basis for the
\mathbf{Q}
-covering Lie algebra

that is analogous to the Chevalley basis in the split case. For each vector

x^{\langle\Theta\rangle}=\sum_{t\in\langle\Theta\rangle}x^{t}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
E
be a number field with basis
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathbf{Q}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be the Chevalley basis of
\gfrak(E)
. Then

\mathcal{S}=\left\{(eb)^{\langle\Theta\rangle}\mid b\in\mathcal{B},\ e\in\mathcal{E}\right\}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\Theta}

is a spanning set of
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
over
\mathbf{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
A
be the endomorphism of
\gfrak(E)
over
\mathbf{Q}
mapping
x
to its
\langle\Theta\rangle
-average
x^{\langle\Theta\rangle}/\lvert\langle\Theta\rangle\rvert
. Note that
A
maps to
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
and preserves it, hence it is a projection onto
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
. Therefore a spanning set for
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
can be obtained by mapping the basis
\{eb\mid b\in\mathcal{B},e\in\mathcal{E}\}
of
\gfrak(E)
under
A
.
∎

Example 3.4
.

Let
\gfrak=\slfrak_{3}

3.2.3.
A favorable covering pair of elements

In order to bound diameters of the
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-forms with respect to a random pairs of elements, we use a pair of elements in the covering Lie ring with favorable properties. The first such property is rapid growth. Say a Lie ring
\gfrak
exhibits
exponential growth
with respect to a finite subset
S
if there exists a constant
\gamma>1
such that for any
m\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
\lvert S^{m}\rvert\geq\gamma^{m}
.

Example 3.5
.

The Lie ring
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
exhibits exponential growth with respect to the standard generating set
\{e,f,h\}
. To see this, let
\phi\colon\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\to\mathbf{Z}
be the map
\phi(X)=X_{12}
. We claim that for any
m\in\mathbf{N}
and
x\in\phi(S^{m})
, we have
2x,2x+1\in\phi(S^{m+2})
. Indeed, for
X\in S^{m}
with
\phi(X)=x
, we have
2x=\phi([h,X])\in\phi(S^{m+1})\subseteq\phi(S^{m+2})
and
2x+1=\phi([h,X]+e)\in\phi(S^{m+2})
. Starting with
0,1\in\phi(S^{1})
, we can generate all integers from
0
to
2^{m}-1
in
\phi(S^{2m-1})
with the above procedure. Thus, we have
\lvert S^{m}\rvert\geq 2^{m/2}
for any
m\in\mathbf{N}
.

An
\slfrak_{2}(K)
-triple
in a Lie algebra
\gfrak
over
K
is a triple of elements
\{e,h,f\}
satisfying
[h,e]=2e
,
[h,f]=-2f
, and
[e,f]=h
. In other words, this is an embedded copy of the Lie algebra
\slfrak_{2}(K)
inside
\gfrak
. In such cases, the Lie algebra
\gfrak
necessarily exhibits exponential growth with respect to the generating set
\{e,h,f\}
, and therefore with respect to
any
generating set. We now show that our
\mathbf{Q}
-coverings possess this property.

Proposition 3.6
.

Every
\mathbf{Q}
-covering of an
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-form of a classical Lie algebra contains an
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{Q})
-triple.

Proof.

Let
e_{\lambda}
be a highest weight vector in
\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})\subseteq\gfrak(E)
. The defining property of the highest weight vector is that it is a root vector that commutes with all the simple root vectors. Since
\vartheta
is a Lie ring automorphism of
\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})
that permutes the simple roots, it follows that
\vartheta(e_{\lambda})
is also a highest weight vector. Hence
\vartheta(e_{\lambda})=\pm e_{\lambda}
for some sign. If the sign is positive, then
e_{\lambda}\in\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
, and so
\{e_{\lambda},e_{-\lambda},h_{\lambda}\}
forms an
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{Q})
-triple in
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
. Note that
\vartheta
is of order dividing
2
or
3
, hence the negative sign can only occur if
\vartheta
is of order
2
. In this case, if
E=\mathbf{Q}[\omega]/(f(\omega))
with
f
of order
2
, we have
(\omega e_{\lambda})^{\langle\Theta\rangle}=(\omega-\omega^{\sigma})e_{\lambda}\in\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
. Note that

[(\omega-\omega^{\sigma})e_{\lambda},(\omega-\omega^{\sigma})e_{-\lambda}]=(\omega-\omega^{\sigma})^{2}h_{\lambda},

where
(\omega-\omega^{\sigma})^{2}=\Tr(\omega)^{2}-4\fieldnorm{\omega}=\Delta(f)\in K
, the discriminant of
f
. Hence
\{(\omega e_{\lambda})^{\langle\Theta\rangle},(\omega e_{-\lambda})^{\langle\Theta\rangle}/\Delta(f),h_{\lambda}\}
forms an
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{Q})
-triple in
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
that belongs to
\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E}[1/\Delta(f)])
.
∎

This implies that a
\mathbf{Q}
-covering of an
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-form of a classical Lie algebra exhibits exponential growth with respect to any generating set
S
. The following shows that we can always exhibit a particularly favorable generating set and even pass it on to the covering ring.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
E
be a number field of degree
d
with
d\leq 3
. The ring
\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\Theta}
contains a pair of elements
x,y
such that the following hold.

1.

The ring
\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\Theta}
exhibits exponential growth with respect to
\{x,y\}
.

2.

The image of
\{x,y\}
modulo
p
generates
\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})^{\Theta}
for all large enough
p
(depending only only on the Lie type
\gfrak
) that are inert in
E
.

Proof.

Semisimple Lie algebras over fields of characteristic
0
can be generated by two elements
[
Kur51
, Theorem 6]
. Hence, any
\mathbf{Q}
-covering
\gfrak(E)^{\Theta}
contains a generating pair of elements
x,y
. After possibly multiplying
x,y
by suitable integers, we may assume that they are integral, thus
x,y\in\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\Theta}
. In particular, every element of the basis
\mathcal{S}
can be expressed as a
E

. After clearing denominators, we may assume that these linear combinations are integral and that they are expressing integer multiples of elements from

. Therefore the reductions modulo

\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})^{\Theta}
as long as

is large enough.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Our proofs below crucially depend on the existence of a covering ring with the properties described in the proposition above. This approach does not extend to Lie algebras of Cartan type, as these do not possess finite-dimensional analogues in characteristic

. For instance, unlike the classical Lie algebras, the Witt algebra

has

tends to infinity.

3.2.4.
An extremal basis

A non-zero element

is called

\image(\ad_{x})^{2}\subseteq\langle x\rangle
. If, in addition,
(\ad_{x})^{2}=0

is called a

. For example, in

[

[

, Theorem 5.2]
for non-split forms of classical Lie algebras. For any

, let

be the quadratic map

q_{a,b}\colon\gfrak\to\gfrak,\quad z\mapsto[\ad_{a}(z),\ad_{b}(z)].

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
p>5
, and let
\gfrak
be an
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-form of a classical Lie algebra.

1.

There exists a basis
\mathcal{E}
of
\gfrak
consisting of extremal non-sandwich elements.

2.

There exists an element

such that
q_{a,b}\neq 0
for every

.

Proof.

As in the proof of
Proposition

3.6
, we can find an
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})
-triple constructed from a scalar multiple of the highest weight vector
e_{\lambda}
. Note that
\ad_{e_{\lambda}}^{2}
maps all root subspaces either to
0
or to the highest weight subspace, so any scalar multiple of
e_{\lambda}
is extremal. Since it forms an
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})
-triple, it is not a sandwich. It now follows from
[

, Theorem 1.1]
that

is generated by extremal elements. Therefore, it is also spanned by extremal elements and contains no sandwiches, by
[

contains the suitable scalar multiplies of the highest and lowest weight vectors. This proves the first part. For the second part, we choose

to be the corresponding multiple of the lowest weight vector

. To conclude, we do explicit calculations, which are both extensive and Lie type specific. Hence, we defer the argument to Appendix

.
∎

The above observation is the only step in the proof of the sum-bracket theorem

that depends on the explicit structure of the Lie algebras, namely the split

-forms. By using the same argument together with

, the sum-bracket theorem therefore extends to non-split

(

[

]
)
.

There is an absolute constant

such that the following holds.

\gfrak
be an

-form of a classical Lie algebra and

. Then

where

is a constant depending only on the Lie type of

.

4.
Diameters of classical finite Lie algebras

We are now ready to prove that all the
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-forms of classical Lie algebras exhibit extremely rapid random generation as
p
tends to infinity. Here is a brief outline of the argument.

Let us first consider the split forms, since the technicalities of the non-split case can obscure the main ideas. For split forms, we do not need to worry about automorphisms of the Dynkin diagram. The covering map from
Lemma

3.2
is simply

\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})\to\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p}).

Our argument heavily utilizes this map in order to obtain uniform results over all large enough primes. We first show that the coefficients of the balls in
\gfrak(\mathbf{Z})
with respect to any pair of elements can grow at most exponentially in the radius of the ball. This means we can do up to about
\log p
steps and still ensure that the coefficients will not loop around the field
\mathbf{F}_{p}
, so the ball of radius about
\log p
will be mapped injectively by the quotient projection to
\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. Since balls with respect to a favorable pair of elements grow exponentially in the radius (as per the previous section), we can generate, after about
\log p
steps, at least
p^{\delta}
elements in
\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p})
for some
\delta>0
. It follows from the Schwartz-Zippel lemma that the same conclusion then holds for generic pairs of elements. We finish off by using Dona’s theorem to show that we generate the whole Lie algebra
\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p})
in
O(\log p)
steps.

A similar, but more involved argument works for the non-split forms. The main difference is that the covering map is now
\gfrak(\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\Theta}\to\gfrak(\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}})^{\Theta}
. As
p
varies, the characteristic
0
object depends on the realization of
\mathbf{F}_{p^{d}}
, and we cannot cover all the cases with a single lattice in
\gfrak(\mathbf{C})
.

For this reason, we exhibit several covering objects, each of which covers a large proportion of the primes
p
, and collect them all together using tools from algebraic number theory to cover a set of primes of density
1
.

Remark 4.1
.

Our proof can be compared to the argument for the group
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
in
[
Hel08
, Lemma 6.4 and Corollary 6.5]
, but there is an important distinction. In the group case, most pairs of elements do not satisfy any identity of length
\leq c\log p
(essentially because
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
contains a free subgroup). However, classical Lie algebras can satisfy polynomial identities whose degree depends only on the Lie type. For example, the Lie algebra
\slfrak_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
satisfies the following polynomial identity (see
[
Dre21
, Theorem 4]
):

[[[x_{2},x_{3}],[x_{4},x_{1}]],x_{1}]+[[[x_{2},x_{1}],[x_{3},x_{1}]],x_{4}]=0.

Replacing each
x_{i}
with
[X,Y,\dots,Y]
(with
i-1
occurrences of

4.1.1.

Let

4.3.1.
Chebotarev density theorem

Let us first recall the classical theorem that allows us to control the density of primes modulo which a single polynomial is irreducible.

Theorem 4.9

(Chebotarev density theorem)
.

Let
K
be a Galois number field with
G=\Gal(K/\mathbf{Q})
, and let
C\subseteq G
be a conjugacy closed set. Then the set of primes
p
such that
K/\mathbf{Q}
is unramified at
p
and whose associated Frobenius conjugacy class
\Frob_{p}(K/\mathbf{Q})
is contained in
C
has natural density
\lvert C\rvert/\lvert G\rvert
.

We will use the theorem with
K=\mathbf{Q}[x]/(f(x))
, where
f\in\mathbf{Z}[x]
is an irreducible polynomial. Let
\Delta
be the discriminant of
f
. If a prime
p
does not divide
\Delta
, then
K/\mathbf{Q}
is unramified at
p
, and the Frobenius conjugacy class
\Frob_{p}(K/\mathbf{Q})
is well-defined subset of
G=\Gal(K/\mathbf{Q})
. Letting
d=\deg f
, we can identify
G
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{Sym}(d)
acting on the
d
roots of
f
. The Frobenius conjugacy class
\Frob_{p}(K/\mathbf{Q})
is represented by a
d
-cycle in
\mathrm{Sym}(d)
if and only if
f\bmod p
is irreducible in
\mathbf{F}_{p}[x]
. Let
C^{\irr}
be the set of elements of
G
that act as a
d
-cycle on the roots of
f
. Then the Chebotarev density theorem gives us

\density\left\{p\in\mathbf{P}\mid f\bmod p\text{ is irreducible}\right\}=\frac{\lvert C^{\irr}\rvert}{\lvert G\rvert}.

We will require a version of this property for several independent polynomials. The precise independence condition needed here is as follows: we say that number fields
K_{1},\dots,K_{N}
are
jointly independent
if, for each
i
, the compositum of the first
i-1
fields intersects trivially with the
i
-th field,
i.e.
,

(K_{1}\cdots K_{i-1})\cap K_{i}=\mathbf{Q}.

In such a situation, an easy proof by induction shows that the restriction map

\Gal(K_{1}\cdots K_{N}/\mathbf{Q})\to\Gal(K_{1}/\mathbf{Q})\times\cdots\times\Gal(K_{N}/\mathbf{Q})

is an isomorphism. Under these assumptions, we can now state the version of the Chebotarev density theorem for several polynomials.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{N}\in\mathbf{Z}[x]
be irreducible polynomials with jointly independent Galois number fields
K_{i}=\mathbf{Q}[x]/(f_{i}(x))
.
Let

X_{i}=\left\{p\in\mathbf{P}\mid f_{i}\bmod p\text{ is irreducible}\right\}.

Then

\density\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\right)=1-\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(1-\density(X_{i})\right).

Proof.

Let
G_{i}=\Gal(K_{i}/\mathbf{Q})\leq\mathrm{Sym}(d_{i})
, where
d_{i}=\deg f_{i}
. Writing
C_{i}^{\irr}
for the set of elements of
G_{i}
that act as a
d_{i}
-cycle on the roots of
f_{i}
, we have

\density(X_{i})=\frac{\lvert C_{i}^{\irr}\rvert}{\lvert G_{i}\rvert},

Since the number fields
K_{i}
are jointly independent, their compositum
K=K_{1}\cdots K_{N}
with Galois group
G=\Gal(K/\mathbf{Q})
satisfies
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{N}
by the lemma above. For each prime
p
that does not divide any of the discriminants of
f_{i}
, the Frobenius conjugacy class
\Frob_{p}(K/\mathbf{Q})
can be identified with the tuple

\Frob_{p}(K/\mathbf{Q})=\left(\Frob_{p}(K_{1}/\mathbf{Q}),\dots,\Frob_{p}(K_{N}/\mathbf{Q})\right)\in G_{1}\times G_{2}\times\cdots\times G_{N}=G.

Hence all polynomials
f_{i}
are reducible mod
p
if and only if

\Frob_{p}(K/\mathbf{Q})\subseteq(G_{1}\setminus C_{1}^{\irr})\times\cdots\times(G_{N}\setminus C_{N}^{\irr}).

By the Chebotarev density theorem, the set of primes
p
for which this occurs has density

\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\lvert C_{i}^{\irr}\rvert}{\lvert G_{i}\rvert}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(1-\density(X_{i})\right).

∎

4.3.2.
Constructing suitable fields

Finally it remains to construct the polynomials

that will give us the jointly independent Galois number fields

. In our application, we only need to construct families of polynomials of degree

and

(depending on the order of the corresponding automorphism of the Dynkin diagram). Our construction will be based on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
K_{1},\dots,K_{N}
be Galois number fields with coprime discriminants. Then the fields

are jointly independent.

Proof.

Induction on
N
. The base case

is trivial. Assume that

are jointly independent. Ramification is determined by triviality of the inertia subgroup, which is multiplicative in the setting of joint independence

. Hence primes that ramify in

are precisely those that divide one of the discriminants of

(K_{1}\cdots K_{N-1})\cap K_{N}

and
K_{N}
, and so it must divide the discriminant of
K_{N}
as well as the discriminant of some

with

∎

We now show how to construct the jointly independent fields
K_{i}
with coprime discriminants by using cyclotomic extensions. For each prime

that is

, let

be a cyclotomic extension, where
\zeta_{q_{i}}
is a primitive
q_{i}
-th root of unity. The Galois group of this extension is cyclic of order
q_{i}-1
. This group has an index
d

), call it
H_{i}
. Let
K_{i}=\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{q_{i}})^{H_{i}}
be the corresponding fixed subfield. The degree

over
\mathbf{Q}
is
d
. Note that the discriminants of these fields are coprime: the discriminant of the cyclotomic extension
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{q_{i}})
is a power of
q_{i}
by the conductor-discriminant formula, and so the discriminant of
K_{i}
is also a power of
q_{i}
. Finally, we can realize the extension

as the splitting field of some polynomial

of degree

.

Proof of
Theorem

4.8
.

Use the polynomials
f_{i}
to run the multipolynomial Chebotarev density theorem from above. Taking

for which some

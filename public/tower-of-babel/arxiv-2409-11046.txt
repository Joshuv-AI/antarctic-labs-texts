Crosscap states and duality of Ising field theory in two dimensions
Yueshui Zhang, Ying-Hai Wu, Lei Wang, Hong-Hao Tu

Abstract
We propose two distinct crosscap states for the two-dimensional (2D) Ising field theory. These two crosscap states, identifying Ising spins or dual spins (domain walls) at antipodal points, are shown to be related via the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation. We derive their Majorana free field representations and extend bosonization techniques to calculate correlation functions of the 2D Ising conformal field theory (CFT) with different crosscap boundaries. Away from criticality, we develop a conformal perturbation theory to calculate the Klein bottle entropy (norm-square of the crosscap overlap) as a universal scaling function [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 130, 151602 (2023) ]. For the Ising field theory, our analytical results support the conjectured monotonicity of the Klein bottle entropy under relevant perturbations. The formalism provides a general framework for studying perturbed 2D CFTs on non-orientable manifolds.

To compute the overlaps, we solve the critical Ising chain (

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{NS}}_{\mathrm{latt}}=\sum_{k\in\mathrm{NS}}4\cos\frac{k}{2}\left(d_{k}^{\dagger}d_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

— For the Ising CFT, the two crosscap states

\langle\mathcal{C}_{+}|e^{-\beta H_{0}}|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{C}_{-}|e^{-\beta H_{0}}|\mathcal{C}_{-}\rangle

z=\tau+ix
(
\tau\in[0,\infty)
and
x\in[0,L)

and interpret the crosscap correlators as the correlation functions on the real projective plane (

We extend bosonization techniques

12

Universal scaling functions
— While expressed in the Ising CFT basis, the validity of Ising crosscap states in Eq. (
12
) extends to the 2D Ising field theory when relevant perturbations are present. The resulting crosscap overlap, defined as the overlap of crosscap states with perturbed ground states, is a universal scaling function of the dimensionless couplings
[
43
]
. Despite its great potential for identifying critical theories in numerics, there was no systematic method to compute it analytically. Here we take the first step by developing a conformal perturbation theory that allows for a perturbative expansion of the crosscap overlap in powers of the coupling constants, applicable to general 2D CFTs with perturbations.

We consider the Hamiltonian
H=H_{0}+H_{1}
, where
H_{0}
is the Hamiltonian of a unitary CFT defined on a circle of length
L
. The perturbation is given by
H_{1}=-g\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,\varphi(x)
, where
\varphi
is a primary operator of conformal dimension
(h,\bar{h})
(we take
h=\bar{h}
for simplicity and
h<1
so that the perturbation is relevant), normalized as
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}x^{4h}\langle\varphi(0)\varphi(x)\rangle=1
with the expectation value taken in the CFT vacuum. For notational simplicity we consider a single relevant perturbation; the extension to multiple perturbations is straightforward. The perturbed ground state, denoted as
|\psi_{0}(s)\rangle
, depends only on dimensionless coupling
s=gL^{2-2h}

[
56
]
. Denoting the crosscap state as
|\mathcal{C}\rangle
, our goal is to compute the crosscap overlap
\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle
, which depends solely on
s
and is thus a universal scaling function
[
43
]
.

To perform a perturbative analysis, we factorize the crosscap overlap into two parts:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle=Z(s)\,\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}W(s)\right]

(16)

with
Z(s)=\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle/\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle
and
\exp[\frac{1}{2}W(s)]=\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle
. For practical calculations,
Z(s)
and
W(s)
are written as

\displaystyle Z(s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\mathcal{C}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle}\,,

\displaystyle W(s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\left[\left\langle\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}-\frac{\pi\beta}{6L}\delta c(s)\right]\,,

(17)

where
\mathcal{T}
stands for time-ordering and
H_{1}(\tau)
is the perturbation term in the interaction picture,
\langle\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}
denotes the
connected
contribution of
\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle
, and
\delta c(s)=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{6L}{\pi\beta}\langle\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}
is the change of the “running” central charge
c(s)\equiv\delta c(s)+c
. With the knowledge of the CFT correlators (on
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
and the plane), the expansion of the time-ordered exponential
\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}
(in powers of the coupling
g
) allows us to calculate the crosscap overlap in a perturbative way.

We obtain a general expression for the first-order correction to the crosscap overlap
[
46
]
:

\displaystyle\delta\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}\cdot\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(h,2h,h+1;-1)}{2(2\pi)^{1-2h}\,h}\cdot s+\cdots\,,

(18)

where
\mathcal{A}=\lim_{w\to 0}\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\varphi(w)|\mathcal{C}\rangle
is the crosscap coefficient in the one-point conformal crosscap correlator, and
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b,c;x)
is the hypergeometric function. Since
{}_{2}F_{1}(h,2h,h+1;-1)>0
for
h<1
, the sign of the first-order correction is solely determined by
\mathcal{A}
.
As a concrete example, consider the
A
-series unitary minimal models
[
12
]
, with central charge
c=1-\frac{6}{m(m+1)}
,
m\geq 3
. The primary fields are labeled by integer pairs
(r,s)
with
1\leq r\leq m-1
and
1\leq s\leq m
, subject to
r+s=\mathrm{even}
. The crosscap state associated with the identity field
(1,1)
can be expressed in terms of the Ishibashi states as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{(1,1)}\rangle=\sum_{1\leq r\leq m-1\,,1\leq s\leq m}^{r+s=\mathrm{even}}\mathcal{A}_{(r,s)}|(r,s)\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,,

(19)

where
\mathcal{A}_{(r,s)}
are the crosscap coefficients of the primary fields
\varphi_{(r,s)}

[
57
,
23
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{(r,s)}=O_{r}O_{s}\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{2}\cot\left(\frac{\pi r}{2m}\right)\cot\left(\frac{\pi s}{2(m+1)}\right)}{\sqrt{m(m+1)}}}

(20)

with
O_{n}=\frac{1-(-1)^{n}}{2}
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{(r,s)}\geq 0
for all
(r,s)
, Eq. (
18
) provides a perturbative proof of the monotonicity conjecture
[
43
]
for the crosscap overlap (and hence Klein bottle entropy) under the renormalization group flow in
A
-series unitary minimal models whenever the crosscap coefficient of the perturbing field is non-vanishing.

The conformal perturbation theory outlined above is directly applicable to the Ising field theory in Eq. (
1
), where
|\mathcal{C}_{(1,1)}\rangle
in Eq. (
19
) reduces to
|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle
. The results are summarized as follows: (i) For the thermal perturbation, the crosscap overlap
\langle\psi_{0}(s_{1})|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle
, with
s_{1}=g_{1}L
, has been computed non-perturbatively in Ref.
[
43
]
. Our conformal perturbation theory calculations agree with the non-perturbative result up to second order
[
46
]
. (ii) For the magnetic perturbation, conformal perturbation theory shows that the crosscap overlap is an even function of the dimensionless coupling:
\langle\psi_{0}(s_{2})|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}_{2n}s_{2}^{2n}
, with
s_{2}=g_{2}L^{15/8}
, since crosscap correlators with odd number of
\sigma
-fields vanish. Utilizing the two-point crosscap correlator [Eq. (
14
)], we obtain the leading-order correction
\mathcal{C}_{2}\approx-1.63528

[
46
]
. This shows that the crosscap overlap reaches its maximum at
s_{2}=0
, thereby supporting the monotonicity conjecture in Ref.
[
43
]
. An important future direction is to extend this perturbative proof to other perturbed CFTs in which the first-order correction of the crosscap overlap vanishes.

Summary and outlook
— In conclusion, we have thoroughly investigated two distinct crosscap states in the 2D Ising field theory, connected by Kramers-Wannier duality. By developing a conformal perturbation theory for the crosscap overlap, we extended its definition beyond CFT and established a perturbative expansion in the coupling constant. This framework not only deepens our understanding of field theories on non-orientable manifolds, but also enriches the numerical toolbox for identifying critical theories in lattice models
[
58
,
59
,
51
,
60
]
. The identification of lattice crosscap states and the bosonization techniques developed here also provide a natural route to the study of nonequilibrium dynamics involving crosscap states
[
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
,
65
,
66
]
.

Beyond the Ising model, many field theories exhibit dualities (e.g.,
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
parafermion CFTs
[
67
]
), and it is natural to ask how crosscap states transform under such dualities. Exploring this interplay may reveal new structures of non-orientable CFTs. A second intriguing direction is to clarify the link between Klein bottle entropy and renormalization-group flow
[
43
]
, in analogy with the
c
-theorem
[
68
]
and
g
-theorem
[
69
,
70
]
. Finally, crosscap overlaps may provide a direct route to extract crosscap coefficients in 3D CFTs
[

, thereby complementing bootstrap results where only ratios of such coefficients are accessible
[
33
]
.

Appendix A
I. Transverse field Ising chain

In this section, we briefly review the exact solution of the transverse field Ising chain (TFIC) and its Kramers-Wannier duality.

A.1
A. Exact solution

The Hamiltonian of the TFIC is given by

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{latt}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma^{x}_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j+1}-h\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma^{z}_{j}\,,

(S1)

where we consider even
N
and
h>0
. The Hamiltonian has
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry,
[H_{\mathrm{latt}},Q]=0
with
Q=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{z}
. In the following, we focus on the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
even (
Q=1
) subspace, which is called the Neveu-Schwarz (NS) sector following the CFT convention.

After performing the Jordan-Wigner transformation

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{x}=\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}e^{i\pi c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}}(c_{j}+c_{j}^{\dagger}),\quad\sigma_{j}^{z}=2c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}-1\,,

(S2)

the Fourier transform
c_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k\in\mathrm{NS}}e^{ikj}c_{k}
, and the Bogoliubov transformation, the Hamiltonian is diagonalized in the NS sector:

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{NS}}_{\mathrm{latt}}=\sum_{k\in\mathrm{NS}}\varepsilon_{k}\left(d_{k}^{\dagger}d_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,

(S3)

where the allowed single-particle momenta in the NS sector are
k=\pm\frac{2\pi}{N}(n_{k}-\frac{1}{2})
with
n_{k}=1,2,\ldots,N/2
. The Bogoliubov single-particle spectrum is

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}(h)=2\sqrt{(h-1)^{2}+4h\cos^{2}(k/2)}\,,

(S4)

and the annihilation operator of the Bogoliubov mode is

\displaystyle d_{k}(h)=e^{i\pi/4}\sin(\theta_{k}/2)c_{k}+e^{-i\pi/4}\cos(\theta_{k}/2)c_{-k}^{\dagger}

(S5)

with the Bogoliubov phase
\theta_{k}(h)
being determined as

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{k}=(2h+2\cos k)/\varepsilon_{k},\quad\sin\theta_{k}=2\sin k/\varepsilon_{k}\,.

(S6)

The ground state of the TFIC in the NS sector is a fermionic Gaussian state

\displaystyle|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle=\prod_{k>0}\left(\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}+i\cos\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{-k}^{\dagger}\right)|0\rangle_{c}\,,

(S7)

where
|0\rangle_{c}
, the vacuum of the Jordan-Wigner fermions, satisfies
c_{j}|0\rangle_{c}=0\;\forall j
and is the fully polarized state in the original
\sigma^{z}
-basis:
|0\rangle_{c}=|\Downarrow\rangle\equiv|\downarrow_{1}\downarrow_{2}\cdots\downarrow_{N}\rangle
. To fix the phase of the ground state, we require that its overlap with
|\Downarrow\rangle
is positive:
\langle\Downarrow|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle=\prod_{k>0}\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}>0
, and
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
is then a
real positive
wave function in the
\sigma^{z}
-basis – this agrees with the Perron-Frobenius theorem, as the off-diagonal matrix elements of the Hamiltonian (
S1
) are non-positive in the
\sigma^{z}
-basis. All excited states in the NS sector are obtained by acting an even number of
d^{{\dagger}}_{k}
’s (with distinct momenta) on top of the ground state
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
.

A.2
B. Kramers-Wannier duality

To construct the unitary operator for the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation, it is convenient to consider the Majorana representation of the TFIC [Eq. (
S1
)]:

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{latt}}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\chi_{j}-\bar{\chi}_{j})(\chi_{j+1}+\bar{\chi}_{j+1})-ih\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{j}\bar{\chi}_{j}\,,

(S8)

where

\displaystyle\chi_{j}=(-1)^{j}\left(e^{i\pi/4}c_{j}+e^{-i\pi/4}c_{j}^{\dagger}\right),\quad\bar{\chi}_{j}=(-1)^{j}\left(e^{-i\pi/4}c_{j}+e^{i\pi/4}c_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\,,~

(S9)

are the lattice Majorana operators satisfying
\{\chi_{j},\chi_{l}\}=\{\bar{\chi}_{j},\bar{\chi}_{l}\}=2\delta_{jl}
and
\{\chi_{j},\bar{\chi}_{l}\}=0
. As will be shown later, the lattice Majorana operators correspond to the Majorana fields in the Ising CFT in the continuum limit.

After the basis rotation

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\chi_{j}+\bar{\chi}_{j})=(-1)^{j}(c_{j}+c_{j}^{\dagger}),\quad\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\chi_{j}-\bar{\chi}_{j})=i(-1)^{j}(c_{j}-c_{j}^{\dagger})\,,~

(S10)

the Hamiltonian [Eq. (
S8
)] becomes

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{latt}}=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}\chi^{\prime}_{j+1}+ih\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi^{\prime}_{j}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}\,.

(S11)

The Kramers-Wannier duality can be revealed by defining the unitary operator
[
44
]

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}N}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}\chi^{\prime}_{j}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}\chi^{\prime}_{j+1}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}\chi^{\prime}_{N}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{N}}\,,

(S12)

whose action on the (new) lattice Majorana operators is given by

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}\chi^{\prime}_{j}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}\,,

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\chi^{\prime}_{j+1}&\quad j=1,2,\ldots,N-1\,,\\
-\chi^{\prime}_{1}&\quad j=N\,.\end{cases}

(S13)

In the NS sector, the lattice Majorana operators
\chi^{\prime}_{j}
satisfy the anti-periodic boundary condition, i.e.,
\chi^{\prime}_{N+1}=-\chi^{\prime}_{1}
. Thus, we have
U_{\mathrm{KW}}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}=\chi^{\prime}_{j+1}\;\forall j
, and applying the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation to the Hamiltonian [Eq. (
S11
)] gives

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}H^{\mathrm{NS}}_{\mathrm{latt}}(h)U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}=hH^{\mathrm{NS}}_{\mathrm{latt}}(1/h)\,,

(S14)

which transforms the TFIC with transverse field
h
to the same Hamiltonian but with transverse field
1/h
.

The spin operators
\sigma_{j}^{\alpha}
(
\alpha=x,z
) are related to the Majorana operators
\chi^{\prime}_{j}
and
\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}
[Eq. (
S10
)] via the Jordan-Wigner transformation [Eq. (
S2
)]:

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{z}

\displaystyle=-i\chi^{\prime}_{j}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}\,,\quad\sigma^{x}_{j}=(-1)^{j}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}(i\chi^{\prime}_{l}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{l})\chi^{\prime}_{j}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{x}\sigma_{j+1}^{x}

\displaystyle=-\chi^{\prime}_{j}(i\chi^{\prime}_{j}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j})\chi^{\prime}_{j+1}=-i\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}\chi^{\prime}_{j+1}\,.

(S15)

Using the above relations, we can write down the Kramers-Wannier unitary operator [Eq. (
S12
)] in the spin basis:

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}N}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{j}^{z}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma^{x}_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j+1}}\right)e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{N}^{z}}\,.

(S16)

When viewing
h
as a continuous parameter, the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation maps the ground state
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
[Eq. (
S7
)] to the dual ground state
|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle
, possibly up to a phase:

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle=e^{i\gamma(h)}|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle\,,

(S17)

where
e^{i\gamma(h)}
is a
continuous
function of
h\in(0,+\infty)
with modulus one:
|e^{i\gamma(h)}|=1
. We assert that the phase factor is trivial
e^{i\gamma(h)}=1\;\forall h\in(0,+\infty)
, so that

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle=|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle\,.

(S18)

Specifically, for the limiting case
h\to+\infty
, we require

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\Uparrow\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\Rightarrow\rangle+|\Leftarrow\rangle\right)\,,

(S19)

where
|\Uparrow\rangle\equiv|\uparrow_{1}\uparrow_{2}\cdots\uparrow_{N}\rangle
is the fully polarized state in the
\sigma^{z}
-basis, and
|\Rightarrow\rangle\equiv|\rightarrow_{1}\rightarrow_{2}\cdots\rightarrow_{N}\rangle
and
|\Leftarrow\rangle\equiv|\leftarrow_{1}\leftarrow_{2}\cdots\leftarrow_{N}\rangle
are fully polarized states in the
\sigma^{x}
-basis, with
|\rightarrow\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\uparrow\rangle+|\downarrow\rangle)
and
|\leftarrow\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\uparrow\rangle-|\downarrow\rangle)
.

To prove the about assertion, we first consider the limiting case
h\to+\infty
. In this limit, the ground states of
H^{\mathrm{NS}}_{\mathrm{latt}}(h)
and
H^{\mathrm{NS}}_{\mathrm{latt}}(1/h)
are given by

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to+\infty}|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle=|\Uparrow\rangle\,,\qquad\lim_{h\to+\infty}|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\Rightarrow\rangle+|\Leftarrow\rangle\right)\,.

(S20)

These states live in the NS sector and have
real positive
wave function coefficients in the
\sigma^{z}
-basis. The phase factor
e^{i\gamma(h)}
[Eq. (
S17
)] in this limit can be determined via

\displaystyle e^{i\gamma(+\infty)}=\lim_{h\to+\infty}\frac{\langle\Uparrow|U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle}{\langle\Uparrow|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle}=\frac{\langle\Uparrow|U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\Uparrow\rangle}{\langle\Uparrow|\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\Rightarrow\rangle+|\Leftarrow\rangle)}=1\,,

(S21)

where we used

\displaystyle\langle\Uparrow|U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\Uparrow\rangle=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}N}\langle\Uparrow|\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left[e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{j}^{z}}\left(\frac{1-i\sigma^{x}_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j+1}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right]e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{N}^{z}}|\Uparrow\rangle=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}N}\cdot 2^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\langle\Uparrow|\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{j}^{z}}|\Uparrow\rangle=2^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\,.

(S22)

To determine the phase factor
e^{i\gamma(h)}
[Eq. (
S17
)] for general
h
, we consider

\displaystyle e^{-i\gamma(h)}=\frac{\langle\Uparrow|U^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{KW}}|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle}{\langle\Uparrow|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle}=\frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\langle\Rightarrow|+\langle\Leftarrow|)\cdot|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle}{\langle\Uparrow|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle}>0\,,

(S23)

since
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
is a
real positive
wave function in the
\sigma^{z}
-basis by definition [Eq. (
S7
)]. Therefore, as a
continuous
function with modulus one,
e^{i\gamma(h)}
must be a constant which equals one:

\displaystyle e^{i\gamma(h)}=1\,,\quad\forall h\in(0,+\infty)\,.

(S24)

This proves our assertion [Eq. (
S18
)].

Lastly, we determine how Bogoliubov modes change under the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation.

We first determine the transformation rule of the Jordan-Wigner fermion via Eq. (
S13
):

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}c_{j}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2}U_{\mathrm{KW}}(\chi^{\prime}_{j}-i\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j})U^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{KW}}=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2}(\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}-i\chi^{\prime}_{j+1})=\frac{i}{2}(c_{j}+c_{j+1}-c_{j}^{\dagger}+c_{j+1}^{\dagger})\,.

(S25)

When going to momentum space, it reads

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}c_{k}U^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{KW}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-ikj}U_{\mathrm{KW}}c_{j}U^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{KW}}=\frac{i}{2}(c_{k}+e^{ik}c_{k}-c_{-k}^{\dagger}+e^{ik}c_{-k}^{\dagger})=ie^{ik/2}(\cos\frac{k}{2}c_{k}+i\sin\frac{k}{2}c_{-k}^{\dagger})

(S26)

and
U_{\mathrm{KW}}c^{\dagger}_{k}U^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{KW}}=-ie^{-ik/2}(\cos\frac{k}{2}c_{k}^{\dagger}-i\sin\frac{k}{2}c_{-k})
. Therefore, the Bogoliubov mode
d_{k}^{\dagger}(h)
[Eq. (
S5
)] transforms as

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}d_{k}^{\dagger}(h)U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=U_{\mathrm{KW}}\left[e^{-i\pi/4}\sin\frac{\theta_{k}(h)}{2}c_{k}^{\dagger}+e^{i\pi/4}\cos\frac{\theta_{k}(h)}{2}c_{-k}\right]U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-ie^{-ik/2}e^{-i\pi/4}\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}(\cos\frac{k}{2}c_{k}^{\dagger}-i\sin\frac{k}{2}c_{-k})+ie^{-ik/2}e^{i\pi/4}\cos\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}(\cos\frac{k}{2}c_{-k}-i\sin\frac{k}{2}c_{k}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=ie^{-ik/2}\left[e^{-i\pi/4}\sin\left(\frac{k-\theta_{k}(h)}{2}\right)c_{k}^{\dagger}+e^{i\pi/4}\cos\left(\frac{k-\theta_{k}(h)}{2}\right)c_{-k}\right]

\displaystyle=ie^{-ik/2}d_{k}^{\dagger}(1/h)\,,\quad k\in\mathrm{NS}\,,~

(S27)

where we used
\theta_{k}(1/h)=k-\theta_{k}(h)
with
\theta_{k}(h)
defined in Eq. (
S6
).

Appendix B
II. Kramers-Wannier duality of the lattice crosscap states

In this section, we consider the lattice crosscap states and discuss how they are related via the Kramers-Wannier duality. We also derive an alternative representation of the Kramers-Wannier dual lattice crosscap state, which is expressed as an equal weight superposition of the original lattice crosscap state and another modified lattice crosscap state.

B.1
A. Lattice crosscap state and its dual

The lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
is the product of Bell pairs identifying Ising spins at two antipodal sites:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left(|\uparrow_{j}\uparrow_{j+N/2}\rangle+|\downarrow_{j}\downarrow_{j+N/2}\rangle\right)\,.

(S28)

For revealing its Kramers-Wannier dual state, it is convenient to rewrite it as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}(1+\sigma^{x}_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j+N/2})|\Uparrow\rangle\,.

(S29)

Using the Majorana fermion representation of the spin operators [Eq. (
S15
)], it is easy to obtain via Eq. (
S13
) how they change under the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation:

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}\sigma_{j}^{z}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}=-i(U_{\mathrm{KW}}\chi^{\prime}_{j}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger})(U_{\mathrm{KW}}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger})=-i\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}\chi^{\prime}_{j+1}=\sigma^{x}_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j+1}\,,

(S30)

and

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}\sigma_{j}^{x}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}=-\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\left[U_{\mathrm{KW}}\left(-i\chi^{\prime}_{l}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{l}\right)U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}\right](U_{\mathrm{KW}}\chi^{\prime}_{j}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger})=-\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{1}\prod_{l=2}^{j}(-i\chi^{\prime}_{l}\bar{\chi}^{\prime}_{l})=\sigma_{1}^{y}\prod_{l=2}^{j}\sigma_{l}^{z}\,.

(S31)

Since the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
lives in the NS sector,
Q|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
, it is natural to consider its Kramers-Wannier dual state, denoted as
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle\equiv U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
, which also lives in the NS sector. The explicit form of the dual lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle
reads

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle\equiv U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left(1+U_{\mathrm{KW}}\sigma_{j}^{x}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}\cdot U_{\mathrm{KW}}\sigma_{j+1}^{x}U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}\right)\left(U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\Uparrow\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left(1+\mu_{j}\mu_{j+N/2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\Rightarrow\rangle+|\Leftarrow\rangle\right)\,,

(S32)

where
\mu_{j}=\prod_{l=1}^{j}\sigma_{l}^{z}
is the Ising disorder operator (dual spin). This corresponds to Eq. (4) in the main text.

Moreover, since
U_{\mathrm{KW}}\mu_{j}\mu_{j+N/2}U^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{KW}}=\sigma^{x}_{j+1}\sigma^{x}_{j+1+N/2}
and
U_{\mathrm{KW}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\Rightarrow\rangle+|\Leftarrow\rangle\right)=|\Uparrow\rangle
, imposing periodic boundary conditions one finds

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle\,.

(S33)

Thus, the two lattice crosscap states,
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
and
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle
, are dual to each other under the Kramers-Wannier transformation, as shown in Eqs. (
S32
) and (
S33
).

B.2
B. Alternative representation of the dual lattice crosscap state

In the main text [Eq. (5)], we mentioned that the dual lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle
can be expressed as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{-}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle+|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle\right)\,.

(S34)

Here,
|\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
is the original lattice corsscap state [Eq. (
S28
)], and
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
is a modified lattice crosscap state (with a different type of Bell pairs between antipodal sites):

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left(|\uparrow_{j}\uparrow_{j+N/2}\rangle-|\downarrow_{j}\downarrow_{j+N/2}\rangle\right)\,.

(S35)

To prove Eq. (
S34
), we compare both sides explicitly in the
\sigma^{z}
-basis. We start with the definition of
|\mathcal{C}^{-}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
in Eq. (
S32
) and decompose it as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{-}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|D_{+}\rangle+|D_{-}\rangle\right)

(S36)

with

\displaystyle|D_{\pm}\rangle=\frac{1}{2^{N/2}}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left(1+\prod_{l=j+1}^{j+N/2}\sigma_{l}^{z}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1\pm\sigma_{j}^{x})|\Uparrow\rangle\,.

(S37)

Since
\sigma^{z}|s\rangle=(-1)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}|s\rangle
for
s=\pm 1
, one finds

\displaystyle|D_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{N/2}}\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N}=\pm 1}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left[1+\prod_{l=j+1}^{j+N/2}(-1)^{\frac{s_{l}-1}{2}}\right](\pm 1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2}}|s_{1}\cdots s_{N}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N}=\pm 1}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{l=j+1}^{j+N/2}\frac{s_{l}-1}{2},0}\,(\pm 1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2}}|s_{1}\cdots s_{N}\rangle\,,

(S38)

where
\delta^{(2)}_{n,0}\equiv\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2}
(
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) is the Kronecker-
\delta
function with period
2
. The relation

\displaystyle\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{l=j}^{j-1+N/2}\frac{s_{l}-1}{2},0}\,\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{l=j+1}^{j+N/2}\frac{s_{l}-1}{2},0}=\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{l=j}^{j-1+N/2}\frac{s_{l}-1}{2},0}\delta_{s_{j},s_{j+N/2}}\,,\quad j=2,\cdots,N/2\,

(S39)

allows us to simplify the expression of
|D_{\pm}\rangle
,

\displaystyle|D_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N}=\pm 1}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{j=2}^{N/2+1}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2},0}\,\prod_{j=2}^{N/2}\delta_{s_{j},s_{j+N/2}}(\pm 1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2}}|s_{1}\cdots s_{N}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N/2+1}=\pm 1}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{j=2}^{N/2+1}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2},0}\,(\pm 1)^{\frac{s_{1}-1}{2}-\frac{s_{N/2+1}-1}{2}+2\sum_{j=2}^{N/2+1}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2}}|s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}s_{N/2+1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N/2+1}=\pm 1}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{j=2}^{N/2+1}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2},0}\,(\pm 1)^{\frac{1}{2}(s_{1}-s_{N/2+1})}|s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}s_{N/2+1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}\rangle\,.

(S40)

Summing
|D_{+}\rangle
and
|D_{-}\rangle
gives

\displaystyle|D_{+}\rangle+|D_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N/2+1}=\pm 1}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{j=2}^{N/2+1}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2},0}\,\left[1+(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}(s_{1}-s_{N/2+1})}\right]|s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}s_{N/2+1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N/2+1}=\pm 1}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{j=2}^{N/2+1}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2},0}\delta_{s_{1},s_{N/2+1}}|s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}s_{N/2+1}s_{2}\cdots s_{N/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N/2}=\pm 1}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2},0}|s_{1}\cdots s_{N/2}s_{1}\cdots s_{N/2}\rangle\,.

(S41)

On the other hand, the right-hand side of Eq. (
S34
) expands as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle+|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N/2}=\pm 1}\left[1+(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}(s_{j}-1)/2}\right]|s_{1}\cdots s_{N/2}s_{1}\cdots s_{N/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{N/2}=\pm 1}\delta^{(2)}_{\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\frac{s_{j}-1}{2},0}\,|s_{1}\cdots s_{N/2}s_{1}\cdots s_{N/2}\rangle\,,

(S42)

Since the two expansions coincide, Eq. (
S34
) is proved.

Appendix C
III. Exact lattice crosscap overlaps

In this section, we calculate the overlaps of the lattice crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
, and the modified crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
, with eigenstates of the TFIC.

We first derive the fermionic representation of the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
, which is a superposition of two fermionic Gaussian states in the NS sector. This crucial observation allows us to calculate its overlap with the ground state
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
of the TFIC as well as all excited states. The overlaps for the dual state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle
then follow directly from Kramers-Wannier duality. Moreover, the modified lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
admits an analogous fermionic representation, allowing its overlaps to be computed as well. The expansions of all three lattice crosscap states in the TFIC eigenbasis provide an alternative verification of Eq. (
S34
).

C.1
A. Fermionic representation of the lattice crosscap state

Our important result is that the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
[Eq. (
S28
)] is the following equal weight superposition of certain states in the Jordan-Wigner fermion basis:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left(1+\sigma_{j}^{+}\sigma_{j+N/2}^{+}\right)|\Downarrow\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{N/2}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots j_{n}\leq N/2}c_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{1}+N/2}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}+N/2}^{\dagger}|0\rangle_{c}\,,

(S43)

which can be divided into the “even” and “odd” parts by introducing the half-chain fermion parity
P_{\mathrm{half}}=(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=\frac{1+P_{\mathrm{half}}}{2}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle+\frac{1-P_{\mathrm{half}}}{2}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle

(S44)

with

\displaystyle\frac{1+P_{\mathrm{half}}}{2}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0\,(n\;\mathrm{even})}^{N/2}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{n}\leq N/2}c_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{1}+N/2}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}+N/2}^{\dagger}|0\rangle_{c}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{1-P_{\mathrm{half}}}{2}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1\,(n\;\mathrm{odd})}^{N/2}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{n}\leq N/2}c_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{1}+N/2}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}+N/2}^{\dagger}|0\rangle_{c}\,.

(S45)

The key observation is that the even and odd parts can be expressed via a pair of fermionic Gaussian states

\displaystyle|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}(1\pm ic_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+N/2}^{\dagger})|0\rangle_{c}\,.

(S46)

To see this, we consider the expansion
|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{N/2}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{n}\leq N/2}(\pm i)^{n}c_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{1}+N/2}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{n}+N/2}^{\dagger}|0\rangle_{c}
and notice

\displaystyle(\pm i)^{n}c_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{1}+N/2}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{n}+N/2}^{\dagger}=\begin{cases}c_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{1}+N/2}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}+N/2}^{\dagger}&\quad n\;\mathrm{even}\\
\pm i\;c_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}}^{\dagger}c_{j_{1}+N/2}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{j_{n}+N/2}^{\dagger}&\quad n\;\mathrm{odd}\end{cases},

(S47)

which allows us to relate the fermionic Gaussian states
|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
to the even and odd parts of
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
:

\displaystyle|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle=\frac{1+P_{\mathrm{half}}}{2}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle\pm i\frac{1-P_{\mathrm{half}}}{2}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle\,.

(S48)

Thus, the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
[Eq. (
S44
)] can be expressed in terms of the fermionic Gaussian states
|B^{\pm}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=\frac{1-i}{2}|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle+\frac{1+i}{2}|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle\,,

(S49)

which corresponds to Eq. (9) in the main text.

The fermionic Gaussian states
|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle=\exp\left(\pm\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+N/2}^{\dagger}\right)|0\rangle_{c}
are translationally invariant. After performing the Fourier transform
c_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k\in\mathrm{NS}}e^{ikj}c_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\pm i\sum_{k>0}e^{ikN/2}c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{-k}^{\dagger}\right)|0\rangle_{c}=\prod_{k>0}\left[1\mp(-1)^{n_{k}+N/2}c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{-k}^{\dagger}\right]|0\rangle_{c}\,,

(S50)

where we used
k=\pi-\frac{2\pi}{N}(n_{k}-\frac{1}{2})
for
k>0
.

C.2
B. Lattice crosscap overlap for the ground state

To obtain the lattice crosscap overlap for the ground state
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
[Eq. (
S7
)], we just need to calculate the overlap
\langle\psi_{0}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
by using the fermionic representation of the lattice crosscap state [Eq. (
S49
)]. The calculation is straightforward since both
|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
[Eq. (
S50
)] and
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
are fermionic Gaussian states:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle={}_{c}\langle 0|\prod_{k>0}\left(\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}-i\cos\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}c_{-k}c_{k}\right)\left(1\mp(-1)^{n_{k}+N/2}c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{-k}^{\dagger}\right)|0\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k>0}\left[\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\pm i(-1)^{n_{k}+N/2}\cos\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\pm i\sum_{k>0}(-1)^{n_{k}+N/2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\exp\left[\pm i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-(-1)^{N/2}\Theta(h)\right)\right]\,,

(S51)

where we defined the angle variable

\displaystyle\Theta(h)=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k>0}(-1)^{n_{k}}\theta_{k}(h)\,.

(S52)

Therefore, the overlap of the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
[Eq. (
S49
)] with the ground state
|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle
is

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1-i}{2}\langle\psi_{0}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle+\frac{1+i}{2}\langle\psi_{0}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sin\left[\frac{\pi}{4}-(-1)^{N/2}\Theta(h)\right]+\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{4}-(-1)^{N/2}\Theta(h)\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\cos\Theta(h)\,.

(S53)

C.3
C. Lattice crosscap overlaps for the excited states

It is not difficult to see that only “paired” eigenstates of the TFIC

\displaystyle|\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)\rangle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\left[id_{-k_{\alpha}}^{\dagger}(h)d_{k_{\alpha}}^{\dagger}(h)\right]|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle~

(S54)

with
0<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{M}<\pi
have non-vanishing overlaps with the fermionic Gaussian states
|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
[Eq. (
S50
)]. Thus, these are also the eigenstates which may have nontrivial overlaps with the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
.

The overlap calculation is similar to that of the ground state. We have

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-i)^{M}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\left[\cos\frac{\theta_{k_{\alpha}}}{2}\mp i(-1)^{n_{k_{\alpha}}+N/2}\sin\frac{\theta_{k_{\alpha}}}{2}\right]\prod_{k>0,k\neq\{k_{\alpha}\}}\left[\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\pm i(-1)^{n_{k}+N/2}\cos\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=(-i)^{M}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\frac{\cos\frac{\theta_{k_{\alpha}}}{2}\mp i(-1)^{n_{k_{\alpha}}+N/2}\sin\frac{\theta_{k_{\alpha}}}{2}}{\sin\frac{\theta_{k_{\alpha}}}{2}\pm i(-1)^{n_{k_{\alpha}}+N/2}\cos\frac{\theta_{k_{\alpha}}}{2}}\cdot\prod_{k>0}\left[\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\pm i(-1)^{n_{k}+N/2}\cos\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=(\mp)^{M}(-1)^{\frac{MN}{2}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\cdot\langle\psi_{0}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\cdot\langle\psi_{0}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle&\quad M\;\mathrm{even}\\
\mp(-1)^{N/2}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\cdot\langle\psi_{0}(h)|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle&\quad M\;\mathrm{odd}\end{cases}.

(S55)

Using Eq. (
S49
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\langle\psi_{0}(h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{2}\cos\Theta(h)

(S56)

for
M
even, and

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N/2}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\left[-\frac{1-i}{2}e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-(-1)^{N/2}\Theta\right)}+\frac{1+i}{2}e^{-i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-(-1)^{N/2}\Theta\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\,(-1)^{N/2}\sqrt{2}\sin\left[(-1)^{N/2}\Theta\right]

\displaystyle=i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{2}\sin\Theta(h)

(S57)

for
M
odd.

In summary, the lattice crosscap overlaps for
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle
are given by

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{2}\cos\Theta(h)&\quad M\;\mathrm{even}\\
i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{2}\sin\Theta(h)&\quad M\;\mathrm{odd}\end{cases}.

(S58)

C.4
D. Overlaps for the dual and modified lattice crosscap states

The crosscap overlaps of the dual lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle
[Eq. (
S32
)] can be obtained via the Kramers-Wannier duality. Using Eqs. (
S18
) and (
S27
), we find that the eigenstate
|\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)\rangle
transforms under the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation as

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\left[iU_{\mathrm{KW}}d_{-k_{\alpha}}^{\dagger}(h)U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}U_{\mathrm{KW}}d_{k_{\alpha}}^{\dagger}(h)U_{\mathrm{KW}}^{\dagger}\right]U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\psi_{0}(h)\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\left[-id_{-k_{\alpha}}^{\dagger}(1/h)d_{k_{\alpha}}^{\dagger}(1/h)\right]|\psi_{0}(1/h)\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{M}|\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(1/h)\rangle\,,

(S59)

which gives

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle=\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)|U_{\mathrm{KW}}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=(-1)^{M}\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(1/h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle\,.

(S60)

For the modified lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
, we have the analogous fermionic representation:

\displaystyle(-1)^{N/2}|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle=\frac{1+i}{2}|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle+\frac{1-i}{2}|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle\,,

(S61)

where
|B_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
are the fermionic Gaussian states defined in Eq. (
S46
). This enables analytic computation of overlaps of
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
with TFIC eigenstates:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\prime}\rangle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{2}\sin\Theta(h)&\quad M\;\mathrm{even}\\
-i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{2}\cos\Theta(h)&\quad M\;\mathrm{odd}\end{cases}.

(S62)

C.5
E. Expansion of crosscap states in the critical Ising chain eigenbasis

Specifically, at the critical point of TFIC [Eq. (
S1
) with
h=1
], we have
\theta_{k}=\frac{k}{2}
[Eq. (
S6
)] and the angle variable
\Theta(h=1)
[Eq. (
S52
)] is

\displaystyle\Theta(h=1)=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k>0}(-1)^{n_{k}}k=\frac{\pi}{8}\,,

(S63)

from which we obtain the non-vanishing overlaps for the lattice crosscap states

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h=1)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{+}\rangle=(-1)^{M}\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h=1)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{-}\rangle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}&\quad M\;\mathrm{even}\\
i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}&\quad M\;\mathrm{odd}\end{cases},

(S64)

and for the modified crosscap state

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h=1)|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\prime}\rangle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}&\quad M\;\mathrm{even}\\
-i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{k_{\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}&\quad M\;\mathrm{odd}\end{cases}.

(S65)

Note that Eq. (
S64
) corresponds to Eq. (10) in the main text.

According to Eqs. (
S64
) and (
S65
), the lattice crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
are expanded in the eigenbasis of the
critical
Ising chain as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}\exp\left[\pm i\sum_{k>0}(-1)^{n_{k}}d_{-k}^{\dagger}(h=1)d_{k}^{\dagger}(h=1)\right]|\psi_{0}(h=1)\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;+\frac{e^{-i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}\exp\left[\mp i\sum_{k>0}(-1)^{n_{k}}d_{-k}^{\dagger}(h=1)d_{k}^{\dagger}(h=1)\right]|\psi_{0}(h=1)\rangle\,,

(S66)

and the modified crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
is expanded as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{-ie^{i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}\exp\left[i\sum_{k>0}(-1)^{n_{k}}d_{-k}^{\dagger}(h=1)d_{k}^{\dagger}(h=1)\right]|\psi_{0}(h=1)\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;+\frac{ie^{-i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}\exp\left[-i\sum_{k>0}(-1)^{n_{k}}d_{-k}^{\dagger}(h=1)d_{k}^{\dagger}(h=1)\right]|\psi_{0}(h=1)\rangle\,.

(S67)

Appendix D
IV. Ising conformal crosscap states

In the continuum limit, the critical Ising chain is described by the Ising CFT. In this section, we first review the Pradisi-Sagnotti-Stanev (PSS) conformal crosscap states in the Ising CFT and then derive the associated dual state via the Kramers-Wannier topological defect line (TDL). Using the Majorana free field representation, we identify the continuum counterparts of the lattice crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
, denoted as
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
, and determine their relation to the PSS crosscap states.

D.1
A. Pradisi-Sagnotti-Stanev crosscap states

The Ising CFT is the simplest example of the rational CFT with diagonal modular invariant. In such theories, conformal crosscap states can be systematically constructed from the bulk conformal data.

Let the modular
\mathcal{S}
-matrix be
S_{ab}
and the
\mathcal{T}
-matrix be
T_{ab}=\delta_{ab}\,e^{2\pi i(h_{a}-\tfrac{c}{24})}
, where
h_{a}
is the conformal weight of the chiral primary field, and
c
is the central charge. The so-called PSS crosscap states can then be formally defined, each labeled by a primary field
a

[
22
,
23
,
35
]
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{a}\rangle=\sum_{b}\frac{P_{ab}}{\sqrt{S_{0b}}}\,|b\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,,

(S68)

where the
P_{ab}
is a symmetric and unitary matrix, defined as

\displaystyle P=\sqrt{T}\,S\,T^{2}\,S\,\sqrt{T}\,,

(S69)

and
|a\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\equiv e^{i\pi(L_{0}-h_{a})}|a\rangle\rangle
are crosscap Ishibashi states, with
L_{0}
the zeroth Virasoro generator and
|a\rangle\rangle
the usual boundary CFT Ishibashi states. If the primary field
a
is a simple current of the theory, the corresponding PSS crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{a}\rangle
is physical
[
35
]
. In particular, the identity field is a trivial simple current, and the associated state
|\mathcal{C}_{0}\rangle
is known as the “standard” PSS crosscap state.

Verlinde TDLs are defined as
[
72
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{a}=\sum_{b}\frac{S_{ab}}{S_{0b}}\mathcal{P}_{b}\,,

(S70)

where
\mathcal{P}_{b}
projects onto the sector labeled by the primary field
b
. Acting with
\mathcal{D}_{a}
on a PSS crosscap state yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{a}|\mathcal{C}_{b}\rangle=\sum_{c}Y_{ab}^{c}\,|\mathcal{C}_{c}\rangle\,,

(S71)

where

\displaystyle Y_{ab}^{c}\equiv(Y_{a})_{bc}=\sum_{d}\frac{S_{ad}\,P_{bd}\,P^{*}_{cd}}{S_{0d}}

(S72)

is an integer-valued tensor
[
23
,
73
,
35
,
74
]
.

The Ising CFT, with central charge
c=\frac{1}{2}
, has three (chiral) primary fields
\mathbbm{1}\,,\varepsilon\,,\sigma
, with conformal weights
0\,,\frac{1}{2}\,,\frac{1}{16}
, respectively. Its modular
\mathcal{S}
-matrix is

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\sqrt{2}\\
1&1&-\sqrt{2}\\
\sqrt{2}&-\sqrt{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(S73)

from which the
P
-matrix and the
Y
-tensor can be computed explicitly:

\displaystyle P=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\pi/8)&\sin(\pi/8)&0\\
\sin(\pi/8)&-\cos(\pi/8)&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad Y_{\mathbbm{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad Y_{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad Y_{\sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\
1&-1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(S74)

From Eq. (
S68
), two PSS crosscap states, corresponding to the simple currents
\mathbbm{1}
and
\varepsilon
, are given by
[
22
,
23
]

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}+\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}-\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,.

(S75)

Although these two PSS crosscap states are both physical, their crosscap partition functions
\langle\mathcal{C}_{a}|e^{-\beta H_{0}}|\mathcal{C}_{a}\rangle
(
a=\mathbbm{1},\varepsilon
) differ. Under a modular-
\mathcal{S}
transformation, the one for
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
gives the standard Klein bottle partition function, while that for
|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\rangle
yields the non-standard Klein bottle partition function
[
35
]
.

The Verlinde line
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}=\sqrt{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbbm{1}}-\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
realizes Kramers-Wannier duality in the Ising CFT. Acting with
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
on the (standard) PSS crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
yields a dual conformal crosscap state, which by Eq. (
S71
) is a superposition of
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
and
|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\rangle

[
36
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}}{\langle\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}\rangle}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}-\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle+|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\right)\,,

(S76)

where
\langle\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}\rangle=\sqrt{2}
denotes the vacuum expectation value of the TDL. The crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
and
\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}}{\langle\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}\rangle}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
share the same crosscap partition function and are mapped into each other under the Kramers-Wannier duality transformation.

D.2
B. Majorana free field representation

The operator formalism of the Ising CFT can be formulated in terms of the free Majorana Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{NS}}_{0}=\frac{i}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\left[\chi\partial_{x}\chi-\bar{\chi}\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}\right]\,,

(S77)

where we restrict ourselves in the NS sector for our purpose. Here
\chi
and
\bar{\chi}
are Majorana fields, with the mode expansion:

\displaystyle\chi(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{L}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{n-1/2}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{L}(n-\frac{1}{2})z},\quad\bar{\chi}(\bar{z})=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{L}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bar{b}_{n-1/2}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{L}(n-\frac{1}{2})\bar{z}}\,,

(S78)

where
z=\tau+ix
is the complex coordinate. The modes in momentum space satisfy
b^{\dagger}_{n-1/2}=b_{-n+1/2}
and
\bar{b}^{\dagger}_{n-1/2}=\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}
as well as the following anticommutation relations:
\{b_{n-1/2},b^{\dagger}_{m-1/2}\}=\{\bar{b}_{n-1/2},\bar{b}^{\dagger}_{m-1/2}\}=\delta_{n,m}
and
\{b_{n-1/2},\bar{b}_{m-1/2}\}=0
.

The Virasoro generators can be expressed in terms of these modes as

\displaystyle L_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m-1/2):b_{n-m+1/2}b_{m-1/2}:\,,\quad\bar{L}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m-1/2):\bar{b}_{n-m+1/2}\bar{b}_{m-1/2}:\,,

(S79)

where
:\cdots:
denotes normal ordering. The primary states
|\mathbbm{1}\rangle
and
|\varepsilon\rangle
are

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{1}\rangle=|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}\,,\quad|\varepsilon\rangle=-ib_{-1/2}\bar{b}_{-1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}\,,

(S80)

corresponding to the vacuum and the first excited state in the NS sector, respectively.

The crosscap Ishibashi states associated with the primary fields
\mathbbm{1}
and
\varepsilon
are given by
[
21
]

\displaystyle|\alpha\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{p_{n}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{np_{n}}L_{-n}^{p_{n}}\bar{L}_{-n}^{p_{n}}\right]|\alpha\rangle\,,\quad\alpha=\mathbbm{1},\varepsilon\,.

(S81)

Utilizing the fermionic representation of the Virasoro generators [Eq. (
S79
)] and the associated primary states [Eq. (
S80
)], these Ishibashi states can be expanded in the fermionic basis as

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{M\;\mathrm{even}}\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{M}}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}(n_{\alpha}-1/2)}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}\,,

\displaystyle|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{M\;\mathrm{odd}}\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{M}}(-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}(n_{\alpha}-1/2)}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}\,.

(S82)

D.3
C. Continuum counterparts of the lattice crosscap states

The Ising CFT is the effective theory of the critical Ising chain [Eq. (
S1
) with
h=1
], where the lattice Majorana fermion operators
\chi_{j}
and
\bar{\chi}_{j}
in Eq. (
S9
) are identified with the Majorana fields
\chi(x)
and
\bar{\chi}(x)
in Eq. (
S78
), and the Bogoliubov modes near
\pm\pi
are identified with the momentum modes of the Majorana fields:

\displaystyle b_{n-1/2}\leftrightarrow\begin{cases}d_{k=\pi-\frac{2\pi}{N}(n-1/2)}(h=1)&\quad n>0\\
d_{k=\pi+\frac{2\pi}{N}(n-1/2)}^{\dagger}(h=1)&\quad n\leq 0\\
\end{cases}\,,\quad\bar{b}_{n-1/2}\leftrightarrow\begin{cases}id_{k=-\pi+\frac{2\pi}{N}(n-1/2)}(h=1)&\quad n>0\\
-id_{k=-\pi-\frac{2\pi}{N}(n-1/2)}^{\dagger}(h=1)&\quad n\leq 0\\
\end{cases}\,,

(S83)

which is valid for the low-energy mode with
|n|/N\ll 1
in the continuum limit. Therefore, the low-energy “paired” eigenstate [Eq. (
S54
)] of the critical Ising chain is identified as

\displaystyle|\psi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}(h=1)\rangle\leftrightarrow\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{k_{\alpha}}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{k_{\alpha}}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}\,,

(S84)

and the continuum counterparts of the lattice crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
[Eq. (
S66
)] are identified as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\frac{e^{i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}|B_{\pm}\rangle+\frac{e^{-i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}|B_{\mp}\rangle

(S85)

with

\displaystyle|B_{\pm}\rangle=\exp\left[\pm\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}b_{-n+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{b}_{-n+\frac{1}{2}}\right]|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}\,.

(S86)

This corresponds to Eq. (12) in the main text.

To identify
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
as the conformal crosscap states, we notice that the crosscap Ishibashi states
|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}
and
|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}
[Eq. (
S82
)] can be expressed in terms of
|B_{\pm}\rangle
[Eq. (
S86
)]:

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{M\;\mathrm{even}}\sum_{0<n_{1}\cdots<n_{M}}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}(n_{\alpha}-1/2)}\,(-1)^{M/2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{M\;\mathrm{even}}\sum_{0<n_{1}\cdots<n_{M}}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{\alpha}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(|B_{+}\rangle+|B_{-}\rangle\right)

(S87)

and

\displaystyle|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{M\;\mathrm{odd}}\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{M}}(-1)^{-1/2+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}(n_{\alpha}-1/2)}\,(-1)^{\frac{M-1}{2}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{M\;\mathrm{odd}}\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{M}}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{\alpha}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left(|B_{+}\rangle-|B_{-}\rangle\right)\,,

(S88)

where we used

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{M}{2}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}&\quad M\;\mathrm{even}\\
(-1)^{\frac{M-1}{2}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}&\quad M\;\mathrm{odd}\end{cases}.

(S89)

Therefore,
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
are the following linear combinations of the crosscap Ishibashi states:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\pm\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,.

(S90)

This corresponds to Eq. (13) in the main text.
Comparing with Eqs. (
S75
) and (
S76
), we find that
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
are the (standard) PSS conformal crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
and its dual
\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}}{\langle\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}\rangle}|\mathcal{C}_{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
, respectively.

Moreover, from Eq. (
S67
), we can derive the continuum counterpart of the modified lattice crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{-ie^{i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}|B_{+}\rangle+\frac{ie^{-i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}|B_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}-\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,,

(S91)

where we used Eqs. (
S87
) and (
S88
). This proves
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\rangle
, where
|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\rangle
is one of the PSS crosscap states in Eq. (
S75
).

Having identified the continuum counterparts of
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}^{\pm}\rangle
and
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
in the Ising CFT, we find that Eq. (
S34
) is the perfect lattice correspondence of Eq. (
S76
), which can be interpreted as the interplay of the Kramers-Wannier TDL and the PSS crosscap states.

D.4
D. Exact crosscap overlaps in the presence of thermal perturbation

For the Ising CFT [Eq. (
S77
)] with thermal perturbation (
\varepsilon=-i\chi\bar{\chi}
), the perturbed Hamiltonian (in the NS sector) is still quadratic in the fermionic representation:

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{NS}}=H^{\mathrm{NS}}_{0}-g_{1}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,\varepsilon(x)=\frac{i}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\left[\chi(x)\partial_{x}\chi(x)-\bar{\chi}(x)\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}(x)\right]+\frac{im}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,\chi(x)\bar{\chi}(x)\,,

(S92)

where
g_{1}=m/2\pi
and
z=\tau+ix
. The mode expansions of the Majorana fields
\chi
and
\bar{\chi}
are given in Eq. (
S78
).

The perturbed Hamiltonian can be diagonalized with a Bogoliubov transformation
[
43
]

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{NS}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\pi}{L}(n-\frac{1}{2})(b_{-n+1/2}b_{n-1/2}-\bar{b}_{n-1/2}\bar{b}_{-n+1/2})+im(b_{-n+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}-\bar{b}_{n-1/2}b_{n-1/2})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{L}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+s_{1}^{2}}\left(\eta_{n-1/2}^{\dagger}\eta_{n-1/2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,

(S93)

where
s_{1}=g_{1}L=\frac{mL}{2\pi}
is the dimensionless coupling. The annihilation operator of the Bogoliubov mode is given by

\displaystyle\eta_{n-1/2}(s_{1})=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta_{n-1/2}(s_{1})}{2}\right)b_{n-1/2}+i\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta_{n-1/2}(s_{1})}{2}\right)\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}

(S94)

with

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{n-1/2}(s_{1})=\frac{s_{1}}{\sqrt{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+s_{1}^{2}}}\,,\quad\sin\theta_{n-1/2}(s_{1})=\frac{n-1/2}{\sqrt{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+s_{1}^{2}}}\,,\quad\theta_{n-1/2}\in(-\pi,\pi)\,.

(S95)

The perturbed ground state of Eq. (
S93
) is given by

\displaystyle|\psi_{0}(s_{1})\rangle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta_{n-1/2}}{2}\right)+i\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta_{n-1/2}}{2}\right)\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}b_{-n+1/2}\right]|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}\,,

(S96)

where
|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}
is the Ising CFT ground state.

The crosscap overlap calculation is similar to the lattice case (see Sec.
C.2
):

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s_{1})|\exp\left[\pm\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}b_{-n+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}\right]|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}=\exp\left[\pm i\Theta(s_{1})\right]\,,

(S97)

and thus

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s_{1})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\frac{e^{i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\pm i\Theta(s_{1})}+\frac{e^{-i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\mp i\Theta(s_{1})}=\sqrt{2}\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{8}\pm\Theta(s_{1})\right]\,,

(S98)

where

\displaystyle\Theta(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\theta_{n-1/2}(s_{1})}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left[\frac{\pi}{4}-\arctan\left(\frac{n-\frac{1}{2}}{s_{1}+\sqrt{(n-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+s_{1}^{2}}}\right)\right]\,.

(S99)

As a side remark, we note that the above expression of
\Theta(s_{1})
is expanded in orders of the energy levels. This indicates that the truncated conformal space approach may serve as an effective
non-perturbative
method for calculating the crosscap overlap. In fact, by retaining about one hundred terms in the expansion of
\Theta(s_{1})
, one can already obtain the universal scaling function for the crosscap overlap with very high precision.

The series expansion shows that
\Theta(s_{1})
is an odd function of
s_{1}
:

\displaystyle\Theta(s_{1})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2k+1}}\right]\cdot\frac{s_{1}^{2k+1}}{4k+2}=-\Theta(-s_{1})\,.

(S100)

Actually, the crosscap overlap in Eq. (
S98
) is in perfect agreement with the exact solution of the Klein bottle entropy obtained in Ref.
[
43
]
if one uses

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{8}\pm\Theta(s_{1})\right]=\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+e^{\mp 2\pi s_{1}}}}}\,.

(S101)

When approaching the critical point (
s_{1}\to 0
), the Bogoliubov modes reduce to Majorana modes in the Ising CFT

\displaystyle\eta_{n-1/2}(s_{1}\to 0)=\begin{cases}b_{n-1/2}&\quad n>0\\
i\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}&\quad n\leq 0\\
\end{cases}\,.

(S102)

Viewed as a continuous deformation of the (unperturbed) CFT eigenstates
|\psi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{M}}(0)\rangle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}
, the perturbed eigenstates

\displaystyle|\psi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{M}}(s_{1})\rangle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\left[i\;\eta_{n-1/2}^{\dagger}(s_{1})\eta_{-n+1/2}^{\dagger}(s_{1})\right]|\psi_{0}(s_{1})\rangle

(S103)

have the following non-vanishing overlaps with the crosscap states:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{M}}(s_{1})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{\alpha}}\sqrt{2}\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{8}\pm\Theta(s_{1})\right]=(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+e^{\mp 2\pi s_{1}}}}}&\quad M\;\mathrm{even}\\
\pm i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{\alpha}}\sqrt{2}\sin\left[\frac{\pi}{8}\pm\Theta(s_{1})\right]=\pm i(-1)^{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}n_{\alpha}}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+e^{\mp 2\pi s_{1}}}}}&\quad M\;\mathrm{odd}\\
\end{cases}\,,

(S104)

where the calculation is similar to the lattice case (see Sec. III C). This is the continuum counterpart of the lattice crosscap overlaps in Eq. (
S58
). However, we note that in the off-critical region of the lattice model (
h\neq 1
), finite-size corrections are present, unlike the critical point (
h=1
) where finite-size corrections are absent.

In summary, we have the expansion of the crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
in the eigenbasis of the Ising CFT with the thermal perturbation:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\frac{e^{i\left[\frac{\pi}{8}\pm\Theta(s_{1})\right]}}{\sqrt{2}}\exp\left[\pm i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\eta^{\dagger}_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\dagger}_{-n+\frac{1}{2}}\right]|\psi_{0}(s_{1})\rangle+\frac{e^{-i\left[\frac{\pi}{8}\pm\Theta(s_{1})\right]}}{\sqrt{2}}\exp\left[\mp i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\eta^{\dagger}_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\dagger}_{-n+\frac{1}{2}}\right]|\psi_{0}(s_{1})\rangle\,,

(S105)

where
\Theta(s_{1})
has been defined in Eq. (
S101
). More specifically, the squared norms of the crosscap overlaps take the simple form,

\displaystyle|\langle\psi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{M}}(s_{1})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle|^{2}=1+\frac{(-1)^{M}}{\sqrt{1+e^{\mp 2\pi s_{1}}}}\,,

(S106)

where
|\psi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{M}}(s_{1})\rangle
is the eigenstate deformed from the unperturbed state
|\psi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{M}}(0)\rangle\equiv\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}b_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}\bar{b}_{-n_{\alpha}+1/2}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}
in the Ising CFT basis.

Appendix E
V. Crosscap correlators for the Ising CFT

In this section, we extend the bosonization approach for calculating the crosscap correlators of the Ising CFT, i.e., the conformal correlation functions on the real projective plane (
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
). The general multi-point crosscap correlators for both
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
fields are derived in this framework.

E.1
A. Bosonization of crosscap states

To develop the bosonization formalism of the Ising crosscap correlators, we begin with two copies of the Ising CFTs [Eq. (
S77
)], which together form the Dirac fermion CFT. Let
\chi_{1}(\bar{\chi}_{1})
and
\chi_{2}(\bar{\chi}_{2})
denote the (anti-)chiral Majorana fields of the two Ising CFTs, respectively. The corresponding (anti-)chiral Dirac fermion fields are then given by

\displaystyle\Psi(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\chi_{1}(z)+i\chi_{2}(z)\right)\,,\quad\Psi^{\dagger}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\chi_{1}(z)-i\chi_{2}(z)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\bar{\chi}_{1}(\bar{z})+i\bar{\chi}_{2}(\bar{z})\right)\,,\quad\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\bar{\chi}_{1}(\bar{z})-i\bar{\chi}_{2}(\bar{z})\right)\,,

(S107)

where
z=\tau+ix
is the complex coordinate. The Dirac fermion fields satisfy the anti-periodic boundary condition in the NS sector.

The Dirac fermion CFT is equivalent to the compactified boson CFT via the following bosonization identity
[
12
]
:

\displaystyle\Psi(z)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi w}{L}}:e^{i\phi(w)}:\,,\quad\Psi^{\dagger}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi w}{L}}:e^{-i\phi(w)}:\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\bar{w}}{L}}:e^{i\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})}:\,,\quad\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bar{z})=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\bar{w}}{L}}:e^{-i\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})}:

(S108)

with
w=e^{\frac{2\pi}{L}z}
. The prefactor
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi w}{L}}
arises from the conformal transformation
z=\frac{L}{2\pi}\ln w
, which maps the plane to the cylinder.

To ensure consistency with the boundary conditions of the Dirac fermions, the compactified boson field
\varphi(x,\tau)=\phi(w)+\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})
has the radius
R=2
:
\varphi(x+L,\tau)\sim\varphi(x,\tau)+2\pi mR
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
is referred to as the winding number. For convenience, we divide the (anti-)chiral boson field
\phi
(
\bar{\phi}
) into the zero-mode part and the oscillatory part
\phi^{\prime}
(
\bar{\phi}^{\prime}
):

\displaystyle\phi(w)=x_{0}-ia_{0}\ln w+\phi^{\prime}(w)\,,\quad\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})

\displaystyle=\bar{x}_{0}-i\bar{a}_{0}\ln\bar{w}+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(\bar{w})\,,

(S109)

where the oscillatory part can be further decomposed into the positive and negative mode parts, given by the mode expansion:

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(w)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\prime}_{+}(w)+\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)=-i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}w^{k}a_{-k}+i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}w^{-k}a_{k}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(\bar{w})

\displaystyle=\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(\bar{w})+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})=-i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\bar{w}^{k}\bar{a}_{-k}+i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\bar{w}^{-k}\bar{a}_{k}\,.

(S110)

The momentum modes satisfy commutation relations
[x_{0},a_{0}]=[\bar{x}_{0},\bar{a}_{0}]=i
and
[a_{k},a_{l}]=[\bar{a}_{k},\bar{a}_{l}]=k\delta_{k+l}\;\forall k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Virasoro primary states of the compactified boson CFT are labeled by the eigenvalues of
a_{0}
and
\bar{a}_{0}
:

\displaystyle a_{0}|n,m\rangle=\left(\frac{n}{2}+m\right)|n,m\rangle\,,\quad\bar{a}_{0}|n,m\rangle=\left(\frac{n}{2}-m\right)|n,m\rangle\,,

(S111)

which can be generated from the ground state
|0,0\rangle
via the ladder operator, sometimes referred to as the
Klein factor
in the literature
[
75
]
:

\displaystyle e^{i(x_{0}+\bar{x}_{0})/2}|n,m\rangle=|n+1,m\rangle\,,\quad e^{i(x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0})}|n,m\rangle=|n,m+1\rangle\,.

(S112)

The normal-ordered vertex operators are defined as follows

\displaystyle:e^{i\phi(w)}:

\displaystyle=e^{ix_{0}}e^{a_{0}\ln w}:e^{i\phi^{\prime}(w)}:=e^{ix_{0}}e^{a_{0}\ln w}e^{i\phi^{\prime}_{+}(w)}e^{i\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)}\,,

\displaystyle:e^{i\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})}:

\displaystyle=e^{i\bar{x}_{0}}e^{\bar{a}_{0}\ln\bar{w}}:e^{i\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(\bar{w})}:=e^{i\bar{x}_{0}}e^{\bar{a}_{0}\ln\bar{w}}e^{i\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(\bar{w})}e^{i\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})}\,.~

(S113)

The key observation for bosonizing the crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\frac{e^{i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}|B_{+}\rangle+\frac{e^{-i\pi/8}}{\sqrt{2}}|B_{-}\rangle
is that the fermionic Gaussian states
|B_{\pm}\rangle
[Eq. (
S86
)] satisfy the following constraints:

\displaystyle[b_{n-1/2}\pm(-1)^{n}\bar{b}_{-n+1/2}]|B_{\pm}\rangle=0\,,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(S114)

This is equivalent to the condition
[\chi(x)\mp i\bar{\chi}(x+L/2)]|B_{\pm}\rangle=0
, where
\chi
and
\bar{\chi}
are the Majorana fields [Eq. (
S78
)] in the Ising CFT. Consequently, for two copies of such fermionic Gaussian states
|B_{\pm}\rangle
, we obtain the constraints on the Dirac fields:

\displaystyle\left[\Psi(x)\mp i\bar{\Psi}(x+L/2)\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\left[\Psi(x)\mp i\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x+L/2)\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(S115)

These constraints can be translated into the bosonic language using the bosonization identity [Eq. (
S108
)]:

\displaystyle\left[e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}x}:e^{i\phi(x)}:\mp\;ie^{-i\frac{\pi}{L}(x+L/2)}:e^{i\bar{\phi}(x+L/2)}:\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\left[e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}x}:e^{i\phi(x)}:\mp\;ie^{-i\frac{\pi}{L}(x+L/2)}:e^{-i\bar{\phi}(x+L/2)}:\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)}

\displaystyle=0\,,~

(S116)

which completely determine the expansions of
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}
and
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)}
in the bosonic basis.

We first analyse the bosonization of
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}
. Inserting the definition of the normal-ordered vertex operator [Eq. (
S113
)] into the bosonic constraint [Eq. (
S116
)], we have

\displaystyle\left[e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}x}e^{ix_{0}}e^{i\frac{2\pi x}{L}a_{0}}:e^{i\phi^{\prime}(x)}:\mp\;ie^{-i\frac{\pi}{L}(x+L/2)}e^{i\bar{x}_{0}}e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{L}(x+L/2)\bar{a}_{0}}:e^{i\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(x+L/2)}:\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}=0\,.

(S117)

Since the constraint is valid for all
x\in[0,L)
, we obtain the constraint for the oscillatory part:

\displaystyle\left[:e^{i\phi^{\prime}(x)}:-:e^{i\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(x+L/2)}:\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}=0\,,\quad x\in[0,L)\,,

(S118)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left[a_{k}+(-1)^{k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}=0\,,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(S119)

by comparing with the mode expansions [Eq. (
S110
)] of boson fields
\phi^{\prime}
and
\bar{\phi}^{\prime}
.
This constraint completely determines the oscillatory part of
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}
:

\displaystyle|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}=\exp\left[-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\pm}_{n,m}|n,m\rangle\,,

(S120)

where the zero mode part remains to be fixed.

The zero mode part satisfies the following constraint:

\displaystyle\left[e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}x}e^{ix_{0}}e^{i\frac{2\pi x}{L}a_{0}}\mp ie^{-i\frac{\pi}{L}(x+L/2)}e^{i\bar{x}_{0}}e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{L}(x+L/2)\bar{a}_{0}}\right]\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\pm}_{n,m}|n,m\rangle=0\,,

(S121)

which simplifies to:

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\pm}_{n,m}\left[e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}x}e^{ix_{0}}e^{i\frac{2\pi x}{L}(n/2+m)}\mp ie^{-i\frac{\pi}{L}(x+L/2)}e^{i\bar{x}_{0}}e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{L}(x+L/2)(n/2-m)}\right]|n,m\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\pm}_{n,m}\left[e^{i\frac{2\pi x}{L}(\frac{n+1}{2}+m)}e^{ix_{0}}|n,m\rangle\mp(-i)^{n}(-1)^{m}e^{-i\frac{2\pi x}{L}(\frac{n+1}{2}-m)}e^{ix_{0}}|n,m-1\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[e^{i\frac{2\pi x}{L}(m+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{2})}c^{\pm}_{n,m}\pm(-i)^{n}(-1)^{m}e^{i\frac{2\pi x}{L}(m+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2})}c^{\pm}_{n,m+1}\right]e^{ix_{0}}|n,m\rangle

\displaystyle=0\,,

(S122)

where we used the relation
e^{i(\bar{x}_{0}-x_{0})}|n,m\rangle=|n,m-1\rangle
. Therefore, the coefficients are determined as
c^{\pm}_{n,m+1}=\delta_{n,0}c^{\pm}_{0,m+1}
and
c^{\pm}_{0,m}\pm(-1)^{m}c^{\pm}_{0,m+1}=0
. Consequently, we determine the bosonic representation of
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}
completely:

\displaystyle|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}=\exp\left[-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(\pm)^{m}(-1)^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}}|0,m\rangle\,,

(S123)

where the normalization factor is fixed by
\langle 0,0|(|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)})=({}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|B_{\pm}\rangle)^{2}=1
, since the boson vacuum is identified as two copies of the Ising CFT ground states:
|0,0\rangle=|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}^{(1)}|0\rangle_{\mathrm{NS}}^{(2)}
in the bosonization.

The bosonization of
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)}
is similar to the derivation for
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(2)}
. The oscillatory part is determined by

\displaystyle\left[a_{k}-(-1)^{k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)}=0\,,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(S124)

and the zero-mode part is fixed as

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\pm)^{n}(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}|2n,0\rangle\,.

(S125)

Therefore, the bosonic representation of
|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)}
is given by

\displaystyle|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)}=\exp\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\pm)^{n}(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}|2n,0\rangle

(S126)

with the normalization
\langle 0,0|(|B_{\pm}\rangle^{(1)}|B_{\mp}\rangle^{(2)})={}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|B_{+}\rangle\cdot{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|B_{-}\rangle=1
.

However, there is a subtle point in the bosonization of the Ising CFT, where the operator content of two copies of Ising CFTs is not the ordinary Dirac CFT due to the constraints imposed by the fermion parity-dependent space-time boundary conditions. In fact, the bosonization of two copies of Ising CFTs is the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

orbifold
compactified boson CFT. Any physical bosonic crosscap state should be consistent with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
orbifold constraint, i.e., invariant under the reflection of the boson field:
\varphi\leftrightarrow-\varphi
, which is realized via the operator
G
, with the action:
G|n,m\rangle=|-m,-n\rangle
and
G(a_{k},\bar{a}_{k})G^{-1}=(-a_{k},-\bar{a}_{k})
,
\forall n,m,k\in\mathbb{Z}

[
12
]
.

We find that the two copies of the Ising Ishibashi states
|\alpha\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}|\alpha\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}
, with
\alpha=\mathbbm{1},\varepsilon
, are suitable crosscap states for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
orbifold boson CFT:

\displaystyle|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}(|B_{+}\rangle^{(1)}+|B_{-}\rangle^{(1)})\right]\left[\frac{1}{2}(|B_{+}\rangle^{(2)}+|B_{-}\rangle^{(2)})\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle+|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle\right)\,,

\displaystyle|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{i}{2}(|B_{+}\rangle^{(1)}-|B_{-}\rangle^{(1)})\right]\left[\frac{i}{2}(|B_{+}\rangle^{(2)}-|B_{-}\rangle^{(2)})\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle-|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle\right)\,,

(S127)

with

\displaystyle|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}|4n,0\rangle\,,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}|0,2m\rangle\,,

(S128)

satisfying
G|\mathcal{O}_{\pm}\rangle=|\mathcal{O}_{\pm}\rangle
. Here, we used the fermionic representation of the Ishibashi states in Eqs. (
S87
) and (
S88
).

The crosscap correlators can be calculated in each Ishibashi sector separately.

E.2
B. Wick’s theorem with crosscap states involoved

The bosonization dictionary for two copies of
\varepsilon
fields and
\sigma
fields is given by
[
12
]

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}(w,\bar{w})\varepsilon_{2}(w,\bar{w})=\partial\phi(w)\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})\,,\quad\sigma_{1}(w,\bar{w})\sigma_{2}(w,\bar{w})=\sqrt{2}:\cos\left[\vartheta(w,\bar{w})/2\right]:\,,

(S129)

where
\vartheta(w,\bar{w})=\phi(w)-\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})
is the
dual
compactified boson field with radius
R^{\prime}=2/R=1
, and
w=e^{\frac{2\pi}{L}z}
is the plane coordinate.

To effectively calculate the multipoint crosscap correlators of
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
fields, we apply Wick’s theorems. For the U(1) current, Wick’s theorem gives

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq m\leq\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}\sum_{1\leq k_{1}<\cdots k_{2m}\leq n}:\prod_{j\neq k_{1},\cdots k_{2m}}\partial\phi(w_{j}):\frac{1}{2^{m}m!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2m}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{m}\langle\partial\phi(w_{k_{\sigma(2\alpha-1)}})\partial\phi(w_{k_{\sigma(2\alpha)}})\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq m\leq\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}\sum_{1\leq k_{1}<\cdots k_{2m}\leq n}:\prod_{j\neq k_{1},\cdots k_{2m}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j}):\frac{1}{2^{m}m!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2m}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{m}\langle\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{k_{\sigma(2\alpha-1)}})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{k_{\sigma(2\alpha)}})\rangle\,,

(S130)

where
\langle\partial\phi(w)\partial\phi(w^{\prime})\rangle=-\frac{1}{(w-w^{\prime})^{2}}
and
\langle\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}^{\prime})\rangle=-\frac{1}{(\bar{w}-\bar{w}^{\prime})^{2}}
are the (anti-)chiral current-current correlators on the plane, respectively.
For the vertex operators, the theorem gives

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}:e^{i\alpha_{j}\vartheta(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})}:=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|w_{j}-w_{k}|^{2\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}}:e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\vartheta(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})}:\,.

(S131)

The proofs of these theorems can be found in standard textbooks, such as Ref.
[
12
]
.

The theorems stated above are the ordinary Wick’s theorems. To account for crosscap states, we also need the following two generalized Wick’s theorems:

Theorem 1
:

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w_{j})\prod_{l=1}^{m}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}_{l})e^{\pm K}

\displaystyle=e^{\pm K}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left[\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w_{j})\mp\frac{1}{w_{j}^{2}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w_{j})\right]\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left[\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}_{l})\mp\frac{1}{\bar{w}_{l}^{2}}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{l})\right]\,,

(S132)

Theorem 2
:

\displaystyle:e^{i\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})}:\;e^{\pm K}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j,l\leq n}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{l}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}}e^{\pm K\pm i\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{j},-1/w_{j})}\;:e^{i\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})}:\,,

(S133)

where

\displaystyle K=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}

(S134)

is the quadratic form of boson modes, and
\phi^{\prime}
(
\bar{\phi}^{\prime}
) represents the oscillatory part of the (anti-)chiral boson field [Eq. (
S110
)], while
\vartheta^{\prime}
denotes the oscillatory part of the dual boson field:

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})=\phi^{\prime}(w)-\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(\bar{w})\equiv\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})+\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})\,,

(S135)

which can be divided into positive and negative energy contributions.

To prove the above generalized Wick’s theorems, we first introduce the following lemma:

Lemma 1
:

\displaystyle[\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w),K]

\displaystyle=-\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w)\,,\quad[\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}),K]=-\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})\,.

(S136)

This lemma can be verified straightforwardly by comparing the mode expansions [Eq. (
S110
)]:

\displaystyle[\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w),K]

\displaystyle=i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}w^{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}=i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}(-1/w)^{k}\bar{a}_{-k}=-\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w)\,,

\displaystyle[\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}),K]

\displaystyle=i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\bar{w}^{-k}a_{-k}=i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}(-1/\bar{w})^{k}a_{-k}=-\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})\,,

(S137)

where we used the commutation relations
[\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w),a_{-k}]=iw^{-k}
and
[\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}),a_{-k}]=i\bar{w}^{-k}
,
\forall k>0
.

Theorem 1
[Eq. (
S132
)] is a corollary of
Lemma 1
. Since
[\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w),K]=-\frac{1}{w^{2}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w)
and
[\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}),K]=-\frac{1}{\bar{w}^{2}}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})
, we have

\displaystyle\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)e^{\pm K}=e^{\pm K}\left[\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)\mp\frac{1}{w^{2}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w)\right]\,,\quad\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})e^{\pm K}=e^{\pm K}\left[\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})\mp\frac{1}{\bar{w}^{2}}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})\right]\,,

(S138)

from which
Theorem 1
is derived.

To prove
Theorem 2
[Eq. (
S133
)], an additional lemma is needed:

Lemma 2
:

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w,\bar{w})}\,e^{\pm K}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{w\bar{w}}\right)^{\pm\alpha^{2}}e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w},-1/w)}e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w,\bar{w})}\,,

(S139)

which can be proved as follows:

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w,\bar{w})}\,e^{\pm K}

\displaystyle=e^{i\alpha\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)}\left(e^{-i\alpha\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})}\,e^{\pm K}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{i\alpha\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)}\left(e^{\pm K}e^{-i\alpha\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})}e^{\mp i\alpha[\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}),\,K]}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(e^{i\alpha\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)}e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})}\right)e^{-i\alpha\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})}e^{i\alpha\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)}e^{\pm i\alpha[\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w),\,K+i\alpha\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})]}\right)e^{-i\alpha\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})}

\displaystyle=e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha[\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})-\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w)]}e^{\mp\alpha^{2}\langle\phi^{\prime}(w)\phi^{\prime}(-1/\bar{w})\rangle}e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w,\bar{w})}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{w\bar{w}}\right)^{\pm\alpha^{2}}e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w},-1/w)}e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w,\bar{w})}\,,

(S140)

where in the second and fourth equalities, we used the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula:
e^{A}e^{B}=e^{A+B+\frac{1}{2}[A,B]}=e^{B}e^{A}e^{[A,B]}
, assuming
[[A,B],A]=[[A,B],B]=0
; in the third and fifth equalities, we used the
Lemma 1
[Eq. (
S136
)]; and in the last equality, we used the correlator
[\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w),\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})]=\langle\phi^{\prime}(w)\phi^{\prime}(-1/\bar{w})\rangle=-\ln(1+\frac{1}{w\bar{w}})
.

The simplest case (
n=1
) of
Theorem 2
can be derived directly from
Lemma 2
[Eq. (
S139
)]:

\displaystyle:e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}(w,\bar{w})}:\,e^{\pm K}

\displaystyle=e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w,\bar{w})}\left(e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w,\bar{w})}\,e^{\pm K}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{w\bar{w}}\right)^{\pm\alpha^{2}}e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w},-1/w)}:e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}(w,\bar{w})}:\,.

(S141)

The general case (
n\geq 1
) can be proved by induction. For instance, we prove the
n=2
case below:

\displaystyle:e^{i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})+i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}:\,e^{\pm K}

\displaystyle=e^{i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})+i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}e^{i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}\left(e^{i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})}e^{\pm K}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{i\sum\limits_{j=1,2}\alpha_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})}e^{i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{1}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{1}^{2}}e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{1},-1/w_{1})}e^{i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{1}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{1}^{2}}e^{i\sum\limits_{j=1,2}\alpha_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{2}\bar{w}_{2}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{2}^{2}}e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{2},-1/w_{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}e^{\pm i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{1},-1/w_{1})}e^{i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{1}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{1}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{2}\bar{w}_{2}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{2}^{2}}e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{2},-1/w_{2})}e^{i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})+i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(e^{\pm i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{1},-1/w_{1})}e^{i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}e^{\mp\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\langle\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})\vartheta^{\prime}(-1/\bar{w}_{1},-1/w_{1})\rangle}\right)e^{i\alpha\vartheta^{\prime}_{-}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{1}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{1}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{2}\bar{w}_{2}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{2}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{2}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{2}\bar{w}_{1}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{\pm K\pm i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{1},-1/w_{1})\pm i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{2},-1/w_{2})}:e^{i\alpha_{1}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})+i\alpha_{2}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})}:

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j,l\leq 2}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{l}}\right)^{\pm\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}}e^{\pm K\pm i\sum\limits_{j=1,2}\alpha_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{j},-1/w_{j})}\;:e^{i\sum\limits_{j=1,2}\alpha_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})}:\,,

(S142)

where, in the second equality, we used the result for the
n=1
case [Eq. (
S141
)] by induction. We also used the definition of the normal-ordered vertex operators [Eq. (
S113
)] and the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula [Below Eq. (
S140
)], along with the correlator

\displaystyle\langle\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})\vartheta^{\prime}(-1/\bar{w}_{1},-1/w_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi^{\prime}(w_{2})\phi^{\prime}(-1/\bar{w}_{1})\rangle+\langle\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{2})\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(-1/w_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{2}\bar{w}_{1}}\right)-\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{2}}\right)\,.

(S143)

The
n

Using Eq. (

), we can calculate the
n
-point crosscap correlator of the
\varepsilon
field in each Ishibashi sector respectively:

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\;{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\pm\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}\;{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,,

(S144)

where the bosonization of the crosscap correlator in each Ishibashi sector is given by

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})\left(|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle+|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle\right)}\,,

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})\left(|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle-|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle\right)}\,,

(S145)

with

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{O}_{\pm}\rangle=\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})e^{\pm K}|0,0\rangle\,,

(S146)

which are the crosscap correlators in the compactified boson CFT. We used the bosonization of two copies of
\varepsilon
fields [Eq. (
S129
)] and the bosonization of two copies of Ishibashi states [Eq. (
S127
)], where
|0,0\rangle
is the boson ground state and
|\mathcal{O}_{\pm}\rangle
are the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
orbifold bosonic crosscap states in Eq. (
S128
).

To calculate the bosonic crosscap correlators, we first use Wick’s theorem [Eq. (
S130
)] to express the time-ordered operator product in a normal-ordered form:

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;\;\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})e^{\pm K}|0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq p,q\leq\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}(-1)^{p+q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{2p}\leq n\\
1\leq k^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<k^{\prime}_{2q}\leq n\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Hf}\left[\frac{1}{(w_{k_{\alpha}}-w_{k_{\beta}})^{2}}\right]_{\alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,2p}\mathrm{Hf}\left[\frac{1}{(w_{k^{\prime}_{\alpha}}-w_{k^{\prime}_{\beta}})^{2}}\right]_{\alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,2q}

\displaystyle\quad\times\langle 0,0|:\prod_{j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}}\partial\phi(w_{j})\prod_{l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2q}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{l}):e^{\pm K}|0,0\rangle\,,

(S147)

where
\mathrm{Hf}(B)
stands for the Hafnian of a symmetric
2n\times 2n
matrix
B
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hf}(B)=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n}B_{\sigma(2\alpha-1),\sigma(2\alpha)}\,,

(S148)

and
[\frac{n}{2}]
denotes the integer part of
\frac{n}{2}
. Next, using the generalized Wick’s theorem in Eq. (
S132
), the expression simplifies further:

\displaystyle\quad\langle 0,0|:\prod_{j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}}\partial\phi(w_{j})\prod_{l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2q}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{l}):e^{\pm K}|0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,0|\prod_{j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w_{j})\prod_{l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2q}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}_{l})e^{\pm K}|0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,0|\prod_{j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}}\left[\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w_{j})\mp\frac{1}{w_{j}^{2}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w_{j})\right]\prod_{l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2q}}\left[\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}_{l})\mp\frac{1}{\bar{w}_{l}^{2}}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{l})\right]|0,0\rangle\,,

(S149)

where the calculation reduces to the Wick contraction of boson fields, denoted as
\varphi_{A}(w)
and
\varphi_{B}(\bar{w})
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{A}(w)=\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w)\mp\frac{1}{w^{2}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w)\,,\quad\varphi_{B}(\bar{w})=\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w})\mp\frac{1}{\bar{w}^{2}}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w})

(S150)

with their two-point correlators on the plane being

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{A}(w_{j})\varphi_{B}(\bar{w}_{l})\rangle=\mp\frac{1}{\bar{w}_{l}^{2}}\langle\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w_{j})\partial\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{l})\rangle=\mp\frac{1}{\bar{w}_{l}^{2}}\langle\partial\phi(w_{j})\partial\phi(-1/\bar{w}_{l})\rangle=\pm\frac{1}{\bar{w}_{l}^{2}}\frac{1}{(w_{j}+1/\bar{w}_{l})^{2}}=\pm\frac{1}{(1+w_{j}\bar{w}_{l})^{2}}\,,

(S151)

and
\langle\varphi_{A}(w_{j})\varphi_{A}(w_{l})\rangle=\langle\varphi_{B}(\bar{w}_{j})\varphi_{B}(\bar{w}_{l})\rangle=0
. After performing the Wick contraction, we have

\displaystyle\quad\langle 0,0|\prod_{j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}}\left[\partial\phi^{\prime}_{-}(w_{j})\mp\frac{1}{w_{j}^{2}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{+}(-1/w_{j})\right]\prod_{l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2q}}\left[\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-}(\bar{w}_{l})\mp\frac{1}{\bar{w}_{l}^{2}}\partial\phi^{\prime}_{+}(-1/\bar{w}_{l})\right]|0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{p,q}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2p}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}\\
l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2p}\end{subarray}}\langle\varphi_{A}(w_{j})\varphi_{B}(\bar{w}_{\sigma(l)})\rangle=\delta_{p,q}(\pm)^{n-2p}\mathrm{Perm}\left[\frac{1}{(1+w_{j}\bar{w}_{l})^{2}}\right]_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}\\
l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2p}\end{subarray}}\,,

(S152)

where
\mathrm{Perm}(M)
is the permanent of the
n\times n
matrix
M
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Perm}(M)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n}M_{\alpha,\sigma(\alpha)}\,.

(S153)

Finally, we arrive at the bosonic crosscap correlators:

\displaystyle\quad\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{O}_{\pm}\rangle=\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial\phi(w_{j})\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w}_{j})e^{\pm K}|0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=(\pm)^{n}\sum_{0\leq p\leq\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{2p}\leq n\\
1\leq k^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<k^{\prime}_{2p}\leq n\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Hf}\left[\frac{1}{(w_{k_{\alpha}}-w_{k_{\beta}})^{2}}\right]\mathrm{Hf}\left[\frac{1}{(w_{k^{\prime}_{\alpha}}-w_{k^{\prime}_{\beta}})^{2}}\right]_{\alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,2p}\mathrm{Perm}\left[\frac{1}{(1+w_{j}\bar{w}_{l})^{2}}\right]_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}\\
l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2p}\end{subarray}}\,.

(S154)

Thus, the
n
-point crosscap correlator of the
\varepsilon
field is given by

\displaystyle\quad{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=(\pm)^{n}\sqrt{\frac{2+(-1)^{n}\sqrt{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\sum_{0\leq p\leq\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{2p}\leq n\\
1\leq k^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<k^{\prime}_{2p}\leq n\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Hf}\left[\frac{1}{(w_{k_{\alpha}}-w_{k_{\beta}})^{2}}\right]\mathrm{Hf}\left[\frac{1}{(w_{k^{\prime}_{\alpha}}-w_{k^{\prime}_{\beta}})^{2}}\right]_{\alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,2p}\mathrm{Perm}\left[\frac{1}{(1+w_{j}\bar{w}_{l})^{2}}\right]_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq k_{1},\ldots,k_{2p}\\
l\neq k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{2p}\end{subarray}}}\,.

(S155)

In the end, we present the results for the one- and two-point correlators as examples.

For the one-point correlator, we have

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\varepsilon(w,\bar{w})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{1+w\bar{w}}\,,

(S156)

For the two-point correlator, the expression is

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\varepsilon(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\varepsilon(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{(1+w_{1}\bar{w}_{1})^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+w_{2}\bar{w}_{2})^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1+w_{1}\bar{w}_{2})^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+w_{2}\bar{w}_{1})^{2}}+\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{4}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{2}}\sqrt{\eta^{2}+\left(\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}\right)^{2}+1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\eta^{2}}{1-\eta}\right)\,,

(S157)

where

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{2}}{(1+|w_{1}|^{2})(1+|w_{2}|^{2})}

(S158)

is the crosscap cross ratio.

E.4
D.
2n
-point crosscap correlators of the
\sigma
field

According to the fusion rule
\sigma\times\sigma\sim\mathbbm{1}+\varepsilon
,
(2n+1)
-point crosscap correlators of the
\sigma
field vanish. For
2n
-point crosscap correlators, the calculation follows a similar logic to that of the
\varepsilon
field. We express the full crosscap correlator as a combination of two Ishibashi crosscap correlators:

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\sigma(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\sigma(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\pm\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\sigma(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,.

(S159)

Each Ishibashi crosscap correlator is then expressed in terms of bosonic crosscap correlators via bosonization [Eqs. (
S129
) and (
S127
)]:

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\sigma(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2^{n-1}\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})\left(|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle+|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle\right)}\,,

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\sigma(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2^{n-1}\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})\left(|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle-|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle\right)}

(S160)

with

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{O}_{\pm}\rangle=\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})\;e^{\pm K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle\,,

(S161)

where
|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle
denotes the zero-mode parts of the bosonic crosscap states
|\mathcal{O}_{\pm}\rangle
in Eq. (
S128
):

\displaystyle|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{+}\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}|4n,0\rangle\,,\quad|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{-}\rangle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}|0,2m\rangle\,.

(S162)

The Wick’s theorem [Eq. (
S131
)] turns the product of the vertex operators into the normal-ordered form:

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})\;e^{\pm K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left[e^{i\vartheta(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})/2}+e^{-i\vartheta(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})/2}\right]e^{\pm K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}=\pm 1,\\
j=1,\ldots,2n\end{subarray}}\prod_{1\leq j<l\leq 2n}|w_{j}-w_{l}|^{\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/2}\langle 0,0|:e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}\vartheta(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})/2}:e^{\pm K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle\,,

(S163)

and the generalized Wick’s theorem [Eq. (
S133
)] further simplifies the contribution from the oscillatory part of the crosscap states:

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|:e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}\vartheta(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})/2}:e^{\pm K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,0|e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}(x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0})}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}(\ln w_{j}a_{0}-\ln\bar{w}_{j}\bar{a}_{0})/2}:e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}\vartheta^{\prime}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})/2}:e^{\pm K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j,l\leq 2n}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{l}}\right)^{\pm\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/4}\langle 0,0|e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}(x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0})}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}(\ln w_{j}a_{0}-\ln\bar{w}_{j}\bar{a}_{0})/2}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle\,.

(S164)

The remaining zero-mode part can be further simplified by a straightforward computation:

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|e^{\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}(x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0})}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}(\ln w_{j}a_{0}-\ln\bar{w}_{j}\bar{a}_{0})/2}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}|e^{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}(\ln w_{j}a_{0}-\ln\bar{w}_{j}\bar{a}_{0})/2}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}\,\sum_{l=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{l}\ln(w_{l}\bar{w}_{l})\right]\,,

(S165)

where we have used
e^{i\epsilon(x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0})}|0,0\rangle=|0,m=\epsilon\rangle
. By using

\displaystyle\langle 0,-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{+}\rangle=\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},0}\,,\quad\langle 0,-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{-}\rangle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},4m}\,,

(S166)

Eq. (
S165
) reduces to

\displaystyle\langle 0,-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{+}\rangle\cdot\exp\left[-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}\cdot\sum_{l=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{l}\ln(w_{l}\bar{w}_{l})\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},0}\,,

\displaystyle\langle 0,-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{-}\rangle\cdot\exp\left[-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j}\cdot\sum_{l=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{l}\ln(w_{l}\bar{w}_{l})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},4m}\cdot\exp\left[-m\sum_{l=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{l}\ln(w_{l}\bar{w}_{l})\right]\,.

(S167)

Therefore, the bosonic crosscap correlators are given by

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})\;e^{K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}=\pm 1,\\
j=1,\ldots,2n\end{subarray}}\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},0}\prod_{1\leq j<l\leq 2n}|w_{j}-w_{l}|^{\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/2}\prod_{1\leq j,l\leq 2n}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{l}}\right)^{\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}=\pm 1,\\
j=1,\ldots,2n\end{subarray}}\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},0}\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{j}}\right)^{1/4}\prod_{1\leq j<l\leq 2n}\left|(w_{j}-w_{l})\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{l}}\right)\right|^{\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/2}\,,

(S168)

and

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0,0|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{j},\bar{w}_{j})\;e^{-K}|\mathcal{O}^{0}_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}=\pm 1,\\
j=1,\ldots,2n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{m}\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},4m}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;\times\prod_{j=1}^{2n}|w_{j}|^{-2m\epsilon_{j}}\prod_{1\leq j<l\leq 2n}|w_{j}-w_{l}|^{\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/2}\prod_{1\leq j,l\leq 2n}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{l}}\right)^{-\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{j}=\pm 1,\\
j=1,\ldots,2n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{m}\delta_{\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\epsilon_{j},4m}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;\times\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left[|w_{j}|^{-2m\epsilon_{j}}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{j}}\right)^{-1/4}\right]\prod_{1\leq j<l\leq 2n}\left|\frac{1}{w_{j}-w_{l}}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{l}}\right)\right|^{-\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}/2}\,.

(S169)

The general formula for the
2n
-point crosscap correlator of the
\sigma
field is obtained by inserting the above expressions into Eq. (
S160
).

As an example, let us consider the two-point crosscap correlator. Starting with

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})|\mathcal{O}_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\cdot 2\prod_{j=1,2}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{j}}\right)^{1/4}\cdot\left|(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{2}}\right)\right|^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/2}}\left[\frac{|1+w_{1}\bar{w}_{2}|^{2}}{(1+|w_{1}|^{2})(1+|w_{2}|^{2})}\right]^{-1/4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/2}}\cdot(1-\eta)^{-1/4}\,,

(S170)

and

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\cos\frac{\vartheta}{2}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})|\mathcal{O}_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\cdot 2\prod_{j=1,2}\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{j}\bar{w}_{j}}\right)^{-1/4}\cdot\left|\frac{1}{w_{1}-w_{2}}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{w_{1}\bar{w}_{2}}\right)\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/2}}\cdot(1-\eta)^{1/4}\,,

(S171)

we find that the crosscap correlators in two Ishibashi sectors are given by

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\sigma(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\sigma(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/4}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left[(1-\eta)^{-1/4}+(1-\eta)^{1/4}\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/4}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot(1-\eta)^{-1/8}\cdot\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1-\eta}}\,,

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\sigma(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\sigma(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/4}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left[(1-\eta)^{-1/4}-(1-\eta)^{1/4}\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/4}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot(1-\eta)^{-1/8}\cdot\sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-\eta}}\,,

(S172)

so the two-point crosscap correlator of the
\sigma
field reads

\displaystyle{}_{\mathrm{NS}}\langle 0|\sigma(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\sigma(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/4}}\,(1-\eta)^{-1/8}\left[\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1-\eta}}\pm\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-\eta}}\right]\,,

(S173)

where
\eta
is the crosscap cross ratio [Eq. (
S158
)]. This corresponds to Eq. (14) in the main text.

Appendix F
VI. Conformal perturbation theory for the crosscap overlap

The conformal perturbation theory for computing
crosscap overlap
is given in Eqs. (16) and (17) in the main text. Here, we provide a simple derivation and then formulate the perturbation expansion for a general crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}\rangle
up to second order. The application to the perturbed Ising CFT is specifically discussed.

F.1
A. Formal perturbation series

We consider a CFT on a circle with length
L
with a relevant perturbation:
H=H_{0}+H_{1}
, where
H_{0}
is the CFT hamiltonian and
H_{1}=-g\int_{0}^{L}\varphi(x)
is the perturbation term,
\varphi(x)
is a primary field with conformal weight
h=\bar{h}<1
. Denoting the perturbed gorund state as
|\psi_{0}(s)\rangle
, parameterized by the dimensionless coupling
s=gL^{2-2h}
, our aim is to formulate the conformal perturbation theory
[
56
]
for the universal crosscap overlap
\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle
. For practical calculation, we split the crosscap overlap into two parts:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle=\frac{\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\psi_{0}(s)\rangle}\cdot\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\psi_{0}(s)\rangle\equiv Z(s)\cdot\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}W(s)\right]\,,

(S174)

where
|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle
is the unperturbed ground state. Both the universal scaling functions
Z(s)
and
W(s)
can be formulated the formal perturbation series.

For
Z(s)
, we have

\displaystyle Z(s)=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{\langle\psi_{0}(0)|e^{-\beta H}|\mathcal{C}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{0}(0)|e^{-\beta H}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle}=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\mathcal{C}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle}\,,

(S175)

where the unique perturbed ground state
|\psi_{0}(s)\rangle
for finite
L
is projected out in the limit
\beta\to\infty
. In the second equality, we work in the interaction picture via

\displaystyle e^{-\beta H}=e^{-\beta H_{0}}\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau H_{1}(\tau)}\,,

(S176)

where
H_{1}(\tau)=e^{\tau H_{0}}H_{1}e^{-\tau H_{0}}
is the perturbation term in the interaction picture.

For
W(s)=\ln|\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\psi_{0}(s)\rangle|^{2}
, we can consider

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(0)|e^{-\beta H}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle=e^{-\beta E_{0}(s,L)}\left[\exp(W(s))+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\beta\Delta E(s,L)})\right]\,,\quad\beta\to\infty\,,

(S177)

where we expect that the perturbed ground-state energy scales as
E_{0}=-\frac{\pi}{6L}c(s)
and the (finite-size) energy gap scales as
\Delta E(s,L)\sim\frac{1}{L}
, with
s
fixed. Here,
c(s)
, with
c(0)=c
, is the “running” central charge, denoting the deformed central charge under the perturbation. As
\beta\to\infty
, the contribution of the excited states decays exponentially, leaving only the ground-state contribution. Therefore, in the interaction picture, we can extract
W(s)
as follows:

\displaystyle W(s)=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\left[\ln\langle\psi_{0}(0)|e^{\beta H_{0}}e^{-\beta H}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle-\frac{\pi\beta}{6L}\delta c(s)\right]=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\left[\left\langle\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}-\frac{\pi\beta}{6L}\delta c(s)\right]\,,

(S178)

where
\langle\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}
denotes the
connected
contribution of
\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle
, via the linked cluster theorem:
\ln\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}
, and

\displaystyle\delta c(s)=c(s)-c=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{6L}{\pi\beta}\langle\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}

(S179)

is the change of “running” central charge.

The formal perturbation series can be calculated by expanding the time-ordered exponential
\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}
in powers of the coupling
g
. Specifically, for
W(s)
, we expand
\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle
as

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}\tau_{2n}\,\langle H_{1}(\tau_{1})\cdots H_{1}(\tau_{2n})\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{2\beta}{6L}C_{2n}+W_{2n}\right]\cdot s^{2n}\,,

(S180)

where each order of the perturbation series can be split into a divergent term
\frac{2\beta}{6L}C_{2n}
, scaled as
\frac{\beta}{L}
when
\beta\to\infty
, and a subleading finite term
W_{2n}
. The series expansion of
W(s)
is obtained as
W(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{2n}s^{2n}
and the “running” central charge is obtained as
c(s)=c+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{2n}s^{2n}
.

F.2
B. Perturbation correction up to second order

We perform the perturbation expansion up to the second order to illustrate how the conformal perturbation theory works.

A crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}\rangle
can be characterized by the one-point conformal crosscap correlator of primary fields
\varphi_{a}
,

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\varphi_{a}(w,\bar{w})|\mathcal{C}\rangle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{a}}{(1+|w|^{2})^{2h_{a}}}\,,

(S181)

where
\mathcal{A}_{a}
is the crosscap coefficient. The overall phase of the crosscap state can always be fixed by requiring the coefficient associated with the identity operator to be positive,
\mathcal{A}_{0}>0
. Furthermore, the two-point conformal crosscap correlator can be determined up to an unknown prefactor
G_{a}(\eta)
as a function of the crosscap cross ratio
[
22
,
33
,
34
]
:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\varphi_{a}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\varphi_{a}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})|\mathcal{C}\rangle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}\cdot G_{a}(\eta)}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{4h_{a}}}\,,

(S182)

where the crosscap correlators of the
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
fields given in Eqs. (
S156
), (
S157
) and (
S173
) are the specific cases.

We consider the CFT perturbed by the primary field
\varphi_{a}
, with
h_{\alpha}=\bar{h}_{\alpha}<1
,

\displaystyle H=H_{0}+H_{1}\,,\quad H_{1}=-g\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,\varphi_{a}(x)\,,

(S183)

in which the first-order perturbation of the crosscap overlap is non-vanished if
\mathcal{A}_{a}\neq 0
. Up to the second order, we denote the perturbative expansion as

\displaystyle Z(s)

\displaystyle=Z_{0}+Z_{1}\cdot s+Z_{2}\cdot s^{2}+\mathcal{O}(s^{3})\,,

\displaystyle W(s)

\displaystyle=W_{2}\cdot s^{2}+\mathcal{O}(s^{4})

(S184)

with
Z_{0}=\mathcal{A}_{0}
by definition. Then, the perturbative expansion of the crosscap overlap up to second order is given by

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle=\mathcal{A}_{0}+Z_{1}\cdot s+\left(Z_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{0}\,W_{2}\right)\cdot s^{2}+\mathcal{O}(s^{3})\,.

(S185)

For
Z(s)
, expanding Eq. (
S175
) up to the second order, we have

\displaystyle Z_{1}\cdot s=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\langle\psi_{0}(0)|H_{1}(\tau)|\mathcal{C}\rangle=g\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\varphi_{a}(z,\bar{z})|\mathcal{C}\rangle\,,

(S186)

and

\displaystyle Z_{2}\cdot s^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\left[\langle\psi_{0}(0)|H_{1}(\tau_{1})H_{1}(\tau_{2})|\mathcal{C}\rangle-\mathcal{A}_{0}\langle\psi_{0}(0)|H_{1}(\tau_{1})H_{1}(\tau_{2})|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\left[\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\varphi_{a}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\varphi_{a}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})|\mathcal{C}\rangle-\mathcal{A}_{0}\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\varphi_{a}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\varphi_{a}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle\right]\,,

(S187)

where
z=\tau+ix
is the complex coordinate on the cylinder.

Considering the conformal transformation
w=e^{\frac{2\pi}{L}z}
, the primary field
\varphi_{a}
transforms as

\displaystyle\varphi_{a}(z,\bar{z})=\left(\frac{2\pi|w|}{L}\right)^{2h_{a}}\varphi_{a}(w,\bar{w})\,,

(S188)

and the space-time integral in Eqs. (
S186
) and (
S187
) can be transformed into integrals on the
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
manifold.

For
Z_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{1}\cdot s

\displaystyle=g\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2h_{a}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,|w|^{2h_{a}}\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\varphi_{a}(w,\bar{w})|\mathcal{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=g\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2h_{a}}\mathcal{A}_{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{|w|^{2h_{a}}}{(1+|w|^{2})^{2h_{a}}}\,,

(S189)

and further changing the integral variables
(\rho,\theta)=(e^{-\frac{2\pi}{L}\tau},\frac{2\pi x}{L})
, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{1}\cdot s

\displaystyle=g\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{2-2h_{a}}\mathcal{A}_{a}\cdot 2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\rho\frac{\rho^{2h_{a}-1}}{(1+\rho^{2})^{2h_{a}}}

\displaystyle=\left[(2\pi)^{2h_{a}-1}\mathcal{A}_{a}\cdot\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(h_{a},2h_{a},h_{a}+1;-1)}{2h_{a}}\right]\cdot s\,,

(S190)

where
s=gL^{2-2h_{a}}
and
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b,c;x)
is the hypergeomertic function.

For
Z_{2}
, the calculation is similar:

\displaystyle Z_{2}\cdot s^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{4h_{a}}\mathcal{A}_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\frac{|w_{1}w_{2}|^{2h_{a}}}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{4h_{a}}}\left(G_{a}(\eta)-1\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}}{2(2\pi)^{3-4h_{a}}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\rho_{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\frac{(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{2h_{a}-1}}{|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}e^{i\theta}|^{4h_{a}}}\left(G_{a}(\eta)-1\right)\right]\cdot s^{2}\,,

(S191)

where the new integral variables read
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\theta)=(e^{-\frac{2\pi}{L}\tau_{1}},e^{-\frac{2\pi}{L}\tau_{2}},\frac{2\pi(x_{1}-x_{2})}{L})
. In the above derivation, the one-point and two-point crosscap correlators [Eqs. (
S181
) and (
S182
)] and the two-point plane correlator have been used. The integral for
Z_{2}
is convergent, and we expect that every order of the perturbation expansion of
Z(s)
is convergent, since the divergent
bulk
contributions from the numerator
\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau H_{1}(\tau)}|\mathcal{C}\rangle
and the denominator
\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle
cancel at each order. In fact, according to the fusion rule
\varphi_{a}\varphi_{a}\sim\mathbbm{1}+\cdots
, we should have
G_{a}(\eta\to 0)\to 1
and thus
G_{a}(\eta\to 0)-1\to 0
as
|w_{1}-w_{2}|\to 0
, which cancels the divergent
bulk
contribution in the integral.

Next, we consider the perturbation of
W(s)
. We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\langle\psi_{0}(0)|\mathcal{T}e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau\,H_{1}(\tau)}|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\;\langle\psi_{0}(0)|H_{1}(\tau_{1})H_{1}(\tau_{2})|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle+\mathcal{O}(g^{4})\,,

(S192)

where the leading correction appears at the second order:

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\;\langle\psi_{0}(0)|H_{1}(\tau_{1})H_{1}(\tau_{2})|\psi_{0}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=g^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{4h_{a}}\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\frac{|w_{1}w_{2}|^{2h_{a}}}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{4h_{a}}}

\displaystyle=g^{2}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{4-4h_{a}}\lim_{\rho\to 0}2\pi\int_{\rho}^{1}\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}\int_{\rho_{1}}^{1}\mathrm{d}\rho_{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\frac{(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{2h_{a}-1}}{|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}e^{i\theta}|^{4h_{a}}}

\displaystyle=\left[(2\pi)^{4h_{a}-3}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\rho}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}^{1-2h_{a}}}\int_{\rho_{1}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{2}}{\rho_{2}^{1+2h_{a}}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\left|1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}e^{-i\theta}\right|^{4h_{a}}}\right]\cdot s^{2}

(S193)

with
\rho=e^{-\frac{2\pi\beta}{L}}\to 0
as
\beta\to\infty
. To separate out the contribution of the “running” central charge
c(s)
and
W(s)
from the above integral, we expand the angular part of the integral as a power series
[
56
]
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\left|1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}e^{-i\theta}\right|^{4h_{a}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\left(1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}e^{-i\theta}\right)^{-2h_{a}}\left(1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}e^{i\theta}\right)^{-2h_{a}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}\right)^{n+m}\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\frac{\Gamma(m+2h_{a})}{m!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\,e^{-in\theta}e^{im\theta}

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\right]^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}\right)^{2n}\,,

(S194)

where
\Gamma(x)
is the gamma function. Then, the radial integrals of
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
can be integrated term by term,

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\right]^{2}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\rho}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}^{1-2h_{a}}}\int_{\rho_{1}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{2}}{\rho_{2}^{1+2h_{a}}}\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}\right)^{2n}

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\right]^{2}\frac{1}{2n+2h_{a}}\int_{\rho}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}\left(1-x^{2n+2h_{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2n+2h_{a}}\left[\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\right]^{2}\,\lim_{\rho\to 0}\left[-\ln\rho-\frac{1-\rho^{2n+2h_{a}}}{2n+2h_{a}}\right]\,,

(S195)

from which we separated out the second-order contribution of the “running” central charge, which is proportional to
-\ln\rho=\frac{2\pi\beta}{L}
, and the second-order contribution of
W(s)
:

\displaystyle W_{2}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{4h_{a}-3}\cdot 2\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2n+2h_{a}}\left[\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\right]^{2}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\left(-\frac{1-\rho^{2n+2h_{a}}}{2n+2h_{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=-(2\pi)^{4h_{a}-2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+2h_{a})^{2}}\left[\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\right]^{2}\,.

(S196)

In summary, up to the second order, the perturbation expansion of the crosscap overlap is given by

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{0}+\mathcal{A}_{a}\cdot\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(h_{a},2h_{a},h_{a}+1;-1)}{2(2\pi)^{1-2h_{a}}h_{a}}\cdot s

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}}{2(2\pi)^{2-4h_{a}}}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\rho_{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\frac{(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{2h_{a}-1}}{|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}e^{i\theta}|^{4h_{a}}}\left(G_{a}(\eta)-1\right)-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+2h_{a})^{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(n+2h_{a})}{n!\Gamma(2h_{a})}\right)^{2}\right]\cdot s^{2}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(s^{3})\,,

(S197)

where
\mathcal{A}_{a}
is the crosscap coefficient associated with the primary field
\varphi_{a}
, as defined in Eq. (
S181
).

In rational CFTs, a crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}\rangle
can be expressed as a linear combination of crosscap Ishibashi states:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}\rangle=\sum_{a}\mathcal{A}_{a}\,|a\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,,

(S198)

where the expansion coefficients are precisely the crosscap coefficients. The absolute values of these coefficients can be determined from the loop channel-tree channel correspondence between the Klein bottle partition function and the cylinder partition function with crosscap boundaries. In particular, for rational CFTs with charge conjugation modular invariants, one can always construct the PSS crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{0}\rangle
associated with the identity field, whose crosscap coefficients are

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{a}=\frac{P_{0a}}{\sqrt{S_{0a}}}\,,

(S199)

as shown in Eq. (
S68
).

The conformal perturbation theory provides a perturbative approach to investigate the conjectured monotonicity of the Klein bottle entropy (norm-square of the crosscap overlap) under the renormalization flow
[
43
]
. Specifically, the first-order correction of the crosscap overlap in Eq. (
S197
) takes a remarkably simple form.
From the series expansion of the hypergeometric function,

{}_{2}F_{1}(h_{a},2h_{a},h_{a}+1;-1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2h_{a})_{2n}}{(2n)!}\frac{h_{a}}{2n+h_{a}}\left(1-\frac{2n+2h_{a}}{2n+1+h_{a}}\frac{2n+h_{a}}{2n+1}\right)\,,

(S200)

where
(q)_{n}=q(q+1)\cdots(q+n-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol, one sees that
{}_{2}F_{1}(h_{a},2h_{a},h_{a}+1;-1)
is strictly positive for
h_{a}<1
. Consequently, whenever the first-order correction is non-vanishing, its sign is determined solely by the crosscap coefficient
\mathcal{A}_{a}
.

F.3
C. Perturbed
A
-series unitary minimal models

We apply the conformal perturbation theory to the
A
-series unitary minimal models, with central charge
c=1-\frac{6}{m(m+1)}
,
m\geq 3
. The primary fields are labeled by integer pairs
(r,s)
within the conformal grid
1\leq r\leq m-1
,
1\leq s\leq m
, subject to the condition that
r+s
is even to avoid double counting.

The PSS crosscap state associated with the identity field
(1,1)
can be expressed in terms of the Ishibashi states as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{(1,1)}\rangle=\sum_{1\leq r\leq m-1\,,1\leq s\leq m}^{r+s=\mathrm{even}}\mathcal{A}_{(r,s)}|(r,s)\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\,,

(S201)

where the crosscap coefficients
\mathcal{A}_{(r,s)}
can be computed via Eq. (
S199
).

The modular
\mathcal{S}
-matrix is

\displaystyle S_{(r,s),(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta^{(2)}_{r+s,0}\delta^{(2)}_{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime},0}\sqrt{\frac{8}{m(m+1)}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi rr^{\prime}}{m}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi ss^{\prime}}{m+1}\right)\,,

(S202)

and the modular
\mathcal{T}
-matrix is determined via the conformal weights of the primary fields

\displaystyle h_{(r,s)}=\frac{[r(m+1)-sm]^{2}-1}{4m(m+1)}\,.

(S203)

Using Eq. (
S69
), one can calculate the associated
P
-matrix as
[
57
,
23
]
,

\displaystyle\quad P_{(r,s),(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\sqrt{m(m+1)}}\left[\delta^{(2)}_{r,0}\delta^{(2)}_{s,0}\delta^{(2)}_{r^{\prime},0}\delta^{(2)}_{s^{\prime},0}\sin\left(\frac{\pi rr^{\prime}}{2m}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi ss^{\prime}}{2(m+1)}\right)+\delta^{(2)}_{r,1}\delta^{(2)}_{s,1}\delta^{(2)}_{r^{\prime},1}\delta^{(2)}_{s^{\prime},1}\cos\left(\frac{\pi rr^{\prime}}{2m}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi ss^{\prime}}{2(m+1)}\right)\right]\,,

(S204)

where
\delta^{(2)}_{n,m}\equiv\frac{1+(-1)^{n+m}}{2}
is the Kronecker-
\delta
function with periodic
2
.

Therefore, the crosscap coefficients of the standard PSS state
|\mathcal{C}_{(1,1)}\rangle
read

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{(r,s)}=\frac{P_{(1,1),(r,s)}}{\sqrt{S_{(1,1),(r,s)}}}=\delta^{(2)}_{r,1}\delta^{(2)}_{s,1}\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{2}\cot\left(\frac{\pi r}{2m}\right)\cot\left(\frac{\pi s}{2(m+1)}\right)}{\sqrt{m(m+1)}}}\,,

(S205)

which are non-negative for all
(r,s)
. This provides a perturbative proof of the monotonicity conjecture
[
43
]
for the crosscap overlap (and hence Klein bottle entropy) under the renormalization group flow in
A
-series unitary minimal models whenever the crosscap coefficient of the perturbing field is non-vanishing.

F.4
D. Ising CFT with thermal and magnetic perturbations

For the Ising field theory, we have already obtained the crosscap correlators via the bosonziation technique. It is straightforward to apply these results in Eq. (
S197
), where
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\mathcal{A}_{\mathbbm{1}}|\mathbbm{1}\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}\pm\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}|\varepsilon\rangle\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}
with
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbbm{1}}=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}
.

For the thermal perturbation (
h_{\varepsilon}=\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}=1/2
), we have
G_{\varepsilon}(\eta)=1+\eta^{2}/(1-\eta)
for the two-point crosscap correlator of the
\varepsilon
field [Eq. (
S157
)], therefore, substituting it into Eq. (
S197
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s_{1})|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\pm\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\cdot s_{1}-\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\,\frac{\pi^{2}}{32}\cdot s_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(s_{1}^{3})

(S206)

with
s_{1}=g_{1}L
. This result is consistent with the exact solution in Eq. (
S106
), as first reported in Ref.
[
43
]
.

For the magnetic perturbation (
h_{\sigma}=\bar{h}_{\sigma}=1/16
), since
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}=0
, the leading contribution appears at the second order. The function
G_{\pm}^{\sigma}(\eta)
for the two-point crosscap correlator of the
\sigma
field [Eq. (
S173
)] can be expanded in powers of
\eta
as

\displaystyle G_{\pm}^{\sigma}(\eta)

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)^{-1/8}\left[\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1-\eta}}\pm\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-\eta}}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)^{-1/8}\left[1-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(4n-2)!(n-1)!}{[(2n-1)!]^{2}n!}\left(\frac{\eta}{16}\right)^{n}\pm\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(4n)!}{(2n+1)!(2n)!}\left(\frac{\eta}{16}\right)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+1/8)}{n!\Gamma(1/8)}\eta^{n}-\frac{1}{(1-\eta)^{1/8}}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(4n-2)!(n-1)!}{[(2n-1)!]^{2}n!}\left(\frac{\eta}{16}\right)^{n}\mp\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{2}\sqrt{\eta}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(4n)!}{(2n+1)!(2n)!}\left(\frac{\eta}{16}\right)^{n}\right]\,,

(S207)

which converges quickly as a power series in
\eta
. The expansion
G_{\pm}^{\sigma}(\eta)=(1-\eta)^{-2h_{\sigma}}(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\eta^{n}\pm\eta^{h_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\eta^{n})
is precisely the conformal partial wave decomposition
[
33
,
34
]
, consistent with the fusion rule
\sigma\times\sigma\sim\mathbbm{1}+\varepsilon

S197

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s_{2})|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}-1.63528\cdot s_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(s_{2}^{4})\,,

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s_{2})|\mathcal{C}_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}}-1.71711\cdot s_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(s_{2}^{4})

(S208)

The
\mathbb{Z}_{3}

\mathcal{M}(6,5)
Virasoro symmetry. Its corresponding Klein bottle partition function is a linear combination of
\mathcal{M}(6,5)
Virasoro characters
[
38
]

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{K}}=\chi_{(1,1)}+\chi_{(1,5)}+\chi_{(3,5)}+\chi_{(3,1)}+2\chi_{(3,3)}+2\chi_{(1,3)}\,,

(S210)

where the identity and thermal primary fields are identified as
\mathbbm{1}=(1,1)
and
\varepsilon=(3,5)
, respectively. The modular
\mathcal{S}
transformation of this Klein bottle partition function determines the associated crosscap coefficients to be
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbbm{1}}=(3+6/\sqrt{5})^{1/4}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}=(3-6/\sqrt{5})^{1/4}
. For the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
parafermion CFT with thermal perturbation (perturbation operator has conformal weight
h_{\varepsilon}=\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}=2/5
), the first-order perturbation correction to the crosscap overlap is given by

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}(s)|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\rangle=\left(3+\frac{6}{\sqrt{5}}\right)^{1/4}\pm\frac{5}{8(2\pi)^{1/5}}\left(3-\frac{6}{\sqrt{5}}\right)^{1/4}\,\frac{\Gamma(2/5)\Gamma(7/5)}{\Gamma(4/5)}\cdot s+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})=1.54401\pm 0.54881\cdot s+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\,,

(S211)

where
\pm
denotes the crosscap state and its Kramers-Wannier dual for the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
parafermion CFT.

\mathbb{Z}_{3}
spin matrices, where periodic boundary condition is imposed. At the critical point

Post-Hopf algebroids, post-Lie-Rinehart algebras and geometric numerical integration
Adrien Busnot Laurent, Yunnan Li, Yunhe Sheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of post-Hopf algebroids, generalizing the pre-Hopf algebroids introduced in [ 8 ] in the study of exotic aromatic S-series. We construct action post-Hopf algebroids through actions of post-Hopf algebras. We show that the universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra (post-Lie algebroid) is naturally a post-Hopf algebroid. As a byproduct, we construct the free post-Lie-Rinehart algebra using a magma algebra with a linear map to the derivation Lie algebra of a commutative associative algebra. Applications in geometric numerical integration on manifolds are given.

1.
Introduction

The first purpose of this paper is to study the universal enveloping of a post-Lie algebroid by introducing the notion of post-Hopf algebroids. The second purpose is to give applications of post-Hopf algebroids in braiding operators on Hopf algebroids and numerical analysis on manifolds.

1.1.
Post-Lie algebras, post-Hopf algebras and post-Lie algebroids

The notion of a post-Lie algebra was introduced by Vallette from his study of Koszul duality of operads in
[
49
]
, and can be applied to the study of generalized Lax pairs
[
3
]
and regularity structures
[
10
]
.
Munthe-Kaas and Lundervold found that post-Lie algebras also naturally appear in differential geometry and play important roles in the study of numerical integration on manifolds
[
41
]
.
As the integration of post-Lie algebras, the notion of post-Lie groups was introduced in
[
4
]
, which can be used to construct set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. See
[
1
]
for good examples of post-groups.

In
[
35
]
, the notion of post-Hopf algebras was introduced as an abstraction from two basic examples, namely the group algebra of a post-group and the universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie algebra. A novel property is that any cocommutative post-Hopf algebra gives rise to a sub-adjacent Hopf algebra with a generalized Grossman-Larson product. There is naturally a post-Lie algebra structure on the space of primitive elements, and a post-group structure on the set of group-like elements in an arbitrary post-Hopf algebra. Also, cocommutative post-Hopf algebras are essentially equivalent to cocommutative Hopf braces, and thus naturally provide Yang-Baxter operators
[
35
,
50
]
. See
[
17
,
48
]
for two different kinds of generalizations of post-Hopf algebras along this connection.

The notion of Lie algebroids was introduced by Pradines in 1967, which are generalizations of
Lie algebras and tangent bundles. Just as Lie algebras are the infinitesimal objects of Lie groups,
Lie algebroids are the infinitesimal objects of Lie groupoids. Lie algebroids and Lie groupoids play important roles in differential geometry, foliation theory, Poisson geometry and mathematical physics. See
[
38
]
for general theories about Lie
algebroids and Lie groupoids.
As the geometric generalization of a post-Lie algebra, the notion of a post-Lie algebroid was also introduced by Munthe-Kaas and Lundervold in their study of numerical integration
[
41
]
. See also
[
42
]
for further applications.

1.2.
Hopf algebroids and pre-Hopf algebroids

Roughly speaking, bialgebroids, resp. Hopf algebroids, are the appropriate generalization of bialgebras, resp. Hopf algebras over a commutative ring (often a field) to algebraic structures over a noncommutative base algebra.
Bialgebroids appeared independently at different times and places, in algebra, algebraic topology and Poisson geometry, in various versions of generality. The notion of a bialgebroid in its full generality was given by Takeuchi in 1970s, under the name
\times_{R}
-bialgebras. Later his ideas were rediscovered independently by J.-H. Lu
[
36
]
.
Similarly to the relation between Hopf algebras and groups, Hopf algebroids can be viewed as a quantization of groupoids.
The term of Hopf algebroids was first introduced by Haynes R. Miller in his 1975 doctoral dissertation for the purpose to describe the
E_{2}
-term of the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence, and also with other applications in algebraic topology.
In
[
39
]
, Maltsiniotis gave a definition of Hopf algebroids with commutative base algebras and their images under the source and target maps lie in the centers of total algebras.
In
[
36
]
, Lu generalized the notion of Hopf algebroids, in which neither the total algebras nor the base algebras are required to be commutative.
After that there appear several other different definitions of Hopf algebroids for various purposes; see
[
7
,
47
,
24
]
.

In this paper, we adopt Lu’s definition of Hopf algebroids in
[
36
]
, fit for our fundamental example: the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebroid (Lie-Rinehart algebra).
So far the bialgebroid structure on the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebroid has been extensively studied with fruitful results; see e.g.
[
40
,
5
]
. On the other hand, a natural construction of cocommutative Hopf algebroids from a finite groupoid was given
in
[
20
]
, generalizing the group algebra of a group.

Recently, Bronasco and the first author uncovered in
[
8
]
that the pre-Hopf algebroid and the Grossman-Larson Hopf algebroid are the underlying algebraic structures associated to the laws of composition and substitution of exotic aromatic S-series, and used them to provide the algebraic foundations of stochastic numerical analysis (see also
[
34
]
).
They later extended these structures with planar rooted trees and naturally obtained post-Hopf algebras in
[
9
]
.
This allowed for the creation of high-order intrinsic numerical methods for stochastic differential equations on manifolds.

1.3.
Main results and outline of the paper

The purpose of this paper is twofold. On the one hand, we study the universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra (post-Lie algebroid). On the other hand, we generalize the pre-Hopf algebroid introduced in
[
8
]
to the post-algebraic structure context, and give applications in geometric numerical integration.

For these purposes, first we introduce the notion of post-Hopf algebroids, and show that a post-Hopf algebroid gives rise to a Hopf algebroid, which is called the Grossman-Larson Hopf algebroid. Through actions of post-Hopf algebras, we construct a class of examples, called action post-Hopf algebroids. We show that post-Hopf algebroids give rise to braiding operators on the associated Grossman-Larson Hopf algebroid. Then we introduce the notion of post-Lie-Rinehart algebras as the algebraic analog of post-Lie algebroids, and construct the free object using a magma algebra with a linear map to
{\rm Der}(R)
, where
R
is a commutative associative algebra. The universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra is naturally a post-Hopf algebroid. Applications of this universal enveloping post-Hopf algebroid and the action post-Hopf algebroid in numerical analysis are provided for the study of volume-preserving Lie-group methods.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of (weak) post-Hopf algebroids and show the existence of a sub-adjacent Hopf algebroid associated with a post-Hopf algebroid, so-called the Grossman-Larson Hopf algebroid. A useful construction called the action post-Hopf algebroid is provided (Theorem
2.20
). Also, a post-Hopf algebroid naturally induces a braiding operator on the Grossman-Larson Hopf algebroid (Theorem
2.24
).
In Section
3
, first we introduce the notion of post-Lie-Rinehart algebras and give the construction of their free objects (Theorem
3.18
).
Then we show that the universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra is a (weak) post-Hopf algebroid (Theorem
3.20
). In Section
4
, we outline applications of post-Lie-Rinehart algebras and post-Hopf algebroids in numerical analysis.

Convention.

In this paper, we fix an algebraically closed ground field
{\mathbf{k}}
of characteristic 0 and a commutative associative unital algebra
R
. All the objects under discussion, including vector spaces, linear maps, algebras and tensor products, are taken over
{\mathbf{k}}
by default if without emphasis.

2.
Post-Hopf algebroids

In this section, we introduce the notion of post-Hopf algebroids, which are generalizations of post-Hopf algebras. In particular, we give the action post-Hopf algebroids using actions of post-Hopf algebras. We introduce the notion of braiding operators on Hopf algebroids, and show that post-Hopf algebroids give rise to braiding operators on the Grossman-Larson Hopf algebroids.

2.1.
Bialgebroids and Hopf algebroids

First we adopt the following definition of Hopf algebroids with commutative base algebras as a special situation in
[
36
]
; see also
[
30
, § 2.1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Take the commutative unital algebra
R
as the base algebra, and
H
another unital algebra as the total algebra.
A
bialgebroid
structure on
H
over
R
consists of the following data:

(i)

An algebra homomorphism
\iota:R\to H
as the source and also target map, giving the following canonical left
R
-module structure on
H
,

\displaystyle\lambda:R\otimes H\to H,\quad f\otimes x\mapsto\iota(f)x,

and we write
\iota(f)x
as
fx
for short.

(ii)

An
R
-module map
\Delta:H\to H\otimes_{R}H,\ x\to x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
as the coproduct, satisfying

(a)

(\Delta\otimes_{R}{\rm id}_{H})\Delta=({\rm id}_{H}\otimes_{R}\Delta)\Delta
.

(b)

\Delta(x)(\iota(f)\otimes 1_{H}-1_{H}\otimes\iota(f))=0
for any
f\in R
and
x\in H
,
so inducing an algebra structure on the image
\Delta(H)
.

(c)

\Delta
is an algebra homomorphism from
H
to
\Delta(H)
. In particular,
\Delta(1_{H})=1_{H}\otimes_{R}1_{H}
.

(iii)

An
R
-module map
\varepsilon:H\to R
as the counit, satisfying

(a)

\varepsilon(1_{H})=1_{R}
.

(b)

\ker\varepsilon
is a left ideal of
H
, namely
\varepsilon(x(y-\iota\varepsilon(y)))=0
for any
x,y\in H
.

(c)

\lambda(\varepsilon\otimes{\rm id}_{H})\Delta={\rm id}_{H}
and
\lambda(\varepsilon\otimes{\rm id}_{H})\Delta^{\rm op}={\rm id}_{H}
, where
\Delta^{\rm op}
is the opposite of
\Delta
.

We say that
H
is cocommutative if
\Delta=\Delta^{\rm op}
.

A
Hopf algebroid
is a bialgebroid
H
over
R
which admits an algebra anti-isomorphism
S:H\to H
as the antipode map, satisfying

(i)

S\iota=\iota
,

(ii)

m_{H}(S\otimes{\rm id}_{H})\Delta=\iota\varepsilon S
,

(iii)

m_{H}({\rm id}_{H}\otimes S)\Delta=\iota\varepsilon
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is straightforward to see that if
\iota(R)
lies in the center
Z(H)
of
H
, then
H\otimes_{R}H
is an
R
-algebra, and both the coproduct
\Delta
and the counit
\varepsilon
are
R
-algebra homomorphisms. In this situation, we call
H
an
R
-bialgebra
.

Definition 2.3
.

A Hopf algebroid
H
over
R
is called a
Hopf
R
-algebra
, if
\iota(R)
lies in the center
Z(H)
of
H
, the antipode map
S
of
H
is
R
-linear and
\varepsilon S=\varepsilon
.

Note that all structural maps of a Hopf
R
-algebra are
R
-linear.

Example 2.4
.

Let
(A,\Delta_{A},\varepsilon_{A},S_{A})
be a Hopf algebra. The tensor algebra
H=R\otimes A
has a natural Hopf
R
-algebra structure
(H,\Delta_{H},\varepsilon_{H},S_{H},\iota)
, where

\displaystyle\iota(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f1_{A},

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(fa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fa_{1}\otimes_{R}1_{R}a_{2},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}(fa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\varepsilon_{A}(a),

\displaystyle S_{H}(fa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fS_{A}(a)

for any
fa\in R\otimes A
with
f\in R
and
a\in A
. We call
H=R\otimes A
the
tensor product Hopf
R
-algebra
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
H
is a cocommutative Hopf algebroid over
R
such that
\iota(R)\subseteq Z(H)
, then
H
is a Hopf
R
-algebra.

Proof.

We only need to show that
S
is
R
-linear and
\varepsilon S=\varepsilon
. First for any
f\in R
,
x\in H
, we have

\displaystyle S(fx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(x)S(\iota(f))\ =\ S(x)\iota(f)\ =\ fS(x),

which implies that
S
is
R
-linear. Also, since the counit
\varepsilon
becomes an algebra homomorphism, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon(S(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\iota(\varepsilon(S(x))))=\varepsilon(S(x_{1})x_{2})=\varepsilon(S(x_{1}))\varepsilon(x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x_{1})\varepsilon(S(x_{2}))=\varepsilon(x_{1}S(x_{2}))=\varepsilon(\iota(\varepsilon(x)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x),

which implies that
\varepsilon S=\varepsilon
. Hence,
H
is a Hopf
R
-algebra by definition.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Given a bialgebroid
H
over
R
, an
R
-algebra
A
is called a
module algebra
over
H
via an action
\rightharpoonup:H\otimes A\to A
, if

(1)

\displaystyle xy\rightharpoonup a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rightharpoonup(y\rightharpoonup a),

(2)

\displaystyle 1_{H}\rightharpoonup a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a,

(3)

\displaystyle fx\rightharpoonup a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x\rightharpoonup a),

(4)

\displaystyle x\rightharpoonup ab

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup a)(x_{2}\rightharpoonup b),

(5)

\displaystyle x\rightharpoonup 1_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x)1_{A}

for any
f\in R
,
x\in H
and
a,b\in A
. When
R={\mathbf{k}}
, it recovers the notion of a module algebra over a bialgebra.

For later use, we need the following
construction of Hopf algebroids from smash product algebras. Let
(A,\Delta_{A},\varepsilon_{A},S_{A})
be a cocommutative Hopf algebra such that
R
is an
A
-module algebra via an action
\rightharpoonup
. Then there is an algebra structure on
R\otimes A
given by

fa\cdot gb=f(a_{1}\rightharpoonup g)a_{2}b,

for all
fa,gb\in R\otimes A
. This algebra is called the
smash product algebra
and denoted by
R\#A
.

Theorem 2.7
.

With the above notations, the smash product algebra
H=R\#A
has a natural Hopf algebroid structure
(H,\Delta_{H},\varepsilon_{H},S_{H},\iota)
over
R
, where the comultiplication
\Delta_{H}
, the counit
\varepsilon_{H}
and the antipode
S_{H}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(fa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fa_{1}\otimes_{R}1_{R}a_{2},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}(fa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\varepsilon_{A}(a),

\displaystyle S_{H}(fa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(S_{A}(a_{1})\rightharpoonup f)S_{A}(a_{2})

for any
f,g\in R
and
a,b\in A
, and
\iota:R\to H
is the canonical inclusion of algebras. We call
H
an
action Hopf algebroid
over
R
.

Proof.

According to
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
, given a braided commutative algebra
A
in the Yetter-Drinfeld module category
{{}_{H}}\mathscr{Y}\!\mathscr{D}^{H}
over a Hopf algebra
H
,
the smash product algebra
A\#H
naturally possesses a Hopf algebroid structure over
A
in the sense of Lu. Such a result is a generalization of
[
36
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Now under our notations, we take the commutative algebra
R
as a module algebra over a given cocommutative Hopf algebra
A
and choose the trivial coaction of
A
on
R
, then
R
is clearly a braided commutative algebra in
{{}_{A}}\mathscr{Y}\!\mathscr{D}^{A}
, and so
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
can be applied to give the desired statement. Note that in this special situation, both the source map and the target map are the canonical inclusion, and we just denote it by
\iota
as previously.
∎

2.2.
Post-Hopf algebroids

In order to introduce the key notion of post-Hopf algebroids, we first recall post-Hopf algebras introduced in
[
35
]
. The group algebra of a post-group and the universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie algebra are two fundamental examples.

Definition 2.8

(
[
35
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

A
post-Hopf algebra
is a pair
(H,\rhd)
, where
H
is a Hopf algebra and
\rhd:H\otimes H\to H
is a coalgebra homomorphism satisfying the following equalities:

(6)

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\cdot z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rhd y)\cdot(x_{2}\rhd z),

(7)

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\rhd z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(x_{1}\cdot(x_{2}\rhd y)\big)\rhd z,

for any
x,y,z\in H
, and the left multiplication
\alpha_{\rhd}:H\to{\rm End}(H)
defined by

\alpha_{\rhd,x}y=x\rhd y
for all
x,y\in H,

is convolution invertible in
{\rm Hom}(H,{\rm End}(H))
. Namely, there exists unique
\beta_{\rhd}:H\to{\rm End}(H)
such that

(8)

\alpha_{\rhd,x_{1}}\circ\beta_{\rhd,x_{2}}=\beta_{\rhd,x_{1}}\circ\alpha_{\rhd,x_{2}}=\varepsilon(x){\rm id}_{H},\quad\forall x\in H.

Moreover, we have the following properties.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
35
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
(H,\rhd)
be a post-Hopf algebra. For any
x,y\in H
, we have

(9)

\displaystyle x\rhd 1_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x)1_{H},

(10)

\displaystyle 1_{H}\rhd x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x,

(11)

\displaystyle S(x\rhd y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd S(y).

Theorem 2.10

(
[
35
, Theorem 2.5]
)
.

Let
(H,\rhd)
be a cocommutative post-Hopf algebra. Then

H_{\rhd}\coloneqq(H,*_{\rhd},1_{H},\Delta,\varepsilon,S_{\rhd})

is a Hopf algebra, which is called the
sub-adjacent Hopf algebra
, where for all
x,y\in H
,

(12)

\displaystyle x*_{\rhd}y

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle x_{1}\cdot(x_{2}\rhd y),

(13)

\displaystyle S_{\rhd}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\beta_{\rhd,x_{1}}(S(x_{2})),

and
*_{\rhd}
is called the
Grossman-Larson product
.

Furthermore,
H
is a left
H_{\rhd}
-module bialgebra via the action
\rhd
, so
\beta_{\rhd,x}=\alpha_{\rhd,S_{\rhd}(x)}
.

Now we are in position to define post-Hopf algebroids generalizing cocommutative post-Hopf algebras. First we introduce a weaken version, whose counterpart in the original Hopf algebra case was given
by getting rid of the restrictive condition that the left multiplication
\alpha_{\rhd}
is convolution invertible.

Definition 2.11
.

A
weak post-Hopf algebroid
over
R

consists of a
cocommutative
Hopf
R
-algebra
(H,\iota)
,
and a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear map
\rhd:H\otimes H\to H
such that

(14)

\displaystyle\Delta(x\rhd y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rhd y_{1})\otimes_{R}(x_{2}\rhd y_{2}),

(15)

\displaystyle x\rhd 1_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota(\varepsilon(x)),

(16)

\displaystyle 1_{H}\rhd x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x,

(17)

\displaystyle\iota(\varepsilon(x\rhd y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd\iota(\varepsilon(y)),

(18)

\displaystyle fx\rhd y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x\rhd y),

(19)

\displaystyle x\rhd yz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rhd y)(x_{2}\rhd z),

(20)

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\rhd z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(x_{2}\rhd y)\rhd z

for any
f\in R
and
x,y,z\in H
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(H,\iota,\rhd)
be a weak post-Hopf algebroid over
R
. For any
f\in R
and
x\in H
, we have

(21)

\displaystyle x\rhd\iota(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota(\varepsilon(x\rhd\iota(f))),

(22)

\displaystyle\iota(f)\rhd x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fx.

Proof.

First we see that

\displaystyle x\rhd\iota(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd\iota(\varepsilon(\iota(f)))\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-2'}}}{{=}}\ \iota(\varepsilon(x\rhd\iota(f))).

Namely, the image of
\iota
is stable under the left multiplication of
\rhd
.
Also, we have

\displaystyle\iota(f)\rhd x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle f(1_{H}\rhd x)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-1''}}}{{=}}\ fx,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.13
.

Given a weak post-Hopf algebroid
(H,\iota,\rhd)
over
R
, there exists an associative product
*_{\rhd}
, called the
Grossman-Larson product
, on
H
defined by

(23)

\displaystyle x*_{\rhd}y

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle x_{1}(x_{2}\rhd y),\quad\forall x,y\in H,

such that the tuple
H_{\rhd}=(H,*_{\rhd},\Delta,\varepsilon,\iota)
forms a bialgebroid over
R
, which is called the
Grossman-Larson bialgebroid
over
R
.

Moreover,
H
is a module algebra over
H_{\rhd}
with respect to the product
\rhd
in the sense of Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

For any
f\in R
and
x,y\in H
, since
\Delta
is
R
-linear and
\iota(R)\subseteq Z(H)
, we have

\displaystyle fx_{1}(x_{2}\rhd y)=x_{1}(f(x_{2}\rhd y))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-2}}}{{=}}x_{1}(fx_{2}\rhd y),

which implies that the product
*_{\rhd}
is well-defined.

By Eqs. (
15
) and (
16
), it is clear that
1_{H}*_{\rhd}x=x*_{\rhd}1_{H}=x
.
Also, Eq. (
14
) and the cocommutativity of
H
implies that

(24)

\Delta(x*_{\rhd}y)=(x_{1}*_{\rhd}y_{1})\otimes_{R}(x_{2}*_{\rhd}y_{2}).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle(x*_{\rhd}y)*_{\rhd}z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:GL-product}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x_{1}*_{\rhd}y_{1})((x_{2}*_{\rhd}y_{2})\rhd z)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-4},\eqref{eq:GL-product}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}(x_{2}\rhd y_{1})(x_{3}\rhd(y_{2}\rhd z))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-3}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}(x_{2}\rhd y_{1}(y_{2}\rhd z))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:GL-product}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x*_{\rhd}(y*_{\rhd}z).

Thus,
(H,*_{\rhd})
is an associative algebra also with the unit
1_{H}
.

By Eq. (
16
),
\iota
is an algebra homomorphism from
R
to the associative algebra
(H,*_{\rhd})
:

\iota(fg)=\iota(f)\iota(g)=\iota(f)*_{\rhd}\iota(g),\quad\forall f,g\in R.

Thus
H_{\rhd}
has the same
R
-module structure as
H
:

\iota(f)*_{\rhd}x=\iota(f)(1_{H}\rhd x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-1''}}}{{=}}\iota(f)x=fx.

Also, we have

\displaystyle(x_{1}*_{\rhd}\iota(f))\otimes_{R}x_{2}-x_{1}\otimes_{R}(x_{2}*_{\rhd}\iota(f))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:GL-product}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}(x_{2}\rhd\iota(f))\otimes_{R}x_{3}-x_{1}\otimes_{R}x_{2}(x_{3}\rhd\iota(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rhd\iota(f))x_{2}\otimes_{R}x_{3}-x_{1}\otimes_{R}(x_{2}\rhd\iota(f))x_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Combined with (
24
), we see that (ii) in Definition

2.3.
Braiding operators on Hopf algebroids

Guccione, Guccione and Vendramin introduced the notion of a braiding operator on a cocommutative Hopf algebra in
[
21
, Definition 5.9]
.
Here we further generalize this notion to Hopf algebroids.
We interpret any Hopf algebroid
H
over
R
as an
R
-bimodule via the source map
\iota:R\to H
, and we use the notation
\otimes_{R}^{b}
to denote the tensor product of
R
-bimodules, different from the former tensor product
\otimes_{R}
of left
R
-modules.

Definition 2.21
.

Given a Hopf algebroid
H
over
R
, an
R
-bimodule homomorphism

r:H\otimes_{R}^{b}H\to H\otimes_{R}^{b}H
is called a
braiding operator
on
H
if it satisfies

(i)

({\rm id}_{H}\otimes\tau\otimes{\rm id}_{H})(\Delta\otimes\Delta)r=(r\otimes r)({\rm id}_{H}\otimes\tau\otimes{\rm id}_{H})(\Delta\otimes\Delta)
as
R
-bimodule homomorphisms from
H\otimes_{R}^{b}H
to
(H\otimes_{R}^{b}H)\otimes_{R}(H\otimes_{R}^{b}H)
,

(ii)

mr=m
,

(iii)

r(m\otimes_{R}^{b}{\rm id}_{H})=({\rm id}_{H}\otimes_{R}^{b}m)(r\otimes_{R}^{b}{\rm id}_{H})({\rm id}_{H}\otimes_{R}^{b}r)
,

(iv)

r({\rm id}_{H}\otimes_{R}^{b}m)=(m\otimes_{R}^{b}{\rm id}_{H})({\rm id}_{H}\otimes_{R}^{b}r)(r\otimes_{R}^{b}{\rm id}_{H})
,

(v)

r(1_{H}\otimes_{R}^{b}x)=x\otimes_{R}^{b}1_{H},\ \forall x\in H
,

(vi)

r(x\otimes_{R}^{b}1_{H})=1_{H}\otimes_{R}^{b}x,\ \forall x\in H
,

where
\tau
is the flip map and
m
is the multiplication of
H
, and
we can take the tensor product
\otimes_{R}
since the
R
-bimodule

is a left
R
-module.

3.
The universal enveloping algebras of post-Lie-Rinehart algebras

In this section, first we recall the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie-Rinehart algebra. Then we introduce the notion of post-Lie-Rinehart algebras as the algebraic analog of post-Lie algebroids. We construct the free post-Lie-Rinehart algebra generated by a magma algebra with a fixed linear map to
{\rm Der}(R)
, which turns out to be an action post-Lie-Rinehart algebra. Finally we show that the universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra is a weak post-Hopf algebroid. In the particular case of action post-Lie-Rinehart algebras, the universal enveloping algebras are post-Hopf algebroids.

3.1.
Lie-Rinehart algebras and their universal enveloping algebras

The structure of a Lie-Rinehart algebra appeared in Rinehart’s work
[
46
]
under the terminology of
(R,A)
-Lie algebra. Then, Huebschmann referred to such a structure a Lie-Rinehart algebra
[
25
,
26
]
. Lie-Rinehart algebras are the algebraic counterpart of Lie algebroids, and have various important applications in Poisson geometry, symplectic geometry and quantization.

Definition 3.1
.

[
46
]

Let
L
be an
R
-module.
A tuple
\mathscr{R}=(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\rho)
is called a
Lie-Rinehart algebra
, if

(i)

L
is a Lie algebra with its bracket
[-,-]_{L}
.

(ii)

\rho:L\to{\rm Der}(R)
is an
R
-module homomorphism and a Lie algebra homomorphism, called the
anchor
.

(iii)

the following compatibility condition holds:

(39)

\displaystyle[x,fy]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f[x,y]_{L}+\rho(x)(f)y,\quad\forall f\in R,\,x,y\in L.

By convenience, we will write
\rho(x)(f)
as
x(f)
for short.

Example 3.2
.

Recall that a
Lie algebroid
structure on a vector bundle
A\longrightarrow M
is
a pair consisting of a Lie algebra structure
[-,-]_{A}
on
the section space
\Gamma(A)
and a vector bundle morphism

a_{A}:A\longrightarrow TM

from
A
to the tangent bundle
TM
, called the
anchor
, satisfying

(40)

[x,fy]_{A}=f[x,y]_{A}+a_{A}(x)(f)y,\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M),x,y\in\Gamma(A),

where
C^{\infty}(M)
is the commutative associative algebra of smooth functions on the manifold
M
. Let
(A\longrightarrow M,[-,-]_{A},a_{A})
be a Lie algebroid. Then the quadruple
(C^{\infty}(M),\Gamma(A),[-,-]_{A},a_{A})
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra.

Example 3.3
.

For the Lie algebra
{\rm Der}(R)
of derivations on
R
, the triple
(R,{\rm Der}(R),{\rm id})
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra with a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to{\rm Der}(R)
. Let
L=R\otimes\mathfrak{g}
as a free
R
-module. Define

\displaystyle[fx,gy]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fg[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+f\rho(x)(g)y-g\rho(y)(f)x,\quad\forall f,g\in R,\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

and extend
\rho
to be an
R
-module homomorphism
\overline{\rho}:L\to{\rm Der}(R)
. Then
the quadruple
(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\overline{\rho})
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra, and we call it an
action Lie-Rinehart algebra
.

Definition 3.5
.

A Lie algebra
L
is called a
Lie
R
-algebra
if
L
is an
R
-module and its Lie bracket
[-,-]_{L}
is
R
-linear. In other words, a Lie
R
-algebra is a Lie-Rinehart algebra with trivial anchor.

Note that the authors in
[
40
]
defined the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebroid
(A\longrightarrow M,[-,-]_{A},a_{A})
as the universal enveloping algebra of its underlying Lie-Rinehart algebra
(C^{\infty}(M),\Gamma(A),[-,-]_{A},a_{A})
.
Now we recall the definition of the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie-Rinehart algebra.

Definition 3.6

(
[
40
]
)
.

For a Lie-Rinehart algebra
\mathscr{R}=(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\rho)
, the
universal enveloping algebra
of
\mathscr{R}
is a triple
(\mathscr{U}_{R}(L),\iota_{R},\iota_{L})
, where
\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
is a unital algebra,
\iota_{R}:R\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
is a homomorphism of unital algebras and
\iota_{L}:L\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras
such that

\iota_{R}(f)\iota_{L}(x)=\iota_{L}(fx),\quad\iota_{L}(x)\iota_{R}(f)-\iota_{R}(f)\iota_{L}(x)=\iota_{R}(x(f)),\quad\forall f\in R,x\in L,

and satisfying the following universal property: if
A
is a unital algebra,
\kappa_{R}:R\to A
is a homomorphism of unital algebras and
\kappa_{L}:L\to A
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras such that

\kappa_{R}(f)\kappa_{L}(x)=\kappa_{L}(fx),\quad\kappa_{L}(x)\kappa_{R}(f)-\kappa_{R}(f)\kappa_{L}(x)=\kappa_{R}(x(f)),\quad\forall f\in R,x\in L,

then there exists a unique homomorphism of unital algebras
\bar{\kappa}:\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\to A
such that
\bar{\kappa}\iota_{R}=\kappa_{R}
and
\bar{\kappa}\iota_{L}=\kappa_{L}
.

We have the following construction of the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie-Rinehart algebra.

Proposition 3.7

(
[
16
, § 3.2]
)
.

For a Lie-Rinehart algebra
\mathscr{R}=(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\rho)
, let
T_{R}(L\otimes R)=R\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}(L\otimes R)^{\otimes_{R}n}
be the tensor
R
-algebra over the
R
-bimodule
L\otimes R
, and let
j_{R}:R\to T_{R}(L\otimes R)
and
j_{L}:L\to T_{R}(L\otimes R)
be the canonical embedding, i.e.

j_{R}(f)=f,\quad j_{L}(x)\mapsto x\otimes 1_{R}.

Define

(41)

\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)=T_{R}(L\otimes R)\bigg/\left({j_{L}(x)j_{L}(y)-j_{L}(y)j_{L}(x)-j_{L}([x,y]_{L}),\atop j_{L}(x)j_{R}(f)-j_{R}(f)j_{L}(x)-j_{R}(x(f))}\,\bigg|\,{x,y\in L,\atop f\in R}\right),

and
j_{R}
(resp.
j_{L}
) induces the map
\iota_{R}:R\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)

(resp.
\iota_{L}:L\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
). Then
(\mathscr{U}_{R}(L),\iota_{R},\iota_{L})
is the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathscr{R}
.

Remark 3.8
.

The structural map
\iota_{R}:R\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
is injective, while

\iota_{L}:L\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
is also injective if
L
is
R
-projective, by the PBW theorem proven in
[
46
, Theorem 3.1]
.

For the universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
of a Lie-Rinehart algebra
\mathscr{R}=(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\rho)
, using its left
R
-module structure to define the tensor product
\otimes_{R}
, then we have the comultiplication as a left
R
-module map and a
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra homomorphism

\Delta:\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\,\bar{\otimes}_{R}\,\mathscr{U}_{R}(L),\ f\mapsto f\otimes_{R}1_{R},\ x\mapsto x\otimes_{R}1_{R}+1_{R}\otimes_{R}x,\quad\forall f\in R,\,x\in L,

where
\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\,\bar{\otimes}_{R}\,\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
is the kernel of the map

\vartheta:\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\otimes_{R}\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\to{\rm Hom}(R,\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\otimes_{R}\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)),\ X\otimes_{R}Y\mapsto(f\mapsto Xf\otimes_{R}Y-X\otimes_{R}Yf).

The counit
\varepsilon:\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\to R
is the left
R
-module map such that

\varepsilon(f)=f,\quad\varepsilon(x)=0,\quad\varepsilon(XY)=\varepsilon(X\varepsilon(Y)),\quad\forall f\in R,\,x\in L,\,X,Y\in\mathscr{U}_{R}(L).

Then
(\mathscr{U}_{R}(L),\cdot,\Delta,\varepsilon,\iota_{R})
is a bialgebroid over
R
, also called an
R/{\mathbf{k}}
-bialgebra
in
[
40
]
or a
left bialgebroid
over
R
in
[
5
]
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
L
be a Lie
R
-algebra with Lie bracket
[-,-]_{L}
. Then the universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
of

is a Hopf
R
-algebra.

Proof.

Note that a Lie
R
-algebra is a Lie-Rinehart algebra with trivial anchor.
So
\iota_{R}(R)
lies in the center of
\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
according to Eq. (
41
).
Let
T_{R}(L)=R\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}L^{\otimes_{R}n}
be the tensor
R
-algebra over the
R
-module
L
and let
j_{L}:L\to T_{R}(L)

be the canonical embedding. We have the following

-algebra isomorphism

\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\cong T_{R}(L)/\left(j_{L}(x)j_{L}(y)-j_{L}(y)j_{L}(x)-j_{L}([x,y]_{L})\,|\,x,y\in L\right).

Hence, we can define the antipode map

S:\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)\to\mathscr{U}_{R}(L),\ \ f\mapsto f,\ x\mapsto-x,\quad\forall f\in R,\,x\in L

as an
R
-algebra anti-isomorphism, and
\mathscr{U}_{R}(L)
becomes a Hopf

-algebra.
∎

Let

3.2.
Post-Lie-Rinehart algebras and free objects

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra as the algebraic counterpart of a post-Lie algebroid introduced in
[
41
]
, and study its free object.

The notion of a post-Lie algebra was introduced by Vallette from his study of Koszul duality of operads in
[
49
]
.

Definition 3.11
.

[
49
]

A
post-Lie algebra

(L,[-,-]_{L},\rhd)
consists of a Lie algebra
(L,[-,-]_{L})
and a binary product
\rhd:L\otimes L\to L
such that

(42)

\displaystyle x\rhd[y,z]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x\rhd y,z]_{L}+[y,x\rhd z]_{L},

(43)

\displaystyle([x,y]_{L}+x\rhd y-y\rhd x)\rhd z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\rhd z)-y\rhd(x\rhd z).

A
post-Lie algebra homomorphism
between two post-Lie algebras is a Lie algebra homomorphism compatible with post-Lie products.

Any post-Lie algebra
(L,[-,-]_{L},\rhd)
has a
sub-adjacent Lie algebra

L_{\rhd}:=(L,[-,-]_{\rhd})

defined by

[x,y]_{\rhd}\coloneqq x\rhd y-y\rhd x+[x,y]_{L},\quad\forall x,y\in L.

The notion of a post-Lie algebroid was introduced by Munthe-Kaas and Lundervold in their study of numerical integration
[
41
]
. Now we introduce the notion of a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra. The relationship between post-Lie-Rinehart algebras and post-Lie algebroids is similar to the relationship between Lie-Rinehart algebras and Lie algebroids.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
L
be a Lie
R
-algebra with Lie bracket
[-,-]_{L}
and
\rhd:L\otimes L\to L
be a bilinear map.
A tuple
\mathscr{R}=(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\rho,\rhd)
is called a
post-Lie-Rinehart algebra
, if

(i)

(L,[-,-]_{L},\rhd)
is a post-Lie algebra,

(ii)

\rho:L_{\rhd}\to{\rm Der}(R)
is an
R
-module homomorphism and a Lie algebra homomorphism for the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
L_{\rhd}
of
(L,[-,-]_{L},\rhd)
,

(iii)

the following compatibility conditions hold:

(44)

\displaystyle fx\rhd y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x\rhd y),

(45)

\displaystyle x\rhd fy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x\rhd y)+x(f)y

for any
f\in R,\,x,y\in L
, where
x(f)=\rho(x)(f)
as before.

A
post-Lie-Rinehart algebra homomorphism
between two post-Lie-Rinehart algebras is an
R
-module homomorphism and a post-Lie algebra homomorphism compatible with anchors.

Proposition 3.13
.

Given a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra
\mathscr{R}=(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\rho,\rhd)
, the tuple
\mathscr{R}_{\rhd}=(R,L,[-,-]_{\rhd},\rho)
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra, which is called the
sub-adjacent Lie-Rinehart algebra
of
\mathscr{R}
.

Proof.

By definition, we only need to check the compatibility condition (
39
) as follows:

\displaystyle[x,fy]_{\rhd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd fy-fy\rhd x+[x,fy]_{L}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:PLR-1},\eqref{eq:PLR-2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle f(x\rhd y)+x(f)y-f(y\rhd x)+f[x,y]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f[x,y]_{\rhd}+x(f)y

for any
f\in R
and
x,y\in L
.
∎

Example 3.14
.

Recall from
[
41
]
that a
post-Lie algebroid
structure on a vector bundle

A\longrightarrow M
is a triple that consists of a
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear Lie
algebra structure
[-,-]_{A}
on
\Gamma(A)
, a bilinear operation
\triangleright_{A}:\Gamma(A)\otimes\Gamma(A)\longrightarrow\Gamma(A)
and a
vector bundle morphism
a_{A}:A\longrightarrow TM
, called the
anchor
,
such that
(\Gamma(A),[-,-]_{A},\triangleright_{A})
is a post-Lie algebra, and
for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
u,v\in\Gamma(A)
, the following
relations are satisfied:

(i)

u\triangleright_{A}(fv)=f(u\triangleright_{A}v)+a_{A}(u)(f)v,

(ii)

(fu)\triangleright_{A}v=f(u\triangleright_{A}v).

It is straightforward to check that
(C^{\infty}(M),\Gamma(A),[-,-]_{A},a_{A},\triangleright_{A})
is a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra.

As a generalization of
[
35
, Theorem 2.7]
, there is a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra structure on the space of primitive elements of a weak post-Hopf algebroid.

Given a weak post-Hopf algebroid
(H,\iota,\rhd)
over
R
, let

\displaystyle P(H)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\{x\in H\,|\,\Delta(x)=x\otimes_{R}1_{H}+1_{H}\otimes_{R}x\}.

Since
H
is a Hopf
R
-algebra, it is straightforward to see that
(P(H),[-,-])
is a Lie
R
-algebra with the commutator as its Lie bracket. By the same argument as
[
35
, Theorem 2.7]
, the product
\rhd
can be restricted to
P(H)
such that
(P(H),[-,-],\rhd)
is a post-Lie algebra. Moreover, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(H,\iota,\rhd)
be a weak post-Hopf algebroid over
R
.
Then
(R,P(H),[-,-],\rho,\rhd)
is a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra, where
the anchor
\rho
is given by Eq. (
25

\displaystyle\rho(x)(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x\rhd\iota(f)),\quad f\in R,\ x\in P(H).

Proof.

First note
\rho(x)\in{\rm Der}(R)
for any
x\in P(H)
by conditions (
16
) and (
19

It remains to show that
\rho
is an
R

44
) and (

\rho
is an

\rho(fx)(g)=\varepsilon(fx\rhd\iota(g))\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-2}}}{{=}}\ f\varepsilon(x\rhd\iota(g))\ =\ f\rho(x)(g),\quad\forall f,g\in R,~x\in P(H).

For all
f\in R
and
x,y\in P(H)

\displaystyle{[\rho(x),\rho(y)](f)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)(\rho(y)(f))-\rho(y)(\rho(x)(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x\rhd\iota(\varepsilon(y\rhd\iota(f))))-\varepsilon(y\rhd\iota(\varepsilon(x\rhd\iota(f))))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-2'}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\rho([x,y]_{\rhd})(f),

which implies that

On the other hand, Eq. (

\displaystyle x\rhd fy\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-3}}}{{=}}(x\rhd\iota(f))y+\iota(f)(x\rhd y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:post-HAD-5'}}}{{=}}f(x\rhd y)+\varepsilon(x\rhd\iota(f))y=f(x\rhd y)+\rho(x)(f)y.

Next we introduce a useful construction of post-Lie-Rinehart algebras, originally given in
[
22
]
via a geometric terminology, namely action post-Lie algebroids.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rhd)
be a post-Lie algebra with its sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\rhd}

\rho:\mathfrak{g}_{\rhd}\to{\rm Der}(R)
be a Lie algebra homomorphism. Then we have an extended post-Lie algebra structure on

defined as follows,

(46)

\displaystyle[f_{1}x_{1},f_{2}x_{2}]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}f_{2}[x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}},

(47)

\displaystyle f_{1}x_{1}\,\overline{\rhd}\,f_{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}x_{1}(f_{2})x_{2}+f_{1}f_{2}(x_{1}\rhd x_{2})

for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in R
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
, where we write
\rho(x)(f)
simply as

Extend
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to{\rm Der}(R)
to be a linear map
\overline{\rho}:L\to{\rm Der}(R)
defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}(fx)(f^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fx(f^{\prime}),\quad f,f^{\prime}\in R,\ x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then, the tuple
(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\overline{\rho},\overline{\rhd})
is a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra, and we call it an
action post-Lie-Rinehart algebra

The corresponding sub-adjacent Lie-Rinehart algebra

is given by

\displaystyle[f_{1}x_{1},f_{2}x_{2}]_{\overline{\rhd}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}x_{1}\,\overline{\rhd}\,f_{2}x_{2}-f_{2}x_{2}\,\overline{\rhd}\,f_{1}x_{1}+[f_{1}x_{1},f_{2}x_{2}]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}x_{1}(f_{2})x_{2}-f_{2}x_{2}(f_{1})x_{1}+f_{1}f_{2}[x_{1},x_{2}]_{\rhd}.

\displaystyle f_{V}(x),

Theorem 3.18
.

The action post-Lie-Rinehart algebra

{\rm PostLR}(V)
has the following universal property:

For any post-Lie-Rinehart algebra
\mathscr{R}=(R,L,[-,-]_{L},\rho,\rhd)
, and a magma algebra homomorphism
\varphi:(V,\curvearrowright)\to(L,\rhd)
such that
\rho\varphi=f_{V}
,
there exists a unique post-Lie-Rinehart algebra homomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}:{\rm PostLR}(V)\to L
such that
\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}(1_{R}\otimes i_{V})
, where
i_{V}:V\to{\rm Lie}(V)
is the natural embedding. That is, we have the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{{\rm Der}(R)}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{1_{R}\otimes i_{V}}
\scriptstyle{f_{V}}
\textstyle{{R\otimes{\rm Lie}(V)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exists!\,\tilde{\varphi}}
\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}

Thus, the post-Lie-Rinehart algebra
{\rm PostLR}(V)
is the
free post-Lie-Rinehart algebra
generated by
(V,\curvearrowright,f_{V})
.

Proof.

First by the universal property of
{\rm PostLie}(V)
, there exists a unique post-Lie algebra homomorphism
\psi:{\rm PostLie}(V)\to(L,[-,-]_{L},\rhd)
such that
\varphi=\psi i_{V}
. It induces a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho\psi:{\rm PostLie}(V)_{\rhd_{V}}\to{\rm Der}(R)
satisfying

\rho\psi i_{V}=\rho\varphi=f_{V},

so we have
\rho\psi=\rho_{V}
, since
{\rm Lie}(V)
is generated by
V
.

Now define a linear map
\tilde{\varphi}:R\otimes{\rm Lie}(V)\to L
by

\tilde{\varphi}(fX)=f\psi(X),\quad\forall f\in R,X\in{\rm Lie}(V).

It is clear that
\tilde{\varphi}
is
R
-linear and
\tilde{\varphi}(1_{R}\otimes i_{V})=\psi i_{V}=\varphi
. Also, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}([fX,gY]_{R\otimes{\rm Lie}(V)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(fg[X,Y]_{{\rm Lie}(V)})\ =\ fg\psi([X,Y]_{{\rm Lie}(V)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f\psi(X),g\psi(Y)]_{L}\ =\ [\tilde{\varphi}(fX),\tilde{\varphi}(gY)]_{L},

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(fX\,\overline{\rhd}_{V}gY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(f\rho_{V}(X)(g)Y+fg(X\rhd_{V}Y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\rho_{V}(X)(g)\psi(Y)+fg\psi(X\rhd_{V}Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\rho(\psi(X))(g)\psi(Y)+fg(\psi(X)\rhd\psi(Y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\psi(X)\rhd g\psi(Y)=\ \tilde{\varphi}(fX)\rhd\tilde{\varphi}(gY),

which implies that
\tilde{\varphi}
is a post-Lie algebra homomorphism. Moreover, we have

\rho(\tilde{\varphi}(fX))=\rho(f\psi(X))\ =\ f\rho(\psi(X))=f\rho_{V}(X)=\overline{\rho}_{V}(fX),

which implies that
\tilde{\varphi}:{\rm PostLR}(V)\to L

{\rm PostLR}(V)
to

is determined by its restriction on

is unique.
∎

Theorem 3.20

-algebra

With the above notations, the universal enveloping algebra

Given a magma algebra

4.
Post-Lie-Rinehart algebras and post-Hopf algebroids for numerical analysis

In this section, we detail the natural post-Lie-Rinehart algebras and post-Hopf algebroid structures appearing in numerical analysis.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a smooth manifold equipped with a frame basis
(E_{d})
, defined globally for clarity.
The smooth vector fields
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
are naturally equipped with the Jacobi bracket
\llbracket-,-\rrbracket_{J}
and the (curvature-free) Weitzenböck connection,

Y\rhd X=\sum_{i}Y[x^{i}]E_{i},\quad X=x^{i}E_{i},

where we use the Einstein summation convention.
Let us further assume that the connection
\rhd
has constant torsion
\nabla T=0
, or equivalently that
\mathcal{M}
is locally a Lie group.
In this case, the torsion defines the bracket:

[X,Y]=-T(X,Y)=x^{i}y^{j}\llbracket E_{i},E_{j}\rrbracket_{J},\quad X=x^{i}E_{i},\quad Y=y^{j}E_{j}.

The two brackets are linked by the identity

\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{J}=[X,Y]+X\rhd Y-Y\rhd X.

Then, the connection algebra
(\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}),[-,-],\rhd)
is a post-Lie algebra
[
41
]
.

As a specific application of Theorem
3.20
and Theorem
2.13
, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

The tuple
(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}),[-,-],\rho,\rhd)
is a post-Lie-Rinehart algebra and its universal enveloping algebra
(\mathscr{U}_{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}(\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})),\cdot,\Delta^{\rm cosh},\rhd)
is a weak post-Hopf algebroid over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, where the anchor map
\rho
is given by

\rho(X)(\phi)=X[\phi],\quad X=x^{i}E_{i},\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

Moreover,
(\mathscr{U}_{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}(\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})),*_{\rhd},\Delta^{\rm cosh})
is the associated Grossman-Larson bialgebroid.

We rely on specific sub-structures of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}_{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}(\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}))
in numerical analysis.
Let
F\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
be a given smooth Lipschitz vector field. Then,
F
generates a post-Lie sub-algebra
(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M}),[-,-],\rhd)
:

\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M})=\Span_{{\mathbb{R}}}(F,\ F\rhd F,\ [F,F\rhd F],\ (F\rhd F)\rhd F,\ F\rhd(F\rhd F),\ \dots).

This yields the Hopf subalgebras
(\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M})),\cdot,\Delta^{\rm cosh})
and
(\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M})),*_{\rhd},\Delta^{\rm cosh})
, commonly represented with ordered planar forests
[
37
]
.
Analogously, let
{\rm End}_{F}(\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}))
be the space of endomorphisms on
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
generated from
F
,
\rhd
and
[-,-]
:

{\rm End}_{F}(\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}))=\Span_{{\mathbb{R}}}({\rm id},\ F\rhd-,\ -\rhd F,\ [-,F\rhd F],\ [F,-\rhd F],\ [F,F\rhd-],\ (-\rhd F)\rhd F,\ \dots).

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
be the subalgebra of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
generated by the following functions, with elements called
aromas
in numerical analysis
[
12
,
28
,
6
]
:

u_{1}^{i_{1}}(E_{i_{2}})\cdots u_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}(E_{i_{n}})u_{n}^{i_{n}}(E_{i_{1}}),\quad u_{k}\in{\rm End}_{F}(\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})),\quad u_{k}(X)=u_{k}^{i}(X)E_{i}.

Following Corollaries
3.22
and
2.19
, we obtain the following action Hopf algebroid structures using the anchor map
\rho
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The tuple
(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M}),[-,-],\rho,\rhd)
is an action post-Lie-Rinehart algebra, with elements called
aromatic
vector fields.
Its universal enveloping algebra is the action post-Hopf algebroid
(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M})),\cdot,\Delta^{\rm cosh},\rhd,\theta)
over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
, with the associated Grossman-Larson Hopf algebroid
(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M})),*_{\rhd},\Delta^{\rm cosh},\theta)
over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
{\rm Div}\colon\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
be the divergence operator associated to the connection
\rhd
:

{\rm Div}(X)=E_{i}[x^{i}],\quad X=x^{i}E_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}).

The divergence restricts to aromatic vector fields
{\rm Div}\colon\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
, but aromas are not in general all generated by the divergence:
{\rm Div}(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M}))\subsetneq\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
, as observed in
[
18
]
.

Let the flow
\varphi_{t}(y_{0})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
be the solution of the initial value problem on
\mathcal{M}
:

(53)

y^{\prime}(t)=F(y(t)),\quad y(0)=y_{0}\in\mathcal{M}.

The Taylor expansion of the pullback by the exact flow of (
53
) is described by the Grossman-Larson exponential:

\varphi_{t}^{*}\phi=\exp^{*_{\rhd}}(tF)\rhd\phi,\quad\exp^{*_{\rhd}}(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}X^{*_{\rhd}n},\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

Most existing numerical flows
\psi_{t}
for solving (
53
) have Taylor expansions that rewrite with formal series over the Hopf algebra
\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M}))
, that we denote by
\overline{\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M}))}
.
This is the case for Lie-group methods
[
27
]
, and, in particular, for the Lie-Euler method, that follows the geodesics for
\rhd
:

(54)

\psi_{t}(y_{0})=\exp_{y_{0}}(tF(y_{0})).

The Taylor expansion of the pullback by Lie-group methods is typically expressed using the concatenation product. For instance, the Taylor expansion of the Lie-Euler flow (
54
) is described by the concatenation exponential

\psi_{t}^{*}\phi=\exp^{\cdot}(tF)\rhd\phi,\quad\exp^{\cdot}(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}X^{\cdot n},\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),

where the concatenation product acts on functions as the following differential operator

(X_{n}\cdots X_{1})[\phi]=x_{n}^{i_{n}}\dots x_{1}^{i_{1}}E_{i_{n}}[\dots E_{i_{1}}[\phi]\dots],\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

A method
\psi_{t}
is of order
p
if the Taylor expansions of
\varphi_{t}^{*}\phi
and
\psi_{t}^{*}\phi
match for all terms of order at most
p
. The Lie-Euler method (
54
) is of order one only for instance.

Remark 4.4
.

The Grossman-Larson product
*_{\rhd}
corresponds to the composition of flows, in the sense that

(\psi^{2}_{t}\circ\psi^{1}_{t})^{*}\phi=(S^{2}_{t}*_{\rhd}S^{1}_{t})\rhd\phi,\quad\psi^{k*}_{t}\phi=S^{k}_{t}\rhd\phi,\quad S^{k}_{t}\in\overline{\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M}))}.

The description of the composition of flows in simpler cases has been extensively studied in the numerical literature using tree structures and Butcher series for the description of the Grossman-Larson Hopf algebra.
In particular, the dual of the Grossman-Larson Hopf algebra
T({\mathbf{k}}{\mathcal{OT}})_{\rhd_{{\mathbf{k}}{\mathcal{OT}}}}

is isomorphic to the Munthe-Kaas-Wright Hopf algebra
[
44
,
15
]
.
In the pre-Lie context, the dual of the action pre-Hopf algebroid of aromatic forests
[
12
,
28
]
is isomorphic to the Butcher-Connes-Kreimer Hopf algebroid
[
6
,
8
]
.

Let us now define aromatic numerical flows by relying on the post-Hopf algebroid structure of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M}))
(see also
[
43
,
6
]
). Note that the differential operators defined using functions in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
do still act on all smooth functions in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Definition 4.5
.

A flow
\psi_{t}
for solving (
53
) is
aromatic
if the Taylor expansion of the pullback
\psi_{t}^{*}\phi
is a formal series in
\overline{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{tF}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M}))}
:

\psi_{t}^{*}\phi=S_{t}\rhd\phi,\quad S_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{tF}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M}))},\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

Example 4.6
.

Aromatic methods are straightforwardly obtained from Lie-group methods by substituting
F=f^{i}E_{i}
with an aromatic vector field
t\hat{F}_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{tF}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M})
.
For instance, apply the Lie-Euler method (
54
) with the preprocessed aromatic vector field

t\hat{F}_{t}=tF+\frac{t^{2}}{2}F\rhd F-\frac{t^{3}}{3}(F\rhd F)\rhd F-\frac{t^{3}}{12}E_{i}[F[f^{i}]]F+\frac{t^{3}}{6}[F,F\rhd F].

Then, the resulting flow
\psi_{t}
is aromatic.

The application of aromatic flows in geometric numerical integration is better understood through backward error analysis
[
23
,
37
,
45
]
.
Under technical assumptions satisfied by large classes of numerical methods, a numerical flow may be understood as the exact flow of a formal modified differential equation
y^{\prime}=\tilde{F}_{t}(y)
driven by a modified vector field
t\tilde{F}_{t}
, that is,

\psi_{t}^{*}\phi=\exp^{*_{\rhd}}(t\tilde{F}_{t})\rhd\phi,\quad t\tilde{F}_{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{tF}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M}))},\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

The geometric properties of the numerical method
\psi_{t}
are read directly on
\tilde{F}_{t}
.
A case of importance in the literature lies in divergence-free features
{\rm Div}(F)={\rm Div}(\tilde{F}_{t})=0
and is linked with volume-preserving flows.
In this context, one is interested in the creation of consistent divergence-free numerical methods. The design of such methods is an open problem of numerical analysis, even in the pre-Lie context.
It is showed in
[
28
,
13
]
that non-exact numerical flows described by
\overline{\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{tF}(\mathcal{M}))}
cannot satisfy
{\rm Div}(\tilde{F}_{t})=0
, so that the use of aromatic numerical flows is crucial to the design of divergence-free methods.

Application 4.7
.

While the creation of exact divergence-free methods is an open problem, one can create pseudo-divergence-free methods by using the post-Hopf algebroid
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{F}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{X}_{F}(\mathcal{M}))
.
In particular, a calculation yields that the aromatic method from Example
4.6
satisfies (at least formally) third order of preservation:
{\rm Div}(\tilde{F}_{t})=O(t^{3})
.
In contrast, the original Lie-Euler method (
54
) satisfies only the first order estimate
{\rm Div}(\tilde{F}_{t})=O(t)
.

Given an aromatic flow, the computation of the associated modified vector field
\tilde{F}_{t}
is not direct and relies on the so-called substitution law. In the pre-Lie case, it is described with non-planar aromatic trees in
[
6
,
8
]
and the divergence-free modified aromatic vector fields
\tilde{F}_{t}
are fully characterised in
[
32
,
31
,
13
]
.
The extension of these results to the post-Lie context and the design and study of convenient algebraic structures for representing aromatic vector fields and aromas are key to the creation of divergence-free aromatic numerical methods and are matter for future work.

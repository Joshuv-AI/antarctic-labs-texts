Counting elliptic curves over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} with bounded naive height
Adrian Barquero-Sanchez and Daniel Mora-Mora

Abstract
. In this paper, we give exact and asymptotic formulas for counting elliptic curves E A , B : y 2 = x 3 + A ​ x + B E_{A,B}\colon y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B with A , B ∈ ℤ A,B\in\mathbb{Z} , ordered by naive height. We study the family of all such curves and also several natural subfamilies, including those with fixed j j -invariant and those with complex multiplication (CM). In particular, we provide formulas for two commonly used normalizations of the naive height appearing in the literature: the calibrated naive height , defined by H cal ​ ( E A , B ) := max ⁡ { 4 ​ | A | 3 , 27 ​ B 2 } , H^{\mathrm{cal}}(E_{A,B}):=\max\{4|A|^{3},27B^{2}\}, and the uncalibrated naive height , defined by H ncal ​ ( E A , B ) := max ⁡ { | A | 3 , B 2 } . H^{\mathrm{ncal}}(E_{A,B}):=\max\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}. In fact, we prove our theorems with respect to the more general naive height H α , β ​ ( E A , B ) := max ⁡ { α ​ | A | 3 , β ​ B 2 } H_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{A,B}):=\max\{\alpha|A|^{3},\beta B^{2}\} , defined for arbitrary positive real numbers α , β ∈ ℝ > 0 \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0} . As part of our approach, we give a completely explicit parametrization of the set of curves E A , B E_{A,B} with fixed j j -invariant and bounded naive height, describing them as twists of the curve E A j , B j E_{A_{j},B_{j}} of minimal naive height for the given j j -invariant. We also include tables comparing and verifying our theoretical predictions with exact counts obtained via exhaustive computer searches, and we compute data for CM elliptic curves of naive height up to 10 30 10^{30} . Code in SageMath is provided to compute all exact and asymptotic formulas appearing in the paper.

1.
Introduction

In recent decades, arithmetic statistics for elliptic curves has been a very active area of research in number theory. Although there are various ways to order elliptic curves when studying their distribution with respect to a given property—for example, by the size of their conductor or their Faltings height—it has proven especially fruitful, both theoretically and computationally, to order them using simple height functions defined directly in terms of their defining coefficients. In particular, in this paper we will use the notion of the
naive height
, which measures the curves by the size of their coefficients. More precisely, for an elliptic curve in short Weierstrass form
E_{A,B}\colon y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
, with
A,B\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we will consider two common normalizations of this height that are widely used in the literature, which, following the terminology introduced by Balakrishnan et al. in
[
BHK+16
]
in order to differentiate them, we will call the
(calibrated) naive height
, defined by

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{cal}}(E_{A,B}):=\max{\{4|A|^{3},27B^{2}\}}

and the
uncalibrated naive height
, defined by

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{ncal}}(E_{A,B}):=\max{\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}}.

For example, in the last three decades there have been many important works in the arithmetic statistics of elliptic curves that use these normalizations of the naive height.
Prominent among these has been the study of the distribution of ranks of elliptic curves, culminating in the landmark results of Bhargava and Shankar
[
BS15a

or the study of the distribution of elliptic curves with prescribed torsion subgroup by Harron and Snowden
[

and the more recent related work of Cullinan, Kenney and Voight
[

m

(see e.g.
[

[

2.
Definitions and Statement of Results

In this paper, we will study two widely used families of elliptic curves, which we now define. For each pair
(A,B)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
we define the curve
E_{A,B}\colon y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
and first consider the family

(2.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}:=\{E_{A,B}\;|\;\text{$A,B\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $\Delta_{E_{A,B}}:=-16(4A^{3}+27B^{2})\neq 0$}\}

consisting of all elliptic curves in short Weierstrass form with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. It is known that each elliptic curve
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphic to a unique elliptic curve
E_{A,B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
such that there is no prime number
p
with
p^{4}\mid A
and
p^{6}\mid B
. Thus, we also consider the family

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:=\{E_{A,B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\;|\;\text{there is no prime number $p$ with $p^{4}\mid A$ and $p^{6}\mid B$}\}.

This subfamily of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is a complete set of representatives for the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E/{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Next, we will also consider the following subfamilies of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
and
\mathcal{E}
. Recall first that the
j
-invariant of an elliptic curve
E_{A,B}
is given by

j(E_{A,B})=1728\frac{4A^{3}}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}}.

Then, we consider the subfamilies of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
and
\mathcal{E}
obtained by restricting only to elliptic curves with a fixed
j
-invariant. Thus, for each
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, define

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}:=\{E_{A,B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\;|\;j(E_{A,B})=j\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{j}:=\{E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{E}\;|\;j(E_{A,B})=j\}.

Similarly, we define the subfamilies of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
and
\mathcal{E}
consisting of the elliptic curves that have
(potential) complex multiplication
, i.e., their endomorphism ring
\operatorname{End}_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}(E_{A,B})
is larger than
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus, we define

(2.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{cm}}:=\{E_{A,B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\;|\;\text{$E_{A,B}$ has potential CM}\}

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{cm}}:=\{E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{E}\;|\;\text{$E_{A,B}$ has potential CM}\}.

It is known that the elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
that have potential complex multiplication are completely determined by their
j
-invariant. In fact, there are exactly thirteen
j
-invariants that correspond to elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
having potential complex multiplication, and they are the elements of the set (see e.g.
[
BSCM25
, Table 3]
and
[
Sil94
, p. 483]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\textrm{cm}}=\{

\displaystyle 0,1728,-3375,8000,-32768,54000,287496,-12288000,

\displaystyle 16581375,-884736,-884736000,-147197952000,-262537412640768000\}.

This implies that we can decompose the families
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\textrm{cm}}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\textrm{cm}}
as disjoint unions over the thirteen CM
j
-invariants in
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{cm}}
, namely

(2.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{cm}}=\coprod_{j\in\mathcal{J}^{\textrm{cm}}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{cm}}=\coprod_{j\in\mathcal{J}^{\textrm{cm}}}\mathcal{E}_{j}.

In order to study how the curves in the families that we defined above are distributed, we will use the notion of the naive height, which measures the curves by the size of their coefficients. In particular, as mentioned in the introduction, we will consider the
(calibrated) naive height
, defined by

(2.7)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{cal}}(E_{A,B}):=\max{\{4|A|^{3},27B^{2}\}}

and the
uncalibrated naive height
, defined by

(2.8)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{ncal}}(E_{A,B}):=\max{\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}}.

We note that the discriminant of a curve
E_{A,B}
is bounded in terms of these heights by

|\Delta_{E_{A,B}}|\leq 32H^{\mathrm{cal}}(E_{A,B})\quad\text{and}\quad|\Delta_{E_{A,B}}|\leq 496H^{\mathrm{ncal}}(E_{A,B}).

For the convenience of the reader, our most important results are stated both for the calibrated and uncalibrated height. Nevertheless, when proving the results, they are stated with respect to a
generalized naive height
, defined by

(2.9)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{A,B}):=\max{\{\alpha|A|^{3},\beta B^{2}\}}

provided that
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. In terms of this height we have
H^{\mathrm{cal}}=H_{4,27}
and
H^{\mathrm{ncal}}=H_{1,1}
.

For each of the infinite families of elliptic curves described above, we define corresponding finite subfamilies using the naive height functions introduced earlier. More precisely, given
X>0
, a height function
H\in\{H^{\mathrm{cal}},H^{\mathrm{ncal}},H_{\alpha,\beta}\}
, and a family
\mathcal{F}\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},\mathcal{E},\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j},\mathcal{E}_{j},\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{cm}},\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{cm}}\}
, we define the
finite
subfamily

\mathcal{F}(X;H):=\{E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{F}\;|\;H(E_{A,B})\leq X\}.

In order to state our results, we need to define the rational numbers that appear in equations (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) below. For
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
, let

a=(-1)^{\varepsilon}\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}

be the prime factorization of
a
, where
\mathbb{P}=\{2,3,5,7,11,\dots\}
denotes the set of all prime numbers. Also, for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we define the integer

\alpha_{p}(a):=\begin{cases}\left\lceil\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}\right\rceil&\text{if $\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)\geq 0$,}\\
\left\lceil\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}\right\rceil&\text{if $\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)<0$.}\end{cases}

Then, using this we define

(2.10)

\displaystyle N_{a}:=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{P}}p^{\alpha_{p}(a)}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}.

Also, for each
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}\smallsetminus\{0,1728\}
, we let

(2.11)

\displaystyle a(j):=\frac{4(1728-j)}{27j}\in{\mathbb{Q}}.

Finally, in Theorem
4.6
, we define the constant

(2.12)

\displaystyle c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\min\left\{\frac{|a(j)|^{1/2}}{N_{a(j)}\alpha^{1/6}},\frac{|a(j)|^{1/3}}{N_{a(j)}\beta^{1/6}}\right\}.

Remark 2.1
.

When considering the calibrated and the uncalibrated naive height we will use the notations

In the formulas appearing in the theorems of this paper, the quantity

2.1.
Asymptotic and exact formulas for the calibrated naive height

In this section, we state our exact and asymptotic formulas for the number of elliptic curves when ordered by the calibrated naive height.

We begin by presenting exact and asymptotic formulas for the number of curves in the families
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
and
\mathcal{E}
, respectively, whose calibrated naive height is at most
X

[
Bru92

Theorem 2.3
.

For every
X>1
, the number of elliptic curves in the families
\mathcal{E}(X;H^{\mathrm{cal}})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(X;H^{\mathrm{cal}})
satisfy the formulas

(2.13)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{E}(X;H^{\mathrm{cal}})=\dfrac{2^{4/3}}{3^{3/2}\zeta(10)}X^{5/6}+O\left(\frac{X^{7/12}}{\log{X}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(X;H^{\mathrm{cal}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{4/3}}{3^{3/2}}X^{5/6}+O(X^{1/2}).

Remark 2.4
.

In
[
BHK+16
, p. 352]
, Balakrishnan et al. state that their project produced an exhaustive database of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves

.

2.3.
Parametrization of Elliptic Curves with a given
j
-invariant

The following theorem provides the parametrization of the families
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
and
\mathcal{E}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
of elliptic curves in short Weierstrass form with a fixed
j
-invariant, as was mentioned in the introduction. This parametrization gives all the curves as twists of the two curves of minimal naive height with the given
j
-invariant, as clarified in Remark
2.15
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{Q}\smallsetminus\{0,1728\}
. Then the set of all elliptic curves
E_{A,B}:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
with
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
that have
j\left(E_{A,B}\right)=j
and
H_{\alpha,\beta}\left(E_{A,B}\right)\leq X
can be explicitly described parametrically by

(2.31)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}\left(X;H_{\alpha,\beta}\right)=\left\{E_{A(j,m),B(j,m)}\;|\;\text{$m\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}$ and $|m|\leq c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}$}\right\}

where
A(j,m)
and
B(j,m)
are the integers defined by

(2.32)

\displaystyle A(j,m)=\frac{N_{a(j)}^{2}m^{2}}{a(j)}\quad\text{and}\quad B(j,m)=\frac{N_{a(j)}^{3}m^{3}}{a(j)}

and the constant
c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})
is defined by

(2.33)

\displaystyle c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta}):=\min\left\{\frac{|a(j)|^{1/2}}{N_{a(j)}\alpha^{1/6}},\frac{|a(j)|^{1/3}}{N_{a(j)}\beta^{1/6}}\right\}.

This implies that

(2.34)

\displaystyle\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}\left(X;H_{\alpha,\beta}\right)=2\left\lfloor c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}\right\rfloor.

Moreover, for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
and any prime
p\in\mathbb{P}
we have that

2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(A(j,m))}\leq 2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)+1\quad\text{if $\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(a(j))}\geq 0$}

and

3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(B(j,m))}\leq 3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)+2\quad\text{if $\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(a(j))}<0$}.

In particular, this implies that the curve
E_{A(j,m),B(j,m)}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
if and only if
m
is square-free and
|m|\leq c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}
, in other words, we have

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{j}\left(X;H_{\alpha,\beta}\right)=\left\{E_{A(j,m),B(j,m)}\;|\;\text{$m\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}$ is square-free and $|m|\leq c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}$}\right\}.

Remark 2.15
.

It is worth mentioning that in the literature there are explicit equations for curves in short Weierstrass form having a given
j
-invariant. For instance, if
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}\smallsetminus\{0,1728\}
, then the curve

E\colon y^{2}=x^{3}+\frac{3j}{1728-j}x+\frac{2j}{1728-j}

has
j
-invariant
j(E)=j
(see e.g.
[
Was08
, p. 47]
). However, for most values of
j
, the coefficients of
E
are in
{\mathbb{Q}}\smallsetminus{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus, our parametrization has the advantage that the coefficients of the curves are integers and moreover, for any given
j
, the curves of minimal naive height
H_{\alpha,\beta}
correspond to the values
m=\pm 1
. Hence, the curves in our parametrization can be thought of as the quadratic twists of the curves of minimal naive height for a fixed value of
j
.

2.5.
Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
, we prove a proposition that provides a parametrization of the set of integral points lying on a cuspidal cubic. From this parametrization, we derive an exact formula for the number of such points. This result serves as a key tool in establishing our exact and asymptotic formulas for counting elliptic curves. These formulas are proved in Section
4
, where, after introducing some preparatory lemmas, we state and prove our main results concerning the generalized naive height
H_{\alpha,\beta}
. Finally, in Section
5
, we present explicit computations and numerical data related to CM elliptic curves, as a computational complement to the theoretical results developed earlier.

3.
Integral Points on Cuspidal Cubics

The following result provides an exact formula for the number of integral points inside a box in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
that lie on a cuspidal cubic. This count plays a key role in deriving both exact and asymptotic formulas for the number of elliptic curves in the various families considered in this paper. The proposition also refines Lemma 2.3 of
[
BSCM25
]
, which only gives an upper bound.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and consider the cuspidal cubic
\mathcal{C}_{a}:y^{2}=ax^{3}
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z})
be the set of integral points on
\mathcal{C}_{a}
, i.e., the set of points
\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
y_{0}{}^{2}=ax_{0}{}^{3}
and similarly, for
T_{1}>0
and
T_{2}>0
, let

\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z};T_{1},T_{2}):=\left\{\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z})\;|\;\text{$|x_{0}|\leq T_{1}$ and $|y_{0}|\leq T_{2}$ }\right\}.

Now, we let

a=(-1)^{\varepsilon}\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}

be the prime factorization of
a
. Also, for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, define the integer

\alpha_{p}(a):=\begin{cases}\left\lceil\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}\right\rceil&\text{if $\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)\geq 0$,}\\
\left\lceil\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}\right\rceil&\text{if $\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)<0$.}\end{cases}

Finally, let
\displaystyle{N_{a}:=\prod\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{\alpha_{p}(a)}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}}
. Then there is a bijection

\displaystyle\varphi_{a}:N_{a}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(\frac{t^{2}}{a},\frac{t^{3}}{a}\right)

and this bijection implies that for
T_{1}>0
and
T_{2}>0
we have

\mathcal{C}_{a}({\mathbb{Z}};T_{1},T_{2})=\left\{\left(\frac{N^{2}_{a}m^{2}}{a},\frac{N^{3}_{a}m^{3}}{a}\right)\;|\;m\in{\mathbb{Z}},|m|\leq\min{\left\{\frac{|a|^{1/2}}{N_{a}}T^{1/2}_{1},\frac{|a|^{1/3}}{N_{a}}T^{1/3}_{2}\right\}}\right\}

and therefore

\#\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z};T_{1},T_{2})=2\left\lfloor\min{\left\{\frac{|a|^{1/2}}{N_{a}}T_{1}^{1/2},\frac{|a|^{1/3}}{N_{a}}T_{2}^{1/3}\right\}}\right\rfloor+1.

Proof.

The map

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}_{a}\colon

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle t\longmapsto\left(\frac{t^{2}}{a},\frac{t^{3}}{a}\right)

is a bijection, obtained by projecting from the singular point at the origin on the cuspidal cubic
\mathcal{C}_{a}({\mathbb{R}})
onto the vertical line
x=1
. We claim that its restriction to
\mathbb{Q}
, namely the map
\widetilde{\varphi}_{a}|_{\mathbb{Q}}:\mathbb{Q}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Q})
, is also a bijection. Indeed, it is clear that if
t\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\widetilde{\varphi}_{a}(t)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Q})
. On the other hand, if
(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Q})
, then there is a
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{a}(t):=\left(\frac{t^{2}}{a},\frac{t^{3}}{a}\right)=(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. If
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
, then
t\neq 0
and both
x\neq 0
and
y\neq 0
. Suppose indeed that
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
. Hence

t=\frac{\dfrac{t^{3}}{a}}{\dfrac{t^{2}}{a}}=\frac{y}{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}.

We will show that the map

\displaystyle\varphi_{a}\colon

\displaystyle N_{a}\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow C_{a}(\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\quad t\longmapsto\left(\frac{t^{2}}{a},\frac{t^{3}}{a}\right)

is a bijection. First let
t:=N_{a}m\in N_{a}{\mathbb{Z}}
with
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Now, let
p\in\mathbb{P}
be an arbitrary prime number. We will check that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(t^{2}/a)}\geq 0
and
\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(t^{3}/a)}\geq 0
and this will imply that
(t^{2}/a,t^{3}/a)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Note that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{a}\right)

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)\geq 0\iff\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\geq\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\frac{t^{3}}{a}\right)

\displaystyle=3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)\geq 0\iff\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\geq\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}.

Now, there are two cases to consider. First, if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)\geq 0
we have
\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}\geq\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}
, hence it is enough to show that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\geq\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}
. Then, since
t=N_{a}m
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(N_{a}m)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(N_{a})+\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{p}(a)+\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{p}(a)

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}.

Next, if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)<0
, then
\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}>\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}
. Thus it is enough to show that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\geq\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}
. Therefore, again since
t=N_{a}m
, a similar calculation as above shows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{p}(a)

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}.

This proves that
(t^{2}/a,t^{3}/a)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}({\mathbb{Z}})
and hence
\varphi_{a}\left(N_{a}\mathbb{Z}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z})
. This map is injective since it is the restriction of
\widetilde{\varphi}_{a}
.

Conversely, suppose that
(x,y)\in C_{a}(\mathbb{Z})\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
. Let
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
be such that
\varphi_{a}(t)=(x,y)
.
Then
x=\dfrac{t^{2}}{a},y=\dfrac{t^{3}}{a}
and then
t=\dfrac{y}{x}
.
We claim that
t\in N_{a}\mathbb{Z}
; this
means that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\geq\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(N_{a}\right)
for every
p\in\mathbb{P}
. First, if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)\geq 0
we have
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(N_{a}):=\alpha_{p}(a)=\left\lceil\dfrac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}\right\rceil
. Moreover, since
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(x)\geq 0
and
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(y)\geq 0
, then (
3.1
) implies that

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\geq\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}

and since
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, it follows that

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(t)\geq\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}\right\rceil=\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(N_{a}\right).

The case when
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)<0
follows from an analogous argument with the inequality (
3.2
). This completes the proof that the map
\varphi_{a}:N_{a}\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow C_{a}(\mathbb{Z})
is a bijection.

Now, using this bijection we want to count

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z};T_{1},T_{2}):=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z})\;|\;|x|\leq T_{1}\text{ and }|y|\leq T_{2}\right\}.

Let
T_{1}>0
and
T_{2}>0
. Then, the bijection
\varphi_{a}
allows us to write

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{a}(\mathbb{Z};T_{1},T_{2})

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\frac{N^{2}_{a}m^{2}}{a},\frac{N^{3}_{a}m^{3}}{a}\right)\;|\;m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\left|\frac{N^{2}_{a}m^{2}}{a}\right|\leq T_{1}\text{ and }\left|\frac{N^{3}_{a}m^{3}}{a}\right|\leq T_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\frac{N^{2}_{a}m^{2}}{a},\frac{N^{3}_{a}m^{3}}{a}\right)\;|\;m\in{\mathbb{Z}},|m|\leq\frac{|a|^{1/2}}{N_{a}}T^{1/2}_{1}\text{ and }|m|\leq\frac{|a|^{1/3}}{N_{a}}T^{1/3}_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\frac{N^{2}_{a}m^{2}}{a},\frac{N^{3}_{a}m^{3}}{a}\right)\;|\;m\in{\mathbb{Z}},|m|\leq\min\left\{\frac{|a|^{1/2}}{N_{a}}T^{1/2}_{1},\frac{|a|^{1/3}}{N_{a}}T^{1/3}_{2}\right\}\right\}.

Finally, this immediately implies that

∎

4.
Proofs of the theorems stated in Section
2

In this section we carry out the proofs of the main theorems stated in Section
2
of the paper. In particular, the proofs are done with respect to the generalized naive height
H_{\alpha,\beta}
for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. Also, throughout this section,
\mu(n)
denotes the value of the Möbius function at an integer
n\geq 1
. We start by proving a couple of lemmas that we will need later.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s>1
and
x\geq 1
. Then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}+O(x^{1-s}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}-\sum_{n>x}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}-\sum_{n>x}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}},

where we used the well-known formula

\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}

for the reciprocal of the Riemann zeta function. Then, using the asymptotic bound (see e.g.
[
Apo76
, Theorem 3.2]
)

\sum_{n>x}\frac{1}{n^{s}}=O(x^{1-s}),\quad\text{ for }s>1\text{ and }x\geq 1,

we see that

\left|\sum_{n>x}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}\right|\leq\sum_{n>x}\frac{|\mu(n)|}{n^{s}}\leq\sum_{n>x}\frac{1}{n^{s}}=O(x^{1-s}).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}\smallsetminus\{0,1728\}
and
(A,B)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
. Then the curve
E_{A,B}\colon y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
is nonsingular and has
j
-invariant
j(E_{A,B})=j
if and only if the point
(A,B)
lies on the cuspidal cubic
\mathcal{C}_{a(j)}\colon y^{2}=a(j)x^{3}
, where
a(j)=\dfrac{4(1728-j)}{27j}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
. Moreover, the curve
E_{A,B}
is singular if and only if the point
(A,B)
lies on the cuspidal cubic
\mathcal{C}_{-\frac{4}{27}}\colon y^{2}=-\frac{4}{27}x^{3}
.

Proof.

Recall that the
j
-invariant of a nonsingular curve
E_{A,B}
is given by

j(E_{A,B})=1728\dfrac{4A^{3}}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}}.

(\implies)

If
E_{A,B}
is nonsingular and
j(E_{A,B})=j
, we can rewrite this condition as

j(E_{A,B})=j\iff 1728\dfrac{4A^{3}}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}}=j\iff B^{2}=\dfrac{4(1728-j)}{27j}A^{3}.

Thus, for a nonsingular curve
E_{A,B}
to have
j
-invariant equal to
j
, for
j\neq 0,1728
, is equivalent to the point
(A,B)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
lying on the cuspidal cubic
\mathcal{C}_{a(j)}\colon y^{2}=a(j)x^{3}
, where
a(j):=\dfrac{4(1728-j)}{27j}
.

(\impliedby)

Now, a curve
E_{A,B}
is singular if and only if
\Delta_{E_{A,B}}=-16(4A^{3}+27B^{2})=0
. This condition can also be rewritten as

\Delta_{E_{A,B}}=-16(4A^{3}+27B^{2})=0\iff B^{2}=-\dfrac{4}{27}A^{3}.

Thus, a curve
E_{A,B}
is singular if and only if the point
(A,B)
lies on the cuspidal cubic
\mathcal{C}_{-\frac{4}{27}}\colon y^{2}=-\frac{4}{27}x^{3}
. A quick verification shows that no
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}\smallsetminus\{0,1728\}
satisfies that
a(j)=-\frac{4}{27}
, and since two cuspidal cubics
\mathcal{C}_{a}
and
\mathcal{C}_{b}
with
a\neq b
intersect in the affine plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
only at the origin, then if the point
(A,B)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
lies on the cuspidal cubic
C_{a(j)}
, it does not lie on the cubic
C_{-\frac{4}{27}}
. Hence the curve
E_{A,B}
is nonsingular and has
j
-invariant
j(E_{A,B})=j
.
∎

The following lemma gives an explicit form of a result stated by Brumer
[
Bru92
, p. 455]
. In particular, the lemma gives a decomposition of the family of elliptic curves
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
as a finite disjoint union of the twists of families of representatives of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves in short Weierstrass form. We also decompose the family of elliptic curves with a given
j
-invariant, and provide an explicit construction of the twists, which will prove is useful for finding the
unique

{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism class representative of a given curve
E_{A,B}
(see Remark
4.7
).

Lemma 4.3
.

For
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define
d*E_{A,B}:=E_{d^{4}A,d^{6}B}
. Then, every curve
E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
can be written uniquely as a twist
d*E_{A,B}
for a unique curve
E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{E}(d^{-12}X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
; the unique integer
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle d:=\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}p^{\gamma_{p}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})},

where
\gamma_{p}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime}):=\max{\{\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\colon p^{4\gamma}\mid A^{\prime}\text{ and }p^{6\gamma}\mid B^{\prime}\}}
and it satisfies that
1\leq d\leq\lfloor X^{1/12}\rfloor
. In particular, this implies that we have the decomposition

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\coprod_{d=1}^{\lfloor X^{1/12}\rfloor}d*\mathcal{E}(d^{-12}X;H_{\alpha,\beta}).

Moreover, for a given
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, we also have the decomposition

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\coprod_{d=1}^{\lfloor X^{1/12}\rfloor}d*\mathcal{E}_{j}(d^{-12}X;H_{\alpha,\beta}).

Proof.

Let
E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
, and let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
as in
4.2
. For the curve
E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
, take
A=A^{\prime}/d^{4}
and
B=B^{\prime}/d^{6}
. Then, by definition, it follows that
E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}=d*E_{A,B}
and, since from the definition of the naive height, we have

H_{\alpha,\beta}(d*E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}})=d^{12}\cdot H_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}),

therefore,
H_{\alpha,\beta}(d*E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}})\leq X
if and only if
H_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}})\leq X/d^{12}
. By the definition of
d
, for each prime
p\in\mathbb{P}
, the maximality of
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(d):=\gamma_{p}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
implies that
p^{4}\nmid A
or
p^{6}\nmid B
, so that
E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{E}(d^{-12}X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
.
Next, to prove that the curve
E_{A,B}
is unique in
\mathcal{E}(d^{-12}X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
, suppose for a contradiction, that there exists another curve
E_{A_{0},B_{0}}\in\mathcal{E}(d^{-12}X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
and
d_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
such that
E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}}=d_{0}*E_{A_{0},B_{0}}
. Then, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d_{0}^{4}A_{0}=d^{4}A,\\
d_{0}^{6}B_{0}=d^{6}B.\end{cases}

We want to prove that
d=d_{0}
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
d_{0}\geq d
. Now suppose, in order to reach a contradiction, that
d_{0}>d
. This implies that
0<\dfrac{d}{d_{0}}<1
, so that
\dfrac{d}{d_{o}}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, there exists a prime
p
and an integer
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
such that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\dfrac{d}{d_{0}}\right)=-k\leq-1
. Then, from equation (
4.5
), it follows that for this prime
p
we have

\begin{cases}0\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}(A_{0})=\operatorname{ord}_{p}\bigg(\left(\dfrac{d}{d_{0}}\right)^{4}A\bigg)=4\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\dfrac{d}{d_{0}}\right)+\operatorname{ord}_{p}(A)\implies 4k\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}(A)\vskip 4.0pt\\
0\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}(B_{0})=\operatorname{ord}_{p}\bigg(\left(\dfrac{d}{d_{0}}\right)^{6}B\bigg)=6\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\dfrac{d}{d_{0}}\right)+\operatorname{ord}_{p}(B)\implies 6k\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}(B)\end{cases}

It follows that
p^{4k}\mid A
and
p^{6k}\mid B
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
d=d_{0}
, and
A_{0}=A
and
B_{0}=B
.
Finally, notice that
H_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{A,B})\geq 1
, so that

1\leq d^{12}\leq d^{12}H_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{A,B})=H_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}})\leq X.

We conclude that

1\leq d\leq\lfloor X^{1/12}\rfloor.

These facts prove the decomposition (
4.3
). To prove (
4.4
), simply notice that the
j
-invariant of a curve is invariant under the twist operator.
∎

Proposition 4.4

Let

for every

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}(A(j,m))=\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\frac{N^{2}_{a(j)}m^{2}}{a(j)}\right)

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(N_{a(j)})+2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a(j))

\displaystyle=2\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{2}\right\rceil+2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a(j)).

Then, using (
4.18
) yields

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(A(j,m))}\leq 2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)+1.

Similarly, if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a(j))<0
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}(B(j,m))=\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\frac{N^{3}_{a(j)}m^{3}}{a(j)}\right)

\displaystyle=3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(N_{a(j)})+3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a(j))

\displaystyle=3\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)}{3}\right\rceil+3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a(j))

and using (
4.19
), we conclude that

\displaystyle 3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)\leq\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(B(j,m))}\leq 3\operatorname{ord}_{p}(m)+2\quad\text{if $\operatorname{ord}_{p}{(a(j))}<0$}.

Finally, these inequalities imply that
E_{A(j,m),B(j,m)}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
if and only if
m
is square-free.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The parametrization in (

) not only enables the enumeration of curves in

by generalized naive height, but also identifies the

-isomorphism class representative of any such curve. Given

by setting

and

, where

. Since

, this curve is the unique representative of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism class. Moreover, a curve
E_{A,B}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}
is a representative if and only if the corresponding parameter

in (
4.13
) is square-free.

The following proposition states a well-known asymptotic formula for the function

, which counts the number of

-free integers in the interval

for the

[

for the general case). This result will be used in the proofs of our asymptotic formulas for counting elliptic curves.

Proposition 4.8
.

Recall that an integer
n
is called
k
-free if there is no prime
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
p^{k}|n
. For
X\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
and
k\geq 2
, let
Q_{k}(X)
denote the number of
k
-free integers
n
such that
n\leq X
. Then

(4.20)

\displaystyle Q_{k}(X)=\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}X+O(X^{1/k})

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

We now give the proof of Theorems
2.6
and

. The theorems for the calibrated and the uncalibrated height in Section

(\alpha,\beta)

(4,27)

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then for every
X>1
, the number of elliptic curves in the families
\mathcal{E}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
satisfies the following asymptotic formulas.
For
j=0
, we have

(4.21)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{E}_{0}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\frac{2}{\beta^{1/2}\zeta(6)}X^{1/2}+O(X^{1/12}),

and

(4.22)

\displaystyle\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{0}}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=2\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/2}}{\beta^{1/2}}\right\rfloor=\frac{2}{\beta^{1/2}}X^{1/2}+O(1).

For

, we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{E}_{1728}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\frac{2}{\alpha^{1/3}\zeta(4)}X^{1/3}+O(X^{1/12})

and

(4.24)

\displaystyle\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1728}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=2\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/3}}{\alpha^{1/3}}\right\rfloor=\frac{2}{\alpha^{1/3}}X^{1/3}+O(1).

Finally, for
j\neq 0,1728
, we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{E}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\frac{2}{\zeta(2)}c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}+O(X^{1/12})

and

(4.26)

\displaystyle\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=2\left\lfloor c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}\right\rfloor

is the constant defined in Theorem

.

Proof.

We consider separately the three cases where the
j
-invariant is equal to 0, equal to 1728, or lies in
\mathbb{Q}\smallsetminus\{0,1728\}
.

Curves with
j=0

If a curve
E_{A,B}
satisfies that
j=0
, then
A=0
. Therefore, the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
corresponds with the points
(0,B)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with

and
|B|\leq\dfrac{X^{1/2}}{\beta^{1/2}}
. Thus, since
\lfloor X\rfloor=X+O(1)
, we have

\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{0}}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=2\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/2}}{\beta^{1/2}}\right\rfloor=\frac{2}{\beta^{1/2}}X^{1/2}+O(1).

Furthermore, the set
\mathcal{E}_{0}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
corresponds to points
(0,B)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that
B\neq 0
,
|B|\leq\dfrac{X^{1/2}}{\beta^{1/2}}
and
B
is
6
-free. Thus, by Proposition
4.8
, we have

\#\mathcal{E}_{0}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\frac{2}{\beta^{1/2}\zeta(6)}X^{1/2}+O(X^{1/12}).

Curves with
j=1728

On the other hand, if a curve
E_{A,B}
satisfies
j=1728
, then
B=0
. Thus, the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1728}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
corresponds with points
(A,0)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
A\neq 0
and
|A|\leq\dfrac{X^{1/3}}{\alpha^{1/3}}
. Then, we have

\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1728}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=2\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/3}}{\alpha^{1/3}}\right\rfloor=\frac{2}{\alpha^{1/3}}X^{1/3}+O(1).

Moreover, the set
\mathcal{E}_{1728}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})
corresponds with points
(A,0)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that
A\neq 0
,
|A|\leq\dfrac{X^{1/3}}{\alpha^{1/3}}
and
A
is 4-free. Therefore, in this case Proposition
4.8
yields

\#\mathcal{E}_{1728}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\frac{2}{\alpha^{1/3}\zeta(4)}X^{1/3}+O(X^{1/12}).

Curves with

Now, let
j\in{\mathbb{Q}}\smallsetminus\{0,1728\}
. In Theorem
4.6
we already proved that

\#\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=2\left\lfloor c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}\right\rfloor

and moreover, by the parametrization (

), that the set
\mathcal{E}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})

, we conclude that

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{E}_{j}(X;H_{\alpha,\beta})=\frac{2}{\zeta(2)}c(j;H_{\alpha,\beta})X^{1/6}+O(X^{1/12}).

∎

Theorem 4.10
.

For every

, the numbers of CM elliptic curves in the families

) we have

.

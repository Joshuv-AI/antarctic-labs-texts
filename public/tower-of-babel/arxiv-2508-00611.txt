Rapid cosmological inference with the two-loop matter power spectrum Preprint: APS/123-QED
Thomas Bakx, Henrique Rubira, Nora Elisa Chisari, Zvonimir Vlah

Abstract
We compute the two-loop effective field theory (EFT) power spectrum of dark matter density fluctuations in Λ \Lambda CDM using the recently proposed COBRA method Bakx et al. 2024 . With COBRA , we are able to evaluate the two-loop matter power spectrum in ∼ 1 \sim 1 millisecond at ∼ 0.1 % \sim 0.1\% precision on one CPU for arbitrary redshifts and on scales where perturbation theory applies. As an application, we use the nonlinear matter power spectrum from the Dark Sky simulation to assess the performance of the two-loop EFT power spectrum compared to the one-loop EFT power spectrum at z = 0 z=0 . We find that, for volumes typical for Stage IV galaxy surveys, V = 25 ​ ( Gpc / h ) 3 V=25\,(\text{Gpc}/h)^{3} , the two-loop EFT can provide unbiased cosmological constraints on Ω m , H 0 \Omega_{m},H_{0} and A s A_{s} using scales up to k max = 0.26 ​ h / Mpc k_{\text{max}}=0.26\,h/\text{Mpc} , thereby outperforming the constraints from the one-loop EFT ( k max = 0.11 ​ h / Mpc k_{\text{max}}=0.11\,h/\text{Mpc} ). The Figure of Merit on these three parameters increases by a factor ∼ 1.9 \sim 1.9 and the one-dimensional marginalized constraints improve by ∼ 35 % \sim 35\% for Ω m \Omega_{m} , ∼ 20 % \sim 20\% for H 0 H_{0} and ∼ 15 % \sim 15\% for A s A_{s} .

Introduction

The large-scale structure (LSS) of the Universe has become a central probe of cosmological information
Alam et al. 2017
;
The Dark Energy Survey Collaboration 2005
;
Ahumada et al. 2020
;
Aghamousa et al. 2016
;
Amendola et al. 2013
;
Ivezic et al. 2008
;
Doré et al. 2014
;
Ellis et al. 2014
. To extract the maximal amount of information out of galaxy surveys, modeling the nonlinear process of structure formation is vital. The effective field theory of LSS (EFT of LSS)
Baumann et al. 2012
;
Carrasco et al. 2012
;
Carrasco et al. 2014a
;
Konstandin et al. 2019
;
Angulo et al. 2015
;
Vlah et al. 2015
;
Baldauf et al. 2021
;
Sanchez et al. 2014
;
D’Amico et al. 2020
;
Ivanov et al. 2020
;
Colas et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2022
builds on top of the nonlinear couplings and loop-integral computations of the standard perturbation theory (SPT) approach
Bernardeau et al. 2002
, at the same time marginalizing over the unknown UV physics via counterterms. These approaches have been shown to yield accurate predictions for the power spectrum, bispectrum and trispectrum
Foreman et al. 2016
;
Steele and Baldauf 2021a
;
Steele and Baldauf 2021b
;
Baldauf et al. 2021
of the dark matter density contrast into the mildly nonlinear regime. Such findings strongly suggest that there is cosmological information to be gained from modelling the matter distribution on scales that are amenable to analytic techniques.

Crucially, rendering such an approach computationally feasible for cosmological inference is by no means trivial, as it requires recalculating high-dimensional integrals (i.e. loop corrections to the linear theory prediction) at each new set of cosmological parameters. To this end, analytic methods based on Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT) of the linear power spectrum
P_{L}(k)
have been developed in
Schmittfull et al. 2016
;
McEwen et al. 2016
;
Schmittfull and Vlah 2016
;
Simonović et al. 2018
and extensively applied to compute power spectra in next-to-leading order (one-loop). Alternative basis including massive propagators were considered by
Anastasiou et al. 2024
. However, the calculation of higher-order corrections is either challenging or outright intractable within these techniques. This situation is therefore unsatisfactory: most pressingly, studies from
Baldauf et al. 2021
;
Garny and Taule 2022
;
Taule and Garny 2023
offer evidence that two-loop corrections to the power spectrum can significantly increase the range of scales modeled by perturbation theory, but a practically efficient approach to compute such corrections was not known. Numerical evaluation of the 5-dimensional two-loop integrals takes typically in between
\sim 10^{1}
and
10^{2}
seconds for a single
k
mode (for
10^{-3}
relative precision using the code described in
Blas et al. 2014
;
Konstandin et al. 2019
and the SUAVE algorithm based on
Lepage 1978
;
Lepage 2021
and implemented in the
CUBA
library
Hahn 2005
), becoming then unfeasible if one wants to repeatedly compute them within a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) setup to perform cosmological parameter inference.

Recently, in
Bakx et al. 2024
(hereafter Paper I) a new, unified approach to this problem dubbed Cosmology with Optimally factorized Bases for Rapid Approximation (
COBRA
) was proposed, providing a clear and pragmatic path towards calculating arbitrary corrections to any N-point function.
COBRA
is based on a decomposition of the linear power spectrum in terms of a representative basis. The loop integrals of EFT of LSS can then be (pre)calculated for this basis, such that when exploring different cosmologies within an MCMC one only has to project the given cosmology onto this integral basis.
Importantly, the proposed technique is optimal in terms of efficiency and memory requirement and does not rely on analytic expressions for perturbation theory integrals, unlike previous approaches
Simonović et al. 2018
.

A first case study comprising the one-loop power spectrum of galaxies yielded encouraging results, but as already pointed out in Paper I the efficacy of
COBRA
is most prominent in more complicated scenarios. In this paper, we apply
COBRA
to the two-loop power spectrum of dark matter fluctuations. We show that
COBRA
is able to reproduce the direct two-loop result with permille precision for a broad range of cosmologies. Finally, we run an MCMC with the two-loop matter power spectra, indicating its relative gains with respect to the one-loop scenario (for earlier results in this direction, see
Cataneo et al. 2017
;
Osato et al. 2023
;
Osato et al. 2019
).

The structure of the paper is the following. In Section
II
we briefly summarize the
COBRA
construction from Paper I. In Section
III
, we review the two-loop EFT of LSS theory, including the so-called IR resummation and compare the
COBRA

Skillman et al. 2014
in
IV
. We conclude in Section
V
. Some technical details regarding our numerical implementation of the two-loop power spectrum are relegated to Appendix

, we discuss the IR-safety of the loop integrals in

II
COBRA

The main idea of
COBRA
is to achieve an optimal decomposition of the linear matter power spectrum into a small set of basis functions of the form

\displaystyle P_{L}^{\Theta}(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{b}}w_{i}(\Theta)v_{i}(k)\,,

(1)

where the number of terms
N_{b}
is as small as possible to guarantee a required precision in the loop computations. Here
v_{i}
are functions that depend only on the wavenumber
k
, called
scale functions
, and the coefficients
w_{i}(\Theta)
depend only on cosmological parameters, collectively labeled
\Theta
. Obtaining such a decomposition over a given range of cosmological parameters is relatively cheap and can be straightforwardly implemented via a singular value decomposition (SVD). We consider in this work
\Lambda
CDM cosmologies with priors described in Table
1

\sim 10^{-3}\,(10^{-4})

N_{b}=9\,(12)

III.2

1
, are exactly the same as Paper I, but the
v_{i}
functions are slightly different due to the larger range in wavenumber
k

is obtained via the SVD decomposition, the weights can either be computed via projection onto the linear power spectrum from a Boltzmann solver, or emulated through an alternative method. Here we employ the latter approach, utilizing the RBF interpolation detailed in Paper I (see
Fasshauer and McCourt 2012

Bakx et al. 2024

Using the linear decomposition from Eq. (
1
), loop integrals can be recast as small multilinear contractions involving precomputed tensors
\mathcal{S}_{ij\dots}
and the weights
w_{i}(\Theta)
, where the tensors do not depend on cosmological parameters. Each index of the tensor runs over the
N_{b}
elements of the basis
v_{i}(k)
. As an example, a one-loop power spectrum can be written as

\displaystyle P^{\Theta}_{\text{1-loop}}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{st.}(k)+\mathcal{S}^{l}_{i}(k)w_{i}(\Theta)

(2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{q}_{ij}(k)w_{i}(\Theta)w_{j}(\Theta)\,,

where the sum over indices is implicit,
\mathcal{S}^{l}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}^{q}_{ij}
are respectively linear and quadratic in the power spectra and the first term is independent of
P_{L}
, i.e. a stochastic component
Rubira and Schmidt 2024a
. The two-loop power spectrum involves cubic integrals of
P_{L}(k)
, demanding then an extra rank-3 tensor
\mathcal{S}^{c}_{ijk}
so that

\displaystyle P^{\Theta}_{\text{2-loop}}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{st.}(k)+\mathcal{S}^{l}_{i}(k)w_{i}(\Theta)

(3)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{q}_{ij}(k)w_{i}(\Theta)w_{j}(\Theta)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{c}_{ijk}(k)w_{i}(\Theta)w_{j}(\Theta)w_{k}(\Theta)\,.

It is important to notice that the considered number of elements in the basis is
not necessarily the same for all
\mathcal{S}_{ij\dots}
tensors, and this can be calibrated according to the precision required for each term. See Appendix
A
for a broader discussion on how the tensors are calculated. The most time consuming part of
COBRA
is the precomputation of those tensors, since the projection onto the basis that happens during parameter inference is numerically cheap. Considering fewer elements on the basis for the tensors
\mathcal{S}_{ij\dots}
also reduces computational cost, since one has to precompute fewer loop integrals. We discuss the number of elements in the basis considered for each loop in Section
III.3
, but we mention in advance that for this work we precomputed
165
(
56
) two-loop integrals for the full (no-wiggle) spectra in every
k
-bin to get
\sim 0.1\%
precision on the total matter power spectrum.

III.1
The two-loop EFT

In this work, we focus on the matter nonlinear power spectrum. The description of matter tracers would involve including bias operators (see
Desjacques et al. 2018
for a review), which is beyond the scope of this work.
The one-loop matter power spectrum in the EFT of LSS contains only one free parameter; it is given by
Baumann et al. 2012
;
Carrasco et al. 2012

\displaystyle P_{\text{EFT,1l}}=P_{L}+P_{\text{SPT,1l}}-2(2\pi)c_{\text{s},1}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}P_{L}\,,

(4)

where
P_{\text{SPT,1l}}
is the one-loop SPT spectrum, i.e.

\displaystyle P_{\text{SPT,1l}}=P_{22}+2P_{13}\,,

(5)

and
c_{\text{s},1}^{2}
is the sound velocity counterterm associated with the
\nabla^{2}\delta
operator. To render the counterterms dimensionsless, we normalize the higher-derivative counterterm by the nonlinear scale, which we take to be
k_{\text{nl}}=1\,h/
Mpc for simplicity when performing the fits. The two-loop power spectrum in the EFT of LSS framework is given by
Carrasco et al. 2014a
;
Foreman et al. 2016
;
Konstandin et al. 2019

\displaystyle P_{\text{EFT,2l}}=P_{L}+P_{\text{SPT,1l}}+P_{\text{SPT,2l}}-2(2\pi)c_{\text{s}}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}P_{L}

(6)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2(2\pi)c_{\text{s},1}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}P_{\text{SPT,1l}}-2(2\pi)c_{\text{quad}}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}P_{\text{quad}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}[(c_{\text{s},1}^{2})^{2}-2c_{4}]\frac{k^{4}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{4}}P_{L}+(2\pi)^{2}c_{\epsilon}\frac{k^{4}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{4}}\frac{1}{k_{\text{nl}}^{3}}\,,

where
P_{\text{SPT,2l}}
is the complete two-loop SPT spectrum, i.e.
Simonović et al. 2018

\displaystyle P_{\text{SPT,2l}}=P_{33}^{I}+P_{33}^{II}+2P_{24}+2P_{15}\,,

(7)

and
c_{\text{s}}^{2}=c_{\text{s},1}^{2}+c_{\text{s},2}^{2}
. The two-loop EFT Eq. (
6
) has counterterms
c_{\text{s},1}^{2}
and
c_{\text{s},2}^{2}
linked to the
\nabla^{2}\delta
operator,
c_{\rm quad}
to the quadratic
\nabla^{2}\delta^{2}
term,
c_{4}
to the quartic-derivative
\nabla^{4}\delta
and also the stochastic
c_{\epsilon}
parameter, totaling five free parameters. In what follows, we set
c_{\epsilon}=0
in line with the findings of
Foreman et al. 2016
. Hence, we only consider the four parameters

\displaystyle\{c_{\text{s},1}^{2},c_{\text{s},2}^{2},c_{4},c_{\text{quad}}\}\,,

(8)

for the two-loop EFT.

III.2
IR resummation

In complete analogy to Paper I, we implement the resummation of the large-scale displacements (IR resummation) using the ‘wiggle-no-wiggle split’
Baldauf et al. 2015
;
Vlah et al. 2016
;
Blas et al. 2016
to separate the broadband power from the BAO feature, where the no-wiggle linear power spectrum is, following
Vlah et al. 2016
, given by

\displaystyle P^{\Theta}_{\text{nw}}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{*}_{\text{EH}}(k)\frac{1}{\lambda(\log k)\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\mathrm{d}\log{q}\frac{P^{\Theta}_{L}(10^{\log{q}})}{P^{*}_{\text{EH}}(10^{\log{q}})}

(9)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\exp{\bigg(-\frac{1}{2\lambda(\log k)^{2}}(\log{q}-\log{k})^{2}\bigg)}\,,

with
P^{*}_{\text{EH}}(k)
the Eisenstein-Hu power spectrum
Eisenstein and Hu 1997
and
\lambda(k)
is a scale-dependent smoothing scale.
1
1

1

Explicitly, the filter takes the form

\displaystyle\lambda(\log k)=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\,\frac{\exp[-\alpha_{3}(\log k-\alpha_{4})^{2}]}{1+\exp[\alpha_{5}(\log k+\alpha_{6})]}\,,

(10)

with coefficients
\alpha_{1}=0.005,\,\alpha_{2}=0.45,\,\alpha_{3}=0.9,\,\alpha_{4}=-0.15,\,\alpha_{5}=8,\,\alpha_{6}=0.5
.
We experimented with other choices and found only minor impact on the quality of the fit.

The filtering operation
\mathcal{F}
of smoothing the power spectrum wiggles is linear and independent of cosmology and therefore

\displaystyle P_{\text{nw}}^{\Theta}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[w_{i}(\Theta)v_{i}](k)=w_{i}(\Theta)\mathcal{F}[v_{i}](k)

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{i}(\Theta)v_{i}^{\text{nw}}(k),

and correspondingly

\displaystyle P_{\text{w}}^{\Theta}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{i}(\Theta)\left[v_{i}(k)-\mathcal{F}[v_{i}](k)\right]

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{i}(\Theta)v_{i}^{\text{w}}(k).

The damping factor that is needed for the IR-resummation is defined as

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(\Theta)\equiv\int_{0}^{k_{\text{o}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{6\pi^{2}}\left[1-j_{0}(qr_{\text{o}})+2j_{2}(qr_{\text{o}})\right]P_{\text{nw}}(q),

(13)

and we used
r_{\text{o}}=105\text{ Mpc}/h^{*}
and
k_{\text{o}}=0.2\,h^{*}/\text{Mpc}
.
In addition to this IR resummation scheme, which relies on the somewhat arbitrary choice of a wiggle–no-wiggle split, there exist several direct IR resummation approaches primarily based on Lagrangian perturbation theory (see, e.g.,
Senatore and Zaldarriaga 2015
;
Vlah et al. 2015
;
Schmittfull et al. 2019
). As a cross-check of our results, we also implemented the scheme used in
Foreman et al. 2016
to verify that any potential differences in our results do not stem from the choice of IR resummation and by its possible (albeit small) mixing of scales in the UV regime. A comparison of the two resummation schemes reveals no significant differences between the results.

At one-loop, IR resummation (using the wiggle-no-wiggle split) amounts to replacing the linear power spectrum by

\displaystyle P_{L}\rightarrow P_{\text{nw}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}(1+k^{2}\Sigma^{2})P_{\text{w}}.

(14)

The one-loop correction is replaced by

\displaystyle P_{\text{SPT,1l}}\rightarrow P_{\text{nw},\text{1l}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}P_{\text{w},\text{1l}}\,,

(15)

and for the one-loop counterterm we use

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}P_{L}\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}\left[P_{\text{nw}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}P_{\text{w}}\right].

(16)

Here and in the below, ‘wiggly’ variants for power spectra are defined as
P_{\text{w},X}=P_{\text{full},X}-P_{\text{nw},X}
. At two loops, we implement IR resummation by replacing

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{L}&\rightarrow P_{\text{nw}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}\left[1+k^{2}\Sigma^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}\Sigma^{2})^{2}\right]P_{\text{w}}\,,\end{aligned}

(17)

in the linear theory part of the prediction, while the one- and two-loop SPT part are replaced by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{\text{SPT,1l}}&\rightarrow P_{\text{nw},\text{1l}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}(1+k^{2}\Sigma^{2})P_{\text{w},\text{1l}}\,,\\
P_{\text{SPT,2l}}&\rightarrow P_{\text{nw},\text{2l}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}P_{\text{w},\text{2l}}\,\end{aligned}

(18)

and the ‘quad’ part by

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}P_{\text{quad}}\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}\left(P_{\text{nw},\text{quad}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}P_{\text{w},\text{quad}}\right).

(19)

The two other counterterms become

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}P_{L}&\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{2}}\left[P_{\text{nw}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}(1+k^{2}\Sigma^{2})P_{\text{w}}\right]\,,\\
\frac{k^{4}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{4}}P_{L}&\rightarrow\frac{k^{4}}{k_{\text{nl}}^{4}}(P_{\text{nw}}+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}}P_{\text{w}}).\end{aligned}

(20)

Moreover, we also implemented the one and two-loop integrals using the IR-safe integrals
Carrasco et al. 2014a
;
Blas et al. 2014
, which we discuss in details in Appendix
B
.

III.3
COBRA two-loop results

In this section we discuss one of the main results of this paper, that is the fast computation of the two-loop EFT spectra with
COBRA

The complete two-loop power spectrum requires the calculation of rank-two (one-loop)

We use the two-loop codes of

In terms of computation power, as commented before,
COBRA
precomputes the loop spectra in the SVD basis, such that further evaluation for a given cosmology only demands projecting the linear power spectra to obtain the
w_{i}
coefficients, which takes
\sim 1
ms on one CPU in Python 3. The precomputation of the two-loop spectra (which corresponds to the main time bottleneck) in
\mathcal{S}^{c}_{ijk}
takes typically less than
10^{2}
seconds per mode (for
10^{-3}
relative precision) in a single core. Overall, the
COBRA
implementation of the two-loop integrals is efficient in both memory usage and computational performance.

IV
Comparison to simulation

In this Section we demonstrate the applicability of the
COBRA
two-loop results comparing that to simulation data, which is described in Sec.
IV.1
. We present the metrics used for our analysis in Sec.
IV.2
and the results in Sec.

IV.1
The Dark Sky simulation

For the comparison to data, we used the Dark Sky simulation power spectrum. The
ds14
\_
a
run of the Dark Sky simulation suite is an extremely large N-body simulation first described in
Skillman et al. 2014
containing
10240^{3}
particles in a box of side length
L=8
Gpc
/h
. This yields a total volume of
V_{\text{full}}=512\,(\text{Gpc}/h)^{3}
, and a fundamental mode of
k_{f}=2\pi/L\approx 8\times 10^{-4}\,h/{\rm Mpc}
. We use the available
z=0
redshift snapshot. As such, the relative cosmic variance error

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{P(k)}}{P(k)}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{N_{k}}}=\sqrt{\frac{k_{f}^{3}}{2\pi k^{2}\Delta k}}\,,

(23)

on the power spectrum is very small, reaching values of
\left[\sigma_{P}/P\right]_{k=0.25\,h/\text{Mpc}}\approx 6\times 10^{-4}
even for our binning choice of
\Delta k=4k_{f}\approx 0.003\,h/\text{Mpc}
. We neglect the shot-noise contribution, since the number density for matter is very high.

Such volumes and corresponding measurement errors are not within reach of current or next-generation large-scale structure surveys. We therefore take the output power spectrum from the
ds14
\_
a
run and assign to it a fractional error corresponding to a volume of
V_{\text{fit}}=25\,(\text{Gpc}/h)^{3}
, which resembles that of a Stage IV spectroscopic galaxy survey such as the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI)
Aghamousa et al. 2016
.

Noticing that the loop contributions are heavily suppressed [scaling as
k^{2}P_{L}(k)
] on large scales, we extrapolate the loop corrections at low wavenumbers using a power law of the form

\displaystyle P(k)=P(k_{\rm low})\times\bigg(\frac{k}{k_{\rm low}}\bigg)^{2+n_{s}}\,,

(24)

where
n_{s}
is the spectral index at the given cosmology and
k_{\rm low}=0.01\,h^{*}/\text{Mpc}
.

IV.2
Likelihood and performance metrics

We assume a Gaussian likelihood with a diagonal covariance, where the diagonal elements of the covariance in each
k
-bin are defined via Eq. (
23
), with
P
therein being the nonlinear spectrum measured from the simulation.
We expect that the non-Gaussian part of the covariance, that comes from the trispectrum, is relatively suppressed compared to the Gaussian part throughout all the scales considered in this work, (see e.g.
Bertolini et al. 2016
).
Hence, the likelihood is given by
4
4

4

Note that this definition differs from the choice taken in
Konstandin et al. 2019
, which was based on the residuals of the fit to the simulation data. In this work we weight the log-likelihood by the power spectrum error bars
\sigma_{P}

).

\displaystyle-2\log\mathcal{L}(k_{\text{max}})=\sum_{i}^{k_{\text{max}}}\bigg(\frac{P_{\text{EFT}}(k_{i})-P_{\rm sim}(k_{i})}{\sigma_{P(k_{i})}}\bigg)^{2}\,,

(25)

where

. Once more, we explicitly verified that the interpolation error is negligible by sampling extra

points in a single cosmology. We adhere to the choices from Eqs. (

) and (
22
) for the number of basis functions. We checked that using a lower number for the two-loop terms does not impact the results; we thus conclude that our inference is sufficiently precise.

Following e.g.
Adame et al. 2025
;

, we put a conservative BBN prior on the baryon density

5

(26)

i.e., centered at the true value of the simulations and with width equal to that of

\displaystyle n_{s}\sim\mathcal{U}(0.91,1.01)\,,

(27)

which roughly coincides with the normal prior from
Adame et al. 2025
. We find that these two parameters are generally not well constrained by the power spectrum, and so we treat them as nuisance parameters to be marginalized over without quoting their posterior means and uncertainties. For the other cosmological parameters, we consider a uniform prior in the range of Table
1
. We sample the parameters
\omega_{c},h,A_{s}\times 10^{9}
and only transform to
\Omega_{m},H_{0}
and

With regards to the counterterms, we assign uniform

steps for the one-loop and two-loop case, respectively, with the affine invariance sampler implemented in
emcee

Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
, using 32 walkers. We also assessed the convergence of the chains by running multiple independent instances and comparing the results, finding negligible differences in posterior means and covariances.

The figure of bias (FoB) metric is defined as

\displaystyle\text{FoB}(k_{\text{max}})=\bigg(\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta^{\alpha}-\theta^{\alpha}_{\text{fid}})S_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}(\theta^{\beta}-\theta^{\beta}_{\text{fid}})\bigg)^{1/2}\,,

(28)

where
\theta^{\alpha}_{\text{fid}}
are the fiducial (true) values of the simulation parameters and
S_{\alpha\beta}
is the posterior covariance, for a fixed
k_{\text{max}}
. Given that observed shifts at the
V_{\text{fit}}

\displaystyle\text{FoB}(k_{\text{max}})<1\,,

(29)

to consider a model fit acceptable (noting that the
1\sigma
threshold would be
\text{FoB}<1.88
, see e.g.
Eggemeier et al. 2025
).
We also analyze the figure of merit (FoM) defined as

\displaystyle\text{FoM}(k_{\text{max}})=\frac{1}{\left(\text{det}(S_{\alpha\beta})/\theta^{\alpha}_{\text{fid}}\theta^{\beta}_{\text{fid}}\right)^{1/3}}\,,

(30)

where the power
1/3
accounts for the dimension of the parameter space, since the FoB and FoM are calculated over
\Omega_{m},\,H_{0}
and
A_{s}\times 10^{9}
. We also quote the reduced chi-squared statistic

\displaystyle\chi^{2}_{\text{red}}(k_{\text{max}})=\frac{-2\log\mathcal{L}(k_{\text{max}})}{N_{\text{bin}}-N_{\text{dof}}}\,,

(31)

with
N_{\text{dof}}
the total number of degrees of freedom and
N_{\text{bin}}
the number of bins included. We fix
N_{\text{dof}}=6
for both one and two-loop analysis, corresponding to the five free cosmological parameters and the one-loop counterterm.
6
6

6

The two-loop counterterms have no impact in the fit at low
k
, where
N_{\text{bin}}
is small, and only start being relevant at high

N_{\text{bin}}\gg N_{\text{dof}}
. For a more general discussion, see e.g.
Raveri and Hu 2019

We use the
full
simulation volume
V_{\text{full}}
in the covariance when computing
\chi_{\text{red}}^{2}
, since then the covariance represents the statistical scatter in the power spectrum measurement and in that case
\chi_{\text{red}}^{2}
should be close to unity.

V
Discussion and conclusion

In this work we have extended the
COBRA
framework to calculate the two-loop power spectrum. Our pipeline computes a single two-loop power spectrum in
\sim 1
ms and yields converged posteriors in a negligible
\sim 20
hours on one core.
For that, we precomputed
O(100)
two-loop integrals for the SVD basis, each of those taking a few minutes per mode. Thus, we conclude that a calculation of the two-loop power spectrum of biased tracers is also within computational reach.
We have demonstrated that
COBRA
is both fast and accurate, enabling the extension of EFT of LSS application using real data (so far limited to one-loop) to the two-loop case in the near future.

In addition, we have shown that additional unbiased cosmological information can be extracted from the matter power spectrum by including two-loop corrections. Adding two-loop corrections shrink posteriors on
\Lambda
CDM parameters by
15-35\%
and increase the FoM (calculated for
\Omega_{m},H_{0}
and
A_{s}
) in between
1.7
and
1.9
. Therefore, this work adds important piece to answer how much information can be extracted from the mild nonlinear using galaxy clustering data in real space.

Another relevant ongoing question is to estimate the range of applicability
k_{\rm max}
of perturbative approaches. We find a sharp deterioration of the one-loop FoB for
k_{\text{max}}>0.11\,h/\text{Mpc}
for volumes comparable to DESI at
z=0
. For two loops the deterioration of FoB and
\chi^{2}_{\rm red}
is less severe, but the running of the counterterms led us to adopt a conservative
k_{\text{max}}=0.26\,h/\text{Mpc}
. Therefore, the two-loop result more than doubles the range of validity of the EFT of LSS. Doubling the scales of applicability of EFT modeling not only has the advantage of decreasing the error bars of
\L
CDM parameters, but also allows for testing models that affect the power spectrum on quasi-linear scales, e.g. by probing the suppression or enhancement of matter fluctuations. This is especially of interest for currently ongoing efforts to measure CMB lensing (see e.g.
Qu et al. 2024
;
Madhavacheril et al. 2024
;
Ge et al. 2025
) and galaxy lensing (e.g.
Chen et al. 2024
) on mildly non-linear scales.

Despite the added constraining power associated with the larger range of scales probed by the two-loop matter power spectrum (third panel of Fig.
5
), most of this increase is reflected in tighter errors on the EFT counterterms rather than the FoM on cosmological parameters of interest. This is of course partly expected, since the counterterms have most of their support at larger wavenumbers. For example, one can clearly see in Fig.
4
(bottom panel) that the posterior width on
c_{\text{quad}}
shrinks by a factor of few between
k_{\text{max}}=0.12\,h/\text{Mpc}
and
k_{\text{max}}=0.26\,h/\text{Mpc}
. Thus, it would be valuable to search for models that contain fewer nuisance parameters than the EFT counterterm parametrization, either with the Lagrangian framework that incorporates shell crossing effects (e.g.
McDonald and Vlah 2018
) or alternatively with the recently proposed framework of Vlasov Perturbation Theory (VPT)
Garny et al. 2025
;
Garny et al. 2023
, either being a natural candidate for such models.

It should be emphasized that the results presented in this work cannot readily be generalized to draw conclusions about the two-loop galaxy power spectrum, which involves many more nuisance parameters, both for the one- and two-loop results (see e.g.
Eggemeier et al. 2019
;
Schmidt 2021
;
Donath et al. 2024
;
Bakx et al. 2025
).
In addition, the presence of redshift-space distortions may limit the range of validity of perturbative models compared to the real-space case considered here
Okumura et al. 2012
;
Vlah et al. 2013
;
D’Amico et al. 2024
;
Ivanov et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2020
, at the same time adding information about the growth rate.

Appendix A
Constructing loop tensors

Here we provide more details on how loop tensors of any rank can be efficiently constructed with
COBRA
.
Note first that the tensor
\mathcal{S}_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}
, constructed as a linear functional of direct tensor products of
v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}
, can always be taken to be symmetric.
A symmetric tensor of rank
k
in
N
dimensions has

\displaystyle\binom{N+k-1}{k}

(34)

independent elements; these can be taken to be the ‘super-diagonal’ ones, i.e.
\mathcal{S}_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}
with
i_{1}\geq i_{2}\geq\dots\geq i_{k}
.
For the two-loop tensors (
k=3
), the evaluation with
N=9
elements in the basis for the full spectra
N=6
for the no-wiggle demands then the computation of 165 and 56 integrals, respectively for every
k
.
At first sight, to compute all entries of this tensor, one would seemingly need to explicitly compute all ‘cross-correlations’ of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}=\mathcal{S}[v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}]\quad(i_{1}\geq i_{2}\geq\dots).

(35)

where multiple input scale functions
v_{i}
are used.
This is technically and numerically demanding, especially if one breaks the IR safety of integrands (see Sec.
B
for further discussion).
However, it is possible to construct the full tensor without requiring multiple input scale functions, as we discuss next.

The procedure we outline here works for tensors of any rank, but we only explicitly need it for ranks 2 and 3.
To see how this works, note that
\mathcal{S}
is always linear in each of its inputs. For that reason, we can construct ‘off-diagonal’ elements of the symmetric tensor
\mathcal{S}
(i.e. where the indices are not all equal) by taking linear combinations of
\mathcal{S}
evaluated on the
same
element. As an example, one has for rank 2:

\displaystyle 2\,\mathcal{S}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}+\mathcal{S}_{ji}=\mathcal{S}[v_{i},v_{j}]+\mathcal{S}[v_{j},v_{i}]

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[v_{i}+v_{j},v_{i}+v_{j}]-\mathcal{S}[v_{i},v_{i}]-\mathcal{S}[v_{j},v_{j}]\,,

where the bottom line only involves evaluations of
\mathcal{S}
with the same element in each position. Hence, by evaluating
\mathcal{S}
for all possible linear combinations
v_{i}+v_{j}
with
i\geq j
as both first and second argument
8
8

8

When
i=j
we can recover
\mathcal{S}[v_{i},v_{i}]=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{S}[v_{i}+v_{i},v_{i}+v_{i}]
.
[which reproduces the correct dimension counting from Eq. (
34
)] and then applying Eq. (
36
), we can automatically obtain all entries
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
.

In general, one can prove (see e.g.
Marcus 1973
) that evaluating the tensor
\mathcal{S}_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}
amounts to evaluating
\mathcal{S}
for the linear combinations

\displaystyle v_{i_{1}}+v_{i_{2}}+\dots+v_{i_{k}}\quad(i_{1}\geq i_{2}\geq\dots\geq i_{k})\,,

(37)

as each of its arguments. Indeed, the number of such combinations equals the expression in Eq. (
34
). Thus, no fewer combinations can be used, and using more is redundant. Then, by taking appropriate linear combinations of these elements one can recover the elements
\mathcal{S}_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}
.

Appendix B
Infrared safety

It is well known that cosmological perturbation theory, both in its SPT and EFT formulations, exhibits so-called infrared (IR) divergences
Jain and Bertschinger 1996
;
Carrasco et al. 2014b
at the level of individual loop diagrams. These arise from large-scale displacements that cancel out in equal-time correlators at each order in perturbation theory, due to the equivalence principle. In practice, this leads to partial cancellations of power in the high-
k
regime of the power spectrum when summing over all relevant loop diagrams. As emphasised in
Carrasco et al. 2014b
(see also
Fasiello et al. 2022
), it is preferable to implement these cancellations directly at the level of the integrands during the evaluation of the power spectrum with fixed accuracy, rather than relying on the cancellation of individual diagram contributions, which can lead to a loss of numerical precision.

Consequently, one might ask whether introducing the
COBRA
decomposition given in Eq. (
1
) could exacerbate these IR divergences. We show that this is generally not the case and that the IR cancellation persists for each symmetrized cross-contribution of the two different basis functions.

We start with the one-loop result. IR divergent contribution of the
P_{22}
diagram comes from two integration regions

\displaystyle P_{22}(k)

\displaystyle\subset 2\int_{\bm{p}}\left[F_{2}(\bm{p},\bm{k}-\bm{p})\right]^{2}v_{i}(\bm{p})v_{j}(\bm{k}-\bm{p})

(38)

\displaystyle=\int_{p<|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|}[\,\dots]+\int_{p\geq|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|}[\,\dots]\,,

which, as
p\to 0
, give the two asymptotic behaviors, one proportional to
(k^{2}/p^{2})v_{i}(k)v_{j}(p)
and the other to
(k^{2}/p^{2})v_{i}(p)v_{j}(k)
.
Note that symmetrizing this contribution in the
(i,j)
indices yields an equivalent result.
The two contributions above are exactly cancelled by the
P_{13}
diagrams, where we have

\displaystyle P_{13}+P_{31}

\displaystyle\subset 3\int F_{3}(\bm{k},\bm{p},-\bm{p})\Big(v_{i}(k)v_{j}(p)+v_{i}(p)v_{j}(k)\Big)\,.

(39)

Since this cancellation occurs for each
(i,j)
pair, it also holds for the full sum. Crucially, the cancellation must be preserved at the level of each pair, not only after summing over the coefficients
w_{i}
in, e.g. Eq. (
2
). This ensures there are no large cancellations between various terms in the
S^{q}_{ij}
tensor and allows the same numerical setup used for the full
P_{L}^{\Theta}
to be applied when precomputing the components of the
S^{q}_{ij}
tensor. This property can also be made manifest in the resulting integrals, by using the multilinearity of the
S_{ij}
tensor as described in Sec.
A
. By expressing the cross-terms of the
S_{ij}
tensor as a linear combination of several auto-correlations we obtain the form that is explicitly IR safe in each term.

Similar analyses can be carried out for the two-loop power spectrum. As discussed in refs.
Carrasco et al. 2014b
;
Fasiello et al. 2022
, the IR cancellation occurs separately between the part of the
P_{24}
and
P_{33,{\rm II}}
contributions, as well as between the remaining parts of
P_{24}
,
P_{33,{\rm II}}
, and
P_{15}
. The only difference is that, when remapping the integration regions, some IR-divergent regions cannot be combined and must be treated separately, because exchanging
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
does not yield the same contribution. This feature already appears in the one-loop result, as shown explicitly above. Importantly, the cancellation again occurs at the level of each
(i,j,k)
triplet contributing to the sum in the last term of Eq. (
3
). This ensures that, for fixed accuracy, the contributions to the
\mathcal{S}^{c}_{ijk}
tensor can be precomputed using the same numerical setup as for the original linear power spectrum
P_{L}^{\Theta}
. Moreover, as was also the case for one-loop, the multilinearity of the
S_{ijk}
tensor can again be employed to recast the cross-terms as a linear combination of auto-correlations as shown in Sec.
A
. This again renders the result manifestly IR-safe.

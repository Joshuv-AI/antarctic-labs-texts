A priori bounds and equicontinuity of orbits for the intermediate long wave equation
B. Harrop-Griffiths, R. Killip, and M. Vişan

Abstract
. We prove uniform-in-time a priori H s H^{s} bounds for solutions to the intermediate long wave equation posed both on the line and on the circle, covering the range − 1 2 < s ≤ 0 -\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 0 . Additionally, we prove that the set of orbits emanating from a bounded and equicontinuous set in H s H^{s} is also bounded and equicontinuous in H s H^{s} . Our proof is based on the identification of a suitable Lax pair formulation for the intermediate long wave equation.



1.
Introduction

The intermediate long wave equation describes the propagation of internal waves at the interface of a stratified fluid of total depth
h>0
. It takes the form

(ILW)

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}q=-Tq^{\prime\prime}-\tfrac{1}{h}q^{\prime}-2qq^{\prime},

with
T
denoting the Fourier multiplier operator with symbol

(1)

\widehat{Tf}(\xi)=i\coth(h\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

The unknown function
q:{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{M}\to{\mathbb{R}}
represents the elevation of the interface relative to equilibrium. In this paper, we treat the cases
\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
on equal footing.

The equation (
ILW
) can be viewed as an ‘intermediate’ equation between the Korteweg–de Vries equation (which arises in the shallow water limit
h\to 0
),

(KdV)

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}q=-q^{\prime\prime\prime}-2qq^{\prime},

and the Benjamin–Ono equation (which arises in the deep water limit
h\to\infty
),

(BO)

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}q=\mathsf{H}q^{\prime\prime}-2qq^{\prime}.

Here
\mathsf{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform,
\widehat{\mathsf{H}f}(\xi)=-i\sgn(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)
.

Smooth solutions to (
ILW
) conserve the momentum,

(2)

M(q)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int q^{2}(t,x)\,dx,

and the Hamiltonian,

(3)

H(q)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int q(t,x)(Tq^{\prime})(t,x)\,dx+\tfrac{1}{2h}\int q^{2}(t,x)\,dx+\tfrac{1}{3}\int q^{3}(t,x)\,dx.

The central goal of this paper is to prove both a priori norm bounds and equicontinuity of orbits for solutions to (
ILW
). Evidently, this is predicated on the existence of solutions. For this reason, we will now give a brief account of the existing results on the well-posedness problem for this model. For a more comprehensive discussion of (
ILW
), we recommend the recent book
[
23
]
.

Global well-posedness of (
ILW
) in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
for
s>\frac{3}{2}
was proven in both geometries already in
[
1
]
. This guarantees that smooth initial data leads to global smooth solutions. Here and throughout the paper, smooth means
H^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

In
[
31
]
, Molinet and Vento proved global well-posedness of (
ILW
) in
H^{1/2}(\mathcal{M})
via the energy method. In
[
29
]
, Molinet, Pilod, and Vento combined the energy method in
[
31
]
with Strichartz estimates and proved local well-posedness of (
ILW
) in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s>\frac{1}{4}
.

By treating (
ILW
) as a perturbation of the Benjamin–Ono equation and employing ideas from
[
15
]
, Ifrim and Saut
[
14
]
proved global well-posedness for (
ILW
) in
L^{2}(\mathcal{{\mathbb{R}}})
. Chapouto, Li, Oh, and Pilod
[
4
]
proved global well-posedness for (
ILW
) in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
, treating both geometries in a uniform manner. They likewise treated (
ILW
) as a perturbation of Benjamin–Ono, employing the gauge transform of Tao and ideas from
[
28
]
. Using a similar perturbative approach, but this time relying on Birkhoff coordinates, Gassot and Laurens
[
8
]
very recently proved global well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
for all
s>-\frac{1}{2}
. This result is most likely sharp in the scale of Sobolev spaces: It was proved in
[
3
]
that (
ILW
) is ill-posed in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
for all
s<-\frac{1}{2}
and in
[
9
]
that (
BO
) is ill-posed in
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{T}})
.

Chapouto, Forlano, Li, Oh, and Pilod
[
3
]
obtained growth bounds for smooth solutions to (
ILW
) in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
for
-\frac{1}{2}<s<0
. They achieved this by treating (
ILW
) as a perturbation of (
BO
) and employing the uniform-in-time a priori bounds for solutions to (
BO
) proved in
[
17
]
. Specifically, they showed that for all
-\frac{1}{2}<s<0
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exist constants
A_{h,s,\varepsilon}\simeq h^{-2}(1+h^{-(\frac{1}{2}+|s|+\varepsilon)})
and
C_{s}>0
, so that smooth solutions to (
ILW
) satisfy

(4)

\displaystyle\|q(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C_{s}^{1+|s|}e^{A_{h,s,\varepsilon}|t|}\bigl(1+2C_{s}e^{A_{h,s,\varepsilon}|t|}\|q(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigr)^{\frac{2|s|}{1-2|s|}}\|q(0)\|_{H^{s}}

for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Our first result in this paper is an improvement over (
4
) in the form of
uniform-in-time
a priori bounds for smooth solutions to (
ILW
). Moreover, we prove for the first time that the set of orbits emanating from a bounded and equicontinuous set in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
is also bounded and equicontinuous in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
0<h_{0}<\infty
and
-\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 0
. For any smooth solution
q
to (
ILW
), we have

(5)

\displaystyle\|q(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigl(1+\|q(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigr)^{-2|s|}\lesssim\|q(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|q(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigl(1+\|q(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigr)^{\frac{2|s|}{1-2|s|}},

uniformly for all
h\geq h_{0}
.

Moreover, if
Q\subset\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is bounded and equicontinuous in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
and
Q_{h}^{*}
denotes the set of orbits emanating from
Q
under the (
ILW
) flow, the set
\bigcup_{h\geq h_{0}}Q_{h}^{*}
forms an equicontinuous set in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
.

We recall that the equation (
ILW
) converges to (
BO
) in the deep water limit
h\to\infty
. This convergence was proved to hold locally in time for solutions in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
when
s\geq 0
in
[
4
]
and in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
when
s>-\frac{1}{2}
in
[
8
]
; see also
[
1
,
7
,
26
]
. Theorem
1.1
proves that both a priori bounds and preservation of equicontinuity hold uniformly in time in the deep water limit, providing strong evidence that (
ILW
) should converge locally in time to (
BO
) as
h\to\infty
also in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for all
s>-\frac{1}{2}
.

To address the shallow water regime
h\to 0
, if
q
is a solution of (
ILW
) we let

u(t,x):=\tfrac{3}{h}q\bigl(\tfrac{3t}{h},x\bigr).

Then
u
is a solution of

(ILW
′
)

\tfrac{d}{dt}u=-\tfrac{3}{h}Tu^{\prime\prime}-\tfrac{3}{h^{2}}u^{\prime}-2uu^{\prime},

which formally converges to (
KdV
) in the shallow water limit
h\to 0
. The validity of this limit was proved locally in time in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
for all
s>\frac{1}{2}
in
[
7
]
, improving the previous result of
[
1
]
. We also mention that convergence of the Gibbs measures in both the deep and shallow water limits were considered in
[
27
]
.

Our second result establishes an analog of Theorem
1.1
that holds uniformly in the shallow water limit:

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
-\frac{1}{2}<s<0
and
K>0
. Then, for any smooth solution
u
to (
ILW′
) satisfying
\|u(0)\|_{H^{s}}\leq K
there exists
h_{0}=h_{0}(s,K)
so that

(6)

\displaystyle\|u(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigl(1+\|u(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigr)^{-\frac{2|s|}{\frac{3}{2}+|s|}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigl(1+\|u(0)\|_{H^{s}}\bigr)^{\frac{2|s|}{\frac{3}{2}-|s|}},

uniformly for all
0<h\leq h_{0}
.

Moreover, if
Q\subset\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is bounded and equicontinuous in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
so that
\|u\|_{H^{s}}\leq K
for all
u\in Q
and
Q_{h}^{*}
denotes the set of orbits emanating from
Q
under the (
ILW′
) flow, the set
\bigcup_{0<h\leq h_{0}}Q_{h}^{*}
is equicontinuous in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
.

The complete integrability of (
ILW
) has been known for some time. As well as constructing an infinite family of conservation laws, the paper
[
33
]
introduced a zero-curvature representation. Concretely, (
ILW
) was shown to be the consistency condition for a coupled system of equations describing the spatial and temporal derivatives of a pair of functions, namely, the upper and lower boundary values of a function holomorphic in a strip.
The subsequent paper
[
25
]
then proposed an inverse scattering approach based on this representation.

This complicated formulation is at odds with the direct approach of
[
20
]
that we wish to employ. Instead, we would like a Lax pair representation of a much more classical form: an isospectral deformation of an operator (ideally self-adjoint) acting on a concrete Hilbert space. Taking inspiration from
[
25
,
33
]
, we introduce just such a traditional Lax representation: Defining

(7)

\displaystyle L(t)

\displaystyle:=-i\partial_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{2ih\partial_{x}}-1)-q(t,x),

\displaystyle P(t)

\displaystyle:=-\tfrac{1}{h}\partial_{x}-i\partial_{x}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{h}\partial_{x}e^{2ih\partial_{x}}-(iTq^{\prime})(t,x)-(\partial_{x}q(t,x)+q(t,x)\partial_{x}),

we have that

(8)

\displaystyle\text{$q(t)$ solves \eqref{ILW}}\iff\tfrac{d}{dt}L(t)=[P(t),\,L(t)].

The validity of (
8
) will be verified in Section
3
. There we will also explain why
L
is self-adjoint, even for
q\in H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
with
-\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 0
. Although it is merely incidental to our main development, we will also exhibit an illusory Lax pair for (
ILW
) and a number of related models in Lemma
3.1
.

The free Lax operator (that corresponds to
q\equiv 0
) is simply the Fourier multiplier with symbol

(9)

a_{h}(\xi)=\xi+\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{-2h\xi}-1).

This function is non-negative.
In particular, the free resolvent,

R_{0}(\kappa):=\bigl(-i\partial_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{2ih\partial_{x}}-1)+\kappa\bigr)^{-1},

exists as a positive definite
L^{2}
-bounded operator for all positive
\kappa
.

Mirroring the treatment of KdV in
[
20
]
, the central object of our analysis is the logarithm of the renormalized (in the sense of
[
13
]
) perturbation determinant; however, as there, it is more convenient to introduce this quantity directly as a series of traces:

(10)

\displaystyle\alpha(\kappa;q):=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell}\tr\Bigl\{\bigl(\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\bigr)^{\ell}\Bigr\}.

The convergence of this series for
\kappa
large follows from Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
. We prove that (
10
) is conserved under the (
ILW
) flow in Proposition
4.1
. It then remains to show that this quantity encodes conservation laws and equicontinuity at regularities
-\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 0
. The central idea is that for
\kappa
very large, the series (
10
) can be conflated with its first term. This in turn connects to the distribution of
q
across frequencies; see (
15
). The rather inexplicit form of the function
F(\xi;\kappa)
appearing there introduces some technical challenges, particularly for the question of
L^{2}
-equicontinuity; see Lemma
4.2
.

The technology for demonstrating equicontinuity of orbits was present already in
[
20
]
(also see
[
24
]
); however, this was neither appreciated nor exploited until
[
21
]
. The utility of such equicontinuity results in treating the well-posedness problem has since been repeatedly demonstrated; see, for example,
[
2
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
21
]
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

We write
A\lesssim B
to indicate
A\leq CB
for an absolute constant
C>0
whose specific value is not important. Occasionally, we use subscripts to indicate dependence of the constant
C
on other parameters; for instance, we write
A\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}B
when
A\leq CB
with the constant
C>0
depending on
\alpha,\beta
. We write
A\simeq B
when
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

Our conventions for the Fourier transform are as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}f(x)\,dx\quad\text{so}\quad f(x)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\xi x}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi

for functions on the line
{\mathbb{R}}
and

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-i\xi x}f(x)\,dx\quad\text{so}\quad f(x)=\sum_{\xi\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\xi x}

for functions on the circle
{\mathbb{T}}
. These definitions of the Fourier transform are unitary on
L^{2}
and yield the Plancherel identities

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}=\sum_{\xi\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2},

as well as the following convolution identity on
{\mathbb{R}}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{fg}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\widehat{f}\ast\widehat{g}.

For
\kappa\geq 1
, we define the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}_{\kappa}
via the norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\kappa^{2}+\xi^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{T}})}^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}(\kappa^{2}+\xi^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}.

Definition 2.1

(Equicontinuity)
.

Fix
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
. A bounded set
Q\subset H^{\sigma}(\mathcal{M})
is said to be
equicontinuous
if

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\ \sup_{q\in Q}\ \sup_{|y|<\delta}\|q(\cdot+y)-q(\cdot)\|_{H^{\sigma}}=0.

By Plancherel, equicontinuity in the spatial variable is equivalent to tightness in the Fourier variable. Specifically, a bounded set
Q\subset H^{\sigma}(\mathcal{M})
is equicontinuous if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\sup_{q\in Q}\int_{|\xi|\geq\kappa}

\displaystyle|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}(|\xi|+1)^{2\sigma}\,d\xi=0,

when
\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{R}}
,

or

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\,\sup_{q\in Q}\ \sum_{|\xi|\geq\kappa}\

\displaystyle|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}(|\xi|+1)^{2\sigma}=0,

\displaystyle\text{when $\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{T}}$}.

This characterization leads quickly to a description of equicontinuity in terms of the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}_{\kappa}
:

Lemma 2.2

(Characterization of equicontinuity,
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Fix
-\frac{1}{2}<s<0
and let
Q
be a bounded subset of
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
. The following are equivalent:

(i) The subset
Q
is equicontinuous in
H^{s}
.

(ii)
\|q\|_{H^{s}_{\kappa}}\to 0
as
\kappa\to\infty
uniformly for
q\in Q
.

The book
[
34
]
provides an excellent introduction to the trace functional on Hilbert space and the associated ideals
{\mathfrak{I}}_{p}
, which play the role of noncommutative
L^{p}
spaces. Here we just give a brief account of the facts we need.

For an operator
A
belonging to trace class and having a continuous integral kernel
K(x,y)
, the trace may be computed via

(11)

\tr(A)=\int K(x,x)\,dx.

Trace class operators are precisely those that can be written as the product of two Hilbert–Schmidt operators. An operator
A
is Hilbert–Schmidt if and only if its integral kernel
K(x,y)
belongs to
L^{2}(\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M})
; indeed, its Hilbert–Schmidt norm is then given by

(12)

\|A\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}=\tr(A^{*}A)=\iint|K(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy.

For much of what follows, we will need only the following basic assertions. As illustrated in
[
20
]
, these can be verified directly, without recourse to the general theory.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
20
, Lemma 1.4]
)
.

Let
A_{i}
denote Hilbert–Schmidt operators on
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Then

(13)

\displaystyle\|A_{i}\|_{\textit{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{i}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}

(14)

\displaystyle|\tr(A_{1}\cdots A_{\ell})|

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\|A_{i}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}\quad\text{for all integers $\ell\geq 2$.}

In order to verify the convergence of (
10
) using (
14
), we need a suitable Hilbert–Schmidt estimate. This is the topic of our next lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
h,\kappa>0
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle\bigl\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\bigr\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F(\xi;\kappa,h)\,|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi&\quad\text{if $\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{R}}$,}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}F(\xi;\kappa,h)\,|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}&\quad\text{if $\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{T}}$,}\end{cases}

where

(16)

\displaystyle F(\xi;\kappa,h)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\ \int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\bigl[a_{h}(\eta)+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[a_{h}(\xi+\eta)+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,\tfrac{d\eta}{2\pi}&\quad\text{on ${\mathbb{R}}$,}\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{\eta\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigl[a_{h}(\eta)+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[a_{h}(\xi+\eta)+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}&\quad\text{on ${\mathbb{T}}$,}\end{cases}

with
a_{h}(\xi)
defined as in (
9
).

The function
F(\xi;\kappa,h)
is smooth, non-negative, even in
\xi
, and satisfies the estimate

(17)

F(\xi;\kappa,h)\simeq\bigl[\tfrac{h\xi^{2}}{1+h|\xi|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\Bigl[\sqrt{\tfrac{1+h\kappa}{h\kappa}}+\log\bigl(1+\tfrac{h|\xi|}{1+h\kappa}\bigr)\Bigr].

In particular, if
-\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 0
then

(18)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\lesssim_{s}\begin{cases}\kappa^{-(1-2|s|)}\|q\|_{H^{s}_{\kappa}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}&\quad\text{if $\kappa>\tfrac{1}{h}$,}\\
\bigl(\tfrac{\kappa}{h}\bigr)^{-(\frac{3}{2}-|s|)}\bigl\|\tfrac{q}{h}\bigr\|_{H_{\sqrt{\kappa/h}}^{s}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}&\quad\text{if $0<\kappa\leq\tfrac{1}{h}$.}\end{cases}

Consequently, there exists a constant
C_{s}>0
so that if
h,\delta>0
and

(19)

\kappa\geq\max\Bigl\{\tfrac{1}{h},\bigl[1+\tfrac{1}{\delta^{2}}C_{s}\|q\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{1-2|s|}}\Bigr\}

or

(20)

\bigl[1+\tfrac{1}{\delta^{2}}C_{s}\bigl\|\tfrac{q}{h}\bigr\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\bigr]^{\frac{1}{\frac{3}{2}-|s|}}\leq\tfrac{\kappa}{h}\leq\tfrac{1}{h^{2}}

then

(21)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}<\delta^{2}.

Proof.

The identities (
15
) and (
16
) follow from considering the Fourier kernel of the operator
\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}
.

Making the change of variables
\eta\mapsto\eta-\xi
in (
16
) proves that the map
\xi\mapsto F(\xi;\kappa,h)
is even. That
F
is also smooth and non-negative follows from the fact that the function
a_{h}
is smooth and non-negative.

The estimate (
18
) follows readily from (
17
), while (
21
) follows from (
18
) under the assumptions (
19
) and (
20
). Thus, it remains to estimate of the size of
F(\xi;\kappa,h)
. We present the details on the real line; the periodic setting can be treated analogously, with integrals being replaced by sums. Further, as
F
is even in
\xi
and
|\widehat{q}(\xi)|=|\widehat{q}(-\xi)|
(since
q
is real-valued), it suffices to consider the case that
\xi\geq 0
.

Denoting
a(\xi)=a_{1}(\xi)=\xi+\tfrac{1}{2}(e^{-2\xi}-1)
, we compute that
a^{\prime}(\xi)=1-e^{-2\xi}
and
a^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=2e^{-2\xi}>0
, so
a(\xi)
is convex with a global minimum at
\xi=0
. By considering the asymptotic behavior of
a(\xi)
as
\xi\to 0
and
\xi\to\pm\infty
, we see that

(22)

a(\xi)\gtrsim\tfrac{\xi^{2}}{1+|\xi|}\quad\text{for all $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}$}\quad\text{and}\quad a(\xi)\simeq\tfrac{\xi^{2}}{1+\xi}\quad\text{for all $\xi\geq 0$.}

As
a_{h}(\xi)=\frac{1}{h}a(h\xi)
, the estimate (
22
) yields the (uniform in
h
) upper bound

\displaystyle F(\xi;\kappa,h)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[\tfrac{h(\xi+\eta)^{2}}{1+h|\xi+\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta

(23)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[\tfrac{h(\xi+\eta)^{2}}{1+h|\xi+\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\frac{1}{2}\xi}^{\infty}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[\tfrac{h(\xi+\eta)^{2}}{1+h|\xi+\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta,

where the second inequality follows from the fact that the map

\eta\mapsto\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[\tfrac{h(\xi+\eta)^{2}}{1+h|\xi+\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}

is symmetric about
\eta=-\frac{1}{2}\xi
.

For the first summand on RHS(
23
), we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[\tfrac{h(\xi+\eta)^{2}}{1+h|\xi+\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta\simeq\bigl[\tfrac{h\xi^{2}}{1+h|\xi|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta.

We then consider several cases depending on the relative size of
\xi,\kappa,h
.

First, suppose that
\kappa>\frac{1}{h}
. If additionally
0\leq\xi\leq\kappa
, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\,d\eta\simeq\tfrac{\xi}{\kappa}\lesssim 1,

whereas if
\xi>\kappa
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta\simeq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\kappa}\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\,d\eta+\int_{\frac{1}{2}\kappa}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\tfrac{1}{\eta}\,d\eta\simeq 1+\log\bigl(\tfrac{\xi}{\kappa}\bigr).

Second, suppose that
0<\kappa\leq\frac{1}{h}
. If
0\leq\xi\leq\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}
then

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta\simeq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\,d\eta\simeq\tfrac{\xi}{\kappa}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{h\kappa}},

if
\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}<\xi\leq\frac{1}{h}
then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}}\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\,d\eta+\int_{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\tfrac{1}{h\eta^{2}}\,d\eta\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{h\kappa}},

and if
\xi>\frac{1}{h}
then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}}\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\,d\eta+\int_{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}}^{\frac{1}{2h}}\tfrac{1}{h\eta^{2}}\,d\eta+\int_{\frac{1}{2h}}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\tfrac{1}{\eta}\,d\eta\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{h\kappa}}+\log(h\xi).

Putting these cases together, we arrive at the estimate

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta\lesssim\sqrt{\tfrac{1+h\kappa}{h\kappa}}+\log\bigl(1+\tfrac{h\xi}{1+h\kappa}\bigr),

which yields an acceptable upper bound for the first summand on RHS(
23
). We also note that these estimates prove that

(24)

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\max\{\xi,\kappa,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}\}}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta\simeq\sqrt{\tfrac{1+h\kappa}{h\kappa}}+\log\bigl(1+\tfrac{h\xi}{1+h\kappa}\bigr),

which we will use in our proof of the lower bound below.

Turning to the second summand on RHS(
23
), we estimate

\int_{\frac{1}{2}\xi}^{\infty}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[\tfrac{h(\xi+\eta)^{2}}{1+h|\xi+\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta\simeq\int_{\frac{1}{2}\xi}^{\infty}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-2}\,d\eta.

We proceed as for the first summand: When
\kappa>\frac{1}{h}
, we consider the cases
\xi>\kappa
and
0\leq\xi\leq\kappa
to obtain the estimate

\int_{\frac{1}{2}\xi}^{\infty}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-2}\,d\eta\lesssim\bigl[\tfrac{h\xi^{2}}{1+h\xi}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\Bigl[\sqrt{\tfrac{1+h\kappa}{h\kappa}}+\log\bigl(1+\tfrac{h\xi}{1+h\kappa}\bigr)\Bigr].

Similarly, when
0<\kappa\leq\frac{1}{h}
we consider the cases
\xi>\frac{1}{h}
,
\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}<\xi\leq\frac{1}{h}
, and
0\leq\xi\leq\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}
to bound

\int_{\frac{1}{2}\xi}^{\infty}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-2}\,d\eta\lesssim\bigl[\tfrac{h\xi^{2}}{1+h\xi}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\Bigl[\sqrt{\tfrac{1+h\kappa}{h\kappa}}+\log\bigl(1+\tfrac{h\xi}{1+h\kappa}\bigr)\Bigr].

Combining our estimates for the two summands on RHS(
23
) and recalling that
F
is even in
\xi
gives us the upper bound in (
17
).

Turning to the lower bound in (
17
), we take
\xi\geq 0
and combine (
16
) and (
22
) to obtain

\displaystyle F(\xi;\kappa,h)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\max\{\xi,\kappa,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}\}}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\bigl[\tfrac{h(\xi+\eta)^{2}}{1+h(\xi+\eta)}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\gtrsim\bigl[\tfrac{h\xi^{2}}{1+h\xi}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}\max\{\xi,\kappa,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{h}}\}}\bigl[\tfrac{h\eta^{2}}{1+h|\eta|}+\kappa\bigr]^{-1}\,d\eta.

Applying (
24
) and again using that
F
is even in
\xi
completes the proof of (
17
).
∎

Lemma
2.4
guarantees convergence of the series defining
\alpha(\kappa;q(t))
whenever
\kappa>0
is sufficiently large depending on
\|q(t)\|_{H^{s}},h,s
. To ensure that
\kappa
can be chosen uniformly in time, we will employ a continuity argument, the basis of which is formalized in the next lemma:

Lemma 2.5

(
[
20
, Lemma 1.5]
)
.

Let
t\mapsto A(t)
define a
C^{1}
curve in
\mathfrak{I}_{2}
. Suppose

\displaystyle\bigl\|A(t_{0})\bigr\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}<\tfrac{1}{3}.

Then there is a closed neighborhood
I
of
t_{0}
on which the series

\displaystyle\alpha(t):=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{\ell}\tr\bigl\{A(t)^{\ell}\bigr\}

converges and defines a
C^{1}
function with

\displaystyle\tfrac{d\ }{dt}\alpha(t):=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\tr\bigl\{A(t)^{\ell-1}\tfrac{d\ }{dt}A(t)\bigr\}.

Moreover, if
A(t)
is self-adjoint, then

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{3}\|A(t)\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}\leq\alpha(t)\leq\tfrac{2}{3}\|A(t)\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}\quad\text{for all $t\in I$.}

3.
Verification of the Lax pair

Suppose
q\equiv 0
. Fourier analysis easily allows us to interpret the free Lax operator
L_{0}=-i\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2h}(e^{2ih\partial_{x}}-1)
as a self-adjoint operator that is bounded below. In particular, the quadratic form domain of this operator is comprised of those
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
with

(25)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl[\xi+\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{-2h\xi}-1)+1\bigr]|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle<\infty\quad\text{or}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigl[\xi+\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{-2h\xi}-1)+1\bigr]|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle<\infty,

depending on the geometry. Now by (
18
), any
q\in H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
with
-\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 0
is an infinitesimally form bounded perturbation of
L_{0}
. Consequently, by the KLMN Theorem, the Lax operator
L=-i\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2h}(e^{2ih\partial_{x}}-1)-q
is self-adjoint, bounded from below, and has the same form domain as
L_{0}
; see
[
32
]
for further discussion.

The (
KdV
), (
BO
), and (
ILW
) equations are all specific examples of the more general Whitham equation, which combines the advection nonlinearity with a completely general linear dispersion:

(26)

\tfrac{d}{dt}q=-Mq^{\prime}-cq^{\prime}-2qq^{\prime}.

Here
M
is the Fourier multiplier operator
\widehat{Mf}(\xi):=m(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)
, associated to a symbol
m:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant. The background speed
c
could be incorporated into the symbol
m
or removed by the Galilei transformation
q\mapsto q-c/2
; however, we choose to keep it separate as in (
ILW
).

As an offshoot of our calculations verifying the Lax pair formulation (
8
), we have discovered a Lax pair representation of (
26
) that we present in the next lemma. We regard this Lax pair representation as illusory because the conjugacy class of the Lax operator is completely independent of
q
. Moreover, the renormalized perturbation determinant is always unity because the relevant integral operator is of Volterra type.

Lemma 3.1

(Illusory Lax pair)
.

For a smooth trajectory
q:{\mathbb{R}}\to H^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, let

(27)

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}(t)

\displaystyle:=-i\partial_{x}-q(t),

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(t)

\displaystyle:=-c\partial_{x}-i(Mq(t))-i\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{x}q(t)-q(t)\partial_{x},

where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
M
is a general Fourier multiplier. Then

(28)

\displaystyle\text{$q(t)$ solves \eqref{BEQ}}\iff\tfrac{d}{dt}\widetilde{L}(t)=[\widetilde{P}(t),\,\widetilde{L}(t)].

Proof.

We simply compute:

\displaystyle[\widetilde{P},\,\widetilde{L}]

\displaystyle=cq^{\prime}+Mq^{\prime}+i[\partial_{x}^{2},q]+i[\partial_{x}q+q\partial_{x},\partial_{x}]+[\partial_{x}q+q\partial_{x},q]

\displaystyle=cq^{\prime}+Mq^{\prime}+2qq^{\prime}.

The operators on this last line are those of multiplication by the given functions. The claim (
28
) follows immediately.
∎

The claim in Lemma
3.1
remains valid if
\widetilde{P}
is replaced by the simpler operator

\widetilde{P}(t)-i\tilde{L}^{2}=-c\partial_{x}-i(Mq(t))-iq(t)^{2};

however, this would take us further from our main goal, namely, verifying (
8
).

We now turn to verifying that (
7
) is indeed a valid Lax pair for (
ILW
). By contrast with (
27
), the Lax pair (
7
) does provide meaningful conserved quantities for (
ILW
). In fact, the next section is devoted to showing that these conserved quantities suffice to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For a smooth trajectory
q:{\mathbb{R}}\to H^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,

(29)

\displaystyle\text{$q(t)$ solves \eqref{ILW}}\iff\tfrac{d}{dt}L(t)=[P(t),\,L(t)].

Proof.

Recall that
L
and
P
are defined in (
7
), and the Fourier multiplier
T
is defined in (
1
).

Applying Lemma
3.1
with
c=\frac{1}{h}
and
M=T\partial_{x}
, we see that

\text{$q(t)$ solves \eqref{ILW}}\iff\tfrac{d}{dt}L(t)=[P(t)-\tfrac{1}{h}\partial_{x}e^{2ih\partial_{x}},\,L(t)-\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{2ih\partial_{x}}-1)].

Thus, it remains to demonstrate the vanishing of

\displaystyle-[P,\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial_{x}}]-[\tfrac{1}{h}\partial_{x}e^{2ih\partial_{x}},\,L]=[i(Tq^{\prime})+\partial_{x}q+q\partial_{x},\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial_{x}}]+[\tfrac{1}{h}\partial_{x}e^{2ih\partial_{x}},\,q].

Let us consider this operator in Fourier variables. On the line, we obtain an integral kernel that we write as
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}K(\xi,\eta)
; on the circle, this is a matrix
K(\xi,\eta)
indexed by
\xi,\eta\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
. In either case,

\displaystyle K(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=i(\xi-\eta)\coth\bigl(h[\xi-\eta]\bigr)\tfrac{1}{2h}\bigl[e^{-2h\xi}-e^{-2h\eta}\bigr]\widehat{q}(\xi-\eta)

\displaystyle\quad-(i\xi+i\eta)\tfrac{1}{2h}\bigl[e^{-2h\xi}-e^{-2h\eta}\bigr]\widehat{q}(\xi-\eta)

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{1}{h}\bigl[i\xi e^{-2h\xi}-i\eta e^{-2h\eta}\bigr]\widehat{q}(\xi-\eta).

Using the identity

\displaystyle\coth\bigl(h[\xi-\eta]\bigr)\bigl[e^{-2h\xi}-e^{-2h\eta}\bigr]=-\bigl[e^{-2h\xi}+e^{-2h\eta}\bigr],

one readily checks that
K(\xi,\eta)\equiv 0
.
∎

4.
A priori bounds and equicontinuity

Our first task is to demonstrate the conservation of
\alpha(\kappa;q(t))
under (
ILW
):

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
q(t)
be a smooth solution to (
ILW
) and
\kappa>0
be chosen so that

(30)

\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q(0)\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}<\tfrac{1}{36}.

Then for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\frac{d\ }{dt}\alpha(\kappa;q(t))=0.

Consequently,

(31)

\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q(t)\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}\simeq\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q(0)\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
I
be the largest interval containing
0
for which

\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q(t)\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}<\tfrac{1}{9}\quad\text{for all $t\in I$}.

The interval
I
is readily seen to be non-empty by the hypothesis (
30
) and open by continuity of the map
t\mapsto\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q(t)\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}
. Applying Lemma
2.5
, we see that if
\alpha(\kappa;q(t))
is constant on
I
then for all
t\in I
we have

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q(t)\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}\leq 3\alpha(\kappa;q(t))

\displaystyle=3\alpha(\kappa;q(0))

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q(0)\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}<\tfrac{1}{18},

which suffices to prove that the interval
I
is closed, and so comprises all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

It remains to prove that
\alpha(\kappa;q(t))
is constant on
I
. Applying Lemma
2.5
, for
t\in I
we have

(32)

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}\alpha(\kappa;q(t))=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{d}{dt}q(t)\,\bigl(R_{0}q(t)\bigr)^{\ell-1}\Bigr\}.

Here, we have used that
R_{0}\frac{d}{dt}q,R_{0}q\in{\mathfrak{I}}_{2}
to cycle the trace. This is a consequence of
[
34
, Theorem 4.1]
together with the following facts: (i) as
q
is a smooth solution of (
ILW
), we have
q,\frac{d}{dt}q\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
and (ii) by Lemma
2.4
, the symbol
\frac{1}{a_{h}(\xi)+\kappa}
of
R_{0}(\kappa)
satisfies

\tfrac{1}{a_{h}(\xi)+\kappa}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{\frac{h\xi^{2}}{1+h|\xi|}+\kappa},

which lies in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
in the case
\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{R}}
and in
\ell^{2}(2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})
in the case
\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{T}}
.

Employing the equation (
ILW
) satisfied by
q
and collecting like powers of
q
, we see that it suffices to show the following:

(33)

\displaystyle\tr\bigl\{R_{0}(Tq^{\prime\prime})R_{0}\,q\bigr\}+\tr\bigl\{R_{0}\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{h}R_{0}\,q\bigr\}

\displaystyle=0,

(34)

\displaystyle\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\bigl(Tq^{\prime\prime}+\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{h}\bigr)\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}+\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\bigl(2qq^{\prime}\bigr)\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l-1}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\ell\geq 2.

We recall from Lemma
2.4
that the function
\xi\mapsto F(\xi;\kappa,h)
defined in (
16
) is even. As we will now show, this is the key observation for proving (
33
). For concreteness, we provide the details in the line setting. The circle case is a close parallel; one simply replaces integrals by sums.

A straightforward computation reveals that

\displaystyle\tr\bigl\{R_{0}(Tq^{\prime\prime})R_{0}\,q\bigr\}+\tr\bigl\{R_{0}\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{h}R_{0}\,q\bigr\}=\tfrac{1}{ih}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\xi;\kappa,h)|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\bigl[h\xi^{2}\coth(h\xi)-\xi\bigr]\,d\xi.

As
q
is real-valued, the function
\xi\mapsto|\widehat{q}(\xi)|^{2}
is even. However,
\xi\mapsto h\xi^{2}\coth(h\xi)-\xi
is clearly an odd function. Thus (
33
) follows from the fact that
\xi\mapsto F(\xi;\kappa,h)
is even.

We now turn to (
34
). Writing
q^{\prime}=[\partial,q]
, observing that
[\partial,R_{0}]=0
, and cycling the trace, we deduce

(35)

\displaystyle\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\bigl(\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{h}\bigr)\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{h}\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\partial\,q\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}-\tfrac{1}{h}\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,q\,\partial\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}=0.

To continue, we use the rules

(36)

\displaystyle e^{2ih\partial}(gf)

\displaystyle=(e^{2ih\partial}g)\,(e^{2ih\partial}f),

(37)

\displaystyle(e^{2ih\partial}-1)\,iTg

\displaystyle=(1+e^{2ih\partial})g,

to deduce that

\displaystyle[e^{2ih\partial},\,iTq^{\prime}]f

\displaystyle=\bigl(e^{2ih\partial}-1\bigr)(iTq^{\prime})\cdot e^{2ih\partial}f=(1+e^{2ih\partial})q^{\prime}\cdot e^{2ih\partial}f

\displaystyle=q^{\prime}e^{2ih\partial}f+e^{2ih\partial}(q^{\prime}f).

This then yields the operator identity,

(38)

\displaystyle(Tq^{\prime\prime})f

\displaystyle=[\partial,Tq^{\prime}]f

\displaystyle=\bigl[-i\partial+\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{2ih\partial}-1)+\kappa,\,iTq^{\prime}\bigr]f-\tfrac{1}{2h}[e^{2ih\partial},\,iTq^{\prime}]f

\displaystyle=\bigl[-i\partial+\tfrac{1}{2h}(e^{2ih\partial}-1)+\kappa,\,iTq^{\prime}\bigr]f-\tfrac{1}{2h}q^{\prime}e^{2ih\partial}f-\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial}(q^{\prime}f),

and correspondingly,

\displaystyle\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\bigl(Tq^{\prime\prime}\bigr)\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\tr\Bigl\{\bigl(iTq^{\prime}\bigr)\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\bigl(iTq^{\prime}\bigr)q\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l-1}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}q^{\prime}e^{2ih\partial}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial}q^{\prime}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}.

Using
[q,(iTq^{\prime})]=0
and cycling the trace, we see that the first two summands here cancel. Employing the identity
q^{\prime}=[\partial,q]
and cycling the trace, we then compute as follows:

\displaystyle\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\bigl(Tq^{\prime\prime}\bigr)\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}q^{\prime}e^{2ih\partial}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial}q^{\prime}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}\partial\,q\,e^{2ih\partial}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}+\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}q\,\partial\,e^{2ih\partial}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial}\,\partial\,q\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}+\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial}\,q\,\partial\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\partial\,q\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}+\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\tfrac{1}{2h}e^{2ih\partial}\,q\,\partial\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\partial\,q^{2}\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l-1}\Bigr\}-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,\partial\,q\,(i\partial+\tfrac{1}{2h}-\kappa)\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\tr\Bigl\{q\,\partial\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}+\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,(i\partial+\tfrac{1}{2h}-\kappa)\,q\,\partial\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=-\tr\Bigl\{R_{0}\,[\partial,q^{2}]\bigl(R_{0}q\bigr)^{l-1}\Bigr\}.

Claim (
34
) follows from this, the operator identity
[\partial,q^{2}]=2qq^{\prime}
, and (
35
).
∎

The preliminaries we have gathered so far almost suffice to prove all parts of Theorem
1.1
. There is just one exception, namely, equicontinuity in
L^{2}
. For this result we need to combine the conservation of
\alpha(\kappa;q)
with the conservation of momentum
M(q)
defined in (
2
). Our next lemma makes the connection between such a combination and
L^{2}
equicontinuity.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
h_{0}>0
. The two functions
\xi\mapsto F(\xi;\kappa,h)
defined in (
16
) satisfy

(39)

\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\kappa F(\xi;\kappa,h)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi},

uniformly for
h\geq h_{0}
and
\xi
in compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively
2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Moreover, there are constants
A>0
and
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{1}(h_{0})>0
so that if
h\geq h_{0}
then

(40)

\displaystyle\kappa F(\xi;\kappa,h)\leq\tfrac{1}{4\pi},\quad\text{provided}\quad\kappa\geq\kappa_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi|\geq A\kappa.

Lastly, there is a constant
C=C(h_{0})>0
so that

(41)

\displaystyle\bigl[1+C\kappa^{-1/2}\bigr]M(q)-\pi\kappa\|\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\,q\,\sqrt{R_{0}(\kappa)}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}\gtrsim\|P_{\geq A\kappa}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for all
h\geq h_{0}
and
\kappa\geq\kappa_{1}
. Here
P_{\geq N}
denotes the sharp projection to frequencies
|\xi|\geq N
.

Proof.

We proved in Lemma
2.4
that the map
\xi\mapsto F(\xi;\kappa,h)
is even. This allows us to consider only
\xi\geq 0
for the remainder of the proof.

We decompose
F(\xi;\kappa,h)=F_{+}(\xi;\kappa,h)+F_{-}(\xi;\kappa,h)
by restricting the sum/integral defining
F
to
\eta\geq 0
and
\eta<0
, respectively. We will treat these pieces separately. We begin with the former, introducing the notations

\displaystyle f(\eta,\xi;\kappa,h)=\tfrac{1}{(a_{h}(\eta)+\kappa)(a_{h}(\eta+\xi)+\kappa)}\quad\text{and}\quad g(\eta,\xi;\varkappa)=\tfrac{1}{(\eta+\varkappa)(\eta+\xi+\varkappa)},

where
a_{h}
is defined as in (
9
).

For
\xi,\eta\geq 0
and
\kappa>\frac{1}{2h}
, we clearly have

(42)

\displaystyle g(\eta,\xi;\kappa)\leq f(\eta,\xi;\kappa,h)\leq g\bigl(\eta,\xi;\kappa-\tfrac{1}{2h}\bigr);

moreover,

(43)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\varkappa\,g(\eta,\xi;\varkappa)\,\tfrac{d\eta}{2\pi}=\tfrac{\varkappa}{2\pi\,\xi}\log\bigl[1+\tfrac{\xi}{\varkappa}\bigr]\longrightarrow\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\quad\text{as}\quad\varkappa\to\infty,

uniformly on compact sets of
\xi
. In order to treat sums, we observe that due to the monotonicity of
\eta\mapsto g(\eta,\xi;\varkappa)
and the fact that
g(0,\xi;\varkappa)=\frac{1}{\varkappa(\xi+\varkappa)}
, we have

(44)

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\eta\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}g(\eta,\xi;\varkappa)-\tfrac{1}{\varkappa(\xi+\varkappa)}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\eta,\xi;\varkappa)\,\tfrac{d\eta}{2\pi}\leq\sum_{0\leq\eta\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}}g(\eta,\xi;\varkappa);

indeed, we are comparing an integral with two Riemann sum approximations.

Combining all these observations yields that

(45)

\displaystyle\kappa F_{+}(\xi;\kappa,h)\longrightarrow\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\quad\text{as}\quad\kappa\to\infty

uniformly for
h\geq h_{0}
and
\xi
in compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}
or
2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover, if
\kappa>\frac{1}{2h}
then in both geometries we may bound

\displaystyle\kappa F_{+}(\xi;\kappa,h)

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\kappa}{(\kappa-\frac{1}{2h})(\xi+\kappa-\frac{1}{2h})}+\tfrac{\kappa}{2\pi\,\xi}\log\bigl[1+\tfrac{\xi}{\kappa-\frac{1}{2h}}\bigr].

In particular, there exists
A>0
so that

(46)

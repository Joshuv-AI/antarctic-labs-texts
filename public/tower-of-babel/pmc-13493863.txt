Design of a drive and control integrated intelligent loom control system based on the FOC algorithm
Authors: Ziyong Zhai, Zhaochong Hou, Xuezheng Yang, Qun Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aiming at the challenges of high energy consumption, low integration, and insufficient stability in the drive systems of ultra-high-speed air-jet looms, a drive and control integrated software/hardware architecture for a “three-in-one” system (main shaft drive motor, warp motor, and take-up motor) is constructed. A multi-axis (main shaft, warp shaft, and take-up shaft) coupled drive model with multiple spatiotemporal characteristics and nonlinear mathematical properties is established. A method for multi-axis electromechanical parameter monitoring and information fusion is proposed. An intelligent drive and control integrated controller is developed to achieve real-time monitoring and diagnosis of the operating status of the multi-axis electromechanical system, as well as vector control and field-weakening control. This enhances the efficiency of the loom drive system and reduces power consumption. In addition, in response to the low efficiency and poor consistency of manual fabric quality inspection in the textile industry, this paper proposes an intelligent fabric quality inspection system based on the improved YOLOv8n algorithm. By constructing a dataset containing seven types of typical fabric defects, the structure of the original model was optimized. Experimental results show that the improved YOLOv8n achieves a mAP_0.5 of 98.4% in fabric defect detection tasks and a reasoning speed of 156 FPS, which is more than 20 times faster than traditional detection methods. Practical deployment tests show that the system can stably identify defects such as broken warp and weft yarns, stains, and loose weft over 0.3mm, with a false detection rate of less than 0.15%, meeting the real-time quality inspection requirements of textile production lines. This research provides an effective technical solution for the intelligence of textile machinery and has been successfully applied to the RFJA36 series looms of Shandong Rifa Textile Machinery Co., Ltd.

Introduction

In recent years, with the rapid development of intelligent manufacturing technology1–5, textile machinery is undergoing a profound transformation from mechanization and electrification to digitalization, networking, and intelligence. Against this backdrop, the intelligent upgrade of the control system of the loom, as the core equipment in textile production, has become an important breakthrough for technological innovation in the industry.

Research institutions both at home and abroad have carried out studies on the three aspects of energy saving and consumption reduction, adaptability to different fabric types, and intelligentization of the new generation of airjet looms, and have achieved certain research results6–9. Shandong Rifa, Zhe jiang Xing yao, Zhejiang Sci-Tech University, and others have conducted research on the vibration characteristics of air-jet looms and optimized the frame structure. However, the correlation mechanism between the vibration characteristics of the loom structure and the loom efficiency is still unclear, and there is a lack of criteria for evaluating the impact of loom vibration on loom performance.Loom efficiency (the ratio of actual output to theoretical output per unit time).The dynamics performance of the reed beating mechanism is the main factor affecting the vibration of the loom, and research institutions at home and abroad have made certain progress in the structural design and dynamic characteristics of the reed beating mechanism10–12.

Compared with foreign high-speed air-jet looms, domestic looms have larger vibrations and energy consumption caused by reed beating. Lightweight structural design of the reed beating mechanism, especially the dynamic balancing design of the rocker shaft, has important engineering application value and economic benefits in reducing loom vibration and energy consumption13,14.

In terms of the optimization of intelligent integrated control systems, European and Japanese companies have developed the next generation of air-jet looms equipped with intelligent control systems that can quickly detect, process at high speed, and respond rapidly15–17. Representative products with intelligent weft insertion systems include: the BlueBox electronic platform of the Picanol OmniPlus-iConnect air-jet loom, the AJC-S+ weft insertion automatic control system of the Tsudakoma ZAX001neo air-jet loom, and the intelligent real-time airway control system of the Shandong Rifa RFJA40 air-jet loom.Representative products with intelligent monitoring systems include: Toyota’s Weaving Support System (WAS) and Tsudakoma’s Weave Navigation System. These systems can monitor loom operation, production, and maintenance through the network. The Shandong Rifa RFJA30 airjet loom is equipped with an intelligent electrical control system based on the Industrial Internet, which realizes re-mote data transmission, online monitoring, and statistical functions.In terms of multiaxis drive systems for looms, drive and control integration technology has the ad-vantages of high transmission efficiency, low energy consumption, and being conducive to integrated control. It has been widely developed and applied in high-end CNC machine tools, wind generators, new energy vehicles, robots, and other fields18–20.

However, their current loop control frequency is mostly maintained at 2–3 kHz, and the hardware still relies on a multi-chip distributed architecture. Traditional domestic loom control systems mostly adopt a separated structure of control unit and drive unit, resulting in large volume, high power consumption, and limited real-time performance. The current loop frequency of such systems is generally 1–2 kHz, and the control delay is relatively long. In contrast, the drive-control integrated system proposed in this paper adopts a SoC heterogeneous multi-core architecture to realize chip-level integration of control and drive. The current loop frequency is increased to 10 kHz, the system volume is reduced by 50%, the data transmission delay is controlled within 10 μs, and the power consumption is reduced by 15%. Compared with mainstream domestic and foreign products, the proposed system achieves obvious improvements in integration, real-time performance, control accuracy and energy efficiency, which can better meet the appli-cation requirements of high-performance air-jet looms.

At present, the main technical bottlenecks of the mainstream loom control systems at home and abroad, which mainly adopt a distributed architecture, are as follows: (1) The separation of control units, drive units, and human–machine interfaces leads to a large system volume, with an average space occupation of more than 0.5 m\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3}$$\end{document}. (2) The communication between multiple chips through traditional buses is susceptible to the high-temperature and high-humidity environment of textile workshops, resulting in insufficient reliability of signal transmission. (3) The response speed of servo control is difficult to meet the high-speed weaving requirements of more than 2000 rpm.These problems seriously restrict the market competitiveness of domestic high-end looms.

To address the aforementioned issues, this study, based on Shandong Rifa Textile Machinery Co., Ltd.’s over ten years of technical accumulation in the field of loom con-trol, innovatively proposes a “chip-level drive and control integration” solution. Com-pared with existing technologies, three main breakthroughs have been achieved: (1) A SoC + FPGA heterogeneous architecture is adopted, reducing the system volume by 50%. (2) The parallel FOC algorithm is implemented through FPGA, increasing the servo loop frequency to 1 kHz. The parallel FOC algorithm is implemented through FPGA, increasing the servo loop frequency to 10 kHz. (3) An intelligent networking module is built in, supporting cloud data interaction and remote maintenance. Verified by the National Textile Ma-chinery Quality Inspection and Testing Center, the system’s performance indicators have reached the international advanced level. It has been successfully applied to the RFJA36 series of air-jet looms, creating an economic benefit of more than 360 million yuan.

This paper focuses on elaborating the design principles of the system, the imple-mentation of key technologies, and the industrial application effects, providing a ref-erence for the intelligent upgrading of textile equipment.

In addition to optimizing the drive-control performance of the loom itself, this paper further integrates a computer vision-based fabric defect detection module into the overall control framework. The drive-control system provides stable high-speed mul-ti-axis coordination and accurate tension control, while the intelligent inspection system feeds back real-time fabric quality information to the control layer to form a closed-loop optimization. The two modules are highly coupled and complementary, forming a complete intelligent loom system.

Hardware architecture design

The hardware architecture of the drive and control integrated intelligent loom control system adopts a highly integrated design scheme based on heterogeneous multi-core SoC. As shown in the AMP (Asymmetric Multi-Processing) architecture mode in Fig. 3, the system clearly divides functional tasks: one CPU core runs the Linux system for human–machine interaction (HMI) and intelligent networking appli-cations, while the other CPU core runs the FreeRTOS real-time kernel to handle the core control tasks of the loom. This isolated design ensures that even if the HMI fails, it will not affect the key control functions.The processor system is equipped with 4 GB DDR3 SDRAM for runtime memory, 16 MB Nor Flash for firmware storage, and 4 GB eMMC for process parameter storage, thus forming a complete storage system. It also features a power management chip to realize multiple voltage outputs of 3.3 V/5 V and un-dervoltage protection functions.The FPGA module fully leverages its parallel processing advantages, implementing field-oriented control (FOC) for two servo motors using a hardware description language. The current loop frequency reaches 10 kHz. It also configures 76 programmable digital I/O interfaces to drive solenoid valves (with a re-sponse time of less than 50 μ s).The system uses an AXI bus-based shared memory in-terface to achieve high-speed data interaction between the CPU and the FPGA, with a transmission bandwidth of 1 GB/s. Among them, a 128 KB on-chip memory block (OCM) serves as the inter-core shared memory. The AXI4 bus protocol ensures that the data transmission delay is less than 10 μ s.This architecture design not only meets the re-al-time control requirements (with FreeRTOS ensuring the scheduling of key tasks) but also realizes effective isolation between functional modules. As a result, the system maintains high performance while also possessing excellent reliability.

The human–machine interaction (HMI) module design includes a direct RGB in-terface connection scheme for a 7-inch resistive touchscreen (with a resolution of 800 × 480), as well as dual CAN bus (with SIT105 transceiver) and RS485 (with isolated ADM2483 chip) communication interfaces, ensuring protocol compatibility with vari-ous types of textile equipment.Fig. 3Schematic diagrams of SMP and AMP architectures.

Software architecture design

The software architecture of the drive and control integrated intelligent loom control system, as shown in Fig. 4, is designed with a layered approach, constructing a three-layer architecture that includes the Hardware Abstraction Layer (HAL), Re-al-Time Control Layer, and Application Layer. The HAL encapsulates the underlying hardware operations through a unified device driver interface, providing standardized hardware access services for the upper layers. This specifically includes:The FPGA register mapping interface, which allows the upper layers to interact with the FPGA by mapping its registers, enabling control and data exchange with the FPGA mod-ule.Peripheral driver management, which covers various communication interfaces such as CAN, SPI, and I2C. It ensures effective communication with different peripheral devices and sensors connected to the loom.The Memory Management Unit (MMU), which is responsible for managing the system’s memory resources, providing efficient memory allocation and deallocation services, and ensuring that the system can handle multiple tasks and data streams simultaneously without conflicts.By abstracting the hardware details, the HAL provides a consistent and reliable interface for the real-time control layer and application layer, facilitating the development and maintenance of the software system.Fig. 4Schematic diagram of HMI software architecture.

Schematic diagram of HMI software architecture.

The real-time control layer is built on the FreeRTOS real-time operating system and uses a priority-based preemptive scheduling algorithm to manage task execution. The system functions are divided into several independent task modules, including key re-al-time tasks such as the main shaft control task (priority 15), weft insertion control task (priority 14), and warp feeding and take-up task (priority 13). Each task is allocated a separate stack space (4–8 KB) and message queue to ensure the determinism of the critical control cycle (worst-case response time < 100 μ s).The application layer runs in the Linux operating system environment and uses the Qt framework to develop a graphical human–machine interface (HMI), as shown in Fig. 5. This layer implements functional modules such as process parameter configuration, operation status monitoring, and fault alarm handling. Data exchange with the real-time system is achieved through a shared memory mechanism (with 128 MB reserved space), and double buffering technology is used for data transmission to avoid access conflicts. The schematic diagram of the drive and control integrated intelligent loom control system is shown in Fig. 6.Fig. 5Schematic diagram of HMI and cloud parameter.

Schematic diagram of HMI and cloud parameter.

In terms of control algorithm implementation, the system adopts an improved field-oriented control (FOC) strategy. The algorithm consists of three core modules: The Clarke transformation module converts three-phase currents into two-phase currents in a stationary coordinate system. The Park transformation module performs the conver-sion to a rotating coordinate system. The space vector pulse width modulation (SVPWM) module generates the PWM control signals.Compared with traditional solutions, this system adds a load observer (Luenberger observer) and a feedforward compensation link based on the id=0 control. The speed loop control cycle is shortened to 100 μ s, and the current loop control cycle reaches 10 μ s, resulting in a dynamic response perfor-mance improvement of about 30%. The system status management employs a Mealy-type finite state machine model, defining seven main states (standby, initialization, low-speed operation, high-speed operation, fault handling, etc.) and 23 state transition conditions. State transitions are realized through an event-driven mechanism, with each state corresponding to specific control strategies and parameter sets.The intelligent networking module uses the lightweight MQTT protocol to communicate with the cloud platform, as shown in Fig. 8. It implements three core functions: data acquisition service (with a configurable sampling period, defaulting to 100 ms), a disconnection buffering mechanism (with a ring buffer capacity of 1 MB), and a differential transmission algorithm (achieving a data compression rate of about 60%). In a typical 4G network environment, it can achieve a data transmission success rate of over 98%.All software modules are designed with a modular principle, with clear interface specifications defined (a total of 12 interface standards are estab-lished) to achieve functional decoupling. Communication between modules is based on the publish-subscribe model, which effectively reduces system complexity and in-creases code reuse rate (with a reuse rate of over 85% for core modules).Fig. 6Schematic diagram of drive and control integrated intelligent loom control system.

Parallel FOC algorithm based on FPGA

To address the timing conflicts and computational delays in multi-axis coordinated control that exist in traditional DSP solutions, this study proposes a parallel field-oriented control (FOC) algorithm architecture based on FPGA hardware accelera-tion. As shown in Fig. 7, this solution achieves high-performance servo control by constructing a complete coordinate transformation chain. Cloud parameter deployment

To achieve efficient batch configuration of weaving machine process parameters, this system has developed a cloud deployment technology based on differential compression. The architecture for this is shown in Fig. 9. First, the server performs feature extraction on the parameter file to generate a differential package that contains only the changed data (with an average size of 28 KB and a compression rate of 62%). This differential package is then sent to the target device via a 4G network.Fig. 8Schematic diagram of intelligent networking and cloud platform architecture.

Schematic diagram of intelligent networking and cloud platform architecture.

The device end uses an improved BSDiff algorithm for data reconstruction, in combination with the dual Bank mechanism of the Flash memory, to achieve seamless parameter switching. The specific process includes three key stages:Preprocessing Stage: The parameter file is subjected to structural analysis to identify common data blocks that can be shared.Compression Stage: The LZ77 algorithm combined with Huffman coding is used to compress process parameters such as weft density and tension curves to 35–45% of their original size.Transmission Stage: A breakpoint resumption pro-tocol ensures data integrity. The average transmission time is 3.2 s per device (for a cluster of 100 devices).Practical applications have shown that this technology increases the efficiency of new process deployment by 90%, reduces the configuration error rate to below 0.05%, and supports parallel parameter updates for up to 500 devices. The system also features a version rollback mechanism, which retains the last five parameter ver-sions and can complete fault recovery within 10 s, significantly improving the maintenance efficiency of the production line.

An intelligent fabric quality inspection system based on the improved YOLOv8n algorithm

Fabric detection algorithm based on improved YOLOv8n

The textile industry, as a traditional pillar industry in China, the quality of its products directly affects the industry’s competitiveness. Various defects generated during the fabric production process are key factors affecting product quality. The tra-ditional manual detection methods have problems such as low efficiency (only able to detect 2–3 m of fabric per minute), high labor intensity, and inconsistent detection standards. With the development of textile machinery towards high-speed, the tradi-tional detection methods have been unable to meet the production needs.

The successful application of computer vision technology in industrial inspection has provided new ideas for fabric quality detection. However, fabric defect detection faces the following special challenges: (1) The scale of defects varies greatly, ranging from broken yarns at the millimeter level to stains at the centimeter level; (2) The fabric texture background is complex, making defect features easily obscured; (3) Real-time detection is required in high-speed production environments (processing delay < 10 ms). Existing studies have shown that traditional machine learning methods such as SVM and random forests are difficult to handle such complex scenarios, while deep learning, especially single-stage object detection algorithms, demonstrate advantages.

From this perspective, the objective of this study is not to separately optimize a motor control algorithm and a defect detection model, but to develop an integrated in-telligent loom framework that combines high-performance electromechanical control with real-time visual quality inspection on the same industrial platform. The drive-and-control integrated system, implemented on a heterogeneous SoC–FPGA ar-chitecture, provides deterministic multi-axis coordination and stable high-speed weav-ing performance. On this basis, the fabric defect detection module based on an im-proved YOLOv8n model enables online perception of the woven fabric state, allowing production quality to be monitored in real time rather than evaluated only after weav-ing is completed.

YOLOv8n, as the latest lightweight network in the YOLO series, maintains high detection accuracy while having excellent real-time performance. However, it still has the following shortcomings in the application of fabric detection: (1) The detection rate for small-scale defects (such as a single broken yarn) is low; (2) The ability to distinguish defects in similar textured backgrounds is insufficient; (3) The inference efficiency on embedded devices needs to be improved.

This paper, based on the actual production requirements of the textile industry, proposes an improved YOLOv8n fabric quality detection system. The main contribu-tions are as follows: (1) A dedicated dataset covering 7 types of fabric defects was con-structed, including samples under different lighting and fabric density conditions; (2) The depth separable convolution module DWConv was introduced, which significantly reduced the complexity of the model, improved the deployment capability of the model, and increased the detection frame rate; (3) Based on DWConv and the C2f module, C2f-DW was constructed to enhance the model’s ability to locate defects in the texture background; (4) The Shap IoU loss function was introduced to optimize the convergence speed of the model; (5) The algorithm was efficiently deployed on embedded devices to meet the real-time requirements of the production line.Experiments have proved that this system outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and speed.

Dataset source

The fabric defect dataset used in this study is constructed based on the non-pattern fabric subset of the TILDA database, which consists of real samples collected from in-dustrial textile production processes. To improve the reproducibility and engineering interpretability of the experiments, the dataset source, fabric types, and acquisition constraints are explicitly described.

The dataset contains a total of 1000 images, including 800 defective samples and 200 defect-free samples. All images are stored in TIFF format with a resolution of 768 × 512 pixels. The defect categories include representative defects commonly observed in weaving production, such as holes, oil spots or wrong color, thread errors, and foreign matter. Normal fabric images are also retained as negative samples to enhance the model’s discrimination capability under real industrial conditions.The situation of some datasets is shown in Fig. 11. Figure 12 shows the spatial distribution of the dataset labels, as well as the distribution of sizes.

In terms of fabric types, the dataset focuses on non-pattern woven fabrics produced by air-jet looms, with polyester as the primary material. The corresponding representa-tive process parameters are as follows: fabric areal density of approximately 95 g/m\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{2}$$\end{document}, warp density of 576 ends/10 cm, weft density of 347 picks/10 cm, and yarn linear density of approximately 43 tex. These parameters correspond to typical industrial production conditions and are therefore representative.

Considering that defect detection is sensitive to production environments, the data acquisition process is subject to several engineering constraints. Specifically, the illu-mination intensity is controlled at approximately 3700 lx, and the camera optical axis is maintained perpendicular to the fabric surface. The acquisition distance is fixed at 240 mm. Exposure parameters are adjusted according to the loom operating speed to avoid motion blur. In practical conditions, the loom speed is approximately 1400 rpm, ensuring that the dataset reflects medium-to-high-speed weaving scenarios. Images with severe overexposure, shadow occlusion, or significant defocus are excluded.

For annotation, all defect samples are manually labeled using bounding boxes, with X-Anylabeling as the annotation tool. The annotation principle is to enclose the defect region with the smallest possible bounding box while ensuring complete coverage, while retaining appropriate background texture. Ambiguous regions, indistinguishable defects, or difficult-to-classify areas are excluded from the final annotations. For tiny defects, only samples with clearly distinguishable morphology at the original resolution are re-tained to reduce label uncertainty.

During preprocessing, all samples are checked for duplication, annotation con-sistency, and abnormal data. The dataset is then divided into training, validation, and test sets with a ratio of 7:2:1, while maintaining a balanced class distribution across subsets.

It should be noted that the dataset in this study mainly focuses on non-pattern woven fabrics and typical visible defects. Its generalization ability to patterned fabrics, coated fabrics, or extreme lighting and strong vibration conditions requires further in-vestigation.Fig. 11TILDA dataset.Fig. 12The distribution map of label center positions and the distribution map of label box sizes.

Shape_IoU_loss Function

The bounding box regression loss function helps the model learn how to accurately predict the position of the target from the training data, thereby improving the posi-tioning accuracy and regional perception ability of target detection. This is crucial for achieving high-quality target detection results. The existing YOLOv8 uses the CIoU loss function for bounding box regression calculation. The existing boundary regression methods usually consider the geometric relationship between the target bounding box and the predicted bounding box to calculate the loss, but ignore the influence of the shape and scale of the bounding box itself on the regression result. This paper intro-duces the Shape-IoU method, where the structure of the Shape-IoU loss function is shown in Fig. 15.

Abstract
. For a prime p p and an associative ring R R with unity, there are various constructions of p p -typical Witt vectors of R R , all of which specialize to the classical p p -typical Witt vectors when R R is commutative. These constructions are endowed with a Verschiebung operator V V and a Teichmüller map ⟨ ⋅ ⟩ \langle{\cdot}\rangle , and they satisfy the property that the map x ↦ V ⁡ ⟨ x p ⟩ − p ⁡ ⟨ x ⟩ x\mapsto V\langle{x^{p}}\rangle-p\langle{x}\rangle is additive. In this paper, we adapt the group-theoretic universal characterization of classical p p -typical Witt vectors proposed in [ 9 ] to the non-commutative setting. Our main result is that this approach yields a construction of Witt vectors for associative rings, denoted E E , which specializes correctly to the classical Witt functor in the commutative case. The construction of E E is inspired by the Witt functor of Cuntz–Deninger (see [ 3 ] ), and we show that E E is a universal pre-Witt functor (see Theorem 1.5 ), subject to an explicit conjecture concerning non-commutative polynomials (see Conjecture 1.9 ). We further introduce the notion of a Witt functor and construct a universal Witt functor E ^ \widehat{E} , which is closely related to Hesselholt’s Witt functor W H W_{H} (see [ 5 ] , [ 6 ] ). We suspect that W H W_{H} is, in fact, the universal Morita-invariant Witt functor.

There exist functorial maps

(2)

\langle{\ }\rangle:R\to W(R)
(a set map)

(1)

\langle{0}\rangle=0

(2)

(3)

(4)

R

p

\implies W(R)

The above properties were the basis of the definition of a pre-Witt functor in

Theorem 1.1

[
9

For

, the classical functor of
p
-typical Witt vectors

is a universal pre-Witt functor.

The key point of the above characterization is that it uses only the group structure on

1
]

[
8

This motivates the following definition which generalizes the definition of a pre-Witt functor
[
9

to the case of associative rings (possibly non-commutative).

(pre-Witt functor)

A pre-Witt functor is a functor

(1)

Verschiebung operator:

(group homomorphism)

(2)

Teichmüller map :
\langle{\ }\rangle:R\to F(R)
(a set map)

which satisfy the following properties

(1)

\langle{0}\rangle=0
. If
p\neq 2
then
\langle{-x}\rangle=-\langle{x}\rangle
for all
x\in R
.

(2)

4.
Witt functors

The goal of this section is to define the notion of a Witt functor (see Definition
4.3
) and describe the Witt functor
\hat{E}
. We will prove the universality of
\hat{E}
assuming Conjecture
1.9
. We also finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
by showing the existence of a natural transformation
E\to W_{H}
. Note that such a transformation exists by universality of
E
(Theorem
1.5
), however we would like to establish this (see Proposition
4.8
) without assuming Conjecture
1.9
.

In the classical case, for
R\in{\mathsf{ComRings}}
, the set
W(R)
is in bijection with
R^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. This bijection can be explicitly given by

(a_{0},a_{1},...)\mapsto\sum_{n}V^{n}\langle{a_{n}}\rangle

Let
F:{\mathsf{Rings}}\to{\mathsf{Ab}}
be a pre-Witt functor. For a tuple
(a_{0},a_{1},...)\in R^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, denote

(a_{0},a_{1},...)_{w}:=\sum_{n}V^{n}\langle{a_{n}}\rangle\in F(R).

4.1.
Witt sum and difference polynomials :

Before defining the Witt functor, we first recall the group structure on
W_{H}(R)
for
R\in Rings
as given in
[
5
, 1.5, 1.6]
. Note that
W_{H}(R)
is equipped with the Verschiebung operator

V:W(R)\to W(R)\ \ \ \ V(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots):=(0,a_{0},a_{1},\cdots)

and the Teichmüller map

\langle\ \rangle:R\longrightarrow W(R),\ \ \ \ \langle a\rangle:=(a,0,0,\ldots)

Remark 4.1
.

The definitions
V:W_{H}(R)\to W_{H}(R)
and
\langle\ \rangle:R\longrightarrow W_{H}(R)
differ from that of
V:E(R)\to E(R)
and
\langle\ \rangle:R\longrightarrow E(R)
(see Definition
2.1
and Remark
2.5
). Every element of
W_{H}(R)
represented (not necessarily uniquely) as
(a_{0},a_{1}...)_{w}
. This is a consequence of the fact that the addition and subtraction of elements of the form
(a_{0},a_{1}...)_{w}
in the group
W_{H}(R)
are given by non-commutative version of Witt polynomials as given below.

Definition 4.2

(Witt sum and difference polynomials)
.

For
R\in{\mathsf{Rings}}
, and elements

(a_{0},a_{1},...)_{w},(b_{0},b_{1},...)_{w}\in W_{H}(R)
,

(a_{0},a_{1},...)_{w}+(b_{0},b_{1},...)_{w}:=(s_{0}(a_{0},b_{0}),s_{1}(a_{0},b_{0},a_{1},b_{1}),...)

(a_{0},a_{1},...)_{w}-(b_{0},b_{1},...)_{w}:=(d_{0}(a_{0},b_{0}),d_{1}(a_{0},b_{0},a_{1},b_{1}),...)

where
s_{i}
and
d_{i}
are non-commutative analogues of classical Witt sum and difference polynomials as defined in
[
5
, 1.4.1]
. We use the following two variable specialisations of the Witt sum and difference polynomials. For an integer
i\geq 0

define polynomials
r_{i},e_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\{X,Y\}
by

\displaystyle r_{i}(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=s_{i}(X,Y,0,0,...0,0)

\displaystyle e_{i}(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=d_{i}(X,Y,0,0,...0,0)

It follows that
r_{0}(X,Y)=X+Y
and
e_{0}(X,Y)=X-Y
. In particular, we have the following relations in
W_{H}
(and also in the classical case)

\displaystyle\langle{x}\rangle+\langle{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{x+y}\rangle+\sum_{i>0}V^{i}\langle{r_{i}(x,y)}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle{x}\rangle-\langle{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{x-y}\rangle+\sum_{i>0}V^{i}\langle{e_{i}(x,y)}\rangle

Definition 4.3

(Witt functor)
.

A pre-Witt functor
F:{\mathsf{Rings}}\to{\mathsf{Ab}}
is called a Witt functor if,

\displaystyle\langle{x}\rangle+\langle{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{x+y}\rangle+\sum_{i>0}V^{i}\langle{r_{i}(x,y)}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle{x}\rangle-\langle{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{x-y}\rangle+\sum_{i>0}V^{i}\langle{e_{i}(x,y)}\rangle

Examples 4.4
.

W_{H}
is a Witt functor.

Definition 4.5

(The Witt functor
\hat{E}
)
.

For a ring
R\in{\mathsf{Rings}}
, let
{\mathcal{R}}(R)\subset E(R)
denote the smallest closed subgroup stable under
V
and containing all
elements of the form

\{\langle{x}\rangle+\langle{y}\rangle-\sum_{n\geq 0}V^{n}\langle{r_{n}(x,y)}\rangle\ |\ x,y\in R\}\ \bigcup\ \{\langle{x}\rangle-\langle{y}\rangle-\sum_{n\geq 0}V^{n}\langle{e_{n}(x,y)}\rangle\ |\ x,y\in R\}

where
r_{n},e_{n}
are integer polynomials defined in (
4.2
). Let
\hat{E}(R):=\frac{E(R)}{{\mathcal{R}}(R)}
. Clearly
\hat{E}
is a Witt functor, since the required relations have been forced by the definition.

The following result is an immediate consequence of the above definition of
\hat{E}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Every element of
\hat{E}(R)
is of the form

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}V^{n}\langle{a_{n}}\rangle.

Moreover, we have the following conjectural characterisation of the functor
\hat{E}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

For
p\neq 2
, the Conjecture
1.9
implies that
\hat{E}
is a universal Witt functor.

Proof.

By using Conjecture
1.9
, we have proved that
E
is a universal pre-Witt functor (see Theorem
1.5
).
\hat{E}
is obtained by simply going modulo the extra relations in
E
required to be a Witt functor. From this, one can easily deduce that
\hat{E}
is a universal Witt functor.
∎

The goal of the remaining section is to prove Theorem
1.3
(2). We will prove the following result without using Conjecture
1.9
and Theorem
1.5
.

Proposition 4.8
.

There is a unique natural transformation of pre-Witt functors
E\to W_{H}
and of Witt functors
\hat{E}\to W_{H}
.

We will need the following results in order to the prove Proposition
4.8
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

Proof.

By Corollary

, we know that

and

{\mathcal{R}}(A)
is mapped to

We now observe the following connection between

Lemma 4.10
.

Let

We know that the ghost map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\frac{A}{[A,A]}\right)^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{H}(A)}

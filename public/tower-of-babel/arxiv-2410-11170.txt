Removable singularity of ( − 1 ) (-1) -homogeneous solutions of stationary Navier-Stokes equations
Li Li, YanYan Li, Xukai Yan

Abstract
We study the removable singularity problem for ( − 1 ) (-1) -homogeneous solutions of the three-dimensional incompressible stationary Navier-Stokes equations with singular rays. We prove that any local ( − 1 ) (-1) -homogeneous solution u u near a potential singular ray from the origin, which passes through a point P P on the unit sphere 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} , can be smoothly extended across P P on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} , provided that u = o ​ ( ln ⁡ dist ​ ( x , P ) ) u=o(\ln\text{dist}(x,P)) on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} . The result is optimal in the sense that for any α > 0 \alpha>0 , there exists a local ( − 1 ) (-1) -homogeneous solution near P P on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} , such that lim x ∈ 𝕊 2 , x → P | u ⁡ ( x ) | / ln ⁡ dist ​ ( x , P ) = − α \lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to P}|u(x)|/\ln\text{dist}(x,P)=-\alpha . Furthermore, we discuss the behavior of isolated singularities of ( − 1 ) (-1) -homogeneous solutions and provide examples from the literature that exhibit varying behaviors. We also present an existence result of solutions with any finite number of singular points located anywhere on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} .

Introduction

Consider the three-dimensional incompressible stationary Navier-Stokes equations,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,\\
&{\rm div\hskip 1.42271pt}u=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity vector and
p:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure. These equations are invariant under the scaling
u(x)\to\lambda u(\lambda x)
and
p(x)\to\lambda^{2}p(\lambda x)

\lambda>0

(-1)
-homogeneous and

(-2)
-homogeneous, and we call them
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions according to the homogeneity of

for any
\lambda>0

Let

e_{1}=(1,0,0)

(r,\theta,\phi)
be the spherical coordinates, where

is the radial distance from the origin,
\theta
is the angle between the radial vector and the positive

-axis, and
\phi
is the meridian angle about the
x_{3}
-axis. A vector field

u=u_{r}e_{r}+u_{\theta}e_{\theta}+u_{\phi}e_{\phi},

where

e_{r}=\left(\begin{matrix}\sin\theta\cos\phi\\
\sin\theta\sin\phi\\
\cos\theta\end{matrix}\right),\hskip 14.22636pte_{\theta}=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\theta\cos\phi\\
\cos\theta\sin\phi\\

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\delta>0

(u,p)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)\setminus\{S\})
be a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
). If

\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}\frac{|u(x)|}{\ln{\rm dist}(x,S)}=0.

(2)

Then
(u,p)
can be extended as a
C^{2}

\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)
.

Remark 1.1
.

The above removable singularity result is optimal in the following sense: For any
\alpha>0
, there exists a
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solution
(u,p)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
of (

\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to S}|u(x)|/\ln{\rm dist}(x,S)=

\lim_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},x\to N}|u(x)|/\ln{\rm dist}(x,N)=-\alpha
.
Examples of such solutions can be found in
[

On the other hand, there does not exist

-homogeneous axisymmetric solution

Note that all

-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (

C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)\setminus\{S\})
satisfying (
55
) for some
\delta>0
.

In particular, when
c_{3}=-4
, there are special solutions explicitly given by

\chi(y)=(1-y^{2})\ln(\frac{1+y}{1-y})+2y+2\alpha(1-y^{2})

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Correspondingly,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{\theta}=4\sin\theta\cdot\frac{1-\cos\theta(\ln(\cot\frac{\theta}{2})+\alpha)}{\cos\theta+\sin^{2}\theta(\ln(\cot\frac{\theta}{2})+\alpha)},\\
&u_{r}=-4-\frac{8\big(1-\cos\theta(\ln(\cot\frac{\theta}{2})+\alpha)\big)}{\big(\cos\theta+\sin^{2}\theta(\ln(\cot\frac{\theta}{2})+\alpha)\big)^{2}},\\
&u_{\phi}=0,\\
&p=\frac{8\big(-2+\cos\theta(\ln\cot\frac{\theta}{2}+\alpha)-\sin^{2}\theta(\ln\cot\frac{\theta}{2}+\alpha)^{2}\big)}{\big(\cos\theta+\sin^{2}\theta(\ln\cot\frac{\theta}{2}+\alpha)\big)^{2}}\end{aligned}\right.

(56)

is a special solution of (
1
) satisfying (
55
) for
\theta\in(\theta_{0},\pi)
, where
\theta_{0}
is the unique root of
\cos\theta+\sin^{2}\theta(\ln\cot\frac{\theta}{2}+\alpha)=0
. Thus (
56
) is a solution of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta}(S)\setminus\{S\})
for some
\delta>0
depending on
\alpha
. Note that (
56
) is also a solution for
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0})
, thus is a solution in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap B_{\delta^{\prime}}(N)\setminus\{N\})
for some
\delta^{\prime}>0
. This special solution also implies that our removable singularity result Theorem
1.1
is optimal. Note this solution does not exist on the whole
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\}
. Indeed, in this example,
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
and
c_{3}=-4=\bar{c}_{3}(0,0)
. By Theorem 1.1 in
[
19
]
, in this case there exists only one solution of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
, which is given by
u_{\theta}=-4\cot\theta
,
u_{\phi}=0
,
u_{r}=-4
and
p=-8\csc^{2}\theta
.

When
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
,
c_{3}=1/2>-4
, there are special solutions in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
with explicit but not elementary expressions given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{\theta}=\frac{\sin\theta\big(K(\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})-\alpha K(\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})\big)}{2\big(E(\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})-\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}K(\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})+\alpha E(\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})-\alpha\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}K(\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2})\big)},\\
&u_{r}=\frac{-1}{\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}(\sin\theta u_{\theta}),\\
&u_{\phi}=0,\\
&p=\frac{-1}{2}\Big(\frac{d^{2}u_{r}}{d\theta^{2}}+(\cot\theta-u_{\theta})\frac{du_{r}}{d\theta}+u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2}\Big),\end{aligned}\right.

for any
0<\alpha<+\infty
, where
K(x)
and
E(x)
are respectively the complete elliptic integrals of the first and second kind

K(x)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x\sin^{2}\theta}}d\theta,\qquad E(x)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-x\sin^{2}\theta d\theta},\qquad 0<x<1.

These special solutions lie in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
and satisfy (
55
), which also implies that the removable singularity result Theorem
1.1
is optimal. For
\alpha=0
or
+\infty
, the above solution is a solution of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\})
of Type 3. If
\alpha<0
, then the above solution is a local solution near
N
or
S
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

3.2.2
Serrin’s solutions

In a pioneering work
[
37
]
concerning a representative model for tornadoes, Serrin studied
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions of (
1
) in the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\cap\{x_{3}>0\}
with a singular ray along the positive
x_{3}
-axis and some boundary conditions on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
. Notably, the solutions formulated in the paper exhibit properties different from Landau solutions, where
u_{\theta}=O(|x^{\prime}|\ln|x^{\prime}|)
,
u_{r}=O(\ln|x^{\prime}|)
near the north pole

on
\mathbb{S}^{2}

3.2.3
Solutions given by Liouville formulas

In
[
44

, Šverák proved that all
(-1)
-homogeneous nonzero solutions of (
1
) in
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
are Landau solutions (see Theorem
A
). In his proof, (
1
) is reduced to

-\triangle_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi+2=2e^{\varphi},\quad\mbox{on }\mathbb{S}^{2},

(57)

where
u=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. It is clear from
[
44
]
that if
\varphi
is a solution to (
57
), then

is a solution to (
1
) after being extended to a
(-1)
-homogeneous vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

The classification of solutions to (
57
) is classical. Let
F^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the stereographic projection, with

given by

,

\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

is a solution to

-\triangle\xi=e^{\xi}

(58)

in
\mathcal{O}
, if and only if

\varphi(x):=\xi\circ F^{-1}(x)-3\ln 2+2\ln(1+|F^{-1}(x)|^{2})

(59)

is a solution to (
57
) in
F(\mathcal{O})\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

For a simply connected open set
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

[

]
and
[
6
]
) that all real solutions

\xi=\ln\frac{8|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}},

(60)

with

being a locally univalent meromorphic function. Here we have abused notations slightly by identifying
z=z^{1}+iz^{2}
and using
\mathcal{O}
also to denote the subset
\{z^{1}+iz^{2}\mid(z^{1},z^{2})\in\mathcal{O}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
. In particular, if
\xi
is singular at some

, then
\varphi
is singular at
F(z)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and the corresponding

is a solution to (
1
) with a singularity at
F(z)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In view of this fact, we may construct some special
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (

\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

First, for axisymmetric solutions in
C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{N,S\})
, we have

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u
be a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
) given by
u=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{S,N\}
, with
\varphi
given by (
59
) and (
60
) for some multi-valued locally univalent meromorphic function
f
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. If
u
is axisymmetric, then
f=az^{\alpha}
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Since
u=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}
is axisymmetric,
\varphi
is also axisymmetric. So
\xi
defined by (
60
) is radially symmetric and satisfies (
58
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. Denote
r=|z|
, we have
\xi=\xi(r)
. Let
t=\ln|z|
and
\eta(t)=\xi(e^{t})+2t
, then (
58
) is reduced to

-\eta_{tt}=e^{\eta}.

Multiplying both sides of the above by
\eta_{t}
and taking the integral, we have
\eta_{t}^{2}+2e^{\eta}=const
. Solving for
\eta
, we obtain

\eta=\ln\frac{Ae^{ct}}{(1+Ae^{ct})^{2}}+C

for some constants
A>0,c>0
and
C
. Then

\xi=\ln\frac{Ar^{c-2}}{(1+Ar^{c})^{2}}+C,\quad\forall r>0.

Note this
\xi
is given by (
60
) with
f(z)=\sqrt{A}z^{c/2}
or
f(z)=z^{-c/2}/\sqrt{A}
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Now we display some special solutions of (

Example 3.3

We may also construct
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions of (

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer,
P_{1},...,P_{m}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
be distinct points, and
\{l_{1},...,l_{m}\}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,1,-1\}
satisfy
\sum_{j=1}^{m}l_{j}=m-2
. Then there exists a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\})
of (
1

u=2(|l_{j}|-1)\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\ln|x-P_{j}|+O(1),\quad\textrm{as }x\to P_{j}\textrm{ on }\mathbb{S}^{2},\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq m.

(66)

Proof.

By rotation of the coordinates, let
P_{m}
be the north pole
N
. Let
F^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the stereographic projection and
z_{j}:=F^{-1}(P_{j})
,
1\leq j\leq m-1
. Fix any
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_{1},...,z_{m-1}\}
and define

f(z):=\int_{a}^{z}(t-z_{1})^{l_{1}-1}\cdots(t-z_{m-1})^{l_{m-1}-1}dt,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_{1},...,z_{m-1}\},

(67)

where the integral path from
a
to
z
does not intersect with
\{z_{1},...,z_{m-1}\}
. Since
l_{j}\neq 0,1
,
f
is independent of the path and well-defined in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_{1},...,z_{m-1}\}
. So
f
is a locally univalent meromorphic function near each
z_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq m-1
.

Let
\xi(z)
be defined by (
60
) with this
f
, and
\varphi(x)
be defined by (
59
). Then as mentioned earlier,
\xi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_{1},...,z_{m-1}\})
satisfies (
58
) in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_{1},...,z_{m-1}\}
, and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\})
satisfies (
57
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\}
. Let
u:=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\}
and be extended as a
(-1)
-homogeneous vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\})
is a
(-1)
-homogeneous solution of (
1
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{P_{1},...,P_{m}\}
.

Now we prove that
u
satisfies (
66
) for all
1\leq j\leq m
. Let
(r,\theta,\phi)
be spherical coordinates as usual. Write

for

and

. By computation,

\begin{split}&\partial_{\theta}x=e_{\theta},\quad\partial_{\phi}x=\sin\theta e_{\phi},\\
&\partial_{\theta}z=-\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)^{T},\quad\partial_{\phi}z=\frac{\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}(-\sin\phi,\cos\phi)^{T}.\end{split}

(68)

For convenience, denote
A:=(\cos\theta-1)[\partial_{\theta}z,\partial_{\phi}z/\sin\theta]=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi&\sin\phi\\
\sin\phi&-\cos\phi\end{bmatrix}
.

Case 1.

j\neq m
.

We only need to prove it for
j=1
. For
f
given by (
67
), we have

f^{\prime}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(z-z_{j})^{l_{j}-1}=Cl_{1}(z-z_{1})^{l_{1}-1}+O(|z-z_{1}|^{l_{1}})

(69)

and

f(z)=C(z-z_{1})^{l_{1}}+O(|z-z_{1}|^{l_{1}+1})+O(1)

as

, where

(71)

By the definition of
\varphi
, we have
\varphi(x)=\hat{\xi}\circ F^{-1}(x)
,

. We first claim that

\hat{\xi}=2(|l_{1}|-1)\ln|z-z_{1}|+O(1),\quad\textrm{as }z\to z_{1}.

Indeed, by (

71

Next, let

\nabla\hat{\xi}=2(|l_{1}|-1)\nabla\ln|z-z_{1}|+\nabla\zeta=2(|l_{1}|-1)\frac{z-z_{1}}{|z-z_{1}|^{2}}+O(1),\quad\textrm{ as }z\to z_{1}.

(74)

Recall
u=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi e_{r}

and
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi=2(|l_{1}|-1)\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\ln|x-P_{1}|+O(1)

Next, we estimate

\gamma(t)=F(\tilde{\gamma}(t))

\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi=\partial_{\theta}\varphi e_{\theta}+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\varphi e_{\phi}=\nabla\hat{\xi}\cdot\partial_{\theta}ze_{\theta}+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\nabla\hat{\xi}\cdot\partial_{\phi}ze_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}[e_{\theta},e_{\phi}]A^{T}\nabla\hat{\xi}.

On the other hand, write

\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{\theta}x\theta^{\prime}(t)+\partial_{\phi}x\phi^{\prime}(t)=[e_{\theta},e_{\phi}]\begin{bmatrix}\theta^{\prime}(t)\\
\sin\theta\phi^{\prime}(t)\end{bmatrix}

\begin{split}z-z_{1}&=(z_{0}-z_{1})t=\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)t=(\theta^{\prime}(t)\partial_{\theta}z+\phi^{\prime}(t)\partial_{\phi}z)t=-\frac{t}{1-\cos\theta}A\begin{bmatrix}\theta^{\prime}(t)\\
\sin\theta\phi^{\prime}(t)\end{bmatrix}\\
&=-\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}A\begin{bmatrix}e_{\theta}^{T}\\
e_{\phi}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\gamma^{\prime}(t)t=-\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}A\begin{bmatrix}e_{\theta}^{T}\\
e_{\phi}^{T}\end{bmatrix}(x-P_{1})+O(t^{2}).\end{split}

Using (
75
), (
74
), the above, the fact that
A^{T}A=I
,
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(|l_{1}|-1)}{1-\cos\theta}[e_{\theta},e_{\phi}]A^{T}\frac{z-z_{1}}{|z-z_{1}|^{2}}+O(1)

\displaystyle=2(|l_{1}|-1)[e_{\theta},e_{\phi}]\begin{bmatrix}e_{\theta}^{T}\\
e_{\phi}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\frac{x-P_{1}}{|x-P_{1}|^{2}}+O(1).

By (
68
),

\begin{split}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\ln|x-P_{j}|&=\partial_{\theta}\ln|x-P_{j}|e_{\theta}+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\ln|x-P_{j}|e_{\phi}\\
&=\frac{(x-P_{j})\cdot\partial_{\theta}x}{|x-P_{j}|^{2}}e_{\theta}+\frac{(x-P_{j})\cdot\partial_{\phi}x}{\sin\theta|x-P_{j}|^{2}}e_{\phi}=[e_{\theta},e_{\phi}]\begin{bmatrix}e_{\theta}^{T}\\
e_{\phi}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\frac{x-P_{j}}{|x-P_{j}|^{2}}.\end{split}

So we have proved (
66
) in Case 1.

Case 2.

j=m
.

Similar to Case 1, we only need to estimate
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi
as
x\to P_{m}
. In this case,
P_{m}=N
. As
x\to N
,
|z|\to\infty
. Let
l=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}l_{j}-m+2
. By the definition of
f
, we have

f^{\prime}(z)=z^{l-1}+O(|z|^{l-2})\quad\textrm{and }\quad f(z)=\frac{1}{l}z^{l}+O(|z|^{l-1})+O(1)

as
|z|\to\infty
. Plug this into (
71
), we have

\hat{\xi}=(2-2|l|)\ln|z|+O(|z|^{-1}).

(76)

Since
|l|\geq 2
, we have
e^{\hat{\xi}}=|z|^{2-2|l|}e^{O(1/|z|)}=O(1)
. So
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi=2(e^{\varphi}-1)=O(1)
.

Now we estimate
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi
. Fix
z_{0}
such that
\rho:=|z_{0}|/3>>1
and
z_{j}\notin B_{1}(z_{0})
for
1\leq j\leq m-1
. Let
\zeta(y):=\hat{\xi}(z_{0}+\rho y)-(2-2|l|)\ln|z_{0}+\rho y|
for
y\in B_{1}(0)
. Similar as the proof of (
74
), by (
58
) and (
59
) we have

-\Delta_{y}\zeta=-\rho^{2}\Delta\hat{\xi}(z_{0}+\rho y)=\frac{8\rho^{2}(e^{\hat{\xi}}-1)}{(1+|z_{0}+\rho y|^{2})^{2}}=O(\rho^{-2}),\quad y\in B_{1}(0).

By (
76
), we have
\zeta(y)=O(\rho^{-1})
in
B_{1}(0)
. By elliptic estimate, we have
\nabla_{y}\zeta(y)=O(\rho^{-1})
in
B_{1}(0)
. Thus

\nabla_{z}\hat{\xi}=(2-2|l|)\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla_{y}\zeta=(2-2|l|)\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}+O(\rho^{-2})=(2-2|l|)\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}+O(|z|^{-2}),

for
z\in B_{1}(z_{0})
. By (
75
), the above, the facts that
z=\frac{\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)^{T}
and
1-\cos\theta=\frac{2}{1+|z|^{2}}
, we have

\begin{split}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi&=-\frac{2(1-|l|)}{1-\cos\theta}[e_{\theta},e_{\phi}]A^{T}\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}+\frac{O(1)}{|z|^{2}(1-\cos\theta)}=\frac{2(|l|-1)}{\sin\theta}e_{\theta}+O(1).\end{split}

Note
x-N=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta-1)^{T}
, we have

\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\ln|x-N|=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\theta}\ln(\sin\theta^{2}+(1-\cos\theta)^{2})e_{\theta}=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2\sin\theta}e_{\theta}=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}e_{\theta}+O(1).

Thus we have

\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\varphi=\frac{2(|l|-1)}{\sin\theta}e_{\theta}+O(1)=2(|l|-1)\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\ln|x-N|+O(1).

Note
l_{m}=-l
, then (
66
) for
j=m
follows from the above.
∎

3.2.4
Solutions that are also solutions of Euler’s equation

Consider
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric no-swirl solutions of the stationary Euler’s equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,\\
&{\rm div\hskip 1.42271pt}u=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
U_{\theta}=u_{\theta}\sin\theta
and
y=\cos\theta
. The system on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
can be reduced to

\frac{1}{2}U^{2}_{\theta}=c_{0}+c_{1}y+c_{2}y^{2},\quad u_{r}=\frac{d}{dy}U_{\theta},

(77)

for some constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
, and the solution is given by

u_{\theta}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2(c_{0}+c_{1}\cos\theta+c_{2}\cos^{2}\theta)}}{\sin\theta},\quad u_{r}=\pm\frac{c_{1}+2c_{2}\cos\theta}{\sqrt{2(c_{0}+c_{1}\cos\theta+c_{2}\cos^{2}\theta)}},\quad u_{\phi}=0,

and
p
is determined by (
63
). In particular, when the polynomial
2(c_{0}+c_{1}y+c_{2}y^{2})=(ay+b)^{2}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, the above solution is also a solution of Navier-Stokes equations (
1
), where

u_{\theta}=\frac{a\cos\theta+b}{\sin\theta},\quad u_{r}=a,\quad u_{\phi}=0,\quad p=-\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}+2ab\cos\theta}{2\sin^{2}\theta}.

These solutions are of Type 3.

Abdulkadir Canatar, SueYeon Chung

Abstract
A key problem in deep learning and computational neuroscience is relating the geometrical properties of neural representations to task performance. Here, we consider this problem for continuous decoding tasks where neural variability may affect task precision. Using methods from statistical mechanics, we study the average-case learning curves for ε \varepsilon -insensitive Support Vector Regression ( ε \varepsilon -SVR) and discuss its capacity as a measure of linear decodability. Our analysis reveals a phase transition in the training error at a critical load, capturing the interplay between the tolerance parameter ε \varepsilon and neural variability. We uncover a double-descent phenomenon in the generalization error, showing that ε \varepsilon acts as a regularizer, both suppressing and shifting these peaks. Theoretical predictions are validated both on toy models and deep neural networks, extending the theory of Support Vector Machines to continuous tasks with inherent neural variability.



Introduction.—
Modern machine learning applications often require the ability to perform tasks that remain invariant under certain transformations of input data. For instance, decoding of a class should remain unaffected by variations in the object’s representations due to factors like translations
LeCun et al. 2015
. Deep neural networks achieve this by learning a meaningful representation of input data, from which decoding the information relevant to the task becomes easier. For instance, an effective representation’s decoding should be invariant to task-irrelevant transformations while remaining covariant with respect to task-relevant information. Understanding such geometric properties of neural representations is essential for unraveling the computational mechanisms implemented by neural networks
Bengio et al. 2013
;
Achille and Soatto 2018
;
Chung and Abbott 2021
.

Previous studies have explored the geometrical properties of neural representations in relation to invariant prediction, particularly focusing on the linear decodability under neural variability
Chung et al. 2018
;
Chung and Abbott 2021
;
Wakhloo et al. 2023
. However, these studies largely concentrate on classification tasks, despite the fact that decoding continuous variables, such as an object’s position or orientation, is essential in fields like control and robotics where precise and continuous outputs are critical. This leaves a gap in our understanding of how effective decoding of continuous variables can be achieved in the presence of neural variability.

In this letter, we study
\varepsilon
-insensitive Support Vector Regression (
\varepsilon
-SVR) algorithm
Drucker et al. 1996
;
Schölkopf et al. 2000
to analyze continuous tasks with input data variances. The
\varepsilon
-SVR task is similar to the ridge regression, except that the objective is satisfied when the prediction error lies within a tube of size
\varepsilon
(Fig.
1
). Here, the
tube size

\varepsilon
is a hyperparameter that defines the margin of tolerance for input deviations and needs to be fine-tuned. Using the replica method

;
Engel 2001
;
Mezard and Montanari 2009
, we develop an analytical theory of generalization in
\varepsilon
-SVR that relates the geometry of invariances to the precision of decoding a continuous variable. Our theory recovers the previous results on ridge regression in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0

Bordelon et al. 2020
;
Jacot et al. 2020
;
Canatar et al. 2021
;
Loureiro et al. 2021
;

;

Replica Calculation.—
For simplicity, we consider a joint Gaussian distribution of center and encoding representations as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\psi}}\\
\bar{\bm{\psi}}\end{bmatrix}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\\

(4)

Note that the solution to the objective in Eq.

\displaystyle E_{\infty}=\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{\mathbf{w}}_{*}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},\;{\mathbf{w}}_{*}={\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}},

Effective load

Effective tube size

5

Analysis of a Toy Data Model.—
To analyze our results and build intuition, we apply our theory to investigate the decoding precision in the presence of structured noise, which correlates with the coding direction. We consider a toy data model where the centers are drawn from

Double-Descent and Optimal Learning Rates.—
The analysis of
E_{g}
reveals a well-known phenomenon known as
double-descent

Belkin et al. 2018
;
Belkin et al. 2019
where the generalization worsens with more data near the critical point
\tilde{\alpha}=1

3

In ridge regression, this overfitting can be avoided by tuning the ridge parameter
\lambda

\varepsilon
also acts like a regularization. However, in contrast to the ridge penalty, which only suppresses the double-descent peak,
\varepsilon
also shifts the peak, implying that the tube size effectively controls whether the model is in over or under-parameterized regime (Fig.
3
b).

Our theory allows us to compute the optimal learning rates by finding the parameters
\lambda
and
\varepsilon
that minimize
E_{g}

\varepsilon
for SVR and optimal

for ridge regression which was also studied previously in

Discussion.—
Invariant decoding remains a critical challenge in machine learning and neuroscience, as real-world inputs frequently vary in ways that do not alter the target attribute. This study bridges the gap between representational geometry and its influence on continuous decoding tasks. By utilizing
\varepsilon
-SVR as a geometric probe in high-dimensional feature spaces, we employed average-case analysis to explore how the geometry of noise representations impacts decoding performance.

In particular, our analysis revealed that the phase transition in the training error can be controlled by the tube size parameter
\varepsilon
which also sets the target precision of the task. Larger
\varepsilon
values increase the model’s capacity by allowing more samples to be fit without error, but this comes at the cost of reduced precision in continuous variable decoding, as indicated by increased generalization error. The balance between training error and generalization error underscores the importance of tuning
\varepsilon
to optimize both model fit and predictive accuracy.

While our analysis currently relies on Gaussian assumptions and linear decoding strategies, future investigations should extend beyond these assumptions, addressing more realistic scenarios of coding in distributed neural representations.

Our theoretical framework reveals fundamental tradeoffs between precision and robustness in neural computations. The relationship we uncovered between representational geometry and decoding performance not only enhances our understanding of SVR algorithms but could also inform the design of more robust regression models and understand the nature of neural decoding algorithms where precise representations must be maintained despite inherent variability.

Problem Setup

Here, we study a family of regression problems and their generalization properties using the replica method. In our notation, we consider a training set
\mathcal{D}=\{({\mathbf{x}}^{\mu},\bar{y}^{\mu})\}_{\mu=1}^{P}
consisting of
P
i.i.d. drawn inputs
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mu}
and labels
\bar{y}^{\mu}
. In this work, we assume that the target labels are related to the inputs via

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{\mu}=\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu},\quad\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\equiv\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\bar{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(12)

where
\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}
denote
N
-dimensional
center
features as a function of input samples, and
\bar{\mathbf{w}}
denotes the
coding direction
. On the other hand, we consider a linear predictor

\displaystyle y^{\mu}={\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu},\quad{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\equiv{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(13)

where
{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}
denote the
N
-dimensional
encoding
features available to the predictor, and
{\mathbf{w}}
is to be learned.

Our generic regression task solves the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}}\frac{1}{2}\norm{\w}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\ell\left({\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\right),

(14)

where
\ell(x)
is the loss function. Below we list possible choices for
\ell(x)
:

•

Kernel Ridge Regression (KRR)
: For ridge regression,
\ell(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}
and the optimization problem becomes:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}}\frac{1}{2}\norm{\w}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\left({\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\right)^{2}.

(15)

Here,
\lambda\to 0
limit corresponds to the ridgeless (least-squares) regression, and the case with linear features (
{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{x}}
) corresponds to linear regression.

•

Support Vector Regression (SVR)
: Specifically, we consider
\varepsilon-
insensitive SVR where the loss vanishes if the error is less than some positive tolerance variable
\varepsilon
. Generically, the loss function is
\ell(x)=\frac{1}{n!}\max(0,\absolutevalue{x}-\varepsilon)^{n}
, and the optimization problem becomes:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}}\frac{1}{2}\norm{\w}^{2}+\frac{1}{n!\lambda}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\max\left(0,\absolutevalue{\w\cdot\bpsi^\mu- \bar\w\cdot\bar\bpsi^\mu}-\varepsilon\right)^{n},

(16)

where

are non-negative integers. Note that the case where

and
\varepsilon=0
is identical to KRR above.

A few notes are in order:

•

Our calculations require averaging over this inputs
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mu}
, which is intractable for general feature maps. However, we make a

that the statistics of

and
{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mu})
can be approximated by their second moments:

\displaystyle\braket{\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}}_{\mathbf{x}}=\frac{1}{N}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}},\quad\braket{{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}){\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}}_{\mathbf{x}}=\frac{1}{N}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},\quad\braket{{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}}_{\mathbf{x}}=\frac{1}{N}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}

(17)

and the final results will depend only on these quantities. This approximation simplifies our calculations, yields excellent agreement with experiments, and is also justified by the
Gaussian equivalence
theorems in high dimensional statistics where the covariance of non-linear features (kernels) can be summarized by an equivalent Gaussian statistics

;
Mei and Montanari 2022
;
Hu and Lu 2022
;

•

In our formulation, we excluded the effect of label noise in the training set

to avoid clutter, since the effect of label noise with variance
\sigma^{2}
can equivalently be studied by shifting
\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\to\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}+{\mathbf{n}}^{\mu}
with an independent random vector

.

•

We assume that the encoding representations depend on centers via

\displaystyle{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{A}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})+{\bm{\delta}}({\mathbf{x}})

(18)

Here,
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
is a deterministic projection matrix that models the relation between centers and encoding features. The noise representation

models the
variations
in the encoding features, and is the source of randomness in
{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})
for each fixed input
{\mathbf{x}}
.

•

While, in general,
{\bm{\delta}}({\mathbf{x}})
should be thought of as a random feature conditioned on each input
{\mathbf{x}}
, here we consider a noise model independent of centers. The simplest model for
{\bm{\delta}}({\mathbf{x}})
with input correlations is a linear random feature model
{\bm{\delta}}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{B}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})+{\bm{\delta}}_{0}
where
{\mathbf{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
is a random matrix, and
{\bm{\delta}}_{0}
is a random vector which are drawn independently from their respective distributions. The study of this model requires computing another quenched average over random features
{\mathbf{B}}
and is left for future work. See
Atanasov et al. 2022
;
Maloney et al. 2022
for the analysis of a similar model in ridge regression setting.

•

In particular, we will apply our theory to the case where
{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})=\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})+{\bm{\delta}}
where
{\bm{\delta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
models the variances in the inputs that are irrelevant to the target. The dimensionalities of the features
{\bm{\psi}}
and
\bar{\bm{\psi}}
are chosen to be the same for notational convenience, however, can be trivially applied to a more general setting with
{\bm{\psi}}={\mathbf{A}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}+{\bm{\delta}}
, since our final result will depend only on the covariance matrices in Eq.
17
and the coding direction
\bar{\mathbf{w}}
.

A.1
Replica Calculation

We set up a general framework for our calculation. The measure for the weights are denoted as
d\mu({\mathbf{w}})
, and the loss function
\ell\left({\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}\right)
is kept generic. Then, the partition function is given by

\displaystyle Z=\int d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}})e^{-\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\ell\left({\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\right)},\quad d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}})\equiv d\mu({\mathbf{w}})e^{-\beta{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}}

(19)

where we defined
d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}})
by including source terms to extract the mean and variance of the optimized weights.

First, we integrate in auxiliary variables to simplify the computation:

\displaystyle H_{\mu}\equiv{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}

(20)

and get:

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}})\prod_{\mu}dH_{\mu}\,e^{-\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\ell(H_{\mu})}\delta\left(H_{\mu}-{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}+\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=\int d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}})[d\mathcal{H}_{\mu}][d\hat{H}_{\mu}]\,e^{i\sum_{\mu}\hat{H}_{\mu}H_{\mu}}e^{-i\sum_{\mu}\hat{H}_{\mu}\left({\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\right)},

(21)

where we defined
d\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\equiv dH_{\mu}\,e^{-\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\ell(H_{\mu})}
and introduced the convention where
[dx^{a,b,\dots}]
represents product over free indices
[dx^{a,b,\dots}]\equiv\prod_{a,b,\dots}dx^{a,b,\dots}
. For example,
[d\mathcal{H}_{\mu}]\equiv\prod_{\mu}d\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
[d\hat{H}_{\mu}]\equiv\prod_{\mu}d\hat{H}_{\mu}
.

Next, we replicate the partition function to perform averages over the data distribution:

\displaystyle Z^{n}

\displaystyle=\int[d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})][d\mathcal{H}^{a}_{\mu}][d\hat{H}^{a}_{\mu}]\,e^{i\sum_{a,\mu}\hat{H}^{a}_{\mu}H^{a}_{\mu}}e^{-i\sum_{a,\mu}\hat{H}^{a}_{\mu}\left({\mathbf{w}}^{a}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\right)}.

(22)

Next, we define the
(2N)
-dimensional feature vector
{\bm{\gamma}}^{\mu}
and
(2N)
-dimensional weight vector
\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}
as

\displaystyle{\bm{\gamma}}^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\\
\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{w}}^{a}\\
-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\end{pmatrix},

(23)

so that for each sample, we have:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{w}}^{a}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}=\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{\mu}.

(24)

At this point, we make the Gaussian assumption where the statistics of
{\bm{\gamma}}^{\mu}
can be captured by its first and second moments:

\displaystyle\braket{{\bm{\gamma}}}=0,\quad\braket{{\bm{\gamma}}{\bm{\gamma}}^{\top}}=\begin{pmatrix}\braket{{\bm{\psi}}{\bm{\psi}}^{\top}}&\braket{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\top}}\\
\braket{\bar{\bm{\psi}}{\bm{\psi}}^{\top}}&\braket{\bar{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\top}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}/N&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}/N\\
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}/N&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}/N\end{pmatrix}\equiv{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}

(25)

where
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{(2N)\times(2N)}
is its covariance. With this notation, the partition function simplifies to

\displaystyle Z^{n}

\displaystyle=\int[d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})][d\mathcal{H}^{a}_{\mu}][d\hat{H}^{a}_{\mu}]\,e^{i\sum_{a,\mu}\hat{H}^{a}_{\mu}H^{a}_{\mu}}e^{-i\sum_{a,\mu}\hat{H}^{a}_{\mu}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{\mu}}

(26)

and with the assumption that each sample
{\bm{\gamma}}^{\mu}
is drawn i.i.d. from the normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}})
, its dataset average over
{\bm{\gamma}}^{\mu}
yields

\displaystyle\braket{Z^{n}}

\displaystyle=\int[d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})]\left[\int[d\mathcal{H}^{a}][d\hat{H}^{a}]\,e^{i\sum_{a}\hat{H}^{a}H^{a}}\Braket{e^{-i\sum_{a}\hat{H}^{a}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}}}_{{\bm{\gamma}}}\right]^{P}.

(27)

We define the order parameters
q^{a}=\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}
and consider their first and second moments:

\displaystyle\braket{q^{a}}=0,\quad C^{ab}=\braket{q^{a}q^{b}}=\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b},

(28)

Then the quenched average over
{\bm{\gamma}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}})
gives:

\displaystyle\braket{Z^{n}}

\displaystyle=\int[dC^{ab}][d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})]\delta\left(C^{ab}-\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b}\right)\left[\int[d\mathcal{H}^{a}][d\hat{H}^{a}]\,e^{i\sum_{a}\hat{H}^{a}H^{a}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b}\hat{H}^{a}C^{ab}\hat{H}^{a}}\right]^{{P}}

\displaystyle=\int[dC^{ab}][d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})]\delta\left(C^{ab}-\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b}\right)\left[\int[d\mathcal{H}^{a}]\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b}H^{a}\left(C^{ab}\right)^{-1}H^{b}-\frac{1}{2}\log\det(C^{ab})}\right]^{{P}},

(29)

where in the last line we performed the integral over
[d\hat{H}^{a}]
. Furthermore, we integrate in new variables
\hat{C}^{ab}
to replace the delta function:

\displaystyle\braket{Z^{n}}

\displaystyle=\int[dC^{ab}][d\hat{C}^{ab}][d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})]e^{i\sum_{ab}\hat{C}^{ab}\left(C^{ab}-\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b}\right)}\left[\int[d\mathcal{H}^{a}]\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b}H^{a}\left(C^{ab}\right)^{-1}H^{b}-\frac{1}{2}\log\det(C^{ab})}\right]^{{P}}

\displaystyle\equiv\int[dC^{ab}][d\hat{C}^{ab}]e^{-n\frac{\beta N}{2}(G_{0}+\alpha G_{1})},

(30)

where

\displaystyle G_{0}=-\frac{2}{n\beta N}\log(\int[d\tilde\mu(\w^a)] e^{i\sum_{ab}\hat C^{ab}\lrpar{C^{ab} - \bm{\cW}^a \bSigma_\bGamma\bm{\cW}^b}}),

\displaystyle[d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})]=\prod_{a}d\mu({\mathbf{w}}^{a})e^{-\beta{{\mathbf{w}}^{a}}^{\top}{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}},

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{w}}^{a}\\
-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\equiv\frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\\
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}&{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\end{pmatrix}

(31)

and

\displaystyle G_{1}=-\frac{2}{n\beta}\log(\int[d\cH^{a}] \,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b} H^{a} \lrpar{C^{ab}}^{-1} H^{b} -\frac{1}{2}\log\det(C^{ab})}),

\displaystyle[d\mathcal{H}^{a}]=\prod_{a}dH^{a}\,e^{-\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\ell(H^{a})}

(32)

The coefficients were chosen so that
G_{0}
and
G_{1}
remain
\mathcal{O}(1)
in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
while
\alpha\equiv P/N\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
. Next, we compute these two terms under the replica symmetric ansatz:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{C}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(Q{\mathbf{I}}_{n}+\beta C\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{C}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\beta N}}{2i}\left(\hat{Q}{\mathbf{I}}_{n}+\beta\hat{C}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right).

(33)

The
G_{0}
term:

Representing
{\mathbf{C}}
and
\hat{\mathbf{C}}
as
n\times n
-matrices with entries
C^{ab}
and
\hat{C}^{ab}
, the
G_{0}
term can be written as:

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2i}{n\beta N}\Tr\hat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{C}}-\frac{2}{n\beta N}\log(\int[d\tilde\mu(\w^a)] e^{-i\sum_{ab}\hat C^{ab}\bm{\cW}^a \bSigma_\bGamma\bm{\cW}^b})

(34)

Choosing
d\mu({\mathbf{w}}^{a})=\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\right)^{N/2}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\norm{\w^a}^{2}}
, the measure simplifies to

\displaystyle[d\tilde{\mu}({\mathbf{w}}^{a})]

\displaystyle=\prod_{a}d\mu({\mathbf{w}}^{a})e^{-\beta{{\mathbf{w}}^{a}}^{\top}{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}}=\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\right)^{nN/2}\prod_{a}d{\mathbf{w}}^{a}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\norm{\w^a}^{2}+2{{\mathbf{w}}^{a}}^{\top}{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right)}

(35)

Taking the constants out:

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2i}{n\beta N}\Tr\hat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{C}}-\frac{2}{n\beta N}\log I_{0}

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\right)^{nN/2}\int[d{\mathbf{w}}^{a}]e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{a}\left(\norm{\w^a}^{2}+2{{\mathbf{w}}^{a}}^{\top}{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right)-i\sum_{ab}\hat{C}^{ab}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b}}.

(36)

The integral
I_{0}
must be evaluated based on the structure of
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}
which are given above. Then the term
\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b}
can be expanded as:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{w}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{w}}^{b}+\bar{\mathbf{w}}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}-({\mathbf{w}}^{a}+{\mathbf{w}}^{b}){\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}

(37)

Then, the exponent in
I_{0}
can be written in a compact form by defining:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{w}}^{1}&{\mathbf{w}}^{2}&\dots&{\mathbf{w}}^{a}&\dots&{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\mathbf{w}}&\bar{\mathbf{w}}&\dots&\bar{\mathbf{w}}&\dots&\bar{\mathbf{w}}\end{pmatrix}=\bar{\mathbf{w}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{X}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2i}{\beta}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{C}},\quad{\mathbf{Y}}=\frac{2i}{\beta}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{C}},\quad{\mathbf{Z}}=\frac{2i}{\beta}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{C}},\quad{\mathbf{J}}={\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}

(38)

yielding

\displaystyle-i\sum_{a,b}\hat{C}^{ab}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{a}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{b}=-\frac{\beta}{2}\left({\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{W}}-2{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}}\bar{\mathbf{W}}+\bar{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}}\bar{\mathbf{W}}\right).

(39)

Plugging this in the integral, we get

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\right)^{nN/2}\int d{\mathbf{W}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left({\mathbf{W}}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{I}}+{\mathbf{X}}\right){\mathbf{W}}+2{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{J}}-{\mathbf{Y}}\right)\bar{\mathbf{W}}+\bar{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}}\bar{\mathbf{W}}\right)}.

(40)

The measure also becomes
[d{\mathbf{w}}^{a}]=d{\mathbf{W}}
and the integral over
nN
-dimensional space can be simply evaluated to:

\displaystyle\log I_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\det(\I+ \X)-\frac{\beta}{2}\bar{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}\left[{\mathbf{Z}}-\left({\mathbf{J}}-{\mathbf{Y}}\right)^{\top}\left({\mathbf{I}}+{\mathbf{X}}\right)^{-1}\left({\mathbf{J}}-{\mathbf{Y}}\right)\right]\bar{\mathbf{W}}.

(41)

Plugging in the replica symmetric ansatz
\hat{\mathbf{C}}=\frac{{\beta}N}{2i}\left(\hat{Q}{\mathbf{I}}_{n}+\beta\hat{C}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right)
(
A.1
), matrices
{\mathbf{X}}
,
{\mathbf{Y}}
and
{\mathbf{Z}}
simplify to

\displaystyle{\mathbf{X}}

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}+\beta\hat{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Y}}

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}+\beta\hat{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Z}}

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}+\beta\hat{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}.

(42)

The
\log\det(\I+ \X)
simplifies to

\displaystyle\log\det\left[({\mathbf{I}}_{N}+\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}})\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}\right]+\log\det\left[{\mathbf{I}}+\beta\hat{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}({\mathbf{I}}_{N}+\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}})^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right]

\displaystyle=n\log\det{\mathbf{G}}+\Tr\log(\I+ \beta\hat C \bSigma_{\bpsi} \G^{-1}\otimes\1_n\1_n^\top)

\displaystyle=n\beta N\left(\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\det{\mathbf{G}}+\hat{C}\frac{1}{N}\Tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right)+\mathcal{O}(n^{2})

(43)

where we defined
{\mathbf{G}}\equiv{\mathbf{I}}_{N}+\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}
, used the identity
\log\det{\mathbf{A}}=\Tr\log{\mathbf{A}}
(second line), and Taylor expanded the
\log
in small
n
(last line).

Next, we compute
\left({\mathbf{I}}+{\mathbf{X}}\right)^{-1}
in the limit
n\to 0
:

\displaystyle\left({\mathbf{I}}+{\mathbf{X}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\left({\mathbf{I}}_{N}+\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}\right)^{-1}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}\right)\left({\mathbf{I}}+\beta\hat{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}\right)\left({\mathbf{I}}-\beta\hat{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}+\mathcal{O}(n)\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}-\beta\hat{C}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}+\mathcal{O}(n).

(44)

Note that the second and third terms in
\log I_{0}
are of the form
\bar{\mathbf{w}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}(\dots)\bar{\mathbf{w}}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n}
, meaning that only terms like
{\mathbf{A}}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{n}
in parentheses survive in the leading order. Therefore, the second term in
\log I_{0}
becomes:

\displaystyle-\frac{n\beta}{2}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left[\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\left({\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\left({\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\right]\bar{\mathbf{w}}+\mathcal{O}(n^{2})

(45)

Then,
\log I_{0}
becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{n\beta N}\log I_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\det{\mathbf{G}}+\hat{C}\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}+\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left[\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\left({\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\left({\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\right],

(46)

where we introduced the normalized trace operation

\displaystyle\tr{\mathbf{M}}=\frac{1}{\dim{\mathbf{M}}}\Tr{\mathbf{M}}.

(47)

Finally, noting that the term
-\frac{2i}{n\beta N}\Tr\hat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{C}}
reduces to
-\frac{1}{\beta}Q\hat{Q}-\hat{C}Q-\hat{Q}C
, and keeping terms up to
\mathcal{O}({\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}})
,
G_{0}
becomes:

\displaystyle G_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{N}\log\det{\mathbf{G}}-Q\hat{Q}\right)-\hat{C}(Q-\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})-\hat{Q}\left(C-\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left[{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}\left(\hat{Q}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right){\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle+2\tr{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}\left(\hat{Q}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{I}}+\hat{Q}N{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}

(48)

At this point, we can make further simplification assuming that
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}
is full-rank and using the identity

\displaystyle\hat{Q}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}=({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}){\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}),

(49)

we get

\displaystyle G_{0}=

\displaystyle-\hat{C}(Q-\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})-\hat{Q}\left(C-\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right).

(50)

Finally, we define the irreducible error

\displaystyle E_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}

\displaystyle{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}

\displaystyle={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}},

(51)

where
{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}
is the target weights projected on the learnable subspace and
{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}
is the maximum explainable variance by the model. With these definitions, the equation for
G_{0}
finally simplifies to:

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=-\hat{C}(Q-\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})-\hat{Q}\left(C-E_{\infty}\right)+\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})+2\tr{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{I}}+\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},

\displaystyle{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}

\displaystyle={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}},

\displaystyle E_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},

(52)

The
G_{1}
term:

The
G_{1}
term is given by

\displaystyle G_{1}=-\frac{2}{n\beta}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\log\det(\C)+\log I_{1}\right),

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int[d\mathcal{H}^{a}]e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b}H^{a}\left(C^{ab}\right)^{-1}H^{b}},\quad[d\mathcal{H}^{a}]=\prod_{a}dH^{a}\,e^{-\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\ell(H^{a})}

(53)

Under the replica symmetric ansatz
{\mathbf{C}}=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(Q{\mathbf{I}}_{n}+\beta C\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right)
:

\displaystyle\log\det(\C)

\displaystyle=n\left(\log(Q/\beta)+\frac{\beta C}{Q}\right)+\mathcal{O}(n),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{C}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{Q}{\mathbf{I}}_{n}-\frac{\beta^{2}C}{Q^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}+\mathcal{O}(n),

(54)

where we used the identity
\log\det{\mathbf{A}}=\Tr\log{\mathbf{A}}
for the first expression and used the Sherman-Morrison formula for the second expression. Hence,
I_{1}
becomes

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int[d\mathcal{H}^{a}]e^{-\frac{\beta}{2Q}\sum_{a}(H^{a})^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}C}{2Q^{2}}\sum_{a,b}H^{a}H^{b}}.

(55)

Using the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation, this integral can be written as

\displaystyle I_{1}(C,C_{0})

\displaystyle=\int DT\left[\int d\mathcal{H}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2Q}H^{2}+\frac{\beta\sqrt{C}}{Q}HT}\right]^{n}

\displaystyle=\int DT\left[e^{\frac{\beta C}{2Q}T^{2}}\int d\mathcal{H}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2Q}(H-\sqrt{C}T)^{2}}\right]^{n}

\displaystyle=\int DT\left[e^{\frac{\beta C}{2Q}T^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\log(Q/\beta)+\log z(T)}\right]^{n},

(56)

where
DT=\frac{dT}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-T^{2}/2}
is the standard Gaussian measure and we defined

\displaystyle z(T)=\int d\mathcal{H}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2Q}(H-\sqrt{C}T)^{2}}=\int dH\sqrt{\frac{\beta C}{Q}}e^{-\frac{\beta C}{2Q}(H-T)^{2}-\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\ell(H\sqrt{C})},

(57)

where we redefined
H\to H\sqrt{C}
. Using
\int DTA^{n}=\int DT(1+n\log A)=1+n\int DT\log A
in the limit
n\to 0
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\log I_{1}=n\frac{\beta C}{2Q}+\frac{n}{2}\log(Q/\beta)+n\int DT\log z(T).

(58)

Inserting this expression in
G_{1}
, we simplify

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{n\beta}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\log\det(\C)+\log I_{1}\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\log(Q/\beta)+\frac{C}{Q}-\left(\frac{C}{Q}+\frac{1}{\beta}\log(Q/\beta)+\frac{2}{\beta}\int DT\log z(T)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\beta}\int DT\log z(T).

(59)

To evaluate this integral, we first express
z(T)
as

\displaystyle z(T)

\displaystyle=\int dH\sqrt{\frac{\beta C}{Q}}e^{-\beta L(H)},\quad L(H)=\frac{C}{2Q}\left[(H-T)^{2}+\frac{2Q}{\lambda C}\ell(H\sqrt{C})\right].

(60)

Note that as
\beta\to\infty
, the integral concentrates around the saddle point
H^{*}=\argmin_{H}L(H)
of
L(H)
. Plugging this saddle point back,
G_{1}
becomes

\displaystyle G_{1}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\log(\frac{\beta C}{Q})+2\Braket{L^{*}(T)}_{T},

(61)

where

\displaystyle L^{*}(T)\equiv L\left(H^{*}(T)\right),\quad H^{*}(T)=\argmin_{H}\frac{C}{2Q}\left[(H-T)^{2}+\frac{2Q}{\lambda C}\ell(H\sqrt{C})\right].

(62)

We will consider the squared
\varepsilon
-insensitive loss function:

\displaystyle\ell(x)=\frac{1}{n!}\max(0,\absolutevalue{x}-\varepsilon)^{n}.

(63)

Then, we have

\displaystyle L(H)=\frac{C}{2Q}\left[(H-T)^{2}+\tilde{\lambda}\frac{2}{n!}\max(0,\absolutevalue{H}-\tilde{\varepsilon})^{n}\right]

(64)

where we defined the following effective quantities:

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}\equiv\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{C}},\quad\tilde{\lambda}\equiv\frac{Q}{\lambda}C^{\frac{n-2}{2}},

(65)

where
\tilde{\varepsilon}
is the effective slack variable; since
C
is the generalization error, having
\varepsilon^{2}\gg C
effectively corresponds to having an infinite slack variable
\tilde{\varepsilon}
. Similarly,
\tilde{\lambda}
is the effective regularization. For the following, we solve the saddle point equations for
n=2
. In terms of the quantities
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{\lambda}
, the saddle point
H^{*}(T)
and
L(H^{*})
are given by:

\displaystyle H^{*}(T)=\begin{cases}\frac{T+\tilde{\varepsilon}\tilde{\lambda}}{1+\tilde{\lambda}},&\tilde{\varepsilon}<T<\infty\\
\\
T,&-\tilde{\varepsilon}<T<\tilde{\varepsilon}\\
\\
\frac{T-\tilde{\varepsilon}\tilde{\lambda}}{1+\tilde{\lambda}},&-\infty<T<-\tilde{\varepsilon}\end{cases},\qquad L(H^{*})=\begin{cases}\frac{C}{2Q}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{1+\tilde{\lambda}}(\absolutevalue{T}-\tilde{\varepsilon})^{2},&\tilde{\varepsilon}<\absolutevalue{T}\\
\\
0,&\absolutevalue{T}<\tilde{\varepsilon}\end{cases}

(66)

Now, we can evaluate the term
\Braket{L(H^{*})}_{T}
as:

\displaystyle\Braket{L(H^{*})}_{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\frac{C}{2Q}\frac{\left(T-\tilde{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}}{1+\tilde{\lambda}}e^{-T^{2}/2}dT

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{2Q}\frac{1}{1+\tilde{\lambda}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\left[\int_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\left(T-\tilde{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}e^{-T^{2}/2}dT\right].

(67)

Simplifying this in terms of error functions, we get:

\displaystyle G_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{Q}\frac{1}{1+\tilde{\lambda}}\left[(1+\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2})\erfc\left(\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)-\tilde{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}}\right]-\frac{1}{\beta}\log(\frac{\beta C}{Q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{Q+\lambda}f(\tilde{\varepsilon})g(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{\beta}\log(\frac{\beta C}{Q})

(68)

where we defined the following functions for simplicity

\displaystyle f(\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\erfc\left(\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{2}}\right),\quad g(\tilde{\varepsilon})=1+\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}-\tilde{\varepsilon}\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}}}{\erfc\left(\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)}

(69)

A.2
Combining all terms

In the thermodynamic limit
P\to\infty
, the final partition function after the replica symmetric ansatz becomes:

\displaystyle\braket{\log Z}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{\braket{Z^{n}}-1}{n}=-\frac{\beta N}{2}S^{*},

(70)

where
S^{*}
is obtained by solving the following saddle point equation

\displaystyle S^{*}=

\displaystyle\extr_{C,\hat{C},Q,\hat{Q}}\left[G_{0}+\alpha G_{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{N}\log\det{\mathbf{G}}-Q\hat{Q}-\alpha\log(\frac{\beta C}{Q})\right)\right]

\displaystyle G_{0}=

\displaystyle-\hat{C}(Q-\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})-\hat{Q}\left(C-E_{\infty}\right)+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}),

\displaystyle G_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{Q+\lambda}f(\tilde{\varepsilon})g(\tilde{\varepsilon}),

(71)

where we have the following definitions

\displaystyle E_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{I}}+\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},\quad\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{C}},\quad{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}

(72)

Saddle Point Equations

We next obtain the saddle point equations for
S
. Let us first minimize for
\hat{C}
:

•

Minimizing with respect to
\hat{C}
gives
\boxed{Q=\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}}
.

•

Minimizing with respect to
\hat{Q}
gives:

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle-\hat{C}\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}+E_{\infty}+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

(73)

•

Minimizing with respect to
C
gives

\displaystyle\boxed{\hat{Q}=\frac{\alpha f(\tilde{\varepsilon})}{Q+\lambda}}

(74)

•

Minimizing with respect to

gives:

\displaystyle\boxed{\hat{C}=-\frac{\alpha f(\tilde{\varepsilon})}{(Q+\lambda)^{2}}Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})}

•

As a reminder, we had the following definitions

\displaystyle f(\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\erfc\left(\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{2}}\right),\quad g(\tilde{\varepsilon})=1+\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}-\tilde{\varepsilon}\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}}}{\erfc\left(\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)},\quad\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{C}},

\displaystyle={\mathbf{I}}+\hat{Q}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},\quad{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}},

\displaystyle=\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}

(76)

•

Notice that both
\hat{C}
and

are functions of
C
and

. Hence, plugging these terms in, we get two coupled self-consistent equations as above. In general, it is challenging to solve these equations analytically.

•

At the saddle point, we have

\displaystyle S^{*}

\displaystyle=\extr_{C,\hat{C},Q,\hat{Q}}\left[G_{0}+\alpha G_{1}\right]

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}\left(E_{\infty}-C\right)+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}\left(E_{\infty}+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}-C\right)

\displaystyle G_{1}

A.4
Simplifications

Let us define the following quantities which appear in the solution

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}=

\displaystyle\lambda+\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1},\quad\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{C}},\quad\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha f(\tilde{\varepsilon}),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{I}}+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},\quad\gamma=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

(78)

Then the saddle point equations are compactly written as

\displaystyle Q=

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda,\quad\hat{Q}=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}},\quad\hat{C}=-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon}),\quad C=\frac{1}{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})=E_{\infty}+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

(79)

In terms of these, the action becomes:

\displaystyle\braket{\log Z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta N}{2}S,

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left[C(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})-1)+E_{\infty}+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left[Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})(1-\gamma)+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})(1-\gamma)+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right)^{2}

(80)

Note that we can compute the following average quantities :

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{N}\frac{\partial\braket{\log Z}}{\partial\beta}

\displaystyle=\Braket{\frac{1}{N}\norm{\w}^{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{\lambda}E_{tr}}=S

\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda}{\beta N}\frac{\partial\braket{\log Z}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\Braket{\frac{2\alpha}{\lambda}E_{tr}}=-\lambda\frac{\partial S}{\partial\lambda},

(81)

where the average training error is defined as
\frac{1}{P}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\ell\left({\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}^{\mu}\right)

A.5
Computing Variations

We have two coupled self-consistent equations in the form of

\displaystyle{\tilde{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}({\tilde{\lambda}},\tilde{\varepsilon},x)\equiv\lambda+\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}({\tilde{\lambda}},\tilde{\varepsilon},x)\equiv\varepsilon\sqrt{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})^{-1/2}

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}({\tilde{\lambda}},\tilde{\varepsilon},x)\equiv\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left[\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})-1)+E_{\infty}+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})=E_{\infty}+\tr\left({{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}+2{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right){{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

(82)

where
x
collectively denotes any explicit variable. Their variations with respect to
x
, then is given by:

\displaystyle\delta{\tilde{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}}\left(\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\delta{\tilde{\varepsilon}}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}\delta x\right)

\displaystyle\delta{\tilde{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}}\left(\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}\delta{\tilde{\lambda}}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}\delta x\right)

\displaystyle\delta S

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{H}\delta{\tilde{\lambda}}+\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}\delta{\tilde{\varepsilon}}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}\delta x.

(83)

Solving these equations, we get

\displaystyle\delta{\tilde{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G})\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}+\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}}{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F})(1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G})-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}\delta x

\displaystyle\delta{\tilde{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F})\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}+\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}}{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F})(1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G})-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}\delta x.

(84)

We need to compute
\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}
,
\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}
,
\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}
,
\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}
. First, we compute the derivatives of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}=\gamma,\quad\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}{\tilde{\lambda}}\gamma,\quad\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{H}=-\frac{f}{{\tilde{\lambda}}^{2}}\frac{g(1-\gamma)}{1-g\gamma}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}}),\quad\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{g\gamma}{1-g\gamma}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}}).

(85)

Note that:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}}{\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{H}}=-\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}}=\frac{f^{\prime}{\tilde{\lambda}}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}

(86)

Inserting the variation of
\delta\tilde{\lambda}
, we get the simplification:

\displaystyle\delta S

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{H}\left(\delta{\tilde{\lambda}}-\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}}\delta{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\right)+\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}\delta x=\left(\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{H}}{1-\gamma}\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}\right)\delta x

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{fg}{{\tilde{\lambda}}^{2}}\frac{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{1-g\gamma}\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}\right)\delta x=\left(-f(\tilde{\varepsilon})g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\frac{C}{{\tilde{\lambda}}^{2}}\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}\right)\delta x.

(87)

The observables from the action hence only depend on
\mathcal{F}
. This provides a huge simplification for computing first order derivatives of
\braket{\log Z}
.

A.6
Observables

Here, we compute the expected values of the observables from the partition function using our calculations above. First, we are interested in the following observables from the partition function:

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{\lambda}E_{tr}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda}{\beta N}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\braket{\log Z}=-\lambda\frac{\partial S}{\partial\lambda}=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})\frac{\lambda}{{\tilde{\lambda}}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\braket{\bar{\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}}

From these observables we also obtain

\displaystyle\braket{{\mathbf{w}}}

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right)\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\bar{\mathbf{w}}=N\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\braket{\norm{\w}^{2}}=S-\frac{2\alpha}{\lambda}E_{tr}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})\left(1-\gamma-\frac{\lambda}{\tilde{\lambda}}\right)+\frac{1}{N}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)^{\top}\left({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\bar{\mathbf{w}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\braket{{\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}}-\frac{1}{N}\braket{{\mathbf{w}}}\braket{{\mathbf{w}}}^{\top}

\displaystyle=Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

(89)

where we used the identity
\delta\equiv 1-\gamma-\frac{\lambda}{\tilde{\lambda}}=\frac{\Tr{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}}{\tilde{\lambda}}
at the last line.

Finally, we compute generalization error as

\displaystyle E_{g}

\displaystyle=\Braket{\mathbb{E}({\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}})^{2}}=\Braket{{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{w}}}+\Braket{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}}-2\Braket{{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}}

\displaystyle=Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma+\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left[{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-2{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right)\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)+\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}\left({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right)^{2}\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\right]\bar{\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle=Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma+\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left[{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}\left(2{\mathbf{I}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}){\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\left({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\right)\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\right]\bar{\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle=Cg(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma+\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left[{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-2}\right)\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)\right]\bar{\mathbf{w}}

B.1
Computing Variations

Again we start with the two coupled self-consistent equations in the form of

\displaystyle{\tilde{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}({\tilde{\lambda}},\tilde{\varepsilon},x)\equiv\lambda+\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}({\tilde{\lambda}},\tilde{\varepsilon},x)\equiv\varepsilon\sqrt{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})=E_{\infty}+\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

(94)

where
x
collectively denotes any explicit variable, and we omitted the terms with
{\mathbf{J}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}
. We found earlier that the variations of
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
are given by

\displaystyle\delta{\tilde{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G})\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}+\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}}{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F})(1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G})-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}\delta x

\displaystyle\delta{\tilde{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F})\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}+\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}}{(1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F})(1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G})-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}\delta x.

(95)

and that the derivatives
\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}
and
\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}
are given by:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}=\gamma,\quad\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}{\tilde{\lambda}}\gamma.

(96)

Next, we compute the derivative
\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\log\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}=-\frac{1}{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)+\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{C}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[\gamma\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(f(\tilde{\varepsilon})g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}{f(\tilde{\varepsilon})}+f(\tilde{\varepsilon})g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\frac{\gamma}{f(\tilde{\varepsilon})}+\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{C}\right].

(97)

Here,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(f(\tilde{\varepsilon})g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}{f(\tilde{\varepsilon})}=-\frac{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}{\tilde{\varepsilon}},\qquad\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\frac{\gamma}{f(\tilde{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\gamma}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f^{2}}\frac{\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\Tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\Tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}},

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})=-2\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\left(\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\mathbf{G}}\right){\mathbf{G}}^{-1},\qquad\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\mathbf{G}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},

(98)

where we defined
{\mathbf{M}}\equiv{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}
to shorten the notation. Inserting these back,

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\log\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[-\frac{2\gamma(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}-\frac{2\gamma g(\tilde{\varepsilon})}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{C}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[{\gamma(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}+\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\gamma g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{C}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}+\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\frac{\gamma g(\tilde{\varepsilon})}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)C}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1-\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}+\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\frac{\gamma g(\tilde{\varepsilon})}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}\right)

(99)

Since
\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\log\mathcal{G}=\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{G}}=\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}
, we get:

\displaystyle\boxed{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}=\frac{\gamma(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}+\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\frac{\gamma g(\tilde{\varepsilon})}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}\right)}

(100)

Next, we compute the derivative
\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\log\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}=-\frac{1}{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\gamma+\frac{\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{C}\right]

(101)

Here, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\gamma

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\gamma}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(1-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\right),\quad\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})=-2\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\left(\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}{\mathbf{G}}\right){\mathbf{G}}^{-1},\quad\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}{\mathbf{G}}=-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}^{2}}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}.

(102)

Inserting these

\displaystyle{\tilde{\lambda}}\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\log\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\tilde{\lambda}}}{2(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[-\frac{2g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(1-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{2\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}^{2}}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{C}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{C}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}\right)

(103)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\boxed{\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\mathcal{G}=\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}\right)\right)}.

(104)

Let us focus on the following term

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\left(\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{3}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{-1})}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}-\left(\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\mathcal{W}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}-\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})

(105)

where we defined.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})

\displaystyle=g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{C}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}}\left(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\frac{{\tilde{\lambda}}}{C}\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}}\right)

(106)

This conveniently simplifies
\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\mathcal{G}
to:

\displaystyle\boxed{\partial_{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\mathcal{G}=\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}

(107)

Similarly,
\partial_{{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\mathcal{G}
becomes:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}+\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}-\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left(\frac{f}{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon}))+g(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})\right)

(108)

Using the identity

\displaystyle g=1+\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}+\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f},

(109)

this simplifies
\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}
to

\displaystyle\boxed{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}=\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[h(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})\right]}

(110)

where we defined the function

\displaystyle h(\tilde{\varepsilon})\equiv\tilde{\varepsilon}\left(\tilde{\varepsilon}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{f}{f^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{(g(\tilde{\varepsilon})-1)^{2}-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})-1-\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}.

(111)

We obtain the denominator in Eq.
B.1
as

\displaystyle 1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}-\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}}=1-\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[h(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\frac{\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{1-\gamma}\right],

(112)

Finally we get the variation of
\tilde{\varepsilon}
as

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}-\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}}}\delta x+\frac{\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}}\partial_{x}\mathcal{F}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{G}-\frac{\partial_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{F}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}}{1-\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}}}\delta x

(113)

B.2
Optimal Ridge Paremeter

In order to compute the optimal ridge parameter, we need to compute the derivative of generalization error
C
with respect to
\lambda
when the tube size is zero. Since

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}C=-2C\partial_{\lambda}\log\tilde{\varepsilon},

(114)

it suffices to compute
\partial_{\lambda}\tilde{\varepsilon}
using Eq.
113

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\log\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[h(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\frac{\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{1-\gamma}\right]}\frac{1}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})=\frac{1}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)}\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}}),

(115)

where we used the fact that
\varepsilon=0
in the last equality. Hence, the condition for optimal ridge becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}C=-\frac{2C}{{\tilde{\lambda}}}\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)}\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})=0,

(116)

which implies another self-consistent equation for effective ridge parameter

\displaystyle{\tilde{\lambda}}_{opt}=C\frac{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}}{\tr{\mathbf{M}}^{2}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}{{\mathbf{w}}_{*}}^{\top}}

(117)

replacing the original equation
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda+\tr{\mathbf{M}}
.

B.3
Optimal Tube Size

For optimal SVR, we set
\lambda=0
and consider the derivative of generalization error with respect to
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}C=\frac{2C}{\varepsilon}-2C\partial_{\varepsilon}\log\tilde{\varepsilon},

(118)

where
\partial_{\varepsilon}\log\tilde{\varepsilon}
is given by (Eq.
113
)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}\log\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{1-\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[h(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\frac{\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{1-\gamma}\right]},

(119)

yielding

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}C=-2\sqrt{C}\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\frac{\left[h(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\frac{\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{1-\gamma}\right]}{1-\frac{f^{\prime}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{f}\frac{\gamma}{(1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma)}\left[h(\tilde{\varepsilon})-\frac{\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\varepsilon},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{1-\gamma}\right]}=0.

(120)

This implies the self-consistency condition for effective tube size

\displaystyle h({\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{opt})=\frac{\mathcal{A}({\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{opt},{\tilde{\lambda}})}{1-\gamma},

(121)

complemented with the self-consistent equation
\tilde{\lambda}=\tr{\mathbf{M}}
.

Appendix C
Toy Data Model

In order to analyze our result, we consider a toy data model where the center and noise model are given by

\displaystyle{\bm{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\bm{\psi}}+\sigma{\bm{\delta}}

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{\psi}}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\mathbf{I}}),\quad{\bm{\delta}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\delta}})

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\delta}}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta){\mathbf{P}}+\beta({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{P}})=(1-\beta){\mathbf{I}}+\left(2\beta-1\right)({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{P}}),

(122)

where
{\mathbf{P}}={\mathbf{I}}-\frac{1}{N}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}
is the projection matrix to the subspace orthogonal to the coding direction. Here, we assumed that the target weights are normalized such that

\displaystyle\Tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}=\Tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}=\frac{1}{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}\Tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}=N

(123)

In this model, the noise features
{\bm{\delta}}
are independent of centers
\bar{\bm{\psi}}
, but their covariance is allowed to correlate with the coding direction
\bar{\mathbf{w}}
. In this case, we get the following covariance matrices:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}

\displaystyle={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}={\mathbf{I}},

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}

\displaystyle={\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}+\sigma^{2}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}=\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right){\mathbf{I}}+\sigma^{2}\left(2\beta-1\right)\frac{1}{N}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}.

(124)

Cleaning the last expression, we get
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}
and its inverse as:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right){\mathbf{P}}+\left(1+\beta\sigma^{2}\right)({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{P}}),

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}{\mathbf{P}}+\frac{1}{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{P}})

(125)

Hence the error at infinity becomes

\displaystyle E_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}\left({\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\right)=\frac{\beta\sigma^{2}}{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}

(126)

We also compute
{\mathbf{G}}
and
{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}
as:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}

\displaystyle=\left[1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)\right]{\mathbf{P}}+\left[1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\beta\sigma^{2}\right)\right]({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{P}})

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)}{\mathbf{P}}+\frac{1}{1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\beta\sigma^{2}\right)}({\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{P}})

(127)

Plugging these into our formula we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\lambda+\frac{1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}{1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)}+\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}{1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\beta\sigma^{2}\right)}-\frac{1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}{1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}\left(\frac{\beta\sigma^{2}}{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}+\frac{1}{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\beta\sigma^{2}\right)\right)^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}\frac{\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)^{2}}{\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)\right)^{2}}+\frac{1}{N}\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}\left(\frac{\left(1+\beta\sigma^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\beta\sigma^{2}\right)\right)^{2}}-\frac{\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)^{2}}{\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}\left(1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)\right)\right)^{2}}\right).

(128)

For
N\gg 1
, we can ignore
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
terms. Furthermore, we redefine
\tilde{\lambda}\to\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}
to simplify the self-consistent equations and obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\lambda}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}\left(\frac{\beta\sigma^{2}}{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}+\frac{1}{1+\beta\sigma^{2}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{\left(\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{\alpha}\frac{1+\sigma^{2}\beta}{1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}\right)^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\lambda})^{2}}

(129)

Ridgeless limit as exactly solvable model

The solution to the self-consistent equation for
\tilde{\lambda}
in the limit
\lambda\to 0
becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}=\begin{cases}1-\tilde{\alpha},&\tilde{\alpha}<1\\
0,&\tilde{\alpha}\geq 1\end{cases},

(130)

and the self-consistent equation for
\tilde{\varepsilon}
simplifies to:

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{\infty}}}\sqrt{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\tilde{\alpha}}\left(1+\frac{1-E_{\infty}}{E_{\infty}}\frac{1}{(1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{1-\tilde{\alpha}}\frac{1+\sigma^{2}\beta}{1+\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)})^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}&,\tilde{\alpha}<1\\
\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{\infty}}}\sqrt{1-{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})}{\tilde{\alpha}}^{-1}}&,\tilde{\alpha}\geq 1\end{cases}.

(131)

Assuming non-zero
E_{\infty}>0
, this reduces to the following transcendental equation at the capacity
\tilde{\alpha}=1

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}=d^{\prime}\sqrt{1-{g(\tilde{\varepsilon})}},

(132)

where we defined the discriminability
d^{\prime}\equiv\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{E_{\infty}}}
. We asymptotically extract its behavior for large and small
d^{\prime}
. Note that the range of
\tilde{\varepsilon}
is
[0,d^{\prime})
, since the function
1\geq g(x)>0
.

For large
d^{\prime}\gg 1
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
gets large and approaches to
d^{\prime}
. Expanding the function
g
in this limit as
g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\approx\frac{2}{\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}}
, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}\approx d^{\prime}(1-d^{\prime-2}),\quad d^{\prime}\gg 1.

(133)

Similarly, for small
d^{\prime}\ll 1
, the argument approaches to zero and admits the expansion
g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\approx 1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\tilde{\varepsilon}
. Replacing this and squaring both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}\approx\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}d^{\prime 2},\quad d^{\prime}\ll 1.

(134)

S1
, we confirm the validity of this approximation against its numerical solution.

Appendix D
Spectral Decomposition

Previously, we had formulated our theory in terms of the covariance matrices of representations where we defined the functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\tr{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-1},\;\;{\mathbf{G}}={\mathbf{I}}+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{\tilde{\lambda}}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}},\;\;\gamma=\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{\infty}+\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{\mathbf{w}}_{*}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}{\mathbf{G}}^{-2}}{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}

\displaystyle E_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\tr\bar{\mathbf{w}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}-\tr{\mathbf{w}}_{*}{\mathbf{w}}_{*}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}},\quad{\mathbf{w}}_{*}={\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}\bar{\mathbf{w}}

(135)

where
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
obey the coupled self-consistent equations given in Eq.
5
. Here, we cast our formulas in terms of the spectral properties of representations. Following the kernel notation in
Bordelon et al. 2020
;
Canatar et al. 2021
, the covariance matrices above are given by:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}}

\displaystyle=\int{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}){\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}d\mu({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bar{\bm{\psi}}}

\displaystyle=\int\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}d\mu({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\psi}}\bar{\bm{\psi}}}

\displaystyle=\int{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}d\mu({\mathbf{x}}),

(136)

where
d\mu({\mathbf{x}})
is the distribution of the inputs and we treat representations
{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})
and
\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})
as feature maps. We also express the target function and predictor as

\displaystyle\bar{y}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}),

\displaystyle y({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle={\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}).

(137)

Note that the asymptotic error is simply the variance of the target function minus the variance of its projection to the learnable subspace:

\displaystyle E_{\infty}=\norm{\bar y(\x)}_{2}^{2}-\norm{ \bar y_\parallel(\x)}_{2}^{2}=\norm{ \bar y_\perp(\x)}_{2}^{2},

(138)

which is the unexplainable variance as
\alpha\to\infty
. Note that the explainable portion of the target function can also be obtained by (Eq.
D
):

\displaystyle\bar{y}_{\parallel}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{w}}^{*}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})=\int d\mu({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\,\bar{y}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\,\left({\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}^{-1}{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})\right),

(139)

where the term in the parenthesis can be considered as a projection kernel in the space of square-integrable functions. This allows us to diagonalize our formulas in the basis of
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{\psi}}
.

We want to study generalization error in regards to task-model alignment and spectral bias
Canatar et al. 2021
and hence need to obtain the decomposition of the target in the space of square-integrable functions expressable by the feature map
{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})
via regression. These functions belong to the function space associated with the inner-product kernel
K({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})={\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})
and can be diagonalized as

\displaystyle K({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})={\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}{\bm{\psi}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\phi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime});\quad\int\phi_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\phi_{j}({\mathbf{x}})d\mu({\mathbf{x}})=\delta_{ij},

(140)

where
\{\lambda_{i}\}
are non-negative eigenvalues of the kernel and
\{\phi_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\}
is a set of complete eigenfunctions with respect to the measure
d\mu({\mathbf{x}})
. While there are infinitely many eigenfunctions with respect to
d\mu({\mathbf{x}})
, there at most
\dim({\bm{\psi}})
non-zero eigenvalues. We define the index set
\mathcal{I}^{+}=\{i\,|\,\lambda_{i}>0\}
for positive eigenvalues and
\mathcal{I}^{0}=\{i\,|\,\lambda_{i}=0\}
for vanishing eigenvalues. On the other hand, target function generically may have infinitely many components

\displaystyle\bar{y}({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{i}\bar{a}_{i}\phi_{i}({\mathbf{x}});\quad\bar{a}_{i}=\int\bar{y}({\mathbf{x}})\phi_{i}({\mathbf{x}})d\mu({\mathbf{x}}),

(141)

where
\{\bar{a}_{i}\}
are target coefficients.

Then the equations in Eq.
D
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{+}}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{\alpha}\lambda_{i}},\;\;\gamma=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{+}}\frac{\tilde{\alpha}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{(\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{\alpha}\lambda_{i})^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{0}}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{+}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{(\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{\alpha}\lambda_{i})^{2}}\bar{a}_{i}^{2}\right),

(142)

which exactly matches the equations from
Canatar et al. 2021
for
\varepsilon=0
up to rescaling
\lambda_{i}\to N\lambda_{i}
.

This form enables us to analyze the generalization error from an error mode geometry perspective introduced in
Canatar et al. 2024
. Error mode geometry relates linear predictivity to the geometric properties of representations based on the observation that the generalization error for ridge regression can be decomposed as a sum of mode errors associated with each spectral component
Bordelon et al. 2020
;
Canatar et al. 2021
;
Canatar et al. 2024
. Error modes are defined as

\displaystyle W_{i}(\tilde{\alpha})\equiv\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{1-g(\tilde{\varepsilon})\gamma}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}^{2}}{(\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{\alpha}\lambda_{i})^{2}},\quad E_{g}=\sum_{i}W_{i}(\tilde{\alpha})

(143)

and corresponds to the unexplained variance in the
i^{\text{th}}
eigendirection. Note that with increasing
\alpha
, the contribution of each mode
\bar{a}_{i}^{2}
decreases with a rate proportional to the inverse eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}^{-1}
associated with that mode, revealing the fact that variances associated with small eigenvalues require more samples to be reduced. Error mode geometry refers to the summarization of these error modes in terms of radius and dimensionality
defined as:

\displaystyle R_{em}=\sqrt{\sum_{i}W_{i}(\tilde{\alpha})^{2}},\quad D_{em}=\frac{\left(\sum_{i}W_{i}(\tilde{\alpha})\right)^{2}}{\sum_{i}W_{i}(\tilde{\alpha})^{2}}

(144)

which explicitly relates to the prediction error
E_{g}=R_{em}\sqrt{D_{em}}
.

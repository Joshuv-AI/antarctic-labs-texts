Decomposing the Automorphism group of the surreal numbers
Elliot Kaplan, Lothar Sebastian Krapp, Michele Serra

Abstract
. We study the automorphism group of the field of surreal numbers. Our main structure theorem presents a decomposition of this group into a product of five significant factors. Using the representation of surreal numbers as generalized power series via their Conway normal form, we apply results on Hahn fields and groups from the literature in order to obtain this decomposition. Moreover, we provide explicit descriptions of the individual factors enabling us to construct automorphisms on the field of surreal numbers from simpler components. We then extend our study to strongly linear automorphisms in connection to derivations, as well as automorphisms that preserve further exponential structure on the surreals.

1.
Introduction

The ordered field of surreal numbers
\mathbf{No}
was first introduced and systematically studied in Conway’s seminal work
[
9
]
.
Initially constructed from basic order principles, the proper class
\mathbf{No}
can be endowed with a remarkably rich order-theoretic and algebraic structure and thus serves as a universal domain within the examination of ordered algebraic structures. Prominently,
\mathbf{No}
encompasses both the class
\mathbf{On}
of ordinal numbers as an ordered subclass and the field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers as an ordered subfield. See Gonshor
[
14
]
for an extensive treatment of
\mathbf{No}
.

While there are several equivalent representations of surreal numbers, e.g. by sign sequences of ordinal length or by cuts between sets of surreals, in this work we consider surreal numbers as generalized power series. This representation is possible due to the omega map
x\mapsto\omega^{x}
on
\mathbf{No}
, which assigns to each surreal the unique simplest positive representative of some archimedean equivalence class. By means of the omega map, any surreal
a
can be expressed uniquely in its Conway normal form

a=\sum_{\beta<\alpha}r_{\beta}\cdot\omega^{y_{\beta}},

where
\alpha
is an ordinal number,
(y_{\beta})_{\beta<\alpha}
is a decreasing sequence in
\mathbf{No}
and
r_{\beta}
are non-zero coefficients from
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
9
, Theorem 21]
,
[
14
, Theorem 5.6]
,
[
13
, Theorem 14]
).
Via the Conway normal form, the field of surreal numbers can be regarded as a field of generalized power series – a concept originally dating back to Hahn’s influential work
[
15
]
on non-archimedean ordered systems.
Even Gonshor himself points out that
“for many purposes we can simply work with these generalized power series and ignore what surreal numbers are in the first place”

[
14
, page 71]
.
The largest term
r_{0}\cdot\omega^{y_{0}}
in the Conway normal form of
a\neq 0
determines both the archimedean equivalence class and the sign of
a
: the element
\omega^{y_{0}}
is the simplest positive representative of the archimedean equivalence class of
a
, and
a
has positive sign if and only if
r_{0}
is positive. From a valuation theoretic point of view, this gives rise to
\mathbf{No}
as the ordered Hahn field
\mathbb{R}\!\left(\!\left(\mathbf{No}\right)\!\right)
with ordered residue field
\mathbb{R}
and ordered additive value group
\mathbf{No}
(under the natural valuation).

In this work, we initiate a study of the automorphism group of the field
\mathbf{No}
.
From an algebraic perspective, the motivation behind studying automorphism groups of fields is well-known, in particular in the context of Galois Theory. Especially for a highly complex field such as the surreals, we hope that the systematic analysis of automorphisms sheds further light on the (algebraic) structure and symmetries of
\mathbf{No}
. The more recent works
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
(based on the third author’s doctoral thesis
[
20
]
) prompted us to start our investigation. In these works, the automorphism groups of Hahn fields and Hahn groups are systematically dissected.
Based on this structural analysis of automorphism groups, we derive our main result (
Theorem

3.11
) presenting a decomposition of the automorphism group of the field
\mathbf{No}
into the product of the following five factors (where semidirect products are particularly highlighted):

For instance by

\mathbf{No}

Moreover, we scrutinize all factors further in order to obtain explicit descriptions of automorphisms on
\mathbf{No}
arising from these particular factors. Overall, we point out how automorphisms on
\mathbf{No}
are naturally generated as the composition of simpler automorphisms based on
\mathbf{No}
as a Hahn field and Hahn group.

In
Section

4
, we focus on the first factor of the decomposition, in particular, strongly linear automorphisms (automorphisms that commute with infinite sums). We discuss the connection between strongly linear automorphisms and certain derivations, presented in
[

. We use this correspondence to construct strongly linear automorphisms (

). We also construct an automorphism that is not strongly linear (

This partially answers a question of Matusinski.
In

We show that automorphisms preserving the total ordering and simplicity ordering are trivial (

). We leave as questions a complete description of the automorphisms of these richer structures.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General conventions

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of natural numbers without
0
. We work in NBG set theory with global choice—a conservative extension of ZFC in which all proper classes are in bijective correspondence with the class
\mathbf{On}
of ordinals. By a
class
, we mean either a set or a proper class; algebraic objects (groups, fields, etc.) may be classes. For more on working with class-sized models, see
[
12
]
. For the reader who would prefer not to think about class-sized models, see
Remark

2.2
.

All orderings we consider are
linear orderings
. Let
(A,<)
and
(B,<)
be ordered classes.
We use standard interval notation such as
A^{>a}
for
\{x\in A\mid a<x\}
.
A map
f\colon A\to B
is
order preserving
if for any
x,y\in A
with
x<y
also
f(x)<f(y)
.
Given two additive abelian groups
(C,+)
and
(D,+)
, we denote by
\Hom((C,+),(D,+))
the group of homomorphism from
C
to
D
, where the group operation on
\Hom((C,+),(D,+))
is given by pointwise addition. More generally, given a vector space
V
, we always consider it as the group
(V,+)
with addition as the group operation. Given a field
k
, we let
k^{\times}\coloneqq k\setminus\{0\}
denote the group of invertible elements of
k
.

Throughout this work, let
k=(k,+,\cdot,<)
be an ordered field and let
G=(G,+,<)
be an ordered abelian group.

2.2.
Hahn fields

The
field of generalized power series
(also called
maximal Hahn field
) with coefficient field
k
and value group
G
is denoted by
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
. It consists of all power series of the form
s=\sum_{g\in G}s_{g}t^{g}
, where
s_{g}\in k
for each
g\in G
and the
support

\supp(s)=\{g\in G\mid s_{g}\neq 0\}
of
s
is a well-ordered
set
.
We may also take
\alpha
to be the ordinal representing the order type of
\supp(s)
and set
s=\sum_{\beta<\alpha}r_{\beta}t^{g_{\beta}}
, where
(g_{\beta})_{\beta<\alpha}
is a strictly increasing enumeration of
\supp(s)
and
r_{\beta}=s_{g_{\beta}}\in k^{\times}
for all
\beta
.
For any
s\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
and
g\in G
, we denote by
s_{g}
the coefficient of
t^{g}
in the power series expansion. We consider
k
as a subfield of
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
by identifying each
r\in k
with
rt^{0}\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
.
We endow
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
with the
t
-adic valuation

v
mapping any non-zero
s
to
\min\supp(s)\in G
.
The ordering on
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
is given by
[s>0:\Leftrightarrow s_{v(s)}>0]
. See
[
17
, Section 2]
for further details on Hahn fields.

Remark 2.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a proper class (the situation for much of this paper). As a consequence of our requirement that the support of any
s\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
is a set, the field
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
has more in common with the
\kappa
-bounded Hahn fields

[
19
]
than with the maximal Hahn fields defined
à la
Hahn. For instance,
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
may not contain pseudolimits to proper class-length pseudocauchy sequences, and it may support a totally defined exponential. Elsewhere in the literature, the maximal Hahn field is written
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)_{\mathbf{On}}
, to indicate the boundedness condition on the supports, but we omit this subscript.

Remark 2.2
.

We note that our results apply just as well to the field
\mathbf{No}(\kappa)
, the
set
of surreal numbers of length
<\kappa
, for any uncountable regular cardinal
\kappa
. This is a consequence of
[
10
, Proposition 4.7]
, which establishes that
\mathbf{No}(\kappa)=\mathbb{R}\!\left(\!\left(\mathbf{No}(\kappa)\right)\!\right)_{\kappa}
is a
\kappa
-bounded Hahn field that is canonically isomorphic to its own value group.

The
natural valuation
on
G
is denoted by
v_{G}
. It maps each element
g\in G
to its
archimedean equivalence class

\{h\in G\mid|g|<n|h|<n^{2}|g|\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Its value set
\{v_{G}(g)\mid g\in G\setminus\{0\}\}
is ordered by
[v_{G}(g)<v_{G}(h):\Leftrightarrow(|g|>|h|\wedge v_{G}(g)\neq v_{G}(h)]
. If
k
is archimedean, then the natural valuation on
(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,<)
coincides with the
t
-adic valuation. In this case,
v
is the finest convex valuation on
K
.

2.3.
Automorphisms

The automorphism group (where the group operation is given by composition of functions) of an algebraic structure
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\Aut(\mathcal{A})
. We always indicate the structure that is preserved by the automorphisms explicitly. More precisely,

•

\Aut(k,+,\cdot,<)
consists of all order preserving field automorphisms on
k
,

•

\Aut(k,+,\cdot)
consists of all field automorphisms on
k
,

•

\Aut(G,+,<)
consists of all order preserving group automorphisms on
G
,

•

\Aut(G,+)
consists of all group automorphisms on
G
,

•

\Aut(G,<)
consists of all order preserving bijections on
G
.

In the rest of this paragraph, we define some groups of automorphisms that will be relevant in the sequel. We refer to
[
17
, Section 3]
and
[
20
, Chapter 2]
for details.

Definition 2.3
.

The group
v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
of all
valuation preserving
(field) automorphisms consists of all automorphisms
\sigma\in\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
satisfying for any
a,b\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
,

v(a)<v(b)\Leftrightarrow v(\sigma(a))<v(\sigma(b)).

Given
\sigma\in v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
, the
induced automorphisms

\sigma_{k}\in\Aut(k,+,\cdot)
and
\sigma_{G}\in\Aut(G,+,<)
are given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{G}\colon

\displaystyle G\to G,g\mapsto v(\sigma(t^{g})),

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\colon

\displaystyle k\to k,a\mapsto[\sigma(at^{0})]_{0}.

Definition 2.4
.

The group
\mathrm{Ext}(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
of
external (field) automorphisms
consists of all automorphisms on
(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
of the form

\sum_{g\in G}s_{g}t^{g}\mapsto\sum_{g\in G}\rho(s_{g})t^{\tau(g)}

for some
\rho\in\Aut(k,+,\cdot)
and
\tau\in\Aut(G,+,<)
.
The group
\Int(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)

and
\sigma_{G}=\id_{G}
. Its subgroup
1\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
of
1
-automorphisms
consists of all
\sigma\in\Int(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)

Remark 2.5
.

An automorphism
\sigma\in v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
is internal if and only if for any
s\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
the following holds:
v(\sigma(s))=v(s)
, and if
v(s)=0
, then
[\sigma(s)]_{0}=s_{0}
.
Thus, an automorphism
\sigma\in v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
is a

.
Alternatively,
\sigma
is a
1
-automorphism if and only if
\sigma
fixes the leading term of each
s\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
.

Next we turn to the ordered additive group reduct

The group of all

.

The group
\mathrm{Ext}(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+)

consists of all automorphisms on

Remark 2.8
.

An automorphism

2.4.
Surreal numbers

The theory introduced in
Section

applies to

by setting
(k,+,\cdot,<)=(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,<)
and

Using this identification, we obtain

.

3.
Decomposing the automorphism group

The first aim of this section is to apply the decomposition of
v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
into four factors as presented in
[
17
, Theorem 3.7.1]
to the field
\mathbf{No}
. To begin with, we show in
Proposition

3.3
below that
v\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
is already the full automorphism group
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
of
\mathbf{No}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F
be a real closed field. Then any
\sigma\in\Aut(F,+,\cdot)
is order preserving, i.e.
\sigma
preserves the unique order
<
on
F
. Thus,
\Aut(F,+,\cdot)=\Aut(F,+,\cdot,<)
.

Proof.

Let
a\in F
with
a>0
. Then there is some
c\in F
such that
c^{2}=a
. Thus,
\sigma(a)=\sigma(c)^{2}>0
, showing that
\sigma
is order preserving.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Any
\tau\in\Aut(G,+,<)
preserves
v_{G}
, i.e. for any
a,b\in G
we have
v_{G}(a)<v_{G}(b)
if and only if
v_{G}(\tau(a))<v_{G}(\tau(b))
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in G
. Then

\displaystyle v_{G}(a)<v_{G}(b)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\colon|a|>n|b|

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\colon|\tau(a)|>n|\tau(b)|

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow v_{G}(\tau(a))<v_{G}(\tau(b)).\qed

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
is real closed. Then

\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)=v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
. In particular,

\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)=v\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot).

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
.
Lemma

3.1
implies that
\sigma\in\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,<)
. Thus by
Lemma

3.2
,
\sigma
is valuation preserving. We obtain
\sigma\in v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
.
∎

In order to apply
[
17
, Theorem 3.7.1]

satisfies the first and canonical second lifting property. We refer the reader to

for the definitions of lifting properties. Indeed,

, Example 3.1.4 (i)]
yields that

Proposition 3.4
.

\displaystyle\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\simeq[1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\rtimes\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R}^{\times},\cdot))]\rtimes\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<).

As

\displaystyle\simeq[1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\rtimes\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R}^{\times},\cdot))]\rtimes[\Aut(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)\times\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)].

It remains to apply

3.3
and to note that
\Aut(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
is the trivial group, as any automorphism on the field of real numbers is already the identity.
∎

The factor
\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R}^{\times},\cdot))
can be further simplified.

Lemma 3.5
.

\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R}^{\times},\cdot))\simeq\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R}^{\times},\cdot))
. Then for any
a\in\mathbf{No}^{\times}
we have

\chi(a)=\chi(2\cdot a/2)=\chi(a/2)^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}
. We obtain
\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R}^{\times},\cdot))=\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R}^{>0},\cdot))
. The lemma follows immediately from the fact that the standard exponential map
\exp
on
\mathbb{R}
is an order preserving isomorphism from
(\mathbb{R},+)
to
(\mathbb{R}^{>0},\cdot)
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\simeq[1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\rtimes\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))]\rtimes\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<).

Proposition

3.4
and
Corollary

3.6
present the first step of the structural decomposition of
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
into three (non-trivial) factors. Indeed, examples of
1
-automorphisms are given in
Example

4.3
below, we give a full description of
\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))
in
Remark

3.8
, and we further decompose
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)
in
Proposition

3.9
.
First, let us demonstrate exactly how to construct an element of
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
from members of these three groups.

Construction 3.7
.

Let
\sigma\in 1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
,
\varphi\in\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))
and
\tau\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)
. Then we obtain an automorphism
\theta(\sigma,\varphi,\tau)
corresponding to the decomposition in
Corollary

3.6
as follows:

\theta(\sigma,\varphi,\tau)\colon\sum_{\beta<\alpha}r_{\beta}t^{y_{\beta}}\mapsto\sigma\!\left(\sum_{\beta<\alpha}\exp(\varphi(\tau(y_{\beta})))r_{\beta}t^{\tau(y_{\beta})}\right)\!=\exp(\varphi(\tau(y_{0})))r_{0}t^{\tau(y_{0})}(1+\varepsilon),

for some
\varepsilon\in\mathbf{No}
with
v(\varepsilon)>0
.

Remark 3.8
.

Since homomorphisms from
(\mathbf{No},+)
to
(\mathbb{R},+)
are exactly the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear maps from
\mathbf{No}
to
\mathbb{R}
, we can fix a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbf{No}
, and subsequently any homomorphism is uniquely determined by the images of the elements of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}}
of all functions from
\mathcal{B}
to
\mathbb{R}
forms a group under pointwise addition. Hence,
(\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+)),+)\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}},+)
via the isomorphism

\varphi\mapsto\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}.

By fixing a bijection
f_{\mathcal{B}}
from
\mathcal{B}
to
\mathbf{No}
, we also obtain
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}},+)\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}},+)
via the isomorphism

h\mapsto h\circ f_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}.

We record the following alternative decompositions based on
Corollary

3.6
:

\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\simeq[1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}}]\rtimes\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)\simeq[1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}}]\rtimes\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)

We now turn to further decomposing the final factor
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)
in
Corollary

3.6
. To this end, we now consider
\mathbf{No}
as the ordered Hahn group
(\mathbb{R}\!\left(\!\left(\mathbf{No}\right)\!\right),+,<)
.

Proposition 3.9
.

\displaystyle\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)

\displaystyle\simeq\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+,<)

\displaystyle\simeq\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\left[\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\Aut(\mathbb{R},+,<)\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\left[\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}}\right].

Proof.

The Hahn group
(\mathbb{R}\!\left(\!\left(\mathbf{No}\right)\!\right),+)
has the canonical lifting property (see
[
20
, Examples 2.2.3 (i)]
for details). Thus,

v\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+)\simeq\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+)

(see
[
20
, Theorem 2.2.17]
and
[
18
, Theorem 3.15]
). Moreover, the isomorphism
\Xi\colon\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+)\to v\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+)
is given by
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\mapsto\tau_{1}\circ\tau_{2}
. Now
\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\leqslant\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)\leqslant v\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+)
by
Remark

2.8
and
[
18
, Remark 2.11]
, respectively. Hence,

\displaystyle\Xi^{-1}(\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<))

\displaystyle=\{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+)\mid\tau_{1}\circ\tau_{2}\text{ is order preserving}\}

\displaystyle=\{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+)\mid\tau_{2}\text{ is order preserving}\}

\displaystyle=\Int(\mathbf{No},+){\rtimes}\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+,<),

establishing the first isomorphism.

Next, the group of external automorphisms decomposes as

\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+)\simeq\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\Aut(\mathbb{R},+)

via the isomorphism

\Theta\colon\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\Aut(\mathbb{R},+)\to\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+),\left(\zeta,(\eta_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\right)\mapsto\left[\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}s_{\gamma}t^{\gamma}\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\eta_{\gamma}(s_{\gamma})t^{\zeta(\gamma)}\right]\!.

For the group of order preserving external automorphisms, we obtain

\displaystyle\Theta^{-1}(\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbf{No},+,<))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\left.\left(\zeta,(\eta_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\right)\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\Aut(\mathbb{R},+)\ \right|\forall\gamma\in\mathbf{No}\ \forall r\in\mathbb{R}\colon[\eta_{\gamma}(r)>0\Leftrightarrow r>0]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\Aut(\mathbb{R},+,<),

yielding the second isomorphism.

Finally, note that
\Aut(\mathbb{R},+,<)
consists of all maps
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},x\mapsto ax
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}
. Hence,
\Aut(\mathbb{R},+,<)\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{>0},\cdot)
. As
(\mathbb{R}^{>0},\cdot)
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{R},+)
via the real logarithm map
\log
, we obtain

\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}\Aut(\mathbb{R},+,<)\simeq\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbf{No}}(\mathbb{R},+)=(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}},+),

as required.
∎

Recall that in
3.7
we presented how
\theta(\sigma,\varphi,\tau)\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
arises from some
\sigma\in 1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},\allowbreak+,\allowbreak\cdot)
,
\varphi\in\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))
and
\tau\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)
.
Based on the decomposition of
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)
in
Proposition

3.9
, we refine
\tau
in the following construction as
\tau(\nu,\zeta,g)
for some internal group automorphism
\nu\in\Int(\mathbf{No},+)
, some order preserving bijection
\zeta\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)
and some map
g\colon\mathbf{No}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Construction 3.10
.

Let
\nu\in\Int(\mathbf{No},+)
,
\zeta\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)
and
g\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}}
. We construct
\tau(\nu,\zeta,g)
following the decomposition presented in
Proposition

3.9
:

\tau(\nu,\zeta,g)\colon\sum_{\beta<\alpha}r_{\beta}t^{y_{\beta}}\mapsto\nu\!\left(\sum_{\beta<\alpha}\exp(g(y_{\beta}))r_{\beta}t^{\zeta(y_{\beta})}\right)\!=\exp(g(y_{0}))r_{0}t^{\zeta(y_{0})}(1+\varepsilon),

for some
\varepsilon\in\mathbf{No}
with
v(\varepsilon)>0
.

Finally, by combining
Corollary

3.6
and
Proposition

3.9
, we obtain our main decomposition result.

Theorem 3.11
.

\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\simeq\left[1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\rtimes\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))\right]\rtimes\left[\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\left[\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}}\right]\right].

Given
\sigma\in 1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
,
\varphi\in\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))
,
\nu\in\Int(\mathbf{No},+)
,
\zeta\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)
and

, one can combine
3.7
and
3.10
to obtain an automorphism

arising from the decomposition presented in

3.11
.

Due to

3.8
, we also obtain the following decomposition, which depends on the choice of a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbf{No}
and a bijection from
\mathcal{B}
to
\mathbf{No}
.

Corollary 3.12
.

\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\simeq\left[1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}}\right]\rtimes\left[\Int(\mathbf{No},+)\rtimes\left[\Aut(\mathbf{No},<)\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}}\right]\right].

Thus, the factor
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{No}},+)
appears twice in the decomposition. We point out that the first of those two factors highly depends on a choice of a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis
\mathcal{B}
of

and a bijection

4.
Strongly additive automorphisms

An automorphism
\sigma\in v\text{-}\!\Aut(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
is said to be
strongly additive
if

\sigma\big(\sum_{g\in G}s_{g}t^{g}\big)=\sum_{g\in G}\sigma(s_{g}t^{g})

for all
s=\sum_{g\in G}s_{g}t^{g}\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
.
Let
v\text{-}\!\Aut^{+}(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
denote the group of strongly additive valuation preserving automorphisms. Note that every external field automorphism is strongly additive.

A
strongly
k
-linear
automorphism of
(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\cdot)
is a strongly additive automorphism that is the identity on
k
. We denote by
{v\text{-}\!\Aut^{+}_{k}(k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right),+,\allowbreak\cdot)}
the group of strongly
k
-linear valuation preserving automorphisms.

Remark 4.1
.

One source of strongly linear automorphisms comes from the
hyperseries program
of Bagayoko, van der Hoeven, and others
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
11
]
. Conjecturally, there is a composition law

(f,g)\mapsto f\circ g\colon\mathbf{No}\times\mathbf{No}^{>\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbf{No}

satisfying some natural conditions, one of which is that for any
g\in\mathbf{No}^{>\mathbb{R}}
, the map
\sigma_{g}\colon\mathbf{No}\to\mathbf{No}
given by
\sigma_{g}(f)\coloneqq f\circ g
is a strongly
\mathbb{R}
-linear automorphism (see
[
2
, page 235]
for a precise conjecture).

In order to study strongly linear automorphisms systematically, we first examine where they fit into our decomposition.
Let
\sigma\in 1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
,
\tau\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,<)
, and
\varphi\in\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))
, and let
\theta=\theta(\sigma,\varphi,\tau)
be as in
3.7
. If
\sigma
is the identity, then
\theta
is strongly
\mathbb{R}
-linear. Thus, in studying strongly additive and strongly
\mathbb{R}
-linear automorphisms of
\mathbf{No}
, it is enough to focus on
1
-automorphisms.

In
[
3
]
, a correspondence is established between strongly
k
-linear
1
-automorphisms of
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
and certain strongly
k
-linear derivations on
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
. A
strongly
k
-linear derivation
is a strongly
k
-linear map

\partial\colon k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)\to k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)

that satisfies
\partial(ab)=a\partial(b)+b\partial(a)
for all
a,b\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
. A derivation
\partial
is said to be
contracting
if
v(\partial a)>v(a)
for all
a\in k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
.

Fact 4.2

(
[
3
, Theorem 3.17]
)
.

If
\partial
a contracting strongly
k
-linear derivation, then the map
\exp(\partial)\colon k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)\to k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
given by

\exp(\partial)(a)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\partial^{i}(a)/i!

is a strongly
k
-linear
1
-automorphism of
k\!\left(\!\left(G\right)\!\right)
. The map
\partial\mapsto\exp(\partial)
is a bijection between the class of contracting strongly
k
-linear derivations and the class of strongly
k
-linear
1
-automorphisms.

In many cases, it is easier to work with contractive strongly linear derivations than
1
-automorphisms, as we will see in
Proposition

5.2
below. It is also easy to find nontrivial contractive derivations, using the following variant of a construction from
[
8
,
16
]
:

Example 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in\Hom((\mathbf{No},+),(\mathbb{R},+))
and consider the derivation
\partial_{\varphi}\colon\mathbf{No}\to\mathbf{No}
given by

\sum_{g\in\mathbf{No}}r_{g}t^{g}\mapsto\sum_{g\in\mathbf{No}}r_{g}\varphi(g)t^{g-1}.

Then
\partial_{\varphi}
is a contractive strongly
\mathbb{R}
-linear derivation, so
\exp(\partial_{\varphi})
belongs to
1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
.

We end this section by giving an example of a
1
-automorphism of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
that is not strongly additive:

Example 4.4
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbf{No}
be the field of Puiseux series over
\mathbb{R}
in
\omega^{-1}
, so
K
consists of all surreal numbers of the form
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{i}\omega^{i/n}
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and where
r_{i}=0
for all sufficiently large
i
. Then
K
is real closed with value group
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
a\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega^{-1/n}
. Then
a
is a pseudolimit of the pseudocauchy sequence
(\sum_{n=1}^{m}\omega^{-1/n})_{m<\omega}
in
K
, so
K(a)
and its real closure
K(a)^{\operatorname{rc}}
are immediate extensions of
K
. Let
\varepsilon\coloneqq\omega^{-\omega}\in\mathbf{No}
, so
0<\varepsilon<K(a)^{>0}
. Thus,
a
and
a+\varepsilon
realize the same cut over
K
, so there is a unique isomorphism
\sigma_{1}\colon K(a)^{\operatorname{rc}}\to K(a+\varepsilon)^{\operatorname{rc}}
that is the identity on
K
and sends
a
to
a+\varepsilon
.

Using global choice, fix a well-ordered
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space basis
(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbf{On}}
of
\mathbf{No}
with
y_{0}=1
and
y_{1}=\omega
. For each
\alpha\in\mathbf{On}
, let
\Gamma_{\alpha}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\beta<\alpha}\mathbb{Q}y_{\beta}
, and let
K_{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\!\left(\!\left(\Gamma_{\alpha}\right)\!\right)
, identified in the usual way with a subfield of
\mathbf{No}
. We define an automorphism
\sigma_{\alpha}
on
K_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\geqslant 2
as follows:

(1)

The field
K_{2}=K\!\left(\!\left(\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}\omega\right)\!\right)
is an immediate extension of
K(a,\omega^{\omega})^{\operatorname{rc}}=K(a,\varepsilon)^{\operatorname{rc}}
. Using that
\omega^{\omega}
is greater than both
K(a)^{\operatorname{rc}}
and
K(a+\varepsilon)^{\operatorname{rc}}
, we extend
\sigma_{1}
to an automorphism of
K(a,\omega^{\omega})^{\operatorname{rc}}
that fixes
\omega^{\omega}
. We further extend this to an automorphism
\sigma_{2}
of
K_{2}
using the uniqueness of immediate extensions.

(2)

Let
\alpha\geqslant 2
and assume that we have defined
\sigma_{\alpha}
. Then
K_{\alpha+1}
is an immediate extension of
K_{\alpha}(\omega^{y_{\alpha}})^{\operatorname{rc}}
. We first extend
\sigma_{\alpha}
to an automorphism of
K_{\alpha}(\omega^{y_{\alpha}})^{\operatorname{rc}}
that fixes
\omega^{y_{\alpha}}
, and then we further extend this to
K_{\alpha+1}
by uniqueness of immediate extensions.

(3)

Let
\lambda>0
be a limit ordinal, and assume we’ve defined
\sigma_{\alpha}
for
\alpha<\lambda
. Then
K_{\lambda}
is an immediate extension of
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}K_{\alpha}
, so the automorphisms
(\sigma_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
extend to an automorphism
\sigma_{\lambda}
of
K_{\lambda}
, again using uniqueness of immediate extensions.

We have
\mathbf{No}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathbf{On}}K_{\alpha}
, and we let
\sigma\colon\mathbf{No}\to\mathbf{No}
be the common extension of the maps
(\sigma_{\alpha})_{0<\alpha\in\mathbf{On}}
. Then
\sigma(\omega^{y})=\omega^{y}
for all
y\in\mathbf{No}
and
\sigma(r)=r
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
, so
\sigma
is a 1-automorphism. Clearly,
\sigma
is not strongly additive since

\sigma\big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega^{-1/n}\big)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega^{-1/n}+\omega^{-\omega}\neq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sigma(\omega^{-1/n}).

Strongly linear automorphisms have the desirable property that they can be easily expressed by declaring them on monomials and extending by strong linearity. It is thus natural to ask whether non-strongly linear automorphisms can be approximated by strongly linear ones. The following is a special case of a question asked by M. Matusinski for the general case of Hahn fields.

Question 4.5
.

Can
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
be endowed with a reasonable topology, such that
\Aut^{+}(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
is a dense subgroup?

The next remark rules out a natural topology.

Remark 4.6
.

Consider the
pointwise convergence topology
—the topology with basic open sets of the form

U_{\sigma}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\coloneqq\{\tau\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)\mid\tau(y_{i})=\sigma(y_{i})\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n\}

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma\in\Aut^{+}(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
, and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathbf{No}
.
This is the analogue of the topology used for the Galois group of an infinite Galois extension.

Example
4.4
shows that
\Aut^{+}(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
is not dense in
\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot)
with respect to this topology.
To see this, let
\sigma
and
a=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega^{-1/n}
be as in Example
4.4
and consider the open neighborhood

U_{\sigma}(a,\omega)=\{\tau\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot):\tau(a)=a+\omega^{-\omega}\text{ and }\tau(\omega)=\omega\}.

For
\tau
in this neighborhood, we have
\tau(\omega^{-1/n})=\tau(\omega)^{-1/n}=\omega^{-1/n}
, so

\tau\big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega^{-1/n}\big)\neq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega^{-1/n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tau(\omega^{-1/n}).

Thus, this neighborhood contains no strongly additive automorphisms.

5.
Preserving exponentiation and the
\omega
-map

In addition to its structure as a field of generalized power series, the field of surreal numbers enjoys a plethora of additional structure:

(1)

There is a well-founded partial ordering
<_{s}
on
\mathbf{No}
, and with this ordering,
(\mathbf{No},<,<_{s})
is a
full lexicographically ordered binary tree
; see
[
13
]
.

(2)

There is an exponential
\exp
on
\mathbf{No}
that extends the real exponential
[
14
]
. With this exponential,
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
is an elementary extension of the field of real numbers
[
10
]
.

(3)

There is the omega map
x\mapsto\omega^{x}
, mentioned in the introduction. With this map,
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\omega^{x})
is a so-called
omega-field

[
7
]
.

In this section, we briefly touch on the automorphism groups of these richer structures (though much remains open in the last two cases).

Proposition 5.1
.

The subgroup
\Aut(\mathbf{No},<,<_{s})
is trivial.

Proof.

As a full lexicographically ordered binary tree,
(\mathbf{No},<,<_{s})
has the following properties: every
x\in\mathbf{No}
has exactly two immediate
<_{s}
-successors (one
<x
and one
>x
), and every
<_{s}
-chain
(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
in
\mathbf{No}
with
\lambda
a limit ordinal has a unique
<_{s}
-supremum (when
\lambda=0
and this chain is empty, then
0
is this supremum). Moreover, any surreal number is either an immediate
<_{s}
-successor of another number, or a
<_{s}
-supremum of such a chain.

Let
\sigma\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},<,<_{s})
. If
x\in\mathbf{No}
is fixed by
\sigma
, then its two immediate
<_{s}
-successors must be as well. If
\sigma
fixes an
<_{s}
-chain
(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
with
\lambda
a limit, then it fixes the unique
<_{s}
-supremum of this chain. As
\sigma
fixes the empty chain, it fixes zero, and thus by induction it fixes all of
\mathbf{No}
.
∎

Now we turn to automorphisms of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
. As an ordered exponential field
\mathbf{No}
is strongly homogeneous: for any set-sized elementary exponential subfield
K\subseteq\mathbf{No}
and any elementary embedding
\sigma\colon K\to\mathbf{No}
, there is an automorphism of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
extending
\sigma
. In addition, any automorphism of
\mathbf{No}
arising from a composition as in
Remark

4.1
is an automorphism of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
. While automorphisms of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
are plentiful, we have not been able to find an example of a nontrivial
1
-automorphism of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
. This is partially explained by the following negative result:

Proposition 5.2
.

The group
1\text{-}\!\Aut^{+}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
of strongly
\mathbb{R}
-linear exponential field
1
-automorphisms is trivial.

Proof.

Let
\partial\colon\mathbf{No}\to\mathbf{No}
be a contractive strongly
\mathbb{R}
-linear derivation and suppose that
\partial
is not identically zero. In light of
4.2
, it is enough to show that the automorphism
\sigma\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}\partial^{i}
does not commute with the exponential map.

We claim that we can find
y\in\mathbf{No}
with
v(\partial y)<0
. First, take
a\in\mathbf{No}
with
\partial a\neq 0
and take
b\in\mathbf{No}
with
v(b)<\min\{0,-v(\partial a)\}
. Then

\partial(ab)=a\partial b+b\partial a,\qquad\partial(ab^{2})=b(2a\partial b+b\partial a).

Either
v(a\partial b+b\partial a)
or
v(2a\partial b+b\partial a)
is at most
v(b\partial a)<0
, so taking either
y=ab
or
y=ab^{2}
, we get
v(\partial y)<0
.

Let
\varepsilon\coloneqq\sigma(y)-y
, so

\exp(\sigma y)=\exp(y+\varepsilon)=\exp(y)\exp(\varepsilon)
. Since
\partial
is contractive, we have
v(\varepsilon)=v(\partial y)<0
, so
v\exp(\varepsilon)\neq 0
. Therefore,

v\exp(\sigma y)=v\exp(y)+v\exp(\varepsilon)\neq v\exp(y)=v(\sigma(\exp y)),

where the last equality uses that
\sigma
is a
1
-automorphism. Thus,
\sigma
doesn’t commute with the exponential.
∎

Automorphisms obtained from compositions as in
Remark

4.1
are always strongly
\mathbb{R}
-linear and commute with the exponential. Thus we have:

Corollary 5.3
.

Automorphisms obtained from compositions are never
1
-automorphisms.

Question 5.4
.

Are there any nontrivial
1
-automorphisms of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\exp)
?

Finally, we turn to automorphisms that commute with the
\omega
-map.

Proposition 5.5
.

Any automorphism of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\omega^{x})
that fixes the valuation is trivial. In particular, the group
1\text{-}\!\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\omega^{x})
is trivial.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism preserving the omega map and let
x\in\mathbf{No}
, so
\sigma(\omega^{x})=\omega^{\sigma(x)}
. Recall that we identify
\omega^{x}
with
t^{-x}\in\mathbb{R}\!\left(\!\left(\mathbf{No}\right)\!\right)
, so
v(\omega^{x})=-x
. Thus, if
v(\sigma(\omega^{x}))=v(\omega^{x})
, then
\sigma(x)=x
.
∎

Question 5.6
.

What are the nontrivial automorphisms of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\omega^{x})
?

The search for these automorphisms seems related to the class of
generalized
\varepsilon
-numbers
(that is, the fixed points of the map
x\mapsto\omega^{x}
). As an ordered class, the generalized
\varepsilon
-numbers are in bijective correspondence with
(\mathbf{No},<)
; see
[
14
, Theorem 9.1]
. Note that any
\sigma\in\Aut(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\omega^{x})
induces an automorphism of the ordered class of generalized
\varepsilon
-numbers: if
\omega^{x}=x
, then
\omega^{\sigma(x)}=\sigma(\omega^{x})=\sigma(x)
, so
\sigma(x)
is a generalized
\varepsilon
-number as well.

Question 5.7
.

Does any order-isomorphism of the generalized
\varepsilon
-numbers extend to an automorphism of
(\mathbf{No},+,\cdot,\omega^{x})
?

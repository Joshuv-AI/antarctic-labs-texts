Expression differences and clinical significance of PD-L1 in lung cancer patients with different histological types and differentiation degrees
Authors: Xincheng Zhou, Shuang Ao, Yanan Guo, Jing Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the expression differences of PD-L1 in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients with different histological types and differentiation degrees.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of 538 NSCLC patients diagnosed at Ningbo Pathological Diagnosis Center (6 small cell lung cancer cases were excluded due to limited sample size). PD-L1 expression was detected by immunohistochemistry (clone 22C3) and evaluated by tumor proportion score (TPS). Univariate chi-square test and multivariate Logistic regression analysis were used to analyze the associations between PD-L1 expression and clinical pathological factors (gender, age, histological type, differentiation degree).ResultsPD-L1 expression showed significant gender-based differences (P = 0.000), with a higher positivity rate in male patients. No significant difference was found in PD-L1 expression among different age groups (P = 0.708). Multivariate Logistic regression analysis confirmed that histological type (OR = 2.135, 95%CI:1.526–2.990, P = 0.000) and differentiation degree (OR = 0.412, 95%CI:0.298–0.569, P = 0.000) were independent influencing factors of PD-L1 high expression in NSCLC patients. The proportion of adenocarcinoma (ADC) patients with negative PD-L1 expression was significantly higher than that of squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) patients (32.45% vs. 19.92%, P = 0.000), while the high expression rate of SCC was significantly higher than that of ADC (30.08% vs. 19.21%, P = 0.000). The negative expression rate of PD-L1 in well-differentiated tumors was the highest (45.71%, P = 0.000), and the high expression rate in poorly differentiated tumors was the highest (35.98%, P = 0.000). Among well-differentiated patients with negative PD-L1 expression, the proportion of ADC was significantly higher than that of SCC (61.11% vs. 29.41%, P = 0.023); among well-differentiated patients with low PD-L1 expression, the proportion of SCC was higher than that of ADC (52.94% vs. 38.89%, P = 0.037).ConclusionPD-L1 expression in NSCLC is significantly correlated with histological type and differentiation degree, which are independent influencing factors of PD-L1 high expression. SCC and poorly differentiated tumors have higher PD-L1 expression levels, suggesting that these patients may have better responsiveness to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor immunotherapy. The study provides more precise pathological evidence for personalized immunotherapy strategies in NSCLC patients.

Introduction

Programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) is currently the most extensively studied inhibitory immune checkpoint molecule, and its expression level has become one of the primary biomarkers for predicting the efficacy of immunotherapy in lung cancer patients. Numerous large-scale clinical trials have shown that patients with high PD-L1 expression respond better to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy [1, 2]. Both NCCN and CSCO guidelines explicitly recommend different immunotherapy strategies based on the immunohistochemical expression level of PD-L1 in lung cancer patients [3, 4]. However, PD-L1 expression in lung cancer is highly heterogeneous, and its expression patterns across different histological types and differentiation degrees require further systematic verification with large sample size and multivariate analysis.

Existing studies suggest that PD-L1 expression is higher in lung squamous cell carcinoma than in lung adenocarcinoma [5], and differentiation degree may be negatively correlated with PD-L1 expression [6]. However, most previous studies only used univariate analysis to explore the correlation, lacking multivariate analysis to control potential confounding factors (e.g., gender, age), which may lead to biased results. In addition, previous studies have not specifically analyzed the combined effect of histological type and differentiation degree on PD-L1 expression in a single large sample of NSCLC patients. Data on PD-L1 expression in small cell lung cancer remain controversial [7–9], but the small sample size of SCLC in single-center studies limits the reliability of results, so it is necessary to focus on NSCLC for in-depth analysis.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1/PD-L1 have shown clinical activity in NSCLC treatment [10]. This study retrospectively analyzed PD-L1 expression data from 538 NSCLC patients (excluding SCLC), used univariate and multivariate statistical methods to systematically evaluate the independent association between PD-L1 expression and histological type/differentiation degree, and further explored the combined distribution characteristics of PD-L1 expression with histological type and differentiation degree. The innovation of this study is to clarify the independent influencing effect of histological type and differentiation degree on PD-L1 expression by controlling confounding factors such as gender and age, and to provide more precise pathological evidence for screening NSCLC patients suitable for immunotherapy and guiding individualized treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

General information

A total of 538 NSCLC patients diagnosed at Ningbo Pathological Diagnosis Center from August 2023 to March 2024 were selected as the study subjects (6 SCLC cases were excluded due to insufficient sample size for meaningful statistical analysis). Among them, 132 were female and 406 were male; 137 were ≤ 60 years old and 401 were > 60 years old. Histological types included 302 cases of adenocarcinoma (ADC) and 236 cases of squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). Differentiation degrees were classified as 268 poorly differentiated, 202 moderately differentiated, and 68 well-differentiated cases.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: ① Age 18–90 years; ② Signed informed consent; ③ Pathologically confirmed diagnosis of NSCLC; ④ Complete PD-L1 immunohistochemical test results and clinical pathological data available.

Exclusion criteria: ① Pathological diagnosis of small cell lung cancer or other non-lung malignant tumors; ② No PD-L1 test results; ③ Incomplete clinical pathological data (e.g., missing gender, age, differentiation degree information).

PD-L1 scoring standard

Tumor proportion score (TPS) was used as the expression standard, which was defined as the percentage of viable tumor cells with partial or complete membranous staining for PD-L1: ① TPS < 1%: negative expression; ② 1% ≤ TPS ≤ 49%: low expression; ③ TPS ≥ 50%: high expression. Differentiation degree was graded according to the WHO Classification of Thoracic Tumours (5th edition) [11], which was evaluated by two senior pathologists in a double-blind manner.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and count data were expressed as n (%). ① Univariate analysis: Chi-square test was used to analyze the differences in PD-L1 expression among different groups (gender, age, histological type, differentiation degree); ② Multivariate analysis: Binary Logistic regression analysis was used to explore the independent influencing factors of PD-L1 high expression (dependent variable: PD-L1 expression status, high expression = 1, negative/low expression = 0; independent variables: gender, age, histological type, differentiation degree, all set as categorical variables). Exact P values were reported for all statistical tests, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Relationship between PD-L1 expression and clinical characteristics (Univariate Analysis)

PD-L1 expression showed significant differences between genders (χ²=26.987, P = 0.000), with a higher proportion of PD-L1 high expression in male patients (27.83%) compared to females (10.61%). No significant difference in PD-L1 expression was found between age groups (≤ 60 years old vs. >60 years old, χ²=0.623, P = 0.708). Differentiation degree showed significant differences in both gender and age groups (gender: χ²=31.254, P = 0.000; age: χ²=28.963, P = 0.000), with a higher proportion of poorly differentiated tumors in male patients and those > 60 years old (Table 1).

Relationship between PD-L1 expression and histological type

There were significant differences in PD-L1 expression between ADC and SCC (χ²=21.365, P = 0.000). The proportion of ADC patients with negative PD-L1 expression was significantly higher than that of SCC patients (32.45% vs. 19.92%), while the proportion of ADC patients with high PD-L1 expression was significantly lower than that of SCC patients (19.21% vs. 30.08%). Relationship between PD-L1 expression and differentiation degree

PD-L1 expression was significantly correlated with tumor differentiation degree (χ²=58.962, P = 0.000), showing an obvious negative correlation. Well-differentiated patients had the highest proportion of negative PD-L1 expression (44.12%) and the lowest proportion of high PD-L1 expression (8.82%). Poorly differentiated patients showed the lowest negative expression rate (14.39%) and the highest high expression rate (35.98%). Moderately differentiated patients had intermediate PD-L1 expression levels in all categories (Table 3).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis of influencing factors for PD-L1 high expression

Multivariate Logistic regression analysis was used to control confounding factors such as gender and age, and the results showed that histological type (OR = 2.135, 95%CI:1.526–2.990, P = 0.000) and differentiation degree (OR = 0.412, 95%CI:0.298–0.569, P = 0.000) were independent influencing factors of PD-L1 high expression in NSCLC patients. SCC patients were 2.135 times more likely to have high PD-L1 expression than ADC patients, and the possibility of high PD-L1 expression decreased with the increase of tumor differentiation degree. Gender and age were not independent influencing factors of PD-L1 high expression (P > 0.05) (Table 4).

Discussion

Lung cancer is one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths worldwide, and NSCLC accounts for about 85% of all lung cancer cases [12]. Immunotherapy based on PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors has become a first-line or second-line treatment option for advanced NSCLC, and PD-L1 expression level is the most important predictive biomarker [1, 2]. This study analyzed the expression characteristics of PD-L1 in 538 NSCLC patients by univariate and multivariate statistical methods, clarified the independent correlation between PD-L1 expression and histological type/differentiation degree, and further explored the combined distribution characteristics of PD-L1 expression with the two factors, which provides a more precise pathological basis for screening populations suitable for immunotherapy and guiding individualized treatment.

PD-L1 expression and clinical pathological factors: independent correlation after controlling confounding factors

This study found that PD-L1 expression was significantly different between genders in univariate analysis, which was consistent with previous studies [13]. However, multivariate Logistic regression analysis showed that gender was not an independent influencing factor of PD-L1 high expression (P = 0.108), indicating that the gender difference in PD-L1 expression may be a secondary phenomenon caused by the difference in histological type and differentiation degree distribution between genders (e.g., more males with SCC and poorly differentiated tumors). No significant correlation was found between PD-L1 expression and age (P = 0.708), which was consistent with the results of Skov et al. [13], suggesting that age is not a key factor affecting PD-L1 expression in NSCLC.

The most important finding of this study is that histological type and differentiation degree are independent influencing factors of PD-L1 high expression in NSCLC patients (both P = 0.000) after controlling confounding factors such as gender and age. This is the core difference between this study and previous studies: most previous studies only used univariate analysis to find the correlation between PD-L1 expression and histological type/differentiation degree [5, 6], but did not confirm their independent predictive effect. Our study excluded the interference of confounding factors through multivariate analysis, making the conclusion more reliable and providing a more precise basis for clinical practice.

PD-L1 expression differences among different histological types: consistency and innovation with previous studies

Consistent with previous studies [5, 13, 14], this study found that PD-L1 expression in SCC was significantly higher than that in ADC (high expression rate 30.08% vs. 19.21%, P = 0.000). Liu et al. [5] found that the negative expression rate of PD-L1 in lung ADC was 59.46% and that in SCC was 43.91%, and Aggarwal et al. [14] found that the TPS < 1% rate in non-SCC was 26% and that in SCC was 19%. The results of this study are consistent with the above conclusions, and further confirmed by multivariate analysis that histological type is an independent influencing factor (OR = 2.135), which further verifies the stability of the PD-L1 expression difference between NSCLC subtypes.

In addition, this study found that the difference in PD-L1 expression between ADC and SCC is more significant in moderately and well-differentiated tumors, while there is no significant difference in poorly differentiated tumors (P = 0.971). This is a new finding of this study that has not been reported in previous studies, suggesting that the regulatory mechanism of PD-L1 expression may be different in tumors with different differentiation degrees: the influence of histological type on PD-L1 expression is weakened in poorly differentiated tumors, which may be due to the high heterogeneity and abnormal activation of multiple signaling pathways in poorly differentiated tumors, leading to the convergence of PD-L1 expression in different subtypes.

PD-L1 expression and differentiation degree: negative correlation and biological mechanism

This study confirmed a significant negative correlation between PD-L1 expression and tumor differentiation degree (P = 0.000), which is consistent with the results of Liu et al. [5] and Tancoš et al. [6]. Well-differentiated tumors have the highest negative expression rate (44.12%) and the lowest high expression rate (8.82%), while poorly differentiated tumors are the opposite (35.98% high expression rate). Multivariate analysis further confirmed that differentiation degree is an independent influencing factor (OR = 0.412), indicating that the lower the tumor differentiation degree, the higher the PD-L1 expression level.

The biological mechanism of the negative correlation between PD-L1 expression and differentiation degree may be related to the following aspects:Tumor cell heterogeneity and immune escape: Poorly differentiated tumor cells have higher genetic and phenotypic heterogeneity, and are more likely to activate immune escape mechanisms to avoid the attack of the body’s immune system. PD-L1, as a key immune checkpoint molecule, is highly expressed in poorly differentiated tumors to bind to PD-1 on the surface of T cells, inhibit T cell activation and proliferation, and thus realize immune escape [15];Abnormal activation of signaling pathways: PD-L1 expression is regulated by multiple intracellular signaling pathways, such as MAPKs [15], the STAT family [16], IRF-1 [17], and PI3K-AKT [18]. Poorly differentiated tumor cells often have abnormal activation of multiple oncogenic signaling pathways (e.g., PI3K-AKT, MAPK), which can up-regulate the transcription and translation of PD-L1 gene, leading to high PD-L1 expression. For example, Gao et al. [18] found that the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway can regulate PD-L1 expression in lung cancer cells by mediating IFN-γ signaling, and the abnormal activation of this pathway in poorly differentiated tumors is the main reason for the high expression of PD-L1;Tumor microenvironment (TME): Poorly differentiated tumors have a more immunosuppressive microenvironment, with high levels of inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ, TNF-α) secreted by tumor-associated macrophages and regulatory T cells. These cytokines can bind to receptors on the surface of tumor cells, activate downstream signaling pathways, and up-regulate PD-L1 expression [17, 19]. In contrast, well-differentiated tumors have a relatively mild microenvironment, with low levels of inflammatory cytokines, leading to low PD-L1 expression.

Biological mechanism of PD-L1 expression difference between ADC and SCC

The significant difference in PD-L1 expression between ADC and SCC (independent factor, OR = 2.135) is related to the inherent differences in their cellular origin, molecular characteristics and tumor microenvironment:Molecular characteristic differences: SCC is often associated with smoking-related gene mutations (e.g., TP53, KEAP1), and these mutations can activate the NF-κB and PI3K-AKT signaling pathways, leading to up-regulation of PD-L1 expression [13, 18]; while ADC is more associated with EGFR, ALK and other driver gene mutations, and these mutations can inhibit the expression of PD-L1 by down-regulating the IFN-γ signaling pathway [20];Tumor microenvironment differences: SCC has a higher density of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and a higher level of inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ) than ADC [5, 13]. The high level of IFN-γ can activate the IRF-1/PD-L1 signaling axis, significantly up-regulating PD-L1 expression on the surface of tumor cells [17];Epigenetic regulation differences: DNA methylation and histone modification of the PD-L1 gene are different between ADC and SCC. The promoter region of the PD-L1 gene in SCC has lower methylation level, leading to higher transcriptional activity and protein expression [14].

Clinical significance of the study results

The results of this study have important clinical guiding significance for the individualized immunotherapy of NSCLC patients:SCC patients: With higher PD-L1 expression levels (independent factor), they may benefit more from PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor monotherapy, which is consistent with the clinical trial results (e.g., KEYNOTE-042) [12], and further supports the priority of immunotherapy monotherapy for SCC patients with high PD-L1 expression;ADC patients: With lower PD-L1 expression levels, especially well-differentiated ADC with almost no high PD-L1 expression, the efficacy of immunotherapy monotherapy may be limited, and combination therapy strategies (e.g., immunotherapy + chemotherapy, immunotherapy + targeted therapy) should be considered first;Poorly differentiated NSCLC patients: Regardless of ADC or SCC, they have high PD-L1 expression levels, so they are potential suitable populations for immunotherapy, and immunotherapy can be actively considered in clinical practice;Well-differentiated NSCLC patients: With low PD-L1 expression levels, the selection of immunotherapy should be more cautious, and the combination of other biomarkers (e.g., TMB, MSI) should be considered for comprehensive evaluation.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations that need to be noted:Single-center retrospective study: The study population is from a single pathological diagnosis center, which may have selection bias, and the results need to be verified by multi-center prospective studies;Exclusion of SCLC: Due to the small sample size of SCLC (only 6 cases), this study excluded SCLC and only focused on NSCLC. The expression characteristics of PD-L1 in SCLC need to be further studied with large sample size;Lack of follow-up data: This study only analyzed the correlation between PD-L1 expression and clinical pathological factors, and did not include follow-up data (e.g., overall survival, progression-free survival) to explore the predictive value of PD-L1 expression combined with histological type/differentiation degree on the efficacy of immunotherapy;Lack of molecular marker detection: The study did not detect driver gene mutations (e.g., EGFR, ALK) and other immune biomarkers (e.g., TMB, MSI) of NSCLC, which cannot further analyze the combined effect of these markers with histological type/differentiation degree on PD-L1 expression.

Conclusion

PD-L1 expression in NSCLC shows significant heterogeneity related to histological type and differentiation degree, and both factors are independent influencing factors of PD-L1 high expression after controlling confounding factors such as gender and age. Squamous cell carcinoma and poorly differentiated tumors have significantly higher PD-L1 expression levels, and the difference in PD-L1 expression between adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma is more significant in moderately and well-differentiated tumors. These findings suggest that NSCLC patients with squamous cell carcinoma or poorly differentiated tumors may be potential suitable populations for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor immunotherapy, while well-differentiated adenocarcinoma patients may have limited efficacy of monotherapy and need to consider combination therapy strategies. The study provides more precise pathological evidence for the selection of biomarkers and the formulation of individualized immunotherapy strategies in NSCLC clinical practice.

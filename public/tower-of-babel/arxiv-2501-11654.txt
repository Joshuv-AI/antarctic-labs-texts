Abstract
The Parker conjecture, which explores whether magnetic fields in perfectly conducting plasmas can develop tangential discontinuities during magnetic relaxation, remains an open question in astrophysics. Helicity conservation provides a topological barrier during relaxation, preventing topologically nontrivial initial data relaxing to trivial solutions; preserving this mechanism discretely over long time periods is therefore crucial for numerical simulation. This work presents an energy- and helicity-preserving finite element discretization for the magneto-frictional system for investigating the Parker conjecture. The algorithm preserves a discrete version of the topological barrier and a discrete Arnold inequality. We also propose extensions of the notion of helicity and the Arnold inequality to certain kinds of topologically nontrivial domains. Numerical experiments demonstrate that helicity preservation is crucial in obtaining physically meaningful simulations of magnetic relaxation, providing an example where structure-preserving schemes are necessary.

Introduction

In 1972, Eugene N. Parker made a conjecture about the behaviour of magnetically ideal plasmas
[
36
]
.
Although several different versions and statements have been discussed in the literature
[
37
]
, the essential claim of the Parker conjecture is as follows:

For almost all possible flows, the magnetic field develops tangential discontinuities (current sheets) during ideal magnetic relaxation to a force-free equilibrium.
This is also known as the force-free version of the Parker conjecture, and it has many important consequences in solar physics, including explaining the mechanism of coronal heating.
For a comprehensive literature review, see
[
37
]
.

We focus in this work on the case energy is dissipated by fluid viscosity (the fluid Reynolds number
R_{e}<\infty
), while the magnetic part is ideal (the magnetic Reynolds number
R_{m}=\infty
).

For general MHD systems, standard energy estimate indicate that the total energy of the fluid and the magnetic field is non-increasing.
Over a bounded, contractible, Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the (magnetic) helicity is defined

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\bm{A}\cdot\bm{B}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(1)

Here
\bm{A}
is any potential for the magnetic field
\bm{B}
satisfying
\bm{B}=\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}
and
\bm{A}\times\bm{n}=0
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
, where
\bm{n}
is the (outward-facing) unit normal.
In the magnetically ideal limit, this is an invariant under relaxation:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}=0.

(2)

The helicity provides a lower bound for the magnetic energy

|\mathcal{H}|\leq C^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{B}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3)

for some constant
C>0
.
This is known as the Arnold inequality
[
8
]
; for completeness, we include a proof in
Appendix

A
.
A significant consequence of the structure (
3
) is that, despite dissipation in the energy, initial data with nonzero
\mathcal{H}
cannot relax to zero, as the total energy cannot decay below a certain multiple of
|\mathcal{H}|
.
As helicity quantifies the knottedness of divergence-free fields
[
7
,
34
]
, this reflects a topological barrier exhibited by knotted magnetic fields.
This property is crucial for the mathematical and physical behaviour of magnetic relaxation.

In numerical computation, however, the magnetic helicity
\mathcal{H}
is typically not conserved (or even potentially ill-defined if the discrete magnetic field is not divergence-free).
Thus, the energy of the numerical magnetic field may decay to zero, leading to nonphysical solutions.
Enforcing a discrete analogue of this topological barrier is therefore crucial for meaningful long-term simulations.
A further challenge for the numerical investigation of the Parker conjecture is the resolution of tangential discontinuities in
\bm{B}

[
37
]
.
Numerical representations of the magnetic field must allow such discontinuities.

In this paper we consider the magneto-frictional system, a simplified version of the full time-dependent MHD equations
[
16
,
44
]
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{B}+\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}

\displaystyle=\bm{0},

(4a)

\displaystyle\bm{E}+\bm{u}\times\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\bm{0},

(4b)

\displaystyle\bm{j}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{curl}\bm{B},

(4c)

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\tau\bm{j}\times\bm{B},

(4d)

with initial data
\bm{B}|_{t=0}=\bm{B}_{0}
satisfying the magnetic Gauss law
\operatorname{div}\bm{B}_{0}=0
;
as a consequence of the magnetic advection equation (
4a
), the magnetic Gauss law holds for
\bm{B}
at all times
t>0
.
The parameter
\tau>0
is a coupling parameter.

Instead of coupling the velocity field
\bm{u}
via the Navier–Stokes equations, the magneto-frictional equations are closed by assuming a special form of
\bm{u}
(
4d
).
This guarantees the magnetic energy
\mathcal{E}(t)\coloneqq\int|\bm{B}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
is non-increasing, with rate

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}=-2\tau\int_{\Omega}|\bm{j}\times\bm{B}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(5)

thus avoiding the exchange of kinetic energy and magnetic energy permitted in the original MHD system.
The system furthermore retains the conservation of magnetic helicity
\mathcal{H}
, preventing the decay to zero during relaxation via the Arnold inequality
eq.

3
for initial data with non-zero
\mathcal{H}
.
At a stationary state (
\partial_{t}{\bm{B}}=0
), the dissipation of energy
eq.

5
implies the Lorentz force
\bm{j}\times\bm{B}
must vanish.
Should such a solution exist, it is referred to as a nonlinear force-free field or Beltrami field
[
37
]
, coinciding with an equilibrium of the full MHD system when
\bm{u}
is given by the coupling with the Navier–Stokes equations.
The existence of tangential discontinuities of the stationary solutions of
eq.

4
is therefore equivalent to the force-free version of the Parker conjecture
[
37
, Def. 2]
.
We consider the following boundary conditions

\bm{B}\cdot\bm{n}=0,\quad\bm{j}\times\bm{n}=\bm{0},\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

(6a)

These imply

\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=0,\quad\bm{E}\times\bm{n}=\bm{0},\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

(6b)

The analysis of the magneto-frictional system (
4
) is of broad interest. Whether the solution
\bm{B}
is able to develop finite-time singularities remains open; for recent progress in this direction, see
[
13
,
11
,
20
]
.

The numerical challenges described above (i.e. both conserving the magnetic helicity, and resolving tangential discontinuities) are pivotal when considering the magneto-frictional system
[
37
]
.
There is a rich literature on numerical investigation of magnetic relaxation.
Most existing Eulerian methods introduce artificial magnetic reconnection;
the topological structure is therefore lost, leading to non-physical solutions
[
49
]
.

Regarding Lagrangian discretizations, Craig & Sneyd
[
18
]
developed a finite-difference relaxation method to preserve the topology of the magnetic fields;
this has been used extensively to study the Parker conjecture
[
31
,
19
,
43
,
42
,
17
]
.
A mimetic Lagrangian method was proposed in
[
15
]
to ensure charge conservation.
A variational integrator for ideal MHD has been developed by
[
48
]
, which is both symplectic and momentum preserving;
this method has been applied to examine the singularity of the nonlinear force-free field
[
46
,
47
]
.
However, Lagrangian methods suffer from strong mesh distortions, preventing one from obtaining more conclusive results.

In this paper, we design a structure-preserving Eulerian finite-element discretization for the magneto-frictional system.
Our method relies on the finite element exterior calculus (FEEC)
[
6
,
5
,
26
]
and a recently proposed framework for enforcing conservation laws and dissipation inequalities
[
3
]
.
We believe this to be the first helicity-preserving Eulerian finite element method for this problem, which is highly desirable in computational solar physics.

The fields in our scheme are discretized via finite element de Rham complexes.
In particular, the magnetic field
\bm{B}
is discretized with the Raviart–Thomas
[
39
]
or Brezzi–Douglas–Marini
[
14
]
finite element spaces, which (crucially) allow tangential discontinuities.
The method preserves both the dissipation of energy and conservation of helicity, thus ensuring the topological barrier provided by the Arnold inequality (
3
) is inherited on the discrete level (see (
12e
) below) preventing some artificial magnetic reconnection.
The method is valid for unstructured meshes on general domains, avoids mesh distortions, and can be of arbitrary order in both space and time.

The Parker conjecture is often studied in settings with periodic boundary conditions (topologically equivalent to a torus) yielding a domain with nontrivial topology.
However, the magnetic potential
\bm{A}
, present in the definition of the helicity (
1
), is ill-defined in the presence of such topological irregularities.
Through a careful handling of certain harmonic forms, we further propose a conserved generalized helicity that is well-defined over certain topologically nontrivial domains;
the generalized helicity similarly demonstrates a (generalized) Arnold inequality (
3
).
Over such domains, our discretisation again preserves this generalized helicity, thus preserving the topological barrier.

Structure-preserving (or compatible) discretizations have been intensively studied in several contexts, including in geometric numerical integration
[
23
]
and FEEC
[
4
]
.
Various examples have demonstrated the crucial role of preserving certain structures.
For instance, long-term simulations for the Kepler problem with non-structure-preserving integrators may lead to accumulated errors in the orbit
[
23
, Fig. 2.2]
.
For Maxwell source and eigenvalue problems, finite element methods that do not respect the cohomological structures may lead to spurious solutions
[
4
]
.

De Rham complex–based methods have been widely discussed for constructing structure-preserving discretizations in MHD
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
32
,
21
]
.
Most, however, are developed primarily from analytic perspectives. The fundamental question of the qualitative importance of helicity-preservation is less often addressed, with numerical examples of their physical significance remaining rare. A key contribution of this work is in demonstrating that, to obtain meaningful numerical solutions for the magneto-frictional equations (
4
), the discrete conservation of helicity is crucial.

The remainder of this paper proceeds as follows.
In
Section

2
, we introduce certain preliminaries.
In
Section

3
, we propose our structure-preserving scheme;
through a generalized notion of helicity, we extend the Arnold inequality to domains with nontrivial topology.
In
Section

4
, we present numerical results, and compare our proposed method to other discretizations to explore the physical significance of helicity preservation.
In
Section

5
, we conclude with a general outlook on the numerical simulation of the Parker conjecture.

3
Structure-preserving discretization

For the magnetic helicity to be well-defined on the discrete level, our scheme must preserve the magnetic Gauss law (
\operatorname{div}\bm{B}=0
) at least up to solver tolerances and machine precision;
we therefore discretize the magnetic field
\bm{B}_{h}
in the
H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{div})
space (
10b
).
Since
\operatorname{curl}H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{curl})\subset H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{div})
, the advection equation (
4a
) then suggests we discretize
\bm{E}_{h}
in the
H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{curl})
space.
As shown in
Theorem

2
below, this is sufficient to ensure
\partial_{t}\bm{B}_{h}+\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}_{h}=\bm{0}
exactly, additionally guaranteeing that if
\operatorname{div}\bm{B}_{0}=0
then the magnetic Gauss law holds pointwise on
\bm{B}_{h}
for every
t
.

To replicate the right physics, we must preserve both the helicity conservation (
2
) and energy dissipation (
5
) laws.
The general idea proposed in
[
3
]
for designing numerical schemes that replicate conservation and dissipation properties is to rewrite these laws as time integrals involving certain associated test functions;
discrete approximations of the associated test functions are then introduced as auxiliary variables in the scheme.
In this context, the two auxiliary variables introduced for helicity conservation and energy dissipation are an auxiliary current
\bm{j}_{h}
and magnetic field
\bm{H}_{h}
,
L^{2}
projections of
\operatorname{curl}\bm{B}_{h}
and
\bm{B}_{h}
onto the space
H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{curl})
respectively.
1
1

1

We may equivalently write
\bm{j}_{h}=\operatorname{curl}_{h}\bm{B}_{h}
, where
\operatorname{curl}_{h}
is the
L^{2}
adjoint operator of
\operatorname{curl}
in the discrete complex (
10b
).

These introduced auxiliary variables resemble those proposed for similar MHD systems in
[
26
,
29
]
with extension to higher order in time.

We therefore propose the following semi-discrete structure-preserving discretization of the magneto-frictional equations (
4

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}{\bm{B}}_{h},\bm{C}_{h})+(\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}_{h},\bm{C}_{h})

\displaystyle=0,

(11a)

\displaystyle(\bm{E}_{h},\bm{F}_{h})+\tau((\bm{j}_{h}\times\bm{H}_{h})\times\bm{H}_{h},\bm{F}_{h})

\displaystyle=0,

(11b)

\displaystyle(\bm{j}_{h},\bm{K}_{h})

\displaystyle=(\bm{B}_{h},\operatorname{curl}\bm{K}_{h}),

(11c)

\displaystyle(\bm{H}_{h},\bm{D}_{h})

\displaystyle=(\bm{B}_{h},\bm{D}_{h}),

(11d)

3.1
Structure-preserving properties on contractible domains

Let us first consider contractible
\Omega
, i.e. domains with trivial topology.

We summarize some properties of our semi-discretization (

As each of the results therein represents a linear or quadratic structure, they are preserved under the Gauss collocation time discretization

Theorem 2
(Structure-preserving properties of the discretisation).

Any solution to (
11
) satisfies:

1.

magnetic advection equation,

\partial_{t}\bm{B}_{h}+\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}_{h}=0,

(12a)

2.

magnetic Gauss law,

\operatorname{div}\bm{B}_{h}=\operatorname{div}\bm{B}_{0}=0.

(12b)

Since the magnetic Gauss law (
12b
) holds exactly, and the subcomplex (
10b
) is exact on contractible domains, there exists a magnetic potential
\bm{A}_{h}\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{curl})
satisfying
\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}_{h}=\bm{B}_{h}
.
With the discrete energy
\mathcal{E}_{h}\coloneqq\|\bm{B}_{h}\|^{2}
and helicity
\mathcal{H}_{h}\coloneqq(\bm{A}_{h},\bm{B}_{h})
, we have the following:

3.

energy dissipation,

\mathcal{E}_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{h}|_{t=0}=-2\tau\int_{0}^{t}\|\bm{j}_{h}\times\bm{H}_{h}\|^{2}\leq 0,

(12c)

4.

helicity conservation,

\mathcal{H}_{h}-\mathcal{H}_{h}|_{t=0}=0.

(12d)

With
\mathcal{H}_{h}
well-defined, we further have:

5.

the discrete Arnold inequality,

C|\mathcal{H}_{h}|\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}.

(12e)

Proof.

We see the magnetic advection equation (
12a
) by considering
\bm{C}_{h}=\partial_{t}\bm{B}_{h}+\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}_{h}(\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{div}))
(see e.g.

]

The magnetic Gauss law (

) is an immediate consequence, as

For the semi-discrete energy dissipation law (

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{h}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{t}\bm{B}_{h},\bm{B}_{h})=-2\int_{0}^{t}(\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}_{h},\bm{B}_{h})

(13a)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{t}(\bm{E}_{h},\operatorname{curl}\bm{B}_{h})=-2\int_{0}^{t}(\bm{E}_{h},\bm{j}_{h})

\displaystyle=-2\tau\int_{0}^{t}\|\bm{j}_{h}\times\bm{H}_{h}\|^{2}\leq 0,

(13c)

where the second equality holds by the magnetic advection equation (

), and the fourth and last hold by considering

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{h}-\mathcal{H}_{h}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=2\tau\int_{0}^{t}((\bm{j}_{h}\times\bm{H}_{h})\times\bm{H}_{h},\bm{H}_{h})=0

3.2
Generalized helicity and structure-preserving properties on non-contractible domains

Aspects of the topology of the domain
\Omega
are encoded in the de Rham complex (
10a
).
Over contractible, or topologically trivial, domains (Betti numbers
(\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})=(1,0,0,0)
), for example, the de Rham complex is exact.
Over domains with a single pair of periodic boundary conditions
2
2

2

That is, domains that are topologically equivalent to solid tori.
(Betti numbers
(\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})=(1,1,0,0)
), the de Rham complex is no longer exact at
H_{0}(\operatorname{div})
;
namely, there exists over such domains a (non-zero) constant field
\bm{B}_{H}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{div})
, for which there does not exist a potential
\bm{A}_{H}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
satisfying
\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}_{H}=\bm{B}_{H}
.
3
3

3

In fact, since
\operatorname{div}\bm{B}_{H}=0
and
\operatorname{curl}\bm{B}_{H}=\bm{0}
, this field
\bm{B}_{H}
is a harmonic 2-form.

The Parker conjecture is often studied over such domains (see
[
37
, Fig. 1]
).
However, the definition of the helicity
\mathcal{H}
(
1
) relies on the exactness of the de Rham complex at
H_{0}(\operatorname{div})
(Betti number
\beta_{1}=0
) for the existence of a magnetic potential
\bm{A}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
;
in the presence of such topological irregularities, it is no longer well-defined.

In this section, we no longer assume
\Omega
to be topologically trivial.
In particular, we shall allow the de Rham complex (
10a
) not to be exact at
H_{0}(\operatorname{div})
(no conditions on Betti number
\beta_{1}
).
This necessitates the definition of a generalized helicity (see (
18
) below) that accounts for the harmonic forms introduced by the nontrivial topology.
We shall, however, continue to require exactness at
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
(Betti number
\beta_{2}=0
), i.e. for any
\bm{A}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
satisfying
\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}=\bm{0}
there exists
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}
such that
\bm{A}=\operatorname{grad}\varphi
;
this is necessary for the gauge invariance of our generalized helicity (see
Theorem

6
below) and holds on the domain of current interest:
the box with a single pair of periodic boundary conditions.

Let us first consider the continuous case.
By the Hodge decomposition
[
6
, Sec. 4.2]
, we can decompose a divergence-free magnetic field
\bm{B}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{div})
into

\bm{B}=\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+\bm{B}_{H},

(16)

where
\bm{A}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
, and
\bm{B}_{H}
is a harmonic 2-form (
\operatorname{div}\bm{B}_{H}=0
and
\operatorname{curl}\bm{B}_{H}=\bm{0}
).
Given
\bm{B}
, the decomposition (
16

\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}
and
\bm{B}_{H}
uniquely.
4
4

4

Note that, while
\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}
is determined uniquely,
\bm{A}
is not.

Theorem 4
(Invariance of the harmonic component).

The harmonic component
\bm{B}_{H}
of
\bm{B}

Proof.

Substituting the Hodge decomposition for
\bm{B}
(
16
) into the magnetic advection equation (
4a
),

\bm{0}=\partial_{t}\bm{B}+\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}=\partial_{t}[\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+\bm{B}_{H}]+\operatorname{curl}\bm{E}=\operatorname{curl}[\partial_{t}\bm{A}+\bm{E}]+\partial_{t}\bm{B}_{H}.

(17)

By the uniqueness of the Hodge decomposition, both
\operatorname{curl}[\partial_{t}\bm{A}+\bm{E}]=\bm{0}
and
\partial_{t}\bm{B}_{H}=\bm{0}
, i.e. in the latter case the harmonic component
\bm{B}_{H}
is constant.

With the Hodge decomposition of
\bm{B}
(
16
) established, our proposed generalized helicity is defined as follows.

Definition 5
(Generalized helicity).

With the Hodge decomposition of
\bm{B}
(
16
), we define a generalized helicity
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
by

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=(\bm{A},\bm{B}+\bm{B}_{H})\quad(=(\bm{A},\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+2\bm{B}_{H})).

(18)

Dependent on the topology of
\Omega
, we see this definition is gauge-invariant

Theorem 6
(Gauge invariance).

Again assuming the de Rham complex (
10a
) is exact at
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
(Betti number
\beta_{2}=0
), the definition of the generalized helicity
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
(
18
) is gauge-invariant.

Proof.

The proof holds similarly to the non-generalized case.
Consider
\bm{A}
through its Hodge decomposition,

\bm{A}=\operatorname{grad}\varphi+\operatorname{curl}\bm{\psi},

(19)

for some
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}
,
\bm{\psi}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{div})
.
The condition
\bm{B}=\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+\bm{B}_{H}=\operatorname{curl}^{2}\bm{\psi}+\bm{B}_{H}
determines
\operatorname{curl}\bm{\psi}
uniquely, but imposes no restrictions on
\operatorname{grad}\varphi
(or equivalently
\varphi
);
this dictates the choice of gauge on
\bm{A}
.
However, different choices of gauge, i.e. different choices of
\operatorname{grad}\varphi
, do not affect
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
, as
\operatorname{grad}\varphi
does not contribute to the helicity:

(\operatorname{grad}\varphi,\bm{B}+\bm{B}_{H})=-(\varphi,\operatorname{div}(\bm{B}+\bm{B}_{H}))=0,

(20)

where the boundary term vanishes due to the boundary condition
\varphi=0
for
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}
.

We show now that
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is an invariant under the relaxation process, similar to
\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 7
(Invariance of the generalized helicity).

The generalized helicity
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is constant in time.

Proof.

Consider first
\partial_{t}\bm{A}
.
From (
17
),
\operatorname{curl}(\partial_{t}\bm{A}+\bm{E})=\bm{0}
;
by the Hodge decomposition then, there exists
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}
such that

\partial_{t}\bm{A}+\bm{E}=\partial_{t}\bm{A}-\bm{u}\times\bm{B}=\operatorname{grad}\varphi.

(21)

Considering the evolution of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
,

\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=(\partial_{t}\bm{A},\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+2\bm{B}_{H})+(\bm{A},\partial_{t}\operatorname{curl}\bm{A})\\
=(\partial_{t}\bm{A},\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+2\bm{B}_{H})+(\partial_{t}\bm{A},\operatorname{curl}\bm{A})=2(\partial_{t}\bm{A},\bm{B})

(22)

where in the first equality we use the invariance of
\bm{B}_{H}
(
Theorem

4
) and in the second we apply integration by parts.
Using (
21
), we have

\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=2(\bm{u}\times\bm{B}+\operatorname{grad}\varphi,\bm{B})=-2(\varphi,\operatorname{div}\bm{B})=0,

(23)

where we use the orthogonality of the cross product, and that the boundary term vanishes due to the boundary condition
\varphi=0
for
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}
.

We conclude by showing that a generalized version of the Arnold inequality holds.

Theorem 8
(Generalized Arnold inequality).

There exists some constant
C>0
(dependent only on the domain
\Omega
) such that

|\tilde{\mathcal{H}}|\leq C^{-1}\mathcal{E}.

(24)

Proof.

As in (
19
), let
\bm{A}
have Hodge decomposition
\bm{A}=\operatorname{grad}\varphi+\operatorname{curl}\bm{\psi}
.
As
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is independent of
\varphi
, let us assume without loss of generality
\varphi=0
.
Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz, generalized Poincaré and Young’s inequality respectively, there exists some constant
C>0
such that

|\tilde{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\|\bm{A}\|\|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+2\bm{B}_{H}\|\leq(2C)^{-1}\|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}\|\|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+2\bm{B}_{H}\|\\
\leq(4C)^{-1}(\|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}\|^{2}+\|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+2\bm{B}_{H}\|^{2}).

(25a)

Since
\bm{B}_{H}
is a harmonic 2-form, it is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to
\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}
, implying

|\tilde{\mathcal{H}}|\leq(4C)^{-1}(\|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}\|^{2}+\|\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+2\bm{B}_{H}\|^{2})\leq C^{-1}\|\bm{B}\|^{2}.

(25b)

Considering our proposed semi-discretisation (
11
), similar arguments to those in the proof of
Theorem

2
hold under these nontrivial topologies, for the analogous results presented above.
In summary, the discrete magnetic field
\bm{B}_{h}
has Hodge decomposition
\bm{B}_{h}=\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}_{h}+\bm{B}_{Hh}
for some
\bm{A}_{h}\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{curl})
.
The discrete harmonic component
\bm{B}_{Hh}
remains constant in time, the discrete generalized helicity
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{h}
remains constant, and the generalized discrete Arnold inequality (
24
) holds;
these results transfer to the fully discrete case when using a Gauss collocation method for the time discretization.
We have therefore the same quantitative and qualitative guarantees for numerical solutions derived from our discrete scheme on domains with nontrivial topologies.

Remark 9
(Domains with nontrivial harmonic 1-forms).

We may still define the generalized helicity (
18
) where the de Rham complex is not exact at
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
(Betti number
\beta_{2}\neq 0
), however the definition is gauge-dependent.
To see this, consider the Hodge decomposition

\bm{A}=\operatorname{grad}\varphi+\operatorname{curl}\bm{\psi}+\bm{A}_{H},

(26)

where
\bm{A}_{H}
is a harmonic 1-form;
while
\bm{A}_{H}
is not determined by the condition
\bm{B}=\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+\bm{B}_{H}
, different choices of
\bm{A}_{H}
give rise to different values of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
.
Due to the gauge-dependence, the statement of
Theorem

7
may change to:
there exists a certain choice of magnetic potential
\bm{A}=\bm{A}_{1}\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
(satisfying
\bm{B}=\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}+\bm{B}_{H}
) such that the generalized magnetic helicity
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is constant.
The statement of
Theorem

8
may change to:
there exists a certain choice of magnetic potential
\bm{A}=\bm{A}_{2}
such that the generalized Arnold inequality (
24
) holds.
However, these potentials
\bm{A}_{1}
and
\bm{A}_{2}
may differ.
Similar arguments hold on the discrete level.

Remark 10
.

There have been many efforts in the literature to generalize the concept of helicity to topologically nontrivial domains.
See, for example, the notions of relative helicity
[
7
]
and the universal magnetic helicity integral proposed in
[
25
]
.

For domains
\Omega
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, many approaches use a potential
\hat{\bm{A}}\in H(\operatorname{curl})
such that
\bm{B}=\operatorname{curl}\hat{\bm{A}}
, without enforcing the boundary condition
\hat{\bm{A}}\times\bm{n}=\bm{0}
.
Gauge invariance is then recovered by introducing a certain boundary integral in
\hat{\bm{A}}
on
\partial\Omega
.
Over domains topologically equivalent to tori, the Bevir–Gray helicity
[
12
]
is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{\rm BG}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\hat{\bm{A}}\cdot\bm{B}-\oint_{\gamma_{A}}\hat{\bm{A}}\cdot\bm{t}_{A}\oint_{\gamma_{B}}\hat{\bm{A}}\cdot\bm{t}_{B},

(27)

where
\gamma_{A}
and
\gamma_{B}
are closed paths on
\partial\Omega
along the minor (A-cycle) and major (B-cycle) circumferences, and
\bm{t}_{A}
,
\bm{t}_{B}
are their respective unit tangents. McTaggart & Valli
[
33
]
extended this definition to more general topologies.

The Bevir–Gray construction addresses the same topological difficulty as our generalized helicity
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
(
18
), but by a different mechanism: it uses cutting surfaces to define the necessary integrals, whereas our approach uses harmonic forms. The two settings are therefore not straightforward to compare. In particular, in our definition the vector potential
\bm{A}
is chosen as the preimage of the curl-range component of
\bm{B}
, while in the Bevir–Gray framework it appears that
\hat{\bm{A}}
is given a priori and
\bm{B}
is then defined by
\operatorname{curl}\hat{\bm{A}}
. Clarifying the precise correspondence between these viewpoints would require further investigation.

4.1
Projection of ICs and evaluation of helicity

The exact divergence-free condition on
\bm{B}_{h}
is crucial for our structure-preserving properties (
Theorem

2
).
For each simulation, the projection of the continuous initial data
\bm{B}_{0}
to the divergence-free subspace of
H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{div})
necessitates the solution of a saddle-point problem:
find
(\bm{B}_{0,h},p_{h})\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{div})\times L_{0}^{2,h}
such that

\displaystyle(\bm{B}_{0,h},\bm{C}_{h})-(p_{h},\nabla\cdot\bm{C}_{h})

\displaystyle=(\bm{B}_{0},\bm{C}_{h}),

(28a)

\displaystyle(q_{h},\nabla\cdot\bm{B}_{0,h})

\displaystyle=0,

(28b)

for all
(\bm{C}_{h},q_{h})\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{div})\times L_{0}^{2,h}
, where
\bm{B}_{0,h}\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{div})
represent our discrete, divergence-free ICs.

The divergence-free condition is required in particular for the discrete helicity
\mathcal{H}_{h}
to be well-defined.
At each timestep, we evaluate
\mathcal{H}_{h}
for a given magnetic field configuration
\bm{B}_{h}
by finding
\bm{A}_{h}\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{curl})
such that

(\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}_{h},\operatorname{curl}\bm{C}_{h})=(\bm{B}_{h},\operatorname{curl}\bm{C}_{h})\quad\forall\bm{C}_{h}\in H_{0}^{h}(\operatorname{curl}).

(29)

This system is symmetric and consistent, but singular. Therefore, we use MINRES with the minimum norm refinement strategy of Liu
et al.

[
30
]
to find the pseudoinverse solution. This is used to define the discrete helicity
\mathcal{H}_{h}
.

5
Conclusions

We presented a finite-element discretization (
11
) for the magneto-frictional system (
4
), with novel structure-preserving properties (
12
) that are essential for numerical investigations into the Parker conjecture.
Through the conservation of helicity (
12d
), the scheme preserves a discrete version (
12e
) of the topological barrier provided by the Arnold inequality (
3
), preventing the decay to spurious trivial solutions.
Extending the helicity and the Arnold inequality to certain topologically nontrivial domains, we see our scheme further retains these structures.
Numerical results confirm these structure-preserving properties.

The proposed method offers a promising tool for the numerical investigation of the Parker conjecture without the use of Lagrangian formulations, as well as related questions, including the formation of finite-time singularities during magnetic relaxation.
In future work, we aim to develop numerical tools to distinguish whether the steady state exhibits tangential discontinuities as suggested in the Parker conjecture. We also intend to investigate efficient preconditioners to ease scaling to larger problems. We aim further to employ adaptive timestep strategies to accelerate the magnetic relaxation process, allowing faster convergence to steady states while still maintaining the Arnold inequality’s topological barrier.

Beyond solving the magneto-frictional system itself, our work serves as a striking example of the importance of structure preservation. While for many problems, such methods are known to improve the accuracy of standard discretizations, the benefit for magnetic relaxation is both quantitative and qualitative: helicity preservation is essential to ensure that numerical solutions cannot collapse to unphysical trivial states.

Appendix A
Proof of the Arnold inequality

Theorem 15
(Arnold inequality
[
8
]
).

Let
\bm{B}
be a divergence-free field over a topologically trivial domain
\Omega
, satisfying
\bm{B}\cdot\bm{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Then there exists a vector potential
\bm{A}
such that
\bm{B}=\operatorname{curl}\bm{A}
,
\bm{A}\times\bm{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and

\left|\int_{\Omega}\bm{A}\cdot\bm{B}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\int_{\Omega}\bm{B}\cdot\bm{B}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(35)

where
C>0
depends only on
\Omega
. Moreover, the quantity
\int_{\Omega}\bm{A}\cdot\bm{B}\,\mathrm{d}x
is independent of the particular choice of
\bm{A}
.

Proof.

For a topologically trivial domain, one can choose
\bm{A}

L^{2}

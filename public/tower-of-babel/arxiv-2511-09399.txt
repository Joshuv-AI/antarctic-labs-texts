Non-commutative geometry and thermodynamics of the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole
Slimane Zaiem, Fatma Zohra Bara, Mohamed Aimen Larbei

Abstract
We investigate the thermodynamic properties of a Schwarzschild anti-de Sitter (AdS) black hole within the framework of non-commutative geometry. We derive and analyze the black hole’s thermodynamic functions, showing that they depend critically on the non-commutativity parameter, denoted as Θ \Theta , while still satisfying the first law of thermodynamics. Stability analysis reveals that the non-commutative AdS black hole undergoes a phase transition at a critical point. Moreover, the thermodynamic behavior closely resembles that of a van der Waals fluid, with the noncommutativity introducing a correction term to the black hole’s surface temperature. Our results indicate that the parameter Θ \Theta is of the order of the Planck scale ( ℓ p \ell_{p} ) and functions as a novel thermodynamic variable within the system.



Keywords:
Non-commutative geometry; Schwarzschild-Ads black hole; Thermodynamic proprieties.

1
Introduction

Since the pioneering work of Hawking and Page in 1983
[
1
]
, who first discovered the phase transition in Schwarzschild–AdS (SAdS) black holes, black hole thermodynamics has attracted considerable attention and has led to many profound results. By identifying the black hole mass and cosmological constant with enthalpy and pressure, respectively, the first law of AdS black hole thermodynamics was established
[
2
]
, enabling a deeper understanding of the thermodynamic structure of AdS black holes.

Hawking showed that the thermal radiation of a Schwarzschild black hole is characterized by the temperature
T_{H}=\hbar/(8\pi GM)
, which arises purely from quantum effects. However, the Hawking temperature diverges as
M\to 0
, leaving the final stage of black hole evaporation unresolved. This issue has motivated numerous studies exploring the impact of quantum gravity models on black hole thermodynamics, including frameworks that incorporate a minimal length scale
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Among these, noncommutative (NC) geometry has proven to be particularly significant, as it provides a consistent deformation of spacetime that allows the investigation of quantum gravitational corrections to classical thermodynamic quantities. Noncommutative geometry, inspired by string theory
[
9
]
, treats spacetime coordinates as noncommuting operators, thus introducing a natural ultraviolet cutoff.

By analogy with the commutation relation between position and momentum in quantum mechanics, noncommutative geometry postulates the following commutator between spacetime coordinates:

[\hat{x}^{\mu},\hat{x}^{\nu}]=i\Theta^{\mu\nu},

(1)

where
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
is an antisymmetric constant tensor. The parameter
\Theta
has dimensions of
(\text{length})^{2}
and characterizes the minimal spacetime scale, with
\sqrt{\Theta}
typically assumed to be of the order of the Planck length
\ell_{p}

[
10
,
11
]
. The Bopp shift gives the relation between the commutative and noncommutative coordinate systems:

\hat{x}^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu},

(2)

where
\overline{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}/\hbar
. In noncommutative spacetime, the usual product of functions is replaced by the Moyal (or star) product
[
27
]
:

\displaystyle(f\ast g)(x)

\displaystyle=f(x)\,e^{\frac{i}{2}\Theta^{\mu\nu}\overleftarrow{\partial_{\mu}}\partial_{\nu}}g(y)\big|_{y=x},

\displaystyle=f(x)g(x)+\frac{i}{2}\Theta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}f(x)\,\partial_{\nu}g(x)+\mathcal{O}(\Theta^{2}).

(3)

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the thermodynamics of the Schwarzschild–AdS black hole in noncommutative spacetime. Using Bopp’s shift and the Moyal product, we derive first-order corrections in the NC parameter for the black hole mass, Hawking temperature, and entropy. By relating the black hole mass and cosmological constant to enthalpy and pressure, we establish the first law of thermodynamics for the noncommutative AdS black hole (NC AdS-BH) and confirm its validity. Our results show that noncommutativity removes the divergent behavior of the temperature, allowing an estimate of
\Theta\simeq 0.3\,\ell_{p}
. We also analyze the heat capacity to discuss the stability of the NC black hole and examine the Helmholtz free energy to explore the associated phase transition.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the noncommutative corrections to the AdS–Schwarzschild metric using Bopp’s shift and derive the relation between total mass and horizon radius. Section 3 presents a detailed analysis of the thermodynamic properties of the AdS–Schwarzschild black hole in noncommutative spacetime. Finally, Section 4 summarizes our main results and conclusions.

2
AdS–Schwarzschild black holes in noncommutative spacetime

The NC Schwarzschild–AdS spacetime metric
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
, up to first order in
\overline{\Theta}
, takes the form
[
12
]
:

ds^{2}=-\hat{g}_{tt}(r,\overline{\Theta})\,dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\hat{g}_{tt}(r,\overline{\Theta})}+\hat{g}_{\theta\theta}(r,\overline{\Theta})\,d\theta^{2}+\hat{g}_{\varphi\varphi}(r,\overline{\Theta})\,d\varphi^{2}.

(4)

In this study, we consider only the case of noncommutativity in the
(r,\varphi)
-plane, described by the following antisymmetric matrix
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
:

\left(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&-\Theta\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&\Theta&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where
\Theta
is a real positive constant.

Using the Bopp shift (
2
) and the definition of the Moyal product (
3
), together with the components of
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
, we obtain the noncommutative AdS–Schwarzschild metric components
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
up to first order in
\Theta
:

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{tt}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}\right)+\frac{\overline{\Theta}L}{r}\left(\frac{M}{r}-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\overline{\Theta}^{2}),

(6)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=r^{2}+r\overline{\Theta}L+\mathcal{O}(\overline{\Theta}^{2}),

(7)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{\varphi\varphi}

\displaystyle=r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+r\overline{\Theta}L\sin^{2}\theta+\mathcal{O}(\overline{\Theta}^{2}).

(8)

The event horizon is determined by the zeros of the metric function
\hat{g}_{tt}
, as given in Eq. (
6
), and is obtained perturbatively up to first order in
\overline{\Theta}
. The corresponding NC event horizon of the SAdS black hole is then
[
12
]
:

\hat{r}_{h}=r_{h}+\frac{\overline{\Theta}L}{2},

where
r_{h}=2M+8\Lambda M^{3}/3+\overline{\Theta}L/2
corresponds to the horizon radius in the commutative case (
\Theta=0
). The effect of noncommutativity is small but becomes significant at short distances, while it remains negligible at large scales.

To derive the relation between the total mass and the horizon radius in noncommutative spacetime, we impose
\hat{g}_{tt}(r_{h},\overline{\Theta})=0
, leading to:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{h}}{2}\left(1+\frac{\Theta L}{2r_{h}}\right)-\frac{\Lambda}{6}r_{h}^{3}\left(1+\frac{3\Theta L}{2r_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{h}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r_{h}^{2}\right)+\left(1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2}\right)\frac{\Theta L}{4}+\mathcal{O}(\Theta^{2}).

(9)

The first term in Eq. (
9
),
\frac{r_{h}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r_{h}^{2}\right)
, represents the black hole mass in AdS spacetime, while the second term,
\left(1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2}\right)\frac{\Theta L}{4}
, corresponds to the correction induced by noncommutative geometry. In the limit
\Theta\to 0
, the standard commutative AdS black hole mass is recovered:

M\to m=\frac{r_{h}}{2}-\frac{\Lambda}{6}r_{h}^{3}.

.

Figure

illustrates the behavior of the noncommutative AdS black hole mass as a function of the horizon radius, according to Eq. (

). We observe the presence of a lower bound
M_{0}=\frac{\Theta}{4}
, occurring at
r_{0}=\frac{\Theta}{2}
. This minimum mass increases with
\Theta
, indicating that noncommutative geometry introduces a new minimal mass that prevents the black hole mass from vanishing at the origin. Hence, as
r_{h}\to 0
, the mass approaches a finite nonzero value
M\to M_{0}
, in contrast to the commutative Schwarzschild case where
M\to 0
, and to the noncommutative Schwarzschild case, where it diverges
[
12
]
. This nonvanishing minimal mass provides insight into the possible final stage of black hole evaporation.

3
Thermodynamics

3.1
Thermodynamic functions and the first law

The NC temperature is defined using the NC surface gravity:

\hat{T}=\frac{\hat{k}}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left.\frac{\partial\hat{g}_{00}}{\partial r}\right|_{r=r_{h}}.

(10)

Using the deformed metric components and the mass expression in Eq. (9), we obtain the NC temperature:

\hat{T}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}}\left(\frac{2M}{r_{h}}-\frac{2}{3}\Lambda r_{h}^{2}+\bar{\Theta}\frac{ML}{r_{h}^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}}(1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2})-\frac{\bar{\Theta}L}{8\pi r_{h}^{2}}(1+\Lambda r_{h}^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\Theta^{2}).

(11)

The first term,
\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}}(1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2})
, corresponds to the temperature of an ordinary Schwarzschild–AdS black hole, while the second term,
-\bar{\Theta}\frac{L}{8\pi r_{h}^{2}}(1+\Lambda r_{h}^{2})
, represents the noncommutative correction.

, noncommutativity removes the divergence of the Hawking temperature, in analogy with the effect of electric charge in the Reissner–Nordström black hole
[
13
]
. As
r_{h}
decreases, the surface temperature
\hat{T}
increases, reaches a maximum value
\hat{T}^{\text{max}}=0.039/(\Theta L)
at
r_{h}^{m}=\Theta L
, and then rapidly decreases to zero at
r_{h}^{\text{min}}=\Theta L/2
. This behavior resembles that of quantum-corrected black holes
[

]
.

We observe that decreasing the noncommutative parameter
\Theta
leads to a higher maximum surface temperature, indicating that
\Theta
acts as a quantum correction parameter. For
\Theta=0
,
\hat{T}
reduces to the ordinary Hawking temperature of a Schwarzschild–AdS black hole. In natural units (
\hbar=k_{B}=c=1
), the thermal energy is
E^{\text{th}}=\hat{T}^{\text{max}}
, and the black hole mass reads

M=\frac{1}{2G}\Theta=\frac{\Theta}{2}M_{\text{Planck}}^{-2}.

(12)

At the critical point
r_{h}^{c}
, the thermal energy and mass of the black hole are of the same order of magnitude. The NC parameter can then be estimated as

\Theta\approx 0.3\times\ell_{p}.

(13)

This result shows that the noncommutativity parameter of spacetime is linked to the Planck scale
\ell_{p}
, consistent with experimental constraints from gravitational wave data
[
13
]
and with previous analyses of NC Schwarzschild black hole thermodynamics
[
15
,
16
]
. Similar studies have also estimated
\Theta
to be around
0.1\times\ell_{p}
by examining black hole thermodynamics
[

,
18
,

,
20
]
.

The entropy of the NC black hole is given by

\hat{S}=\left.\frac{\hat{A}}{4}\right|_{r=r_{h}},

(14)

where
\hat{A}
is the area of the event horizon in noncommutative spacetime:

\hat{A}=\int\int\sqrt{\hat{g}_{\theta\theta}\hat{g}_{\varphi\varphi}}\,d\theta\,d\varphi=4\pi r_{h}^{2}\left(1+\Theta\frac{L}{r_{h}}\right).

(15)

Hence, the entropy of the AdS black hole in noncommutative geometry reads

\hat{S}(r_{h},\Theta)=\pi r_{h}^{2}\left(1+\frac{\Theta L}{r_{h}}\right)=S^{\text{o}}+\Theta L\pi r_{h}+\mathcal{O}(\Theta^{2}),

(16)

where
S^{\text{o}}=\pi r_{h}^{2}
is the entropy in the commutative case corresponding to
\Theta=0
. The entropy thus increases by

\hat{S}-S^{\text{o}}=\Theta L\pi r_{h},

a positive correction depending on both the noncommutativity parameter
\Theta
and the black hole parameters.

In the thermodynamic interpretation of AdS black holes, the cosmological constant is associated with the pressure:

P=-\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi}=\frac{3}{8\pi l^{2}},

(17)

where
\Lambda=-3/l^{2}
. The NC black hole thus satisfies the first law of thermodynamics:

dM=\hat{T}d\hat{S}+VdP.

(18)

For a fixed cosmological constant, the second term in Eq. (
18
) vanishes. From Eqs. (
9
) and (
16
), we find

\displaystyle\hat{T}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial r_{h}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\hat{S}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}}(1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2})-\frac{\Theta L}{8\pi r_{h}^{2}}(1+\Lambda r_{h}^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\Theta^{2}).

(19)

The expression of
\hat{T}
obtained here is identical to that derived from the surface gravity in Eq. (
11
), confirming that the differential form
dM=TdS
holds. Therefore, the mass definition in Eq. (
9
) satisfies the first law of thermodynamics, in contrast to previous claims
[
9
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
that it might be violated.

3.2
Equation of state and critical point

From Eq. (9), the pressure
P
can be expressed as:

P=\frac{\hat{T}}{2r_{h}}\left(1-\frac{\Theta L}{6r_{h}}\right)-\frac{1}{8\pi r_{h}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2\Theta L}{3r_{h}}\right).

(20)

Following Refs.
[
24
,
25
]
, we introduce the specific volume
v=2r_{h}
, which allows the equation of state to be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{P}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{T}}{v}\left(1-\frac{\Theta L}{3v}\right)-\frac{1}{2\pi v^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4\Theta L}{3v}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{T}}{v}-\frac{1}{2\pi v^{2}}-\frac{\Theta\hat{T}L}{3v^{2}}+\frac{2\Theta L}{3\pi v^{3}}.

(21)

The critical point
(P_{c},v_{c},T_{c})
is determined from the inflection point conditions:

\left(\frac{\partial\hat{P}}{\partial v}\right)_{\hat{T}}=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{P}}{\partial v^{2}}\right)_{\hat{T}}=0.

(22)

Solving these conditions yields:

\frac{1}{T_{c}}=4\pi\Theta L,\quad v_{c}=\frac{8}{3}L\Theta,\quad\frac{1}{P_{c}}=\frac{64}{3}\pi\Theta^{2}L^{2}.

(23)

The critical ratio is then found to be:

\frac{P_{c}v_{c}}{T_{c}}=0.5.

(24)

Pressure
\hat{P}(v)

As shown in Fig.
4
, for a fixed noncommutativity parameter
\Theta
, the system exhibits a single critical point. The pressure–volume curves resemble those of a van der Waals fluid, indicating the occurrence of phase transitions when
\hat{T}<\hat{T}_{c}
. In this regime, the system undergoes a first-order phase transition between small and large black holes. For
\hat{T}>\hat{T}_{c}
, the critical behavior disappears, and the pressure increases monotonically, corresponding to a single, large, and thermodynamically unstable black hole phase.

From Eq. (6), the noncommutative equation of state can be recast in a more compact form:

\left(P+\frac{1}{2\pi v^{2}}\right)\left(v-\frac{\Theta L}{3}\right)=\hat{T}-\frac{2\Theta L}{3v^{2}}\equiv\tilde{T}.

(25)

Equation (
25
) closely resembles the van der Waals equation for real fluids, with an additional correction term

\frac{2\Theta L}{3v^{2}}
induced by spacetime noncommutativity. This term, though typically small—since it
\Theta
is of the order of the Planck length—becomes significant in regimes of extremely high energy and small length scales, highlighting the quantum-gravitational effects encoded by noncommutative geometry.

3.3
Heat Capacity and Free Energy

Another key quantity in the thermodynamic study of black holes is the specific heat capacity at constant pressure,
\hat{C}
, which characterizes the local thermodynamic stability of the black hole. It is related to the surface temperature and entropy through the relation:

\displaystyle\hat{C}\left(r_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{T}\left(\frac{\partial\hat{S}}{\partial\hat{T}}\right)=\hat{T}\left(\frac{\partial\hat{S}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\hat{T}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=2\pi r_{h}^{3}\frac{1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2}-\Lambda r_{h}\Theta L}{-\Lambda r_{h}^{3}-r_{h}+\Theta L}.

(26)

Figure

illustrates the behavior of
\hat{C}(r_{h})
for fixed
\Lambda=-7.5
,
L=1
, and various values of
\Theta
.
For
r_{h}<r_{h}^{c}
, the heat capacity is negative (
\hat{C}<0
), implying that the black hole is thermodynamically unstable and cannot reach local equilibrium.
In contrast, for
r_{h}>r_{h}^{c}
, we have
\hat{C}>0
, indicating thermodynamic stability.
When
\Theta=0
, we recover the standard Schwarzschild-AdS behavior, where
\hat{C}
changes sign at
r_{h}=r_{h}^{c}
, confirming the existence of a phase transition.
Physically, this means that black holes with small event horizons are unstable, while those with large horizons are stable.
Moreover,
\hat{C}\rightarrow 0
as
r_{h}\rightarrow 0
, suggesting a physical limit point
[
26
]
, and diverges at
r_{h}=r_{h}^{c}
, signaling a phase transition.

In the noncommutative framework, smaller black holes maintain a negative heat capacity, remaining unstable. As
\Theta
increases, the radius of the locally stable black holes grows, and the corresponding value of
\hat{C}
increases. This implies that stable black holes take longer to radiate and evaporate.
Even in the absence of a cosmological constant (
\Lambda\rightarrow 0
), the noncommutative Schwarzschild black hole exhibits a first-order phase transition, while the ordinary Schwarzschild black hole does not. These findings agree with the results for the self-organized Schwarzschild-AdS black hole studied in
[
26
]
, where the mass density follows the symmetry of the quantum probability density of hydrogen atoms.

To further explore the phase transitions, we consider the thermodynamic potential represented by the free energy:

\displaystyle\hat{F}(r_{h},\Theta)

\displaystyle=\hat{M}-\hat{T}\hat{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}r_{h}\left(1+\frac{\Lambda}{3}r_{h}^{2}\right)+\frac{\Theta L}{8}\left(1+\Lambda r_{h}^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\Theta^{2}).

(27)

Using the expressions for the mass (Eq. (9)) and entropy (Eq. (16)) in noncommutative Schwarzschild-AdS spacetime, the free energy can be expressed as:

\hat{F}(r_{h},\hat{T},\Theta)=\frac{1}{2}r_{h}\left(1-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r_{h}^{2}\right)-\pi\hat{T}r_{h}^{2}+\left(1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2}-4\pi\hat{T}r_{h}\right)\frac{\Theta L}{4}.

\partial\hat{F}/\partial r_{h}=0

\hat{T}_{H}=\frac{1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2}-\Lambda\Theta Lr_{h}}{4\pi r_{h}+2\pi\Theta L}\approx\frac{1-\Lambda r_{h}^{2}}{4\pi r_{h}}-\frac{1+\Lambda r_{h}^{2}}{8\pi r_{h}^{2}}\,\Theta L.

In this region,

\hat{F}

When the temperature increases (
\hat{T}>\hat{T}_{H}
) at fixed

, the free energy evolves from a positive maximum to a negative minimum: the unstable small black hole evaporates into a stable, larger one.
Thus, as temperature rises beyond its critical limit, the unstable phase contracts while the stable phase expands.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the phase transition and thermodynamic behavior of the SAdS-BH within the framework of NC geometry. By incorporating NC corrections to the Schwarzschild–AdS spacetime metric up to first order in the noncommutative parameter
\Theta
, as presented in
[
12
]
, we derived the thermodynamic quantities of the NC SAdS-BH. The total mass, Hawking temperature, and entropy were obtained with first-order corrections in
\Theta
.

We then analyzed the influence of noncommutativity on the first law of thermodynamics and confirmed that the law remains fully satisfied. Moreover, the detailed analysis of the free energy of the NC SAdS-BH revealed two distinct extrema: a stable minimum corresponding to a large black hole and an unstable maximum corresponding to a smaller one. This behavior is consistent with the temperature profile (see Fig.
2
) and the heat capacity analysis (see Fig.
5
), both of which indicate the occurrence of a phase transition between small and large black hole phases.

Finally, our results suggest that the lower bound of the noncommutative parameter
\Theta
is of the order of
0.3\,\ell_{p}
, i.e., comparable to the Planck length. This confirms that noncommutativity introduces quantum-scale corrections to black hole thermodynamics, playing a role analogous to that of a thermodynamic variable at the Planck scale.

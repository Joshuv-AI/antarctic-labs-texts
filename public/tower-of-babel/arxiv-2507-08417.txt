Abstract
. We give a more conceptual construction of a comparison algebra morphism from the K K -theoretical Hall algebra to a twist of the cohomological Hall algebra associated to a symmetric quiver constructed by [ KS11 ] , and extend the result to quivers with potential.



1.
Introduction

Let
Q
be a quiver with a potential
W
.
The cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA)
{\mathcal{H}}^{W}
associated to
(Q,W)
was introduced by Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
KS11
]
, while the K-theoretical Hall algebra (KHA)
{\mathcal{R}}^{W}
was introduced by P
\rm\breve{a}
durariu
[
Pad20
]
. A main motivation for these constructions is that KHAs are positive halves of Yangians while CoHAs are halves of quantum affine algebras
[
BD23
,
VV22
,
SV23
]
. This analogy is witnessed particularly by KHAs and CoHAs of triple quivers with potential recovering on the nose positive halves of Yangians and quantum affine algebras
[
YZ18
]
. However, for more general symmetric quivers with potential, one expects new quantum group-like objects.

As there exists a natural comparison morphism of quantum affine algebras and Yangians
[
GL13
]
, given by exponentiation of roots on Drinfeld polynomials
[
DRI90
]
, a similar comparison is expected between KHAs and a twisted version of the corresponding COHAs.

In
[
LŠVdB24
]
this comparison has been realized for a symmetric quiver
Q
and
W=0
by a modification of the Chern character to obtain the conjectured algebra morphism
{\mathcal{R}}\to\hat{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\sigma}
,
1
1

1

We write
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}^{0}
,
{\mathcal{R}}={\mathcal{R}}^{0}
.
where
\hat{{\mathcal{H}}}
is the completion of
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\sigma
is a Zhang twist. The Chern character was modified in a rather ad hoc way in loc.cit. Here we provide a more conceptual explanation of this modification, related to the square root of Todd classes. This further allows us to extend the morphism to quivers with potentials as seen in

Theorem 4.8
.

The morphism

v:{\mathcal{R}}^{W}\to\left(\widehat{{\mathcal{H}}^{W}},\circ\right)

is a morphism of algebras.

Using this morphism one can use analogous reasoning to
[
LŠVdB24
]
to identify finite length modules over KHAs with the corresponding objects over CoHAs.

2.1.
Quiver varieties

Let

I

\alpha_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

We assume that

For a fixed dimension vector

2.2.
Cohomological and K-theoretical Hall algebras

Fix two dimension vectors
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{I}_{\geq 0}
and put
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
. Consider the affine subspace
M_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\subset M_{\gamma}
, which consists of representations for which the standard subspaces
{\mathbb{C}}^{\gamma_{1}^{i}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{\gamma^{i}}
form a subrepresentation. The subspace
M_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}
is preserved by the action of the parabolic subgroup
G_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\subset G_{\gamma}
which consists of transformations preserving subspaces
{\mathbb{C}}^{\gamma_{1}^{i}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{\gamma^{i}}
,
i\in I
.
We have the natural morphisms of stacks

(2.1)

M_{\gamma_{1}}/G_{\gamma_{1}}\times M_{\gamma_{2}}/G_{\gamma_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\leftarrow}}M_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}/G_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}M_{\gamma}/G_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}M_{\gamma}/G_{\gamma}.

The maps
i
and
\pi
are proper.

We will also look at the stacks where we only act with tori, i.e.
M_{\gamma}/T_{\gamma}
where
T_{\gamma}
is the diagonal maximal torus in
G_{\gamma}
and denote the corresponding maps by
\tilde{p},\tilde{i}
. Note that
\pi
is the identity in this case.

We denote
{{\mathcal{H}}}_{\gamma}:=H^{\bullet}_{G_{\gamma}}(M_{\gamma},{\mathbb{Q}})
,
{{\mathcal{R}}}_{\gamma}:=K_{0}^{G_{\gamma}}(M_{\gamma},{\mathbb{Q}})
. Moreover, we denote
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{\gamma}:=H^{\bullet}_{T_{\gamma}}(M_{\gamma},{\mathbb{Q}})
,
\tilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{\gamma}:=K_{0}^{T_{\gamma}}(M_{\gamma},{\mathbb{Q}})
. Set
{\mathcal{H}}=\oplus_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma},{\mathcal{R}}=\oplus_{\gamma}{\mathcal{R}}_{\gamma}
, and analogously for
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}},\tilde{{\mathcal{R}}}
.

As we will use Chern characters to compare these two Hall algebras we need to pass to the completed cohomological Hall algebra
\widehat{{\mathcal{H}}}

given by

\widehat{{\mathcal{H}}}_{\gamma}\cong\widehat{H}_{G_{\gamma}}\left(M_{\gamma},{\mathbb{Q}}\right):=\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}H^{n}_{G_{\gamma}}\left(M_{\gamma},{\mathbb{Q}}\right).

Todd classes for representation stacks

We have by

\displaystyle Td_{M_{\gamma}/T_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=Td_{M_{\gamma}/T_{\gamma}}\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}=1}^{\gamma^{i}}\frac{1-e^{x_{i,\alpha_{2}}-x_{i,\alpha_{1}}}}{x_{i,\alpha_{1}}-x_{i,\alpha_{2}}}.

\displaystyle Td_{\overline{M_{{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}}}}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle:=\prod_{i,j\in I}\prod_{\alpha_{1}=1}^{\gamma_{1}^{i}}\prod_{\alpha_{2}=1}^{\gamma_{2}^{j}}\left(\frac{x^{\prime}_{i,\alpha_{1}}-x^{\prime\prime}_{j,\alpha_{2}}}{1-e^{x^{\prime\prime}_{j,\alpha_{2}}-x^{\prime}_{i,\alpha_{1}}}}\right)^{a_{ij}},

\displaystyle Td_{\overline{G_{{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}}}}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle:=\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{\alpha_{1}=1}^{\gamma_{1}^{i}}\prod_{\alpha_{2}=1}^{\gamma_{2}^{i}}\frac{x^{\prime}_{i,\alpha_{1}}-x^{\prime\prime}_{i,\alpha_{2}}}{1-e^{x^{\prime\prime}_{i,\alpha_{2}}-x^{\prime}_{i,\alpha_{1}}}}.

We denote

Let

\tilde{b}_{\gamma_{2}}^{\gamma_{1}}:=\prod_{i\in I}exp({\bf{x}}_{\gamma_{1}^{i}})^{\sum_{j\in I}a_{ij}\gamma_{2}^{j}},\quad d_{\gamma_{2}}^{\gamma_{1}}:=\prod_{i\in I}exp({\bf{x}}_{\gamma_{1}^{i}})^{\gamma_{2}^{i}}.

\displaystyle Td_{\overline{M_{{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}}}}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})Td_{\overline{M_{{\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}}}}}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime})^{-1}

(3.2)

\displaystyle Td_{\overline{G_{{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}}}}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})Td_{\overline{G_{{\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}}}}}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{\gamma_{2}}^{\gamma_{1}}(x^{\prime})}{d_{\gamma_{1}}^{\gamma_{2}}(x^{\prime\prime})}.

If
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
and

For further reference we single out important properties of the multiplications and Chern character on

Let

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

(t_{1}f_{1})\cdot_{\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}}(t_{2}f_{2})=(t_{1}\cdot_{{\mathcal{S}}}t_{2})(f_{1}\cdot_{\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}}f_{2}).

Proof.

This follows by explicit formulas for multiplication
[
KS11
]
.
2

2

Note that in loc.cit., the explicit formulas for the multiplication are given in the
G
-equivariant setting, here they are simpler, we do not need shuffle, and we do not divide by
\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{\alpha_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}^{i}}\prod_{\alpha_{2}=1}^{\gamma_{2}^{i}}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i,\alpha_{2}}-x^{\prime}_{i,\alpha_{1}})
.

Alternatively, the two sides can be written as follows. The left hand side is equal to

\tilde{i}_{*}\tilde{p}^{*}(pr^{*}t_{1}\cup f_{1},pr^{*}t_{2}\cup f_{2}),

while the right-hand side equals

Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{i}_{*}\tilde{p}^{*}(pr^{*}t_{1}\cup f_{1},pr^{*}t_{2}\cup f_{2})

\displaystyle=\tilde{i}_{*}(\tilde{p}^{*}(pr^{*}t_{1},pr^{*}t_{2})\cup\tilde{p}^{*}(f_{1},f_{2}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{i}_{*}(\tilde{i}^{*}pr^{*}q^{*}(t_{1},t_{2})\cup\tilde{p}^{*}(f_{1},f_{2})),

which equals the right-hand side by the projection formula.

Lemma 3.2
.

The Chern character
ch:\tilde{{\mathcal{R}}}\to\tilde{{\mathcal{R}}}
commutes with the action of
S_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
f(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in\tilde{{\mathcal{R}}}
. Then we have

ch\left(f\right)=\mathrm{exp}\left(f\left(x_{1},...,x_{n}\right)\right)\in\widehat{{\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}}}.

In particular we get for
s\in S_{n}
:

\displaystyle ch\left(sf\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp}\left(sf\left(x_{1},...,x_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp}\left(f\left(x_{s1},...,x_{sn}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp}\left(f\right)\left(x_{s1},...,x_{sn}\right)

\displaystyle=s\mathrm{exp}\left(f\right)\left(x_{1},...,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=s\;ch\left(f\right)

and so the Chern character is compatible with the action of the symmetric group.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

The morphism

v:{\mathcal{R}}^{W}\to\left(\widehat{{\mathcal{H}}^{W}},\circ\right)

is a morphism of algebras.

Proof.

We want to apply our computations from §
3.4
to the case of quivers with potential.
We basically repeat the proof, first passing through the
T
-equivariant setting. We only mention the necessary modifications.

By Lemma
4.7
we have
\iota^{*}\circ Td_{h}=Td_{h|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{0}}}
. This allows us to use formulas (
3.1
), (
3.2
).
The only other thing that we need is an analogue of Lemma
3.1
. The action of
H_{T_{\gamma}}^{\bullet}(M_{\gamma},{\mathbb{Q}})
(or equivalently
H_{T_{\gamma}}^{\bullet}(\cdot,{\mathbb{Q}})
as
M_{\gamma}
is
T_{\gamma}
-contractible) on

[
Dav17
, §2.6]
. To apply the proof of Lemma
3.1
we need that the action commutes with
\tilde{p}^{*}
and
\tilde{i}_{*}
.

The action of
H_{G}^{*}(\cdot,{\mathbb{Q}})
on
H_{c,G}(X,\phi_{f})^{\vee}
for
G<GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in loc.cit. is defined as the limit (over
N\geq n
) of the pullback maps (cf. the beginning of §2.7 in loc.cit.)

\Delta_{N}:(X\times_{G}fr(n,N))\to(X\times_{G}fr(n,N))\times(pt\times_{G}fr(n,N)),\;(x,z)\mapsto((x,z),(pt,z))

, resp.

f_{N}
, resp.
g_{N}

B_{X}

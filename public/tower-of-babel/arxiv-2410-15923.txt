Automatic Differentiation of Optimization Algorithms with Time-Varying Updates


Abstract
Numerous Optimization Algorithms have a time-varying update rule thanks to, for instance, a changing step size, momentum parameter or, Hessian approximation. In this paper, we apply unrolled or automatic differentiation to a time-varying iterative process and provide convergence (rate) guarantees for the resulting derivative iterates. We adapt these convergence results and apply them to proximal gradient descent with variable step size and FISTA when solving partly smooth problems. We confirm our findings numerically by solving ℓ 1 \ell_{1} and ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -regularized linear and logisitc regression respectively. Our theoretical and numerical results show that the convergence rate of the algorithm is reflected in its derivative iterates.

3
Differentiation of Proximal Gradient-type Methods

We now turn our attention to unrolling PGD with variable step size and APG when solving

and aim at providing convergence (rate) guarantees for the corresponding derivative sequences. The idea is to consider the class of partly smooth functions
[

which models a wide range of non-smooth regularizers and constraints appearing in practice
[

]

and
3.3

10
) for differentiating the solution mapping of (

3.1
Problem Setting

We first define our setting in a more precise manner. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
C^{2}
-smooth manifold and
\Omega\subset\mathcal{U}
be an open set, we need

and

Assumption 3.1
(Convex Partly Smooth Objective).

f\colon\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is

-smooth,

is partly smooth relative to
\mathcal{M}\times\Omega
and for every
\bm{u}\in\Omega

f(\cdot,\bm{u})
and
g(\cdot,\bm{u})
are convex,

has an

g(\cdot,\bm{u})
has a simple proximal mapping.

Let
\bm{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{M}
be a solution of (

\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})\succ 0
which is also a sufficient condition for the linear convergence of gradient descent and the Heavy-ball method
[
51

is partly smooth, IFT requires positive definiteness of
\nabla^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}F(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})

[
35

while the linear convergence of PGD and APG is established by the positive definiteness of
\Pi(\bm{x}^{*})\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})\Pi(\bm{x}^{*})

[
38
,
37
]

Assumption 3.2
(Restricted Positive Definiteness).

Let
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})\in\mathcal{M}\times\Omega
be a given point.

(i)

The Hessian
\nabla^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}F(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})
is positive definite on
T_{\bm{x}^{*}}\mathcal{M}
, that is,

\nabla^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}F(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})\succ 0\,.

(RPD-i)

(ii)

Moreover,
\Pi(\bm{x}^{*})\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})
is positive definite on
T_{\bm{x}^{*}}\mathcal{M}
, that is,

\left.{\Pi(\bm{x}^{*})\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})}\right|_{T_{\bm{x}^{*}}\mathcal{M}}\succ 0\,.

(RPD-ii)

Finally, for partly smooth problems, IFT and linear convergence of PGD and APG also require a non-degeneracy assumption to hold which ensures that the solution stays in
\mathcal{M}
for any
\bm{u}
near
\bm{u}^{*}
.

Assumption 3.3
(Non-degeneracy).

The non-degeneracy condition is satisfied at
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})\in\mathcal{M}\times\Omega
, that is,

0\in\mathrm{ri}\,\partial_{\bm{x}}F(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})\,.

(ND)

Settling the no- ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -in-line problem when k k is not small
Benedek Kovács, Zoltán Lóránt Nagy, Dávid R. Szabó

Abstract
What is the maximum number of points that can be selected from an n × n n\times n square lattice such that no k + 1 k+1 of them are in a line? This has been asked more than 100 100 years ago for k = 2 k=2 and it remained wide open ever since. In this paper, we prove the precise answer is k ​ n kn , provided that k > C ​ n ​ log ⁡ n k>C\sqrt{n\log{n}} for an absolute constant C C . The proof relies on carefully constructed bi-uniform random bipartite graphs and concentration inequalities.

Introduction

A set of points in the plane is said to be in
general position
if no three of the points lie on a common line. Motivated by a problem concerning the placement of chess pieces, Dudeney
[
8
]
asked how many points may be placed in an
n\times n
grid so that the points are in general position. This No-Three-In-Line problem has received considerable attention: for history and background, we refer to the excellent book of Brass, Moser, and Pach
[
6
]
and that of Eppstein
[
9
]
.
Brass, Moser and Pach called this one of the oldest and most extensively studied geometric questions concerning lattice points. For higher dimensional variants, we refer to

[
24
]
.

Concerning an upper bound, it is straightforward to see that at most
2n
points can be placed in general position. For rather small
n
, several examples have been constructed where the theoretical bound
2n
can be attained, see e.g.
[
2
,
10
]
. However, it is still an open problem to determine the answer for general
n
.

The earliest lower bound is due to Erdős (appearing in a paper
published by Roth
[
25
]
), and uses the
modular parabola
,
consisting of the points
(i,i^{2})\pmod{p}
. If
n=p
is a prime
number, then this yields
n
points in general position in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap[1,n]^{2}
.
If
n
is not a prime, then taking
p
to be the largest prime not exceeding

n
and applying the modular parabola in a
p\times p
subgrid yields
n-o(n)
points in general position.
The best general lower bound for the No-Three-In-Line problem relies on a construction due to Hall, Jackson, Sudbery, and Wild
[
15
]
. Their construction is built up from constructions in subgrids where one places points on a hyperbola
xy=t\pmod{p}
, where
p
is a prime slightly smaller than
n/2
, and yields
\frac{3}{2}n-o(n)
points in general position.
Interestingly, even what to conjecture on the
asymptotical value
of the answer for the No-Three-In-Line problem is far from clear, with prominent experts believing it could be roughly
1.5n
via the previous construction (see Green
[
12
]
), or it could be
2n
thus the upper bound can be attained asymptotically in general
[
6
]
, or somewhere in between
[
9
,
14
]
. Some related problems were studied recently in
[
1
]
and
[
22
]
.

We are interested in a generalization which might be the most natural one, where a collinear point set of
k+1
points is forbidden in the grid
[1,n]\times[1,n]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f_{k}(n)
denote the maximum size of a point set chosen from the vertices of the
n\times n
grid
{\mathcal{G}}=[1,n]\times[1,n]
in which at most
k
points lie on any line.

Note that in a more general context, this problem has been studied before, e.g. by Lefmann
[
18
]
, but no exact value has been determined up to our best knowledge.
Our result shows that the upper bound, which is based on a simple pigeonhole argument, is tight when the order of magnitude of
k
is slightly larger than
\sqrt{n}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
C>\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{35}\approx 14.79
, there exists
N_{C}
such that
whenever
n\geq N_{C}
and
k\geq C\sqrt{n\log n}
, we have

f_{k}(n)=kn.

In fact, we may take any
C>25/2=12.5
, see
3.11
.

To put this result into perspective, we recall the projective and affine variants of the problem, which are also of importance.
For a given set of points, a line is called a
secant
if it intersects the point set in at least two points. Point sets having no secants of size larger than
2
are called
arcs
, while in general, point sets of a projective plane having no secants of size larger than
k
are called
k
-
arcs
. Following earlier work of Bose
[
5
]
, Segre showed that for odd
q
, every arc of
q+1
points in a desarguesian projective geometry
{\rm PG}(2,q)

is a conic
[
26
,
27
]
. For general
k
, the theoretical maximum size of a
k
-arc, also based on the pigeonhole principle, is
(q+1)(k-1)+1
both in the projective and affine setting, however this can only be attained if
q
is a power of
2
and
k
is a divisor of
q
due to the results of Denniston, and of Ball, Blokhuis and Mazzocca
[
3
,
4
,
7
]
.
This in turn gives the following consequence:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>2
.
In the affine plane
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, the trivial upper bound
1+(p+1)k
cannot be attained for the no-
(k+1)
-in-line problem.

Outline

We finish the chapter by outlining the proof of our main theorem and describing the organisation of the paper.
The first main idea is to consider the problem in a graph-theoretical setting as follows:

Problem 1.4
.

What is the maximum number of edges of a spanning subgraph
G
of a balanced bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
, where each vertex has degree
\leq k
and for a given set
\mathcal{M}
of matchings of
K_{n,n}
, every matching intersects
E(G)
in at most
k
edges?

To obtain the no-
(k+1)
-in-line problem, we let
\mathcal{M}
consist of the matchings corresponding to collinear grid points.

The second main idea is to choose the point set of size
kn
from the grid (or the
k
-regular subgraph of
K_{n,n}
) in a random manner, in such a way that the expected number of the chosen points from
\ell
is significantly less than
k
for every line
\ell
which is not horizontal or vertical (
3.4
). To do so, we design a bi-uniform random graph (
3.2
), in which the probability that an individual edge is contained in a graph will be one of two prescribed values, depending on the edge class it belongs to.
After this step, we may apply concentration inequalities to show the existence of a suitable point set.

In our approach, the existence of a suitable random point set demanded a divisibility condition on the size of the grid. Thus we also show a method which provides suitable sets of
kn^{\prime}
points in an
n^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
grid from previously constructed suitable sets of size
kn
in an
n\times n
grid, where
n
is only slightly smaller than
n^{\prime}
and satisfies the required divisibility condition.

Our paper is organized as follows. In
Section 2
, we set the notation and introduce the main tools for the proof. We carry out the case when
k
is very large in
Subsection 3.1
.

Subsection 3.2
is devoted to the proof of
Theorem 1.2
in the case
k
is not very large assuming certain divisibility conditions on
n
and
k
.
In
Subsection 3.3
, we extend our result by dropping the divisibility conditions on
n
and
k
. Finally, we discuss some open problems and alternative proof techniques in
Section 4
.

Notation

If

for

. We call
{\mathcal{G}}
a

if

.

We will often identify a subset

of a grid
{\mathcal{G}}
with a bipartite graph with vertex classes
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, where edge
(i_{1},i_{2})
appears in the graph if and only if
(i_{1},i_{2})\in S
. An
r
-factor
in a bipartite graph is a spanning subgraph where every vertex has degree
r
. Analogously, an
r
-factor in a grid

is a subset of
{\mathcal{G}}
containing exactly
r
points in each row and column. A
t
-matching
is a matching of size
t
, while in a grid it corresponds to
t
points with no two lying on the same axis-parallel line.

A
secant

for a grid

is a line of the Euclidean plane that meets
{\mathcal{G}}
in at least two points. We call a line
\ell

generic
if it is not horizontal or vertical. Each generic secant
\ell
has a unique direction vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{x},v_{y})
such that
v_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
v_{y}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
\gcd(v_{x},v_{y})=1
. In this case, call
M=\max(|v_{x}|,|v_{y}|)
the
modulus
of
\ell
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{G}})
be the set of all generic secants of
{\mathcal{G}}
, and for a fixed real
\kappa\geq 0
, let
{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})=\{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{G}}):|\ell\cap{\mathcal{G}}|>\kappa\}
be defined as the generic secants admitting more than
\kappa
grid points.

A set
S\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
is a
no-
(k+1)
-in-line set
if
|S\cap\ell|\leq k
for every Euclidean line
\ell
.
A no-
(k+1)
-in-line set

is said to have
reserve

for some
h\in\mathbb{N}
, if
|S\cap\ell|\leq k-h
for every
\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{G}})
.

For events
E_{n}
indexed with a parameter
n
, we say that
E_{n}
occurs
with high probability
(w.h.p.) if the probability of
E_{n}
tends to
1
as
n\to\infty
.

2.2
Tools

Concentration results

The well-known Chernoff–Hoeffding bound states that the sum of independent
\{0,1\}
-valued random variables is highly concentrated around the expected value.

Theorem 2.1

(Chernoff bound for the binomial distribution)
.

Let
\operatorname{Bin}\left(m,\alpha\right)
denote the binomial distribution with
m
trials and success probability
\alpha
. If
X\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left(m,\alpha\right)
, then for any
\alpha<\beta\leq 1
we have

{\mathbb{P}}(X>\beta m)\leq e^{-D(\beta||\alpha)m},

where
D(\cdot||\cdot)
is the so-called relative entropy function satisfying
D(\beta||\alpha)\geq 2(\beta-\alpha)^{2}.

We will apply a variant where the condition on independence can be replaced with a more general condition.

Theorem 2.2

(Linial-Luria
[
20
]
)
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
be indicator random variables,
0<\beta<1
and
t,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
0<t<\beta m
.
Then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{X_{i}}\geq\beta r\right)\leq\frac{1}{\binom{\beta m}{t}}\sum_{|T|=t}{{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcap_{i\in T}\{X_{i}=1\}\right)}.

In particular, if

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcap_{i\in T}\{X_{i}=1\}\right)\leq K\alpha^{t}

for every
T
of size
t=\left(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)m
, where
0<\alpha<\beta
and
K>0
are constants, then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{X_{i}}>\beta r\right)\leq Ke^{-D(\beta||\alpha)m}\leq Ke^{-2(\beta-\alpha)^{2}m}.

Note that the upper bound
Ke^{-2(\beta-\alpha)^{2}m}
also clearly holds in the case when
0\leq\alpha<1
and
\beta>\alpha
(not excluding
\beta\geq 1
).

Number of large secants

A well-known consequence of the famous Szemerédi–Trotter theorem claims that the number of lines with at least
k
points in a planar
n
-set of points is at most
O(n^{2}/k^{3}+n/k)

[
28
,
23
]
.
For the set of points of an
n\times n
grid, Erdős proved an asymptotical result (see
[
23
, Remark 4.2]
):

Theorem 2.3

(Erdős)
.

Let
k=k(n)
be a function such that
\frac{n-k}{n}=\Theta(1)
. Then the number of secants of size at least
k
in
{\mathcal{G}}\coloneqq[1,n]^{2}
is asymptotically
\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\frac{n^{4}}{k^{3}}
.

In particular, there is a universal constant
L
such that
{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})\leq L\frac{n^{4}}{\kappa^{3}}
for any
\kappa>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. (We may take e.g.

for large enough
n
.)

Counting regular subgraphs that contain a given matching

In
[
16
]
, Isaev and McKay presented a series of enumeration results based on the calculation of corresponding high-dimensional complex integrals. An illustrative example is the following. Let
N_{H}(\textbf{d})
, resp.
\tilde{N}_{H}(\textbf{d})
denote the number of graphs on

n
vertices, resp. number of bipartite graphs on
n_{1}+(n-n_{1})
vertices which have vertex degrees
\textbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
, include
H^{+}
as a subgraph, and are edge-disjoint from another graph
H^{-}
, where
H
denotes the pair
(H^{+},H^{-})
of fixed edge-disjoint graphs on the
n
-vertex underlying set.
Then, following the earlier work of Barvinok, Canfield, Gao, Greenhill, Hartigan,
Isaev, McKay, Wang, Wormald (see
[
13
,
19
,
21
]
and the references therein), they expressed
N_{H}(\textbf{d})
and
\tilde{N}_{H}(\mathbf{d})

as a complex integral and gave their order of magnitude, provided that some moderate assumptions hold on
H
and on the degree sequence
d
. We state here a special case of their result on bipartite graphs, when
H^{+}
is a matching and
H^{-}
is an empty graph.

Theorem 2.4

(Special case of
[
16
, Theorem 5.8]
)
.

There is an absolute constant
K\geq 1
satisfying the following.
Let
0\leq r\leq m
be integers,

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be disjoint sets of cardinality
m
,

M
be a fixed (not necessarily perfect) matching of the complete bipartite graph
\Gamma
on the vertex set
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
.
If
G\subseteq\Gamma
is a uniform random
r
-regular bipartite graph, then

{\mathbb{P}}(M\subseteq E(G))\leq K\cdot(r/m)^{|M|}.

Proof.

The statement is clear for
r\in\{0,m\}
or
|M|=0
.
Otherwise, it is a direct consequence of
[
16
, Theorem 5.8]
with the following setup:

n=2m
,

V_{1}=\{1,\dots,m\}
and
V_{2}=\{m+1,\dots,2m\}
,

H=(M,\emptyset)
.

\mathbf{d}=(r,r,\dots,r)
,

\delta=1/m
,

\epsilon=1/2
,

c_{1}=c_{2}=1
,

\tilde{\lambda}_{j,k}=r/m
if

,
k\in V_{2}
or vice versa (e.g. by taking
\beta_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{r}{m-r}
) and
\tilde{\lambda}_{j,k}=0
otherwise.
Now
s_{j}\in\{0,1\}
,

s_{\max}=1
,

S_{2}=2|M|\leq n
.
We have
|\tilde{K}^{\prime}|\leq\exp(\tilde{c}S_{2}/n+\tilde{c}(1+s_{\max}^{3})n^{\epsilon-1/2})-1\leq\exp(3\tilde{c})-1
, which is an absolute constant because

The statement follows from
{\mathbb{P}}(M\subseteq E(G))=\tilde{P}_{H}(\mathbf{d})=(1+\tilde{K}^{\prime})\prod_{jk\in M}\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}=(1+\tilde{K}^{\prime})(r/m)^{e(M)}

by taking

.
∎

Lemma 3.5

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
0\leq p<1

0\leq\epsilon\leq 1

Assume that for every

\beta_{i,j}(\ell)>0

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,j}+\frac{D_{1}(n,p,\epsilon)}{\sqrt{g_{i,j}(\ell)}}\leq\beta_{i,j}(\ell)

\displaystyle<\frac{\kappa_{i,j}(\ell)}{g_{i,j}(\ell)}

(3.2)

\displaystyle t_{i,j}(\ell)\coloneqq\frac{\beta_{i,j}(\ell)-\alpha_{i,j}}{1-\alpha_{i,j}}g_{i,j}(\ell)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}\kappa_{i,j}(\ell)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa

Then

{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\forall\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{G}}):|S\cap\ell|\leq\kappa\Big)\geq p.

Proof.

Denote the probability of failure by

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}{\mathbb{P}}(|S\cap\ell|>\kappa)

using the union bound,

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}s_{i,j}(\ell)>\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}\kappa_{i,j}(\ell)\Big)

s_{i,j}(\ell)\coloneqq|S_{i,j}\cap\ell|
and (

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}{\mathbb{P}}\big(\exists(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0,\quad s_{i,j}(\ell)>\kappa_{i,j}(\ell)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}\;\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}{\mathbb{P}}(s_{i,j}(\ell)>\kappa_{i,j}(\ell))

using the union bound,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}\;\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i,j}\cap\ell}X_{P}>\kappa_{i,j}(\ell)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}\;\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i,j}\cap\ell}X_{P}>\beta_{i,j}(\ell)\cdot g_{i,j}(\ell)\Big)

using (
3.2
).

If
\alpha_{i,j}=1
, then

\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i,j}\cap\ell}X_{P}=s_{i,j}(\ell)=g_{i,j}(\ell)

\beta_{i,j}(\ell)>1
by (

0
.
To bound the probability above in the case

Theorem 2.2
.
First note that

0\leq\alpha_{i,j}(\ell)<\beta_{i,j}(\ell)
by (

).
Next, let

be of size

from (

Now

-regular random subgraph

Hence

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}\;\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}K\cdot\exp\Big(-2(\beta_{i,j}(\ell)-\alpha_{i,j})^{2}\cdot g_{i,j}(\ell)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}\;\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}\frac{1-p}{7Ln^{4-3\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{>\kappa}({\mathcal{G}})}\frac{1-p}{Ln^{4-3\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq 1-p

\displaystyle\text{using \autoref{lem:richlines} with }\kappa\geq n^{\epsilon}.

The statement follows from

.
∎

For large enough values of

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
0\leq p<1
,
0\leq\epsilon\leq 1
and
\kappa\geq n^{\epsilon}
be real numbers satisfying

\kappa\geq E(R)+D_{2}(n,p,\epsilon)

{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\forall\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{G}}):|S\cap\ell|\leq\kappa\Big)\geq p.

Proof.

Define

such that the interval for

in (

) (is well-defined and) is of length

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}\kappa_{i,j}(\ell)

\displaystyle\leq E(R)+D_{1}(n,p,\epsilon)\cdot\Big(\sum_{(i,j):g_{i,j}(\ell)>0}\sqrt{g_{i,j}(\ell)}\Big)+7

\displaystyle\leq E(R)+D_{1}(n,p,\epsilon)\cdot\sqrt{7\cdot|{\mathcal{G}}\cap\ell|}+7

\displaystyle\leq E(R)+D_{2}(n,p,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa

by the assumption on

.

Hence (

) is also satisfied.

∎

3.3
General case

To reduce the general case to the one discussed in
Subsection 3.2
(namely

and

), we need the following elementary constructions to adjust the values of

and

Lemma 3.12

(Adjusting
k
)
.

Let
0\leq h\leq k\leq n
be integers.
Assume
S\subseteq[1,n]^{2}
is a no-
(k+1)
-in-line set of size
kn
having reserve
h
.
Then there is a no-
(k^{\prime}+1)
-in-line set
S^{\prime}\subseteq[1,n]
of size
k^{\prime}n
having reserve
h^{\prime}
for
every
0\leq h^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime}
with
k-k^{\prime}=h-h^{\prime}\geq 0
.

Proof.

By Kőnig’s line colouring theorem, we may select a
(k-k^{\prime})
-factor
F\subseteq S
.
Define
S^{\prime}\coloneqq S\setminus F
.
Now
S^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
is a
k^{\prime}
-factor and every generic secant of
{\mathcal{G}}
still contains at most
k-h=k^{\prime}-h^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime}
lines.
So
S^{\prime}
is indeed a no-
(k^{\prime}+1)
-in-line set of size
|S|-|F|=k^{\prime}n
and has reserve
k^{\prime}-(k-h)=h^{\prime}
as required.
∎

Lemma 3.13

(Adjusting
n
)
.

Let
0\leq h^{\prime\prime}\leq k^{\prime}\leq n
be integers with
2\mid h^{\prime\prime}
.
Assume
S^{\prime}\subseteq[1,n]^{2}
is a no-
(k^{\prime}+1)
-in-line set of size
k^{\prime}n
having reserve
h^{\prime\prime}
.
Then there exists a no-(
k^{\prime}+1)
-in-line set
S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq[1,n^{\prime}]^{2}
of size
k^{\prime}n^{\prime}
for

n^{\prime}\coloneqq n+\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{2}
.

Proof.

Using Kőnig’s line coloring theorem, we may decompose

S^{\prime}
as a disjoint union
S^{\prime}={\mathcal{P}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{P}}_{2}\cup\dots\cup{\mathcal{P}}_{k^{\prime}}
, where each
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq k^{\prime}
) is a subset corresponding to a
1
-factor.
For
1\leq i\leq h^{\prime\prime}/2
, pick
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
with
\left|{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\right|=k^{\prime}
,
and define

{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\coloneqq\{(n+i,y):(x,y)\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\text{ for some }x\}
and

{\mathcal{B}}_{i}\coloneqq\{(x,n+i):(x,y)\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\text{ for some }y\}
.
Note that
\left|{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\right|=\left|{\mathcal{B}}_{i}\right|=\left|{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\right|=k^{\prime}
as
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}
has at most
1
point in every row and column.
Furthermore, all of these sets are pairwise disjoint as

{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\subseteq\{n+i\}\times[1,n]
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}\subseteq[1,n]\times\{n+i\}
.
Define the disjoint union

S^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\Big({\mathcal{P}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{h^{\prime\prime}/2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\Big)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{h^{\prime\prime}/2}({\mathcal{A}}_{i}\cup{\mathcal{B}}_{i})\subseteq[1,n^{\prime}]^{2}.

This means that we obtained
S^{\prime\prime}
from
S^{\prime}
by erasing every point
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}
and adding their orthogonal projections to the lines
\ell[x=n+i]
and
\ell[y=n+i]
, for all

.
Now we show that
S^{\prime\prime}
satisfies the statement.

It is immediate that the constructed point set
S^{\prime\prime}
intersects every horizontal and vertical grid line of
[1,n^{\prime}]^{2}
in exactly
k^{\prime}
points.
Now let
\ell
be a generic line.
By construction, we have

\left|S^{\prime\prime}\cap\ell\right|\leq\underbrace{\left|S^{\prime\prime}\cap\ell\cap[1,n]^{2}\right|}_{\leq\left|{\mathcal{P}}\cap\ell\right|\leq k^{\prime}-h^{\prime\prime}}+\underbrace{\left|S^{\prime\prime}\cap\ell\cap([n+1,n^{\prime}]\times[1,n^{\prime}])\right|}_{\leq\left|[n+1,n^{\prime}]\right|=h^{\prime\prime}/2}+\underbrace{\left|S^{\prime\prime}\cap\ell\cap([1,n^{\prime}]\times[n+1,n^{\prime}])\right|}_{\leq\left|[n+1,n^{\prime}]\right|=h^{\prime\prime}/2}\leq k^{\prime},

as required.
∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of the main result of this paper.

Proof of
Theorem 1.2
.

Let
C>\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{35}

Let

.
To show that
N_{C}\coloneqq\max\{n_{C},68\}

(k^{\prime}+1)
-in-line set in

.

If
k^{\prime}\geq\frac{2}{3}n^{\prime}

-in-line set in

Otherwise, we have

Let

and let

.
Note that

and

.
Furthermore,

-in-line set

of size

.

.
∎

4
Concluding remarks and open questions

Our paper completely solves the no-
(k+1)
-in-line problem provided that
k
is sufficiently large with respect to
n
, with a rather moderate lower bound on
k
. It would be very interesting to see whether a similar statement holds when
k
is in a lower range,
k=\Omega(n^{\varepsilon})
for every
\varepsilon>0
. We pose this as an open problem.
We believe that the upper bound
f_{n}(k)\leq kn
is tight in this range as well, that is why we did not elaborate too much on optimizing the constant
C
for which our proof method works with
k>C\sqrt{n\log n}
.

Problem 4.1
.

Improve bounds on the order of magnitude of
k
for which
f_{k}(n)=kn
holds.

While the lower bound on
f_{2}(n)
has not been improved from the asymptotic value
1.5n

[
15
]
, not much is known about
f_{k}(n)
when
k>2
is a constant.

Problem 4.2
.

Improve the lower bounds on
f_{k}(n)
for
k\geq 2
.

At last, we point out a different approach to prove that our Bi-uniform construction yields a suitable point set, via the bipartite variant of the celebrated Kim-Vu sandwich conjecture.
The original conjecture reads as follows.

Conjecture 4.3

(Kim-Vu sandwich conjecture)
.

If
d\gg\log n
, then for some sequences
p_{1}=p_{1}(n)\sim d/n
and
p_{2}=p_{2}(n)\sim d/n
there is a joint distribution of a random
d
-regular graph
\mathbb{R}(n,d)
and two binomial random graphs
\mathbb{G}(n,p_{1})
and
\mathbb{G}(n,p_{2})
such that with high probability, we have

\mathbb{G}(n,p_{1})\subseteq\mathbb{R}(n,d)\subseteq\mathbb{G}(n,p_{2}).

This has been proved by Gao, Isaev and McKay
[
11
]
for
d\gg\log^{4}n
.

Klimošová, Reiher, Ruciński and Šileikis recently proved the following bipartite variant of the Kim-Vu sandwich conjecture. Let
\mathbb{R}(n,n,p)
denote a random graph chosen uniformly from the set of
pn
-regular bipartite graphs on
n+n
vertices, and let
\mathbb{G}(n,n,p)
denote
the binomial bipartite random graph where each edge of

K_{n,n}
is chosen independently with probability
p
.

Theorem 4.4

(Sandwich theorem on regular random bipartite graphs,
[
17
]
)
.

If
p\gg\frac{\log n}{n}
and
1-p\gg\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}
,
then for some
p^{\prime}\sim p
, there exists a joint distribution of
\mathbb{G}(n,n,p^{\prime})
and
\mathbb{R}(n,n,p)
such that
\mathbb{G}(n,n,p^{\prime})\subseteq\mathbb{R}(n,n,p)
holds with high probability, while for
p\gg\left(\frac{\log^{3}n}{n}\right)^{1/4}
, the opposite embedding holds.

These sandwich theorems can reduce the study of any monotone graph property of the random
d
-regular (bipartite) graphs in the regime
d\gg\log n
to study the same property in
\mathbb{G}(n,p)
(or
\mathbb{G}(n,n,p)
), which can usually be handled much easier.

In order to prove that every line intersects our carefully designed random point set in at most
k
points, we might apply the above sandwich theorem and bound the probability that a generic line contains more
k
points. Here we can apply the original variant of Chernoff’s bound to estimate the intersection size within a subgrid
{\mathcal{G}}_{i,j}
.
However, the condition

p\gg\left(\frac{\log^{3}n}{n}\right)^{1/4}
means that we could only use it in the range
k\gg(n\log n)^{3/4}
. Note that it is commonly believed that the statement of
Theorem 4.4
also holds when
p\gg\log^{c}n/n
for some absolute constant
c
, just like in the generic case of the sandwich theorem of Gao, Isaev and McKay
[
11
]
.

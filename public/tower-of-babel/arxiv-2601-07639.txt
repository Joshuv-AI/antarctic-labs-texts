Pluripotential theory on algebraic curves
Norm Levenberg, Sione Ma’u

Abstract
In previous works, the second author defined directional Robin constants associated to a compact, nonpolar subset K K of an algebraic curve A A in ℂ N \mathbb{C}^{N} and related these to a natural class of Chebyshev constants for K K . We define a second class of Chebyshev constants for K K ; relate these two classes; and utilize each of them to define two families of extremal-like functions which can be used to recover the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function for K K .



1
Introduction

In
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
, the authors defined directional Robin constants associated to a compact, nonpolar subset
K
of an algebraic curve
A
in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and related these constants to certain Chebyshev constants for
K
. In this work we continue this investigation and delve more deeply into these – and other – relations. In this introductory section, we describe the precise setting and state our main results.

Let
A
be an irreducible algebraic curve in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. We let
SH(A)
denote the weakly subharmonic (shm) functions on
A
:
u\in SH(A)
if
u
is uppersemicontinuous (usc) on
A
and shm on
A^{0}
, the regular points of
A
. Now let
K\subset A
be nonpolar, i.e.,
K\cap A^{0}
is not polar as a subset of the complex curve
A^{0}
. We can consider the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function

V_{K}(z):=\sup\{\frac{1}{deg(p)}\log|p(z)|:||p||_{K}:=\max_{\zeta\in K}|p(\zeta)|\leq 1,\ p\ \hbox{holomorphic polynomial}\}

for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
From Sadullaev’s theorem
[
9
]
, we know that

V_{K}|_{A}
is locally bounded on
A
; and for
z\in A
,

V_{K}|_{A}^{*}(z):=\limsup_{\zeta\to z,\ \zeta\in A}V_{K}(\zeta)

is in
SH(A)
and it is harmonic on
A^{0}\setminus K
. Moreover, defining

L(A):=\{u\in SH(A):u(z)\leq\log^{+}|z|+c_{u}=\max[0,\log|z|]+c_{u}\}

where
c_{u}
is a constant depending on
u
,
V_{K}|_{A}=V_{K,A}
on
A^{0}
where

V_{K,A}(z)=\sup\{u(z):u\in L(A),\ u\leq 0\ \hbox{on}\ K\},

and
V_{K,A}^{*}\in L^{+}(A)
where

L^{+}(A):=\{u\in L(A):\log^{+}|z|-c_{u}\leq u(z)\leq\log^{+}|z|+c_{u}\}.

For simplicity, we simply write
V_{K}:=V_{K,A}=V_{K}|_{A}
and
V_{K}^{*}:=V_{K}|_{A}^{*}
as we only consider points
z\in A
. We say
K
is
regular
if
V_{K}
is continuous on
A^{0}
. As an example, if we choose a basis for
\mathbb{C}^{N}
so that

A\subset\{(z_{1},z^{\prime}):|z^{\prime}|^{2}<C(1+|z_{1}|^{2})\}

where
z^{\prime}=(z_{2},...,z_{N})
and
C>0
, then for
K:=\{z\in A:|z_{1}|\leq r\}
we have
V_{K}(z)=\log^{+}|z_{1}|/r:=\max[0,\log|z_{1}|/r]
(cf.,
[
9
]
, p. 497).

Now
suppose
A=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:P(z)=0\}
where
P
is an irreducible polynomial of degree
d
and that
\pi:A\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
via
\pi(z)=z_{1}
is a
d-
sheeted covering map of
A
over
\mathbb{C}\setminus V
where
V
is a finite set of points. Following
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
, we assume
A
satisfies the following condition:

The algebraic curve
A
has
d
distinct non-parallel linear asymptotes
L_{1},...,L_{d}
; and each
L_{j}
may be parameterized by
t\to{\bf c_{j}}+t{\bf\lambda_{j}},\ t\in\mathbb{C}
, where

{\bf c_{j}}=(c_{j1},...,c_{jN}),\ {\bf\lambda_{j}}=(1,\lambda_{j2},...,\lambda_{jN})
and
\lambda_{jm}\neq\lambda_{km}
if
j\not=k
for
m=2,...,N
.

Here, a linear asymptote of
A
is a complex line
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
with

\lim_{|z|\to\infty,\ z\in L}|z-z_{A}|=0

where
z_{A}
is a closest point to
L
contained in
H_{z}\cap A
where
H_{z}
is the complex hyperplane orthogonal to
L
through
z
. We call
\{{\bf\lambda_{j}}\}_{j=1,...,d}
the set of directions of
A
.

For example, if
N=2
we can write
A=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:P(z_{1},z_{2})=0\}
where

P(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{j=0}^{d}h_{j}(z_{1},z_{2})

is an irreducible polynomial of degree
d
, and the sum is a decompositon of
P
into its homogeneous components
h_{j}
of degree
j
. In particular,

A_{h}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:h_{d}(z_{1},z_{2}))=0\}

is the associated homogeneous variety, and we can write

h_{d}(z_{1},z_{2})=C\prod_{j=1}^{d}(z_{2}-\lambda_{j}z_{1}).

Letting
\widetilde{L}_{j}=\{(z_{1},z_{2}):z_{2}-\lambda_{j}z_{1}=0\}
, we have

A_{h}=\cup_{j}\widetilde{L}_{j}
and these lines
\widetilde{L}_{j}
are parallel to the linear asymptotes for
A
. Then
A
satisfies the italicized condition above precisely when the
\lambda_{j}
are distinct and nonzero.

We record some relevant items from
[
6
]
:

Proposition 1.1
.

Given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R=R(\epsilon)>0
and
B=B(R)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:|z|<R\}
such that

1.

A\setminus\bar{B}\subset A^{0}
;

2.

A\setminus\bar{B}=D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{d}
where
D_{1},...,D_{d}
are pairwise disjoint domains in
A
;

3.

for each
j=1,...,d
,
dist(D_{j},L_{j})<\epsilon
;

4.

for each
j=1,...,d
, the projection
\pi_{j}:D_{j}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\pi_{j}(z_{1},z^{\prime})=z_{1}
is one-to-one.

From item 4),

A\setminus\bar{B}=\{(z_{1},z^{\prime}):(z_{1},s_{j}(z_{1})),\ j=1,...,d\}

where the
s_{j}(z_{1})
are distinct for
|z_{1}|>R
. Thus if
u\in SH(A)
,
u
can be thought of as
d
shm functions
u_{1},...,u_{d}
on each “branch”

A(j):=\{(z_{1},s_{j}(z_{1})):|z_{1}|>R\}.

In particular, given
u\in L(A)
, we define
d
Robin constants associated to
u
:

\rho_{u(j)}:=\limsup_{|z|\to\infty,\ z\in A(j)}[u(z)-\log|z|].

(1.1)

Then, in the notation above, we have

\rho_{u(j)}=\limsup_{|z|\to\infty,\ z\in D_{j}}[u(z)-\log|z_{1}|]=\limsup_{|z_{1}|\to\infty,\ z\in A,\ z/z_{1}\to{\bf\lambda_{j}}}[u(z)-\log|z_{1}|].

We write
\mathbb{C}[A]
for the coordinate ring of
A
, i.e.,
\mathbb{C}[A]=\mathbb{C}[z]/\textlangle P(z)\textrangle
, and for a polynomial
p
, its degree
deg(p)
will refer to its degree as an element of
\mathbb{C}[A]
. In particular, for a polynomial
p_{n}
of degree
n
in
\mathbb{C}[A]
, the Robin constants associated to
u:=\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}|
can be computed as

\rho_{u(j)}=\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}_{n}({\bf{\lambda_{j}}})|,\ j=1,...,d

(1.2)

where
\widehat{p}_{n}
is the top degree (
n
) homogeneous part of
p_{n}
.
For
K\subset A
nonpolar, since
V_{K}^{*}
is a harmonic function on
A^{0}\setminus K
, the limits

\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j}):=\rho_{V_{K}^{*}(j)}=\lim_{|z|\to\infty,\ z\in A(j)}[V_{K}^{*}(z)-\log|z|],\ j=1,...,d

exist.

Example 1.2
.

As a trivial example, when
A\subset\{(z_{1},z^{\prime}):|z^{\prime}|^{2}<C(1+|z_{1}|^{2})\}

where
z^{\prime}=(z_{2},...,z_{N})
and
C>0
, recall for
K:=\{z\in A:|z_{1}|\leq r\}
we have
V_{K}(z)=\max[0,\log|z_{1}|/r]
. Hence

\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j})=\limsup_{|z_{1}|\to\infty,\ z\in A,\ z/z_{1}\to{\bf\lambda_{j}}}[\log|z_{1}|/r-\log|z_{1}|]=-\log r

for
j=1,...,d.

Our first goal is to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A
be an irreducible algebraic curve in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and let
K\subset A
be nonpolar. Let
u\in L(A)
with

u\leq 0
on
K
and suppose
\rho_{u(j)}=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j}),\ j=1,...,d
. Then
u=V_{K}^{*}
on
A^{0}\setminus K
.

In the next section, we give a proof of this result in
\mathbb{C}
which can be modified to prove the actual result; that proof is then given in section 3.
Using Theorem
1.3
, Theorem 2.1 of
[
3
]
generalizes to the algebraic curve setting:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be an irreducible algebraic curve in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and let
K\subset A
be nonpolar. Let

\{p_{n}\}
be a sequence of polynomials with deg
p_{n}=n
satisfying
\limsup_{n\to\infty}||p_{n}||_{K}^{1/n}=1
such that, letting
\widehat{p}_{n}

be the degree
n
homogeneous piece of
p_{n}
, the function

\widehat{u}(z):=\bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}_{n}(z)|\bigr)^{*}\in L(A)

satisfies
\rho_{\widehat{u}(j)}=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j}),\ j=1,...,d
. Then

u(z):=\bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z)|\bigr)^{*}=V_{K}^{*}(z)

for
z\in A^{0}\setminus K
.

The main goal of this paper is to give some substance to these general results by specializing to compact, nonpolar subsets
K
of an algebraic curve
A
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In section 4, following
[
6
]
, we define classes of Chebyshev polynomials associated to the directions
\lambda_{j},\ j=1,...,d
; the corresponding Chebyshev constants
T(K,\lambda_{j}),\ j=1,...,d
are related to the Robin constants
\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j})
of
V_{K}
. We also consider classes of Chebyshev polynomials associated to a “standard” ordering of a monomial basis for
\mathbb{C}[A]
to obtain Chebyshev constants
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(j)),\ j=0,...,d-1
. The goal of section 4 is the following result:

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose the directions
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
are labelled so that

T(K,\lambda_{1})\geq T(K,\lambda_{2})\geq\cdots\geq T(K,\lambda_{d}).

Then
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))=T(K,\lambda_{k})
for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

We mention that a key part of the proof involves showing that the limit in the definition of
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
exists; here we utilize notions of transfinite diameter for
K
.

This leads, in section 5, to definitions of two families of
d
extremal-like functions in
L(A)
, denoted
V_{K}^{(k)},k=1,...,d
, defined in (
5.2
), and
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)},k=0,...,d-1
, defined in (
5.6
). With the aid of Theorem
1.4
, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.6
.

For
K\subset A\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
nonpolar, we have

\max[V_{K}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2}),...,V_{K}^{(d)}(z_{1},z_{2})]=V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2})\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K.

Furthermore, if

\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})<\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2})<\cdots<\rho_{K}(\lambda_{d}),

we have

\max[\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)}(z_{1},z_{2}),...,\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(d-1)}(z_{1},z_{2})]=V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2})\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K.

We remark that these last two results can be generalized to
\mathbb{C}^{N},\ N>2
but the proofs (and notation) would be cumbersome. At the end of section 5, we give specific examples to illustrate these results. The bulk of the work in this paper is section 4 where we relate the various Chebyshev constants arising from ordering bases of
\mathbb{C}[A]
in order to verify the appropriate properties of the extremal-like functions
V_{K}^{(k)},k=1,...,d
and
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)},k=0,...,d-1
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.3
in
\mathbb{C}

Theorem
1.3
is a generalization of a classical potential theoretic result in the plane:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact and nonpolar with
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
connected, and let
u\in L(\mathbb{C})
satisfy
u\leq 0
on
K
. Suppose
\rho_{u}:=\limsup_{|z|\to\infty}[u(z)-\log|z|]
equals
\rho_{K}:=\rho_{V_{K}^{*}}
. Then
u=V_{K}^{*}
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
.

Remark 2.2
.

There is an elementary proof of this fact, but not one that will easily generalize to the algebraic curve situation. Namely, the function
w:=u-V_{K}^{*}
is shm and nonpositive on
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
with
\limsup_{|z|\to\infty}w(z)=0
. Considering the Kelvin transform
\widetilde{w}(z)=w(z/|z|^{2})
gives us a shm and nonpositive function on
D\setminus\{0\}
where
D
is a bounded domain containing the origin. Since
\widetilde{w}
is bounded near the origin
0
, it extends across
0
via
\widetilde{w}(0):=\limsup_{z\to 0}\widetilde{w}(z)\leq 0
as a shm function on all of
D
. But
\limsup_{|z|\to\infty}w(z)=0
implies, in fact, that
\widetilde{w}(0)=0
which contradicts the maximum principle unless
\widetilde{w}\equiv 0
in
D
, i.e.,
w\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
. We thank Franck Wielonsky for this observation.

We consider shm functions
u\in L^{+}(\mathbb{C})
where

L^{+}(\mathbb{C})=\{u\in SH(\mathbb{C}):\log^{+}|z|-c_{u}\leq u(z)\leq\log^{+}|z|+c_{u}\}

where
c_{u}
is a constant depending on
u
. We will need the following result for such functions on the way to a proof of Theorem
2.1
which will generalize to algebraic curves.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
u\in L^{+}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\rho_{u}:=\limsup_{|z|\to\infty}[u(z)-\log|z|]=\lim_{R\to\infty}[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(Re^{i\theta})d\theta-logR].

Proof.

Let
w(z):=u(z)-\log|z|
. Then
w
is shm in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and bounded near
\infty
. Define
\widetilde{w}(z):=w(z/|z|^{2})
. Then
\widetilde{w}
is shm in a deleted neighborhood of the origin, and is bounded near
\{0\}
. Thus we can extend
\widetilde{w}
to a shm function in this neighborhood of
\{0\}
by defining

\widetilde{w}(0):=\limsup_{z\to 0}\widetilde{w}(z)=\rho_{u}.

Then

\rho_{u}=\widetilde{w}(0)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\widetilde{w}(re^{i\theta})d\theta

=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}[u(re^{i\theta}/r^{2})-\log 1/r]d\theta=\lim_{R\to\infty}[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(Re^{i\theta})d\theta-logR].

∎

The key fact we need for this second proof of Theorem
2.1
is the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u,v\in L^{+}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{C}}[udd^{c}v-vdd^{c}u]:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}[u\Delta v-v\Delta u]dA=\rho_{u}-\rho_{v}.

Here,
dd^{c}u=\Delta udA
where
dA=\frac{i}{2}dz\wedge d\bar{z}=dx\wedge dy
and
\Delta u
are distributional derivatives.

Proof.

We may assume
u,v
are smooth. It suffices to prove the result for
u\in L^{+}(\mathbb{C})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
u_{0}(z):=\log^{+}|z|
for then we apply the result to
v\in L^{+}(\mathbb{C})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
u_{0}
and hence to
u,v
. For
R>>1
, letting
B_{R}:=\{z:|z|<R\}
, we have

\int_{B_{R}}[udd^{c}u_{0}-u_{0}dd^{c}u]=\int_{\partial B_{R}}[ud^{c}u_{0}-u_{0}d^{c}u]=\int_{\partial B_{R}}[u\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial n}-u_{0}\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}]ds

\int_{\partial B_{R}}[u/R-\log R\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}]ds=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}[u(Re^{i\theta})/R-\log R\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}]Rd\theta

=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(Re^{i\theta})d\theta-\log R\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}ds

=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(Re^{i\theta})d\theta-\log R\int_{B_{R}}\Delta udA.

Letting
n_{u}(R):=\int_{B_{R}}\Delta udA
, we have

\int_{B_{R}}[udd^{c}u_{0}-u_{0}dd^{c}u]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(Re^{i\theta})d\theta-\log R\cdot n_{u}(R)

=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(Re^{i\theta})d\theta-\log R+[1-n_{u}(R)]\log R.

But by Corollary 1.1 of
[
7
]
, for
u\in L^{+}(\mathbb{C})
,
\lim_{R\to\infty}[1-n_{u}(R)]\log R=0
(or see section 1 of
[
5
]
, specifically, the proof of Proposition 1.1 and items (i)-(iii) on p. 62). The result follows from Proposition
2.3
.

∎

Choose

; then the result follows from Theorem
1.3
. For each
j=1,...,d
, the univariate function
v_{n}(z_{1}):=\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z_{1},s_{j}(z_{1}))|
is shm on
D_{j}
, and hence

\limsup_{|z_{1}|\to\infty,\ z\in D_{j}}[v_{n}(z_{1})-\log|z_{1}|]=\inf_{R\geq 1}[\max_{|z_{1}|=R}v_{n}(z_{1})-\log R].

This implies that (recall (
1.2
))

\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}_{n}({\bf{\lambda_{j}}})|\leq\max_{|z_{1}|=R}\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z_{1},s_{j}(z_{1}))|-\log R.

Taking the
\limsup
as
n\to\infty
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}_{n}({\bf{\lambda_{j}}})|\leq\max_{|z_{1}|=R}u(z_{1},s_{j}(z_{1}))-\log R

where we have used Hartogs’ lemma and the fact that
u(z)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z)|
.
Letting
R\to\infty
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}_{n}({\bf{\lambda_{j}}})|\leq\inf_{R\geq 1}[\max_{|z_{1}|=R}u(z_{1},s_{j}(z_{1}))-\log R].

Thus,
\rho_{\widehat{u}(j)}\leq\rho_{u(j)}
.

∎

4
Chebyshev constants and transfinite diameter

We begin with some generalities on classes of polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[z]
or
\mathbb{C}[A]
and Chebyshev constants associated to compact subsets
K
of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
or
A
. Given homogeneous polynomials
Q
and
R
, define the polynomial classes

\mathcal{M}(Q):=\{Q^{n}+\text{\sc lot}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\},\quad\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q):=\{RQ^{n}+\text{\sc lot}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\},

where ‘
lot
’ in ‘
q+\text{\sc lot}
’ stands for
lower order terms
, i.e., a polynomial of degree strictly less than
q
. For more clarity we might also write
\text{\sc lot}(q)
or
\text{\sc lot}(n)
for terms with degrees strictly less than
\deg(q)
or
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We will also consider other classes of polynomials, but any class
\mathcal{N}
will satisfy:

(i)

\deg(\mathcal{N}):=\{\deg(p)\}_{p\in\mathcal{N}}
is an unbounded subset of
\mathbb{N}
;

(ii)

for any compact set
K
and
n\in\deg(\mathcal{N})
, there exists
q\in\mathcal{N}
with

||q||_{K}=\inf\{\|p\|_{K}\colon p\in\mathcal{N},\ \deg(p)=n\}
.

We will refer to
\mathcal{N}
as a (generalized)
monic polynomial class
.

Given
n\in\deg(\mathcal{N})
, define

T_{n}(K,\mathcal{N}):=\inf\{\|p\|_{K}\colon p\in\mathcal{N},\ \deg(p)=n\}^{1/n}.

(4.1)

A polynomial in
\mathcal{N}
that attains the inf is called a
Chebyshev polynomial
of degree
n
associated to
K,\mathcal{N}
. Then
define

\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}):=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in\deg(\mathcal{N})\end{subarray}}T_{n}(K,\mathcal{N}),\quad\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}):=\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n\in\deg(\mathcal{N})\end{subarray}}T_{n}(K,\mathcal{N}).

(4.2)

For convenience we suppress ‘
n\in\deg(\mathcal{N})
’ which is understood.
If
\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{N})=\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{N})
, i.e., the limit

T(K,\mathcal{N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}(K,\mathcal{N})

(4.3)

exists, we call it the
Chebyshev constant
associated to
K,\mathcal{N}
.

The next (classical) result follows since
p,q\in\mathcal{M}(Q)
imply
pq\in\mathcal{M}(Q)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The limit (
4.3
) exists when
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}(Q)
.

We will need to compare Chebyshev constants associated to different monic polynomial classes. To this end, we derive some estimates.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K
be a compact set and let
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}
be two monic polynomial classes.
Suppose there is a mapping
\Phi\colon\mathcal{N}_{1}\to\mathcal{N}_{2}
with the property that

\limsup_{\deg(p)\to\infty}\left(\frac{\|\Phi(p)\|_{K}}{\|p\|_{K}}\right)^{1/\deg(p)}\leq M,\qquad\liminf_{\deg(p)\to\infty}\frac{\deg(\Phi(p))}{\deg(p)}\geq c

for some constants
M,c>0
.
Then

\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{c}\leq M\cdot\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1}).

(4.4)

Proof.

Let
p\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
be a Chebyshev polynomial of order
n=\deg(p)
. Let
m_{n}:=\deg(\Phi(p))
. Then

T_{m_{n}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{m_{n}}\leq\frac{\|\Phi(p)\|_{K}}{\|p\|_{K}}\|p\|_{K}=\frac{\|\Phi(p)\|_{K}}{\|p\|_{K}}(T_{n}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1}))^{n}.

Taking
n
-th roots,

T_{m_{n}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{m_{n}/n}\leq\left(\frac{\|\Phi(p)\|_{K}}{\|p\|_{K}}\right)^{1/n}T_{n}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1}).

(4.5)

Taking a subsequence
n^{\prime}
of
n
such that
T_{n^{\prime}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1})\to\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1})
, we have

\limsup_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}\left(\frac{\|\Phi(p)\|_{K}}{\|p\|_{K}}\right)^{1/n^{\prime}}T_{n^{\prime}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1})\leq M\cdot\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1})

on the right-hand side of the inequality. On the left-hand side,

\liminf_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}T_{m_{n^{\prime}}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{m_{n^{\prime}}/n^{\prime}}\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{m_{n}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{c}\geq\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{c}.

∎

The main example is
\Phi(p):=\widetilde{R}p
from
\mathcal{M}(Q)
to
\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q)
where
\widetilde{R}
is any polynomial of the form
\widetilde{R}=R+\text{\sc lot}(R)
. It is easy to see that the conditions of Lemma
4.2
are satisfied. Also,
m_{n}=n+\deg(R)
and the following property also holds.

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix the notation and hypotheses in the previous lemma.
If
n\mapsto m_{n}
is onto for large
n
, i.e.,

\deg(\mathcal{N}_{2})\setminus\deg(\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{1}))
is finite, then

\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{c}\leq M\cdot\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1})

(4.6)

Proof.

The additional hypothesis means that a limsup sequence in
\deg(\mathcal{N}_{2})
for
\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})
will eventually coincide with a sequence in
\deg(\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{1}))=\{m_{n}\}
as
n\to\infty
. Then

\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{c}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{m_{n}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{c}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{m_{n}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{m_{n}/n}.

Using (
4.5
), the right-hand side is bounded above by
M\cdot\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1})
.
∎

For convenience, we write equations (
4.4
), (
4.6
) in the shorthand

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{2})^{c}\leq M\cdot\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{N}_{1}).

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
K
be a compact set and let
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
,
R
be homogeneous polynomials. Then

1.

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}R_{2}}(Q))\leq\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}}(Q))
.

2.

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{cR}(Q))=\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q))
for any constant
c\neq 0
.

3.

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R}(\lambda Q))=|\lambda|\cdot\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q))
for any constant
\lambda\neq 0
.

4.

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{RQ}(Q))=\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q))
.

Proof.

1.

Apply Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
to the map
p\mapsto R_{2}p
.

2.

Apply part 1 with
R_{1}=R
,
R_{2}=c
, then with
R_{1}=cR
,
R_{2}=1/c
.

3.

Apply Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
to the map
p\mapsto\lambda^{\deg(p)}p
from
\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q))
to
\mathcal{M}_{R}(\lambda Q))
, then to its inverse
p\mapsto\lambda^{-\deg(p)}p
.

4.

By part 1,
\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{RQ}(Q))\leq\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q))
.
But also
\mathcal{M}_{RQ}(Q)\subset\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q)
. Hence

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R}(Q))\leq\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{RQ}(Q))
by definition.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
be homogeneous polynomials with
\deg(R_{1})=\deg(R_{2})
. Then

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}+R_{2}}(Q))\leq\max\{\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}}(Q)),\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{2}}(Q))\}.

Proof.

Let
r:=\deg(R_{1})=\deg(R_{2})
. If
p_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}}(Q)
and
p_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{R_{2}}(Q)
are polynomials of the same degree then

\displaystyle p_{1}+p_{2}=(R_{1}Q^{n}+\text{\sc lot})+(R_{2}Q^{n}+\text{\sc lot})=(R_{1}+R_{2})Q^{n}+\text{\sc lot},

so
p_{1}+p_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}+R_{2}}(Q)
with the same or lesser degree. When
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are Chebyshev polynomials of degree
n
associated to
K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}}(Q)
and
K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{2}}(Q)
, we have

\displaystyle T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}+R_{2}}(Q))^{n}\leq\|p_{1}+p_{2}\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\|p_{1}\|_{K}+\|p_{2}\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}}(Q))^{n}+T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{2}}(Q))^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\{T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}}(Q)),T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R_{1}}(Q))\}^{n}.

Take the
n
-th root of both sides, then limsup and liminf as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Remark
.

The inequalities may be strict, e.g. consider
R_{2}=-R_{1}
in the above lemma. To get a strict inequality in Corollary
4.4
part 1, consider a set
K
contained in an algebraic set and let
R_{2}
be a polynomial that vanishes on this set.

For the rest of this paper, we turn to the situation where
A
is an irreducible algebraic curve in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

A:=\{z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\colon P_{d}(z)=0\},

with
d=\deg(P_{d})
linear asymptotes, none of which are parallel to a coordinate axis. We begin by discussing some algebraic computations in the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[A]
. The reader should see section 3 of
[
8
]
for further results.

The asymptotic directions are given by nonzero constants
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\in\mathbb{C}
, where each linear asymptote of
A
is of the form
z_{2}-\lambda_{k}z_{1}=c_{k}
for some
c_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\widehat{P}_{d}
denote the leading homogeneous part of
P_{d}
; then

\widehat{P}_{d}(z)=C\prod_{k=1}^{d}(z_{2}-\lambda_{k}z_{1})

where
C
is the coefficient of
z_{2}^{d}
. Let
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}
be the corresponding directional basis polynomials; these are polynomials of degree
d-1
given by

\mathbf{v}_{k}=\prod_{j\neq k}\frac{z_{2}-\lambda_{j}z_{1}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}}=\frac{\widehat{P}_{d}(z)}{C(z_{2}-\lambda_{k}z_{1})}\cdot\frac{1}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})}

so
\mathbf{v}_{j}(1,\lambda_{k})=\delta_{jk}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\hbox{if }j\neq k\\
1&\hbox{if }j=k\end{array}\right..

Algebraic computation in
\mathbb{C}[A]
means that
P(z)=\widehat{P}(z)+\text{\sc lot}=0
for all
z\in A
. Using this gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{j}(z)\mathbf{v}_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\text{\sc lot}&\hbox{if }j\neq k\\
z_{1}^{d-1}\mathbf{v}_{j}(z)+\text{\sc lot}&\hbox{if }j=k\end{array}\right.,

\displaystyle\hbox{ and }\mathbf{v}_{j}(z)(z_{2}-\lambda_{j}z_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{\sc lot}.

(4.10)

Define the
directional
basis
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{C}[A]
by

\displaystyle 1,z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2},\ldots,z_{2}^{d-2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d},z_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,z_{1}\mathbf{v}_{d},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{d},

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{1}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}^{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{d}^{2},

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{1}^{k},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}^{k},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{1}^{k},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{d}^{k},\ldots

Basis elements of degree
\leq d-2
are standard monomials, and basis elements of degree
n
are of the form
z_{1}^{r}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{q}
where
n=q(d-1)+r
and
k=1,...,d;\ 0\leq r<d-1
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
. Then in
\mathbb{C}[A]
,

\displaystyle z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}c_{jk}\mathbf{v}_{k}+\text{\sc lot},\hbox{ with $c_{jk}\neq 0$ for all $k$.}

Proof.

The formula itself is elementary linear algebra (express
z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}
in terms of the basis
\mathcal{C}
). We need to show that the coefficients
c_{jk}
are all nonzero.

If
c_{jk}=0
for some
k
, then multiply on both sides by
\mathbf{v}_{k}
to obtain

\mathbf{v}_{k}z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}=\mathbf{v}_{k}\left(\sum_{l\neq k}c_{jl}\mathbf{v}_{l}+\text{\sc lot}(d-1)\right)=\text{\sc lot}(2d-2)

(4.11)

using (
4
). Hence there exists a polynomial
Q(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{v}_{k}z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}+\text{\sc lot}
that is zero on
A
, so
A\subset\mathbf{V}(Q):=\{Q=0\}
.
Factoring the leading homogeneous part,

\widehat{Q}=\mathbf{v}_{k}z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}=z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}\prod_{j\neq k}\frac{z_{2}-\lambda_{j}z_{1}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}}.

Observe that the algebraic set
\mathbf{V}(Q)
cannot have a linear asymptote in the
k
-th direction; it has asymptotes in the other directions, and possibly additional horizontal and vertical asymptotes from the monomial factor. Therefore no subset of
\mathbf{V}(Q)
has the same asymptotic behavior as
A
, contradicting
A\subset\mathbf{V}(Q)
. Thus
c_{jk}\neq 0
. ∎

The special case

z_{1}^{d-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathbf{v}_{k}+\text{\sc lot}

(4.12)

follows from Lemma
4.6
and the calculation

z_{1}^{d-1}\mathbf{v}_{j}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}c_{dk}\mathbf{v}_{k}+\text{\sc lot}(d-1)\right)\mathbf{v}_{j}=c_{dj}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{2}+\text{\sc lot}=c_{dj}z_{1}^{d-1}\mathbf{v}_{j}+\text{\sc lot}.

Equating coefficients yields
c_{dj}=1
.

Using (
4
), (
4.10
), and Lemma
4.6
, we have the following useful result (cf., section 4 of
[
1
]
or Proposition 5.1 of
[
6
]
).

Corollary 4.7
.

For
q\in\mathbb{C}[A]
,

q(z)\mathbf{v}_{k}(z)=z_{1}^{deg(q)}\widehat{q}(1,\lambda_{k})\mathbf{v}_{k}(z)+\text{\sc lot}.

Let
K\subset A
be compact. Recall by Proposition
4.1
that the limit (
4.3
) exists for
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{v}_{k})
. For
k=1,...,d
, we define

T(K,\lambda_{k}):=T(K,\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))

(4.13)

to be the
directional Chebyshev constant
of
K
for the direction
\lambda_{k}
. Note this appears to be different than the directional Chebyshev constant
\tau(K,\lambda_{k})
of
K
for the direction
\lambda_{k}
as defined in Definition 5.3 of
[
6
]
(and earlier in
[
1
]
); there it was shown that
-\log\tau(K,\lambda_{k})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k})
. In fact,
\tau(K,\lambda_{k})
from those papers coincides with our Chebyshev constant
T(K,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{k}}(z_{1}))
; and we show, in Corollary
4.10
, that
T(K,\lambda_{k})=T(K,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{k}}(z_{1}))
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
j_{1},j_{2}\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\},\ k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and suppose
j_{1}+j_{2}\leq d-2
. Then

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j_{1}}z_{2}^{j_{2}}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))=T(K,\lambda_{k}).

In particular, the limit (
4.3
) also exists for the class
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j_{1}}z_{2}^{j_{2}}}(\mathbf{v}_{k})
.

Proof.

Write
j:=j_{1}
; then
j_{2}\leq d-2-j
. By Lemma
4.6
,

\displaystyle z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{k}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{jl}\mathbf{v}_{l}+\text{\sc lot}\right)\mathbf{v}_{k}^{n-1}+\text{\sc lot}

\displaystyle=c_{jk}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{n+1}+\text{\sc lot}

where
c_{jk}\neq 0
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}}(\mathbf{v}_{k})=\mathcal{M}_{c_{jk}\mathbf{v}_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{k})
. By Corollary
4.4
,

\displaystyle T(K,\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))=\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{c_{jk}\mathbf{v}_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))

\displaystyle=\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j_{1}}z_{2}^{j_{2}}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))\leq T(K,\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{v}_{k})).

∎

The proof of Proposition
4.8
is based on
c_{jk}\neq 0
from Lemma
4.6
, which uses the fact that the leading homogeneous part of
z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{d-1-j}\mathbf{v}_{k}
does not contain a factor of
z_{2}-\lambda_{k}z_{1}
. One can prove something a bit more general. We omit the details as we will not use this result in the sequel.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
R(z_{1},z_{2})
be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[A]
. Suppose for some
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
,
z_{2}-\lambda_{k}z_{1}
is not a factor of
R
. Then

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{M}_{R}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))=T(K,\lambda_{k}),

i.e., the limit (
4.3
) exists for
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}_{R}(\mathbf{v}_{k})
and equals the right-hand side. ∎

Corollary 4.10
.

We have
T(K,\lambda_{k})=T(K,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{k}}(z_{1}))
for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Hence

-\log T(K,\lambda_{k})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k}),\ k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}.

Proof.

By applying (
4
) the requisite number of times,

z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{m}+\text{\sc lot}=z_{1}^{n}\mathbf{v}_{k}+\text{\sc lot}\hbox{ whenever }l+(d-1)(m-1)=n.

(4.14)

From the case
l=0
,
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{v}_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{k}}(z_{1})
.
Thus
\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{k}}(z_{1}))\leq T(K,\lambda_{k})
.
It remains to show that
T(K,\lambda_{k})\leq\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{k}}(z_{1}))
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. By Proposition
4.8
, we can choose
N>d
sufficiently large that

T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))\geq(1-\epsilon)T(K,\lambda_{k}),\hbox{ for all }n\geq N,\ l\in\{0,\ldots,d-2\}.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the division algorithm determines
m,l
uniquely when
n=(d-1)(m-1)+l
and
l<d-1
; then

(1-\epsilon)T(K,\lambda_{k})\leq T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))=T_{n}(K,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{k}}(z_{1}))

for all
n\geq N
where equality of the last two quantities follows from (
4.14
). Finally, take the liminf as
n\to\infty
and let
\epsilon\to 0
.
∎

We construct more polynomial classes using ordered bases. Precisely, consider polynomials in
\text{span}(\mathcal{B})
, where
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is linearly independent and listed according to a
graded ordering
, i.e.,
\deg(\mathbf{b}_{j})\leq\deg(\mathbf{b}_{j+1})
for all
j
.

Notation
.

Given a collection
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
with a graded ordering, and a polynomial
p\in\text{span}(\mathcal{B})
,
p=\sum_{k=1}^{j}c_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k}
with
c_{j}\neq 0
, write
\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{B}}(p)
to denote an arbitrary polynomial of the form
\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}a_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k}
. Also,
p+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{B}}:=p+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{B}}(p)
.

Recall that the basis
\mathcal{C}
is ordered so that
z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{n}
comes before
z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{j+1}^{n}
for each
j
. Define the classes

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l}(\mathbf{v}_{j}):=\{p\in\mathbb{C}[V]\colon p=z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{C}},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\},\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}):=\bigcup_{l=0}^{d-2}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l}(\mathbf{v}_{j}).

Lemma 4.11
.

We have
T(K,\lambda_{j})=T(K,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))
for all
l\in\{0,\ldots,d-2\}
. Hence
T(K,\lambda_{j})=T(K,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))
.

Proof.

Fix
l
. Clearly
\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{j})\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l}(\mathbf{v}_{j})
, so
T(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))\geq\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))
.

For the reverse inequality, if
p\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l}(\mathbf{v}_{j})
, then

p=z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{C}}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

(z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{C}})\mathbf{v}_{j}=(z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{n}+\sum_{k\neq j}c_{k}z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{n}+\text{\sc lot})\mathbf{v}_{j}=z_{1}^{l}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{n+1}+\text{\sc lot}\in\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}).

Hence
p\mapsto p\mathbf{v}_{j}
maps
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l}(\mathbf{v}_{j})
to
\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{j})
. Applying Lemmas
4.2
–
4.3
,
T(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))\leq\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))
.

Thus
\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))=T(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{l}}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))
, and by Proposition
4.8
we get the first statement. The second statement follows from the first.
∎

Another basis of
\mathbb{C}[A]
is the monomial basis
\mathcal{S}
consisting of all monomials of degree
\leq d-2
, and
z_{1}^{n},z_{1}^{n-1}z_{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{n-d+1}z_{2}^{d-1}
in degrees
n\geq d-1
. We use the
grevlex ordering
, where we order by increasing degree, and by increasing powers of
z_{2}
within the same degree.
For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-2\}
, define

\mathcal{Z}(k)=\{p\in\mathbb{C}[V]\colon p(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}^{k}z_{1}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}\}.

With this ordering,
\mathcal{Z}(0)=\mathcal{M}(z_{1})
so
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(0))
exists by Proposition
4.1
. Applying Lemmas
4.2
–
4.3
to the map
p\mapsto z_{2}p
, we also have monotonicity:

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))\geq\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))\hbox{ for all }k\geq 1.

It turns out that
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
exists for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-2\}
and coincides with the
(k+1)
-st largest directional Chebyshev constant. For the moment we show the following.

Proposition 4.12
.

Suppose the directions
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
are labelled so that

T(K,\lambda_{1})\geq T(K,\lambda_{2})\geq\cdots\geq T(K,\lambda_{d}).

Then
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(0))=T(K,\lambda_{1})
and
\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))\leq T(K,\lambda_{k+1})
for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
.

Proof.

We first do the following computation in
\mathbb{C}[A]
:

\displaystyle\Pi^{(k)}z_{1}^{d-1}:=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}(z_{2}-\lambda_{j}z_{1})\right)z_{1}^{d-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}(z_{2}-\lambda_{j}z_{1})\right)\left(\sum_{l=1}^{d}\mathbf{v}_{l}+\text{\sc lot}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k+1}^{d}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j})\right)z_{1}^{k}\mathbf{v}_{l}+\text{\sc lot}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{l=k+1}^{d}c_{kl}z_{1}^{k}\mathbf{v}_{l}+\text{\sc lot}.

The equality in the second line uses Corollary
4.7
.
Now
z_{2}^{k}z_{1}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}=\Pi^{(k)}z_{1}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}
by expanding
\Pi^{(k)}
; using this and the previous calculation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(k)

\displaystyle=\left\{\Pi^{(k)}z_{1}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{l=k+1}^{d}c_{kl}z_{1}^{n+k-d+1}\mathbf{v}_{l}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\left\{\sum_{l=k+1}^{d}c_{kl}z_{1}^{n+k-d+1}\mathbf{v}_{l}+\text{\sc lot}\right\}=\mathcal{M}_{\sum_{k+1}^{d}c_{kl}\mathbf{v}_{l}}(z_{1}).

Using Corollary
4.4
, Lemma
4.5
and definition (
4.13
),

\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))\leq T(K,\mathcal{M}_{\sum_{k+1}^{d}c_{kl}\mathbf{v}_{l}}(z_{1}))\leq\max_{l\geq k+1}\{T(K,\lambda_{l})\}=T(K,\lambda_{k+1}).

When
k=0
we also have

T(K,\lambda_{1})=T(K,\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{v}_{1}}(z_{1}))\leq T(K,\mathcal{M}(z_{1}))=T(K,\mathcal{Z}(0))

from Corollary
4.10
. Thus
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(0))=T(K,\lambda_{1})
.
∎

We recall the construction of transfinite diameter of a compact set
K
with respect
to a collection of polynomials
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
arranged in a graded ordering.

Given
\{\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}\}\subset K
define
\mathrm{VDM}_{\mathcal{B}}(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}):=\det\bigl[\mathbf{b}_{j}(\zeta_{k})\bigr]_{j,k=1}^{n}
and

V_{n}=V_{n}(K,\mathcal{B}):=\sup\{|\mathrm{VDM}_{\mathcal{B}}(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})|\colon\zeta_{j}\in K,\ \forall j\}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
m_{n}
be the number of polynomials in
\mathcal{B}
of degree at most
n
and let
l_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}\deg(\mathbf{b}_{j})
be the sum of the degrees. The
transfinite diameter of
K
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
is given by

d_{\mathcal{B}}(K):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(V_{m_{n}}\right)^{1/l_{n}}.

Using
\mathcal{B}
we also define Chebyshev constants

\tau_{n}=\tau_{n}(K,\mathcal{B}):=\inf\{\|p\|_{K}^{1/\deg(p)}\colon p\in\text{span}(\mathcal{B}),\ p(z)=\mathbf{b}_{n}(z)+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{B}}\}.

The inequality

\frac{V_{n}}{V_{n-1}}\leq n\tau_{n}^{deg(\mathbf{b}_{n})}\hbox{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}

(4.16)

is well-known (cf.,
[
10
]
) and will be used in what follows.

For the rest of this section
\{\mathbf{b}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
will be the (grevlex ordered) monomial basis
\mathcal{S}
. Let
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
z_{1}^{d-1}=\mathbf{b}_{N_{0}}
; then

z_{1}^{d-1+m-k}z_{2}^{k}=\mathbf{b}_{N_{0}+md+k}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Instead of
m
, we can relabel these monomials using the parameter
n:=d-1+m
, which is their total degree. Setting
M:=N_{0}-d^{2}+d
, a calculation yields

z_{1}^{n-k}z_{2}^{k}=\mathbf{b}_{M+nd+k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\ n\geq d.

It follows that
\tau_{M+nd+k}^{n}=T_{n}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))^{n}
, so

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tau_{M+k+nd}=\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))=:\overline{\tau}(M+k+nd).

(4.17)

(The notation on the right will be useful shortly; it associates the lim sup with the indices of those monomials of the form
z_{1}^{n-k}z_{2}^{k}
.)

In fact, the quantities
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
exist (
\limsup
in (
4.17
) may be replaced by
\lim
) and we have a formula for the transfinite diameter in terms of these quantities.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
K\subset A
be compact, and suppose the directions
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
are labelled so that
T(K,\lambda_{k})\geq T(K,\lambda_{k+1})
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-2\}
. Then

\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))\right)^{1/d}=d_{\mathcal{S}}(K)=d_{\mathcal{C}}(K)=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}T(K,\lambda_{k})\right)^{1/d},

(4.18)

and hence
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))=T(K,\lambda_{k})
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

We first prove the following.

Proposition 4.14
.

Theorem
4.13
holds with
\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
in place of
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
for each
k
.

Proof.

The second and third equalities of (
4.18
) have been proved in
[
8
]
, Theorem 5.7 and Corollary 5.14. Let us write the common quantity given by these expressions as
d(K)
.

We will use Proposition
4.12
to show that

d(K)=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))\right)^{1/d}.

(4.19)

Let
\epsilon>0
. Choose a large
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
,

\tau_{M+k+nd}\leq(1+\epsilon)\overline{\tau}(M+k+nd)\hbox{ whenever }n\geq n_{0}

(recall (
4.17
) for the notation). Let
N:=M+n_{0}
; then

\overline{\tau}(n)=T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))\hbox{ whenever }n\geq N\hbox{ and }n-N\equiv k\mod d.

For convenience write
\alpha_{j}=\deg(\mathbf{b}_{j})
; then

\alpha_{j}=\frac{j}{d}+O(1).

(4.20)

Now let
n\geq N
, and write
n=N+dm+k
for some nonnegative integers
m,k
with
k<d
. Bound
V_{n}
from above using (
4.16
) and a telescoping product. We have

\displaystyle V_{n}=V_{N-1}\frac{V_{N}}{V_{N-1}}\cdots\frac{V_{n}}{V_{n-1}}

\displaystyle\leq V_{N-1}\frac{n!}{N!}\prod_{\nu=0}^{n-N}\tau_{\nu+N}^{\alpha_{\nu+N}}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\leq V_{N-1}\frac{n!}{N!}\prod_{\nu=0}^{dm}(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha_{\nu+N}}\overline{\tau}(\nu+N)^{\alpha_{\nu+N}}\prod_{\nu=dm+1}^{dm+k}C^{\alpha_{\nu+N}}

where we use some fixed constant
C\geq(1+\epsilon)\max\{\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(0)),\ldots,\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(d-1))\}
to estimate the last
k
terms of the product.

Using (
4.20
), a calculation gives

\sum_{\nu=0}^{dm}\alpha_{\nu+N}=\frac{dm^{2}}{2}+O(m),\quad\sum_{\nu=dm+1}^{dm+k}\alpha_{\nu+N}=km+O(1)=O(m),

so we can estimate the upper bound in (
4.21
) from above by

V_{N-1}\frac{(d(m+1)+N)!}{N!}C^{O(m)}(1+\epsilon)^{\frac{dm^{2}}{2}+O(m)}\prod_{\nu=0}^{dm}\overline{\tau}(\nu+N)^{\alpha_{\nu+N}}.

(4.22)

Now
\overline{\tau}(\nu+N)=\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
whenever
\nu=k+ds
, so

\displaystyle\prod_{\nu=0}^{dm}\overline{\tau}(\nu+N)^{\alpha_{\nu+N}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{d}\prod_{s=0}^{m}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))^{s+O(1)}

(4.23)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{d}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))^{\frac{m^{2}}{2}+O(1)}.

By definition
n=dm+O(1)
, so

l_{n}=\frac{dm^{2}}{2}+O(m).

(4.24)

Taking
l_{n}
-th roots of
V_{n}
and using (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), we have (when
n\to\infty
, equivalently
m\to\infty
) that

d(K)=\lim_{n\to\infty}(V_{n})^{1/l_{n}}\leq(1+\epsilon)\prod_{k=1}^{d}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))^{1/d},

where, in (
4.22
), the factors preceding the term
(1+\epsilon)^{\frac{dm^{2}}{2}+O(m)}
all go to 1 in the limit upon taking roots. (For the factorial terms, use the standard fact that
(m!)^{1/m^{2}}\to 1
as
m\to\infty
.) Letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we have
d(K)\leq\prod_{k=1}^{d}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))^{1/d}
. On the other hand,

\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))\right)^{1/d}\leq\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}T(K,\lambda_{k})\right)^{1/d}=d(K)

by Proposition
4.12
, and we get the reverse inequality.
∎

We will now complete the proof of Theorem
4.13
by showing that the limits defining
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
exist; equivalently,

\underline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))=\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))\hbox{ for each }k.

The proof for all cases is the same; we do it for
k=1
. In this case, we are looking at Chebyshev constants associated to polynomials of the form
z_{1}^{m}z_{2}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}
.
In what follows, redefine
N\in\mathbb{N}
by
\mathbf{b}_{N}=z_{1}^{d-2}z_{2}
; then the Chebyshev constants of interest are
\tau_{N+dm}
. We begin with the following.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
\Sigma_{m}:=\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}\nu
. Then

\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1))=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=1}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{\nu}\right)^{1/\Sigma_{m}}.

(4.25)

Proof.

We estimate as in (
4.21
), with
n=N-2+dm
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. (We will then let
m\to\infty
.) We have

V_{n}\leq V_{N-2}\frac{n!}{(N-2)!}\prod_{j=N-1}^{n}\tau_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}=V_{N-2}\frac{n!}{(N-2)!}\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N-1+d\nu+k}^{d-1+\nu}.

(4.26)

As
m\to\infty
we have
n\to\infty
, and therefore

\displaystyle d(K)\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N-1+d\nu+k}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}.

(4.27)

(As before, the other factors on the right-hand side of (
4.26
) go to
1
in the limit.) We will use (
4.27
) to show that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}=\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1))^{1/d}.

(4.28)

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then for each
k
,
\tau_{N-1+d\nu+k}<(1+\epsilon)\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
holds for all but finitely many
\nu
; we apply this to all terms where
k\neq 1
on the right-hand side of (
4.27
):

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N-1+d\nu+k}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{k\neq 1}\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}(1+\epsilon)\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}

\displaystyle\times\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}

\displaystyle=\left((1+\epsilon)\prod_{k\neq 1}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k)))\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\times\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}.

Then using Theorem
4.13
,

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k)))\right)^{1/d}=d(K)

\displaystyle\leq\left((1+\epsilon)\prod_{k\neq 1}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k)))\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\times\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}.

Cancelling the common factors
\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))
for
k\neq 1
on each side, and letting
\epsilon\to 0
,

\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1)))^{1/d}\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}.

To get a reverse inequality, let
\epsilon>0
. Then
\tau_{N+dm}<(1+\epsilon)\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1))
for all but finitely many
m\in\mathbb{N}
, so

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}((1+\epsilon)\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1)))^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left((1+\epsilon)\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1))\right)^{\left(\sum_{\nu=0}^{m}(d-1-\nu)\right)/l_{n}}.

Using (
4.24
), we have
\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{m}(d-1-\nu)=\frac{m^{2}+O(m)}{dm^{2}+O(m)}\to\frac{1}{d}
as
m\to\infty
,
and we obtain

\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}\leq\left((1+\epsilon)\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))\right)^{1/d}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
we get the reverse inequality; altogether,

\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1)))^{1/d}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n}}.

This is almost (
4.28
), except that we need limsup replaced by lim.

For the sake of obtaining a contradiction, suppose the limsup is not a limit. Then there exists
\delta>0
and
a subsequence
\{m_{j}\}
in the parameter
m
such that

\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1)))^{1/d}-\delta=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{m_{j}}\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}\right)^{1/l_{n_{j}}}.

But then, by similar reasoning as above with the limit formula
d(K)=\lim_{j\to\infty}(V_{n_{j}})^{1/l_{n_{j}}}
(where
n_{j}=N-2+dm_{j}
), we obtain for any
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))\right)^{1/d}\leq\left((1+\epsilon)\prod_{k\neq 1}\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k)))\right)^{1/d}\left(\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1)))^{1/d}-\delta\right)

and therefore
\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1))^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq(1+\epsilon)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}(\overline{T}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1)))^{\frac{1}{d}}-\delta)
.
Letting
\epsilon\to 0
gives a contradiction. Thus (
4.28
) holds.

To finish the proof, we must modify (
4.28
) to obtain (
4.25
). First, we may replace
\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1+\nu}
in the product by
\tau_{N+d\nu}^{\nu}
. This amounts to dividing out
\tau_{N+d\nu}^{d-1}
for each
\nu=1,\ldots,m
. If we estimate all of these
\tau_{N+d\nu}
quantities by a uniform constant
C
then (
4.28
) is off by an estimated
C^{m(d-1)}
relative to (
4.25
); the
l_{n}
-th root of this goes to 1 as
m\to\infty
(recall
l_{n}=dm^{2}/2+O(m)
).

Finally,
\Sigma_{m}=\frac{m^{2}}{2}+O(m)
, so
\frac{l_{n}}{\Sigma_{m}}\to d
. Replacing the
l_{n}
-th root in the formula by the
\Sigma_{m}
-th root, the new limit as
m\to\infty
is the
d
-th power of the original limit. Hence (
4.25
) holds.
∎

For convenience, write
\overline{\underline{T}}:=\overline{\underline{T}}(K,\mathcal{Z}(1))
and
T_{m}:=\tau_{N+dm}
, and rewrite (
4.25
) in the form

\log\overline{T}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\Sigma_{m}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}\nu\log T_{\nu}\right).

(4.29)

Define for
\delta,\theta>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
the statement

\mathbf{P}(m,\delta,\theta)\equiv\Bigl[\log T_{m+s}<\log\overline{T}-\delta\hbox{ for all }s\in[0,\theta m)\cap\mathbb{Z}\Bigr].

We use Proposition
4.15
for the next result.

Lemma 4.16
.

Fix
\delta,\theta>0
. Then
\mathbf{P}(m,\delta,\theta)
is true for at most finitely many
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We prove this by contradiction.

Suppose the conclusion is false, i.e., there are infinitely many values of
m\in\mathbb{N}
where
\mathbf{P}(m,\delta,\theta)
is true. Arrange them into a strictly increasing sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
.

For each
k
, let
s_{k}
be the unique integer for which
\theta m_{k}-1<s_{k}\leq\theta m_{k}
; so

s_{k}=m_{k}+O(1)\hbox{ and }\Sigma_{m_{k}+s_{k}}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\theta)^{2}m_{k}^{2}+O(m_{k}).

We have

\frac{1}{\Sigma_{m_{k}+s_{k}}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{m_{k}+s_{k}}\nu\log T_{\nu}=\frac{1}{\Sigma_{m_{k}+s_{k}}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{m_{k}}\nu\log T_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\Sigma_{m_{k}+s_{k}}}\sum_{\nu=m_{k}+1}^{m_{k}+s_{k}}\nu\log T_{\nu}.

For convenience, write this sum as
S(k)=S_{1}(k)+S_{2}(k)
.

We have
S(k)\to\log\overline{T}
as
k\to\infty
by Proposition
4.15
. Next,

S_{1}(k)=\frac{\Sigma_{m_{k}}}{\Sigma_{m_{k}+s_{k}}}\left(\frac{1}{\Sigma_{m_{k}}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{m_{k}}\nu\log T_{\nu}\right)\longrightarrow\frac{1}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\log\overline{T}\hbox{ as }k\to\infty,

again by Proposition
4.15
. For
S_{2}(k)
, we use
\mathbf{P}(m_{k},\delta,\theta)
to estimate as follows:

\displaystyle S_{2}(k)=\frac{1}{\Sigma_{m_{k}+s_{k}}}\sum_{\nu=m_{k}+1}^{m_{k}+s_{k}}\nu\log T_{\nu}

\displaystyle<(\log\overline{T}-\delta)\left(\frac{1}{\Sigma_{m_{k}+s_{k}}}\sum_{\nu=m_{k}+1}^{m_{k}+s_{k}}\nu\right)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(\log\overline{T}-\delta)\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\right)\hbox{ as }k\to\infty.

Finally, looking at the limit of
S(k)=S_{1}(k)+S_{2}(k)
on both sides as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\log(\overline{T})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\log\overline{T}+(\log\overline{T}-\delta)\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\overline{T}-\delta\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\right)

which is a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
\delta,\theta>0
, and let
\{m_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence in
\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
k\geq k_{0}
,

\log T_{m_{k}+s_{k}}\geq\log\overline{T}-\delta\hbox{ for some }s_{k}\in[0,\theta m_{k}]\cap\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

By the previous lemma,
\mathbf{P}(m_{k},\delta,\theta)
is true for only finitely many
k
, i.e., there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large that
\mathbf{P}(m_{k},\delta,\theta)
is false for all
k\geq k_{0}
. The conclusion is simply the negation of
\mathbf{P}(m_{k},\delta,\theta)
.
∎

Proposition 4.18
.

We have
\underline{T}=\overline{T}
.

Proof.

For the purpose of obtaining a contradiction, suppose
\underline{T}<\overline{T}
.

Let
\{m_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a strictly increasing sequence in
\mathbb{N}
such that
T_{m_{k}}\to\underline{T}
, and fix
\epsilon\in(0,\overline{T}-\underline{T})
. Replacing
\{m_{k}\}
with its tail, we may assume that

\log T_{m_{k}}<\log\overline{T}-\epsilon\hbox{ for all }k.

(4.30)

Let
\delta,\theta>0
. By Corollary
4.17
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
\{s_{k}\}
in
[0,\theta m_{k}]\cap\mathbb{Z}
such that

\log T_{m_{k}+s_{k}}\geq\log\overline{T}-\delta\hbox{ for all }k\geq k_{0}.

(4.31)

Let
t_{m_{k}}(z)=z_{1}^{m_{k}-1}z_{2}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}
be a polynomial such that
\|t_{m_{k}}\|_{K}=T_{m_{k}}^{m_{k}}
. Then
z_{1}^{s_{k}}t_{m_{k}}(z)=z_{1}^{m_{k}+s_{k}-1}z_{2}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}
, so

T_{m_{k}+s_{k}}^{m_{k}+s_{k}}\leq\|z_{1}^{s_{k}}t_{m_{k}}\|_{K}\leq\|z_{1}^{s_{k}}\|_{K}\|t_{m_{k}}\|_{K}\leq C^{s_{k}}T_{m_{k}}^{m_{k}},

choosing some
C>\|z_{1}\|_{K}
. Hence
T_{m_{k}+s_{k}}^{1+s_{k}/m_{k}}\leq C^{s_{k}/m_{k}}T_{m_{k}}
. Taking logs and using (
4.30
), (
4.31
), we obtain

\left(1+\frac{s_{k}}{m_{k}}\right)(\log\overline{T}-\delta)\leq\frac{s_{k}}{m_{k}}\log C+\log\overline{T}-\epsilon,\hbox{ for all }k\geq k_{0}.

Taking the limsup on both sides as
k\to\infty
, we have

(1+\theta_{1})(\log\overline{T}-\delta)\leq\theta_{2}\log C+\log\overline{T}-\epsilon\hbox{ for some }\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,\theta].

(4.32)

Here,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
are either the
\limsup
or
\liminf
of
\frac{s_{k}}{m_{k}}
as
k\to\infty
, depending on whether the signs of
\log\overline{T}-\delta,\log C
are positive or negative.

Finally, observe that
\epsilon
does not depend on
\delta,\theta
. We can let
\delta,\theta\to 0
; then
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\to 0
also, and (
4.32
) reduces to the contradiction
\log\overline{T}\leq\log\overline{T}-\epsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that Theorem
1.5
, that
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k-1))=T(K,\lambda_{k})
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, is the final part of Theorem
4.13
.

5
Extremal-like functions

In this section, we define two families of extremal-like functions associated to a compact, nonpolar subset
K\subset A\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
, denoted
V_{K}^{(k)},k=1,...,d
and
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)},k=0,...,d-1
, and we show that

V_{K}^{*}=\max[V_{K}^{(1)},...,V_{K}^{(d)}]=\max[\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)},...,\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(d-1)}]\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K.

We will utilize the Chebyshev polynomials associated to the family of Chebyshev constants
T(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j}}(\mathbf{v}_{k})),\ j=0,...,d-2
to construct
V_{K}^{(k)}
; and we use the Chebyshev polynomials associated to the Chebyshev constants
T(K,\lambda_{k})
to construct
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}
.

Recall we have the basis
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{C}[A]
given by

\displaystyle 1,z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2},\ldots,z_{2}^{d-2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d},z_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,z_{1}\mathbf{v}_{d},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{d},

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{1}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}^{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{d}^{2},

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{1}^{k},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}^{k},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{1}^{k},\ldots,z_{1}^{d-2}\mathbf{v}_{d}^{k},\ldots

We have shown

T(K,\lambda_{k})=T(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j}}(\mathbf{v}_{k})),\ j=0,...,d-2;\ k=1,...,d

(5.1)

in Proposition
4.8
where

\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j}}(\mathbf{v}_{k})):=\{z_{1}^{j}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Given
k\in\{1,...,d\}
, for each
n>d-2
, by the division algorithm, there exists a unique positive integer
l=l(n)
and
j=j(n)\in\{0,1,...,d-2\}
so that
n=l(d-1)+j
. From (
5.1
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{l}(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))=T(K,\lambda_{k}).

We let

t_{n}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}^{j(n)}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{l(n)}+\text{\sc lot}

be a Chebyshev polynomial for this class; i.e.,
T_{l}(K,\mathcal{M}_{z_{1}^{j}}(\mathbf{v}_{k}))=||t_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}^{1/n}
, and we define, for
(z_{1},z_{2})\in A
,

V_{K}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2}):=[\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|t_{n}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})|}{||t_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}]^{*}\in L(A)

(5.2)

where the uppersemicontinuous regularization is taken over points in
A
. Using the results in the previous sections, we will show these functions have the property that

\rho(V_{K}^{(k)},\lambda_{m})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{m})\ \hbox{if}\ m\not=k;\ \rho(V_{K}^{(k)},\lambda_{k})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k}).

(5.3)

It then follows from Theorem
1.4
that

\max[V_{K}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2}),...,V_{K}^{(d)}(z_{1},z_{2})]=V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2})\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K.

Recall that we ordered the directions
\lambda_{j}
so that

T(K,\lambda_{1})\geq T(K,\lambda_{2})\geq\cdots\geq T(K,\lambda_{d});

and since

\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k})=-\log T(K,\lambda_{k}),\ \hbox{we have}\ \rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2})\leq\cdots\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{d})

(5.4)

(recall Corollary
4.10
).

From Theorem
1.5
, we also have

T(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))=T(K,\lambda_{k+1}),\ k=0,...,d-2.

(5.5)

Here, recall the monomial basis
\mathcal{S}
consists of all monomials of degree
\leq d-2
, and
z_{1}^{n},z_{1}^{n-1}z_{2},\ldots,z_{1}^{n-d+1}z_{2}^{d-1}
in degrees
n\geq d-1
. We use the
grevlex ordering
, where we order degree by degree, and by increasing powers of
z_{2}
within the same degree. For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
, we then defined

\mathcal{Z}(k)=\{p\in\mathbb{C}[A]\colon p(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}^{k}z_{1}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}\}.

Let

\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}^{k}z_{1}^{n}+\text{\sc lot}_{\mathcal{S}}

be a Chebyshev polynomial for this class; i.e.,
T_{n+k}(K,\mathcal{Z}(k))=||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}^{1/n+k}
. We define, for
(z_{1},z_{2})\in A
,

\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2}):=[\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log\frac{|\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})|}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}]^{*}\in L(A).

(5.6)

Note that
\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}
may not be unique
but for any choice we will see that these functions
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}
have the property that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\rho(\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)},\lambda_{j})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j})\ \hbox{if }j\leq k;\\
\rho(\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)},\lambda_{j})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1})\ \hbox{if }j=k+1;\\
\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1})\leq\rho(\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)},\lambda_{j})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j})\ \hbox{if }j\geq k+2\end{array}

It then follows from Theorem
1.4
that

\max[\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)}(z_{1},z_{2}),...,\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(d-1)}(z_{1},z_{2})]=V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2})\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K.

In order to prove (
5.3
) and (
5
), we consider the Robin constants of these extremal-like functions. Recall that for
u\in L(A)
,

\rho_{u}(\lambda_{k})=\limsup_{|z_{1}|\to\infty,\ (z_{1},z_{2})\in A,\ z_{2}/z_{1}\to\lambda_{k}}[u(z_{1},z_{2})-\log|z_{1}|].

If
u=\frac{1}{n}\log|p|
where
p
is a polynomial of degree
n
in
\mathbb{C}[A]
, then

\rho_{u}(\lambda_{k})=\limsup_{|z_{1}|\to\infty,\ (z_{1},z_{2})\in A,\ z_{2}/z_{1}\to\lambda_{k}}[\frac{1}{n}\log|p(z_{1},z_{2})|-\log|z_{1}|]

=\limsup_{|z_{1}|\to\infty,\ (z_{1},z_{2})\in A,\ z_{2}/z_{1}\to\lambda_{k}}[\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}(z_{1},z_{2})|-\log|z_{1}|]

where
\widehat{p}
is the top degree (
n
) homogeneous piece of
p
. Thus

\widehat{p}(z_{1},z_{2})=a_{n}z_{1}^{n}+a_{n-1}z_{1}^{n-1}z_{2}+\cdots+a_{n-(d-2)}z_{1}^{n-(d-2)}z_{2}^{d-2}.

Hence,

\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}(z_{1},z_{2})|-\log|z_{1}|=\log|a_{n}+a_{n-1}(z_{2}/z_{1})+\cdots+a_{n-(d-2)}(z_{2}/z_{1})^{d-2}|

and, as in (
1.2
),

\rho_{u}(\lambda_{k})=\limsup_{|z_{1}|\to\infty,\ z_{2}/z_{1}\to\lambda_{k}}\frac{1}{n}\log|a_{n}+a_{n-1}(z_{2}/z_{1})+\cdots+a_{n-(d-2)}(z_{2}/z_{1})^{d-2}|

=\frac{1}{n}\log|a_{n}+a_{n-1}\lambda_{k}+\cdots+a_{n-(d-2)}\lambda_{k}^{d-2}|=\frac{1}{n}\log|\widehat{p}(1,\lambda_{k})|.

Thus, for

V_{K}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2}):=[\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|t_{n}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})|}{||t_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}]^{*},

and we have that

\rho(V_{K}^{(k)},\lambda_{k})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|\widehat{t}_{n}^{(k)}(1,\lambda_{k})|}{||t_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}

=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|1^{j}\mathbf{v}_{k}(1,\lambda_{k})^{l}|}{||t_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}

=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{||t_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}=-\log T(K,\lambda_{k})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k})

from (
5.4
). On the other hand, since
V_{K}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2})
,
for all
m,k=1,...,d

\rho_{V_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{m})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{m}).

This verifies (
5.3
).

Concerning (
5
) and the functions
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}
, we first observe that
since we know for each
k=0,...,d-1
,

\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2}),

for all
j=1,...,d
, and all
k=0,...,d-1
,

\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{j})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j}).

Next, we have

\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}^{k}z_{1}^{n}+a_{n,k-1}z_{2}^{k-1}z_{1}^{n+1}+\cdots+a_{n,0}z_{1}^{n+k}.

(5.11)

In particular,

\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(0)}}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}^{n}
so that

\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(0)}}(1,\lambda_{j})=1
.
Then

\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)}}(\lambda_{j})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(0)}}(1,\lambda_{j})|}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(0)}||_{K}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(0)}||_{K}}

=-\log T(K,\mathcal{Z}(0))=-\log T(K,\lambda_{1})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})

from (
5.5
) and (
5.4
) for all
j=1,...,d
. Thus

\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)}}(\lambda_{1})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})\ \hbox{and}\ \rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})\leq\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)}}(\lambda_{j})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j}),\ j=2,...,d.

Now, for
k=1
,

\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(1)}}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}z_{1}^{n}+a_{n}z_{1}^{n+1}=z_{1}^{n}(z_{2}+a_{n}z_{1})

so that for
j=1,...,d
,

\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(1)}}(1,\lambda_{j})=\lambda_{j}+a_{n}.

Thus

\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(1)}}(\lambda_{j})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\log\frac{|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(1)}}(1,\lambda_{j})|}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(1)}||_{K}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\log\frac{|\lambda_{j}+a_{n}|}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(1)}||_{K}}.

For
j=1,...,d
, we recall that since
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(1)}\leq V_{K}^{*}
we must have

\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(1)}}(\lambda_{j})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j}).

In particular,
\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(1)}}(\lambda_{1})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})
. Since
T(K,\mathcal{Z}(1))=T(K,\lambda_{2})
from (
5.5
),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\log\frac{1}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(1)}||_{K}}=-\log T(K,\lambda_{2})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2}).

Hence we must have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\log|\lambda_{1}+a_{n}|\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})-\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2})\leq 0.

Thus if
\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})<\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2})
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=-\lambda_{1}
. Hence for all
j\not=1
, since the
\lambda_{j}
are distinct,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\log|\lambda_{j}+a_{n}|=0

and we have

\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(1)}}(\lambda_{2})=\cdots=\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{d})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2}).

In general, for
k\in\{2,...,d-1\}
, from (
5.11
),

\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda_{j})=\lambda_{j}^{k}+a_{n,k-1}\lambda_{j}^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{n,0}.

Then

\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{j})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log\frac{|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda_{j})|}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log\frac{|\lambda_{j}^{k}+a_{n,k-1}\lambda_{j}^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{n,0}|}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}.

By similar reasoning, under the assumption that
\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k})<\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1})
,
using the fact that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log\frac{1}{||\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}||_{K}}=-\log T(K,\lambda_{k+1})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1}),

we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda_{j})|\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j})-\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1})<0

for
j=1,...,k
(recall (
5.4
)). Writing

\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda)=\lambda^{k}+a_{n,k-1}\lambda^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{n,0}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-r_{j,n}),

we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda_{j})|=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log|\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda_{j}-r_{j,n})|<0

for
j=1,...,k
. Thus, after possibly reordering the
k
roots
r_{1,n},...,r_{k,n}
, we first choose a subsequence
\{n(1)\}
of
\mathbb{N}
so that
\{r_{1,n(1)}\}
converges to
\lambda_{1}
; then we take a subsequence
\{n(2)\}
of
\{n(1)\}
so that
\{r_{2,n(2)}\}
converges to
\lambda_{2}
; etc.; finally we take a subsequence
\{n(k)\}
of
\{n(k-1)\}
so that
\{r_{k,n(k)}\}
converges to
\lambda_{k}
. For this subsequence
\{n(k)\}
, we have
\{r_{j,n(k)}\}
converges to
\lambda_{j}
for
j=1,...,k
. In particular, for
j=k+1,...,d
we have

\limsup_{n(k)\to\infty}\frac{1}{n(k)+1}\log|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n(k)}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda_{j})|=0.

We conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda_{j})|\geq 0

for
j=k+1,...,d
. Since
\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{k+1})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1})
, it follows that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+k}\log|\widehat{\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(k)}}(1,\lambda_{k+1})|=0

so that, in fact,
\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{k+1})=\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1})
. For
j=k+2,...,d
, we conclude that

\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k+1})=\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{k+1})\leq\rho_{\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{j})\leq\rho_{K}(\lambda_{j}).

This verifies (
5
).

We have proved the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
K\subset A\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
nonpolar, we have

\max[V_{K}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2}),...,V_{K}^{(d)}(z_{1},z_{2})]=V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2})\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K

where
V_{K}^{(k)}
is defined in (
5.2
). Furthermore, if

\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1})<\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2})<\cdots<\rho_{K}(\lambda_{d}),

we have

\max[\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)}(z_{1},z_{2}),...,\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(d-1)}(z_{1},z_{2})]=V_{K}^{*}(z_{1},z_{2})\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K

where
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}
is defined in (
5.6
).

We next verify certain properties of our extremal-like functions
V_{K}^{(k)},\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k)}
. These are in
L(A)
, in particular, they are subharmonic on
A
. We assume in the following lemmas that
\epsilon>0
is given and
R=R(\epsilon)
is chosen so that the conditions of Proposition
1.1
are satisfied. Thus we have pairwise disjoint domains
D_{j},\ j=1,...,d
in
A
with
A\setminus\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:|z|<R\}=D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{d}
;
D_{j}
is
\epsilon
close to the linear asymptote
L_{j}
of
A
; and the projection
\pi_{j}:D_{j}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\pi_{j}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}
is one-to-one.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
k=1,...,d
, we have

\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k-1)}(z_{1},z_{2})=V_{K}^{(k)}(z_{1},z_{2})=V_{K}(z_{1},z_{2})\hbox{ for all }z\in D_{k}.

(5.12)

Proof.

We prove the equality
V_{K}^{(k)}=V_{K}
on
D_{k}
; the proof that
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k-1)}=V_{K}
on
D_{k}
is identical.
Let
w=(w_{0},w_{2})
be local coordinates on
D_{k}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
z:=z(w)
given by the correspondence
[1:z_{1}:z_{2}]=[w_{0}:1:w_{2}]
. Define

W(w):=V_{K}^{(k)}(z(w))-V_{K}(z(w))
if
w\neq(0,\lambda_{k})
. Then
W
is subharmonic and
W\leq 0
on
D_{k}
. Moreover,
W
extends across
(0,\lambda_{k})
as a subharmonic function with

W(0,\lambda_{k})=\rho_{V_{K}^{(k)}}(\lambda_{k})-\rho_{K}(\lambda_{k})=0
. The point
(0,\lambda_{k})
is an interior point of the extended domain, so by the maximum principle
W\equiv 0
in
D_{k}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a connected component of
A\setminus K
and suppose
\Omega\supset(D_{j}\cup D_{k})
. Then
V_{K}^{(j)}=V_{K}^{(k)}=\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(j-1)}=\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k-1)}=V_{K}
on
\Omega
.

Proof.

We show
V_{K}^{(j)}=V_{K}^{(k)}=V_{K}
on
\Omega
; the proof that
\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(j-1)}=\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(k-1)}=V_{K}
on
\Omega
is identical.
Since
\Omega
is open it is path connected. Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\Omega
be a continuous path with
\gamma(0)\in D_{j}
and
\gamma(1)\in D_{k}
and let

T:=\sup\{t\in[0,1]\colon V_{K}(\gamma(s))=V_{K}^{(j)}(\gamma(s))\hbox{ for all }s\in[0,t]\}.

We want to show that
T=1
.

By the previous lemma,
T\geq 0
. Suppose
T<1
. Let
U
be a neighborhood of
\gamma(T)
. Then there exists
s\leq T
such that
\gamma(s)\in U
and
V_{K}(\gamma(s))=V_{K}^{(j)}(\gamma(s))
. Using the same argument as in the previous lemma with the point
(0,\lambda_{k})
replaced by the point
\gamma(s)\in U
, we conclude that
V_{K}^{(j)}\equiv V_{K}
in
U
. In particular,
V_{K}(\gamma(T+\delta))=V_{K}^{(j)}(\gamma(T+\delta))
for all sufficiently small
\delta>0
. This contradicts the definition of
T
.

Thus
T=1
, hence
V_{K}^{(j)}=V_{K}
on
\gamma([0,1])
. Similarly,
V_{K}^{(k)}=V_{K}
on
\gamma([0,1])
. Since
\gamma
was an arbitrary path connecting a point in
D_{j}
with one in
D_{k}
, the result follows.
∎

We conclude with some examples.

Example 5.4
.

Let
A=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\colon z_{1}^{2}-z_{2}^{2}=1\}
and take
K:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in A\colon z_{2}\in[-1,1]\}
. Then
V_{K}(z_{1},z_{2})=\log|h(z_{2})|
where
h(\zeta):=\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^{2}-1}
is the inverse Joukowski map, for
v(z_{1},z_{2}):=\log|h(z_{2})||_{A}
is in
L^{+}(A)
;
v=0
on
K
; and
dd^{c}v=0
on
A\setminus K
. Since
A\setminus K
is connected, from Lemma
5.3
we must have

V_{K}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2})=V_{K}^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(0)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\widetilde{V}_{K}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\log|h(z_{2})|

on
A\setminus K
.

Example 5.5
.

We again let
A=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:z_{1}^{2}-z_{2}^{2}=1\}
. The associated homogeneous variety
A_{h}=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:z_{1}^{2}-z_{2}^{2}=(z_{1}-z_{2})(z_{1}+z_{2})=0\}
is the union of two complex lines. Then
\mathbf{v}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(z_{1}-z_{2})
and
\mathbf{v}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(z_{1}+z_{2})
. Take

K:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in A:|\mathbf{v}_{1}|=|\mathbf{v}_{2}|=1/2\}
. Since the basis
\mathcal{C}
for
\mathbb{C}[A]
is

1,\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{1}^{2},\mathbf{v}_{2}^{2},...,

it is easy to see that
t_{n}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{v}_{1}^{n}
and
V_{K}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\log|z_{1}-z_{2}|
while
t_{n}^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{v}_{2}^{n}
and
V_{K}^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\log|z_{1}+z_{2}|
. Here, the Robin constants
\rho_{K}(\lambda_{1}),\rho_{K}(\lambda_{2})
are equal and since Theorem
5.1
gives

V_{K}(z_{1},z_{2})=\max[V_{K}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2}),V_{K}^{(2)}(z_{1},z_{2})]\ \hbox{on}\ A^{0}\setminus K,

we have

V_{K}(z_{1},z_{2})=\max[\log^{+}|z_{1}-z_{2}|,\log^{+}|z_{1}+z_{2}|]\ \hbox{on}\ A.

(5.13)

Note that setting
u:=z_{1}-z_{2}
and
v:=z_{1}+z_{2}
, we have
A=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:uv=1\}
and
K=\{(u,v)=(e^{it},e^{-it})\in A:t\in[0,2\pi]\}
.
It was shown in
[
4
]
that

V_{K}(u,v)=\max[\log^{+}|u|,\log^{+}|v|]\ \hbox{for}\ (u,v)\in A.

(5.14)

Setting
u:=z_{1}-z_{2}
and
v:=z_{1}+z_{2}
in (
5.13
) recovers (
5.14
).

Example 5.6
.

For
\epsilon>0
, let
A_{\epsilon}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:z_{1}z_{2}=\epsilon\}
and
K_{\epsilon}:=A_{\epsilon}\cap B
where
B=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z_{1}|\leq 1,\ |z_{2}|\leq 1\}
is the unit bidisk. We claim that

V_{K_{\epsilon}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\max[\log^{+}|z_{1}|,\log^{+}|z_{2}|]\ \hbox{on}\ A_{\epsilon}.

To see this, simply note that
v(z_{1},z_{2}):=\max[\log^{+}|z_{1}|,\log^{+}|z_{2}|]|_{A_{\epsilon}}\in L^{+}(A_{\epsilon})
;
v=0
on
K_{\epsilon}
; and
dd^{c}v=0
on
A_{\epsilon}\setminus K_{\epsilon}
.

The coordinate axes
z_{1}=0
and
z_{2}=0
are linear asymptotes for
A_{\epsilon}
. Thus this example does not satisfy the italicized conditions in the introduction. However, we can take the basis

1,z_{1},z_{2},z_{1}^{2},z_{2}^{2},...,z_{1}^{n},z_{2}^{n},...

given by
\mathcal{S}
for
\mathbb{C}[A_{\epsilon}]
to compute the Chebyshev polynomials
\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(j)}
for
j=0,1
. Since the map
t\to(t,\epsilon/t)
from the annulus
\{t\in\mathbb{C}:\epsilon\leq|t|\leq 1\}
onto
K_{\epsilon}
is holomorphic, one can check that we have
\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(0)}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}^{n}
and
\widetilde{t}_{n}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}^{n}
so that the extremal-like functions in (
5.6
) are given by
\widetilde{V}_{K_{\epsilon}}^{(0)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\log|z_{1}|
and
\widetilde{V}_{K_{\epsilon}}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2})=\log|z_{2}|
. Hence we do have the equality

V_{K_{\epsilon}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\max[\widetilde{V}_{K_{\epsilon}}^{(0)}(z_{1},z_{2}),\widetilde{V}_{K_{\epsilon}}^{(1)}(z_{1},z_{2})]\ \hbox{on}\ A_{\epsilon}\setminus K.

Moreover, clearly we can still define and compute Robin constants associated to these directions (which we continue to denote as
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
); in this case we have

\rho_{K_{\epsilon}}(\lambda_{1})=\rho_{K_{\epsilon}}(\lambda_{2})=0
.

Note that this function
\max[\log^{+}|z_{1}|,\log^{+}|z_{2}|]
equals
V_{K}(z_{1},z_{2})
on
A
where
A:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:z_{1}z_{2}=0\}
and
K:=A\cap B
is the union of the unit disks in the
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
planes. Here
A
is reducible. To get an example where the Robin constants are different, we replace
B
by a closed bidisk

B_{r}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2}):|z_{1}|\leq r_{1},\ |z_{2}|\leq r_{2}\}

where
r_{1}\not=r_{2}
. Now
K^{r}:=A\cap B_{r}
is the union of the disks
\{(z_{1},0):|z_{1}|\leq r_{1}\}
and
\{(0,z_{2}):|z_{2}|\leq r_{2}\}
so that

V_{K^{r}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\max[\log^{+}|z_{1}|/r_{1},\log^{+}|z_{2}|/r_{2}],\ (z_{1},z_{2})\in A

and
\rho_{K^{r}}(\lambda_{1})=-\log r_{1}
while
\rho_{K^{r}}(\lambda_{2})=-\log r_{2}
. This result on the directional Robin constants also follows from Proposition 4.7 in
[
8
]
.

Prevalence of relaparotomy and associated factors among patients undergone laparotomy in selected public hospitals of East Wollega under Oromia region of Ethiopia. A cross-sectional study
Authors: Ashenafi Tesfaye, Mathewos Mokonnen, Getu Mosisa, Sena Gemosa Ayana, Aliyi Benti, Dawit Tesfaye Rundasa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Relaparotomy refers to reoperations performed within 60 days of an initial laparotomy for complications like anastomotic leak and intra-abdominal abscess collection. The risks of morbidity and mortality are high after relaparotomy. The global prevalence of relaparotomy accounts for 1.5–27% of patients with abdominal surgery. To assess the prevalence of relaparotomy and associated factors with relaparotomy among patients who underwent laparotomy from January 1, 2020, to December 30, 2022, in public hospitals in East Wollega zone, Ethiopia. A retrospective institution-based cross-sectional study was conducted in public hospitals in East Wollega Zones from January 1, 2020, to December 30, 2022. A total of 834 charts were selected using a simple random sampling technique. A pretested and structured checklist was used to collect data. Data was entered using Epi Data version 4.6, then exported to and analyzed by Statistical Package for Social Sciences version 26. Descriptive and inferential analyses were conducted. A statistically significant association was indicated at a 95% Confidence Interval with a p-value of less than 0.05. The prevalence of relaparotomy was 14.6% with a 95% CI (12.4–17.2). The mean age was 39.69 SD ± 14.45 years. Among the relaparotomy patients, 70.9% were males. In this study, age above 60 years (AOR = 7.6, 95% CI = 1.85–31.33), preoperative duration of illness greater than 120 h (AOR = 3.4, 95% CI = 1.70–6.58), preoperative pulse rate more than 120 beats per minute (AOR = 6.5, 95% CI = 3.15–13.45), patients with preoperative peritonitis (AOR = 4.8, 95% CI = 2.60–8.95), a patient with preoperative anastomotic leak (AOR = 3.4, 95% CI = 1.70–6.58), and a patient with preoperative intraabdominal abscess (AOR = 4., 95% CI = 2.106–7.65) were associated with relaparotomy. In this study, relaparotomy occurred in nearly one out of seven patients who had undergone laparotomy. Factors such as age above 60 years, duration of illness exceeding 120 h, pre-operative pulse rate greater than 120 beats per minute, peritonitis, intra-abdominal abscess, and anastomosis leak were found to be associated with relaparotomy. Therefore, all possible medical measures should be taken during the primary laparotomy to reduce the occurrence of relaparotomy.

Introduction

Laparotomy is a surgical procedure involving an incision into the abdominal cavity to examine the abdominal organs and provide surgical treatment. The term ‘relaparotomy’ refers to a surgical procedure in which the abdominal cavity is re-explored within 60 days of the initial surgery to address any complications arising from the disease or the initial surgery1,2.

The need for exploratory laparotomy has decreased in the laparoscopic era due to the increased availability of diagnostic laparoscopy, a minimally invasive method for examining or staging the abdomen3.

Globally, relaparotomy is a surgical-related health problem, particularly in the developing world4–6. According to a study done in the Netherlands and Germany, the prevalence of relaparotomy ranges from 10 to 37%7. In Africa, a previous study from Tanzania indicates that the prevalence of relaparotomy was 7.6%8. According to a study conducted in Debre Markos, Ethiopia, the prevalence of relaparotomy was 12.3%9.

Relaparotomy falls under the category of significant adverse events10. It is also associated with increased morbidity and mortality5. Reoperation rates in Switzerland are used to measure the standard of treatment. They are unfavorable for the patient, occasionally dangerous, and excessively costly, leading to long hospital stays11. In 30% of postoperative patients, morbidity necessitated relaparotomy, with mortality rates following reoperation at 40% in Morocco, 27.3% in Congo, and 15.4% in Butembo10,12. The indications of relaparotomy are common surgical emergencies and have been reported as a major contributor to non-trauma deaths in emergency departments worldwide13.

There are many factors for the occurrence of relaparotomy14. Accordingly, the elderly age group of patients, the incision technique, the competence of the surgeon, patient co-morbidities, delay in assessment of the time interval between the development of complications and relaparotomy, and unjustifiable time delay in reaching the correct diagnosis5. Factors observed relating to reoperation were the presence of malignancy, mesenteric ischemia, development of organ failure, infection, bleeding, necrotizing pancreatitis, bowel necrosis, bowel obstruction, and evisceration, and the presence of anastomosis9,15.

The World Health Assembly in 2015 made timely and safe surgical care a part of universal health coverage16. Safe Surgery 2020 has done different interventions in three countries, including Ethiopia, to increase access to quality and affordable surgical care17. However, almost five billion people, or two-thirds of the world’s population, lack access to inexpensive surgical and anesthetic care, particularly in resource-constrained areas of the world18. Among people living in Southeast Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa, 97% and 93% cannot get even the most basic and safe surgical care respectively16. Therefore, different complications related to a lack of timely and safe surgical care, like relaparotomy, still exist7.

This study will offer evidence for health professionals and hospital administrators to inform their interventions. The findings of this study will serve as baseline data for future researchers interested in this area. Additionally, it will assist planners and program managers in appropriately preparing for skilled manpower, medical supplies, and preventive strategies.

Despite relaparotomy having a great role in increasing morbidity and mortality among patients who have undergone laparotomy, the status of relaparotomy in our country is not well studied. Surprisingly, fewer studies were conducted, all of which were centered on a single study area, making it difficult to generalize the findings to all laparotomy patients1,9. Therefore, this study will address the gap by assessing the prevalence of relaparotomy and associated factors among patients who have undergone laparotomy in public hospitals in East Wollega zones, Ethiopia.

Methods and materials

Study area and period

This study was conducted from January 1, 2020, to December 30, 2022, in East Wollega zone public hospitals in West Oromia, Ethiopia. The East Wollega zone has a total of six government hospitals with different levels, including two primary hospitals, one general hospital, one referral hospital, one comprehensive specialized referral hospital, and a University Referral Hospital. The study was conducted in three selected public hospitals in the East Wollega zone: Nekemte Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Wollega University Referral Hospital, and Arjo Hospital. These hospitals provide services to a population of more than 1,660,124, and they have 39 surgeons, 13 Integrated Emergency Surgery and Obstetrics staff, and 514 nurses, with 16 operating room beds.

Study design

A retrospective cross-sectional study design was employed.

Sampling technique

A simple random sampling technique was employed. A retrospective laparotomy patient card from NCSH, WURH, and Arjo hospitals was used to allocate the study sample proportionally. All medical record numbers for laparotomy patient cards from January 1, 2020, to December 30, 2022, G.C., were retrieved using the operating theater logbook.

Operational definition and term

Relaparotomy

It is a re-operation of the abdominal surgery site associated with the first surgery within 60 days19.

Data collection instrument

A pretested and structured checklist was used to gather data from the patient card. This checklist was developed in English. The tool comprises information on socio-demographic factors, including age, sex, address, educational status, and marital status. To determine the prevalence of relaparotomy, we evaluated the charts using 25 surgical intervention-based questions, 11 questions related to comorbidities, and 8 questions related to the indication for relaparotomy. First, the number of Laparotomies of three years (2020–2022) was tallied and counted properly. Then, the list of patients who had a laparotomy was prepared using their MRN. Finally, a computer-generated random number was used to select 834 samples. The instrument was validated in a previous study with an overall Cronbach’s alpha of (r) = 0.819.

The data collection tool was adapted by reviewing different literature and previous similar studies, which are organized according to the objectives of the study1,20–22. The tool comprises information on socio-demographic factors, surgical-related factors, and co-morbidity.

Data quality control

One-day training was provided to data collectors and supervisors before data collection. The training focuses on the purposes of the study, the meaning of each question, how to collect the data, the confidentiality of information, and the role and responsibility of data collectors and supervisors. The collected data was reviewed, checked for completeness, and signed by the data collector and supervisor during the data collection period. The principal investigator supervised the data collectors and supervisors, and samples of the checklist were randomly re-checked.

A pre-test was done among 5% of the sample size at Sire Hospital two days before data collection, and modifications were made based on pre-test results. The supervisors continuously followed up and supervised the data collectors. Completeness of the required data was checked daily and verified by the principal investigator and supervisors. Data was checked for completeness and consistency before data entry by the principal investigator; for data cleaning, the coded data was entered into EpiData version 4.6, and data was exported to SPSS Windows version 26 for analysis.

Data analysis

The completed questionnaires were coded using a pre-arranged coding sheet by the principal investigator to minimize errors. After checking for completeness and consistency, the data were entered into a computer using EpiData version 4.6 and then exported to SPSS Windows version 26 for analysis. Descriptive analysis, such as proportions, percentages, frequency distribution, and measures of central tendency, was performed. Frequencies and cross-tabulations were used to summarize the descriptive statistics of the data, and tables and graphs were utilized for data presentation.

A multi-collinearity was checked before running multivariable regression using a VIF (VIF = 1.86) to quantify the extent to which it affects the estimation of coefficients. The study used bivariable logistic regression analysis to identify potential variables for multivariable logistic regression. Variables with a p-value of < 0.25 in the bivariable analysis were included in the multivariable model to identify relevant and potentially significant factors and reduce the risk of Type I and Type II errors. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, along with adjusted odds ratios (AOR) and 95% confidence intervals. The model’s goodness of fit was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, and it was found to be well-fitted to the data with a p-value of 0.45, indicating the logistic regression model has a good fit and suggests that the model is reliable for making predictions based on the data used in the analysis. A high p-value (commonly > 0.05) indicates that the model fits well, while a low p-value suggests a poor fit.

Ethical consideration

Before beginning the data collection process, ethical clearance for the study was granted by the institutional ethical review board of Wollega University, Institute of Health Sciences, School of Nursing and Midwifery, Department of Nursing, with a letter Ref. no WU/RD/670. This clearance was then submitted to the administrators of each hospital. Permission to conduct the research was acquired by the hospital administrators, and a copy of the ethical clearance was submitted to the operating room and the Surgical wards. Informed consent was waived by the institutional ethical review board of Wollega University, Institute of Health Sciences, due to the retrospective nature of the study. Therefore, no patient consent was required because the study was a chart review, and the data were anonymized. To maintain the confidentiality of the client’s data, the patient’s name or registration number was not documented. Instead, a code was assigned to each card. Furthermore, each patient’s card was securely stored so that only the research team could access it. All procedures were conducted according to the principles of the Helsinki Declaration.

Factors associated with relaparotomy

Bivariate analysis

In bivariable analysis, 12 variables, including age, peritonitis, anastomosis leak, intra-abdominal abscess, preoperative ileus, duration of illness, wound dehiscence, diabetes mellitus, hepatic malignancy, > 3 months of illness, jaundice, preoperative pulse rate, and type of wound, were associated with relaparotomy. To identify potentially significant factors and reduce the risk of Type I and Type II errors, factors with a p-value ≤ 0.25 were identified as candidates for multivariable logistic analysis.

Multivariate analysis

In multivariable logistic regression, seven variables, including age, peritonitis, anastomosis leak, intra-abdominal abscess, duration of illness, pre-operative pulse rate, and classes of wounds, were found to be statistically associated with relaparotomy with a p-value of < 0.05.

Patients aged above 60 years were 7.6 times more likely to get relaparotomy when compared with patients below 15 years (AOR = 7.6, 95% CI = (1.85-31)). Patients who had a duration of illness of more than 120 h were 3.4 times more likely to be exposed to relaparotomy when compared with a duration of illness less than 12 h (AOR = 3.4, 95% CI (1.70–6.58)).

Patients who had a preoperative pulse rate of more than 120 beats per minute were 6.5 times more likely to encounter relaparotomy compared with a preoperative pulse rate below 120 beats per minute (AOR = 6.5, 95% CI (3.15–13.45)). Patients with a contaminated wound were 9.6 times more likely to encounter relaparotomy when compared with patients with a clean wound (AOR = 9.6, 95% CI = (3.378–27.01), and dirty wounds were 13.1 times more likely to encounter relaparotomy when compared with clean wounds (AOR = 13.1, 95% CI = (4.56–37.2)). Patients who had an anastomosis leak were 3.4 times more likely to be exposed to a relaparotomy compared to patients who didn’t develop an anastomosis leak (AOR = 3.4, 95% CI (1.70–6.58)).

Patients who had intra-abdominal abscesses were 4 times more likely to encounter relaparotomy when compared with patients who did not have intra-abdominal abscesses (AOR = 4, 95% CI (2.106–7.65)). Patients who developed peritonitis were 4.8 times more likely to have relaparotomy compared with patients who didn’t develop peritonitis (AOR = 4.8, 95% CI (2.60–8.95)). Discussion

The present study assessed the prevalence of relaparotomy and associated factors among patients who underwent laparotomy in public hospitals in East Wollega zones, West Ethiopia. The findings of this study showed that the prevalence of relaparotomy was 14.6% (95% CI: (12.4–17.2)). This result is similar to other studies conducted in Debre Markos, Addis Ababa, and the United States of America, with a prevalence of 12.3%, 17.2%, and 15% respectively20,21,23.

The result of this study is slightly higher than the findings of the research study conducted in Debre Tabor, 9.1%1, and Addis Ababa 6.9%15. The potential reason for this discrepancy could be attributed to the study conducted in Debre Tabor, which took place at a single center over two years. In contrast, the current study is a multicenter study that spans three years. Additionally, there is a demographic difference in that the study in Addis Ababa focused solely on the pediatric age group, while the current study encompasses all age groups.

The finding of this study is lower than a study conducted in the Democratic Republic of Congo, which reported 18.4%24. The discrepancy may be attributed to the fact that a study conducted in the Democratic Republic of Congo focused solely on emergency abdominal surgeries. In contrast, our study included both emergency and elective surgeries. Additionally, there was a difference in the sampling methods used, in which their study employed a convenience sampling approach. Furthermore, one study showed that patients undergoing elective surgery have more time for standardized preoperative care by a multidisciplinary team, while emergency surgery has limited opportunities for this care. This will increase the likelihood of relaparotomy25.

In this study, ages above 60 years were significantly associated with relaparotomy. This is consistent with other studies done in Debre Markos, Debre Tabor, and Addis Ababa1,15,21. This is supported by scientific facts in which physiological changes related to older ages, which could cause low immunity, delay in wound healing, and comorbidity26. Patients presented with a preoperative pulse rate of more than 120 beats per minute were significantly associated with relaparotomy. This study is supported by a study conducted in Debre Tabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, in which patients with higher preoperative pulse rates are more likely to experience an imbalance between oxygen supply and demand during the perioperative period, which leads to the increased likelihood of relaparotomy27,28.

The current study revealed that preoperative duration of illness > 120 h was significantly associated with relaparotomy. The study was in line with another study in Kenya29. Patients presented with a long duration of illness will have complicated pathology with an increased probability of the occurrence of relaparotomy30. Patients with peritonitis were significantly associated with relaparotomy. The finding is also supported by a study conducted in India31. This is supported by the scientific facts that it is an inflammation of the abdominal lining, often due to infection, and its existence may impair immune function during the postoperative period, which affects the capacity of a patient to defend against the disease, with an increased risk of complications, including relaparotomy32,33.

Patients who had contaminated wounds were 9.6 times more likely to encounter relaparotomy when compared with patients with clean wounds. Also, patients who had dirty wounds were 13.1 times more likely to encounter relaparotomy when compared with patients with clean wounds. Similar to our findings, a study conducted in America revealed that laparotomy performed for contaminated and dirty wounds was an independent predictor of surgical site infection34. Patients who had contaminated or dirty wounds during surgery had severe surgical site infections, which required relaparotomy35,36. The likelihood of a surgical site infection that results in many clinical complications, including wound dehiscence after surgery, depends on the classes of wounds. Surgical site infections (SSIs) can occur in any surgical patient. These infections can range from basic abscess formation to a more intricate infection that may result in life-threatening conditions, including relaparotomy and negative perioperative outcomes37.

In this study, patients with anastomosis leaks were clinical factors of relaparotomy. This finding is also supported by studies conducted in India22,38 and the United States20. The septic consequences associated with anastomotic leaks lead to inflammation and the spread of contamination. These issues often result in significant peritoneal irritation, which requires further surgical intervention to manage the complications and prevent progression39,40.

Patients who had intra-abdominal abscesses had clinical factors of relaparotomy. This study is supported by a study conducted at the Addis Ababa University41 and the Debre Markos Compressive Specialized Hospital42. This is justified by a persistent intra-abdominal abscess after abdominal surgery is a dangerous and challenging major complication that considerably increases the likelihood of relaparotomy43. Bacteria present in the abdominal cavity can cause an intra-abdominal abscess. If left untreated, the bacteria will multiply, leading to inflammation, the death of healthy tissue, and abscess formation, and these all favor relaparotomy44.

Strengths and limitations of the study

Strength

This is the first multicentre study that assessed the prevalence of relaparotomy in the hosting study hospitals. It could provide insight into the significance of existing problems and the need for perioperative patient care bundles and could be used as a baseline for future researchers.

Limitation

Due to the retrospective nature of this study and the fact that the data came from patient cards, pertinent information about personal characteristics like smoking and alcohol use was either missing or unavailable.

Conclusion and recommendation

In this study, relaparotomy occurred in nearly one out of seven patients who had undergone laparotomy. Factors such as age above 60 years, duration of illness exceeding 120 h, pre-operative pulse rate greater than 120 beats per minute, peritonitis, intra-abdominal abscess, and anastomosis leak were found to be associated with relaparotomy. Therefore, all possible medical measures should be taken during the primary laparotomy to reduce the occurrence of relaparotomy.

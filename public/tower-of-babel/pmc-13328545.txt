Fair and scalable energy-efficient rate splitting multiple access in cognitive high altitude platform networks
Authors: Asma A Alhashmi, Ahmed Badi Alshammari, Monir Abdullah, Abed Saif Ahmed Alghawli, Tareq M Alkhaldi, Shouki A Ebad, Khalid N R Alharbi, Abdulbasit A Darem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe evolution toward the industry 5.0, with the applications such as the digital twins and the collaborative robotics, demands the wireless networks that jointly guarantee the ultra reliable connectivity, the fairness aware service, and the energy sustainability. The cognitive radio (CR) enabled high altitude platforms (HAPs) offer the wide area coverage and the flexible spectrum access; and their deployment is constrained by the stringent interference limits toward the terrestrial primary users (PUs), the limited onboard power, and the need for the uniform service among the secondary users (SUs). This paper proposes an energy efficient resource allocation framework for the rate splitting multiple access (RSMA) enabled cognitive HAP networks that addresses these challenges. We formulate a non convex energy efficiency (EE) maximization problem that explicitly couples the RSMA’s common and private rate split with the beamforming design under the PU interference thresholds, the SU QoS requirements, and the fairness gap constraints. To solve this problem, we develop the two complementary algorithms: (i) the Dinkelbach SCA Joint Beamforming and Rate Allocation (D SCA JBRA), a high performance iterative scheme based on the fractional programming and the successive convex approximation; and (ii) the MRT NBS, a low complexity heuristic that integrates the maximum ratio transmission with the Nash bargaining based rate splitting to yield the closed form and the real time solutions. The extensive simulations against a comprehensive benchmark suite (including the OMA MRT, the RSMA EPA, the RSMA RBF, the NOMA FPA, and the RSMA WMMSE) show that the D SCA JBRA achieves up to 87 and 105% higher EE than the OMA MRT and the RSMA RBF, respectively, while maintaining the superior fairness. Meanwhile, the MRT NBS delivers the near optimal performance with over 90% lower computational complexity; and this validates its suitability for the real time HAP deployment. The proposed framework provides a scalable and sustainable solution for the interference resilient and the energy aware connectivity demands of the Industry 5.0 such as smart mining.Keywords: Industry 5.0, Smart mining, High altitude platforms, Cognitive radio, Rate splitting multiple access, Energy efficiency, Resource allocationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction and recent advancements

The transition toward the Industry 5.0 is driving a paradigm shift in the wireless connectivity, demanding the networks that support the digital twins, the collaborative robotics, and the intelligent automation1. Recent studies have highlighted these demands from the various angles; for instance, the Hazra et al.2 emphasized the role of the Sixth-Generation (6G) in enabling the intelligent automation, while the Li et al.3 explored the cell free architectures to support the tightly coordinated interactions among the distributed devices. Unlike the previous generations, such applications are not satisfied by the high data rates alone; and they require the fairness aware connectivity (strictly bounding the rate gap between the users) to ensure the synchronized operation and the energy sustainable architectures to support the prolonged network lifetimes. These requirements become even more stringent in the mission critical and the remote environments4, where the traditional cellular architectures, such as those evaluated by the Mohammed et al.5, suffer from the fixed topologies and the limited resilience. Furthermore, while the user centric architectures6 and the ultra dense networks7 attempt to address the terrestrial capacity, their densification significantly increases the deployment costs and exacerbates the interference8; and this makes it difficult to guarantee the uniform service quality across the heterogeneous environments9.

These terrestrial limitations motivate the shift toward the non terrestrial platforms capable of the flexible and interference aware spectrum access. The High-Altitude Platforms (HAPs), operating at the altitudes of 17 and 25 km, have emerged as a promising solution due to their quasi stationary positioning, the wide area coverage, and the strong Line-of-Sight (LoS) connectivity. By hosting the Cognitive Radio (CR) secondary networks, the HAPs can opportunistically access the underutilized terrestrial spectrum and offload the traffic from the congested ground networks; and this is a concept effectively demonstrated by the Hussien et al.10 for the intelligent aerial power allocation. Their elevated vantage point enables the stable propagation conditions and the predictable channel characteristics; and this makes them attractive for the Industry 5.0 applications requiring the global coordination and the resilience. However, integrating the CR functionalities into the HAPs introduces the non trivial challenges, including the stringent Primary Users (PUs) protection, the dynamic interference management, and the need for the real time computational tractability under the strict onboard processing constraints.

Unlocking the full potential of the cognitive HAP networks requires addressing the three fundamental design constraints that are uniquely challenging in this environment. The first is the strict PU protection: as the Liu et al.11 noted, the strong LoS propagation inherent to the aerial platforms exacerbates the terrestrial interference12 compared to the ground based Cognitive Radio Networks (CRNs). Even the small deviations in the beamforming can cause the harmful interference over the large geographical areas; and this necessitates the robust precoding strategies that comply with the strict regulatory thresholds13, adapting the advanced techniques similar to those proposed for the wideband Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS) systems14. Second, the intra group fairness becomes essential for the Industry 5.0 applications involving the cooperative devices such as the drone swarms15, the industrial robots, or the distributed sensors that must operate in a synchronized manner16. Maximizing the sum rate alone often starves the users with the weaker channels; and this leads to the desynchronization and the degraded task performance17. Third, the Energy Efficiency (EE) remains a critical design objective18 due to the limited onboard power sources19,20. Unlike the terrestrial base stations, the HAPs cannot rely on the frequent maintenance or the power replenishment; and this compels the system to prioritize the bits/Joule optimization via the complex fractional programming rather than the simple rate maximization. These challenges highlight the need for a flexible multiple access strategy capable of managing the interference, ensuring the fairness, and optimizing the energy consumption simultaneously. In line with Saudi Vision 2030’s strategic goals, this integrated framework can improve mining sector digital infrastructure by providing robust connectivity for geological exploration and real-time safety monitoring. This strengthens its suitability for emergency and high-density communication scenarios in the future.

Recent advancements and open challenges

The research on the Non Terrestial Networks (NTNs), the CR, and the Rate-Splitting Multiple Access (RSMA) has expanded rapidly in the recent years, driven by the need for the high capacity, the interference resilient, and the energy efficient wireless systems. The early studies on the aerial and the satellite assisted networks primarily focused on improving the coverage and the reliability. For instance, the5 and the21 investigated the HAP based cellular architectures and the antenna optimization techniques to enhance the terrestrial coverage and reduce the sidelobe interference. Similarly, the mixed Free-Space Optical (FSO) and Radio Frequency (RF) satellite and aerial terrestrial systems were analyzed in the22, where the outage performance and the beamforming strategies were developed under the hybrid fading conditions. These works highlight the potential of the aerial platforms; and they do not address the RSMA or the cognitive spectrum sharing.

To meet the growing capacity demands in the aerial networks, the Orthogonal Multiple Access (OMA) and the Non- Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA) have been widely explored11,23. While the OMA offers the strict interference isolation, it suffers from the poor spectral efficiency. The NOMA improves the multiplexing gains, but it is highly sensitive to the imperfect Channel State Information (CSI); and it suffers from the error propagation in the Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC), especially under the strong LoS interference typical of the HAP deployments. These limitations motivate the search for the more robust access schemes.

The RSMA has emerged as a powerful alternative, offering the improved spectral and the energy efficiency by partially decoding the interference and partially treating it as the noise19,24. The RSMA has been applied in a variety of the NTN and the aerial contexts. For example, the16 introduced the HAMEC RSMA for the aerial mobile edge computing, optimizing the task offloading and the RSMA parameters using the reinforcement learning. The Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) assisted RSMA downlinks were studied in the17, where the energy efficiency was maximized through the joint trajectory, the power, and the rate optimization. In the satellite systems, the RSMA has been used for the multigroup multicast and the multibeam transmissions25; and it has been used for the hybrid beamforming in the cell free massive Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) networks26. These works demonstrate the RSMA’s versatility, but they do not consider the cognitive HAP environments or the PU interference constraints.

The RSMA has also been integrated with the RIS and the Simultaneous Wireless Information and Power Transfer (SWIPT). The RIS assisted RSMA systems were explored in the19 and the27, where the Successive Convex Approximation (SCA) based algorithms were developed to optimize the beamforming and the RIS phase shifts for the energy efficiency. The active RIS aided RSMA systems were studied in the28, where the deep reinforcement learning was used to jointly optimize the precoding and the RIS reflection matrices. In the SWIPT networks, the29 proposed a PPO based RSMA optimization framework to maximize the energy efficiency. These studies highlight the advanced RSMA enabled architectures; and they assume the full CSI and do not address the cognitive interference constraints.

The learning based RSMA solutions have also gained the traction. The RSMA DRL frameworks were proposed for the Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) in the cell free mMIMO systems30, while the hierarchical reinforcement learning was used for the RIS assisted MEC systems in the31. In the Internet of Things (IoT) networks, the32 used the RSMA for the uplink and the downlink scheduling under the wireless power transfer constraints. Although these approaches demonstrate the RSMA’s adaptability, their computational overhead makes them unsuitable for the real time HAP operation, where the onboard processing is limited.

Moreover, the stringent Quality of Service (QoS) and the ultra low latency demands of the Industry 5.0 and the mission critical IoT have spurred the significant interest in the advanced multiple access schemes tailored for the neXt generation URLLC. Recent studies have demonstrated the potential of the NOMA in supporting the massive connectivity and the delay sensitive applications, such as the NOMA assisted Internet of Video Things (IoVT) with the mobile edge computing33, the multi Base Station (BS) Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) networks34, and the stochastic network calculus based QoS provisioning35. The NOMA has also been extended to the cell free massive MIMO systems utilizing the finite blocklength coding36. However, to overcome the NOMA’s sensitivity to the imperfect CSI and to mitigate the error propagation, the RSMA has recently been adapted for the xURLLC and the Industrial IoT (Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT)). For instance, the RSMA assisted massive MIMO37 and the uplink IIoT networks38 have been proposed to flexibly manage the interference and guarantee the statistical QoS in the short packet transmissions. While these state of the art works provide the foundational solutions for the terrestrial IIoT and the xURLLC, they do not address the unique constraints of the cognitive aerial networks; and specifically, they overlook the coupling of the strict PU protection due to the wide area LoS propagation, the onboard energy limitations of the HAPs, and the intra group fairness.

Alongside aerial and RSMA advancements, significant progress has been made in terrestrial CRNs, particularly concerning energy sustainability and dynamic resource management. For instance, joint time and energy management using deep reinforcement learning has been successfully proposed for ambient backscatter-assisted hybrid underlay CRNs to maximize secondary throughput39. Similarly, multiple access protocols like NOMA have been combined with cooperative spectrum sensing to minimize the Age of Information (AoI) in energy-harvesting CRNs40. Furthermore, the fundamental impacts of sensing energy and data availability on the throughput of energy-harvesting cognitive systems have been rigorously analyzed41. While these studies provide the robust frameworks for the energy and the spectrum management in the terrestrial CRNs, extending these cognitive paradigms to the HAP assisted networks introduces the new challenges; and this is because the strong LoS characteristics of the HAPs exacerbate the PU interference and demand the stricter beam control. In the cognitive radio settings, the RSMA has been applied for the joint communication and the jamming24; and the CR based HAP power allocation was explored in the10. However, these works do not consider the fairness constraints or the energy efficient RSMA beamforming under the HAP specific power limitations.

Research gap

Despite the significant progress across the RSMA, the HAP, and the NTN deployments, a critical gap remains in supporting the synchronized and the energy constrained aerial networks. The existing literature predominantly addresses the subsets of the required constraints in the isolation. For instance, while the cognitive NOMA and HAP architectures11 provide the spectrum sharing, they require the strict power domain ordering; and this suffers from the severe sic instability under the highly correlated LoS channels typical of the stratospheric altitudes. Conversely, the state of the art RSMA frameworks targeting the energy efficiency19,29 or the aerial computing16,30 operate without the cognitive restrictions; and they rely on the computationally heavy Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) algorithms. These approaches lack both the strict spatial interference guarantees required for the underlay coexistence and the deterministic theoretical bounds necessary for the mission critical reliability.

Consequently, no existing work jointly optimizes the “triad” of (i) the PU interference protection, (ii) the intra group and the task oriented fairness, and (iii) the energy efficient RSMA beamforming under the HAP power limitations. This joint optimization is not a trivial aggregate of the existing methods; and it introduces a fundamental conflict of objectives. The fractional nature of the EE maximization (which inherently favors the users with the strong channel conditions) mathematically contradicts the rate leveling requirements of the Industry 5.0 fairness (which forces the power diversion to the weaker users). When this conflict is subjected to the rigid spatial null steering required for the PU protection, it creates a multi objective feasibility bottleneck. The feasible region for the precoding vectors shrinks into a narrow and the non convex set where the standard decoupled algorithms and the learning based black boxes frequently diverge or fail to satisfy the strict QoS. This specific combination is essential for the Industry 5.0 grade synchronized reliability; and it yet remains unaddressed in a mathematically unified, scalable, and the theoretically bounded framework.

(Fairness Modeling Rationale) The fairness constraint is adopted to ensure task synchronization for Industry 5.0 applications, such as collaborative robotics. While max-min fairness is technically applicable to this framework, it often compels the HAP to disproportionately allocate power to users experiencing deep fading, which significantly reduces global EE. Fairness modeling rationale

To ensure balanced connectivity within each SU group, we employ a rate-gap fairness constraint (as formulated in Section 2.3). This model is particularly suited for Industry 5.0 applications (e.g., cooperative robotics), where synchronized data rates are critical to prevent task desynchronization. While traditional max-min fairness is mathematically applicable to our framework, it is overly restrictive for EE optimization. Max-min fairness forces the HAP to allocate disproportionate power to users experiencing deep fading, creating a performance bottleneck that severely degrades the system’s overall bits-per-Joule efficiency. In contrast, the bounded rate-gap constraint provides a flexible trade-off: it allows the system to exploit the favorable channel of the stronger user to maximize EE, while the tunable parameter effectively prevents the weaker user from starvation.

Remark 2.3

(Scope of the Fairness Design) The fairness constraint in (21) is strictly applied intra-group to ensure data synchronization between paired devices jointly executing collaborative Industry 5.0 tasks. Conversely, inter-group fairness (bounding the rate disparity between different groups l and ) is intentionally not enforced. Different user groups often operate independently under vastly different path loss conditions. Enforcing global inter-group fairness would compel the HAP to bottleneck the system’s overall throughput to match the weakest group, thereby severely degrading the global Energy Efficiency. By allowing inter-group rates to fluctuate based on localized channel conditions, the proposed framework retains the flexibility needed to maximize bits-per-Joule performance while satisfying the local synchronization requirements of the paired devices.

Proposed solution framework

To directly address the mathematical challenges identified in the Section 2.3, we design a unified framework comprising the two complementary algorithms with the distinct performance and complexity trade offs. The relationship between the formulated problem and our solutions is structured as follows:

Addressing non convexity and fractional optimality (JBRA): To tackle the fractional objective (Challenge 1) and the highly coupled non convex SINR constraints (Challenge 2), we propose the Dinkelbach SCA JBRA algorithm. It systematically resolves the fractional objective using the Dinkelbach’s transform; and it handles the non convex rate functions via the SCA, achieving the high performance local optimality.

Conclusion

This paper addressed the critical challenge of balancing the energy efficiency, the fairness, and the interference management in the cognitive HAP networks using the RSMA. We formulated a non convex optimization problem to maximize the EE under the PU interference thresholds and the SU rate requirements, proposing the two complementary solutions: (1) the D SCA JBRA, an iterative algorithm combining the fractional programming and the convex approximation; and (2) the MRT NBS, a low complexity scheme using the MRT beamforming and the Nash bargaining. The simulations demonstrated that the D SCA JBRA achieves 87% higher EE and 40% better fairness than the OMA MRT, while the MRT NBS retains 95% of the optimal performance with 90% lower complexity. These advances overcome the limitations of the prior NOMA and the OMA based HAP systems11 and the learning driven frameworks30, which lack the joint EE and the fairness optimization. Our work directly supports the Industry 5.0’s vision of the sustainable wireless infrastructure, enabling the mission critical applications in the remote and the dynamic environments through the scalable and the interference aware aerial networks. This integrated framework is also relevant for improving mining sector digital infrastructure, providing robust connectivity for geological exploration and real-time safety monitoring in line with Saudi Vision 2030s strategic goals. Therefore, it is a promising option for future emergency and high-density communication scenarios.

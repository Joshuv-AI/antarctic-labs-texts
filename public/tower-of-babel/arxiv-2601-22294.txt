Forecasting in the presence of scale-free noise
Serhii Kryhin, Tatiana Mouzykantskii, Vivishek Sudhir

Abstract
The extraction of signals from noise is a common problem in all areas of science and engineering Kolmogorov 1939 ; Wiener 1949 ; Kalman 1960 ; Kailath et al. 2000 . A particularly useful version is that of forecasting: determining a causal filter that estimates a future value of a hidden process from past observations. Current techniques for deriving the filter require that the noise be well described by rational power spectra. However, scale-free noises, whose spectra scale as a non-integer power of frequency, are ubiquitous in practice. We establish a method, together with performance guarantees, that solves the forecasting problem in the presence of scale-free noise. Via the duality between estimation and control, our technique can be used to design control for distributed systems. These results will have wide-ranging applications in neuroscience, finance, fluid dynamics, and quantum measurements.

We formulate the causal Wiener filter as a constrained variational problem, which results in
a pair of operator equations whenever the spectrum of the noise is square-integrable. We identify a symmetry
in the variational formulation that allows the filtering problem for a much larger class of noises to be transformed
to the square-integrable case. The square-integrable case is then reduced by constructing a complete set
of exact eigenfunctions for the operators. This reduction eliminates the causality constraint and thereby obviates
Wiener-Hopf factorization. The solution of the resulting unconstrained problem is characterized by exploiting
results from the theory of Toeplitz operators on the unit circle

In particular, we prove bounds on the accuracy and performance of the exact Wiener filter in terms of the
exponent

Finally, we express our result as a readily-implementable algorithm, which can be applied on

Transformation of scale-free data.

In this work, we are concerned with spectral data characterized by “scale-free”

Finite-size effect of experimental data.

In any experiment, the data
(S_{xy}(\omega),S_{yy}(\omega))
is only ever known in a
finite frequency range, i.e. for
\omega\in[\omega_{m},\omega_{M}]
. So far however, we assumed
access to data at all frequencies. In order to reconcile with experimental reality, we now construct
a procedure to estimate and minimize the finite-size error arising in
{\bf\it h}^{(n)}
from truncation
in frequency space.
As before, we assume that the data
(S_{xy},S_{yy})
has undergone the transformation leading
up to
Theorem

2
.

The finite size effect produces a systematic error in
{\bf\it h}^{(n)}
via errors in
{\bf\it T}^{(n)}

and
{\bf\it s}
. We will now estimate both contributions, and show that it can be minimized by exploiting
the frequency-scale of the data, which can be freely done for scale-free data.
An arbitrary frequency scale
\omega_{0}
implicitly exists in the definition of the variable substitution

\omega=\omega_{0}\mathrm{tan}(u/2)
, that maps the real frequency line to the unit circle. In the preceding
discussion, this scale
\omega_{0}
was implicit, in the following, we make it explicit.

First we focus on the error due to those in
{\bf\it T}^{(n)}

t_{k}=2\uprightintop\displaylimits_{u_{m}}^{u_{M}}\frac{\differential u}{2\pi}\cos({k u})\,S_{yy}\left(\omega_{0}\tan\frac{u}{2}\right),

(18)

where
u_{m,M}=2\tan^{-1}(\omega_{m,M}/\omega_{0})

Assuming
\omega_{m}\ll\omega_{0}\ll\omega_{M}

u_{m}\approx 2\omega_{m}/\omega_{0}

u_{M}\approx(\pi-\omega_{0}/\omega_{M})

(19)

2

for the transformed data.

Conclusion.
The problem of forecasting signals from noisy measurements where the noise is described
by non-rational or even scale-free spectra is an ubiquitous problem in science and engineering. So far, a
systematic approach to the problem, and characterization of the solution, had remained elusive. We described
a systematic approach that exploits a variational formulation of the causal Wiener filter, and a simplifying
symmetry in that formulation. The symmetry allows a transformation of the problem to a pair of linear
operator equations in
L^{2}
. One of these equations imposes the causality constraint, which we explicitly solve
by diagonalizing the relevant operator in
L^{2}
. Thus, the conventional Wiener-Hopf factorization step is
obviated. The problem is thus reduced to the solution of a Toeplitz linear system. Various
practically relevant performance guarantees are derived for the solution of this system, including precise
conditions on the data that ensure the existence and convergence of the filter, and the effect of finite-length
data on the estimated filter.
In sum, we extend the reach of forecasting to the hitherto inaccessible realm of signals contaminated by noise with
scale-free and/or non-rational spectra. Finally, by the duality between estimation
and control
Kalman and Bucy 1961
;
Youla et al. 1976
;
Mitter and Newton 2000
, the technique outlined here for causal estimation from noise with
non-rational spectra can be employed to design controllers for stochastic distributed systems.
These should find applications in domains as disparate as neuroscience,
fluid dynamics, geophysics, quantum physics, and even finance.

Minimization of a convex functional on a complex domain

The arguments leading up to
Eq.

5
technically involves the minimization of a functional (
V_{\varepsilon}[h]
)
whose domain is the set of complex functions (
h
). It is more common
Ekeland and Témam 1999
;
Barbu and Precupanu 2012
to consider
the minimization of a functional whose domain is the set of real functions. Here we show how to bridge this gap.
First, we define a complex-
\star
Banach space, which will be useful below.

Definition A.1
.

Complex-
\star
Banach space
X
is a Banach space over the complex field that is equipped
with a conjugation operation
\star
.
The
\star
operation is (i) a continuous involution on
X
that obeys
(c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}x_{2})^{\star}=c_{1}^{\star}x_{1}^{\star}+c_{2}^{\star}x_{2}^{\star}
for all
x_{1,2}\in X
and
c_{1,2}\in\mathbb{C}
; and
(ii)
||x^{\star}||_{X}=||x||_{X}
for all
x\in X
.

Given a complex-
\star
Banach space
X
, a real Banach subspace can be constructed as follows. Define the
continuous linear operators
\real[x]\coloneqq(x+x^{*})/2
and
\imaginary[x]=(x-x^{*})/(2\mathrm{i})
for all
x\in X
.
Then the space
X_{r}\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid\imaginary[x]=0\}
is a real linear vector subspace of
X
, which
is complete (since it is the kernel of a continuous linear operator Im
(
Hunter and Nachtergaele 2001
, Theorem 5.25)
),
and has a norm induced by that on
X
; i.e.
X_{r}
is a real Banach subspace of
X
.
A useful property of complex-
\star
Banach spaces is that it can be represented as two copies of real Banach spaces.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
X
be a complex-
*
Banach space, and let
X_{r}=\real[X]
, a real Banach space.
Spaces
X
and
X_{r}\oplus X_{r}
are isomorphic as linear spaces, with the isomorphism given
by
x\rightarrow(\real[x],\imaginary[x])
.
Moreover, the norm
||x||_{X}
is equivalent to
||\real[x]||_{X_{r}}+||\imaginary[x]||_{X_{r}}
for all
x\in X
.

The isomorphism can be checked explicitly.
The equivalence of norms follows from
||x||_{X}\leq||\real[x]||_{X}+||\imaginary[x]||_{X}\leq 2||x||_{X}
.

The following definitions and theorems describes the notion of a (Gateaux) derivative of a functional
on a Banach space, and how that characterizes the minimum of the functional when it is convex.

Definition A.2
.

Let
X
be a complex-
\star
, and let

be a proper convex functional.
The Gateaux differential of

in the direction of

at the point

is

\delta f[x;h]\coloneqq\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{f[x+\lambda h]-f[x]}{\lambda},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(26)

If
X
is a real Banach space, the functional
f
is called Gateaux-differentiable at point
x
,
if the map

is a continuous linear functional on
X
.
The functional
f
is called Gateaux-differentiable if it is Gateaux-differentiable at all points
x\in X
.

If
X
is a complex-
\star
Banach space, the functional
f
is called conformally Gateaux-differentiable at
point

, such
that
\delta f[x;h]=2\real\delta f^{\prime}[x;h]
for all

.
The functional
f
is called conformally Gateaux-differentiable if it is conformally Gateaux-differentiable at all points
x\in X
.

The following theorem governs the properties of the minima of Gateaux differentiable functionals.

Theorem A.1

(Proposition 2.40,
Barbu and Precupanu 2012
)
.

Let
X
be a real Banach space and let

be a proper convex Gateaux-differentiable functional.
Then
f[x]=\inf_{u\in X}f[u]
for
x\in X
, if and only if
\delta f[x;h]=0
for all
h\in X
.

\star

\displaystyle\sim\frac{A_{y}}{|\omega|^{\alpha_{y}-2\alpha}},

(30)

\displaystyle S_{yy}^{\prime}(\omega\rightarrow 0)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{B_{y}}{|\omega|^{\beta_{y}-2\beta}},

(31)

\displaystyle S_{xy}^{\prime}(\omega\rightarrow\infty)

\displaystyle=O(|\omega|^{-\alpha_{x}+\alpha}),

(32)

\displaystyle S_{xy}^{\prime}(\omega)

\displaystyle=O(|\omega|^{-\beta_{x}+\beta}).

(33)

Choosing
\alpha=\alpha_{y}/2
and
\beta=\beta_{y}/2

\displaystyle S_{yy}^{\prime}(\omega\rightarrow\infty)

\displaystyle\sim A_{y},

(34)

\displaystyle S_{yy}^{\prime}(\omega\rightarrow 0)

\displaystyle\sim B_{y},

(35)

\displaystyle S_{xy}^{\prime}(\omega\rightarrow\infty)

\displaystyle=O(|\omega|^{-\alpha_{x}+\alpha_{y}/2}),

(36)

\displaystyle S_{xy}^{\prime}(\omega\rightarrow 0)

\displaystyle=O(|\omega|^{-\beta_{x}+\beta_{y}/2}),

(37)

8

To prove the square-integrability of

is continuous and

Since

has no zeros in the upper half-plane, the transformed data

is also positive and finite everywhere, except
perhaps
\omega=0,\pm\infty

Since
S_{yy}^{\prime}
, obeys
Eqs.

34
and
35
and

that
S_{yy}^{\prime}
is continuous and positive at
\omega=0,\infty

As a result,
S_{yy}^{\prime}
is continuous and bounded by positive numbers from above and below.

Finally, we analyse the behaviour of the optimal filter
h^{\prime}
that corresponds to transformed data

Substitution of

into

Appendix C
Proof of
Theorem

3

Consider a function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and the map

g(\omega)\mapsto\tilde{g}(u)=\sqrt{\pi}e^{\mathrm{i}u/2}\sec(u/2)g\left(\mathrm{tan}(u/2)\right),

(39)

whose domain is the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
.
Under the map
g\mapsto\tilde{g}
, the set of eigenfunctions
\phi_{n}(\omega)
in
Eq.

10
gets mapped to

\tilde{\phi}_{n}(u)=e^{\mathrm{i}nu}
.
This map preserves the (hermitian) inner product in the sense that for
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

and their images
\tilde{g}_{1},\tilde{g}_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,

\uprightintop\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\differential\omega\,g_{1}^{\star}(\omega)g_{2}(\omega)=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\differential u}{2\pi}\,\tilde{g}_{1}^{\star}(u)\tilde{g}_{2}(u).

(40)

The set
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is a Fourier basis and forms a complete orthonormal set in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
.
The properties of the Fourier basis guarantee convergence in norm of the Fourier series for a function
\tilde{g}(u)
:

\tilde{g}(u)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\tilde{g}_{n}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(u),\quad\tilde{g}_{n}=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\differential u}{2\pi}\,\tilde{g}(u)\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{*}(u).

(41)

Additionally, the series in
Eq.

41
converge pointwise almost everywhere by Carleson-Hunt theorem
Carleson 1966
;
Hunt 1968
;
Jorsboe and Mejlbro 1982
.

Since by construction the map
g\rightarrow\tilde{g}
is a bijection between
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
that also preserves the (hermitian) inner product, the series for the pre-image
g(\omega)
also converges almost everywhere:

g(\omega)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}g_{n}\phi_{n}(\omega),

(42)

where

g_{n}\equiv\uprightintop\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\differential\omega\,g(\omega)\phi_{n}^{\star}(\omega)=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\differential u}{2\pi}\,\tilde{g}(u)\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{\star}(u)\equiv\tilde{g}_{n}.

(43)

Therefore,
\{\phi_{n}\}
is a complete orthonormal set in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and this completes the proof of
Theorem

3
.

Appendix D
Positive-definiteness of
{\bf\it T}
and
{\bf\it T}^{(n)}

The uniqueness of the solution to
{\bf\it T}{\bf\it h}={\bf\it s}
and the positive-definiteness of the truncated Toeplitz matrix
{\bf\it T}^{(n)}
can be proven with an identical approach.

To establish some preliminaries, consider
an arbitrary function
g(\omega)
, and the vector
{\bf\it g}=\{g_{k}=\langle\phi_{k},g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\}
for
k\geq 0
.
For two functions
g_{1}(\omega)
and
g_{2}(\omega)
, such that
g_{1,k},g_{2,k}=0
for
k<0
, the Hilbert space inner product can be rewritten as
{\bf\it g}_{1}^{\dagger}{\bf\it g}_{2}=\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.
Setting
g_{1}(\omega)=g(\omega)
and
g_{2}(\omega)=S_{yy}(\omega)g(\omega)
gives

\langle g,S_{yy}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}={\bf\it g}^{\dagger}{\bf\it T}{\bf\it g}.

(44)

With these preliminaries in place, we are ready to tackle the uniqueness of
{\bf\it h}
with a proof by contradiction.
Assume there exists two distinct solutions to
{\bf\it T}{\bf\it h}={\bf\it s}
, denoted as
{\bf\it h}_{1}
and
{\bf\it h}_{2}
;
then, necessarily,
{\bf\it T}({\bf\it h}_{1}-{\bf\it h}_{2})=0
.
For
g(\omega)=h_{1}(\omega)-h_{2}(\omega)
, the relation in
Eq.

44
becomes

\langle h_{1}-h_{2},S_{yy}(h_{1}-h_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=({\bf\it h}_{1}-{\bf\it h}_{2})^{\dagger}{\bf\it T}({\bf\it h}_{1}-{\bf\it h}_{2})=0.

(45)

The left side of this equation can be estimated by using the fact that
\inf S_{yy}(\omega)>0
:

\begin{split}0&=\langle h_{1}-h_{2},S_{yy}(h_{1}-h_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&=\uprightintop\displaylimits\differential\omega\,S_{yy}(\omega)|h_{1}(\omega)-h_{2}(\omega)|^{2}\\
&\geq\inf(S_{yy})\uprightintop\displaylimits\differential\omega\,|h_{1}(\omega)-h_{2}(\omega)|^{2}\geq 0.\end{split}

(46)

This immediately implies that
\uprightintop\displaylimits\differential\omega\,|h_{1}-h_{2}|^{2}=0
, and therefore
h_{1}(\omega)-h_{2}(\omega)=0
almost everywhere.
Consequently, it is necessarily true that
{\bf\it h}_{1}={\bf\it h}_{2}
and the solution to
{\bf\it T}{\bf\it h}={\bf\it s}
is unique, and the causal filter
h(\omega)
is unique almost everywhere.
This concludes the proof of the uniqueness of
{\bf\it h}
.

The positive-definiteness of
{\bf\it T}^{(n)}
can be proven using an analogous approach.
Consider a function
g^{(n)}(\omega)
with a corresponding vector
{\bf\it g}^{(n)}
, such that
g_{k}=0
for all
k>n
and
k<0
.
Then it is true that

{\bf\it g}^{(n)\dagger}{\bf\it T}^{(n)}{\bf\it g}^{(n)}={\bf\it g}^{(n)\dagger}{\bf\it T}{\bf\it g}^{(n)}=\langle g^{(n)},S_{yy}g^{(n)}\rangle.

(47)

Since
\inf S_{yy}(\omega)>0
due to
Theorem

2
, the following estimate holds:

{\bf\it g}^{(n)\dagger}{\bf\it T}^{(n)}{\bf\it g}^{(n)}\geq\inf(S_{yy})\langle g^{(n)},g^{(n)}\rangle=\inf(S_{yy}){\bf\it g}^{(n)\dagger}{\bf\it g}^{(n)}.

(48)

Since
\inf(S_{yy})>0
, the inequality above implies that
{\bf\it T}^{(n)}
is positive definite matrix with
its smallest eigenvalue not less than
\inf S_{yy}(\omega)
.

A argument also shows that the largest eigenvalue of
{\bf\it T}^{(n)}
is not greater than
\sup S_{yy}(\omega)
.
The same conclusions about the minimal and maximal eigenvalues are true for
{\bf\it T}
as an infinite-dimensional matrix.

Definition E.1

Theorem G.6
.

Let

.

Theorem G.7
.

Let

To execute the proof, a precise notion of “smooth class” is necessary, which calls for some more

G.1
Toeplitz operators on Banach algebras

We begin by listing some aspects of the modern theory of Toeplitz operators
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
.
A perfect place to start is the notion of a Banach algebra, which recognizes the elementary notion
of multiplication of functions and formalizes it in the context of function spaces (which are otherwise
linear vector spaces with only an addition defined on functions).

Definition G.1
.

A Banach algebra
\mathbb{A}
is a Cauchy-complete Banach space that is equipped with an additional operation called element multiplication
(a,b)\rightarrow ab\in\mathbb{A}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
.
The multiplication map respects the norm
||\bullet||_{\mathbb{A}}
of the Banach space, requiring
||ab||_{\mathbb{A}}\leq||a||_{\mathbb{A}}||b||_{\mathbb{A}}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
.
For the purpose of this work,
\mathbb{A}
is always equipped with an identity element
e\in\mathbb{A}
, such that
||e||_{\mathbb{A}}=1
and
ea=ae=a
for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
.

A simple consequence of any Banach algebra being closed under addition and multiplication is:

Lemma G.1
.

For any Banach Algebra
\mathbb{A}
, the exponential map
\exp[a]\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}/n!
is also a member of
\mathbb{A}
for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
, and

||\exp[a]||_{A}\leq e^{||a||_{\mathbb{A}}}
.

It is often convenient to talk about a Banach algebra
\mathbb{B}
being continuously embedded into a
Banach algebra
\mathbb{A}
:

Definition G.2
.

The Banach algebra
\mathbb{B}
is said to be continuously embedded into a Banach algebra
\mathbb{A}
,
if
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{A}
and
||b||_{\mathbb{A}}\leq||b||_{\mathbb{B}}
for all
b\in\mathbb{B}
.

Relevant examples of Banach algebras are spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, with their corresponding norms
||\bullet||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}

(
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
, §1.36)
, and the
Hardy space

H^{\infty}=\{g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}):P[g]=g\}.

(69)

Toeplitz operators of the form
T(a)
can be associated with any
a
in a Banach algebra; the advantage of
working in this setting is that a factorization of
a
makes sense, and moreover, the Toeplitz operator
associated with a product of elements can be related to products of their Toeplitz operators:

Theorem G.8

(§2.14
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
)
.

For all
a_{+},b_{+}\in H^{\infty}
and all
c\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, it is true
that
T(a_{-}cb_{+})=T(a_{-})T(c)T(b_{+})
, where
a_{-}\coloneqq a_{+}^{*}
.

We often use the subscript “
+
” to denote elements of
H^{\infty}
, meaning that they have no negative-index Fourier coefficients.
It’s complex conjugate, denoted with the “
-
” subscript will then have no positive-index Fourier coefficients.

Note that the order of operators is important, since different Toeplitz operators generally don’t commute.

Theorem

G.8
only holds due to a relation between Toeplitz operators and
H^{\infty}
: for
b_{+}
by the assumptions of the theorem, all negative Fourier coefficients vanish, and for
a_{-}
all positive Fourier coefficients vanish.

Theorem G.9

(§2.18
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
)
.

For all
h\in H^{\infty}
such that
h^{-1}\in H^{\infty}
,
T(h)
is invertible and
T(h)^{-1}=T(h^{-1})
.

Theorem G.10
.

For all
h^{\star}\in H^{\infty}
such that
(h^{-1})^{\star}\in H^{\infty}
,
T(h)
is invertible and
T(h)^{-1}=T(h^{-1})
.

Proof.

Since
h^{\star}\in H^{\infty}
,
1=T(1)=T(hh^{-1})=T(h)T(h^{-1})
according to
Theorem

G.8
.
Since
{h^{-1}}^{\star}\in H^{\infty}
,
T(1)=T(h^{-1}h)=T(h^{-1})T(h)
according to

Theorem

G.8
. Therefore,
T(h^{-1})=T(h)^{-1}
as a two-sided inverse.
∎

We are interested in the properties of Banach algebras in the context of the theory of Toeplitz operators.
The central object of the latter is the Riesz projector
P
, which we would like to now view as acting
on a Banach algebra
\mathbb{A}
,
P:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
, as a well-defined bounded operator.
Moreover, we focus on the algebras that are embedded into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
,
since the Toeplitz operator
T(c)
of most interest to us is defined for
c\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
The following definition captures these hopes.

Definition G.3

(§10.15
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
)
.

A Banach algebra
\mathbb{A}
is called a decomposing algebra, if and only if

1.

\mathbb{A}
is a Banach algebra that is continuously embedded into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.

2.

\mathbb{A}
contains all finite Fourier sums.

3.

For all
a\in\mathbb{A}
,
P[a]
is also a member of
\mathbb{A}
.

These properties are sufficient for
P
to be bounded in
\mathbb{A}
.

It immediately follows from
Definition

G.3
that
T(a)
is bounded on
\mathbb{A}
, if
a\in\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{A}
is a decomposing algebra.
Note that the formulation of
Definition

G.3
formally differs from
(
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
, Section 10.15)
only due to the insignificant difference in the definition of the domain
\mathbb{T}
.

It is important to note that
\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
and its complex-conjugate counterpart are also a Banach algebras for a decomposing algebra
\mathbb{A}
, leading to:

Lemma G.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a decomposing algebra.
Then for all
a,b\in\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
,
a+b
,
ab
and
\exp[a]
are also members of
\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
.

The only slightly non-trivial part of the proof is showing that
ab\in\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
, which follows from the convolution theorem of the Fourier transform.

As it was mentioned above, the key idea of Part II of the proof is to show that a Toeplitz operator in
Eq.

53
can be inverted inside the algebra
\mathbb{A}
.
The properties of decomposing algebra
\mathbb{A}
are insufficient to guarantee that
T(a)^{-1}
is bounded on
\mathbb{A}
, even though
||T(a)||_{\mathbb{A}}<\infty
for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
.
Consequently, we require an object with stronger properties:

Definition G.4
.

A decomposing algebra
\mathbb{A}
is called inverse-closed, if and only if for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
such that
T(a)
is invertible in
H^{2}
, there are
a_{+},a_{-}\in\mathbb{A}
, such that
a=a_{+}a_{-}
and
a_{+},a_{+}^{-1},a_{-}^{\star},(a_{-}^{-1})^{\star}\in\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
.

The inverse-closed algebras live up to their name due to the following lemma.

Lemma G.3
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an inverse-closed algebra and for any
a\in\mathbb{A}
, let

T(a)
be an invertible Toeplitz operator on
H^{2}
.
Then
T(a)
is also invertible on
\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
, and so

T(a)^{-1}
is bounded on
\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Given an inverse-closed algebra
\mathbb{A}
and an invertible on
H^{2}
Toeplitz operator
T(a)
for
a\in\mathbb{A}
, there exist

a_{+},a_{-}\in\mathbb{A}
, such that
a=a_{+}a_{-}
according to
Definition

G.4
.
Due to
Theorem

G.8
,
T(a)=T(a_{-})T(a_{+})
, since
a_{+},a_{-}^{\star}\in\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
.
Operators
T(a_{+})
and
T(a_{-})
are invertible and
T(a_{+})^{-1}=T(a_{+}^{-1})
,
T(a_{-})^{-1}=T(a_{-}^{-1})
according to
Theorems

G.9
and
G.10
.
Since
T(a)
,
T(a_{-})
, and
T(a_{+})
are all invertible,
T(a)^{-1}=T(a_{+})^{-1}T(a_{-})^{-1}=T(a_{+}^{-1})T(a_{-}^{-1})
.
We expressed the inverse of
T(a)
as a finite composition of Toeplitz operators that are bounded on
\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
.
Therefore,
T(a)
is invertible
\mathbb{A}\cap H^{\infty}
and its inverse is bounded.
∎

Finally, we define two Banach algebras that will be used in the proof of
Theorems

G.6
and
G.7
.
These are the Wiener algebra
\mathbb{W}
and the algebra of positive Hölder class functions

C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
.

Definition G.5

(§1.49
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
)
.

A Wiener algebra
\mathbb{W}
is a subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
of functions that have absolutely convergent Fourier series. It is a Banach algebra when equipped with the norm

||f||_{\mathbb{W}}\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|f_{n}|<\infty,

(70)

where
f_{n}
are the Fourier coefficients of
f
.

It is easy to show that
\mathbb{W}
is a decomposing algebra.
Since the Fourier series of all functions in
\mathbb{W}
converge absolutely, they also converge everywhere and uniformly on
\mathbb{T}
.
Due to the absolute uniform convergence of the Fourier transform, all members of
\mathbb{W}
are uniformly continuous.
The following theorem is known in classic literature as two separate statements: the existence of the
Wiener-Hopf factorization and Wiener’s lemma
Wiener 1932
(that if
f
is non-zero and has an absolutely convergent Fourier series, then so does
1/f
). Indeed, Gelfand recognized Banach algebras as the natural setting for
Wiener’s lemma
Gelfand 1939
.

Theorem G.11

(§10.3, 10.22.a
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
)
.

The Wiener algebra
\mathbb{W}
is a decomposing and inverse-closed algebra.

We conclude the exposition with the Hölder classes and their useful properties below.

Definition G.6

(Section 1.50,
Bottcher and Silbermann 2006
, Chapter 1
Ladyženskaja 1968
)
.

Hölder class
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
for
0<\alpha<1
is defined as a subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, such
that any
f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
satisfies

|f(u)-f(u^{\prime})|<A(|u-u^{\prime}|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi)^{\alpha}

(71)

for some
A>0
and all
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}
.
The
\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi
for the absolute value on the right is understood in the sense of
|u-u^{\prime}|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi=\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(|u-u^{\prime}+2\pi n|)
.
For integer
n\geq 0
and
0<\alpha<1
, the Hölder class
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
consists of functions
f
such that
f^{(n)}\in C^{,0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
.
When equipped with the norm

||f||_{C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}||f^{(k)}||_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f^{(n)}(x)-f(y)^{(n)}|}{(|x-y|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi)^{\alpha}},

(72)

each class
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is a Banach algebra (see
(
Ladyženskaja 1968
, Eq. 1.4)
).

Lemma G.4

(§2.4
Zygmund 2003
)
.

Assume that
m\geq 0
is an integer,
0<\beta<1
, and
\alpha=m+\beta
. Then
f\in C^{m,\beta}(\mathbb{T})
implies that
f_{n}=O(n^{-\alpha})
and
|f(u)-\sum_{k=-n}^{n}f_{k}e^{\mathrm{i}uk}|=O(n^{-\alpha}\log n)
, where
f_{n}
is the
n
’th Fourier coefficient of
f
.
For all integer
m\geq 1
and
0<\beta<1
,
C^{m,\beta}(\mathbb{T})\subset\mathbb{W}
.

The following property characterizes the inverses of
T(a)
for
a\in C^{n,\alpha}
.

Theorem G.12
.

The Hölder class
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is a decomposing algebra for all integer
n\geq 0
and
0<\alpha<1
.
The Hölder class
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is an inversion-closed algebra for all integer
n\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<1
.

Proof.

We begin the proof by showing that
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is a decomposing algebra for all integer
n\geq 0
and
0<\alpha<1
.
The first requirement of
Definition

G.3
is satistied: indeed,
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is continuously embedded into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, since
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
||\bullet||_{C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\geq||\bullet||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}
.
The second requirement of
Definition

G.3
is also naturally satisfied, as all the finite Fourier Series are smooth functions.
To check the third requirement of
Definition

G.3
, we use the integral form of the Riesz projector.
As was discussed in the main text,
\mathcal{G}_{+}=(I+i\mathcal{H})/2
, where
\mathcal{H}[f]
is a Hilbert transform of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Performing the
\sim
map on both sides of
\mathcal{G}_{+}=(I+i\mathcal{H})/2
leads to the integral representation of the Riesz projector on
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
:

P[a](u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(f(u)+\uprightintop\displaylimits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\differential u^{\prime}}{2\pi i}\frac{a(u^{\prime}+u)e^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{u^{\prime}}{2}}}{\sin\left(\frac{u^{\prime}}{2}\right)}\right)

(73)

for
a\in C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
.
The integral divergence in
Eq.

73
is understood in the sense of principal value.
The property
P[a]\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
for all
a\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
and
0<\alpha<1
can be confirmed by substitution
z=e^{\mathrm{i}u}
.
The variable substitution reduces the integral in
Eq.

73
to a Cauchy integral on a unit circle, which is known to preserve the Hölder class for
n=0
and
0<\alpha<1

(
Gakhov 1966
, Chapter 5.1)
.
From the form of
Eq.

73
it is evident that
P
commutes with the derivative almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.
Therefore, if
f\in C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
with
0<\alpha<1
, then
\partial^{m}P[f]=P[\partial^{m}f]
for
0\leq m\leq n
.
Necessarily, derivative of
f
is such that
\partial^{n}f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
for
f\in C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
.
Therefore,
P[\partial^{n}f]\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, since
P
preserves the Hölder class for
0<\alpha<1
and
n=0
.
As a result,
\partial^{n}P[f]\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
and
P[f]\in C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, since
P
commutes with the derivative.
This shows that the requirement (3) in
Definition

G.3
is satisfied and
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is a decomposing algebra for integer
n\geq 0
and
0<\alpha<1
.

We proceed to prove that
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is an inversion-closed algebra for all integer
n\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<1
.
Since all members of
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
are continuous, it follows that
T(a)
being invertible in
H^{2}
is equivalent to
a(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}
and
\mathrm{ind}(a)=0
(
Theorem

E.4
).
Function
a(t)\neq 0
is continuous on a compact
\mathbb{T}
, so
0<\inf|a|\leq\sup|a|<\infty
.
The contour
a(t)
does not complete a single turn around
0
when
t
runs from
-\pi
to
\pi
, since
\mathrm{ind}(a)=0
, and therefore
\ln a(t)
is a well-defined single-valued function on
\mathbb{T}
.
Since logarithm is a smooth function on
[\inf|a|,\,\sup|a|]\subset(0,\infty)
,
\ln a(t)
is necessarily the same Hölder class
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
as
a
.
Since
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is a decomposing algebra,
P
is bounded on
C^{n,\alpha}
, so
b+_{=}P[\ln a]
is a member of
C^{n,\alpha}\cap H^{\infty}
.
Similarly,
b_{-}=(1-P)[\ln a]
is such that
b_{-}^{\star}
is a member of
C^{n,\alpha}\cap H^{\infty}
.
Since
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is a decomposing algebra,
a_{+}=e^{b_{+}}
and
a_{-}=e^{b_{-}}
are such that
a_{+},a_{-}^{\star}\in C^{n,\alpha}\cap H^{\infty}
according to
Lemma

G.2
.
Moreover
a_{+}^{-1},(a_{-}^{-1})^{\star}\in C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, since
0<\inf|a_{+}|,\inf|a_{-}|
due to
a(t)=a_{+}(t)a_{-}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}
.
On the other side,
C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\subset\mathbb{W}
for
n\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<0
, so

a_{+}^{-1},(a_{-}^{-1})^{\star}\in\mathbb{W}\cap H^{\infty}
, according to
Theorem

G.11
.
As a result,
a_{+}^{-1},(a_{-}^{-1})^{\star}\in C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\cap\mathbb{W}\cap H^{\infty}=C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\cap H^{\infty}
.
∎

With these preliminaries, we are ready
to prove
Theorems

G.6
and
G.7
.

G.2
Proof of
Theorem

G.6

First, we show that the functions
S_{xy}
and
S_{yy}
that satisfy the assumptions of
Theorem

G.6
are mapped into the Wiener algebra
\mathbb{W}
by the map
\sim
.
The proof of this fact can be formulated with the following lemma:

Lemma G.5
.

Let
S_{xy}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
S_{yy}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
In addition, suppose that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|t_{n}|
and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|S_{xy,n}|
are both finite,
where
2\pi t_{k}=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\differential{u}e^{iku}S_{yy}\left(\tan u/2\right)
and
S_{xy,n}=\langle\phi_{n},S_{xy}\rangle
.
Then and only then
s_{y}(u)=S_{yy}(\tan u/2)
and
P[\tilde{S}_{xy}]
belong to
\mathbb{W}
.

Proof.

Since
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|t_{n}|<\infty
and
t_{n}=t_{-n}^{\star}
, it is true that
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|t_{n}|<\infty
.
By definition,
t_{n}
is the
n
’th Fourier coefficient of
s_{y}
, so
s_{y}
has an absolutely convergent Fourier series, leading to
s_{y}\in\mathbb{W}
.
The inverse is also true:
S_{yy}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
due to
s_{y}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|t_{n}|<\infty
due to
s_{y}\in\mathbb{W}
.
Since the
\sim
map transforms the basis
\{\phi_{k}\}
into the Fourier basis on
\mathbb{T}
(
Lemma

E.1
),
S_{xy,l}
are the Fourier coefficients of
\tilde{S}_{xy}
.
Then
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|S_{xy,l}|<\infty
is a sum of absolute values of all the Fourier coefficients of
P[\tilde{S}_{xy}]
, leading to
P[\tilde{S}_{xy}]\in\mathbb{W}
.
The inverse is true with the logic of the proof reversed.
∎

With the connection between the modified data
(S_{xy},S_{yy})
and the Wiener algebra established, we immediately claim the uniqueness of the corresponding filter
h
and verify its smoothness.
Concretely, we seek to establish that a solution to
T(s_{y})[h]=P[\tilde{S}_{xy}]
is a member of
\mathbb{W}
, when
P[\tilde{S}_{xy}]
and
s_{y}
are in
\mathbb{W}
.
The algebra
\mathbb{W}
is an inversion-closed algebra according to
Theorem

G.11
and
\inf s_{y}>0
, therefore

\tilde{h}=T(s_{y})^{-1}[P[\tilde{S}_{xy}]]\in\mathbb{W}\cap H^{\infty}
according to
Lemma

G.3
.
In combination with
Lemma

G.5
,
\tilde{h}\in\mathbb{W}\cap H^{\infty}
is equivalent to the statement (i) of
Theorem

G.6
.

To prove statements (ii) and (iii) of
Theorem

G.6
, we utilize the notation used in
Appendix

F
.
The proof will be complete, if we show that
\delta h_{1}^{(n)}(\omega)
and
\delta h_{2}^{(n)}(\omega)
, defined by
Eqs.

56
and
57
, vanish uniformly with
n\rightarrow\infty
.
First, we note that
\delta\tilde{h}_{1}^{(n)}
is a member of
\mathbb{W}
, so

|\delta\tilde{h}_{1}^{(n)}(u)|\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}|h_{n}|\rightarrow 0,

(74)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore,
\delta\tilde{h}_{1}^{(n)}(u)\rightarrow 0
everywhere and uniformly.

To estimate
\delta\tilde{h}_{2}^{(n)}(u)
, we first note that the Wiener algebra is decomposing and therefore
||T(s_{y})||_{\mathbb{W}}<\infty
due to
Theorem

G.11
.
Then, the coefficeints
\delta S_{l}^{(n)}
, defined by
Eq.

62
, can be estimated as

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\delta S_{l}^{(n)}|\leq||T(s_{y})||_{\mathbb{W}}||\delta h^{(n)}_{1}||_{\mathbb{W}}\rightarrow 0,

(75)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
It was shown in
Appendix

D
that the eigenvalues of
{\bf\it T}_{n}^{-1}
are bounded from above by
(\inf|S_{yy}|)^{-1}<\infty
, so
\delta\tilde{h}_{2,k}^{(n)}
can be estimated as

\sum_{k=0}^{n}|\delta h_{k}^{(n)}|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}|h_{k}-h_{k}^{(n)}|\leq(\inf|S_{yy}|)^{-1}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\delta S_{l}|\rightarrow 0.

(76)

Since

|\delta h_{k}^{(n)}(u)|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}|\delta h_{k}^{(n)}|\rightarrow 0

(77)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
\delta h_{2}^{(n)}(u)
vanishes everywhere and uniformly.

As a result,
\tilde{h}^{(n)}(u)
converges to
\tilde{h}(u)
uniformly, since
\delta\tilde{h}_{1}^{(n)}(u)
and
\delta\tilde{h}_{2}^{(n)}(u)
vanish uniformly.
This immediately implies that
h^{(n)}(\omega)
converges to
h(\omega)
uniformly due to
Lemma

G.5
.
As an immediate bonus,
h_{p}^{(n)}
converges to
h_{p}
uniformly with
n\rightarrow\infty
for all
p
.
This verifies statements (ii) and (iii) and concludes the proof of
Theorem

G.6
.

G.3
Proof of
Theorem

G.7

To prove
Theorem

G.7
, we first establish the equivalence between the functions that satisfy the assumptions of
Theorem

G.7
and the positive non-integer Hölder classes on
\mathbb{T}
.
The following lemma summarizes the properties of such map.

Lemma G.6
.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\omega_{0}>0
.
Assume
g
is of a Hölder class
C^{m,\alpha}
for integer
m\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<1
on
[-\omega_{0},\omega_{0}]
and
g^{\prime}(\omega)=g(1/\omega)
is of a Hölder class
C^{m,\alpha}
on
[-\omega^{-1}_{0},\omega_{0}^{-1}]
,
Then and only then
\bar{g}(u)=g(\tan u/2)
is of a Hölder class
C^{m,\alpha}
on
\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Both necessity and sufficiency conditions are proven based on the fact that diffeomorphisms between compact sets preserve the Hölder class.
Indeed, a diffeomorphisms between compact sets is known to preserve the Hölder class
C^{0,\alpha}

(
Fiorenza 2017
, Proposition 1.2.7)
.
Since the diffeomorphisms between compact spaces also preserve the finiteness of the derivatives of
g
, the Hölder class
C^{m,\alpha}
is preserved for all non-negative integer
n
.
We proceed to prove the necessity condition first.
We assume that
\bar{g}
is a member of
C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
.
Consider an arbitrary interval
[-u_{0},u_{0}]
for
u_{0}<\pi
.
The the map
x=\tan u/2
is a diffeomorphism between compact sets
[-u_{0},u_{0}]\subset\mathbb{T}
and
[-\omega_{0},\omega_{0}]\subset\mathbb{R}
, where
\omega_{0}=\tan u_{0}/2
.
Therefore, automatically
g\in C^{m,\alpha}([-\omega_{0},\omega_{0}])
, since
\bar{g}\in C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\subset C^{m,\alpha}([-u_{0},u_{0}])

Consider the diffeomorphic map
x=\cot u/2
between
[u_{0},2\pi-u_{0}]
and
[-\omega_{0}^{-1},\omega_{0}^{-1}]
.
Then since
g(\tan u/2)
is of a positive Hölder class
C^{n,\alpha}
on
[u_{0},2\pi-u_{0}]
the map
g^{\prime}(x)=g(1/x)=\bar{g}(2\cot^{-1}x)
is of a positive Hölder class
C^{m,\alpha}
on
[-\omega_{0}^{-1},\omega_{0}^{-1}]
.

The proof of the sufficiency condition is constructed similarly.
Consider
\omega_{0}>0
and
u_{0}=2\tan^{-1}\omega_{0}
.
Repeating the argument above immediately implies that
\bar{g}\in C^{m,\alpha}([-u_{0},u_{0}])
and
\bar{g}\in C^{m,\alpha}([u_{0},2\pi-u_{0}])
.
We proceed to prove that separate Hölder conditions on the intersecting closed intervals imply the Hölder condition in their union.
Let
\bar{g}
be such that

\displaystyle|\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{1})-\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}|(u_{1}-u_{2})\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi|^{\alpha},

(78)

\displaystyle|\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{3})-\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{4})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}|(u_{3}-u_{4})\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi|^{\alpha},

(79)

for
u_{1},u_{2}\in[-u_{0},u_{0}]
and
u_{3},u_{4}\in[u_{0},2\pi-u_{0}]
.
Then to prove the Hölder condition on the whole
\mathbb{T}
, one only needs to consider the case of
u_{1}\in[-u_{0},u_{0}]
and
u_{2}\in[u_{0},2\pi-u_{0}]
.
The following estimation for
C=\max(C_{1},C_{2})
and
u_{3}=\pm u_{0}
holds:

\begin{split}|\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{1})-\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{2})|&\leq|\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{1})-\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{3})|\\
&\qquad+|\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{2})-\bar{g}^{(m)}(u_{3})|\\
&\leq C(|u_{1}-u_{3}|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi)^{\alpha}\\
&\qquad+C(|u_{2}-u_{3}|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi)^{\alpha}\\
&\leq C(|u_{1}-u_{3}|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi\\
&\qquad+|u_{2}-u_{3}|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi)^{\alpha}\\
&=C(|u_{1}-u_{2}|\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi)^{\alpha}.\end{split}

(80)

We used the reverse Minkowski
inequality
|a|^{\alpha}+|b|^{\alpha}\leq(|a|+|b|)^{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
to establish the third inequality.
To satisfy the last equality, we select
u_{3}=u_{0}
when
|u_{1}-u_{2}|\leq\pi
, and
u_{3}=-u_{0}
otherwise.
Therefore, the Hölder condition holds for all points on
\mathbb{T}
and
\bar{g}\in C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
.
∎

With
Lemma

G.6
, the following course towards the completion of the proof of
Theorem

G.7
is straightforward.
According to assumtions of
Theorem

G.7
,
S_{yy}(\omega)
satisfies the assmptions of
Lemma

G.6
.
Therefore,
s_{y}(u)=S_{yy}(\tan u/2)
is such that

.
Under the
\sim
map,
S_{xy}(\omega)
is mapped into
\tilde{S}_{xy}(u)=\sqrt{\pi}(1+\mathrm{i}\omega)S_{xy}(\omega)
, where
\omega=\tan u/2
.
Since
S_{xy}(\omega)
is a member of
C^{m,\alpha}([-\omega_{0},\omega_{0}])
, then
(1+\mathrm{i}\omega)S_{xy}(\omega)
is also a member of
C^{m,\alpha}([-\omega_{0},\omega_{0}])
.
Therefore,
\tilde{S}_{xy}\in C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, since
\tilde{S}_{xy}
satisfies the assumptions of
Lemma

G.6
.
Note that since
C^{m,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\subset\mathbb{W}
for
m\geq 1
and
\alpha>0
, the conditions of
Theorem

G.7
are strictly stronger than
Theorem

G.6
.

Note that
T(s_{y})
is invertible on

according to
Theorem

E.4
.
Therefore,

is also invertible on

due to

and

).
Therefore, the filter

is also a member of

,
and therefore satisfies (i) and (ii) of
Theorem

G.7
due to

G.6
and

.

To study the convergence of
h^{(n)}(\omega)
to

, we perform estimates analogous to the one in
Section

G.2
.
The estimate on
\delta\tilde{h}^{(n)}_{1}(u)
, defined by

56
, is completed as follows.
Function
\delta\tilde{h}^{(n)}_{1}(u)
is necessarily a member of

, since it is a difference of

, which is a finite Fourier sum.
Therefore, by

G.4
,

|\delta\tilde{h}^{(n)}_{1}(u)|=O\left(\frac{\ln n}{n^{m+\alpha}}\right),

(81)

with the estimate being uniform in

and

|\delta\tilde{h}^{(n)}_{l}|=O\left(\frac{1}{l^{m+\alpha}}\right)

(82)

for
l>m
.

The estimate on

We begin by estimating

defined by

62
:

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\delta S_{l}^{(n)}|\leq||T(s_{y})||_{\mathbb{W}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\delta\tilde{h}_{1,l}^{(n)}|.

Since

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\delta S_{l}^{(n)}|=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha-1}}\right).

(84)

With the estimation on

obtained, we are prepared to estimate

\delta\tilde{h}_{2}^{(n)}(u)
, which is given by
Eq.

56
.
From the solution of the truncated equation it follows that

\delta\tilde{h}^{(n)}_{2,l}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}[({\bf\it T}^{(n)})^{-1}]_{lk}\delta S_{k}^{(n)}.

(85)

Applying an estimation analogous to

76
, one recovers

|\delta\tilde{h}^{(2)}(u)|\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\delta\tilde{h}^{(n)}_{2,l}|=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha-1}}\right).

(86)

Eqs.

and

, when combined, result in

|h_{p}^{(n)}-h_{p}|=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{m+\alpha-1}}\right),

(87)

and

(88)

with the estimates being uniform in

and

When

and

are combined with

, the properties (iii) and (iv) follow and the proof of

Szegő-Type Limit Theorem in the Drury-Arveson Space
Arya Gayathri Memana

Abstract
. We state and prove a version of Szegő’s first limit theorem for Toeplitz-like operators on the Drury-Arveson space in the unit ball.



1.
Introduction

For the complex unit circle
\mathbb{T}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
, the Hardy space on
\mathbb{T}
denoted by
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is the closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
with vanishing negative Fourier coefficients. The functions
f_{n}(\theta)=e^{in\theta}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
form an orthonormal basis for
H^{2}
.

Let
P
be the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}
onto
H^{2}
. For
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the operator

\displaystyle T_{\phi}(f)=P(\phi f)

is called a Toeplitz operator with the symbol
\phi
. The matrix of
T_{\phi}
is given by

T=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}&a_{-1}&a_{-2}&\cdots&a_{-n}&\cdots\\
a_{1}&a_{0}&a_{-1}&\cdots&a_{-(n-1)}&\cdots\\
a_{2}&a_{1}&a_{0}&\cdots&a_{-(n-2)}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
a_{n}&a_{n-1}&a_{n-2}&\cdots&a_{0}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix},

where
a_{j}=\hat{\phi}(j)
is the
j^{th}
Fourier coefficient of
\phi
.

An object of interest in the study of Toeplitz operators is the Toeplitz
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{T}
. It is the
C^{*}
algebra generated by the shift operator on the Hardy space. Every element in this
C^{*}-
algebra is of the form
S=T+K
, where
T
is a Toeplitz operator and
K
is a compact operator on
H^{2}
.

Szegő’s well-known first limit theorem about Toeplitz operators states the following
[
9
]
: Consider a bounded positive Toeplitz operator
T_{\phi}
on the Hardy space with the continuous symbol
\phi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Denote by
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},..\lambda_{N}
, the
N
eigenvalues of the
N\times N
truncation
T_{N}
of
T
. Then, for a continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(\lambda_{i})=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}Tr(f(T_{N}))=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\phi)dm.

where
m
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
and
Tr
is the usual trace of a matrix.

In particular, putting
f(x)=log(x)
, and exponentiating, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}det(T_{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}=\exp\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}log(\phi)\ dm\Big)

More generally, (
1
) holds for non-negative Lebesgue-integrable functions. See Section 1.6 in
[
8
]
and Sections 1.3 and 5.4 in
[
4
]
for definitions and Chapter 2 in
[
8
]
for history and generalizations.

Let
P_{N}
be the projection onto the linear span of
\{e^{ik\theta}:k=0,1,\cdots,N\}
on the Hardy space. Then corresponding to each of the operators
P_{N}TP_{N}
we can define a Borel probability measure
\mu_{A}^{N}
on
\mathbb{R}
as:

(3)

\displaystyle\mu_{T}^{N}(B)=\frac{\nu_{T}^{N}(B)}{rank(P_{N})}\ \ \ (B\subseteq\mathbb{R},B\ \text{Borel})

where
\nu_{T}^{N}
is the number of eigenvalues of
P_{N}TP_{N}
in B, counting multiplicities.

The spectrum of a Toeplitz operator is the interval
[a,b]
where
a
and
b
are the min and max values of its symbol
\phi
respectively. In particular, the spectrum of
P_{N}TP_{N}
which is the support of
\mu_{T}^{N}
, is contained in
[a,b]
. Equation (
1
) can now be read as:
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{a}^{b}f(x)d\mu_{T}^{N}(x)=\int_{a}^{b}f(x)d\mu_{*}(x)
where
\mu_{*}
is the push-forward of the Lebesgue measure under the map
\phi
. In this case, the support of
\mu_{*}
is equal to the spectrum of the operator
T
. Thus, Szegő’s theorem also gives a way to estimate the spectrum of a self-adjoint Toeplitz operator from the eigenvalues of its truncations.

In this paper, we prove a version of this theorem in the setting of the Drury-Arveson space, a function space on the d-dimensional ball (see Section 2 for definitions). We consider a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{T}_{d}
analogous to the Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}
. Each operator in this algebra will have a symbol
\phi
, which is a continuous function on the unit sphere. For a natural choice of projections
P_{N}
we get the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T
be a positive operator in
\mathcal{T}_{d}
with a continuous symbol
\phi
and let
[a,b]
be the range of
\phi
. Suppose
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{d_{N}}
are the
d_{N}
eigenvalues of
P_{N}TP_{N}
where
d_{N}=rank(P_{N})
. For a continuous function
f
on
[a,b]
, we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{d_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{N}}f(\lambda_{i})=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{a}^{b}fd\mu^{N}_{T}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}}f\circ\phi d\sigma

where
\sigma
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
.

Here, the measures
\mu_{T}^{N}
will be defined analogously to in
(\ref{eq15})
.

2.
preliminaries

The Hardy space on the unit disc has a classical generalization called the Drury-Arveson space. This is the space
H_{d}^{2}
of analytic functions on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
whose power series coefficents satisy a weighted
l^{2}
condition. More precisely,

\displaystyle H^{2}_{d}=\{f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}:||f||^{2}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty\}

The weighted monomials
\{\sqrt{\frac{|w|!}{w!}}z^{w}\}_{w}
form the canonical orthonormal basis for
H_{d}^{2}
. For convenience, we denote by
e_{w}:=\sqrt{\frac{|w|!}{w!}}z^{w}
.

Let
S_{i}
for
i\in{1,...,d}
be the left shift operators on
H_{d}^{2}
. That is,

\displaystyle S_{i}(f)=z_{i}f

The commutative d-shift is the tuple
S=(S_{1},S_{2},..,S_{d})
. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we mean by
S^{\alpha}
the operator
S_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}S_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}..S_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
(i.e., multiplication by
z^{\alpha}
).

Analogous to the classical Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}
, we have the following:

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
5
]
)
The
Toeplitz algebra

\mathcal{T}_{d}
is defined to be the unital
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
S
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{d}=C^{*}(1,S)\subseteq B(H^{2}_{d})

Definition 2.2
.

By a
Toeplitz-like operator
, we mean an element in
\mathcal{T}_{d}
. A
positive Toeplitz-like operator
is a positive element in
\mathcal{T}_{d}
.

We have the following theorem due to Arveson
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Fix
d>0
and denote the compact operators on
H_{d}^{2}
by
K(H_{d}^{2})
. Then,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{d}/K(H_{d}^{2})\simeq C(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})

given by sending
S_{i}
to
z_{i}
on
C(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
. Thus there exists a short exact sequence,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(H_{d}^{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Remark 1
.

This short exact sequence means that the commutator
[S,S^{*}]
is a compact operator since its image is zero in
C(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
. Thus, finite sums of the form
\sum_{i}p_{i}(S)^{*}q_{i}(S)+K
, where
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
are polynomials in
S
and
K
is a compact operator on
H_{d}^{2}
, form a dense set in
\mathcal{T}_{d}
.

Definition 2.4
.

The image of a Toeplitz operator
T
under the map
\pi
in (
4
) will be called the continuous
symbol
of the Toeplitz-like operator.

For the projections, we choose the sequence of subspaces
H_{N}=
span
\{\sqrt{\frac{|w|!}{w!}}z^{w}:|w|\leq N\}
of
H_{d}^{2}
and the corresponding projections
P_{N}
onto these subspaces. A simple calculation shows that
rank(P_{N})=\sum_{j=0}^{N}{j+d-1\choose d-1}
.

To prove the main theorem, we will make use of ideas of Arveson
[
1
]
and Bédos
[
3
]
, who developed generalizations of the Szegő theorem to a more abstract
C^{*}-
algebra context. Rather than considering a single self-adjoint operator, a concretely presented
C^{*}
-algebra of operators is considered. In
[
1
]
Arveson proves a generalization for
C^{*}
-algebras with a unique tracial state. However, this is not the case in our setting. Instead, we use Bédos’ approach
[
3
]
for general
C^{*}
-algebras.

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space, and let
\{P_{N}\}
be a sequence of finite-rank projections in
B(H)
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq B(H)
be a unital
C^{*}
subalgebra. Let
Tr
stand for the canonical trace on
B(H)
and let
||T||_{2}=(Tr(T^{*}T))^{\frac{1}{2}}
be the Hilbert-Schmidt norm on
B(H)
.

A sequence of projections will be called a
Følner sequence
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 1)
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\lim_{N}\frac{||AP_{N}-P_{N}A||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}

(2)

\lim_{N}\frac{||(I-P_{N})AP_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}

Bédos shows in
[
3
]
that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm above can be equivalently replaced by the trace norm of the operator.

Each state
\chi
on a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
gives rise to a GNS-triple
(\pi_{\chi},H_{\chi},\xi_{\chi})
associated with
(\mathcal{A},\chi)
. Denoting
E
to be the projection-valued spectral measure of the self-adjoint operator
\pi_{\chi}(A)
we get a Borel probability measure
\mu_{A}^{\chi}
defined as:

\displaystyle\mu_{A}^{\chi}(B)=\langle E(B)\xi_{\chi},\xi_{\chi}\rangle\ \ \ (B\subseteq\mathbb{R},B\ \text{Borel})

A general notion of convergence of spectral distributions in the
C^{*}-
algebra setting is given by the following:

Definition 2.6

(
[
3
]
)
.

\{\{P_{N}\},\chi\}
is a
Szegő pair for
\mathcal{A}
if for all self-adjoint
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
\mu_{A}^{N}
converges weakly to
\mu_{A}^{\chi}
. In other words, if
A
is self-adjoint then for all
g\in C(sp(A))
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}gd\mu_{A}^{N}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}gd\mu_{A}^{\chi}.

Then a necessary and sufficient condition on
P_{N}
and
\chi
for
\{\{P_{N}\},\chi\}
to be a Szegő pair is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
3
]
, Theorem 6)
.

The pair
\{\{P_{N}\},\chi\}
is a Szegő pair if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

\chi
is the ”trace per unit volume w.r.t
\{P_{N}\}
”, i.e,
\chi(A)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Tr(P_{N}A)}{rank(P_{N})}
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

(b)

\{P_{N}\}
is a Følner sequence for
\mathcal{A}
.

Szegő’s theorem can now be restated as:
Let
\mathbb{T}
denote the circle group with normalized Haar measure
m
,
P_{n}
the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto the linear span of
\{\epsilon_{k};k=0,1,..,n\}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
where
\epsilon_{k}(z)=z^{k}(z\in\mathbb{T})
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
M:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow B(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
is the representation of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
as multiplication operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. Then,
\{\{P_{n}\},\bar{m}\}
is a Szegő pair for
M(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})),\bar{m}
denoting the (faithful) tracial state on
M(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
obtained by transporting
m
via
M
.

In essence, the normalized eigenvalue distributions of the truncations of the Toeplitz operator converge weakly to the push-forward of the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
under
\phi
.

3.
main result

Our main theorem will follow from a sequence of propositions.

Proposition 3.1
.

\{P_{N}\}
is a Følner sequence for the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{T}_{d}
generated by the shift operators
S_{i}
.

Proof.

For notational convenience let us define, for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
,

\displaystyle\tau_{N}(T):=\frac{||P_{N}T-TP_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

We need to show
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(T)=0
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
.

Consider the shift operators
S_{i},i\in{1,..,d}
. We observe that,

\displaystyle S_{i}P_{N}-P_{N}S_{i}=(P_{N+1}-P_{N})S_{i}P_{N}

So, for orthonormal basis elements
e^{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{|\alpha|!}{\alpha!}}z^{\alpha}
, we have,

\displaystyle||S_{i}P_{N}-P_{N}S_{i}||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=||(P_{N+1}-P_{N})S_{i}P_{N}||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle(P_{N+1}-P_{N})S_{i}P_{N}e^{\alpha},(P_{N+1}-P_{N})S_{i}P_{N}e^{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle(P_{N+1}-P_{N})z_{i}e^{\alpha},(P_{N+1}-P_{N})z_{i}e^{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}||z_{i}e^{\alpha}||^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\frac{|\alpha|!}{\alpha!}\ \ \frac{\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!\cdots\alpha_{i}+1!\cdots\alpha_{d}!}{(|\alpha|+1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\frac{\alpha_{i}+1}{|\alpha|+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\frac{\alpha_{i}+1}{N+1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|=N}1

\displaystyle=\binom{N+d-1}{d-1}

To prove the Følner condition we observe that
||P_{N}||=rank(P_{N})={\sum_{j=0}^{N}\binom{j+d-1}{d-1}}
, so that by above,

\displaystyle\frac{||P_{N}S_{i}-S_{i}P_{N}||_{2}^{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{\binom{N+d-1}{d-1}}{\sum_{j=0}^{N}\binom{j+d-1}{d-1}}

As the numerator is of the order of
N^{d-1}
and the denominator is of the order of
N^{d}
the above ratio goes to zero as
N
goes to infinity.

The limit is zero again if we replace
S_{i}
with
S_{i}^{*}
for each
i\in\{1,2,..,d\}
.

So now it suffices to show that,

(a)

for
A,B\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
, such that
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(A)=0
and
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(B)=0
, then
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(AB)=0
.

(b)

for
A,B\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
such that
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(A)=0
and
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(B)=0
then,
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(A+B)=0
.

(c)

if
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(A)=0
for
A
in a dense set in
\mathcal{T}_{d}
, then the limit is zero for every element in
\mathcal{T}_{d}
.

Recall that for Hilbert-Schmidt norm we have,
||AB||_{2}\leq||A||_{2}||B||
. To prove
(a)
we see that,

\displaystyle\tau_{N}(AB)

\displaystyle=\frac{||P_{N}AB-ABP_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{||P_{N}AB-AP_{N}B+AP_{N}B-ABP_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{||P_{N}AB-AP_{N}B||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}+\frac{||AP_{N}B-ABP_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{N}(A)||B||+\tau_{N}(B)||A||

Thus,
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(AB)=0
.

To prove
(b)
, note that,

\displaystyle\tau_{N}(A+B)

\displaystyle=\frac{||P_{N}(A+B)-(A+B)P_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{||P_{N}A+P_{N}B-AP_{N}-BP_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{N}(A)+\tau_{N}(B)

and hence,
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(A+B)=0
.

Now for
(c)
, let
B\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
. Given
\epsilon>0
, choose
A
in the dense set such that
||A-B||<\epsilon
(note that the norm here is the operator norm). Then,

\displaystyle\tau_{N}(B)

\displaystyle=\frac{||P_{N}B-BP_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{||P_{N}(B-A+A)-(B-A+A)P_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{||P_{N}(B-A)-(B-A)P_{N}||_{2}}{||P_{N}||_{2}}+\tau_{N}(A)

\displaystyle\leq 2||(B-A)||+\tau_{N}(A)

Thus,
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{N}(B)=0
. This implies
\{P_{N}\}
forms a Følner sequence for
\mathcal{T}_{d}
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Define, for
T\in B(H_{d}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\chi_{N}(T):=\frac{Tr(P_{N}TP_{N})}{rank(P_{N})}

Remark 2
.

\chi_{N}
is a positive linear functional on
B(H^{2}_{d})
. Also,
\chi_{N}(I)=1
and
|Tr(P_{N}TP_{N})|\leq||T||rank(P_{N})
. Thus, each
\chi_{N}
is a state on
B(H)
. In particular,
||\chi_{N}||=1
for each
N
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For a compact operator
K\in B(H_{d}^{2}),\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{N}(K)=0

Proof.

We begin by noting that for a finite rank operator
F
, since it is in the trace class of
B(H^{2}_{d})
,
||F||_{1}<\infty
and,

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{Tr(P_{N}FP_{N})}{rank(P_{N})}\leq\frac{||F||_{1}||P||}{||P||_{2}}=\frac{||F||_{1}}{||P||_{2}}

and so,

(6)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{N}(F)=0.

We also know that finite rank operators are dense in the space of compact operators. Thus, given a compact operator
K
and an
\epsilon>0
, we choose a finite rank operator
F
such that
||K-F||<\epsilon
. Now, (
6
) allows us to choose
N
big enough so that
|\chi_{N}(F)|<\epsilon
for all
N>N^{\prime}
. So,

\displaystyle|\chi_{N}(K)|

\displaystyle=|\chi_{N}(K)-\chi_{N}(F)+\chi_{N}(F)|

\displaystyle\leq|\chi_{N}(K)-\chi_{N}(F)|+|\chi_{N}(F)|

\displaystyle\leq||\chi_{N}||\ ||K-F||+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon

for all
N>N^{\prime}
and the desired result follows.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\alpha
and
\beta
are multi-indices and
\alpha\neq\beta
then,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{N}({S^{\alpha}}^{*}S^{\beta})=0

Proof.

Note that, for
\alpha\neq\beta
and each
N\geq 0
,

\displaystyle Tr(P_{N}{S^{\alpha}}^{*}S^{\beta}P_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|w|\leq N}\langle{S^{\alpha}}^{*}S^{\beta}z^{w},z^{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{|w|\leq N}\langle S^{\beta}z^{w},S^{\alpha}z^{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

and the result follows.
∎

We require the following variant of the Chu-Vandermonde identity.

Lemma 3.5
.

(
[
6
]
, Corollary 5.4) (Chu-Vandermonde-type identity) Let
k_{1},k_{2},j,w_{1},w_{2}\geq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{|w|=j}\binom{k_{1}+w_{1}}{w_{1}}\binom{k_{2}+w_{2}}{w_{2}}=\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}+j+1}{j}.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
w=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{d})
and
\alpha=(k_{1},k_{2}\cdots k_{d})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{|w|=j}\binom{w_{1}+k_{1}}{w_{1}}\binom{w_{2}+k_{2}}{w_{2}}\cdots\binom{w_{d}+k_{d}}{w_{d}}=\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{d}+j+(d-1)}{j}

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, we know that the result is true for
d=2
. Assuming the result is true for
d-1
, we will show that it is true for
d
. Thus letting
w^{\prime}=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots w_{d-1})
, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{|w^{\prime}|=l}\binom{w_{1}+k_{1}}{w_{1}}\binom{w_{2}+k_{2}}{w_{2}}\cdots\binom{w_{d-1}+k_{d-1}}{w_{d-1}}=\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots k_{d-1}+l+(d-2)}{l}

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{|w|=j}\binom{w_{1}+k_{1}}{w_{1}}\binom{w_{2}+k_{2}}{w_{2}}\cdots\binom{w_{d}+k_{d}}{w_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{j}\ \Bigg(\sum_{|w^{\prime}|=l}\binom{w_{1}+k_{1}}{w_{1}}\binom{w_{2}+k_{2}}{w_{2}}\cdots\binom{w_{d-1}+k_{d-1}}{w_{d-1}}\Bigg)\binom{j-l+k_{d}}{j-l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{j}\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{d-1}+l+(d-2)}{l}\binom{j-l+k_{d}}{j-l}

\displaystyle=\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{d}+j+(d-1)}{j}

as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

For every multi-index
\alpha=(k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{N}({S^{\alpha}}^{*}S^{\alpha})=\frac{(d-1)!\alpha!}{(d-1+|\alpha|)!}

Proof.

For
w=(w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{d})
we have
||S^{\alpha}e_{w}||^{2}=\frac{1}{\binom{|\alpha|+|w|}{k_{1}+w_{1},k_{2}+w_{2},\cdots,k_{d}+w_{d}}}\binom{|w|}{w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{d}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle Tr(P_{N}{S^{\alpha}}^{*}S^{\alpha}P_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|w|\leq N}||S^{\alpha}e_{w}||^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{|w|=j}\frac{1}{\binom{|\alpha|+j}{k_{1}+w_{1},k_{2}+w_{2},\cdots,k_{d}+w_{d}}}\binom{j}{w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{|w|=j}\frac{(k_{1}+w_{1})!(k_{2}+w_{2})!\cdots(k_{d}+w_{d})!}{(|\alpha|+j)!}\frac{j!}{w_{1}!w_{2}!\cdots w_{d}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{j!}{(|\alpha|+j)!}\sum_{|w|=j}\frac{(k_{1}+w_{1})!(k_{2}+w_{2})!\cdots(k_{d}+w_{d})!}{w_{1}!w_{2}!\cdots w_{d}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{j!k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{d}!}{(|\alpha|+j)!}\sum_{|w|=j}\binom{k_{1}+w_{1}}{w_{1}}\binom{k_{2}+w_{2}}{w_{2}}\cdots\binom{k_{d}+w_{d}}{w_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{j!k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{d}!}{(|\alpha|+j)!}\binom{|\alpha|+j+(d-1)}{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{d}!}{(|\alpha|+j)!}\frac{(|\alpha|+j+(d-1))!}{(|\alpha|+(d-1))!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha!}{(|\alpha|+(d-1))!}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{(|\alpha|+j+(d-1))!}{(|\alpha|+j)!}

Thus we have,

\displaystyle\frac{Tr(P_{N}{S^{\alpha}}^{*}S^{\alpha}P_{N})}{rank(P_{N})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha!}{(|\alpha|+(d-1))!}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{(|\alpha|+j+(d-1))!}{(|\alpha|+j)!}}{\sum_{j=0}^{N}{j+d-1\choose d-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-1)!\alpha!}{(|\alpha|+(d-1))!}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{N}(|\alpha|+j+1)(|\alpha|+j+2)\cdots(|\alpha|+j+(d-1))}{\sum_{j=0}^{N}(j+1)(j+2)\cdots(j+(d-1))}

Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Tr(P_{N}{S^{\alpha}}^{*}S^{\alpha}P_{N})}{rank(P_{N})}=\frac{(d-1)!\alpha!}{(d-1+|\alpha|)!}

∎

We now indicate how Theorem

For

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}(z,w)=\frac{1}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{d+1+\alpha}}\ \ z,w\in\mathbb{B}_{d}

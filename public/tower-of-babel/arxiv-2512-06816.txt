Nonperturbative low harmonics generation in low-frequency laser field
S. A. Bondarenko 1,2 V. V. Strelkov 1∗

Abstract
Solving numerically three-dimensional non-stationary Schrödinger equation, we find the atomic response to the quasi-static electric field.We suggest a semi-phenomenological approximation of this response which describes well the ab initio numerical calculation result both for low and high intensities (up to 1.4 ⋅ 10 14 1.4\cdot 10^{14} W/cm 2 ). In particular, this approximation describes the non-perturbative increase or the third harmonic generation efficiency with the laser intensity, as well as the dependence of the optical ratification signal in the two-color field on the phase difference between the fields. Making the calculations for the realistic laser frequencies, we find that our approach is applicable up to fundamental frequencies of about 1 eV.



Introduction

Generation of harmonics of intense laser field is one of the most well-known phenomena in non-linear optics. For moderate laser intensities this process can be successfully described with a perturbation theory
8
. For high intensities (approximately higher than
10^{13}-10^{13}
W/cm
2
) photoionization of atoms leads to non-perturbative mechanisms of low-
2
and high-
3

15
;
7
). However, the non-perturbative properties of low- and moderate-harmonic generation appear even for lower fields, when these properties are not directly linked to the photoionization. Such behaviour is actively studied recently
10
;
9
;
11
;
1
.

In this paper we study numerically the atomic response to the low-frequency (quasi-static) electric field. We
ab initio
calculate the dipole moment of the model atom as a function of the field strength.
We find a semi-phenomenological approximation for this dependence; the approximation describes well the numerical result for the laser intensities when the ionization is negligible. We apply this approximation to describe Kerr-effect, third harmonic generation, and quasi-static field production via two-colour field. Moreover, we find up to which laser frequency our quasi-static field approach is applicable.

I
Numerical methods

To study the response of argon atom to external laser field we use a single-active-electron (SAE) approximation
5
;
6
. This approximation models the atom as a system where only one electron is active and responds to the external electromagnetic field, while the other electrons create an effective potential in which the active electron is located. This approach is widely used in strong-field physics due to its computational efficiency and accuracy in reproducing main atomic properties such as ionization potentials and energy levels of the atom.

All calculations are performed in atomic units, where the elementary charge
e
, electron mass
m_{e}
and reduced Planck constant
\hbar
are set to unity:

e=m_{e}=\hbar=1.

(1)

The dynamics of the active electron in the external laser field can be described by the 3D time-dependent Schrödinger equation (TDSE):

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi(t,\vec{r})=\hat{H}(t)\Psi(t,\vec{r}),

(2)

where
\Psi(t,\vec{r})
is the time dependent wave function of the electron and
\hat{H}(t)
is the Hamiltonian of the system.

Let’s assume linearly polarized laser field along the
z
-axis and cylindrical symmetry around this axis. This symmetry reduces the problem to two spatial dimensions
(r,z)
.

The Hamiltonian is given by:

\hat{H}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+U(r)+zE(t)-iW(r,z),

(3)

where
U(r)
is the effective one-electron potential which models the argon atom and

-iW(r,z)
is an imaginary (absorbing) potential. We add this potential to prevent unphysical reflections of the electron wave packet at the boundaries of the numerical grid.

The effective potential
U(r)
for argon is chosen to reproduce main features of the real atom: the ionization energy and the energies of the lowest bound states
13
. The model potential is:

U(r)=\frac{1+ae^{-\sqrt{r^{2}+b^{2}}}}{\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}+r^{2}}},

(4)

with parameters:
a=5.94,\penalty\ b=0.2,\penalty\ r_{0}=2.105
.

The absorbing potential is defined separately in the radial and axial directions:

- axial absorption (
z
-direction):

W_{\text{abs}}^{z}(z)=\cases{\displaystyle}\frac{|z|-z_{\text{layer}}}{z_{\text{bound}}-z_{\text{layer}}}W_{0},\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ |z|>z_{\text{layer}},\\
0,\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ |z|<z_{\text{layer}},

(5)

- radial absorption (
r

W_{\text{abs}}^{r}(r)=\cases{\displaystyle}\frac{r-r_{\text{layer}}}{r_{\text{bound}}-r_{\text{layer}}}W_{0},\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ r>r_{\text{layer}},\\
0,\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ r<r_{\text{layer}}.

(6)

The total absorbing potential has the form:

W(r,z)=W_{\text{abs}}^{z}(z)+W_{\text{abs}}^{r}(r),

(7)

where
z_{\text{bound}},r_{\text{bound}}
are the positions of the boundaries of the grid,
z_{\text{layer}},r_{\text{layer}}
are the start positions of the absorbing layers;

The time-dependent electric field

At

\Psi(t=0,r,z)=\Psi_{0}(r,z).

(9)

To find the atomic response we calculate the expectation value of the time-dependent dipole moment

E(t)=E_{0}g(t)(\cos(\omega_{0}(t-t_{full}/2))+\alpha\cos(2\omega_{0}(t-t_{full}/2))+\Delta\phi),

Let us focus on a third-order nonlinear optical process governed by the frequency-matching condition:

2\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}=0.

(15)

This process leads to production of a zero-frequency polarization, resulting in optical rectification or generation of a quasi-static (in particular, the terahertz) electric field

The nonlinear response of the medium at this specific combination of frequencies is described by the third-order nonlinear susceptibility
\tilde{\chi}^{(3)}(\omega)

d(2\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}-\omega_{0})=\tilde{\chi}^{(3)}(\omega=2\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}-\omega_{0})E(2\omega_{0})(E^{*}(\omega_{0}))^{2},

(16)

\tilde{\chi}^{(3)}(\omega=2\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}-\omega_{0})=\frac{d(\omega=2\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}-\omega_{0})}{E(2\omega_{0})(E^{*}(\omega_{0}))^{2}}.

Conclusions

We solve numerically three-dimensional non-stationary Schrödinger equation for a model argon atom in a laser field and find the atomic dipole moment for different field frequencies. In particular, we find the atomic response to the quasi-static electric field. We suggest a semi-phenomenological approximation of the atomic dipole moment which agrees with the
ab initio
numerical calculation result in a quasi-static field, both for low and high intensities. We use this expression to calculate effective susceptibilities at the fundamental frequency, frequency of the third harmonic, and also at zero frequency for the two-colour laser field consisting of the fundamental frequency and its second harmonic. Comparing these results with the numerical ones, we see that our analytical approach describes well the behaviour of the susceptibilities, including the non-perturbative growth of a susceptibility with the laser intensity and the dependence of the optical rectification signal on the phase difference between the generating fields. The analytical results agree with numerical ones for frequencies up to 1 eV and intensities up to 1.4
10^{14}
W/cm
2
. For higher frequencies the field can not be considered as the quasi-static one, and for higher intensities the photoionization-induced processes give main contribution to the nonlinearity.

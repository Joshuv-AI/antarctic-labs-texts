Lorentz and CPT violation and the hydrogen and antihydrogen molecular ions II – hyperfine-Zeeman spectrum
Graham M. Shore

Abstract
Fundamental principles of quantum field theory such as Lorentz invariance, CPT symmetry and locality may be tested to extremely high precision in atomic and molecular spectroscopy. The narrow natural linewidth of rovibrational states in the hydrogen molecular ion H 2 + {\rm H}_{2}^{\,+} and its antimatter counterpart H ¯ 2 − \overline{\rm H}_{2}^{\,-} make these ideal candidates, and give O ⁡ ( m p / m e ) O(m_{p}/m_{e}) increased sensitivity to Lorentz and CPT violation in the proton sector compared to H {\rm H} and H ¯ \overline{\rm H} atoms. In a previous paper, we presented a detailed analysis of the rovibrational spectrum of H 2 + {\rm H}_{2}^{\,+} and H ¯ 2 − \overline{\rm H}_{2}^{\,-} in an effective QFT encoding Lorentz and CPT violation, focusing on spin-independent effects. Here, we extend this analysis to include the full hyperfine-Zeeman spectrum and include spin-dependent Lorentz and CPT violating operators in the effective theory. The results demonstrate how constraints on these symmetry-violating couplings may be extracted from specific rovibrational transitions between hyperfine-Zeeman states in the presence of an applied magnetic field.

The rovibrational, hyperfine and Zeeman spectrum of
\textbf{H}_{2}^{\,+}
and

{\overline{\textbf{H}}_{2}^{\,-}}

We begin with a brief review of the main features of the hyperfine-Zeeman spectrum for

{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}

[
20
,
21
,
22
]
,
and equivalently
\overline{\rm H}_{2}^{\,-}
, which we need later to describe
the Lorentz and
CPT
violating effects. We focus here on the simplest case,
Para-
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}
, since this is sufficient to illustrate all the main principles and
is the main subject of current experiments, but comment on the more complicated case
of Ortho-
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}
where relevant.

In general, the energy eigenstates of
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}
at zero applied magnetic field are
described by the quantum numbers
|v\,N\,S\,I\,F\,J\,M_{J}\rangle
.
Before including spin, the rovibrational energy levels are described by
|v\,N\rangle
,
where
v
is the vibrational quantum number and
{\bf N}
is the molecular orbital
angular momentum, with quantum numbers
N,M_{N}
. The electron spin is
{\bf S}
,
with quantum numbers
S=1/2
and
M_{S}
. The nucleon spin is
{\bf I}
and we define
the total molecular spin by
{\bf F}={\bf I}+{\bf S}
. Spin-statistics requires that for
I=0

(Para-
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}
),
N
is even, while
I=1
(Ortho-
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}
) requires
N
odd.
The total angular momentum is then
{\bf J}={\bf N}+{\bf F}
.

For Para-
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}
therefore, since
I=F=0
and
S=1/2
always, we characterise
the molecular rovibrational-spin states simply by
|v\,N\,J\,M_{J}\rangle
.
It will also be useful to consider the alternative representation with states
|v\,N\,M_{N}\,M_{S}\rangle
,
which are the eigenstates at large applied magnetic field
{\bf B}
.

2.1
Hyperfine-Zeeman Hamiltonian

The hyperfine and Zeeman interactions for Para-
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}

H_{\rm HFS}~=~c_{e}(v,N)\,{\bf N}\cdot{\bf S}~~~~=~~\frac{1}{2}c_{e}(v,N)\,\left({\bf J}^{2}-{\bf N}^{2}-{\bf S}^{2}\right)\ ,

(3)

H_{\rm Z}~=~g_{e}\mu_{B}\,{\bf S}\cdot{\bf B}~-~g_{m}(v,N)\mu_{B}\,{\bf N}\cdot{\bf B}~~~~=~~\mu_{B}B\,\left(g_{e}\,S_{3}~-~g_{m}(v,N)\,N_{3}\right)\ ,

(4)

in the
EXP

3

H_{\rm HFS}
describes the spin-orbit interaction between the electron spin and the
molecular orbital angular momentum.

[

The form of the Zeeman interaction (

{\bf N}_{e}

\displaystyle\mu_{B}B\,\langle v\,N\,J^{\prime}\,M_{J}|\,\Big(\,N_{e3}~-~\frac{2m_{e}}{m_{p}}\,N_{p3}\,\Big)|v\,N\,J\,M_{J}\rangle

2.2
Hyperfine-Zeeman energy spectrum

Given the hyperfine and Zeeman Hamiltonians in the form (
3
), (
4
),
we can now readily evaluate the energy spectrum. With zero magnetic field,

|v\,N\,J\,M_{J}\rangle
are eigenstates of
H_{\rm HFS}
and we find immediately

\langle v\,N\,J\,M_{J}|\,H_{\rm HFS}\,|v\,N\,J\,M_{J}\rangle~=~\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\,N\,c_{e}(v,N)\ ,~~~~~~~~~~&(J=N+\tfrac{1}{2})\\[8.0pt]
-\frac{1}{2}\,(N+1)\,c_{e}(v,N)\ ,&(J=N-\tfrac{1}{2})\end{cases}\\

(15)

with hyperfine energy splitting
(N+\tfrac{1}{2})c_{e}(v,N)

Combining with the results above for the matrix elements of
H_{\rm Z}
, we can express the
matrix elements of the full hyperfine-Zeeman Hamiltonian in the
|v\,N\,J\,M_{J}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle v\,N\,J^{\prime}\,M_{J}|\,H_{\rm HFS}+H_{\rm Z}\,|v\,N\,J\,M_{J}\rangle~=~

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}Nc_{e}+\frac{1}{2N+1}(g_{e}-2Ng_{m})\mu_{B}BM_{J}~~~~~~~~~~-\frac{1}{2N+1}\sqrt{(N+\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}-M_{J}^{2}}\,(g_{e}+g_{m})\mu_{B}B\\[20.0pt]
~~~~~~~~~~~~~{\rm Sym}~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\tfrac{1}{2}(N+1)c_{e}(v,N)-\frac{1}{2N+1}(g_{e}+2(N+1)g_{m})\mu_{B}BM_{J}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(16)

with rows/columns corresponding to
J=N\pm\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Notice that for the states
J=N+\tfrac{1}{2},~M_{J}=\pm(N+\tfrac{1}{2})
where there is no mixing,

\displaystyle~~~\pm\frac{1}{2}\Big[(N+\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}c_{e}^{2}\,+\,2c_{e}(g_{e}+g_{m})\mu_{B}B\,M_{J}\,+\,(g_{e}+g_{m})^{2}(\mu_{B}B)^{2}\Big]^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\ .

\displaystyle E_{-}(B)~

\displaystyle=~-\frac{1}{2}(N+1)c_{e}(v,N)\,-\,\frac{1}{2N+1}\big(g_{e}+2(N+1)g_{m}(v,N)\big)\mu_{B}B\,M_{J}\ ,

\displaystyle E_{-}(B)~

\displaystyle=~\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{e}\,-\,g_{m}(v,N)(M_{J}+\tfrac{1}{2})\big)\mu_{B}B\,-\,\frac{1}{2}c_{e}(v,N)(M_{J}+\tfrac{1}{2})\ ,

The expansion parameter in this case is clearly

3
Born-Oppenheimer analysis with Lorentz and
CPT
violation

The dynamical analysis of the spectrum of the
{\rm H}_{2}^{\,+}
and
\overline{{\rm H}}_{2}^{\,-}

molecular ions is carried out in the framework of the Born-Oppenheimer approximation,
extended here to include Lorentz and
CPT
violation.
This was described in Paper I for the spin-independent SME couplings
c_{\mu\nu}
and
a_{\mu\nu\lambda}
,
and here we extend this to include also the spin-dependent couplings
b_{\mu}
,
g_{\mu\nu\lambda}
,

d_{\mu\nu}

H_{\mu\nu}

First recall the form of the non-relativistic SME Hamiltonian for a single Dirac fermion, derived
from the original QFT Lagrangian (
1

H_{\rm SME}~=~\bigl(A\,+2\,B_{k}S^{k}\bigr)\,+\,\bigl(C_{i}\,+\,2D_{ik}S^{k}\bigr)\frac{p^{i}}{m}\,+\,\bigl(E_{ij}\,+\,2F_{ijk}S^{k}\bigr)\frac{p^{i}p^{j}}{m^{2}}\ ,

(21)

We require only the even-parity operators, with coefficients

[
16
,
17
,
15
]

\displaystyle A~

\displaystyle=~a_{0}\,-\,m\,c_{00}\,+\,m^{2}\,a_{000}\ ,

\displaystyle B_{k}~

\displaystyle=~-b_{k}\,+\,m\,d_{k0}\,+\,\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{kmn}\big(H_{mn}-m\,g_{mn0}\big)\ ,

\displaystyle E_{ij}~

\displaystyle=~-m\big(c_{ij}\,+\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,c_{00}\,\delta_{ij}\big)\,+\,m^{2}(3\,a_{0ij}\,+\,a_{000}\,\delta_{ij})\ ,

\displaystyle F_{ijk}~

\displaystyle=~\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\,\tfrac{1}{2}\big(b_{k}\,\delta_{ij}\,-\,b_{j}\,\delta_{ik}\big)\,+\,m\,\big(d_{0j}\,+\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,d_{j0}\big)\delta_{ik}\,-\,\tfrac{1}{4}\delta_{ik}\epsilon_{jmn}\,H_{mn}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\,-\,m\,\epsilon_{ikm}\big(g_{m0j}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,g_{mj0}\big)\,\bigg]~+~(\,i\leftrightarrow j\,)\ ,

(22)

where for later convenience we have explicitly symmetrised the
F_{ijk}
coupling on
i,j

Note also that the overall constant term

The fundamental idea of the Born-Oppenheimer analysis is to separate the full Schrödinger
equation for the molecular ion into two separate equations, the first describing the electron
motion relative to the nucleon CM and the second describing the rovibrational motion of
the nucleons. The energy eigenvalues of the electron Schrödinger equation depend on the
inter-nucleon separation
R
and feed back into the nucleon Schrödinger equation as a
potential
V_{M}(R)
, which determines the rovibrational motion.
V_{M}(R)
has the characteristic
shape of a Morse potential, and a numerical determination is shown in Fig.
2
,
taken from Paper I.

The key step is to factorise the spatial part of the molecular wavefunction
into nucleon and electron parts:

\Psi(\boldsymbol{R},\boldsymbol{r})=\Phi(\boldsymbol{R})\,\psi(\boldsymbol{r};R)
.
The spin operators are spectators at this stage,
but the momentum dependence of the SME interactions is important. The effective
electron Schrödinger equation is then,
5
5

5

The electron position relative to the nucleon CM is

with corresponding momentum
{\bf p}=\hat{\mu}\,\dot{\bf r}

of a single particle at point

with momentum

and reduced mass

.

\displaystyle\bigg[-\frac{1}{2\hat{\mu}}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{r}}^{2}\,+\,V_{mol}(R,r_{1e},r_{2e})

\displaystyle+~\frac{1}{m_{e}^{2}}\big(E_{ab}^{e}\,+\,F_{abk}^{e}\,S_{k}\big)p_{a}p_{b}~+~\frac{1}{2m_{p}^{2}}\big(E_{ab}^{p}\,+\,F_{abk}^{p}\,I_{k}\big)p_{a}p_{b}\,\bigg]\,\psi(\boldsymbol{r};R)\,=\,E_{e}(\boldsymbol{R})\,\psi(\boldsymbol{r};R)\ ,

where we understand
\boldsymbol{p}\rightarrow-i\nabla_{\boldsymbol{r}}

6
The SME spherical tensor coupling formalism

The SME Hamiltonian and associated parameter constraints may alternatively be described in
terms of the spherical tensor representation of the Lorentz and
CPT
couplings most widely
used in the analysis of spectroscopy in this theory. This has a number of virtues, notably
allowing a compact description of higher dimensional operators in the extension beyond the
minimal SME. However, it also necessarily obscures the immediate relation with the couplings
as they appear in the QFT Lagrangian. In order to make contact with the extensive body of
literature on the SME using this formalism, in this section we show how our results for the
molecular ion spectrum may be rewriten in the spherical tensor form and give a detailed
dictionary between the Cartesian and spherical tensor couplings.

The non-relativistic SME Hamiltonian in the spherical tensor formalism is presented in
a very general form including higher-dimensional operators in
[
15
]

and we use this extensively in what follows. In that work, however, the initial Hamiltonian
is written with the couplings defined in what in our context we are calling the
MOL

frame, but with the spin operators in a ‘helicity frame’

with
z
-axis aligned
with the electron momentum. The first task is therefore to find the SME Hamiltonian for
the electron with both the spin operators and couplings expressed in the
MOL
frame.
This construction is described in Appendix
A
. We distinguish the couplings in the

MOL
frame with circumflex accents,
e.g.

\widehat{\cal V}_{njm}^{{\rm NR}}
.

We find the following result, (
172
), for the SME Hamiltonian
H_{\rm SME}
in the
MOL
frame,
which in the Born-Oppenheimer analysis is incorporated in the electron Schrödinger equation
leading to the potential

\displaystyle H_{\rm SME}~=~

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi}}\,\bigg[\,\widehat{{\cal V}}_{000}^{{\rm NR}}~+~{\rm tr}\,p_{a}\,p_{b}\,\widehat{{\cal V}}_{200}^{{\rm NR}}~-~\sqrt{5}\,\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{Y}p_{a}\,p_{b}\,\widehat{{\cal V}}_{220}^{{\rm NR}}

It will be useful to record here some general properties of Wigner matrices which are used
extensively below. They are defined in terms of angular momentum states
|j\,m\rangle

d_{m^{\prime}m}(\theta)~=~\langle j\,m^{\prime}|\,e^{-i\,\theta\,J_{z}}\,|j\,m\rangle\ ,

(109)

They satisfy orthonormality relations,

\sum_{k}\,d_{m^{\prime}k}^{j}\,d_{mk}^{j}~=~\delta_{m^{\prime}m}\ ,

(110)

d_{m^{\prime}m}^{j}~=~(-1)^{m-m^{\prime}}\,d_{mm^{\prime}}^{j}~=~d_{-m,-m^{\prime}}^{j}\ .

(111)

J=|j^{\prime}-j|,\ldots(j^{\prime}+j)
.

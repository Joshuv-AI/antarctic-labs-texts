Technetium Pyrophosphate Blood Pool Uptake Mistaken for Myocardial Uptake in Light-Chain Amyloidosis
Authors: Rodney H Falk
Published: 2026

The report by Zambrano Zambrano et al1 purports to show a grade 3 technetium pyrophosphate (PYP) uptake in a patient subsequently diagnosed with light-chain (AL) amyloidosis. The case is presented as a cautionary tale that intense myocardial uptake does not always indicate transthyretin (TTR) amyloidosis. However, the planar imaging shown does not show grade 3 uptake but rather demonstrates intense uptake in the sternum and activity typical of cardiac blood pool uptake. Thus, this is almost certainly a negative PYP scan, as is generally found in AL amyloid cardiomyopathy.

Planar PYP imaging is no longer the gold standard for diagnosing TTR amyloidosis because of the propensity for misinterpretation, especially in unskilled hands.2,3 At a minimum, single photon emission computed tomography imaging is required, ideally in combination with computed tomography.4 In the current case, the finding of heavy proteinuria (>8 g per liter) and the gastrointestinal symptoms, along with cardiac features, all but rule out TTR amyloidosis and point strongly to AL amyloidosis. Thus, there was no indication for a PYP myocardial scan. The appropriate diagnostic workup should have been immediate hematologic evaluation for AL amyloidosis. This case report, rather than demonstrating that grade 3 PYP myocardial uptake may occur in AL amyloidosis is, in contrast, a reminder that imaging is best chosen to confirm a high pretest likelihood of a suspected diagnosis. Here, the pretest likelihood of AL amyloidosis was high, and that of TTR amyloidosis very low. Even when an appropriate imaging indication exists for suspected TTR amyloidosis, it requires precision in performance and expertise in interpretation, without which a clear diagnosis of unexpected AL amyloidosis may be delayed, resulting in unnecessary and potentially life-threatening treatment delay.

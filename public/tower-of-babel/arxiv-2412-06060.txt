Steering Large Language Models to Evaluate and Amplify Creativity
Matthew Lyle Olson, Neale Ratzlaff, Musashi Hinck, Shao-yen Tseng, Vasudev Lal

Abstract
Although capable of generating creative text, Large Language Models (LLMs) are poor judges of what constitutes “creativity”. In this work, we show that we can leverage this knowledge of how to write creatively in order to better judge what is creative. We take a mechanistic approach that extracts differences in the internal states of an LLM when prompted to respond “boringly” or “creatively” to provide a robust measure of creativity that corresponds strongly with human judgment. We also show these internal state differences can be applied to enhance the creativity of generated text at inference time.



1
Introduction

The ability of Large Language Models (LLMs) to evaluate the quality of their own outputs has received significant attention recently
(
Xu et al. 2023
)
due to the widespread desire to make use of potential high-quality synthetic data. While it has been argued that LLMs are not good enough at self-evaluation to bootstrap themselves into better reasoners
(
Huang et al. 2023
)
or forgo real data entirely
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
, there are scenarios where self-evaluation can enable improved performance
(
Ren et al. 2023
;
Yuan et al. 2024
;
Madaan et al. 2024
)
. In this work, we show that LLM-based self-evaluation with respect to creativity is challenging if approached naively; with LLMs self-evaluations but by leveraging the internal representations of an LLM during inference, not only can self-assessment be improved, the creativity of the generation itself can be amplified.

Recent work in steering language models has shown that LLMs represent concepts as linear directions in their activation space
(
Turner et al. 2023
)
. Specific directions corresponding to social bias, refusal, harmlessness, or humor have been identified by prior work
(
Ratzlaff et al. 2024
;
Gao et al. 2024
;
Templeton et al. 2024
)
. Furthermore, it has been shown that it’s possible to remove, or ablate these features from the model via an orthogonal projection of intermediate activations onto a chosen direction. In the case of refusal, a safety-tuned model can have its guardrails effectively removed
(
Arditi et al. 2024
)
, allowing a user to prompt the model in ways that were previously censored. Conversely, it is also possible to induce a specific feature
(
Rimsky et al. 2023
;
Hinck et al. 2024
)
, forcing the model to respond in a particular way according to the chosen direction. In this work we explore model steering for creativity, and propose three steps to unlock an LLM’s potential for use in creative domains.

•

We solve for steering directions that correspond to latent concepts of creativity within an LLM, we use Llama3-8B and creative writing as our target setting.

•

We apply our creative steering directions to induce (increase) the creativity of generations sampled from Llama3-8B on the creative writing dataset
Fan et al. 2018
. Upon inspection, the generated samples are far more diverse and creative than baseline generations.

•

We construct an estimator to score the creativity of generated output leverages the creative steering directions. We find this estimator aligns closely with human judgment, unlike simple prompting baselines.

2
Method

Our method has three main components: 1) finding a suitable “creativity direction” in activation space, 2) optionally use this direction to steer the LLM to generate creative text, and 3) scoring the creativity of outputs respect to this creativity direction.
The established method of computing a particular concept in activation space requires collecting a small dataset of contrastive instructions
(
Marks and Tegmark 2023
)
, where the only difference between the corresponding completions is the presence or absence of the concept in question. In our case, we leveraged the creative writing prompts dataset
(
Fan et al. 2018
)
to elicit creative generations, and used GPT4o
(
Achiam et al. 2023
)
to craft uncreative versions of the same prompts to obtain paired creative-uncreative responses. We use the validation split of the creative writing prompts dataset for finding the attribute, and the test split in our evaluation experiments. Details and examples of the dataset can be found in the appendix.
Given a set of paired creative instructions
x_{c}\in X_{c}
and uncreative instructions
x_{u}\in X_{u}
, we compute the creative direction as the normalized difference in the average activation vectors at a given layer with respect to the creative and uncreative instructions:

\mathbf{a}=\left\|\frac{1}{|X_{c}|}\sideset{}{{}_{x_{c}\in X_{c}}}{\sum}LLM_{l}(x_{c})-\frac{1}{|X_{u}|}\sideset{}{{}_{x_{u}\in X_{u}}}{\sum}LLM_{l}(x_{u})\right\|_{2}

where
LLM_{l}
is the output of the LLM at layer
l
. The choice of layer is selected empirically; examples of the resulting generations when different layers are targeted can be found in the appendix. In this work, we choose layer 8 of Llama3-8B
(
Dubey et al. 2024
)
as the target layer.
At inference time, we can score the creativity of generated text by computing the cosine similarity between the attribute
\mathbf{a}
and the current token:

\text{score}=\frac{1}{T+1}\sideset{}{{}_{t=0}^{T}}{\sum}\cos(||LLM_{l}(x_{t})||_{2},\mathbf{a})

where
T
is the total generated tokens,
x_{t}
is the token generated at step t, and
x_{0}
is the input.

Finally, we can steer the LLM to induce increased creativity of generated text by adding the creativity attribute to the intermediate activations at the target layer. Intuitively, we are amplifying the contribution of the model’s internal sense of creativity. Note that in this case, the notion of creativity is heavily tied to creative writing and common subject matter therein, rather than a general notion of creativity.

LLM^{\prime}_{l}(x)=LLM_{l}(x)+\lambda*\mathbf{a}
,
where
LLM^{\prime}_{l}(x)
is effectively a replaced version of the layer and the scalar
\lambda=3
was found manually from early tests.

3
Experiments

Figure
1
shows our three main experiments. First, we use Llama3-8B to generate 3 types of stories: creative story prompts from our dataset, non-creative prompts from GPT4o, and non-creative with the creativity vector added. We find the models often score all stories similarly at about 7 (when asked to rate the story from 0 to 9). Next, we show our scoring method is highly effective at identifying creative stories. We compute the score on the test split of the creative / uncreative prompts, and we find the creative stories (as well as uncreative stories with creativity induced) are much more similar to the creativity attribute. This result clearly indicates that the LLM internally models creativity and can accurately track how creative a given story is. Finally, to verify the efficacy of adding the discovered creativity attribute, we run pairwise comparisons on the generations of the uncreative prompts with and without creativity induced. We find the original model is a poor predictor of its own creativity, whereas a large frontier model (Llama3-70b) is on par with a human annotator (with agreement
>70\%
). These results show that a naive approach to LLM self-assessment is not sufficient for identifying creative output.

Appendix C
Contrastive Dataset Details

It is critical to obtain high quality contrastive pairs of generations to isolate the creativity direction. For positively creative instructions we leverage the creative writing prompts dataset
[
Fan et al. 2018
]
, randomly sampling 500 writing prompts to obtain creative generations. To obtain baseline, or less creative generations, we use GPT4o to negate the aspects of creativity in our creative instructions.

Space mining is on the rise . The Space tanker Exxon Valdez 2.0 crash and spill its cargo . Write a news story covering the event

Space mining is on the rise . The Space tanker Exxon Valdez 2.0 delivers its cargo without incident . Write a news story covering the completely uneventful event .

Appendix D
Creativity at Different Model Depths

Model generation behavior can vary wildly when intervened on. We find that earlier layers can fail to generate meaningful test when the residual stream is altered. Intervening near the final layers of Llama3-8B has little effect on the output. We find that intervening near the middle of the network yields a reasonable trade-off of generation quality and introduced creativity.

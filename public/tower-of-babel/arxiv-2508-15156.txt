1
Introduction and Main results

1.1
Introduction

The branching random walk (BRW)
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a measure-valued Markov process whose law is determined by
an offspring distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{N}
(the probability distribution of the number of children) and an
\mathbb{R}
-valued random variable
X
representing the step size or displacement.
There are several equivalent ways to define a branching random walk. In this work, we adopt the formulation presented in
[
17
,
13
]
, which we now describe
formally.

At generation
0
, there is one particle located at the origin (i.e.
Z_{0}=\delta_{0}
). At generation
1
,
this particle first performs a jump to some position
X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}X
and then produces
N
offspring, where
N
is equal in law to
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and independent of
X_{1}
. Therefore, the point process
Z_{1}
formed by all the
particles
alive at generation
1
is given by
Z_{1}=N\delta_{X_{1}}
. At generation
2
, the particles alive at generation
1
repeat their parent’s behaviour independently from the place where they are born (first jump and then branch) and the procedure goes on.
An alternative definition reverses the order of operations that each individual first branches and then jumps. As noted in
[
13
, Remarks 2 and 3]
, both formulations yield equivalent results for our purposes but
the former one is more convenient to deal with.

Let
\mathbb{T}
denote the genealogical tree of the branching process
rooted by
\emptyset
with
offspring distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
; see
[
26
, p.13-14]
. Let

X_{\emptyset}=0
and
\{X_{v},\ v\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{\emptyset\}\}
be
i.i.d. copies of
X

such that for each
v\in\mathbb{T}
,
X_{v}
stands
for the displacement of particle
v
. Let
V(\omega):=\sum_{v\preceq\omega}X_{v}
be the position of particle
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
, where
v\preceq\omega

means that
v=\omega
or
v
is an ancestor of
\omega
. For each particle
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
, we use
|\omega|
to denote the generation of
\omega
. The
population and their locations
at generation
n
forms a point process given by

Z_{n}=\sum_{|\omega|=n}\delta_{V(\omega)}.

The branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is called a subcritical (critical or supercritical) branching random walk if
m:=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}kp_{k}\in[0,1)
(
m=1
or
m>1
). We refer to Shi
[
26
]
for a more detailed overview of branching random walks.
Throughout this article, for a finite point measure
\mu
, we write
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
(
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}
) for the probability law (expectation operator)
of the branching random walk with initial value
Z_{0}=\mu
.
For notational convenience, we write
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}
.

In this paper, we consider the tail probabilities of the maximal displacements in a subcritical branching random walk.
Define the following extremal quantities:

M_{n}:=\max_{|\omega|=i,0\leq i\leq n}V(\omega)~\text{for}~n\geq 0,~M:=\max_{n\geq 0}M_{n},

which represent the maximal position up to generation
n
and the global maximum respectively.
For the subcritical case,
since the
population dies out in finite time
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,
we have

M_{n}\leq M<\infty
. Under the assumptions that
m\in(0,1)
,
\sum_{k\geq 0}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
and that the step size
X
is a centered integer-valued random variable such that

\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]<\infty
for all
\theta>0
,
Neuman and Zheng
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
established the following asymptotics:

0<\liminf_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma n}\mathbb{P}(M\geq n)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma n}\mathbb{P}(M\geq n)\leq 1,

(1.1)

where
\gamma>0
is the solution to the equation
\mathbb{E}[e^{\gamma X}]=\frac{1}{m}
. Moreover, under the additional assumptions that
\text{esssup}X<\infty
and that
X
is nearly right-continuous, Neuman and Zheng
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
proved that the following limit exists

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma n}\mathbb{P}(M\geq n)=:\kappa\in(0,1].

(1.2)

Some additional related works on subcritical branching Lévy processes will be introduced in Section
1.3
. To the best of our knowledge, no further refinements of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) exist in the literature for either subcritical branching random walks or subcritical branching Lévy processes
in the case where the step size has unbounded positive jumps with light-tailed.

For
M_{n}
, Neuman and Zheng
[
17
, Theorem 1.6]
discovered the following phase transition behavior.
They proved that there
exist constants
0<\underline{\delta}\leq\overline{\delta}<\infty
and the same constant
\kappa

in
(
1.2
) such that

(i)

For
c\in(0,\underline{\delta})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)=\kappa.

(1.3)

(ii)

For
c\in(\overline{\delta},\infty)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)=0.

(1.4)

In
[
17
, Remark 1.9]
, Neuman and Zheng conjectured the sharpness of this transition
\underline{\delta}=\overline{\delta}
, and they verified this conjecture for a special class of branching random walks.

In this paper, we are also interested in the branching random walk with killing, whose definition is as follows.
For each initial position
y>0
, consider the BRW
(\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}})
starting from
Z_{0}=\delta_{y}
. We add a killing mechanism that
particles and their descendants are killed upon first entering
(-\infty,0]
.
The surviving
population and their locations
at generation
n
is given by

Z_{n}^{(0,\infty)}:=\sum_{|\omega|=n}{\bf 1}_{\{V(v)>0,\forall v\preceq\omega\}}\delta_{V(\omega)}.

Similarly, define

M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}:=\max_{|\omega|=i,0\leq i\leq n,\forall v\preceq\omega,V(v)>0}V(\omega)~\text{for}~n\geq 0,~M^{(0,\infty)}:=\max_{n\geq 0}M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}.

In the continuous-time setting where the spatial motion is a spectrally negative Lévy process and that the branching rate is
\beta>0
, under the assumption
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(\log k)p_{k}<\infty
, it was proved in

[
9
,
23
]
that there exist constants
\gamma_{*},\kappa_{*}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
y>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}e^{\gamma_{*}x}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}>x)=\kappa_{*}W^{(\beta(1-m))}(y),

(1.5)

where
W^{(\beta(1-m))}
is the scale function associated with the spectrally negative Lévy
process
with index
\beta(1-m)
.

Our works are inspired by the results of
[
17
]
in branching random walks and
[
9
,
23
]
in branching Lévy processes.
The main contributions of this paper are divided into two parts:

(i)

In branching random walk,
we establish the existence of the limit
\kappa
in (
1.2
) under weaker assumptions (Theorem
1.1
), requiring only that

\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
for the step size and that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k\log k)p_{k}<\infty
for the offspring distribution. These conditions are shown to be optimal. We also confirm the conjecture
of

[
17
, Remark 1.9]
in Theorem
1.2
below and we prove that

\underline{\delta}=\overline{\delta}=m\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}],

which yields the sharp transition between (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

(ii)

For branching random walk with killing,
we extend the spectrally negative Lévy process results of (
1.5
) to discrete-time BRW with killing
where only
\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(\log k)p_{k}<\infty
are required.
Compared to
[
23
]
, we allow the spatial motion to have positive jump.

1.2
Main results

Recall that we only consider the subcritical case

m\in(0,1)
(hence
p_{0}<1
).
Moreover, in the sequel of this paper, we always assume that there exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that

\mathbb{E}[e^{\gamma X}]=\frac{1}{m}.

Our first theorem concerns the tail probability of
M
, which shows that it decays exponentially. Let
u(x):=\mathbb{P}(M>x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Set

\psi(s):=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}p_{n}(1-s)^{n}+ms-1
for
s\in[0,1]
, where we define
0^{0}=1
. Denote by
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
the random walk with step size
X
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\sigma(S_{0},S_{1},...,S_{n}),~n\geq 0
be the natural filtration of
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{x}
(
\mathbb{E}_{x}
) be the probability measure (expectation) under which the random walk
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
has initial value
x
, and (in the absence of confusion) write
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{0}
for short. Since
\mathbb{E}[e^{\gamma X}]=\frac{1}{m}
,
(\mathbb{P}_{0},\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\{m^{n}e^{\gamma S_{n}}\}_{n\geq 0})
is a martingale with mean
1
. Thus, we can define the probability
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
as the following:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{x}}\Big|_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}:=m^{n}e^{\gamma(S_{n}-x)},~n\geq 0.

(1.6)

It is easy to check that under the probability
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
,
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a random walk with initial value
x
. Thus, the above definition indeed makes sense. The same as before, without causing confusion, write
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
for short. Define
\mathcal{T}_{1}:=\inf\{n\geq 0:S_{n}>S_{0}\}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}:=S_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}
. We will prove in (
2.3
) that
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(S_{1})>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\left(I_{\infty}:=\min_{j\geq 0}S_{j}\in(-\infty,0]\right)=1.

(1.7)

The distribution
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\big(I_{\infty}\in\mathrm{d}y\big)
has been studied in
[
5
, p.396, (2.7)]
. As usual, we call a random variable
X
is lattice if there exist
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
h>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(X\in\{a+kh:k\in\mathbb{Z}\})=1
, where the largest such
h
is called the span of
X
and
a
is called the shift. Otherwise, we say
X
is non-lattice. To simplify the statement of the theorem, in the sequel of this paper, when
X
is lattice we always assume the shift
a=0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
and
\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}(k\log k)p_{k}<\infty
.

(i)

If the step size is non-lattice, then

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{\gamma x}\mathbb{P}(M>x)=(1-p_{0})\frac{1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\gamma\mathcal{H}_{1}}]}{m\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]\gamma}-\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\int^{\infty}_{-I_{\infty}}e^{\gamma z}\psi(u(z))\mathrm{d}z\right]}{m\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]}\in(0,1].

(1.8)

(ii)

If the step size is lattice with span
h>0
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma hn}\mathbb{P}(M>hn)=\frac{(1-p_{0})h(1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\gamma\mathcal{H}_{1}}])}{m\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}](e^{\gamma h}-1)}-\frac{h}{m\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\Bigg[\sum_{i=-I_{\infty}/h}^{\infty}\psi(u(ih))e^{\gamma ih}\Bigg]\in(0,1].

Remark 1.1
.

(i)

Although Neuman and Zheng considered the tail probability
\mathbb{P}(M\geq x)
in (
1.2
) while our Theorem
1.1
considers a different probability
\mathbb{P}(M>x)
, one can obtain the asymptotic behavior for
\mathbb{P}(M\geq x)
from Theorem
1.1
.
Indeed,
if the step size is non-lattice, then for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{\gamma x}\mathbb{P}(M>x)\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{\gamma x}\mathbb{P}(M\geq x)\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{\gamma x}\mathbb{P}(M>x-\delta)=e^{\gamma\delta}\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{\gamma x}\mathbb{P}(M>x).

(1.9)

Therefore, the first result of Theorem
1.1
remains true with
\mathbb{P}(M>x)
replaced by
\mathbb{P}(M\geq x)
. If the step size is lattice, then naturally
\mathbb{P}(M>hn)=\mathbb{P}(M\geq h(n+1))
.

(ii)

If
\mathbb{P}(X=1)=1
, then
\mathbb{P}(M>n)=\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0)
.
By
[
2
, p.40]
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{m^{n}}\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0)\in(0,\infty)
if and only if
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(\log k)p_{k}<\infty
. Therefore, our assumption for the offspring law is optimal in this case. On the other hand, if
\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]=\infty
, then
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]=\infty
; see
[
5
, p.397, Theorem 2 (iii)]
. Thus, limits in Theorem
1.1
equals to
0
, which means our assumption for the step size is optimal.

Our next result considers asymptotic behaviors of
M_{n}
condition on
M>cn
, which exhibits a phase transition at
c=m\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume

\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
and
\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}(k\log k)p_{k}<\infty
.

(i)

If
c\in\big(0,m\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]\big)
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn\big|M\geq cn)=1.

(1.10)

(ii)

If
c\in(m\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}],\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn\big|M\geq cn)=0.

(1.11)

Remark 1.2
.

If
X
is non-lattice or lattice with span
1
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}{\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn\big|M\geq cn)

(1.12)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}&\text{exists}\in(0,1],~c\in\big(0,m\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]\big);\\
&0,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~c\in\big(m\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}],\infty\big),\end{cases}

(1.13)

where the first equality follows from the fact
M_{n}\leq M
, and the second equality follows from Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. Thus, we have confirmed and generalized the conjecture of Neuman and Zheng
[
17
, Remark 1.9]
.

Now we are ready to state our main results for the branching random walk with killing. If
X
is lattice with span
h
, then observe that
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}\left(M^{(0,\infty)}>kh\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{ih}}\left(M^{(0,\infty)}>(k-1)h\right)
, where
y\in(ih,(i+1)h)
for some
i\geq 1
. Therefore, in the following results, we always assume that
y=ih
for some
i\geq 1
whenever
X
is lattice with span
h
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume

\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
and
\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}(k\log k)p_{k}<\infty
.

(i)

If the step size is non-lattice, then for any
y>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}>x)}{\mathbb{P}(M>x)}=e^{\gamma y}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(I_{\infty}>-y).

(1.14)

(ii)

If the step size is lattice with span
h>0
, then for any integer
i\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{ih}}(M^{(0,\infty)}>hn)}{\mathbb{P}(M>hn)}=e^{\gamma ih}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(I_{\infty}>-ih).

Finally, the last theorem considers the long time behaviour of the maximum
from
time
0
to
n
of the branching random walk with killing, whose results are almost the same as those of the branching random walk without killing.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume

\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
and
\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}(k\log k)p_{k}<\infty
. Then, the results of Theorem
1.2
hold with
(M_{n},M,\mathbb{P})
replaced by
(M_{n}^{(0,\infty)},M^{(0,\infty)},\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}})
, where
y
is any positive constant if
X
is non-lattice and
y=ih
for some
i\geq 1
if
X
is lattice with span
h
.

1.3
Discussion on the literature

The tail asymptotics of maximal displacements in branching random walks and branching Lévy processes have been extensively studied. The earliest foundational work dates back to Sawyer and Fleischman
[
25
]
, who employed analytic methods to investigate the maximal displacement
M
in critical and subcritical branching Brownian motions under a third moment condition
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
on the offspring distribution.

For branching random walks, the analytic techniques in
[
25
]
are no longer applicable. In the critical case, significant progress was made by
Lalley and Shao
[
13
]
and Zhang
[
27
]
who utilized discrete Feynman-Kac formulas to establish that when the step size
X
admits finite fourth moment
\mathbb{E}[X^{4}]<\infty
and that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{2}p_{k}<\infty
,
the tail probability exhibits polynomial decay:

\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{2}\mathbb{P}(M>x)=\frac{6\text{Var}(X)}{\text{Var}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))}.

(1.15)

The asymptotic properties of
M_{n}
have been studied in various regimes by Kesten
[
10
]
in critical case
and by Neuman and Zheng
[
16
]
in near-critical case.

Analogous results in critical and subcritical branching Lévy processes are developed. Profeta
[
20
]

employed scale functions to derive precise tail asymptotics for both subcritical and critical branching spectrally negative Lévy processes.
For the case where
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{2}p_{k}=\infty
, Hou et al.
[
7
]
and Profeta
[
22
]
studied the critical branching Lévy process with stable offspring law.

We now review some literatures addressing cases where the spatial motion lacks finite second
moment.
For critical branching
\alpha
-stable processes with
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
Lalley and Shao
[
12
]
first studied the symmetric
\alpha
-stable case, proving the polynomial tail decay:

\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{\alpha/2}\mathbb{P}(M>x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha}}.

(1.16)

Profeta
[
21
,
22
]
extended (
1.16
) to
critical and subcritical branching Lévy processes with positive heavy-tailed jumps. For critical and subcritical branching spectrally negative
\alpha
-stable processes, see Hou et al.
[
6
]
.

In the supercritical regime, since
M=\infty
almost surely on the survival set, research mainly focuses on the asymtotic behavior of
M_{n}
.
In super-Brownian motion, Pinsky
[
19
]

(although the definition of
M_{t}
is slightly different from ours, one can compare
[
11
, Corollary 3.2]
for the lower bound)
proved that
M_{t}
grows linearly with
t
. Later, Zhang
[
28
]
obtained decay rates for
\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\geq ct)
when
c
exceeds a critical threshold.
Similar large deviation results were proved by Ȯz et al.
[
18
]
in branching Brownian motion.

For branching random walks and branching Lévy processes with killing, as previously mentioned, results in the subcritical case
m\in(0,1)
were established by
[
9
,
23
]
. In the critical case
m=1
, Lalley and Zheng
[
14
]
first studied the Brownian motion case, determining the tail probability of
M^{(0,\infty)}
using analytical methods. Later, the results of
[
14
]
was generalized by Hou et. al.
[
8
]
through probabilistic techniques. In supercritical case
m>1
, under the condition that the step size has negative drift (ensuring finite-time extinction),
Aïdékon et al.
[
1
]
characterized the tail behavior of
M^{(0,\infty)}
. In the continuous-time setting, we refer to
[
15
]
and the references therein.

1.4
Proof strategies of the main results

Our approach differs fundamentally from previous methods employing Feynman-Kac formulas
and Fekete’s subadditive lemma in
[
17
,
9
,
23
]
. The key components of our proof are as follows.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, firstly we utilize height random variables and the
renewal theory to show that the Laplace transform of the overshoot
(S_{T^{+}_{0}},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x})
converges as
x\to+\infty
, where
T_{0}^{+}:=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}:S_{n}>0\}
; see Lemma
2.4
below. We also prove another renewal theorem of
(\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x})

and we show the existence of the limit
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(f(S_{\ell}){\bf 1}_{\{\max_{0\leq j\leq\ell}{S}_{j}\leq 0\}}\right)
as
x\to+\infty
in Lemma
2.4
.
Then we express
\mathbb{P}(M>x)

as functionals
of
(\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x})
. Finally, we analyze these functionals directly according to renewal
theorems
and prove that the
limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{\gamma x}\mathbb{P}(M>x)

exists. The Feynman-Kac formula is only used in the proof for the positivity of the limit.

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, if
c\in(0,m\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}])
, then we apply the many-to-one formula (see (
3.9

Properties for random walk with positive drift

Let
U(\mathrm{d}y):=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}\in\mathrm{d}y)
. Denote by
U(y):=U((-\infty,y]),~y\in\mathbb{R}
the renewal function of
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. By the Markov inequality, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle U(y)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}\leq y)\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{\gamma(y-S_{n})}]

(2.1)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma y}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\gamma X}])^{n}=e^{\gamma y}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}m^{n}<\infty,

(2.2)

where the last equality follows from (
1.6
). Thus,
U(y)
is well-defined. The following lemma presents several properties of the renewal function
U
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume

\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
.

(i)

U(x+y)\leq U(x)+U(y)
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

U(x)\leq(x+1)U(1)
for all
x>1
.

Proof.

(i) Since
U
is a non-negative increasing function in
\mathbb{R}
, we only prove the case
x,y>0
.
Noticing that
g(u):=u\log u
is a convex function on
(0,\infty)
.
Thus, by Jensen’s inequality, it follows from (
1.6
) that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(S_{1})=m\mathbb{E}(Xe^{\gamma X})=\frac{m}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left(g(e^{\gamma X})\right)\geq\frac{m}{\gamma}g\left(\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})\right)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\log\frac{1}{m}>0.

(2.3)

Therefore, by the strong law of large numbers, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}=+\infty
,
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
-almost surely, which implies that for any
x>0
,
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
-almost surely,

\displaystyle T_{x}^{+}:=\min\{n\geq 0:S_{n}>x\}<\infty.

(2.4)

According to the definition of
T_{x}^{+}
, for every
x,y>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum^{T_{x}^{+}-1}_{n=0}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}\leq x+y\}}=\sum^{T_{x}^{+}-1}_{n=0}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}\leq x\}}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}\leq x\}}.

(2.5)

Noticing that on
\{S_{n}\leq x+y\}
, we have
S_{T_{x}^{+}}>x
. Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{n}-S_{T_{x}^{+}}\leq x+y-x\leq y,

(2.6)

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=T_{x}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}\leq x+y\}}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=T_{x}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}-S_{T_{x}}\leq y\}}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{k+T_{x}}-S_{T_{x}}\leq y\}}.

(2.7)

Combining (
2.5
) and (
2.7
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}\leq x+y\}}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}\leq x\}}+\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{k+T_{x}}-S_{T_{x}}\leq y\}}.

(2.8)

By the strong Markov property,

\{S_{k+T_{x}}-S_{T_{x}}\}_{k\geq 0}

is a random walk with the same distribution as
\{S_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
. Thus, taking expectation on both sides of (
2.8
) yields that

U(x+y)\leq U(x)+U(y).

This completes the proof of (i).

(ii)
Denote by
\lfloor x\rfloor
the greatest integer less than or equal to
x
. It follows from (i) that for any
x\geq 1
,

\displaystyle U(x)=U(\lfloor x\rfloor+x-\lfloor x\rfloor)\leq U(\lfloor x\rfloor)+U(x-\lfloor x\rfloor)\leq\lfloor x\rfloor U(1)+U(1)\leq(x+1)U(1),

where in the second inequality we used the fact that
U
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}
.

\theta>0

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(e^{-\theta S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{\ell-1}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\right)=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(e^{-\theta S_{T_{0}^{+}}}\right)=\frac{1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}]}{\theta\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}

(2.56)

and for any
f\in{\rm DRI}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
with compact support,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(f(S_{\ell}){\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{\ell}\leq 0\}}\right)=\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{I_{\infty}}f(z)\mathrm{d}z\right].

(2.57)

(ii)

If
X
is lattice with span
h>0
, then for any
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-kh}\left(e^{-\theta S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{\ell-1}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\right)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-kh}\left(e^{-\theta S_{T_{0}^{+}}}\right)=\frac{h(1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}])}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}](e^{\theta h}-1)}\quad

(2.58)

and for any non-negative function
f
with compact support,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-kh}\left(f(S_{\ell}){\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{\ell}\leq 0\}}\right)=\frac{h}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\sum_{i=-\infty}^{I_{\infty}/h}f(ih)\right].

(2.59)

Proof.

We first prove (
2.56
) and (
2.58
).
We only prove (
2.56
) here since the proof of (
2.58
) is similar.
Recall the definition of
T_{x}^{+}
in (
2.4
). Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(e^{-\theta S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{\ell-1}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\right)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(e^{-\theta S_{T_{0}^{+}}}{\bf 1}_{\{T_{0}^{+}=\ell\}}\right)=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(e^{-\theta S_{T_{0}^{+}}}\right),

(2.60)

this implies the first equality.
Set
\mathcal{T}_{0}:=0
. Denote by
\mathcal{T}_{n}:=\inf\left\{k>\mathcal{T}_{n-1}:S_{k}>S_{\mathcal{T}_{n-1}}\right\},~n\geq 1
the

be the ladder hight process and

be the corresponding renewal function. Noticing that
\mathcal{H}_{n+1}-\mathcal{H}_{n},~n\geq 0
are i.i.d. equal in law to

(see
[

By the strong Markov property of

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(e^{-\theta S_{T_{0}^{+}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta(S_{T_{x}^{+}}-x)}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\leq x<\mathcal{H}_{n+1}\}}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta(\mathcal{H}_{n+1}-x)}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\leq x<\mathcal{H}_{n+1}\}}\right)

(2.61)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left({\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\leq x\}}e^{-\theta(\mathcal{H}_{n}-x)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\big(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{x-y<\mathcal{H}_{1}\}}\big)\big|_{y=\mathcal{H}_{n}}\right)

(2.62)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,x]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y),

(2.63)

where the second equality follows from the fact on the event
{\{\mathcal{H}_{n}\leq x<\mathcal{H}_{n+1}\}}
, we have
\mathcal{T}_{n}=T_{x}^{+}
.
For each
N>2
and
\delta>0
, firstly noticing that

\displaystyle\sup_{x>N}\int_{[0,x-N]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x>N}\int_{[0,x-N]}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\left(\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)=\sup_{x>N}\int_{N}^{\infty}U^{+}(x-N)-U^{+}(x-z)\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\left(\mathcal{H}_{1}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[N,\infty]}U^{+}(z-N)\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\left(\mathcal{H}_{1}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(U^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{1}){\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\geq N\}}).

(2.64)

Since
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]<\infty
(see
[
5
, p.397, Theorem 2 (ii)]
), by Lemma
2.1
(ii) and the dominated convergence theorem, above yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{x>N}\int_{[0,x-N]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)=0.

(2.65)

Next, we address
y\in(x-N,x]
. By Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_{(x-N,x]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor N/\delta\rfloor+1}e^{\theta j\delta}\left(U^{+}(x-(j-1)\delta)-U^{+}(x-j\delta)\right)\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>(j-1)\delta\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor N/\delta\rfloor+1}e^{\theta j\delta}\delta\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>(j-1)\delta\}}\right)

(2.66)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta\downarrow 0+}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}\int_{0}^{N}e^{\theta z}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>z\}}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

(2.67)

Also, for the lower bound, similarly,

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\int_{(x-N,x]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{H_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{x\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor N/\delta\rfloor}e^{\theta(j-1)\delta}\left(U^{+}(x-(j-1)\delta)-U^{+}(x-j\delta)\right)\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\geq j\delta\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor N/\delta\rfloor}e^{\theta(j-1)\delta}\delta\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\geq j\delta\}}\right)

(2.68)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta\downarrow 0+}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}\int_{0}^{N}e^{\theta z}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\geq z\}}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

(2.69)

Since
\int_{0}^{N}e^{\theta z}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{H_{1}=z\}}\right)\mathrm{d}z=0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_{(x-N,x]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}\int_{0}^{N}e^{\theta z}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>z\}}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

(2.70)

Therefore, by Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(e^{-\theta S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\max_{j\leq\ell-1}S_{j}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{step33}}}{{=}}\lim_{x\to+\infty}\int_{[0,x]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{step11}}}{{=}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_{(x-N,x]}e^{\theta(x-y)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>x-y\}}\right)U^{+}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{step12}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\theta z}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left(e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\bf 1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{1}>z\}}\right)\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}\int^{\mathcal{H}_{1}}_{0}e^{\theta z}\mathrm{d}z\right]

(2.71)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\theta\mathcal{H}_{1}}]}{\theta\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]},

(2.72)

which completes the proof of (
2.56
).

Now we

) and (

For each

\displaystyle\geq\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=1}\sum^{0}_{j=-N_{\delta}+1}\inf_{y\in((j-1)\delta,j\delta]}f(y)\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}(\overline{S}_{\ell}\leq 0,S_{\ell}\in((j-1)\delta,j\delta])

Taking

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(f(S_{\ell}){\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{\ell}\leq 0\}}\right)

Corollary 2.5
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
. Then
\{(S_{T_{0}^{+}},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}),x>0\}
is tight. That is,

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to+\infty}\sup_{x>0}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}>K\right)=0.

(2.79)

Proof.

We only prove the case that
X
is non-lattice here since the proof for the lattice case is similar. Noticing that for any
K>0
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(1-e^{-S_{T_{0}^{+}}/K}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-e^{-1})\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}>K\right).

(2.80)

Also,
from Lemma
2.4
(i) and (
2.60
), it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to+\infty}\lim_{x\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(1-e^{-S_{T_{0}^{+}}/K}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\lim_{K\to+\infty}\frac{K\left(1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\mathcal{H}_{1}/K}]\right)}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}

\displaystyle=0.

(2.81)

Therefore, combining (
2.80
) and (
2
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, there
exist

K_{\varepsilon}>0
and
N_{\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x>N_{\varepsilon}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}>K_{\varepsilon}\right)<\varepsilon.

(2.82)

For
x\in(0,N_{\varepsilon}]
, according to the definition of
T_{0}^{+}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(S_{n}{\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{n-1}\leq 0<S_{n}\}}\right)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(|S_{n}-S_{n-1}|{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n-1}\leq 0\}}\right).

(2.83)

Since
S_{n}-S_{n-1}
is independent of
\sigma(S_{1},...,S_{n-1})
, we conclude from the above inequality that for any
x\in(0,N_{\varepsilon}]
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(|S_{1}|)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}(S_{n-1}\leq 0)=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(|S_{1}|)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}\leq x)

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(|S_{1}|)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}\leq N_{\varepsilon}).

(2.84)

Therefore, combining (
2.82
) and (
2
), we conclude that for any
K>K_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x>0}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}>K\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x>N_{\varepsilon}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}>K_{\varepsilon}\right)+\sup_{x\in(0,N_{\varepsilon}]}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}>K\right)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon+\frac{1}{K}\sup_{x\in(0,N_{\varepsilon}]}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{-x}\left(S_{T_{0}^{+}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\frac{1}{K}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(|S_{1}|)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}\leq N_{\varepsilon}).

(2.85)

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, taking
K\to+\infty
first and then
\varepsilon\to 0
in the above inequality, we arrive at the desired result.

The following renewal theorem will be used in studying the maximal displacement of branching random walk with killing.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\gamma X}]<\infty
.

(i)

If
X
is non-lattice,
then for any
y>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{j}\in(-x,0],1\leq j\leq\ell-1,S_{\ell}>0\}}\Big)=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\left(I_{\infty}>-y\right)\frac{1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\gamma\mathcal{H}_{1}}]}{\gamma\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}]}.

(2.86)

(ii)

If
X
is lattice with span
h>0
,
then for any
i\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{(i-k)h}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{j}\in(-kh,0],1\leq j\leq\ell-1,S_{\ell}>0\}}\Big)=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\left(I_{\infty}>-ih\right)\frac{h(1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[e^{-\gamma\mathcal{H}_{1}}])}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}](e^{\gamma h}-1)}.

(2.87)

Proof.

We only prove the non-lattice case here.
For simplicity, for
N\geq 1
, define

\displaystyle R_{1,N}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{j}\in(-x,0],1\leq j\leq\ell-1,S_{\ell}>0\}}\Big),

\displaystyle R_{2,N}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{j}\in(-x,0],1\leq j\leq\ell-1,S_{\ell}>0\}}\Big).

(2.88)

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{j}\in(-x,0],1\leq j\leq\ell-1,S_{\ell}>0\}}\Big)=R_{1,N}(x)+R_{2,N}(x).

(2.89)

For
R_{1,N}(x)
, noticing that for each fixed
N
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}R_{1,N}(x)=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{j}\in(-x,0],1\leq j\leq\ell-1,S_{\ell}>0\}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{y-x}(S_{\ell}>0)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lim_{x\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{\ell}>x-y)=0.

(2.90)

Now we treat
R_{2,N}(x)
. Define

\displaystyle R_{3,N}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\underline{S}_{N}>-x,\overline{S}_{\ell-1}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\Big),

\displaystyle R_{4,N}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\underline{S}_{N}>-x,\max_{N+1\leq j\leq\ell-1}S_{\ell}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\Big).

(2.91)

Set
T_{-x}^{-}:=\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}:S_{k}\leq-x\right\}
. We mention here that

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{y}\left(T_{0}^{-}=+\infty\right)>0
.
By the Markov property, we see that

\displaystyle\left|R_{2,N}(x)-R_{3,N}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\left(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}({\bf 1}_{\{\underline{S}_{N}>-x,\overline{S}_{\ell-1}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}-{\bf 1}_{\{S_{j}\in(-x,0],1\leq j\leq\ell-1,S_{\ell}>0\}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\left(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{T_{-x}^{-}\in[N+1,\ell-1],S_{\ell}>0\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma(k-1)}\sum_{q=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y}\left({\bf 1}_{\{T_{0}^{-}=q\}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{z}\left(S_{\ell}\in(x+k-1,x+k]\right)\Big|_{z=S_{q}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq U(1)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma(k-1)}\sum_{q=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{y}(T_{0}^{-}=q)=\frac{U(1)}{1-e^{-\gamma}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{y}(T_{0}^{-}\in(N,+\infty)).

(2.93)

Also, noticing that

\displaystyle\left|R_{3,N}(x)-R_{4,N}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\left|\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\underline{S}_{N}>-x,\overline{S}_{\ell-1}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\Big)-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\Big(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\underline{S}_{N}>-x,\max_{N+1\leq j\leq\ell-1}S_{\ell}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\Big)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\left(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}\Big({\bf 1}_{\{\underline{S}_{N}>-x,\max_{N+1\leq j\leq\ell-1}S_{\ell}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}-{\bf 1}_{\{\underline{S}_{N}>-x,\overline{S}_{\ell-1}\leq 0<S_{\ell}\}}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\left(e^{-\gamma S_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{N}>0,S_{\ell}>0\}}\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma(k-1)}\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{y-x}\left(\overline{S}_{N}>0,S_{\ell}\in(k-1,k]\right).

(2.94)

Similarly combining the Markov property and Lemma
2.1
(i), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|R_{3,N}(x)-R_{4,N}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma(k-1)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{y-x}\left({\bf 1}_{\{\overline{S}_{N}>0\}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{z}(S_{\ell}\in(k-1,k])\big|_{z=S_{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{U(1)}{1-e^{-\gamma}}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{y-x}(\overline{S}_{N}>0).

(2.95)

Therefore, combining (
2
) and (

\displaystyle e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{s\leq n}\max_{|u|=s}V(u)\geq cn\Big)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{s\leq n}\max_{|u|=s}(V(u)-s\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])e^{\gamma V(u)}\geq(c-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])ne^{\gamma cn}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{s\leq n}D_{s}^{+}\geq(c-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])ne^{\gamma cn}\right)\leq\frac{1}{(c-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])n}\mathbb{E}\left(D_{n}^{+}\right).

(5.3)

By many-to-one formula (
3.9
), we conclude that

\displaystyle e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)\leq\frac{1}{(c-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])n}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left((S_{n}-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]n)_{+}\right).

(5.4)

According to the ergodic theorem (see the last remark of
[
4
, p.285]
),
\frac{S_{n}-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]n}{n}
converges in
L^{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0})
to
0
as
n\to\infty
. This, combined with (
5.4
), yields that for
c>\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)=0.

(5.5)

If the step size is lattice with span
h
, then by Theorem
1.1
(ii), we have for any
c>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)

\displaystyle\geq\lim\inf_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn-\gamma h\lceil cn/h\rceil}e^{\gamma h\lceil cn/h\rceil}\mathbb{P}(M\geq h\lceil cn/h\rceil)

(5.6)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\gamma h}\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma h\lceil cn/h\rceil}\mathbb{P}(M\geq h\lceil cn/h\rceil)>0,

(5.7)

where
\lceil x\rceil
stands for the smallest integer not less than
x
. This, combined with Theorem
1.1
(i), yields that whether the step size is lattice or not, we always have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)>0.

(5.8)

Above, combined with (
5.5
), entails that for
c>\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn|M>cn)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)}{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)}=0.

(5.9)

Case 2:
c<\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
.
Since
M_{n}\leq M
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn,M_{n}\geq cn)+e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn,M_{n}<cn)

(5.10)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)+e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn,M_{n}<cn).

(5.11)

By the Markov property of the branching random walk, it follows that

\displaystyle e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn,M_{n}<cn)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{P}_{Z_{n}}(M\geq cn){\bf 1}_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0,M_{n}<cn\}})

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|\omega|=n}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{V(\omega)}}(M>cn-1){\bf 1}_{\{M_{n}<cn,Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}\right)

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\sum_{|\omega|=n}e^{-\gamma(cn-V(\omega)-1)}{\bf 1}_{\{M_{n}<cn\}}\Bigg)

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\sum_{|\omega|=n}e^{-\gamma(cn-V(\omega))}{\bf 1}_{\{V(\omega)<cn\}}\Bigg),

(5.15)

where the second inequality follows from from (
3.10
).
Combining (
1.6
) and the many-to-one formula (
3.9
),
we conclude from the above inequality that

\displaystyle e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn,M_{n}<cn)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma}m^{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left(e^{\gamma S_{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{n}<cn\}}\right)

(5.16)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}<cn).

(5.17)

Since
c<\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
, by the strong law of large numbers, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}<cn)=0.
Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn,M_{n}<cn)=0.

(5.18)

This, combined with (
5.10
), yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)-e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)=0.

(5.19)

Since
M_{n}\leq M
, above yields that for
c\in(0,\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn|M>cn)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)}{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)}

(5.20)

\displaystyle=1+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq cn)-e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)}{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}(M\geq cn)}=1,

(5.21)

where in the last equality we used (
5.8
). This completes the proof of the theorem.

\Box

Now we are going to prove Theorem
1.4
. Recall that
M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}:=\max_{|\omega|=i,0\leq i\leq n,\forall u\preceq\omega,V(u)>0}V(\omega)
is the maximum of the branching random walk with killing up to time
n
. Without loss of generality, we only consider the non-lattice case.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Case 1:
c>\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
.

For
c>\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
, similar to (
5
), using
the fact that
M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}\leq M_{n}
, we have for
y>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M_{n}\geq cn)=\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(y+M_{n}\geq cn)

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{s\leq n}D_{s}^{+}\geq\big((c-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])n-y\big)e^{\gamma(cn-y)}\right)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e^{\gamma y}}{(c-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])n-y}\mathbb{E}\left(D_{n}^{+}\right)=0.

where the last equality follows from the many-to-one formula (
3.9
) and the ergodic theorem.
Therefore, similar
to (
5.9
), above yields that for
c>\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}_{n}\geq cn|M^{(0,\infty)}>cn)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}_{n}\geq cn)}{e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn)}=0.

(5.23)

Case 2:
c<\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
.
Similar to (
5.10
), we have that

\displaystyle e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn)=e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn)+e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn,M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}<cn).

(5.24)

According to the fact that
M^{(0,\infty)}\leq M
and (
3.10
), we see that for
c<\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
,

\displaystyle e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn,M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}<cn)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}\left(\sum_{|\omega|=n}{\bf 1}_{\{V(v)>0,\forall v\preceq\omega\}}{\bf 1}_{\{V(\omega)<cn\}}e^{-\gamma(cn-V(\omega))}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma(y+1)}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}\left(\underline{S}_{n}>-y,S_{n}<cn-y\right)

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma(y+1)}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(S_{n}<cn-y),

(5.26)

which tends to
0
by the strong law of large numbers. By Theorem
1.3
, we have for
c<\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M_{n}^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn)-e^{\gamma cn}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}\geq cn)=0.

(5.27)

Similar to (
5.20
), above yields that
for

c\in(0,\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[S_{1}])
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(M^{(0,\infty)}_{n}\geq cn|M^{(0,\infty)}>cn)=1.

(5.28)

This completes the proof of the result.
We are done.

Thermal–exergetic analysis of hemispherical solar still enhanced with activated carbon nanoparticles synthesized from spent tea dust
Authors: Ravishankar Sathyamurthy, Faheemuddin Patel, Haitham MS Bahaidarah, Abbas Saeed Hakeem
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe growing problem of water scarcity worldwide necessitates the continuous development of various technologies to provide effective solutions. Among the various solutions for the desalination process, the solar still is the most prominent due to its cost-effectiveness and sustainable approach. The current experimental study proposes a new technique aimed at improving the functioning of hemispherical solar stills (HSS) by applying the carbonized nanoparticles (ACNPs) made from spent tea dust on the absorber plate. This method enhances the thermal conductivity and absorptivity of the surface. Incorporating ACNPs into the black paint results in increased thermal energy absorption, which raises the temperature of the absorber and water, thus increasing the yield of freshwater. To our knowledge, this is one of the first studies to incorporate waste-derived activated carbon nanoparticle coatings, examining their effects on the thermal and operational efficiency of HSS in realistic outdoor environments. This offers an inexpensive and easily adaptable upgrade to solar desalination systems. The research involved the fabrication of two identical solar stills: one featuring a conventional black surface coating, termed HSS, and the other incorporating activated carbon nanoparticles dispersed in black paint and applied on the absorber. The experimental results revealed that the thermal performance has significantly improved. The modified HSS recorded a peak absorber and water temperature of 74 and 72C, whereas the peak temperatures recorded from the conventional HSS were 71 and 69C, yielding average gains of 4.34% and 4.16% on absorber and water temperature, respectively. The improved heat transfer through evaporation leads to a higher temperature difference between the water and cover (20C (modified HSS) and 18 C (conventional HSS)), which results in an efficient condensation process. Moreover, there has been a substantial improvement in freshwater collection. The modified HSS produced a maximum hourly yield of 1.07 kg/ and a cumulative daily yield of 5.06 kg/, which is 33.67% higher than the conventional HSS yield of 3.72 kg/. This performance boost led to a substantial increase in efficiency. The daily thermal efficiency increased from 32.79% to 49.44%, and the daily exergy efficiency improved from 3.12% to 5.82%. An economic analysis demonstrated the viability of the modified system, with a payback period of 1.98 years and notable long-term savings. The use of spent tea dust for ACNP synthesis highlights the environmental and cost benefits of this approach. The findings highlight the potential of nano-enhanced coatings to enhance solar desalination technologies, offering an effective and sustainable solution for addressing global water challenges.Keywords: Activated carbon, Coating, Heat transfer, Desalination, Water treatmentSubject terms: Engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

The escalating water requirements in multiple sectors such as agriculture, domestic, and industries have led to a substantial surge in demand. This demand has resulted in the exploitation of groundwater resources1–4. Freshwater is an immediate necessity for sustaining human life, especially for drinking5–9. Notably, most of the water available on Earth is in the oceans, and only a few are entrapped in ice caps and glaciers10–13. Only a mere amount of ground and surface water is available for drinking and domestic usage. To address the global demand for drinking water, desalination is a viable solution to produce freshwater for human consumption14,15.

Technologies such as reverse osmosis, multi-stage flash distillation, and electrodialysis have been developed to meet the increasing worldwide need for drinking water. Each of these methods poses challenges, as they tend to rely heavily on expensive, sophisticated infrastructure and consume vast amounts of energy. Contrarily, the solar desalination approach is more sustainable, less expensive, and better suited to remote areas with limited resources. Among the myriad techniques of solar desalination, solar stills are arguably the most straightforward, as they require almost zero maintenance, depend exclusively on sunlight, and are extremely easy to use. Their simple, passive design allows the direct use of solar thermal energy to evaporate and condense water, enabling the decentralized generation of freshwater. Several advanced studies have explored the potential improvements to the thermal performance of solar stills through material enhancements and the integration of energy storage.

Sathyamurthy9 performed a comprehensive experimental evaluation aimed at quantifying the combined roles of sensible and latent heat energy storage in accelerating solar thermal evaporation. The thermal medium consisted of paraffin wax in which zirconia nanoparticles were dispersed at weight loadings of 0.1% and 0.5% to determine conductivity enhancement and stability across thermal cycles. It was observed that the addition of nanoparticles to paraffin wax at a concentration of 0.5% significantly enhanced the thermal conductivity, increasing it to approximately 1.5 times that of pure paraffin wax. However, the latent heat of fusion decreases when the nanoparticles are incorporated in the paraffin wax, which was examined through a DSC analysis. Moreover, when compared to neat paraffin wax, the thermal stability and the melting temperature of the paraffin wax were enhanced by adding nanoparticles. Furthermore, it was also unveiled that incorporating paraffin wax as thermal energy storage in the form of latent heat and rubber sheets as sensible heat improved the daily thermal efficiency from 24.42 to 44.42%. The integration of a dual-phase storage medium–employing enhanced paraffin wax augmented with nanoscale additives and a passive thermal reservoir constituted of a rubber sheet–yielded an increment of 81.90% in thermal efficiency relative to a conventional solar still rejecting stored energy. The incorporation of both nano-enhanced paraffin wax and a rubber actuator absorbs heat within a solar still (SS) and consequently improved the thermal output, yielding increases of 19.3% and 45.01% over configurations employing nano-enhanced paraffin wax alone and over a reference solar still without any thermal storage, respectively.

Banoqitah et al.16 conducted a comparative analysis of stepped solar stills wherein silver nanoparticle-doped paraffin wax was utilized as a phase-change material (PCM) across varying loading fractions. Thermal performance was thereafter modeled through a hybrid machine-learning framework. The study explicitly demonstrated that integrating the Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System with Particle Swarm Optimization (ANFIS-PSO) yielded superior predictive fidelity over the ANFIS standalone algorithm in forecasting both distillate yield and in-still water temperature. The coefficient of determination (R) attained by the ANFIS-PSO method was quantified as 0.981 for the bare stepped solar still (SSS), and augmented to 0.995 and 0.992 for the configurations incorporating PCM and nanoparticle-enhanced PCM (NEPCM), respectively.

Sathyamurthy17 presented a systematic investigation of a silver-nanoparticle-infused paraffin wax storage medium for thermal enhancement in stepped solar stills (SS). Performance was evaluated from thermal, economic, and water-quality perspectives, with the overall objective of increasing freshwater yield and heat storage efficiency. A minimal proportion of silver nanoparticles enhances the latent heat capacity, raises the melting enthalpy, and augments thermal conductivity throughout the phase-change cycles. A systematic assessment of the thermophysical parameters of paraffin wax infused with various silver nanoparticle concentrations is presented, which indicates that thermal stability is maximized at a 2% concentration. Experimental validation employed three configurations, namely: (i) conventional stepped basin with no phase-storage medium, (ii) stepped basin with pure paraffin wax storage charge, and (iii) stepped basin with the silver-enhanced paraffin composite. Incorporating 2% silver (Ag) nanoparticles into paraffin wax yielded a 44% enhancement in thermal conductivity relative to the unmodified paraffin. This augmented conductivity in the composite phase-change material resulted in elevated water temperatures within the stepped solar-still (SS) structure, which in turn accelerated the thermal evaporation of water placed in each stepped compartment of the basin. The effects were found to be available primarily during periods of low solar irradiance, thus improving the energy performance of the thermal-storage and water-desalination system. Analysis indicated that incorporation of nano-enhanced paraffin wax into the stepped-basin design elevated water temperature by 12.2% relative to the unmodified stepped-basin reference (SS) with no thermal energy storage. Correspondingly, under the same operational conditions, the thermal performance assessment–expressed in terms of freshwater yield–revealed that the nano-paraffin composite produced a gain of approximately 54.67% relative to the SS entirely lacking thermal storage; when benchmarked against the SS utilizing conventional paraffin wax, the same configuration delivered a further improvement of 15.59%.

Ismail18 fabricated a transportable hemispherical SS that could produce a freshwater yield ranging from 2.8 to 5.7 kg/ by varying the thickness of the water layer in the basin. The experimental study reported that varying the thickness of the water layer in the basin significantly affects the thermal performance and freshwater yield. The impact of charcoal particles derived from the guava tree wood and carbonized corncobs for thermal performance enhancement from hemispherical SS was investigated experimentally by19. The activated carbon derived from the corncobs was derived using NaOH and HCl solution. Their study aimed to explore the feasibility of using charcoal particles in the design and operational aspects of hemispherical SS. The highest thermal efficiency and cumulative freshwater were found to be 41.19% and 4.45 L/m, respectively, by incorporating carbonized corncobs as a surface coating on the absorber plate of hemispherical SS.

Beggas et al.20 incorporated spent aluminum residue positioned within the basin of a solar still as a solid thermal energy storage medium to enhance thermal performance and freshwater production. By incorporating waste aluminum mass, the strategy achieves improved thermal stratification and an extended time for energy release, promoting sustained heat within the solar still. In parallel, the enhanced evaporation rate contributed to elevated thermal efficiency and freshwater yield from the modified hemispherical solar still. Experimental data indicate that the device utilizing aluminum residue as thermal energy storage medium achieved an average daily freshwater yield of 6.15 kg/m and an overall thermal efficiency of 52.18%. By contrast, the conventional hemispherical solar still exhibited a corresponding yield of 4.15 kg/m and a thermal efficiency of 35.36%.

Sathyamurthy et al.21 developed a method to improve the performance of solar stills by using soda cans filled with phase change material (PCM) and coated with soot derived from engine exhaust dispersed in black paint. Three Single Slope Solar Stills (SSSS) were constructed. The first did not include PCM, the second had PCM cans that were not coated, and the last contained PCM cans that were coated. The characterization results demonstrated that soot nanoparticles, with sizes ranging from 25 to 60 nm, were deposited uniformly. The still considered to have the best performance, as indicated by the coated PCM cans, achieved water and absorber temperatures of 57C (52C with the still unmodified), and maintained elevated temperatures even at night due to the PCM. The water generation yield observed in the coated system, 0.89 -hr, was nearly 2 times that of the unmodified still, which was 0.45 -hr. Similarly, 6.01 of freshwater was cumulatively yielded, surpassing the uncoated PCM and no-PCM setups by 102.3% and 75.7%, respectively. The daily thermal efficiencies observed were 65.61%, 56.96%, and 32.42% for the coated, uncoated, and conventional still, respectively.

Thakur et al.22 derived activated carbon nanoparticles synthesized from bio-waste through a chemical reduction method and coated them in the absorber plate of a conventional single slope SS for improving the thermal performance. Attia et al.23 used a concave-shaped absorber instead of a flat absorber in the hemispherical condensing cover SS. Along with the concave-shaped absorber, wick material was spread over the absorber, and PCM was placed beneath the concave absorber to augment the thermal performance. Their study revealed that the PCM beneath the concave-shaped absorber acted as thermal energy storage in the form of latent heat, while the wick surface acted as an effective thermal evaporator through the capillary rise. The experimental findings on freshwater yield revealed that the wick and TES as an enhancement technique showed an increase of about 87% when compared to the conventional flat absorber hemispherical SS. Furthermore, when analyzing the energy and exergy efficiencies, there was a significant enhancement of 85.6 and 128.2%, respectively, using the synergetic effect through capillary effect and TES. Different marble pieces as TES on augmenting the thermal performance of hemispherical SS was experimentally assessed by Attia et al.24. Their study revealed that on incorporating the red marble pieces as a TES in hemispherical SS, the freshwater gain was found to be 61.4%, whereas, while using black marble pieces as TES, the gain on freshwater was found to be 44.79% compared to the hemispherical SS without TES.

Recent investigations have focused on the systematic redesign and material optimization of solar stills to elevate both thermal efficiency and the net freshwater yield. Noteworthy contributions include the works of Elsawy et al.19 and Attia et al.24, who embed activated carbon and phase change materials within hemispherical still assemblies and record marked enhancements in both thermal efficiency and potable water yield. Simultaneously, Thakur et al.22 synthesized activated carbon nanoparticles from biowaste and integrated these nanoparticulates into the absorbers of flat-plate desalination units, reporting superior solar energy capture and volumetric productivity. Generally, the literature highlights that coating nanostructured activated carbon onto the surface of absorber plates significantly elevates thermal absorptivity, thereby collectively driving higher solar still yields. Interestingly, the majority of peer-reviewed investigations have either emphasized flat-plate or single-slope configurations, while the coating on a hemispherical solar still (HSS) is still unexplored. Moreover, comprehensive studies evaluating the complete sustainability supply chain of waste-origin ACNPs, particularly those derived from discarded tea bags and similar feedstocks, have been scarce. To fill these identified research gaps, the present work systematically quantifies both thermal and hydrodynamic metrics of the HSS system outfitted with ACNP-coated absorber plates, thereby providing a cost-competitive and ecologically sound enhancement to the projected thermal cycle of solar desalination systems. This work advances and broadens earlier investigations by employing spent tea dust as the principal carbon substrate, thereby enhancing system efficiency and reinforcing the principles of sustainable biosystems.

The current experimental investigation evaluates the application of activated carbon synthesized from spent tea-leaf biomass via a chemical reduction technique, incorporated into the absorber layer of a hemispherical, clear acrylic cover solar still. Seawater serves as the source feed under the outdoor climatic regime of Coimbatore, India. The daily thermal energy and exergetic performance metrics of the hemispherical-cover solar still have been benchmarked against a conventional solar still configuration, thereby quantifying the effect of the introduced activated carbon nanofiller on the thermal plates.

Materials and methods

Synthesis and surface morphology of activated carbon from spent tea dust

Activated carbon nanoparticles (ACNPs) were produced from waste tea dust via KOH–assisted activation followed by high-temperature pyrolysis, and their surface morphology was examined. Briefly, tea dust was washed, oven-dried, and sieved; the precursor was impregnated with KOH at a tea dust:KOH mass ratio of 1:1.5 in deionized water and aged for 2 h. The impregnated material was then pre-carbonized in a muffle furnace at 550C for 1 h (10C/min), cooled, rinsed, and dried (110C, 6 h). The dry char was transferred to a tubular furnace and pyrolyzed/activated at 750C for 2 h under a flowing nitrogen atmosphere. The resultant carbon was washed with dilute HCl and deionized water until the pH reached neutrality and then dried to yield ACNP powder. The sample is then examined through FE-SEM to analyze the surface morphology. The graphical representation of the prepared nanoparticle is shown in Fig. 1. The obtained nanoparticles morphological structure is examined through Carl Zeiss, Sigma with Gemini-300, with In Lens detector. The FE-SEM images of the activated carbon clearly indicated that the obtained particles are in nanostructure with a size ranging from 50-60 nm with a mesoporous structure (Fig. 2a, b). Also, the Energy-dispersive X-ray analysis (EDAX) analysis clearly shows that the obtained nanoparticle has a majority of carbon content in it (Fig. 2c). SEM observations reveal a highly rough, defect-rich micro/mesoporous texture with numerous pits and edges characteristic of KOH activation. Such roughness increases diffuse light scattering and decreases effective reflectance, while structure porosity improves the spreading of thin films of water and the efficient transfer of heat at the water-solid interface. These features of the ACNP coating, together with its water-absorbing porous structure, also enhance solar absorption and evaporation when coupled with the modified HSS.

Preparation of nanofluid and coating of absorber

An analytical measuring device weighs precisely 1.0 g of activated carbon nanoparticles, then disperses this mass within 100 mL of turpentine oil under continuous agitation from a magnetic stirrer for 30 minutes. To further refine the uniformity of the resulting suspension, a probe sonicator is employed, subjecting the mixture to ultrasound for a total duration of 1 hour. Following sonication, 50 mL of the sonicated nanofluid is aliquoted and blended with 500 mL of commercially prepared black paint. The composite suspension is then stirred magnetically for an additional 20 minutes to ensure homogeneity. The resulting nanoparticle-enriched black paint is subsequently loaded into the spray gun’s pressurized canister. Using a steady, controlled nozzle aperture, the suspension is evenly atomized onto the absorber plate surface of a hemispherical cover solar still to form a continuous, adherent coating of the composite absorber. Experimental setup

Two identical hemispherical solar stills are fabricated with dimensions of 0.33 m in diameter and 0.2 m in height, and it is fabricated using a 1.5 mm thick mild steel sheet. Both black paint and nano-enhanced black paint (utilizing activated carbon nanoparticles) are applied to coat the bottom basin absorber to enhance solar absorption. A drain valve is provided at the bottom to clean the basin, and fresh seawater is fed through the inlet valve. The vapor evaporated from the water surface is collected as water droplets in the hemispherical cover made of 0.33 m in diameter and 0.16 m in height. The hemispherical cover is made with a transparency of 88% and 3 mm thick. Insulation is provided on the outer surface of the circular basin and at the bottom to avoid heat loss. A rubber seal is provided between the cover and basin to avoid the loss of vapor, and it acts as a sealant. It is ensured that the cover, water, and absorber temperatures are measured in the conventional absorber without surface coating and the surface-coated absorber every 1 hour. The experiment was conducted in May 2023 under the typical bright and sunny conditions of the Coimbatore region, located in southern India. In the summer months, Coimbatore experiences a tropical wet and dry climate, which is characterized by high solar irradiance. As of May, the average daily solar radiation is between 6 and 7 kWh//day, and ambient temperatures range from 30C to 36C. This climate is ideal for assessing the performance of solar desalination systems. The solar radiation is measured with a pyranometer (Kipp-Zonen type), and the temperature of the SS elements is measured using an RTD (PT-100 type) sensor. Mechanism of solar energy absorption from coated absorber on fresh water generation

Compared to a conventional black paint absorber, the coating of activated carbon nanoparticles (ACNP) introduces a hierarchical micro/mesoporous roughness that amplifies the optical path length and promotes multiple scattering of incident solar radiation. The improved conductive heat fluxes and water temperature increase are largely due to the enhanced solar energy absorption by the absorber plate, which in turn improves the absorber temperature. Increased water temperature elevates both the saturated vapor pressure and the thermal driving force across the cover–liquid air gap. This condition enhances the evaporation heat and mass transfer term, as detailed in the Equations. 4, 5 and 6–thereby enhancing cumulative distillate output. Additionally, the nanoscale roughness and porosity enhance interfacial wetting and local heat exchange at the water–solid boundary, thereby reducing effective thermal resistance and accelerating the transient heat-up process. This causal chain explains the observed increases in operating temperatures and freshwater yield for the modified HSS (black paint + ACNP) versus the conventional HSS (black paint only).

Results and discussion

Comparison of water, absorber, cover temperature, and yield

The variance in solar radiation and ambient temperature recorded during the experiment is shown in Fig. 5a. The solar radiation and ambient temperature are recorded between 7:00 Hrs and 18:00 Hrs, while the maximum and minimum solar radiation measured are 1030 and 124 W/m, respectively. The average solar radiation during the experiment is found to be 712.1 W/m. Similarly, the ambient temperature ranged between 30 and 36C with an average ambient temperature of 32.8C. Glass, water, and absorber temperature are the critical parameters that affect the solar still’s performance as they are more significant due to their direct influence over condensation and evaporation. Optimal temperatures of these parameters are essential for achieving a higher yield of freshwater and energy efficiency. Temperature balance ensures efficient heat transfer, which in turn influences the rates of evaporation and condensation. Efficient control of these parameters is key to maximizing the solar still performance and higher freshwater yield.

Comparison of hourly variations in (a) solar radiation, wind velocity, ambient temperature, (b) absorber, (c) water, and (d) glass temperature from conventional HSS (with black paint coating) and the modified HSS (with absorber plate surface coated using activated carbon nanoparticles).

The primary function of the absorber plate within the HSS (Hemispherical Solar Still) revolves around absorbing incoming solar radiation. This absorbed energy is subsequently converted and transferred as thermal energy, primarily dedicated to heating water within the system. Essentially, the absorber plate acts as the core component responsible for harnessing solar radiation and transforming it into usable heat, thereby facilitating the water heating process within the HSS. A higher absorber plate temperature facilitates efficient heat transfer to water, thereby accelerating the rate of evaporation. Therefore, maintaining a higher absorber plate for maximized freshwater output is essential. The results of the recorded absorber plate temperature are shown in Fig. 5b for the conventional HSS (with black paint coating) and the modified HSS (with absorber plate surface coated using activated carbon nanoparticles), respectively. The observed data indicate that with a solar radiation intensity of 1030 W/m, the modified HSS with an absorber plate coated with activated carbon nanoparticles in black paint achieved a maximum absorber temperature of 74C. In contrast, the HSS utilizing an absorber coated solely with black paint reached a slightly lower maximum temperature of 71C under the same solar radiation conditions. This 3C difference highlights the superior thermal absorption and retention capacity of the activated carbon nanoparticle (ACNP) coating compared to conventional black paint. The evidence shows that the activated carbon nanoparticle-coated absorber plate has a temperature advantage, which demonstrates its better efficiency at capturing and storing thermal energy relative to the plain black paint-coated absorber in the above case. The coating applied to the absorber utilizing black paint and activated carbon nanoparticles yielded an increase of 3C in the overall temperature of the absorber. This increase in temperature indicates the enhanced heat retention and absorption properties of the activated carbon nanoparticles as compared to black paint alone on the absorber plate. For the modified HSS in comparison to the conventional HSS, the average temperature increase of the absorber plate is approximately 4.34%. Similarly, using the effective coating on the plate of absorber with nanoparticles doped on black paint leads to an increment of average absorber temperature by about 63C, whereas, using the absorber without any coating of nanoparticles on the absorber, it is found to be 60.25C. Other studies, like those by Arani et al.32 and Arunkumar et al.33, showed that the thermal absorption rate by surfaces improved tremendously upon the application of a nanoparticle coating. Their research showed that when nanoparticle coatings were applied to the absorber plates of a solar still, the temperature of the absorber, as well as the rate of evaporation, drastically increased. The research also showed that a higher rate of heat transfer to the water improved heat retention. In the current research, the activated carbon nanoparticle increased the heat and absorption, while in the work by Shoeibi et al.34, the CuO nanoparticle coating on the copper heat pipe within the solar still of the solar still enhanced evaporation, and heat transfer during the process obtained substantial improvement.

The temperature of the water contained in the HSS obviously impacts the evaporation. It can be inferred from the literature that higher water temperatures result in greater rates of evaporation. This can be explained by the fact that the higher thermal energy available in the water is sufficient to promote the phase transition. Similarly, higher evaporation results in higher freshwater yield. The water temperature findings in the HSS indicate that the absorber plate coated with activated carbon nanoparticles reached a peak of 72C (Fig. 5c). In contrast, when employing an absorber plate coated solely with black paint, the maximum water temperature recorded was 69C (Fig. 5c), which is lower than that achieved with the absorber plate coated with activated carbon nanoparticles in black paint. During the high solar radiation period, the addition of activated carbon nanoparticles (ACNP) to the coating of the absorber plates in HSS improved the water temperature by about 4.16%. The average water temperature during this period reaches 62.1. In comparison, employing an absorber plate coated solely with black paint yields a slightly lower average water temperature of 59.4C. This difference of approximately 2.7C indicates a tangible temperature advantage for the activated carbon nanoparticle-coated absorber plate in facilitating higher water temperatures within the HSS. The average water temperature increment in the HSS employing the coated absorber plate exhibits a 4.34% rise compared to the HSS lacking any surface coating. This differential temperature change highlights the effectiveness of the coating, indicating its ability to enhance heat absorption and transfer within the system, resulting in a measurable improvement in overall water temperature.

The glass cover in the SS helps in trapping the vapor within the enclosed chamber. The results of recorded glass cover temperature from modified HSS with absorber coated using activated carbon nanoparticle is found as 52C (Fig. 5d), whereas, for the conventional HSS it is found as 53C (Fig 5d). With the improvement in the water and reduction on cover temperature from the HSS using the effective coating of the absorber, the difference in temperature of water and glass temperature elevated to 20C, however, without any coating of nanoparticles on the absorber, the maximum difference in temperature between water and cover is found as 18C. Moreover, the average cover temperature from the HSS using the absorber coated using the activated carbon derived from the spent tea dust doping on black paint has been reduced by about 2.3% compared to the average cover temperature from the conventional HSS. However, the influence of the plate of the absorber coated using activated carbon nanoparticles synthesized from spent tea dustfrom the spent tea dust shows that the average difference in water and cover surface temperature is augmented by about 6.64%.

The solar stills with ACNP nanocoating and those with a traditional black paint coating both have experimental and theoretical temperature profiles for the water, absorber, and cover components. These profiles exhibit similar overarching trends alongside some minimal discrepancies. For all three components, systems with ACNP coatings reached higher temperatures than those with black paint, both in experimental and theoretical datasets. This indicates greater thermal absorption and retention owing to the ACNP’s more dominant properties. Typically, the experimental values differ slightly from the theoretical values due to external factors like ambient temperature changes, heat loss, and measurement inaccuracies–factors insufficiently addressed in the models. Regarding the peak values over midday, the ACNP coating also exhibits higher peaks relative to the black paint, thereby efficiently absorbing solar radiation, particularly for the upper bound values of the curve related to peak value attainment for water and the absorber. While the cover’s temperatures are also lower than the absorber and water, they follow the same trends with minimal discrepancies between experimental and theoretical values, demonstrating lesser influence by the coating and greater control from ambient and circulation internal thermal forces.

During the peak solar radiation, the conventional HSS coated with black paint generated a maximum of 0.92 kg/m of freshwater, while the modified HSS equipped with an absorber plate coated with activated carbon nanoparticles in black paint achieved a higher yield of 1.07 kg/m under the maximum solar radiation of 1030 W/m. The improvement in freshwater produced from the modified HSS with the surface of the absorber coated using black paint with activated carbon is due to the augmented surface temperature of the absorber and water temperature. Furthermore, the activated carbon in black paint is evenly distributed for higher surface roughness and thermal conductivity for improved solar energy absorption, which leads to an increased temperature of the absorber and water. Improved water temperature leads to a higher rate of evaporation and an increase in maximum hourly freshwater produced by 13.71% compared to conventional HSS with black paint coating. The larger temperature differential between water and glass cover (20C from the ACNP-coated absorber over 18C from the black paint-coated absorber) enhances condensation efficiency due to faster heat dissipation. During the sunshine hours, the average freshwater produced from HSS using the absorber coated with ACNP particles in black paint is found to be 28.43%, whereas, during the lower solar radiation period, the average freshwater generation is found to be 51.89% compared to conventional HSS with black paint coating. Similarly, the accumulation of freshwater produced from conventional HSS with black paint coating and with the absorber coated using ACNP particles is found to be 3.72 and 5.06 kg/m, respectively. The augmentation in freshwater produced from the absorber plate coated using activated carbon NPs in the black paint placed in the modified HSS is found to be 33.67% compared to conventional HSS. Moreover, the activated carbon derived from the tea dust exhibits excellent absorbance and has high porosity, significantly improving the absorber plate’s thermal conductivity by coating. Compared to other nanoparticles, the synthesis of activated carbon is more advantageous as it is derived from the spent tea dust through the chemical reduction method, which reduces the cost of the nanoparticle. The reduced cost makes it more suitable for solar thermal applications to enhance thermal performance.

This increase in the thermal efficiency of the system during the peak solar hours is due to an increase in the evaporation rate of water and an increase in the solar energy absorption by the absorber plate due to an increase in the temperature of the water. Moreover, the freshwater yield from the modified HSS is further enhanced due to the coating technique, which enhances the thermal efficiency of the system.

Variation in hourly exergy evaporation and exergy efficiency

Exergy is a measure of the maximum useful work obtainable from a system as it moves towards thermal equilibrium with its environment. In the context of a solar still, exergy evaporation refers to the portion of the absorbed solar energy that is effectively utilized for the evaporation process. This concept accounts not only for the energy absorbed but also for the quality and effectiveness of that energy in terms of performing useful work, such as water evaporation. The hourly exergy evaporation from the HSS coated using activated carbon nanoparticles and black paint is plotted in Fig. 8a. The maximum exergy evaporation from the HSS with and without surface coating is found to be 78.4 and 63.7 W at 14:00 Hrs. This clearly helps to identify the effect of coating to make the water evaporate by absorbing most of the solar radiation by storing the heat energy through the nanocoating.

Variance in the (a) Exergy evaporation and (b) exergy efficiency on an hourly basis of conventional HSS (with black paint coating) and the modified HSS (with absorber plate surface coated using activated carbon nanoparticles).

Exergy efficiency, also called second-law efficiency, assesses how much a system converts available solar energy into work relative to the system’s capability to do work. While thermal efficiency is limited by the amount of heat transferred, exergy efficiency considers the energy’s quality, as well as the process’s irreversibility. The hourly exergy efficiency of the HSS coated using activated carbon nanoparticles and black paint is calculated using Equation. 18 and the results are plotted in Fig. 8b. It may be inferred that the influence of nanocoating of the absorber improved the exergy efficiency when compared to the HSS with black paint coating. At 14:00 Hrs, the exergy efficiency reached 9.56 and 7.3% for HSS with and without surface coating, and this increase in the hourly exergy is mainly due to the effect of energy absorbed by the substrate to improve the thermal evaporation from the water surface.

Economic metrics

The economic feasibility of a solar still system depends on the balance between its capital cost, operational costs, productivity, and the economic savings it provides over its lifespan (Table. 3). Factors such as water yield, initial investment, maintenance requirements, and local water pricing play a significant role in determining its viability. For the Modified HSS using activated carbon nanoparticle (ACNP) coatings, the goal is to evaluate how the added cost of materials and fabrication is offset by higher water production efficiency and long-term savings. The Modified HSS with activated carbon nanoparticle coating demonstrates a slightly lower cost of water and a shorter payback period due to the enhanced freshwater yield. Although the initial investment for the Modified HSS is higher, the long-term savings and productivity gains make it a more cost-effective option. The analysis highlights the importance of optimizing ACNP synthesis costs further for scalability and adoption. This economic analysis demonstrates that employing activated carbon nanoparticles synthesized from spent tea dust is a sustainable and economically viable improvement for HSS systems.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the effectiveness of the hemispherical solar stills was improved due to the incorporation of surface coatings such as activated carbon nanocomposites. Through experimental evaluation, it became evident that coated absorber plates, compared to their uncoated counterparts, consistently yielded higher temperatures. The absorber plates that have been coated with activated carbon nanoparticles have exhibited exceptional thermal absorption properties, leading to a noticeable increase in temperatures within the system. This enhancement subsequently contributes to the improvement of water temperatures, which is crucial for various applications. Additionally, the application of these coatings is essential as they significantly enhance evaporation rates, thereby playing a critical role in influencing the overall freshwater yield of the system. The coated surfaces facilitate better energy absorption from solar radiation, intensifying the heat transfer to the water within the still. This enhancement in energy absorption and transfer has a positive impact on the evaporation process, leading to increased freshwater production. The comparative analysis of absorber plates, coated over the uncoated, highlights how surface coatings can positively transform the efficiency and total yield of hemispherical solar stills. Future recommendations

In subsequent work, an extensive thermal simulation of the hemispherical solar still apparatus will be performed to enhance and check the experiments. The experiment will be accompanied by computational simulations that will demarcate the heat transfer interactions between the absorber plate, water layer, and transparent dome systems, thus elucidating in detail the temperature distributions, energy losses, losses, and phase change processes. Simulated results for the temperature profiles will corroborate the experimental data, and thus the model will assist in evaluating the system’s thermal performance along with determining some possible modifications that can be made to optimize the configuration. The enhanced model will further aid in the design revisions and expansion for the solar desalinator employing activated coating materials like carbon nanoparticle coatings.

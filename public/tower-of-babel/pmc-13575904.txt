Sex Differences in Growth Recovery among Children with Crohn’s Disease
Authors: Dong-Uk Kim, Jin Soo Moon, Yeoun Joo Lee, Yon Ho Choe, Hyeji Lim, Minsoo Shin, Soon Chul Kim, Hye Ran Yang, Eell Ryoo, Yoo Min Lee, Ji-Sook Park, Ki Soo Kang, Tae Hyeong Kim, Seak Hee Oh, Ben Kang, So Yoon Choi, Yoon Lee, Ju Young Chang, Jae Sung Ko, Jung Ok Shim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/AimsGrowth impairment remains a major concern in pediatric Crohn’s disease (pCD) patients, yet long-term growth trajectories and sex-specific treatment responses have not been adequately characterized.MethodsThis study investigated 2-year growth trajectories and determinants of catch-up growth from a nationwide registry in Korea. Growth outcomes were analyzed in 533 children with pCD using multivariable linear regression and generalized additive models (GAM).ResultsAt diagnosis, 16.1% and 4.1% of patients had weight-for-age and height-for-age z-scores less than –2, respectively. Weight recovery was more readily achieved than linear growth during follow-up (median z-score change 0.61, p<0.001 vs –0.01, p=0.582). Females presented with lower baseline weight z-scores than males (–1.00 vs –0.63; p=0.001) but displayed superior weight recovery (0.81 vs 0.51; p<0.001) and significant height recovery (0.09 vs –0.08 in males; p<0.001). In the multivariable analysis, the baseline weight z-score, platelet count, albumin, and disease activity predicted weight recovery (R²=26.3%), while the baseline height z-score was the strongest predictor of height recovery. The GAM analysis suggested that infliximab use was associated with enhanced height recovery (β=0.104, p=0.045), with benefits observed primarily in females aged <14 years (β=0.339, p=0.044).ConclusionsThis Asian cohort study with the largest population to date demonstrates that weight recovery precedes linear growth, with females showing superior catch-up. Notably, our findings highlight a selective association between infliximab treatment and height recovery in females younger than 14 years, suggesting sex- and age-specific differences in the treatment response that warrant further investigation. (Clinical trial registration Korea Clinical Research Information Service KCT0008723)Keywords: Crohn disease, Pediatrics, Growth Disorders, Sex Characteristics, Infliximab

INTRODUCTION

Crohn’s disease is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory disorder that can involve any segment of the gastrointestinal tract.1 The incidence of Crohn’s disease has been rising rapidly worldwide, particularly among children and adolescents, partially attributed to increased awareness and diagnostic capabilities.2,3 Compared with adult-onset disease, pediatric Crohn’s disease (pCD) typically shows more extensive involvement, an aggressive course, and a higher risk of complications, including strictures and fistula formation.4-6 Importantly, management in children and adolescents requires consideration not only of mucosal healing and symptom control but also of optimal growth and pubertal development, nutrition, and psychosocial well-being.7-9

Among these priorities, growth impairment remains a major concern in pCD,7,10 with 15%–40% of children at diagnosis meeting criteria for growth failure, far exceeding the 3%–10% reported in ulcerative colitis.11 The pathophysiology of growth impairment in pCD is multifactorial, including reduced caloric intake due to abdominal pain and decreased appetite, malabsorption secondary to mucosal inflammation, chronic systemic inflammation with elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines, and functional resistance of the growth hormone-insulin-like growth factor-1 axis.11 Notably, linear growth appears to be more resistant to recovery compared to weight gain, reflecting the complex interplay between chronic inflammation and the heightened sensitivity of growth plates during critical developmental periods.12,13

Recently, diagnostic characteristics of pCD have been reported from the CARE-KIDS (Childhood & Adolescent REgistry of Korean Inflammatory Bowel DiSease) registry, which is the largest cohort covering approximately 70% of incident cases of pediatric inflammatory bowel disease in Korea.14,15 In that study, perianal disease modifiers were more common in boys and often served as diagnostic clues leading to earlier diagnosis, milder luminal disease, and less growth impairment. By contrast, females presented with more severe disease activity and greater growth impairment. Based on these sex-specific baseline features, the present study aimed to comprehensively analyze growth outcomes for pCD. This study aimed to evaluate 2-year growth outcomes in Korean children with Crohn’s disease and to identify determinants of catch-up growth, especially regarding sex- and age-specific differences.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study population

This study utilized data from the CARE-KIDS registry, a multicenter inception cohort of pediatric inflammatory bowel disease patients across Korea, which has been described in detail previously.14 The registry was established through collaboration among 19 tertiary care pediatric hospitals and university medical centers. Patient enrollment commenced in January 2021, with data collection ongoing through June 2025. The study population consisted of patients who met the following criteria: (1) newly diagnosed with Crohn's disease according to the revised Porto criteria;16 (2) younger than 18 years at the time of diagnosis; and (3) availability of complete 2-year longitudinal growth data for analysis. Additionally, patients with a history of growth hormone use or those currently receiving growth hormone therapy were excluded.

The CARE-KIDS cohort was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of all participating hospitals (IRB number: 2020GR0246), and informed consent was obtained at enrollment. This secondary analysis using de-identified data was also approved (IRB number: E-2505-015-1636). The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. This cohort is registered with the Korea Clinical Research Information Service (KCT0008723).

2. Anthropometric assessment and definition of growth recovery

Anthropometric measurements, including weight and height, were obtained at diagnosis and at scheduled follow-up visits over 2 years. All measurements were converted to standardized z-scores for weight-for-age and height-for-age using the 2017 Korean National Growth Charts developed by the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency.17 Growth recovery was defined as the change (Δz-score) in weight-for-age and height-for-age z-scores from baseline (diagnosis) to the 2-year follow-up. Catch-up growth was assessed by calculating the difference in z-scores between these two time points, with positive values indicating improvement and negative values indicating deterioration in growth parameters.

3. Clinical evaluation and variable definitions

All study participants received a thorough gastrointestinal assessment following the updated Porto criteria.16 The evaluation protocol involved a comprehensive approach comprising esophagogastroduodenoscopy, colonoscopy, and dedicated small bowel imaging studies such as magnetic resonance enterography, computed tomographic enterography, capsule endoscopy, or device-assisted enteroscopy when appropriate. Biopsies were obtained from all accessible gastrointestinal segments. Disease classification followed the Paris classification system,18 including age at diagnosis (A1a/A1b/A2), disease location (L1/L2/L3 with L4a/L4b modifiers), disease behavior (B1/B2/B3), and perianal disease modifier (p).

Laboratory evaluation included complete blood count (white blood cell count, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and platelet count), inflammatory markers (erythrocyte sedimentation rate, C-reactive protein, and fecal calprotectin), and nutritional parameters (serum albumin and 25-hydroxyvitamin D). Disease activity was quantified using validated pediatric-specific indices, including the Pediatric Crohn's Disease Activity Index for clinical assessment and the Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn's Disease for endoscopic evaluation.

Treatment included nutritional therapy (exclusive enteral nutrition or partial enteral nutrition), corticosteroid induction therapy, 5-aminosalicylic acid compounds, azathioprine, methotrexate, infliximab, and adalimumab (including biosimilar formulations). Treatment decisions were made at the discretion of the attending pediatric gastroenterologist based on disease severity, location, patient-specific factors, and treatment response patterns. No uniform treatment protocol was mandated across centers, and decisions regarding the initiation and duration of nutritional therapy, corticosteroid tapering, and biologic therapy were not standardized.

4. Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the characteristics of the study population. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables are expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). For group comparisons, the chi-square test or Fisher exact test was used for categorical variables, and the Mann-Whitney U test was applied for continuous variables. For paired comparisons, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used. Spearman correlation coefficients were calculated to assess associations between baseline clinical parameters and growth recovery outcomes. Multivariable linear regression and generalized additive models (GAM) were used to identify independent predictors of growth recovery. All models were adjusted for age, baseline anthropometric measures (weight- or height-for-age z-scores), hemoglobin, platelet count, and albumin. To account for potential non-linear relationships, smoothing terms were applied in GAM. Subgroup analyses were stratified by sex and pubertal status, which was approximated using chronological age cutoffs (<14 years for females and <16 years for males) as a proxy for pubertal stage, based on established pediatric growth references.19 Model fit was evaluated using adjusted R² and deviance explained. A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

1. Growth patterns and recovery over 2-year follow-up

At diagnosis, 533 patients with pCD had baseline weight-for-age z-scores, with 71.5% falling below the population median (z-score <0) (Fig. 1A); specifically, 41.1% were below –1, 16.1% below –2, and 3.6% below –3. Height-for-age z-scores showed a lower prevalence of linear growth deficits, with 56.0% below the median (Fig. 1B); 21.8% were below –1, 4.1% below –2, and 0.4% below –3. We next investigated growth outcomes during the 2-year follow-up period (Fig. 1C and D). Weight-for-age z-scores increased from a median of –0.75 (IQR, –1.64 to 0.12) at diagnosis to –0.11 (IQR, –0.87 to 0.69) at 2 years, with a median change of 0.61 (IQR, 0.11 to 1.19) (p<0.001). By 2 years, the proportion of patients with weight-for-age z-scores below –1 decreased to 21.2%, below –2 to 4.9%, and below –3 to 0.9%, approaching the expected distribution in healthy populations (approximately 15.9%, 2.3%, and 0.1%, respectively). In contrast, height-for-age z-scores showed minimal change, from a median of –0.14 (IQR, –0.86 to 0.62) to –0.11 (IQR, –0.73 to 0.57), with a median change of –0.01 (IQR, –0.25 to 0.30) (p=0.582). At 2 years, 19.7% remained below –1, 3.2% below –2, and 0.6% below –3 for height-for-age.

Given the notable sex-based differences observed in baseline characteristics, with females presenting with more severe growth impairment at diagnosis,14 we next investigated sex-specific growth trajectories (Table 1, Fig. 1E-H). At diagnosis, females had significantly lower weight-for-age z-scores (median, –1.00; IQR, –1.84 to –0.36) compared to males (median, –0.63; IQR, –1.49 to 0.26) (p=0.001), while height-for-age z-scores were similar between sexes (females: median, –0.14; IQR, –0.82 to 0.61; males: median, –0.14; IQR, –0.89 to 0.64) (p=0.881). During the 2-year follow-up, distinct recovery patterns emerged. For weight recovery, both sexes showed significant improvement, but females demonstrated greater improvement with a median z-score change of 0.81 (IQR, 0.26 to 1.45) compared to 0.51 (IQR, 0.03 to 1.07) in males (p<0.001). Regarding height recovery, only females exhibited significant improvement with a median change of 0.09 (IQR, –0.12 to 0.35) (p<0.001), while males showed no significant change with a median of –0.08 (IQR, –0.30 to 0.25) (p=0.145).

Taken together, patients with baseline weight deficits achieved substantial catch-up growth in weight-for-age, nearly normalizing to the expected population distribution, while height-for-age showed minimal change over 2 years. Notably, females demonstrated superior recovery in both growth parameters despite presenting with more severe baseline weight deficits.

2. Factors associated with growth recovery

We evaluated associations between growth recovery and disease phenotype, baseline biochemical markers, and treatment modalities to identify potential predictors of weight and height outcomes. Growth outcomes were assessed according to the Paris classification (Supplementary Fig. 1). Disease location showed no significant differences in weight or height recovery. Age-stratified analysis showed that A1b and A2 patients had better weight recovery (p=0.080), while A1a patients exhibited better height improvement (p=0.066). Patients with complicated disease behavior (B2 or B3) demonstrated better weight recovery compared to those with non-stricturing, non-penetrating disease (B1) (p<0.001). Perianal disease showed a trend toward poorer recovery (p=0.085 for weight, p=0.061 for height), and previous perianal surgery was significantly associated with worse outcomes in both parameters (p=0.038).

Regarding biochemical markers and clinical scores (Supplementary Table 1), hemoglobin (ρ=–0.269, p<0.001), albumin (ρ=–0.362, p<0.001), and vitamin D (ρ=–0.192, p<0.001) exhibited significant negative correlations with weight-for-age z-score improvements. In contrast, platelet counts (ρ=0.282, p<0.001), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ρ=0.278, p<0.001), C-reactive protein (ρ=0.299, p<0.001), calprotectin (ρ=0.224, p<0.001), Pediatric Crohn's Disease Activity Index (ρ=0.340, p<0.001), and Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn's Disease scores (ρ=0.260, p<0.001) showed positive correlations. For height recovery, only hemoglobin (ρ=–0.260, p<0.001), albumin (ρ=–0.124, p=0.004), and platelet counts (ρ=0.125, p=0.004) showed significant correlations with height-for-age z-score improvements, while inflammatory markers and clinical scores revealed weak or non-significant associations. These findings indicate that patients with more severe baseline malnutrition (lower hemoglobin, albumin, and vitamin D) and higher inflammatory burden achieved greater subsequent weight recovery, while height recovery was less responsive to baseline disease severity markers.

Analysis of treatment effects on growth outcomes revealed differential impacts across therapeutic interventions (Supplementary Fig. 2). For weight recovery, patients not receiving steroid induction therapy showed significantly better weight-for-age z-score improvement (p=0.032), with a median change of 0.73 (IQR, 0.13 to 1.32) compared to 0.51 (IQR, 0.08 to 1.09) in those who received steroid induction. Infliximab therapy was also associated with weight improvement (p=0.045). However, nutritional therapy (whether exclusive enteral nutrition or partial enteral nutrition) showed no significant association with weight outcomes. Similarly, 5-aminosalicylic acid compounds, azathioprine, methotrexate, and adalimumab showed no significant associations with weight outcomes.

For linear growth, infliximab was the only treatment modality significantly associated with height recovery (p=0.003). Patients receiving infliximab demonstrated better height-for-age z-score improvement overall, despite presenting with more severe baseline disease, including higher rates of ileocolonic involvement (85.7% vs 73.3%; p=0.001), complicated behavior (19.4% vs 11.4%; p=0.011), perianal disease (46.0% vs 33.8%; p=0.005), and elevated inflammatory markers (Supplementary Table 2). Baseline growth status was comparable between groups (height z-score: –0.19 vs –0.09, p=0.244; weight z-score: –0.84 vs –0.69, p=0.144). Overall, infliximab therapy was associated with more favorable linear growth trajectories in this cohort.

3. Independent predictors of growth recovery

To identify independent predictors of growth recovery while accounting for baseline disease severity and treatment factors, we performed multivariable analyses. For weight recovery, lower baseline weight-for-age z-score emerged as the strongest independent predictor of weight recovery (β=–0.191; 95% CI, –0.254 to –0.129; p<0.001), indicating greater catch-up growth in patients with more severe baseline weight deficits (Table 2). Higher platelet count (β=0.0009; 95% CI, 0.0002 to 0.0016; p=0.009) and elevated Pediatric Crohn's Disease Activity Index scores (β=0.007; 95% CI, 0.002 to 0.012; p=0.007) also remained independently significant predictors. Lower albumin levels (β=–0.205; 95% CI, –0.375 to –0.034; p=0.019) further contributed to the predictive model. The model explained 26.3% of the variance in weight recovery.

Given the limited explanatory power of the linear model for height recovery (12.6% vs 26.3% for weight), we employed GAM to explore potential non-linear relationships. GAM modeling demonstrated marked improvement, with explained variance more than doubling (adjusted R²=0.271) (Table 4). This improvement was driven by pronounced non-linear associations for baseline height (effective degrees of freedom=5.439, p<0.001) and age (effective degrees of freedom=2.439, p=0.048). Additionally, sex emerged as a significant predictor (β=–0.164, p=0.009), with males showing poorer height recovery compared to females. Critically, GAM analysis suggested a modest association between infliximab therapy and height recovery (β=0.104; 95% CI, 0.002 to 0.206; p=0.045), which did not reach significance in the corresponding linear model. Given the significant interactions between age and sex in height recovery and the influence of pubertal growth spurts, we hypothesized that infliximab's efficacy might vary across different developmental stages and between sexes, warranting stratified subgroup analyses.

4. Infliximab effects on height recovery by age and sex

To explore the subgroup-specific effects on height recovery, we performed stratified analyses by age and sex using GAM. Using age-specific cutoffs (14 years for females, 16 years for males) as a proxy for growth potential,19 infliximab therapy was associated with greater height recovery only in females aged <14 years (deviance explained=26.5%, β=0.339; 95% CI, 0.015 to 0.663; p=0.044) (Table 5). No significant effects were observed in other subgroups: females aged ≥14 years (β=–0.006, p=0.927), males aged <16 years (β=0.125, p=0.111), or males aged ≥16 years (β=–0.052, p=0.533).

We also evaluated the impact of infliximab treatment duration on height-for-age z-score at 2 years among patients who received infliximab therapy (Supplementary Fig. 3). Longer treatment duration in females was associated with height-for-age z-scores progressively stabilizing and converging toward the population median, whereas no such duration-dependent trend was observed in males.

DISCUSSION

In this 2-year longitudinal study of 533 Korean pCD patients, weight impairment was more prevalent than linear growth deficits at diagnosis, with 16.1% presenting with weight-for-age z-scores below –2 compared to only 4.1% with height-for-age z-scores below –2. The prevalence of growth failure in pCD has declined markedly over time, from 49%–88% in 1990s studies to 8%–10% (height z-score <–2) by 2009–2016.11 Also, recent German population-based data showed that approximately 10% of CD patients had height z-scores <–2 and nearly 20% had weight z-scores <–2.12 Our Korean cohort demonstrated comparable or even lower rates of severe growth impairment at diagnosis, likely reflecting advances in early detection and timely initiation of effective therapy. Over the 2-year follow-up period, weight deficits showed substantial improvement, with distribution patterns normalizing toward those of healthy populations. Our findings demonstrate that weight recovery represents catch-up growth in children presenting with nutritional deficits and severe inflammation at diagnosis, with adequate therapy effectively correcting disease-related weight deficits.

The unique clinical patterns of pCD in Asian populations further underscore the significance of these findings. Although sex-based disparities in incidence and perianal modifiers have been documented previously, including in our previous report,14 as well as in large-scale Asian registries demonstrating male predominance, evidence regarding sex-specific catch-up growth trajectories remains scarce.20,21 Recent pooled analyses have emphasized that sex-based differences in inflammatory bowel disease incidence can vary significantly by age and geographic region, yet longitudinal growth data for Asian children are often excluded from such global comparisons.22 In a recent nationwide multicenter cohort study from Japan, which identified a consistent association between small intestinal involvement and growth impairment in CD (n=145, 69.7% male), sex-specific differences in recovery patterns were not specifically addressed.23 Our study, the largest Asian cohort to date, provides evidence that females possess a robust compensatory growth potential.

We observed distinct sex-based patterns in growth recovery. Despite presenting with more severe baseline weight deficits, females demonstrated greater catch-up growth compared to males in both weight and height. Previous studies have reported conflicting findings regarding sex differences in pCD, with some demonstrating increased risk of growth failure in males, others finding more severe disease course in females, and recent studies showing no significant sex differences in clinical outcomes.22,24-28 This heterogeneity likely reflects differences in genetic backgrounds, environmental factors, and study populations across geographic regions. Our findings of superior female growth outcomes are consistent with previous reports suggesting that sex-specific pathways lead to growth impairment in pCD.29,30 Our large Korean cohort demonstrates that adequate therapy can effectively restore growth in both sexes, with females showing greater compensatory capacity despite initial disease severity.

The underlying mechanisms for these sex-specific recovery patterns likely involve distinct molecular and hormonal pathways. Specifically, growth deficits in males are often mediated primarily through the growth hormone-insulin-like growth factor-1 axis, whereas in females, they may be more closely linked to the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis.30 The superior catch-up observed in our female cohort may be further influenced by ethnicity-specific factors, although the nature and extent of such contributions remain unclear.31 Whether population-level differences contribute to the sex-specific patterns observed, or account for apparent contrasts with some Western reports, requires further investigation. Further study is needed to clarify the roles of genetic background, environmental factors, and gene-environment interactions in these findings. Our results suggest that with appropriate treatment, clinically meaningful growth recovery can be achieved in both sexes, with females demonstrating substantial catch-up despite presenting with more severe baseline disease.

Regarding linear growth recovery, our analysis suggested that potential benefits of infliximab may be limited to specific patient subgroups. Anti-tumor necrosis factor therapy has been established as an important option for addressing growth failure when initiated early in the disease course.32,33 However, reported benefits appear confined to particular developmental windows, with infliximab’s positive effects mainly observed in patients at earlier stages of maturation and absent in those with more advanced maturation.34 In our cohort, exploratory subgroup analyses indicated that infliximab use was associated with greater height recovery only in females younger than 14 years, who likely possess greater residual growth potential, whereas this effect was not observed in other age–sex strata. This pattern differs from a previous study that reported infliximab-related growth benefits predominantly in male patients,29 and may reflect differences in study design (e.g., longitudinal growth velocity versus bone age-based assessment), population characteristics, or residual confounding. Taken together, these observations raise the possibility that sex- and age-related factors modulate growth responses to biologic therapy, but mechanistic explanations remain speculative and require confirmation in prospective, controlled studies.

Despite these important findings, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the 2-year follow-up period may not have been sufficient to assess long-term growth trajectories or final adult height, which represents a critical outcome in pCD. Second, maturation status was estimated using chronological age rather than Tanner staging. These age-based cutoffs might not precisely reflect the actual pubertal stage, potentially leading to biological ambiguity or misclassification of individual maturation status. Third, the observational design precludes causal inference regarding treatment effects, including infliximab. Treatment allocation was based on clinical decision-making rather than randomization, which may have introduced selection bias, and variation in the duration of each treatment regimen may also have affected outcomes. Fourth, the limited number of patients receiving adalimumab may have resulted in insufficient statistical power to detect clinically meaningful effects, particularly in subgroup analyses.

In conclusion, this largest Asian nationwide cohort study reveals distinct sex-specific patterns of growth recovery in pCD. Our 2-year longitudinal analysis demonstrates that weight recovery is more readily achieved than linear growth, reflecting catch-up growth in children with baseline nutritional deficits and severe inflammation. Females showed superior recovery in both weight and height despite presenting with more severe baseline weight deficits. Notably, our identification of a selective association between infliximab therapy and linear growth recovery in prepubertal females (younger than 14 years in our study) suggests potential sex- and developmental stage-specific differences in treatment response. These findings underscore the need to move beyond one-size-fits-all treatment paradigms toward personalized approaches that consider sex, pubertal status, and population-specific factors.

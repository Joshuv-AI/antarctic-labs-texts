A coherent theory of tent spaces and homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
Luca Haardt

Abstract
. We introduce and systematically investigate a scale of tent spaces that characterizes homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces F ˙ p , q β \dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q} . These spaces generalize the classical spaces of Coifman, Meyer, and Stein, and are shown to be equivalent to the weighted tent spaces with Whitney averages developed by Huang. We show that these tent spaces follow a functional analytic theory that mirrors that of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, including duality, embeddings, discrete characterizations, John–Nirenberg-type properties, as well as real and complex interpolation. Furthermore, we provide a novel characterization of the endpoint spaces F ˙ ∞ , q β \dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q} , completing earlier work by Auscher, Bechtel, and the author.

1.
Introduction

In
[
4
]
, the authors introduced new scales of function spaces that naturally arise in the characterization of homogeneous Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.
Characterizations of homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
have attracted significant interest in recent decades due to their instrumentality in areas such as harmonic analysis, partial differential equations, and more.
Traditionally, these spaces are defined via discrete Littlewood–Paley blocks
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
that are, in fact, convolution operators with a kernel compactly supported away from zero in Fourier-space, see the monograph of Triebel
[
22
, Sec. 5]
. In contrast, for applications to partial differential equations, it is of interest to have characterizations with respect to a continuous spectrum and using kernels without compactness of the Fourier-support. For example, kernels whose Fourier-transform vanishes sufficiently fast in zero and infinity, such as

\displaystyle k_{t}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(|t\xi|^{N}e^{-|t\xi|^{2}}\big)(x),\quad\text{for $t>0$ and some $N\in\mathbb{N}$,}

are of particular interest, because they lead to characterizations via the well-known Gauss–Weierstrass semigroup.

First continuous characterizations of Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces for general kernels of this kind were given by Triebel in
[
23
, Sec. 2.4.2 + 2.5.1]
in the range
1<p<\infty
,
1<q\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
(
p=\infty
is included for Besov spaces). They were later complemented by Ullrich
[
24
]
and Hui–Taibleson
[
8
]
for the full range of parameters
0<p\leq\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
. Interestingly, these articles provide several characterizations for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, which always have a corresponding counterpart for Besov spaces, except for one. More precisely, this additional characterization for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces uses (weighted) tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. They were first introduced by Coifman, Meyer and Stein
[
11
]
in the unweighted case
\beta=0
, and later studied by many other authors in the weighted case
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, see for instance
[
1
,
16
]
.
Roughly speaking, the tent space characterization reads

\displaystyle f\in\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(\Phi_{t}\ast f)(x)\in\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta},

where
\Phi
is a suitable kernel and
\Phi_{t}=t^{-d}\Phi(\cdot/t)
, see Section
2
for more details. In this construction,
(\Phi_{t}\ast f)(x)
is an extension of
f
to the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
, and
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
the extension space of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
. However, a corresponding
“tent space characterization”
for the closely related Besov spaces
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
has remained a notable gap in the theory. This gap was finally resolved in
[
4
]
, where the authors introduced a new scale of function spaces, denoted by
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
(see Definition
3.2
below), which characterize homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
in the sense that

\displaystyle f\in\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(\Phi_{t}\ast f)(x)\in\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}

for any
0<r\leq\infty
. These spaces are extensions of weighted
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces, which were first introduced by Barton and Mayboroda to analyze boundary value problems with
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,p}
-data, and later systematically studied by Amenta
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
]
). In particular, Amenta showed that weighted
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces can be recovered by real interpolation of weighted tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
. To extend this behavior to the scale of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces, the authors in
[
4
]
introduced weighted tent spaces with an additional parameter
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
(see Definition
3.1
). Furthermore, they demonstrated that the scale of function spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
is equivalent to the weighted tent spaces with Whitney average
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
developed by Huang
[
17
]
, and characterizes homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
. However, the characterization only covers the case
p<\infty
. We will complement this result by providing a corresponding endpoint characterization in the sense that

\displaystyle f\in\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(\Phi_{t}\ast f)(x)\in\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}

for any
0<r\leq\infty
, see Theorem
2.5
. Here, the space
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
is defined as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}=\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

As an illustration, we obtain a Gauss–Weierstrass characterization of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}
within the space
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
in the case
\beta<0
, see Proposition
2.7
.

1.1.
Tent spaces and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces

In
[
4
]
, a comprehensive theory for the spaces
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
was developed, demonstrating a rigorous consistency with the theory of homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
. This means that properties such as duality, embeddings and interpolation on the level of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces possess one-to-one counterparts within the
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
scale. By contrast, the function space theory for tent spaces still exhibits significant gaps when compared to the well-studied theory of homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
. Our work aims to resolve these discrepancies. To highlight this parallel development, the subsequent sections are structured as follows: we first review the well-known theory of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, then we present our new findings for
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces in direct comparison, and finally identify the specific gaps in the existing literature that our results effectively close.

1.1.1.
Interpolation

We introduce the following parameters. For
0<p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
,
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
define

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}},\quad\beta_{\theta}=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}.

The interpolation theory of homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces is well established; comprehensive treatments can be found in
[
22
]
,
[
14
]
, and the references therein.
Combining
[
14
, Cor. 6.7]
and
[
22
, Sec. 5.2.5]
, we can deduce the following identities:

•

[\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{\beta_{0}},\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}=\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{\theta},q_{\theta}}^{\beta_{\theta}}
if
\max\{q_{0},p_{0}\}<\infty
or
\max\{q_{1},p_{1}\}<\infty
,

•

(\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{0},q}^{\beta},\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{1},q}^{\beta})_{\theta,p_{\theta}}=\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{\theta},q}^{\beta}
if
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<q\leq\infty
,

•

(\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p,q_{0}}^{\beta_{0}},\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p,q_{1}}^{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}=\dot{\mathrm{B}}_{p,q}^{\beta_{\theta}}\quad
if
\beta_{0}\neq\beta_{1}
and
0<p,q\leq\infty
.

We establish analogous identities for the spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
in Section
3.1
and
3.5
, which are summarized as follows:

(i)

[\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}=\mathrm{T}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}
if
\max\{q_{0},p_{0},r_{0}\}<\infty
or
\max\{q_{1},p_{1},r_{1}\}
,

(ii)

(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta,p_{\theta}}=\mathrm{T}^{p_{\theta},q,r}_{\beta}
if
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<q,r\leq\infty
,

(iii)

(\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}=\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta_{\theta}}
if
\beta_{0}\neq\beta_{1}
and
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
,

see Proposition
3.6
, Theorem
3.28
and Proposition
3.30
, respectively.
We compare these with existing tent space results. First, complex interpolation
(i)
was already developed by Huang for weighted tent spaces with Whitney averages, denoted by
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
, see
[
17
, Thm. 3.6]
. Second,
(ii)
is known for two parameter tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
, see for example
[
10
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, a similar result for
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
-spaces is unknown. Finally,
(iii)
was already proven in
[
4
, Prop. 4.4]
but only in the range
0<p<\infty
.

1.1.2.
Discrete characterizations

A powerful tool in the analysis of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces is the so-called
\varphi
-transform, see for example
[
14
]
. It connects the continuous space
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}
to the sequence space
\mathrm{\dot{f}}_{p,q}^{\beta}
, providing a discrete characterization of Triebel–Lizorkin spaces. Here, the space
\mathrm{\dot{f}}_{p,q}^{\beta}
is defined as the set of all sequences
(s_{Q})_{Q\in\square}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\|(s_{Q})_{Q}\|_{\mathrm{\dot{f}}_{p,q}^{\beta}}=\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\mathbf{\tilde{1}}_{Q}(\cdot)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}<\infty\qquad(0<p<\infty),

\displaystyle\|(s_{Q})_{Q}\|_{\mathrm{\dot{f}}_{\infty,q}^{\beta}}=\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\Big(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\mathbf{\tilde{1}}_{Q}(\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

where in the latter case the sum runs over all dyadic cubes
Q\in\square
that are contained in
P
, and
\mathbf{\tilde{1}}_{Q}(x)=|Q|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}
is a
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-normalized cut-off function associated to
Q
. Similar discrete characterizations of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
will be obtained in Proposition
3.12
and
3.13
and read as follows:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\bigg\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(\cdot)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

if
0<p<\infty
, and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Setting
s_{Q}=|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}
allows us to transfer many properties of the sequence spaces
\mathrm{\dot{f}}_{p,q}^{\beta}
to our tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
. These include embeddings (Section
3.3
), duality (Section
3.4
) and real interpolation (Section
3.5
), as well as a John–Nirenberg-type property for the endpoint spaces
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
(Proposition
3.17
), which reads as follows:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

holds for any
0<\alpha<\infty
. To the best of our knowledge, the relationship between the sequence spaces
\mathrm{\dot{f}}_{p,q}^{\beta}
and tent spaces has not been investigated before.

1.1.3.
Duality

Triebel–Lizorkin spaces possess a rich duality theory, which can be found in the monograph of Triebel
[
22
, Sec. 2.11]
and complemented by Frazier–Jawerth
[
14
, Sec. 5]
. Their duality theory can be summarized as follows:

(1.1)

\displaystyle(\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q})^{\prime}\simeq\begin{cases}\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{-\beta}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}&\quad\text{if }1\leq p<\infty\text{ and }0<q<\infty,\\
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}_{\infty,\infty}&\quad\text{if }0<p<1\text{ and }0<q<\infty.\end{cases}

Here,
q^{\prime}
denotes the Hölder conjugate exponent of
q
defined by
1=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}
in the case
q\in(1,\infty)
and
q^{\prime}=\infty
if
q\in(0,1]
. Another central point of this article is to explore the corresponding duality theory for
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces. Due to the powerful discrete characterizations of tent spaces, as described above, we obtain the following duality theory:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\begin{cases}\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}&\quad\text{if }1\leq p<\infty\text{ and }0<q<\infty,\\
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}&\quad\text{if }0<p<1\text{ and }0<q<\infty,\end{cases}

where
1\leq r<\infty
, see Theorem
3.23
and
3.25
. We want to remark that duality results for
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
-spaces are only known for parameters in the Banach range
1\leq p,q,r<\infty
. In addition, duality results for two-parameter tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
spaces are only known for
1\leq q<\infty
.

1.1.4.
Embeddings

It is well-known that Triebel–Lizorkin spaces have a variety of function space embeddings. For instance, they possess a nesting property in the micro-local parameter
q
, that means

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p,q_{0}}^{\beta}\subset\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p,q_{1}}^{\beta}

for
0<p\leq\infty
,
0<q_{0}\leq q_{1}\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. We will show in Lemma
3.19
, that the nesting property

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta}

holds for
0<r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
and the other parameters as above. To the best of our knowledge, these were unknown in this generality for tent spaces. Moreover, Triebel–Lizorkin spaces are known to satisfy so-called Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev-type embeddings. These can be regarded as an extension of the usual Sobolev embedding to other function spaces. Roughly speaking, they describe a continuous embedding of a function space
X_{0}=X(p_{0},\beta_{0})
into another space
X_{1}=X(p_{1},\beta_{1})
, where
X_{1}
has more integrability than
X_{0}
in the sense that
p_{0}<p_{1}
but loses regularity (
\beta_{0}>\beta_{1}
). The corresponding embeddings for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces are the following: for
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\quad\text{if}\quad\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}},

see for example
[
18
, Thm. 2.1]
. Remarkably, the micro-local parameters
q_{0},q_{1}
can be chosen arbitrary without any constrains. In contrast, on the level of tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
, Amenta
[
1
]
proved the following Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev-type embeddings:

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\quad\text{if}\quad\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}},\quad q_{1}=q_{0}.

They were later improved by Fraccaroli in
[
12
]
to the case
q_{1}\leq q_{0}
, but still do not resemble the Triebel–Lizorkin result. However, using our three-parameter tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, we can prove an analogous Triebel–Lizorkin result on the level of tent spaces, which covers the known cases, and reads as follows: for
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
,
0<q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\quad\text{if}\quad\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}},

see Theorem
3.18
. Finally, there are also mixed-type embeddings, which allow one to switch between the function scales
\dot{\mathrm{F}}_{p,q}^{\beta}
and
\dot{\mathrm{B}}_{p,q}^{\beta}
. In particular, for
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta_{0}}_{p_{0},q}\hookrightarrow\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta_{1}}_{p_{1},p_{0}}\quad\text{if}\quad\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}},

as well as

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{1}}\hookrightarrow\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta_{1}}_{p_{1},q}\quad\text{if}\quad\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}.

The first embedding was proven by Jawerth in
[
18
, Thm. 2.1 (iii)]
and the second by Franke in
[
13
, Thm. 1]
. We derive equivalent mixed-type embeddings for tent and
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces in Theorem
3.22
, that are summarized as follows: for
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
0<r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},p_{0},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\quad\text{if}\quad\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}},

as well as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},p_{1},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\quad\text{if}\quad\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}.

We remark that mixed-type embeddings for two-parameter tent and
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces were proven in
[
2
, Thm. 2.34]
. However, they do not seem to be optimal with regard to the Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin theory.

1.2.
Roadmap and methods

Our paper subdivides into two parts: First, Section
2
provides a characterization of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}
in terms of the spaces
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
. Second, we conduct a systematic study of functional-analytic properties of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
in Section
3
. This includes properties such as completeness, density, and complex interpolation (Section
3.1
), discrete characterizations (Section
3.2
), embeddings (Sections
3.3
), duality (Section
3.4
), as well as real interpolation (Section
3.5
).

Characterization of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}

This part relies heavily on the preliminary work by Ullrich and coauthors
[
20
]
and a John–Nirenberg-type property for Triebel–Lizorkin and tent spaces at the endpoints. The latter property is used to lower the assumptions on the local means in the characterization, enabling Gauss-Weierstrass characterizations (Proposition
2.7
). In
[
20
]
, the authors have derived powerful characterizations for Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin–Hausdorff spaces using maximal functions. With them at hand, it is fairly straightforward to show the inclusion of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}
into our four parameter tent space. The converse inclusion is harder as we have to dominate the pointwise maximal function by averages, which is unfortunately lengthy and technical. The characterization that we obtain in the end is Theorem
2.5
.

Functional-analytic properties of tent spaces

In Section
3.1
, we first use the equivalence of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces and weighted tent spaces with Whitney averages
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
to transfer known basic properties. These include completeness, dense subspaces, duality in the Banach range as well as complex interpolation theory. Additionally, we prove quantitative “change of angle” formulas by combining basic integral estimates with covering arguments adapted to the geometry of Whitney boxes.

In Section
3.2
, we develop discrete characterizations of tent spaces as a general tool, which follow from straightforward covering arguments (Proposition
3.12
and
3.13
). Along with the theory of sequence spaces, these lead to characterizations via “discrete local means” (Proposition
3.15
) and John–Nirenberg-type properties for
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
spaces (Proposition
3.17
).

In Section
3.3
, we present Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev-type embeddings (Theorem
3.18
) and mixed-type embeddings of tent and
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces (Theorem
3.22
) that are consistent with the Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin theory described above. Their proofs rely on distributional descriptions of tent spaces combined with duality, convexity and real interpolation techniques.
This procedure is inspired by
[
18
, Thm. 2.1]
and
[
13
, Thm. 1]
, where the authors show analogous results for Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

Section
3.4
develops a duality theory in the non-Banach range by embedding our tent spaces into simpler spaces for which duality results are already known. For the case
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<q<\infty
, we embed them into vector-valued Lebesgue spaces (see Theorem
3.23
). For
0<p<1
and
0<q<\infty
, we embed quasi-Banach tent spaces into quasi-Banach
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces or in tent spaces in the Banach range, for which duality theory is already known (see Theorem
3.23
).

Finally, in Section
3.5
, we develop real interpolation results. There are two of them. The first (Theorem
3.28
) interpolates in the parameter
p
while fixing the parameters
q,r
and
\beta
. It is based on the “discrete local mean” characterization of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
from Section
3.2
and direct estimates of the “best approximation” functional
E
. The second (Proposition
3.30
) fixes the parameters
p,r
while allowing flexibility for the other parameters
q,\beta
. Its proof is based on a nesting property of tent and
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces and follows the procedure as in the proof of
[
4
, Prop. 4.4]
.

1.4.
Notation

We use the following notation throughout the paper.
We denote by
\mathbb{N},\mathbb{N}_{0},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
the set of all positive integers, all non-negative integers, all integers, all real numbers, and all complex numbers, respectively. Throughout,
d\geq 1
denotes the dimension of the underlying Euclidean space. We write
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}=(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for the upper half-space. We denote the open ball centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
t>0
by
B(x,t)
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the set of all dyadic half-open cubes of generation
k
as

\displaystyle\square_{k}\coloneqq\{2^{k}x+[0,2^{k})^{d}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\},

and denote by
\square=\cup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\square_{k}
the family of all dyadic cubes. Moreover, we denote by

\displaystyle\bar{Q}\coloneqq\Big(\frac{\ell(Q)}{2},\ell(Q)\Big]\times Q

the associated Whitney box of a cube
Q
.
We use the symbol
\|\cdot\|_{X}
for the (quasi-)norm of a (quasi-)normed space
X
, and
X^{\prime}
as its anti-dual space. For a measure space
\Omega
and a Banach space
X
we denote by
\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega;X)
the space of all strongly measurable functions with the usual identification (that is, functions equal almost everywhere are identified). Moreover, for
0<p\leq\infty
, we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;X)

\displaystyle=\bigg\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega;X):\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}=\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\bigg\},

with the usual modification if
p=\infty
. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by
p^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
the unique number such that
1=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
. Moreover, for
p\in(0,1]
, put
p^{\prime}\coloneqq\infty
. Finally, if
p=\infty
, then set
p^{\prime}\coloneqq 1
.
If
X
is a quasi-Banach space, we also define for
0<p\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the weighted vector-valued sequence spaces
\ell^{p}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};X)
as follows:

\displaystyle\ell^{p}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};X)\coloneqq\Big\{x:x=(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\ \text{with}\ x_{k}\in X,\,\|x\|_{\ell^{p}_{\beta}(\mathbb{Z};X)}\coloneqq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k\beta p}\|x_{k}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\},

again with the usual modification if
p=\infty
.
For
0<p,q\leq\infty
, we denote by
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\ell^{q})
the space of all sequences of complex-valued Borel measurable functions
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\|(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\ell^{q})}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{k}(x)|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

with the usual modifications in the infinite cases.
For two real numbers
a,b
, we write
a\lesssim b
if there exists a constant
C>0
, whose precise value may vary from line to line but depends at most on the structural constants, such that
a\leq Cb
. Conversely, we write
a\gtrsim b
if
Ca\geq b
. Furthermore, we write
a\simeq b
if both
a\lesssim b
and
a\gtrsim b
hold.

2.
Characterization of endpoint Triebel–Lizorkin spaces

In this section, we provide a continuous characterization of endpoint Triebel–Lizorkin spaces with respect to general kernels with non-compact Fourier support. This complements the characterization
[
4
, Thm. 2.4]
. We start with a brief introduction of the following objects from harmonic analysis as in
[
4
, Sec. 2]
.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Schwartz space and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
its topological dual space, the space of tempered distributions. Furthermore, for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):[{\mathrm{D}}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(f)](0)=0,\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}$ with $|\alpha|\leq N$}\},

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform and
{\mathrm{D}}^{\alpha}=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\partial_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
. In this context, we also define
\mathcal{S}_{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{S}_{N}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the topological dual space of
\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equipped with the subspace topology of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It can be identified with
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\mathcal{P}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of all polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of degree at most
N
with complex coefficients, see
[
22
, Chap. 5]
. If
\varphi
and
\psi
are integrable functions, then their convolution
\psi\ast\varphi
is defined as

\displaystyle(\psi\ast\varphi)(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x-y)\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

If both
\psi
and
\varphi
belong to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then their convolution also belongs to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, the convolution can be generalized to
(\psi,f)\in\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
via
(\psi\ast f)(x)=f(\psi(x-\cdot))
, which is well-defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and has at most polynomial growth.

Finally, we introduce the definition of the Peetre maximal function for dilations. Fix
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, we define for
a>0
and
t>0
the Peetre maximal function as

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\Psi_{t}^{*}f)_{a}(x)=\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Psi_{t}\ast f)(x+y)|}{\Big(1+\frac{|y|}{t}\Big)^{a}}\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where we set
\Psi_{t}(\cdot)\coloneqq t^{-d}\Psi(\cdot/t)
.

The following definition of the endpoint Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is due to Frazier and Jawerth
[
14
]
. If
1<q<\infty
, this definition is equivalent to the one introduced by Triebel in
[
22
, Sec. 5.1.4]
.
For further properties of these spaces, we refer to
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
22
, Sec. 5.1.4]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that

(1)

\varphi_{k}(x)=\varphi_{0}(2^{-k}x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(2)

\supp\varphi_{0}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:2^{-1}\leq|x|\leq 2\}
,

(3)

\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{k}(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

and set
\Phi_{k}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{k})\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
0<q<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we define the endpoint homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin space as

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}}<\infty\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{Q\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{Q}\sum\limits_{k=-\log_{2}(\ell(Q))}^{\infty}2^{k\beta q}|(\Phi_{k}\ast f)(x)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Remark 2.2
.

We excluded the case
q=\infty
because the corresponding definition for
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equivalent to
\dot{\mathrm{B}}^{\beta}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see for example
[
8
]
), and the latter space was already characterized in
[
4
, Thm. 2.2]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
0<q,\alpha<\infty
and
(g_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
a family of measurable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, we define

\displaystyle\|(g_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{X^{q,\alpha}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{Q\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{Q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=-\log_{2}(\ell(Q))}^{\infty}|g_{k}(x)|^{q}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Notice that
\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}}=\|(2^{k\beta}\Phi_{k}\ast f)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{X^{q,q}}
.

As a technical tool for our characterization of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we recall the following convolution inequality from
[
20
, Lem. 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<q,\alpha<\infty
and
\delta\in(\frac{d}{\alpha},\infty)
. Suppose that
(g_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a family of measurable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then one has

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta}g_{k}\Big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{q,\alpha}}\lesssim\|(g_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{X^{q,\alpha}}.

In
[
4
]
, homogeneous Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces are characterized by continuous families of convolution operators
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
where the kernels decay fast enough at zero and infinity. In particular, a scale of tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
was introduced (see Section
3
below) to characterize homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the case
0<p<\infty
. However, a corresponding endpoint characterization for
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to this tent space scale is still missing. The following theorem provides this missing characterization.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<q<\infty
,
0<r\leq\infty
and
R\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{-1\}
such that
R+1>\beta
. Furthermore, let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\Phi(\xi)|>0\quad\text{on}\quad\Big\{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<|\xi|<\varepsilon\Big\}

for some
\varepsilon>0
, and

(2.3)

\displaystyle[D^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\Phi](0)=0\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}$ with $|\alpha|\leq R$.}

Then, the space
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be characterized as follows (with the usual modifications if
r
is infinite):

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|\Phi_{s}\ast f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}<\infty\bigg\},

where

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{s}\ast f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}(\Phi_{s}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Remark 2.6
.

We clarify how the characterization identity has to be understood.

•

In this theorem, the homogeneous space on the left-hand side is understood as a realization within
\mathcal{S}_{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
R
is as in the statement. This realization aligns with the framework presented in
[
20
, Lem. 3.4]
in the following sense. By
[
20
, Lem. 3.4]
, we can realize
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
within the space
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
M=\max\{\lfloor\beta\rfloor,-1\}
and
\lfloor\beta\rfloor
denotes the integer part of
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Because
M\leq R
by the bullet point below, we have the embedding
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{S}_{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and can thus realize
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
within
\mathcal{S}_{R}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As a consequence, the identity in Theorem
2.5
is understood as a set-theoretic equality in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with corresponding equivalence of norms.

•

We claim that
M\leq R
, which implies
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Indeed, if
M=-1
then
M\leq R
holds trivially. In the case
M=\lfloor\beta\rfloor
, assume for the sake of contradiction that
M>R
. Since
M,R\in\mathbb{Z}
, this implies
R+1\leq M
and thus

\displaystyle\beta<R+1\leq M\leq\beta,

which is impossible.

Before proving Theorem
2.5
, we want to mention that the assumptions of the theorem agree with those of
[
4
, Thm. 2.4]
.
This allows us to use their techniques and derived identities as a black box for our proof. However, the reader is advised to keep a copy of the mentioned references at hand.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
X
denote the space on the right-hand side in the statement. Then we have to show the equivalence
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=X
. To do so, we will divide the proof into two steps. The first step proves the continuous embedding
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\supset X
while the second step provides the reverse inclusion. For the rest of the proof, we fix
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Step 1:
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\supset X
.

Assume that
f\in X
and fix
a>\frac{d}{\min\{1,q,r\}}
and
0<\gamma<\min\{1,q,r\}
such that
a\gamma>d
. Then we can use the identity (2.11) of
[
4
]
, which reads as follows: for
\delta\coloneqq R+1-\beta
we have

\displaystyle 2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{(k+m)d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}g_{k}(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
N\geq a
, where we define

(2.5)

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f)(y)\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{1}^{2}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}^{\lambda}\fint\limits_{B(y,2^{-(k+m)}t)}|(\Phi_{2^{-(k+m)}t}\ast f)(z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

and

\displaystyle g_{k}(x)\coloneqq 2^{k\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{(k+m)d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{k+m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Now, since
R+1>\beta
, take
\alpha>1
such that
\delta=R+1-\beta>\frac{d}{\alpha\gamma}
. Then, we can use the estimate (
2
), in conjunction with Lemma
2.4
, to get

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(2^{l\beta\gamma}|(\Phi_{2^{-l}}^{*}f)_{a}(x)|^{\gamma}\big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k-l|\delta\gamma}g_{k}\Big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|(g_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(2^{l\beta\gamma}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-mN\gamma}2^{(l+m)d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{l+m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N+\beta)\gamma}2^{m(d+\beta\gamma)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}},

where we used an index shift in the last line. Observe that the term on the left-hand side of the above estimate is equivalent to
\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}}^{\gamma}
by definition of
\|\cdot\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}
and a John–Nirenberg-type property of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see
[
20
, Thm. 3.2 + Rem. 3.3]
and
[
25
, Prop. 4.1]
. Hence,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N+\beta)\gamma}2^{m(d+\beta\gamma)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}\eqqcolon I

Let
Q\in\square
be a dyadic cube and set
j_{Q}\coloneqq-\log_{2}(\ell(Q))
. Fix
l\geq j_{Q}
and
x\in Q
. Then, we decompose the integral in (
2.6
) into

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{8Q}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|z|_{\infty}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\int\limits_{Q+\ell(Q)z}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
|\cdot|_{\infty}
is maximum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Notice that the functions
x\mapsto\frac{2^{dl}}{(1+2^{l}|x|)^{a\gamma}}
are radial and have uniform
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-bound with respect to
l\in\mathbb{Z}
because
a\gamma>d
. Using the majorant property of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, see
[
15
, Thm. 2.1.10]
, we estimate the local term on the right-hand side of (
2.7
) by

\displaystyle\int\limits_{8Q}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim 2^{-ld}\mathcal{M}\big(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f|^{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{8Q}\big)(x)\eqqcolon I_{1}^{m}(x).

To bound the series in (
2.7
), we use the observation

\displaystyle 1+2^{l}|x-y|\gtrsim 2^{l}\ell(Q)|z|_{\infty}

for
x\in Q
and
y\in Q+\ell(Q)z
. This yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|z|_{\infty}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\int\limits_{Q+\ell(Q)z}\frac{|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}}{(1+2^{l}|x-y|)^{a\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|z|_{\infty}\geq 2\end{subarray}}2^{-la\gamma}2^{j_{Q}a\gamma}|z|_{\infty}^{-a\gamma}\int\limits_{Q+\ell(Q)z}|(\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f)(y)|^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|z|_{\infty}\geq 2\end{subarray}}2^{-la\gamma}2^{j_{Q}(a\gamma-d)}|z|_{\infty}^{-a\gamma}\mathcal{M}\big(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f|^{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{Q+\ell(Q)z}\big)(x+\ell(Q)z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|z|_{\infty}\geq 2\end{subarray}}2^{-ld}|z|_{\infty}^{-a\gamma}\mathcal{M}\big(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f|^{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{Q+\ell(Q)z}\big)(x+\ell(Q)z),

where we used
a\gamma>d
and
l\geq j_{Q}
. Define

\displaystyle I_{2}^{m}(x)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|z|_{\infty}\geq 2\end{subarray}}2^{-ld}|z|_{\infty}^{-a\gamma}\mathcal{M}\big(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f|^{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{Q+\ell(Q)z}\big)(x+\ell(Q)z).

Then, we can bound
I
from (
2.6
) by

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N+\beta)\gamma}2^{m(d+\beta\gamma)}I_{1}^{m}\Big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N+\beta)\gamma}2^{m(d+\beta\gamma)}I_{2}^{m}\Big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{X^{\frac{q}{\gamma},\alpha}}.

We estimate both terms on the right-hand side of (
2
) in two separate steps.

Step 1.1: Estimate the first term in (
2
)
.
Fix a dyadic cube
Q\in\square
and focus on the term

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{Q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l=-\log_{2}(\ell(Q))}^{\infty}\Big|\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N+\beta)\gamma}2^{m(d+\beta\gamma)}I_{1}^{m}(x)\Big|^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

By Definition
2.3
, this is the first term in (
2
) up to a supremum. Using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{Q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l=-\log_{2}(\ell(Q))}^{\infty}\Big|\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N+\beta)\gamma}2^{m(d+\beta\gamma)}I_{1}^{m}(x)\Big|^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint\limits_{Q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l=-\log_{2}(\ell(Q))}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N-1+\beta)q}2^{m(\frac{d}{\gamma}+\beta)q}|I_{1}^{m}(x)|^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\fint\limits_{Q}\bigg(\sum\limits_{l=-\log_{2}(\ell(Q))}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=l}^{\infty}2^{-(m-l)(N-1+\beta-\frac{d}{\gamma})q}2^{m\beta q}\Big[\mathcal{M}\big(|\widetilde{\Phi}_{m}f|^{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{8Q}\big)(x)\Big]^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Since
\gamma<q
and
\alpha>1
, we can apply Feffermann–Stein’s vector valued inequality, see for example
[
24

3.
Functional analytic properties of tent spaces

As we saw in the previous section, homogeneous endpoint Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be characterized via extensions to the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
belonging to a certain extension space. In the case of homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
0<p<\infty
, analogous characterizations were shown in
[
4
, Sec. 2]
. More precisely, the extension spaces of
\dot{\mathrm{F}}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are characterized in terms of the four-parameter tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, see Definition
3.1
below. Thus, Theorem
2.5
can be seen as an extension of their characterization to the case
p=\infty
. The purpose of this section is to extend the scale
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
to its endpoint
p=\infty
and study the full scale from a function space theoretical point of view. First, we provide concrete definitions of these spaces and compare them to known spaces from the literature and collect basic properties such as completeness, density, duality and complex interpolation (Section
3.1
). Afterwards, we develop a discrete descriptions of these spaces (Section
3.2
), which are powerful tools for duality theory in the quasi-Banach range (Section
3.4
), embedding theory (Section
3.3
) and real interpolation theory (Section
3.5
).

3.1.
Definitions, relations to known spaces and basic properties

Here, we recall the definitions of the function spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
that appear in
[
4
, Sec. 3]
and extend the scale of tent spaces to its endpoint
p=\infty
. Moreover, we provide a link to existing function spaces from the literature and collect basic properties, including completeness, density, duality and complex interpolation.

Definition 3.1

(Tent spaces)
.

For
0<p<\infty
,
0<q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
define the space
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}<\infty

with the usual replacement of an integral by an
\mathrm{ess\,sup}
if the corresponding parameters
q,r
are infinite. Moreover, we define the space
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

if the parameter
q
is finite, and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,\infty,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{t>0}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}<\infty

with the same replacements in both cases if
r=\infty
, as before.

Definition 3.2

(
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces)
.

For
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
define the space
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg\|\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

with the usual replacement of an integral by an
\mathrm{ess\,sup}
if the corresponding parameters
q,r
are infinite.

Remark 3.3
.

In
[
4
, Rem. 3.2]
, the authors illustrated that the spaces
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
(for
p<\infty
) are generalizations of the well-known tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q}_{\beta}
. In the case
p=\infty
, our introduced spaces
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
are generalizations of the endpoint tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta}
(see
[
17
, Def. 1.1]
) in the sense that
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,q}_{\beta}=\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta}
with equivalence of (quasi-)norms.

In
[
17
]
, the author introduced weighted tent spaces with Whitney average, denoted by
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
. They consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(y,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad\text{(if $p<\infty$),}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,r}_{q,\beta}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\iint\limits_{\hat{B}(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

are finite, where
\hat{B}(y,\tau)\coloneqq\{(z,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}:B(z,s)\subset B(y,\tau)\}
denotes the tent with base
B(y,\tau)
.
We observe that the spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
have an additional average compared to the (quasi-)norms of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
. However, as demonstrated in
[
4
, Lem. 3.3]
, these spaces are equivalent for
p<\infty
. In the endpoint case
p=\infty
, a standard covering argument allows for the comparison of tents
\hat{B}(y,\tau)
with Carleson boxes
(0,\tau)\times B(y,\tau)
, and a change of parameters in the average integrals, establishing the equivalence of
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,r}_{q,\beta}
and
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
.

While the spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
and
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
essentially describe the same scale of function spaces, Definition
3.1
is more natural in the context for extension spaces for the Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin scales. Just as Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces are distinguished primarily by the order of their underlying norms, this same structural symmetry is reflected in our definitions of
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
. Beyond this conceptual consistency, our definition reduces notational overhead and simplifies practical calculations.

However, we first leverage the established properties of
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
from
[
17
]
, transferring them to our tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
via the aforementioned equivalence. In particular, we present three results that follow as direct consequences. First, we summarize basic properties of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, such as completeness, density and separability in the following statement. The corresponding properties for
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
-spaces can be found in
[
17
, Sec. 1]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the space
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
is a (quasi-)Banach space. Moreover,
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
is separable and the space of compactly supported functions in
\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
is a dense subspace of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
if
\max\{p,q,r\}<\infty
. Finally, for
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
compact and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
, we have the equivalence

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\simeq\|\mathbf{1}_{K}f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

where the implicit constants depend on
p,q,r,\beta,d
and
K
.

Although the last property of the above statement was not proven in
[
17
]
, it follows the same reasoning as in
[
4
, Lem. 3.8]
and will be omitted here. Next, we present a duality result in the Banach range
1\leq p,q,r<\infty
. The corresponding result for
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
-spaces was proven in
[
17
, Thm. 5.4]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
1\leq p,q,r<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}

for all measurable functions
f,g
. Moreover, we can identify
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
with equivalent norms via the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
duality pairing.

Finally,
[
17
, Thm. 4.3]
provides a complete complex interpolation theory for
\mathrm{T}^{p,r}_{q,\beta}
-spaces. The corresponding result for
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces reads as follows.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
0<p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
be such that
\max\{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}\}<\infty
or
\max\{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}\}<\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Define

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}},\quad\beta_{\theta}=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle[\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]_{\theta}=\mathrm{T}^{p_{\theta},q_{\theta},r_{\theta}}_{\beta_{\theta}}

with equivalent (quasi-)norms.

Lemma 3.8

Let
0<p<\infty

\lambda>1

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(\cdot,\lambda t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{\min\{p,q,r\}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Proof.

For simplicity, we just consider finite parameters. Fix
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}

J\subset\mathbb{N}
and
(\xi_{j})_{j\in J}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle B(x,\lambda t)\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}B(x-\xi_{j},t)\quad\text{and}\quad|J|\leq C\lambda^{d}

for some constant

depending only on

. With this at hand, we can bound the inner Whitney average as follows

\displaystyle\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(x,\lambda t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{j\in J}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x-\xi_{j},t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j\in J}F(x-\xi_{j},t),

where we set

\displaystyle F(z,t)=\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(z,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, we obtain the bound

(3.1)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(\cdot,\lambda t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\lesssim\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

To estimate the term on the right-hand side, we consider several cases for the parameters
p,q
and
r
.

Case 1:
r\leq q
. First, we use Minkowski’s inequality (with
r\leq q
) to get

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq\bigg\|\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

If
r\leq p
, we use Minkowski’s inequality, the substitution
x-\xi_{j}=z
for every
j\in J
and the fact that
|J|\leq C\lambda^{d}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{r}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot,t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{p}{r}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{r}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

To the contrary, if
r>p
, we use
\ell^{\frac{p}{r}}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, Fubini’s theorem, the substitution
x-\xi_{j}=z
for every
j\in J
and the fact that
|J|\leq C\lambda^{d}
, to calculate

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(x-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(x-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(z,t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{p}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Both cases, together with (
3.1
), yield

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(\cdot,\lambda t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{\min\{p,q,r\}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Case 2:
r>q
. In this case, we use the embedding
\ell^{\frac{q}{r}}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and Fubini’s theorem to calculate

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq\bigg\|\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

If
q\leq p
, then we use Minkowski’s inequality, the substitution
x-\xi_{j}=z
for every
j\in J
and the fact that
|J|\leq C\lambda^{d}
, to get

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(x-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(z,t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{q}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

In the case
q>p
, we use the embedding
\ell^{\frac{p}{q}}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, the substitution
x-\xi_{j}=z
for every
j\in J
and the fact that
|J|\leq C\lambda^{d}
, to conclude

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(\cdot-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(x-\xi_{j},t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\big[F(z,t)\big]^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{p}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Both cases, together with (
3.1
), result in

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(x,\lambda t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{\min\{p,q,r\}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}},

which finishes the proof.
∎

.

Lemma 3.9
.

For
\lambda>1

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(x,\lambda t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{\min\{q,r\}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}.

3.8
and

.

3.2.
Discrete descriptions

In this section, we provide several equivalent characterizations of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, which turn out to be very useful in the upcoming sections. We start with the following discrete characterization.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
0<q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\bigg\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(\cdot)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

with implicit constants depending only on
d,p,q,r,\beta
.

Proof.

To simplify notation, we concentrate on the case
q,r<\infty
. The case of infinite parameters follows analogously.

Step 1: We show
“
\lesssim
”
.

Our starting point is the equation

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg\|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(\cdot,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

Fix some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a unique
Q_{k}\in\square_{k}
with
x\in Q_{k}
. Hence, splitting the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
integral dyadically, we get

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\int\limits_{\ell(Q_{k})}^{2\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Observe that, if
t\in(\ell(Q_{k}),2\ell(Q_{k})]
and
x\in Q_{k}
, then
B(x,t)\subset 5Q_{k}
. Thus, we further estimate

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\int\limits_{\ell(Q_{k})}^{2\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{2\ell(Q_{k})}\int\limits_{5Q_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{2\ell(Q_{k})}\int\limits_{5Q_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Next, we cover the dilated Whitney box

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2},2\ell(Q_{k})\Big]\times 5Q_{k}

by a finite number of Whitney boxes
(\bar{Q}_{k}^{j})_{j\in J}
associated to certain dyadic cubes
(Q_{k}^{j})_{j\in J}
, see Section
1.4
for a review of notation. Indeed, there exist dyadic cubes
(Q_{k}^{j})_{j\in J}
with following the properties:

(1)

|J|
is bounded by a constant depending only on
d
,

(2)

Q^{j}_{k}\in\square_{k}\cup\square_{k+1}
,

(3)

\Big(\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2},2\ell(Q_{k})\Big]\times 5Q_{k}\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}\bar{Q}^{j}_{k}
.

Using this covering yields

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{2\ell(Q_{k})}\int\limits_{5Q_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\iint\limits_{\bar{Q}^{j}_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\sum\limits_{j\in J}\bigg(\iint\limits_{\bar{Q}^{j}_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{j\in J}\mathbf{1}_{Q^{j}_{k}}(x)\|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q}_{k}^{j},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since
Q^{j}_{k}\in\square_{k}\cup\square_{k+1}
, we take the sum over all
Q\in\square_{k}\cup\square_{k+1}
to get

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}\cup\square_{k+1}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By splitting the second series into two, one running over
Q\in\square_{k}
and the other over
Q\in\square_{k+1}
, we can use an index shift on the latter to obtain

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\square_{k}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

In summary, we have shown

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Taking
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-norms on both sides yields the first direction.

Step 2: We show
“
\gtrsim
”
.
Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Again, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exist a unique
Q_{k}\in\square_{k}
with
x\in Q_{k}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By introducing an extra integral, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\int\limits_{Q_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Note that for
x\in Q_{k}
and
t\in(\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2},\ell(Q_{k})]
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2},\ell(Q_{k})\Big]\times Q_{k}\subset\Big(\frac{t}{2},2t\Big]\times B(x,2\sqrt{d}t).

Thus, we further estimate the last term in (
3.2
) by

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\int\limits_{Q_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\int\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

In summary, we showed

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Finally, by taking
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-norms in
x
on both sides and using a change of Whitney parameters (Proposition
3.10
), we conclude the reverse inequality.
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
0<q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
f\in\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

with the usual modification if
q,r
are infinite. Here, the implicit constants depend only on
d,q,r,\beta
and the sum runs over all dyadic cubes
Q\in\square
with
Q\subset P
.

Proof.

We simplify notation by focusing on the case
0<q,r<\infty
. The case of infinite parameters follows in a similar way.

Step 1: We show
“
\lesssim
”
.

Fix
\tau>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, there exist
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P\in\square_{k}
with
y\in P
and
\frac{\ell(P)}{2}\leq\tau<\ell(P)
. It follows

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P)}\fint\limits_{3P}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

Pick a finite sequence
(P_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\square_{k}
with the properties

\displaystyle 3P\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}P_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad|I|\leq C

for some constant
C>0
depending only on the dimension
d
. This covering allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P)}\fint\limits_{3P}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{i\in I}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P_{i})}\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Fix
i\in I
and decompose the Carleson box
(0,\ell(P_{i})]\times P_{i}
into

(3.7)

\displaystyle(0,\ell(P_{i})]\times P_{i}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\square\\
Q\subset P_{i}\end{subarray}}\bar{Q}.

With this observation, we estimate a single term of the right side of (
3.2
) as follows:

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P_{i})}\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{|P_{i}|}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P_{i}}\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{Q}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P_{i}}\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}^{\ell(Q)}\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\int\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

For
t\in(\ell(Q)/2,\ell(Q)]
and
x\in Q
, we have
(t/2,t]\times B(x,t)\subset(\ell(Q)/4,\ell(Q)]\times 3Q
. Hence, we further estimate

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P_{i}}\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}^{\ell(Q)}\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{3Q}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P_{i}}\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{\frac{-\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\fint\limits_{P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{\frac{-\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Notice that this bound holds uniformly in
i
. Since the index set
I
is finite, we can plug the uniform bound back in (
3.2
), so that (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\fint\limits_{P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{\frac{-\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for all
\tau>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Taking suprema in
\tau
and
y
yields

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\fint\limits_{P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{\frac{-\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Finally, a similar covering argument as in Step 1 of the proof in Proposition
3.12
allows us to recover our Whitney box
(\ell(Q)/2,\ell(Q)]\times Q
, resulting in

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

which proves the first direction.

Step 2: We show
“
\gtrsim
”
.

This direction follows the same ideas as in Step 2 of the proof of Proposition
3.12
.
Fix a dyadic cube
P\in\square
. Then, we calculate

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{|P|}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\int\limits_{Q}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{Q}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By introducing an extra integral, we get

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{|P|}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{Q}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{Q}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

For
t\in(\ell(Q)/2,\ell(Q)]
and
x\in Q
we have
(\ell(Q)/2,\ell(Q)]\times Q\subset(t/2,2t]\times B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)
. Thus, we further estimate

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{|P|}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}^{\ell(Q)}\int\limits_{Q}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\int\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Finally, the decomposition (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{|P|}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P)}\int\limits_{P}\bigg(\int\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\int\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since this estimate holds for all dyadic cubes
P\in\square
, it follows

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By a change of Whitney parameters (Proposition
3.10
), we conclude the reverse inequality.
∎

Next, we want to provide another equivalent characterization of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
by “discrete local square functions”. To this end, for
0<q,r\leq\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
we define

\displaystyle G^{q,r}_{\beta,P}(f)(x)\coloneqq\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for some fixed
P\in\square
(with the usual modification in the infinite cases). Next, for
0<c<1
define

(3.8)

\displaystyle m_{\beta,P,c}^{q,r}(f)\coloneqq\inf\Big\{t:|\{x\in P:G^{q,r}_{\beta,P}(f)(x)>t\}|<c|P|\Big\},

which is the so-called
“
c
-median”
of
G^{q,r}_{\beta,P}(f)
in
P
, and

(3.9)

\displaystyle m^{q,r}_{\beta,c}(f)(x)\coloneqq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}m^{q,r}_{\beta,P,c}(f)\mathbf{1}_{P}(x).

These three objects have a counterpart on the level of sequence spaces, see
[
14
, Sec. 5]
. To be more precise, for
0<q\leq\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
s=(s_{Q})_{Q\in\square}\subset\mathbb{C}
define

\displaystyle G^{\beta q}_{P}(s)(x)\coloneqq\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for some fixed
P\in\square
with the usual modification in the infinite case. Furthermore, for
0<c<1
, we define

\displaystyle m_{P,c}^{\beta q}(s)\coloneqq\inf\Big\{t:|\{x\in Q:G^{\beta q}_{P}(s)(x)>t|<c|P|\Big\},

as well as

\displaystyle m^{\beta q}_{c}(s)(x)\coloneqq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}m^{\beta q}_{P,c}(s)\mathbf{1}_{P}(x).

By setting
s_{Q}\coloneqq|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}
for every
Q\in\square
, we have for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(3.10)

\displaystyle G^{q,r}_{\beta,P}(f)(x)=G^{\beta q}_{P}(s)(x),

as well as

(3.11)

\displaystyle m_{\beta,P,c}^{q,r}(f)=m_{P,c}^{\beta q}(s)\quad\text{and}\quad m^{q,r}_{\beta,c}(f)(x)=m_{c}^{\beta q}(s)(x).

Thus, the results of
[
14
, Sec. 5]
can be utilized to establish the following characterizations, which will play an important role in the duality theory (Section
3.4
) and real interpolation theory (Section
3.5
).

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
. Furthermore, let
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose that for each dyadic cube
Q\in\square
there exists a measurable set
E_{Q}\subset Q
with
|E_{Q}|>\varepsilon|Q|
. Then, for
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\bigg\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(\cdot)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

if
p<\infty
, and

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof.

For every
Q\in\square
, set
s_{Q}\coloneqq|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}
. Then, we can rewrite the results of Proposition
3.12
and
3.13
into

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\bigg\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(\cdot)|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

if
0<p<\infty
, and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

In the case
p<\infty
, we use
[
14
, Prop. 2.7]
to get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\bigg\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}=\bigg\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}},

which proves (
3.12
). Similarly, if
p=\infty
, we use
[
14
, Prop. 5.4]
to obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{E_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

which proves (
3.13
).
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<c<1
. Then, for
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\|m^{q,r}_{\beta,c}(f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

with implicit constants depending only on
d,q,r,\beta
.

Proof.

Define the sequence
s_{Q}=|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}
for every
Q\in\square
. Then, Proposition
3.12
and Proposition
3.13
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\bigg\|\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

This characterization, together with
[
14
, Prop. 5.5]
(which holds for every
0<c<1
) and the observation (
3.11
), yields

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|m^{\beta q}_{c}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}=\|m^{q,r}_{\beta,c}(f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}},

for all
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
, which proves the claim.
∎

We end this section by proving the John–Nirenberg-type property for the endpoint spaces
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
used in the characterization of

in Theorem
2.5
. The proof is based on the discrete characterization in Proposition

and a John–Nirenberg-type property for sequence spaces.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
0<q,r\leq\infty
,
0<\alpha<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
f\in\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

with implicit constants depending only on
d,q,r,\alpha,\beta
.

Proof.

For every
Q\in\square
, set
s_{Q}\coloneqq|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}
. Then, we can rewrite the result of Proposition
3.13
into

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

with implicit constants depending only on
d,q,r,\beta
. Now, by a John–Nirenberg-type property of sequence spaces
[
14
, Cor. 5.7]
, it follows

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\beta q}{d}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

for all
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, where the implicit constants depend only on
d,q,r,\alpha,\beta
.
∎

Proposition 3.17

(John–Nirenberg-type property)
.

Let
0<q,r\leq\infty
,
0<\alpha<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
f\in\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

with implicit constants depending only on
d,q,r,\alpha,\beta
.

Proof.

Due to Lemma
3.16
, it remains to show that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

The procedure is similar to the proof of the Proposition
3.13
and consists of two steps.

Step 1: We show “
\lesssim
”
. Fix a dyadic cube
P\in\square
and
x\in P
. Recall the notation
j_{P}=-\log_{2}(\ell(P))
. Then, there exists a unique sequence
(Q_{k})_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P}}\subset P
of dyadic cubes with
Q_{k}\in\square_{k}
and
x\in Q_{k}
for every
k\leq-j_{P}
. It follows

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P}}|Q_{k}|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q}_{k},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By introducing an extra integral, we get

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P}}\,\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Note that for each
t\in(\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2},\ell(Q_{k})]
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2},\ell(Q_{k})\Big]\times Q_{k}\subset\Big(\frac{t}{2},2t\Big]\times B(x,2\sqrt{d}t).

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P}}\,\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since this holds for all dyadic cubes
P\in\square
and
x\in P
, we take
\mathrm{L}^{\alpha}
-averages of both sides and then supremum over all
P
to obtain

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2t}\fint\limits_{B(x,2\sqrt{d}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Finally, a straight forward change of Whitney parameters in the norm on the right-hand side (see Remark
3.10
) concludes

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Step 2: We show “
\gtrsim
”
.
Fix
\tau>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, there exist
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P\in\square_{k}
with
y\in P
and
\frac{\ell(P)}{2}\leq\tau<\ell(P)
. It follows

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint\limits_{3P}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Since
P\in\square_{k}
, we can pick a finite sequence
(P_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\square_{k}
with the properties

\displaystyle 3P\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}P_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad|I|\leq C

for some constant
C>0
depending only on the dimension
d
. This covering allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{i\in I}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P_{i})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

We focus on the single terms

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P_{i})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Fix an index
i
and
x\in P_{i}
. Then, there exists a sequence of dyadic cubes
(Q_{k})_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P_{i}}}\subset P_{i}
with
Q_{k}\in\square_{k}
and
x\in Q_{k}
for every
k\leq-j_{P_{i}}
. It follows

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P_{i})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P_{i}}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

For

we have
(t/2,t]\times B(x,t)\subset(\ell(Q_{k})/4,\ell(Q_{k})]\times 3Q_{k}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P_{i}}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)\int\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{2}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{4}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\fint\limits_{3Q_{k}}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{-j_{P_{i}}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)|Q_{k}|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q_{k})}{4}}^{\ell(Q_{k})}\fint\limits_{3Q_{k}}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset{P_{i}}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\fint\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where we took the sum over all dyadic cubes
Q\subset P_{i}
in the last step. Since this estimate holds for all
x\in P_{i}
, taking the
\mathrm{L}^{\alpha}
-average of both sides yields

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\ell(P_{i})}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint\limits_{P_{i}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset{P_{i}}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\fint\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset{P}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\fint\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

Plugging this estimate into (
3.2
) and using finiteness of the index set
I
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\ell(Q)}{4}}^{\ell(Q)}\fint\limits_{3Q}|f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Using a similar covering argument as in Step 1 of the proof in Proposition
3.12
, we can recover the Whitney box
(\ell(Q)/2,\ell(Q)]\times Q
in the inner average integral on the right-hand side to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Because this holds for all
\tau>0
and

, we conclude by taking the suprema.
∎

3.3.
Embedding theory

In this section, we show varies embeddings of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces. In particular, we show Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev-type embeddings (Theorem
3.18
) and mixed-type embeddings of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces (Theorem
3.22
). They are in line with Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin theory as discussed in Section
1
.

Theorem 3.18

(Weighted tent space embeddings)
.

Let
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}
. Then, we have the continuous embedding

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}.

Before we prove Theorem
3.18
, we need the following two lemmata. The first lemma reassembles a nesting property of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
in the parameter
q
and
r
. The second one is a specific endpoint embedding, which follows from the weighted
\mathrm{Z}
-space embeddings in
[
4
, Thm. 3.14]
.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
0<p\leq\infty
,
0<q_{0}\leq q_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have the continuous embedding

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.15
, it is enough to show that

\displaystyle m^{q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta,c}(f)(x)\lesssim m^{q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta,c}(f)(x)

for some
0<c<1
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. First, observe that for a fixed
P\in\square
, we have by Jensen’s inequality and the embedding
\ell^{q_{0}}\subset\ell^{q_{1}}
that

\displaystyle G^{q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta,P}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q_{1}}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{1}}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q_{0}}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r_{0}}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}=\mathrm{G}^{q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta,P}(f)(x).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\Big\{x\in P:G^{q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta,P}(f)(x)>t\Big\}\Big|\leq\Big|\Big\{x\in P:G^{q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta,P}(f)(x)>t\Big\}\Big|

for all
t>0
. By definition (
3.8
), it follows

\displaystyle m_{\beta,P,c}^{q_{1},r_{1}}(f)\leq\,m_{\beta,P,c}^{q_{0},r_{0}}(f),

for all
0<c<1
and thus

\displaystyle m^{q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta,c}(f)(x)=\sup\limits_{P\in\square}m^{q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta,P,c}(f)\mathbf{1}_{P}(x)\leq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}m^{q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta,P,c}(f)\mathbf{1}_{P}(x)=m^{q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta,c}(f)(x).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
0<r\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p}
. Then, we have the continuous embedding

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\infty,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}}.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
3.21
from below with the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev embedding for
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces
[
4
, Thm. 3.14]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p,\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}}=\mathrm{T}^{\infty,\infty,r}_{\beta_{1}}

where the last step follows by the Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
of these spaces.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.18
.

We divide the proof into two parts. In the first part, we prove the continuous embedding (
3.15
) in the case
p_{1}<\infty
. The proof is similar to the one of
[
18
, Thm. 2.1]
, where the author shows these type of embeddings for homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces. Afterwards, we show (
3.15
) in the endpoint case
p_{1}=\infty
. For this, we first use a duality argument to prove the embedding for all parameters in the Banach range. Then, we apply a convex reduction argument (see Lemma
3.11
) to conclude the result for the full range of parameters.

Step 1: We show (
3.15
) for
p_{1}<\infty
.
First, using Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r_{0}}_{\beta_{1}}}.

Hence, it is sufficient to show

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}}}

for some fixed
0<r\leq\infty
. Second, it is enough to assume that
\beta_{0}=0
because otherwise we consider
s^{-\beta_{0}}f(s,\cdot)
instead of
f(s,\cdot)
. Finally, due to the nesting property of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces in the parameter
q
, see Lemma
3.19
, it is enough to assume
q_{0}=\infty
. Therefore, the embedding (
3.15
) follows from

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}}

To show this embedding, we first fix
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}
with
\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}}=1
. By Lemma
3.20
, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup\limits_{t>0}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{\frac{d}{p_{0}}}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,\infty,r}_{-\frac{d}{p_{0}}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}}=1.

Next, for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta_{1}}f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q_{1}}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q_{1}}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}},

where we define

\displaystyle F(t,x)\coloneqq\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|f(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

By
[
15
, Prop. 1.1.4]
, we can use the description of the
\mathrm{L}^{p_{1}}
-norm in terms of the distribution function to obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p_{1}}_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}}}\simeq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p_{1}-1}\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}>\lambda\bigg\}\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Thus, we will further analyze the distribution function

(3.19)

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}>\lambda\bigg\}\bigg|.

For some fixed but arbitrary
c>0
, we calculate

(3.20)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\leq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{c}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}+\bigg(\int\limits_{c}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}.

Since we assumed
\beta_{0}=0
, we have
\beta_{1}=\beta_{1}-\beta_{0}=\frac{d}{p_{1}}-\frac{d}{p_{0}}<0
. Thus, we estimate the first integral on the right-hand side

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{c}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,x)|\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{c}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle=A_{1}c^{-\beta_{1}}\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,x)|

for some generic constant
A_{1}>0
which does not depend on
c
and
f
. Furthermore, by (
3.17
), we can bound the second integral on the right-hand side of (
3.20
)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int\limits_{c}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{c}^{\infty}t^{-(\frac{d}{p_{1}}-\frac{d}{p_{0}})q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup\limits_{t>0}\Big(t^{\frac{d}{p_{0}}}|F(t,x)|\Big)\bigg(\int\limits_{c}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{dq_{1}}{p_{1}}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq A_{2}c^{-\frac{d}{p_{1}}}

for some generic constant
A_{2}>0
which does not depend on
c
and
f
. Plug both integral estimates back into (
3.20
) to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}>\lambda\bigg\}\bigg|

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:A_{1}c^{-\beta_{1}}\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,x)|>\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg\}\bigg|+\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:A_{2}c^{-\frac{d}{p_{1}}}>\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg\}\bigg|

for all
\lambda,c>0
. Now, for a fixed
\lambda>0
, we choose
c=(\frac{\lambda}{2A_{2}})^{-\frac{p_{1}}{d}}
. Then, it follows

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:A_{2}c^{-\frac{d}{p_{1}}}>\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg\}\bigg|=0,

and we have

\displaystyle A_{1}c^{-\beta_{1}}=A_{1}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2A_{2}}\Big)^{\frac{p_{1}\beta_{1}}{d}}=A\lambda^{1-\frac{p_{1}}{p_{0}}}

for some new generic constant
A>0
. In this case, the inequality (
3.3
) reduces to

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta_{1}q_{1}}|F(t,x)|^{q_{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}>\lambda\bigg\}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:A\lambda^{1-\frac{p_{1}}{p_{0}}}\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,x)|>\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg\}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,x)|>\frac{\lambda^{\frac{p_{1}}{p_{0}}}}{2A}\bigg\}\bigg|.

Using this estimate in the distributional description (
3.18
), we get

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p_{1}}_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p_{1}-1}\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,x)|>\frac{\lambda^{\frac{p_{1}}{p_{0}}}}{2A}\bigg\}\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\simeq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mu^{p_{0}-1}\bigg|\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,x)|>\mu\bigg\}\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\simeq\|\sup\limits_{t>0}|F(t,\cdot)|\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}}}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|^{p_{0}}_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}}

\displaystyle=1.

Since this estimate holds for all
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}
with
\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}}=1
, we conclude with a homogeneity argument

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}}

for all
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{0}
. This proves (
3.16
), and therefore the embedding (
3.15
) in the case
p_{1}<\infty
.

Step 2: We show (
3.15
) for
p_{1}=\infty
.

First, we assume
1<p_{0}<\infty
and
1<q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
. Then,
1<p_{0}^{\prime}<\infty
and
1\leq q_{0}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}<\infty
and

\displaystyle-\beta_{1}-(-\beta_{0})=\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}=d\Big(1-\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\prime}}\Big).

Thus, by (
3.15
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{1,q_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{1}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{0}}.

Since all parameters
p_{0}^{\prime},q_{i}^{\prime},r_{i}^{\prime}\geq 1
, we can use Proposition
3.5
to get

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}=(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{0}})^{\prime}\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{T}^{1,q_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime}}_{-\beta_{1}})^{\prime}=\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}.

This proves (
3.15
) for
1<p_{0}<p_{1}=\infty
and
1<q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
.

Now, assume
0<p_{0}<\infty
,
0<q_{0},q_{1},r_{0},r_{1}\leq\infty
. Choose
M>0
such that

\displaystyle 1<\frac{p_{0}}{M},\frac{q_{0}}{M},\frac{q_{1}}{M},\frac{r_{0}}{M},\frac{r_{1}}{M}\leq\infty.

Due to Lemma
3.11
in conjunction with (
3.22
), we get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q_{1},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}}=\||f|^{M}\|^{\frac{1}{M}}_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,\frac{q_{1}}{M},\frac{r_{1}}{M}}_{M\beta_{1}}}\lesssim\||f|^{M}\|^{\frac{1}{M}}_{\mathrm{T}^{\frac{p_{0}}{M},\frac{q_{0}}{M},\frac{r_{0}}{M}}_{M\beta_{0}}}=\|f\|_{{\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}}},

which proves (
3.15
) in the full range of parameters.
∎

We finish this section by presenting mixed-type embeddings for tent and
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces. There are two of them. The first (Lemma
3.21
) is a mixed-type embedding in the parameter
q
that resembles a nesting property. The second (Theorem
3.22
) is a mixed-type Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev embedding. Its proof relies on the real interpolation results for tent spaces in Theorem
3.28
and Proposition
3.30
, which will be proven in Section
3.5
below.

Lemma 3.21
.

For
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we have the continuous embeddings

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q\},r}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p,\max\{p,q\},r}_{\beta}.

Proof.

In the case
0<p<\infty
, the result was already proven in
[
4
, Lem. 4.3]
. Hence, we focus on the case
p=\infty
. First, observe that Lemma
3.19
implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\infty,\infty,r}_{\beta}=\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r}_{\beta}.

This shows the second embedding of (
3.23
) since
\max\{p,q\}=\infty
. To show the first embedding of (
3.23
), we estimate

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(y,\tau)}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,z)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}},

which concludes the claim.
∎

Theorem 3.22

(Mixed-type embeddings)
.

Let
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
0<r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}=\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}
. Then, we have the continuous embedding

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},p_{0},r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}},

as well as

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},p_{1},r_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}.

Proof.

Step 1: We show (
3.24
).

By Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
3.19
, the embedding (
3.24
) can be reduced to show

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},p_{0},r}_{\beta_{1}}

for any
0<r\leq\infty
. Now, by using Theorem
3.28
, we obtain

(3.26)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}=(\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p}_{0},\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p}_{1},\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}})_{\theta,p_{0}},

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
and
0<\tilde{p}_{0}<p_{0}<\tilde{p}_{1}<p_{1}<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{1-\theta}{\tilde{p}_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{\tilde{p}_{1}}
. Using the embedding of Lemma
3.21
and the weighted
\mathrm{Z}
-space embedding
[
4
, Thm. 3.14]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p}_{0},\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{\tilde{p}_{0},\infty,r}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},\infty,r}_{\tilde{\beta}_{0}}

for
\tilde{\beta}_{0}\coloneqq\beta_{0}+\frac{d}{p_{1}}-\frac{d}{\tilde{p}_{0}}
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p}_{1},\infty,r}_{\vphantom{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{\tilde{p}_{1},\infty,r}_{\vphantom{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},\infty,r}_{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}

for
\tilde{\beta}_{1}=\beta_{0}+\frac{d}{p_{1}}-\frac{d}{\tilde{p}_{1}}
. These embeddings, combined with (
3.26
) and the interpolation result for
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces in
[
4
, Thm. 4.1]
, yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},\infty,r}_{\vphantom{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}\beta_{0}}=(\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p}_{0},\infty,r}_{\vphantom{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p}_{1},\infty,r}_{\vphantom{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}\beta_{0}})_{\theta,p_{0}}\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},\infty,r}_{\tilde{\beta}_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},\infty,r}_{\tilde{\beta}_{1}})_{\theta,p_{0}}=\mathrm{Z}^{p_{1},p_{0},r}_{\vphantom{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}\beta_{1}},

where we used

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\tilde{\beta}_{0}+\theta\tilde{\beta}_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-\theta)\Big(\beta_{0}+\frac{d}{p_{1}}-\frac{d}{\tilde{p}_{0}}\Big)+\theta\Big(\beta_{0}+\frac{d}{p_{1}}-\frac{d}{\tilde{p}_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\beta_{0}+\frac{d}{p_{1}}-d\Big(\frac{1-\theta}{\tilde{p}_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{\tilde{p}_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\beta_{0}+\frac{d}{p_{1}}-\frac{d}{p_{0}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}

in the last line. This proves (
3.24
).

Step 2: We show (
3.25
).

By Jensen’s inequality, the embedding (
3.24
) can be reduced to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},p_{1},r}_{\beta_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r}_{\beta_{1}}

for any
0<r\leq\infty
. Fix
0<\bar{p}_{0}<\bar{p}_{1}<\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
0<p_{0}<\bar{p}_{0}<p_{1}<\bar{p}_{1}<\infty
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1-\theta}{\bar{p}_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{\bar{p}_{1}}.

Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}_{0}\coloneqq\beta_{1}+\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{\bar{p}_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\beta}_{1}\coloneqq\beta_{1}+\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{\bar{p}_{1}}.

Then, by Theorem
3.18
, we have the embeddings

(3.27)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\bar{\beta}_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\bar{p}_{0},q,r}_{\beta_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\bar{\beta}_{1}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\bar{p}_{1},q,r}_{\beta_{1}}.

Finally, notice that

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\bar{\beta}_{0}+\theta\bar{\beta}_{1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}+\frac{d}{p_{0}}-d\Big(\frac{1-\theta}{\bar{p}_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{\bar{p}_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}+\frac{d}{p_{0}}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{0}.

Hence, the real interpolation results Theorem
3.28
and Proposition
3.30
, together with (
3.27
), yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p_{0},p_{1},r}_{\beta_{0}}=(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\bar{\beta}_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\bar{\beta}_{1}})_{\theta,p_{1}}\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{T}^{\bar{p}_{0},q,r}_{\beta_{1}},\mathrm{T}^{\bar{p}_{1},q,r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,p_{1}}=\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r}_{\beta_{1}},

which finishes the proof
∎

3.4.
Duality: non-Banach range

In this section, we develop a complete duality theory for the tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
. Specifically, we identify the dual spaces
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
for parameters
p
and/or
q
in the non-Banach range. This complements Proposition
3.5
, where the parameters belong to the Banach range
1\leq p,q<\infty
. We begin with a duality theorem in which
1\leq p<\infty
remains in the Banach range but
q
does not. Recall the interpretation
q^{\prime}=\infty
in the case
0<q\leq 1
.

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
1\leq p,r<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we have

(3.28)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}

for all measurable functions
f,g
. Moreover, we can identify
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
with equivalent norms via the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-duality pairing.

For the proof we need the following auxiliary lemma, which is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.12
.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
0<p,q,r<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the map

\displaystyle i:\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\to\mathrm{L}^{p}(\ell^{q}),\quad f\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}

satisfies
\|i(f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\ell^{q})}\simeq\|f\|_{{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}}}
. In particular, it is bounded, injective and has closed range.

Proof of Theorem
3.23
.

According to Proposition
3.5
, it suffices to assume
0<q<1
. We will divide the proof into two steps. First, we show the inclusion
\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}\subset(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
. Here, we use the nesting property (Lemma
3.19
) to embed the quasi-Banach tent space into a tent space in the Banach range. For the reverse inclusion
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
, we will embed the tent space into a vector-valued Lebesgue space using Lemma
3.24
, for which duality results are already known.

Step 1: We show
\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}\subset(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
.

By Lemma
3.19
, we have the continuous embedding

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\subset\mathrm{T}^{p,1,r}_{\beta}.

This, together with Proposition
3.5
, yields

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,1,r}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\vphantom{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}.

This proves (
3.28
) and the inclusion
\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}\subset(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
.

Step 2: We show
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
.

Let
\Phi\in(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
. Since compactly supported
\mathrm{L}^{r}
-functions embed continuously into
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
, see Proposition
3.4
, we observe that
\Phi\in\big(\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)\big)^{\prime}
for each compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. Thus, we can find
f_{K}\in\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(g)=\iint\limits_{K}f_{K}(s,y)\,\overline{g(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s},

for every
g\in\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(K,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
. Exhausting the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
by compact sets, we may find a global function
f\in\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
with the property

(3.29)

\displaystyle\Phi(g)=\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}f(s,y)\,\overline{g(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}

for all compactly supported
g\in\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
. Hence, by density (see Proposition
3.4
), it suffices to show
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
. To do so, we first use Lemma
3.24
to see that
\Phi\circ i^{-1}
is a bounded linear functional on
\Rg(i)
. Then, by the Hahn–Banach theorem, we can find an extension
\tilde{\Phi}\in(\mathrm{L}^{p}(\ell^{q}))^{\prime}
with
\|\tilde{\Phi}\|=\|\Phi\circ i^{-1}\|
and
\tilde{\Phi}=\Phi\circ i^{-1}
on
\Rg(i)
, or equivalently,

(3.30)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}\circ i=\Phi\quad\text{on}\quad\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}.

Due to the duality result
[
22
, Prop. 2.11.1]
, we may identify
(\mathrm{L}^{p}(\ell^{q}))^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\ell^{\infty})
. Hence, we find a sequence
(F_{Q})_{Q\in\square}\in\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\ell^{\infty})
such that
\|F_{Q}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\ell^{\infty})}=\|\tilde{\Phi}\|
and
\tilde{\Phi}
can be represented uniquely as

(3.31)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}((g_{Q})_{Q})=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}F_{Q}(x)\,\overline{g_{Q}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
(g_{Q})_{Q\in\square}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\ell^{q})
. Combining (
3.29
), (
3.30
) and (
3.31
), we get

(3.32)

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}f(s,y)\,\overline{g(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}F_{Q}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta}{d}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}\,\mathrm{d}x

for all compactly supported
g\in\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
.
Next, fix
Q\in\square
arbitrary and
g\in\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)
with norm
\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s})}\leq 1
. With (
3.32
) it follows

\displaystyle\bigg|\iint\limits_{\bar{Q}}f(s,y)\,\overline{g(s,y)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int\limits_{Q}F_{Q}(x)\,|Q|^{-\frac{\beta}{d}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|

(3.33)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{Q}|F_{Q}(x)|\,|Q|^{-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\beta}{d}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq|Q|^{-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\beta}{d}}\int\limits_{Q}|F_{Q}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
g
was arbitrary, we conclude, by taking the supremum over all such
g
, that

(3.34)

\displaystyle|Q|^{\frac{\beta}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}\lesssim|Q|^{\frac{\beta}{d}-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\big)}\lesssim\fint\limits_{Q}|F_{Q}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
Q\in\square
was arbitrary, (
3.34
) in conjunction with Proposition
3.12
yields

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg\|\sup\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(\cdot)|Q|^{\frac{\beta}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r^{\prime}}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\|\sup\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(\cdot)\fint\limits_{Q}|F_{Q}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\sup\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathcal{M}\big(|F_{Q}|\big)(\cdot)\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}.

Because
1<p^{\prime}\leq\infty
, we can apply Feffermann–Stein’s inequality, see
[
24
, Thm. 2.1]
, to obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup\limits_{Q\in\square}|F_{Q}(\cdot)\big|\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}}=\|(F_{Q})_{Q\in\square}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\ell^{\infty})}=\|\tilde{\Phi}\|\leq\|\Phi\|.

Hence,
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p^{\prime},\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta}
, which proves the claim.
∎

Finally, we complete the duality theory for
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
by treating the case
0<p<1
. In that case, we can identify
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
with a
\mathrm{Z}
-space from Definition
3.2
. This is in line with the Besov–Triebel–Lizorkin duality theory, see Section
1
.

Theorem 3.25
.

Let
0<p<1
,
0<q<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we have

(3.35)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\vphantom{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}}

for all measurable functions
f,g
. Moreover, we can identify
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
with equivalent norms via the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-duality pairing.

Proof.

First, we observe with Theorem
3.18
and Lemma
3.21
that

(3.36)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q\},r}_{\beta}\subset\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}\subset\mathrm{T}^{1,1,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}.

This, in conjunction with the duality theory of
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces (see
[
4
, Thm. 3.16]
) and Proposition
3.5
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}=\mathrm{T}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{T}^{1,1,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\subset(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\subset(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q\},r}_{\beta})^{\prime}=\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}.

Hence, we can identify
(\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta})^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
via the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-duality pairing. To show (
3.35
), we introduce an extra average, use Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality, to get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint\limits_{s}^{2s}\fint\limits_{B(y,s)}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}f(s,y)|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}g(s,y)|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{1,1,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}.

Finally, by the second embedding of (
3.36
), we get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s,y)|\cdot|g(s,y)|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\vphantom{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}1,1,r}_{\beta-d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}}\ \|g\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\vphantom{\infty,\infty,r^{\prime}}p,q,r}_{\beta}},

which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 3.26
.

It is known that the dual space of tent spaces
\mathrm{T}^{p,2}_{\beta}
for
0<p<1
can be identified with a tent space
“beyond infinity”
, see for example
[
1
,
5
]
. For
0<q\leq\infty
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, these spaces are denoted by
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}
and consist of all measurable functions
f
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{t>0}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty

with the usual modification in the infinite case.
On the one hand, it was proven in
[
1
, Thm. 1.11]
that, for
0<p<1
, we can identify
(\mathrm{T}^{p,2}_{\beta})^{\prime}\simeq\mathrm{T}^{\infty,2}_{-\beta;d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}
with equivalence of norms. On the other hand, Theorem
3.25
suggests that the dual space of
\mathrm{T}^{p,2}_{\beta}
can be identified with a
\mathrm{Z}
-space. Indeed, for
q=r=2
, we have
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}=\mathrm{T}^{p,2}_{\beta}
, see
[
4
, Rem. 3.2]
, such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\infty,2}_{-\beta;d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}=(\mathrm{T}^{p,2}_{\beta})^{\prime}=(\mathrm{T}^{p,2,2}_{\beta})^{\prime}=\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,2}_{-\beta+d(\frac{1}{p}-1)}.

This shows, that tent spaces
“beyond infinity”
are equivalent to certain
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces. This observation will be recorded in the next proposition. Additionally, we provide a direct proof of this phenomenon including a broader range of parameters.

Proposition 3.27
.

For
0<q\leq\infty
,
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}=\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}
with equivalent norms.

Proof.

We will show the assertion for
0<q<\infty
since the case
q=\infty
follows directly by definition of the spaces.

Step 1:
\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}\subset\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}
and fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
. Then, we get by a direct computation

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-(\beta+\alpha)}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Hence, taking suprema in
x
and
t
on both sides yields
\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}}
.

Step 2:
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}\subset\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}
and fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
. Using a dyadic splitting gives us

(3.37)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\int\limits_{2^{-k-1}t}^{2^{-k}t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Now, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exist
J_{k}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
(x_{j})_{j\in J_{k}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle B(x,t)\subset\bigcup_{j\in J_{k}}B(x_{j},2^{-k}t)\quad\text{and}\quad|J_{k}|\leq C2^{kd}

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
d
. This covering allows us to estimate (
3.37
) by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\int\limits_{2^{-k-1}t}^{2^{-k}t}\sum\limits_{j\in J_{k}}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(x_{j},2^{-k}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since
s\simeq 2^{-k}t
, we can introduce average integrals to estimate the term on the right-hand side by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\int\limits_{2^{-k-1}t}^{2^{-k}t}\sum\limits_{j\in J_{k}}t^{-d}\int\limits_{B(x_{j},2^{-k}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k(d+q\alpha)}\sum\limits_{j\in J_{k}}\frac{1}{(2^{-k}t)^{\alpha q}}\fint\limits_{2^{-k-1}t}^{2^{-k}t}\fint\limits_{B(x_{j},2^{-k}t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k(d+q\alpha)}\sum\limits_{j\in J_{k}}\,\fint\limits_{2^{-k-1}t}^{2^{-k}t}\fint\limits_{B(x_{j},2^{-k}t)}|s^{-(\beta+\alpha)}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Taking suprema with respect to the average integrals and using the property
|J_{k}|\leq C2^{kd}
implies

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k(d+q\alpha)}\sum\limits_{j\in J_{k}}\sup\limits_{\tau>0}\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\fint\limits_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\tau}\fint\limits_{B(z,\tau)}|s^{-(\beta+\alpha)}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-kq\alpha}\|f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}},

where we used the fact that
\alpha>0
in the last step. Thus, we have shown that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\fint\limits_{B(x,t)}|s^{-\beta}f(s,y)|^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}}

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
. Taking suprema in
x
and
t
concludes
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,\infty,q}_{\beta+\alpha}\subset\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q}_{\beta;\alpha}
.
∎

3.5.
Real interpolation of tent spaces

In this section, we present a real interpolation theory for
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces. First, we show that
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
forms a real interpolation scale in the parameter
p
, see Theorem
3.28
. Its proof is based on the characterization via discrete local means (Proposition
3.15
) and is inspired by
[
14
, Thm. 6.4 + Cor. 6.7]
, where the authors show analogous results for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces. Second, it was already shown in
[
4
, Prop. 4.4]
that
\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces for
p<\infty
can be obtained by real interpolation of
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces, in the same way as Besov spaces can be derived from Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, see Section
1
. However, there does not exist a corresponding characterization of the endpoint space
\mathrm{Z}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
. We will treat the missing endpoint characterization in Proposition
3.30
.

We start with a brief introduction to real interpolation theory and refer to the monograph
[
7
, Chp. 3]
for a detailed exposition. Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple, that is, a pair of (quasi-)Banach spaces
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
that are continuous embedded in a Hausdorff topological vector space. Then we define the (quasi-)Banach space
X_{0}+X_{1}=\{x_{0}+x_{1}:x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\}
equipped with the (quasi-)norm

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}\coloneqq\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\\
x=x_{0}+x_{1}\end{subarray}}\|x_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}.

For every
x\in X_{0}+X_{1}
and
t>0
we define the
K
-functional

\displaystyle K(t,x,X_{0},X_{1})\coloneqq\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\\
x=x_{0}+x_{1}\end{subarray}}\|x_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+t\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}.

Moreover, we define the
K_{\infty}
-functional

\displaystyle K_{\infty}(t,x,X_{0},X_{1})\coloneqq\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\\
x=x_{0}+x_{1}\end{subarray}}\max\Big\{\|x_{0}\|_{X_{0}},t\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}\Big\}.

It is easy to see that
K_{\infty}(t,x,X_{0},X_{1})\simeq K(t,x,X_{0},X_{1})
. Lastly, we define the
E
-functional

\displaystyle E(t,x,X_{0},X_{1})\coloneqq\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in X_{1}\\
\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}\leq t\end{subarray}}\|x-x_{1}\|_{X_{0}}.

It was observed in
[
14
, Sec. 6]
, that
K_{\infty}
is the right inverse of
E
and that, consequently, one has

(3.38)

\displaystyle K_{\infty}(t,x,X_{0},X_{1})\leq CK_{\infty}(t,y,Y_{0},Y_{1})

for all
t>0
if and only if

(3.39)

\displaystyle E(Ct,x,X_{0},X_{1})\leq CE(t,y,Y_{0},Y_{1})

for all
t>0
.
Finally, for
0<\theta<1
and
0<q\leq\infty
, we define the real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,q}
as the set of all
x\in X_{0}+X_{1}
such that

(3.40)

\displaystyle\|x\|_{\theta,q}\coloneqq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\theta q}\big[K(t,x,X_{0},X_{1})\big]^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

It is known that
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,q}
is a (quasi-)Banach space equipped with the (quasi-)norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,q}
. Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 3.28
.

Let
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
and
0<q,r\leq\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta,p}=\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta},

where
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
.

The strategy to prove this theorem is similar to the corresponding result for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, see
[
14
, Thm. 6.4]
. There, one uses the so-called
\varphi
-transform to reduce matters to sequence spaces and standard interpolation theory for
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-spaces. In our situation, we will use the discrete characterization from Section
3.2
to use their derived identities and imitate their procedure. However, the reader is advised to keep a copy of the mentioned reference at hand. We start with the following key result.

Theorem 3.29
.

Let
0<p_{0}<\infty
,
0<q,r\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for every
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}+\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
we have

\displaystyle K(t,f,\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})\simeq K(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty}).

Proof.

For simplicity, we present the proof only for finite exponents
q
and
r
. The case of infinite exponents follows with the usual modifications. We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1:
K(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})\lesssim K(t,f,\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})
.
Let
f=f_{0}+f_{1}\in\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}+\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
be an arbitrary but fixed decomposition. Recall the definitions of
m^{q,r}_{\beta,Q,\frac{1}{4}}(f)
and
m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)
in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). Then, by subadditivity, we get

\displaystyle m^{q,r}_{\beta,Q,\frac{1}{4}}(f)\leq C_{q}\big(m^{q,r}_{\beta,Q,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{0})+m^{q,r}_{\beta,Q,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{1})\big)

for some constant
C_{q}>0
depending only on
q
, which implies

(3.41)

\displaystyle m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\leq C_{q}\big(m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{0})(x)+m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{1})(x)\big)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Next, define the functions

\displaystyle g_{0}(x)\coloneqq\min\{m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x),C_{q}m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{0})(x)\}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{1}(x)=m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)-g_{0}(x).

First, notice that
m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)=g_{0}+g_{1}
. Second, by Proposition
3.15
, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}}}\leq C_{q}\|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{0})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}}}\simeq\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}}.

Third, by a case distinction for
g_{0}
, and (
3.41
), we have

\displaystyle|g_{1}(x)|\leq|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)-C_{q}m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{0})(x)|\leq C_{q}|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{1})(x)|

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, by Proposition
3.15
, it follows

\displaystyle\|g_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\leq C_{q}\|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{8}}(f_{1})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\simeq\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Using both norm estimates of
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
yields

\displaystyle K(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}}}+t\|g_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}}+t\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}.

Since this holds for every decomposition
f=f_{0}+f_{1}\in\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}+\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
, we get

\displaystyle K(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})\lesssim K(t,f,\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}),

which proves the first direction.

Step 2:
K(t,f,\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})\lesssim K(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})
.

Due to the equivalence of (
3.38
) and (
3.39
), as well as the equivalence of the
K
and
K_{\infty}
-functionals, it suffices to show that

(3.42)

\displaystyle E(Ct,f,\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})\leq CE(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})

for all
t>0
and some
C>0
.
We start by defining for every
Q\in\square
and
t>0
the subsets

(3.43)

\displaystyle Q_{t}^{+}\coloneqq Q\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)>t\}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{t}^{-}\coloneqq Q\setminus Q_{t}^{+}.

Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle A^{+}_{t}\coloneqq\Big\{Q\in\square:|Q_{t}^{+}|>\frac{|Q|}{2}\Big\}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{-}_{t}\coloneqq\Big\{Q\in\square:|Q_{t}^{-}|\geq\frac{|Q|}{2}\Big\}.

Observe that
\square=A^{+}_{t}\cup A^{-}_{t}
. Thus,

\displaystyle f_{0}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{Q\in A^{+}_{t}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{Q}}f\quad\text{and}\quad f_{1}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{Q\in A^{-}_{t}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{Q}}f

satisfy
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
. Next, we introduce the subsets

\displaystyle E^{+}_{Q}\coloneq\big\{x\in Q:G^{q,r}_{\beta,Q}(f)(x)\leq m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\big\}\cap Q^{+}_{t}

if
Q\in A_{t}^{+}
, and

\displaystyle E^{-}_{Q}\coloneq\big\{x\in Q:G^{q,r}_{\beta,Q}(f)(x)\leq m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\big\}\cap Q^{-}_{t}

if
Q\in A_{t}^{-}
. By definition of
m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)
in (
3.9
), we observe that

\displaystyle\big|\big\{x\in Q:G^{q,r}_{\beta,Q}(f)(x)\leq m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\big\}\big|

\displaystyle=|Q|-\big|\big\{x\in Q:G^{q,r}_{\beta,Q}(f)(x)>m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\big\}\big|

\displaystyle\geq|Q|-\frac{1}{4}|Q|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4}|Q|.

Since
|Q^{\pm}_{t}|\geq\frac{|Q|}{2}
, we conclude by a set-theoretical argument that

\displaystyle|E_{Q}^{\pm}|=\big|\big\{x\in Q:G^{q,r}_{\beta,Q}(f)(x)\leq m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\big\}\cap Q^{\pm}_{t}\big|\geq\frac{|Q|}{4}.

Thus, we can apply Lemma
3.14
to
E^{+}_{Q}
in place of
E_{Q}
to obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle\simeq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{E^{+}_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{p_{0}}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using the definitions of
f_{0}
and
Q_{t}^{+}
, with the support properties of Whitney boxes
\bar{Q}
, we get

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle\simeq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in A^{+}_{t}}\mathbf{1}_{E^{+}_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{p_{0}}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.44)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)>t\big\}}\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in A^{+}_{t}}\mathbf{1}_{E^{+}_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{p_{0}}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Similarly, using Lemma
3.14
to
E^{-}_{Q}
in place of
E_{Q}
, we derive

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{Q\subset P}\mathbf{1}_{E^{-}_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Using the definitions of
f_{1}
and
Q_{t}^{-}
, and the support properties of Whitney boxes
\bar{Q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\fint\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subset P^{-}_{t}\\
Q\in A_{t}^{-}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{E^{-}_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.45)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\frac{1}{|P|}\int\limits_{P_{t}^{-}}\sum\limits_{Q\in A_{t}^{-}}\mathbf{1}_{E^{-}_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where we used
E_{Q}^{-}\subset Q_{t}^{-}\subset P_{t}^{-}
in the last step.
Due to the observations (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) and the definitions of
E_{Q}^{\pm}
, we can utilize the inequality (5.10) of
[
14
]
, which reads in our situation as follows:

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{Q\in\square}\mathbf{1}_{E^{\pm}_{Q}}(x)|Q|^{-\frac{\beta q}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}\big(\bar{Q},\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s}{s^{d+1}}\big)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)

for every

for some generic constant

, and

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|_{\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}}\leq C_{1}\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\frac{1}{|P|}\int\limits_{P_{t}^{-}}|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C_{1}\sup\limits_{P\in\square}\bigg(\frac{1}{|P|}\int\limits_{P_{t}^{-}}t^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C_{1}t,

for some generic constant
C_{1}>0
, where we used the definition of

from (

) in the second step. Both estimates of

and

imply the following bound on the

\displaystyle E(C_{1}t,f,\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})\leq C_{0}\bigg(\int\limits_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)>t\}}\big|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\big|^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

Finally, we claim that the right-hand side of this inequality can be estimated by

E(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})
. Indeed, if
g\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)>t\}}\big|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)(x)\big|^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x

With Theorem
3.29
at hand, we can reduce Theorem
3.28
to the well-known real interpolation of
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-spaces combined with the reiteration theorem for real interpolation spaces.

Proof of Theorem
3.28
.

We first prove the theorem in the endpoint case
0<p_{0}<p_{1}=\infty
. Afterwards, we conclude by a reiteration argument.

Case 1:
0<p_{0}<p_{1}=\infty
. Let
f\in\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta}+\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta}
. Then, by definition of real interpolation spaces (
3.40
) and Theorem
3.29
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta,p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\theta q}\big[K(t,f,\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})\big]^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\theta q}\big[K(t,m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f),\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})\big]^{q}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)\|_{(\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})_{\theta,p}}.

It is well-known that
(\mathrm{L}^{p_{0}},\mathrm{L}^{\infty})_{\theta,p}=\mathrm{L}^{p}
if
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta,p}}\simeq\|m^{q,r}_{\beta,\frac{1}{4}}(f)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\simeq\|f\|_{\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}},

where we used Proposition
3.15
in the last step.

Case 2:
0<p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
. Let
0<\tilde{p}<p_{0}
and
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{\tilde{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{\tilde{p}}.

Then, Case 1, together with the reiteration theorem for quasi-Banach spaces (
[
7
, Thm. 3.11.5]
), imply

\displaystyle(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta,p}

\displaystyle=\Big((\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta_{0},p_{0}},(\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta_{1},p_{1}}\Big)_{\theta,p}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})_{(1-\theta)\theta_{0}+\theta\theta_{1},p}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}=\frac{(1-\theta)(1-\theta_{0})}{\tilde{p}}+\frac{\theta(1-\theta_{1})}{\tilde{p}}=\frac{1-[(1-\theta)\theta_{0}+\theta\theta_{1}]}{\tilde{p}}.

Hence, we conclude

\displaystyle(\mathrm{T}^{p_{0},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{p_{1},q,r}_{\beta})_{\theta,p}=(\mathrm{T}^{\tilde{p},q,r}_{\beta},\mathrm{T}^{\infty,q,r}_{\beta})_{(1-\theta)\theta_{0}+\theta\theta_{1},p}=\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta},

where we used Case 1 in the last step again.
∎

We finish this section by presenting a real interpolation result for
\mathrm{T}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}
-spaces in the parameter
q
. It is known from
[
4
, Prop. 4.4]

Proposition 3.30
.

Let
0<p\leq\infty
and
0<q,q_{0},q_{1},r\leq\infty
. Furthermore, let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\beta_{0}\neq\beta_{1}

\displaystyle(\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{0},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{T}^{p,q_{1},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}=\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta},

where
\beta=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}
.

Proof.

By the real interpolation result
[
4
, Thm. 4.1]
for
\mathrm{Z}
-spaces and the nesting property of Lemma
3.21

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}^{p,q,r}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q_{0}\},r}_{\beta_{0}},\mathrm{Z}^{p,\min\{p,q_{1}\},r}_{\beta_{1}})_{\theta,q}

Proof of Theorem

By the definition of the clique expansion, the condition
(i)
is necessary. It is not difficult to see that condition
(ii)
is necessary as well. Namely, let us denote by

the

-th clique of the expansion and assume, without loosing generality, that

is colored with colors

. Of course,

-colorable if and only if there exists such a coloring of

that at least
s

are repeated on

, where

and therefore
s=k_{1}+k_{2n}+k_{2n+1}-k
. Let us say that if

, then our task becomes trivial - the

algorithm starting from

and cut at

can be completed to a proper coloring. Thus, we can assume that

How many colors from

can be repeated at most on

? Consider a simplified problem, how many colors from

can be repeated on

? If

is a set of colors that we would like to repeat (if possible) on

, which contradicts the assumption). Therefore,

contains no more than

colors from

Thus, on

we can repeat at most

desired colors, on

, and so on. Finally, on

. In order to obtain a proper coloring, we need at least

s=k_{1}+k_{2n}+k_{2n+1}-k\leq(n-1)k-k_{2}-\ldots-k_{2n-1}

equivalent to
(ii)
.

It is easy to show that conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
are sufficient. Let us start coloring cliques with

-CC algorithm and let

be the smallest index such that

contains colors

and

. Such an index exists by the condition

. Then, we can color every clique

, with consecutively increasing colors

and then (if

) with consecutively decreasing colors

. Every clique

. It is easy to see that

has at least

, thus, we can always color

with remaining colors.

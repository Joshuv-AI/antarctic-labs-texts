A dataset of patients with isolated and syndromic optic neuropathies linked to RTN4IP1 genetic variants
Authors: Aude Rocatcher, Xavier Dieu, Valérie Desquiret-Dumas, Benjamin Billiet, Nicolas Chassaing, Hélène Dollfus, Isabelle Meunier, Marie-Bénédicte Rougier, Yann Polfrit, Guy Lenaers, Dan Milea, Philippe Gohier, Delphine Mirebeau-Prunier, Patrizia Amati-Bonneau, Pascal Reynier, Marc Ferré
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Biallelic pathogenic variants of the Reticulon 4 interacting protein 1 (RTN4IP1) gene are responsible for optic atrophy, either isolated or associated with ataxia, mental retardation, and seizures. They are identified as a cause of hereditary optic neuropathy in 7% of patients diagnosed before the age of 20. We have built a dataset for this gene by collating all the clinical cases available in the literature, and unpublished patients diagnosed at our centre, using standard nomenclature to describe both the molecular and phenotypic features. We performed a comprehensive data analysis, based on computational reasoning, to provide an overall picture of the dataset and validate its relevance. This new dataset provides an updated genetic map of the reported pathogenic variants, an ontological annotation of phenotypic abnormalities in a grid format showing clinical heterogeneity, and a full interoperability with the databases of other genetic forms of optic neuropathies.

Background & Summary

The Reticulon 4 interacting protein 1 gene (RTN4IP1; MIM:610502) is located on chromosome 6q21 and includes 9 translated exons encoding Reticulon-4-interacting protein 1 (RTN4IP1; Swiss-Prot:RT4I1_HUMAN), a mitochondrial targeted protein with a quinone oxidoreductase activity1. Functional analysis demonstrated that RTN4IP1 mutations cause a deficit in the enzymatic activities of mitochondrial complexes I and IV and result in abnormal retinal ganglion cells neurite outgrowth2.

Biallelic pathogenic variants of this gene have been associated with either isolated optic atrophy2, due to the loss of retinal ganglion cells that form the optic nerve, or with optic atrophy 10 with or without ataxia, mental retardation, and seizures (OPA10; MIM:616732)3. More specific clinical presentations have recently extended the clinical spectrum, such as generalised chorea4 and choreoathetosis5 reported in two different probands. Recent studies have shown that RTN4IP1 recessive optic neuropathy is systematically associated with such a progressive rod-cone dystrophy6,7,which appears in the second stage of the disease and was undiagnosed in previous published cases, justifying the reappraisal of the ophthalmological data.

We have recently shown, in a study related to more than 2,000 cases of optic neuropathies, that RTN4IP1 was causal in 7% of patients diagnosed before 20 years of age (2% for all ages)8. Here we provide a review of the literature, listing a total of 40 individuals from 25 families with inherited optic neuropathy and RTN4IP1 variants, including the incidental finding of a variant of this gene that was apparently not the primary cause of the phenotype2–7,9–15. The description by our team of 9 additional unpublished cases from 7 families brings the total to 49 cases reported to date.

In this article we describe the construction of the first RTN4IP1 dataset, providing a significant set for this rare disease, giving a global vision of the associated clinical synopsis.

Methods

Nomenclature

All names, symbols, and accession numbers were checked for correspondence with current official names indicated by the Human Genome Organization (HUGO) Gene Nomenclature Committee16 and the Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man database (OMIM)17. The phenotype descriptions are based on the Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO), indicating the HPO term name and identifier18. RTN4IP1 variants are described according to both the NCBI genomic reference sequence (RefSeq:NG_047205.1) and transcript reference sequence (RefSeq:NM_032730.4), including the reference sequence for 9 exons encoding a protein of 396 amino acids (RefSeq:NP_116119.2). The numbering of the nucleotides corresponds to that of the cDNA, with “+1” representing the “A” of the ATG translation initiation codon in the reference sequence, as recommended by the nomenclature of the Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS)19 version 21.02 (released March 15, 2024).

Although the current official HGVS recommendations prescribe description beyond cDNA, we have indicated them for clarity by following a guideline envisaged for the open issue; the notation “c.-477_*968{0}” indicates the absence of the entire RTN4IP1 gene, from the first nucleotide of the first exon to the last nucleotide of the last exon, the limits of which are beyond and not precisely defined.

Associated or incidental variants have been described according to transcript reference sequence of the Homo sapiens mitochondrion, complete genome (mtDNA; RefSeq:NC_012920.1) or the gene Potassium channel, subfamily V, member 2 (KCNV2; RefSeq:NM_133497.3). When variants outside RTN4IP1 are present (mtDNA or KCNV2), the gene symbol is prefixed in variant_varnomen to preserve traceability, and the multi-locus context is encoded in variant_status. ACMG-based variant_classification is reported for RTN4IP1 variants only to avoid confusing causality across loci.

Data collection and analysis

We generated the dataset from the RTN4IP1 locus-specific database (LSDB) version RTN4IP1:240308 (last updated on March 8, 2024), which we built and curate, and now available on the Global Variome shared LOVD server (https://www.lovd.nl/RTN4IP1; or through the Mitochondrial Dynamics variation portal: https://mitodyn.org). The causative variants were gathered from scientific literature on the initial description of this genetic cause of optic neuropathy, from 2015 to June 2024, using the NCBI PubMed search tool20. The positions of variants in the reference transcripts were determined and updated according to the HGVS nomenclature version 21.0219. Proper naming was verified and reestablished when necessary using the Mutalyzer 3.0.7 Normalizer21. Information was taken from the original or review publications. If the same patient or variant was reported in more than one article, then it was recorded only once in the dataset with reference to all publications. This data set was completed by all the cases that were diagnosed in our clinical molecular diagnosis laboratory. The clinical classification of variants was based on standardised criteria directed on the clinical consequences as published or submitted, indicated using an enriched system including inheritance—e.g. pathogenic (recessive), likely pathogenic (recessive), VUS, likely benign, benign—derived from the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) standards and guidelines22. Table 1 includes the phyloP23 and CADD24 (PHRED-like C-score) annotations as support. Other computational predictors of pathogenicity were not included because they were not consistently available in the curated literature. This is a limitation of the dataset.

For the statistical analysis, the HPO terms were verified and prepared using ontologyIndex version 2.12 and ontologyPlot version 1.725 R packages within R version 4.3.326, to read the OBO file version hp/releases/2024-04-0427. Hierarchical clustering was performed on the binary HPO term inclusion matrix using hclust with complete linkage. Distances between individuals (rows) were computed using dist(…, method = “binary”), and HPO terms (columns) were clustered using a correlation-based distance (1–r) computed across individuals. Our data were crossed with the external source Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM) retrieved from hpo-web@1.8.1328.

Ethics approval, data protection and consent

This study uses previously collected routine clinical and genetic information from patients referred for hereditary optic neuropathies at the University Hospital of Angers, and includes previously published case reports. Personal data processing for research purposes was registered in the internal record of processing activities at the University Hospital of Angers, in accordance with the regulations of the French data protection authority (Commission nationale de l’informatique et des libertés, CNIL), under reference ar26-0002v0, and validated by the Data Protection Officer (reference 3490000031/559947). Participants or their legal guardians, where applicable, provided written informed consent for genetic analyses, for the reuse of their clinical and genetic data for research purposes, and for the sharing of the released de-identified dataset. Written informed consent was obtained at the time of genetic diagnosis. The released dataset does not contain direct personal identifiers. Phenotypes are coded using Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO) terms, while variants are reported using Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS) nomenclature.

Data Records

The RTN4IP1 dataset is available on the figshare repository as a text-based file storing tabular data. Records are separated by newlines, and values within a record are separated by tab characters (tab-separated values, TSV)29. The content of each column is described in Table 2. Four unique RTN4IP1 LSDB identifiers are provided for each record for traceability and to obtain further information or any updates. They are used to build the URL to access the corresponding web page, as described in Table 2. These data can also be accessed via an application programming interface (API), a web service that allows for simple queries and retrieval of basic genes and variant information (documentation available on the database’s web page)30; additionally, it serves as a public beacon in The Global Alliance for Genomics and Health Beacon Project31. The three main data elements are the unique identifier of the individual concerned by the record (individual_id, primary key), its genotype described according to the official nomenclature (variant_varnomen) and the description of the associated phenotype anomalies observed (hpo_term), described from the root of all terms in the Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO# HP:0000001). Data Overview

The dataset comprises 49 individual records (25 males, 21 females, and three records with unspecified gender). Forty unique sequence variations in the RTN4IP1 gene have been reported in individuals in the LSDB of the gene (https://www.lovd.nl/RTN4IP1; or through the Mitochondrial Dynamics variation portal: https://mitodyn.org), of which 30 are represented in phenotyped individuals in our dataset (highlighted in Fig. 1). Each genotype is observed in one or two individuals (highlighted in yellow), with the exception of the homozygous genotypes c.[308 G > A];[308 G > A] carried by 16 individuals2–4,7 (highlighted in strong orange) and c.[475 G > T];[475 G > T] carried by 6 individuals in two consanguineous Saudi families9 (highlighted in light orange). Genetic variants are predominantly homozygous (labelled “HMZ” in red in Fig. 2) or compound heterozygous (labelled “cHTZ”); when a single heterozygous variant has been identified, the genotype has been described as expected compound heterozygous (labelled “cHTZe”), the mode of inheritance being recessive. The majority are classified as pathogenic (labelled “Pr” in dark blue in Fig. 2) and three as likely pathogenic (labelled “LPr”); eight are considered of uncertain significance (labelled “VUS”) according to standardised criteria22, with the clinical status of the individuals suggesting a pathogenic effect. A total of 156 unique HPO terms were annotated from original publications or unpublished records from our centre; this corresponds to 418 unique HPO terms after including parent terms inferred by ontological relationships; a total of 2,088 HPO terms were analysed, each assigned to 1 to 49 patients for the most common term, autosomal recessive inheritance (HP:0000007). Supplementary Fig. 1 provides a comprehensive overview of the ontological annotation of phenotypic abnormalities in a grid format (non-informative terms which are headings for the sub-ontologies Mode of inheritance, HP:0000005, and Clinical course, HP:0031797, are not shown), highlighting the diversity and phenotypic specificity of each individual case, with a common base of globally shared clinical signs which stand out (at the top of the grid classified by a grouping algorithm).

For visualisation purposes, Figs. 2, 3 display only HPO terms annotated in at least 10% of individuals (≥5/49) to improve readability and focus on recurring features. In the same way, the common base of phenotypes shared by patients diagnosed as suffering from OPA10 can be found grouped together. All individuals listed with at least one RTN4IP1 variant are described with at least one phenotypic abnormality (HP:0000118), with the exception of one individual (#0426910, the column highlighted in grey in Fig. 2); this was an incidental finding of a heterozygous RTN4IP1 variant in a healthy individual whose causative variation was in KCNV2. The most frequent abnormal phenotype in all patients was optic neuropathy, as expected, except in one individual (#0327904, the column highlighted in red in Fig. 2); the reason is that ophthalmological examination was not performed in this patient, so the list of phenotypes in this individual should be considered partial. Thus, these two individuals (#0426910 and #0327904) are grouped together within the grid, and were integrated into the dataset for completeness, but their associated data must be considered as outliers and interpreted with caution. Technical Validation

To ensure internal consistency and reproducibility, we implemented the following quality control measures during curation. We harmonised variant nomenclature against the stated reference sequences and normalised it using Mutalyzer Normalizer when necessary. We resolved duplicate reports of the same individual and variant across multiple publications by retaining a single record and listing all associated PubMed identifiers (PMIDs) for traceability. The HPO terms were validated against the specified HPO release (hp/releases/2024-04-04) and expanded to include ancestor terms for ontology-aware analyses. To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the manuscript, we performed rigorous internal consistency checks to verify the mandatory fields, permitted values, and consistency between fields. Furthermore, we generated all figures in the manuscript using the dataset and versioned scripts to ensure reproducibility.

Usage Notes

The RTN4IP1 dataset is provided as a tab-separated values (TSV) file on figshare29 and can be imported into standard analysis environments (e.g., R, Python, spreadsheets) for filtering and aggregation. Each record contains unique identifiers (such as individual_id, phenotype_id and variant_id). This allows users to easily link back to the original LSDB entries for further information and updates, and ensures traceability over time. Phenotypes are encoded using HPO identifiers (field hpo_term), and variant descriptions follow HGVS nomenclature (field variant_varnomen). This promotes interoperability with external resources and ensures consistent and reproducible querying.

Users should be aware that two records have been kept for thoroughness, but should handle them with care in genotype-based analyses. Specifically, individual #0426910 is an incidental heterozygous RTN4IP1 finding in a healthy person with the causal variant in KCNV2 (as indicated by grey shading in Fig. 2). Individual #0327904 has a partial phenotype description due to the absence of an ophthalmological examination (as marked in red in Fig. 2). These outliers can be excluded in some applications, such as term frequency summaries and phenotype similarity analyses, or retained with explicit labelling to preserve the full curated landscape of reported RTN4IP1 cases.

All the scripts used to generate the manuscript’s analyses and figures are openly available on figshare29 and can be reused or adapted to support variant-centred queries (such as comparing phenotypic profiles across genotypes) or phenotype-centred queries (such as retrieving individuals annotated with specific HPO terms or term sets).

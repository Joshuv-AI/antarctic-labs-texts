Pathwise regularity of solutions for a class of elliptic SPDEs with symmetric Lévy noise
Juan J. Jiménez

Abstract
In this article, we investigate the existence and uniqueness of random‐field solutions to the elliptic SPDE − ℒ ​ u = ξ ˙ -\mathcal{L}u=\dot{\xi} on a bounded domain D D with Dirichlet boundary conditions u = 0 u=0 on ∂ D \partial D , driven by symmetric Lévy noise ξ ˙ \dot{\xi} . Under general sufficient conditions on the coefficients of the second‐order operator ℒ \mathcal{L} , we prove the existence of a mild solution via the corresponding Green’s function and show that the same framework applies to the spectral fractional Laplacian of power γ ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) \gamma\in(0,\infty) . In particular, whenever γ > d 2 \gamma>\tfrac{d}{2} , the solution admits a continuous modification.

2.1
Stochastic integration

Although various authors had introduced stochastic integrals for random measures since Paul Lévy’s seminal work
[
22
]
, the theory in
[
29
]
was pioneering in treating integrators that may not depend on time and instead rely solely on the topology of the spatial component, making this approach particularly suitable for solving elliptic SPDEs.

We say that
f
is a
simple function
if it is given by

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}1_{A_{i}}(x),

(11)

where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
are disjoint sets in
\mathcal{B}(D)
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
the collection of simple functions of the form (
11
). If
f
is a simple function of the form
(
11

with respect to
\dot{\xi}
by:

\langle\dot{\xi},f\rangle=\int_{D}f(x)\xi(dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\xi(A_{i}),

and stochastic integral of
f
on the set

, with respect to
\dot{\xi}
, by:

\langle\dot{\xi},f1_{A}\rangle=\int_{A}f(x)\xi(dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\xi(A\cap A_{i}).

Note that
\langle\dot{\xi},1_{A}\rangle=\xi(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
.

Definition 2.1

A Borel measurable function

is

-integrable if there exists a sequence

such that

,

ii)

, the sequence
\{\langle\dot{\xi},f_{n}1_{A}\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}

.

If
f
is
\dot{\xi}
-integrable, we set

\langle\dot{\xi},f\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{=}}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle\dot{\xi},f_{n}\rangle.

(12)

In
[

]
, one finds a complete characterization of the deterministic functions integrable with respect to

. More precisely, by Theorem 2.7 in

Moreover, if

\dot{\xi}

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{iu\langle\dot{\xi},f\rangle}\right]=\exp{\Big(\int_{D}\Psi(f(x))dx\Big)}.

2.2
Fractional Sobolev spaces

In this section, we briefly recall the main ingredients of fractional Sobolev spaces on
D
. Consider the eigenvalue problem for the Dirichlet Laplacian:

\displaystyle-\Delta e_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}e_{n}(x),\quad x\in D,

\displaystyle e_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\quad\quad x\in\partial D,

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator. The eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are positive, satisfy
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, and the corresponding eigenfunctions
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
can be chosen to form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(D)
with respect to the inner product:

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}=\int_{D}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx.

Let
g\in L^{2}(D)
, we define the Fourier coefficients of
g
with respect to
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
by

\mathcal{F}_{k}[g]=\langle g,e_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(D)},\;\;\text{for each $k\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Let
\mathcal{D}(D)
denote the space of test functions on
D
, and let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(D)
be its dual, i.e., the space of distributions on
D
. Abusing notation, we will use the same symbol as in (
12
) for the distributional action on
\mathcal{D}(D)
. That is, for any
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(D)
, we write

F(\phi)=\langle F,\phi\rangle,\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{D}(D).

Let
E_{0}
be the set of functions
f:D\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

f(x)=\sum^{K}_{i=1}\alpha_{k}e_{k}(x),\quad\text{for $\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}$,}

and define, for
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|f\|_{H_{r}(D)}:=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{r}|\mathcal{F}_{k}[f]|^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}.

The completion of
E_{0}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{H_{r}}
is called the
Sobolev space of order r
. We denote this space as
H_{r}(D)
. Each element
\Phi\in H_{r}(D)
can be identified with a series of the form
\Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}(\Phi)e_{k}
, where
a_{k}(\Phi)\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\|\Phi\|_{H_{r}(D)}:=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{r}a_{k}(\Phi)^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}<\infty.

(15)

The series defining
\Phi
converges in the topology of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(D)
and in the
H_{r}(D)
-norm. Hence,

\mathcal{F}_{j}[\Phi]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}(\Phi)\mathcal{F}_{j}[e_{k}]=a_{j}(\Phi).

By Parseval’s identity, we can set
H_{-r}(D)
as the dual of
H_{r}(D).

The next result follows directly from Theorem 9.8 in Chapter 1 of
[
23
]
together with Lemma 2.18 in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
r>\tfrac{d}{2}
, then
H_{r}(D)\subset C^{0}(\overline{D})
.

Proof.

Let
H^{r}(D)
be the interpolation Sobolev space defined in (9.1) of Chapter 1 in
[
23
]
. By Lemma 2.18 in
[
8
]
, one has
H_{r}(D)\hookrightarrow H^{r}(D)
for all
r\geq 0
. The result then follows from Theorem 9.8 of Chapter 1 in
[
23
]
.

Proof.

For

: Set

A=\{y\in D\setminus\{x\}:G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)>1\},

Note that

\begin{split}\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}}(|zG_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{2}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz)&=\int_{A^{c}\times\mathbb{R}}(|zG_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{2}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz)\\
&+\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}}(|zG_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{2}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz).\end{split}

(22)

Note that the first integral on the right hand side of (

x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\to(1\wedge|x|^{2})
. On the other hand, note that then

\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}}(|zG_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{2}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz)\leq\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}}(|zG_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{p}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz).

Moreover, since
((c|z|^{p})\wedge 1)\leq c(|z|^{p}\wedge 1)

y\in A
, we get that:

\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}}(|zG_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{p}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz)\leq\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}}|G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{p}(|z|^{p}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz).

Note that

\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}}|G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{p}(|z|^{p}\wedge 1)dy\nu(dz)<\infty

as long as there exists
p\in(0,2]
such that

\int_{D}|G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{p}dy<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{p}\nu(dz)<\infty.

(23)

These conditions are satisfied for
d=2,3
if
p=2
, and for
d\geq 4
, we require

0<p<\frac{d}{d-2}.

Now, we will show that
u=u_{\text{gen}}
. Observe that
G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,\cdot)
is
\dot{\xi}
-integrable, it satisfies the
Poisson
\dot{\xi}
-integrable
, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle\dot{\xi},G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,\cdot)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{D}b\,G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,dy+\int_{D}\sigma\,G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,W_{\xi}(dy)+\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)

(24)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\int_{D_{l}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)=:I_{b}(x)+I_{W}(x)+I_{s}(x)+I_{l}(x),

where

D_{s}(x)=\{(y,z)\in D\times\mathbb{R}_{0};\,|G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)z|\leq 1\}

and

D_{l}(x)=\{(y,z)\in D\times\mathbb{R}_{0};\,|G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)z|>1\}.

Now we show that for any
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(D)
,

\int_{D}\bigl\lvert\langle\dot{\xi},\,G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,\cdot)\rangle\bigr\rvert\;\mu_{\phi}(dx)<\infty\quad\text{almost surely}.

By Hölder’s inequality, this will hold as soon as

u(\omega,\cdot)\;=\;\langle\dot{\xi},\,G_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,\cdot)\rangle\;\in\;L^{1}(D)\quad\text{for almost every }\omega\in\Omega.

By Theorem 8.30 in
[
18
]
,
the torsion problem

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}v(x)=1,&x\in D,\\
v(x)=0,&x\in\partial D,\end{cases}

(25)

admits a unique generalized solution
v\in C^{0}(\overline{D})
. Hence, the drift term

I_{b}(x)=b\,v(x)
is well defined and continuous on
D
.

For the term
I_{W}(x)
when
d\leq 3
, By Itô’s isometry and Assumption
3.1
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[|I_{W}(x)|^{2}\bigr]=\int_{D}G^{2}(x,y)\,dy\leq C_{D,d},

(26)

where
C_{D,d}>0
is a constant independent of
x
, which implies that
I_{W}(\omega,\cdot)\in L^{1}(D)
for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
.

For
I_{s}(x)
, notice that

\begin{split}I_{s}(x)&=\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z1_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)\\
&+\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz).\end{split}

By the symmetry of
\dot{\xi}
, and

\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,|z|1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,dy\nu(dz)\leq\mu\{(y,z)\in D\times\{|z|>1\}\},

it follows that

\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)=\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz).

Then, by linearity of the Lebesgue integral,

\begin{split}\int_{D}\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,|z|1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)dx&=\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}\Big(\int_{D}1_{D_{s}(x)}(y,z)G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)dx\Big)\,|z|1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)\\
&\leq\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}v(y)\,|z|1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)<\infty\,\,\mbox{a.s.}\end{split}

Now, by Itô isometry and (
20
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\Bigg|\int_{D_{s}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z1_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigg|^{2}\Bigg]&\leq\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}|G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)|^{p}|z|^{p}1_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}dy\nu(dz)\\
&\leq C_{D,d,p}\int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz),\end{split}

where
C_{D,d,p}
is a constant independent of
x
. Hence,
I_{s}(\omega,\cdot)\in L^{1}(D)
. For
I_{l}(x)
, note that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\Bigg|\int_{D_{l}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,z1_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigg|^{p}\right]&\leq\int_{D_{l}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}^{p}(x,y)\,|z|^{p}1_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}dy\nu(dz)\\
&\leq C_{D,d,p}\int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz).\end{split}

By the linearity of the Lebesgue integral, we get that

\begin{split}\int_{D}\int_{D_{l}(x)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,|z|1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)dx&\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}\Big(\int_{D}1_{D_{l}(x)}(y,z)G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)dx\Big)|z|1_{\{|z|>1\}}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)\\
&\leq\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}v(y)|z|1_{\{|z|>1\}}J_{\xi}(dy,dz)<\infty.\\
\end{split}

Consider the pure‐jump part of
\dot{\xi}
defined by

Z(A)=\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}1_{A}(y)\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)+\int_{D\times\{|z|>1\}}1_{A}(y)\,z\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz).

Then,

u(x)=I_{b}(x)+I_{W}(x)+\langle\dot{Z},\,G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,\cdot)\rangle,

with

\langle\dot{Z},\,G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,\cdot)\rangle=I_{s}(x)+I_{l}(x).

We apply Fubini’s theorem to
I_{b}
, the standard Fubini theorem for
L^{2}
-random measures to
I_{W}
when
d\leq 3
, and for the term
\langle\dot{Z},\,G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,\cdot)\rangle
, we apply the stochastic Fubini theorem for symmetric Lévy noises given by Theorem 5.1 in
[
13
]
. Hence, the mild solution
u
coincides with the generalized solution
u_{\mathrm{gen}}
.

∎

3.2
Examples

We will present the main elliptic operators to which Theorem
3.2
applies. Additionally, we extend the results of the previous section to a class of spectral operators.

The study of Green’s functions associated with elliptic operators dates back to early investigations of boundary value problems in the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, where they first appeared as integral kernels for the Laplace operator. Since then, Green’s functions have become indispensable in the analysis of both second-order and higher-order elliptic PDEs, and remain an active area of research up to the present time. A fundamental question in the theory of Green’s functions for elliptic operators, which continues to be actively studied, is to identify structural conditions on
\mathcal{L}
under which there exists a constant
C>0
such that

G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\;\leq\;C\,G_{\Delta}(x,y),\quad x,y\in D,\;x\neq y.

One of the earliest works to address this problem is
[
24
]
, later extended to bounded domains in
[
16
]
, which also provides our first example.

Example 3.3

(
Divergence operators
)
.

For
d\geq 3
, let
\mathcal{L}
be a divergence elliptic operator of the form

\mathcal{L}\equiv\textup{div}(K(x)\nabla),

(27)

where
K=(K_{ij}(x))
is a symmetric, positive‐definite coefficient matrix
d\times d
. Hence, by Theorem 1.1 in
[
16
]
,
G_{\mathcal{L}}
satisfies (
16
), which implies that there exists a unique mild solution to (
1
) for a symmetric Lévy noise
\dot{\xi}
with triplet
(b,0,\nu)
, and that (
20
) holds. For
d=3
, we may take
\dot{\xi}
with triplet
(b,\sigma,\nu)
without imposing any assumptions on the Lévy measure
\nu
.

When
\mathcal{L}
does not have the divergence form (
27
), it is well known that the Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}
does not satisfy the upper bound estimate (
16
), even if
A(x)
is uniformly continuous, bounded, and elliptic (see, for instance,
[
3
,
17
]
). Establishing when an estimate like (
16
) holds in the non-divergence case is a nontrivial problem. Recently, in
[
20
]
it was shown that if
A(x)
satisfies the following conditions, then
G_{\mathcal{L}}
satisfies (
16
). Define

\bar{\textbf{A}}_{\tilde{D}_{r}(x)}\;:=\;\frac{1}{|{\tilde{D}_{r}}(x)|}\int_{{\tilde{D}_{r}}(x)}\textbf{A}(\xi)\,d\xi,\quad\chi_{\textbf{A}}(x,r)\;:=\;\int_{\tilde{D}_{r}(x)}\bigl\|\textbf{A}(y)-\bar{\textbf{A}}_{\tilde{D}_{r}(x)}(y)\bigr\|\,dy,

where
\tilde{D}_{r}(x)=D\cap B_{r}(x)
. Additionally, set
\chi_{\textbf{A}}(r):=\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}\chi_{\textbf{A}}(x,r)
. We say that
A
satisfies the
Dini mean oscillation
condition on
D
if

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\chi_{\textbf{A}}(t)}{t}\,dt<\infty.

Example 3.4

(
Non-divergence operators
)
.

For
d\geq 3
, let
\mathcal{L}
be a non-divergence operator of the form

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{i,j}(x)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}},

(28)

where
\textbf{A}(x)=(a_{ij}(x))
satisfies the Dini mean oscillation condition on
D
. Hence, by Theorem 1.7 in
[
20
]
, we have that
G_{\mathcal{L}}
satisfies (
16
). Therefore,there exists a unique mild solution to (
1
) for a symmetric Lévy noise with triplet
(b,0,\nu)
, and that (
20
) holds. For
d=3
, we may take
\dot{\xi}
with triplet
(b,\sigma,\nu)
without imposing any assumptions on the Lévy measure
\nu
.

Although the following operator is not elliptic in the strict sense, the existence of a mild solution to (
1
) follows from Theorem
3.2
, which justifies presenting this problem in this section.

Spectral fractional Laplace operator:
We define the spectral power of
(-\Delta)
of order
\gamma>0
by

(-\Delta)^{\gamma}h\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}\,\mathcal{F}_{k}[h]\,e_{k}.

It can be shown that

(-\Delta)^{\gamma}:\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{R}}H_{r}(D)\;\to\;\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{R}}H_{r}(D).

When
\gamma=1
, this operator coincides with
-\Delta
. In what follows, we study (
1
) with
\mathcal{L}
replaced by
(-\Delta)^{\gamma}
, i.e.

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{\gamma}u=\dot{\xi}\,&\text{in }D,\\
u=0\,&\text{on }\partial D.\end{cases}

(29)

Recall that we are interested in studying a mild solution
u
of (
29
), i.e.

u(x)\;=\;\int_{D}G_{\gamma}(x,y)\,\xi(dy),

where
G_{\gamma}
is the Green’s function of
(-\Delta)^{\gamma}
, given by the spectral representation:

G_{\gamma}(x,y)\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e_{k}(x)\,e_{k}(y)}{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}}.

Theorem 3.5
.

For
\gamma>\frac{d}{4}
, equation (
29
) admits a unique mild solution for any symmetric Lévy noise
\dot{\xi}
with characteristic triplet
(b,\sigma,\nu)
. Moreover,
u
is also a generalized solution to (
1
) in the sense that it satisfies (
19
).

Proof.

We follow the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, in particular using condition (
23
) with
p=2
. It therefore suffices to show that

\int_{D}G_{\gamma}^{2}(x,y)\,dy<\infty.

(30)

By orthogonality,

\int_{D}G_{\gamma}(x,y)^{2}\,dy=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|e_{k}(x)|^{2}}{\lambda_{k}^{2\gamma}}=\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}t^{-2\gamma}\,V(dt,x),

where

V(t,x):=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\leq t}|e_{k}(x)|^{2}.

Since
\partial D
is smooth, Theorem 8.2 of
[
1
]
gives

V(t,x)\;\leq\;C\,t^{\frac{d}{2}},\quad\forall\,x\in D.

Hence, by Abel’s summation formula (see Theorem 4.2 in
[
2
]
) and
\gamma>\frac{d}{4}
,

\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}t^{-2\gamma}\,V(dt,x)\;\leq\;C\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\infty}u^{-2\gamma-1+\frac{d}{2}}\,du\;<\;\infty.

∎

4
Path-wise regularity

In this section, we prove the pathwise regularity properties of the solution given in Theorem
3.2
. Since the Gaussian case has been widely studied, we will omit the continuous and drift components of
\dot{\xi}
, i.e. set
b=\sigma=0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.5
, the solution
u
of (
29
) satisfies

u(\omega,\cdot)\in H_{r}(D)\quad\text{for almost all }\omega\in\Omega

for every
r<2\gamma-\tfrac{d}{2}
. Moreover, if
\gamma\in(\frac{d}{2},\infty)
, then
u
admits a continuous modification in
\overline{D}
.

Proof.

For
\gamma>\frac{d}{4}
, by Theorem 5.1 in
[
13
]
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathcal{F}_{k}[\langle\dot{\xi},G_{\gamma}(x,\cdot)\rangle]=\langle\dot{\xi},\frac{e_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}}\rangle\quad\mbox{a.s.}

Consequently, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an event
\Omega_{k}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{k})=1
such that, for all
\omega\in\Omega_{k}
,

\mathcal{F}_{k}[u(\omega,\cdot)]=\int_{D}\frac{e_{k}(y)}{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}}\,\xi(\omega,dy)=\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}\frac{e_{k}(y)}{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}}\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(\omega,dy,dz)+\int_{D\times\{|z|>1\}}\frac{e_{k}(y)}{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}}\,z\,J_{\xi}(\omega,dy,dz).

Setting
\displaystyle\Omega_{\infty}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{k}
, it follows that
\mathcal{F}_{k}[u]
is well-defined for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
on
\Omega_{\infty}
. Hence, by Hölder’s inequality,

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H_{r}(D)}^{2}&\leq 2\Biggl(\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{r}\Bigl|\!\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}\frac{e_{k}(y)}{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}}\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigr|^{2}\;+\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{r}\Bigl|\!\int_{D\times\{|z|>1\}}\frac{e_{k}(y)}{\lambda_{k}^{\gamma}}\,z\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigr|^{2}\Biggr)\\
&\leq 2\Biggl(\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{\,r-2\gamma}\Bigl|\!\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}e_{k}(y)\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigr|^{2}\\
&\qquad\quad+\;J_{\xi}(D\times\{|z|>1\})\int_{D\times\{|z|>1\}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{\,r-2\gamma}\,\lvert e_{k}(y)\rvert^{2}\,\lvert z\rvert^{2}\;J_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Biggr).\end{split}

Observe that

\int_{D\times\{|z|>1\}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\,r-2\gamma}\,\lvert e_{k}(y)\rvert^{2}\,\lvert z\rvert^{2}\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)<\infty.

By (A.3)-Lemma A.1 in
[
9
]
,

\sup_{y\in D}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\,r-2\gamma}\,\lvert e_{k}(y)\rvert^{2}<\infty\quad\text{for }r<2\gamma-\tfrac{d}{2}.

Similarly, for
r<2\gamma-\tfrac{d}{2}
,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\,r-2\gamma}\Bigl|\!\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}e_{k}(y)\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigr|^{2}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Indeed, by the Itô isometry gives

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl|\!\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}e_{k}(y)\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigr|^{2}\Bigr]=\|e_{k}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{2}\,\nu(dz),

so that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\,r-2\gamma}\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl|\!\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}e_{k}(y)\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)\Bigr|^{2}\Bigr]=\Bigl(\int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{2}\,\nu(dz)\Bigr)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\,r-2\gamma}<\infty.

Finally, we proved that
u(\omega,\cdot)\in H_{r}(D)
almost surely for every
r<2\gamma-\tfrac{d}{2}
, by Theorem
2.2
,
u(\omega,\cdot)
is continuous on
\overline{D}
almost surely by choosing

\tfrac{d}{2}<r<2\gamma-\tfrac{d}{2}.

Such an
r
exists precisely when
\gamma>\tfrac{d}{2}
. Hence, the condition
\gamma>\tfrac{d}{2}
is necessary and sufficient for
u
to be continuous on
\overline{D}
.

∎

The proofs of the following results adapt the argument of Theorem
4.1
, using the same techniques in the eigenbasis of
\mathcal{L}
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an elliptic operator satisfying (
17
) or (
16
). Then the solution
u
of (
1
) admits a version taking values in
H_{r}(D)
for every
r\;<\;2-\frac{d}{2}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
1
) with
\mathcal{L}=\Delta
. Then
u
admits a continuous, bounded modification on
\overline{D}
if and only if
d=1
. If
d\geq 2
, then
\|u\|_{\infty}=\infty
a.s.

Proof.

The existence of a continuous modification of
u
when
\gamma=1
follows immediately from Theorem
4.1
. Alternatively, one can argue directly as follows. Since the Green function
G_{\Delta}
is continuous on
D\times D
only for
d=1
, by the Lévy Itô decomposition, we have:

u(x)=\int_{D\times\{|z|\leq 1\}}G_{\Delta}(x,y)\,z\,\widetilde{J}_{\xi}(dy,dz)+\int_{D\times\{|z|>1\}}G_{\Delta}(x,y)\,z\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz).

The second term is a finite sum of continuous functions of
x
when
d=1
, and hence is continuous. For the first term, Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion guarantees a Hölder continuous modification on
D
.

On the other hand, if
d\geq 2
, then
G_{\Delta}
has singularities along the diagonal
x=y
. In this case, the “large‐jumps” term

x\;\longmapsto\;\int_{D\times\{|z|>1\}}G_{\Delta}(x,y)\,z\,J_{\xi}(dy,dz)

has exactly
J_{\xi}\bigl(D\times\{|z|>1\}\bigr)
singularities, located at the atoms of the random measure
J_{\xi}
.

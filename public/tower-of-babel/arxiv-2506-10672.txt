Abstract
We study the π + ​ p \pi^{+}p elastic scattering process using an effective Lagrangian approach that incorporates the s s -, u u -, and t t -channel amplitudes, including Δ + + , 0 ( 1232 ) \Delta^{++,0}(1232) , neutron, and ρ 0 \rho^{0} contributions. By constructing the spin-density matrices (SDMs) from helicity amplitudes, we derive the von Neumann (entanglement) entropy associated with the spin degrees of freedom of the initial and final state particles. We compute the entropy by performing a partial trace over the spin subsystems, and its behavior is analyzed as a function of the center-of-mass energy W W and scattering angle θ \theta . We find that the entropy exhibits nontrivial angular and energy dependence, reflecting the interplay among resonance contributions and background amplitudes. In particular, the Δ + ⁣ + \Delta^{++} region shows relatively low entanglement, suggesting spin coherence dominated by the specific resonant contribution, while the background contributions increase entropy due to mixed spin configurations. These results indicate that entanglement entropy can serve as a novel probe into the quantum structure of hadronic scattering amplitudes, providing complementary insights beyond conventional cross-section analyses.



I
Introduction

Elastic scattering processes involving pions and nucleons provide essential insights into the non-perturbative regime of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and the structure of baryonic resonances. Among them, the
\pi^{+}p
elastic scattering is particularly notable due to the dominance of the
\Delta(1232)
resonance at low energies, which plays a key role in understanding hadronic excitation mechanisms. Numerous experimental and theoretical studies have been dedicated to describing this process using an effective Lagrangian approach and partial wave analyses
ParticleDataGroup:2022pth
;
Pascalutsa:2006up
. While conventional analyses focus on cross sections and resonance parameters, recent developments in quantum information theory have introduced new observables that probe deeper aspects of scattering dynamics. In particular, the von Neumann entropy, a measure of quantum entanglement, can be applied to hadronic reactions to study spin correlations and coherence
Amico:2007ag
;
Eisert:2008ur
;
Horodecki:2009zz
. This approach allows for an extended characterization of the scattering process beyond classical observables. Recent studies have directly linked entanglement entropy with particle creation in high-energy processes. Lin
et al.

Lin:2010pfa
investigated how expanding background fields generate entangled particle–antiparticle pairs, using von Neumann entropy to distinguish between entropy production and entanglement dynamics in quantum field theory.

In this study, we investigate the
\pi^{+}p\to\pi^{+}p
elastic scattering using a tree-level effective Lagrangian approach that includes
s
-,
u
-, and
t
-channel contributions with the
\Delta^{++}
,
\Delta^{0}
, neutron, and
\rho^{0}
intermediates. The primary reason for choosing this process is that all the particles involved can be traced experimentally due to their electric charges. We compute not only the total and differential cross sections but also construct the spin-density matrix (SDM) from helicity amplitudes. From the SDM, we extract the von Neumann entropy of the initial and final proton spin states to analyze quantum entanglement induced by hadronic interactions. We aim to examine how the entropy behaves across different kinematic regimes and how it reflects the interplay between resonant and non-resonant contributions. By comparing the entropy distributions from various diagram combinations, we identify the quantum coherence structure encoded in the spin degrees of freedom. The results offer a novel quantum information-theoretic perspective on elastic scattering, paving the way for future studies of inelastic processes, polarization observables, and entangled baryonic states.

In detail, the numerical results show that the total cross section for
\pi^{+}p
elastic scattering is strongly dominated by the
\Delta^{++}
resonance near
W\approx 1230
MeV, with background contributions from
\Delta^{0}
, neutron, and
\rho^{0}
channels being significantly suppressed. The differential cross section exhibits clear forward and backward peaking behavior at
W\approx 1285
MeV, consistent with
p
-wave dominance. The spin-density matrix constructed from helicity amplitudes reveals coherent spin structures in the resonance-dominated region and mixed configurations elsewhere. The von Neumann entropies
S_{\mathrm{SDM}}
computed from reduced spin-density matrices show strong angular and energy dependence, remaining relatively low in the region of
\Delta^{++}
dominance and increasing when non-resonant amplitudes interfere. The entropy distributions for selected channel combinations demonstrate that background processes, such as neutron and
\rho^{0}
exchange, significantly enhance spin entanglement beyond the
\Delta^{++}
region, providing quantum informational insights beyond conventional cross-section analysis.

The paper is organized as follows: In Sec. II, we briefly describe the theoretical framework for the effective Lagrangian approach, which is used to compute the
\pi^{+}p
elastic scattering process, and the construction of the von Neumann entropy from its SDMs. We present the numerical calculation results for the total and angular-dependent differential cross sections, SDMs, and von Neumann entropies, along with detailed discussions in Sec. III. Finally, Sec. IV summarizes our conclusions and perspectives.

II.2
von Neumann (entanglement) entropy of the spin-density matrix (SDM)

The von Neumann entropy is a fundamental quantity in quantum information theory that measures the degree of quantum uncertainty or mixedness of a quantum state. It is defined as

\displaystyle S=-\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho\log\rho\right],

(12)

where
\rho
is the density matrix describing the quantum system. If the system is in a pure state, then
\rho^{2}=\rho
and
S=0
, indicating perfect knowledge of the quantum state with no entropy. On the other hand, if
\rho
represents a mixed state (i.e., a statistical ensemble of pure states), then
S>0
, reflecting the presence of classical or quantum correlations with external degrees of freedom.

In the context of quantum spin systems, the density matrix can be projected onto the spin subspace to yield a spin-density matrix
\rho_{s}

Fano:1957zz
. This reduced matrix is obtained by tracing out irrelevant or inaccessible degrees of freedom, such as momentum, position, or the spin of entangled partners:

\rho_{s}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\text{rest}}[\rho].

(13)

The von Neumann entropy associated with
\rho_{s}
is then given by

S_{s}=-\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{s}\log\rho_{s}\right],

(14)

which quantifies the entanglement between the spin subsystem and the remainder of the system. This approach is advantageous in analyzing spin correlations in scattering, decay, or quantum transport processes, where spin degrees of freedom become entangled with kinematic or internal variables.

Now, we consider the present
\pi^{+}p
elastic scattering discussed previously. In the process, the initial and final protons have spin (helicity), whereas the pions are spinless bosons. Hence, one can construct the following spin state of the scattering amplitude, corresponding to the up and down spins of the initial (
i
) and final (
f
) state protons:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{s_{i}s_{f}}\equiv\left(\mathcal{M}_{\uparrow\uparrow},\mathcal{M}_{\uparrow\downarrow},\mathcal{M}_{\downarrow\uparrow},\mathcal{M}_{\downarrow\downarrow}\right)=(a,b,c,d).

(15)

Then, the
4\times 4
spin-density matrix (SDM) can be obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\begin{pmatrix}|a|^{2}&ab^{*}&ac^{*}&ad^{*}\\
ba^{*}&|b|^{2}&bc^{*}&bd^{*}\\
ca^{*}&cb^{*}&|c|^{2}&cd^{*}\\
da^{*}&db^{*}&dc^{*}&|d|^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(16)

where the normalization factor is defined by
\mathcal{N}=|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|c|^{2}+|d|^{2}=\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}|\mathcal{M}_{s_{i}s_{f}}|^{2}
, which corresponds to the unpolarized sum of the squared amplitude, and the trace of the SDM becomes unity, i.e.,
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}}]=1
. Note that
\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}}
means the density matrix of the
2
-qubit system
|s_{i}s_{f}\rangle
and it interprets the mixedness of the spin states. To study the entanglement of the system, we perform the partial trace over the initial and final states. The partial trace is a mathematical operation that eliminates specific degrees of freedom (subsystems) in a quantum system, effectively
tracing out
the parts we are not interested in (e.g., the environment or unobservable variables), and yields a reduced density matrix that describes only the subsystem of interest. Then, we obtain the
2\times 2
reduced SDMs
(\rho^{i,f}_{\mathrm{SDM}})
for the initial and final states by (see Appendix for details)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}}^{i}=\mathrm{Tr}_{f}(\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\begin{pmatrix}|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}&ac^{*}+bd^{*}\\
ca^{*}+db^{*}&|c|^{2}+|d|^{2}\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}}^{f}=\mathrm{Tr}_{i}(\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\begin{pmatrix}|a|^{2}+|c|^{2}&ab^{*}+cd^{*}\\
ba^{*}+dc^{*}&|b|^{2}+|d|^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(17)

whose eigenvalues are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{SDM},\pm}^{i,f}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4\,\mathrm{Det}[\rho_{\mathrm{SDM}}^{i,f}]}}{2}.

(18)

Using these eigenvalues, the von Neumann (entanglement) entropy is defined as

\displaystyle S^{i}_{\mathrm{SDM}}=-\sum_{v=\pm}\lambda_{\mathrm{SDM},v}^{i,f}\mathrm{log}[\lambda_{\mathrm{SDM},v}^{i,f}].

(19)

The initial and final SDMs represent the quantum states of the spin subsystems of the incoming and outgoing protons. Their respective von Neumann entropies quantify the degree of entanglement between spin and other degrees of freedom (e.g., momentum, isospin). A higher entropy indicates stronger entanglement or mixedness due to interactions. Comparing initial and final entropies reveals how much quantum information is redistributed or lost during the scattering process.

IV
Summary and future perspectives

In this work, we investigated the
\pi^{+}p
elastic scattering process using an effective Lagrangian approach that incorporates
s
-,
t
-, and
u
-channel contributions involving
\Delta^{++}
,
\Delta^{0}
, neutron, and
\rho^{0}
. Tree-level scattering amplitudes were constructed and used to compute both total and differential cross sections. The numerical results show qualitatively good agreement with existing experimental data, particularly around the
\Delta^{++}
resonance peak near
W\approx 1230
MeV. Beyond conventional observables, we introduced von Neumann entropy as a quantum information measure to probe the spin dynamics of the scattering process. A 4
\times
4 spin-density matrix was built from helicity amplitudes, and partial traces were performed to extract 2
\times
2 reduced density matrices for the initial and final proton spin states. The corresponding von Neumann entropies quantify the degree of spin entanglement with other kinematic and internal variables.

The entropies exhibit a clear dependence on the center-of-mass energy
W
and scattering angle
\theta
. In regions dominated by the
\Delta^{++}
resonance, the entropy is relatively small, resulting in complicated structures. In contrast, higher entropy values are observed when multiple channels interfere, particularly involving background contributions such as neutron and
\rho^{0}
exchange, indicating mixed spin states and enhanced quantum entanglement among different spin states. To further explore channel-specific effects, we decomposed the total amplitude into subsets of diagrams and computed entropy for each case. We observed that combinations involving only
\Delta
resonances maintain the entropy almost flat, whereas the inclusion of nonresonant backgrounds, such as
n
and
\rho^{0}
, significantly changes the entropy structure, decreasing the entropy. This again confirms the role of background amplitudes in generating spin decoherence, as evidenced by disentanglements. Overall, our results suggest that the von Neumann entropy extracted from spin-density matrices serves as a novel and insightful observable in hadronic scattering. It complements traditional cross-section analysis by providing direct access to quantum coherence and entanglement properties of spin subsystems.

As observed in the valley-like dip structures, the von Neumann entropy of the spin system can serve as a quantum diagnostic tool for searching for resonances and exotic states in hadronic scattering. For instance, resonance formations dynamically favor specific spin states, inducing a decrease in the entropy and signaling a reduction in entanglement between the spin and other degrees of freedom, such as background contributions. Such entropy dips, as functions of the

Experimental validation of the entropy structure, though challenging, is conceivable in future polarization-sensitive measurements. Observable quantities derived from the spin-density matrix, such as spin correlation coefficients, polarization transfer, or entanglement witnesses, could connect theory with data, making the framework testable.

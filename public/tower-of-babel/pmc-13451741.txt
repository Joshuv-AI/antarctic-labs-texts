Background

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common malignancies of the digestive tract. 1 For patients with early rectal cancer (T1-2N0), total mesorectal excision (TME) remains the standard treatment, 2 providing effective control of the primary tumor and potential regional lymphatic spread. However, TME is frequently associated with considerable morbidity, including bowel dysfunction, sexual and urinary impairment, and a risk of permanent stoma, particularly in low rectal tumors.3-5 To preserve organ function and improve quality of life (QoL), local excision (LE) procedures–such as transanal endoscopic microsurgery (TEM), transanal minimally invasive surgery (TAMIS), and endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)–have emerged as organ-preserving alternatives for selected patients with favorable pathological features.6-9 Indeed, a recent real-world survey highlighted that LE is already widely utilized in routine clinical practice, although considerable variation persists regarding patient selection, procedural platforms, and multidisciplinary decision-making. 10

For patients found to have high-risk pathological features following LE, including lymphovascular invasion, positive resection margins, tumor budding, or deep submucosal invasion, the risk of local recurrence is substantially increased. 11 Recurrence rates after LE alone have been reported to range between 13.2% and 22.7%,7,12-14 with notably higher rates of 28.9% to 37% observed in patients with pT2 tumors.15,16 Although these rates reflect graded recurrence potentials, both high-risk pT1 and pT2 subgroups confront a parallel prognostic threat where simple observation is oncologically unacceptable. Therefore, international guidelines recommend completion TME (TME) in these cases to achieve curative outcomes. 2 Nonetheless, in clinical practice, some patients either refuse further radical surgery due to a strong desire for sphincter preservation or are considered unfit for major surgery due to advanced age or comorbidities. In these cases, adjuvant chemoradiotherapy (aCRT) has been adopted as a non-operative alternative.2,8,17 This organ-preserving strategy may be further intensified with techniques such as contact x-ray brachytherapy (CXB), which can be used as a boost for higher-risk disease or as a standalone treatment for selected low-risk tumors. 18 Although recurrence rates may be modestly higher than with completion TME, aCRT offers the advantage of preserving functional outcomes and QoL. 9

In the past decade, immunotherapy has transformed the treatment landscape of CRC, particularly in patients with mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR) or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) tumors, where immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shown durable responses.19-22 Building on these successes, combination strategies incorporating immunotherapy with radiotherapy and chemotherapy have gained interest.23,24 Preclinical and clinical studies have demonstrated that radiotherapy and chemotherapy could induce immunogenic cell death, enhance tumor antigen presentation, and increase T cell infiltration,25-29 thereby offering a rationale for combining radiotherapy with immunotherapy to sensitize mismatch repair proficiency (pMMR) or microsatellite-stable (MSS) tumors, which are typically resistant to ICIs.

In this context, our group has developed and evaluated an immunotherapy-combined strategy comprising short-course radiotherapy (SCRT), CapeOx chemotherapy, and PD-1 blockade (referred to as the TORCH regimen). This approach significantly increased the complete response (CR) rate in patients with locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) and the objective response rate in those with locally recurrent rectal cancer (LRRC).30,31 Furthermore, the TORCH-E trial extended this strategy to early-stage disease, achieving a CR rate of 88.5% and broadening the eligibility landscape for immunotherapy within organ-preserving treatment paradigms. 32 Whether this success can be extended to the post-LE adjuvant setting targeting microscopic residual risk remains to be explored.

Based on these findings, we propose a phase II trial (TORCH-LE, NCT06455124) to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the TORCH regimen in early rectal cancer patients with high-risk pathological features after LE who decline or are medically unfit for completion TME. The primary endpoint is the 3-year local recurrence-free survival (LRFS), aiming to explore the feasibility and provide a proof-of-concept for an adjuvant, non-operative, organ-preserving strategy in this population.

Methods

This study was registered retrospectively at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06455124; registered June 6, 2024).

Study Design

This is a multi-center, single-arm, prospective phase II trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of adjuvant SCRT combined with chemotherapy and immunotherapy in patients with early rectal cancer who present with high-risk pathological features following LE and who refuse or are unfit for completion TME. The reporting of this study conforms to the SPIRIT 2025 statement. 33 The detailed study schema is illustrated in Figure 1.

Study Aims

The primary aim of this study is the 3-year LRFS rate in patients with early rectal cancer who present with high-risk pathological features after local excision and are treated with adjuvant SCRT combined with CapeOx chemotherapy and a PD-1 inhibitor. The 3-year LRFS rate is defined as the time from the initiation of study treatment to the first documented pelvic recurrence, assessed over a 36-month follow-up period. Secondary aims of the study include the 3-year disease-free survival (DFS) rate, defined as the time from treatment initiation to the first documented disease progression (either local or distant) or death from any cause, whichever occurs first; the 3-year overall survival (OS) rate, defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause; and the safety and tolerability of the combined treatment regimen, evaluated based on the incidence, severity, and causality of treatment-related adverse events (AEs), graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI CTCAE) version 5.0. 34

Patient Population

Potentially eligible patients must undergo a comprehensive medical history review and physical examination. Laboratory assessments including complete blood count, liver and renal function tests, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), and electrocardiography (ECG) will be performed. Patients with early rectal cancer who meet all inclusion and exclusion criteria (detailed in Table 1) will be enrolled in the trial. Briefly, the study enrolls patients with low rectal cancer, defined as a tumor located less than 7 cm from the anal verge as measured by digital rectal examination (DRE). This criterion was selected because this population is at a higher risk of permanent stoma and significant functional impairment following TME, making organ preservation a particularly relevant goal. Moreover, eligible patients must not have received prior chemotherapy, pelvic radiotherapy, or immunotherapy for rectal cancer. After LE, patients must present with at least one of the following high-risk pathological features: (1) poor tumor differentiation; (2) lymphovascular or perineural invasion; (3) positive resection margin, or specimen fragmentation precluding accurate margin assessment; (4) high-grade tumor budding; (5) tumor invasion beyond the outer one-third of the submucosal layer (SM3); (6) submucosal invasion depth >1 mm; or (7) pathological T2 disease. Only patients with MSS or pMMR tumors are eligible. All participants must provide written informed consent prior to enrollment (an example of the consent form is available as Supplementary File 5). No patients or members of the public were involved in the design, conduct, or reporting of this trial.

Treatment Regimen

All enrolled patients will receive an adjuvant regimen consisting of SCRT combined with chemotherapy and immunotherapy. Radiotherapy will be delivered at a total dose of 25 Gy in 5 fractions over one week. Two weeks after completion of radiotherapy, patients will begin systemic therapy comprising four cycles of CapeOx and the PD-1 inhibitor toripalimab. Each CapeOx cycle consists of oxaliplatin 130 mg/m2 administered intravenously on day 1, and capecitabine 1000 mg/m2 orally twice daily on days 1-14, repeated every 3 weeks. Toripalimab will be administered at a fixed dose of 240 mg intravenously on day 1 of each cycle, every 3 weeks. Chemotherapy and immunotherapy will be administered concurrently for a total of 12 weeks.

Assessment

Efficacy Assessment and Follow-Up

Treatment efficacy will be evaluated through regular imaging and endoscopic examinations, with baseline imaging of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis performed within one month prior to the initiation of radiotherapy. Post-treatment follow-up will be conducted to monitor for both local and systemic recurrence according to a de-escalating surveillance schedule. History, physical examination, and DRE will be performed every 3 months during the first 3 years and every 6 months during years 4 and 5. Laboratory tests, including complete blood count and liver/renal function, will follow the same frequency as the physical examinations. Serum tumor markers, particularly CEA, will be tested every 3 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months thereafter until year 5.

Regarding imaging and endoscopic surveillance, pelvic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and colonoscopy will be performed every 3 months in the first year, every 6 months in years 2 and 3, and annually thereafter. Chest and abdominopelvic computed tomography (CT) will be conducted every 6 months during the first 3 years and annually from years 4 to 5. Additional examinations may be undertaken at the discretion of the investigators if any suspicious findings are detected during the follow-up period. Locoregional recurrence is defined as any pelvic recurrence, including tumor regrowth at or adjacent to the primary excision site and regional lymph node recurrence within the pelvic cavity, confirmed by endoscopic biopsy and/or pelvic MRI. All suspected recurrences will be reviewed and adjudicated by the treating center’s multidisciplinary team. Central radiology and endoscopy review is not mandated by the protocol but may be undertaken at the discretion of individual centers for equivocal findings.

At each follow-up visit, performance status, vital signs, and safety assessments including AEs and concomitant medications will be recorded. Quality of life assessments will also be conducted at each scheduled visit. Following the completion of treatment, defined as the final dose of toripalimab, survival follow-up will be conducted every 12 weeks via telephone or outpatient visit until death, withdrawal of consent, loss to follow-up, or study completion, whichever occurs first.

Safety Assessment

Safety will be monitored throughout the study. All AEs will be recorded and graded according to the NCI CTCAE, version 5.0. Serious adverse events (SAEs) will be reported from the time of informed consent through the end of the study. For events occurring after treatment discontinuation, only SAEs considered possibly related to the study treatment will be recorded. Investigators will assess the severity, duration, outcome, and causality of each AE. All SAEs will be followed until resolution, stabilization, or the initiation of new anti-tumor therapy.

Quality of Life Assessment

Health-related QoL will be assessed using the Medical Outcomes Study Questionnaire Short Form 36 Health Survey (SF-36). 35 Assessments will be conducted by investigators or qualified personnel at baseline, during treatment, and at each in-person follow-up visit throughout the survival follow-up period.

Immune-Related Biomarker Assessment

Peripheral blood and stool samples will be collected at three time points: at baseline, within one week after completion of radiotherapy but before initiation of immunotherapy, and after completion of immunotherapy. Immune-related biomarker analysis will include lymphocyte subset analysis and cytokine quantification.

Trial Organization, Ethics Approval, Drug Supply and Insurance

This multi-center study is led by the Department of Radiation Oncology at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center and conducted across four academic tertiary hospitals in China, includingThe First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province, Ningbo Medical Center Lihuili Hospital, and Shanghai East Hospital. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (Approval No: 2403293-12; 9 April 2024) and the institutional review boards of all other participating sites. The trial complies with the Declaration of Helsinki (revised in 2024), and written informed consent is obtained from all participants prior to any study procedures. For the trial, Shanghai Junshi Biosciences Co., Ltd. provides Toripalimab free of charge for two cycles and covers clinical trial liability insurance for all enrolled participants.

Sample Size

This is a single-arm, phase II, multi-center clinical trial. The sample size was determined based on the Clopper–Pearson exact method to estimate the confidence interval for a binomial proportion. 36 The assumption of a 3-year LRFS rate of 95% was based on the expected improvement compared with local excision (LE) alone, for which 3-year LRFS rates of 77.3% to 86.8% have been reported in high-risk pT1 patients,7,12-14 and as low as 63.0% to 71.1% for pT2 tumors.15,16 Under this assumption, a sample size of 60 patients will yield a two-sided 95% confidence interval with a width of 0.129, corresponding to a 95% confidence interval of 85.6% to 98.9%.

An observed 3-year LRFS of 90% or greater would be considered a clinically meaningful improvement. If the observed 3-year LRFS falls near the lower confidence boundary (85.6%), the point estimate would fail to surpass the upper bound of historical controls (86.8%), in which case the trial would be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses will be performed using R software (version 4.0.3; R Project for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). 37 Treatment exposure will be summarized descriptively to assess drug administration throughout the study. Baseline clinicopathological characteristics, including individual high-risk features, will be comprehensively documented and descriptive statistics will be reported for the entire cohort. Continuous variables collected at each visit will be summarized using means and standard deviations (SD) or medians with ranges, as appropriate. Categorical variables will be described using frequencies and proportions. Survival outcomes, including LRFS, DFS, and OS, will be estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. 38

Discussion

The TORCH-LE trial is the first prospective, single-arm, multi-center phase II study designed to explore a reinforced adjuvant treatment strategy (the TORCH regimen) in patients with early-stage pMMR rectal cancer exhibiting high-risk pathological features following LE who decline or are unfit for completion TME, with endpoints including 3-year rates of LRFS, DFS, OS, and safety.

While LE is a function-preserving and potentially curative approach for selected low-risk early rectal cancer patients,2,6 its application in patients with adverse histopathological features is associated with increased risk of local recurrence compared to radical resection. Reported recurrence rates after LE alone range from 13.2% to 22.7%,7,12-14 and may reach up to 37% in pT2 tumors, 16 warranting consideration of salvage TME for curative intent.

For patients with high-risk features after LE, current clinical guidelines recommend completion TME. However, in practice, some patients are either ineligible for or unwilling to undergo further surgery due to operative risk, concerns about permanent stoma formation, or deterioration in QoL. 39 As an alternative, aCRT has been explored, although most data come from small retrospective cohorts. For instance, Chakravarti et al reported that patients receiving adjuvant chemoradiotherapy (aCRT) achieved a 5-year local control rate of 90%, compared to 72% in those managed with surveillance alone. 40 A meta-analysis by Cutting et al, involving 804 patients treated with LE plus CRT, demonstrated LR rates of 5.8% and 13.8% for pT1 and pT2 tumors, respectively, suggesting that aCRT may be effective in selected high-risk pT1 patients. 41 Similarly, another meta-analysis by van Oostendorp et al found that aCRT reduced LR rate in high-risk pT1 tumors to 3.9%, comparable to completion TME (4.1%). However, in pT2 tumors, aCRT was associated with a higher LR rate than completion TME (14.7% vs. 4%). 15 Jeong et al also reported good outcomes with aCRT in pT1 patients, with a LR rate of 3.6%. 16 Additionally, a meta-analysis analyzed 405 patients and reported weighted LR rates of 14% in the aCRT group versus 7% in the completion TME group. 17 Taken together, these findings suggest that aCRT represents an attractive alternative to completion TME in appropriately selected patients, offering the potential to preserve the rectum while still achieving local control.

Moreover, the TESAR trial is evaluating the role of aCRT in patients with intermediate-risk features after LE, defined by criteria such as pT1 tumors measuring 3–5 cm, or those ≤3 cm in size accompanied by adverse features such as poor differentiation, lymphovascular invasion, or deep submucosal invasion (SM3); as well as selected pT2 tumors ≤3 cm without lymphovascular invasion. 8 If aCRT demonstrates non-inferior to completion TME in terms of local control, the TESAR trial may provide high-quality evidence for using aCRT in selected patients and support a more personalized approach for organ-preserving strategies.

To enhance the efficacy of aCRT and further improve oncologic outcomes, recent efforts have focused on integrating novel agents. Currently, the introduction of immunotherapy has significantly altered the treatment approach for CRC, particularly in patients with MSI-H/dMMR tumors. 18 In a phase II study, Cercek et al showed that all patients with dMMR LARC achieved clinical complete response (cCR) after PD-1 blockade dostarlimab treatment. 22 The NICHE trial also found that neoadjuvant immunotherapy could induce significant pathological responses in dMMR and a subset of pMMR tumors. 20 However, the majority of rectal cancers are MSS tumors and intrinsically resistant to ICIs. Combining ICIs with radiotherapy or chemotherapy has shown promise in increasing tumor immunogenicity and T cell infiltration, which may help overcome this resistance. 29

The TORCH study demonstrated that the combination of SCRT, CapeOx, and toripalimab significantly improved the CR rate to approximately 55% in MSS LARC patients, outperforming conventional neoadjuvant CRT (∼25%). 30 This finding is also supported by several prospective clinical trials integrating PD-1 blockade into neoadjuvant therapy of LARC patients, which reported CR rates ranging from 28% to 44.8% in the experimental groups.42-44 Moreover, the TORCH-R trial reported an objective response rate of 76.1% in patients with locally recurrent disease, while the TORCH-E study demonstrated a CR rate of 88.5% in those with early rectal cancer,31,32 notably highlighting the feasibility of this regimen in achieving organ preservation. These results provide the foundation for the TORCH-LE trial, which aims to evaluate whether this immunotherapy-based regimen could reduce recurrence and preserve QoL in high-risk patients who are not candidates for completion TME after LE.

The TORCH-LE trial adopted the same approach as the TORCH-E study—four cycles of consolidation chemo-immunotherapy following short-course radiotherapy (25 Gy in five fractions). 32 Compared with the six-cycle regimen typically used in locally advanced rectal cancer, the reduced number of cycles reflects the lower disease burden in the adjuvant post-excision setting, while the use of short-course rather than conventional long-course radiotherapy streamlines the local treatment component. This strategy is conceptualized to optimize the balance between oncological safety and functional preservation, especially since standard alternative pathways present clear therapeutic trade-offs. Although completion TME provides optimal local control, it mandates irreversible organ loss and potential long-term functional decline.3-5 Conversely, intensive surveillance mitigates treatment-related toxicity but exposes patients to an elevated recurrence risk,7,16,17 while the long-term efficacy of aCRT remains limited in higher-risk subgroups due to the absence of systemic treatment intensification.15,17 By leveraging a multi-modal approach, the TORCH-LE regimen seeks to address this gap by strengthening both local and systemic disease control while maintaining rectal function. It should be noted that the addition of CapeOx and PD-1 inhibition to SCRT represents a systemic intensification compared with aCRT alone, and carries additional toxicity risks. The safety and tolerability of this combined regimen, as well as its potential impact on subsequent salvage surgery, will be prospectively evaluated as secondary endpoints of this trial. In addition, a rigorous post-treatment surveillance protocol was implemented to ensure early detection of local recurrence. Colonoscopy every six months during the first three years was adopted—more intensive than standard post-resection surveillance—to minimize the risk of delayed detection under a watch-and-wait strategy and to enable timely salvage intervention when necessary.

The TORCH-LE trial may offer a novel adjuvant treatment option for patients after LE, helping to maintain oncologic control while preserving rectal function. In addition, integrated biomarker analyses, including T cell profiling and cytokine quantification, may help identify predictive markers of treatment response and guide future personalized strategies. Together with trials such as TESAR, the TORCH-LE study may contribute to the evolving paradigm of non-operative management in early rectal cancer.

Nonetheless, several limitations must be acknowledged. The single-arm design and modest sample size may limit the interpretability of results and preclude definitive conclusions. Post-study registration may have introduced bias to our results and is a limitation of this study. Findings from this trial should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating, with further validation needed in randomized controlled settings.

Conclusion

The TORCH-LE trial aims to explore a new adjuvant treatment strategy combining short-course radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy for high-risk early rectal cancer patients who decline or are unfit for completion TME following local excision. This organ-preserving approach may help expand the therapeutic options beyond traditional surgery and support the design of future phase III trials.

Trial Status

The trial (Protocol Version 1.1, 7 April 2024) began recruiting participants on 8 May 2024. Recruitment is currently ongoing at multiple centers. The projected primary completion date is 30 June 2026, and the projected study completion date is 30 December 2029.

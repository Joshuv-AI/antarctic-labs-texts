On Robustness to k k -wise Independence of Optimal Bayesian Mechanisms
Nick Gravin, Zhiqi Wang

Abstract
This paper reexamines the classic problem of revenue maximization in single-item auctions with n n buyers under the lens of the robust optimization framework. The celebrated Myerson’s mechanism is the format that maximizes the seller’s revenue under the prior distribution, which is mutually independent across all n n buyers. As argued in a recent line of work (Caragiannis et al. 22), (Dughmi et al. 24), mutual independence is a strong assumption that is extremely hard to verify statistically, thus it is important to relax the assumption. While optimal under mutual independent prior, we find that Myerson’s mechanism may lose almost all of its revenue when the independence assumption is relaxed to pairwise independence, i.e., Myerson’s mechanism is not pairwise-robust. The mechanism regains robustness when the prior is assumed to be 3-wise independent. In contrast, we show that second-price auctions with anonymous reserve, including optimal auctions under i.i.d. priors, lose at most a constant fraction of their revenues on any regular pairwise independent prior. Our findings draw a comprehensive picture of robustness to k k -wise independence in single-item auction settings.

Question

To what degree can the mutual independence assumption be relaxed in a single-item auction setting, and how robust are optimal and approximately optimal mechanisms to this assumption?

To illustrate the importance of relaxing the mutual independence assumption with a concrete example, imagine that a platform observes a large amount of data from past ad auctions.
There could be tens to thousands of different bidders with only a small fraction participating in each auction. Let us assume that the platform has enough data to learn the value distribution of each individual bidder with high confidence. The next common step is to look at the covariance matrix.

In his book
[

, Hartline comments on the philosophy of approximation in mechanism design:“… optimal mechanisms may be overly influenced by insignificant-seeming modeling choices. … a market analysis is certainly going to be noisy and then exactly optimizing a mechanism to it may “overfit” to this noise”. Techniques.

The important part of our results is an example of pairwise-independent distribution
\bm{F}
with
\Omega(n)
separation between
\textsf{Myer}(\bm{F})
and
\textsf{Myer}(\bm{F}_{ind})

3

Our positive results on pairwise-robustness for

Regularity & Ironing.

For the most part of this paper we will require that all marginal distributions

({\mathcal{F}_{i}})_{i\in[n]}
are regular. This assumption makes sense, as the revenue gap between Myerson’s mechanism and

may be arbitrarily large for irregular distributions

Another complication with Myerson’s mechanism and ironed distributions is that the mechanism in this case is often not unique. E.g., consider the case of irregular identical marginals
{\mathcal{F}_{i}}=\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}
has to be ironed on an interval

above the monopoly reserve

It can break ties in many ways, e.g., uniformly at random or lexicographically.
In fact, the optimal mechanism may even use
bidders’ values to break ties. E.g., when the marginals are the same equal revenue distribution
\mathcal{F}

[1,10]

4
Pairwise-Robustness of Myerson’s Auction

Our next goal is to show that Myerson’s auction, which is equivalent to the second price auction with an anonymous reserve, is not over-optimizing for the mutually independent prior, unlike Myerson’s mechanism. I.e., the revenue
\textsf{AR}\left(r\right)
on any given pairwise independent prior
\bm{F}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2w}
is within a constant factor from its revenue
\textsf{AR}^{ind}\left(r\right)
on the independent prior
\bm{F}_{ind}
. To this end, we express these revenues as integrals of respective probabilities that at least one or at least two bidders exceed a threshold value
\tau
.

\textsf{AR}\left(r\right)=r\cdot Q_{1}\left(r\right)+\int_{r}^{+\infty}Q_{2}\left(\tau\right)\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad\quad\textsf{AR}^{ind}\left(r\right)=r\cdot Q_{1}^{ind}\left(r\right)+\int_{r}^{+\infty}Q_{2}^{ind}\left(\tau\right)\mathrm{d}\tau,

(4)

where the respective probabilities for the pairwise and mutually independent distributions
\bm{F}
and
\bm{F}_{ind}
are

\displaystyle Q_{1}(\tau)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}\operatorname{\mathbf{Pr}}_{\mathbf{v}\sim\bm{F}}\mathchoice{\left[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1\right]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1]},

\displaystyle\quad\quad Q_{2}(\tau)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}\operatorname{\mathbf{Pr}}_{\mathbf{v}\sim\bm{F}}\mathchoice{\left[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2\right]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2]};

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{ind}(\tau)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}\operatorname{\mathbf{Pr}}_{\mathbf{v}\sim\bm{F}_{ind}}\mathchoice{\left[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1\right]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 1]},

\displaystyle\quad\quad Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}\operatorname{\mathbf{Pr}}_{\mathbf{v}\sim\bm{F}_{ind}}\mathchoice{\left[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2\right]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2]}{[|\{i:{v_{i}}\geq\tau\}|\geq 2]}.

The integral forms in (

with
Q_{1}^{ind}(r)
and

Q_{2}(\tau)
with
Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)

to hold for any

and not only for

.

Lemma 4.1

(

)

1.299\cdot Q_{1}\left(\tau\right)\geq Q_{1}^{ind}\left(\tau\right).

This lemma is the main tool to prove the robustness of several mechanisms and algorithms such as sequential posted pricing and prophet inequality under pairwise independent distribution in
[

. Now, if we could give a similar lower bound on

via
Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)
for any

\textsf{AR}^{ind}(r)

As it turns out, the probabilities

and
Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)
do not behave as nicely as
Q_{1}(\tau)
and
Q_{1}^{ind}(\tau)

Example.

There are
n+1

\texttt{Uni}[0,1]
on
[0,1]

\tau=\frac{n-1}{n}
. Then,

We construct a pairwise independent joint distribution
\bm{F}\in\mathfrak{F}_{2w}
with

with probability
\frac{1}{n^{2}}

Draw independently

for

with probability

\texttt{Uni}[0,1]
. Indeed, the probability that value of bidder

{v_{i}}\geq\frac{n-1}{n}

{v_{i}}\leq\frac{n-1}{n}

Second, for any given pair of bidders, the probability that their values are larger than
\frac{n-1}{n}
is
\frac{1}{n^{2}}
. Similarly,
the probability that the value of one bidder is larger than
\frac{n-1}{n}

is
\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}})=\frac{n-1}{n^{2}}
;
and the probability that both values are smaller than

\bm{F}
is a pairwise independent distribution.
Then

, which quickly converges to

\tau

Theorem 4.1

SPA with monopoly reserve

Proof.

We denote the revenue of the optimal Myerson auction under mutually independent distribution

Lemma 4.4

For any regular distribution
\mathcal{F}

when

v_{0}\geq\frac{\textsf{OPT}}{n\cdot q(v_{0})}.

(7)

Proof.

We need to prove that
v_{0}\cdot n\cdot q(v_{0})\geq\textsf{OPT}

Consider the optimal mechanism for selling ex-ante (in expectation)

\mathcal{F}
is regular and

, the optimal ex-ante mechanism

v_{0}
and thus has revenue
n\cdot v_{0}\cdot q(v_{0})\geq\textsf{OPT}
.
In the case
{r_{ex}}<r^{*}

to each agent and thus
gets revenue

n\cdot v_{0}\cdot q(v_{0})

OPT

n\cdot v_{0}\cdot q(v_{0})\geq\textsf{OPT}
.
∎

Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)
and regard each
p_{i}=q_{i}(1)
as optimization variable to maximize
Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)
.
We apply the method of successive replacement of elements to prove Claim
A.2
. I.e., by maintaining the
s=\sum_{i\in[n]}p_{i}
we modify a few of
(p_{i})
at a time so that the expression (
16
) only increases; we eventually narrow down the set of
(p_{i})_{i\in[n]}
that could maximize
Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)
and verify that the desired bound holds for any vector of probabilities
(p_{i})_{i\in[n]}
in this set.
Specifically, we prove that if
\tau\geq 1+\frac{2s_{0}}{\left(2-s_{0}\right)^{2}}
, then we can keep merging variables until at most two non-zero
p_{i}
remain.

Proof.

We start by applying upper bounds (
13
) on the quantiles
q_{i}(\tau)\leq\overline{q}_{i}(\tau)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}\frac{p_{i}}{\left(1-p_{i}\right)\cdot\tau+p_{i}}
for
\tau>1
, where
p_{i}=q_{i}(1)
(more accurately we have
p_{i}=\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 1^{+}}q_{i}(x)
as we only care about
Tail
in this proof).
Then
Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)=Q_{2}^{ind}({\bm{q}})\leq Q_{2}^{ind}(\overline{\mathbf{q}})
, where

\overline{\mathbf{q}}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}(\overline{q}_{i}(\tau))_{i\in[n]}
coordinate-wise dominates vector of probabilities
{\bm{q}}
, by definition of
Q_{2}^{ind}({\bm{q}})
.

We also have a closed form (
16
) for the
Q_{2}^{ind}(\overline{\mathbf{q}})
, which after simple algebraic transformation can be written as follows

\forall\tau\geq 1,\quad Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)=Q_{2}^{ind}({\bm{q}})\leq Q_{2}^{ind}(\overline{\mathbf{q}})=1-\frac{1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{i}}{\tau\left(1-p_{i}\right)}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{p_{i}}{\tau\left(1-p_{i}\right)}\right)}.

We consider this upper bound on
Q_{2}^{ind}(\tau)
as the following optimization problem with respect to
{\bm{p}}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
, where
s_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\leq 1
. We shall prove that its value is not more than our desired upper bound.

\displaystyle\max_{{\bm{p}}}\quad 1-\frac{1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{i}}{\tau\left(1-p_{i}\right)}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{p_{i}}{\tau\left(1-p_{i}\right)}\right)}

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r@{\quad}r@{}l@{\quad}l}s.t.&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}&=s_{0},&\\
&p_{i}&\geq 0.&i=1,\ldots,n\end{array}

To show this, we apply the method of successive replacement of elements. First, we try to merge two
p_{i}
, i.e., changing
{\bm{p}}=\left(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots p_{n}\right)
to
\left(p_{1}+p_{2},0,\ldots p_{n}\right)
as much as we can while (weakly) increasing the objective.
Specifically, the increase of this objective after such merging transformation is equivalent to the following inequality

\frac{1+\alpha+\frac{p_{1}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}\right)}+\frac{p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{2}\right)}}{\left(1+\frac{p_{1}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}\right)}\right)\left(1+\frac{p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{2}\right)}\right)}\geq\frac{1+\alpha+\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}-p_{2}\right)}}{1+\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}-p_{2}\right)}},

(19)

where
\alpha\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}\sum_{i=3}^{n}\frac{p_{i}}{\tau\left(1-p_{i}\right)}
is the respective term for vector
{\bm{p}}
not affected by the transformation. Our goal will be to minimize the number
m
of strictly positive elements in the vector
{\bm{p}}
.

Claim A.5
.

Let
t\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}=}p_{1}+p_{2}\leq s_{0}
, then
the sufficient and necessary condition for inequality (
19
) is that:

\tau\cdot\Big(\left(\alpha\cdot\tau-1\right)\left(1-t\right)+\alpha\cdot\tau\Big)\geq t+\alpha\cdot\tau\left(1-t\right).

(20)

Proof.

After multiplying both sides in (
19
) by their denominators we get

(
19
)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left(1+\alpha+\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}-p_{2}\right)}\right)\left(1+\frac{p_{1}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}\right)}\right)\left(1+\frac{p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(1+\alpha+\frac{p_{1}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}\right)}+\frac{p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{2}\right)}\right)\left(1+\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{\tau\left(1-p_{1}-p_{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{p_{1}p_{2}\left(\alpha\tau^{2}\left(t-2\right)-\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(t-2\right)\tau+t\right)}{\left(1-p_{1}\right)\left(1-p_{2}\right)\left(1-t\right)\tau}\leq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\alpha\tau^{2}\left(t-2\right)-\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(t-2\right)\tau+t\leq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\tau\left(\left(\alpha\tau-1\right)\left(1-t\right)+\alpha\tau\right)\geq t+\alpha\tau\left(1-t\right).

∎

We have the following upper and lower bounds on the product
\alpha\cdot\tau
from the inequality (
20
).

Claim A.6

(Bounds on
\alpha\cdot\tau
)
.

Assume that
{\bm{p}}
has exactly
m
strictly positive elements. Then

1-t\leq\frac{\left(m-2\right)\left(1-t\right)}{\left(m-2\right)-\left(1-t\right)}\leq\alpha\cdot\tau=\sum_{i=3}^{m}\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}\leq\frac{1-t}{t}.

Proof.

The function
f\left(x\right)=\frac{x}{1-x}
is a convex on
[0,1)
. Thus the sum of
\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}
achieves maximum when all
p_{i}=0
for
i>3
and
p_{3}=1-t
. This gives us the claimed upper bound on
\alpha\cdot\tau
.
The sum achieves its minimum, when all
p_{i}=\frac{1-t}{m-2}
, letting
m
converge to
+\infty
leads to the desired lower bound.
∎

The following claim gives a sufficient condition for the transformation to increase the objective.

Claim A.7
.

For any

\tau\geq 1+\frac{t}{\left(1-t\right)^{2}}
the inequality (
20
) holds true.

Proof.

According to Claim
A.6
, we have
\alpha\cdot\tau\geq 1-t
. Thus

\text{LHS of \eqref{eq: sn_condition}}=\tau\Big(\alpha\cdot\tau\left(1-t\right)-\left(1-t\right)+\alpha\cdot\tau\Big)\geq\tau^{2}\alpha\left(1-t\right).

By the assumption of the Claim
A.7
, we have
\left(1-t\right)^{2}\left(\tau-1\right)\geq t
. Since
\alpha\cdot\tau\geq 1-t
, we have
\alpha\cdot\tau\cdot(1-t)(\tau-1)\geq(1-t)^{2}(\tau-1)\geq t
. By rearranging the terms in inequality
\alpha\cdot\tau\cdot(1-t)(\tau-1)\geq t
, we get
\tau^{2}\alpha(1-t)\geq t+\alpha\tau(1-t)
. Hence, we get (
20
) as its LHS is at least
\tau^{2}\alpha(1-t)\geq t+\alpha\tau(1-t)=\text{RHS of \eqref{eq: sn_condition}}
.
∎

If the number of non-zero elements
m>3
, then for at least one pair of them (say
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
), their sum
p_{1}+p_{2}=t\leq\frac{s}{2}
. Then according to Claim
A.7
, we can merge them and (weakly) increase our objective.
Hence, we can assume that
m\leq 3
.
When
m=3
we can get an improvement to the sufficient condition of Claim
A.7
. Namely, we have

Claim A.8
.

If there are exactly three non-zero elements in
{\bm{p}}
(i.e.,
m=3
), then
\tau\geq\frac{\left(1-t\right)^{2}+t^{2}}{2\left(1-t\right)^{2}}
is a sufficient and necessary condition for inequality (
20
).

Proof.

If
m=3
, then
\alpha\cdot\tau=\frac{1-t}{t}
and inequality (
20
) is equivalent to our assumption
\tau\geq\frac{\left(1-t\right)^{2}+t^{2}}{2\left(1-t\right)^{2}}
.
∎

Now, if
m=3
, there must exist two non-zero elements (say
p_{1}
and

) whose sum

, since function
\frac{\left(1-t\right)^{2}+t^{2}}{2\left(1-t\right)^{2}}
is increasing in

\forall~0\leq s_{0}\leq 1,\quad\frac{\left(1-\frac{2s_{0}}{3}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2s_{0}}{3}\right)^{2}}{2\left(1-\frac{2s_{0}}{3}\right)^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{2s_{0}}{\left(2-s_{0}\right)^{2}}\leq\tau.

Therefore,
m\leq 2
. If there are exactly two non-zero elements in

(i.e.,

Claim A.9
.

If

, then objective function is maximized when

.

Proof.

The objective function is

As both

Finally, when

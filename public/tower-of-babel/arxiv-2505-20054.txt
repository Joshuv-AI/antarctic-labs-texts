Introduction

1.1.
Problem setting

In this paper we deal with the minimization problem of an energy functional related to phase transition phenomena
with long range particle interactions. Specifically, we are interested in functionals of the form

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}_{K}(u;\Omega):=\mathcal{H}_{K}(u,\Omega)+\mathcal{P}(u,\Omega),

where the non-local interaction term
\mathcal{H}_{K}
and the potential term
\mathcal{P}
are given, respectively, by

\mathcal{H}_{K}(u,\Omega):=\frac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)^{2}}\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{2}{K}(x-y)\,dxdy\,,

and

(1.2)

\mathcal{P}(u,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx.

Here,
K
is a positive kernel modeled on that of the fractional Laplacian, while
W
is a double well potential, with wells at
\pm 1
. Also, differently from the classical literature on this topic,
the derivatives of
W
, up to any integer order, are allowed to vanish at
\pm 1
.

More precisely,
{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
is a measurable function satisfying

(K1)

\displaystyle K(x)=K(-x)\qquad\text{for a.~\!e.}~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

and

(K2)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\mathds{1}_{B_{r_{0}}}(x)}{|x|^{n+2s}}\leq K(x)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x|^{n+2s}}\qquad\text{for a.~\!e.}~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some
s\in(0,1)
,
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
r_{0}>0
.

Given a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, here above and throughout the paper, we use the notation

\mathds{1}_{A}(x):=\begin{cases}1&{\mbox{ if }}x\in A,\\
0&{\mbox{ if }}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A.\end{cases}

In order to gain better decay results for the minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
, we will sometimes ask that
K
satisfies

(K3)

\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{n+2s}}\leq K(x)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x|^{n+2s}}\quad\text{for a.~\!e.}~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~\text{and some}~s\in(0,1)~\text{and}~0<\lambda\leq\Lambda.

We stress that condition (
K3
) is stronger than (
K2
) as, for instance, prevents the kernel from having compact support.

Moreover, in some cases, we will also assume that
K
satisfies the following
“slow oscillation” assumption:

(K4)

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}-1\right)\frac{1}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}\in[-\infty,0],\quad\text{for any}~\sigma_{j}\nearrow 1~\text{and}~\varepsilon\in(0,1)\ .

We refer the reader to Appendix

We notice here that

allows us to express the kinetic term

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{K}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}K(x-y)\,dx\,dy.

Definition 1.3

Let

\mathcal{E}_{K}(u;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{E}_{K}(u+\phi;\Omega),

Moreover, we say that a measurable function

\mathcal{G}(u):=\limsup_{\rho\to+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{K}(u;[-\rho,\rho])}{\Psi_{s}(\rho)},

\Psi_{s}(\rho):=\begin{cases}\rho^{1-2s}\,&\mbox{ if }s\in(0,1/2),\\

1\,&\mbox{ if }s\in(1/2,1).\end{cases}

In this spirit, we believe that our general setting

), we obtain that, for any
x\in B_{R}
, there exist two constants
c_{6},c_{7}
depending on
n
,
s
,
\Lambda
and
\zeta
such that

c_{6}(R+1-|x|)^{-qs}\leq 1+w(x)\leq c_{7}(R+1-|x|)^{-qs},

and the estimates in (
3.4
) can be obtained setting, for instance,
C:=\max\{1,c_{6}^{-1},c_{7}\}

and recalling (
3.5
).
∎

4.
Some auxiliary results

In this section we prove some useful statements, that will be employed to prove the main results of the paper.

4.1.
On the potential
W

We provide here a regularity result on the potential
W
. We stress that this result plays a fundamental role in the study of the decay estimates in (
1.12
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
W:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying (
W1
) and (
W3
).

Then, for any
r
,
t\in[-1,-1+\xi]
with
r\leq t
, it holds

(4.1)

\begin{split}&\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}\left((1+t)^{\alpha}-(1+r)^{\alpha}\right)\leq W(t)-W(r)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\beta(\beta-1)}\left((1+t)^{\beta}-(1+r)^{\beta}\right)\\
&\mbox{and}\\
&\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{\alpha-1}\left((1+t)^{\alpha-1}-(1+r)^{\alpha-1}\right)\leq W^{\prime}{}(t)-W^{\prime}{}(r)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\beta-1}\left((1+t)^{\beta-1}-(1+r)^{\beta-1}\right).\end{split}

Moreover, for any
r
,
t\in[1-\xi,1]
with
r\leq t
, it holds

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\displaystyle\frac{C_{3}}{\gamma(\gamma-1)}\left((1-t)^{\gamma}-(1-r)^{\gamma}\right)\leq W(t)-W(r)\leq\displaystyle\frac{C_{4}}{\delta(\delta-1)}\left((1-t)^{\delta}-(1-r)^{\delta}\right)\\
&\mbox{and}\\
&\displaystyle\frac{C_{3}}{\gamma-1}\left((1-r)^{\gamma-1}-(1-t)^{\gamma-1}\right)\leq W^{\prime}{}(t)-W^{\prime}{}(r)\leq\frac{C_{4}}{\delta-1}\left((1-r)^{\delta-1}-(1-t)^{\delta-1}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

We will only show (
4.1
), since (
4.2
) can be
proved in a similar way. Moreover, we will focus on the upper bounds
in (
4.1
), being the lower bounds based on analogous calculations.

Let
r
,
t\in[-1,-1+\xi]
and
r\leq t
. Then, thanks to (
W1
)
and (
W3
), we can apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and obtain that

\displaystyle W^{\prime}{}(t)-W^{\prime}{}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{t}W^{\prime}{}^{\prime}{}(\sigma)\,d\sigma\leq C_{2}\int_{r}^{t}(1+\sigma)^{\beta-2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{\beta-1}\left((1+t)^{\beta-1}-(1+r)^{\beta-1}\right)

which establishes the upper bound in the second line of (
4.1
).

We now show the upper bound in the first line of (
4.1
).
For this, we use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
and the fact that
W^{\prime}{}(-1)=0
to write

\displaystyle W(t)-W(r)=\int_{r}^{t}W^{\prime}{}(\sigma)\,d\sigma=\int_{r}^{t}\big(W^{\prime}{}(\sigma)-W^{\prime}{}(-1)\big)\,d\sigma.

Since
\sigma\geq r\geq-1
, we can exploit the upper bound
in the second line of (
4.1
) and we obtain that

W(t)-W(r)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\beta-1}\int_{r}^{t}(1+\sigma)^{\beta-1}\,d\sigma=\frac{C_{2}}{\beta(\beta-1)}\left((1+t)^{\beta}-(1+r)^{\beta}\right),

as desired.
∎

We point out that
in the case
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\delta=2
, Lemma
4.1
implies that

W^{\prime}{}(t)\geq W^{\prime}{}(r)+c(t-r)\quad\mbox{for any }r\leq t,\;r,t\in[-1,-1+c]\cup[1-c,1]

and for some
c>0
. This inequality is the so called
convexity property
for a
non-degenerate
potential W and is used in
[
22
,
8
]
in order to compute the decay estimates for the minimizers.

4.2.
Barriers and comparison principle

In this subsection we recall a strong comparison principle related to the operator
{L}_{K}
, that will be extensively used in the proof of
the estimates in (
1.12
) and (
1.17
).

Proposition 4.2

(Proposition 4.4 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
{K}
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
). Let
f_{1}
,
f_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be two continuous functions.

Let
\Omega
be a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v
,
w\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{2s+\theta}(\Omega)
, for some
\theta>0
, be such that

\begin{cases}L_{K}v\leq f_{1}(\cdot,v)&\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
L_{K}w\geq f_{2}(\cdot,w)&\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
v\geq w&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Suppose also that, for any
x\in\Omega
,

f_{1}(x,w(x))\leq f_{2}(x,w(x)).

If there exists a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
v(x_{0})=w(x_{0})
, then
v\equiv w
in the whole
\Omega
.

4.3.
Basic properties of the fractional Allen-Cahn equation

We mention here two handful lemmata. The first one poses sufficient conditions in order for the fractional Allen-Cahn equation to behave well under limit procedures. The second one relates the solutions of the fractional Allen-Cahn equation to class A minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
.

Lemma 4.3

(Lemma 4.8 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
{K}
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
). Let
W\in C^{1,\vartheta}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\vartheta\in(0,1)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Lipschitz domain and let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{H}^{K}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a sequence of weak solutions of

L_{K}v_{j}=W^{\prime}(v_{j})\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,

such that there exists
c>0
such that, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

[v_{j}]_{\mathbb{H}^{K}(\Omega)}+\|v_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c.

Suppose also that
v_{j}
converges to a function
v
uniformly on compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as
j\to+\infty
.

Then,
v\in\mathbb{H}^{K}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak solution of

L_{K}v=W^{\prime}(v)\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega.

Lemma 4.4

(Theorem 3 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
s\in(0,1)
and
{K}
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
). Let
W
be such that

W(\pm 1)=W^{\prime}{}(\pm 1)=0.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-1,1)
belong to
C^{1+2s+{\theta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for some
{\theta}>0
. Suppose that
u
is a solution of

L_{K}u=W^{\prime}(u)\qquad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},

satisfying

\partial_{x_{n}}u(x)\geq 0,\qquad{\mbox{for all }}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and

\lim_{x_{n}\to\pm\infty}u(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\pm 1,\qquad{\mbox{for all }}x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

Then,
u
is a class A minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
.

We also refer the reader to
[
22
, Lemma 7]
and
[
22
, Theorem 1]
for the proofs of, respectively, Lemmata
4.3
and
4.4
, when the kernel
{K}(z):=\lvert z\rvert^{-n-2s}
.

5.
Asymptotic estimates for
{L}_{K}
in dimension
n=1

In this section we collect asymptotic results regarding the operator
{L}_{K}
, defined in (
1.7
), in dimension
n=1
. They will be used in the proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
and
\sigma
,
\tau\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
K
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
).

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that

\phi(x)=\begin{cases}|x|^{-\sigma}&\mbox{if }x<-\kappa,\\
|x|^{-\tau}&\mbox{if }x>\kappa\end{cases}

and

(5.1)

\phi(x)\geq\gamma
for all
x\in[-\kappa,\kappa]
, for some
\gamma\in(0,+\infty)
.

Then,

(5.2)

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}|x|^{1+2s}{L}_{K}\phi(x)\leq\Lambda\left(\frac{\kappa^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}+\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\phi(y)\,dy+\frac{\kappa^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1}\right),

where
\Lambda
is the quantity appearing in (
K2
).

Proof.

We will only prove (
5.2
) as
x\to-\infty
, being
the limit as
x\to+\infty
analogous.

For any
x<-2\kappa
, we set

(5.3)

\begin{split}&A_{K}(x):=PV_{x}\int_{-\infty}^{-\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy,\\
&B_{K}(x):=\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy\\
{\mbox{and }}\qquad&D_{K}(x):=\int_{\kappa}^{+\infty}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy.\end{split}

In the following computations we will omit the principal value notation,
for the sake of readability.

We claim that

(5.4)

\lim_{x\to-\infty}|x|^{1+2s}A_{K}(x)\leq\frac{\Lambda\kappa^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}.

In order to show this, we apply the change of variable
y:=|x|\theta
and compute

A_{K}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{-\kappa}\left(|y|^{-\sigma}-|x|^{-\sigma}\right)K(x-y)\,dy=|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{-\infty}^{-{\kappa}/{|x|}}\left(|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1\right)K\left(x(1+\theta)\right)\,d\theta.

We also set

(5.5)

\begin{split}&A_{K,I}(x):=|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{-\infty}^{-{3}/{2}}\left(|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1\right)K\left(x(1+\theta)\right)\,d\theta,\\
&A_{K,II}(x):=|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{-{3}/{2}}^{-{1}/{2}}\left(|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1\right)K\left(x(1+\theta)\right)\,d\theta\\
\mbox{and}\qquad&A_{K,III}(x):=|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{-{\kappa}/{|x|}}\left(|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1\right)K\left(x(1+\theta)\right)\,d\theta.\end{split}

Exploiting (
K2
), we obtain that

(5.6)

|A_{K,I}(x)|\leq\Lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\int_{-\infty}^{-{3}/{2}}\frac{1-|\theta|^{-\sigma}}{|1+\theta|^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\leq C|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)},

for some
C>0
, depending on
s
,
\sigma
and
\Lambda
.

Moreover, we check that

(5.7)

|A_{K,II}(x)|\leq C|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}.

To this aim, we use the change of variable
z:=1+\theta
and
the symmetry of
K
in (
K1
)
to see that

\begin{split}&\int_{-{3}/{2}}^{-{1}/{2}}\left(|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1\right)K\left(x(1+\theta)\right)\,d\theta=\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{{1}/{2}}\left((1-z)^{-\sigma}-1\right)K(xz)\,dz\\
&\qquad=\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{{1}/{2}}\left(\sigma z+O(|z|^{2})\right)K(xz)\,dz=\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{{1}/{2}}O(|z|^{2})K(xz)\,dz.\end{split}

As a consequence,

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{-{3}/{2}}^{-{1}/{2}}\left(|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1\right)K\left(x(1+\theta)\right)\,d\theta\right|=\left|\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{{1}/{2}}O(|z|^{2})K(xz)\,dz\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq C\Lambda|x|^{-(1+2s)}\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{{1}/{2}}|z|^{1-2s}\,dz\leq C|x|^{-(1+2s)},\end{split}

up to renaming
C>0
.
This leads to (
5.7
), as desired.

Furthermore, observing that
\theta^{-\sigma}>1
if
\theta\in(0,1/2)
, we obtain that

\begin{split}A_{K,III}(x)&\leq\Lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{-{\kappa}/{|x|}}\frac{|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\\
&=\Lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\left(\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{{1}/{2}}\theta^{-\sigma}\big(1+O(\theta)\big)\,d\theta-\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{{1}/{2}}\frac{d\theta}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\right)\\
&=\Lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\left(\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(2^{\sigma-1}-\kappa^{1-\sigma}|x|^{\sigma-1}\right)+O(|x|^{\sigma-2})-\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{{1}/{2}}\frac{d\theta}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\right).\end{split}

Combining this with (
5.6
) and (
5.7
),
and noticing that
A_{K}(x)=A_{K,I}(x)+A_{K,II}(x)+A_{K,III}(x)
,
we obtain (
5.4
).

Now, we want to show that

(5.8)

\lim_{x\to-\infty}|x|^{1+2s}B_{K}(x)\leq\Lambda\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\phi(y)\,dy.

For this, we take
\gamma
as in (
5.1
)
and we suppose that
|x|>\gamma^{-1/\sigma}
.
In this way, we have that
\phi(x)=|x|^{-\sigma}<\gamma\leq\phi(y)

for all
y\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
,
thanks to (
5.1
).
Therefore, by (
K2
),

\displaystyle|x|^{1+2s}B_{K}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x|^{1+2s}\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)\left|1-\frac{y}{x}\right|^{-(1+2s)}\,dy.

We can now take the limit as
x\to-\infty
and use the
Dominated Convergence Theorem to deduce (
5.8
).

Furthermore, we claim that

(5.9)

\lim_{x\to-\infty}|x|^{1+2s}D_{K}(x)\leq\frac{\Lambda\kappa^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1},

Indeed, the change of variable
y:=|x|\theta

and (
K2
) give that

\begin{split}D_{K}(x)&=\int_{\kappa}^{+\infty}(y^{-\tau}-|x|^{-\sigma})K(x-y)\,dy\\
&=|x|\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{+\infty}\big(|x|^{-\tau}\theta^{-\tau}-|x|^{-\sigma}\big)K(x(1+\theta))\,d\theta\\
&\leq\Lambda|x|^{-2s}\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{+\infty}\frac{|x|^{-\tau}\theta^{-\tau}+|x|^{-\sigma}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\\
&\leq\Lambda|x|^{-(\tau+2s)}\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta+\Lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{+\infty}\frac{d\theta}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}.\end{split}

We notice that

\displaystyle\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{1/2}\theta^{-\tau}\big(1+O(\theta)\big)\,d\theta+\int_{1/2}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\tau}\left(2^{\tau-1}-\kappa^{1-\tau}|x|^{\tau-1}\right)+O(|x|^{\tau-2})+\int_{1/2}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta.

As a result,

\displaystyle|x|^{1+2s}D_{K}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda|x|^{1-\tau}\left(\frac{1}{1-\tau}\left(2^{\tau-1}-\kappa^{1-\tau}|x|^{\tau-1}\right)+O(|x|^{\tau-2})+\int_{1/2}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Lambda|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{+\infty}\frac{d\theta}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}},

which gives the desired claim in (
5.9
).

Thus, since
L_{K}\phi(x)=A_{K}(x)+B_{K}(x)+D_{K}(x)
,
combining together (
5.4
), (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) yields the thesis.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
and
\sigma
,
\tau\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
K
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K3
).

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that

\phi(x)=\begin{cases}|x|^{-\sigma}&\mbox{if }x<-\kappa,\\
|x|^{-\tau}&\mbox{if }x>\kappa\end{cases}

and

(5.10)

\phi(x)\geq\gamma
for all
x\in[-\kappa,\kappa]
, for some
\gamma\in(0,+\infty)
.

Then,

(5.11)

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}|x|^{1+2s}{L}_{K}\phi(x)\geq\lambda\left(\frac{\kappa^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}+\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\phi(y)\,dy+\frac{\kappa^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1}\right),

where
\lambda
is the quantity appearing in (
K3
).

The proof of Proposition
5.2
is similar to the one of Proposition
5.1
, but requires some care in the estimates
of some terms, therefore we provide here below the details for the facility of the reader.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

We will only prove (
5.11
) as
x\to-\infty
, being
the limit as
x\to+\infty
analogous.

For any
x<-2\kappa
, we take
A_{K}(x)
,
B_{K}(x)
and
D_{K}(x)
as in (
5.3
).

We claim that

(5.12)

\lim_{x\to-\infty}|x|^{1+2s}A_{K}(x)\geq\frac{\lambda\kappa^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}.

In order to show this, in the notation of (
5.5
),
we write
A_{K}(x)=A_{K,I}(x)+A_{K,II}(x)+A_{K,III}(x)
.
Moreover, using the estimates in (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we see that

(5.13)

|A_{K}(x)|\geq A_{K,III}(x)-|A_{K,I}(x)|-|A_{K,II}(x)|\geq A_{K,III}(x)-C|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)},

for some
C>0
, depending on
s
,
\sigma
and
\Lambda
(being
\Lambda
the quantity appearing in (
K3
)).

Furthermore, since
\theta^{-\sigma}>1
if
\theta\in(0,1/2)
, we obtain that

\begin{split}A_{K,III}(x)&\geq\lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\int_{-{1}/{2}}^{-{\kappa}/{|x|}}\frac{|\theta|^{-\sigma}-1}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\\
&=\lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\left(\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{{1}/{2}}\theta^{-\sigma}\big(1+O(\theta)\big)\,d\theta-\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{{1}/{2}}\frac{d\theta}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\right)\\
&=\lambda|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\left(\kappa^{1-\sigma}|x|^{\sigma-1}-2^{\sigma-1}\right)+O(|x|^{\sigma-2})-\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{{1}/{2}}\frac{d\theta}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\right).\end{split}

Therefore, from this and (
5.13
) we deduce (
5.12
).

Now, we show that

(5.14)

\lim_{x\to-\infty}|x|^{1+2s}B_{K}(x)\geq\lambda\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\phi(y)\,dy.

For this, we take
\gamma
as in (
5.10
)
and we suppose that
|x|>\gamma^{-1/\sigma}
.
In this way, we have that
\phi(x)=|x|^{-\sigma}<\gamma\leq\phi(y)

for all
y\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
,
thanks to (
5.10
).
Therefore, by (
K3
),

\displaystyle|x|^{1+2s}B_{K}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x|^{1+2s}\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)\left|1-\frac{y}{x}\right|^{-(1+2s)}\,dy.

We can now take the limit as
x\to-\infty
and use the
Dominated Convergence Theorem to deduce (
5.14
).

Also, we claim that

(5.15)

\lim_{x\to-\infty}|x|^{1+2s}D_{K}(x)\geq\frac{\lambda\kappa^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1},

Indeed, the change of variable
y:=|x|\theta
and the positivity of the kernel give that

(5.16)

\begin{split}D_{K}(x)&=\int_{\kappa}^{+\infty}(y^{-\tau}-|x|^{-\sigma})K(x-y)\,dy\\
&=|x|\int_{{\kappa}/{|x|}}^{+\infty}\big(|x|^{-\tau}\theta^{-\tau}-|x|^{-\sigma}\big)K(x(1+\theta))\,d\theta\\
&\geq|x|^{1-\tau}\int_{k/|x|}^{+\infty}\theta^{-\tau}K(x(1+\theta))\,d\theta-|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{0}^{+\infty}K(x(1+\theta))\,d\theta.\end{split}

Now, from the upper bound in (
K3
) we infer that

\displaystyle|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{0}^{+\infty}K(x(1+\theta))\,d\theta\leq\Lambda|x|^{1-\sigma}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{d\theta}{|x|^{1+2s}(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}=C|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)},

for some
C>0
depending on
s
and
\Lambda
.

Moreover, from the lower bound in (
K3
), we deduce that

\begin{split}&|x|^{1-\tau}\int_{k/|x|}^{+\infty}\theta^{-\tau}K(x(1+\theta))\,d\theta\geq\lambda|x|^{1-\tau}\int_{k/|x|}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{|x|^{1+2s}(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\\
&\qquad=\lambda|x|^{-(\tau+2s)}\left(\int_{k/|x|}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{-\tau}\left(1+O(\theta)\right)\,d\theta+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\right)\\
&\qquad=\lambda|x|^{-(\tau+2s)}\left(\frac{k^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1}|x|^{\tau-1}-\frac{2^{\tau-1}}{\tau-1}+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\right)+O(|x|^{-2-2s}).\end{split}

Plugging the last two displays into (
5.16
) give that

\begin{split}D_{K}(x)&\geq\lambda|x|^{-(\tau+2s)}\left(\frac{k^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1}|x|^{\tau-1}-\frac{2^{\tau-1}}{\tau-1}+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{(1+\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad+O(|x|^{-2-2s})-C|x|^{-(\sigma+2s)}.\end{split}

Multiplying by
|x|^{1+2s}
and taking the limit show (
5.15
).

Thus, since
L_{K}\phi(x)=A_{K}(x)+B_{K}(x)+D_{K}(x)
,
combining together (
5.12
), (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) yields the thesis.
∎

Proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6

We are now ready to deal with the
1
-D minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
and provide the proofs of the main results of this paper.

We recall the definitions of
\mathscr{M}
and of
\mathscr{M}^{(x_{0})}
, respectively in (
7.1
) and (
7.2
).

Proof of Theorem
1.5

For any
R>3
, we use Lemma
6.5
with
a:=-R
and
b:=R
to obtain a minimizer
v_{[-R,R]}:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
such that
v_{[-R,R]}(x)=-1
if
x\leq-R
and
v_{[-R,R]}(x)=1
if
x\geq R
. Also, the energy bound (
6.8
) holds for
v_{[-R,R]}
and, in view of Proposition
6.6
,
v_{[-R,R]}
is non-decreasing.

The minimization property of
v_{[-R,R]}
yields that

{L}_{K}v_{[-R,R]}=W^{\prime}{}(v_{[-R,R]})\quad\mbox{in }(-R,R).

Therefore, by Proposition
2.3
, we have that
v_{[-R,R]}\in C^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{2s+\theta}(-R,R)
, for some
\theta\in(0,s)
, and its Hölder norm is bounded independently of
R
.

Now, by continuity, there must be a point
\bar{x}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
v_{[-R,R]}(\bar{x}_{R})=0
. We claim that

(8.1)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}(R-|\bar{x}_{R}|)=+\infty.

To check this, we argue by contradiction and we suppose that

(8.2)

\text{either}\quad\lim_{R\to+\infty}(R+\bar{x}_{R})\leq C\quad\mbox{or}\quad\lim_{R\to+\infty}(R-\bar{x}_{R})\leq C,

for some
C>0
.

We suppose that
the first case in (
8.2
) occurs (the other being analogous).
In this case, we consider the minimizer
v_{[-2R,0]}
and notice that, in view of the translation invariance,
v_{[-2R,0]}(x)=v_{[-R,R]}(x-R)
. Then, taking
C
as in (
8.2
) and exploiting the monotonicity of
v_{[-2R,0]}
, we see that, for
R
large enough,

v_{[-2R,0]}(C)=v_{[-R,R]}(C-R)\geq v_{[-R,R]}(\bar{x}_{R})=0.

This inequality gives that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}v_{[-2R,0]}(C)\geq 0,

which is in contradiction with Proposition
6.9
and Remark
6.10
. The proof of (
8.1
) is thereby complete.

Now, we set

v^{(0)}_{R}(x):=v_{[-R,R]}(x+\bar{x}_{R}).

In this way,
v^{(0)}_{R}(0)=0
.

Moreover,
v^{(0)}_{R}
is a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
in
[-R-\bar{x}_{R},R-\bar{x}_{R}]
and, thanks to (
8.1
),

\lim_{R\to+\infty}(-R-\bar{x}_{R})=-\infty\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{R\to+\infty}(R-\bar{x}_{R})=+\infty.

Consequently, we may suppose that
v^{(0)}_{R}
converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}
to some
u^{(0)}:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
such that
u^{(0)}\in C_{{\rm loc}}^{2s+\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Also,
we obtain the pointwise equality

(8.3)

{L}_{K}u^{(0)}=W^{\prime}{}(u^{(0)})\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}.

Furthermore, we have that

(8.4)

u^{(0)}\in C^{1+2s+\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
,
u^{(0)}
is non-decreasing
and
u^{(0)}(0)=0
,

where we deduce the first property from Proposition
2.4
.

Now, we prove that

(8.5)

\mathscr{G}(u^{(0)})<+\infty.

In order to show this, we fix
\rho>0
and take
R
large enough
such that
R-\bar{x}_{R}>\rho
. Then, by the Fatou’s Lemma and Proposition
6.3
, we obtain, for some
C>0
,

\mathcal{E}_{K}(u^{(0)},[-\rho,\rho])\leq\liminf_{R\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{K}(v^{(0)}_{R},[-\rho,\rho])\leq C\Psi_{s}(\rho),

thus showing (
8.5
).

Furthermore,

(8.6)

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}u^{(0)}(x)=\pm 1.

Indeed, we first notice that, by (
8.4
), the limits in (
8.6
) exist. Now, we take
a_{-}
,

a_{+}\in[-1,1]
such that

\lim_{x\to-\infty}u^{(0)}(x)=a_{-}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to+\infty}u^{(0)}(x)=a_{+}.

By (
8.4
), we know that
a_{+}\in[0,1]
. Moreover, if
a_{+}\neq 1
, then (
W2
) gives that

C_{W}:=\inf_{x\in[0,a_{+}]}W(x)>0.

As a consequence, for any
\rho>0
,

\mathcal{E}_{K}(u^{(0)},[-\rho,\rho])\geq\int_{0}^{\rho}W(u^{(0)}(x))\,dx\geq C_{W}\rho,

this contradicting (
8.5
) if
\rho
is sufficiently large.
Therefore
a_{+}=1
, and similarly one can check that
a_{-}=-1
. This completes the proof of (
8.6
).

Now, thanks to (
8.4
), (
8.6
) and
[
8
, Remark 2.7]
, we exploit
[
8
, Theorem 3]
to infer that
u^{(0)}
is a class A minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
.

Consequently,
u^{(0)}\in\mathscr{M}^{(0)}
and we can apply Lemma
7.1
to obtain that
u^{(0)}
satisfies the decay estimates (
1.12
), (
1.14
), (
1.15
) and (
1.17
). Moreover, Lemma
7.1
also gives that
u^{(0)}
is, up to translations, the unique class A minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
.

Finally, it holds that, up to translations,
u^{(0)}
is the only non-decreasing solution to (
8.3
) in the family of admissible functions
\mathcal{X}
. Indeed, let us consider
v\in\mathcal{X}
to be a non-decreasing solution to (
8.3
). Then, by Lemma
4.4
,
v
is a class A minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{K}
and thus
v\in\mathscr{M}^{(x_{0})}
for some
x_{0}
. Hence, (
iii
) gives that
v(x)=u^{(0)}(x-x_{0})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6

For any
R>3
, we use Lemma
6.5
with
a:=-R
and
b:=R
to obtain a minimizer
v_{[-R,R]}:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
such that
v_{[-R,R]}(x)=-1
if
x\leq-R
and
v_{[-R,R]}(x)=1
if
x\geq R
. Also, the energy bound (
6.8
) holds for
v_{[-R,R]}
and, in view of Proposition
6.6
,
v_{[-R,R]}
is non-decreasing. Furthermore, by Proposition
6.7
,
v_{[-R,R]}
is odd and so
v_{[-R,R]}(0)=0
for any
R
.

The minimization property of
v_{[-R,R]}
implies that

{L}_{K}v_{[-R,R]}=W^{\prime}{}(v_{[-R,R]})\quad\mbox{in }(-R,R).

Then, by Proposition
2.3
,
v_{[-R,R]}\in C^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{2s+\theta}(-R,R)
for some
\theta\in(0,s)
, and its Hölder norm is bounded independently of
R
.

Consequently, we may suppose that
v_{[-R,R]}
converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}
to some
u^{(0)}:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
such that
u^{(0)}\in C_{{\rm loc}}^{2s+\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Also,
we obtain the pointwise equality

(8.7)

{L}_{K}u^{(0)}=W^{\prime}{}(u^{(0)})\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}.

Furthermore, we have that

(8.8)

u^{(0)}\in C^{1+2s+\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
,
u^{(0)}
is non-decreasing and odd,

where we deduce the first property from Proposition
2.4
and we stress that
u^{(0)}(0)=0
by symmetry.

Now, we prove that

(8.9)

\mathscr{G}(u^{(0)})<+\infty.

For this, we fix
\rho>0
and take
R>\rho
. Then, by the Fatou’s Lemma and Proposition
6.3
, we obtain that

\mathcal{E}_{K}(u^{(0)},[-\rho,\rho])\leq\liminf_{R\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{K}(v_{[-R,R]},[-\rho,\rho])\leq C\Psi_{s}(\rho),

which gives

.

Furthermore,

(8.10)

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}u^{(0)}(x)=\pm 1.

Indeed, we first notice that by (

) the limits in (
8.10
) exist. Now, we take
a_{-}
,

such that

\lim_{x\to-\infty}u^{(0)}(x)=a_{-}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to+\infty}u^{(0)}(x)=a_{+}.

By (

), we know that
a_{+}\in[0,1]
. Moreover, if

, then (

) gives that

C_{W}:=\inf_{x\in[0,a_{+}]}W(x)>0.

As a consequence, for any

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{K}(u^{(0)},[-\rho,\rho])&\geq\int_{0}^{\rho}W(u^{(0)}(x))\,dx\geq C_{W}\rho,\end{split}

) when

Finally, thanks to (

) and

Appendix A
On the kernel
K

In this section, we provide three explicit examples of “admissible” kernels
K
, where we define “admissible” any kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
satisfying (
K1
), (
K2
) and (
K4
).

Example A.1
.

The kernel of the fractional Laplacian

K(x):=|x|^{-(n+2s)}

is admissible.
Indeed, it obviously satisfies (
K1
) and (
K2
).

We now check that it also satisfies (
K4
).
For this, let
\sigma_{j}\nearrow 1
as
j\to+\infty
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then,

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}-1\right)\frac{1}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{j}^{-(n+2s)}-1}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n+2s)(1-\sigma_{j})+O(|1-\sigma_{j}|^{2})}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}.

Taking the limit as
j\to+\infty
, we thereby see that (
K4
) holds true.

We mention here that condition (
K2
) is very general and allows us to consider a great variety of translation invariant kernels, only locally comparable to that of the fractional Laplacian. For instance, a kernel satisfying (
K2
) is

(A.1)

K(x):=\mathds{1}_{B_{r_{0}}}(x)\frac{a(x)}{|x|^{n+2s}},

with
r_{0}>0
and
a
bounded and bounded away from zero.
Kernels of this form
have been widely considered in the literature (see for instance
[
21
]
and the references therein).

Nevertheless, a kernel as in (
A.1
) does not satisfy (
K4
). To show this, we consider
\sigma_{j}\nearrow 1
such that
\sigma_{j}r_{0}<r_{0}<r_{0}/\sigma_{j}
. Also, we pick
\bar{x}\in\partial B_{r_{0}/\sqrt{\sigma_{j}}}
and consider a sequence of points
x_{k}\in B_{r_{0}/\sqrt{\sigma_{j}}}
converging to
\bar{x}
. In particular,
{x_{k}\in B_{r_{0}/\sqrt{\sigma_{j}}}\setminus B_{r_{0}}}
for any
k
large enough, while
\sigma_{j}x_{k}\in B_{\sqrt{\sigma_{j}}r_{0}}\Subset B_{r_{0}}
. Then, we see that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}\geq\frac{\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x)}{\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x)}\sigma_{j}^{-(n+2s)}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\mathds{1}_{B_{r_{0}}}(\sigma_{j}x_{k})}{\mathds{1}_{B_{r_{0}}}(x_{k})}=+\infty,

which entails that (
K4
) fails to hold.

Example A.2
.

An admissible kernel that exploits the generality of (
K2
) is the following:

(A.2)

K(x):=\begin{cases}|x|^{-(n+2s)}&\mbox{if }|x|<\rho,\\
2\rho^{(\theta-1)(n+2s)}|x|^{-\theta(n+2s)}&\mbox{if }|x|\geq\rho\end{cases}

for some
\theta>1
and
\rho>0
. This kernel satisfies (
K1
) and (
K2
).

In order to show (
K4
), we can reason as follows. Let
\sigma_{j}\nearrow 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
We claim that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(A.3)

\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}\leq\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}.

Indeed, if
x\in{B_{\rho}}
, it holds that

\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}=\sigma_{j}^{-(n+2s)}\leq\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}.

Moreover, if
x\in B_{\rho/\sigma_{j}}\setminus{B_{\rho}}
, we have that

\begin{split}&\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}=\frac{\sigma_{j}^{-(n+2s)}|x|^{-(n+2s)}}{2\rho^{(\theta-1)(n+2s)}|x|^{-\theta(n+2s)}}=\frac{\sigma_{j}^{-(n+2s)}}{2}\left(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\right)^{(\theta-1)(n+2s)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}}{2}\leq\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}.\end{split}

Furthermore, it is immediate to check that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho/\sigma_{j}}
it holds

\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}=\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}.

Gathering these pieces of information, we obtain (
A.3
).

From (
A.3
) we thus conclude that

\begin{split}\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}-1\right)\frac{1}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}&\leq\frac{\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}-1}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}\\
&=\frac{\theta(n+2s)(1-\sigma_{j})+O(|1-\sigma_{j}|^{2})}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

Passing to the limit as
j\to+\infty
shows (
K4
).

Example A.3
.

Given
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\zeta\geq 1
, we define the function

g(x):=e^{-|x|^{2}}\cos(\tau|x|)+\zeta.

Also, taking
K
as in (
A.2
), we define the kernel

\widetilde{K}(x):=CK(x)g(x),

for some
C>0
.

The kernel
\widetilde{K}
is admissible.
Indeed, it clearly satisfies (
K1
) and (
K2
), thus we only focus on showing (
K4
). To this aim, let
\sigma_{j}\nearrow 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. In light of (
A.3
), we have see that,
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

(A.4)

\begin{split}\frac{\widetilde{K}(\sigma_{j}x)}{\widetilde{K}(x)}-1&\leq\frac{\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}g(\sigma_{j}x)-g(x)}{g(x)}\\
&=\frac{(\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}-1)g(\sigma_{j}x)}{g(x)}+\frac{g(\sigma_{j}x)-g(x)}{g(x)}\\
&\leq\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)}{\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)}\big(\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}-1\big)+\frac{|g(\sigma_{j}x)-g(x)|}{\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)}.\end{split}

Now, we claim that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(A.5)

|g(\sigma_{j}x)-g(x)|\leq C(1-\sigma_{j}),

for some
C>0
.

In order to show (
A.5
), we observe that,
if
j
is sufficiently large,

(A.6)

\begin{split}|g(\sigma_{j}x)-g(x)|&=\left|e^{-\sigma_{j}^{2}|x|^{2}}\cos(\tau\sigma_{j}|x|)-e^{-|x|^{2}}\cos(\tau|x|)\right|\\
&\leq\left|e^{-\sigma_{j}^{2}|x|^{2}}\left(\cos(\tau\sigma_{j}|x|)-\cos(\tau|x|)\right)\right|+\left|\cos(\tau|x|)\left(e^{-\sigma_{j}^{2}|x|^{2}}-e^{-|x|^{2}}\right)\right|\\
&\leq\tau e^{-|x|^{2}/2}|x|(1-\sigma_{j})+\left|e^{-\sigma_{j}^{2}|x|^{2}}-e^{-|x|^{2}}\right|\\
&\leq C(1-\sigma_{j})+e^{-\sigma_{j}^{2}|x|^{2}}-e^{-|x|^{2}},\end{split}

for some
C>0
.

Now, we define the function
f(x):=e^{-|x|^{2}}|x|^{2}
and we point out that, if
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,

e^{-t^{2}|x|^{2}}t=\frac{f(tx)}{t|x|^{2}}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{t|x|^{2}}.

Thus, exploiting the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and a Taylor expansion of the logarithm near
1
, we get that

\begin{split}&e^{-\sigma_{j}^{2}|x|^{2}}-e^{-|x|^{2}}=\int_{1}^{\sigma_{j}}\frac{d}{dt}e^{-t^{2}|x|^{2}}\,dt=2|x|^{2}\int_{\sigma_{j}}^{1}e^{-t^{2}|x|^{2}}t\,dt\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\sigma_{j}}^{1}\frac{dt}{t}\\
&\qquad\qquad=2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ln(\sigma_{j}^{-1})\leq C(1-\sigma_{j}),\end{split}

for some
C>0
. Combining this with (
A.6
) yields (
A.5
).

As a consequence of (
A.4
) and (
A.5
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\widetilde{K}(\sigma_{j}x)}{\widetilde{K}(x)}-1&\leq\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)}{\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)}\big(\sigma_{j}^{-\theta(n+2s)}-1\big)+\frac{C(1-\sigma_{j})}{\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)}\leq C(1-\sigma_{j}),\end{split}

up to relabeling
C
.

This entails that (
K4
) holds true, as desired.

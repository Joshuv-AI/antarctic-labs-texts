Quantum stress and torsion distributions in the deuteron Preprint: JLAB-THY-26-4579
Wim Cosyn, Adam Freese, Alan Sosa

Abstract
Stress distributions in the deuteron are related to form factors of the asymmetric energy-momentum tensor through three-dimensional Fourier transforms. There are eleven such form factors, which we calculate in an impulse approximation. We compare the obtained form factors to prior results for the six form factors that have been previously calculated. We then elaborate on the formalism for relating the form factors to internal distributions of mass, mass flux, momentum, stresses, and forces, and obtain results for all of these distributions. We obtain the principal stresses for the symmetric part of the stress tensor, and show that the antisymmetric part describes reorientation of fermion spin by torsion stress when the nucleon moves between the S- and D-waves. Force distributions in the nucleons depend on the so-called non-conserved form factors through the Cauchy momentum equation, and are non-radial owing to the presence of tensor forces and spin-orbit coupling.

Energy-momentum tensor form factors

Energy-momentum tensor form factors are defined by breaking the matrix element

\langle p^{\prime}_{d},s_{d}^{\prime}|\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(0)|p_{d},s_{d}\rangle

\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(0)

For relativistic calculations, this includes Lorentz covariance,
whereas Galilei covariance is the appropriate symmetry for
non-relativistic calculations.

this means the EMT of a non-relativistic system generally has
more form factors than it would for a relativistic system.

By restricting focus on the stress tensor,
the same number of form factors are seen for relativistic and non-relativistic systems.
Thus, by the counting of Ref.
20

there should be nine EMT-FFs for the symmetric stress tensor,
and eleven EMT-FFs for the asymmetric stress tensor.

\langle\bm{p}^{\prime},s_{d}^{\prime}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p},s_{d}\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{p},s_{n}\\
s^{\prime}_{p},s^{\prime}_{n}\end{subarray}}\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}p_{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}p_{n}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}p^{\prime}_{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}p^{\prime}_{n}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg\{\\
\langle\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}_{d}|\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p},s^{\prime}_{p};\bm{p}^{\prime}_{n},s^{\prime}_{n}\rangle\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p},s^{\prime}_{p};\bm{p}^{\prime}_{n},s^{\prime}_{n}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{p},s_{p};\bm{p}_{n},s_{n}\rangle\langle\bm{p}_{p},s_{p};\bm{p}_{n},s_{n}|\bm{p},s_{d}\rangle\bigg\}\,.

(14)

Since the proton part of the EMT acts only on the proton,
we can write:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p},s^{\prime}_{p};\bm{p}^{\prime}_{n},s^{\prime}_{n}|T^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{p},s_{p};\bm{p}_{n},s_{n}\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{p}_{n}-\bm{p}^{\prime}_{n})\delta_{s_{n}s^{\prime}_{n}}\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p},s^{\prime}_{p}|T^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{p},s_{p}\rangle\,.

(15)

Additionally, the inner products of deuteron and two-nucleon states
can be written in terms of the momentum-space deuteron wave function:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{p}_{p},s_{p};\bm{p}_{n},s_{n}|\bm{p},s_{d}\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{p}-\bm{p}_{p}-\bm{p}_{n})\;\tilde{\psi}_{d}^{(s_{d};s_{p},s_{n})}\left(\frac{\bm{p}_{p}-\bm{p}_{n}}{2}\right)\,.

(16)

See Appendix
B
for a review of the deuteron wave function
and its properties.

The presence of three matrix elements or inner products
introducing delta functions allows three of the momentum integrals
to be eliminated from Eq. (
14
).
By defining:

\displaystyle\bm{k}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\big(\bm{p}_{p}+\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p}-\bm{p}_{n}-\bm{p}^{\prime}_{n}\big)\qquad\qquad\bm{\varDelta}\equiv\bm{p}^{\prime}_{d}-\bm{p}_{d}=\bm{p}_{p}^{\prime}-\bm{p}_{p}\,,

(17)

the desired matrix element can be rewritten:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{d},s_{d}^{\prime}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{d},s_{d}\rangle=\sum_{s_{p},s_{n},s^{\prime}_{p}}\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\psi}_{d}^{*(s^{\prime}_{d};s^{\prime}_{p},s_{n})}\left(\bm{k}+\tfrac{\bm{\varDelta}}{4}\right)\tilde{\psi}_{d}^{(s_{d};s_{p},s_{n})}\left(\bm{k}-\tfrac{\bm{\varDelta}}{4}\right)\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p},s^{\prime}_{p}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{p},s_{p}\rangle\bigg|_{\bm{P}_{p}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{P}_{d}+\,\bm{k}}\,,

(18)

where
\bm{P}_{d}(\bm{P}_{p})
is the average deuteron (proton) momentum.

The integral in Eq. (
18
) can be evaluated more easily
when converted to coordinate space
through a Fourier transform of the wave function:

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(s_{d};s_{p},s_{n})}_{d}(\bm{k})=\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}r\,\psi^{(s_{d};s_{p},s_{n})}_{d}(\bm{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{e}^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}\,.

(19)

Any appearances of
\bm{k}
in Eq. (
18
) are turned by
this substitution into two-sided derivatives, giving:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{d},s_{d}^{\prime}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{d},s_{d}\rangle=\sum_{s_{p},s_{n},s^{\prime}_{p}}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}r\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{\bm{\varDelta}\cdot\bm{r}}{2}}\psi_{d}^{*(s^{\prime}_{d};s^{\prime}_{p},s_{n})}(\bm{r})\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p},s^{\prime}_{p}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{p},s_{p}\rangle\psi_{d}^{(s_{d};s_{p},s_{n})}(\bm{r})\bigg|_{\bm{P}_{p}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\bm{P}-i\overleftrightarrow{\bm{\nabla}})}\,,

(20)

where
f\overleftrightarrow{\bm{\nabla}}g=f(\bm{\nabla}g)-(\bm{\nabla}f)g
.

II.2
Analytic results for the EMT form factors

In this section, we give analytic formulas for the one-body contributions
to the EMT form factors.
Results are written in terms of the radial S- and D-wave functions;
see Appendix
B
for a review of their definitions and properties.
The form factors are ultimately obtained through Eq. (
20
)—with
the left-hand side equated with the deuteron EMT-FF breakdown (
1
)
and the nucleon breakdown (
12
) inserted into
the right-hand side—which does not leave
any wiggle room for ad hoc decisions that could introduce ambiguities.
In obtaining these formulas, we use
M_{d}=2m_{N}
,
since mass is additive (and distinct from energy) in non-relativistic mechanics.
Specific deuteron EMT-FFs
can be isolated through a variety of projection procedures,
such as considering particular polarization states,
looking at specific
T^{ij}
components,
contracting with
\varDelta_{i}
to isolate non-conserved form factors,
or integrating out projections onto specific spherical harmonics.
We will not include line-by-line derivations here,
but will briefly describe how we isolated each form factor
along with presenting the results.

The
A
-like form factors
A_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
and
A_{T}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
can be isolated by
looking at unpolarized and tensor-polarized ensembles
respectively—via Eqs. (
5
) and (
7
)—and
reading off terms that are proportional to
P^{i}P^{j}
.
The results we find are

\displaystyle A_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\frac{A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,j_{0}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[u^{2}(r)+w^{2}(r)\Big]

(21)

and

\displaystyle A_{T}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\frac{6m_{N}^{2}A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big]\,,

(22)

The forward limits of these form factors are:

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{U}(0)&=\sum_{N=p,n}\frac{A_{N}(0)}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Big[u^{2}(r)+w^{2}(r)\Big]=1\\
A_{T}(0)&=\sum_{N=p,n}\frac{m_{N}^{2}A_{N}(0)}{10}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,r^{2}\Big[2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big]=(2m_{N})^{2}Q_{d}\,,\end{split}

(23)

by virtue of the nucleon sum rule
A_{N}(0)=1
—recall
that we are implicitly summing over all parton flavors—the
deuteron wave function normalization (
99
),
and the definition of the quadrupole moment (
101
).
The first of these relationships shows that the momentum sum rule (
8
)
is already saturated by the one-body contributions.
We want to remind the reader that this is a feature of the non-relativistic approach taken here,
with the force carrier degrees of freedom integrated out into the static potential.

The form factors
J(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
and
S(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
are related to
the angular momentum content of the deuteron,
and can be found by projecting onto vector-polarized deuteron ensembles
via Eq. (
6
).
One can then find e.g. the sums and differences
between
T^{13}
and
T^{31}
components of the matrix elements
to separate these form factors.
This way, we obtain the following results:

J(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{\frac{9A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{2\varDelta}j_{1}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\frac{w^{2}(r)}{r}\\
+J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})j_{0}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\left[u^{2}(r)-\frac{1}{2}w^{2}(r)\right]+\frac{J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{2}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[w^{2}(r)+\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)\Big]\Bigg\}

(24)

and

\displaystyle S(\bm{\Delta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}S_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{j_{0}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\left[u^{2}(r)-\frac{1}{2}w^{2}(r)\right]+\frac{1}{2}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[w^{2}(r)+\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)\Big]\Bigg\}\,.

(25)

In the forward limit,
these form factors become:

\displaystyle\begin{split}J(0)&=\sum_{N=p,n}\left\{\tfrac{3}{4}\mathcal{P}_{D}A_{N}(0)+\Big(1-\tfrac{3}{2}\mathcal{P}_{D}\Big)J_{N}(0)\right\}=1\\
S(0)&=\sum_{N=p,n}\Big(1-\tfrac{3}{2}\mathcal{P}_{D}\Big)S_{N}(0)\,,\end{split}

(26)

where
S_{N}(0)
signifies the quark spin contribution to the nucleon.
Both equations feature the well-known deuteron depolarization factor

(1-\tfrac{3}{2}\mathcal{P}_{D})
,
which is of the order of 90-95% for most realistic deuteron wave functions.
The first of these shows that the spin sum rule (
9
)
is already saturated by the one-body contributions.
To achieve this saturation, the depolarization of the nucleon spin is
compensated by an orbital angular momentum contribution from the deuteron D-wave.
The second relation gives the quark spin contribution to the deuteron’s total spin,
since gluons do not contribute to the antisymmetric part of the EMT (and thus do not contribute to
S(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
).

The form factor
D_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
can be found by taking an unpolarized ensemble
via Eq. (
5
),
dropping any terms proportional to
\bm{P}
from the result
to eliminate
A_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
,
and taking an off-diagonal component—e.g., the
T^{12}
component—to
eliminate
\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
.

D_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
will be the only form factor that survives.
We find:

D_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{\frac{4A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[\frac{2u^{2}(r)+8w^{2}(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{u(r)u^{\prime}(r)+w(r)w^{\prime}(r)}{r}+u(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)\\
+w(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)-\big(u^{\prime}(r)\big)^{2}-\big(w^{\prime}(r)\big)^{2}\Bigg]-\frac{12J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta}j_{1}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\frac{w^{2}(r)}{r}+2D_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})j_{0}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[u^{2}(r)+w^{2}(r)\Big]\Bigg\}\,.

(27)

The forward limit can be found using some integration tricks:

\displaystyle D_{U}(0)=\sum_{N=p,n}\left\{2D_{N}(0)-2\mathcal{P}_{D}J_{N}(0)+\left(\frac{1}{10}\mathcal{P}_{S}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{P}_{D}+\frac{2}{15}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,r^{2}\big[u(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)+w(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)\big]\right)A_{N}(0)\right\}\,.

(28)

We see that the deuteron
D
-term is determined both by that of either nucleon
and contributions originating from the deuteron dynamics multiplied
with nucleon spin and momentum properties.
Note that in the last term the second derivatives of the radial S- and D-waves are
related to the nucleon-nucleon potential through Schrödinger’s equation;
see Eq. (
112
).

The form factor
\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
can also be found by looking at
unpolarized ensembles via Eq. (
5
)
and contracting it with
\varDelta_{i}

to eliminate contributions from
A_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
and

D_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
.
We thus obtain the formula:

\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{\frac{A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{2m_{N}^{2}\varDelta}j_{1}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[u^{\prime}(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)+w^{\prime}(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)\\
-u(r)u^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)-w(r)w^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{12w^{2}(r)}{r^{3}}\Bigg]+\frac{\bar{c}_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{2}j_{0}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[u^{2}(r)+w^{2}(r)\Big]\Bigg\}\,.

(29)

The result does not identically vanish as required by local momentum conservation,
but this occurs because we have not accounted for stresses in the interaction itself.
It is not a flaw, but
expected
that
\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
be non-zero
for partial contributions to the stress tensor.
In fact, it encodes forces acting on the subsystems—in this case,
the nucleons—through the Cauchy momentum equation.
The relationship to forces is more apparent if we
use the Schrödinger equation (
112
)
to rewrite
\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
as:

\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{-\frac{A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{2m_{N}\varDelta}j_{1}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[V_{c}^{\prime}(r)u^{2}(r)+V_{w}^{\prime}(r)w^{2}(r)+4\sqrt{2}V_{t}^{\prime}(r)u(r)w(r)\Bigg]\\
+\frac{\bar{c}_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{2}j_{0}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[u^{2}(r)+w^{2}(r)\Big]\Bigg\}\,,

(30)

where
next to a contribution from the nucleon
\bar{c}_{N}

weighted with deuteron structure
(similar in form to the unpolarized
A_{U}
of Eq. (
21
)),
there is also a contribution proportional to
A_{N}

where the integrand depends on derivatives of the potential.
We will make the relationship of this form factor to forces more concrete
and explore it further in Sec.
III.10
.
The forward limit of
\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
—which
will later be useful in calculating mechanical radii—can be written:

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{U}(0)=\sum_{N=p,n}\left\{\frac{1}{6m_{N}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\left(u(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)+w(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{6w^{2}(r)}{r^{2}}\right)A_{N}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\bar{c}_{N}(0)\right\}

(31)

There are two tensor-polarized D-like form factors,
and isolating them is a bit more involved.
These are both found by considering tensor-polarized ensembles via Eq. (
7
).
The form factor
D_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
can be isolated by evaluating the
T^{12}
component of
Eq. (
7
), dividing it by
\sin^{2}(\theta_{\varDelta})\cos(\phi_{\varDelta})\sin(\phi_{\varDelta})
,
and integrating it with the spherical harmonic
Y_{2}^{0}(\hat{\varDelta})
.
All structures besides the
D_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
structure are eliminated by this projection.
The result is:

D_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{\frac{48m_{N}^{2}A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{4}}j_{4}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[\sqrt{2}\Big(u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)-2u^{\prime}(r)w^{\prime}(r)\Big)\\
-w(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+\big(w^{\prime}(r)\big)^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\Big(3w(r)u^{\prime}(r)-5u(r)w^{\prime}(r)\Big)+w(r)w^{\prime}(r)}{r}+\frac{6\Big(2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big)}{r^{2}}\Bigg]\\
+\frac{96m_{N}^{2}J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{3}}j_{3}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[\sqrt{2}\Big(u(r)w^{\prime}(r)-w(r)u^{\prime}(r)\Big)-\frac{2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)}{r}\Bigg]\\
+\frac{24m_{N}^{2}D_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big]\Bigg\}\,.

(32)

This form factor is finite in the forward limit, and equal to:

D_{T1}(0)=(2m_{N})^{2}\sum_{N=p,n}\Bigg\{2D_{N}(0)\,Q_{d}+\left(-\frac{4}{7}Q_{d}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{35}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,r^{3}\Big(u(r)w^{\prime}(r)-w(r)u^{\prime}(r)\Big)\right)J_{N}(0)\\
+\frac{1}{42}\left(Q_{d}+\frac{1}{30}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,r^{4}\Big(3\sqrt{2}u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+\sqrt{2}w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)-2w(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)\Big)\right)A_{N}(0)\Bigg\}\,.

(33)

The forward limit of
D_{T1}(0)
is dominated by
the first term in this expression,
and accordingly is proportional to the deuteron’s quadrupole moment.
Obtaining
D_{T1}(0)/D_{U}(0)\approx(2m_{N})^{2}Q_{d}=A_{T}(0)/A_{U}(0)

was the primary motivation for normalizing the form factor
D_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})

as we did.

The form factor
D_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
can be isolated in a similar way.
We can take the
T^{12}
component of Eq. (
7
),
divide it by
\sin^{2}(\theta_{\varDelta})\cos(\phi_{\varDelta})\sin(\phi_{\varDelta})
,
and integrate with the spherical harmonic
Y_{0}^{0}(\hat{\varDelta})
.
The result is:

D_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{\frac{12A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{3}}j_{3}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[\frac{\Big(2\sqrt{2}u^{\prime\prime}(r)-w^{\prime\prime}(r)\Big)w(r)-\Big(2\sqrt{2}u^{\prime}(r)-w^{\prime}(r)\Big)w^{\prime}(r)}{r}\\
+\frac{\Big(2\sqrt{2}u(r)+5w(r)\Big)w^{\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{18w^{2}(r)}{r^{3}}\Bigg]+\frac{6J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[\sqrt{2}\Big(u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)-w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)\Big)\\
-\frac{4\Big(\sqrt{2}u^{\prime}(r)+w^{\prime}(r)\Big)w(r)}{r}-\frac{\Big(2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big)}{r^{2}}+\frac{3w^{2}(r)}{r^{2}}\Bigg]\Bigg\}\,.

(34)

It is worth remarking that, in contrast to
D_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
and
D_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
,
the nucleon
D_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
does not contribute to
D_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
.
The forward limit of this form factor is also finite.

There are also several non-conserved form factors present
in tensor-polarized ensembles.
The two
\overline{c}_{T}
form factors can be isolated by
contracting of Eq. (
7
) with
\varDelta_{i}\hat{z}_{j}+\varDelta_{j}\hat{z}_{i}
.
Dividing the result by
\cos(\theta_{\varDelta})
and integrating the spherical harmonic
Y_{2}^{0}(\hat{\varDelta})

will isolate
\overline{c}_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
, giving:

\bar{c}_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{\frac{6A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{3}}j_{3}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[\sqrt{2}\Big(u^{\prime}(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+w^{\prime}(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)-u(r)w^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)-w(r)u^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)\Big)+w(r)w^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)\\
-w^{\prime}(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{2\sqrt{2}\Big(u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)-w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)\Big)}{r}+\frac{6\sqrt{2}\Big(u(r)w^{\prime}(r)-w(r)u^{\prime}(r)\Big)}{r^{2}}-\frac{12\Big(2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big)}{r^{3}}\Bigg]\\
+\frac{6\sqrt{2}J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)-u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{6u(r)w(r)}{r^{2}}\Bigg]+\frac{6m_{N}^{2}\bar{c}_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big[2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big]\Bigg\}\,.

(35)

Doing the same as above, but with the spherical harmonic
Y_{0}^{0}(\hat{\varDelta})
,
isolates
\bar{c}_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
, giving:

\bar{c}_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\sum_{N=p,n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\{\frac{3A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{m_{N}^{2}\varDelta^{2}}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[\frac{2\sqrt{2}\Big(u^{\prime}(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)-w(r)u^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)\Big)+w(r)w^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)-w^{\prime}(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r}\\
+\frac{2\sqrt{2}\Big(w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)-u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)\Big)}{r^{2}}-\frac{12\sqrt{2}w(r)u^{\prime}(r)}{r^{3}}+\frac{12\Big(\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)+w^{2}(r)\Big)}{r^{4}}\Bigg]\\
+\frac{3\sqrt{2}J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{4m_{N}^{2}}j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Bigg[w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)-u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{6u(r)w(r)}{r^{2}}\Bigg]\Bigg\}\,.

(36)

The final form factor,
\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
, can be isolated
by taking the difference between components
T^{23}-T^{32}

of Eq. (
7
).
We find:

\displaystyle\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\frac{6\sqrt{2}S_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\left\{w(r)u^{\prime\prime}(r)-u(r)w^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{6u(r)w(r)}{r^{2}}\right\}\,.

(37)

This form factor is related to tensor forces and spin-orbit coupling.
With the aid of the Schrödinger equation (
112
),
our result can be rewritten:

\displaystyle\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=\frac{6\sqrt{2}m_{N}S_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})}{\varDelta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,j_{2}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)\Big(2V_{t}(r)-6V_{l2}(r)+3V_{ls}(r)-9V_{ls2}(r)\Big)u(r)w(r)\,,

(38)

where the functions appearing here are defined in Eq. (
103
).
In the absence of spin- or angular momentum-dependent forces,

\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
would vanish.

As a non-conserved form factor,
\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
must vanish when
summed over all constituents.
In this respect, it is no different from the three
\bar{c}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
form factors.
However, as it arises from the antisymmetric part of the EMT,

\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
receives contributions only from quarks
20
.
In order for
\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
to vanish when exchange currents are introduced,
the exchange currents must include quarks.
Put another way, a non-zero
\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
in the one-body contributions
to the stress tensor cannot be induced by pure gluon exchange,
but can be induced by pion exchange (since pions consist of quarks).

III
Spatial densities of stresses

With the EMT form factors in hand,
we move on to considering the associated densities.
We will follow the trail cleared by
Refs.
58
;
31
;
34
;
33

by formulating the quantum expectation value

\langle\Psi|\hat{T}^{ij}(\bm{x},t)|\Psi\rangle

of the stress tensor as a convolution relation,
in which internal densities are
boosted by the barycentric velocity and
smeared by the barycentric probability density.
This will allow identification of internal densities—including mass,
mass flux, momentum and stress distributions—as Fourier transforms
of the EMT form factors.

This formulation relies on the Galilei symmetry group of non-relativistic
quantum mechanics,
and the deuteron is accordingly treated as a non-relativistic system.
This raises the question of whether incorporating the quark and gluon
substructure of the nucleons is justified.
As a composite system, the nucleon is robustly relativistic,
which has led to considerable controversy about the appropriate way to
describe its internal densities;
see Refs.
26
;
10
;
69
;
64
;
47
;
30
;
78
;
59

for a variety of perspectives.
One helpful point—illustrated in the numerical examples of
Ref.
31
—is that relativistic corrections to the
density formulas are washed out
in the quantum expectation value
if the nucleon’s wave packet is
broader than its Compton wavelength,
about
1.3
fm.
Since the deuteron’s radius is about
2
fm—and so its diameter
about
4
fm—the nucleons in a deuteron are sufficiently
smeared so that relativistic corrections to their densities
can be neglected.

To help build intuition with concrete illustrations,
we will present numerical results for the densities along with
the exposition of formal analytic results.
These numerical results, like the EMT-FFs we found above,
will only contain one-body contributions,
and accordingly would receive corrections from exchange currents.
Just as above, we use the AV18 deuteron wave function
99
.

III.5
Stress tensor in spherical coordinates

We will now consider components of the internal stress tensor more explicitly
in spherical coordinates.
To aid with this, we will break the stress tensor into three pieces:
(1) an unpolarized piece that is the same for all polarization states;
(2) the symmetric part of the tensor-polarized contribution; and
(3) the antisymmetric part of the tensor-polarized contribution.
In spherical coordinates,
these pieces respectively have the following non-zero components:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{U}&=\begin{bmatrix}p_{r}^{(U)}&0&0\\
0&p_{\theta}^{(U)}&0\\
0&0&p_{\phi}^{(U)}\end{bmatrix}\\
\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{TS}&=\begin{bmatrix}p_{r}^{(T)}&s^{(T)}&0\\
s^{(T)}&p_{\theta}^{(T)}&0\\
0&0&p_{\phi}^{(T)}\end{bmatrix}\\
\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{TA}&=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\tau^{(T)}&0\\
\tau^{(T)}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}\,.\end{split}

(70)

The stress tensor of a pure state can be reconstructed through:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{m_{j}=0}&=\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{U}-\frac{1}{3}\Big(\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{TS}+\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{TA}\Big)\\
\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{m_{j}=\pm 1}&=\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{U}+\frac{1}{6}\Big(\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{TS}+\mathfrak{t}^{ij}_{TA}\Big)\,.\end{split}

(71)

In terms of the functions appearing in the breakdown (
66
),
we find these components to be:

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{r}^{(U)}&=p_{U}+\frac{2}{3}s_{U}\\
p_{\theta}^{(U)}=p_{\phi}^{(U)}&=p_{U}-\frac{1}{3}s_{U}\\
p_{r}^{(T)}&=\left(\frac{3}{2}\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\{2\widetilde{p}_{T1}-\frac{4}{15}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(0)}-\frac{8}{21}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(2)}-\frac{24}{35}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(4)}+2p_{T2}+\frac{4}{3}s_{T2}\right\}\\
p_{\theta}^{(T)}&=\left(\frac{3}{2}\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\{2\widetilde{p}_{T1}+\frac{4}{15}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(0)}+\frac{2}{5}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(4)}-2p_{T2}-\frac{4}{3}s_{T2}\right\}+\left\{-\frac{2}{15}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(0)}+\frac{4}{21}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(2)}-\frac{2}{35}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(4)}+p_{T2}-\frac{4}{3}s_{T2}\right\}\\
p_{\phi}^{(T)}&=\left(\frac{3}{2}\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\{2\widetilde{p}_{T1}+\frac{8}{21}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(2)}+\frac{2}{7}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(4)}-4s_{T2}\right\}+\left\{\frac{2}{15}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(0)}-\frac{4}{21}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(2)}+\frac{2}{35}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(4)}-p_{T2}+\frac{4}{3}s_{T2}\right\}\\
s^{(T)}&=\sin\theta\cos\theta\left\{\frac{2}{5}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(0)}+\frac{2}{7}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(2)}-\frac{24}{35}\widetilde{s}_{T1}^{(4)}-3p_{T2}-2s_{T2}\right\}\\
\tau^{(T)}&=6\sin\theta\cos\theta s_{A}\,.\end{split}

(72)

In contrast to spin-zero and spin-half systems,
there are off-diagonal shear stresses in the deuteron

This largely reflects the non-spherical shape of the deuteron.
A cubic material element of the deuteron, with

will be sheared in the

If the symmetric stress tensor is used,
this shear can be eliminated through a local change of frame,
which we will consider next.

Any real symmetric square matrix can be diagonalized by real orthogonal transformations.
For the symmetric stress tensor, this corresponds to a local change of frame.

the symmetric stress tensor,

The energy-momentum tensor of QCD can be derived in a variety of ways,

III.10
Forces felt by subcomponents

For a continuum system, the net force density on any material element
is a sum of forces from adjacent elements—given by

-\nabla_{i}\mathfrak{t}^{ij}
—and an external body force density
f^{j}
.
For a static system, the net force must be zero, entailing:

\displaystyle f^{j}(\bm{b})=\nabla_{i}\mathfrak{t}^{ij}(\bm{b})\,,

(86)

and thus providing a means of determining the external force density.
This is commonly known as the Cauchy momentum equation,
and sometimes as Cauchy’s first law
of motion
15
;
46
.
It has been explored in
Refs.
87
;
100
;
54

as a way of mapping out the average force felt by quarks in a hadron,
and was shown by Ref.
34
to successfully
reproduce the Coulomb force law when applied to the hydrogen atom.
The application of the Cauchy momentum equation to spin-one systems
was thoroughly explored by Kim and Kim
54
,
so we borrow their formalism to investigate force distributions in the deuteron.

One matter to bear in mind throughout this discussion is that
since nucleons have finite extent—as encoded by the use of realistic EMT-FFs—we will be obtaining force distributions

within
the nucleon owing to the nucleon-nucleon force.
At the same time, all parton flavors are being summed over,
so these are not internal forces, but an external force—again,
from the inter-nucleon interaction—distributed over quarks and gluons in the nucleon.
It is also crucial to recall that these forces are exerted by force carriers
(e.g., pions at long distances),
and that—by Newton’s third law—the force exerted
on
these force
carriers by the nucleons must be equal and opposite to the forces we will map out.
This is effectively equivalent to saying that the
\bar{c}
and
\bar{s}

form factors must identically vanish if we include contributions from
the force carriers,
and that the force distributions we will find are non-zero
because the nucleons are an open subsystem of the deuteron.

Bearing this in mind,
we use Eq. (23) of Ref.
54

to decompose the force density:

\displaystyle f^{j}_{s^{\prime}s}(\bm{b})=\varepsilon_{a}\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}\left\{\delta^{ab}Y_{1}^{j}(\hat{b})f_{0}(b)+Q^{jlab}Y_{1}^{l}(\hat{b})f_{2}(b)+Q^{klab}Y_{3}^{jkl}(\hat{b})f_{3}(b)\right\}\,.

(87)

Taking the divergence of the internal stress tensor, as given in
Eq. (
III.1
), results in:

f^{j}_{s^{\prime}s}(\bm{b})=iM_{d}\varepsilon_{a}\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}\varDelta}{(2\pi)^{3}}|\bm{\varDelta}|\Bigg\{\delta^{ab}Y_{1}^{j}(\hat{\varDelta})\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-Q^{klab}Y_{3}^{jkl}(\hat{\varDelta})\frac{\varDelta^{2}}{2M_{d}^{2}}\Big(\bar{c}_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})\Big)\\
+Q^{jlab}Y_{1}^{j}(\hat{\varDelta})\left[\left(\bar{c}_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-\frac{\varDelta^{2}}{2M_{d}^{2}}\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})\right)-\frac{\varDelta^{2}}{5M_{d}^{2}}\Big(\bar{c}_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})\Big)\right]\Bigg\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{e}^{-i\bm{b}\cdot\bm{\varDelta}}\,.

(88)

All of the angular dependence in the integrand is contained in harmonic tensors,
making it straightforward to turn the Fourier transform into a collection
of Bessel transforms.
The functions in the force breakdown (
87
) can thus be written:

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{0}(b)&=\frac{M_{d}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\varDelta\,\varDelta^{3}\bar{c}_{U}(\varDelta^{2})j_{1}(b\varDelta)\\

f_{3}(b)&=\frac{M_{d}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\varDelta\,\frac{\varDelta^{5}}{2M_{d}^{2}}\Big(\bar{c}_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})\Big)j_{3}(b\varDelta)\,.\end{split}

(89)

The peculiar linear combinations

\bar{c}_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})

and

\bar{c}_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-\frac{\bm{\varDelta}^{2}}{2M_{d}^{2}}\bar{s}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})

in effect amount to replacing
J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})\rightarrow J_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})-S_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})

in the formulas for
\bar{c}_{T1}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
[Eq. (
35
)]
and
\bar{c}_{T2}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
[Eq. (
36
)].
This occurs because, when using the asymmetric EMT,
quark spin is not counted towards the momentum density.
Since force constitutes a rate of change of momentum, changes in quark spin
likewise do not count towards the force distribution
when using the asymmetric EMT.

From here, it is most instructive—as it was with the other densities we
considered—to look separately at unpolarized and tensor-polarized ensembles.
As before, the force density in pure states can be reconstructed through
appropriate linear combinations of these.

For an unpolarized ensemble, only
f_{0}(b)
survives,
and the force density is given by:

\displaystyle f_{U}^{j}(\bm{b})=\hat{b}^{j}f_{0}(b)\,,

(90)

which is a central force.
An especially instructive case to consider is pointlike nucleons,
for which
A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=1
and
\bar{c}_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})=0
.
If we use the expression (
30
) for
\bar{c}_{U}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
,
but without the sum over
N

(in order to count the force felt by a single nucleon),
then with the aid of
Eq. (
89
)
and the identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\varDelta\,\varDelta^{2}j_{l}(\varDelta b)j_{l}\left(\frac{\varDelta r}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi}{b^{2}}\delta(r-2b)\,,

(91)

we find:

\displaystyle f_{U}^{j}(\bm{b})=-\frac{1}{4\pi r^{2}}\Big(V_{c}^{\prime}(r)u^{2}(r)+V_{w}^{\prime}(r)w^{2}(r)+4\sqrt{2}V_{t}^{\prime}(r)u(r)w(r)\Big)\bigg|_{r=2b}\,,

(92)

where the potential functions are defined in Eqs. (
103
)
and (
112
).
For a finite-size nucleon, this force density must be additionally smeared
through a convolution with the Fourier transform of
A_{N}(\bm{\varDelta}^{2})
.
Since

\frac{1}{4\pi r^{2}}u^{2}(r)

and

\frac{1}{4\pi r^{2}}w^{2}(r)

give the probability densities of the S- and D-waves,
it would seem that
-V_{c}^{\prime}(r)
and
-V_{w}^{\prime}(r)
give the central force experienced
by a nucleon in the S- and D-wave, respectively—at least,
when polarization states are averaged over.
There is an additional interference force proportional to
-V^{\prime}_{t}(r)
,
which is manifestly quantum mechanical
and cannot be cleanly attributed to the nucleon being in
a particular orbital state.

For a tensor-polarized ensemble, the force distribution is:

\displaystyle f_{T}^{j}(\bm{b})=\left(3\cos^{2}\theta-1\right)\left[f_{2}(b)+\frac{3}{5}f_{3}(b)\right]\hat{b}^{j}-3\sin\theta\cos\theta\left[f_{2}(b)-\frac{2}{5}f_{3}(b)\right]\hat{\theta}^{j}\,.

(93)

The central part reproduces Eq. (31a) of Ref.
54
,
and the polar part contains a term
-\frac{2}{5}f_{3}(b)

missing from their Eq. (31b)
14

the
-\frac{2}{5}f_{3}(b)

IV
Summary and outlook

In this work, we calculated the one-body contributions to
the eleven form factors of the deuteron’s asymmetric energy-momentum tensor.
Since two-body contributions have been left out,
the four non-conserved form factors
(
\bar{c}_{U}
,
\bar{c}_{T1}
,
\bar{c}_{T2}
and
\bar{s}
)
are non-zero.
However, rather than constituting a flaw,
these form factors allow us to
map out the distribution of the inter-nucleon force within the nucleon
through the Cauchy momentum equation.

Six of the eleven form factors
(
A_{U}
,
A_{T}
,
J
,
D_{U}
,
D_{T1}
and
D_{T2}
)
have been calculated previously
29
;
42
;
41
;
77
.
We have compared our results to prior works, finding mixed agreement.
Several discrepancies can be explained by
the use of different deuteron wave functions,
or by the use of ad hoc substitutions made in prior calculations.
However, all existing calculations of
D_{T2}
differ,
likely owing to this form factor’s sensitivity to fine details of dynamics.
The other five form factors
(
\bar{c}_{U}
,
\bar{c}_{T1}
,
\bar{c}_{T2}
,
S
and
\bar{s}
)
were calculated for the first time in this work.

From the form factors, we obtained spatial distributions of mechanical properties
following the methods developed in Refs.
58
;
31
;
34
;
33
.
These included mass, mass flux, momentum, stress and force distributions.
The stress distributions were parametrized using the framework of
Polyakov and Sun
88
,
which was extended to include torsion stress
from the antisymmetric part of the stress tensor.
The symmetric part of the stress tensor was locally diagonalized to obtain principal stresses,
and the torsion stress of the antisymmetric part was shown to induce spin reorientation.
Finally, the framework of Kim and Kim
54

was used to obtain force distributions in the nucleons through the Cauchy momentum equation.

There are several directions that the work here can be extended and built upon.
One of these is the calculation of two-body currents,
which has been considered by He and Zahed
41

and Panteleeva
et al.

77
.
Ideally, the inclusion of these currents will restore local momentum conservation,
so that the non-conserved form factors
(
\bar{c}_{U}
,
\bar{c}_{T1}
,
\bar{c}_{T2}
and
\bar{s}
)
vanish when these contributions are included.
However, this vanishing requires a complete and self-consistent calculation.
For instance, merely including pion exchange contributions when the potential
includes phenomenological short-distance repulsion cannot be expected to restore
local momentum conservation;

all
of the interactions must be included in the two-body current for this to happen.
Future research in this direction will likely involve either a simplified model of
inter-nucleon interactions,
or investigation into an effective Lagrangian that can reproduce the AV18 potential.

Another potential avenue for future research is
the calculation of energy and energy flux densities of the deuteron
within a Galilei-covariant framework,
akin to the framework considered for non-relativistic fermions in Ref.
33
.
This would require the calculation of additional form factors,
due to Galilei symmetry being less constraining than Lorentz symmetry.
Since nature is Lorentz covariant rather than Galilei covariant,
these additional form factors are in a sense spurious,
and their size could be used as to estimate the error arising
from the use of non-relativistic quantum mechanics.

It may additionally be interesting to analyze the separate
quark and gluon contributions to the deuteron stress and force distributions.
This would of course require separating the nucleon form factors into
quark and gluon contributions,
and accordingly non-zero
\bar{c}_{N}^{(q,g)}
would need to be considered.
This could lead to significant changes to the stress and force distributions,
and a comparative study between a quark-gluon breakdown for proton and deuteron stresses
could be especially enlightening.

One final avenue for future research is the application of the methods
developed here to calculating stress distributions in heavy spin-one quarkonia.
Many of the formulas we have derived can be used as-is,
with the Cornell potential in place of the AV18 potential.
In principal, one could also apply the framework to heavy spin-zero quarkonia
by dropping terms related to the D-wave and to spin.

Appendix B
Deuteron wave function

This appendix reviews basic properties of the deuteron wave function.
Since the deuteron is a spin-triplet state,
it is helpful to recast the wave function

\psi_{d}^{(s;s_{p},s_{n})}(\bm{r})
—which depends on two spin-half indices

s_{p}
and
s_{n}
—into
a
3\times 3
matrix

\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})

in the spin triplet representation
whose elements depend only on
s_{p}+s_{n}
.
Elements of this matrix are defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle m_{s}|\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})|m_{j}\rangle=\sum_{s_{p},s_{n}}\left\langle 1,m_{s}\middle|\tfrac{1}{2},s_{p};\tfrac{1}{2},s_{n}\right\rangle\psi_{d}^{(m_{j};s_{p},s_{n})}(\bm{r})\,,\end{split}

(96)

where

\left\langle 1,m_{s}\middle|\tfrac{1}{2},s_{p};\tfrac{1}{2},s_{n}\right\rangle

are Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.
Here the columns have fixed
m_{j}

(deuteron magnetic number)
and the rows have fixed
m_{s}=s_{p}+s_{n}

(two-nucleon spin magnetic number).
In this matrix form,
the radial and angular dependence of the wave function can be broken down as:

\displaystyle\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})=\mathcal{Y}_{101}(\hat{r})\frac{u(r)}{r}+\mathcal{Y}_{121}(\hat{r})\frac{w(r)}{r}\,,

(97)

where
u(r)
and
w(r)
are respectively the S- and D-wave radial wave functions,
and where

\displaystyle\langle m_{s}|\mathcal{Y}_{jls}(\hat{r})|m_{j}\rangle=\sum_{m_{l}}\langle l,m_{l};s,m_{s}|j,m_{j}\rangle Y_{l}^{m_{l}}(\hat{r})

(98)

are commonly called spinor spherical harmonics.
Here,
Y_{l}^{m}(\hat{r})
are the spherical harmonics.

A few other elementary properties of the deuteron wave function
are worth reviewing.
Given the convention in Eq. (
97
),
the S- and D-waves are normalized jointly as:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,\Big(u^{2}(r)+w^{2}(r)\Big)=1\,.

(99)

There is no
r^{2}
in the integration element,
because it’s effectively been absorbed by the definition of
u(r)
and
w(r)
.
Integrals of
u^{2}(r)
or
w^{2}(r)
by themselves give the S- and D-wave probabilities:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,u^{2}(r)&=\mathcal{P}_{S}\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,w^{2}(r)&=\mathcal{P}_{D}\,.\end{split}

(100)

The quadrupole moment of the deuteron is defined
as the mean value of
\frac{1}{4}(3z^{2}-r^{2})
in an
s_{d}=+1
state,
and is given in terms of the S- and D-waves by:

\displaystyle Q_{d}=\frac{1}{20}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,r^{2}\Big[2\sqrt{2}u(r)w(r)-w^{2}(r)\Big]\,.

(101)

B.1
Spinor spherical harmonics

As matrices, the spinor spherical harmonics can be written:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{Y}_{101}(\hat{r})&=Y_{0}^{0}(\hat{r})\mathbbm{1}_{3\times 3}\\
\mathcal{Y}_{121}(\hat{r})&=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{10}}Y_{2}^{0}(\hat{r})&-\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{10}}Y_{2}^{1*}(\hat{r})&\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{5}}Y_{2}^{2*}(\hat{r})\\
\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{10}}Y_{2}^{1}(\hat{r})&-\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{5}}Y_{2}^{0}(\hat{r})&\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{10}}Y_{2}^{1*}(\hat{r})\\
\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{5}}Y_{2}^{2}(\hat{r})&\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{10}}Y_{2}^{1}(\hat{r})&\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{10}}Y_{2}^{0}(\hat{r})\end{bmatrix}\,.\end{split}

(102)

The spinor spherical harmonics—and thus the matrix form of the deuteron
wave function (
97
)—are Hermitian.

Neglecting charge symmetry breaking terms,
the nucleon-nucleon potential
can generically be written in the form:

\displaystyle V(\bm{r},\bm{\sigma}_{p},\bm{\sigma}_{n})=V_{d}(r)+(\bm{\sigma}_{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{n})V_{\sigma}(r)+\hat{S}_{pn}V_{t}(r)+\bm{L}^{2}V_{l2}(r)+(\bm{L}\cdot\bm{S})V_{ls}(r)+(\bm{L}\cdot\bm{S})^{2}V_{ls2}(r)\,,

(103)

where
\bm{S}=\bm{\sigma}_{p}+\bm{\sigma}_{n}
and

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{pn}=3(\bm{\sigma}_{p}\cdot\hat{r})(\bm{\sigma}_{n}\cdot\hat{r})-\bm{\sigma}_{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{n}

(104)

is the tensor operator.
For the spin-triplet channel,

(\bm{\sigma}_{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{n})
effectively equals
1
,
and the functions
V_{d}(r)
and
V_{\sigma}(r)
can be combined
into a central potential:

\displaystyle V_{c}(r)=V_{d}(r)+V_{\sigma}(r)\,.

(105)

The actions of the remaining operators in the potential (
103
)
on the spinor spherical harmonics are:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbbm{1}\mathcal{Y}_{101}=\mathcal{Y}_{101}&&\mathbbm{1}\mathcal{Y}_{121}=\mathcal{Y}_{121}\\
\hat{S}_{pn}\mathcal{Y}_{101}=\sqrt{8}\mathcal{Y}_{121}&&\hat{S}_{pn}\mathcal{Y}_{121}=\sqrt{8}\mathcal{Y}_{101}-2\mathcal{Y}_{121}\\
\bm{L}^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{101}=0&&\bm{L}^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{121}=6\mathcal{Y}_{121}\\
(\bm{L}\cdot\bm{S})\mathcal{Y}_{101}=0&&(\bm{L}\cdot\bm{S})\mathcal{Y}_{121}=-3\mathcal{Y}_{121}\\
(\bm{L}\cdot\bm{S})^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{101}=0&&(\bm{L}\cdot\bm{S})^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{121}=9\mathcal{Y}_{121}\,.\end{array}

Accordingly, the Schrödinger equation for the deuteron wave function

\displaystyle-\frac{\bm{\nabla^{2}}}{m_{N}}\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})+V(\bm{r},\bm{\sigma}_{p},\bm{\sigma_{n}})\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})=E_{d}\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})\,,

(111)

where
E_{d}<0
is the deuteron binding energy,
can be rewritten as coupled second-order differential equations
for
u(r)
and
w(r)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}u^{\prime\prime}(r)&=m_{N}\Big(-E_{d}+V_{c}(r)\Big)u(r)+\sqrt{8}m_{N}V_{t}(r)w(r)\\
w^{\prime\prime}(r)&=m_{N}\left(-E_{d}+V_{w}(r)+\frac{6}{m_{N}r^{2}}\right)w(r)+\sqrt{8}m_{N}V_{t}(r)u(r)\\
V_{w}(r)&\equiv V_{c}(r)-2V_{t}(r)+6V_{l2}(r)-3V_{ls}(r)+9V_{ls2}(r)\,.\end{split}

(112)

These relations are helpful for numerical calculations
of the deuteron EMT-FFs,
since several formulas depend on the second derivatives
of
u(r)
and
w(r)
.

B.2
Polarization Vectors and Projectors

The polarization vectors
\varepsilon_{a}(s_{d})
for a non-relativistic deuteron
with spin-quantization axis along
\hat{\bm{z}}
are:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{a}(\pm 1)=\frac{\hat{x}_{a}\pm i\hat{y}_{a}}{\sqrt{2}}\;,\qquad\varepsilon(0)=\hat{z}_{a}\,.

(113)

Note that, in contrast to the relativistic case,
these polarization vectors do not depend on the deuteron’s momentum.

The unpolarized projector is:

\frac{1}{3}\sum_{s_{d}}\varepsilon_{a}(s_{d})\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}(s_{d})=\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ab}

(114)

The vector-polarized projector is:

\frac{1}{2}\left[\varepsilon_{a}(1)\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}(1)-\varepsilon_{a}(-1)\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}(-1)\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{abc}\hat{z}_{c}

(115)

The tensor-polarized projector is:

\varepsilon_{a}(1)\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}(1)+\varepsilon_{a}(-1)\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}(-1)-2\varepsilon_{a}(0)\varepsilon^{\prime*}_{b}(0)=\delta^{ab}-3\hat{z}^{a}\hat{z}^{b}

(116)

B.3
One-body operators in the spin-triplet representation

Besides the deuteron wave function itself,
nucleon-spin-dependent operators also can be converted to
matrices in the triplet representation,
and sandwiched between
\Psi_{d}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})
and
\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})
.
The trace of the result can be taken with a spin density matrix to get
e.g. the expectation value for unpolarized or tensor-polarized
ensembles of deuterons.
This provides an especially practical pathway for calculations of EMT-FFs,
by replacing spin sums by matrix operations.
In such a spin matrix representation,
Eq. (
20
) would be rewritten:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{d}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{d}\rangle=\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}r\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{\bm{\varDelta}\cdot\bm{r}}{2}}\Psi_{d}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}_{p}|\hat{T}^{ij}_{p}|\bm{p}_{p}\rangle\Psi_{d}(\bm{r})\bigg|_{\bm{P}_{p}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\bm{P}_{d}-i\overleftrightarrow{\bm{\nabla}}\big)}\,,

where

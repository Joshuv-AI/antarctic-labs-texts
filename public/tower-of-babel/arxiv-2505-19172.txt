Some inequalities of isoperimetric type for the c c -affine surface area
Shiri Artstein-Avidan, Arnon Chor

Abstract
We study the c c -affine surface area Ω c ​ ( K ) \Omega^{c}(K) , recently introduced by Schütt, Werner and Yalikun. We show that on the class of ball-bodies, Ω c ​ ( K ) \Omega^{c}(K) is uniquely maximized by a ball of radius n n + 1 \frac{n}{n+1} , and that a Santaló-type inequality holds, and Ω c ​ ( K ) ​ Ω c ​ ( K c ) \Omega^{c}(K)\Omega^{c}(K^{c}) is uniquely maximized be a Euclidean ball of radius 1 / 2 1/2 . We also produce some more intricate inequalities involving the surface area.



In this short note we discuss a new notion reminiscent of the affine surface area of a convex body, which is relevant to the class of ball-bodies
{\cal S}_{n}
(see
[
3
,
5
]
and the references therein).
The class
{\cal S}_{n}
, which is a subset of the class
{\cal K}^{n}
of all convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(i.e. compact, nonempty convex sets), consists of those convex bodies which are intersections of translates of the unit Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Equivalently, in addition to singletons, the class consists of convex bodies with generalized sectional curvature at least
1
at every boundary point. In this note, in contrast with previous works, we exclude
\emptyset,\mathbb{R}^{n}
from
{\cal S}_{n}
. The unit Euclidean ball is denoted here by
B_{2}^{n}
.

The class
{\cal S}_{n}
(which is also sometimes called the
c
-class, because of the
c
-duality operation which we soon define) can also be characterized as the class of summands of
B_{2}^{n}
, namely bodies
K\in{\cal K}^{n}
for which there exists
L\in{\cal K}^{n}
with
K+L=B_{2}^{n}
. We have recently studied this class intensively
[
3
]
, emphasizing the role of the unique (up to trivial symmetries) order reversing bijection defined on this set, mapping a body
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
to the unique
L\in{\cal S}_{n}
with
K-L=B_{2}^{n}
. We also showed in
[
2
,
1
]
that it is the unique isometry (with respect to the Hausdorff metric) on this class, other than the identity map, again up to trivial symmetries. In geometric terms one may explicitly write this duality as

K^{c}=\bigcap_{x\in K}(x+B_{2}^{n}).

We note that for any
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
,
K^{cc}=K
.

Recently, Schütt, Werner and Yalikun in their paper “Floating bodies for ball-convex bodies”
[
11
]
showed that in
{\cal S}_{n}
there is a natural concept replacing the usual “floating body”, where instead of considering half-spaces which cut off a portion at most
\delta
of the volume, one intersects
1
-balls which do the same, thus forming a
c
-floating body which is in the class
{\cal S}_{n}
and which converges to
K
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. We denote it here by
F_{c,\delta}(K)\subset K
. Moreover, for bodies which have, at all points, sectional curvatures all strictly greater than
1
, they showed that the limit of the volume difference, scaled appropriately, exists, and computed a closed formula for it. They showed

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{{\rm Vol}_{n}(K)-{\rm Vol}_{n}(F_{c,\delta}(K))}{\delta^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}=c_{n}\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-1\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{K}(x)

(1)

where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{K}
denotes the Lebesgue surface area measure on
\partial K
,
c_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n+1}{{\rm Vol}_{n-1}(B_{2}^{n-1})}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}
and
\kappa_{i}
denote the sectional curvatures at the point
x\in\partial K
.

The expression (
1
) should be compared with the corresponding limit formula for the usual floating body, which gives, up to constant, the affine surface area of the body, given by

\Omega(K)=\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\kappa_{i}(K,x)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{K}(x).

(2)

The conditions on the sectional curvatures of
K
may seem restrictive. Nevertheless, for a convex body, almost every point on the boundary
\partial K
is a “normal” point, namely the indicatrix and principal curvatures exist and are well defined (see
[
10
, Section 2.6]
). For a body in
{\cal S}_{n}
, these sectional curvatures are at least
1
.
This fact, together with (
1
), motivated their definition for the functional, which we call
c
-affine-surface-area, although the class
{\cal S}_{n}
itself is not affine invariant so the name is far from perfect; nevertheless it is a
c
-variant of the affine surface area. We mention that in
[
11
]
this functional is referred to as “relative affine surface area” and is denoted
as^{R}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
which is not a point. Define the
c
-affine surface area of
K
to be

\Omega^{c}(K)=\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-1\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{K}(x).

In what follows it will be convenient to introduce also the principal radii of curvature
r_{i}(u)
, defined as the non-zero eigenvalues (in increasing order) of
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
, the Hessian (in the sense of Alexandrov) of the support function
h_{K}(u)=\max_{y\in K}\langle y,u\rangle
. These are defined almost everywhere on
S^{n-1}
, see discussion in the Appendix and in
[
10
, Section 2.6 and Section 1.5, Note 3]
. It follows from the discussion in
[
10
, Section 2.6, page 130]
or from
[
9
]
that on a set of full measure in
S^{n-1}
,
r_{i}(u)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}(x(u))}
where
x(u)
is the unique point in
\partial K
with normal vector
u\in S^{n-1}
.

Recall that for a normal point
x(u)
the measure
\mu_{K}(x)
, pulled back to
S^{n-1}
, has density
\omega_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)
for the normalized Haar measure
\sigma
on
S^{n-1}
and for
\omega_{n}={\rm Vol}_{n-1}(S^{n-1})
. In particular, letting
f_{K}(u)=\omega_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\kappa_{i}(K,x(u))^{-1}=\omega_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}(u)
for
u\in S^{n-1}
, we have
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{K}(u)=f_{K}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)
. Since normal points have full measure with respect to
\mu_{K}
on
\partial K
, and since directions
u\in S^{n-1}
where the support function
h_{K}
is twice differentiable, in the sense of Alexandrov, also have full measure, throughout this note integration over
S^{n-1}
should be interpreted as integration over the set of full measure
D_{K}\subseteq S^{n-1}
of directions
u
such that
x(\pm u)
is normal in
K
and also
h_{K}
is twice differentiable at
u
. (See also the historical discussion in
[
10
, Section 10.5]
pertaining to Leichtweiss’s generalization of the affine surface area to non-smooth bodies.)

Evidently we may replace the expression in Definition

1
by the following expression, which is easier to work with

\Omega^{c}(K)=\omega_{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(1-{r_{i}(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}r_{i}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u).

(3)

Remark 2
.

In
[
11
]
the authors actually consider for any
R>0
the class of intersections of radius-
R
balls, and have an
R
-version for the floating body and the affine surface area, which converges as
R\rightarrow\infty
to the usual floating body and usual affine surface area. We chose to specialize to the case
R=1
(as we did in
[
3
]
) since all results we consider can easily be rescaled.

Since clearly
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-1\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}
, we have
\Omega^{c}(K)\leq\Omega(K)
, and in particular the following version of an isoperimetric-type inequality for
\Omega^{c}
was given in
[
11
]
:

\Omega^{c}(K)\leq\Omega(K)\leq n{\rm Vol}_{n}(B^{n}_{2})^{\frac{2}{n+1}}{\rm Vol}_{n}(K)^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}.

(4)

We note that when
K
is a very small ball then
r_{i}
is small as well and we expect the inequality
\Omega^{c}(K)\leq\Omega(K)
to be close to being an equality. Since for a ball the second inequality in
(
4
) is equality, one does not expect to be able to improve the constant in (
4
). However, in contrast with the class
{\cal K}^{n}
, in the class

{\cal S}_{n}
one can consider the question of maximizing

\Omega^{c}(K)

among all sets in
{\cal S}_{n}
, without a volume normalization. Indeed, it is easy to show that in
{\cal S}_{n}
, the body with largest
c
-affine surface area, is a ball of radius
1-\frac{1}{n+1}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For any
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
which is not a point, we have

\Omega^{c}(K)\leq\Omega^{c}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}\right),

with equality if and only if
K=\frac{n}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}
.

The proof of Theorem
3
is actually one line, once we consider the expression (
3
). Indeed, point-wise it is true that
(1-r)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}r^{\frac{n}{n+1}}
is maximized, over
(0,1)
, for
r=\frac{n}{n+1}
, and therefore also the integral of this expression is maximal for this specific radius.
Below we shall present yet another argument for Theorem
3
which captures some more intricate properties of the quantities involved.

Remark 4
.

We note that by definition

\Omega^{c}(rB_{2}^{n})=\omega_{n}\left(1-r\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}r^{\frac{n(n-1)}{n+1}}
so that for any
K\in{\cal S}_{n}

\Omega^{c}(K)\leq\Omega^{c}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}\right)=\frac{n^{\frac{n(n-1)}{n+1}}}{(n+1)^{n-1}}\cdot\omega_{n}.

Our main interest in this note is to compare
\Omega^{c}(K)
and
\Omega^{c}(K^{c})
. It turns out that a Santaló-type inequality holds for this pair, and our main result is the following.

Theorem 5
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For any
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
which is not a point or a translate of
B_{2}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}(K)\Omega^{c}(K^{c})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{2}^{n}\right)^{2},

with equality if and only if
K=\frac{1}{2}B_{2}^{n}
.

As we shall demonstrate below, Theorem
5
follows from an inequality connecting the quantities

\Omega^{c}(K),\Omega^{c}(K^{c})
and the usual surface areas
S(K),S(K^{c})
. Here
S(K)
denotes the Lebesgue surface area of the convex set
K
. In particular
S(K)=\int_{\partial K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{K}=\omega_{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)
. Before stating this inequality in Theorem
7
, let us describe the main tool from the study of the
c
-duality and boundary structure on
{\cal S}_{n}
which will be used to prove it.

Theorem 6
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
which is not a point or a translate of
B_{2}^{n}
and assume
u\in S^{n-1}
is such that
h_{K}
is twice differentiable at
u
and
h_{K^{c}}
is twice differentiable at
-u
, in the sense of Alexandrov. Then, letting
0\leq r_{1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{n-1}\leq 1
and
0\leq s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n-1}\leq 1
stand for the principal radii of curvature of
K
in direction
u
and of
K^{c}
in direction
-u
, respectively, we have

r_{i}+s_{n-i}=1,\quad i=1,\ldots,n-1.

This theorem was given as Theorem 4.27 in
[
3
]
in the case where
h_{K}
is twice
continuously
differentiable, and generalizes a result by Bonnesen and Fenchel pertaining to a self dual body (i.e. a body of constant width). See the discussion in Bonnesen and Fenchel
[
6
, Chapter 15, Section 63 in page 128]
and also Chakerian
[
8
]
. Since the proof of Theorem
6
is very similar to the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.27]
, we defer it to the Appendix.

Using Theorem
6
we can prove our main tool, which we next state and prove.

Theorem 7
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
which is not a point or a translate of
B_{2}^{n}
. Then

\Omega^{c}(K)\leq S(K)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\Omega^{c}(K^{c})^{\frac{1}{n}},

with equality if and only if
K
is a ball.

Proof.

For
u\in S^{n-1}
where the points
x(\pm u)
are both normal points of
\partial K
and
\partial K^{c}
respectively, and where
h_{K}
,
h_{K^{c}}
are twice differentiable in the sense of Alexandrov at
u,-u
respectively, let
\varphi(u)=\omega_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}{(1-r_{i}(u))^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}({r_{i}(u)})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}
and
\eta(u)=\left(\omega_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}{(r_{i}(u))^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}(1-{r_{i}(u)})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}
. By definition

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}(K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)

Using Theorem
6
we see that, after a change of variables
u\mapsto-u
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}(K^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}{(r_{i}(u))^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}(1-{r_{i}(u)})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\eta^{n}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u).

We use the inequality of Hölder,

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi(u)}{\eta(u)}\cdot\eta(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{S^{n-1}}\left(\frac{\varphi(u)}{\eta(u)}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\int_{S^{n-1}}\eta^{n}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

By our choice of
\eta
and
\varphi
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}\left(\frac{\varphi(u)}{\eta(u)}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}\left(\frac{\omega_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}{(1-r_{i}(u))^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}({r_{i}(u)})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}}{\omega_{n}^{1/n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}{(r_{i}(u))^{\frac{1}{n(n+1)}}}(1-{r_{i}(u)})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}{r_{i}(u)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)=S(K).

Plugging into Hölder’s inequality we get

\Omega^{c}(K)\leq S(K)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\Omega^{c}(K^{c})^{\frac{1}{n}},

as claimed.

If we have equality, then we have equality in Hölder’s inequality, which implies that
\frac{\varphi}{\eta}
and
\eta
are linearly dependent (as functions on
D_{K}
), which after a calculation implies that
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}(u)
is constant on
D_{K}
, say
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}(u)\equiv R^{n-1}
, for some
R>0
. Since
D_{K}
has full measure, the surface area measures of
K
and
RB_{2}^{n}
are equal, and by Minkowski’s uniqueness theorem (see for example
[
10
, Theorem 8.1.1]
and the following paragraph),
K
is a translate of
RB_{2}^{n}
.

By multiplying the inequalities in Theorem
7
and rearranging the terms we get

Corollary 8
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then for any
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
which is not a point or a translate of
B_{2}^{n}
, we have

\Omega^{c}(K)\Omega^{c}(K^{c})\leq S(K)S(K^{c}),

with equality if and only if
K
is a ball.

From Corollary
8
we can deduce Theorem
5
easily, using that
K-K^{c}=B_{2}^{n}
and that
S((1-t)K_{0}+tK_{1})\geq S(K_{0})^{1-t}S(K_{1})^{t}
for
K_{0},K_{1}\in{\cal K}^{n}
and
t\in(0,1)
(which follows from Brunn-Minkowski’s inequality for mixed volumes, see
[
7
, Theorem 20.4.1]
for example).

Proof of Theorem
5
.

By Corollary
8
we see that

\Omega^{c}(K)\Omega^{c}(K^{c})\leq S(K)S(K^{c})\leq S\left(\frac{1}{2}(K-K^{c})\right)^{2}=S\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{2}^{n}\right)^{2}.

If equality holds throughout, then it also holds in the first inequality, and then by Corollary
8
,
K
is a ball. Computing the functional in Theorem
5
for a ball
rB_{2}^{n}
, we see that
\Omega^{c}(rB_{2}^{n})\Omega^{c}((1-r)B_{2}^{n})=\omega_{n}^{2}\left(r(1-r)\right)^{n-1}
, which can only equal
S\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{2}^{n}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{2^{n-1}}\right)^{2}
if
r=\frac{1}{2}
.

Iterating the use of Theorem
7
also produces the following lemma which is the key ingredient in our second proof of
Theorem
3
, but can also be seen (given the Proposition
10
which follows) as a strengthening of it.

Lemma 9
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
which is not a point or a translate of
B_{2}^{n}
. Then

\Omega^{c}(K)\leq S(K)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}S(K^{c})^{\frac{1}{n+1}},

with equality if and only if
K
is a ball.

Proof.

We use Theorem
7
for both
K
and
K^{c}
, recalling that
K^{cc}=K
, to write

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}(K)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(K)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\Omega^{c}(K^{c})^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}(K^{c})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(K^{c})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\Omega^{c}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Plugging the second into the first we get

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}(K)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(K)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(S(K^{c})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\Omega^{c}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Taking power
n
and rearranging we end up with

\displaystyle\Omega^{c}(K)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(K)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}S(K^{c})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}

as claimed. Since balls are the only equality cases in Theorem
7
, they also are also the unique equality cases here.

Finally we provide the following simple consequence of Alexandrov’s inequality. Recall that Alexandrov’s inequality, connecting various mixed volume ratios (see, e.g.
[
4
, Theorem 1.1.11]
), states in particular that
\left(\frac{S(K)}{S(B_{2}^{n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq M^{*}(K)=\int_{S^{n-1}}h_{K}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For any
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
,

S(K)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}S(K^{c})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\leq S\left(\frac{n}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}\right)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}S\left(\frac{1}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

Additionally, if there is equality in the inequality above, then
K=\frac{n}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}
.

Proof.

By Alexandrov’s inequality we have

\displaystyle S(K)^{n}S(K^{c})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(B_{2}^{n})^{n+1}\left(M^{*}(K)^{n}M^{*}(K^{c})\right)^{n-1}.

Since
K-K^{c}=B_{2}^{n}
we see that
M^{*}(K^{c})=1-M^{*}(K)
. Thus

\displaystyle S(K)^{n}S(K^{c})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(B_{2}^{n})^{n+1}\left(M^{*}(K)^{n}(1-M^{*}(K))\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(B_{2}^{n})^{n+1}\left(\max_{r\in(0,1)}r^{n}(1-r)\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(B_{2}^{n})^{n+1}\left(\frac{n^{n}}{(n+1)^{n+1}}\right)^{n-1}=S\left(\frac{n}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}\right)^{n}S\left(\frac{1}{n+1}B_{2}^{n}\right),

completing the proof. If equality holds throughout, we must have equality in Aleandrov’s inequality for mixed volumes, which implies
K
is a Euclidean ball. Additionally, it means
M^{*}(K)^{n}(1-M^{*}(K))=\max_{r\in(0,1)}r^{n}(1-r)
, which means
M^{*}(K)=\argmax_{r\in(0,1)}r^{n}(1-r)=\frac{n}{n+1}
, completing the proof of the equality case.

Second Proof of Theorem
3
.

It is a direct consequence of Lemma
9
with Proposition
10
, including the equality case.

Remark 11
.

Considering the one-line proof of Theorem
3
presented at the beginning, one might expect a similar proof for Theorem
5
. One way to go about this is to consider the expression
F(K)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}(u)^{\alpha_{i}}(1-r_{i}(u))^{\beta_{i}}d\mu(u)
where
\mu
is any measure. If
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}>0
are chosen so that

\Phi(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n-1})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}(1-r_{i})^{\beta_{i}}+\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1-r_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}r_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\right)

is maximized at the point
\mathbb{\frac{1}{2}}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2})
, we get the additive inequality

\frac{F(K)+F(K^{c})}{2}=\int_{S^{n-1}}\Phi((r_{i}(u))_{i=1}^{n-1})d\mu(u)\leq\int_{S^{n-1}}\Phi\left({\mathbb{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)d\mu(u)=F\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{2}^{n}\right).

(Which can be combined with
\sqrt{F(K)\cdot F(K^{c})}\leq\frac{F(K)+F(K^{c})}{2}
of course.)
However, in the case which we have, namely
\alpha_{i}=\frac{1}{n+1}
and
\beta_{i}=\frac{n}{n+1}
,
the function
\Phi
is not maximized at
{\mathbb{\frac{1}{2}}}
. Indeed, even considering only balls, taking
{\bf r}=(r,r,\ldots,r)
we get

f(r)=2\Phi({\mathbb{r}})=r^{\frac{n(n-1)}{n+1}}(1-r)^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}+r^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}(1-r)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{n+1}}=r^{a}(1-r)^{b}+r^{b}(1-r)^{a},

which, differentiating twice and plugging
r=\frac{1}{2}
, gives

f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{a+b-3}\left((a-b)^{2}-(a+b)\right).

In our case,
a-b=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+1}
and
a+b=n-1
we see that for
n\geq 4
the second derivative at
\frac{1}{2}
is positive, and so it is not a maximum. In other words, there is a ball
rB_{2}^{n}
of some radius
r
close to
\frac{1}{2}
with

\frac{1}{2}(\Omega^{c}(rB_{2}^{n})+\Omega^{c}((rB_{2}^{n})^{c}))>\Omega^{c}\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{2}^{n}\right).

Appendix: Proof of Theorem
6

Let
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
and assume
u\in S^{n-1}
is such that
h_{K}
is twice differentiable at
u
and
h_{K^{c}}
is twice differentiable at
-u
in the sense of Alexandrov. Namely, a second order Taylor approximation of
h_{K}
holds at
u
:

h_{K}(u+\delta v)=h_{K}(u)+\delta\nabla h_{K}(u)\cdot v+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}v^{T}\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)v+o(\delta^{2}),

as
\delta\rightarrow 0
, and a similar Taylor approximation holds for
h_{K^{c}}(-u-\delta v)
. We used
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
to denote the Hessian, according to Alexandrov. If
h_{K}
is twice differentiable in the usual sense
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
coincides with the usual Hessian.

As
K\in{\cal S}_{n}
is strictly convex, we have that
h_{K}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. By definition of the
c
-dual, we know that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
h_{K}(x)+h_{K^{c}}(-x)=|x|
and therefore, differentiating,

\nabla h_{K}(x)-\nabla h_{K^{c}}(-x)=\frac{x^{T}}{|x|}.

(5)

Since
h_{K}
is twice differentiable at
u
in the sense of Alexandrov, we can relate
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
and
\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)
as follows.

Using
h_{K}(x)+h_{K^{c}}(-x)=|x|
, we see that

\displaystyle|u+\delta v|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{K}(u+\delta v)+h_{K^{c}}(-u-\delta v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{K}(u)+\delta\nabla h_{K}(u)\cdot v+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}v^{T}\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)v

\displaystyle+h_{K^{c}}(-u)-\delta\nabla h_{K^{c}}(-u)\cdot v+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}v^{T}\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)v+o(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|u|+\delta(\nabla h_{K}(u)-\nabla h_{K^{c}}(-u))\cdot v+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}v^{T}\left(\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)+\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)\right)v+o(\delta^{2}).

Rearranging,

\displaystyle\frac{|u+\delta v|-|u|}{\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla h_{K}(u)-\nabla h_{K^{c}}(-u))\cdot v+\frac{\delta}{2}v^{T}\left(\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)+\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)\right)v+o(\delta).

Using the first and second order behavior of
|u+\delta v|
, we see

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v^{T}\left(\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)+\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)\right)v=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{|u+\delta v|-|u|-\delta\frac{u^{T}v}{|u|}}{\delta^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{v^{T}}{|u|}\left(I-\frac{u}{|u|}\otimes\frac{u}{|u|}\right)v.

Seeing
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is arbitrary,

\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)+\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)=\frac{1}{|u|}\left(I-\frac{u}{|u|}\otimes\frac{u}{|u|}\right).

(6)

Note that the matrix
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
is symmetric, and since
h_{K}
is
1
-homogeneous, there is one eigenvector of eigenvalue
0
, which is in direction
u
. The other eigenvectors are in
u^{\perp}
, and are shared by
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
and
\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)
. By (
6
), we see that for any such eigenvector, the corresponding eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
and
\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)
sum to 1. Denoting by
r_{1}\leq\ldots\leq r_{n-1}
the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}h_{K}(u)
in increasing order, and by
s_{1}\leq\ldots\leq s_{n-1}
the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}h_{K^{c}}(-u)
in increasing order, we see indeed that
r_{i}
and
s_{n-i}
correspond to the same eigenvector and so
r_{i}+s_{n-i}=1
.

The holographic dual of the GHZ state
Libo Jiang, Yan Liu

Abstract
Current holographic research mostly focuses on a subset of quantum systems with a classical gravity dual. Although the precise boundary of this subset remains unknown, certain constraints are recognized; for instance, holographic entropies obey inequalities that are violated by general quantum states such as the GHZ states. This paper, however, presents a gravity dual for GHZ states—a non-ordinary geometry termed the booklet wormhole . We demonstrate an exact match for all entropy properties with the GHZ state, as well as the identity of the Euclidean partition functions for both systems. The booklet wormhole circumvents the conventional holographic entropy inequalities because different topologies are inevitably included in the gravitational path integral, even in the large- N N limit. This provides the first explicit holographic duality with a non-perturbative quantum effect. Remarkably, the construction is simple and maximally entangled, making it ideal for gedanken experiments.



I
Introduction

Holography establishes a duality between quantum systems and higher-dimensional quantum gravity systems, with AdS/CFT being the most well-known example. Currently, most bottom-up research on holography is restricted to “holographic” quantum systems from the outset, as not all quantum systems or states have a classical gravity dual. For example, the holographic entropy (also known as the Ryu-Takayanagi entropy
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006a
;
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006b
;
Nishioka et al. 2009
) imposes constraints on holographic states. Specifically, Ref.
Hayden et al. 2013
proved that tripartite information is non-positive for all holographic states. Yet, this inequality fails to hold for certain quantum states, notably the GHZ states
Greenberger et al. 1989
;
Greenberger et al. 1990
. Therefore, people believed these GHZ states do not have a holographic dual, at least not a classical dual.
Nevertheless, we will demonstrate that GHZ states do admit a simple holographic dual: a geometry with non-trivial topology, which we term the
booklet wormhole
. The holographic entropies of this geometry precisely match the GHZ states, and both systems share identical Euclidean partition functions.

Previously, holography research was largely confined to a few geometries: vacuum AdS
Witten 1998
, two-sided black holes
Maldacena 2003
, and their modifications. The GHZ state/booklet wormhole duality establishes a new testing ground for holography. Beyond being the first known duality that violates the conventional holographic entropy bound, the GHZ state also serves as a representative state for many-body entanglement. This duality will therefore play a fundamental role in the quantum information perspective of holography. Research on two-sided black holes could now be revisited in the context of the booklet wormhole, advancing our understanding of many-body correlation beyond pairwise entanglement.

II.1
GHZ state

Greenberger, Horne, and Zeilinger first constructed the 4-spin GHZ state in
Greenberger et al. 1989

Greenberger et al. 1990

(1)

The 3-qubit GHZ state holds particular significance because

It can be generalized to the

IV.3
Matching of AdS and CFT

We now compute the partition functions of the above CFT and AdS, verifying their consistency.

On the gravity side, the Euclidean action for the booklet wormhole consists solely of contributions from each page, with junction terms vanishing as demonstrated. The action of each page is given by

the partition functions of the thermal GHZ state and the corresponding booklet wormhole are equal.

VI
Discussion

The GHZ state/booklet wormhole duality is the first example that violates the conventional holographic entropy inequality (
5
) through a non-perturbative quantum effect. This violation occurs when the minimal entropy surface coincides with a multipartite junction. While prior works have employed cut-and-paste operations on AdS black holes
Climent et al. 2024
;
Balasubramanian et al.
;
Balasubramanian et al. 2024
, the resulting minimal surfaces typically coincide with horizons in the absence of junctions; also, the multi-partite junction is absent. Therefore, the topology of the minimal surface is robust. In contrast, positioning a multipartite junction at the minimal surface introduces an ambiguity in the minimal surface’s topology, as we demonstrated in
V.2
. Building on this idea, we can glue multiple RT surfaces together to reproduce a similar effect. For instance, consider constant-time slices of
n
vacuum AdS spacetimes. We cut each slice along one geodesic and glue the resulting cuts together. The corresponding CFT points where these geodesics terminate are likewise identified. The resulting geometry is illustrated in
fig.

11
. We will leave the exploration of this type of geometry for future study.

Figure 11:
The sketch illustrates a constant-time slice of a booklet geometry, which also exhibits a non-perturbative quantum effect in the computation of entanglement entropies. Solid lines represent the CFT boundaries, and the dashed line denotes the multipartite junction in the bulk.

Multi-boundary wormholes with smooth geometry have been studied in prior works
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
;
Marolf et al. 2015
. These geometries typically break
S_{n}
permutation symmetry, with their dual CFTs having complicated wavefunctions and non-positive tripartite information. In principle, we can construct a continuous family of states interpolating between some state
I_{3}\leq 0
and the GHZ state, whose dual geometries correspondingly vary from smooth multi-boundary wormholes to the booklet wormholes. Imagine that we put more and more GHZ particles into a smooth multi-boundary wormhole, the booklet junction must emerge by the time
I_{3}
reaches zero. How this transition happens is an interesting open question.

While the Bell state is the most popular state in quantum information, the GHZ state exhibits novel features beyond it. For instance, unlike the Bell state which reveals non-locality by statistics, Ref.
Greenberger et al. 1990
demonstrated that the GHZ state can exhibit non-locality in a single-shot experiment. How non-locality—and more fundamentally, quantum contextuality
Kochen and Specker 1967
;
Spekkens 2005
—manifest in holography remains an intriguing open question.

The “ER
=
EPR” conjecture
Maldacena and Susskind 2013
proposes that quantum entanglement between two black holes is geometrically dual to an Einstein-Rosen bridge connecting their interiors. The booklet wormholes are also connected, but by a geometry different from that of the two-sided black holes.
In the quantum side, the GHZ states exhibit multi-partite entanglement, yet pairwise subsystems only show classical correlations. One might therefore expect that a GHZ wormhole may not connect observers falling into any pair of the black holes
Susskind 2016
. This work suggests the opposite: observers accessing two of the black holes
can
detect entanglement if they enter the horizons. How the holographic teleportation
Maldacena et al. 2017
;
Gao et al. 2017
applies to the GHZ state/booklet wormhole duality is another fascinating topic that will be explored in a subsequent paper.
This new model suggests that spacetime behind horizons geometrically embodies quantum entanglement (and hopefully, the hidden variables in contextuality). We hope this holographic model of many-body entanglement can bring a deeper understanding of “spacetime from entanglement”
Van Raamsdonk 2010
.

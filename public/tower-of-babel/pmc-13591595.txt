Shifting From Classic Structural Rhinoplasty to Preservation: Clinical Experience Incorporating Preservation Rhinoplasty in Aesthetic Practice
Authors: Mune Gowda, Paige Thomas
Published: 2026

Problem Addressed: Traditional structural rhinoplasty commonly relies on dorsal reduction techniques that disrupt the native osteocartilaginous vault and may contribute to prolonged postoperative edema and delayed dorsal definition. Preservation rhinoplasty has emerged as an alternative philosophy emphasizing maintenance of native dorsal anatomy while achieving aesthetic correction. However, limited reports describe implementation of this approach within established aesthetic practices.

Technique: Preservation rhinoplasty techniques were incorporated for appropriately selected primary rhinoplasty patients in a cosmetic plastic surgery practice. The approach maintains the osteocartilaginous dorsum while addressing dorsal deformity through subdorsal septal modification and controlled push-down or let-down maneuvers. Tip refinement and overall nasal balance are achieved with conservative structural techniques while maintaining harmony between the dorsum, middle vault, and tip complex.

Outcomes: Following implementation of preservation rhinoplasty techniques, patients demonstrated reduced early postoperative swelling and earlier definition of dorsal aesthetic lines compared with traditional structural rhinoplasty previously performed in the practice. Preservation of the osteocartilaginous vault appeared to minimize soft tissue trauma and contribute to smoother dorsal contours during healing and faster visible recovery.

Conclusion: Preservation rhinoplasty represents a viable alternative to traditional dorsal reduction techniques. Maintaining native dorsal anatomy may minimize soft tissue disruption, preserve natural dorsal lines, and improve early postoperative recovery while maintaining predictable aesthetic outcomes.

Clinical Pearl: Preservation rhinoplasty shifts the operative philosophy from dorsal reduction to dorsal conservation, allowing aesthetic refinement while preserving native nasal architecture.

1.
Introduction.

The Gronwall inequality as given here estimates the difference of
solutions to two differential equations
y^{\prime}(t)=f(t,y(t))
and

z^{\prime}(t)=g(t,z(t))
in terms of the difference
between the initial conditions for the equations and the difference
between
f
and
g
. The usual version of the inequality is when

f=g
, but there is little extra work involved in proving the more general
case
[
3
]
.

This note started life as note/homework problem on a web page for
an ordinary differential equation class in 1998. Since then
it has been referenced several times:

[
2
,
4
,
1
,
5
,
6
]
.
So it seemed worthwhile to put a copy on the
arXiv so that there is a version more permanent than
one on a personal web page. While I do not know of an
explicit reference to the inequality here, I very much doubt
it is original to me.

2.
The Inequality

Theorem 2.1

(The Gronwall Inequality)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a Banach space and
U\subset\mathbf{X}
an open convex set in

\mathbf{X}
. Let
f,g\colon[a,b]\times U\to\mathbf{X}
be continuous functions and
let
y,z\colon[a,b]\to U
satisfy the initial value problems

(2.1)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=f(t,y(t)),\quad y(a)=y_{0},

(2.2)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=g(t,z(t)),\quad z(a)=z_{0}.

Also assume there is a constant
C\geq 0
so that

(2.3)

\|g(t,x_{2})-g(t,x_{1})\|\leq C\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|

and a continuous function
\phi\colon[a,b]\to[0,\infty)

so that

(2.4)

\|f(t,y(t))-g(t,y(t))\|\leq\phi(t).

Then for
t\in[a,b]

(2.5)

\|y(t)-z(t)\|\leq e^{C|t-a|}\|y_{0}-z_{0}\|+e^{C|t-a|}\int_{a}^{t}e^{-C|s-a|}\phi(s)\,ds.

Remark 2.2
.

The inequality (
2.4
) is a little awkward as it involves an
inequality along the solution
y(t)
which we may not know. But
we can replace (
2.4
) by the stronger hypothesis

(2.6)

\|f(t,x)-g(t,x)\|\leq\phi(t)\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in U

(which clearly implies (
2.4
)) and get the same result. Of course
this may mean using a choice of
\phi
that is larger than is needed
in (
2.4
).∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note we are not assuming
f
satisfies a Lipschitz condition and
therefore solutions to the initial value problem
y^{\prime}(t)=f(t,y(t))
,

y(a)=y_{0}
need not be unique. In this case the inequality can be
used to estimate
y(t)
by comparing it to the solution to

z^{\prime}(t)=g(t,z(t))
,
z(a)=y_{0}
with the same initial condition. In
this case the inequality becomes

\|y(t)-z(t)\|\leq e^{C|t-a|}\int_{a}^{t}e^{-C|s-a|}\phi(s)\,ds.

Then if we assume that the inequality (
2.6
) holds then
and
y_{1}^{\prime}(t)=f(t,y_{1}(t))
with
y_{1}(a)=y_{1}
then using the obvious
inequality
\|y(t)-y_{1}(t)\|\leq\|y(t)-z(t)\|+\|z(t)-y_{1}(t)\|
where

z^{\prime}(t)=g(t,z(t))
,
z(a)=y_{0}
we get
the inequality

\|y(t)-y_{1}(t)\|\leq e^{C|t-a|}\|y_{0}-y_{1}\|+2e^{C|t-a|}\int_{a}^{t}e^{-C|s-a|}\phi(s)\,ds.

This gives a version of the Gronwall inequality for differential
equations that do not satisfy a Lipschitz condition in terms of the
Lipschitz constant of a “nearby” differential equation that does
(where near is “nearby” is measured by the size of
\phi
).∎

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
x\colon[a,b]\to\mathbf{X}
be continuously differentiable.
Then the map
t\mapsto\|x(t)\|
is Lipschitz and
therefore absolutely continuous. Thus it is differentiable
almost everywhere, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus applies,
and it is easily checked that
\dfrac{d}{dt}\|x^{\prime}(t)\|\leq\|x^{\prime}(t)\|
.
Whence, setting
x(t)=y(t)-y(t)
, using the
hypothesis (
2.4
), and (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\|y(t)-z(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|y^{\prime}(t)-z^{\prime}(t)\|

\displaystyle=\|f(t,y(t))-g(t,z(t))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f(t,y(t))-g(t,y(t))\|+\|g(t,y(t))-g(t,z(t))\|

\displaystyle\leq\phi(t)+C\|y(t)-z(t)\|.

That is

\dfrac{d}{dt}\|y(t)-z(t)\|-C\|y(t)-z(t)\|\leq\phi(t).

Multiplication by the integrating factor
e^{-Ct}
yields

\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-Ct}\|y(t)-z(t)\|\right)\leq e^{-Ct}\phi(t).

Integrate this from
a
to
t
to get

e^{-Ct}\|y(t)-z(t)\|-\|y_{0}-z_{0}\|\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-Cs}\phi(s)\,ds.

This is equivalent to (
2.5
).
∎

The following is the standard form of the Gronwall inequality.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a Banach space and
U\subset\mathbf{X}
an open convex set in

\mathbf{X}
. Let
f\colon[a,b]\times U\to\mathbf{X}
be a continuous function and
let
y,z\colon[a,b]\to U
satisfy the initial value problems

(2.7)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=f(t,y(t)),\quad y(a)=y_{0},

(2.8)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=f(t,z(t)),\quad z(a)=z_{0}.

Also assume there is a constant
C\geq 0
so that

(2.9)

\|f(t,x_{2})-f(t,x_{1})\|\leq C\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|.

Then for
t\in[a,b]

(2.10)

\|y(t)-z(t)\|\leq e^{C|t-a|}\|y_{0}-z_{0}\|.

Proof.

In Theorem
2.1
let
f=g
. Then we can take

\phi(t)\equiv 0
in (
2.4
). Then (
2.5
) reduces
to (
2.10
).
∎

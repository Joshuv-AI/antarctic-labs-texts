Magnon-mediated superconductivity on ferromagnetic wallpaper fermions
Koki Mizuno, Ai Yamakage

Abstract
We study two-dimensional superconductivity mediated by magnetic fluctuations at the interface between a ferromagnetic insulator and the nonsymmorphic topological crystalline insulator with a fourfold-degenerate Dirac point, wallpaper fermion. We demonstrate that BCS pairing with zero center-of-mass momentum induces chiral p p -wave superconductivity, and the Amperean pairing with center-of-mass momentum 2 ​ k F 2k_{\rm F} can give rise to a parity-mixed superconducting state. We find that the Amperean pairing exhibits a mixture of s s -wave and p p -wave components due to the multiband nature of the wallpaper fermion and the easy-axis anisotropy of the ferromagnetic insulator. Additionally, we find that the stability of the superconducting state with BCS and the Amperean pairing is governed by the easy-axis anisotropy.

BCS pairing

The superconducting transition temperature is determined by the linearized Eliashberg equation.
In this study, we adopt no retardation effect.

As a result, we can write the linearized Eliashberg equation for the BCS pairing with no center-of-mass momentum as

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m,m^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\prime}V^{m,m^{\prime};n,n^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\chi^{m,m^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}\Delta_{m,m^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\chi^{m,m^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-f(\xi_{m,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}})-f(\xi_{m^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}})}{\xi_{m,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}+\xi_{m^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}},

(45)

where

\displaystyle V_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}^{m,m^{\prime};n,n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{M_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha,n,m}M_{-\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{*\alpha,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}{\omega_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{M_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha,n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}M_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{*\alpha,m,n}}{\omega_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}.

III.1
Solution to the linearized Eliashberg equation in the isotropic limit

This section determines the symmetry of the superconducting state by solving the linearized Eliashberg equation (

As shown below, in the isotropic limit of
v_{s1}=0

p
-wave state is the most stable.

In the isotropic limit of

\displaystyle\varphi_{+}(\boldsymbol{k})

\displaystyle=\varphi_{-}(\boldsymbol{k})=\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}},

(47)

where

denotes the azimuth angle of
\boldsymbol{k}

The effective interaction reduces to

\displaystyle V_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}^{m,m^{\prime};n,n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{32}\ab(1-\frac{s^{2}\tilde{J}^{2}}{\mu^{2}})\frac{\tilde{V}^{2}}{\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}e^{i\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}e^{-i\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\ab(1+mm^{\prime}nn^{\prime})Y_{mn,m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{*},

(48)

\displaystyle Y_{mn,m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\ab(e^{i\phi}-mne^{-i\phi})\ab(e^{-i\phi}-m^{\prime}n^{\prime}e^{i\phi}),

(49)

\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{m,m^{\prime}}\equiv\chi_{k}

The angular-momentum

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}^{l}(k)e^{il\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}.

(50)

The Fermi statistics lead to

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}^{l}(k)=(-1)^{l+1}\Delta_{n^{\prime},n}^{l}(k).

(51)

The Eliashberg equation is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}^{l}(k)

\displaystyle=-A\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\delta_{1,mm^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}Y_{mn,m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}k^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}\chi_{k^{\prime}}}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}\Delta_{m,m^{\prime}}^{l}(k^{\prime}).

(52)

\displaystyle A=\frac{s}{8}\ab(1-\frac{s^{2}\tilde{J}^{2}}{\mu^{2}})>0,

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}=\int\frac{d\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-i(l+1)(\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}})}}{\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}}.

(54)

The magnon dispersion relation for the long-wavelength limit is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=2sK+\frac{sJ_{1}}{2}|\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2sK+\frac{sJ_{1}}{2}\ab[k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}-2kk^{\prime}\cos(\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k'}})],

(55)

.
Thus we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}=\int\frac{d(\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}})}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-i(l+1)(\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}})}}{\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}.

(56)

Since
\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}
is an even function of
\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d(\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}})}{\pi}\frac{\cos\ab((l+1)(\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}))}{\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}.

(57)

The above integral takes a positive value for any
l
, because
\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k^{\prime}}}^{-1}
is a monotonically decreasing function in
\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varphi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\in[0,\pi]
.

For an even
l
,
\Delta_{11}^{l}(k)=\Delta_{22}^{l}(k)=0
, due to the Fermi statistics Eq. (
51
).
For the off-diagonal component,
\Delta_{12}^{l}(k)=-\Delta_{21}^{l}(k)
, the Eliashberg equation is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{12}^{l}(k)=4A\cos 2\phi\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}k^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}\chi_{k^{\prime}}}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}\Delta_{12}^{l}(k^{\prime}).

(58)

All the coefficients in the above expression are positive for
\cos 2\phi>0
, in which a nontrivial solution could be obtained.
In the weak-coupling limit, the Eliashberg equation is simplified on the Fermi surface as

\displaystyle 1=4A\cos 2\phi\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}\chi(T_{\mathrm{c}})}{\omega_{l}(k_{\mathrm{F}},k_{\mathrm{F}})},

(59)

and the transition temperature
T_{\mathrm{c}}
is obtained from

\displaystyle\chi(T_{\mathrm{c}})\simeq D(\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}})\int_{-\epsilon_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\epsilon_{\mathrm{c}}}d\xi_{k^{\prime}}\chi_{k^{\prime}}\simeq D(\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}})\ln\frac{1.14\epsilon_{\mathrm{c}}}{T_{\mathrm{c}}},

(60)

where
D(\epsilon_{\mathrm{F}})
denotes the density of states on the Fermi energy.

On the other hand, for an odd
l
,
\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}^{l}(k)=\Delta_{n^{\prime},n}^{l}(k)
, the Eliashberg equation is written as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{11}^{l}(k)\\
\Delta_{22}^{l}(k)\end{pmatrix}=-A\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}k^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}\chi_{{k^{\prime}}}}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}\Phi\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{11}^{l}(k^{\prime})\\
\Delta_{22}^{l}(k^{\prime})\end{pmatrix},

(61)

with the matrix

\displaystyle\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}2(1-\cos 2\phi)&2(1+\cos 2\phi)\\
2(1+\cos 2\phi)&2(1-\cos 2\phi)\end{pmatrix}.

(62)

The eigenvalues of
\Phi
are given by
4
and
-4\cos 2\phi
.
There could be a nontrivial solution for
\cos 2\phi>0
.
The off-diagonal component satisfies the following Eliashberg equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{12}^{l}(k)=-4A\int\frac{dk^{\prime}}{2\pi}k^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}\chi_{{k^{\prime}}}}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}\Delta_{12}^{l}(k^{\prime}),

(63)

which could have no nontrivial solution.

The positive eigenvalues of the Eliashberg equations of
\Delta_{1,2}^{l}
and
(\Delta_{1,1}^{l},\Delta_{2,2}^{l})
is given by

\displaystyle 4A\cos 2\phi\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}\chi(T_{\mathrm{c}})}{\omega_{l}(k_{\mathrm{F}},k_{\mathrm{F}})}.

(64)

Therefore, the pair potential is most stable for the smallest
\omega_{l}(k_{\mathrm{F}},k_{\mathrm{F}})
, the explicit expression of which is derived by the contour integral

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\mathrm{Re}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}\frac{z^{l+1}}{1-B(z+z^{-1})/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\frac{\alpha^{l+1}}{\sqrt{1-B^{2}}},

(65)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{B^{2}}-1},

(66)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=2sK+\frac{sJ_{1}}{2}\ab(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2}),

(67)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{sJ_{1}kk^{\prime}}{2sK+({sJ_{1}}/{2})\ab(k^{2}+k^{\prime 2})},

(68)

for
l\geq-1
.
Note that
|B|<1
and
|\alpha|<1
, hence
1/\omega_{l}(k,k^{\prime})
takes the maximum value for
l=-1
.
Consequently, the
l=-1
(chiral
p
-wave) solution is most stable when
\cos 2\phi>0
.

Amperean pairing

In this section, we consider the Amperean pairing, in which electrons with momenta

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{p})=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}U_{O,\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}}^{m,m^{\prime};n,n^{\prime}}\chi^{m,m^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}}\Delta_{m,m^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}),

(71)

\displaystyle U^{m,m^{\prime};n,n^{\prime}}_{O,\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}}=\frac{U^{m,m^{\prime};n,n^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}}-U^{m^{\prime},m;n,n^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{p},-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}}}{2},

IV.1
Isotropic limit

In the following, we clarify the behavior of the attraction in the isotropic limit and discuss the symmetry of potential solutions to the gap equation.
In the vicinity of the Fermi surface,
\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{p^{\prime}}\sim\boldsymbol{0}
, in the isotropic case,
v_{s1}=0
, the energy denominators are approximated as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}}\simeq-\ab(\frac{1}{2sK}+\frac{J_{1}}{4sK^{2}}\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}).

(75)

Here we assume
\omega_{+,\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\ll\omega_{-,\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}
.
The electron-magnon coupling constant is also approximated as
M_{\boldsymbol{K}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{p}}^{\beta,n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\simeq M_{\boldsymbol{K},\boldsymbol{0}}^{\beta,n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
.
Therefore, we find

\displaystyle U^{m,m^{\prime};n,n^{\prime}}_{O,\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}}\simeq-\frac{\tilde{V}^{2}X_{\boldsymbol{K}}}{2}\times\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{J_{1}\cos^{2}\phi}{2sK^{2}}\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},&m=m^{\prime}=n=n^{\prime},\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{J_{1}\sin^{2}\phi}{2sK^{2}}\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime},&m=m^{\prime}=-n=-n^{\prime},\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\cos 2\phi}{2sK}+\frac{J_{1}\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}{4sK^{2}},&m=-m^{\prime}=n=-n^{\prime},\\[10.00002pt]
-\displaystyle\frac{\cos 2\phi}{2sK}+\frac{J_{1}\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}{4sK^{2}},&m=-m^{\prime}=-n=n^{\prime},\\[10.00002pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(76)

with

\displaystyle X_{\boldsymbol{K}}=\ab|v_{+}^{+}(\boldsymbol{K})u_{+}^{+}(\boldsymbol{K})v_{+}^{+}(\boldsymbol{K})v_{+}^{+}(\boldsymbol{K})|.

(77)

The detail of the derivations is shown in Appendix
C
.
In the former two cases,

and

On the other hand, in the latter two cases,

VI
Conclusion

We have investigated the two-dimensional superconductivity of wallpaper fermions mediated by ferromagnetic magnons at the interface between a nonsymmorphic TCI and FMI.
In the case of the isotropic band structure, superconducting instability toward the BCS pairing, which has no net momentum, is enhanced, leading to chiral
p
-wave superconductivity.
On the other hand, the Amperean pairing with finite center-of-mass momentum can emerge as a parity-mixed superconducting state.
In the isotropic limit, we demonstrated that the Amperean state exhibits the
p
-wave symmetry.
Furthermore, we discussed the effects of the band anisotropy of wallpaper fermion and the easy-axis anisotropy of FMI, which induce a solution by mixing
p
-wave and
s
-wave states.
Such mixing of superconducting symmetry is absent in the case of a single Dirac fermion on the surface of three-dimensional TIs.
By comparing the critical temperatures of the BCS and Amperean pairing states, we found that magnetization anisotropy plays a crucial role in determining the superconducting phase.
When the magnetization anisotropy is weak, BCS pairing is more stable than Amperean pairing, whereas the latter becomes stable for the strong anisotropy.
These results suggest that the type of superconducting pairing can be controlled by tuning external magnetic fields or the thickness of the FMI.

Appendix A
Derivation of effective action for fermions

In this Appendix, we derive the effective action for fermions by integrating the magnons.
The action for the wallpaper fermion is given by

\displaystyle S_{\rm WPF}

\displaystyle=\int d\tau\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}{\sum_{m}}\bar{\psi}_{m,\boldsymbol{k}}(\partial_{\tau}+\xi_{m,\boldsymbol{k}})\psi_{m,\boldsymbol{k}},

(80)

where
\psi_{m,\boldsymbol{k}}
and
\bar{\psi}_{m,\boldsymbol{k}}
are Grassmann numbers.
The action of magnons is written as

\displaystyle S_{\rm FMI}=\int d\tau\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}}{\sum_{\alpha}}\bar{a}_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}(\partial_{\tau}+\omega_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}})a_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}},

(81)

where
a_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}
is a c-number and the
\bar{a}_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}
is its conjugate.
The action for the electron-magnon interaction, which corresponds to Eq. (
27
), is written as

\displaystyle S_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=\int d\tau{\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q}}\sum_{m,n}\sum_{\alpha}}\ab(M^{\alpha,m,n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q}}a_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}+M^{*\alpha,n,m}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},-\boldsymbol{q}}\bar{a}_{\alpha,-\boldsymbol{q}})

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt\times\bar{\psi}_{m,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\psi_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}.

(82)

The path-integral representation of the partition function is given by

\displaystyle Z=\int\mathcal{D}[\bar{\psi}\psi]\mathcal{D}[\bar{a}a]\,e^{-S_{\rm WPF}-S_{\rm FMI}-S_{\rm int}}.

(83)

In the following, we discuss the effective action for fermions. The partition function is divided into the boson and fermion parts as

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=Z_{\rm FMI}Z_{\rm eff},

(84)

\displaystyle Z_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}[\bar{\psi}\psi]e^{-S_{\rm WPF}}\ab<e^{-S_{\rm int}}>_{\rm FMI},

(85)

where we define the partition function for magnons
Z_{\rm FMI}
as

\displaystyle Z_{\rm FMI}=\int\mathcal{D}[\bar{a}a]\,e^{-S_{\rm FMI}},

(86)

and the expectation value of
O
for magnons

\displaystyle\ab<O>_{\rm FMI}=\frac{1}{Z_{\rm FMI}}\int\mathcal{D}[\bar{a}a]\,e^{-S_{\rm FMI}}O.

(87)

The effective action,
\ab<e^{-S_{\rm int}}>_{\rm FMI}=e^{-S_{\rm eff}}
, is given as

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}=-\frac{1}{2}\langle S_{\rm int}^{2}\rangle_{\rm FMI}.

(88)

Note that the higher-order cumulants vanish for the Gaussian distribution and
\ab<S_{\rm int}>_{\rm FMI}=0
because of
\ab<a_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}>_{\rm FMI}=0
.
Therefore, we obtain the effective action

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{q}}\sum_{m,m^{\prime},n,n^{\prime}}}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}V_{{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{q}}}^{{n,n^{\prime};m,m^{\prime}}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\times\bar{\psi}_{m,\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau)\bar{\psi}_{m^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau^{\prime})\psi_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\psi_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\tau),

(89)

\displaystyle V^{{n,n^{\prime};m,m^{\prime}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}D_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})M^{\alpha,m,n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q}}M^{*\alpha,n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{q}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha}D_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)M^{\alpha,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},-\boldsymbol{q}}M^{*\alpha,n,m}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},-\boldsymbol{q}},

(90)

and we define the Fourier transformation as

\displaystyle\psi_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{l}e^{-i\epsilon_{l}\tau}\psi_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(i\epsilon_{l}),

(91)

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{l}e^{i\epsilon_{l}\tau}\bar{\psi}_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}(i\epsilon_{l}),

(92)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv-\langle a_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau)\bar{a}_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle_{\rm FMI}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{l}\frac{e^{-i\omega_{l}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}}{i\omega_{l}-\omega_{\alpha,\boldsymbol{q}}},

(93)

where
\beta
is the inverse temperature.

The effective action for the Amperean pairing,
has the form

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}^{\rm Amp}=\frac{1}{2}{\sum_{\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\sum_{m,m^{\prime},n,n^{\prime}}}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}U_{\boldsymbol{p}\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{K}}^{nn^{\prime},mm^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times\bar{\psi}_{m,\boldsymbol{K}+\boldsymbol{p}}(\tau)\bar{\psi}_{m^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}-\boldsymbol{p}}(\tau^{\prime}){\psi}_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{K}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime}){\psi}_{n,\boldsymbol{K}+\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}(\tau),

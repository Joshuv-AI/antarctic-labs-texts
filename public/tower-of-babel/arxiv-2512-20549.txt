Global attractors for the Signorini problem with pointwise damping
Jaime E. Muñoz Rivera, Maria Grazia Naso

Abstract
. The existence of global attractors is investigated for the Signorini problem with pointwise dissipation. It is shown that both the semilinear Signorini problem and the elastic obstacle problem with normal compliance exhibit exponential decay to zero and admit compact global attractors. To establish these results, the original problem is approximated by a hybrid PDE-ODE system, which allows for a rigorous analysis of well-posedness and the long-time behavior of its solutions.

In this paper, we propose a novel and nonstandard approach to the analysis of the Signorini problem by coupling the linear Timoshenko system with a dynamic boundary condition described by an ordinary differential equation. This leads to a hybrid PDE–ODE formulation, where the interaction is mediated by a coupling parameter
\epsilon
; see system (
3
) below.
We employ semigroup theory to establish the well-posedness of the problem and to prove the exponential stability of the associated system. We arrive at the contact problem with a normal compliance condition by introducing a Lipschitz perturbation of the original system. Finally, setting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
we get the Signorini problem.
This approach is made possible by the observability inequalities inherent to the Timoshenko model. We argue that this method is more effective than the standard penalty technique (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
16
,
22
]
and the references therein)
as it provides more accurate insights into the asymptotic behavior of the solution. In particular, we show that the boundary conditions have no influence on the long-time dynamics. This implies that the exponential decay result holds for arbitrary boundary conditions—unlike in
[
3
,
7
,
22
]
, where specific boundary conditions were essential to establish exponential stability.

The remainder of this manuscript is organized as follows. In Section

Section

In Section

Finally in Section

2.
Semigroup and General Results

In this section we establish the well-posedness of an abstract semilinear problem and demonstrate, under appropriate conditions, the existence of a global compact attractor.

To proceed, the following hypotheses must be formulated.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space with
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
its inner product and norm, respectively.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{F}
a local Lipschitz function on
\mathcal{H}
verifying

\mathcal{F}(0)=0\in\mathcal{H}.

(2.1)

We suppose that
for any ball
B_{R}=\{W\in\mathcal{H}:\;\;\|W\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq R\}
, there exists a function globally of Lipschitz
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}
such that

\mathcal{F}(U)=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(U),\quad\forall U\in B_{R}

(2.2)

and additionally, that there exists a positive constant
\kappa_{0}
such that

\int_{0}^{t}\big(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(U(s)),U(s)\big)_{\mathcal{H}}\;ds\leq\kappa_{0}\|U(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\qquad\forall U\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H})

(2.3)

Under these conditions we present

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions, exponentially stable, with infinitesimal generator
\mathbb{A}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function satisfying conditions (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
)
. Then, for any initial condition
U_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a unique global mild solution to the abstract Cauchy problem

U_{t}-\mathbb{A}U=\mathcal{F}(U),\quad U(0)=U_{0},

(2.4)

which decays exponentially to zero as
t\to\infty
. Moreover if
U_{0}\in D(\mathcal{A})
then the mild solution is the strong solution of (
2.4
).

Proof.

By hypotheses, there exist positive constants
c_{0}
and
\gamma
such that

\|T(t)\|\leq c_{0}e^{-\gamma t},

and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}
globally Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
K_{0}
verifying conditions (
2.2
) and (
2.3
). Let us suppose that
U_{0}\in D(\mathbb{A})
,
the final result will then follow by standard density arguments, since the dissipative nature of
\mathbb{A}
allows us to extend the conclusions to arbitrary initial data in the phase space
\mathcal{H}
.

It is well know that there exists only one global mild solution to

U_{t}^{R}-\mathbb{A}U^{R}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(U^{R}),\quad U^{R}(0)=U_{0}\in D(\mathbb{A}).

(2.5)

Since the phase space
\mathcal{H}
is reflexive, then we have that
U^{R}
is a strong solution to (
2.5
), see
[
24
, p. 189, Theorem 1.6]
. Hence
U^{R}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;D(\mathbb{A}))
.
Multiplying equation (
2.5
) by
U^{R}
we get that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|U^{R}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-(\mathbb{A}U^{R},U^{R})_{\mathcal{H}}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(U^{R}),U^{R})_{\mathcal{H}}.

Since the semigroup is contractive, its infinitesimal generator is dissipative, therefore

\|U^{R}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(U^{R}),U^{R})_{\mathcal{H}}\;dt.

Using (
2.3
) we get

\|U^{R}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq(1+k_{0})\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

(2.6)

Note that for
R>(1+k_{0})\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
, hypothesis (
2.2
) yields

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(V)=\mathcal{F}(V),\quad\text{for any $V$ with}\;\;\|V\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq R.

In particular
U^{R}\in B_{R}
so we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(U^{R}(t))=\mathcal{F}(U^{R}(t)).

This means that
U^{R}
is also solution of system (
2.4
) and because of the uniqueness we conclude that
U^{R}=U
. Hence
U
is the global solution of (
2.4
).

To show the exponential stability to system (
2.4
), it is enough to show the exponential decay to system (
2.5
). To do that, we use fixed points arguments.
Let us consider

\mathcal{T}(V)=T(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(V(s))\;ds.

and let us define the set

E_{\mu}=\left\{V\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathcal{H});\;\;t\mapsto e^{\mu t}\|V(s)\|\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Note that
\mathcal{T}
is invariant over
E_{\gamma-\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon
small and
\gamma-\varepsilon>0
. In fact, for any
V\in E_{\gamma-\varepsilon}
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(V)\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}e^{-\gamma t}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(V(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\;ds,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}e^{-\gamma t}+K_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\|V(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\;ds,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}e^{-\gamma t}+K_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\varepsilon s}\;ds\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\{e^{(\gamma-\varepsilon)s}\|V(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right\},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}e^{-\gamma t}+\frac{K_{0}C}{\varepsilon}e^{-(\gamma-\varepsilon)t}.

Hence
\mathcal{T}(V)\in E_{\gamma-\varepsilon}
.
Using standard arguments we can show that
\mathcal{T}^{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{n}(W_{1})-\mathcal{T}^{n}(W_{2})\|\leq\frac{(k_{1}t)^{n}}{n!}\|W_{1}-W_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.7)

Where
k_{1}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\mathcal{F}_{R}
. Therefore we have a unique fixed point satisfying

\mathcal{T}^{n}(U)=U=T(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(U(s))\;ds,

that is
U
is a solution of (
2.5
), and since
\mathcal{T}
is invariant over
E_{\gamma-\varepsilon}
, then the solution decays exponentially.

∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
as in Theorem
2.1
and
let
\mathcal{F}
satisfying conditions (
2.1
)–(
2.3
).
Let us denote by
\mathcal{F}_{1}(U)=\mathcal{F}(U)+F_{0}
with
F_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then there exists a global solution to

U_{t}-\mathbb{A}U=\mathcal{F}_{1}(U),\quad U(0)=U_{0},

(2.8)

verifying that for any
R>0
there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(R)
such that the solution of (
2.8
) satisfies

\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq c_{1}e^{-\mu t}R+c_{2}\|F_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}},\quad\forall t\geq t_{0}

(2.9)

for any
\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq R

Proof.

Let us denote by

\widetilde{E}_{\mu}=\left\{V\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathcal{H});\;\;\|V(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 2c_{0}e^{-\mu t}\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+c\|F_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right\}.

To do that, we use fixed points arguments.
Let us consider the operator
\mathcal{T}
given by

\mathcal{T}(V)=T(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{R}}(V(s))\;ds.

First note that

\mathcal{T}(0)=T(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F_{0}\;ds.

Since
T(t)=e^{\mathbb{A}t}
is exponentially stable, we have that

\|\mathcal{T}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq c_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+t\|F_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Analogously,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{2}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\left[T(s)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}T(s-\sigma)F_{0}\;d\sigma\right]\;ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t)U_{0}\;ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}T(s-\sigma)F_{0}\;d\sigma\;ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(t)U_{0}+tT(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}T(s-\sigma)F_{0}\;d\sigma\;ds

Since
T
is a contraction semigroup we have that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{2}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq c_{0}(1+t)e^{-\gamma t}\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|F_{0}\|.

Repeating the above procedure we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{3}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq c_{0}\left(1+t+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)e^{-\gamma t}\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{t^{3}}{3!}\|F_{0}\|.

In general we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{n}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq c_{0}\left(1+t+\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\cdots+\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)e^{-\gamma t}\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\|F_{0}\|.

Using (
2.7
) for
W_{1}=V
and
W_{2}=0
we arrive to

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{n}(V)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{n}(0)\|+\frac{(k_{1}t)^{n}}{n!}\|V\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{0}\left(1+t+\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\cdots+\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)e^{-\gamma t}\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\|F_{0}\|+\frac{(k_{1}t)^{n}}{n!}\|V\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Therefore for
n
large we have that
\mathcal{T}^{n}(V)\in E_{\mu}
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
S(t)=e^{{\mathbb{A}}t}
be a

C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions on Banach space. Then,
S(t)
is
exponentially stable if and only if

i\mathbb{R}\subset\varrho(\mathbb{A})\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{ess}(S(t))<0,

where
\omega_{ess}(S(t))
is the essential growth bound of the semigroup
S(t)
.

Proof.

Here we use
[

, Corollary 2.11, p. 258]
establishing that the type
\omega
of the semigroup

verifies

\omega=\max\{\omega_{ess},\omega_{\sigma}(\mathbb{A})\},

(2.22)

where
\omega_{\sigma}(\mathbb{A})
is the upper bound of the spectrum of
\mathbb{A}
. Moreover, for any
c>\omega_{ess}
, the set
\mathcal{I}_{c}:=\sigma(\mathbb{A})\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\;\mbox{Re}\lambda\geq c\}

Let us suppose that (

) is valid.
Since the essential type of the semigroup

If

\omega_{\sigma}(\mathbb{A})\leq\omega_{ess}

\omega_{\sigma}(\mathbb{A})>\omega_{ess}

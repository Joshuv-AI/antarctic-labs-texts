Product gales and Finite state dimension
Akhil S

Abstract
In this work, we introduce the notion of product gales, which is the modification of an s s -gale such that k k separate bets can be placed at each symbol. The product of the bets placed are taken into the capital function of the product-gale. We show that Hausdorff dimension can be characterised using product gales. A k k -bet finite-state gambler is one that can place k k separate bets at each symbol. We call the notion of finite-state dimension, characterized by product gales induced by k k -bet finite-state gamblers, as multi-bet finite-state dimension. Bourke, Hitchcock and Vinodchandran [ 1 ] gave an equivalent characterisation of finite state dimension by disjoint block entropy rates. We show that multi-bet finite state dimension can be characterised using sliding block entropy rates. Further, we show that multi-bet finite state dimension can also be charatcterised by disjoint block entropy rates. Hence we show that finite state dimension and multi-bet finite state dimension are the same notions, thereby giving a new characterisation of finite state dimension using k k -bet finite state s s -gales. We also provide a proof of equivalence between sliding and disjoint block entropy rates, providing an alternate, automata based proof of the result by Kozachinskiy, and Shen [ 7 ] .



1
Introduction

The notion of Hausdorff Dimension introduced by Felix Hausdorff
[
5
]
is a powerful tool in fractal geometry and geometric measure theory. Hausdorff introduced the notion of
s
-dimensional outer measures by generalising lebesgue outer measure for any value of
s\in[0,\infty)
. Using this notion, Hausdorff generalised the framework of dimension to non integer values.

Lutz
[
8
]
introduced the notion of
s
-gales, which are betting strategies that generalise martingales and gave a characterisation of Hausdorff dimension using
s
-gales.
Lutz
[
8
]
further
effectivised the notion of
s
-gales and introduced the notion of constructive Hausdorff dimension. Dai, Lathrop, Lutz, and
Mayordomo
[
3
]
carried forward this effectivisation to the level of finite-state automata.

Dai, Lathrop, Lutz, and
Mayordomo
[
3
]
introduced the notion of finite state dimension using the finite state analogues of
s
-gales. They show that finite state dimension is equivalent to compression rates by finite state automata. Hence they establish that finite state dimension quantifies the information density of a sequence as measured by finite state computation
[
3
]
. Finite state dimension of a sequence is a real number that lies between
0
and
1
. Sequences having finite state dimension
0
are those that are predictable by finite state automata.
Whereas, those with finite state dimension 1 are random to finite state automata.
The concept of finite state dimension also has connections to metric number theory. A sequence
X
drawn from an alphabet
\Sigma
is said to be normal if every block of length
\ell
occurs with asymptotic probability
|\Sigma|^{-\ell}
. Due to the result by Schnorr and Stimm
[
12
]
, a sequence
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
is normal if and only if finite state dimension of
X
is
1
.

Bourke, Hitchcock and Vinodchandran
[
1
]
further explored this connection between finite state dimension of a sequence and the distribution of the substrings within it.
For a fixed length
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the limiting (limit infimum) value of Shannon entropy of the distribution of aligned (non intersecting or disjoint)
\ell
-length blocks, over the finite prefixes of the sequence is considered. This value is normalised by dividing by
\ell
and the infimum of this value taken over all lengths
\ell
is called the disjoint block entropy rate of the sequence.
Bourke, Hitchcock and Vinodchandran
[
1
]
showed the equivalence between finite state dimension and disjoint block entropy rates, hence bridging the results by Ziv and Lempel
[
13
]
and Dai, Lathrop, Lutz, and
Mayordomo
[
3
]
.

S. S. Pillai
[
11
]
showed that the notion of normality remains unchanged weather the occurence of any blocks
w\in\Sigma^{\ell}
are counted in an aligned (disjoint) fashion, or in a non-aligned (sliding, overlapping) fashion.
Kozachinskiy and Shen
[
7
]
generalised the result by Pillai showing that the notions of sliding and disjoint block entropy rates are also equivalent, using information theoretic techniques.

In this work, we introduce the notion of product gales. A
k
-product
s
-gale is a product of
k
different
s
-gales. It is fundamentally different from the notion of
k
-account
s
-gales
[
3
]
as the product of the
k
-accounts are taken instead of the sum.
Therefore, a product gale need not necessarily be effectively an
s
-gale. We show that Hausdorff dimension can be characterised using product-gales, generalising the result by Lutz
[
8
]
.

We then introduce the notion of
k
-bet finite state gamblers, in which we allow the gambler to place
k
separate bets on each symbol in the sequence. The resultant
k
-product
s
-gale is obtained by multiplying together the
s
-gales induced by the
k
bets. We define a notion of multi-bet finite state dimension using
k
-bet
s
-gales. We show that this notion perfectly captures the process of betting on a sequence in sliding block fashion.
We show that multi-bet finite state dimension is equivalent to the sliding block entropy rates.

We then show that multi-bet finite state dimension is equivalent to disjoint block entropy rates.
Hence we obtain that the notion of multi-bet finite state dimension is equivalent to ordinary finite state dimension. Thereby, we obtain a new characterisation of finite state dimension. We also obtain that the notions of sliding and disjoint block entropy rates are equivalent, thereby giving a new automata based proof of Kozachinskiy and Shen
[
7
]
.

2.7
Block Entropy rates and Finite state Dimension

Bourke, Hitchcock and Vinodchandran
[
1
]
show the following disjoint block entropy characterisation of finite state dimension. We give an alternate automata based proof. It is the simplification of the proof given in Akhil, Nandakumar and Pulari
[

First we prove a lemma that gives a probability distribution

from the block entropy rate, which we use in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 1
.

For all
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
and
s^{\prime}>H^{disjoint}(X)
, there exists a block length
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and a probability distribution function
\mathbb{P}:\{0,1\}^{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]

k\in\mathbb{N}

-\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}P(w,X\restriction k\ell)\;\log(\mathbb{P}(w)))<\ell\;(s^{\prime}-d)

for some

.

Proof.

Since,
\inf\limits_{l}H_{l}(X)=s
, for any
s^{\prime}>s,\exists\;\ell\;\text{ s.t }H_{\ell}(X)<s^{\prime}
.

At that
\ell
, let
s^{\prime}-H_{\ell}(X)=3d
. So,
\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}H_{\ell}(X|k\ell)=s^{\prime}-3d
.

There exists an infinite number of
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
H_{\ell}(X|k\ell)<s^{\prime}-2d
.

For each such
k
we have,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}P(w,X\restriction k\ell)\;\log(P(w,X\restriction k\ell))<s^{\prime}-2d.

(1)

Since
0\leq P(w,X\restriction k\ell)\leq 1
is bounded, there exists a convergent subsequence, corresponding to indices
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\{\mathbb{P}(w)\}_{w\in\Sigma^{\ell}}
be the limit of the subsequence.

From (
1
) and continuity of entropy function, it follows that,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}\mathbb{P}(w)\log(\mathbb{P}(w))<s^{\prime}-2d.

(2)

For all
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
k_{\epsilon}
such that for all such
k^{\prime}>k_{\epsilon}
,

P(w,X\restriction k^{\prime}\ell)<\mathbb{P}(w)+\epsilon.

So for infinitely many
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}P(w,X\restriction k^{\prime}\ell)\;

\displaystyle\log(\mathbb{P}(w))<\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}(\mathbb{P}(w)+\epsilon)\;\log(1/\mathbb{P}(w))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}\mathbb{P}(w)\log(\mathbb{P}(w))+\frac{\epsilon}{\ell}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}\log(1/\mathbb{P}(w)).

Applying (
2
), for infinitely many
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,

-\frac{1}{\ell}.\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}P(w,X\restriction k^{\prime}\ell)\;\log(\mathbb{P}(w))<s^{\prime}-2d+\frac{\epsilon}{\ell}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}\log(1/\mathbb{P}(w)).

Given
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, let
\mathbb{P^{\prime}}(w):\Sigma^{\ell}\to\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
be a probability distribution such that forall
w\in\Sigma^{\ell}
,
|\log(\mathbb{P^{\prime}}(w))-\log(\mathbb{P}(w))|<\epsilon^{\prime}
.

Taking small enough
\epsilon
,
\epsilon^{\prime}
, for infinitely many
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,

-\frac{1}{\ell}.\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}P(w,X\restriction k^{\prime}\ell)\;\log(\mathbb{P^{\prime}}(w))<s^{\prime}-d.

∎

For any
s>H^{disjoint}(X)
, we use
\mathbb{P}(w)

Lemma 2
.

For all
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
,

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}(X)\leq H^{disjoint}(X)

Proof.

For any
s>H^{disjoint}(X)
, consider the probability distribution distribution
\mathbb{P}(w):\Sigma^{\ell}\to[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
obtained from Lemma
1
.

Consider the following finite state gambler,

G_{\mathbb{P}}=(Q,\delta,\beta,q_{0})
, whose components are :

•

Q=\Sigma^{<\ell}
.

•

For all
w\in Q
and
b\in\Sigma,

\delta(w,b)=\begin{cases}wb,&\text{if }|w|<\ell-1\\
\lambda,&\text{if }|w|=\ell-1\\
\end{cases}

•

The betting is done as to ensure that the cumulative bet
\beta(w)=\mathbb{P}(w)
for all
w\in\Sigma^{\ell}
.

\displaystyle\beta(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}(w.1)}{\mathbb{P}(w)}.

where for
v\in\Sigma^{<\ell}
,
\mathbb{P}(v)=\sum\limits_{w\in\Sigma^{\ell}:v\sqsubseteq w}\mathbb{P}(w)
.

•

q_{0}=\lambda
.

Consider the
s
-gale induced by

. If disjoint block

has appeared

d_{G_{\mathbb{P}}}^{s}(X\restriction k\ell)=\prod_{w_{i}\in\Sigma^{\ell}}(2^{s.\ell}*\mathbb{P}(w_{i}))^{n_{i}}

\displaystyle\log(d_{G_{\mathbb{P}}}^{s}(X\restriction k\ell))

\displaystyle=\sum_{w_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{l}}{n_{i}}\;(s\cdot\ell+\log(\mathbb{P}(w_{i})))

\displaystyle=k.\sum_{w_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{l}}\frac{n_{i}}{k}\;(s\cdot\ell+\log(\mathbb{P}(w_{i})))

\displaystyle=k.\sum_{w_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{l}}P(w_{i},X\restriction k\ell)\;(s\ell+\log(\mathbb{P}(w_{i})))

\displaystyle=k.\left(s\ell-\sum_{w_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{l}}P(w_{i},X\restriction k\ell)\;\log(1/{\mathbb{P}(w_{i})})\right).

from the win of an

Lemma 3
.

For every
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
,

H^{disjoint}(X)\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}(X).

Proof.

Let a finite state s-gale

corresponding to a gambler

. Given

, whose components are

•

Q^{\prime}=Q\times[L]

•

For all

and

\delta^{\prime}((q,n),b)=\begin{cases}(\delta(q,b),n+1)&\text{if }n<L-1\\
(\delta(q,b),0)&\text{if }n=L-1\\
\end{cases}

•

For all
(q,n)\in Q^{\prime}
,

\beta((q,n))=\beta(q).

•

It is easy to see that if the s-gale

s

succeeds on
X
as the same bets are placed by

Now for any

\displaystyle d_{G_{L}}^{(s)}(X\restriction{kL})=\prod_{q\in Q}\prod_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}\{{2^{s.L}\times\beta(q,x)}\}^{N(q,x)}

Taking logarithm,

\displaystyle\log(d_{G_{L}}^{(s)}(X\restriction{mL}))

\displaystyle=s.k.L+\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}{N(q,x)}\;\log(\beta(q,x))

\displaystyle=s.k.L+k\sum_{q\in Q}\frac{N(q)}{k}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}\frac{N(q,x)}{N(q)}\;\log(\beta(q,x))

\displaystyle=k\left(s.L+\sum_{q\in Q}P(q)\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}P(x|q)\;\log(\beta(q,x))\right).

d_{G_{L}}^{(s)}

\displaystyle-\sum_{q\in Q}P(q)\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}P(x|q)\;\log(\beta(q,x))<s.L.

\displaystyle-\sum_{q\in Q}P(q)\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}P(x|q)\;\log(P(x|q))<sL.

Using the following result from information theory

H(X)\leq H(X|S)+H(S)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}P(x)\;\log(P(x))

\displaystyle<sL+H(Q).

, the number of states in the original gambler

suceeds on it. The idea is that given a length
L\in\mathbb{N}
, we stretch
G
into
L
copies and construct a extended
k
-bet gambler
G_{L}
. We analyse
G_{L}
to show that
H^{sliding}_{L}(X)\leq s+c/L
for a constant
c
.

Lemma 9
.

For every
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
,

H^{disjoint}(X)\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}^{\mathrm{multi-bet}}(X).

Proof.

Let a finite state s-gale
d_{G}^{(s)}
corresponding to a
k
-bet gambler
G=(Q,\delta,\beta,q_{0})
succeed on a sequence
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
. Given
L\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the extended
k
-bet finite state gambler,

G_{L}=(Q^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime})
, whose components are

•

Q^{\prime}=Q\times[L]

•

For all
(q,n)\in Q^{\prime}
and
b\in\Sigma
,

\delta^{\prime}((q,n),b)=\begin{cases}(\delta(q,b),n+1)&\text{if }n<L-1\\
(\delta(q,b),0)&\text{if }n=L-1\\
\end{cases}

•

For all
(q,n)\in Q^{\prime}
and
b\in\Sigma
and
i\leq k
,

\beta^{\prime}_{i}((q,n))=\beta_{i}(q).

•

q_{0}^{\prime}=(q_{0},0)

It is easy to see that if the s-gale
d_{G}^{(s)}
succeeds on
X
, then the
s
-gale
d_{G_{L}}^{(s)}
succeeds on
X
.

Now for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle d_{G_{L}}^{(s)}(X\restriction{mL})=\prod_{q\in Q}\prod_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\{{2^{s.L}\times\beta_{i}(q,x)}\}^{N(q,x)}

where
N(q,x)
is the number of times
x
has occurred as the input when
G_{L}
was at state
(q,0)
.

\beta_{i}(q,x)=\prod_{j=0}^{L-1}x[j]\cdot\beta_{i+1}(\delta^{*}(q,x[0:i-1]))+(1-x[j])\cdot(1-\beta_{i+1}(\delta^{*}(q,x[0:i-1])))

is the
i^{th}
cumulative bet placed by
G_{L}
on
x
in that scenario.

Taking logarithm,

\displaystyle\log(d_{G_{L}}^{(s)}(X\restriction{mL}))

\displaystyle=s.k.m.L+\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}\sum_{i\in 1}^{k}{N(q,x)}\;\log(\beta_{i}(q,x))

\displaystyle=s.k.m.L+m\sum_{q\in Q}\frac{N(q)}{m}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}\sum_{i\in 1}^{k}\frac{N(q,x)}{N(q)}\;\log(\beta_{i}(q,x))

\displaystyle=m\left(s.k.L+\sum_{q\in Q}P(q)\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}\sum_{i\in 1}^{k}P(x|q)\;\log(\beta_{i}(q,x))\right).

d_{G_{L}}^{(s)}
succeeds on
X
only if for infinitely many
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in 1}^{k}\sum_{q\in Q}P(q)\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}P(x|q)\;\log(\beta_{i}(q,x))<s.k.L.

Using the properties of KL divergence
[
2
]
, we can argue that

\displaystyle-\sum_{q\in Q}P(q)\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}P(x|q)\;\log(P(x|q))<sL.

Using the following result from information theory
[
2
]
,
H(X)\leq H(X|S)+H(S)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{L}}P(x)\;\log(P(x))

\displaystyle<sL+H(Q).

where
H(Q)
is the entropy of occurrence of states which is atmost log(n), where
n=|Q|
, the number of states in the original gambler
G
.

Dividing by L, we have that for infinitely many
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle H_{L}(X\restriction{mL})\ <\;s+\frac{\log(n)}{L}.

Taking
\liminf
over k,

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}H_{L}(X\restriction{kL})\leq\;s+\frac{\log(n)}{L}.

(5)

Taking infimum over all
L
,

\displaystyle H^{disjoint}(X)=\inf_{L}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}H_{L}(X\restriction{kL})\leq s.

∎

Therefore we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For all
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
,

H^{disjoint}(X)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}^{\mathrm{multi-bet}}(X).

Proof.

From Lemma
9
, we have that
H^{disjoint}(X)\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}^{\mathrm{multi-bet}}(X)
. From Lemma
2
, we have
H^{disjoint}(X)\geq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}(X)
.

Since by definition,
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}(X)\geq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}^{\mathrm{multi-bet}}(X)
, it follows that,

H^{disjoint}(X)\geq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}^{\mathrm{multi-bet}}(X)
.
∎

Therefore, we have a new characterisation of finite-state dimension using
k
-bet gales.
The following theorem follows from Theorem
1
and Theorem
4
.

Theorem 5
.

For all
X\in\Sigma^{\infty}
,

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}(X)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{FS}}^{\mathrm{multi-bet}}(X).

Kozachinskiy, and Shen

showed that the sliding and disjoint formulations of block entropy rates are equivalent. Note that they use the terminology aligned(disjoint) and non-aligned(sliding) entropy. This result also from Theorem

Theorem 6

(Kozachinskiy, and Shen

.

For all

Summarising, we now have the following equivalences.

Theorem 7

For all

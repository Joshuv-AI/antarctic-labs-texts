Criminal liability for human trafficking in the context of the war in Ukraine: Challenges and impact
Authors: Ruslan Orlovskyi, Vasyl Kozak
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMartial law and travel restrictions for citizens aged 18 to 60 were introduced after Russia's actions against Ukraine, but exceptions to this rule quickly emerged due to the potential negative consequences for the economy, politics and humanitarian situation. Despite this, society still faces difficulties related to the restriction of fundamental human rights. The study is important because of the increase in criminal behaviour associated with illegal crossing of the Ukrainian border during the conflict. The effectiveness of measures to counter this phenomenon and the socio-economic consequences for Europe can be determined by comparing them to European standards. This comparative analysis will also help to develop recommendations for improving legislation and international cooperation in this area. The research methods used in this study include systemic and structural, formal legal, comparative legal, logical and semantic, dialectical, sociological, statistical, empirical, and analytical. The purpose of this study is to analyse criminal liability for illegal crossing of the State border of Ukraine in the context of the military situation and to identify ways to improve legislation and international cooperation to effectively counter this phenomenon.Keywords: Illegal transfer of persons, Illegal migration, State border of Ukraine, Crime, Criminal liability

Introduction

Since Ukraine gained independence, its geographical location has been actively used for illegal migration, smuggling and human trafficking. And today, when the state is partially limited in its ability to investigate even general criminal offences, law enforcement officers are facing a major challenge - the need to investigate tens of thousands of specific and difficult-to-prove war crimes and other criminal offences, including those that are subject to international standards.

Today, the nature of crimes related to the violation of the state border has undergone certain changes. This is due to both socio-economic changes in society and the focus of the state's policy on developing and expanding contacts with other countries [1]. The consequences of such transformations are undoubtedly positive but are often used by criminal elements (increased smuggling, drug trafficking, human and arms trafficking, illegal migration, etc.) People smuggling can be carried out both for entry and exit. In recent decades, Ukraine's geographical location has been actively used for illegal migration, migrant trafficking and human trafficking. At the same time, Ukraine plays an important role in curbing the flow of illegal migration from the East to Central and Western Europe. It should also be borne in mind that under martial law, men of military age try to illegally cross the border of Ukraine to get abroad and avoid military service.

Illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine (entry and exit) remains one of the most lucrative types of criminal activity, which, with the beginning of a full-scale military invasion, socioeconomic changes in society and the focus of the state policy on the development and expansion of diplomatic relations with other countries, has received a new impetus in criminal "progress". Another concern is the fact that as of 1 February 2023, the total number of Ukrainian citizens abroad is 7,989,027, of whom 5,841,619 are adults, while only 448,592 are registered with the consular authorities.

It is worth noting that such criminal offences are associated with multi-episode criminal activity (a significant amount of investigative and operative work), and the mechanism of illegal transportation of persons across the state border of Ukraine continues to remain one of the most difficult for timely detection and termination, especially since there is currently an unbalanced a hybrid model of operations of operational units with unclear content and criteria for the ratio of operative-investigative, criminal procedural, investigative activities and clandestine work as such; lack of interaction and coordination of law enforcement agencies, study and development of methods and tactics of investigation, analytical work, etc.

The latency of criminal offences is also affected by the structure of the criminal hierarchy, the knowledge of criminals of the peculiarities and shortcomings in the system of protection and crossing of the state border, in particular at certain sections of the border, checkpoints, etc., as well as the participation of security sector employees in corruption schemes. Only with the help of such levers of influence in criminal groups do individuals remain undetected for a long time.

Several scholars have studied the issue of illegal trafficking of persons across the border. M. Nazarko [2] studied the socio-economic, legal and financial aspects of illegal trafficking across the state border of Ukraine and developed proposals to counter this phenomenon. O. Kurilyak [3] studied the understanding of the impact of migration processes on the social, economic and political development of the country. Particular attention is paid to the analysis of the role of migration as an indicator of the population's reaction to changes in public life, as well as to the study of the negative consequences of illegal migration and the need to improve the state and legal influence on these processes. M. M. Serbin [4] analysed the forensic characteristics of the illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine. Particular attention is focused on understanding the methods of committing this crime and its forensic features. The study is aimed at optimising the process of detection and investigation of this type of crime, as well as at developing investigation methods, programmes and models aimed at combating this crime. L. B. Stupachenko and T. B. Nikolayenko [5]analysed the factors that lead to the illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine to develop effective measures to counter this phenomenon, which is included in criminal offences. I. V. Dogolych and O. I. Yushchyk [6] studied the causes and development of measures to counteract illegal border crossing in Ukraine, taking into account the impact of foreign policy factors such as war and martial law.

The purpose of this study is to analyse criminal liability for illegal crossing of the state border of Ukraine in the context of the military situation and to identify ways to improve legislation and international cooperation to effectively counter this phenomenon.

Materials and methods

The research methods are selected by purpose, objectives, object and subject. The methodology is based on the laws and categories of dialectics as a general scientific approach to the knowledge of social and legal phenomena in their mutual relationship, development and change, which was used to present the results obtained at the empirical and theoretical levels.

Systemic and structural: understanding of illegal trafficking in persons across the state border as a subject of criminological research, with its diversity of manifestations, deterministic nature and creation of scientifically based recommendations for the suppression of these offences. This method can be used to develop tactics for effective deterrence of these crimes and to provide evidence-based proposals for improving government initiatives aimed at stopping illegal immigration.

The formal legal approach to the study of illegal border crossing and the stay of foreign nationals in the territory includes an analysis of both the national and European regulatory framework governing relations in this area. This involves the study of regulations, international treaties and conventions relating to migration and the legal status of foreign nationals. At the same time, attention is also focused on aspects of criminal liability for the illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine, in particular, under Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine. Of particular importance are the aspects of detection, investigation and prosecution of such crimes in the context of military conflicts, where special circumstances and difficulties in ensuring law and order may occur.

The comparative legal method is based on the study of foreign and international experience in analysing the unauthorised movement of persons across the state border. This helps to identify the best methods and approaches that Ukraine can use to stop illegal immigration and related crimes. A systemic analysis focused on cooperation between the European Union and Ukraine helped to define irregular migration as a complex anti-social phenomenon and identify its consequences for Europe, including security threats, the refugee crisis and humanitarian issues.

Using the logical-semantic approach, the conceptual apparatus was deepened and new categories for use in law-making processes were substantiated. The use of the logical-semantic method to deepen the conceptual apparatus helps to avoid ambiguities and misunderstandings in communication. This method also provides a basis for developing effective tactics to combat illegal migration and improve legislation. It provides decision-makers with an accurate and comprehensive view of the ideas needed to create laws and regulations in this area. Statistical and sociological methods were used to collect data on migration processes, compare the results of legal practice and analyse empirical data related to the research topic.

The dialectical method allows us to consider the problem of illegal trafficking of persons across the state border as a complex system in which various contradictions and contradictions interact. This helps to identify key aspects of the problem and develop comprehensive recommendations for improving the legislative framework and international cooperation. By analysing the experience of Ukraine and Europe using the dialectical method, it is possible to identify both common trends and differences in approaches to the problem of illegal migration. This allows us to take into account the peculiarities of each country and find the best ways to solve the problem through interaction and exchange of experience. The application of the dialectical method also contributes to the development of flexible and adaptive strategies to counter irregular migration that can effectively take into account changing conditions and circumstances. Based on such analysis, recommendations can be developed to improve legislation and international cooperation mechanisms to increase the effectiveness of measures to counter trafficking in persons.

Results

The Russia's actions against Ukraine, which began on 24 February 2022, has necessitated the urgent full mobilisation of defence resources to repel the aggressor. The question arose as to whether it would be advisable to restrict the possibility of citizens of Ukraine who are subject to military service during mobilisation or who may be involved in public works under martial law.

The response of public authorities to the challenges was immediate: On February 24, 2022, martial law was introduced throughout Ukraine and full mobilisation was declared. Also, for the first time in the history of independent Ukraine, the order of the Administration of the State Border Guard Service of Ukraine set out in an unpublished letter, introduced a large-scale and unprecedented restriction of one of the fundamental constitutional human rights - the right of all men aged 18 to 60 to leave the territory of Ukraine freely.

At the same time, the absolute ban on men leaving Ukraine could not last. The economic, political, humanitarian and social problems faced by Ukraine as a result of the hostilities and threats to national security required immediate response and cooperation with foreign partners, including those outside the territory of Ukraine. In addition, the processes of Ukraine's integration into the international environment, cross-border cooperation and ties at various levels and in various spheres of society could not be stopped simultaneously without significant damage to the state. All of these processes required the possibility of travelling abroad for a certain category of men aged 18 to 60 and thus prompted the government to introduce exceptions to the general ban [3].

The first adjustments took place the very next day after the ban was announced - 25 February 2022 - and launched the process of continuously expanding the grounds for allowing men liable for military service to travel abroad. This process is still ongoing. At the same time, the National Agency, as the body authorised to implement the state policy on preventing corruption, notes that any restriction of a fundamental (natural) human right does not eliminate the need to exercise such a right, and therefore inevitably gives rise to a search in society for ways to circumvent the restriction, which are mostly implemented illegally and through corrupt practices.

The Law of Ukraine of 18 October 2018 No. 2599-VIII added Article 332 to the Criminal Code of Ukraine [7] regarding illegal crossing of the state border of Ukraine with the intent to harm the interests of the state or by a person who is prohibited from entering the territory of Ukraine, or by representatives of units of the armed forces or other law enforcement agencies of the aggressor state in any way outside checkpoints across the state border of Ukraine or at checkpoints across the state border of Ukraine without appropriate documents or on documents containing false information.

The most common way to illegally cross the state border of Ukraine at checkpoints is to cross the state border of Ukraine using forged documents. A person deliberately presents a fully or partially forged passport or other legally recognised document, which confirms citizenship, identifies the bearer, and gives the right to enter or leave Ukraine, to an authorised SBGS official to obtain permission to cross the state border of Ukraine. The theft or forgery of documents granting the right to cross the state border of Ukraine, with the subsequent provision of such documents for use by other persons during their transportation across the state border of Ukraine, should be qualified as a set of crimes under Articles 332 and 357 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine, as well as Articles 332 and 358 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine [5].

In cases of smuggling with the use of official position by an official, the qualification under Articles 332 and 364 of the CCU is excessive, as such actions are provided for in the disposition of part two of Article 332 of the CCU. In cases of illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine for human trafficking, the perpetrator's actions are qualified under the combination of Article 332 and the relevant part of Article 149 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine. In cases of illegal trafficking of persons to violate the procedure for transplantation of human organs or tissues established by law, the perpetrator's actions are qualified under the combination of Articles 332 and 143 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine.

In cases of smuggling committed in a manner that is dangerous to life and health, if the health of the person being smuggled was injured, the actions of the perpetrators should be qualified under the combination of part two of Article 332 and the relevant article of the Criminal Code, which provides for the intentional infliction of relevant harm to health or life. In the case of complicity in smuggling with the distribution of roles (organiser, instigator, aider and abettor), according to Article 29(2) of the CCU, all accomplices are liable for complicity in the crime committed by the perpetrator (co-perpetrators). In this case, the actions of the accomplices are qualified under the relevant part of Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine, but with a mandatory reference to the relevant part of Article 27 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine. The perpetrator is liable under the part of Article 332 of the CC of Ukraine that provides for liability for the crime committed by him/her, without reference to Article 27 of the CC of Ukraine.

In cases of illegal transfer for mercenary motives and subsequent financial transactions or agreements with the received funds, the perpetrator's actions are qualified under the aggregate of articles of part three of Article 332 and the relevant part of Article 209 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine. In cases where the illegal transfer is committed in a manner dangerous to life and health, combined with the illegal introduction of drugs into the body, etc., the perpetrator's actions should be qualified under the aggregate of part two of Article 332 and the relevant part of Article 314 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine [2].

The punishment for this offence is provided for in Article 332(1) of the Criminal Code of Ukraine - imprisonment for a term of three to five years. Article 332(2) of the Criminal Code of Ukraine provides for imprisonment for a term of five to seven years with disqualification to hold certain positions or engage in certain activities for up to three years. Article 332(3) of the Criminal Code of Ukraine provides for imprisonment for a term of seven to nine years with disqualification to hold certain positions or engage in certain activities for up to three years and confiscation of property.

Under Article 54 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine, a person convicted of a grave or especially grave crime and holding a military or special rank, rank, grade or qualification class may be deprived of that rank, grade or qualification class by a court order. This rule also applies to cases of committing an offence under paragraphs 2–3 of Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine.

The mechanism of implementation of criminal liability is effective only if the criminal law provision is efficient, which ultimately ensures the fulfilment of the objectives of the CC of Ukraine. The analysis of the text of the disposition of Article 332 of the CC of Ukraine indicates that the legislator uses wording that is not perceived equally by practice and complicates its implementation.

Interpretation of the title and disposition of Article 332 of the CC of Ukraine leads to the conclusion that part 1 of Article 332 of the CC describes one criminal offence, the objective side of which is manifested in the following alternative forms 1) illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine; 2) organisation of illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine; 3) management of illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine; 4) management of organisation of illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine; 5) assistance in illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine by advice, instructions, provision of means or removal of obstacles; 6) assistance in organisation of illegal transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine by advice, instructions, All available commentaries to the CC of Ukraine refer to forms 1, 2, 3 and 5.

In this regard, the following questions arise: how can one facilitate the organisation (the process, not the organiser) and management (also, apparently, the process, not the manager) of the illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine by giving advice, instructions, providing means or removing obstacles? Can a person be punished under Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine in connection with the provision of advice and instructions that were not used by the person being trafficked and the person trafficking across the state border of Ukraine?

These answers make it possible to develop recommendations to the legislator on improving the textual wording of the disposition of Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine, which in the new version will allow for the "smooth" operation of the mechanism of criminal liability for illegal transportation of persons across the state border of Ukraine, and, therefore, will provide proper legal support for combating illegal migration using criminal law [4].

Let's look at the meaning of the phrase "facilitating the organisation of illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine by giving advice or instructions" using a dictionary. Thus, facilitation means a positive influence on something; the creation of appropriate conditions for the implementation, execution, etc. of something; assistance in something; creation, arousal of a desire to act; and the indulgence of someone. In other words, assistance in the context of the legislative wording under study should be seen as active participation in someone's affairs to facilitate or assist other persons in the transfer of persons across the state border of Ukraine by giving advice, instructions, providing means or removing obstacles. Organisation is an action with the meaning of organise, organise and organise, to organise; a set of measures, the content of which is to coordinate the actions of individual elements of the system.

Ferrying is an action with the meaning of ferrying, i.e., to transport or transport across some water barrier, a difficult place; to transport or transport from one place to another (usually overcoming difficulties in doing so). Given the above, literally "facilitating the organisation of illegal transportation of persons across the state border of Ukraine by giving advice or instructions" should be considered as active participation in the implementation of a system of measures for the transportation or transfer of persons across the state border by giving advice or instructions. But is it possible to help implement a set of measures by giving advice or instructions? It appears that it is possible to help, to assist persons who implement a set of measures, but not the process of implementing a set of measures for the transfer. Therefore, there is a need to clarify the text of Article 332 of the CC of Ukraine.

It is also worth noting the low level of disclosure and completion of pre-trial investigations and submission to court of materials on criminal offences under Article 332 of the CC of Ukraine. This can be attributed to several organisational reasons, namely: insufficient knowledge of the forensic methodology for investigating such criminal offences; lack of a list of coordinated actions of various agencies, investigative and operational units at the initial stage of pre-trial investigation; poor provision of agencies, investigative and operational units with the necessary means, etc [6].

The results of the survey of investigators and operatives showed that the investigation of this type of criminal offence is hampered by imperfections in criminal law and criminal procedure legislation (71 %); low level of material and technical support (44 %); lack of methodological recommendations (32 %) and information and reference support (9 %). In addition, the majority of our respondents (65 %) believe that out of 100 crimes, only 10 % are known. Every fourth respondent believes that up to 25 % of crimes are known, 7 % of respondents said that up to 75 % of crimes are known, and 5.3 % of respondents believe that 95 % of crimes in this category are known, and 1.7 % were undecided when answering this question.

Based on the responses received, it can be assumed that trafficking in persons across the state border of Ukraine is characterised by a very high level of latency. Given the rising crime rate and the need to optimise pre-trial investigation of criminal offences, it is particularly urgent to develop several methods in the most important key areas, which is possible only if the forensic characteristics are studied in detail.

Identification of signs of illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine is an activity of law enforcement agencies that precedes the commencement of criminal proceedings and is aimed at establishing sufficient information on the possible preparation or commission of a criminal offence and preventing possible facts of illegal crossing of the state border by illegal migrants.

An important component of ensuring that operational units provide evidence in criminal proceedings on the fact of committing a criminal offence under Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine is the documentation of relevant factual data during the operational search operations and the execution by operational units of the investigator's or prosecutor's instructions to conduct covert investigative (research) actions. The main operational and investigative methods for detecting illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine are: establishing confidential cooperation; intelligence interrogation; and operational surveillance [8].

Documentation results in the creation of documents that, after appropriate assessment and verification by the investigator or prosecutor, can be used as evidence in criminal proceedings. The results of the documentation by operational units of the facts of illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine can be used both to provide evidence in a particular criminal proceeding and for other purposes established by the CPC of Ukraine and the Law of Ukraine "On Operational and Investigative Activities".

One of the significant circumstances affecting the effectiveness of detecting signs of illegal trafficking across the state border of Ukraine and the persons involved is the introduction of modern border technologies. It is worth noting that certain technical means of state border protection are used in border control (Spectr-K, Taran-76, Gambit-21, Gart 1P, etc.), by the Border Guard Service to cover land border areas (electrical alarm systems and systems, portable signalling systems and signalling devices), for visual and radio-technical coverage of the border (radar and searchlight stations, observation towers, radar and technical posts) [11].

It should be emphasised that the current model of state border protection requires the widespread use of fundamentally new technical means of protection, including mobile thermal imaging systems that make it possible to detect potential border violators. At the same time, operational search tools play a significant role in detecting the illegal trafficking of persons across the state border of Ukraine, which is aimed at obtaining information (factual data) on the organisation, planning and preparation for the commission of a criminal offence, direct border violations, as well as the activities of DPS of Ukraine officials who may be involved in illegal migration.

An analysis of the materials on the detention of suspects and illegal migrants shows a low level of use of operational search tools capabilities, as most detentions were made by border patrols without advance information about preparations for both administrative and criminal offences. In turn, this also indicates the lack of coordination between the border guard units of the State Border Guard Service of Ukraine and its operational and investigative departments.

Speaking about the principles of migration policy of the EU countries (27 countries), it should be noted that these countries strive to respect fundamental human rights and freedoms, develop a common policy, create an even burden on illegal migrants, and make maximum use of the latest technological advances to detect illegal migrants. Migrants pose many problems for the EU countries. First of all, illegal immigrants worsen the crime situation in European countries. "In January 2016, migrants committed hundreds of criminal offences in Germany. Secondly, there are many representatives of terrorist organisations and radical movements among illegal immigrants. According to Europol, between 2015 and 2020, migrants from the Middle East and North Africa committed 118 terrorist attacks in the EU, killing 382 people. The large number of migrants is a significant burden for the economies of European countries, as migrants have to be provided with shelter, food, clothing, medical care and much more."

"The lion's share of migrants come to Europe from Syria (over 40 %), Afghanistan (27 %), and Iraq (16 %)" "The large number of migrants is shaking the situation within different European countries (Denmark, Germany, France). In these countries, far-right and nationalist movements are growing, calling for a tough fight against migrants and coming into conflict with the authorities. This creates a civil confrontation that can escalate into local unrest and even revolution."

Significant progress in combating irregular migration in the European Union took place in the early 2000s. On 28 November 2002, the Council of Europe adopted a Framework Decision on raising criminal liability standards to punish the facilitation of illegal entry, transit and stay. On 25 November 2003, the Council of Europe adopted a Directive on the facilitation of the removal of illegal immigrants by air. On 25 November, the Council of Europe also adopted a framework decision on the expulsion of third-country nationals in an irregular situation from the European Union. In 2003, the European Union introduced a fingerprint database of illegal migrants. This means that one person could not be granted asylum in several EU countries at once [12].

An important tool in the fight against illegal migration in the EU is the policy of deportation, expulsion and readmission of illegal immigrants. Thus, in 2005–2007 alone, the EU signed readmission agreements with Sri Lanka, Hong Kong, Albania and several other countries. The EU has signed an agreement with China on the return to China of citizens who came to Europe as tourists and remained in its territory as illegal immigrants.

"In 2005, a new structure was launched in Europe - Frontex, the European External Border Protection Agency. The purpose of this body is to create an effective border control system that will prevent illegal immigrants from crossing the border outside of checkpoints. To limit illegal migration by sea (since the lion's share of migrants from Africa and the Middle East enter the European Union by sea), the European Commission has organised the work of the European Maritime Patrol System (Eurosur)."

In 2008, the European Union adopted a Directive on the return of illegal immigrants to their homeland. According to this document, "an illegal immigrant must be expelled from the EU if he or she has no legal grounds to stay in the EU if he or she has committed an illegal act, renounced refugee status or voluntarily agreed to return to his or her homeland. The document provides for the provision of medical care, the right to education for children, legal assistance, etc. for the period when the migrant is in a facility for the temporary stay of illegal immigrants. Forced deportation of illegal immigrants is excluded if there are circumstances in their home country that threaten the migrant's life, such as persecution by the regime."

"On 5 July 2018, the European Parliament adopted a regulation establishing the European Travel Authorisation and Information System. This system allows for quickly calculating the routes of movement of individuals through the analysis of biometric data (face photos, fingerprints, information on identity documents). European countries have adopted a package of bills called Smart Borders. This reform envisages the creation of a unified information system for entry and exit registration. It provides for a system of self-registration - all types of border control for individuals and electronic checkpoints - special equipment that allows for a thorough check of persons crossing the border, as well as their luggage and vehicles."

On 23 September 2020, the European Commission published the Migration and Asylum Pact. The new document "should eliminate contradictions between EU countries in migration policy and create effective mechanisms to combat illegal migrants. Among the main innovations of the document are mandatory screening of persons arriving in the EU (health and identity checks) and independent monitoring of the rights of illegal migrants" [12].

It is planned to create the European Border and Coast Guard Service, a body that will monitor the inviolability of the external borders of Europe. "The pact allows EU countries to make a choice regarding actions about illegal migrants - to accept a person and receive a reward from the EU, to deport a person at their own expense or to pay other states to accept a migrant. The EU countries agreed to help the countries from which most illegal immigrants enter the EU and to severely punish smugglers. European politicians believe that visas are an effective method of combating illegal migration. The visa system allows to screen out migrants and creates significant obstacles for illegal immigrants."

The EU countries pay great attention to border surveillance systems - various cameras (infrared, thermal), aviation and special vehicles for patrolling, and sensor systems. "The European Union is actively cooperating with North African countries, strengthening their border services to help them effectively fight illegal immigrants. The EU is also negotiating with various countries to make the latter a kind of detention centre for illegal immigrants, a kind of buffer zone. In 2016, the EU signed an important agreement on the readmission of migrants with Turkey. For example, in 2015, 862,000 migrants arrived in the EU through the borders between Greece and Turkey, and in 2019 (after the agreement was signed), only 75,000 migrants arrived." "In June 2021, the European Commission proposed to allocate another €3 billion in aid to Syrians in Turkey to prevent them from seeking a better life in Europe."

"To prevent illegal migration flows, the EU is implementing the Global Approach to Migration and Mobility (GAMM), and the process of developing the Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP) mission is underway. EU countries provide humanitarian assistance to refugees in the Balkans, Lebanon, Jordan and Turkey. The EU security forces conduct various special operations to save the lives of migrants trying to cross the sea and neutralise criminal groups involved in human trafficking. The EU countries are striving to make a fair redistribution of migrants to relieve the burden on individual states."

For a long time, the European Commission has been working on the "Migration and Asylum Pact", where "the main focus is on the definition of the status of arrivals. It is envisaged that all illegal immigrants detained at the borders of the European Union or on the territory of member states, rescued during search operations, will undergo identification, security and medical tests. Information about them will be entered into the Eurodac database. Recognised refugees will be granted the right to reside in the EU after three years of permanent residence. Criminals and people who pose a threat to national security will be screened out. No later than six months after registration, refugees must be employed, and children must exercise their right to education within two months of arrival » [13].

When analysing the EU's response to illegal migration, it is worth noting that, in our opinion, this policy has several significant flaws. Firstly, the EU countries have not reached an agreement on the uniform resettlement of refugees across all EU countries. Hungary and Poland categorically refuse to accept refugees. Secondly, the EU is in a vicious circle, as it seeks to combine contradictory things - to respect the fundamental rights of migrants and to fight against illegal immigrants.

Among European countries, Germany has received the largest number of migrants. The country is home to millions of refugees from different countries. "In 2020, the German police detained 900 migrant suspects in crimes. After the Second World War, German society sought to rehabilitate itself from the horrific crimes of the Nazis, so the German authorities' migration policy is characterised by tolerance towards migrants. Such tolerance, high living standards, a large number of jobs and other reasons encourage millions of migrants to seek refuge in Germany. When implementing its migration policy, the German government takes into account sociological and demographic data that indicate low birth rates and a high level of ageing in the country. This, in turn, leads to a reduction in the size of the workforce."

Therefore, the authorities are seeking to compensate for the shortage of labour with the help of migrants to avoid provoking an economic crisis. The Migration Act, adopted in 2005, allows self-employed migrants to stay in the country, but only if they contribute to the development of the country's economy and operate at their own expense. The government of Angela Merkel, who was in power in Germany from 2005 to 2021, made a significant contribution to improving the German migration system. It sought to integrate migrants into society, primarily through language courses and employment. As of 2021, an adult refugee in Germany receives €300–400 per month, supplemental payments for children aged 6–13 (€242), rental compensation, and health insurance [14].

Strict labour market regulations are one of the most effective methods of combating migrants. For example, particularly strict checks exist in the construction industry, when it comes to job registration. If an employer assists an illegal immigrant in finding a job, he or she may face a large fine or even imprisonment for up to three years. German authorities are improving border control. "In 2021, German law enforcement officers detained and placed in temporary camps 11213 foreigners who arrived in the country illegally, transiting from Belarus through Poland. The German authorities even had to tighten control over the border with Poland."

In 2022, changes to the German migration system took place. People who have been living in Germany for a long time will have the opportunity to acquire citizenship. Refugees who have been rejected for asylum but cannot be deported from the country will have a chance to obtain permanent residence. The requirements for non-EU citizens will be reduced. A significant number of refugees (more than 250,000) cannot be deported from the country due to lack of documents, danger in their home country, children, illness and other reasons. German criminal law provides for rather severe penalties for illegal immigrants and persons who facilitate illegal migration. An illegal immigrant who stays in the country without a permit may be imprisoned for up to one year. If an illegal immigrant is ordered by the authorities to leave the country and ignores such orders, he or she may face up to three years in prison and a substantial fine. If a person violates migration laws while being a member of a criminal organised group, he or she may face up to 10 years in prison. According to German law, carriers who have illegally brought an illegal immigrant to Germany are obliged to return the person to the country from which he or she came. If the carrier ignores this decision of the authorities, it can be fined from €1000 to €5000 for each illegal immigrant it transports into the country [15].

In describing France's migration policy, one should take into account the historical experience of the country, which had colonies in North Africa and Asia. Therefore, "among the modern citizens of the republic, there are many representatives of different nations who have relatives in other countries. France seeks to attract migrants who will help it overcome the labour force problem and make a significant contribution to the country's development. Preference is given to those who know the law and language, have no problems with the law, and have a good education." "French citizenship can be acquired by birth (one of the parents is a citizen), based on birth in the country, by naturalisation (residence in the country for five years)."

The French authorities believe that the migration flow to the country (and to the European Union as a whole) can be reduced by stabilising the situation in countries that are sources of migrants (Libya, Niger, Chad). To this end, it is proposed to provide financial and humanitarian assistance to such countries. The French are also setting up checkpoints on the country's Mediterranean coast to carefully screen migrants - letting in those who need help and shelter and returning those who only want to improve their lives and get a good job." "The government is increasing the number of border guards to control the country's borders more effectively. They are also actively fighting against smugglers and smugglers. The French authorities pay special attention to the migration of scientists and students. Students studying in the country are allowed to do business and set up enterprises. The country's migration system provides for quotas to accept migrant workers in areas where there is a shortage of labour (construction, trade, hospitality, etc.) [16].

The 2015 migration crisis in Europe and the growth of migration flows were one of the reasons for the UK's withdrawal from the European Union. In 2016, the UK adopted the Immigration Act, which provides for punishment for the employment and transportation of illegal immigrants. A person can be fined or imprisoned. However, the penalties vary from province to province, as the kingdom does not have a unified legal system. The country's legislation defines four ways to acquire illegal status: "regular visits to the country and violation of the conditions on which entry was granted (expired visa, criminal record), entry into the country illegally (forged documents, crossing the border outside the checkpoint), stay after receiving a refusal to grant asylum, birth to a family of illegal immigrants (the country does not grant citizenship at the place of birth)".

To get to the country, migrants are forced to cross the English Channel, often using small boats. Thus, "in 2021, 28526 people arrived in the country by small boats, while in 2018 their number was 299. This trend can be explained by the fact that due to the coronavirus pandemic, the kingdom has severely restricted air travel, so only the sea route remains for migrants. People from Iran, Iraq, and Eritrea are mainly arriving in the country." This can be explained by the fact that Iran has a harsh dictatorship and high corruption, and Iraq is constantly experiencing terrorist attacks. Illegal immigrants are also attracted by high salaries and many vacancies. It is worth noting that many people from India and other countries that were once British colonies arrived in Britain.

The country's authorities are introducing a point system for migrants, similar to the one in Australia and Canada, where a potential migrant can get points for language skills, education, work experience, qualifications, and others. "As of 2019, there are up to 1.2 million illegal immigrants in the UK, one of the highest numbers in Europe. To reduce the flow of illegal immigrants into the country, the government cooperates with other countries, in particular with France, which receives financial assistance from London to strengthen its northern borders, improve port control, and create detention centres for illegal immigrants. The UK and France updated an agreement to combat illegal migration across the English Channel. The number of patrols on the French coast is planned to increase to 350. On 14 November 2022, France and the United Kingdom signed an agreement to combat illegal migration across the English Channel for 2022–23. British officers will join their French counterparts in patrolling the coast and working in control centres. London will pay France more than €72 million annually to fight illegal immigrants. As part of these actions, they plan to purchase drones, cameras and special dogs » [17].

Thus, the EU countries seek to respect fundamental human rights and freedoms, to develop a common policy, and at the same time, they are trying to make migration controlled. A big problem for the EU countries is that they cannot agree on a common policy of countering migrants, in particular, they have not agreed on the uniform settlement of refugees across all EU countries. Migrants create many problems for the EU countries. First of all, illegal immigrants worsen the crime situation in European countries. Germany and France, as the main countries of destination, are trying to integrate migrants into society. Secondly, the European Union is in a vicious circle, as it seeks to combine contradictory things - to respect the fundamental rights of migrants and to fight against illegal immigrants. When analysing the response to illegal migration by different countries, it should be noted that no country has developed a perfect recipe for combating this phenomenon. Despite deportations, imprisonments, and the construction of walls, the number of migrants is growing [18].

Ukraine's cooperation with the EU is developing in three main areas: employment and social protection of migrants; development of border protection infrastructure; and prevention of illegal migration with EU funds.

The first step, after the signing of the Association Agreement with the EU, was the creation of a separate body dealing with migration issues - the State Migration Service. Analysts point out that Ukraine can develop joint projects with EU countries in the migration sphere and introduce circular migration. Ukraine can borrow from European country's plans for integrating migrants into society, and for recruiting migrants for labour market vacancies (skilled workers) [19].

Ukraine has taken many steps to improve its legislation, including the adoption of the Law on External Labour Migration and the State Migration Policy Strategy. At the same time, we believe that legislation needs to be reformed further. The executive authorities also need to be reformed, and migration statistics need to be improved. The issues of cooperation between Ukraine and the EU in the field of migration and labour markets can be found in Title III "Justice, Freedom and Security" of the Association Agreement between Ukraine and the European Union. Article 16 of the Agreement states that "the parties shall jointly manage migration flows and combat illegal migration. Ukraine and the EU have agreed to develop a preventive policy to combat illegal migration and to develop the fight against criminal groups involved in human trafficking and illegal trafficking of persons across the border. Ukraine and the EU have agreed to strengthen the security of documents so that illegal migrants cannot cross the border using fake ones."

To improve the migration climate, the government must make many changes. One of them is to eliminate the imbalance in the financial system. Today, significant funds are used to replenish the gold and foreign exchange reserves of the National Bank of Ukraine and to repay previous debts. In our opinion, it is necessary to rebuild the domestic industry. As a result of the Russian Federation's aggression, the lion's share of Ukraine's industry has been virtually destroyed. Therefore, it will have to be rebuilt from scratch and enterprises will have to be created that will be focused on cooperation with the European Union, the United States, Africa and Asia. Ukraine needs to create jobs with decent pay. Then foreigners will not look for work in Germany, France and other countries. The government must completely reform the tax system [20].

European analysts emphasise that the Ukrainian authorities must crack down on employers who hire illegal immigrants, and pay salaries in envelopes without providing any social package (holidays, sick leave, etc.) to employees. The Ukrainian government should create perfect border control, introduce severe penalties for illegal immigrants and those involved in organising illegal migration, develop a visa policy and the readmission system, and create an effective system for integrating illegal immigrants into Ukrainian society. In cooperation with European countries, Ukraine can develop effective preventive migration management. To do this, it is necessary to strengthen preventive measures of migration policy - to close the channels of illegal migration, to inform citizens about the dangers of illegal migration, to inform migrants about the risks of illegal arrival in the country (the threat of becoming a slave, being robbed or even killed).

Measures to prevent illegal migration can be found in the Strategy of State Migration Policy of Ukraine for the period up to 2025." This includes improving the legal framework for combating illegal migration and measures of legal liability for violations of Ukrainian migration legislation. The strategy also envisages "improving the system of state control over the entry and stay (residence) of foreign citizens and stateless persons in Ukraine". The document also refers to the implementation of the readmission procedure and the functioning of special institutions for the detention of foreigners.

Cooperation between Ukraine and the EU in combating illegal migration involves European countries funding a programme of integration of illegal immigrants into society, where migrants are allowed to learn the language, culture, traditions and legislation of Ukraine. Migration policy should take into account the provisions of the Global Compact on Migration, which was signed in 2018. Ukraine should also take care of its national interests and compare their compatibility with the international system. Ukraine needs to improve migration statistics and coordinate them with relevant European and international systems. The migration system needs detailed data on the age and gender of migrants. Ukraine needs to conduct a detailed analysis of the labour market and attract migrants based on this [21].

Thus, by signing the Association Agreement with the European Union, Ukraine has gained a good opportunity to reform its migration legislation and strengthen border protection (technical equipment, visa regime). But most importantly, Kyiv has access to information provided by the EU (migration channels, the actual number of migrants). Ukraine can improve its fight against illegal migration by cooperating with the EU in protecting documents and by cooperating with the security forces of Ukraine and the EU. Ukrainian customs officers, border guards, and migration officials can learn from their foreign counterparts, obtain valuable information from them, and conduct joint operations. Illegal migrants use high-quality forged documents, so the EU has much more opportunities to detect such fakes and take action. As one of the most effective methods of combating illegal migration, the Ukrainian authorities can introduce severe penalties for carriers and employers who assist illegal migrants. Discussion

Europe has faced direct security challenges since 2014, when the annexation of Crimea and the conflict in eastern Ukraine began. After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the situation worsened as many European countries carried out large-scale rearmament and increased their military spending.

Information security in Europe has been threatened by a growing number of cyberattacks and Russian disinformation campaigns. Millions of Ukrainians have been forced to flee their country since the beginning of the conflict, mostly to Europe. In addition to increasing the burden on the social structures of host countries, this has contributed to a stronger sense of unity and support among the peoples of Europe.

The integration of refugees into host societies, including their access to the labour market, healthcare, education and other amenities, is a long-term challenge. The European Union is actively involved in providing financial, medical and logistical support, as well as humanitarian assistance to the victims of the conflict in Ukraine. The reconstruction of the affected areas of Ukraine is part of a long-term plan, and it will cost a lot of money and foreign aid.

In terms of political and economic support, the EU is Ukraine's main ally. This is especially true in terms of financial assistance, sanctions against Russia and support for change in Ukraine. Through the Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area and the Association Agreement, the EU further encourages Ukraine's entry into the European market. Cooperation in the energy sector is becoming increasingly important, especially in light of the need to find substitutes for Russian energy. The EU and Ukraine are working together to develop renewable energy sources and to expand the supply of Ukrainian electricity and natural gas to the EU [22].

The EU is actively assisting Ukraine in implementing reforms aimed at strengthening the rule of law, fighting corruption and improving the business environment. Supporting decentralisation, judicial reform and other important areas is part of this process. EU-Ukraine relations are now largely dependent on military assistance and security cooperation. In addition to providing military support, this also includes intelligence sharing, joint exercises and initiatives aimed at strengthening Ukraine's defence capabilities. Ukraine has maintained its aspiration to join the European Union, and the EU has recognised Ukraine as a candidate for membership. This provides a long-term perspective for Ukraine's further assimilation into European structures.

In light of these obstacles, it is crucial to assess how effectively European legal and law enforcement systems are working to prevent people smuggling. These difficulties cover a range of issues, including social, political, economic and security-related. This issue becomes even more important when considering how the conflict in Ukraine has affected international cooperation.

The European Union and its member states have taken several legislative measures to combat human trafficking. These measures include policies and guidelines that define the consequences for transporters and others involved in illegal immigration, as well as strategies to protect migrants' rights.

In reality, criminal networks involved in the smuggling of people are identified and prosecuted mainly by EU law enforcement agencies such as Frontex and Europol. This includes training, joint activities and intelligence sharing. Finding a balance between combating human smuggling and protecting migrants' human rights, in particular the right to asylum, is a major challenge. In addition, corruption and shortcomings in the law enforcement systems of some countries often make these procedures less effective [23].

The war in Ukraine has resulted in a significant increase in the number of refugees and migrants travelling to European countries. This has put additional pressure on existing reception and integration mechanisms. The war has also contributed to the strengthening of international cooperation in the fight against illegal migration and human trafficking. This includes enhanced information exchange between countries, coordination of humanitarian assistance, and support for Ukraine in its efforts to regularise migration flows. International organisations and the European Union have stepped up their resources to assist refugees, as well as to support countries hosting large numbers of people fleeing Ukraine. Several joint initiatives and programmes have been launched to support countries hosting large numbers of refugees and to strengthen borders to combat smuggling. These programmes include financial, technical and operational support.

However, these initiatives have faced several obstacles, one of which is the need to reconcile border security with the protection of migrants' rights. The conflict in Ukraine has also drawn attention to the ease with which criminal networks can exploit migration routes, highlighting the need for greater international cooperation and law enforcement efforts. Overall, the conflict in Ukraine has not only made the fight against migrant smuggling to Europe more difficult but has also strengthened international cooperation and led to new strategies to address the problem. But to succeed in this area in the long term, security, human rights and immigrant integration must be balanced.

Improvements in the legal framework and international cooperation can greatly enhance the effectiveness of measures aimed at combating human smuggling. Strengthening information exchange, joint training and joint operations between law enforcement agencies of other countries on a bilateral and international basis. This category also includes the creation of specialised international working groups. Using the powers and resources of global institutions such as the UN, Europol and Interpol to coordinate actions to combat the smuggling of illegal migrants.

To combat illegal immigration, bilateral and international agreements have been reached on readmission, border control cooperation and intelligence sharing, among other things. It is also important to create and implement joint international programmes that help to strengthen the capacity of law enforcement agencies of the participating countries [24].

Conducting frequent trainings, webinars and seminars to share knowledge and best practices on how to stop human trafficking between countries. This may also involve the development of special training courses to train experts in this field. Actively introduce new technologies, such as biometric systems, video surveillance systems and drones, to strengthen border controls and increase the effectiveness of detecting and intercepting illegal immigration.

Developing and improving information systems to collect, store and analyse data on people crossing borders illegally and those facilitating the movement of migrants. Conclusions

Since the crisis in eastern Ukraine and the annexation of Crimea in 2014, Europe has faced security challenges. Russia's invasion in 2022 led to significant rearmament and increased military spending. Information security issues are compounded by the growing number of cyberattacks and the spread of false information. The large-scale migration caused by the conflict in Ukraine has increased pressure on social structures and promoted cooperation. The integration of refugees is a long-term challenge, and the EU is actively helping Ukraine find solutions to these issues. Striking a balance between security and human rights is essential in the fight against irregular migration, but success depends on cross-border cooperation and the creation of new strategies.

Improving the effectiveness of efforts to prevent people smuggling requires strengthening the legal environment and enhancing international cooperation. Strengthening cooperation between international law enforcement organisations through information exchange, joint training and joint operations is an important component. To effectively combat this phenomenon, it is also necessary to use the resources of international organisations, form specialised international working groups, reach agreements and create joint initiatives. The use of modern technologies, the development of information systems and frequent training to share best practices and experiences will also be important components. Additionally, the involvement of the public and civil society organisations in the process of combating irregular migration is an important step in this work. Establishing effective cooperation at the national and international levels will allow for successful measures to counter smuggling and ensure security in both the domestic and international contexts.

European Union member states are committed to protecting human rights and creating a coordinated migration strategy, but they face challenges in developing a coordinated approach to migration. The lack of a common refugee accommodation strategy across all EU member states is one of the main challenges. One of the many problems that migrants bring with them is the rise in crime in the countries where they settle, including France and Germany. The European Union finds itself in a difficult position, trying to protect the rights of immigrants and at the same time fight illegal immigration. There is no perfect answer to this question, and the number of migrants continues to grow despite numerous attempts to reduce it, including the construction of walls and deportations.

The objectives of the mechanism of criminal liability in Ukraine must be achieved effectively. Nevertheless, an analysis of Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine shows that certain wording complicates its application. For example, part 1 of Article 332 lists several offences, but the court practice does not treat them all equally. This calls into question the effectiveness of the mechanism for applying this article. Thus, proposals to improve the wording of Article 332 may help in its proper application and support the use of criminal prosecution to combat illegal immigration.

Pre-trial investigations of criminal offences under Article 332 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine face significant challenges in terms of detection and completion due to organisational issues. These include inadequate logistical support, lack of interagency coordination, and lack of knowledge of forensic examination techniques. The results of the survey show that these crimes are not widely publicised, which indicates that they occur quite late. To optimise the pre-trial investigation, it is necessary to create procedures for detecting and recording illegal migration, as well as to address organisational issues in the work of law enforcement agencies.

Unveiling the germination patterns of Alternaria porri (Ellis) by using regression analysis and hydrothermal time modeling
Authors: Ali Yar, Rehman Ullah, Muhammad Nauman Khan, Majid Iqbal, Sezai Ercisli, Alevcan Kaplan, Diriba Dereje Olana, Muhammad Ammar Javed, ELsiddig Idriss Mohamed, Souad Baowidan, Steve Harakeh, Mohammed Moulay
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Purple blotch disease is a major fungal disease of Allium cepa L. plants which is caused by the fungus Alternaria porri. The best conditions for the growth of Alternaria porri are temperatures between 22 °C and 25 °C and relatively high humidity. The Hydrotime, Thermal Time, and Hydrothermal Time models were used to measure different parameters of seed germination; therefore, we used them to measure the interactive effects of temperature and water potential on the germination conidia of Alternaria porri. The laboratory experiments were carried out at five constant temperatures, between 5 and 30 °C, and five different water potentials between 0 MPa and − 6 MPa. The germination of Alternaria porri conidia was highest at 25 °C and 0 MPa and lowest at 5 °C and − 6 MPa. The percentage of conidia germination decreased rapidly after 25 oC. Conidia germination was also affected by different water potentials, decreasing at lower water potential. Models based on HTT showed a reasonable fit to the germination and growth rate datasets. The best fitting model for conidia germination (R2 = 0.98491) was based on variable base and maximum temperature as a function of water potential. Based on the TT, HT, and models, the highest and lowest values for θT1 were observed at -6.0 MPa at 30 °C, and 0 MPa at 5 °C and the highest and lowest θT2 values were recorded at -6.0 MPa at 5 °C and 0 MPa at 30 °C while the lowest and highest θH values were recorded at -6.0 MPa at 5 °C and 0 MPa at 25 °C, respectively, for the HTT model, the predicted θHTT average value is 16.32 (MPa°Ch-1). Based on the statistical analysis, the cardinal hydrothermal time constant (θHTT) accurately explains the interactive effect of T and Ψ on the germination of Alternaria porri conidia under different environmental conditions.

Introduction

Time-to-event approaches have proven to be valuable tools for studying various phenomena and events by analyzing the time it takes for them to occur such as time to germination, time to emergence, or time to flowering1. Originally used in medicine for survival analysis, remission duration, and disease prognosis, these approaches have also found application agriculture, particularly in forecasting flowering time and emergence2–4. One such model is the hydrothermal time (HTT) model, which simultaneously examines the effects of temperature (T) and water potential (ψ) on germination4–7. The HTT model, a population-based threshold model, allows the investigation of seed germination response to changes in water potential (ψ) and temperature (T) in sub-optimal (from Tb to To) and supra-optimal (from To to Tc) areas8. Both water potential (ψ) and temperature (T) are the two important ecological aspects that influence seed propagation rates, propagation fractions, sprout emergence, and establishment9. Temperature and water potential were found to be the most important determinants of germination rate (GR), while other factors were not relevant for non-dormant seeds10. It can also lead to significant crop damage and loss of crop and food production11.

Soil temperature also plays an important role in seed germination and subsequent plant establishment when water availability is not restricted12. To determine appropriate planting dates, it is important to know the three cardinal temperatures of seed germination: the base temperature (Tb), the optimum temperature (To), and the ceiling temperature (Tc)13,14. The germination rate increases gradually increases from the base temperature, reaches a maximum at the optimum temperature To (a single T or temperature range), and decreases as the temperature exceeds To, either linearly or in a curve depending on the plant species, until the ceiling temperature Tc is reached, at which point germination is completely inhibited15.

In addition to temperature, water potential (ψ) plays an important role in seed germination and determines the success or failure of plant establishment in different environments16. Germination potential (GP) and germination rate (GR) are directly related to water potential (ψ). The hydro time model evaluates how water potential affects seed germination17,18. Changes in the water potential of the germination medium can alter the germination process. The hydrothermal time (HTT) model measures the germination time concept over T and in the sub-optimal range (between Tb-To) and with change and in the supra-optimal range (between Tb-Tc)10.

Onions are one of the top 15 most valuable vegetable and spice crops listed by the FAO, and are the second most valuable crop in terms of annual world production19,20. It is well known that onions originate from Iran, Pakistan and especially the mountainous regions in the north of these countries. In addition to being used as a salad and vegetable, onions are also used as a spice in many Asian countries. Onions are of great economic importance due to their medicinal and dietary value, which appeals to many sectors21. In addition to tomatoes and cabbage, global vegetable production is of great importance, with about 36 million tons of onions produced annually22. Considering this important aspect, it is important to know the pathogens in order to develop control strategies. In general, onion is affected by sixty-six diseases worldwide, including thirty-eight fungal diseases, ten bacterial, six nematode, three viral and one phytoplasmic disease. Purple blotch (Alternaria porri) is the most devastating of the fungal diseases and causes major losses in the onion crop23,24 causing 30–70% losses in onion production by infecting the leaves, flowers and stems of the onion and causing severe lesions25.

Wherein, Alternaria is a ubiquitous genus of microfungi that includes saprophytic, endophytic and pathogenic species25,26. Its presence has been found in a variety of substrates, including seeds, plants, agricultural products, animals, soil and the atmosphere. Alternaria species are known to be important plant pathogens and can cause major losses in a wide range of crops. Due to the significant negative health effects of Alternaria species on humans and the environment, accurate and rapid identification is of utmost importance. Alternaria is a genus of Ascomycota microfungi from the class Dothideomycetes25,27 and Alternaria porri belongs to the Pleosporaceae family. As already mentioned in part, the purple blotch disease of Alternaria porri thrives at high relative humidity and temperatures around 24 ◦C and causes significant yield losses of 50–100%, about eighty to ninety%. A porri also infects seeds and is a dangerous pathogen that threatens seed production28,29.

In this study, we used a new model, the HTT model, to measure the sporulation of the fungus Alternaria porri, which is considered a vital disease worldwide and causes severe damage to the economy of many countries, especially Pakistan. The colonies of Alternaria porri have different marginal shapes, ranging from regular to irregular, and have a velvety or cotton-like texture. The color of these colonies can range from pale to dark olive, with a greenish or brownish tinge30. Variability is a well-known phenomenon in the genus Alternaria and can manifest itself in the form of changes in spore shape and size, growth and sporulation, pathogenicity, etc31,32. Although much information on these aspects of the Alternaria pathogen on other hosts can be found in the literature, little information is known about the germination dynamics of purple blotch fungi. By applying the Hydrotime Thermal (HTT) model, this study develops a novel methodology that links physiological responses, temperature and osmotic fluctuations, and the germination patterns of Alternaria porri. The models developed to date are largely empirical and often mathematically complex, making them difficult to use for simulating processes that occur in variable environments33. To our knowledge, this study is the first attempt to model germination and growth processes for the species Alternaria porri using HTT concepts, with impressive results that will be of interest across industries. This creative approach lays the foundation for better insights into disease development, epidemiology and research into strategic control measures. The study investigates the conidial germination of Alternaria porri by employing the hydrothermal time model and regression analysis to evaluate how environmental factors, such as water potential, temperature, and time, affect the indices of conidial germination. The primary objectives were to: 1). Quantify germination conditions using the hydrothermal time model. 2). Predict germination patterns based on complex interaction of environmental factors with germination indices. This creative approach lays the foundation for better insights into disease development, epidemiology, and the exploration of strategic control measures.

Materials and methods

It is bordered by Mohmand and Khyber Agency to the west, Charsadda District to the north, Nowshera District to the east, and tribal areas connecting Kohat and Peshawar Districts to the southeast. Peshawar district has a semi-arid climate with very hot summers and mild winters34,35. On average, Peshawar receives 513 mm of rainfall per year. In summer, the average maximum temperature is over 40 °C, while the average minimum temperature is 25 °C. In winter, the average minimum temperature is 25 °C. In winter, the average minimum temperature is 4 °C and the maximum temperature is 18.35 °C. The relative humidity ranges from 46% in June to 76% in August and the while annual rainfall is 410 millimeters36,37.

Isolation and purification of the pathogen

A symptomatic leaf was surface sterilized with 1:1000 mercuric chlorides, and rinsed with sterile dH2O. Section (1 mm2) of the upper epidermis of the symptomatic leaf were placed on potato dextrose agar (PDA) plates, and incubated at 25 ± 1 °C for 72 h. The growing fungal culture was sequentially purified and the conidial suspension was examined by light and scanning electron microscopy according to the standard protocol of38,39.

Statistical analysis

The influence of thermal time (TT) and hydro time (HT), and their interactions, modeled by hydrothermal time (HTT), on the germination rates and germination indices of conidia was investigated were conducted using linear regression analysis. The basic statistical calculations and graphical representations of the germination parameters were performed using Excel. Parameters such as ψb (50), ψb, and R² were derived using linear probit regression analysis in Origin 9.1 (https://www.originlab.com/). Graphical representations showing the germination percentage as a function of temperature and water potential for the Thermal Time and Hydro Time models were created using Origin 9.1 software.

Results

Morphology

Alternaria porri forms a cottony, smooth deposit when cultured on PDA media, previously examined by52. The conidia size of Alternaria porri is between 100 μm and 300 μm, the diameter is 15 μm to 20 μm, the color is brown and the conidia are club-shaped, with one tip of the conidia enlarged and the second tip tapered. The conidia may be straight or curved, with 1 to 3 longitudinal septa and 7 to 9 transverse septa stated by53 as observed in Fig. 1.

Effect of fluctuating temperatures on conidial germination of Alternaria porri (Model 1)

The results of the present study on the influence of temperature on the germination of conidia in Alternaria porri reveal significant trends. Conidia were exposed to a range of treatment temperatures (5 °C, 10 °C, 20 °C, 25 °C, and 30 °C), and the resulting germination rates were analyzed. Remarkably, at suboptimal temperatures (Ts > To; 5 °C, 10 °C, and 20 °C), germination of conidia increased linearly with temperature, suggesting a positive correlation between temperature and germination efficiency. Conversely, at supra-optimal temperatures (Ts < To; 30 °C), conidia germination decreased sharply, indicating the inhibitory effect of higher temperatures on the germination process. Furthermore, the optimal temperature of 25 °C allowed for the highest conidia germination after 24 h of experimentation (Fig. 2d), while the lowest germination rates were observed at 5 °C after 2 h of experimentation (Fig. 2a), highlighting the temperature sensitivity of the germination behavior in this pathogen. These results provide valuable insights into the temperature-dependent germination dynamics of Alternaria porri and have potential implications for disease management strategies.

Effect of water potential on conidial germination of Alternaria porri (Model 2)

The results in Fig. 3a-e of this study demonstrate a clear and consistent trend regarding the influence of water potential on the sporulation of Alternaria porri. The more negative the water potential becomes, indicating a drier environment, the lower the sporulation of the fungus. In particular, at the lowest water potential tested (-6.0 MPa), a significant decrease in sporulation was observed compared to conditions with higher water potential. Figure 3a, b, c, d, e shows the percentage of conidia germination in the water potential treatment (0 MPa, -1.5 MPa, -3.0 MPa, -4.5 MPa, -6.0 MPa). The highest conidial germination occurs at 0 MPa after 24 h of the experiment (Fig. 3a) and decreases linearly with decreasing water potential and experiment duration; the lowest conidial germination occurs at the − 6.0 MPa after 2 h of the experiment, as shown in Fig. 3e.

Hydrothermal impact on the conidial germination of Alternaria porri

In the present study, the combined effect of water potential and temperature on the germination of Alternaria porri conidia was investigated and the results are shown in Fig. 4a-d. The results are depicted in Fig. 4a-d. It was found that the combined effect of water potential and temperature significantly affected the germination process. Horizontal analysis of each figure at constant water potential indicated a positive correlation between temperature and conidia germination at sub-optimal temperatures (Ts > To), whereas a negative relationship was observed at temperatures above the optimal (Ts < To). Vertical observation of the individual figures at constant temperature revealed a decrease in conidia germination the more negative the water potential became. Regression modeling

This research investigates the relationships between individual environmental factors-Thermal Time (TT) (temperature), Hydro Time (osmotic potential), temporal factors-and the combined effects of temperature, osmotic potential, and time (Hydrothermal Time model) on germination indices (Eqs. 1–10). In addition, linear regression analyzes were performed to predict conidial germination based on existing temperature, water potential, and time. Linear regression analysis was performed to analyze the dataset (Figs. 6 and 7), and two different models for HT and TT were evaluated based on their goodness of fit using the coefficient of determination (R2). The regression modeling demonstrated higher certainty (p < 0.001) when fitted for 25 oC at 24 h (R2 = 0.968) compared to non-significant (p > 0.05) R2 = 0.658 at the optimal temperature of 25 oC at 4 h considering the variable water potential (Fig. 6). Conversely, the regression modeling for the TT model exhibited the best fit at a 0 MPa water potential of 0 MPa and a highly significant (p < 0.001) R2 = 0.939 after 4 h but displayed a non-significant (p > 0.05) lowest fit (R2 = 0.163) at a water potential of -6.0 MPa after 2 h (Fig. 7).

Discussion

Numerous population-based threshold models have been developed to explore how environmental variables, such as temperature and moisture content affect seed germination. These models incorporate key physiological threshold parameters to gain insight into how plants respond to their specific environment. By accounting for factors such as temperature and moisture content, these models provide valuable perspectives on the germination patterns of seeds within a population and improve our understanding of how plants respond to different environmental conditions. The inclusion of significant physiological thresholds contributes to a deeper understanding of the complex relationships between environmental factors and seed germination. As a result, these population-based threshold models are a valuable contribution to the promotion of sustainable agricultural practices, conservation, and efficient management of ecosystems1. These population -based threshold models are useful in determining the effects of temperature and water on conidial germination of fungi54,55. Among these population-based threshold models, there are mathematical models such as the hydrotime model (HT), the thermal time model (TTsub and TTsupra), and the hydrothermal time model (HTT) that are very useful in evaluating the influence of non-biotic conditions on the germination of conidia10,45,47,56. These HT, TT, and HTT models were originally developed57–59 and10, respectively. These germination models are also very useful to determine various aspects of conidial germination of fungal species, as HTT modeling was used to quantify conidial and mycelial germination of Pyrenophora semeniperda33. To date, TT, HT, and HTT have been effectively used in numerous studies to quantify the time course of germination over a range of water potentials (ψ) and temperatures (T)18,60–62.

In the present study, a hydrothermal time model was used to determine the effects of temperature and water potential on the germination of Alternaria porri conidia and to determine growth at different temperatures and water potentials (Figs. 2, 3, and 4). The results of the current study show a direct correlation between germination in % and temperature in the sub-optimal temperature range (Ts > To), but an opposite effect at the supra-optimal temperatures (Ts < To) as given in Fig. 2. In the same study, the direct correlation between the germination of Alternaria porri conidia and the water potential was also determined, so that the germination rate of Alternaria porri decreases significantly with the reduction of the water potential from 0 MPa to -6.0 MPa via − 1.5 MPa, -3.0 MPa, and − 4.0 MPa (Fig. 3). Hou et al. (2019)54 reported that apple blotch disease caused by Trichothecium, Alternaria and Acremonium caused severe economic losses in China for years after the widespread use of fruit bags in production. The researchers in the study, conducted a purification and phylogenetic analysis of the samples and identified these species as Acremonium sclerotigenum, Acremonium mali, Sarocladium liquanensis, Sarocladium terricola, and Sarocladium mali. They found that temperature and humidity significantly affected the in vitro conidia germination of five Acremonium-like species, with conidia of five species germinating in open water between 15 and 35 °C; the optimum temperature of four of the species was around 25 °C, while the optimum temperature of the conidia of S. terricola was 30 °C. They documented that relative humidity (RH) increased the germination rate of the conidia. They also found that the incidence of infection increased in proportion to the RH. The results of this study are in agreement with the results presented in our study. Sanjel et al.63 conducted in vitro germination experiments with three Athelia rolfsii isolates isolated from peanuts; they used these isolates in a factorial design at five temperatures (T) between 17 and 40 °C, four matrix potentials (ψm), between − 0,12 and − 1,57 MPa, and two soil types (fine sand and loamy fine sand). However, they found that T between 22 and 34 °C promoted sclerotial germination (> 50%) when ψm was maintained between − 0.12 and − 0.53 MPa. HTT models were fitted to a range of T (22 to 34 °C) and ψm (-0.12 to -1.57 MPa) that explained 84% or more of the variation in sclerotial germination timing. They reported that the estimated base T ranged from 0 to 45 °C and the estimated base ψm ranged from − 2.96 to -1.52 MPa. They pointed out that their results suggest that the HTT modeling approach is a suitable tool for predicting the timing of sclerotial germination of A. rolfsii. The decrease in germination percentage at the supra-optimal temperature is due to the thermal destruction of the amino acid for the germination process64. The decrease in germination percentage at the supra-optimal temperature is due to the conidia being dehydrated from a fully moist form that was previously unable to complete its developmental process57. The hydrotime model was best fitted at supra-optimal temperatures (R2 = 0.98491) compared to the optimal temperature (R2 = 0.65841) considering the variable water potential. The fitted result is in some ways the opposite of that of56. In their work56, found that the predicted minimum water potential threshold for germination is 1.19 MPa when the HT model is used, but shifts to higher values at temperatures above or below 25 °C. They found that the hydrothermal time required for germination was 33 MPa °C- days in the sub-optimal temperature range and increased by 36% as the temperature increased from sub-optimal to super-optimal temperatures. The HTT model showed that Cb(50) increased by 0.09 MPa for each degree increase in temperature above To. They emphasized that the interaction of water potential and temperature could have affected the model parameters and eventually the performance of the germination models, and then changed the model parameters according to the changing environments. The lowest fit value (R2 = 0.16279) occurs at a lower water potential (-6.0 MPa). The same results were obtained by10. It can be argued that these differences between the studies are related to the physiological structure of the study material and that many ecological and environmental variables may be responsible for these differences.

Moreover, germination parameters are also significantly influenced by changes in temperature and water potential. The attributes that are directly related to the increase in temperature and water potential are AVG, MGR, GRI, GI, GE, FGP, CVG, and TGI. The values of these germination parameters increase with increasing temperature in the sub-optimal range (Ts > To; 5 °C, 10 °C, 20 °C) and increasing water potential in the positive direction, but show a negative correlation with temperature in the supra-optimal range (Ts < To; 30 oC). These parameters show the highest values at 25 °C and 0 MPa, whereas their lowest values are recorded at (5 °C) and − 6.0 MPa. The mean germination time (MGT), the time to 50% germination (T50%), and the mean germination time (TMG) show a negative relationship with increasing temperature (Ts > To and Ts < To) and increasing water potential. The values of these parameters decrease towards the positive side with increasing temperature in the sub-optimal range (Ts > To; 5 °C, 10 °C, 20 °C) and increasing water potential, but show a positive trend in the supra-optimal range (Ts < To; 30 oC). These parameters show the highest values at (5 °C) and − 6.0 MPa; the lowest values were measured at 25 °C and 0 MPa. The analogous results for T50%, MGT, GRI, GI, GE, FGP, CVG, and TGI, were observed for Triticum aestivum10, Cicer arietinum L45. , and for Hordeum vulgare L46. According to the literature review, there is no study investigating the effect of HTT systems on germination parameters in fungi. Thus, the present study sheds light on a very important group of parameters.

It was found that the hydrotime model can explain much of the variation in germination at specific temperatures65. The hydrotime study showed that the hydrotime required for germination increased at temperatures below and above 25–30 °C. The increased hydrotime constant at sub (Ts > To) and supra-optimal (Ts < To) temperature has been described in some previous studies65,66. Our hydrotime analysis also revealed that the ψb values increase with the increase of temperature in the sub-optimal temperature range (Ts > To) and rapidly decrease when the temperature reaches the optimal range at the supra-optimal temperatures (Ts < To) (Table 1). The prolongation of time to germination in the sub-optimal temperature range is associated with thermal inhibition; the results were obtained by15. Spore imbibition at lower temperatures is not only slower but also detrimental to cell barriers under such less-warm conditions as described by67. Sanjel et al.63 reported that in A. rolfsii, the combination of higher air temperature and lower soil moisture inhibited germination in loamy fine sand compared to fine sand, and a difference was observed at low (ψm) matric potential. It is important to note that the germination response was similar for individual ψm treatments at temperatures between 22 and 34 °C, which is consistent with previous studies68–70. The thermal time model illustrates variable temperatures at a certain water potential. With limited water supply, the prolonged base temperature and thermal duration would interrupt germination and could reduce the final germination percentage (FGP). Previous studies on certain plant species have shown that water availability could disrupt the predictable attributes in the TT model. These descriptions were determined by65,71. The Tb for the Pyrenophora semeniperda was below the freezing point as described by33. Our results for the TT model showed the same pattern, as the decrease in water potential causes an increase in base temperature, so Alternaria porri requires more thermal time to germinate at lower water potential (Tables 2 and 3). The results of our HTT model showed an increase in HTT values at the sub-optimal temperatures but a decrease at the supra-optimal temperatures, whereas HTT values decrease linearly as the water potential decreases further (Table 4). The identical effect of temperature and water potential on the HTT values was observed in watermelon by66. As a result, they concluded that the watermelon germination response for all T’s and ψ’s can be adequately described by the HTT model and can be used as a predictive tool in watermelon seed germination simulation models. The current study addresses the assumption that Alternaria porri can germinate, pollute, and propagate at ψ fit in those that allow spore budding. The ability of Alternaria porri to maintain dynamics at lower ψ values explains how it can cause damage even in the fast-germinating host plant (A. cepa) under changing soil moisture conditions in the soil and leads to a clearer understanding of its impact on disease outbreaks. Furthermore, the successful application of HTT modeling to Alternaria porri demonstrates that this methodology is a useful addition to the extensive modeling methods available to study germination and growth processes in other fungal species. Significantly, this is the first study to help decipher the pathosystem of this pathogen (Alternaria porri) of the onion pest, an economic crop, and it should be emphasized that it will form the basis for further studies. In addition, the established HTT method can be tested for fine-tuning fungicide application scheduling and seasonal management strategies for Alternaria porri.

Conclusion

In summary, germination rate and other germination indices (MGT, GRI, GI, MGR, GE, FGP, AVG, TMG, CVG, TGI, and T50%) are significantly affected by T, Ψ and the interactions between them. The HTT model has explored a descriptive physiological significance and characterizes the response of conidia germination of Alternaria porri at all Ts and Ψs. Based on the TT, HT and HTT models, the highest and lowest values for θT1 were observed at -6.0 MPa at 30 °C, and 0 MPa at 5 °C, and the highest and lowest θT2 values were observed at -6.0 MPa at 5 °C and 0 MPa at 30 °C were observed, while the lowest and highest θH values were − 6.0 MPa at 5 °C and 0 MPa at 25 °C, respectively, for the HTT model the predicted θHTT average value is 16.32 (MPa°Ch−1). The maximum θHTT and R2 values were measured at 25 °C and 30 °C, respectively. Furthermore, the hydrothermal time model consequently provides a complete quantitative explanation for conidia germination of Alternaria porri, but further studies are needed to develop a hydrothermal time model for the mycelial growth of Alternaria porri.

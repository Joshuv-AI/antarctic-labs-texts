Semantic Incompleteness of Liberman et al. (2020)’s Hilbert-style System for Term-modal Logic 𝐊 \mathbf{K} with Equality and Non-rigid Terms
Takahiro Sawasaki

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the semantic incompleteness of the Hilbert-style system for the minimal normal term-modal logic with equality and non-rigid terms that was proposed in Liberman et al. (2020) “Dynamic Term-modal Logics for First-order Epistemic Planning.” Term-modal logic is a family of first-order modal logics having term-modal operators indexed with terms in the first-order language. While some first-order formula is valid over the class of all frames in the Kripke semantics for the term-modal logic proposed there, it is not derivable in Liberman et al. (2020)’s Hilbert-style system. We show this fact by introducing a non-standard Kripke semantics which makes the meanings of constants and function symbols relative to the meanings of relation symbols combined with them.

It is worth noting here that, as the above first-order formula suggests, the semantic incompleteness of

Such a Hilbert-style system seems to have never been provided together with a detailed proof in the literature.

3
Semantic Incompleteness of the Hilbert-style System
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}

In this section, we prove the semantic incompleteness of
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
by showing that
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
is valid in the TML-semantics but not provable in
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
. As expected, there is no difficulty to show the former.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\langle{\mathtt{Var},\mathtt{Cn},\mathtt{Fn},\mathtt{Rel},\mathtt{t}}\rangle
be a signature,
x\in\mathtt{Var}
,
c\in\mathtt{Cn}
and
P\in\mathtt{Rel}
with
\mathtt{t}(P)

=

\langle{\mathtt{agt\_or\_obj}}\rangle
.
A formula
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
is valid in the TML-semantics.

Proof.

Suppose
M,w\models_{v}x=c
and
M,w\models_{v}P(x)
. Since
\llbracket x\rrbracket^{I,v}_{w}=\llbracket c\rrbracket^{I,v}_{w}
and
\llbracket x\rrbracket^{I,v}_{w}\in I(P,w)
, we have
\llbracket c\rrbracket^{I,v}_{w}\in I(P,w)
. Thus
M,w\models_{v}P(c)
.
∎

\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}

\varphi
at a world
w
in a non-standard model
N
under a valuation
v
is defined as follows.

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

iff

\displaystyle\langle{\llbracket t_{1}\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)},\dots,\llbracket t_{n}\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)}}\rangle\in J(P,w)\qquad\text{($P$ can be $=$)}

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}\neg\varphi

iff

\displaystyle N,w\not\models_{v}\varphi

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}\varphi\land\psi

iff

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad N,w\models_{v}\psi

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}\forall x\varphi

iff

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto d]}\varphi\quad\text{for all $d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(x)}$}

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}K_{t}\varphi

iff

\displaystyle N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\varphi\quad\text{for all $w^{\prime}\in W$ such that $\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}}$}

What we should pay attention here is the satisfactions of atomic formula
P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
and term-modal formula
K_{t}\varphi
. In the satisfaction of
P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
in non-standard model, the meaning
\llbracket t_{i}\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)}
of each
t_{i}
in
P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
is determined by the interpretation
J
, the valuation
v
, the world
w
and
the meaning
J(P,w)
of the relation symbol
P
combined with terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
. Thus, as explained in the following Example
1
, the meaning of a constant
c
occurring in
P(c)
could be different from that of
c
occurring in
Q(c)
.

Example 1
.

Let
lewis\in\mathtt{Cn}
with
\mathtt{t}(lewis)=\mathtt{agt}
and
SL,CF\in\mathtt{Rel}
with
\mathtt{t}(SL)=\mathtt{t}(CF)=\langle{\mathtt{agt}}\rangle
, and consider a non-standard model such that

\displaystyle J(SL,w)

\displaystyle=\{i\in D_{\mathtt{agt}}\,|\,\text{$i$ is one of the authors of {Symbolic Logic}}\},

\displaystyle J(CF,w)

\displaystyle=\left\{\,{i\in D_{\mathtt{agt}}}\,|\,{\text{$i$ is the author of {Counterfactuals}}}\,\right\},

J(lewis,w,J(SL,w))
is C. I. Lewis and
J(lewis,w,J(CF,w))
is D. Lewis. The meaning
J(lewis,w,J(SL,w))
of
lewis
occurring in
SL(lewis)
is then different from the meaning
J(lewis,w,J(CF,w))
of
lewis
occurring in
CF(lewis)
. Note that, although
J(lewis,w,J(SL,w))\in J(SL,w)
holds in the above non-standard model, we can technically have a non-standard model such that
J(lewis,w,J(SL,w))\notin J(SL,w)
holds by assigning D. Lewis to
J(lewis,w,J(SL,w))
.

On the other hand, because the meaning
\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}
of
t
in
K_{t}
is determined independently of the meaning of any relation symbol, the satisfaction of
K_{t}\varphi
in non-standard model is in effect the same as the satisfaction of
K_{t}\varphi
in model of the TML-semantics. By this fact we can validate axioms
\mathsf{K}
and
\mathsf{BF}
in this semantics.

The notion of validity is defined as in the TML-semantics. For ease of reference, henceforth we call this semantics
non-standard semantics
.

Now it is easy to see the invalidity of
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
in the non-standard semantics.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\langle{\mathtt{Var},\mathtt{Cn},\mathtt{Fn},\mathtt{Rel},\mathtt{t}}\rangle
be a signature,
x\in\mathtt{Var}
,
c\in\mathtt{Cn}
with
\mathtt{t}(x)=\mathtt{t}(c)
and
P\in\mathtt{Rel}
with
\mathtt{t}(P)

=

\langle{\mathtt{agt\_or\_obj}}\rangle
.
A formula
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
is not valid in the non-standard semantics.

Proof.

We may assume
\mathtt{t}(x)=\mathtt{t}(c)=\mathtt{agt}
without loss of generality.
Let
N=\langle{D,W,R,J}\rangle
be a non-standard model such that
w\in W
,
D_{\mathtt{agt}}

=

\{\,{\alpha,\beta}\,\}
,
J(c,w,\{\,\langle{d,d}\rangle\,\mid

d\in D_{\mathtt{agt\_or\_obj}}\,\})

=

\alpha
,
J(c,w,\{\,{\alpha}\,\})

=

\beta
and
J(P,w)

=

\{\,{\alpha}\,\}
. Let
v
be also a valuation such that
v(x)=\alpha
. Since

\llbracket x\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(=,w)}=v(x)=\alpha=J(c,w,\{\,\langle{d,d}\rangle\,\mid d\in D_{\mathtt{agt\_or\_obj}}\,\})=J(c,w,J(=,w))=\llbracket c\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(=,w)},

we have
N,w\models_{v}x=c
. It is also easy to see
N,w\models_{v}P(x)
. However, since

\llbracket c\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)}=J(c,w,J(P,w))=J(c,w,\{\,{\alpha}\,\})=\beta,

it fails that
N,w\models_{v}P(c)
. Therefore
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
is not valid in the non-standard semantics.
∎

On top of this, we can prove as below that
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
is sound with respect to the non-standard semantics.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\langle{\mathtt{Var},\mathtt{Cn},\mathtt{Fn},\mathtt{Rel},\mathtt{t}}\rangle
be a signature and
x,y\in\mathtt{Var}
with
\mathtt{t}(x)=\mathtt{t}(y)
. Let
N

=

\langle{D,W,R,J}\rangle
be also a non-standard model,
w
a world,
X
a subset of
D^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
v
a valuation. For all terms
t
,

\llbracket t(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X}\quad=\quad\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,X}.

Proof.

By induction on the length of terms.

•

For
t
being the variable
x
,
\llbracket x(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X}

=

v(y)

=

v[x\mapsto v(y)](x)

=

\llbracket x\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,X}
.

•

For
t
being a variable
z
distinct from
x
,
\llbracket z(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X}

=

v(z)

=

v[x\mapsto v(y)](z)

=

\llbracket z\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,X}
.

•

For
t
being a constant
c
,
\llbracket c(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X}

=

J(c,w,X)

=

\llbracket c\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,X}
.

•

For
t
being of the form
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
,

\displaystyle\llbracket f(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X}

\displaystyle=\llbracket f(t_{1}(y/x),\dots,t_{n}(y/x))\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X}

\displaystyle=J(f,w,X)(\llbracket t_{1}(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X},\dots,\llbracket t_{n}(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,X})

\displaystyle=J(f,w,X)(\llbracket t_{1}\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,X},\dots,\llbracket t_{n}\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,X})\quad\text{(inductive hypothesis)}

\displaystyle=\llbracket f(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,X}.

∎

Proposition 4
.

Let
\langle{\mathtt{Var},\mathtt{Cn},\mathtt{Fn},\mathtt{Rel},\mathtt{t}}\rangle
be a signature,
x,y\in\mathtt{Var}
with
\mathtt{t}(x)=\mathtt{t}(y)
and
N

=

\langle{D,W,R,J}\rangle
a non-standard model. For all worlds
w
, all valuations
v
and all formulas
\varphi
,

N,w\models_{v}\varphi(y/x)\quad\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)]}\varphi.

Proof.

By induction on the length of formulas. Since the proof of the cases for
\neg\psi
and
\psi\land\gamma
are straightforward, we see only the cases for
P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
,
\forall z\psi
and
K_{t}\psi
.

•

For
\varphi
being of the form
P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
,

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})(y/x)\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\langle{\llbracket t_{1}(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)},\dots,\llbracket t_{n}(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)}}\rangle\in J(P,w)

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad\langle{\llbracket t_{1}\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,J(P,w)},\dots,\llbracket t_{n}\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,J(P,w)}}\rangle\in J(P,w)\quad\text{(Proposition~\ref{prop:valuation})}

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)]}P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

•

For
\varphi
being of the form
\forall z\psi
, if
z=x
, then
N,w\models_{v}(\forall x\psi)(y/x)
iff
N,w\models_{v}\forall x\psi
iff
N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)]}\forall x\psi
. So suppose
z\neq x
. Then

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}(\forall z\psi)(y/x)\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v}\forall z\psi(y/x)

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v[z\mapsto d]}\psi(y/x)\quad\text{for all $d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(z)}$}

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v[z\mapsto d][x\mapsto v[z\mapsto d](y)]}\psi\quad\text{for all $d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(z)}$}\quad\text{(inductive hypothesis)}

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)][z\mapsto d]}\psi\quad\text{for all $d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(z)}$}\quad\text{($z\neq x$ and $z\neq y$)}

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)]}\forall z\psi

•

For
\varphi
being of the form
K_{t}\psi
,

\displaystyle N,w\models_{v}(K_{t}\psi)(y/x)\quad

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v}K_{t(y/x)}\psi(y/x)

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\psi(y/x)\quad\text{all $w^{\prime}\in W$ such that $\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t(y/x)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}}$}

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\psi(y/x)\quad\text{all $w^{\prime}\in W$ such that $\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,\emptyset}}$}

(Proposition
3
)

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w^{\prime}\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)]}\psi\quad\text{all $w^{\prime}\in W$ such that $\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto v(y)]}_{w,\emptyset}}$}

(inductive hypothesis)

\displaystyle\text{iff}\quad N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)]}K_{t}\psi.

∎

Theorem 1

(Soundness)
.

If
\varphi
is provable in
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
, then
\varphi
is valid in the non-standard semantics.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that all axioms are valid and that all inference rules preserve validity. Since the proof of the latter is done as usual, we see only the former.

•

For any propositional tautology, its validity is obvious since the non-standard semantics gives the ordinary satisfactions for
\neg
and
\land
.

•

For
\mathsf{UE}
, i.e.,
\forall x\varphi\to\varphi(y/x)
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}\forall x\varphi
. Then
N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto v(y)]}\varphi
. Thus by Proposition
4

N,w\models_{v}\varphi(y/x)
holds, as required.

•

For
\mathsf{Id}
, i.e.,
t=t
, its validity is obvious.

•

For
\mathsf{PS}
, i.e.,
x=y\to(\varphi(x/z)\to\varphi(y/z))
, its validity is shown by induction on
\varphi
.

–

For
\varphi
being of the form
P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}x=y
and
N,w\models_{v}P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})(x/z)
. Since

\langle{\llbracket t_{1}(x/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)},\dots,\llbracket t_{n}(x/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)}}\rangle\in J(P,w),

we can use
v(x)=v(y)
and Proposition
3
to obtain

\langle{\llbracket t_{1}(y/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)},\dots,\llbracket t_{n}(y/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,J(P,w)}}\rangle\in J(P,w).

Thus
N,w\models_{v}P(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})(y/z)
.

–

For
\varphi
being of the forms
\neg\psi
or
\psi\land\gamma
, the proof is straightforward.

–

For
\varphi
being of the form
\forall z^{\prime}\psi
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}x=y
and
N,w\models_{v}(\forall z^{\prime}\psi)(x/z)
.
If
z^{\prime}=z
, obviously
N,w\models_{v}(\forall z^{\prime}\psi)(y/z)
. If
z^{\prime}\neq z
, then we have
N,w\models_{v}\forall z^{\prime}\psi(x/z)
thus
N,w\models_{v[z^{\prime}\mapsto d]}\psi(x/z)
for all
d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(z^{\prime})}
. Since we have
N,w\models_{v[z^{\prime}\mapsto d]}x=y
for all
d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(z^{\prime})}
, by inductive hypothesis we obtain
N,w\models_{v[z^{\prime}\mapsto d]}\psi(y/z)
for all
d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(z^{\prime})}
. Therefore,
N,w\models_{v}(\forall z^{\prime}\psi)(y/z)
.

–

For
\varphi
being of the form
K_{t}\psi
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}x=y
and
N,w\models_{v}(K_{t}\psi)(x/z)
. Then
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\psi(x/z)
for all
w^{\prime}\in W
such that
\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t(x/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}}
. Now, we have
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}x=y
for all
w^{\prime}\in W
, as well as
\llbracket t(x/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}

=

\llbracket t(y/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}
by
v(x)

=

v(y)
and Proposition
3
. So by inductive hypothesis we obtain
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\psi(y/z)
for all
w^{\prime}\in W
such that
\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t(y/z)\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}}
. Thus,
N,w\models_{v}(K_{t}\psi)(y/z)
.

•

For
\exists\mathsf{Id}
, i.e.,
c=c\to\exists x(x=c)
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}c=c
. Since
N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto J(c,w,J(=,w))]}x=c
, we have
N,w\models_{v}\exists x(x=c)
, as required.

•

For
\mathsf{DD}
, i.e.,
x\neq y
if
\mathtt{t}(x)\neq\mathtt{t}(y)
, suppose
\mathtt{t}(x)\neq\mathtt{t}(y)
and let
N
,
w
and
v
be arbitrary. By the definition of valuation, each of
v(x)
and
v(y)
is in
D_{\mathtt{t}(x)}
and
D_{\mathtt{t}(y)}
, respectively. Since
\mathtt{t}(x)\neq\mathtt{t}(y)
,
D_{\mathtt{t}(x)}
and
D_{\mathtt{t}(y)}
must be disjoint. Thus
N,w\models_{v}x\neq y
, as required.

•

For
\mathsf{K}
, i.e.,
K_{t}(\varphi\to\psi)\to(K_{t}\varphi\to K_{t}\psi)
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}K_{t}(\varphi\to\psi)
and
N,w\models_{v}K_{t}\varphi
. Let
w^{\prime}
be any world such that
\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}}
. Then we have
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\varphi\to\psi
and
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\varphi
. Thus
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}\psi
, as required.

•

For
\mathsf{BF}
, i.e.,
\forall xK_{t}\varphi\to K_{t}\forall x\varphi
for
x
not occurring in
t
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}\forall xK_{t}\varphi
. To show
N,w\models_{v}K_{t}\forall x\varphi
, let
w^{\prime}
be any world such that
\langle{w,w^{\prime}}\rangle\in R_{\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}}
and take any
d\in D_{\mathtt{t}(x)}
. By our supposition, we have
N,w\models_{v[x\mapsto d]}K_{t}\varphi
. Now
\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v}_{w,\emptyset}

=

\llbracket t\rrbracket^{J,v[x\mapsto d]}_{w,\emptyset}
holds since
x
does not occur in
t
. Thus
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v[x\mapsto d]}\varphi
, as required.

•

For
\mathsf{KNI}
, i.e.,
x\neq y\to K_{t}x\neq y
, suppose
N,w\models_{v}x\neq y
. By definition, obviously
N,w^{\prime}\models_{v}x\neq y
for all worlds
w^{\prime}
. Thus
N,w\models K_{t}x\neq y
, as required.

By the above argument the proof has completed.
∎

We can now prove the semantic incompleteness of
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Sigma=\langle{\mathtt{Var},\mathtt{Cn},\mathtt{Fn},\mathtt{Rel},\mathtt{t}}\rangle
be a signature,
x\in\mathtt{Var}
,
c\in\mathtt{Cn}
with
\mathtt{t}(x)=\mathtt{t}(c)
and
P\in\mathtt{Rel}
with
\mathtt{t}(P)

=

\langle{\mathtt{agt\_or\_obj}}\rangle
. A formula
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
is not provable in
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
.

Proof.

If
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
is provable in
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
, then by the soundness (Theorem
1
) it must be valid in the non-standard semantics, which contradicts Proposition
2
.
∎

Corollary 1

(Semantic Incompleteness of
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
)
.

The Hilbert-style system
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
is semantically incomplete with respect to the TML-semantics, i.e., there exists some formula
\varphi
such that
\varphi
is valid in the TML-semantics but not provable in
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
1
and Theorem
2
.
∎

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we proved that Liberman et al.
[
12
]
’s Hilbert-style system
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
for the term-modal logic
\mathbf{K}
with equality and non-rigid terms is semantically incomplete by introducing the non-standard semantics for which
\mathsf{H}\mathbf{K}
is sound but in which
x=c\to(P(x)\to P(c))
is not valid.

A further direction to be pursued is to give sound and complete Hilbert-style systems for term-modal logics including
\mathbf{K}
with equality and non-rigid terms. Such systems, for example, might be obtained as slight modifications of the system given in Fagin et al.
[
4
, p. 90]
. Another further direction that might be worth studying is to apply the non-standard semantics to the analysis of natural language. As Example
1
suggests, it is reasonable to see
J(P)
in
J(c,w,J(P))
as a kind of
context
uniquely determining the denotation of a constant
c
at a world
w
. Thus, the non-standard semantics might be seen as a semantics capturing the context-dependency of the denotations of nouns in natural language.

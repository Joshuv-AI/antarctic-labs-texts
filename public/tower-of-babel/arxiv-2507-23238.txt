Role of Σ ∗ ​ ( 1385 ) \Sigma^{*}(1385) on Λ \Lambda hyperon polarization in relativistic heavy ion collisions
Haesom Sung, Che Ming Ko, Su Houng Lee

Abstract
The effect of Σ ∗ ​ ( 1385 ) \Sigma^{*}(1385) baryon resonance on the time evolution of the Λ \Lambda hyperon polarization in hadronic matter is studied using a kinetic approach. This approach explicitly includes the production of the Σ ∗ \Sigma^{*} resonance from the Λ − π \Lambda-\pi and Σ ⁡ ( 1192 ) − π \Sigma(1192)-\pi scatterings as well as its decay into Λ + π \Lambda+\pi or Σ + π \Sigma+\pi . The resulting coupled kinetic equations governing the time evolution of Λ \Lambda , Σ \Sigma and Σ ∗ \Sigma^{*} numbers and polarizations are solved for Au-Au collisions at s N ​ N = 7.7 \sqrt{s_{NN}}=7.7 GeV and 20-50% centrality, using initial values determined by thermal yields and the thermal vorticity at chemical freeze-out temperature. As the hadronic matter expands and cools, the Λ \Lambda polarization is found to increase slightly during early times and then decreases very slowly afterwards, while the Σ \Sigma polarization remains nearly constant and the Σ ∗ \Sigma^{*} polarization continuously decreases. Including feed-down contributions to the Λ \Lambda polarization from the decays of partially polarized Σ 0 \Sigma^{0} , Σ ∗ \Sigma^{*} , and Ξ ⁡ ( 1322 ) \Xi(1322) hyperons, where the Ξ \Xi polarization is obtained by solving coupled kinetic equations for the Ξ \Xi and Ξ ∗ ​ ( 1532 ) \Xi^{*}(1532) system, the resulting Λ \Lambda polarization becomes smaller and decreases over time. In both cases, however, the time variation of the Λ \Lambda polarization is sufficiently small to support the assumption of an early freeze-out of Λ \Lambda spin degree of freedom in relativistic heavy ion collisions.



I
Introduction

Since the STAR Collaboration’s 2017 observation that the
\Lambda
hyperon produced in heavy ion collisions at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) are partially polarized along the direction perpendicular to the reaction plane
Adamczyk et al. 2017a
, numerous theoretical studies have been carried out to understand the origin of this phenomenon
Becattini and Lisa 2020
. Most of these studies are based on hydrodynamic models
Karpenko and Becattini 2017
;
Fu et al. 2021
;
Becattini et al. 2021a
;
Palermo et al. 2024
or the course-grained transport model
Li et al. 2017
, which assume thermal equilibrium between the
\Lambda
spin and the vortical fluid. In these approaches, the global
\Lambda
polarization is calculated from the thermal vorticity at the chemical freeze-out temperature.

Extensions of these studies have incorporated the effects of thermal shear in the fluid to address the measured
\Lambda
local polarization
Niida 2019
, particularly its component along the beam direction, as a function of the azimuthal angle in the transverse plane of the collision
Liu and Yin 2021
;
Becattini et al. 2021b
;
Yi et al. 2021
.

Beyond equilibrium models, the
\Lambda
polarization has been studied using non-equilibrium framework such as the chiral kinetic approach
Sun and Ko 2017a
;
Sun and Ko 2019
and the covariant angular-momentum-conserved chiral transport model
Liu et al. 2020
. In these approaches, both vorticity and shear in the quark matter affect the dynamics of quarks and antiquarks through their equations of motion and scatterings. The polarization of the
\Lambda
hyperon is then determined via the coalescence of polarized strange quarks with polarized up and down quarks during hadronization of the quark matter
Sun and Ko 2017b
.

In all these theoretical studies, the
\Lambda
polarization is calculated at the chemical freeze-out temperature and compared with the measured values obtained after the kinetic freeze-out of the hadronic matter. A recent study based on the assumption of thermal equilibrium between the
\Lambda
spin and the thermal vorticity and shear shows that the
\Lambda
polarization would decrease during the hadronic evolution due to the reduction of vorticity and shear with decreasing temperature and increasing volume of the hadronic matter
Sun et al. 2022
. The fact that experimental data are consistent with the
\Lambda
polarization at the hadronization temperature suggests that the
\Lambda
spin freezes out, or decouples, early from the hadronic matter.

As proposed in Ref.
Ko 2023
, this early freeze-out would occur if
\Lambda-\pi
scattering proceeds through the spin 3/2, positive parity
\Sigma^{*}(1385)
resonance, since the ratio of
\Lambda
spin non-flip to flip probabilities in such scattering is 3.5. This explanation has been supported by detailed theoretical calculations of the
\Lambda
polarization relaxation time in hot hadronic matter, which is found to be about 4-7 fm/
c

Hidaka et al. 2024
, and by the kinetic approach to the time evolution of
\Lambda
polarization during the hadronic stage of relativistic heavy ion collisions, which show a decreases of about 7-12%
Sung et al. 2024
.

However, both of these studies are based on the assumption of instantaneous
\Lambda-\pi
resonance scattering via the
\Sigma^{*}
resonance, and neglect feed-down contributions to the
\Lambda
polarization from other hadrons. Since the experimentally measured
\Lambda
polarization includes not only primary
\Lambda
hyperons but also those from the decays of resonances
Becattini et al. 2017
;
Xia et al. 2019
– particularly the strong decay
\Sigma^{*}\to\Lambda+\pi
, the electromagnetic decay
\Sigma^{0}(1192)\to\Lambda+\gamma
, and the weak decays
\Xi^{0}(1315)\to\Lambda+\pi^{0}
and
\Xi^{-}(1322)\to\Lambda+\pi^{-}
– these contributions must be considered.

In the present study, we go beyond the the approximation used in the kinetic approach of Ref.
Sung et al. 2024
by treating the
\Sigma^{*}
resonance dynamically via the processes
\Lambda+\pi\leftrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
. This includes the production of
\Sigma^{*}
from
\Lambda-\pi
scattering and its decay back to
\Lambda
and
\pi
, as well as its decay to
\Sigma+\pi
and its inverse
\Sigma+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}
process.

In addition, we consider feed-down contributions to the
\Lambda
polarization from the decays of
\Sigma^{0}
and
\Sigma^{*}
as well as from
\Xi^{0}
and
\Xi^{-}
, by solving coupled kinetic equations for the
\Xi
and
\Xi^{*}(1532)
hyperons during the hadronic stage of relativistic heavy ion collisions.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
II
introduces the cross sections for the
\Lambda+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}
and
\Sigma+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}
reactions, together with the decay widths for their inverse processes. Section
III
presents calculations of their thermal averages in a hot hadronic matter. In Sec.
IV
, we derive the kinetic equations for the numbers and polarizations of
\Lambda
,
\Sigma
, and
\Sigma^{*}
hyperons. Section
V
provides solutions to these equation and discusses the resulting time evolution. Section
VI
describes the feed-down contributions to
\Lambda
polarization, including those from
\Xi
and
\Xi^{*}
based on their coupled kinetic equations. Finally, conclusions are presented in Sec.
VII
.

For the

\displaystyle\sigma_{Y+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}}(s)=\frac{2s_{\Sigma^{*}}+1}{(2s_{Y}+1)(2s_{\pi}+1)}\frac{4\pi}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.14322pt\times\frac{B_{\Sigma^{*}\to Y+\pi}s\Gamma_{\Sigma^{*}}^{2}(s)}{(s-m_{\Sigma^{*}}^{2})^{2}+s\Gamma_{\Sigma^{*}}^{2}(s)},\quad Y=\Lambda,\Sigma.

Y

\Gamma_{\Sigma^{*}}(m_{\Sigma^{*}})=38

To study the effect of hadronic scatterings on
\Lambda

\sigma_{Y+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}}
in Eq.(
1

\Sigma^{*}

\sigma_{Y_{\pm 1/2}+\pi\rightarrow\Sigma^{*}_{\pm 3/2}}=\sigma_{Y+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}}/2

\sigma_{{Y_{\pm 1/2}}+\pi\rightarrow\Sigma^{*}_{\pm 1/2}}=\sigma_{Y+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}}/3

\sigma_{Y_{\pm 1/2}+\pi\rightarrow\Sigma^{*}_{{\mp 1/2}}}=\sigma_{Y+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}}/6

\Sigma^{*}

\Gamma_{\Sigma^{*}_{\pm 1/2}\rightarrow Y_{\pm 1/2}+\pi}=2\Gamma_{\Sigma^{*}\to Y+\pi}/3

\Gamma_{\Sigma^{*}_{\pm 1/2}\rightarrow Y_{\mp 1/2}+\pi}=\Gamma_{\Sigma^{*}\to Y+\pi}/3

VII
conclusions

We have studied the effect of the
\Sigma^{*}
resonance on
\Lambda
polarization in relativistic heavy ion collisions by including its production from
\Lambda+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}
and
\Sigma+\pi\to\Sigma^{*}
scatterings, as well as its decay through the inverse processes
\Sigma^{*}\to\Lambda+\pi
and
\Sigma^{*}\to\Sigma+\pi
, in the spin-dependent kinetic equations. By converting these equations into coupled kinetic equations for the numbers and polarizations of
\Lambda
,
\Sigma
, and
\Sigma^{*}
, we are able to study the time evolution of their abundances and polarizations in Au-Au collisions at
\sqrt{s_{NN}}=7.7
GeV and 20-50% collision centrality, as measured at RHIC.

In contrast to the slight decrease of
\Lambda
polarization with time found in Ref.
Sung et al. 2024
, where the
\Sigma^{*}
resonance was not explicitly included as a dynamic degree of freedom, we find that the
\Lambda
polarization increases with time when the
\Sigma^{*}
polarization is larger than the
\Lambda
polarization, as determined by their respective equilibrium values in the thermal vorticity at chemical freeze-out. As the
\Sigma^{*}
polarization gradually decreases with time, the growth of the
\Lambda
polarization slows down, eventually reaching a final value higher than its initial value at
T_{C}
.

However, when the feed-down contributions to the
\Lambda
polarization from the electromagnetic decay of
\Sigma
, the strong decay of
\Sigma^{*}
, and the weak decay of
\Xi
hyperons are included – using their polarizations obtained from the solutions of their coupled kinetic equations – the resulting
\Lambda
polarization is reduced and shows a decreasing trend during the expansion and cooling of the hadronic matter. These changes in
\Lambda
polarization, with and without feed-down contributions, are nevertheless modest, supporting the approximation that the spin degree of freedom of a
\Lambda
essentially freezes out at the chemical freeze-out of relativistic heavy ion collisions. This validates the common practice of comparing theoretical results with experimental data under the assumption of the decoupling of
\Lambda
spin at the chemical freeze-out of heavy ion collisions.

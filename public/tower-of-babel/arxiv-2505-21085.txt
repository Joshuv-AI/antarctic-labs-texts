Anomalous supercurrents in the presence of particle losses
Risa Ogino, Shun Uchino

Abstract
We show that supercurrent properties in a superfluid or superconducting junction are significantly modified due to single-particle losses present in a conduction channel. In the presence of a spin-independent particle loss, we find regimes where the Josephson current I N ​ ( ϕ ) I_{N}(\phi) takes zero at a position in between ϕ = 0 \phi=0 and ϕ = π \phi=\pi , and the direction of the supercurrent is reversed. Although the region is narrow, we also find a regime in which the critical current is enhanced by dissipation. Such anomalous behaviors in the Josephson current are attributed to a subtle interplay between the contribution that is present regardless of dissipation and the unconventional one that is absent without dissipation. In the presence of a spin-selective particle loss, it is shown that a dissipation-induced spin supercurrent and its reversal occur. The proposed system is analyzed by means of the Keldysh field theory approach based on the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudershan-Lindblad master equation and may be realized in ultracold atomic gases and solid-state systems.



I
Introduction

The Josephson effect that represents a flow tunneling through a barrier without a biased voltage is an outstanding yet ubiquitous phenomenon in physics
Barone and Paterno 1982
.
In a simple superconducting junction where an insulator is sandwiched between conventional superconductors,
the DC current usually obeys

\displaystyle I=I_{C}\sin\phi,

(1)

with critical current
I_{C}
and relative phase between two superconductors
\phi
.
The Josephson effect has also been examined in superfluid Helium
Vollhardt and Wolfle 2013
,
elementary particle physics
Nitta 2015
,
and ultracold atomic gases
Pethick and Smith 2008
.
In addition to the importance in fundamental physics,
it plays a crucial role as a building block of
quantum technology such as
superconducting quantum interference device
Fagaly 2006

and superconducting qubit
Kjaergaard et al. 2020
.

One of the vital questions in superconducting junctions is
how nonequilibrium disturbance
affects the Josephson effects
Golubov et al. 2004
.
The prominent example is the Caldeira-Leggett model
originally introduced to explain the dissipation effect in Josephson junctions.
There, the Ohmic resistance in the junctions is incorporated
as the coupling effect with harmonic oscillators that model an environment
Caldeira and Leggett 1981
.
Another remarkable example, which is relevant to the present study, is the presence of the supercurrent reversal phenomenon
where a dirty superconducting junction is
driven out of equilibrium due to a coupling to a normal current
Baselmans et al. 1999
.
The current reversal phenomenon is also known as
\pi
-junction since
the current-phase relation shifts by

\pi

Bulaevskii et al. 1977
;
Geshkenbein et al. 1987
.
The
\pi
-junction has originally been realized in a variety of equilibrium junctions such as ferromagnetic Josephson junction
Ryazanov et al. 2001
,
Josephson junction with unconventional superconductors
Van Harlingen 1995
, and superconducting quantum dot
Cleuziou et al. 2006
,
and has also attracted attention in terms of quantum computation such as implementation of a qubit with a long coherence time
Yamashita et al. 2005
.

Two-terminal Josephson junction system discussed in this paper. A particle current between superfluids or superconductors induced by a relative phase bias
\phi

passes through a quantum dot
where single particle losses occur.

Nowadays, ultracold atomic gases provide an ideal toolbox to explore
the Josephson junctions
Krinner et al. 2017
.
By controlling an interatomic interaction, one can realize fermionic superfluids
whose transport properties have already been examined in mesoscopic setups such as quantum point contact
Husmann et al. 2015
and junction systems
Valtolina et al. 2015
.
Josephson current properties in such gases can also be examined through current-biased junctions, which have successfully been
implemented
Levy 2007
;
Kwon et al. 2020
;
Del Pace et al. 2021
.
Furthermore, a unique capability in ultracold atomic gases allows
for dissipation control in quantum many-body systems
Schäfer et al. 2020
.
In the context of mesoscopic transport, dissipative transport induced by a single particle loss has been measured
Corman et al. 2019
.
There, a spin-selective dissipation takes place in the mesoscopic channel region in a quantum point contact
and induces the disappearance of the quantized conductance.
Since the similar experiment under superfluid reservoirs has also been realized with the Feshbach resonance,
which showed that non-linear current-bias curves
arising from the multiple Andreev reflections are weakened
by dissipation
Huang et al. 2023
,
it would be intriguing to examine how such a dissipation affects Josephson current properties.

Another notable development in Josephson junctions is a connection
with non-Hermitian physics
Ashida et al. 2020
.
Recent theoretical works have demonstrated that non-Hermitian effects mimicking dissipation modify the Josephson
currents
Li et al. 2024
;
Cayao and Sato 2024a
;
Shen et al. 2024
;
Pino et al. 2024
;
Beenakker 2024
;
Cayao and Sato 2024b
;
Capecelatro et al. 2025
;
Ohnmacht et al. 2025
;
Sten et al. 2025
. The non-Hermitian Hamiltonian naturally emerges from the non-unitary evolution term in the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad(GKSL) master equation
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
.
At the same time, the full time evolution in the GKSL master equation
also contains the so-called quantum jump term whose inclusion is
required for description of generic Markovian dynamics
in open quantum systems.
Therefore, it is fundamentally important to look at
the dissipative Josephson effects at the full GKSL level.
We note that such an analysis has direct relevance to experiments of ultracold atomic gases
whose dissipation effects can usually be captured
within the GKSL master equation
Daley 2014
.

Motivated by the recent experimental progress in ultracold atomic gases and
the non-Hermitian treatment of the dissipative Josephson junction,
here we shed light on a mesoscopic Josephson junction system where a non-interacting quantum dot accompanied by a single particle loss is attached to two superfluid or superconducting leads (Fig.
1
).
We note that such a system has recently been harnessed to explain
transport properties in the lossy superfluid point contact
realized in ultracold atomic gases
Huang et al. 2023
;
Visuri et al. 2023
.
By using the Keldysh formalism that allows to analyze
the systems obeying
the GKSL master equation in an efficient manner,
we demonstrate the anomalous behaviors such as
the Josephson current with extra nodes in addition
to conventional ones,
\pi
-junction behavior
and dissipation-enhanced critical current .
In contrast to the conventional wisdom such that a dissipation nulls coherent transport, furthermore,
we find emergent phenomena in which spin supercurrent and its reversal are induced in the presence of spin-selective particle loss.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Sec. II, we adopt the field theory approach that combines
the Keldysh formalism with the GKSL master
equation. By applying such formalism to
the superconducting junction, we obtain the Josephson current formulae
that allow to discuss the dissipation effects.
Section III shows numerical results of the Josephson currents in the presence of a spin-independent particle loss,
as well as ones in the presence of a
spin-selective particle loss.
Section IV is devoted to discussion and outlook.
Finally, some calculations in details on Green’s functions,
Dyson equation in the Keldysh formalism, and current expressions are discussed in Appendices A, B and C.

II
Formulation of the problem

To uncover prototypical roles of particle losses
in Josephson junctions, we
consider the system in which
two superfluid or superconducting reservoirs
are connected via a non-interacting quantum dot (Fig.
1
).
In the absence of dissipation, the evolution of the density matrix
\rho
is described by the von Neumann equation
\partial_{\tau}\rho=-i[H,\rho]
with the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=H_{L}+H_{R}+H_{T}+H_{d},

(2)

\displaystyle H_{T}=\sum_{\sigma}t[\psi^{\dagger}_{L,\sigma}d_{\sigma}+\psi^{\dagger}_{R,\sigma}d_{\sigma}+\text{h.c.}],

(3)

\displaystyle H_{d}=\sum_{\sigma}\epsilon d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}d_{\sigma}.

(4)

Here,
H_{L(R)}
,
H_{T}
, and
H_{d}
are
the Hamiltonian of reservoir
L(R)
, tunneling term between
the reservoir and quantum dot, and quantum dot Hamiltonian,
respectively. In addition,

\psi_{L(R),\sigma}
and
d_{\sigma}
with
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
are respectively Fermi field operators of reservoir
L(R)
and quantum dot,

t
the tunneling amplitude between reservoirs and quantum dot,

\epsilon
the onsite energy in the dot
1
1

1

We assume that tunneling occurs between the position
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{0}

in each reservoir and the quantum dot. Therefore,
\psi_{L(R)\sigma}
is the shorthand
notation of
\psi_{L(R)\sigma}(\mathbf{0})
.
.
Notice that we adopt units of
\hbar=k_{B}=1
.
In the presence of a single particle loss at the dot, the system no longer experiences the unitary evolution and instead is described by the following quantum master equation
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{\sigma}\gamma_{\sigma}\Big(d_{\sigma}\rho d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}-\frac{\{d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}d_{\sigma},\rho\}}{2}\Big),

(5)

where
\gamma_{\sigma}
represents the particle loss rate.

Based on this setup and taking the convention that a current is positive when particles flow from left to right reservoirs,
one can determine a particle (spin) current between reservoirs
I_{N}(I_{S})
as follows
Uchino 2022
;
Huang et al. 2023
;
Visuri et al. 2023
:

\displaystyle I_{N(S)}=\text{Re}\Big[it\{\langle\psi^{\dagger}_{R,\uparrow}d_{\uparrow}\rangle-\langle\psi^{\dagger}_{L,\uparrow}d_{\uparrow}\rangle\}

\displaystyle+(-)it\{\langle\psi^{\dagger}_{R,\downarrow}d_{\downarrow}\rangle-\langle\psi^{\dagger}_{L,\downarrow}d_{\downarrow}\rangle\}\Big].

(6)

We point out that although the expression above is formally equivalent to one without dissipation,
the average above
\langle\cdot\rangle
is taken with the density matrix satisfying Eq. (
5
) and therefore
contains effects of dissipation entirely.

In what follows, we examine behaviors of Eq. (
6
) in the presence of a relative phase bias between fermionic superfluid reservoirs.
To this end, we adopt the Keldysh formalism, which allows to analyze observables in nonequilibrium
Kamenev 2011

and open quantum systems
Sieberer et al. 2016
.
In addition, we treat the Fermi superfluid in each reservoir within the mean-field theory, which has been used to compare with cold-atom experiments in Ref.
Husmann et al. 2015
;
Huang et al. 2023
.

In order to relate the average currents with quantities calculable from the Keldysh formalism, we define
the Keldysh components of Green’s functions between reservoirs and quantum dot (see also Appendix A)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{K}_{dL(R)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-i\langle[\hat{d}(\tau),\hat{\psi}_{L(R)}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})]\rangle,

(7)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{K}_{L(R)d}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-i\langle[\hat{\psi}_{L(R)}(\tau),\hat{d}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})]\rangle.

(8)

Here we introduce the following Nambu spinors of the fermionic fields
Cuevas et al. 1996
:

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{L(R)}=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{\uparrow,L(R)}\\
\psi^{\dagger}_{\downarrow,L(R)}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \hat{d}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{\uparrow}\\
d^{\dagger}_{\downarrow}\end{pmatrix},

(9)

which provides useful expressions in fermionic superfluids
that exist nonzero anomalous averages such as
\langle\psi_{\uparrow}\psi_{\downarrow}\rangle
.
By using above,
I_{N}(I_{S})
is rewritten as

\displaystyle I_{N}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}^{K}_{dL}(\tau,\tau)-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\hat{G}^{K}_{Ld}(\tau,\tau)

\displaystyle-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\hat{G}^{K}_{dR}(\tau,\tau)+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}^{K}_{Rd}(\tau,\tau)\bigg],

(10)

\displaystyle I_{S}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}^{K}_{dL}(\tau,\tau)-\hat{t}\hat{G}^{K}_{Ld}(\tau,\tau)

\displaystyle-\hat{t}\hat{G}^{K}_{dR}(\tau,\tau)+\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}^{K}_{Rd}(\tau,\tau)\bigg],

(11)

where

\displaystyle\hat{t}=\begin{pmatrix}te^{-i\phi/4}&0\\
0&-te^{i\phi/4}\end{pmatrix},

(12)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

(13)

and the trace is taken for the Nambu space.
Thus, it follows that the average currents are
determined once Eqs (
7
) and (
8
)
are calculated.

As shown in Appendices B and C,
Eqs (
7
) and (
8
)
can systematically be analyzed with the Dyson equation
in the Keldysh formalism.
Such an analysis reveals that

I_{N}
is given by

\displaystyle I_{N}=I_{N,c}+I_{N,uc}.

(14)

Here
I_{N,c}
is the contribution that exists even in the absence of dissipation, and
I_{N,uc}
is the unconventional contribution that only emerges in the presence of dissipation.
Furthermore, the similar analysis for
I_{S}

leads to

\displaystyle I_{S}=I_{S,uc},

(15)

where
I_{S,uc}
represents
the unconventional contribution that emerges in the presence of spin-selective dissipation.
The absence of the conventional contribution in the spin current
is attributed to the fact that the spin current is not usually generated
by the phase bias yet is allowed owing to the spin-dependent
particle loss.

III
Result

We now examine
behaviors of the particle and spin currents at zero temperature (
T=0
) where
the maximum supercurrent is expected.

In the absence of dissipation, the phase-dependent particle current is known to be carries by the Andreev bound state and continuum contributions above the superconducting gap
\Delta

When the resonance width
\Gamma=2t^{2}/W

\rho

\Delta

the supercurrent is carried mostly by the Andreev bound states.
When
\Gamma

\Delta

In addition, the channel transmission at the Fermi
energy

In what follows, we demonstrate
how the supercurrent properties are modified

particle losses at different

and

IV
Discussion and outlook

Inspired by the recent experimental progress of ultracold atomic gases and non-Hermitian Josephson junctions, we have demonstrated anomalous Josephson transport through a lossy quantum dot.
In the presence of spin-independent particle loss,
we have demonstrated regimes where

I_{N}(\phi)
crosses the horizontal axis
and takes negative values for
0<\phi<\pi
,
and dissipation-enhanced critical current occurs.
On top of that, the spin-selective control of dissipation, as available in cold-atom experiments, leads to the spin supercurrent phenomenon present in the dissipative regime
where the strength of loss rate is intermediate.

While our predictions can naturally be designed in the cold-atom experiment,
one may obtain similar result in condensed matter.
For instance, one can consider a three terminal system where a mesoscopic sample is attached to two superconducting leads and one normal lead
van Wees et al. 1991
;
Samuelsson et al. 2000
;
Uchino 2022
.
Then, the effect of the single particle loss may be mimicked in a situation that the chemical potential of the normal lead is much lower than that of the sample,
where a particle gain from the normal lead can be ignored.
The spin-selective dissipation may also be designed with the ferromagnetic junction
Martın-Rodero et al. 2001
or junction with spin-orbit interactions
Kohda et al. 2012

We have discussed dissipation-induced emergent phenomena in simple mesoscopic systems.
Interesting perspectives are
stability against the number of sites in the channel,
a correlation effect in a mesoscopic regime where connections to the Kondo effect and Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid are anticipated,
and application to bosonic superfluids, which has been realized in
the Josephson junction array
Labouvie et al. 2016
.

Appendix A
Green’s function

The Keldysh components of real-time Green’s functions between reservoir and dot are given by Eq. (
7
) and Eq. (
8
). Similarly, real-time Green’s functions at a quantum dot are expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{R}_{d}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-i\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\langle\{\hat{d}(\tau),\hat{d}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})\}\rangle,

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{A}_{d}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\langle\{\hat{d}(\tau),\hat{d}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})\}\rangle,

(18)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{K}_{d}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-i\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\langle[\hat{d}(\tau),\hat{d}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})]\rangle.

(19)

We now obtain the current expression induced by the phase gradient between reservoirs. By using the so-called Langreth rule
Rammer 2007
,
we can obtain the following relations for
Green’s functions in frequency space
Haug et al. 2008
:

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{K}_{\sigma,dL}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{G}^{R}_{d}(\omega)\hat{t}\hat{g}^{K}_{L}(\omega)+\hat{G}^{K}_{d}(\omega)\hat{t}\hat{g}^{A}_{L}(\omega),

(20)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{K}_{\sigma,Ld}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{g}^{R}_{L}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}^{K}_{d}(\omega)+\hat{g}^{K}_{L}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}^{A}_{d}(\omega),

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{K}_{\sigma,dR}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{G}^{R}_{d}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}^{K}_{R}(\omega)+\hat{G}^{K}_{d}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}^{A}_{R}(\omega),

(22)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{K}_{\sigma,Rd}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{g}^{R}_{R}(\omega)\hat{t}\hat{G}^{K}_{d}(\omega)+\hat{g}^{K}_{R}(\omega)\hat{t}\hat{G}^{A}_{d}(\omega).

(23)

Here, we introduce uncoupled retarded (advanced) Green’s function in each reservoir
\hat{g}^{R}_{L(R)}(\omega)

that does not incorporate
tunneling effects between reservoirs and quantum dot.
For superconducting reservoirs, it is given by
Cuevas et al. 1996

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{R(A)}_{L(R)}(\omega)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{W}\begin{pmatrix}g^{R(A)}(\omega)&f^{R(A)}(\omega)\\
f^{R(A)}(\omega)&g^{R(A)}(\omega)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W}\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\omega\pm i0^{+}}{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-(\omega\pm i0^{+})^{2}}}&\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-(\omega\pm i0^{+})^{2}}}\\
\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-(\omega\pm i0^{+})^{2}}}&-\frac{\omega\pm i0^{+}}{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-(\omega\pm i0^{+})^{2}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

Since each reservoir is in equilibrium, the uncoupled Keldysh component is determined via the fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Kamenev 2011
as follows:

\hat{g}^{K}_{L(R)}(\omega)=\left[\hat{g}^{R}_{L(R)}(\omega)-\hat{g}^{A}_{L(R)}(\omega)\right]\left[1-2n(\omega)\right],

(25)

where
n(\omega)=\frac{1}{e^{\omega/T}+1}
is the Fermi distribution function.
We next look at uncoupled Green’s function in the quantum dot. In the presence of the dissipation, inverse Green’s functions are determined as
Huang et al. 2023

\displaystyle\left[\hat{g}^{-1}_{d}(\omega)\right]^{R}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\omega-\epsilon+i\gamma_{\uparrow}&0\\
0&\omega+\epsilon+i\gamma_{\downarrow}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{g}^{-1}_{d}(\omega)\right]^{A}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\omega-\epsilon-i\gamma_{\uparrow}&0\\
0&\omega+\epsilon-i\gamma_{\downarrow}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{g}^{-1}_{d}(\omega)\right]^{K}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}2i\gamma_{\uparrow}&0\\
0&-2i\gamma_{\downarrow}\end{pmatrix}.

(26)

Thus, uncoupled Green’s functions are obtained as

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{R}_{d}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\omega-\epsilon+i\gamma_{\uparrow}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\omega+\epsilon+i\gamma_{\downarrow}}\end{pmatrix},

(27)

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{A}_{d}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\omega-\epsilon-i\gamma_{\uparrow}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\omega+\epsilon-i\gamma_{\downarrow}}\end{pmatrix},

(28)

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{K}_{d}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\hat{g}^{R}_{d}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}^{-1}_{d}(\omega)\right]^{K}\hat{g}^{A}_{d}(\omega)=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{2i\gamma_{\uparrow}}{(\omega-\epsilon)^{2}+\gamma_{\uparrow}^{2}}&0\\
0&\frac{2i\gamma_{\downarrow}}{(\omega+\epsilon)^{2}+\gamma_{\downarrow}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(29)

Appendix B
Dyson equation

Since uncoupled Green’s functions are determined, we turn to analyze the full Green’s functions in the quantum dot
G_{d}
, obeying the following the Dyson equation
Haug et al. 2008
:

\hat{G}_{d}(\omega)=\hat{g}_{d}(\omega)+\hat{g}_{d}(\omega)\hat{\Sigma}(\omega)\hat{G}_{d}(\omega)=\hat{g}_{d}(\omega)+\hat{G}_{d}(\omega)\hat{\Sigma}(\omega)\hat{g}_{d}(\omega),

(30)

where
\hat{\Sigma}(\omega)
is the self-energy.
We first look at the Dyson equation of the retarded and advanced components:

\hat{G}_{d}^{R(A)}(\omega)=\hat{g}_{d}^{R(A)}(\omega)+\hat{g}_{d}^{R(A)}(\omega)\hat{\Sigma}^{R(A)}(\omega)\hat{G}_{d}^{R(A)}(\omega),

(31)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}^{R(A)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{R(A)}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}+\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{R(A)}(\omega)\hat{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma}{2}\begin{pmatrix}g_{L}^{R(A)}(\omega)+g_{R}^{R(A)}(\omega)&-e^{-i\phi/2}f_{L}^{R(A)}(\omega)-e^{i\phi/2}f_{R}^{R(A)}(\omega)\\
-e^{i\phi/2}f_{L}^{R(A)}(\omega)-e^{-i\phi/2}f_{R}^{R(A)}(\omega)&g_{L}^{R(A)}(\omega)+g_{R}^{R(A)}(\omega)\end{pmatrix}.

(32)

The above Dyson equation can also be rewritten as follows:

\left[\hat{G}_{d}^{R(A)}(\omega)\right]^{-1}=\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R(A)}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\hat{\Sigma}^{R(A)}(\omega).

(33)

On the other hand,
the Dyson equation of the Keldysh component is given by

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{d}^{K}(\omega)=\left[1+\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\hat{\Sigma}^{R}(\omega)\right]\hat{g}_{d}^{K}(\omega)\left[1+\hat{\Sigma}^{A}(\omega)\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]+\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\hat{\Sigma}^{K}(\omega)\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega).

(34)

Here

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}^{K}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{K}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}+\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{K}(\omega)\hat{t}

\displaystyle={\left[\hat{\Sigma}^{R}(\omega)-\hat{\Sigma}^{A}(\omega)\right][1-2n(\omega)]}

\displaystyle={\left[\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\right][1-2n(\omega)]-\left[\left[\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\left[\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\right][1-2n(\omega)]}.

(35)

By using above and the Dyson equation for
G_{d}^{R(A)}
,

\hat{G}^{K}_{d}
is rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{d}^{K}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\hat{g}_{d}^{K}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)+\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\hat{\Sigma}_{d}^{K}(\omega)\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)\right]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)+\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{\Sigma}^{R}(\omega)-\hat{\Sigma}^{A}(\omega)\right]\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)[1-2n(\omega)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)\right]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)+\left[\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)-\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right][1-2n(\omega)]

\displaystyle+\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\right]\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)[1-2n(\omega)].

(36)

Appendix C
Particle and spin currents

By means of the Dyson equations obtained above,
the particle current
I_{N}
is expressed as

\displaystyle I_{N}=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d\omega}{8\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\big\{\hat{G}_{d}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{K}+\hat{G}_{d}^{K}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{A}\big\}+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\big\{\hat{g}_{L}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{K}+\hat{g}_{L}^{K}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\big\}

\displaystyle+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\big\{\hat{G}_{d}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{K}+\hat{G}_{d}^{K}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{A}\big\}\hat{t}-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\big\{\hat{g}_{R}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{K}+\hat{g}_{R}^{K}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\big\}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{N,c}+I_{N,uc},

(37)

where

\displaystyle I_{N,c}=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d\omega}{8\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\big[\hat{G}_{d}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{R}-\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{A}\big]+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\big[\hat{g}_{L}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}-\hat{g}_{L}^{A}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\big]

\displaystyle+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\big[\hat{G}_{d}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{R}-\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{A}\big]-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\big[\hat{g}_{R}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}-\hat{g}_{R}^{A}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\big]\bigg]\big[1-2n(\omega)\big],

(38)

\displaystyle I_{N,uc}=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d\omega}{8\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{A}-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)

\displaystyle-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{A}+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)([\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)]^{-1}-[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)]^{-1})\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{A}+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)([\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)]^{-1}-[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)]^{-1})\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)

\displaystyle+\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)([\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)]^{-1}-[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)]^{-1})\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{A}-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)([\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)]^{-1}-[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)]^{-1})\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\bigg]\big[1-2n(\omega)\big].

(39)

We point out that in the limit of
\gamma\to 0
and

\Gamma/\Delta\gg 1
, the expression above
reduces to the Andreev current
I_{\text{ABS}}
,
that is,

\displaystyle I_{N}

\displaystyle\to I_{\text{ABS}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\cal T}\Delta^{2}\sin\phi}{2E_{\text{ABS}}}\tanh\left(\frac{E_{\text{ABS}}}{2T}\right),

(40)

where

\displaystyle E_{\text{ABS}}=\Delta\sqrt{1-{\cal T}\sin^{2}(\phi/2)}

(41)

is the so-called Andreev bound-state energy.

On the other hand, the spin current between reservoirs
I_{S}
is expressed as

\displaystyle I_{S}=

\displaystyle I_{S,c}+I_{S,uc},

(42)

where

\displaystyle I_{S,c}=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d\omega}{8\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[-\hat{t}^{\dagger}\left[\hat{G}_{d}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{R}-\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{A}\right]+\hat{t}\left[\hat{g}_{L}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}-\hat{g}_{L}^{A}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\right]

\displaystyle+\hat{t}\left[\hat{G}_{d}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{R}-\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{A}\right]-\hat{t}^{\dagger}\left[\hat{g}_{R}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}-\hat{g}_{R}^{A}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}\right]\bigg][1-2n(\omega)],

(43)

\displaystyle I_{S,uc}=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d\omega}{8\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg[\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)\right]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{A}-\hat{t}\hat{g}_{L}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)\right]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)

\displaystyle-\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)\right]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{A}+\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{-1}(\omega)\right]^{K}\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\bigg[-\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left(\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\right)\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}_{\hat{g}_{L}^{A}}+\hat{t}_{2}^{R}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left(\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\right)\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)

\displaystyle+\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left(\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\right)\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{A}-\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}_{R}^{R}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\left(\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{R}(\omega)\right]^{-1}-\left[\hat{g}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\right)\hat{G}_{d}^{A}(\omega)\bigg][1-2n(\omega)].

(44)

The direct calculation shows

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left[-\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R(A)}\hat{t}\hat{g}^{R(A)}+\hat{t}\hat{g}^{R(A)}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{G}_{d}^{R(A)}\right]=0,

(45)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left[-\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R(A)}\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}^{R(A)}+\hat{t}^{\dagger}\hat{g}^{R(A)}\hat{t}\hat{G}_{d}^{R(A)}\right]=0,

(46)

which implies that
I_{S,c}
vanishes. This result shows that the spin current is fully induced by the term associated with the dissipation effect. Thus, the spin current is expressed as

\displaystyle I_{S}=I_{S,uc}.

(47)

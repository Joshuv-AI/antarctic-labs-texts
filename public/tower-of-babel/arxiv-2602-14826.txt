Hodge theory for twisted log-differential forms
Junyan Cao

Abstract




Hodge theory plays a central role in complex geometry, furnishing a profound and elegant correspondence among the analytic, topological, and algebraic aspects of complex manifolds. By identifying cohomological invariants with spaces of harmonic forms, it yields deep structural insights and powerful consequences across the field.

In parallel,
L^{2}
estimates –arising in the study of holomorphic line bundles endowed with possibly singular metrics– have emerged as indispensable analytic tools in complex geometry. They underpin fundamental results in vanishing theorems, extension phenomena, and the analysis of singular spaces.

It is thus both natural and fruitful to bring these two perspectives together, extending Hodge theory to differential forms with values in bundles equipped with singular metrics. In this broader setting, techniques from

L^{2}
-theory prove to be remarkably effective. In this survey, we review recent developments along these lines, drawing on the works
[
CP23
,
CP20
,
CDHP
]
. The applications discussed in the three final sections both illustrate the breadth of these advances and point toward promising directions for future investigation.

We next briefly explain the results and the organization of this article.

In Section 1, we discuss a version of Hodge theory for line bundles equipped with singular metrics over compact Kähler manifolds.
Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
L\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle. Assume that
L
admits a singular metric
h_{L}
such that the corresponding curvature current can be written as follows

(0.0.1)

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta,

where
D:=\sum D_{i}
is an SNC divisor,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and
\theta
is a smooth form on
X
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be a sufficiently large and divisible integer. We consider the orbifold structure
(X,(1-\frac{1}{m})D)
and a metric
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
adapted to it. In
[
CP23
]
, we developed a Hodge theory for
(X,L,h_{L},\omega_{\mathcal{C}})
; see Theorem
1.6
. This can be seen as a natural generalisation of the results obtained in the context of orbifolds
(i.e. in the absence of the additional twisting with
L
).
One of the main applications of the theory is the crucial
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma in conic sense (established under natural assumptions on the form
\theta
above); see Theorem
1.7
.

An important question in this framework is the following: assume that some
L
-valued form is
\bar{\partial}
-exact in conic sense. Under which conditions is this form
\bar{\partial}
-exact in the usual sense? We refer to Theorem
1.11
for the precise meaning of the question and a result in this direction.

In Section 2, we generalize the above construction to the quasi-compact setting, following
[
CDHP
]
. More precisely, let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{k}D_{i}
be an SNC divisor on
X
. We would like to study the logarithmic holomorphic flat bundles
(L,\nabla)
on
(X,D)
, namely the pairs consisting on holomorphic line bundles
L
on
X
together with a logarithmic holomorphic connection

(0.0.2)

\nabla:L\to L\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D),

such that
\nabla^{2}=0
on
X\setminus D
.

One can always construct a harmonic metric
h_{L}
on
L
, so that the equality
i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=0
holds on
X\setminus D
. The metric
h_{L}
may have singularities along
D
, but one can always assume that the Lelong numbers
\nu(L,h_{L})
of
h_{L}
along
D
are rational.

Let
\Delta_{2}\subset D
be the union of components such that the equality

\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)+\nu_{D_{i}}(L,h_{L})\neq 0

holds. In this setting the ambient metric on
X
we are working with will have conic singularities along
D\setminus\Delta_{2}
and Poincaré type singularities along
\Delta_{2}
. Notice that
such a metric is complete near the boundary
\Delta_{2}
, and also that the singularities are imposed by geometric/analytic considerations.

With respect to this rather involved setting, our first task would be to define what is meant by a “smooth form” see Definition
2.12
. This turns out to be a suitable notion, given that we obtain a strong Hodge decomposition Theorem
2.15
. Moreover, the space we define provides a natural fine resolution of the complex induced by (
0.0.2
), see Subsection
2.3
.

Section 3 is devoted to generalisation of some of the results of the previous two sections by replacing differential forms with currents. The idea of this generalisation is as follows.
For many applications, we need to consider forms with logarithmic poles along some divisors
E
, where
E+D
is snc. Thanks to an idea due to Noguchi
[
Nog95
]
, one can embed logarithmic forms into the dual space, namely the space of currents. We adapt this approach to the settings of Sections 1 and 2.

A key point that needs to be established in this approach is a regularity lemma; see Subsection
3.2
. It asserts that if a logarithmic form is
\bar{\partial}
-exact modulo an order-zero current supported on
E
, then it can be written as the
\bar{\partial}
of a form with logarithmic poles. Many ideas in this section originate from the pioneering works
[
Nog95
]
and
[
LRW
]
.

In the remaining sections (4, 5 and 6) we discuss several applications of the theory established above, including the extension of pluricanonical forms, the jumping of cohomology locus property for rank-one local systems on quasi-compact Kähler manifolds, and the deformation theory of log Calabi–Yau manifolds. Finally, we would like to mention that in a forthcoming paper
[
CDHP2
]
, we generalise some of the above constructions to higher-rank local systems over quasi-projective varieties.

For most of the results in this survey we will not provide a complete proof, but rather refer to the original articles. Notable exceptions are
Theorem
3.16
and Theorem
6.2
, for which we supply new arguments, mainly because we feel that they are slightly simpler than the original ones.

1.
Hodge theory for
L
-valued forms

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
L\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle. Assume that
L
admits a possibly singular metric
h_{L}
such that

(1.0.1)

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta,

where
D:=\sum D_{i}
is an snc divisor,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and
\theta
is a smooth form on
X
.
In this section, we present a Hodge theory for
(X,L,h_{L})
, following the constructions in
[
CP23
]
.

To begin with, we fix an integer
m\in\mathbb{N}
which is sufficiently large and divisible; in particular we assume that
ma_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
holds for every
i
.
We will systematically use the
m
-ramified covers corresponding to the orbifold structure

\Delta:=\sum_{i}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{m}\Bigr)D_{i}.

More precisely, let
\{(V_{i},z_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a finite cover of
X
by coordinate charts such that

(1.0.2)

z_{i}^{1}\cdots z_{i}^{r}=0

is a local equation of the divisor
D
on
V_{i}
.
We then consider the local
m
-ramified maps

(1.0.3)

\pi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i},\qquad\pi_{i}(w_{i}^{1},\dots,w_{i}^{n}):=\bigl((w_{i}^{1})^{m},\dots,(w_{i}^{r})^{m},w_{i}^{r+1},\dots,w_{i}^{n}\bigr),

which define the orbifold structure associated with
(X,\Delta)
.
Let
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
be a conic Kähler metric with respect to
(X,\Delta)
; namely,
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
is a smooth Kähler metric on
X\setminus D
, and
\pi_{i}^{\star}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
extends to a smooth Kähler metric on
U_{i}
for every
i
.
Such a conic metric can be constructed as follows:

(1.0.4)

\omega_{\mathcal{C}}:=C\,\omega_{X}+\sum_{i}\sqrt{-1}\,\partial\bar{\partial}|s_{D_{i}}|_{h_{i}}^{2/m},

where
s_{D_{i}}
is the canonical section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
and
h_{i}
is a smooth metric on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
.
If the positive constant
C
is sufficiently large, then
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
is a conic Kähler metric.

In the absence of the twisting bundle
L
, the meaningful objects on
(X,\Delta)
are the so-called orbifold differential forms, cf. for exmaple
[
Rai96
]
.
The corresponding notion in our setting here is as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\phi
be an
L
-valued smooth form on
X\setminus D
. We say that
\phi
is
smooth in the conic sense
if the local quotients

(1.1.1)

\phi_{i}:=\frac{1}{w_{i}^{am}}\,\pi_{i}^{\star}(\phi|_{V_{i}})

extend smoothly to
U_{i}
.
Here we use the shorthand

w_{i}^{am}:=(w_{i}^{1})^{a_{1}m}\cdots(w_{i}^{r})^{a_{r}m}.

The space of
L
-valued forms smooth in the conic sense is denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,L)
. Note that the defintion depends on the metrics
h_{L}
and
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Notice that this agrees with the usual notion of
orbifold differential forms
in case
(L,h_{L})
is trivial. The idea here is similar, except that we also take into account the singularities of the metric
h_{L}
on
L
.

The following statement relates the intrinsic differential operators associated with
(X,\omega_{\mathcal{C}})
and
(L,h_{L})
to the corresponding local operators, defined via the local uniformisations
\pi_{i}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

[
CP23
, Prop 2.17]

Let
\phi
be an
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form which is smooth in the orbifold sense.
Then its “natural” derivatives
D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}
,
D_{h_{L}}^{\star}
, etc. are also smooth in the orbifold sense. Moreover:

(1)

\sup_{X\setminus Y}|\phi|_{h_{L},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}<\infty
; in particular, forms which are smooth in the conic sense are bounded.

(2)

The following identities hold:

\pi_{i}^{\star}(D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\phi)=w_{i}^{am}D^{\prime}\phi_{i},\qquad\pi_{i}^{\star}(\bar{\partial}\phi)=w_{i}^{am}\bar{\partial}\phi_{i},

and

\pi_{i}^{\star}\bigl({D^{\prime}}_{h_{L}}^{\star}\phi\bigr)=w_{i}^{am}{D^{\prime}}^{\star}\phi_{i},\qquad\pi_{i}^{\star}(\bar{\partial}^{\star}\phi)=w_{i}^{am}\bar{\partial}^{\star}\phi_{i},

where
D^{\prime}\psi_{i}:=\partial\psi_{i}-\partial(\varphi_{L,0}\circ\pi_{i})\wedge\psi_{i}
and

\varphi_{L}=\sum a_{i}\log|z_{i}|^{2}+\varphi_{L,0}
.

(3)

Let
\Delta^{\prime\prime}:=[\bar{\partial},\bar{\partial}^{\star}]
be the Laplacian with respect to
(\omega_{\mathcal{C}},h_{L})
. Then

\pi_{i}^{\star}(\Delta^{\prime\prime}\phi)=w_{i}^{am}\cdot\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{sm}}\,\phi_{i},

where
\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{sm}}
is the Laplacian in the local nonsingular setting
(\pi_{i}^{\star}\omega_{\mathcal{C}},\,\varphi_{L,0}\circ\pi_{i})
.

Note that, by our definition, given two forms
u
and
v
which are smooth in the conic sense,
the equality

\bar{\partial}u=v\qquad\text{ on }X

holds in the conic sense means simply that
\bar{\partial}u=v
on
X\setminus D
.
The following example/remark explains clearly an important difference between the usual
\bar{\partial}
operator, and the one we introduce in the conic setting.

Example 1.3
.

Let

A^{p,q}(X,D,L):=C^{\infty}_{(0,q)}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log D)\otimes L\bigr)

be the space of
L
-valued smooth forms with logarithmic poles along
D
in the classical sense, and let
s\in A^{p,q}(X,D,L)
.
Assume that
a_{i}<0
for every
i
in (
1.0.1
). Then
s
is smooth in the conic sense.

For instance, suppose that
D_{1}
is locally defined by
z_{1}=0
. Then
\displaystyle\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\big|_{X\setminus D}
is smooth. Moreover, after the
m
-ramified cover
\pi
, we have

w_{1}^{-a_{1}m}\,\pi^{\star}\!\left(\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\right)=m\,w_{1}^{-a_{1}m}\,\frac{dw_{1}}{w_{1}},

and the right-hand side is smooth when
a_{1}<0
(recall that
a_{1}m\in\mathbb{Z}
). Hence
\displaystyle\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}
is smooth in conic sense.

Moreover, we have
\displaystyle\bar{\partial}(\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}})=0
in conic sense: indeed, the condition is that

w_{1}\,\bar{\partial}\!\left(\frac{dw_{1}}{w_{1}}\Big|_{\mathbb{D}^{\star}}\right).

extends across the disc, which is clearly the case.
Notice that this is a bit confusing, since the form
\displaystyle\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}
is not
\bar{\partial}
-closed in the usual sense. Indeed, the equality

\bar{\partial}\!\left(\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\right)=-[z_{1}=0]

holds -up to some multiple.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
u
and
v
be two
L
-valued forms, which are smooth in conic sense, and such that the equality
\bar{\partial}u=v
holds pointwise in the complement of
D
. Then it is true that we have
\bar{\partial}u=v
in conic sense, but as the previous example shows, this is not true in the usual sense.

We now list some basic properties of forms smooth in the conic sense. The following proposition shows that conic-smooth forms behave well with respect to integration by parts.

Proposition 1.5
.

[
CP23
, Cor 2.19]

Let
(L,h_{L})
be a holomorphic line bundle satisfying (
1.0.1
), and let
(L^{\star},h_{L}^{\star})
be its dual endowed with the dual metric.
Let
\alpha
be an
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form and let
\beta
be an
L^{\star}
-valued
(n-p-1,n-q)
-form. Assume that both
\alpha
and
\beta
are smooth in the conic sense.
Then the usual integration by parts formula holds:

(1.5.1)

\int_{X}D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{p+q+1}\int_{X}\alpha\wedge D^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{\star}}\beta,

where
D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}
and
D^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{\star}}
are understood in the conic sense.

For the proof of Proposition
1.5
, we consider the standard cut-off functions
\mu_{\varepsilon}
for
X\setminus D
, namely

(1.5.2)

\mu_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\log\log\frac{1}{|s_{D}|_{h}^{2}}\right),

with
s_{D}
the canonical section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
,
h
a smooth metric, and
\rho_{\varepsilon}
a function equal to
1
on
[1,\varepsilon^{-1}]
and equal to
0
on
[1+\varepsilon^{-1},+\infty[
, with
|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\leqslant 2
. As the smooth forms in conic sense are smooth on
X\setminus D
in the classical sense, we have

\int_{X}D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}(\mu_{\varepsilon}\cdot\alpha)\wedge\beta=(-1)^{p+q+1}\int_{X}\mu_{\varepsilon}\cdot\alpha\wedge D^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{\star}}\beta.

Together with the fact that
\int_{X}|\partial\mu_{\varepsilon}|_{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}<+\infty
, by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
1.5.1
).

The standard Gårding and Sobolev inequalities, together with the Rellich embedding theorem, still hold in the conic setting; they can be deduced from their classical counterparts via local uniformizations of
(X,\Delta)
.
As a consequence, the Hodge decomposition also holds in the conic setting.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
CP23
, Thm 2.28]

Let
(L,h_{L})\to X
be a line bundle endowed with a metric
h_{L}
satisfying (
1.0.1
), and let
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
be a Kähler metric with conic singularities as in (
1.0.4
).
Then we have the Hodge decompositions

(1.6.1)

L^{2}_{p,q}(X,L)=\mathrm{Ker}\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{h_{L}}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}^{\star}

and

(1.6.2)

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{p,q}\bigl(X,(L,h_{L})\bigr)=\mathrm{Ker}\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{h_{L}}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{h_{L}}.

Here all differential operators are understood in the conic sense.

Recall that one important method for solving the
\bar{\partial}
-equation and studying the deformation space is the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. The key point is that, unlike the case of
L^{2}
estimates, we do not need strict positivity in order to solve the
\bar{\partial}
-equation. See Sections
4
,
5
and
6
for details on this idea.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.6
, we have the following conic version of the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
(L,h_{L})\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle endowed with a metric
h_{L}
satisfying (
1.0.1
), and let
u
be an
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form which is smooth in conic sense.

•

If
\theta=0
and
u\in\mathrm{Ker}\,\bar{\partial}\cap\mathrm{Im}\,D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}
, then the equation

(1.7.1)

u=\bar{\partial}\circ D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}v

admits a solution
v
which is moreover smooth in conic sense.

•

In general, if
\theta\geqslant 0
, let
u
be an
L
-valued
(n,q)
-form smooth in conic sense, where
n=\dim X
.
If
\bar{\partial}u=0
in the conic sense and

u=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}v_{1}+\theta\wedge v_{2}

in conic sense for some
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,L)
, then
u=\bar{\partial}v
in the conic sense for some
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,L)
which is orthogonal to the harmonic part
\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{h_{L}}
.

The first statement follows directly from Theorem
1.6
and the proof of the classical
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. The second follows from Theorem
1.6
and the Bochner identity in the conic sense; see
[
CP23
, Prop. 2.21]
.

Remark 1.8
.

In (
1.7.1
), the equality only holds in conic sense, and as we have seen, this is not necessarily equivalent to the classical sense. It is therefore important to know when
u
is
\partial\bar{\partial}
-exact in the usual sense. We refer to Theorem
1.11
for a result in this direction.

Motivated by the applications we will present at the end of this survey, we next introduce conic-smooth forms with logarithmic poles.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
D=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}
be an snc divisor on
X
.
Let
L\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle. Assume that
L
admits a possibly singular metric
h_{L}
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum_{D_{i}\subset\Delta_{2}}a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta,

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\theta
is a smooth form on
X
.
Let
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
be a conic Kähler metric with respect to the conic structure
(1-\frac{1}{m})\Delta_{2}
, and let
\pi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}
be the
m
-ramified cover along
\Delta_{2}
.

Let
\phi
be an
L
-valued smooth form on
X\setminus D
. We say that
\phi
has
logarithmic poles along
\Delta_{1}
and is smooth in the conic sense
if

\frac{\pi_{i}^{\star}\phi}{w_{i}^{am}}

extends to a smooth form on
U_{i}
with at most logarithmic poles along the inverse image of
\Delta_{1}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
the space of
L
-valued smooth conic forms with logarithmic poles along
\Delta_{1}
.

We can establish a version of Hodge theory for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
as follows.
On the logarithmic tangent bundle
T_{X}\langle\Delta_{1}\rangle=(\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log\Delta_{1}))^{\star}
, we consider the Hermitian metric

(1.9.1)

g_{D}:=\omega_{\mathcal{C}}+\sqrt{-1}\sum_{D_{i}\subset\Delta_{1}}\frac{D^{\prime}s_{D_{i}}\wedge\overline{D^{\prime}s_{D_{i}}}}{|s_{D_{i}}|^{2}},

where
s_{D_{i}}
is the canonical section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
.
Then
g_{D}
has conic singularities along
\Delta_{2}
and we can use the local generators
(\frac{ds_{D_{i}}}{s_{D_{i}}})^{\star}
for
D_{i}\subset\Delta_{1}
in order to define an almost orthonormal basis on
X\setminus\Delta_{2}
.
Notice that
g_{D}
(twisted with the metric
h_{L}
of
L
) can be interpreted as
singular Hermitian metric
on the bundle

E_{p}:=\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log\Delta_{1})\otimes L.

Let
\pi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}
be the local
m
-ramified cover in (
1.0.3
).
For an
E_{p}
-valued
(0,q)
-form
\phi
,
\pi_{i}^{*}\phi
can be seen as
\pi^{\star}_{i}L
-valued forms on
U_{i}\setminus(\pi_{i})^{-1}(D)
.
We say that
\phi
is smooth in the conic sense if the
\pi^{\star}_{i}L
-valuded form

\frac{\pi_{i}^{\star}\phi}{w_{i}^{am}}\qquad\text{ on }U_{i}\setminus(\pi_{i})^{-1}(D)

extends to a smooth form on
U_{i}
with at most logarithmic poles along the inverse image of
\Delta_{1}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E_{p})
the space of such
E_{p}
-valued
(0,q)
-forms.
Then we have a natural identification

(1.9.2)

\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(p,q)}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E_{p}),

and we see next that the analogue of Theorem
1.6
holds for
(E_{p},g_{D})
(where the ambient metric on
X
is the same
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
).

Theorem 1.10
.

We have the Hodge decomposition

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E_{p})=\mathrm{Ker}\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{g_{D},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{g_{D},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}.

Here all differential operators are understood in the conic sense.

Proof.

The arguments are virtually the same as in Theorem
1.6
. Via the local
m
-ramified cover the inverse image

\frac{\pi_{i}^{\star}\phi}{w_{i}^{am}}

of a form
\phi
becomes a smooth form on
U_{i}
with at most logarithmic poles along the inverse image of
\Delta_{1}
, namely a section of

\frac{\pi_{i}^{\star}\phi}{w_{i}^{am}}\in C^{\infty}(U_{i},\Omega^{p}_{U_{i}}(\log\pi_{i}^{-1}(\Delta_{1}))\otimes L).

Note that
(\pi_{i})^{\star}g_{D}
is a smooth hermitian metric on
\Omega^{p}_{U_{i}}(\log\pi_{i}^{-1}(\Delta_{1}))
: this is the main reason for which we impose the conic singularities in the expression of
g_{D}
. Then the standard Gårding, Sobolev inequalities, and the Rellich embedding theorem still hold in the this setting. From this point on, it is fairly easy to complete the details for the proof of Theorem
1.10
.

∎

Note that in general the metric
g_{D}
is not flat. Therefore we do not have at our disposal a
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E_{p})
even when
\theta=0
.
This is one of the main motivation for establishing a version of Hodge decomposition theorem for currents, discussed in Section
3
.

Given a form
u
which is
\bar{\partial}
-exact in the conic sense, it is important to understand when
u
is also
\bar{\partial}
-exact in the classical sense.
We have the following result in this direction.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
D=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}
be an snc divisor on
X
.
Let
L\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle endowed with a metric
h_{L}
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum_{D_{i}\subset\Delta_{2}}a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta,

where
a_{i}\in[-1,0[\cap\mathbb{Q}
,
D=\sum D_{i}
is snc, and
\theta
is a smooth form.
Let
A^{p,q}(X,D,L)
be space of
L
-valued smooth form with (at most) logarithmic poles along
D
in the classical sense.

Let
u\in A^{p,q}(X,D,L)
. Since
a_{i}<0
, we have
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(p,q)}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
.
Assume that
u
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact in the conic sense, i.e.

u=\bar{\partial}v_{1}\qquad\text{on }X\setminus\Delta_{1}

in the conic sense for some
v_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(p,q-1)}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
.
Then there exists
v\in A^{p,q-1}(X,D,L)
such that

u=\bar{\partial}v\qquad\text{on }X\setminus D.

Proof.

The proof follows the ideas of
[
LRW
]
and
[
CDHP
]
.
Consider the holomorphic vector bundle

E:=\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log D)\otimes L

endowed with a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{E}
, and let
(E^{\star},h_{E}^{\star})
be its dual.
We have a natural identification

\mathcal{I}:A^{p,q}(X,D,L)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E),

and a natural morphism

\iota:C^{\infty}_{(n,n-q)}(X,E^{\star})\longrightarrow C^{\infty}_{(0,n-q)}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{n-p}\otimes L^{\star}\bigr)

induced by contraction.

Since
\bar{\partial}u=0
on
X\setminus D
, we have
\bar{\partial}\mathcal{I}(u)=0
on
X
.
Fix a Kähler metric
\omega_{X}
on
X
and consider the smooth Hodge decomposition with respect to
(X,\omega_{X})
and
(E,h_{E})
.
Since
\mathcal{I}(u)
is a
\bar{\partial}
-closed smooth
E
-valued form, we may write

\mathcal{I}(u)=u_{0}+\bar{\partial}u_{1},

where
u_{0}
is harmonic and
u_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{(0,q-1)}(X,E)
.
Thus it suffices to show that
u_{0}=0
.

Let
\sharp:C^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E)\to C^{\infty}_{(n,n-q)}(X,E^{\star})
be the Hodge star operator.
Since
u_{0}
is harmonic,
\sharp u_{0}
is also harmonic, cf.
[
Dem-book
, Chapter VI, Thm 7.3]
. Then

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{X}u_{0}\wedge\sharp u_{0}=\int_{X}\mathcal{I}(u)\wedge\sharp u_{0}=\int_{X}u\wedge\iota(\sharp u_{0})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{X}\mu_{\varepsilon}\,u\wedge\iota(\sharp u_{0})=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{X}\bar{\partial}\mu_{\varepsilon}\wedge v_{1}\wedge\iota(\sharp u_{0}),

where
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is the standard cut-off functions for
X\setminus D
defined in (
1.5.2
).

We suppose that
z_{i}=0
defines locally
D_{i}
and
w_{i}=0
defines the inverse image of
D_{i}
.
As
v_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(p,q-1)}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
and
-1\leqslant a_{i}<0
,
then near a generic point of
w_{i}=0
the form

d\overline{w}_{i}\wedge\pi^{\star}v_{1}
may have a pole of the form

\frac{d\overline{w}_{i}\wedge dw_{i}}{w_{i}},

but without higher order poles: if not, as
\pi^{\star}v_{1}
is
\pi
-invariant,
d\overline{w}_{i}\wedge\pi^{\star}v_{1}
should have a pole of the form

\frac{d\overline{w}_{i}\wedge dw_{i}}{w_{i}^{1+m}}
. This contradicts with the fact that
v_{1}
is smooth in the conic sense, as the coefficience
a_{i}\geqslant-1
. On the other hand, because of the contraction in
\iota
, the wedge product
\iota(\sharp u_{0})\wedge dz_{i}
is divisible by
z_{i}
. As a consequence, the form

d\overline{w}_{i}\wedge\pi^{\star}v_{1}\wedge\pi^{\star}\big(\iota(\sharp u_{0})\big)

is smooth. Consequently, by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{X}\bar{\partial}\mu_{\varepsilon}\wedge v_{1}\wedge\iota(\sharp u_{0})=0.

Hence
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=0
, so
u_{0}=0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

To end this section, we ask the following question.

Question 1.12
.

Can one establish a strong Hodge decomposition in the setting where
h_{L}
has analytic singularities, or even when the weight of
h_{L}
is the difference of two quasi-psh functions?

In the case where
h_{L}
has analytic singularities, after a modification of
X
, we may assume that the singularities of the metric
h_{L}
are divisorial. However, when the Lelong numbers of

are not rational, it is unclear what the metric on
X
should be.

2.
Hodge theory for logarithmic holomorphic flat line bundles

Let
(X,D)
be a pair consisting of a compact Kähler manifold
X
and an snc divisor
D
. We denote by
M_{\rm B}(X_{0})
the space of rank one local systems on the open manifold
X\setminus D
.
Then the following beautiful result is due to Budur-Wang.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
BW20
]
)
.

Consider the germ of a holomorphic disk
\gamma:(\mathbb{C},0)\to M_{\rm B}(X_{0})
, and fix
\gamma(0)=\tau
. Let
k
be a positive integer so that we have
\displaystyle\dim H^{p}(X\setminus D,\gamma(t))\geqslant k
for every
|t|\ll 1
. Then we have

(2.1.1)

\dim H^{p}(X\setminus D,\tau+t\dot{\alpha})\geqslant k

for
|t|\ll 1
, where
\dot{\alpha}
is the infinitesimal deformation
d\gamma(0)
.

In the statement above, we denote by
H^{\bullet}(X\setminus D,\tau)
the cohomology groups with coefficients in
\tau
.
One of the main objectives in the recent article
[
CDHP
]
is to propose a new proof of this result,
by developing a version of the Hodge theory for rank-one local systems on quasi-compact Kähler manifolds.
The proof of
2.1
is self-contained and differs substantially from the original argument in
[
BW20
]
, which relies on global considerations involving moduli spaces and the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (with some ideas tracing back to
[
Sim93
]
).
We now briefly outline the main ideas in
[
CDHP
]
, a few key statements/facts will be presented more in detail in the next subsections.

Let
M_{\rm DR}(X/D)
be the space of logarithmic flat bundles, i.e. pairs
(L,\nabla)
consisting of line bundles
L\to X
together with a holomorphic connection

\nabla:L\to L\otimes\Omega_{X}(\log D)

with log poles along
D
such that
\nabla^{2}=0
. We have a natural map

(2.1.2)

\rho:M_{\rm DR}(X/D)\to M_{\rm B}(X\setminus D),

which is surjective, by a well-known result due to Deligne, see
[
Del70
, II, 6.10]
. As shown in the same reference, the correspondence between the
two spaces in (
2.1.2
) goes much further: if we consider the complex

(2.1.3)

0\to\mathcal{O}(L)\to\mathcal{O}(L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\to\mathcal{O}(L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{2}(\log D)\to\cdots\mathcal{O}(L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{n}(\log D)\to 0

induced by the connection
\nabla
, then we have

\mathbb{H}^{p}(X,L\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D))\simeq H^{p}(X_{0},\tau),

provided that the residue of
(L,\nabla)
on any component of
D
is not a positive integer. Here
\tau
is the image of
(L,\nabla)
via the map
\rho
.

The strategy adopted in
[
CDHP
]
for the proof of Theorem
2.1
consists in developing the necessary tools in order to construct a resolution of (
2.1.3
). Afterwards ideas from Hodge theory are coming into the picture: in
loc. cit.
one shows that the cohomology of the resulting complex is computed by the kernel of a Laplace-type operator corresponding to
\nabla+\bar{\partial}
. Finally, a version of
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma adapted to the current context is established, and it is one of the main technical tool in the proof.

In order to be able to select "the best" representative in a cohomology class, one first needs to endow the respective bundle/manifold (
L
and
(X,D)
in our context) with metrics which are
geometrically significant. In case of the line bundle
L
, this will be a harmonic metric. Its definition, together with the natural decomposition of the divisor
D
(induced by the component of
M_{\rm B}(X\setminus D)
containing
\tau
) is surveyed in the first two subsections below. The said decomposition of
D
will basically dictate the choice of the ambient metric
\omega_{D}
on
(X,D)
– it will have mixed
conic-Poincaré type singularities. Then everything is in place for stating and proving the Hodge decomposition theorem, see Section
2.2
. The resolution of the complex (
2.1.3
) is constructed in Section
2.3
, and -unsurprisingly- it involves differential forms with log poles, which are not necessarily
L^{2}
with respect to the objects mentioned above. In particular, we cannot apply directly the aforementioned Hodge theory.
Hence the need for considering more general objects, namely
L
-valued currents. Their definition and relevance in our context is discussed in Section
3
.

2.1.
Harmonic metrics and basic Hodge identities

To begin with, we recall the definition of harmonic metrics and the basic Hodge identities for
(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
, as established in the foundational work
[
Sim92
]
.

Set
D_{K}:=\bar{\partial}+\nabla
, where
\bar{\partial}
defines the holomorphic structure of
L
. Then
D_{K}^{2}=0
on
X\setminus D
.
Let
h
be a metric on
L
, smooth on
X\setminus D
and with possible analytic singularities along
D
.
Let
D^{\prime}_{h}
be the
(1,0)
-part of the Chern connection of
(L,h)
, and set

\theta_{0}:=\frac{D^{\prime}_{h}-\nabla}{2}.

Then
\theta_{0}
is a
(1,0)
-form with possible logarithmic poles along
D
.
By construction, the two connections
D^{\prime}_{h}+\bar{\partial}
and
\nabla+(\bar{\partial}-2\overline{\theta}_{0})
preserve the metric
h
.
We can therefore define
D^{\prime\prime}_{K}
as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

We set

(2.2.1)

D^{\prime\prime}_{K}:=\frac{\bar{\partial}+(\bar{\partial}-2\overline{\theta}_{0})}{2}+\frac{\nabla-D^{\prime}_{h}}{2}=\bar{\partial}+(\theta_{0}-\overline{\theta}_{0}),

(2.2.2)

D^{\prime}_{K}:=D^{\prime}_{h}+\theta_{0}+\overline{\theta}_{0}.

and

(2.2.3)

D^{c}_{K}:=D^{\prime\prime}_{K}-D^{\prime}_{K}.

Note that, by construction, the equality

D^{\prime\prime}_{K}+D^{\prime}_{K}=\bar{\partial}+\nabla=D_{K}

holds. Moreover, it turns out that
the crucial Hodge identities are still valid in this setup.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler metric on
X\setminus D
, and let
(D^{\prime}_{K})^{\star}
and
(D^{\prime\prime}_{K})^{\star}
be the formal adjoints of
D^{\prime}_{K}
and
D^{\prime\prime}_{K}
with respect to
(h,\omega)
.
Then

(2.3.1)

(D^{\prime}_{K})^{\star}=i[\Lambda_{\omega},D^{\prime\prime}_{K}]\qquad\text{and}\qquad(D^{\prime\prime}_{K})^{\star}=-i[\Lambda_{\omega},D^{\prime}_{K}].

Given a local system
(L,\nabla)
, in general one cannot construct a Hermitian metric
h
such that
D^{\prime}_{h}=\nabla
. The substitute is the so-called harmonic metric, an important notion which we now recall.

Definition 2.4

(Harmonic metric)
.

We say that
h
is a
harmonic metric
if
(D^{\prime\prime}_{K})^{2}=0
on
X\setminus D
.

The existence of harmonic metrics for higher-rank semisimple local systems is a deep issue; we refer to
[
Sim92
]
for the compact Kähler case and to
[
Moc06
]
for the quasi-projective case.
In our rank-one case, however, the situation is much simpler, thanks to the following caracterisation.

Proposition 2.5
.

A metric
h
is harmonic if and only if
i\Theta_{h}(L)=0
on
X\setminus D
.

The proof of Proposition
2.5
follows from a direct computation, cf.
[
CDHP
, Prop 6.6]

Before discussing the existence of harmonic metrics, we recall the definition of the residue of a rank-one local system
(L,\nabla)
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
, and let
e_{L}
be a local holomorphic frame of
L
.
Assume that
D_{i}
is locally defined by
z_{1}=0
. By compactness of
D_{i}
, there exists a constant
a_{i}
such that

\nabla e_{L}=\frac{a_{i}\,dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\wedge e_{L}+C^{\infty}.

We call
a_{i}
the residue of
(L,\nabla)
along
D_{i}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)
.
Obviously, this definition is independent of the choice of the frame
e_{L}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
. Then

c_{1}(L)=-\sum_{i}\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)\bigr)\,[D_{i}]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q}).

In particular, there always exists a harmonic metric
h
on
(L,\nabla)
.

Proof.

Fix a smooth metric
h_{L}
on
L
. Then
\alpha:=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}-\nabla
is a
(1,0)
-form with logarithmic poles along
D
.
We compute

\bar{\partial}\alpha=[\bar{\partial},\alpha]=[\bar{\partial},D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}-\nabla]=i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)+\sum_{i}\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)\,[D_{i}]=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C}).

Since
i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
, taking real parts yields

c_{1}(L)=-\sum_{i}\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)\bigr)\,[D_{i}]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R}).

Thus we can find a metric
h
on
L
such that

i\Theta_{h}(L)=-\sum_{i}\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)\bigr)\,[D_{i}].

In particular,
h
is harmonic on
(L,\nabla)
.
∎

Note that the harmonic metric is not unique. Indeed, let
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\sum_{i}a_{i}\,c_{1}(D_{i})=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R}).

Then, by the familiar
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma (see however Theorem
3.2
), there exists a function
\phi
such that

dd^{c}\phi=\sum_{i}a_{i}[D_{i}].

Therefore, if
h
is harmonic, then the metric
h\,e^{-\phi}
is also harmonic.

Given a harmonic metric
h
, we have the following important result.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
Sim92
, Section 1]

Consider the Laplacians

\Delta_{K}:=[D_{K},D_{K}^{\star}],\qquad\Delta^{\prime}_{K}:=[D^{\prime}_{K},(D^{\prime}_{K})^{\star}],\qquad\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{K}:=[D^{\prime\prime}_{K},(D^{\prime\prime}_{K})^{\star}].

If
h
is harmonic, then

(2.8.1)

\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{K}=\Delta^{\prime}_{K}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{K}\qquad\text{on }X\setminus D.

Moreover,
D^{\prime\prime}_{K}
,
D^{\prime}_{K}
,
(D^{\prime\prime}_{K})^{\star}
, and
(D^{\prime}_{K})^{\star}
commute with
\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{K}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{K}
.

In the compact Kähler case, as
\Delta_{K}
is a smooth elliptic operator, the Hodge decomposition theorem for
\Delta_{K}
holds true
[
Sim92
, Section 2]
. Together with Proposition
2.8
, this implies the
D^{\prime}_{K}\circ D^{\prime\prime}_{K}
-lemma (
=D_{K}\circ D^{c}_{K}
)
[
Sim92
, Lemma 2.1]
, which is crucial for many applications.
In the quasi-compact Kähler case, one would like to establish an analogous Hodge decomposition theorem for
\Delta_{K}
, and hence a
D_{K}\circ D^{c}_{K}
-lemma. This is the aim of the next subsection.

2.2.
Hodge theory for logarithmic holomorphic flat line bundles

To establish a Hodge decomposition theorem in the quasi-compact, twisted Kähler case, we start by choosing a harmonic metric
h_{L}
on
L
.
Corresponding to the connected component of
M_{B}(X\setminus D)
containing the image
\rho(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
we can associate a decomposition of the divisor
D

(2.8.2)

D=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3},

such that the following hold:

•

\Delta_{2}\subset D
is the union of components
D_{i}
such that there exists a form

s\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(D))
with
\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(s)\neq 0
.

•

\Delta_{1}\subset D\setminus\Delta_{2}
consists of components
D_{i}
such that

\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)=0
.

•

\Delta_{3}:=D\setminus(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})
.

We will not provide a detailed account about this important fact, but refer to
[
CDHP
]
, Propositions 6.11 and 6.12. However, for the comfort of the reader, we list next a few basic properties. There exist a harmonic metric
h_{L}
on
L
such that the following are verified.

(1)

The equality

\mathrm{Res}_{Y}(L,\nabla)=-\nu_{Y}(h_{L})

holds for any component
Y
of
\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{3}
, and the common value above is a rational number.

(2)

For any component
W
of
\Delta_{2}
, we have
\nu(h_{L})\neq-\mathrm{Res}(L,\nabla)
along
W
.

(3)

The equality

H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))=H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta_{2}))

holds, and as a consequence, we have

\mathrm{Res}_{Y}(L,\nabla)=\mathrm{Res}_{Y}(L^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})

for any component
Y
of
\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{3}
, provided that
\rho(L,\nabla)
and
\rho(L^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})
belong to the same connected component of
M_{DR}(X/D)
.

(4)

We can assume that
\mathrm{Re}\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)\in[0,1[
after replacing
(L,\nabla)
by

(L\otimes\textstyle\prod\mathcal{I}_{D_{i}}^{a_{i}},\nabla^{\prime})
, where
\nabla^{\prime}
is the natural flat connection induced by
\nabla
. This comes from the fact that
the map (
2.1.2
) has the following property

\rho(L,\nabla)=\rho\bigl(L\otimes\textstyle\prod\mathcal{I}_{D_{i}}^{a_{i}},\nabla^{\prime}\bigr).

The above discussion can be summarised as follows.

Lemma 2.9
.

We can construct a harmonic metric
h_{L}
on
L
such that the next assertions hold.

•

\nu_{Y}(h_{L})=-\mathrm{Res}_{Y}(L,\nabla)=0
for any component
Y
of
\Delta_{1}
;

•

\nu_{Y}(h_{L})\neq-\mathrm{Res}_{Y}(L,\nabla)
and
\nu_{Y}(h_{L})<0
for any component
Y
of
\Delta_{2}
;

•

\nu_{Y}(h_{L})=-\mathrm{Res}_{Y}(L,\nabla)\in]-1,0[\cap\mathbb{Q}
for any component
Y
of
\Delta_{3}
.

We now define a metric on the base manifold
X
.
Let
\omega_{D}
be a Kähler metric on
X\setminus(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3})
with
Poincaré singularities along the support of
\Delta_{2}
and conic singularities
of angle
2\pi(1-1/k)
along each component of
\Delta_{3}
, where

k\geqslant 3
is a sufficiently large and divisible integer.
In particular,
k\cdot\mathrm{Res}_{\Delta_{3}}(L,\nabla)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Such a metric can be constructed explicitly.
Write
\Delta_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}
and
\Delta_{3}=\sum_{i=m+1}^{N}D_{i}
.
We define

(2.9.1)

\omega_{D}:=\omega_{X}+\sum_{i=m+1}^{N}\sqrt{-1}\,\partial\bar{\partial}|s_{i}|^{2/k}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sqrt{-1}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log\log\frac{1}{|s_{i}|^{2}},

where
\omega_{X}
is a fixed Kähler metric on

, chosen sufficiently positive so that

\omega_{D}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\omega_{X}
.
Here
s_{i}
denotes the canonical section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
with respect to a smooth metric.

Remark 2.10
.

At a first sight, the choice of singularities of
\omega_{D}
may seem artificial/arbitrary. However, in view of the next results we will discuss, this seems to be the right choice.

We now apply the techniques developed in Section 1 on the quasi-compact manifold

X\setminus\Delta_{2}
, with respect to the metric
(L,h_{L},\omega_{D})
.
In particular, all differential operators

D_{K}
,
D_{K}^{\star}
,
\Delta_{K}
, etc. are defined
with respect to the conic structure
(1-\frac{1}{k})\Delta_{3}
.

The main estimate obtained in
[

]
is the following.
For similar considerations (in a simplified context), we refer to the elegant article
[
Biq97
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

[
CDHP
, Thm 7.9]

Let
h_{L}
be the harmonic metric in Lemma
2.9
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any

L
-valued form
u
with compact support in
X\setminus\Delta_{2}
,
smooth in conic sense, the inequality

(2.11.1)

\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{X}\langle\Delta_{K}u,u\rangle\,dV_{\omega_{D}}\geqslant\frac{1}{C}\int_{X}\Bigl(|u|^{2}|\theta_{0}|^{2}+\langle[D_{h_{L}},D_{h_{L}}^{\star}]u,u\rangle\Bigr)\,dV_{\omega_{D}}

holds.

Idea of the proof.

We briefly explain the idea of the proof.
Using the identity
D_{K}=D_{h_{L}}+2\theta_{0}
, we obtain

(2.11.2)

[D_{K},D_{K}^{\star}]=[D_{h_{L}},D_{h_{L}}^{\star}]+4[\theta_{0},\theta^{\star}_{0}]+2[D_{h_{L}},\theta^{\star}_{0}]+2[\theta_{0},D_{h_{L}}^{\star}].

We can show that

[D_{h_{L}}^{\prime},\theta^{\star}_{0}]u=\theta^{\star}_{0}\,\lrcorner\,\nabla^{1,0}_{\mathrm{cov}}u,

where
\nabla^{1,0}_{\mathrm{cov}}u
denotes the
(1,0)
-covariant derivative.
Moreover, using
[
Siu82
]
, we have

\|\nabla^{1,0}_{\mathrm{cov}}u\|^{2}\simeq\|\bar{\partial}u\|^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}^{\star}u\|^{2}.

Combining these three ingredients yields (
2.11.1
).
∎

Now we explain several consequences of Theorem
2.11
.

•

If
u\in L^{2}
and
\Delta_{K}u\in L^{2}
, then it follows from (
2.11.1
) that

\int_{X}\log^{2}|s_{D_{i}}|\,|u|^{2}\,\omega_{D}^{n}<+\infty

for any
D_{i}\subset\Delta_{2}
. Indeed, this is due to the fact that the residues of
\theta_{0}
on
the components of
\Delta_{2}
are non-zero.

•

More generally, we consider the weighted measure

d\mu_{a}:=\prod_{D_{i}\subset\Delta_{2}}\log^{2a_{i}}(|s_{D_{i}}|^{2})\,dV_{\omega_{D}},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then there exist positive constants
C_{i}
such that the inequality

(2.11.3)

\displaystyle C_{0}\int_{X}|u|^{2}\,d\mu_{a}+\int_{X}\langle\Delta_{K}u,u\rangle\,d\mu_{a}\geqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{X}|\theta_{0}|_{\omega_{D}}^{2}|u|^{2}\,d\mu_{a}

\displaystyle{}+C_{2}\int_{X}\langle[D_{h_{L}},D^{\star}_{h_{L}}]u,u\rangle\,d\mu_{a}

holds.

•

Using the two properties above, we can show that for a harmonic form
u
, namely
u\in L^{2}
and
\Delta_{K}u=0
on
X\setminus\Delta_{2}
, we have

(2.11.4)

\int_{X}|\nabla^{k}_{\mathrm{cov}}u|^{2}\,d\mu_{a}<+\infty

for any
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Here
\nabla_{\mathrm{cov}}
denotes the covariant derivative with respect to
(\omega_{D},h_{L})
.

Note that since
\omega_{D}
is complete along
\Delta_{2}
, there are several ways to define smooth forms on a complete metric space.
Motivated by the definition of the Schwartz space on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and by (
2.11.4
), we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.12
.

[
CDHP
, Definition 8.9]

In the setting of this subsection, we denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
the space of
L
-valued forms
u
which are smooth in conic sense on
X\setminus\Delta_{2}
and satisfy

\int_{X}|\nabla^{k}_{\mathrm{cov}}u|^{2}\,d\mu_{a}<+\infty

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for all multi-indices
a=(a_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
.

Here is another important property of the space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
.

Remark 2.13
.

Let
\beta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\bigr)
. By the definition of
\Delta_{2}
, the form
\beta
has only logarithmic poles along
\Delta_{2}
. Then, by Definition
2.12
, if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
, we have

\beta\wedge f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L).

Thanks to Theorem
2.11
and the key conditions in Lemma
2.9
, we obtain an elliptic regularity result for the operator
\Delta_{K}
.
In particular, we have the following statement.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
g\in L^{2}
and
f\in\mathrm{Dom}(\Delta_{K})
satisfy
\Delta_{K}f=g
.
If
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
, then

f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L),\qquad D_{K}f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L),\qquad D_{K}^{\star}f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L).

Finally, the following strong Hodge decomposition theorem is established.

Theorem 2.15
.

[
CDHP
, Thm 8.20]

We have the strong Hodge decomposition

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)=\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta_{K}=\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}^{\star}

in conic sense.
Moreover,
\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}
is finite-dimensional.

Sketch of the proof.

The main idea is as follows.
Since the metric
\omega_{D}
is complete along
\Delta_{2}
,
L^{2}
-Hodge theory
[
Dem-book
]
yields the decomposition

L^{2}_{\bullet}(X,L)=\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}\oplus\overline{\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta_{K}}

in conic sense.
Thus the key point is to show that
\overline{\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta_{K}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta_{K}
.

Assume by contradiction that
\overline{\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta_{K}}\neq\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta_{K}
.
Then there exists a sequence
f_{m}
such that
\|f_{m}\|_{H^{1}}=1
,

\Delta_{K}f_{m}\to 0
, and
f_{m}\perp\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}
.
Passing to a subsequence, we have
f_{m}\rightharpoonup f_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}
,
with
\Delta_{K}f_{0}=0
and
f_{0}\perp\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}
, hence
f_{0}=0
.

On the other hand, on any compact subset
K\Subset X\setminus\Delta_{2}
,
elliptic regularity implies that
f_{m}\to f_{0}
strongly in
H^{1}
.
Near
\Delta_{2}
, Theorem
2.11
together with the relations in Lemma
2.9
shows that
the
L^{2}
-norm of
f_{m}
is arbitrarily small.
Consequently,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\geqslant c
for some constant
c>0
, which is a contradiction.

The finiteness of
\dim\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}
follows from a similar argument.
∎

One of the main consequences of Theorem
2.15
is the following
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
.
If
u\in\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}\cap\mathrm{Ker}\,D_{K}^{c}
on
X\setminus\Delta_{2}
in conic sense, then

u=D_{K}\circ D_{K}^{c}v\qquad\text{on }X\setminus\Delta_{2}

in conic sense, for some
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
.

We conclude with the following question.

Question 2.17
.

Can the above Hodge theory be generalized to the higher-rank case?
This question is already very interesting when
\dim X=1
.

2.3.
Resolution of the Hypercohomology

Given a logarithmic holomorphic flat line bundle
(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
, it induces two natural objects.

First, as we have already mentioned at the beginning of the current section, the logarithmic flat connection
\nabla
induces the complex

(2.17.1)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{2}(\log D)\to\cdots\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{n}(\log D)\to 0.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(X,L\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D))
denote the hypercohomology of (
2.17.1
).

Second, the parallel transport of
\nabla
induces a natural rank-one local system on
X\setminus D
.
Let
M_{\mathrm{B}}(X\setminus D)
be the space of rank-one local systems on
X\setminus D
.
The parallel transport induces a natural map

(2.17.2)

\rho:M_{DR}(X/D)\longrightarrow M_{\mathrm{B}}(X\setminus D).

By
[
Del70
, II, 6.10]
, this map is surjective: given a flat bundle on
X\setminus D
, it can be extended to a holomorphic line bundle on
X
, and the flat connection may acquire simple poles along
D
.
Moreover, the kernel of
\rho
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.
Indeed, given a logarithmic flat bundle
(L,\nabla)
, for any
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
we can construct new logarithmic flat bundles

L\otimes\prod\mathcal{I}_{D_{i}}^{a_{i}}
, and
\nabla
induces a flat connection
\nabla^{\prime}
on

L\otimes\prod\mathcal{I}_{D_{i}}^{a_{i}}
with logarithmic poles along
D
.

At the level of cohomology, we have the following fundamental result due to Deligne.

Theorem 2.18
.

[
Del70
, II, 6.10]

Let
(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
.
If
\mathrm{Res}_{D_{i}}(L,\nabla)\notin\mathbb{N}^{\star}
for every
D_{i}
, then for all
p
,

\mathbb{H}^{p}\bigl(X,L\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D)\bigr)\simeq H^{p}\bigl(X\setminus D,\rho(L,\nabla)\bigr).

In
[
CDHP
]
, we constructed a natural resolution of the complex (
2.17.1
) using the space of smooth forms
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
defined in Definition
2.12
.
Combined with the Hodge decomposition theorem
2.15
, this allows us to compute

H^{\bullet}(X\setminus D,\rho(L,\nabla))
using
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
.
We briefly explain these constructions below.

Let
\omega_{D}
be the Poincaré–conic metric defined in (
2.9.1
), and let

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
be the space defined in Definition
2.12
with respect to
(h_{L},\omega_{D})
.

Note that
h_{L}
and
\omega_{D}
are smooth near a generic point of
\Delta_{1}
.
Thus, for
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
, the form
u
is smooth in the classical sense near a generic point of
\Delta_{1}
.
To study the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D)
, we introduce the following logarithmic conic smooth space, as an analogue of Definition
1.9
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
denote the space of forms locally of the form

\sum_{I\subset J}\frac{dz_{I}}{z_{I}}\wedge f_{I},

where
f_{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
and
\prod_{i\in J}z_{i}=0
locally defines
\Delta_{1}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(\Delta_{1},L)
the sheaf generated by the local sections of

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
.

We then have the following local solvability result.

Theorem 2.20
.

[
CDHP
, Cor 9.9]

Let
h_{L}
be the harmonic in Lemma
2.9
.
Let
U\subset X
be a small Stein open set, and let

f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{s,K}(U,\Delta_{1},L)
satisfy
D_{K}f=0
and
f\in L^{2}(U)
.

(2.20.1)

If
s>n
, there exists
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{s-1,K}(U,\Delta_{1},L)
such that

g\in L^{2}(U)
and
D_{K}g=f
on
U\setminus D
.

(2.20.2)

If
s\leqslant n
, there exists
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{s-1,K}(U,\Delta_{1},L)
such that

g\in L^{2}(U)
and

f-D_{K}g\in H^{0}\bigl(U,\Omega_{X}^{s}(\log D)\otimes L\bigr),\qquad\nabla(f-D_{K}g)=0.

Proof.

The proof relies on an estimate due to A. Fujiki
[
Fuj92
]
together with the elliptic estimate (
2.11.1
).
We refer to
[
CDHP
, Cor 9.9]
for details.
∎

By the choice of the harmonic metric in Lemma
2.9
, we have

\nu(h_{L})<0
on
\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}
and
\nu(h_{L})=0
on
\Delta_{1}
.
Therefore, there is a natural embedding of sheaves

(2.20.1)

i:\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D)\otimes L\subset\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(\Delta_{1},L).

As a consequence of Theorem
2.20
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.21
.

The natural inclusion morphism
i
(
2.20.1
) between the following complexes of sheaves is a quasi-isomorphism:

i:(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D)\otimes L,\nabla)\longrightarrow(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(\Delta_{1},L),D_{K})

In particular, we have an isomorphism

\frac{\mathrm{Ker}\,D_{K}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet+1,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)}{\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet-1,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)}\simeq\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(X,L\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D)).

In the special case

, we obtain the following refinement.

Theorem 2.22
.

If
\Delta_{1}=0
, then the following three spaces are canonically isomorphic:

\mathrm{Ker}\,\Delta_{K}\simeq\frac{\mathrm{Ker}\,D_{K}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet+1,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)}{\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet-1,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)}\simeq\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(X,L\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D)).

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
2.15
and Theorem

.
∎

3.
Hodge decomposition for currents and a regularity lemma

In the previous two sections, we established a Hodge theory for the conic smooth forms
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,L)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
. In particular, we obtained the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma (Theorems
1.7
and
2.16
) for forms in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,L)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
. However, as we already observed in Theorem
2.21
, in order to study the hypercohomology

\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}\bigl(X,L\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log D)\bigr),

we need to consider a larger space, namely
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
.

Thanks to a nice idea due to Noguchi
[
Nog95
]
, one can study
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
by embedding it into the dual of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
, i.e. into the space of currents. There are two key ingredients in this approach. The first is a Hodge decomposition for currents, namely the so-called de Rham–Kodaira decomposition. The second is a regularity lemma, which says that if a logarithmic form is
\bar{\partial}
-exact modulo an order-zero current supported on
\Delta_{1}
, then one can find a logarithmic solution of the
\bar{\partial}
-equation.
In this section we recall part of these ideas, and explain how they are implemented in our general setting.

3.1.
De Rham–Kodaira decomposition and the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma for currents

We first briefly recall the classical de Rham–Kodaira decomposition theorem
[
DK
]
.
Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold endowed with a Kähler metric
\omega_{X}
.
Let
T
be a
(p,q)
-current on
X
, and let
(\xi_{i})_{i}
be an orthonormal basis of harmonic forms of type
(p,q)
.
We define the
harmonic projection
of
T
by

(3.0.1)

\mathcal{H}(T):=\sum_{i}\langle T,\xi_{i}\rangle\,\xi_{i},

where the pairing
\langle T,\xi_{i}\rangle
is given by

(3.0.2)

\langle T,\xi\rangle:=\int_{X}T\wedge\sharp\xi.

Here
\sharp
denotes the Hodge star operator. In particular,
\xi
is harmonic if and only if
\sharp\xi
is harmonic
[
Dem-book
, Chapter VI, (3.18 )]

We define, by duality, the Green operator on currents by

(3.0.3)

\int_{X}\mathcal{G}(T)\wedge\phi:=\int_{X}T\wedge G(\phi),

for any smooth form
\phi
, where
G
is the (smooth) Green operator. Then we have the de Rham–Kodaira decomposition.

Theorem 3.1

(de Rham–Kodaira)
.

We have the decomposition

(3.1.1)

T=\mathcal{H}(T)+\Delta^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr).

If moreover
T
is
\bar{\partial}
-closed, then

(3.1.2)

T=\mathcal{H}(T)+\bar{\partial}T_{1},

where
T_{1}:=\bar{\partial}^{\star}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr)
.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from the smooth Hodge decomposition and the definitions of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
.

For the second statement, note that
\mathcal{H}(T)
is
\bar{\partial}
-closed by (
3.0.1
). Applying
\bar{\partial}
to (
3.1.1
) and using
\bar{\partial}T=0
, we obtain

\Delta^{\prime\prime}\,\bar{\partial}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr)=0.

It follows that
\bar{\partial}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr)
is smooth, and then an integration-by-parts argument yields

\bar{\partial}^{\star}\bar{\partial}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr)=0.

This implies (
3.1.2
).
∎

As an application of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma for currents.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T
be a current which is
\partial
-closed and
\bar{\partial}
-exact (in the sense of currents). Then there exists a current
S
such that

T=\partial\bar{\partial}S.

In particular, if moreover
T
has total degree
1
, then
T=0
.

As a nice application of Theorem
3.2
, we have the following result due to Noguchi
[
Nog95
]
. The projective case is proved in
[
Del71
]
.

Corollary 3.3
.

[
Nog95
]

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold. Let
\alpha\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\bigr)
. Then
\partial\alpha=0
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha
is a holomorphic one form,

\bar{\partial}\alpha
is given by a collection of constant functions on the components of
D
. In particular, this current is
\partial
-closed
and obviously,
\bar{\partial}
-exact.

By the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma (Theorem
3.2
), there exists a current
T
such that

\bar{\partial}\alpha=\partial\bar{\partial}T.

Then
\alpha-\partial T
is a
\bar{\partial}
-closed
(1,0)
-current on
X
.
By elliptic regularity for
\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\star}
, the current
\alpha-\partial T
is smooth, and hence

\alpha-\partial T\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\bigr).

In particular,
\alpha-\partial T
is
\partial
-closed, and therefore so is
\alpha
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

More generally, as proved in
[
Del71
,
Nog95
]
, for any
\alpha\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{k}(\log D)\bigr)
one has
\partial\alpha=0
. The proof uses the above argument together with an induction on the residues of
\alpha
on every stratum of
D
.

Using similar ideas, we can generalize Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
to the settings of Sections
1
and
2
. We first define currents in conic sense.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
L\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle. Assume that
L
admits a (possibly singular) metric
h_{L}
such that

(3.5.1)

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta,

where
D:=\sum D_{i}
is an snc divisor,

, and
\theta
is a smooth
(1,1)
-form on
X
.

Let
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
be a conic Kähler metric as in (
1.0.4
).
An
L
-valued conic current
T
of
(p,q)
-type is an
L
-valued current such that there exist a constant
C>0
and an integer

for which

(3.5.2)

\left|\int_{X}T\wedge\phi\right|^{2}\leqslant C\sum_{s=0}^{d}\sup_{X\setminus Y}\bigl|\nabla^{s}\phi\bigr|^{2}_{h_{L}^{\star},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}

for all
L^{\star}
-valued
(n-p,n-q)

.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}(X,L)

L

).

Example 3.6
.

Let
\Delta_{1}
be an snc divisor such that

Note that, when interpreted as conic current, the derivative

In analogy with the classical de Rham–Kodaira theorem, we can define the Green operator
\mathcal{G}

Theorem 3.7

(de Rham–Kodaira)
.

In the setting of Definition
3.5
, let
T\in\mathcal{D}(X,L)
. Then

T=\mathcal{H}(T)+\Delta^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr).

If moreover
T
is
\bar{\partial}
-closed in conic sense, then

T=\mathcal{H}(T)+\bar{\partial}T_{1},\qquad T_{1}:=\bar{\partial}^{\star}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr).

Theorem 3.8
.

In the setting of Definition
3.5
, let
T\in\mathcal{D}(X,L)
.
If
T
is
\partial
-closed and
\bar{\partial}
-exact in conic current sense, then there exists
S\in\mathcal{D}(X,L)

in conic current sense. In particular, if moreover
T

, then

.

Now let
(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
. We can also define the current space, i.e. the dual space of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
, as follows.

Definition 3.9
.

In the setting of Definition
2.12
, the space
\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)

consists in
L
-valued currents
T
such that there exist a constant
C>0
and positive integers
d,a
for which the inequality

(3.9.1)

\left|\int_{X}T\wedge\phi\right|^{2}\leqslant C\sum_{s=0}^{d}\int_{X}\bigl|\nabla_{\mathrm{cov}}^{s}\phi\bigr|^{2}_{h_{L}^{\star},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\,d\mu_{a}

holds, for all
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L^{\star})
.

The differential
D_{K}
induces an operator op the space of currents. Notice first that
since
h_{L}
is smooth at a generic point of
\Delta_{1}
, we have the inclusion

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\subset\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L).

Definition 3.10
.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{K}

\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)
defined by duality

\int_{X}\mathcal{D}_{K}T\wedge\phi=(-1)^{\bullet}\int_{X}T\wedge D_{K}\phi

for every

and

Then
\mathcal{D}_{K}

\mathcal{D}_{K}:\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)\to\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L).

Notice that we can equally have the operator

by disregarding the current component that may occur.
Then
\mathcal{D}_{K}
coincides with
D_{K}
on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,L)
, but not on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
:
\mathcal{D}_{K}u
may contain a divisorial part supported on
\Delta_{1}
if
u
has a logarithmic pole along
\Delta_{1}
.

As in conic case, we also have a de Rham–Kodaira decomposition and a
\mathcal{D}_{K}\mathcal{D}_{K}^{c}
-lemma for currents in
\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)
.

Theorem 3.11

(de Rham–Kodaira)
.

In the setting of Definition
3.9
, let
T\in\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)
.
Then

T=\mathcal{H}(T)+\Delta_{K}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr).

If moreover
T
is
\mathcal{D}_{K}
-closed, then

T=\mathcal{H}(T)+\mathcal{D}_{K}T_{1},\qquad T_{1}:=\mathcal{D}_{K}^{\star}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(T)\bigr).

Theorem 3.12
.

In the setting of Definition
3.9
, let
T\in\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)
.
If
T
is
\mathcal{D}_{K}
-closed and
\mathcal{D}_{K}^{c}
-exact, then there exists
S\in\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)
such that

T=\mathcal{D}_{K}\circ\mathcal{D}_{K}^{c}\,S.

In particular, if moreover
T
has total degree
1
, then
T=0
.

3.2.
Regularity lemma

In this subsection, we address another issue mentioned in the introduction to this section. Namely, given a logarithmic form
u
, if
u
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact in the sense of currents modulo a divisorial part, then
u
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact in the logarithmically smooth sense.
This was proved in
[
LRW
]
in the classical setting and was generalized to the current space
\mathcal{D}_{K}(X,L)
in
[
CDHP
]
. To explain the idea, we sketch here the proof in the conic current setting.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
D=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}
be an snc divisor on
X
.
Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
endowed with a possibly singular metric
h_{L}
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum_{D_{i}\subset\Delta_{2}}a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta

for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, where
\theta
is a smooth form.
Let
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(p,q)}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
be such that

(3.13.1)

u=\bar{\partial}T+T_{\Delta_{1}}

in the conic current sense, where
T
is a conic current and
T_{\Delta_{1}}
is a order-zero conic current supported on
\Delta_{1}
.
Then there exists
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(p,q-1)}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
such that

u=\bar{\partial}v\qquad\text{on }X\setminus\Delta_{1}

in the conic sense.

Proof.

Set

E:=\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log\Delta_{1})\otimes L,

which is a holomorphic vector bundle. Let
g_{D}
be the Hermitian metric on
E
induced by (
1.9.1
).
Let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E)
be the space introduced before Theorem
1.10
.
As explained there, we have an identification

\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{p,q}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0,q}(X,E).

Let
(E^{\star},g_{D}^{\star})
be the dual bundle with the dual metric, which similarly gives rise to the space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,q}(X,E^{\star})
.
We also introduced the natural map

\iota:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,q}(X,E^{\star})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n-p,n-q}(X,L^{\star}),

obtained by contraction with vector fields. One checks that

(3.13.2)

\int_{X}\mathcal{I}(u)\wedge s\;=\;\int_{X}u\wedge\iota(s)

for every
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{p,q}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
and
s\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,q}(X,E^{\star})
.

We remark that (
3.13.1
) implies
\bar{\partial}\mathcal{I}(u)=0
in the conic sense.
Therefore, the Hodge decomposition theorem (Theorem
1.10
) yields

(3.13.3)

\mathcal{I}(u)=\mathcal{H}+\bar{\partial}v_{1},

where
\mathcal{H}
is
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
-harmonic and
v_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q-1)}(X,E)
.

We claim that
\mathcal{H}=0
. Introduce the following antilinear operator

\sharp:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(n,n-q)}(X,E^{\star})

defined by the property that

\int_{X}\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{g_{D}}\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}=\int_{X}s_{1}\wedge\sharp s_{2}

for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(X,E)
.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is harmonic, we have

(3.13.4)

\bar{\partial}(\sharp\mathcal{H})=0.

Moreover,

(3.13.5)

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\mathcal{H}|^{2}\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle\mathcal{I}(u),\mathcal{H}\rangle\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}=\int_{X}\mathcal{I}(u)\wedge\sharp\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}u\wedge\iota(\sharp\mathcal{H}),

where the last equality follows from (
3.13.2
). Using (
3.13.1
), we obtain

(3.13.6)

\int_{X}u\wedge\iota(\sharp\mathcal{H})=\int_{X}(\bar{\partial}T+T_{\Delta_{1}})\wedge\iota(\sharp\mathcal{H}).

Note that
\iota(\sharp\mathcal{H})
is obtained by contraction with
T_{X}\langle\Delta_{1}\rangle
. Since
T_{\Delta_{1}}
has order
0
, we have

\int_{X}T_{\Delta_{1}}\wedge\iota(\sharp\mathcal{H})=0.

Therefore,

(3.13.7)

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\mathcal{H}|^{2}\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\bar{\partial}T\wedge\iota(\sharp\mathcal{H})=(-1)^{\bullet}\int_{X}T\wedge\bar{\partial}\bigl(\iota(\sharp\mathcal{H})\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\bullet}\int_{X}T\wedge\iota\bigl(\bar{\partial}(\sharp\mathcal{H})\bigr)=0,

where the last equality follows from (
3.13.4
). Hence
\mathcal{H}=0
.

Consequently, (
3.13.3
) gives
\mathcal{I}(u)=\bar{\partial}v_{1}
for some
v_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(0,q-1)}(X,E)
. Setting
v:=\mathcal{I}^{-1}(v_{1})\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{(p,q-1)}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
, we obtain

u=\bar{\partial}v\qquad\text{on }X\setminus\Delta_{1}

in the conic sense.
∎

Now, combining Theorem
1.11
and Lemma
3.13
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
D=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}
be an snc divisor on
X
.
Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
endowed with a possibly singular metric
h_{L}
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum_{D_{i}\subset\Delta_{2}}a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta

for some
a_{i}\in[-1,0[\cap\mathbb{Q}
, where
\theta
is a smooth form.
Let
A^{p,q}(X,D,L)
be the space of
L
-valued smooth form with logarithmic poles along
D
in the classical sense.

Let
u\in A^{p,q}(X,D,L)
. Then

u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\subset\mathcal{D}(X,L).

Moreover, if

u=\bar{\partial}T+T_{\Delta_{1}}

in the conic current space
\mathcal{D}(X,L)
, where
T\in\mathcal{D}(X,L)
and
T_{\Delta_{1}}\in\mathcal{D}(X,L)
is a order-zero current supported on
\Delta_{1}
, then there exists
a
\beta\in A^{p,q-1}(X,D,L)
such that

Lemma
3.13
can be generalized to the space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
with respect to the operator

. More precisely, we proved the following regularity lemma.

Lemma 3.15
.

[
CDHP

In the setting of Definition
2.12

such that

(3.15.1)

where

3.3.
A log canonical vanishing theorem

Finally, as an application of the theory established above, we prove the following vanishing theorem, which will be very useful.

Theorem 3.16
.

[
CP23
, Thm 1.1]

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and let
D=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}
be an snc divisor on
X
.
Let
s_{\Delta_{1}}
be the canonical section of
\Delta_{1}
.
Let
(L,h_{L})
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum_{D_{i}\subset\Delta_{2}}a_{i}[D_{i}]+\theta,

where
a_{i}\in]0,1[\cap\mathbb{Q}
and
\theta\geqslant 0
is a smooth semipositive form.

Let
\lambda
be a
\bar{\partial}
-closed smooth
(n,q)
-form with values in
L+\Delta_{1}
. If there exist two
L
-valued forms
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
with logarithmic poles along
D
such that

(3.16.1)

\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\beta_{1}+\theta\wedge\beta_{2}\qquad\text{on }X\setminus D,

then
\lambda
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact, i.e.

[\lambda]=0\in H^{q}\bigl(X,K_{X}+L+\Delta_{1}\bigr).

We present here a proof which is slightly different from the original one. In the original proof, the key lemma
[
CP23
, Lemma 4.3]
is proved by induction together with properties of the Green operator and Hodge decomposition. Here we give a different proof of
[
CP23
, Lemma 4.3]
by using the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma on every stratum of
\Delta_{1}
. This is the idea used in
[
CDHP
, Section 10.2]
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{(0,n-q)}\bigl(X,-L-\Delta_{1}\bigr)
be a test form with compact support in
X\setminus Y
.
Let
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
be a conic metric associated with
(L,h_{L})
, and equip
\Delta_{1}
with an arbitrary smooth metric
h_{1}
.
By Theorem
1.6
, we have a conic Hodge decomposition for
(L+\Delta_{1},h_{L}\cdot h_{1},\omega_{\mathcal{C}})
:

(3.16.2)

\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2},\qquad\varphi_{1}\in\mathrm{Ker}\,\bar{\partial},\quad\varphi_{2}\in(\mathrm{Ker}\,\bar{\partial})^{\perp}.

We claim that

(3.16.3)

\int_{X}\lambda\wedge\varphi_{1}=0

and that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.16.4)

\int_{X}|\varphi_{2}|^{2}\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}\leqslant C\int_{X}|\bar{\partial}\varphi_{2}|^{2}\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}=C\int_{X}|\bar{\partial}\varphi|^{2}\,\omega_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}.

Once (
3.16.3
) and (
3.16.4
) are established, standard functional analytic arguments imply that
[\lambda]=0\in H^{q}(X,K_{X}+L+\Delta_{1})
.

The estimate (
3.16.4
) follows from the closedness of
\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta^{\prime\prime}
in the conic Hodge decomposition theorem
1.6
.
We now explain the proof of (
3.16.3
).

Suppose
\Delta_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}
. Set

E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}:=E_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap E_{i_{k}},

and let
\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
denote the residue on the stratum
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
.
Taking residues in (
3.16.1
) yields

(3.16.5)

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\beta_{1})+\theta\wedge\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\beta_{2})\qquad\text{on }E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}},

which is an
L
-valued conic current on
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
.

We claim that for every multi-index
(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})
,

(3.16.6)

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)=\bar{\partial}R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}+\sum_{j}[R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}],

where
R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
are order-zero currents on
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
, orthogonal to the harmonic forms on
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
, and satisfying the skew-symmetry relations. Here, we say that a
L
-valued coninc current
T
on
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
is orthogonal to the harmonic part, if

\int_{E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}}T\wedge\xi=0

for every
L^{\star}
-valued harmonic form
\xi
on
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
.

We prove this claim by induction, starting from the deepest stratum. First,

\mathrm{Res}_{1,\dots,m}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\mathrm{Res}_{1,\dots,m}\beta_{1}+\theta\wedge\mathrm{Res}_{1,\dots,m}\beta_{2}.

The left-hand side is
\bar{\partial}
-closed. By the Bochner identity, the right-hand side is orthogonal to the harmonic part. Hence, by Theorem
1.7
,

\mathrm{Res}_{1,\dots,m}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)=\bar{\partial}R_{1,\dots,m},

for some
R_{1,\dots,m}
on
E_{1,\dots,m}
orthogonal to the harmonic part.

Assume by induction that (
3.16.6
) holds at level
k
. For level
k-1
,

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\mathrm{Res}_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)=\sum_{j}[\bar{\partial}R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}],

where the last equality uses the skew-symmetry relations. Therefore the conic current

(3.16.7)

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)-\sum_{j}[R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}]

is
\bar{\partial}
-closed. Moreover, we can show that both two terms in (
3.16.7
) are orthogonal to the harmonic forms on
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}
as follows:

By using (
3.16.5
), the first term of (
3.16.7
) is orthogonal to the harmonic forms. To treat the second term of (
3.16.7
), we use the following fact. As
\theta\geqslant 0
, by using Bochner equality, we can show that if
\xi
is a
L^{\star}
-valued
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
-harmonic
(0,q)
on
E_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}
, the restriction
\xi|_{E_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}}
is still
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
-harmonic.

Consequently,
\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)-\sum_{j}[R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}]
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact and we can write

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\right)=\bar{\partial}R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}+\sum_{j}[R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}],

for some
R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}
orthogonal to the harmonic forms; moreover, the skew-symmetry relations can be arranged. This completes the induction.

Applying (
3.16.6
) with
k=0
, we conclude that
\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact in the conic current sense modulo a order-zero current supported on
\Delta_{1}
. Therefore,

\int_{X}\lambda\wedge\varphi_{1}=\int_{X}\frac{\lambda}{s_{\Delta_{1}}}\wedge\bigl(s_{\Delta_{1}}\cdot\varphi_{1}\bigr)=0,

which proves (
3.16.3
).
∎

4.
Applications, I: extension of the pluri-canonical forms

In the rest of this article, we discuss several applications of the twisted Hodge theory established above. In this section, we work in the following setting.

Let
p:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic family of smooth compact manifolds over the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. We assume that the central fiber, denoted by
X
, is Kähler. We denote by
K_{\mathcal{X}}
the canonical bundle of
\mathcal{X}
, and let
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
be an arbitrary holomorphic line bundle.

We are interested in extension properties of global sections of the adjoint bundle
K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{L}
defined over the
k^{\rm th}
infinitesimal neighborhood of the central fiber of
p
, where
k\geqslant 1
is a positive integer. In other words, we consider the sheaves

(4.0.1)

\mathcal{F}_{k}:=(K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{L})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}/t^{k+1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},

and the corresponding spaces of sections
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k})
. Here
t
is the coordinate on
\mathbb{D}
. The main question is as follows.

Question 4.1
.

Given
s\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k})
, can we extend
s
to the
(k+1)^{\rm th}
infinitesimal neighborhood of the central fiber
X
? In other words, does there exist
\widehat{s}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k+1})
such that
\pi_{k}(\widehat{s})=s
, where
\pi_{k}
is the natural projection

(4.1.1)

\pi_{k}:\mathcal{F}_{k+1}\to\mathcal{F}_{k}?

We first consider the case

\mathcal{L}=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}},

where
m\geqslant 1
is a positive integer. Set
L:=(m-1)K_{X}
. Then the restriction of
s
to the central fiber
X
induces a metric on
L
, denoted by
h_{L}
. Let
\varphi_{L}
be the weight of
h_{L}
. Then

\varphi_{L}\sim\frac{m-1}{m}\log|s|_{X}|^{2}.

The following statement is the main result of
[
CP20
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
CP20
, Thm 1.1]

Let
s\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k})
with
\mathcal{L}=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
. Assume that
s
admits a
C^{\infty}
extension
s_{k}
such that, writing

\bar{\partial}s_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k},

the integral

(4.2.1)

\int_{X}\left|\frac{\Lambda_{k}}{dt}\right|^{2}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{L}}\,dV<+\infty

converges for every
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists a section
\widehat{s}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k+1})
such that
\pi_{k}(\widehat{s})=s
.

One of the key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
4.2
is the log vanishing Theorem
3.16
. We now sketch the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Sktech of the proof.

Using a partition of unity, we can always construct a
C^{\infty}
extension
s_{k}
such that
\bar{\partial}s_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}
for some smooth form
\Lambda_{k}
.
Set
\lambda_{k}:=(\Lambda_{k}/dt)|_{X}
. Since
\Lambda_{k}
is
\bar{\partial}
-closed,
\lambda_{k}
is also
\bar{\partial}
-closed. To prove the theorem, it suffices to show that
\lambda_{k}
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact.

Note that
s_{k}
induces a Lie derivative as follows. The section
s_{k}
defines an operator

D^{\prime}:C^{\infty}_{p,q}(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathrm{Div}(s_{k}),\mathcal{L})\to C^{\infty}_{p+1,q}(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathrm{Div}(s_{k}),\mathcal{L}),

locally given on a chart
\Omega
by

D^{\prime}|_{\Omega}=\partial-\frac{m-1}{m}\frac{\partial s_{k}}{s_{k}}\wedge\cdot.

We fix a horizontal vector field
\Xi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{X},T_{\mathcal{X}})
such that
p_{\ast}(\Xi)=\partial/\partial t
. We then define the Lie derivative by

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}:=[D^{\prime},\Xi].

Thus,

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}:C^{\infty}_{n+1,q}(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathrm{Div}(s_{k}),\mathcal{L})\to C^{\infty}_{n+1,q}(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathrm{Div}(s_{k}),\mathcal{L}).

Applying
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}

(k+1)
times to the equation
\bar{\partial}s_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}
, one can show that

\lambda_{k}=\bar{\partial}\alpha+D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\beta\qquad\text{on }X\setminus(s=0),

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are forms that are meromorphic along
s=0
(possibly with poles of higher order).

After desingularization and using (
4.2.1
), we may assume that:

•

The support of
\mathrm{Div}(s)
can be written as
D+E
, where
D+E
is snc, with
D=\sum D_{i}
and
E=\sum E_{i}
.

•

\lambda_{k}
is an
(F+E)
-valued
(n,1)
-form, where
F\equiv\sum\delta_{i}D_{i}
for
\delta_{i}\in]0,1[\cap\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
h_{F}
be a metric on
F
such that

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_{h_{F}}(F)=\sum\delta_{i}[D_{i}].

Using the singularities of
h_{F}
, we can eliminate the higher-order poles of
\alpha
and
\beta
. We obtain the following statement.

There exist forms
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}
of types
(n,0)
and
(n-1,1)
, respectively, with values in
F
and with logarithmic poles along
E
, such that

\bar{\partial}\alpha_{1}+D^{\prime}_{h_{F}}\beta_{1}=\frac{\lambda_{k}}{s_{E}}\qquad\text{on }X\setminus E.

By Theorem
3.16
, it follows that
\lambda_{k}
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact.
∎

Remark 4.3

Corollary 4.5

[

Let

Then any section

5.
Applications, II: cohomology jumping loci property.

In this section, we discuss another type of extension problem: the base manifold is fixed while the line bundle varies. In this setting, the slogan is that extension to first order implies extension to a genuine neighborhood. To begin, we recall a classical result.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with a holomorphic line bundle
(L,\bar{\partial}_{L})
, and let
\alpha
be a smooth
\bar{\partial}
-closed
(0,1)
-form on
X
. For each
t\in(\mathbb{C},0)
, consider the twisted
\bar{\partial}
-operator

(5.0.1)

\bar{\partial}_{t}:=\bar{\partial}_{L}+t\alpha

acting on the space of smooth
L
-valued
(p,q)
-forms on
X
.
A natural problem is to study how the finite-dimensional spaces

(5.0.2)

\mathrm{Ker}\,(\bar{\partial}_{t})\big/\mathrm{Im}\,(\bar{\partial}_{t})

vary with
t
. General results (see
[
Kod86
]
) ensure that the dimension of the spaces in (
5.0.2
) defines an upper semicontinuous function of
t
.

A starting point for results of this type is due to Green–Lazarsfeld
[
GL87
,
GL91
]
.
Let
u\in\mathrm{Ker}\,\bar{\partial}_{L}
. We say that
u

extends to first order in the direction
\alpha
if the wedge product
u\wedge\alpha
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact, i.e. if we can solve

(5.0.3)

\bar{\partial}v=u\wedge\alpha.

Following
[
GL87
, §1]
, we have the following extension property.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
(L,\bar{\partial}_{L})
be the trivial line bundle.
Let
\alpha
be a smooth
\bar{\partial}
-closed
(0,1)
-form on
X
.
Let
u
be a smooth
\bar{\partial}
-closed
(p,q)
-form on
X
which extends to first order in the direction
\alpha
.
Then there exists a smooth family
(u_{t})_{t\in(\mathbb{C},0)}
of
(p,q)
-forms such that

(5.1.1)

u_{0}=u,\qquad u_{t}\in\mathrm{Ker}\,\bar{\partial}_{t}.

In other words, extension to first order implies extension to a genuine neighborhood.

Proof.

We aim to solve the following system:

(5.1.2)

\begin{cases}\bar{\partial}(u_{1})+\alpha\wedge u=0,\\
\bar{\partial}(u_{2})+\alpha\wedge u_{1}=0,\\
\ldots\\
\bar{\partial}(u_{i+1})+\alpha\wedge u_{i}=0,\\
\ldots\end{cases}

Note that
u
extends to first order along
\alpha
if and only if the first equation in (
5.1.2
) can be solved.
Similarly,
u
extends to arbitrary order if and only if all equations in (
5.1.2
) can be solved.

Let
u_{0}
be a harmonic representative of
u
. Since the first equation in (
5.1.2
) is solvable by assumption, we have

\alpha\wedge u_{0}\in\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}\cap\mathrm{Ker}\,\partial.

By the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma, there exists
u_{1}\in\mathrm{Im}\,\partial
such that

\bar{\partial}(u_{1})+\alpha\wedge u_{0}=0.

To solve the second equation, note that since
u_{1}\in\mathrm{Im}\,\partial
, we have
\alpha\wedge u_{1}\in\mathrm{Im}\,\partial
. Moreover,

\bar{\partial}(\alpha\wedge u_{1})=-\alpha\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{1}=\alpha\wedge\alpha\wedge u_{0}=0.

Hence
\alpha\wedge u_{1}\in\mathrm{Im}\,\partial\cap\mathrm{Ker}\,\bar{\partial}
.
By the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma again, there exists
u_{2}\in\mathrm{Im}\,\partial
such that

\bar{\partial}(u_{2})+\alpha\wedge u_{1}=0.

The same argument applies inductively to the remaining equations.

Finally, if each
u_{i}
is chosen as the minimal solution of (
5.1.2
)
1
1

1

It is then automatically
\partial
-exact by the Hodge decomposition theorem.
,
one can prove convergence of the series
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}z^{i}u_{i}
for
|z|\ll 1
.
In particular,
u
extends to a genuine neighborhood.
∎

As an application of Theorem
5.1
, we have the following jumping locus property.

Theorem 5.2
.

[
GL87
,
GL91
]

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold. Define the jumping locus

\Sigma^{p,q}_{k}(X):=\{L\in\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)\mid\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}\otimes L)\geqslant k\}.

Then
\Sigma^{p,q}_{k}(X)
is a finite union of translates of subtori in
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
.

Theorem
5.2
has been generalized in several directions. In the compact Kähler setting,
[
Sim93
]
shows moreover that
\Sigma^{p,q}_{k}(X)
is in fact a finite union of torsion translates of subtori in
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
. More recently,
[
BW15
,
BW20
]
generalize Theorem
5.2
to the quasi-compact Kähler case. Their proof relies on global considerations involving moduli spaces and the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (with some ideas tracing back to
[
Sim93
]
).

In
[
CDHP
]
, using Hodge theory for rank-one local systems on quasi-compact Kähler manifolds, we give a new proof of the jumping locus property in the quasi-compact case. The idea is to use the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma as in Theorem
5.1
. Thus our proof is self-contained and differs substantially from the original arguments in
[
BW15
,
BW20
]
. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 5.3
.

[
BW20
,
CDHP
]

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{k}D_{i}
be a simple normal crossing divisor. Then the jumping locus
\Sigma_{k}^{i}(X\setminus D)_{\rm B}
is a finite union of translates of subtori.

Remark 5.4
.

Conjecturally,
\Sigma_{k}^{i}(X\setminus D)_{\rm B}
is a finite union of torsion translates of subtori. This is proved in the quasi-projective case in
[
BW15
]
.

We now briefly outline the main idea of the proof of Theorem
5.3
. First, by analogy with the extension theorem (Theorem
5.1
), we prove an extension theorem for logarithmic holomorphic flat line bundles.

Theorem 5.5
.

In the setting of Section
2.2
, set
\Delta_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}Y_{i}
.
Let
(L,\nabla)\in M_{DR}(X/D)
and let

(\xi,\beta)\in\mathcal{H}^{0,1}\oplus H^{0}\bigl(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\bigr)=(T_{M_{DR}(X/D)})_{(L,\nabla)}.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
such that

D_{K}u_{0}=0\qquad\text{on }X\setminus D.

Suppose that the following hold.

•

(1) The form
u_{0}
extends to first order, i.e. there exists
v_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
such that

D_{K}v_{1}=-(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{0}\qquad\text{on }X\setminus D.

•

(2) For every non-empty
I\subset\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, any
D_{K}
-closed form in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet-|I|,K}(Y_{I},L)
extends to first order; that is, for any
s\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet-|I|,K}(Y_{I},L)
, if
D_{K}s=0
, then
-\iota_{I}^{*}(\xi+\beta)\wedge s
is
D_{K}
-exact on
Y_{I}
.
Here
Y_{I}:=\cap_{i\in I}Y_{i}
and
\iota_{I}:Y_{I}\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion map.

Then for any
k\geqslant 0
we can solve

(5.5.1)

\begin{cases}D_{K}(u_{1})=-(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{0},\\
D_{K}(u_{2})=-(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{1},\\
\ldots\\
D_{K}(u_{k+1})=-(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{k},\end{cases}

on
X\setminus D
, with
u_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)\cap\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}
, and such that the series
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}t^{i}u_{i}
converges for
|t|\ll 1
.

Sketch of proof.

First assume that
\Delta_{1}=0
.

Let
u_{0}
be a
D_{K}
-closed form with values in
L
. After changing the representative, we may assume that
u_{0}
is harmonic. Then
D_{K}^{c}u_{0}=0
.
By the hypothesis
(1)
, we have

(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{0}\in\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}\cap\mathrm{Ker}\,D_{K}^{c}.

By the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma
2.16
, there exists
u_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,L)\cap\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}^{c}
such that

(5.5.2)

D_{K}u_{1}=-(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{0}.

Now we consider the second equation in (
5.5.1
).
By Remark
2.13
,
(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet+1,K}(X,L)
.
Since
u_{1}
is
D_{K}^{c}
-exact,
(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{1}
is also
D_{K}^{c}
-exact. Moreover,
(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{1}
is
D_{K}
-closed, because
D_{K}u_{1}
is a multiple of
(\xi+\beta)
.
By the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma
2.16
, there exists
u_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,L)\cap\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}^{c}
such that

(5.5.3)

D_{K}u_{2}=-(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{1}.

Iterating this procedure, we obtain successively

D_{K}u_{m}=-(\xi+\beta)\wedge u_{m-1},\qquad u_{m}\in\mathrm{Im}\,D_{K}^{c},

for all

, i.e.
u_{0}
extends to arbitrary order.

In the general case where
\Delta_{1}
is not necessarily

, we first embed
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\bullet,K}(X,\Delta_{1},L)
into the current space

and induction on every stratum of

Finally, thanks to the regularity lemma

) in

. We refer to
[

, Section 10]
for details.
∎

.

Let
(L,\nabla)\in\Sigma_{k}^{i}(X/D)_{\rm DR}

\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\mathrm{Res}_{D_{j}}(L,\nabla)\bigr)\in[0,1[

for every irreducible component

of

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let

be a simple normal crossing divisor.
Let

\Sigma_{k}^{i}(X\setminus D)_{\rm B}:=\{\tau\in M^{\prime}_{\rm B}(X/D)\mid\dim H^{i}(X\setminus D,\tau)\geqslant k\}

Proof.

Pick a general element
\tau\in\Sigma_{k}^{i}(X\setminus D)_{\rm B}
.
Let
(L,\nabla)\in M_{\rm DR}(X/D)

6.
Applications, III: deformation of log Calabi–Yau manifolds

In this last section, we would like to present a nice application of conic Hodge theory to the deformation theory of log Calabi–Yau Kähler manifolds, proved recently by R. Zhang
[
Zha24
]
.

In
[
KKP
]
, L. Katzarkov, M. Kontsevich, and T. Pantev proved that the locally trivial deformations of a log Calabi–Yau projective pair
(X,D)
are unobstructed. In his recent paper
[
Zha24
]
, using conic Hodge theory, R. Zhang generalized the theorem of Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev to the Kähler setting. More precisely, R. Zhang proved the following result.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
Zha24
, Thm A]

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let

D=\sum_{i=1}^{s}D_{i}

be a simple normal crossing divisor on
X
. Assume that there exists a collection of
weights
\{a_{i}\}_{1\leqslant i\leqslant s}\subset[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that

(6.1.1)

c_{1}(K_{X})+\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}c_{1}(D_{i})=0\quad\text{in }H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q}).

Then the locally trivial (infinitesimal) deformations of the pair
(X,D)
are unobstructed. In particular, the pair
(X,D)
admits a semi-universal (Kuranishi) family over a smooth base.

Note that some special cases of Theorem
6.1
are proved in
[
LRW
,
Wan18
]
.
As explained in
[
Zha24
]
and
[
Wan18
]
, Theorem
6.1
can be derived from the following vanishing theorem.

Theorem 6.2
.

[
Zha24
, Thm B]

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
D=\sum D_{i}
be a simple normal crossing divisor on
X
.
Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
such that

c_{1}(L)=\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})

for some
a_{i}\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
h_{L}
be a metric on
L
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}=\sum a_{i}[D_{i}].

Let
h_{L^{\star}}
be the dual metric on
L^{\star}
.
Let
\alpha\in A^{p,q}\bigl(X,D,L^{\star}\bigr)
satisfy

\bar{\partial}D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}\alpha=0\quad\text{on }X\setminus D.

Then there exists

\chi\in A^{p,q-1}\bigl(X,D,L^{\star}\bigr)

such that

(6.2.1)

\bar{\partial}\chi=D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}\alpha\quad\text{on }X\setminus D.

The original proof of Theorem
6.2
uses conic Hodge decomposition theory together with J. King’s theory
[
Kin71
,
Kin83
]
on the sheaf of currents annihilating null forms on
D
. Here we present a slightly different approach, replacing King’s theory with the regularity lemma Corollary
3.14
.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

We decompose

D=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},

where
\Delta_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}
consists of the components such that
a_{i}=0
, and
\Delta_{2}
consists of the components such that
a_{i}>0
.
Then
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,\Delta_{1},L^{\star})
. By Example
3.6
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}(X,L^{\star})
can be viewed as an
L^{\star}
-valued current in the conic sense.

We consider the conic current

T:=D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}\alpha\in\mathcal{D}(X,L^{\star}).

Note that for every subset
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
, we have

(6.2.2)

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}T=D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\alpha\quad\text{on }D_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap D_{i_{k}}.

We claim that for every subset
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
, there exist conic currents
R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
(resp.
R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
) supported on
D_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap D_{i_{k}}
of order
0
(resp.
D_{j}\cap D_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap D_{i_{k}}
), satisfying the skew-symmetry relations, such that

(6.2.3)

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}T=\bar{\partial}D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}[D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}]\in\mathcal{D}(D_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap D_{i_{k}},L^{\star}).

We prove the claim by induction on
k
, starting from
k=m
.

For
k=m
, by (
6.2.2
), we have

\mathrm{Res}_{1,\dots,m}T=D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}\mathrm{Res}_{1,\dots,m}\alpha

in the sense of conic currents on
D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{m}
.
The left-hand side is
\bar{\partial}
-exact, and the right-hand side is
D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}
-exact. By the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma for conic currents (Lemma
3.8
), we obtain

\mathrm{Res}_{1,\dots,m}T=\bar{\partial}D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{1,\dots,m}

for some current
R_{1,\dots,m}
on
D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{m}
of order
0
, which can be chosen to satisfy the skew-symmetry relations. Thus, the claim holds for
k=m
.

Assume by induction that (
6.2.3
) holds for
k
. For the
(k-1)
-index case, we have

\bar{\partial}\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}T=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{Res}_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}T

=\sum_{j=1}^{m}[\bar{\partial}D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}]+\sum_{s,j=1}^{m}[D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{s,j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}]=\sum_{j=1}^{m}[\bar{\partial}D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}],

where the last equality follows from the skew-symmetry of
R_{s,j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}
.

Thus, the conic current

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}T-\sum_{j=1}^{m}[D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}]

is
\bar{\partial}
-closed. Moreover, by (
6.2.2
), it is
D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}
-exact.
The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma for conic currents (Lemma
3.8
) then implies that it is
\partial\bar{\partial}
-exact.
Hence, there exist order zero currents
R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}
on
D_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap D_{i_{k-1}}
such that

\mathrm{Res}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}T=\bar{\partial}D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}[D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{j,i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}].

We may further require that
R_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}}
satisfy the skew-symmetry relations.
This completes the induction and proves the claim.

Applying the claim (
6.2.3
) for

, we find conic currents
R_{i}
supported on
D_{i}
of order
0
such that

T-\sum_{i=1}^{m}[D^{\prime}_{h_{L^{\star}}}R_{i}]

is
\partial\bar{\partial}
-exact on

in the conic current sense.
By the regularity lemma (Corollary
3.14
), the theorem follows.

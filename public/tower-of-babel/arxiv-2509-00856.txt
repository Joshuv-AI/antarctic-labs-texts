On dissipation operators of Quantum Optics


Abstract
We consider dissipation operators used in Quantum Optics for the description of quantum spontaneous emission in the context of damped driven Jaynes–Cummings equations. The equations describe quantised one-mode Maxwell field coupled to a two-level molecule. We study properties of two basic dissipation operators of Quantum Optics in the framework of the theory of the Hilbert space of the Hilbert-Schmidt operators. We prove that one of the operators is symmetric and nonpositive, while another is neither symmetric nor nonpositive.

1
Jaynes–Cummings equation

The
Jaynes–Cummings equation (JCE) is basic model of Quantum Optics. The model without damping and pumping was introduced in 1963 by
Jaynes and Cummings

[
6
]
(the survey can be found in
[
9
]
).
The damped driven Jaynes–Cummings equations read as

[
13
, (5.107)]
:

\dot{\rho}(t)={\cal A}(t)\rho(t):=-i[H(t),\rho(t)]+\gamma D\rho(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

(1.1)

Here
\rho(t)
is the density operator of the coupled field-molecule system,
i.e., it is a nonnegative Hermitian operator with the trace one, in the Hilbert space

X=F\otimes{\mathbb C}^{2}
, where
F
is the separable Hilbert space
endowed with
an orthonormal basis
|n\rangle
,
n=0,1,\dots

and the annihilation operator
a
and its adjoint, creation operator

a^{\dagger}
:

a|n\rangle=\sqrt{n}|n-1\rangle,\qquad a^{\dagger}|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle,\qquad[a,a^{\dagger}]=1.

(1.2)

The Hamiltonian
H(t)
is the sum

H(t)\!=\!H_{0}\!+\!pV(t),\,\,\,H_{0}\!=\!H_{F}\!+\!H_{A},\,\,\,H_{F}\!=\!\omega_{c}a^{\dagger}a,\,\,\,H_{A}\!=\!\frac{1}{2}\omega_{a}\sigma_{3},\,\,\,V(t)\!=\!\sigma_{1}(a\!+\!a^{\dagger})\!+\!A^{e}(t).

(1.3)

Here
H_{0}
is the Hamiltonian of the free field and atom without interaction,
while
pV(t)
is the interaction Hamiltonian,

\omega_{c}>0
is the cavity resonance frequency,

\omega_{a}>0
is the molecular
frequency,
\gamma>0
and
D
is a dissipation operator,

p\in{\mathbb R}
is proportional to the molecular dipole moment.
By

A^{e}(t)
we denote the pumping,

\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}
are the Pauli matrices
acting on
{\mathbb C}^{2}
, so
[a,\sigma_{k}]=[a^{\dagger},\sigma_{k}]=0
.

2
Dissipation operators and main results

The dissipation operator
D
in (
1.1
) describes the
quantum spontaneous emission which provides an energy
loss to balance the pumping.
The dissipation operators must satisfy the following conditions.

We will consider the dissipation operators

[
13
, (5.107)]
and
[
4
, (5.6.20)]
:

D\rho=a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}a\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho a^{\dagger}a+a^{\dagger}\rho a-\frac{1}{2}aa^{\dagger}\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho aa^{\dagger},\qquad{\Delta}\rho=a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}a\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho a^{\dagger}a.

(2.1)

Definition 2.1
.

i)
|n,s_{\pm}\rangle=|n\rangle\otimes s_{\pm}
is the orthonormal basis in
X
, where

s_{\pm}\in{\mathbb C}^{2}
and

\sigma_{3}s_{\pm}=\lambda(s_{\pm})s_{\pm}
with

\lambda(s_{\pm})=\pm 1.

ii)
X_{0}
is the space
of finite linear combinations of the vectors
|n,s_{\pm}\rangle
.

iii)
{\cal D}\subset{\rm HS}
is the subspace of finite rank Hermitian operators

\rho=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}}\,\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm}}\rho_{n,n^{\prime}}|n,s\rangle\otimes\langle n^{\prime},s^{\prime}|.

(2.2)

Every density operator
\rho\in{\rm HS}

is defined uniquely by its matrix entries

\rho_{n,n^{\prime}}=\langle n|\rho|n^{\prime}\rangle,

(2.3)

which are Hermitian
2\times 2
matrices.
The Hilbert–Schmidt norm, corresponding to the inner product (
2.5
), can be written as

\|\rho\|_{\rm HS}^{2}=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho^{2}]=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\rho^{2}_{n,n^{\prime}}<\infty.

(2.4)

Definition 2.2
.

{\rm HS}
is the Hilbert space of Hermitian
Hilbert–Schmidt operators with the inner product
[
11
, Thm VI.22 (c)]

\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS}=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}\rho_{2}].

(2.5)

The operator
D

is a modification of
the version
{\Delta}
.
The version
{\Delta}
has been introduced
in
[
1
]
–
[
3
]
, and
used
in

[
4
, (5.6.20)]

and
[
5
, (18)]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
, (4)]
. The version
is symmetric in the following two aspects:
for
\rho\in{\cal D}
,

{\Delta}\rho
is the
selfadjoint operator
with zero trace that is necessary for the dynamics of
\rho(t)
in selfadjoint
operators with constant trace.
The modification
D

restores the third symmetry with respect to the interchange of
a
and
a^{\dagger}
.

The main result of present paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

i) The dissipation operator
D
is
defined on the domain
{\cal D}\subset{\rm HS}
and it is
nonpositive and symmetric:

\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}\leq 0,\qquad\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}=\langle D\rho,\rho\rangle_{\rm HS},\qquad\rho\in{\cal D}.

(2.6)

ii) The operator
{\Delta}:{\cal D}\to{\cal D}
is
neither symmetric nor nonpositive.

iii) The operator
D:{\cal D}\to{\cal D}
is injective.

Corollary 2.4
.

The dissipation operator
D
admits the selfadjoint Friedrichs extension.

Let us comment on related previous results.
The dissipation operators
{\Delta}
and
D
were introduced
analysing quantum spontaneous emission in
[

[

]

,

.

In

]
and
[

in adiabatic single-frequency asymptotics for solutions to
the semiclassical Maxwell–Bloch equations.

3
Nonpositivity and symmetry

Here we prove Theorem
2.3
i) and ii).

i) First,
let us
prove the nonpositivity of
D
. For
\rho\in{\cal D}
,

\displaystyle\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(\rho D\rho\Big)=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(\rho\big[a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}a\rho(t)-\frac{1}{2}\rho a^{\dagger}a+a^{\dagger}\rho a-\frac{1}{2}aa^{\dagger}\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho aa^{\dagger}\big]\Big)

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(\rho a\rho a^{\dagger}-\rho a^{\dagger}a\rho+\rho a^{\dagger}\rho a-\rho aa^{\dagger}\rho\Big)=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(2\rho a\rho a^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a\rho^{2}-aa^{\dagger}\rho^{2}\Big).

Now we use the fact that
\rho
is a finite rank Hermitian operator (
2.2
).
Then
all operators
\rho a\rho a^{\dagger}
,
a^{\dagger}a\rho^{2}

and
aa^{\dagger}\rho^{2}
have only finite
number of nonzero entries (
2.3
), so their traces
are well defined.
Moreover,

\rho
admits a finite spectral resolution
\rho=(\rho_{i}\delta_{ij})
in the orthonormal basis of its eigenvectors from
X_{0}
:

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{i}\,e_{i}\!\otimes\!e_{i},\qquad e_{i}\in X_{0},

(3.2)

where only finite number of the eigenvalues
\rho_{i}
are nonzero.
In this basis,
the entries

a_{jk}=\langle e_{j},ae_{k}\rangle

and
a^{\dagger}_{kl}=\langle e_{k},a^{\dagger}e_{l}\rangle

of the operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
are well defined.
Hence,
with summation in repeated indices,

\displaystyle\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(2\rho_{i}\delta_{ij}a_{jk}\rho_{k}\delta_{kl}a^{\dagger}_{li}-a^{\dagger}_{kl}a_{lj}\rho_{j}^{2}(t)\delta_{jk}-a_{jk}a^{\dagger}_{kl}\rho_{l}^{2}(t)\delta_{lj}\Big)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(2\rho_{i}a_{ik}\rho_{k}a^{\dagger}_{ki}-a^{\dagger}_{kl}a_{lk}\rho_{k}^{2}-a_{jk}a^{\dagger}_{kj}\rho_{j}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(2\rho_{i}a_{ik}\rho_{k}a^{\dagger}_{ki}-a^{\dagger}_{ki}a_{ik}\rho_{k}^{2}-a_{ik}a^{\dagger}_{ki}\rho_{i}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{ik}a^{\dagger}_{ki}[2\rho_{i}\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}^{2}-\rho_{i}^{2}]=-|a_{ik}|^{2}[\rho_{i}-\rho_{k}]^{2}\leq 0

since
a^{\dagger}_{ji}=\overline{a}_{ij}
. Hence, the nonpositivity of
D
is proved.

Second, let us check the symmetry of
D
:

\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}D\rho_{2}]=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[D\rho_{1})^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}],\qquad\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in{\cal D}.

(3.4)

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}D\rho_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}(a\rho_{2}a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{2}a^{{\dagger}}a+a^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}a-\frac{1}{2}aa^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{2}aa^{{\dagger}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}a\rho_{2}a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}a^{{\dagger}}a+\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}a-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}aa^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}aa^{{\dagger}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[a^{\dagger}\rho_{1}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{1}\rho_{2}+a\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}aa^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}-\frac{1}{2}aa^{{\dagger}}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[(a^{\dagger}\rho_{1}a-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}a-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{1}+a\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}aa^{{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}aa^{{\dagger}}\rho_{1})\rho_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[(a^{\dagger}\rho_{1}a-\frac{1}{2}a^{{\dagger}}a\rho_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}a+a\rho_{1}a^{{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}aa^{{\dagger}}\rho_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}aa^{{\dagger}})^{{\dagger}}\rho_{2}]=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[(D\rho_{1})^{\dagger}\rho_{2}].

Hence (
3.4
) is proved.

ii)
The calculations above show that the operator
{\Delta}
is neither symmetric nor nonpositive.
In particular, the calculations (
3.1
)–(
3.3
) give in this case

\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho D\rho]=|a_{ik}|^{2}[\rho_{i}\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}^{2}]

(3.5)

which is not enough for the nonpositivity.

4
Injectivity

Let us prove Theorem
2.3
iii).
We must show that
\rho=0
if
D\rho=0
and
\rho\in{\cal D}
.
In the case
D\rho=0
, we have also

\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle=0
.
The
formula (
3.3
) gives

\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}=-|a_{ik}|^{2}[\rho_{i}-\rho_{k}]^{2},\qquad\rho\in{\cal D}.

(4.1)

It implies
that
\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle=0

only if
a_{ik}=0
for
\rho_{i}\neq\rho_{k}
. Therefore, the eigenspaces corresponding to the eigenvalues
\rho_{i}
are invariant with respect to
a
and

a^{\dagger}
. However, there is only one such eigenspace, the entire
Hilbert space
X
. Accordingly,
all eigenvalues
\rho_{i}
coinside.
Hence, all the eigenvalues
are zeros since the zero is in spectrum
of any density operator
\rho\in{\cal D}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The last step uses the fact that
\rho\in{\cal D}
.
Alternatively, we can use the argument that
for
\rho\in{\cal D}
with identical nonzero eigenvalues,
the Hilbert–Schmidt norm (
2.4
) is infinite.
This argument also works for general
\rho\in{\rm HS}

if the relation (
4.1
) holds.
However, our proof of this relation is correct only for

\rho\in{\cal D}
.

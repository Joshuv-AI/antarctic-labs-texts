Hybrid deep learning with attention fusion for enhanced colon cancer detection
Authors: Süheyla Demirtaş Alpsalaz, Emrah Aslan, Yıldırım Özüpak, Feyyaz Alpsalaz, Hasan Uzel, Viktoria Bereznychenko
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study introduces a hybrid deep learning model integrating EfficientNet-B3 and Vision Transformer with an Attention Fusion mechanism for automated colon cancer detection using the Kvasir endoscopic dataset. The model leverages EfficientNet-B3’s strength in capturing fine-grained local textures and Vision Transformer’s ability to model global contextual relationships. A multi-head attention-based fusion block harmonizes these features, achieving comprehensive representations and enhanced classification stability. Model optimization was guided by the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), alongside evaluations of accuracy, F1-score, and Brier Score. Experimental results demonstrate a 96.2% accuracy and an MCC of 0.961, surpassing standalone baselines and existing benchmark architectures. Cross-validation confirmed robust generalization, while Grad-CAM analyses improved interpretability by visualizing salient histopathological regions influencing predictions. Despite slight overfitting tendencies, the model maintained strong performance across all eight image classes. These findings highlight the model’s ability to address limitations of single-architecture approaches by combining local and global feature extraction, offering rapid, objective, and reliable diagnostic support. The proposed framework shows significant promise for integration into computer-aided colonoscopy systems, paving the way for enhanced clinical diagnostics and reduced pathologist workload through AI-driven precision medicine.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common and lethal cancers worldwide1. Its early diagnosis and accurate classification are essential for improving patient survival and guiding effective treatment strategies2. Traditional diagnostic approaches rely heavily on manual histopathological examination, which is time-consuming and subject to inter-observer variability3. In recent years, machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) have emerged as powerful tools in medical image analysis and clinical decision-making4. These technologies can automatically process and interpret complex histopathological and molecular data, identifying subtle patterns that may not be visible to the human eye. AI-based diagnostic systems, particularly those using deep learning and convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have shown high performance in differentiating cancerous from non-cancerous tissues5. ML algorithms such as support vector machines6, random forests, and ensemble models further contribute to improving classification accuracy and robustness7. The integration of these computational approaches into colorectal cancer research enables earlier detection8, more consistent diagnosis, and better prediction of disease progression9. As AI and ML continue to evolve, their applications in colorectal cancer diagnostics are expected to play a vital role in advancing precision medicine and reducing the global burden of this disease10.

Recent advances in deep learning (DL) and machine learning (ML) have achieved significant success in the automated analysis of histopathological images11. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Vision Transformers (ViTs) and hybrid models used for feature extraction and classification tasks provide significant advantages over traditional methods in terms of sensitivity, specificity and overall diagnostic performance12. The ability of DL models to learn complex patterns and subtle morphological differences in histopathologic images provides an important foundation for the development of robust and scalable automated colon cancer detection systems13. Moreover, optimization algorithms for hyperparameter tuning and model refinement improve the predictive performance of the model, making AI-assisted approaches an indispensable tool in modern medical diagnosis14. While significant progress has been made in AI-based colon cancer detection, there are still challenges15. Existing models suffer from overfitting due to imbalanced datasets, lack generalizability across diverse histopathological datasets, and require high computational power for training and inference processes16. Moreover, feature extraction methods cannot effectively capture both local and global dependencies in histopathologic images, limiting their ability to distinguish between benign and malignant tissue structures17. To overcome these limitations, our work proposes a new hybrid model using EfficientNet and Vision Transformer architectures, integrated with the Attention Fusion block18. This hybrid approach aims to improve model robustness and classification accuracy by combining the strengths of convolutional feature extraction with the global attention mechanisms of transformers.

In this study, the proposed hybrid deep learning model was trained on the Kvasir dataset, a widely recognized benchmark for colon cancer detection. The optimization process was guided by the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) to ensure the highest possible classification reliability and robustness. Within the proposed framework, the complementary feature extraction capabilities of EfficientNet and the Vision Transformer are effectively combined through an attention-based fusion mechanism, enabling richer and more discriminative representations of histopathological structures. This integrated strategy enhances the accuracy and interpretability of colon cancer detection, while also contributing to the broader field of AI-assisted medical diagnostics by introducing a computationally efficient and scalable architecture. The motivation behind combining EfficientNet-B3 and Vision Transformer lies in overcoming the inherent limitations of purely convolutional or transformer-based networks. EfficientNet-B3 excels in capturing fine-grained, localized texture features due to its convolutional inductive bias, while the Vision Transformer effectively models long-range dependencies through its self-attention and shifted window mechanisms. However, CNNs alone struggle with global context understanding, and transformers often require large datasets and computational resources to generalize local details. The proposed Attention Fusion block harmonizes these complementary strengths—enhancing local-to-global feature continuity and context-aware representation. This hybridization thus mitigates the trade-off between localization precision and contextual comprehension, leading to improved diagnostic robustness and generalization.

Our research distinguishes itself from previous studies in several key aspects. Unlike earlier approaches that predominantly rely on single-architecture or conventional CNN-based feature extraction methods, the proposed framework integrates transformer-based self-attention mechanisms with CNN-driven hierarchical feature extraction, yielding a more robust and context-aware classification system. The incorporation of the Attention Fusion block further enhances model interpretability and generalizability by jointly preserving fine-grained local textures and long-range spatial dependencies. Additionally, optimization guided by the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) ensures balanced classification performance across all categories, addressing potential issues associated with dataset imbalance.

The outcomes of this study carry significant implications for digital pathology and clinical practice. The proposed automated colon cancer detection framework offers a reliable decision-support tool that can reduce the diagnostic workload of pathologists, minimize subjective interpretation, and improve overall diagnostic precision. Integrating such AI-assisted systems into clinical workflows has the potential to revolutionize cancer diagnostics through early detection and personalized treatment planning. As medical AI continues to advance, future research will focus on improving model generalizability, reducing computational demands, and incorporating multimodal imaging and clinical data. Overall, this study marks an important step toward realizing the full potential of deep learning in colon cancer diagnosis and underscores the transformative role of AI in modern healthcare systems.

Literature review

In addition to conventional methods used in the diagnosis and treatment of colon cancer, increasing efforts to develop artificial intelligence-supported automated diagnostic systems have an important place in the current research literature. In particular, in the context of histopathological image analysis, the advantages of convolutional neural networks, transformer-based approaches and hybrid model architectures in terms of diagnostic accuracy and generalizability have been studied in detail. In this context, comparative analyses of various model configurations and optimization methods contribute significantly to the development of innovative approaches to colon cancer detection.

Su et al. used TCGA gene expression data for colon cancer diagnosis and staging. Genes identified by WGCNA and LASSO were classified using RF, SVM, and decision trees. The RF model achieved high accuracy, and eight prognostic genes were identified19. Sakr et al. proposed a lightweight CNN-based deep learning model for colon cancer detection using normalized histopathological images. Tested on public datasets, the model achieved high accuracy, outperforming most existing methods and offering high computational efficiency20. Attallah et al. developed a multi-model lightweight DL framework for early lung and colon cancer detection using histopathology images. Features from ShuffleNet, MobileNet, and SqueezeNet were reduced via PCA and FHWT-DWT, achieving high accuracy with low computational complexity21. Bokhorst et al. proposed an AI-based method for segmenting multiple tissue compartments in H&E-stained colorectal cancer slides. Using data from five centers and public datasets, the model improved automated diagnosis and biopsy classification, aiding pathologists in CRC risk stratification22. Ben Hamida et al. explored DL architectures for colon cancer classification and segmentation using sparsely annotated histopathological images. Transfer learning with CNNs such as ResNet and DenseNet improved accuracy on multiple datasets, while UNet and SegNet enabled effective WSI-level segmentation23. Narayan et al. introduced TrionixNet, a tri-modular deep learning model for prostate cancer segmentation using multiparametric MRI. Incorporating attention and boundary refinement mechanisms, it enhanced tumor delineation and outperformed existing models, demonstrating strong clinical applicability24.

Singh et al. proposed the ESMXO algorithm and a Deep Residual Attention Multivariate Sequencing Model for breast cancer analysis. Integrating CNNs like Xception and NASNetMobile, their approach achieved high accuracy across magnifications, improving genetic pattern detection and diagnostic precision25. Banerjee introduced the BCB–CSPA deep learning network for automated skin cancer detection. Combining bipartite convoluted blocks and semantic perceptual attention, the model enhanced feature extraction and interpretability, achieving superior diagnostic performance across dermatological datasets26. Banerjee et al. reviewed deep learning and traditional ML methods for diabetic retinopathy detection using datasets like MESSIDOR-2, IDRiD, DDR, and EyePACS. DenseNet-169 achieved the best accuracy, and transfer learning improved performance, highlighting future potential for hybrid intelligent frameworks27. Singh et al. evaluated multiple DNN architectures for ocular disease detection using the ODIR dataset. After image preprocessing, ResNet50 and DenseNet121 achieved the best results, while ADAM proved the most stable optimizer. The study emphasizes architecture and optimizer choice for optimal diagnostic accuracy28. Rehman et al. proposed a Vision-ViT-based deep learning framework with modified pre-trained models for multi-label CKD image classification. Using transfer learning, the model enhanced accuracy and generalization on small datasets, while Grad-CAM improved interpretability and lesion-focused precision29. Mahmood et al. developed advanced DL algorithms for breast cancer detection and classification, integrating cLSFSO optimization, transfer learning with modified VGGNet and SE-ResNet152, and hybrid CNN-based models. Grad-CAM enhanced interpretability, achieving high diagnostic accuracy and aiding radiological assessment30. Mahmood et al. introduced two AD diagnostic models: D3LM-LAN, a dual-channel ResNet-50–based deep learning network for PET and MRI fusion, and MLM-MCSVM, an optimized SVM using particle swarm optimization. Both improved early-stage Alzheimer’s detection accuracy and supported personalized treatment strategies31.

Gowthamy and Ramesh proposed a hybrid DL framework combining ResNet-50, InceptionV3, and DenseNet with KELM for lung and colon cancer diagnosis. Using feature fusion and MB-DMOA optimization, the model enhanced classification accuracy and computational efficiency in histopathology analysis14. Jo et al. developed a plasmonic gold nanopolyhedron (AuNH)-coated needle-based SERS sensor integrated with endoscopy for noninvasive colorectal cancer detection. Using Raman spectroscopy and machine learning, the system accurately differentiated cancerous from normal tissue, enabling biomarker identification and early CRC diagnosis32. Shahadat et al. proposed a lightweight, mobile-embedded 1D CNN with squeeze-and-excitation layers for lung and colon cancer detection. Using the LC25000 dataset, the model achieved state-of-the-art performance with high accuracy and computational efficiency, enabling rapid and reliable diagnosis33. Di Giammarco et al. developed an explainable deep learning approach for identifying adenocarcinoma in colon tissue images. By comparing multiple architectures, the model accurately detected and localized cancerous regions while providing visual interpretability to enhance diagnostic transparency34. Karthikeyan et al. proposed a CNN-based model with a ranking algorithm for automated colorectal cancer detection using digital pathology images. The approach reduced diagnostic subjectivity and pathologist workload, outperforming existing methods in recall, precision, and accuracy35.

Junaid et al. proposed a hybrid DL–ML framework for colorectal cancer diagnosis, integrating MobileNetV2 and DenseNet121 for feature extraction with transfer learning, SMOTE for balancing, and Chi-square feature selection. The model improved accuracy and robustness, outperforming existing CRC diagnostic approaches36. Gowthamy and Ramesh developed a CNN–transformer hybrid model with attention and Siamese network components for colon cancer classification using the CRC-VAL-HE-7 K dataset. Through preprocessing, augmentation, and optimization, the method enhanced multi-class accuracy and outperformed existing diagnostic models37. Minh et al. developed a colorectal cancer diagnosis system using EMR data and ML models including CART, Random Forest, and XGBOOST. After expert-guided feature selection and preprocessing, 21 key diagnostic attributes were identified, with XGBOOST performing best, aiding early detection and clinical decision-making38.

The reviewed studies indicate that hybrid deep learning architectures are gaining increasing importance in colon cancer detection. While CNN-based models effectively capture local texture features, Transformer-based approaches provide superior global contextual understanding. Integrating these complementary strengths through attention-based fusion mechanisms has been shown to enhance diagnostic accuracy and produce more stable, generalizable, and clinically relevant results compared to conventional methods.

Dataset

The Kvasir Dataset, comprising endoscopic images of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, was utilized. Collected by the Vestre Viken Health Trust in Norway, the dataset includes images sourced from four distinct hospitals and has been meticulously annotated and validated by expert endoscopists39. It encompasses a variety of categories, including anatomical landmarks (e.g., Z-line, pylorus, cecum), pathological conditions (e.g., esophagitis, polyps, ulcerative colitis), and lesion removal procedures. The images vary in resolution from 720 × 576 to 1920 × 1072 pixels, providing a robust sample size suitable for tasks such as image retrieval, machine learning, deep learning, and transfer learning. This dataset supports the creation of AI-driven systems for automated disease detection and medical report generation. To enhance model generalization to real-world endoscopic imagery, preprocessing involved data augmentation techniques such as random resized cropping, horizontal flipping, rotation, and color jittering. The dataset was divided into training (70%), validation (10%), and test (20%) subsets, employing stratified sampling to ensure balanced representation across classes. Representative images from the dataset are presented in Fig. 1. Although the Kvasir dataset includes samples from four hospitals, imaging devices and illumination conditions vary across institutions, introducing potential bias. While class balancing was achieved through stratified sampling, variations in color tone and resolution may influence the model’s generalization ability. To address this, normalization and histogram equalization were applied during preprocessing. In future work, external validation on the Hyper-Kvasir and Warwick-QU datasets and domain adaptation via style transfer will be pursued to evaluate robustness across imaging domains.

It utilizes squeeze-and-excitation blocks and depthwise separable convolutions to extract fine-grained local features with reduced computational cost, making it highly effective for medical image classification and feature extraction tasks40. The Attention Fusion Mechanism integrates feature maps from multiple networks—such as EfficientNet-B3 and the Vision Transformer—by applying multi-head attention to emphasize the most informative spatial and channel-wise features. This process harmonizes local and global representations, enhances contextual understanding, and improves classification accuracy while maintaining model interpretability and robustness45.

Proposed hybrid model

The proposed model is a hybrid architecture that integrates EfficientNet-B3 and a Vision Transformer via an Attention-Fusion module. EfficientNet-B3 extracts compact low-level visual descriptors, while the Vision Transformer captures higher-order, long-range structural relationships. The Attention-Fusion layer aligns and adaptively weights these complementary representations to form a unified feature embedding, which is subsequently fed to a multilayer perceptron (MLP) classification head that produces the final decision. This design leverages the local texture sensitivity of convolutions and the global context modeling of transformers within a single end-to-end framework.The training process was conducted using mixed-precision training via PyTorch’s AMP (Automatic Mixed Precision) to accelerate computations and reduce memory consumption. The best model was selected based on Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) scores, and confusion matrix analysis was used for performance evaluation. Evaluation of performances

The evaluation of deep learning (DL) models in this study was carried out using various performance metrics to ensure a comprehensive analysis47. In particular, confusion matrices were utilized alongside model accuracy and error rate assessments to provide a detailed understanding of the model’s classification performance48. The accuracy score quantifies the overall correctness of the model’s predictions, whereas precision measures the proportion of correctly identified positive cases49. Recall (also known as sensitivity) evaluates the model’s ability to correctly detect true positive instances, while the F1-score provides a harmonic mean of precision and recall to balance these two metrics. Additionally, the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) was included as a metric to assess the model’s ability to handle imbalanced data, offering a more robust performance evaluation. The confusion matrix visually represents the model’s prediction outcomes, distinguishing between correct and incorrect classifications. By analyzing false positives (FP) and false negatives (FN), the model’s weaknesses in different classification categories can be identified50. This is particularly crucial for colorectal cancer (CRC) diagnosis, where misclassification may have significant clinical implications51. To further assess the model’s learning behavior, accuracy and error rate curves were analyzed throughout the training process. The model accuracy curve illustrates the change in classification success across epochs, whereas the error rate curve highlights variations in the model’s misclassification trends over time. These insights help detect potential training issues such as overfitting or underfitting52. Result and discussion

In this section, the experimental results of the proposed hybrid deep learning model integrating EfficientNet-B3 and the Vision Transformer through the Attention Fusion mechanism are presented and analyzed. The performance of the model was evaluated using the Kvasir dataset to assess its effectiveness in automated colon cancer detection. Various quantitative metrics, including Accuracy, F1-score, Precision, Recall, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), were employed to ensure a comprehensive performance assessment. Comparative analyses with state-of-the-art architectures were conducted to highlight the advantages of the proposed approach in terms of classification accuracy, interpretability, and generalization capability. Furthermore, visual explanations using Grad-CAM was analyzed to provide insights into the model’s decision-making process, emphasizing its clinical relevance and potential for real-world medical applications.

Training details

For its training, the proposed hybrid model was implemented using the Kvasir dataset. To improve the model’s generalization capabilities, images were uniformly rescaled to 224 × 224 pixels and processed with data augmentation techniques such as random cropping, horizontal flipping, rotation, and color alterations. The training process involved a batch size of 16 and was run for 50 epochs, utilizing a learning rate of 0.001. The AdamW optimization algorithm was chosen for model updates, paired with a 1e-4 weight decay. The loss function was defined as cross-entropy loss with label smoothing. A ReduceLROnPlateau strategy was used to dynamically adjust the learning rate based on tracking the validation loss. Additionally, to enhance computational efficiency and optimize memory consumption, mixed precision training was applied using PyTorch’s Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) method. The model’s architecture is built on the integration of EfficientNet-B3 and Vision Transformer components, which were pre-trained on ImageNet. These components are unified via an Attention Fusion block. The resulting combined features are then fed into a multilayer perceptron (MLP) block for the final classification. The optimal model was identified based on the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) criterion calculated from the validation data, and this specific model was subsequently evaluated on the test data.

Hyperparameters such as batch size (8, 16, 32), learning rate (1e − 2 to 1e − 5), and weight decay were tuned using grid search on the validation set. Early stopping (patience = 10) and dropout scheduling (from 0.3 to 0.5) were implemented to prevent overfitting. Additionally, stochastic depth regularization was applied within the Vision Transformer blocks to enhance training stability. These measures reduced the training–validation accuracy gap from 6% to 2.8%, improving overall model generalization. To ensure statistical robustness, 5-fold cross-validation was conducted in addition to the fixed split. The mean accuracy across folds was 95.7 ± 0.35%, with standard deviation of MCC = 0.008, confirming consistent generalization across training subsets.

Experimental results

The experimental findings demonstrate that the suggested hybrid model delivers dependable and highly accurate results in the detection of colon cancer. The model’s learning dynamics are effectively captured by the loss and accuracy figures recorded during training. Concurrently, an increase in the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) during both validation and testing phases indicates that the model successfully maintains inter-class balance. The performance achieved highlights that combining the unique feature extraction abilities of EfficientNet-B3 and Vision Transformer through the Attention Fusion mechanism positively impacts the model’s overall success. Furthermore, an examination of the accuracy curves, error rate graphs, and confusion matrices from the experimental results shows a high general classification performance, though the model does exhibit occasional confusion between some classes. Computational efficiency and feasibility

The proposed hybrid architecture contains 44.2 million parameters and 8.9 GFLOPs per inference, with an average inference time of 46 ms per 224 × 224 image on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. Compared with the standalone EfficientNet-B3 (38 M parameters, 7.1 GFLOPs) and Vision Transformer (48 M, 9.7 GFLOPs), the hybrid model achieves higher accuracy with minimal latency overhead. Future research will explore model compression through pruning and knowledge distillation to enable deployment on low-resource clinical devices.

This study underscores the efficacy of hybrid deep learning approaches for colon cancer diagnosis. By combining the robust local feature extraction of EfficientNet-B3 with the global contextual analysis of the Vision Transformer through an Attention Fusion mechanism, the proposed model attains a remarkable 96.2% accuracy on the Kvasir dataset. The results affirm the model’s ability to effectively capture both fine-grained tissue characteristics and broader structural patterns associated with tumors, reinforcing the potential of automated diagnostic systems to deliver rapid, objective, and reliable outcomes in clinical settings. Nevertheless, the observed tendency toward overfitting suggests the need for enhanced regularization techniques and further data augmentation strategies. Future research should prioritize integrating diverse data modalities and conducting thorough clinical validations to enhance the model’s applicability and robustness.

Conclusion

The proposed hybrid model, integrating EfficientNet-B3 and Vision Transformer with an Attention Fusion mechanism, achieves superior performance in colon cancer detection on the Kvasir dataset, with 96.2% accuracy and a 0.961 MCC score. Its low error rates and high classification stability, particularly on imbalanced datasets, underscore its robustness compared to traditional diagnostic methods. The Attention Fusion mechanism effectively combines local texture details and global contextual information, enhancing interpretability and generalization. However, minor overfitting observed during training suggests the need for advanced regularization, early stopping, and expanded data augmentation strategies. Future research should focus on integrating multimodal data and conducting extensive clinical validations to improve generalizability and real-world applicability. The model’s potential as a real-time decision-support tool in colonoscopy workflows could reduce inter-observer variability and pathologist workload. Compliance with regulatory frameworks like GDPR and HIPAA, alongside federated learning and differential privacy for multi-institutional training, will ensure data security and enhance clinical translation. This study marks a significant step toward leveraging hybrid deep learning for precise, efficient, and reliable colon cancer diagnostics, contributing to the advancement of AI-driven healthcare solutions.

New reference equations for lung function can affect eligibility for lung cancer surgery
Authors: Nicoline Mathiessen Andersen, Kathrine Nybo Holgersen, Christian B. Laursen, Jann Mortensen, Flemming Madsen, Morten Bendixen, Maibritt Rævdal Christensen, Amanda Dandanell Juul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionThe standard treatment for early-stage non-small cell lung cancer is surgical resection. Assessment of operability includes evaluation of lung function. Current European guidelines are based on reference equations from the former European Community for Steel and Coal (ECSC). New reference equations from Global Lung Initiative (GLI) and Danish Lung Function Material (DALFUMAT) have been published for forced expiratory volume in the first second (FEV1) and Lung carbon monoxide diffusing capacity (DLCO).ObjectiveTo evaluate whether the use of GLI or DALFUMAT instead of ESCS reference equations result in a higher number of patients with predicted values for FEV1 and DLCO below 30% and 40%MethodsThis is a retrospective cohort study. Data were collected from medical records of 1400 patients. Percent of predicted values calculated using the ECSC reference equations were compared with those using the GLI and DALFUMAT reference equations.ResultsGLI reference equations did not result in a significant change in number of patients with FEV1 or DLCO percent of predicted below 30% or 40%.DALFUMAT reference equations made a significant change in the number of patients who had a FEV1 percent of predicted below 40% (n = 69 ~ 86%) and the number of females with DLCO percent of predicted below 40% (23~ − 37%). When using a threshold of 30%, there was an increase in number of females with DLCO over 30% (n = 17 ~ 155%), but this was not significant.ConclusionUsing national reference equations can affect the pre-operative assessment of patients with lung cancer.

Introduction

Background

The standard treatment for early-stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is surgery, and the 5-year survival rate among patients with stage I NSCLC after curative surgery is 70% [1,2].

The patient’s lung function must be assessed before surgery [3]. Lung function is evaluated by forced expiratory volume in the first second (FEV1) and lung carbon monoxide diffusing capacity (DLCO) [1]. The measured values are converted into percent predicted when compared to reference values from comparable healthy subjects with similar sex, height, age, and ethnicity. More than 400 reference equations are available for a wide range of populations [4].

The current guidelines on pre-operative lung function assessment from the European Respiratory Society (ERS) were released in 2009, and they are based on the former European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC) reference equations [5–7].

The Global Lung Initiative (GLI) published new reference equations for predicted FEV1 values in 2012, and new reference equations for predicted DLCO values were released in 2017 [8]. The GLI reference equations have since been widely implemented. They are based on a larger population, including more ethnicities, and cover an age range of 3–93 years [4,9]. The use of GLI instead of ECSC reference equations decreases FEV1 and increases DLCO percent of predicted [10,11]. New reference equations are available from the Danish Lung Function Material (DALFUMAT) [12]. The DALFUMAT reference equations are based on 908 healthy Danish subjects aged 18–97 years. Until now, no previous studies have compared the DALFUMAT reference equations to the old ECSC reference equations.

If the values of the predicted postoperative (ppo) FEV1 and DLCO are below 40% a treatment approach other than surgery should be considered according to the Danish National Guidelines [3,13]. ERS guidelines recommend a threshold of 30% [14]. Low lung function is associated with higher rates of mortality and morbidity and can predict the risk of complications [14,15]. Hence, the determination of lung function and measured percent predicted values is an essential step when determining the choice of treatment for early-stage NSCLC. GLI or DALFUMAT have yet to be proven comparable to the above-mentioned guidelines.

This retrospective cohort study aims to evaluate whether the use of GLI or DALFUMAT instead of ESCS reference equations results in a higher number of patients with predicted values for FEV1 and DLCO below 30% and 40%, potentially leading to a larger number of patients disqualified for surgery.

Methods

Design and setting

This study was conducted as a retrospective cohort study at the Centre for Thoracic Oncology, Department of Respiratory Medicine, Odense University Hospital. The centre functions as a tertiary referral centre and employs pulmonologists subspecialized in the diagnosis of patients with suspected lung cancer. Annually, approximately 1500 patients undergo invasive investigations for malignancy of the chest. At our centre, all patients suspected of malignancy based on imaging undergo spirometry. DLCO is measured if there is a possibility of surgical treatment or if a transthoracic biopsy is considered. If a patient is eligible for surgery and pulmonary operability is uncertain based on initial lung function tests (FEV1 and DLCO), the patient is referred for pulmonary scintigraphy and/or ergospirometry to estimate postoperative lung function and work capacity.

Data collection

Patients were identified by reviewing lists of patients from all lung cancer MDT conferences held from the 21st of February 2022 to the 29th of February 2024.

The patient records were searched for information on age, sex, comorbidities, smoking status, excess alcohol consumption, performance status [16], TNM status [17], final diagnosis, treatment, lung function, height, and weight. All data were stored in Research Electronic Data Capture (RedCap) version 13.7.18 [18,19].

Comorbidities were summarized according to the Charlson’s Comorbidity Index [20].

Some patients were discussed at multiple MDT conferences. Data were collected from the first conference at which the patient received a malignant diagnosis.

Calculation of FEV1 and DLCO percent of predicted

At our institution, GLI was used to calculate FEV1 percent of predicted and ECSC was used to calculate DLCO percent of predicted throughout the inclusion period. These numbers were directly entered from the spirometry software.

The predicted values were calculated using the Vyaire Software SentrySuite version 3.0.10. The formulas used in this software were reviewed. Height was measured according to ATS/ERS 2005 standards [21].

Predicted FEV1 values for ECSC [6] and predicted FEV1 and DLCO values for DALFUMAT were calculated using the formulas presented in supplementary materials, table S1. Predicted DLCO values for GLI were calculated using the GLI Lung Function Calculator from the European Respiratory Society [22]. Patients older than 85 yrs were excluded from the calculations for DLCO, as the calculator couldn’t determine the predicted DLCO for people of this age. A total of 37 patients were excluded from these results on this basis.

European ethnicity was used for all the participants, as information on ethnicity was not available in the charts.

Study size

No previous studies on the effect of the choice of reference material on operability has been published in which data are available for a more accurate sample size estimation. We expected the proportion assessed as the primary endpoint to be lower than 5% in the total population of patients diagnosed with lung cancer, and below 10% in a population of patients receiving curative treatment. At a 5% margin of error and 95% confidence level, the recommended sample size for estimating the proportion in a population is 73 (5% proportion), 139 (10% proportion), and 383 (50% proportion). As we included 1400 patients in the study, we were able to accurately determine the proportion.

Statistical methods

Stata (StataCorp, version 18.0) was used for all statistical analyses. Results were considered significant if the p-value < 0.05.

Shapiro Wilks test was used to test for normal distribution.

Normally distributed data were summarized as mean (SD). Non-normally distributed data were summarized as median (Interquartile range). Some of the baseline data were summarized as number (%).

A Fisher’s exact test was used to determine whether there was a significant number of patients, in whom the GLI or DALFUMAT reference equations lowered the FEV1 or DLCO percent predicted below 40%.

Paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-ranked test were used to calculate, whether there was a significant difference in mean or median values of FEV1 and DLCO percent of predicted between the ECSC and GLI reference equations and between ECSC and DALFUMAT reference equations.

All statistical methods were reviewed by a statistician.

Primary outcome

There was a slight increase (n = 8 ~ +9%) and decrease (n = 1 ~ − 3%) in the number of patients with FEV1 percent predicted below 40% and 30%, when using GLI reference equations compared to ECSC reference equations, however, this was not significant (Supplementary materials, table S2 and Figure 2). The DALFUMAT reference equations lowered FEV1 percent of predicted below 40% for a significant number of both males (n = 33 ~ +66%) and females (n = 36 ~ +100%). When using a threshold of 30%, there was also an increase for both males (n = 2 ~ 11%) and females (n = 17 ~ 154%). This was significant for females (Supplementary materials, table S2 and Figure 2). Five percent of patients who were offered surgical treatment had ergospirometry performed prior to surgery. Some patients could not cooperate to do a diffusion test, thus ergospirometry was performed to evaluate exercise capacity and gas exchange before surgery. The remaining patients had either PPO FEV1 below 40% according to GLI reference equations or PPO DLCO below 40% according to ECSC reference equations. Only two patients had PPO FEV1 or DLCO below 30%. The minimum FEV1 percent of predicted was 32% and the minimum DLCO percent of predicted was 29%.

For patients who received surgery the mean (SD) PPO FEV1 was 64% (16), which is significantly lower than the measured spirometry FEV1 percent of predicted after surgery of 71% (17) (p = 0.0028).

The median [Q1-Q3] PPO DLCO was 47% (39–58), which is significantly lower than the median [Q1-Q3] measured DLCO percent of predicted after surgery, which was 50% (44–59) (p = 0.0271).

In the study cohort 31% of the patients were qualified for surgery based on tumor stage and lung physiology. 59% of patients eligible for surgery based on tumor stage and lung physiology were offered surgical treatment and 21% were offered lung stereotactical radiation therapy (Supplementary materials, table S4). Of those not referred for surgery, 18% were disqualified based on comorbidities (Supplementary materials, table S4).

No difference in FEV1 or DLCO was found when comparing patients with Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC) and patients with other types of lung cancer. Nor was there any difference in FEV1 or DLCO when comparing patients eligible for surgery with patients ineligible for surgery based on stage of disease (Supplementary materials, table S5).

Discussion

This is to our knowledge the first study to investigate the effect on the operability assessment for European patients with lung cancer when using reference equations other than the ECSC reference equations.

The study found that using different reference equations for FEV1 and/or DLCO percent predicted than those on which the European guidelines [13,14] are based, could alter some patients’ FEV1 or DLCO percent of predicted to such a degree that they fall above or below the threshold for surgery. This implies that a change in the choice of reference equations can affect the treatment options offered to the patients.

The DALFUMAT and GLI reference equations impact on FEV1 and DLCO percent of predicted

This study could not demonstrate a change in the number of patients who would be potentially disqualified for surgery when using the GLI reference equations instead of the ECSC reference equations. However, the study did demonstrate a change in predicted values which could affect the overall evaluation of a patient prior to surgery. The biggest effect was found when changing from ECSC to DALFUMAT reference equations. This study assessed lung function in the complete lung cancer cohort, however, our data indicate that similar results are found in patients who are eligible for curative intended treatments based on the TNM stage. However, the number of patients in this subgroup was too small to detect a significant difference.

Assessing a patient’s eligibility for surgery is an overall evaluation of TNM-stage, performance status, age, and comorbidities. Borderline cases may be more affected by slight changes in lung function assessment as patients with FEV1 or DLCO percent of predicted over 40% could be denied surgery if they for instance have other comorbidities or poor performance status. This might indicate that the use of GLI and DALFUMAT reference equations instead of the ECSC reference equations may lead to even more patients being denied surgery, as a slight decrease in lung function could lead to a worse overall evaluation of the patients. Therefore, the use of the GLI and DALFUMAT reference equations instead of the ECSC reference equations may have a greater impact on the number of patients declared eligible for surgical treatment than this study could prove. However, a prospective study is needed to evaluate this theory.

The predicted post operative values

This study found that the estimated PPO values were significantly lower than the values measured with spirometry after surgery. Due to the small number of patients who underwent post-operative spirometry, the precision is not very high, and it is not adequate to make definitive conclusions. Other studies also have found a minimal loss in lung function after lobectomy [23–26]. These findings can be explained by the ‘lung volume reduction effect’, which means that surgery reduces the loss of functional lung function in patients with obstructive lung diseases [14].

As the PPO values are lower than the measured values, they may not represent the best way to evaluate patients for surgery, as some patients could be denied surgery on a faulty foundation. Inspite statistically significant results, the difference is only 3%. Therefore, the clinical relevance of the difference is questionable.

Strengths and limitations

In a total of 604 test subjects, diffusion tests were not measured. If the initial spirometry demonstrated a very low FEV1, DLCO was not measured since the patient was already disqualified for surgery. This may have led to a selection bias, due to the DLCO not being available for patients with very low FEV1, and therefore only results for the healthier patients were available. If diffusion tests had been available for all patients, evaluating the effects of changes between the reference equations would have had higher precision. Conversely, one could argue that this is also one of the strengths of this study, as data from the large cohort represent everyday clinical life.

The patients without a diffusion test were not surgical candidates. Therefore, whether a change of reference equations would have lowered or increased these patients’ DLCO percent predicted below 40% is clinically irrelevant.

Even though this study focused on operability assessment among lung cancer patients, we included patients with all types of lung cancer, including SCLC, and patients with all stages of the disease. This could have led to a potential spectrum bias, as most of these patients were never surgical candidates. To address this, we compared FEV1 and DLCO between patients with SCLC and patients with other types of lung cancer. We also compared FEV1 and DLCO between patients eligible and ineligible for surgery based on tumour stage. This study found no significant differences in FEV1 and DLCO between the groups. Therefore, it was decided to include these patients to increase the sample size and achieve higher precision.

A limitation of this study is that FEV1 and DLCO were assessed in isolation. This was due to the lack of data on ergospirometry. Only five percent of the patients had ergospirometry performed, as the thoracic surgeons tend to use the chair climbing test as an alternative to ergospirometry [27]. Ergospirometry is an important component in evaluating surgical eligibility for lung cancer patients. This represents a relevant limitation of the study. Consequently, we cannot precisely determine how many patients would be reclassified by changes in reference equations. However, the results may still be used to illustrate overall trends in classification differences.

Clinical perspectives

Several previous studies have compared the ECSC reference equations to national reference equations and found differences in FEV1 and/or DLCO percent predicted [28–32].

Since the mentioned studies primarily focused on obstructive pulmonary diseases or on changes in predicted values of healthy individuals, it is unknown how implementing national reference equations changes the assessment of lung cancer patients with poor lung function. Our results demonstrate that use of the Danish national reference material [12] significantly alters FEV1 percent predicted and DLCO percent predicted in several patient groups. Other countries might need to investigate the effects of their national reference equations to ensure proper assessment of pulmonary operability.

Conclusion

This study did not support that the use of the GLI reference equations instead of ECSC reference equations changes the number of patients who are qualified for surgery.

The implementation of DALFUMAT reference equations may increase the number of patients who become disqualified for surgery due to FEV1 percent of predicted below 40%. It may also increase the number of females with FEV1 percent predicted below 30%. However, it would decrease the number of especially female patients who are disqualified due to DLCO percent predicted below 40%. The effect of using a national reference equation on the estimation of operability should therefore be investigated before implementation.

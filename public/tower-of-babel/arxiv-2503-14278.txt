Controllability concepts for mean-field dynamics with reduced-rank coefficients Thanks: This work was supported by the NSF of Shandong Province (NO. ZR2023ZD35), the NSF of P.R. China (NO. 12031009), National Key R and D Program of China (NO. 2018YFA0703900).
Dan Goreac, Juan Li, Xinru Zhang

Abstract




Abstract
. In this paper we explore several novel notions of exact controllability for mean-field linear controlled stochastic differential equations (SDEs). A key feature of our study is that the noise coefficient is not required to be of full rank. We begin by demonstrating that classical exact controllability with
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-controls necessarily requires both rank conditions on the noise introduced in
[
8
]
and subsequent works. When these rank conditions are not satisfied, we introduce alternative rank requirements on the drift, which enable exact controllability by relaxing the regularity of the controls. In cases where both the aforementioned rank conditions fail, we propose and characterize a new notion of exact terminal controllability to normal laws (ETCNL). Additionally, we investigate a new class of Wasserstein-set-valued backward SDEs that arise naturally associated to ETCNL.

Keywords
. Exact controllability, mean-field SDEs, rank conditions, exact controllability in law, Wasserstein set-valued BSDEs.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space endowed with a scalar standard Brownian motion
W(\cdot)
and its natural filtration
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
augmented by all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. We consider the following mean-field linear controlled stochastic differential equation (MFSDE, for short):

\displaystyle

\displaystyle dX(t)={\left(A_{1}^{0}X(t)+A_{2}^{0}\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}+B_{1}u(t)+B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}dt

(1.1)

\displaystyle+{\left(C_{1}^{0}X(t)+C_{2}^{0}\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}+D_{1}u(t)+D_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}dW(t),

where
A_{j}^{0},\ C_{j}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
B_{j},\ D_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
for every index
j\in{\left\{1,2\right\}}
. Here,
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
are the sets of all
(d\times d)
and
(d\times n)
real matrices, respectively.
In the above, the state space is a standard Euclidean one
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, while the controls are
n
-dimensional, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. The concept of exact controllability describes the ability of a system to reach any given terminal point from an arbitrary initial point accurately, and the precise definition will be given in the next section.

Referring strictly to this kind of mean-field systems, exact controllability with
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-controls is shown to require

rank(D_{1}+D_{2})=d,

(1.2)

and a sufficient condition is obtained by adjoining

rank(D_{1})=d,

(1.3)

cf.
[
8
]
, condition employed in subsequent papers, e.g.,
[
22
, Assumption H]
,
[
6
, Assumption H4]
.
The standard reasoning is that, given (
1.3
), there exists
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
such that
D_{1}M=C_{1}^{0}
and the equation (
1.1
) and its qualitative features are equivalent to those of

\displaystyle

\displaystyle dX(t)={\left(A_{1}X(t)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}+B_{1}u(t)+B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}dt

(1.4)

\displaystyle+{\left(C\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}+D_{1}u(t)+D_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],

with
A_{k}=A_{k}^{0}-B_{k}M,\ k\in{\left\{1,2\right\}},\ C=C_{2}^{0}-D_{2}M
. Therefore, in this paper we focus on the controllability of (
1.4
) and give an affirmative answer to the following questions:

Q1.

Is (
1.3
) a necessary condition for the exact controllability of (
1.4
) with
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-controls?

Q2.

When both
D_{1}=0=D_{2}

?

Q3.

When
D_{1}=0

1.3

1.2

1.1
State of the art

Since it deals with targeted behavior of systems governed by ordinary or partial differential dynamics, the controllability is a concept that has been intensively investigated. In the framework of deterministic control systems, the controllability of linear systems is characterized by the controllability Gramian and Kalman’s rank condition, respectively. For comprehensive surveys on controllability results for non-random systems, see, e.g., Lee and Markus
[
13
]
for ordinary differential equation (ODE, for short) systems and Russell
[
19
]
, Lions
[
14
]
and Zuazua
[
23
]
for partial differential equation (PDE, for short) systems.

Within the extensive literature on stochastic differential equation (SDE), we would like to highlight the following results that are particularly relevant to our current research. Peng
[
18
]
initially introduced the concepts of exact terminal controllability and exact controllability in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
for stochastic control systems. In the case where the coefficients are deterministic and time-invariant, Peng derived a necessary condition for the terminal controllability of stochastic control systems. Additionally, for linear stochastic control systems, a purely algebraic necessary and sufficient criterion was introduced. Later on, Liu and Peng
[
15
]
expanded his research findings to include linear SDE systems with bounded, time-varying deterministic coefficients, leading to the development of a random version of the controllability Gramian. Lü and Zhang
[
17
]
simplified Peng’s rank condition from infinite number of columns to finite number of columns and derived a stochastic Kalman-type rank condition. In a related study, presented in
[
16
]
, Lü, Yong and Zhang put forth the representation of the Itô’s integral as Lebesgue/Bochner integral, which proved to be a valuable tool. Wang et al.
[
20
]
introduced the notion of
\mathbb{L}^{p}
-exact controllability for linear controlled stochastic differential equations with random coefficients. The authors then established several sufficient conditions for achieving this exact controllability, as well as deriving several equivalent conditions. On the other hand, Buckdahn, Quincampoix and Tessitore
[
5
]
and Goreac
[
7
,
9
]
delved into the concept of approximate controllability, specifically investigating the existence of admissible controls that steer trajectories arbitrarily close to the terminal conditions. In particular, in
[
7
]
the author showed that approximate and exact controllability are not equivalent.

Mean-field stochastic differential equation (MFSDE, for short), also known as the McKean-Vlasov equation, can be traced back to the work of Kac
[
10
,
11
]
in the 1950s. With the pioneering works on mean-field stochastic differential games by
Lasry and Lions
[
12
]
, the study of mean-field problems has garnered significant attention from researchers, see, e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
as well as the references therein. At the same time, the results on the controllability of McKean-Vlasov SDEs are rather scarce. Goreac
[
8
]
studied some exact and approximate controllability properties for mean-field linear stochastic systems with deterministic time-invariant coefficients, showing that (
1.2
) is a necessary condition and, together with (
1.3
), this is a sufficient set of conditions guaranteeing exact controllability. Yu
[
22
]
obtained a mean-field version of the Gramian matrix criterion for the general time-variant case and a mean-field version of the Kalman rank condition for the special time-invariant case, also in the deterministic framework. Ye and Yu
[
21
]
were interested in the exact controllability of linear stochastic systems (but with random time-variant coefficients) and obtained the equivalence for a series of problems. Recently, Chen and Yu
[
6
]
considered the exact controllability of a linear mean-field type game-based control system generated by a linear-quadratic Nash game. In an effort to deal with the exact controllability, the aforementioned papers require the conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) or some variations.

Structure of the paper and main contributions

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

Preliminaries

We let
T>0
be a fixed finite time horizon and
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
denote the set of all
(n\times m)
matrices. The superscript
∗
denotes the transpose of a vector or a matrix. We introduce the following functional spaces, when the state space
H
is a Hilbert one.

1) For
p\in{\left[1,\infty\right]}
,
\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}
denotes the space of
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable,
H
-valued random variables
\xi
such that
\mathbb{E}{\left[\left\|\xi\right\|^{p}\right]}<\infty
, for
p<\infty
, and
{\rm esssup}_{\omega\in\Omega}\left\|\xi(\omega)\right\|<\infty
, for
p=+\infty
.

2) For
p,\ q\in[1,\infty]
,

(a)
\mathbb{A}^{p,q}(H):=\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{q}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}\right)}
denotes the family of progressively measurable processes
\phi
such that

\left\|\omega\mapsto\left\|\phi(\cdot,\omega)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

(b)
\mathbb{B}^{q,p}(H):=\mathbb{L}^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right)}
denotes the family of progressively measurable processes
\phi
such that

\left\|t\mapsto\left\|\phi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega;H)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

3) For
p\in{\left[1,\infty\right]}
,
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{p}
(resp.,

) is a progressively measurable control such that
B_{1}u(\cdot)\in\mathbb{A}^{p,1}(H)
, and
B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(\cdot)\right]}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}
(resp.,
B_{1}v(\cdot)\in\mathbb{B}^{1,p}(H)
, and
B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[v(\cdot)\right]}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}

4) For

, we simply write
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(H):=\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega\times{\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}

5) Finally, for a random variable
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;H)
, we let
\mathbb{P}_{\xi}
denote its law.

The classical controllability concepts for a
d

.

) is said to be

2) The system (
1.4
) is said to be
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-

if, for every target

, there is an
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}
-regular control

of (
1.4
) starting at
x
and controlled by

3
Q1.
Necessity of (
1.3
)

This section is presented as a complement to
[
8
]
and provides an affirmative answer to
Q1
. Building on the work in
[
18
, inequality (8)]
, which relies on a deterministic oscillating function, it has been established in
[
8
]
that
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-exact terminal controllability cannot be achieved unless
D_{1}+D_{2}
covers the entire state space. When this necessary condition is complemented with the requirement that
D_{1}
can span the state space, the two conditions together imply the exact terminal controllability. In other words, when
D_{1}
is of full rank, the forward controlled equation (
1.4
) can be interpreted as a classical BSDE, which is solvable. This condition has subsequently been utilized in several follow-up papers, e.g.,
[
22
, Assumption H]
,
[
6
, Assumption H4]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
Range(C)\subset Range(D_{1})
. Then (
1.4
) is
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-exactly terminal controllable if and only if
Rank(D_{1}+D_{2})=Rank(D_{1})=d
.

Proof.

We begin our proof with a simple remark. Let
\alpha
be a real-valued process on
\Omega\times{\left[0,T\right]}
. Then, for every Borel function
r:{\left[0,T\right]}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathbb{E}{\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left|\alpha_{s}-r_{s}\right|^{2}ds\right]}\geq\int_{t}^{T}Var{\left(\alpha_{s}\right)}ds.

In particular, for
0\leq t\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}{\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left|\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{T}}W(s)-r_{s}\right|^{2}ds\right]}\geq\frac{T^{2}-t^{2}}{T}\geq T-t.

(3.1)

We only prove the necessity of the condition on
D_{1}
, the sufficiency and the rank condition on
D_{1}+D_{2}
already appearing in
[
8
, the Propositions 2 and 5]
. We proceed by contradiction and assume the range of
D_{1}
does not cover all
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, there exists some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
\left|a\right|=1
and
a^{*}D_{1}=0
. Under the assumption of exact terminal controllability, there exists some control
u
such that the associated trajectory satisfies

X(T)=\frac{W(T)^{2}-T}{\sqrt{2T}}a={\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}W(s)dW(s)\right)}a.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}W(s)dW(s)=a^{*}x+\int_{0}^{T}a^{*}{\left(A_{1}X(s)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[X(s)\right]}+B_{1}u(s)+B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(s)\right]}\right)}ds

\displaystyle\indent\quad\hskip 79.6678pt+\int_{0}^{T}a^{*}{\left(C\mathbb{E}{\left[X(s)\right]}+D_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(s)\right]}\right)}dW(s).

By taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}{\left[\ \cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}{\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}W(s)-a^{*}{\left(C\mathbb{E}{\left[X(s)\right]}+D_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(s)\right]}\right)}\right]}dW(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}b(s)ds-\mathbb{E}{\left[\int_{t}^{T}b(s)ds\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]},\ t\in[0,T],

where

b(s):=a^{*}\Big(A_{1}X(s)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[X(s)\right]}+B_{1}u(s)+B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(s)\right]}\Big),\ s\in[0,T]
.
By using the first remark in our proof, more precisely, (
3.1
), it follows that

\mathbb{E}{\left[{\left(\int_{t}^{T}b(s)ds-\mathbb{E}{\left[\int_{t}^{T}b(s)ds\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]}\right)}^{2}\right]}\geq T-t,

for every
0\leq t\leq T
. By Cauchy’s inequality, the left-hand term is dominated by
(T-t)\mathbb{E}{\left[\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\left|b(s)\right|^{2}ds\right]}
. Therefore, we have
\mathbb{E}{\left[\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\left|b(s)\right|^{2}ds\right]}\geq 1
, for every
0\leq t<T
. In virtue of the square integrability of
b
, we get immediately a contradiction by taking the limit as
t\uparrow T
. We conclude that
D_{1}
must have a full
d
rank.

The result in Theorem
3.1
is, of course, very strong as it states that one still needs access to all the components (via
D_{1}
) and that
D_{2}
does not bring anything new (it is obvious that the system is exactly terminal controllable with
D_{2}=0
). On the contrary,
D_{2}
has to “play nice” and not affect the spectrum of
D_{1}
(this is the meaning of
D_{1}+D_{2}
being of full rank).

4
Q2.
Exact controllability with controls of lower regularity

We recall the following interesting result from
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
p\geq 1
. Then, for every
T>0
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}
, there exists
\zeta\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right)}
such that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\zeta(r)\ dr=\xi
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

In this section we deal with the following controlled MFSDE, for which we have assumed that
D_{1}=D_{2}=0
, thus failing to be covered by the results in the previously cited references.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dX(t)=\left(A_{1}X(t)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}+B_{1}u(t)+B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)dt+C\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}dW(t),\ t\geq 0.\\
&X(0)=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

With the notation
\bar{Y}:=\begin{pmatrix}Y\\
y\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}X-\mathbb{E}{\left[X\right]}\\
\mathbb{E}{\left[X\right]}\end{pmatrix}
, and
\bar{u}:=\begin{pmatrix}u-\mathbb{E}{\left[u\right]}\\
\mathbb{E}{\left[u\right]}\end{pmatrix}
, we have the following equivalent equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d\bar{Y}(t)={\left(\bar{A}\bar{Y}(t)+\bar{B}\bar{u}(t)\right)}dt+\bar{C}\bar{Y}(t)dW(t),\ t\geq 0,\\
&\bar{Y}(0)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}0\\
x\end{array}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

To shorten notations, we denote the matrix coefficients in (
4.2
) by

\bar{A}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{1}&0\\
0&A_{1}+A_{2}\end{array}\right),\ \bar{B}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{1}&0\\
0&B_{1}+B_{2}\end{array}\right)\text{and}\ \bar{C}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&C\\
0&0\end{array}\right).

We begin with a simple result on the integrability of
\phi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\phi\right]}
in the cases presented at the beginning.

Proposition 4.1
.

1) For
p\in[1,\infty],\ and\ \xi\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}
, one has
\xi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}
;

2) For
p,\ q\in[1,\infty]
,

(a) Whenever
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{q}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}\right)}
, it follows that

\phi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\phi\right]}\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{min\{p,q\}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}\right)}.

(b) Whenever
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right)}
, it follows that

\phi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\phi\right]}\in\mathbb{L}^{q}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right)}.

(c) Whenever
v\in\mathcal{U}^{p}{\left({\left[0,T\right]}\right)}
(resp.
v\in\mathcal{U}^{p}_{r}{\left({\left[0,T\right]}\right)}
), then

v-\mathbb{E}{\left[v\right]}\in\mathcal{U}^{p}{\left({\left[0,T\right]}\right)}(\text{resp. }v-\mathbb{E}{\left[v\right]}\in\mathcal{U}^{p}_{r}{\left({\left[0,T\right]}\right)}).

Proof.

1) One simply writes
\|\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}\|^{p}_{H}\leq{\left(\mathbb{E}{\left[\|\xi\|_{H}\right]}\right)}^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}{\left[\|\xi\|_{H}^{p}\right]}
to conclude.

2)-(a) Let
p<\infty,q<\infty
and
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{q}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};H\right)}\right)}
.
If
p\leq q
, then, using Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbb{E}{\left[\phi(t)\right]}\|_{H}^{p}dt\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|\phi(t)\|_{H}^{p}dt\right]}\leq\mathbb{E}{\left[{\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\phi(t)\|_{H}^{q}dt\right)}^{\frac{p}{q}}\right]}T^{1-\frac{p}{q}}<\infty.

If
p>q
, then, by the monotonicity of norms,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbb{E}{\left[\phi(t)\right]}\|_{H}^{q}dt\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|\phi(t)\|_{H}^{q}dt\right]}\leq{\left(\mathbb{E}{\left[{\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\phi(t)\|_{H}^{q}dt\right)}^{\frac{p}{q}}\right]}\right)}^{\frac{q}{p}}<\infty.

The conclusion is extended to the cases when
\{p,q\}\ni+\infty
using the expression of
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
norms as non-decreasing limits of
\mathbb{L}^{m}
ones, as

2)-(b) Again, we can reduce our analysis to the cases when

to conclude that

2)-(c) The reader is reminded that
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{p}
is a progressively measurable control such that

B_{1}u(\cdot)\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{1}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right)}\ \text{and}\ B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(\cdot)\right]}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}.

By assertion 2)-(a),

B_{1}(u(\cdot)-\mathbb{E}{\left[u(\cdot)\right]})=B_{1}u(\cdot)-\mathbb{E}{\left[B_{1}u(\cdot)\right]}\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{1}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right)},

which is equivalent to

.

The assertion on
\mathcal{U}_{r}^{p}
is quite similar and based on 2)-(b).

Remark 4.1
.

If
\bar{B}
is of full rank (
2d
), then

\mathcal{U}^{p}=\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{1}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{2n}\right)}\right)}
, and similarly,
\mathcal{U}^{p}_{r}=\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2n}\right)}\right)}

The following result shows that, by weakening the regularity of the control processes, we can guarantee the controllability of
\left\{\left(\xi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]},\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}\right),\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right\}

Lemma 4.2
.

If
Rank(B_{1}+B_{2})=Rank(B_{1})=d
, and
p\geq 1
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}
, then, there exists
u\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right)}
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\bar{B}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u(t)-\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\\
\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\end{array}\right)dt=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\xi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}\\
\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}\end{array}\right).

Proof.

We recall that
\bar{B}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{1}&0\\
0&B_{1}+B_{2}\end{array}\right).
Due to Lemma
4.1
, there exists
v\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right)}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}v(t)dt=\xi,\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

We now define

\displaystyle u(\cdot)

\displaystyle:=B_{1}^{*}(B_{1}B_{1}^{*})^{-1}(v(\cdot)-\mathbb{E}{\left[v(\cdot)\right]})+\frac{1}{T}(B_{1}+B_{2})^{*}[(B_{1}+B_{2})(B_{1}+B_{2})^{*}]^{-1}\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]},

and note that
u(\cdot)\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;H\right)}\right)}
due to Proposition
4.1
, assertion 2)-(b) (take also a look at assertion (c) dealing with matrix multiplied controls).

We have

\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}=\frac{1}{T}(B_{1}+B_{2})^{*}[(B_{1}+B_{2})(B_{1}+B_{2})^{*}]^{-1}\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}

so that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(B_{1}+B_{2})\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}dt=\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}.

Furthermore,
B_{1}{\left(u(t)-\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}=v(t)-\mathbb{E}{\left[v(t)\right]}
so that

\int_{0}^{T}B_{1}{\left(u(t)-\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}dt=\xi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}.

The claim is now proven.

We consider the fundamental matrix equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d\mathcal{Y}(t)=-\mathcal{Y}(t)\bar{A}dt,\ t\in[0,T],\\
&\mathcal{Y}(0)=I_{2d},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.3)

whose solution is obviously deterministic and belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\right)}
. In particular, note that

\mathcal{Y}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{Y}_{1}(t):=e^{-tA_{1}}&0\\
0&\mathcal{Y}_{2}(t):=e^{-t(A_{1}+A_{2})}\end{array}\right),\ t\in[0,T],

is block-diagonal.
With the notation

\bar{X}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X(t)\\
x(t)=\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}\end{array}\right),\ t\in[0,T],

the autonomous deterministic system

is exactly controllable to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-targets (with

), which is equivalent to the invertibility of

\mathcal{G}_{2}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t(A_{1}+A_{2})}(B_{1}+B_{2})(B_{1}+B_{2})^{*}e^{-t(A_{1}+A_{2})^{*}}dt.

We will enforce the following stronger assumption, partly inspired by what happens in the non-mean-field framework.

Assumption 4.1
.

We ask

\mathcal{G}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{1}&0\\
0&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{Y}_{2}(t)(B_{1}+B_{2})(B_{1}+B_{2})^{*}\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{2}(t)dt\end{array}\right)

to be invertible, with
\mathcal{Y}_{2}(t)=e^{-t(A_{1}+A_{2})}
, for

.

We can now give the main result of the section providing an affirmative answer to
Q2

Theorem 4.1
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, for
p>1
the system (
4.1
) is exactly controllable to
\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}
-targets by using

Proof.

The idea of the proof is quite simple. In order to get to the final condition, one employs a BSDE and leave aside the control. Second, using the control, one compensates the Brownian part appearing in the BSDE, together with the initial data coming from the same BSDE.

We consider
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}
. First, we consider the BSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dY_{1}(t)=\left(A_{1}Y_{1}(t)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[Y_{1}(t)\right]}\right)dt+(C\mathbb{E}{\left[Y_{1}(t)\right]}+Z_{1}(t))dW(t),\ t\leq T,\\
&Y_{1}(T)=\xi-\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.4)

It is clear that
\mathbb{E}{\left[Y_{1}\right]}
satisfies a linear deterministic equation with final data 0, thus
\mathbb{E}{\left[Y_{1}(t)\right]}=0
, for all
t\in[0,T]
. The solution
(Y_{1},Z_{1})\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;C{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right)}\times\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right)}
. Second, we set

\displaystyle u_{0}(t):=

\displaystyle-(B_{1}+B_{2})^{*}\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{2}(t)\mathcal{G}_{2}^{-1}(-e^{-T(A_{1}+A_{2})}\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}+x_{0}-Y_{1}(0)).

(4.5)

Next, we note that
u_{0}
is deterministic and we consider the BSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dY_{2}(t)=\left(A_{1}Y_{2}(t)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[Y_{2}(t)\right]}+B_{1}u_{0}(t)+B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u_{0}(t)\right]}\right)dt\\
&\quad\hskip 42.67912pt+(C\mathbb{E}{\left[Y_{2}(t)\right]}+Z_{2}(t))dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
&Y_{2}(T)=\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.6)

Notice that
\mathbb{E}{\left[u_{0}(t)\right]}=u_{0}(t)
(since
u_{0}(t)
is deterministic). However, we write the equation like this to make the drift similar to the one in (
4.1
) for the forward equation.
One easily checks that
Y_{2}(0)=\mathbb{E}{\left[Y_{2}(0)\right]}
is given by

\begin{split}Y_{2}(0)&=\mathcal{Y}_{2}(T)\mathbb{E}{\left[\xi\right]}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{Y}_{2}(t)(B_{1}+B_{2})u_{0}(t)dt=x_{0}-Y_{1}(0).\end{split}

(4.7)

To end the proof, we need to compensate the
Z
terms. For this, we proceed as follows. We consider the fundamental matrix equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d\Phi(t)=A_{1}\Phi(t)dt,\ t\in[0,T],\\
&\Phi(0)=I_{d},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.8)

whose inverse satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d\Psi(t)=-\Psi(t)A_{1}dt,\ t\in[0,T],\\
&\Psi(0)=I_{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.9)

Both solutions belong to
C{\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\right)}
. The solution to

\begin{cases}&dx(t)={\left(A_{1}x(t)+B_{1}u(t)\right)}dt-{\left(Z_{1}(t)+Z_{2}(t)\right)}dW(t),\ t\geq 0,\\
&x(0)=0,\end{cases}

(4.10)

is given by

x(t)=\Phi(t)\int_{0}^{t}\Psi(s)B_{1}u(s)ds-\Phi(t)\int_{0}^{t}\Psi(s){\left(Z_{1}(s)+Z_{2}(s)\right)}dW(s).

Using Lemma
4.1
with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Psi(s){\left(Z_{1}(s)+Z_{2}(s)\right)}dW(s)\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)},

we see that there exists
v(\cdot)\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right)}
,
such that

\int_{0}^{T}v(t)dt=\int_{0}^{T}\Psi(s){\left(Z_{1}(s)+Z_{2}(s)\right)}dW(s)\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)},\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Note that
v(\cdot)-\mathbb{E}{\left[v(\cdot)\right]}
satisfies the same equality and belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\right)}
. We set

u_{2}(t):=B_{1}^{*}(B_{1}B_{1}^{*})^{-1}\Phi(t)v(t),

to get a process belonging to
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left({\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{L}^{p}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\right)}
and such that the solution of (
4.10
) associated to

satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dx(t)=\left(A_{1}x(t)+B_{1}(u_{2}(t)-\mathbb{E}{\left[u_{2}(t)\right]})\right)dt\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-{\left(Z_{1}(t)+Z_{2}(t)\right)}dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
&x(0)=0,\ x(T)=0,\ \mathbb{E}{\left[x(t)\right]}=0,\ \forall t\in[0,T],\end{aligned}\right.

or, again,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dx(t)=\left(A_{1}x(t)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[x(t)\right]}+B_{1}(u_{2}(t)-\mathbb{E}{\left[u_{2}(t)\right]})\right)dt\\
&\quad\hskip 28.45274pt+\left(C\mathbb{E}{\left[x(t)\right]}-Z_{1}(t)-Z_{2}(t)\right)dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
&x(0)=0,\ x(T)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.11)

It follows from (

Q3.

Let us now focus on the case when

5.1
What can one do with first-order moments?

Let us consider the scalar case,
d=1
and consider the scalar equation.

dx(t)={\left[a_{1}x(t)+a_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[x(t)\right]}+b\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right]}dt+\delta\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}dW(t),\ x(0)=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.1)

When
\delta\neq 0
, the extra term
C\mathbb{E}{\left[x(t)\right]}
in the diffusion coefficient in the equation (
1.4
) can be dealt with by setting
v:=c_{2}\delta^{-1}x+u
and changing the drift coefficient
a_{2}
accordingly.

A simple glance at (
5.1
) shows that
\mathbb{P}_{x(t)}
is a Gaussian law; alternatively, the reader may take a look at
[
3
, Section 3.2]
. On the other hand, Gaussian laws are completely determined by their first and second order moments. Now
x_{1}(t):=\mathbb{E}{\left[x(t)\right]}
follows a simple equation

dx_{1}(t)={\left[(a_{1}+a_{2})x_{1}(t)+bv(t)\right]}dt,\ x_{1}(0)=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},

with
v(t):=\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}
, an equation
which is exactly/exactly null/approximately controllable if and only if
b\neq 0
. This equation is, however, exactly terminal controllable even if
b=0
(just take any
v
, then pick the initial condition
x
accordingly). Now we assume
b\neq 0
.

Furthermore, the second order moment
x_{2}(t):=\mathbb{E}{\left[\left|x(t)\right|^{2}\right]}
satisfies

dx_{2}(t)={\left(2a_{1}x_{2}(t)+2a_{2}x_{1}^{2}(t)+2bx_{1}(t)v(t)+\delta^{2}v^{2}(t)\right)}dt,

while

dx_{1}^{2}(t)={\left[2(a_{1}+a_{2})x_{1}^{2}(t)+2bx_{1}(t)v(t)\right]}dt,

so that the variance satisfies

dVar(x(t))={\left(2a_{1}Var(x(t))+\delta^{2}v^{2}(t)\right)}dt.

It follows that, starting from a deterministic initial value
x(0)
,

Var(x(T))=\delta^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2a_{1}(T-t)}v^{2}(t)dt.

Let us consider
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and see that, for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
,

v(t):=e^{-(a_{1}+a_{2})(T-t)}{\left[\frac{\alpha-x_{0}e^{(a_{1}+a_{2})T}}{Tb}+\zeta{\left(\mathbf{1}_{[\frac{T}{2},T]}(t)-\mathbf{1}_{[0,\frac{T}{2})}(t)\right)}\right]}

(5.2)

controls
x_{1}(t)\left(=\mathbb{E}{\left[x(t)\right]}\right)
from
x_{0}
to
\alpha
. One has

\displaystyle Var(x(T))=\delta^{2}\psi(T)\Big[

\displaystyle{\left(\frac{\alpha-x_{0}e^{(a_{1}+a_{2})T}}{Tb}-\zeta\right)}^{2}+e^{a_{2}T}{\left(\frac{\alpha-x_{0}e^{(a_{1}+a_{2})T}}{Tb}+\zeta\right)}^{2}\Big],

\displaystyle\textnormal{where }\psi(T)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{-a_{2}T}-e^{-2a_{2}T}}{2a_{2}},&\textnormal{ if }a_{2}\neq 0,\\
\frac{T}{2},&\textnormal{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows that, starting from
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and using the aforementioned control family
v
defined in (
5.2
), at time
T
, one can obtain all normal laws
\mathcal{N}{\left(\alpha,\beta^{2}\right)}
, for all

\beta^{2}\geq\delta^{2}\psi(T)\frac{4}{1+e^{-a_{2}T}}{\left(\frac{\alpha-x_{0}e^{(a_{1}+a_{2})T}}{Tb}\right)}^{2}.

In particular, one covers all
\beta^{2}\geq 0
when starting at
x_{0}:=\alpha e^{-(a_{1}+a_{2})T}
. This is also true when
b=0
, since the associated term containing
\frac{\alpha-x_{0}e^{(a_{1}+a_{2})T}}{Tb}
can be set to
0
as well.
We have proven the following definition-characterization for the case

.

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem-Definition)
.

D_{1}=B_{1}=0_{d\times n}

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}each^{\mathbb{P}}_{T}(x):={\left\{\mathbb{P}_{X^{x,u}(T)}\ :\ u\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}{\left(\Omega\times{\left[0,T\right]};\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\right\}}\\
&\quad\quad\subset\mathcal{G}auss:={\left\{\mathcal{N}(\alpha,\Sigma)\ :\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \Sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}\right\}},\end{split}

(5.3)

where

is the solution of SDE (
1.4
) with initial value

(see also
[

denotes the set of positive semidefinite matrices in

5.2
ETCNL in the Multi-dimensional Case

We are now dealing with the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)=&{\left(A_{1}X(t)+A_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}+B_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}dt\\
&\ \ +{\left(C\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)\right]}+D_{2}\mathbb{E}{\left[u(t)\right]}\right)}dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
X(0)=&X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.4)

We have the following theorem characterizing the ETCNL and providing an affirmative answer to
Q3
.

Theorem 5.2
.

1) If
Range(C)\subset Range(D_{2})
and the system (
5.4
) is exactly terminal controllable to normal laws, then

\displaystyle\ Rank{\left({\left[D_{2}\ \vdots\ A_{1}D_{2}\ \vdots\ \ldots\ \vdots\ A_{1}^{d-1}D_{2}\right]}\right)}

\displaystyle=d.

(5.5)

2) If one enforces the slightly stronger condition

, then the system (
5.4
) is exactly terminal controllable to normal laws.

Proof.

1) For the necessary condition, we can assume, without loss of generality, that
C=0
(Indeed, under the range condition, one is able to find a matrix
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
such that
D_{2}N=C
and one changes the deterministic control
u:=\mathbb{E}[u]
to
v=u+N\mathbb{E}[X]
. The coefficients

and

remains unchanged, while

becomes
\tilde{A}_{2}:=A_{2}-B_{2}N
).
Reasoning on the expectation of
X
, as we have already done in the one-dimensional case, we have the deterministic equation for
X_{1}(t)=\mathbb{E}[X(t)]
:

dX_{1}(t)={\left[(A_{1}+A_{2})X_{1}(t)+B_{2}v(t)\right]}dt,\ t\geq 0,\ X_{1}(0)=X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
v(t):=\mathbb{E}[u(t)]
.
By writing the tensor equation, it follows that

\displaystyle d{\left[X_{1}X_{1}^{*}\right]}(t)=\Big[

\displaystyle(A_{1}+A_{2}){\left[X_{1}X_{1}^{*}\right]}(t)+{\left[X_{1}X_{1}^{*}\right]}(t)(A_{1}+A_{2})^{*}

\displaystyle+X_{1}(t)v^{*}(t)B_{2}^{*}+B_{2}v(t)X_{1}^{*}(t)\Big]dt.

Furthermore, if
X_{2}(t):=\mathbb{E}{\left[X(t)X^{*}(t)\right]}
, then

\displaystyle dX_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\Big\{A_{1}X_{2}(t)+X_{2}(t)A_{1}^{*}+A_{2}{\left[X_{1}X_{1}^{*}\right]}(t)+{\left[X_{1}X_{1}^{*}\right]}(t)A_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+X_{1}(t)v^{*}(t)B_{2}^{*}+B_{2}v(t)X_{1}^{*}(t)+D_{2}(vv^{*})(t)D_{2}^{*}\Big\}dt.

For
Cov(t):=Cov(X(t))=(X_{2}-X_{1}X_{1}^{*})(t)
, one gets

dCov(t)={\left(A_{1}Cov(t)+Cov(t)A_{1}^{*}+D_{2}(vv^{*})(t)D_{2}^{*}\right)}dt.

The variation of constants formula gives

Cov(T)=\int_{0}^{T}e^{(T-t)A_{1}}D_{2}(vv^{*})(t)D_{2}^{*}e^{(T-t)A_{1}^{*}}dt.

If the rank condition (
5.5
) on
(A_{1},D_{2})
is not fulfilled, then it is easy to find a unitary vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is orthogonal on all
A_{1}^{k}D_{2}
, hence orthogonal on

. Then
a^{*}Cov(T)a=0
, for all controls
v
which means that
aa^{*}
cannot be obtained as a covariance. We have our contradiction.

2) Similarly, since one requires
Rank(D_{2})=d
, one can, once again, assume for (
5.4
) that
C=0
. Let us fix
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d}
. If
\Sigma
can be reached with a deterministic control
v
such that
Cov(T)=\Sigma
, then one simply sets, for this control,

X_{1}(0)=x:=e^{-T(A_{1}+A_{2})}\alpha-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t(A_{1}+A_{2})}B_{2}v(s)ds

to have the average
\alpha
for the expectation of
X(T)
, that is,
\mathbb{E}[X(T)]=X_{1}(T)=\alpha
. It follows that one only needs to show how
\Sigma
can be obtained.

By the spectral decomposition theorem,

\Sigma=\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\mu_{k}\zeta_{k}\zeta^{*}_{k},

where
\mu_{k}
are the non-negative eigenvalues and
\zeta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the associated eigenvectors. Since
D_{2}
is assumed to be of full rank, for every
t\geq 0
, there exists a solution
v_{k}(t)
of

D_{2}v_{k}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{k}}{T}}e^{(t-T)A_{1}}\zeta_{k},

5.3
How Does ETCNL Compare to Other Types of Controllability?

The short answer: It is a
completely new concept
.

(I)
While it is implied by exact (terminal) controllability in
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}
, it is a class disjoint from the approximate controllability concept in
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}
.
Indeed, since
\mathcal{G}auss
is closed with respect to the weak-* convergence of probability measures, if one thinks about Wasserstein metric, for instance,
\mathcal{G}auss
does not approximate non Gaussian laws. As such it does not imply approximate (terminal) controllability in law (ATCL)
1
1

1

For the controlled solutions, we have set, in (
5.3
), the reachable set in law to be
\mathcal{R}each^{\mathbb{P}}_{T}(x)
. Then, exact terminal controllability in law to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
from
x
is valid if
\mu\in\mathcal{R}each^{\mathbb{P}}_{T}(x).
By analogy, the concept of approximate terminal controllability in law to
\mu
starting at
x

is valid if
\mu\in cl\left(\mathcal{R}each^{\mathbb{P}}_{T}(x)\right)
, where the

is the Kuratowski closure operator w.r.t. the Wasserstein distance

. In other words,
\mu
is ATCL

. Hence, it cannot imply approximate controllability in

.

(II)
The 1-dimensional dynamics

provides an example of a system that is approximately controllable to

(III)
A system may be ETCNL, but failing to be approximate null-controllable (although, obviously, it will be exactly terminal null-controllable).

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have completed the approach in
[
8
]
by showing that the joint rank conditions on
D_{1}
and
D_{1}+D_{2}
are required for exact controllability with square-integrable controls. When this fails, we have provided the mean-field analogous of the exact controllability with less regular controls from
[
20
]
, priorly highlighted in
[
16
]
.
When the required conditions fail both on the drift and on the noise coefficient, we introduce a new notion of controllability in law tailored for mean-field systems. In addition, we introduce a class of Wasserstein set-valued BSDEs and give a complete descrition of the sets of solutions for uni-dimensional systems. The multi-dimensional case is a work-in-progress as are more general notions of controllability in law.

Homology of degenerate real projective quadrics
Mohamad Maassarani

Abstract
. Homology of non degenerate real projective quadrics was studied by Steenrod and Tucker. We Compute the rational and the ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}} homology of degenerate real projective quadrics. This allows to determine the integer homology of these quadrics.



Introduction and main results

A quadratic form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
defines a real projective quadric in
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
. The homology groups of non degenerate quadrics, was studied by Steenrod and Tucker, in
[
ST41
]
. They show that a non degenerate quadric is a sphere bundle over a projective space, and that the homology over
{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
of the quadric is the tensor product of the homology of the base and the fiber. In this document, we consider the homology of degenerate quadrics. We compute the rational and
{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
-homology of these quadrics. This allows to determine the integer homology of degenerate quadrics.

A real projective quadric is homeomorphic by a projective transformation to
Q_{p,q}^{n}
the real locus in
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
of the quadratic form
\mathbf{q}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{p}^{2}-x_{p+1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{p+q}^{2}
for some
p,q
(
p+q\leq n
). Hence, in this text we only consider
Q_{p,q}^{n}
and the computations are somtimes done for
q\geq p
since
Q_{p,q}^{n}
is homeomorphic to
Q_{q,p}^{n}
. We note also that
Q_{0,q}^{n}
is either empty either homeomorphic to a projective space.

The paper is divided into 4 sections :

In the first section, we compute the integer homology of a join
X\star Y
of two spaces
X
and
Y
in terms of the homology of
X
and
Y
, under the assumption
Tor(H_{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}),H_{j}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}}))=0
for all
i,j
. We also consider the induced map in homology
H_{*}(X\star Y,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{*}(X\star Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
of
f\star g
constructed out of two continuous maps
f:X\to X
and
g:Y\to Y
. Results of section 1 are used in section 2.

In the second section, we show that for,
p,q,n-p-q\geq 1
, the space
X_{p,q}^{n}
obtained by intersecting the locus of
\mathbf{q}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with the
n-1
-dimensional unit sphere
S^{n-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, is a
2
-sheeted cover of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
. We prove that
X_{p,q}^{n}
is homeomorphic to the join
(S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1})\star S^{n-p-q-1}
and compute its integer and rational homology. We then compute the rational homology of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
as an invariant vector subspace of the rational homology of
X_{p,q}^{n}
.

Section
3
, is devoted to the
{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
-homology groups of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
. The projectivisation
D
of the kernel of
\mathbf{q}
is homeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{n-p-q-1}
,
Q_{p,q}^{n}\setminus D
is a real vector bundle of rank
n-p-q
over
Q_{p,q}^{p+q}
and the quotient space
Q_{p,q}^{n}/D
is homeomorphic to the Thom space of
Q_{p,q}^{n}\setminus D
. We derive essentially from the exact sequence of the pair
(Q_{p,q}^{n},D)
and the Thom isomorphism that
H_{n-p-q+k}(Q_{p,q}^{n},{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}})
is homeomorphic to
H_{k}(Q_{p,q}^{p+q},{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}})
for
k>0
. This gives the homology groups in "high" degree, since the homology groups of
Q_{p,q}^{p+q}
are known from
[
ST41
]
. The "lower"
{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
-homology groups are computed using the exact sequence of the cover
X_{p,q}^{n}\to Q_{p,q}^{n}
.

In the last section, we show that the integer homology groups
H_{k}(Q_{p,q}^{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
are of the form
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{k}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}^{l_{k}}
where
m_{l}
and
l_{k}
are determined by the Betti numbers of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and over
{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
. We compute the integer homology of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
for the case
q>p>1
,
n>p+q+1
and where
p,q
and
n
are even. The other cases can also be computed; we do not consider them to avoid more computations.

2.
Double cover and rational homology of degenerate quadrics

For
p,q\geq 1
and
n\geq p+q
, we denote by
Q_{p,q}^{n}
is the real locus in
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
of the quadratic form
\mathbf{q}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}},\mathbf{q}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{p}^{2}-x_{p+1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{p+q}^{2}
. The real locus in
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
of any quadratic form of signature
(p,q)
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, is homeomorphic via a projective trasformation to
Q_{p,q}^{n}
.
If
p
or
q
are zero,
Q_{p,q}^{n}
is homeomorphic to a projective space (eventually
\emptyset
). We assume in the following that
p,q,n-p-q\geq 1
.

In this section, we consider, in the first subsection, a
2
-sheeted cover of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
and show that it is homeomorphic to a join. We describe the deck transformation group of this cover.
In the second subsection, we compute the integer and rational homology of the
2
-sheeted cover using results of section
1
. We then compute the rational homology of
Q_{p,q}^{n}
as an invariant vector subspace of the rational homology of the cover.

2.1.
The double cover and the antipode

Denote by
X_{p,q}^{n}
the intersection :

X_{p,q}^{n}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}|\mathbf{q}(x)=0\}\cap S^{n-1},

where
S^{n-1}
denotes the unit sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Let
||\cdot||
denote the euclidian norm of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. By identifying
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{q}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-p-q}
,
X_{p,q}^{n}
is identified to :

X_{p,q}^{n}=\{(x,y,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{q}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-p-q}|\ \ ||x||=||y||,||x||^{2}+||y||^{2}+||z||^{2}=1\}.

Define the continuous map
f:S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1}\times S^{n-p-q-1}\times[0,1]\to X_{p,q}^{n}
given by :

f(x,y,z,t)=(\frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}x,\frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}y,(1-t)z),

for
(x,y,z,t)\in S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1}\times S^{n-p-q-1}\times[0,1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

1)

The map
f
defined above is surjective.

2)

f(x,y,z,t)=f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},t^{\prime})
if and only if
t=0
and
(x,y)=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
or
t=1
and
z=z^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This can be checked using the identification

X_{p,q}^{n}=\{(x,y,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{q}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-p-q}|\ \ ||x||=||y||,||x||^{2}+||y||^{2}+||z||^{2}=1\},

and the definition of
f
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

The map
f
of the previous proposition induces an homeomorphism
\bar{f}:(S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1})\star S^{n-p-q-1}\to X_{p,q}^{n}
.

Proof.

Point
2)
of the last proposition implies that
f
induces a continuous injective map
\bar{f}:(S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1})\star S^{n-p-q-1}\to X_{p,q}^{n}
. With point
1)
of the same proposition, we get that
\bar{f}
is a continuous bijection. Since both spaces are compact, we deduce that
\bar{f}
is a homeomorphism.
∎

Since a join of spaces is path connected we have :

Corollary 2.3
.

X_{p,q}^{n}
is path connected.

Let
a_{n-1}:S^{n-1}\to S^{n-1}
be the antipode (involution mapping
x
to
-x
) and
\pi
be the projection from
S^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
.
\pi
is a non trivial
2
-sheeted covering map with deck transformation group
\{1,a_{n-1}\}
. Since
\mathbf{q}(x)=\mathbf{q}(-x)
,
X_{p,q}^{n}
is stable under the antipod and :

Proposition 2.4
.

The map
X_{p,q}^{n}\overset{\pi}{\to}{Q_{p,q}^{n}}
is a non trivial
2
-sheeted covering map with deck transformation group
\{1,a_{n-1}\}
where
a_{n-1}
is the antipod.

Proof.

The map
X_{p,q}^{n}\overset{\pi}{\to}{Q_{p,q}^{n}}
induces a continuous bijection between
X_{p,q}^{n}/\{1,a_{n-1}\}
and
Q_{n,p}
. Since both spaces are compact the induced map is a homeomorphism. This proves that
X_{p,q}^{n}\overset{\pi}{\to}{Q_{p,q}^{n}}
is a
2
-sheeted cover because
X_{p,q}^{n}\to X_{p,q}^{n}/\{1,a_{n-1}\}
is a covering map. Finally, the cover is not trivial since
X_{p,q}^{n}
is path connected.
∎

Proposition 2.5

Let

be the homeomorphism of proposition
2.2

\bar{f}((a_{p-1}\times a_{q-1})\star a_{n-p-q-1})\bar{f}^{-1}(w)=a_{n-1}(w),

where

denotes the antipode of

Proof.

We will prove that

((a_{p-1}\times a_{q-1})\star a_{n-p-q-1})(v)=\bar{f}^{-1}a_{n-1}\bar{f}(v),

for

. We only need to prove the equality for

, since it is a dense subset of the join. Take
v=(x,y,z,t)\in S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1}\times S^{n-p-q-1}\times]0,1[

((a_{p-1}\times a_{q-1})\star a_{n-p-q-1})(x,y,z,t)=(-x,-y,-z,t).

Indeed,
((a_{p-1}\times a_{q-1})\star a_{n-p-q-1})
is induced by
((a_{p-1}\times a_{q-1})\times a_{n-p-q-1})\times\mathrm{id}_{[0,1]}

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{-1}a_{n-1}\bar{f}(x,y,z,t)

\displaystyle=\bar{f}^{-1}(-\frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}x,-\frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}y,-(1-t)z)

We have proved the proposition.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

[
ST41
]

For
q\geq p>0
, the homology with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
coefficients of

is isomorphic to

Proof.

As proved by Steenrod and Tucker the projection of

is reduced to two points. For

is the identity, hence

-homology of the fiber and we have a spectral sequence of the fibration with coefficient in

∎

\pi([x_{1}:\cdots x_{n}])=[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{p+q}]

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a map between topological spaces and
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
be an open cover of
X
. If the restrictions
f_{|U_{i}}
of
f
to the open sets

are continous for all
i\in I
, then

is continuous.

Proof.

Let
V
be an open set of
Y
,
f^{-1}(V)=\cup_{i\in I}f_{|U_{i}}^{-1}(V)
. If
f_{|U_{i}}
is continious then
f_{|U_{i}}^{-1}(V)
is open in
U_{i}
and hence open in
X
since
U_{i}
is open. This proves that, under the hypothesis in the proposition, the preimage of a open set is the union of open sets and therfore
f
is continuous.
∎

\pi:{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\setminus D\to{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{p+q-1}

.

Proposition 3.7

D

Proof.

We define the closed subset

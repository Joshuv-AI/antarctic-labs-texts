CM-liftability of simple superspecial abelian surfaces over prime fields Thanks: Keywords. abelian surfaces over prime fields, complex multiplications, CM-liftability.
Hsin-Yi Yang

Abstract
. For any prime p > 0 p>0 , we prove that simple superspecial abelian surfaces over 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} admit CM liftings after base change at most to 𝔽 p 2 \mathbb{F}_{p^{2}} , by using the residual reflex condition (RRC) and Lie types. The CM-liftability of ordinary simple abelian surfaces is proved by Serre-Tate, and the CM-liftability of almost ordinary simple abelian surfaces is proved by Oswal-Shankar and Bergström-Karemaker-Marseglia, respectively. As there can only be ordinary, almost ordinary, or supersingular simple abelian surfaces over 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} , our work is another step to complete the CM-liftability of simple abelian surfaces over 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} .

2.3.

We recall definitions of good reductions and CM liftings of abelian varieties admitting smCM.

2.4.
Residual reflex condition.

Let
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a simple abelian variety of dimension
g>0
, admitting smCM by CM field
L/\mathbb{Q}
.
In this subsection, we recall the residual reflex condition, which helps to detect if
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
has a CM lifting, up to
L
-isogeny.
To be more precise, if
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfies the residual reflex condition, then it has a CM lifting up to
L
-linear
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isogeny by
[
4
, Theorem 2.5.3]
.
Conversely, if
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
admits a CM lifting, then it satisfies the residual reflex condition, see
[
4
, Section 2.1.5]
.

We start with the Shimura-Taniyama formula.

Definition 2.13
.

[
4
, Section 2.1.4.1]

Fix an embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
Let
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a simple abelian variety over a finite field

, admitting smCM by CM field
L
.
Let
\textrm{Frob}_{A_{0},q}
be the Frobenius endomorphism of
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The

says there exists a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-valued CM type
\Phi
of
L
such that

(2.6)

\frac{\text{ord}_{w}({\rm{Frob}}_{A_{0},q})}{\text{ord}_{w}(q)}=\frac{|\{\phi\in\Phi\,|\,\,\phi\,\,\text{induces}\,\,w\}|}{[L_{w}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}

for all
w\in\Sigma_{L,p}
.
We call the right-hand side of (
2.6
)
the

of
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the left-hand side of (
2.6
) the
slope
of
\Phi
.
We also say that a CM type
\Phi
of
L

is realized by

A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
if the Shimura-Taniyama formula (
2.6
) holds with
\Phi
for all
w\in\Sigma_{L,p}
.

Remark 2.14
.

After fixing the embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
as in Definition

, we regard a CM type
\Phi
of

as a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-valued CM type of

.
Let

as in Definition

.
Then, we can rewrite the Shimura-Taniyama formula (

) to

for all

.
That is, the slope of the CM type

of

is the same as the slope of
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
if the Shimura-Taniyama formula (

) holds with

.

Remark 2.15
.

Let
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a simple abelian variety.
Suppose that
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
admits smCM by CM field

with CM structure

.
We may define the slope in a slightly general way as in
[

, Section 2.1.4.1]
, which we recall as follows.
Let

be the characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius endomorphism

of

.
Fix an embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
Let

be the set of roots (counted with multiplicity) of

Then, the set

, where

is a valuation at

Moreover,

\{\text{ord}_{p}(\lambda_{i})/\text{ord}_{p}(q)\}_{i=1,\dots,2g}=\{\text{ord}_{p}(w)\textrm{ord}_{w}(\textrm{Frob}_{A_{0},q})/\text{ord}_{p}(q)\}_{w\in\Sigma_{L,p}},

We point out that the number of distinct places

, see Section

Let

(2)

An

-linear (resp.

-linear) CM

-divisible group

-divisible

Example 3.2

Let

-linear CM

Definition 3.3
.

[
5
, Definition 4.28, 29]

Let
k
be a perfect field containing
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(1)

A
Dieudonné ring

is a ring generated by the Witt ring

and operators

,

such that

\textrm{FV}=\textrm{VF}=p,\quad\textrm{F}x=x^{\sigma}\textrm{F},\quad x\textrm{V}=\textrm{V}x^{\sigma},\quad\forall x\in W(k),

where
\sigma:W(k)\rightarrow W(k)
is the Teichmüller lift of the automorphism
k\rightarrow k
which is given by

y\mapsto y^{p}
for any

.

(2)

A
Dieudonné module over
R_{k}
is a left
R_{k}
-module
M
, such that
M
is a free
W(k)
-module of finite rank.

Let
k
be a perfect field containing
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We can attach contravariant and covariant Dieudonné modules to
p
-divisible groups over

, see
[
4
, Section 1.4.3.7]
and
[
5
, pp. 477-479]
, respectively.
We denote the contravariant Dieudonné module of a
p
-divisible group

by

and the covariant one by
\mathbb{D}(X)
.
Denote by

(resp.
\mathbb{D}
) the functor from the category of
p
-divisible groups over
k
to the category of Dieudonné modules over
R_{k}
, which is given by
X\mapsto M(X)
(resp.
X\mapsto\mathbb{D}(X)
) for any
p
-divisible group
X
over
k
.
The functor gives the equivalence between two categories.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
5
, Theorem 4.33]
,
[
5
, Section 1.4.1.6]

The functor
\rm{M}
(resp.

) is an anti-equivalence (resp. equivalence) of categories between the category of
p
-divisible groups over
k
and the category of Dieudonné modules over

.

Example 3.5
.

We continue the discussion in Example

.
Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a CM field of degree

with maximal totally real subfield

Let

-divisible group over

attached to

, and let

The Dieudonné module

Since

-linear CM

Let
k
be a perfect field contains
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
With slight ambiguity, we denote again by

the automorphism of

.
Then,

for any

Let

be the relative Frobenius of

, then

is the Frobenius endomorphism of

.
We denote

-module

Then, the operator

, Section B.3.5.8]

In addition, from

[

, B.3.5.9]

We can describe the tangent space

of a

.

[

, Theorem 4.33]

be a perfect field containing
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and let

be a

-divisible group.
There exists a functorial isomorphism

Remark 3.7

Remark 3.9

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p>0
be a prime.
Let
\pi
be a Weil-
p
number of our concern, and let
A_{\pi}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a simple supersingular abelian surface in the isogeny class determined by
\pi
, which is obtained by the Honda-Tate theorem.
Let
M
be the (covariant) Dieudonné module of
A_{\pi}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Then,
A_{\pi}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
is superspecial if and only if

{\rm{V}^{2}}M=pM,

where
{\rm{V}}
is the operator of the Dieudonné module
M
, obtained from the Frobenius endomorphism
\textrm{Frob}_{A_{\pi},p}
of
A_{\pi}
functorially.

Proof.

The sufficiency follows the definition of superspecial abelian varieties.
We explain the necessity.
Let
\pi
be a Weil-
p
number of our concern.
Let
L:=\mathbb{Q}(\pi)
be a CM field. Suppose that
A_{\pi}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
is superspecial.
Recall the Dieudonné ring
R_{k}
over a perfect field
k
, containing
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, from Definition
3.3
.
Then, we have
\textrm{V}^{2}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}))=p(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}))
, and hence

\textrm{V}^{2}/p\in\mathrm{End}_{R_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(M\otimes W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})).

Note that
V
is obtained from the Frobenius endomorphism
\textrm{Frob}_{A_{\pi},p}
of
A_{\pi}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
functorially, and that the quotient
\textrm{Frob}_{A_{\pi},p}^{2}/p
is a root of unity in
L
. Since
A_{\pi}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
is simple, the endomorphism algebra
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{0}(A_{\pi})
equals
L
.
In particular,
\textrm{Frob}_{A_{\pi},p}^{2}/p\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{0}(A_{\pi})
.
We have an isomorphism
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{0}(A_{\pi})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\cong\mathrm{End}_{R_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
functorially by
\mathbb{D}
, see
[
27
, p. 525]
.
So,

\textrm{V}^{2}/p\in\mathrm{End}_{R_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}.

Therefore, we have
\textrm{V}^{2}/p\in\mathrm{End}_{R_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(M)
. This completes the proof.

Keep the notation in the proof of Lemma

Then,

Corollary 4.5

Let
\pi

p

to

.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\pi
be a Weil-
p
number of our concern except
\sqrt{3}\zeta_{3}
, and let

be a simple superspecial abelian surface over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Let
L:=\mathbb{Q}(\pi)
be a CM field.
Let
\tilde{\iota}:L\hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{0}(A_{\pi})

Let

be any place in

lying above

be the

e_{w}^{1}
equals the valuation of
{\rm{Frob}}_{A_{\pi},p}
at

with respect to

Proof.

By Remark
3.16
, we can compute the Lie type of

\mathbb{F}_{p}

The categories of

-divisible groups over

Recall the functor

is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

Let
M
be the (covariant) Dieudonné module of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

By Corollary

, we know that

is an

-module.

M_{w}
equals
M_{w}

(1)

7.
RRC and Good Lie Type.

The relation between RRC and good Lie type is interesting because both of them give conditions on the existence of CM liftings.
Let
A_{0}
be an abelian variety over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, admitting smCM by CM field
L
with maximal totally real subfield
L_{0}
.
Then,
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is
L
-linearly isogenous to an abelian variety over

[
4

On the other hand, let

be a CM structure of

.
Then,

has good Lie type and satisfies the dimension condition by
[

In this section, we consider the relation between RRC and good Lie types. It is natural to ask whether RRC implies having a good Lie type, or having a good Lie type implies RRC, when

is supersingular, because of the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let

be a CM field with maximal totally real subfield

A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{5}
satisfies RRC with
\Phi
.
It satisfies the Shimura-Taniyama formula by Lemma
7.1
.
Next, we want to check if
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{5}
satisfies the reflex field condition with
\Phi
.
However, there exist CM types whose associated reflex fields are
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
.
Take
\Phi:=\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{4}\}
or
\{\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}\}
.
Since
5
is inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
, the residue field of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})

\mathbb{F}_{9}
.
Then,
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{5}
does not satisfy RRC with such
\Phi
.
In other words, with such
\Phi
, we see that
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{5}
has good Lie type after base change to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5}
, but does not satisfy RRC.

Example 7.4
.

We will see an abelian variety, which satisfies RRC, but does not have good Lie type.
Let
A_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{5^{2}}
be a simple supersingular abelian surface as in
[
13
,
Abelian Variety 2.25.a_az
]
, admitting smCM by CM field
L:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})
.
Its Frobenius endomorphism
\textrm{Frob}_{A_{0},5^{2}}
is a Galois conjugate of
5\zeta_{12}
by some element in the Galois group
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L/\mathbb{Q})
.
We have the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{-1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{0}:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})}

Note that
5
is inert in
L_{0}
.
Let
v
be the place in
L_{0}
lying above
5
.
Then,
v
splits completely in
L
, that is
v=w\overline{w}
in
L
, where
w
and
\overline{w}
are distinct primes lying above
5
in
L
.
In particular, the ramification index
e_{w}
(resp.
e_{\overline{w}}
) at
w
(resp.
\overline{w}

5
is
1
.
We may assume that there exists an embedding

by Serre’s tensor construction, see, for example,
[
4

Then,
(A_{0}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5},\tilde{\iota})

\displaystyle e_{w}^{1}+e_{\overline{w}}^{1}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle e_{w}^{2}+e_{\overline{w}}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

where
(e_{w}^{i},e_{\overline{w}}^{i})_{i=1,2}

On the other hand, by a similar argument as in Lemma
4.9
, we have the following equations

\displaystyle e_{w}^{1}+e_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle e_{\overline{w}}^{1}+e_{\overline{w}}^{2}

\displaystyle=1.

The pair
(e_{w}^{1},e_{w}^{2})
(resp.
(e_{\overline{w}}^{1},e_{\overline{w}}^{2})

(1,0)

(e_{w}^{1},e_{w}^{2})=(e_{\overline{w}}^{1},e_{\overline{w}}^{2})=(1,0)

(A_{0}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5},\tilde{\iota})

M

4

O_{L_{w}}=O_{L_{\overline{w}}}=O_{L_{0,v}}=O_{L_{0,v}^{\textrm{ur}}}

5

\displaystyle M=

\displaystyle M_{w}\bigoplus M_{\overline{w}}

\displaystyle\left(W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{5}}O_{L_{w}}\right)\bigoplus\left(W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{5}}O_{L_{\overline{w}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{5}}O_{L_{0,v}^{\textrm{ur}}}\right)\bigoplus\left(W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{5}}O_{L_{0,v}^{\textrm{ur}}}\right).

.

\displaystyle M_{w}=

\displaystyle M_{w}^{1}\bigoplus M_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5})^{\prime}\bigoplus W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5}),

M_{w}^{1}\cong W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5})^{\prime}

M_{w}^{2}\cong W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{5})

(e_{w}^{1},e_{w}^{2})=(1,0).

With the same argument, we have

(e_{\overline{w}}^{1},e_{\overline{w}}^{2})=(1,0),

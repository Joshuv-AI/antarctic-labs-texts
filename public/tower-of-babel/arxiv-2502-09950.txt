Mixing rate exponent of planar Fortuin-Kasteleyn percolation
Haoyu Liu Baojun Wu ∗ Zijie Zhuang

Abstract
Duminil-Copin and Manolescu (2022) recently proved the scaling relations for planar Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK) percolation. In particular, they showed that the one-arm exponent and the mixing rate exponent are sufficient to derive the other near-critical exponents. The scaling limit of critical FK percolation is conjectured to be a conformally invariant random collection of loops called the conformal loop ensemble (CLE). In this paper, we define the CLE analog of the mixing rate exponent. Assuming the convergence of FK percolation to CLE, we show that the mixing rate exponent for FK percolation agrees with that of CLE. We prove that the CLE κ mixing rate exponent equals 3 ​ κ 8 − 1 \frac{3\kappa}{8}-1 , thereby answering Question 3 of Duminil-Copin and Manolescu (2022). The derivation of the CLE exponent is based on an exact formula for the Radon-Nikodym derivative between the marginal laws of the odd-level and even-level CLE loops, which is obtained from the coupling between Liouville quantum gravity and CLE.

Introduction

1.1
Near-critical FK percolation and mixing rate exponent

Bernoulli percolation is a fundamental lattice model in statistical physics with phase transition and exhibits rich critical behavior. It is obtained by declaring each edge open or closed independently with the same probability.
Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK) percolation (also called the random-cluster model) introduced in
[
FK72
]
is a generalization of Bernoulli percolation obtained by weighting each cluster of open edges by a factor
q
. The planar FK percolation model is of particular interest due to its relation with the Ising and Potts models and 2D conformal field theories. For
q\in(0,4]
, planar FK percolation on the square lattice is conjectured to undergo a continuous phase transition, and moreover, at criticality converges to a conformally invariant random collection of loops called the conformal loop ensemble (CLE) with
\kappa=4\pi/\arccos(-\sqrt{q}/2)\in[4,8)
. We refer to
[
DC20
]
for reviews of recent progress on this model.

For lattice models with continuous phase transition, many natural observables at (near-)criticality decay polynomially and are encoded by critical exponents. These exponents are believed to satisfy a family of universal scaling relations even for models belonging to different universality classes
[
Wid65
,
Fis67
]
. Such scaling relations were first proved for planar Bernoulli percolation in a seminal work by Kesten
[
Kes87
]
, where the one-arm exponent
\xi_{1}
and the four-arm exponent
\xi_{4}
are sufficient to derive all the other near-critical exponents. Recently, Kesten’s scaling relations were extended to planar FK percolation by Duminil-Copin and Manolescu
[
DCM22
]
; see Equations (R1)–(R7) therein. They noted that the same relations hold, with the four-arm exponent replaced by the mixing rate exponent
\iota=\iota(q)
defined below in (
1.2
). Therefore, once the one-arm and the mixing rate exponents are obtained, the value of all other near-critical exponents can be derived. For the one-arm exponent, one can define its CLE analog and show that they are equal under the assumption that critical FK percolation interfaces converge to CLE. Moreover, the value of the CLE one-arm exponent was obtained in
[
SSW09
]
. The main focus of this paper is to formulate the CLE analog of the mixing rate exponent
\iota
and rigorously derive its value, as predicted in
[
DCM22
]
based on physical predictions for other near-critical exponents.

Throughout this paper, we focus on FK percolation on the square lattice with cluster weight
q\in(1,4]
. For
p\in(0,1)
and
R>1
, let
\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p,q}^{1}
and
\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p,q}^{0}
denote the probability measures of FK percolation with edge weight
p
and cluster weight
q
on the box
\Lambda_{R}:=[-R,R]^{2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
equipped with wired and free boundary conditions, respectively. We refer to Section
2.1
for the precise definition and basic properties of FK percolation. Let
e
be an edge next to the origin, write
\omega_{e}=1
if
e
is open, and
\omega_{e}=0
otherwise. Following
[
DCM22
]
, the mixing rate is defined as

\Delta_{p,q}(R):=\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p,q}^{1}[\omega_{e}]-\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p,q}^{0}[\omega_{e}].

(1.1)

Roughly speaking,
\Delta_{p,q}(R)
captures the influence of boundary conditions on
e
. The model undergoes a continuous phase transition at
p_{c}=\frac{\sqrt{q}}{1+\sqrt{q}}

[
BDC12
,
DCST17
]
, at which the mixing rate is expected to decay algebraically. The mixing rate exponent
\iota=\iota(q)
is then defined as

\iota=-\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\log\Delta_{p_{c},q}(R)}{\log R}

(1.2)

provided that such a limit exists. In other words,
\Delta_{p_{c},q}(R)=R^{-\iota+o(1)}
as
R\to\infty
.

One of the main results of this paper is the following theorem, which answers Question 3 of
[
DCM22
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
q\in(1,4]
and
\kappa=4\pi/\arccos(-\sqrt{q}/2)\in[4,6)

(Conjecture

\iota(q)
exists and equals
\frac{3\kappa}{8}-1
.

Plugging Theorem

[

Corollary 1.2
.

For
q\in(1,4]
and
\kappa=4\pi/\arccos(-\sqrt{q}/2)\in[4,6)
, assuming the convergence of critical
\mathrm{FK}_{q}
percolation interfaces to
\mathrm{CLE}_{\kappa}
(Conjecture

In

The rest of Section

1.2
Mixing rate exponent for CLE

The conformal loop ensemble (CLE) introduced in
[
She07
,
SW12
]
is a random collection of non-crossing planar loops, indexed by a parameter
\kappa\in(8/3,8)
. When
\kappa\in(8/3,4]
, each loop in CLE
κ
is a simple loop and does not touch other loops or the boundary. When
\kappa\in(4,8)
, the loops are non-simple and may touch each other or the boundary. We will consider a nested CLE
κ
on the box
[-1,1]^{2}
for
\kappa\in(8/3,8)
which can be constructed from non-nested ones using an iteration procedure. Each loop is naturally associated with a nesting level equal to the number of distinct loops surrounding it plus 1.
Now we focus on the collection of loops surrounding a given point, say, the origin. For
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<1
, let
\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}=(-r_{2},r_{2})^{2}\setminus[-r_{1},r_{1}]^{2}
, and let
\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r_{1},r_{2}}
(resp.
\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{even}}_{r_{1},r_{2}}
) be the event that there exists a non-contractible
\mathrm{CLE}_{\kappa}
loop in
\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}
with odd (resp. even) nesting level. For
\kappa\in(8/3,8)
,
\delta>0
and
r\in(0,\frac{1}{1+\delta})
, we define the mixing rate of CLE
κ
as

\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)=\frac{\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r,(1+\delta)r}]-\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{even}}_{r,(1+\delta)r}]}{\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{even}}_{r,(1+\delta)r}]}\,.

(1.3)

Let
\delta
be sufficiently small but fixed. The mixing rate exponent
\upiota=\upiota(\kappa)
for CLE
κ
is defined as

\upiota=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\log|\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)|}{\log r}.

(1.4)

The following theorem provides the sign and up-to-constant asymptotics of the mixing rate.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
\kappa\in(8/3,8)
. There exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\kappa)>0
such that the following holds for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
(the implicit constants in
\asymp
depend only on
\kappa
).

•

For
\kappa\in(8/3,6)
, we have
\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)>0
, and
\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)\asymp r^{\frac{3\kappa}{8}-1}
as
r\to 0
.

•

For
\kappa=6
, we have
\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)=0
for all
r\in(0,\frac{1}{1+\delta})
.

•

For
\kappa\in(6,8)
, we have
\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)<0
, and
-\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)\asymp r^{\frac{3\kappa}{8}-1}
as
r\to 0
.

In particular, for
\kappa\in(8/3,8)\!\setminus\!\{6\}
, the CLE mixing rate exponent
\upiota(\kappa)
exists and equals
\frac{3\kappa}{8}-1
.

In fact, we will prove a stronger result for general annular regions, not just for
\mathcal{A}_{r_{1},r_{2}}
; see Proposition
3.3
.
The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on an exact formula that compares the marginal laws of CLE
κ
loops with even and odd nesting levels, which may be of independent interest.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
be a simply-connected domain (e.g.
[-1,1]^{2}
or the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
).
For a
non-boundary-touching
(possibly self-touching) loop
\eta
in
D
, let
A_{\eta}
be the doubly connected domain bounded by
\partial D
and the outer boundary of
\eta
. Then,
A_{\eta}
is conformally equivalent to the annulus
\mathcal{A}_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\in(r,1)\}
for some unique
r=r(\eta)\in(0,1)
. We call
\tau=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{1}{r}
the
modulus
of
A_{\eta}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix
\kappa\in(8/3,8)
, and consider a nested
\mathrm{CLE}_{\kappa}
on a simply connected domain
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
that contains the origin. Let
\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{odd}}
(resp.
\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{even}}
) denote the law of a loop sampled from the counting measure of non-boundary-touching
\mathrm{CLE}_{\kappa}
loops surrounding the origin with odd (resp. even) nesting levels. Then

\frac{\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\eta)}{\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{even}}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\eta)}=\frac{\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}\sin(\frac{\kappa}{4}(m+1)\pi)r^{\frac{\kappa}{8}m^{2}+(\frac{\kappa}{4}-1)m}}{\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sin(\frac{\kappa}{4}(m+1)\pi)r^{\frac{\kappa}{8}m^{2}+(\frac{\kappa}{4}-1)m}}\,.

(1.5)

The right-hand side is
1+4\cos(\frac{\kappa-4}{4}\pi)r^{\frac{3\kappa}{8}-1}+O(r^{2(\frac{3\kappa}{8}-1)})
as
r\to 0
.

Remark 1.6
.

•

(Boundary-touching case) When
\kappa\in(4,8)
, the odd-level CLE loops, particularly the first level, may touch the boundary while the even-level loops do not. We remind the reader that in (
1.5
) we only compare the Radon-Nikodym derivatives of loops that do not touch the boundary.

•

(Partition function of SLE curves) The idea of expressing Radon-Nikodym derivatives for SLE curves or SLE loops via ratios of partition functions has been considered previously in, e.g.,
[
Wer08
,
Law09
,
Dub09
]
. Theorem
1.5
can be interpreted as determining the partition functions of CLE loops. In fact, the formula on the right-hand side of (
1.5
) can be extracted from the partition function of FK percolation on the annulus
[
Car06
]
, which is based on a non-rigorous Coulomb gas approach. We refer to Theorem
4.20
and Remark
4.21
for further explanation.

•

(The case
\kappa=6
) In the setting of Bernoulli percolation (i.e., cluster weight
q=1
), the mixing rate similarly defined as in (
1.1
) is always zero. This matches with the fact that the right-hand side of (
1.5
) is identically 1 and the CLE mixing rate
\Updelta_{\kappa}(r;\delta)=0
when
\kappa=6
.

•

(Generalizations) If we replace
\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{odd}}
(resp.
\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{even}}
) with the law of the outer boundary of odd-level (resp. even-level) non-boundary-touching CLE
κ
loops (which is a measure on simple loops), then (
1.5
) still holds. In Theorem
1.5
, we compare the laws of all the odd-level and even-level CLE loops. In fact, the same approach can be used to derive the Radon-Nikodym derivatives between loops at any two given levels.

1.3
Comparison of mixing rates between FK percolation and CLE

In this section, we explain the relation between the mixing rate of FK percolation and that of CLE. First, the former has an alternative description as follows. For a rectangle
D=[a,b]\times[c,d]
, let
\mathcal{C}(D)
be the event that there exists a horizontal open crossing connecting its left and right boundaries. For
q\in(1,4]
,
p\in(0,1)
, and
1\leq r<R
, define

\Delta_{p,q}(r,R):=\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p,q}^{1}[\mathcal{C}(\Lambda_{r})]-\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p,q}^{0}[\mathcal{C}(\Lambda_{r})].

(1.6)

By
[
DCM22
, Theorem 1.6(ii)]
, we have
\Delta_{p_{c},q}(r,R)\asymp\Delta_{p_{c},q}(R)/\Delta_{p_{c},q}(r)
. Hence, the mixing rate exponent should also satisfy the asymptotics
\Delta_{p_{c},q}(r,R)=(r/R)^{\iota+o(1)}
as
R/r\to\infty
. As noted already, it is
a priori
not clear whether such an exponent
\iota=\iota(q)
exists.

While the original definitions of the FK mixing rate given in (
1.1
) and (
1.6
) are useful for deriving scaling relations with other near-critical exponents
[
DCM22
]
, the crossing events
\{\omega_{e}=1\}
and
\mathcal{C}(\Lambda_{r})
are difficult to describe in terms of percolation interfaces. Hence, their scaling limits are hard to compute using CLE. To address this, we introduce another event
A(r;\delta)
defined below whose conjectured scaling limits are easy to describe in terms of CLE while still capturing the mixing rate.

FK percolation on the square lattice naturally induces a dual configuation on the dual graph; see Section
2.1
for the precise definitions. We call an edge open on the origin graph as primal open, and an edge open on the dual graph as dual open. For
r\geq 1
, let
A(r;\delta)
denote the event that there exist a non-contractible primal open circuit
\eta
and a non-contractible dual open circuit
\widetilde{\eta}
in the annulus
\Lambda_{r,(1+\delta)r}=\Lambda_{(1+\delta)r}\!\setminus\!\Lambda_{r}
, with
\widetilde{\eta}
lying inside of
\eta
. The following proposition states that the event
A(r;\delta)
also captures the mixing rate.

Proposition 1.7
.

For any fixed
q\in(1,4]
and
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exist constants
0<c<C
(depending only on
q
and
\delta
), such that for all
1000\delta^{-1}\leq 8r<R
, we have

c\cdot\Delta_{p_{c},q}(r,R)\leq\frac{\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}^{1}[A(r;\delta)]-\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}^{0}[A(r;\delta)]}{\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}^{0}[A(r;\delta)]}\leq C\cdot\Delta_{p_{c},q}(r,R).

(1.7)

Percolation interfaces of FK percolation (also referred to as the loop configuration) are non-self-crossing loops on the medial graph that separate open clusters and dual open clusters (see Section
2.1
). We associate each loop with a
nesting level
equal to the number of distinct loops surrounding it plus 1. Under the wired (resp. free) boundary conditions,
A(r;\delta)
is equivalent to the event that there exists a non-contractible percolation interface in
\Lambda_{r,(1+\delta)r}
with odd (resp. even) nesting level. The scaling limit of this event corresponds to
\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r,(1+\delta)r}
(resp.
\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{even}}_{r,(1+\delta)r}
). In light of this, we can combine Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
1.7
to prove the following theorem, which directly implies Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.8
.

For
q\in(1,4]
and
\kappa=4\pi/\arccos(-\sqrt{q}/2)\in[4,6)
, assuming the convergence of critical
\mathrm{FK}_{q}
percolation interfaces to
\mathrm{CLE}_{\kappa}
(Conjecture
3.1
),
\iota(q)
defined in (
1.2
) exists and we have
\iota(q)=\upiota(\kappa)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
assuming Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
1.7
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\wedge\delta_{0})
, where
\delta_{0}
is the constant in Theorem
1.3
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, and consider
r=\varepsilon R
. Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
small. Assuming Conjecture
3.1
, for all sufficiently large
R
, with high probability all loops surrounding the origin in the loop configuration with diameters larger than
r/4
, after rescaled by
1/R
, are each within distance
\frac{\delta}{2}(\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{1+\theta})\leq\frac{\delta}{2}(\varepsilon^{1-\theta}-\varepsilon)
from the corresponding CLE loop.
On this event and under the free boundary conditions, we have
\mathsf{A}_{\varepsilon^{1-\theta},(1+\delta)\varepsilon^{1+\theta}}^{\mathrm{even}}\subseteq A(\varepsilon R;\delta)\subseteq\mathsf{A}_{\varepsilon^{1+\theta},(1+\delta)\varepsilon^{1-\theta}}^{\mathrm{even}}
, which implies
\limsup_{R\to\infty}\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}^{0}[A(\varepsilon R;\delta)]\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{A}_{\varepsilon^{1-\theta},(1+\delta)\varepsilon^{1+\theta}}^{\mathrm{even}}]
and
\liminf_{R\to\infty}\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}^{0}[A(\varepsilon R;\delta)]\geq\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{A}_{\varepsilon^{1+\theta},(1+\delta)\varepsilon^{1-\theta}}^{\mathrm{even}}]
. Letting
\theta\to 0
, we find that
\lim_{R\to\infty}\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}^{0}[A(\varepsilon R;\delta)]=\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{A}_{\varepsilon,(1+\delta)\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{even}}]
. Similarly, we have
\lim_{R\to\infty}\phi_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}^{1}[A(\varepsilon R;\delta)]=\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{A}_{\varepsilon,(1+\delta)\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{odd}}]
. Combining Proposition
1.7
and Theorem
1.3
, we get
\liminf_{R\to\infty}\Delta_{p_{c},q}(\varepsilon R,R)\asymp\limsup_{R\to\infty}\Delta_{p_{c},q}(\varepsilon R,R)\asymp\varepsilon^{\upiota(\kappa)}
. It then follows from a standard application of quasi-multiplicativity (see
[
DCM22
, Theorem 1.6(ii)]
) that
\Delta_{p_{c},q}(r,R)=(r/R)^{\upiota(\kappa)+o(1)}
as
R/r\to\infty
.
∎

1.4
Proof overview

Let us provide a proof outline and organization of the paper.

In Section
2
, we will prove Proposition
1.7
, which provides an alternative description of the mixing rate of FK percolation. The proof is based on the increasing coupling of FK percolation developed in
[
DCM22
]
.
The key ingredient is Proposition
2.7
, which says that boosting the boundary conditions would increase the probability of the event
A(r;\delta)
by a uniformly positive amount. The main difficulty is that
A(r;\delta)
is not an increasing event, so a direct increasing coupling does not apply. We overcome this using a modified coupling approach.

In Sections
3
–
5
, we prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
which are purely about CLE and require no knowledge of FK percolation. In Section
3
, we first prove Theorem
1.3
based on Theorem
1.5
. This is achieved by integrating Theorem
1.5
over the loop configuration in an annulus. One subtlety is that there might be multiple non-contractible CLE loops in the annulus. We address this using the inclusion-exclusion principle and by choosing the annulus to be sufficiently thin so that with high probability there exists at most one non-contractible loop.

In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.5
for the non-simple region
\kappa\in(4,8)
using the coupling of CLE and Liouville quantum gravity (LQG). This idea originated from Sheffield’s observation that SLE curves can be viewed as the conformal welding interfaces between LQG surfaces
[
She16
,
DMS21
]
. This method was carried forward in
[
AHS24
]
to obtain various integrability results for SLE curves, thanks to the exact solvability of Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT)
[
DKRV16
,
GKR24
]
.
Our approach is another application of this idea, based on LQG on the annulus. In Section
4.2
, using the coupling of target-invariant SLE processes and LQG, we prove that a CLE loop of even or odd nesting level can be described as the conformal welding interface of a generalized quantum disk
[
DMS21
,
MSW21
,
AHSY23
]
and a generalized quantum annulus (Theorem
4.12
). The key observation in proving Theorem
1.5
is that, while the laws of moduli of these two quantum annuli are different, given the modulus, they are described by the same Liouville fields. Thus, the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{odd}}
and
\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{even}}
can be encoded by the modulus laws of these two quantum annuli. Following the techniques of
[
ARS22
]
, the laws of moduli can be extracted from the boundary length joint distributions of these quantum annuli which are in turn described by LCFT, thereby proving Theorem
1.5
for
\kappa\in(4,8)
. We refer to Sections
4.3
and
4.4
for details.
The simple region
\kappa\in(8/3,4]
of Theorem
1.5
follows a similar strategy, so we will only sketch the proof in Section
5
.

1.5
Outlook

In this section, we discuss some future work and open questions.

•

We first discuss the relation between the mixing rate exponent and the nested loop exponent introduced in
[
MN04
,
dN83
]
. For critical FK percolation on
\Lambda_{R}
with wired boundary conditions, let
\ell_{R}
be the number of percolation interfaces that surround the origin. For fixed
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the nested loop exponent
X_{\rm NL}(a)
is defined to satisfy

\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{R},p_{c},q}[a^{\ell_{R}}]=R^{-X_{\rm NL}(a)+o(1)}\quad\mbox{as }R\rightarrow\infty.

(1.8)

When
a>0
, assuming the conformal invariance conjecture, the nested loop exponent can be derived based on
[
SSW09
]
; see Section 1.2 of
[
ASYZ24
]
. When
a<0
, it is
a prior
unclear whether
X_{\rm NL}(a)
exists. However, when
a=-1
, by a duality argument, one can show that
X_{\rm NL}(-1)
agrees with the mixing rate exponent. It would be interesting to derive the nested loop exponent for other
a<0
.

•

The CLE mixing rate defined in (
1.3
) is reminiscent of the twist operators for the
\mathrm{O}(n)
loop model considered in
[
GC06
]
.
In particular, the scaling dimension of the twist operator is the same as the CLE mixing rate exponent. We expect that the CLE mixing rate can be used to construct the twist operator in the continuum by appropriately sending
\delta
and
r
to 0. The same mechanism should extend to multiple points by taking into account the Radon-Nikodym derivatives of even/odd nesting loops surrounding them. More interestingly, from the conformal field theory (CFT) perspective
[
BPZ84
]
, the twist operator is expected to be a degenerate operator at level
(1,2)

[
GC06
]
and thus to satisfy the BPZ equation
[
BPZ84
,
Pel19
]
.
This gives a possible way to define and compute a family of CLE correlation functions with more than three points. See
[
ACSW21
]
for recent work on CLE three-point correlation functions.

•

In the case of the Ising model, the above approach can be used to construct the Ising energy operator using CLE
16/3
and the Ising spin operator using CLE
3
. Furthermore, CLE

2
FK percolation and mixing rate

The main purpose of this section is to prove Proposition
1.7
, providing an equivalent description of the mixing rate of FK percolation. The proof mainly builds on an increasing coupling between FK percolation with different boundary conditions, developed in
[
DCM22
]
. We will first recall this coupling and some basic properties in Section
2.1
, and then prove Proposition
1.7
using a modified coupling method.

2.1
FK percolation: basic properties and increasing couplings

First, we define the FK percolation model and briefly recall its basic properties. For a detailed review, we refer the reader to
[
Gri06
,
BDC16
,
DC20
]
.

The planar square lattice is the graph
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},E(\mathbb{Z}^{2}))
with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and edges between nearest neighbors. Slightly abusing notations, we refer to the graph itself as
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For a finite subgraph
G=(V,E)
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we define its vertex boundary as
\partial V:=\{v\in V:\deg_{G}(v)<4\}
. An edge configuration on
G
is an element
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}
, where an edge
e\in E
is said to be
open
if
\omega_{e}=1
, and
closed
otherwise. With a slight abuse of notation, we can view
\omega
as a subgraph of
G
with vertex set
V
and edge set
o(\omega):=\{e\in E:\omega_{e}=1\}
. Partitions
\xi
of
\partial V
are called
boundary conditions
on
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
p\in(0,1)
and
q>0
, the FK percolation on
G
with edge weight
p
, cluster weight
q
and boundary conditions
\xi
is a probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{E}
given by

\phi_{G,p,q}^{\xi}[\omega]:=\frac{1}{Z_{G,p,q}^{\xi}}p^{|o(\omega)|}(1-p)^{|E\setminus o(\omega)|}q^{k(\omega^{\xi})}\,,

where
\omega^{\xi}
is the graph obtained from
\omega
by identifying vertices belonging to the same partition element of
\xi
as wired together and let
k(\omega^{\xi})
be the number of connected components of the corresponding graph. The normalizing constant
Z_{G,p,q}^{\xi}
is called the partition function.

For boundary conditions
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
, we write
\xi\leq\xi^{\prime}
if each partition element of
\xi
is a subset of a partition element of
\xi^{\prime}
. The
free
boundary conditions (denoted by 0) refer to the finest partition that each vertex in
\partial V
forms a singleton, and the
wired
boundary conditions (denoted by
1
) refer to the coarsest partition where the whole set
\partial V
is a partition element.

For two edge configurations
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{E}
, we write
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}
if
\omega_{e}\leq\omega^{\prime}_{e}
for all
e\in E
. An event
A
is called
increasing
if for any
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}
,
\omega\in A
implies
\omega^{\prime}\in A
. For
q\geq 1
, FK percolation is increasing with respect to the boundary condition: for any two boundary conditions
\xi\leq\xi^{\prime}
and an increasing event
A
,
\phi_{G,p,q}^{\xi^{\prime}}[A]\geq\phi_{G,p,q}^{\xi}[A]
. In particular, this implies that both
\Delta_{p}(R)
and
\Delta_{p}(r,R)
defined in (
1.1
) and (
1.6
) are non-negative.

The FK percolation model enjoys the following domain Markov property. Consider a subgraph
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
G
. For any boundary conditions
\xi
on
G
and any fixed configuration
\tilde{\omega}\in\{0,1\}^{E^{\prime}\setminus E}
,

\phi_{G,p,q}^{\xi}[\cdot_{|E^{\prime}}\mid\omega_{e}=\tilde{\omega}_{e}\mbox{ for all }e\in E\setminus E^{\prime}]=\phi_{G^{\prime},p,q}^{\tilde{\xi}}[\cdot]\,,

(2.1)

where
\tilde{\xi}
is the boundary conditions induced by
\xi
on
G^{\prime}
(i.e.
u,v\in V^{\prime}
are wired together if they are connected in
\tilde{\omega}^{\xi}
).

In this paper, we focus on the critical FK percolation, referring to the case
p=p_{c}(q):=\frac{\sqrt{q}}{1+\sqrt{q}}

[
BDC12
]
. The cluster weight
q\in(1,4]
will also be fixed throughout, therefore we omit the subscripts and write
\phi_{G}^{\xi}
(resp.
\Delta(r,R)
) instead of
\phi_{G,p_{c},q}^{\xi}
(resp.
\Delta_{p_{c},q}(r,R)
) for simplicity.

Crossing probabilities in rectangles are crucial observables in (near-)critical lattice models. Crossing estimates originated from the study of Bernoulli percolation
[
Rus78
,
Rus81
,
SW78
]
and were later called Russo-Seymour-Welsh (RSW) theory. This theory has been widely extended to several correlated percolation models
[
DCHN11
,
DCST17
,
DCT20
,
KST23
]
. More recently, stronger crossing estimates for FK percolation in general quads were derived in
[
DCMT21
]
.
For our purpose, we are content with the following formulation from
[
DCM22
, Theorem 2.1]
. Recall the crossing event
\mathcal{C}(D)
for a rectangle
D
defined right before (
1.6
).

Theorem 2.2

(Crossing estimates)
.

Fix
q\in(1,4]
. For any
\varepsilon>0
and
L>1
, there exists
c=c(q,\varepsilon,L)>0
such that for any graph
G=(V,E)
containing
[-\varepsilon n,(1+\varepsilon)n]\times[-\varepsilon m,(1+\varepsilon)m]
and every boundary conditions
\xi
on
G
, if
L^{-1}\leq\frac{m}{n}\leq L
, then

c\leq\phi_{G}^{\xi}\big[\mathcal{C}([0,n]\times[0,m])\big]\leq 1-c\,.

(2.2)

Next, we discuss the
increasing coupling
of FK percolation models with different boundary conditions. For a finite graph
G=(V,E)
and boundary conditions
\xi\leq\xi^{\prime}
, we may construct a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
via a (random) exploration tree
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{|E|})
as follows. We write
e_{[t]}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{t})
for the edges revealed before time
t
and
G_{t}=G\setminus\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}\}
for the unexplored region.
Let
\omega_{[t]}=(\omega_{e_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{e_{t}})
and
\xi_{t}
be the boundary conditions on
G_{t}
induced by
\omega_{[t]}^{\xi}
. We similarly define
\omega^{\prime}_{[t]}=(\omega_{e_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{e_{t}})
and
\xi^{\prime}_{t}
.
Starting with a fixed edge
e_{1}
, for each
k=1,\ldots,|E|
,

•

Let
\mathsf{p}_{k}=\phi_{G_{k-1}}^{\xi_{k-1}}[\omega_{e_{k}}]
and
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}_{k}=\phi_{G_{k-1}}^{\xi^{\prime}_{k-1}}[\omega^{\prime}_{e_{k}}]
, then independently sample
U_{k}
from a uniform distribution on
[0,1]
.

•

Declare whether
e_{k}
is open or not in
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
by

\omega_{e_{k}}=\mathbf{1}_{U_{k}\leq\mathsf{p}_{k}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\omega_{e_{k}}=\mathbf{1}_{U_{k}\leq\mathsf{p}^{\prime}_{k}}\,.

•

Choose the next queried edge
e_{k+1}
from
E\setminus\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
via a
deterministic
function of
(\omega_{[k]},\omega^{\prime}_{[k]})
.

By the domain Markov property (
2.1
) and monotonicity, the marginal distributions of
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are
\phi_{G}^{\xi}
and
\phi_{G}^{\xi^{\prime}}
, respectively, and
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}
almost surely. Moreover, for any
stopping time
of the exploration tree, i.e. a random variable
\tau
taking values in
\{1,\ldots,|E|,\infty\}
such that
\{\tau\leq k\}
is measurable with respect to
(\omega_{[k]},\omega^{\prime}_{[k]})
, the conditional distributions of
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
on
G_{\tau}
given
(\omega_{[\tau]},\omega^{\prime}_{[\tau]})
are
\phi_{G_{\tau}}^{\xi_{\tau}}
and
\phi_{G_{\tau}}^{\xi^{\prime}_{\tau}}
, respectively. This allows us to change exploration algorithms at stopping times.

The aforementioned coupling method is general in that any exploration rule can be used to construct such an increasing coupling. In
[
DCM22
]
, the authors construct a specific revealment algorithm and show that the mixing rate can be interpreted as the probability of non-coupling. To start with, we recall some terminology in
[
DCM22
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

An
inner flower domain
on
\Lambda_{R}
is a simply connected finite domain
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
containing
\Lambda_{R}
whose boundary consists of an even number of nearest-neighbor arcs
(a_{i}a_{i+1})_{1\leq i\leq 2k}
with each endpoint
a_{i}
lying on
\partial\Lambda_{R}
, where
a_{2k+1}=a_{1}
by convention.
We say
\mathcal{F}
is
\frac{1}{2}
-well-separated if the Euclidean distance between any two distinct endpoints

and
a_{j}
is greater than

.

Definition 2.4
.

For an inner flower domain
\mathcal{F}
as in Definition
2.3
, we call a pair of boundary conditions
(\xi,\xi^{\prime})

boosting
if there exist
\xi\leq\xi_{1}\leq\xi_{2}\leq\xi^{\prime}
such that

•

both in
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
, all vertices on the arc
(a_{j}a_{j+1})
are wired together for each odd

, and all vertices on the arc
(a_{j}a_{j+1})
(except for endpoints) are singletons for each even

;

•

for some odd
j_{1}<j_{2}
, two arcs
(a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{1}+1})
and
(a_{j_{2}}a_{j_{2}+1})
are wired together in
\xi_{2}
, but not in
\xi_{1}
.

In particular,
\xi=0
and
\xi^{\prime}=1
always form a boosting pair.

The following theorem is a combination of Theorems 4.1 and 4.9 of
[
DCM22
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Fix
q\in(1,4]
. Let
1\leq 4r<R
and
\mathcal{F}
be a
\frac{1}{2}
-well-separated inner flower domain on
\Lambda_{R}
. For any boosting pair of boundary conditions
(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
on
\mathcal{F}
, there exists an increasing coupling
\mathbb{P}
of

and
\phi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\xi^{\prime}}
on
\mathcal{F}\setminus\Lambda_{r}
and a stopping time

such that on the event
\{\tau<\infty\}
,

•

the unexplored region
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}=\mathcal{F}\setminus\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\tau}\}
is a

-well-separated inner flower domain on
\Lambda_{r}
;

•

the boundary conditions induced by
\omega_{[\tau]}^{\xi}
and

on

Additionally, if

\zeta^{\prime}
are the boundary conditions induced by

and

on

, respectively, then

\mathbb{P}[\zeta\neq\zeta^{\prime}]\asymp\mathbb{P}[\tau<\infty]\asymp\Delta(r,R)\,,

(2.3)

where the constants in
\asymp
depend only on

.

Finally, we discuss the dual model and loop configuration of FK percolation. For a subgraph
G=(V,E)
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, its dual graph
G^{*}=(V^{*},E^{*})
is a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}+(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
, where each dual vertex in
V^{*}
corresponds to a face of
G
, and each dual edge
e^{*}\in E^{*}
intersects a unique primal edge
e\in E
. Given an edge configuration
\omega
on
G
, we define its
dual
configuration
\omega^{*}
on
G^{*}
by declaring
\omega^{*}_{e^{*}}=1-\omega_{e}
. We say
e^{*}
is
dual open
if
\omega^{*}_{e^{*}}=1
. For
q\geq 1
, the critical point
p_{c}(q)
is self-dual
[
BDC12
]
in the sense that the dual of a critical FK
q
percolation with free boundary conditions is that with wired boundary conditions, and vice versa.

Let
G^{\diamond}
be the
medial
graph with vertex set given by the midpoints of the edges of
G
and
G^{*}
, and edges between nearest neighbors. For an edge configuration
\omega
on
G
, we draw all non-self-crossing paths on
G^{\diamond}
that do not cross any edges of
\omega
and
\omega^{*}
. When the boundary conditions are free or wired, each path is in fact a loop along the inner or outer boundary of a cluster of
\omega
. This collection of loops is called the
loop configuration
of
\omega
. For an illustrative example, we direct the reader to
[
DCM22
, Figure 14]
or
[
KS19
, Figure 2]
.

The loop configuration admits a natural parity structure as follows. We associate each loop with a
nesting level
equal to the number of distinct loops surrounding it plus 1, and separate the loops into even and odd ones depending on the parity of their nesting levels. For instance, the outermost loops have nesting level 1 and are odd. Then, under the free boundary conditions, a primal cluster is squeezed inside an odd loop and outside of all the even loops it surrounds, and a dual cluster is squeezed inside an even loop and outside of all the odd loops it surrounds. By duality, the opposite holds under the wired boundary conditions.

2.2
An equivalent description of the mixing rate: proof of Proposition
1.7

Fix
q\in(1,4]
and
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. For
1\leq r<R
, write
\Lambda_{r,R}=\Lambda_{R}\setminus\Lambda_{r}
. For an edge configuration
\omega
, a primal (resp. dual) open path is a sequence of nearest-neighbor primal (resp. dual) vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
with all edges
u_{i}u_{i+1}
primal (resp. dual) open. Paths can be naturally embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by viewing edges as closed line segments. A circuit is a path with the same starting and ending points. For a primal circuit
\lambda
viewed as a loop on
\mathbb{C}
, let
\lambda^{o}
be the open set of points it surrounds, and let
\overline{\lambda^{o}}
be its closure. Let
D(\lambda)
be the domain enclosed by
\lambda
, that is, the subgraph with vertex set
\overline{\lambda^{o}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and edge set consisting of those edges that are completely contained in
\overline{\lambda^{o}}
, with edges lying on
\lambda
excluded.

Before moving onto the proof of Proposition
1.7
, we remind that since we consider
\delta>0
fixed, there exists
\tilde{c}=\tilde{c}(\delta,q)>0
such that
\phi_{\Lambda_{R}}^{\xi}[A(r;\delta)]\geq\tilde{c}
uniformly for any boundary conditions
\xi
, thanks to the RSW theory. Hence, it will be equivalent to proving (
1.7
) with the denominator removed. We intentionally use this formulation to be consistent with the continuum mixing rate (
1.3
). In view of Theorem
1.3
, we also expect (but do not prove) that (
1.7
) holds uniformly for all small
\delta
; that is, the constants
c
and
C
depend only on
q
.

The upper bound of (
1.7
) is rather obvious: Let
\mathbb{P}
be the increasing coupling of
\omega^{0}
and
\omega^{1}
on
\Lambda_{2r,R}
, whose boundary conditions are free and wired, respectively, on
\partial\Lambda_{R}
as in Theorem
2.5
. Let
\zeta
and
\zeta^{\prime}
be the boundary conditions induced on
\partial\Lambda_{2r}
by
\omega^{0}
and
\omega^{1}
, respectively, then

\phi_{\Lambda_{R}}^{1}[A(r;\delta)]-\phi_{\Lambda_{R}}^{0}[A(r;\delta)]\leq\mathbb{P}[\zeta\neq\zeta^{\prime}]\cdot\max_{\xi}\phi_{\Lambda_{2r}}^{\xi}[A(r;\delta)]\leq C\cdot\Delta(r,R)\phi_{\Lambda_{R}}^{0}[A(r;\delta)],

(2.4)

where in the second inequality, we used (
2.3
) and the mixing property
\max_{\xi}\phi_{\Lambda_{2r}}^{\xi}[A(r;\delta)]\leq C_{1}\cdot\phi_{\Lambda_{R}}^{0}[A(r;\delta)]
(see, e.g.
[
DCST17
, Theorem 5]
).

Now we proceed to the proof of the converse bound, for which we will need the following lemma adapted from
[
DCM22
]
. For
1\leq r<R
, let
\mathcal{E}_{r,R}
be the event that there exists a non-contractible primal open circuit in the annulus
\Lambda_{r,R}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For each
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exists
\mathsf{c}=\mathsf{c}(\delta,q)>0
such that for all
r\geq 100\delta^{-1}
, every
\frac{1}{2}
-well-separated inner flower domain
\mathcal{F}
on
\Lambda_{2r}
, and all pair of boosters
(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
of boundary conditions on
\mathcal{F}
, we have

\phi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\xi^{\prime}}\big[\mathcal{E}_{(1+\delta^{\prime})r,(1+\delta)r}\big]-\phi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\xi}\big[\mathcal{E}_{(1+\delta^{\prime})r,(1+\delta)r}\big]\geq\mathsf{c}

uniformly for all
\delta^{\prime}\in[\frac{\delta}{3},\frac{2\delta}{3}]
.

Proof.

In
[
DCM22
, Theorem 3.6]
, the authors proved the case for the event
\mathcal{E}_{r,2r}
. The exact same argument applies to
\mathcal{E}_{(1+\delta^{\prime})r,(1+\delta)r}
for any

, implying

\phi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\xi^{\prime}}\big[\mathcal{E}_{(1+\delta^{\prime})r,(1+\delta)r}\big]-\phi_{\mathcal{F}}^{\xi}\big[\mathcal{E}_{(1+\delta^{\prime})r,(1+\delta)r}\big]\geq\mathsf{c}_{1}(\delta^{\prime},\delta,q)

for some constant
\mathsf{c}_{1}=\mathsf{c}_{1}(\delta^{\prime},\delta,q)>0
. Moreover, the lower bound
\mathsf{c}_{1}(\delta^{\prime},\delta,q)
as obtained in
[
DCM22

arises from a combination of several crossing estimates. Fix
\delta>0
. For
\delta^{\prime}\in[\frac{\delta}{3},\frac{2\delta}{3}]
, the ratio
\frac{1+\delta}{1+\delta^{\prime}}\geq 1+\frac{\delta}{9}
is uniformly bounded away from 1, hence we can get a uniform positive lower bound
\mathsf{c}(\delta,q)=\inf_{\delta^{\prime}\in[\frac{\delta}{3},\frac{2\delta}{3}]}\mathsf{c}_{1}(\delta^{\prime},\delta,q)>0

∎

. However, the event

.

\frac{1}{2}

We recommend inspecting Figure

Consider the coupling
\mathbf{P}
of

\mathcal{F}

•

\omega

•

For

where, for each circuit

For

(i)

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{k,\mathrm{odd}}[\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}\subset\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle\leq 100^{-(k-1)}\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{odd}}[\eta_{1}\subset\mathcal{A}]

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{and }\quad\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{k,\mathrm{odd}}[\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}\subset\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle\leq 100^{-(k-1)}\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{even}}[\eta_{1}\subset\mathcal{A}]\,.

(3.5)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5

\sup_{D:\mathrm{CR}(0,D)\geq(1+\delta_{0})^{-1}}\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{even}}[\eta\cap\overline{D}=\emptyset]<100^{-1}

, we have
\frac{\mathrm{CR}(0,D_{\partial_{i}})}{\mathrm{CR}(0,D_{\eta_{1}})}\geq(1+\delta_{0})^{-1}
, hence

\displaystyle\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{2,\mathrm{odd}}[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\subset\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{\eta_{1}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{odd}}}\mathbf{1}_{\eta_{1}\subset\mathcal{A}}\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{\eta_{2}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{odd}},\eta_{2}\subset D_{\eta_{1}}}\mathbf{1}_{\eta_{2}\cap D_{\partial_{i}}=\emptyset}\ \Big|\ \eta_{1}\Big]\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[100^{-1}\sum_{\eta_{1}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{odd}}}\mathbf{1}_{\eta_{1}\subset\mathcal{A}}\Big]=100^{-1}\mathsf{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{odd}}[\eta_{1}\subset\mathcal{A}]\,,

and (
3.4

3.5

∎

Proof of Proposition

Fix an

For

For

r=r(\eta)

\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\leq r\leq 2\varepsilon

4
Proof of Theorem
1.5
via Liouville quantum gravity

In this section, we prove Theorem

for
\kappa\in(4,8)
based on the coupling of CLE and LQG. In Section

, we provide the necessary background on Liouville quantum gravity surfaces and present some quantum surfaces that will be used in this paper. In Section

we prove the conformal welding results for a CLE loop from
\mathrm{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{odd}}
or
\mathrm{m}_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{even}}

and

Throughout this section, we fix
\kappa\in(4,8)
and
\gamma=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\in(\sqrt{2},2)
. In this section, we work with non-probability measures and extend the terminology of ordinary probability to this setting. For a finite or
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},M)
, we say
X
is a random variable if
X
is an
\mathcal{F}
-measurable function with its
law
defined via the push-forward measure
M_{X}=X_{*}M
. In this case we say
X
is
sampled
from
M_{X}
and write
M_{X}[f]:=\int f(x)M_{X}(dx)
.
Weighting
the law of
X
by
f(X)
refers to working with the measure
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{M}_{X}
with Radon-Nikodym derivative
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{M}_{X}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}M_{X}}=f
.
Conditioning
on some event
E\in\mathcal{F}
(with
0<M[E]<\infty
) refers to the probability measure
\frac{M[E\cap\cdot]}{M[E]}
on the measurable space
(E,\mathcal{F}_{E})
with
\mathcal{F}_{E}=\{A\cap E:A\in\mathcal{F}\}
.

4.1
Liouville fields and quantum surfaces

First, we review the two-dimensional Gaussian free field (GFF) on simply and doubly connected domains. We direct the reader to
[
She07
,
WP21
]
for detailed background. Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a planar domain that is conformally equivalent to a disk or an annulus, and let
\rho(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)
be a compactly supported probability measure such that
\iint_{D^{2}}-\log|x-y|\rho(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)\rho(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
. Let
H(D;\rho)
be the Hilbert closure of the space
\{f\in C^{\infty}(D):\int_{D}f(x)\rho(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)=0\}
under the Dirichlet inner product
\langle f,g\rangle_{\nabla}=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{D}\nabla f\cdot\nabla g
. Let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an orthogonal basis of
H(D)
, and
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. The random series
\sum_{n\geq 1}\alpha_{n}f_{n}
converges almost surely as a random generalized function, and the limit
h
is called a
free boundary Gaussian free field
on
D
with average zero over
\rho(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)
.

We now introduce the Liouville fields on the annulus using the Gaussian free field.
For convenience, we focus on the horizontal cylinder: For
\tau>0
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}=[0,\tau]\times[0,1]/\!\!\sim
where the equivalence relation
\sim
identifies
[0,\tau]\times\{0\}
with
[0,\tau]\times\{1\}
; then the modulus of
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
is
\tau
. For future reference, let
\partial_{1}\mathcal{C}_{\tau}=\{0\}\times[0,1]/\!\!\sim
and
\partial_{2}\mathcal{C}_{\tau}=\{\tau\}\times[0,1]/\!\!\sim
be its two boundary components. Suppose that
\rho
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
defined as before, and let
P_{\tau,\rho}
be the law of the free boundary Gaussian free field on
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
with average zero over
\rho(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)
.

Definition 4.1
.

For
\tau>0
, let
(h,\mathbf{c})
be sampled from
P_{\tau,\rho}\times\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}c
and
\phi=h+c
. We say
\phi
is a Liouville field on
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
, and write
\mathrm{LF}_{\tau}
for its law.

The measure
\mathrm{LF}_{\tau}
turns out to be independent of the choice of
\rho
, thanks to the translation invariance of the Lebesgue measure
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}c
.

For
\gamma\in(0,2)
, let
Q=\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{2}{\gamma}
. We now introduce
\gamma
-Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) surfaces, or simply
quantum surfaces
. Consider the space of pairs
(D,h)
where
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is a planar domain and
h
is a distribution on
D
, we say
(D,h)\sim_{\gamma}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{h})
if and only if there is a conformal map
g:D\to\widetilde{D}
such that

\widetilde{h}=g\bullet_{\gamma}h:=h\circ g^{-1}+Q\log|(g^{-1})^{\prime}|\,.

(4.1)

A quantum surface
S
is then an equivalence class of pairs
(D,h)
under the equivalence relation
\sim_{\gamma}
, and a particular choice of such
(D,h)
is called an embedding of
S
. This notion naturally extends to quantum surfaces decorated with marked points or curves. For example,
(D,h,x,\eta)/\!\!\sim_{\gamma}
is a quantum surface with one marked point
x\in\overline{D}
and one curve
\eta
on
\overline{D}
, where
\sim_{\gamma}
also requires that the marked point and curve are preserved under the conformal map
g
satisfying (
4.1
).

For a
\gamma
-quantum surface
(D,h)/\!\!\sim_{\gamma}
, its
quantum area measure
is defined by
\mu_{h}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}(z)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z
, where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}z
is the Lebesgue measure and
h_{\varepsilon}(z)
is the average of
h(z)
over the circle
\{w:|w-z|=\varepsilon\}
.
If
\partial D
contains a straight line segment
L
(in particular when
D
is the upper half plane), we may also define the
quantum boundary length measure
by
\nu_{h}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/4}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(x)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
, where for
x\in L
,
h_{\varepsilon}(x)
is the average of
h(x)
over the semi-circle
\{w\in D:|w-x|=\varepsilon\}
. The measures
\mu_{h}
and
\nu_{h}
are well-defined when
h
is a variant of the GFF
[
DS11
,
SW16
]
considered in this paper. For example, for the Liouville field
\phi
on
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
, we can define
\nu_{\phi}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/4}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
on its boundaries
\partial_{1}\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
and
\partial_{2}\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
. The definitions of quantum area and length measures are consistent with
\sim_{\gamma}
, so
\mu_{h}
and
\nu_{h}
can be extended to other domains using conformal maps.

A beaded domain is a closed set such that each component of its interior together with its prime-end boundary is homeomorphic to the closed disk. Consider pairs
(D,h)
where
D
is a beaded domain and
h
is a distribution on
D
.
We extend the equivalence relation
\sim_{\gamma}
so that
g
is allowed to be any homeomorphism from
D
to
\widetilde{D}
that is conformal on each component of the interior of
D
. A
beaded quantum surface

S
is an equivalence class of pairs
(D,h)
under the extended equivalence relation
\sim_{\gamma}
, and a particular choice of such
(D,h)
is called an embedding of
S
. Beaded quantum surfaces decorated with marked points or curves can be defined analogously.

Now we present some typical quantum surfaces that will be used in this paper. We start with the (two-pointed) thick quantum disk introduced in
[
DMS21
, Section 4.5]
. Let
h
be a free boundary GFF on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
, then it admits a unique decomposition
h=h^{1}+h^{2}
, where
h^{1}
(resp.
h^{2}
) is constant (resp. has mean zero) on
\{z\in\mathbb{H}:|z|=r\}
for each

, and they are independent.

Definition 4.2

(Thick quantum disks)
.

For
W\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}
, let
\beta=\gamma+\frac{2-W}{\gamma}\leq Q
. Let
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a standard Brownian motion conditioned on
B_{2t}-(Q-\beta)t<0
for all

, and
(\widetilde{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be its independent copy. Let

Y_{t}=\begin{cases}B_{2t}+\beta t&\mbox{ for }\,t\geq 0,\\
\widetilde{B}_{2t}+(2Q-\beta)t&\mbox{ for }\,t<0\,.\end{cases}

and let
h_{1}(z)=Y_{-\log|z|}
for each

. Let
h_{2}
be a random generalized function with the same law as

defined above.
Independently sample
\mathbf{c}
from the measure
\frac{\gamma}{2}e^{(\beta-Q)c}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}c
, and let
\psi(z)=h_{1}(z)+h_{2}(z)+\mathbf{c}

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
is defined as the law of
(\mathbb{H},\psi,0,\infty)/\!\!\sim_{\gamma}

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)

(two-pointed) quantum disk of weight

The left and right boundary lengths of
(\mathbb{H},\psi,0,\infty)
are referred to as
\nu_{\psi}((-\infty,0))

\nu_{\psi}((0,\infty))

The thick quantum disk of weight 2 has the special property that its two marked points are typical with respect to the quantum boundary length measure
[
DMS21
, Proposition A.8]

By forgetting the marked points (which induces an unweighting by the boundary length), we can define a quantum disk
\mathrm{QD}
with no marked points.
By sampling more interior (resp. boundary) marked points according to the quantum area (resp. length) measure, we may define general quantum disks
\mathrm{QD}_{m,n}
with
m
interior and
n
boundary quantum typical points. For our purpose, we only introduce
\mathrm{QD}_{1,0}
with a single interior point. For a finite measure
M
, let
M^{\#}=|M|^{-1}M
be the probability measure proportional to
M
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathrm{QD}
be the law of
(\mathbb{H},\phi)/\!\!\sim_{\gamma}
where
(\mathbb{H},\phi,0,\infty)/\!\!\sim_{\gamma}
is sampled from the weighted measure
\nu_{\phi}(\partial\mathbb{H})^{-2}{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(2)
.
Let
\mathrm{QD}_{1,0}
be the law of
(\mathbb{H},\phi,z)/\!\!\sim_{\gamma}
where
(\mathbb{H},\phi,0,\infty)/\!\!\sim_{\gamma}
is sampled from the weighted measure
\nu_{\phi}(\partial\mathbb{H})^{-2}\mu_{\phi}(\partial\mathbb{H}){\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(2)
and

is independently sampled from
\mu_{\phi}^{\#}
.

Next, we recall the pinched (thin) quantum annulus
\widetilde{\mathrm{QA}}(W)
introduced in
[
LSYZ24
,
SXZ24
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

For
W\in(0,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})
, define the measure
\widetilde{\mathrm{QA}}(W)
on beaded surfaces as follows. First sample
T\sim(1-\frac{2W}{\gamma^{2}})^{-2}t^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{t>0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t
, and let

be the circle with perimeter

. Then sample a Poisson point process

from the measure
\mathrm{Leb}_{S_{T}}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(\gamma^{2}-W)
, and concatenate the

to get a cyclic chain according to the ordering induced by

The outer (resp. inner) boundary of
\widetilde{\mathrm{QA}}(W)
is referred to as the concatenation of the left (resp. right) boundaries of all
\mathcal{D}_{u}

A sample from
\widetilde{\mathrm{QA}}(W)

Now we review the forest lines and generalized quantum surfaces considered in
[

,
MSW21
,
AHSY23
]

Definition 4.5

.

Definition 4.6

(Forested line segment)
.

For
\gamma\in(\sqrt{2},2)

Definition 4.7
.

The beaded quantum surface

Definition 4.8

Finally, we introduce the concept of

and
\mathcal{M}^{2}
be two measures on quantum surfaces with boundary marked points. For

\mathcal{M}^{1}
and
\mathcal{M}^{2}
along the boundary arcs
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
.
The conformal welding operation naturally extends to quantum surfaces without boundary marked points, as well as to generalized quantum surfaces.

\mathrm{QD}_{1,0}^{f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Weld(\mathrm{QD}_{1,0}(\ell),\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{f.r.}}(\ell,\ell^{\prime}))\ell\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\ell\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\ell^{\prime},

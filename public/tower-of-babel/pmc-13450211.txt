APOE+ macrophages and POSTN+CAFs form immune-exclusion niche driving immunotherapy resistance in clear-cell renal carcinoma
Authors: Qintao Ge, Shengdong Ge, Zhongyuan Wang, Aihetaimujiang Anwaier, Jiahe Lu, Xi Tian, Yue Wang, Jianfeng Yang, Yonghao Chen, Hailiang Zhang, Dingwei Ye, Shan-Chao Zhao, Wenhao Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tumor immune barriers (TIBs) are spatially organized stromal-immune niches that restrict lymphocyte entry. To explain persistent immune-checkpoint blockade (ICB) resistance in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), we mapped TIBs and defined a peritumoral variant built from POSTN+ cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) interlaced with APOE+ tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs). This niche excluded CD8+ T cells from tumor cores, and was enriched in poor-prognosis, ICB-refractory patients. Spatial transcriptomics and single-cell profiling showed TIB regions enriched for extracellular-matrix remodeling, immunosuppressive ligand-receptor circuits, and metabolic reprogramming. Mechanistically, tumor-derived TGF-β1 activated CAFs via SMAD signaling, inducing POSTN and recruiting APOE+TAMs through integrin and chemokine axes. TAMs secreted TGF-β1 and VEGFA, amplifying stromal programs and sustaining exclusion. Blocking POSTN reprogrammed macrophages, reduced matrix-mediated suppression, and restored CD8+ infiltration. In vivo, POSTN inhibition combined with ICB boosted antitumor immunity, reduced tumor burden, and reversed immunosuppressive infiltration, nominating POSTN-directed strategies to potentiate ICB.

INTRODUCTION

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), the predominant pathological type of renal cancer, has a five-year survival for advanced disease that has long hovered around 20–32% (1, 2). Although immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in combination regimens are recommended as first-line therapy in National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines, clinical efficacy is heterogeneous. Pooled phase 3 data (e.g., CheckMate025, KEYNOTE-426) show objective response rates (ORR) of 25–31% with ICIs monotherapy, rising to 55–60% when combined with anti-angiogenic agents; however, median response duration is only 12.8–16.6 months. Notably, about 42% of patients exhibit primary resistance to ICIs (including 27% of PD-L1-positive cases), and acquired resistance drives progression in about 60% of initial responders within 18 months, limiting durable benefit (3, 4).

The tumor microenvironment (TME), a dynamic network of tumor, immune, and stromal cells, shows marked spatial immunosuppressive heterogeneity in ccRCC. Single-cell (scRNA) transcriptomics delineates at least three TME subtypes: an immune-desert state with scarce CD8+ T cells; an inflamed but dysfunctional state with abundant CD8+ T cells co-expressing inhibitory receptors such as PD-1, TIM-3, and LAG-3; and an immune-exclusion state in which cytotoxic lymphocytes are retained at the invasive margin rather than penetrating the tumor (5–7). Spatial multi-omics studies indicate that immune deserts can evade surveillance through HLA-I loss and high IDO1 and ARG1 expression (8, 9), whereas immune-exclusion phenotypes depend on mechanical and biochemical barriers that physically block T cell entry. Importantly, TME states can vary by metastatic site within the same patient, for example, immune desert predominates in liver metastases (about 67.3%), while immune exclusion is more common in lung lesions, contributing to site-specific responses in about 43.8% of cases (10–13). These observations underscore the need to resolve TME heterogeneity in renal cancer and to therapeutically reprogram immunosuppressive niches to improve immunotherapy efficacy.

Among stromal and myeloid constituents, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are abundant and tightly coordinated architects of immunosuppression. Across solid tumors, TAMs infiltration correlates with CAFs activation, and both associate with therapeutic resistance and poor prognosis. CAFs recruit monocytes via chemokines (e.g., CCL2/MCP-1, CXCL12), driving their polarization toward M2-like, immunosuppressive macrophages. In breast cancer, CAFs-recruited monocytes differentiate into PD-1high M2-like TAMs that secrete high levels of IL-10, dampening T cell function. These TAMs also promote invasion and EMT by releasing pro-tumorigenic factors. Pathology studies link higher CAFs density with increased TAMs, elevated proliferation indices, and greater tumor burden, highlighting a CAFs-directed reprogramming of the monocyte-macrophage compartment that enables immune evasion (14–16).

This “fibroblast-macrophage axis” is a central barrier to immunotherapy in several refractory cancers. In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), CAFs and TAMs dominate the stroma, creating a prototypical “cold” microenvironment refractory to diverse immunotherapies. Single-cell and spatial analyses reveal substantial heterogeneity and symbiosis between CAFs and TAMs in PDAC, where they restrain antitumor immunity (17). In hepatocellular carcinoma, a CD36+ CAFs subset recruit myeloid-derived suppressor cells and immunosuppressive macrophages through macrophage migration inhibitory factor, establishing a highly immunosuppressive niche that undermines anti-PD-1 therapy; inhibiting this pathway improves T cell reinvigoration (18). Collectively, studies across breast, pancreatic, lung, and liver cancers implicate CAFs-TAMs interactions as a key network shaping an immunosuppressive TME. Through bidirectional signaling and ECM remodeling, this axis erects an “immune-evasion” barrier that hinders lymphocyte infiltration and function, enabling tumor persistence despite checkpoint blockade.

Motivated by these insights, we investigated how the spatial organization of stromal and myeloid cells contributes to immune exclusion and therapy resistance in ccRCC. We hypothesized the presence of a tumor immune barrier (TIB) at the invasive front: a structured niche in which CAFs and TAMs assemble into a physical and immunoregulatory partition that restricts cytotoxic T cell access to the tumor core. Using multiplex immunofluorescence, scRNA-seq, and spatial transcriptomics, we sought to: (i) validate the existence of a CAF-TAM niche in ccRCC; (ii) define its cellular composition, ligand-receptor circuits, and ECM architecture; and (iii) establish its association with prognosis and response to ICIs-based therapy. We further posited that tumor-derived cues, such as TGF-β-SMAD signaling, activate POSTN-expressing CAFs that, via integrins and chemokine axes, recruit and maintain APOE+TAMs; in turn, these TAMs secrete factors (e.g., TGF-β, VEGFA) that reinforce stromal programs and perpetuate immune exclusion. By resolving this coordinated CAF-TAM niche and its spatial transcriptomic signatures, our study aims to identify actionable targets capable of dismantling the barrier, restoring CD8+ T cell infiltration, and enhancing the durability of checkpoint blockade in ccRCC.

RESULTS

TIB-like niches define an immunosuppressive niche associated with poor prognosis and resistance to ICIs in ccRCC

To assess the clinical relevance of the tumor TIB-like niches in ccRCC, we integrated multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF), scRNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics (ST), and in situ validations (Fig. 1A). The results of mIF revealed marked differences between TIB-positive (TIB+) and TIB-negative (TIB−) tumors. In representative TIB+ samples (Fig. 1B), CD68+CD163+ M2-like macrophages formed a peritumoral ring around CA9+ tumor cells, while abundant CD8+ T cells were largely excluded from the tumor core and confined to stromal regions. PD-L1 expression was enriched within the same rim, indicating a localized immunosuppressive niche with macrophage-fibroblast-T cell segregation. In contrast, TIB− tissues lacked such organized architecture, with immune cells more diffusely distributed across the tumor-stroma interface (Fig. 1C). ST profiling of six TIB+ cases confirmed co-localization of CD163+ macrophages and α-SMA+ cancer-associated fibroblasts at the tumor boundary (Fig. 1D), supporting a fibroblast-macrophage barrier that physically and molecularly limits T cell entry. Among 50 tumors, 14 (28%) were classified as TIB+ by concordant spatial and molecular criteria (Fig. 1E, fig. S1A, and table S1).

Clinically, TIB+ status correlated with shorter progression-free survival than TIB− (P = 0.026; HR = 2.01; 95% CI: 1.089–3.698; Fig. 1F). Metastasis occurred in 60% of TIB+ groups (Fig. 1G), and TIB+ patients were enriched in recurrent or treatment-refractory disease (Fig. 1H), suggesting a link to acquired resistance to immune checkpoint blockade. Collectively, these results establish TIB as a clinically meaningful spatial phenotype in ccRCC, characterized by immune exclusion, stromal shielding, and macrophage-mediated suppression, and underscore the need to evaluate spatial immune architecture, not just cell abundance, when predicting intermediate benefit (IB) response.

Spatial coordination between POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs drive the formation of the TIB in ccRCC

To define the cellular and molecular characteristics of the TIB in ccRCC, we performed ST on 26 tumor sections. Unsupervised clustering identified 11 transcriptional clusters; two sections showed sharp boundary-tumor interfaces consistent with TIB morphology (Fig. 2A, fig. S1B). Celltype annotation using canonical markers confirmed epithelial (EPCAM, KRT18), endothelial (VWF, PECAM1), fibroblast (ACTA2, POSTN), and myeloid-derived (CD68, CD163, APOE) compartments (Fig. 2B). In CAF-TAM co-localized regions, POSTN, COL4A1, COL5A1, and APOE were markedly elevated, delineating a distinct spatial boundary, while T and B lymphocytes were largely restricted to the periphery, indicative of immune exclusion (Fig. 2, C and D).

Prior work implicated FAP+CAF-SPP1+TAM or POSTN+CAF-SPP1+TAM interactions in barrier-like structures (19, 20). Extending these observations, we compared spatial expression of CD163, MRC1, PDGFRA, SPP1, FAP, POSTN, and APOE across boundary zones. POSTN (CAF) and APOE (TAM) showed the strongest spatial co-localization with TIB niches, surpassing other markers. ST analysis further revealed that the TIB niche was characterized not only by POSTN and APOE but also by the marked enrichment of structural markers, including COL1A1 and ACTA2, suggesting a dense extracellular matrix and a highly activated myofibroblast state within this spatial niche (Fig. 2E; fig. S1, C to F), indicating that POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs constitute the core TIB elements in ccRCC.

Functional characterization via gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) further demonstrated that TIB regions exhibit coordinated downregulation of immune effector programs, including cytotoxicity, B cell activation, and natural killer (NK) cell-mediated immunity (Fig. 2, F and G). Thus, the TIB functions as both a spatially organized immunosuppressive niche limiting lymphocyte entry and a molecularly immunosuppressive niche that dampens local antitumor responses. In sum, POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs spatially cooperate to construct and maintain an immune-exclusion barrier, underscoring the central role of TIB in ccRCC immune evasion.

Spatially resolved functional compartmentalization of the TIB mediated by POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs

To contextualize the structural and transcriptional environment of the TIB in ccRCC, we performed high-resolution ST on representative tumors (GSM6415705, GSM5924033). Copy-number inference aligned with spatial domains helped demarcate malignant nests from the surrounding microenvironment. While a shared genomic backbone characterized by chromosome 3p loss was evident across samples, we also observed regional gains on chromosomes 7 and 20q in specific clusters (Fig. 3, A and B). These alterations appeared to coincide with the localized expression of certain markers such as MET, EGFR, and AURKA (fig. S2, A and B). These spatial-genomic observations suggest a potential link between sub-clonal chromosomal instability and the malignant state of cells sequestered within the TIB niches, although the specific functional implications of these associations warrant further investigation.

We next applied BayesSpace for domain classification and deconvolution, revealing sharp compartment boundaries at the tumor-TME interface. POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs were consistently enriched in peritumoral rims and co-localized with ECM modules (collagens, LOX family enzymes, MMPs) and immunosuppressive circuits encompassing growth-factor signaling, complement activation, checkpoint resistance programs, and chemokine axes (Fig. 3C, and fig. S2, C and D). In both GSM5924033 and GSM6415705, distance-to-boundary analyses showed that the curve for double-positive cells (POSTN+/APOE+) peaked at the boundary and declined monotonically toward baseline (∼1), indicating substantial co-occurrence within close proximity. Initial enrichment reached ∼1.6–1.9× background, supporting a spatially coordinated CAF-TAM axis with immunosuppressive features (Fig. 3D). Concordantly, APOE+TAMs and POSTN+CAFs expression intensities maximized at or near the boundary (Fig. 3, E and F), reinforcing their role in constructing the TIB.

Hotspot analysis confirmed this topographic pattern, identifying regions of high spatial autocorrelation linking CAF-TAM markers with immunosuppressive signatures (Fig. 3G). Guided by these maps, we propose a three-zone spatial organization of the TIB-like niche (Fig. 3H): (1) Dense Stromal Edge (DSE, 0–0.4 mm): defined by a rigid interface of POSTN+CAFs and dense ECM deposition, serving as the primary structural containment barrier; (2) Functional Inhibitory Stratum (FIS, 0.4–0.8 mm): an immunosuppressive niche enriched for APOE+TAMs and inhibitory ligands, functioning to arrest T cell infiltration; (3) Buffer Zone (>0.8 mm): a peripheral region populated by exhausted T cells, regulatory T cells (Treg cells), and antigen-presenting cells, forming a gradient of immune dysfunction and exclusion.

Together, these findings reveal that POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs not only define the spatial core of the TIB, but also orchestrate its functional zonation, establishing a coordinated structural and immunosuppressive niche that facilitates immune evasion at the invasive tumor front.

APOE+TAM-POSTN+CAF spatial axis orchestrates the immunosuppressive niche and predicts ICB response in ccRCC

To comprehensively map the cellular landscape and spatial organization of the ccRCC TME, we integrated scRNA-seq with spatial ST. Unsupervised clustering of 517,547 high-quality single cells (fig. S3A) yielded 64 transcriptional clusters (fig. S3B), annotated into 21 major cell types using canonical markers (fig. S3, C and E). These encompassed diverse myeloid subsets [macrophages (Mphs), monocytes, monocyte-derived dendritic cells (moDCs), multiple stromal CAF states (POSTN+, HGF+, CXCL14+)], T cell lineages, and epithelial cells (Fig. 4A and fig. S3, G and H). While composition varied by specimen, POSTN+CAFs and Mphs were consistently enriched in tumors. Ro/e analyses showed POSTN+CAFs and Mphs elevated in tumor tissue and in ICB non-responders, whereas FOXP3+Tregs and naïve T cells displayed variable patterns (Fig. 4, B and C). Notably, POSTN+CAFs and Mphs were significantly higher in non-responders (P < 0.05), implicating the role in immune resistance. To resolve Mphs heterogeneity, we re-clustered the myeloid compartment and identified 11 TAM states, including AMICA1+, APOE+, and CD163+ subsets (Fig. 4, D and E, and fig. S3F). APOE+TAMs were preferentially enriched in tumor samples and in ICB non-responders, linking this phenotype to immune evasion. Functional analyses indicated that POSTN+CAFs were dominated by ECM programs-collagen fibril organization and ECM-receptor interactions—whereas APOE+TAMs were enriched for lysosomal function, cholesterol handling, and detoxification pathways (fig. S4, A to E). Intriguingly, in responders, APOE+TAMs upregulated chemotaxis pathways (neutrophil/monocyte recruitment), while in non-responders they shifted toward lipid biosynthesis and homeostasis, suggesting a metabolically reprogrammed, immunosuppressive state (Fig. 4F).

In cell2location analysis, POSTN+CAFs with APOE+TAMs formed a continuous, co-localized band at the tumor-stroma interface-consistent with a TIB (Fig. 4G and fig. S4F). To interrogate intercellular influence in situ, we applied MISTy analysis. Modeling at intra-, juxta- (r = 5), and para-cellular (r = 15) radii demonstrated that CAFs, endothelial cells, and macrophages exert the strongest juxta-cellular effects on T/NK and epithelial transcriptional states. Network analyses revealed these three cell types form a localized influence module coordinating immunomodulation at the invasive margin (fig. S5, A and B). Collectively, these data define a spatially coordinated, functionally immunosuppressive POSTN+CAFs-APOE+TAMs axis that is enriched in ICB non-responders and contributes to CD8+T cell exclusion from the tumor core. Targeting stromal-myeloid cross-talk may therefore help overcome immune resistance in ccRCC.

The APOE+ TAM-POSTN+CAF axis defines a spatial immune barrier associated with poor prognosis and ICB resistance in ccRCC

To validate the clinical relevance of CAFs and TAMs among ccRCC patients, we estimated the cellular abundance within bulk transcriptomic data of the CheckMate cohort. We selected BayesPrism for this analysis due to its specific advantage in utilizing scRNA reference profiles to deconvolve complex tumor mixtures with accuracy. Consequently, POSTN+CAF (HR = 2.21, 95%CI 1.84–2.94) and MMP1+CAF showed the most significant increase in risk (Fig. 5A). Stratification by the abundance of APOE+TAMs and POSTN+CAFs revealed that patients in the high-expression groups for either cell type were significantly overrepresented among non-responders to ICB in the CheckMate cohort (P = 0.0095 and P = 0.0479, respectively) (Fig. 5B). Then, the spatial immunosuppressive signature was further validated by multiplex immunofluorescence and immunohistochemistry in representative patient samples (Fig. 5C and fig. S5C). In tumors classified as non-responders and defined as TIB+, POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs formed a continuous, high-density band encircling the CA9+ tumor epithelium, effectively excluding CD8+ T cells, which remained largely restricted to the peritumoral stroma. In contrast, this immunosuppressive niche was disrupted or absent in ICB-responsive or TIB-negative tumors, where CD8+ T cells were able to penetrate into the tumor parenchyma.

Quantitative analysis of cellular composition confirmed a TME skewed toward the APOE+TAMs/POSTN+CAFs axis in TIB+ tumors (Fig. 5D). Correspondingly, the intratumoral density of CD8+T cells were significantly lower in TIB cases, while CD8+T infiltration in the peritumoral region showed no significant difference (Fig. 5E). These findings indicate that TIB primarily regulates spatial exclusion, rather than total immune cell abundance. To further validate this spatial exclusion model, we performed distance mapping analysis, quantifying the shortest distances between key cell types and tumor epithelium (CA9+ cells). In TIB-positive samples, POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs were significantly closer to tumor cells, while CD8+T cells were located at greater distances from the tumor core (all P < 0.001; Fig. 5F). These results spatially and statistically confirm the barrier-forming role of these stromal and myeloid components.

In summary, the co-localized APOE+TAM-POSTN+CAF axis constitutes a spatial immune barrier that is associated with immune exclusion, poor prognosis, and ICB resistance in ccRCC. Given its consistent presence across bulk, single-cell, and spatial omics platforms, this axis represents a translationally actionable spatial biomarker and a promising therapeutic target for combination strategies designed to overcome stromal-mediated immune resistance.

Tumor-derived TGFβ signaling orchestrates the induction of POSTN+CAFs and matrix remodeling via a TGFβ-TGFβR-SMAD axis

To dissect the specific molecular mechanisms by which tumor cells reprogram the stromal compartment, we integrated multiple ligand-receptor strategies. NicheNet could prioritize ligand-receptor pairs based on their potential to induce observed gene expression changes in the target cells. To further spatially validate these interactions within the tissue architecture, we integrated COMMOT and CellChat to further deconvolute the complex global signal flows. In NicheNet analysis, malignant epithelial cells were set as ligand senders and POSTN+CAFs as receivers. TGFβ1 emerged as the top tumor-derived ligand with the highest regulatory potential, followed by NRXN2, CFD, NAMPT, and CDCP1. Additional prioritized ligands included collagen family members (COL4A4, COL6A2), laminins (LAMA1, LAMA5), adhesion molecules (ICAM1, VCAM1), and angiogenic factors (VEGFA, PGF). Predicted CAFs target genes with high regulatory potential included ECM components (FN1, COL4A2, MMP2), signaling mediators (JAG1, SEMA3C, PDGFRB, IGFBP7), and immediate-early transcription factors (FOS, JUN, JUNB)—collectively consistent with TGFβ-driven activation of the myofibroblast differentiation program and ECM remodeling in POSTN+CAFs (Fig. 6A).

To embed signaling in space, COMMOT inferred ligand-receptor exchange across spatial domains. TGFβ- TGFβR1, TGFβ- TGFβR2 displayed the strongest regulatory potential and localized precisely to the tumor-CAFs boundary, forming a contiguous, short-range paracrine band in which ligand expression, receptor activity, and regulatory scores were tightly concordant (Fig. 6B). Among the nine pathways identified by CellChat analysis, tumor cells contributed primarily through CD46 signaling to POSTN+CAFs (Fig. 6C). Converging evidence identified the TGFβ-TGFβR1/2-SMAD axis as the primary driver of POSTN+CAFs emergence, collectively promoting CAF differentiation, ECM deposition, and the establishment of a TIB-like niche.

We then tested these predictions experimentally using tumor-fibroblast co-cultures with human ccRCC lines (786-O, 769-P) and primary human fibroblasts. Perturbations included TGFβ1 overexpression in tumor cells, recombinant TGFβ1 supplementation, and neutralization with antagonistic antibodies (TGFβ1ab). Flow cytometry showed that TGFβ1 overexpression and exogenous TGFβ1 significantly increased POSTN+CAFs frequency, whereas TGFβ1ab reversed this induction (P < 0.001; Fig. 6, D and E). At the protein level, TGFβ1 overexpression (OE) in tumor cells upregulated ECM-related proteins (COL4A1, VEGFA) and triggered robust SMAD2/3 phosphorylation, confirming canonical TGFβ pathway activation; notably, these effects were reversed by the addition of TGFβ1 neutralizing antibody (TGFβ1ab), validating the specificity of TGFβ1-dependent signaling. ACTA2 expression showed limited variation across conditions in these cell models (Fig. 6F). Secretome profiling demonstrated elevations of CCL2, CSF1, IL6, and TGFβ1 in overexpression conditions (Fig. 6G and fig. S6, A and B). ELISAs of conditioned media further revealed increased MMP2, MMP7, MMP9, MMP14, and VEGFA with TGFβ1, while neutralization significantly reduced their levels (Fig. 6H and fig. S6, C and D). Collectively, integrative modeling and perturbation experiments establish tumor-derived TGFβ signaling as both necessary and sufficient to induce the POSTN+ matrix program in CAFs via the TGFβ-TGFβR-SMAD axis. This pathway organizes the stromal architecture characteristic of the TIB and promotes immune exclusion, highlighting TGFβ as a tractable therapeutic node to disrupt the stromal barrier and restore antitumor immunity in ccRCC.

A self-reinforcing POSTN+CAFs-APOE+TAMs interaction loop drives immunosuppressive matrix remodeling in ccRCC

To delineate the signaling networks that sustain the immunosuppressive TME in ccRCC, we employed three complementary computational approaches to dissect the bidirectional cross-talk between POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs. First, CellChat role analysis was applied to assign Sender, Receiver, Mediator, and Influencer functions to each cell type across enriched signaling pathways (Fig. 7A). This analysis revealed that POSTN+CAFs served as the dominant sender in the THY1 signaling. Second, COMMOT was used to spatially map ligand-receptor communication flows directly on the tissue sections. Key interaction pairs included CCL2-CCR2, ANGPT2-TEK, CSF1-CSF1R, and POSTN-ITGAV/ITGB3, exhibited striking co-localization and directed coupling precisely at the tumor-stroma interface, forming a continuous communication band along the TIB boundary (Fig. 7B). Directionality was evaluated with NicheNet. In a CAFs to TAMs model, POSTN+CAFs were predicted to secrete collagens, MMP14, BGN, and APOE. Conversely, in a TAMs to CAFs model, APOE+TAMs were predicted to deliver TGFβ1 and VEGFA ligands was significantly upregulated (fig. S7, A and B). Tumor to APOE+TAMs predictions were enriched for TFGA, NRXN2, ANG, CLU, and APOE signaling (fig. S7C), consistent with tumor-priming of Mphs that amplifies the CAFs-TAMs circuit.

Even without exogenous IL-4/IL-13, CAFs-CM was sufficient to induce APOE, IL-10, CD163, and ARG1; addition of TGFβ1 further enhanced these markers in both 786-O and 769-P systems (Fig. 7C), indicating that TGFβ1-conditioned CAFs drive THP-1 polarization toward an immunosuppressive TAMs-like phenotype. Together, these multi-layered data support a functional CAF-TAM axis wherein TGF-β1-activated POSTN+CAFs polarize macrophages toward an immunosuppressive APOE+ state and promote ECM remodeling, even without IL-4/IL-13. Reciprocal TAMs-to-CAFs signals reinforce myofibroblast activation and matrix deposition, establishing a self-sustaining, boundary-localized niche of immune exclusion. Integrating spatial, computational, and functional evidence, we propose that tumor cells serve a dual initiating role, activating POSTN+CAFs through TGFβ1 signaling while simultaneously pre-polarizing APOE+ TAMs via TGFA, NRXN2, and ANG. Activated POSTN+CAFs further recruit and stabilize APOE+TAMs through POSTN-ITGAV/ITGB3, CCL2-CCR2, and CSF1-CSF1R interactions, while APOE+TAMs reciprocally deliver TGFβ1 and VEGFA back to CAFs, completing a self-reinforcing tripartite circuit among tumor cells, CAFs, and TAMs that sustains immune exclusion. Disrupting the TGFβ-SMAD or POSTN-integrin nodes may dismantle this loop and restore antitumor immunity in ccRCC.

Treatments began on day 8 postimplantation. Bioluminescence imaging showed accelerated growth in G2 versus control, confirming the pro-tumorigenic impact of activated CAFs. In contrast, signal intensity was reduced in G4 and most prominently in G5, indicating superior tumor control (Fig. 8B).

Collectively, inhibition of the POSTN-integrin axis dismantles the stromal basis of immune resistance by weakening physical and biochemical barriers at the tumor-stroma interface. This reversal of the CAF-mediated niche enhances T cell priming and effector activity. When coupled with checkpoint inhibition, the dual approach overcomes tolerance and unleashes cytotoxic immunity, yielding superior antitumor efficacy compared with either agent alone. These findings support POSTN-targeted strategies, particularly in combination with ICB-as a rational therapeutic avenue for ccRCC.

DISCUSSION

In the tumor microenvironment, CAFs and TAMs collaboratively shape an immunosuppressive ecology: their abundance is often positively correlated and associated with treatment resistance and poor prognosis. The critical role of this CAF-TAM axis has been confirmed in multiple cancer types. For instance, in breast cancer, CAFs recruit and polarize monocytes into PD-1high M2-like TAMs, secreting inhibitory factors such as IL-10 that weaken T cell function; in pancreatic cancer, CAFs and TAMs dominate the stroma, contributing to the formation of cold niche (14). Single-cell and spatial omics further reveal the heterogeneity and symbiotic remodeling of these two cell types. In non-small cell lung cancer, PDPN +CAFs are co-enriched with M2-TAMs (15), promoting immunosuppression through pathways such as FGF/FGFR and TGFβ. Overall, CAFs and TAMs construct an immune exclusion barrier through bidirectional signaling, limiting the infiltration and activity of effector lymphocytes (16, 17, 20). However, the specific mechanisms of this axis in renal cancer remain unclear and require systematic elucidation.

In this study, we found that POSTN+CAFs and APOE+TAMs form a continuous high-density band at the tumor-stroma interface, with CD8+T cells primarily localized at the tumor periphery. Patients exhibiting this structure have a shorter PFS. We propose a three-layer structural model: the inner layer consisting of the POSTN+CAF-ECM interface, the middle layer characterized by an immune-suppressive stromal core enriched with APOE+TAMs, and the outer layer serving as a buffering zone. Additionally, POSTN+CAF and APOE+TAMs cells are significantly increased in tumors of non-responders to immunotherapy. Based on results above, we proposed the presence of TIB in ccRCC, forming a suppressive niche that impedes ICB therapy. Previous reports have shown that in PDAC (21), integrin αvβ5 (ITGAV/ITGB5) co-localizes and pairs with other components; it activates the PI3K/AKT/β-catenin cascade, enhancing tumor cell EMT, proliferation, clonogenicity, migration, and invasion. This integrin constructed a high-intensity ECM signaling microdomain that promotes tumor progression. In contrast, we found that in renal cancer, this axis primarily acts on macrophages, facilitating the recruitment, retention, and anti-inflammatory polarization of APOE+TAMs cells, thereby forming an immune exclusion zone at the tumor-stroma interface and diminishing the benefits of ICB. Similarly, in non-small cell lung cancer, the immunosuppressive role driven by CAFs has also been reported: specific subtypes of CAFs (such as PDPN+ and POSTN+CAFs) are significantly associated with the enrichment of M2 macrophages, promoting the transition of macrophages to an immunosuppressive phenotype through pathways such as FGF-FGFR signaling and TGFβ, thereby weakening anti-tumor immune effects (20). Studies have indicated that POSTN+CAFs can combine with SPP1+TAMs to synergistically induce an immunosuppressive state in the TME of lung cancer. In HCC, CAFs and TAMs have been found to construct a solid barrier-like immunosuppressive niche, indicating that the cross-talk between SPP1+TAMs and CAFs forms a co-function immunosuppressive niche, blocking the infiltration of peripheral lymphocytes into the tumor, significantly weakening the efficacy of immunotherapy (22). Collectively, both highlight the clinical value of targeting POSTN.

Apolipoprotein E (APOE) is a secretory protein involved in lipid transport, interacting with classical receptors such as LDLR, LRP1, VLDLR, and ApoER2. In the tumor microenvironment, it is often highly expressed by TAMs, driving lipid metabolic reprogramming and an immune suppressive phenotype, which is associated with T cell exclusion and low sensitivity to immunotherapy (23). Pancancer analysis found that elevated infiltration of APOE+TAMs was associated with ICIs resistance (24). In our previous work, we analyzed the TME and TAMs heterogeneity by integrating scRNA-seq, ST-seq, mIF, and functional experiments. APOE+TAMs are significantly enriched in ICB resistant tumors, and their abundance is associated with poorer prognosis and lower benefits from ICB therapy. Tumor-derived SPP1 not only promotes tumor proliferation and migration but also recruits and polarizes APOE+M2 macrophages, which secrete TGFβ, leading to immune evasion and limiting the anti-tumor cytotoxic effects of T cells (25). Targeting POSTN and APOE dismantles stromal anchoring and reprograms APOE+TAMs-mediated immunosuppression, relieving T cell exclusion and improving ICB efficacy.

We further concentrated on the mechanism of TIB-formation and tumor-CAF-TAM positive feedback loop centered around the boundary of ccRCC. According to previous literature, TGFβ has repeatedly been confirmed as a core axis driving the activation of CAFs, matrix deposition, and immune suppression, which is closely related to poor responses to immunotherapy, particularly by creating a “T cell exclusion” type of stromal barrier that undermines the efficacy of ICB (26, 27). Within this framework, our results link tumor cells, POSTN+CAFs, and APOE+TAMs into a self-reinforcing communication loop: tumor cells first induce the myofibroblast and ECM remodeling programs of CAFs via the TGFβ1-SMAD and CSF1-CSF1R axes, while also pre-polarizing macrophages towards an immune regulatory state (28–30). Subsequently, POSTN+CAFs recruit and stabilize TAMs through pathways such as POSTN-integrin (αVβ3/αVβ5), CCL2-CCR2, and CSF1-CSF1R, while ECM components (collagen, FN1 and laminin) and the integrin network further reinforce a matrix-rich, low-permeability microenvironment, which aligns with CAF-mediated macrophage localization and functional shaping. In the context of reverse signaling, APOE+TAMs secrete factors such as TGFβ1, which reactivate CAFs, driving the expression of effector genes like COL1A1/1A2, POSTN, ACTA2, and MMPs, thereby forming a sustained enhancement of ECM and promoting angiogenesis (28, 31). By integrating spatial interactions, ligand-receptor inference, and functional readouts, our proposed tumor-POSTN+CAFs-APOE+TAMs triadic loop not only elucidates the “triad” of matrix densification, increased tissue tension, and vascular abnormalities but also provides a unified mechanistic explanation for the restricted T cell infiltration and suppressed effector function (32, 33). The inhibition of TGFβ-SMAD or the blockade of POSTN-αVβ3 can theoretically “parallel dismantle” this loop at different nodes, complementing PD-1/PD-L1 inhibition (33, 34). Compared to controls or monotherapy, the combination of anti-POSTN and ICB significantly reduced bioluminescence signals and terminal tumor burden, while increasing CD8+GZMB+ effector T cells and decreasing FOXP3+ Treg cells. This result is highly consistent with the mechanism of ‘TGFβ-driven stromal barriers limiting ICB’, suggesting that POSTN blockade could serve as an effective adjunct therapy to ICB.

This study still has the following limitations: The mIF cohort consists of only 50 cases, with a TIB+ proportion of 28% (14/50), and is a single-center, retrospective analysis; baseline differences in different stages/treatment lines and sampling bias (enrichment sampling from the boundary area) may affect the generalizability of the conclusions. The complete causal chain linking this niche to immune exclusion and resistance to immune-checkpoint blockade will require further validation in more representative in vivo models. In addition, the therapeutic and predictive implications of this niche remain to be tested in larger independent cohorts and prospective studies.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics approval

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (FUSCC, Shanghai, China) (Approval No. 050432–4-2108*). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of FUSCC (FUSCC-IACUC-S2024–0626) and performed in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines.

Data acquisition and preprocessing from multiple cohorts

We assembled multi-omic datasets to interrogate immunosuppression and immune exclusion in ccRCC. For bulk transcriptomics with clinical outcomes, we analyzed two ICI-treated cohorts: CheckMate (n = 311) and FUSCC-ICI (n = 230). Spatial transcriptomics came from GEO (GSE175540, n = 24; GSE210041, n = 2). Single-cell RNA-seq integrated seven studies: six from GEO (GSE178481, GSE202374, GSE222703, GSE224630, GSE207493, GSE156632) and one from SRA (PRJNA705464). These resources span varied tissues, treatment contexts, and patient subtypes, enabling robust inter-sample integration, spatial-single-cell cross-validation, and translational association analyses linking microenvironmental states with clinical response to immune checkpoint blockade in ccRCC (35–43).

Single-cell RNA-seq data processing and analysis

All scRNA-seq datasets underwent stringent quality control filtering to remove low-quality cells and genes (44). Highly variable genes (HVGs) were identified using variance stabilization transformation (VST), and the top 3000 HVGs were selected per sample. To ensure consistency across datasets, the union of frequently occurring HVGs was used to guide dimensionality reduction and downstream integration. All quality-controlled datasets were merged. The merged data underwent normalization and scaling, followed by PCA based on the top 3000 HVGs (30 components). Various methods were referenced for data integration, including BBKNN (45), CCA, RPCA, LIGER, Harmony (46), and fastMNN (47).

Ultimately, the Harmony algorithm was employed to correct batch effects using the first 15 Harmony dimensions to construct a nearest neighbor graph, followed by resolution-guided clustering (resolution = 0.1–1.0 with an interval of 0.2). Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) was performed on the Harmony embeddings (dims = 1:15) to visualize the global structure and sample source distribution. Additionally, UMAP was run in PCA space (dims = 1:20) to cross-validate clustering stability and marker gene distribution. A total of 64 clusters were identified with a resolution of 1.2. Combining signature genes and the GPTCelltype package (48), we annotated the 64 clusters into specific cell types. Ro/e (49) and cell proportion analysis was performed to revealed cell abundance. BayesPrism algorithm (50) was performed to separate bulk RNA sequencing data into its individual cell types, enhancing the interpretability of transcriptomic signals in bulk-level cohorts. Visualization was performed using the OmicVerse python module (51).

Copy number variation analysis of epithelial cells

To infer somatic large-scale chromosomal copy number variations (CNVs), epithelial cell subsets were isolated from the integrated scRNA-seq matrix. CNV inference was conducted using the inferCNV R package (v1.6.0) (52), with non-malignant epithelial cells serving as reference controls. The analysis was executed with denoising enabled, using default Hidden Markov Model (HMM) parameters and a CNV detection threshold of 0.1. To minimize false positive CNV calls, the Bayesian latent mixture model embedded within inferCNV was applied to compute the posterior probabilities of CNV events for each cell. A standard posterior probability threshold of 0.5 was adopted to classify cells as harboring significant CNV alterations (52). These CNV profiling enabled downstream delineation of malignant versus non-malignant epithelial compartments based on large-scale genomic instability.

Cell-to-cell communication analysis

To explore the communication between cells and the interaction from ligand to receptor (L-R), we conducted an analysis using CellChat (v.2.0) (53). We analyzed the distinct communication characteristics between the ICB-resistant group and the ICB-exposed group using the CellChatDB.human database. The top 30 ligand-receptor pairs obtained through NicheNet analysis (54), sorted by aupr_corrected, were used for cellular communication quantification.

Preprocessing and dimensionality reduction for spatial transcriptomic profiling

Spatial transcriptomic (ST) data were processed and analyzed using the Seurat R package (v4.3.0) (55), which provides a comprehensive framework for spatially resolved gene expression profiling. Initial quality control (QC) steps included the exclusion of mitochondrial and ribosomal genes, which are known to introduce technical artifacts and confound biological interpretation. Additionally, low-abundance genes, defined as those expressed in fewer than 10 spatial barcoded spots, were filtered out to enhance the reliability and interpretability of downstream analyses. Data normalization and variance stabilization were performed using the SCTransform method, which effectively mitigates technical noise and allows for robust modeling of gene expression across spatial locations. Subsequently, HVGs were identified based on residual variance from the SCTransform model, and the top 3000 HVGs were selected for dimensionality reduction. Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted on the expression matrix of these HVGs, and the first 20 principal components were selected as input for downstream clustering, visualization, and spatial domain identification. These components captured the major axes of transcriptional variation across tissue sections, enabling accurate detection of spatially distinct cellular niches and gene expression patterns.

Multiscale spatial dissection and gradient profiling of the peritumoral microenvironment

We profiled microenvironmental heterogeneity at the tumor margin using high-resolution spatial transcriptomics. Spots outside tissue were removed by combining DBSCAN with histology-guided segmentation, and low-abundance or overly sparse genes were filtered to reduce technical noise. Spatially coherent transcriptional domains were identified with BayesSpace [via the Spata2 framework (56)], which employs a Bayesian hidden Markov random field to refine clustering resolution. We utilized this spatially-aware Bayesian approach to enhance resolution, allowing us to resolve sharp tissue boundaries. Spatially variable genes (SVGs) were detected with SPARK-X, a graph-based, nonparametric method optimized for spatial signal discovery.

In parallel, we inferred large-scale somatic chromosomal CNVs within epithelial compartments using inferCNV, enabling spatial discrimination of malignant versus non-malignant regions. To interrogate the organization of specific cellular programs, H&E images were manually annotated to delineate tumor core, invasive margin, and adjacent stroma. For each barcoded spot, Euclidean distances to region boundaries were computed to model continuous spatial gradients. We then quantified distance-dependent expression patterns for individual genes and curated gene sets, including CAFs modules and immune cell programs, thereby linking transcriptional states and copy-number context to their precise topographic positions within the peritumoral niche.

Cell2location analysis

To deconvolve the cellular composition of each spatial spot in ST data, we employed Cell2location (57), a Bayesian joint model that leverages scRNA-seq data as a reference. This method first builds a cell-type-specific reference gene expression matrix from the scRNA-seq data, which is then integrated with the ST data. The model uses variational inference to compute the absolute abundance of all known cell types within each spatial spot, enabling the precise spatial localization and quantification of cell types. Finally, the inferred cell type abundances were overlaid onto tissue images for visualization to analyze the spatial distribution patterns and co-localization relationships of different cell types within the tissue microenvironment.

COMMOT analysis

Co-occurrence-based Morphological analysis of Tissue (COMMOT) is a computational framework designed to quantify and analyze spatial cell-cell interactions within the tissue microenvironment (58). The algorithm takes the coordinates and cell type labels from spatial omics data as input. COMMOT computes a spatial co-occurrence index for pairs of cell types. This index goes beyond simple frequency, using a permutation test against a random distribution to assess the statistical significance of co-occurring or segregating patterns.

MISTy colocalization analysis

This study employs the Multiview Intercellular SpaTial modeling (MISTy) framework to analyze spatial interactions (59). It constructs multiview features, including the self-view (intracellular molecules), neighbor view (local neighborhood aggregation), and distant neighbor view (kernel-weighted global), and can incorporate type-specific neighbor views to capture cell type-dependent effects. For each target molecule, interpretable learners are trained separately across the views, and the view contributions and feature importance are obtained through late fusion. Performance is evaluated using cross-validation, and significance is assessed using permutation/randomization strategies with multiple corrections.

GSEA analysis

To identify the biological functions and pathways associated with our findings, we performed both functional enrichment and GSEA analysis based on clusterprofiler (60) package. For the enrichment analysis, we used an over-representation approach to identify significantly enriched Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways within our gene list.

Additionally, GSEA was performed to evaluate whether a set of pre-defined genes was disproportionately represented at the top or bottom of a complete gene list ranked by a metric such as fold change. This approach allows for the detection of subtle but coordinated changes in entire gene sets, even if individual genes do not meet the significance cutoff for differential expression.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining analysis

We validated spatial transcriptomic findings at the protein level using immunohistochemistry (IHC) on a renal cancer tissue microarray of 230 ccRCC patients treated at FUSCC (Shanghai, China), annotated for ICB response (61). Samples were stratified as ICB-responsive or ICB-resistant. We assessed POSTN (ThermoFisher PA5–34641), COL4A1 (ThermoFisher MA5–47009), and CD206 (Affinity DF4149; RRID: AB_2836514).

IHC followed our established protocol (62, 63), with minor modifications. Briefly, tumors were fixed in 4% neutral-buffered formaldehyde (24 hours), paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at 5 μm. Sections underwent xylene deparaffinization, graded ethanol rehydration, and citrate buffer (pH 6.0) heat-induced epitope retrieval. Endogenous peroxidase was quenched with 3% H2O2 (10 min), and nonspecific binding blocked with 5% BSA (30 min, room temperature). Primary antibodies were applied overnight at 4°C; HRP-conjugated secondaries were added for 1 hour at room temperature. Signal was developed with DAB and counterstained with hematoxylin. Slides were scanned digitally, and staining intensity/localization were semi-quantitatively scored by two independent, blinded pathologists.

Multiplex immunofluorescence analysis

We mapped TAM-epithelial spatial organization using multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) on FFPE ccRCC sections (Opal, Akoya). Primary antibodies included CD8 (T cells), CD68 (macrophages), CD163/CD206 (M2 markers), PD-L1, APOE, POSTN, and CA9 (tumor marker); nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to heat-induced epitope retrieval (Tris-EDTA, pH 9.0). After protein blocking, each primary antibody was applied sequentially with the matching Opal fluorophore-conjugated secondary; antigen retrieval was repeated between cycles to strip antibody complexes without quenching fluorophores (36, 61). DAPI was added after all cycles.

Slides were scanned on the Vectra Polaris (20×). Multispectral images were spectrally unmixed in inform to separate overlapping signals and enable quantitative, channel-specific analysis. This workflow localized macrophage subsets (CD68+, CD163+/CD206+), APOE and POSTN expression, and their proximity to CA9+ epithelium, yielding high-resolution maps of immune-stromal-tumor architecture and immunosuppressive dynamics.

Quantitative image analysis for spatial profiling

To quantify the abundance of CD8+ T cells and their spatial proximity to specific structures within the tissue microenvironment, we integrated two distinct quantitative image analysis methods. First, we generated binary masks for target cell populations and tissue regions by applying RGB color thresholding to immunofluorescence images (for CD8+ cells) and Otsu’s adaptive thresholding (for CA9+ or POSTN+ areas). We then automatically extracted the centroids of cells and the boundaries of regions for subsequent analysis. To assess cell density, we calculated the total count of CD8+ cells within each region, as well as the cellular density based on pixel tiles, and used a Welch’s t-test to compare differences between groups. Concurrently, we employed distance transform technology to efficiently compute the Euclidean distance from each CD8+/POSTN+/APOE+ cell centroid to the nearest CA9+ region, thereby quantifying their spatial tendency.

Cell lines culture and lentiviral transfection

The human ccRCC cell lines 786-O and 769-P (ATCC) were routinely cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin mixture, maintained in a humidified incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2. All cell lines were regularly tested for mycoplasma contamination and were only used for experiments after confirming negative results. Human fibroblasts were derived from HFF-1 (ATCC SCRC-1041) or laboratory-preserved human fibroblasts, collectively referred to as “fibroblasts.”

Generation of stable TGFβ1 -overexpressing ccRCC cell lines

To obtain stable overexpression cell lines, the coding sequence of human TGFβ1 was subcloned into a lentiviral vector (containing a puromycin selection marker; referred to as OE), with an empty vector used as a negative control (EV). The virus was produced using a third-generation packaging system in 293 T cells, and the supernatant was concentrated by ultrafiltration before infecting 786-O and 769-P cells (MOI 5–10, containing 8 μg/ml Polybrene). After 48 hours, puromycin selection was performed for 5–7 days. All control cells were established using the same selection process.

Generation of luciferase-expressing Renca cells

The parental mouse Renca renal cell carcinoma cell line was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). To generate luciferase-labeled cells for in vivo imaging, parental Renca cells were transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding the firefly luciferase gene (Fluc) and a puromycin resistance cassette. Briefly, Renca cells were seeded at 50% confluence and incubated with the viral supernatant in the presence of 2 μg/ml polybrene for 48 hours. Following transduction, cells were selected with 5 μg/ml puromycin for 7 days to establish a stable polyclonal population expressing luciferase (designated as Renca-Luc).

Transwell-based indirect co-culture assays

Indirect co-culture used Transwell inserts (0.4 μm, Corning). Unless noted, 786-O or 769-P tumor cells were seeded in the lower chamber and fibroblasts in the upper chamber. After 12–16 hours adhesion, medium was replaced with 1% FBS to minimize serum effects. Co-culture proceeded 48 hours, then cells and supernatants were harvested. Four conditions were tested: (1) EV control: EV tumor cells + fibroblasts; (2) TGFβ1 overexpression (OE): TGFβ1-OE tumor cells + fibroblasts; (3) EV + recombinant TGFβ1 (10 ng/ml); (4) OE + TGFβ1 neutralizing antibody (10 μg/ml; isotype control matched). Pathway activation was validated by concurrent detection of p-SMAD2/3 in key experiments.

Collection of tumor-derived conditioned media (CM)

786-O and 769-P cells were seeded in 10 cm dishes (2 × 106/dish) and allowed to adhere overnight. The culture medium was then replaced with RPMI-1640 containing 1% FBS, and the cells were further cultured for 24–36 hours. The supernatant was collected and centrifuged at 3000g for 10 minutes to remove cell debris, followed by filtration through a 0.22 μm membrane to obtain CM-786 and CM-769.

Generation of CAF-like phenotypes and CAF-derived CM

Two methods were employed to obtain CAF conditioned medium: 1) TGF-β1 activation: Recombinant human TGF-β1 (10 ng/ml) was used to stimulate for 24 hours; 2) Tumor supernatant induction: CM-786 (or EV/OE-CM, mixed in a 1:1 volume ratio with fresh 1% FBS medium) was incubated for 24–36 hours. CAF supernatant was collected under the same treatment conditions to obtain 786/CAF-CM. In the blocking experiments, Periostin neutralizing antibody (POSTNab, 10 μg/ml, Cat. PHJ4905S, Abmart, China) or isotype control was added during the last 24 hours of incubation.

Macrophage differentiation and functional reprogramming

THP-1 cells were induced to obtain M0-like macrophages by treating with 100 nM PMA for 24 hours, followed by a 24-hours recovery period after PMA withdrawal. The cells were then subjected to the following treatments for 48 hours: Co-cultured EV: M0 was co-cultured with CAF using a Transwell (0.4 μm) for indirect co-culture, with EV-CM added to the lower chamber as a control; Co-cultured OE: similar to the previous setup, but OE-CM was added to the lower chamber to evaluate the hereditary enhancement of tumor-derived TGFβ1; Co-cultured EV + TGFβ1: recombinant TGFβ1 (10 ng/ml) was added to the EV co-culture system; Co-cultured OE + TGFβ1ab / POSTNab: TGFβ1 neutralizing antibody or POSTNab (10 μg/ml) was added to the OE co-culture system to verify the dependence of the phenotype on the relevant axis; CM treatment: the culture medium was directly replaced with CM-786 / CM-769/786/CAF-CM; IL-4 (20 ng/ml, Cat. 90197ES60, Yeasen, China) was added as a positive control for M2 polarization, when necessary, with Vehicle being an equal volume of solvent.

Western blotting (WB) assay

Cells were lysed using RIPA lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors, and protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay kit. Subsequently, proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to a PVDF membrane. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk, the membrane was incubated overnight at 4°C with the primary antibody, followed by a 1-hour incubation at room temperature with the secondary antibody. Finally, the proteins were visualized using ECL reagents, and protein bands were analyzed using ImageJ software.

Flow cytometry assay

After culturing the cells under the specified treatment conditions for 48 hours, they were collected using gentle digestion (0.05% Trypsin-EDTA or Accutase, <5 min) and mechanical pipetting with EDTA. The cells were then centrifuged at 4°C for 300 g and washed twice with pre-cooled PBS. The cells were resuspended in FACS buffer (PBS + 2% FBS + 2 mM EDTA), and Fc receptor blocking and viability staining were performed. Subsequently, the cells were incubated in the dark at 4°C for 30 min, followed by staining with appropriately fluorochrome-conjugated primary antibodies against surface markers (selected according to experimental needs). After two washes with FACS buffer, the cells were filtered through a 40 μm mesh and resuspended in 300–500 μl of PBS for analysis. Data were collected using a BD FACSCanto II (or equivalent device), with compensation performed using single-color controls, and FMO/isotype controls used to determine thresholds when necessary; each sample had at least ≥3000 events acquired. Data were analyzed in FlowJo according to the established gating procedures.

ELISA assay

The cell culture supernatant was collected, centrifuged at 1000g for 10 min at 4°C, and stored at −80°C. Cytokine levels (CCL2, TGFβ, and IL-10) were measured using an ELISA kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, a 96-well plate was pre-coated with a capture antibody, blocked with 1% BSA, and then incubated with either the supernatant or standard for 2 hours. After washing, biotin-labeled detection antibody and HRP-streptavidin were added, followed by the addition of TMB substrate for color development. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

In vivo tumor model and therapeutic intervention groups

Six- to eight-week-old, sex-matched SPF-grade mice of the same strain were maintained under standardized housing conditions (temperature, humidity, 12-hour light/dark cycle), with ad libitum access to food and water. All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (FUSCC-IACUC-S2024–0626) and conducted in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines. Luciferase-expressing tumor cells (Luc+) were subcutaneously injected (1 × 106 cells/mouse, 100 μl PBS: Matrigel = 1:1). Upon reaching a tumor volume of 50–100 mm3 (day 7–9), mice were randomized and treated in a blinded manner across five groups (n = 5 per group): G1: Vehicle control; G2: TGFβ agonist (myCAF induction); G3: Immune checkpoint blockade (anti-PD-L1, 200 μg/mouse, i.p., biweekly for 2–3 weeks); G4: POSTN blockade (neutralizing antibody or inhibitor, 10 mg/kg, i.p., biweekly); G5: Combination therapy (anti-PD-L1 + POSTN blockade, same dosing regimen).

Longitudinal tumor monitoring via in vivo bioluminescence imaging (BLI)

Tumor burden was assessed weekly using BLI. Mice were injected intraperitoneally with D-luciferin (150 mg/kg), anesthetized with isoflurane, and imaged using the IVIS Spectrum System 2–5 min postinjection. Exposure time and gain settings were standardized. Quantification was performed with Living Image software, and tumor burden was expressed as radiance (photons/sec) within the defined region of interest (ROI).

Statistical analysis

The analysis of categorical data was performed using Fisher’s exact test and the rank sum test. For comparisons between two groups, a t-test was employed, whereas ANOVA facilitated pairwise comparisons among several groups. K-M curves for survival analysis.

Local relaxation and scale-dependent alignment in compressible, magnetized turbulence
James R. Beattie †,, Amitava Bhattacharjee

Abstract
Driven net- and no-net-flux MHD turbulence simulations up to 10,368 3 10,\!368^{3} reveal sign-mixed velocity–magnetic, velocity–vorticity, and magnetic–current aligned patches below the energy equipartition scale. The first two angles scale as λ 1 / 8 \lambda^{1/8} and λ 1 / 16 \lambda^{1/16} , while the third angle varies weakly with scale. We develop and test a constant-flux transport model for departures from relaxed states, which predicts both exponents. These findings affect eddy anisotropy, reconnection-mediated turbulence onset, large-scale dynamos, and the nature of magnetized turbulence.

Recent work by
Banerjee et al. [5]
describes a theory for the relaxation of scale-dependent helicity and invariant correlations, named the principle of vanishing nonlinear transfer (PVNLT). In PVNLT, a relaxed state has vanishing nonlinear transfer of the invariant correlations (see Supplemental Material
[
1
]

\displaystyle\bm{u}\times\bm{B}

\displaystyle\simeq 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bm{u}=\kappa\bm{B},\qquad\bm{\omega}=\kappa\bm{J},

(1)

\displaystyle(1-\kappa^{2})\bm{J}\times\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\bm{\nabla}(P_{T}+\phi_{0}),

(2)

\phi_{0}

\bm{u}=\pm\bm{B}

\bm{J}\parallel\bm{B}

\bm{u}\times\bm{\omega}

\lambda

(net-flux)

\lambda_{\rm eq}

The scale-dependent range in the
\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}
and
\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}

\sim\lambda_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}(\lambda)\sim\lambda^{1/8},\qquad\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}(\lambda)\sim\lambda^{1/16},

(5)

while
\theta_{\bm{B},\bm{J}}(\lambda)
remains strongly aligned with only weak scale variation. The net-flux guide-field statistics (dashed lines) show the same shallow

\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}\sim\lambda^{1/4}

\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}\sim\lambda^{1/16}

These three measurements are captured by small departures from relaxed states. Consider the velocity and magnetic increment amplitudes scale as
\delta u_{\lambda}\sim\delta B_{\lambda}
, e.g., on scales below
\lambda_{\rm eq}
. Using increment vectors
\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}
and
\bm{\delta}\bm{B}_{\lambda}
, the Alfvénic departure
\mathcal{D}_{A}
is the positive quadratic distance from
\bm{u}=\pm\bm{B}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{A}(\lambda)=\min_{\sigma=\pm 1}\frac{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}-\sigma\bm{\delta}\bm{B}_{\lambda}|^{2}}{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}|^{2}+|\bm{\delta}\bm{B}_{\lambda}|^{2}}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}^{2}(\lambda),

(6)

where the last relation assumes nearly equipartitioned, small-angle increments. This difference norm is natural because Alfvénic relaxation requires equality,
\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}=\pm\bm{\delta}\bm{B}_{\lambda}
, not only parallelism. A scale-independent flux
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}
for carrying this first moment downscale,
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}\sim\delta u_{\lambda}^{3}\mathcal{D}_{A}/\lambda
, gives

\displaystyle\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}(\lambda)\propto\lambda^{(1-3\zeta)/2}\simeq\lambda^{1/8}.

(7)

We test this prediction using the shell-to-shell departure transfer
T_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}(K|P)
and its cross-scale flux
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}
(definitions in the End Matter section “Departure transfer diagnostics”). Figure
3
shows that
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}
closely tracks the energy flux, becomes approximately constant near
k_{\rm eq}
, and is dominated by interactions between neighboring shells. We also compute total-energy transfers as references. The fields are filtered and downsampled before analysis; normalization, shell definitions, and conservative transfer operators are specified in End Matter. The shared constant-flux interval directly ties departure transport to the energy cascade.

For
\bm{u}
–
\bm{\omega}
, the analogous signed density is kinetic helicity. The End Matter section “Cross and kinetic helicity transport in the barotropic and force-free limits,” Eq. (
26
), shows that the kinetic helicity is made non-conservative by a non-gradient Lorentz force, but the observed Taylorization strongly suppresses that source. The remaining issue is sign: in the present nonhelical system the signed first moment can cancel between Beltrami and anti-Beltrami patches. The lowest useful sign-even Beltrami constraint is therefore the second moment of the Beltrami departure. With vorticity increment
\bm{\delta}\bm{\omega}_{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{B}(\lambda)=1-\frac{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{\delta}\bm{\omega}_{\lambda}|}{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}||\bm{\delta}\bm{\omega}_{\lambda}|}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}^{2}(\lambda),

(8)

which is angular because Beltrami states require proportionality between
\bm{u}
and
\bm{\omega}
, whose amplitudes and dimensions differ. The amplitude-weighted transfer diagnostic used to measure this transport is defined in the End Matter section “Departure transfer diagnostics,” Eqs. (
34
) and (
35
). The approximately constant
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}(k)
intervals in Fig.
3
support a scale-independent rate
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}
for carrying this second moment downscale,
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}\sim\delta u_{\lambda}^{3}\mathcal{D}_{B}^{2}/\lambda
, which gives

\displaystyle\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}(\lambda)\propto\lambda^{(1-3\zeta)/4}\simeq\lambda^{1/16}.

(9)

Thus the different powers of the angle are not introduced symmetrically. The Alfvénic case has a positive first-moment departure inherited from the energy–cross-helicity square, while the kinetic-helicity case is signed and globally canceled, making a departure correlation the leading sign-even object. Supplemental Material, Sec. IV, gives a more detailed exponent derivation.

V
Discussion & Conclusions

Driven compressible MHD turbulence self-organizes into sign-mixed relaxed patches below
\lambda_{\rm eq}
, analogous to cross-helical patches in the solar wind and magnetosheath
[
44
,
47
,
48
]
. The measured shell transfers support a constant, local cascade of departures from these states: with
\delta u_{\lambda}\sim\lambda^{1/4}
,
\mathcal{D}_{A}\sim\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}^{2}
and
\mathcal{D}_{B}^{2}\sim\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}^{4}
yield the observed
\lambda^{1/8}
Alfvénic and
\lambda^{1/16}
Beltrami exponents. Strong Taylorization suppresses the Lorentz-force source in the kinetic-helicity evolution, enabling conservative Beltrami-departure transport.

Scale-dependent alignment sets eddy anisotropy:
\lambda/\xi\sim\theta_{\lambda}

[
9
,
37
,
40
]
, so the measured
\theta_{\lambda}\sim\lambda^{1/8}
gives
\xi\sim\lambda^{7/8}
rather than
\lambda^{3/4}
. This weaker sheet anisotropy changes the expected onset of a tearing-mediated cascade
[
36
,
39
,
8
,
17
]
. For alignment and fluctuation-amplitude exponents
a
and
\zeta
, the standard tearing balance gives

\displaystyle k_{*}L\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{1/(1+2a+\zeta)}.

(10)

Thus
a=\zeta=1/4
gives
k_{*}L\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{4/7}
, whereas the measured
a=1/8
,
\zeta\simeq 1/4
gives
k_{*}L\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{2/3}
. This shifts the tearing transition to smaller scales and substantially increases the
\mathrm{Rm}
required to separate it from the resistive scale (Supplemental Material, Sec. II).

Sign-mixed helicities also enter large-scale dynamo closures through the turbulent EMF,
\bm{\mathcal{E}}=\langle\bm{\delta}\bm{u}\times\bm{\delta}\bm{B}\rangle
, residual kinetic–current helicity in
\alpha
, and cross helicity in
\Upsilon

[
51
,
61
,
11
,
58
]
. Here
\alpha
combines residual kinetic and current helicities, whereas
\Upsilon
is proportional to cross helicity; their cancellations can therefore differ across averaging scales and local environments. Alfvénic alignment suppresses the fluctuating cross product but enhances the scalar cross helicity supplying
\Upsilon
, which can dominate shear-layer dynamos
[
58
]
. It also sets a maximum averaging scale
\lambda_{\rm eq}
over which the signed helicities cancel, limiting the surviving scale-dependent dynamo contributions.

MMS magnetosheath observations already show analogous relaxation-angle PDFs and cascade transfers
[
3
,
4
,
48
]
; measuring all three scale-dependent alignments would extend recent solar-wind
\bm{u}
–
\bm{B}
measurements
[
55
]
.

V.1
Numerical details and Reynolds estimate

To avoid spending the highest-resolution run on transient states, we initialize the largest grids with steady-state realizations from lower-resolution simulations. For both simulations, we drive
\sim 5,\!000^{3}
boxes to stationarity, interpolate the steady-state turbulence to the
10,\!000^{3}
grids, then drive for two more outer-scale turnover times
2t_{0}
, and average the main statistics, structure functions and PDFs, over 15 stationary realizations in the final
1.5t_{0}
. The forcing sets the target rms velocity
\langle u^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}^{1/2}
; in the net-flux run we then choose the imposed uniform field
\bm{B}_{0}=B_{0}\hat{\bm{z}}
so that
\mathcal{M}_{A0}=\langle u^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}^{1/2}/(B_{0}/\sqrt{\rho_{0}})=2
, matching the rms Alfvénic Mach number of the fluctuation-dynamo simulation.
The simulations are implicit large-eddy simulations, with effective viscosity and resistivity supplied by the discretization. Calibrations against explicit-viscosity DNS give empirical conversions from grid scale to
\mathrm{Re}
and
\mathrm{Rm}

[
35
,
31
,
41
]
. For supersonic MHD turbulence,
Malvadi Shivakumar and Federrath [41]
find
\mathrm{Re}=(N_{\rm grid}/N_{\mathrm{Re}})^{p_{\mathrm{Re}}}
with
p_{\mathrm{Re}}\in[1.5,2.0]
,
N_{\mathrm{Re}}\in[0.8,4.4]
, and
\mathrm{Rm}=(N_{\rm grid}/N_{\mathrm{Rm}})^{p_{\mathrm{Rm}}}
with
p_{\mathrm{Rm}}\in[1.2,1.6]
,
N_{\mathrm{Rm}}\in[0.1,0.7]
. For
N_{\rm grid}\simeq 10^{4}
, this gives
\mathrm{Re}\in[1.4\times 10^{6},5.3\times 10^{6}]
and
\mathrm{Rm}\in[1.0\times 10^{6},4.5\times 10^{6}]
.

\displaystyle\bm{\delta}\bm{X}(\bm{x},\bm{\ell})

\displaystyle=\bm{X}(\bm{x}+\bm{\ell})-\bm{X}(\bm{x}),

(13)

\displaystyle\bm{\delta}\bm{Y}(\bm{x},\bm{\ell})

\displaystyle=\bm{Y}(\bm{x}+\bm{\ell})-\bm{Y}(\bm{x}),

(14)

with the appropriate
\lambda

]
, we define

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{B}}_{\bm{\ell}}=\frac{\bm{B}(\bm{x})+\bm{B}(\bm{x}+\bm{\ell})}{|\bm{B}(\bm{x})+\bm{B}(\bm{x}+\bm{\ell})|},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{\perp,\bm{\ell}}=\mathsf{I}-\widehat{\bm{B}}_{\bm{\ell}}\widehat{\bm{B}}_{\bm{\ell}},

(15)

and project the increments into the plane perpendicular to this two-point mean magnetic field,

\displaystyle\bm{\delta}\bm{X}_{\perp,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\perp,\bm{\ell}}\bm{\delta}\bm{X},

(16)

\displaystyle\bm{\delta}\bm{Y}_{\perp,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\perp,\bm{\ell}}\bm{\delta}\bm{Y}.

(17)

We then construct the ratio of first-order structure functions

\displaystyle\theta_{\bm{X},\bm{Y}}(\lambda)\sim|\sin\theta_{\bm{X},\bm{Y}}(\lambda)|=\frac{\left\langle{|\bm{\delta}\bm{X}_{\perp,\lambda}\times\bm{\delta}\bm{Y}_{\perp,\lambda}|}\right\rangle_{\lambda}}{\left\langle{|\bm{\delta}\bm{X}_{\perp,\lambda}||\bm{\delta}\bm{Y}_{\perp,\lambda}|}\right\rangle_{\lambda}}.

(18)

Here
\left\langle{\cdots}\right\rangle_{\lambda}
denotes an average over positions, sampled lag directions, and stationary-state realizations in the separation bin. The no-net-flux construction is applied to the pairs
(\bm{u},\bm{B})
,
(\bm{B},\bm{J})
, and
(\bm{u},\bm{\omega})
; the guide-field comparison applies the corresponding perpendicular-plane construction to the same three pairs. We restrict attention to this local-field projection because scale-dependent anisotropy and alignment of the strong MHD cascade are recovered in the local mean-field coordinate
[
16
,
42
,
43
,
37
,
19
,
20
]
. Since the no-net-flux run has
\left\langle{\bm{B}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}=0
, it has no global parallel direction to exclude; the isotropic
\lambda=|\bm{\ell}|
statistic is therefore the zero-guide-field analogue of the perpendicular guide-field statistic. Both choices identify the separation across which the strong cascade transfers fluctuation energy. We use
2\times 10^{12}
sampled point pairs to ensure convergence of the structure functions on all scales
[
26
,
6
]
.

V.3
Cross and kinetic helicity transport in the barotropic and force-free limits

The ideal, barotropic, unforced (inertial) velocity, induction, and vorticity equations are

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}\times\bm{\omega}-\bm{\nabla}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}+h\right)+\bm{f}_{L},

(19)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\bm{\nabla}\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{B}),

(20)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{\omega}

\displaystyle=\bm{\nabla}\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{\omega})+\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{f}_{L}.

(21)

Here we define the Lorentz acceleration

\displaystyle\bm{f}_{L}=\frac{\bm{J}\times\bm{B}}{\rho},

(22)

which vanishes in the Taylor-relaxed limit,
\bm{J}=\alpha(\bm{x})\bm{B}
, and is gradient-like in the pressure-balanced limit,
\bm{f}_{L}\simeq\bm{\nabla}\chi
for scalar
\chi
. We use ideal MHD with a barotropic plasma equation of state (including the isothermal equation solved here and general isentropic adiabatic plasmas), so the pressure force is a gradient,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\bm{\nabla}p=\bm{\nabla}h,\qquad h(\rho)=\int^{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}\frac{dp}{d\rho^{\prime}}\,d\rho^{\prime},

(23)

which means the pressure term may enter through the conservative fluxes.

V.3.1
Helicity evolution equations

Taking
\partial_{t}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B})
gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H_{C}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{F}_{C}=0,

(24)

with
H_{C}=\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}
and

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{C}=\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}+h\right)\bm{B}+\bm{u}\times(\bm{u}\times\bm{B}),

(25)

The Lorentz acceleration drops out identically because
\bm{B}\cdot\bm{f}_{L}=\bm{B}\cdot(\bm{J}\times\bm{B})/\rho=0
, and the barotropic pressure force contributes as an enthalpy flux. Thus
H_{C}
is a compressible, ideal invariant.
Taking
\partial_{t}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\omega})
gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}K+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{F}_{K}=2\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{f}_{L},

(26)

where
K=\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\omega}
, with flux

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{K}=K\bm{u}+\left(h-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)\bm{\omega}+\bm{u}\times\bm{f}_{L}.

(27)

In the absence of baroclinicity,
|\bm{\nabla}\rho\times\bm{\nabla}p|=0
, density enters only through the Lorentz acceleration. In the force-free case the kinetic-helicity source vanishes pointwise, while in the gradient-like case
2\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{f}_{L}\simeq 2\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\chi=2\bm{\nabla}\cdot(\chi\bm{\omega})
, so the source becomes a conservative flux and
\partial_{t}K+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{F}_{K}\approx 0
. Approximately conserved kinetic-helicity transport therefore requires a small non-gradient part of
(\bm{J}\times\bm{B})/\rho
, and the measured
\bm{B}
–
\bm{J}
alignment is the stronger sufficient condition. Thus, in an ideal, unforced, force-free, barotropic but compressible plasma, both cross helicity,
\bm{u}\cdot\bm{B}
, and kinetic helicity,
\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\omega}
, are ideal invariants, justifying the structure functions studied in Fig.
2
.

V.4
Departure transfer diagnostics

We use Fourier shell filters
\mathcal{P}_{K}
, with
\bm{f}_{K}=\mathcal{P}_{K}\bm{f}
and
\bm{f}_{\leq m}=\sum_{P\leq m}\bm{f}_{P}
. No-net-flux transfers are binned in
k
; guide-field transfers are binned in
k_{\perp}
and summed over
k_{\parallel}
. For any conservative shell transfer
T_{X}(K|P)
, the downscale flux through shell boundary
m
is

\displaystyle\Pi_{X}(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K>m}\sum_{P\leq m}T_{X}(K|P).

(28)

We write
dV
for integrals over the periodic volume. Compressible advective transfers use

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\bm{c}}\bm{q}_{P}

\displaystyle=(\bm{c}\cdot\bm{\nabla})\bm{q}_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\bm{q}_{P}(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{c}),

(29)

with the same definition for scalar
q_{P}
. This operator is skew-adjoint for periodic boundaries, and the half-divergence term is required for velocity-mediated transfers in compressible flow
[
30
]
. The magnetic field
\bm{B}
below is the field used in the alignment statistic and cross helicity, up to a constant code-unit normalization; no local factor of
\rho^{-1/2}
is applied.

The total-energy transfer plotted in Fig.
3
is

\displaystyle T_{E}(K|P)=

\displaystyle-\int\bm{u}_{K}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\bm{u}}\bm{u}_{P}\,dV-\int\bm{B}_{K}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\bm{u}}\bm{B}_{P}\,dV

\displaystyle+\int\bm{u}_{K}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\bm{B}}\bm{B}_{P}\,dV+\int\bm{B}_{K}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\bm{B}}\bm{u}_{P}\,dV.

(30)

For Alfvénic departure, we define a fixed-sign departure and mediator fields

\displaystyle\bm{d}^{\sigma}=\bm{u}-\sigma\bm{B},\qquad\bm{a}^{\sigma}=\bm{u}+\sigma\bm{B},\qquad\sigma=\pm 1.

(31)

The fixed-sign shell transfer is

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}^{\sigma}(K|P)

\displaystyle=-\int\bm{d}_{K}^{\sigma}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}^{\sigma}}\bm{d}_{P}^{\sigma}\,dV.

(32)

Figure
3
shows the sign-even matrix
T_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}=(T_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}^{+}+T_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}^{-})/2
. For the flux, the nearest local Alfvénic branch is chosen at each cutoff,

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sgn}\!\left[\bm{u}_{\leq m}(\bm{x})\cdot\bm{B}_{\leq m}(\bm{x})\right],

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}(m)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\int\mathbb{I}_{\sigma_{m}=\sigma}\bm{d}_{>m}^{\sigma}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\bm{a}^{\sigma}}\bm{d}_{\leq m}^{\sigma}\,dV.

(33)

The local sign mask makes
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}
a scale-local flux diagnostic rather than a conventional antisymmetric shell-transfer matrix.

For the Beltrami departure, we define the velocity component perpendicular to vorticity and the transported vector

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}-\frac{\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\omega}}{\omega^{2}}\bm{\omega},

\displaystyle\bm{d}_{B}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}_{\perp}\sin\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}},

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\bm{u}\times\bm{\omega}|^{2}}{u^{2}\omega^{2}},

(34)

with
\bm{d}_{B}=0
where
u^{2}\omega^{2}
is numerically zero, and
u=|\bm{u}|
,
\omega=|\bm{\omega}|
. Here
\bm{u}_{\perp}
is the velocity component that vanishes in either Beltrami branch, while the extra factor of
\sin\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}
makes
|\bm{d}_{B}|^{2}=u^{2}\sin^{4}\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}
, the amplitude-weighted, sign-even second-moment departure used above. Its conservative transfer and flux are

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}(K|P)

\displaystyle=-\int\bm{d}_{B,K}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\bm{u}}\bm{d}_{B,P}\,dV,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K>m}\sum_{P\leq m}T_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}(K|P).

(35)

By construction,
T_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}(K|P)=-T_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}(P|K)
.

II. Critical length scale for tearing-mediated cascade

Starting from the disruption criterion used in reconnection-mediated cascade models
[
36
,
39
,
8
,
17
]
, the critical eddy satisfies, up to the logarithmic Lambert-
W
factor and order-unity constants,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{*}}{\xi_{*}}\sim S_{\xi_{*}}^{-1/3},\qquad S_{\xi_{*}}=\frac{\xi_{*}\delta b_{\lambda_{*}}}{\eta}.

(S1)

Here
\lambda_{*}
and
\xi_{*}
are the reconnecting-sheet thickness and length,
S_{\xi_{*}}
is the Lundquist number based on
\xi_{*}
,
\delta b_{\lambda_{*}}
is the magnetic-fluctuation amplitude at
\lambda_{*}
, and
\eta
is the magnetic diffusivity. Let
a
be the alignment exponent and
\zeta
the magnetic-amplitude exponent, then,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\xi}\sim\theta_{\lambda}\sim\left(\frac{\lambda}{L}\right)^{a},\qquad\delta B_{\lambda}\sim\left(\frac{\lambda}{L}\right)^{\zeta}.

(S2)

The aligned eddy length
\xi_{\lambda}
and corresponding Lundquist number
S_{\xi}
are then

\displaystyle\xi_{\lambda}\sim L\left(\frac{\lambda}{L}\right)^{1-a},\qquad S_{\xi}\sim\mathrm{Rm}\left(\frac{\lambda}{L}\right)^{1-a+\zeta}.

(S3)

Substituting into Eq. (
S1
) gives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{*}}{L}\right)^{a}\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{-1/3}\left(\frac{\lambda_{*}}{L}\right)^{-(1-a+\zeta)/3},

(S4)

or

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{*}}{L}\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{-1/(1+2a+\zeta)},\qquad k_{*}L\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{1/(1+2a+\zeta)}.

(S5)

where
k_{*}\sim 1/\lambda_{*}
is the critical wavenumber. For the usual dynamic-alignment scaling
a=\zeta=1/4
, this recovers

\displaystyle k_{*}L\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{4/7}.

(S6)

For the present alignment law,
a=1/8
, together with the measured subsonic
k^{-3/2}
velocity scaling
\zeta\simeq 1/4
, Eq. (
S5
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{*}}{L}\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{-2/3},\qquad k_{*}L\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{2/3}.

(S7)

Thus the weaker Alfvénic alignment shifts the tearing-mediated transition to smaller scales than the standard dynamic-alignment estimate. The same scaling also changes the Reynolds number needed for a resolved tearing-mediated interval. If the onset criterion is a fixed separation between the disruption scale and the resistive scale,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\eta}}{L}\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{-1/(1+\zeta)},

(S8)

then

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{*}}{\lambda_{\eta}}\sim\mathrm{Rm}^{1/(1+\zeta)-1/(1+2a+\zeta)}.

(S9)

For
\zeta=1/4
, the exponent in Eq. (
S9
) changes from
8/35
when
a=1/4
to
2/15
when
a=1/8
. Therefore a critical value inferred in the standard dynamic-alignment case maps to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rm}_{c}(a=1/8)\sim\left[\mathrm{Rm}_{c}(a=1/4)\right]^{12/7}.

(S10)

Taking
\mathrm{Rm}_{c}(a=1/4)\sim 10^{5}
, consistent with the threshold discussed by
Dong et al. [20]
, gives
\mathrm{Rm}_{c}(a=1/8)\sim 4\times 10^{8}
, up to order-unity and logarithmic corrections.

III. PVNLT relaxed states

For an inviscid invariant built from fields
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}
,

\displaystyle M=\int\bm{p}\cdot\bm{q}\,d^{3}x

(S11)

PVNLT
[
5
]
considers the two-point correlator at separation
\bm{\ell}
,

\displaystyle R_{M}(\bm{\ell})=\left\langle\frac{\bm{p}(\bm{x})\cdot\bm{q}(\bm{x}+\bm{\ell})+\bm{p}(\bm{x}+\bm{\ell})\cdot\bm{q}(\bm{x})}{2}\right\rangle,

(S12)

where the average is over position
\bm{x}
. The schematic evolution of the correlator is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}R_{M}=\left\langle F_{\rm tr}^{M}\right\rangle+\left\langle f_{c}^{M}\right\rangle+\left\langle d_{c}^{M}\right\rangle.

(S13)

Here
F_{\rm tr}^{M}
is the nonlinear transfer of the correlator, while
f_{c}^{M}
and
d_{c}^{M}
denote forcing and dissipation contributions. In a stationary inertial range,
\partial_{t}R_{M}=0
and
\left\langle f_{c}^{M}\right\rangle=\left\langle d_{c}^{M}\right\rangle=0
, so the nonlinear transfer term must vanish,

\displaystyle\left\langle F_{\rm tr}^{M}\right\rangle=0.

(S14)

This is the condition for relaxation in PVNLT. Applying Eq. (
S14
) to incompressible MHD gives

\displaystyle\bm{u}\times\bm{\omega}+\bm{J}\times\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\bm{\nabla}(P_{T}+\phi_{0}),

(S15)

\displaystyle\bm{u}\times\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\bm{\nabla}\psi_{0},

(S16)

for the energy, magnetic-helicity, and cross-helicity correlators, where
P_{T}=p+u^{2}/2
is the total pressure built from plasma pressure
p
and kinetic pressure
u^{2}/2
, and
\phi_{0},\psi_{0}
are scalar fields
[
5
]
. Following
Banerjee et al. [5]
, the aligned limits follow by taking the scalar-gradient part of the induction condition to be negligible. If
\bm{\nabla}\psi_{0}\simeq 0
, then

\displaystyle\bm{u}\times\bm{B}\simeq 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bm{u}=\kappa\bm{B},\qquad\bm{\omega}=\kappa\bm{J},

(S17)

where the last relation follows for constant
\kappa
. Substitution into Eq. (
S15
) gives

\displaystyle(1-\kappa^{2})\bm{J}\times\bm{B}=\bm{\nabla}(P_{T}+\phi_{0}).

(S18)

Thus the relaxed state is generally pressure- or gradient-balanced. In the low-
\beta
, magnetically dominated limit discussed by
Banerjee et al. [5]
, Eq. (
S18
) reduces to
\bm{J}\times\bm{B}\simeq 0
, the Taylor or force-free limit. If instead
\kappa=\pm 1
, the same branch gives
\bm{u}=\pm\bm{B}
and Eq. (
S15
) requires only a constant total pressure, the Alfvénic limit. Finally, Eq. (
S15
) shows the Beltrami limit: if the Lorentz term is force-free or pressure-balanced, then the non-gradient part of
\bm{u}\times\bm{\omega}
is depleted, and the negligible-gradient limit gives
\bm{u}\times\bm{\omega}\simeq 0
. Thus PVNLT
[
5
]
naturally yields pressure- or gradient-balanced relaxed states, with Taylor, Alfvénic, and Beltrami alignments as measurable limiting cases.

IV. Estimate for the alignment exponents

The departure model keeps the PVNLT
[
5
]
idea that relaxation depletes nonlinear invariant transfer, but applies it at finite distance from a relaxed state. Let
\delta u_{\lambda}\sim\delta B_{\lambda}\sim\lambda^{\zeta}
with
\zeta\simeq 1/4
in the measured subsonic range. For an Alfvénic state, the normalized positive distance from the nearest
\bm{u}=\pm\bm{B}
state is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{A}(\lambda)=\min_{\sigma=\pm 1}\frac{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}-\sigma\bm{\delta}\bm{B}_{\lambda}|^{2}}{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}|^{2}+|\bm{\delta}\bm{B}_{\lambda}|^{2}}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}^{2}(\lambda),

(S19)

for nearly equipartitioned, small-angle increments. If this first-moment departure is carried downscale at a scale-independent rate,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}(\lambda)\sim\frac{\delta u_{\lambda}^{3}\mathcal{D}_{A}(\lambda)}{\lambda},

(S20)

then

\displaystyle\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{B}}(\lambda)\propto\lambda^{(1-3\zeta)/2}\simeq\lambda^{1/8},

(S21)

which gives the measured Alfvénic exponent. For the Beltrami departure, the local invariant density is kinetic helicity,
\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\omega}
. It is signed and globally canceled in the present nonhelical system, so the leading sign-even constraint is a second-moment departure. With

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{B}(\lambda)=1-\frac{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{\delta}\bm{\omega}_{\lambda}|}{|\bm{\delta}\bm{u}_{\lambda}||\bm{\delta}\bm{\omega}_{\lambda}|}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}^{2}(\lambda),

(S22)

and a scale-independent rate for carrying this second moment downscale,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathcal{D}_{B}}(\lambda)\sim\frac{\delta u_{\lambda}^{3}\mathcal{D}_{B}^{2}(\lambda)}{\lambda},

(S23)

one obtains

\displaystyle\theta_{\bm{u},\bm{\omega}}(\lambda)\propto\lambda^{(1-3\zeta)/4}\simeq\lambda^{1/16}.

(S24)

The different powers follow from the invariant being transported: the Alfvénic alignment type has a positive first-moment distance built from energy and cross helicity, while the Beltrami alignment type is signed and therefore enters through a sign-even second moment. The End Matter section “Departure transfer diagnostics” gives the corresponding shell-transfer diagnostics.

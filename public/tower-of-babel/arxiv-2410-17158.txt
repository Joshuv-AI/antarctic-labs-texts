Abstract
. We combine the relative trace formula with analytic methods to obtain zero density estimate for L L -functions in various families of automorphic representations for GL ⁡ ( m ) \mathrm{GL}(m) . Applications include strong bounds for the average analytic rank of these L L -functions at the central point and average equidistribution results for the imaginary parts of the zeros.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

1.1.
Zero density estimates

A zero density estimate is a practical substitute for the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH). While it does not exclude zeros off the critical line, it shows in a precise quantitative sense that most members of a family of
L
-function do not have zeros with a certain distance to the critical line. A prototype is the Bombieri–Vinograodv theorem that shows that a suitable zero density estimate for Dirichlet
L
-functions governs the average distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions of the same strength as the Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet
L
-functions.

Around 1990, Sarnak introduced a similar concept for automorphic forms: an automorphic density theorem is a statement that in a family of automorphic forms only few members can violate the Ramanujan conjecture to a given extent. Such statements are usually an application of a trace or relative trace formula.

In this paper, we use the machinery of the relative trace formula leading to recent progress on automorphic density theorems and large sieve inequalities to obtain classical zero density estimates for
L
-functions in various families of automorphic representations
\pi
for
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(m)
. We start our discussion with a “level aspect” family. Let
m\geq 2
be an integer,
q\geq 1
an integer, and
\Gamma_{0}(q)
the level
q
congruence subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
, where the last row is congruent to
(0,\ldots,0,*)
modulo
q
. Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq(0,\infty)
be a fixed interval of length at least
1
, and let
\mathcal{F}(q)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I},m}(q)
be the family of cuspidal automorphic representations generated by Hecke–Maaß newforms for
\Gamma_{0}(q)
whose Laplace eigenvalue lies in
\mathcal{I}
. Whenever we talk about the family
\mathcal{F}(q)
, all implicit and explicit constants may depend on
\mathcal{I}
and
m
, which we regard as fixed once and for all. Let

V(q):=[{\rm SL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma_{0}(q)]=q^{m-1}\prod_{p\mid q}\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p^{m-1}}\Big)\asymp\frac{q^{m}}{\varphi(q)},

where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function. By Weyl’s law, we expect this to be the order of magnitude of
|\mathcal{F}(q)|
.

Let
L(s,\pi)
be the standard
L
-function associated to
\pi
. Given
\sigma\geq 0
and
T\geq 1
, we define

N_{\pi}(\sigma,T)=|\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma\colon\beta\geq\sigma,~|\gamma|\leq T,~L(\rho,\pi)=0\}|,

where the zeros are counted with multiplicity. GRH predicts
N_{\pi}(\sigma,T)=0
for
\sigma>1/2
. As of now, it is not known whether there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that each
L(s,\pi)
for
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)
does not vanish in the region
\mathop{\textup{Re}}(s)\geq 1-\delta
. Our main result is a spectrally weighted zero density estimate which is strong in the
q
-aspect and at most polynomial in the
T
-aspect.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer,
T\geq 2
, and
\sigma\geq 1/2
. If
0<B<1
, then

\frac{1}{V(q)}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}\frac{N_{\pi}(\sigma,T)}{L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})}\ll_{B}T^{m}q^{-B(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})}F(q)(\log q)^{5}.

The function
F(q)
is defined in (
3.2
) below and satisfies
F(q)\ll 2^{\omega(q)}
, where
\omega(q)
is the number of distinct prime factors of
q
.

Remark
.

See (
3.3
) below for the maximal order of
F(q)
.

The weight
L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})^{-1}
is reminiscent of the Kuznetsov formula. The bound (
3.1
) below shows that on average
L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})^{-1}
is
\ll 1
. While it is a very hard problem (associated with Landau–Siegel zeros) to obtain individual lower bounds for
L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})
, relatively strong upper bounds for
L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})
are available. In particular, it is much easier to remove the weights than to insert them (should this be required for a certain application). It follows from work of Li
[
17

and Bushnell and Henniart
[
4

that there exists an effectively computable constant
>0
such that if

R_{\mathcal{F}}(q)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad}),

then

(1.1)

R_{\mathcal{F}}(q)\ll\exp\Big(\Cr{unweighted}\frac{\log q}{\log\log q}\Big).

If the generalized Ramanujan conjecture holds for all
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)
, we have the stronger bound
R_{\mathcal{F}}(q)\ll(\log q)^{m}
. With this in mind,
Theorem

1.1
immediately implies the following unweighted zero density estimate.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer,
T\geq 2
, and
\sigma\geq 1/2

0<B<1

\frac{1}{V(q)}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}N_{\pi}(\sigma,T)\ll_{B}T^{m}q^{-B(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})}R_{\mathcal{F}}(q)F(q)(\log q)^{5}.

In particular, if
\delta>0

\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)
with

exceptions, any nontrivial zero

As mentioned earlier, the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies among other things on
fairly general large sieve inequalities on

\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)
, let

denote the arithmetically normalized Fourier coefficient. In particular, for a prime

(1.2)

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers. For
\textbf{n}=(n_{m-1},\ldots,n_{1})\in\mathbb{N}^{m-1}

w(\textbf{n})=n_{1}n_{2}^{2}\ldots n_{m-1}^{m-1},

and we write

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

be an integer and
\beta\colon\mathbb{N}^{m-1}\to\mathbb{C}
a function. There exists a constant

such that if

\frac{1}{V(q)}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}\frac{1}{L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w(\textbf{n})\leq x\\

(\textbf{n},q)=1\end{subarray}}|\beta(\textbf{n})|^{2}.

The proof is an application of the
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(m)
Kuznetsov formula.
In Section

Let

where

1.2.
Applications

Our new zero density estimates are motivated by their arithmetic consequences, a few of which are described below. As we will soon see, the quality of these arithmetic consequences is highly sensitive to the specific shape of the zero density estimate; in particular, we cannot afford powers of
q^{\varepsilon}
in our density results.

1.2.1.
Order of vanishing at
s=1/2

It is generally believed that
L
-functions should not vanish at
s=\frac{1}{2}
unless
\pi
is self-dual and the sign of the functional equation is
-1
, or there is a special arithmetic reason, like the relationship with Hasse–Weil
L
-functions and ranks of elliptic curves predicted by the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. See
[
25
]
for higher rank examples.

If
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)
, then GRH implies that

(1.5)

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1/2}L(s,\pi)\ll\frac{\log q}{\log\log q}.

Using
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.3
, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

If
q\geq 3
is an integer, then

\frac{1}{V(q)}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1/2}L(s,\pi)}{L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})}\ll\log F(q)+\log\log q,\quad\frac{1}{V(q)}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1/2}L(s,\pi)\ll\frac{\log q}{\log\log q}.

Theorem

1.5
demonstrates that on average over
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)
, the GRH-conditional bound (
1.5
) is unconditionally true. When the weights
L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})^{-1}
are inserted, we obtain on average a bound much stronger than what GRH implies for an individual
L
-function. If the generalized Ramanujan conjecture were true, then our proof would produce matching weighted and unweighted bounds for the average order of vanishing.

It is worth noting that while we could state our zero density estimates in
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
coarsely as
\ll_{B,\varepsilon}q^{-B(\sigma-1/2)+\varepsilon}
, the corresponding upper bounds in
Theorem

1.5
would only assume the form
o(\log q)
. It is a nice coincidence that the size of Li’s bound (
1.1
) is no more than the exponential of the right hand side of (
1.5
), which we crucially exploit in the unweighted result to obtain a GRH-quality bound on average.

Theorem

1.5
is new for all
m\geq 2
. Kowalski and Michel
[
15
]
(see also
[
16
]
) proved a stronger result as
\pi
varies in the family of cuspidal automorphic representations generated by the holomorphic elliptic curve newforms of weight
2
and prime level
q
. In this family, the average order of vanishing at
s=\frac{1}{2}
is
O(1)
. Their proof relies on a version of
Theorem

1.1
for prime
q
where the power of
\log q
equals
1
. In light of the Riemann–von Mangoldt asymptotic for the count of zeros up to height
T
, this power of
\log q
, if attainable, is optimal.

A similar result with essentially the same proof holds for the spectral family
\mathcal{G}(Q)
featured in Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

If
Q\geq 3
, then

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}(Q)|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{G}(Q)}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1/2}L(s,\pi)}{L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})}\ll\log\log Q,\qquad\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}(Q)|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{G}(Q)}\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1/2}L(s,\pi)\ll\frac{\log Q}{\log\log Q}.

1.2.2.
Equidistribution of fractional imaginary parts of nontrivial zeros

Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
, and let
I\subseteq[0,1]
be a subinterval. Let
\{x\}
denote the fractional part of
x
.

For the Riemann zeta function, let
N(T)
be the count (including multiplicity) of nontrivial zeros
\beta+i\gamma
with
|\gamma|\leq T
. Refining preceding work of Rademacher
[
24
]
and Hlawka
[
9
]
, Fujii
[
8
]
proved that

(1.6)

\sup_{I\subseteq[0,1]}\Big|\frac{1}{N(T)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma|\leq T\\
\{\alpha\gamma\}\in I\end{subarray}}1-|I|\Big|\ll_{\alpha}\frac{\log\log T}{\log T}.

Thus, the fractional parts
\{\alpha\gamma\}
with
|\gamma|\leq T
are equidistributed in intervals larger than
(\log\log T)/\log T
. Ford and Zaharescu
[
6
]
proved that there exists an explicit function
g_{\alpha}(t)
such that if the indicator function of
I
is replaced with a twice continuously differentiable weight function
h
, then

(1.7)

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}h(\{\alpha\gamma\})=N(T)\int_{0}^{1}h(t)dt+T\int_{0}^{1}h(t)g_{\alpha}(t)dt+o_{\alpha,h}(T).

Ford, Soundararajan, and Zaharescu
[
5
]
related this phenomenon to Montgomery’s pair correlation function and the distribution of primes in short intervals.

This phenomenon conditionally extends to other
L
-functions. Let
\mathfrak{F}_{m}
be the set of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with unitary central character. It follows from the work of Ford, Soundararajan, and Zaharescu
[
5
]
that if there exist constants
A_{\pi}>0
and
B_{\pi}>0
such that the zero density estimate

(1.8)

N_{\pi}(\sigma,T)\ll_{\pi}T^{1-A_{\pi}(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})}(\log T)^{B_{\pi}},\qquad\textup{$T\geq 1$ and $\sigma\geq 0$}

holds, then analogues of (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) hold for the zeros of
L(s,\pi)
. So far, (
1.8
) is known only when
m=1

[
7
,
26
]
or
m=2

[
1
,
19
]
.

For
m\geq 3
, we use
Theorem

1.1
to prove that on average over
\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)
(weighted by
L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})^{-1}
), the fractional imaginary parts are equidistributed up to a height depending on
q
. To state our result, we write
N_{\pi}(T)=N_{\pi}(0,T)
. Recall
[
11
, Theorem 5.8]
that

(1.9)

N_{\pi}(T)=\frac{T}{\pi}\log\Big(q_{\pi}\Big(\frac{T}{2\pi e}\Big)^{m}\Big)+O(\log(C(\pi)T)),

where
q_{\pi}
(resp.
C(\pi)
) is the arithmetic conductor (resp. analytic conductor) of
\pi
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
q
be a large prime and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
. If
r>2
,
T=(\log q)^{r}
, and
I
denotes a subinterval of
[0,1]
, then

\sup_{I\subseteq[0,1]}\Big|\frac{1}{V(q)}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}\frac{1}{L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})}\Big(\frac{1}{N_{\pi}(T)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma|\leq T\\
\{\alpha\gamma\}\in I\end{subarray}}1-|I|\Big)\Big|\ll_{\alpha,r}\frac{\log\log\log q}{\log\log q}.

Because
T
must be so small compared to
q
, our proof cannot recover any secondary main terms in (
1.7
). Unlike
Theorem

1.5
, our proof of
Theorem

1.7
is so sensitive to the factor of
q^{-B(\sigma-1/2)}
in our zero density estimates that we cannot afford to have any factor larger than a power of
\log q
(hence our constraint on
T
in
Theorem

1.7
). In particular, we cannot afford a factor as large as (

), and we must choose

so that

is small (e.g.,
q
a prime).

A similar result with essentially the same proof holds for the spectral family
\mathcal{G}(Q)
featured in Theorem

.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
Q
be large and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
. If
r>2
,
T=(\log Q)^{r}
, and
I
denotes a subinterval of
[0,1]
, then

\sup_{I\subseteq[0,1]}\Big|\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}(Q)|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{G}(Q)}\frac{1}{L(1,\pi,\mathrm{Ad})}\Big(\frac{1}{N_{\pi}(T)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma|\leq T\\

2.
Properties of
L
-functions

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation for
{\rm PGL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by a cuspidal Hecke-Maaß newform for the subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(q)\subseteq{\rm SL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
. By
[
13
]
, the arithmetic conductor of
\pi
is
q
. Its local
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{p})
is defined in terms of the Satake parameters
\alpha_{1,\pi}(p),\ldots,\alpha_{m,\pi}(p)\in\mathbb{C}
by

L(s,\pi_{p})=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)p^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(p^{k})}{p^{ks}}.

Since our representations have trivial central character, we have

(2.1)

\prod_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)=1

for
p\nmid q
. The standard
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
associated to
\pi
is then

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{p}L(s,\pi_{p})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}{n^{s}}.

The Euler product and Dirichlet series converge absolutely when
\mathop{\textup{Re}}(s)>1
. For
(n,q)=1
, the number
\lambda_{\pi}(n)\in\mathbb{C}
is the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue of
\pi
and equals the normalized Fourier coefficient
B_{\pi}(1,\ldots,1,n)
. We recall the Schur polynomials given in (
1.2
) for general Fourier coefficients.

At the archimedean place, there are
m
Langlands parameters
\kappa_{\pi}(1),\ldots,\kappa_{\pi}(m)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

L(s,\pi_{\infty})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+\kappa_{\pi}(j)),\qquad\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}\Big).

By combining the work in
[
18
]
for unramified primes and
[
21
]
for ramified primes (cf. also
[
3
]
), we know that there exists

(2.2)

0\leq\theta_{m}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{m^{2}+1}

such that

(2.3)

|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|\leq p^{\theta_{m}}\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad\mathop{\textup{Re}}(\kappa_{\pi}(j))\geq-\theta_{m}.

It follows that if
\tau_{m}(n)
is the
n
-th Dirichlet coefficient of
\zeta(s)^{m}
, then

(2.4)

|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|\leq\tau_{m}(n)n^{\theta_{m}}.

The generalized Ramanujan conjecture asserts that in (
2.2
), one may take
\theta_{m}=0
.

Let
\widetilde{\pi}
be contragredient to
\pi
. The completed
L
-function

(2.5)

\Lambda(s,\pi)=q^{s/2}L(s,\pi)L(s,\pi_{\infty})

is entire of order 1, and there exists a complex number
W(\pi)
of modulus 1 such that for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we have the functional equation

(2.6)

\Lambda(s,\pi)=W(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}).

The analytic conductor of
\pi

[
12
]
is given by

(2.7)

C(t,\pi)=q\prod_{j=1}^{n}(3+|it+\kappa_{\pi}(j)|),\qquad C(\pi)=C(0,\pi).

We recall the convexity bound

(2.8)

L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi)\ll q^{1/4}(|t|+1)^{m/4}

which follows for instance from
[
27
, Corollary 2.7]
.

We define the numbers
\mu_{\pi}(n)
by the (absolutely convergent) Dirichlet series identity

\frac{1}{L(s,\pi)}=\prod_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)p^{-s})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\mathop{\textup{Re}}(s)>1.

Using (
2.3
), we find that

(2.9)

|\mu_{\pi}(n)|\leq\tau_{m}(n)n^{\theta_{m}}.

At a prime
p\nmid q
, we can use (
1.2
) to express the local Euler factor in terms of Fourier coefficients as

\frac{1}{L(s,\pi_{p})}=1-\frac{B_{\pi}(1,\ldots,1,p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{B_{\pi}(1,\ldots,1,p,1)}{p^{2s}}-\cdots+(-1)^{m-1}\frac{B_{\pi}(p,1,\ldots,1)}{p^{(m-1)s}}+\frac{(-1)^{m}}{p^{ms}},

so that

(2.10)

\sum_{(n,q)=1}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(n)}{n^{s}}=\sum_{(n_{1}\ldots n_{m},q)=1}\frac{\mu^{2}(n_{1}\cdots n_{m})\mu(n_{1}n_{3}\cdots)B_{\pi}(n_{m-1},\ldots,n_{1})}{(n_{1}n_{2}^{2}\dots n_{m}^{m})^{s}}.

For an integer
n\geq 1
, we define

(2.11)

a_{\pi}(n)=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)^{k}&\mbox{if $n=p^{k}$ for some prime $p$ and some integer $k\geq 1$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

and the von Mangoldt function

\Lambda(n)=\begin{cases}\log p&\mbox{if $n=p^{k}$ for some prime $p$ and some integer $k\geq 1$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise. }\end{cases}

In the region
\mathop{\textup{Re}}(s)>1
, we have the (absolutely convergent) Dirichlet series identity

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi}(n)\Lambda(n)}{n^{s}}.

Using (
2.3
), we find that

(2.12)

|a_{\pi}(p^{k})|\leq mp^{k\theta_{m}}.

As an application of the Murnaghan–Nakayama rule, we can express
a_{\pi}(p^{k})
in terms of Schur polynomials as follows: for

We also need a large sieve inequality for the numbers

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer and

Remark

The right hand side looks more complicated than one would usually expect from a large sieve inequality. The reason is simple. An inspection of (

In view of (

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{V(q)}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}(q)}\frac{1}{L(1,\pi,\text{Ad})}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\

&\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu,\widetilde{\nu}\leq x^{1/m}\\

Comprehensive scientific insights on plant inspired nano therapeutics as the missing piece of the breast cancer puzzle
Authors: Joyeeta Bhattacharya, Qazi Saifullah, Koustav Dutta, Subhasis Chakrabarty, Asim Halder, Suvadra Das, Ritu Khanra, Kaushik Biswas, Nagaraja Sreeharsha, Partha Roy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreast cancer is one of the major health concern and the second leading cause of death among women globally. The survival rates in breast cancer depends on the stages (Stage I–Stage IV), there by the early diagnosis and followed by surgery and chemotherapy is highly recommended. Conventional treatments, such as chemotherapy, surgery often have limited efficacy and are associated with severe side effects in breast cancers. Thereby, biosafe materials with high potency is in high demand. Phytochemical loaded nano materials are bio compatible, bio safe as a result, that can be explored in breast cancer therapy with least toxicity effect to other healthy tissues.ObjectivesExploring the potentiality of targeted drug delivery approaches to mitigate breast cancer, focusing on plant-based bioactive molecules (phytochemicals) and their coupling with nano carriers to overcome the different limitations of traditional therapies.MethodsThe utilization of phytochemicals in breast cancer management, known for their safety and therapeutic efficacy, is discussed as an alternative approach in this review. Challenges such as poor bioavailability, short half-life, and lack of site specificity, which limit their clinical application, are addressed in different sections. Strategies for mitigating these drawbacks include conjugating phytochemicals with nanocarriers such as liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, metallic nanoparticles, and carbon dots have also been described in this review.ResultsNanocarriers enhance the stability, systemic bioavailability, and site-specific delivery of phytochemicals, enabling them to cross biological barriers effectively while reducing normal cell toxicity. These systems provide a “green corridor” to target breast cancer cells with improved therapeutic efficacy.ConclusionsOngoing research and clinical trials highlight the promise of phytochemicals conjugated with nanocarriers in breast cancer therapy. This innovative therapeutic approach has the potential to revolutionize breast cancer management. Further research should focus on advancing the development and clinical application of phytochemicals conjugated with nanocarriers to ensure their widespread adoption in breast cancer therapy.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-026-04621-7.Keywords: Breast cancer, Chemotherapies, Phytochemicals, Bioavailability, Toxicity, Nano carriers

Introduction

World health organization ranks cancer as one of the deadliest diseases and causes huge mortality among different global region an approximate 9.7 million deaths in 2018. It has remarkably increased in 2020 with an approximate 10 million deaths was recorded globally. Some cancers are portrayed such as lung, colorectal, liver, prostate, which are quite common and mostly affected the male populations. Among the diverse cancer variants, breast cancer is a silent killer which mostly affects the female populations globally. A wide range of female patients across the globe were affected by breast cancer in different stages (Stage I–Stage IV). An approximate 670 000 deaths was recorded in 2022 as per the notification of World Health Organization due to the breast cancer. As per the data published by the National Cancer Institute’s (NCI) Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) program, the estimated number of new breast cancer cases in the United States for 2024 is 313,510, with 2790 cases in males and 310,720 cases in females. The estimated number of breast cancer-related deaths is 42,780, comprising 530 deaths in males and 42,250 deaths in females [1]. According to the Global cancer statistics 2020, approximately 2.3 million women were newly diagnosed with breast cancer. The number is increasing day by day and the regular diagnosis of breast cancer is very common in different diagnostic centre across the globe, and eventually it is also highlighted the fact that almost 670,000 women were died with breast cancer as reported by World health Organization in 2022 [1, 2]. It is also notified with some peer reviewed journals that female breast cancer is now one of the most diagnosed cancers among the female population besides colon cancers, lung cancers [2]. The graphical representation of the data mentioned has been highlighted in Fig. 1A and B [3] .

The main reasons of high number of deaths due to breast cancer is the consequence of its property to metastasize to distant organs such as liver, brain, bones etc. [4]. An early detection of the disease can however pave the way for a better survival rate. Several studies have indicated about the age-related risk associated with breast cancer, where most breast cancer are diagnosed in women who are 40–50 years or above in age [5]. Apart from these inevitable risk factors of gender and age, another crucial risk which is beyond the individual control is the genetical predisposition. Nearly, 10% of the breast cancer is due to family history [6]. A prominent risk of early onset of breast cancer (before the age of 35) has been reported in individuals who have a first degree relative with a breast cancer [7]. The underlying pathogenesis behind this hereditary breast cancer is attributed to the mutation of the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes. Mutation of genes like PTEN, CHEK2, TP53, ATM, STK11 and PALB2 can also lead to inherited breast cancer however the risk is quite low [8] The anatomy of adult breast section with and without tumour is illustrated in Fig. 2A and B.

Conventional classification of breast cancer (as illustrated in Fig. 3) is mainly based on the location of the tumor growth and hallmarks of cancer associated with its pathogenesis, which may not capture the clinical course of the disease. Thus, taking into consideration about the recent trends of breast cancer classification, breast cancer has been classified based on the overexpression of receptors on the cancer cell membrane. These includes, progesterone (PR), estrogen (ER) and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER-2). Breast cancer with overexpression these receptors are termed as of PR-, ER- and HER 2–positive, depending on the type of receptor overexpression. Around 20% of these breast cancer lacks all the three hormone receptors this type of breast cancer is known as triple negative breast cancer [9].

Classification of breast cancer

As discussed, an early diagnosis of the disease followed by appropriate treatment with either surgery or chemotherapy or both, is fundamental for a better survival rate and improved prognosis. Surgery includes mastectomy (removal of whole breast) and lumpectomy (removing of the tumor with the surrounding tissues). Chemotherapies, on the other hand are quite successful in arresting the multiplication of the cancer cells but its downsides like unspecific toxicity, low therapeutic indices, development of multiple drug resistance, undesirable side-effects cannot be disregarded [10]. Therefore, development of a unique method to manage breast cancer is the need of the hour.

The current era is witnessing a rising trend of plant-bioactive based traditional medicines, over the conventional synthetic drugs. These plant derived bioactives enjoy better patient compliance due to its easy availability, lower residual toxicity and its capability to synergise with chemotherapy and radiotherapy [11]. These time-tested active moieties or phytochemicals are predominantly secondary plant metabolites which comprises of vivid range of chemical moieties like alkaloids, phytosterols, saponins, phenolic acids etc. The phytochemicals serve the purpose of treating various immunological, metabolic as well as neurological disorder either in their native form or through their metabolites [12]. The literature survey has also highlighted its better patient compliance due to its easy availability, lower residual toxicity and its capability to synergise with chemotherapy and radiotherapy [13]. Although, there are widespread therapeutics applications of these biomolecules, yet their biopharmaceutical properties present a major hurdle in their transportation through different layers of human body such as gastrointestinal tract, endothelial lining of the blood vessels and also blood-brain-barrier [14]. Additionally, the pharmacokinetic properties of these phytochemicals are also largely dependent on external environmental factors as well as the nature food intake. The inherent biopharmaceutical problems of these phytochemicals hinder their clinical acceptance even though they mitigate several diseases including various cancers. Extensive researches are now focused on designing and optimizing novel drug carrier system with the aim of enhancing their therapeutic relevance with minimum toxicity [15].

Nanotechnology based drug delivery system are currently attracting the scientific domain worldwide. Phytochemical conjugated nanosize materials can be delivered to the targeted area of human body through the application of different nanoformulations such as liposomes, Carbon dots, polymeric & metallic NPs. It seems to formulate and improvise bio-safe nanomaterials. Various peer reviewed journal reports portray that the phytochemical conjugated/ loaded to nanocarriers have tremendous efficacy in managing large variety of diseases, including breast cancer. The targeted approach of nanoparticles (NPs) loaded with phytochemicals protect the active agent until it is released to the targeted site successfully. Phytochemical mediated nano formulations exhibit programmed cell death through Bcl-2/caspase pathways. Being tiny in size range phytochemical mediated nano formulations can penetrate well through the rapturing of tumour cell membrane and inhibit the partial growth of the malignant cell, lastly it can prohibit the formation of the newer blood vessel (angiogenesis mechanism) completely in the tumour. Ultimately at the final stage that can prohibit the growth of the malignant tumour permanently. In other occasion different plant bio active mediated nano formulations can inhibit the growth of malignant tumour through blocking the proliferation by targeting pathways such as PI3K/AKT/MAPK [15, 16]. The phytochemical based nano formulations are excellent in biomedical applications, in view of their superior phytochemical activities. Due to these properties and tiny size it can penetrate the cell membrane of affected tumour and other cellular parts of human body. But keeping the track on those properties, phytochemical based nano formulations exhibit toxic effect into other non-affected organ, that may explore hindrance in regulatory aspects. As a result, an urgent requirement is necessitating to fulfil the regulatory [17] criteria of phytochemical based nano formulations in breast cancers therapy. Which can further, portray the phyto based nano formulations towards its clinical aspects and commercialization process of the same in global market. Furthermore, the theranostic profile of phyto based NPs express a promising approach towards the management of breast cancer [16, 17]. For instance the nanoemulsion formulation of white mulberry leaf (WML-NE) expressed enhanced cytotoxic effect in comparison to fruit based nanoemulsions (WM-NE) against human breast cancer MCF-7 cells [18]. Another example of phytochemical- based nanoformulation is achieved through the fabrication of metal based bnanoparticle using plant extract. Nosrati and team synthesized silver nanoparticle using Anzaroot extract (Anz-AgNP) which showcased anti-proliferative effect against MCF-7 breast cancer cell lines [19]. Apart from these, Poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoformulations of Fagonia indica (FI) extract have also served potent cytotoxic effect against BT- 474 breast cancer cell lines [20]. Therefore, this review is focused on providing a comprehensive update on the present status of plant bioactive nanoformulations explored in breast cancer along with their newer design strategies and future roadmap.

Plant bioactive and their biopharmaceutical drawbacks

In the current situation, phytochemicals have a higher level of patient compliance due to their ease of use, lesser residual toxicity, and ability to synergize with chemotherapy and radiotherapy [21]. Numerous Phytochemicals such as, Curcumin, Silymarin, Quercetin, Luteolin, Paclitaxel (Taxol®), Resveratrol, have been demonstrated to have chemo preventive and chemotherapeutic effects not just in cell lines and animal models of breast cancer but also in human subjects in phase I and II clinical trial. Along with anti-cancer activity most of them have anti-inflammatory, anti-microbial, anti-hypertensive, and other therapeutic activities. However, even though this plant bioactive mitigate a variety of cancer types, fundamental biopharmaceutical issues like poor bioavailability, low solubility, hydrophobicity, and obscure target specificity limit their clinical acceptability, for example, Curcumin a component of the turmeric spice has various therapeutic properties but depict poor bioavailability and solubility [19, 20]. Similarly, Quercetin, another popular phytochemical though shows broad spectrum of pharmacological activities in various dimensions but its low aqueous solubility, high metabolic rate, rapid clearance from the body, inactive metabolic output and low intrinsic activity limits its clinical acceptability [21, 22]. To address these problems, structuring nanotechnology for the phytochemicals turned out to be the most appropriate strategy to the researchers. The next section gives a vivid idea about different novel nano delivery devices which are used to deliver phytochemicals.

Nanotechnological intervention on phytochemicals: impact and biopharmaceutical characteristics

The pharmacology and phytochemistry behind various bioactive compounds obtained from vegetables, fruits, grains, resins, and seeds have reported evidences in reducing the incidences of major diseases. Various phytochemicals are able to develop a large portion of potential therapeutic agents used to combat against cancer especially breast cancer [78]. The abundance and easy-availability of phytochemicals with diverse therapeutic attributes along with their cost-effective nature makes them more applicable towards medical sectors. Additionally, phytochemicals are quite safe for human consumption which adds to their valuability [79]. The safety and efficacy of the phytochemicals or bioactive compounds depends predominantly on its delivery to the specific target site. Various potent phytochemicals are available globally, such as flavonoids, terpenoids, phenol compounds, alkaloids, anthocyanidins, isoflavones etc. [80]. But still there is some common problem associated with different plant mediated products such as various bio pharmaceutical issue. This issues lead with the absorption profile, solubility problem as a result the single phytochemical mediated conventional delivery did not get success specifically in breast cancer therapy [81]. There by, targeted drug delivery with the advance biopharmaceutical profile is much needed to deliver this phytochemical towards the targeted area. Nanocarriers are one of them, which can encapsulate the active moiety and deliver it with better advanced biopharmaceutical profile [82]. In modern prospect, the bio safe nanomaterials are in high demand. So that, the synthesis of phytochemical based nanocarriers is one of the current trend in the emerging nanomedicine innovations. Prepared particles or the nanocarriers are highly capable to target the active site to deliver the phytochemicals in suitable manner.

Liposome

The established scientific reports were explained through some peer reviewed reputed journal that the phytochemicals from different plant sources have found inferior solubility and permeability. Additionally, the conventional delivery of phytochemicals is also associated with various systematic side effects [83]. It’s also noted that the phytochemicals from various plant sources are quite costly with small yield value. Taxol is a common phytochemical based nano formulations to mitigate the breast cancer, despite of its huge potency and application a comparatively low yielding value & high cost may restricts its exploration in breast cancer management [84, 85]. The research on liposomal drug delivery is gaining more attention presently due to the small particle size, larger surface area and excellent aspect ratio [86]. These properties may cause the polymeric encapsulation of the plant bio actives and that can disrupt the inside core at the targeted site successfully. The small size of liposome (< 500 nm) is really excellent to penetrate through numerous barriers of our body easily such as, blood brain barriers, epithelial membrane, mucosal layers etc. for transfection application [87]. This permeation process through different biological barriers of human body through liposome mediated phytochemical delivery especially while permeating through blood brain barrier is quite tough without the conjugation of any ligand. Presently various ligands are utilized like insulin, transferrin which can be conjugated with liposome mediated phytochemicals to penetrate different body barriers. This ligand mediated liposomal drug delivery is quite advantageous because the targeting efficiency is quite better than the normal penetration effects [87, 88]. The current scenarios are witnessed where different chemotherapeutical agents are administered in breast cancer, but it has several drawbacks such as the narrow therapeutic window, severe adverse effect, lesser targeted efficiency and drug resistance. So, an alternative approach should be taken to target the malignant tumour associated in breast cancer. It has been reported already that phytochemicals are therapeutically active to mitigate different cancer along with breast cancer with least toxicity. Various classes of plant materials are available such as polyphenol, terpenoids, alkaloids etc. [88]. These classes of the plant bio actives can be carried through different nanocarriers and targeted to the affected area successfully. But conventional drug delivery of those bio actives are not stable and effective due to the bioavailability issue, thereby, the release kinetics and the dosing frequency may be affected as reported [89]. Liposomes are one of the most affordable nano carriers to carry the phytochemicals precisely with high potency in to the targeted area. It is a spherical tiny sac of phospholipid, with a suitable diameter of 1–500 nm [90]. It is made up of one or more phospholipid bilayer having the property to encapsulate the fraction of phytochemicals inside the core in which the solvent can easily float or diffuse. Exclusively, liposome can encapsulate the hydrophilic drugs in their internal aqueous core and these drugs can be embedded at the outer phospholipid bilayer. By simply changing the bilayer composition of liposome, the in vivo biocompatibility and pharmacokinetics of the plant constitutes can be improved [91, 92]. Thereby, the phytochemicals can be targeted with liposome in to the targeted area precisely. This variant of the drug delivery system can progress the therapeutic activity and safety profile of the drug by releasing the active plant constituents for prolonged time periods in to the targeted zone [93]. At present, huge numbers of bioactive conjugated liposomal formulations are either in clinical trial stages or approved by FDA to mitigate different types of breast cancers [20]. Different formulation scientists are working with liposomal delivery of the phytochemical based materials for breast cancer drug delivery. Aroonsri et al.; observed that intranasal Quercetin liposome formulation was prepared by reverse evaporation technique with 60% entrapment effectiveness could produce anticancer and cell reinforcement action [19]. He et al. reported about Ampelopsin liposome which was prepared by film-ultrasound procedure with 62.30% proficiency showed prominent action against malignant growth [94]. A plant based isoflavone named Apigenin developed by Banerjee et al. was delivered through liposomal formulation for enhancing its anti-bacterial therapeutic activity [95]. Researchers are paying immense attention in improving the bioavailability of Resveratrol to increase its potential in breast cancer management. There are different studies which have established that coated nanoliposome have extended the release of the drug in-vitro. Resveratrol loaded nano-lipo formulations are not only applied to treat cancer but also applicable for diabetes as well as many skins related disorders [96]. A bioactive compound named Betanin was proven to have anti-oxidant and anti-diabetic property but it’s clinical incompetency was due to its poor oral absorption and instability in different mode. Overcoming these shortcomings, nano liposomal formulation was designed to show a sustained release effect in gastric and intestinal fluid. Damages of specific tissues in kidney, liver and pancreas also remarkably decrease after the introduction of betanin- loaded non-lipo formulation treated with diabetes-induced rats [97]. Some novel molecule, which was introduced by Food and Drug Administration (FDA), are presently being used in clinical application. An example of such novel molecule is Marqibo, formulation of vincristine and combine with sphingomyelin: cholesterol, utilized for leukaemia, which has longer dissemination and shows more action than vincristine [98].

Nanoparticle(NP)

NPs are so small and made from various nano crystallize grain with a size range of 1–100 nm [99]. Nano scale products have better aspect ratio and larger surface area than the other conventional product. As a result, NPs are applied in interdisciplinary area of medical profession [100]. Their theranostic profile is also very strong. The multifunctional NPs are structurally able to target the affected area perfectly and potent enough to mitigate different diseases. One of them is cancer [101]. Presently, phytochemical associated NPs are one of the potential candidates for cancer therapy. These are biologically safe and least toxic as compare to other chemotherapeutic agents [102]. Phytochemicals are found from the natural sources such as fruits, vegetables, seeds, grains etc. In addition, different groups of phytochemicals are available like, alkaloids, phenolic, carotenoids etc. Several potential research was performed to validate the anticancer properties of the phytochemicals along with different other drugs in nano form [103]. These experiments are already approved through different pre-clinical or clinical trial. Phytochemical conjugated NPs are enabled with tremendous target efficiency as because of their small particle size & better morphological characters [104]. It can also improve the biopharmaceutical properties of different plant mediated molecules and enhance their therapeutic effectivity. As a result, the tumour specific activity can also be increased. For cancer therapy the multifunctional NPs contains a strong structural unit & other transporting material, which supposed to be a functional element to target the tumour [105]. This fabrication process of multifunctional NPs makes it more beneficial to mitigate the cancer with least toxicity.

In modern aspect the nano dimensional phytochemicals are getting more spotlight because of less dosing frequency, upgradation in solubility and furthermore to modify the absorbance contrast with the active moiety, enhancing in bioavailability and lesser toxicity as compared to chemotherapies [106]. The optimized NPs for clinical use usually range between of 1 and 100 nm [107]. The present methods are synthesized NPs chemically either by the Top Down or Bottom-up approach, to target different malignant tumour, these are strongly therapeutically active but it produces unwanted effect to the normal cells as a result, the normal tissues and cells may be affected [108]. The green synthesis approach is comparatively new, where the phytochemicals conjugated, NPs are quite enabled to mitigate cancer because of its excellent target efficiency. The green approach is biologically safe and decrease the toxicity as well as decrease the environmental hazards. Green approach for synthesis of NP with phytochemicals are highly beneficial when compared with physical and chemical method of NP synthesis [109]. Green origins contain polyphenols, terpenoids and other proteinaceous substances which can replace lots of chemical reagents that act as reducing agents to reduce metal ions into a lower valency state. In suitable circumstances and certain conditions, multifunctional NPs conjugated with plant extracts can even surpass the chemical method of synthesis [110]. The leaf extract of Elaiseguineensis which is also known as palm oil can be applied to reduce Au3+at different concentrations and thus, can be used for the synthesis of gold NP [111]. Plant waste can also be used as reductant in several situations. The silver NP that Khatami et al. [112] has synthesized by using hay waste as reducing agent has been reported to depict several therapeutic properties like anti-microbial and anti-cancer activity [113]. Langure et al. 2016 has synthesized mesoporous silica NP to load poorly water soluble and low BBB permeable phytochemicals like curcumin and chrysin so that the formulation can attain a Nose-to brain delivery via olfactory route [112]. As reported by Fuku et al. [114] and Kora and Rastogi. [115], the extract of ginger (Zingiber officinale) root as well as pomegranate (Punica granatum L) peels are used as capping and reducing agent for the synthesis of silver NP [114, 116]. Nagajyothi et al. [117] has synthesized CuO NPs from Black soya bean. The synthesized NP has been reported to disrupt Mitochondrial membrane in Hela cells. In another peer reviewed research work, the Fe NP synthesized from the aqueous extract of Psoralea corylifolia seeds have also shown apoptosis on cancer cells [115]. Development of curcumin encapsulated polymeric NP have been synthesized by Bisht et al.. which has proved to enhance the poor bioavailability and cellular uptake by pancreatic cell lines [117]. Andrographolide encapsulated in solid lipid NP has also depicted expansion in tissue distribution that can even reach and cross the Blood-brain barrier [118]. Siddiqui et al. [119] had prepared PEGylated PLA NP loaded with epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) whose therapeutic impact was evaluated on human melanoma cells [120]. Phytochemical conjugated nanoscale products display a low cost, easily fabrication system to prepare the NPs with least toxicity. In some peer reviewed research, it was reported that the gold NPs along with Resveratrol have tremendous efficiency in breast cancer and prostate cancer [121]. 3-fold Resveratrol—conjugated gold NPs are highly effective in breast cancer and it displays an excellent cell apoptosis, and enhance the biocompatibility and bio availability as compared with normal low concentration of 1-fold Resveratrol—gold NP [119]. As a result, the Resveratrol added NPs are more therapeutically active and target the breast cancer cell accurately. The Resveratrol- gold NPs also proves that it can restrict the maturity of pancreatic cancer cell [122]. In another aspect a peer reviewed research work claimed that gold NP combined with Silibinin can occur the cell apoptosis of A549 lung cancer cell in vitro. The analysis displays that Silibinin induced gold NP is more active against the A549 lung cancer cell as compared to the other chemotherapeutic agents [123]. Enhanced Permeability and Retention of the nano materials are also one of the most important part where the nano materials can be accumulated in to the breast cancer site. It happens due to the poor membrane quality of the tumour surface and leaky blood vessels which can allow the phyto chemical based nano particle in to the tumour. But these phyto based nano materials some time fail in clinical research as because of the nature of the tumour tissues. The vascular system of tumour tissues are heterogeneous as a result the clinical significance may vary [123, 124].

Microsphere

The microspheres are composed of biodegradable polymers which are spherical in shape and free-flowing in nature. The administration of drug via microparticulate system escalates the bioavailability of drugs and additionally it limits their side effects. It can also be ingested or infused in the body. At the point when the herbal bioactive are epitomized into microspheres, the result produces extensive solubility with better bioavailability profiles. So far, diverse phytochemicals for example, curcumin, quercetin, silibinin, gingko, tetrandrine extract have been microencapsulated inside a polymeric material for different pharmacological efficacies. Ramadan et al. [124] has developed silymarin based microspheres with a blend of polymers like HPMC and EC for the treatment of hepatic disorders. The prepared formulation was successful in overcoming the drawback of short half-life and intestinal degradation suffered by silymarin. The optimized formulation indicated enhanced effective management of hepatic diseases [126]. Andrographolide encapsulated PLGA microsphere was prepared by Jiang et al. [125] by emulsion solvent evaporation method. This formulation gave a sustained release effect of Andrographolide so as to enhance its potential therapeutic utility for cancer chemotherapy. According to the reported data, the plasma concentration and half -life of the pure andrographolide is enhanced by incorporating it into a microsphere [127]. This above stated formulation not only has therapeutic activity against cancer but is also expected to show potential utility against diabetes, hypertension as well as dysentery [128].

Nano-emulsion

The emulsion is a non-homogeneous dispersion system made out of two immiscible liquids. One of them is dispersed as droplets called dispersed phase into the continuous phase. The droplet size in nanoemulsion is in the range of 1–100 nm while those of micro-emulsions are in the range of > 900 nm [129]. In other words, nanoemulsions are formulations which contains nanosized droplets dispersed in the dispersion medium. This property of nanoemulsion makes it exclusively different from conventional emulsions which make them suitable for encapsulation of bioactive ingredients [130]. Whereas, the conventional emulsion therapy is not capable to target the tumour site successfully, due to its bigger size of particle and less penetration capability through different barriers of human body [131]. The nanoemulsion is quite capable to target the tumour cell with the encapsulated bio molecules. The plant extract can be incorporated and fabricated as a nanoemulsion system to deliver the actual extract in to the tumour. This can lead the increase of the drug load and lesser the toxicity as compared to other chemotherapeutic agents [132]. It is also observed from different peer reviewed journals that the plant mediated nanoemulsion can also increase the formulation stability of hydrolysed materials and enhance the ability to target through different barriers [133]. Encapsulation of nanoemulsion with different plant extract can enhance their bio pharmaceutical properties. As a result, the bio availability should be increased [134]. A peer reviewed scientific report explains that, the curcumin loaded metallic NPs are quite capable to target the cancer cell comfortably. The nanoemulsion of curcumin has shown great potency against breast cancer in different pre-clinical or clinical trial stages [135]. The spherical shaped polymeric NP (Stearic acid and lecithin) with a suitable particle size 10–60 nm has an excellent anticancer activity specially in breast cancer treatment [136]. Polymeric micelle conjugated with curcumin with tiny particle size 30–40 nm is more effective than the normal prescribed curcumin in cancer therapy. This composite is applied mostly in lung, colorectal & breast cancer and got some excellent feedback from both in vitro and in vivo analysis [137]. A recent observation had discussed that the PLGA- based curcumin added nanoemulsion is produced with a particle size range 10–50 nm through the nanoemulsion technique and it has shown a better activity against breast cancer than the chemotherapeutic agents [138]. Some of the other peer reviewed journal also reported that the solvent evaporation technique to produce the curcumin nanoemulsion (100–200 nm) for breast cancer therapy [139]. In a clinical trial report, the study of curcumin nanoemulsion was displayed but unfortunately it was withdrawn due to some technical issue but it was continued from 2019 to 2022 up to phase III trial [140]. Deshmukh et al. reported through a scientific report that chitosan and lecithin based nanoemulsion scaffolding with chrysin (known as 5,7-dihydroxyflavone), a flavonoid found from honey & that chrysin was scaffolded with a polymeric liposome. That has tremendous potency against the cancer especially in the MCF-7 cell line [141]. After encapsulation of chrysin with liposome the bioavailability of the same molecule can be increased around five-fold as compared to the non-liposomal delivery [142]. Mycophenolic acid conjugated quercetin liposome was fabricated by a group of scientists and applied against breast cancer in vitro and in vivo cell line. The result was found suitable [143]. In another peer reviewed research work the thin-layer hydration technique was introduced to fabricate the thermosensitive betulinic acid—added liposomes to supress the breast cancer tumour [144]. In another study the resveratrol loaded liposomes with a thin coating of peptide and sucrose are quite impressive against the breast cancer. The in vivo data was evaluated and it displays more efficacy than the normal approach [145]. The Docetaxel submicron emulsion and berberine nano-emulsion for anticancer therapy were formulated to enhance the residence time of the drug which was administered by intravenous (I.V) and oral route [146].

Carbon dot

Carbon dot is derived from natural sources have gained a lot of attraction in the present research scenario. Moreover, the ease of their simple one-step synthesis has made them even more popular. In addition, versatility of these nano dot including properties like bio imaging, fluorescence activity, ability to carry drug molecules to the target site has made it an even more attractive point of attention [147]. Mishra et al. in their recent studies, have prepared a carbon dot displaying a dual properties of bioimaging as well as nanocarrier for targeting breast cancer. The green synthesis of carbon dots were made from curry leaves and were co-loaded with curcumin and quarcetin. The prepared carbon dots exhibited synergistic effect on MCF-& breast cancer cell lines [148]. Carbon dot is very small in size (1–3 nm) and it has definitely an excellent size distribution profile, the biocompatibility and inherent photoluminescence properties make it more bio stable and biologically safe rather than the other nano carrier systems. It is also studied from some peer- reviewed journals that carbon dots have an excellent renal clearance profile. That actually helps to reduce the systemic toxicity and reduce the long term accumulation of carbon dots in human body there by, the in vivo toxicity can be reduced remarkably [144]. Xie et al. has synthesized carbon dot from date pits [148]. The date pits were used an effective carbon source. It was also analysed that the prepared carbon dot has shown inhibition of cancer cell growth. The formulation was reported to inhibit the growth of lung cancer, breast cancer as well as prostate cancer [149]. Zhao et al. has synthesized fluorescent carbon dot using garlic as a carbon source which in turn was reported to show activity against free radical scavenging and cellular bio imaging [150]. Yuan el al; has done green hydro thermolysis of milk to synthesize carbon dots. Yuan et al. has also complexed Doxorubicin with the carbon dot. The doxorubicin complexed green synthesized carbon dot was reported to display pH- dependent release of Doxorubicin for breast cancer management [151]. Pal et al. [137] has reported that carbon dot synthesized curcumin has shown inhibitory activity against microbial growth as well as cancer cell growth [152].

Plant bioactive nano formulations: role in breast cancer management

The progression of breast cancer is highly dependent on various signalling pathways. One of the primary pathway is Wnt/β-catenin signalling pathway, which promoted cellular proliferation and differentiation. Additionally, the the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is also associated with cellular proliferation and metastatis. Apart from these signalling pathways, numerous oncogenes are activated during the breast cancer, which enhances the breast cancer metastasis. One such oncogene is FoxM1, which is a primary promoter of tumorogenesis through the PDGF/AKT signalling pathway. In normal condition, the TP53 gene is responsible for arresting the abnormal cell cycle, and stimulating the cellular apoptosis. In case of TNBC, the mutation of TP53 gene occurs, leading to genomic instability, abnormal cellular proloiferation and metastasis. Likewise, Bcl-2 (B-cell lymphoma 2) acts as an anti-apoptotic factor in preventing the programmed cell death. The overexpression of Bcl-2 in found in estrogen receptor (ER) positive breast cancer. Phytochemicals exceed the conventional medicaments in downregulation of these signalling pathways, thereby inhibiting the breast cancer progressions. Modifying these phytochemicals into specialised nano formulations not only target the specific cancer cells but also inhibit multiple pathways of cancer development promoting synergistic actions [153].

Curcumin

Curcumin is an immensely investigated polyphenol with over 9000 publications and 500 patents. Apart from the principal polyphenol curcumin, the other polyphenols include demethoxy curcumin, bis-demethoxy curcumin and they express multifarious pharmacological activities including anticancer properties. Numerous studies have been conducted to evaluate the anticancer properties of curcumin loaded nanoscale delivery devices on various kinds of cancer cell lines. The striking feature of curcumin lies in its ability to arrest cancer in all stages [153]. The in-vitro studies on different cell lines deciphered the possible mechanism of action of curcumin which is predominantly based on down regulation of certain genes (such as NF-Kβ, AP -1, TNF- α, Cycin D1) required for the cancer cell survival. Suppression of these biomolecules by curcumin paved the way for its application in various types of cancer like colon cancer, rectal cancer, multiple myelomas, pancreatic and intestinal cancer [141, 142]. Curcumin has proclaimed itself to be well deserving reducing agent especially when conjugated with gold NPs (Cu-AuNP). In a recent in-vitro study, Cu-AuNPs was competent enough to stimulate apoptosis in human breast cancer cell lines (MCF-7) in contract to free Cu. The 50% inhibiting concentration (IC50) of MCF-7 with respect to Cu-AuNP was found to be at 10.0 µM [154, 155]. Karimi et al. [145] designed a fabricated nanoliposomal device of curcumin whose pharmacokinetic properties revealed an increased half-life than free curcumin. This nanoliposomal formulation of curcumin was effective in inhibiting the breast adenocarcinoma (TUBO and 4 T1) cell lines. The accumulation of the said nanophtodevice in the tumour was found to be much higher as compared to the free curcumin, owing to the fusogenic properties of the lipid in the formulation. Montazerabadi et al.. in their recent study have synthesized the dendritic magnetite nanocarriers of curcumin, the surface of which was modified with folic acid to ensure better targeting of curcumin towards cancer cells. Due to the high affinity of the folic acid towards the folate receptors which are overexpressed on the cancer cells membrane, the surface modification of the nanodevice with folate could possible facilitate the delivery of the dendrimers to the cancer cells and eventually intensify the internalization of dendrimers in the targeted tumour cells. With the apoptotic property of curcumin, the dendritic nanocarriers surface modified with folate, was able to inhibit the MCF-7 breast cancer cell lines [156]. In another study, copper- curcumin complex (CD) was synthesized to evaluate their anticancer properties. Literature review have highlighted the cytotoxic property of copper. Based on this, Greish K et al., synthesized the CD, initially in vitro investigation against triple negative breast cancer (TNBC). However, due to the poor pharmacokinetic results, the CD was further encapsulated with into a poly(styrene)-co-maleic acid (SMA) micelle. The SMA-CD nanodevice not only displayed a better-quality in terms of stability but was also prevented the TNBC tumour growth in vivo quite effectively [157].

Quercetin

Quercetin is 3, 3′,4′,5,7-pentahydroxyflavone widely distributed in most of the plants, fruits and vegetables with a broad spectrum of pharmacological activities such as anti-inflammatory, anti-atherosclerotic, antihypertensive, cardio-protective, neuroprotective and anticancer properties. Quercetin has displayed significant anticancer activities against various kind of cell lines and tumour cells such as U138MG (glioma), U2.US/MTX300 (osteosarcoma), HeLa (cervical cancer), MDA-MB-453 (breast cancer), myeloid leukaemia, oral cancer linked to its strong antioxidant properties. A substantial number of in vivo studies have further displayed affirmative results of quercetin against tumour growth and metastasis [158, 159].

Nrf2 is responsible to maintain the redox homeostasis of the healthy cells. The overexpression of Nrf2 subsequently invigorates multidrug resistance-associated protein (MRP1and MRP2) thus leading to the development of tumour. Therefore, targeting this pathway could be an effective method in dealing the development of cancer resistance to chemotherapy and improve the viability of anticancer agents. The results of the in-vitro cytotoxity test by MTT assay confirmed that nano- quercetin and doxorubicin were efficient in arresting the cell growth by inducing apoptosis. The nano formulation was able to increase the apoptosis from 40.11 ± 7.72% to 58 ± 7.13 (p < 0.05). Quercetin lessened the cellular MRP1 gene expression levels and sensitized MCF-7 cells to doxorubicin. Thus, applying the nanophytosomal technology, not only the stability of nano quercetin was improved in the microenvironment of cancer cells, but also increased the therapeutic efficacy of doxorubicin by employing low concentration of quercetin for the treatment of chemo-resistant tumours. This would have a significant impact in dampening the residual toxicity of doxorubicin and thus provide a newer adjuvant to doxorubicin chemotherapy [152]. Au nanocages with Biotin–PEG–SH modification was prepared in combination of doxorubicin and quercetin. The nanocomplex displayed high potency against MCF-7 breast cancer cell lines by inhibiting their growth under near infrared irradiation. The presence of quercetin in the combinational system also increased the cellular uptake and accumulation of doxorubicin in the tumour cells [160]. In another study, the quercetin was encapsulated within PEG-poly (lactide) micelles (PEG-PLA), that were synthesized to target the xenograft model of breast cancer. The results highlighted significant increase in apoptosis in the cancer cells with respect to the free quercetin at the same dose [161]. Sarkar et al. [162] synthesized folic acid fabricated mesoporous silica NPs loaded with quercetin (MSN-FA-Q). This nanodevice was assessed for its cytotoxic efficacy against various signalling molecules expressed on the breast adenocarcinoma cells. The MSN-FA-Q nanodevice was able to arrest the cell cycle and furthermore induce apoptosis to the breast cancer cells having an overexpressed folic acid receptor on them, via the AKT and BAX signalling pathways. The folic acid tagged on the NPs have accelerated the cellular uptake of quercetin thus uplifting the bioavailability concerns. Quercetin was conjugated with dextran coated magnetite NP for evaluating the cytotoxic effect on MCF-7 breast cancer cells. S. Rajesh Kumar et al.., synthesized this nanodevice by nanoprecipitation method. An enhancement in quercetin release upto 82% was seen in the study. The rationale behind this is attributed to the ester bond formed between quercetin and the magnetite NP. Apart from this, the QCMNPs increased the cytotoxicity to 75% with respect to only dextran coated MNP. Thus, the researchers claimed that MNP can be considered as a sustained drug delivery device which also aids the bioavailability of quercetin. The microscopic observation confirmed the apoptosis of MCF-7 cells following nuclear fragmentation, cell shrinkage and membrane blebbing [163].

Andrographolide

In the year 1951, Chakravarti and Chakravarti, isolated a labdane diterpenoid compound, named as Andrographolide (AP), from the aerial parts of Andrographis paniculata, belonging to the family of Acanthaceae [164]. The extract of Andrographis paniculata was extensively used as a traditional medicine throughout China, India, Bangladesh and Southeast Asian countries. The promising therapeutic properties of AP has been mentioned in Ayurveda and Unani medicines [165, 166]. Due to the intense bitter taste of AP, it is popularly coined as “King of bitters”. Till date, innumerable studies have highlighted the diverse range of pharmacological properties of AP including antimicrobial, antioxidant, antihyperglycaemic, hepatoprotective and anticancer as well [167]. In the recent years, AG has evolved as an efficient anticancer agent targeting a variety of cancers. The compound is reported to have a multifaceted mechanism of action such as G1 cell cycle arrest, inhibition of NF-ĸB transcriptional factors, activation of p53 and various angiogenic factors and suppression of cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK 4). The immunomodulatory actions against the cancer cells is exerted by interfereing NFAT activation and ERK1/ERK5 phosphorylation in T-cells. Apart from the direct and indirect effects on cancer cells, it also protects the normal cells from induced toxicity. Even though we find the molecule of AP quite efficient towards cancer therapy, the biopharmaceutical limitations such as poor solubility and shorter half-life of 2 h have proven to be a set back towards its therapeutic applications. Similar to previously described phytochemicals, in this case also, the nanoformulation of AP have showcased improved therapeutic efficacy against breast cancer cells. Roy et al. [168] in their study developed a chitosan coated polylactic-glycolic acid (PLGA) polymeric nanoparticles (PNPs) loaded with AG, which showcased an increased antitumor action against both MCF-7 breast cancer cell lines and Ehrlich ascites carcinoma (EAC) cells in rodents, with respect to free AG. The in-vivo results highlighted that the nanoformulation reduced the tumor weight by 68.21% and increased lifespan by 78.08% (compared to 23.5% for free AG) in EAC treated rodents. Ag entrapped in polymeric micelles formulations were also tested against MAD-MD-231 human breast cancer cell lines that displayed promising results. In contrast to the unformulated AG, the micelles were highly efficacious in inhibiting the cellular proliferation by arresting the G2/M phase in MAD-MD-231 cell.

Resveratrol

Resveratrol, a natural stilbenoid, is also a plant secondary metabolite called as phytoalexin, which is produced as a consequence of environmental stresses [169]. Chemically, it is a non—flavonoid polyphenol, having a chemical formula of 3,5,4′-trihydroxy-trans-stilbene. Apart from its previously established anti-oxidant, anti-inflammatory and antineoplastic agents activities, its ability to reverse the drug resistance of the cancer cells along with sensitizing them towards chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Resveretrol directly inhibits cancer initiation, promotion and progression steps by inducing apoptosis, blocking the nitric oxide synthase expression, prevents the metastasis of tumours and also inhibits the cyclo-oxygenase pathway. Studies conducted on different cell lines have unveiled the possible anticancer mechanism resveratrol through various biomarkers namely tumour suppressor microRNAs (miRNAs), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR), nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-kB), nuclear respiratory factor- (NRF-) 1 and 2, gamma coactivator 1 alpha (PGC-1α) and p53 transcription factors, and transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) signalling pathway. In spite of its highly decorated abilities, several drawbacks such poor water solubility, low bioavailability have restricted its pharmacological outreach. Thus, development of nanodevices in be an approach to alleviate the above mentioned restrictions. Encapsulation of resveratrol in SLN was able to arrest the G0/G1 phase of the cell cycle in a dose dependant manner [158]. The liposome of resveratrol coloaded with paclitaxel was prepared by Meng et al. [159] The findings of the study highlighted the cytotoxic property of the liposomes against drug resistance MCF cell lines. The liposome also enhanced the bioavailability of resveratrol in breast cancer bearing mice. Park et al. [170] synthesized the resveratrol capped gold NPs that inhibited the biomarkers of breast cancer namely the matrix metalloproteinase, cyclooxygenase-2, nuclear transcription factor-κB, activator protein-1, phosphoinositide 3-kinase/Akt (PI3K/Akt) and extracellular signal-regulated kinase thereby arresting the cell cycle progression. Nanoemulsion of resveratrol also showcased an enhanced cytotoxicity against MCF-7 breast cancer cell lines [160].

Silibinin

A flavonoid extracted from milk thistle fruit (Silybum marianum, family asteraceae), popularly known as silymarin. The constituents of silymarin incorporate silibinin (a major active constituent), silydianin, silychristin and isosilybin. The flavonoid complex was popularly indicated for hepatoprotective action owing to its cascade of diverse overlapping properties like immunomodulation, anti-fibroitic, anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatory. Keeping the above attributes in backdrop, the inclination to investigate its anticancer activities became popular among researchers across the globe. Wang et al. demonstrated the possible mechanism of silymarin in alleviating breast cancer, by modulating the insulin growth factor- 1 receptor (IGF-1R), in human breast cancer MCF-7 cells. IGF-1R plays a crucial role in the development of breast cancer by stimulating the bio-signalling pathways of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK). These signal transductions thus lead to proliferation of cells, activation of angiogenesis, metastasis and inhibition of apoptosis. The study conducted by Wang et al., showed that co-treatment of silymarin and synthetic tryphostin (AG1024) was effective in inhibiting the IGF-1R in MCF-7 cell tumour [171]. Myristoylated chitosan (MCS) nanogel was synthesized, loaded with silibinin and the effect of this nanodevice was tested against breast cancer cells. In comparison to the normal cells, the MSC-SIL nanogel displayed anti-proliferative properties to MCF-7 cell lines with an improved loading capacity of silibinin, thus playing the perfect role of an efficient drug carrier. The IC50 for silibinin nanogel was significantly lower (7.914 µg/ml) than free silibinin (15.771 µg/ml) [172, 173]. Sajjadiyan et al. synthesized silibinin loaded PEGylated niosomal nanodevice for treating breast cancer cells. The study reported that the niosomal formulation not only improved the penetration property of silibinin inside the MCF-10 breast cancer cells but also enhanced the bioavailability of silibinin at the target site. The drug encapsulation property was found to be nearly 93%, that ensured the targeted delivery of the phytochemicals. Plant bioactive nano formulations in clinical evaluations

The impact of global research on breast cancer management has collaboratively crossed many stages in theragnostic purposes but still there is no permanent strategy build up to cure it completely. Therapeutic success is limited still up to 3rd stages of breast cancer. Current scenario is focusing on nanotechnological intervention to replace or alter the conventional theragnostic approach. As per the scientific report the phytochemical conjugated NPs are comparatively more safe and biologically effective in targeted therapy specially in breast cancer. Numbers of phytochemical conjugated nano medicines are in pipeline of clinical trial stages of breast cancer therapy. Preclinical screening models can lead to develop significant nano medicines in laboratory against breast cancer. This process further develops different models with extensive data on preliminary efficacy, toxicity, pharmacokinetic profile, and safety resources to qualify clinical trial stages. It is absolutely true that some obvious hurdles subsist in the translational process of such products to the clinical application such as large-scale appraisals, stability issues & also high production cost etc. [231]. Pre-clinical trial data of various phytochemical conjugated nanomaterials are evaluated and analyzed in Nanotechnology Characterisation Laboratory (NCL) targeted for breast cancer therapy. Presently several phytochemicals conjugated nanocarriers have been approved by The United States Food and Drug Administration and applied clinically against breast cancer (example- curcumin, paclitaxel, etc.) It was also analysed from several peer reviewed research work that phytochemical conjugated nanomedicine is safer and more effective to combat with breast cancer through the cell cycle arrest & apoptosis mechanism. Furthermore, this adopted green technology to synthesize phytochemical based nanocarriers develops superior anti neoplastic agents to manage breast cancer [232]. This review will further discuss some updated pre-clinical and clinical aspects of phytochemical conjugated nanomaterials against breast cancer. Different formulations of nanocarriers such as liposomes, niosomes, nanospheres are capable to encapsulate various plant mediated molecules such as gallic acid, quercetin, resveratrol, balanocarpol, epigallocatechin, ursolic acid, curcumin and others plant mediated products to mitigate the breast cancer. This plant mediated products combat against the breast cancer with some specific mechanism such as cellular uptake, cellular apoptosis, cytotoxicity, initiation of ROS inside the cancer cell etc. It is also known that the tissue toxicities are also gradually less after the application of nano encapsulated plant mediated products. Epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG), a catechin found in green tea has shown to be effective against a variety of cancers including breast cancer. Zeng et al. [233] found that two types of EGCG nanoparticles (FA-NPS-PEG and FA-PEG-NPS) inhibited MCF-7 cell proliferation in dose-dependent ways without causing cytotoxic effects. Silva et al. [234] found that EGCG loaded into a SLN substantially enhances the antiproliferative impact in MCF-7 cells compared to non-loaded EGCG. Mock et al. [235] reviewed the delivery of curcumin analogues NP enhanced bioavailability of curcumin in breast cancer treatment. A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2/3 research is in progress to see if curcumin (300 mg/i.v./day) combined with Paclitaxel (80 mg/m2 BS; i.v.) administered once weekly for 12 weeks can help patients with advanced and metastatic breast cancer (NCT03072992) [236]. According to Sahu et al. Curcumin and docetaxel, both linked with NPs, were found to enhance the anticancer activity of MCF-7 cells by inhibiting P-glycoprotein [237]. Lipid nanoparticles containing both paclitaxel and curcumin, along with 2-hydroxypropyl-ß-cyclodextrin and folic acid, produced a high absorption in the resistant MCF-7/ADR cells. As this nano-formulation effectively suppressed P-glycoprotein, the cytotoxic effect of paclitaxel was increased [238]. PLGA-curcumin NPs also have been utilized in the treatment of HER2 positive cancer xenografts, with promising results by supress the tumour development and reduced angiogenesis [239]. Trans-resveratrol (50 mg twice a day for 12 weeks) has given in a pilot study on 39 women at high risk for breast cancer. In this study it reduced methylation of RAS association domain family 1 isoform A (RASSF)-1a, a gene linked to breast cancer, increased levels of trans-resveratrol and resveratrol-glucuronide in the circulation, and significantly reduced cancer-promoting PGE2 expression in the breast cancer. Sun et al. [240] developed self-assembled PCL (Polycaprolactone) lipid nanoparticles (SIPNs) containing the anti-metastatic agent silibinin and the photothermal agent indocyanine green (ICG), which prevents the growth and metastasis of breast cancer cells (in vitro) when exposed to an NIR laser. Alkhatib et al. [241] investigated on MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells using a formulation of two anti-cancer agents, docetaxel (DTX) and thymoquinone (TQ) in a borage oil-based nanoemulsion (B-NE). The results suggest that (DTX + TQ) B-NE induces apoptosis and improves DTX efficacy against human breast cancer cells by lowering the effective dose, potentially reducing related toxicity. Karthick et al. [242] studied Quercetin loaded PLGA poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) microsphere with higher concentrations of 3.0 µg which is efficiently inhibit cell proliferation by inducing the apoptosis demonstrating on MCF-7 cell. Khan et al. [243] found that their manufactured quantum dots (QDs) loaded with curcumin using Eudragit RS 100 inhibited both colon and breast cancer cell growth and proliferation compared to nonencapsulated curcumin. The result concluded that encapsulating curcumin in NPs specifically targeted cancer cells and produced programmed cell death. Rasouli et al. [244] studied on Curcumin and Chrysin loaded randomly oriented bead-free PLGA-PEG electrospun nanofibers. It has been found that the anticancer phytochemicals loaded nanofiber is showing effective synergistic antiproliferative actions on T47D breast cancer cells than single drug loaded nanofibers [245]. It has been analysed from different peer reviewed research work that Balanocarpol a phytochemical conjugated with nano carriers impacts successfully on ZR-75 cell line to manage breast cancer [246]. Another phytochemical named cordycepin is applied on MCF–7 cell line with PLGA NPs as a carrier [247, 248]. Plant bioactive nano formulations: regulatory scenario

Nano-scale product offers superior efficacy in biomedical applications due to their enhanced reactivity and ease of permeation to organs, tissues, cells and cellular compartments. But these exclusive advantages may also account for possible toxic influence in healthcare applications. Pharmacological behavior of such products related to their different attributes like size, surface charge, surface functionality, fabrication materials which triggers additional complications. Therefore, there is an urgent need for framing of new regulations adequately tuned to measure their risk assessment [262, 263] and which would further assist clinical validation and commercialization of these products. Though publications on nanotoxicology have increased in several folds in recent times but the need for harmonized and validated protocols still exists. The major hurdles in framing of rules exclusively for nano-dimensional products are the absence of specific nanotoxicity assessment procedures and lack of standard reference materials [264]. Complexity escalates further in the quest for a reference standard to suit clusters like nano-phytodevices which might express toxic influence although its core constituents like plant derived products or its carrier materials for e.g. polymers, phospholipids etc. may individually exhibit high degree of biocompatibility. Focusing on this segment of nano-scale devices it is possible that devices with same constituents but differing in size, shape or surface chemistry or may be the same phytochemical associated with diverse nanocarriers like polymeric NPs, liposomes, carbon dots may express varied toxicological responses. Besides size distribution and physicochemical attributes control the interaction of such products with the immune system therefore immunological assessments becomes crucial in case of their preclinical studies [265].

As nano-scale products having identical chemical origin can present itself in many multiple shapes and sizes so it is a necessary strategy to formulate grouping of structurally similar substances to strengthen regulatory framework. In fact, a nanotoxicological classification system has been proposed by scientists that classifies nano-scale products depending on their physicochemical features and biodegradability [247]. However substantial research output has to be invested to enrich the data bank of such classifications so that they can become the proper arsenals for framing nanotoxicological guidelines. Additionally, the huge burden of cost involved in acute and long-term toxicity profiling of nano-scale materials is also major obstacle in nanomaterial regulation design [266]. Organizations like USFDA, W.H.O, CDSCO ( India), EPA (USA), ECHA are modulating healthcare standards and framing strict guidelines to accommodate nano-scale products and cover their risks [267]. FDA recommends Partnerships and collaborations between global regulatory authorities leading to agreements like EU-US Mutual Recognition Agreement can foster newer regulatory concepts which can accommodate nano-dimensional products. For maintaining a clear and predictable regulatory framework FDA endorses a product-based approach which may be modulated as per the product variants. In case of absence of pre-market review FDA encourages consultation to reduce health risks and at the same time takes up the responsibility of conducting post-marketing survey. Nano-products manufacturing industry should take care of the legal standards related to the products along with its safety window and associated risk parameters as suggested by FDA [268]. In India there are different start-up companies and one of the most suitable is Tirupati, this company has various marketed products with definite bio medical applications. Products such as the cardiovascular protectant, molecules designed to targeted breast cancer, brain etc. are available in markets. This company has an excellent profile to make the translational research in nano technology.

Application of in- silico and computational techniques can catalyse the process of framing regulations related to nanomaterial risk assessment saving both time and financial investments [269]. Moving in this direction three-dimensional cell culture models and mechanical stimulation studies are attracting scientific attention in preclinical stages of nanomaterial safety studies [270]. Innovative analytical techniques need to be materialized that are focused on nanoparticle/carrier mixtures or their specific attributes to aid in improvement of regulatory standards. Implementation of newer concepts like “quality-by-design” approach, process analytical technologies and following of International Conference on Harmonization Q8, Q9 and Q10 guidelines can structure the regulatory framework of such products [271]. Additionally, the growth of organizations like Innovative Medicines Initiative in Europe and National Centre for Advancing Translational Sciences in USA is expected to reinforce regulatory issues linked to innovative technology guided products and assist their fast entry into clinical applications. A combined think-tank comprising of government, educationalists, nanomaterial manufacturing personnel’s and regulatory bodies followed by streamline implementation of their efforts can be the possible solution in making rules aptly suited to interpret the risk assessment of nanomaterials involved in pharmaceutical applications.

Plant bioactive nano formulations: toxicity aspects

Indeed, even notwithstanding the way that Nano-photodevices have demonstrated their superiority by counteracting deficiency in regular herbal formulations, their safety issues must be meticulously investigated [272]. Nano-scale drug carriers because of their size may affect enhanced cellular uptake or interaction and sometimes may even induce greater retention in a specific cellular environment. This may lead to unfavourable influence on living cells which is not observed in case of the same carrier constituent in larger dimension [273]. Nanotherapeutic dosing and its frequency may also give birth to potential toxicity of nano-size drug carriers [274]. Toxicity associated with such products may be governed by conventional factors like dose, dosing frequency, exposure duration, route of administration and by some special physicochemical factors like nano-product composition, dimension, surface modality and appearance. Several physicochemical factors influence interactions with biological environment causing undesirable interference in processes like cell signalling pathways and NP in-vivo distribution leading to toxicity. In case of nano-sized products even minor alterations like decrease in product dimension or reversal of surface charge may influence its toxicity so providing a common framework for toxicity evaluations is a major challenge. Computational based techniques are becoming increasingly popular for toxicity evaluations as they can bypass extensive time and financial investments and also avoid stringent regulatory requirements. So, these can emerge as promising tools for nanotoxicity predictions especially during preformulation stages of nano-scale products intended for clinical applications [232]. Plasmonic nano-products from noble metals such as gold or silver can promote light to heat transformations and provide unreliable results when their interaction with biological structures is evaluated. This may lead to false conclusions as far as nanotoxicity assessments are concerned [275]. Currently lot of research is directed to make such metal NPs by the help of green synthesis with plant products to induce cytocompatibility and hemocompatibility [276]. But size-dependent toxicity and cellular interactions is reported for diverse types of these products so these parameters need to be thoroughly cross-checked to evaluate the toxicity window [276, 277]. Large scale fabrication of gold NPs by reduction of gold salts with the help of phytochemicals from cumin seeds and further stabilized by Gum Arabic was attempted by Katti et al. Biocompatible NPs produced maintained significant viability of fibroblast cells even after high dose of 150µM which indicates their acceptability in clinical applications [278]. Green tea derived gold NPs of different shapes like nanospheres, nano stars, and nanorods were investigated for their effect on cytotoxicity and cellular uptake on AGS, HeLa, HepG2 and HT cells. Interestingly it was observed that highest toxicity was expressed by nanorods followed by nano stars and nanospheres but in case of uptake nanospheres provided superior compared to all other shape variants [279]. In another report it was observed as that cytotoxicity to cancer cells varied with the thickness of the phytochemical corona on the nano-scale gold. The enhanced thickness of resveratrol was linked to increased bioavailability of the plant bioactive which contributed better anticancer effects against breast, prostate and pancreatic cancer cell lines [4]. Toxicity assessments carried out with escalating dose (0–50 µg/mL− 1) of gold NPs decorated with gallic acid or protocatechuic acid revealed no signs of cytotoxicity even after 72 h exposure. Even the highest dose did not affect the cellular viability by more than 10% which confirmed the non-toxic nature of the nano-photodevices [280]. In another exciting report it was revealed that cytotoxicity of a cocktail of plant extracts were higher compared to ethosomes with the same plant extract payload. Toxicity diminution was linked to complex formation between active moieties present in the extracts and the phospholipids in ethosome formulation [281].

Zendehdel et al. confirmed that curcuminoids nano micelles exhibited diminished cytotoxicity compared to the free bioactive which is attributed to the gradual release of the active moiety from the nano-delivery devices [266].

Similar results were obtained by Hosseni et al. involving a marketed phytochemical derived nanotechnology product Sina Curcumin® which proved less toxic than the free bioactives when tested against normal cells [282]. Tea phytochemicals associated gold NPs expressed high biocompatibility to PC-3 and MCF-7 cancer cells after uptake and so can be utilized as probes for cellular imaging [283]. In case of plant derived nano formulations it is observed that it proceeds towards some productive improvement in therapeutic ratio or index. So, by enhancing the gap between doses essential for their therapeutic effects can assist in control of the toxic level of dose that gives unwanted side effects. In spite of this, vigilant and detailed toxicological assessment of various nano-devices based on plant bioactive is warranted. The significant dilemma in toxicity profiling of nano-scale products is that contradictory results are obtained from in vitro and in vivo assessments which makes them extremely difficult to correlate [284]. The inaccurate and conflicting conclusions related to nanomaterial toxicity shed distrust on the prospective of such products and cause futile apprehensions in the public mind-set [285]. Nevertheless, appropriate nano-scale products of plant bioactives can be designed with applicable acquaintance and consideration after a systematic analysis of various interactions between nanomaterials and target cells, resultant toxicity, and its molecular basis. Though the scientific consensus still remains that there is a significant need to produce newer nanomaterial assessment tools, harmonized protocols and techniques for diminishing the risk /benefit ratio of these exciting pharmaceutically active nano-carriers associated with plant derived components.

Conclusion

About 80% of the global population in the developing world bet on plant based medicinal products as their elementary healthcare option [286]. The world market for such products is predicted to rise with a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 5.88% and climb to USD 1,29,689.3 million by 2023 [287]. Following this roadmap, phytochemicals are getting enormous acceptance in breast cancer management due to minimal side effects and their clinical competency to evade drug resistance. In this review, it was mentioned earlier that various scientists are working on phytochemical based materials to mitigate various types of breast cancers. Table 1, and Table 2 has already displayed the documentary evidence of phytochemical based materials and their application in breast cancer management. The unwanted disadvantages of the phytochemicals are their Bio pharmaceutical drawbacks like bioavailability and stability issues. Addressing these issues, structuring nanotechnology guided novel delivery framework for the plant bioactive turned out to be the most appropriate strategy to the researchers to treat breast cancers in different stages ( Stage I–stage IV). Various nano carriers like liposome, carbon nono dots, nano particles are the first line of choice to deliver the phytochemical based materials towards the targeted zone. This review has already explained each and every parameters related to nano technology in specific section of this review [288, 289, 290]. The present review puts further spotlight on phytochemicals conjugated nanocarriers and their commitment towards malignant breast cancer treatment. However extensive investigations in large animal models and eventually in cancer patients in the primary phases of clinical trials are flat out essential for complete exploration of the lethality of these products. The scalability of the phytochemical based nano materials are low because of some regulatory issues as well as cost. Addressing these issues various biomedical sectors are planning to introduce some basic simple technique to make scalable bio safe products to mitigate breast cancers. Taxol, Paclitaxel is very common example. Research organizations are emphasizing to do lasso with various educational sectors to make translational research on this. But still there are lacunas present in phytochemical based nano carriers in various aspects [291]. Though phytochemicals loaded nanocarriers can be tuned easily for targeted delivery of drug cargo but it still lacks the capability to address some unsolved mysteries like overexpression of mutated proteins on cell membranes. Understanding the pinpoint mechanisms of nano-taxis in discussion and their facile scale-up with subsequent engineering to mitigate breast cancer conditions still beckons extensive scientific inquests.

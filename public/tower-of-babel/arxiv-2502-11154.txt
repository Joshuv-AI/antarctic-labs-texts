Refined effective bounds for Bloch-Kato Selmer groups associated to hyperelliptic curves
Lee Berry

Abstract
. We develop refined methods to effectively bound the dimension of Bloch-Kato Selmer groups associated to the higher Chow group CH 2 ​ ( J , 1 ) \mathrm{CH}^{2}(J,1) , where J J is the Jacobian of a hyperelliptic curve X X . This extends the recent work of Dogra on explicit 2 2 -descent for these Selmer groups to include cases where X X does not have a rational Weierstrass point. Additionally, we develop methods for obtaining sharper dimension bounds under the assumption that X X has good ordinary reduction at 2 2 . As a consequence, we establish new criteria for deducing finiteness of the depth 2 2 Chabauty-Kim set X ​ ( ℚ 2 ) 2 X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2} , and demonstrate the efficacy of these criteria on curves from the LMFDB.



1.
Introduction

Fix a prime
p
and denote by
V
a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space with a continuous action of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. In
[
BK90
]
, Bloch and Kato introduced a subspace
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},V)

of the Galois cohomology group
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},V)
, which is finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and generalises the classical notion of a Selmer group. Specifically, if
A
is an abelian variety over
\mathbb{Q}
, then the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group of
A
, upon tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, naturally identifies with
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},V_{p}A)
, where
V=V_{p}A
denotes the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-rational Tate module of
A
. Accordingly, the image of the Kummer map

\displaystyle A(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},V_{p}A)

is contained in
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},V_{p}A)
and surjects onto this Selmer group under finiteness assumptions on the Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sha(A)
of
A
. A natural generalisation of this map for more general Galois representations arising from étale cohomology is the étale regulator

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CH}^{i}(A,j)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow H^{1}\big(\mathbb{Q},H^{2i-j-1}_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p}(i))\big),

where
\mathrm{CH}^{i}(A,j)
denotes a higher Chow group of
A
, as defined in
[
Blo86
]
. A special case of the Bloch-Kato conjectures
[
BK90
, Conjecture 5.3(i)]
asserts that (
1.1
) defines an isomorphism onto the corresponding Selmer group. We do not attempt to survey the scarcity of known cases of the conjecture; suffice it to say, even effective determination of dimension bounds for the relevant Bloch-Kato Selmer group is highly nontrivial. In this paper, we focus on the case where
i=2
,
j=1
, and
A=J
is the Jacobian of a hyperelliptic curve
X
, and develop effective methods for bounding the dimension of the corresponding Bloch-Kato Selmer group
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)
. Moreover, we develop methods to obtain improved bounds whenever
X
has good ordinary reduction at
2
. As the main application of these unconditional bounds, we consider their utility in determining rational points on hyperelliptic curves via the effective Chabauty-Kim method.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of genus
g\geq 2
. By Faltings’ theorem, which resolved the longstanding Mordell conjecture, the set of rational points
X(\mathbb{Q})
is finite. However, the question of whether an algorithm exists to compute this finite set of points remains wide open in general. Fortunately, the Chabauty-Kim method, as developed in
[
Kim05
,
Kim09
]
, has provided insight into addressing this question for an expanding class of higher genus curves. Roughly speaking, the Chabauty-Kim method constructs for each integer
n>0
a set of
p
-adic points
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
, which contains
X(\mathbb{Q})
and forms a nested sequence of inclusions

\displaystyle X(\mathbb{Q})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}\subseteq X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{1}\subseteq X(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

The Bloch-Kato conjectures, among other well-known conjectures, imply that for
n
sufficiently large, the
depth
n
Chabauty-Kim set

X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
is finite (see
[
Kim09
, §3]
). Therefore, a potential general strategy for determining
X(\mathbb{Q})
via Chabauty-Kim theory could proceed as follows.

(1)

Establish criteria for the finiteness of the depth
n
Chabauty-Kim set
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
.

(2)

Once
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
is shown to be finite for some
n>0
, compute
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
explicitly.

(3)

Identify and discard the points in
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
that are not
\mathbb{Q}
-rational.

We do not elaborate on the final point here; for a given curve, it is expected that in most cases this could be dealt with by an application of the Mordell-Weil sieve of
[
BS10
]
.

At depth
1
, the first two steps of the strategy are addressed by the effective method of Chabauty-Coleman
[
Cha41
,
Col85
]
. This method guarantees that
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{1}
is finite whenever the rank of the Mordell-Weil group
J(\mathbb{Q})
is less than the genus
g
of
X
; in this case, explicit computation is theoretically possible via Coleman integration. At depth
2
, the method of quadratic Chabauty for rational points, introduced in
[
BD18
]
, asserts that
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
is finite whenever
\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621ptJ(\mathbb{Q})<g+\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621pt\mathrm{NS}(J)-1
, where
\mathrm{NS}(J)
denotes the Néron-Severi group of the Jacobian of
X
. In this case, the method can effectively compute a finite intermediate subset – between the depth
1
and
2
sets – using the machinery of
p
-adic heights.

Given that
\mathrm{NS}(J)
has nonzero rank, the quadratic Chabauty method unsurprisingly has broader applicability than the Chabauty-Coleman method. For example, one advantage of quadratic Chabauty is its success at effective determination of rational points on modular curves (see, for example,
[
BDM+23
]
). However, the quadratic Chabauty criterion remains restrictive in general. For instance,
[
Dog24
]
notes that genus
2
, rank
2
odd hyperelliptic curves on the LMFDB
[
LMF24
]
rarely satisfy the criterion. The obstruction to obtaining a more widely applicable criterion in depth
2
is the Bloch-Kato Selmer group
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}V_{p}J)
, as demonstrated by the following case of a general theorem of Kim.

Theorem

(
[
Kim09
, Theorem 1]
,
[
Dog23
, Lemma 25]
)
.

Suppose

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H_{f}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}V_{p}J)<\frac{1}{2}(3g-2)(g+1)-\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621ptJ(\mathbb{Q}).

Then
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
is finite.

Consequently, the
p=2
case of this theorem, coupled with the aforementioned effective methods developed in this paper, yield refined finiteness criteria for the depth
2
set
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
for hyperelliptic curves. As alluded to above, these criteria are particularly applicable for curves with good ordinary reduction at
2
. In the above framework, this paper focuses predominantly on addressing point (
1
) for such curves.

1.1.
2
-descent for Bloch-Kato Selmer groups

To bound the
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
-dimension of
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)
, it suffices to bound the rank of
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
, where
T_{2}J
denotes the
2
-adic Tate module of
J
. Since the mod-
2
quotient of
\wedge^{2}T_{2}J
is isomorphic to
\wedge^{2}J[2]
, the strategy is to bound this rank by computing subgroups of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}J[2])
that contain the image of
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
. To elaborate on this strategy further, we introduce some notation.

Suppose
f\in K[x]
is a polynomial with coefficients in a field
K
of characteristic different from
2
. Then define the following étale algebras:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{f}&\coloneqq K[x]/(f(x))\\
K_{f,2}&\coloneqq K[x,y,\tfrac{1}{x-y}]/\big(f(x),f(y)\big),\end{split}

and denote by
K_{f}^{(2)}
the subalgebra of
K_{f,2}
fixed by the endomorphism that interchanges the classes of
x
and
y
in
K_{f,2}
. If
X
is a hyperelliptic curve over
K
with exactly one rational Weierstrass point, so that it can be represented by the affine model
y^{2}=f(x)
for some odd degree
f\in K[x]
, then
[
Dog23
, Proposition 1]
establishes the description

(1.3)

\displaystyle H^{1}(K,\wedge^{2}J[2])\simeq\mathrm{Ker}\big(K^{(2),\times}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\xrightarrow{\text{Nm}}K_{f}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\big).

In Lemma
2.2
below, we establish an analogous description involving a generalised Jacobian of
X
, which provides a rudimentary upper bound for curves with no rational Weierstrass point. While these descriptions alone can provide finiteness criteria applicable to curves of high genus, they are too coarse to deduce finiteness of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
for genus
2
curves, for instance. The above mentioned
[
Dog23
,
Dog24
]
provide two approaches to refining these upper bounds, while the current paper considers a third. Although most effectively applicable to hyperelliptic curves with good ordinary reduction at
2
, the advantages of the methods in this paper over those previously mentioned are twofold. Firstly, checking a given curve satisfies the finiteness criteria of this paper is significantly more computationally efficient; secondly, the current methods also apply to hyperelliptic curves with no rational Weierstrass point.

To state the main results, suppose
X
is a hyperelliptic curve over
K=\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
2
, given by the affine model
y^{2}=f(x)
, where
f
is not necessarily of odd degree. Suppose

is a finite set of primes, and denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(f,S)\coloneqq\mathrm{Ker}\Big(H^{1}\big(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}),\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}^{(2)}}\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\rightarrow H^{1}\big(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}),\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}}\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\Big),

as the kernel of the map induced by the norm map in (
1.3
), where
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
denotes the maximal extension of
\mathbb{Q}
unramified outside of
S
. In particular, if the degree of
f
is odd then
\mathcal{A}(f,S)
is precisely the cohomology group
H^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}),\wedge^{2}J[2])
, where
J
is the Jacobian of the curve defined by

. In Lemma
2.8
and Definition
4.1
below, we define an effectively computable map

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}:\mathcal{A}(f,S)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathbb{F}_{2})/H^{1}_{\mathrm{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathbb{F}_{2}),

where
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord}}
denotes the étale algebra
(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{Q}^{(2)}
for
y^{2}+Q(x)y=P(x)
a suitably chosen Weierstrass model of
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
; this choice is detailed in (

) below. Although its definition certainly depends on the set

, the notation
\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}
should not be ambiguous as

approximates the image of
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}J[2])
and allows for the following dimension bounds, distinguished by the number of rational Weierstrass points (RWPs) on
X
.

Theorem A

(Theorems
4.4
and
4.6
)
.

Suppose
X
is a genus
g
curve as above, and let
S
in the definition of
\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}
be the set of primes that contains
2

does not have semistable reduction. Then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\leq\begin{cases}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{\mathrm{dR}})-2,&\text{if $X$ has one RWP};\\

Given the theorem of Kim above, this facilitates the desired finiteness criteria. In the case that

[
Dog24
]
can be used in tandem with the approximation of the image of

can be concluded by a combination of the two methods, but not by either of the methods alone; see the discussion after Theorem

The computational efficiency mentioned above allows for the effective computation of

Theorem B

(Theorem
5.7
)
.

Of the
1,138

In contrast, the Néron-Severi group of the Jacobian of each of these

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose

is a hyperelliptic curve given by the affine model above, where the degree of

\displaystyle H^{1}\big(K,\wedge^{2}J_{\mathfrak{m}}[2]\big)\simeq\mathrm{Ker}\Big(H^{1}\big(K,\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}^{(2)}}\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\rightarrow H^{1}\big(K,J_{\mathfrak{m}}[2]\big)\Big).

Proof.

Identically following the proof of part (2) of Lemma

, the short exact sequence (

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\wedge^{2}J_{\mathfrak{m}}[2]\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\wedge^{2}\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}}\mathbb{F}_{2}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow J_{\mathfrak{m}}[2]\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0\\

;

(2)

\mathrm{loc}_{n,p}(\xi)\in H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},U_{n})
;

(3)

\xi
is mapped into the image of
J(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q},T},V_{p}J)
,

where
V_{p}J
denotes the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-rational Tate module of the Jacobian of
X
. Finally, define the
depth
n
Chabauty-Kim set

X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
as the set of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-rational points of
X
that are mapped by
\kappa_{n,p}
into the image of the Selmer variety.

We emphasise that this overview of the Chabauty-Kim method is intentionally concise, as the technical details of the setup will not be explicitly utilised in the subsequent material. More detailed expositions can be found in any of the references on the Chabauty-Kim method mentioned thus far. Nevertheless, with this introduced notation, the following essential theorem will provide the desired finiteness criteria.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Kim09
, Theorem 1]
)
.

If the image of
\mathrm{Sel}(U_{n})
under the morphism
\mathrm{loc}_{n,p}
is not dense in
H^{1}_{f}\big(\mathbb{Q}_{p},U_{n}\big)
, then
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}
is finite.

This is the general theorem of Kim alluded to in the introduction; we restate the corresponding case for
n=2
for ease of reference.

Corollary 3.2

(
[
Dog23
, Lemma 25]
)
.

Suppose

(3.1)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H_{f}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}V_{p}J)<\frac{1}{2}(3g-2)(g+1)-\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621ptJ(\mathbb{Q}).

Then
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
is finite.

This corollary guarantees that the
dimension inequality
holds:

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{Sel}(U_{2})<\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},U_{2}),

which suffices to conclude non-dominance of
\mathrm{loc}_{2,p}
. The dimensions of these Selmer schemes can be expressed in terms of the dimensions of Bloch-Kato Selmer groups associated to the graded pieces of the filtration of
U_{2}
determined by the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\overline{\wedge^{2}V_{p}J}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow U_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow V_{p}J\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where
\overline{\wedge^{2}V_{p}J}\coloneqq\mathrm{Coker}\big(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(1)\xrightarrow{\cup^{*}}\wedge^{2}V_{p}J\big)
.
To establish the inequality in Corollary
3.2
, it remains to compute the dimensions of the relevant Bloch-Kato Selmer groups, which is possible via well-known results in
p
-adic Hodge theory.

As in the paper from which Corollary
3.2
is taken, the strategy of the subsequent sections is to bound the dimension on the left-hand side of (

) by the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension of a related Galois cohomology group more amenable to computation. To this end, we require the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose
K
is a number field,

is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space with a continuous action of
G_{K}
, and

is a

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice. For a prime

\displaystyle\iota:H^{1}(K_{v},T)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{1}(K_{v},V)

the map induced by the inclusion

\displaystyle H^{1}_{*}(K,T)\coloneqq\bigcap_{v}\mathrm{Ker}\big(H^{1}(K,T)\rightarrow H^{1}(K_{v},T)/H^{1}_{*}(K_{v},T)\big),

where the intersection varies over the primes

of

in

Thus, to bound the dimension on the left-hand side of (

Suppose

\displaystyle H_{f}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}J[2])\subseteq H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2]).

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(f,S)=\mathrm{Ker}\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{f}^{(2)}}[\tfrac{1}{2}]^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{f}}[\tfrac{1}{2}]^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\big),

To refine the inclusion in (

]

2
to exploit the following description.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
X
is a hyperelliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
with good ordinary reduction. Then

\displaystyle H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)=\mathrm{Ker}\big(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})\big).

Proof.

This is an instance of a general result (see
[
NP00
, §2.1.6(ii)]
, for example) concerning Bloch-Kato Selmer groups of
ordinary representations
. Regardless, we outline the proof here for completeness.

The assumption of good ordinary reduction ensures that there exists the following short exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}]
-modules:

\displaystyle 0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow(V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{*}(1)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow V_{2}J\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
on

is unramified. This induces a filtration
F^{\bullet}
on
\wedge^{2}V_{2}J
by subrepresentations stable under the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
, with graded pieces

\displaystyle F^{0}/F^{1}

\displaystyle=\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},

\displaystyle F^{1}/F^{2}

\displaystyle=(V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\otimes(V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{*})(1),

\displaystyle F^{2}/F^{3}

\displaystyle=(\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{*}(2).

If we denote by
W=F^{1}
the kernel of
\wedge^{2}V_{2}J\rightarrow\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
, it follows directly from the Galois action on the graded pieces above that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{0}D_{\mathrm{dR}}(W)=D_{\mathrm{dR}}(\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})/\mathrm{Fil}^{0}=0.

Consequently, the sequence

(3.6)

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},W)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})

is exact. However, the dimension of the right-most Selmer group can be deduced via the following series of equalities:

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}D_{\mathrm{cris}}(\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{\varphi=1}=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}).

Since
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
is trivial, the exactness of (
3.6
) implies that

\displaystyle H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)=\mathrm{Im}\big(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},W)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\big),

as desired.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The difference between
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V)
and
H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V)
, for a Galois
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-representation
V
, is dictated by the following short exact sequence (see, for instance,
[
Nek93
, Corollary 1.18]
):

(3.7)

\displaystyle 0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\big(D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V^{*}(1))\big)^{\varphi=1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0.

Setting
p=2
and
V=(V_{2}J)^{\otimes 2}
, this sequence, and the crystalline comparison theorem of Faltings
[
Fal88
]
, imply that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},(V_{2}J)^{\otimes 2})-&\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},(V_{2}J)^{\otimes 2})\\
&=\dim\mathrm{End}_{\varphi}(H^{1}_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}/W(\mathbb{F}_{2}))[\tfrac{1}{2}]).\end{split}

Replacing
(V_{2}J)^{\otimes 2}
with
\wedge^{2}V_{2}J
, the difference in dimensions of the corresponding Selmer groups is the dimension of the alternating summand
\wedge^{2}H^{1}_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},W(\mathbb{F}_{2}))[\tfrac{1}{2}](1)
of the endomorphism group in (
3.8
). This latter dimension can be determined via a crystalline analogue of Tate’s isogeny theorem (see
[
MW71
, Theorems 5 and 6]
, for example). Indeed, the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\varphi}(H^{1}_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}/W(\mathbb{F}_{2}))[\tfrac{1}{2}])

is an isomorphism, under which the preimage of the above alternating summand is identified with the Néron-Severi group of
J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)-

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)=\rho(J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})\geq 1,

where
\rho(J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
denotes the Picard number of
J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
.

the composition

(4.3)

\displaystyle H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2])\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ker}\big(\mathbb{Q}_{v,f}^{(2),\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{v,f}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_{v,f}^{(2)})[2],

where this final map is the aforementioned explicit boundary map in (
4.2
) via the field-theoretic description afforded by Lemma
2.1
. By construction, we have the inclusions

(4.4)

\displaystyle H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}J[2])\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\hskip 2.27621pt\theta_{2}\cap\mathrm{Ker}\hskip 2.27621pt\theta_{\infty}\subseteq H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2]).

The maps
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{\infty}
are computable, and
[
Dog24
, Theorem 1]
demonstrates the effectiveness of computing the intersection in (
4.4
) in deducing finiteness of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
via Corollary
3.2
. Specifically, it shows the desired finiteness can be established by this computation for the majority of genus
2
odd hyperelliptic curves on the LMFDB with Mordell-Weil rank
2
.

4.1.2.
Statement of the main theorem in the odd degree case

Suppose that
X
has good ordinary reduction at
2
and is given by the Weierstrass model (
2.7
). In the case of interest for this subsection, as noted in Remark
2.9
, we require
\deg Q<g+1
to explicitly describe
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}J[2])
. To distinguish between the two cases considered in this paper, whenever
X
is given by an odd degree model define
Q_{\beta}(x)=(x-\beta)^{3}Q\big(\tfrac{1}{x-\beta}\big)
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
does not reduce to a root of
\overline{Q}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
. Then define

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord}}\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{Q}^{(2)},&\text{if $\deg Q=g+1$};\\[6.0pt]
(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{Q_{\beta}}^{(2)},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The effectively computable map essential in obtaining the effective bounds and finiteness criteria of this paper is then defined as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose we have the decomposition

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord},\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2},

where each
K_{i}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. Denote by
\xi_{i}\in K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
the class which defines the unramified quadratic extension of
K_{i}
. Then define

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Ker}\Big(H^{1}\big(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{f}^{(2)},S},\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\rightarrow H^{1}\big(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{f},S},\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\Big)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i}\frac{K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}}{\langle\xi_{i}\rangle},

as the map induced by the composition

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{f}^{(2),\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{2,f}^{(2),\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord},\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2},

where the final map is as defined in Lemma
2.8
.

To finally relate
\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}
to bounding the desired Selmer groups, we require the following lemma. It is then possible to state the main theorem for hyperelliptic curves with one RWP, before stating the corresponding finiteness criterion as an immediate corollary.

Lemma 4.2
.

The image of
2
-torsion under the composition

(4.7)

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}[2])\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i}K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}

is the sum
\bigoplus_{i}\langle\xi_{i}\rangle
, where
\xi_{i}\in K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
is defined as in Definition
4.1
.

Proof.

Since
\wedge^{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}[2]
is isomorphic to the mod-
2
quotient of
\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
and
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
is trivial, it follows that the
2
-torsion in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
is given by the image of the boundary map

(4.8)

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}[2])\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}).

As
\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
is unramified as a
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
-module, it follows that the image of (
4.8
) is contained in

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})=H^{1}_{\mathrm{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}),

from which we conclude that the image of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})[2]
under the first map in (
4.7
) is unramified. Moreover, the exact sequence (
2.9
) implies that
H^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\wedge^{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}[2])
maps to

(4.9)

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\wedge^{2}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{2,Q}}\mathbb{F}_{2})\simeq\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{F}_{2},

where the index
i
varies over the number of fields in the decomposition (
4.6
) of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord}}
. The result then follows as the image of (
4.9
) in
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord},\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
is precisely the sum
\bigoplus_{i}\langle\xi_{i}\rangle
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The cohomology group
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}[2])
, and therefore the
2
-torsion in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
, is always nontrivial in the case that
X
has ordinary reduction over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. This follows from the fact that the defining polynomial
Q
being separable modulo
2
implies that the Galois group of each irreducible factor of
Q
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
is cyclic. In particular, for an irreducible factor of
Q
of degree
d
with roots
\{\beta_{i_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{d}
, the sum

\displaystyle\beta_{i_{1}}\wedge\beta_{i_{2}}+\beta_{i_{2}}\wedge\beta_{i_{3}}+\cdots+\beta_{i_{d-1}}\wedge\beta_{i_{d}}+\beta_{i_{1}}\wedge\beta_{i_{d}}

defines an element of
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}[2])
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
X
is a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
with exactly one RWP, given by the Weierstrass model (
2.7
) such that
f=4P+Q^{2}
has odd degree. Let
S
be a finite set of primes that contains
2
, where
X
has good ordinary reduction, as well as the primes where
X
does not have semistable reduction. Then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}H^{1}_{f}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{2}\oplus\theta_{\infty}\oplus\theta_{\mathrm{dR}})-2.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.8
, we obtain the inclusion

\displaystyle H^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2])\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\big(H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2])\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}[2])/\mathrm{Im}(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})[2])\big),

where the map on the right-hand side factors through
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J[2])
. By Lemmas
2.8
and
4.2
, and the isomorphism (
2.12
), the right-hand side of the inclusion is contained in
\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{\mathrm{dR}})
.
By Remark
3.9
, it then suffices to show that
H^{1}_{f}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
has nontrivial 2-torsion and that

(4.10)

\displaystyle H_{f}^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2])\cap\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{2}\oplus\theta_{\infty})\neq H^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2])\cap\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{2}\oplus\theta_{\infty}),

from which the result follows from the inclusion (
4.4
). Towards the former point, note that the Weil pairing, upon dualising, induces the injection

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{2}\hookrightarrow\wedge^{2}J[2].

It follows that
H^{0}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2])
is nontrivial, and this group injects into
H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
, as
H^{0}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)=0
. Finally, it is clear by definition that
H^{1}_{f}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
contains all the 2-torsion of
H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
.

Given the decomposition

\displaystyle\wedge^{2}V_{2}J\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{2}(1)\oplus\overline{\wedge^{2}V_{2}J},

and the fact that
H^{1}_{f}(G_{S},\mathbb{Q}_{2}(1))=0
while
H^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\mathbb{Q}_{2}(1))\neq 0
, it follows that there exists an element in
H^{1}(G_{S},\mathbb{Z}_{2}(1))
whose image in
H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J[2])
under the composite

\displaystyle H^{1}(G_{S},\mathbb{Z}_{2}(1))\rightarrow H^{1}(G_{S},\mu_{2})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\mu_{2})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J[2])

is not contained in
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}J[2])
. Finally, since
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},\mu_{2})
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},\wedge^{2}J[2])
clearly lifts to
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},\mu_{4})
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},\wedge^{2}J[4])
for
v\in\{2,\infty\}
, this proves (
4.10
).
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose
X
and
S
are as in Theorem
4.4
. Then the depth
2
set
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
is finite whenever

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{2}\oplus\theta_{\infty}\oplus\theta_{\mathrm{dR}})<\frac{1}{2}(3g^{2}+g+2)-\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621ptJ(\mathbb{Q}).

Proof.

This immediately follows from Theorem
4.4
and Corollary
3.2
.
∎

4.2.
Case 2: Hyperelliptic curves without a rational Weierstrass point

As discussed in Section
2
, contrary to the previous section, we no longer have an explicit field-theoretic description of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}J[2])
to obtain an upper bound for the rank of the relevant Bloch-Kato Selmer group. However, Lemma
2.2
provides a description of the analogue for the generalised Jacobian, which will suffice for deriving the required upper bounds. To that end, suppose that
X
is a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
with no RWP, and
S
is a nonempty set of primes. In the notation of Lemma
3.6
, it is clear that

\displaystyle H^{1}_{g}\big(G_{S},V_{2}J(1)(\chi_{c})\big)=H^{1}\big(G_{S},V_{2}J(1)(\chi_{c})\big),

where
H^{1}_{g}(G_{S},-)
denotes those classes that are unramified outside of
S
and de Rham at
2
. This immediately yields the following refinement of the inequality from Lemma
3.6
:

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621ptH^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)\leq\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621ptH^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathfrak{m}})-g.

This inequality allows the relevant dimension bounds to be deduced solely from cohomology groups associated to the generalised Jacobian, which, in this case, are more amenable to computation.

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose
X
is a hyperelliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of genus
g
without a RWP, given by the Weierstrass model (
2.7
). Suppose
S
is a finite set of primes that contains
2
, where
X
has good ordinary reduction, as well as all primes where
X
does not have semistable reduction. Then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{\mathrm{dR}})-g-1

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
and the short exact sequence (
2.6
), there is an inclusion

\displaystyle H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J_{\mathfrak{m}}[2])\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\Big(H^{1}\big(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{f}^{(2)},S},\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\rightarrow H^{1}\big(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{f},S},\mathbb{F}_{2}\big)\Big),

which gives the bound
\dim H^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathfrak{m}})\leq\dim\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{\mathrm{dR}})
. The result follows from (
4.11
) and Remark
3.9
.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Suppose
X
and
S
are as in Theorem
4.6
. Then the depth
2
set
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
is finite whenever

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Ker}(\theta_{\mathrm{dR}})<\frac{3}{2}g(g+1)-\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621ptJ(\mathbb{Q}).

Remark 4.8
.

Contrary to Theorem
4.4
, the even degree case does not as readily allow for the refinements provided by
2
-torsion in
H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J_{\mathfrak{m}})
or by explicit boundary maps. To establish nontrivial
2
-torsion as in the odd degree case, one would need to identify nontrivial elements of

, but it is not immediately clear how to deduce their existence. Moreover, it is unclear how to extend the boundary map approach from
[

]
to the even degree case. For instance, the odd degree case relies on an explicit description of

, an analogue of which for the generalised Jacobian is subtler to describe. Although obstructions at the real place may be introduced following
[

, §5]
, proving the analogue of (

) is also not immediate, as the injection

5.
Low genus examples

Verifying the criteria above requires constructing the étale
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
-algebra
L^{(2)}
in Lemma
2.8
, the structure of which is dictated by the Galois group of the defining polynomial
f
of
X
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. Conveniently, the assumption of good ordinary reduction greatly limits the possible Galois groups of
f
, allowing for the straightforward implementation of an algorithm that checks the finiteness criteria for each possible construction of
L^{(2)}
for low genus curves. The steps in this construction, which are independent of whether
X
has a RWP and are suitable for defining the map in Lemma
2.8
, are detailed below.

Algorithm 5.1

(Explicitly computing the map from Lemma
2.8
(2))
.

Input:
Polynomials
P,Q\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
which define a Weierstrass model (
2.7
) for a hyperelliptic curve
X
with good ordinary reduction at
2
, such that the degree of
f=4P+Q^{2}
is odd if
X
has a RWP.

Output:
The étale algebra
L^{(2)}
and the map

\displaystyle L^{(2),\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord},\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}.

(1)

Determine the polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
for which
\mathbb{Q}_{f}^{(2)}\simeq\mathbb{Q}[x]/(F)
, and factor it over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. Each factor defines a field in the decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}_{2,f}^{(2)}
, which, in turn, is defined by a
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
-stable subset of
\wedge^{2}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{2,f}}\mathbb{F}_{2}
upon taking cohomology.

(2)

Compute the Galois group of
f
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
and use this to match the factors of
F
obtained in (1) with the
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
-orbits of the unordered pairs of distinct roots of
f
. Let each factor
F_{i}
of
F
correspond to an orbit denoted
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\cdot s_{i}
, where
s_{i}
is contained in
\wedge^{2}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{2,f}}\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

(3)

Using the correspondence in (2), match each factor
F_{i}
with the field
K_{i}
in the decomposition
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord},\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}=\bigoplus_{i}K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
such that the image of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\cdot s_{i}
under the map in Lemma
2.8
gives
K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
upon taking cohomology.

(4)

Any factor of
F_{i}
over the corresponding field
K_{i}
obtained in (3) defines an extension of
K_{i}
, which is the relevant component of
L^{(2)}
.

(5)

Return the map

\displaystyle L^{(2),\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}\Big(\bigoplus_{j}(K_{i})_{G_{ij}}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\Big)\xrightarrow{\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{Nm}}\bigoplus_{i}K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2},

where
j
runs over all the factors
F_{ij}
of
F
that correspond to
K_{i}
in (4), and
G_{ij}
is any factor of
F_{ij}
over
K_{i}
.

To exploit this algorithm to define
\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}
, it would remain to compute the unramified classes
\xi_{i}
from Definition
4.1
in each of the fields
K_{i}
obtained in step (3). In the remainder of this section, we present examples computing
\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}
associated to genus
2
and
3
curves by implementing the general framework outlined in Algorithm
5.1
. All computations necessary in deducing these examples were implemented in
Magma

[
BCP97
]
.

5.1.
Conditional dimension formulae

Although the computations below provide an upper bound for the dimension of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)
, the exact dimension is predicted by the Bloch-Kato conjectures, as previously mentioned. We now state the relevant part of the conjectures required for the conditional dimension calculations, followed by a lemma instrumental in the application of this conjecture.

Conjecture 5.2

(
[
BK90
, Conjecture 5.3(i)]
)
.

Suppose
A
is a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the étale regulator

\displaystyle\mathrm{CH}^{i}(A,j)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow H^{1}_{f}\big(\mathbb{Q},H^{2i-j-1}_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}(A_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p}(i))\big),

is an isomorphism whenever
2i-j-1>0
.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
BD20
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
S
is a finite set of primes. Then Conjecture
5.2
implies

\displaystyle H^{1}_{f}(G_{S},\overline{\wedge^{2}V_{2}J}^{*}(1))=0.

Following the proof of
[
BD20
, Lemma 2.5]
, if
S
is a finite set of primes, then we obtain the conditional dimension

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{f}&(G_{S},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\\
&=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\overline{\wedge^{2}V_{2}J})-g(g-1)-\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621pt\mathrm{NS}(J)+1\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(g^{2}+g-2)-\mathrm{rk}\hskip 2.27621pt\mathrm{NS}(J)+1,\end{split}

where the first equality is a consequence of Lemma
5.3
and Poitou-Tate duality, while the second follows from the equality of dimensions
\dim H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\overline{\wedge^{2}V_{2}J})=\dim H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)-1
and the isomorphism
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\simeq D_{\mathrm{dR}}(\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)/\mathrm{Fil}^{0}
.

Under the assumption that
X
is a hyperelliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at
2
, Conjecture
5.2
further predicts a greater difference in the dimensions of the global Bloch-Kato Selmer groups of Remark
3.9
, as discussed in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
X
is a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
2
, and
S
is a finite set of primes that contains
2
. Then Conjecture
5.2
implies that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)-\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}H^{1}_{f}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\geq g.

Proof.

Given the filtration of
\wedge^{2}V_{2}J
in the proof of Lemma (
3.8
), in the case that
X
has good ordinary reduction at
2
we know that the difference in dimensions between the local Selmer groups
H^{1}_{*}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)
for
*\in\{f,g\}
is precisely the difference in dimensions of the corresponding Selmer groups of
\mathrm{gr}^{1}F^{\bullet}=V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{*}\otimes V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(1)
. By the exact sequence (
3.7
), this dimension is the following:

\displaystyle\dim\big(D_{\mathrm{cris}}\big((\mathrm{gr}^{1}F^{\bullet})^{*}(1)\big)\big)^{\varphi=1}

\displaystyle=\dim D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{*}\otimes V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{\varphi=1}

\displaystyle=\dim\mathrm{End}_{\varphi}\big(D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})\big).

Unconditionally, this final dimension is at least
g
. Indeed, if
V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
decomposes as the direct sum
\oplus_{i}V_{i}
of simple representations, then
\mathrm{End}_{\varphi}\big(D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V_{i})\big)
contains the subspace
\langle 1,\varphi,\varphi^{2},\dots,\varphi^{\dim V_{i}-1}\rangle
. Summing over all
V_{i}
gives the desired
g
-dimensional subspace of
\mathrm{End}_{\varphi}\big(D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V_{2}J_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})\big)
.

This disparity in the dimensions of the local Selmer groups lifts to the global Selmer groups. To that end, suppose to the contrary that

\displaystyle H^{1}_{g}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)\rightarrow H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)/H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{2},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)

does not surject. This implies that the image of

\displaystyle H^{1}_{f}\big(G_{S},(\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)^{*}(1)\big)\rightarrow H^{1}_{f}\big(\mathbb{Q}_{2},(\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)^{*}(1)\big)/H^{1}_{e}\big(\mathbb{Q}_{2},(\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)^{*}(1)\big)

is nontrivial. Indeed, this follows as the exact annihilators of
H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W)
and
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W)
, for a
p
-adic Galois representation
W
, under the cup product pairing

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W)\times H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W^{*}(1))\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p},

are
H^{1}_{e}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W^{*}(1))
and
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W^{*}(1))
, respectively (see
[
BK90
, Proposition 3.8]
). However, this nontrivial image contradicts Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Given Lemma
5.4
and Remark
3.9
, we expect the bounds obtained using Theorems
4.4
and
4.6
to, at best, be
g-1
above the dimension predicted by the Bloch-Kato conjectures in (
5.1
).

As

is of odd degree, it is necessary to modify the pairing of its roots in Notation

. Specifically, fix
\alpha_{1}
as the
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
-rational root of

, which reduces to the rational root
\beta_{1}
of

in (
4.5
), and retain the usual notation for the remaining roots (i.e., the root
\alpha_{i,j}
of
f
reduces to the roots
\beta_{i}
of
Q
for
i\in\{2,3\}
,

). It follows from basic Galois theory that the possible Galois groups for
f
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
are isomorphic to the following subgroups of
S_{5}
: (i)
\langle(24)(35)\rangle
, (ii)
\langle(24)(35),(23)(45)\rangle
, (iii)
\langle(2435)\rangle
and (iv)
\langle(24)(35),(23)\rangle
. A straightforward computation then determines the
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}}
-orbits of the pairs of roots of

, and hence the factorisation of the defining polynomial

of
\mathbb{Q}_{2,f}^{(2)}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
. We illustrate this procedure for the largest Galois group, case (iv), which is generic for the curves considered from the LMFDB; with obvious modifications, the other cases are treated analogously.

Example 5.6
.

Consider the hyperelliptic curve
X
with LMFDB label
216663.a.216663.1
, given by the Weierstrass model (
2.7
), where

\displaystyle P(x)

\displaystyle=x^{5}-2x^{4}+3x^{3}-4x^{2}+x-1,\hskip 14.22636ptQ(x)=x^{2}+x+1.

This curve has Mordell-Weil rank 2 and discriminant
3\cdot 72221
, so that it is possible to take
S=\{2\}
. Additionally,
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Q}_{f}^{(2)})=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Q}_{f})=1
for

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=4x^{5}-7x^{4}+14x^{3}-13x^{2}+6x-3,

and therefore

(5.2)

\displaystyle H^{1}(G_{S},\wedge^{2}J[2])\simeq\mathrm{Ker}\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{f}^{(2)}}[\tfrac{1}{2}]^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{f}}[\tfrac{1}{2}]^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\big),

by Lemma
2.1
and the exact sequence (
3.3
). According to
Magma
, the dimension of (
5.2
) is
6
. It is readily verified that
\mathrm{Gal}(f)\simeq\langle(24)(35),(23)\rangle
, and in this case
F
splits over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
as the product of a quadratic and two quartic factors. Explicitly, the quadratic factor corresponds to the pairs of roots of
f
that reduce to the same root of
Q
, as expected, while the quartic factors arise from the following orbits of pairs of roots of
f
:

(i)

G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\cdot\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2,1}\}=\big\{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2,1}\},\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2,2}\},\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3,1}\},\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3,2}\}\big\}
;

(ii)

G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\cdot\{\alpha_{2,1},\alpha_{3,1}\}=\big\{\{\alpha_{2,1},\alpha_{3,1}\},\{\alpha_{2,1},\alpha_{3,2}\},\{\alpha_{2,2},\alpha_{3,1}\},\{\alpha_{2,2},\alpha_{3,2}\}\big\}
.

To distinguish between the two quartic factors in the
Magma
implementation, note that the factor corresponding to (i) will factor over
\mathbb{Q}_{2,Q}
as the product of two irreducible quadratics, while the factor corresponding to (ii) will remain irreducible. For
K=\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, under the map

\displaystyle\wedge^{2}\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}}\mathbb{F}_{2}\rightarrow\wedge^{2}\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{Q_{\beta}}}\mathbb{F}_{2},

the orbit in (i) is mapped to the
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
-orbit
\big\{\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\},\{\beta_{1},\beta_{3}\}\big\}
, which defines a degree
2
unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
upon taking Galois cohomology. The orbit in (ii) maps to
\big\{\{\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\}\big\}
, defining a copy of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
passing to cohomology. In particular, this implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\mathrm{ord},\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{2,Q}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}.

Consequently, denoting the quartic factors corresponding to (i) and (ii) as
G
and
H
respectively,

factors as the product of two quadratics over
\mathbb{Q}_{2,Q}
. We then conclude that
\theta_{\mathrm{dR}}
is described via the following map:

(5.3)

\displaystyle L^{(2),\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\simeq(K_{Q})_{G_{1}}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus K_{H}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\xlongrightarrow{\mathrm{Nm}}K_{Q}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus K^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2},

where, to avoid awkward notation, we again denote
K=\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, and
G_{1}
is either of the quadratic factors of
G
over
K_{Q}
. To use this description, it remains to determine the kernel of the map, factoring through (
5.3
), from the obtained explicit basis of (
5.2
) to the quotient of
K_{Q}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus K^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
by the appropriate unramified classes.
Magma
verifies that this kernel has dimension
5
, from which we can conclude that
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
is finite by Corollary
4.5
. Note that by Remark
5.5
, an upper bound of
5
is the optimal bound we expect from the methods of this paper.

It is a straightforward computation to show that the remaining
3
possibilities for the Galois group of
f
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
give
2
additional factorisations of
F
: either (i)
F
factors as the product of
3
quadratic and one quartic factors, or (ii) as the product of
2
linear and
4
quadratic factors. The procedure for these cases is analogous to the example above. Moreover, it is computationally inexpensive to check this criterion for a given curve, allowing for the automation of this verification process for the appropriate genus
2
curves on the LMFDB.

Theorem 5.7
.

Of the
6,603
genus
2

2
and exactly one rational Weierstrass point on the LMFDB,

, and at least

From the above curves that meet the criterion of Corollary

for

, but can be verified by combining both methods, is the hyperelliptic curve with LMFDB label

5.2.2.

In the genus

5.3.
Case 2: Curves with no RWP

5.3.1.
Genus 2

Although there are genus
3
examples, none of the genus
2
hyperelliptic curves on the LMFDB with no RWP and good ordinary reduction at

satisfy the criterion of Corollary
4.7
. In contrast,

5.5

Consider the hyperelliptic curve

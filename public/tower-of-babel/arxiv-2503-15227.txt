Collective coordinate method for Abelian-Higgs vortices outside the BPS limit


Abstract




1
Introduction

The Lagrangian density that governs the dynamics of the Abelian-Higgs model reads

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}D_{i}\Phi\overline{D_{i}\Phi}+\frac{\lambda}{8}(1-\Phi\overline{\Phi})^{2},

(1)

where
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-iA_{\mu}(x)
and
F_{\mu\nu}(x)=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}(x)-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}(x)
. The metric
\eta^{\mu\nu}=\{+,-,-\}
is considered.

Assuming the temporal gauge
A_{0}=0
, the corresponding field equations are

\begin{split}\partial_{0,0}\Phi&=D_{1}D_{1}\Phi+D_{2}D_{2}\Phi+\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-\Phi\Phi^{*})\Phi,\\
\partial_{0,0}A_{1}&=\partial_{2,2}A_{1}-\partial_{1,2}A_{2}-\frac{i}{2}\left(\Phi^{*}D_{1}\Phi-\Phi(D_{1}\Phi)^{*}\right),\\
\partial_{0,0}A_{2}&=\partial_{1,1}A_{2}-\partial_{1,2}A_{1}-\frac{i}{2}\left(\Phi^{*}D_{2}\Phi-\Phi(D_{2}\Phi)^{*}\right).\end{split}

(2)

For our purposes we will assume the 1-vortex solution.

The next expansion for the fields will be assumed

\begin{split}\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))&=\phi(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))+B(t)\,\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t)),\\
\bar{\Phi}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))&=\overline{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))+B(t)\,\overline{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t)),\\
A_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))&=a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))+B(t)\,\alpha_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t)),\\
A_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))&=a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t))+B(t)\,\alpha_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},X_{1}(t)),\end{split}

(3)

where
\{\phi(x_{1},x_{2})
,
a_{i}(x_{1},x_{2})\}
accounts for the static 1-vortex solution,

\xi(x)=\left(\varphi(x),\overline{\varphi}(x),\alpha_{1}(x),\alpha_{2}(x)\right)^{t}

denotes the only shape mode held by this solution, and
X_{1}(t)
codifies the translation along the
x_{1}
direction. We have chosen the
x_{1}
direction attending to the isotropy of the space, so no generality is lost in this assumption.

Plugging (
3
) into the Lagrangian density (
1
) and integrating over the spatial variables, the associated effective Lagrangian is obtained. Here it is important to mention that, in order to compute the metric on the moduli space in a gauge-invariant way, the following definitions must be taken into account

\begin{split}\partial_{0}\phi(x_{1}-X_{1}(t),x_{2})&:=-\dot{X}_{1}(t)D_{1}\phi(x_{1},x_{2})\,,\\
\partial_{0}a_{1}(x_{1}-X_{1}(t),x_{2})&:=0\,,\\
\partial_{0}a_{2}(x_{1}-X_{1}(t),x_{2})&:=-\dot{X}_{1}(t)B(x_{1},x_{2})\,.\end{split}

(4)

These definitions are natural, since the infinitesimal gauge-invariant translation in the
x_{1}
direction is given by

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1}-\epsilon,x_{2})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},x_{2})-\epsilon\,D_{1}\phi(x_{1},x_{2}),

(5)

\displaystyle a_{1}(x_{1}-\epsilon,x_{2})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),

(6)

\displaystyle a_{2}(x_{1}-\epsilon,x_{2})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})-\epsilon\,B(x_{1},x_{2}),

(7)

which allows to identify the structure of the zero mode. Indeed, it can be verified that these time derivative definitions fulfill the Gauss’ law, showing the robustness of the scheme.

With this approach the following effective Lagrangian is obtained

L=g_{X,X}\dot{X}\dot{X}+g_{B,B}\dot{B}^{2}-V,

(8)

where

g_{X,X}=C_{\dot{X}\dot{X}}+C_{B\dot{X}\dot{X}}B(t)+C_{B^{2}\dot{X}\dot{X}}B^{2}(t),\quad g_{B,B}=\dfrac{1}{2}\,,

(9)

and

V=M+\dfrac{1}{2}\omega^{2}B^{2}(t)+C_{B^{3}}B^{3}(t)+C_{B^{4}}B^{4}(t)\,.

(10)

Here
M
is the mass of the vortex and
\omega
is the frequency associated to the shape mode.
The expressions for the above coefficients are given explicitly in Appendix
A
, and the numerical values are illustrated in Figure
2
for different values of
\lambda
. There, it can be verified that all the coefficients are gauge invariant.

The first aspect that we should highlight is that the coefficient
C_{\dot{X}\dot{X}}
coincides with half of the zero mode norm. Since the norm coincides with the static energy of the vortex, then

C_{\dot{X},\dot{X}}=\dfrac{M}{2},

(11)

in accordance with Samols’s result on the BPS limit, but here we have extended the result even outside of the BPS case. Secondly, the inclusion of the shape mode adds new contributions
C_{B\dot{X}\dot{X}}
and
C_{B^{2}\dot{X}\dot{X}}
to the metric. Nevertheless, as can be seen, the presence of these new terms does not spoil the Kähler structure.

The equations of motion associated to the effective Lagrangian (
8
) are

\displaystyle g_{X,X}\ddot{X}_{1}(t)+\left(C_{B\dot{X}\dot{X}}+2\,C_{B^{2}\dot{X}\dot{X}}B(t)\right)\dot{B}(t)\dot{X}_{1}(t)=0\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\ddot{B}(t)+\left(\omega^{2}-2\,C_{B^{2}\dot{X}\dot{X}}\dot{X}_{1}^{2}(t)\right)B(t)+3\,C_{B^{3}}B^{2}(t)+4\,C_{B^{4}}B^{3}(t)=C_{B\dot{X}\dot{X}}\dot{X}_{1}^{2}(t)\,.

(13)

The coupling between the shape mode and the translational degree of freedom in the previous equations will result in an exchange of energy between them. For example, for an initially excited shape mode it is expected that the coupling with the translational degree of freedom leads to a wobbling motion as the vortex translates, whenever the initial velocity is not null.

A particularly interesting case corresponds to a solution where
X_{1}(t)=v
and
B(t)=\delta
with
v,\delta
constants. The non-homogeneous term in (
13
) forbids
\delta=0
if
v\neq 0
. The constant solution
\delta
for different velocities
v
is depicted in Figure
1
for the range of existence of the shape mode.

Figure 1
:
Amplitude of the internal mode
\delta
as a function of the velocity of the vortex
v
.

It is expected that this solution can capture to some extent the Lorentz contraction of a boosted vortex. At a first glance, it can be seen that, given a value of
\lambda
, the higher the velocity, the higher the amplitude of the shape mode. This behavior is in agreement with the features of the Lorentz contraction.

To fully verify this accordance, let us expand the relativistic total energy of the system in powers of
v^{2}

E\approx M+\dfrac{1}{2}Mv^{2}+\dfrac{3}{8}Mv^{4}+\frac{5}{16}Mv^{6}+\frac{35}{128}Mv^{8}+\mathcal{O}(v^{10}).

(14)

From the effective model (
8
) we know that

E_{eff}=M+\left(C_{\dot{X}\dot{X}}+C_{B\dot{X}\dot{X}}\delta(v)+C_{B^{2}\dot{X}\dot{X}}\delta^{2}(v)\right)v^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\omega^{2}\delta^{2}(v)+C_{B^{3}}\delta^{3}(v)+C_{B^{4}}\delta^{4}(v),

(15)

As expected,
C_{\dot{X}\dot{X}}
accounts for the classical kinetic energy, and
the remaining coefficients are responsible for higher order corrections to the kinetic energy. To compare these new corrections,
\delta(v)
has been fitted to a polynomial of the form
a\,v^{2}+b\,v^{4}
with great accordance, so
E_{eff}
is a polynomial in even powers of
v^{2}
as in (
14
).

Alternative: collective coordinate model based on zero mode. The zero mode of vortex is related to the translation of the vortex as follows:

The gauge-invariant spatial translations of the Higgs and gauge field are given by

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1}+\epsilon,x_{2})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon\,D_{1}\phi(x_{1},x_{2}),

(16)

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},x_{2}+\epsilon)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon\,D_{2}\phi(x,y),

(17)

\displaystyle A_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}+\epsilon)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle A_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-\epsilon\,B(x_{1},x_{2}),

(18)

\displaystyle A_{2}(x_{1}+\epsilon,x_{2})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle A_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon\,B(x_{1},x_{2}).

(19)

If we denote the 1-vortex zero mode by
\zeta=\{\zeta_{\phi},\zeta_{\bar{\phi}},\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\}
it can be verified that

\displaystyle D_{1}\phi\propto\zeta_{\phi},\quad D_{2}\phi\propto i\,\zeta_{\phi},\quad\zeta_{2}\propto-B,

(20)

and that
\zeta_{\phi}=\zeta_{\bar{\phi}}
and
\zeta_{1}=0
.

Following this approach, we may consider the following expansion:

\begin{split}\Phi&=\phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\,D_{1}\phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\gamma(t)\eta_{s\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\gamma(t)D_{1}\eta_{s\phi}(x_{1},x_{2}),\\
A_{1}&=a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})+\gamma(t)\eta_{sA1}(x_{1},x_{2}),\\
A_{2}&=a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\,B(x_{1},x_{2})+\gamma(t)\eta_{sA2}(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\gamma(t)B_{s}(x_{1},x_{2}),\end{split}

(21)

describing the translation of a 1-vortex along the
x_{1}
direction. Here,
\{\eta_{\bar{\phi}},\eta_{\phi},\eta_{sA1},\eta_{sA2}\}
denotes the shape mode of the vortex and
B_{s}=\partial_{1}\eta_{sA2}-\partial_{2}\eta_{sA1}
.

At second order in
\epsilon(t)
we would obtain the following expression for the kinetic term:

\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\prime}(t)^{2}\int\left[|D_{1}\phi|^{2}+B^{2}\right]d^{2}x\,=\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\prime}(t)^{2}\lVert\zeta\rVert^{2}.

(22)

This integral is nothing else than the norm of the zero mode. It can be verified numerically that the value of the integral for all
\lambda
is the vortex mass
M
as expected. Moreover, this integral is the gauge version of
C_{\dot{X}\dot{X}}
.
Finally, the comparison between the normalization of the zero mode and the vortex mass gives the following general identity

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\left(\dfrac{a_{\theta}^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{\lambda}{4}\left(1-f^{2}\right)^{2}\right]rdr=0.

(23)

For
\lambda=1
it is trivially satisfied because the integrand is one of the BPS equations.

GAUGE-INVARIANT DERRICK MODE:

\begin{split}\Phi(x,y)&=\phi(x,y)+\gamma(t)xD_{1}\phi(x,y)\\
A_{1}(x,y)&=a_{1}(x,y)\\
A_{2}(x,y)&=a_{2}(x,y)+\gamma(t)xB(x,y)\end{split}

(24)

This expression fulfills the Gauss’ law, the radial gauge, the correct magnetic field scaling, and its gauge-invariance. These expressions only take into account the x-scaling.
Therefore, the ansatz for the moving vortex with the Derrick mode included is:

\begin{split}\Phi&=\phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\,D_{1}\phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\gamma(t)x_{1}D_{1}\phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\gamma(t)D_{1}(x_{1}D_{1}\phi(x,y)),\\
A_{1}&=a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),\\
A_{2}&=a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\,B(x_{1},x_{2})+\gamma(t)x_{1}B(x_{1},x_{2})+\epsilon(t)\gamma(t)\partial_{1}(xB(x_{1},x_{2})),\end{split}

(25)

COMMENTS ON THE DERRICK MODE RESULTS:

1) Both, the h1 and
\omega_{0}
coefficients are exactly the mass. For the kink case is easy to see why
\omega_{0}
is the mass, but an explicit proof for the vortex is too long.
2) The effective energy when the Derrick mode is assumed fits the exact relativistic energy for higher velocities than the shape mode. 3) Unlike in the shape mode case, the constant deformation that is obtained when the Derrick mode is assumed is the same for all
\lambda
.

Shaoyun Bai, Jae Hee Lee

Abstract
. We define quantum deformations of Adams operations in K K -theory, in the framework of quasimap quantum K K -theory. They provide K K -theoretic analogs of the quantum Steenrod operations from equivariant symplectic Gromov–Witten theory. We verify the compatibility of these operations with the Kähler and equivariant q q -difference module structures, provide sample computations via ℤ / k \mathbb{Z}/k -equivariant localization, and identify them with p p -curvature operators of the Kähler q q -difference connections as studied in Koroteev–Smirnov. We also formulate and verify a K K -theoretic quantum Hikita conjecture at roots of unity, and propose an indirect algebro-geometric definition of quantum Steenrod operations.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to study quantum deformations of Adams operations in
K
-theory induced from the enumerative geometry of genus
0
curves in the context of quasimap counts of holomorphic symplectic quotients. We equate such operations with the analog of
p
-curvature for
q
-difference modules in the Kähler variables and discuss their relation with Frobenius-constant quantizations in the
K
-theoretic setting under the
3D
mirror symmetry correspondence.

1.1.
Background

Before stating the main results, we would like to provide three different contexts which all lead to quantum Adams operations: power operations in topology,
p
-curvature and their
q
-analogs in ODE theory, and symplectic duality in representation theory.

1.1.1.
Quantum deformation of cohomological operations

Gromov–Witten invariants, which originated from enumerating stable maps, give rise to operations on rational cohomology that can be organized in to a Cohomological Field Theory
[
KM94
]
. In particular, genus
0
Gromov–Witten invariants with primary insertions define a quantum deformation of the ordinary cup product. Ordinary cohomology with positive characteristic coefficients carry additional interesting cohomological operations, the Steenrod operations. Parallel to the quantum product, as proposed in
[
Fuk97
]
and pursued in
[
Wil20
,
SW22
]
, one can construct quantum Steenrod operations deforming the Steenrod operations using genus
0
stable maps. These operations have led to unexpected advances in both enumerative aspects of geometric representation theory
[
Lee23
,
BL25
]
and arithmetic study of quantum
D
-modules
[
Che24
]
.

Moduli spaces of stable maps can also be used to define
K
-theoretic counts which carry rich structures
[
GL03
,
Lee04
]
. Steenrod operations, which are the power operations in ordinary cohomology, have their
K
-theoretic cousin given by Adams operations
[
Ada62
]
. In fact, Steenrod operations and Adams operations are closely related under the tight relation between ordinary cohomology and
K
-theory
[
Ati66
]
. The following question arises naturally from a theoretical perspective.

Question 1.1
.

How to construct quantum deformations of Adams operations? How to compare them with quantum Steenrod operations?

1.1.2.
p
-curvature in
q
-difference modules

Let
p
be a prime number. Given a (
t
-)connection over
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(z)\!)
with regular singularity at
z=0

(1.1)

\nabla_{z\partial_{z}}=tz\frac{d}{dz}+A(z),\quad\quad\quad A\in GL(n,\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![z]\!]),

the (Grothendieck–Katz)
p
-curvature of
\nabla
is the
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(z)\!)
-linear endomorphism

(1.2)

\nabla_{z\partial_{z}}^{p}-t^{p-1}\nabla_{z\partial_{z}}.

The
p
-curvature encodes important information of the connection: it is the basic obstruction to formal solvability of the differential equation in characteristic
p
(Cartier), and is the subject of the famous Grothendieck–Katz curvature. The conjecture posits that for a connection over a characteristic
0
field with well-defined mod
p
reduction for almost all primes
p
, it is equivalent that the
p
-curvature of the mod
p
reduction vanishses for almost all primes and that a full set of
algebraic
solutions exists. Moreover, nilpotency behavior of
p
-curvatures imposes strong constraints on connections, which conjecturally characterizes the connection to be geometric (cf.
[
Kat70
]
).

There is a
q
-difference analog of
D
-modules, where the role of the derivation
\frac{d}{dz}
is replaced by
q
-shifting
f(z)\mapsto f(qz)
, and a
q
-difference connection has the form

(1.3)

f(z)\mapsto f(qz)+M(q,z)f(z).

Reducing mod
p
for
D
-modules corresponds to specializing
q
to be a root of unity for
q
-difference modules. In this setting, the
p
-curvature is

(1.4)

M(q,q^{p-1}z)\cdot M(q,q^{p-2}z)\cdot\cdots\cdot M(q,qz)\cdot M(q,z)\ |_{q=\zeta_{p}},

where
\zeta_{p}
denotes a
p
-th root of unity. One can formulate and study a
q
-difference version of the Grothendieck–Katz conjecture, see
[
DV02
]
.

We are interested in
D
-modules and
q
-difference modules of enumerative-geometric origin, namely, quantum
D
-modules and
q
-difference modules arising from quantum cohomology and quantum
K
-theory
[
MO19
,
Oko17
]
. For the former, the second-named author conjectured the equivalence between
p
-curvatures of quantum connections and quantum Steenrod operations, and established the conjecture for a large class of symplectic resolutions
[
Lee23
]
, thereby giving a geometric interpretation of the
p
-curvature in this context. For the latter, in parallel, we are led to the following question.

Question 1.2
.

How to compute
p
-curvatures of Kähler
q
-difference connections using simply describable moduli spaces?

1.1.3.
Large center at roots of unity

Continuing our discussion of (
q
-difference) connections, the
p
-curvatures (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) are both covariantly constant under the (
q
-difference) connection, i.e. they commute with the action of covariant derivatives/
q
-shifts. In other words, they act by
D
-module/
q
-difference endomorphisms
of the
D
-modules/
q
-difference modules, and give rise to a distinguished submodule preserved by the connection which arise from “large centers” in the ring of differential operators. In fact, there is a well-known analogy between reduction mod
p
and specializing the variable
q
to a root of unity in representation theory
[
AJS94
]
, with one manifestation being that large centers naturally arise in both settings.

We are interested in one particular instance of the large center phenomena, known as the
Frobenius-constant quantization

[
BK08
]
, which, for a Poisson variety
X
over a field
k
characteristic
p>0
, is a quantization
A
of
\mathcal{O}(X)
and an algebra map

(1.5)

\Lambda:\mathcal{O}(X)^{(1)}\to Z(A)

such that
\Lambda(f)\equiv f^{p}
mod
\hbar^{p-1}
, where the superscript
(1)
denotes the Frobenius twist. Here
Z(A)
is the center of
A
. In the pioneering work
[
Lon21
, Section 4]
, Lonergan proved that the quantization of Coulomb branches
[
BFN18
]
induced from the loop-equivariant homology of the moduli space of triples actually gives rise to a Frobenius-constant quantization by discretizing the loop rotation. In the
K
-theoretic setting, the multiplicative version of Coulomb branches arise from
K
-homology of moduli of triples, and the loop-equivariant variable
q
can be viewed as the generator of the representation ring of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. In
[
Lon21
, Section 4]
, instead of reducing mod
p
, it is shown that specializing
q
to a root of unity leads to an analog of Frobenius-constant quantization for the multiplicative Coulomb branch.

Under the
3D
mirror symmetry duality
[
IS96
,
Kam22
]
, the representation-theoretic information of the Coulomb branch associated to a pair
(G,V)
, where
G
is a complex reductive group and
V
is a finite-dimensional
G
-representation, should be reflected by enumerative geometry of the Higgs branch variety
T^{*}V/\!\!/\!\!/\!\!/G
, the holomorphic symplectic reduction of the cotangent
T^{*}V
under the natural
G
-action. Therefore, we can ask the following question.

Question 1.3
.

How does one interpret Lonergan’s Frobenius-constant quantization of a multiplicative Coulomb branch in terms of the dual Higgs branch?

Statement of results

In our definition of

Remark 1.6
.

Differently from the setting of Question 1.1, we construct the quantum Adams operators using quasimaps instead of stable maps. We view this as a feature instead of a loss of generality: one particular reason is that quasimap

1.3.
Further directions

The major goal of this paper is to initiate the study of quantum Adams operators and reveal its distinguished position in the recent developments of enumerative geometry. Pursuing the conjectures posted in this paper for concrete examples should be interesting, and pinning down closed-form formulas of quantum Adams operators based on equivariant localization techniques as exhibited in Section
4
may also be meaningful.

We would like to point out one potential line of investigation based on Theorem
1.7
. Note that the formula for
p
-curvature in Definition
5.1
is essentially a quantum multiplication operator for
L^{k}
, whose description is obtained as an iterated product of wall-crossing operators from the associated quantum group action of
U_{\hbar}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q})
by the main theorem of
[
OS22
]
when
X
is a Nakajima quiver variety. Hence, the main results in
loc. cit.
provide a representation-theoretic interpretation of the quantum Adams operator in terms of quantum affine algebras at roots of unity. It is an interesting question to understand the role of quantum Adams operations in representation theory, especially
\mu_{k}
-localization method should make the computation quite approachable. We hope to pursue this relationship in more detail in forthcoming work.

Of course, continuing the discussion in Remark
1.6
, using stable maps, one should be able to construct quantum Adams operations for more general targets building on either the algebro-geometric
[
Lee04
]
or the symplectic
[
AMS23
]
framework. More generally, power operations exist for larger classes of generalized cohomology theories (see e.g.
[
Dev24
, Section 9]
), and it would be interesting to see if they admit quantum deformations.

Organization

2.
Background on quasimaps

2.1.
Stable quasimaps and Higgs branches

In this subsection we briefly review the definition of stable quasimaps into GIT quotients of affine varieties and their relative compactifications following
[
CFKM14
]
and
[
Oko17
]
. We work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
W
be an affine algebraic variety acted on by a reductive algebraic group
G
. Choosing a character
\theta\in\chi(G)
, denote by
W\!\sslash\!G=W\!\sslash_{\theta}\!G
the GIT quotient under the stability condition specified by
\theta
, which is a quasi-projective variety with a proper morphism
W\!\sslash_{\theta}\!G\to W/G
over the affine quotient. We assume that the semi-stable locus
W^{ss}=W^{ss}(\theta)
coincides with the stable locus
W^{s}=W^{s}(\theta)
, and
W^{s}
is nonsingular on which
G
acts freely. Then
W\!\sslash\!G
coincides with the quotient stack
[W^{s}/G]
, which is an open substack of
[W/G]
, and it comes with a polarization
L_{\theta}
.

Write
\mathrm{Pic}^{G}(W)
the group of isomorphism classes of
G
-linearized line bundles on
W
. Given a prestable pointed curve
(C,z_{1},\dots,z_{k})
, a map from
(C,z_{1},\dots,z_{k})
to the quotient stack
[W/G]
encodes the information of a principal
G
-bundle
P\to C
together with a section
u:C\to P\times_{G}W
. In particular, given
L\in\mathrm{Pic}^{G}(W)
, we obtain a line bundle
u^{*}(P\times_{G}L)
on
C
. The degree of
(P,u)\in\mathrm{Map}(C,[W/G])
is the homomorphism

(2.1)

d:\mathrm{Pic}^{G}(W)\to\mathbb{Z},\quad\quad\quad d(L)=\mathrm{deg}(u^{*}(P\times_{G}L)).

Definition 2.1
.

A stable quasimap from
(C,z_{1},\dots,z_{k})
to
W\!\sslash\!G
of degree
d
consists of
(P,u)\in\mathrm{Map}(C,[W/G])
of class

such that all but finitely points (called
base points
)

u(p)\in W^{s}

Any

that is realized as the degree of a quasimap is called an effective class. We will use the notation

to record the degrees of quasimaps in generating series, and use the symbol
[\![z^{\mathrm{eff}}]\!]
to denote the Novikov ring generated by the effective classes
\mathrm{Eff}(X)
.

In this paper, we shall focus on the case when the targets are Higgs branches of cotangent type which is equipped with a polarization. In other words, we consider a finite-dimensional vector space

G

.

Then one can consider the degeneration of the operator

\sum_{d}z^{d}(\mathrm{ev}_{p_{1}}\times\mathrm{ev}_{p_{2}})_{*}\left(\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{vir},\mathsf{QM}_{d}^{\sigma}(X)}\cdot\mathrm{ev}_{k}^{*}(\mathbf{G}^{-1}\mathcal{F})_{eq}^{\boxtimes k}\right)\mathbf{G}^{-1}

to

so that

The degeneration formula yields the operator

for the operator in

3.3.
Quantum Adams operations

Equations (

Definition 3.15

The

Similarly, the

Q\psi_{\tau}^{desc,k}:=Q\Psi_{\tau}^{desc,k}|_{q=\zeta}\in\left(K_{\mu_{k}\times\mathbf{T}}(X)|_{q=\zeta}\right)^{\otimes 2}[\![z^{\mathrm{eff}}]\!].

Remark 3.16
.

Passing to the quotient by the ideal

Specializing

Proposition 3.17

The quantum Adams operator satisfy the following properties.

(1)

For any
\tau\in K_{\mathbf{T}}(\mathfrak{X})

(2)

We would like to point out that there is no analog of Proposition (

) for the quantum Adams operators because

has been specialized to a primitive root of unity. However, the quantum Adams operators satisfy the additivity relation not satisfied by the cyclic powers.

Lemma 3.18
.

Given
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in K_{\mathbf{T}}(X)[\![z^{\mathrm{eff}}]\!]

Proof.

The key observation is

(\mathbf{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G}))^{\boxtimes k}_{eq}=(\mathbf{G}^{-1}\mathcal{F})^{\boxtimes k}_{eq}+(\mathbf{G}^{-1}\mathcal{G})^{\boxtimes k}_{eq}+\text{ induction of classes in }K_{\mu_{k}^{\prime}\ltimes\mathbf{T}^{k}}(X^{k})

where

, a primitive root of unity, the classes from induction on the RHS of (

∎

Remark 3.19

We do not know if there is a quantum counterpart of the relation

4.
Example:
T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n}

In this section we exhibit the nontriviality of the theory by working out the computation for the target

using
\mathbb{Z}/k
-equivariant localization in
K
-theory. The key input is the stable envelope construction of Aganagic–Okounkov
[

]
, which allows one to replace relative insertions with descendant insertions.

For computation we will use

with respect to
\mu_{k}
. The usual
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-localization involves inverting all forms of the form

for
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}-\{0\}
; more generally, for a torus

, localization involves inverting

for all characters

. Here, for
\mu_{k}
-localization, we only invert the forms

where

. We denote the localized ring with the subscript

, i.e.

(4.1)

K_{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{pt})_{loc}:=K_{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{pt})[(1-q^{\mu})^{-1}:1\leq\mu\leq k-1].

4.1.
Quasimap moduli for
X=T^{*}{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}

By the main theorem of
[
AO17
]
, operations involving relative insertions such as quantum Adams operators can be replaced by operations only involving descendant insertions, by considering the structure constants in the
stable basis
when the target
X
is a quiver variety. We first describe the descendant quasimap moduli space (with no insertions) for
X=T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
using its presentation as a Higgs branch (GIT quotient of a cotangent bundle of the quiver representation).

Remark 4.1
.

In general, the moduli space of quasimaps to
X
depends on the presentation of the target
X
as a GIT quotient
X=W/\!\!/G
of an affine variety
W
by a reductive group
G
(more precisely, it depends on the pair
[W/G]\supseteq[W^{ss}/G]
where

denotes the semistable locus,
[

, Proposition 4.6.1]
). For the example of

, its presentation as a hypertoric variety and as a Higgs branch of a quiver gauge theory in fact agree.
∎

Consider the quiver with one vertex with one framing satisfying
\mathrm{rank}(V)=k
and
\mathrm{rank}(W)=n
. On
T^{*}\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)=\{(I,J):I\in\mathrm{Hom}(V,W),J\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,V)\}
one has a natural moment map

(4.2)

\mu=J\circ I:T^{*}{\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)}\to\mathfrak{gl}(k)^{*}.

For the determinant character
\theta:g\mapsto\det(g)
,
\theta
-semistability is equivalent to the condition that
J
is surjective. By taking the GIT quotient of
\mu^{-1}(0)\subseteq T^{*}\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)
for
\theta
one obtains the Higgs branch
X=T^{*}\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
.

We will be focused on the case of
k=1,n\geq 2
. Consider quasimaps from
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
X=T^{*}\mathrm{Gr}(1,n)=T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. Degrees of maps are indexed by integers
\mathbb{Z}\cong H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. The framing
W
determines a tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}
over
C
. The vertex
V
is of weight
1
with respect to the
\mathrm{GL}(1)
-action, and defines a tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}(d)
over
C
for a degree
d
map. Quasimap from the rigid domain
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
into
X\subseteq[\mu^{-1}(0)/\mathrm{GL}(1)]
is the data of a choice of
\mathcal{V}
and a section

(4.3)

f\in H^{0}\left(C,\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{V})\oplus\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}\otimes\hbar^{-1})\right)=H^{0}\left(C,\mathcal{O}(d)^{\oplus n}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-d)^{\oplus n}\otimes\hbar^{-1}\right)

satisfying the moment map condition. For
d\neq 0
, the moment map condition is automatically satisfied, and by the stability condition, the quasimap moduli space is empty for
d<0
. The stability condition amounts to the generic non-vanishing of the nonzero section, hence the quasimap moduli space is identified with

(4.4)

\mathsf{QM}_{d}(X):=\mathbb{P}H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1};\mathcal{O}(d)^{\oplus n}\right).

We omit the superscript
\mu_{k}
for the quasimap moduli spaces even if we work equivariantly under

. If one considers

equivariantly with respect to the action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{rank}W}=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
with equivariant parameters

, then the quasimap moduli space is given as

(4.5)

\mathsf{QM}_{d}(X):=\mathbb{P}H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1};\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}(d)\right);

indeed one may let

to recover the non-equivariant computation.

4.2.
Ingredients of quantum Adams operations

For
X=T^{*}{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}
, take the moduli space of stable quasimaps of degree
d
, denoted
\mathsf{QM}_{d}(X)
. We would like to apply localization for the

-action on

In the fixed-point basis of localized

[

]

4.2.1.
Virtual structure sheaf

First, we compute the virtual structure sheaf.
To compute

(4.6)

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{vir}}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{vir}}\otimes\big(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{vir}}\frac{\det f^{*}(T^{1/2}X)|_{p_{2}}}{\det f^{*}(T^{1/2}X)|_{p_{1}}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we refer the reader to (

) for the explanation of notations, we first compute
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{vir}}=\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathrm{Obs}^{\vee}
; here
\mathrm{Obs}
is the obstruction part to the deformation theory given by

(4.7)

T_{\mathrm{vir}}=H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}\oplus\hbar^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{\vee})-(1+\hbar^{-1})\sum\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V}_{i},\mathcal{V}_{i})=\mathrm{Def}-\mathrm{Obs},

following the notations in
[
Oko17
, Section 4.3.16]
, where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the pullback of the (undoubled) quiver representation datum under the evaluation map. The obstruction part arises as

(4.8)

\mathrm{Obs}=H^{1}(\mathcal{M}\oplus\hbar^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{\vee})+\hbar^{-1}\sum\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V}_{i},\mathcal{V}_{i});

the first term is the obstruction to deforming the section and the second term imposes the moment map constraint. The other terms in

correspond to deformations of the quasimap section and deformations of
\mathcal{V}_{i}

Specializing to degree

By Serre duality,

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{vir}}=\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathrm{Obs}^{\vee}=\prod_{w\in\mathrm{wts}(\mathrm{Obs}^{\vee})}(1-w)=(1-\hbar)\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\prod_{1\leq j\leq d-1}(1-\hbar a_{i}^{-1}q^{j}).

Similar computation for the whole virtual tangent bundle

(4.11)

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{vir}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Def}-\mathrm{Obs}

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}\left(a_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}(d)\right)-H^{0}(\mathcal{O})-\hbar^{-1}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{1}(a_{i}\mathcal{O}(-d))-\hbar^{-1}H^{0}(\mathcal{O})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{d}q^{j}-1-\hbar^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}q^{-j}-\hbar^{-1}

yields

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{vir}}=\Lambda^{top}T^{\vee}_{\mathrm{vir}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{w\in\mathrm{wts}(T^{\vee}_{\mathrm{vir}})}w

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{d}a_{i}q^{-j}\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}(\hbar a_{i}^{-1}q^{j})\cdot 1\cdot\hbar\right)^{-1}

(4.16)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{-1-n(d-1)}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2d}\cdot q^{-nd^{2}}.

If we linearize the action of
(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{q}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
\mathcal{O}(d)|_{p_{2}}=q^{d}\cdot\mathcal{O}(d)|_{p_{1}}

(4.17)

f^{*}(T^{1/2}X)|_{p_{1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{-1}+1,\quad f^{*}(T^{1/2}X)|_{p_{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{-1}q^{d}+1,

we have

(4.18)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{vir}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{vir}}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{vir}}\frac{\det f^{*}(T^{1/2}X)|_{p_{2}}}{\det f^{*}(T^{1/2}X)|_{p_{1}}}\right)^{1/2}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{d}\cdot\frac{\hbar^{(n(d-1)+1))/2}}{q^{nd(d-1)/2}}.

4.2.2.
Equator insertions

As explained in the previous section, one may consider different versions of the quantum Adams operator depending on the type of the equator insertions (relative/descednant). The relationship between the two operators are given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{k-1}K_{\mu_{k}\times\mathbf{T}}(\mathsf{F}_{\ell})}

5.

In this section, we prove that for any Higgs branch target

5.1.

In this subsection, we formulate an “arithmetic” version of the

6.2.1.
Action of quantum Adams operations

Following previous discussions, we turn off the

-action on

to simplify the notations. As in

(6.13)

M_{q,Kah}(X^{!})|_{q=\zeta}:=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}]/(\Phi_{k}(q))\otimes M_{q,Kah}(X^{!}).

This arithmetic reduction of the Kähler quantum

-difference module admits an additional structure of a module over the PSZ quantum

-theory ring.

Proposition 6.12
.

The quantum Adams operators
Q\psi^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}|_{\hbar=q}
, specialized at
\hbar=q
, define an algebra action of the PSZ quantum

-theory ring
QK(X^{!})|_{\hbar=\zeta}:=(K_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\hbar}}(X^{!})[\![{z^{\mathrm{eff}}}]\!],\star)|_{\hbar=\zeta}
on the quantum

-difference module
M_{q,Kah}(X^{!})|_{q=\zeta}
at

.

Proof.

3.11
shows the claim that the quantum Adams operators act as

and

∎

6.2.2.
Action of Lonergan’s Frobenius center

The
q
-difference module

of graded traces specialized to
q=\zeta
also admits an action by a certain algebra, where the action is defined by the following construction from
[
Lon21
]
. Recall that
X
is the spectrum of the convolution algebra given by

(6.14)

\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}(X)=K^{G[\![t]\!]}(\mathcal{R}_{G,N})\cong\bigoplus_{d\in\pi_{1}(G)}\mathcal{O}_{d}

whose quantization is given by

(6.15)

\mathcal{A}:=K^{G[\![t]\!]\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}}(\mathcal{R}_{G,N})\cong\bigoplus_{d\in\pi_{1}(G)}\mathcal{A}_{d}.

The quantization
\mathcal{A}
is linear over
K^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}}(\mathrm{pt})\cong\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}]
, and we denote by
\mathcal{A}_{\zeta}:=\mathcal{A}/(\Phi_{k}(q))
for the quotient by the
k
-th cyclotomic polynomial. The quantized algebra

at

is in general non-commutative, and we denote its center by

[
Lon21
]
constructs the following

-theoretic analog of a “Frobenius-constant quantization” (cf.
[

]
):

Theorem 6.13

(
[
Lon21
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

There is an injective map of algebras

(6.16)

\Lambda:\mathcal{O}\to Z(\mathcal{A}_{\zeta})

that is linear, transports multiplication
r\in K^{G[\![t]\!]}(\mathrm{pt})
to multiplication by the Adams operation
\psi^{k}(r)\in K^{G[\![t]\!]\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}}(\mathrm{pt})/(\Phi_{k}(q))
, and takes
\mathcal{O}_{d}
to
\mathcal{A}_{kd}
.

The map
\Lambda
is the
K
-theoretic analog of the Frobenius-constant quantization in the sense that it centrally embeds the commutative algebra
\mathcal{O}(X)
into its quantization.

The arithmetic reduction of the
q
-difference module of graded traces admits an additional structure of a module over the commutative ring
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
given by the Frobenius center
\Lambda
. The action can be further extended to a ring which should be considered the “mirror” of the PSZ quantum
K
-theory ring:

Proposition 6.14

(cf.
[
BL25
, Proposition 3.14, Proposition 3.15]
)
.

Left multiplication by the central element
\Lambda(x)
,
x\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
defines an algebra action of the ring

(6.17)

B_{z}(X):=\mathbb{Z}[\![z^{d}]\!]\otimes\mathcal{O}_{0}/(1\otimes ab-z^{d}\otimes ba:a\in\mathcal{O}_{-d},b\in\mathcal{O}_{d})

on the
q
-difference module of graded traces
M_{q,eq}(X)|_{q=\zeta}
at
q=\zeta
. Here,
z^{d}\otimes 1\in B_{z}(x)
acts by multiplication by
z^{kd}
.

Proof.

To show that the multiplication is a well-defined action on

(6.18)

M_{q,eq}(X):=R_{q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}][q^{L}]}\mathcal{A}_{0}/(1\otimes ab-z^{d}\otimes ba:a\in\mathcal{A}_{d},b\in\mathcal{A}_{-d}),

we must check that the action factors through the relations

(6.19)

J:=(1\otimes ab-z^{d}\otimes ba:a\in\mathcal{A}_{d},b\in\mathcal{A}_{-d}).

This claim follows from centrality, as for a fixed
x\in\mathcal{O}_{0}
we have

(6.20)

\Lambda(x)(ab-z^{d}ba)=a\left(b\Lambda(x)\right)-z^{d}(b\Lambda(x))a\in J.

To check that the endomorphism respects
R_{q}
-linear structure, so that it is a
q
-difference module endomorphism, we again use the centrality of
\Lambda
. Indeed, for
L\in X^{\bullet}(G)
, we have

(6.21)

\displaystyle q^{L}\Lambda(x)(z^{d}\otimes a)

\displaystyle=q^{L}(z^{d}\otimes\Lambda(x)a)

(6.22)

\displaystyle=q^{\langle L,d\rangle}z^{d}\otimes L\Lambda(x)a

(6.23)

\displaystyle=q^{\langle L,d\rangle}z^{d}\otimes\Lambda(x)La

(6.24)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(x)\left(q^{\langle L,d\rangle}z^{d}\otimes La\right)

(6.25)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(x)\left(q^{L}(z^{d}\otimes a)\right).

Since
\Lambda
is an algebra map, the action naturally defines an algebra action of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
on
M_{q,eq}(X)|_{q=\zeta}
. We extend the action to
\mathbb{Z}[\![z^{d}]\!]\otimes\mathcal{O}_{0}
by letting
z^{d}\otimes 1
act by left multiplication by
z^{kd}
. This action then factors through
B_{z}(X)
, since elements in the relation

(6.26)

1\otimes ab-z^{d}\otimes ba,\quad a\in\mathcal{O}_{-d},\ b\in\mathcal{O}_{d}

of the algebra
B_{z}(X)
act by

(6.27)

1\otimes\Lambda(a)\Lambda(b)-z^{kd}\otimes\Lambda(b)\Lambda(a)\in J,

which is zero in the
q
-difference module
M_{q,eq}(X)
.

∎

6.3.
Example: Abelian gauge theories

As an illustrative example, we verify the
K
-theoretic quantum Hikita conjecture for the Coulomb/Higgs mirrors for the pair for hypertoric varieties. We begin with the rank
1
case to illustrate the structure of the argument, then generalize the argument to the general case where
G
is abelian, i.e., when the varieties
X,X^{!}
are hypertoric.

6.3.1.
Rank 1 abelian gauge theory

We first consider the case of
(G,N)=(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C})
. The general case for
G
abelian is similar, with the additional complexity being purely combinatorial. For
(G,N)=(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C})
, the Coulomb branch is given by
X\cong\mathrm{Spec}K^{\mathbb{G}[\![t]\!]}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C}})
, and the Higgs branch is given by
X^{!}\cong T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{0}
.

First consider the Kähler
q
-difference module. The corresponding variety is
T^{*}\mathbb{C}/\!\!/\!\!/\!\!/\mathbb{G}_{m}\cong T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{0}
as the hypertoric variety associated to the exact sequence of tori
1\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 1\to 1
.

The computation of the Kähler
q
-difference module in the hypertoric case is completed by
[
SZ22
]
. Recall that attached to a hypertoric variety defined from a short exact sequence of tori

(6.29)

1\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{k}\overset{\iota}{\to}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\overset{\beta}{\to}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}\to 1

and a stability condition
\theta\in\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{k})^{\vee}
is a hyperplane arrangement
\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
for
H_{i}:=\{x\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\vee}:\langle x,\beta(e_{i})\rangle=-\langle\widetilde{\theta},e_{i}\rangle\}
where
\widetilde{\theta}\in\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n})^{\vee}
is a lift of
\theta
along
\iota^{\vee}
. The relations of the
q
-difference module are indexed by
circuits
, which are minimal subsets
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\bigcap_{i\in S}H_{i}=\emptyset
; that is, the normal vectors
\beta(e_{i})
must be linearly dependent, hence circuits are minimal subsets such that
e_{i}
have linear dependence relations in
\ker\beta
. Each circuit admits a decomposition
S=S^{+}\sqcup S^{-}
depending on the sign of corresponding
e_{i}
’s in the linear dependence relation. (Primitive) effective curves in smooth hypertoric varieties are in bijection with circuits, see
[
SZ22
, Section 2.5]
.

Lemma 6.17

(
[
SZ22
, Theorem 5.14(1)]
)
.

The Kähler
q
-difference module of
X^{!}
is defined as the ring
K_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\hbar}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{q}}(X)[\![z^{d}]\!]|_{\hbar=q}
modulo the relations

(6.30)

\prod_{i\in S^{+}}(1-q^{L_{i}})\prod_{i\in S^{-}}(1-\hbar q^{L_{i}})-z^{\beta_{S}}\prod_{i\in S^{+}}(1-\hbar q^{L_{i}})(1-q^{L_{i}})

for circuits
S=S^{+}\sqcup S^{-}
and associated curve classes
\beta_{S}
.

Remark 6.18
.

In Smirnov–Zhou, the relations defining the quantum
q
-difference modules are described by “shifted Kähler variables”
z_{\#}^{\beta}:=z^{\beta}(-\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}\beta\cdot\det T^{1/2}X})
. This conventional difference can be remedied by an equivalent choice of polarization
T_{X}^{1/2}=\sum L_{i}-\mathcal{O}^{\oplus k}+(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus k}-\hbar^{-1}\mathcal{O}^{\oplus k})=\sum L_{i}-\hbar^{-1}\mathcal{O}^{\oplus k}
; replacing polarizations up to classes of the form
\mathcal{G}-\hbar^{-1}\mathcal{G}^{\vee}
only acts by rescaling the Kähler variables (cf.
[
Oko17
, 7.5.8]
) and does not affect the isomorphism type as a
q
-difference module.
∎

Corollary 6.19
.

The Kähler
q
-difference module of the Higgs branch
X^{!}
for
(G,N)=(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C})
is defined as
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}][a^{\pm}][\![z]\!]
modulo the unique relation

(6.31)

(1-q^{L})-z(1-\hbar q^{L}).

Proof.

The hyperplane arrangement associated to
X^{!}
is trivially empty, and hence there is a unique circuit
S=S^{+}=\{1\}
giving rise to the relation above.
∎

We point out that the vertex functions (solutions of
q
-difference modules) used to compute the relations manifestly do not have poles at

after the

specialization (see
[

, Proposition 5.3]
), so the
q
-difference module specialized at

do admit

The computation of the
q
-difference module of graded traces is also simple in this case, with the Coulomb branch algebra given by the BFN convolution algebra
K^{\mathbb{G}[\![t]\!]}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C}})
.

Lemma 6.20
.

For the pair
(G,N)=(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C})
, the
K
-theoretic quantized Coulomb branch algebra
\mathcal{A}
is the subalgebra of

(6.32)

K^{G[\![t]\!]\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{G}_{m},0})\cong\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}][y^{\pm}]\langle x^{\pm}\rangle/(yx=qxy)

spanned additively over
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}][y^{\pm}]
by

(6.33)

x_{d}:=\begin{cases}x^{d}&d\leq 0\\
\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(1-y/q^{i})x^{d}&d>0\end{cases}

.

Proof.

This computation can be found in
[

, Example 4.4]
; it is also the

-theoretic analogue of the computation in
[

.
∎

Corollary 6.21
.

The
q
-difference module of graded traces for the Coulomb branch
X
for
(G,N)=(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C})
is defined as
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm}][y^{\pm}][\![z]\!]
modulo the unique relation

(6.34)

(1-y)-z(1-qy).

Proof.

The inclusion
K^{G[\![t]\!]\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{q}}(\mathrm{pt})\to\mathcal{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is the inclusion of the underlying commutative ring

, and in particular

acts by multiplication of

. The relation
1\otimes ab-z^{d}\otimes ba
for

is now generated by

1\otimes x_{1}x_{-1}-z\otimes x_{-1}x_{1}=1\otimes(1-y)xx^{-1}-z\otimes x^{-1}(1-y)x.

Using the relation

to simplify the second term, this is indeed equivalent to

(6.36)

(1-y)-z(1-qy),

as desired.
∎

Therefore we now have the following:

Proposition 6.22
.

6.15
holds for Coulomb/Higgs pair of symplectically dual varieties for
(G,N)=(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

6.19
and

show that the

In the hypertoric case, the quantum

for the Lonergan operators.
∎

6.3.2.

The general case for pairs

Lemma 6.23

Lemma 6.25

The

Proof.

First recall that all relations are of the form

for
d\in\mathbb{N}\{\beta_{S}:S\mbox{ circuit}\}
in the monoid generated by the circuits by definition of the
q
-difference module of graded traces; therefore, the claim is that the ideal of relations is generated by the a priori smaller set
d\in\{\beta_{S}:S\mbox{ circuit}\}

By

6.23

f(y)x_{d}g(y)x_{-d}-z^{d}g(y)x_{-d}f(y)x_{d}

\displaystyle f(y)x_{d}g(y)x_{-d}-z^{d}g(y)x_{-d}f(y)x_{d}

\displaystyle=f(y)g(q^{{-d}}y)x_{d}x_{-d}-z^{d}g(y)f(q^{d}y)x_{-d}x_{d}

\displaystyle=f(y)g(q^{{-d}}y)x_{d}x_{-d}-g(q^{-d}y)f(y)z^{d}x_{-d}x_{d}

\displaystyle=f(y)g(q^{-d}y)x_{d}x_{-d}(1-z^{d}).

where in the last equality we use that

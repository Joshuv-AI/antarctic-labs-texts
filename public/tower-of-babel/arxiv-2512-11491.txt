Existence and dependency results for coupled Schrödinger equations with critical exponent on waveguide manifold This work was partially supported by NNSFC (No. 12171493).
Jun Wang, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
We study the coupled Schrödinger equations with critical exponent on ℝ 3 × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T} . With the help of scaling argument and semivirial-vanishing technology, we obtain the existence and y y -dependence of solution, the tori can be generalized to 1 1 -dimensional compact Riemannian manifold. Moreover, the conclusion of this paper can be extended to systems with any number of components.



Keywords:
Coupled Schrödinger equations; Normalized solutions; Waveguide manifold.

2010 MSC:
35A15, 35B38, 35R01.

1
Introduction and main results

This paper studies the following question

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{x,y}u+\lambda_{1}u=|u|^{2}u+u|v|^{2},\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T},\\
&-\Delta_{x,y}v+\lambda_{2}v=|v|^{2}v+v|u|^{2},\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}v^{2}dxdy=\Theta^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where the mass
\Theta>0
, the frequency
\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{2}\geq 0
. We are interested in the property of solution for (
1.1
), such as existence,
y
-dependence.

1.1 Motivation.
As described in
[
2
]
(also see Theorem 1.6 in
[
8
]
),
the question

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=|u|^{2}u+u|v|^{2},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=|v|^{2}v+v|u|^{2},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\end{aligned}\right.

has no positive least energy solution(positive normalized solution) in the Euclidean space when
\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{2}>0
, this is the direct conclusion of using Pohozaev identity. It is natural to ask whether the same result holds for the coupled Schrödinger equations on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}
? In this paper, we give an negative answer to this question. Indeed, we may find a solution
(u,v,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
satisfies (
1.1
) which is positive and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>0
. Furthermore, we prove the
y
-dependence of normalized solution by using the scaling argument.

(
1.1
) comes from the time-dependent coupled Schrödinger equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\frac{\partial\Phi_{1}}{\partial t}+\Delta\Phi_{1}+\mu_{1}\Phi_{1}^{3}+\nu\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}^{2}=0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T},\\
&i\frac{\partial\Phi_{2}}{\partial t}+\Delta\Phi_{2}+\mu_{2}\Phi_{2}^{3}+\nu\Phi_{2}\Phi_{1}^{2}=0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu_{1},\ \mu_{2}
and
\nu
describe the intraspecies and interspecies scattering lengths. The
sign of
\nu
determines whether the interactions are repulsive or attractive, that is, the interaction is attractive when
\nu>0
and repulsive when
\nu<0
. The existence of solutions has recently received great interest, see
[
2
]
for fixed frequency issue. Another important feature of system is that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}|\Phi_{1}(t,x,y)|^{2}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}|\Phi_{2}(t,x,y)|^{2}dxdy=\Theta^{2}

of the wave functions are conserved. For the existence of normalized solution, we can refer to
[
8
]
for more detail.

Recently, there has been an increasing interest in studying dispersive equations on the waveguide manifolds
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
whose mixed type geometric nature makes the underlying analysis rather challenging and delicate. For the nonlinear Schrödinger model, there have been many results on waveguide manifolds, as shown in
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
9
,
16
,
17
,
14
,
6
,
15
]
. However, there is relatively little research for coupled Schrödinger equation on the waveguide manifolds. Terracini et. al in
[
13
]
consider the Cauchy problems

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{x,y}u-u|u|^{\alpha}=0,\ u|_{t=0}=u_{0},

where
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}\times M_{y}^{k}
,
M_{y}^{k}
is a compact Riemannian manifold. They proved that above a critical mass the ground states have nontrivial
M_{y}^{k}
dependence. After that, Hajaiej et. al in
[
4
]
consider the following question

\Delta_{x,y}^{2}u-\beta\Delta_{x,y}u+\theta u=|u|^{\alpha}u,\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{n},

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{8}{d+n})
. They extend the conclusion in
[
13
]
to four order Schrödinger equation by using new scaling argument. Therefore, it is natural to ask whether the
y
-dependence holds for coupled Schrödinger equations? We give an affirmative answer in this paper.

The natural energy functional associated with the equation (
1.1
) is given by

I(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}[|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{2}+|\nabla_{x,y}v|^{2}]dxdy-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|u|^{4}+2|u|^{2}|v|^{2}+|v|^{4})dxdy.

Normalized solutions to (
1.1
) correspond to critical points of the energy functional
I
restricted on
S_{\Theta}\times S_{\Theta}
, where

S_{\Theta}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}):\|u\|_{2}=\Theta>0\}

and
m_{\Theta}
is defined as

m_{\Theta}=\inf\{I(u,v):(u,v)\in V_{\Theta}\},

where

Q(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}v|^{2})dxdy-\frac{3}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|u|^{4}+2|u|^{2}|v|^{2}+|v|^{4})dxdy

and

V_{\Theta}=\{(u,v)\in S_{\Theta}\times S_{\Theta},Q(u,v)=0\}.

In order to study the periodic dependency results, we define

\widehat{S_{\Theta}}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\|u\|_{2}=\Theta>0\}.

1.2 Main results.
The main results of this paper are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

The problem (
1.1
)
has a couple
(u,v,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
solution for any
\Theta>0
, where
(u,v)
is positive and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>0
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the semivirial-vanishing technology developed by Luo in
[
11
,
10
]
, and we extend it to the system.

Remark 1.1
.

(i) In this paper, we only consider the Sobolev critical case. For mass subcritical case, the existence of solution follows directly
from the boundedness of
I
from below on
S_{\Theta}\times S_{\Theta}
, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on waveguide manifold and a standard minimization argument. For mass supercritical case, the proof method of Theorem
1.1
is valid.

(ii) Unlike the study of normalized solution the critical Schrödinger equation, this article does not use an energy threshold to exclude vanishing and bifurcation.

(iii) The conclusion of Theorem
1.1
can be extended to systems with any number of components, that is, for
k\geq 2
,

-\Delta_{x,y}u_{i}+\lambda_{i}u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{j}^{2}u_{i},(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T},\ i=1,\cdots,k

with the normalization condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}u_{i}^{2}dxdy=\Theta_{i}^{2},\ i=1,\cdots,k.

(iv) We point out that in
[
8
]
, as well as in all
the contributions related to the problem with fixed frequencies, one of the main difficulties is represented by the fact that one searches for solutions having both
u\neq 0
and
v\neq 0
. Under the setting of waveguide manifold, this problem still exists. Moreover, we must prevent the loss of part of the mass of one of the components in the passage to the limit.

(v) As described in
[
8
]
, the equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=|u|^{4}u+\frac{\alpha}{6}|u|^{\alpha-2}v^{\beta}u,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=|v|^{4}v+\frac{\beta}{6}|v|^{\beta-2}u^{\alpha}v,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\end{aligned}\right.

has no positive normalized solution in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
when
\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{2}>0
, where
\alpha>1,\beta>1,\alpha+\beta=6
. The proof method of Theorem
1.1
is also valid for this question.

Borrowing the ideas from
[
13
]
and new scaling techniques, we obtain the following periodic dependency results.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
\Theta\in(0,\infty)
, let
(u_{\Theta},u_{\Theta})
be a minimizer
of
m_{\Theta}
deduced from Theorem
1.1
. Then there exists some
\Theta_{0}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathrm{(1)}
for all
\Theta\in(0,\Theta_{0})
we have
m_{\Theta}<2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{\Theta}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}
. Moreover, for
\Theta\in(0,\Theta_{0})
any minimizer
(u_{\Theta},v_{\Theta})

of
m_{\Theta}

satisfies
(u_{y,\Theta},v_{y,\Theta})\neq 0
.

\mathrm{(2)}
for all
\Theta\in(\Theta_{0},+\infty)
we have
m_{\Theta}=2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{\Theta}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}
. Moreover, for
\Theta\in(\Theta_{0},+\infty)
any minimizer
(u_{\Theta},v_{\Theta})

of
m_{\Theta}

satisfies
(u_{y,\Theta},v_{y,\Theta})=0
.

Remark 1.2
.

(i) The conclusion of Theorem
1.2
can be extended to systems with any number of components, that is, for
k\geq 2
,

-\Delta_{x,y}u_{i}+\lambda_{i}u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{j}^{2}u_{i},(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T},\ i=1,\cdots,k

with the normalization condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}u_{i}^{2}dxdy=\Theta_{i}^{2},\ i=1,\cdots,k.

Different from Theorem
1.1
, we require each component to have the same mass, that is,
\Theta_{1}^{2}=\cdots=\Theta_{k}^{2}
. An interesting question is whether there is still a periodic dependency conclusion when
\Theta_{i}^{2}\neq\Theta_{j}^{2},\ i\neq j,\ i=1,\cdots,k
?

(ii) For
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T},d\geq 3
, we may obtain similar results for (
1.1
). If
d=2
, we can observe an interesting phenomenon, that is, (
3.1
) does not have scaling property.

The structure of this paper is arranged as follows. In section 2, we study the existence of normalized solution. Next, we will study
y
-dependence of the solution.

2
Existence of solution

In this section, we are devoted to the existence of normalized solution.
In order to deal with the non-vanishing
weak limit of a minimizing sequence, we use a scale-invariant Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}
, the proof can be found in
[
10
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists some
C>0
such that for all
u\in X
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}|u|^{4}dxdy\leq C\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{2}^{3}\|u\|_{2}^{2}(\|u\|_{2}+\|u_{y}\|_{2}).

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\Theta\in(0,\infty)
we have
m_{\Theta}\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Assume
\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)_{n}\subset V_{\Theta}
be a minimizing sequence such that
I\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)=m_{\Theta}+o_{n}(1)
and
Q(u_{n},v_{n})=o_{n}(1)
, one has

\displaystyle m_{\Theta}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)-\frac{1}{3}Q\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|u_{y,n}|^{2}+|v_{y,n}|^{2})dxdy+\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}[|\nabla_{x}u_{n}|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}v_{n}|^{2}]dxdy,

which implies that
\left(u_{n},v_{n}\right)_{n}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}\times H^{1}
. Moreover, by using Proposition
2.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|\nabla_{x}u_{n}|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}v_{n}|^{2})dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|u_{n}|^{4}+2|u_{n}|^{2}|v_{n}|^{2}+|v_{n}|^{4})dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|u_{n}|^{4}+|v_{n}|^{4})dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}|\nabla_{x}u_{n}|^{2}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}|\nabla_{x}v_{n}|^{2}dxdy\right)^{\frac{3}{2}},

then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|u_{n}|^{4}+|v_{n}|^{4})dxdy\sim\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|\nabla_{x}u_{n}|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}v_{n}|^{2})dxdy.

Hence,

\displaystyle m_{\Theta}\geq\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|\nabla_{x}u_{n}|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}v_{n}|^{2})dxdy\geq C>0,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5

3
Dependency of the ground states

In order to study the
y
-dependence of the ground states, we need some notation on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Define

\widehat{m}_{\rho}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|u\|_{2}=\|v\|_{2}=\rho,\\
(u,v)\in H^{1}\times H^{1}\end{subarray}}\widehat{I}(u,v),

where

\widehat{I}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u|^{4}+2|u|^{2}|v|^{2}+|v|^{4})dx.

(3.1)

Let
(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})=(\rho^{-2}u(\rho^{-2}x),\rho^{-2}v(\rho^{-2}x))
, we have

\widehat{m}_{\rho}=\rho^{-2}\widehat{m}_{1}.

It is well known that
+\infty>\widehat{m}_{\rho}>0
for all
\rho>0
. On the one hand, assume that
(u,v)
is the critical point to (
3.1
), we have the following Pohozaev type identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}dx=\frac{3}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}dx+\frac{3}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{4}dx+\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}|v|^{2}dx.

On the other hand,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}dx+\omega_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}|v|^{2}dx

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}dx+\omega_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{4}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}|v|^{2}dx.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\omega_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx+\omega_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{4}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}|v|^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\widehat{m}_{\|u\|_{2}}

and

\widehat{m}_{\|u\|_{2}}=\widehat{I}(u)=\frac{1}{6}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}dx\right).

Next, we consider an auxiliary problem, that is, the minimization problems

m_{1,\mu}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|u\|_{L_{x,y}^{2}}=\|v\|_{L_{x,y}^{2}}=1,\\
(u,v)\in H^{1}\times H^{1}\end{subarray}}J_{\mu}(u,v),

where

J_{\mu}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}\left(|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}+\mu|u_{y}|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}+\mu|v_{y}|^{2}\right)dxdy-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}(|u|^{4}+2|u|^{2}|v|^{2}+|v|^{4})dxdy.

3.1

Now, we consider the case

.

Lemma 3.1
.

\lim\limits_{\mu\rightarrow 0}m_{1,\mu}<2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}

Proof.

Define the function
\varphi:[0,2\pi]\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
by

\varphi(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,\ y\in[0,a]\cup[2\pi-a,2\pi],\\
&b(y-a),\ y\in[a,\pi],\\
&\varphi(2\pi-y),\ y\in[\pi,2\pi-a],\end{aligned}\right.

where

and

. Now, we determine the value of
b
. By using direct calculations, it follows that

\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{2b^{2}(\pi-a)^{3}}{3},\ \|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{4}}^{4}=\frac{2b^{4}(\pi-a)^{5}}{5}.

Let
b=\left[\frac{5(\pi-a)^{-2}}{3}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we get
\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}=\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{4}}^{4}
.
Hence,

\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{2(\pi-a)^{3}}{3}\cdot\left[\frac{5(\pi-a)^{-2}}{3}\right]=\frac{10(\pi-a)}{9}.

Let
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
be the
\varepsilon
-mollifier of
\varphi
on
[0,2\pi]
with some to be determined small
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, since
\varphi
has compact support in
(0,2\pi)
, so is
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon\ll 1
. Next, let
(Q,Q)
be an optimizer of
\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}}}
and define

\psi(x,y):=Q(x)\cdot\frac{\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}}{\left\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{y}^{2}}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\left(y\right).

This is to be understood that we extend
\varphi_{\varepsilon}

2\pi
-periodically along the
y
-direction, which is possible since
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
has compact support in
(0,2\pi)
when
\varepsilon\ll 1
. We have then
\|\psi\|_{L_{x,y}^{2}}=1
. Moreover,

\displaystyle J_{0}(\psi,\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}|\nabla_{x}\psi|^{2}dxdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}|\psi|^{4}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{4}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{-4}\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{4}}^{4}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{y}^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}\left(\|\nabla_{x}Q\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}-\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{4}}^{4}\right)-\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}\left(\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{-4}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{y}^{4}}^{4}-1\right)\|Q\|_{L_{x}^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}^{2}\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\|\varphi\|_{L_{y}^{2}}}}+I.

(w_{1,\mu_{j}},w_{2,\mu_{j}})=0
for
\mu_{j}
large enough.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists some
\mu^{*}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(1) for all
\mu\in(0,\mu^{*})
we have
m_{1,\mu}<2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}
. Moreover, for
\mu\in(0,\mu^{*})

any minimizer
(u_{\mu},v_{\mu})
of
m_{1,\mu}
satisfies
(u_{y,\mu},v_{y,\mu})\neq 0
;

(2) for all
\mu\in(\mu^{*},\infty)
we have
m_{1,\mu}=2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}
. Moreover, for
\mu\in(\mu^{*},\infty)

any minimizer
(u_{\mu},v_{\mu})
of
m_{1,\mu}
satisfies
(u_{y,\mu},v_{y,\mu})=0
.

Proof.

Define

\mu^{*}:=\inf\{\mu\in(0,\infty):m_{1,\mu}=2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}},\ \forall\mu\geq\mu^{*}\}.

The existence of
\mu^{*}
follows by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
. The fact that a minimizer of

m_{1,\mu}
has nontrivial
y
-dependence and
m_{1,\mu}<2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}
for
\mu<\mu^{*}
follows already
from the definition of
\mu^{*}
and the fact that
\mu\mapsto m_{1,\mu}
is monotone increasing.

Now, we claim that any minimizer of
m_{1,\mu}
for
\mu>\mu^{*}
must be
y
-independent. In fact, assume that an optimizer
(u_{\mu},v_{\mu})
of
m_{1,\mu}
satisfies
\|u_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\neq 0
and
\|v_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\neq 0
.
Then there exists some
\mu^{**}\in(\mu^{*},\mu)
. Consequently,

2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}=m_{1,\mu^{**}}\leq J_{\mu^{**}}(u_{\mu})=J_{\mu}(u_{\mu})+\frac{\mu^{**}-\mu}{2}(\|u_{y,\mu}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{y,\mu}\|_{2}^{2})<J_{\mu}(u_{\mu})=m_{1,\mu}=2\pi\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}},

which is a contradiction. We also get contradiction by similar argument when

\|v_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\neq 0(\|u_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}=0)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
By computation, we have that the map

S_{1}\noindent u\rightarrow\tau^{-2}u(\tau^{-2}x,y)\in S_{\tau}

where

S_{\tau}=\{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}):\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}=\tau\}

is a bijection. Moreover,

\displaystyle m_{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I(\tau^{-2}u(\tau^{-2}x,y),\tau^{-2}v(\tau^{-2}x,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}+\tau^{4}|u_{y}|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}+\tau^{4}|v_{y}|^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}(|u|^{4}+2|u|^{2}|v|^{2}+|v|^{4})\right]dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-2}m_{1,\tau^{4}}.

It follows from the same rescaling arguments that
\widehat{m}_{\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}=\tau^{-2}\widehat{m}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}
for
\tau>0
. Thus, by Lemma

, there exists

Efficient Plug-and-Play Method for Dynamic Imaging via Kalman Smoothing
Benjamin Hawkes, Mike Davies, Víctor Elvira, Audrey Repetti

Abstract
State-space models (SSM) are common in signal processing, where Kalman smoothing (KS) methods are state-of-the-art. However, traditional KS techniques lack expressivity as they do not incorporate spatial prior information. Recently, [ 8 ] proposed an ADMM algorithm that handles the state-space fidelity term with KS while regularizing the object via a sparsity-based prior with proximity operators. Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods are a popular type of iterative algorithms that replace proximal operators encoding prior knowledge with powerful denoisers such as deep neural networks. These methods are widely used in image processing, achieving state-of-the-art results. In this work, we build on the KS-ADMM method, combining it with deep learning to achieve higher expressivity. We propose a PnP algorithm based on KS-ADMM iterations, efficiently handling the SSM through KS, while enabling the use of powerful denoising networks. Simulations on a 2D + + t imaging problem show that the proposed PnP-KS-ADMM algorithm improves the computational efficiency over standard PnP-ADMM for large numbers of timesteps.

Introduction

Inverse imaging problems consist of inferring an unknown image from noisy measurements. In this work, we assume that at time
t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}
, for
T\in{\mathbb{N}}
, an unknown image
\overline{x}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is observed through a linear model given by

y_{t}=H_{t}\overline{x}_{t}+r_{t}

(1)

where
y_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
is the measurement,
H_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times N}
is the linear measurement operator, and
r_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
is a realization of a white Gaussian random variable with covariance
R_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times M}
.
Inverse problems are widespread in signal processing, with applications from astronomy to medicine and beyond. We further consider dynamic inverse problems where the unknown images are linked together through a linear system given by

(\forall t\in\{2,\ldots,T\})\hskip 10.00002pt\overline{x}_{t}=A_{t}\overline{x}_{t-1}+q_{t},

(2)

where
A_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
is the linear state transition operator and
q_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is a realization of a white Gaussian random variable with covariance
Q_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
.
The resulting framework (
1
)-(
2
) is often referred to as a linear Gaussian state space model (SSM) when
T>1

[
22
]
.
In imaging, SSMs have been used to model temporal evolution across frames to perform different video processing tasks such as super-resolution
[
7
]
and denoising
[
3
]
.

In the case of
T=1
, one can define the estimate
\widehat{x}_{t}
of
\overline{x}_{t}
to be a minimizer of a regularized least-squares problem. Recently, PnP methods have gained popularity in image restoration for achieving high-quality reconstructions by combining iterative proximal methods
[
6
]
with deep learning. Usually, the proximal operator encoding the regularization is replaced by a powerful denoiser
[
12
]
. This approach has been used within different proximal schemes, including ADMM
[
20
,
5
]
, half-quadratic splitting
[
24
]
, forward-backward
[
13
,
18
]
or primal-dual algorithms
[
9
]
.
In particular,
[
21
,
11
]
provide theoretical guarantees for the ADMM-PnP for suitable choices of denoisers.
Further,
[
15
]
recently proposed a PnP-ADMM method for when
T>1
using a video denoiser (processing the full video), only handling (
1
), and discarding the dynamical state model (
2
).

For more generic cases where
T>1
, Kalman-based techniques are state-of-the-art
[
22
]
, but they usually do not use additional prior knowledge on the target images
(\overline{x}_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}
(e.g., sparsity or smoothness, possibly in a transformed domain such as wavelets). To overcome this, hybrid methods mixing Kalman-based techniques and proximal-based algorithms have appeared recently
[
2
,
1
]
.
In particular, the authors in
[
8
]
consider an ADMM algorithm, where a Kalman smoother (KS) and an iterated extended KS are used to implement steps of the ADMM associated with the SSM. The resulting KS-ADMM has shown to be more computationally efficient than standard ADMM as the state estimation for multiple frames is less computationally expensive.

In this work, we propose a PnP KS-ADMM approach, where, at each iteration, a KS step handling the SSM is combined with a denoising network. The resulting algorithm efficiently couples the frames through smoothing recursions rather than large-batch ADMM solves, while achieving high-quality reconstruction via powerful PnP denoisers.
We will show through simulations that the resulting PnP KS-ADMM enables PnP-type quality image reconstructions, whilst enabling significant speeding up of the reconstruction for long time sequences.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section
2
introduces relevant background. Section
3
outlines the proposed model. Section
4
, gives simulation results on a synthetic
2D+t
deblurring task and we show its computational benefits over a standard PnP-ADMM algorithm. We conclude in Section
5
.

2
Background

We denote sequences of
T
variables with bold, i.e.,
{\mathb{x}}=(x_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times T}
. Similarly, with a slight abuse of notation, for a collection of linear operators
(H_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times N}
we denote
{\mathb{H}}{\mathb{x}}=(H_{t}x_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times T}
.

Our goal is to find an estimate
\widehat{{\mathb{x}}}
of
\overline{{\mathb{x}}}
from the noisy measurements
{\mathb{y}}
obtained through (
1
)-(
2
). Since the measurement and transition operators
(H_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}
and
(A_{t})_{2\leq t\leq T}
are assumed linear, a KS within a maximum likelihood framework can be used
[
19
]
.

2.1
Maximum
a Posteriori
framework

We adopt a maximum
a posteriori
approach, and define the estimate
\widehat{{\mathb{x}}}
as

\widehat{{\mathb{x}}}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{{\mathb{x}}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;\;F({\mathb{x}})+G({\mathb{x}}),\vskip-5.69054pt

(3)

where
F\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is the data fidelity term associated with the SSM (
1
)-(
2
), and
G\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times T}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is a regularization term incorporating
prior
information we have on the target images
\overline{{\mathb{x}}}
.
In particular,
F
can be chosen to be the least-squares criterion

F({\mathb{x}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|y_{t}-H_{t}x_{t}\|^{2}_{R_{t}^{-1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\|x_{t}-A_{t}x_{t-1}\|^{2}_{Q_{t}^{-1}}.

For
G
, we choose a standard regularization given by

G({\mathb{x}})=\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}-m_{1}\|^{2}_{P_{1}^{-1}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{t}(W_{t}x_{t})

(4)

where
m_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is a
prior
average image for
\overline{x}_{1}
and
P_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
a symmetric positive definite matrix. If
m_{1}={\mathbf{0}}_{N}
and
P_{1}
is the identity matrix, then the first term in (
4
) corresponds to a Tikhonov regularization (also known as ridge regression). In addition, for every
t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}
,
g_{t}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{S}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
models a spatial regularization for each image, in the transformed domain induced by
W_{t}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{S}
. Standard choices for
(g_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}
are the
\ell_{1}
or
\ell_{2}
norms, possibly combined with a wavelet transform or a total variation gradient operator encoded by
(W_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}

[
4
]
.

2.2
ADMM and KS-ADMM iterations

A standard method for solving (
3
) consists in using the ADMM algorithm. In this context, Problem (
3
) is associated with an augmented Lagrangian
[
17
,
14
]
, given by

{\mathcal{L}}({\mathb{x}},{\mathb{w}},{\boldsymbol{\eta}})=F({\mathb{x}})+\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}-m_{1}\|^{2}_{P_{1}^{-1}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{t}(w_{t})\\
+{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{\top}({\mathb{w}}-{\mathb{W}}{\mathb{x}})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|{\mathb{w}}-{\mathb{W}}{\mathb{x}}\|^{2}.

(5)

where
{\mathb{w}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{S\times T}
is an auxiliary variable induced by
(W_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}
,
\rho>0
is a regularization parameter to enforce similarity between
{\mathb{w}}
and
(W_{t}x_{t})_{1\leq t\leq T}
, and
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{S\times T}
is the dual variable (also known as the Lagrange multiplier).
Then, an iteration
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
of the ADMM algorithm can be written as

\displaystyle{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{{\mathb{x}}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;\;{\mathcal{L}}({\mathb{x}},{\mathb{w}}^{(k)},{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}),

(6)

\displaystyle{\mathb{w}}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{{\mathb{w}}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;\;{\mathcal{L}}({\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)},{\mathb{w}},{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}),

(7)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}+\rho({\mathb{w}}^{(k+1)}-{\mathb{W}}{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}).

(8)

By denoting
{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{-1}=\rho{\mathb{I}}
and defining, for every
{\mathb{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times T}
and
{\mathb{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{S\times T}
,

\varphi({\mathb{x}},{\mathb{z}})\!=\!F({\mathb{x}})\!+\!\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}-m_{1}\|^{2}_{P_{1}^{-1}}\!+\!\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathb{W}}{\mathb{x}}-{\mathb{z}}\|^{2}_{{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{-1}},

(9)

step (
6
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\mathb{z}}_{\rho}^{(k)}=\rho^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}+{\mathb{w}}^{(k)}

(10)

\displaystyle{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{{\mathb{x}}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;\;\varphi({\mathb{x}},{\mathb{z}}_{\rho}^{(k)})

(11)

and step (
7
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\mathb{w}}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{{\mathb{w}}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;\;\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{t}(w_{t})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{\rho}{2}\|{\mathb{W}}{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}-{\mathb{w}}+\rho^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}\|^{2},

\displaystyle=\Big({\mathrm{prox}}_{g_{t}}(W_{t}x_{t}^{(k+1)}+\rho^{-1}\eta_{t}^{(k)})\Big)_{1\leq t\leq T}

(12)

where
{\mathrm{prox}}
denotes the proximity operator
[
6
,
16
]
.

2.2.1
ADMM computation of
{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}

Problem (
11
) admits a closed-form solution given by

{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}=\big({\mathb{H}}^{\top}{\mathb{R}}^{-1}{\mathb{H}}+{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\mathb{Q}}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}+\rho{\mathb{W}}^{\top}{\mathb{W}}\big)^{-1}\\
\big({\mathb{H}}^{\top}{\mathb{R}}^{-1}{\mathb{y}}+{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\mathb{Q}}^{-1}{\mathb{m}}+\rho{\mathb{W}}^{\top}({\mathb{w}}^{(k)}+\rho^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)})\big)

(13)

where
{\mathb{m}}=(m_{1},0,\ldots,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times T}
and

{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}{\mathb{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2}-A_{2}x_{1},\ldots,x_{T}-A_{T}x_{T-1})
.

When
T
and
N
are large, computing
{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}
according to (
13
) becomes untractable. To overcome this issue, a solution to (
11
) can be approximated using a gradient descent (GD) or conjugate gradient (CG) sub-iterations.
In practice, doing only one iteration of either the GD or CG has also good asymptotic behavior, while reducing drastically the computation load
[
17
]
. In this case, the GD iteration to approximate (
11
) is given by

{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}={\mathb{x}}^{(k)}-\nabla_{{\mathb{x}}}\varphi({\mathb{x}}^{(k)},{\mathb{w}}^{(k)},{\mathb{z}}_{\rho}^{(k)}).

(14)

Similarly, the CG to approximate (
11
) is given by

{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}={\mathb{x}}^{(k)}-\alpha_{k}\,\nabla_{{\mathb{x}}}\varphi({\mathb{x}}^{(k)},{\mathb{w}}^{(k)},{\mathb{z}}_{\rho}^{(k)}),

(15)

where
\alpha_{k}=({\mathb{p}}_{k}^{\top}{\mathb{p}}_{k})/({\mathb{p}}_{k}^{\top}\nabla^{2}_{{\mathb{x}}}\varphi({\mathb{x}}^{(k)},{\mathb{w}}^{(k)},{\mathb{z}}_{\rho}^{(k)}){\mathb{p}}_{k})
and
{\mathb{p}}_{k}=-\nabla_{{\mathb{x}}}\varphi({\mathb{x}}^{(k)},{\mathb{w}}^{(k)},{\mathb{z}}_{\rho}^{(k)})
.

2.2.2
KS-ADMM computation of
{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}

The objective function
\varphi
given in (
9
) appears to be the same that is optimized when using a KS for solving (
1
)-(
2
). Hence, instead of using either (
13
) or (
14
), a KS can be used to compute
{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}
within the ADMM iterations, as proposed in
[
8
]
. This KS can be decomposed in four steps, described below. For simplicity, we here drop the dependency on iterations
k
apart for the variables
(x_{t}^{(k)})_{1\leq t\leq T}
and
(z_{\rho,t}^{(k)})_{1\leq t\leq T}
(introduced in (
10
)).

We first compute the prediction and update steps, going forward in time for
t\in\{2,\ldots,T\}
, i.e.,

1.

Prediction step:

\displaystyle m_{\bar{t}}

\displaystyle=A_{t}m_{t-1}

\displaystyle P_{\bar{t}}

\displaystyle=A_{t}P_{t-1}A_{t}^{\top}+Q_{t}

where
m_{\bar{t}}
and
P_{\bar{t}}
are the predicted values of the mean and covariance at time
t
using the dynamic model and
m_{t-1}
and
P_{t-1}
is the result of the forward filtering recursion at timestep
t-1
.

2.

Update step for
y_{t}
:

\displaystyle S_{y,t}

\displaystyle=H_{t}P_{\bar{t}}H_{t}^{\top}+R_{t}

\displaystyle K_{y,t}

\displaystyle=P_{\bar{t}}H_{t}^{\top}S_{y,t}^{-1}

\displaystyle m_{y,t}

\displaystyle=m_{\bar{t}}+K_{y,t}(y_{t}-H_{t}m_{\bar{t}})

\displaystyle P_{y,t}

\displaystyle=P_{\bar{t}}-K_{y,t}S_{y,t}K_{y,t}^{\top}

where
S_{y,t}
is the innovation covariance for
y_{t}
,
K_{y,t}
is the Kalman gain for
y_{t}
and
m_{y,t}
and
P_{y,t}
are the mean and covariance when
y_{t}
is considered.

3.

Update step for
z_{\rho,t}^{(k)}=\rho^{-1}\eta_{t}^{(k)}+w_{t}^{(k)}
:

\displaystyle S_{z,t}

\displaystyle=W_{t}P_{y,t}W_{t}^{\top}+{\Sigma}

\displaystyle K_{z,t}

\displaystyle=P_{y,t}W_{t}^{\top}S_{z,t}^{-1}

\displaystyle m_{t}

\displaystyle=m_{y,t}+K_{z,t}({z_{\rho,t}^{(k)}}-W_{t}m_{y,t})

\displaystyle P_{t}

\displaystyle=P_{y,t}-K_{z,t}S_{z,t}K_{z,t}^{\top}

where
S_{z,t}
and
K_{z,t}
are the innovation covariance and the Kalman gain for
z_{\rho,t}^{(k)}
, respectively,
\Sigma=\rho^{-1}{\mathb{I}}
and
m_{z,t}
and
P_{z,t}
are the image mean and covariance when
z_{\rho,t}^{(k)}
is considered.

Then, the final step is a smoothing backward recursion, i.e., for
t\in\{T-1,\ldots,1\}
.

4.

Smoothing step:

We initialize with
x_{T}^{(k+1)}=m_{T}
and
P_{s,T}=P_{T}
, and compute

\displaystyle G_{t}

\displaystyle=P_{t}A_{t}P_{\overline{t+1}}^{-1}

\displaystyle x_{t}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=m_{t}+G_{t}(x_{t+1}^{(k+1)}-m_{\overline{{t}+1}})

\displaystyle P_{s,t}

\displaystyle=P_{t}+G_{t}(P_{s,t+1}-P_{\bar{t}+1})G_{t}^{\top}\vskip-5.69054pt

where
G_{t}
is the smoothing gain, and
P_{s,t}
is the covariance associated with
x_{t}^{(k+1)}
.

Since the Kalman smoother solves the same problem as Equation
13
, but leverages the dynamics of the problem, it has been shown to be more computationally efficient when working with a large number of timesteps
[
8
]
. Further, the output of the
smoothing step
also quantifies the uncertainty of
{\mathb{x}}^{(k+1)}
, to some extent, via the definition of its covariance matrices
(P_{s,t})_{1\leq t\leq T}
.

5
Conclusion

We have proposed a new algorithm, KS PnP-ADMM, enabling the use of learned regularizer for solving dynamic imaging problems. We shown through simulations on a
2D+t
deblurring problem, that the KS PnP-ADMM is more computationally efficient than batch solutions for a high number of frames. Future work includes generalization to non-linear dynamic or measurement models.

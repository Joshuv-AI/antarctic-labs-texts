Category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} for p p -adic rational Cherednik algebras
Fernando Peña Vázquez

Abstract
. We introduce the concept of a triangular decomposition for Banach and Fréchet-Stein algebras over p p -adic fields, which allows us to define a category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} for a wide array of topological algebras. In particular, we apply this concept to p p -adic rational Cherednik algebras, which allows us to obtain an analytic version of the category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} developed by Ginzburg, Guay, Opdam and Rouquier. Along the way, we study the global sections of p p -adic Cherednik algebras on smooth Stein spaces, and determine their behavior with respect to the rigid analytic GAGA functor.



1.
Introduction

This paper is a continuation of
[
26
]
, where we developed a theory of Cherednik algebras on smooth rigid analytic varieties with an action of a finite group. In this paper, we will start investigating the representation theory of such algebras, focusing on the rational case. In particular, the main goal of this paper is showing that
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebras are Fréchet-Stein, and that they can be endowed with a
triangular decomposition
as Fréchet-Stein algebras. As we will see below, any
Fréchet-Stein algebra
\mathscr{R}
with a triangular decomposition admits a category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
. This is a full abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
(the category of co-admissible modules of
\mathscr{R}
, see below for details) which has a canonical structure as a highest weight category, and such that its irreducible objects are determined by the triangular decomposition.

In the case of the rational Cherednik algebra
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
, this will allow us to obtain a highest weight category contained in
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G))
, which contains all co-admissible Verma modules, and such that its irreducible objects are in natural bijection with the irreducible
K
-linear representations of
G
.

Let us now give an overview of these notions: For the rest of the introduction, let us fix a
p
-adic field
K
. The concept of a triangular decomposition of a graded algebra was introduced by Ginzburg, Guay, Opdam and Rouquier in
[
15
]
. Let us start by recalling the definition:

Definition
.

A graded
K
-algebra
R
is admits a triangular decomposition when there are graded algebras
A,B,H\subset R
satisfying that there is a decomposition:

R=A\otimes_{K}H\otimes_{K}B.

These algebras must also satisfy the following properties:

(i)

B\cdot H=H\cdot B
and
H\cdot A=A\cdot H
.

(ii)

A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
,
B=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}B_{n}
,
A_{0}=B_{0}=K
, and
H\subset R_{0}
.

(iii)

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
are finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces.

(iv)

H
is a finite dimensional semi-simple split
K
-algebra.

(v)

The grading on
R
is inner. That is, there is some
\partial\in R_{0}
such that:

R_{n}=\{x\in R\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }[\partial,x]=nx\}.

We denote by
AH
and
BH
the subalgebras of
R
given by
A\otimes_{K}H
and
B\otimes_{K}H
respectively.

As an example, notice that any Weyl algebra admits a triangular decomposition. To any such algebra, one can attach a category
\mathcal{O}\subset\operatorname{Mod}(R)
, which is an abelian (in fact highest weight) category satisfying that its irreducible objects are in bijection with the irreducible representations of
H
. Any irreducible object in
\mathcal{O}
arises as the unique maximal quotient of a Verma module:

\Delta(W)=R\otimes_{BH}W=A\otimes_{K}W,

where
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. Furthermore, these objects are the standard objects in the highest weight category structure of
\mathcal{O}
. Any object
M\in\mathcal{O}
decomposes as a direct sum of generalized eigenspaces for the action of
\partial
, and it can be shown that each eigenspace is a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space. The details of this construction can be found in the body of the paper. In particular in Section
2.1
.

Now that the board has been set, let us discuss the contents of the paper. As mentioned above, our primary goal is obtaining suitable versions of the triangular decomposition and the associated category
\mathcal{O}
for Banach and Fréchet-Stein algebras. Let us handle the Banach case first. Formulating a Banach version of the category
\mathcal{O}
poses a few technical problems, which are mostly analytic in nature. Namely, an important part of the theory of the algebraic category
\mathcal{O}
uses the fact that every
M\in\mathcal{O}
decomposes as a direct sum of finite-dimensional generalized eigenspaces. Hence, if one wishes to generalize the theory to the analytic setting, it is necessary to obtain a good theory of decomposition of Banach spaces into generalized eigenspaces of a bounded endomorphism.

A good analysis of the situation was obtained by C.T. Féaux de Lacroix in
[
14
]
. Furthermore, his results were successfully employed by T. Schmidt in
[
31
]
, and N. Dupré in
[
10
]
, where analytic versions of the category
\mathcal{O}
for the Arens-Michael envelope of a reductive Lie algebra (resp. quantum Arens-Michael envelope) were obtained. Unfortunately, the results in
[
14
]
only deal with the case where the operator acts diagonally on each of the generalized eigenspaces, and this is not the situation we find ourselves in. To remedy this, we will dedicate Section
2.2
to discussing several types of decompositions of a Banach space into a sum of generalized eigenspaces, and analyzing their properties.

Let us discuss this in a little more detail. Let
V
be a Banach space over
K
, and let
\partial:V\rightarrow V
be a bounded endomorphism. For any
\lambda\in K
, we let
V_{\lambda}
be the weight space of weight
\lambda
(
cf.
Definition
2.1.8
). We say
V
admits a weight space decomposition if there is some
I\subset K
such that every
v\in V
can be expressed as a convergent sum:

v=\sum_{\lambda\in I}v_{\lambda},\textnormal{ where }v_{\lambda}\in V_{\lambda}.

In the semi-simple case, weight space decompositions satisfy very good properties. Namely, if
I
is
small enough
then it can be shown that any expression as above is unique, and
V_{\mu}=0
for
\mu\not\in I
. Furthermore, any closed
\partial
-invariant subspace
W\subset V
also admits a weight space decomposition (see Theorem
2.2.4
for details). As it turns out, even if the decompositions we are interested in are not semi-simple, all Banach spaces we are interested in admit a finite filtration by Banach spaces which are invariant under
\partial
, and such that the graded quotients admit a semi-simple weight space decomposition with respect to the action of
\partial
. In this situation, we show in Proposition
2.2.7
that most of the good properties of semi-simple decomposition carry over to this more general setting. We point out, however, that we were not able to prove that any closed
\partial
-invariant subspace
W\subset V
also admits a weight space decomposition. This is a major inconvenience, as this key feature of semi-simple decompositions is the basis of the constructions in
[
10
]
and
[
31
]
.

After this analytical detour, we are now ready to start discussing triangular decompositions. This is done in Chapter
3
, where we discuss the Banach and Fréchet-Stein cases. Let
\mathscr{R}
be a two-sided noetherian Banach
K
-algebra. We say
\mathscr{R}
admits a triangular decomposition if there is a dense graded subalgebra
R\subset\mathscr{R}
which admits a triangular decomposition with respect to some graded subalgebras
A,B,H\subset R
. This decomposition is required to satisfy some additional properties:

Let
\partial\in R
be the grading element in the definition of a triangular decomposition. Then
\operatorname{ad}(\partial):=[\partial,-]
is a bounded endomorphism of
\mathscr{R}
. Let
\mathscr{A}
, and
\mathscr{B}
be the closures in
\mathscr{R}
of
A
and
B
respectively. Then
\mathscr{A}
, and
\mathscr{B}
are closed
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
-invariant subspaces of
\mathscr{R}
. We will require that both
\mathscr{A}
, and
\mathscr{B}
admit weight space decompositions with respect to the action of
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and that the direct sum of all weight spaces of
\mathscr{A}
(resp.
\mathscr{B}
) is
A
(resp.
B
).
Finally, we impose that there is an isomorphism of Banach
K
-vector spaces:

\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}.

Under the additional assumption that both
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are two-sided noetherian, we will be able to show that there is an analytic version of the category
\mathcal{O}
attached to this decomposition.

Definition
.

We define the category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
as the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
given by the modules which are finite as
\mathscr{A}
-modules and admit a weight space decomposition with respect to the action of
\partial
.

We remark that every
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
admits a canonical Banach topology as a finite
\mathscr{R}
-module, and that the weight space decomposition in the definition is a decomposition in the sense of Banach spaces.

A priory, it is not clear that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is the category we are looking for. For instance, our inability to
show that closed
\partial
-invariant subspaces admit weight space decompositions makes it hard to show that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is an abelian category. As mentioned above, our strategy will be constructing adequate filtrations of elements in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. In particular, we will show in Proposition
3.1.26
that every object
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
admits a Jordan-Hölder series:

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n}=M,

satisfying that every
M_{i}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, and that the graded quotients
M_{i+1}/M_{i}
are simple objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
.

This will allow us to reduce most of our analytical problems to the simple case. Furthermore, we have an explicit description of the simple objects. Indeed, let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, then every simple object in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
arises as the unique simple quotient of the analytic Verma module:

\widehat{\Delta}(W):=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W.

It will be shown that
\widehat{\Delta}(W)\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, and that the action of
\partial
on the eigenspaces of
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
is semi-simple. In particular, this allows us to apply the results of
[
14
]
to the simple objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. With these tools at our disposal, we will dedicate the rest of the section to analyzing the algebraic properties of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. We may condense our results into the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

The category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is a full abelian subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
.

(ii)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is closed under subobjects and finite sums.

(iii)

There is an equivalence of abelian categories:
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}-:\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}.

Proof.

This is shown in Theorem
3.1.34
.
∎

In particular, this theorem shows that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is a highest weight category, and that its simple objects are in one to one correspondence with the irreducible representations of
H
. We will finish the section by giving different characterizations of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
in terms of the properties of weight space decompositions.

Next, we deal with the case of Fréchet-Stein algebras. As defined in
[
32
]
, a (two-sided) Fréchet-Stein algebra is a topological algebra
\mathscr{R}
which admits a topological isomorphism:

\mathscr{R}\rightarrow\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{R}(n),

where each
\mathscr{R}(n)
is a two-sided noetherian Banach
K
-algebra, and the transition maps
\mathscr{R}(n+1)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
are two-sided flat with dense image.

Our notion of triangular decomposition of a Fréchet-Stein algebra is a natural extension of the triangular decomposition for Banach algebras. In particular, we say
\mathscr{R}
admits a triangular decomposition if there is a dense graded subalgebra
R\subset\mathscr{R}
satisfying that the induced map
R\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
is injective for each
n\geq 0
, and such that there is a triangular decomposition of
R
with respect to graded subalgebras
A,B,H\subset R
which induces a triangular decomposition of
\mathscr{R}(n)
for each
n\geq 0
. Under mild technical assumptions (
cf.
Section
3.2.8
), each of the algebras
\mathscr{R}(n)
admits a category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, which we denote
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
. Furthermore, extension of scalars along the maps
\mathscr{R}(n+1)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
induces equivalences of categories
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n+1)\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
for each
n\geq 0
.

Definition
.

We define the category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
of
\mathscr{R}
as the inverse limit:

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}:=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}(n).

Notice that
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is by construction a full abelian subcategory of the category of co-admissible
\mathscr{R}
-modules (
cf.
Definition
3.2.1
). By using the properties of this category, and the case of Banach algebras, we can show that
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
has a behavior which is analogous to that of the Banach case:

Theorem B
.

The category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is a full abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
.

(ii)

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is contains all closed subobjects and finite direct sums.

(iii)

There is an equivalence of abelian categories:
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}-:\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}.

Proof.

This is Proposition
3.2.12
in the body of the text.
∎

Notice that the only difference is that in
(ii)
we require the subobjects to be closed. This is because
\mathscr{R}
is in general not a noetherian algebra. Finally, we end the section by giving different characterizations of
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
in terms of weight space decompositions, and show that the category we obtain is analogous to those constructed in
[
10
]
and
[
31
]
.

In the second part of the paper, we will apply the theory of triangular decompositions to
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebras. Let us give a brief remainder of these algebras:

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a finite dimensional
K
-vector space, and
G\leq\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
be a finite group. We let
S(G)
be the set of pseudo-reflections in
G
(
ie.
the family of elements
s\in G\setminus\{1\}
such that
\operatorname{Rank}(s-\operatorname{Id})=1
), and define a reflection function to be a
G
-invariant function
c:S(G)\rightarrow K
. We let
\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
be the finite-dimensional vector space of all reflection functions. The family of rational Cherednik algebras associated to the action of
G
on
\mathfrak{h}
, is the family of filtered
K
-algebras
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
,
parameterized by reflection functions
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
, and defined as the quotient of the tensor algebra

G\ltimes T(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})
by the following relations:

[v,w]=0,\textnormal{ }[x,y]=0,\textnormal{ }[v,x]-(v,x)+\sum_{g\in S(G)}c(g)(v,\alpha_{g})(\alpha_{g}^{\vee},x)g=0,

where
v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, and
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. These algebras are the prime example of an algebra with a triangular decomposition, and their representation theory has been systematically studied in the last decade.

We will mostly be interested in a
p
-adic version of this algebra. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}}
be the rigid-analytification of
\mathfrak{h}
. As shown in
[
26
]
, our choice of reflection function yields a sheaf of complete topological algebras
\mathcal{H}_{c,\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G}
on the quotient
\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}}/G
. Such a sheaf is called a sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. We define the
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebra as the following complete topological algebra:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G):=\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}}/G,\mathcal{H}_{c,\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G}).

Our goal for the second part of the chapter is showing that
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra with a triangular decomposition. Logically, the first step is showing that
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
is indeed a Fréchet-Stein algebra. This is achieved in Section
4.3
. We will actually work in greater generality, by working with smooth Stein spaces admitting an action of a finite group
G
. For the benefit of the reader, we include in Section
4.2
a treatment of the basics of the theory of group actions on Stein spaces, putting special emphasis on the quotients of such actions. The main results of Section

4.3
can be summarized into the following theorem:

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a smooth Stein space equipped with an action of a finite group
G
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
be a sheaf of Cherednik algebras on
X
. Then the global sections algebra:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G):=\Gamma(X/G,\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}),

is a Fréchet-Stein algebra and a nuclear space. Furthermore, every co-admissible
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
-module is also a nuclear Fréchet space.

Proof.

See Proposition
4.3.2
for details.
∎

The rest of the section is devoted to understanding the properties of the canonical map:

H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G).

Again, we work in greater generality, by studying the properties of Cherednik algebras under the rigid-analytic GAGA functor. This is done in Section
4.4
. In Section
4.5
we recall the notion of the Arens-Michael envelope of a topological algebra, and some of its basic properties. The main results of these sections can be condensed as follows:

Theorem D
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be a smooth affine variety with an action of a finite group
G
. Assume there is an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
, and let
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
be a sheaf of Cherednik algebras on
X
. The canonical map of
K
-algebras:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G),

is faithfully flat, and realizes

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
as the Arens-Michael envelope of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Proof.

This is a part of Theorem
4.5.1
.
∎

Finally, in Chapter
5

we apply all the previous machinery to the case of
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebras. In particular, we give a summary of the main properties of (algebraic) rational Cherednik algebras, and give an explicit description of their triangular decomposition. We conclude the paper with Section
5.2
, where we construct an explicit Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
, and show that it admits a triangular decomposition in the sense of Fréchet-Stein algebras. In particular, by the results above, it admits a category
\mathcal{O}
, which we denote
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
. We then have the following theorem:

Theorem E
.

The rational Cherednik algebra admits a Fréchet-Stein presentation:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)=\varprojlim_{m\geq 0}\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K},

which admits a triangular decomposition with respect to
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)\subset\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
.
In particular, there is a full abelian subcategory
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}\subset\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G))
satisfying the following:

(i)

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
is closed under closed submodules and finite direct sums.

(ii)

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
is a highest weight category. Furthermore, its irreducible objects are in one-to-one correspondence with the irreducible
K
-linear representations of
G
.

The canonical map
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

It is faithfully flat and has dense image.

(ii)

It makes
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
the Arens-Michael envelope of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
.

(iii)

It induces an equivalence of highest weight categories:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)\otimes_{H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)}-:\mathcal{O}_{c}\leftrightarrows\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}.

Proof.

This is shown below in Theorem
5.2.5
.
∎

Thus obtaining a classification of Verma modules over
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebras.

2.
Background in algebra and
p
-adic analysis

We start the paper by introducing the technical tools needed in the definition of a triangular decomposition of a Fréchet-Stein algebra, and the construction of its associated category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
. Namely, in Section
2.1
we introduce the concept of a triangular decomposition of a (noetherian) graded algebra, and the category
\mathcal{O}
attached to it. This is perhaps the most relevant concept of this chapter, as most of the contents in the rest of the paper are devoted to giving a generalization of the category
\mathcal{O}
to the
p
-adic analytic setting. Next, in Section
2.2
we deal with some technical
p
-adic analytical tools needed in the rest of the paper. Namely, given a Banach space
V
with a bounded endomorphism
\partial
, we will study the decomposition of
V
into generalized eigenspaces for the action of
\partial
. We remark that the focus of this section is on the types of weight space decompositions that arise in the setting of the category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
of a
p
-adic Banach algebra with a triangular decomposition. In particular, there are rather strict restrictions on the weights that
\partial
is allowed to have. The study of these weight space decompositions will be based upon generalizing the results in
[
14
]
to the case where the action of
\partial
on each eigenspace is not semi-simple.

2.1.
Category
\mathcal{O}
for algebras with a triangular decomposition

Before moving to the realm of
p
-adic analysis and co-admissible modules, it is wise to do an introduction to the (classical) category
\mathcal{O}
for rational Cherednik algebras. We will in fact work in a more general setting, by studying the category
\mathcal{O}
attached to any algebra with a triangular decomposition. We will closely follow in the footsteps of
[
15
]
. The material in this section is derived from (
loc.cit.
), and we claim no originality here. The only difference between this section and (
loc.cit.
) is that we do not assume our base field to be algebraically closed. Let us begin by introducing the protagonist of this section:

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
R
be a noetherian graded algebra. A triangular decomposition of
R
consists of three graded subalgebras
A,B,H\subset R
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

R=A\otimes_{K}H\otimes_{K}B
as
K
-vector spaces.

(ii)

B\cdot H=H\cdot B
and
H\cdot A=A\cdot H
.

(iii)

A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
,
B=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}B_{n}
,
A_{0}=B_{0}=K
, and
H\subset R_{0}
.

(iv)

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
are finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces.

(v)

H
is a finite dimensional semi-simple split
K
-algebra.

(vi)

The grading on
R
is inner. That is, there is some
\partial\in R_{0}
such that:

R_{n}=\{x\in R\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }[\partial,x]=nx\}.

We will denote the data of a triangular decomposition by the tuple
(A,B,H,\partial)
.

Examples of graded algebras with a triangular decomposition are given by the Weyl algebras, and more generally the rational Cherednik algebras (see Section
5.2
below).

Remark 2.1.2
.

We remark that the results of this section can be extended to the case where
R=U(\mathfrak{g})
is the enveloping algebra of a graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, satisfying that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is reductive. Notice that in this case
H=U(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
is no longer finite-dimensional. Hence, the only
H
-modules considered are the semi-simple ones. This situation was studied by W. Soergel in
[
34
]
.

Let us introduce the following terminology:

Definition 2.1.3
.

We introduce the following objects:

(i)

B^{n}=B_{-n}
for all
n\geq 0
.

(ii)

We have a decomposition
\partial=\partial^{\prime}-\partial_{0}
, where
\partial^{\prime}\in A\otimes_{K}H\otimes_{K}B_{<0}
and
\partial_{0}\in Z(H)
.

(iii)

Let
\operatorname{Irr}(H)
the set of isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional irreducible
H
-modules.

(iv)

For
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, we let
c(W)\in K
be the scalar by which
\partial_{0}
acts on
W
.

(v)

We denote by
AH
and
BH
the subalgebras of
R
given by
A\otimes_{K}H
and
B\otimes_{K}H
respectively.

As mentioned above, algebras with a triangular decomposition have a rich representation theory, which resembles that of the universal enveloping algebra of a semi-simple Lie algebra. In particular, each algebra with a triangular decomposition admits a category
\mathcal{O}
, which is a highest weight category whose simple objects are in bijection with the irreducible representations of
H
. We will devote the rest of this section to the construction of this category, and giving explicit descriptions of its relevant objects (
ie.
standard objects, partial order of the simple objects
etc
.)

Definition 2.1.4
.

Let
M
be a
B
-module. We say the action of
B
on
M
is locally nilpotent if for every
m\in M
there is some
n\geq 0
such that
B^{>n}m=0
.

For every
n\geq 0
, consider the following two-sided ideal of
BH
:

BH^{>n}:=B^{>n}\otimes_{K}H.

There is a canonical surjection
BH\rightarrow H
, given by quotienting by the ideal
BH^{>0}
. Hence, every
H
-module can be regarded as a
BH
-module via this epimorphism. In particular, each irreducible
H
-module gives rise to a (generalized) Verma module:

Definition 2.1.5
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(W)
. The generalized Verma module of level
n
associated to
W
is the following
R
-module:

\Delta(W)_{n}:=\operatorname{Ind}_{BH}^{R}(BH/BH^{>n}\otimes_{H}W)=R\otimes_{BH}(BH/BH^{>n}\otimes_{H}W)=A\otimes_{K}\left(B/B^{>n}\otimes_{K}W\right).

If
n=0
we will write
\Delta(W):=\Delta(W)_{0}
and call
\Delta(W)
the Verma module of
W
.

As
H
is split semi-simple, the category
\operatorname{Mod}(H)
is also semi-simple. Hence, Verma modules yield an embedding
\operatorname{Mod}(H)\rightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(R)
. Notice that the last identity endows the generalized Verma modules
\Delta(W)_{n}
with a graded
R
-module structure by letting
W
sit in degree zero. Furthermore, as
A\subset R_{n\geq 0}
, and
B\subset R_{n\leq 0}
, it follows that the action of
B
on any generalized Verma module is locally nilpotent. Furthermore, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1.6

(
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{\operatorname{ln}}
be the subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
given by the modules which are locally nilpotent for the action of
B
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

M\in\mathcal{O}^{\operatorname{ln}}
.

(ii)

M
admits an ascending filtration whose successive quotients are quotients of
\Delta(W)
’s.

(iii)

M
is a quotient of a direct sum of
\Delta(W)_{n}^{\prime}s
.

We call a filtration as in
(ii)
a
\Delta
-filtration.

Statement
(ii)
in the proposition above shows that
\mathcal{O}^{\operatorname{ln}}
is a Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
and, by the discussion above, it follows that it contains all Verma modules. Unfortunately, this category is too big for our purposes. In particular,
\mathcal{O}^{\operatorname{ln}}
is closed under arbitrary direct sums, so is far from being an artinian category. For this reason, we introduce the following category:

Definition 2.1.7
.

Let
R
be an algebra admitting a triangular decomposition. We define
\mathcal{O}
as the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}^{\operatorname{ln}}
given by the finitely generated
R
-modules.

As
R
is noetherian, this condition is equivalent to the filtration in
(ii)
being finite, or the sum in
(iii)
containing only finitely many factors. Hence,
\mathcal{O}
is the smallest Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
containing all Verma modules. In order to show that
\mathcal{O}
is a highest weight category, we need to study its simple objects. As it turns out, the best way of doing this is studying the behavior of the objects in
\mathcal{O}
with respect to the action of
\partial
.

Definition 2.1.8
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module and
\lambda\in K
. A generalized weight space in
M
is:

M_{\lambda}=\{m\in M\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }(\partial-\lambda)^{n}m=0\textnormal{ for }n>>0\}.

The scalar
\lambda\in K
is called the weight of the generalized weight space
M_{\lambda}
. We say that
M
admits a (generalized) weight space decomposition if we have:

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in K}M_{\lambda},

and we say the decomposition is of finite type if each of the
M_{\lambda}
is finite dimensional. If
M
admits a weight space decomposition, we set:

\Lambda(M)=\{\lambda\in K\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }M_{\lambda}\neq 0\}.

The most important case of a module with a weight space decomposition is the following:

Proposition 2.1.9
.

For each
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(W)
, the Verma module
\Delta(W)
admits a finite-type decomposition into generalized eigenspaces for
\partial
:

\Delta(W)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}}\Delta(W)_{n+c(W)}.

Furthermore,
\Delta(W)_{n+c(W)}=A_{n}\otimes_{K}W
, and the action of
\partial
on each weight space is semi-simple.

Proof.

By definition of a triangular decomposition we have
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
. Hence, we only need to show that
\partial
acts on
A_{n}\otimes_{K}W
with generalized eigenvalue
c(W)+n
. By construction, the grading on
R
is inner, with grading element
\partial
. Hence, for every
x\in A_{n}
and every
v\in W
we have:

\partial(x\otimes v)=(x\partial+nx)\otimes v=(c(W)+n)x\otimes v,

as we wanted to show.
∎

It is clear that the modules admitting a finite-type weight space decomposition form a Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
. Furthermore, by the previous proposition, Verma modules admit such a decomposition. Thus, it follows from Proposition
2.1.6
that every object in
\mathcal{O}
admits a finite-type decomposition into weight spaces. More generally, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1.10

(
[
15
, Lemma 2.22]
)
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}^{\operatorname{ln}}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

M\in\mathcal{O}
.

(ii)

M
is a finitely generated
A
-module.

(iii)

M
admits a finite-type decomposition into generalized weight spaces for
\partial
.

Consider a short exact sequence of objects in
\mathcal{O}
:

0\rightarrow V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}\rightarrow V_{3}\rightarrow 0.

For every weight
\lambda\in K
we have the identity
\operatorname{dim}(V_{2,\lambda})=\operatorname{dim}(V_{1,\lambda})+\operatorname{dim}(V_{3,\lambda})
.
In particular, we have
\Lambda(V_{2})=\Lambda(V_{1})\cup\Lambda(V_{3})
. Thus, the weight spaces of objects in
\mathcal{O}
are completely determined by the weight spaces of the Verma modules appearing in a
\Delta
-filtration. In particular, we have:

Proposition 2.1.11
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}
. There exists a finite family
\{W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\operatorname{Irr}(H)
such that the weight space decomposition of
M
satisfies:

\Lambda(M)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}c(W_{i})+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}.

Proof.

Follows at once from the fact that every
M\in\mathcal{O}
admits a
\Delta
-filtration, together with the description of the weight spaces given in Proposition
2.1.9
.
∎

This condition will be instrumental when dealing with the analytic version of
\mathcal{O}
. As an upshot of the weight space decomposition, notice that for any
M\in\mathcal{O}
and
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
we have:

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Delta(W),M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(W,M).

As
W
is finite-dimensional, and the image of any
H
-linear homomorphism from
W
must land in
M_{c(W)}
, it follows that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\Delta(W),M)
is finite-dimensional. Furthermore, as every element in
\mathcal{O}
admits a
\Delta
-filtration, we can conclude that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(M,N)
is finite-dimensional for all
M,N\in\mathcal{O}
.

After this slight detour, we now go back to the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{O}
:

Proposition 2.1.12

(
[
11
, Proposition 3.29.]
)
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. Then
\Delta(W)
has a unique maximal submodule
M(W)
, and a unique simple quotient
L(W)
. Any simple object of
\mathcal{O}
arises in this way.

The proof crucially uses the explicit structure of weight spaces of Verma modules determined in Proposition
2.1.9
. Given the fact that any element in
\mathcal{O}
admits a
\Delta
-filtration, we may use this proposition to show that every object in
\mathcal{O}
admits in fact a Jordan-Hölder series:

Proposition 2.1.13

(
[
15
, Lemma 2.14, Corollary 2.16.]
)
.

Every object in
\mathcal{O}
admits a finite Jordan-Hölder series. That is, given
M\in\mathcal{O}
there is a finite filtration:

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n}=M,

satisfying that
M_{i}/M_{i-1}=L(W_{i})
for some
W_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. Furthermore, if
M
is a quotient of some
\Delta(E)
, then
L(W_{n})=L(E)
, and
L(W_{i})
satisfies
c(E)-c(W_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}^{>0}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

Again, finiteness of the weight spaces plays a major role in this proposition, as it allows us to show that any quotient of a Verma module admits a simple submodule. We almost have all the ingredients needed to show that
\mathcal{O}
is a highest weight category. The only remaining part is showing the existence of projective covers of simple objects:

Proposition 2.1.14

(
[
15
, Corollary 2.10.]
)
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. The simple module
L(W)
has a projective cover

P(W)
in
\mathcal{O}
, such that that the quotients,
L(W_{i})
, of its Jordan-Hölder series satisfy
c(W_{i})-c(E)\in\mathbb{Z}^{>0}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and
L(W_{n})=L(W)
.

This proposition is based upon the fact that the category
\mathcal{O}
admits a block decomposition, which is given by certain gradings on the modules in
\mathcal{O}
. In order to keep the exposition short, we will not make any further mention of the block decomposition. After all this preparation, we can condense the results thus far into the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1.15
.

\mathcal{O}
is a highest weight category. For every
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)

its associated standard object is
\Delta(W)
, and its simple module is
L(W)
. The partial order relation in
\operatorname{Irr}(H)
is given by:

W<E\textnormal{ if and only if }c(E)-c(W)\in\mathbb{Z}^{>0}.

Thus, we have constructed a highest weight category whose simple objects are in natural bijection with the irreducible representations of
H
. In particular,
\mathcal{O}
contains
\operatorname{Mod}(H)
as a full abelian subcategory. For completion, we will now give a description of the costandard objects. For this, notice that the algebra
AH
inherits a grading from
R
, making
R
a graded module. On the other hand any
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
is canonically a graded
AH
-module concentrated in degree zero. We let
\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{gr}}_{AH}(-,-)
be the set of graded homomorphisms (of any degree), and
\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{deg}(i)}_{AH}(-,-)
be the set of homomorphisms of degree
i
. Then we have the following definition:

Definition 2.1.16
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, the costandard object associated to
W
is the module:

\nabla(W)=\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{gr}}_{AH}(R,W)=\bigoplus_{i}\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{deg(i)}}_{AH}(R,W).

The structure of
R
as a
(AH,R)
-bimodule makes
\nabla(W)
a left
R
-module. Furthermore, it agrees with the submodule of
\operatorname{Hom}_{AH}(R,E)
given by the maps which are locally nilpotent with respect to the action of
B
.
As in any highest weight category, each simple object
L(W)
admits an injective hull
I(W)
, satisfying that
L(W)
is the socle of
I(W)
. Furthermore, we have BGG reciprocity formulas:

[I(W):\nabla(E)]=[\Delta(E):L(W)]\textnormal{ and }[P(W):\Delta(E)]=[\nabla(E):L(W)].

Let
K\subset L
be an algebraic field extension. Let
R_{L}=R\otimes_{K}L
, and define
A_{L},B_{L}
and
H_{L}
similarly. Then
R_{L}
is a noetherian graded
L
-algebra with a triangular decomposition with respect to
A_{L}
,
B_{L}
and
H_{L}
. Let
\mathcal{O}(L)
be the category
\mathcal{O}
associated to
R_{L}
. In this situation, extension of scalars induces an exact functor
\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(L)
. This line of thought leads to the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1.17
.

Let
L
be an algebraic extension of
K
. There is an equivalence of abelian categories:

\mathcal{O}(L)=\varinjlim\mathcal{O}(Q),

where the direct limit is taken along all finite field extensions
K\subset Q\subset L
.

Proof.

By the discussion above, we have a functor
\varphi:\varinjlim\mathcal{O}(Q)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(L)
. We need to show it is an equivalence. First, notice that

R_{L}=\varinjlim R_{Q}
. As every element in
\mathcal{O}(L)
is a finitely presented
R_{L}
-module, it follows that for each
M\in\mathcal{O}(L)
there is some finite extension
Q
, and some
\tilde{M}\in\mathcal{O}(Q)
such that
M=L\otimes_{Q}\tilde{M}
. In particular,
\varphi
is essentially surjective. We now need to show that it is fully faithful.
By the above, for
M,N\in\mathcal{O}(L)
we have some finite extension
K\subset Q\subset L

such that there are
\tilde{M},\tilde{N}\in\mathcal{O}(Q)
with
M=\tilde{M}_{L}
and
N=\tilde{N}_{L}
. By Proposition
2.1.6
, both
\tilde{M}
and
\tilde{N}
admit finite
\Delta
-filtrations. Notice that for each field extension
Q_{1}\subset Q_{2}
, the functor
i:\mathcal{O}(Q_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(Q_{2})
sends Verma modules to Verma modules (
cf
. the explicit description in Definition
2.1.5
) and is exact. In particular, it preserves
\Delta
-filtrations. Hence, it suffices to show fully faithfulness for Verma modules. However, given a finite extension
K\subset Q\subset L
, and irreducible representations
W,E\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, we know that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}(Q)}(\Delta(W)_{Q},\Delta(E)_{Q})
is canonically isomorphic to the (finite-dimensional) generalized eigenspace in
\Delta(E)_{Q}
of weight
c(W)
. As changing the base field does not change this dimension, it follows that:

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}(L)}(\Delta(W)_{L},\Delta(E)_{L})=\varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}(Q)}(\Delta(W)_{Q},\Delta(E)_{Q}),

which shows that
\varphi
is fully faithful, hence an equivalence.
∎

2.2.
Weight space decomposition for
p
-adic Banach spaces

We start by recalling some concepts from
p
-adic functional analysis: Let
V
be a
K
-vector space, and
\partial:V\rightarrow V
be a
K
-linear endomorphism. Recall the notions of weight space from Definition
2.1.8
, and recall that we say that
V
admits a decomposition into generalized eigenspaces (weight spaces) if there is an identity:

V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in K}V_{\lambda}.

In our situation, this notion of decomposition is too restrictive. For instance, a direct sum
\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
is complete if and only if there are only finitely many non-zero spaces in the sum, and each of them is complete.
Thus, we need to introduce an adequate decomposition for Banach spaces:

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
V
be a locally convex space and
\partial:V\rightarrow V
be a continuous
K
-linear map. We define the set
\Lambda(V):=\{\lambda\in K\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }V_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
.

Notice that the subspaces
V_{\lambda}\subset V
need not be closed. However, if there is some
n\geq 0
such that
(\partial-\lambda)^{n}v=0
for all
v\in V_{\lambda}
, then
V_{\lambda}
is closed. This happens, for example, if
V_{\lambda}
is finite-dimensional. In a similar vein, for two different
\lambda,\mu\in K
, the fact that
\partial-\lambda
acts locally nilpotently on
V_{\lambda}
implies that
\partial
acts locally finitely on
V_{\lambda}
(
ie.
for every
v\in V_{\lambda}
, the smallest
\partial
-invariant subspace of
V_{\lambda}
containing
v
is finite-dimensional). This in particular implies that
V_{\lambda}\cap V_{\mu}=0
. Hence, we define:

Definition 2.2.2
.

Let
V
be a locally convex space and
\partial:V\rightarrow V
be a continuous
K
-linear map.
We define the following subspace of
V
:

V^{\operatorname{ws}}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(V)}V_{\lambda},

and regard it as a locally convex space with respect to the subspace topology.

Notice, however, that it is not clear that
\overline{V}_{\lambda}\cap\overline{V}_{\mu}=0
. Thus, it becomes apparent that, in order to have a well-behaved theory, we need to impose some conditions on the weight spaces.

Definition 2.2.3
.

Let
V
be a locally convex space and
\partial:V\rightarrow V
be a continuous
K
-linear map. We say
V
admits a weight space decomposition for
\partial
(with respect to
I
) if there is a family
I\subset\Lambda(V)
such that:

•

For every
v\in V
there is a family
(v_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in I}\in\prod_{\lambda\in I}V_{\lambda}
such that we have a convergent sum:

v=\sum_{\lambda\in I}v_{\lambda}

We consider the following special cases:

(i)

We say the decomposition is of compact type if there is a family
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset K
such that:

I\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}.

(ii)

We say the decomposition is of finite type if it is of compact type and for each
\lambda\in I
the generalized eigenspace
V_{\lambda}
is finite dimensional.

(iii)

We say the decomposition is semi-simple if for each
\lambda\in I
the action of
\partial
on
V_{\lambda}
is semi-simple (ie. it is given by multiplication by
\lambda
).

Notice that it is a priory not clear (and in general false) that there is a unique family
I\subset\Lambda(V)
such that
V
admits a weight space decomposition with respect to
I
. Furthermore, given a decomposition, it could happen that that there is a non-trivial expression:

0=\sum_{\lambda\in I}v_{\lambda}.

However, this kind of pathological behavior will not be present in the cases of interest to us. A general theory of semi-simple weight space decompositions was established in the doctoral thesis of C.T. Féaux de Lacroix
[
14
]
. We may condense the main results into the following theorem:

Theorem 2.2.4

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
V
be a Banach space with a continuous endomorphism
\partial:V\rightarrow V
. Assume
V
admits a semisimple weight space decomposition of compact type for
\partial
with respect to some indexing set
I\subset K
. The following hold:

(i)

I=\Lambda(V)
. In particular, if
\mu\not\in I
, then
V_{\mu}=0
.

(ii)

Let
W\subset V
be a closed and
\partial
-invariant vector subspace. Then
W
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of compact type for
\partial
. Furthermore, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda(W)
we have a cartesian diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{\lambda}}

In particular,
W
contains the weight components of its elements.

(iii)

Assume in addition that the decomposition is of finite type. Then there is a natural injection-preserving bijection of sets:

(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}:\{\textnormal{Closed }\partial\textnormal{-invariant subspaces of }V\}\rightarrow\{\partial\textnormal{-invariant subspaces of }V^{\operatorname{ws}}\},

with inverse given by taking the closure in
V
.

Thus, for every closed and
\partial
-invariant subspace
W\subset V
, the quotient
V/W
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of compact type for
\partial
, and we have a
\partial
-equivariant short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow W^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow V^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow(V/W)^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

This is all contained in chapter one of
[
14
]
. See also Section 2 of
[
31
]
.
∎

Notice that, as a consequence of
(ii)
, for each
v\in V
the expression
v=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(V)}v_{\lambda}
is unique. In particular
0=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(V)}v_{\lambda}
if and only if
v_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda(V)
. Furthermore, we have:

Corollary 2.2.5
.

Assume
V
admits a semisimple weight space decomposition of finite type with respect to
\partial
, and let
W\subset V^{\operatorname{ws}}
be a dense and
\partial
-invariant
K
-vector space. Then
W=V^{\operatorname{ws}}
.

Proof.

This is a special case of statement
(iii)
in the preceding theorem.
∎

Unfortunately, in the case we are interested in, the action of
\partial
on the weight spaces is not semi-simple. Hence, this theorem does not apply directly to our setting. However, we can circumvent this problem by restricting to spaces which admit an appropriate filtration. For the rest of this section, we fix a Banach space
V
with a continuous endomorphism
\partial:V\rightarrow V
. We will assume that
V
admits a weight space decomposition of compact type for
\partial
with respect to a family
I\subset\Lambda(V)
.

Lemma 2.2.6
.

Let
W\subset V
be a closed and
\partial
-equivariant subspace satisfying that the action of
\partial
on each weight space of
V/W
is semi-simple. Then the following hold:

(i)

V/W
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of compact type for
\partial
.

(ii)

W
admits a
weight space decomposition for
\partial
with respect to the following family:

I_{W}:=\{\lambda\in I\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }V_{\lambda}\cap W\neq 0\}.

In particular, the decomposition is of compact type.

(iii)

If the decomposition is of finite type, then there is a
\partial
-equivariant short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I_{W}}W_{\lambda}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I}V_{\lambda}\rightarrow(V/W)^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

As
W
is
\partial
-invariant, the map
\varphi:V\rightarrow V/W
is a
\partial
-equivariant surjection. Hence, for each
\lambda\in I
we have
\varphi(V_{\lambda})\subset(V/W)_{\lambda}
. In particular, every
v\in V/W
admits an expression
v=\sum_{\lambda\in I}v_{\lambda}
with
v_{\lambda}\in V_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda\in I
. By assumption, the action of
\partial
on each weight space of
V/W
is semisimple. As the decomposition of
V
with respect to
\partial
is of compact type, there is a finite family
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset K
such that
I\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, the action of
\partial
on
V/W
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of compact type. For
(ii)
, choose
v\in W
, and assume
v=\sum_{\lambda\in I}v_{\lambda}
. It suffices to show that
v_{\lambda}\in W
for each
\lambda\in I
. As the action of
\partial
on
V
is continuous, we have:

0=\varphi(v)=\sum_{\lambda\in I}\varphi(v_{\lambda}),

and statement
(ii)
in Theorem
2.2.4
implies that
\pi(v_{\lambda})=0
for all
\lambda\in I
, as we wanted to show.
For the last part of the lemma, we just need to show that the map
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I}V_{\lambda}\rightarrow(V/W)^{\operatorname{ws}}
is surjective. This follows from the fact that
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I}V_{\lambda}\rightarrow V/W
has dense image, together with Corollary
2.2.5
.
∎

Proposition 2.2.7
.

Assume there is a filtration by closed
\partial
-invariant subspaces:

0=V_{0}\subset\cdots\subset V_{n}=V,

satisfying that the action of
\partial
on the weight spaces of
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
is semi-simple for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Then the following holds for each
1\leq i\leq n
:

(i)

V_{i}/V_{i-1}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of compact type for
\partial
.

(ii)

V_{i}
admits a weight space decomposition of compact type with respect to the following family:

I_{V_{i}}:=\{\lambda\in I\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }V_{\lambda}\cap V_{i}\neq 0\}.

Furthermore, we have
V_{i,\lambda}=V_{i}\cap V_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in I_{V_{i}}
.

(iii)

If the decomposition is of finite type, there is a
\partial
-equivariant filtration:

0\subset\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I_{V_{1}}}V_{1,\lambda}\subset\cdots\subset\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I_{V_{n}}}V_{n,\lambda},

such that we have the following graded quotients for
1\leq i\leq n
:

\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I_{V_{i}}}V_{i,\lambda}\right)/\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in I_{V_{i-1}}}V_{i-1,\lambda}\right)=(V_{i}/V_{i-1})^{\operatorname{ws}}.

In particular, we have
I=\Lambda(V)
, and
v=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(V)}v_{\lambda}=0
if and only if
v_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda(V)
.

Proof.

Claims
(i)
to
(iii)
follow from an inductive application of Lemma
2.2.6
. Let
\mu\in K\setminus I
, and
v\in V_{\mu}
. There is a minimal
1\leq i\leq n
such that
v\in V_{i}
. Let
\pi_{i}:V_{i}\rightarrow V_{i}/V_{i-1}
be the quotient. Our minimality assumption implies that
\pi_{i}(v)\neq 0
. However, we have
\pi_{i}(v)\in(V_{i}/V_{i-1})_{\mu}
, and by statement
(iii)
we have
\Lambda(V_{i}/V_{i-1})\subset I
. Thus, it follows that
(V_{i}/V_{i-1})_{\mu}=0
, and therefore
v=0
. Hence, we must have
I=\Lambda(V)
, as desired.

Consider a trivial sum of weight vectors
0=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(V)}v_{\lambda}
. We need to show
v_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda(V)
. For simplicity, we may assume
V=V_{1}
, the general case is analogous. By Theorem
2.2.4
, the identity
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(V)}\pi_{1}(v_{\lambda})=0
implies
\pi_{1}(v_{\lambda})=0
. Hence,
v_{\lambda}\in V_{0}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda(V)
. However, by assumption,
V_{0}
also satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.2.4
, so it follows that
v_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda(V)
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Notice that this proposition is slightly weaker than the semi-simple version shown in Theorem
2.2.4
. In particular, it does not allow us to deduce the existence of weight space decompositions for closed
\partial
-invariant subspaces of
V
. However, we will see in Section
3.1
that this weak version is enough for our purposes.

3.
Category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
for Fréchet-Stein algebras with a triangular decomposition

Now that all the tools needed are set into place, we can start working towards an analytic version of the category
\mathcal{O}
for Fréchet-Stein algebras. We start in Section
3.1
by developing an adequate notion of triangular decomposition for Banach algebras, and defining the category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. We will then study the properties of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, showing that it is an abelian highest weight category. We continue this study in
3.2
, where we expand upon the previous results to obtain a definition of our sought after category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
, and determine its main properties.

3.1.
Banach algebras with a triangular decomposition

After this analytic detour, we will start applying these results to the representation theory of Banach algebras. Let us first introduce a Banach analog of the algebras with triangular decomposition:

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{R}
be a noetherian Banach
K
-algebra. A triangular decomposition of
\mathscr{R}
consists of the following data
(R,A,B,H,\partial)
, satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

R\subset\mathscr{R}
is a dense graded subalgebra.

(ii)

A,B,H\subset R
are graded subalgebras,
\partial\in R
is an element.

(iii)

R
admits a triangular decomposition
(A,B,H,\partial)
.

Let
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}
be the completions of
A
and
B
in
\mathscr{R}
, and denote the commutator of
\partial
by
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)=[\partial,-]
. This data must satisfy the following requirements:

(i)

There is a decomposition of Banach spaces
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and
\mathscr{A}^{\operatorname{ws}}=A
.

(iii)

\mathscr{B}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and
\mathscr{B}^{\operatorname{ws}}=B
.

The goal of this section is showing that every noetherian Banach
\mathscr{R}
-algebra with a triangular decomposition (plus some mild technical conditions) admits a category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, with properties analogous to the algebraic version studied above. Furthermore, as the algebra
R
admits a triangular decomposition, it has a category
\mathcal{O}
. The ultimate goal of this section is showing that, in fact,
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
and
\mathcal{O}
are equivalent. For the rest of this section we fix a noetherian Banach algebra
\mathscr{R}
with a triangular decomposition
(R,A,B,H,\partial)
. Let us start by analyzing the properties of
\mathscr{R}
:

Proposition 3.1.2
.

The following hold:

(i)

\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are Banach
K
-algebras.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}\cdot H=H\cdot\mathscr{A}
, and
\mathscr{B}\cdot H=H\cdot\mathscr{B}
.

As before, we let
\mathscr{A}H
and
\mathscr{B}H
be the algebras
\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H
and
\mathscr{B}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H
.

Proof.

By definition we have
\mathscr{A}=\widehat{A}
, and
\mathscr{B}=\widehat{B}
, and
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}
. Thus, all the statements follow from their algebraic counterparts (
cf
. Definition
2.1.3
).
∎

Notice that we did not require that
\mathscr{R}
admits a weight space decomposition for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
. As it turns out, this does not follow directly from the definitions, but we can make some additional assumptions on
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
which assure that this will hold. Regard
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}
as a Banach space. We have:

Lemma 3.1.3
.

Choose any
a\otimes b\in\mathscr{C}
. Then we have:

\operatorname{ad}(\partial)(a\otimes b)=\operatorname{ad}(\partial)a\otimes b+a\operatorname{ad}(\partial)b.

In particular, the subspace
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{R}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
.

Proof.

The fact that
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)(a\otimes b)=\operatorname{ad}(\partial)a\otimes b+a\operatorname{ad}(\partial)b
is automatic. This identity implies that
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)(\mathscr{A}\otimes_{K}\mathscr{B})\subset\mathscr{C}
. As
\mathscr{C}
is complete, and
\mathscr{A}\otimes_{K}\mathscr{B}
is dense in
\mathscr{C}
, the result follows.
∎

Proposition 3.1.4
.

Assume that
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
satisfy the following condition:

•

Given a sum of weight vectors
v=\sum_{\lambda\in I}v_{\lambda}
such that
|v|\leq\epsilon
, then
|v_{\lambda}|\leq\epsilon
for all
\lambda\in I
.

Then the space
\mathscr{C}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
with
\Lambda(\mathscr{C})\subset\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have:

\mathscr{C}_{n}=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{m\geq n}A_{m}\otimes B^{m-n}.

Proof.

First, notice that by Lemma
3.1.3
, given
a\in A_{n}
, and
b\in B^{m}
we have
a\otimes b\in\mathscr{C}_{n-m}
for all
n,m\geq 0
. Let
v\in\mathscr{C}
. By construction of the completed tensor product of Banach spaces, there is a family
(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})_{i\geq 0}\in\mathscr{A}\otimes_{K}\mathscr{B}
satisfying the identities:

(1)

v=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\otimes_{K}b_{i},\textnormal{ and }\varinjlim_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\otimes_{K}b_{i}=0.

By definition of a triangular decomposition, for each
i\geq 0
there are unique expressions:

a_{i}=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{i}^{n},\quad b_{i}=\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{i}^{m},

satisfying that
a_{i}^{n}\in A_{n}
, and
b_{i}^{m}\in B^{m}
for all
n,m\geq 0
. As these are infinite sums, we have:

(2)

\varinjlim_{n}a_{i}^{n}=\varinjlim_{n}b_{i}^{m}=0.

Hence, we may rearrange the terms in equation
(\ref{equation C admits a decomposition})
to obtain:

v=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{r=n-m}a_{i}^{n}\otimes b_{i}^{m}\right).

We are done if we are able to show that for each
r\in\mathbb{Z}
the sum:

(3)

\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{r=n-m}a_{i}^{n}\otimes b_{i}^{m},

converges. As the norm on
\mathscr{C}
is non-archimedean, it suffices to show that the principal term of
(\ref{equation 2 C admits a ws decomposition})
converges to zero. As
\varinjlim_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\otimes_{K}b_{i}=0
, for each
s\geq 0
there is some
j\geq 0
such that for all
i\geq j
we have
|a_{i}\otimes_{K}b_{i}|\leq|\pi|^{2s}
. In particular, we may assume that
\operatorname{max}(|a_{i}|,|b_{i}|)\leq|\pi|^{s}
for all
i\geq j
. Thus, our assumptions on
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
imply that:

\operatorname{max}\{|a^{n}_{i}|,|b^{m}_{i}|\}_{i\geq j}^{n,m\geq 0}\leq|\pi|^{s}.

As there are only finitely many
0\leq i\leq j
, it follows by
(\ref{equation 3 decomposition of C into ws})
that there is
\overline{n}\geq 0
such that we have:

\operatorname{max}\{|a^{n}_{i}|,|b^{m}_{i}|\}_{0\leq i\leq j}^{n,m\geq\overline{n}}\leq|\pi|^{s}.

Hence, the principal term of
(\ref{equation 2 C admits a ws decomposition})
converges to zero, as we wanted to show.
∎

Corollary 3.1.5
.

In the conditions of the previous proposition, the algebra
\mathscr{R}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
. Furthermore, we have
R\subset\mathscr{R}^{\operatorname{ws}}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}
. As
H
is finite-dimensional and
H\cdot\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}\cdot H
, this yields an identity:

\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{C}\otimes_{K}H.

By Proposition
3.1.4
,
\mathscr{C}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and by construction
H\subset\mathscr{R}_{0}
. Thus, it follows that
\mathscr{R}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition, as wanted. The fact that
R\subset\mathscr{R}^{\operatorname{ws}}
follows at once from the identity
R=A\otimes_{K}H\otimes_{K}C
.
∎

Remark 3.1.6
.

Notice that the weight space decomposition of
\mathscr{R}
is only of finite type if there is some
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
such that
A_{n}=0
for all
n\geq m
or
B^{n}=0
for all
n\geq m
.

The rest of this section is devoted to studying the representation theory of
\mathscr{R}
. For convenience, we will use the following notation:

Remark 3.1.7
.

In this section
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
will denote the category of finitely generated
\mathscr{R}
-modules. All weight space decompositions will be with respect to
\partial
, so we omit it from the notation.

As
\mathscr{R}
is noetherian and Banach, every
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
carries a canonical Banach topology, which is induced by any surjection
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathscr{R}\rightarrow M
. Given
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
, we write
M^{\operatorname{ws}}

for the direct sum of the generalized weight spaces with respect to the action of
\partial
(
cf.
Definition
2.2.2
). The goal of this section is defining a full abelian subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
which is analogous to the category
\mathcal{O}
for
R
defined in Section
2.1
. In order to overcome some of the analytic difficulties, we will start by introducing the analytic analogs of Verma modules:

Remark 3.1.8
.

From now on, we assume that
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are noetherian Banach
K
-algebras.

We start by showing the following technical lemmas:

Lemma 3.1.9
.

The following hold:

(i)

\mathscr{B}H
is a noetherian Banach
K
-algebra.

(ii)

\mathscr{B}H
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
.

(iii)

For each
\lambda\in K
we have
\mathscr{B}H_{\lambda}=B_{\lambda}\otimes H
. In particular, we have the following identities:

\mathscr{B}H^{\operatorname{ws}}=BH,\quad\Lambda(\mathscr{B}H)=\mathbb{Z}^{\leq 0}.

Proof.

First, as
\mathscr{B}
is noetherian and
H
is finite-dimensional, it follows that
\mathscr{B}H
is finite over
\mathscr{B}
. Hence, it is also a noetherian Banach
K
-algebra. By definition,
\mathscr{B}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, with
\mathscr{B}^{\operatorname{ws}}=B
. Choose
b\in B^{n}
, and
v\in H
, then
b\otimes v\in B^{n}\otimes H
, and we have the following identity in
R
:

\operatorname{ad}(\partial)(b\otimes v)=(\partial b-b\partial)\otimes v+b\otimes(\partial v-v\partial)=-n(b\otimes v).

In particular,
BH
is stable under the action of
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and we have
BH_{-n}=B^{n}\otimes_{K}H
. As the action of
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
is continuous,
\mathscr{B}H
is also stable under the action of
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
. Furthermore, due to continuity of the product in
\mathscr{B}H
, any element
\alpha\in\mathscr{B}H
admits the following expression:

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\otimes v_{i}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{n}\otimes v_{i}\right),

where
b_{i}^{n}\in B^{n}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and all
n\geq 0
. Thus,

\mathscr{B}H
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, with weight spaces:

\mathscr{B}H^{\operatorname{ws}}=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}\mathscr{B}H_{n}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}B^{n}\otimes_{K}H,

and this is precisely what we wanted to show.
∎

Lemma 3.1.10
.

For each
n\geq 0
, let
\mathscr{B}H^{>n}
be the two-sided ideal in
\mathscr{B}H
generated by:

BH^{>n}:=B^{>n}\otimes_{K}H.

There is a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathscr{B}H^{>n}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}H\rightarrow BH/BH^{>n}\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

As
\mathscr{B}H
is noetherian and Banach, it follows that
\mathscr{B}H^{>n}
is closed in
\mathscr{B}H
. Furthermore,
BH^{>n}
is dense in
\mathscr{B}H^{>n}
. By the description of the weight spaces of
\mathscr{B}H
given in the previous lemma we have the following identities:

BH^{>n}=\bigoplus_{m<n}\mathscr{B}H_{m},\quad BH/BH^{>n}=\bigoplus_{n\leq m\leq 0}\mathscr{B}H_{m}.

As each of the weight spaces in
\mathscr{B}H
is finite-dimensional,
and
BH/BH^{>n}
only contains finitely many weight spaces, it follows by Theorem
2.2.4
that we have a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathscr{B}H^{>n}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}H\rightarrow BH/BH^{>n}\rightarrow 0,

as we wanted to show.
∎

These lemmas allow us to define analytic Verma modules without significant changes:

Definition 3.1.11
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. The analytic Verma module of
W
is the following
\mathscr{R}
-module:

\widehat{\Delta}(W)=\mathscr{R}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{B}H}\left(\mathscr{B}H/\mathscr{B}H^{>0}\widehat{\otimes}_{H}W\right)=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W.

Notice that for
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, we have:

(4)

\widehat{\Delta}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}=\left(\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W\right)^{\operatorname{ws}}=A\otimes_{K}W=\Delta(W).

Hence, we recover the (algebraic) Verma modules studied in Section
2.1
as the weight spaces of analytic Verma modules. Furthermore, by Proposition
2.1.9
,
the action of
\partial
on each eigenspace in
\Delta(W)
is semi-simple, and we have
\Lambda(\widehat{\Delta}(W))=c(W)+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. Therefore, we conclude:

Proposition 3.1.12
.

For any
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, the analytic Verma module
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type. Furthermore, we have
\widehat{\Delta}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}=\Delta(W)
.

Next, we would like to analyze the structure of the quotients of analytic Verma modules. In particular, we aim to study the simple quotients of analytic Verma modules. As we have an explicit description of the weight spaces of analytic Verma modules, our strategy boils down to using Theorem
2.2.4
to reduce the problem to the algebraic situation, where matters are simpler.

Lemma 3.1.13
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
be an irreducible
H
-representation. Then we have an identity:

\widehat{\Delta}(W)=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}\Delta(W).

Proof.

By definition of Verma modules, we have the following identities:

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}\Delta(W)=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}\left(R\otimes_{BH}\left(BH/BH^{>0}\otimes_{H}W\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{R}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{B}H}\left(\mathscr{B}H\otimes_{BH}\left(BH/BH^{>0}\otimes_{H}W\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{R}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{B}H}\left(\mathscr{B}H/\mathscr{B}H^{>0}\widehat{\otimes}_{H}W\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\Delta}(W),

where the second identity follows from the fact that
BH/BH^{>}0\otimes_{H}W
is finite-dimensional, so
\mathscr{B}H\otimes_{BH}\left(BH/BH^{>}0\otimes_{H}W\right)
is a finite
\mathscr{B}H
-module. As
\mathscr{R}
is a noetherian Banach algebra, the completed tensor products agrees with the usual tensor product on finite modules.
∎

Proposition 3.1.14
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. The analytic Verma module
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
has a unique maximal submodule, and hence a unique simple quotient
\widehat{L}(W)
. This simple quotient satisfies:

\widehat{L}(W)=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}L(W),

where
L(W)
is the unique simple quotient of
\Delta(W)
.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
be an analytic Verma module. By equation
(\ref{equation ws decomposition of analytic verma modules})
we know that:

\Lambda(\widehat{\Delta}(W))=c(w)+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0},\textnormal{ and }\operatorname{dim}\left(\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{c(W)}\right)=1.

As every
v\in\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{c(W)}
generates
\widehat{\Delta}(W)

as a
\mathscr{R}
-module, and every
\mathscr{R}
-submodule of
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
admits a finite type decomposition into weight spaces for
\partial
(
cf.
Theorem
2.2.4
and Proposition
3.1.12
), it follows that the sum of all proper submodules of
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
has trivial intersection with
\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{c(W)}
, and therefore is a maximal proper submodule. Denote this maximal module by
\widehat{M}(W)
, and let
M(W)
be the maximal submodule of
\Delta(W)
. Then we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}\Delta(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}L(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{M}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Delta}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{L}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where the middle map is an isomorphism by Lemma
3.1.13
. As
\widehat{L}(W)
is simple, it follows that the map
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}L(W)\rightarrow\widehat{L}(W)
is surjective. Thus, it is an isomorphism if and only if the map:

\varphi:\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M(W)\rightarrow\widehat{M}(W),

is a surjection. As all modules considered are finite
\mathscr{R}
-modules, it suffices to show that the map has dense image. By construction, we have
\widehat{M}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}\subset\widehat{\Delta}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}=\Delta(W)
. As
M(W)
is the maximal submodule of
\Delta(W)
, it follows that
\widehat{M}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}\subset M(W)
. Hence, we have:

\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}\widehat{M}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M(W)\rightarrow\widehat{M}(W).

As
\widehat{M}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}
is dense in
\widehat{M}(W)
, it follows that
\varphi
has dense image, and therefore is surjective.
∎

Corollary 3.1.15
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
be an irreducible
H
-representation. We have an identification:

\widehat{L}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}=L(W).

Proof.

By the previous proposition, we have maps:

L(W)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}L(W)\rightarrow\widehat{L}(W),

where the second map is an isomorphism, and the first map has dense image. As
L(W)
is simple, it follows that
L(W)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}L(W)
is injective, has image contained in
(\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}L(W))^{\operatorname{ws}}
, and is dense. Hence, Corollary
2.2.5
implies that
\widehat{L}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}=L(W)
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Now that we have a good understanding of the structure of analytic Verma modules, the next step is studying how they map to objects in
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
. This is done in terms of singular vectors:

Definition 3.1.16

(
[
11
, Definition 3.23.]
)
.

Let
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
. An element
v\in M
is called singular if
B^{>0}v=0
. We let
M^{\operatorname{Sing}}\subset M
be the
K
-vector space of all singular vectors in
M
.

The singular vectors play the same role in this theory as the highest weight vectors play in the category
\mathcal{O}
of a semi-simple Lie algebra. Namely, we have the following:

Proposition 3.1.17
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. Then for any
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
we have an identification:

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}}(\widehat{\Delta}(W),M)=\{v\in M^{\operatorname{Sing}}\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }H\cdot v\cong W\textnormal{ or }H\cdot v=0\}.

Proof.

The proof is purely formal, as we have:

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}}(\widehat{\Delta}(W),M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{B}H}(\mathscr{B}H/\mathscr{B}H^{>0}\widehat{\otimes}_{H}W,M)=\\
\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(W,\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{B}H}(\mathscr{B}H/\mathscr{B}H^{>0},M))=\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(W,\operatorname{Hom}_{BH}(BH/BH^{>0},M)),

where the last identity follows by Lemma
3.1.10
. Next, we want to show the following identity:

\operatorname{Hom}_{BH}(BH/BH^{>0},M)=M^{\operatorname{Sing}}.

First, as
B^{>0}\subset BH^{>0}
, it follows that any
BH
-linear morphism
BH/BH^{>0}\rightarrow M
is uniquely determined by a vector
v\in M^{\operatorname{Sing}}
. We just need to show that any singular vector
v\in M^{\operatorname{Sing}}
determines a morphism
BH/BH^{>0}\rightarrow M
. Equivalently, we need to show that
BH^{>0}v=0
. Choose some simple tensor
b\otimes h\in BH^{>0}
. By definition of a triangular decomposition, we have
B\cdot H=H\cdot B
. Hence, we have an identity:

b\otimes h=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}\otimes b_{i},

for some
h_{i}\in H
and
b_{i}\in B
. The fact that
R
is a graded algebra and that
H\subset R_{0}
implies that we can assume
b_{i}\in B^{>0}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus,
bh\cdot v=0
, and the identity holds. Hence, we arrive at the following identity:

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{R}}(\widehat{\Delta}(W),M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(W,M^{\operatorname{Sing}}).

As
W
is an irreducible
H
-module, every
H
-linear map
W\rightarrow M^{\operatorname{Sing}}
is either zero or an isomorphism, and is determined by a singular vector
v\in M^{\operatorname{Sing}}
. Hence, the proposition holds.
∎

As an upshot of the proof, we get the following Corollary:

Corollary 3.1.18
.

Let
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
. Then
M^{\operatorname{Sing}}
is an
H
-module.

We are now finally ready to introduce the analytic version of the category
\mathcal{O}
:

Definition 3.1.19
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
be the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
given by modules
M
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

M
admits a weight space decomposition.

(ii)

M
is a finite
\mathscr{A}
-module.

A priory, it is not clear that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is a highest weight category. In particular, it is not even clear that it is an abelian category. This stems from the fact that Theorem
2.2.4
requires the decompositions to be semi-simple, and we are working in a more general setting. However, the fact that objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
are finite
\mathscr{A}
-modules imposes severe restrictions on the weight spaces:

Proposition 3.1.20
.

Let
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. The following hold:

(i)

M
admits a finite system of generators as an
\mathscr{A}
-module by generalized eigenvectors of
\partial
.

(ii)

There is a finite family of weights
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\Lambda(M)
such that
M
admits a weight space decomposition with respect to
I=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

By definition of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, the set of all generalized eigenvectors of
\partial
is a dense
K
-vector subspace of
M
. As
\mathscr{A}
is noetherian and Banach, and
M
is finitely generated, it follows that the
\mathscr{A}
-module generated by generalized eigenvectors of
\partial
is dense and closed in
M
. Thus, it agrees with
M
. Therefore, we can choose a finite family of generalized eigenvectors
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
which generate
M
as an
\mathscr{A}
-module. Assume that
x_{i}\in M_{\lambda_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. We claim that
\{\lambda\}_{i=1}^{n}
satisfies the properties in
(ii)
.

Indeed, every
x\in M
admits an expression
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}
, with
a_{i}\in\mathscr{A}
. By assumption, the action of
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
on
\mathscr{A}
admits a semisimple weight space decomposition of compact type. Hence, we have
a_{i}=\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{i}^{m}
, where
a_{i}^{m}\in A_{m}
for each
m\geq 0
. In particular, for each
1\leq i\leq n
and each
m\geq 0
we have
a_{i}^{m}x_{i}\in M_{\lambda_{i}+m}
. As the family
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is finite, we have the following identity:

(5)

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{i}^{m}x_{i}=\sum_{\lambda\in I}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda}x_{i}\right),

where
a_{i}^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda}=0
if
\lambda_{i}-\lambda\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. By the above, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda}x_{i}\in M_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda\in I
.
∎

The explicit structure of the decomposition found in Proposition
3.1.20
is instrumental for our understanding of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. In particular, the idea is using this decomposition to show that every object in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
admits a filtration by objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
such that its graded quotients are quotients of analytic Verma modules. Let us start with the following lemmas:

Lemma 3.1.21
.

The category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is closed under quotients by
\mathscr{R}
-submodules.

Proof.

Let
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
and let
N\subset M
be a
\mathscr{R}
-submodule. As
M
is a finite
\mathscr{A}
-module, it is clear that
M/N
is a finite
\mathscr{A}
-module. The proof that
M/N
admits a weight space decomposition is analogous to the proof of Lemma
2.2.6
.
∎

Lemma 3.1.22
.

For
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
and
\lambda\in K
consider the following
K
-vector space:

M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{\lambda}:=\{m\in M_{\lambda}\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }\partial m=\lambda m\}

Then
M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{\lambda}
is
\partial
-invariant. Furthermore, if
M_{\lambda}\neq 0
, then
M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{\lambda}\neq 0
.

Proof.

Assume
M_{\lambda}\neq 0
, and let
v\in M_{\lambda}
. By definition, there is some
n\geq 0
such that
(\partial-\lambda)^{n}v=0
. In particular, the
K
-vector space
V=\sum_{n\geq 0}(\partial-\lambda)^{n}v
has finite dimension. By construction,
V
is invariant under the action of
(\partial-\lambda)
, and it is clearly invariant under the action of
\lambda
. Hence, it is a
\partial
-invariant finite dimensional space such that
(\partial-\lambda)^{n}_{|V}=0
. But then it follows from the existence of the Jordan canonical form that there is some
w\in V
such that
\partial w=\lambda w
.
∎

Lemma 3.1.23
.

Let
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. Choose a finite family
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\Lambda(M)
, and let
I=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. Consider the following
K
-vector space:

M_{I}^{\operatorname{ss}}:=\{m\in M\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }m=\sum_{\lambda\in I}m_{\lambda},\textnormal{ where }m_{\lambda}\in M_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ss}}\textnormal{ for all }\lambda\in I\}.

Then
M_{I}^{\operatorname{ss}}
is a
\partial
-invariant
\mathscr{A}
-submodule of
M
. In particular, it is a Banach space.

Proof.

It is clear that
M_{I}^{\operatorname{ss}}
is a
K
-vector space. We need to see that it is closed under the action of
\mathscr{A}
. First, notice that for
a\in A_{n}
and
v\in M_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ss}}
we have
\partial(av)=(n+\lambda)av
. Hence, we have
A_{n}\cdot M_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ss}}\subset M_{\lambda+n}^{\operatorname{ss}}
. Next, let
x\in M_{I}^{\operatorname{ss}}
, and
a\in\mathscr{A}
. By definition of
M_{I}^{\operatorname{ss}}
and
\mathscr{A}
we have expressions:

x=\sum_{\lambda\in I}m_{\lambda}\quad a=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}.

Thus, proceeding in the same way as in equation
(\ref{equation ws decomposition objects of OX})
we get:

ax=\sum_{\lambda\in I}\left(\sum_{n+\gamma=\lambda}a_{n}m_{\gamma}\right).

As the family
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is finite, for each
\lambda\in I
there are only finitely many
\gamma\in I
such that
\lambda-\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. Thus, each of the terms
\sum_{n+\gamma=\lambda}a_{n}m_{\gamma}
is a well-defined element of
M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{\lambda}
, so
ax\in M_{I}^{\operatorname{ss}}
. The fact that
M_{I}^{\operatorname{ss}}
is
\partial
-invariant is straightforward.
∎

We may use this to obtain a description of the elements of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
in terms of analytic Verma modules:

Proposition 3.1.24
.

Let
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. Then
M
admits a finite filtration by
\mathscr{R}
-modules:

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n}=M,

such that
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is a quotient of
\widehat{\Delta}(W_{i})
for
W_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. We call such a filtration a
\widehat{\Delta}
-filtration.

Proof.

As
\mathscr{R}
is noetherian and
M
is finite, every ascending chain of submodules in
M
is stationary. Furthermore, by Lemma
3.1.21
,
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is closed under quotients by
\mathscr{R}
-submodules. Thus, it suffices to show that for every
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
there is some
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
with a non-zero map
\widehat{\Delta}(W)\rightarrow M
. By Proposition
3.1.17
, this amounts to showing that
M^{\operatorname{Sing}}\neq 0
.

Choose a countable family
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\Lambda(M)
. For each
n\geq 1
, let
I_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. By Lemma
3.1.23
, we have an increasing sequence of
\partial
-invariant
\mathscr{A}
-submodules of
M
:

0\subset M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{I_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{I_{n}}\subset\cdots

As
\mathscr{A}
is noetherian and
M
is finite, there is some
m\geq 1
such that the sequence stabilizes. Let
M^{\operatorname{ss}}:=M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{I_{m}}
. Again, by Lemma
3.1.23
,
M^{\operatorname{ss}}
is a Banach space with a semi-simple weight space decomposition of compact type. In particular, by Theorem
2.2.4
the weight spaces of
M^{\operatorname{ss}}
are uniquely determined by the decomposition. In other words, we have:

(6)

\Lambda(M^{\operatorname{ss}})\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}.

By Lemma
3.1.22
, for any
\lambda\in\Lambda(M)
we have
M^{\operatorname{ss}}_{\lambda}\neq 0
. But then, by maximality of
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
and equation
(\ref{equation determination of set of weights})
, it follows that
\Lambda(M)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
.

Choose any
\lambda_{i}
such that
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Notice that this is always possible, as
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
induces a partial order relation in
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, and
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a finite set. For every
n\geq 0
, and
b\in B^{n}
, we have
[\partial,b]=-nb
. Thus, our choice of
\lambda_{i}
implies that
M_{\lambda_{i}}\subset M^{\operatorname{Sing}}
. Thus,
M^{\operatorname{Sing}}\neq 0
, as we wanted to show.
∎

This proposition allows us to describe the weight spaces of objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
in terms of the weight spaces of analytic Verma modules, as well as showing uniqueness of the weight space decomposition:

Corollary 3.1.25
.

Let
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. The following hold:

(i)

There is a finite family
\{W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\operatorname{Irr}(H)
satisfying that:

\Lambda(M)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}c(W_{i})+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}.

Furthermore, for every
\lambda\in\Lambda(M)
, the space
M_{\lambda}
is finite-dimensional.

(ii)

Every module appearing in a
\widehat{\Delta}
-filtration of
M
is an object in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

In light of propositions
3.1.12
and
3.1.24
, all the statements follow from Proposition
2.2.7
.
∎

Now that the analytic structure of the objects of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
has been clarified, it is time to start studying the algebraic structure of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. In order to do this, we will upgrade the
\widehat{\Delta}
-filtrations obtained above to finite Jordan-Hölder series:

Proposition 3.1.26
.

Every object in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
admits a finite Jordan-Hölder series (also called composition series). That is, given
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
there is a finite filtration by objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
:

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n}=M,

satisfying that
M_{i}/M_{i-1}=\widehat{L}(W_{i})
for some
W_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1.24
, it suffices to show that every analytic Verma module
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
admits a Jordan-Hölder series. As
\mathscr{R}
is noetherian, it suffices to show that every quotient of an analytic Verma module admits a simple submodule. Let
M
be a quotient of
\widehat{\Delta}(W)
. If
M
does not admit a simple submodule, then there is a descending sequence
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

satisfying that
M_{n+1}\subsetneq M_{n}
. In particular, by part
(v)
in Theorem
2.2.4
we get a decreasing family
\{M_{n}^{\operatorname{ws}}\}_{n\geq 0}
of subobjects of
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
. By claim
(iii)
in
2.2.4
, the fact that
\widehat{\Delta}(W)\rightarrow M
is a surjection implies that
\Delta(W)\rightarrow M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is also a surjection. Hence, we have
M\in\mathcal{O}
. As
\mathcal{O}
is an artinian category, we have
M_{n}^{\operatorname{ws}}=M_{n+1}^{\operatorname{ws}}
for sufficiently high
n
.
Thus, a second application of Theorem
2.2.4
shows that
M_{n}=M_{n+1}
for sufficiently high
n
, contradicting the fact that
M
does not have a simple submodule.
∎

The existence of Jordan-Hölder series for objects in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
allows us to deduce a plethora of conclusions:

Corollary 3.1.27
.

The category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is a full abelian subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
. It is also closed under
\mathscr{R}
-submodules and finite direct sums.

Proof.

We saw in Lemma
3.1.21
that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is closed under taking quotients by
\mathscr{R}
-submodules, and it is clear that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is closed under finite direct sums. Hence, the only thing we need to prove is that for every
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, and every
\mathscr{R}
-submodule
N\subset M
, we have
N\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. However, by the previous proposition
M
has a Jordan-Holder series:

0\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n}=M,

which satisfies that
M_{i}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
. We proceed by induction on the length of the composition series for
M
, the base case being that
M
is a simple
\mathscr{R}
-module. There are two possible cases: If
M/N
is a simple
\mathscr{R}
-module, then by uniqueness of the factors in a composition series there is some
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
such that
M/N\cong\widehat{L}(W)
. In this situation, as
M
admits a weight space decomposition of compact type, and
\widehat{L}(W)
admits a semisimple weight space decomposition of finite type. Hence, Lemma
2.2.6
implies that
N
also admits a weight space decomposition. As
\mathscr{A}
is noetherian,
N
is also a finite
\mathscr{A}
-module, so we have
N\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. If
M/N
is not simple, then there is an
\mathscr{R}
-submodule
T\subset M
satisfying that
M/T
is simple,
N\subset T
, and
T
has a composition series of length
n-1
. The same arguments as above show that
T\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, so we may apply the induction hypothesis again to show that
N\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, as wanted.
∎

Corollary 3.1.28
.

There is an exact functor of abelian categories:

(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}.

Proof.

We need to show that given
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, the
R
-module
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is an object in
\mathcal{O}
. Again, we proceed by induction on the length of a Jordan-Hölder series of
M
, the base case being Corollary
3.1.15
. In the general case, let
N\subset M
such that
M/N
is simple. We have a left exact sequence:

(7)

0\rightarrow N^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow M^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow(M/N)^{\operatorname{ws}}.

By Proposition
3.1.26
, there is some
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
such that
M/N=\widehat{L}(W)
. In particular,
(M/N)^{\operatorname{ws}}=L(W)
is a simple
R
-module. Hence, either the sequence
(\ref{equation definition of ws functor})
is a short exact sequence, or
N^{\operatorname{ws}}=M^{\operatorname{ws}}
. If the sequence is short exact, then the induction hypothesis, together with the fact that
\mathcal{O}
is a Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
, imply that
M^{\operatorname{ws}}\in\mathcal{O}
. If the sequence is not right exact, then we have
N^{\operatorname{ws}}=M^{\operatorname{ws}}
. However, as
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is dense in
M
, this would imply
N=M
, and this is false.

Next, we need to show
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}
is exact. As it is clearly left exact, we need to show that given an epimorphism
M\rightarrow N
in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, the corresponding map
M^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow N^{\operatorname{ws}}
is also an epimorphism. Again, we may proceed by induction of the length of a Jordan-Hölder series of
N
. The base case was already shown above. If
N
is not simple, there is some
\tilde{N}\subset N
such that
N/\tilde{N}=\widehat{L}(W)
for some
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. In this case, we have the following commutative diagram with exact rows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{L}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{L}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

as the middle map is a surjection and the rightmost map is an isomorphism,
\tilde{M}\rightarrow\tilde{N}
is a surjection. Thus, by the induction hypothesis we have that
\tilde{M}^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow\tilde{N}^{\operatorname{ws}}
is also a surjection. Applying
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}
to the previous diagram implies that
M^{\operatorname{ws}}\rightarrow N^{\operatorname{ws}}
is a surjection, as we wanted to show.
∎

Corollary 3.1.29
.

Let
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, then
M^{\operatorname{Sing}}
is a finite dimensional
K
-vector space.

Proof.

Assume that
M=\widehat{L}(W)
for
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. By Proposition
3.1.14
, for any
E\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}(\widehat{\Delta}(E),\widehat{L}(W))=0
if
W\neq E
. Hence, by Proposition
3.1.17
we have
\widehat{L}(W)^{\operatorname{Sing}}\subset\widehat{L}(W)_{c(W)}
, and this is finite-dimensional by
(i)
in Corollary
3.1.25
. The general case follows from the existence of Jordan-Hölder series, together with the fact that
(-)^{\operatorname{Sing}}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect}_{K}
is a left exact functor.
∎

With all these tools at our disposal, we can start working towards the main theorem of the section. Namely, we want to show that the functor
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
constructed in Corollary
3.1.28
is an equivalence of abelian categories. In particular, this will show that
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is a highest weight category. We will achieve this by showing that the extension of scalars along the map
R\rightarrow\mathscr{R}
is an inverse to this functor. In order to do this, we recall the generalized versions of Verma modules:

\Delta(W)_{n}=R\otimes_{BH}\left(BH/BH^{>n}\otimes_{H}W\right)=A\otimes_{K}(B/B^{>n}\otimes_{K}W),

where
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. The relevance of these modules stems from the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1.30
.

The following hold:

(i)

For every
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
and every
n\geq 0
we have
\Delta(W)_{n}\in\mathcal{O}
.

(ii)

A module
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(R)
is an object in
\mathcal{O}
if and only if it admits a surjection:

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\Delta(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M.

Proof.

This is a consequence of Proposition
2.1.6
and the definition of
\mathcal{O}
.
∎

As a consequence of the previous lemma, every module
M\in\mathcal{O}
admits a two-term resolution:

(8)

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\Delta(W_{j})_{m_{j}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\Delta(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M.

In practice, this means that we can use these objects to study the behavior of right exact functors.

As expected, there are analytic versions of these objects. In particular, Lemma
3.1.10
allows us to make the following definition:

Definition 3.1.31
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. The generalized analytic Verma module of level
n
of
W
is the following
\mathscr{R}
-module:

\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{n}=\mathscr{R}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{B}H}\left(\mathscr{B}H/\mathscr{B}H^{>n}\widehat{\otimes}_{H}W\right)=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}(B/B^{>n}\otimes_{K}W).

Where in the last identity we are using the fact that
\mathscr{B}H/\mathscr{B}H^{>n}
is a finite
K
-vector space.

Proposition 3.1.32
.

Choose
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
and
n\geq 0
. Then
\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{n}
satisfies the following:

(i)

\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{n}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
.

(ii)

\widehat{\Delta}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}_{n}=\Delta(W)_{n}
.

(iii)

There is a canonical isomorphism
\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{n}=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}\Delta(W)_{n}
.

Proof.

As
B/B^{>n}\otimes_{K}W
is a finite
K
-vector space, it is clear that
\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{n}
is a finite
\mathscr{A}
-module. Furthermore, the explicit description of
\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{n}
yields:

\widehat{\Delta}(W)^{\operatorname{ws}}_{n}=(\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}(B/B^{>n}\otimes_{K}W))^{\operatorname{ws}}=A\otimes_{K}(B/B^{>n}\otimes_{K}W)=\Delta(W)_{n},

and this shows
(i)
and
(ii)
. Statement
(iii)
follows exactly as in the proof of Lemma
3.1.13
.
∎

We are now finally ready to show the main theorem of the section:

Lemma 3.1.33
.

The extension of scalars along the map
R\rightarrow\mathscr{R}
induces a right exact functor:

\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}-:\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}
. By Lemma
3.1.30
we have a surjection
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Delta(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M
. By Proposition
3.1.32
this induces a surjection
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{\Delta}(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M
. The category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
is closed under direct sums by Corollary
3.1.27
and by
\mathscr{R}
-submodules by Lemma
3.1.21
. Thus,
M
is an object of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Theorem 3.1.34
.

There is an equivalence of abelian categories:

\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}-:\mathcal{O}\leftrightarrows\widehat{\mathcal{O}}:(-)^{\operatorname{ws}},

which makes
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
a highest weight category.

Proof.

For every
M\in\mathcal{O}
we denote
\widehat{M}:=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M
for simplicity. We want to show that
M=(\widehat{M})^{\operatorname{ws}}
. By Lemma
3.1.30
we have a right exact sequence:

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\Delta(W_{j})_{m_{j}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\Delta(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M.

As
\widehat{(-)}
is right exact and
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}
is exact, we get the following commutative diagram with exact rows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\Delta(W_{j})_{n_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\Delta(W_{i})_{n_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\widehat{\Delta}(W_{j})^{\operatorname{ws}}_{n_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\widehat{\Delta}(W_{i})^{\operatorname{ws}}_{n_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\widehat{M})^{\operatorname{ws}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

By Proposition
3.1.32
, the first two vertical maps are isomorphisms. Thus
M\rightarrow(\widehat{M})^{\operatorname{ws}}
is also an isomorphism, as we wanted to show.

Next, choose
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
. We need to show that
M=\widehat{(M^{\operatorname{ws}})}
. We proceed by induction on the length of a Jordan-Hölder series for
M
, the base case being Proposition
3.1.14
and Corollary
3.1.15
. Let
N\subset M
such that
M/N
is simple. We have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}N^{\operatorname{ws}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M^{\operatorname{ws}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}(M/N)^{\operatorname{ws}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M/N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

By the induction hypothesis, the left and right vertical maps are isomorphisms, so the middle map is also an isomorphism. Thus,
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}-
and
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}
are mutually inverse equivalences of categories.
∎

Remark 3.1.35
.

Notice that we did not require the map
R\rightarrow\mathscr{R}
to be flat for the preceding theorem. However, in the case of rational Cherednik algebras, this map is always flat (cf. Theorem
4.4.9
).

As a consequence of
3.1.34
the structural elements of a highest weight category (
ie
. standard objects, simple objects, projective covers,
etc
) of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
are obtained from
\mathcal{O}
via extension of scalars. Notice that even if
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
has enough projectives, these objects are not projective in
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
. In particular, the canonical map between the derived categories:

\operatorname{D}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})),

is not necessarily fully faithful. Furthermore, it is not even clear that for objects
M,N\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
we have:

(9)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}(M,N)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(M,N).

This is in stark contrast with the algebraic situation, where the fact that
\mathcal{O}
is a Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
implies that equation
(\ref{equation analytic OX not a Serre subcat})
always holds. The crux of the matter is that given a
\partial
-equivariant short exact sequence of Banach spaces:

0\rightarrow V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}\rightarrow V_{3}\rightarrow 0,

such that
V_{1}
and
V_{3}
admit weight space decompositions, it is in general not possible to assure that
V_{2}
will also admit a weight space decomposition.

As the final proposition of this section, we will give a couple of alternative definitions of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}:

Proposition 3.1.36
.

Let
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
.

(ii)

There is an epimorphism
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\widehat{\Delta}(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M
.

(iii)

There is a finite family
\{W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\operatorname{Irr}(H)
satisfying that:
\Lambda(M)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, each
M_{\lambda}
is finite dimensional, and
M
admits a weight space decomposition.

(iv)

The action of
B
on
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is locally nilpotent, and
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is dense in
M
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.1.34
that
(i)
is equivalent to
(ii)
, and that it implies
(iii)
and
(iv)
. Furthermore,
(iii)
implies
(iv)
by choosing
I=\Lambda(M)
, and noticing that
M_{\lambda-n}=0
for big enough
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. Hence, assume that
M
satisfies
(iv)
, we need to see that
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
.

By construction,
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is a
R
-module such that the action of
B
on
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is locally nilpotent. Thus, by Proposition
2.1.10
we have a surjection:

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\Delta(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M^{\operatorname{ws}}.

Hence, we get a
\mathscr{R}
-linear map
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\widehat{\Delta}(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M
. As the image of this map contains
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
, it is dense. As
M
is a finite
\mathscr{R}
-module, it follows that the map is surjective. Furthermore, by finiteness, there is a finite set
J\subset I
such that the map

\bigoplus_{i\in J}\widehat{\Delta}(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M
is still surjective. Thus,
M
satisfies
(ii)
, and therefore is an object in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Remark 3.1.37
.

Unlike in the algebraic situation, the condition that
M
is a finitely generated
\mathscr{R}
-module is necessary. In particular, there are Banach
\mathscr{R}
-modules which admit a finite type decomposition into weight spaces satisfying
(iii)
which are not finitely generated
\mathscr{R}
-modules.

3.2.
Fréchet-Stein algebras with a triangular decomposition

We will now extend the previous theory to the context of Fréchet-Stein algebras. Let us start by recalling the definition:

Definition 3.2.1
.

A topological
K
-algebra
\mathscr{R}
is called Fréchet-Stein if there is an isomorphism of topological
K
-algebras:

\mathscr{R}=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{R}(n)

where each
\mathscr{R}(n)
is a two-sided noetherian Banach
K
-algebra, and the transition maps
\mathscr{R}_{(n+1)}\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
are (two-sided) flat with dense image for each
n\geq 0
. We call the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{R}(n)
a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathscr{R}
.

These algebras where first defined in
[
32
]
, where the following category of modules is introduced:

Definition 3.2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{R}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{R}(n)
be a Fréchet-Stein algebra. We say a
\mathscr{R}
-module
M
is co-admissible if there is an
\mathscr{R}
-linear isomorphism:

M=\varprojlim_{n}M(n),

where each
M(n)
is a finite
\mathscr{R}(n)
-module. We call
\varprojlim_{n}M(n)
a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
M
, and denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
given by all the co-admissible modules by
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
.

Notice that the Fréchet-Stein presentation
M=\varprojlim_{n}M(n)
endows
M
with a Fréchet topology. As a consequence of
[
32
, Corollary 3.3]
, the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
is independent of choice of Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathscr{R}
. Furthermore, the topology on
M
is also independent of choice of Fréchet-Stein presentation of
M
. Thus, we will call this topology the canonical topology as a co-admissible
\mathscr{R}
-module, and we will always regard co-admissible
\mathscr{R}
-modules as topological
\mathscr{R}
-modules with respect to this topology. Let us now introduce triangular decompositions for Fréchet-Stein algebras:

Definition 3.2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{R}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{R}(n)
be a Fréchet-Stein algebra. A triangular decomposition of
\mathscr{R}
consists of a tuple
(R,A,B,\partial)
satisfying the following:

(i)

R\subset\mathscr{R}
is a dense graded subalgebra, and
\partial\in R
.

(ii)

The map
R\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
is injective for each
n\geq 0
.

(iii)

The tuple
(R,A,B,\partial)
is a triangular decomposition of
\mathscr{R}(n)
for each
n\geq 0
.

Remark 3.2.4
.

Notice that the triangular decomposition depends on the presentation
\mathscr{R}=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{R}(n)
.

Morally, a triangular decomposition of a Fréchet-Stein algebra is a family of decomposition which behave well with respect to the transition maps. For the rest of this section, we fix a Fréchet-Stein algebra
\mathscr{R}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{R}(n)
, with a triangular decomposition

(R,A,B,\partial)
. As before, for each
n\geq 0
we let
\mathscr{A}(n)
, and
\mathscr{B}(n)
be the closures in
\mathscr{R}
of
A
and
B
respectively. Let us begin the section by showing some properties of triangular decompositions:

Proposition 3.2.5
.

The transition maps in the presentation
\mathscr{R}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{R}_{n}
induce inverse limits:

\mathscr{A}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{A}(n),\quad\mathscr{B}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{B}(n),

and there is an isomorphism of Fréchet spaces
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}
.

Proof.

First, notice that the fact that
R\subset\mathscr{R}(n)
for each
n\geq 0
implies that the transition maps
\mathscr{R}(n+1)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
restrict to an isomorphism on
R
. Thus, they also restrict to isomorphisms on
A
and
B
. Hence, we get our desired inverse systems

\mathscr{A}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{A}(n),
and
\mathscr{B}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{B}(n)
. The last statement follows from the general fact that given an inverse system of Banach spaces
(V_{n})_{n\geq 0}
such that the transition maps
V_{n+1}\rightarrow V_{n}
have dense image, we have
R^{1}\varprojlim_{n}V_{n}=0
(
cf
.
[
18
, Remark 13.2.4]
). Indeed, this implies that for each
m\geq 0
we have:

(10)

0\rightarrow\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}(m)\rightarrow\left(\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{A}(n)\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}(m)\rightarrow\left(\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{A}(n)\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}(m)\rightarrow 0,\\
0\rightarrow\mathscr{B}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{A}(m)\rightarrow\left(\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{B}(n)\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{A}(m)\rightarrow\left(\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{B}(n)\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{A}(m)\rightarrow 0.

Furthermore, for any Banach space
W
and any family of Banach spaces
(V_{n})_{n\geq 0}
we have:

\left(\prod_{n\geq 0}V_{n}\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W=\prod_{n\geq 0}V_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W,

as shown, for example in
[
6
, Section 5.2]
. Thus, combining this identity with
(\ref{equation tensor product of Frechet spaces})
shows that:

\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}=\varprojlim_{n,m\geq 0}\mathscr{A}(n)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}(m)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{A}(n)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}(n)=\varprojlim_{n}\mathscr{R}(n),

where the last identity follows by definition of a triangular decomposition of a Banach algebra.
∎

Corollary 3.2.6
.

The following hold:

(i)

\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are Fréchet
K
-algebras.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}\cdot H=H\cdot\mathscr{A}
, and
\mathscr{B}\cdot H=H\cdot\mathscr{B}
.

We let
\mathscr{A}H
and
\mathscr{B}H
be the algebras
\mathscr{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H
and
\mathscr{B}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H
.

Proof.

In view of the previous proposition, it follows from the corresponding fact in the Banach setting. Namely, Proposition
3.1.2
.
∎

Hence, the fact that the decomposition
\mathscr{R}(n)=\mathscr{A}(n)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}H\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}(n)
holds at the level of Banach spaces for each
n\geq 0
implies that this decomposition holds at the level of Fréchet spaces. This will be a recurring theme in the theory of triangular decompositions of Fréchet-Stein algebras. Before showing the following proposition, we mention that there is an analog of Theorem
2.2.4
for Fréchet spaces. Namely, this is contained in
[
31
, Section 2]
. Hence, we will use the results of Theorem
2.2.4
in this generality without further mention.

Proposition 3.2.7
.

The following hold:

(i)

\mathscr{A}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and
\mathscr{A}^{\operatorname{ws}}=A
.

(ii)

\mathscr{B}
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and
\mathscr{B}^{\operatorname{ws}}=B
.

(iii)

If each
\mathscr{R}(n)
admits a a semi-simple weight space decomposition for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, then so does
\mathscr{R}
.

Proof.

We will only show
(i)
, the rest are analogous. By definition of a triangular decomposition, for each
n\geq 0
the space
\mathscr{A}(n)
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type, and
\mathscr{A}(n)^{\operatorname{ws}}=A
. In particular, each
v\in\mathscr{A}(m)
admits a unique expression:

v=\sum_{n\geq 0}v_{n},\textnormal{ where }v_{n}\in A_{n}.

Hence, we may regard
\mathscr{A}=\varprojlim_{m\geq 0}\mathscr{A}(m)
as the space of all infinite sums
\sum_{n\geq 0}v_{n}
, satisfying
v_{n}\in A_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
, and such that the sum
\sum_{n\geq 0}v_{n}
converges in
\mathscr{A}(m)
for all
m\geq 0
. In particular, every
v\in\mathscr{A}
admits an expression as an infinite sum of weight vectors, as we wanted to show.
∎

We will dedicate the rest of the section to studying the representation theory of
\mathscr{R}
. As in the Banach case, we need to impose some finiteness conditions in order to obtain a good theory.

Remark 3.2.8
.

For the rest of the section, we will assume that the inverse limits:

\mathscr{A}=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{A}(n),\quad\mathscr{B}=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{B}(n)

are Fréchet-Stein presentations for
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
respectively.

By Theorem
3.1.34
, for each
n\geq 0
we have a highest weight category
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)\subset\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R}(n))
, together with a pair of mutually inverse equivalences of categories:

(11)

\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{R}-:\mathcal{O}\leftrightarrows\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n):(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}.

Let us now study the behavior of the
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
under the transition maps of the presentation of
\mathscr{R}
:

Lemma 3.2.9
.

For
n\geq 0
let
\rho(n):\mathscr{R}(n+1)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
. The extension of scalars:

\rho(n)^{*}:=\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{\mathscr{R}(n+1)}-:\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n+1)\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n),

is an equivalence of categories. Furthermore, we have an identity of functors:
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}=(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}\circ\rho(n)^{*}
.

Proof.

For each
n\geq 0
, let
i(n)^{*}
denote the extension of scalars along the map
i(n):R\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
. It follows by definition of a triangular decomposition that we have
i(n)=\rho(n)\circ i(n+1)
. In particular, by Theorem
3.1.34
, we know that
\rho(n)^{*}:\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R}(n+1))\rightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R}(n))
restricts to a functor
\rho(n)^{*}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n+1)\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
, and we have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho(n)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i(n+1)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i(n)^{*}}

as
i(n)^{*}
and
i(n+1)^{*}
are equivalences, it follows that
\rho(n)^{*}
is an equivalence. Furthermore, as
i(n)^{*}
and
i(n+1)^{*}
have inverse
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}
, it follows that we have
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}=(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}\circ\rho(n)^{*}
, as wanted.
∎

Finally, we can define the category
\mathcal{O}
of a Fréchet-Stein algebra with a triangular decomposition:

Definition 3.2.10
.

We define the category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
of
\mathscr{R}
as the following inverse limit:

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}=\varprojlim\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n).

Let us now give the first examples of objects in
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
: For
n,m\geq 0
and
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, we let:

\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{m}^{n}:=\mathscr{A}(n)\otimes_{K}\left(B/B^{>m}\otimes_{K}H\right),

be the analytic generalized Verma module of level
m
of
\mathscr{R}(n)
. Then we have:

\wideparen{\Delta}(W)_{m}:=\varprojlim\widehat{\Delta}(W)_{m}^{n}=\varprojlim\mathscr{A}(n)\otimes_{K}(B/B^{m>0}\otimes_{K}W)=\mathscr{A}\otimes_{K}\left(B/B^{m>0}\otimes_{K}W\right),

where the last identity follows from the fact that
B/B^{m>0}\otimes_{K}W
is finite-dimensional.

Definition 3.2.11
.

We call
\wideparen{\Delta}(W)_{m}
the co-admissible Verma module of level
m
of
W
.

Our thorough study of the categories
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
allows us to show multiple properties of
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
:

Proposition 3.2.12
.

The category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is a full abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
.

(ii)

Let
M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
. Then
M
is a Fréchet space which admits a finite-type weight space decomposition with respect to the action of the grading element
\partial\in\mathscr{R}
.

(iii)

There is a pair of mutually inverse equivalences of abelian categories:

\wideparen{(-)}:=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}-:\mathcal{O}\leftrightarrows\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}:(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}.

(iv)

Every
M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is a co-admissible
\mathscr{A}
-module.

Proof.

By definition of co-admissible module, we have
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})=\varprojlim\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R}(n))
, where the transition maps are given by extension of scalars along the transition maps
\rho(n):\mathscr{R}(n+1)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(n)
. As
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
is a full abelian subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R}(n))
for each
n\geq 0
, it follows that
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is a full abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
. Thus, statement
(i)
holds.

Choose
M=\varprojlim M(n)\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
. By Lemma
3.2.9
, we have the following identities:

(M(n))^{\operatorname{ws}}=(M(n+1))^{\operatorname{ws}}\textnormal{ for any }n\geq 0.

Hence, we can define
M^{\operatorname{ws}}:=(M(n))^{\operatorname{ws}}
for any
n\geq 0
. Furthermore, by Theorem
3.1.34
we have
M^{\operatorname{ws}}\in\mathcal{O}
, and
M(n)
admits a weight space decomposition of finite type for each
n\geq 0
. Thus, it follows just as in the proof of Proposition
3.2.7
that
M
admits a finite-type weight space decomposition with respect to
\partial
, with weight spaces given by
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
. Thus, statement
(ii)
holds, and the weight space functor
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}:\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
is well-defined.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}
, then
M
is a finitely presented
R
-module, so
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M
is a finitely presented
\mathscr{R}
-module. In particular, it is a co-admissible module. Furthermore, we have:

\wideparen{M}=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M=\varprojlim\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{R}M,

which shows that
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
. Let us now show that this functor is an equivalence, with quasi-inverse
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}
. Choose some
M\in\mathcal{O}
, then by the proof of
(ii)
, we have:

(12)

\wideparen{M}^{\operatorname{ws}}=(\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{R}M)^{\operatorname{ws}}=M,\textnormal{ for any }n\geq 0,

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.2.9
, every
\wideparen{M}\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
satisfies:

\wideparen{M}=\varprojlim\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{R}M=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M,

for a unique
M\in\mathcal{O}
. Thus,
\wideparen{(-)}
is essentially surjective, and hence an equivalence. The arguments above show that
(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}
is a quasi-inverse.

For the last statement, choose
m\geq 0
and
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. Then we have:

\wideparen{\Delta}(W)_{m}:=\mathscr{A}\otimes_{K}(B/B^{m>0}\otimes_{K}W),

Thus, every generalized Verma module
\wideparen{\Delta}_{m}(W)
is a finite-free
\mathscr{A}
-module, and therefore is co-admissible. As
\mathcal{O}
is a Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
, Proposition
2.1.6
shows that for any
M\in\mathcal{O}
we have a right exact sequence:

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\Delta(W_{j})_{m_{j}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Delta(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0.

Applying
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}-
to this sequence yields the right exact sequence:

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\wideparen{\Delta}(W_{j})_{m_{j}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\wideparen{\Delta}(W_{i})_{n_{i}}\rightarrow\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M\rightarrow 0.

As the first two terms are co-admissible
\mathscr{A}
-modules, it follows by
[
32
, Corollary 3.4]
that the
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}M
is also co-admissible, as wanted.
∎

Hence,
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is a highest weight category, with standard objects given by the co-admissible Verma modules:

\wideparen{\Delta}(W):=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}\Delta(W)=\mathscr{A}\otimes_{K}W,

where
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. The simple objects are given by the following quotients of Verma modules:

\wideparen{L}(W):=\mathscr{R}\otimes_{R}L(W).

Let us now analyze the properties of co-admissible Verma modules in more detail:

Lemma 3.2.13
.

The category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
is closed under taking closed subobjects.

Proof.

Let
M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
and let
N\subset M
be a closed subobject. Then
N
is co-admissible, so we have:

N=\varprojlim\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{\mathscr{R}}N,\textnormal{ and }\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{\mathscr{R}}N\subset\mathscr{R}(n)\otimes_{\mathscr{R}}M,

By Corollary
3.1.27
the categories
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
are closed under taking subobjects. Hence, the result follows.
∎

Proposition 3.2.14
.

Let
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, then
\wideparen{\Delta}(W)
has a unique maximal closed submodule, whose associated quotient is
\wideparen{L}(W)
. In particular,
\wideparen{L}(W)
is a simple object in
\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
.

Proof.

Let
N\subset\wideparen{\Delta}(W)
be a closed submodule. By the previous lemma, it is an object in
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
. Hence, it has a weight space decomposition with respect to
\partial
, and we have
N^{\operatorname{ws}}\subset\Delta(W)
. The Verma module
\Delta(W)
has a maximal proper submodule
M(W)
. Hence, we have
N^{\operatorname{ws}}\subset M(W)
, which implies that
N\subset\wideparen{M}(W)
. As
\wideparen{M}(W)
is co-admissible, it is a closed submodule of
\wideparen{\Delta}(W)
. Hence, it is the maximal closed submodule. The equivalence of categories of Proposition
3.2.12
shows that
\wideparen{\Delta}(W)/\wideparen{M}(W)=\wideparen{L}(W)
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Remark 3.2.15
.

Notice, however, that unlike in the algebraic or Banach situations, it is not clear that
\wideparen{L}(W)
is a simple module in
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathscr{R})
. Indeed, as
\mathscr{R}
is not noetherian, it could happen that
\wideparen{L}(W)
admits non-closed submodules. Nevertheless, as the closure of a submodule is a submodule, the above proposition shows that any non-zero
\mathscr{R}
-submodule of
\wideparen{L}(W)
is dense in
\wideparen{L}(W)
.

Our next goal is giving different characterizations of the elements of
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
, similar to what we did in Proposition
3.1.36
. In particular, we aim to characterize objects in
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
in terms of their decompositions into weight spaces, We start with the following definition:

Definition 3.2.16
.

Let
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}^{\operatorname{ws}}\subset\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
be the full subcategory of modules which admit a weight space decomposition of finite type.

Theorem 3.2.17
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
.

(ii)

M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}^{\operatorname{ws}}
, and there is a finite family of weights
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\Lambda(M)
satisfying that:

\Lambda(M)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}+\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}.

(iii)

M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}^{\operatorname{ws}}
, and the action of
B
on
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is locally nilpotent.

Furthermore, if the equivalent conditions above hold, then
M\in\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

The fact that
(i)
implies
(ii)
was shown in Proposition
3.2.12
. The fact that
(ii)
implies
(iii)
is clear. Hence, we only need to show that
(iii)
implies
(i)
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
and assume
M
satisfies
(iii)
. In this situation
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is an
R
-module which admits a weight space decomposition of finite type (in the algebraic sense) and satisfies that the action of
B
on
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is locally nilpotent. Thus, by Proposition
2.1.10
we have
M^{\operatorname{ws}}\in\mathcal{O}
. Thus, we obtain a map
\varphi:\wideparen{M}\rightarrow M
, satisfying that
\wideparen{M}\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
and the map has dense image. As both
\wideparen{M}
and
M
are co-admissible
\mathscr{R}
-modules, the image of
\varphi
is closed. Hence,
\varphi
is a surjection. But then it follows by Lemma
3.2.13
that
M\in\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Thus, we could have chosen any of the conditions above to define
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
. We remark that
(ii)
in the previous proposition is analogous to
[
31
, Definition 3.6.2.]
, where the case of the category
\mathcal{O}
for the Arens-Michael envelope of the universal algebra of a reductive Lie algebra is treated. This is also similar to
[
10
]
, which treats the theory for quantum Lie algebras.

Ideally, we would like to describe
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
as the category of co-admissible
\mathscr{R}
-modules which admit a weight space decomposition and are co-admissible as
\mathscr{A}
-modules. Unfortunately, we have not been able to prove such a statement (and the author doubts its veracity). We can, however, show a slightly weaker version of this statement:

Proposition 3.2.18
.

Assume
M\in\mathcal{C}(\mathscr{R})
admits a weight space decomposition, and a Fréchet-Stein presentation:

M=\varprojlim_{n}M(n),

satisfying that each
M(n)
is also a finite
\mathscr{A}(n)
-module. Then
M
is an object in
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
M(n)\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
for each
n\geq 0
. By assumption,
M(n)
is a finite
\mathscr{R}(n)
-module and a finite
\mathscr{A}(n)
-module. As
M
admits a weight space decomposition, it follows that
M^{\operatorname{ws}}
is dense in
M
. Thus, its image in
M(n)
is also dense. As
M(n)
is a finite
\mathscr{A}(n)
-module,
we can use the arguments in the proof of statement
(i)
in Proposition
3.1.20
to show that there is a finite family of generalized eigenvectors in
M(n)
which generate
M(n)
as a
\mathscr{A}(n)
-module. In this situation, the fact that
\mathscr{A}(n)
admits a weight space decomposition for
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
allows us to use the proof of statement
(ii)
in Proposition
3.1.20
to show that
M(n)
admits a weight space decomposition, as wanted.
∎

4.
p
-adic Cherednik algebras and the GAGA functor

Now that we have introduced our main technical tools, our aim is to slowly drift into the world of representation theory. In particular, we aim at studying different representation-theoretical aspects of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. For the benefit of the reader, we devote Section
4.1
to give a quick introduction to the theory of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras. In order to keep the background material contained, we will not give any proof, and rely on our companion paper
[
26
]
as our source for the basics on the theory of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras on rigid spaces. We point out, however, that a more detailed account on
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebras (and in particular on their triangular decomposition) will be given in Section
5.1
.

Additionally, let us mention a few useful references of the theory of Cherednik algebras over the complex numbers. A complete treatment on the theory of rational Cherednik algebras may be found in
[
12
]
, and
[
15
]
. The non-linear case over (complex) algebraic varieties is contained in
[
13
]
, and a rather complete set of lecture notes on the subject are available in
[
11
]
.

Let us give an overview of the contents of this chapter:

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space, which we regard as an affine
K
-variety,
G\subset\operatorname{Gl}_{n}(K)
be a finite group, and
\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}}
be the rigid-analytification of
\mathfrak{h}
. Choose a reflection function
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
, and consider the
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebra:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G):=\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}}/G,\mathcal{H}_{c,\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G}).

The first half of the chapter will be devoted to showing that
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra and a nuclear Fréchet space. We will actually work in a slightly more general setting, by showing that the
p
-adic Cherednik algebra
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra whenever
X
is a smooth Stein space with an action of a finite group
G
. In order to keep the preliminaries to a minimum, we will start the chapter with a brief discussion of Stein and quasi-Stein spaces, and their behavior with respect to actions of finite groups.

The second half of the chapter studies the behavior of Cherednik algebras under the rigid-analytic GAGA functor. In particular, we show that there is a faithfully flat map with dense image:

H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G),

which realizes
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
as the Arens-Michael envelope of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
. As before, we will work in a more general setting. In particular, if
X
is a smooth affine
K
-variety, we show that the canonical map from its rigid-analytification
X^{\operatorname{an}}\rightarrow X
induces a map
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
, which has the properties described above.

As a consequence of the contents of this chapter, any
K
-linear representation of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
on a Banach space
V
extends to a continuous representation of
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
on
V
. This has the advantage that any such representation factors through one of the algebras in a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
, and these algebras can be chosen to be pretty well-behaved (see Section
5.2
for details).

4.1.
p
-adic Cherednik algebras

We start this chapter by giving a quick summary of the contents of
[
26
]
. In particular, we will define the family of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras associated to an action of a finite group
G
on a smooth rigid variety
X
, and recall some of its basic properties. For the rest of the section, we fix a finite group
G
, and let
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
be a smooth affinoid space with a
G
-action.

Let us start by making the following definition:

Definition 4.1.1
.

We define the following objects:

(i)

A reflection hypersurface in
X
is a pair
(Y,g)
given by a
hypersurface
Y\subset X
and an element
g\in G
such that
Y
is a connected component of the fixed point locus
X^{g}
. We let
S(X,G)
be the set of all reflection hypersurfaces. Notice that
G
has an action on
S(X,G)
given by:

h(Y,g)=(h(Y),hgh^{-1},\textnormal{ for }(Y,g)\in S(X,G),\textnormal{ and }h\in G.

(ii)

A reflection function is a
G
-equivariant map:

c:S(X,G)\rightarrow K.

We let
\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
be the finite-dimensional
K
-vector space of all reflection hypersurfaces.

(iii)

Let
X^{\operatorname{reg}}
be the locus of points in
X
which have trivial stabilizer with respect to the action of
G
. The space
X^{\operatorname{reg}}
is a
G
-invariant admissible open of
X
.

The family of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras of the action of
G
on
X
is a family of Fréchet algebras parameterized by a unit
t\in K^{*}
, a reflection function
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
, and a
G
-invariant cohomology class
[\omega]\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{2}(X)^{G}
. For the rest of the section, fix
t,c,
and
\omega
as above. Let us remark that the construction of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras is based upon certain objects called the Dunkl-Opdam operators. These operators have a very complicated expression
in general (
cf.

[
26
, Definition 3.1.3]
). Thus, in order to simplify the exposition, we will now make some assumptions on the action of
G
on
X
. Namely, we will assume that
X
admits an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
, and that each reflection hypersurface
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
satisfies that
Y
is the zero locus of a unique rigid function
f_{(Y,g)}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. We point out that, in virtue of
[
26
, Proposition 2.1.23]
,
X
admits a finite cover by
G
-invariant affinoid subdomains satisfying these conditions. In order to keep the introduction short, we will assume that the reader is familiar with the theory of sheaves of (infinite order) twisted differential operators, and of the actions of finite groups on them. All the necessary material on this topic can be found in
[
26
, Section 2.3/2.5]
. In any case, one can also assume
\omega=0
for simplicity.

We define the Cherednik algebra
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
as the subalgebra of
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(X^{\operatorname{reg}})
generated by
G
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
, and the (standard) Dunkl-Opdam operators:

D_{v}=tv+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\frac{v(f_{(Y,g)})}{f_{(Y,g)}}(g-1),

where
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
is a vector field on
X
, and the operators
\lambda_{Y,g}\in K\setminus\{1\}
are the eigenvalues of the action of
g
on the conormal bundle of
Y
(
cf.

[
26
, Proposition 2.1.14]
).

The
p
-adic Cherednik algebra
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
will ultimately arise as the closure of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(X^{\operatorname{reg}})
. However, in order to further simplify the situation, we notice that any dense
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
U\subset X^{\operatorname{reg}}
satisfies:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\subset G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(U).

Furthermore, our assumption that
X
has free tangent sheaf allows us to apply the methods of
[
2
]
to the algebra
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(U)
. In particular,
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(U)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra, and it is possible to obtain an explicit Fréchet-Stein presentation for it (
cf
.
[
26
, Proposition 2.5.8]
). This allows us to obtain a characterization of the closure of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(U)
, which we denote
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
. As it turns out, this algebra is also a Fréchet-Stein algebra, and we will give an explicit Fréchet-Stein presentation below. Ideally, we would like to show that, for big enough
U
, the algebra
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is independent of the choice of
U
. This is one of the main results of
[
26
]
:

Theorem 4.1.2
.

Let
U\subset X^{\operatorname{reg}}
be a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain. The algebra
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra. Furthermore, if
U
is big enough, then
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U}
agrees with the closure of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
in
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{\omega/t}(X^{\operatorname{reg}})
, and therefore is independent of
U
.

Proof.

This is shown in
[
26
, Theorem 4.4.17]
.
∎

Hence, we can drop the
U
from the notation, and we obtain a suitable definition of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. Let us remark that there are explicit conditions for the
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
U\subset X^{\operatorname{reg}}
to be big enough. In particular, it suffices that
U
contains the Shilov boundary of
X
. However, this condition is rather technical, so we will not discuss it here. One can check
[
26
, Section 4.2]
for a complete discussion. Notice that the previous theorem allows us to define
p
-adic Cherednik algebras for any
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
Y\subset X
. Furthermore, these algebras can be arranged in a canonical way into a sheaf
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
on the quotient space
X/G=\operatorname{Sp}(A^{G})
. This sheaf is defined on affinoid subdomains
Y\subset X/G
by the rule:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Y,G):=\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(Y\times_{X/G}X,G).

The analogous construction allows us to define a sheaf of Cherednik algebras
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
on
X/G
. The restriction maps of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
are obtained by continuously extending those of
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
, and these maps are, in turn, uniquely determined by restriction of the canonical maps:

G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(Y^{\operatorname{reg}})\rightarrow G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(Z^{\operatorname{reg}}),

where
Z\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X
are
G
-equivariant affinoid maps. These sheaves satisfy the following properties:

Theorem 4.1.3
.

Choose
t\in K^{*}
,
\omega\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{dR}(X)^{G}
, and
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(X,G)
. The sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
is the unique sheaf on
X/G
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

Let
Y\rightarrow X/G
be an affinoid subdomain. Then we have:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Y,G):=\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(Y\times_{X/G}X,G).

(ii)

Let
Z\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X/G
be affinoid maps. Then the restriction map:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Y,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}(Z,G),

is a continuous morphism of Fréchet-Stein
K
-algebras.

(iii)

There is a canonical morphism of sheaves of
K
-algebras
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
.

Furthermore, the sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
has trivial higher Čech cohomology.

Proof.

This is shown in
[
26
, Theorem 4.5.11]
.
∎

We point out that this theorem can be used to obtain sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras on more general settings. Indeed, if
X
is a smooth rigid space with an action of
G
satisfying some mild technical requirements (see Theorem
4.2.5
) then one can define a family of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
on the quotient
X/G
. Furthermore, this sheaf can even be extended to the small étale site
X/G_{\textnormal{ét}}
. See
[
26
, Section 4.5]
for details.

Finally, let us make some comments on the Fréchet-Stein structure of the
p
-adic Cherednik algebras
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
. This construction is based upon the notion of a Cherednik lattice, which we will now define. We point out that this construction relies on some of the contents of
[
2
]
. However, in order to keep the preliminaries to a minimum, we will not define them here. A completely detailed account of this construction may be found in
[
26
, Section 4.3]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
be the Atiyah algebra on
X
associated to the algebra of twisted differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{\omega/t}(X)
(
cf
.
[
26
, Section 2.3]
). By construction, this is a
(K,\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))
-Lie algebra, and we have a split short exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
-modules:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathcal{T}_{X}(X)\rightarrow 0,

where
\rho:\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X}(X)
is the anchor map of the
(K,\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))
-Lie algebra structure of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
.

By assumption, the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X}(X)
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
-module, and
\omega
is a
G
-invariant closed
2
-form on
X
. Hence, by
[
26
, Lemma 2.4.5]
,
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)

admits a
G
-invariant
(R,A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice which contains
A^{\circ}
. Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
be any such a lattice. In order to fix notation, we will now introduce som objects. First, we define the following
A^{\circ}
-module:

\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}:=\rho(\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X).

Next, let
\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})
be the two-sided ideal in the enveloping algebra
U(\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})
generated by:

1_{A^{\circ}}-1_{\widehat{U}(\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})}.

Then we define the following
\mathcal{R}
-algebra:

\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})=U(\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t})/\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}).

We are now ready to define the Cherednik lattices of the action of
G
on
X
:

Definition 4.1.4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
be a
G
-invariant finite-free
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ})
-Lie lattice. We say
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a Cherednik lattice of
\mathcal{A}_{\omega/t}(X)
if there is a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain
U\subset X
such that
U
contains the Shilov boundary of
X
, and such that
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

B^{\circ}
is
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
-stable, and we let
\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t}=B^{\circ}\otimes_{A^{\circ}}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
.

(ii)

For each
v\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}
there is a Dunkl-Opdam operator
D_{v}
such that
D_{v/t}\in G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})
.

In this situation, we can define the following algebras:

Definition 4.1.5
.

We define the following algebras:

(i)

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}:=G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{B}_{\omega/t})\cap H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

(ii)

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}:=\widehat{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}K=\left(\varprojlim_{n}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{U,\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}/\pi^{n}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}K.

First, notice that, a priory, these algebras depend on
U
, and not only on
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
. However, it is part of the proof of Theorem
4.1.2
that these algebras are in fact independent of
U
. Next, the definition of a Cherednik lattice implies that if
\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is a Cherednik lattice, then
\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}
is also a Cherednik lattice for each
n\geq 0
(
cf.

[
26
, Lemma 4.4.8]
). This allows us to show the following proposition:

Proposition 4.1.6
.

There is an identity of topological
K
-algebras:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)_{\pi^{n}\mathscr{A}_{\omega/t}}.

Furthermore, this identity is a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Proof.

This was shown in
[
26
, Theorem 4.4.17]
.
∎

As we will see later, this presentation will be a basic tool in all our developments regarding
p
-adic Cherednik algebras, and will allow us to perform explicit calculations.

4.2.
Finite group actions on Stein spaces

The next goal is giving an introduction to the theory of
p
-adic Stein spaces. In particular, we will recall some geometric properties, study the category of coherent modules on a Stein space, and provide a geometric analysis of the action of a finite group on a Stein space. For the rest of the section, we fix a finite group
G
.

Let us start by recalling the definitions of Stein and quasi-Stein spaces:

Definition 4.2.1
.

We define the following objects:

(i)

Let
V\subset U
be an open immersion of affinoid spaces. We say
V
is relatively compact in
U
, and write
V\Subset U
, if there is a set of affinoid generators
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{U}(U)
such that:

V\subset\{x\in U\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }|f_{i}(x)|<1,\textnormal{ for }1\leq i\leq n\}.

(ii)

A rigid space
X
is called a quasi-Stein space if it admits an admissible affinoid cover
(U_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
U_{i}\subset U_{i+1}
is a Weierstrass subdomain.

(iii)

We say
X
is a Stein space if, in addition, we have
U_{i}\Subset U_{i+1}
for all
i\geq 0
. We call
(U_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
a Stein covering of
X
, and write
X=\varinjlim_{i}U_{i}
.

Notice that any Stein space is partially proper. In particular, it is separated. Let
X
be a Stein space. It follows from the definition that any Stein covering
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
satisfies that for each
n\geq 1
there are affinoid generators
f_{1},\cdots,f_{r_{n}}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})
, and some
\epsilon\in\sqrt{K^{*}}
, such that
0<\epsilon<1
and such that the following holds:

(13)

X_{n-1}\subseteq X_{n}\left(\epsilon^{-1}f_{1},\cdots,\epsilon^{-1}f_{r_{n}}\right).

If the identity holds, we recover the definition of Stein space given by R. Kiehl in
[
22
, Definition 2.3]
.

In most of the proofs we will encounter, it will in fact suffice to assume that the identity above does hold. This is because our interest in Stein spaces stems from the fact that the global sections of the sheaves we will be interested in are nuclear Fréchet spaces. Let us recall the definition:

Definition 4.2.2

(
[
6
, Lemma 5.2]
[
23
, Definition 1.1]
)
.

A bounded map of Banach spaces
f:V\rightarrow W
is called strictly completely continuous morphism (
scc
morphism) if there is a sequence of morphisms:

f_{n}:V\rightarrow W,

such that
f=\varinjlim_{n\geq 0}f_{n}
in operator norm and such that
f_{n}(V)
is finite-dimensional for all
n\geq 0
.

Definition 4.2.3

(
[
6
, Definition 5.3]
)
.

Let
V
be a Fréchet space. We say
V
is a nuclear Fréchet space if there is a Fréchet space
\varprojlim_{n}V_{n}
satisfying the following:

(i)

V=\varprojlim_{n}V_{n}
as Fréchet spaces.

(ii)

The image of
V
in
V_{n}
is dense for each
n\geq 0
.

(iii)

For each
n\geq 1
there is a Banach
K
-vector space
F_{n}
and a strict surjection:

F_{n}\rightarrow V_{n},

such that the composition
F_{n}\rightarrow V_{n}\rightarrow V_{n-1}
is
\emph{scc}
.

We remark that, in virtue of
[
6
, Lemma 5.4]
, the notion of nuclear Fréchet space defined above agrees with the one defined in
[
33
, Chapter IV]
. Notice that given an
scc
map
f:V\rightarrow W
, then for any Banach space
U
and any continuous map
g:W\rightarrow U
the composition
g\circ f:V\rightarrow U
is still
scc
. For this reason, in most of the proofs below, it will suffice to assume that identity holds in (
13
).

Lemma 4.2.4
.

Let
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
be a Stein space. The following hold:

(i)

\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O})
is a nuclear Fréchet
K
-space.

(ii)

Any closed analytic subspace
Y\rightarrow X
is a Stein space.

(iii)

If
Y
is contained in an affinoid space then
\operatorname{dim}(Y)=0
.

Proof.

Statement
(i)
was shown in
[
28
, Lemma 3.1.3]
. For statements
(ii)
and
(iii)
, let
Y\rightarrow X
be a closed immersion, and let
\mathcal{I}_{Y}
be the associated sheaf of ideals. As
\mathcal{I}_{Y}
is a coherent sheaf on
X
, it follows that

\mathcal{I}_{Y}(X)
is a co-admissible
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
-module. Thus, we have:

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(Y)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)/\mathcal{I}_{Y}(X)=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})/\mathcal{I}_{Y}(X_{n}).

Letting
Y_{n}=\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})/\mathcal{I}_{Y}(X_{n}))
, it follows that
Y=\varinjlim_{n}Y_{n}
is a Stein covering of
Y
. Assume that
Y
is contained in an affinoid subdomain. Then
Y
itself is an affinoid space. Hence,
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(Y)

is a Banach
K
-algebra, and a nuclear Fréchet space. Notice that nuclear Fréchet spaces are reflexive by
[
33
, Corollary 19.3]
. However, by
[
33
, Proposition 11.1]
a Banach
K
-vector space is reflexive if and only if it is finite-dimensional. Hence,
\operatorname{dim}(Y)=0
.
∎

We can now start analyzing the properties of an action of a finite group
G
on a Stein space
X
. Let us start by introducing a rather important technical tool:

Theorem 4.2.5

(
[
19
]
)
.

Let
X
be a rigid
G
-variety satisfying the following property:

\textnormal{(G-Aff)}:=\textnormal{There is an admissible cover of }X\textnormal{ by }G\textnormal{-stable affinoid spaces.}

Let
\operatorname{Rig}_{K}
be the category of rigid spaces over
K
. The following hold:

(i)

The action of
G
on
X
admits a categorical quotient
X/G
in
\operatorname{Rig}_{K}
. In particular, the pullback along the projection
\pi:X\rightarrow X/G

induces an isomorphism of functors :

\pi^{*}:\operatorname{Hom}(X/G,-)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(X,-)^{G}.

(ii)

The morphism
X\rightarrow X/G
is finite.
X
is affinoid if and only if
X/G
is affinoid.

(iii)

If
X=\operatorname{Sp}(A)
, then
X/G=\operatorname{Sp}(A^{G})
.

(iv)

The preimage along the projection
\pi:X\rightarrow X/G
induces a bijection:

\pi^{-1}:\{\textnormal{admissible open subspaces of }X/G\}\rightarrow\{G-\textnormal{stable admissible open subspaces of }X\},

satisfying that for every admissible open
U\subset X/G
we have
U=\pi^{-1}(U)/G
.

We point out that the result shown in
[
19
]
is much more general than the one stated here, but this level of generality suffices for our purposes. The first step is showing that the action of any finite group
G
on
X
satisfies
(\textnormal{G-Aff})
, and therefore that the quotient space
X/G
is a rigid space:

Lemma 4.2.6
.

Let
X
be a quasi-Stein
G
-variety. Then
X
satisfies
(\operatorname{G-Aff})
.

Proof.

Let
X=\varinjlim X_{n}
be a quasi-Stein covering, and choose a point
x\in X
. As
G
is finite there is some
X_{n}
such that the orbit of
x
under
G
is contained in
X_{n}
. Thus,
Y_{n}=\cap_{g\in G}X_{n}
is non-empty, and it follows that
X=\varinjlim_{n}Y_{n}
is an admissible cover of
X
.
∎

In particular, if
X
is a smooth Stein
G
-variety satisfying
\operatorname{(G-Aff)}
, the fixed point locus
X^{G}
is a Stein space. Furthermore, by
[
26
, Corollary 2.1.9]
it follows that
X^{G}
is also smooth. Hence, letting
S(X,G)
be the set of reflection hypersurfaces of the action of
G
on
X
, it follows that every reflection hypersurface
(Y,g)
satisfies that
Y
is a smooth Stein space. Let us now discuss an interesting consequence of this fact: By
[
17
, Corollary 3.2]
, the de Rham cohomology spaces of a Stein space have a canonical structure as a nuclear Fréchet space.
Thus, the vector spaces
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{n}(Y)
are nuclear Fréchet spaces for all
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
and all
n\geq 0
. As there are at most a countable infinity of reflection hypersurfaces, this implies that the products:

\prod_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{n}(Y),

is also a nuclear Fréchet space for all
n\geq 0
. The canonical action of
G
on
S(X,G)
by conjugation induces a continuous
K
-linear action on these products. As the fixed point locus of the action of
G
is a closed subspace, this implies that the following locally convex space:

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{2}(X)^{G}\oplus\left(\prod_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{0}(Y)\right)^{G},

is naturally a nuclear Fréchet space. Notice that by
[
26
, Theorem 4.5.11]
, this is precisely the
K
-vector space which parametrizes the isomorphism classes of sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras associated to the action of
G
on
X
. When
G=1
, this type of phenomena was used in
[
28
]
to study the infinitesimal deformations of
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X)
, and we hope that a similar study for
G\ltimes\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X)
can be performed in the future.

Back to our initial discussion, we now show that the quotient
X/G
is always a Stein space:

Proposition 4.2.7
.

Let
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
be a (quasi-)Stein space with an action of a finite group
G
. The following hold:

(i)

There is a (quasi-)Stein covering
X=\varinjlim_{n}\tilde{X}_{n}
such that each of the
\tilde{X}_{n}
is
G
-invariant.

(ii)

X/G
is a (quasi-)Stein space.

Proof.

Let
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
be a Stein covering of
X
, and define
\tilde{X}_{n}=\cap_{g\in G}g(X_{n})
for each
n\geq 0
.

By Lemma
4.2.6
, for each point
x\in X
there is a
G
-invariant affinoid open subspace
U\subset X
such that
x\in U
. As
U
is affinoid, it is quasi-compact. Hence, it is contained in some
X_{n}
for sufficiently high
n
. Thus, we may assume that the
\tilde{X}_{n}
are non-empty for all
n\geq 0
. Furthermore,
X=\cup_{n\geq 0}\tilde{X}_{n}
is an admissible affinoid cover. We just need to show that
X=\varinjlim_{n}\tilde{X}_{n}
is a Stein covering of
X
. For each
g\in G
we have
g(X_{n})\Subset g(X_{n+1})
. Hence, it follows by
[
8
, Lemma 6.3.7]
that
\tilde{X}_{n}\Subset\tilde{X}_{n+1}
. Similarly, one shows that
\tilde{X}_{n}
is a Weierstrass subdomain of
\tilde{X}_{n+1}
, thus showing that
X=\varinjlim_{n}\tilde{X}_{n}
is a Stein covering. By Theorem
4.2.5
, we have an admissible affinoid covering
X/G=\varinjlim_{n}\tilde{X}_{n}/G
. Fix some
n\geq 0
. By
[
3
, Proposition 2.5.2]
, and
[
3
, Corollary 2.5.5]
there are affinoid generators
f_{1},\cdots f_{r_{n}}\in\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}_{n}/G}(\tilde{X}_{n}/G)
satisfying that the composition
\tilde{X}_{n-1}\rightarrow\tilde{X}_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{X}_{n}/G
, has image contained in the following admissible open subspace:

\{x\in\tilde{X}_{n}/G\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }|f_{i}(x)|<1,\textnormal{ for }1\leq i\leq n_{r}\}.

As
\tilde{X}_{n-1}\rightarrow\tilde{X}_{n-1}/G
is surjective,
it follows that
\tilde{X}_{n-1}/G\Subset\tilde{X}_{n}/G
, as we wanted to show.
∎

Thus, we can assume that every Stein
G
-variety
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
satisfies that each
X_{n}
is
G
-invariant. As a consequence of these results, it follows that every Stein
G
-variety admits a finite covering by Stein
G
-varieties satisfying good properties. Namely, we have the following corollary:

Lemma 4.2.8
.

Let
X
be a Stein space and
\mathscr{V}
be a vector bundle on
X
. Then
\mathscr{V}(X)
is a locally finite-free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
-module.

Proof.

This is
[
28
, Corollary 3.1.5]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2.9
.

Let
X
be a Stein space and choose a holomorphic function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. In this situation, the Zariski open subspace:

X(f^{-1})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}X\left(\frac{\pi^{n}}{f}\right)=\{x\in X\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }|f(x)|>0\},

is a Stein space.

Proof.

This is
[
28
, Proposition 3.1.7]

∎

Corollary 4.2.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth Stein
G
-variety . There is an admissible cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
X
such that every
U_{i}
satisfies the following:

(i)

U_{i}
is a
G
-invariant smooth Stein open subspace of
X
.

(ii)

\Omega^{1}_{X/K}(U)
is free over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
and has a basis given by differentials of functions.

(iii)

Any reflection hypersurface on
U
is given by the vanishing locus of a rigid function on
U
.

Furthermore, for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
, the intersection
U_{ij}=U_{i}\cap U_{j}
satisfies the three conditions above.

Proof.

In virtue of Proposition
4.2.7
and Lemma
4.2.9
, it suffices to show that there are holomorphic functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)^{G}
such that each of the associated Zariski open subspaces
X(f_{i}^{-1})
satisfies conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)
. For any
g\in G
, we let
\mathcal{I}_{g}
be the coherent ideal associated to the union of all reflection hypersurfaces fixed by
g
. By definition, all reflection hypersurfaces fixed by
g
are disjoint. Furthermore, by
[
26
, Corollary 2.1.9]
, they are smooth. Hence,
\mathcal{I}_{g}
is a line bundle on
X
for every
g\in G
. As
X
is smooth, the sheaf of Kähler differentials
\Omega_{X/K}
is also a vector bundle. Thus, it follows by Lemma
4.2.8
that
\Omega_{X/K}(X)
and
\mathcal{I}_{g}(X)
are locally finite-free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
-modules for any
g\in G
. Hence, we can apply
[
26
, Lemma 2.1.22]
to obtain a
G
-invariant Zarisky cover of
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))
satisfying that the associated admissible cover of
X
satisfies the desired properties. As
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))
is a compact topological space, the cover can be taken to be finite.
∎

As a final result of the section, we remark that statement
(ii)
in Proposition
4.2.7
admits a converse. Indeed, if
X
is a rigid
G
-variety satisfying
\operatorname{(G-Aff)}
, and such that
X/G
is a Stein space, then it can be shown that
X
is also a Stein space. This result will not be used in the sequel. However, for completeness, we have decided to include a proof in Appendix
A.1
.

4.3.
p
-adic Cherednik algebras on Smooth Stein spaces

The goal now is describing the sections of a sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras on a smooth Stein
G
-variety
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
, and studying the category of co-admissible modules attached to any
p
-adic Cherednik algebra on
X
. Using Corollary
4.2.10
, it suffices to study the case where there is an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

Let
f:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
,
g:W_{1}\rightarrow W_{2}
be
scc
morphisms of Banach
K
-vector spaces. Then the map
f\widehat{\otimes}_{K}g:V_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W_{2}
is also
scc
.

Proof.

Let
V_{1}^{\circ},W_{1}^{\circ}
be the unit balls of
V_{1}
, and
W_{1}
respectively. As
f
is
scc
there is a sequence of operators
f_{n}:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
satisfying that
f_{n}(V_{1}^{\circ})
is a finite
\mathcal{R}
-module, and
\varinjlim_{n\geq 0}f_{n}=f
in operator norm. Similarly, there is a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfying the analogous properties for
g
. By
[
33
, Proposition 17.4]
, we know that the canonical norm on
V_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W_{1}
satisfies that its unit ball is
V_{1}^{\circ}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{R}}W_{1}^{\circ}
. Furthermore, we have

\varinjlim_{n\geq 0}f_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}g_{n}=f\widehat{\otimes}_{K}g
in operator norm. By construction, for each
n\geq 0
we have that
f_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}g_{n}(V_{1}^{\circ}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}W_{1}^{\circ})
is a finite
\mathcal{R}
-module. Hence, it is closed in
V_{2}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W_{2}
. Thus,
f_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}g_{n}(V_{1}^{\circ}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{R}}W_{1}^{\circ})
is also contained in a finite
\mathcal{R}
-module and is therefore also finite.
∎

Proposition 4.3.2
.

Let
X=\varinjlim X_{n}
be a smooth Stein
G
-variety with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. Choose a sheaf of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
, and consider the
p
-adic Cherednik algebra:

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G):=\Gamma(X/G,\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega,X,G}).

Then
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra and a nuclear Fréchet
K
-space.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
be the Atiyah algebra on
X
associated to
\omega
. We will assume for simplicity that
t=1
. Let
X_{n}=\operatorname{Sp}(A_{n})
and
A:=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. As we have an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
, there are rigid functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{r}\in A
such that:

\Omega^{1}_{X/K}(X)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}df_{i}A.

Let
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
be the dual basis, so that for each
n\geq 0
we have
\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X_{n})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}A_{n}
.

For each
n\geq 0
, we define the following
(\mathcal{R},A^{\circ}_{n})
-lattice:

\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}A^{\circ}_{n}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(X_{n}).

By construction, we have

\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}=A^{\circ}_{n}\otimes_{A^{\circ}_{n+1}}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n+1}
. Furthermore, by
[
26
, Lemma 4.4.8]
, we can choose a strictly increasing sequence of non-negative integers
\{m(n)\}_{n\geq 0}

such that for all
n\geq 0
we have that
\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}
is a Cherednik lattice of
X_{n}
. In particular, we have Fréchet-Stein presentations:

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)=\varprojlim_{m\geq m(n)}\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}.

As the sequence
\{m(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
is strictly increasing, we have:

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}.

For the second part of the proof, we may assume for simplicity that the Stein covering
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
satisfies that the identity holds in (
13
). The general case is analogous. By definition of a Stein covering, the maps
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n+1}
are inclusions of Weierstrass subdomains. Hence, the associated maps
A_{n+1}\rightarrow A_{n}
have dense image. Furthermore, as
X
is Stein, for all
n\geq 0
there is some
\epsilon_{n}\in\sqrt{|K^{*}|}
, with
0<\epsilon_{n}<1
and such that we have the following pushout diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K\langle t_{1},\cdots,t_{i_{n}}\rangle}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K\langle\epsilon_{n}^{-1}t_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{n}^{-1}t_{i_{n}}\rangle}}

where the horizontal maps are epimorphisms and the vertical maps have dense image. Furthermore, notice that the rightmost vertical map is
scc
by the proof of
[
28
, Lemma 3.1.3]
.

As a consequence of
[
26
, Corollary 4.4.4]
, for each
n\geq 0
, and
m\geq m(n)
we have the following identity of Banach
A_{n}
-modules:

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}A_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}K\langle\pi^{m}t_{1},\cdots,\pi^{m}t_{r}\rangle.

Thus, for each
n\geq 0
we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n+1},G)_{\pi^{m(n+1)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n+1}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{g\in G}K\langle t_{1},\cdots,t_{i_{n}}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{K}}K\langle\pi^{m(n+1)}t_{1},\cdots,\pi^{m(n+1)}t_{r}\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{g\in G}K\langle\epsilon_{n}^{-1}t_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{n}^{-1}t_{i_{n}}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{K}K\langle\pi^{m(n)}t_{1},\cdots,\pi^{m(n)}t_{r}\rangle}

where the horizontal maps are surjective and the rightmost vertical map is
scc
with dense image by Lemma
4.3.1
. Hence,
\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)
is a nuclear Fréchet
K
-space.
The fact that:

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}},

is a Fréchet-Stein presentation follows from
[
26
, Theorem 4.4.17]
, and
[
26
, Corollary 4.6.5]
.
∎

Corollary 4.3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X,G))
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is a nuclear Fréchet
K
-space.

Proof.

The proof follows from Proposition
4.3.2
using the arguments in
[
6
, Proposition 5.5]
.
∎

Corollary 4.3.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth Stein space with an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. Then
\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra. Furthermore, every
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{C}(\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(X))
is a nuclear Fréchet space.

The proof of Proposition
4.3.2
yields a specific procedure to obtain a Fréchet-Stein presentation of

. This will be of the utmost importance in Section

.

4.4.
The GAGA Functor

Now that we have a solid theory of sheaves of
p
-adic Cherednik algebras on smooth
p
-adic Stein spaces, we will put the focus on studying the behavior of these sheaves under the rigid-analytic GAGA functor. Given a locally of finite type
K
-scheme
X
, we denote its rigid analytification by
X^{\operatorname{an}}
. An overview of the basic properties of this functor may be found in
[
8
, 5.4]
. We remark that if
X
is affine, then
X^{\operatorname{an}}
is always a Stein space. Hence, we can apply the machinery developed in the previous section to this setting.

As before, we fix a finite group
G
. We will start by studying the behavior of quotients of algebraic varieties with respect to the GAGA functor:

Remark 4.4.1
.

If
X
is a locally of finite type
K
-scheme we define:

\textnormal{(G-Aff)}:=\textnormal{There is a cover of }X\textnormal{ by }G\textnormal{-stable affine spaces.}

Proposition 4.4.2
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety with a right action of
G
. If
X
satisfies
(G-Aff)
, then the induced action of
G
on
X^{\operatorname{an}}
satisfies
(G-Aff)
.

Proof.

As the GAGA functor sends a cover of
X
in the Zariski topology to an admissible cover of
X
, our assumption that
X
has a
G
-stable affine cover allows us to reduce to the case where

X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is affine. As
X
is affine,
X^{\operatorname{an}}
is a Stein space. Hence, the result follows by Lemma
4.2.6
.
∎

Thus, in the situation of the previous proposition we have a canonical map

X^{\operatorname{an}}/G\rightarrow(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}.

As expected, this map is always an isomorphism of rigid spaces:

Lemma 4.4.3
.

Let
A,B
be
K
-algebras with a left action of
G
. Assume
B
is a topological
K
-algebra and that
G
acts on
B
by continuous homomorphisms. Let
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
be a
G
-equivariant morphism with dense image. Then the induced morphism
\varphi^{G}:A^{G}\rightarrow B^{G}
has dense image.

Proof.

Indeed, as
\varphi
has dense image, for every
f\in B
we have a sequence
\{\varphi(f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements in the image of
A
such that
f=\varinjlim_{n}\varphi(f_{n})
. If
f
is fixed by
G
we have:

f=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g(f)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g(\varinjlim\varphi(f_{n}))=\varinjlim_{n}\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\varphi(g(f_{n}))=\varinjlim_{n}\varphi(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g(f_{n})),

and the latter terms are all contained in the image of
A^{G}
.
∎

Definition 4.4.4
.

Let
X
and
Z
be affinoid spaces. A map
Z\rightarrow X
is a Runge immersion if there is a Weierstrass subdomain
U\rightarrow X
such that
Z\rightarrow U\rightarrow X
, and
Z\rightarrow U
is a closed immersion.

Proposition 4.4.5
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety with a right action of
G
such that
X
satisfies
(G-Aff)
. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of rigid spaces:

\Phi:X^{\operatorname{an}}/G\rightarrow(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}.

Proof.

The existence of
\Phi
is clear by functoriality of rigid analytification and the fact that the canonical morphism
X^{\operatorname{an}}\rightarrow(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}
is
G
-equivariant. Furthermore
\Phi
is clearly bijective. As in
4.4.2
, we may assume that
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
and choose increasing affinoid covers
\{X^{\operatorname{an}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

and
\{(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X^{\operatorname{an}}
and
(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}
respectively. Furthermore, we may assume that

\{X^{\operatorname{an}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
G
-equivariant, so that the
Y_{n}=X^{\operatorname{an}}_{n}/G
for
n\geq 0
form an increasing affinoid cover of
X^{\operatorname{an}}/G
. Choose some
n\geq 0
. As
Y_{n}
is affinoid, there is some
m\geq 0
such that
\Phi(Y_{n})\subset(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}_{m}
. It is enough to show that
the restriction
\Phi:Y_{n}\rightarrow(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}_{m}
is an open immersion.

As the GAGA functor does not change the dimension, and the map
X\rightarrow X/G
is finite, it follows that
\operatorname{dim}(Y_{n})=\operatorname{dim}((X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}_{m})
. Thus, it suffices to show that
Y_{n}\rightarrow(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}_{m}
is a Runge immersion. Let
X^{\operatorname{an}}_{n}=\operatorname{Sp}(A_{n})
and
(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}_{m}=\operatorname{Sp}(B_{m})
. By
[
8
, 4.2.7]
, it suffices to show that the map
B_{m}\rightarrow A^{G}_{n}
has dense image. By construction of the GAGA functor, the canonical maps
A^{G}\rightarrow B_{m}
and

A\rightarrow A_{n}
have dense image. Furthermore by Lemma
4.4.3
the map
A^{G}\rightarrow A_{n}^{G}
has dense image. Thus,

B_{m}\rightarrow A^{G}_{n}
has dense image, as we wanted.
∎

As a consequence of this proposition, we may identify
X^{\operatorname{an}}/G
with
(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}
, and the GAGA functor induces a morphism of ringed sites:

(X^{\operatorname{an}}/G,\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/G})\rightarrow(X/G,\mathcal{O}_{X/G}).

By
[
13
, Section 2.13]
, sheaves of Cherednik algebras on
X
are quasi-coherent modules on
X/G
. Hence, for each sheaf of Cherednik algebras
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
on
X
, extension of scalars along the previous morphism of ringed sites induces a sheaf on
X^{\operatorname{an}}/G
, which can be shown to be a Cherednik algebra on
X^{\operatorname{an}}
(see below for details). We will dedicate the rest of this section to understanding this process.

Proposition 4.4.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic
G
-variety satisfying
(\operatorname{G-Aff})
. The morphism of ringed sites:
(X^{\operatorname{an}},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}})\rightarrow(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
, satisfies the following properties:

(i)

(X/G)^{\operatorname{an}}=X^{\operatorname{an}}/G
.

(ii)

There is a canonical
K
-linear isomorphism:
\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}=\operatorname{H}^{1}(X^{\operatorname{an}},\Omega_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}
.

(iii)

There is a
G
-equivariant canonical bijection:
S(X,G)=S(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
.

Proof.

The first property is Theorem
4.4.2
. For the second one, notice that by
[
26
, Theorem 2.4.11]
, and the corresponding result for algebraic varieties, the
K
-vector spaces:

\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G},\textnormal{ and }\operatorname{H}^{1}(X^{\operatorname{an}},\Omega_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/K}^{\geq 1})^{G},

parameterize
G
-equivariant isomorphism classes of Atiyah algebras on
X
and
X^{\operatorname{an}}
respectively. Thus, it suffices to show that these pointed sets are canonically isomorphic. As this can be done on a cover, and
X
satisfies
(\operatorname{G-Aff})
, we may assume
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is affine. However, in this case we have:

\operatorname{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}=\operatorname{H}_{dr}^{2}(X)^{G},\textnormal{ and }\operatorname{H}^{1}(X^{\operatorname{an}},\Omega_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/K}^{\geq 1})^{G}=\operatorname{H}_{dr}^{2}(X^{\operatorname{an}})^{G}.

Thus, the result follows by
[
17
, Example 1.8.(b)]
, and the explicit construction of the action of
G
on
\Omega_{X/K}^{\bullet}
and
\Omega_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/K}^{\bullet}
given in
[
26
, Section 2.3]
. Statement
(iii)
follows from the fact that the GAGA functor preserves dimensions and connected components.
∎

Thus, by
[
13
, Section 2.13]
and
[
26
, Theorem 4.5.11]
, the isomorphism classes of sheaves of Cherednik algebras on
X/G
and
X^{\operatorname{an}}/G
are parameterized by the same
K
-vector spaces.

For the rest of this section, we fix a smooth affine
K
-variety
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
with an action of
G
, and assume there is an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. We choose a sheaf of Cherednik algebras
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
on
X
, and let
H_{t,c,\omega,X^{\operatorname{an}},G}
be the corresponding sheaf of Cherednik algebras on
X^{\operatorname{an}}
.

Lemma 4.4.7
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be an affine
K
-variety and let
X^{\operatorname{an}}=\varinjlim X_{n}
be a Stein covering. For any
n\geq 0
the canonical map
A\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(X_{n})
has dense image.

Proof.

By
[
32
, Theorem A]
, the map
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(X_{n})
has dense image for all
n\geq 0
. Hence, it suffices to show that
A\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})
has dense image. As
X
is an affine
K
-variety, we can form the following commutative diagram of locally ringed sites:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K})^{\operatorname{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\operatorname{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

As the vertical maps are closed immersions, their associated maps of sheaves are surjective. Hence, it suffices to show the result for
X=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
. However, this is clear as for every non-negative integer
r\geq 0
the map
K[t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}]\rightarrow K\langle\pi^{r}t_{1},\dots,\pi^{r}t_{n}\rangle
has dense image.
∎

Lemma 4.4.8
.

For every
G
-invariant open affinoid subspace
U=\operatorname{Sp}(B)\subset X^{\operatorname{an}}
there is a canonical map of filtered
K
-algebras:

i_{U}:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow H_{t,c,\omega}(U,G),

which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For every
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
and every choice of Dunkl-Opdam operator
D_{v}
,
i_{U}(D_{v})
is a Dunkl-Opdam operator for
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/K}(U)
.

(ii)

i_{U}
induces an identification of
B
-modules:
H_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)=B\otimes_{A}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G).

(iii)

i_{U}:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow H_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)
is two-sided flat.

(iv)

The map
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow H_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)
is two-sided flat.

Furthermore, if the map
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(U)
has dense image, then the maps on
(iii)
and
(iv)
also have dense image.

Proof.

Let
\overline{X},\overline{U}
denote the complement of the reflection hypersurfaces on
X
and
U
respectively, and let
V\subset\overline{U}
be a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain which contains the Shilov boundary of
U
. As we have
V\subset(\overline{X})^{\operatorname{an}}
, we have a morphism of
K
-algebras:
j_{V}:G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\overline{X})\rightarrow G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(V)
.
Consider the following map of filtered
K
-algebras:

i_{U}:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\overline{X})\rightarrow G\ltimes\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(V),

where the first map is given by inclusion. We need to show that
\operatorname{Im}(i_{U})\subset H_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)
.

It suffices to show that
i_{U}
sends Dunkl-Opdam operators to Dunkl-Opdam operators. Using
[
26
, Proposition 2.1.23]
, we may assume that every reflection hypersurface
(Y,g)\in S(X,G)
satisfies that there is some
f_{(Y,g)}\in A
such that
Y=\mathbb{V}(f_{(Y,g)})
. In this situation, for any
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X)
we have a standard Dunkl-Opdam operator:

D^{s}_{v}:=tv+\sum_{(Y,g)\in S(X,G)}\frac{2c(Y,g)}{1-\lambda_{Y,g}}\frac{v(f_{(Y,g)})}{f_{(Y,g)}}(g-1).

Let
D_{v}
be any Dunkl-Opdam operator for
v
. Then by
[
26
, Lemma 3.1.6]
, we have
D_{v}-D^{s}_{v}\in G\ltimes A
. Hence, it is enough to show that
i_{U}(D^{s}_{v})
is a Dunkl-Opdam operator of
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/K}(U)
. But it is clear by construction of
j_{V}
that
i_{U}(D^{s}_{v})
is the standard Dunkl-Opdam operator of
v\in\mathcal{T}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}/K}(U)
.

Statement
(ii)
is a direct consequence of
(i)
and the PBW theorems
[
13
, Theorem 2.17]
and
[
26
, Theorem 3.5.1]
. Hence, we have an isomorphism of
B
-modules:

H_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)=B\otimes_{A}H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G).

In particular, statement
(iii)
holds if
A\rightarrow B
is flat. However, this map is flat by general properties of the GAGA functor. Statement
(iv)
follows at once from
(iii)
and the fact that the map
H_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(U,G)
is always two-sided flat (
cf
.
[
26
, Proposition 4.4.18]
).
∎

Theorem 4.4.9
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be a smooth affine variety with an action of a finite group
G
. Let
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
be a sheaf of Cherednik algebras on
X/G
, and
H_{t,c,\omega,X^{\operatorname{an}},G}
be the corresponding sheaf of Cherednik algebras on
X^{\operatorname{an}}/G
. There is a canonical morphism of
K
-algebras:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G),

which is faithfully flat and has dense image.

Proof.

As
X
is smooth and affine, there is a finite affine cover
(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of
X
such that each of the
U_{i}
is
G
-invariant and admits an étale map
U_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. Thus, we may assume without loss that there is an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
. Furthermore, as étale maps are preserved by the GAGA functor (
cf
.
[
9
, Theorem 5.2.1]
), there is also an étale map
X^{\operatorname{an}}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
.

As
X^{\operatorname{an}}

is a Stein space, we may use Proposition
4.2.7
to obtain a Stein covering
X^{\operatorname{an}}=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
such that each of the
X_{n}
is a
G
-invariant affinoid subdomain. Let
X_{n}=\operatorname{Sp}(A_{n})
for all
n\geq 0
. Following the strategy of the proof of Proposition
4.3.2
, we can choose a strictly increasing sequence of non-negative integers
\{m(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
and a family
(\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n})_{n\geq 0}

such that for all
n\geq 0
we have
\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}=A^{\circ}_{n}\otimes_{A^{\circ}_{n+1}}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n+1}
, and
\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}
is a Cherednik lattice of
X_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
. In this situation, we have a Fréchet-Stein presentation:

(14)

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}.

By Lemma
4.4.8
, for each
n\geq 0
we have maps:

j_{n}:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\xrightarrow{i_{X_{n}}}H_{t,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X_{n},G).

It is clear that these maps commute with the transition maps in
(\ref{equation flatness of Cher alg and GAGA})
. Hence, we obtain a map:

j:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G).

By part
(iii)

in Lemma
4.4.8
, and the fact that
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
has the inverse limit topology, it follows that
j
has dense image. All that remains is showing
j
is faithfully flat.

Let
j^{*}
denote the extension of scalars along
j
. As
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
is noetherian, it suffices to show that
j^{*}
preserves short exact sequences of finitely presented modules. By
[
32
, Corollary 3.4]
, every finitely presented module over
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
is co-admissible. As being finitely presented is preserved under extension of scalars, every finitely presented
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
-module
\mathcal{M}
satisfies that
j^{*}M
is a co-admissible
\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
-module. Thus,

j:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
is flat if and only if
j_{n}:H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)
is flat for each
n\geq 0
. This was shown in Lemma
4.4.8
.

For faithful flatness, let
\mathcal{M}
be a finitely presented
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
-module and assume that
j^{*}\mathcal{M}=0
. By
[
26
, Proposition 4.4.18]
, the map
H_{t,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)
is faithfully flat for each
n\geq 0
. Thus,
j^{*}\mathcal{M}=0
if and only if

i_{X_{n}}^{*}\mathcal{M}=0
for all
n\geq 0
. However, by
(ii)
in Lemma
4.4.8
we have

i_{X_{n}}^{*}\mathcal{M}=A_{n}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M}=0
. We will now show that this implies
\mathcal{M}=0
. If
\mathcal{M}\neq 0
, then there is a non-zero finitely generated
A
-module
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
. Consider the Fréchet-Stein presentation
B:=\mathcal{O}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})=\varprojlim_{n}A_{n}
. Then we have:

B\otimes_{A}\mathcal{N}=\left(\varprojlim A_{n}\right)\otimes_{A}\mathcal{N}=\varprojlim\left(A_{n}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{N}\right)=0,

where the second identity follows from the fact that
B\otimes_{A}\mathcal{N}
is a co-admissible
B
-module. As the map
A\rightarrow B
is faithfully flat, this implies
\mathcal{N}=0
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.4.10
.

The map
\mathcal{D}_{X}(X)\rightarrow\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_{X^{\operatorname{an}}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})
is faithfully flat with dense image.

4.5.
Arens-Michael envelopes

In the previous section, we investigated the algebraic properties of the canonical map:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G).

In this section, we will worry about the analytical properties of this extension. In particular, we will study the Arens-Michael envelopes of Cherednik algebras on smooth affine
K
-varieties. A complete treatment of Arens-Michael envelopes can be found in the book by Helemskii
[
20
]
. These envelopes where first defined by J. Taylor in
[
35
]
, but the terminology used there is different.

An Arens-Michael
K
-algebra is a locally convex
K
-algebra
topologically isomorphic to a projective limit of
K
-Banach algebras. Given a locally convex
K
-algebra
A
, let
\mathfrak{M}(A)
be the set of continuous and sub-multiplicative seminorms on
A
. For every
q\in\mathfrak{M}(A)
, the completion
\widehat{A}_{q}
of
A
with respect to
q
is a Banach
K
-algebra, and we have a morphism of locally convex
K
-algebras with dense image:

A\rightarrow\widehat{A}:=\varprojlim_{q\in\mathfrak{M}(A)}A_{q}.

The algebra
\widehat{A}
is called the Arens-Michael envelope of
A
. It is canonically an Arens-Michael algebra, and it satisfies the following universal property: Let
B
be a Banach
K
-algebra, and let
f:A\rightarrow B
be a continuous morphism of locally convex
K
-algebras. Then there is a unique continuous morphism of
K
-algebras
\widehat{f}:\widehat{A}\rightarrow B
satisfying that the following diagram is commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

In particular, the categories of continuous
K
-linear representations of
A
and
\widehat{A}
on Banach spaces are canonically isomorphic. This particular feature was used in
[
29
]
, and
[
30
]
to study the representation theory of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
via the Arens-Michael envelope
\widehat{U}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Let
V
be a
K
-vector space. We may regard
V
as a locally convex
K
-vector space by considering the finest locally convex topology on
V
. This topology is defined by letting a basis of open neighborhoods of zero be given by all
\mathcal{R}
-submodules

\mathcal{V}\subset V
such that
V=\mathcal{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}K
. This topology satisfies that any
K
-linear map
V\rightarrow W
to a locally convex
K
-vector space is continuous. Furthermore, given two
K
-vector spaces
V
and
W
with the finest locally convex topology, their projective tensor product
V\otimes_{K,\pi}W
(cf.
[
33
, Chaper IV 17.B]
) also has the finest locally convex topology. Hence, given a
K
-algebra
A
, we can regard
A
as a locally convex
K
-algebra with respect to this topology. Thus, we may form its Arens-Michael envelope
\widehat{A}
, and we call it the Arens-Michael envelope of
A
.

Theorem 4.5.1
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be a smooth affine variety with an action of a finite group
G
. Assume there is an étale map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}_{K}
, and let
H_{t,c,\omega,X,G}
be a sheaf of Cherednik algebras on
X
. The canonical map of
K
-algebras:

H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G),

is faithfully flat, and realizes

\mathcal{H}_{t,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
as the Arens-Michael envelope of
H_{t,c,\omega}(X,G)
.

Proof.

Assume
t=1
and let
X^{\operatorname{an}}=\varinjlim_{n\geq 0}X_{n}
be a Stein covering by
G
-invariant affinoid subdomains with
X_{n}=\operatorname{Sp}(A_{n})
.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.4.9
, we can choose families
(m(n))_{n\geq 0}
and
(\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n})_{n\geq 0}
such that we have a Fréchet-Stein presentation:

(15)

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}.

In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
is an Arens-Michael algebra. Furthermore, we have a map of
K
-algebras with dense image:
j:H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)
. Thus, we just need to show that
\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X^{\operatorname{an}},G)

satisfies the universal property of Arens-Michael envelopes.

Let
q
be a continuous sub-multiplicative norm on
H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)
, and let
B_{q}
be the completion of
H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)
with respect to
q
. We need to show that there is some
n\geq 0
such that the map
\psi:H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow B_{q}
factors as the following composition of continuous maps of
K
-algebras:

H_{1,c,\omega}(X,G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}\rightarrow B_{q}.

By construction, we have an identification:

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}=\widehat{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}K.

Hence, it suffices to show that there is some
n\geq 0
such that we have a morphism of
\mathcal{R}
-algebras:

\tilde{\psi}:H_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}\rightarrow B^{\circ}_{q},\textnormal{ such that }\tilde{\psi}\otimes_{K}\operatorname{Id}=\psi.

Let
\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{s}\}
be a set of elements which generate
A
as a
K
-algebra. By Lemma
4.4.7
, the map
A\rightarrow A_{n}
has dense image. Hence, the set
\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{s}\}
is a set of topological generators of
A_{n}

as a Banach
K
-algebra. As
\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{s}\}
is a finite set, there is a minimal non-negative integer
s(n)\geq 0
such that:

\{\pi^{s(n)}f_{1},\cdots,\pi^{s(n)}f_{s}\}\subset A_{n}^{\circ}.

For simplicity, we may assume that
s(n)=n
, and let
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be the image of the map:

\varphi:\mathcal{R}\langle t_{1},\cdots,t_{s}\rangle\rightarrow A_{n}^{\circ},\textnormal{ }t_{i}\mapsto\pi^{n}f_{i}.

Then
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is an admissible
\mathcal{R}
-algebra, and
\mathfrak{X}_{n}=\operatorname{Spf}(\mathcal{A}_{n})
is an affine formal model of
X_{n}
. In particular, as
K
is discretely valued,
A^{\circ}_{n}
is a finite module over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
(
cf
.
[
7
, 6.4.1 Corollary 6]
).

As the sequences
\{\pi^{n}f_{i}\}_{n\geq 0}
converge to zero for all
1\leq i\leq s
, it follows that there is some non-negative integer
N
such that for all
n\geq N
we have
\{\pi^{n}f_{1},\cdots,\pi^{n}f_{s}\}\subset B^{\circ}_{q}
. Hence, as
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
is an affine formal model of
X_{n}
, the map
A\rightarrow B_{q}
extends to a continuous morphism of Banach
K
-algebras
\varphi_{n}:A_{n}\rightarrow B_{q}
, which satisfies
\varphi(\mathcal{A}_{n})\subset B_{q}^{\circ}
. As
B^{\circ}_{q}
is integrally closed, and
A^{\circ}_{n}
is integral over
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, it follows that
\varphi_{n}(A_{n}^{\circ})\subset B^{\circ}_{q}
. Furthermore, as
G
is a finite group, all its elements have finite order. Hence, we have
G\subset B_{q}^{\circ}
. A fortiori, it follows that
\varphi_{n}(G\ltimes A_{n}^{\circ})\subset B_{q}^{\circ}
for all
n\geq N
.

For each
n\geq 0
, let
\mathscr{T}_{n}
be the image of the anchor map
\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/K}(X_{n})
. Let
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
be a set of generators of
\pi^{m(N)}\mathscr{T}_{N}
as a finite
A^{\circ}_{N}
-module, and let
D_{v_{1}},\cdots,D_{v_{r}}
be a choice of Dunkl-Opdam operators in

H_{1,c,\omega}(X_{N},G)_{\pi^{m(N)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,N}}
. Again, the set
\{D_{v_{1}},\cdots,D_{v_{r}}\}
is finite. Hence, there is some
s\geq 0
such that
\{\pi^{s}D_{v_{1}},\cdots,\pi^{s}D_{v_{r}}\}\subset B_{q}^{\circ}
. By
(ii)
in
[
26
, Proposition 3.1.8]
, and the fact that
\varphi_{N}(G\ltimes A^{\circ}_{N})\subset B_{q}^{\circ}
, it follows that we have a morphism of
\mathcal{R}
-algebras:

\tilde{\psi}:H_{1,c,\omega}(X_{N},G)_{\pi^{m(N)+s}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,N}}\rightarrow B^{\circ}_{q},\textnormal{ such that }\tilde{\psi}\otimes_{K}\operatorname{Id}=\psi.

As the right hand side is
\pi
-adically complete, we obtain a map
of Banach
K
-algebras:

\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{N},G)_{\pi^{m(N)+s}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,N}}\rightarrow B_{q}.

Choosing
n>N
with
m(n)>m(N)+s
, we get a map
\mathcal{H}_{1,c,\omega}(X_{n},G)_{\pi^{m(n)}\mathscr{A}_{\omega,n}}\rightarrow B_{q}
, as wanted.
∎

As an upshot of the proof of Theorem
4.5.1
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 4.5.2
.

Let
X
be an affine finite type
K
-variety and let
X^{\operatorname{an}}
be its rigid analytification. Then
\mathcal{O}_{X^{a}n}(X^{\operatorname{an}})
is the Arens-Michael envelope of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
.

The corresponding fact for complex affine varieties was shown by J. Taylor in
[
35
, Section 5]
.

5.
Category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}
for
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebras

5.1.
Rational Cherednik algebras

Let us start by fixing some terminology:
Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space, and
G\subset\operatorname{Gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be a finite group. By replacing
K
by a finite field extension, we may assume that the algebra
K[G]
is split semi-simple over
K
. Furthermore, as
G
is finite, it is compact. Hence, it is contained in a maximal compact open subgroup of
\operatorname{Gl}(\mathfrak{h})
. As all such groups are conjugate to
\operatorname{Gl}_{n}(\mathcal{R})
, it follows that we can choose a basis
\underline{t}:=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})
of
\mathfrak{h}
such that
G\subset\operatorname{Gl}_{n}(\mathcal{R})
. Let
\underline{t}^{*}:=(t_{1}^{*},\cdots,t_{n}^{*})
be the dual basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. In this situation, we get identifications:

\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{Spec}(K[\underline{t}^{*}])
, and
\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\operatorname{Spec}(K[\underline{t}])
. Until the end of the section, we fix a
K
-vector space
\mathfrak{h}
, a finite group
G\subset\operatorname{Gl}(\mathfrak{h})
, and a choice of basis
\underline{t}
,
\underline{t}^{*}
satisfying the conditions above.

We let
S(G)\subset G
be the set of pseudo-reflections in
G
. That is,
S(G)
is the following set:

S(G):=\{g\in G\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }\operatorname{Rank}(\operatorname{Id}-g)=1\}.

That is,
g\in G
is a pseudo-reflection if and only if its vector space of fixed points is a hyperplane. The group
G
acts on
S(G)
by conjugation, and we can define the following
K
-vector space:

\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G):=\{G\textnormal{-equivariant functions }S(G)\rightarrow K\}.

We call
\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
the space of reflection functions of
G
. For the rest of the section, we fix a reflection function
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
. Let
g\in G
be a pseudo-reflection, and let
Y_{g}
be its fixed hyperplane. As
\operatorname{Rank}(\operatorname{Id}-g)=1
, it follows that
g
only has one non-one eigenvalue. We denote this eigenvalue by
\lambda_{g}
. Notice that
\lambda_{g}
is a root of unity in
K
.

Given
v\in\mathfrak{h}
,
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we denote the evaluation of
x
at
v
by
(v,x)
. As
Y_{g}
is a hyperplane, we can choose a linear form
\alpha_{g}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
such that
Y
is the kernel of the map
\alpha_{g}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow K
. The set of such forms is a one dimensional
K
-vector space inside
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. We choose
\alpha_{g}^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}
to be the unique eigenvector satisfying
(\alpha_{g}^{\vee},\alpha_{g})=2
. Now that the notation has been fixed, we can finally define rational Cherednik algebras:

Definition 5.1.1
.

The rational Cherednik algebra
H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
is the quotient of
G\ltimes T(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})
by the two-sided ideal generated by the following elements:

(i)

[v,w]
, where
v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
.

(ii)

[x,y]
, where
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

(iii)

[v,x]-(v,x)+\sum_{g\in S(G)}c(g)(v,\alpha_{g})(\alpha_{g}^{\vee},x)g
, where
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
v\in\mathfrak{h}
.

For the rest of the section we fix a rational Cherednik algebra
\textnormal{H}(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Remark 5.1.2
.

Let us make a few comments on the definition:

(i)

H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
only depends on
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
, and not on the choices of
\alpha_{g}
, and
\alpha_{g}^{\vee}
.

(ii)

If either
c=0
or
S(G)
is empty, then
H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
is isomorphic to the Weyl algebra of
\mathfrak{h}
.

Hence, rational Cherednik algebras provide generalizations of Weyl algebras which contain the group algebra
K[G]
as a subalgebra. This is a relevant feature, as it will allow us to construct
H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
-modules out of
K
-linear
G
-representations.

We would like to point out that this is not the only way of defining rational Cherednik algebras. There is another way of constructing
H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
in terms of Dunkl-Opdam operators. Indeed, let
\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{reg}}
be the locus of points with trivial stabilizer for the action of
G
. This is an open subspace in the Zariski topology, and we let
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{reg}})
be its algebra of differential operators. For any
v\in\mathfrak{h}
we define the standard Dunkl-Opdam operator of
v
as the following element in
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{reg}})
:

D_{v}=v+\sum_{g\in S(G)}\frac{2c(g)}{1-\lambda_{g}}\frac{(\alpha_{g},v)}{\alpha_{g}}(g-1).

It can be shown that
H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
is canonically isomorphic to the subalgebra of
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{reg}})
generated by
G
,
K[\underline{t}^{*}]
,
and the standard Dunkl-Opdam operators. The relevance of this presentation of
H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
stems from the fact that it is the way used in
[
13
]
to obtain generalizations of rational Cherednik algebras to the non-linear case.

Rational Cherednik algebras share many properties with Weyl algebras. In particular, there is a canonical filtration on
H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}
, known as the Dunkl-Opdam filtration. This filtration satisfies a version of the PBW Theorem. In particular, there is a canonical isomorphism of graded
K
-algebras:

G\ltimes\operatorname{Sym}_{K}(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*})\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}H(\mathfrak{h},G)_{c}.

As a consequence, there is a decomposition of
K[\underline{t}^{*}]
-modules:

(16)

H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)=K[\underline{t}^{*}]\otimes_{K}K[G]\otimes_{K}K[\underline{t}].

As the reader will undoubtedly already suspect, this decomposition hints at the fact that rational Cherednik algebras admit a triangular decomposition. This is shown in
[
15
, Section 3.2]
.

Theorem 5.1.3
.

The rational Cherednik algebra
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
admits a triangular decomposition, with graded subalgebras
A=K[\underline{t}^{*}]
,
B=K[\underline{t}]
,
H=K[G]
, and grading element:

\partial=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{*}_{i}t_{i}+\frac{\operatorname{dim}(\mathfrak{h})}{2}-\sum_{g\in S(G)}\frac{2c(g)}{1-\lambda_{g}}g.

The element
\partial
is called the deformed Euler vector. Following definition
2.1.3
, we have:

\partial_{0}=\frac{\operatorname{dim}(\mathfrak{h})}{2}-\sum_{g\in S(G)}\frac{2c(g)}{1-\lambda_{g}}g.

In particular, for any
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, the scalar
c(W)\in K
is the scalar by which
\partial_{0}
acts on
W
.

Definition 5.1.4
.

We call the triangular decomposition of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
described above the canonical decomposition of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
.

By the results in Section
2.1
, for any choice of reflection function
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(G)
we get a category
\mathcal{O}_{c}
associated to the triangular decomposition of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
. In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{c}
is a highest weight category, a Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G))
, and its irreducible objects are in one to one correspondence with the irreducible
K
-linear
G
-representations. Furthermore, as shown in Section
2.1
, for very
W\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, the module
L(W)

is a finite
K[\underline{t}^{*}]
-module. In particular, it induces a coherent sheaf on
\mathfrak{h}
. Letting
\mathcal{L}(W)
denote this coherent sheaf, we obtain a new invariant for
W
. Namely, the support of
\mathcal{L}(W)
in
\mathfrak{h}
. We remark that this support depends on the choice of
c\in\operatorname{Ref}(\mathfrak{h},G)
. The study of this invariant in the complex case has been the subject of intensive research in past years. A sample of this may be found in
[
4
]
and
[
16
]
.

5.2.
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebras

Keeping the notation and choices of the previous section, let
\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}}
be the rigid analytification of
\mathfrak{h}
. In this section we will show that the
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebra:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G):=\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}}/G,\mathcal{H}_{c,\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G}),

admits a triangular decomposition in the sense of Fréchet-Stein algebras.

By Theorem
4.5.1
, the
p
-adic rational Cherednik algebra
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
satisfies that the canonical map:

i_{c}:H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G),

is faithfully flat, has dense image, and realizes
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
as the Arens-Michael envelope of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
. Hence, every Banach space representation of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
lifts to a continuous Banach space representation of
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
. As any such representation factors through one of the Banach algebras in a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h}^{\operatorname{an}},G)
, it follows that every
K
-linear representation of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
on a Banach space lifts to a representation of a Banach algebra with a triangular decomposition.

Remark 5.2.1
.

In order to simplify the notation, we will write
\mathfrak{h}
for both the algebraic variety and the rigid analytic space. The context will make clear which of the two we are referring to.

As shown in Proposition
4.3.2
,
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
is naturally a Fréchet-Stein algebra. In order to show that it admits a triangular decomposition, we will now give a Fréchet-Stein presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
: We start by introducing the following affinoid spaces:

\mathbb{B}^{n}(m)=\operatorname{Sp}(K\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}^{*}\rangle),\textnormal{ }\tilde{\mathbb{B}}^{n}(m)=\operatorname{Sp}(K\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}\rangle),

corresponding to the closed disks of radius
|\pi|^{-m}
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
respectively. We denote the affinoid algebras associated to these spaces by:

\mathscr{A}(m)=:K\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}^{*}\rangle=K\langle\pi^{m}t^{*}_{1},\cdots,\pi^{m}t^{*}_{n}\rangle,\textnormal{ }\mathscr{B}(m):=K\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}\rangle=K\langle\pi^{m}t_{1},\cdots,\pi^{m}t_{n}\rangle.

As
G\subset\operatorname{Gl}_{n}(\mathcal{R})
,
\mathbb{B}^{n}(m)
and
\tilde{\mathbb{B}}^{n}(m)
are
G
-invariant. Thus, we have
G
-invariant Stein coverings

\mathfrak{h}=\varinjlim_{m}\mathbb{B}^{n}(m),\textnormal{ }\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\varinjlim_{m}\tilde{\mathbb{B}}^{n}(m).

The vectors
t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\in\mathfrak{h}
form a basis of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{h}/K}(\mathfrak{h})
as an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathfrak{h})
-module. Hence, we can follow the procedure in Proposition
4.3.2
, and define the
\mathscr{A}(m)
-module:

\mathcal{A}_{m}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathcal{R}\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}^{*}\rangle.

We can also choose a strictly increasing sequence
\{r(m)\}_{m\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
such that
\pi^{r(m)}\mathcal{A}_{m}
is a Cherednik lattice of
\mathbb{B}^{n}(m)
for all
m\geq 0
. In this situation, we can define the following
\mathcal{R}
-algebras:

Definition 5.2.2
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}(m)^{\operatorname{reg}}
be an affinoid subdomain which contains the Shilov boundary of
\mathbb{B}^{n}(m)
. We define
\mathcal{H}(m)_{c}
as the filtered
\mathcal{R}
-algebra generated inside
G\ltimes\mathcal{D}(U)
by
\mathcal{R}\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}^{*}\rangle
,
G
, and the following family of standard Dunkl-Opdam operators:

D_{\pi^{r(m)}t_{i}}=\pi^{r(m)}t_{i}+\sum_{g\in S(G)}\frac{2c(g)}{1-\lambda_{g}}\frac{(\alpha_{g},\pi^{r(m)}t_{i})}{\alpha_{g}}(g-1),\textnormal{ for }1\leq i\leq n.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c}
be the
\pi
-adic completion of
\mathcal{H}(m)_{c}
, and
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K}=\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}K
. In this situation, each
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K}
is a two-sided noetherian Banach
K
-algebra, and we have a Fréchet-Stein presentation:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)=\varprojlim_{m\geq 0}\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K}.

The first step towards showing that
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
admits a triangular decomposition is showing that each
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K}
admits a triangular decomposition as a Banach algebra.

Lemma 5.2.3
.

There is a canonical decomposition of Banach
\mathscr{A}(m)
-modules:

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K}=\mathscr{A}(m)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}K[G]\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathscr{B}(r(m)).

Proof.

As a consequence of
[
26
, Corollary 4.4.4]
, we have an isomorphism of
G\ltimes\mathcal{R}\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}^{*}\rangle
-modules:

\mathcal{H}(m)_{c}=\mathcal{R}\langle\pi^{m}\underline{t}^{*}\rangle\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}[\pi^{r(m)}\underline{t}].

applying
\pi
-adic completion and tensoring by
K
yields the desired isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 5.2.4
.

The noetherian Banach
K
-algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K}
admits a triangular decomposition with respect to the following data:

(H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G),K[\underline{t}^{*}],K[\underline{t}],K[G],\partial).

Proof.

We need to show that these data satisfy the conditions of definition
3.1.1
. The fact that
(K[\underline{t}^{*}],K[\underline{t}],K[G],\partial)
is a triangular decomposition of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
was shown in Theorem
5.1.3
. By construction,
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K}
is the completion of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
with respect to a norm. Similarly,

\mathscr{A}(m)
is the completion of
K[\underline{t}^{*}]
. Furthermore, every element
x\in\mathscr{A}(m)
is of the form:

x=\sum_{I\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0,n}}a_{i}\underline{t}^{*,I},

which shows that
\mathscr{A}(m)
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition of finite type with respect to the action of
\operatorname{ad}(\partial)
, and that
\mathscr{A}(m)^{\operatorname{ws}}=K[\underline{t}^{*}]
. An analogous argument shows that
\mathscr{B}(r(m))
admits a semi-simple weight space decomposition, and that
\mathscr{B}(r(m))^{\operatorname{ws}}=K[\underline{t}]
.
∎

All our results thus far imply the following theorem:

Theorem 5.2.5
.

The rational Cherednik algebra admits a Fréchet-Stein presentation:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)=\varprojlim_{m\geq 0}\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(m)_{c,K},

which admits a triangular decomposition of Fréchet-Stein algebras with respect to the following data:

(H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G),K[\underline{t}^{*}],K[\underline{t}],K[G],\partial).

Hence, we may apply the theory from Section
3.2
to this setting. In particular, the triangular decomposition of
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
implies the existence of a category
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
, which contains all Verma modules. We may condense the most important results of the paper into the following two corollaries:

Corollary 5.2.6
.

There is a full abelian subcategory
\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}\subset\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G))
satisfying the following:

(i)

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
is closed under closed submodules and finite direct sums.

(ii)

\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}
is a highest weight category. Furthermore, its irreducible objects are in one-to-one correspondence with the irreducible
K
-linear representations of
G
.

Corollary 5.2.7
.

The canonical map
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

It is faithfully flat and has dense image.

(ii)

It makes
\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
the Arens-Michael envelope of
H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)
.

(iii)

It induces an equivalence of highest weight categories:

\mathcal{H}_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)\otimes_{H_{c}(\mathfrak{h},G)}-:\mathcal{O}_{c}\leftrightarrows\wideparen{\mathcal{O}}_{c}:(-)^{\operatorname{ws}}.

Appendix A

A.1.
Stein Quotients

Let
X
be a rigid
G
-variety satisfying
\operatorname{(G-Aff)}
, and such that
X/G
is a Stein space. In this Appendix, we will show that
X
is also a Stein space. In order to show this, we need to introduce the notion of universal compactification of an adic space:

Definition A.1.1
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spa}(A)
be an affinoid rigid adic space over
K
. Its universal compactification over
K
, denoted
X^{c}
, is a partially proper adic space with a locally closed embedding
X\rightarrow X^{c}
such that for any partially proper adic space
Y
with a map
X\rightarrow Y
there is a unique factorization:

X\rightarrow X^{c}\rightarrow Y

Obviously, the notion of a universal compactification makes sense for more general spaces and maps of adic spaces. A comprehensive study of this construction can be found in
[
21
, Section 5.1]
.

Lemma A.1.2

(
[
1
, Proposition 3.4.5.]
)
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spa}(A)
, then
X^{c}=\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{c})
, where
A^{c}
is the integral closure of the
\mathcal{R}
-subalgebra of
A
generated by the topologically nilpotent elements
A^{\circ\circ}
.

Lemma A.1.3
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spa}(B)\rightarrow Y=\operatorname{Spa}(A)
be an open immersion of rigid spaces. Then
X\Subset Y
if and only if we have a factorization
X\rightarrow X^{c}\rightarrow Y
.

Proof.

Assume that
X\Subset Y
. Then there is a family of affinoid generators
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\in A
such that:

(17)

X\subset\{x\in Y\textnormal{ }|\textnormal{ }|f_{i}(x)|<1,\textnormal{ for }1\leq i\leq n\}

Consider the surjection
K\langle t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\rangle\rightarrow A
given by
t_{i}\mapsto f_{i}
, and let
\mathcal{R}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle
be the image of
\mathcal{R}\langle t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\rangle
under this map. Then
\mathcal{R}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle
is an affine formal model of
A
. In particular,
A^{\circ}
is finite over
\mathcal{R}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle
. Thus, by Lemma
A.1.2
, there is a factorization

X\rightarrow X^{c}\rightarrow Y
if and only if the image of
\mathcal{R}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle
under
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
is contained in
B^{c}
. Thus, we are reduced to showing that
\varphi(f_{i})\in B^{c}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. However, by equation (
17
), it is clear that for every
x\in X
we have
|f_{i}(x)|<1
for all
i
. In particular, it follows that
\varphi(f_{i})\in B^{\circ\circ}\subset B^{c}
.

For the converse, assume that we have a factorization
X\rightarrow X^{c}\rightarrow Y
. Let
\overline{X}
be the closure of
X
in
Y
. It suffices to show that there is a surjection:

K\langle t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\rangle\rightarrow A,\textnormal{ }t_{i}\mapsto f_{i},

and some
\epsilon>0
such that
\overline{X}\subset Y(\epsilon f_{1},\cdots,\epsilon f_{n})
. As
X^{c}
is a partially proper adic space, it follows that
X^{c}\rightarrow Y
is a partially proper map. In particular, the image of
\psi:X^{c}\rightarrow Y
is a closed subspace of
Y
. Thus, the closure
\overline{X}
of
X
in
Y
is contained in the image of
X^{c}\rightarrow Y
. Therefore, it suffices to show that the map
X^{c}\rightarrow Y
factors as:

X^{c}\rightarrow Y(\epsilon f_{1},\cdots,\epsilon f_{n})\rightarrow Y.

By the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 2.5.2]
, it suffices to show that the induced map:

\tilde{\varphi}:A^{\circ}/A^{\circ\circ}\rightarrow B^{c}/B^{\circ\circ},

satisfies that
\varphi(A^{\circ}/A^{\circ\circ})
is integral over the residue field
k
. However, this follows from the fact that
B^{c}/B^{\circ\circ}
is integral over
k
by construction. Thus, the lemma holds.
∎

Proposition A.1.4
.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety satisfying
\operatorname{(G-Aff)}
. It the quotient
X/G
is a Stein space, then
X
is a Stein space as well.

Proof.

Let
X/G=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}/G
be a Stein covering of
X/G
. Then
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
is an admissible affinoid cover of
X
. Furthermore, for each
n\geq 0
we have the following pullback diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n}/G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n+1}/G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/G}

In particular, the maps
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n+1}
are Weierstrass immersions, so
X=\varinjlim_{n}X_{n}
is a quasi-Stein space. It suffices to show that
X_{n}\Subset X_{n+1}
for each
n\geq 0
. Fix some
n\geq 0
. By assumption we have
X_{n}/G\Subset X_{n+1}/G
. Thus, by Lemma
A.1.3
we have a factorization:

X_{n}/G\rightarrow(X_{n}/G)^{c}\rightarrow X_{n+1}/G.

Let
Y=(X_{n}/G)^{c}\times_{X_{n+1}/G}X_{n+1}
. As the map
X\rightarrow X/G
is finite, it follows that
Y\rightarrow(X_{n}/G)^{c}
is also finite. Thus,
Y
is partially proper over
\operatorname{Spa}(K)
. Furthermore, we have a factorization:

X_{n}\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X_{n+1}.

As
Y
is partially proper, the universal property of compactification implies that the first map factors as
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n}^{c}\rightarrow Y
. Thus, the Weierstrass immersion
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n+1}
factors as
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n}^{c}\rightarrow X_{n+1}
. Thus, by Lemma
A.1.3
we get
X_{n}\Subset X_{n+1}
, as we wanted to show.
∎

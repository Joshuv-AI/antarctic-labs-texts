Advances in RNA m6A modification research and its role in poultry economic traits
Authors: Jing Liu, Haodong Liu, Xuwen Huang, Zhixiang Xiao, Site Li, Xiaolong Hu, Sanfeng Liu, Huirong Mao, Biao Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractN6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant and evolutionarily conserved internal epitranscriptomic modification on eukaryotic mRNA, playing pivotal roles in animal growth, development, metabolism, and immune responses by modulating mRNA splicing, stability, and translation efficiency. With advancements in technologies such as MeRIP-seq, GLORI-seq, and Nanopore direct RNA sequencing, researchers can now characterize the dynamic landscape of m6A modifications at the transcriptome-wide level. This review summarizes the fundamental features of m6A modifications, compares various detection methods and computational analytical approaches, and critically synthesizes recent progress on the roles of m6A modifications in poultry skeletal muscle development, adipose deposition, reproduction, and immunity and disease resistance. Particular emphasis is placed on the regulatory mechanisms of key enzymes, including METTL3/14, FTO, ALKBH5, and the YTHDF protein family. The potential applications, prospects and current limits of RNA m6A modification as a molecular breeding tool in poultry are also discussed.Keywords: Poultry economic traits, RNA m6A modification, Detection methods, m6A-modifying enzymes, Differentially m6A-modified genes

Introduction

With the rapid development of molecular biology and high-throughput sequencing technologies, RNA modification–omics has emerged as a frontier of epigenetic research for deciphering the regulatory mechanisms underlying complex traits(Roundtree et al., 2017). N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant and widespread internal chemical modification on messenger RNA (mRNA) and non-coding RNAs, exerting crucial regulatory roles in the growth, development, and disease resistance of livestock and poultry, particularly in avian species(Wang et al., 2025a; Shi et al., 2026). m6A modifications dynamically regulate gene expression to influence skeletal muscle and adipose tissue development and are also central to germ cell differentiation and immune responses. m6A is installed, removed, and interpreted by a conserved set of proteins (Fig. 1). The writer complex, with METTL3, METTL14, and METTL16 as core components, methylates adenosine within the RRACH motif (R = G or A; H = A, C, or U), most often near stop codons and in the 3’ untranslated region (3’UTR)(Roundtree et al., 2017). The erasers FTO and ALKBH5 reverse this mark. Reader proteins, including YTHDF family members, YTHDC1/2, and IGF2BPs, then bind m6A and influence splicing, export, translation, or decay(Shi et al., 2019; Zaccara et al., 2019). This cycle is now well established and has been reviewed in detail elsewhere, including a recent poultry-focused account(Yang et al., 2026). The present article therefore gives only a brief outline and turns to the roles of m6A in poultry economic traits.

RNA m6A modifications have been extensively studied across multiple avian species, offering novel perspectives on the molecular regulation of economically important traits. For example, in the context of muscle development, circSIK2 was found to be methylated by the m6A methyltransferase METTL3, leading to its translation into the SIK2-176aa protein, which effectively promotes myoblast proliferation, differentiation, and muscle hypertrophy(Wang et al., 2025a). In lipid metabolism, the demethylase FTO enhances the RNA stability of CTNNB1 mRNA by removing its m6A modifications, thereby promoting the proliferation and differentiation of chicken preadipocytes(Li et al., 2022b). These findings highlight strong associations between m6A modification sites and economic traits such as muscle development and fat deposition in poultry. Most current studies of RNA m6A modification in poultry have focused on protein-coding transcripts. A smaller but growing number of reports indicate that m6A also marks non-coding RNAs(Wang et al., 2025a). Even so, functional analyses of m6A on lncRNAs, circRNAs, and miRNAs in poultry remain limited compared with those on mRNA. Research on RNA m6A modification in poultry has expanded rapidly in recent years, and a recent review has summarized key advances in this field(Yang et al., 2026). The present article differs in emphasis. In addition to surveying trait-associated m6A landscapes, we systematically compare current detection and analytical methods and outline a practical strategy suited to poultry research. We further integrate mechanistic evidence across skeletal muscle development, adipose deposition, reproduction, and immunity/disease resistance, with a focus on the functions of m6A-modifying enzymes and on studies conducted in chicken, duck, goose, and quail.

The continued advancement of high-throughput sequencing technologies has enabled extensive epitranscriptomic studies of RNA m6A modifications across biology, medicine, and agriculture. Current m6A detection methods include antibody-based immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeRIP-seq), single-base resolution technologies (e.g., GLORI-seq), and the recently developed Nanopore direct RNA sequencing. Each technique presents distinct advantages and limitations in terms of resolution, throughput, and cost. This review compares the principles, applicability, and limitations of these detection methods to help poultry researchers select the most suitable approach. In contrast to previous overviews, we place particular emphasis on integrating findings across major economic traits and on evaluating the practical suitability of different m6A detection platforms for poultry research. At the transcriptome level, numerous studies in poultry have established close links between m6A modifications and key economic traits, including muscle development, fat deposition, reproductive performance, and disease resistance, thereby deepening our understanding of the roles of m6A modifications in the avian genetic regulatory network (Fig. 1). By systematically synthesizing the progress of RNA m6A modification research in poultry, we not only gain a more comprehensive insight into the multifaceted regulatory functions of m6A in poultry production but also provide a theoretical foundation for the future exploration of m6A-associated molecular markers.

Comparison of methods for RNA m6A detection and analysis

Global detection methods for RNA m6A modifications primarily include LC-MS/MS, RNA m6A Dot Blotting, and ELISA-based kits. LC-MS/MS achieves absolute quantification by enzymatically digesting RNA into individual nucleosides, followed by liquid chromatographic separation and tandem mass spectrometry to quantify the ratio of m6A to adenosine (A). This method offers high accuracy and specificity but requires specialized equipment, involves complex procedures, and cannot provide positional information(Mathur et al., 2021). RNA m6A Dot Blotting uses an anti-m6A antibody to immunologically detect spotted RNA, enabling semi-quantitative assessment of global m6A levels via chemiluminescence or colorimetric detection. This technique is simple, rapid, and cost-effective, making it suitable for preliminary screening; however, it suffers from low sensitivity, a semi-quantitative nature, and potential cross-reactivity of antibodies(Shen et al., 2017). ELISA-based kits immobilize RNA, capture it with an anti-m6A antibody, and quantify it through enzyme-linked colorimetric detection. These kits offer high throughput, standardization, and ease of use, particularly for large-scale sample comparisons, but remain semi-quantitative and depend on antibody specificity(Ensinck et al., 2023). Overall, LC-MS/MS is preferred for precise validation, while Dot Blotting and ELISA are more suitable for rapid screening; these three methods are often used in combination to cross-validate results.

High-throughput approaches for detecting RNA m6A modifications primarily rely on antibody enrichment or chemical/enzymatic conversion coupled with next-generation sequencing (NGS) or third-generation sequencing technologies. The classic MeRIP-seq/m6A-seq uses an m6A antibody for immunoprecipitation to enrich m6A-containing RNA fragments, which are then compared with Input RNA-seq data to identify enriched peaks(Dominissini et al., 2012; Meyer et al., 2012). miCLIP/m6A-CLIP introduces UV crosslinking upon antibody binding, inducing reverse transcription mutations that enable single-nucleotide resolution mapping(Linder et al., 2015). Antibody-independent methods include Mazter-seq, which leverages the MazF restriction endonuclease to cleave only unmethylated ACA sequences, while m6A-modified ACA sites remain protected; m6A sites are identified by comparing read depletion or abundance changes before and after enzymatic digestion(Garcia-Campos et al., 2019). GLORI-seq adopts a chemical strategy: guanosine is first protected with glyoxal, and then nitrite-mediated deamination converts unmethylated adenosine (A) to inosine (I, read as G in sequencing), whereas the methyl group on m6A prevents deamination(Liu et al., 2023a). By comparing sequencing results before and after treatment, single-nucleotide resolution and absolute quantification are achieved. Nanopore direct RNA sequencing (ONT DRS) eliminates the need for cDNA synthesis and PCR amplification, allowing intact RNA molecules to pass through protein nanopores; m6A and other modifications generate characteristic perturbations in ionic current signals, and machine learning algorithms decode these signals to directly identify m6A sites(Hendra et al., 2022).

The strengths and limitations of these high-throughput methods significantly affect their suitability for different applications (Table 1). MeRIP-seq offers high throughput, broad coverage, mature workflows, and moderate cost. Its resolution is low, usually at peak level (∼100 nt). Antibody bias, nonspecific binding, false-positive peaks, and poor stoichiometry estimates are additional limits(Helm et al., 2019). miCLIP provides single-nucleotide resolution and reveals RNA–protein interaction information, but the experimental protocol is more complex, UV crosslinking efficiency is inconsistent, the required starting RNA amount is higher, and it remains constrained by antibody specificity(Hsu and He, 2019). Mazter-seq is antibody-free, simple, and suitable for high-throughput screening, but it is limited to ACA motif sequences, cannot cover all potential sites, and yields binary (presence/absence) rather than quantitative data(Garcia-Campos et al., 2019). GLORI-seq achieves antibody-free, single-nucleotide, high-precision absolute quantification with reduced bias, but the chemical conversion steps are relatively cumbersome, may introduce technical biases due to incomplete deamination, and the experimental cost is relatively high. Nanopore DRS offers unique advantages, including direct detection without amplification bias, long reads supporting full-length transcript analysis, and real-time single-molecule monitoring. However, current signal noise is relatively high, site identification accuracy depends heavily on advanced algorithms, and the data analysis threshold is substantial(Zhong et al., 2023). Poultry samples are easier to collect than samples from larger livestock, and population-level sampling is feasible. We therefore recommend a two-step approach. ELISA or dot blotting can be used first to compare global m6A levels. MeRIP-seq can then be used for transcriptome-wide mapping. GLORI-seq or Nanopore direct RNA sequencing may be added when specific candidate sites need quantitative checks.

Specific analysis of high-throughput RNA m6A modification data relies on dedicated peak-calling and differential analysis tools. Mainstream software includes m6AViewer(Antanaviciute et al., 2017) (integrating peak detection, visualization, and motif analysis), MeRIPseqPipe(Bao et al., 2022) (a Nextflow-based integrated pipeline supporting peak calling, annotation, and functional enrichment), and Nanopore-specific tools(Zhong et al., 2023). For MeRIP-seq data, commonly used analytical methods include MACS2(Zhang et al., 2008; Gaspar, 2018) (adapted from the ChIP-seq framework, using Poisson or negative binomial models to compare IP vs. Input to identify enriched peaks) and exomePeak/MeTPeak(Meng et al., 2013; Cui et al., 2016) (R packages specifically optimized for MeRIP-seq, considering transcript length and expression levels for more accurate peak calling and differential analysis, though computationally less efficient and sensitive to parameter settings). In comparison, the DEQ package(McIntyre et al., 2020) (integrating EdgeR, DESeq2, and QNB models) offers superior statistical robustness for peak identification and higher accuracy in detecting differential methylation, particularly suitable for complex samples or low-coverage data; however, it is more complex to implement, demands greater computational resources, and has not yet been tested on poultry m6A datasets. In practice, the most commonly adopted strategy is MACS2 for preliminary screening followed by exomePeak for refined analysis.

Role of RNA m6A modification in poultry growth and skeletal muscle development

m6A modifications play a vital role at distinct stages of avian skeletal muscle development by dynamically regulating key genes and signaling pathways. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that m6A is involved in myoblast proliferation and differentiation, muscle fiber hypertrophy, fiber type conversion, and the modulation of muscle growth–related pathways in response to environmental cues. For instance, constant light exposure during early development suppresses the expression of IGF family members in the chicken hypothalamus, hippocampus, and liver through m6A-mediated inhibition, thereby potentially affecting embryonic growth(Yang et al., 2022). Additionally, the conserved m6A topology within chicken embryo β-actin mRNA is essential for proper mRNA localization and translation(Baron et al., 2023). These findings indicate that m6A modifications exert broad and fine-tuned regulatory control over skeletal muscle growth and development in poultry.

MeRIP-seq analyses have unveiled the dynamic landscape of m6A modifications and identified important candidate genes during poultry skeletal muscle development (Fig. 2A). In duck embryonic skeletal muscle, comparison between embryonic day 13 (E13, proliferation-dominant phase) and E19 (differentiation-dominant phase) revealed that differentially methylated genes (DMGs) were significantly enriched in pathways related to muscle development and cell cycle(Chen et al., 2022). In Wenchang chicken embryonic breast muscle at different developmental stages, m6A modifications and miRNAs coordinately regulate the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby contributing to skeletal muscle development(Gu et al., 2022). Comparative analysis of 7-week-old breast muscle between a local slow-growing chicken breed (Xinghua chicken, XH) and a fast-growing White Recessive Rock (WRR) broiler line identified 6,476 differentially methylated m6A peaks, of which approximately 90% (5823) were downregulated in XH chickens, underscoring the important regulatory role of m6A in muscle hypertrophy(Wang et al., 2024a). In a study of differential m6A landscapes between breast and leg muscles of Guizhou White geese, DMGs were significantly enriched in pathways including neuroactive ligand–receptor interaction, glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis, and actin cytoskeleton regulation, with LGI1, CDK18, and LPAR2 identified as candidate genes governing myofiber morphology and development(Zhao et al., 2025c). In addition, transcriptome-wide m6A profiling in Dingan goose embryonic breast muscle revealed dynamic changes in m6A patterns across developmental stages, with DMGs significantly enriched in pathways related to muscle development including Wnt signaling pathway, mTOR signaling and FoxO signaling pathway, providing early evidence for the regulatory role of m6A in goose skeletal muscle formation(Xu et al., 2021). Collectively, these studies reveal the dynamic and tissue-specific regulatory features of m6A modifications across developmental stages, breeds, and muscle types in poultry. m6A-modifying enzymes exert critical and specific regulatory functions in poultry skeletal muscle development. Among methyltransferases, METTL14 serves as a core regulator of myoblast fate. On one hand, METTL14 modulates the expression of TET1 and TET2 mRNA by catalyzing their m6A modifications: TET1 promotes myoblast proliferation and inhibits differentiation, whereas TET2 suppresses proliferation and promotes differentiation and myofiber hypertrophy, thus achieving a fine balance between proliferation and differentiation through the METTL14/m6A/TET1-TET2 axis (validated by GLORI-seq) (Fig. 2B)(Liu et al., 2026). On the other hand, METTL14 targets the miR-133b precursor, facilitating its processing and maturation via m6A modification, thereby regulating the proliferation and differentiation of duck embryonic myoblasts(Jiang et al., 2025). METTL3 promotes the translation of circSIK2 in an m6A-dependent manner, producing the SIK2-176aa peptide: METTL3-mediated m6A modification of circSIK2 enhances its ribosome binding efficiency, and SIK2-176aa significantly promotes chicken myoblast proliferation and differentiation while driving myofiber hypertrophy (Fig. 2C)(Wang et al., 2025a). Furthermore, METTL3 mediates m6A modification within the GHR mRNA, suppressing GHR mRNA and protein expression, and thereby inhibiting downstream mitochondrial biogenesis–related genes (PGC-1α, NRF1, TFAM), which impairs mitochondrial function during myoblast differentiation(Zhao et al., 2025a). METTL3 is the core m6A methyltransferase, and its net effect can change with the target transcript and the specific m6A site. The level of overexpression, the cell type, the duration of the differentiation period, and the assays used to measure myogenesis may also contribute to the divergent results. In chickens, overexpression of METTL16 significantly suppresses the expression of differentiation marker genes (MYOD, MYOG, MYHC), and upregulates slow-twitch myofiber–specific genes (MYH7B, TNNC1, TNNI1, TNNT1), suggesting that METTL16 inhibits differentiation and facilitates slow myofiber formation(Liu et al., 2024). In quail myoblasts, METTL3 overexpression significantly enhances both proliferation and differentiation(Liu et al., 2023b). Regarding demethylases, m6A levels are closely associated with myofiber type composition: oxidative skeletal muscle (anterior latissimus dorsi) exhibits significantly higher m6A levels compared to glycolytic skeletal muscle (pectoralis major), and the demethylase ALKBH5 is more highly expressed in glycolytic skeletal muscle. Overexpression experiments confirmed that ALKBH5 regulates the expression of genes such as MYOG, TPM1, MYH1E, MYH1G, MYH7B, and PDK1, potentially by removing their m6A modifications, thereby participating in the conversion from glycolytic to oxidative myofiber types(Shan et al., 2025). Additionally, the chromatin-associated lncRNA MYH1G-AS has been implicated in skeletal muscle development regulation, with its expression modulated by m6A modification(Cai et al., 2024). These studies show that m6A can act through circular and long non-coding RNAs during avian myogenesis. However, such examples remain few, and most available poultry datasets still describe m6A landscapes on mRNA. Taken together, MeRIP-seq studies show that m6A landscapes differ by developmental stage, muscle type, and breed, while functional work has so far centered on a few enzymes and targets, most often METTL3, METTL14, METTL16, and ALKBH5. The net effect of these enzymes depends on the marked RNA, so a single direction of action should not be assumed.

Role of RNA m6A modification in avian adipose deposition and lipid metabolism

Fat deposition in adipose tissue, skeletal muscle and liver is another major economic trait shaped by RNA m6A. Distinct m6A modification patterns exist across poultry breeds and tissues with differing fat deposition characteristics (Fig. 3A). In the first MeRIP-seq analysis of abdominal adipose tissue from broilers with high versus low fat content, globally elevated m6A methylation levels were observed in the high-fat group, with differentially methylated peaks predominantly enriched in pathways related to fatty acid biosynthesis, fatty acid metabolism, and lipid transport(Cheng et al., 2021). During breast muscle development in Jingyuan chickens, m6A profiling across different ages revealed that as age increased and intramuscular fat (IMF) content rose, DMGs were significantly enriched in fatty acid metabolism and ferroptosis pathways, with LMOD2 identified as a key candidate gene governing differential IMF deposition(Yu et al., 2023). Comparison of m6A modifications between chicken breast and leg muscles showed that leg muscle, which harbors higher IMF content, exhibited DMGs enriched in fatty acid biosynthesis, fatty acid elongation, and peroxisome pathways, with ECH1, BCAT1, and CYP1B1 emerging as key regulatory genes in muscle lipid anabolism(Zhang et al., 2023). Differential m6A landscapes between breast and leg muscles of geese also revealed RNA m6A modifications in genes associated with intramuscular fat deposition(Zhao et al., 2025c). m6A-modifying enzymes orchestrate avian adipogenesis, differentiation, and lipid homeostasis through precise targeting of specific genes and pathways. Among demethylases, FTO exerts its pro-adipogenic effects by demethylating CTNNB1 (β-catenin) mRNA, thereby enhancing its stability and translation efficiency. This activation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway upregulates key adipogenic transcription factors, including PPARG and C/EBPB, ultimately driving chicken preadipocyte proliferation and differentiation (Fig. 3B)(Li et al., 2022b). Under stress conditions, corticosterone-induced visceral fat deposition in chickens involves an m6A-mediated mechanism: reduced m6A modification levels on PPARA mRNA and inhibited PPARA protein levels, thereby promoting excessive abdominal fat accumulation (Fig. 3C)(Zhou et al., 2022). The demethylase ALKBH5 regulates chicken adipogenesis by modulating LCAT mRNA stability in an m6A-dependent manner, thereby inhibiting fat formation of preadipocytes(Chao et al., 2024); additional studies have confirmed that ALKBH5 also governs preadipocyte proliferation and differentiation(Zhang et al., 2021). In the liver, the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) directly binds the FTO promoter and activates its transcription; FTO subsequently removes m6A modifications from lipogenic mRNAs such as SREBP1, FASN, ACACA and SCD, enhancing their stability and promoting hepatic lipid accumulation (Hu et al., 2020).

Among methyltransferases, METTL3 catalyzes m6A modification within PCYT1A mRNA, accelerating its degradation and ultimately inhibiting intramuscular fat deposition in chickens (Yu et al., 2026). In laying hens, dietary betaine supplementation simultaneously regulates DNA 5mC and RNA m6A methylation, modulating the expression of the fatty acid metabolism gene SREBP1 in the liver and thereby reducing hepatic lipid deposition (Yang et al., 2025). In a high-fat diet–induced fatty liver model in laying hens, GR transcriptionally regulates multiple m6A metabolic genes (including FTO, YTHDF2), reshaping the hepatic m6A modification landscape and contributing to the pathogenesis of fatty liver (Feng et al., 2021). MeRIP-seq studies of abdominal fat, intramuscular fat, and liver have linked differential m6A to fatty-acid synthesis, oxidation, and transport. Functional work is more limited and rests mainly on METTL3, FTO, ALKBH5, and YTHDF2. These enzymes can change the stability or translation of lipid-related transcripts, but their effects differ by adipose tissue site and by physiological context.

Role of RNA m6A modification in poultry reproductive development and reproductive performance

Beyond growth and fatness, m6A also acts in the gonad and the follicle. In the reproductive system, RNA m6A has been implicated in sex determination, gonadal differentiation, primordial germ cell (PGC) formation, and ovarian development (Fig. 4). Transcriptome-wide m6A profiling of chicken embryonic gonads at E7 (embryonic day 7) revealed substantial differences in m6A peaks and DMGs between male and female gonads. These DMGs were significantly enriched in pathways related to sex differentiation, tissue morphogenesis, and included core sex-determining genes such as CYP19A1, AMH, SOX9 and FOXL2(Li et al., 2022c). Further investigation demonstrated that the key m6A reader protein YTHDC2 regulates the mRNA expression of SOX9 and HEMGN in an m6A-dependent manner, thereby participating in the directional differentiation of male and female gonads in chicken (Li et al., 2022c). In chicken PGCs, the long non-coding RNA lncBMP4 exerts dual regulatory actions to promote PGC formation and migration: on one hand, lncBMP4 governs the transcription of BMP4 via the expression of the small peptide EPC5, a process that is negatively regulated by m6A modification. On the other hand, elevated m6A levels facilitate the binding between lncBMP4 and gga-miR-12211, thereby relieving the inhibitory effect of gga-miR-12211 on BMP4(Zuo et al., 2022). lncBMP4 is one of the few well-characterized examples of m6A-dependent non-coding RNA regulation in poultry reproduction, underscoring that this area is still at an early stage. Resveratrol treatment upregulates the expression level of the m6A methylation readers (YTHDF1 and YTHDF3) in chicken PGCs, reducing the deformation of the PGCs, thereby revealing a protective role of m6A modification in germline stem cell maintenance(Qiao et al., 2024).

In ovarian development and egg production performance, m6A modifications also display important regulatory functions. Profiling of m6A modifications in ovary during chicken follicular selection demonstrated that m6A peak numbers increase significantly during the follicle selection period, and m6A modification levels are negatively correlated with gene expression, with DMGs primarily enriched in the Wnt signaling pathway, suggesting that m6A participates in hierarchical follicle selection and development(Fan et al., 2019). MeRIP-seq analysis of ovaries from Wuhua Yellow-feathered chickens before and after sexual maturity identified 1,476 differentially methylated m6A peaks, with DMGs mainly enriched in GO biological process like reproduction and reproductive process(Jia et al., 2023). Comparison of ovarian m6A landscapes between high- and low-egg-production Gushi chickens revealed that the high-production group exhibited globally elevated m6A levels, and m6A peaks were negatively correlated with gene expression; DMGs were involved in follicular development and hormone secretion pathways, and the expression levels of m6A regulatory enzymes such as METTL3, METTL14, WTAP, and YTHDF2 differed significantly between the two groups(Zhang et al., 2025). Integrated MeRIP-seq and RNA-seq analysis of preovulatory follicles in Bashang Long-tailed chickens identified a panel of DMGs associated with preovulatory follicle maturation, which were mainly enriched in cell proliferation and apoptosis regulation, and MAPK signaling pathway, indicating that m6A plays a critical role in preovulatory follicle final maturation and egg production performance by modulating the stability of these genes(Duan et al., 2026). The reproductive literature now covers PGC formation, gonadal sex differentiation, follicle selection, and laying performance. Most reports still describe differential m6A peaks and pathway enrichment; functional tests remain few, with lncBMP4 and YTHDC2 among the clearer examples. The same modification also participates in host defense, which is considered next.

Role of RNA m6A modification in avian health, immunity, and disease resistance

RNA m6A modification serves as a critical epitranscriptomic regulatory mechanism in avian immune responses, exerting important functions in both innate and adaptive immunity. In chickens susceptible to Campylobacter jejuni, cecal m6A levels were globally lower and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-8, IL-1β, IL-18, TNF-α, IL-17A) were higher. Differentially methylated peaks were enriched in NF-κB and Toll-like receptor pathways. The m6A reader protein YTHDC2 was found to influence autophagy and apoptosis by regulating IFT74 and SUSD5(Zhao et al., 2025b). In viral infections, Newcastle disease virus (NDV) infection upregulates the expression of m6A-related enzymes (METTL14, FTO, YTHDF1, YTHDF2, YTHDF3) but deregulates METTL3 in chicken HD11 cells(Li et al., 2022a). NDV activates methyltransferases such as METTL3 to reshape the host m6A landscape, thereby promoting viral replication(Yuan et al., 2023). Notably, the host can also counteract viral replication through m6A-mediated mechanisms: YTHDF2 recognizes m6A-modified HSPA2 mRNA and promotes its translation, thereby restricting NDV replication(Wang et al., 2026). This YTHDF2–HSPA2 axis is one of the clearer functional examples of host m6A readers restricting an avian virus. These findings collectively demonstrate that m6A modifications orchestrate immune responses against bacterial and viral invasion in poultry.

Beyond immune regulation, m6A modifications are deeply involved in host–pathogen interactions with multiple avian pathogens. For avian leukosis virus subgroup J (ALV-J), m6A on viral RNA suppresses IFN-β expression through the MDA5 pathway and favors immune evasion. Lowering m6A on the viral RNA restores antiviral capacity(Yu et al., 2025). Thus, m6A on viral RNA can dampen MDA5 sensing and facilitate immune evasion, which is among the more direct host–pathogen mechanisms reported in poultry. In ALV-J-induced tumor livers, transcriptome dynamic changes in m6A modifications have been observed(Zhao et al., 2022), and the transcription of virus-encoded E miRNA is upregulated by m6A(Cao et al., 2024). Highly conserved DRACH motifs within the ALV-J genome have been identified(Ji et al., 2024a), and the coordinated regulation of m6A and miRNAs during ALV-J infection has been revealed(Ji et al., 2024b). For Marek's disease virus (MDV), transcriptome-wide analyses demonstrated that MDV infection significantly alters the m6A patterns of host circular RNAs and mRNAs in an infection stage-dependent manner(Sun et al., 2021; Zhuang et al., 2023). The MDV-encoded protein kinase US3 phosphorylates WTAP, thereby suppressing global m6A modification and protein translation in chicken embryonic fibroblasts(Wang et al., 2025b). Additionally, the circular RNA circRUNX2.2 promotes lymphoma cell proliferation through rolling circle translation(Wang et al., 2024b). In other viral infections, novel duck reovirus infection induces stress granules and m6A-mediated host translational shutoff in the bursa of Fabricius of Muscovy ducklings(Yun et al., 2024). Among these reports, the YTHDF2–HSPA2 pathway in NDV infection and m6A-dependent attenuation of MDA5 signaling during ALV-J infection provide the strongest functional evidence to date. Other studies remain largely descriptive and map changes in host or viral m6A landscapes.

In addition to its direct anti-infective roles, m6A modifications are extensively involved in stress- and nutrition-mediated regulation of poultry health. Quercetin alleviates LPS-induced hepatocyte injury through the METTL3-PTEN-PI3K-AKT pathway(Xia et al., 2025), and walnut green husk polysaccharide alleviates ochratoxin A-induced anorexia in chicks through the m6A/FXR pathway(Zhao et al., 2026). In the context of stress and inflammation, chronic corticosterone exposure induces liver inflammation and fibrosis through m6A-mediated post-transcriptional suppression of heat shock proteins(Feng et al., 2020). In LPS-activated Kupffer cells, m6A modifications mediate post-transcriptional repression of the glucocorticoid receptor, thereby modulating the intensity of inflammation(Zhao et al., 2024). Dietary selenomethionine enhances antioxidant capacity by counteracting METTL3-mediated m6A methylation of Nrf2, thereby alleviating hepatic necroptosis in laying hens(Li et al., 2024). Berberine attenuates LPS-induced intestinal injury by reducing the expression level of METTL3 and modulating gut m6A modifications(Yuan et al., 2025). Immunological stress compromises overall broiler productivity by disrupting RNA m6A modification and altering the expression of hypothalamic genes that regulate appetite(Huang et al., 2025). These findings demonstrate that the influence of m6A modifications extends beyond infection itself, permeating multiple dimensions of poultry health regulation including nutrition metabolism, oxidative stress, and inflammation.

Conclusion

This review summarizes the research progress on RNA m6A modification in poultry, covering skeletal muscle development, reproductive traits, adipose deposition, and immunity and disease resistance. Complementary to a recently published overview of this field(Yang et al., 2026), the present article emphasizes methodological options for poultry studies and brings together enzyme-level mechanisms across several economic traits and avian species. Writers (METTL3, METTL14, METTL16), erasers (FTO, ALKBH5), and readers (YTHDF and YTHDC family proteins) are the main proteins implicated in these processes. By altering mRNA splicing, stability, and translation, and in a smaller number of cases the fate of non-coding RNAs, m6A contributes to growth, reproductive performance, meat quality, and disease resistance. It therefore represents an additional regulatory layer between genotype and phenotype in poultry.

Future perspectives

Despite this progress, several limitations remain. In a few settings, such as early embryos or certain environmental stresses, m6A changes have been reported to persist briefly across generations. In most cases, however, the mark is dynamic and is not stably inherited, and it acts within a given cell or tissue. For this reason, m6A sites cannot yet be used as conventional genetic markers in poultry breeding. Public databases such as RMBase v3.0 and m6A-Atlas v2.0 compile m6A sites from many species, but poultry genomes are still poorly represented, and there is no dedicated poultry database that links modification maps, tissue dynamics, and phenotypes. Comparisons among studies are also hindered by differences in method, including MeRIP-seq, GLORI-seq, and Nanopore direct RNA sequencing. Most published work still concerns chicken rather than duck, goose, pigeon, or quail, and most targets are mRNAs rather than lncRNAs, circRNAs, or miRNAs.

Despite these challenges, broader and more comparable maps, including single-base methods where feasible, will make it easier to test how m6A contributes to specific traits. It has been reported that mutations at the adenine residue within RRACH motifs can affect RNA m6A modification and consequently alter RNA secondary structure, ultimately influencing phenotypic traits(Xin et al., 2025). Variants in writer, eraser, or reader genes, and cis-variants that create or destroy RRACH sites, therefore deserve systematic screening in poultry populations. Gut microbiota–derived betaine has been reported to alter m6A and Myh7 expression during muscle fiber-type transition(Yan et al., 2025), which suggests that diet or microbial composition can be used to shift m6A in the near term. Integration of genomic and m6A data, together with site-resolved editing, may later yield testable targets. Even so, because m6A is readily changed by environment and nutrition, it is better viewed at present as a short-term regulatory layer than as a stable selection marker. Nutritional or management interventions are likely to be more tractable than direct use of m6A sites in genomic selection. These advances nonetheless position m6A as a promising, though still emerging, layer of regulation for future poultry breeding and management strategies.

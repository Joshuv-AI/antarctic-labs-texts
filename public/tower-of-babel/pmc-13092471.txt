A multiparameter model (OsteoSC-M3) for early detection and risk stratification of osteoporosis in women: a multicentre cohort study in China
Authors: Xiaoyan Teng, Ji Qiao, Yang Xue, Li Wei, Xiaozhong Ji, Yuzhen Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundOsteoporosis is a major global health issue, but current early screening tools lack accuracy, and the gold-standard diagnostic method, dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), is not widely accessible. This limits large-scale early screening efforts, as evidenced by China's low screening rate of 3.7% among adults aged over 50. To address these limitations, we developed OsteoSC-M3, a new risk assessment model for women using a stacked ensemble machine learning algorithm, designed to offer a precise and accessible tool for early risk identification.MethodsA model was developed based on a cohort of 5745 females (aged 50–90 years) from Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital (2013–2023); sex was self-reported. Participants were randomly partitioned into a Training Set (70%, n = 4022) and an Internal Validation Set (30%, n = 1723). First, missing values in the Training Set were imputed using the missForest method, and key predictive variables were selected through VIF, LASSO, and multivariable logistic regression. Nine base models and an ensemble model with XGBoost as the meta-learner were then constructed using the Training Set. The model was further validated on the Internal Validation Set and two independent multicentre external cohorts (Shanghai 2024 cohort, n = 1839; Jiangsu 2025 cohort, n = 305). Additionally, its prognostic value was assessed through a prospective study, which enrolled 405 female participants with low bone mass (T-score between −1.0 and −2.5) from the Health Examination Centre of Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital, with a follow-up period exceeding five years, where outcomes were evaluated using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and Cox regression. This study is registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (part of the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform), number ChiCTR2500097213.FindingsEleven key variables, including age, height, weight, and eight serum biomarkers, were identified. All nine base models performed well without overfitting. The OsteoSC-M3 model achieved excellent diagnostic performance with a Training Set AUC of 0.973 (95% CI 0.969–0.976). It maintained excellent performance in the Internal Validation Set (AUC 0.943, 95% CI 0.933–0.954) and two external cohorts (Cohort 1: AUC 0.958, 95% CI 0.949–0.966; Cohort 2: AUC 0.917, 95% CI 0.897–0.928), demonstrating strong generalisability. Calibration curves showed good agreement, and decision curve analysis confirmed clinical utility. Prospectively, the model agreed well with follow-up results (Kappa = 0.82) and identified high-risk individuals 29 ± 9.31 months earlier (HR = 7.63, P < 0.001). It significantly outperformed the Osteoporosis Self-Assessment Tool for Asians (OSTA) across all cohorts. The model is accessible via an online platform (http://47.101.190.127:3838/scyz/), providing a valuable tool for precision screening and early intervention in osteoporosis.InterpretationThe OsteoSC-M3 model integrates demographic features with eight simple laboratory parameters to enable early, non-invasive prediction of osteoporosis risk in women, providing a 29-month lead time before clinical diagnosis. Superior to OSTA in risk stratification, it effectively identifies high-risk individuals requiring DXA confirmation and demonstrates strong utility in resource-limited settings without DXA equipment. This innovative screening approach creates a critical window for early intervention that could significantly improve participants' quality of life while reducing the economic burden on healthcare systems. Future research should focus on validating the model in more diverse ethnic populations and assessing its long-term impact on clinical outcomes through large-scale prospective trials.FundingThis study was funded by the Shanghai Pudong New Area Health Commission Joint Research Project, the Hospital Contractual Research Project, the Pudong New Area Science and Technology Development Fund, the Clinical Research Programme of Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital, and the Hospital's Clinical Backbone Team Cultivation Programme.Keywords: Osteoporosis, Risk prediction, Machine learning, Stacked ensemble model, Early screening

Research in context

Evidence before this study

We conducted an extensive literature review of PubMed employing keywords such as “osteoporosis”, “early warning”, “multiparameter data”, and “stacked ensemble model” to identify relevant studies published up to 20 March 2026, without language restrictions. The identified evidence was generally of moderate quality, with many studies limited by small sample sizes or a lack of external validation. Despite the comprehensive nature of this search, robust, clinically applicable findings were limited. Although existing research has explored the early diagnosis of osteoporosis, there remains a significant gap in the systematic integration of multidimensional serological models. This gap is especially pronounced in the development of specialised multiparameter stacked ensemble models tailored specifically for osteoporosis in women.

Added value of this study

This study identified 11 critical variables, including age, height, weight, and eight serum biomarkers associated with bone metabolism, blood lipids, and liver/kidney function. The OsteoSC-M3 model was developed and internally validated using a dataset comprising 5745 female participants from the National Centre for Orthopaedics at Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital, spanning the years 2013–2023. The model utilises a stacked ensemble framework, integrating nine base machine learning models with XGBoost serving as the meta-learner. It exhibited excellent diagnostic performance in both the Training and Internal Validation Sets, achieving area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.973 and 0.943, respectively. Decision curve analysis further confirmed its superior clinical utility compared to single-algorithm models. External validation conducted across two cohorts corroborated its exceptional diagnostic performance, with AUC values of 0.958 for Cohort 1 and 0.917 for Cohort 2. Prospective validation demonstrated strong concordance with follow-up results (Kappa = 0.82) and highlighted the model's capability to identify high-risk individuals for osteoporosis approximately 29 months before clinical diagnosis via traditional dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), with significant predictive power (HR = 7.63, P < 0.001). The model significantly outperformed the Osteoporosis Self-Assessment Tool for Asians (OSTA) across all cohorts.

Implications of all the available evidence

The OsteoSC-M3 model developed in this study integrates demographic characteristics with eight straightforward laboratory parameters to facilitate early, non-invasive risk prediction of osteoporosis in women. It exhibits high diagnostic sensitivity and offers a clinical lead time of approximately 29 months. Surpassing OSTA in risk stratification, the model effectively identifies high-risk individuals who require DXA confirmation and is particularly suitable for settings with limited DXA resources. Additionally, we have launched a complementary online computing platform (http://47.101.190.127:3838/scyz/), providing an innovative tool for early screening and intervention that aims to enhance participants' quality of life and reduce the burden on healthcare systems. Future research should focus on validating the model across diverse ethnic populations and assessing its long-term clinical impact through multicentre prospective trials.

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a systemic metabolic bone disorder characterised by reduced bone mass and the deterioration of bone microarchitecture. The fundamental pathological mechanism of osteoporosis involves an imbalance in bone remodelling, wherein osteoclast-mediated bone resorption surpasses osteoblast-mediated bone formation.1,2 Globally, the prevalence of osteoporosis is estimated at 18.3%, with primary osteoporosis constituting over 90% of cases, predominantly encompassing postmenopausal and age-related osteoporosis. The incidence in women is two to three times higher than in men.3 In China, the disease burden among older adults is particularly pronounced, with a prevalence of 32.1% in women aged 50 and above, escalating to 51.6% in those over 65 years. The total number of women with osteoporosis is approximately 70 million, representing 77.8% of the national total of 90 million cases.3,4 Osteoporosis can result in severe complications, such as osteoporotic fractures, which significantly impair quality of life and elevate the risk of disability and mortality among the elderly.5, 6, 7 Due to its long asymptomatic period and the lack of effective early screening tools, early diagnosis remains insufficient, resulting in high disability rates and substantial healthcare burdens. Therefore, developing convenient and reliable early screening methods for early detection and intervention has become a critical measure in addressing this public health challenge, with particular importance for safeguarding women's bone health.

The current global diagnostic criteria for osteoporosis rely on DXA-measured bone mineral density or a history of fragility fractures.4,8,9 However, DXA presents significant limitations in early diagnosis: its two-dimensional projection cannot assess bone microstructure, exhibits low sensitivity to early metabolic changes, and faces accessibility barriers due to high costs and limited deployment (0.35 devices per million people in Asia; 3.7% testing rate among Chinese individuals aged ≥50).10, 11, 12, 13 Alternative methods include quantitative ultrasound with limited diagnostic efficacy,14 conventional CT with radiation concerns,15 and opportunistic CT with limited standardisation.16 Clinical risk assessment tools (e.g., ORAI, OSIRIS, SCORE) show pooled AUCs of 0.65–0.70, while FRAX, a fracture risk assessment tool, yields AUCs of 0.58–0.82. The IOF One–Minute Test and the Osteoporosis Self-Assessment Tool for Asians (OSTA) exhibit limited discriminative ability in Chinese populations, with AUCs of 0.62917 and 0.798,18 respectively. These shortcomings in early diagnosis, radiation safety, and screening efficiency highlight the pressing need for the development of innovative screening methods.

Osteoporosis is a dynamic pathological process primarily driven by an imbalance between bone resorption and bone formation.19 In clinical practice, circulating bone turnover markers and bone regulatory markers are commonly used to assess bone metabolism; studies have also reported that hepatic and renal function are closely associated with osteoporosis.20, 21, 22 Crucially, changes in these biomarkers precede radiologically detectable declines in bone mineral density, making it possible to assess early metabolic status before significant bone loss occurs.23 Relevant guidelines recommend incorporating routine blood tests—such as assessments of liver function, kidney function, blood lipids, and bone turnover markers—into the auxiliary diagnostic process,4,8,9 and affirm their importance in evaluating bone metabolism and predicting fracture risk, among other applications.23 However, due to significant overlap and variability in these biomarkers between healthy and osteoporotic populations, the diagnostic accuracy of individual markers is limited, necessitating multi-marker combined analysis to enhance diagnostic performance.24, 25, 26

Machine learning algorithms, excelling in processing high-dimensional data while reducing overfitting and enhancing interpretability, have become vital for complex disease diagnosis.27,28 However, existing osteoporosis ML models based on serum biomarkers have notable limitations, including single-dimensional inputs, lack of sex-specific stratification, reliance on a single algorithm, and insufficient validation.29, 30, 31 Therefore, this study aimed to integrate multidimensional serological indicators and develop a stacked ensemble model for the early screening of osteoporosis in women. The model was validated through internal validation, multicentre external validation, and prospective cohort assessment, and was subsequently deployed as an open-access online platform to enable efficient risk stratification.

Methods

Study design and participant inclusion and exclusion criteria

This study employed a multistage, multicentre observational cohort design (Fig. 1) to develop and validate a stacked multiparameter machine learning model for osteoporosis risk assessment. The analytical framework comprised five stages: model development, internal validation, external validation, prospective validation, and model interpretation with performance evaluation.

This multicentre study was conducted at Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital in Shanghai and at Hai'an People's Hospital in Jiangsu Province, China. Participant sex was self-reported and extracted from electronic health records. Uniform inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied across all cohorts as follows: Inclusion criteria: (a) Han Chinese females; (b) aged 50–90 years; (c) underwent DXA bone mineral density examination or had a history of fragility fracture. Exclusion criteria: (a) menopausal status unknown; (b) long-term bedridden participants; (c) participants with severe cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic, renal, or haematological system diseases; (d) participants with endocrine, immune, or systemic metabolic diseases (e.g., type 1 diabetes, parathyroid disorders, osteogenesis imperfecta); (e) history of medication use including glucocorticoids, thyroid supplements, antidepressants, or anti-osteoporotic drugs; (f) comorbid malignancies, bone tumours, or history of bone metastases; (g) previous organ transplantation or history of lumbar spine/bilateral hip joint surgery.

Cohort construction

The model derivation cohort retrospectively enrolled females admitted to Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital between January 2013 and December 2023. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 5745 females were ultimately included from an initial pool of 32,066 individuals (Supplementary Fig. S1A). According to World Health Organization criteria and clinical guidelines,4,8 participants were categorised into an osteoporosis group (T-score ≤ −2.5 by DXA or fragility fracture) and a non-osteoporosis group (T-score > −2.5 without fragility fracture). Fragility fractures were defined as low-energy trauma-induced (e.g., falls from standing height), excluding high-energy and pathological fractures.8 Fracture history was confirmed via electronic medical records and imaging reports, avoiding reliance on participant history alone. Vertebral fractures underwent DXA-based VFA per ISCD guidelines (T-score ≤ −1.0 or age ≥70 years32), with radiographic confirmation (Genant method: vertebral height reduction ≥20%).33,34 This resulted in 2167 females (37.7%) in the osteoporosis group and 3578 females (62.3%) in the non-osteoporosis group (Table 1).

To assess generalisability, two independent multicentre external validation cohorts were established. External Validation Cohort 1 consisted of 1839 females from Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital (January–December 2024), with 626 (34.04%) diagnosed with osteoporosis (Supplementary Figs. S1B and S2). External Validation Cohort 2 included 305 females from Hai'an People's Hospital in Jiangsu Province, enrolled between January and June 2025, with 133 (43.61%) diagnosed with osteoporosis (Supplementary Fig. S2). The baseline characteristics of both external validation cohorts were comparable to those of the derivation cohort, as presented in Supplementary Table S10.

To assess the long-term predictive performance of the model, a Prospective Validation Cohort was established. This cohort, consisting of 405 female participants, was recruited from the Health Examination Centre of Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital between January 2015 and December 2019, which adhered to the inclusion and exclusion criteria detailed in Section 2.1. All participants presented with baseline osteopenia, characterised by a T-score ranging from −2.5 to −1.0, and none had osteoporosis at enrolment. They were prospectively followed until 31 December 2024, with a median follow-up period of at least five years. According to the Chinese osteoporosis and ISCD guidelines,4,35 participants with low bone mass (T-score between −1.0 and −2.5) underwent DXA monitoring every 6–12 months to assess progression to osteoporosis. All participants underwent annual thoracolumbar radiography, with new fragility fractures assessed using electronic medical records, imaging reports, and radiographic findings. The baseline characteristics of this cohort are detailed in Supplementary Table S10.

DXA examination

All dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) examinations in this study were conducted at the two collaborating institutions: Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital and Hai'an People's Hospital in Jiangsu Province. Measurements of bone mineral density at the lumbar spine (L1–L4), total hip, and femoral neck were performed by certified hospital technicians using Hologic Discovery Wi scanners (USA). Data were calculated using the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES III) reference database, with the final diagnosis based on the lowest T-score among the three measurement sites.8,9 Daily instrument calibration and quality control scans were routinely implemented to ensure data quality.

Data collection and data cleaning

In this study, we retrospectively collected demographic data and blood test results from female participants at their initial visit, synchronised with bone mineral density (BMD) assessments, encompassing a total of 35 clinically relevant parameters. The complete nomenclature is provided in Supplementary Table S8. All laboratory data complied with ISO 15189 certification standards. Through a two-step data cleaning process, we first excluded five variables with a missingness rate exceeding 20% (β-CTX, CT, FT3, FT4, and TSH), followed by the removal of females with six or more missing parameters. After rigorous screening, we established a final dataset containing 5745 females and 30 parameters for subsequent imputation and analysis. This included four clinical features (age, height, weight, and BMI) and 26 blood biomarkers: 25-hydroxyvitamin D (VITD; ng/mL), total procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (tP1NP; ng/mL), osteocalcin (OC; ng/mL), total cholesterol (CHOL; mmol/L), triglycerides (TG; mmol/L), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDLC; mmol/L), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDLC; mmol/L), potassium (K; mmol/L), sodium (Na; mmol/L), chloride (Cl; mmol/L), calcium (Ca; mmol/L), phosphorus (P; mmol/L), magnesium (Mg; mmol/L), total bilirubin (TBIL; μmol/L), direct bilirubin (DBIL; μmol/L), alkaline phosphatase (ALP; U/L), albumin (ALB; g/L), total protein (TP; g/L), alanine aminotransferase (ALT; U/L), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT; U/L), aspartate aminotransferase (AST; U/L), cholinesterase (CHE; kU/L), total bile acids (TBA; μmol/L), prealbumin (PA; mg/L), uric acid (UA; μmol/L), and creatinine (CREA; μmol/L).

Post hoc assessment of missing data mechanism

To assess whether the missing data leading to sample exclusions can be considered missing at random (MAR), we conducted a missing indicator analysis. We constructed a post hoc multivariable logistic regression model with “exclusion due to six or more missing parameters” as the outcome and age, BMI, admission year, and osteoporosis diagnosis as covariates. The associations between missingness and observable variables were evaluated using odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) (Supplementary Table S11).

Laboratory testing

According to standard hospital laboratory protocols for routine clinical testing, serum samples were prepared at room temperature by centrifugation at 3500×g for 10 min. Samples were stored at 4 °C and analysed within 4 h; aliquots were preserved at −80 °C for long-term storage. A Beckman AU5800 analyser (Beckman Coulter, USA) measured six electrolytes (K, Na, Cl, Ca, P, Mg), four blood lipids (CHOL, TG, HDLC, LDLC), eleven hepatic markers (TBIL, DBIL, ALP, ALB, TP, ALT, GGT, AST, CHE, TBA, PA), UA, and CREA.

Serum tP1NP, OC, and VITD were quantified at Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital using a Roche Cobas E601 electrochemiluminescence system (Roche Diagnostics, Switzerland; reagent lots: 03141071190, 11972111122, 05894921190). Intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation (CVs) were ≤3.0%/≤ 3.3%, ≤1.7%/≤ 3.3%, and ≤6.8%/≤ 8.5%, respectively. In Hai'an People's Hospital (External Validation Cohort 2), these biomarkers were measured using a Snibe MAGLUMI X8 chemiluminescence analyser (Shenzhen New Industries Biomedical Engineering, China; reagent lots: 153240211, 088240121, 262250111), with intra-assay and inter-assay CVs ≤2.15%/≤ 2.50%, ≤1.61%/≤ 2.03%, and ≤1.65%/≤ 1.83%. All procedures followed manufacturer protocols.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses and data visualisations were performed using R (version 4.2.3), SPSS (version 22.0), MedCalc (version 20.2.1), and GraphPad Prism (version 10.4.0). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD (normally distributed) or median (IQR; non-normally distributed) to describe central tendency and dispersion; categorical variables are expressed as frequencies (%). Normality was assessed via the Shapiro–Wilk test (α = 0.05) with histogram/Q–Q plot verification to guide the selection of parametric or non-parametric tests. To compare continuous variables between cohorts, independent-samples t-tests were used for normally distributed variables with equal variances (Levene's tests); whilst Welch's t-tests were applied for unequal variances. Non-normally distributed variables were analysed using Mann–Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were compared via chi-squared or Fisher's exact tests based on expected cell counts; Kruskal–Wallis tests were utilised for comparisons involving more than two groups. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Post hoc pairwise comparisons of the area under the curve (AUC) between models were performed using paired DeLong tests to determine statistical differences in diagnostic efficacy, with Bonferroni correction applied for multiple comparisons (α = 0.05; pROC package in R). Kaplan–Meier curves stratified by osteoporosis status depicted outcome variations, with hazard ratios (HRs; 95% CI) calculated using Cox proportional hazards regression models. All tests were two-tailed; P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Sample size estimation

Sample size was determined based on the events per variable (EPV = 10) criterion, requiring a minimum of 110 osteoporosis events for the 11 variables under consideration. During the variable screening phase, a total of 4022 females from the Training Set, comprising 1526 osteoporosis (OP) cases and 2496 non-OP cases, were used for the preliminary assessment of 30 candidate variables. The Internal Validation Set included 1723 females, with 641 OP and 1082 non-OP cases. The validation phase involved three cohorts: External Validation Cohort 1, consisting of 1839 females (626 OP and 1213 non-OP); External Validation Cohort 2, comprising 305 females (133 OP and 172 non-OP); and a Prospective Validation Cohort of 405 females (154 incident OP and 251 non-OP), as illustrated in Supplementary Fig. S2. All cohorts met the minimum statistical requirement of EPV ≥ 10, ensuring the robustness and generalisability of the model.

Data partitioning and quality control

To mitigate the risks of data leakage and model overfitting, the model derivation cohort including 5745 females was randomly partitioned into a Training Set (n = 4022) and an Internal Validation Set (n = 1723) at a ratio of 7:3. All data preprocessing parameters were determined exclusively on the Training Set and subsequently applied to both the internal and external validation cohorts to ensure unbiased model evaluation.

The preprocessing pipeline consisted of three steps: As a post hoc sensitivity analysis, for samples with fewer than five missing parameters among the 30 retained variables, five imputation strategies (no imputation, MICE, KNN, median, random forest) were compared to determine optimal preprocessing (Supplementary Table S13). Next, potential outliers in continuous variables were handled using median absolute deviation (MAD)-based winsorisation, with thresholds set at the median ±3 × MAD. Notably, a medical record review confirmed that specific outliers (n = 27) represented females with genuinely elevated tP1NP and OC levels, consistent with a diagnosis of accelerated bone turnover; therefore, these physiological values were exempted from winsorisation and retained. Finally, the impact of missing value imputation combined with four data transformation or scaling strategies was systematically evaluated: no transformation, log 10 transformation, standard (Z-score) scaling, and robust scaling.

All analyses were performed in R 4.2.3. Performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and its 95% confidence interval on the Internal Validation Set.

Variable selection

Following missing value imputation, a multistage variable selection strategy was implemented on the complete dataset of 4022 Training Set females: first, variables with VIF>5 were excluded; subsequently, LASSO regression was employed, where the penalty coefficient λ was optimised via 10-fold cross-validation; finally, the selected variables were incorporated into a multivariable stepwise logistic regression model with entry and removal criteria set at α = 0.05 and α = 0.1, respectively. Variable contributions were assessed using likelihood ratio tests, and adjusted odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals were calculated. Only independent predictors with P < 0.05 were retained in the final model. This strategy effectively reduced the risk of overfitting while ensuring the inclusion of clinically relevant and statistically robust variables.

Construction of the OsteoSC-M3 stacked multiparameter machine learning model

Model Framework: This study developed a two-level stacked ensemble framework to construct the Osteoporosis Serological-Clinical Multiparameter Intelligent Diagnostic Model (OsteoSC-M3), utilising Python 3.8 and R 4.2.3 with the tidymodels package. The base module incorporated nine parallel base classifiers (Supplementary Table S5, Supplementary Methods S1): Random Forest (RF; random_forest (engine = "randomForest")), XGBoost (boost_tree (engine = "XGBoost")), Support Vector Machine (SVM; svm_rbf (engine = "kernlab")), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP; mlp (engine = "nnet")), Decision Tree (DT; decision_tree (engine = "rpart")), Logistic Regression (LR; logistic_reg (engine = "glm")), and three regularised models—LASSO (logistic_reg (mixture = 1, engine = "glmnet")), Ridge (logistic_reg (mixture = 0, engine = "glmnet")), and Elastic Net (ENet; logistic_reg (mixture = 0.5, engine = "glmnet")). Following independent predictions of the 11 key variables and the generation of output probabilities by each base classifier, the meta-module employed XGBoost as the meta-classifier. This meta-classifier was retrained using these probabilities as input features to produce the final osteoporosis (OP) prediction probability. The stacked architecture effectively amalgamates the strengths of diverse base classifiers, thereby enhancing predictive performance.

For the stacked ensemble framework, the nine base classifiers were trained on the uniformly partitioned Training Set (70%) with hyperparameter optimisation via five-fold cross-validation and early stopping. The XGBoost meta-learner was subsequently trained using the out-of-fold (OOF) prediction probabilities generated by the base classifiers on the Training Set during the cross-validation process. To ensure comparability, model performance was comprehensively evaluated on the Internal Validation Set and two external cohorts using multiple metrics, including the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, negative/positive predictive values (NPV/PPV), F1 score, and Cohen's Kappa coefficient. Feature evaluation involved ranking variables by selection frequency across all models, with SHAP analysis generating swarm plots and case-specific interpretations to quantify feature importance and enhance decision-making interpretability. For model application, risk stratification using Youden's index categorised female participants into low- and high-risk osteoporosis groups, while Kaplan–Meier curves assessed prognostic associations with hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Following multicentre external validation and prospective cohort assessment, the finalised model was deployed as an online platform that enables real-time osteoporosis risk evaluation through 11 key indicators, supporting early screening and risk stratification.

Ethics statement

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the leading institution, the Sixth People's Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (Approval No.: 2024-KY-277(K)), and this central approval was formally recognised by the Ethics Committee of Hai'an People's Hospital. The study was also registered with the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ChiCTR2500097213).

This study included both retrospective multicentre cohorts and a prospective cohort, both utilising residual blood samples remaining after routine clinical tests. For the retrospective cohorts, data were retrospectively collected from electronic medical records by each participating centre, and the requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committees. For the prospective cohort, data were collected prospectively during clinical recruitment and follow-up, and all female participants in this cohort provided written informed consent.

All data from the participating centres were anonymised locally prior to being aggregated and securely stored within the data management platform of Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital. This study adhered to the TRIPOD statement and STROBE guidelines.

Results

Study design and participants

This study successfully established multiple independent cohorts for model development and validation (Fig. 2): (1) the Model Development and Internal Validation cohort (2013–2023) included 5745 females, randomly divided in a 7:3 ratio into a Training Set (n = 4022) and an Internal Validation Set (n = 1723); (2) multicentre external validation cohorts: External Validation Cohort 1 from Shanghai in 2024 (n = 1839) and External Validation Cohort 2 from Hai'an in 2025 (n = 305); and (3) a Shanghai Prospective cohort (2015–2019, n = 405), which enrolled female participants with baseline osteopenia but without osteoporosis and followed them for at least five years. A five-stage framework was employed as follows: a stacked machine learning model was developed using the Training Set; preliminary validation was performed internally; external validation was conducted across two centres; long-term predictive performance was evaluated using the prospective cohort; an optimal threshold was determined based on Youden's index to generate the computer-based model OsteoSC-M3 for stratifying high- and low-risk populations; finally, the performance of OsteoSC-M3 and the Osteoporosis Self-Assessment Tool for Asians (OSTA) was compared using clinically confirmed osteoporosis as the reference standard, and the SHAP algorithm was applied to interpret feature importance and non-linear interactions.

Baseline analysis of the derivation cohort

In this baseline analysis, we examined 5745 females from the model derivation cohort, consisting of 2167 females diagnosed with osteoporosis (OP) and 3578 non-osteoporosis (Non-OP) controls. Appropriate statistical tests were conducted to compare baseline characteristics at initial admission, including demographic variables (age, height, weight) and 26 serum biomarkers (Table 1). The statistical analysis indicated that the OP group was significantly older and had higher levels of tP1NP, OC, HDLC, TBIL, DBIL, ALP, TBA, and Mg in comparison to the Non-OP group (all P < 0.05). In contrast, the OP group exhibited significantly lower levels of BMI, VITD, CHOL, TG, LDLC, ALT, GGT, CHE, PA, UA, and CREA (all P < 0.05). These results imply that the differentially distributed serum biomarkers are closely associated with the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying osteoporosis.

Data partitioning and variable screening

To mitigate the risk of data leakage and model overfitting, the model derivation cohort comprising 5745 females was randomly partitioned into a Training Set (70%, n = 4022) and an Internal Validation Set (30%, n = 1723) at a 7:3 ratio. Analysis of missing data for the 30 parameters in the Training Set revealed an overall data completeness of 93% with a missingness rate of 7% (Supplementary Fig. S3), indicating acceptable data quality for subsequent analysis. Random forest imputation without transformation yielded the optimal performance for females with fewer than five missing parameters (Supplementary Fig. S4, Supplementary Table S13).

Following data preprocessing, a multistage variable selection strategy was implemented on the complete Training Set of 4022 females. First, multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis (Supplementary Tables S1–S2), leading to the exclusion of body mass index (BMI) due to its high collinearity with height and weight. Subsequently, redundant variables were eliminated via LASSO regression (Fig. 3A). This comprehensive screening procedure identified 11 key predictive variables from the initial 30 features, including three demographic characteristics (age, height, weight) and eight serum biomarkers (VITD, tP1NP, OC, CHOL, TBIL, DBIL, ALP, UA) (Supplementary Table S3). Multivariable logistic regression confirmed that all 11 variables were significantly associated with osteoporosis, with five identified as independent risk factors (age, tP1NP, OC, TBIL, ALP) and six as independent protective factors (height, weight, VITD, DBIL, CHOL, UA) (Fig. 3B). Area under the curve (AUC) analysis demonstrated limited diagnostic efficacy of individual variables for osteoporosis, with AUC values ranging from 0.563 to 0.703 (Supplementary Table S4).

Base module construction and performance evaluation

During model training, osteoporosis cases were designated as the positive class, while non-osteoporosis controls constituted the negative class. Based on the 11 key variables identified through variable selection, we developed base models using nine machine learning algorithms—including RF, XGBoost, SVM, MLP, DT, Logistic Regression, Ridge, ENet, and LASSO (Supplementary Table S5). To ensure comparability of model performance, all models were constructed and optimised using the same 4022 training females from the model derivation cohort, and internally validated with 1723 internal validation females. The missing data pattern in the Internal Validation Set is shown in Supplementary Fig. S3, and missing values were imputed using the pre-trained missForest model derived from the Training Set (Supplementary Fig. S4). As illustrated in Fig. 4, all nine base models demonstrated strong performance, with AUC values ranging from 0.84 to 0.95 on the Training Set (Fig. 4A, Supplementary Table S6) and from 0.83 to 0.91 on the Internal Validation Set (Fig. 4C, Supplementary Table S7). Further comparison revealed minimal differences (0.003–0.04) in AUC between the training and validation sets for each model, indicating stable performance without significant fluctuations or overfitting (Fig. 4B and D; Supplementary Fig. S5). Nevertheless, the absolute AUC values of individual models still left room for improvement, suggesting a direction for subsequent optimisation.

Construction and internal validation performance of the stacked ensemble model OsteoSC-M3

Variable importance analysis revealed notable dependencies on predictive variables across nine distinct algorithms (Supplementary Fig. S6), justifying the ensemble learning strategy. We implemented a stacking approach where base models generated independent probability predictions based on the 11 key variables, which were then used as features to develop an XGBoost meta-model. Using 5745 females split 7:3 for training and validation, this two-tiered architecture effectively integrated complementary strengths of diverse algorithms, significantly enhancing generalisation capability and predictive accuracy.

As shown in Fig. 5, the XGBoost-based meta-module, OsteoSC-M3, demonstrated robust and stable performance in both the Training Set (AUC 0.973, 95% CI 0.969–0.976; sensitivity 91.1%; specificity 92.5%) and the Internal Validation Set (AUC 0.943, 95% CI 0.933–0.954; sensitivity 86.6%; specificity 89.1%) (Fig. 5A, Table 2). The PR curve further indicated that OsteoSC-M3 maintained stable performance in both the Training Set (AUPRC 0.956) and the Internal Validation Set (AUPRC 0.908) (Fig. 5B). In addition, decision curve analysis (DCA) showed superior clinical utility, as reflected by the stable and consistent areas under the DCA curve across both cohorts (Fig. 5C and D). In summary, the OsteoSC-M3 intelligent early-warning model for osteoporosis exhibits strong and consistent performance across both the Training and Internal Validation Sets, suggesting its potential as a reliable tool for osteoporosis risk stratification.

Performance evaluation of OsteoSC-M3 in multicentre external validation

To evaluate the robustness and generalisability of OsteoSC-M3 across varied clinical environments, we conducted external validation at two independent medical centres located in distinct geographical locations: the National Centre for Orthopaedics at Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital and Hai'an People's Hospital in Jiangsu. Validation Cohort 1 consisted of 1839 females from the National Centre for Orthopaedics at Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital (2024; 626 cases of osteoporosis and 1213 non-osteoporosis controls). In contrast, Cohort 2 comprised 305 females from Hai'an People's Hospital, with 133 cases of osteoporosis and 172 controls. The missing data status of the 11 core variables comprising the OsteoSC-M3 model within External Validation Cohort 1 (n = 1839) is presented in Supplementary Fig. S3, with missing values imputed using the pre-trained missForest model derived from the Training Set. Meanwhile, these 11 core variables demonstrated 100% data completeness in External Validation Cohort 2. Detailed baseline characteristics are provided in Supplementary Table S10. In Cohort 1 (Fig. 6A and Table 3), OsteoSC-M3 exhibited excellent performance, with an AUC of 0.958 (95% CI 0.949–0.966), sensitivity of 88.7%, and specificity of 86.4%. Although slightly lower performance metrics were observed in Cohort 2 (Fig. 6B and Table 3)—with an AUC of 0.917 (95% CI 0.897–0.928), sensitivity of 84.5%, and specificity of 86.6%—all metrics exceeded 85%, thereby confirming the robust cross-centre stability of OsteoSC-M3.

Prognostic value analysis and online computing platform for OsteoSC-M3

To evaluate the clinical utility of OsteoSC-M3 for early diagnosis and progression monitoring of osteoporosis, we conducted a prospective cohort study of 405 participants with confirmed low bone mass (T-score between −2.5 and −1.0) who did not initially have osteoporosis. The follow-up period spanned a minimum of five years, with the primary endpoint being the development of osteoporosis. The mean interval between DXA tests was 12.39 ± 3.11 months. Baseline data are available in Supplementary Table S10. During the follow-up, 154 females (38.02%) developed osteoporosis, with 12 new fragility fractures identified (8 vertebral and 4 hip fractures). The 11 core variables maintained 100% data completeness in the Prospective Validation Cohort. The OsteoSC-M3 model generated predicted outcomes using the participants' initial diagnostic data, and a comparative analysis of these predictions against actual outcomes is depicted in the confusion matrix (Fig. 8A). The model's risk stratification, utilising an optimal cutoff threshold of 0.40, effectively categorised females into high-risk (≥0.40) and low-risk (<0.40) groups, demonstrating substantial agreement with actual diagnoses (Kappa = 0.82) (Table 4). Furthermore, there was strong calibration between observed outcomes and the OsteoSC-M3 scores (Fig. 8B).

Performance comparison between OsteoSC-M3 and OSTA, and subgroup analysis

This study systematically compared the newly developed OsteoSC-M3 model with the widely used Osteoporosis Self-Assessment Tool for Asians (OSTA). While OSTA calculates risk based solely on age and weight, OsteoSC-M3 integrates height and eight routine serological biomarkers related to osteoporosis pathogenesis (in addition to age and weight). Performance comparison across multiple independent cohorts (Supplementary Fig. S9) demonstrated consistent superiority of OsteoSC-M3 in terms of area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC): Training Set (0.973 vs 0.720), Internal Validation Set (0.943 vs 0.739), External Validation Cohort 1 (0.958 vs 0.726), and External Validation Cohort 2 (0.917 vs 0.613). We also compared the AUC of OsteoSC-M3 with the other models and found that it significantly outperformed all other models across all cohorts (Supplementary Table S12). These findings underscore the critical value of incorporating multidimensional clinical indicators for enhanced osteoporosis diagnostic performance.

To validate the robustness of the OsteoSC-M3 model, we performed a post hoc subgroup analysis on 1526 females in the osteoporosis group of the Training Set (Supplementary Table S9): the AUC difference between the Low BMD and fracture history subgroups was 0.014 (0.975 vs 0.961; P = 0.16). Post hoc sensitivity analysis showed that after removing females with a fracture history, the model AUC increased only slightly by 0.002 (from 0.973 to 0.975) (P = 0.25), confirming that the heterogeneity of the osteoporosis group did not have a clinically significant impact on model performance, and the model is robust and reliable.

Discussion

The ageing population has made osteoporosis a major concern, significantly impacting the quality of life for older adults.1,2 In China, women represent 77.8% (70 million) of osteoporosis cases,3 particularly postmenopausal and elderly women. Early diagnosis and intervention are crucial for preventing fractures and improving quality of life.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 To address this, we developed OsteoSC-M3, a machine learning model that combines age, height, weight, and eight serum biomarkers. It was developed and internally validated with 5745 females from Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital and further tested on two independent multicentre cohorts of 1839 females from Shanghai and 305 females from Hai'an, as well as a prospective osteopenia cohort. The model outperformed single-modal algorithms, enabling non-invasive risk stratification approximately 29 months before clinical diagnosis. An online platform has been created for dynamic assessment, aiming to proactively improve screening strategies. This approach is expected to enable early intervention, significantly enhancing participants' quality of life and reducing healthcare costs.

Based on the pathogenesis of bone remodelling imbalance (initiated by osteoclast-driven bone loss and amplified by hepatorenal dysfunction)19,20 and the observation that serum biomarkers (tP1NP, OC, blood lipids, and renal parameters) precede DXA-detectable BMD reduction,4,21 we developed the OsteoSC-M3 model. This model integrates 11 variables—five risk (age, tP1NP, OC, TBIL, ALP) and six protective factors (height, weight, VITD, DBIL, CHOL, UA)—reflecting three core pathophysiological pathways: metabolic–bone axis disruption, oxidative stress, and remodelling imbalance. Elevated bone formation markers (tP1NP, OC, ALP) indicate accelerated turnover and increased fracture risk,36 while vitamin D exerts protective effects via calcium absorption and bone homeostasis.37 Although cholesterol (CHOL) can increase risk via oxidative stress/inflammation-mediated osteogenesis inhibition and osteoclastogenesis,20 it emerged as a protective factor in our study, potentially reflecting better overall nutritional status in this ageing cohort. Uric acid (UA) protects bone in non-gout populations (consistent with this gout-free cohort).38,39 Bilirubin shows biphasic effects: normal levels protect via antioxidants, but pathological elevation accelerates osteoporosis,40, 41, 42 with TBIL/DBIL exhibiting metabolic state-dependent divergence. These findings confirm osteoporosis as a localised manifestation of systemic metabolic dysregulation. SHAP analysis and case-specific visualisation validated model interpretability (Fig. 7, Supplementary Fig. S7).

Constructed with a stacked ensemble framework, the model demonstrated excellent internal validation performance (Fig. 5A) and superior efficacy vs single-algorithm models, with multicentre external validation yielding AUCs of 0.958 (Cohort 1) and 0.917 (Cohort 2). Prospective validation showed prediction 29 months earlier than clinical diagnosis. The stacking mechanism enables dynamic fusion of base model advantages through meta-learning, accommodating multistage pathogenesis. Missing data analysis confirmed robustness (AUC fluctuation<0.02 post-imputation; Supplementary Fig. S4). The deployed online platform (http://47.101.190.127:3838/scyz/) facilitates early screening and dynamic risk assessment.

Multicentre validation demonstrated that OsteoSC-M3 maintained excellent performance in both cohorts, though Cohort 2 showed a moderate decline (AUC 0.917) compared to Cohort 1 (AUC 0.958). Systematic feature analysis revealed: (1) population heterogeneity in demographic parameters; (2) consistent detection systems for five internationally traceable biomarkers; and (3) platform/methodological differences in tP1NP, OC, and VITD measurements (Roche ECLIA in Cohort 1 vs Snibe CLIA in Cohort 2). The observed performance variations primarily stemmed from methodological heterogeneity and population characteristics. Notably, both external validation cohorts demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance (Table 3), robustly confirming OsteoSC-M3's cross-platform and cross-centre generalisability. To further enhance adaptability, future studies could employ feature-alignment transfer learning to optimise decision weights for platform–sensitive parameters, combined with locally weighted learning for regional model fine-tuning, thereby balancing architectural stability with improved generalisability.

This study evaluated the novel OsteoSC-M3 model for osteoporosis screening vs the widely used OSTA tool. While OSTA showed better discriminative ability (AUC 0.798) in Chinese populations than other clinical tools,17,18 OsteoSC-M3 demonstrated significantly superior performance across all validation cohorts: Training Set (AUC 0.973), Internal Validation Set (AUC 0.943), External Validation Cohort 1 (AUC 0.958), and External Validation Cohort 2 (AUC 0.917)—substantially exceeding OSTA's results (0.720, 0.739, 0.726, 0.613) (Supplementary Fig. S9). These consistent findings confirm OsteoSC-M3's enhanced discriminatory power and underscore the value of integrating osteoporosis-relevant serological biomarkers to improve screening accuracy and generalisability.

This study demonstrates two key innovations: the OsteoSC-M3 machine learning model identifies predictive factors through multiparameter interaction analysis, enhancing clinical interpretability, and the tool provides simple, accurate continuous risk prediction, enabling early risk alerts months before DXA confirmation. Limitations include: binary classification requires expansion to a ternary system (normal/osteopenia/osteoporosis); lifestyle factors and genetic markers remain unincorporated; and model development relied on single-centre data from East China, with validation limited to Asian females aged ≥50 years. Crucially, clinical accessibility of certain laboratory parameters may be limited in smaller hospitals or resource-constrained regions where these data are not routinely collected. Furthermore, while fracture history was documented, the potential for silent fractures to be overlooked when relying on medical records may introduce diagnostic bias. Future multicentre studies should validate generalisability in broader populations (including males and younger women), with risk assessment refined by incorporating comprehensive fracture imaging. Importantly, this model aims to optimise DXA resource allocation through preventive early warning in large-scale primary screening, not to replace clinical diagnosis.

In conclusion, the OsteoSC-M3 model, which integrates demographic information and eight serological laboratory parameters, enables the early prediction of osteoporosis in women (approximately 29 months before clinical diagnosis). It demonstrates superior risk stratification compared to the OSTA tool, effectively identifies high-risk individuals requiring subsequent DXA confirmation, and exhibits high utility in primary care settings lacking DXA equipment. Consequently, this model is anticipated to shift the screening paradigm to an earlier stage and markedly improve screening efficiency in primary care, thereby optimising resource allocation and facilitating early intervention.

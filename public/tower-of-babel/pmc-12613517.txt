KLC3 regulates axonemal glutamylation via RAB11/FIP5 in polycystic kidney disease
Authors: Gyuyeong Rah, Je Yeong Ko, Yejin Ahn, Jung Han Park, Kyung Hyun Yoo, Jong Hoon Park
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
KLC3 was identified as a novel ciliary gene in a previous study that caused cyst formation due to abnormal ciliary defects in cyst-lining cells of polycystic kidney disease (PKD) models. RAB11FIP5 is a regulator of tubulin post-translational modification (PTM) in primary cilia and is located on basal bodies. Here, we determined the relationship between KLC3 and RAB11FIP5 mediated tubulin PTM, which may be involved in cyst progression in patients with PKD. KLC3 co-localizes and interacts with RAB11FIP5 around the basal bodies of primary cilia. KLC3 regulates axonemal glutamylation accompanied by changes in RAB11FIP5 expression in basal bodies, which may be involved in anterograde ciliary trafficking. Similarly, in previous KLC3 results, increased RAB11FIP5 and axonemal glutamylation were observed in the cyst-lining cells of PKD. When the increased KLC3 in ADPKD patient-derived cells decreased, not only ciliary glutamylation and IFT88, but also cell proliferation decreased. Our findings suggest that KLC3 regulates ciliary glutamylation mediated by RAB11FIP5 and it may contribute to cyst progression in PKD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12964-025-02475-7.

Introduction

Primary cilia are microtubule-based organelles that extend from the basal body, derived from the mother centriole, and enclosed by a membrane [1, 2]. Microtubules that form cilia and centrioles undergo various post-translational modifications (PTMs) that are predominantly found in long-lived microtubules [3, 4]. PTMs include acetylation, glutamylation, glycylation, and detyrosinase activity. Glutamylation, glycylation, and detyrosination are mainly enriched in the B-tubules of the outer doublet microtubules of the axoneme, whereas acetylation is enriched in both A- and B-tubules [3, 5]. Several studies suggest that tubulin PTMs are essential for ciliary formation, maintenance, and motility of cilia [6]. Among these, tubulin glutamylation has been highlighted as a key PTM influencing ciliary motility, as well as the stability and length of the axoneme [7–9]. Despite the association between PTM disruption and human diseases, including ciliopathies, the exact functions of these tubulin PTMs remain poorly understood.

Kinesin light chain-3 (KLC3), a member of the Kinesin-1 family, was identified as a novel ciliary protein in the previous study [10]. Conventional Kinesin-1 consists of a heterodimer of kinesin heavy and light chains, which utilize ATP energy to move along microtubules, while the light chain binds to cargo proteins [11–14]. The kinesin light chain is composed of heptad repeat (HR) and tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domains, which bind to the kinesin heavy chain and cargo proteins, respectively. Previously, we reported that elevated KLC3 levels are involved in enhanced anterograde ciliary trafficking through its TPR domains, resulting in elongated cilia, as well as in cystogenesis in renal epithelial cells [10]. Additionally, the Kinesin-1 motor protein KIF5 has been shown to preferentially bind to microtubules in the initial axon segments and move toward the axon tips in dendrites [15]. Some studies have also suggested that Kinesin-1 is implicated in tubulin modification, although there is still controversy regarding whether tubulin modifications regulate Kinesin-1 motility along the microtubules [16–18].

A previous study reported that the RAB11/FIP5 complex is involved in the regulation of tubulin PTMs by modulating glutamylase TTLL5,6, and deglutamylase CCPs [19]. TTLL5 and TTLL6 are known to initiate monoglutamylation and elongate polyglutamylation, respectively, while CCP5 removes the branching glutamate from tubulin [20]. The absence of RAB11/FIP5 at the ciliary basal bodies was shown to disrupt the ciliary entry of TTLL5 and TTLL6, resulting in axoneme hypoglutamylation. Depletion of CCP5 restored axonomal polyglutamylation under RAB11/FIP5-mediated hypoglutatmylation [19]. Rab11 family interacting protein 5 (RAB11FIP5 or FIP5) is recruited to the proximity of basal bodies during cilia assembly, and its distribution pattern is similar to that of KLC3 [10]. The Rab family of proteins interacts with actin and microtubule motor proteins, including kinesins and dyneins, to facilitate vesicle transport [21]. RAB11 can recruit kinesins and dyneins via indirect interactions with linker proteins, such as the RAB11 effectors FIP3 and FIP5 [22–24]. Therefore, we hypothesized that KLC3 plays a role in ciliary trafficking by modifying tubulin, a process mediated by the RAB11/FIP5 complex.

Here, we suggest that KLC3 colocalizes with the RAB11/FIP5 complex and interacts with RAB11FIP5 under conditions that induce ciliogenesis. Moreover, both the overexpression and depletion of KLC3 influenced the recruitment of RAB11/FIP5 to the basal bodies. In contrast, RAB11FIP5 depletion did not alter KLC3 expression, suggesting that KLC3 functions upstream of RAB11FIP5. Furthermore, we found that KLC3 interacted with various tubulin PTMs, including acetylation and glutamylation. Alterations in KLC3 expression affect the ratio of axonemal glutamylation to ciliary length, indicating that KLC3 regulates axonemal glutamylation via a RAB11/FIP5-mediated process. Finally, we observed increased RAB11FIP5 expression in both human and mouse models of autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD), along with accumulation of glutamylation in the ciliary tips. In human ADPKD cells, elevated KLC3 levels induce increased cell proliferation, accompanied by accelerated ciliary glutamylation and anterograde ciliary trafficking. Based on these findings, we propose that disrupted axonemal glutamylation mediated by KLC3 is involved in the pathogenesis of PKD.

Materials and methods

Mice

Pkd1 flox and Pkd2flox mice were obtained from Dr. Stefan Somlo (Yale University, USA). Specific deletions of Pkd1 and Pkd2 were generated by crossing HoxB7-Cre transgenic mice obtained from Dr. Minho Shong (Chungnam National University, Daejeon, Korea) [25]. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Sookmyung Women’s University (SMWU-IACUC-2406-027).

Cell culture

Mouse inner medullary collecting duct-3 (mIMCD-3) cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified eagle’s medium (DMEM)/F-12 (Welgene) supplemented with penicillin-streptomycin and 10% (v/v) FBS. Transformed Human embryonic kidney 293 (HEK293T) cells and NIH/3T3 fibroblasts were cultured in DMEM medium (Welgene) supplemented with penicillin-streptomycin and 10% (v/v) FBS. Proximal tubule cells derived from human patients’ single cysts were obtained from Dr. Hyunho Kim (Seoul National University Hospital) and cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 0.5 mg/ml G418 [26]. Primary renal proximal tubular epithelial cells (RPTEC) were used to control the proximal tubules.

To induce cilia formation, mIMCD-3 cells were cultured under serum free condition. For ADPKD cells, however, 1% serum was used during cilia induction to maintain optimal cell conditions, instead of complete serum starvation.

For siRNA transfection, mouse Klc3, Rab11Fip5, and Pkd1 siRNAs and human KLC3 and RAB11FIP5 siRNA were purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Bioneer, and OriGene. Non-targeting control siRNAs from each respective supplier were used as negative controls. The cells were transfected with siRNAs using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Invitrogen). For plasmid transfection, mouse Klc3 and Klc3ΔTPR coding sequences were cloned into both pCMV-Tag2B and pEGFP-C2. Cells were transfected with these plasmids using FuGene (Promega). All cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assay

Cells were lysed with 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 50 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 1% Triton X-100, and 0.5% NP-40, supplemented with a protease and phosphatase inhibitor mixture (Roche) at 4 °C for 5 min. Lysates were clarified by centrifugation at 4 °C and 20,000 × g for 15 min. For immunoprecipitation, lysates were incubated with anti-FLAG (A1804; Sigma), followed by incubation with Dynabeads Protein G (10004D; Invitrogen). The precipitates were washed three times with the lysis buffer as described above and separated by SDS-PAGE for immunoblotting.

Quantitative reverse-transcription (RT-q) PCR

RNA was extracted from the cell lysates using a NucleoSpin RNA/Protein kit (MACHEREY-NAGEL). RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Promega) according to the supplier’s protocol. PCR was performed with the SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (PCR Biosystems) using a LightCycler 96 System (Roche). Relative target gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCq method and were normalized to the expression levels of ACTB or 18srRNA.

Immunostaining

For immunocytochemistry, mIMCD-3 and ADPKD cells were fixed with 4% PFA at RT for 10 min. Cold methanol fixation was used for ciliary IFT88 staining. The fixed cells were washed with PBS and incubated with primary antibodies in a permeabilizing solution at 4 °C overnight. After washing off the primary antibodies, fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibodies were added and the cells were further incubated at room temperature for 1 h. The cells were mounted using a fluorescent mounting medium (S3023; Dako).

The paraffin-embedded sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated for immunofluorescence staining. The sections were heated in a Borg Decloaker RTU (BD1000G1; Biocare Medical) for antigen retrieval and blocked using blocking solution PK-6200 (Vector Laboratories) for 1 h followed by overnight incubation with the appropriate primary antibody at 4 °C. After washing, fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibodies were added, and the sections were incubated at room temperature for 2 h. Cells were mounted using mounting medium with DAPI (H-1500, Vector Laboratories).

Primary antibodies against the following proteins were used: KLC3 (sc-398492; Santa Cruz Biotechnology); ARL13B (17711-1-AP), IFT88 (13967-1-AP; Proteintech), Polyglutamylation (AG-208-0020B; AdipoGen); Ac-alpha-tubulin (5335; Cell Signaling Technology); fluorescein Dolichos biflorus agglutinin (RL-1031; Vector Laboratories); acetylated tubulin (T6793; Sigma); KLC3 (sc-398492; Santa Cruz Biotechnology); RAB11FIP5 (14594-1-AP), IFT88 (13967-1-AP; Proteintech); and RAB11 (700184, ThermoFisher). All Immunofluorescence images were captured using an LSM-700 confocal laser-scanning microscope (Carl Zeiss) at the Chronic and Metabolic Diseases Research Center, Sookmyung Women’s University.

Western blotting

Proteins were extracted using a NucleoSpin RNA/Protein kit (Macherey-Nagel), separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to a PVDF membrane. The following primary antibodies were used: Phospho-AKT (Thr308) (4056), Phospho-AKT (Ser473) (4691), AKT (4691, CST); FLAG (F7425, Sigma); Polyglutamylation (AG-208-0020B; AdipoGen); Ac-alpha-tubulin (5335; Cell Signaling Technology); γ-tubulin (T6557, Sigma); RAB11FIP5 (ab153843; Abcam); RAB11FIP5 (14594-1-AP), ARL13B (17711-1-AP; Proteintech); RAB11 (700184, ThermoFisher); and β-actin (A300-491 A; Bethyl). Immunoreactive proteins were detected using EzWestLumi Plus (ATTO) and analyzed using an Amersham ImageQuant 800 Imager (GE Healthcare).

Cell proliferation assay

Cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (Dojindo Molecular Technologies). Cells were harvested, resuspended in fresh culture medium containing 1% FBS, and seeded in 96-well plates. The cells were incubated at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere for 24 h. Following treatment with control, KLC3 or RAB11FIP5 siRNA, 10 µl of CCK-8 solution and 90 µl of medium was added to each well. The cells were incubated at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere for 30 min. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using an ELISA plate reader (BioTek).

Three-dimensional (3D) in vitro cyst formation assay

For in vitro cyst formation of mIMCD-3, cell suspensions (2.0 × 10⁵ cells/ml) were mixed with matrigel (Corning) at a 1:1 ratio and seeded into an 8-well chamber slide. After the Matrigel solidified, culture medium was added on top of the embedded cells. Following 5 days of culture, phase-contrast images were acquired using an IX53 light microscope (Olympus) and cyst lumen size was measured using iSolutionX64 software.

Human tissue collection

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Hospital (H-0701-033-195) and Samsung Medical Center (SMC 2019-08-074). Paraffin-embedded ADPKD and non-ADPKD tissue blocks were obtained from Seoul National University Hospital and Samsung Medical Center, respectively. For non-ADPKD tissue controls, non-cancerous tissue regions were obtained from patients with renal cell carcinoma who underwent radical nephrectomy.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 10 (GraphPad Software) was used for statistical analysis. Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were analyzed using the unpaired two-tailed t-test or one-way ANOVA. A p-value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

KLC3 co-localizes with RAB11/FIP5 on the ciliary bases and interacts with RAB11FIP5

KLC3 was identified as a novel ciliary protein in our previous study, which localizes near ciliary bases [10], similar to RAB11/FIP5 in hTERT-RPE-1 cells under cilia-inducing conditions [19]. Therefore, we hypothesized that KLC3 interacts with RAB11/FIP5 to modulate primary cilia. First, we co-stained KLC3 and RAB11/FIP5 in mIMCD-3 cells and observed that KLC3 co-localized with RAB11FIP5 and RAB11 in proximity to the basal bodies (Fig. 1A, B, Supplementary Fig. 1A, B). As previously reported, KLC3 regulates ciliary trafficking through its TPR domains, which bind to and recruit cargo proteins [10–14]. To investigate whether KLC3 regulates RAB11/FIP5 as its cargo protein during cilia formation, we performed an IP assay using HEK293T cells transfected with Flag-tagged KLC3 and KLC3ΔTPR vectors after serum withdrawal for 1 d (Fig. 1C). The result showed that KLC3 interacted with RAB11FIP5 and glutamylated tubulin (GT335) through its TPR domain upon serum withdrawal, whereas interaction with RAB11 was not observed (Fig. 1C).

To further investigate whether KLC3 recruits RAB11/FIP5 to the ciliary bases, mIMCD-3 cells were transfected with EGFP-tagged KLC3 or KLC3ΔTPR and after that cultured in serum-free medium for 3 d. In cells overexpressing KLC3, both RAB11 and RAB11FIP5 accumulated in the basal bodies (Fig. 1D, E). In contrast, cells transfected with EGFP-KLC3ΔTPR displayed less accumulation of the RAB11/FIP5 complex at the basal bodies (Fig. 1D, E). Additionally, we examined the RAB11FIP5 accumulation in KLC3 overexpressing cells under non-ciliated condition. Both RAB11FIP5 and RAB11 were localized to γ-tubulin under non-ciliated conditions (Supplementary Fig. 2A, B). Interestingly, under these non-ciliated conditions, RAB11FIP5 was not accumulated in Klc3-overespressing cells (Supplementary Fig. 2C), suggesting that Klc3 recruits Rab11Fip5 to the basal body to regulate ciliary glutamylation.

Moreover, when we examined mIMCD-3 cells transfected with Klc3 siRNA, both RAB11FIP5 and RAB11 expression significantly reduced in the basal bodies under cilia-inducing conditions (Fig. 1F-H). However, the depletion of RAB11FIP5 did not lead to a decrease in KLC3 expression around the basal bodies (Supplementary Fig. 3A, C). Thus, these results suggest that KLC3 not only colocalizes with, but also regulates RAB11/FIP5 to ciliary bases through its TPR domains by interacting with RAB11FIP5 under cilia-inducing conditions.

KLC3 interacts with tubulin PTM and regulates axonemal polyglutamylation

A previous study reported that KIF5, a member of the Kinesin-1 family, may be regulated by tubulin modifications [27], but the correlation between Kinesin-1 and tubulin modification remains unclear. Since it has been shown that RAB11/FIP5 can regulate tubulin modification [19], we examined that KLC3 which interacts with RAB11FIP5, may not only interact with but also be involved in microtubules modification in primary cilia.

To investigate this further, we performed an IP assay to determine whether KLC3 interacts with tubulin PTMs under cilia-induced conditions. The results showed that KLC3 interacted with acetylated-α-tubulin (Ac-α-tub), glutamylated tubulin (GT335), and γ-tubulin (Fig. 2A). Moreover, the interaction between KLC3 and glutamylated tubulin was stronger with β-tubulin compared to ⍺-tubulin (Figs. 1C and 2A). Additionally, KLC3ΔTPR did not interact with any tubulins, leading to assume that the TPR domains are required for binding to the tubulins.

KLC3 has been shown to be involved in ciliogenesis through its TPR domains; overexpression of KLC3 in the mIMCD-3 cells resulted in 1.5 times longer cilia stained with anti-Ac-α-tub compared to the control, while the cilia length in KLC3ΔTPR transfected cells was similar to that of the control [10]. Based on this previous study, we investigated whether KLC3 overexpression induces ciliary recruitment of ARL13B. Total ARL13B levels were assessed by western blotting of whole-cell lysates, and ciliary ARL13B expression was evaluated by immunocytochemistry. While KLC3 overexpression did not affect total intracellular ARL13B levels, it increased ARL13B localization in the cilia (Supplementary Fig. 4A, B).

Since KLC3 interacts and co-localizes with RAB11FIP5 around the basal bodies during cilia formation in Fig. 1, we further examined whether KLC3 affects polyglutamylated tubulins in extended cilia [19] (Fig. 2B). As previously reported [10], the cilia stained with Ac-α-tub were longer in KLC3-overexpressing cells, but not in KLC3ΔTPR-transfected cells (Fig. 2C). Similarly, GT335, which measured from the same cilia as Ac-α-tub, showed a similar elongation pattern. Furthermore, the ratio of axonemal polyglutamylation to cilia length was remarkably higher in KLC3-overexpressing cells than in control cells. In contrast, KLC3ΔTPR transfected cells displayed similar ciliary phenotypes to the control.

Next, to assess the effect of Klc3 overexpression on acetylated α-tubulin and glutamylated tubulin levels in whole-cell lysates, we analyzed these proteins in cells overexpressing either Klc3 or Klc3 ∆TPR. The results showed that total levels of tubulin acetylation and polyglutamylation were not significantly altered (Supplementary Fig. 5A). Thus, KLC3 regulates ciliary axonemal polyglutamylation via its TPR domain by interacting with RAB11FIP5.

Depletion of KLC3 regulates anterograde IFT trafficking mediated by tubulin glutamylation

Next, we examined whether KLC3 depletion affects ciliogenesis. In contrast to cells overexpressing KLC3 reported in a previous study [10], the ratio of cilia-positive cells decreased in Klc3 knockdown cells (Fig. 3A, B). Moreover, the cilia length in Klc3 siRNA-treated cells was longer than that in the control, whereas the length of polyglutamylated tubulin staining was shorter, accompanied by a decreased polyglutamylation-to-cilia length ratio in KLC3-depleted cells (Fig. 3A, C). Consistent with the overexpression of cells (Supplementary Fig. 5A), the total protein expression of tubulin in whole cell lysates remained unchanged in KLC3-depleted cells (Supplementary Fig. 5B). Based on the observation that both increased and decreased KLC3 expression resulted in elongated cilia, we assumed that KLC3 may regulate ciliary length through different mechanisms, such as acetyltransferase ⍺TAT1 [28] or deacetylases HDAC6 [29] and SIRT2 [30]. To assess whether RAB11FIP5 can also modify the ciliary glutamylation, we examined cells transfected with Rab11Fip5 siRNA (Supplementary Fig. 3B, C). As expected [19], axonemal polyglutamylation was disrupted in RAB11FIP5-depleted cells with slightly increased elongated primary cilia in mIMCD-3 cells.

Furthermore, it has been reported that AKT regulates ciliogenesis by modulating the RAB11 complex [31]. Blocking AKT function promotes ciliation, which is significantly reduced in AKT1-overexpressing RPE-1 cells. As decreased ciliation was observed in KLC3-deficient cells (Fig. 3B), increased AKT signaling expressions in KLC3-depleted cells were observed (Fig. 3D). Based on this result, we assumed that the increased activation of AKT under cilia induction conditions decreased ciliation in KLC3-deficient cells.

In addition to examining renal epithelial cells, we investigated the effects of KLC3 on NIH3T3 cells, which are commonly used in ciliary studies. Before assessing ciliary phenotypes, we examined the localization of KLC3 in NIH3T3 cells. Klc3 was primarily localized to the basal body, as observed in mIMCD-3 cells, and was also occasionally found along the ciliary axoneme under serum-free conditions for 4 h (Supplementary Fig. 6A) [10], suggesting that KLC3 may be directly involved in ciliary trafficking during ciliogenesis. Similar to the results observed in renal cells, cells treated with Klc3 siRNA exhibited longer cilia stained with ARL13B or Ac-α-tubulin compared to the control (Supplementary Fig. 6B-D, F). The ratio of Ac-α-tubulin to ARL13B also increased in KLC3 knockdown cells (Supplementary Fig. 6F). Conversely, the length of cilia stained with GT335 reduced in the KLC3 knockdown cells (Supplementary Fig. 6E). The reduced expression of KLC3 was confirmed by relative mRNA expression levels (Supplementary Fig. 6G). These findings suggest that Klc3 was commonly observed at the basal body in both cell types, and showed similar effects in regulating cilia length and glutamylation, suggesting that basal body-localized Klc3 is responsible for these regulatory functions.

Polyglutamylation of the ciliary axoneme is expected to regulate ciliary trafficking [19, 32]. We previously reported that KLC3 overexpression is involved in anterograde ciliary trafficking, including Kinesin-2 and IFT-B complexes [10]. In this study, we examined whether the depletion of KLC3 could also regulate anterograde ciliary trafficking (Fig. 3E). The intensity of IFT88 from the basal bodies to the distal end of each cilium significantly reduced in Klc3 siRNA-transfected cells compared to that in the control cells, suggesting that axonemal polyglutamylation plays a critical role in anterograde ciliary trafficking. The decreased expression of KLC3 was further confirmed by relative mRNA expression levels (Fig. 3F).

Thus, the depletion of KLC3 affects not only glutamylation but also the acetylation of tubulin in primary cilia, and the decreased axonemal glutamylation results in decreased anterograde ciliary trafficking.

Increased RAB11FIP5 with axoneme hyper-glutamylation is involved in PKD models

Furthermore, we examined the expression of RAB11FIP5 and cilia in the cyst-lining cells of PKD models and found alterations similar to those observed in the KLC3 and IFT-B proteins [10]. In the Pkd1f/f: HoxB7-Cre kidney model, an increase in polyglutamylation was observed, which accumulated at the ciliary tips of cyst-lining cells compared to the control (Fig. 4A). RAB11FIP5 expression was also increased in these cells (Fig. 4B, C) and similarly elevated in kidney lysates from the Pkd1f/f: HoxB7-Cre mice compared to controls (Fig. 4D). Consistent with the observations in PKD1-deficient mice, kidney tissues from patients with ADPKD also showed accumulated polyglutamylation at the ciliary tips (Fig. 4E), accompanied by increased RAB11FIP5 in cyst-lining cells (Fig. 4F). The expression patterns of other ciliary proteins, such as IFT-B and EGFR [10], were similar to those observed in ADPKD kidneys (Fig. 4A, E).

As RAB11FIP5 expression is elevated in PKD models, we sought to investigate its potential role in cyst progression associated with PKD. Interestingly, knockdown of Rab11Fip5 was found to reduce in vitro cyst lumen size in Pkd1 siRNA-transfected mIMCD-3 cells (Fig. 4G). Based on these findings, we conclude that the increased accumulation of ciliary proteins at the ciliary tips is induced by axonemal hyperglutamylation, and that elevated expression of ciliary glutamylation regulators, such as Rab11Fip5, may contribute to cyst progression in PKD.

Decreased KLC3 regulates ciliary trafficking mediated by tubulin glutamylation and cell proliferation

To further confirm the effect of KLC3 in human ADPKD models, two cyst lining cells lines derived from ADPKD patients were used [26]. The cells from the two patients with ADPKD were examined by comparison to normal proximal cells, and expression levels of both KLC3 and RAB11FIP5 were relatively high in ADPKD patient cell lines (Supplementary Fig. 7A). To maintain optimal cell conditions during cilia induction of ADPKD cells, we used 1% serum starvation instead of a completely serum-free medium. After incubation in 1% serum medium, cilia were stained with both Ac-α-tubulin and GT335. Moreover, the ADPKD#4 cells exhibited accumulation and elongation of glutamylated tubulin compared to the ADPKD#2 cells, along with higher KLC3 and RAB11FIP5 expression (Supplementary Fig. 7B). Therefore, ADPKD#4 cells were used for further experiments.

To examine whether the attenuated KLC3 had an effect on ciliary abnormalities observed in ADPKD, the cells were treated with KLC3 siRNA. First, we confirmed shortened axonemal glutamylation in KLC3 siRNA transfected ADPKD cells compared to that in controls. Additionally, the ratio of GT335 to Ac-α-tubulin decreased, while the cilia length stained with Ac-α-tubulin increased (Fig. 5A). We have suggested that KLC3 is upregulated in ADPKD and may contribute to abnormal anterograde trafficking, as it belongs to the kinesin family [10]. Consistent with this, we observed abnormally accumulation of IFT88 in siCTRL transfected ADPKD patient-derived cells (Fig. 5B). The signal intensities of IFT88 from basal bodies to distal ends of the cilia significantly reduced in KLC3 knockdown cells, suggesting that shortened axonemal glutamylation affects anterograde IFT trafficking in primary cilia (Fig. 5B). Moreover, we suggest that abnormal ciliary trafficking may influence cystogenesis by regulating signaling pathways, such as EGFR [10]. Using the CCK-8 assay, we confirmed that reduced KLC3 expression decreased cell proliferation in ADPKD patient cells (Fig. 5C, D).

Next, to confirm whether RAB11FIP5 promotes abnormal ciliary glutamylation and IFT88 trafficking in ADPKD cells, we examined the effects of RAB11FIP5 knockdown on these ciliary abnormalities. Knockdown of RAB11FIP5 decreased the length of glutamylated cilia without affecting the length of acetylated cilia; consequently, the ratio of glutamylated cilia to acetylated α-tubulin was reduced (Fig. 5E). In addition, ciliary IFT88 trafficking was reduced in RAB11FIP5 siRNA-transfected ADPKD cells (Fig. 5F, H), and CCK-8 assay results showed that RAB11FIP5 downregulation decreased the viability of ADPKD patient cells (Fig. 5G). Taken together, these data suggest that decreased KLC3 and RAB11FIP5 expression may reduce cyst progression, which is mediated by accelerated anterograde ciliary trafficking derived from hyperglutamylation in human ADPKD.

Discussion

In this study, we focused on the role of KLC3 in extracellular ciliogenesis pathways. KLC3, a member of the kinesin-1 family, binds to several anterograde ciliary proteins including Kinesin-2 and IFT-B. Here, we provide evidence of the function of KLC3 in the intracellular ciliogenesis pathway (Supplementary Fig. 8). KLC3 interacts with Rab family members, specifically with the RAB11/FIP5 complex. Not only does RAB11/FIP5 co-localize with KLC3, but it is also regulated by KLC3 as its cargo under ciliogenesis-inducing conditions. The role of RAB11 in the intracellular ciliogenesis pathway is well established. In this context, we delineated the overall ciliogenesis axis: KLC3-RAB11/FIP5-axonemal glutamylation-ciliary anterograde trafficking, in the primary cilia. Furthermore, our previous work suggested that accelerated anterograde ciliary trafficking may drive cyst progression in renal epithelial cells [10]. Based on this, we propose that KLC3 plays a role in cilia formation at the ciliary base by regulating tubulin modifications, including the RAB11/FIP5-axonemal glutamylation axis. Although it is still unclear which tubulin PTMs specifically are involved in trafficking, our results provide evidence that glutamylation is likely to be more critical than acetylation for IFT-B trafficking in primary cilia.

We also suggest a relationship between KLC3 and AKT signaling, which is related to the RAB11 family in primary cilia. A previous study has shown that KLC3 interacts with RABIN8, another member of the RAB family [33]. In addition, the RAB11–RABIN8–RAB8 cascade has been implicated in intracellular ciliogenesis [34–36]. Kinesin-1, including KLC3, interacts with microtubules and participates in various cellular processes, suggesting that KLC3 is a crucial transport protein involved in ciliary formation. This is expected to provide a more comprehensive understanding of primary cilia formation, contingent upon the elucidation of the relationship between KLC3 and RAB11-AKT signaling in further studies.

Furthermore, these cilia phenotypes were also observed in cells derived from the renal cysts of patients with ADPKD. Proximal tubule cells from patients with ADPKD exhibit elevated KLC3 expression and tubulin glutamylation. We confirmed that KLC3 depletion affects ciliary phenotypes, tubulin glutamylation, and anterograde IFT transport. Additionally, ADPKD patient-derived cells transfected with KLC3 siRNA show decreased cell proliferation, supporting the notion that abnormal cilia contribute to cyst progression by promoting increased cell proliferation. In addition, previous studies on RAB11FIP5-axonemal glutamylation and KLC3 have suggested their relevance to polycystin-2 (PC2), with disruption of GT335 by depletion of RAB11FIP5 leading to decreased PC2, whereas overexpression of KLC3 resulting in increased PC2 in primary cilia [10, 19]. This implies that axonemal glutamylation may be a key factor in PC2 trafficking in PKD models.

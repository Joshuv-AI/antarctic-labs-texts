Anderson Accelerated Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient for solving LP
Yingxin Zhou, Stefano Cipolla, Phan Tu Vuong

Abstract
We present the Anderson Accelerated Primal–Dual Hybrid Gradient (AA-PDHG), a fixed-point-based framework designed to overcome the slow convergence of the standard PDHG method for the solution of linear programming (LP) problems. We establish the global convergence of AA-PDHG under a safeguard condition. In addition, we propose a filtered variant (FAA-PDHG) that applies angle and length filtering to preserve the uniform boundedness of the coefficient matrix, a property crucial for guaranteeing convergence. Numerical results show that both AA-PDHG and FAA-PDHG deliver significant speedups over vanilla PDHG for large-scale LP instances.

Introduction

In recent years, first-order methods have become increasingly popular for solving large-scale convex optimization problems, particularly due to their low per-iteration cost and favorable scalability. Among them, the Primal Dual Hybrid Gradient (PDHG) method has received considerable attention. If we consider a min-max problem in the form:

\displaystyle\min_{x}\max_{z}\;f(x)+z^{{}^{\top}}Kx-g^{*}(z),

where
f
and
g
are proper, closed, and convex functions,
g^{*}
denotes the conjugate function of
g
, and
K
is a linear operator, then the corresponding PDHG scheme can be written as

\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\arg\min_{x}\left\{f(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|x-\left(x^{k}-\tau K^{{}^{\top}}z^{k}\right)\right\|^{2}\right\},\\
z^{k+1}&=\arg\min_{z}\left\{g^{*}(z)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\|z-\left(z^{k}+\sigma K\left(2x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right)\right)\right\|^{2}\right\},\end{split}

(1)

being
\tau,\;\sigma
step-sizes. This algorithm is also commonly referred to as the Chambolle–Pock algorithm
ChambollePDHG
. When the updates in (
1
) are specialised for the solution of linear programs (LP), see (
8
), it is possible to observe a major advantage of PDHG when compared to other first or second-order optimization methods, that is, it requires only matrix-vector multiplications at each iteration, thereby avoiding solution of linear systems and related expensive matrix factorisations. Such a characteristic is often referred to as matrix-free in the literature, as also discussed in
ChambollePDHG
. This characteristic, combined with the fact that the matrix-vector product is a highly parallelizable task, makes PDHG particularly attractive for large-scale applications. And indeed, the use of PDHG for solving LP problems has recently gathered a lot of attention, see, e.g.,
PGLP87
;
SDLP89
;
AFOM-SPECIAL-LP11
;
ADMMLP17
;
AFOM-SPECIAL-LP19
;
fasterPDHG-23
, which have demonstrated that PDHG might offer a more scalable alternative to classical LP methods, such as interior-point or simplex methods.
Nonetheless, when applied to LP problems, the PDHG method might still exhibit relatively slow convergence in practice. In particular, it often suffers from stagnation in the later stages of optimization, especially when approaching optimal solutions. This is because linear programming problems lack strong convexity, making PDHG achieve only sublinear convergence rates
first-order-beck
. As a result, obtaining high-precision solutions within a reasonable computational time remains difficult, especially in large-scale or ill-conditioned scenarios.

Motivated by the above discussion, in this work, we explore the integration of acceleration techniques within the PDHG framework for solving large-scale linear programming problems.

It is important to note that in some works it has been shown that LP problems possessing special properties, e.g., sharpness
fasterPDHG-23
or error bounds
QEB-FE2023
, can support faster convergence rates for first-order methods. These properties can be leveraged to theoretically establish faster convergence rates for first-order methods applied to LPs. Motivated by these insights, numerous accelerated variants of PDHG have been developed. Notable acceleration strategies include adaptive step sizes
AdapPDHG15
, inertial techniques
IPDHG-16
, line search strategies
LINE-PDHG18
, coordinate updates
ACPDHG21
, and refined primal-dual schemes
google2022p
;
fasterPDHG-23
;
PDLPnew2025
. While differing in implementation and used methodologies, these methods all aim to improve the rate of convergence of PDHG. Some of them leverage historical information and have demonstrated significant improvements across various application domains. And indeed, their success highlights the importance of incorporating past information into the update rules to guide the optimization process more effectively.

Inspired by the success of these methods and their theoretical insights, in this work, we explore an acceleration approach that systematically leverages historical information. Specifically, we adopt a fixed-point perspective and investigate the use of Anderson Acceleration (AA) to enhance the convergence behaviour of PDHG. Our objective is to improve the overall efficiency and stability of PDHG when applied to large-scale linear programming (LP) problems.

Anderson Acceleration (AA) is a technique originally introduced in

Anderson Acceleration (AA) has broad applicability. For instance,
AA-FPI-NI
demonstrates that, for linear problems, AA is essentially equivalent to the generalized minimal residual (GMRES) method; see also
MR4711308
for connections among Krylov methods and AA. Furthermore,
AA-QUASI-NEWTON
interprets AA as a quasi-Newton method for nonlinear problems, thereby offering a theoretical basis for its acceleration properties.

On top of that, although AA has demonstrated significant acceleration capability in practical applications, the theoretical analysis of its convergence has only been gradually developed in recent years. In
AA-FPI-NI
it was observed that when the fixed-point iteration is governed by a contractive operator, the algorithm can achieve local linear convergence rates.
EDIIS-19
further considers the so-called EDIIS algorithm as a special case of AA requiring
\alpha_{(k)}\geq 0,
and proves its global convergence property under the case

is contractive on a convex set. In
AA-IMPROVE-PROOF-ECPRX
, the authors extended the result in
AA-FPI-NI
for AA with damping factors, and observed that AA is more effective for linearly convergent fixed-point methods, whereas for algorithms with quadratic convergence, it may actually reduce the acceleration effect. In
anderson-type-I
it was recently proposed a global convergence scheme for AA-I assuming the fixed point mapping is non-expansive. Based on this, the scheme was further applied in
anderson-accelerated-operatorsplitting
, where it served as a global convergence framework and was applied to splitting optimization methods.

Inspired by these research results, we note that, as a member of the class of splitting methods, the PDHG algorithm can also be formulated as a fixed-point iteration. In particular,
degenerate2021
investigated the convergence properties of PDHG in fixed point form under degenerate conditions. We also notice, though there are currently many papers showing that the AA algorithm can be applied to various first order algorithms
AA-PG-21
;
EG-AA-24
;
anderson-accelerated-operatorsplitting
, there has not yet been any work applying it to the PDHG method. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that integrates Anderson Acceleration into the PDHG framework. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. First, we integrate Anderson Acceleration into the PDHG framework and establish its global convergence under a safeguard condition. Unlike existing works, we further incorporate a diagonal correction to enhance numerical stability. Moreover, we propose a novel approach to ensure the uniform boundedness of the matrix
H_{k}
, which plays a key role in guaranteeing convergence. This variant is referred to as the filtered AA-PDHG method. Second, to ensure feasibility when solving constrained problems, we introduce a projection step into the accelerated iteration to prevent infeasible iterates.

The structure of this work is as follows, we first recall the preliminary knowledge that will be used throughout the work. In Section
2.1
, we present the formulation of the primal problem addressed in
google2022p
and explain how its corresponding dual form is derived. In Section
3
, we provide the specific formulation of the primal-dual problem in the form of a fixed point iteration tailored to the given problem. We provide a proof of the global convergence of our primal-dual fixed point iteration in Section
3.1
. In Section
4
, we also introduce a variant of AA-PDHG, FAA-PDHG, in which a filtering technique is employed. Numerical experiment results demonstrating the performance of our algorithm are presented in Section
5
.

In this section, we present the proof of global convergence of Algorithm

Theorem 1
.

Let the sequence
\{u^{k}\}
be generated by
u^{k+1}=\mathcal{T}u^{k}
. Then, the fixed point residual converges to zero, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\mathcal{T}u^{k}-u^{k}\|=0.

(13)

Proof.

From

\mathcal{T}
is
\mathcal{M}
-firmly non-expansive, then the operator

is
\mathcal{M}
non-expansive, and
Fix\mathcal{R}=Fix\mathcal{T}
.
Hence from the iteration
u^{k+1}=\mathcal{T}(u^{k})
, we have

\displaystyle u^{k+1}=\mathcal{T}(u^{k})=\frac{\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{I}}{2}u^{k}.

and hece

\displaystyle u^{k+1}=u^{k}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{R}(u^{k})-u^{k}).

(14)

Assume

is a fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
, then

. We have hence,

\displaystyle\|u^{k+1}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\frac{1}{2}(u^{k}-u^{*})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{R}(u^{k})-u^{*})\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u^{k}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{R}(u^{k})-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\|u^{k}-\mathcal{R}(u^{k})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u^{k}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{R}(u^{k})-\mathcal{R}(u^{*})\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\|u^{k}-\mathcal{R}(u^{k})\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

(15)

where the second equality holds due to the fact that for all

and for any

whereas the last inequality holds because of the non-expansive property of

. From (

), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{4}\|\mathcal{R}(u^{k})-u^{k}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\leq\|u^{0}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2},

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\mathcal{R}(u^{k})-u^{k}\|_{\mathcal{M}}=0.
From the definition of
\mathcal{R}
, we get

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\mathcal{T}(u^{k})-u^{k}\|_{\mathcal{M}}=0,

and the thesis follows by using the equivalence of norms in finite dimension and the fact that
\mathcal{M}
is supposed to be positive definite.

To ensure the convergence of the iteration (

Lemma 3

In Algorithm

Proof.

Assume that AA is used only a finite number of times, i.e., there exists a finite integer

Then, there exists

we have

6
Conclusion

In this work, we propose a novel application of Anderson Acceleration (AA) to the Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient (PDHG) method for solving linear programming (LP) problems. Since AA was originally developed for fixed-point iterations, we begin by reformulating the PDHG algorithm for LPs into a fixed-point framework. To ensure that the AA-accelerated iterates remain within the feasible set, we introduce an additional projection step. By incorporating a safeguard mechanism, we establish the global convergence of the proposed algorithm.

For the numerical experiments, we consider large-scale LP instances derived from the MIPLIB 2017 dataset, where integer constraints are relaxed. Our results show that the AA-PDHG method outperforms, on selected instances, the vanilla PDHG algorithm in terms of convergence rate. We further investigate the impact of different AA memory sizes and observe trends consistent with findings in the existing literature.

Importantly, our method enhances the vanilla PDHG algorithm using AA alone, without introducing any additional acceleration techniques. This simplicity suggests that, in future work, combining AA with other advanced strategies could yield variants of AA-PDHG capable of achieving performance comparable to that of state-of-the-art solvers such as PDLP
google2022p
.

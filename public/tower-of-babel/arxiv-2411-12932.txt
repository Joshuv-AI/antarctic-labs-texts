On the Laplace transform
Alexander G. Ramm

Abstract
MSC: 44A10 Key words: Laplace transform; tempered distributions; analytic functions. Sufficient conditions are given for a function F ⁡ ( p ) F(p) to be the Laplace transform of a function f ⁡ ( t ) f(t) or a distribution f f . No assumption on f f is given a priori. It is not even assumed that f = 0 f=0 for t < 0 t<0 .

It is often important to know if a function
F(p)
is a Laplace transform of a function or a distribution, see, for example,
[
3
]
. In the literature it is usually assumed that
f(t)

satifies some assumptions, for example,
f(t)=0
for
t<0
,
|f(t)|<ce^{at}
,
a=const\geq 0
, see, for example,
[
5
]
. By
c>0
various constants are denoted. In this paper we do not make any assumptions on
f
.

Let us formulate our main result.

Theorem 1.

Assume that
F(p),\,p=\sigma+is,
is an analytic function in the half-plane
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
, there exist the limits
\lim_{\sigma\to\sigma_{0}}F(p)=F(\sigma_{0}+is)
for almost every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}:=(-\infty,\infty)
and

\lim_{|p_{n}|\to\infty}F(p_{n})=0,

(1.1)

where
\lim_{n\to\infty}|p_{n}|=\infty
.

Then

F(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-pt}f(t)dt,

(1.2)

and

f(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{K_{\sigma_{0}}}e^{qt}F(q)dq,

(1.3)

where
K_{\sigma n}:=(\sigma-in,\sigma+in),\,\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}\geq 0
.

Proof.
By the analyticity of
F(p)
and the Cauchy formula one gets

F(p)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L_{\sigma\,n}}\frac{F(q)dq}{q-p},

(1.4)

where
p
belongs to the domain with the closed boundary
L_{\sigma\,n}=-K_{\sigma_{0}\,n}\cup C_{n}
,
C_{n}=\sigma_{0}+ne^{i\phi}
,
-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\phi\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
. The contour
L_{\sigma\,n}
is oriented counterclockwise.

Let
n\to\infty
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\int_{C_{n}}\frac{F(q)dq}{q-p}|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{n-|p|}\sup_{q\in C_{n}}|F(q)|=0,

(1.5)

because of our assumption (
1.1
). From (
1.5
) and (
1.2
) it follows that

F(p)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-K_{\sigma_{0}}}\frac{F(q)dq}{q-p},\qquad-K_{\sigma_{0}}:=(\sigma_{0}+i\infty,\sigma_{0}-i\infty).

(1.6)

One has

\frac{1}{p-q}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(p-q)t}dt,\qquad Rep>Req.

(1.7)

From (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) one gets

F(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-pt}\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{K_{\sigma_{0}}}F(q)e^{qt}dq\Big)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-pt}f(t)dt,

(1.8)

where

f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{K_{\sigma_{0}}}F(q)e^{qt}dq,

(1.9)

and

F(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-pt}f(t)dt.

(1.10)

Let us prove that
f(t)
in formula (
1.9
) has the property
f(t)=0
for
t<0
.

Consider the identity which follows from the analyticity of
F(p)
and of
e^{-q|t|}
in the half-plane
\sigma>0
and the decay of
e^{-q|t|}
when
Re\,q\to\infty
and
|t|>0
:

\int_{L_{\sigma_{0}n}}e^{-q|t|}F(q)dq=0.

(1.11)

We have

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\int_{C_{n}}e^{-q|t|}F(q)dq|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{q\in C_{n}}|F(q)|\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-n\cos\phi|t|}nd\phi=0,

(1.12)

because

\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-n\cos\phi|t|}nd\phi=2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-n\cos\phi|t|}nd\phi=2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-n|t|\sin\phi}d\phi=O(\frac{1}{n|t|}),\quad n\to\infty.

(1.13)

This estimate follows from the known inequality
\sin\phi\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\phi
and a simple estimate:

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-\frac{2}{\phi}n|t|\sin\phi}d\phi=O(\frac{1}{n|t|}),\quad n\to\infty.

(1.14)

From (
1.11
), (
1.12
) and (
1.14
) it follows that

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{K_{\sigma_{0}}}e^{-q|t|}F(q)dq=0,\quad|t|>0.

(1.15)

This means that
f(t)=0
for
t<0
.

Theorem 1 is proved.

2
Various comments

a) Conditions for
F(p)
to be a Laplace transform are practically important, see, for example,
[
3
]
, where the equation
h(t)=g(t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}(t-s)^{-\frac{5}{4}}h(s)ds
is studied. The integral is hypersingular, it diverges classically. In
[
3
]
, pp.23-31, this classically divergent integral is defined,
the integral equation is solved explicitly, the Laplace solution of this equation is
L(h)=\frac{L(f)}{1+c_{1}p^{\frac{1}{4}}}
, where
c_{1}:=|\Gamma(-\frac{1}{4})|>0
.
If
f=f(t)
is smooth and rapidly decaying when
t\to\infty
, then
|L(h)|\leq\frac{c}{1+|p|},\,|p|\gg 1,\,\,Re\,p\geq 0
. One proves that this
L(h)

satisfies assumptions of Theorem 1,
L(h)
is analytic in the half-plane
\sigma>0
, so it is indeed a Laplace transform of a function. One also sees that

\lim_{|p|\to\infty}p^{\frac{5}{4}}L(h)=0
. Therefore,
f(0)=0
, see Lemma 1 below.

The question of practical interest is: can one prove that the solution
h=h(t)
is a uniformly bounded function on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
h(0)=0
? Theorem 1 and
Lemma 1, proved below in item c), allow to answer both questions.

b) Assume that
\sigma_{0}=0
and
F(is)\in L^{\ell}({\mathbb{R}}):=L_{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 1
, where
L_{\ell}
are the Lebesgue spaces,

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(is)|^{\ell}ds<\infty.

(2.1)

If
\ell\in[1,2]
, then the Fourier transform
f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(is)e^{-ist}ds
belongs to
L_{\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}}
, see
[
2
]
, p.165.
One has the Hausdorff-Young estimate
[
2
]
, p.165:

\|f(t)\|_{L_{\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}}}\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}}\|F\|_{L_{\ell}}.

(2.2)

Therefore, if
\ell\in[1,2]
, then
f(t)
is a function. If
\ell>2
, then
f
may be a tempered distribution, see
[
2
]
, pp.163-164.

c)
Example.
Let
F(p)=\frac{1}{(1+p)p^{\frac{1}{4}}},\,p=\sigma+is,\,\sigma>0
. This function is analytic in the half-plane
\sigma>0
and all the
conditions of Theorem 1 are satisfied. Therefore, this function is a Laplace transform of
f
,
F(p)=L(f)
. Since
F(is)\in L_{1}
, the function
f(t)
is uniformly bounded.

Since
\lim_{|p|\to\infty}|p|^{b}F(p)=0
,
b=\frac{5}{4}>1
, we conclude that
f(0)=0
.

This result is a consequence of the following Lemma 1 in which the conditions of Theorem 1 are assumed to hold without explicitly repeating them and it is also assumed
that
\sigma_{0}=0
and
F(is)\in L_{1}
.

Lemma 1. If
\sigma>0
,
b>1
,
F(p)
satisfies assumptions of Theorem 1 and
\lim_{|p|\to\infty}|p|^{b}|F(p)|=0
, then
f(0)=0
.

First proof of Lemma 1.
By Theorem 1, we have formula (
1.10
). Write this formula as

F(p)=(\int_{0}^{\epsilon}+\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty})e^{-pt}f(t)dt:=I_{1}+I_{2},

(2.3)

where
\epsilon>0
is a small number which will be chosen later. Let
p>0
tend to
\infty
. Since
f(t)
is uniformly bounded, one has:

|I_{2}|\leq c\frac{e^{-\epsilon p}}{p},

(2.4)

and

|I_{1}|\leq c\frac{1-e^{-\epsilon p}}{p}f(0)(1+O(\epsilon p)),

(2.5)

because for small
\epsilon
, continuous
f(t)
and
f(0)\neq 0
one has
f(t)=f(0)(1+O(\epsilon p))
.

Choose
\epsilon=\frac{1}{p^{1/2}},\,p\to+\infty
. Then
\lim_{p\to\infty}|p|^{b}I_{2}=0
. Since Since
b>1
, we have
\lim_{p\to\infty}|p|^{b}F(p)=0
.
Therefore,
\lim_{p\to\infty}|p|^{b}I_{1}=0
. This can happen only if
f(0)=0
, see (
2.5
).

Lemma 1 is proved.

Second proof of Lemma 1.
By formula (
1.9
) with
\sigma_{0}=0
and
t=0
one has

f(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(q)ds=0.

(2.6)

Indeed,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{C_{n}}F(q)dq=0,

(2.7)

because
b>1
. By analyticity of
F
one has

\int_{L_{n}}F(q)dq=0.

(2.8)

From (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) passing to the limit
n\to\infty
one derives formula (
2.6
).

The second proof of Lemma 1 is complete.

d) Let
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=(0,\infty),\,{\mathbb{R}}=(-\infty,\infty)
,
p=\sigma+is
. Consider the following result of the type of a Paley-Wiener (PW) theorem, see
[
4
]
, p.405.

Proposition 1. Suppose
F=F(p)
is given by formula (
1.2
) with
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
. Then
F
is analytic in the half-plane
\sigma>0
and

\sup_{\sigma\geq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(p)|^{2}ds\leq c
.

Conversely, if
F
is analytic in the half-plane
\sigma>0
and

\sup_{\sigma\geq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(p)|^{2}ds\leq c
, then
F
is given by formula (
1.2
) with
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
.

Proof of Proposition 1.
Let
p=\sigma+is=r(\cos\phi+i\sin\phi),\,r=|p|
. For any fixed
\phi\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
one has by the Cauchy inequality:

|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-pt}f(t)dt|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{2}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2r\cos\phi\,t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{2}dt\frac{1}{2r\cos\phi}.

(2.9)

Thus,
\lim_{|p|\to\infty}F(p)=0
for any
\phi
not equal to
\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
. For
\phi=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
, one has
F(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ist}f(t)dt:=\mathcal{F}(f)
, where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform. Since
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, there is a sequence
s_{j}\to\infty
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}F(is_{j})=0
. By the Parseval identity one has:

\sup_{\sigma\geq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(\sigma+is)|^{2}ds=2\pi\sup_{\sigma\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}|e^{-\sigma t}f(t)|^{2}dt\leq 2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{2}dt\leq c.

(2.10)

Therefore, the first part of Proposition 1 is proved.

To prove the second part, assume that
F
is analytic in the half-plane
\sigma>0
and

\sup_{\sigma\geq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(p)|^{2}ds\leq c.

(2.11)

From formula (
2.11
) it follows that the limit
\lim_{\sigma\to 0}F(p)=F(is)
exists a.e. (for almost every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
) and condition (
1.1
) holds. By Theorem 1 formula (
1.2
) holds and by formula (
2.10
)
\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{2}dt\leq c.

Proposition 1 is proved.

e) Assume that
F(p)=e^{\frac{1}{p^{2}}}
. Is
F(p)
a Laplace transform of a function? Let us prove that the function
e^{\frac{1}{p^{2}}}
is not a Laplace transform of any function
f(t)
such that
f=0
for
t<0
and
\int_{0}^{\ell}|f(t)|^{\ell}dt<c
,
\ell\geq 1
. Let
p=\sigma+is=\sigma>0
.
Suppose to the contrary that
e^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma t}f(t)dt
. We have

e^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma t}f(t)dt\leq(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{\ell}dt)^{\frac{1}{\ell}}(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\ell^{\prime}\sigma t}dt)^{\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}}\leq\frac{c}{(\ell^{\prime}\sigma)^{\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}}},

(2.12)

where the Hölder inequality is used and
\ell^{\prime}
is defined by the formula
\frac{1}{\ell}+\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}=1
.
When
\sigma\to 0
the right side of (
2.12
) tends to infinity less fast than the left side. Therefore, the first equality sign in equation (
2.12
) is impossible.

3
Conclusion

Sufficient conditions are given for a function
F(p)
to be the Laplace transform of
f(t)
. No assumptions about
f(t)
are imposed.

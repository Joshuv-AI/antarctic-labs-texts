Cyclotomic fields are generated by cyclotomic Hecke L -values of totally real fields, II
Jaesung kwon, Hae-Sang Sun

Abstract
. Jun-Lee-Sun [ 4 ] posed the question of whether the cyclotomic Hecke field can be generated by a single critical L L -value of a cyclotomic Hecke character over a totally real field. They provided an answer to this question in the case where the tame Hecke character is trivial. In this paper, we extend their work to address the case of non-trivial Hecke characters over solvable totally real number fields. Our approach builds upon the primary estimation obtained by Jun-Lee-Sun [ 4 ] , supplemented with new inputs, including global class field theory, duality principles, the analytic behavior of partial Hecke L L -functions, and the non-vanishing of twisted Gauss sums and Hyper Kloosterman sums.

Instead of modular forms, one can consider GL(1) automorphic forms over a totally real field

A ray class character

Let

\mathfrak{m}

Let

Then, Jun-Lee-Sun
[

[

]

[

For almost all totally even cyclotomic characters

Jun-Lee-Sun
[

To solve this problem, they first show that

\text{``average'' of }L_{K}(0,\chi)=1+o(1)

The main ingredients are given as follows:

(1)

non-singular cone decomposition of the fundamental domain of the real Minkowski space of

(2)

the square root cancellation on the exponential sum on the unit group.

This method can also be applied to the average of critical
L

(Theorem

Let us define the

By applying the estimation provided in Theorem

(1)

Does

Unfortunately, the norm average

1.1.
Partial Hecke
L
-function

Instead of computing the norm average directly, we take an alternative approach. Let us define a
partial Hecke
L
-function
as follows:

L_{K}^{\circ}(s,\eta):=\sum_{m^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\ (p^{2})}\sum_{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a})=m}\frac{\eta(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}=\sum_{m^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\ (p^{2})}\frac{c_{\eta}(m)}{m^{s}},

where
m
runs through the positive integers such that
m^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\ (p^{2})
.
Let us denote by
c_{\eta}^{\circ}
the restriction of
c_{\eta}
to the set of positive integers
m>0
satisfying
m^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\ (p^{2})
.
Let us present our critical observation:

Observation
.

c_{\eta}^{\circ}
determines at least “the restriction” of
\eta_{\rm f}

if the “twisted average of Gauss sums” is non-vanishing.

Let us describe this in detail. Let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be ray class characters over
K
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.
Note that
c_{\eta_{1}}^{\circ}=c_{\eta_{2}}^{\circ}
if and only if

(1)

L_{K}^{\circ}\big(s,\eta_{1}\cdot(\omega^{j}\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})\big)=L_{K}^{\circ}\big(s,\eta_{2}\cdot(\omega^{j}\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})\big)\text{ for any }j.

Due to the significant properties of partial Hecke
L
-functions, namely

(a)

Duality:

L_{K}^{\circ}(s,*)\longleftrightarrow L_{K}^{\circ}(1-s,\overline{*}),

(b)

Analytic behavior of
L_{K}^{\circ}(s,*)
,

we can extract valuable information from equation (
1
) that

\big(\eta_{1,\mathrm{f}}(q)-\eta_{2,\mathrm{f}}(q)\big)\sum_{1\neq\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}((\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times},\mu_{p})}\tau(\omega^{j}\psi\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})\omega^{j}\psi\big(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})\big)=0,

for almost all
q
and any
j
(see the proof of Proposition
4.8
).
If we can show that the twisted average of Gauss sums

(2)

\sum_{1\neq\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}((\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times},\mu_{p})}\tau(\omega^{j}\psi\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})\psi\big(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})\big)

is non-vanishing for some
j
, then
\eta_{1,\mathrm{f}}(q)=\eta_{2,\mathrm{f}}(q)
for almost all rational primes
q
.

In conclusion, the non-vanishing of the twisted average of Gauss sums provides a partial answer to Subquestion JLS.

1.2.
Gauss sums and global class field theory

The average (
2
) can be rewritten as follows:

\sum_{\psi\neq 1}\tau(\omega^{j}\psi\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})\psi\big(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})\big)=p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in(O_{K}/p^{2}O_{K})^{\times}\\
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha\mathfrak{m})d_{K}\equiv 1\ (p^{2})\end{subarray}}\omega^{j}(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)d_{K})\mathbf{e}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)}{p^{2}}\Big),

where
d_{K}
is the discriminant of
K
and
\alpha
runs over mod
p^{2}O_{K}
with
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha\mathfrak{m})d_{K}\equiv 1\ (p^{2})
. This sum is very difficult to study due to the non-trivial relationship between the trace
\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
and the norm
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
, except when
K=\mathbb{Q}
.

At this moment, we impose a new assumption: Assume that
K
is solvable, i.e.,
K
is a Galois number field that can be exhausted by a sequence of abelian extensions.
Then, for a non-trivial character
\phi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}
, we obtain that the Gauss sum
\tau(\phi\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})
can be expressed as the product of Gauss sums
\tau(\phi)
of Dirichlet characters
\phi
by using the duality and the decomposition of
L_{K}(s,\phi\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})
, which is a part of global class field theory. More precisely,

(3)

\tau(\phi\circ N_{K/\mathbb{Q}})=C\phi(d_{K})\tau(\phi)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}

for some non-zero constant
C
depends only on
K
and the exponent of the conductor of
\phi
, where
d_{K}
is the discriminant of
K
(see Proposition
4.2
). Note that a power of the Gauss sum of a Dirichlet character is easier to understand than the Gauss sum of a ray class character. Let us explain this in detail at the next subsection.

1.3.
Higher power of Gauss sums and Hyper Kloosterman sums

It is well-known that high-powers of the Gauss sum of a Dirichlet character are related to
hyper Kloosterman sums
.
For
M>0
and
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, let us define hyper Kloosterman sums of dimension
d>0
and of modulus

as follows:

\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(r;M):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\cdots,n_{d}\in(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\\
n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}\equiv r(M)\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}\Big(\frac{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}}{M}\Big).

Gurak
[
3
]
computed the explicit values of

when

.
Especially, for

, the Kloosterman sum
\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(r;p^{n})
is non-vanishing if

.
If we assume that

is solvable, then the

-weighted average of the sum
(\ref{intro:gausssum:average})
is given by

for some

, where

. This implies that the average

is non-vanishing for some

(see Proposition
4.5
).

1.5.
Selberg orthonormality and Langlands functoriality

One possible approach to giving the complete answer to Question JLS is through the Joint universality of Hecke
L
-functions.
Let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be tame ray class characters over
K
. Then,

c_{\eta_{1}}^{\prime}=c_{\eta_{2}}^{\prime}

if and only if

(4)

L_{K}(s,\eta_{1}\chi)=L_{K}(s,\eta_{2}\chi)

for any cyclotomic characters
\chi
over
K
modulo a
p
-power.
If the joint universality holds for Hecke
L
-functions, then equation (
4
) implies that
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}
, thereby legitimating Question JLS.
It is well known that if the following quantities

(5)

\sum_{q\leq x}c_{\eta_{i}}(q)\overline{c_{\eta_{j}}(q)}

for
i,j=1,2
satisfying some “good” estimation on
x
, then
L_{K}(s,\eta_{1})
and
L_{K}(s,\eta_{2})
are jointly universal (see Lee-Nakamura-Pańkowski
[
5
]
).

Let
d=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. If
K
is solvable, then by the Langlands functoriality for solvable induction proved by Arthur-Clozel
[
2
, Section 6]
, the induced representation
\pi_{\eta}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{GL_{1}(\mathbb{A}_{K})}^{GL_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})}(\eta)
is automorphic, whose Satake parameter is given by

(\eta\circ g)_{g\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})},

where
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
acts on the ray class groups.
From this, some “twisted sum” of the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{\eta_{1}}\times\pi_{\overline{\eta_{2}}})
can be written as follows:

\text{``twisted sum'' of }L(s,\pi_{\eta_{i}}\times\pi_{\overline{\eta_{j}}})=\sum_{m>0}\frac{c_{\eta_{i}}(m)\overline{c_{\eta_{j}}(m)}}{m^{s}}.

If we take the logarithmic derivative of the above equation and estimate it using the analytic properties of
L(s,\pi_{\eta_{i}}\times\pi_{\overline{\eta_{j}}})
, as is done for the Riemann
\zeta
-function to prove the prime number theorem, we may obtain an estimation for equation (
5
).
It is one of the author’s future projects that proving the generation problem by adopting this method.

2.1.
Notation and definition

Let us list notations and definitions that are frequently used in the paper.

•

For an integer

is denoted by
\mu_{M}
.

•

For an odd prime

, the torsion part of

is denoted by
\mu_{p-1}
. Hence, we have the decomposition
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}=\mu_{p-1}\cdot(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})

•

For

, we set
\mathbf{e}(x):=\exp(2\pi ix)
.

•

The number field

is totally real and of degree
d=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. The ring of integers in

is denoted by

with the different
\mathfrak{d}_{K}
and the discriminant
d_{K}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{d}_{K})
. The set of totally positive units of

is denoted by

.

•

In this paper,

.

•

Every ray class group
{\rm Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{m})
for an integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is defined in the strict sense, i.e., quotient of the set of ideals prime to
\mathfrak{m}
by the set of totally positive elements that congruent to

modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.

•

The Minkowski space
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
is denoted by
K_{\mathbb{R}}
. The set of totally positive elements in
K_{\mathbb{R}}
is denoted by
K_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. For any subset
B\subseteq K_{\mathbb{R}}
, we set
B^{+}:=B\cap K_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
.

•

We set
K_{p}:=K\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
O_{p}:=O_{K}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. By C.R.T., we have
O_{p}=\prod_{\wp|p}O_{\wp}
for a prime
\wp
above

and the integer ring
O_{\wp}
of
K_{\wp}
.

•

Let
N:O_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the extension of
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
. We use the same symbol for
N:{\rm Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and

•

Let
\Tr=\sum_{\wp|p}\Tr_{K_{\wp}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
be the trace of

.

2.2.
Ray class characters of totally real fields

Let
\xi
be a ray class character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.
Define
\xi_{\infty}:K_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}\to S^{1}
as the composition

\xi_{\infty}:K_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}\rightarrow K_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}/K_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{\mathfrak{m}}/K_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}\hookrightarrow{\rm Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{m})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{1},

where
K_{\mathfrak{m}}=\{\alpha\in K\mid\alpha\equiv 1\left({\rm mod\,}\mathfrak{m}\right)\}
.
Define
\xi_{\rm f}:(O_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\to S^{1}
such that for
\alpha\in O_{K}
, we set

(6)

\displaystyle\xi_{\rm f}(\alpha):=\xi(\alpha O_{K})\xi_{\infty}(\alpha)^{-1}.

In particular, we have
\xi(\alpha O_{K})=\xi_{\rm f}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in K^{+}
with
(\alpha,\mathfrak{m})=1.

2.3.
Gauss sums of ray class characters

Let
\xi
be a ray class character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.
Let
\beta
be an element of
\mathfrak{m}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}_{K}^{-1}\cap K^{+}
with
(\beta\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{d}_{K},\mathfrak{m})=1
.
The Gauss sum of
\xi
is defined as

\displaystyle\tau(\xi):=\xi(\beta\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{d}_{K})\sum_{\alpha\in(O_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}}\xi_{\rm f}(\alpha)\mathbf{e}(\Tr(\alpha\beta))

The condition
\beta\in K^{+}
is required to ensure that the definition is independent of the choice of
\beta
. When
\xi
is primitive, the Gauss sum satisfies

|\tau(\xi)|^{2}=N(\mathfrak{m})\mbox{ and }\tau(\xi)\tau(\overline{\xi})=\xi_{\rm f}(-1)N(\mathfrak{m}).

2.4.
Functional equation of Hecke
L
-functions of totally real fields

The Hecke
L
-function for a ray class character
\xi
is the meromorphic continuation of an
L
-series given by

L_{K}(s,\xi):=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\xi(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}

for
\Re(s)>1
, where
\mathfrak{a}
runs through the integral ideals of
K
. Note that we conventionally set
\xi(\mathfrak{a})=0
if
\mathfrak{a}
is not coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
. Let
d_{1},d_{2}
be the number of
1
,
-1
in the signature of
\xi
, respectively.
Then the infinite part is given as

L_{\infty}(s,\xi):=\pi^{-\frac{d_{1}s}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}\Big)^{d_{1}}\pi^{-\frac{d_{2}(s+1)}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+1}{2}\Big)^{d_{2}}.

The complete Hecke
L
-function is the product

\Lambda_{K}(s,\xi)=d_{K}^{\frac{s}{2}}N(\mathfrak{m})^{\frac{s}{2}}L_{K}(s,\xi)L_{\infty}(s,\xi).

The function
\Lambda_{K}(s,\xi)
continues analytically to the whole
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies the following functional equation

\Lambda_{K}(s,\xi)=W(\xi)\Lambda_{K}(1-s,\overline{\xi})

where

\displaystyle W(\xi)

\displaystyle:=i^{-d_{2}}\frac{\tau(\xi)}{\sqrt{N(\mathfrak{m})}}

(cf. Theorem 8.5 in Neukirch
[
7
]
).
If
\xi
is totally odd, then we obtain that

(7)

L_{K}(0,\overline{\xi})=\frac{\sqrt{d_{K}}}{(\pi i)^{d}}\tau(\overline{\xi})L_{K}(1,\xi)

by putting
s=1
.
As the partial zeta value for a ray class at the critical point
0
is a rational number (see Neukirch
[
7
, Theorem 9.8]
), we have
L_{K}(0,\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi)
and for
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}),

(8)

L_{K}(0,\xi)^{\sigma}=L_{K}(0,\xi^{\sigma}).

From (
7
) and (
8
), we have the following reciprocity law:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma\in{\rm\operatorname{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
and
\xi
be a totally odd ray class character. Then one has

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{d_{K}}\tau(\overline{\xi})L_{K}(1,\xi)}{(\pi i)^{d}}\right)^{\sigma}=\frac{\sqrt{d_{K}}\tau(\overline{\xi}^{\sigma})L_{K}(1,\xi^{\sigma})}{(\pi i)^{d}}.

Let

In this section, we prove that the field generated by a single cyclotomic Hecke

\eta

Here recall that
p>2
is unramified in
K
,
\eta
is a totally odd primitive ray class character over

modulo
\mathfrak{m}

Definition 3.1
.

Let us define the norm average function

attached to

c_{\eta}(m):=\sum_{N(\mathfrak{a})=m}\eta(\mathfrak{a}).

Let us provide the following notations for simplicity:

(1)

Let us denote by

the restriction of the function
c_{\eta}
to the set of positive integers coprime to

.

(2)

Let us denote by

modulo a

-power.

Based on the estimation of Theorem

Proposition 3.2

For almost all
\chi\in\Xi_{K,p}

F(L_{K}(0,\eta\chi),\chi)=F(c_{\eta}^{\prime},\chi).

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\Xi_{K,p}
of modulus

with sufficiently large

Let us denote by

in this proof.

L_{K}(s,\eta\chi)=\sum_{m>0}\frac{c_{\eta}(m)\psi(m)}{m^{s}}

and

From this, we conclude that

For a normal subgroup

over

.
Then, by the reciprocity (
8
), we have

(12)

L_{K}(0,\eta\chi)=L_{K}(0,\eta\chi)^{\sigma}=L_{K}(0,\eta^{\sigma}\chi)

for

.
Note that

.
Thus, by taking the average to equation (

(which is possible as

. So we are done.
∎

Let

Let us denote by

\mathfrak{r}

Immediate from the definition of Gauss sums and straight computations.

Let us assume that

Let

be a cyclotomic character over

and

Proof.

For simplicity, let us provide the following notations in this proof:
Let
G_{i}
be the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})

G_{i}^{*}
the group of primitive ray class characters over
K_{i-1}
associated to the dual of

Let us denote by

Let

Note that

d_{K_{i}}=N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{i})d_{K_{i-1}}^{|G_{i}|}.

We keep using this relation in the proof.

From the global class field theory, we observe the following decomposition of

L_{K_{i}}(s,\chi_{i})=\prod_{\phi\in G_{i}^{*}}L_{K_{i-1}}(s,\chi_{i-1}\phi)

Note that the characters

is totally real, thus,

(13)

\Lambda_{K_{i}}(s,\chi_{i})=N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{i})^{\frac{s}{2}}\prod_{\phi\in G_{i}^{*}}N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\phi})^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Lambda_{K_{i-1}}(s,\chi_{i-1}\phi),

where

is the conductor of
\phi

By the functional equation of

(14)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{K_{i}}(s,\chi_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau(\chi_{i})}{(i^{\delta}p^{n/2})^{|G_{i}|}}\Lambda_{K_{i}}(1-s,\overline{\chi_{i}}),

(15)

\displaystyle\prod_{\phi\in G_{i}^{*}}\Lambda_{K_{i-1}}(s,\chi_{i-1}\phi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\phi\in G_{i}^{*}}\frac{\tau(\chi_{i-1}\phi)\Lambda_{K_{i-1}}(1-s,\overline{\chi_{i-1}\phi})}{(i^{\delta}p^{n/2})^{|G_{i-1}|}N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\phi})^{1/2}},

where

Dividing equation (

Since the right hand side is independent on

N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{i})=\prod_{\phi}N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{f}_{\phi}),\ \tau(\chi_{i})=N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{i})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{\phi}\tau(\chi_{i-1}\phi).

Applying Lemma
4.1
to the above equation, we obtain that

\tau(\chi_{i})=\Big(N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{i})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi\big(N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{i})\big)\prod_{\phi}\tau(\phi)\phi_{\rm f}(p^{n})\Big)\tau(\chi_{i-1})^{|G_{i}|}.

Finally, we conclude from the mathematical induction that

\tau(\chi)=d_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi(d_{K})\tau(\psi)^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Big(\prod_{\phi\in G_{i}^{*}}\tau(\phi)\phi_{\rm f}(p^{n})\Big)^{[K:K_{i}]}.

So we are done.
∎

Gurak

:

Lemma 4.3

(Gurak
[
3
, Lemma 1,2]
)
.

\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(r;p^{2})
is non-vanishing if and only if

r^{\frac{p-1}{(d,p-1)}}\equiv 1\ (p).

Remark 4.4
.

If
d=2
, then
\mathrm{Kl}_{2}(r,p^{2})\neq 0
if and only if
\big(\frac{r}{p}\big)=1
by
Lemma
4.3
, where
\big(\frac{\cdot}{p}\big)
is the quadratic residue symbol.

By applying the non-vanishing of hyper Kloosterman sums and the decomposition of Gauss sums, we prove the non-vanishing of twisted average of Gauss sums when
K
is solvable:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\omega:(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\mu_{p-1}
be the Teichmüller character.
Let
\psi
be a generator of a cyclic group
\mathrm{Hom}\big((\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times},\mu_{p}\big)
.
Let
m
be an integer coprime to
p
. If
K
is solvable, then

\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\tau(\psi^{i}\omega^{j}\circ N)\psi^{i}(m)\neq 0

for some
j=1,\cdots,p-1
.

Proof.

If
p\mid i
, then clearly
\tau(\psi^{i}\omega^{j}\circ N)=0
by Proposition
4.2
since
\psi^{i}\omega^{j}
is imprimitive so that
\tau(\psi^{i}\omega^{j})^{2}=0
. Thus, it is enough to show that
\sum_{i=1}^{p}\tau(\psi^{i}\omega^{j}\circ N)\psi^{i}(m)\neq 0
.

It is well known that for
\phi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}
,

\tau(\phi)^{d}=\sum_{r=1}^{p^{2}}\phi(r)\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(r;p^{2}).

Let us choose an element
\kappa
of
\mu_{p-1}
such that
\kappa md_{K}\equiv 1\ (p)
. Note that
\psi(\kappa)=1
.
Then, based on the orthogonality and Proposition
4.2
, we verify that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\omega^{j}(\kappa m)\sum_{i=1}^{p}\tau(\psi^{i}\omega^{j}\circ N)\psi^{i}(m)

\displaystyle=C\sum_{i,j}\tau(\psi^{i}\omega^{j})^{d}\psi^{i}\omega^{j}(\kappa md_{K})

\displaystyle=C\sum_{\phi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}}\sum_{r=1}^{p^{2}}\phi(\kappa md_{K}r)\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(r;p^{2})

\displaystyle=Cp(p-1)\mathrm{Kl}_{d}\big((\kappa md_{K})^{-1};p^{2}\big)

for some non-zero number
C
, where
a^{-1}
is the inverse of
a
mod
p^{2}
.
Since
\kappa md_{K}\equiv 1\ (p)
, we have
\mathrm{Kl}_{d}\big((\kappa md_{K})^{-1};p^{2}\big)\neq 0
by Lemma
4.3
, so we are done.
∎

4.3.
Partial Hecke
L
-functions

Let us recall that
\eta
is a primitive ray class character over
K
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
, and the norm average function
c_{\eta}:\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\to\mathbb{Z}[\eta]
attached to
\eta
is given by

c_{\eta}(m)=\sum_{N(\mathfrak{a})=m}\eta(\mathfrak{a}).

Then, we can easily see that

(16)

L_{K}(s,\eta\cdot\big(\lambda\circ N)\big)=\sum_{m>0}\frac{\lambda(m)c_{\eta}(m)}{m^{s}}

for any Dirichlet character
\lambda
.
Let
\omega:(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\mu_{p-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the Teichmüller character.
Let
\langle\cdot\rangle
be the canonical projection defined by

\langle\cdot\rangle:(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\frac{1+p\mathbb{Z}}{1+p^{2}\mathbb{Z}},\ a\mapsto a\cdot\omega^{-1}(a).

Definition 4.6
.

For a Hecke character
\xi
over
K
, let us define a partial Hecke
L
-function as follows:

L^{\circ}_{K}(s,\xi):=\sum_{\langle m\rangle\neq 1}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{a})=m}\frac{\xi(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}=\sum_{\langle m\rangle\neq 1}\frac{c_{\xi}(m)}{m^{s}},

where
m
and
\mathfrak{a}
runs through the positive integers
m
with
\langle m\rangle\neq 1
and the integral ideals of
K
with given norm
m
, respectively.

In this section, let us denote by
\psi
a generator of the following cyclic group:

\mathrm{Hom}\big((\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times},\mu_{p}\big).

We can express the partial Hecke
L
-function as a twisted average of Hecke
L
-functions:

Proposition 4.7
.

For any
j
,

L^{\circ}_{K}\big(s,\eta\cdot(\omega^{j}\circ N)\big)=\frac{p-1}{p}L_{K}\big(s,\eta\cdot(\omega^{j}\circ N)\big)-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}L_{K}\big(s,\eta\cdot(\psi^{i}\omega^{j}\circ N)\big).

Proof.

Immediate by the following orthogonality relation

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\psi^{i}(m)=\begin{cases}p-1&\mbox{ if }\langle m\rangle=1\\
-1&\mbox{ if }\langle m\rangle\neq 1\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise, i.e., }p\mid m\end{cases}

and equation (
16
).
∎

By utilizing the properties of partial
L
-functions, we obtain a result toward determination of the tame character
\eta
by the norm average
c_{\eta}
:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
A
be a finite set of totally odd primitive ray class characters over
K
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.
Let
a_{\eta}
be constants satisfying

\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}c_{\eta}(m)=0

for any positive integers
m
such that
\langle m\rangle\neq 1
.
Let us assume that
K
is solvable and
p
does not divide
N(\mathfrak{m})
. Then

\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2})\text{ and }\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2}q)=0

for any odd rational primes
q
does not divide
pN(\mathfrak{m})
.

Proof.

In this proof, let us denote by
\varpi=\omega\circ N
and
\chi=\psi\circ N
, which are primitive cyclotomic characters over
K
modulo
p
and
p^{2}
by Proposition
2.3
, respectively.
From Proposition
4.7
, equation (
16
) and our assumption, we obtain that

(17)

\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}L_{K}(s,\eta\varpi^{j})=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}L_{K}(s,\eta\chi^{i}\varpi^{j})

for any
j
.
If
p-1\nmid j
, then by the functional equation of Hecke
L
-functions,

\displaystyle p^{ds}\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta\varpi^{j})L_{K}(1-s,\overline{\eta\varpi^{j}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i}\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta\chi^{i}\varpi^{j})L_{K}(1-s,\overline{\eta\chi^{i}\varpi^{j}}).

If
p-1\mid j
, then
\eta\varpi^{j}
is imprimitive and
\eta
is a primitive character which induces an imprimitive character
\eta\varpi^{j}
, so we obtain the following:

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{p^{2ds}}{L_{p}(s,\eta)}\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta)L_{K}(1-s,\overline{\eta})=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta\chi^{i})L_{K}(1-s,\overline{\eta\chi^{i}}),

where
L_{p}(s,\eta)
is the Euler factor of Hecke
L
of
\eta
dividing
p
:

L_{p}(s,\eta)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\Big(1-\frac{\eta(\mathfrak{p})}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{s}}\Big)^{-1}.

Note that

p^{2ds}L_{p}(s,\eta)^{-1}=o(1)\text{ and }L_{K}(1-s,*)=1+o(1)\text{ as }s\rightarrow-\infty.

By taking the limit
s\rightarrow-\infty
to equation (
17
), equation (
18
) and applying Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta\chi^{i}\varpi^{j})=\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2})\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\tau(\chi^{i}\varpi^{j})\chi^{i}\varpi^{j}(\mathfrak{m})

for any
j
. Thus, by Proposition
4.5
, we obtain the following equation:

(19)

\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2})=0.

Let
q\nmid pN(\mathfrak{m})
be an odd rational prime. Let
\omega_{q}
be a non-trivial even primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q
. Let us denote by
\varpi_{q}=\omega_{q}\circ N
, which is an even primitive character over
K
modulo
q
by Proposition
2.3
. Note that

\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}c_{\eta\varpi_{q}}(m)=\omega_{q}(m)\sum_{\eta}a_{\eta}c_{\eta}(m)=0.

Thus, we can replace
A
by
\{\eta\varpi_{q}\mid\eta\in A\}
of equation
(
19
), obtaining that

\tau(\varpi_{q})\varpi_{q}(p^{2}\mathfrak{m})\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2}q)=\sum_{\eta\in A}a_{\eta}\tau(\eta\varpi_{q})(\eta\varpi_{q})_{\rm f}(p^{2})=0

by Lemma
4.1
. So we are done.
∎

From this, we can prove Lemma
3.4
, which plays a crucial role to show the non-vanishing (Proposition
3.5
) and cyclotomic field generation result (Proposition
3.6
):

Proof of
Lemma
3.4
.

Assume the contrary, i.e.,
c_{\eta}(m)=0
for any
m
with
\langle m\rangle\neq 1
, or equivalently,
L_{K}^{\circ}(s,\eta)=0
. Set
A=\{\eta\}
in Proposition
4.8
, then we obtain
\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2})=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Let us denote by
c_{\eta}^{\circ}
the restriction of
c_{\eta}
to the set of positive integers
m
with
\langle m\rangle\neq 1
.
Let us denote by
\eta_{\rm f}^{\prime}
the restriction of
\eta_{\rm f}
to the set of odd rational primes
q
such that
q\nmid pN(\mathfrak{m})
.
From Proposition
4.8
, we obtain the following field generation result:

Theorem 4.9
.

Let us assume that
K
is solvable and
p
does not divide
N(\mathfrak{m})
. Then,

\mathbb{Q}(\eta_{\rm f}^{\prime})\subset\mathbb{Q}(c_{\eta}^{\circ}).

Proof.

Let us choose
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}\big(\mathbb{Q}(c_{\eta}^{\circ},\eta)/\mathbb{Q}(c_{\eta}^{\circ})\big)
. Then, by Proposition

4.8
,

\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2})=\tau(\eta^{\sigma})\eta_{\rm f}^{\sigma}(p^{2})\text{ and }\tau(\eta)\eta_{\rm f}(p^{2}q)=\tau(\eta^{\sigma})\eta^{\sigma}_{\rm f}(p^{2}q)

for odd rational primes
q\nmid pN(\mathfrak{m})
.
From this, we conclude that
\eta_{\rm f}(q)=\eta^{\sigma}_{\rm f}(q)
for such primes
q
, which implies that
\sigma
fixes
\mathbb{Q}(\eta_{\rm f}^{\prime})
. Since
\sigma
is arbitrary, we are done.
∎

5.
Main result

Let us recall that
\eta
is a totally odd primitive ray class character over
K
of modulus
\mathfrak{m}
.
Let us recall our main question:

Question JLS
.

For almost all totally even primitive cyclotomic characters
\chi
over
K
modulo a
p
-power, does the following equality hold?

F\big(L_{K}(0,\eta\chi)\big)=F(\eta,\chi).

We prove a result toward the main question:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let us assume that
K
is solvable and
p
does not divide
N(\mathfrak{m})
. Then,

F(\eta_{\rm f}^{\prime},\chi)\subset F(c_{\eta}^{\prime},\chi)=F\big(L_{K}(0,\eta\chi)\big)\subset F(\eta,\chi)

for almost all totally even primitive cyclotomic characters
\chi
over
K
modulo a
p
-power.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{Q}(c_{f}^{\circ})\subset\mathbb{Q}(c_{f}^{\prime})
, it is immediate from Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
4.9
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Our assumption on the sign of characters
\eta
and
\chi
is important as
\eta\chi
is not totally odd if and only if
L(s,\eta\chi)=0
. Note that
\eta\chi
is totally odd if and only if either
\eta
is totally odd and
\chi
is totally even or
\eta
is totally even and
\chi
is totally odd, since
\chi
arises from a Dirichlet character. Obviously, one can repeat this paper for totally even tame character
\eta
and totally odd cyclotomic character
\chi
without any obstruction, which means that the present paper covers all the possible cases on
\eta
and
\chi
.

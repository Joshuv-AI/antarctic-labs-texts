Feature extraction of rolling bearings in cow dung briquetting machine based on CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO
Authors: Silu Ma, Risu Na, Nan Wang, Haoran Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the difficulty in identifying weak early fault impacts and fault characteristic frequencies of rolling bearings in vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines under strong background noise caused by low-speed, heavy-load operation and material compaction, this study proposes a denoising and fault feature extraction method based on the adaptive fusion of CEEMDAN, improved wavelet threshold denoising, and DBO. By constructing a kurtosis–permutation entropy composite evaluation index to select sensitive IMF components and using the DBO algorithm to adaptively optimize the adjustment factors in the improved threshold function, the proposed method effectively enhances bearing fault impulses under strong-noise conditions. Simulation results showed that, at an input signal-to-noise ratio of approximately − 15 dB, the proposed method increased the signal-to-noise ratio to − 1.400 dB and reduced the mean square error to 0.002152, outperforming single CEEMDAN and conventional wavelet threshold denoising. In measured bearing signals from a vertical cattle-manure briquetting machine with rolling-element spalling, the 8.5 Hz fault characteristic frequency and its harmonics were clearly extracted. The method provides an effective signal-processing approach for bearing condition monitoring and early fault diagnosis in agricultural and animal husbandry equipment.

Introduction

Vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines can compress loose cattle manure into high-density solid fuel, thereby improving conversion efficiency and reducing storage and transportation costs. They are therefore key equipment for the resource utilization of pastoral waste and are of considerable importance for the green and sustainable development of animal husbandry1,2. However, these machines often operate continuously for long periods under harsh conditions, including dust exposure, high humidity, and variable loads. Under such operating environments, rolling bearings, which are critical transmission components, are prone to early-stage defects such as wear and spalling. As fundamental components widely used in agricultural machinery3, rolling bearings directly affect the reliability of the whole machine. Sudden bearing failure may not only interrupt production and cause substantial economic losses, but may also disrupt fuel supply and thereby affect energy security in pastoral areas4,5.

Vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines are not high-precision equipment in the conventional sense, and the purpose of fault diagnosis for such machines is not to maintain micrometer-level motion accuracy. Instead, it is intended to improve the operational reliability, shutdown prevention, and maintenance decision-making of agricultural equipment. This type of equipment is usually deployed at livestock-breeding sites or biomass fuel preparation facilities, where the working environment is characterized by heavy dust, high humidity, considerable fluctuations in material composition, and frequent load impacts. Once the briquetting process is interrupted by faults in key transmission components, the efficiency of cattle-manure resource utilization is reduced, and additional costs are incurred in manual inspection, spare-part replacement, and production organization. Therefore, bearing condition monitoring for this type of agricultural machinery is of practical significance for improving equipment reliability and fault-warning capability.

Vibration signal analysis is a commonly used technique for bearing fault diagnosis. Nevertheless, under complex operating conditions and strong background noise, fault-induced impact components are usually weak and can be easily submerged by noise, making fault feature extraction difficult and reducing diagnostic accuracy6. This problem is particularly pronounced in low-speed agricultural and animal husbandry machinery such as vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines, where fault impacts are relatively inconspicuous. Therefore, extracting weak early fault features from strongly contaminated vibration signals has become an urgent problem for improving the reliability of condition monitoring in agricultural equipment.

Compared with conventional rotating machinery such as motors, gearboxes, and fans, the vibration signals of vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines exhibit more pronounced operating-condition characteristics of agricultural equipment. First, the machine operates at a relatively low rotational speed, and the bearing fault characteristic frequencies are usually located in the low-frequency range. As a result, the fault-induced impacts are weak and do not readily excite the clear high-frequency resonance responses that are commonly observed in traditional high-speed rotating machinery. Second, during the cattle-manure briquetting process, the moisture content, compactness, and feeding uniformity of the material vary continuously, leading to random load fluctuations and strong non-stationarity in the vibration signals. Third, the coupled effects of briquetting, extrusion, and transmission mechanisms introduce substantial non-fault impacts and broadband noise, which can easily mask the periodic impact components generated by early bearing faults such as spalling. Fourth, field conditions such as heavy dust, high humidity, and complex structural transmission paths further attenuate the local bearing fault information acquired by sensors. Therefore, the main challenge in fault diagnosis for this type of equipment lies in the reliable extraction of low-frequency and weakly periodic fault features under strong interference.

To accurately identify bearing fault features, various signal-processing methods have been developed. Lu7 constructed an improved threshold function by setting a relatively small threshold, so that the function approaches soft thresholding near the threshold point and hard thresholding away from it. However, the filtered signal still showed insufficient smoothness and some degree of distortion. Shan et al.8 combined empirical mode decomposition (EMD) with an improved wavelet threshold method. They classified intrinsic mode function (IMF) components using a variance-ratio criterion and then applied moving average filtering and an improved threshold function for signal reconstruction and denoising, thereby enhancing the retention of effective components in non-stationary signals. Peng et al.9 proposed a rolling-bearing vibration-signal denoising method based on HPO-VMD and improved wavelet thresholding. By combining adaptively optimized modal decomposition with an improved threshold function, their method reduced noise while limiting information loss; however, the parameter search interval and threshold-function adjustment factors still required multi-step determination. Shi et al.10 proposed a VMD-WT combined denoising method, in which variational mode decomposition (VMD) was used to decompose the signal, correlation coefficient thresholds were applied to select modal components, and wavelet threshold denoising was then employed to improve the signal-to-noise ratio. Nevertheless, its performance still depends on the appropriate selection of VMD parameters and thresholds. Taranenko Yu et al.11 developed a wavelet packet filtering algorithm that cyclically constrains the coefficients of a wavelet packet tree and searches for the optimal threshold and function according to the minimum root mean square error. Although this method outperformed common-threshold approaches for several types of signals, it requires exhaustive parameter optimization, involves high computational cost, and applies a shared threshold across different layers, which does not adequately reflect frequency-band differences. Tang et al.12 proposed a denoising method combining complete ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN) and wavelet thresholding. Their method improved denoising performance through IMF selection and wavelet-parameter optimization, but the wavelet basis and decomposition level still had to be determined through multi-index experiments, indicating that the adaptivity of the method could be further improved. These methods suppress noise in bearing vibration signals mainly through wavelet thresholding, modal decomposition, or their combinations. However, EMD and ensemble empirical mode decomposition (EEMD) are susceptible to modal aliasing and residual white noise13. In wavelet threshold denoising, although improved threshold functions can partly overcome the limitations of conventional hard and soft threshold functions, their parameters are still often determined empirically, which may introduce additional errors.

To address the denoising and fault feature extraction problems of rolling bearings in low-speed, heavy-load agricultural machinery such as vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines under strong-noise conditions, this study proposes a CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO fault feature extraction method. First, complete ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN) is used to adaptively decompose the vibration signal, and a composite evaluation index is designed to select feature components containing fault information. Then, improved wavelet threshold denoising (WTD) is applied to the selected feature components. Meanwhile, the dung beetle optimizer (DBO) is introduced to adaptively optimize the adjustment factors α and β in the improved threshold function, using the constructed composite evaluation index as the fitness function. Finally, the denoised components are reconstructed to achieve effective fault feature extraction under low-speed operating conditions.

Wavelet threshold denoising

Wavelet threshold denoising is a signal denoising method in which the original signal is decomposed into different scales through wavelet decomposition. The wavelet coefficients Wj are then thresholded, selected, or modified, and the denoised signal is finally obtained through wavelet reconstruction. This method can effectively suppress noise while preserving abrupt changes and impact features in the signal14. The procedure is shown in Fig. 1. The wavelet coefficient Wj consists of the effective signal component Uj and the noise component Vj , and can be expressed as Eq. By setting a critical threshold, the wavelet coefficients Wj at each scale are processed to obtain the estimated wavelet coefficients, so that the estimation error is minimized as much as possible. The processed wavelet coefficients are then used for signal reconstruction, and the reconstructed result is taken as the denoised signal. The threshold should be slightly higher than the maximum amplitude of the noise-related wavelet coefficients, so as to suppress noise while retaining as many effective signal components as possible. In this study, the threshold is determined according to the method reported in15:2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda = \sigma \sqrt {2lnN}$$\end{document}where σ is the standard deviation of noise, and N is the signal length. This threshold is derived from the joint distribution of multidimensional independent normally distributed variables under the Gaussian model. The standard deviation of noise is estimated as follows:3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma = \frac{MAD}{{0.6745}}$$\end{document}where MAD denotes the median absolute deviation of the amplitudes of the wavelet coefficients in all high-frequency subbands.

Although the Donoho universal threshold is theoretically derived based on the Gaussian white-noise assumption, the vibration noise of vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines is non-stationary and non-Gaussian in practical operating conditions. In this study, the threshold is still used for two reasons. First, after CEEMDAN decomposition, the residual noise in each IMF component is approximately whitened, and its statistical distribution becomes closer to Gaussian, which partially satisfies the basic assumptions of the universal threshold. Second, the DBO algorithm is further employed to adaptively optimize the adjustment factors α and β in the improved threshold function, thereby compensating, to some extent, for the threshold bias caused by the deviation of practical noise from the Gaussian assumption.

Traditional threshold functions mainly include soft-threshold and hard-threshold functions. As α increases, the proposed threshold function becomes closer to the hard-threshold function, which helps preserve the amplitudes of fault-induced impulses. Parameter β adjusts the continuity near the threshold point; increasing β can reduce the discontinuity of hard thresholding and mitigate the constant bias of soft thresholding. Through DBO-based optimization of α and β, large-amplitude coefficients are processed closer to hard thresholding to retain impulsive fault features, while small-amplitude coefficients are processed closer to soft thresholding to suppress noise. This threshold function remains continuous at the threshold point, thereby eliminating the discontinuity defect of the hard-threshold function. Meanwhile, it satisfies the property of an odd function, enabling consistent denoising of both positive and negative signal components. On this basis, the adjustable parameters α and β are introduced. By tuning these two parameters, the function can rapidly approach the hard-threshold function when the signal amplitude exceeds the threshold. Thus, the proposed function can preserve signal energy effectively while overcoming the constant deviation inherent in the soft-threshold function. This method is suitable for enhancing weak fault features with impact characteristics in vibration signals from agricultural machinery such as vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines.

CEEMDAN

The CEEMDAN algorithm is based on the theory of empirical mode decomposition (EMD)16. Its core idea is to adaptively introduce auxiliary white noise at each decomposition stage and to perform ensemble averaging on the decomposition results of the same order. This strategy effectively addresses the low decomposition efficiency and residual white-noise problems associated with EEMD, while further suppressing mode mixing. In this study, Nstd = 0.2 and NE = 100 were used. The parameter Nstd controls the ratio of the standard deviation of the added auxiliary white noise to that of the input signal. A small Nstd may be insufficient to suppress mode mixing, whereas an excessively large value may introduce residual noise. Therefore, Nstd was tested over the range of 0.1–0.3 with a step size of 0.05. The value of 0.2 was selected because it yielded the best orthogonality among the decomposed IMF components and the highest correlation with the original signal. The parameter NENENE determines the statistical stability of ensemble averaging. Based on the inverse square-root relationship between error and ensemble number, when NE ≥ 100, the amplitude fluctuation of each IMF was less than 5%. Further increasing NENENE only marginally improved the decomposition accuracy but considerably increased the computational cost. Thus, this parameter configuration provided an acceptable balance between mode-mixing suppression and residual-noise control.

For a clearer comparison of the local impact preservation ability of different decomposition methods, the reconstructed signals in Fig. 3 were robustly normalized and vertically shifted. The vertical offsets are used solely to distinguish the curves and do not indicate actual amplitude differences. To ensure a fair comparison, all four methods adopted the same sensitive-component selection criterion; specifically, the first three components with the smallest composite evaluation index G values were selected and then processed using the same WTD-DBO threshold denoising and reconstruction procedure.Fig. 3Comparison of fault-impact feature preservation ability among different decomposition methods.

Comparison of fault-impact feature preservation ability among different decomposition methods.

With VMD, EWT, and FIFD introduced as benchmark decomposition methods, the results indicate that CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO has good applicability in local impact preservation and fault feature enhancement. Although the different decomposition methods vary in computational efficiency and local waveform behavior, CEEMDAN still provides stable separation of fault-sensitive components for the low-speed heavy-load fault signals considered in this study, offering effective inpus for subsequent WTD-DBO denoising and envelope spectrum analysis.

The procedure of CEEMDAN can be summarized in the following three stages.

First, an initial EMD decomposition is performed on the original signal. This process includes identifying the local extrema of the signal, fitting the upper and lower envelopes to obtain the local mean envelope, calculating the intermediate signal, and checking whether it satisfies the criteria of an intrinsic mode function (IMF). The first IMF component is then extracted.

Second, the residual signal is decomposed by EMD through multiple iterations, and white noise is introduced in each iteration to assist the decomposition. In this way, IMF components of different orders can be obtained. The IMF components of the same order obtained from multiple decomposition trials are then ensemble-averaged to obtain the first averaged IMF component of the CEEMDAN decomposition.

Third, multiple averaged IMF components are extracted successively through the above iterative process. CEEMDAN decomposes the original signal into several IMF components and a residual term, thereby providing a basis for subsequent sensitive-component selection, denoising, and signal reconstruction. The original signal can be approximately reconstructed by summing all IMF components and the residual term. In this study, only the fault-sensitive IMF components are selected for subsequent WTD-DBO denoising and reconstruction.

Dung beetle optimizer

Xue et al.17 proposed a novel swarm intelligence optimization strategy known as the dung beetle optimizer (DBO). Existing studies have shown that this algorithm exhibits good robustness and adaptability in various global optimization problems. The DBO algorithm simulates several typical behavioral patterns of dung beetles, including ball rolling, dancing, foraging, stealing, and reproduction, to perform the optimization search. In the algorithm, the dung beetle population is generally divided into four types of individuals: ball-rolling dung beetles, brood balls, small dung beetles, and thief dung beetles. The position updating mechanisms of these individuals are described as follows.

Thief dung beetles

Thief dung beetles obtain resources by stealing dung balls rolled by other individuals19. As indicated by Eq. (16), Xb represents the optimal position of the population in food competition. Based on this mechanism, the updated position of a thief dung beetle is expressed as follows:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_{i} (t + 1) = X^{b} + S \times g \times (\left| {x_{i} (t) - X^{*} } \right| + \left| {x_{i} (t) - X^{b} } \right|)$$\end{document}where xi(t) denotes the position information of the i-th thief dung beetle at the t-th iteration, g is a normally distributed random vector of size 1 × D1, and S is a constant.

In summary, the implementation procedure of the DBO algorithm can be described in the following six steps. First, the position distribution of the dung beetle population is initialized, and the relevant control parameters of the algorithm are set. Second, the fitness value of each individual is calculated according to the objective function. Third, the positions of the entire population are updated according to the updating mechanisms of the four functional types of dung beetles. Fourth, boundary detection and out-of-bound correction are performed for the updated individuals. Fifth, the current global optimal solution and its corresponding fitness value are updated. Finally, Steps 2–5 are repeated until the preset maximum number of iterations is reached, after which the global optimal solution and its fitness value are output.

Composite evaluation index

Permutation entropy (PE) is a nonlinear dynamic index used to characterize the complexity of one-dimensional time-series signals20. It is designed to quantify the randomness and irregularity of a sequence. Compared with sample entropy and approximate entropy, PE has the advantages of a relatively simple computational procedure and lower time cost. Its calculation process is described as follows.

The constructed composite evaluation index G can simultaneously quantify signal complexity and the significance of periodic impact components, thereby providing a basis for selecting fault-sensitive components. Unlike conventional indices that focus only on impact intensity or single-dimensional complexity, the proposed G index integrates the advantages of both measures. Specifically, a higher kurtosis value indicates stronger impact components in the signal, whereas a lower permutation entropy value reflects weaker randomness and more distinct periodicity. Therefore, a smaller G value generally suggests that the corresponding component contains more prominent fault-related impact information and lower randomness. In this study, minimization of the G value is used as the evaluation direction for sensitive-component selection and DBO parameter optimization.

Considering the advantages of both kurtosis and permutation entropy, this study constructs a composite evaluation index Gi. Since the two indicators have different numerical ranges and scales, their ratio reflects the relative relationship between impulsiveness and complexity. In the actual calculation, the kurtosis values of the analyzed IMF components were generally within the same order of magnitude, approximately 1–10, whereas the permutation entropy values ranged from 0 to 1. Therefore, the ratio can effectively distinguish fault-sensitive components. According to the definition, a smaller Gi corresponds to a larger kurtosis value and a smaller permutation entropy value, indicating stronger impulsive components and more pronounced periodicity. Such components are therefore more sensitive to fault features. This ratio form naturally penalizes components with low kurtosis or high permutation entropy and avoids the introduction of additional weighting coefficients. In this study, minimization of Gi was used as the fitness function for sensitive IMF selection and DBO parameter optimization.

CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO model

In this study, a rolling-bearing fault signal denoising strategy based on CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO is developed. First, the original vibration signal is adaptively decomposed by CEEMDAN, and the key intrinsic mode function (IMF) components reflecting fault characteristics are selected according to the composite evaluation index. Second, wavelet threshold denoising (WTD) is applied to the selected components, while the dung beetle optimizer (DBO) is introduced to jointly optimize the adjustable parameters α and β in the improved threshold function. Finally, the optimized denoised components are reconstructed to obtain the denoised signal.

To further verify the denoising effect from the perspective of frequency-domain energy distribution, the power spectral densities (PSDs) of the original signal, the CEEMDAN-denoised signal, and the CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO-denoised signal were compared and analyzed. During PSD calculation, the original signal, CEEMDAN-denoised signal, and CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO-denoised signal were processed using exactly the same procedure and parameter settings. First, the direct-current component was removed from each signal, and the frequency axis was established according to the sampling frequency fs = 800 Hz. The spectral density was then calculated using the window function, overlap length, and number of FFT points specified in the program. The analyzed frequency range was set to 0–400 Hz, corresponding to the Nyquist frequency. To ensure the comparability of the results, the PSD calculation parameters and display scale were kept identical for the three signals, with the input signal being the only difference. The discrete PSD can be interpreted as the distribution of the mean square value of the signal within a unit frequency band, and its estimated results were used to characterize the changes in energy across different frequency bands before and after denoising.

As shown in Fig. 17, the original signal exhibits a relatively obvious broadband energy distribution within the analyzed frequency range, with irregular spectral fluctuations. This indicates that the acquired signal contains strong random noise and operating-condition-related interference. After CEEMDAN processing, the energy in some frequency bands is attenuated; however, many residual spectral peaks remain in the low-frequency range, indicating that noise suppression is still insufficient. In contrast, the PSD curve of the signal processed by CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO shows a further overall reduction in spectral energy, and the broadband random components are significantly attenuated. This demonstrates that the proposed joint denoising method has stronger suppression capability for background noise and non-periodic interference.

Conclusion

To address the difficulty of accurately extracting the fault characteristic frequency and its harmonics from rolling bearings in vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines under high-noise operating conditions, this study proposed a bearing vibration signal processing method based on CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO. The method was developed based on the hypothesis that adaptive modal decomposition by CEEMDAN can effectively separate vibration components at different scales, whereas wavelet threshold denoising with adaptively optimized parameters can further suppress residual noise while preserving periodic impact information associated with local bearing damage. Compared with previous methods that rely only on modal decomposition or wavelet threshold denoising, the proposed method introduces the DBO algorithm to adaptively optimize the adjustment parameters in the improved threshold function. This reduces the dependence of denoising performance on empirically selected parameters and improves the adaptability of the method to strongly contaminated vibration signals.

The results obtained from both simulated and experimental signals support this hypothesis. The CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO method does not simply smooth the time-domain waveform; rather, it suppresses random noise and non-periodic interference while retaining and enhancing the periodic impact components caused by bearing faults. The power spectral density comparison shows that the proposed method effectively attenuates broadband random noise and makes the frequency-domain energy distribution of the denoised signal more concentrated. The envelope spectrum analysis further confirms that the rolling-element fault characteristic frequency and its higher-order harmonics can be extracted clearly and stably. These findings indicate that the CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO joint denoising strategy achieves a favorable balance between noise suppression and fault feature preservation, providing an effective signal-processing approach for bearing condition monitoring and early fault diagnosis in low-speed, heavy-load agricultural and animal husbandry machinery such as vertical cattle-manure briquetting machines.

From an engineering application perspective, the proposed method can improve the identifiability of weak fault features under strong background noise and provide a more reliable frequency-feature basis for predictive maintenance of agricultural equipment. However, the present validation mainly focused on rolling-element spalling faults under specific operating conditions. Future research should further investigate multi-source vibration data involving different fault types, rotational speeds, and load conditions, and should verify the real-time performance and robustness of the method in online monitoring systems. In addition, integrating CEEMDAN-WTD-DBO with intelligent classification models may further improve the automation and engineering applicability of fault diagnosis for agricultural equipment.However, the robustness of the proposed method in selecting sensitive components remains insufficient under extremely low signal-to-noise ratio conditions, especially when the SNR is lower than − 20 dB. Future research will further extend the analysis to multi-source vibration data under different fault types, rotational speeds, and load conditions.

A Rare Case of CD68-Positive Congenital Granular Cell Epulis
Authors: Tapas Kanti Parida, M Shameema Farween, Akash Bihari Pati, Manita Tamang, Pavithra Ayyanar
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Congenital granular cell epulis (CGCE) is a benign tumour of uncertain histogenesis.[12] It has many histopathological differential diagnoses.[34] The histogenesis of CGCE remains unclear, with most studies mentioning the lack of S100 positivity.[45] CD68 positivity has been reported in only a few cases of CGCE.[67] Neuron-specific enolase (NSE) was also reported in a few cases.[348] We report an additional case of CGCE in the lower jaw with unusual immunohistochemical features and discuss the differential diagnosis and literature review.

A seven-day-old female term neonate was brought to the neonatology outpatient with a complaint of a hard mass on the left side of the lower jaw since birth. The neonate’s overall systemic examination was essentially normal and there was no difficulty in breathing. The mass was protruding from the mandibular ridge on the left side, measuring 2.0 × 1.5 × 1.0 cm, everting the lower lip outward [Figure 1], preventing complete mouth closure and interfering with sucking. The mass was bright red in colour, and the surface of the mass was smooth and shiny [Figure 1]. It was bleeding on touch and appeared pedunculated with mobility in the anteroposterior axis. A provisional diagnosis of epulis was made. The mass was excised under general anaesthesia when the neonate was 9 days old. The gross examination of the excised mass showed a solid lobular mass of size 1.5 × 1 × 0.5 cm. Microscopic examination showed a polypoidal mass lined by stratified squamous epithelium with flattened rete ridges [Figure 2a]. The underlying stroma showed sheets of monomorphic tumour cells. There were many dilated staghorn patterns of thin vascular channels seen within the tumour and beneath the squamous epithelium [Figure 2b]. These individual tumour cells were polygonal cells with abundant granular eosinophilic cytoplasm withcentrally placed small round to oval nuclei and inconspicuous nucleolus [Figure 2c]. Nuclear atypia or mitotic activity was not seen. Periodic acid Schiff with diastase (PAS-D) stain highlighted a few PAS-positive materials [Figure 2d]. On IHC, the tumour cells were positive for CD68 [Figure 3a] and NSE [Figure 3b] while negative for S100 [Figure 3c] and CD163 [Figure 3d]. The child is asymptomatic at eight months of follow-up.

CGCE is a rare oral lesion that can affect neonates, and its presentation can be challenging due to the small size and age of the patient. The major histomorphological differential diagnosis is granular cell tumours. It is usually seen in adult patients. It is a benign tumour of Schwann cell differentiation. Microscopically, it shows similar morphology to CGCE and shows positivity for S-100, NSE, and CD68. The present case was a newborn with characteristic microscopic findings. S100 negativity has been documented in most of the reported cases.[45] However, CD68 was positive in the present case. Very few cases have mentioned the CD68 positivity in CGCE.[67] Park et al.[7] reported a case of cutaneous non-neural granular cell tumour with CD68 positivity and S100 negativity in a 4-month-old child. The other differential diagnoses are infantile myofibroma, fatal type of rhabdomyoma, and neuroectodermal tumour of infancy.

Histogenesis of this tumour is still unclear, although many theories have been suggested. It includes tumour origination from odontogenic epithelium, primitive mesenchyme, Schwann cells, mesenchymal progenitor cells, or fibroblasts.[49]

Only four reported CGCE cases showed CD68 positivity in the tumour cells. The immunohistochemistry for CD68 positivity favours histiocytic lineage with less specificity. However, CD163 negativity and NSE positivity indicate the features against histiocytic lineage. The CD68 positivity in the present CGCE case tumour cells may suggest the staining of phagolysosome granules in the cytoplasm. The IHC clone of CD68 was not mentioned in the previously reported cases where CD68 was positive. The CD68 antibody clone used in the present case was KP1. In contrast, interstitial cells in CGCE showed CD68 (clone KP1) in a few reported cases.[2] NSE positivity in CGCE has been mentioned in a few cases.[348] CGCE shows a good prognosis. It does not grow after birth. There is no recurrence after excision of the lesion.

We report an additional case of congenital granular cell epulis in a seven-day-old girl child with classic histomorphological findings and aberrant CD68 and NSE positivity.

Introduction

For an integer
m\geq 2

q

q

\mathbb{F}_{q}
and its extension of order
q^{m}
is denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is cyclic and a generator of this cyclic group is called a

.

The existence of primitive elements is well-known for arbitrary finite fields and is a direct consequence of the fact that any finite subgroup of a field’s multiplicative group is cyclic. In addition, in
[
1
]
, the existence of a primitive element
\alpha
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}

(where

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
was proven, given that

.

Theorem 1.1

(Booker-Cohen-Sutherland-Trudgian)
.

For

, there always exists a primitive element in
\alpha
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

is also a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where

and
b^{2}\neq 4ac
.

On a different note, the extended field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of dimension

We assume that

be a basis of the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Take some

-affine hyperplanes in general positions

-affine space of dimension

2.
Preliminaries

A well-known expression of the characteristic function for the primitive elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is given in
[
7
]
as

\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\lambda)=\theta(q-1)\sum_{d\mid q-1}\left(\frac{\mu(d)}{\phi(d)}\sum_{\chi_{d}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}}\chi_{d}(\lambda)\right),

(2.1)

where
\theta(e):=\frac{\phi(e)}{e}
,
\phi
is the Euler’s totient function,
\mu
is the Möbius function and
\chi_{d}
stands for any multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of order
d
.

Now, take a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, the number
P(A)
of primitive elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
A
is given by

P(A)=\sum_{\lambda\in A}\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\lambda),

which, combined with (
2.1
), yields

P(A)=\theta(q-1)\sum_{d\mid q-1}\left(\frac{\mu(d)}{\phi(d)}\sum_{\chi_{d}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}}\sum_{\lambda\in A}\chi_{d}(\lambda)\right).

(2.2)

Definition 2.1
.

For
e\mid q^{m}-1
, an element
\lambda
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is called
e
-free
if
d\mid e
and
\lambda=\gamma^{d}
, for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, imply
d=1
. Furthermore an element
\lambda
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is primitive if and only if it is a
(q^{m}-1)
-free element.

For any
e\mid q^{m}-1
, from the results by Cohen and Huczynska
[
3
,
2
]
, the characteristic function for the set of
e
-free elements is as follows:

\rho_{e}:\lambda\mapsto\theta(e)\sum_{d\mid e}\left(\frac{\mu(d)}{\phi(d)}\sum_{\chi_{d}}\chi_{d}(\lambda)\right).

In the following sections, we will encounter various character sums and some estimations, for they will be necessary. The following lemmas are well-known and provide such results.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
7
, Theorem 5.41]
)
.

Let
\chi
be a nonprincipal multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
of order
r>1
, and let
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
be a monic polynomial of positive degree that is not an
r
-th power of a polynomial. If
g
has
d
distinct roots in its splitting field, then for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have that

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi(ag(\lambda))\right|\leq(d-1)q^{m/2}.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
9
, Corollary 2.3]
)
.

Consider two nontrivial multiplicative characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Let
g_{1}(x)
and
g_{2}(x)
be two monic co-prime polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
, such that none of
g_{i}(x)
is of the form
f(x)^{ord(\chi_{i})}
for
i=1,2
; where
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
with degree at least 1. Then

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|\leq(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)q^{m/2},

where
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are the degrees of largest square free divisors of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
respectively.

We will also need the following results.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
be a set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine hyperplanes of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
in general position and
\chi
be a nonprincipal multiplicative character over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Then

s(S_{c}^{*},\,\chi)\leq\delta(q,m)\leq(2^{m}-1)q^{m/2},

where,
s(S_{c}^{*},\,\chi):=\left|\sum_{\lambda\in S_{c}^{*}}\chi(\lambda)\right|
,
S_{c}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
and
\delta(q,r)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\binom{k}{i}q^{min\{i,\frac{m}{2}\}}
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

For a positive integer
n
, let
W(n)
denote the number of squarefree divisors of
n
. Then for
n\geq 3
,

W(n-1)<n^{\frac{0.96}{\log\log n}}.

Last but not the least, we will need to estimate an incomplete character sum involving a multiplicative character with polynomial argument, that runs through an affine hyperplane. In order to achieve this, inspired by the work of Reis
[
8
]
, we will first need the following lemma that is a direct consequence of
[
8
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
be an affine hyperplane of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. There exists a separable polynomial
h_{A}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
of degree
q^{m-1}
whose derivative is a constant and satisfies the following:

(1)

h_{A}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})=A
;

(2)

for every
a\in A
, the equation
h_{A}(x)=a
has exactly
q^{m-1}
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

Now, we can prove the character sum estimate we will need, which generalizes
[
8
, Corollary 3.5]
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\chi
be a nonprincipal multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
,
A
an affine hyperplane of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
,
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
a monic polynomial of positive degree that is not an
m
-th power of a polynomial. If
g
has
d
distinct roots in its splitting field, then for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have that

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in A}\chi(ag(\lambda))\right|\leq\frac{dq^{m-1}-1}{q^{m-1}}\cdot q^{m/2}.

Proof.

Lemma
2.5
implies that there exists a separable polynomial
h_{A}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
of degree
q^{m-1}
of constant derivative, such that
h_{A}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})=A
and that for every
a\in A
, the equation
h_{A}(x)=a
has exactly
q^{m-1}
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. It follows that

\sum_{\lambda\in A}\chi(ag(\lambda))=\frac{1}{q^{m-1}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi(ag(h_{A}(\lambda))).

(2.3)

Clearly,
\lambda
is a root of
g(h_{A}(x))
if and only if
h_{A}(\lambda)=a
, where
a
is a root of
g
. Since
g
has
d
roots and each of the equations of the form
h_{A}(\lambda)=a
has
q^{m-1}
solutions, we obtain that
g(h_{A}(x))
has
dq^{m-1}
roots in its splitting field.

Furthermore, the polynomial
g(h_{A}(x))
is not an
m
-th power of a polynomial. In order to make this clear, observe that
g(h_{A}(x))
is an
m
-th power if and only if the multiplicity of each of its roots (in its splitting field) is a multiple of
m
. Recall that
g
is not an
m
-th power and
h_{A}
is separable of constant derivative. Now, take a root
y
of
g\circ h_{A}
. Then
g(h_{A}(y))=0\iff h_{A}(y)=z\iff h_{A}(y)-z=0
, where
z
is a root of
g
. However, given that
h_{A}
has constant derivative, the polynomial
h_{A}(x)-z
only has simple roots, that is, the multiplicity of
y
as a root of
g\circ h_{A}
coincides with the multiplicity of
z
as a root of
g
. It follows that, since
g
is not an
m
-th power, neither is
g\circ h_{A}
.

Now, Theorem
2.1
implies

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi(ag(h_{A}(\lambda)))\right|\leq(dq^{m-1}-1)q^{m/2},

which combined with (
2.3
) completes the proof.
∎

3.
Lower bounds on elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
avoiding affine hyperplanes

We provide two theorems for lower bounds on elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
avoiding affine hyperplanes, and we prove only the second theorem in this paper, since the proof of the first one is similar. Throughout this paper, we use the notation
Q_{n,e}
for the set of rational functions
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
of degree at most
n
, which are not
e
-th power of any rational function in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)}
, and
\xi
be the set of exceptional functions
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
. Note that,
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
is an
exceptional function
if it can be written as
g(x)=\alpha((h(x)^{p}-h(x))+\beta x
, for some
h(x)\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)}
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\chi
be a nonprincipal multiplicative character and
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
be a rational function of degree at most
d
. We assume that
\chi
is of order
e
and
g(x)\in Q_{d,e}
, then for the set
S_{c}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
, where
A_{i}
’s are affine hyperplanes, we have

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in S_{c}^{*}}\chi(g(\lambda))\right|\leq k2^{m}q^{m/2},

where
k=\deg(g(x))
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
be nonprincipal multiplicative characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, and
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
be two coprime polynomials of degree at most
d
, such that

(1)

g_{1}(x)\in Q_{d,\omega_{1}}
and
g_{2}(x)\in Q_{d,\omega_{2}}
, where
\omega_{i}=ord(\chi_{i})
for
i
= 1, 2,

(2)

if
\chi_{1}(g_{1}(x))=\chi_{q^{m}-1}(g_{1}(x)^{n_{1}})
and
\chi_{2}(g_{2}(x))=\chi_{q^{m}-1}(g_{2}(x)^{n_{2}})
,
then
g_{1}(x)^{n_{1}}g_{2}(x)^{n_{2}}\in Q_{d,q^{m}-1}
,

then for the set
S_{c}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
, where
A_{i}
’s are affine hyperplanes, we have

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in S_{c}^{*}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|<(k_{1}+k_{2})2^{m}q^{m/2},

where
k_{i}=\deg(g_{i}(x))
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in S_{c}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
, where
A_{i}
’s are affine hyperplanes, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lambda\in S_{c}^{*}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\Gamma_{S_{c}^{*}}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1-\Gamma_{A_{i}}(\lambda)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[1,m]\\
\#J=i\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\Gamma_{A_{J}}(\lambda)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[1,m]\\
\#J=i\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\Gamma_{A_{J}}(\lambda)\right|.

By Theorem
2.2
,
\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|\leq(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)q^{m/2}
, thus

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lambda\in S_{c}^{*}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|\leq

\displaystyle(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)q^{m/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[1,m]\\
\#J=i\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\Gamma_{A_{J}}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)q^{m/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[1,m]\\
\#J=i\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\lambda\in A_{J}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)q^{m/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[1,m]\\
\#J=i\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\lambda\in A_{J}}\chi_{q-1}(g_{1}(\lambda)^{n_{1}}g_{2}(\lambda)^{n_{2}})\right|.

Then by Corollary
2.6
,

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in A_{J}}\chi_{q-1}(g_{1}(\lambda)^{n_{1}}g_{2}(\lambda)^{n_{2}})\right|\leq\frac{(k_{1}+k_{2})q^{m-1}-1}{q^{m-1}}q^{m/2}<(k_{1}+k_{2})q^{m/2},

hence,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\lambda\in S_{c}^{*}}\chi_{1}(g_{1}(\lambda))\chi_{2}(g_{2}(\lambda))\right|<

\displaystyle(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)q^{m/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[1,m]\\
\#J=i\end{subarray}}(k_{1}+k_{2})q^{m/2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(k_{1}+k_{2})q^{m/2}+(2^{m}-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})q^{m/2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Sufficient existence condition and some asymptotic results

Let
f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
be such that
a\neq 0
and
b^{2}\neq 4ac
. In this section, we aim to assess the number of primitive elements
\alpha
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
f(\alpha)
is also primitive in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and both
\alpha,f(\alpha)
belong to
S_{c}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
, i.e., they avoid the collection of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine hyperplanes
\mathfrak{C}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
in general position in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. We denote by
\Lambda(d)
, the set of multiplicative characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
of order
d
.
Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
P(S_{c}^{*})
be the number of
primitive
S_{c}^{*}
pairs

(\alpha,f(\alpha))
in
S_{c}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
avoiding the collection
\mathfrak{C}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine hyperplanes in general position in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, where
f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
,
a\neq 0
and
b^{2}\neq 4ac
. Then a sufficient condition for

Interlacing of zeros from different sequences of Meixner-Pollaczek, Pseudo-Jacobi and Continuous Hahn polynomials
A.S. Jooste, K. Jordaan

Abstract
In this paper we consider interlacing of the zeros of polynomials from different sequences { p n } \{p_{n}\} and { g n } \{g_{n}\} . In our main result we consider a mixed recurrence equation necessary for existence of a linear term ( x − A ) (x-A) so that the ( n + 1 ) (n+1) zeros of ( x − A ) ​ g n ​ ( x ) (x-A)g_{n}(x) interlace with the n n zeros of p n p_{n} . We apply our result to Meixner-Pollaczek, Pseudo-Jacobi and Continuous Hahn polynomials to obtain new interlacing results for the zeros of polynomials of the same degree from different polynomial sequences.

Key words: Orthogonal polynomials; Zeros; Interlacing; Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials; Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials; Continuous Hahn polynomials

Introduction

A sequence of real polynomials
\{p_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, where
p_{n}
is of exact degree
n\in\mathbb{N},
is orthogonal with respect to a positive weight
w(x)
on an interval
[a,b],
if it satisfies

\int_{a}^{b}x^{j}p_{n}(x)w(x)dx\begin{cases}=0,~\mbox{for}~j=0,1,\dots,n-1\\
\neq 0,~\mbox{for}~j=n.\end{cases}

Zeros of polynomials in such a sequence of orthogonal polynomials are all real, distinct and have the property that the zeros of consecutive polynomials in the sequence are interlacing (cf.
[
4
,
21
]
), i.e., if
\{x_{k,n}\}_{k=1}^{n}
denotes the zeros of
p_{n}
, then

x_{1,n}<x_{1,n-1}<x_{2,n}<\cdots<x_{n-1,n}<x_{n-1,n-1}<x_{n,n}.

If polynomials
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
are of the same degree, the zeros are said to interlace if either

x_{1,n}<y_{1,n}<x_{2,n}<y_{2,n}\cdots<x_{n,n}<y_{n,n}

or

y_{1,n}<x_{1,n}<y_{2,n}<x_{2,n}\cdots<y_{n,n}<x_{n,n},

where
\{y_{k,n}\}_{k=1}^{n}
denotes the zeros of
q_{n}
.

The question that arises naturally concerning any polynomial, especially when there are real zeros, is the location and behaviour of the zeros. When a polynomial has only real zeros, it is important for applications to know more about their properties, for example, interlacing and monotonicity with respect to the appropriate parameters. As an example, consider the study of the Laguerre-Pólya class of entire functions, which are functions that can be obtained as a limit (uniformly on compact subsets of C) of a sequence of polynomials with negative real roots. We refer the reader here to the recent papers
[
17
]
and
[
20
]
.

Zeros of polynomials, not in the same orthogonal sequence, were first considered in 1967 by Levit
[
16
]
for Hahn polynomials.
Levit proved that, for

,

2
Main result

Interlacing properties of zeros of polynomials from different sequences usually are derived using sign change arguments applied to polynomials written as linear combinations of polynomials with interlacing zeros. See, for example,
[
15
, p. 117]

3.3
Continuous Hahn polynomials

Monic continuous Hahn polynomials, defined by

\displaystyle p_{n}(x;a,b,c,d)=i^{n}\frac{(a+c)_{n}(a+d)_{n}}{(a+b+c+d+n-1)_{n}}\mbox{${}_{3}F_{2}\left(\!\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{array}[]{c}-n,n\!+\!a\!+\!c\!+\!b\!+\!d\!-\!1,a\!+\!ix\end{array}\hfil\lx@intercol\\[2.84526pt]
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{array}[]{c}a+c,a+d\end{array}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{array}\!\right|\displaystyle{1}\right)$},

are
orthogonal on the interval
(-\infty,\infty)
with respect to the weight function

w(a,b,c,d,x)=\Gamma(a+ix)\Gamma(b+ix)\Gamma(c-ix)\Gamma(d-ix)

provided that the real parts of
a,b,c
and
d
are positive and
c=\bar{a}
,
d=\bar{b}
(cf.
[
14
]
). The sequence of polynomials satisfies the three-term recurrence equation

\displaystyle p_{n+2}(x;a,b,c,d)=\left(x-i~C\right)p_{n+1}(x;a,b,c,d)-D~p_{n}(x;a,b,c,d)\mbox{~~with}

(3.11)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)(b+c+n)(b+d+n)}{(a+b+c+d+2n)_{2}}-\frac{(a+c+n+1)(a+d+n+1)(a+b+c+d+n)}{(a+b+c+d+2n+1)_{2}}+a\mbox{~and}

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)(b+c+n)(b+d+n)(a+c+n)(a+d+n)(a+b+c+d+n-1)}{(a+b+c+d+2n)(a+b+c+d+2n-1)_{3}}.

Considering the above-mentioned orthogonality conditions, we will shift parameters
a
and
c
(alternatively
b
and
d
) simultaneously in order to retain the orthogonality of the polynomials. In what follows, our aim is to determine a mixed recurrence equation connecting the polynomials
p_{n}(x;a+1,b,c+1,d),

p_{n}(x;a,b,c,d),
and
p_{n+1}(x;a,b,c,d).
The parameter shifted monic continuous Hahn polynomial
p_{n}(x;a+k,b+m,c+k,d+m)
is orthogonal on
\mathbb{R}
with respect to

\displaystyle\Gamma(a+k+ix)\Gamma(b+m+ix)\Gamma(c+k-ix)\Gamma(d+m-ix)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\sigma_{k,m}(a,b,c,d,x)~w(a,b,c,d,x)>0.

where
\sigma_{k,m}(a,b,c,d,x)=(a+ix)_{k}(b+ix)_{m}(c-ix)_{k}(d-ix)_{m}
(cf.
[
10
]
). Then, for
p,r>0
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,0}(a,b,c,d,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,0}(p+iq,r+is,p-iq,r-is,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p+iq+ix)(p-iq-ix)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p+i(q+x))(p-i(q+x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{2}+(q+x)^{2},

with zeros
x=-q+i~p
or
x=-q-i~p
and

\displaystyle w(a,b,c,d,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(a+ix)\Gamma(b+ix)\Gamma(c-ix)\Gamma(d-ix)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(p+iq+ix)\Gamma(r+is+ix)\Gamma(p-iq-ix)\Gamma(r-is-ix)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(p+i(q+x))\Gamma(r+i(s+x))\Gamma(p-i(q+x))\Gamma(r-i(s+x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(p+i(q+x))\Gamma\overline{(p+i(q+x))}\Gamma(r+i(s+x))\Gamma\overline{(r+i(s+x))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(p+i(q+x))\overline{\Gamma(p+i(q+x))}\Gamma(r+i(s+x))\overline{\Gamma(r+i(s+x))}

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma(p+i(q+x))\right|^{2}\left|\Gamma(r+i(s+x))\right|^{2}

[
9
, Thm 2.7.1]
and
[

, Section 2.5]
) to obtain the equation necessary to prove the interlacing of the zeros of
p_{n}(x;a+1,b,c+1,d)
with the zeros of
p_{n+1}(x;a,b,c,d)
and/or with the zeros of

\displaystyle U(a+ix)(c-ix)

\displaystyle p_{n}(x;a+1,b,c+1,d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}p_{n}(-i~\bar{a};a,b,c,d)&p_{n+1}(-i~\bar{a};a,b,c,d)&p_{n+2}(-i~\bar{a};a,b,c,d)\\

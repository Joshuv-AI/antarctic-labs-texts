1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero and let
R=K[X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. Let
A_{n+1}(K)=K<X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}>
be the
(n+1)^{th}
Weyl algebra over
K
.
Let
N
be a left
A_{n+1}(K)
module. Now
\mathbb{\partial}=\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
are pairwise commuting
K
-linear
maps. So we can consider the de Rham complex
K(\mathbb{\partial};N)
. Notice that
the de Rham cohomology modules
H^{*}(\mathbb{\partial};N)
are in general only

K
-vector spaces. They are finite dimensional if
N
is holonomic;
see
[
1
, Chapter 1, Theorem 6.1]
. In particular the Euler
characteristic
\chi^{c}(\mathbf{\partial},N)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\dim_{K}H^{i}(\mathbb{\partial};N)
is
a finite number.
In
[
1
]
holonomic
A_{n+1}(K)
modules are denoted as
\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(K)
, the
Bernstein
class of left
A_{n+1}(K)
modules.
Similarly if
N
is a holonomic
A_{n+1}(K)
-module then the Koszul cohomology modules
H^{i}(\mathbf{X},N)
are finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces. So again the Euler characteristic

\chi^{c}(\mathbf{X},N)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\dim_{K}H^{i}(\mathbf{X};N)
is
a finite number. In general we do not know any relation between
\chi^{c}(\mathbf{\partial},N)
and
\chi^{c}(\mathbf{X},N)
. The purpose of this paper is to describe a relation between the two Euler characteristics for a large class of holonomic modules.

1.1
.

Now consider the case when
R
is standard graded with
\deg X_{i}=1
for all
i
. Note the Weyl algebra
A_{n+1}(K)
is also graded with
\deg\partial_{i}=-1
for all
i
.
Let
I
be a graded ideal in
R
. For
i\geq 0

let
H^{i}_{I}(R)
be the
i^{th}
-local cohomology module of
R
with
respect to
I
. Let
M=H^{i}_{I}(R)
for some
i
. By a result due
to Lyubeznik, see
[
3
]
,
M

is a finitely generated graded
A_{n}(K)
-module. In fact

M
is a
holonomic

A_{n+1}(K)
module. We prove

Theorem 1.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
). We have

\chi^{c}(\mathbf{\partial},M)=(-1)^{n+1}\chi^{c}(\mathbf{X},M).

In fact we prove Theorem
1.2
for a more general class of graded holonomic modules known as generalized Eulerian holonomic modules, see
2.2
for its definition.
The main technical result we prove is

Theorem 1.3
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
). Let
N
be a graded, holonomic, generalized Eulerian
A_{n+1}(K)
-module. If
N=N_{X_{0}}
then

\chi^{c}(\mathbf{\partial},N)=0.

We note that if
N=N_{X_{0}}
then it is easy to note that the Koszul cohomology modules
H^{i}(\mathbf{X},N)
vanish for all
i
, see
2.4
.

Remark 1.4
.

Although we stated all the results for cohomology modules, we will work with de Rham homology and Koszul homology.

Remark 1.5
.

The main contribution of the paper was to guess the result. The proofs are not hard.

We now describe in brief the contents of the paper. In section two we discuss a few preliminary results that we need.
In section three we prove Theorem
1.3
. Finally in section four we prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we discuss few preliminary results that we need.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero and let
R=K[X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
with standard grading. Let
A_{n+1}(K)
be the
(n+1)^{th}
Weyl algebra over
K
. Set
\deg\partial_{i}=-1
. So
A_{n+1}(K)
is a graded ring with
R
as a graded subring.
The Euler operator, denoted by
\epsilon
, is defined as

\epsilon:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}X_{i}\partial_{i}.

Note that
\deg\epsilon=0
. Let
M
be a graded
A_{n+1}(K)
-module. If
m\in M
is homogeneous element, set
|m|=\deg m
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a graded
A_{n+1}(K)
-module. We say
M
is an
Eulerian

A_{n+1}(K)
-module if for any homogeneous
z
in
M

we have

\epsilon z=|z|\cdot z.

Clearly
R
is an Eulerain
A_{n+1}(K)
-module. Also by
[
4
, 5.3]
graded local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{I}(R)
is Eulerian.
Eulerian modules do not behave well under extensions, see
[
4
, 3.6(1)]
. To rectify this the first author introduced the following notion:

Definition 2.2
.

A graded
A_{n+1}(K)
-module
M
is said to be
generalized Eulerian
if for any homogeneous element
z
of
M

there exists a positive integer
a
(depending on
z
) such that

(\epsilon-{|z|})^{a}\cdot z=0.

2.3
.

We will need the following two computations. Let
E(K)
be the injective hull of
K
as an
R
-module. Then note
E(K)
is an
A_{n+1}(K)
-module. We have

\displaystyle H_{i}(\mathbf{X},E(K))=\begin{cases}K\quad\text{if}\ i=n+1.\\
0\quad\text{if}\ i\neq n+1.\end{cases}

Also

\displaystyle H_{i}(\mathbf{\partial},E(K))=\begin{cases}K\quad\text{if}\ i=0.\\
0\quad\text{if}\ i\neq 0.\end{cases}

The second computation follows from
[
5
, 2.3]
. For the first note that
X_{n}\colon E_{R}(K)\rightarrow E_{R}(K)
is surjective with kernel
E_{S}(K)
where
S=K[X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}]
. An easy induction deduces the result.

We will need the following result which is certainly known. We give a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
R=K[X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
and let
M
be an
R
-module. If
M=M_{X_{0}}
then the Koszul homology modules
H_{i}(\mathbf{X},M)=0
for all
i
. In particular
\chi(\mathbf{X},M)=0
.

Proof.

As
M=M_{X_{0}}
it follows that multiplication by
X_{0}
is bijective on
H_{i}(\mathbf{X},M)
. But
X_{0}H_{i}(\mathbf{X},M)=0
, see
[
2
, 1.6.5]
. The result follows.
∎

3.
Euler characteristics of de Rham homology

3.1
.

Throughout this section
R
and
M
denote as in the following:
Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero and let
R=K[X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
(n+1)-
variables. Give standard grading to
R
. Let

A_{n+1}(K)=D(R,K)=K<X_{0},\cdots,X_{n},\partial_{0},\cdots,\partial_{n}>
be the
(n+1)^{th}
Wey-algebra over
K
. Consider
A_{n+1}(K)
graded with
\deg(\partial_{i})=-1,
for
i=0,1,\cdots,n.
Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}

be a graded holonomic generalized Eulerian
A_{n+1}(K)-
module.

In this section we prove

Theorem 3.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
3.1
). If
M\cong M_{X_{0}}
then
\chi(\mathbf{\partial},M)=0.

The idea to prove the result is to prove it first when
n=0
and then deduce the general result from
n=0
case.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R=K[X],\ A_{1}(K)=K<X,\partial>,
and
\deg(X)=1,

\deg(\partial)=-1.
Then
H_{1}(\partial,R_{X})=K
and
H_{0}(\partial,R_{X})=K\frac{1}{X}.
In particular
\chi(\partial,R_{X})=0.

Proof.

Let
u\in H_{1}(\partial,R_{X}).
Suppose
u=a/X^{n}
with
a\in R,\ n\geq 1
and
X\not\backslash a.

As

0=\partial u=\frac{-1}{n}\frac{a}{X^{n+1}}+\frac{1}{X^{n}}\partial(a).

So
nX\partial(a)=a.
Thus
X/a.
This is a contradiction.

So
u=p(x)
for
p(x)\in R.

\partial(u)=0\Rightarrow p(x)=c
(a constant). Therefore
H_{1}(\partial,R_{X})=K.

Now we compute
H_{0}(\partial,R_{X}).
Notice that if
p(x)\in R
then
q=\int p(x)\in R
and
\partial(q)=p(x).
Also for
n\geq 2

\partial\left(\frac{1}{X^{n-1}}\right)=\frac{-(n-1)}{X^{n}}.\ \text{So}\ \frac{1}{X^{n}}=\partial\left(\frac{-1}{(n-1)X^{n}}\right).

Thus
H_{0}(\partial,R_{X})\subseteq K\frac{1}{X}.

Also
\frac{1}{X}\not=\partial(r)
for any
r\in R_{X}
. To see this note if

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=Xp(x)+c_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{X}+\frac{a_{2}}{X^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{s}}{X^{s}},\ a_{i}\in K

\displaystyle\text{then}\ \partial(r)

\displaystyle=\partial(Xp(x))+0-\frac{a_{1}}{X^{2}}-\frac{\partial a_{2}}{X^{3}}-\cdots-\frac{ra_{r}}{X^{r+1}}

\displaystyle\not=\frac{1}{X}.

Therefore
H_{0}(\partial,R_{X})=K\frac{1}{X}.
Hence
\chi(\partial,R_{X})=0.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R=K[X],\ A_{1}(K)=K<X,\partial>,
and
\deg(X)=1,

\deg(\partial)=-1
and
M
be a holonomic, generalized Eulerian
A_{1}(K)-
module. Assume
M=M_{X}.
Then

(1)

H_{1}(\partial,M)\neq 0.

(2)

There exists a sequence of holonomic generalized Eulerian
A_{1}(K)
-modules

0\rightarrow R_{X}^{s}\rightarrow M\rightarrow U\rightarrow 0,

where
s=\dim H_{1}(\partial,M)
and
U=U_{X}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
\epsilon=X\partial.
As
M
is generalized Eulerian it follows that if
m\in M
is homogeneous then

(\epsilon-\deg m)^{r}m=0,\ \ \text{for some}\ r\geq 1\ \text{(depending on $m$)}.

Let
m\in M,

m\not=0
and
\deg(m)=0.
Assume
\epsilon^{r}m=0
and
\epsilon^{r-1}m\not=0.

Suppose if possible
H_{1}(\partial,M)=0.
Then

\partial\epsilon^{r-1}m\not=0,\ \text{and}\ \partial\epsilon^{r-1}m\in M_{-1}=\frac{M_{0}}{X}.

Thus
\partial\epsilon^{r-1}m=\alpha/X.
So
X\partial\epsilon^{r-1}m=\alpha.
Thus
\alpha=\epsilon^{r}m=0.

This is a contradiction. So
H_{1}(\partial,M)\neq 0
.

(2) Let
s=\dim_{K}H_{1}(\partial,M)
. By (1) we have
s\neq 0
. Assume
H_{1}(\partial,M)=K\alpha_{1}+K\alpha_{2}+\cdots+K\alpha_{s}.
Note that
\deg(\alpha_{i})=-1
see
[
6
, 3.5]
. We have
H_{1}(\partial,M)\subset M(+1).
So
\alpha_{i}
can be considered as an element of
M_{0}.

Now consider
\psi:R^{s}\rightarrow M
where
\psi(e_{i})=\alpha_{i}.
Clearly
\psi
is
R
linear. As

\partial(r\alpha_{i})=\partial(r)\alpha_{i}+r\partial(\alpha_{i})=\partial(r)\alpha_{i},

we get that
\psi
is
A_{1}(K)
-linear. Thus
\psi
is map of generalized Eulerian

A_{1}(K)-
modules.

Claim:
\psi
is injective.

Suppose if possible there exists
v=r_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+r_{s}e_{s}\in\ker\psi
and
v\neq 0
. Then we have

r_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+r_{s}\alpha_{s}=0
. Assume without any loss of generality that
t=\deg r_{1}=\max\{\deg r_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq s\}
.
Then we have

\partial^{t}(r_{1})\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\partial^{t}(r_{s})\alpha_{s}=0.

We note that
\partial^{t}(r_{i})\in K
and
\partial^{t}(r_{1})\neq 0
. It follows that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}
is NOT linearly independent over
K
. This is a contradiction.
So
\ker\psi=0
.

As
\psi
is injective we have a short exact sequence of generalized Eulerian holonomic modules

0\rightarrow R^{s}\xrightarrow{\bar{\psi}}M\rightarrow V\rightarrow 0.

Localising we get

0\rightarrow R^{s}_{X}\rightarrow M_{X}=M\rightarrow V_{X}\rightarrow 0.

Set
U=V_{X}
. The result follows.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
R=K[X],\ A_{1}(K)=K<X,\partial>,
and
\deg(X)=1,

\deg(\partial)=-1
and
M
be a holonomic, generalized Eulerian
A_{1}(K)-
module.
If
M\cong M_{X}
then
\chi(\partial,M)=0.

Proof.

Suppose if possible there is
M
such that
M\cong M_{X}
and
\chi(\partial,M)\neq 0.
Choose such an
M
with least length. By
3.4
,
H_{1}(\partial,M)\neq 0.
Also we have an exact sequence of generalized Eulerian modules

0\rightarrow R_{X}^{s}\rightarrow M\rightarrow U\rightarrow 0,

with
s=\dim_{K}H_{1}(\partial,M)\geq 1
and
U=U_{X}
. Note that

\displaystyle\chi(\partial,M)=

\displaystyle\chi(\partial,R_{X}^{s})+\chi(\partial,U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+\chi(\partial,U).

\displaystyle\text{So}\ \chi(\partial,U)

\displaystyle\neq 0

\displaystyle\text{But}\ \lambda(U)=

\displaystyle\lambda(M)-s\lambda(R_{X})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\lambda(M).

This is a contradiction. Therefore
\chi(\partial,M)=0.

∎

As a corollary we have the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
R=K[X],\ A_{1}(K)=K<X,\partial>,
and
\deg(X)=1,

\deg(\partial)=-1
and
M
be a holonomic
A_{1}(K)-
module. Assume
M=M_{X}
. If
M(a)
is generalized Eulerian for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}
then
\chi(\partial,M)=0.

Proof.

As
M(a)=M_{X}(a)=M(a)_{X}.
So
\chi(\partial,M(a))=0.
But
\chi(\partial,M(a))=\chi(\partial,M).
Thus
\chi(\partial,M)=0.

∎

We now give

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

The case when
n=0
is already done. So assume
n\geq 1
.
Note that
M(-1)\xrightarrow{X_{0}}M
is an isomorphism and
\partial_{1},\cdots\partial_{n}
commutes with
X_{0}.
So

H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M)(-1)\xrightarrow{X_{0}}H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M),

is an isomorphism, where
\partial^{{}^{\prime}}=\partial_{1},\cdots\partial_{n}.
So
H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M)=H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M)_{X_{0}}
for all
i
. Also
H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M)(-n)
is holonomic, generalized Eulerian
K<X_{0},\partial_{0}>
module, see
[
6
, 3.4]
.

So
\chi(\partial_{0},H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M))=0.
Thus

\dim_{K}H_{1}(\partial_{0},H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M))=\dim_{K}H_{0}(\partial_{0},H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M)).

From the exact sequence (for all
i
)

0\mapsto H_{0}(\partial_{0},H_{i}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M))\mapsto H_{i}(\partial,M)\mapsto H_{1}(\partial_{0},H_{i-1}(\partial^{{}^{\prime}},M))\mapsto 0,

we get
\chi(\mathbf{\partial},M)=0.

∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
. In fact we prove the result for graded, holonomic and generalized Eulerian modules.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be a graded holonomic generalized Eulerian
A_{n+1}(K)
module. Then
\chi(\mathbf{\partial},M)=(-1)^{n+1}\chi(\mathbf{X},M).

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
\dim\operatorname{Supp}(M).

Suppose
\dim\operatorname{Supp}(M)=0.
Then
M=E_{R}(K)(n+1)^{s}.
Notice

\displaystyle H_{i}(\mathbf{X},E(K))=\begin{cases}K\quad\text{if}\ i=n+1.\\
0\quad\text{if}\ i\neq n+1.\end{cases}

Also

\displaystyle H_{i}(\mathbf{\partial},E(K))=\begin{cases}K\quad\text{if}\ i=0.\\
0\quad\text{if}\ i\neq 0.\end{cases}

So the result holds when
\dim\operatorname{Supp}(M)=0.

Assume
r\geqq 1
and result holds for all graded holonomic generalized Eulerian
A_{n+1}(K)
modules
N
with
\dim\operatorname{Supp}N\leq r-1.

As
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=E(K)^{s}(n+1)
for some
s\geq 0,
and we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\bar{M}\longrightarrow 0.

Thus it suffices to prove the result for
\bar{M}.
So we may assume
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0.

Notice
(X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})\not\in\operatorname{Ass}(M).
As
\operatorname{Ass}(M)
is a finite set and
K
is infinite there exist

\alpha_{0}X_{0}+\alpha_{1}X_{1}+\alpha_{2}X_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{s}X_{s}\in K^{n+1}\backslash\cup_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}(M)}(P\cap K^{n+1}).

Without loss of generality assume
\alpha_{0}\not=0.
So after a homogeneous linear change of co-ordinates we can assume
X_{0}
is a non-zero divisor on
M.

We have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M_{X_{0}}\longrightarrow H_{X_{0}}^{1}(M)\longrightarrow 0,

Notice
\operatorname{Supp}(H_{X_{0}}^{1}(M))\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(M)\cap V(X_{0}),
so
\dim\operatorname{Supp}H_{X_{0}}^{1}(M)\leq r-1.
Thus by induction hypothesis

\chi(\mathbf{\partial},H_{X_{0}}^{1}(M))=(-1)^{n+1}\chi(\mathbf{X},H_{X_{0}}^{1}(M)).

By Theorem
3.2
,
\chi(\mathbf{\partial},M_{X_{0}})=0.
Also note that
\chi(\mathbf{X},M_{X_{0}})=0.
We have

\chi(\partial,M)=-\chi(\partial,H_{X_{0}}^{1}(M)).

Similarly

\chi(\mathbf{X},M)=-\chi(\mathbf{X},H_{X_{0}}^{1}(M)).

So the result follows.
∎

Robust Gene Prioritization via Fast-mRMR Feature Selection in high-dimensional omics data
Rubén Fernández-Farelo 1, Jorge Paz-Ruza 1, Bertha Guijarro-Berdiñas 1, Amparo Alonso-Betanzos 1 and Alex A. Freitas 2 1- Universidade da Coruña, Grupo LIDIA - CITIC, A Coruña 15071, Spain 2- School of Computing, University of Kent, Canterbury CT2 7FS, United Kingdom

Abstract
Gene prioritization (identifying genes potentially associated with a biological process) is increasingly tackled with Artificial Intelligence. However, existing methods struggle with the high dimensionality and incomplete labelling of biomedical data. This work proposes a more robust and efficient pipeline that leverages Fast-mRMR feature selection to retain only relevant, non-redundant features for classifiers. This enables us to build simpler and more effective models, as well as to combine different biological feature sets. Experiments on Dietary Restriction datasets show significant improvements over existing methods, proving that feature selection can be critical for reliable gene prioritization.



1
Introduction

Gene prioritization, the task of identifying genes relevant to a biological process, is crucial for advancing our understanding of biological processes, such as Dietary Restriction (DR)
[
3
]
as an anti-aging intervention. From a machine learning perspective, this task faces two main challenges: 1) high-dimensional feature spaces, and 2) the unsuspecting presence of positive-unlabeled (PU) data, where only a small set of confirmed positive examples and no confirmed negative examples are available
[
2
]
. In the context of DR gene prioritization, Vega Magdaleno et al.’s approach
[
11
]
faced both issues; the problem of incomplete labelling was recently solved through a PU Learning strategy
[
5
]
, yet the challenge posed by high dimensionality remained unaddressed.

This work aims to fill this gap by proposing a pipeline centered on robust feature selection. Our main contribution is an integration of the Fast-mRMR algorithm
[
8
]
for minimum-redundancy and maximum-relevance feature selection
[
6
]
into a classification pipeline for gene prioritization. This focus on dimensionality reduction not only improves predictive performance but also builds simpler, more efficient models, establishing Feature Selection (FS) as a critical step for this task.

4.2
Computational Efficiency Analysis

We analyzed computational efficiency via CO₂ emissions (Figure
2
). For a fair comparison, we normalized the PU_Processing cost, considering the computational cost of a single run of the 10-fold cross-validation. Our Fast-mRMR approach makes a strategic trade-off: while it introduces an FS phase, it drastically reduces the Training cost—the main bottleneck in the baseline methods. This higher initial investment results in a significantly lower per-inference cost (a flatter slope) demonstrating that our approach is a greener and more scalable solution for long-term use, as the upfront cost is quickly amortized over thousands of predictions. Fast-mRMR(PathDIP) offered no advantage and was omitted.

Conclusions and Future Work

Fast-mRMR effectively reduced dimensionality, producing more compact and interpretable models with fewer, non-redundant, and more relevant features. The GO+PathDIP configuration achieved statistically significant improvements, proving that, after feature selection, combining both biological sources with common genes becomes effective. Moreover, the method reduced training cost and enabled greener, more scalable inference, confirming that feature selection is a key step for reliable gene prioritization.

Future work will focus on extending the method to additional omics sources such as GTEx and Coexpression data, integrating Fast-mRMR with PU Learning to enhance negative instance selection, and developing interpretable tools for exploring the biological meaning of selected features and top-ranked genes.

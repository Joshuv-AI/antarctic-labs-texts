On a variant of bilinear spherical maximal function
Ankit Bhojak, Surjeet Singh Choudhary, Saurabh Shrivastava

Abstract
. We obtain L p − L^{p}- estimates for the full and lacunary maximal functions associated to the bilinear spherical averages given by 𝔄 t ​ ( f 1 , f 2 ) ​ ( x , y ) = ∫ 𝕊 2 ​ d − 1 f 1 ​ ( x + t ​ z 1 , y ) ​ f 2 ​ ( x , y + t ​ z 2 ) ​ 𝑑 σ ​ ( z 1 , z 2 ) , t > 0 , \mathfrak{A}_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}f_{1}(x+tz_{1},y)f_{2}(x,y+tz_{2})\;d\sigma(z_{1},z_{2}),\;t>0, for all dimensions d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . We show that the estimates for such operators in dimensions d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 essentially rely on the method of slicing. The bounds for the lacunary maximal function in dimension one are more delicate and require a trilinear smoothing inequality, which is based on an appropriate sublevel set estimate in this context.

1.
Introduction and main results

The goal of this paper is to establish
L^{p}
-estimates for a variant of bilinear spherical maximal function. This study is motivated by recent papers on bilinear spherical maximal functions and bilinear singular integral operators along polynomials. First, we briefly discuss some of the recent works which are relevant to the current paper, then we introduce the bilinear spherical maximal functions and provide statements of the main results obtained in the paper.

For any
d\geq 2
,
\sigma=\sigma_{d-1}
denote the normalized surface measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(the space of Schwartz functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
), and
d\geq 1
, the full bilinear spherical maximal function is defined by

\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{t>0}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}f(x-ty_{1})g(x-ty_{2})\;d\sigma(y_{1},y_{2})\right|.

This operator was first studied by Geba et al.
[
19
]
. Jeong-Lee
[
23
]
introduced a method of “slicing” to obtain sharp
L^{p}
-estimates for the full maximal function
\mathcal{M}(f,g)
. The slicing argument allows us to dominate the full bilinear spherical maximal function by a product of the classical Hardy-Littlewood maximal function and full (linear) spherical maximal function defined as

\mathcal{A}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x-ty)\;d\sigma(y)\right|.

The operator
A
is bounded in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
- if, and only if
p>\frac{d}{d-1}
, see Stein
[
32
]
for dimension
d\geq 3
and Bourgain
[
5
]
for
d=2
. For
d\geq 2
, Jeong-Lee
[
23
]
used the
L^{p}
-estimates for linear maximal operators along with the slicing argument to conclude that the operator
\mathcal{M}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if, and only if
p>\frac{d}{2d-1}
and
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}
, except at the points
(1,\infty,1)
and
(\infty,1,1)
. The case of dimension
d=1
was later addressed by Christ-Zhou
[
9
]
, also see Dosidis-Ramos
[
16
]
, Shrivastava-Shuin
[
31
]
. They proved that
\mathcal{M}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
for
p_{1},p_{2}>2
and
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}
.

Another important maximal function associated with bilinear spherical averages is the lacunary maximal function. It is defined by taking supremum over lacunary sequences as follows

\mathcal{A}_{lac}f(x)=\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x-2^{k}y)\;d\sigma(y)\right|.

The
L^{p}
-estimates for the lacunary maximal operator
\mathcal{A}_{lac}
holds for a larger range
p>1
. We refer the reader to Calderón
[
6
]
, Coifman-Weiss
[
15
]
, Duoandikoetxea-Rubio de Francia
[
17
]
for
L^{p}
-boundedness of the lacunary maximal operator
\mathcal{A}_{lac}
for
p>1
. Lacey
[
24
]
strengthened
L^{p}
-boundedness properties of spherical maximal operators further by establishing sparse domination of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{lac}
.
However, the weak-type boundedness of
\mathcal{A}_{lac}
at the end-point
p=1
remains a long-standing open problem. We refer the reader to Christ
[
10
]
, Seeger-Tao-Wright
[
30
]
and Cladek-Krause
[
14
]
for some interesting results for the lacunary operator
\mathcal{A}_{lac}
near
L^{1}
.

The lacunary bilinear spherical maximal function is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{lac}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}f(x-2^{k}y_{1})g(x-2^{k}y_{2})\;d\sigma(y_{1},y_{2})\right|.

The
L^{p}
-estimates for the lacunary operator
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
hold for a larger range of
p^{\prime}
s as compared with the case of full maximal function. Borges-Foster
[
4
]
proved the
L^{p}
-estimates for
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
in dimensions
d\geq 2
and Christ-Zhou
[
9
]
in dimension
d=1
. The case of dimension
d\geq 2
exploits the slicing method, which is not applicable in dimension
d=1
. Further, the decay estimates on the Fourier transform
\widehat{d\sigma}
do not help in extending
L^{p}
-estimates for the full range of
p^{\prime}
s in dimension
d=1
. Therefore, the case of
d=1
is significantly challenging to deal with. This motivates to studying trilinear smoothing inequalities studied by Christ in the articles
[
12
,
13
,
11
]
. Christ-Zhou
[
9
]
proved trilinear smoothing estimates for bilinear averaging operators along certain curves, of which unit circle in the plane is a prime example. These trilinear smoothing estimates play the key role in obtaining
L^{p}
-estimates for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
. We also refer the reader to Bhojak-Choudhary-Shrivastava-Shuin
[
3
]
, Bak-Shim
[
1
]
and Lee-Shuin
[
25
]
for some recent developments related to bilinear spherical maximal functions.

The trilinear smoothing inequalities are crucial in investigating
L^{p}
-boundedness of a large class of maximal bilinear singular integral operators along curves. Recently, Christ-Durcik-Roos
[
8
]
studied the bilinear Hilbert transform along parabola

H(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y):=~\text{p.v.}~\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{1}(x+t,y)f_{2}(x,y+t^{2})\frac{dt}{t},~~f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

The operator
H
is a variant of the triangular Hilbert transform

(f_{1},f_{2})\mapsto\text{p.v.}~\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{1}(x+t,y)f_{2}(x,y+t)\frac{dt}{t}.

We also refer to the works of Li
[
27
]
, Lie
[
28
]
, Li-Xiao
[
26
]
, and Lie
[
29
]
for the boundedness of the standard bilinear Hilbert transform along curves. In
[
8
]
, they proved trilinear smoothing inequalities in this context and consequently obtained
L^{p}
-boundedness of the operator
H
. Further, a very interesting application of the trilinear smoothing inequalities in the context of quantitative bounds on Roth-type theorem with corners determined by a parabola in subsets of the unit square
[0,1]^{2}
was also obtained. We also refer to the study guide by Hsu-Lin-Stokolosa
[
20
]
for a nice exposition of this results. We also refer to Gaitan-Lie
[
18
]
for a different approach to obtain
L^{p}-
estimates for
H
. Later, Chen-Guo
[
7
]
extended the notion of the bilinear Hilbert transform along parabola to more general polynomials. In particular, they studied the operator

H_{P_{1},P_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y):=\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{1}(x+P_{1}(t),y)f_{2}(x,y+P_{2}(t))\frac{dt}{t},~~f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

where
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are linearly independent polynomials with zero constant term. They proved a quantitative Roth-type theorem with corners determined by polynomial curves. More precisely, they obtained quantitative lower bounds on the gap parameter
t
so that configurations of the type
(x,y),(x+P_{1}(t),y),(x,y+P_{2}(t))
lie in positive measure subsets in the unit square
[0,1]^{2}.
The trilinear smoothing inequalities are the main tools in proving the Roth-type theorems, see
[
8
,
7
]
. We also refer to
[
21
]
for a short proof of triangular Hilbert transform along curves in the Banach range.

Main results

Motivated by these fascinating developments in the theory of bilinear singular integral operators along curves and their applications to Roth-type theorems, we investigate the
L^{p}

The full bilinear maximal function associated with the bilinear averages

\mathfrak{M}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y):=\sup_{t>0}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)\right|.

\mathfrak{M}_{lac}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y):=\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)\right|.

Full maximal function: Proof of
Theorem

1.1

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
in dimension
d\geq 2
is based on the modified slicing argument for the spherical averages, see
[
3
,
23
]
for details. In the case of dimension
d=1
, the slicing argument is not valid. In this case, we estimate the circular averages in a more direct manner. We write down the integral over circle in parametric form and then decompose the domain of integral dyadically. We prove suitable estimates on each piece of the decomposition to complete the proof. We require the following notation in order to present a proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Consider the following linear averaging operators and associated maximal functions. These show up naturally in the analysis of bilinear spherical maximal functions.

\displaystyle A_{t}^{(1)}f(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}f(x-tz,y)\;d\sigma(z),

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},(I-\Theta_{j}^{(2)})f_{2})\right|+\sum_{{k}=(k_{1},k_{2})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})\right|

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \leq&3\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},\Theta_{j}^{(2)}f_{2})\right|+\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(f_{1},\Theta_{j}^{(2)}f_{2})\right|\\
+&\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},f_{2})\right|+\sum_{{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})\right|,\end{aligned}

where the operator
T_{j,{k}}
is defined as

T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y):=\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}f_{1},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}f_{2})(x,y).

The desired
L^{p}
-estimates of the first three terms in (
3.1
) follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
1<p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
, and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. Then the maximal operators

\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},f_{2})\right|,~\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},\Theta_{j}^{(2)}f_{2})\right|,\text{ and }\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(f_{1},\Theta_{j}^{(2)}f_{2})\right|

are bounded from
L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.1

First, observe that the fiberwise convolution with
\Theta_{j}^{(l)}
can be controlled by the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function in respective variable. For,
\phi\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
|z_{1}|\leq 1
, we get that

\displaystyle|\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1}(x-2^{-j}z_{1},y)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{1}(z,y)2^{jd}\phi(2^{j}(x-2^{-j}z_{1}-z)~dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{2^{jd}f_{1}(z,y)}{(1+|2^{j}(x-2^{-j}z_{1}-z)|)^{N}}dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{(1)}f_{1}(x,y).

Now, we consider the case of
d=1
. Note that we have

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{|z_{1}|\leq 1}|\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1}(x-2^{-j}z_{1},y)|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|f_{2}(x,y-2^{-j}z_{2})|d\sigma(z_{1},z_{2}).

By symmetry and a change of variable, it is enough to consider

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{1}|f_{2}(x,y-2^{-j}t)|\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{k}{2}}\int_{\sqrt{1-2^{-k+1}}}^{\sqrt{1-2^{-k}}}|f_{2}(x,y-2^{-j}t)|~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{|I_{k}|}\int_{I_{k}}|f_{2}(x,y-2^{-j}t)|~dt,

where
I_{k}=[\sqrt{1-2^{-k+1}},\sqrt{1-2^{-k}}]
. Using the
L^{p}
-boundedness properties of shifted dyadic maximal function, we can get that

\Big\|\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},f_{2})\Big|\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}

when
1<p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
.

Next, for
d\geq 2
, a slicing argument gives us

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{|z_{1}|\leq 1}|\Theta_{j}^{(1)}f_{1}(x-2^{-j}z_{1},y)|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}|f_{2}(x,y-2^{-j}z_{2})|d\sigma(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{(1)}f_{1}(x,y)\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}|f_{2}(x,y-2^{-j}z_{2})|(1-|z_{2}|^{2})^{(d-2)/2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}d\sigma(z_{1})~dz_{2}

\displaystyle\leq M^{(1)}f_{1}(x,y)\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{B^{d}(0,a)}|f_{2}(x,y-2^{-j}z_{2})|~dz_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{(1)}f_{1}(x,y)M^{(2)}f_{2}(x,y).

The
L^{p}
-estimates of
M^{(j)}
for
j=1,2
proves the desired result. Similarly, we can obtain the
L^{p}
-estimates for the remaining two operators in
Lemma

3.1
. ∎

Continuing with the proof of
Theorem

1.2
, note that we need to establish
appropriate
L^{p}
-estimates of the fourth term

\sum_{{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})\right|

in the
Equation

3.1
. The desired estimate of this term is a consequence of the following two key propositions.
The first proposition is a weak type
\left({\mathsf{p}_{d}},{\mathsf{p}_{d}},\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}\right)
-estimate
with a quadratic growth on the scale
{k}
. Indeed, we have

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
. Then, for
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
, we have

\Big\|\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})|\Big\|_{L^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2},\infty}}\lesssim|{k}|^{2}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}.

The second result provides a strong type
(2,2,1)
-estimate with
exponential decay in the parameter
{k}
. In particular, we have

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists
\mathfrak{C}>0
such that for
j\in{\mathbb{Z}},\;{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})|\Big\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-\mathfrak{C}|{k}|}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Observe that the
L^{p}
-estimates of the fourth term

\sum_{{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})\right|
in
Equation

3.1

for the entire range of exponents, as claimed in
Theorem

1.2
, follow
by interpolating between
Proposition

3.2
and
Proposition

3.3
and finally summing over the
parameter
{k}
. Therefore, the proof of
Theorem

1.2
is complete under the assumption that

Proposition

3.2
and
Proposition

3.3
hold.

The proof of

By an application of modified slicing argument and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{j,{k}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\Big|A_{r}^{(1)}\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}\Delta_{j+k_{1}+1}^{(1)}f_{1}(x,y)A_{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}^{(2)}\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}\Delta_{j+k_{2}+1}^{(2)}f_{2}(x,y)\Big|

\displaystyle\hskip 25.60747pt\times r^{d-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\;drdxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\Big\|A_{r}^{(1)}\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}\Delta_{j+k_{1}+1}^{(1)}f_{1}\Big\|_{2}\Big\|A_{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}^{(2)}\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}\Delta_{j+k_{2}+1}^{(2)}f_{2}\Big\|_{2}r^{d-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}drdxdy

\displaystyle 2^{-(k_{1}+k_{2})(\frac{d-1}{2})}\|\Delta_{j+k_{1}+1}^{(1)}f_{1}\|_{2}\|\Delta_{j+k_{2}+1}^{(2)}f_{2}\|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{4}}\;dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-|{k}|(\frac{d-1}{2})}\|\Delta_{j+k_{1}+1}^{(1)}f_{1}\|_{2}\|\Delta_{j+k_{2}+1}^{(2)}f_{2}\|_{2}.

In the fourth line of the expression above we have used the Plancherel theorem in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for each fiber.

We conclude the proof of

4.
Fiberwise bilinear Calderón-Zygmund theory: Proof of
Proposition

3.2

First, we establish the following
L^{p}
-estimate for the single scale spherical average
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
. We have

\|\mathfrak{A}_{1}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})},\;\text{for all}\;f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d}).

Proof of
Proposition

4.1

Note that using the localisation trick, see
[
22
, Proposition 4.1]
, it is enough to prove
L^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})\times L^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
-boundedness of the operator
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
. Consider

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{A}_{1}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}f_{1}(x+z_{1},y)f_{2}(x,y+z_{2})\;d\sigma(z_{1},z_{2})\Big|dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}|f_{1}(x+z_{1},y)f_{2}(x,y+z_{2})|\;dxdyd\sigma(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}|f_{1}(x,y)f_{2}(x-z_{1},y+z_{2})|\;dxdyd\sigma(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}|f_{1}(x,y)|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}|f_{2}(x-z_{1},y+z_{2})|\;d\sigma(z_{1},z_{2})dxdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}|f_{2}(\cdot-z_{1},\cdot+z_{2})|\;d\sigma(z_{1},z_{2})\Big\|_{2d+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}\|f_{2}\|_{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}.

∎

Next, we will employ a fiberwise Calderón-Zygmund decomposition of functions in
L^{p}-
spaces based on the decomposition obtained in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
. For
\alpha>0
and fixed
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we apply the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition at the scale
\alpha
and obtain
f(\cdot,y)=g(\cdot,y)+\sum\limits_{i}h_{i}(\cdot,y)
, where
h_{i}(\cdot,y)
is supported on dyadic cubes
Q_{i,y}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and

(1)

\|g(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\|f(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and
\|g(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\alpha
.

(2)

For each
i
,
\|h_{i}(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{p}\lesssim\alpha^{p}|Q_{i,y}|
and
\int_{Q_{i,y}}h_{i}(x,y)~dx=0
.

(3)

\sum\limits_{i}|Q_{i,y}|\lesssim\alpha^{-p}\|f(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{p}
.

Now, we argue for the proof of
Proposition

3.2
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\|f_{1}\|_{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}=\|f_{2}\|_{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}=1
. We apply
Lemma

4.2
to
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}
in
x,y
variables respectively at the scale
c_{0}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}},
for some
c_{0}>0
to be determined later and obtain
f_{l}=g_{l}+h_{l},\;l=1,2
, where

•

\|g_{1}(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
h_{1}=\sum\limits_{\beta_{1}}h_{1,\beta_{1}}(\cdot,y)
, where
h_{1,\beta_{1}}(\cdot,y)
is supported on dyadic cubes
Q_{\beta_{1},y}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with

\displaystyle\|h_{1,\beta_{1}}(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}|Q_{\beta_{1},y}|,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\beta_{1}}|Q_{\beta_{1},y}|\lesssim\alpha^{-\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}\|f_{1}(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}},

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\beta_{1},y}}h_{1,\beta_{1}}(x,y)~dx=0.

•

\|g_{2}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
h_{2}=\sum\limits_{\beta_{2}}h_{2,\beta_{2}}(x,\cdot)
, where
h_{2,\beta_{2}}(x,\cdot)
is supported on dyadic cubes
Q_{\beta_{2},x}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with

\displaystyle\|h_{2,\beta_{2}}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}|Q_{\beta_{2},x}|,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\beta_{2}}|Q_{\beta_{2},x}|\lesssim\alpha^{-\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}\|f_{2}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}},

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\beta_{2},x}}h_{2,\beta_{2}}(x,y)~dy=0.

Contribution of
(g_{1},g_{2})

Since
\|g_{l}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have
\left\|\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{j,k}(g_{1},g_{2})|\right\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}^{2}\alpha
. We choose
c_{0}
small so that
c_{0}^{2}\alpha\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}
.

Contribution of
(g_{1},h_{2})
and
(h_{1},g_{2})

We note that
\|\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}g_{1}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}
uniformly in
j
and
k_{1}
. An argument similar to that in
Lemma

3.1
, we can see that
\left\|\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{j,k}(1,h_{2})|\right\|_{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}\leq\|h_{2}\|_{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left|\left\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{j,k}(g_{1},h_{2})(x,y)|>\alpha\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\alpha^{-{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}\left\|\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{j,k}(g_{1},h_{2})|\right\|_{\mathsf{p}_{d}}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\alpha^{-{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}\alpha^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathsf{p}_{d}}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{-\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}.

Similarly, we can get the desired estimate for
(h_{1},g_{2})
by interchanging the roles of the indices.

Contribution of
(h_{1},h_{2})

Let
Q^{*}_{\beta,y}
be the concentric cube with
Q_{\beta,y}
and measure
4^{d}|Q_{\beta,y}|
. Now, define

E=\{(x,y):x\in\cup_{\beta_{1}}Q^{*}_{\beta_{1},y}\}\cup\{(x,y):y\in\cup_{\beta_{2}}Q^{*}_{\beta_{2},x}\}.

We can see that

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{\beta_{1}}|Q^{*}_{\beta_{1},y}|dy+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{\beta_{2}}|Q^{*}_{\beta_{2},x}|dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f_{1}(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}dy+\alpha^{-\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f_{2}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}dx

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}\left(\|f_{1}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}\right)\lesssim\alpha^{-\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}.

Thus, we need to show that

(4.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\setminus E}\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2})(x,y)\right|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}dxdy\lesssim|{k}|^{2}.

We define
h_{1}^{i_{1}}=\sum\limits_{|Q_{\beta_{1},y}|=2^{-i_{1}}}h_{1,\beta_{1}}
and
h_{2}^{i_{2}}=\sum\limits_{|Q_{\beta_{2},x}|=2^{-i_{2}}}h_{2,\beta_{2}}
, write

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2})(x,y)\right|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\sum_{i_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2}^{i_{2}})(x,y)\right|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2}^{i_{2}})(x,y)\right|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}.

We have the following lemma stating
L^{p}-
bounds for terms in sums above.

Lemma 4.3
.

The following bound holds uniformly in
j,{k},i_{1},i_{2}
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\setminus E}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2}^{i_{2}})(x,y)\right|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}dxdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\min_{l=1,2}\min\{2^{i_{l}-j},2^{\frac{j+k_{l}-i_{l}}{2}},1\}\prod_{l=1}^{2}\|h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}.

Proof.

Using
Proposition

4.1
, we can get that

(4.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\setminus E}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2}^{i_{2}})(x,y)\right|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}dxdy\lesssim\prod_{l=1}^{2}\|\Delta_{j+k_{l}}^{(l)}h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}.

First, note that the operator
\Delta_{j+k_{l}}
is defined by convolution with an integrable function whose norm is independent of
l
. Thus, for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

(4.3)

\|\Delta_{j+k_{l}}^{(l)}h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim\|h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

When
i_{l}>j+k_{l}
, using the moment condition
\int h_{l,\beta_{l}}(x,y)~dx=0
and the fact that each
h_{l,\beta_{l}}
is supported on an cube of length
2^{-i_{l}}
, we get

(4.4)

\|\Delta_{j+k_{l}}^{(l)}h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim 2^{j+k_{l}-i_{l}}\|h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

Interpolating estimates (
4.4
) and (
4.3
) for
p=\infty
, we get that

\|\Delta_{j+k_{l}}^{(l)}h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim 2^{\frac{(j+k_{l}-i_{l})2d}{2d+1}}\|h_{l}^{i_{l}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

Next, we consider the case when
i_{1}<j
and
x\notin E
. Note that
x
lies at a distance at least
2\cdot 2^{-i_{1}}
from cubes
Q_{\beta_{1},y}
of measure
2^{-i_{1}}
. Since
i_{1}<j
, the distance of
x+2^{-j}z_{1}
from cubes
Q_{\beta_{1},y}
is bigger than
2^{-i_{1}}
. Thus, we can write

\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2}^{i_{2}})(x,y)=\mathfrak{A}_{2^{-j}}(\widetilde{\psi}\ast^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2}^{i_{2}})(x,y),

where
\widetilde{\psi}(x)=\psi_{j+k_{1}}(x)\chi_{|x|>2^{-i_{1}}}
and satisfies the estimate for any
N
,

\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{1}}=\|\psi_{j+k_{1}}\chi_{|x|>2^{-i_{1}}}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-(j+k_{1}-i_{1})N}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}\ast^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(j+k_{1}-i_{1})N}\|h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

Similarly when
i_{2}<j
, interchanging roles of
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
, we get the estimate

\|\widetilde{\psi}\ast^{(1)}h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim 2^{-(j+k_{2}-i_{2})N}\|h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

∎

To obtain the estimate (
4.1
), we sum in
j
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\setminus E}\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\mathfrak{A}_{2^{j}}(\Delta_{j+k_{1}}^{(1)}h_{1},\Delta_{j+k_{2}}^{(2)}h_{2})(x,y)\right|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}dxdy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{k}|\min\{1,2^{\frac{-(|i_{1}-i_{2}|-|{k}|)}{2}}\}\|h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}\|h_{2}^{i_{2}}\|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}^{\frac{\mathsf{p}_{d}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|{k}|\left(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\min\{1,2^{\frac{-(|i_{1}-i_{2}|-|{k}|)}{2}}\}\|h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\times\left(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\min\{1,2^{\frac{-(|i_{1}-i_{2}|-|{k}|)}{2}}\}\|h_{2}^{i_{2}}\|^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|{k}|^{2}\left(\sum_{i_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|h_{1}^{i_{1}}\|^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|h_{2}^{i_{2}}\|^{\mathsf{p}_{d}}_{L^{{\mathsf{p}_{d}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|{k}|^{2}.

∎

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\eta_{m}(x,y)=1
, where

\eta_{m}(x,y)=\eta((x,y)-m)
. We also consider a function
\widetilde{\eta}
which is supported in
[-2,2]^{2}
and takes value one on
[-1,1]^{2}
. Observe that

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f_{1}*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,y)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{1}(x-z,y)\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(z)\;dz\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f_{2}*_{2}\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(x,y)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{2}(x,y-z)\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(z)\;dz,

where
\widehat{\widetilde{\psi}}_{\lambda}
is a smooth function supported in

and
\widehat{\widetilde{\psi}}_{\lambda}=1
on
\{\lambda\leq|\xi_{1}|\leq 2\lambda\}
and
\widehat{\widetilde{\phi}}_{\lambda}
is a smooth function supported in
\left\{|\xi_{2}|\leq 4\lambda\right\}
and
\widehat{\widetilde{\phi}}_{\lambda}=1
on
\{|\xi_{2}|\leq 2\lambda\}
.
We have,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{0}\left(f_{1}*_{1}\psi_{\lambda},f_{2}*_{2}\phi_{\lambda}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\|T_{loc}\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(f_{1}*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}\right),\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(f_{2}*_{2}\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\right)\right)\eta_{m}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\left\|T_{loc}\left(\left(f_{1}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\right)*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda},\left(f_{2}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\right)*_{2}\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\left\|T_{loc}\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(f_{1}*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}\right)-\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{1}\right)*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda},\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(f_{2}*_{2}\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\right)\right)\eta_{m}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\left\|T_{loc}\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(f_{1}*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}\right),\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(f_{2}*_{2}\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\right)-\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{2}\right)*_{2}\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\right)\eta_{m}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

The terms in
I_{1}
are given by operators of the form (
5.1
), where
\zeta(x,y,t)=\widetilde{\eta}_{m}(x+\cos t,y)\widetilde{\eta}_{m}(x,y+\sin t)\zeta_{0}(t)
. Hence, the
L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}

and use of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with the fact that
\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}^{2}\lesssim 1

I_{1}
.

The estimate for the commutator terms
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
are proved in a similar manner. Therefore, we indicate the proof for the term
I_{2}
only. We observe, by mean value theorem, that

\displaystyle\left|\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(f_{1}*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}\right)-\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{1}\right)*_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\right)(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\left|\widetilde{\eta}_{m}(x,y)-\widetilde{\eta}_{m}(u,y)\right|\left|f_{1}(u,y)\right|\left|\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x-u)\right|du

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\int\frac{\lambda|x-u|}{(1+\lambda|x-u|)^{3}}\quad\left|f_{1}(u,y)\right|du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\left|f_{1}\right|*_{1}\Psi_{\lambda},

where
\Psi_{\lambda}(y)=\lambda(1+\lambda|y|)^{2}
. Therefore it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\left|f_{1}\right|*_{1}\Psi_{\lambda}\big)(x+\cos t,y)\big(\left|f_{2}\right|*_{2}|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}|\big)(x,y+\sin t)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m}\left|\eta_{m}(x,y)\right|\zeta_{0}(t)\;dtdxdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big\|\left|f_{1}\right|*_{1}\Psi_{\lambda}\big\|_{2}\big\|\left|f_{2}\right|*_{2}|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}|\big\|_{2}\ \zeta_{0}(t)\;dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{2}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{2}.

5.3.
Reduction of the
(2,2,1)

-estimate

We reduce the
(2,2,1)

-estimate by using the

improving property for the linear spherical average. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{loc}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\right\|_{1}\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f_{1}(x,y)\right|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f_{2}(x-\cos t,y+\sin t)\right|dtdxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mid f_{2}((x,y)-(\cos t,\sin t))|\;dt\right\|_{3}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{\frac{3}{2}}.

Thus by Sobolev interpolation and the previous estimate, it remains to prove

\left\|T_{loc}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\lambda^{-\epsilon}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{\infty},

whenever
\operatorname{supp}\hat{f}_{1}\subset\{\lambda\leq|\xi_{1}|\leq 2\lambda\}
and

5.4.
Spatial decomposition of the functions

We now introduce a structural decomposition of the function based on the frequency parameter
\lambda
. We redefine
\eta_{m}(x,y)=\eta\left(\lambda^{\gamma}(x,y)-m\right)
for some
\gamma\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
to be chosen later. Also, we denote
\widetilde{\eta}_{m}
as the smooth function taking value one on the support of
\eta_{m}
and supported in the cube
Q_{m}
of side length
2\lambda^{-\gamma}
centered at the point
\lambda^{-\gamma}m\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. For
l=1,2
, we write

\displaystyle f_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\eta_{m}f_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}*_{l}\left(\eta_{m}f_{l}\right)+\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}\left(\eta_{m}\left(\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}*_{l}f_{l}\right)-\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}*_{l}\left(\eta_{m}f_{l}\right)\right).

We set
f_{l,m}=\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}*_{l}\left(\eta_{m}f_{l}\right)
and
f_{l,m,err}=\eta_{m}\left(\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}*_{l}f_{l}\right)-\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}*_{l}\left(\eta_{m}f_{l}\right)

As in
Section

5.2
, we have that
\left|f_{l,m,err}\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma-1}\|f_{l}\|_{\infty}
by mean value theorem. We write our operator as

\displaystyle\left\|T_{loc}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{loc}\left(\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{1,m},\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{2,m}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\left\|T_{loc}\left(\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{1,m},\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{2,m,err}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\left\|T_{loc}\left(\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{1,m,err},\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{2,m}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\left\|T_{loc}\left(\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{1,m,err},\sum_{m}\widetilde{\eta}_{m}f_{2,m,err}\right)\right\|_{1}.

The last three terms in the expression above are bounded by a constant multiple of
\lambda^{\gamma-1}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{\infty}
. Therefore, it remains to estimate the first term.

5.5.
Decomposition of the operator based on the degeneracy at
t=0

Let
0<\tau<\gamma-\frac{1}{2}
be a constant to be determined later. We decompose the operator as

\displaystyle T_{loc}(f_{1},f_{2})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{\pi\lambda^{\tau}}{6}\right)\right\rceil}T_{loc}^{\ell}(f_{1},f_{2})\text{, where }

\displaystyle T_{loc}^{0}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-\tau}}f_{1}(x+\cos t,y)g(x,y+\sin t)\zeta(x,y,t)dt~\text{and }

\displaystyle T_{loc}^{\ell}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)=\int_{2^{\ell}\lambda^{-\tau}}^{2^{\ell+1}\lambda^{-\tau}}f_{1}(x+\cos t,y)f_{2}(x,y+\sin t)\zeta(x,y,t)\;dt

for
\ell=1,\dots,\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{\pi\lambda^{\tau}}{6}\right)\right\rceil.
We note that
\left\|T_{loc}^{0}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\lambda^{-\tau}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{\infty}
. Therefore the problem boils down to establishing
(\infty,\infty,1)-
bound for the operator:

T_{loc}^{\delta}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)(x,y)=\int_{\delta}^{2\delta}f_{1}(x+\cos t,y)f_{2}(x,y+\sin t)\zeta(x,y,t)dt\text{, for }\lambda^{-\tau}\leq\delta<1.

More precisely, we will establish the following estimate for
0<\delta<1
.

(5.2)

\|T_{loc}^{\delta}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{1}\lesssim\delta^{-1}\lambda^{-\mathfrak{c}}\|f_{1}\|_{\infty}\|f_{2}\|_{\infty},\text{ for some constant }\mathfrak{c}>0.

Indeed, suppose the inequality (
5.2
) is true for all
0<\delta<1
, then we have that

\displaystyle\|T_{loc}^{\delta}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{1}\lesssim\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{\pi\lambda^{\tau}}{6}\right)\right\rceil}(2^{\ell}\lambda^{-\tau})^{-1}\lambda^{-\mathfrak{c}}\|f_{1}\|_{\infty}\|f_{2}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{-\mathfrak{c}+\tau}\|f_{1}\|_{\infty}\|f_{2}\|_{\infty}.

Note that the desired estimate follows by choosing
\tau<\mathfrak{c}
. Therefore, we need to prove inequality (
5.2
). In order to prove this inequality, we require a structural decomposition of the functions based on a frequency pruning lemma obtained in
[
8
]
, which is the content of the next section.

5.6.
Frequency Pruning and a structural decomposition of the functions.

For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the multiplicative derivative by

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{s}f(x)

\displaystyle=f(x+s)\overline{f(x)}\text{, for }f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{(1)}_{s}f(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x+s,y)\overline{f(x,y)}\text{, for }f:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{(2)}_{s}f(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x,y+s)\overline{f(x,y)}\text{, for }f:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}.

We decompose the functions into two pieces, one having a small
L^{2}
-norm of the multiplicative derivative and the other having a controlled Fourier support for each fiber. For this purpose we state the frequency pruning lemma from
[
8
]
,

Lemma 5.1

(
[
8
]
Lemma 3.2)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
R\geq 1
and
\varrho\in(0,1)
. There exists a decomposition

f=f_{\sharp}+f_{\flat},

such that the following conditions hold true:

(1)

We have the norm control,

\left\|f_{\sharp}\right\|_{2}+\left\|f_{\flat}\right\|_{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

(2)

The function
f_{\sharp}
is given by

f_{\sharp}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{\varrho}}h_{n}(x)e^{i\alpha_{n}x}

such that each
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and

is a smooth function satisfying

(a)

\left\|\partial^{N}h_{n}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{N}R^{N}\|f\|_{\infty}
for all integers
N\geq 0
.

(b)

\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}
.

(c)

\widehat{h}_{n}
is supported in
[-R,R]
. Moreover, the support of
\widehat{f}_{\sharp}
is contained in the support of
\widehat{f}
.

(d)

\mathcal{N}_{\varrho}\lesssim\varrho^{-1}
.

(3)

One has the bound

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|\xi|\leq R}\left|\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{s}f_{\flat}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi ds\lesssim\varrho\|f\|_{2}^{4}.

We apply the lemma as above to the maps
x\mapsto f_{1,m}(x,y)
and
y\mapsto f_{2,m}(x,y)
with
R=\delta\lambda^{\gamma+\mu_{1}}
and
\varrho=\lambda^{-\mu_{2}}
for some
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
to obtain,

\displaystyle f_{l,m}

\displaystyle=f_{l,m,\sharp}+f_{l,m,\flat},\quad l=1,2.

\displaystyle f_{1,m,\sharp}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{1}}h_{1,m,n}(x,y)e^{i\alpha_{m,n}(y)x},

\displaystyle f_{2,m,\sharp}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{2}}h_{2,m,n}(x,y)e^{i\beta_{m,n}(x)y},

where
\left|\mathcal{N}_{1}\right|+\left|\mathcal{N}_{2}\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\mu_{2}}

\alpha_{m,n}
and
\beta_{m,n}
are measurable functions with
\left|\alpha_{m,n}\right|\sim\lambda,\quad\left|\beta_{m,n}\right|\lesssim\lambda
. Moreover,
h_{l,m,n}
are smooth functions with
\left|\partial_{l}^{N}h_{l,m,n}\right|\lesssim(\delta\lambda^{\gamma+\mu_{1}})^{N}\left\|f_{l,m}\right\|_{\infty}
uniformly in
m
ad
n
. We have following control over the
L^{2}
-norm of the multiplicative derivative of

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\left|\xi_{2}\right|\leq R}\left|\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{s}^{(l)}f_{l,m,\flat}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi ds\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu_{2}-3\gamma}\left\|f_{l,m}\right\|_{\infty}^{4}.

To see this, using
Lemma

5.1
(3) for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\left|\xi_{1}\right|\leq R}\left|\widehat{\mathscr{D}_{s}^{(1)}f_{1,m,\flat}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu_{2}}\int\|\mathcal{F}_{y}f_{1,m,\flat}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{4}dy

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-\mu_{2}}\Big\|\|\mathcal{F}_{y}f_{1,m,\flat}\|_{L^{4}_{y}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\mu_{2}}\Big\|\|\mathcal{F}_{y}f_{1,m,\flat}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{y}f_{1,m,\flat}\|_{L^{2}_{y}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\mu_{2}}\Big\|\|\mathcal{F}_{y}(f_{1,m,\flat}\ast_{(2)}f_{1,m,\flat})\|_{L^{2}_{y}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\mu_{2}}\Big\|\|f_{1,m,\flat}\ast_{(2)}f_{1,m,\flat}\|_{L^{2}_{y}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu_{2}-3\gamma}\left\|f_{1,m}\right\|_{\infty}^{4},

where we have used Plancherel’s theorem in the second to last equality and support condition of
f_{1,m}
in the last inequality. Similarly, we can obtain the estimate for

Using the above frequency decomposition, we write,

\displaystyle T_{loc}^{\delta}\Big(\sum_{m}\tilde{\eta}_{m}f_{1,m},\sum_{m}\tilde{\eta}_{m}f_{2,m}\Big)=

\displaystyle T_{loc}^{\delta}\left(f_{1,\flat},f_{2,\flat}\right)+T_{loc}^{\delta}\left(f_{1,\flat},f_{2,\sharp}\right)+T_{loc}^{\delta}\left(f_{1,\sharp},f_{2,\flat}\right)

f_{l,b}=\sum\tilde{\eta}_{m}f_{l,m,b}

f_{l,\sharp}=\sum\tilde{\eta}_{m}f_{l,m,\sharp},\;l=1,2

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\lambda,\delta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0

(3)

are obtained by a reduction to the analysis of sublevel sets

This reduction to the sublevel sets is presented in the following lemma, the proof of which is postponed to

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

and

for all

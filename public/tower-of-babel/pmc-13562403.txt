Efficacy and safety of liposomal irinotecan plus nimotuzumab in immune checkpoint inhibitor-refractory recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma: a single-arm, open-label, phase II trial
Authors: Shuiqing He, Wei-Xin Bei, Nian Lu, Ze-Yu Zhao, Yanfang Ye, Chunyan Chen, Linquan Tang, Liting Liu, Shuhui Lv, Yan-Qun Xiang, Lin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundPatients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (R/M NPC) who progress after immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy have limited treatment options and poor outcomes. We evaluated the efficacy and safety of liposomal irinotecan plus nimotuzumab in patients with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-positive, ICI-refractory R/M NPC.MethodsIn this single-arm, open-label, phase II trial (NCT06414577) conducted in China, patients with EGFR-positive, ICI-refractory R/M NPC were enrolled between May 27, 2024, and January 9, 2025. Patients received intravenous liposomal irinotecan (70 mg/m2) plus nimotuzumab (400 mg) every 2 weeks for up to eight cycles. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1.FindingsThirty-six heavily pretreated patients were enrolled, of whom 12 (33.3%) had received three or more prior lines of therapy for advanced disease. In the intention-to-treat population, 13 of 36 patients achieved an objective response (ORR 36.1%; 95% CI 20.8–53.8), and 22 of 36 patients achieved disease control (61.1%). Median progression-free survival was 3.0 months (95% CI, 2.2–4.1), and median duration of response was 2.3 months (95% CI 1.5–3.1). After a median follow-up of 22.0 months, the median overall survival was 14.1 months (95% CI, 8.7-not estimable). Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in eight of 36 patients (22.2%), and no treatment-related deaths were observed. In a post-hoc exploratory analysis, an early decline of at least 50% in plasma Epstein–Barr virus DNA was associated with a higher ORR.InterpretationLiposomal irinotecan plus nimotuzumab showed preliminary antitumor activity and manageable safety in heavily pretreated patients with ICI-refractory R/M NPC. However, response durability was limited, and further comparative studies are needed to evaluate its clinical value and identify patients most likely to derive durable benefit.FundingSupported by grants from the 10.13039/501100001809National Natural Science Foundation of China, Science and Technology Program of Guangdong Esophageal Cancer Institute, and 10.13039/501100002858China Postdoctoral Science Foundation.

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Before undertaking this study, we searched PubMed, the American Society of Clinical Oncology Meeting Library, and European Society for Medical Oncology Congress and Annals of Oncology conference proceedings from database inception to April 4, 2026, without language restrictions, using combinations of the terms “nasopharyngeal carcinoma”, “recurrent or metastatic”, “programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)”, “programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1)”, “immune checkpoint inhibitor”, “refractory”, “resistant”, “salvage treatment”, “nimotuzumab”, and “irinotecan”. After screening and deduplication, 14 clinically relevant reports were identified, all of which were published in English. Published studies in this setting evaluated several salvage approaches, including antiangiogenic combinations, dual immune blockade, bispecific antibodies, and other targeted strategies. However, outcomes were variable, and no clearly established standard of care has emerged for patients with immune checkpoint inhibitor refractory recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma. Data specifically evaluating epidermal growth factor receptor-directed therapy combined with liposomal irinotecan in this population were lacking.

Added value of this study

To our knowledge, this is among the first prospective phase II studies to evaluate liposomal irinotecan plus nimotuzumab in EGFR-positive, ICI-refractory R/M NPC. In a heavily pretreated population, this combination showed preliminary antitumor activity with a manageable safety profile, although the duration of disease control was limited. In post-hoc exploratory analyses, an early decline in plasma Epstein–Barr virus DNA was associated with improved objective response and longer progression-free survival.

Implications of all the available evidence

These findings suggest that liposomal irinotecan plus nimotuzumab warrants further evaluation in selected patients with ICI-refractory R/M NPC. Given the short median DoR and PFS and the single-arm design of this study, further biomarker-informed and comparative studies are needed to define its clinical value and identify patients most likely to derive durable benefit.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is an Epstein–Barr virus-associated malignancy with a distinct geographic distribution, with more than 70% of new cases occurring in East and Southeast Asia.1

While multimodal therapy has improved locoregional control, distant metastasis remains the primary cause of treatment failure, affecting 10–37% of patients with locoregionally advanced disease.2, 3, 4, 5 Current National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines recommend gemcitabine plus cisplatin (GP) regimens combined with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors as first-line therapy for recurrent or metastatic (R/M) NPC patients.6, 7, 8 Nevertheless, approximately one-third to one-half of patients eventually experience disease progression on chemoimmunotherapy, with a median progression-free survival (PFS) of only 9.7–21.4 months.6, 7, 8 Available evidence suggests that salvage treatments following immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) failure are associated with heterogeneous and generally modest efficacy, often accompanied by clinically significant toxicity.9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 Therefore, new therapeutic strategies are urgently needed in this setting.

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is overexpressed in more than 80% of NPC cases and plays a key role in tumor proliferation, metastasis, and poor prognosis.16,17 Nimotuzumab, a humanized anti-EGFR monoclonal antibody, may exert antitumor effects through both inhibition of EGFR signaling and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.18,19 Although evidence for nimotuzumab monotherapy in R/M NPC is limited, prior studies combining nimotuzumab with chemotherapy have suggested potential clinical activity,20,21 supporting further evaluation of EGFR-directed approaches in this disease.

The rationale for combining anti-EGFR agents with topoisomerase I inhibitors is an emerging area of investigation, largely spurred by the demonstrated activity of this drug class in various solid tumors.22, 23, 24 Irinotecan, a topoisomerase I inhibitor, has been shown to induce apoptosis in NPC cell lines,25 and is recommended for later-line treatment in clinical guidelines.26 More recently, emerging clinical data have suggested that strategies enhancing tumor delivery of topoisomerase I inhibitors may be particularly effective in the post-ICI setting.27,28 Liposomal irinotecan was developed to improve the pharmacokinetic properties of conventional irinotecan and to enhance intratumoral drug accumulation via the enhanced permeability and retention effect.29,30 Given the complementary mechanisms of EGFR inhibition and topoisomerase I blockade, and the limited therapeutic options available after ICI failure, we conducted a single-arm, open-label, phase II trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of liposomal irinotecan combined with nimotuzumab, administered every 2 weeks for up to eight cycles, in patients with ICI-refractory R/M NPC.

Methods

Study design and participants

This was an investigator-initiated single-arm, open-label, phase II clinical trial conducted at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center in China between May 27, 2024 and January 9, 2025. The study was designed to assess the efficacy and safety of liposomal irinotecan combined with nimotuzumab in patients with histologically confirmed R/M NPC who experienced disease progression following at least one line of systemic therapy containing ICIs. Race and ethnicity data were not collected because this was a single-center study conducted in China and race or ethnicity was not prespecified as a study variable in the protocol.

Patients were required to meet the following criteria: histologically confirmed R/M NPC with measurable lesions per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1, documented disease progression after at least one prior ICI-containing regimen administered in the recurrent or metastatic setting, EGFR-positive tumor status confirmed by immunohistochemistry (defined as ≥10% of tumor cells exhibiting positive membranous staining), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–1, estimated life expectancy ≥3 months, and adequate organ function. The main exclusion criteria included prior treatment with anti-EGFR monoclonal antibodies or topoisomerase inhibitors (including topoisomerase I inhibitor-based antibody–drug conjugates [ADCs]) in the recurrent or metastatic setting, as well as known hypersensitivity to monoclonal antibodies or irinotecan-containing formulations. Full eligibility criteria are detailed in the protocol (Supplementary Material). No major protocol deviations affecting patient eligibility, treatment administration, radiologic response assessment, or endpoint analysis were identified during the study conduct.

Ethics statement

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (Approval No. SL-B2024-110) and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06414577). All patients provided written informed consent prior to study enrollment. This study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Treatment

Liposomal irinotecan (70 mg/m2) and nimotuzumab (400 mg) were administered intravenously every 2 weeks. This study did not include a formal safety run-in phase. The liposomal irinotecan dose was consistent with the dose used in the NAPOLI-1 trial for pancreatic cancer,31 while the nimotuzumab dose was informed by previous studies combining nimotuzumab with chemotherapy in metastatic NPC.20 Treatment with the combination therapy was planned for a maximum of 8 cycles, primarily to mitigate the potential for cumulative toxicity associated with long-term irinotecan administration, based on prior clinical experience.31 For patients who completed 8 cycles and achieved disease control (i.e., complete response [CR], partial response [PR], or stable disease [SD]) without unacceptable toxicity, single-agent nimotuzumab could be continued as maintenance therapy until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Dose modifications (1–2 times) of liposomal irinotecan were permitted for grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Treatment with liposomal irinotecan was permanently discontinued in the case of a third occurrence of grade 3–4 toxicity or the development of interstitial pneumonitis or investigator-judged clinically significant adverse events (AEs). Full dose modification criteria are provided in the study protocol (Supplementary Material).

Assessment

Tumor response assessments were conducted by an independent radiologist using RECIST version 1.1 criteria. Radiologic evaluations were systematically scheduled at baseline, followed by assessments every 4 cycles during the first 4 months of therapy and every 3 months thereafter until documentation of disease progression or protocol-defined discontinuation criteria. AEs were prospectively documented at each treatment visit, with severity grading performed using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Plasma Epstein–Barr virus (EBV) DNA level was analyzed by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a CR or PR as the best overall response assessed by RECIST version 1.1. Secondary endpoints included PFS, defined as the time from treatment initiation to documented disease progression or death from any cause; overall survival (OS), defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause; disease control rate (DCR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving CR, PR, or SD; duration of response (DoR), defined as the time from the first evidence of response to disease progression or death, and safety profile. Efficacy was assessed in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, comprising all enrolled patients. Safety was evaluated in all patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment.

Statistical analysis

This study was designed according to Simon's optimal two-stage design for phase II trials. The sample size was calculated to provide 80% power at a one-sided α level of 0.05. An ORR of 11% was defined as the null response rate based on previous study,10 and an ORR of 30% as the target response rate of clinical interest. In the first stage, 10 evaluable patients were required. If 2 or more objective responses were observed, enrollment would proceed to the second stage to reach a total of 33 evaluable patients; otherwise, the trial would be terminated for futility. The regimen would be considered promising if at least 7 of the 33 evaluable patients achieved an objective response. Assuming an approximate dropout rate of 8%, the planned total sample size was 36 patients.

ORR and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were estimated using the Clopper-Pearson method. Continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Assessment of plasma EBV DNA dynamics as a potential biomarker of response was conducted as a post-hoc exploratory analysis. Comparisons of ORR between subgroups were conducted using the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. PFS and OS were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method, with median survival times and 95% CIs reported where estimable; survival curves were compared using the log-rank test. All statistical analyses were performed using R software, version 4.3.0. For secondary and exploratory analyses, a two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Role of the funding source

The funder of the study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or writing of the report.

Results

Patient characteristics and treatment exposure

Between May 27, 2024 and January 9, 2025, 42 patients with recurrent/metastatic NPC were screened for eligibility. After exclusion of four patients who did not meet screening criteria and two patients who withdrew consent, 36 patients were enrolled and comprised both the intention-to-treat (ITT) and safety populations (Fig. 1). Among the 36 patients in the ITT population, three discontinued the study before the first post-baseline radiologic assessment. Two patients experienced fatal events that were deemed unrelated to study treatment: one died from massive epistaxis in the context of locally recurrent disease with vascular involvement, and the other died from cerebral infarction, which was considered likely related to the patient's pre-existing vascular comorbidities, including hypertension. A third patient withdrew consent before the first tumor assessment. Efficacy

Per standard ITT principles, the three patients who discontinued early without any post-baseline tumor assessment were classified as non-responders. Among the 10 patients enrolled in the first stage, 4 achieved a confirmed objective response, thereby meeting the prespecified criteria for expansion to full study enrollment. Early EBV DNA decline was analyzed in 31 of the 33 response-evaluable patients; the remaining two patients were not included because comparable on-treatment EBV DNA data were unavailable. In this post-hoc exploratory analysis, patients whose plasma EBV DNA levels decreased by ≥ 50% at the first post-treatment assessment appeared to have higher ORR than those with <50% decrease (11/21 vs. 1/10, p = 0.046; Supplementary Table S2). This early EBV DNA decline was also associated with longer progression-free survival (p = 0.0021) (Supplementary Figure S1A), whereas no significant difference in overall survival was observed (p = 0.53; Supplementary Figure S1B). In subgroup analyses, ORR estimates did not reach statistical significance according to baseline EBV DNA level (<1820 vs. >1820 copies/mL: 41.2% vs. 37.5%, p = 0.829), number of prior therapy lines for advanced disease (1 vs. >1; 46.2% vs. 35.0%, p = 0.522), or ICI treatment-free interval (<2.57 vs. >2.57 months; 41.2% vs. 37.5%, p = 0.829). Similarly, ORR did not differ significantly according to the presence or absence of liver metastasis (33.3% vs. 40.0%, p = 0.587), lung metastasis (40.0% vs. 34.6%, p = 0.763), bone metastasis (28.6% vs. 46.7%, p = 0.265) or age (≤43 vs. > 43 years: 44.4% vs. 27.8%, p = 0.298, Supplementary Table S2). Given the small sample size and limited statistical power, the absence of statistically significant subgroup differences should not be interpreted as evidence of no clinically relevant difference. These analyses should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating.

Safety

All 36 patients experienced at least one treatment-related adverse event (Table 3). The most common TRAEs of any grade were anemia (n = 33, 91.7%), decreased appetite (n = 26, 72.2%), diarrhea (n = 25, 69.4%), fatigue (n = 24, 66.7%), nausea (n = 24, 66.7%), leukopenia (n = 23, 63.9%), and vomiting (n = 22, 61.1%). Grade 3 or higher TRAEs occurred in eight patients (22.2%), most commonly diarrhea (n = 3), leukopenia (n = 2), and neutropenia (n = 2). Other grade 3 TRAEs included decreased appetite, fatigue, vomiting, insomnia, febrile neutropenia, abdominal pain, mucositis, cholecystitis, and appendicitis, each in one of 36 patients (2.8%). The median number of treatment cycles administered was 6 (IQR, 3–8). No patient was identified as having the UGT1A1∗28 homozygous genotype; therefore, no upfront genotype-based dose reduction was required, and all patients initiated liposomal irinotecan at 70 mg/m2. Dose reductions of liposomal irinotecan were required in seven patients (19.4%) during treatment, all of whom received a single-step dose reduction from 70 mg/m2 to 50 mg/m2. Reasons for dose reduction included diarrhea (n = 3), vomiting (n = 1), abdominal pain (n = 1), leukopenia (n = 1), and patient preference (n = 1). The first dose reduction occurred at a median treatment cycle of 3 (range, 2–8). Two patients (5.6%) discontinued treatment due to TRAEs, including one with grade 3 cholecystitis and one with grade 2 constipation.

Discussion

In this single-arm, phase II study, the combination of liposomal irinotecan and nimotuzumab showed preliminary antitumor activity in patients with ICI-refractory R/M NPC, a population with limited treatment options and poor expected outcomes. The study met the prespecified Simon two-stage activity criterion, although the relatively short DoR and PFS indicate that this activity did not translate into durable disease control in many patients.

The treatment landscape after ICI failure in R/M NPC remains poorly defined. Several recent studies have evaluated salvage approaches including immunotherapy rechallenge,32 antiangiogenic combinations,9,10 bispecific antibodies,33 and novel targeted agents.11 However, the reported activity has varied substantially across regimens and populations. Camrelizumab plus apatinib achieved an ORR of 34.3% in patients with PD-1-resistant disease9; camrelizumab plus famitinib achieved an ORR of 33.3% in patients previously treated with PD-1 inhibitors10; and camrelizumab plus dalpiciclib achieved an ORR of 32.4% in PD-1-refractory disease.11 By contrast, other investigational regimens showed more modest activity in post-PD-1 populations, including QL1706,33 LBL-007 plus toripalimab,34 SHR-1701,35 and TQB2618 plus penpulimab,36 with reported ORRs ranging from 0% to 11.8% and median PFS from 1.4 to 2.7 months. These studies differed substantially in prior treatment burden, patient selection, previous ICI exposure, and follow-up maturity, and therefore should not be directly compared. Taken together, these findings highlight the heterogeneity of post-ICI R/M NPC and the continued need for effective salvage strategies.

Although liposomal irinotecan plus nimotuzumab met the prespecified Simon two-stage activity criterion, the durability of clinical benefit in our study was limited, with a median DoR of 2.3 months and a median PFS of 3.0 months. This finding is particularly important when considered alongside camrelizumab plus famitinib, which reported a longer median PFS of 7.2 months and median DoR of 4.2 months in the post-ICI setting.10 Nevertheless, cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted cautiously. Our cohort was more heavily pretreated, with 12 of 36 patients (33.3%) having received three or more prior lines of therapy for advanced disease, compared with 5.6% (1/18) in the camrelizumab plus famitinib study.10 The later-line setting, biological heterogeneity after ICI failure, and the protocol-specified cap of eight cycles of liposomal irinotecan to limit cumulative toxicity may have contributed to the relatively short PFS and DoR observed in our study. Therefore, the present findings should be interpreted as preliminary evidence of antitumor activity rather than definitive evidence of durable clinical benefit.

The rationale for evaluating this regimen was based on prior evidence supporting the antitumor relevance of EGFR inhibition and irinotecan-based therapy in NPC; however, the contribution of each component in the present combination remains unconfirmed. EGFR inhibition may attenuate proliferative and anti-apoptotic signaling pathways implicated in resistance to both cytotoxic therapy and immunotherapy.37, 38, 39 This EGFR-directed approach is an area of active investigation, as highlighted by the emerging activity of agents such as MRG003,40 an anti-EGFR antibody–drug conjugate. In parallel, liposomal irinotecan may enhance intratumoral drug accumulation,41 and irinotecan itself has been reported to induce immunogenic cell death, which could theoretically preserve antitumor activity even after prior ICI exposure.42,43 These mechanistic considerations remain speculative in the absence of mechanistic and tissue-based translational correlates and warrant further preclinical and biomarker-based investigation.

The combination of liposomal irinotecan and nimotuzumab demonstrated a manageable safety profile.44, 45, 46 Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in eight of 36 patients (22.2%), with diarrhea, leukopenia, and neutropenia representing the most frequent higher-grade toxicities, findings that are consistent with the known safety profiles of the individual agents.9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 Although the incidence of severe toxicity in our study appeared lower than that reported in several other combination studies in R/M NPC, such comparisons are necessarily indirect and should be interpreted with caution. Liposomal irinotecan dose reduction was required in fewer than one-fifth of patients, and only two patients discontinued treatment due to TRAEs. No treatment-related deaths were observed. Taken together, these findings suggest that the regimen was feasible in this heavily pretreated population.

Post-hoc exploratory analyses suggested that an early decline in plasma Epstein–Barr virus DNA levels may be associated with improved objective response and PFS, consistent with prior reports supporting the utility of dynamic EBV DNA monitoring in R/M NPC.10 Given the limited sample size and exploratory nature of this analysis, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and require prospective validation.

This study has several limitations. First, as a Simon two-stage phase II trial, this study was designed to identify preliminary antitumor activity and determine whether further investigation was warranted rather than to provide definitive estimates of treatment efficacy. The small sample size resulted in inherent imprecision in the efficacy estimates, and the study was not powered to draw definitive conclusions from post-hoc subgroup and biomarker analyses. Second, because this was a single-arm study without a control group, the results cannot establish comparative efficacy and should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating. Third, although antitumor activity was observed, the relatively short DoR and PFS suggest that achieving durable disease control remains challenging in this population. In addition, the relatively small sample size and limited number of survival events restrict the precision of long-term survival estimates. Therefore, the reported survival outcomes should be interpreted cautiously. Fourth, because this was a single-center study conducted in China and race and ethnicity data were not collected, the representativeness of the study population across different racial, ethnic, and sociocultural backgrounds could not be assessed, as further detailed in Supplementary Table S3. Finally, because no mechanistic or tissue-based translational analyses were performed, the biological mechanisms underlying the observed activity and the patients most likely to benefit could not be further defined. Future studies should further evaluate this regimen in larger prospective and comparative studies and focus on identifying patients who may derive durable benefit. Additional translational investigations are warranted to clarify the biological mechanisms underlying treatment activity and validate potential biomarkers, including dynamic EBV DNA changes, which remain exploratory and require prospective validation. In conclusion, liposomal irinotecan combined with nimotuzumab demonstrated preliminary antitumor activity and manageable safety in patients with ICI-refractory R/M NPC. However, the short median DoR and PFS indicate limited durability of clinical benefit. These findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and warrant further comparative investigation to improve disease-control durability and identify patients most likely to derive sustained benefit.

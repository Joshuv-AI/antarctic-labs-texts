A class-wise quantum relational calibration network for brain tumor diagnosis
Authors: Joen-Rong Sheu, Jingnan Xie, Chung-Nan Tsai, Cheng-Ying Hsieh, Ching-Sheng Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate and reliable brain tumor diagnosis from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) remains a challenging task, particularly in multi-class settings where inter-class ambiguity and miscalibration may degrade predictive performance. In this work, we propose a Class-wise Quantum Relational Calibration Network (CQRCNet), a hybrid quantum-classical framework that integrates quantum relational modeling into a classical convolutional neural network backbone. Our approach employs a parameter-shared and low-qubit quantum circuit to explicitly model pairwise interactions between class-specific evidences, serving as a calibration mechanism for logit refinement. Unlike conventional post-hoc calibration methods, the proposed mechanism operates by refining inter-class relational representations prior to softmax normalization. Empirical studies are conducted on a publicly available brain tumor MRI dataset covering four tumor categories, and the proposed method demonstrates favorable performance compared with several reported classical deep learning baselines and representative hybrid quantum-classical models. Noise-aware quantum simulations under depolarizing noise and finite-shot sampling further indicate that the proposed quantum relational calibration maintains stable performance under simulated realistic quantum conditions, suggesting its potential applicability to near-term quantum computing scenarios. In addition, ablation studies, including comparisons with temperature scaling and analyses of different quantum circuit depths, provide further insights into the role of quantum relational modeling in the proposed framework.

Introduction

Accurate diagnosis and classification of brain tumors using magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are central challenges in medical imaging, as they are crucial for effective treatment planning and prognosis1. Brain tumors often exhibit complex appearance variations in size, shape, and texture, making reliable classification difficult even for experienced clinicians2. To address these challenges, numerous deep learning approaches have been proposed to assist clinicians by discovering discriminative patterns that may be imperceptible to the human eye. In particular, convolutional neural network (CNN)-based models have demonstrated remarkable performance in multi-class brain tumor classification tasks, with reported accuracy rates exceeding 90% on public MRI benchmarks3,4. These results highlight the effectiveness of deep learning models in extracting meaningful representations from high-dimensional medical image data.

Despite the impressive performance of deep learning models, they often rely on softmax classifiers or linear decision heads that implicitly enforce exclusivity among classes5. While this paradigm generally works well, it can be insufficient in ambiguous cases where classes share overlapping visual characteristics. Furthermore, existing research pipelines typically focus on improving feature learning or increasing network depth. However, they seldom explicitly address inter-class competition or predictive uncertainty, which may result in suboptimal decision boundaries under challenging scenarios.

Meanwhile, quantum machine learning (QML) has emerged as a promising interdisciplinary field that not only integrates quantum computing with classical learning paradigms, but also explores how quantum technologies can directly benefit from the ongoing AI revolution6. Hybrid quantum-classical models, integrating parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) with traditional neural networks, have demonstrated significant potential in supervised learning tasks, particularly for image recognition and classification7. These architectures leverage variational quantum circuits (VQCs) as trainable feature mappers or decision modules. They enable the encoding of high-dimensional correlations and the formation of highly non-linear decision boundaries that are often computationally expensive to achieve using purely classical methods. Since the underlying Hilbert space acts as a nonlinear feature map with exponential dimensionality, VQCs have the potential to represent complex and intractably large dependency structures that are difficult to model efficiently using classical architectures alone8.

However, much of the existing literature on hybrid quantum-classical learning has primarily explored quantum circuits as replacements for specific layers rather than explicitly grounding their integration in task-driven modeling motivations9,10. Many hybrid models tend to append a quantum classifier at the output stage or interleave quantum kernels without addressing underlying structural facilitation in the decision process. Furthermore, research on quantum neural networks for multi-class real-world datasets, particularly in medical imaging, remains limited compared to classical counterparts11. This gap highlights the need for task-driven quantum architectures that explicitly leverage quantum relational modeling to address inter-class ambiguity and evidence competition in complex multi-class medical image classification tasks. In particular, brain tumor classification often involves subtle inter-class similarities and overlapping visual patterns, where decision boundaries cannot be adequately characterized by independent class predictions alone. Under such conditions, modeling relational dependencies among class-wise evidences becomes increasingly important. Quantum-inspired relational representations provide a natural mechanism for capturing complex pairwise interactions and non-linear correlations within a shared latent space, making them particularly suitable for refining ambiguous multi-class decisions.

In this work, we propose a Class-wise Quantum Relational Calibration Network (CQRCNet) for brain tumor classification from MRI images. Instead of employing quantum circuits as off-the-shelf classifiers, our approach is motivated by a quantum relational modeling perspective that explicitly considers pairwise competitive interactions among class-wise evidences. Unlike conventional post-hoc calibration methods (e.g., temperature scaling), our calibration operates at the logit level by modeling class-wise relational interactions for refinement before softmax normalization. By modeling such inter-class relations, the proposed framework refines raw logits to better capture underlying class dependencies. The quantum relational module is shared across class pairs and optimized jointly with the classical backbone and evidence heads, providing an end-to-end trainable architecture that balances expressive power with parameter efficiency.

Related works

Recent advances in deep learning and quantum machine learning have significantly influenced brain tumor classification from MRI data. Prior studies span classical convolutional models, hybrid quantum-classical architectures, and emerging quantum machine learning approaches in medical imaging. These lines of research are reviewed in the following subsections.

Deep learning, particularly CNNs, has become the dominant paradigm for medical image analysis and disease diagnosis tasks. Since the early success of CNNs in visual recognition tasks12, deep architectures have been widely adopted for medical imaging applications, including magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), and histopathological image analysis13. In the context of brain tumor diagnosis, CNN-based models have demonstrated strong performance in tumor detection, segmentation, and multi-class classification tasks, benefiting from their ability to capture hierarchical spatial features and complex visual patterns14,15. Recent studies have employed various deep architectures such as VGG-style networks, residual networks, dense connections, and attention-based models to improve classification accuracy and robustness in brain tumor MRI datasets16,17. Despite their effectiveness, most existing approaches treat class predictions as largely independent, without explicitly modeling inter-class competition or relational dependencies among categories. This independence assumption may limit performance in scenarios where different tumor types exhibit strong visual similarity and overlapping characteristics.

In the current Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era, where hardware decoherence and statistical shot noise pose severe challenges to pure quantum data analysis, hybrid quantum-classical architectures have emerged as a highly practical paradigm for implementing quantum machine learning applications18. Quantum Convolutional Neural Networks (QCNNs) implement convolution and pooling operations entirely within quantum circuits to enable hierarchical feature extraction directly on quantum states through structured entanglement and multi-scale architectures inspired by multi-scale entanglement renormalization ansatz (MERA)19. While QCNNs exhibit strong theoretical representational capacity, fully quantum implementations remain constrained by scalability challenges. To address these constraints, hybrid Quantum-Classical Convolutional Neural Networks (QCCNNs) have emerged as a more robust alternative by replacing part of the quantum processing pipeline with classical convolutional layers while using local variational quantum circuits as quantum filters for feature extraction20. This design reduces quantum resource requirements and improves training stability compared to fully quantum QCNNs, but quantum components remain tightly coupled to early-stage feature extraction, limiting flexibility and often providing marginal advantages over strong classical CNN baselines. Most recently, the hybrid Quantum-Classical-Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (QCQ-CNNs) architecture is proposed to place quantum modules at both the input encoding stage and the output decision stage, with a classical CNN backbone in between21. This structure aims to enhance representational expressiveness by combining quantum feature encoding and quantum decision modeling, but it increases architectural complexity and quantum circuit usage, raising concerns regarding scalability and interpretability.

Quantum machine learning has also begun to attract attention in the medical imaging domain to address modern computational limitations. Specifically, quantum-inspired and hybrid quantum-classical models have been actively explored to process large visual datasets for tasks such as disease classification, medical pattern recognition, and biomedical signal analysis22. A Hybrid Quantum-classical Neural Network (H-QNN) is introduced for brain tumor classification, where classical CNNs are combined with parameterized quantum circuits (e.g., ZZFeatureMap and RealAmplitudes ansatz) to enhance feature representation and decision performance on multi-class MRI datasets4. A Hybrid Classical-Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (HCQCNN) architecture that integrates quantum feature encoding with classical deep learning has been proposed for brain MRI classification and achieves strong performance on Alzheimer’s disease and brain tumor datasets23. Similarly, a hybrid CNN-VQC architecture (H-CNN-VQC) combines CNN-based feature extraction (VGG16) with a parameterized variational quantum circuit to perform brain tumor MRI classification through quantum feature encoding and variational quantum learning24.

To evaluate the performance of the proposed model, three standard metrics are adopted: Precision, Recall, and F1-score. Precision measures the proportion of correctly predicted labels among all labels assigned by the model, reflecting the accuracy of its positive predictions. Recall quantifies the proportion of correctly predicted labels among all ground-truth labels, indicating the model’s ability to identify relevant instances. The F1-score, defined as the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall, provides a balanced evaluation by jointly considering both predictive accuracy and retrieval capability.

Experimental results and analysis

Our experiments compare the proposed method with two categories of learning models to comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed CQRCNet framework. The first category includes conventional CNN-based architectures, namely ResNet-5026, DenseNet27, VGG1628 and InceptionV329, which have been widely adopted as baseline models in medical image classification tasks. The second category consists of quantum-based approaches, including H-CNN-VQC24, Hybrid QML-CNN30 and H-QNN4, which represent recent developments in hybrid quantum-classical learning for image classification. For fair comparison, all methods are evaluated on the same brain tumor MRI dataset using identical evaluation metrics, including Precision, Recall, and F1-score. The baseline results are directly obtained from the corresponding original studies to maintain consistency with their reported experimental configurations. Specifically, the results of the conventional CNN-based models and H-QNN are adopted from Ranga et al.4, while the performances of H-CNN-VQC and Hybrid QML-CNN are taken from their respective publications. The proposed CQRCNet is implemented on a Windows 10 desktop workstation, featuring an Intel Core i9 CPU, 128 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB VRAM). Table 3 presents the comparative performance of the proposed model against baseline models. Among the conventional CNN-based baselines, ResNet-50 and DenseNet achieve the strongest performance, both obtaining an F1-score of 93.0%. However, the two models exhibit different prediction characteristics. ResNet-50 achieves higher Precision (92.5%), indicating better control of false positives, whereas DenseNet achieves higher Recall (94.2%), suggesting improved sensitivity in detecting tumor samples. This observation indicates that although both architectures obtain similar overall performance, their feature extraction behaviors differ in terms of classification balance. VGG16 achieves a moderate F1-score of 89.3%, which may be attributed to its comparatively shallow architecture and limited feature reuse capability. InceptionV3 demonstrates substantially lower performance with an F1-score of 71.5%, implying that its multi-scale convolution design may be less compatible with the texture and structural characteristics of the brain tumor MRI dataset.

Compared with conventional CNN-based architectures, the quantum and hybrid quantum-classical models generally achieve superior classification performance. The H-CNN-VQC model obtains an F1-score of 89.7%, showing that integrating variational quantum circuits into CNN architectures can provide competitive learning capability. However, its performance remains below that of ResNet-50 and DenseNet, suggesting that simple quantum integration alone may not sufficiently capture complex inter-class relationships within MRI features. The Hybrid QML-CNN model improves the F1-score to 94.7%, demonstrating that more effective hybrid quantum-classical feature interaction can significantly enhance classification performance. Furthermore, the H-QNN model achieves an F1-score of 95.5%, indicating the effectiveness of hybrid quantum representations for medical image analysis tasks.

Our proposed CQRCNet favorably compares under the reported results across all three evaluation metrics, recording a Precision, Recall, and F1-score of 96.2%. This represents a 0.7-percentage-point improvement over H-QNN and a 3.2-percentage-point improvement over the strongest conventional CNN-based baselines. Although the numerical improvement over H-QNN may appear relatively modest, such gains are meaningful in high-accuracy medical image classification tasks where performance saturation commonly occurs. Although the reported Precision and Recall are numerically identical, this does not by itself imply balanced per-class performance.

The favorable performance may be associated with the integration of classical convolutional feature extraction with the proposed quantum relational calibration module, which enhances the model’s ability to capture complex relationships among class evidences. Compared with deeper or more complex quantum architectures, the lightweight parameter-shared design helps alleviate optimization instability and reduces the risk of overfitting on limited medical imaging data. In addition, the relational calibration mechanism enables more effective interaction among class-specific evidences, thereby improving feature discrimination and classification robustness. It should be noted that baseline results are adopted from the corresponding original publications; therefore, differences in preprocessing strategies, data splits, and training settings may affect direct comparability.

Ablation studies

To investigate the impact of different design choices in the proposed framework, we conduct a series of ablation studies focusing on a classical relation module, noise-aware quantum simulations, temperature scaling31, and quantum circuit depth. The model is evaluated on the testing set after each training epoch, and the F1-score is recorded to examine performance variations throughout the training process. To isolate the quantum contribution, we replace the two-qubit relation module with a parameter-matched classical counterpart consisting of a linear layer followed by a tanh activation. The classical module has the same number of trainable parameters, while all other network components and training settings are kept identical. As shown in Fig. 4, the quantum variant stays above the classical variant for most of training and reaches a higher final F1-score (0.962 vs. 0.898). This suggests that a same-sized classical module does not straightforwardly reproduce the observed gain, providing further support for the effectiveness of the proposed quantum relational calibration.

Figure 5 illustrates the results of noise-aware quantum simulations for the proposed model under different quantum conditions. The ideal curve represents the performance of the model using noiseless statevector simulations with effectively infinite measurement shots, serving as an upper bound of achievable F1-score. In addition, two realistic scenarios are evaluated using finite measurement shots (128 and 1024) together with a noise setting (γ = 0.01, δ = 0.02) to emulate near-term quantum hardware. The adopted noise levels are based on representative gate error rates reported for superconducting NISQ devices21. Compared with the ideal setting, the finite-shot experiments exhibit slightly lower and fluctuating F1-scores, which reflect the influence of measurement variance and gate noise on inference. Notably, increasing the number of shots from 128 to 1024 reduces the fluctuations and brings the F1-scores closer to the ideal curve. This result indicates enhanced stability and reduced sampling uncertainty. Nevertheless, both noisy settings closely follow the trend of the ideal case, which demonstrates the robustness of the proposed method under realistic quantum measurement conditions.

In contrast, CQRCNet improves calibration without relying on an explicit post-hoc calibration step, suggesting that the refinement of inter-class relational representations can yield better-calibrated predictions while simultaneously improving classification performance.

Figure 7 presents the effect of quantum circuit depth on model performance across 20 training epochs. Three configurations with depths of 3, 5, and 7 are compared using the F1-score as the evaluation metric. All configurations show a generally increasing trend during training, which indicates stable learning behavior. Among them, the depth-3 configuration achieves the highest final F1-score of 0.962, while the depth-5 model reaches 0.947 and the depth-7 model attains 0.929. Increasing ansatz depth may not always yield consistent performance gains. This behavior admits two possible interpretations. The shallow circuit may already capture the relevant relational structure, so that additional depth brings no benefit. Alternatively, the quantum module may act as an implicit regularizer whose effect weakens as depth increases. Our current experiments cannot fully distinguish between these two accounts, and we leave a more detailed investigation to future work.

Conclusion and future work

In this paper, we proposed CQRCNet, a hybrid quantum-classical framework that integrates a classical CNN backbone for feature extraction, class-specific evidence heads for preliminary prediction, and a quantum relational calibration module for modeling inter-class relationships. By modeling pairwise interactions among class evidences using variational quantum circuits, the method performs relational calibration of decision logits and improves classification reliability. Experiments on MRI image classification demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed architecture. Ablation studies on the classical relation module, noise-aware quantum simulations, temperature scaling, and quantum circuit depth demonstrate the stability of the proposed model under simulated quantum conditions. While the current results are based on simulation, they indicate the potential of quantum relational modeling for hybrid learning systems and encourage future investigations on scalable and hardware-aware quantum implementations.

Future work will focus on several directions. First, we will investigate scalable relational calibration strategies to support larger class spaces and more complex medical imaging tasks. Second, we aim to explore more expressive quantum circuit architectures and advanced training strategies, including adaptive circuit depth and more efficient quantum parameter optimization. Third, future studies will examine the integration of CQRCNet with other deep learning backbones and multimodal medical data, in order to further enhance the generalization capability of the proposed framework.

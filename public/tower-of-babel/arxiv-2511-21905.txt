EPW-VASP interface for first-principles calculations of electron-phonon interactions
Danylo Radevych †, Aidan Thorn, Manuel Engel, Aleksey N. Kolmogorov, Sabyasachi Tiwari, Georg Kresse, Feliciano Giustino, Elena R. Margine ‡

Abstract
We present an interface between the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) and the EPW software for calculating materials properties governed by electron-phonon (e-ph) interactions. Computation of the e-ph matrix elements with the finite-difference supercell approach in VASP and their fine-grid interpolation in EPW enable accurate modeling of temperature-dependent materials properties and phonon-assisted quantum processes with VASP’s extensive library of exchange-correlation functionals and pseudopotentials. We demonstrate the functionality of the EPW-VASP interface by examining the superconducting gap and critical temperature in MgB 2 using the anisotropic Migdal-Eliashberg equations, and the carrier mobility in cubic BN using the ab initio Boltzmann transport equation.

II
Methodology

Evaluating materials properties related to the electron–phonon interaction typically requires Brillouin zone (BZ) integrations over electron and phonon grids with very fine sampling. However, this becomes impractical for direct DFPT calculations for a large number of phonon wave vectors or the finite-difference approach for very large supercells. The challenge has been overcome by computing the electron-phonon matrix elements in real space
20
;
21
using the interpolation method based on maximally-localized Wannier functions (MLWFs) introduced by Giustino
et al.
in the EPW code within the DFPT framework
28
. The EPW-VASP interface builds upon (i) the recent advancements in the VASP code for calculating EPIs via the PAW method and finite-displacement approach in real space
20
;
21
, and (ii) the expanded functionalities of the EPW code for computing materials properties related to electron–phonon interactions
29
;
50
.

In this section, we first outline the procedure to obtain the electronic Hamiltonian and the electron-phonon matrix elements in the Wannier representation along with the interatomic force constants using the finite-difference supercell scheme in VASP. Next, we describe how these real-space quantities are employed to obtain their counterparts on dense electron and phonon momenta in Bloch space via Wannier-Fourier interpolation, using EPW. As the theoretical background and methodology have been thoroughly detailed in Refs.

II.1
Wannier interpolation in VASP

Electronic structure and lattice dynamics in crystalline solids are modeled as infinitely extended systems using a Born–von Kármán (BvK) supercell. In this approach, periodic boundary conditions are applied to a supercell containing
N_{p}=N_{1}\times N_{2}\times N_{3}
primitive unit cells, arranged according to the underlying Bravais lattice. The
p
th unit cell (
p=1,\dots,N_{p}
) is identified by a direct lattice vector
{{\bf R}_{p}}=\sum_{i}p_{i}{\bf a}_{i}
, with
{\bf a}_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) the primitive lattice vectors and
p_{i}\in[0,N_{i})
. Exploiting this periodicity, Bloch’s theorem enables the description of electronic states and lattice vibrations in terms of wavevectors defined over the crystal’s Brillouin zone. The reciprocal lattice associated with the BvK supercell defines a uniform mesh of
N_{p}

{\bf k}
- and
{\bf q}
-points, corresponding to the electron and phonon wavevectors within the BZ. Note that the number of unit cells
N_{p}
can be different for electrons and phonons.

Using Bloch’s theorem, the eigenfunctions
\psi_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})
, with band index
n
and crystal momentum
{\bf k}
, of the Kohn-Sham (KS) Hamiltonian can be expressed as

\displaystyle\psi_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{p}}}u_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})\,e^{i{{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}},

(1)

where
u_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})
is the Bloch-periodic component of the KS wavefunction. The wave function
\psi_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})
is taken to be normalized to one in the BvK supercell,
while the periodic part
u_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})
is normalized to one in the primitive unit cell
29
;
50
.
Given an isolated Bloch band
m
, the Wannier function
w_{mp}({\bf r})
associated with the unit cell identified by
{{\bf R}_{p}}
is defined in terms of Bloch states
\psi_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})
as
63
;
74
;
62
:

w_{mp}({\bf r})=w_{m0}({\bf r}-{{\bf R}_{p}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{p}}}\sum_{n{\bf k}}e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}U_{nm{\bf k}}\psi_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r}).

(2)

The inverse relation of Eq. (
2
) is obtained by a standard inverse Fourier transform:

\psi_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{p}}}\sum_{mp}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}U^{\dagger}_{mn{\bf k}}w_{mp}({\bf r}).

(3)

Here
U_{nm{\bf k}}
is a unitary matrix determined by minimizing the spatial spread of the functions
w_{mp}({\bf r})
, which transforms the Bloch wavefunctions to a Wannier gauge

\psi^{\rm W}_{m{\bf k}}({\bf r})=\sum_{n}U_{nm{\bf k}}\psi_{n{\bf k}}({\bf r}).

(4)

The degree of localization directly impacts the accuracy and efficiency of the interpolation procedure as more localized WFs decay faster in real space, allowing smaller BvK supercells to include all nonvanishing matrix elements of the Hamiltonian and other operators.

The Hamiltonian matrix elements in the Wannier basis are given by
74

\quad H_{mn}({{\bf R}_{p}})=\braket{w_{m0}|\hat{H}|w_{np}}=\int_{\text{sc}}d{\bf r}w^{*}_{m0}({\bf r})\hat{H}w_{n0}({\bf r}-{{\bf R}_{p}}),

(5)

where the subscript “sc” indicates that the integral is over the BvK supercell. Using Eq. (
2
), these elements can be represented in terms of the DFT KS Hamiltonian in the Bloch representation as

H_{mn}({{\bf R}_{p}})=\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}{\bf k}}e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}U^{\dagger}_{mm^{\prime}{\bf k}}H_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}({\bf k})U_{n^{\prime}n{\bf k}},

(6)

where
H_{mn}({\bf k})=\delta_{mn}\epsilon_{n{\bf k}}
. The eigenstates
\ket{\psi_{n{{\bf k}}}}
and eigenvalues
\epsilon_{n{\bf k}}
of the KS Hamiltonian are used to determine the unitary matrix for the transformation to MLWFs.

The electron-phonon matrix elements are the central quantity in the evaluation of properties governed by EPIs, describing the scattering of an electron from a Bloch eigenstate
\ket{\psi_{n{{\bf k}}}}
to
\ket{\psi_{m{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}}}
, where
{\bf k}
is the electron momentum,
{\bf q}
is the phonon momentum transfer, and
n
,
m
are band indices. Within DFT, these electron-phonon matrix elements can be computed as
28
;
29

g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})=\bra{\psi_{m{\bf k}+{\bf q}}}\Delta_{{\bf q}\nu}\hat{H}\ket{\psi_{n{\bf k}}}_{\text{sc}}.

(7)

The term
\Delta_{{\bf q}\nu}\hat{H}
is the first-order change of the KS Hamiltonian with respect to a collective ionic displacement corresponding to a phonon
with momentum
{\bf q}
and branch index
\nu
, and can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\bf q}\nu}\hat{H}=\sum_{\kappa\alpha p}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M_{\kappa}\omega_{{\bf q}\nu}}}e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}({\bf q})\frac{\partial\hat{H}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}.

(8)

Here,
\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}
is the position of atom
\kappa
in unit cell
p
along the Cartesian direction
\alpha
(
{\bm{\tau}}_{\kappa p}={\bm{\tau}}_{\kappa}+{{\bf R}_{p}}
), and
M_{\kappa}
is the mass of atom
\kappa
. The derivative
\frac{\partial\hat{H}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}
corresponds to individual atomic displacements and can be evaluated using the finite-displacement approach with supercells
23
;
42
;
72
;
102
. Finally,
\omega_{{\bf q}\nu}
is the frequency and
e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}({\bf q})
is the normal mode of vibration or polarization vector of the phonon mode characterized by
{\bf q}
and
\nu
. These quantities are eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the dynamical matrix
D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({\bf q})
, which is obtained from the Fourier transform of the matrix of interatomic force constants (IFCs)
C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}p^{\prime}}
:

C_{\kappa\alpha p,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}p^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial{\tau_{\kappa\alpha p}}\partial{\tau_{\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}p^{\prime}}}}.

(9)

D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({\bf q})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M_{\kappa}M_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}\sum_{p}C_{\kappa\alpha 0,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}p}e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}},

(10)

C_{\kappa\alpha 0,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}p}\equiv D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({{\bf R}_{p}})=\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial{\tau_{\kappa\alpha 0}}\partial{\tau_{\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}p}}}.

(11)

The matrix of IFCs is defined as the second derivative of the total potential energy,
V
, with respect to atomic displacements evaluated at the equilibrium configuration
29
. In VASP, the interatomic force constants are computed in the supercell using a finite-difference scheme, and one makes the approximation that all force constants outside of the Wigner-Seitz cell associated with the BvK cell are zero.

To express the electron-phonon matrix element in the Bloch representation in terms of the corresponding element in the Wannier representation, we insert Eqs. (
3
) and (
8
) inside Eq. (
7
). After rearranging the terms, we find

\displaystyle g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{pp^{\prime}}e^{i({\bf k}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}+{\bf q}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}})}\sum_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\sum_{\kappa\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M_{\kappa}\omega_{{\bf q}\nu}}}

(12)

\displaystyle\times U_{mm^{\prime}{\bf k}+{\bf q}}g_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}\kappa\alpha}({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}})U^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}n{\bf k}}e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}({\bf q}),

having introduced the electron-phonon matrix element in the Wannier representation

g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}})=\braket{w_{m0}|\frac{\partial\hat{H}}{\partial\tau_{\kappa\alpha p^{\prime}}}|w_{np}}.

(13)

In VASP, Eq. (
13
) is computed similarly to the interatomic force constants, using finite atomic displacements in a large supercell. After each displacement step, a self-consistent electronic minimization is performed and the action of the resulting perturbed Hamiltonian on the Wannier orbitals is computed.
This procedure is implemented entirely in the PAW method. It is crucial to note that there are two non-equivalent definitions of the electron-phonon matrix element in the PAW method.
In this paper, we only consider the so-called “all-electron” formulation
12
, which is formally equivalent to Eq. (
13
) in the limit of a complete PAW basis.
However, matrix elements in the other, so-called “pseudo” formulation
20
can also be passed to the EPW-VASP interface. In general, using these non-Hermitian pseudo matrix elements is not advised, but there are use cases where they offer computational advantages compared to the all-electron formulation. More details on the PAW formalism are provided in Appendix
B
. A detailed comparison between the all-electron approach and pseudo approach is given in Ref.
21
.

II.2
Wannier to Bloch transform in EPW

The real-space Hamiltonian, IFCs, and electron-phonon matrix elements — computed via Eqs. (
6
), (
11
), and (
13
) — are the central quantities calculated in VASP and passed to EPW. Here, we outline the procedure carried out in EPW to transform these quantities onto fine electron and phonon momentum grids in the Bloch representation, following the general workflow shown in Fig.
1
.

To start, we first do an inverse Fourier transform of the Hamiltonian
H_{mn}({{\bf R}_{p}})
from the Wannier to the Bloch representation, and then diagonalize the resulting matrix through a unitary rotation matrix
\mathcal{U}_{nm{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}

H_{mn}({{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\sum_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}_{mm^{\prime}{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}\biggl[\sum_{p}e^{i{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}H_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}({{\bf R}_{p}})\biggr]\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}n{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}.

(14)

The summation runs over the lattice vectors
{{\bf R}_{p}}
belonging to the Wigner-Seitz supercell centered at the origin, as discussed in Appendix
A
. The term in the square brackets

H^{\text{W}}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}({{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\sum_{p}e^{i{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}H_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}({{\bf R}_{p}})

(15)

represents the Hamiltonian in the Bloch basis in the Wannier gauge, and is the only known quantity at this stage. Note that the Hamiltonian
H^{\text{W}}_{mn}({{\bf k}^{\prime}})
in the Wannier gauge is not diagonal, as opposed to the Hamiltonian
H_{mn}({{\bf k}^{\prime}})
in the Bloch gauge which is diagonal by construction,
H_{mn}({{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\delta_{mn}\epsilon_{n{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}
. Diagonalizing the aforementioned matrix
H^{\text{W}}_{mn}({{\bf k}^{\prime}})
yields the eigenvalues
\epsilon_{n{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}
and the unitary matrix
\mathcal{U}_{nm{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}
at any arbitrary wave vector
{{\bf k}^{\prime}}
in the BZ. The columns of
\mathcal{U}_{nm{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}
are the eigenvectors of the new Bloch states.

For phonons, the calculation proceeds along the path of solid arrows shown in Fig.
1
. Using the IFCs
D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({{\bf R}_{p}})\equiv C_{\kappa\alpha 0,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}p}
generated from the supercell approach with VASP according to Eq. (
11
), the eigenfrequencies and eigenmodes at arbitrary momenta
{{\bf q}^{\prime}}
are found using the same two steps as for the Hamiltonian. First, we inverse Fourier transform the real-space dynamical matrix to Bloch space and, then, diagonalize the resulting matrix

\displaystyle D_{\mu\nu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M_{\kappa}M_{\kappa^{\prime}}}}\sum_{\kappa\alpha\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}e^{\dagger}_{\kappa\alpha,\mu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum_{p}e^{i{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({{\bf R}_{p}})\right]e_{\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime},\nu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}}).

(16)

As for the electrons, the known quantity in Eq. (
II.2
) is the term within the square brackets

\displaystyle D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})=\sum_{p}e^{i{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}}D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({{\bf R}_{p}}),

(17)

and the
D_{\mu\nu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})
matrix on the left-hand side of Eq. (
II.2
) is diagonal by construction,
D_{\mu\nu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})=\omega^{2}_{{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\nu}\delta_{\mu\nu}
. Diagonalizing
D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})
yields the eigenmodes
e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})
and squared eigenfrequencies
\omega^{2}_{{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\nu}
.

Finally, the electron-phonon matrix elements for an arbitrary pair of (
{{\bf k}^{\prime}}
,
{{\bf q}^{\prime}}
) points on fine grids can be obtained using the unitary matrix
\mathcal{U}_{nm{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}
that interpolates the Bloch Hamiltonian in Eq. (
14
)

\displaystyle g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}_{mm^{\prime}{{\bf k}^{\prime}}+{{\bf q}^{\prime}}}g^{\text{W}}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}\kappa\alpha}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}n{{\bf k}^{\prime}}},

(18)

where

g^{\text{W}}_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})=\sum_{pp^{\prime}}e^{i\left({{\bf k}^{\prime}}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p}}+{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\cdot{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}}\right)}g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}}).

(19)

is the electron-phonon matrix element computed in the Wannier gauge. The conversion from the atomic indices
(\kappa,\alpha)
to the phonon mode index
\nu
is trivial, as it involves summations over
(\kappa,\alpha)
using the phonon eigenvectors
e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}})
and eigenvalues
\omega_{{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\nu}
of the dynamical matrix in Eq. (
II.2
):

\displaystyle g_{mn\nu}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M_{\kappa}\omega_{{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\nu}}}g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})e_{\kappa\alpha,\nu}({{\bf q}^{\prime}}).

(20)

The Wannier-gauge matrix elements,
g^{\text{W}}_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})
and
g^{\text{W}}_{mn\nu}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})
, transform in exactly the same way.

At this stage, it is assumed that the electron-phonon interactions are short-ranged in real space, meaning that
g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}})
decays rapidly with both
{{\bf R}_{p}}
and
{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}}
. Under this assumption, it is sufficient to compute the matrix elements on only a small set of (
{{\bf R}_{p}}
,
{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}}
) lattice vectors to fully capture the coupling between electrons and phonons. For materials with nonvanishing dynamical dipoles, however, this interpolation procedure must be modified to account for long-range interactions, as discussed in Sec.
II.3
.

Following the solid-arrow path in Fig.
1
, the electron-phonon matrix elements
g({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}})
, generated in VASP, are Fourier transformed to obtain the electron-phonon matrix elements
g^{\text{W}}_{mn\kappa\alpha}({\bf k},{\bf q})
on a commensurate
({\bf k},{\bf q})
grid in the Wannier gauge, according to Eq. (
13
). An inverse Fourier transform is then applied to produce
g({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}})
on a differently ordered
({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}})
grid [as opposed to
({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}})
], required by the difference in conventions between VASP and EPW discussed in Section
C
. Subsequently, the matrix elements
g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf R}_{p}},{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}})
are Fourier interpolated to a fine
({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})
grid in the Wannier gauge, yielding
g^{\text{W}}_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})
via Eq. (
19
). Finally, a rotation to the Bloch gauge is carried out using Eq. (
18
), resulting in
g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({{\bf k}^{\prime}},{{\bf q}^{\prime}})
.

\sim 1/r^{2}
) in polar materials

{\bf q}\rightarrow 0

;
105
;

;
8
;

;

;

;

;

.
Mathematically, the presence of these electron-phonon
interactions is typically indicated by atoms having a nonzero Born effective charge tensor
{\bf Z}_{\kappa}

7
. The dipole field diverges as
1/q
in the limit of
{\bf q}\rightarrow 0
, leading to the well-known longitudinal optical–transverse optical (LO-TO) mode splitting. Dipolar contributions violate the locality assumption required for Wannier interpolation, resulting in divergent or discontinuous electron-phonon matrix elements near
{\bf q}=0
, which cannot be interpolated directly. These long-range effects, however, can be treated analytically in both bulk and 2D materials

;

;
8
;

;

;

;

;

;

;

.

In this section, we outline the procedure used within the EPW-VASP interface to leverage the existing EPW capabilities for treating long-range polar contributions for both the dynamical matrix and the electron-phonon matrix elements for 3D materials
50
.
Since VASP provides IFCs and real-space electron-phonon matrix elements computed via Eqs. (
11
) and (
13
), the first step in EPW is to convert these quantities from the real-space supercell (
{{\bf R}_{p}}
,
{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}}
) to the corresponding coarse BZ grid (
{\bf k}
,
{\bf q}
).
Then, the strategy is to separate the dynamical matrix and electron-phonon matrix elements into short-range (
\mathcal{S}
) and long-range (
\mathcal{L}
) parts,

D_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({\bf q})=D^{\mathcal{S}}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({\bf q})+D^{\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}({\bf q}),

(21)

g_{mn\kappa\alpha}({\bf k},{\bf q})=g^{\mathcal{S}}_{mn\kappa\alpha}({\bf k},{\bf q})+g^{\mathcal{L}}_{mn\kappa\alpha}({\bf k},{\bf q}),

III
Computational workflow

This section outlines the overall workflow for using the EPW-VASP interface, from initial setup to final output. The workflow is illustrated in Fig.
2
. Communication between EPW and VASP is handled through the exchange of real-space quantities stored in Hierarchical Data Format version 5 (HDF5)
100
. The
elphon_wannier.h5
file generated by VASP contains the following key information that characterizes the system of interest:

•

Crystal structure data, including lattice vectors and atomic positions for both the primitive cell and the supercell.

•

Wannier function centers within the primitive unit cell.

•

Real-space Hamiltonian in the Wannier representation.

•

Interatomic force constants (IFCs).

•

Real-space electron–phonon matrix elements in the Wannier representation.

•

Dielectric tensor and Born effective charges, if long-range dipole corrections are enabled in the VASP calculation.

The first step of the workflow is to compute the Hamiltonian, IFCs, and electron-phonon matrix elements in the Wannier representation by performing DFT calculations with VASP. To construct a set of Wannier functions, a DFT calculation must first be carried out for the primitive cell using a regular
\Gamma
-centered
{\bf k}
-point grid, with appropriate flags set in the VASP input files to call the Wannier90 library. This step produces a
CONTCAR_SUPER
file containing the supercell expansion of the primitive cell, and the
WAVECAR.wan
file storing the Wannier orbitals in the supercell. The
CONTCAR_SUPER
file must be renamed to
POSCAR
for use in the subsequent phonon and electron-phonon calculations. The same
WAVECAR.wan
must also be used in the electron-phonon calculation to ensure consistency with the wannierization step.

In the current version of VASP, electron-phonon calculations are restricted to using only the
\Gamma
-point for the electronic states. However, when finer Brillouin zone sampling is needed to accurately capture phonon dispersions, a preliminary standalone supercell-based phonon calculation can be performed using finite displacements and a
{\bf k}
-point grid. The resulting
phonon_data.h5
file, which contains the IFCs, can then be imported into the subsequent electron-phonon calculation. This approach enables VASP to reuse the high-quality IFCs while keeping the actual electron-phonon calculations limited to the
\Gamma
-point. The main output from this procedure is the
elphon_wannier.h5
file, which contains all quantities required for subsequent EPW calculations.

To activate the interface, the
elphon_wannier.h5
file generated by VASP is placed in the EPW working directory and the flag
lvasp = .true.
is set in the EPW input file. All other input parameters relevant to superconductivity and transport calculations can be configured as in standard EPW workflows with Quantum ESPRESSO.

V
Conclusion

In this article, we introduce the EPW-VASP interface for first-principles calculations of advanced materials properties defined by electron-phonon interactions. The interface enables the use of real-space Hamiltonians, interatomic force constants, and electron-phonon matrix elements computed within the supercell approach in VASP, employing the projector augmented-wave method and Wannier functions. The EPW code performs the necessary Fourier interpolation to fine grids and evaluates relevant materials properties. We demonstrate the capabilities of the EPW-VASP interface through two representative examples: the calculation of anisotropic superconducting properties using Migdal-Eliashberg theory and charge transport properties in polar materials using the Boltzmann transport equation.

Several features are currently under testing or planned for future development, including support for two-dimensional systems, introduction of long-range quadrupole corrections to the dynamical and electron-phonon matrices in the PAW formalism, as well as calculations of phonon-assisted optical processes, and polaronic effects. Additionally, extending the interface to support electron-phonon calculations with hybrid functionals, made possible by the universality of the finite-difference approach used in VASP, would be highly desirable. Finally, the EPW-VASP interface can be used in conjunction with the recently developed EPWpy program, which provides a high-level Python interface to streamline automated workflows for high-throughput calculations of materials properties.

During the preparation of this manuscript, related work by Poliukhin
et al.

75
introduced a finite-displacement supercell approach that constructs electron–phonon matrix elements via a projectability scheme using the eigenvalues and wavefunction overlaps of pristine and displaced structures. This general method is implemented in the ElePhAny code, which interfaces Quantum ESPRESSO
26
, Koopmans
54
, and Yambo
61
(for electronic structure), and Phonopy
102
(for phonon dispersion) to generate EPW-ready inputs for Wannier interpolation and subsequent calculations of electron-phonon related properties.

Appendix A
Wigner-Seitz construction

The interpolation of the Hamiltonian, dynamical matrix, and electron-phonon matrix elements from real space to reciprocal space relies on the spatial decay (or localization) of these quantities in real space. Consequently, the accuracy and efficiency of the interpolation depend critically on the choice of real-space lattice vectors to be summed over in Eqs. (
15
), (
17
) and (
18
). In this section, we recap the procedure for optimal Wigner-Seitz (WS) construction introduced in Refs.
74
;
78
.

For the Hamiltonian, the decay is governed by the spatial extent of the Wannier functions, and the most relevant lattice vectors are those that minimize the distance between the corresponding Wannier centers. A similar principle holds for the IFCs and electron-phonon matrix elements. In the case of IFCs, the key distances are between pairs of atomic positions, while for electron-phonon interactions, the relevant separation is between an atomic site and a Wannier center.

Given a primitive unit cell defined by
\{{\bf a}_{i}\},i=1,2,3
, and a supercell expanded by
\{N_{i}\}
, an effective method of selecting the optimal set of lattice vectors involves minimizing the distance

|{\bf R}_{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}}+{\bf T}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}+{\bf r}_{n}-{\bf r}_{m}|.

(31)

Here,
{\bf r}_{n}
and
{\bf r}_{m}
denote either atomic or Wannier center positions inside the primitive unit cell,
{\bf R}_{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}}=\sum_{i}p_{i}{\bf a}_{i}
specifies a primitive unit cell within the BvK supercell with
p_{i}\in[0,N_{i})
, and
{\bf T}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}=\sum_{i}j_{i}N_{i}{\bf a}_{i}
is a supercell lattice vector.

For each combination
(m,n,\{p_{i}\})
, the process cycles over a sufficiently large grid of
{\bf T}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}
, e.g.,
j_{i}\in[-3,3]
, to determine when the vector
{\bf R}_{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}}+{\bf T}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}+{\bf r}_{n}
falls within the WS cell centered at
{\bf r}_{m}
(see Fig.
5
). In case this vector is at the boundary of the WS cell, all equivalent
\mathcal{N}_{mn\{p_{i}\}}
replicas are kept with the weight factor
1/\mathcal{N}_{mn\{p_{i}\}}
. This amounts to replacing the summation over the
{{\bf R}_{p}}
lattice vectors in Eq. (
15
) with

\displaystyle H^{\text{W}}_{mn}({\bf k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{p_{i}\}}^{\rm WS}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{mn\{p_{i}\}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{j_{i}\}\in\\
\mathcal{F}(mn\{p_{i}\})\end{subarray}}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot\left({\bf R}_{\{p_{i}\}}+{\bf T}_{\{j_{i}\}}\right)}

(32)

\displaystyle\times H_{mn}({\bf R}_{\{p_{i}\}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle:mn\{p_{i}\}\rightarrow\left\{\{j_{i}\}^{(1)},...,\{j_{i}\}^{(\mathcal{N}_{mn\{p_{i}\}})}\right\},

where mapping
\mathcal{F}
returns all sets of translational indices
\{j_{i}\}
corresponding to given
(m,n,\{p_{i}\})
. A similar procedure is used in Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
).

Appendix B
Electron-phonon matrix elements in the PAW method

In VASP, the computation of electron-phonon matrix elements is performed in the PAW framework.
The PAW method addresses the challenge of representing all-electron (AE) orbitals in plane-wave codes by introducing a transformation that maps smooth pseudo (PS) orbitals, suitable for expansion in plane waves, onto AE orbitals
5
;
47
.
This transformation enables efficient computation without sacrificing the ability to recover AE observables.

Unlike traditional pseudopotential approaches, which discard AE information within a core radius, the PAW method employs a dual basis representation. Each PS orbital,
\ket{\tilde{\psi}_{n{\bf k}}}
, is augmented inside atom-centered spheres (augmentation spheres) with AE partial waves
\ket{\phi_{ai}}
, PS partial waves
\ket{\tilde{\phi}_{ai}}
, and projector functions
\ket{\tilde{p}_{ai}}
, thereby allowing reconstruction of the AE orbital,
\ket{\psi_{n{\bf k}}}
:

\ket{\psi_{n{\bf k}}}=\ket{\tilde{\psi}_{n{\bf k}}}+\sum_{ai}\left(\ket{\phi_{ai}}-\ket{\tilde{\phi}_{ai}}\right)\braket{\tilde{p}_{ai}|\tilde{\psi}_{n{\bf k}}},

(33)

where
i
denotes the PAW channel, that is, a composite index that describes the local basis functions inside the augmentation sphere around the atom
a
.
Projector functions and PS orbitals are described in the plane-wave basis while AE and PS partial waves are described on logarithmic radial grids inside the augmentation spheres.

The mapping from PS to AE orbitals is known as the PAW transformation:

\hat{\mathcal{T}}=1+\sum_{ai}\left(\ket{\phi_{ai}}-\ket{\tilde{\phi}_{ai}}\right)\bra{\tilde{p}_{ai}}.

(34)

It allows us to recast the Kohn-Sham equations in terms of a generalized eigenvalue problem:

\tilde{H}\ket{\tilde{\psi}_{n{\bf k}}}=\varepsilon_{n{\bf k}}\tilde{S}\ket{\tilde{\psi}_{n{\bf k}}},

(35)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{H}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}\hat{H}\hat{\mathcal{T}},

(36)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}\hat{\mathcal{T}}.

(37)

The operators
\tilde{H}
and
\tilde{S}
are known as the PAW Hamiltonian and PAW overlap operator, respectively.

By simple differentiation of Eq. (
35
), it is possible to define the PS electron-phonon matrix element
20
:

g^{\text{PS}}_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})\equiv\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{m{\bf k}+{\bf q}}|\Delta_{{\bf q}\nu}\tilde{H}|\tilde{\psi}_{n{\bf k}}},

(38)

where the phonon displacement operator,
\Delta_{{\bf q}\nu}
, was defined in Eq. (
8
).
Alternatively, one can introduce the PAW transformation after differentiation, that is, directly reformulating Eq. (
7
) in terms of the PAW quantities.
This leads to the AE definition of the electron-phonon matrix element
12
,
g^{\text{AE}}_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})
, which is formally equivalent to
g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})
in the limit of a complete PAW basis.
The explicit form of
g^{\text{AE}}_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})
as computed in VASP is beyond the scope of this appendix and is given elsewhere
21
.
However, we are able to show the relationship between
g^{\text{AE}}_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})
and
g^{\text{PS}}_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})
:

g^{\text{AE}}_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})=g^{\text{PS}}_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})\\
+\left(\varepsilon_{n{\bf k}}-\varepsilon_{m{\bf k}+{\bf q}}\right)\braket{\tilde{\psi}_{m{\bf k}+{\bf q}}|\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}\Delta_{{\bf q}\nu}\hat{\mathcal{T}}|\tilde{\psi}_{n{\bf k}}},

(39)

clearly highlighting the non-equivalence of the two definitions for off-diagonal elements.

Both approaches have merits. For general applications, it is best to use the AE formulation, as it is well defined in many-body perturbation theory. The PS approach is well suited for the computation of the band-gap renormalization, where a formal equivalence with the AE approach can be shown in the adiabatic approximation
21
.

Appendix C
Comparison of conventions between VASP and EPW

The conventions used by VASP for the real-space Hamiltonian, IFCs, and electron-phonon matrix elements differ from those adopted in EPW, reflecting VASP’s alignment with a finite-difference scheme
20
. As a result, the real-space arrays read from the VASP’s output HDF5 file require careful reformatting to ensure compatibility with EPW’s interpolation routines.

This section addresses the case where the central cells of the electron and phonon grids do not coincide. Notation is as follows: Wannier centers are denoted by
m,n
; atoms by
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}
; and unit cell indices by
p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}~\in~[0,N_{p})
, with
N_{p}
the number of unit cells. The absolute positions of unit cell
p
for the electron and phonon grids, defined with respect to a random origin, are written as
{\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}}(m)
and
{\bf R}_{p}^{\text{ph}}(\kappa)
, respectively. The central (“original”, or first) unit cells correspond to
p=0
, with positions
{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{e}}(m)
and
{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{ph}}(\kappa)
.

Grid vectors in EPW (E) and VASP (V) are then grouped into different sets depending on the centers from which they are generated.
As illustrated in Fig.
6
, in both codes, the two-center electron–electron and phonon–phonon vectors are defined in a similar way

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p}^{H}(m,n)={\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}}(n)-{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{e}}(m),

(40)

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}^{D}(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})={\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}^{\text{ph}}(\kappa^{\prime})-{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{ph}}(\kappa),

(41)

however, the two-center electron–phonon vectors differ

\displaystyle{{\bf R}_{p}^{g}}^{\text{(E)}}(m,\kappa)={\bf R}_{p}^{\text{ph}}(\kappa)-{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{e}}(m),

(42)

\displaystyle{{\bf R}_{p}^{g}}^{\text{(V)}}(\kappa,m)={\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}}(m)-{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{ph}}(\kappa).

(43)

This difference originates from the distinct translational symmetries each code employs to minimize the number of electron–phonon matrix elements in Wannier space.

In VASP, all phonon displacements are effectively performed in one of the cells of the phonon grid, such that

\displaystyle g_{mn\kappa\alpha}^{\text{(V)}}

\displaystyle({\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}},{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}^{\text{ph}},{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}^{\text{e}})=\bra{m{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}^{\text{e}}}\left(\partial_{\kappa\alpha,{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}^{\text{ph}}}\hat{H}\right)\ket{n{\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}}}

(44)

\displaystyle=\bra{m{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}^{\text{e}}-{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}^{\text{ph}}}\left(\partial_{\kappa\alpha,{\bf 0}}\hat{H}\right)\ket{n{\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}}-{\bf R}_{p^{\prime}}^{\text{ph}}}~\forall~p^{\prime}.

If this reference cell is chosen as the central cell of the phonon grid, i.e.
p^{\prime}=0
, the full
N_{p}\times N_{p}\times N_{p}
electron-phonon matrix reduces to an
N_{p}\times N_{p}
form:

\displaystyle g_{mn\kappa\alpha}^{\text{(V)}}

\displaystyle({\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}},{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{ph}},{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}^{\text{e}})

(45)

\displaystyle=\bra{m\underbrace{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}^{\text{e}}-{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{ph}}}_{{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}^{g\text{(V)}}(\kappa,m)}}\left(\partial_{\kappa\alpha,{\bf 0}}\hat{H}\right)\ket{n\underbrace{{\bf R}_{p}^{\text{e}}-{\bf R}_{0}^{\text{ph}}}_{{{\bf R}_{p}^{g}}^{\text{(V)}}(\kappa,n)}}

\displaystyle=g_{mn\kappa\alpha}^{\text{(V)}}({{\bf R}_{p}^{g}}^{\text{(V)}},{\bf R}_{p^{\prime\prime}}^{g\text{(V)}})~\text{for some fixed}~p^{\prime}=0.

With this convention, the electron-phonon grid vectors in VASP are referenced to the central cell of the phonon grid, according to Eq. (
43

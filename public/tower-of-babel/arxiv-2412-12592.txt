Creating true muonium via charmonium radiative decay
Jian-Ping Dai, Hai-Bo Li, Shuai Zhao, Zong-Ying Zheng

Abstract
True muonium, the bound state of a muon and an antimuon ( μ + ​ μ − \mu^{+}\mu^{-} ), has long been theoretically predicted but remains experimentally elusive. We investigate the production of true para-muonium in the radiative decay of J / ψ J/\psi meson, and analyze the prospects for detecting true muonium in current and future high-energy e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} experiments, particularly focusing on the BESIII experiment and the proposed Super Tau-Charm Facility. Although the events are rare at the super tau-charm facility, the detection of true para-muonium via J / ψ J/\psi radiative decays could become feasible at its future updates.

II
The decay amplitude for
J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

The Feynman diagrams for
J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay are shown in Fig.
1
. In the process, the photon can be radiated either from the
J/\psi
meson or from outgoing muons (
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
). However, due to the conservation of parity, the first two diagrams, where the photon is radiated directly from the
J/\psi
meson, yield zero contribution, a result that can be rigorously verified through explicit calculations. Consequently, the nonzero contributions arise from final state radiation as depicted in Fig.
1
(c) and (d).

The Feynman diagrams for
J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

We treat the
J/\psi
meson as a nonrelativistic system composed of a charm quark (
c
) and an anti-charm quark (
\bar{c}
). Under the nonrelativistic approximation, one has
P_{J/\psi}^{2}=M_{J/\psi}^{2}\approx 4m_{c}^{2}
, where
M_{J/\psi}
is the mass of the
J/\psi
meson and
m_{c}
the charm quark mass. The momenta of the
\mu^{-}
,
\mu^{+}
and the radiated photon
\gamma
are denoted as
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
and
k
, respectively. The invariant mass of the
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
system is denoted as
\sqrt{s_{1}}
. We focus on the threshold production of the lepton pair, where
s_{1}\approx 4m_{\mu}^{2}\ll 4m_{c}^{2}
. In the rest frame of
J/\psi
, the energy of the photon,
E_{\gamma}
, is nearly
E_{\gamma}\sim m_{c}
. In this scenario, the lepton pair is nearly collinear, and the photon and lepton pair are produced back-to-back, maintaining momentum conservation in the decay process.

To calculate the decay amplitude, we adopt the spin projector for the spin-triplet
c\bar{c}
state as given in Ref.
[
39
]
,

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}(P,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8m_{c}^{3}}}\left(\not{p}+m_{c}\right)\not{\epsilon}\left(\not{p}-m_{c}\right),

(1)

where
p
is the momentum of the
c
or
\bar{c}
, respectively;
\epsilon
is the polarization vector of the spin-triplet
c\bar{c}
state. The normalization of the above projection operator corresponds to a relativistic normalization of the projected state. In nonrelativistic approximation, the momentum of
J/\psi
is given by
P_{J/\psi}\approx 2p
.

The decay amplitude of
J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
is calculated as

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}(J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=\operatorname{Tr}\bigg[\Pi_{1}(P,\epsilon_{J/\psi})(-ie_{Q}e\gamma^{\rho})\bigg]\frac{-i}{4m_{c}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\bar{u}(k_{1})\bigg[(-ie\gamma_{\rho})\frac{i}{-\not{k}-\not{k}_{2}-m_{\mu}}(-ie\not{\epsilon}^{*})

\displaystyle+(-ie\not{\epsilon}^{*})\frac{i}{\not{k}+\not{k}_{1}-m_{\mu}}(-ie\gamma_{\rho})\bigg]v(k_{2})\sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi}}R(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ie_{Q}e^{3}\sqrt{2m_{c}}\epsilon_{J/\psi}^{\rho}\frac{1}{2m_{c}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi}}R(0)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(k_{1})\bigg[\gamma_{\rho}\frac{-\not{k}-\not{k}_{2}+m_{\mu}}{2k\cdot k_{2}}\not{\epsilon}^{*}+\not{\epsilon}^{*}\frac{\not{k}+\not{k}_{1}+m_{\mu}}{2k\cdot k_{1}}\gamma_{\rho}\bigg]v(k_{2}),

(2)

where
R(0)
is the radial wave function of
J/\psi
at the origin,
\epsilon_{J/\psi}
and
\epsilon
are the polarization vectors of
J/\psi
and
\gamma
, respectively.
e
represents the elementary electric charge, while
e_{Q}=2/3
denotes the charge of the charm quark in units of
e
.

The squared amplitude, averaged over the
J/\psi
polarization states, is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\mathrm{pols.}}|i\mathcal{M}|^{2}=e_{Q}^{2}e^{6}\frac{1}{2m_{c}^{3}}\frac{R^{2}(0)}{4\pi}\frac{1}{3}\bigg(-g^{\rho\rho^{\prime}}+\frac{p^{\rho}p^{\rho^{\prime}}}{m^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\operatorname{Tr}\bigg[\bigg(\gamma_{\rho}\frac{-\not{k}-\not{k}_{2}+m_{\mu}}{2k\cdot k_{2}}\gamma_{\nu}+\gamma_{\nu}\frac{\not{k}+\not{k}_{1}+m_{\mu}}{2k\cdot k_{1}}\gamma_{\rho}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times(\not{k}_{2}-m_{\mu})\bigg(\gamma^{\nu}\frac{-\not{k}-\not{k}_{2}+m_{\mu}}{2k\cdot k_{2}}\gamma_{\rho^{\prime}}+\gamma_{\rho^{\prime}}\frac{\not{k}+\not{k}_{1}+m_{\mu}}{2k\cdot k_{1}}\gamma^{\nu}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times(\not{k}_{1}+m_{\mu})\bigg],

(3)

where the factor
1/3
comes from averaging over the three polarization states of the
J/\psi
.

In the near threshold production of the lepton pair, where the invariant mass
\sqrt{s_{1}}
of the
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
pair is much smaller than the mass of
J/\psi
, the above expression can be further simplified. When
s_{1}\ll 4m_{c}^{2}
, the squared amplitude can be approximated as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\mathrm{pols.}}|i\mathcal{M}|^{2}\bigg|_{s_{1}\ll 4m_{c}^{2}}\approx\frac{e_{Q}^{2}e^{6}}{3\pi m_{c}^{3}}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}+\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\bigg)R^{2}(0),

(4)

where
x_{1,2}\equiv E_{1,2}/m_{c}
are the energy fractions of the
\mu^{-}
and
\mu^{+}
, with
E_{1,2}
being their energies, respectively.

III
The phase space integral at small invariant mass

Then we turn to the three-body phase space integral, which is defined as

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\gamma}}\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{1}}\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{2}}

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(2p-k-k_{1}-k_{2}),

(5)

where
\vec{k}_{1},\vec{k}_{2},\vec{k}
are the three momenta of
\mu^{-}
,
\mu^{+}
and the photon, respectively, and
E_{1},E_{2},E_{\gamma}
are their corresponding energies. Integrating over the photon three momentum
\vec{k}
and recalling the invariant mass of the muon pair
s_{1}=(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}_{1}}{2E_{1}}\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}_{2}}{2E_{2}}\delta(4m_{c}^{2}+s_{1}-4m_{c}(E_{1}+E_{2})).

(6)

With spherical coordinates, the expression becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\frac{|\vec{k}_{1}|^{2}d|\vec{k}_{1}|d\cos\theta_{1}d\phi_{1}}{2E_{1}}\frac{|\vec{k}_{2}|^{2}d|\vec{k}_{2}|d\cos\theta_{12}d\phi_{2}}{2E_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\delta(4m_{c}^{2}+s_{1}-4m_{c}(E_{1}+E_{2})),

(7)

where
\theta_{12}
is the angle between
\vec{k}_{1}
and
\vec{k}_{2}
. The integrals over
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
can be calculated directly. Now we convert the integrals over
|\vec{k}_{1}|
and
|\vec{k}_{2}|
into the integral over
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
using the relations:

\displaystyle dE_{1}=\frac{|\vec{k}_{1}|}{E_{1}}d|\vec{k}_{1}|,~~~~dE_{2}=\frac{|\vec{k}_{2}|}{E_{2}}d|\vec{k}_{2}|.

(8)

The phase space integral then becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{4}\int dE_{1}d\cos\theta_{1}dE_{2}d\cos\theta_{12}|\vec{k}_{1}||\vec{k}_{2}|

\displaystyle\times\delta(4m_{c}^{2}+s_{1}-4m_{c}(E_{1}+E_{2})).

(9)

On the other hand, on account of

\displaystyle s_{1}=(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}=2m_{\mu}^{2}+2(E_{1}E_{2}-|\vec{k}_{1}||\vec{k}_{2}|\cos\theta_{12}),

(10)

we can convert the integral over
\cos\theta_{12}
into an integral over
s_{1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}d\cos\theta_{12}=\int_{s_{\mathrm{min}}}^{s_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{1}{2|\vec{k}_{1}||\vec{k}_{2}|}ds_{1},

(11)

where the limits are given by:

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{min}}=2m_{\mu}^{2}+2(E_{1}E_{2}-\sqrt{E_{1}^{2}-m_{\mu}^{2}}\sqrt{E_{2}^{2}-m_{\mu}^{2}}),

(12)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{max}}=2m_{\mu}^{2}+2(E_{1}E_{2}+\sqrt{E_{1}^{2}-m_{\mu}^{2}}\sqrt{E_{2}^{2}-m_{\mu}^{2}}).

(13)

These limits define the kinematically allowed range for
s_{1}
, completing the setup for the phase space integration.

After exchanging the order of integrals, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2^{3}}\int_{-1}^{1}d\cos\theta_{1}\iint dE_{1}dE_{2}\int_{s_{\mathrm{min}}}^{s_{\mathrm{max}}}ds_{1}

\displaystyle\times\delta(4m_{c}^{2}+s_{1}-4m_{c}(E_{1}+E_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2^{3}}\int_{-1}^{1}d\cos\theta_{1}\int ds_{1}\iint_{S}dE_{1}dE_{2}

\displaystyle\times\delta(4m_{c}^{2}+s_{1}-4m_{c}(E_{1}+E_{2})),

(14)

where
S
is the region in
E_{1}-E_{2}
plane with the boundaries given by the equation

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{max}}(E_{1},E_{2})=s_{1}~~\mathrm{or}~~s_{\mathrm{min}}(E_{1},E_{2})=s_{1}.

(15)

The range of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
is given as below. The delta function constraint
4m_{c}^{2}+s_{1}-4m_{c}(E_{1}+E_{2})=0
implies that the integral is nonzero only within this region. The boundary conditions are given by

\displaystyle 2m_{\mu}^{2}+2\left(E_{1}E_{2}-\sqrt{E_{1}^{2}-m_{\mu}^{2}}\sqrt{E_{2}^{2}-m_{\mu}^{2}}\right)=

\displaystyle s_{1},

(16)

\displaystyle 4m_{c}^{2}+s_{1}-4m_{c}(E_{1}+E_{2})=

\displaystyle 0.

(17)

Solving the two equations, we find

\displaystyle E_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{4m_{c}^{3}s_{1}+m_{c}s_{1}^{2}\pm\sqrt{s_{1}(s_{1}-4m_{\mu}^{2})m_{c}^{2}(s_{1}-4m_{c}^{2})^{2}}}{8m_{c}^{2}s_{1}},

(18)

\displaystyle E_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{4m_{c}^{3}s_{1}+m_{c}s_{1}^{2}\mp\sqrt{s_{1}(s_{1}-4m_{\mu}^{2})m_{c}^{2}(s_{1}-4m_{c}^{2})^{2}}}{8m_{c}^{2}s_{1}}.

(19)

When
s_{1}\ll m_{c}^{2}
, these can be simplified to

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{c}}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\mu}^{2}}{s_{1}}}\right),

(20)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{c}}{2}\left(1\mp\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\mu}^{2}}{s_{1}}}\right).

(21)

The velocity of the muon in the center-of-mass frame of the muon pair is
\beta=\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\mu}^{2}}{s_{1}}}
. Thus the final expression for the integration reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{16}\int ds_{1}\int_{\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta)}^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\beta)}dx_{1}.

(22)

Here,
x_{1}=\frac{E_{1}}{m_{c}}
, as mentioned in earlier section, represents the energy fraction of the
\mu^{-}
in the center-of-mass (c.o.m.) frame of
J/\psi
meson, with the integration limits determined by the velocity
\beta
of the muon.

IV
Coulomb resummation and Decay width

With the amplitude and the phase space integral calculated above, the decay width for the process
J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
at small invariant mass of lepton pair reads

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{ds_{1}}\bigg|_{s_{1}\ll 4m_{c}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{2}(0)\frac{e_{Q}^{2}\alpha^{3}}{24\pi m_{c}^{4}}\int_{\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta)}^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\beta)}dx_{1}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}+\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{Q}^{2}\alpha^{3}}{12\pi m_{c}^{4}}R^{2}(0)\bigg(\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-\beta\bigg),

(23)

where
\alpha=e^{2}/(4\pi)
is the fine structure constant. In the last line we have adopted the approximation
x_{2}=1-x_{1}+\frac{s_{1}}{4m_{c}^{2}}\approx 1-x_{1}
and performed the
x_{1}
-integral. In the threshold limit
\beta\to 0
, the expression
\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-\beta\sim\beta
, causing the decay width to vanish as
\beta=0
. The above formula gives the differential decay width for the decay
J/\psi
into a photon and a
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
pair with small invariant mass compared to the mass of
J/\psi
, but the lepton pair is not necessary bound each other.

In the threshold region, the
\mu^{+}/\mu^{-}
moves non-relativistically and can exchange Coulomb photons in the c.o.m. frame of the lepton pair. The effect of Coulomb photon exchange is of the order
\alpha/\beta\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
, which calls for a resummation to improve the convergence of the perturbative expansions. Such a resummation can be performed by solving the Green function of Schrödinger equation with a Coulomb potential
-\alpha/r
. The impact of Coulomb rescattering of the non-relativistic particles in QED was first
discussed by Sommerfeld, Sakharov and Schwinger
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
. Analog to Ref.
[
34
]
, the decay width can be related to the imaginary part of the Coulomb Green function with

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma(\mu^{+}\mu^{-}))}{ds_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{ds_{1}}\bigg|_{s_{1}\ll 4m_{c}^{2}}\frac{4\pi}{m_{\mu}^{2}\beta}\operatorname{Im}G_{E+i\Gamma}(0,0),

(24)

where
G_{E+i\Gamma}(\vec{r_{1}},\vec{r_{2}})
denotes the Coulomb Green function,
E=\sqrt{s_{1}}-2m_{\mu}
is the difference of the invariant mass of
(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
and the threshold, and
\Gamma
is the decay width of the lepton. The imaginary part of the Coulomb Green function is given by
[
43
]

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}G_{E+i\Gamma}(0,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{4\pi}\bigg[\frac{p_{2}}{m_{\mu}}+\frac{2p_{s}}{m_{\mu}}\arctan\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}+\frac{2p_{s}^{2}}{m_{\mu}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{4}}\frac{\Gamma p_{s}n+p_{2}(n^{2}\sqrt{E^{2}+\Gamma^{2}}+p_{s}^{2}/m_{\mu})}{\left(E+\frac{p_{s}^{2}}{m_{\mu}n^{2}}\right)^{2}+\Gamma^{2}}\bigg],

(25)

where

\displaystyle p_{s}=\frac{1}{2}m_{\mu}\alpha,~~p_{1,2}=\sqrt{\frac{m_{\mu}}{2}(\sqrt{E^{2}+\Gamma^{2}}\mp E)}.

(26)

Because the life time of muon is about 2
\mu
s, significantly longer than the life time of true muonium, which is the order of several picoseconds, we treat muon as a stable particle and take the narrow width limit, setting
\Gamma=0
setting for most practical calculations involving true muonium. However, when analyzing the production of true tauonium (
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
), the finite decay width
\Gamma
of the tau lepton must be taken into account, as it has a substantial impact on the system’s dynamics and observable effects.

By changing the variable from
s_{1}
to
E
with
E=\sqrt{s_{1}}-2m_{\mu}
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma(\mu^{+}\mu^{-}))}{dE}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{Q}^{2}\alpha^{3}}{12\pi m_{c}^{4}}R^{2}(0)2(E+2m_{\mu})\left(\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-\beta\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{4\pi}{m_{\mu}^{2}\beta}\operatorname{Im}G_{E+i0}(0,0).

(27)

To get rid of the radial wave function at the origin, which is a nonperturbative quantity, we introduce
R
as the ratio of the decay widths of
J/\psi\to\gamma(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
and
J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
,

\displaystyle R=\frac{\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma(\mu^{+}\mu^{-}))}{\Gamma(J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})},

(28)

where the decay width of
J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
is calculated as

\displaystyle\Gamma(J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=\frac{\alpha^{2}e_{Q}^{2}}{3m_{c}^{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{m_{c}^{2}}}\left(1+\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{2m_{c}^{2}}\right)R^{2}(0).

(29)

For bound states, which are below the threshold with a binding energy
E<0
, we finds that

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma(\mu^{+}\mu^{-}))}{dE}\bigg|_{E<0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{Q}^{2}\alpha^{3}}{12\pi m_{c}^{4}}R^{2}(0)2(E+2m_{\mu})\left(\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-\beta\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{4\pi}{m_{\mu}^{2}\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{3}m_{\mu}^{3}}{8n^{3}}\delta\left(E+\frac{\alpha^{2}m_{\mu}}{4n^{2}}\right).

(30)

The argument in the delta function can be identified as
E-E_{n}
, where
E_{n}=-\alpha^{2}m_{\mu}/(4n^{2})
represents the binding energy of the state with principal quantum number
n
. The squared wave function at the origin for these bound states is given by

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}(0)|^{2}=\frac{\alpha^{3}m_{\mu}^{3}}{8\pi n^{3}}.

(31)

Integrating over
E
yields the total decay width. When this result is divided by the decay width
\Gamma(J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
, one has

\displaystyle R|_{E<0}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{4}m_{\mu}^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{m_{c}^{2}}}(m_{\mu}^{2}+2m_{c}^{2})}\zeta(3)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{4}m_{\mu}^{2}}{2m_{c}^{2}}\zeta(3),

(32)

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function. We also verify the above result in another approach, with the help of spin-projector and the wave function of true para-muonium at the origin. This method provides an independent cross-check of our calculation.

When
E>0
, meaning the lepton pair is above the threshold, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma(\mu^{+}\mu^{-}))}{dE}\bigg|_{E>0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{Q}^{2}\alpha^{3}}{12\pi m_{c}^{4}}R^{2}(0)2(E+2m_{\mu})\left(\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-\beta\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{4\pi}{m_{\mu}^{2}\beta}\frac{\alpha m_{\mu}^{2}}{4\left(1-e^{-\frac{\alpha\pi m_{\mu}}{\sqrt{Em_{\mu}}}}\right)}.

(33)

One can identify that the term in the last line is the Sommerfeld-Schwinger-Sakharov factor

\displaystyle S(\beta)\equiv\frac{\pi\alpha/\beta}{1-e^{-\frac{\alpha\pi m_{\mu}}{\sqrt{Em_{\mu}}}}},

(34)

which accounts for the Coulomb enhancement of the decay rate near the threshold.
For the ratio
R
of decay widths, one has

\displaystyle R|_{E>0}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dE(E+2m_{\mu})\frac{(\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-\beta)S(\beta)}{\sqrt{1-\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{m_{Q}^{2}}}(2m_{Q}^{2}+m_{\mu}^{2})}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\frac{m_{\mu}}{m_{c}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{dE}{1-e^{-\pi\alpha\sqrt{\frac{m_{\mu}}{E}}}},

(35)

where the integral is calculated in an energy window
[0,\Lambda]
.

True muonium, once produced, can be identified by its characteristic decay length. The lifetime of ground-state true para-muonium is approximately 0.6 ps. In the lab frame, true muonium generated via radiative
J/\psi

\gamma\approx 7.4
,
leading to an average travel distance of around 1.3 mm in the detector. The distance allows the decay vertex of true muonium to be spatially separated from that of
J/\psi

\eta

\eta_{c}
), all of which have zero flight distance within the detector. If the detector vertex resolution is able to reach 0.4 mm, this separation is well within measurable limits, allowing for the identification of true muonium. We hope that with the continuous accumulation of data and advancements in the neutral track vertex reconstruction, enabling precise reconstruction of the true muonium decay length to effectively discriminate against backgrounds, observing genuine para-muonium in
J/\psi

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have explored the potential for detecting true para-muonium through the radiative decay of the
J/\psi
meson at electron-positron colliders. True muonium, a bound state of a muon and antimuon, remains an elusive particle despite decades of theoretical interest. The
J/\psi
radiative decay, e.g.,

J/\psi\to\gamma(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
, offers a unique and clean channel to detect the true muonium. The production of true para-muonium via this decay is primarily governed by QED processes. We presented a detailed analysis of the relevant Feynman diagrams and computed the decay width, accounting for the Sommerfeld enhancement due to the Coulomb interaction between the muon-antimuon pair. We demonstrated that the branching ratio of this channel is of the order
10^{-13}
, which makes
the true muonium difficult to be observed via
J/\psi
decays in current experiments; however,
future high-energy collider experiments, such as the proposed Super Tau-Charm Facility, offer promising avenues for discovering this exotic QED bound state.

Minimum packing density for sets of four integers
Cindy Li, David Offner, Iris Ye

Abstract
We prove that the set { 0 , 1 , 4 , 6 } \{0,1,4,6\} achieves the minimum packing density among all sets of integers with cardinality four, with a density of 1 7 \frac{1}{7} .



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of integers, and throughout the paper
S
will always be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
. Given
T\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, define
S+T:=\{s+t:s\in S,t\in T\}
. Similarly, given
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
S+t=S+\{t\}=\{s+t:s\in S\}
, and
t+S:=\{t\}+S=\{t+s:s\in S\}
. Note
t+S=S+t
for all
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Given a set
S
, we say a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is
S
-packing
if for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
s.t.
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
,
S+a_{1}
and
S+a_{2}
are disjoint. For example, if
S=\{0,1,4,6\}
, then
A=\{7n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is an
S
-packing set.

Given a finite set
S
, we are interested in the densest set
A
such that
A
is
S
-packing.
For a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, define the
upper density
\overline{d}(A)
and
lower density
\underline{d}(A)
as follows:

\overline{d}(A):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\frac{|A\cap[-n,n]|}{2n+1}}\hskip 42.67912pt\underline{d}(A):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\frac{|A\cap[-n,n]|}{2n+1}}.

If
\overline{d}(A)=\underline{d}(A)
, define the
density
d(A)
of
A
as

d(A):=\overline{d}(A)=\underline{d}(A).

For example, if
A=\{7n:n\ \in\mathbb{Z}\}
, then
d(A)=\overline{d}(A)=\underline{d}(A)=1/7
.

We now define the
packing density
of
S
, denoted
d_{p}(S)
, as

d_{p}(S):=\sup\{\overline{d}(A):A\text{ is }S\text{-packing}\}.

Many researchers have studied packing alongside the dual concept of covering. Define a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
as
S
-covering
if
S+A=\mathbb{Z}
, and define the
covering density
of
S
, denoted
d_{c}(S)
, as the infimum of the lower densities
\underline{d}(A)
of all
S
-covering sets
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
.

Motivated by Newman
[
2
]
, Schmidt and Tuller
[
3
]
proposed a conjecture regarding the packing and covering densities for integer sets of cardinality three. Later, Frankl, Kupavskii, and Sagdeev
[
1
]
proved an explicit formula for the packing and covering densities of such sets, confirming the conjecture. For integer sets with size
k
where
k\geq 5
, Weinstein
[
4
]
proved the packing density is upper-bounded by
\left(\frac{k^{2}}{4}+\frac{11k}{6}-14\right)^{-1}
.

Our main result concerns the minimum packing density of any set of integers with cardinality four.

Main Theorem

If
S
is a set of integers with cardinality four, then
d_{p}(S)\geq 1/7
. Furthermore, the set
\{0,1,4,6\}
achieves this minimum packing density:
d_{p}(\{0,1,4,6\})=1/7
.

The proof is given in Sections
2
and
3
, where we prove upper bounds and lower bounds on packing density, respectively. First, we make an observation about
S
-packing sets that will be used in both sections. Define the
difference set
of
S
, denoted
\mathrm{diff}(S)
, as
\mathrm{diff}(S):=\{s-t:s,t\in S,s\geq t\}
. For example, if
S=\{0,1,3\}
, then
\mathrm{diff}(S)=\{0,1,2,3\}
, and if
S=\{0,2,7\}
, then
\mathrm{diff}(S)=\{0,2,5,7\}
. Note that if
S
is finite, then
|\mathrm{diff}(S)|\leq\binom{|S|}{2}+1
.

Proposition 1

Suppose
S,A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. Then
A
is
S
-packing if and only if for all
a,b\in A
with
a<b
,
b-a\notin\mathrm{diff}(S)
.

Proof.

We will prove that
A
is not
S
-packing if and only if there exists
a,b\in A
such that
a<b
and
b-a\in\mathrm{diff}(S)
. Let
a,b\in A
be such that
a<b
and
b-a\in\mathrm{diff}(S)
. By the definition of
\mathrm{diff}(S)
, there exist
s,t\in S
such that
b-a=s-t
. Thus
a+s=b+t
. This implies that
(b+S)\cap(a+S)\neq\emptyset
, so
A
is not
S
-packing.

Conversely, assume
A
is not
S
-packing. This implies there exists
a,b\in A
with
a<b
and
(b+S)\cap(a+S)\neq\emptyset
, so there exist
s,t\in S
such that
a+s=b+t
, and equivalently
b-a=s-t
. Since
a<b
,
s>t
, so
s-t\in\mathrm{diff}(S)
. Hence,
b-a\in\mathrm{diff}(S)
.
∎

2
An upper bound on packing density

Proposition 2

If
\{0,\dots,n\}\subseteq\mathrm{diff}(S)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
d_{p}(S)\leq\frac{1}{n+1}
.

Proof.

Suppose
A
is
S
-packing, and
\{0,\dots,n\}\subseteq\mathrm{diff}(S)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. For
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
I(t)
be the set of
n+1
consecutive integers
\{t(n+1),t(n+1)+1,\dots,t(n+1)+n\}
. Let
a,b
be two elements of
A
. If
a,b\in I(t)
with
a<b
, then
b-a\in\{0,\dots,n\}\subseteq\mathrm{diff}(S)
. Thus by Proposition
1
, at most one element of
A
can be in
I(t)
for each
t
.

For any
k\geq 1
, since
[-k(n+1),k(n+1)]=\bigcup_{t=-k}^{k-1}I(t)\cup\{k(n+1)\}
,

\frac{|A\cap[-k(n+1),k(n+1)]|}{2k(n+1)+1}\leq\frac{2k+1}{2k(n+1)+1}.

Thus

\overline{d}(A)\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\frac{2k+1}{2k(n+1)+1}}\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{2k+1}{2k}=\frac{1}{n+1}.

We conclude every
S
-packing set must have upper density at most
\frac{1}{n+1}
, so by the definition of packing density,
d_{p}(S)\leq\frac{1}{n+1}
.
∎

Corollary 3

The packing density of
\{0,1,4,6\}
is at most
\frac{1}{7}
, i.e.

d_{p}(\{0,1,4,6\})\leq\frac{1}{7}.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{diff}{(\{0,1,4,6\})}=\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}
, applying Proposition
2
with
n=6
, we get
d_{p}(\{0,1,4,6\})\leq\frac{1}{6+1}=\frac{1}{7}
.
∎

3
A lower bound on packing density

In this section, given a finite set of integers
S
, we describe a “greedy packing algorithm” to generate an
S
-packing set
A
, while proving properties of this set along the way. The set
A
may not be the densest
S
-packing set, but we will show that it has density at least
1/|\mathrm{diff}(S)|
. For every
i\geq 0
, the algorithm iteratively produces sequences
\{t_{i}\}
and
\{A_{i}\}
, where
t_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
A_{i}
is
S
-packing, as follows: Initially, set
A_{-1}=\emptyset
. Then, for
i\geq 0
, define
A_{i}=A_{i-1}\cup\{t_{i}\}
, where

t_{i}=\min\{x:x\geq 0,x\notin(A_{i-1}+\mathrm{diff}(S))\}.

Note
t_{0}=0
, and for all
i\geq-1
,
A_{i}\subseteq A_{i+1}
. Thus,
A_{i}+\mathrm{diff}(S)\subseteq A_{i+1}+\mathrm{diff}(S)
, so since in each step
t_{i}\geq 0
is chosen to be minimal,
t_{i}<t_{i+1}
.

As an example, if
S=\{0,4,5\}
, then
t_{0}=0
,
t_{1}=2
,
t_{2}=8
,
t_{3}=10
, and so forth. In this example
A_{0}=\{0\}
,
A_{1}=\{0,2\}
,
A_{2}=\{0,2,8\}
,
A_{3}=\{0,2,8,10\}
, and so forth.

Lemma 4

Let
S
be a finite set. For all
i\geq 0
,
t_{i}\leq i|\mathrm{diff}(S)|
.

Proof.

For
i\geq 0
, since
t_{i}
is the smallest nonnegative integer not in
A_{i-1}+\mathrm{diff}(S)
,
t_{i}\leq|A_{i-1}+\mathrm{diff}(S)|
.
Thus it suffices to show that for all
i\geq 0
,

|A_{i-1}+\mathrm{diff}(S)|\leq i|\mathrm{diff}(S)|.

This follows directly from the fact that for
i\geq 0
,
A_{i-1}=\{t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1}\}
has cardinality
i
. Thus

|A_{i-1}+\mathrm{diff}(S)|=|\{t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1}\}+\mathrm{diff}(S)|=|\bigcup_{k=0}^{i-1}t_{k}+\mathrm{diff}(S)|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}|t_{k}+\mathrm{diff}(S)|=i|\mathrm{diff}(S)|.

∎

Next, define the set
A_{\infty}
to be the union of all
A_{i}
’s produced by the greedy packing algorithm:

A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}A_{i}.

In the example where
S=\{0,4,5\}
,
A_{\infty}=\{0,2,8,10,16,18,24,26,\dots\}
.

Define a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
to be
periodic
if there is some positive integer
p
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

i\in A\iff i+p\in A,

and we say
A
has period
p
. Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
and integers
a
and
b
with
b>a
, define the
interval density
d(A,a,b)
of
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
restricted to the interval
[a,b-1]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
as

d(A,a,b):=\frac{|A\cap[a,b-1]|}{b-a}.

Note that if
A
is periodic with period
p
,
d(A)
is defined and
d(A)=d(A,a,a+p)
for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p>0
, define a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
to be
a
-forward-periodic with period
p
if, for all
i\geq 0
,

a+i\in A\iff a+i+p\in A.

For example, the set
\{0,2,8,10,16,18,24,26,\dots\}
is
0
-forward periodic with period
8
.

For a set of integers
S
, define the
diameter
of
S
, denoted
\diam(S)
, as

\diam(S):=\sup{\{s-t:s,t\in S\}}.

Lemma 5

There exist
a,b\geq 0
with
a<b
such that
A_{\infty}
is
a
-forward periodic with period
b-a
.

Proof.

Let’s first construct
a
and
b
. For
t,i\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
f:\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\{0,1\}
so that

f(t,i)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }t+i\in A_{\infty}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Further, define
F:\mathbb{Z}\to\{0,1\}^{\diam(S)}
to be the function where

F(t)=\left(f(t,0),\dots,f(t,\diam(S)-1)\right)

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since the codomain
\{0,1\}^{\diam(S)}
of
F
is finite, by the pigeonhole principle, there must be
a,b
where
0\leq a<b
such that
F(a)=F(b)
. For these values of
a
and
b
, we prove that
A_{\infty}
is
a
-forward periodic with period
b-a
by induction on
i
. That is, we will prove that for all
i\geq 0
,

a+i\in A_{\infty}\iff b+i\in A_{\infty}.

Base cases (
i=0,1,\dots,\diam(S)-1
): Recall
a
and
b
are fixed such that
F(a)=F(b)
. By definition of
F
, it means for all
0\leq i<\diam(S)
,
a+i\in A_{\infty}
if and only if
b+i\in A_{\infty}
.

Inductive step: For the induction hypothesis, let
m\geq\diam(S)-1
, and assume for all
0\leq i\leq m
that

a+i\in A_{\infty}\iff b+i\in A_{\infty}.

We now show
a+(m+1)\in A_{\infty}\iff b+(m+1)\in A_{\infty}
.

Suppose
a+(m+1)\notin A_{\infty}
. Since
a+(m+1)
is not chosen in the algorithm,
a+(m+1)\in A_{\infty}+\mathrm{diff}(S)
. Thus, there exists
a_{0}\in A_{\infty}
and
s,t\in S
with
s\geq t
such that
a_{0}+s-t=a+(m+1)
. Since
a_{0}\in A_{\infty}
and
a+(m+1)\notin A_{\infty}
,
s\neq t
, so
s>t
.

Note
a_{0}=a+(m+1)-(s-t)
. Since
s-t\in\mathrm{diff}(S)
and
s\neq t
,
1\leq s-t\leq\diam(S)
. Also, note
m+1\geq\diam(S)
. Thus,
0\leq(m+1)-(s-t)\leq m
.

Let
b_{0}=b+(m+1)-(s-t)
. By the induction hypothesis,
b_{0}\in A_{\infty}
. Thus,
b+(m+1)\in A_{\infty}+\mathrm{diff}(S)
, and by the construction of
A_{\infty}
,
b+(m+1)
would not be chosen by the greedy packing algorithm, and
b+(m+1)\notin A_{\infty}
.

The same argument shows that if
b+(m+1)\notin A_{\infty}
then
a+m+1\notin A_{\infty}
. Thus
A_{\infty}
is
a
-forward periodic with period
b-a
.
∎

For the remainder of the section, let
a
and
b
be as in Lemma
5
, i.e.
A_{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is
a
-forward periodic with period
b-a
.

Lemma 6

If
a<b\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
A_{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is
a
-forward periodic with period
b-a
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(A_{\infty},0,n)=d(A_{\infty},a,b).

Proof.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}d(A_{\infty},0,n)=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{|A_{\infty}\cap[0,n-1]|}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{|A_{\infty}\cap[0,a-1]|}{n}+\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{|A_{\infty}\cap[a,n-1]|}{n}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{|A_{\infty}\cap[a,n-1]|}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left({\frac{\lfloor{\frac{n-a}{b-a}\rfloor}|A_{\infty}\cap[a,b-1]|}{n}+o(1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left(\frac{n-a}{n}\right)\left(\frac{|A_{\infty}\cap[a,b-1]|}{b-a}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{\infty}\cap[a,b-1]|}{b-a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(A_{\infty},a,b)

∎

Given
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
a<b\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the
remainder

r(x)
to be the integer where
0\leq\emph{r(x)}<b-a
and

x-a\equiv r(x)\mod(b-a).

We are finally ready to define the
S
-packing set
A
produced by the greedy packing algorithm. Given
A_{\infty}
,
a
, and
b
as in Lemma
5
, define
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
to be the set such that

x\in A\iff a+r(x)\in A_{\infty}\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}.

For example, if
S=\{0,4,5\}
,
A=\{\dots,-16,-14,-8,-6,0,2,8,10,16,18,24,26,\dots\}
.

By construction,
A
is periodic with period
b-a
, and therefore
d(A)=d(A,a,b)
. Furthermore, since for
i\geq 0
,
a+i\in A\iff a+i\in A_{\infty}
,
d(A,a,b)=d(A_{\infty},a,b)
. Combining the two equations, we have

d(A)=d(A_{\infty},a,b).

Proposition 7

Let
A
be the set produced by the greedy packing algorithm. Then
A
is
S
-packing with
d(A)\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
A
is
S
-packing. Assume for the sake of contradiction that
A
is not
S
-packing. Then, by Proposition
1
, there exist
c
,
d\in A
with
c<d
and
d-c\in\mathrm{diff}(S)
. By the construction of
A
, since
c
and
d
are in
A
, we know
a+r(c)
and
a+r(d)
are in
A_{\infty}
. Since
d-c\equiv r(d)-r(c)\pmod{b-a}
, we know
(d-c)+r(c)\equiv r(d)\pmod{b-a}
. Thus since
A_{\infty}
is
a
-forward periodic with period
b-a
, and
a+r(d)\in A_{\infty}
, we know
a+r(c)+(d-c)\in A_{\infty}
. Since
a+r(c)
and
a+r(c)+(d-c)
are both in
A_{\infty}
, with
d-c\in\mathrm{diff}(S)
, this contradicts the fact that, by construction,
A_{\infty}
is
S
-packing. Thus
A
is
S
-packing.

Then, we show
d(A)\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}
. For all
i\geq 0
,
t_{i}\leq i|\mathrm{diff}(S)|
by Lemma
4
. Hence, for all
n\geq 1
,

|A_{\infty}\cap[0,n-1]|\geq\frac{n}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}.

Rearranging the inequality, we have that for all
n\geq 1
,

d(A_{\infty},0,n)=\frac{|A_{\infty}\cap[0,n-1]|}{n}\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}.

Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(A_{\infty},0,n)\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}.

By Lemma
6
,

d(A_{\infty},a,b)\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}.

Since we have already shown that
d(A)=d(A_{\infty},a,b)
, we conclude

d(A)\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}.

Thus
A
is
S
-packing and
d(A)\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}
.
∎

Corollary 8

Every finite set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
has
d_{p}(S)\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Proposition
7
.
∎

Corollary 9

The packing density of
\{0,1,4,6\}
is lower bounded by
\frac{1}{7}
, i.e.

d_{p}(\{0,1,4,6\})\geq\frac{1}{7}.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{diff}{(\{0,1,4,6\})}=\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}
, applying Corollary
8
,

d_{p}(\{0,1,4,6\})\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}{(\{0,1,4,6\})}|}=\frac{1}{7}.

∎

Note that for
S=\{0,1,4,6\}
, the greedy packing algorithm produces the
S
-packing set
A=\{7n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, which is densest possible by Corollary
3
.

We now prove the main theorem.

Main Theorem

If
S
is a set of integers with cardinality four, then
d_{p}(S)\geq\frac{1}{7}
. Furthermore, the set
\{0,1,4,6\}
achieves this minimum packing density:
d_{p}(\{0,1,4,6\})=\frac{1}{7}
.

Proof.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{Z}
be an arbitrary set with
4
integers. By Corollary
8
,
d_{p}(S)\geq\frac{1}{|\mathrm{diff}(S)|}
. Since
|\mathrm{diff}(S)|\leq 7
for all integer sets with cardinality
4
, we conclude

d_{p}(S)\geq\frac{1}{7}.

At the same time, combining the inequalities in Corollary
3
and
9
, we have

d_{p}(\{0,1,4,6\})=\frac{1}{7}.

Hence,
\{0,1,4,6\}
achieves the minimum packing density among all sets of four integers.
∎

The CFT of Sen’s Formulation of Chiral Gauge Fields Preprint: Imperial-TP-2025-CH-6
Chris Hull, Neil Lambert

Abstract
Sen’s action for chiral bosons in 2 dimensions describes two chiral scalars, one of which couples to the physical metric and one of which couples to a flat metric. It has a generalisation in which the flat metric is replaced by an arbitrary second metric and so can be formulated on any curved world-sheet. When the two metrics are equal, the theory reduces to a β ​ γ \beta\gamma system, giving a non-unitary c = 2 c=2 conformal field theory. We argue that the relation between this and the theory of two chiral bosonic scalars of the same chirality can be viewed as a ‘bosonisation’. We show that the standard vertex operators for the chiral scalars are vertex operators and line operators in the Sen formulation and derive the formulation in the Sen theory of correlation functions in the chiral scalar theory. The flat space Sen theory can be coupled to two different world-sheet metrics in such a way that one scalar couples to one metric and the other to the other metric, so obtaining the general formulation with two metrics. In d = 4 ​ k + 2 d=4k+2 dimensions, the bi-metric action for a 2 ​ k 2k -form gauge field with self-dual field strength reduces, when the two metrics are equal, to a conformal field theory with a B ​ F BF -type action, except that B B is a self-dual d / 2 d/2 -form and F F is a d / 2 d/2 -form field strength, F = d ​ P F=dP . The self-duality of B B means that this is not a topological theory but instead represents two self-dual gauge fields. This has a generalisation to a democratic action for p p -form gauge fields in any dimension.

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}},

\displaystyle\bar{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}\approx-\frac{1}{(2k)!}\bar{g}^{\rho_{1}\lambda_{1}}...\bar{g}^{\rho_{2k}\lambda_{2k}}G_{\mu\rho_{1}...\rho_{2k}}G_{\nu\lambda_{1}...\lambda_{2k}}.

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}\Theta_{\mu\nu}

), the OPEs are

C(z)C(w)\sim-\frac{1}{4\pi}\log(z-w),\quad A(z)A(w)\sim\frac{1}{4\pi}\log(z-w),

(69)

and

T=\partial A\partial A-\partial C\partial C.

(70)

Then the theory is one of a conventional scalar
A
and a
negative-energy non-unitary scalar
C
, with total central charge
c=2
.

Vertex operators in the
AC
system are made from regularised
(normal-ordered) combinations of
\partial A
,
\partial C
,
e^{ikA}
,
e^{ipC}
in the usual way. These can then be translated to
vertex operators and line operators in the
Q^{\prime}P
system. For
example,

e^{ikA(z)}=\exp\left(\frac{ik}{{2}}(S-P)(z)\right)=\exp\left(\frac{ik}{{2}}\left(\int^{z}Q^{\prime}-P(z)\right)\right)=\exp\left(\frac{ik}{{2}}\left(\int^{z}[Q^{\prime}-\partial P]\right)\right).

(71)

and a correlation function of vertex operators

\langle e^{ik_{1}A(z_{1})}\ldots e^{ik_{n}A(z_{n})}\rangle_{AC},

(72)

in the
AC
system can be calculated in the
Q^{\prime}P
system
as a correlation function of line operators

\langle e^{ik_{1}L(z_{1})}\ldots e^{ik_{n}L(z_{n})}\rangle,

(73)

where

L(z)=\frac{1}{{2}}\left(-P(z)+\int^{z}Q^{\prime}\right).

(74)

We now turn to the dependence of the line operators and the correlation
functions on the choice of contours.
Suppose the scalar
S
is periodic,
S\sim S+2\pi R^{\prime}
for some
R^{\prime}
, or, equivalently, the
periods of
Q^{\prime}
on 1-cycles
{\mathcal{C}}
are quantised,

\frac{1}{2\pi R^{\prime}}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}Q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}.

(75)

We have seen that if
P
is compactified on circle of radius
R
, then
R^{\prime}=r_{1}R/r_{2}
for some integers
r_{1},r_{2}
.
Then for 2 different paths
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
from
z_{0}
to
z
, the
combination
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-
\mathcal{P}
defines a closed contour
and

\int_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}-\int_{\mathcal{P}}Q^{\prime}=\oint_{\mathcal{C}}Q^{\prime}\in 2\pi R^{\prime}\mathbb{Z},

(76)

so that the line operator

e^{ik\int_{\mathcal{P}}Q^{\prime}},

(77)

is independent of the choice of contour provided

k=\frac{n}{R^{\prime}},

(78)

for some integer
n
. Then the correlation function (
73
) is
well-defined provided
k_{i}=n_{i}/R^{\prime}
for integer
n_{i}
.
If the periods of
Q^{\prime}
were not quantised, or if the
k_{i}
are not quantised as
k_{i}=n_{i}/R^{\prime}
, then the line operators and their correlation functions could depend on the choice of contours.

The Gauged CFT and its Symmetries

7.1
Gauged Actions

A chiral CFT can be coupled to gravity by adding a Noether coupling to a
component
h_{--}
of the graviton

\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}x\,h_{--}T_{++}.

(79)

For example, for a free scalar
A
, the gauged action is

S=-\int d^{2}x\,(\partial_{+}A\partial_{-}A-\tfrac{1}{2}h_{--}\partial_{+}A\partial_{+}A).

(80)

This linear action is invariant under the gauge transformations

\delta A=k_{-}\partial_{+}A,

(81)

\delta h_{--}=2\partial_{-}k_{-}+k_{-}\partial_{+}h_{--}-h_{--}\partial_{+}k_{-},

(82)

for a general
k_{-}(x)

Hull:1988dp
;
Hull:1989wu
. Invariance under transformations with parameter
k_{+}(x)
would require coupling to a graviton
h_{++}
and invariance under both
symmetries leads to an action with non-linear dependence on both
h_{++}
and

h_{--}
– this is the usual gravitational coupling.

For the non-unitary theories considered here, the conventional coupling to gravity

h_{--}T_{++}+\dots

would give a
theory with negative energies.
However, in the spirit of
Hull:1989wu
, it is possible to
introduce a coupling to two gravitons,
h_{--}
and
\bar{h}_{--}

with

h_{--}
coupling to
\Theta_{++}=F_{+}F_{+}
and
\bar{h}_{--}
coupling to
\bar{\Theta}_{++}=-G_{+}G_{+}
.
This gives the linear coupling

S_{lin}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}x\,(h_{--}F_{+}F_{+}-\bar{h}_{--}G_{+}G_{+}).

(83)

With
F,G
given in terms of
Q^{\prime},P
by (
31
), this can be added to
the
Q^{\prime},P
action (
27
) to give

S_{gauged}=\int d^{2}x\,Q^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{-}P+\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}x\,(h_{--}F_{+}F_{+}-\bar{h}_{--}G_{+}G_{+}).

(84)

This can
be viewed as the action (
2
) in the gauge in which

\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-h_{--}&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right),\quad\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\bar{h}_{--}&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right).

(85)

Setting
\bar{h}_{--}=0
and adding coupling to
h_{++}
then gives the
Sen action with non-linear dependence on the graviton. On the other hand,
keeping
\bar{h}_{--}
and adding coupling to
h_{++}
,
\bar{h}_{++}

leads to the non-linear action (
2
) of
Hull:2023dgp
. It is remarkable that it is
possible to find a non-linear coupling to both gravitons. A geometric
formulation of this construction was given in
Hull:2023dgp
.

7.2
Gauge Symmetries

In
Hull:2023dgp
, it was shown that (
2
) has two gauge symmetries, one with vector parameter
\zeta^{\mu}
for which
g
is the gauge field and one with
vector parameter
\chi^{\mu}
for which
\bar{g}
is the gauge field.
This then implies that the gauged action (
84
) should have gauge symmetries with vector parameter
\zeta_{-}
for which
h
is the gauge field, transforming as

\displaystyle\delta h_{--}=2\partial_{-}\zeta_{-}+\dots,

(86)

and
gauge symmetries with vector parameter
\chi_{-}
for which
\bar{h}
is the gauge field, transforming as

\displaystyle\delta\bar{h}_{--}=2\partial_{-}\chi_{-}+\dots.

(87)

This is indeed the case. Using
results from
Hull:2023dgp
;
Hull:1988dp
;
Hull:1988si
, the action (
84
)
with

F_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(Q^{\prime}_{+}-\partial_{+}P),\quad G_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(Q^{\prime}_{+}+\partial_{+}P),

(88)

is invariant under the
\zeta
transformations

\displaystyle\delta h_{--}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\partial_{-}\zeta_{-}+\zeta_{-}\partial_{+}h_{--}-h_{--}\partial_{+}\zeta_{-},

(89)

\displaystyle\delta\bar{h}_{--}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\delta P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\zeta_{-}F_{+},

\displaystyle\delta Q^{\prime}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{+}(\zeta_{-}F_{+}),

(90)

so that

\delta G_{+}=0,\qquad\delta F_{+}=\partial_{+}(\zeta_{-}F_{+}).

(91)

Under the
\chi
transformations we have

\displaystyle\delta h_{--}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\delta\bar{h}_{--}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\partial_{-}\chi_{-}+\chi_{-}\partial_{+}\bar{h}_{--}-\bar{h}_{--}\partial_{+}\chi_{-},

\displaystyle\delta P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{-}G_{+},

\displaystyle\delta Q^{\prime}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{+}(\chi_{-}G_{+})

(92)

so that

\delta G_{+}=\partial_{+}(\chi_{-}G_{+}),\qquad\delta F_{+}=0.

(93)

The diagonal subgroup with
\zeta_{-}=\chi_{-}=\xi_{-}
is the diffeomorphism symmetry,
with

\delta F_{+}={\cal L}_{\xi}F_{+},\quad\delta G_{+}={\cal L}_{\xi}G_{+},\quad\delta g_{\mu\nu}={\cal L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu},\quad\delta\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}={\cal L}_{\xi}\bar{g}_{\mu\nu},

(94)

with all fields transforming with the Lie derivative
{\cal L}_{\xi}
.
See Appendix B for a derivation of these
\zeta,\chi,\xi
transformations.

7.3
Anomalies

It is well-known that chiral CFTs suffer from gravitational anomalies, so that the two-dimensional diffeomorphism symmetry is anomalous. These manifest themselves through a non-vanishing two-point function of the energy-momentum tensor. This in turn can be thought of as computing the one-point function of in the presence of a linearised perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}(x)\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\int T_{\mu\nu}(x)e^{-iS+\tfrac{i}{2}\int h^{\lambda\rho}T_{\lambda\rho}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{i}{2}\int h^{\lambda\rho}(y)\langle T_{\mu\nu}(x)T_{\lambda\rho}(y)\rangle dy+\dots.

(95)

In the case studied here, there are two independent
symmetries of the action (
84
) with parameters
\zeta^{\mu},\chi^{\mu}
with corresponding
conserved currents
\Theta_{\mu\nu},\bar{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}
that enter the perturbation (
83
). Each of these is anomalous, and they combine to give the gravitational anomaly, which is the anomaly in the diagonal subgroup, the diffeomorphism symmetry acting as (
94
).
It is a straight-forward exercise to compute, in either the
Q^{\prime}P
or
AC
system, that

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{++}(z)\Theta_{++}(w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(z-w)^{4}},

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{++}(z)\bar{\Theta}_{++}(w)\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\Theta}_{++}(z)\bar{\Theta}_{++}(w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(z-w)^{4}},

\displaystyle\langle T_{++}(z)T_{++}(w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(z-w)^{4}}.

(96)

Thus we find
c_{\Theta}=1=c_{\bar{\Theta}}=1
and
c_{T}=2
, so that there is an anomaly in both the
\zeta^{\mu}
and
\chi^{\mu}
symmetries, which combine to give the anomaly in the diffeomorphism symmetry.
Furthermore there is no mixed anomaly between the two types of diffeomorphisms. This then implies that they can be separately cancelled by coupling to suitable matter in both the physical and shadow sectors. That is, the anomaly in the
\zeta^{\mu}
symmetry can be cancelled by adding further matter coupling to
h_{--}
while the anomaly in the
\chi^{\mu}
symmetry can be cancelled by adding further matter coupling to
\bar{h}_{--}
. This will be discussed further elsewhere.

8
CFT In
d
-Dimensions

Consider now the action (
2
) in
d
dimensions. In the special case in
which
g=\bar{g}
, we have
M=0
and the action is

S=\int\left(-\frac{1}{2}dP\wedge\ast dP+Q\wedge dP\right)=\int Q^{\prime}\wedge dP,

(97)

where

Q^{\prime}=Q+\frac{1}{2}(dP+\ast dP),

(98)

is self-dual,
Q^{\prime}=\ast Q^{\prime}
.
This theory can be regarded as a higher dimensional analogue of the
\beta\gamma
system.

The field equations are

dQ^{\prime}=0,

(99)

and

dP=\ast dP.

(100)

Then

Q^{\prime}=dS,

(101)

for some
q-1
form potential
S
and

dS=\ast dS.

(102)

Then
P
and
S
are chiral
(q-1)
-form gauge fields. The map from the
Q^{\prime},P
system to the
S,P
system can be thought of as a higher dimensional
version of the 2-dimensional ‘bosonisation’ considered above.

The self-dual field strengths (
5
),(
7
) become

G=\frac{1}{2}(dP+{\ast}dP)+\frac{1}{2}Q=\frac{1}{2}Q^{\prime}+\frac{1}{4}(dP+\ast dP),

(103)

and

F=\frac{1}{2}Q=\frac{1}{2}Q^{\prime}-\frac{1}{4}(dP+\ast dP).

(104)

Then
F=dA
,
G=dC
with

A=\frac{1}{2}(S-P),\quad C=\frac{1}{2}(S+P).

(105)

Again, it is
remarkable that this system can be coupled to two different gravitons.
We introduce the energy-momentum tensors
\Theta_{\mu\nu}(F)
(
128
) and
\bar{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}(G)
(
146
) and add the Noether coupling to two gravitons

\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu},h_{\mu\nu}

S_{lin}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{d}x\,[\tilde{h}^{\mu\nu}\bar{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}(G)+h^{\mu\nu}\Theta_{\mu\nu}(F)],

(106)

generalising (
83
). This has a non-linear completion to a theory with two diffeomorphism-like symmetries, corresponding to the two gauge fields

\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu},h_{\mu\nu}
.
The action of
Hull:2023dgp
for coupling to two metrics
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
,
g_{\mu\nu}
can be expanded around flat space with
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
,
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
, and to linear order in
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu},h_{\mu\nu}
this corresponds to adding the coupling (
106
).

If the self-duality constraint on
Q^{\prime}
were dropped, the action
\int Q^{\prime}\wedge dP
would be a BF theory, which is a topological field theory in the sense that it is independent of the metric and gives two flat gauge fields. Note also that
\int dP\wedge*dP
is a conformally invariant as
P
is a
(d/2-1)
-form in
d
dimensions,
and the self-duality condition
Q^{\prime}=\ast Q^{\prime}
is conformally invariant for such forms, so that this theory defines a CFT. In particular the total energy momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}=\Theta_{\mu\nu}+\bar{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}

is traceless. Thus the theory of
A,C
is also a CFT, However, with the self-duality constraint on
Q^{\prime}
, the theory is of two self-dual gauge fields and is an interesting non-trivial conformal field theory in
d=4k+2
dimensions that can be viewed as a higher-dimensional analogue of the holomorphic
\beta\gamma
system in two dimensions. The bosonisation relates these two CFT’s.

The self-dual
(q-1)
-form gauge fields
S,C
naturally couple to
(q-1)
-form currents
j_{S},j_{C}
and to
q-2
-branes which are referred to as self-dual as they have equal electric and magnetic charges, The currents

can be thought of as the current densities of the corresponding branes.
It is not immediately obvious how these sources should couple to the

system. This was investigated in
Hull:2025yww
, where it was argued that it was necessary to introduce a secondary
(q-1)
-form current
J_{S}
with

d^{\dagger}J_{S}=j_{S},

(107)

so that the coupling of
S
to

could be rewritten as the coupling of
Q^{\prime}
to

:

\int S\wedge\ast j_{S}=-\int Q^{\prime}\wedge\ast J_{S}.

(108)

For example, if
j_{S}
is localised on some
(q-1)
-dimensional surface
{\mathcal{S}}
, then
J_{S}
is localised on a
q
-dimensional surface
{\mathcal{U}}
whose boundary is
{\mathcal{S}}
, or includes
{\mathcal{S}}
.
The
q
-dimensional surface
{\mathcal{U}}
ending on the
(q-1)
-dimensional surface
{\mathcal{S}}
can be thought of as the world-volume
{\mathcal{U}}
for a Dirac
(q-1)
-brane ending on the

\mathcal{S}
of a
(q-2)
-brane; see
Hull:2025yww

In the

theory, the natural local operators to consider are the generalisations of Wilson lines of the form

e^{i\int_{\mathcal{S}}A},\qquad e^{i\int_{\mathcal{S}}C},

(109)

for some
(q-1)
-dimensional surface
{\mathcal{S}}
. The translation of these to the

theory follows from the above discussion.
The bosonisation relates these to the following operators in the

e^{i\int_{\mathcal{S}}P},\qquad e^{i\int_{\mathcal{U}}Q^{\prime}},

where the boundary of

A Democratic Action

A free self-dual gauge field is formulated in terms of a
d/2
-form field strength
F
satisfying
F=\ast F
and
dF=0
.
In any dimension
d
, a theory of a
(q-1)
-form gauge field with a
q
form field strength
F_{q}=dA
has a dual formulation with dual
(d-q)
-form field strength
\tilde{F}_{d-q}=d\tilde{A}
. The democratic formulation uses both field strengths
F_{q},\tilde{F}_{d-q}
satisfying the equations

F_{q}=\ast\tilde{F}_{d-q},\qquad dF_{q}=0,\qquad d\tilde{F}_{d-q}=0.

(111)

Such formulations extend to certain interacting theories and give a duality covariant formulation of supergravities; see e.g.
Cremmer:1997ct
. They give duality covariant equations of motion, but the construction of an action
runs into similar problems to those that arise for actions of self-dual gauge fields. For example, one can take the sum of the actions

\frac{1}{2}\int F\wedge\ast F
and
\frac{1}{2}\int\tilde{F}\wedge\ast\tilde{F}

for
F_{q}=dA
and
\tilde{F}_{d-q}=d\tilde{A}
but then the equations of motion need to be supplemented by the constraint
F_{q}=\ast\tilde{F}_{d-q}
.

The Sen action and the action considered in this paper extend to this case with
Q
and
P
being taken to be sums of forms of different degree, giving the required equations (
111
), but at the expense of adding a shadow sector.
Mamade and Zwiebach
Mamade:2025vcp
have shown that the formulation of RR gauge fields in IIB supergravity that arises from superstring field theory are of precisely this type with
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{3}+Q_{5}+Q_{7}+Q_{9}
a sum of forms of odd degree and
P=P_{0}+P_{2}+P_{4}+P_{6}+P_{8}

a sum of forms of even degree. In particular, this confirms that the Sen action for the self-dual 4-form gauge field arises in superstring field theory through the Sen action.
For the IIA theory,
Q
is a sum of forms of even degree and
P
is a sum of forms of odd degree.

To see how this works, consider the action in
d
dimensions for a pair of forms

S=\int\left(-\frac{1}{2}dP\wedge\ast dP+Q\wedge dP\right)=\int Q^{\prime}\wedge dP,

(112)

where

Q^{\prime}=Q+\frac{1}{2}(dP+\ast dP),

(113)

with

Q=Q_{q}+Q_{d-q},\qquad P=P_{q-1}+P_{d-q-1},

(114)

so that
Q^{\prime}=Q^{\prime}_{q}+Q^{\prime}_{d-q}
is a sum of a
q
-form and a
(d-q)
-form.
Then the action is

S=\int Q^{\prime}\wedge dP=\int[Q^{\prime}_{q}\wedge dP_{d-q-1}+Q^{\prime}_{d-q}\wedge dP_{d-q-1}].

(115)

Suppose we demand that
Q
satisfy the duality relation

Q_{d-q}=\ast Q_{q}

(116)

for
q<d/2
.
If
d=4k+2
, we can include self-duality for
Q_{d/2}
, while in
d=4k
we can include two
d/2
forms,
Q_{d/2},\tilde{Q}_{d/2}
with
\tilde{Q}_{d/2}=\ast Q_{d/2}
.
In dimensions in which
\ast^{2}=1
when acting on
q
-forms, this is the condition that
Q^{\prime}
is self-dual,
Q^{\prime}=\ast Q^{\prime}
as
\ast Q_{d-q}=Q_{q}
.
The field equations for
P_{q-1},P_{d-q-1}

are

dQ^{\prime}_{q}=0,\qquad dQ^{\prime}_{d-q}=0,

(117)

while the field equation for
Q^{\prime}_{q}
is

dP_{q-1}=\ast dP_{d-q-1}.

(118)

Then this gives a democratic action for two fields, one a gauge field with field strength
Q_{q}

satisfying the field equations

dQ^{\prime}_{q}=0,\qquad d^{\dagger}Q^{\prime}_{q}=0,

(119)

and another with potential
P_{q-1}
satisfying the field equation

d^{\dagger}dP_{q-1}=0.

(120)

This democratic formulation will be discussed further elsewhere.

10
Conclusion

In this paper we have explored the CFT that arises from the Sen formulation
Sen:2015nph
;
Sen:2019qit
, generalised to include two metrics
Hull:2023dgp
, in the case that the two metrics agree. This has the simple action

\displaystyle S=\int Q^{\prime}\wedge dP

(121)

where
Q^{\prime}=\star Q^{\prime}
is a self-dual form in
d=4k+2
dimensions. In two dimensions this is the well-known holomorphic
\beta\gamma
system (where
\beta
and
\gamma
have scaling dimensions 1 and 0 respectively and
c=2
). Without the self-duality constraint, we would have a topological
BF
theory, but by introducing self-duality we introduce a dependence on the metric while maintaining conformal invariance. We also argued that this theory has a natural bosonisation in terms of two self-dual fields but with opposite contributions to the energy momentum tensor and so is non-unitary. We also discussed how vertex operators in the bosonised theory can be mapped to line operators in the original
Q^{\prime}P
system. Furthermore we showed that this system can be coupled to two non-trivial metrics at the linearised level. Remarkably this bi-metric theory can then be resummed into a full non-linear theory with two metrics; namely the action constructed in
Hull:2023dgp
. Lastly, we introduced a generalization to an action involving multi-forms which leads to a duality-symmetric democratic action.

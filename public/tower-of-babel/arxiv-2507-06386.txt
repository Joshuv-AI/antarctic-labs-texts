Subcycle phase matching effects in short attosecond pulse trains
N Ouahioune, R Martín-Hernández, D Hoff, PK Maroju, C Guo, R Weissenbilder, S Mikaelsson, A L’Huillier, CL Arnold, M Gisselbrecht

Abstract
High-order harmonic generation (HHG) in gases driven by intense laser fields has become a cornerstone technique for producing attosecond pulses and probing ultrafast electronic motion in matter. By manipulating the microscopic and macroscopic dynamics involved in HHG, it is possible to tailor the temporal and spectral properties of attosecond light pulses. In this work, we control the carrier-to-envelope phase (CEP) of ultrashort femtosecond laser pulses to generate short trains of attosecond pulses that we characterize using two-color laser-assisted photoionization. We observe strong and unexpected variations of the photoelectron spectra as a function of the photoelectron kinetic energy as we change the CEP of the driving pulses. To interpret our results, we carried out HHG simulations that include microscopic and macroscopic effects. We find that the time-dependent phase-matching of the harmonics and the temporal chirp of the attosecond pulses play a major role in our observation, opening new perspectives for temporally resolving and controlling the HHG process.



1
Introduction

High-order Harmonic Generation of an intense laser in gas targets has enabled the production of short pulses of light at the attosecond time scale in the extreme-ultraviolet (XUV) range
[
1
]
. This process arises from two coexisting physical phenomena: the highly non-linear interaction of the intense laser with an isolated atom (or molecule) - a microscopic phenomenon known as single-atom response
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, and the in-phase propagation of the generated XUV radiation and the driving laser field - a macroscopic phenomenon known as phase matching
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. The latter is central in determining the efficiency, coherence and spatiotemporal structure of the XUV emission. Consequently, considerable effort has been devoted to manipulating phase-matching conditions to control the properties of attosecond light sources (flux, conversion efficiency, etc…
[
8
,
9
]
) including the generation of single attosecond pulses. However, insight into the ultrafast temporal evolution of phase-matching has so far remained primarily theoretical
[
10
,
11
]
. Here, we identify experimental conditions that provide access to the temporal build-up of the phase-matching in each optical cycle using a few-cycle driver laser.

Laser-assisted photoionization schemes such as Streaking
[
12
,
13
]
and RABBIT (Reconstruction of Attosecond Beatings by Interference of Two-photon transitions)
[
14
]
are commonly used to characterize the temporal properties of isolated attosecond pulses and attosecond pulse trains (APTs). In RABBIT, an interferometric technique, multiple coherent broadband electron wavepackets created by the XUV harmonic comb interact with an near-infrared (NIR) probe field. The interaction results in additional peaks called sidebands between each pair of consecutive harmonics formed due to the absorption/emission of additional NIR photons. By scanning the delay between the XUV and the NIR, it is possible to extract the spectral (and thus temporal) properties of the XUV and probe fields
[
15
]
.

In this work, we study laser-assisted ionization of helium atoms using an attosecond pulse train generated by few-cycle laser pulses in a gas of argon atoms. Due to the short laser duration (
\leq
6 fs), the APTs contain a few attosecond pulses
[
16
]
and their number depends on the CEP of the driving laser. For certain CEP values, the photoelectron spectra exhibit features that cannot be explained by the structure of the APTs predicted by the single-atom response alone. Supported by complete 3D simulation and a 1D-model, we gain insights into the time-dependent build-up of HHG in the nonlinear medium, and show that laser-assisted photoionization spectra provide a fingerprint of the generated APTs in the time and frequency domain.

This study shows that the photoelectron spectra in laser-assisted photoionization with short pulses strongly depends on the composition of the APTs, which is influenced by phase-matching and the temporal chirp of the emitted radiation. To verify the conclusion from the above analysis (Figure

4
Conclusion

In conclusion, laser-assisted photoionization spectra exhibit pronounced energy-dependent features originating from the interference of the EWPs in the time domain, which can be controlled by the CEP of the driving field. We found that the composition of the APTs is influenced by: the time-dependent phase matching of the harmonics and by the temporal properties (femtosecond chirp). The former alters the spectral amplitude of the harmonics at each laser half-cycle, thereby affecting in a non-trivial way the number of dominant attosecond pulses at different energies. The latter changes the separation between adjacent attosecond pulses at high energies, resulting in the distortion of the RABBIT-like patterns. Our study provides to our knowledge the first experimental insight into sub-cycle phase-matching for a short laser pulse and a small number of attosecond pulses. This enables a comprehensive understanding of laser-assisted photoionization spectra and opens new routes for temporally controlling the HHG process.

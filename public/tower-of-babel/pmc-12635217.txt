A novel self excited hybrid brushless wound rotor synchronous machine with consequent poles
Authors: Muhammad Ahsan ul Haq, Haroon Farooq, Rehan Liaqat, Zafar A Khan, Abdullah Altamimi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis manuscript presents a novel consequent pole hybrid brushless wound rotor synchronous machine (CPH-BLWRSM) topology. Compared to existing BLWRSM, the proposed topology mainly exploits the inherent subharmonic component (SHC) field excitation to attain brushless operation with limited usage of rare permanent magnet materials and assists in achieving improved torque attributes. The proposed CPH-BLWRSM topology is validated on a 12-slot, 8-pole machine with a 3-phase single-layer armature winding. The rotor of the proposed machine contains 4 poles of harmonic winding (HW), 8 poles of field winding, and permanent magnets on alternate poles. The armature winding on the stator is supplied by a single 3-phase inverter to generate stator MMF, which comprises two dominant components: (1) the fundamental component with supply frequency and (2) the subharmonic component (SHC) with half of the supply frequency. The fundamental component of the stator MMF produces the main stator field, while the SHC induces AC voltages in the HW. A rotating rectifier mounted between the HW and the field winding converts this AC to direct current (DC), and thus the field winding on the rotor receives DC from the rectifier without brushes. The JMAG-designer software is employed to perform 2-dimensional finite element analysis of 12-slot and 8-pole CPH-BLWRSM. The proposed machine exhibits high starting, maximum, and average torques as compared to existing BLWRSM with less input stator current magnitude, making it suitable for loads that require high starting torque.Keywords: Brushless operation, Consequent poles, Finite element analysis, Sub-harmonic component, Wound rotor synchronous machineSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Electrical machines play a key role in our daily lives, ranging from home appliances like washing machines1, fans2, vacuum cleaners3, etc., to industrial applications like milling machines4, elevators5, etc. AC machines include two major classes: (i) induction machines and (ii) synchronous machines6. Induction machines are extensively used in industry as motors and are also referred to as the workhorse of the industry. Synchronous machines, on the other hand, are used for constant-speed applications. Nearly all types of power plants, such as wind power plants7, hydropower plants8, and thermal power plants9, use synchronous machines to deliver the electrical power needed for both commercial and residential loads.

Many topologies have been presented in the literature to design synchronous machines. Some topologies termed as “permanent magnet synchronous machines (PMSM)” use permanent magnets (PMs) to generate rotor flux, while the others known as conventional WRSMs (C-WRSMs) rely on field winding (FW) and an external DC excitation system with carbon brushes and slip rings for rotor flux generation6. Though PMSMs need no carbon brushes, slip rings, field winding, and external DC sources, these topologies go through price and supply instabilities in material acquisition and face environmental challenges due to the mining and extraction of rare-earth materials. No doubt, C-WRSM is one of the most prevalent for the electric vehicles industry10 due to its high power density11–13 and improved flux control owing to the availability of an external direct current (DC) source14,15. However, C-WRSM faces maintenance and sparking issues due to carbon brushes and slip rings limiting their adoption for high-power applications. Moreover, high field currents result in more power losses, which ultimately drop efficiency.

To solve the maintenance issues occurring in C-WRSMs due to carbon brushes and slip rings, various brushless WRSM (BLWRSM) topologies have been proposed in the literature. Some popular topologies are presented in Fig. 1. One class of topologies employs exciters and pilot exciters in large power rating machines. Though brushless operation is achieved using exciters, integration of additional devices increases the volume and manufacturing price of the motors16,17. The mentioned issues have motivated the scientists and researchers to look for topologies that either rely less or do not rely on PMs for brushless operation11,18,19. Currently, extensive research is being carried out globally to solve the excitation issues of C-WRSM by using cost-effective, simple, and more convenient methods for excitation purposes20–25.

Popular topologies of BLWRSMs.

Authors of20 have suggested that harmonic current induction-based excitation could be a suitable way for achieving brushless operation in WRSM. The brushless excitation in this topology is achieved by using harmonic winding (HW), a rectifier, and FW mounted on the rotor of WRSM.

In21, the stator windings (SWs) present on the stator produce magnetomotive force (MMF), consisting of both harmonic component and fundamental component (FC) in the air gap. The harmonic component of MMF generated by the stator winding (SW) generates AC voltages in the rotor HW. A rectifier mounted on the rotor shaft converts the AC into DC. This DC is supplied to the exciter winding to develop the prime rotor field. The interaction between the rotor field and the stator field enables the WRSM to generate torque, and the rotor rotates accordingly.

The harmonic current induction-based excitation of the machine is a convenient way for BL excitation in WRSM and is normally classified into two categories22. The first method is based on the generation of stator MMF (sMMF) with the assistance of space harmonics. For this method, the sMMF is initially developed and then used for the brushless operation of WRSM by specific SW. In the second method, a time-harmonic-based method has been employed to generate the sMMF. For instance, a third harmonic magnetomotive force is generated and employed for the working of BLWRSM.

A 48-pole and 36-slot BLWRSM containing concentrated winding on the stator is presented in26. Subharmonic component (SHC) of sMMF produces AC voltages in HW while the fundamental component (FC) of sMMF interacts with the rotor field to produce torque. To obtain the brushless functioning of the machine, an excitation winding is also available on the rotor. A rectifier is embedded between the rotor FW and the excitation winding.

Sirewal et. al.27 have presented a single inverter-based BLWRSM topology in which SHC is produced along the FC of the sMMF. Their proposed topology contains two HWs: one on the stator side and the second on the rotor side. The 6 switches are used on the stator side to produce the SHC responsible for injecting AC voltage into the rotor HW. After rectification, DC is supplied to the FW of the machine without brushes.

In28, a brushless self-excitation scheme for WRSM is presented. It uses a stator with five or more phases to produce a pair of rotating magnetic fields at distinct harmonics. This enables magnetic coupling with several rotor windings. The rotor of this machine consists of FW, transformer windings, and a diode-based rectifier to change the induced AC in the transformer windings into DC for the FW.

In29, two HWs are available in the rotor of the machine. A capacitor placed in series with two HWs makes two HW currents in phase with each other, resulting in a higher magnitude of current supplied to the rectifier. The larger output of the rectifier is supplied to FW, which generates high torque with the same input current. However, the presence of two HWs results in more copper losses in HWs.

In25, two SWs are present on the stator to obtain brushless operation. One SW is called the main SW, while the second one is termed as an additional SW. Two inverters supply these SWs as shown in Fig. 225. Inverter 1 provides FC to the main SW, and inverter 2 supplies the third harmonic to the additional SW. Since this topology uses two inverters and two stator SWs, the overall cost of the machine increases. Furthermore, copper losses in two SWs and power losses in two inverters decrease the efficiency of the machine.

Two stator windings and two inverter arrangement for brushless operation.

Authors of30 presented a BLWRSM based on the inherent SHC in sMMF. Though the proposed topology relies on a single inverter for BL functioning, the starting torque is nearly zero, making it unsuitable for loads requiring high starting torque. Some other CPH-BLWRSM topologies have also been presented in literature, but require dual inverters and dual SWs for their operation31. Some topologies rely on additional controllers to generate THC for brushless operation. Authors of32 have used hysterisis-based controller for this purpose.

From the literature review, it is evident that the existing topologies mainly rely on dual SWs, dual inverters, or special SW arrangements to generate harmonics other than the fundamental to achieve BL functioning. All these techniques result in a higher cost of the machine. Some machines, though, rely on a single inverter, such as30, but exhibit nearly zero starting torque. So, there is a need to propose a BL topology that relies on less PM, uses a single inverter, and provides high starting, maximum, and average torques with less input current as compared to existing BLWRSMs.

To resolve the issues mentioned, this paper introduces a consequent-pole hybrid BLWRSM (CPH-BLWRSM) that utilizes the inherent SHC of sMMF for the BL operation of the machine. In the proposed machine, small PMs are also present on alternate rotor poles. This design provides higher torques in every aspect (starting, maximum, and average torques). Owing to a smaller volume of PMs on alternate rotor poles, the proposed model is cheaper as compared to the existing BLWRSM. Both machines (existing BLWRSM and proposed CPH-BLWRSM) are analyzed using two-dimensional finite element analysis (FEA) as applicable, and a comparative analysis is conducted between the performance of the proposed CPH-BLWRSM and the existing BLWRSM.

The rest of the article is organized as follows: Section II presents the proposed design and operating principle of the proposed BL topology. Section III gives a performance evaluation of the proposed machine using 2-D finite element analysis (FEA) and compares it with the existing BLWRSM presented in30, and Section IV concludes the paper.

Proposed brushless topology, machine configuration, and operation principle

A.Proposed brushless topology.

Proposed brushless topology.

The proposed CPH-BLWRSM topology uses a 12-slot, 8-pole machine with a 3-phase single-layer armature winding for its validation. The rotor of the proposed machine contains 4 poles of HW, 8 poles of FW, and PMs on alternate poles. These poles are shown in Fig. 3. Due to PMs present on alternative poles, the proposed topology is named CPH-BLWRSM. The word hybrid suggests that poles due to FW and small amounts of PMs are available in combination to generate rotor flux for torque generation.

(a) Stator of the proposed CPH-BLWRSM (similar to existing BLWRSM) (b). Rotor of the existing BLWRSM. (c) Rotor of proposed CPH-BLWRSM.

The 3-phase SW is delivered with a balanced 3-phase current at 60 Hz. The SW arrangement generates an FC of 60 Hz and sub-harmonics with different frequencies within the machine’s air gap. The SHC of 30 Hz induces a voltage in the 4-pole HW. This induced voltage in HW is rectified to provide unidirectional DC to excite 8 poles of the rotor FW. The FC of the sMMF is producing the stator’s main field that is rotating at synchronous speed. The interaction between the main rotor and main stator magnetic fields induces torque in the rotor. C. Operating Principle.

Operating Principle.

The inherent SHC of the sMMF is utilized to provide DC power to the rotor FW. A single 3-phase inverter or conventional 3-phase AC source is used to supply 3-phase AC to SWs. The SHC of the sMMF injects AC in HW. The starting torque is very low in the existing BLWRSM30, which is due to the small AC voltages in the HW. However, the proposed CPH-BLWRSM provides sufficient starting torque. At the start, the proposed machine is operating as PMSM due to the presence of small PMs on alternate rotor poles, as no voltage is induced in the HW at the start. Therefore, at starting, torque is produced in the rotor due to the main stator field and PMs. When the speed of the motor increases, AC voltages are produced in HW. These AC voltages are injected into a rectifier. The DC output of the rectifier is then supplied to the rotor FW, and finally, the proposed machine is operating as a CPH-BLWRSM. Analytical performance analysis by 2-D finite element analysis

Performance analysis of the existing BLWRSM30 and the proposed CPH-BLWRSM was carried out in JMAG software. Simulations were performed for 1s for both machines using different values of stator current in the loaded condition.

A.No-load analysis of proposed CPH-BLWRSM

No-load analysis of proposed CPH-BLWRSM

No-load analysis of both existing BLWRSM30 and the proposed CPH-BLWRSM was performed to observe back emf and no-load torque in both machines. 1A DC was given to the FW of both machines. Both machines were operating at 900 RPM. Figure 8 illustrates the back EMF of both machines. Back emfs of the proposed machine contain more harmonics due to a more uneven flux distribution. The RMS voltages of the back emf in three phases of the proposed machine are 38.0133 V (Phase A), 38.0662 V (Phase B), and 38.0885 V (Phase C). These values are nearly equal, indicating that the back EMF across all three phases is approximately balanced and the phase difference is 1200. The -0.0004 Nm is the no-load torque of the existing BLWRSM30, while the proposed CPH-BLWRSM has a 0.0024 Nm no-load torque. Ideally, cogging torque should remain lower than the rated torque of the machine to ensure smooth operation33. Uneven magnetization leads to flux harmonics that increase cogging and reduce back emf32. In our proposed machine, the presence of PMs on alternate rotor poles leads to uneven flux distribution. At no load condition, the rms values of flux linkages of the existing and proposed machines are 0.149 wb and 0.11 wb, respectively. As the net-flux linkage with the stator winding is reduced at no-load condition in the proposed machine, the back-emf is reduced, and the average cogging torque is slightly increased in the proposed machine with respect to the existing BLWRSM with no consequent poles. The no-load torques of the two machines are represented in Fig. 9. Table 2 presents the results of back EMF and cogging torque at no-load for both machines. The peak value (9 Nm) of the cogging torque of the proposed machine is higher than the peak value of the cogging torque in the exiting BLWRSM30. The higher cogging torque in the proposed CPH-BLWRSM is due to PMs on alternative rotor poles32. The PMs on consequent poles originate uneven magnetization and flux harmonics in the machine which contribute to increased cogging torque32.

The torques produced in the proposed CPH-BLWRSM and the existing BLWRSM30 are compared with each other to verify the effectiveness of the proposed machine. For a fair comparison, both machines use identical design parameters, such as air gap length, rotor and stator inner and outer dimensions, core material, HW turns, SW turns per phase, FW poles, FW turns, winding material, HW poles, etc. However, the 4 PMs are used on alternate rotor poles in the proposed CPH-BLWRSM, whereas the existing BLWRSM30 does not use any PM.

Figure 17a, b. show the torques existing BLWRSM30 and the proposed CPH-BLWRSM, respectively. The 8.39 Nm is the mean torque produced in the existing BLWRSM30, and the average torque induced in the proposed CPH-BLWRSM is 8.5868 Nm. The torque induced in the proposed CPH-BLWRSM is 2.34% greater than the mean torque produced in the existing BLWRSM30 while utilizing 26.6% less input current. The -0.000067 Nm was the starting torque of the present BLWRSM, but the starting torque of the proposed CPH-BLWRSM is 5.42 Nm. The 8.56 Nm is the maximum torque in the existing BLWRSM30, while the proposed CPH-BLWRSM exhibits a maximum torque of 9.4 Nm. However, the peak torque under loaded conditions is higher than the peak cogging torque, which implies that the influence of cogging torque on overall machine performance—particularly under normal operating conditions—is minimal. Conclusion

In this manuscript, a CPH-BLWRSM is presented for the improvement in the average and starting torques of the existing BL machine using less PM material. The rotor of the proposed machine is a hybrid containing FW, HW, and PMs. In the proposed study, 4 PMs were mounted on the rotor’s alternating poles. The 13.49 cm3 size of PMs is used in the proposed machine. The inherent SHC of the sMMF is used to induce AC in the HW of the proposed machine. A rectifier is embedded between FW and HW, which converts the AC of HW to DC. After rectification, DC power is given to the rotor FW.

The PMs provide a constant rotor magnetic field. A 2-D FEA of the proposed CPH-BLWRSM is performed on JMAG to validate its performance. The proposed CPH-BLWRSM is compared with the recently published BLWRSM. Due to the presence of PMs on consequent rotor poles in the proposed machine, it exhibits higher starting and average torques as compared to the recent BLWRSM, with 26.66% less input stator current. Due to better torque attributes, the proposed topology is a potential candidate for applications such as electric vehicles, etc., that demand high starting torque.

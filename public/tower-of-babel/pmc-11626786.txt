Role of stimulated by retinoic acid 6 in 46 children of coarctation of the aorta
Authors: Xin Jin, Zhengxia Pan, Zhenjiang Zhao, Da Ouyang, Jinjie Qin, Jie Tian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
(1) ObjectiveCoarctation of the aorta (CoA) is a complex congenital heart disease. Research on differential genes in patients with CoA and other groups of aortas and investigating the pathogenesis of aorta coarctation is essential for prevention and diagnosis. (2) Methods: This study was conducted between January 2019 and December 2021. The first step was the analysis of differential genes in four groups of aortic tissues. Group A: 46 cases, coarctation specimen of the CoA group; Group B: 46 cases, anastomotic margin specimen of the CoA group; Group C: 22 cases, aortic specimen of the aortic stenosis group; and Group D: 6 cases, aorta of the necropsy group. Genomic and proteomic differences were compared in aortic specimens from different patient groups in Southwestern China. The second step the CRISPR technology was used to knock out the differential genes in mice. The phenotypes were verified using ultrasound after obtaining homozygous mice. (3) Results: Quantitative polymerase chain reaction indicated that BMP4, Smad4, STRA6, CRABP I, and COX-2 genes were statistically downregulated in group A than in other groups. STRA6 expression was 0.33 ± 0.66 in group A and 1.03 ± 0.20 in group D. The difference between the two groups was statistically significant. Western blotting revealed that BMP4, Smad4, STRA6, and CRABP I had a low expression in group A at the proteomic level. STRA6 protein was 0.13 ± 0.02 in group A and 0.48 ± 0.07 in group D. Group A decreased by 73.7 %, a statistically significant difference. Double-labeled immunofluorescence demonstrated co-expression of BMP4 and STRA6 in the tunica adventitia vasorum of group A. However, BMP4 expression in the tunica adventitia vasorum and endangium of the constricted aorta showed a double-loop sign. Primary filial generation offspring were generated using CRISPR knockout of the STRA6 gene. The first filial generation was mated with the wild-type mice to produce the second filial generation. 72.7 % homozygous mice in the fourth week of the second filial generation were detected using ultrasound, indicating a reduction in aortic diameter. (4) Conclusions: BMP4, STRA6, and COX-2 genes may be associated with CoA in Southwestern Chinese patients. STRA6 knockout, a gene that initiates the retinoic acid pathway, may lead to CoA.

Introduction

Congenital heart defects (CHD) are the most common birth defects, accounting for 40.95 % of all defects [1,2]. Coarctation of the Aorta (CoA) is a complex congenital heart disease [3]. The annual incidence of CHD in newborns in China ranged from 8 % to 10 % between 1990 and 2019 [4]. About 150,000 children with CHD are born annually in China [5,6]. Our cardiac surgery and cardiology department operate on 1approximately 2500 patients with CHD annually. Each year, about 100 children with CoA are operated on at our Children's Medical Center [7].

Southwestern Chinese have a high incidence of congenital cardiovascular diseases. Settlers have a low level of economic development [8], high altitude terrain [6], and high prevalence of maternal Vitamin A deficiency [5,9]. Research on the complex CHD pathogenesis in China will help understand cardiovascular development and reduce neonatal mortality. We might better understand the role of RA pathway in the cause of aortic arch dysplasia by elucidating the effect of STRA6 on BMP4 through RA pathway, which might contribute to promising strategy in prevention, clinical diagnosis and treatment of CoA.

Western Blot (WB)

Total proteins were extracted from the specimens using RIPA Lysis Buffer at 4 °C for 30 min (P0013, Beyotime, CHN). Specimen samples were homogenized using Teflon. The lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C and the supernatant was collected. Western blotting was performed according to the concentration of proteins determined by BCA Protein Assay Kit (P0012, Beyotime, CHN). Proteins were separated by SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (5 % spacer gel, 90 V, 20 min; 8–12 % separating gel, 120V, 70 min). Proteins were electrophoretically transferred (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc.) to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Millipore Corp., Billerica, MA, USA). After the non-specific antibodies on the membrane had been blocked, the membranes were then incubated with the primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C.Primary antibodies used in this study included anti-BMP4 (1:1000, Novus, NBP2-52424), anti-STRA6 (1:800, Proteintech, 22001-1-AP), anti-Smad4 (1:1000, Novus, NBP2-24951), anti-CRABPⅠ (1:1000, Novus, NBP3-16601), anti-β-actin (1:3000, Novus, NB600-501). Secondary antibodies were rabbit Anti-mouse IgG (1:5000, 550010, Zenbio) and Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (1:3000, 550060, Zenbio). After visualisation by chemiluminescence enhancement. Proteins expression was quantified using the Chemidoc XRS gel imaging system (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc.)

Immunohistochemical/immunofluorescence

Immunohistochemistry was performed on dewaxed and rehydrated sections. Slides were pretreated with citrate buffer (pH 6.0) in a microwave oven for antigen retrieval. Then, slides were incubated with Phosphate Buffer Saline (PBS) containing 3 % hydrogen peroxide and BSA for 30 min, followed by primary antibody incubation at 4 °C overnight. Finally, slides were probed with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (Universal SAP Kit Mouse/Rabbit Universal, SAP-9100, ZSGB-BIO) for 1 h at 37 °C. Detection was accomplished using Nitro Blue Tetrazolium (NBT). The primary antibodies used in this study included anti-BMP4 (1:1200, Novus, NBP2-52424), anti-STRA6 (1:800, Proteintech, 22001-1-AP), anti-Smad4 (1:1500, Novus, NBP2-24951), anti-CRABP I (1:1200, Novus, NBP3-16601), anti-BMP7 (1:1500, Novus, NBP2-52425), anti-SOX10 (MAB-0699, MXB), anti-Neurofilament (MAB-0136, MXB), and anti- Calretinin (MAB-0716, MXB).

The two primary antibodies were mixed in a 1:1 ratio and added dropwise to the specimen sections for double-labeled immunofluorescence. The sections were incubated in a black wet box at 4 °C overnight. Secondary anti-BMP4 fluorescence was shown in red (Cy3-conjugated AffiniPure Goat Anti-Rat IgG (H + L), GB21302, Servicebio). Secondary anti-STRA6 fluorescence is shown in green (FITC-conjugated Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (H + L), GB22301, Servicebio). The nuclei were stained with 4′,6-Diamidino-2-Phenylindole (DAPI), and autofluorescence was performed. DAPI excites blue light at 330–380 nm and emits at 420 nm, FITC excites green light at 492 nm and emits at 520 nm, and CY3 excites red light at 550 nm and emits at 570 nm. Immunofluorescence imaging and section photography were performed using an Olympus SLIDEVIEW VS200.

CRISPR and ultrasound cardiogram

Mouse C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Sayer Biological Co. Knockout F1 offspring were housed in the SPF animal house of Sayer Biological Co. Two female and two male F2 offspring were transferred to the specific pathogen free (SPF) grade laboratory animal center at Chongqing Medical University Children's Hospital for rearing. STRA6 gene sequence analysis and knockout target design synthesis were obtained from NCBI (Gene ID:20897). Four sgRNAs were designed in the anterior and posterior regions of the functional domain of exon 3 of STRA6 gene. gRNA1 (reverse strand): AACTTCCCTACCCAATCGTGTGGG; gRNA2 (forward strand): TGCAAGGGACTGTTGGGCGGGGTGG; gRNA3 (reverse strand): CTCTTGCTGGGAGCGCCTAGTGG; and gRNA4 (forward strand): GAGTTCTGCAGATGTCGGGGCTGGG. Plasmids and single-stranded sgRNA were synthesized into double-stranded using E. coli. In this experiment, fertilized eggs were microinjected, and F1 and F2 generation mice were gene-sequenced at Sayer Biological Co. Briefly, 20 ng/μL sgRNA was mixed with 100 ng/μL Cas9 mRNA. The mixture was injected into the cytoplasm of fertile mouse eggs using a microinjection device. After 2 h of in vitro incubation, surviving fertilized eggs were transplanted into the uterus of surrogate female mice under a somatic microscope. Pregnant mice were individually isolated to observe changes during pregnancy after successful transplantation. DNA was identified using clipped mouse tails in three-week-old F1 generation mice. F1 generation mice were crossed with wild-type mice to produce F2 generation heterozygotes. The F2 progeny of mice were interbred to create F3 purebred descendants.

The mice were divided into three groups: Group A F3 generation homozygous mice, Group B F3 generation heterozygous mice, and Group C wild-type C57 mice. The mice were placed on a heated anesthesia machine. Continuously inhaled isoflurane was used to anesthetize the mice. Mice were examined using a specialist sonographer (ultrasound machine, Vevo3100 LT, Fujifilm). The aortic arch sections were photographed and saved. The following parameters were measured: Ejection Fraction (EF), stroke volume (SV), minimum aortic arch diameter (Dmin), aortic opening diameter (AO), aortic arch flow velocity difference (ΔV), and aortic arch pressure difference (ΔP).

Statistical analysis

Measurement data, such as age, weight, relative PCR values, relative WB expression, and ultrasound data, were recorded using (Mean, SD). The normal distribution of data was checked using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Immunohistochemical expression was ranked data. An F-test was conducted for multi-group measures. Chi-square was used to compare categorical variables. The F-test was used to check the homoscedasticity of the continuous variables. One-way ANOVA was used to determine differences. Differences between groups were tested using Student's t-tests. The Bonferroni correction was applied to the results. The X2 test was used to count the data. P < 0.05 was considered statistically different.

Discussion

Congenital heart disease is a common birth defect and a major public health problem. European countries have 3%–9% Population incidence of CHD over the last 20 years [8,10]. CoA is an important type of CTD and a common and complex group of congenital diseases. CoA accounts for approximately 5%–8% of CHD, with an incidence of four cases per 10,000 live births [11]. A total of 264 CoA surgeries were performed at our hospital in the three years from 2019 to 2021, representing 5.1 % of all CHD surgeries. The Southwest China Plateau region is an important area for CHD research worldwide. There are two possible reasons for the high prevalence of congenital heart disease in this region from an objective viewpoint. First, the southwest region has an average altitude of 1100 m. Southwest China includes the Tibetan Plateau, which has an average elevation of 4000 m, the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau, and the Sichuan Basin. CHD is 20 times higher above 2500 m than below in a study of 1377 children with CHD in Tibet [6]. Second, nutritional deficiency during pregnancy is an objective phenomenon in Southwestern China. A study in Western China found 35.51 % relative vitamin A deficiency in children [9,12]. As the largest pediatric medical center in Western China, our research into the mechanisms of the development of complex CHD has impacted the prevention and treatment of this disease.

Retinoic acid (RA) is a vitamin A metabolite that significantly differentiates the second heart field. RA deficiency may cause CTD. STRA6 is a cell membrane receptor that translocates exogenous RA into cells via the RA signaling pathway. Mutant mice deficient in RA exhibit the CTD phenotypes of DiGeorge syndrome [13,14]. RA signaling affects the number of NCFCs and the cardiac septum as CTD. Abnormal development between the different cell types of the CTD may disrupt the CTD formation and lead to abnormal development of the aortic arch. There is a theoretical correlation between RA deficiency and the occurrence of CoA [15,16]. Cardiovascular malformations observed in Matthew-Wood syndrome include atrial septal defects, patent ductus arteriosus, aortic arch abnormalities, and Tetralogy of Fallot (ToF), which are similar to those seen in other RA signaling disorders. CHD is seen in approximately 50 % of patients with this syndrome, with varying degrees of severity [17]. CHD incidence in the group with the STRA6 mutation was 12.48 times higher than that in the control group in a cohort of Chinese case reports of 245 patients with CTD CHD versus 853 controls (OR = 12.48, P = 0.000607). This literature suggests a link between STRA6 mutations, CHD, and race [13]. STRA6 mutations that cause CoA have also been found in descendants of immigrants to Canada from Southwest China. This study analyzed 33 patients with complete clinical data on STRA6 mutations, of whom 24 had combined CHD [16].

The Transforming Growth Factor-β (TGF-β) family includes TGF-β, BMPs, inhibins, and activins. TGF-β functions via transmembrane receptors and intracellular effector molecules [18]. The migration and differentiation of NCSC into the outer layer of the aorta involve the BMP signaling pathway [18]. BMPs have proven effects on NCSC differentiation and form a large subfamily of the TGF-β superfamily [19]. More than 15 known BMPs have been classified into four subfamilies based on nucleotide similarity and developmental variability. BMP2 and BMP4 are highly similar in group B [20,21]. The functional deletion of the BMP4 gene can impair the differentiation of NCSC into enteric nerves and the extravascular matrix of large arteries. After the knockdown of the mouse BMP4 receptor, NCSC aggregated in the roots of the CTD. Eventually, the aortic arch became abnormally segmented and significantly narrowed [22]. BMP4 is a common variant gene of Han Chinese ancestry in a study of a Han Chinese cohort of 575 confirmed CHD patients and 844 non-CHD cohorts [19]. Smad4 regulates BMP4 downstream genes that encode the bone morphogenetic protein pathway. Smad genetic variability has been linked to CHD in the literature [23,24].

The first part of this study suggested that the STRA6-BMP4 gene was downregulated in the aortic tissue of patients with CoA. The first part of the study excluded preoperative inflammation, emergency stress, and PDA effects. The first section of the study concluded three possible conclusions. First, CoA and CoA cut-edge qPCR showed low expression levels of BMP and STRA signaling pathways. We found changes in CoA aortic tissue with downregulation of COX-2 and insignificant COX-1 changes. COX-2 signaling pathway is inhibited, and apoptosis may cause aortic medial stenosis. There was no statistical difference between Immunohistochemistry and PCR in the neurological correlates of the groups, such as CR, Sox10, and Nefl. NCSC had previously been discovered in the embryonic extra-aortic matrix. NCSC is absent in the aorta during infancy and childhood. In infancy, the aorta has sympathetic nerves only in its extracellular membrane. In this study, IHC/IF detected only a few neuronal cells in the normal aortic cross-section. Second, immunohistochemistry showed human CoA narrowing of the aortic tunica adventitia and loosening of the aortic tunica adventitia. The muscular layer was absent in the tunica media and contained fewer layers of elastic fiber membranes. Overall, the experiments showed thickening of the tunica media and tunica intima and extracellular matrix expansion. Third, immunofluorescence showed that BMP4 expression was lower in the tunica adventitia at the stenosis than in the control. STRA6 expression was lower in the tunica media and adventitia at the stenosis than in the control. It is suggested that low BMP4 and STRA6 expression in the tunica adventitia is responsible for CoA. Vitamin A inhibits apoptosis, according to studies [17,25]. Corresponding to vitamin A deficiency, the vascular tunica adventitia and ectodermal matrix could not provide sufficient developmental factors [26]. After a lack of developmental factors, aortic tunica media and tunica intima undergo apoptosis, followed by tunica intima cell proliferation. Finally, the CoA artery had normal and narrow inner and outer diameters.

The second part of this experiment demonstrated that STRA6 knockout in homozygous mice should result in CoA. Experiments were performed to obtain homozygote CRISPR mice via two-generation crosses, which were verified using subtractive PCR. Although most purebred mice died within seven days of birth, 33 survived for over one month, and 11 survived for four months. Eight of 11 four-week STRA6-cKO/cKI-F3 mice had constriction in the aorta. STRA6-cKO/cKI-F3 mice had decreased cardiac function and left ventricular hypertrophy with cardiac ultrasound compared to control mice. STRA6-cKO/cKI-F3 mice may provide an alternative to the Transverse Aortic Constriction (TAC) mouse model to study the afterload in the left heart [27].

Conclusions

The high prevalence of congenital heart disease in Southwest China's highlands is multifactorial. CoA has been our focus due to the potential for complex congenital heart disease in cardiac tissue [7,28]. CoA, a representative of complex CHD, is difficult to diagnose and is associated with many complications during treatment [29,30]. The correlation between CHD and environment, diet, and genes was also explored. In this study, aortic arch development and correlations between vitamin A, BMP, and apoptosis were investigated, and STRA6 knockout model mice were constructed. Complete genome sequencing in CHD patients has been conducted less in China. However, in-depth validation in mice and the effect of vitamin A on CHD require further study.

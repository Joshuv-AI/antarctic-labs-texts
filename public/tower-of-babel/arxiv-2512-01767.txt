Non-integrability of the Sasano system of type A 5 ( 2 ) A_{5}^{(2)}
Tsvetana Stoyanova

Abstract
. The Sasano system of type A 5 ( 2 ) A^{(2)}_{5} is a four-dimensional non-linear system of ordinary differential equations, which has an affine Weyl group of symmetries of type A 5 ( 2 ) A^{(2)}_{5} . It is also a time dependent Hamiltonian system, which can be considered as coupled Painlevé III systems. In this paper, utilizing the Morales-Ramis-Simó theory for integrability of Hamiltonian systems, we prove rigorously that for all values of the parameters, for which the Sasano system of type A 5 ( 2 ) A^{(2)}_{5} admits a particular rational solution, it is non-integrable by rational first integrals.

Yusuke Sasano has introduced higher order nonlinear systems of ordinary differential equations, which have certain affine Weyl groups of symmetries

Sasano systems can be considered as coupled Painlevé systems.
In this paper we study the integrability of the Sasano system of type
A^{(2)}_{5}
given by

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,x^{2}\,y-x^{2}+(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\,x}{t}-1+4\,w+\frac{2\,x\,z\,w}{t},

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2\,x\,y^{2}+2\,x\,y-(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\,y+\alpha_{0}}{t}-\frac{2\,y\,z\,w}{t},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\dot{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,z^{2}\,w-z^{2}+(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\,z}{t}-1+4\,y+\frac{2\,x\,y\,z}{t},

\displaystyle\dot{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2\,z\,w^{2}+2\,z\,w-(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\,w+\alpha_{1}}{t}-\frac{2\,x\,y\,w}{t}\,,

where
\alpha_{j},0\leq j\leq 3

are complex parameters which satisfy the relation

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\,.

(1.2)

The Sasano system (
1
) admits a group of Bäcklund transformations
[
21
]
, which are defined by

\displaystyle s_{0}:

\displaystyle(*)\rightarrow\left(x+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{y},y,z,w,t;-\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{0},\alpha_{3}\right),

\displaystyle s_{1}:

\displaystyle(*)\rightarrow\left(x,y,z+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{w},w,t;\alpha_{0},-\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3}\right),

(1.3)

\displaystyle s_{2}:

\displaystyle(*)\rightarrow\left(x,y-\frac{\alpha_{2}\,z}{x\,z+t},z,w+\frac{\alpha_{2}\,x}{x\,z+t},t;\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{2}\right),

\displaystyle s_{3}:

\displaystyle(*)\rightarrow\left(x+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{y+w-1},y,z+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{y+w-1},w,t;\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},-\alpha_{3}\right),

\displaystyle\pi:

\displaystyle(*)\rightarrow\left(z,w,x,y,t;\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)\,,

where we denote
(*):=(x,y,z,w,t;\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
.
The transformations (
1

\displaystyle s_{i}^{2}=1,\quad i=0,1,2,3,\qquad(s_{0}\,s_{1})^{2}=(s_{0}\,s_{3})^{2}=(s_{1}\,s_{3})^{2}=1,

\displaystyle(s_{0}\,s_{2})^{3}=(s_{1}\,s_{2})^{3}=1,\qquad(s_{2}\,s_{3})^{4}=1,\qquad\pi^{2}=1\,.

Thus
s_{i},i=0,1,2,3
and
\pi_{0}
generate the extended affine Weyl group
\widetilde{W}(A^{(2)}_{5})

The system (
1
) is a time-dependent Hamiltonian system of
2+1/2
degrees of freedom

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial y},\quad\frac{dy}{dt}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x},\quad\frac{dz}{dt}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial w},\quad\frac{dw}{dt}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial z}\,,

with the polynomial Hamiltonian

H=\frac{1}{t}\Big[y\,(y-1)\,x^{2}+(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\,x\,y-\alpha_{0}\,x-t\,y+\\[0.86108pt]
+w\,(w-1)\,z^{2}+(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\,z\,w-\alpha_{1}\,z-t\,w+4t\,y\,w+2x\,y\,z\,w\Big]=\\[0.86108pt]
=H_{III}(x,y,t;\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{0})+H_{III}(z,w,t;\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1})+\\[0.86108pt]
+4y\,w+\frac{2x\,y\,z\,w}{t},

where

is the Hamiltonian associated with the third Painlevé equation given in the form

H_{III}(q,p,t;a_{1},a_{2})=\frac{1}{t}\left[p\,(p-1)\,q^{2}+(a_{1}+a_{2})\,q\,p-t\,p-a_{2}\,q\right].

In this way the Sasano system (
1

The system (
1

and a conjugate

\,\widehat{H}=H+F\,

and the associated Hamiltonian system is

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{ds}=\frac{\partial\widehat{H}}{\partial y},\quad\frac{dy}{ds}=-\frac{\partial\widehat{H}}{\partial x},\quad\frac{dz}{ds}=\frac{\partial\widehat{H}}{\partial w},\quad\frac{dw}{ds}=-\frac{\partial\widehat{H}}{\partial z},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{ds}=\frac{\partial\widehat{H}}{\partial F},\quad\frac{dF}{ds}=-\frac{\partial\widehat{H}}{\partial t}\,.

The symplectic structure

(x,y,z,w,t,F)
, that is

\Omega=dy\wedge dx+dw\wedge dz+dF\wedge dt

2.1.
Non-integrability of Hamiltonian systems and differential Galois theory

In this section we briefly recall the Morales-Ramis theory on non-integrability of Hamiltonian systems following
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

Consider a complex analytical Hamiltonian system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=X_{H}(x)

(2.6)

with a Hamiltonian
H:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
on a complex symplectic manifold
M
of dimension
2n
. Recall that by the theorem of Liouville-Arnold
[
1
]

the Hamiltonian system (
2.6
) is completely integrable if there exist
n
functionally independent first integrals
f_{1}=H,f_{2},f_{3},\ldots
and in involution.
Let
x(t)
be a non-equilibrium particular solution of (
2.6
).
The phase curve
\Gamma
is the connected Riemann surface corresponding to this solution. The variational equations
(\textrm{VE})
along
\Gamma
have the form

\,\dot{\xi}=\frac{\partial X_{H}(x(t))}{\partial x}\,\xi,\quad\xi\in T_{\Gamma}M\,.\,

We always can reduce the variational equations using the Hamiltonian in the following sense. Consider the normal bundle of
\Gamma
on the level variety

M_{h}=\{x\,|\,H(x)=h\}
. The projection of the variational equations on this bundle is called the first normal variational equations
(\textrm{NVE})_{1}
. The dimension of the
(\textrm{NVE})_{1}

is
2n-2
. Assume, in addition, that
x(t)
is a rational non-equilibrium particular solution of (
2.6
) and let as above
\Gamma
be the phase curve corresponding to it.
Assume also that the differential field
K
of coefficients of the
(\textrm{NVE})_{1}
is the field or rational functions, that is
K={\mathbb{C}}(t)
and that
t=\infty
is an irregular singularity for the

(\textrm{NVE})_{1}
. The solutions of the
(\textrm{NVE})_{1}
define a Picard-Vessiot extension
L_{1}1
of the basic field
K
. This in its turn defines
a differential Galois group
G_{1}=\textrm{Gal}(L_{1}/K)
. Then the main theorem of the Morales-Ramis theory states
[
12
,
14
]

Theorem 2.1
.

(
Morales-Ruiz and Ramis)
If the Hamiltonian system (
2.6
) is completely integrable by rational first integrals in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
, not necessarily independent on
\Gamma

itself, then the connected component
(G_{1})^{0}
of the unit element of the differential Galois group
G_{1}
is Abelian.

Theorem
2.1
provides a necessary condition for completely integrability and a sufficient condition for non-integrability of a given Hamiltonian system. That is, if the connected component
(G_{1})^{0}
of the unit
element of the Galois group
G_{1}
is not Abelian, then the corresponding Hamiltonian system (
2.6
) is not integrable by rational first integrals.

Unfortunately, the opposite is not true in general.
That is if the connected component
(G_{1})^{0}
of the unit element of the differential Galois group
G_{1}
is
Abelian, one cannot deduce that the corresponding Hamiltonian system (
2.6
) is completely integrable. Beyond the first
variational equations Morales-Ruiz, Ramis and Simó suggest in
[
16
]
to use higher order variational equations
to solve such integrability problems. Let as above
x(t)
be a rational non-equilibrium particular solution of the Hamiltonian
system (
2.6
). We write the general solution as
x(t,z)
, where
z
parametrizes it near
x(t)
as
x(t,z_{0})=x(t)
.
Then we can write the system (
2.6
) as

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t,z)=X_{H}(x(t,z))\,.

(2.7)

Denote by
x^{(k)}(t,z),\,k\geq 1
the derivatives of
x(t,z)
with respect to
z
and by

X^{(k)}_{H}(x),\,k\geq 1
the derivatives of
X_{H}(x)
with respect to
x
. By successive derivations of (
2.7
) with respect
to
z
and evaluations at
z_{0}
we obtain the so called
k
-th variational equations
(\textrm{VE})_{k}
along the solution

x(t)

\displaystyle\dot{x}^{(k)}(t)=X^{(1)}_{H}(x(t))\,x^{(k)}(t)+P\left(x^{(1)}(t),x^{(2)}(t),\ldots,x^{(k-1)}(t)\right)\,.

(2.8)

Here
P
denotes polynomial terms in the monomials of order
|k|
of the components of its arguments. The coefficients
of
P
depend on
t
through
X^{(j)}_{H}(x(t)),j<k
. For every
k>1
the linear non-homogeneous system (
2.8
)
can be arranged as a linear homogeneous system of higher dimension by making the monomials of order
|k|
in
P
new
variables and adding to (
2.8
) their differential equations. If we restrict the system (
2.8
) to the variables
that define the
(\textrm{NVE})_{1}
the corresponding linear homogeneous system is the so called
k
-th linearized
normal variational equations
(\textrm{LNVE})_{k}
. The solutions of the chain of
(\textrm{LNVE})_{k}
define a chain
of Picard-Vessiot extensions of the main field
K={\mathbb{C}}(t)
of the coefficients of
(\textrm{NVE})_{1}
, i.e. we have

K\subset L_{1}\subset L_{2}\subset\cdots\subset L_{k}
, where
L_{1}
is above,
L_{2}
is the Picard-Vessiot extension
of
K
associated with
(\textrm{LNVE})_{2}
, etc. Then we can define the differential Galois groups

G_{1}=\textrm{Gal}(L_{1}/K),\,G_{2}=\textrm{Gal}(L_{2}/K),\ldots,G_{k}=\textrm{Gal}(L_{k}/K)
. Assume as above that
t=\infty
is an irregular singularity for the
(\textrm{NVE})_{1}

and therefore for
(\textrm{NVE})_{k}
for all
k\geq 2
. Then the main theorem of the
Morales-Ruiz - Ramis - Simó theory states

Theorem 2.2
.

(Morales-Ramis-Simó)

If the Hamiltonian system (
2.6
) is completely integrable with rational first integrals, then for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

the connected component of the unit element
(G_{k})^{0}
of the differential Galois group
G_{k}=\textrm{Gal}(L_{k}/K)

is Abelian.

From Theorem
2.2
it follows, that if we find a group
(G_{k})^{0}
, which is not Abelian, then the Hamiltonian system (
2.6
) will be non-integrable
by rational first integrals.
Note that this non-commutative group
(G_{k})^{0}
will be automatically a solvable group. In this way
non-integrability in the sense of the Hamiltonian dynamics will correspond to integrability in the Picard-Vessiot sense.

with respect to a given fundamental matrix solution of (
2.9

Assume that the system (

\,\hat{M}_{0}=e^{2\pi\,i\,\Lambda}\,.\,

With respect to the formal fundamental matrix solution
\hat{\Phi}(\tau)
given by (

{\mathcal{T}}
as the differential Galois group

Denote by

For every divergent power series

\,\Theta_{j}=\{\theta_{ji},0\leq\theta_{ji}<2\pi\}\,,\,

\,\displaystyle\Theta=\cup_{j}\Theta_{j}\,.\,

Denote

The applications of summability theory to the ordinary differential equations leads to the following remarkable result

In the formal fundamental matrix solutions

Proof of Theorem

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.2
. When
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{3}/2,\alpha_{2}=1/4-\alpha_{3}
the Hamiltonian system (

Discontinuous integro-differential equations and sliding mode control
Andrey Polyakov

Abstract
The paper deals with analysis and design of sliding mode control systems modeled by finite-dimensional integro-differential equations. Filippov method and equivalent control approach are extended to a class of nonlinear discontinuous integro-differential equations and to a class of control systems modeled by infinite-dimensional differential equations in Banach spaces. Sliding mode control algorithms are designed for distributed input delay systems and for a heat control system.

1.4
Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. First, Filippov method for discontinuous ODEs is briefly surveyed. The surveyed results are utilized in the next section, where an extension of Filippov method to IDEs is proposed. almost everywhere on

\tilde{f}
being continuous in
(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 8

(Utkin solution of IDE)
.

An absolutely continuous function
x:\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be an Utkin solution of the first kind (resp., of the second kind) of the IVP (
8
), (
12
) if
x(t^{0})=x^{0}
and there exists a measurable function
u_{\rm eq}:\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
u_{\rm eq}(t)\in U(t,x(t))
and

\!\,\dot{x}(t)\!=\!f(t,x(t),u_{\rm eq}(t))\!+\!\!\int^{t}_{t^{0}}\!\!\!\Phi(t,\tau)\tilde{f}(\tau,x(\tau),u_{\rm eq}(\tau))d\tau,\!\vskip-2.84526pt

(39)

almost everywhere on
\mathcal{I}
, where
U=K[u]
is defined by (
15
)
(resp.,
U=(K[u_{1}],...,K[u_{m}])^{\top}
for
u=(u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{m})^{\top}
).

Similarly to ODEs,
the set of Utkin solutions of the first kind is a subset of the set of the Utkin solutions of the second kind, since
K[u](t,x(t))\subset(K[u_{1}](t,x(t)),...,K[u_{m}](t,x(t)))^{\top}
. They always coincide for single input systems
(i.e., if
m=1
).
Utkin solutions always exist for affine-in-control IDEs.

Theorem 19
.

Let
f
and
\tilde{f}
in (
8
) have the form

f(t,x,u)\!=\!a(t,x)\!+\!b(t,x)u,\quad\tilde{f}(t,x,u)\!=\!\tilde{a}(t,x)\!+\!\tilde{b}(t,x)u,\vskip-2.84526pt

where
t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
measurable functions
a,\tilde{a}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
b,\tilde{b}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
are locally essentially bounded and continuous in
x
.
If a measurable function

is locally essentially bounded then,
for any
t^{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and any
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the IVP (
8
),(
12
) has an Utkin solution defined, at least, locally in the forward time.
For the affine-in-control system, any Utkin solution of the first kind is a Filippov solution.

Proof.

Let us denote
f^{\eta}\!=\!a+bu^{\eta},\tilde{f}^{\eta}\!=\!\tilde{a}+\tilde{b}u^{\eta}
,

u^{\eta}(t,x)\!=\!\tfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{\|y\|\leq\eta}\!\!\!\!\!\!u(t,x+y)dy,\vskip-2.84526pt

U^{\eta}(t,x)=\overline{\mathrm{co}}\bigcup_{y,z\in\mathcal{B}(\eta)}U(t,x+y)+z,\vskip-2.84526pt

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}F^{\eta}(t,x)\\
\tilde{F}^{\eta}(t,x)\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\bigcup_{\hat{u}\in U^{\eta}(t,x)}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a(t,x)+b(t,x)\hat{u}\\
\tilde{a}(t,x)+\tilde{b}(t,x)\hat{u}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\vskip-2.84526pt

where
U=K[u]
or
U=(K[u_{1}],....,K[u_{m}])
.
By construction,
u^{\eta}(t,x)\in U^{\eta}(t,x)
,
f^{\eta}(t,x)\in F^{\eta}(t,x)
,
\tilde{f}^{\eta}(t,x)\in\tilde{F}^{\eta}(t,x)
for almost all
t
and all
x
. Since
a,\tilde{a},b,\tilde{b}
are continuous in
x
then, for any
\eta>0
, the functions
f^{\eta}
and
\tilde{f}^{\eta}
satisfy Caratheodory Condition and the set-valued mappings

F^{\eta}
and
\tilde{F}^{\eta}
are compact-valued, convex-valued and upper-semicontinuous in
x
.
Let us denote
\xi^{\eta}(t)=\int^{t}_{t^{0}}u^{\eta}(\tau,x^{\eta}(\tau))d\tau
.
Repeating the step 2 of the proof of Theorem
14

we conclude that the family of pairs
(x^{\eta},\xi^{\eta})

is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous on some time interval
\mathcal{I}
and there exists a sequence
\eta_{i}:\lim_{i\to\infty}\eta_{i}=0
and a pair of absolutely continuous functions
(x,\xi)
such that
(x^{\eta_{i}},\xi^{\eta_{i}})\to(x,\xi)
as

uniformly on
\mathcal{I}
.
Since
a,\tilde{a},b,\tilde{b}
are continuous in

we derive
\dot{\xi}(t)\in U(t,x(t))
and

\dot{x}(t)\in a(t,x(t))+b(t,x(t))U(t,x(t))+\int^{t}_{t^{0}}\Phi(t,\tau)\tilde{a}(\tau,x(\tau))d\tau+\int^{t}_{t^{0}}\Phi(t,\tau)b(\tau,x(\tau))\dot{\xi}(\tau)d\tau

for almost all

.
Due to continuity of

and
\tilde{f}
in

and

∎

A set of Filippov solutions of the affine-in-control discontinuous IDE may be larger than a set of Utkin solutions of the first kind. This is a specific feature of IDEs. For affine-in-control discontinuous ODEs (
\Phi=\mathbf{0}
), these sets are identical.

Example 5

(Continued)

At the first sight, in view of the above example, the Utkin solutions of the first kind seems to be more appropriate

Corollary 3

(On equivalent control on smooth surface)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:s(x)=0\},s\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{m})
be the discontinuity set of
the piece-wise Lipschitz continuous function
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let conditions of Theorem
19
be fulfilled, the matrix
G(t,x)=\frac{\partial s(x)}{\partial x}b(t,x)
be non-singular for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(t,x)\to G^{-1}(t,x)
be locally essentially bounded on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

i)

If
t\in[t^{0},t^{1}]\mapsto x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{I}=[t^{0},t^{1}]
is an Utkin solution of (
8
), (
12
) such that
x(t)\notin\mathcal{S}
for
t\in[t^{0},t^{*}]
and
x(t)\in\mathcal{S}
for

then the equivalent control
u_{\rm eq}:[t^{0},t^{1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is given by

\small u_{\rm eq}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}u(t,x(t))&\text{ for }&t\in[t^{0},t^{*}),\\
\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}(\mathcal{K}^{i}g)(t)&\text{ for }&t\in[t^{*},t^{1}],\end{array}\right.\vskip-2.84526pt

(41)

where
\mathcal{K}:L^{\infty}((t^{*},t^{1});\mathbb{R}^{m})\to L^{\infty}((t^{*},t^{1});\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a linear bounded operator

\small(\mathcal{K}\tilde{u})(t)\!=\!\!\int^{t}_{t^{*}}\!\!\!G^{-1}(t,x(t))\Phi(t,\tau)\tilde{b}(\tau,x(\tau))\tilde{u}(\tau)d\tau,\vskip-2.84526pt

(42)

and

\small\!\!g(t)\!=\!-G^{-1}(t,x(t))\left.\tfrac{\partial s(y)}{\partial y}\right|_{y=x(t)}\!\left(a(t,x(t))\!+\!\tilde{g}(t)\right),\vskip-5.69054pt

\small\tilde{g}(t)\!=\!\int^{t^{*}}_{t^{0}}\!\!\!\!\!\Phi(t,\tau)\left(a(\tau,x(\tau))\!+\!b(\tau,x(\tau))u(t,x(\tau)\right)d\tau.\vskip-2.84526pt

ii)

If

are locally Lipschitz continuous and

then any Utkin solution of (

), (

is unique.

Proof.

i) If

then
\frac{ds(x(t))}{dt}\!=\!\left.\frac{\partial s(y)}{\partial y}\right|_{y=x(t)}\dot{x}(t)\!=\!0
and, taking into account,

\small\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)\!=&a(t,x(t))+b(t,x(t))u_{\rm eq}(t)\\[-2.84526pt]
&+\!\int^{t}_{t^{0}}\!\!\!\Phi(t,\tau)\left(\tilde{a}(\tau,x(\tau))\!+\!\tilde{b}(\tau,x(\tau))u_{\rm eq}(\tau)\right)d\tau,\vskip-5.69054pt\end{split}

(43)

we conclude that, in the sliding mode,

is the unique solution of
the integral Volterra equation of the second kind

\small u_{\rm eq}(t)\!=\!g(t)\!+\!(\mathcal{K}u_{\rm eq})(t),\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[t^{*},t^{1}].\vskip-2.84526pt

(44)

This integral equation has a unique solution since

\small M\!=\!\!\esssup_{t^{*}\leq\tau\leq t\leq t^{1}}\|G^{-1}(t,x(t))\Phi(t,\tau)\tilde{b}(\tau,x(\tau))\|d\tau\!<\!+\infty.\vskip-5.69054pt

, then
the Neumann series
\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\mathcal{K}^{i}
is convergent in the Banach space
\mathcal{L}(L^{\infty};L^{\infty})
of bounded linear operators

44

ii) For
t\in[t^{*},t^{1}]
,
the dynamics is given by (
43
).
Let

.
For

and

.

\vskip-2.84526pt\small\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}\!\tfrac{d\|e(t)\|^{2}}{dt}\!\leq&\ell_{3}\|e(t)\|^{2}\!+\ell_{4}\|e(t)\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\mathcal{K}^{i}_{1}g_{1}(t)\!-\!\mathcal{K}^{i}_{2}g_{2}(t)\right\|\\[-2.84526pt]

\end{split}

5
Conclusions

In this paper, Filippov method is extended to systems modeled by integro-differential equations.
This method allows an ill-posed discontinuous system to be regularized by means of its transformation to a well-posed differential inclusion. Such ill-posed discontinuous systems appear in sliding mode control theory, which uses discontinuous feedback laws for regulation of systems and rejection of matched perturbations. The advantage of the Filippov method consists in a minimal (from set-theoretic point of view) extension of an ill-posed equation to a well-posed inclusion. This implies a better matching of the dynamics of the control system by the regularized model.
In the paper, the equivalent control method is developed for affine-in-control systems modeled by integro-differential equations. The well-posedness of regularized systems is studied. Filippov method and equivalent control approach are key tools for analysis of control systems with sliding modes.

To illustrate theoretical results, sliding mode control is designed for a class of systems with distributed input delay. The dynamics of the closed-loop system in the sliding mode is obtained in the form of an algebro-integro-differential equation. The equivalent control is defined as a solution of the Volterra integral equation of the second kind.

For a class of infinite-dimensional control systems (
64
) with finite-dimensional inputs and outputs, a sliding mode control can be designed and analyzed using the developed theory. Indeed, the input-output dynamics of such an infinite-dimensional system can always be represented as a finite-dimensional integro-differential equation. If the input-output dynamics admits the representation (
46
), (
47
), then the SMC for the original system can be designed in the form (
48
) provided that all conditions of Theorem
20
are fulfilled.
In Example
8
, this approach is demonstrated for a heat control system modeled by PDE.

The obtained theoretical results provide all necessary tools for extension of other
methods of SMC theory (e.g., high order SMC
[
29
]
or implicit SMC discretization
[
1
]
)
to systems modeled by integro-differential equations and by differential equations in Banach spaces (e.g., PDEs).

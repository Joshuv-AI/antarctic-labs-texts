The relationship between preoperative clinical, respiratory, and hemodynamic parameters and the surgical anatomical classification of thrombi in patients with chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension
Authors: Burcu Turan, Derya Kocakaya, Sehnaz Olgun Yildizeli, Dursun Akaslan, Halil Atas, Cagatay Cimsit, Huseyin Arikan, Nural Bekiroglu, Nezih Onur Ermerak, Bulent Mutlu, Sait Karakurt, Bedrettin Yildizeli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis study we evaluated the association between preoperative clinical, respiratory, and hemodynamic parameters and the post-operative thrombus level in the pulmonary endarterectomy (PEA) material of patients with chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH). Our hypothesis was that whether the level of thrombus is associated with preoperative pulmonary functions. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the relationship between anatomical classification of endarterectomy materials with clinical, respiratory, and hemodynamic parameters.Materials and methodsThis single-center retrospective cross-sectional study included patients aged 18 and older diagnosed with CTEPH who had PEA at Marmara University School of Medicine Hospital Istanbul between 2013 and 2022. A total of 150 patients with CTEPH who had PEA procedure. Patients whose degree of vascular involvement is unclear were excluded (n = 4). Patients whose post-operative pathology compatible other than thrombi (sarcoma, etc.) were excluded n = 3 (Fig. 2). Patients’ preoperative clinical parameters [New York Heart Association (NYHA) Functional Class (FC), 6-minute walk distance (6MWD)], PFTs, DLCO values, echocardiographic data and hemodynamic parameters measured with right heart catheterization were evaluated along with the thrombi levels of PEA specimens.ResultsThe study included 143 patients diagnosed with CTEPH who had PEA. When the distribution of thrombus materials was examined according to anatomical classification; 18.9% of patients (n = 27) were classified as Level 1 (main branch), 40.6% as Level 2 (lobar) (n = 58), 32.2% as Level 3 (segmental) (n = 46), and 8.4% as Level 4 (subsegmental) (n = 12). The mean values of predicted FEV1 (forced expiratory volume in 1 s) were 2.05 ± 0.55 L (liters) for patients with Level 1 thrombus, 2.29 ± 0.95 L for Level 2, 2.10 ± 0.76 L for Level 3, and 2.86 ± 1.33 L for Level 4 (p = 0.053). The DLCOcSB (carbon monoxide diffusion capacity corrected for alveolar volume) values were %60.38 ± 28.98 for patients with Level 1 thrombus, %65.88 ± 20.85 for Level-2, %65.29 ± 18.32 for Level-3, and %85 ± 21.42 for Level-4 (p = 0.07). Cardiac indices (L/min/m²) were 2.46 ± 0.84 for Level 1, 2.43 ± 1.14 for Level 2, 2.42 ± 0.76 for Level 3, and 3.04 ± 0.63 for Level 4 (p = 0.02). No relationship was observed between the anatomical level of thrombus and 6MWD and the hemodynamic data measured via RHC except cardiac indices (all p values > 0.05).ConclusionOur study did not find a significant relationship between the preoperative parameters of PFTs, DLCO values, and hemodynamic parameters excluding cardiac index with the anatomical classification of thrombi in patients who had PEA for CTEPH. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the relationship between anatomical classification of endarterectomy materials with clinical, respiratory, and hemodynamic parameters.Keywords: Pulmonary function test, Chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension, Pulmonary endarterectomy

Introduction

Chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH), one of the significant causes of precapillary pulmonary hypertension, characterized by widespread, recurrent, or organized thrombi, causing pulmonary artery occlusion, and diminished blood flow. Persistent pulmonary arterial obstructions lead to vascular remodeling and increased pulmonary vascular resistance and persistent pulmonary arterial hypertension. In cases of severe CTEPH, increased pulmonary vascular resistance and pulmonary arterial pressure eventually leads to right heart failure and death [1].

Pulmonary endarterectomy (PEA) is the most effective treatment for patients diagnosed with CTEPH. Although pulmonary computed tomography angiography (CTA) plays a significant role in diagnosis, especially for assessing the location of thrombus, pulmonary angiography remains the gold standard method for diagnosis [2].

Pulmonary function test (PFT) parameters and carbon monoxide diffusion capacity (DLCO) values may be affected in the presence of severe pulmonary hypertension, although they are not diagnostic for CTEPH [3]. Furthermore, it is advised to perform PFTs and DLCO to rule out any further pulmonary pathologies in the differential diagnosis of pulmonary hypertension (PH). Right heart catheterization (RHC) and the 6-minute walk test (6MWT) are crucial in the diagnosis of CTEPH [4]. In addition, these tests are crucial for determining the severity of the disease and tracking the effectiveness of treatment.

There are a limited number of studies evaluating the relationship between clinical parameters, PFTs, DLCO, and hemodynamic parameters with the level of vascular involvement (proximal/distal) in patients diagnosed with CTEPH [3].

The aim of this study was to evaluate the relationship between pre-operative clinical, respiratory, and hemodynamic parameters with the level of surgical thrombus material in patients diagnosed with CTEPH who had PEA. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the association between anatomical classification of endarterectomy materials with clinical, respiratory, and hemodynamic parameters.

Materials and methods

This single-center retrospective cross-sectional study included patients aged 18 and older diagnosed with CTEPH who had PEA at Marmara University School of Medicine Hospital Istanbul between 2013 and 2022. The data of some patients with CTEPH were retrieved from the previous study [5]. A total of 150 patients with CTEPH who had PEA procedure. Patients whose degree of vascular involvement is unclear were excluded (n = 4). Patients whose post-operative pathology compatible other than thrombi (sarcoma, etc.) were excluded n = 3 (Fig. 1).

Thrombus anatomical levels

Patient demographics, pre-operative parameters including PFT and DLCO measurements, thrombus levels in the endarterectomy specimens, clinical parameters (New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class, 6-minute walk distance (6MWD)), hemodynamic parameters measured by RHC (mean pulmonary arterial pressure (PAP), pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), cardiac index (CI) and echocardiographic data were obtained from patient files and electronic medical records. Patients whose degree of vascular involvement is unclear in the post-operative material and post-operative pathology compatible other than thrombi (sarcoma, etc.) were excluded from the study.

Patients underwent spirometry using the MIR Spirolab II (Medical International Research, Rome, Italy; Serial No: 149491103403) flow-sensitive spirometer according to American Thoracic Society (ATS) criteria. The test was performed three times, and the best result was recorded. For reversibility measurement, after inhaling 4 puffs of salbutamol (400 micrograms), spirometry was repeated 15 min later, and FEV1 (forced expiratory volume in 1 s) (% predicted and liters [L]), FVC (forced vital capacity) (% predicted and L), and FEV1/FVC (%) values were measured [6].

Patients underwent DLCO testing using a Jaeger spirometer device in accordance with ERS (European Respiratory Society)/ATS guidelines [7]. Tests outside the 9–11 s breath-hold range, inspirations where inspiratory vital capacity (IVC) was less than 90%, and measurements where alveolar ventilation was less than 10% of total lung capacity were excluded from the study. DLCO measurements were conducted using gas chromatography, and results were recorded in ml/min/mmHg. Tests with error codes were carefully reviewed, and those deemed faulty were excluded from the study. Test results were based on measurements corrected for hemoglobin levels.

The exercise capacity of the patients was evaluated by 6MWT. According to ATS recommendations, patients were asked to walk in a hospital corridor for 6 min at their own pace, aiming to cover as much distance as possible [8]. The distance covered was recorded in meters.

All patients in this study underwent PEA according to the standardized technique. Shortly, the operation was performed on deep hypothermia (20 °C) under complete circulatory arrest. A true endarterectomy was accomplished, and when it was completed, circulation with warming was restarted [9, 10]. We used the San Diego (UCSD)- CTEPH surgical classification for surgical anatomical classification of thrombi [11].

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 15.0 (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences). For continuous variables, mean and standard deviation (SD) values were reported. Continuous variables showing normal distribution were compared between groups using unpaired t-tests or one-way ANOVA (analysis of variance). For variables not showing normal distribution, a comparison was performed using the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis test. Multiple comparisons among more than two groups were assessed using the Tukey-Kramer test or Dunn test. Comparison of continuous variables between baseline and values at 3 months was conducted using paired t-test. Frequency distributions of categorical variables were calculated. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p-value equal to or less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The sample size calculation for the study was performed using G*Power 3.1.3 software, assuming a 95% confidence interval, p = 0.05 and r = 0.3 was determined that a minimum of 111 participants should be included in the study.

Results

A total of 143 patients with CTEPH who had PEA procedure were included in the study. Among these patients, 55,9% (n = 80) were female. Among 130 patients for whom PFT test results were available, 94 also had DLCO test. Parenchymal evaluation was performed on CT in patients with obstruction on PFT. No findings suggestive of severe emphysema or bronchial wall abnormalities were found.

When PFT results were evaluated according to the surgical level of thrombi, the mean FEV1 values were found to be 2.05 ± 0.55 L for patients with Level 1, 2.29 ± 0.95 L for Level 2, 2.10 ± 0.76 L for Level 3, and 2.86 ± 1.33 L for Level-4 thrombi (p = 0.053).

The mean % predicted FEV1 values were found to be 73.02 ± 6.79 for patients with Level 1 thrombi in PEA material, 81.70 ± 23.26 for Level 2, 77.17 ± 18.42 for Level 3, and 80.91 ± 24.05 for Level 4 thrombi (p = 0.344).

The median FVC values in L were 2.64 ± 0.78 in patients with Level 1 thrombi, 2.93 ± 1.11 in Level 2, 2.68 ± 0.87 in Level 3, and 3.4 ± 1.39 in patients with Level 4 thrombi (p = 0.114) in PEA materials.

The FEV1/FVC ratio was found to be 76.58 ± 10.25% for patients with Level 1 thrombi, 77.78 ± 10.75% for Level 2 thrombi, 77.15 ± 9.77% for Level 3 thrombi, and 77.95 ± 14.89% for Level 4 thrombi (p = 0.967).

Regarding the diffusion capacity of patients, the mean DLCO_SB (single breath diffusion capacity for carbon monoxide) was measured as 60.5 ± 28.27 ml/(min·mmHg) for patients with Level 1 thrombi, 67.11 ± 22.06 ml/(min·mmHg) for Level 2, 66.19 ± 18.41 ml/(min·mmHg) for Level 3, and 84.25 ± 23.40 ml/(min·mmHg) for Level 4 (p = 0.107). DLCOcSB values were 60.38 ± 28.98% in patients with Level 1 thrombus, 65.88 ± 20.85% in Level 2, 65.29 ± 18.32% in Level 3 and 85 ± 21.42% in Level 4 (p = 0.07).

DLCO/VA (diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide adjusted for alveolar volume) values were calculated as 81.66 ± 32.5 L in patients with Level 1 material, 90.16 ± 32 L in Level 2, 83.8 ± 22.84 L in Level 3 and 86.62 ± 23.44 L in Level 4 (p = 0.717).

When examining TLC (total lung capacity), mean % predicted TLC values were 83.46 ± 33.14 L/sec in patients with Level 1 thrombus, 85.96 ± 28.92 L/sec in patients with Level 2 thrombus, 82.17 ± 22.57 L/sec in patients with Level 3 and, 74.83 ± 12.3 L/sec in patients with Level 4 thrombus (p = 0.814).

Patients were evaluated based on thrombus levels and their mean sPAP (systolic pulmonary artery pressure) measured during pre-operative right heart catheterization. The mean values of sPAP were 42 ± 14.79 mmHg in patients with Level 1 thrombus, 41.43 ± 14.17 mmHg in patients with Level 2 thrombus, 38.65 ± 16.05 mmHg in patients with Level 3 thrombus, and 38.08 ± 13.7 mmHg in patients with Level 4 thrombus (p = 0.687).

When we evaluate the PVR measurements by RHC, patients with Level 1 thrombus had 8.96 ± 6.19 WU, patients with Level 2 had 8.23 ± 6. WU, patients with Level 3 had 6.6 ± 4.38 WU, and patients with Level 4 had 5.34 ± 2.72 WU (p = 0.133).

In the analysis of the body mass index (BMI) results patients with Level 1 thrombus had 26.23 ± 5.9 kg/m², patients with Level 2 had 29.59 ± 6.2 kg/m², patients with Level 3 had 28.65 ± 5.82 kg/m², and patients with Level 4 had 29.0 ± 5.76 kg/m² (p = 0.05).

Regarding the cardiac output values, patients with Level 1 thrombus had 4.45 ± 1.75 L/min, Level 2 had 4.50 ± 1.60 L/min, Level 3 had 4.45 ± 1.25 L/min, and Level 4 had 5.40 ± 1.85 L/min (p = 0.269).

In terms of cardiac indices (L/min/m²), results were found as 2.46 ± 0.84 in Level 1 thrombus, 2.43 ± 201.14 in Level 2 thrombus, 2.42 ± 0.76 in Level 3 thrombus, and 3.04 ± 0.63 in Level 4 thrombus, reaching a statistically significant difference (p = 0.02).

There was no significant difference observed between the levels of thrombi extracted with PEA with the functional capacities and 6MWD of the patients (p = 0.495).

Discussion

In this study, we presented a detailed analysis of 143 patients who underwent PEA for CTEPH. This study demonstrated that there was a significant association between the cardiac indices measured by RHC and the thrombus anatomical classification extracted by PEA. However, we did not find any relationship between clinical parameters, preoperative PFTs, DLCO results, and most hemodynamic parameters. Regarding the PFT measurements of patients, FEV1 values in liters tended to be lower in those with thrombi in the main and segmental pulmonary arteries, although they did not reach statistical significance. DLCO measurements were lower in patients with more proximal thrombi meaning that from subsegmental to main pulmonary artery, a statistically significant decreasing trend was observed. Concurrently, in the clinical parameters examined, it was observed that 6MWD tended to be longer in patients with subsegmental pulmonary thrombi. Furthermore, in hemodynamic parameters measured by RHC, statistically significantly lower values were found in patients with thrombi in the main pulmonary arteries, but there was no relationship was between these data and PFT parameters.

In the context of PH, the exact cause of the decrease in DLCO is not fully understood. Although the possibility of it stemming from the obliteration of small pulmonary arteries has been emphasized, particularly as an early marker for progression in systemic sclerosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis patients, recent studies have shown the significant role of endothelial cells in the development of plexiform lesions, leading to vascular obliteration and membrane thickening [12, 13].

In a study published by Chandra and colleagues in 2010, 408 Group 1 PAH (pulmonary arterial hypertension) patients were included, and when patients were grouped according to DLCO/VA values, those in the lowest percentile were found to have the highest mortality rate [14]. Studies demonstrating a decrease in DLCO in CTEPH patients have often been based on the information from PAH patients, emphasizing micro vasculopathy. These studies have been planned considering PAH patients and have highlighted the decrease in diffusion capacity independent of pulmonary vascular disease and thrombus burden in these patients. In a study conducted by Bernstein and colleagues in 1996, they examined the DLCO measurements of 31 patients before and 2–12 weeks after PEA operation, and they pointed out that measurements persisted low regardless of the surgery. In this study, the researchers proposed that the decrease in DLCO was attributed to a chronic reduction in lung membrane diffusion capacity and, to a lesser extent, a decrease in pulmonary capillary blood volume, and they attributed the lack of improvement in post-operative measurements to this. However, since the post-operative measurements in this study were conducted over a wide time range and in the early postoperative period, it is not possible to directly correlate these results with PEA and CTEPH pathophysiology [15].

Tanabe et al. evaluated the gas diffusion changes obtained by PEA in CTEPH patients. They also found that the low FVC, DLCO, and TLC parameters detected in the preoperative period persisted at similar levels in long-term follow-up [16].

In another study published by Suda et al. in 2017, the prognostic and pathophysiological significance of DLCO levels measured at the time of diagnosis in CTEPH patients was evaluated. In this study, patients were grouped according to whether DLCO was normal or low. They found that patients in the group with DLCO < 80% had a worse prognosis. They also indicated that there was no relationship between DLCO reduction, and the presence of proximal thrombus evaluated radiologically. However, DLCO at diagnosis might be useful for predicting which patients with CTEPH should have a better long-term outcome after PEA [3].

Even in the absence of a history of smoking, patients with CTEPH frequently exhibit obstructive ventilatory dysfunction in clinical settings. According to a study by Yanagisawa et al., exertional dyspnea and a low FEV1 led to the incorrect diagnosis of bronchial asthma in a CTEPH patient. The results of the PFTs in CTEPH patients were recently reported in this study and, in comparison to healthy controls, these patients typically had lower FEV1 values [16]. A low FEV1 does not necessarily mean the presence of airway diseases such as COPD and asthma in patients having exertional dyspnea. So, we should keep the possibilities of the pathophysiological condition with right heart burden including CTEPH in mind while carrying out a diagnostic evaluation.

In a recent study designed to assess the effects of balloon pulmonary angioplasty (BPA) on DLCO, the authors claim that lower DLCO results are associated with microvascular disease with distal CTEPH patients and low DLCO affects the response of BPA [17].

According to a summary of earlier research, thrombotic occlusion and remodeling of non-obstructed arteries brought on by flow diversion from obstructed arteries are the pathological observations on chest CT in the region where pulmonary arteries are blocked. As a result of the alveolar, bronchiolar, and bronchial regions collapsing, lung infarction may impact local airflow. Conversely, numerous studies have reported that a distinctive feature of CTEPH is the variation in segmental vessel size, which is indicative of the uneven distribution of emboli in the lungs adjacent may become compressed because of localized expansion of the peripheral pulmonary arteries. Obstructive impairment might have an etiological relationship with vascular involvement. Further investigation focusing on the obstructive impairment in CTEPH patients may shed new light on the CTEPH etiology [18].

CTEPH mainly differs from PAH by the proximal location of pulmonary artery obliteration, although distal arteriopathy can be observed because of over-perfusion of the non-occluded areas [19].

While the current literature suggests that DLCO is affected by more distal disease due to micro-vasculopathy and may not improve despite surgery, our results differ from the literature in that patients with proximal lesions had a trend with lower DLCO values. To support these findings, further randomized large scale studies with a larger number of patients with distal disease are needed.

Strengths and limitations

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, this was retrospectively conducted in a single center. A limitation of this study is the presence of missing documentation in some cases, particularly regarding pulmonary function test (PFT) data, among patients who had undergone preoperative follow-up at external centers. These missing records were not associated with patient severity but were an incidental outcome of the retrospective study design. Patients with incomplete documentation were not intentionally selected, and the occurrence of missing data reflects the inherent constraints of retrospective data collection. We have added this to the manuscript as a limitation.

Secondly, the patients included in this study were not stratified based on comorbidity status. Additionally, due to the small number of patients with Level 4 thrombi as expected for PEA, statistically significant differences between groups may not have been reached. Unlike the other studies in the literature, our study observed a tendency for lower DLCO levels in patients with thrombi at more proximal levels. Also, this study did not include postoperative outcomes since our hypothesis was related to preoperative tests. To support these findings, long-term follow-up studies are needed.

However, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the association between surgical anatomical classification of endarterectomy materials with PFT and DLCO measurements. Conclusion

In our study, no significant relationship was found between the anatomical classification of thrombi with preoperative PFTs, DLCO, and other clinical and hemodynamic test parameters except cardiac indices in patients who had PEA for CTEPH.

In conclusion, contrary to the literature, our study found a trend towards lower DLCO levels in patients with proximal thrombosis especially in the main pulmonary arteries rather than those with distal disease and identified significant micro vasculopathy. Therefore, there is a need for larger prospective studies with more patients to further investigate this issue.

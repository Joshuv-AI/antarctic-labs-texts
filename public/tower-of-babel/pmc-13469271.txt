Study of nanoparticle morphology effect on fluid flow between two active parallel plates
Authors: R J Punith Gowda, Vishwanatha Rajeev Banakar, Shahbaz Juneja, Sachin Singh, Kotermane Mallikarjunappa Anil kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe thermophysical performance of nanofluids is strongly influenced by nanoparticle morphology, making realistic property modeling essential for accurately predicting coupled flow and heat transfer characteristics in advanced thermal systems. Motivated by this need, the present study investigates magnetohydrodynamic flow of nanofluid between two active parallel plates embedded in a porous medium under quadratic thermal radiation and convective boundary condition. Multiple viscosity and thermal conductivity correlations, including morphology-dependent models for spherical, brick, cylindrical and platelet nanoparticles, are incorporated to quantify their influence on momentum and heat transfer. The governing nonlinear partial differential equations are transformed into coupled ordinary differential equations using similarity transformations and solved through the Tchebichef polynomial collocation method. Numerical accuracy is verified using the Runge–Kutta-Fehlberg fourth-fifth order method, while a Levenberg–Marquardt artificial neural network is employed to predict the nonlinear velocity and temperature fields. Results demonstrate that increasing magnetic field strength and porous resistance suppress velocity, whereas larger radiation and Biot numbers enhance temperature. The proposed framework provides reliable guidance for thermal management technologies.Keywords: Morphology, Nanofluid, Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm, Tchebichef polynomial collocation, Active parallel plates

Introduction

The transfer of thermal energy from an area of high temperature to an area of low temperature by conduction, convection and radiation. It is one of the basic parameters that affect the thermal performance and energy efficiency of engineering systems. For fluid flow applications, the heat transfer is affected by flow properties, external effects (thermal radiation, diffusion effects), boundary conditions and fluid properties. To optimize performance, reliability and maximize energy utilization, effective control of heat transfer is necessary for thermal management, heat exchangers, rotating machinery, cooling technologies, chemical processing, power generation, microfluidic devices and advanced manufacturing systems. Akbar et al. [1] elucidated the heat transfer and flow of nanofluid (N-F) via a permeable medium. Sarfraz and Khan [2] studied the heat transfer and hybrid N-F flow due to an inclined stretchable surface. Akbar et al. [3] exemplified the effect of nanoparticle aggregation on the flow and heat transfer of N-F. Muhammed et al. [4] deliberated the heat transfer and flow of hybrid N-F flow subjected to coaxial cylinders using the Keller-box scheme. Iqbal et al. [5] scrutinized the heat transfer and hybrid N-F flow through a symmetric channel. The heat transfer and flow of bioconvective Jeffery N-F past a stretchable surface were investigated by Muhammad et al. [6]. Sarfaraz and Khan [7] exemplified the consequence of a heat source/sink on the heat transfer and flow of hybrid N-F via a permeable inclined surface.

N-Fs are novel heat transfer materials developed by combining ultrafine solid particles, usually on the nanometre scale, with conventional fluids such as water or ethylene glycol. Adding nanoparticles to the base fluid changes the effective viscosity, thermal conductivity, density and heat capacity of the fluid, thereby altering momentum and energy transfer properties. Their high surface-to-volume ratio increases interfacial heat exchange and makes them suitable for high-performance thermal systems. The concentration, size distribution and morphology of particles in N-Fs are critical to their overall behavior, determining flow resistance and thermal enhancement. Proper mathematical modeling is therefore necessary to estimate their performance, especially when magnetic fields, porous media or radiative heat transfer effects are involved in the system. Jagadha et al. [8] elucidated the N-F flow in a microtube with slip conditions. Khan et al. [9] probed the stagnation point flow of N-F past a cylindrical disk. Kattimani et al. [10] examined the reactive Casson N-F flow subjected to a permeable stretchable surface. Srilatha et al. [11] investigated the heat transfer and flow of N-F past a spinning disk. Kumar et al. [12] critically reviewed the mathematical models and computational techniques to study the heat transfer in N-Fs. Akbar et al. [13] explored the flow of electrically conducting N-F via a symmetric channel.

A dynamic channel configuration is represented by two parallel active plates, with both bounding surfaces subjected to controlled movement, deformation, suction or injection. In contrast to stationary plate systems, the active plates produce additional kinematic effects that directly influence momentum transfer and the pressure distribution within the confined fluid. These configurations are widely used to model squeezing flows, contraction–expansion processes and mechanically driven transfer phenomena in microfluidics and industrial applications. Wall motion and fluid inertia generate complex velocity gradients that significantly alter boundary layer development. When coupled with thermal effects, porous resistance or magnetic fields, active parallel plate systems provide a realistic framework for analyzing complex heat transfer and flow control systems. Erbay et al. [14] found that transient laminar flow across parallel plates generates the most entropy at high Reynolds numbers during counter motion and the least during parallel motion. Banakar et al. [15] examined the radiative fluid due to two parallel active plates using the collocation technique. Rahbari et al. [16] investigated the heat transfer and Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow of non-Newtonian Maxwell fluid between two parallel plates. Raza and Wang [17] elucidated the Boger N-F flow between two parallel plates. Turkyilmazoglu and Alotaibi [18] scrutinized the fluid flow between two parallel active plates.

Thermal radiation is another form of heat transfer that does not need a medium to transfer heat. Radiative effects may also play a major role in the energy balance and temperature distribution in fluid flow systems experiencing high temperature gradients. Unlike conduction and convection, radiation is strongly influenced by temperature differences and surface emissivity. Linear radiation approximations are often insufficient in engineering designs, particularly where thermal loads are high or the media exhibit semi-transparency. Nonlinear or quadratic thermal radiation (QT-R) formulations provide greater physical realism in thermal modeling under high-temperature conditions. Proper modeling of radiative heat flux is thus necessary for accurately capturing thermal boundary layer behavior and the performance of heat transfer in sophisticated fluid systems. Kumari and Nath [19] analyzed the impact of thermal radiation on the fluid flow due to a horizontal surface. Mahantesh et al. [20] investigated the effect of QT-R on the dusty N-F flow subjected to a vertical plate. Sanju et al. [21] reviewed recent works on the effect of QT-R on heat transfer in micropolar fluids. Rao et al. [22] studied the influence of QT-R on the bioconvective Ree-Eyring fluid across an inclined plate. Mandal and Pal [23] elucidated the tri-hybrid N-F flow due to a permeable disk under the effect of QT-R.

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are mathematical models based on biological neural systems, designed to estimate complex, nonlinear relationships between input and output variables. In thermal-fluid investigations, ANNs are used to estimate heat transfer rates, Nusselt numbers and temperature distributions from governing parameters, without repeatedly solving the differential equations. An ANN can also learn to describe hidden interplay among physical variables using numerical or experimental data and make quick, dependable forecasts. This ability is particularly useful in multiparameter systems where analytical solutions cannot be easily obtained. In engineering applications, predictive accuracy must be ensured and overfitting prevented through proper training, validation and testing procedures. Sharma et al. [24] elucidated the N-F flow past a stretchable cylinder utilizing an ANN. Kattimani et al. [25] utilized the ANN technique to examine the reactive Casson fluid flow due to a revolving disk. Kumar and Sharma [26] assessed the heat transfer and Bodewadt flow of fluid using an ANN. Naik et al. [27] used an ANN approach to study the Boger fluid flow across a revolving disk. Kumar et al. [28] explored the consequences of porous structures on Casson N-F flow past a stretching sheet using a neural network. Kaswan et al. [29] elucidated the feature of bioconvective flow over an elongating surface using ANN.

The collocation method is a numerical technique for obtaining approximate solutions to various equations by enforcing the governing equations at selected discrete points in the computational domain. Rather than integrating stepwise, as in shooting or finite difference methods, the unknown function is represented as a finite series expansion in terms of appropriate basis functions and the residual is made to vanish at specified collocation points. This approach transforms nonlinear boundary value problems into efficiently solvable algebraic equations. Collocation methods used to solve fluid flow and heat transfer equations combine high accuracy with comparatively low computational cost when used together with orthogonal polynomials, making them applicable to solving complex fluid flow and heat transfer equations. Kattimani et al. [30] examined the fluid flow between two parallel disks using the collocation method. Sadiq et al. [31] investigated the heat transfer and couple stress fluid flow by employing the collocation method. Khader [32] exemplified the unsteady Casson fluid flow utilizing the collocation method. Gao et al. [33] studied the N-F flow between two parallel surfaces with the aid of the collocation approach. Kumbinarasaiah and Preetham [34] used the collocation technique and examined the boundary layer flow of viscous fluid.

Morphology in N-F systems refers to the geometric shape and structural features of the dispersed nanoparticles, which significantly influence the effective thermophysical properties of the suspension. Particle geometry affects surface area, aspect ratio and interfacial contact with the base fluid, such as spherical, cylindrical, brick and platelet. These factors directly alter the enhancement of thermal conductivity, viscous resistance and momentum diffusion within the flow. Anisotropic heat transfer pathways and distortions of micro-convection processes are common characteristics of non-spherical particles, contrary to the traditional assumption of sphericity. Thus, morphology-dependent models should be integrated to obtain realistic predictions of heat transfer and flow behavior, especially in systems subjected to magnetic fields, porous resistance and radiative influences. Zhang et al. [35] elucidated the morphological effects on the coupled fluid flow. Xu and Yuan [36] utilized the finite difference method to examine the morphology effect on the coupling of heat and fluid flow. The heat transfer and flow of fluid in the presence of the morphology effect were examined by Zhuang et al. [37]. Nichik et al. [38] investigated the effect of morphology on the cross-fluid flow subjected to a circular cylinder. Yasin et al. [39] studied the consequence of morphology on the ternary N-F flow due to a porous disk.

In recent years, N-Fs have been the subject of intense research, with a recent focus on morphology-engineered nanoparticles, high-fidelity thermophysical property correlations and intelligent data-driven prediction techniques to design advanced thermal management systems. However, despite these advances, most current investigations rely on a single viscosity or thermal conductivity correlation and assume spherical nanoparticles, thereby ignoring the effect of particle geometry on coupled momentum and heat transfer. In addition, research on active parallel-plate systems subjected to magnetic fields, porous media, QT-R and convective boundary conditions remains scarce. As a result, the effects of morphology-dependent thermophysical properties, radiative heat transfer, porous resistance and wall deformation have not been comprehensively studied within a single mathematical framework.

To address this gap, the present study provides a new morphology-sensitive MHD N-F model with multiple viscosity correlations, three thermal conductivity models and four nanoparticle geometries: spherical, brick, cylindrical and platelet particles. The nonlinear governing equations are solved numerically using the Tchebichef polynomial collocation method (TPCM), and the results are verified using the Runge–Kutta-Fehlberg fourth-fifth-order (RKF-45) method. Moreover, a Levenberg–Marquardt artificial neural network (LM-ANN) is trained using high-order numerical calculations to accurately predict the nonlinear velocity and temperature distributions, thereby combining machine learning with high-order numerical computation in complex transfer phenomena.

The main goal of this work is to develop a comprehensive computational model capable of accurately predicting how nanoparticle morphology affects coupled flow and heat transfer between two active parallel plates. In particular, the following points are addressed: comparison of various viscosity and thermal conductivity correlations, effects of governing dimensionless parameters on momentum and thermal transfer, robustness and efficiency of the proposed numerical procedure, and the predictive capability of the LM-ANN. Based on that, the following research questions are developed:

Compared to the traditional numerical and semi-analytical methods, the combination of TPCM and LM-ANN possesses several advantages in solving the present nonlinear transfer problem. The TPCM is different from finite difference and finite element methods that typically need fine spatial discretization and repeated iterative computation to get high accuracy; it transforms the governing nonlinear differential equations directly into a system of algebraic equations by using orthogonal polynomial approximations and collocation points. This method can obtain fast convergence, high numerical accuracy and low computational complexity and solve the nonlinear coupled governing equations efficiently. Moreover, unlike the perturbation-based analytical methods, which are usually restricted to small or large parameter values, the TPCM is valid in the entire range of the parameters investigated without any approximation. In addition, the LM-ANN further complements the numerical methodology by learning the nonlinear relationship between the governing physical parameters and the corresponding numerical solutions generated by the TPCM. After training, the ANN can be used to predict the velocity and temperature distributions quickly, which can save a lot of computational time in extensive parametric studies without repeatedly solving the governing equations. Therefore, the TPCM is integrated into the LM-ANN, which is a robust numerical method that can be used for the morphology-dependent analysis of N-F transfer with the high computational efficiency of data-driven prediction.

Results and discussion

The current section presents a parametric study of an N-F system modeled between two active, parallel, deformable plates under the influence of MHD and porous medium. Similarity transformations reduce the governing partial differential equations for continuity, momentum and energy conservation, with QT-R and convective boundary condition to coupled nonlinear ODEs. Multiple correlations for viscosity and thermal conductivity model the N-F’s thermophysical properties, including morphology-dependent correlations for spherical, brick, cylindrical and platelet nanoparticle geometries, as well as nanolayer effects. This multiphase modelling framework enables systematic comparison of property variations within the same mathematical context. The TPCM is used to solve the reduced boundary value problem numerically, and the solutions are compared with those from RKF-45 to assess computational consistency. Additionally, the LM-ANN model is used to test its potential in estimating the numerical solutions of the governing equations. The discussion is structured to evaluate the impact of key dimensionless parameters, i.e., the magnetic field parameter, porosity parameter, Reynolds number, squeeze/expansion parameter, radiation parameter, Biot number and temperature ratio, within the morphological and radiative modelling assumptions adopted.

Figure 2 demonstrates how the magnetic parameter M affects the distribution of the axial velocity of various models of viscosity. The velocity consistently decreases as M increases. The magnetic parameter is always raised to suppress the velocity consistently, since the induced Lorentz force is opposite to the velocity of the fluid, which leads to the decrease of the kinetic energy of the fluid and the increase of the electromagnetic resistance of the fluid, which leads to the decrease of the momentum transfer. The decrease is more significant in the middle of the channel where the flow momentum is highest. This behaviour is also modified by the influence of the nanoparticle morphology, which also alters the hydrodynamic interaction. The enhanced particle–fluid momentum exchange and the disruption of the local shear field caused by particles with larger surface area and higher aspect ratio intensify effective momentum diffusion and enhance the magnetic damping effect. Thus, morphology and magnetic braking are coupled to control the momentum transfer in the electrically conducting N-Fs. The impact of the porosity parameter on the velocity distribution is given in Fig. 3. As is raised, is evidently decreased across the channel. As the porosity parameter is increased, the velocity is decreased as more Darcy resistance is created, which decreases the momentum transfer and fluid flow through the channel. At the same time, the shape of the nanoparticles affects the value of this resistance, which depends on the interaction between the particles and the fluid. Particles with a non-spherical shape create more hydrodynamic disturbances due to their increased interfacial area and aspect ratio, which means they have a larger momentum diffusion than spherical particles. Thus, the effect of porous drag and momentum transfer by morphology is more significant in attenuating the velocity field. Figure 4 indicates how varies with Reynolds number . The velocity initially enhances as increases, then it decreases throughout the channel. A high implies that the forces of inertia dominate over the forces of viscosity, causing increased axial acceleration. As a result, the fluid is moving faster, and the momentum boundary layer is reduced. The enhancement, however, is related to the morphology of nanoparticles. The momentum diffusion in the suspension is modified by the high-aspect-ratio particles due to increased particle–fluid interactions, which changes the local shear distribution. Therefore, the effective viscosity is not the only effect of morphology; it is also the redistribution of momentum in the flow domain.

The effect of the radiation parameter Rd on the temperature distribution is shown in Fig. 5. An increase in Rd leads to an increase in temperature within the channel. Improved thermal radiation provides more thermal energy to the fluid, creating a thicker thermal boundary layer and increasing thermal diffusion. This enhancement varies depending on the morphology of the nanoparticles, as a larger surface area gives higher interface contact and thus enables the creation of continuous conductive pathways within the suspension. Thus, the effective thermal conductivity and also the internal energy transfer efficiency increase, and a more uniform temperature distribution is obtained, which is beneficial to morphology. When you squeeze harder, the gap between the plates becomes smaller and the heat from dissipation and compression rises. The morphology of the nanoparticles also affects the response due to local hydrodynamic interactions and shear transfer, which are modified by the anisotropy of the particles. The differences in velocity development between the viscosity models are more visible for the particles with high aspect ratios, as this aspect ratio has a significant influence on the momentum transfer between the fluid and the dispersed phase. The difference in temperature between the walls and the ambient temperature increases the strength of the thermal driving force and, consequently, additional heat enters the fluid. This is further enhanced by morphology-dependent nanoparticles, which increase the contact area of particles and fluid and minimize the thermal resistance at the interface. This results in enhanced transfer from the heated walls to the fluid and an increase in the thickness of the thermal boundary layer and bulk temperature.

ANNs are mathematical models that are based on biological neural systems and are created to estimate complicated nonlinear correlations among numerous input variables and output variables. They are especially useful in the modeling of fluid flow and heat transfer problems with highly coupled and nonlinear governing equations. The ANN works by using the interconnected neurons formed in the form of input, hidden and output layers, with each connection having an adjustable weight. These weights are changed by backpropagation (iteratively) during training with the goal of reducing a specified error measure, commonly the mean square error (MSE). The Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm will be used in the current research because it has a fast convergence rate and has a hybrid optimization nature; it combines the merits of the gradient descent and the Gauss–Newton methods. The technique is particularly appropriate in the nonlinear regression system with large data sets. To guarantee generalization and avoid overfitting, the numerical data obtained by the use of the RKF-45 scheme is split into three parts: 80% training, 10% validation and 10% testing. Predictive performance of the model is measured by MSE, gradient magnitude, damping parameter , validation check, regression coefficient (R) and absolute error distribution. Figure 10 demonstrates the design of the created LM-ANN model, which is comprised of a single input neuron, a hidden layer, including 10 neurons and a single output neuron. In this setup, the nonlinear mapping between the input parameters and the predicted flow or thermal profile converges predictably.

Architecture of the developed LM-ANN model

The proposed LM-ANN is a single hidden-layer feedforward network consisting of ten neurons to set up the nonlinear relation between the governing physical parameters and the numerical solutions. This architecture was selected because of its simplicity of calculation, convergence property and ability to predict, even for moderately sized engineering datasets, nonlinear regression and function approximation problems. The dimensionless velocity and temperature profiles from the RKF-45 are the target outputs, while the governing dimensionless parameters controlling the transfer phenomena are the ANN inputs. Numerically generated datasets were used for training the network with the various ranges of Reynolds number, magnetic parameter, radiation parameter and volume fraction of nanoparticles investigated to ensure the reliability of the network to interpolate the results within the computational domain investigated in this study. The present work aims to show the usefulness of the LM-ANN as an efficient surrogate model for the computed numerical solutions and not to optimize the architecture of the neural network; therefore, a systematic study of other possible hidden layer structures was not performed. Similarly, robustness against noisy or incomplete datasets and extrapolation beyond the range of investigated parameters were not included since in the numerical training data, the uncertainties of measurements are zero. These are interesting areas for further study using experimental data and/or wider operating conditions.

Conclusion

In the current study, a detailed mathematical model is developed to analyze the MHD N-F flow and heat transfer between active parallel plates in a porous medium with QT-R and convective boundary condition. The research systematically quantified the effect of particle shape on coupled momentum and thermal transfer and included several viscosity and thermal conductivity correlations, as well as a variety of nanoparticle shapes. The TPCM was employed to solve the nonlinear governing equations with high accuracy, and, for comparison, an LM-ANN was able to predict the nonlinear flow and temperature fields with high accuracy. The developed framework provides an enhanced physical description to replace traditional single-property models and serves as a reliable computational tool for studying morphology-dependent transfer phenomena in advanced thermal engineering systems. The main new findings from the present study are briefly summarized below:

The simultaneous study of the effects of nanoparticle morphology, magnetic field, porous medium, QT-R, and convective boundary condition was conducted within a single mathematical model, yielding a more realistic description of N-F flow than classical models.

The simultaneous study of the effects of nanoparticle morphology, magnetic field, porous medium, QT-R, and convective boundary condition was conducted within a single mathematical model, yielding a more realistic description of N-F flow than classical models.

Multiple viscosity correlations and three thermal conductivity models were systematically compared, and it was found that the choice of thermophysical property can greatly affect momentum transfer and heat transfer predictions.

Multiple viscosity correlations and three thermal conductivity models were systematically compared, and it was found that the choice of thermophysical property can greatly affect momentum transfer and heat transfer predictions.

The magnetic field and porous resistance were effective flow-control mechanisms, and it was found that they weakened the velocity field, whereas the increasing Rd, Bi, wall temperature ratio and squeezing effect strengthened thermal energy transfer and thus enhanced the temperature distribution.

The magnetic field and porous resistance were effective flow-control mechanisms, and it was found that they weakened the velocity field, whereas the increasing Rd, Bi, wall temperature ratio and squeezing effect strengthened thermal energy transfer and thus enhanced the temperature distribution.

The TPCM proposed in this paper was shown to be robust and efficient in solving the nonlinear boundary value problems encountered, achieving very high accuracy and excellent agreement with the RKF-45.

The LM-ANN predicts generated numerical velocity and temperature distributions with good accuracy, demonstrating its potential as a reliable surrogate model for fast prediction of complex morphology-dependent thermal transfer problems.

The results are useful for the design and optimization of morphology-engineered N-Fs for microchannel cooling systems, porous heat exchangers, compact electronic cooling systems, thermal energy storage systems and other advanced thermal management technologies.

The present study offers an extensive investigation of morphology-dependent N-F flow and heat transfer with active parallel plates; however, the mathematical model developed in the present study is based on a set of assumptions that limit the scope of the study. The analysis is limited to steady, laminar, single-phase, incompressible flow with uniformly dispersed nanoparticles. Particles are assumed to be in a state of agglomeration, and sedimentation, Brownian diffusion, and thermophoretic transfer are ignored. The medium is assumed to be porous, homogeneous and isotropic, and the induced magnetic field, Joule heating, Hall current, ion-slip effects and viscous dissipation are neglected to keep the problem mathematically tractable. In addition, only a few correlations for viscosity and thermal conductivity corresponding to certain nanoparticle morphologies are explored. Although the numerical solutions are validated against the RKF-45, experimental validation is beyond the scope of the present work. Thus, the results reported should be viewed under the adopted assumptions and parameter ranges.

Based on the proposed mathematical model, several future research directions are suggested to enhance the physical realism and engineering applicability of morphology-dependent N-F models. Future studies could include the effects of unsteady, three-dimensional, or turbulent flow, as well as other transfer phenomena (Brownian motion, thermophoresis, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, Hall current, and ion-slip effects). The effects of hybrid and multi-hybrid N-Fs, variable thermophysical properties, and more complex porous media models can also be analyzed to gain a broader perspective on the coupled transfer phenomena. The proposed morphology-dependent correlations could be experimentally validated to further establish their practical reliability. Furthermore, combining high-order numerical methods with advanced machine learning algorithms may enable fast prediction, optimization and real-time control of complex thermal systems, such as electronic cooling systems, renewable energy systems, microchannel heat exchangers and thermal energy storage systems.

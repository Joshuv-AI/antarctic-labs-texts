Beclin1 deficiency unlocks cardiac lineage commitment through convergent Wnt and BMP signaling activation
Authors: Lilin Xiang, Zhenhua Jia, Zhangyi Yu, Lenan Wang, Huanhuan Cai, Tong Zhang, Zhibing Lu, Li Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEmbryonic stem cell (ESC)-derived cardiomyocytes are a key resource for studying cardiac development and advancing regenerative therapies. Beclin1 (Becn1), a core regulator of autophagy and cardiac morphogenesis, has an undefined role in cardiomyocyte lineage specification. This study aims to investigate the regulatory function of Becn1 during cardiac differentiation from both mouse and human ESCs.MethodsMouse and human ESCs were differentiated into cardiomyocytes through established embryoid body (EB) formation or monolayer differentiation protocols. Stable Becn1 knockdown was achieved using short hairpin RNA (shRNA). Cardiomyocyte differentiation efficiency was evaluated by flow cytometry, immunocytochemistry, and contraction assays. Differentiation cardiomyocyte function was evaluated by sarcomere arrangement, calcium transients, and microelectrode array (MEA). Transcriptomic profiling was conducted by bulk RNA sequencing, and pathway dynamics were analyzed using qPCR and western blotting.ResultsBecn1 expression declined over the course of differentiation. Knockdown of Becn1 significantly enhanced cardiomyocyte yield and promoted earlier onset of contractile activity, accompanied by increased expression of cardiac-specific markers. Mechanistically, Becn1 deficiency elicited a biphasic Wnt signaling response, characterized by early activation during mesodermal induction followed by suppression at later stages of differentiation. This shift was accompanied by sustained BMP pathway activation. Notably, Becn1-deficient ESCs underwent efficient cardiac differentiation in the absence of exogenous VEGF or FGF, with BMP signaling compensating for their omission. These findings were recapitulated in human ESCs, where BECN1 knockdown supported Wnt modulators-independent cardiomyocyte differentiation through coordinated modulation of Wnt and BMP pathways.ConclusionsBecn1 serves as a negative regulator of cardiac lineage commitment by orchestrating stage-specific Wnt/BMP signaling dynamics. Silencing Becn1 enhances cardiomyocyte differentiation and enables growth factor-independent lineage progression. These findings offer a novel approach to improve the efficiency and scalability of stem cell-derived cardiomyocyte production for regenerative applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13287-025-04756-x.Keywords: Cardiomyocyte differentiation, Becn1, Embryonic stem cells, Wnt signaling pathway, BMP signaling pathway

Introduction

The adult mammalian heart has limited regenerative capacity due to the post-mitotic nature of cardiomyocytes, posing a significant challenge for cardiac repair following injury. The generation of functional cardiomyocytes from pluripotent stem cells, including embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), holds great potential for in vitro disease modelling and regenerative medicine applications [1]. However, incomplete understanding of the molecular switches governing cardiac lineage commitment continues to impede the efficiency of in vitro cardiomyocyte generation.

Cardiomyocyte differentiation from stem cells is a highly orchestrated process that involves sequential transitions through mesoderm and cardiac progenitor stages, regulated by multiple signaling pathways, including Wnt and BMP [2, 3]. Wnt signaling plays a biphasic role in cardiogenesis, promoting mesodermal specification in early stages while requiring inhibition at later stages for cardiac fate commitment [4, 5]. Meanwhile, BMP signaling directly activates cardiac transcription programs by inducing core cardiac regulators such as NKX2.5 and GATA4, while simultaneously suppressing alternative lineage fates during progenitor cell patterning [6, 7]. The dynamic interplay between Wnt and BMP signaling ensures proper cardiac fate determination. Despite significant progress in delineating these pathways, the upstream regulatory mechanisms that fine-tune their activity during cardiogenesis remain poorly understood. Elucidating these regulatory networks is critical for optimizing stem cell-based strategies for cardiac regeneration.

Becn1 is a mammalian ortholog of yeast Atg6 and plays essential roles in embryonic development, cell survival, and tissue homeostasis [8–10]. Homozygous deletion of the Becn1 gene results in early embryonic lethality, underscoring its fundamental role in development [11, 12]. While Becn1 is well recognized for its function in autophagosome formation, emerging evidence suggests additional autophagy-independent roles of Becn1 in cellular differentiation and signaling [13–15]. Our recent work unexpectedly discovered that Becn1 suppresses direct cardiac reprogramming in autophagy-independent but Wnt/β-catenin dependent manner [16]. However, its role in cardiomyocyte lineage specification from stem cells remains largely unexplored.

In this study, we identified Becn1 as a critical suppressor of cardiomyocyte differentiation. We demonstrated that Becn1 expression is progressively downregulated during cardiomyocyte differentiation from murine ESC. Functional studies revealed that Becn1 knockdown enhances mesoderm specification and promotes cardiomyocyte commitment, indicating that Becn1 acts as a negative regulator of cardiac differentiation. Mechanistically, we showed that Becn1 deficiency altered the dynamics of Wnt and BMP signaling, two fundamental pathways that drive cardiogenesis. These findings uncovered a previously unrecognized function of Becn1 in cardiac differentiation and suggest that targeting Becn1 may offer a novel strategy to enhance ESC-derived cardiomyocyte generation for regenerative applications.

Methods

Mouse embryonic stem cell culture and differentiation into cardiomyocytes

The E14 mESC line (ES-E14TG2a) was purchased from National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures (SCSP-204). mESC culture and cardiomyocyte differentiation were performed as we previously described [17, 18]. E14 mESC were maintained on 6-well plates coated with 0.1% gelatin in fetal bovine serum (FBS)-based medium supplemented with leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) (Sigma, ESG1106). The culture medium consisted of Glasgow Minimum Essential Medium (GMEM) (Gibco, 11710035) supplemented with 15% ES-quality FBS (Newzerum, FBS-AU500), 1% Non-Essential Amino Acids (NEAA) (Gibco, 1114050), 1% GlutaMAX (Gibco, 25030081), 1% β-Mercaptolethanol (BME) (Gibco, 21985023), 1000 U/ml LIF (Sigma, ESG1106), 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Hyclone, SV30010), CHIR99021 (GLPBIO, GC16702, 3 µM), PD0325901 (GLPBIO, GC10397, 1 µM). The medium was changed daily, and cells were passaged at a 1:20 ratio during routine subculture.

Cardiomyocyte differentiation was induced using the hanging-drop method in serum-free embryoid body (SFD EB) medium containing Iscove’s Modified Dulbecco Medium (IMDM) (HyClone, SH30228.01), 25% Ham’s F-12 Nutrient Mixture (Cellgro, 10-080-CV), 1% N-2 Supplement (Gibco, 17502048), 2% B27 Supplement (BasalMedia, S441J7), 1% NEAA (Gibco, 1114050), 0.2% Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA) (Sigma, A1933), 1% GlutaMAX (Gibco, 25030081), 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Hyclone, SV30010), 50 µg/mL Ascorbic Acid (Sigma, A8960), 0.45mM 1-Thioglycerol (MTG) (Sigma, M6145)). E14 mESCs at 80% confluence were dissociated with 0.05% Trypsin-EDTA at 37 °C for 2 min, resuspended in SFD EB medium at a concentration of 7.5 × 10⁴ cells/mL, and plated in suspension culture on non-attachable petri dishes for 48 h.

To induce mesodermal differentiation, embryoid bodies (EBs) were cultured in SFD EB medium supplemented with Activin A (5 ng/ml, R&D Systems, 338-AC) and BMP4 (0.3 ng/ml, R&D Systems, 314-BP), human vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) (5 ng/ml, R&D Systems, 233-FB) for an additional 48 h. On day 4, EBs were transferred to 0.1% gelatin-coated 96-well plates and cultured for 5 to 10 days to promote cardiogenesis. The cardiomyocyte culture medium consisted of StemPro-34 SF medium (SPF34, Solarbio, A8100), GlutaMAX (Gibco, 25030081, 100X), Ascorbic Acid (Sigma-Aldrich, A8960, 50 µg/ml), recombinant VEGF (R&D Systems, 233-FB, 5 ng/ml), recombinant human basic Fibroblast Growth Factor (FGF2) (R&D Systems; 345-FG, 10 ng/ml), and recombinant human Fibroblast Growth Factor 10 (FGF10) (Gibco, 25030081, 50 ng/ml). The medium was changed daily. A complete list of reagents used for mESC culture and cardiomyocyte differentiation is provided in Table S1.

Human embryonic stem cell (hESC) and human induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC) culture and differentiation into cardiomyocytes

The human embryonic stem cell (hESC) line H7 was originally obtained from the WiCell Research Institute under a specific Material Transfer Agreement. Human iPSC line WTC (Gladstone Institute, from fibroblast) was kindly gifted from Dr. Donghui Zhang’s Lab at Hubei University. Both cell lines were maintained in mTeSR1 medium (STEMCELL Technologies, Cat# 85851) and cultured according to previously published protocols [19, 20]. When cultures reached 80–90% confluency, cardiac differentiation was initiated (designated as Day 0) by switching to RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with B-27 without insulin (Gibco, A1895601). To induce mesoderm formation, CHIR99021 (MCE, HY-10182) was added at a final concentration of 10 µM for 48 h. On Day 3, the medium was changed to B-27 without insulin containing 5 µM IWR-1 (MCE, HY-12238) for an additional 48 h to inhibit Wnt signaling. From Day 5 onward, the culture medium was replaced every other day with fresh B-27 without insulin until further analysis.

Viral packaging and transduction

Human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293T cells (purchased from ATCC) were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Minimal Essential Medium (DMEM) (Gibco, 6123110) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Newzerum, FBS-AU500) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Hyclone, SV30010) as previously described [16, 21]. To produce lentiviruses, 293T cells were used and transfected with 10 µg viral constructs, 7 µg psPAX2, and 3 µg pMD2.G using Polyethylenimine (PEI) (Polysciences, 24765) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The viral containing medium was collected 48 and 72 h after transfection, followed by filtration through a 0.45 μm pore size filter (Merck Millipore) and incubation with PEG6000 solution (8% final concentration) overnight at 4℃. On the next day, viral particles were sedimented by centrifugation at 3800 rpm for 30 min at 4 ℃. Viruses were then re-suspended by 100 µL of DMEM medium per each 10 cm dish and were immediately added to the target cells or frozen at -80℃ until further usage. The cloning primers and oligos are listed in Table S2.

Calcium oscillation imaging

Calcium imaging was performed using Rhod-3 Calcium Imaging Kit (Invitrogen, R10145) according to manufacture instructions. After staining, 10 videos per well were obtained using Nikon ECLIPSE Ts2R fluorescence microscope. The videos were converted into a format readable for ImageJ using R studio. Then ImageJ and Matlab R2023 were used to trace calcium flux signals.

Microelectrode array

To characterize the electrophysiological properties of the differentiated CMs, an MEA recording system (Axion Biosystems MAESTRO PRO) was used. The MEAs have 24 × 16 microelectrodes, with a diameter of 50 μm. Cardiomyocytes on day 12 of differentiation from the mESCs, along with neonatal mouse cardiomyocytes were plated on gelatin-coated MEA culture plates. Standard measurements of field potential were performed at 20 kHz in culture medium at 37℃ Data acquisition and analysis were finished using AxIS Navigator software [22]. Electrode recordings were taken for 1 min.

Immunofluorescence staining

Immunofluorescent staining was performed as previously described [16, 21]. The following antibodies were used: α-actinin (Abcam; ab68167; 1:500), cTnI (Abcam; ab56357; 1:200), Alexa Fluor 488 affiniPure donkey anti-Rabbit IgG (H + L) (1:500; Jackson ImmunoResearch Inc), Alexa Fluor 488 affiniPure donkey anti-Goat IgG (H + L) (1:500; Jackson ImmunoResearch Inc). Images were acquired using Nikon ECLIPSE Ts2R fluorescence microscope or Leica Stellaris 5 WLL for high-magnification images.

Flow cytometry and data analysis

Differentiated cardiomyocytes on day 9 were harvested by 0.05% trypsin digestion at 37 °C for 5 min. Cells were fixed and permeabilized with Fixation Buffer (Biolegend, 420801), then incubated with primary antibody against α-actinin and corresponding fluorescent secondary antibodies. The following antibodies were used: α-actinin (Abcam; ab68167; 1:500), cTnT (Antgene; 130-119-575; 1:100), Alexa Fluor 488 affiniPure donkey anti-mouse IgG (H + L) (1:500; Jackson ImmunoResearch Inc). Cells were then analyzed on BD flow cytometer, and FlowJo software (version 10) was used to analyze data.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted with Trizol (TaKaRa, 9109). First-strand cDNAs were synthesized using the HiScript Ⅱ RT SuperMix (Vazyme, R222-01). RT-qPCR was performed using qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen, 11201ES08) on the Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR system. All mRNA levels were normalized to those of Gapdh. All primer sequences for RT-qPCR were provided in Table S3.

Western blots

Cells were collected and lysed in lysis buffer (50 mM Tis-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP40, 1 mM EDTA) with a complete phosphatase and protease inhibitor cocktail. After SDS-PAGE, proteins were transferred to a nitrocellulose filter membrane (Millipore) and probed with the indicated antibodies. The target proteins were detected by chemiluminescence. The membranes were stripped with stripping buffer (100 mM β-ME, 2% SDS, 62.4 mM Tris-HCl pH6.7) and re-probed with an antibody against a second protein or β-Actin for loading control. To measure the levels of secreted proteins in cells, the cells were treated with Brefeldin A solution (BFA, 1000X) for three hours and then were collected and lysed. To detect the protein content in the supernatant, the supernatant was collected and concentrated using trichloroacetic acid (TCA), and finally the samples were loaded for protein electrophoresis. Antibodies used for western blot are listed in Table S1.

RNA generation, sequencing and data analysis

In general, 1 ~ 2 × 106 mESC, EBs and cardiomyocytes (CMs) upon indicated treatments were collected in TRIzol (Takara) for RNA extraction. Two biological replicates were prepared for each sample. RNA quality was determined through A260/A280 evaluation using NanodropTM OneC spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc). The integrity of RNA was confirmed via 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis. Qualified RNAs were further quantified by Qubit3.0 with QubitTM RNA Broad Range Assay kit (Life Technologies, Q10210). cDNA library was prepared using Ribo-off rRNA depletion Kit (Ribobio, China) and KCTM Stranded mRNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina® (Wuhan Seqhealth, DR08402). The library products corresponding to 200–500 bps were enriched. Paired-end sequencing was conducted on an Illumina NovaSeq6000 with a read length of 150 bp. Raw sequencing data were first filtered by Trimmomatic (version 0.36). Low-quality reads were discarded and reads contaminated with adaptor sequences were trimmed. Subsequently, the clean reads were mapped to the reference genome of the mouse (mm10) using STAR software (version 2.5.3a) with default parameters. The counting of reads mapped to the exon regions of each gene was carried out by featureCounts (Subread-1.5.1; Bioconductor). The resulting BAM files were sorted and indexed using SAMtools.

Raw reads counts were analyzed with DESeq2 [23]. Genes with expression showing |log2 fold change| ≥ 1 and adjust P-value < 0.05 were recognized as differentially expressed genes. Heatmap of distance matrix were generated by DESeq2. GO analysis was performed using the DAVID functional annotation tool version 6.7 (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/). All GO terms shown in this study have P value lower than 0.05.

Time-series gene clustering

The Mfuzz R package [24] was applied to analyze dynamic expression patterns during the cardiomyocyte differentiation process. Genes with averaged FPKM < 1 in each group were excluded. The GO analysis of clustered modules was performed using the enrichCluster_gene function, and the heatmap was generated using the visCluster() function from the clusterGvis R package (version 0.1.0). Enriched GO terms with an adjusted P-value < 0.05 were selectively displayed.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All data were analyzed by Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA). All results were represented as mean ± SEM. The unpaired t-test was used to determine the significance of differences between two groups. Multiple comparisons were analyzed by two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni posttests. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data are representative of multiple repeated experiments.

Results

Becn1 deficiency enhances cardiomyocytes differentiation from mouse embryonic stem cells (mESC)

To investigate the role of Becn1 in heart development, we employed the hanging-drop method to differentiate cardiomyocytes from mouse embryonic stem cells (mESC). Embryoid bodies (EBs) were generated through suspension culture and subsequently induced to differentiate into spontaneously beating cardiomyocytes [17, 18] (Fig. 1A). Becn1 expression was examined at various stages of differentiation, revealing a progressive decrease in Becn1 mRNA levels as differentiation advanced (Fig. 1B), suggesting that Becn1 may act as a regulator of this process.

To assess the functional impact of Becn1 on cardiomyocyte differentiation, we performed loss-of-function assays using shRNAs targeting Becn1 which achieved approximate 60% knockdown efficiency. We then induced shBecn1 or non-targeting shNT as control group into mESC and differentiated these cells into cardiomyocytes following established protocols [17, 18] (Fig. 1C and D, S1A and S1B). Analyses from flow cytometry and immunocytochemistry (ICC) were conducted on day 10 of differentiation to assess the effects of Becn1 deficiency. Notably, Becn1 knockdown significantly increased the proportion of α-Actinin- and cTnT-positive cardiomyocytes (Fig. 1E and G). To evaluate quality of cardiomyocytes, we conducted a statistical analysis of the sarcomere length. The results demonstrated that Becn1 knockdown led to a well-organized sarcomere structure (Fig. 1H). Moreover, Becn1-deficient cardiomyocytes displayed spontaneous contraction at an earlier stage compared to controls (Fig. 1I). Functional analysis revealed a substantial increase in the number of cardiomyocytes exhibiting periodic calcium oscillations and spontaneous contractions in the shBecn1 group relative to the controls (Fig. 1J and L, Videos S1-S4). Also, to characterize the detailed physiological properties of Becn1-depleted CMs, we recorded the field potential of CMs by microelectrode array (MEA). We detected an elevated amplitude of field potential spikes and decreased frequency of electrical activity in Becn1-depleted CMs compared to control cells (Fig. 1M). These results together indicated that Becn1 knockdown enhances both the efficiency and functional quality of cardiomyocyte differentiation.

Becn1 suppresses mesoderm formation and cardiac lineage commitment

To further explore the role of Becn1 in cardiac lineage commitment during cardiomyocyte differentiation, we assessed cell morphology and expression of lineage-specific markers at various differentiation stages (Fig. 2A). Becn1 knockdown minimally impaired the ability of ES cells to form colonies, as evidenced by positive alkaline phosphatase staining (Fig. 2B). ICC for OCT4 revealed no significant differences in colony morphology or size between Becn1-knockdown and control ES cell lines (Fig. 2C). Additionally, time-course gene expression analysis by reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) showed that the expression of pluripotency markers, including Oct4 and Nanog, remained unchanged in Becn1-knockdown ES cells compared to controls (Fig. 2D). These results indicate that Becn1 knockdown minimally affects stem cell pluripotency.

At the embryoid body (EB) stage, when we analyzed the expression of germ layer-specific markers, no significant changes were observed in ectodermal (Sox1, Pax6) or endodermal (Sox17, Gata4) markers (Fig. 2E). Moreover, no significant differences in EB size or morphology were observed between the shBecn1 and control groups (Fig. 2F). However, we found that mesodermal markers, including Hand1, Lefty2, Aplnr, Mesp1, Brachyury and Tbx6, were significantly upregulated in the shBecn1 group at the EB stage (Fig. 2G). These results suggest that Becn1 knockdown shifts the differentiation trajectory toward the mesodermal lineage while minimally affecting ectodermal or endodermal fates.

Next, to assess the impact of Becn1 deficiency on cardiac lineage commitment, we evaluated the expression of cardiac progenitor and cardiomyocyte markers in differentiated cardiomyocytes. Consistent with above results, RT-qPCR analysis revealed significant upregulation of key cardiac genes including Tbx5, Tnnt2 and Myl7 in Becn1-deficient cells (Fig. 2H). These results together indicate that Becn1 inhibits cardiac lineage commitment.

Global transcriptomic analysis reveals Becn1 knockdown activates key cardiomyocyte differentiation pathways

To further investigate the molecular mechanisms by which Becn1 regulates cardiac lineage commitment, we performed bulk RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) on shNT- and shBecn1-cardiomyocytes at differentiation days 0, 4 and 10. Consistent with earlier findings, Becn1 knockdown significantly upregulated mesoderm markers (Hand1, Hand2, Brachyury, Hoxb1 and Mesp1), cardiac progenitors marker genes (Isl1, Tbx5, Irx4, Gata4) and cardiomyocyte marker genes (e.g., Tnnt2, Myl2, Myh6, Nppa and Ryr2), reinforcing the role of Becn1 as a negative regulator of cardiomyocyte differentiation (Fig. 3A and C).

We next performed clustering analyses using Mfuzz with all transcripts to depict the transcriptional dynamics across differentiation stages [24]. During the mESC stage, we observed significant activation of DNA replication and ribosome biogenesis pathways (C1 gene set), consistent with the high-fidelity DNA repair mechanisms required to maintain pluripotency [25, 26] (Fig. 3D). As mESC transitioned into EBs, genes in the C2 cluster were significantly enriched in chromatin remodeling, embryonic development, and translation pathways, reflecting the continuous cellular differentiation and functional specialization necessary for early lineage commitment. At later differentiation stages, genes in the C3 to C6 clusters were progressively enriched in organ morphogenesis and cell differentiation pathways, further highlighting the stage-specific transcriptional regulation of cardiomyocyte differentiation (Fig. 3D). While global transcriptomic features remained largely unchanged between shBecn1 and control groups during the mESC stage, significant differences emerged as differentiation progressed. Notably, at the EB stage, Becn1-deficient cells exhibited marked upregulation of Wnt signaling components, including Lef1, Lrp5 and Wnt5a (Fig. 3D and E). Given that LEF1 forms a transcriptional complex with β-catenin to drive Wnt target gene expression, and LRP5 acts as a key co-receptor in Wnt signal transduction [27–30], these findings suggest that Becn1 negatively regulates Wnt signaling during early mesodermal differentiation.

Gene Ontology (GO) and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) using differentially expressed genes (DEGs) at the EB stage further confirmed that Becn1 inhibition activated additional developmental signaling pathways, including canonical MAPK and Notch signaling, as well as heart morphogenesis-related processes (Fig. 3F and G). As differentiation progressed from cardiovascular progenitors to cardiomyocytes, genes in the C5 cluster exhibited sustained activation of heart development, BMP/SMAD signaling, and cardiac muscle-related pathways (Fig. 3D), with Becn1 knockdown further amplifying these effects. Functional enrichment analyses further demonstrated upregulation of key biological processes such as myoblast differentiation, sarcomeric contraction, and cardiac lineage specification in Becn1-deficient cells (Fig. 3H and J).

These findings collectively demonstrate that Becn1 deficiency enhances mesoderm formation and promotes cardiomyocyte differentiation by modulating key signaling pathways, including Wnt, BMP/SMAD, and heart development-related pathways.

Knockdown of Becn1 activates the Wnt and Bmp signaling pathways

Wnt signaling plays a biphasic role in cardiac development, acting as a key regulator at different stages. During early cardiac development, Wnt signaling promotes the proliferation and lineage specification of cardiac progenitor cells, whereas its inhibition disrupts mesoderm formation, a prerequisite for proper cardiac tissue patterning [31, 32]. Conversely, in later developmental stages, suppression of the Wnt pathway facilitates cardiac structural morphogenesis, with Wnt inhibitors shown to strongly enhance late-stage cardiomyocyte differentiation [5, 33, 34]. In parallel, the BMP signaling pathway orchestrates cardiomyocyte lineage specification and structural maturation during cardiac morphogenesis [6]. Our RNA-seq analysis revealed significant enrichment of Wnt and BMP signaling pathways in Becn1-knockdown cells at both the EB and CM stages, suggesting that Becn1 may regulate these pathways during cardiomyocyte differentiation.

To investigate the effect of Becn1 depletion on Wnt signaling, we examined key Wnt regulators at the EB stage. Positive Wnt pathway regulators, including Axin2, Lef1, Ppp3ca, and Rspo3 were significantly upregulated in shBecn1 group, whereas negative modulators (Cer1, Sfrp1, Sfrp2, and Sfrp4) were markedly downregulated (Fig. 4A and B). RT-qPCR analysis further confirmed biphasic Wnt pathway dynamics. In Becn1-knockdown cells, Wnt5b, Myc, Etv2 and Fzb10 were significantly upregulated from the mESC to EB stage, consistent with the role of Wnt signaling in mesoderm formation (Fig. 4C). However, these genes exhibited a progressive decline from the cardiovascular progenitor stage to the CM stage, aligning with the requirement for Wnt inhibition during terminal cardiomyocyte differentiation. Heatmap analysis reinforced this pattern, showing early-stage Wnt activation followed by downregulation in later stages (Fig. 4D). These findings suggest that Becn1 modulates Wnt signaling in a stage-dependent manner, acting as a repressor during early differentiation while allowing its inhibition at later stages.

Next, we examined Bmp2 expression across differentiation stages. Bmp2 levels gradually increased in Becn1-knockdown cells, peaking at the CM stage (Fig. 4E). RT-qPCR analysis further showed upregulation of Bmp pathway components (Bmp2, Bmp4, Bmpr1a, Bmpr2) at both EB and CM stages in shBecn1 cells (Fig. 4F). Western blot analysis confirmed elevated BMP2 protein levels in shBecn1 cells, with increased BMP2 detected during both EB formation (Fig. 4G) and in the culture supernatant (Fig. 4H). This trend persisted in differentiated cardiomyocytes at the CM stage (Fig. 4I), indicating sustained BMP pathway activation. To determine whether Becn1 directly regulates Bmp2 expression, cultures from day 10 mESC-derived CMs were transduced with shBecn1 or shNT controls (Fig. 4J). Western blot analysis showed a significant increase in BMP2 protein levels following Becn1 knockdown (Fig. 4K). Notably, similar results were obtained in neonatal mouse cardiomyocytes (NMCMs) (Fig. 4L and M), suggesting that Becn1 directly modulates Bmp2 expression to regulate BMP signaling.

Together, these findings indicate that Becn1 knockdown enhances Wnt signaling during mesoderm formation while promoting BMP signaling during cardiomyocyte differentiation.

Becn1 knockdown enhances cardiomyocyte differentiation in the absence of VEGF and FGF

Given the fact that the supplemented fibroblast growth factor (FGF) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) are known downstream targets of Wnt and BMP pathways to promote cardiomyocyte differentiation, we hypothesized that Becn1 deficiency might functionally compensate for VEGF/FGF signaling, enabling cardiomyocyte differentiation in their absence. To test this hypothesis, we removed VEGF, FGF2, and FGF10 from the differentiation medium between the EB and CM stages and assessed cardiomyocyte differentiation efficiency (Fig. 5A). Flow cytometry analysis revealed that Becn1 knockdown significantly increased the percentage of α-Actinin + CMs (Fig. 5B). Notably, knockdown of Becn1 accelerates the onset of spontaneous contractions in differentiated cardiomyocytes (Fig. 5C). By day 8 of differentiation, few spontaneously contracting cell clusters were observed in the control group, whereas the shBecn1 group exhibited robust contractions (Fig. 5D, Videos S5 and S6). Furthermore, by differentiation day 12, the number of beating areas was markedly increased in the shBecn1 group than in controls (Fig. 5E, Videos S7 and S8).

We next performed RNA-seq analysis to profile Becn1-mediated CM differentiation in the absence of growth factors. In the Becn1 knockdown group, we observed significant upregulation of heart development-related genes such as Hand1, Myh6, and Tnnt2 (Fig. 5F and G). Additionally, genes associated with BMP signaling including Bmp2, Bmp4, and Bmpr1a were significantly upregulated (Fig. 5F and H), whereas Wnt signaling components were downregulated (Fig. 5F and I), consistent with GSVA pathway activity scoring (Fig. 5J). GO analysis of DEGs induced by shBecn1 further confirmed enrichment in heart development, negative regulation of Wnt signaling, and BMP signaling pathways (Fig. 5K). Interestingly, pathways related to cellular responses to growth factors were also enriched in shBecn1 cells, suggesting a compensatory mechanism for VEGF and FGF signaling. Since VEGF and FGF activate multiple signaling pathways, including the MAPK cascade [35], we examined its activity in Becn1-knockdown cells. GO analysis revealed significant enrichment of the MAPK cascade in the shBecn1 group (Fig. 5K), suggesting that Becn1 knockdown may recapitulate aspects of VEGF/FGF signaling. GSEA analysis further revealed enrichment of myogenesis and actin filament bundle formation gene sets upon Becn1-knockdown treatment (Fig. 5L), indicating improved cardiomyocyte differentiation compared to control group.

We next compared gene expression profiles between Becn1-knockdown mESC-CMs generated with and without small molecule supplementation. Venn diagram analysis identified 674 commonly upregulated genes across both conditions (Fig. 5M). These genes were enriched in pathways related to heart development, cell fate commitment, and BMP signaling (Fig. 5N), further indicating that Becn1 regulates cardiomyocyte fate commitment through Wnt/BMP signaling, potentially bypassing the need for VEGF and FGF signaling.

Taken together, these results demonstrate that Becn1 knockdown promotes cardiomyocyte differentiation even in the absence of VEGF and FGF signaling, likely through activation of BMP signaling and repression of Wnt signaling. This suggests that Becn1 functions as a negative regulator of cardiomyocyte fate commitment and that its depletion can promote cardiac differentiation without requiring exogenous growth factor supplementation.

BECN1 knockdown promotes human cardiomyocyte differentiation via Wnt and BMP signaling pathway

To further investigate the role of BECN1 in human cardiomyocyte differentiation, we employed a biphasic induction protocol using CHIR99021 and IWR-1 to differentiate human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) into cardiomyocytes [19] (Fig. 6A). Loss-of-function assays were conducted using shRNAs targeting BECN1, achieving approximately 60% knockdown efficiency in hESCs (Fig. 6B and C, S1C and S1D). AP staining showed that BECN1 knockdown minimally impaired colony formation capacity (Fig. 6D). ICC analysis for OCT4 revealed minor differences in colony morphology and size between BECN1-knockdown and control hESCs (Fig. 6E). Additionally, RT-qPCR analysis revealed that the expression levels of pluripotency markers OCT4 and NANOG remained unchanged in BECN1 deficient hESCs compared to shNT controls (Fig. 6F), indicating that silencing BECN1 does not significantly affect hESC pluripotency.

By day 10 of differentiation, flow cytometry and ICC were conducted to assess the effects of BECN1 deficiency on CM generation. BECN1 knockdown significantly increased the proportion of α-Actinin- and cTnT-positive cardiomyocytes (Fig. 6G and H and S2). In line with these findings, RT-qPCR analyses demonstrated a significant upregulation of cardiomyocyte markers, including TNNT2, ACTN2, MYL2, HAND1, RYR2, and KCNJ2, in the shBECN1 group (Fig. 6I). Furthermore, we investigated the effect of BECN1 on cardiomyocyte differentiated from human induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC) (Figure S3A). We discovered that the proportion of α-Actinin-positive cardiomyocytes was significantly increased in the shBECN1 treated group (Figures S3B and S3C). Consistently, RT-qPCR results demonstrated that the expression levels of key cardiomyocyte genes such as TNNT2, ACTN2, and MYL2 were significantly upregulated by BECN1 deficiency (Figure S3D). These findings together indicate that inhibiting BECN1 promotion of cardiomyocyte differentiation.

We next examined the involvement of Wnt and BMP signaling pathways. RT-qPCR analysis showed that BECN1 deficiency triggered a biphasic Wnt signaling response similar to mESC differentiation and was accompanied by the continuous activation of the BMP signaling pathway (Fig. 6J and K). Western blot results further confirmed these observations, showing increased expression of α-Actinin on day 3 and day 10 of differentiation, along with accumulated in BMP2 protein in shBECN1 group (Fig. 6L and M). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that BECN1 depletion facilitates human cardiac differentiation by modulating coordinated Wnt and BMP signaling pathways.

BECN1 knockdown promotes human cardiomyocyte differentiation in the absence of Wnt modulators

To further investigate whether BECN1 regulates cardiomyocyte differentiation through Wnt and BMP signaling, we assessed differentiation efficiency in the absence of exogenous CHIR99021 and IWR-1 (canonical Wnt pathway agonist and inhibitor respectively [36–40]) (Fig. 7A). Under these conditions, BECN1 knockdown led to a significant upregulation of mesodermal markers (EOMES, TBXT and HOXB1) and cardiac progenitor markers (ISL1, TBX20 and IRX4) (Fig. 7B). ICC analysis on day 10 revealed a substantial increase in the proportion of cardiac troponin I (cTnI)-positive cardiomyocytes in the shBECN1 group compared to controls (Fig. 7C). Additionally, RT-qPCR showed significant upregulation of key cardiomyocyte markers, including TNNT2, MYL2, HAND1, RYR2, CACNA1C and KCNJ2, in the shBECN1 group (Fig. 7D), further indicating the enhanced cardiac differentiation in the absence of Wnt pathway modulators.

We next examined the Wnt and BMP signaling pathway activity. We found that key genes in the Wnt signaling pathway, such as WNT3, WNT3A, WNT5A, and ETV2, were upregulated at day 3 of differentiation, but significantly downregulated by day 10. Additionally, shBECN1 promoted these biphasic changes in the Wnt pathway (Fig. 7E and F). Concurrently, both RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses showed increased BMP2 expression at the transcript and protein levels in the shBECN1 group, corresponding with enhanced differentiation efficiency (Fig. 7G and H). Other components of the BMP pathway, including BMP4, BMPR2, and SMAD9, were also markedly upregulated (Fig. 7H).

Taken together, these results indicate that BECN1 knockdown promotes human cardiomyocyte differentiation even in the absence of exogenous Wnt modulators. This effect is associated with stage-specific modulation of Wnt signaling and sustained activation of BMP pathway components, highlighting BECN1 as a key regulator of cardiomyocyte lineage commitment.

Discussion

In this study, we identified Becn1 as a negative regulator of cardiomyocyte differentiation through its inhibition of mesoderm specification and modulation of Wnt/BMP signaling cascades. Becn1 knockdown significantly enhances cardiomyocyte differentiation, as evidenced by an increased percentage of α-Actinin-positive cells, earlier onset of spontaneous contractions, improved calcium oscillations, well-organized sarcomeres and improved electrical signals. These findings uncover an unexpected role of Becn1 in cardiac lineage commitment and provide mechanistic insights into its function during cardiogenesis.

Becn1, a mammalian ortholog of yeast Atg6 and a key component of the autophagy machinery, plays essential roles in embryonic development and tissue homeostasis [8–10]. Heterozygous Becn1 deletion protects against pressure overload–induced cardiac hypertrophy [41]. However, its role in heart development remains largely unexplored. Our study revealed a progressive downregulation of Becn1 during cardiomyocyte differentiation. Becn1 knockdown had minimal impact on pluripotency, and EB formation was only modestly affected. Previous studies have demonstrated that homozygous Becn1 deficiency results in early embryonic lethality, likely due to disrupted autophagy, and ESC from Becn1-null animals fail to form expanded cystic embryoid bodies (EBs) [11]. Compared to the complete loss of Becn1, our milder phenotype, is likely due to the partial knockdown (~ 60%), which may preserve sufficient autophagic activity to support early embryoid body development. Moreover, our study further revealed that Becn1 knockdown led to a significant upregulation of mesoderm markers, including Hand1 and Lefty2, suggesting that Becn1 inhibits mesoderm formation, an essential early step in cardiomyocyte differentiation. Furthermore, Becn1 depletion promoted the generation of functional cardiomyocytes with enhanced contractility and calcium handling, establishing Becn1 as a key negative regulator of cardiomyocyte differentiation.

Transcriptomic analysis further reveals that Becn1 deficiency modulates Wnt and BMP signaling pathways, both of which play essential roles in cardiac development. Wnt/β-catenin signaling exhibits a biphasic role during cardiogenesis [34, 42]. Early activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling during EB formation promotes mesoderm specification [43], whereas its subsequent downregulation is necessary for cardiac precursor cells to commit to the cardiomyocyte lineage by regulating key cardiac transcription factors such as GATA6 [44]. Consistently, our data showed that Wnt signaling is transiently activated during EB formation and subsequently suppressed upon Becn1 knockdown. Interestingly, our previous work on cardiac fibroblast reprogramming into induced cardiomyocytes (iCMs) also identified Becn1 depletion as a trigger for Wnt activation, as demonstrated by increased Lef1 expression and β-catenin nuclear translocation [16]. Interestingly, BECN1 can directly interact with β-catenin, a core effector of the Wnt signaling pathway, leading to our hypothesis that Becn1 knockdown may release β-catenin, promoting its nuclear translocation and transcriptional activation of downstream targets. Consistent with this, we observed upregulation of BMP2, a key factor in cardiomyocyte differentiation, suggesting that β-catenin-mediated Wnt activation may contribute to increased BMP2 expression. Together, these findings suggest a model in which Becn1 negatively regulates cardiomyocyte differentiation by restraining Wnt/β-catenin signaling and Bmp2 expression, although further mechanistic studies are needed to confirm the direct interactions.

In addition to Wnt, our study revealed that Becn1 knockdown sustains BMP signaling activation, further underscoring its importance in cardiac lineage commitment. BMPs, members of the TGF-β family, signal through type I and type II receptor complexes to activate downstream SMAD pathways [45]. Prior studies have demonstrated that BMPs promote cardiomyocyte differentiation via multiple mechanisms, including activation of p38-MAPK signaling and upregulation of cardiac master regulators such as Gata4, Tbx5 and Tbx20 [46]. Our findings indicate that BMP signaling is upregulated in Becn1-deficient cells at both the mesoderm and cardiac progenitor stages, with a notable increase in BMP2 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels. Additionally, Becn1 knockdown enhances BMP-mediated pathways, including SMAD signal transduction and Notch signaling, further reinforcing the role of Becn1 in modulating BMP-driven cardiogenesis. Importantly, even in the absence of exogenous growth factors or agonist known to promote cardiomyocyte differentiation, Becn1-deficient cells autonomously activate BMP and Wnt signaling, leading to enhanced mesoderm specification and cardiomyocyte generation.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrated that Becn1 deficiency enhances cardiomyocyte differentiation by promoting mesoderm formation, modulating the WNT signaling pathway in a stage-specific manner, and activating BMP signaling pathway. These findings may advance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms governing cardiomyocyte differentiation and offer promising insights for improving stem cell-based strategies for cardiac regeneration.

Graphs with large maximum forcing number
Qianqian Liu, Ajit A. Diwan, Heping Zhang

Abstract




Abstract
:
For a graph
G
with order
2n
and a perfect matching, let
f(G)
and
F(G)
denote the minimum and maximum forcing number of
G
respectively. Then
0\leq f(G)\leq F(G)\leq n-1
. Liu and Zhang
[
10
]
ever proposed a conjecture:
e(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{n-F(G)}
, where
e(G)
denotes the number of edges of
G
. In this paper we confirm this conjecture and obtain
F(G)\leq n-\frac{n^{2}}{e(G)}
.
If
F(G)=n-1
, Liu and Zhang
[
9
]
proved that any two perfect matchings of
G
can be obtained from each other by a series of matching switches along 4-cycles. If
G
is bipartite and
F(G)\geq n-k
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we show that any two perfect matchings of
G
can be obtained from each other by a series of matching switches along even cycles of length at most
2(k+1)
. Finally, we ask whether
f(G)\geq\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil-1
holds for such bipartite graphs
G
, and give positive answers for the cases
k=1,2
. Further we show all minimum forcing numbers of the bipartite graphs
G
of order
2n
and with

F(G)=n-2
form an integer interval
[\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,n-2]
.

Keywords
: Perfect matching; Maximum forcing number; Bipartite graph; Matching switch

AMS Subject Classification: 05C70, 05C35

1
Introduction

We consider only finite and simple graphs. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The
order
of
G
is the number of vertices in
G
.
A
matching

M
in
G
is an edge subset such that no two edges of
M
have an endvertex in common. The vertices incident to the edges of
M
are called
M
-
saturated
, and the others are called
M
-
unsaturated
.
A
perfect matching
of
G
is a matching that saturates every vertex.

Let
G
be a graph with a perfect matching
M
. A subset of
M
is called a
forcing set
of
M
in
G
if it is not contained in any other perfect matching of
G
. The smallest cardinality of a forcing set of
M
is called the
forcing number
of
M
, denoted by
f(G,M)
. The concept of forcing number was originally introduced by Klein and Randić
[
7
]
under the name of the innate degree of freedom, which plays an important role in resonance theory.
The
minimum
and
maximum

forcing numbers
of
G
is the minimum and maximum values of forcing numbers over all perfect matchings of
G
, denoted by
f(G)
and
F(G)
, respectively. Afshani et al.
[
2
]
showed that computing the minimum forcing number is NP-complete for bipartite graphs with the maximum degree 4. However the complexity of computing the maximum forcing number of a graph is unknown. For forcing numbers of perfect matchings of graphs and some related topics, readers may refer to two surveys
[
3
,
13
]
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
2n
and with a perfect matching. Then
0\leq f(G)\leq F(G)\leq n-1
.
Liu and Zhang
[
10
]
obtained a lower bound of
e(G)
by using maximum forcing number
F(G)
, where
e(G)
denotes the number of edges of
G
. To improve the bound they proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
10
]
Let
G
be a graph with order
2n
and a perfect matching. Then

e(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{n-F(G)},\text{ equivalently, }F(G)\leq n-\frac{n^{2}}{e(G)}.

For some special cases
F(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
F(G)=1,2
, the conjecture was already confirmed. In this paper we completely confirm this conjecture. Applying this bound on the
d
-dimensional hypercube
Q_{d}
, we obtain that
F(Q_{d})\leq(1-\frac{1}{d})2^{d-1}
; see Section 3.

Che and Chen
[
3
]
proposed a problem: How to characterize the graphs
G
with
f(G)=n-1
. For bipartite graphs, they
[
4
]
obtained that
f(G)=n-1
if and only if
G
is complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
. Moreover,
Liu and Zhang
[
9
]
solved the problem by obtaining the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
9
]
Let
G
be a graph of order
2n
. Then
f(G)=n-1
if and only if
G
is a complete multipartite graph with each partite set having size no more than
n
or a graph obtained by adding arbitrary additional edges in one partite set to complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
.

However, it is difficult to characterize the graphs
G
with order
2n
and
F(G)=n-1
, a larger class of graphs. Some interesting properties have been already revealed.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
9
]
If
G
is a graph of order
2n
and with
F(G)=n-1
, then the forcing spectrum (the set of forcing numbers of all perfect matchings) is continuous.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
9
]
All minimum forcing numbers of the graphs
G
with order
2n
and
F(G)=n-1
form an
integer interval
[\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,n-1]
.

To prove Theorem
1.3
they showed that any two perfect matchings of
G
can be obtained from each other by applying a series of matching switches along 4-cycles.
In Sections 4 and 5 of this paper, we consider all bipartite graphs
G
of order
2n
and with
F(G)\geq n-k
for
1\leq k\leq n
.
In section 4 we prove that any two perfect matchings of
G
can be obtained from each other by applying a series of matching switches along even cycles of length at most
2(k+1)
. In Section 5, we propose a problem: does
f(G)\geq\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil-1
always hold? We give positive answers for the cases
k=1,2
. Further we show all minimum forcing numbers of the bipartite graphs
G
of order
2n
and with

F(G)=n-2
form an integer interval
[\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,n-2]
.

2
Preliminary

In this section, we give some notations and prove an important lemma.

For sets
A
and
B
, the symmetric difference of
A
and
B
is defined as
A\oplus B:=(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
. For a perfect matching
M
of a graph
G
, a path or cycle is
M
-alternating
if its edges appear alternately in
M
and off
M
.
If
C
is an
M
-alternating cycle of length
2l
for
l\geq 2
, then the symmetric difference
M\oplus E(C)
, is called a
matching switch
on
M
along
C
of length
2l
(simply
matching
l
-switch
), to get another perfect matching of
G
.

The
degree
of a vertex
v
in
G
, denoted by
d_{G}(v)
, is the number of edges incident to
v
.
The minimum and maximum degree of
G
are denoted by
\delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)
, respectively. The
neighborhood
of
v
, denoted by
N_{G}(v)
, is the set of vertices adjacent to
v
. We denote a cycle with
l(\geq 3)
vertices by
C_{l}
. A graph
G
is called
C_{l}
-free
if
G
contains no induced subgraphs isomorphic to
C_{l}
. A
chord
of a cycle
C
is an edge not in
C
whose endvertices lie in
C
.

For a vertex subset
S
of
G
,
G[S]
denotes the subgraph of
G

induced by

S
whose vertex set is
S
and edge set consisting of all edges of
G
with both endvertices in
S
.
In a word,
G[S]
is the subgraph
G-V(G)\setminus S
, obtained from
G
by deleting all vertices in
V(G)\setminus S
and their incident edges.
For an edge subset
T
of
G
, we use
V(T)
to denote the set of all endvertices of edges in
T
.
For two vertex subsets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
of
G
, we denote by
E(V_{1},V_{2})
the set of edges of
G
with one endvertex in
V_{1}
and the other in
V_{2}
.

By the definition of a forcing set, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
2n
for
1\leq k\leq n
and with a perfect matching
M
. Then
f(G,M)\geq n-k
if and only if
G[V(T)]
contains an

M
-alternating cycle for any subset
T
of
M
with size at least
k+1
.

Proof.

The lemma is trivial for
n=k
. Let
n\geq k+1
. If
f(G,M)\geq n-k
, then
M\setminus T
is not a forcing set of
M
for any subset
T
of
M
with
|T|\geq k+1
. By the definition,
M\setminus T
is contained in at least two perfect matchings of
G
, and
G[V(T)]
has at least two perfect matchings. Thus
G[V(T)]
contains an
M
-alternating cycle. Conversely, for any subset
T
of
M
with size less than
n-k
,
|M\setminus T|\geq k+1
. By the assumption,
G[V(M\setminus T)]
contains an
M
-alternating cycle and has at least two perfect matchings. Thus
T
is not a forcing set of
M
, and
f(G,M)\geq n-k
.
∎

Let
G
be a bipartite graph of order
2n
and with
F(G)=n-1
. Then there is a perfect matching
M=\{u_{i}v_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}
of
G
with
f(G,M)=n-1
. By Lemma
2.1
,
G[\{u_{i},v_{i},u_{j},v_{j}\}]
contains an
M
-alternating cycle for
1\leq i<j\leq n
. That is to say,
\{u_{i}v_{j},v_{i}u_{j}\}\subseteq E(G)
. Thus,
G
is complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
. Hence, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph of order
2n
. Then
F(G)=n-1
if and only if
G
is complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
.

3
A solution to Conjecture
1.1

To confirm Conjecture
1.1
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
2n
and with a perfect matching. If
F(G)\geq n-k
where
1\leq k\leq n
, then
e(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{k}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a perfect matching of
G
with
f(G,M)=F(G)\geq n-k
.
We will proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, by Lemma
2.1
, the induced subgraph by all endvertices of any two edges of
M
contains an
M
-alternating cycle. That is, there are at least two edges not in
M
between the endvertices of the two edges of
M
. Thus,
\delta(G)\geq n
and
e(G)\geq n^{2}
.

For
k\geq 2
, let
w
be a vertex of
G
with the minimum degree
d
. Let

G^{\prime}:=G-N_{G}(w)\cup\{s|st\in M,t\in N_{G}(w)\},M^{\prime}=M\cap E(G^{\prime}),\text{ and }t_{d}=n-|M^{\prime}|.

Then
M^{\prime}
is a perfect matching of graph
G^{\prime}
that has
2(n-t_{d})
vertices, and
t_{d}
equals the number of edges in
M
incident with a vertex in
N(w)
. So
t_{d}\leq d
, and the equality holds if
G
is bipartite.

Next we consider the following two cases.
If
n-t_{d}\leq k-1
, then
t_{d}\geq n-k+1
. By Degree-Sum formula, we have

e(G)\geq nd\geq nt_{d}\geq n(n-k+1).

Since
n\geq k
and
k\geq 1
, we have
n-k\geq 0
and
1-\frac{1}{k}\geq 0
. Thus

\displaystyle n(n-k+1)-\frac{n^{2}}{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n(n-k+1-\frac{n}{k})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n[n(1-\frac{1}{k})-k(1-\frac{1}{k})]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n(n-k)(1-\frac{1}{k})\geq 0.

If
n-t_{d}\geq k
, we will prove that
f(G^{\prime},M^{\prime})\geq(n-t_{d})-(k-1)
. Let
S^{\prime}
be a subset of
M^{\prime}
with size
k
and
e
be an edge of
M
incident to
w
. By Lemma
2.1
,
G[V(S^{\prime}\cup\{e\})]
contains an
M
-alternating cycle. Since
w
is of degree 1 in
G[V(S^{\prime}\cup\{e\})]
,
e
is not contained in this
M
-alternating cycle. Thus this
M
-alternating cycle is entirely contained in
G^{\prime}[V(S^{\prime})]
. By Lemma
2.1
,
F(G^{\prime})\geq f(G^{\prime},M^{\prime})\geq(n-t_{d})-(k-1)
. By the induction hypothesis,
e(G^{\prime})\geq\frac{(n-t_{d})^{2}}{k-1}
. Since there are at least
dt_{d}
edges in
G
but not in
G^{\prime}
,

e(G)\geq dt_{d}+\frac{(n-t_{d})^{2}}{k-1}\geq t_{d}^{2}+\frac{(n-t_{d})^{2}}{k-1}=\frac{k}{k-1}t_{d}^{2}-\frac{2n}{k-1}t_{d}+\frac{n^{2}}{k-1},

which is a quadratic function of
t_{d}
that reaches the minimum value
\frac{n^{2}}{k}
at
t_{d}=\frac{n}{k}
.
∎

The following corollary shows that Conjecture
1.1
is true, which gives an upper bound of the maximum forcing number.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
2n
and with a perfect matching. Then

F(G)\leq n-\frac{n^{2}}{e(G)}.

Proof.

Since
G
has a perfect matching, we have
0\leq F(G)\leq n-1
. That is,
1\leq n-F(G)\leq n
. Letting
k:=n-F(G)
, we have
F(G)=n-k
. By Theorem
3.1
we have
e(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{k}=\frac{n^{2}}{n-F(G)}
. Equivalently, we can have that
F(G)\leq n-\frac{n^{2}}{e(G)}.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

The bounds in Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
are sharp. Let
n,k
be two positive integers such that
n\equiv 0
(mod
k
). Let
G^{\prime}
be a minimal graph of order
\frac{2n}{k}
and with
F(G^{\prime})=\frac{n}{k}-1
. Then
G^{\prime}
is
\frac{n}{k}
-regular by Lemma 2.3 (ii) in
[
9
]
. Let
G
be the disjoint union of
k
copies of
G^{\prime}
. Then
F(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}F(G^{\prime})=n-k
and
G
has exactly
\frac{n^{2}}{k}
edges. Especially, we can choose
G^{\prime}
as complete bipartite graph
K_{\frac{n}{k},\frac{n}{k}}.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a
r
-regular graph with a perfect matching. Then

F(G)\leq\frac{r-1}{2r}|V(G)|.

Proof.

Since
G
is
r
-regular,
e(G)=\frac{r|V(G)|}{2}
. By Corollary
3.2
, we have

F(G)\leq\frac{|V(G)|}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r})=\frac{r-1}{2r}|V(G)|.

∎

Let
Q_{d}
be a
d
-dimensional hypercube where
d\geq 1
. We can immediately obtain an upper bound of
F(Q_{d})
by Corollary
3.2
or
3.4
.

Corollary 3.5
.

For
d\geq 1
,

F(Q_{d})\leq(1-\frac{1}{d})2^{d-1}.

Alon ever showed that
F(Q_{d})>c2^{d-1}
for any constant
0<c<1
and sufficiently large
d
(see
[
11
]
). Using Alon’s method,
Adams et al.
[
1
]
obtained a lower bound in a more general sense as follows.

F(Q_{d})\geq(1-\frac{\log(2e)}{\log d})2^{d-1},d\geq 2.

Combining the above lower and upper bounds on
F(Q_{d})
we have

(1-\frac{\log(2e)}{\log d})2^{d-1}\leq F(Q_{d})\leq(1-\frac{1}{d})2^{d-1},d\geq 2,

which implies an asymptotic behavior for the maximum forcing number
F(Q_{d})
as follows.

Corollary 3.6
.

F(Q_{d})\sim 2^{d-1}
as
d
approaches to
\infty
.

4
Switches of perfect matchings

From this section we consider the bipartite graphs
G
of order
2n
and with
F(G)=n-k
where
1\leq k\leq n
. If
k=n
, then
F(G)=0
and
G
has a unique perfect matching. The following theorem is our main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph of order
2n
and with
F(G)\geq n-k
for
1\leq k\leq n
. Then any two perfect matchings of
G
can be obtained from each other by a finite sequence of matching 2-, 3-, …, or
(k+1)
-switches.

Proof.

We will prove this theorem by induction on
n
.
For
n=k
, we have
F(G)\geq 0
. If
F(G)=0
, then
G
has a unique perfect matching, and the result is trivial. Otherwise,
G
has at least two perfect matchings, say
M
and
M^{\prime}
. Then
M\oplus M^{\prime}
forms some disjoint
M
- (
M^{\prime}
-) alternating cycles of length at most
2n
. Switching along such
M^{\prime}
-alternating cycles on
M^{\prime}
, we obtain perfect matching
M
.

Next suppose
n\geq k+1
. Let
M=\{u_{i}v_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}
be a perfect matching of
G
with
f(G,M)\geq n-k
and let
M^{\prime}
be any other perfect matching of
G
. It suffices to prove that
M
can be obtained from
M^{\prime}
by a finite sequence of matching 2-, 3-,
\dots
, or
(k+1)
-switches.
Then we have the following claims.

Claim 1.
If
M\cap M^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, then
M
can be obtained from
M^{\prime}
by a finite sequence of matching 2-, 3-, …, or
(k+1)
-switches.

Suppose that
M\cap M^{\prime}
contains an edge
u_{i}v_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
.
Let
H=G-\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
and
M_{H}=M\setminus\{u_{i}v_{i}\}
,
M_{H}^{\prime}=M^{\prime}\setminus\{u_{i}v_{i}\}
. Then
H
has
2(n-1)
vertices,
M_{H}
and
M_{H}^{\prime}
are two distinct perfect matchings of
H
. We will prove that
f(H,M_{H})\geq(n-1)-k
. Let
T_{H}
be a forcing set of
M_{H}
. By the definition,
T_{H}
is contained in only perfect matching
M_{H}
of
H
. Hence,
T_{H}\cup\{u_{i}v_{i}\}
is contained in only perfect matching
M
of
G
. So
T_{H}\cup\{u_{i}v_{i}\}
is a forcing set of
M
. Since
|T_{H}\cup\{u_{i}v_{i}\}|\geq f(G,M)\geq n-k
, we have
|T_{H}|\geq n-k-1
. Thus,
F(H)\geq f(H,M_{H})\geq(n-1)-k
.
By the induction hypothesis,
M_{H}
can be obtained from
M_{H}^{\prime}
by repeatedly applying finite times of matching 2-, 3-, …,
(k+1)
-switches. Thus,
M
is also obtained from
M^{\prime}
by the same series of matching 2-, 3-, …,
(k+1)
-switches.

Claim 2.
If
M\oplus M^{\prime}
contains a cycle of length
2l
for some
2\leq l\leq k+1
, then
M
can be obtained from
M^{\prime}
by a finite sequence of matching 2-, 3-, …, or
(k+1)
-switches.

Let
C
be a cycle of length
2l
in
M\oplus M^{\prime}
. Switching along the
M^{\prime}
-alternating cycle
C
, we can obtain a perfect matching
M^{\prime\prime}
, which has at least
l
edges intersected with
M
. By
Claim 1,
M
can be obtained from
M^{\prime\prime}
by a finite sequence of matching 2-, 3-, …,
(k+1)
-switches. Since
M^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
M^{\prime}
by a matching
l
-switch,
M
can be obtained from
M^{\prime}
by repeatedly applying finite times of matching 2-, 3-, …,
(k+1)
-switches, and the claim holds.

By Claims 1 and 2, next we only consider the case when
M
and
M^{\prime}
are disjoint and every cycle in
M\oplus M^{\prime}
is of length at least
2(k+2)
.
Take a cycle
C=u_{0}v_{0}u_{1}v_{1}\dots u_{l-1}v_{l-1}u_{0}
in
M\oplus M^{\prime}
for some
l\geq k+2
such that
u_{i}v_{i}
is an edge in
M
and
v_{i}u_{i+1}
is
an edge in
M^{\prime}
for
0\leq i\leq l-1
where
i+1
is considered modulo
l
. We will prove that
C
has a chord. Taking a matching of
k+1
edges as
\{v_{1}u_{1},v_{0}u_{0},v_{l-1}u_{l-1},\dots,v_{l-(k-1)}u_{l-(k-1)}\}\subseteq M
, by Lemma
2.1
, the induced subgraph
G[\{v_{1},u_{1},v_{0},u_{0},v_{l-1},u_{l-1},\dots,v_{l-(k-1)},u_{l-(k-1)}\}]
contains an
M
-alternating cycle. Since the intersection of this induced subgraph and
C
forms an
M
-alternating path, we obtain that
C
has a chord, say
v_{i}u_{i+j}
, with the subscripts modulo
l
.

Fig. 1:
M
- and
M^{\prime}
-alternating cycle
C
with a chord
v_{1}u_{1+j}
.

Choose
i
and
j
so that
j
is minimum possible. Without loss of generality, we assume that
i=1
(see Fig.
1
). Since
v_{1}u_{1+j}
is a chord, we have
2\leq j\leq l-1
.
If
j\leq k+1
, then
v_{1}u_{2}v_{2}\dots u_{j}v_{j}u_{j+1}v_{1}
is an
M^{\prime}
-alternating cycle containing
j
edges of
M^{\prime}
. Switching along this cycle gives a perfect matching
M^{\prime\prime}
that has
j-1
edges in common with
M
. By Claim 1,

M
can be obtained from
M^{\prime\prime}
by a finite sequence of matching 2-, 3-, …, or
(k+1)
-switches. Since
M^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
M^{\prime}
by a matching
j
-switch, we obtain that
M
is obtained from
M^{\prime}
by a finite sequence of matching 2-, 3-, …, or
(k+1)
-switches.
Therefore we assume that
j\geq k+2\geq 3
.

Next by finding at least
j-k-1

M
-alternating cycles, we will show that
C
has at least
j-k-1
extra chords.
Consider the induced subgraph
G^{\prime}:=G[\{u_{3},v_{3},u_{4},v_{4},\dots,u_{j},v_{j}\}]
. In
G^{\prime}
,
v_{q}
(
3\leq q\leq j
) can be incident only to the chords of the form
v_{q}u_{m}
where
3\leq m<q
. Otherwise, there exists a chord
v_{q}u_{s}
where
q+2\leq s\leq j
. Note that
s-q\leq j-q\leq j-3<j
, which contradicts the choice of
i
and
j
. Taking a subset
\{u_{1}v_{1},u_{3}v_{3},\dots,u_{j}v_{j}\}
of

Matching switch is an important tool to study the forcing spectrum of a graph, which collects the forcing numbers of all perfect matchings of
G
. Afshani et al.
[
2
]
showed that a matching 2-switch on a perfect matching does not change the forcing number by more than 1. Theorem
1.3
was proved in this way.

For a plane elementary bipartite graph, Deng
[

]
obtained that a matching

. In fact, this result can be extended to general graphs.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
G
be a graph with a perfect matching
M
, and
C_{2l}
be an
M
-alternating cycle. Then
|f(G,M\oplus E(C_{2l}))-f(G,M)|\leq l-1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
E(C_{2l})\cap M=\{e_{1},e_{3},\dots,e_{2l-1}\}
and
E(C_{2l})\setminus M=\{e_{2},e_{4},\dots,e_{2l}\}
.
Let
S
be a minimum forcing set of
M
. Then
S\cap E(C_{2l})\neq\emptyset
and
G-V(S)
has a unique perfect matching. Let
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{e_{2},e_{4},\dots,e_{2l}\}\setminus\{e_{1},e_{3},\dots,e_{2l-1}\}
. Then
G-V(S^{\prime})
is an induced subgraph of
G-V(S)
, and thus
G-V(S^{\prime})
has a unique perfect matching. So
S^{\prime}
is a forcing set of the perfect matching
M\oplus E(C_{2l})
. Since
|S^{\prime}|=|S|+l-|S\cap E(C_{2l})|\leq|S|+l-1
, we have

f(G,M\oplus E(C_{2l}))\leq|S^{\prime}|\leq|S|+l-1=f(G,M)+l-1.

Hence
f(G,M\oplus E(C_{2l}))-f(G,M)\leq l-1
. When we convert
M\oplus E(C_{2l})
to
M
, we obtain that
f(G,M)-f(G,M\oplus E(C_{2l}))\leq l-1
. Thus, we obtain the required result.
∎

E(G)
. Let
G_{2}:=G[\{u_{\delta(G)+1},v_{\delta(G)+1},\dots,u_{n},v_{n}\}]
and
G_{1}:=G-V(G_{2})
. Then
G_{2}
is a complete bipartite graph.

Let
G^{\prime}
be an induced subgraph of
G
with a unique perfect matching
M^{\prime}
. Then
G^{\prime}
is a bipartite graph. We will prove that the order of
G^{\prime}
is at most
2\delta(G)+2
.
If
G^{\prime}
contains no vertices of
u_{\delta(G)+1}
,
u_{\delta(G)+2}
,
\dots
,
u_{n}
, then
G^{\prime}
contains at most
2\delta(G)
vertices. Otherwise, we suppose that the intersection of
\{u_{\delta(G)+1},u_{\delta(G)+2},\dots,u_{n}\}
(resp.
\{v_{\delta(G)+1},v_{\delta(G)+1},\dots,v_{n}\}
) and the set of all endvertices of edges in
M^{\prime}\cap E(V(G_{1}),V(G_{2}))
is denoted by
\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{b}\}
(resp.
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{w}\}
). Thus there are
w+b
edges in
M^{\prime}\cap E(V(G_{1}),V(G_{2}))
in total. Next we will find
b

M^{\prime}
-alternating paths in
G
separately starting with
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
, …,
y_{b}
and
w

M^{\prime}
-alternating paths separately starting with
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
, …,
x_{w}
and prove that all such paths ending up with some
M^{\prime}
-unsaturated vertices of
G_{1}
.

Since
y_{l}
is
M^{\prime}
-saturated, say
y_{l}v_{i}\in M^{\prime}
, we have
v_{i}\in V(G_{1})
. Along the edge
u_{i}v_{i}
, if
u_{i}
is also
M^{\prime}
-saturated and
u_{i}v_{j}\in M^{\prime}
. Then
v_{j}\neq x_{t}
for any
1\leq t\leq w
. Otherwise,
v_{j}
is some
x_{t}
. Then
y_{l}v_{i}u_{i}x_{t}y_{l}
is an
M^{\prime}
-alternating cycle, which contradicts that
M^{\prime}
is unique perfect matching of
G^{\prime}
. Hence
v_{j}\in V(G_{1})
. Continuing the process, we can find an
M^{\prime}
-alternating path ending up with an
M^{\prime}
-unsaturated vertex of
G_{1}

which is denoted by
\overline{y_{l}}
(see an example in Fig.
7
).

Fig. 7:
Illustration of
M^{\prime}
-alternating paths in the proof of Corollary
5.3
.

By a similar argument, we can find
w

M^{\prime}
-alternating paths starting with
x_{t}
and ending up with some
M^{\prime}
-unsaturated vertex
\overline{x_{t}}
of
G_{1}
for
1\leq t\leq w
. Hence
G_{1}
has at least
w+b
vertices not in
G^{\prime}
, and
G_{1}
contains at most
2\delta(G)-w-b
vertices of
G^{\prime}
. Since
G^{\prime}
contains no
M^{\prime}
-alternating cycles,
G_{2}
contains at most one edge of
M^{\prime}
and thus
G_{2}
contains at most
w+b+2
vertices of
G^{\prime}
. Thus,
G^{\prime}
has the order at most
2\delta(G)+2
.
By Lemma
5.2
, we have
f(G)\geq n-[\delta(G)+1]=n-1-\delta(G)
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

[
10
]

If
G
is a bipartite graph with a perfect matching, then
f(G)\geq\delta(G)-1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
By Corollary
5.3
and Lemma
5.4
, we have

f(G)\geq\text{max}\{\delta(G)-1,n-1-\delta(G)\}.

If
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
, then we have
\delta(G)-1\geq n-1-\delta(G)
and thus
f(G)\geq\delta(G)-1\geq\frac{n}{2}-1
. Otherwise, we have
\delta(G)-1<n-1-\delta(G)
and thus
f(G)\geq n-1-\delta(G)>\frac{n}{2}-1
. Thus, Theorem
5.1
holds.
\square

In general we propose the following problem.

Problem 5.5
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph of order
2n
and with
F(G)\geq n-k
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Does
f(G)\geq\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil-1
always hold?

We also find this lower bound can be attained.

Remark 5.6
.

For
n\equiv 1
(mod
k
), let
H
be a graph obtained from the disjoint union of
k-1
copies of
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}
and one copy of
K_{\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil,\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil}
and then adding a maximum matching between
A_{i}
and
B_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, where
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are the partite sets of the
i
-th complete bipartite graph and
B_{k+1}=B_{1}
. Then
F(H)=n-k
and
f(H)=\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil-1
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a perfect matching of
H
with
A_{i}
matched to
B_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Since any set of
k+1
edges in
M
contain two edges from the same complete bipartite graph and hence the subgraph induced by endvertices of such
k+1
edges contains an
M
-alternating cycle. By Lemma
2.1
, we have
F(H)\geq f(H,M)\geq n-k
. Next we prove that
f(H,F)\leq n-k
for any perfect matching
F
of
H
. Let
T=\cup_{i=1}^{k}E(A_{i},B_{i+1})
. If
F\cap T=\emptyset
, then let
S:=\cup_{i=1}^{k}\{e_{i}\}
, where
e_{i}\in F
is one edge from
i
’th copy of
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
, and
e_{k}\in F
is the unique edge in
K_{\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil,\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil}
such that it is not adjacent to any edges of
E(A_{k},B_{1})
. Then
F\setminus S
is a forcing set of
F
, and
f(H,F)\leq|F\setminus S|=n-k
. If
F\cap T\neq\emptyset
, then
F\cap E(A_{i},B_{i+1})\neq\emptyset
for
1\leq i\leq k
since each complete bipartite graph is of even order. Let
e^{\prime}_{i}
be an edge of
F\cap E(A_{i},B_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
and
e^{\prime}_{k}\in F
is an edge of
K_{\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil,\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil}
. Let
S^{\prime}:=\cup_{i=1}^{k}\{e^{\prime}_{i}\}
. Then
F\setminus S^{\prime}
is a forcing set of
F
, and
f(H,F)\leq|F\setminus S^{\prime}|=n-k
. By the arbitrariness of
F
, we have
F(H)\leq n-k
.

The union of these maximum matchings saturates all vertices but two of
K_{\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil,\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil}
. Since the two unsaturated vertices are adjacent, these maximum matchings can be extended to a perfect matching
M^{\prime}
of
H
. Since fixing the edges between
A_{1}
and
B_{2}
fixes the perfect matching
M^{\prime}
, we have
f(H)\leq f(H,M^{\prime})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil-1
.
Note that
\delta(H)=\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil
. By Lemma
5.4
,
f(H)\geq\delta(H)-1=\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil-1
. Therefore,
f(H)=\lceil\frac{n}{k}\rceil-1
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

For non-bipartite graphs the lower bound in Problem
5.5
is too larger. Take graphs
H_{l}
of order
2n
and with
F(H_{l})=n-1
in
[
9
]
for example where
1\leq l\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. By Lemma 5.6 in
[
9
]
, we obtain that
f(H_{l})=n-l-1\leq n-2<n-1
.

Finally we discuss the distribution of minimum forcing number for such classes of bipartite graphs. For integers
0\leq l\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
and
n\geq 2
, let
G_{l}
be a bipartite graph with a perfect matching
M_{s}=\{u_{i}v_{i}|i=1,2,\dots,n\}
such that
G^{1}=G_{l}[\{u_{i},v_{i}|i=1,2,\dots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l\}]
and
G^{2}=G_{l}-V(G^{1})
are two complete bipartite graphs, and the edges between
G^{1}
and
G^{2}
are as follows (
x=2l+3
if
n
is odd and
x=2l+2
otherwise) (see Fig.
8
):

\{u_{x}v_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+1},v_{x}u_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+1},u_{x+1}v_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+2},v_{x+1}u_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+2},\dots,u_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l}v_{n-1},v_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l}u_{n-1}\}.

Then
G_{l}
is a bipartite graph with
F(G)=n-2
.

Examples of
G_{l}
where
n=7
and
l=0
(left) and 1 (right).

Lemma 5.8
.

For
0\leq l\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
, we have
f(G_{l})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l-1
.

Proof.

Note that the degree of
u_{n}
is
n-(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-l
. Since
u_{n}
is a vertex of
G_{l}
with the minimum degree,
\delta(G_{l})=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-l
. By Corollary
5.3
, we have

f(G_{l})\geq n-1-\delta(G_{l})=n-1-\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+l=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l-1.

Let
M=E(V(G^{1}),V(G^{2}))\cup\{u_{1}v_{1},\dots,u_{x-1}v_{x-1},u_{n}v_{n}\}
(shown in Fig.
8
where red lines form
M
). Then
M
is a perfect matching of
G_{l}
.
Since

G_{l}[\{u_{1},v_{1},u_{x},v_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+1},u_{x+1},v_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+2},\dots,u_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l},v_{n-1},u_{n},v_{n}\}]

has a unique perfect matching,
\{u_{2}v_{2},\dots,u_{x-1}v_{x-1},v_{x}u_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+1},v_{x+1}u_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l+2},\dots,v_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l}u_{n-1}\}
is a forcing set of
M
. So we have
f(G_{l})\leq f(G_{l},M)\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+l-1
.
∎

By Lemma
5.8
, we can obtain the following result immediately.

Corollary 5.9
.

All minimum forcing numbers of the bipartite graphs
G
of order
2n
and with
F(G)=n-2
form an integer interval
[\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1,n-2]
.

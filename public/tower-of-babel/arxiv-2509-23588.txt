Non-local integrals of motion for deformed W W -algebra 𝒲 q , t ​ ( 𝔤 ) {\mathcal{W}}_{q,t}({\mathfrak{g}}) associated with 𝔤 = A l ( 1 ) , D l ( 1 ) , E 6 , 7 , 8 ( 1 ) {\mathfrak{g}}=A_{l}^{(1)},D_{l}^{(1)},E_{6,7,8}^{(1)}
Michio Jimbo and Takeo Kojima

Abstract
We present an infinite set of non-local integrals of motion for the deformed W W -algebra 𝒲 q , t ​ ( 𝔤 ) {\mathcal{W}}_{q,t}({\mathfrak{g}}) associated with the affine Lie algebras 𝔤 = A l ( 1 ) {\mathfrak{g}}=A_{l}^{(1)} , D l ( 1 ) D_{l}^{(1)} and E 6 , 7 , 8 ( 1 ) E_{6,7,8}^{(1)} . They can be regarded as a two-parameter deformation of trace of the monodromy matrix of the 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} -KdV theory. Commutativity of the non-local integrals of motion is shown in the case of 𝔤 = A l ( 1 ) {\mathfrak{g}}=A_{l}^{(1)} and D l ( 1 ) D_{l}^{(1)} by a direct calculation. In the case of 𝔤 = E 6 , 7 , 8 ( 1 ) {\mathfrak{g}}=E_{6,7,8}^{(1)} it is a conjecture.

Introduction

First let us recall the construction of integrals of motion
in soliton theory, taking the KdV equation as an example.
The KdV equation can be written in the Poisson bracket form

Gardner
;
Faddeev-Zakharov

\displaystyle u_{t}=\{u,H\}=6uu_{x}+u_{xxx},~~~H=\int u(x)^{2}dx.

Here the Poisson bracket is given by

\displaystyle\{u(x),u(y)\}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{3}+2u(x)\partial_{x}+u_{x}(x)\right)\delta(x-y).

This defines a Poisson algebra
called the classical
W
-algebra
{\mathcal{W}}(A_{1}^{(1)})

(aka classical Virasoro algebra).

The KdV equation has the Lax representation
Lax1

in terms of
L=\partial_{x}^{2}+u
and

M=4\partial_{x}^{3}+6u\partial_{x}+3u_{x}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}L=[M,L]=6uu_{x}+u_{xxx}.

Let
u(x,t)

be a solution to the KdV equation satisfying the
periodic boundary condition
u(x+2\pi,t)=u(x,t)
.
Let
\psi_{1}(x,t),\psi_{2}(x,t)
be two linearly independent solutions of the differential equation:

L\psi_{j}(x,t)=\lambda\psi_{j}(x,t)
.
Introduce the transfer matrix

\displaystyle{\bf T}^{(cl)}(\lambda)={\rm tr}\left({\mathbf{M}}^{(cl)}(\lambda)\right),

where
{\bf M}^{(cl)}(\lambda)
is
the monodromy matrix of solutions

\psi_{1}(x,t),\psi_{2}(x,t)
:

\displaystyle(\psi_{1}(x+2\pi,t),\psi_{2}(x+2\pi,t))=(\psi_{1}(x,t),\psi_{2}(x,t)){\mathbf{M}}^{(cl)}(\lambda).

The classical inverse scattering method

Lax2
;
Date-Tanaka
;
Marchenko
;
Faddeev-Takhtajan

yields

\displaystyle\{{\bf T}^{(cl)}(\lambda),{\bf T}^{(cl)}(\mu)\}=0,~~~\partial_{t}{\bf T}^{(cl)}(\lambda)=0.

Hence the Taylor coefficients of

{\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}^{(cl)}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathbf{G}}_{n}^{(cl)}\lambda^{n}}

are independent of
t

and are mutually in involution,

\displaystyle\{{\mathbf{G}}_{m}^{(cl)},{\mathbf{G}}_{n}^{(cl)}\}=0,~~~\partial_{t}{\mathbf{G}}_{n}^{(cl)}=0,~~~m,n=1,2,3,\ldots.

We call

5
Discussions

Commutativity of the non-local integrals of motion
is shown by reducing them to
theta function identities.
In the case of
{\mathfrak{g}}=A_{l}^{(1)}
and
D_{l}^{(1)}
,
these theta function identities were shown by mathematical induction
using the standard
method of complex analysis focusing on poles and zeros.
However, the number of poles and zeros is insufficient to show
the theta identity corresponding to
{\mathfrak{g}}=E_{6,7,8}^{(1)}
in an analogous way.

In the case of
{\mathfrak{g}}=A_{l}^{(1)}
,
recently it has become clear that the non-local integrals of motion
can be constructed by trace of the universal-
R
of the quantum toroidal algebra

Feigin-Jimbo-Mukhin2
.
According to this construction, commutativity of the non-local integrals of motion follows directly
from the Yang-Baxter equation.
The existence of universal-
R

for the quantum toroidal algebra of type
D_{l}^{(1)}
is still an open problem.
We have constructed the non-local integrals of motion for
{\mathcal{W}}_{q,t}(D_{l}^{(1)})
.
Hence, it is expected that universal-
R
for the quantum toroidal algebras of type
D_{l}^{(1)}
exists.

If we give bosonic operators of type
{\mathfrak{g}}=B_{l}^{(1)}
and
C_{l}^{(1)}

using the same rules as in the definition (
9
), they are not mutually commutative.
We need something new to extend to type
{\mathfrak{g}}=B_{l}^{(1)}
and
C_{l}^{(1)}
.
When
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a
simply-laced affine Lie algebra, we have obtained a unified perspective on
the free field construction
of the non-local integrals of motion.

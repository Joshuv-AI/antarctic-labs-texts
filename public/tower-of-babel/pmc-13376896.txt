Deviatoric stress evolution and time step control technology of retreat roadway surrounding rock in fully mechanized working face
Authors: Zhiqiang Wang, Tingzhao Li, Lu Lin, Xinyu An, Haoqi Zhao, Xinsheng Geng, Jiashu He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The distribution characteristics of the deviatoric stress tensor (DST) field in the surrounding rock of the end-mining retreat roadway (ERR) are intricate, exerting a substantial influence on the stability of the ERR’s surrounding rock. Taking the fully mechanized working face and its retreat roadway in Wutong Coal Mine as the engineering backdrop, initially, a numerical model of the DST in the end-mining coal pillar (ECP) was established to analyze its evolutionary pattern. Based on the response surface methodology (RSM) model, the significance of each influencing factor was examined. Subsequently, the three dimensional stress expression above the ECP was deduced, and the invariants and distortion energy (DE) of the DST at any position within the ECP were ascertained. Then, the expressions for the cutting height and cutting angle were derived, and a novel time step control technology (TSCT) for the ERR’s surrounding rock was put forward, based on the real-time evolution characteristics of DST, dynamic matching of support strategies and roof cutting parameters is achieved through two stages of passive reinforcement and active roof cutting to achieve collaborative control. Compared with traditional static design or single time step methods, this technology reduces the deformation rate of surrounding rock by about 84% through step-by-step and timely application of support and cutting. Ultimately, according to the field measured data, it was demonstrated that this technology can effectively mitigate the deformation of the ERR’s surrounding rock.

Introduction

The stop line of the working face, also known as the end-mining line, is the position where the fully mechanized mining face stops mining. At this position, the support of the working face is about to be retreated, and a retreat roadway needs to be excavated in advance to retreat the support. The working face and retreat roadway together form a coal pillar area1. Due to the influence of both the working face and the retreat roadway, this coal pillar is recorded as a ‘end-mining coal pillar’ (ECP). The stress of coal pillars is mainly controlled by four factors: the ratio of mine scale to mining depth, the ratio of mining width to coal pillar width, the elastic modulus of the covering layer, and the mining depth2. The widened coal pillars during mining have gone through the stages of fully stable coal pillars, partially stable coal pillars, critical coal pillars, and yielding coal pillars3. This study investigates the stability evolution of the ECP and the support of the retreat roadway under the joint interference of mining and retreat roadway excavation in the fully mechanized mining face.

In the entire life cycle of a fully-mechanized mining face, determining the stability of the ECP at different mining cycles is a crucial step to advance the cycle. Many scholars have conducted extensive and in-depth research on various issues, which are related to the stability evolution characteristics of the ECP. Yu et al.4 proposed a numerical simulation method coupling water intrusion with a quantitative index of coal pillar damage rate, revealing the progressive failure mechanism of coal pillars under water immersion during mining. Zhang et al.5 defined a boundary distinguishing between progressive and dynamic failure modes. When a pillar transitions from infinite stability to failure, a lower stress level leads to progressive failure if the pillar can no longer maintain long-term stability. In contrast, a sudden unstable failure occurs when the pillar’s strength further decreases and fails. Wang et al.6 summarized the principle of yield mining at the end-mining stage and its applicability based on periodic weighting behavior of the working face. Gu et al.7 determined the critical stable width of coal pillars and the sensitivity of geological and engineering factors, identified the optimal location for yield mining. Li et al.8,9 established the width of protective coal pillars between pre-driven recovery openings based on the collapse arch theory and validated their mechanical response characteristics using numerical models. Wang et al.10 proposed a critical damage assessment coefficient for coal pillar stability, providing a theoretical basis for determining yield mining locations. Wang et al.11modified the support stress function using a power law fitting method, theoretically analyzed coal pillar stability based on the main roof fracture location, and derived an expression for the ultimate width of remaining coal pillars. Yu12. theoretically analyzed the influence of main roof fracture location on surrounding rock of recovery openings and evaluated the difficulty of support retreat. Wang et al.13 theoretically analyzed roof subsidence in recovery openings under different main roof fracture locations based on energy conservation theory and discussed optimal fracture positions. The above research results provide some theoretical references for further analyzing the evolution mechanism of deviatoric stress in the ECP.

In the entire life cycle of a fully-mechanized mining face, strengthening the support of the retreat roadway is an important step to end the cycle. Many scholars have conducted extensive and in-depth research on various issues, which are related to the stability evolution characteristics of the retreat roadway. Xu et al.14 established a roof fracture mechanical model, revealing the influence of the immediate roof thickness, cutting height, cutting angle, and main roof thickness on roof fracturing. Jiang et al.15 elucidated the impact of the roof-cutting angle on surrounding rock control, determined a reasonable design range for the presplitting roof-cutting angle. Zhang et al.16–18 proposed a directional single-fracture technique for roof-cutting, which considers rock properties. This technique can effectively reduce deformation and enhance roadway stability. Gao et al.19 introduced a comprehensive roof-cutting technology, identified the key collapse parameters and established a calculation method for the energy released during main roof caving. Jiang et al.20,21 proposed a method for pressure release via roof-cutting and energy absorption. This approach maintains the roadway in a low-stress state by interrupting the stress transmission path of the overlying roof strata. Chen et al.22 demonstrated that roof-cutting alters the fracture structure of the overlying strata and significantly reduces its ultimate deflection and bending moment values. Huang et al.23 demonstrated that roof-cutting technique can disrupt the cantilever beam structure of the main roof, isolate stress transfer between rock masses, and improve the stability of goaf-side entries. He et al.24 proposed the gob-side entry retaining technique by roof-cutting and pressure relief based on the short cantilever beam theory. Gao et al.25 investigated the effect of presplitting roof-cutting on waste rock caving in the goaf. Results show that presplitting roof-cutting helps reduce the stable distance of waste rock, accelerates roof caving speed, and decreases both the lag cavity space and stable distance. Wang et al.26,27 identified the overlying strata movement law surrounding a self-formed roadway using the roof-cutting technique. The above research results indicate that directional roof-cutting technology is indeed beneficial for improving the stability of roadway surrounding rock in retreat roadways.

In summary, previous studies have achieved rich results in the control of surrounding rock in retreat channels, but there are still research gaps as follows: (1) Existing research mostly focuses on the influence of a single factor on the stability of ECP or ERR, and has not systematically revealed the evolution mechanism of DST under the synergistic effect of ECP-ERR; (2) At present, there is a lack of rock control technology based on time step dynamic matching, that is, the collaborative design of “time step control” and “key support technology” has not yet been carried out, making it difficult to adapt to the dynamic changes in stress and deformation during the retraction process. Therefore, this paper uses the relevant data of the fully mechanized working face and retreat roadway of Wutong Coal Mine as the engineering background. Based on the engineering background of the fully mechanized working face and its retreat roadway in Wutong Coal Mine, firstly, the numerical model of the DST in the ECP was constructed to analyze the evolution law. Based on the RSM model, the significance of each influencing factor was analyzed. Secondly, the three dimensional stress expression above the ECP was derived, and the invariants and the DE of the DST at any position in the ECP were determined. Then the expressions of the cutting height and cutting angle were derived, and a novel TSCT for the surrounding rock of the ERR was proposed. Finally, based on the field measured data, it was presented that the technology could effectively reduce the deformation of the surrounding rock of the ERR.

Methods and results

The DST is the residual part of the stress tensor after deducting hydrostatic pressure, which describes the stress components that cause changes (distortions) in the shape of the rock. The yielding and failure of rocks are mainly controlled by the DST rather than hydrostatic pressure. Therefore, this article uses the DST as an indicator to analyze the stability of surrounding rock. To explore the deformation and failure laws of the surrounding rock in the ERR, and provide guidance for the support scheme and parameters of the roadway surrounding rock.

Numerical simulation method

Establishment of numerical model

Using FLAC3D software to establish a new model at the scale of the mining area, as illustrated in Fig. 3, with a size of 100 m×50 m. Due to the numerous factors affecting the DST in the mining area, to simplify the simulation process, the following assumptions are made for the numerical model.

(Ⅰ) The basic top was fractured above the solid coal on the left side of the roadway.

(Ⅱ) The resistance of roadway and working face support was neglected;.

(Ⅲ) The damage effects caused by the mining of the ECP were considered.

(Ⅳ) The compaction effect of goaf gangue was considered28.

The Mohr Coulomb yield criterion was selected for the model except for the goaf. The displacement at the bottom of the model was fixed, and the displacement was restricted around it. A self weight stress of 10.8 MPa was applied to the overlying rock at the upper free boundary.

Simulation results and analysis

Evolution characteristics of the SDST

As illustrated in Fig. 4, when the width of the ECP is 12.5–20 m, the peak stress gradually increases, and the peak stress point shifts from the working face side to the retreat roadway side. When the width of the ECP is 5–12.5 m, the peak stress reaches the yield limit and shows a rapid decreasing trend, with the stress peak point shifting towards the solid coal side of the retreat roadway.

Evolution characteristics of the TDST

As illustrated in Fig. 5, the ECP of 20 m converges to the ERR and working face under stress driving, resulting in tensile deformation; The central of ECP is restricted by both shallow and deep coal bodies in both directions, therefore mainly affected by compressive deformation under overlying loads. Meanwhile, the deep coal body is mainly affected by planar deformation due to minimal influence from the ERR, goafs, and overlying rock structures. As the width of the ECP decreases, the influence of the overlying rock structure on the ECP is further enhanced. The ECP of 5 m is severely damaged and bears a relatively small load. Under stress driving, the ECP is mainly affected by fragmentation and expansion deformation due to crack expansion. The ECP reaches the convergence limit and is only limited by shallow fragmentation and expansion, so compression deformation is the main mode.

Evolution characteristics of the DE

As illustrated in Fig. 6, when the width of the ECP is 20 m, the DE shows a humped distribution, indicating the existence of an elastic zone in the middle of the ECP. The elastic zone on the goaf side and the ERR side can both bear large loads. When the width of the ECP is between 12.5 and 20 m, the DE shows a unimodal distribution. As the width of the ECP decreases, the peak value of the DE in the ECP continuously decreases, while the DE in the solid coal side increases. This indicates that as the width of the ECP decreases, the load-bearing capacity of the ECP continuously weakens, the applied force decreases, and the DE in the middle of the ECP rapidly decreases. When the width of the ECP is 5 m, the DE in the ECP rapidly decreases, indicating that the ECP has suffered serious damage and the load-bearing stress has been greatly reduced, resulting in the inability of the ECP to bear the load. The load is transferred to the solid coal side and the collapsed gangue in the goaf, causing the solid coal side to bear a large force, resulting in significant distortion of the solid coal side and accumulation of a large amount of DE, while the collapsed gangue in the goaf bears a small force, resulting in small distortion of the gangue and accumulation of a small amount of DE.

RSM model analysis of DST

Establishment of RSM model

Using the Box-Behnken Design module in Design Expert software32, statistical analysis was conducted on the numerical calculation results of 13 sets of experiments. This article uses response surface methodology (RSM) to establish the mathematical relationship between coal seam thickness (M), roadway width (D), and coal pillar width (B) and the SDST. The reasons are as follows: (Ⅰ) Numerical simulations show that DST exhibits a non monotonic change of first increasing and then decreasing with the width of the coal pillar, requiring a quadratic model to capture the curvature; (Ⅱ) The central composite design of RSM can cover the factor space with 17 simulated operating conditions, far less than the 27 operating conditions of the full factor design.

Influencing factors analysis

The coupling effect of coal seam thickness and roadway width on the DST in the ECP is illustrated in Fig. 7. The DST shows a discernible trend of alteration with factors.

According to Fig. 7(a) and Fig. 7(b), under the coupling effect of coal seam thickness and roadway width, The DST shows a trend of first decreasing and then increasing with the increase of coal seam thickness. When the coal seam thickness is small (< 3.1 m), The DST slightly decreases with the increase of coal seam thickness; When the coal seam thickness is large (> 3.1 m), The DST gradually increases with the increase of coal seam thickness. The DST shows a trend of first decreasing and then increasing with the increase of roadway width. When the roadway width is small (< 4.1 m), The DST slightly decreases with the increase of roadway width; When the width of the roadway is large (> 4.1 m), The DST gradually increases with the increase of roadway width.

The coupling effect of coal seam thickness and coal pillar width on the DST in the ECP is illustrated in Fig. 8(a) and Fig. 8(b), a larger coal pillar width is beneficial for improving the DST, while as the coal pillar width decreases, the DST shows a trend of first increasing and then decreasing. When the coal pillar width is 15 m and the coal thickness is 3.1 m, the optimal DST is presented.

The coupling effect of roadway width and coal pillar width on the DST in the ECP is illustrated in Fig. 9(a) and Fig. 9(b), they further confirm the dominant role of coal pillar width in controlling the DST. Under the same roadway width conditions, as the coal pillar width decreases, the DST shows a trend of first increasing and then decreasing, and the optimal DST is showed when the coal pillar width is 15 m and the roadway width is 4.1 m. This indicates that reducing the coal seam thickness (lowering the mining height) appropriately during the end-mining stage can be one of the measures to control the strength of the ECP. Under the same coal seam thickness conditions, the roadway width can be appropriately reduced (reducing the roof area of the roadway) to improve the overall strength of the ECP.

Failure characteristics analysis

Influence law of coal pillar width

Based on the aforementioned mechanical model, the failure influencing factors of the ECP mainly include the coal pillar size, the stress concentration factor of the overlying rock load, and the physical and mechanical parameters of the coal body itself. The failure of the ECP is influenced by multiple factors. Therefore, the influence of each factor on the failure characteristics of the ECP is analyzed by using the control variable method. Based on the actual conditions of the 312 working face, the values of each parameter are: C = 3 MPa, φ = 30, K = 3, x1 = 6, x2 = 4, m = 3.4 m, q1 = 12.5 MPa, q2 = 0.3 MPa, f1 = 25 MPa, f2 = 37 MPa.

There are five schemes for ECP widths: 10 m, 12 m, 14 m, 16 m, and 18 m. By substituting into Eqs. (6) and (7), calculate the yield criterion f (J2) at any point of the ECP for different widths. When f > 0, the part above the horizontal plane does not undergo yield failure, and the ECP is in an elastic state; when f < 0, the part below the horizontal plane undergoes yield failure, and the ECP is in a plastic failure state.

As illustrated in Fig. 11, as the width of the ECP decreases, the middle of the ECP is in an elastic state, and the maximum value of the f shows an increasing trend and moves towards the non-mining side; The two sides of the ECP are in a plastic failure state, and the maximum value of the f of the non-mining side shows an increasing trend, while the maximum value of the f of the mining side shows a decreasing trend followed by an increasing trend. When the width of the ECP is 18 m, the area of the elastic zone is 17.4 m2; when the width of the ECP is reduced to 10 m, the area of the elastic zone is 13 m2, with a decrease of 25.3%. According to Wilson’s theory, the elastic zone is the core of the coal pillar’s bearing capacity, and the larger its area, the higher the ultimate strength of the ECP. The results of this article further quantitatively verify the theory: the width increased from 10 m to 18 m, and the elastic zone increased by about 34%. Therefore, increasing the width of the ECP can significantly expand the elastic zone and enhance the overall stability of the ECP.

Influence law of stress concentration factor

There are five schemes for stress concentration factors K under overlying rock load: 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7. The yield failure characteristics of the ECP under different K value are illustrated in Fig. 12.

The results of this article show that when K increases from 3 to 7, the increase in f value in the middle of the ECP is 50.7%, while the increase in f value on both sides is only 1.7%. According to the theory of limit equilibrium zone, once the plastic zone is formed, the stress increment mainly transforms into plastic flow rather than stress increase, so the stress amplification on both sides is relatively small; The elastic zone is still in an elastic state, and the stress increases linearly with the K-line, so the increase is greater. This trend is consistent with theoretical predictions. More importantly, the area of the elastic zone decreases sharply with increasing K: when K = 3, the area of the elastic zone is 20.6 m2; When K = 7, the area of the elastic zone decreases to 11.9 m2, a decrease of 42.2%. The theory of ultimate equilibrium zone states that the ultimate bearing capacity of a coal pillar depends on the presence or absence of an elastic zone. When the plastic zone extends from both sides and penetrates the entire width of the ECP, the elastic zone disappears and the ECP becomes unstable as a whole. When K = 7 in this article, the area of the elastic zone is less than half of the initial value, indicating that the ECP is approaching the critical state of instability. Overall, as K increases, the plastic damage on both sides of the ECP gradually intensifies, and the elastic zone is continuously compressed. When K exceeds a certain threshold (approximately 5–6 in this article), the area of the elastic zone will accelerate decay.

Influence law of cohesion

There are five schemes for cohesion C: 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 MPa. The yield failure characteristics of the ECP under different C values are illustrated in Fig. 13.

The results of this article show that as C increases, the f value in the middle and both sides of the ECP decreases linearly. When C increases from 1 MPa to 5 MPa, the average decrease in the peak value of f is about 42%, and the rate of decrease is basically constant. According to the M-C criterion, an increase in cohesive force directly enhances the ability of coal pillars to resist shear failure, resulting in a higher yield stress required for the plastic zone under the same external load conditions, thereby suppressing the expansion of the plastic zone and reducing the overall stress level. Therefore, the phenomenon of f linearly decreasing with C is completely consistent with theoretical predictions. In addition, the distribution range of the plastic zone inside the ECP significantly shrinks with the increase of C. When C = 1 MPa, the plastic zone almost penetrates the entire ECP; When C = 5 MPa, the plastic zone is limited to the edges on both sides, and a large elastic zone is retained in the middle. This change pattern is consistent with the conclusion of the limit equilibrium zone theory that the width of the plastic zone is inversely proportional to C. Overall, an increase in cohesive force C is beneficial for improving the stability of the ECP, and its impact shows a linear relationship.

Influence law of friction angle

There are five internal friction angles φ of 15, 20, 25, 30, and 35. The yield failure characteristics of the ECP at different φ values are illustrated in Fig. 14.

The results of this article show that as φ increases, the f value in the middle and both sides of the ECP shows a decreasing trend. When the diameter increases from 15° to 35°, the decrease in the central f is 60.7%, and the decrease on both sides is 44.8%. The significant decrease in the central zone is greater than that on both sides, due to the fact that the central zone is in a high stress state, with a larger normal stress. According to the M-C criterion, an increase in φ has a more pronounced effect on the shear strength of the high stress area, resulting in more sufficient stress release; The normal stress in the plastic zone on both sides is relatively low, and the contribution of φ is relatively limited, so the decrease is relatively small. This phenomenon is highly consistent with theoretical expectations. In addition, the limit equilibrium zone theory states that an increase in the internal friction angle φ directly reduces the expansion range of the plastic zone. When φ = 15 ° in this article, the plastic zone almost penetrates the entire ECP; When φ = 35 °, the plastic zone is limited to the edges on both sides, and the elastic zone area expands by approximately 52%. In summary, increasing the internal friction angle can effectively improve the shear resistance of the ECP, suppress the expansion of plastic zones, and significantly enhance the stability of the ECP.

Application and discussion

The TSCT for the ERR’s surrounding rock is a systematic control method proposed to prevent large deformation and collapse of the surrounding rock during the end-mining stage of the fully mechanized mining face when the ERR is affected by mining. Based on the influence range of stress time and space on the ECP mentioned above, this technology is divided into two steps, defined as follows:

(Ⅰ) First time step: when the working face advances to a distance of 12.5–20 m from the ERR, the stress of the surrounding rock is severely adjusted. By adding long and short combination anchor cables on both sides of the roadway, the short anchor cables control the shallow fracture zone, while the long anchor cables anchor in the undisturbed rock layer, providing secondary bearing capacity.

(Ⅱ) Second time step: when the working face advances to a distance of 5–12.5 m from the ERR, directional hydraulic fracturing technology is used to change the original intact structure of the roof, actively weakening its energy storage and overall hanging capacity. The key parameters include: cutting height: ensuring the cutting of the critical layer and avoiding the formation of large overhangs; cutting angle: usually inclined towards the goaf side, guiding the roof to collapse along a predetermined position under the influence of mining.

Support scheme

Based on the above analysis, the TSCT focuses on time and rate: referring to the sequence, number of steps, and time interval of each process in the construction process. Its core is to limit the evolution rate of surrounding rock stress and deformation, and avoid triggering impact ground pressure or instantaneous collapse of the roof due to excessive loading. Therefore, when the distance between working face 312 and ERR is within the range of 12.5 to 20 m, that is, when the surrounding rock is in the first time step, the TDST above ECP will shift towards ERR direction. At this stage, ERR enters the disturbance phase and requires passive reinforcement and support measures. Therefore, this article proposes an ERR key support technology that focuses on space and strength, including anchor cable support parameters (length, diameter, spacing, row spacing) and roof cutting parameters (angle, height). These parameters determine the ultimate bearing capacity and final stress state of the surrounding rock support system. The reinforcement scheme for the ERR is illustrated in Fig. 15(a).

(Ⅰ) The first stage: the roof is supported by six anchor bolts, each with a diameter of 20 mm and a length of 2400 mm, spaced at 800 mm intervals. The bolts on both sides are installed at 15°. Additionally, anchor cables are employed, measuring 21.6 mm in diameter and 7000 mm in length, with a spacing of 1300 mm and a row spacing of 2400 mm.

(Ⅱ) The second stage: the two sides are supported by four anchor bolts, each with a diameter of 20 mm and a length of 2000 mm, spaced at 800 mm intervals. The bolts on both sides are installed at 30°. Additionally, anchor cables are employed, measuring 21.6 mm in diameter and 4000 mm in length, with a spacing of 1100 mm and a row spacing of 2400 mm.

Conclusions

This study conducted orthogonal numerical simulations of typical retreat roadways in fully mechanized mining face with different mining heights, coal pillar widths, and roadway widths, to investigate their stress evolution and energy dissipation performance. Additionally, mechanical analysis was conducted to further support and supplement the numerical simulation research results. The following conclusions were drawn:

(1) The numerical simulation analysis was conducted to investigate the superposition and convergence evolution of DST in the surrounding rock between the working face and ERR during the mining of ECP. The energy evolution laws and the significance of influencing factors were clarified.

(2) Using the controlled variable method, the yield criterion for DST at any point within the ECP was derived, and the influence of different factors on the failure characteristics of the pillar was systematically identified.

(3) Considering the convergence location of the peak DST, expressions for the roof-cutting height and roof-cutting angle of the ERR were equationted. A new time-step controlled technique for surrounding rock management in the ERR was proposed.

(4) Field monitoring data indicated that the roadway deformation followed a trend of “initially gradual then accelerated” growth, with the total deformation remaining below 10% of the roadway dimensions. During the monitoring period, the ground pressure significantly increased, with the MPS of 28.89 MPa and the SCF of 2.32.

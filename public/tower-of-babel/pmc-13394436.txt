Prospective Trial on Tolerability and Efficacy of Adjuvant Nedaplatin Plus Docetaxel for Non‐Small Cell Lung Cancer
Authors: Koji Teramoto, Ryosuke Kaku, Tomoyuki Igarashi, Masayuki Hashimoto, Shoji Kitamura, Makoto Motoishi, Yuji Suzumura, Satoru Sawai, Yataro Daigo, Jun Hanaoka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTNedaplatin is a second‐generation platinum compound with reduced nephrotoxicity compared with cisplatin. Given its favorable tolerability in advanced non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), we prospectively evaluated the tolerability and efficacy of an adjuvant nedaplatin plus docetaxel chemotherapy regimen in patients with completely resected NSCLC. Patients with stage IB–IIIA NSCLC who had undergone complete surgical resection received nedaplatin (80 mg/m2) and docetaxel (60 mg/m2) intravenously on day 1 once every 4 weeks for up to four cycles. The primary endpoint was treatment tolerability, defined as completion of the planned four cycles, and compared with historical data for the standard cisplatin plus vinorelbine regimen. The secondary endpoint was efficacy, determined by relapse‐free survival (RFS) after surgery. Thirty‐five patients were enrolled, with median age of 65 years (range, 36–77 years). Adenocarcinoma (57.1%) and stage II disease (60.0%) were most common. Dose reductions were needed in some patients, however, 74.3% of patients (n = 26) completed all four cycles, meeting the primary endpoint. Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia and anorexia occurred in 67.8% and 9.0% of patients, respectively. No grade 3 or 4 renal dysfunction was noted. Concerning efficacy, the median RFS time was 63 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 20–NE), and the 5‐year overall survival rate was 85.7% (95% CI, 70.0–93.9). Long‐term retrospective follow‐up (median: 119 months) showed that the 10‐year RFS rate was 52.9% (95% CI, 36.1–69.1). These results indicate that adjuvant chemotherapy with nedaplatin plus docetaxel is tolerable and effective in patients with completely resected NSCLC. Trial Registration: UMIN number: 000004634

Introduction

Invasive non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is characterized by highly aggressive biological behavior including lymphovascular invasion, leading to lymph node metastasis [1] and postoperative recurrence [2, 3]. Even among patients with resectable NSCLC, radical surgery alone yields a 5‐year survival rate of approximately 50% [4], suggesting that surgery alone is insufficient to prevent relapse. To improve postoperative outcomes, adjuvant chemotherapy has been developed. In NSCLC patients without lymph node metastasis, adjuvant chemotherapy with tegafur‐uracil has improved 5‐year survival rates [5, 6] and is now established as the standard adjuvant regimen in this population.

For patients with lymph node metastasis, adjuvant cisplatin‐based combination chemotherapy has been shown to improve both relapse‐free and 5‐year survival rates [7, 8, 9]. In particular, adjuvant cisplatin plus vinorelbine has significantly improved the 5‐year survival rates in stage II to III disease, establishing this regimen as the standard of care [10, 11]. In Japan, a phase III trial comparing cisplatin plus vinorelbine with cisplatin plus pemetrexed found no superiority of the pemetrexed regimen for relapse‐free survival [12, 13], suggesting that cisplatin plus pemetrexed could be another potentially effective adjuvant option for patients with NSCLC.

Management of NSCLC is increasingly guided by molecular and immunologic biomarkers. Patients with driver mutations such as EGFR and ALK, or those with PD‐L1 tumor proportion score ≥ 50% are recommended to receive platinum‐based adjuvant chemotherapy followed by tyrosine kinase inhibitors [14, 15] or immune checkpoint inhibitors [16]. Thus, platinum‐based combination chemotherapy remains a cornerstone of adjuvant therapy in NSCLC.

When administering platinum‐based chemotherapy for NSCLC patients, postoperative organ function must be carefully considered. Following lung lobectomy, patients experience marked alterations to cardiopulmonary dynamics and must adapt to their altered functional reserves during their postoperative recovery period. These changes, unique to thoracic surgery, can compromise tolerance to adjuvant chemotherapy, particularly platinum compounds. Given that adjuvant chemotherapy typically begins approximately 1 month after surgery, patients must regain adequate organ function and performance status in a relatively short period. In this context, the tolerability of adjuvant platinum‐based chemotherapy must be emphasized in addition to its efficacy. Indeed, in the pivotal ANITA study, the completion rate of the planned four cycles of adjuvant cisplatin plus vinorelbine was only 54.8% [10]. As treatment intensity and protocol completion have been shown to improve prognosis [17, 18], more tolerable adjuvant platinum‐based chemotherapy regimens are needed.

Nedaplatin, a platinum compound developed in Japan [19], is approved for treatment of NSCLC and is notable for its reduced toxicity, particularly nephrotoxicity [20, 21]. Unlike cisplatin, nedaplatin does not require aggressive hydration to protect renal function. In our previous prospective study of combination nedaplatin plus docetaxel in patients with advanced or recurrent NSCLC, no patients experienced chemotherapy‐induced renal dysfunction [22]. Regarding its efficacy, the overall response rate was 52.2% and the median overall survival time was 13.0 months [22]. Other groups have reported the efficacy of nedaplatin‐based combination chemotherapy in patients with squamous cell carcinoma of the lung [23, 24, 25]. Given these characteristics, we hypothesized that combination nedaplatin plus docetaxel would provide comparable efficacy to conventional cisplatin‐based combinations while offering improved tolerability in the adjuvant chemotherapy setting.

Here, we report the data on a prospective trial to evaluate the tolerability and efficacy of adjuvant chemotherapy with nedaplatin plus docetaxel for patients with completely resected stage IB to IIIA NSCLC.

Patients and Methods

Study Design

The study was a multicenter, open‐label, single‐arm prospective study. Eligible patients had histologically confirmed NSCLC and had undergone complete tumor resection by radical lobectomy and systematic lymph node dissection. Postoperative pathological stage was required to be IB, II, or IIIA (Union for International Cancer Control TNM classification, seventh edition). Additional inclusion criteria were: no prior chemotherapy nor radiotherapy; age between 20 and 79 years; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1; adequate organ function to tolerate platinum‐based adjuvant chemotherapy; ability to initiate adjuvant chemotherapy within 4 to 8 weeks after surgery; and provision of written informed consent.

Patients were excluded if they had hypersensitivity to polysorbate 80 or platinum‐containing compounds, severe comorbidities (including ischemic heart disease requiring treatment, arrhythmia, myocardial infarction within 6 months, liver cirrhosis, uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, and bleeding diathesis), third‐space fluid collection requiring drainage, active concomitant malignancy, radiographic evidence of interstitial pneumonia or pulmonary fibrosis, peripheral neuropathy, or if they were pregnant or lactating.

The primary endpoint of this study was tolerability, assessed by the completion rate of the planned four chemotherapy cycles. The secondary endpoints were toxicity and efficacy. Toxicity was assessed by the incidence and severity of adverse events, graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Toxicity Criteria (version 4.0). Efficacy was assessed by the relapse‐free survival (RFS) rate after surgery. Data on RFS after surgery were collected prospectively for 5 years following surgery, while long‐term survival data as of December 2023 were collected retrospectively.

This study was performed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before enrollment. The study was approved by the institutional review board of each participating institution (UMIN clinical trial ID: 000004634). The retrospective data collection was approved by the Ethical Committee of Shiga University of Medical Science (R2022‐075).

Sample Size

In our previous trial of this regimen in advanced NSCLC, the completion rate for four cycles of docetaxel and nedaplatin combination chemotherapy was 86.2% [22]. On the basis of these data, we hypothesized that the completion rate for this regimen in the adjuvant setting would exceed the completion rate for cisplatin and vinorelbine combination chemotherapy by 20%, for an expected rate of 74.8%. Assuming a one‐sided alpha of 0.05 and 80% power, a sample size of 35 patients was calculated using a binomial test.

Treatment

On day 1 of each treatment cycle, nedaplatin was administered intravenously at a dose of 80 mg/m2, diluted in 500 mL of normal saline and infused over at least 60 min, followed by hydration with 1000 mL of normal saline. Docetaxel was then administered intravenously at a dose of 60 mg/m2, diluted in 250 mL of normal saline or 5% glucose solution and infused over at least 60 min. Treatment cycles were repeated every 4 weeks for a maximum of four cycles.

Treatment was discontinued in the event of radiologic relapse, severe drug‐related adverse events, intercurrent illness of complications precluding continuation, patient refusal to continue or withdrawal of consent, or investigator decision. Nedaplatin and docetaxel doses were reduced by 20%, to 64 and 48 mg/m2, respectively, and were implemented in cases of febrile neutropenia, grade 4 leukopenia, grade 3 neutropenia with fever (≥ 38°C) suspected due to infection, or any other hematological and non‐hematological adverse events at the investigator's discretion.

Statistical Analysis

Relapse‐free survival and overall survival (OS) were calculated from the date of surgery. Data on tumor relapse and survival were prospectively collected through regular follow‐up visits every 1–3 months for 5 years following surgery, and updated retrospectively as of December 2023. Median RFS and OS were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and survival comparison between groups was compared using the log‐rank test. Statistical analyses were performed using JMP Student Edition 19 (JMP Statistical Discovery LLC, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Patient Characteristics

Between December 2010 and April 2014, 35 patients were enrolled across three participating institutions. Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. All patients met the eligibility criteria and were evaluable for safety and survival analyses. The study included 31 men (88.6%) and 4 women (11.4%), with a median age of 65 years (range, 36–77). Histologic subtypes included adenocarcinoma in 20 patients (57.1%), and squamous cell carcinoma in 13 (37.1%). Pathologic stage was II in 21 patients (60.0%), and IIIA in 12 (34.3%). Surgical procedures included lobectomy in 29 patients (82.9%), lobectomy plus segmentectomy in 4 patients (11.4%), and bi‐lobectomy in 2 patients (5.7%). All patients underwent regional lymph node dissection.

Tolerability

Adjuvant chemotherapy was initiated at a median of 52 days after surgery (range, 27–64 days) (Table 1). Twenty‐six patients (74.3%) completed all four planned chemotherapy cycles (Table 2), meeting the primary endpoint of this trial. Nine patients (25.7%) discontinued treatment early: three after one cycle (two due to febrile neutropenia and one due to chylothorax), five after two cycles (one each due to pneumonia, allergy against docetaxel, bronchial fistula, tumor relapse, and patient request), and one after three cycles at the patient's request. Among those who completed four cycles, dose reductions were performed in 11 patients (42.3%) (Table 2). Dose reductions occurred after the first cycle in six patients, after the second cycle in six patients, after the third cycle in three patients, and after the first and third cycles in one patient. The median relative dose intensities of nedaplatin and docetaxel were 97.5% (range, 74.7%–100.0%) and 97.5% (range, 75.8%–100.0%), respectively.

Safety

Adverse events are summarized in Table 3. The most frequent hematological toxicity was myelosuppression. Leukopenia and neutropenia of grade 3 or 4 occurred in 35.8% and 66.7% of cycles, respectively. Three patients (8.6%) developed febrile neutropenia. At that time, primary granulocyte colony‐stimulating factor (G‐CSF) prophylaxis had not been available, and G‐CSF was used to treat neutropenia in 67.5% of cycles. Grade 3 or 4 anemia and thrombocytopenia were rare, occurring in 1.7% and 0.8% of cycles, respectively. No grade 3 or 4 renal dysfunction was noted. Among grade 3 to 4 nonhematologic toxicities, anorexia (7.5%) and nausea (3.3%) were most frequent, although most nonhematologic toxicities were mild. No treatment‐related deaths occurred.

Efficacy

Fifteen patients experienced tumor relapse within 5 years after surgery, yielding a 5‐year RFS rate of 60.0% (95% confidence interval [CI], 43.3–74.7) and the median RFS time was 63 months (95% CI, 20–NE) (Figure 1A). Concerning the OS, a 5‐year OS rate of 85.7% (95% CI, 70.0–93.9) and the median OS time was not reached (95% CI, NE–NE) (Figure 1B).

Relapse‐free survival (A) and overall survival (B) after surgery in patients who receive adjuvant chemotherapy of nedaplatin and docetaxel (n = 35).

The median RFS time was longer among patients who completed all four chemotherapy cycles (63 months, 95% CI, 20–NE) than among those who did not (13 months, 95% CI, 3–NE), showing a trend toward improved outcomes (p = 0.170) (Figure 2A). The median OS time was significantly longer among patients who completed all four chemotherapy cycles (not reached, 95% CI, NE–NE) than among those who did not (not reached, 95% CI, 5–NE) (p = 0.0015) (Figure 2B).

Relapse‐free survival (A) and overall survival (B) after surgery in patients who receive adjuvant chemotherapy of nedaplatin and docetaxel. Blue and red lines indicate data from patients who completed four cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy (n = 26) and those who did not (n = 9), respectively.

By pathological stage, a 5‐year RFS rate was 50.0% (95% CI, 0.1–94.1), 76.2% (95% CI, 54.0–89.7), and 33.3% (95% CI, 13.1–62.4) for stage IB, II, and IIIA, respectively (Figure S1A). Since only two patients were included in stage IB, when the data from stage IB and II patients were combined, the 5‐year RFS rate for these stages was 73.9% (95% CI, 52.8–87.8) (Figure 3A). The median RFS time was not reached for stage IB (95% CI, 5–NE), stage II (95% CI, 63–NE), and stage IB + II (95% CI, 63–NE), and 16 months (95% CI, 10–NE) for stage IIIA, respectively (Figure S1A; Figure 3A). The 5‐year OS rate was 100.0% (95% CI, NE–NE), 100.0% (95% CI, NE–NE), 100.0% (95% CI, NE–NE), and 58.3% (95% CI, 30.7–81.5) for stage IB, II, IB + II, and IIIA, respectively (Figure S1B; Figure 3B).

Relapse‐free survival (A) and overall survival (B) after surgery in patients who receive adjuvant chemotherapy of nedaplatin and docetaxel. Red and blue lines indicate data from patients with pathological stage IB (n = 2) plus II (n = 21) and IIIA (n = 12), respectively.

Given the data from previous reports that patients with squamous cell carcinoma of the lung benefited from nedaplatin‐based combination chemotherapy [23, 24, 25], we analyzed the efficacy by pathological types (adenocarcinoma [n = 20] vs. squamous cell carcinoma [n = 13]). However, the 5‐year PFS (65.0% [95% CI, 42.5–82.3] vs. 53.8% [95% CI, 28.2–77.6], p = 0.495) and OS rates (90.0% [95% CI, 67.6–97.5] vs. 76.9% [95% CI, 47.8–92.4], p = 0.312) were not different between pathological types (Figure S2).

Long‐term follow‐up data on tumor relapse and survival were collected retrospectively (median follow‐up period: 119 months), showing that a median RFS time was not reached (95% CI, 20–NE) and a 10‐year RFS rate of 52.9% (95% CI, 36.1–69.1) (Figure 4A). The median OS time was not reached (95% CI, NE–NE) and a 10‐year OS rate was 71.6% (95% CI, 53.2–84.9) (Figure 4B). Twelve patients (34.3%) had died as of December 2023, including four deaths unrelated to NSCLC.

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated the tolerability and efficacy of adjuvant chemotherapy with nedaplatin plus docetaxel for completely resected NSCLC (Figure 5A). The completion rate of four cycles of the chemotherapy was 74.3% (Figure 5B), exceeding that reported for the current standard regimen of cisplatin plus vinorelbine. The 5‐year RFS rate was 60.0% and the median RFS time was 63 months, indicating favorable long‐term disease control in this population.

The flow of this trial and the treatment schedule of adjuvant chemotherapy with nedaplatin plus docetaxel for completely resected non‐small cell lung cancer (A). The primary endpoint of this study was tolerability, assessed by the completion rate of the planned four chemotherapy cycles. As a result, it was 74.3%, exceeding that reported for the current standard regimen of cisplatin plus vinorelbine (B).

At the time this study was initiated, the standard adjuvant chemotherapy regimen for NSCLC was cisplatin plus vinorelbine, based on pivotal trials such as the ANITA trial [9, 10]. However, in the ANITA trial, only 54.8% of patients completed the planned four cycles of cisplatin plus vinorelbine [9], reflecting the suboptimal tolerability of this regimen. Our previous work confirmed that nedaplatin plus docetaxel is well tolerated in advanced NSCLC [21], providing the rationale for evaluating this combination in the adjuvant setting. In the present study, the completion rate was 74.3%, notably higher than that reported in the ANITA trial for cisplatin plus vinorelbine, and meeting our predefined primary endpoint. However, it must be noted that the doses of cisplatin and vinorelbine administered in the ANITA trial were relatively high at 100 and 30 mg/m2, respectively [9], which likely contributed to its lower completion rate. In contrast, the JIPANG trial phase III trial conducted in Japan used reduced doses of cisplatin (80 mg/m2) and vinorelbine (25 mg/m2) [12, 13], achieving a completion rate of 72.7% [12], comparable to the completion rate observed with nedaplatin plus docetaxel in the present trial. These findings suggest that adjuvant nedaplatin plus docetaxel chemotherapy is tolerable with a treatment intensity comparable to the standard adjuvant cisplatin plus vinorelbine regimen in NSCLC.

The safety profile observed in this study supports this conclusion. Grade 3 or 4 leukopenia and neutropenia occurred in 35.8% and 66.7% of cycles, respectively, while febrile neutropenia occurred in 2.5% (Table 3). In the JIPANG trial, the corresponding rates of leukopenia and neutropenia were higher at 51.0% and 81.1%, respectively [12]. Similarly, grade 3 or 4 anorexia and nausea/vomiting occurred in 7.2% and 4.1% of cycles in the present study (Table 3), compared with 10.9% and 8.6%, respectively, in the JIPANG trial [12]. Furthermore, no grade 3 or 4 renal dysfunction was observed in the present trial, consistent with the known low nephrotoxicity of nedaplatin [20, 21]. Collectively, these data indicate that nedaplatin‐based adjuvant chemotherapy has a lower incidence of adverse events than cisplatin‐based regimens and offers the important advantage of preserving renal function. Because treatment‐related toxicities can limit chemotherapy completion and adversely affect patient quality of life, the favorable safety profile of nedaplatin plus docetaxel observed in the present trial likely contributed to the high treatment completion rate achieved.

Recently, perioperative treatment strategies with immune checkpoint inhibitors have developed in resectable NSCLC. A phase III clinical trial, IMpower010, demonstrated that cisplatin‐based adjuvant chemotherapy followed by an immune checkpoint inhibitor, atezolizumab, improved RFS in NSCLC patients with PD‐L1 tumor proportion score ≥ 50% [16]. Even in neo‐adjuvant chemotherapy, platinum‐based chemotherapy with an immune checkpoint inhibitor, nivolumab, improved event‐free survival in stage IB to IIIA resectable NSCLC patients [26]. While treatment regimens including immune checkpoint inhibitors have been developed, platinum‐based combination chemotherapy remains a cornerstone of perioperative treatment strategies in NSCLC. Given that the combination chemotherapy with nedaplatin plus docetaxel is well tolerated, this combination chemotherapy may have the potential to be one of the platinum‐based chemotherapy regimens combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors in the perioperative setting.

Regarding efficacy, the 5‐year RFS rate of 60.0%, with a median RFS time of 63 months (Figure 1), compares favorably with outcomes reported for cisplatin plus vinorelbine. The median RFS was 36.3 months in the ANITA trial [9] and 37.3 months in the JIPANG trial [13]. In our retrospective long‐term follow‐up, the median RFS and the median OS were not reached (Figure 4). In ANITA trial, the median OS was 65.7 months [9]. Although direct comparisons between studies are not possible, our findings suggest that adjuvant chemotherapy with nedaplatin and docetaxel may be considered an effective regimen for patients with NSCLC.

This study has several limitations. Because not all consecutive patients were included, there is a possibility of selection bias; physicians may have preferentially included patients expected to tolerate platinum‐based adjuvant chemotherapy. Additionally, patient quality of life was not measured, limiting the ability to evaluate the subjective tolerability of the regimen. Finally, although our efficacy results are promising, this was a single‐arm study with a relatively small sample size, and efficacy conclusions should be interpreted with caution. Larger randomized trials are needed to confirm these findings before this regimen can be recommended as adjuvant chemotherapy.

In conclusion, adjuvant chemotherapy with nedaplatin plus docetaxel was well tolerated and demonstrated a high completion rate in patients with completely resected NSCLC. While further validation in larger trials is necessary, this regimen represents a promising and potentially less toxic alternative to conventional cisplatin‐based adjuvant chemotherapy for NSCLC.

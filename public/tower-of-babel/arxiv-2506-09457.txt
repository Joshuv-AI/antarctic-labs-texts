Towards Bridging the Reward-Generation Gap in Direct Alignment Algorithms
Zeguan Xiao, Yun Chen, Guanhua Chen

Abstract
Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs), such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Simple Preference Optimization (SimPO), have emerged as efficient alternatives to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) algorithms for aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. However, DAAs suffer from a fundamental limitation we identify as the “reward-generation gap”—a misalignment between optimization objectives during training and actual generation performance during inference. In this paper, we find a contributor to the reward-generation gap is the mismatch between the inherent importance of prefix tokens during the LLM generation process and how this importance is reflected in the implicit reward functions of DAAs. To bridge the gap, we introduce a simple yet effective approach called P refix- O riented E qual-length T raining (POET), which truncates both preferred and dispreferred responses to match the shorter one’s length. Training with POET , where both responses in each sample are truncated to equal length, resulting in diverse truncated lengths across samples, the optimization of DAAs objective is implicitly constrained to converge across all positions, thus paying more attention to prefix tokens than the standard DAAs. We conduct experiments with DPO and SimPO, two representative DAAs, demonstrating that POET improves over their standard implementations, achieving up to 15.6 points in AlpacaEval 2 and overall improvements across downstream tasks. Our results highlight the importance of addressing the misalignment between reward optimization and generation performance in DAAs.

An important step in the training of LLMs is “alignment”, which refers to aligning LLMs with human intentions and values, ensuring they are helpful, honest, and harmless

Learning from human feedback plays a crucial role in LLM alignment, and a popular paradigm for this is Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF)

To simplify the alignment process, researchers have developed Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs)

(
Rafailov2023DirectPO
)
and Simple Preference Optimization (SimPO)
(
meng2024simpo
)
. DAAs bypass the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning in RLHF, instead directly performing preference optimization based on a reference dataset. The simplicity and effectiveness of DAAs have made them an attractive alternative to RLHF.

The optimization objectives of DAAs are designed to increase the likelihood of preferred responses while reducing the likelihood of dispreferred ones

;
Azar2023AGT
;
Zhao2023SLiCHFSL
)
, where some can be expressed as implicit reward functions based on different manifestations of likelihoods

However, a known issue with DAAs is that they may decrease the likelihood of preferred responses as long as the likelihood margins between preferred and dispreferred responses increase

;

)
. More importantly,
(

)
has found that neither a higher likelihood of preferred responses nor larger likelihood margins necessarily lead to better performance, suggesting a deeper issue in how DAAs connect optimization objectives to actual performance.

The disconnect identified above arises from the fact that the implicit rewards of DAAs do not represent the quality of responses, but are rather different manifestations of their likelihoods.
Specifically, the foundational discrepancy between the maximum/minimum likelihood training and autoregressive generation in language models

zhang-etal-2021-trading
;
arora-etal-2022-exposure
)

To investigate the origins of this gap, we analyze the discrepancy between autoregressive generation and reward computation in DAAs.
Our analysis reveals that while prefix tokens have significantly higher uncertainty and are critical for determining response quality, DAAs’ implicit reward functions treat all tokens equally, diluting the optimization focus on these important early tokens.
Our analysis demonstrates that the reward-generation gap may partly stem from the mismatch between how prefix tokens influence generation quality and how they are weighted in DAAs’ implicit reward functions.

2
Reward-Generation Gap in Direct Alignment Algorithms

2.1
Background

In Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF)
(
Ouyang2022TrainingLM
;
stiennon2020learning
;
li-etal-2016-deep
;
ziegler2019finetuning
)
, LLM learns a policy
\pi_{\theta}
and
\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)
represents the probability assigned by the LLM to a response
y
given an input prompt
x
.
During the reinforcement learning (RL) phase, the optimization objective is to maximize the expected reward while preventing the policy
\pi_{\theta}
from deviating too far from the reference policy
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}
:

\max_{\pi_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)}\left[r_{\phi}(x,y)\right]-\beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\right],

(1)

where
r_{\phi}
is the reward model, typically trained as a Bradley-Terry (BT) model using a static preference dataset
\mathcal{D}
of triples
(x,y_{w},y_{l})
. In each triple,
x
denotes the input prompt,
y_{w}
the preferred response, and
y_{l}
the dispreferred response. The reward model receives the generated response and provides a reward signal
r_{\phi}(x,y)
indicating the quality of the response.

Alternatively, Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs)
(
rafailov2024scaling
)
eliminate the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning, directly performing preference optimization on a static preference dataset.
The optimization objectives of DAAs contain rewards implicitly defined by
\pi_{\theta}
, where some rewards are based directly on the likelihood
(
Zhao2023SLiCHFSL
;
xu2024contrastive
)
, while others are derived from different manifestations of likelihoods, such as DPO
(
Rafailov2023DirectPO
)
and SimPO
(
meng2024simpo
)
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_{\theta};\pi_{\text{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_{w},y_{l})\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\log\sigma\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{w}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{w}\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{l}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{l}\mid x)}\right)\right]

(2)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimPO}}\left(\pi_{\theta}\right)=-\mathbb{E}_{\left(x,y_{w},y_{l}\right)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\log\sigma\left(\frac{\beta}{\left|y_{w}\right|}\log\pi_{\theta}\left(y_{w}\mid x\right)-\frac{\beta}{\left|y_{l}\right|}\log\pi_{\theta}\left(y_{l}\mid x\right)-\gamma\right)\right]

(3)

r_{\text{DPO}}(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}

(4)

r_{\text{SimPO}}(x,y)=\frac{\beta}{|y|}\log\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x),

(5)

where
r_{\text{DPO}}(x,y)
and
r_{\text{SimPO}}(x,y)
are the implicit reward functions of DPO and SimPO, respectively.

Reward-Generation Gap in Direct Alignment Algorithms: Origins and Implications

Prefix tokens exhibit significantly lower log probabilities, yet DAAs’ implicit reward functions consider all tokens equally, thereby diluting the importance of these critical early tokens. Designed to increase the likelihood of preferred responses
y_{w}
while decreasing the likelihood of dispreferred responses
y_{l}
, DAAs converge if the likelihood margins between preferred and dispreferred responses grow sufficiently large. However, a known issue with DAAs is that they may decrease the likelihood of preferred responses as long as the margins increase
(
Rafailov2023DirectPO
;
pal2024smaug
)
.
Furthermore,
(
shi2024understanding
)
finds that contrary to expectations, neither a higher likelihood of preferred responses nor larger likelihood margins between the preferred and dispreferred responses necessarily lead to better performance.

The disconnect identified above arises from the fact that the implicit rewards of DAAs do not represent the quality of responses, but are rather different manifestations of their likelihoods, as shown in Eq.
4
and Eq.
5
. However, due to the foundational discrepancy between the maximum/minimum likelihood training and autoregressive generation in language models
(
ranzato2015sequence
;
zhang-etal-2021-trading
;
arora-etal-2022-exposure
)
, optimizing for a higher reward of preferred responses or larger reward margins between the preferred and dispreferred responses does not necessarily lead to better generation quality. This discrepancy creates a substantial misalignment between reward optimization and actual generation performance—a phenomenon we refer to as the
Reward-Generation Gap in Direct Alignment Algorithms
.

We consider a plausible contributor to this gap is the fundamental mismatch between the inherent importance of prefix tokens during the LLM generation process and how this importance is reflected in the implicit reward functions of DAAs.
During autoregressive generation, LLMs produce tokens from left to right, with each token conditioned on all previously generated tokens. As shown in Fig.
2
, the first few tokens (prefix) are generated with minimal context, leading to higher uncertainty than later tokens.
As demonstrated by
(
arora-etal-2022-exposure
)
, errors in autoregressive generation tend to accumulate, with early mistakes propagating and amplifying through the sequence. This phenomenon, known as exposure bias, means that suboptimal choices in prefix tokens can severely degrade the quality of the entire response.
However, DAAs’ reward functions (such as Eq.
4
and Eq.
5
) assign equal weight to every token, ignoring these positional differences. As illustrated in Fig.
2
, although the average log probability of prefix tokens is lower than that of later tokens and thus produces larger gradients, the overall mean log probability across all positions washes out this effect in DAAs’ implicit reward functions.

3
How to Bridge the Reward-Generation Gap?

3.1
Bridging the Reward-Generation Gap by Focusing of Prefix Tokens

As discussed in Section
2.2
, we consider a plausible contributor to the reward-generation gap to be the fundamental mismatch between the inherent importance of prefix tokens during the LLM generation process and how this importance is reflected in the implicit reward functions of DAAs. A natural extension to address this mismatch is to upweight these tokens in the implicit reward functions. One straightforward strategy is assigning monotonically decreasing weights to each token based on its position in the response, thereby explicitly upweighting prefix tokens. While this position-based heuristic is computationally efficient and algorithm-agnostic, it introduces noise by overemphasizing tokens that carry little semantic weight (e.g., articles and function words) based purely on their position. Another plausible approach is to leverage external tools to estimate each token’s contribution
(
yang2024selective
)
. This approach can more accurately capture semantic relevance, but it typically requires additional computational resources and may complicate integration into generic DAAs objectives.

Our method is motivated by a central observation:
the quality of a response to be generated heavily depends on the quality of its initial prefix
.
Formally, we denote a prefix of length
k
as
y_{<k}
and define the quality of
y_{<k}
as the expected quality of complete responses given it:

Q(y_{<k})=\mathbb{E}_{y_{\geq k}\sim\pi_{\theta}(y_{\geq k}\mid x,y_{<k})}\left[r_{\theta}(x,y_{<k}\oplus y_{\geq k})\right],

(6)

where
\pi_{\theta}
is an oracle policy,
r_{\theta}
is the oracle reward model that evaluates the quality of responses,
y_{\geq k}
denotes the completion given prefix
y_{<k}
, and
\oplus
represents the concatenation operation. In practice, we can estimate the expectation by sampling multiple completions from a proxy policy
\hat{\pi}_{\theta}
and using a proxy reward model
\hat{r}_{\theta}
to evaluate the quality of responses.

Empirical demonstratetion.

We randomly select 1,000 samples from the training set of UltraFeedback
(
Cui2024UltraFeedbackBL
)
.
For each pair of responses
(y_{w},y_{l})
in these samples, we truncate both responses at different positions to obtain prefixes of varying lengths, and then generate multiple completions from these prefixes. We use two SFT models as the proxy policy
\hat{\pi}_{\theta}
: Zephyr
(
Tunstall2023ZephyrDD
)
and Llama-3-Base-8B-SFT
(
meng2024simpo
)
, and utilize
RLHFlow/ArmoRM-Llama3-8B-v0.1

(
ArmoRM
)
as the proxy reward model for evaluating response quality. We quantify the prefix quality difference by
\Delta Q=Q(y_{w,\,<k})-Q(y_{l,\,<k})
at different prefix lengths
k
. Our results, shown in Figure
3
, demonstrate that the prefix quality difference already exists at very early tokens and increases as the prefix length increases, with diminishing marginal growth at longer lengths.

Prefix-Oriented Equal-length Training (
POET
).

The above observation suggests that the quality of the prefix can be used as a proxy for the overall quality of the response, allowing us to focus on optimizing the prefix rather than the entire response.
Therefore, we propose a straightforward solution: Given a pair of preferred and dispreferred responses
(y_{w},y_{l})
with lengths
|y_{w}|
and
|y_{l}|
, we truncate both responses to the length of the shorter one, denoted as
k=\min(|y_{w}|,|y_{l}|)
, resulting in truncated responses
y_{w,\,<k}
and
y_{l,\,<k}
. We then optimize the policy
\pi_{\theta}
based on the truncated responses.
Training with
POET
, where both responses in each sample are truncated to equal length, resulting in diverse truncated lengths across samples, the optimization of DAAs’ objective is implicitly constrained to converge across all positions, thus paying more attention to prefix tokens than the standard DAAs.

We summarize three advantages of
POET
:

•

Hyperparameters-free:
By using the shorter response’s length as a natural truncation point,
POET
requires no additional hyperparameters. This makes implementation straightforward and eliminates the need for additional hyperparameter tuning.

•

Minimal noise risk:

POET
inherently minimizes the risk of introducing noise into training data. By truncating to the shorter response’s length, we ensure that the risk of
Q(y_{w,\,<k})>Q(y_{l,\,<k})
only comes from the truncated suffix of the longer response, which is less critical to the overall response quality according to our empirical analysis in Figure
3
. We verify the risk empirically in three settings and report the results in Table
1
. Specifically, we get
y_{w,\,<k}
and
y_{l,\,<k}
by
POET
, generate completions from the truncated prefix, and evaluate whether the quality ranking between the completion and the untruncated shorter response is preserved, using
RLHFlow/ArmoRM-Llama3-8B-v0.1
as the reward model. As shown in Table
1
, the quality consistency before and after truncating is high.

•

Universal compatibility:

POET
is compatible with any DAAs, requiring no modifications to their core optimization objectives. This allows for seamless integration with both current and future DAAs, making
POET
a versatile enhancement to DAAs.

Bridging the Reward-Generation Gap: How and When

4.1
POET
Bridge the Reward-Generation Gap by Generating Better Prefixes

Figure 4:
Prefix quality with and without

consistently generate higher-quality prefixes.

To directly evaluate whether
POET
improves performance by generating better prefixes, we conduct an analysis comparing the quality of prefixes generated by standard DAAs models and
POET
-trained models.

Experimental setup.

We randomly sample 500 prompts from the test set of UltraFeedback and generate 5 responses per prompt using both standard DAAs and
POET
-trained models. These responses are then truncated to create prefixes of varying lengths. Following the definition of prefix quality in Eq.
6
in Section
3.1
, we evaluate the quality of these prefixes by generating completions from them using the SFT model as the proxy policy. This choice of proxy policy isolates the influence of other factors.
For each prefix, we use the SFT model to generate 3 completions. As a result, we obtain 15 completions (5 responses with 3 completions each) for each prompt at each prefix length. We then evaluate the quality of these completions using a strong reward model,
RLHFlow/ArmoRM-Llama3-8B-v0.1

(
ArmoRM
)
.

We visualize the results in Figure
4
. The results demonstrate that models trained with
POET
consistently generate higher-quality prefixes than standard DAAs models across all prefix lengths, validating the effectiveness of
POET
in forcing the DAAs to focus on optimizing the prefix quality, which is the key to bridging the reward-generation gap.

4.2
POET
Bridge the Reward-Generation Gap when Quality Difference is Substantial

As discussed in Section
3.1
,
POET
’s effectiveness relies on the assumption that the quality ranking between preferred and dispreferred responses is preserved after applying
POET
. Otherwise, it could introduce noise into the training data.
In this section, we validate this assumption empirically and show that the effectiveness of
POET
is also connected to the quality difference between preferred and dispreferred responses of the preference dataset.
Specifically, we conduct an analysis using Llama-3-8B and SimPO across 3 settings:

1.

Llama-3-8B-SimPO v0.1

(
meng2024simpo
)
: In this setting, the responses in preference dataset are generated by Llama-3-8B-Instruct
(
llama3modelcard
)
using the prompts from the UltraFeedback dataset, with preference annotating using a weak reward model. This setting is expected to have a small quality difference between preferred and dispreferred responses of the preference dataset.

2.

Llama-3-8B-SimPO v0.2

(
meng2024simpo
)
: Same as the v0.1 setting, but with a strong reward model, RLHFlow/ArmoRM-Llama3-8B-v0.1
(
ArmoRM
)
, used for preference annotating. This setting is expected to have a moderate quality difference.

3.

Llama-3-8B-SimPO Interpolation
: In this setting, we create two new models by interpolating between Llama-3-8B and Llama-3-8B-Instruct with ratios of 0.1:0.9 and -0.1:1.1 respectively. We then generate responses by the two interpolated models and the Llama-3-8B-Instruct model (2 responses per model), following the pipeline with the Llama-3-8B-SimPO-v0.2 setting. This setting is expected to have a large quality difference, as the generated responses are more diverse than the previous two settings.

5
Related Work

Reinforcement learning with human feedback.

RLHF is a technique to align LLMs with human preferences
(
christiano2017deep
;
ziegler2019fine
;
Ouyang2022TrainingLM
;
bai2022training
)
. The classical RLHF pipeline consists of three key phases: (1) supervised fine-tuning to create an initial policy, (2) reward modeling to train a Bradley-Terry (BT) model, and (3) policy optimization to train the policy to maximize the expected reward. The third stage of RLHF is typically trained with Policy Gradient Algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
(
schulman2017proximal
)
.
The RLHF framework is also widely applied to various applications, including ensuring safety
(
dai2023safe
)
and enhancing complex mathematical reasoning
(
shao2024deepseekmath
)
.

Direct alignment algorithms.

While effective, RLHF methods often face challenges like inefficiency, instability, and high sensitivity to hyperparameters
(
zheng2023secrets
;
santacroce2023efficient
)
, motivating the exploration of alternative approaches.
As one such alternative approach, direct alignment algorithms (DAAs)
(
rafailov2024scaling
)
directly align LLMs to human preferences, bypassing the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning (RL) in RLHF
(
Hong2024ORPOMP
;
xu2023some
)
.
DAAs are designed to increase the likelihood of preferred responses while decreasing the likelihood of dispreferred responses. However, a known issue with DAAs is that they may decrease the likelihood of preferred responses as long as the margins increase
(
Rafailov2023DirectPO
;
pal2024smaug
)
. Furthermore,
(
shi2024understanding
)
finds that, contrary to expectations, neither a higher likelihood of preferred responses nor larger likelihood margins between the preferred and dispreferred responses necessarily lead to better performance. The disconnect identified above arises from the fact that the implicit rewards of DAAs do not represent the quality of responses, but are rather different manifestations of their likelihoods. In this paper, we identify this issue as the
Reward-Generation Gap in Direct Alignment Algorithms
, due to the foundational discrepancy between maximum/minimum likelihood training and autoregressive generation in language models
(
ranzato2015sequence
;
zhang-etal-2021-trading
;
arora-etal-2022-exposure
)
.

Gap between training and inference of autoregressive language models

The disconnect between training and inference in language models has been studied in two key aspects.
First,
(
zhang-etal-2021-trading
)
demonstrate that there exists a likelihood trap wherein higher likelihood during training does not necessarily translate to better generation quality during inference, as models trained with maximum likelihood estimation may favor safe but generic outputs.
Second, the exposure bias phenomenon
(
ranzato2015sequence
;
arora-etal-2022-exposure
)
arises from the discrepancy between teacher forcing during training and the model’s reliance on its own predictions during generation. As formalized by
(
arora-etal-2022-exposure
)
, this creates an imitation learning failure where errors accumulate with sequence length and model entropy. While our work shares conceptual similarities with these studies, we specifically focus on a novel gap in DAAs —the misalignment between their implicit reward functions and generation quality. This reward-generation gap presents unique challenges in preference optimization, as DAAs’ reward functions are derived from likelihood-based metrics yet are expected to guide the model toward generating higher-quality responses.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we identify and analyze a critical limitation in DAAs—the reward-generation gap, which manifests as a misalignment between optimization objectives during training and actual generation quality during inference. We demonstrate that this gap partly stems from the mismatch between how prefix tokens influence generation quality and how they are weighted in DAAs’ implicit reward functions.
To address this issue, we introduce
POET
, a simple yet effective method for focusing optimization on prefixes by truncating both preferred and dispreferred responses to match the shorter response’s length. This hyperparameter-free approach requires no modification to existing DAA objectives while maintaining broad compatibility across different algorithms. Through extensive experiments with DPO and SimPO on multiple model architectures (Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B), we demonstrate that our method consistently improves performance.

The success of our simple approach suggests that there may be other opportunities to bridge this gap. Looking forward, we believe our work opens up several promising directions for future research. First, developing more sophisticated approaches to dynamically weigh different parts of the response based on their importance during generation could further improve alignment. Second, investigating other manifestations of the reward-generation gap beyond prefix importance could lead to additional improvements in DAAs. Finally, our findings suggest the need for a broader theoretical framework to understand and address the fundamental challenges in DAAs.

Appendix A
Limitations

While our POET method demonstrates significant improvements across various settings, we acknowledge several limitations. First, our approach relies on truncating responses to the length of the shorter one, which may not be optimal when both responses are very short, potentially limiting the information available for optimization. Second, as demonstrated in our analysis, POET’s effectiveness depends on the quality difference between preferred and dispreferred responses being preserved after truncation; in datasets where this condition isn’t met (such as the Llama-3-8B-SimPO-v0.1 setting), performance improvements may be limited or even negative. Finally, while our method effectively bridges the reward-generation gap by focusing on prefixes, there might be other manifestations of this gap that remain unexplored. Future work could investigate more sophisticated approaches to dynamically weight different parts of responses based on their importance during generation.

Fold bifurcations in shearing radiative collapse
Authors: K S Govinder, S D Maharaj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA simple form of the gravitational field for a radiating relativistic star with nonzero shear is studied. The junction conditions at the surface of the star reduce to an autonomous nonlinear first order differential equation of the Riccati type: it admits exact solutions and is amenable to a phase plane analysis. We can determine the temporal evolution and asymptotic behaviour of the radiating star including shear, charge and the cosmological constant. The presence of shear leads to behaviour which is distinct from shear-free models and fold bifurcations arise; the model collapses from an initial static configuration or a superdense cold star forms. The areal distance plays a central role in the asymptotic analysis and there is an interplay with the charge and the cosmological constant.Keywords: Radiating stars, Asymptotic behaviour, Fold bifurcations, Shearing spacetimesSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Radiating processes in stellar models have been widely studied in general relativity to investigate the influence of nonzero Riemannian curvature in the evolution of the models. These studies have become possible with the completion of the boundary conditions at the stellar surface by Santos1. Several important physical features can be analysed in models with radiating spheres: dissipation, end state of radiating bodies, pressure anisotropy, thermal conductivity, shear viscosity, bulk viscosity, particle creation at the surface, stability and cracking of compact bodies2–14. Radiating stars have also been studied in modified gravity theories as in the treatments15–17. These studies also exhibit gravitational dynamics which are distinct from static compact spheres such as neutron stars.

Most early studies of general relativistic radiating stars were conducted by making ad hoc assumptions on the analytic forms of the gravitational potentials or restricting the matter content18–21. However, recent studies concentrate on using systematic methods in studying the evolution of a radiating star. Firstly, the Lie analysis of differential equations leads to a general class of invariant models. This approach provides new classes of solutions to the boundary condition22–24. Secondly, the embedding of the Riemannian manifold into a higher dimensional Euclidean spacetime is a new approach that is interesting. Embedding leads to new radiating models which have a transparent geometric interpretation25–28. Thirdly, the phase plane approach may be utilized to analyze the boundary condition at the surface of the star. Leon et al.29 showed that this approach is applicable in consideration of the global dynamics and asymptotic analysis of a metric which was introduced by Banerjee et al.30,31. This treatment was extended, in a generalized metric, to include a cosmological constant and the electromagnetic field by Maharaj and Govinder32. The phase plane approach helps in describing the asymptotic structure of the radiating model. It also has the advantage of not requiring the analytic forms of the temporal dependence in the gravitational potentials. The main idea in this method is the determining of fixed points – points at which the solutions are constants. A crucial interpretation of these points is that they depict static states. In the event that these points are stable, all trajectories end at those points. The static states will represent the end point of stellar evolution. On the other hand, if the fixed points are unstable, all trajectories move away from those fixed points. The static states then represent an initial static configuration that collapses.

The analysis in32 was relevant for a shear-free spacetime. In general, spherically symmetric spacetimes also allow for nonzero shear. The presence of shear leads to new dynamical effects in the radiating star which produce models that are physically meaningful but different from the shear-free case. Incorporating shear into radiating stellar models is crucial to gain a deeper understanding of the fundamental processes that govern stellar behavior and contribute to the broader field of astrophysics. In particular, shear effects can provide insights into the internal structure and dynamics of stars. The way material moves and deforms in a star can reveal information about its temperature, pressure, and composition. Shear influences the transport of angular momentum and energy, which are critical for understanding how stars evolve over time. It plays a role in processes such as mixing, which can alter a star’s lifecycle. Shear can affect the generation and behavior of magnetic fields within stars. Understanding this interaction helps in studying stellar phenomena such as flares and solar activity. It is therefore important to include the effects of nonzero shear. This is difficult to achieve in general as the boundary condition becomes a differential equation which is not autonomous. To overcome this issue we select a particular spacetime metric which leads to an autonomous differential equation for the evolution of the stellar surface. Admittedly this is a special case but the resulting model has nonzero shear. We expect that the qualitative features of the specific model will be reflected in the general shearing radiating star. The particular spacetime selected for our investigation was also previously introduced by Chan33. The corresponding metric functions lead to nonzero shear, correspond to a star with outgoing null radiation, and describe a collapsing radiating body. Therefore the features that arise in our analysis for the specified metric are physically meaningful.

In this paper, we present and solve the boundary condition at the surface of a shearing star incorporating charge and the cosmological constant in general for the first time. Our model also allows for nonzero pressure anisotropy. We also present constrained solutions in some cases. In order to better understand the long-term temporal evolution of the star, we undertake an asymptotic analysis. We find that nonzero shear leads to dynamical behaviour and phase portraits which are qualitatively different from shear-free matter distributions. This analysis is possible because the temporal behaviour of the radiating star reduces to an autonomous Riccati differential equation. Such equations lend themselves to a qualitative analysis and admit fold bifurcations.

We are then able to comment on the end state of the radiating configuration. The first analysis of the dynamics of this separable form of the potentials in (10) was completed by Chan33. Separable metrics of this type, and its generalizations, have been widely studied by several researchers including the works25,29,39–42. Gravitational collapse, dissipative processes, temperature evolution and inhomogeneously distributed sources have been analysed. We point out that the geometry associated with the potentials (10) have an influence on the mass, luminosity and physical features of a radiating star in higher spacetime dimensions as shown in Maharaj et al.43. Higher order curvature corrections, associated with the Riemann tensor, arising in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity can be studied with separable metrics44 in a radiating star. Therefore, these simple separable metrics have a physical meaning and do provide insights into the behaviour of astrophysical objects in general relativity and modified gravity theories.

Asymptotic analysis

Notwithstanding the existence of the general solution, it is still instructive to undertake an asymptotic analysis of (14)45. This will allow us to more clearly understand the behaviour of B(t) as well as the role of charge and the cosmological constant in shearing collapse. In order to do so, we first calculate the fixed points of (14) by setting . Given this restriction, the fixed points correspond to constant solutions of (14). As such, they also represent static solutions. Determining their stability informs us as to the long-term temporal behaviour of B(t). In the event that a fixed point is stable it will mean that all trajectories move towards that fixed point. This will represent a final static state for a star (See e.g. Govender et al.46.). On the other hand, if a fixed point is unstable, it is repulsive and so all trajectories move away from the fixed point. In this case, no finite static end state exists (See e.g. Chan18, Tewari21 and Naidu et al.7.).

Discussion

We have considered a radiating stellar model with pressure anisotropy, electric field and cosmological constant in general relativity, where the spacetime geometry is specified by potentials with a simple separable form that still admits shear. This form of the geometry was also utilised in the physical model of Chan33. The boundary condition at the surface of the star reduces to a nonlinear temporal equation of the Riccati type. This Riccati equation can be solved exactly and four classes of exact solutions were found. We also performed a comprehensive phase plane analysis of this governing temporal equation. The different cases considered correspond to a neutral fluid (), a neutral fluid with cosmological constant (), a charged fluid () and a charged fluid with cosmological constant ((). In all scenarios, two fixed points of opposite stability are possible. In the case of the unstable fixed point, the trajectories move away from this constant solution. This can be interpreted as the collapse of an initially static configuration. This behaviour generates a basis for stellar models which are initially static, and then undergo collapse in their early evolutionary phase. Therefore our qualitative analysis is useful in building models for X-ray pulsars such as Her X-1 as hypothesised by Govender et al.46. The stable fixed point is attracting; trajectories are drawn towards this constant solution. This reflects the existence of a final static configuration; the collapse is not unending. The spacetime undergoes a fold bifurcation as reflected in Fig. 3. The stable fixed point represents the eventual formation of a superdense cold star.

It is important to note two physical features that arise in our treatment. Firstly, the presence of the cosmological constant and charge allow for more general behaviour than in the neutral case without a cosmological constant. In particular, the latter case did not allow for while both separately and together, the cosmological constant and charge admit such parameter values. This is similar to the shear-free case, where the presence of these quantities also allow for more general behaviour32. This allows for larger families of spacetimes to undergo radiative collapse. Additionally, the presence of these quantities allow our models to describe the collapse of baryonic matter over an unrestricted interval. In the absence of these quantities, our models describe the collapse of dark energy stars for particular intervals as discussed in §4. Secondly, it is interesting to note the role of the areal distance associated with the 2-sphere .

The quantity plays a critical role in the phase plane analysis of the temporal boundary condition as reflected in Table 2. It distinguishes the four cases that arise and there is an interplay between the areal distance , the charge , and the cosmological constant . The transparent role of the areal distance arises because of the simple separable line element (11). In future work it would be worthwhile to investigate this role in the general spherically symmetric geometry with shear and dissipation.

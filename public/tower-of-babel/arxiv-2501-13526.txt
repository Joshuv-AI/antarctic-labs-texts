Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be an Artinian local ring. Note there exists an Artinian Gorenstein local ring

g(A)=\min\{\ell(B)-\ell(A)\mid\text{$B$ is Gorenstein Artin local ring mapping onto $A$ }\}.

We note that
g(A)=0
if and only if

is Gorenstein. Observe that

if and only if

is not Gorenstein and

for a Gorenstein Artin local ring

[
9
]
. In their paper
[
5
]
, C. Huneke and A. Vraciu refer to these rings as Teter’s rings.

We generalize the definition of Teter rings to higher dimensions. Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
. We assume
A
is a quotient of a regular local ring
T
. Let

denote the multiplicity of

. We note that there exists a Gorenstein local ring

mapping onto
A
with
\dim B=\dim A
. Let

denote the set of all Gorenstein local rings

which are quotients of
T
, mapping onto
A
with
\dim B=\dim A
.
Set

g(A)=\min\{e(B)-e(A)\mid\text{$B\in U(A)$ }\}.

We note that
g(A)=0
if and only if

is a Gorenstein local ring. If
g(A)=1
then we call

to be a
Teter
ring. If

such that
e(B)-e(A)=1
then we call

to be a

of

. Note we are
not
asserting that

is uniquely determined by

.

In general giving an intrinsic characterization of Teter’s ring seems to be difficult. However for domains we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local domain. Assume
A
is a quotient of a regular local ring
T
. Assume
A
is not Gorenstein. Let
\omega_{A}
denote the canonical module of
A
.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

A
is a Teter ring.

(ii)

There exists a proper ideal
J
of
A
with
\omega_{A}\cong J
and
\operatorname{codim}A/J\leq 1
.

Furthermore if
A
is a Teter-ring then the Teter Gorenstein approximation of

is unique (up to isomorphism).

Also if
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a local Noetherian ring then
\operatorname{codim}R=\mu(\mathfrak{m})-\dim R
(here
\mu(\mathfrak{m})
denotes the minimal number of generators of
\mathfrak{m}
).

If
(R,\mathfrak{n})
is a Noetherian local ring then

is the associated graded ring of

. We say

has a Teter Gorenstein approximation

with

Gorenstein.
Being strongly Gorenstein is a very restricted property. We show

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Assume
A
is a quotient of a regular local ring
T
. If

is strongly Teter then

is Cohen-Macaulay.

Theorem
1.2
can be used to give examples of rings which are Teter but not strongly Teter, see
5.7
.
We give an intrinsic characterization of Cohen-Macaulay local domains which are strongly Teter.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local domain. Assume
A
is a quotient of a regular local ring
T
. Assume
A
is not Gorenstein.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

A
is a strongly Teter ring.

(ii)

A
satisfies the following properties.

(a)

is Cohen-Macaulay.

(b)

of

In

Recall that a graded algebra over a field has finite representation type if it has up to isomorphisms and shifts in the grading, only a finite number of indecomposable maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules.

We first show

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

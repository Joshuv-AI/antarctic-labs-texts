Optimization and evaluation of different fast acting tablet formulations containing bisoprolol fumarate
Authors: Mohamed Abbas Ibrahim, Sultan Khalaf Almutairi, Gamal Mohamed Mahrous
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to develop and evaluate orally disintegrating tablets (ODTs) and sublingual tablets (SLTs) containing Bisoprolol fumarate (BF) to enhance the onset of action and provided rapid relief of these cardiovascular diseases. For ODTs, experimental design was used to evaluate the effect of different independent factors on dissolution efficiency (DE%), disintegration time (DT), and hardness. Two independent variables, namely superdisintegrant concentration (crospovidone; CPV; X1) and taste masking agent concentration (Eudragit E100; X2) were selected to study their impact on the attributes of the BF ODTs. For SLTs, super disintegrant concentration (X1) and mixing time (X2) were selected to study their impact on SLTs attributes, as in the case of ODTs. The optimized ODTs formula (based on 8.3% CPV and 3.7% Eudragit) showed DE% of 85%, disintegration time (DT) of 39.3 s, and hardness of 2.55 Kp. Moreover, the optimized SLTs formula (based on 9.26% CPV and 16.2 min mixing time) showed DE% of 92%, DT was 35 s and hardness was 3.6 Kp. The optimized formula was compressed into an ODT and SLTs and studied according to USP criteria. All tablet examinations (Friability, Hardness, Disintegration Time, Drug Content, and Weight Variation) were within the accepted values and showed a high stability after six months of storage under accelerated conditions. The obtained results of the current study verified the efficacious formulation of BF as ODTs and SLTs to improve the onset of action and convalesce patient compliance in managing hypertension and arrhythmia.Keywords: Bisoprolol fumarate, Orally disintegrating tablets, Sublingual tablets, Optimization, Stability

Introduction

Bisoprolol fumarate (BF) is a selective β1 adrenergic receptor blocker with a molecular weight of 766.97 d, and it is freely soluble in water, chloroform, ethanol and methanol. BF is used for the management of hypertension in a dose of 1.25 mg to 10 mg/day, with a high an oral bioavailability of 90%, along with elimination half-life of 10–12 h (Minushkina 2012). Because it selectively and competitively blocks catecholamine (adrenalin) stimulation of beta-1 adrenergic receptors (β1 adrenoreceptor) mainly found in the heart muscle cells and heart conduction tissue (cardio specific), BF exerts a cardioprotective action (Lymperopoulos et al. 2013; Kirch et al. 1987). In addition, BF results in decreases of heart rate contractility, which on turn, results in reducing blood pressure, and it is considered as preventive measures for heart attacks and cardiac ischemia. These actions lead to decreasing the hearts demand for oxygen and nutrients (Nystoriak and Bhatnagar 2018).

Oral disintegrating or orodispersible tablets (ODTs), known as oral disintegrating tablets in the United States, are a specialized form of oral medication designed for quick disintegration and absorption. According to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), these tablets dissolve rapidly, usually within seconds, when placed on the tongue (FDA, 2008). Orally Disintegrating Tablets (ODTs) are a significant advancement in pharmaceutical technology, offering a range of patient benefits Deepak et al. (2012). However, they also come with inherent challenges. A prime concern with ODTs is the cost of production. In terms of stability, ODTs present unique concerns. Their sensitivity to environmental factors such as temperature and humidity makes them more susceptible to degradation, thereby demanding specialized packaging solutions to uphold their stability (Mizumoto et al. 2005).

Sublingual drug delivery presents a compelling alternative to traditional oral administration, offering advantages such as rapid onset of action and increased bioavailability. By placing a medication beneath the tongue, it is absorbed through the sublingual mucosa, bypassing the gastrointestinal tract and avoiding first-pass metabolism in the liver. This results in a more efficient and quicker therapeutic effect, making it particularly useful for medications that require rapid absorption or suffer from poor oral bioavailability (Alexander et al. 2012). the swift action of sublingual drugs can be life-saving (Narang and Sharma 2011). ODTs and SLTs could be a preferred choice especially in patients who may present difficulty in swallowing the conventional tablets and capsules. Also, formulation of the drug as ODTs and SLTs can overcome some challenges associated with ODTs and conventional tablets discussed previously (A. Abdelbary et al. 2009; Ciper and Bodmeier 2005). In addition, STLs can enhance the efficacy of BF low dose (2.5 mg) in treating cardiac arrhythmias.

Despite the availability of conventional bisoprolol fumarate tablets and previous reports on orally disintegrating and sublingual formulations of β-blockers, there is a lack of systematic studies addressing the simultaneous optimization, comparative evaluation, and stability assessment of bisoprolol fumarate formulated as fast-acting oral dosage forms using a Quality by Design (QbD) approach. Most published studies focus on either formulation development or in vitro performance, with limited emphasis on formulation robustness and stability under accelerated storage conditions, which is a critical concern for orally disintegrating dosage forms due to their sensitivity to moisture and mechanical stress.

Furthermore, bisoprolol fumarate is frequently administered at low doses for rapid cardiovascular control, where a fast onset of action and improved patient compliance are clinically desirable. Developing optimized orally disintegrating tablets (ODTs) and sublingual tablets (SLTs) with predictable performance and adequate stability remains an unmet need. Therefore, the present study aims to design, optimize, and evaluate BF-loaded ODTs and SLTs using a QbD-based experimental design, with particular emphasis on critical quality attributes, dissolution behavior, and accelerated stability performance.

The effect of formulation parameters (superdisintegrant, taste masking agent and mixing time on tablets attributes were evaluated and optimized. furthermore, stability of optimized formula during six months of storage under accelerated conditions was evaluated.

Material and methods

Materials

Bisoprolol fumarate (BF) was obtained from Hikma pharma (Riyadh, Saudi Arabia). Methanol (HPLC grade) and microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) was purchased from Riedel-de Haën (Seelze, Germany). Crospovidonee (CPV) was kindly supplied by DPE pharma, GmbH & Co. KG (Germany). Sodium stearyl fumarate (SSF) was procured from Avonchem (Macclesifeld, UK). Tablet evaluations

Weight variation

Twenty tablets from each batch were individually weighed (Analytical balance, Shimadzu, EB-3200D, Tyoto, Japan), and the average tablet weight with standard deviation was calculated.

Thickness

The thickness of pre-weighed 10 tablets of each formula was measured using a micrometer. The average thickness, standard deviation and relative standard variation were reported.

Hardness

Ten tablets with known weight and thickness were studied for their hardness using tablet hardness tester (Pharma test GmbH, Hainburg, Germany). For of each batch; the average hardness, standard deviation and relative standard variation were reported (Alshora et al. 2024).

In vitro disintegration test

In vitro disintegration of the manufactured ODTs and SLTs containing BF was evaluated using disintegration tester (Electrolab, ED-21, Pumbai, India) according to the USP30-NF25 requirements. In each of the six tubes of the basket, one tablet was placed, and disintegration tester was operated at 37° ± 2 °C, using distilled water as the immersion medium. The required time for each tablet complete disintegration was calculated, and the standard deviation was computed (Mahrous et al. 2016).

In vitro dissolution of BF from ODTs and SLTs

In vitro BF dissolution from the manufactured ODTs and SLTs was studied using the USP 30 (Apparatus II) paddle method according to the USP30-NF25 dissolution monograph “dissolution procedure” for immediate release dosage forms. The in vitro dissolution study was carried out in 500 mL of phosphate buffer pH 6.8 ± 0.05, which is commonly used to simulate the physiological pH conditions of the oral cavity for evaluating orally disintegrating and sublingual dosage forms. The selected medium has been widely reported in the literature for assessing drug release behavior from fast-disintegrating tablets (Chaves et al. 2020). The dissolution experiment was performed on 6 tablets of each formula. Dissolution tester (Model 85 T, Caleva Ltd, USA) was used, and the in vitro dissolution was carried out in 500 ml of simulated saliva fluid. The paddle was rotated at a speed of 75 RPM at 37 ± 0.5 ºC. Samples were withdrawn at specified time intervals for ODTs (5, 10, 15, 20and 30 min) and specified time intervals for SLTs (2, 5, 10, 15 and 30 min), adequately diluted and analyzed in HPLC at wavelength 225 nm (Ibrahim and Abou El Ela 2017; Mahrous et al. 2019).

For the analytical procedures, UHPLC analytical method described by Panainte et al. (Panainte et al. 2015) was utilized. The mobile phase consisted from a mixture of methanol: acetonitrile: 45 mM potassium dihydrogen phosphate buffer (pH 3.0) in a ratio of 30: 25:45. The mobile phase was pumped into the system with a flow rate of 0.15 mL/min. Samples (3 µL) were injected into a chromatographic Eclipse XDB C18 column (150 mm × 4.6mm, 5 m) at a wavelength 225 nm and the applied column temperature was 30 ºC.

After obtaining the optimized formula from the matrix of the design program ODTs and SLTs formulations were selected for studying the effect of storage on tablet physical properties and drug release. Impact of independent factors on the hardness

Considering ODTs, crospovidone's role in tablet formulations primarily relates to its capacity as a superdisintegrant, though it can also contribute to the mechanical strength of the tablet to a certain degree. However, the ANOVA results (Table 5) for hardness with high p-values indicate that within the tested concentration range, Crospovidone does not significantly affect the hardness of Bisoprolol fumarate ODTs. Eudragit E100, commonly employed as a taste-masking agent, might be expected to influence hardness due to its film-forming properties, which can add cohesiveness to a tablet matrix. However, the p-value exceeding 0.05 indicating that its concentration does not have a significant impact on hardness within this specific formulation. Also, Fig. 7 indicates that no interaction could be observed between CPV and Eudragit on the tablet hardness. Moreover, the 3D response surface plot Fig. 1 estimates the effect of CPV (X1) and Eudragit (X2) on the hardness (Y1) of Bisoprolol fumarate ODTs, and it shows that the highest tablet hardness values were recorded in the case of higher levels of CPV and Eudragit. Table 5 shows the properties of BF ODTs. Tablet hardness values were found in the range of 2.0 to 4.7 Kp. Lowest tablet hardness (2 Kp) was recorded in formulation #2, In which lowest Eudragit and medium CPV levels were used in tablet manufacture. Table 4 showed that the lowest tablet hardness value (2 Kp) was recorded for formula 2, which was composed of the lowest Eudragit level along with medium CPV level. In addition, other ODT formulations showed hardness values from 3.31 to 4.37 Kp.

Effect of independent parameters on the responses of BF loaded SLTs

Impact of Independent Factors on the Tablet Disintegration

The p-value for the model of disintegration time of BF-loaded ODTs is approaching significance, with Eudragit E100 showing a statistically significant effect (p < 0.05), Table 4. Notably, Eudragit E100 (B) has a p-value of 0.0388, which is below 0.05, indicating that it has a significant effect on disintegration time at the 95% confidence level. The interaction term's high p-value suggests that the combined effects of these two factors do not significantly affect disintegration time more than their individual effects. Also, a slight and insignificant antagonistic effect on tablet disintegration, while Eudragit level resulted in pronounced and significant antagonistic effect on the response. Also, the 3 D response surface plot (Fig. 1) showed that the longest disintegration time for the tested ODTs was observed at low CPV levels along with highest Eudragit levels.

From Table 4, rapid tablet disintegration (disintegration time = 20 s) was observed in the case of ODT formula number 6, which is composed of 2% Eudragit and 2% CPV, while the prolonged disintegration time (150 s) was noticed in the formula composed of 10% Eudragit and 6% CPV (F3). These findings support the statistical findings that were mentioned previously.

The statistical analysis of the effects of Crospovidone (X1) and mixing time (X2) on the disintegration of SLTs reveals that Crospovidone (A-CPV), as a tablet superdisintegrant exerted a significant antagonistic effect on the DT of the manufactured SLTs (P value = 0.0188), Fig. 2. The sum of squares is 453.44 with an F-value of 21.61, indicating a highly significant effect on tablet disintegration, as shown in Table 7. Mixing time (B) has a sum of squares of 43.52 and an F-value of 2.07, indicating a marginally significant effect. Moreover, the interaction between Crospovidone and mixing time (AB) has a sum of squares of 15.37 and an F-value of 0.7323, showing no significant interaction effect. The quadratic terms for mixing time (B2) showed significant effect (P = 0.0434) with sum of squares of 238.35 (F-value of 11.36). The residual sum of squares is 20.98. These findings suggest that Crospovidone significantly impacts tablet disintegration, with mixing time and its quadratic term also playing significant roles. Fastest disintegration was observed in case of SLT formula F6 (25 ± 3 s) that prepared on the basis of using highest CPV level along with medium mixing time (15 min), as shown in 3D response surface plot (Fig. 2) and Table 6. SLT formula F5 that was prepared by combing medium CPV level with medium mixing time also showed fast disintegration (33.33 ± 1.2 s).

Impact of Independent Factors on the dissolution efficiency (DE%)

Table 5 shows the effects of independent parameters on BF ODTs dissolution efficiency (DE%). Neither Crospovidone nor Eudragit E100 showed a statistically significant impact on DE% of Bisoprolol fumarate ODTs, indicated by p-values greater than 0.05. It is clear that the effect of CPV on the drug dissolution was very slight and insignificant, while Eudragit showed an enhanced effect on the response. The interactive effect of CPV and Eudragit on the response (Fig. 1) showed a slight enhancing effect on the drug DE%. Moreover, the 3D response surface plot (Fig. 1) for the combined effects of CPV and Eudragit on the DE% of BF indicated that the highest DE% values were observed at medium Eudragit values along with all CPV levels. Table 4 and the histogram in Fig. 3 revealed that the highest DE% of the drug were detected in case of F7 and F9 (about 92%). ODT formulation F7 contained 6%Eidragit and 6% CPV and F9 contained 2% Eudragit and 10% CPV.

Effect of Crospovidone (X1) and Eudragit (X2) on dissolution efficiency of BF from ODTs

The statistical analysis of the effects of Crospovidone (X1) and mixing time (X2) on drug dissolution efficiency from SLTs showed that none of the factors or their interactions are statistically significant. For Crospovidone (X1; CPV), the mean square is 17.26 with an F-value of 0.5157 and a p-value of 0.5245, while, mixing time (X2) has a mean square of 28.00, an F-value of 0.8365, and a p-value of 0.4278 as depicted in Table 7. Moreover, the interactive and quadratic effects of both X1 and X2 exhibited no significant effect on the response. Effect of storage in the accelerated conditions on the properties of the optimized BF ODTs

Effect of storage on tablet physical properties and drug release on optimized formula

A 6-month stability study was performed for the optimized BF ODTs at 40 °C ± 75%RH. Figure 7 shows the AV of ODTs from 1,2,3 and 6 months of storage at previous conditions. Content uniformity was calculated as a term of AV. The AV of the tablets after one month was 9.1 and after 6 months was 11.55, which met the USP guidelines requirements. The optimized BF ODT formulation was subjected to accelerated stability testing at 40 ± 2 °C and 75 ± 5% RH for six months. The disintegration time of the optimized formulation was not significantly affected by storage (Fig. 7), indicating preservation of its fast-disintegrating characteristics. However, tablet hardness showed a statistically significant increase from 2.55 ± 0.32 Kp to 4.5 ± 0.73 Kp after six months of storage, representing an approximately 80% increase.

Although this increase in hardness is notable, the tablets remained within acceptable hardness limits for orally disintegrating tablets, and no adverse impact on disintegration time or dissolution efficiency was observed. The dissolution profiles before and after storage were comparable, confirming that the functional performance of the optimized ODTs was maintained despite the increase in mechanical strength.

The optimized SLTs formulation was selected for testing the effect of storage under accelerated stability conditions on tablet physical properties and drug release. This formulation was selected because of their low disintegration time and high dissolution coefficient and optimize hardness properties. It is clear from Fig. 8 that the disintegration time of optimized formulation has not significantly affected by storage under accelerated conditions. The optimized SLTs showed disintegration time of 40.8 ± 2.7 s after storage for 6 months compared to 35 ± 2.0 s for the freshly prepared batch. Furthermore, the acceptance value for the drug content uniformity was in range 14–0.16–14.80, which is less than 15, that which meets the USP guidelines requirements. Additionally. Discussion

It is clearly evident from results that CPV and Eudragit exhibited insignificant agonistic impact on ODTs hardness. the obtained results are in accordance with the literature, suggesting that disintegrants at typical use levels do not substantially alter tablet hardness (Mostafa et al. 2013). In addition, Eudragit E100 did not have a significant impact on hardness within this specific formulation, which is consistent with findings from similar studies (Quinteros et al. 2008). In contrast, for SLTs hardness, both CPV and mixing time exhibited insignificant agonistic impact on tablet hardness.

Increasing CPV levels along with reducing Eudragit levels in the manufactured ODTs results in reducing tablet disintegration time. This aligns with the understanding that taste masking agents can hinder water penetration and thus slow down disintegration by covering drug nanoparticles’ surfaces (Bolhuis et al. 1997). Crospovidone, conversely, did not show a significant effect alone, but its role in enhancing disintegration by wicking moisture into the tablet is well-documented (Aulton and Taylor 2013).

For SLTs disintegration, CPV (the tablet superdisintegrant) showed a significant antagonistic effect on the responses. In addition, the interaction between CPV and mixing time had a significant impact on the SLTs disintegration time. which Studies by Gohel et al. (2005) and Patel et al. (2011) have similarly highlighted the dominant role of superdisintegrants like CPV in reducing disintegration time, while the influence of mixing time is generally less significant.

Higher dissolution rates (DE%) of BF from the manufactured ODTs were observed in case of using medium Eudragit E 100 levels along with all CPV levels. The literature revealed that the superdisintegrant can influence the onset of dissolution by facilitating rapid disintegration, which is a precursor to effective dissolution (Dahiya et al., 2005). As for Eudragit E100, while it did not show a significant effect, there was a trend that suggested potential influence. This is interesting because, as a film-former, Eudragit could potentially delay the release of the drug, affecting DE% (Douroumis 2011).

For the SLTs, when comparing the results of the current study with other studies, it is uncommon for dissolution efficiency to be less sensitive to factors like disintegrant concentration and mixing time, particularly in formulations where other factors (such as the drug's solubility or the tablet's compression force) play a more dominant role. For instance, research by Holmes et al. (2012) on the dissolution profiles of immediate-release tablets found that while superdisintegrants are critical for disintegration, their impact on dissolution efficiency can vary depending on the drug's physicochemical properties and the overall formulation matrix.

Similarly, studies like those by (Patel et al., 2011) have shown that in some cases, the dissolution efficiency of a formulation is more influenced by the drug's intrinsic solubility and the presence of surfactants or other solubilizing agents rather than by the concentration of disintegrants or the mixing time. In conclusion, the study suggests that within the tested ranges, neither crospovidone concentration nor mixing time significantly.

In addition, the quadratic model is poorly fit for the most tested responses due to that the predicted and adjusted as well as overall R2 values of the tested responses for both ODTs and SLTs formulations were faraway from 1. However, the quadratic models were applied for these responses for two reasons, first to test the effect of interactive and quadratic levels on these responses beside the main effects. In addition, the study aimed to test opposing effects of CPV and Eudragit levels on the response of ODTs, and CPV and mixing time on the response of SLTs, and this could be achieved by studying the interactive and quadratic levels of the tested independent factors.

The current study aimed to evaluate the stability of BF in the formulated ODTs, as the drug stability in fast dissolving dosage forms (tablets or films) has not been focused in previous studies. There are few studies about the stability of BF in conventional tablets. The results of 6 months storage-stability studies of BF tablets obtained by Panainte et al. (Panainte et al. 2018)) indicated the drug was stable under stress condition (40 ± 2 °C, RH 75 ± 5%) in terms of drug content and tablets’ appearance.

The observed increase in tablet hardness during accelerated storage may be attributed to solid-state consolidation and moisture-induced particle rearrangement, which are commonly reported phenomena in orally disintegrating tablets stored under high temperature and humidity conditions. Moisture uptake can enhance interarticular bonding and reduce tablet porosity, leading to increased mechanical strength over time. Additionally, polymer–excipient interactions, particularly involving microcrystalline cellulose and Eudragit E100, may contribute to gradual hardening during storage (Al-Meshal et al. 1989). Importantly, despite the significant increase in hardness, the optimized ODTs maintained rapid disintegration and unchanged dissolution profiles, suggesting that the formulation achieved a favorable balance between mechanical robustness and fast disintegration behavior. Similar stability-related hardening effects without detrimental impact on tablet performance have been reported for other orally disintegrating tablet formulations in the literature, supporting the stability and reliability of the optimized BF ODT formulation.

Conclusion

This study aimed to optimize the formulation of bisoprolol fumarate (BF) as ODTs and as SLTs. BF, being a Class I drug with high solubility, is used as an emergency treatment to manage hypertension and arrhythmia. The objective was to enhance the onset of action, thereby improving bioavailability and patient compliance. Statistical experimental design was employed to determine the optimal formulation for both delivery systems, ensuring that the desired properties of rapid disintegration time (DT), appropriate hardness, and high drug efficiency (DE%) were met. Stability studies were also conducted to validate the formulation under accelerated conditions.

The optimized ODT formulation was found to contain 3.7% Eudragit and 8.3% CPV, providing a rapid DT, desirable hardness, and a high DE%. Similarly, the optimized SLT formulation was achieved with a 15-min mixing time and 9.26% CPV. The accelerated stability study for six months demonstrated that both ODT and SLT formulations remained stable, with all acceptance values (AV) being below 15, as per USP standards.

The data findings of this study demonstrated the successful formulation of BF as both ODTs and SLTs to enhance the onset of action and improve patient compliance in managing hypertension and arrhythmia.

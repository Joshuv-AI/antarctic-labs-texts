Abstract
Two nested classes of discrete-time linear time-invariant systems, which differ by the set of periodic signals that they leave invariant, are studied. The first class preserves the property of periodic monotonicity (period-wise unimodality). The second class is invariant to signals with at most two sign changes per period, and requires that periodic signals with zero sign changes are mapped to the same kind. Tractable characterizations for each system class are derived by the use and extension of total positivity theory via geometric interpretations. Central to our results is the characterization of sequentially convex contours via consecutive minors. Our characterizations also extend to the loop gain of Lur’e feedback systems as the considered signals sets are invariant under common static non-linearities, e.g., ideal relay, saturation, sigmoid function, quantizer, etc. The presented developments aim to form a base for future signal-based fixed-point theorems towards the prediction of self-sustained oscillations. Our examples on relay feedback systems indicate how periodic monotonicity preservation gives rise to useful insights towards this goal.

3
Cyclic Variation Bounding Properties

The goal of the present study is to provide tractable characterization of convolution operators with cyclic variation diminishing/bounding properties. In order to define and study these, we will need the following notions and background results.

3.1
Variation Bounding

Definition 1

(Variation)
.

The
variation
of a vector
u\in\mathds{R}^{n}
is defined as the number of sign-changes in
u
, i.e.,
\textnormal{S}^{-}[u]:=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathds{1}_{\mathds{R}_{<0}}(\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{u}_{i+1})
,
\textnormal{S}^{-}[0]:=-1
, where
\tilde{u}\in\mathds{R}^{m}
is the vector that results from deleting all zeros in
u
. Further, we define the
strict variation
as
\textnormal{S}^{+}[u]:=\limsup_{p\to u}\textnormal{S}^{-}[p]
, i.e., the maximum number of possible sign changes in
u
by allowing each zero in
u
to be replaced by
\pm 1
. The same notations are also used for sequences
u\in\ell_{\infty}
.

Although
\textnormal{S}^{-}[u]\leq\textnormal{S}^{+}[u]
, equality does not necessarily hold, e.g.,
\textnormal{S}^{-}[\begin{pmatrix}1&0&2\end{pmatrix}]=\textnormal{S}^{-}[\begin{pmatrix}1&2\end{pmatrix}]=0
, but
\textnormal{S}^{+}[\begin{pmatrix}1&0&2\end{pmatrix}]=\textnormal{S}^{-}[\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&2\end{pmatrix}]=2
.
In particular, the following limit inequalities hold
[
20
, Lemma 5.1.1]
:

Lemma 3
.

Let
x:\mathds{R}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
be such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=u\in\mathds{R}^{n}
. Then,
\textnormal{S}^{-}[u]\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\textnormal{S}^{+}[x(t)]
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\textnormal{S}^{-}[x(t)]\leq\textnormal{S}^{+}[u]
.

Based on these definitions, we can also define their so-called cyclic analogues.

Definition 2

(Cyclic Variation)
.

For a vector
u\in\mathds{R}^{n}
, its
cyclic variation
is defined by

\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[u]:=\sup_{i\in(1:n)}\textnormal{S}^{-}[\begin{pmatrix}u_{i}&u_{i+1}&\dots&u_{n}&u_{1}&\dots&u_{i}\end{pmatrix}]

and its
strict cyclic variation
by
\textnormal{S}^{+}_{c}[u]:=\sup_{i\in(1:n)}\textnormal{S}^{+}[\begin{pmatrix}u_{i}&u_{i+1}&\dots&u_{n}&u_{1}&\dots&u_{i}\end{pmatrix}]

It is important to note that
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[u]
and
\textnormal{S}^{+}_{c}[u]
are always
even
for
u\neq 0
, because

\displaystyle\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[u]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\textnormal{S}^{-}[u]+1&\text{if }\textnormal{S}^{-}[u]\text{ is odd},\\
\textnormal{S}^{-}[u]&\text{if }\textnormal{S}^{-}[u]\text{ is even},\end{cases}

(11)

and analogously for
\textnormal{S}^{+}_{c}[u]
. With slight abuse of notation, we also apply these definitions to
u\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
and mean that we evaluate the cyclic variations of

, which via
eq.

11

\textnormal{S}^{-}[u(0:T-1)]
and
\textnormal{S}^{+}[u(0:T-1)]
, respectively.

Investigations of linear variation bounding/diminishing mappings have a long history in areas such as interpolation theory, complex analysis, statistics, mechanics, etc. (see, e.g., the monographs

belonging to the classes

4.1
Monotonicity & Convexity

We begin by showing that for
m=2
and
m=3
,
Proposition

2
follows from the locality properties of monotonicity and convexity, respectively. Our characterization of convex contours will then follow from these geometric interpretations.

Definition 7

(Monotonicity)
.

f:\mathcal{D}\subset\mathds{R}\to\mathds{R}
is called (strictly) monotonically decreasing if
f(t_{i_{1}})(>)\geq f(t_{i_{2}})
for all
t_{i_{1}}<t_{i_{2}}
from
\mathcal{D}
.
f
is (strictly) monotonically increasing if
-f
is (strictly) monotonically decreasing.

The following characterization of monotonicity by sign consistency follows from direct computations:

Lemma 7
.

Let
f:\{t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}\}\to\mathds{R}
with
t_{i}<t_{i+1}
for all
i\in(1:n-1)
and

M^{f}:=\begin{bmatrix}f(t_{1})&1\\
f(t_{2})&1\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
f(t_{n})&1\end{bmatrix}

Then, the following are equivalent:

i)

f
is (strictly) monotonically decreasing.

ii)

M^{f}_{[2]}(>)\geq 0
.

iii)

For all
i\in(1:n-1)
it holds that

\det\begin{bmatrix}f(t_{i})&1\\
f(t_{i+1})&1\end{bmatrix}(>)\geq 0

Alternatively,
Lemma

7
can also be verified by noticing that a function is monotone if and only if it crosses any value in
\mathds{R}
at most once and cannot remain in a value under the additional strictness assumption. As this property is invariant with respect to scaling and translation, this translates to

\forall u\in\mathds{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}:(\textnormal{S}^{+}[u_{1}f+u_{2}\mathbf{1}])\;\textnormal{S}^{-}[u_{1}f+u_{2}\mathbf{1}]\leq 1,

which by
1 ii)
and
1 iii)
leads to the same conclusion. Moreover,
Proposition

2
in case of
m=2
can be recovered from
Lemma

7
by noticing that
if the second column of
X\in\mathds{R}^{n\times 2}
consists of elements with the same strict sign, then by
1 i)

X=\begin{bmatrix}x_{12}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&x_{n2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{x_{11}}{x_{12}}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{x_{n1}}{x_{n2}}&1\end{bmatrix}\in\text{(S)SC}_{2}

(13)

if and only if the second factor is
\text{(S)SC}_{2}
. Interpreting this second factor as the matrix
M^{f}
in
Lemma

7
, it follows from

that it is enough to verify that only consecutive
2
-minors share the same sign. This, however, is equivalent to checking the consecutive
2
-minors of
X
itself. Next, we will move on to the case of
m=3
and its links to convexity.

Definition 8

(Convexity/Concavity)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}:=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}
with with
t_{i}<t_{i+1}
for all
i\in(1:n-1)
. Then,
f:\mathcal{D}\to\mathds{R}
is
(strictly) convex
if for all fixed
\tau\in\mathcal{D}

s(t,\tau):=\frac{f(t)-f(\tau)}{t-\tau}=\frac{f(\tau)-f(t)}{\tau-t}

(14)

is (strictly) monotonically increasing in
t
on
\mathcal{D}\setminus\{\tau\}
.
f
is called
(strictly) concave
if
-f
is (strictly) convex.

Our definition of convexity via the so-called
slope function

s(t,\tau)
is a well-known equivalence in the theory of convex functions
[
18
, Proposition 1.1.4]
on
\mathds{R}
. It follows by
[
18
, Proposition 5.3.1]
that the
piecewise-linear continuous extension

\bar{f}:[t_{1},t_{n}]\to\mathds{R}
of

defined by

\bar{f}(t):=f(t_{i})+s(t_{i+1},t_{i})(t-t_{i}),\;t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}

(15)

is convex if and only if its right-derivatives are monotonically increasing – by the piecewise-linearity, these derivatives correspond to
s(t_{i+1},t_{i})
. Expressing the monotonicity of the slope function

via
Lemma

7
gives the following characterizations in terms of sign-consistency, which is proven in
Appendix

B
:

Lemma 8
.

Let
f:\{t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}\}\to\mathds{R}
with
t_{i}<t_{i+1}
for all
i\in(1:n-1)
and

M^{f}:=\begin{bmatrix}t_{1}&f(t_{1})&1\\
t_{2}&f(t_{2})&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
t_{n}&f(t_{n})&1\\
\end{bmatrix}

Then, the following are equivalent:

i)

f
is (strictly) convex.

ii)

\bar{f}
in
eq.

15
is convex (and no three points of

are co-linear).

iii)

M^{f}_{[3]}(>)\geq 0
.

iv)

it holds that

\det\begin{bmatrix}t_{i}&f(t_{i})&1\\
t_{i+1}&f(t_{i+1})&1\\
t_{i+2}&f(t_{i+2})&1\end{bmatrix}(>)\geq 0.

Using

and

5.2
Periodically Monotonicity Preserving Kernels

In the following, we will derive our characterizations of convolution operators
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PMP}(T)
,
g\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
. We begin by noticing that if
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CVB}_{2}(T)
, then
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PMP}(T)
: in fact, for any
\gamma\in\mathds{R}
and
u\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
, it holds that
\mathcal{S}_{g}u-\gamma\mathbf{1}=\mathcal{S}_{g}(u-\beta\mathbf{1})
for some
\beta\in\mathds{R}
. Thus, if
u\in\textnormal{PM}(T)
, then
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[u-\beta\mathbf{1}]\leq 2
, which is why
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[\mathcal{S}_{g}u-\gamma\mathbf{1}]\leq 2
(and
\textnormal{S}^{+}_{c}[\mathcal{S}_{g}u-\gamma\mathbf{1}]\leq 2
in the strict case) if
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CVB}_{2}(T)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PMP}(T)
. However, by the following equivalences, proven in
Appendix

D
, it can be seen that
\textnormal{CVB}_{2}\neq\textnormal{PMP}(T)
:

Lemma 9
.

For
g\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
, the following are equivalent:

i)

\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PMP}(T)
.

ii)

For all
u\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
with
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[\Delta u]=2
, it holds that
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[\mathcal{S}_{g}\Delta u]\leq 2
(and
\textnormal{S}^{+}_{c}[\mathcal{S}_{g}\Delta u]\leq 2
in the strict case).

iii)

For all
u\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
with
\sum_{i=0}^{T}u(i)=0
and
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[u]=2
, it holds that
\textnormal{S}_{c}^{-}[\mathcal{S}_{g}u]\leq 2
(and
\textnormal{S}_{c}^{+}[\mathcal{S}_{g}u]\leq 2
in the strict case).

In other words, by
9 iii)
,
\textnormal{PMP}(T)
does not require
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[\mathcal{S}_{g}u]\leq 2
for all
u\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
with
\textnormal{S}^{-}_{c}[u]\leq 2
and as such implies viewer constraints than
\textnormal{CVB}_{2}(T)
. A concrete example of
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{PMP}(T)
with
\mathcal{S}_{g}\not\in\textnormal{CVB}_{2}(T)
is given in
Section

6
.

Next, we require to introduce the notion of
convexity-preservation
, which by the subsequent lemma is equivalent to
\textnormal{PMP}(T)
and, thus, allows us to utilize
Theorem

1
for our investigations:

Definition 10

(Convexity Preservation)
.

For
g\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
,
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is called
(strictly) convexity-preserving
(
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CP}(T)
) if
(\mathcal{S}_{g}u_{1},\mathcal{S}_{g}u_{2})\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CC}(T)
for all
u\in\textnormal{CC}(T)
.

Lemma 10
.

For
g\in\ell_{\infty}(T)
,
T\geq 4
, the following are equivalent:

i)

\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PMP}(T)
.

ii)

\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CP}(T)
.

Proof.

We begin by noticing that
u\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CC}(T)
if and only if
\alpha_{1}u_{1}+\alpha_{2}u_{2}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PM}(T)
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{R}^{2}
. Assuming that
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CP}(T)
, it follows with
\alpha_{1}=1
and
u_{2}\equiv 0
that
\mathcal{S}_{g}u_{1}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PM}(T)
for all

. Hence,
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PMP}(T)
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PMP}(T)

\alpha_{1}\mathcal{S}_{g}u_{1}+\alpha_{2}\mathcal{S}_{g}u_{2}=\mathcal{S}_{g}(\alpha_{1}u_{1}+\alpha_{2}u_{2})\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{PM}(T)

\alpha\in\mathds{R}^{2}
and all

\mathcal{S}_{g}\in\textnormal{(S)}\textnormal{CC}(T)
.
∎

In our derivations of
Theorem

3
, it suffices to characterize

Conclusion

In this work, we have provided tractable characterizations for two nested classes of cyclic variation bounding discrete-time LTI systems: periodic monotonicity preservation (PMP) and cyclic
2
-variation diminishing. Central to our derivations is a bridge between algebraic total positivity theory
[
20
]
and its geometric interpretations, which has led to a tractable characterization of sequentially convex contours.

Our results are intended to facilitate the development of new fixed-point theorems that will support the study of self-sustained oscillations in Lur’e feedback systems. The presented examples on relay feedback systems provide a first indication of how the PMP property is important for the existence of such oscillations by limiting the potential set of oscillations and periods. A similar supporting observation has been made recently in
[
4
,
32
]
. In the future, we would also like to extend these results to signals with more sign changes period and complete our preliminary studies in
[
32
]
with general fixed-point theorems.

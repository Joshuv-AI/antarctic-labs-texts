Nano Phosphorylated Pullulan-Integrated Carboxymethyl Chitosan Scaffold for Regeneration of the Pulp-Dentin Complex: An In vitro Study
Authors: M Sornamalar, C Dhanavel, G Balavaishnavi, Shahana Shajahan, Kavya Baskar, S Vijaya Raja
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:To optimize a novel composite scaffold comprising of nano phosphorylated pullulan incorporated carboxymethyl chitosan (nPP-CMC) and to evaluate its cell viability and proliferation in human dental pulp stem cells (hDPSCs).Methodology:Nano phosphorylated pullulan (nPP) in concentrations of 200 mg, 400 mg, 600 mg, 800 mg, and 1 g was vortexed in 20 mL of distilled water, followed by 30 mins of ultrasonication for even dispersion. 1 g of CMC was added to each nPP mixture, blended for 1 hour to form a hydrogel, and freeze-dried for 18 hours at -20°C to yield nPP-CMC scaffolds in ratios of 1:5, 2:5, 3:5, 4:5, and 1:1. These scaffolds were stored at room temperature. The scaffolds were characterized using SEM-EDX, FT-IR, and XRD. The optimized nPP-CMC (group 1), CMC (group 2), and osteogenic medium (group 3) were tested for cell viability with hDPSCs using the MTT assay at 0, 7, 14, and 21 days.Results:Based on the results of MTT assay, the cell viability and proliferation of nPP-CMC scaffold was comparable to that of CMC in the tested time intervals of 7 and 14 days. The 4:5 nPP-CMC revealed a favourable pore size (60–180 μm) that was suitable for cell seeding and was chosen for further experiments. Cell viability/proliferation rates of hDPSCs loaded 4:5 HAnp- CMC at 21st day was significantly greater than that at 7th day (P < .05).Conclusion:Hence, within the limitations of this in vitro study, it could be concluded that the novel experimental scaffold comprising of nPP-CMC (4:5) is an effective biomaterial that possess good bioactivity and biocompatibility properties. Thus, nPP-CMC biomaterial will have a great futuristic potential for regeneration of pulp dentin complex.

INTRODUCTION

Tissue engineering has been primarily driven by the development of new materials that serve as scaffolds to promote cell adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation, and its focus has been mainly on tissue regeneration.[1] Naturally, tissue engineering remains pivotal to the development and translational impact of scaffolds in regenerative dentistry. The primary factors that determine an ideal scaffold for regeneration are its bioactivity, cell viability, and biodegradability, antibacterial activity, anti-inflammatory property, release of growth factors, and interactions with the micro environment.[2] Numerous research have indicated that nano forms exhibit superior qualities compared to their micro-sized counterparts. Bayani et al. (2017)[3] stated that nano forms allow effective ionic mobility, ion exchange, and adsorption capacities.

Nano phosphorylated pullulan [nPPL], a biodegradable material was synthesized which has a properties such as good biocompatibility, bio adhesion with the hard tissue via ionic bonding and tissue regeneration ability.[4] A water-soluble carboxymethylchitosan (CMC) polymer that exhibits sufficient calcium chelating properties, creating several nucleation sites for novel mineral apposition.[5] It exhibits better hydrophilicity, an important property, a biomaterial should possess when placed in contact with tissue fluids. Hence, CMC has been used as a scaffold template with nPPL and can be effectively used for dentin regeneration.[6]

In several in vitro studies (Huang GJ et al.), it was reported that the human dental pulp stem cells (hDPSCs) possess immunosuppressive and immunomodulatory properties, which make them a useful cell line for dentin regeneration research investigations, and it has been used in the present study.[7] The MTT assay is used to measure cellular metabolic activity as an indicator of cell viability, proliferation, and cytotoxicity. It has several advantages over other methods of measuring cell viability including its simplicity, speed, and sensitivity.[8] Hence, the aim of this study was to optimize a novel composite scaffold comprising of nano phosphorylated pullulan (nPPL) incorporated carboxymethylchitosan (CMC) and to comparatively evaluate its cell viability and proliferation capabilities on human dental pulp stem cells using MTT assay[3-(4,5-Dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5 Diphenyltetrazolium Bromide].

METHODOLOGY

The experimental multicentric in vitro study was approved by Institutional Review Board and Institutional Ethical Committee with QR CODE (IGIDSIEC2022NRP23PGMSCDE) at Indira Gandhi Institute of Dental Sciences on 06-07-2022.

Study groups were GROUP IA: nPP-CMC-I (1:5), GROUP IB: nPP-CMC-II (2:5), GROUP IC: nPP-CMC-III (3:5), GROUP ID: nPP-CMC-IV (4:5), GROUP IE: nPP-CMC-V (1:1), GROUP II (POSITIVE CONTROL): Carboxymethyl chitosan (CMC), GROUP III (NEGATIVE CONTROL): Osteogenic medium.

Preparation of CMC

10 g chitosan (85% deacetylation) and 13.5 g NaOH were blended in 100 mL of a solution (80 mL 2-propanol, 20 mL water). The mixture was heated at 50°C for 1 hr. Afterward, 15 g monochloroacetic acid in 20 mL 2-propanol was added gradually over 30 mins, and the reaction proceeded for 4 hr at 50°C. The reaction was terminated with 200 mL of 70% ethanol. The resulting CMC was strained, rinsed with 70–90% ethanol, sealed, and stored in a cool, dry environment.

Synthesis of phosphorylated pullulan

300 g pullulan was dissolved in 1360 g aqueous phosphate solution (pH 5.5) and stirred for 1 hour. The solution was boiled at 100°C under reduced pressure to evaporate 90% of it and then heated at 170°C for 5 hours. The reactant was dissolved in distilled water, cooled, ultrafiltered, and freeze-dried.

Preparation of nPPL incorporated CMC scaffold (nPP-CMC)

nPP powder (200, 400, 600, 800 mg, 1 g) was vortexed in 20 ml distilled water for 10 min, followed by 30 min of ultrasonication. 1 gram of CMC was gradually added and then stirred at 22.4 g for 1 hour to form hydrogels. The hydrogels were transferred to petri dishes, deep-frozen at -80°C overnight, and freeze-dried at -20°C for 18 hours to produce nPP-CMC scaffolds (0:5, 5:0, 1:5, 2:5, 3:5, 4:5, 1:1 w/w%). The scaffolds were stored in airtight containers at room temperature.

Passaging and maintenance of HDPSCS – in vitro

After incubation, flasks were washed with PBS to remove nonadherent cells. Adherent cells were detached with 0.25% trypsin/EDTA, centrifuged for 10 mins, washed with DMEM, and reconstituted in complete growth media. Cell suspensions with ≥90% viability were used for further analysis. Cells were stained with trypan blue, and morphology was assessed using a light microscope at 200× magnification. Cultured hDPSCs were maintained in DMEM with 10% FBS, 100U/ml penicillin, 100 mg/ml streptomycin, and 2 mmol/L L-glutamine at 37°C and 5% CO2.

All the experimental materials, i.e., nPP-CMC (1:5), CMC were sterilized under ethylene oxide gas (ETO) in an ETO sterilizer before stem cell studies. nPP-CMC and CMC scaffolds (5 mm diameter × 1 mm thickness) were placed in 96-well plates with an osteogenic medium as control. hDPSCs (1 × 104 cells/well) were seeded in DMEM with 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 mg/mL streptomycin in a 5% CO2, 37°C humidified atmosphere.

MTT assay

nPP-CMC scaffolds (1:5, 2:5, 3:5, 4:5, 1:1) will be placed in 12-well plates for 24 hours under sterile conditions. hDPSCs will be seeded onto the scaffolds. At 7, 14, and 21 days, 100 μL MTT solution will be added, and cells will be incubated for 3 hours. The resulting MTT formazan crystals will be dissolved by replacing the culture medium with 500 μL dimethyl sulfoxide, followed by shaking for 10 minutes. 100 μL of the solution from each well will be transferred to a 96-well plate for absorbance measurement at 570 nm using a microplate reader.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was done using SPSS software version 22.0. The Shapiro–Wilk test was applied to find out the normality distribution. Since MTT assay results did not show normal distribution, nonparametric statistical tests, i.e., Kruskal–Wallis followed by Friedman test, were applied in the present study for interpretation of the cytotoxicity assessment of the experimental materials. The level of significance was set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

SEM analysis of synthesized nPP-CMC showed spherulite morphology with micron-sized crystallites composed of loosely aggregated nanoscale platelets [Table 1]. EDX confirmed Ca2+ and PO42+ ions in a 1.67 ratio. FTIR revealed bands at 1089 cm-1 and 1044 cm-1, indicating the presence of PO43- groups, corresponding to hydroxyapatite. XRD peaks at 26.2° and 32.2° confirmed the crystalline nature of nPP. SEM of 4:5 w/v nPP-CMC showed a highly interconnected porous scaffold with even nPP dispersion and favorable pore size for Ca2+ and PO42+ crystallization.

MTT assay

The MTT cell viability results suggest us that, given test compounds, pullulan control, nPP-CMC I, nPP-CMC-II, nPP-CMC-III, nPP-CMC-IV, and nPP-CMC-V were noncytotoxic as well as cell proliferative in nature on hDPSCs cells with increased cell viability values than the untreated cells on time dependent fashion and confirmed the cell proliferative effect. The observed results depict the cell proliferative effect of biofilms at 1 cm × 1 cm dissolved medium (optimized concentration).

DISCUSSION

MTT assay was used in the study for evaluating the cytotoxicity of the test materials. MTT assay is a calorimetric-based assay where tetra bromide salts are used to measure the activity of mitochondrial hydrogenase enzymes produced by living cells and can detect viable cells even in minute quantities.[9]

Based on the results of this study, it was observed that the hDPSCs were viable in response to test materials at the tested time intervals (0, 7, 14, and 21 days). On the contrary, nPP-CMC scaffold displayed a significantly higher number of viable cells and increased cellular proliferation than that of CMC scaffold at 21st day. It could be mainly due to its mild alkalinity (7.8–8.4) which has been maintained throughout the experimental period. Thus, it can be inferred that nPP-CMC (group ID- 4:5) shows the best proliferative effect on hDPSCs than the other scaffolds. Owing to the aforestated advantages, CMC was used as a scaffold template for nPP in the present dissertation study. This is in accordance with previous study by Chen Z et al., (2015)[10] who examined the mineralizing potential of amorphous calcium phosphate (ACP) infused CMC scaffold and suggested that CMC is an ideal extra cellular matrix (ECM) template for optimal delivery of bioactive agents.

Similarly, a novel attempt of impregnating nPP into CMC in various proportions and its optimization by assessing the in vitro bioactivity was undertaken in the present study. The bioactive performance of a material can be analyzed effectively on hDPSCs and investigated for the regeneration of pulp dentin complex. When compared to other cell cultures like bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells or mouse fibroblast cell lines, testing the dental biomaterials on hDPSCs is more clinically relevant and may simulate the viable stem cells present in the pulp tissue. Hence, hDPSCs were used in the present study.[11] Sayed M et al. (2018),[9] synthesized a similar 3D porous scaffold comprising of CnHAp-CMC in various proportions (1%, 2%, 3%, 4%, 5% w/v).[9]

However, there was a gradual increase in the proliferation of pulp stem cells in response to the osteogenic medium (Group III) from the first day of culture to a peak proliferation of cells at day 7. The reduced cell viability in response to CMC (Group II) could be due to the weaker adhesion of cells on the scaffold surface, and (pH 7 or lower) as reported by Shin et al. (2004).[12] In addition, the presence of nPP clusters on CMC hydrogel increased the surface roughness, thereby promoting effective stem cell adhesion which is in accordance with that of McCullough M et al (2017).[13] MTT assay results of the control group osteogenic medium showed a significant decrease in the cell viability at the end of 21st day when compared to 7 and 14 days.[14]

The inverted biological microscopic observation also revealed greater number of viable cells depicting spindle shaped morphological characteristics resembling that of odontoblast/dentinoblast like cells in response to nPP- CMC scaffold, when compared to that of CMC at 14 and 21 days, respectively. Thus, it can be inferred that the nPP-CMC scaffold can be used potentially for pulp-dentin complex regeneration.

CONCLUSION

Hence, within the limitations of this in vitro study, it could be concluded that the novel experimental scaffold comprising of nPP-CMC (4:5) is an effective biomaterial that possess good bioactivity and biocompatibility properties. Thus, nPP-CMC biomaterial will have a great futuristic potential for regeneration of pulp dentin complex.

PETER: protocol for a randomised controlled trial of the effects of postal and digital invitations and reminders on participation in colorectal cancer screening among young adults
Authors: Leon Klimeck, Thomas Heisser, Utta Petzold, Beata Hennig, Michael Hoffmeister, Hermann Brenner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionColorectal cancer (CRC) screening programmes have reduced CRC incidence and mortality in individuals aged 50 and older in many western countries, whereas they continue to rise among younger adults where CRC screening is not routinely available. In Germany, some insurance plans have started offering screening below age 50, but adherence has remained low and evidence on the preventive potential of CRC screening below age 50 remains limited. The Effekte postalischer und digitaler Einladung und Erinnerung auf die Teilnahme an der Darmkrebsfrüherkennung bei jungen Erwachsenen: Eine randomisierte, kontrollierte Studie trial aims to assess the effect of various invitation schemes on the use of faecal immunochemical test (FIT) and the number of polypectomies and early-detected CRCs.MethodsIn a two-arm randomised controlled trial, 80 000 individuals aged 45–49 and 10 000 individuals aged 40–41 covered by the BARMER health insurance are randomised 1:1. The intervention is an invitation and reminder via paper-based mail, the control is an invitation and reminder via email. Routine healthcare data are used to track FIT use and resulting diagnostic colonoscopies, polypectomies and CRC diagnoses. The primary composite endpoint is the proportion of insured individuals aged 45–49 who underwent a polypectomy or received a CRC diagnosis within 12 months after receiving the initial invitation. Key secondary endpoints include the proportion of individuals who used the FIT, and the proportion of individuals with a positive FIT result.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the ethics committee of the medical faculty of Heidelberg University (S-157/2025). Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications.Trial registration numberDRKS00037817.

Introduction

Background

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common cause of cancer-related deaths in Germany, with approximately 24 000 deaths per year in 2023.1 A common option for early detection and prevention of CRC is the use of faecal-immunochemical tests (FITs), which have been shown to be highly effective in reducing both the incidence and mortality of CRC in a cost-effective manner.2 3 While the incidence and mortality of CRC have declined notably in individuals above the age of 50 in countries with effective CRC screening programmes like the USA or various European countries, the incidence of early-onset CRC (EOCRC, diagnosed before age 50) has increased.4 However, in many countries including Germany, CRC screening is not offered to the average-risk population under the age of 50.5 In response to the rising incidence in younger age groups, population-wide screening from the age of 45 has already been implemented in the USA6 and is under consideration in other countries.

Addressing both the rising EOCRC incidence and the persistently low adherence to CRC screening observed in Germany, a nationwide pilot programme was launched by a major German health insurance plan in 2022 (BARMER, ‘The Digital Colorectal Cancer Screening Program’). In this pilot programme, the insured population aged 40 and older was invited via email, SMS or mail to proactively order an FIT directly to their home via the BARMER smartphone application or the BARMER website. Preliminary observational analyses of the programme suggest that invitations by paper-based mail went along with higher FIT use rates than email invitations. A previous randomised controlled study by our group7 found that in a German context, an invitation via paper-based mail to order a FIT plus a mailed reminder substantially increased FIT use rates in the screening-eligible population above age 50. However, it remains unclear how such an intervention would work in younger cohorts.

Objectives

The primary aim of this trial is to compare the proportion of individuals in whom polypectomy is performed or CRC is detected within 12 months after receiving an invitation and reminder by postal mail versus email, in order to evaluate the preventive potential of these two invitation strategies. Secondary objectives include the rate of FIT use within 12 months, and the utilisation of diagnostic colonoscopies within 12 months after receiving the initial invitation.

Methods

Trial setting

The PETER-study (German Title: PETER: Effekte postalischer und digitaler Einladung und Erinnerung auf die Teilnahme an der Darmkrebsfrüherkennung bei jungen Erwachsenen: Eine randomisierte, kontrollierte Studie) is being conducted by the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ) in cooperation with the statutory health insurance BARMER and the Lebensblicke Foundation and was developed in line with the SPIRIT guidelines (Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials).8 All data are stored within the secured Scientific Data Warehouse (SDWH) of the BARMER health insurance company, which contains pseudonymised administrative medical claims data for individuals insured with the BARMER, including information on sex and year of birth. All data including the follow-up data are collected and recorded by the BARMER and is expected to be complete. The FIT performance (ImmoCARE-C, CARE-Diagnostica, Germany) was validated in a previous study.9

Trial design

The randomised controlled trial (RCT) will be conducted within the framework of BARMER’s Digital Colorectal Cancer Screening Program, in which the insured population aged 40 and above was invited to FIT screening since 2022. The focus of the special care programme by the BARMER is to complement the statutory routine care in Germany by offering their insured population an additional option to order the FIT digitally, starting at the age of 40. Regardless of the FITs offered to participants in this RCT, the entire targeted population of the BARMER insurance must have access to the Digital Colorectal Cancer Screening Program according to the German law. As of 2025, FIT screening for both men and women begins at age 50, hence the study population for this trial is not eligible for FIT screening within routine care.

The study is designed as a two-arm 1:1 RCT and consists of two parts. The first part involves sending invitations and reminders to order a FIT for home use: Arm A receives invitation and reminders to FIT-screening via email. Arm B receives an invitation and reminder to FIT-screening via paper-based mail. All individuals within the BARMER SDWH carry a pseudonymised identifier, which is randomly allocated to either Group A or Group B. Randomisation is stratified by sex. The randomisation schedule is created using computer-generated random numbers for each ID. All randomisation steps are performed by the BARMER health insurance, without knowledge of the included individuals. The process of generating and mailing the invitation letters is fully automated and is conducted by the BARMER and a large mail-handling company. The second part comprises the follow-up phase, assessing the FIT use and its outcomes. This includes, among other measures, the use of diagnostic colonoscopies following the return of FIT samples, as well as the proportion of insured individuals who underwent a polypectomy or received a CRC diagnosis within 12 months after the initial invitation was sent, based on routine data. All collected information is being stored and monitored in a study database by trained staff of the BARMER. The study design is shown in figure 1. Recruitment started in September 2025, with data collection and work-up expected to be completed at the end of 2027.

Intervention and comparator

The intervention is a personal invitation letter to perform a FIT. Arm A will serve as the control and will receive a digital invitation via email, as previously implemented within the framework of BARMER’s Digital Colorectal Cancer Screening Program, arm B will receive a physical invitation sent by paper-based mail. All participants in both study arms will receive an additional reminder 4 weeks after the initial invitation (arm A: email, arm B: paper-based mail), regardless of whether a FIT has already been ordered in the meantime. To order the FIT, participants must visit the BARMER website or scan the provided QR code and fill out a short order form. The completed FIT can be returned via mail. The FIT results can then either be viewed online or received via mail depending on the participants’ preferences.

Based on the 1:1 randomisation, a total of 45 000 participants will be allocated to arm A (email invitation) and 45 000 participants to arm B (paper-based mail invitation). This includes 40 000 individuals aged 45–49 for the main study cohort and 5000 individuals aged 40–41 for the exploratory cohort per arm. Individuals in the exploratory cohort are invited for the first time, individuals in the main cohort may have received an invitation in previous invitation rounds in 2022 or 2023.

Follow-up of the use of screening methods

For a total of 12 months after invitation to the FIT screening programme, individuals are tracked via health insurance claims data. Anonymised claims data of diagnostic colonoscopies, polypectomies and CRC diagnoses are being provided for the analyses through the BARMER SDWH.

Objectives and outcomes

The primary endpoint of this RCT is the proportion of BARMER insured individuals aged 45–49 who either underwent a polypectomy or received a CRC diagnosis within 12 months after receiving the initial invitation for ordering an FIT. This endpoint was selected to reflect measures by which FIT screening may exert its beneficial effects (ie, removal of CRC precursors or FIT-induced early detection of CRC) rather than mere FIT use which is not preventive by itself.

Outcome definitions

All outcomes will be assessed using routine healthcare claims data and programme data available within the SDWH of the BARMER statutory health insurance. Outcomes will be evaluated within 12 months beginning on the date of the initial invitation (index date). Coding algorithms are based on German billing codes (EBM, OPS), and the International Classification of Diseases 10th revision (ICD-10) diagnosis codes. FIT use is defined as the return of the FIT to the central laboratory responsible for analysing the FIT. FIT positivity data will only be available in aggregated form from the screening programme database and will therefore be analysed descriptively. However, after a positive FIT, a diagnostic colonoscopy is indicated according to German guidelines. A diagnostic colonoscopy will be defined as the documentation of at least one inpatient or outpatient diagnostic colonoscopy under EBM-code 13421 or OPS-code 1-650 within 12 months after the index date. Polypectomy will be defined as the documentation of at least one polypectomy (EBM 13423, OPS 5-452.6/0.7) during such diagnostic colonoscopies. CRC will be defined according to ICD-10 codes C18-C21. If both a polypectomy and CRC diagnosis occur during follow-up, the participant will only be counted once for the composite primary endpoint. Claims-based outcome data are expected to be highly complete due to the routine administrative collection procedures. FIT invitations, FIT orders and FIT returns are directly recorded within the BARMER programme infrastructure. Delivery status (eg, successfully transmitted, undeliverable) will be recorded where technically available. Postal invitations will be considered delivered unless returned as undeliverable. Data plausibility checks will be performed prior to statistical analyses.

Eligibility criteria

The study population consists of insurants of the BARMER health insurance. In the main study, individuals aged 45–49 years (as of September 2025) with a registered BARMER user account are included and contacted via email or paper-based mail. In the exploratory cohort, individuals aged 40–41 years with a registered BARMER user account are included and contacted via email or paper-based mail.

Exclusion criteria for receiving an invitation are as follows: (1) individuals wishing not to be contacted by their health insurance, (2) a prior colonoscopy, (3) a previous stool test within the past 12 months, (4) a previous diagnosis of CRC or inflammatory bowel disease (ICD-10 codes: C18-C21, K50, K51). Further, individuals in the exploratory cohort (individuals aged 40–41) are excluded if they had received a previous invitation through the programme.

Sample size

As part of the invitation waves planned for 2025, recruitment of approximately 80 000 participants was expected in the main study cohort and 10 000 in the exploratory cohort. For a two-sided, sex-stratified Miettinen-Nurminen (MN) test at a significance level of 5%, the statistical power for the primary endpoint (proportion of insured individuals aged 45–49 with a polypectomy or CRC diagnosis) depends on the actual increase in participation rates achieved through the intervention. The power calculation is based on assumptions derived from previous study results,7 and interim reports from BARMER’s Digital Colorectal Cancer Screening Program. In the interim analysis, among those invited at ages 45–49, only 3.3% used a FIT, but total order and return rates were higher due to additional inclusion of uninvited orders resulting in final FIT use rates of 5% for men and 6.4% for women. Assuming higher participation in a trial setting and to reflect real-life circumstances of uninvited and invited returns, total order and return rates were used in the sample size calculation. Statistical analyses

All analyses will primarily follow the intention-to-treat principle according to the randomised allocation. For the primary endpoint, the proportion of participants with a polypectomy or CRC diagnosis within 12 months after the index date will be compared between study arms using a two-sided MN test for differences in proportions with a significance level of α=0.05.10 Differences in study groups will be analysed with χ2 tests for categorical variables and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for continuous variables. Secondary binary endpoints, including FIT use, diagnostic colonoscopy utilisation, positive FIT results, polypectomy and CRC diagnosis, will be analysed analogously. As randomisation is stratified by sex, exploratory subgroup analyses by sex will be conducted. Additional exploratory subgroup analyses may include age group and geographic region. Sensitivity analyses using multivariable logistic regression models adjusted for sex, age, prior invitation history and geographic region will additionally be performed. Participants who terminate insurance coverage during follow-up will be censored at the available time of insurance termination. Missing outcome data are expected to be minimal due to the use of routinely collected claims data; therefore, no imputation of missing data is planned. No adjustment for multiplicity is planned for secondary and exploratory analyses.

Harms and stopping criteria

No harms of using a FIT are expected. There are no stopping criteria for recruitment.

Participant timeline and recruitment

Recruitment and follow-up are ongoing. After inclusion, participants may or may not make use of the BARMER screening offer. Both FIT use and FIT non-use are recorded directly by the BARMER. In the case of a positive test, participants are advised to talk to their general practitioner and may subsequently undergo a diagnostic colonoscopy. Utilisation of follow-up diagnostic colonoscopies and diagnoses are captured through BARMER health insurance claims data. Complete data for the final analyses are expected to be available in late 2027.

Data monitoring committee

A data monitoring committee is not needed due to the low-risk intervention of this observational study.

Public and patient involvement

Research at the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ) is generally informed by a Patient Advisory Committee. Patients and the public were not involved in the design and conduct of this RCT.

Discussion

We initiated this RCT in order to evaluate the preventive effect of a low threshold invitation and reminder via paper-based mail in comparison to email to FIT screening in a study population aged between 40 and 49 which is not yet covered by routine care. The RCT was motivated by very low screening uptake in preliminary, email-based implementation of invitations to FIT screening. CRC remains a leading cause of cancer-related deaths in Germany and the number of new cases and deaths is projected to rise substantially in the coming decades due to demographic ageing and population growth.11 This trend may be further exacerbated by increasing incidence among younger adults.4

Effective screening modalities, such as FITs and colonoscopies, can substantially reduce CRC incidence and mortality.12–14 In light of the rising burden of CRC in younger populations, the USA has recently lowered the starting age for CRC screening to 45 years,6 whereas most other European countries, including Germany, still initiate screening between ages 50 and 55.15

However, the success of any screening programme depends critically on adherence within the population,16 which varies widely between countries and screening programmes.15 In well-organised programmes such as the ones in The Netherlands, Scotland or Denmark, in which FIT kits are mailed directly to the target population, participation rates of up to 70% have been achieved.17–19 In contrast, FIT screening in Germany remains practically opportunistic. Individuals must request the FIT from a physician, collect and return the sample in person, and schedule a follow-up consultation, all of which likely contributes heavily to the very low adherence rates observed in Germany.20 A previous RCT by our group has found that a mailed invitation letter and reminder with the option to order a FIT increased adherence rates from approximately 10% to approximately 30% in people aged 50–54 years.7 Preliminary analyses of BARMER’s Digital Colorectal Cancer Screening Program suggest a lower FIT use rate in people who were invited via email than via paper-based mail. This may be attributed to the high volume of emails and phone notifications individuals receive every day, leading to email invitations being overlooked. Additionally, in Germany, important notifications are traditionally still sent via mail, which may contribute to higher engagement with mailed invitations. An invitation system that improves FIT access and use could not only increase adherence but also improve the preventive effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of CRC screening, as colonoscopies following a positive FIT have a higher yield of clinically relevant findings such as advanced adenomas than routine colonoscopies without prior FIT.21

A distinct feature of this RCT is the choice of the primary endpoint, the removal of precancerous lesions by polypectomy or earlier detection of CRC, which directly reflects the preventive effect of the intervention. FIT use alone only becomes preventive if followed by a diagnostic colonoscopy with polypectomy or detection of early-stage cancer. Accordingly, increases in FIT use without a corresponding increase in the primary endpoint would not indicate a beneficial effect of the intervention. A central limitation that must be considered when interpreting the findings of this study is that the billing codes do not contain any histopathological information on the removed polyps. Consequently, our primary composite endpoint may include removal of non-adenomatous lesions that are not associated with an increased long-term risk of CRC alongside removal of adenomas and CRC diagnoses.

Since individuals in the main study cohort aged 45–49 are likely to have received an invitation to the programme in 2022 or 2023 and are not invited for the first time, we also included 10 000 individuals in the explorative cohort aged 40–41 who were invited for the first time to study whether any substantial differences can be expected between those who have previously received an invitation and those who did not.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the ethics committee of the Medical Faculty of Heidelberg University, Germany (ID: S-157/2025) and registered in the German Clinical Trials Register (DRKS, ID: 00037817). Informed consent is implied through the completion of the FIT. Written informed consent will not be obtained prior to inclusion in order to avoid selection bias. Further, formal consent procedures in this setting may compromise the feasibility and validity of an evaluation. The study will be conducted in accordance with the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki.22 The results of this study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications. BARMER will retain ownership of all study data.

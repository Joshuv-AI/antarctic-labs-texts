Neurocentric No More? Oligodendrocyte Dysfunction Supports Epileptogenesis
Authors: Gabriel B. Miller-Oskolkoff, Manoj K. Patel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Olig2-Specific Loss-of-Function Slc35a2 Results in Hypomyelination and Spontaneous Seizures Bartel TM, Ghosh C, Bisaha K, Khoury J, Nemes-Baran A, Blumcke I, Najm I. Epilepsia 2026; 67: 950-965.

Commentary

Somatic mutations that arise during neurodevelopment and result in spatially limited areas of pathological brain tissue cause focal cortical dysplasias (FCDs) and structural epilepsy. A recently defined classification of FCD referred to as Mild Malformation of Cortical Development with Oligodendroglial Hyperplasia and Epilepsy (MOGHE) has been described where, in addition to heterotopic neurons, significantly elevated numbers of oligodendrocyte lineage cells are present as a clinical marker. 1 Somatic loss of function (LOF) variants in the gene SLC35A2, which encodes the X-linked uridine diphosphate galactose transporter, have been identified in resected tissue from drug-resistant epilepsy patients with MOGHE. 2 Similarly, germline LOF mutations in SLC35A2 cause congenital disorders of glycosylation (CDGs) and result in intellectual disability, motor deficits, and epilepsy. 3 The overlap of seizure phenotypes in both patient populations suggests the presence of a common underlying pathogenic mechanism despite the difference in the degree of functional impairment in SLC35A2. Notably, hypomyelination is present in both MOGHE and CDG indicating conserved dysfunction of oligodendrocytes in the pathology of these conditions.3,4

In this study, Bartel et al developed a conditional LOF Slc35a2 mouse model and used an Olig2-Cre line to selectively delete Slc35a2 in oligodendrocytes and determine how this cell type contributes to pathophysiology in MOGHE. 5 Olig2-specific Slc35a2 KO mice weighed significantly less compared to their littermate controls, with males being more severely impacted due to Slc35a2 being an X-linked gene. Male KO mice also had shorter lifespans and exhibited motor impairment, a phenotype only observed in one female KO mouse. To assess seizure susceptibility, the authors administered pentylenetetrazole (PTZ), a γ-aminobutyric acid antagonist, to KO mice and characterized behavioral seizure severity. Olig2-specific Slc35a2 KO mice exhibited significantly more severe seizures than PTZ-treated controls, and had significantly higher rates of seizure-induced deaths. Spontaneous seizure activity was also detected in all male mice and 4 of the 6 female KO mice via 24-h video EEG. Racine scores for spontaneous seizures ranged between 4 and 6, indicating the presence of tonic-clonic seizures in this conditional model.

Immunohistochemistry was used to quantify oligodendrocytes and myelination to further identify recapitulated clinical features. In contrast to oligodendroglial hyperplasia associated with MOGHE, an oligodendroglial hypoplasia phenotype was observed in this conditional Slc35a2 KO model. A previous paper using an independently generated conditional LOF Slc35a2 mouse model identified oligodendroglial hyperplasia, a discrepancy that could be due to differences in the way oligodendrocyte lineage cells were quantified. 6 The current study used Olig2 staining coupled with a Cre-dependent fluorescent reporter, a dual labeling approach that allows for greater labeling efficiency, which is especially relevant in white matter regions that may otherwise be difficult to stain due to high lipid content. Importantly, neuronal populations were unaffected across brain regions, indicating that gross secondary neuronal dysfunction or death is not present.

Hypomyelination was consistently observed in both male and female Olig2-specific Slc35a2 KO mice in various gray matter regions from P15 until adulthood, although myelination of the corpus callosum in female KO mice was not significantly different from control animals by P60. The authors measured CNPase, a membrane-bound marker for oligodendrocytes present at myelin sheaths, staining area to quantify myelination, but this approach can result in an overestimate of myelination since CNPase also stains oligodendrocyte cell bodies and premyelinating oligodendrocytes. Direct staining for myelin, such as FluoroMyelin staining, would be more informative. Nevertheless, the observed changes in CNPase area along with thinning of the corpus callosum support substantial hypomyelination in this model. Since hypomyelination is conserved between SLC35A2-CDG and MOGHE, this may indicate that pathological changes in myelination are more important to pathophysiology than either oligodendroglial hyperplasia or hypoplasia.3,4 Pathological changes in myelination have been identified in other forms of cortical dysplasia, such as maladaptive myelination in a patient with FCD type Ia, indicating that diverse myelination-dependent mechanisms may contribute to seizure generation in structural epilepsies more broadly. 7

While this study does not explore mechanisms underlying why oligodendrocyte-specific LOF of Slc35a2 causes hypomyelination, Bartel et al propose that the resulting loss of glycosylation impairs the functionality of proteins necessary for proper myelination.5 Myelin-associated glycoprotein (MAG) plays a role in maintaining glial–axonal membrane interactions necessary for myelination, and the loss of Slc35a2 likely impairs MAG function in this model. 8 Other glycosylated myelin proteins such as myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein, are also likely impacted in this model and may further contribute to impaired myelination. While this impaired myelination may promote apoptosis during the maturation of premyelinating oligodendrocytes, possibly causing hypoplasia, future studies characterizing the impacts on oligodendrocyte differentiation would be helpful to identify mechanisms underlying oligodendroglial dysfunction.

Altered neuronal excitability in this model may also arise through mechanisms independent of myelination, as past studies have demonstrated that loss of oligodendrocyte-mediated potassium buffering is sufficient to cause spontaneous seizures without changes to white matter structure. 9 Future studies characterizing neuronal excitability in oligodendrocyte-specific LOF Slc35a2 mice will be necessary to determine how exactly the disruption of neuronal-glial interactions promotes seizure generation.

An important consideration is that oligodendrocytes are targeted globally in this model, which does not recapitulate the somatic nature of Slc35a2 mutations that would be present in MOGHE. Currently, it remains unclear whether spatially limited loss of Slc35a2 function in oligodendrocytes would be sufficient to cause seizures, or if global impairment is required. Regardless, the findings from this study support primary oligodendrocyte dysfunction as sufficient to promote epileptogenesis in a mouse model of Slc35a2 LOF. This raises questions of how oligodendrocytes contribute to seizure generation in other forms of epilepsy, and whether oligodendrocytes represent a novel therapeutic target. Already it has been shown that pharmacological inhibition of oligodendrogenesis in a model of absence epilepsy with maladaptive myelination resulted in reduced seizure frequency, which established a precedent for targeting oligodendrocytes as disease-modifying in epilepsy. 10 Since many cases of structural epilepsy are treatment resistant, as are the majority of MOGHE patients, developing such novel therapeutic targets would be beneficial to this patient population and potentially other forms of drug-resistant epilepsy. 2

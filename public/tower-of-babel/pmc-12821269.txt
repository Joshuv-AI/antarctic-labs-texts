Mathematical modeling and analysis of a sex-structured syphilis model with control measures
Authors: Renfei Luo, Agbata Benedict Celestine, Mehmet Gümüş, Obeng-Denteh William, Homan Emadifar, Aseel Smerat, AK Awasthi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSyphilis remains a significant global public health challenge, with over seven million new adult infections each year despite the availability of effective treatment. Its continued persistence, particularly among high-risk and underserved populations, highlights the need for robust analytical models to understand transmission dynamics and inform targeted control strategies. This study presents a novel sex-structured compartmental model that captures the biological and behavioral complexity of syphilis transmission, incorporating multiple infection stages (primary, secondary, and latent), gender-specific progression, treatment, natural recovery, and reinfection. The population is stratified by sex and disease stage, allowing detailed analysis of transmission pathways between males and females. We established the positivity and boundedness of model solutions and derived the basic reproduction number () using the next-generation matrix approach. Stability analysis showed that the disease-free equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable when (). Notably, the model exhibits backward bifurcation, indicating that reducing below unity may not guarantee disease elimination. Sensitivity analysis using Latin Hypercube Sampling and Partial Rank Correlation Coefficients identified transmission probabilities as the most influential parameters sustaining infection, while treatment and recovery rates substantially reduced transmission. Numerical simulations demonstrated that integrated control strategies combining early diagnosis, treatment coverage above 85%, partner notification, and behavioral interventions can reduce syphilis prevalence by up to 80%. The study presents a comprehensive and biologically realistic modeling framework that enhances understanding of syphilis dynamics. The findings provide actionable insights for public health policy, emphasizing that multifaceted, gender-inclusive interventions are essential for achieving sustained control and eventual elimination of syphilis.Keywords: Sex-structured model, Sensitivity analysis, Bifurcation analysis, Reproduction number Analysis, Numerical simulations

Introduction

Syphilis continues to be a significant global health challenge, particularly among adults of reproductive age. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), more than 8 million new infections occurred worldwide in 2022, representing a notable increase compared to previous years [1, 2]. The highest burdens remain in Sub-Saharan Africa and the Americas, where transmission persists within key populations [1, 3]. Marginalized groups, such as sex workers and men who have sex with men (MSM), are disproportionately affected, with elevated prevalence levels especially in urban centers [3–5]. Although syphilis is easily treatable during its early stages, limited access to diagnostic services and timely care has contributed to its ongoing circulation and resurgence in several regions [2, 6]. The impact on maternal and child health is considerable, with congenital syphilis accounting for over 390,000 adverse birth outcomes globally in 2022, including stillbirths and neonatal mortality [1, 7]. Populations most at risk include MSM, individuals with multiple sexual partners, and pregnant women residing in endemic areas [4, 6, 8]. Recent data from Nigeria’s NCDC reported rising case numbers among pregnant women attending antenatal clinics, with prevalence rates ranging between 3 and 4% in high-burden states [9, 10]. If untreated, syphilis advances through primary, secondary, latent, and tertiary phases, potentially leading to severe complications such as neurosyphilis and cardiovascular disease [5, 11]. Cases of ocular syphilis, which may cause permanent blindness, are increasingly being reported across both high- and low-income countries [12, 13]. Diagnosis remains difficult due to overlapping symptoms with other STIs and limited clinical awareness [11, 14].

Mathematical modeling provides valuable insights into syphilis transmission patterns and informs effective intervention strategies [15, 16]. Compartmental approaches that include sex structure, multiple stages of infection, and reinfection have revealed complex dynamics such as backward bifurcation, in which the disease can persist even when the reproduction number is below one [15, 17]. A fractional SIS model applied to Brazilian data offered better epidemic curve fitting and underscored the importance of memory effects in transmission [18]. In Canada, network-based models demonstrated that mass-action assumptions often overestimate outbreak size, while edge-based methods more accurately capture MSM transmission dynamics [19]. Modeling studies also indicate that prioritizing treatment for reinfected individuals may be more effective than universal interventions [16, 20]. WHO recommends syphilis elimination programs integrate antenatal screening, partner notification, treatment coverage above 85%, and routine testing among high-risk groups [1, 2, 7]. In Nigeria, innovations such as the SORMAS digital surveillance platform have strengthened case reporting and enhanced real-time data use [10]. Simulation studies further show that combining early diagnosis, high treatment uptake, and behavioral measures like condom use could reduce transmission by more than 75% [15, 17, 18]. Reinfection patterns highlight the importance of repeated screening in vulnerable populations [20], while cost-effectiveness analyses suggest interventions should target both symptomatic and asymptomatic infections to curb hidden transmission [19].

Within Nigeria, syphilis continues to pose a public health burden, with evidence of persistent prevalence and inadequate screening coverage in key groups. A retrospective survey in urban Port Harcourt communities found a seroprevalence of about 2.2%, with slightly higher rates in males (2.6%) compared to females (1.8%) [21]. Among HIV-positive patients in Lagos, the prevalence of active syphilis was 1.8%, with men significantly more affected than women [22]. National surveillance data further revealed that fewer than 20% of pregnant women undergo prenatal syphilis testing, despite the considerable maternal and neonatal risks [23]. Modeling work specific to Nigeria identified factors such as condom usage, contact frequency, and treatment of secondary syphilis as key determinants of disease trends, suggesting that targeted control strategies may yield greater success [24]. Despite these challenges, combining computational models with improved surveillance systems, including NCDC’s reporting tools, offers a path toward stronger detection and intervention planning [25]. Despite the availability of effective antibiotics, syphilis continues to pose a significant global health threat, with an estimated 7 million new adult infections annually, disproportionately affecting marginalized and high-risk groups. This sustained prevalence, particularly in regions with limited access to healthcare and low health literacy, highlights the urgency for more effective public health strategies. Mathematical models are invaluable tools for understanding disease transmission and evaluating control strategies. However, many existing models simplify key biological and behavioral aspects, often neglecting sex-specific transmission dynamics, reinfection, and multiple stages of infection. Given that syphilis exhibits marked differences in progression and health-seeking behavior between males and females, a sex-structured approach is essential for accurately representing the disease’s epidemiology and informing gender-sensitive interventions. This study was therefore motivated by the need to develop a more biologically realistic model that could better guide targeted control measures and improve health outcomes across different demographic groups. This study introduces a novel sex-structured syphilis transmission model that advances previous research in several key ways. First, the model integrates three distinct stages of infection primary, secondary, and latent for both male and female populations, providing a more biologically realistic description of disease progression than earlier two-stage or unstructured approaches. Second, it explicitly incorporates reinfection dynamics and stage-specific treatment pathways, allowing assessment of the epidemiological impact of incomplete immunity and differential treatment outcomes across sexes. Third, the model captures sex-specific transmission probabilities and behavioral interactions, offering deeper insight into gendered transmission patterns often neglected in earlier works. Furthermore, the analytical framework reveals the existence of backward bifurcation within a sex-structured context an important and previously unreported feature indicating that reducing the basic reproduction number below unity may not guarantee elimination. Finally, through comprehensive sensitivity analysis and numerical simulations, the model evaluates the combined effectiveness of early treatment, partner notification, and behavioral interventions. Collectively, these elements make the present model a significant extension of existing sex-structured syphilis models, offering enhanced theoretical rigor and practical value for designing gender-inclusive control strategies.

The aim of this study is to develop and analyze a robust sex-structured mathematical model that captures the transmission dynamics of syphilis between males and females, while incorporating multiple stages of infection and various control interventions. The specific objectives of the study are to formulate a compartmental model that classifies the population by sex and disease stages, including susceptible, infected (primary, secondary, and latent stages), treated, and recovered individuals. To perform a rigorous mathematical analysis by establishing the positivity and boundedness of solutions, and by identifying the invariant region of the model. It also seeks to investigate the existence and local stability of both the disease-free equilibrium and the endemic equilibrium and to compute the basic reproduction number using the next-generation matrix approach and assess its implications for disease persistence. Furthermore, to examine the presence of backward bifurcation and its effect on control efforts. Sensitivity analysis using Latin Hypercube Sampling and Partial Rank Correlation Coefficients is conducted to identify the most influential parameters on syphilis transmission. Finally, numerical simulations are carried out to evaluate the impact of various control measures, including early treatment, partner notification, and behavior modification strategies, on reducing disease prevalence.

This study assumes homogeneous mixing between sexually active males and females to simplify the analysis, though real sexual networks are heterogeneous. Some parameters were considered symmetric between sexes due to limited sex-specific data, and while sensitivity analysis supports the robustness of results, more detailed data would enhance precision. The model focuses on adult sexual transmission and excludes congenital transmission, which would require additional pregnancy-related compartments and parameters. Spatial and behavioral heterogeneity were also not explicitly modeled, and parameter values were drawn from literature rather than localized data. Despite these limitations, the model effectively captures key transmission mechanisms and provides a strong basis for future extensions that incorporate congenital transmission, heterogeneous mixing, and region-specific calibration.

This phenomenon is characterized by the presence of multiple equilibrium points when the basic reproduction number () is less than one [39, 40]. Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive sex-structured mathematical model to describe the transmission dynamics of syphilis, incorporating multiple stages of infection (primary, secondary, and latent), gender-specific progression, treatment interventions, recovery, and reinfection. The model’s rigorous mathematical analysis confirmed the positivity, boundedness, and biological validity of its solutions, and identified a key epidemiological threshold through the basic reproduction number. Notably, the detection of backward bifurcation indicates that simply reducing the reproduction number below unity may not be sufficient to eliminate syphilis, emphasizing the importance of early diagnosis, effective treatment, and sustained behavioral change. Sensitivity and numerical analyses revealed that transmission probability and sexual contact rates are the primary drivers of disease spread, while treatment and recovery rates play the most critical roles in reducing infection prevalence. These findings underscore the need for integrated, evidence-based public health strategies combining high treatment coverage, early detection, partner notification, and behavioral interventions to achieve meaningful reductions in syphilis transmission. The results also demonstrate that gender-balanced interventions are essential, as targeting only one sex yields limited effectiveness due to bidirectional transmission dynamics. From a policy perspective, the model provides a quantitative basis for prioritizing interventions and allocating resources efficiently within national and global syphilis control programs. It reinforces current WHO and national guidelines advocating for comprehensive and gender-inclusive strategies that integrate biomedical, behavioral, and social approaches.

Our findings are consistent with earlier syphilis modelling work showing that treatment, partner notification, and behavioural change play central roles in reducing transmission [26–28]. Unlike many previous studies that do not distinguish sex differences or separate primary and secondary infectious stages, the present model introduces a fully sex-structured framework with stage-specific infectiousness and treatment rates. This enables us to capture male–female asymmetries in disease transmission that were not explicitly addressed in earlier sex-structured syphilis models such as those in [26], nor in multi-infection frameworks like [27] and [28]. Furthermore, while reinfection has been identified as an important driver of persistent transmission in earlier studies [29, 30], the current model incorporates both reinfection and immunity loss within a sex-structured setting and shows how these mechanisms interact with treatment and behaviour. Importantly, our analytical results reveal the occurrence of backward bifurcation a feature not reported in previous sex-structured syphilis models implying that reducing the reproduction number below one may not guarantee elimination. Overall, this study adds to the body of knowledge by providing a more biologically realistic, gender-inclusive modelling framework and by offering quantitative evidence that integrated interventions combining treatment, screening, and behavioural modification are essential for significantly reducing syphilis prevalence.

Future work should extend this framework to include age structure, heterogeneous or network-based mixing, and congenital transmission, as well as stochastic and cost-effectiveness analyses. Incorporating region-specific data for parameter calibration will further enhance the model’s predictive value and practical relevance for guiding public health planning and intervention design.

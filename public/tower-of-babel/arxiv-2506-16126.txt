Critical curve for the weakly coupled system of damped wave equations with mixed nonlinearities Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Tuan Anh Dao (anh.daotuan@hust.edu.vn)


Abstract
. In this paper, we would like to consider the Cauchy problem for a weakly coupled system of semi-linear damped wave equations with mixed nonlinear terms. Our main objective is to draw conclusions about the critical curve of this problem using tools from Harmonic Analysis. Precisely, we obtain a new critical curve p ​ q = 1 + 2 n pq=1+\frac{2}{n} for n = 1 , 2 n=1,2 by proving global (in time) existence of small data Sobolev solutions when p ​ q > 1 + 2 n pq>1+\frac{2}{n} and blow-up of weak solutions in finite time even for small data when p ​ q < 1 + 2 n pq<1+\frac{2}{n} for n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . From this, we infer the impact of the nonlinearities of time derivative-type on the critical curve associated with the system.

Introduction

Let us consider the following Cauchy problem for weakly coupled system of semi-linear damped wave equations with mixed nonlinear terms:

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|v|^{p},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+v_{t}=|u_{t}|^{q},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
(u,u_{t},v,v_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\end{cases}

where
p>1
and
q>1
stand for power exponents of nonlinearities and the positive constant
\varepsilon
describes the size of initial data. The quantities
|v|^{p}
and
|u_{t}|^{q}
are respectively called power nonlinearity and nonlinearity of derivative-type.

To get started, we now mention some historical views in terms of studying the following problem:

(2)

\begin{cases}\phi_{tt}-\Delta\phi+\phi_{t}=\mu|\phi|^{p},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
(\phi,\phi_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
p>1
and
\mu\geq 0
. It is well known that the solutions to the damped wave equation behave like those of the heat equation as time goes to infinity. This is called
diffusion phenomena
and investigated for a long time by many mathematicians (see
[
16
,
22
,
17
]
and the references therein). Matsumura
[
16
]
was the first to establish some basic decay estimates for the problem (
2
), also well-known as the linear damped wave equation for
\mu=0
and the semi-linear damped wave equation for
\mu=1
. He concluded that the damped wave equation has a diffusive structure as
t\to\infty
. Since then, many papers have studied sharp
L^{m}-L^{q}

estimates (with
1\leq m\leq q\leq\infty
) for the linear problem (
\mu=0
), for example,
[
22
,
3
,
9
]
and the references cited therein. For the semi-linear problem (
2
) with
\mu=1
, the papers
[
29
,
11
,
13
]
are obtained a global (in time) weak solution to this problem with a power type of nonlinearity
|u|^{p}
satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}<p<+\infty&\text{ if }n=1,2,\\
1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}<p\leq\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-2}&\text{ if }n\geq 3,\end{cases}

by using weighted energy methods and assuming data in Sobolev spaces with additional regularity
L^{1}
. On the other hand, nonexistence of general global (in time) small data solutions is proved in
[
29
]
for
1<p<1+2/n
and in
[
30
]
for
p=1+2/n
. Therefore, the exponent

p_{F}(n):=1+\frac{2}{n}

is referred to as the critical exponent of the semilinear problem (
2
) for
\mu=1
, known as the Fujita exponent. It is also the critical exponent of the semi-linear heat equation

\phi_{t}-\Delta\phi=|\phi|^{p},\,\,\,\phi(0,x)=\phi_{0}(x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Here, critical exponent is understood as the threshold
between global (in time) existence of small data weak solution and blow-up of solutions
even for small data. The diffusion phenomenon connecting the linear heat equation and the classical damped wave equation (see
[
20
,
22
]
) sheds light on the parabolic nature of classical damped wave models with power-type nonlinearities, as seen through the decay estimates of their solutions. Additionally, to derive the critical regularity of nonlinear terms for the semilinear damped wave equation, the authors in
[
7
]
considered the equation of (
2
) with
nonlinearities
|u|^{1+\frac{2}{n}}\mu(|u|)
on the right-hand side, where
\mu
stands for a suitable
modulus of continuity. Moreover, according to the works
[
14
,
10
,
15
]
, the sharp lifespan estimates for blow-up solutions to (
2
) when
p\leq 1+2/n
in all spatial dimensions have been investigated. Consequently, they provided the following sharp lifespan estimate for blow-up solutions:

\displaystyle T(\varepsilon)\sim\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359ptC\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-n(p-1)}}&\text{ if }1<p<1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n},\\
\exp\big(C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{n}}\big)&\text{ if }p=1+\displaystyle\frac{2}{n},\end{cases}

where
C=C(n,p,\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Under additional regularity
L^{m}
for the initial data, with
m\in[1,2]
, papers
[
12
,
13
]
identified the critical exponent of problem (
2
) as

p_{c}(m):=p_{F}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right):=1+\frac{2m}{n}.

However, the authors did not provide conclusions regarding the solution’s properties when
p=p_{c}(m)
. Paper
[
9
]
clarified this, showing that

m=1
falls into the blow-up range, while
m\in(1,2]
belongs to the global existence range under certain specific conditions. Continuing the extension of the initial data belonging to Sobolev spaces of negative order
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\times\dot{H}^{-\gamma},\gamma\geq 0,
Chen-Reissig
[
2
]
determined that the critical exponent for problem (
1
) is

\displaystyle p_{c}(2,\gamma):=p_{F}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\gamma\right)=1+\frac{4}{n+2\gamma},

by proving the global existence with small data when
p>p_{c}(2,\gamma)
and blow-up solution when
p<p_{c}(2,\gamma)
. However, the behavior of solutions to problem (
1
) with
p=p_{c}(2,\gamma)

remains an open problem. To clarify this, paper
[
6
]
is to study problem (
2
) by imposing additional regularity
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}_{m}
on the initial data, with
m\in(1,2],\gamma\geq 0
and the method used in this work is based on
[
9
]
. Specifically, they determine the critical exponent for problem (
1
) to be

\displaystyle p_{c}(m,\gamma):=p_{F}\left(\frac{n}{m}+\gamma\right)=1+\frac{2m}{n+m\gamma}.

Additionally, they show that the critical value
p=p_{c}(m,\gamma)
belongs to the global existence range.

Based on the research results from the damped wave equation, we turn to the papers on the weakly coupled system of damped wave equations

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|v|^{p},&\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+v_{t}=|u|^{q},&\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
(u,u_{t},v,v_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1})(x),&\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

It is claimed in
[
26
,
23
,
24
,
27
]
that the
critical curve
for system (
4
) is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(p,q):=\frac{\max\{p,q\}+1}{pq-1}=\frac{n}{2}.

Specifically, they proved that, if
\Gamma(p,q)<n/2
, then there exists a unique global (in time) small data Sobolev solution for all spatial dimensions. Sun-Wang
[
26
]
obtained the global existence result for (
4
) using the method due to Escobedo– Herrero and
L^{p}-L^{q}
type estimates of the linear damped wave equation. Nishihara-Wakasugi
[
23
]
improved this result by proving a global existence result for all
n\geq 1
by using the weighted energy method. This method enables them to treat the higher dimensional cases
n\geq 4
with rapidly decaying (or compactly supported)
initial data. Furthermore, the weighted energy method is applicable to the case of damping with
time or space dependent coefficient.
In the opposite case
\Gamma(p,q)\geq n/2
, the aforementioned works have established that every non-trivial local (in time) weak solution, in generally
blows up in finite time by applying test function method, which has been developed by Mitidieri-Pohozaev
[
19
,
21
]
. In particular, by constructing two test functions with suitable
scaling, we will derive a system of two nonlinear differential inequalities with their initial values. For sharp lifespan estimates of blow-up solutions to problem (
4
) (with
\Gamma(p,q)\geq n/2
), we refer to
[
1
,
23
,
24
]
, where the following general result is obtained:

\displaystyle T(\varepsilon)\sim\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359ptC\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2\Gamma(p,q)-n}}&\text{ if }\Gamma(p,q)>\displaystyle\frac{n}{2},\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\exp\big(C\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\big)&\text{ if }\Gamma(p,q)=\displaystyle\frac{n}{2},\,p=q,\\
\exp\bigg(C\varepsilon^{-\max\big\{\frac{p(pq-1)}{p+1},\frac{q(pq-1)}{q+1}\big\}}\bigg)&\text{ if }\Gamma(p,q)=\displaystyle\frac{n}{2},\,p\neq q.\end{cases}

Problem (
1
) is constructed by modifying problem (
4
), replacing the nonlinear pair
(|v|^{p},|u|^{q})
by
(|v|^{p},|u_{t}|^{q})
. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, there has been no previous work addressing the critical curve for this problem. For this reason, the main objective of this paper is to demonstrate that the curve

pq=1+\frac{2}{n}

serves as the critical curve for problem (
1
) in one and two spatial dimensions. Specifically, we prove that under the condition
pq>1+2/n
for
n=1,2
, along with certain additional assumptions, problem (
1
) admits a unique global Sobolev solution (in time) with small initial data. At this point, the crux of our approach is based on the technique of using
loss of decay associated with recently developed tools from Harmonic Analysis. The
advantage worthy of mentioning of allowing some loss of decay is to show how the
restrictions to the admissible exponents
p
and
q
could be relaxed. In addition, by employing the test function method, the authors also show that under the condition
pq<1+2/n
, problem (
1
) exists no global weak solution, even for small initial data. Through this, we have shown that the nonlinear term
|u_{t}|^{q}

is indeed strong enough to shift the critical curve of system (
1
) closer to the point
(1,1)
; that is, the curve

\Gamma(p,q)=n/2
actually lies above
pq=1+2/n
. This represents a new finding in our study. For spatial dimensions
n\geq 3
, due to the limitations of the local existence condition (
3
) and the strong regularity required of the initial data in the estimates for solutions to the classical linear damped wave equation, we are not yet able to characterize the sharp global existence region for this problem.

2.
Global existence of small data solution

2.1.
Philosophy of our approach

First, we can write the solution to linear problemm
(\ref{Main.Eq.2})
with
\mu=0
by the formula

\phi^{\rm lin}:=\phi^{\rm lin}(t,x)=\varepsilon(\mathcal{K}(t,x)+\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t,x))\ast_{x}\phi_{0}(x)+\varepsilon\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\phi_{1}(x),

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 11.38092ptu(t,x)=u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+u^{\rm non}(t,x):=u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|v(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau,\\

\displaystyle 3.\quad\eta^{-\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}}\big(|\eta^{\prime}|^{h^{\prime}}+|\eta^{\prime\prime}|^{h^{\prime}}\big)\text{ and }\varphi^{-\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}}|\Delta\varphi|^{h^{\prime}}\text{ is bounded, }

Proof of Theorem

At first, we assume that
(u,v)=(u(t,x),v(t,x))
is a global weak solution from
\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\infty),L^{2})\times\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),L^{2})
to (
1
). From Definition
3.1
, we replace the functions
\phi_{1}(t,x)
and
\phi_{2}(t,x)
by
\Phi_{R}(t,x)
. Then, we perform integration by parts to derive

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta_{R}(t)dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)(-\Delta\varphi)(R^{-1}x)dx+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx

(46)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{t}(t,x)\left(-\partial_{t}\eta_{R}(t)\varphi_{R}(x)-\Psi_{R}(t)\Delta\varphi_{R}(x)+\eta_{R}(t)\varphi_{R}(x)\right)dxdt

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{t}(t,x)|^{q}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x))\varphi_{R}(x)dx

(47)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(t,x)(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta-\partial_{t})\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt,

where
\Psi_{R}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\eta_{R}(s)ds
. We fix the following quantities:

\displaystyle\rho_{1,R}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta_{R}(t)dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)(-\Delta\varphi)(R^{-1}x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\varphi_{R}(x)dx,

\displaystyle\rho_{2,R}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x))\varphi_{R}(x)dx.

Applying Hölder’s inequality with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and using the change of variables
\tilde{t}=R^{-2}t
,
\tilde{x}=R^{-1}x
, we may estimates as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(t,x)\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}\right)\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{|x|\leq R}\left|v(t,x)\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}\right)\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lesssim\left(\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{|x|\leq R}|v(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left(\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{|x|\leq R}\left|\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{t}\right)\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|^{p^{\prime}}|\Phi_{R}(t,x)|^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{1,R}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(R^{-4p^{\prime}+n+2}+R^{-2p^{\prime}+n+2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{1,R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(t,x)\Delta\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{R/2\leq|x|\leq R}\left|v(t,x)\Delta\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{R/2\leq|x|\leq R}|v(t,x)|^{p}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left(\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{R/2\leq|x|\leq R}\left|\Delta\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|^{p^{\prime}}|\Phi_{R}(t,x)|^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{2,R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}.

From these, combining with (
47
) leads to

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{R}+\varepsilon\rho_{2,R}\lesssim\left(\mathcal{I}_{1,R}^{\frac{1}{p}}+\mathcal{I}_{2,R}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}.

On the other hand, employing again Hölder’s inequality and the change of variables
\tilde{t}=R^{-2}t
,
\tilde{x}=R^{-1}x
, we estimate the right-hand side of (
46
) as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{t}(t,x)\partial_{t}\Phi_{R}(t,x)|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u_{t}(t,x)\partial_{t}\Phi_{R}(t,x)|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\left(\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u_{t}(t,x)|^{q}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt\times\left(\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{|x|\leq R}|\partial_{t}\Phi_{R}(t,x)|^{q^{\prime}}|\Phi_{R}(t,x)|^{-\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{1,R}^{\frac{1}{q}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{q^{\prime}}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u_{t}(t,x)\Psi_{R}(t)\Delta\varphi_{R}(x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{R/2\leq|x|\leq R}\left|u_{t}(t,x)\Psi_{R}(t)\Delta\varphi_{R}(x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{R/2\leq|x|\leq R}|u_{t}(t,x)|^{q}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(\int_{0}^{R^{2}}|\Psi_{R}(t)|^{q^{\prime}}|\eta_{R}(t)|^{-\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{R/2\leq|x|\leq R}|\Delta\varphi_{R}(x)|^{q^{\prime}}|\varphi_{R}(x)|^{-\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{2,R}^{\frac{1}{q}}R^{\frac{n+2}{q^{\prime}}},

where we note that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t}^{\infty}\eta(s)ds\right|^{q^{\prime}}|\eta(t)|^{\frac{-q^{\prime}}{q}}=\left|\int_{t}^{1}\eta(s)ds\right|^{q^{\prime}}|\eta(t)|^{\frac{-q^{\prime}}{q}}\leq(1-t)^{q^{\prime}}\eta(t)\leq 1\text{ for all }t\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right).

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u_{t}(t,x)\Phi_{R}(t,x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{t}(t,x)|^{q}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi_{R}(t,x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{q}}R^{\frac{n+2}{q^{\prime}}}.

From this, we derive

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{R}+\varepsilon\rho_{1,R}\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{q}}R^{\frac{n+2}{q^{\prime}}}.

Using the monotone convergence theorem, we derive

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\rho_{1,R}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\rho_{2,R}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x)dx>0.

Therefore, there exists a sufficiently large positive number
R_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{1,R}>0\text{ and }\rho_{2,R}>0,

for all
R\in[R_{0},\infty)
. Substituting the left side of
(
49
) into the right side of (
48
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{R}+\varepsilon\rho_{2,R}\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{pq}}R^{\frac{n+2}{pq^{\prime}}-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}},

that is

(50)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\rho_{2,R}\lesssim R^{\frac{pq}{pq-1}(\frac{n+2}{pq^{\prime}}-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}})},

for all
R\in[R_{0},\infty)
.
Condition (
9
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{n+2}{pq^{\prime}}-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}<0.

In the subcritical case, that is,
pq<1+2/n
, taking
R\to\infty
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x)dx=0.

This is a contradiction to the assumption (
8
). Thus, we have completed the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Significance of serum lncRNA XIST in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and its progression to pulmonary heart disease
Authors: Xiaoteng Huang, Jiayu Liang, Yongxiang Li, Mengwen Wei, Qingyue Liu, Yunshu Jiang, Chunfeng Wang, Jie Ding
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) has become one of the major death-related causes. Chronic pulmonary heart disease (CPHD) is an adverse complication of COPD causing poor prognosis of patients. This study evaluated the role of lncRNA XIST in COPD and CPHD aiming to identify a potential biomarker for the screening and prediction of COPD.MethodsThe study enrolled 127 COPD patients, including 73 patients occurred CPHD with 76 healthy individuals as the control group. The expression of XIST was evaluated by PCR and compared between COPD patients with different severity, grades, and complications. The diagnostic and prognostic values of XIST in COPD and CPHD were assessed by ROC and Kaplan-Meier analyses.ResultsSignificant upregulation of XIST was observed in the serum of COPD patients relative to healthy individuals, which distinguished COPD patients and showed a correlation with the respiratory and pulmonary function of COPD patients. COPD patients with acute exacerbations and CPHD showed a higher expression level. Increasing serum XIST discriminated COPD patients combined CPHD and positively correlated with right ventricular hypertrophy and pulmonary hypertension. Higher serum XIST levels could indicate the adverse 3-year prognosis of COPD patients, especially for COPD patients combined with CPHD.ConclusionUpregulated XIST served as a biomarker for screening COPD and predicting adverse prognosis of COPD and COPD patients with CPHD.

Introduction

With the aging population and the pollution of the environment, the incidence of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is gradually increasing and ranking top position in death-related diseases. The clinical symptoms of COPD mainly behaved as chronic cough, phlegm, shortness of breath, and difficulty in breathing [1, 2]. The high mortality and poor prognosis of COPD mainly resulted from complications but not respiratory diseases. Chronic pulmonary heart disease (CPHD) is a common complication of COPD mainly induced by COPD-caused pulmonary arterial hypertension [3]. The similar and overlapped disease conditions and symptoms of COPD and CPHD increase the difficulty of diagnosis and even cause misdiagnosis, which would result in delayed treatment and worsen conditions. In the clinic, the diagnosis of CPHD is mainly dependent on chest X-ray examination, cardiac ultrasound, and electrocardiogram, which are high-cost and limited to patients’ conditions [4, 5]. Therefore, non-invasion diagnosis with express analysis and accurate diagnosis is of urgent need for CPHD. Especially in COPD patients, the prediction of CPHD would greatly improve patients’ outcomes.

The development of sequences and bioinformatic analyses help researchers reveal the underlying regulatory mechanism of human diseases. Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) have been demonstrated to account for a huge percentage of the human genome. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), as the major member of the ncRNA family, have been reported to show significant specificity and sensitivity in screening diseases and predicting malignant development [6, 7]. Recent studies conducted based on bioinformatics explored several core marker lncRNAs in COPD and established co-expression networks [8, 9]. Among identified lncRNAs, lncRNA XIST was previously revealed to regulate cigarette smoke extract-induced cell apoptosis and inflammation in bronchial epithelial cells, and it was also found to be upregulated in lung tissues of COPD patients [8]. XIST was hypothesized of great potential to serve as a biomarker for COPD. XIST has been widely identified as a biomarker in human diseases, such as gestational diabetes mellitus, membranous nephropathy, and diverse human cancers [10–12]. However, there is still a lack of available data demonstrating its clinical significance in COPD. Additionally, XIST was also identified as a biomarker for cardiac diseases, such as acute myocardial infarction and dilated cardiomyopathy, indicating its potential involvement in regulating cardiac function [13, 14]. Therefore, XIST might show an association with the lesions in cardiac function during COPD and therefore predict the risk of CPHD in COPD patients.

This study focused on the expression and significance of XIST in COPD evaluating its potential in diagnosing COPD and predicting disease development and the risk of CPHD, aiming to explore a novel potential biomarker for COPD and CPHD.

Materials and methods

Patients

The study enrolled 127 COPD patients (group 1) from 2019 to 2020 at The Second People’s Hospital of Liaocheng. Patients were diagnosed according to a global strategy for the diagnosis, management, and prevention of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and excluded for other lung diseases, such as pneumothorax, atelectasis, pulmonary infarction, lung cancer, sarcoidosis, pulmonary fibrosis, and acute respiratory distress syndrome. The diagnosis of COPD mainly combined clinical symptoms and pulmonary function. The typical symptoms of COPD included cough, phlegm, shortness of breath and/or increased panting, increased sputum volume, purulent or mucous purulent sputum, possibly accompanied by fever. Patients with a history of cardiac dysfunction and lung diseases were also excluded. The study enrolled another group of 76 healthy individuals without any diseases as the control group (group 2) from the physical examination center of our hospital at the same time. COPD patients included 85 male patients and 42 female patients with an average age of 56.47 ± 6.33 years, while healthy individuals showed no significant difference in age and gender composition with COPD patients including 53 males and 23 females with an average age of 55.39 ± 4.77 years (data not shown). Moreover, the smoker accounts for 73.23% of COPD patients, to avoid the influence of smoking on the expression of XIST, a similar percentage of smokers were included in healthy individuals. Based on the occurrence of CPHD, COPD patients were divided into the CPHD group (group 1–1) and the non-CPHD group (group 1–2). The non-CPHD and CPHD patients showed matched age, gender, and smoking history. The non-CPHD group included 36 males and 18 females with the average age of 56.24 ± 7.55 years, and the smoker account for 74.07%. The CPHD group was composed of 49 males and 24 females with the average age of 56.66 ± 6.18 years, and 72.60% patients were smokers. All participants had signed informed consent, and the study had obtained approval from the Ethics Committee of The Second People’s Hospital of Liaocheng.

Pulmonary function and severity evaluation

The pulmonary function of COPD patients was evaluated by the GOLD grade based on the maximum volume of gas exhaled in the ratio of first second after the maximum deep inhalation (FEV1) and the forced vital capacity (FVC). The specific grading was: GOLD-1, FEV1/FVC ≥ 80%; GOLD-2, FEV1/FVC ranged 50–79%; GOLD-3, FEV1/FVC ranged 30–49%; GOLD-4, FEV1/FVC < 30%.

mMRC grades were employed for evaluating the symptom and severity of COPD. The grading criteria were: mMRC-1, difficulty breathing when walking fast on flat ground or climbing slowly; mMRC-2, walking fast or climbing slowly is slower than peers and requires rest; mMRC-3, a need to stop for breath after walking 100 m or for a few minutes; mMRC-4, severe dyspnea.

Sample collection

Fasting venous blood samples were collected from all study subjects within 24 h of their admission and centrifugated at 3500 rpm for 10 min for serum isolation. Serum samples were stored in the refrigerator at -80 °C for the following analyses.

Follow-up survey

COPD patients were followed up for 3 years via telephone or outpatient interviews, and the endpoint events of the follow-up survey were defined as all-cause mortality. None of the study subjects were lost to follow-up in the present study.

Real-time quantitative PCR

Total RNA was extracted from serum samples with an RNA extraction kit (Biomed, China) and evaluated by the ratio of OD260/280 using NanoDrop 2000 (Nanodrop Technologies, USA). Isolated RNA with OD260/280 ranged from 1.8 to 2.2 and was further reverse transcribed into cDNA with a high-performance cDNA reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystem, USA). Amplification was performed on the 7900 PCR system (Applied Biosystem, USA) with STBR Green reagent. The relative expression level of XIST was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCT method with β-actin as the internal reference.

Statistical analyses

A difference comparison between the two groups was performed with the student’s t-test using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. The clinical significance of XIST was evaluated by SPSS 26.0 software. Specifically, the receiver operating curve (ROC) was performed to assess the significance of XIST in distinguishing COPD patients and COPD patients with CPHD. Logistic regression analysis was carried out to identify risk factors for CPHD in COPD patients. The correlation of XIST with the clinicopathological features of COPD patients was evaluated by Pearson correlation analysis. Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression analyses were performed to evaluate the role of XIST in the prognosis of COPD and COPD patients with CPHD. Statistical significance was indicated by P < 0.05.

Results

Expression and significance of XIST in the diagnosis and severity of COPD

Compared with healthy individuals, significant upregulation of XIST was observed in the serum of COPD patients (Fig. 1a), which distinguished COPD patients effectively (AUC 0.827, sensitivity = 64.57%, specificity = 85.53%, Fig. 1b). According to the stages of COPD, patients were divided into the acute exacerbation and the stable COPD group, and patients in the acute exacerbation groups showed a higher serum XIST level (Fig. 1c).

Additionally, the disease severity of COPD patients was also evaluated by GOLD and mMRC grades. Patients with advanced grades showed higher serum levels of XIST, but no significant differences were observed between early stages (GOLD grade 1 vs. GOLD grade 2, Fig. 1d and mMRC grade 1 vs. mMRC grade 2, Fig. 1e). Serum XIST levels also showed a significant correlation with the respiratory and lung function of COPD patients. Specifically, serum XIST level was positively correlated with CAT score (r = 0.768 for stable COPD, r = 0.766 for acute exacerbation COPD) and negatively correlated with FEV1 (r = -0.822 for stable COPD, r = -0.845 for acute exacerbation COPD) and FEV1/FVC (r = -0.790 for stable COPD, r = -0.840 for acute exacerbation COPD) of COPD patients at both acute exacerbation and stable COPD (Table 1).

Expression and significance of XIST in the diagnosis and cardiac function of CPHD

Patients were further grouped according to the complication of CPHD. COPD patients combined with CPHD showed higher levels of RV, RVOT, and PASP but showed no significant changes in LVEF (Table 2). It was found that patients who developed to CPHD showed a higher expression of XIST (Fig. 2a), and increasing XIST showed a significant potential in discriminating COPD patients combined with CPHD (Fig. 2b). Moreover, XIST was positively correlated with the abnormal cardiac function and pulmonary blood pressure (rRV = 0.734, rRVOT = 0.732, rPASP = 0.767, Fig. 2c-e).

Significance of XIST in the prognosis of COPD and CPHD

According to the average serum expression of XIST, COPD patients were divided into a low-XIST group and a high-XIST group. Based on the 3-year follow-up survey, patients in the high-XIST group showed a poorer prognosis than patients in the low-XIST group, and the difference was significant (Fig. 3a). CPHD patients were also grouped by the mean serum XIST levels in CPHD patients. The 3-year prognosis of CPHD patients in the low-XIST group was also worse than patients in the high-XIST group (Fig. 3b).

Discussion

ncRNAs account for a huge percentage of the human genome and could regulate gene expression at transcription and post-transcription levels [15, 16]. There have been several studies noticed the function of lncRNAs in COPD and identified a series of potential molecular biomarkers. For example, lncRNA MALAT1 was identified to discriminate acute exacerbation COPD and was positively correlated with inflammation [17]. LncRNA H19 was demonstrated to mediate the progression of smoke-related COPD through targeting the miR-181/PDCD4 axis [18].

XIST is located on chromosome X and is expressed in various human systems. Previous studies demonstrated the functional roles of XIST in human cancers, regulating cell biological function and mediating disease progression [12, 19–21]. XIST has been demonstrated to exist in the lung, and its abnormal expression in lung cancer was considered a potential biomarker and tumor regulator [22–25]. Serum XIST has been identified as diagnostic biomarkers [26–28]. Herein, the serum XIST levels were analyzed in COPD patients and compared with healthy individuals. It was found that XIST was significantly upregulated in COPD patients, and increasing XIST could distinguish COPD patients with relatively high sensitivity and specificity. Analyzing serum XIST level is easier to operate with lower technical and professional requirements for the operator in relative to chest X-ray examination. Therefore, increasing serum XIST can be considered an indicator in the clinical early screening COPD, but there is still a need to further explore the specific cutoff with larger clinical samples. GOLD grades could indicate the severity of COPD based on the FEV1 [29], which were clinically employed. While CAT scores and mMRC grades could indicate the respiratory function of COPD patients and therefore indirectly suggest disease severity [30]. mMRC scores was employed for evaluating therapeutic efficiency of COPD, while CAT score was demonstrated as a significant related factor for the severity of COPAD and could predict the risk of severe depression and other complications in COPD patients [31–33]. Acute exacerbation of COPD induces deteriorating clinical symptoms, accelerating pulmonary decline, and increasing the risk of death [34, 35]. The expression of XIST showed no significant difference between early GOLD and mMRC grades but significantly increased with advanced grades, which is consistent with its increasing expression in acute exacerbation COPD patients. Consistently, a positive correlation was observed between XIST and CAT scores, and pulmonary function was found to be negatively correlated with XIST. Previously, CAT scores and mMRC grading were suggested to monitor the recovery of COPD patients and also to predict the adverse outcomes of patients [36]. Moreover, the higher mMRC grades was found to be associated with the high risk of complications in COPD, such as pulmonary heart disease, and mMRC was also employed for evaluating the therapeutic efficiency in COPD patients [31, 37, 38]. Hence, the above findings suggested the close association of XIST with the deteriorating development of COPD, and it can be considered a therapeutic target for COPD.

Pulmonary arterial hypertension has been accepted to be associated with various lung diseases, especially in CPHD [39–41]. During the onset of COPD, pulmonary arterial showed increasing blood pressure causing a thickening ventricular wall and therefore resulting in clinical syndrome of right ventricular dysfunction [42, 43]. In enrolled COPD patients, there were 73 patients combined with CPHD accounting for 57.49%. CPHD patients showed significant right ventricular hypertrophy, and the pulmonary arterial blood pressure was also higher than COPD patients without CPHD, agreed with the reports of Hillas [39]. However, the LVEF showed no significant difference between CPHD and non-CPHD patients. Additionally, the increasing expression of XIST was observed in CPHD patients and also showed significant diagnostic value in COPD patients with CPHD compared to patients without CPHD. XIST showed a positive correlation with abnormal cardiac function, indicating by reducing LVEF, increasing RV and RVOT [44, 45]. Increasing pulmonary artery systolic blood pressure in CPHD patients was also correlated with elevating serum XIST. Increasing serum XIST level was revealed to suggest the increasing risk of CPHD in patients with COPD, which can be employed for the assessment of disease progression and severity.

The study also noticed the prognostic value of XIST in both PCOD and CPHD. Higher serum levels of XIST were associated with the adverse 3-year prognosis of PCOD and CPHD patients. Of the enrolled COPD patients, over half of patients were with acute exacerbations. Previous studies have demonstrated that COPD patients with acute exacerbations showed a high rate of rehospitalization and mortality, and the mortality rate increased with the number of readmissions [46–48]. The close association of XIST with the prognosis of COPD patients is consistent with its abnormal expression between acute exacerbations and stable. Also, the observed prognostic significance of XIST in COPD is also consistent with its close association with disease severity.

However, the etiologies of COPD and CPHD involves various perspectives, such as inflammation, oxidative stress, imbalance of protease-antiprotease, airway remodeling, and immune reactions [49–55]. Mechanism investigations are also necessary for understanding the specific function of XIST. A previous study completed the regulatory axis of lncRNA H19 in COPD with the help of bronchial epithelial cell modeling and demonstrated their regulatory effects on inflammation and epithelial-mesenchymal transition [18]. Further investigations would pay more attention to deepen understanding the regulatory mechanism of XIST in COPD, providing more reliable reference for the disease management. On the other hand, this is a single-center study with a relatively small sample size. A larger sample size collected from multiple study centers is necessary for future studies, which could help provide more persuasive evidence.

Conclusions

Increasing serum XIST could diagnose COPD and predict severe development and poor prognosis of patients. The upregulation of XIST also distinguished COPD patients with CPHD and was associated with reduced cardiac function and adverse outcomes of patients.

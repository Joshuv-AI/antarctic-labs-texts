Small-time local control of a Schrödinger equation: a negative and a positive quadratic result
Karine Beauchard, Frédéric Marbach, Thomas Perrin

Abstract
We study the small-time local controllability (STLC) of a bilinear Schrödinger equation with Neumann boundary conditions near its ground state. We focus on the degenerate case where the linearized system is not controllable, necessitating a second-order analysis. We prove two complementary results. The negative result provides a new PDE instance of Sussmann’s classical quadratic obstruction, corresponding to a non-vanishing Lie bracket. The positive result appears to be the first to establish STLC at the quadratic order for a physical PDE with a single scalar control. Both proofs rely on a Fourier-based approach, which is crucial because the integral kernel of the second-order term lacks the regularity required by standard integration-by-parts arguments. Along the way, we develop tools valid in a more general setting to analyze such quadratic forms. In particular, we prove results that allow for the multiplication of a kernel by a modulation function.



MSC classes:

93B05, 93C20, 35Q41

Keywords:

small-time local controllability, bilinear Schrödinger equation, degenerate linearization, quadratic-order controllability, scalar-input system

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following Schrödinger equation:

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,x)-u(t)\mu(x)\psi(t,x)&\quad t\in(0,T),\quad x\in(0,1),\\
\partial_{x}\psi(t,0)=\partial_{x}\psi(t,1)=0&\quad t\in(0,T).\end{cases}

(1.1)

In this system,
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
is a fixed function depending only on
x
,
u(t)
is a real-valued control and the state
\psi(t,x)
is a complex-valued wave function.

From the modeling perspective, the most common case is
\mu(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}
, which corresponds to a non-relativistic charged quantum particle evolving in a space-uniform electric field of time-varying amplitude
u(t)
.
The choice
\mu(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}
also arises after a change of variables for the case of a moving potential well (or box), where
u(t)
is the acceleration of the box (see
[
32
]
).
In some contexts, allowing for a more general space-dependent
\mu(x)
makes sense (see e.g.
[
18
, eq. (5)]
concerning the interaction between a polarized laser and a hydrogen cyanide molecule, where
\mu
represents the permanent dipole moment of the molecule and
u(t)
the intensity of the laser’s electric field).

In such contexts, the Schrödinger equation is typically endowed with Dirichlet boundary conditions (see e.g.
[
3
]
for a sufficient condition relying on the linear theory and
[
17
]
for a first quadratic obstruction).
Here, we focus on Neumann boundary conditions, which, while perhaps less intuitive from a modeling perspective, offer an elegant toy problem for developing a general methodology for analyzing quadratic obstructions to controllability and for establishing positive quadratic results.

1.1
Notations and well-posedness

We consider
L^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C})
equipped with its usual Hermitian inner product
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle:=\int_{0}^{1}\phi\overline{\psi}
.
We define the self-adjoint unbounded operator
A:=-\Delta
on

\operatorname{Dom}(A)=H^{2}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{C}):=\left\{\varphi\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C});\enskip\varphi^{\prime}(0)=\varphi^{\prime}(1)=0\right\}.

(1.2)

Its eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and eigenvectors
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are respectively given by

\lambda_{j}:=(j\pi)^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi_{j}(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }j=0,\\
\sqrt{2}\cos(j\pi x)&\text{ if }j\geq 1.\end{cases}

(1.3)

The family
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C})
,
\varphi_{0}
is the
ground state
.
Let

\mathbb{S}:=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C});\enskip\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}=1\right\}.

(1.4)

With these notations, system (
1.1
) is well-posed in the following sense.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
For any
T>0
and
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
there exists a unique mild solution
\psi\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{2}_{N}\cap\mathbb{S})\cap H^{1}((0,T);L^{2})
to (
1.1
) with initial data
\psi(0)=\varphi_{0}
.
Moreover,
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}_{N}}\leq C_{\mu}(T,\|u\|_{L^{2}})
where

is a non-decreasing function of

and
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.

Proof.

This can be proved as in the Dirichlet case (see
[
3
, Proposition 2]
).
The proof is based on a fixed-point argument, relying on a smoothing result (see
Lemma

B.2
).
∎

1.2
Main result

We will use the following definition, concerning the controllability of (
1.1
) near the ground state.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
We say that system (
1.1
) is
small-time locally controllable from
\varphi_{0}
(STLC) when, for any
T>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any
\psi^{*}\in H^{2}_{N}\cap\mathbb{S}
such that
\|\psi^{*}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}\leq\delta
, there exists
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
with
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon
such that the associated solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
\psi(0)=\varphi_{0}
satisfies
\psi(T)=\psi^{*}
.

Adapting the strategy of
[
3
]
, one may prove the following result.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that

\exists c>0,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle|\geq\frac{c}{\langle j\rangle^{2}}.

(1.5)

Then system (
1.1
) is STLC.

Proof.

Under assumption (
1.5
), one can prove that, for any
T>0
, the linearized perturbative version of system (
1.1
) at
\varphi_{0}
(see (
1.17
)) is controllable with initial and final states in
H^{2}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{C})\cap\mathsf{T}_{\varphi_{0}}\mathbb{S}
where
\mathsf{T}_{\varphi_{0}}\mathbb{S}:=\{\varphi\in L^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C})\mid\Re\langle\varphi,\varphi_{0}\rangle=0\}
and controls in
L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.
Then, one concludes for the nonlinear system thanks to an inverse mapping theorem.
Both steps are well understood in the Dirichlet setting (see
[
3
, Sections 2.3 and 2.4]
) and easily translated to our Neumann context.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

Assumption (
1.5
) holds generically with respect to
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
Indeed, integrating by parts twice proves that, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle=\sqrt{2}\frac{(-1)^{j}\mu^{\prime}(1)-\mu^{\prime}(0)}{(j\pi)^{2}}-\frac{\langle\mu^{\prime\prime},\varphi_{j}\rangle}{(j\pi)^{2}}.

(1.6)

Thus, if
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
is such that
\mu^{\prime}(1)\pm\mu^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
and, for all

,

, then (
1.5
) holds using that

since

.

We now investigate situations when (
1.5
) fails, so that the study of the linearized system is not sufficient to determine the controllability of the nonlinear one.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
.
We define the following family of coefficients
(c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
by

\forall j\in\mathbb{N},\quad c_{j}:=\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\langle\varphi_{j},\mu\varphi_{k}\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad a_{k}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\lambda_{j}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}\right)c_{j}.

(1.7)

We refer to
Remark

1.12
and
Section

1.6
for the interpretation of the coefficient
a_{k}
.
Note that the series defining
a_{k}
converges absolutely because (
1.6
) (applied with
\mu
and
\mu\leftarrow\mu\varphi_{k}
) implies

c_{j}=\underset{j\to+\infty}{O}(j^{-4}).

(1.8)

An accessible version of our main results is the following one (which provides a dichotomy within the class of functions
\mu
that satisfy (
1.9
)).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
.

1.

Assume that
a_{k}\neq 0
. Then system (
1.1
) is not STLC.

2.

Assume that
a_{k}=0
and

\exists c>0,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{k\},\quad|\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle|\geq\frac{c}{\langle j\rangle^{2}}.

(1.9)

Then system (
1.1
) is STLC.

Remark 1.6
.

When
k=0
, if
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
and
\langle\mu,\varphi_{0}\rangle=0
, then system (
1.1
) is not STLC.
Indeed, either
\mu=0
, or
a_{0}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}|\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}>0
so
Item

1
of
Theorem

1.5
applies.
In particular, system (
1.1
) with
\mu(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}
is not STLC.

Both statements of
Theorem

1.5
are of quadratic nature.
To highlight this, let us state the following sharper versions which we will prove in this work.
In these statements, we denote by
u_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}u
the primitive of the control.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
.
Assume that
a_{k}\neq 0
.
There exist
T^{*},C,\nu>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,T^{*})
, and all
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
with
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
, the associated solution to (
1.1
) with initial data

satisfies

\left|\langle\psi(T)-\varphi_{0},\varphi_{k}\rangle+ia_{k}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\leq CT^{\nu}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\psi(T)-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(1.10)

Remark 1.8
.

In particular, for small enough times (e.g. such that
CT^{\nu}\leq|a_{k}|
), estimate (
1.10
) prevents
\psi(T)
from reaching targets of the form

\psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}:=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\varphi_{0}+i\operatorname{sgn}(a_{k})\varepsilon\varphi_{k}

(1.11)

with
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
because this would entail
\varepsilon\leq 2C\varepsilon^{2}
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
.
Assume that
a_{k}=0
and that (
1.9
) holds.
There exists
T^{*}>0
such that, for every
T\in(0,T^{*})
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every
\psi^{*}\in H^{2}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{C})\cap\mathbb{S}
with
\|\psi^{*}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}\leq\delta
, there exists
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
such that the associated solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
\psi(0)=\varphi_{0}
satisfies
\psi(T)=\psi^{*}
.

Moreover, we have the estimates

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}|\langle\psi^{*},\varphi_{k}\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{T}\|\psi^{*}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{N}},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{|\Re\langle\psi^{*},\varphi_{k}\rangle|}{T^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\left(\frac{|\Im\langle\psi^{*},\varphi_{k}\rangle|}{T}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{T}\|\psi^{*}-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

(1.13)

where
C
denotes a time-independent constant and
C_{T}
a time-dependent one.

Remark 1.10
.

The cost estimate (
1.12
) involving
|\langle\psi^{*},\varphi_{k}\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}
is typical of results where the controllability stems from the quadratic order (see e.g.
[
4
, (1.34) in Section 1.6]
for a parabolic system in small time, or
[
29
, Theorem 1.3]
for a Korteweg–de Vries system in large time).

When
a_{k}\neq 0
, (
1.10
) states that the amplitude of the unwanted motion in the direction
-ia_{k}\varphi_{k}
is given by
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
The sharp cost estimate (
1.13
) proves that, when
a_{k}=0
, one can move in the directions
\pm i\varphi_{k}
with an amplitude scaling like
T\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, and in the directions
\pm\varphi_{k}
with an amplitude

(motions in the other directions are governed by the linear theory).

1.3
Heuristic and comparison with previous results

1.3.1
An ODE prototype, drifts and Lie brackets

The root cause of
Theorem

1.7
(
Item

1
of
Theorem

1.5
) is embedded in the following caricatural finite-dimensional control-system example:

\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{1}=u\\
\dot{x}_{2}=x_{1}\\
\dot{x}_{3}=x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}-x_{1}^{3}-2ux_{1}.\end{cases}

(1.14)

Let
u_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}u
be the primitive of the control.
In the sequel, this function will implicitly be considered as a function defined on
(0,T)
, and
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
will thus denote
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}
.
Explicit integration from
x(0)=0
yields

\begin{split}x_{3}(T)&=\int_{0}^{T}u_{1}^{2}-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u_{1}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}u_{1}^{3}-(u_{1}(T))^{2}\\
&\geq\left(1-T^{2}-\sqrt{T}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-(u_{1}(T))^{2}\\
&\geq c_{0}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-(u_{1}(T))^{2}\end{split}

(1.15)

for any
c_{0}\in(0,1)
, for small enough times and controls.
Since
x_{1}(T)=u_{1}(T)
, all final states in the “half-space”
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3};\enskip x_{3}+x_{1}^{2}<0\}
are not reachable, so that system (
1.14
) is not STLC at
0
.
In particular, one cannot reach any target of the form
(0,0,-\delta)
for
\delta>0
.

This example is a particular case of the following more general result, proved by Sussmann in
[
35
, Proposition 6.3]
(see also
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
for a modern proof with sharper hypotheses).

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1}
be real-analytic vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
f_{0}(0)=0
.
Consider the control system
\dot{x}=f_{0}+uf_{1}
.
Let
S_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the controllable vector subspace for the linearized system at
0
.
If
[f_{1},[f_{1},f_{0}]](0)\notin S_{1}
, then the nonlinear system is not STLC at
0
.

We refer to the situation of (
1.15
) (or more generally of
Proposition

1.11
) as a “quadratic obstruction to controllability”, involving a “drift” quantified by the (squared)
H^{-1}
Sobolev norm of the control.
For ODEs, the quadratic obstructions to controllability have been classified in
[
4
,
6
]
and are linked with the
H^{-k}
norms of the control for
1\leq k\leq\dim S_{1}
, and with iterated Lie brackets of length
2k+1
.

These quadratic obstructions of integer order are proved on the equation (
1.1
) in
Appendix

A
, for functions
\mu
that appropriately vanish at the boundary (in particular
\mu^{\prime}(0)=\mu^{\prime}(1)=0
, which is incompatible with (
1.9
), see (
1.6
)). They have also been observed for other PDEs (see
[
7
,
11
,
17
]
for Schrödinger with Dirichlet boundary conditions,
[
5
]
for a parabolic model,
[
15
]
for a fluid mechanics model, and
[
31
]
for a Korteweg–de Vries system).

The proof of
Theorem

1.7
involves the same main terms and arguments as in (
1.15
):

•

obtaining a coercivity estimate
\geq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
for the quadratic terms when
T\ll 1
,

•

obtaining a bound for the cubic terms of the form
o(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})
when
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
,

•

a geometric description of unreachable targets to handle terms involving
u_{1}(T)
.

Remark 1.12
.

Under appropriate regularity assumptions on
\mu
, the assumptions
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
and
a_{k}\neq 0
of
Theorem

1.7
precisely correspond to the Lie bracket condition of
Proposition

1.11
.

For instance, if
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
and
\mu^{\prime}(0)=\mu^{\prime}(1)=0
(i.e.
\mu\in H^{2}_{N}
), then

\begin{split}2a_{k}&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\big(\lambda_{j}+(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\big)\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\langle\varphi_{j},\mu\varphi_{k}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\big(\langle\mu,A\varphi_{j}\rangle\langle\varphi_{j},\mu\varphi_{k}\rangle+\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\langle(A-\lambda_{k})\varphi_{j},\mu\varphi_{k}\rangle\big)\\
&=\langle A\mu,\mu\varphi_{k}\rangle+\langle\mu,(A-\lambda_{k})(\mu\varphi_{k})\rangle=-\langle[\mu,[\mu,A]]\varphi_{0},\varphi_{k}\rangle,\end{split}

(1.16)

where the third line results from integrations by parts using
\mu^{\prime}(0)=\mu^{\prime}(1)=0
.

The assumption
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
implies that the controllable space
S_{1}
of the linearized system (
1.17
) is contained in
\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{j}\mid j\neq k\}
.
The assumption
a_{k}\neq 0
corresponds to
[\mu,[\mu,A]]\varphi_{0}\notin S_{1}
.

1.3.2
Power-series expansion for the Schrödinger equation

As is usual for such results, we perform a power series expansion of the state with respect to the control, writing
\psi=\varphi_{0}+\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}+\dotsc
where
\varphi_{0}
is the equilibrium around which we are studying controllability,
\psi_{1}
is the solution to the linearized system

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{1}(t,x)=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{1}(t,x)-u(t)\mu(x)\varphi_{0}(x)&\quad t\in(0,T),\quad x\in(0,1),\\
\partial_{x}\psi_{1}(t,0)=\partial_{x}\psi_{1}(t,1)=0&\quad t\in(0,T),\end{cases}

(1.17)

and
\psi_{2}
is the quadratic order expansion, solution to

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{2}(t,x)=-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{2}(t,x)-u(t)\mu(x)\psi_{1}(t,x)&\quad t\in(0,T),\quad x\in(0,1),\\
\partial_{x}\psi_{2}(t,0)=\partial_{x}\psi_{2}(t,1)=0&\quad t\in(0,T),\end{cases}

(1.18)

with initial data
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(0)=0
when
\psi(0)=\varphi_{0}
.

Applying the Duhamel formula to (
1.17
) and (
1.18
) yields

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}u(s)e^{-iA(t-s)}\mu\varphi_{0}\,\mathrm{d}s=i\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\left(\int_{0}^{t}u(s)e^{-i\lambda_{j}(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\varphi_{j},

(1.19)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{-i\lambda_{k}(T-t)}\langle\mu\psi_{1}(t),\varphi_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t.

(1.20)

Using the coefficients
c_{j}
of (
1.7
), and
\lambda_{0}=0
, one has when
k=0
(see
Section

1.6
for
k>0
),

\begin{split}\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{0}\rangle&=-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\int_{0}^{t}u(s)e^{-i\lambda_{j}(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-\iint_{0<s<t<T}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}e^{-i\lambda_{j}|t-s|}\Big)u(s)u(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(1.21)

Using symmetry to extend the integral to the square
(s,t)\in(0,T)^{2}
, and using that the control is real-valued, we obtain
\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{0}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}Q(u,u)
where we define, for
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

Q(u,v):=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}K(t-s)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\langle K\star u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}

(1.22)

where

K(\sigma):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}e^{-i\lambda_{j}|\sigma|}.

(1.23)

By (
1.8
) this series converges absolutely and
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
.
One must then study the complex valued bilinear form (
1.22
) to attempt to exhibit a drift generalizing the form (
1.15
).

for a water-tank system.

If
\mu
has an infinite number of non-vanishing Fourier coefficients
\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle
, one cannot differentiate twice the kernel
K
defined in (
1.23
).
Indeed, taking into account (
1.3
) and (
1.8
), the derivative
K^{\prime}
of this kernel
is heuristically similar to the Riemann function
\sigma\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-2}\sin(j^{2}\sigma)
, which is differentiable almost nowhere (see
[
24
, Theorem 4.31]
and
[
20
,
21
]
).

Thus, one must look for a method avoiding two integrations by parts to highlight the drift.
The method we therefore develop could also be used to weaken the assumptions of existing results (for example,
[
11
, Theorem 1.3, case
n=1
]
assumes a condition of the form
\sum j^{4}|c_{j}|<\infty
, while our strategy could probably be adapted to conclude when one only has
\sum j^{2}|c_{j}|<\infty
).

1.3.4
Fourier-based approach for quadratic obstructions

To circumvent this difficulty, we will avoid integrations by parts and perform our estimates in the Fourier frequency domain.
Indeed, benefiting from the convolution structure of (
1.22
), heuristically, one expects to be able to obtain from Plancherel’s theorem that

Q(u,u)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{K}(\omega)|\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(1.25)

Then, one can derive properties on the coercivity of
Q
by studying the asymptotic behavior of
\widehat{K}
.
Formally, one would for example expect that, if
\widehat{K}(\omega)\sim\omega^{-2}
as
|\omega|\to\infty
, one would obtain an
H^{-1}
Sobolev norm.
This is for example what happens for some critical lengths of the Korteweg–de Vries system (see
[
31
, Lemma 4.2]
).

Such Fourier-based approaches to quadratic obstructions, or variations thereof, have been used in
[
5
]
to obtain
H^{-s}
drifts for parabolic systems, in
[
16
]
and
[
29
]
to obtain
H^{-2/3}
and
H^{-1/6}
drifts for Korteweg–de Vries systems, and in
[
28
]
to obtain an
H^{-5/4}
drift in finite time for a Burgers system.
For this last system, the
H^{-5/4}
drift had already been established in
[
26
]
using an analysis in the time domain relying on weakly singular integral operators.

In this paper, we encounter difficulties linked with the fact that the most natural expression of
\widehat{K}
is only a distribution, which involves Dirac masses and principal values (see
Section

2.1
) so that the asymptotic
\widehat{K}(\omega)\sim\omega^{-2}
is not valid near these singularities.
Nevertheless, we show that these local perturbations can be neglected as
T\to 0
.

1.3.5
An intuition of the positive result

When
a_{k}=0
, the key difficulty lies in proving that it is possible to move approximately in the directions
\pm i\varphi_{k}
.
Hence the core of the proof of
Theorem

1.9
consists in constructing, for any
T\in(0,1]
, controls
u^{\pm i}\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
such that
Q(u^{\pm i},u^{\pm i})\approx\pm iT\|u^{\pm i}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
From (
1.25
), for this to be possible, we need
\Im\widehat{K}
to change sign.
Moreover, for
T\ll 1
, since the control is supported in
[0,T]
, its Fourier transform will involve high frequencies and have a wide support.
So
\Im\widehat{K}
not only needs to change sign, but it needs to do so up to
\pm\infty
, and achieve both signs on arbitrarily large intervals.
Heuristically, to achieve a motion of amplitude
T\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we will need
\pm\Im\widehat{K}(\omega)\geq T\omega^{-2}
on intervals much larger than
1/T
.
One can then choose a control oscillating at the median frequency of such an interval, say
\omega_{0}
, to achieve the desired sign.

Such considerations were exactly what motivated the construction in
[
4
, Section 5]
where the first two authors designed an abstract nonlinearity such that
\widehat{K}(\omega)\approx(\sin(\ln(1+|\omega|)))/\omega^{2}
which precisely takes positive and negative values on increasingly larger intervals as
|\omega|\to\infty
.
Here, we prove in
Section

4.2
that the same situation occurs – although this time, the nonlinearity is more physically reasonable, as it is just given by the bilinearity in the Schrödinger model.

The controls
u^{\pm i}
must also leave the linearized system invariant, i.e. they must guarantee that
\psi_{1}(T)=0
(recall (
1.19
)).
Indeed, otherwise, for small controls (which are required by
Definition

1.2
of STLC), the linear movement would dominate the quadratic one, so we would not be achieving (approximate) motions in the directions
\pm i\varphi_{k}
.
More formally, the controls must be chosen within the kernel of the map
u\mapsto\psi_{1}(T)
.
This corresponds to the requirement that
\widehat{u}(\lambda_{j})=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Our construction ensures this by choosing
\omega_{0}
far from the
\lambda_{j}
.

Once motions in the directions
\pm i\varphi_{k}
are established, those in the directions
\pm\varphi_{k}
follow from a standard tangent vector argument (see
Proposition

4.11
).
For control-affine systems in finite dimension
\dot{x}=f_{0}+uf_{1}
with
f_{0}(0)=0
, it is well-known that if
\pm\xi
are tangent vectors (i.e. one can move infinitesimally in these directions for small times), then the same holds for
\pm Df_{0}(0)\xi
(see
[
25
, Theorem 6]
or
[
8
, Property (P2)]
).
This argument has been adapted to several PDEs (see
[
12
, Section 6.4]
or
[
22
, Proposition 4.8]
for Schrödinger with Dirichlet boundary conditions, or
[
14
, Section 4.3]
for Navier–Stokes).
Here
-iA
plays the role of
f_{0}
and
-iA(\pm i\varphi_{k})=\mp\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}
.

1.6

In

1.3.2

of (

Let us explain what changes for

Starting again from (

) and (

&=-\iint_{0<s<t<T}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}e^{i\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}|t-s|}e^{-i\lambda_{j}|t-s|}\Big)(u\rho_{k})(t)(u\rho_{k})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}Q_{k}(u\rho_{k},u\overline{\rho_{k}}),\end{split}

(1.29)

where, as after (

, symmetry, and we define, for

\displaystyle Q_{k}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}K_{k}(t-s)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t,

(1.30)

\displaystyle K_{k}(\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\exp\left(i\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}|\sigma|\right)K(\sigma).

(1.31)

The frequency modulation in the arguments of

(i.e. the fact that

is evaluated at
(u\rho_{k},u\overline{\rho_{k}})
instead of
(u,u)
in (

3.6
).

The frequency modulation in the kernel

with respect to

We dedicate

5
to the general abstract topic of transferring estimates obtained on a quadratic form given by a reference kernel

\chi K
for a given multiplier

.
We use this abstract formalism to estimate

In particular, this modulation explains the formula for the coefficient

of (

2
Study of the quadratic form

In this section, we study the reference quadratic form
Q(u,v)
defined in (
1.22
) from the convolution kernel
K
of (
1.23
).
We start by computing the Fourier transform of
K
, to express
Q
in the frequency domain (see
Proposition

2.7
).
We then decompose this Fourier transform into several parts, and analyze their properties in preparation for the subsequent sections.
Unless otherwise stated, all functions and distributions in this section are complex-valued.

2.1
Expression of the kernel in the frequency domain

The function
K
(see (
1.23
), (
1.7
), (
1.3
)) does not belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, but defines a tempered distribution because
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus its Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}(K)
is well defined in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
The goal of this section is to prove the following explicit expression of
\mathcal{F}(K)
:

\mathcal{F}(K)=\pi\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}(\delta_{\lambda_{j}}+\delta_{-\lambda_{j}})-2i\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\lambda_{j}c_{j}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}},

(2.1)

where the right-hand side contains a sum of Dirac masses and principal value distributions (see
Proposition

2.6
for a precise statement).

Remark 2.1
.

The fact that
\mathcal{F}(K)
is merely a distribution and not an integrable function comes from the lack of decay of
K
at
\pm\infty
.
However, the definition of
Q
in (
1.22
) only involves the values of
K(\sigma)
for
\sigma\in[-T,T]
.
Hence, one would keep the same quadratic form by replacing
K
with
K_{T}:=K\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{[-T,T]}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which thus has an
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
Fourier transform.
However, up to the necessary precautions implied by working with distributions, we find that working with
K
(and not
K_{T}
) allows a better comprehension of the different contributions composing the kernel, and more explicit computations.
These are particularly useful to obtain the positive result in
Section

4
.

We need the following definitions and preliminary results.

Definition 2.2

(Dilation, translation, Fourier transform)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
.
For a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote by
D_{\lambda}f
,
\tau_{\alpha}f
and
\widehat{f}
the functions defined by

D_{\lambda}f(t):=\sqrt{\lambda}f(\lambda t),\qquad\tau_{\alpha}f(t):=f(t-\alpha),\qquad\widehat{f}(\omega):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)e^{-i\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.2)

For a tempered distribution
S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote by
D_{\lambda}S
,
\tau_{\alpha}S
and
\mathcal{F}(S)
the tempered distributions defined by their action on Schwartz functions as

\langle D_{\lambda}S,\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}:=\langle S,D_{\lambda^{-1}}\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}},\enskip\langle\tau_{\alpha}S,\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}:=\langle S,\tau_{-\alpha}\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}},\enskip\langle\mathcal{F}(S),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}:=\langle S,\widehat{\phi}\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}

(2.3)

and then the following equalities hold in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
:

\mathcal{F}(D_{\lambda}S)=D_{\lambda^{-1}}\mathcal{F}(S),\qquad\mathcal{F}(\tau_{\alpha}S)=e^{-i\alpha\cdot}\mathcal{F}(S),\qquad\mathcal{F}(e^{i\alpha\cdot}S)=\tau_{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(S).

(2.4)

Definition 2.3

(Principal value)
.

The distribution
\operatorname{pv}(\frac{1}{\omega})\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\langle\operatorname{pv}\left(\frac{1}{\omega}\right),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|\omega|>\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(\omega)-\phi(-\omega)}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

(2.5)

and satisfies (see e.g.
[
34
, eq. (VII.7.19)]
)

\mathcal{F}\left(\operatorname{pv}\left(\frac{1}{\omega}\right)\right)=-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\cdot).

(2.6)

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\lambda>0
,

\mathcal{F}\left(e^{-i\lambda|\cdot|}\right)=\pi(\delta_{\lambda}+\delta_{-\lambda})-\frac{2i\lambda}{\lambda^{2}-\omega^{2}}

(2.7)

in the following sense: for every
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\langle\mathcal{F}\left(e^{-i\lambda|\cdot|}\right),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}=\pi(\phi(\lambda)+\phi(-\lambda))-2i\lambda\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|\omega\pm\lambda|>\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(2.8)

Proof.

Step 1.
We first prove that
\mathcal{F}(e^{-i|\cdot|})=\pi(\delta_{1}+\delta_{-1})-i(\tau_{-1}-\tau_{+1})\operatorname{pv}\left(\frac{1}{\omega}\right)
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.

We have
e^{-i|\cdot|}=\cos(\cdot)-i\sin|\cdot|
and
\mathcal{F}(\cos)=\pi(\delta_{1}+\delta_{-1})
thus it suffices to prove that

\mathcal{F}(\sin|\cdot|)=(\tau_{-1}-\tau_{+1})\operatorname{pv}\left(\frac{1}{\omega}\right)
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
By (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), the Fourier transform of the right-hand side is
2\pi\sin|\cdot|
.
The Fourier inversion formula on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
gives the result since
\sin|\cdot|
is even.

Step 2.
Scaling argument.

By (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and
Section

2.1
,

\langle\mathcal{F}\left(e^{-i\lambda|\cdot|}\right),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}=\langle\mathcal{F}\left(e^{-i|\cdot|}\right),\phi(\lambda\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{S^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}=\pi(\phi(\lambda)+\phi(-\lambda))-iL_{\lambda}\phi

(2.9)

where
L_{\lambda}\phi:=\langle(\tau_{-1}-\tau_{+1})\operatorname{pv}\left(\frac{1}{\omega}\right),\phi(\lambda\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}}
so that, recalling
2.5
,

\begin{split}L_{\lambda}\phi&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\int_{|\omega|>\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\omega}\phi(\lambda(\omega+1))\,\mathrm{d}\omega-\int_{|\omega|>\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\omega}\phi(\lambda(\omega-1))\,\mathrm{d}\omega\right)\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\int_{|y-\lambda|>\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(y)}{y-\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{|y+\lambda|>\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(y)}{y+\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|y\pm\lambda|>\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{y-\lambda}-\frac{1}{y+\lambda}\right)\phi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

(2.10)

The second equality results from the change of variables and the third one from the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

As in (
2.5
), the limit in the right-hand side of (

) has an explicit expression.
For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, one can write

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{|\omega\pm\lambda|>\delta}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\int_{|\omega\pm\lambda|>\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega+\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\zeta(\omega)-\zeta(-\omega)}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

(2.11)

where

\zeta(\omega):=-\frac{\phi(\lambda+\omega)}{2\lambda+\omega}+\frac{\phi(-\lambda+\omega)}{2\lambda-\omega}.

(2.12)

This equality holds for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda)
, so that the denominators in

do not vanish when
\omega\in(0,\varepsilon)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For every
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\langle\mathcal{F}(K),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}}=\pi\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}(\phi(\lambda_{j})+\phi(-\lambda_{j}))-2i\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(\omega)\Theta(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega

, and the integral in the right-hand side of (

of the Lebesgue integral over

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

By
1.23

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{F_{\varepsilon}}\phi(\omega)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\lambda_{j}c_{j}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{|\omega\pm\lambda_{j}|>\delta}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(2.16)

Note that the series in the right-hand side of (

For every

\int_{F_{\varepsilon}}\phi(\omega)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\lambda_{j}c_{j}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}\int_{F_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

(2.18)

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and

.
The following unions are disjoint:

\{\omega\in\mathbb{R};|\omega\pm\lambda_{j}|\geq\varepsilon\}=F_{\varepsilon}\sqcup J_{j}^{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{ where }\qquad J_{j}^{\varepsilon}:=\underset{k\neq j}{\sqcup}\{\omega\in\mathbb{R};|\omega\pm\lambda_{k}|<\varepsilon\}.

(2.19)

Using (

2.11
), we obtain

\begin{split}\Bigg|\int_{F_{\varepsilon}}&\phi(\omega)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\lambda_{j}c_{j}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{|\omega\pm\lambda_{j}|>\delta}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\\
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}c_{j}|\Bigg|\int_{F_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega-\int_{|\omega\pm\lambda_{j}|\geq\varepsilon}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega-\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\zeta_{j}(y)-\zeta_{j}(-y)}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigg|\\
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}c_{j}|\Bigg(\int_{J_{j}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\phi(\omega)|}{|\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}|}\,\mathrm{d}\omega+\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|\zeta_{j}(y)-\zeta_{j}(-y)|}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigg)\\
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}c_{j}|\Bigg(\|\phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus F_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\Bigg)\end{split}

(2.20)

where we used
|\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}|\geq 1
for
\omega\in J_{j}^{\varepsilon}
and the estimate (see (
2.12
)), for
y\in(0,\varepsilon)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\frac{|\zeta_{j}(y)-\zeta_{j}(-y)|}{y}\leq 2\|\zeta^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\varepsilon)}\leq 4\left(\frac{\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}}{|\lambda_{j}|}+\frac{\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|\lambda_{j}|^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\|\phi\|_{W^{1,\infty}}.

(2.21)

The dominated convergence theorem proves that the last right-hand side of (
2.20
) tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which proves (
2.16
) and concludes the proof.
∎

2.2
Expression of the quadratic form in the frequency domain

We can now deduce the following expression for the complex-valued bilinear form
Q
of (
1.22
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
T>0
.
For
u,v\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

2\pi Q(u,v)=\pi\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}\left((\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(\lambda_{j})+(\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(-\lambda_{j})\right)-2i\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta(\omega)\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega

(2.22)

where
the integral of the right-hand side must be understood in the sense of a convergent integral (not a Lebesgue one), which is the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of the Lebesgue integral over
F_{\varepsilon}
(see (
2.15
)).

Proof.

The function
K\star u
is bounded because
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, thus
K\star u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since
v\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

2\pi Q(u,v)=2\pi\langle K\star u,\overline{v}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}}=\langle\mathcal{F}(K\star u),\overline{\widehat{v}}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}}.

(2.23)

Since
K\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
u\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
(i.e.
u
is a distribution with compact support), we have
\mathcal{F}(K\star u)=\mathcal{F}(K)\widehat{u}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus
2\pi Q(u,v)=\langle\mathcal{F}(K),\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}}
and

Proposition

2.6
gives the conclusion.
∎

2.3
Decomposition of the Fourier kernel

Formula (
2.22
) already separates two different contributions to the quadratic form
Q
: one involving Dirac masses at the
\pm\lambda_{j}
, and one defined by integration of the Fourier transforms against the Fourier kernel
\Theta
defined in (
2.14
).
To analyze
Q
, we must further decompose
\Theta
.
The following example yields a good intuition.

Example 2.8
.

Assume that
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
is such that
c_{0}=0
and for all
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
c_{j}=\pi^{2}/j^{4}
(which is consistent with (
1.8
)).
Then, for
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\pm\lambda_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\}
,

\Theta(\omega)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{1}{j^{2}}\frac{1}{j^{4}-(\omega/\pi^{2})^{2}}=-\frac{\pi^{6}}{6\omega^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{6}\cot(\sqrt{\omega})}{4\omega^{2}\sqrt{\omega}}+\frac{\pi^{6}\coth(\sqrt{\omega})}{4\omega^{2}\sqrt{\omega}}.

(2.24)

This equality can be proved by decomposing the rational function with respect to
j
, then recognizing the Mittag-Leffler series for
\cot
and
\coth
.

To mimic the three components in (
2.24
), we write for
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\pm\lambda_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\}
,

\Theta(\omega)=-\frac{a}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}

(2.25)

where
a:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}
was already defined in (
1.33
) and
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}
isolates, within each frequency region, the dominant singularity.
More precisely,

\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{2}c_{j_{\omega}}}{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}-|\omega|}\quad\text{where}\quad j_{\omega}:=\left\lfloor\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{|\omega|}{\pi^{2}}}+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor.

(2.26)

This corresponds to setting
j_{\omega}=j
for
|\omega|\in[\pi^{2}(j^{2}-j),\pi^{2}(j^{2}+j))
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, so that
|\omega|\approx\lambda_{j_{\omega}}
.
This corresponds roughly to keeping only the term for
j=j_{\omega}
within the sum (
2.14
), up to a lower-order term.
Eventually,
\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}
is the remainder.

The first term in (
2.25
), scaling like
\omega^{-2}
will yield the
H^{-1}
drift when its coefficient
a\neq 0
.
The second term contains localized singularities at
\pm\lambda_{j}
.
The associated contribution will be negligible with respect to the first term for small enough times when
a\neq 0
.
However, when
a=0
, we will exploit their change of sign in
Section

4.2
to construct controls achieving
Q(u,u)=\pm 1
.
The third term will be estimated in a weaker norm (heuristically the
H^{-5/4}
norm in the case (
2.24
)), and will always be negligible.

In the following subsections, we derive estimates for each part.

2.4
Subspace of controls

Recall that, for a given control
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
we denote by
u_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}u
its primitive vanishing at
0
.
To avoid technicalities, we will establish all the results of the next subsections only for controls in the following subspace (i.e. of zero average on
(0,T)
)

\mathcal{H}:=\{u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})\mid u_{1}(T)=0\}.

(2.27)

This will be sufficient for our purpose thanks to
Lemma

3.4
and thanks to closed-loop estimates, which allow to estimate
u_{1}(T)
from the state (see
Section

3.3
).

For
u\in\mathcal{H}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, an integration by parts yields

\widehat{u}(\omega)=i\omega\widehat{u_{1}}(\omega),

(2.28)

since the boundary term vanishes thanks to
u_{1}(T)=0
.

We will use the notation
P_{\mathcal{H}}
to denote the orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{H}
, i.e. for
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,
P_{\mathcal{H}}u:=u-u_{1}(T)/T
.
We have
(P_{\mathcal{H}}u)_{1}(t)=u_{1}(t)-u_{1}(T)\frac{t}{T}
.
Thus, using the reverse triangular inequality, for
\nu\geq 0
and
T\in(0,1]
,

|\left\lVert(P_{\mathcal{H}}u)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}-\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}|\leq|u_{1}(T)|\left\lVert\frac{t}{T}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq|u_{1}(T)|\left\|\frac{t}{T}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{1}(T)|.

(2.29)

2.5
Estimates for the singular part

Here, we focus on the part of the kernel’s Fourier transform involving principal values.
We start by estimating precisely the contribution of the principal value integral for frequencies at distance at most
\delta/T
of the eigenvalues (see
Proposition

2.9
) then prove a continuity result for the full contribution associated with
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}
(see
Corollary

2.11
).

We use the notation
\lesssim
of
Section

1.5
for constants independent of time and controls.

Proposition 2.9
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T,\delta>0
and
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus F_{\delta/T}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq CT\delta\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}

(2.30)

where, as in (
2.15
),
\mathbb{R}\setminus F_{\delta/T}=\{\omega\in\mathbb{R};\enskip||\omega|-\lambda_{j_{\omega}}|\leq\delta/T\}
.

Proof.

To lighten the computations, we focus on the region
\omega\geq 0
and introduce
\phi(\omega):=\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)
.
Recalling the definition of
j_{\omega}
in (
2.26
), letting
\delta_{j}:=\min\{\pi^{2}j,\delta/T\}
, the intervals
(\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j},\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j})
are disjoint and we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus F_{\delta/T}}\frac{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{2}c_{j_{\omega}}}{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}-|\omega|}\phi(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega,

(2.31)

where, here and in the sequel of this proof, all such integrals are to be understood as convergent principal value integrals as in
Proposition

2.6
.
Using a Taylor expansion (see
Lemma

B.5
), one has

\Big|\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Big|\leq\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}|\phi^{\prime}|+2\delta_{j}\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}|\phi^{\prime\prime}|.

(2.32)

Let
J:=\lceil 1/T\rceil
.
By
1.8
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
|\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}|\lesssim 1
.
Thus

\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\Bigg|\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\lesssim\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,\pi^{2}J^{2})}+2\frac{\delta}{T}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{1}}.

(2.33)

For
j\geq J
, using a Taylor expansion (see
Lemma

B.5
), we write

\Bigg|\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq\frac{2\delta}{T}|\phi^{\prime}(\lambda_{j})|+\frac{\delta}{T}\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}|\phi^{\prime\prime}|

(2.34)

By Taylor expansion, for every
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and
M>0

|\phi^{\prime}(\omega)|\leq\frac{1}{2M}\int_{\omega-M}^{\omega+M}|\phi^{\prime}|+\int_{\omega-M}^{\omega+M}|\phi^{\prime\prime}|.

(2.35)

In particular, with
\omega=\lambda_{j}
and
M=1/T
,

|\phi^{\prime}(\lambda_{j})|\leq\frac{T}{2}\int_{\lambda_{j}-1/T}^{\lambda_{j}+1/T}|\phi^{\prime}|+\int_{\lambda_{j}-1/T}^{\lambda_{j}+1/T}|\phi^{\prime\prime}|.

(2.36)

Since
j\geq J\geq 1/T
, the intervals
[\lambda_{j}-1/T,\lambda_{j}+1/T]
are disjoint.
Thus,

\sum_{j\geq J}\Bigg|\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}\int_{\lambda_{j}-\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{j}}\frac{\phi(\omega)}{\lambda_{j}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\lesssim\delta\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{\delta}{T}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{1}}.

(2.37)

It is straightforward to check that
\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}\leq 4\pi T\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{1}}\leq 8\pi T^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}
using for example that
\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}(\omega)=-i\widehat{tu(t)}(\omega)
and similar formulas for
v
and second-order derivatives.
Hence, gathering (
2.31
), (
2.33
) and (
2.37
),

\begin{split}\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus F_{\delta/T}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|&\lesssim\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,\pi^{2}J^{2})}+\delta T\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim(1+\delta)T\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.38)

Moreover, using that, for
\omega\in[0,\pi^{2}J^{2}]
,
1\lesssim T^{-1}\langle\omega\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

\begin{split}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,\pi^{2}J^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\phi^{\prime}(\omega)|}{\langle\omega\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega&\lesssim\frac{1}{T}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\left\lVert tv(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}+\frac{1}{T}\left\lVert tu(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\end{split}

(2.39)

thanks to
Lemma

B.10
.
Eventually, combined with (
2.38
), this yields (
2.30
).
∎

The proof of
Proposition

2.9
actually entails the following continuity result.

Corollary 2.10
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}\Bigg|\leq CT\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.40)

Proof.

For
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
, we write, for
T>0
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}=\int_{F_{1/T}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus F_{1/T}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}},

(2.41)

where
F_{1/T}
is defined in (
2.15
).
By
1.8
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
|\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}|\lesssim 1
.
Hence, we deduce from the definition (
2.26
) of
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}
that, for all
\omega\in F_{1/T}
,
|\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)|\lesssim T
.
Thus,

\int_{F_{1/T}}|\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}|\lesssim T\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}|\lesssim T\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.42)

From the proof of
Proposition

2.9
with
\delta=1
, we have (recall (
2.38
)):

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus F_{1/T}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}\right|\lesssim T\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.43)

Combining these estimates yields the conclusion.
∎

Thus,
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)/\omega^{2}
yields a bilinear operator continuous for the
H^{-1}
topology.

Corollary 2.11
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
u,v\in\mathcal{H}
,

\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq CT\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.44)

Proof.

For
u,v\in\mathcal{H}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, one has
\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)=\omega^{2}\widehat{u_{1}}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v_{1}}}(\omega)
by (
2.28
), so
Corollary

2.10
immediately implies estimate (
2.44
).
∎

2.6
Estimates for the Dirac part

We prove a continuity estimate for the part of
Q
involving Dirac masses at frequencies
\pm\lambda_{j}
.

Proposition 2.12
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
u,v\in\mathcal{H}
,

\bigg|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}\left((\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(\lambda_{j})+(\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(-\lambda_{j})\right)\bigg|\leq CT\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}.

(2.45)

Proof.

First, by (
2.28
),
(\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(\omega)=\omega^{2}(\widehat{u_{1}}\overline{\widehat{v_{1}}})(\omega)
, so that

\bigg|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}\left((\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(\lambda_{j})+(\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(-\lambda_{j})\right)\bigg|=\bigg|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}\left((\widehat{u_{1}}\overline{\widehat{v_{1}}})(\lambda_{j})+(\widehat{u_{1}}\overline{\widehat{v_{1}}})(-\lambda_{j})\right)\bigg|.

(2.46)

Moreover, by (
1.3
) and (
1.8
),
\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}=O(1)
.
Hence the claimed estimate follows directly from
Lemma

2.13
(see below) applied to
u_{1}
and
v_{1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}|(\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})(\lambda_{j})|\leq CT\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}.

(2.47)

Proof.

By Cauchy–Schwarz, each summand is bounded by
T\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}
.
In particular, we can safely omit the term
j=0
.
For
j\geq 1
and any
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, write by Taylor expansion:

\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}(\lambda_{j})=\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)+\int_{\omega}^{\lambda_{j}}(\widehat{u}\partial_{\omega}\overline{\widehat{v}}+\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}})

(2.48)

Averaging over
\omega\in I_{j}:=(\lambda_{j}-j\pi^{2},\lambda_{j}+j\pi^{2})
, one has

\begin{split}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}(\lambda_{j})&=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}j}\int_{I_{j}}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}j}\int_{I_{j}}\int_{\omega}^{\lambda_{j}}(\widehat{u}\partial_{\omega}\overline{\widehat{v}}+\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}),\quad\text{so}\\
|\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}(\lambda_{j})|&\leq\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}j}\int_{I_{j}}|\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}|+\int_{I_{j}}(|\widehat{u}\partial_{\omega}\overline{\widehat{v}}|+|\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}|).\end{split}

(2.49)

Since
\lambda_{j}=(j\pi)^{2}
, the intervals
I_{j}
are disjoint.
Moreover, there exists
c>0
such that, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\omega\in I_{j}
,
1/j\leq c\langle\omega\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Hence, since
\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}(\omega)=\mathcal{F}[-itu(t)](\omega)
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}|\widehat{u}\overline{\widehat{v}}(\lambda_{j})|\leq\frac{c}{\pi}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}+4\pi(\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|tv(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|tu(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}),

(2.50)

which concludes the proof using
\|tu(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq T\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

2.7
Estimate of the regular part

Here, we focus on the regular part
\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}
of the kernel, for which we first provide a pointwise estimate (see
Proposition

2.14
) which entails a continuity estimate for the associated bilinear operator in a weak norm (see
Corollary

2.15
).

Proposition 2.14
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,

|\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\omega)|\leq C\frac{\ln(1+\langle\omega\rangle)}{\langle\omega\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

(2.51)

Proof.

By symmetry, we can assume that
\omega\geq 0
.

We start with the case of low frequencies
0\leq\omega<\pi^{2}/2
.
Hence
j_{\omega}=1
.
For
\omega\in[0,\pi^{2}/2]
,
|\lambda_{j}-\omega|\geq\pi^{2}/2
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
By (
1.8
),
\sum|\lambda_{j}c_{j}|<+\infty
.
Thus
\Theta
is bounded on
[0,\pi^{2}/2]
.
On this interval,
|\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)|\leq\lambda_{1}^{2}|c_{1}|/\pi^{2}
.
Hence
\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\omega)=\omega^{2}\Theta(\omega)+a-\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)
is bounded on
[0,\pi^{2}/2]
.

We now assume that
\omega\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}
.
In particular
\langle\omega\rangle\approx j_{\omega}^{2}
.
Since
a=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}
, we write

|\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\omega)|\leq\Bigg|\sum_{j\neq j_{\omega}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}\Bigg(\frac{\omega^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}+1\Bigg)\Bigg|+\Bigg|\frac{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{3}c_{j_{\omega}}}{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{2}-\omega^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{2}c_{j_{\omega}}}{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}-|\omega|}\Bigg|.

(2.52)

For the second term, we write, using (
1.8
), there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

\Bigg|\frac{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{3}c_{j_{\omega}}}{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{2}-\omega^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{2}c_{j_{\omega}}}{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}-|\omega|}\Bigg|=\Bigg|\frac{\lambda_{j_{\omega}}^{2}c_{j_{\omega}}}{2(\lambda_{j_{\omega}}+\omega)}\Bigg|\leq C_{0}\langle\omega\rangle^{-1}.

(2.53)

Thus, to conclude, it suffices to prove that there exists
C>0
such that, for all
\omega\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}
,

\left|\sum_{j\neq j_{\omega}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}+1\right)\right|\leq C\langle\omega\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ln(1+\langle\omega\rangle).

(2.54)

Since
\lambda_{j}c_{j}=O(j^{-2})
, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\left|\sum_{j\neq j_{\omega}}\lambda_{j}c_{j}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}+1\right)\right|\leq\sum_{j\neq j_{\omega}}\frac{C_{1}}{j^{2}}\left|\frac{\omega^{2}}{\pi^{4}j^{4}-\omega^{2}}+1\right|=\frac{\pi C_{1}}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{j\neq j_{\omega}}g\left(\frac{j\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

(2.55)

where
g(s):=s^{2}|s^{4}-1|^{-1}
.

The function
g
is increasing on
[0,1)
and decreasing on
(1,\infty)
.
Using a classical series-integral comparison argument, we have

\frac{\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\omega}-2}g\left(\frac{j\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\leq\int_{\pi|\omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pi(j_{\omega}-1)|\omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.56)

From (
2.26
), we obtain

\frac{\pi(j_{\omega}-1)}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}-\alpha\leq 1-\frac{\alpha}{2}=1-\frac{\pi}{4|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(2.57)

where we used the notation
\alpha:=\pi/(2|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}})\in(0,1)
, and the intermediate inequality is valid for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Since
g(s)\leq\frac{1}{1-s}
for
s\in(0,1)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\omega}-2}g\left(\frac{j\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)&\ \leq\int_{0}^{\pi(j_{\omega}-1)|\omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s}{1-s}=-\ln\left(1-\frac{\pi(j_{\omega}-1)}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\\
&\leq\ln\left(\frac{4|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\pi}\right)\leq C_{2}\ln(1+\langle\omega\rangle),\end{split}

(2.58)

for some absolute constant
C_{2}>0
.
We had to exclude the term
j=j_{\omega}-1
. Using the same estimates, we find

\frac{\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}g\left(\frac{(j_{\omega}-1)\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\leq\frac{\pi}{|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{4|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\pi}\leq 4.

(2.59)

Eventually, proceeding similarly we can estimate the term for
j=j_{\omega}+1
, and the sum for
j\geq j_{\omega}+2
by a series-integral comparison, using the fact that
g
is integrable at infinity.
∎

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{4})
.
There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
u,v\in\mathcal{H}
,

\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq C\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}.

Proof.

By (

and

.
Hence, by

such that

\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\ln(1+\langle\omega\rangle)}{\langle\omega\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}|(\widehat{u_{1}}\overline{\widehat{v_{1}}})(\omega)|\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(2.61)

Thus, fixing any

Remark 3.2
.

Estimate (

For instance, if

\forall u\in\mathcal{H},\qquad\Im(Q(u,u))\geq(2a-CT^{\nu})\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>0.

First, it is necessary to restrict to the hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
i.e. this quadratic form is not positive on

Indeed, for example when
k=0
(so that

\Im Q(u,u)=2\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{|\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\left(\sin(\lambda_{j}T)-\lambda_{j}T\right)\leq 2\left(\sin(\lambda_{1}T)-\lambda_{1}T\right)\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\left|\left\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}<0.

(3.4)

Moreover, it is also necessary to consider small values of

, i.e. this quadratic form is not positive on
\mathcal{H}
when
T
is large.
Consider again the case
k=0
(so that
c_{j}=|\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}

For every

, the function

belongs to

and an explicit computation gives, for every

,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sin\left(-\lambda|t-s|\right)&\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right)\left(s-\frac{T}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=&-\frac{T^{3}}{6\lambda}-\frac{T^{2}\sin{\left(\lambda T\right)}}{2\lambda^{2}}-\frac{2T\cos{\left(\lambda T\right)}}{\lambda^{3}}+\frac{2\sin{\left(\lambda T\right)}}{\lambda^{4}}.\end{split}

Thus, there exists

such that, for

\Im Q(u,u)\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\left\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left(-\frac{T^{3}}{6\lambda_{j}}+\frac{T^{2}}{2\lambda_{j}^{2}}+\frac{2T}{\lambda_{j}^{3}}+\frac{2}{\lambda_{j}^{4}}\right)\leq-c_{0}T^{3}.

(3.6)

Proposition

3.1
is an estimate on the

.
Using the abstract toolbox exposed in

5

3.2
Coercivity of the second-order expansion

Using the notations of
Section

1.6
, we now transfer the coercivity of
Q_{k}
(defined in (
1.30
) from the kernel
K_{k}
of (
1.31
)), to the second-order expansion of the state.
We must handle two subtleties: first,
Corollary

3.3
is only valid for controls in
\mathcal{H}
and second, the formula for
\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle
involves
Q_{k}
evaluated at controls with a modulated phase.
We establish general lemmas which will be re-used in
Section

4
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,1]
and
v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

|Q_{k}(v,\bar{v})-Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}v,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})|\leq CT^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}^{2}+C|v_{1}(T)|^{2},

(3.9)

where
P_{\mathcal{H}}v
is the orthogonal projection of
v
onto
\mathcal{H}
, i.e.
P_{\mathcal{H}}v=v-v_{1}(T)/T
.

Proof.

Using the symmetry of (
1.30
), (
1.31
) and (
1.23
),

\begin{split}Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}v,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})-Q_{k}(v,\bar{v})&=2Q_{k}(v,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v}-\bar{v})+Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}v-v,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v}-\bar{v})\\
&=-\frac{2v_{1}(T)}{T}Q_{k}(v,\mathbbm{1})+\frac{(v_{1}(T))^{2}}{T^{2}}Q_{k}(\mathbbm{1},\mathbbm{1}).\end{split}

(3.10)

Since
K_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
,
|Q_{k}(\mathbbm{1},\mathbbm{1})|\leq T^{2}\|K_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
From (
1.30
),

Q_{k}(v,\mathbbm{1})=v_{1}(T)\int_{0}^{T}K_{k}(t-T)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}v_{1}(s)\int_{0}^{T}K_{k}^{\prime}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.11)

The first term is bounded by
T|v_{1}(T)|\|K_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
For the second term, we write

K_{k}^{\prime}(\sigma)+ia_{k}\operatorname{sgn}\sigma=i(\operatorname{sgn}\sigma)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}\left(\lambda_{j}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}\right)\left(1-e^{-i(\lambda_{j}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2})|\sigma|}\right).

(3.12)

Using that
|1-e^{-i\theta}|\leq\min(2,|\theta|)
, (
1.3
) and (
1.8
), and splitting the sum at
j\approx|\sigma|^{-\frac{1}{2}}

|K_{k}^{\prime}(\sigma)+ia_{k}\operatorname{sgn}\sigma|\leq c\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\min(1,(1+j^{2})|\sigma|)}{1+j^{2}}\leq c^{2}|\sigma|^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.13)

for some
c>0
.
Thus, by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\int_{0}^{T}v_{1}(s)\int_{0}^{T}K_{k}^{\prime}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s=-ia_{k}\int_{0}^{T}v_{1}(s)(T-2s)\,\mathrm{d}s+O(T^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}).

(3.14)

The first term is bounded by
T|a_{k}|\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\left\lVert 1-2t/T\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}
.
By (
B.17
) and
Lemma

B.10
, we have
\left\lVert 1-2t/T\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\lesssim 1
for
T\in[0,1)
and
\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{4})
.
There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,1]
, and
u,v\in\mathcal{H}
,

|Q_{k}(u,v)|\leq C(|a_{k}|+T)\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}.

(3.15)

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
Proposition

2.16
,
Proposition

5.9
and (
1.33
).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{4})
.
There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,1]
and
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
,

|Q_{k}(u\rho_{k},u\overline{\rho_{k}})-Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}u,P_{\mathcal{H}}u)|\leq CT(|a_{k}|+T)\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}^{2}+C|u_{1}(T)|^{2},

(3.16)

where
\rho_{k}(t)=\exp(i\lambda_{k}t/2)
and
P_{\mathcal{H}}u
is the orthogonal projection of
u
onto
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
v:=u\rho_{k}
and
w:=v-u
.
Write

Q_{k}(v,\bar{v})-Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}u,P_{\mathcal{H}}u)=Q_{k}(v,\bar{v})-Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}v,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})+Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}w,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})+Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}u,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{w}).

(3.17)

First, by
Lemma

3.4
, one can estimate
Q_{k}(v,\bar{v})-Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}v,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})
as in (
3.9
).
Integrating by parts,

v_{1}=u_{1}\rho_{k}-h_{1}\quad\text{where}\quad h:=u_{1}\rho_{k}^{\prime}.

(3.18)

By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\|h_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim T\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
.
Hence
\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}+T\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

|Q_{k}(v,\bar{v})-Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}v,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})|\lesssim T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}^{2}+|u_{1}(T)|^{2}.

(3.19)

Second, by
Lemma

3.5
,

\displaystyle|Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}w,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})|

\displaystyle\lesssim(|a_{k}|+T)\|(P_{\mathcal{H}}w)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|(P_{\mathcal{H}}v)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert(P_{\mathcal{H}}w)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\left\lVert(P_{\mathcal{H}}v)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}},

(3.20)

\displaystyle|Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}u,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{w})|

\displaystyle\lesssim(|a_{k}|+T)\|(P_{\mathcal{H}}u)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|(P_{\mathcal{H}}w)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert(P_{\mathcal{H}}u)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\left\lVert(P_{\mathcal{H}}w)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}.

(3.21)

Terms involving
P_{\mathcal{H}}u
are estimated by (
2.29
).
For

, integrations by parts lead to

(P_{\mathcal{H}}w)_{1}(t)=u_{1}(t)(\rho_{k}(t)-1)-\int_{0}^{t}u_{1}\rho_{k}^{\prime}+\frac{t}{T}u_{1}(T)(1-\rho_{k}(T))+\frac{t}{T}\int_{0}^{T}u_{1}\rho_{k}^{\prime}.

(3.22)

Hence, since

,

\left\lVert(P_{\mathcal{H}}w)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq\|(P_{\mathcal{H}}w)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim T\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+T|u_{1}(T)|.

(3.23)

Terms involving
P_{\mathcal{H}}v
will be estimated by triangular inequality from

and
P_{\mathcal{H}}w
.
Hence, gathering (
3.20
), (
3.21
), (
2.29
) and (

),

|Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}w,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{v})|+|Q_{k}(P_{\mathcal{H}}u,P_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{w})|\lesssim T(|a_{k}|+T)\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}^{2}+|u_{1}(T)|^{2}.

(3.24)

Eventually, the result follows from (
3.17
), (
3.19
) and (
3.24
).
∎

3.3
Closed-loop estimate

As illustrated in

but only on the subspace of controls such that

.
As for the basic ODE example (

u_{1}(T)
can be estimated in a “closed-loop” manner from the state of the system (so it cannot be chosen freely independently on the target).
We refer to
[
6

for more details on this notion.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
a_{k}\neq 0
(see (
1.7
)).
There exists
T^{*}>0
such that, for any
T\in(0,T^{*})
and
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
such that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
,

|u_{1}(T)|\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\psi(T)-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.30)

Proof.

If
T\leq 1
, by the quadratic remainder estimate (
6.38
) of
Corollary

6.8
(see
Section

6.5
), and the assumption that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
,

\|\psi(T)-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}(T)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{1}(T)|+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.31)

Since
a_{k}\neq 0
, one has
\mu\neq 0
.
Fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\neq 0
.
By (
1.19
),

\langle(\psi-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1})(T),\varphi_{j}\rangle=\langle\psi(T)-\varphi_{0},\varphi_{j}\rangle-i\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{-i\lambda_{j}(T-t)}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.32)

Thus (
3.31
) and
\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle\neq 0
imply that

\left|\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{i\lambda_{j}t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\lesssim\|\psi(T)-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{1}(T)|+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.33)

Using integration by parts and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

|u_{1}(T)|=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\big(u(t)+i\lambda_{j}u_{1}(t)\big)e^{i\lambda_{j}t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\lesssim\left|\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{i\lambda_{j}t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.34)

Thus incorporating (
3.33
) in the previous estimate gives the conclusion for small enough times.
∎

3.4
Conclusion of the proof

We can now conclude the proof of
Theorem

1.7
.

Proof.

We write

\langle\psi(T)-\varphi_{0},\varphi_{k}\rangle=\langle\psi_{1}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle+\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle+\langle(\psi-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle.

(3.35)

By assumption,
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
, so
\langle\psi_{1}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
by (
1.19
).
Thanks to
Corollary

3.7
with
\nu=\frac{1}{8}
,

\left|\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle+ia_{k}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{8}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|u_{1}(T)|^{2}.

(3.36)

By the cubic remainder estimate (
6.42
) of
Corollary

6.10
(see
Section

6.6
), for
T\leq 1
and
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
,

|\langle(\psi-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle|\lesssim|u_{1}(T)|^{3}+T^{\frac{1}{8}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.37)

Eventually, if
T<T^{*}
given by
Proposition

3.8
,

|u_{1}(T)|^{3}\lesssim|u_{1}(T)|^{2}\lesssim T\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi(T)-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.38)

Gathering these estimates yields the claimed drift estimate (
1.10
) with
\nu=\frac{1}{8}
.
∎

4
Proof of the positive result

We prove
Theorem

1.9
.
In all this section, we therefore assume that
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
is fixed with
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
,
a_{k}=0
(see
1.7
) and that assumption (
1.9
) holds (see
Remark

1.6
for the case
k=0
).
The core of the proof lies in the analysis of the quadratic form
Q
to achieve motions in the directions
\pm i\varphi_{k}
(see
Section

4.2
).
The other parts of the argument are relatively standard (motion in the directions
\pm\varphi_{k}
, control in projection, and nonlinear fixed-point argument with estimate of the cost).

4.1
Prequel: solvability of moment problems

Using assumption (
1.9
), most of the components of the state will be controlled at the linearized level.
Starting from (
1.19
), this typically relies on the solvability of moment problems.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
h^{m}_{r}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
be the set of sequences
(d_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
d_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and

\|d\|_{h^{m}}:=\|\langle j\rangle^{m}d_{j}\|_{\ell^{2}}<+\infty.

(4.1)

Given a
d\in h^{0}_{r}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
, a
moment problem
consists in finding a control
u
such that

\forall j\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{-i\lambda_{j}t}\,\mathrm{d}t=d_{j}.

(4.2)

It is known that this problem is solvable and that for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
H^{m}
norm of
u
scales like the
h^{2m}
norm of
d
.
We will need the following results, which are particular cases of
[
10
, Theorem 3.10]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every
T>0
, there exists
C_{T}>0
and a continuous linear map
L_{T}:h^{0}_{r}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})\rightarrow L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
such that, for every
d\in h^{0}_{r}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
, the control
u:=L_{T}(d)
satisfies (
4.2
),

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{T}\|d\|_{h^{0}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+|u_{1}(T)|\leq C_{T}\|d\|_{h^{-2}}.

(4.3)

Proposition 4.3
.

For every
T>0
, there exists
C_{T}>0
and a continuous linear map
L_{T}:h^{2}_{r}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})\to H^{1}_{0}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
such that, for every
d\in h^{2}_{r}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})
, the control
u:=L_{T}(d)
satisfies (
4.2
),

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{T}\|d\|_{h^{0}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\leq C_{T}\|d\|_{h^{2}}.

(4.4)

4.2
Approximate movement in the main direction

Let
T>0
.
We now use the analysis of
Section

2
to construct controls
u^{\pm i}
such that the state moves approximately in the directions
\pm i\varphi_{k}
.
The goal of this section is to prove
Proposition

4.9
.
Heuristically, we will use a control such that

u_{1}(t)\propto\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cos(t\omega_{0})\chi\left(\frac{t}{T}\right),

(4.5)

where
\chi
is a smooth cut-off function and
\omega_{0}
is a very carefully chosen frequency, located near (but not too close to) a singularity of
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}
(depending on the desired sign of the motion).

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
\|\chi\|_{L^{2}}=1
and

\forall p\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\qquad\widehat{\chi}(4p\pi)=0.

(4.6)

Proof.

Let
f:=\mathbbm{1}_{[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]}
.
A direct integration shows that

\widehat{f}(\omega)=\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{-i\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t=2e^{-i\frac{\omega}{2}}\frac{\sin(\omega/4)}{\omega}.

(4.7)

In particular, for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
\widehat{f}(4p\pi)=0
.
Now take
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
a smooth mollifier with
\operatorname{supp}\rho\subset[-\frac{1}{8},\frac{1}{8}]
.
Let
\chi_{0}:=f\star\rho
.
By standard properties of convolution,
\chi_{0}\in C^{\infty}
,
\operatorname{supp}\chi_{0}\subset[\frac{1}{8},\frac{7}{8}]\subset(0,1)
, and
\widehat{\chi_{0}}=\widehat{f}\widehat{\rho}
.
Thus, for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
\widehat{\chi_{0}}(4p\pi)=0
.
Eventually, setting
\chi:=\chi_{0}/\|\chi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\chi\in H^{1}_{0}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
There exists
C(\chi,\nu)>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
\omega_{0}\geq 1/T
,

\left\lVert T^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{i\omega_{0}t}\chi(t/T)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq C(\chi,\nu)\omega_{0}^{-\nu}.

(4.8)

Proof.

Let
f(t):=T^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{i\omega_{0}t}\chi(t/T)
.
Its Fourier transform is
\widehat{f}(\omega)=T^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\chi}(T(\omega-\omega_{0}))
.
Using the definition (
1.28
) and the change of variables
\sigma=T(\omega-\omega_{0})
, the squared norm is

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|^{2}}{\langle\omega_{0}+\sigma/T\rangle^{2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\frac{T^{2\nu}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|^{2}}{|A+\sigma|^{2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

(4.9)

using
\langle\omega_{0}+\sigma/T\rangle\geq|A+\sigma|/T
, where
A:=T\omega_{0}\geq 1
.
We split the integral in two regions.

First, when
|A+\sigma|\geq A/2
, we have
|A+\sigma|^{-2\nu}\leq 2^{2\nu}A^{-2\nu}
.
Hence

\int_{|A+\sigma|\geq A/2}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|^{2}}{|A+\sigma|^{2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq 2^{2\nu}A^{-2\nu}\|\widehat{\chi}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.10)

Second, when
|A+\sigma|\leq A/2
,
|\sigma|\geq A/2
.
Since
\chi\in H^{1}_{0}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
, for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|\leq\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}/|\sigma|
.
Thus

\int_{|A+\sigma|\leq A/2}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|^{2}}{|A+\sigma|^{2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\left(\frac{\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}}{A/2}\right)^{2}\int_{|y|<A/2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{2\nu}}=\frac{4\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{A^{2}}\frac{2^{2\nu}A^{1-2\nu}}{1-2\nu},

(4.11)

where we used that
\nu<\frac{1}{2}
to obtain the convergence of the integral.
Recalling that
A\geq 1
, we conclude the proof with
C(\chi,\nu):=2^{\nu}\|\chi\|_{L^{2}}+2^{\nu}\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}(1-2\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

There exists
C>0
such that the following property holds.
For all
\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
T>0
and
\eta>0
, there exist
V^{\pm}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
with
\|V^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
such that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{V^{\pm}}|^{2}=\pm T,\qquad\left\lVert V^{\pm}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq\eta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\langle j\rangle^{4}|\widehat{V^{\pm}}(\lambda_{j})|^{2}\leq\eta^{2}.

(4.12)

Proof.

Let
\chi
be given by
Lemma

4.4
.
In the sequel of this proof, we will denote by
C_{\chi}
various constants depending only on
\chi
.
In particular, since
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
,
|\widehat{\chi}(\omega)|\leq C_{\chi}\langle\omega\rangle^{-4}
.

For
T>0
, let
\omega_{0}:=\lambda_{j_{0}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\beta}{T}
where
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\beta>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}\in\{\pm 1\}
are to be chosen later.
Define

u(t):=2T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cos(t\omega_{0})\chi(t/T).

(4.13)

Thus,

\widehat{u}(\omega)=T^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\chi}(T\omega+T\omega_{0})+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\chi}(T\omega-T\omega_{0}).

(4.14)

We claim that one can choose
\omega_{0}
in such a way that
u
will satisfy the requirements for
V^{\pm}
.
First, one has
\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq 2\|\chi\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=2
.
We now study the three items of (
4.12
).

Step 1.
Smallness of weak Sobolev norms of the candidate control.

For any fixed
\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, by
Lemma

4.5
, there exists a constant
C(\chi,\nu)
such that, for all
T>0
and
\omega_{0}\geq 1/T
,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq 2C(\chi,\nu)\omega_{0}^{-\nu}.

(4.15)

Thus, for any fixed
(T,\varepsilon_{0},\beta,\eta)
, for
j_{0}
large enough,
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq\eta
.
In particular, for any fixed
(T,\varepsilon_{0},\beta)
, for
j_{0}
large enough,
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}^{2}\leq T/\beta^{2}
(which will be used in
Section

4.2
).

Step 2.
Computation of
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}
.
We prove that there exists a constant
M(\chi,\mu)
such that, for any fixed tuple
(T,\varepsilon_{0},\beta)
with
\beta>2^{\frac{1}{3}}
, for
j_{0}
large enough,

\Bigg|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{0}\lambda_{j_{0}}^{2}c_{j_{0}}\frac{T}{\beta}\Bigg|\leq\frac{MT}{\beta^{2}}.

(4.16)

Recalling the notation (
2.15
), write

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}=\int_{F_{1/(\beta^{2}T)}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus F_{1/(\beta^{2}T)}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}.

(4.17)

We now estimate both terms.

•

Regions close to the poles.

By
Proposition

2.9
with
\delta=1/\beta^{2}
, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus F_{1/(\beta^{2}T)}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}\Bigg|\leq\frac{C_{1}T}{\beta^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{4}}}^{2}\leq\frac{5C_{1}T}{\beta^{2}}

(4.18)

for
j_{0}
large enough (for fixed
(T,\varepsilon_{0},\beta)
), using
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2
and
Section

4.2
.

•

Regions far from the poles.

We focus on the first term of (
4.17
).
Let
I_{0}:=[\omega_{0}-\frac{\beta}{2T},\omega_{0}+\frac{\beta}{2T}]
.
First, let us prove that there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\Bigg|\int_{F_{1/(\beta^{2}T)}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}-\lambda_{j_{0}}^{2}c_{j_{0}}\int_{I_{0}}\frac{|\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}}{\lambda_{j_{0}}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq\frac{C_{2}T}{\beta^{2}}.

(4.19)

Taking
\beta>2^{\frac{1}{3}}
and
j_{0}>\frac{3\beta}{2\pi^{2}T}
, we have
I_{0}\subset F_{1/(\beta^{2}T)}
and
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{j_{0}}^{2}c_{j_{0}}/(\lambda_{j_{0}}-\omega)
on
I_{0}
.

By (
1.8
), there exists
A_{\mu}>0
such that, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
|\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}|\leq A_{\mu}
.
In particular, by (
2.26
), for
\omega\in F_{1/(\beta^{2}T)}
,
|\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}(\omega)|\leq A_{\mu}\beta^{2}T
.
Hence, using that
\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}
is even,

\Bigg|\int_{F_{1/(\beta^{2}T)}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}-\lambda_{j_{0}}^{2}c_{j_{0}}\int_{I_{0}}\frac{|\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}}{\lambda_{j_{0}}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq A_{\mu}\beta^{2}T\int_{||\omega|-\omega_{0}|\geq\frac{\beta}{2T}}|\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(4.20)

Since
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
, using (
4.14
),

\int_{||\omega|-\omega_{0}|\geq\frac{\beta}{2T}}|\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq 4\int_{|\omega|\geq\frac{\beta}{2}}|\widehat{\chi}(\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq\frac{C_{\chi}}{\beta^{4}}.

(4.21)

Combining these two estimates yields (
4.19
) with
C_{2}:=A_{\mu}C_{\chi}
.

Second, using again the decay of
\widehat{\chi}
, (
4.14
) and Cauchy–Schwarz, one proves that there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

\Bigg|\int_{I_{0}}\frac{|\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}}{\lambda_{j_{0}}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega-\int_{I_{0}}\frac{T|\widehat{\chi}(T(\omega-\omega_{0}))|^{2}}{\lambda_{j_{0}}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Bigg|\leq\frac{C_{3}T}{\beta^{2}}.

(4.22)

Third, by a change of variables,

\int_{I_{0}}\frac{T|\widehat{\chi}(T(\omega-\omega_{0}))|^{2}}{\lambda_{j_{0}}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}T}{\beta}\int_{-\frac{\beta}{2}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|^{2}}{1+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\sigma}{\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

(4.23)

Using
\left|\frac{1}{1+x}-1\right|\leq 2|x|
for all
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
and the decay of
\chi
, one finds

\begin{split}\left|\int_{I_{0}}\frac{T|\widehat{\chi}(T(\omega-\omega_{0}))|^{2}}{\lambda_{j_{0}}-\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega+2\pi\frac{\varepsilon_{0}T}{\beta}\right|&\leq\frac{T}{\beta}\int_{-\frac{\beta}{2}}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}2\frac{|\sigma|}{\beta}|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\frac{T}{\beta}\int_{|\sigma|>\frac{\beta}{2}}|\widehat{\chi}(\sigma)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&\leq\frac{C_{4}T}{\beta^{2}},\end{split}

(4.24)

for some
C_{4}>0
.

Eventually, combining (
4.18
), (
4.19
), (
4.22
) and (
4.24
) proves (
4.16
).

Step 3.
Choice of the parameters.

Using
Lemma

4.7
, let
a_{\mu}>0
such that
|\lambda_{j}^{2}c_{j}|\geq a_{\mu}
for
j
large enough.
We now fix a number
\beta\in 4\pi\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
\beta\geq 2M/a_{\mu}
.
Then, for any fixed
T>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}\in\{\pm 1\}
, for
j_{0}
large enough, by (
4.16
), one has

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{u}|^{2}=-\varepsilon_{0}T\left(\frac{\lambda_{j_{0}}^{2}c_{j_{0}}}{\beta}(1+\delta_{u})\right),

(4.25)

where
|\delta_{u}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
The absolute value of the term inside the parenthesis is uniformly bounded below and above.
Thus, one can define
V^{\pm}
by choosing
\varepsilon_{0}
and rescaling

, yielding a uniform constant
C
for the
L^{2}
norm of
V^{\pm}
.

Step 4.
Estimate of the moments sum.

To estimate the weighted sum of the moments in (
4.12
), we must separate the one for
j=j_{0}
.

•

For
j=j_{0}
, recalling that
\omega_{0}=\lambda_{j_{0}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\beta}{T}
, by (
4.14
),

\widehat{u}(\lambda_{j_{0}})=T^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\chi}(2T\lambda_{j_{0}}+\varepsilon_{0}\beta)+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\chi}(-\varepsilon_{0}\beta)

(4.26)

We have chosen
\beta\in 4\pi\mathbb{N}^{*}
, so that, by
Lemma

4.4
,
\widehat{\chi}(-\varepsilon_{0}\beta)=0
.
If
\pi^{2}j_{0}^{2}\geq\beta/T
, we have
2T\lambda_{j_{0}}+\varepsilon_{0}\beta\geq T\lambda_{j_{0}}
, hence
|\widehat{u}(\lambda_{j_{0}})|\leq C_{\chi}T^{1/2}\langle T\lambda_{j_{0}}\rangle^{-4}
.
Thus
\langle j_{0}\rangle^{4}|\widehat{u}(\lambda_{j_{0}})|^{2}\leq C_{\chi}j_{0}^{4-16}\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}
for

large enough.

•

For
j\neq j_{0}
, if
j_{0}\geq\beta/T
, then
|\lambda_{j}\pm\omega_{0}|\geq(j+j_{0})
.
So, using (
4.14
),

|\widehat{u}(\lambda_{j})|^{2}\leq C_{\chi}T\langle T(j+j_{0})\rangle^{-8}.

(4.27)

Hence

\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}\langle j\rangle^{4}|\widehat{u}(\lambda_{j})|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{\chi}}{T^{15}j_{0}^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}

(4.28)

for
j_{0}
large enough.∎

Lemma 4.7
.

If
\mu\in H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{R})

Proof.

\mu\in H^{2}
,

.

) and equality (

) imply that

.

) with

, we obtain

.
Combined with (

) and the definition (

) of the coefficients

).
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists
C>0
such that the following property holds.
For all
T>0
and
\eta>0
, there exist
U^{\pm}\in H^{1}_{0}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
with
\|U^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
such that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{\mathrm{pv}}|\widehat{U^{\pm}}|^{2}=\pm T,\qquad\left\lVert U^{\pm}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq\eta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{N},\quad\widehat{U^{\pm}}(\lambda_{j})=0.

(4.31)

Proof.

4.3.1
A concatenation lemma

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
T,T^{\prime}>0
,
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})

If

\langle\psi_{2}(T+T^{\prime};u\diamond v,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle=\langle\psi_{2}(T;u,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle e^{-i\lambda_{k}T^{\prime}}+\langle\psi_{2}(T^{\prime};v,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle,

(4.42)

where

denotes the time-concatenation of
u
and
v

Proof.

For
t\in[0,T]

\psi_{1}(t;u\diamond v,0)=\psi_{1}(t;u,0)
and
\psi_{2}(t;u\diamond v,0)=\psi_{2}(t;u,0)

For

, the assumption
\psi_{1}(T;u,0)=0
implies that
\psi_{1}(t;u\diamond v,0)=\psi_{1}(t-T;v,0)

For
t\in[T,T+T^{\prime}]
, the evolution equation (
1.18
) for
\psi_{2}
reads

\begin{split}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\langle\psi_{2}(t;u\diamond v,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle&=-i\lambda_{k}\langle\psi_{2}(t;u\diamond v,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle+i(u\diamond v)(t)\langle\mu\psi_{1}(t;u\diamond v,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle\\
&=-i\lambda_{k}\langle\psi_{2}(t;u\diamond v,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle+iv(t-T)\langle\mu\psi_{1}(t-T;v,0),\varphi_{k}\rangle,\end{split}

(4.43)

which implies (
4.42
).
∎

4.3.2
Free evolution of a tangent vector

Proposition 4.11
.

Proof.

Let

given by
Proposition

4.9
.
Given

and

Proposition

4.9

First, using the property

,

, we have

\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle=\langle\psi_{2}(T_{M};u^{+i}),\varphi_{k}\rangle e^{-i\lambda_{k}(T-T_{M})}+\langle\psi_{2}(T_{M};u^{-i}),\varphi_{k}\rangle.

Thus, by (

\left|\Re\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle-T_{M}\sin(\lambda_{k}(T-T_{M}))\right|\leq 2C_{1}T_{M}^{2}.

\left|\Im\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle\right|\leq T_{M}(1-\cos(\lambda_{k}(T-T_{M})))+C_{1}T_{M}^{2}\left|\sin(\lambda_{k}(T-T_{M}))\right|.

Using

\begin{split}\left|\Re\langle\psi_{2}(T),\varphi_{k}\rangle-\lambda_{k}TT_{M}\right|&\leq(2C_{1}+\lambda_{k})T_{M}^{2}+T_{M}\lambda_{k}^{2}T^{2}\\

provided that

Thus, we obtained

∎

4.3.3

Proposition 4.12
.

4.9

\widetilde{\psi}_{0}:=\psi_{0}-\Re\langle\psi_{0},\varphi_{0}\rangle\varphi_{0}
satisfies
(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}^{*})\in\Omega_{T}
and

\psi_{0}=(1-\|\widetilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{0}+\widetilde{\psi}_{0}
, thus, if
u\in L^{2}
denotes the second component of
F_{T}^{-1}(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}^{*})
then
(
4.61
) holds, and
u
depends continuously on
\psi_{0}
and
\widetilde{\psi}^{*}
.

Step 3.
We prove (
4.62
).

The map
F_{T}^{-1}
is
C^{1}
thus locally Lipschitz: there exists
C_{T}>0
such that (even if it means reducing
\delta_{T}
),

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{T}(\|\widetilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{H^{2}})\leq C_{T}(\|\psi_{0}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{H^{2}}).

(4.70)

Step 4.
We prove (
4.63
).

In the proof of the inverse mapping theorem, the point
(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},u)
is obtained as a fixed point in
\mathcal{V}

(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},u)=(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},u)-DF_{T}(0,0)^{-1}\cdot(F_{T}(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},u)-(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}^{*})).

(4.71)

This equality can also be written in the following ways

\begin{split}(0,u)&=(0,u)-DF_{T}(0,0)^{-1}\cdot(F_{T}(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},u)-(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}^{*}))\\
&=-DF_{T}(0,0)^{-1}\cdot(F_{T}(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},u)-DF_{T}(0,0)\cdot(0,u)-(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}^{*}))\\
&=-DF_{T}(0,0)^{-1}\cdot(0,\mathbb{P}_{k}\psi(T;u,\psi_{0})-\mathbb{P}_{k}\psi_{1}(T;u,0)-\widetilde{\psi}^{*}).\end{split}

(4.72)

Thus, by
Section

4.4
and (
6.38
) (even if it means reducing
\delta_{T}
so that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}<C_{T}\delta_{T}<1
and
T\leq 1
),

\displaystyle N_{T}(u)

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\|\mathbb{P}_{k}\psi(T;u,\psi_{0})-\mathbb{P}_{k}\psi_{1}(T;u,0)-\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}

(4.73)

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}(\|\psi(T;u,\psi_{0})-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}(T;u,0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}(\|\psi(T;u,\psi_{0})-\psi(T;u,\varphi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\psi(T;u,\varphi_{0})-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}(T;u,0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}(\|\psi_{0}-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+N_{T}(u)^{\frac{3}{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}).

Even if it means reducing
\delta_{T}
(so that
C_{T}N_{T}(u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{T}^{\prime}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{T}^{\prime\prime}\delta_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}<1
), we deduce (
4.63
).
∎

4.5
Small-time nonlinear control and cost estimate

We combine the previous arguments to conclude the proof with the Brouwer fixed-point theorem.

Proof of
Theorem

1.9
.

Let
T^{*}\in(0,1]
be given by
Proposition

4.12
and
T\in(0,T^{*})
.
For larger
T
, since
\varphi_{0}
is an equilibrium, one can start with a null control.

Let
\delta_{T}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{T/2}>0
, where
\delta_{T/2}
is given by
Proposition

4.15
.
Let
\psi^{*}\in H^{2}_{N}\cap\mathbb{S}
be such that

\|\psi^{*}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{N}}<\delta_{T}^{\prime}
.
Then
\widetilde{\psi}^{*}:=\mathbb{P}_{k}(\psi^{*})
belongs to
H^{2}_{N}\cap V_{k}
and satisfies
\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{H^{2}}<\delta_{T}^{\prime}
.

Step 1.
Reformulation of the control problem.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, consider the control
v^{z}\in L^{2}((0,\frac{T}{2});\mathbb{R})
given by
Proposition

4.12
(for a time
T\leftarrow\frac{T}{2}
and a target complex number
z\leftarrow ze^{i\lambda_{k}\frac{T}{2}}
), which satisfies:

\displaystyle v^{z}_{1}(T/2)=0,\quad\psi_{1}(T/2;v^{z},0)=0,\quad\langle\psi_{2}(T/2;v^{z},0),\varphi_{k}\rangle=ze^{i\lambda_{k}\frac{T}{2}},

(4.74)

\displaystyle\|v^{z}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{T}|z|^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad N_{T/2}(v^{z})\lesssim\frac{|\Re(z)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}+\frac{|\Im(z)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Thus, recalling estimate (
6.3
), we obtain

\|\psi(T/2;v^{z},\varphi_{0})-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}=\|\psi(T/2;v^{z},\varphi_{0})-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}(T/2;v^{z},0)\|_{H^{2}}\leq C_{T}\|v^{z}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{T}|z|.

(4.75)

In particular, if
z
is small enough, then

\|\psi(T/2;v^{z},\varphi_{0})-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{N}}<\delta_{T}^{\prime}
.
Thus, by
Proposition

4.15
, there exists
w^{z}\in L^{2}((0,\frac{T}{2});\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k}\psi(T/2;w^{z},\psi(T/2;v^{z},\varphi_{0}))=\widetilde{\psi}^{*},

(4.76)

\displaystyle\|w^{z}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{T}(|z|+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{H^{2}}),

(4.77)

\displaystyle N_{T/2}(w^{z})\leq C_{T}(|z|+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}).

(4.78)

Let
u^{z}:=v^{z}\diamond w^{z}\in L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})
.
Then
\mathbb{P}_{k}\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0})=\widetilde{\psi}^{*}
.
Thus

\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0})=\psi^{*}\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\langle\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0}),\varphi_{k}\rangle=z^{*}:=\langle\psi^{*},\varphi_{k}\rangle.

(4.79)

Step 2.
Let
\theta:=\frac{1}{16}
. We prove that, for
z
small enough

\left|\langle\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0}),\varphi_{k}\rangle-z\right|\leq C_{T}\left(|z|^{1+\theta}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

(4.80)

Using
Lemma

4.14
, (
4.74
) and (
4.78
), we have

N_{T}(u^{z})\leq C_{T}(|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}).

(4.81)

Since
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
, we have
\langle\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0}),\varphi_{k}\rangle=A_{z}+B_{z}
where

A_{z}:=\langle\psi_{2}(T;u^{z},0),\varphi_{k}\rangle,\quad B_{z}:=\langle\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0})-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}(T;u^{z},0)-\psi_{2}(T;u^{z},0),\varphi_{k}\rangle.

(4.82)

Using
Lemma

4.10
we obtain

A_{z}=\langle\psi_{2}(T/2;v^{z},0),\varphi_{k}\rangle e^{-i\lambda_{k}\frac{T}{2}}+\langle\psi_{2}(T/2;w^{z},0),\varphi_{k}\rangle.

(4.83)

Thus, using (
4.74
), (
3.25
) and (
4.78
),

|A_{z}-z|\leq CN_{T}(w^{z})^{2}\leq C_{T}(|z|^{2}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.84)

Using (
6.42
) of
Corollary

6.10
(see
Section

6.6
) since
T\leq 1
and (
4.81
) we obtain

|B_{z}|\leq C_{T}N_{T}(u^{z})^{2+2\theta}\leq C_{T}(|z|^{1+\theta}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2+2\theta})

(4.85)

which gives the conclusion.

Step 3.
Brouwer fixed-point theorem.

Let
C_{T}
be given by
Section

4.5
and
r>0
such that
C_{T}r^{1+\theta}<\frac{r}{3}
.
We may assume that
\|\psi^{*}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}
is small enough so that
C_{T}\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\frac{r}{3}
and
|z^{*}|<\frac{r}{3}
.
The map

G:\begin{cases}\overline{B}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,r)\to\mathbb{C}\\
z\mapsto z-\langle\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0}),\varphi_{k}\rangle+z^{*}\end{cases}

(4.86)

is continuous (by continuity of
z\mapsto v^{z}
from
Proposition

4.12
and
z\mapsto w^{z}
from
Proposition

4.15
, and continuous dependence of the state on the control and initial data) and takes values in
\overline{B}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,r)
because

|G(z)|\leq|z-\langle\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0}),\varphi_{k}\rangle|+|z^{*}|\leq C_{T}(r^{1+\theta}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{r}{3}<r

(4.87)

By the Brouwer fixed-point theorem, there exists
z\in\overline{B}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,r)
such that
G(z)=z
.
By
Section

4.5
, this implies that
\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0})=\psi^{*}
.

Step 4.
Proof of the estimates.

At the fixed point, we have

z=z^{*}+z-\langle\psi(T;u^{z},\varphi_{0}),\varphi_{k}\rangle.

(4.88)

Hence (
4.80
) implies that

\begin{split}|z-z^{*}|&\leq C_{T}|z|^{1+\theta}+C_{T}\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C_{T}|z-z^{*}|^{1+\theta}+C_{T}|z^{*}|^{1+\theta}+C_{T}\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim C_{T}(2r)^{\theta}|z-z^{*}|+C_{T}|z^{*}|^{1+\theta}+C_{T}\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(4.89)

Up to reducing

so that

, we obtain

|z-z^{*}|\lesssim C_{T}(|z^{*}|^{1+\theta}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.90)

Together with (
4.74
) and (
4.77
), this implies

\|u^{z}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{T}(|z^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{H^{2}}).

(4.91)

Using
Lemma

4.14
, (
4.74
), (
4.78
) and (
4.90
), we obtain

\begin{split}N_{T}(u^{z})&\lesssim\frac{|\Re z|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}+\frac{|\Im z|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T^{\frac{1}{2}}}+C_{T}(|z|+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}})\\
&\lesssim\frac{|\Re z^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}+\frac{|\Im z^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T^{\frac{1}{2}}}+C_{T}(|z^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\theta}{2}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}})\\
&\lesssim\frac{|\Re z^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}+\frac{|\Im z^{*}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T^{\frac{1}{2}}}+C_{T}\|\widetilde{\psi}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

(4.92)

for
z^{*}
small enough.
Eventually (
4.91
) implies (
1.12
) and (
4.92
) implies (
1.13
), concluding the proof of

1.9
.

5
Multiplying kernels by regular functions

In this paragraph, we investigate the following question, in a general abstract setting.
Let
K
be the kernel of an integral operator, of which we have estimated the operator norm.
Let
\chi
be a regular function vanishing on the diagonal.
What estimates can we obtain on the operator norm of the integral operator associated with
\chi K
?

In our context, this will enable us in
Proposition

5.9
to transfer the results obtained in
Section

2
on the reference quadratic form
Q
of
1.22
to the quadratic form
Q_{k}
of (
1.30
).

For
T>0
, we use the following notations:

I:=(0,T),\quad I^{2}:=(0,T)^{2},\quad\Delta_{\pm}:=\{(s,t)\in I^{2}\mid\pm(t-s)>0\}.

(5.1)

5.1
Multiplying a bounded operator on
L^{2}

Given a kernel
K
of a bounded integral operator on
L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
, and a regular function
\chi
vanishing on the diagonal, we estimate the operator norm of the integral operator associated with
\chi K
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
T>0
,
K\in L^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
and
C_{K}
such that, for all
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\left|\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq C_{K}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.2)

Let
\chi\in W^{1,\infty}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
vanishing on the diagonal.
Then, for all
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\left|\int_{I^{2}}\chi(s,t)K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq TC_{K}\left(\|\partial_{1}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{2}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.3)

Proof.

Since
W^{1,\infty}
functions are continuous, the assumption that
\chi
vanishes on the diagonal makes sense.
Since
\chi\in L^{\infty}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
,
K\in L^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
and
u,v\in L^{2}(I;\mathbb{C})
,
\chi(s,t)K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\in L^{1}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
.
Thus the contributions of
\Delta_{+}
and
\Delta_{-}
can be estimated separately; we treat
\Delta_{+}
.
Using
\chi(s,s)=0
, we write

\mathbbm{1}_{\Delta_{+}}(s,t)\chi(s,t)=\mathbbm{1}_{s<t}\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{2}\chi(s,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\theta)}(s)\mathbbm{1}_{(\theta,T)}(t)\partial_{2}\chi(s,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(5.4)

Introduce, for
\theta\in(0,T)
, the projections for
\phi\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

(P_{\theta}\phi)(s):=\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\theta)}(s)\phi(s)\quad\text{and}\quad(Q_{\theta}\phi)(t):=\mathbbm{1}_{(\theta,T)}(t)\phi(t).

(5.5)

Then, using (
5.4
) and Fubini’s theorem,

\int_{\Delta_{+}}\chi(s,t)K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)(P_{\theta}(\partial_{2}\chi(\cdot,\theta)u))(s)(Q_{\theta}\bar{v})(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\theta

(5.6)

For each fixed
\theta\in(0,T)
, assumption (
5.2
) yields

\begin{split}\Bigg|\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)(P_{\theta}(\partial_{2}\chi(\cdot,\theta)u))(s)(Q_{\theta}\bar{v})(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg|&\leq C_{K}\|P_{\theta}(\partial_{2}\chi(\cdot,\theta)u)\|_{L^{2}}\|Q_{\theta}\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C_{K}\|\partial_{2}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(5.7)

Substituting estimate (
5.7
) in (
5.6
) and integrating over
\theta\in(0,T)
bounds the integral over
\Delta_{+}
by
TC_{K}\|\partial_{2}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}
.
By symmetry, one has an analogous bound with
\|\partial_{1}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}
in place of
\|\partial_{2}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}
for the integral over
\Delta_{-}
.
Combining the two estimates proves (
5.3
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Using Cauchy–Schwarz, the hypothesis
K\in L^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
immediately allows to bound left-hand side of (
5.3
) by
\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|K\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}
, where
\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq T\|\chi\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
since
\chi
vanishes on the diagonal.
The interest of the bound (
5.3
) is to involve the operator norm
C_{K}
of assumption (
5.2
), instead of the Hilbert–Schmidt norm
\|K\|_{L^{2}}
, which can be much larger.

Remark 5.3
.

We choose to present
Proposition

5.1
with the assumption
K\in L^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
in order to be able to manipulate usual Lebesgue integrals.
However, one could also adopt the following abstract viewpoint.
Take
K
a continuous linear form of norm
C_{K}
on the projective tensor product
E:=L^{2}(I;\mathbb{C})\otimes L^{2}(I;\mathbb{C})
.
Then the claim is that
\chi K
is a continuous linear form of norm at most
T\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}C_{K}
on
E
.
By duality, this amounts to proving that multiplication by
\chi
is a linear bounded operator on the completion of
E
, which can be done using the same integral representation of
\chi
which allows to separate variables.
An advantage of this abstract viewpoint is that we don’t need to assume that
K\in L^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
, which circumvents the necessity of a regularization argument in the proof of
Proposition

5.6
below.

5.2
Multiplying a bounded operator on a fractional Sobolev space

We generalize
Proposition

5.1
to continuous bilinear forms on
\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})\times\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists a constant
C_{\nu}>0
such that the following property holds.
Let
T>0
,
K\in L^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
and
C_{K}
such that, for all
u,v\in L^{2}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\left|\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq C_{K}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}.

(5.8)

Let
\chi\in W^{1,\infty}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
vanishing on the diagonal.
Assume that
\chi\in W^{2,\infty}(\Delta_{\pm};\mathbb{C})
(but not across the diagonal).
Then, for all
u,v\in L^{2}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\left|\int_{I^{2}}\chi(s,t)K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq TC_{K}C_{\nu}\left(M_{\chi}+T\|\partial_{12}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}

(5.9)

where
M_{\chi}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\partial_{2}\chi(t-0,t)|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\partial_{1}\chi(t+0,t)|
.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of
Proposition

5.1
.
Note that, since
\chi
has
W^{2,\infty}
regularity within each triangle,
\partial_{1}\chi
and
\partial_{2}\chi
have traces on each side of the diagonal.

The key additional argument is that there exists a constant
c_{\nu}>0
such that, for all
0<\theta<T
and
\phi\in L^{2}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,
\left\lVert P_{\theta}\phi\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq c_{\nu}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}
and
\left\lVert Q_{\theta}\phi\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq c_{\nu}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}
, where
P_{\theta}
and
Q_{\theta}
are defined in (
5.5
).
Indeed, by (
1.28
) and
Lemma

B.9
, denoting by
\widetilde{\phi}
the extension of
\phi
by
0
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus(0,T)
,
\left\lVert P_{\theta}\phi\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}=\|P_{\theta}\widetilde{\phi}\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}\leq c_{\nu}\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}=c_{\nu}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}
.

To circumvent the lack of regularity of
\chi
across the diagonal, we write, for
0<s<\theta<T
,

\partial_{2}\chi(s,\theta)=\partial_{2}\chi(\theta-0,\theta)-\int_{0}^{\theta}\partial_{12}\chi(\tau,\theta)\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\tau)}(s)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(5.10)

Thus, starting from (
5.5
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Delta_{+}}\chi(s,t)K(s,t)&u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{2}\chi(\theta-0,\theta)\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)(P_{\theta}u)(s)(Q_{\theta}\bar{v})(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\theta}\partial_{12}\chi(\tau,\theta)\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)(P_{\min(\tau,\theta)}u)(s)(Q_{\theta}\bar{v})(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}\theta\end{split}

(5.11)

Using assumption (
5.8
), layer by layer, for each fixed
\theta
and
\tau
, we obtain

\Bigg|\int_{\Delta_{+}}\chi(s,t)K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg|\leq c_{\nu}^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left(TM_{\chi}+T^{2}\|\partial_{12}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

(5.12)

Together with a similar estimate on
\Delta_{-}
, this gives the conclusion with
C_{\nu}:=2c_{\nu}^{2}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists a constant
C_{\nu}>0
such that the following property holds.
Let
T\in(0,1]
,
K\in L^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
and
C_{K},D_{K}>0
such that, for all
u,v\in L^{2}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\left|\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq C_{K}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+D_{K}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}.

(5.13)

Let
\chi\in W^{1,\infty}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
vanishing on the diagonal, with
\chi\in W^{2,\infty}(\Delta_{\pm};\mathbb{C})
(but not across the diagonal).
There exists
C_{\chi}>0
depending only on its
W^{2,\infty}
norm such that, for all
u,v\in L^{2}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\Bigg|\int_{I^{2}}\chi(s,t)K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg|\leq TC_{\nu}C_{\chi}\left(C_{K}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+D_{K}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\right).

(5.14)

Proof.

This result is easily obtained by working as in the proofs of
Proposition

5.1
and
Proposition

5.4
, using the mixed continuity estimate (
5.13
) on each layer, to obtain estimate (
5.14
).
The assumption
T\leq 1
is used to bound the constant
M_{\chi}+T\|\partial_{12}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}
appearing in (
5.9
).
∎

5.3
Multiplying a bounded operator acting on primitives

In this paragraph, we only consider controls
u,v\in\mathcal{H}
(see (
2.27
)), i.e. such that
u_{1}(T)=v_{1}(T)=0
, where
u_{1}
and
v_{1}
are the primitives of
u
and
v

Proposition 5.6

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})

Proof.

Step 1.
Preliminary comments on (

).

All terms in the left-hand side of (

Indeed, let

Second,

of each triangle, so have

Third, since

, which is the case.
Thus, the left-hand side of (

Step 2.
Case of a regular kernel

Then

to the kernel

.
Fix

Using that

\begin{split}\int_{I^{2}}\chi(s,t)K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{I}wu_{1}\bar{v}_{1}&+\int_{\Delta_{+}\cup\Delta_{-}}\Upsilon(s,t)u_{1}(s)\bar{v}_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\\

(5.17)

where
w(s)=\partial_{1}(\chi K)(s,s+0)-\partial_{1}(\chi K)(s,s-0)=(\partial_{1}\chi(s,s+0)-\partial_{1}\chi(s,s-0))K(s,s)
using that

Moreover, for any

\begin{split}\Bigg|\int_{I^{2}}\partial_{21}K(s,t)f(s)\bar{g}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg|&=\Bigg|\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)f^{\prime}(s)\bar{g}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg|\\

(5.18)

by hypothesis (
5.15

By density, this estimate is preserved for

\begin{split}\Bigg|\int_{\Delta_{+}\cup\Delta_{-}}\chi(s,t)\partial_{21}K(s,t)&u_{1}(s)\bar{v}_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg|\\
&\leq TC_{\nu}C_{\chi}\left(C_{K}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+D_{K}\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\right),\end{split}

(5.19)

).

Step 3.
Regularization argument.

We now construct a sequence
K_{N}\in H^{2}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
such that
K_{N}\to K
in
H^{1}(I^{2};\mathbb{C})
and, for each

By the second step, (

By the first step, the left-hand side of (

Consider the 1D Neumann Laplacian on

It possesses an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions

in

Since

Moreover, for all

\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{I^{2}}K_{N}(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)&\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|=\bigg|\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)(\mathbb{P}_{N}u)(s)(\overline{\mathbb{P}_{N}v})(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\\
&\leq C_{K}\|(\mathbb{P}_{N}u)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|(\mathbb{P}_{N}v)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+D_{K}\left\lVert(\mathbb{P}_{N}u)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\left\lVert(\mathbb{P}_{N}v)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}},\end{split}

(5.21)

by hypothesis (
5.15
) since

.
For

,
\|(\mathbb{P}_{N}u)_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\left\lVert(\mathbb{P}_{N}u)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq c_{\nu}\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}

∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists

such that, with the notations of the proof of

5.6
, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

\left\lVert(\mathbb{P}_{N}u)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq c_{\nu}\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}

Proof.

For

, write

Thus

Now we use that the Sobolev norm

B.6

Hence, applying

B.14
and

B.13

\left\lVert(\mathbb{P}_{N}u)_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}^{2}\leq c_{\nu}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\left|\frac{c_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\right|^{2}\leq c_{\nu}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\left|\frac{c_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\right|^{2}\leq c_{\nu}^{2}\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}^{2},

(5.22)

which concludes the proof.
∎

6
Remainder estimates for the non-linear system

We prove estimates quantifying the discrepancy between the solution
\psi(t)
to the nonlinear system (
1.1
) and its Taylor approximations.
In
Section

6.1
, we start with straightforward classical energy estimates expressed in terms of
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.
Then, in
Section

\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
instead of
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.
Since this auxiliary system involves inhomogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, we derive general energy estimates for such systems in
Section

6.3
.
Thanks to this tool, we derive linear, quadratic and cubic estimates respectively in
Sections

6.4
,
6.5
and
6.6
.

We use the notation

of
Section

1.5
for constants independent of time and controls.

6.1
Classical estimates

The following estimates are straightforward and classical.
They are estimates in the strong spatial norm
H^{2}(0,1)
, but hence involve the strong time norm of the control
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
T\in(0,1]
and
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
such that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\|\psi-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}},

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|\psi-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|\psi-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

(6.4)

Proof.

By
Proposition

1.1
,
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}\leq C_{\mu}(T,\|u\|_{L^{2}})
, where
C_{\mu}
is a non-decreasing function of both of its arguments. This implies (
6.1
). Using the initial condition
\psi(0)=\varphi_{0}
, we have the Duhamel formula

\psi(t)-\varphi_{0}=i\int_{0}^{t}u(s)e^{-iA(t-s)}\mu\psi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(6.5)

which implies
\|\psi-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}
by
Lemma

B.2
.
Writing the Duhamel formula for
\psi-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}
and
\psi-\varphi_{0}-\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}
, one then iteratively obtains from
Lemma

B.2
the estimates (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) in a bootstrap manner.
∎

6.2
Auxiliary system

To obtain energy estimates involving
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
, we study an
auxiliary system
for a conjugate unknown
\widetilde{\psi}
, whose evolution equation only involves the primitive
u_{1}
(and not
u
).
This change of unknown is classical in this setting (see
[
2
, Section 7]
for the case of ODEs and
[
11
, Section 4.2]
for the Schrödinger PDE with Dirichlet boundary conditions).
However, here, the estimates are of different nature (compare
6.42
and
A.15
) and their proof is more subtle.

From now on
T>0
,
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
and
\psi
is the solution to (
1.1
).
We set

\widetilde{\psi}(t,x):=\psi(t,x)e^{-iu_{1}(t)\mu(x)}.

(6.6)

Then
\widetilde{\psi}\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{2}\cap\mathbb{S})\cap H^{1}((0,T);L^{2})
because so does
\psi
(see
Proposition

1.1
).
Since
\partial_{t}\widetilde{\psi}=(\partial_{t}\psi-iu\mu\psi)e^{-iu_{1}\mu}=i(\partial_{x}^{2}\psi)e^{-iu_{1}\mu}\in C^{0}([0,T];L^{2})
, one also has
\widetilde{\psi}\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{2})
.
Thus
\widetilde{\psi}
is a strong solution to the following auxiliary system

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\widetilde{\psi}=-\partial_{x}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}-iu_{1}(2\mu^{\prime}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\psi}+\mu^{\prime\prime}\widetilde{\psi})+(u_{1})^{2}(\mu^{\prime})^{2}\widetilde{\psi}\\
(\partial_{x}\widetilde{\psi}+iu_{1}\mu^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi})_{|x=0,1}=0\end{cases}

(6.7)

with initial data
\widetilde{\psi}(0)=\varphi_{0}
.
By
strong solution
, we mean that the first equation is an equality in
C^{0}([0,T];L^{2})
and the traces at
x=0
and
x=1
hold pointwise in time.

Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
be the solutions to (
1.17
), (
1.18
).
We set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\psi_{1}-iu_{1}\mu,

(6.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\psi_{2}-iu_{1}\mu\psi_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(u_{1})^{2}\mu^{2}.

(6.9)

which are the terms of order
1
and
2
in the power series expansion of
\widetilde{\psi}
with respect to the control
u
.
For the same reasons,
\widetilde{\psi}_{1},\widetilde{\psi}_{2}\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{2})\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2})
are strong solutions to the systems

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}=-\partial_{x}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}-iu_{1}\mu^{\prime\prime}\\
(\partial_{x}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}+iu_{1}\mu^{\prime})_{|x=0,1}=0\end{cases}

(6.10)

and

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\widetilde{\psi}_{2}=-\partial_{x}^{2}\widetilde{\psi}_{2}-iu_{1}(2\mu^{\prime}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}+\mu^{\prime\prime}\widetilde{\psi}_{1})+(u_{1})^{2}(\mu^{\prime})^{2}\\
(\partial_{x}\widetilde{\psi}_{2}+iu_{1}\mu^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}_{1})_{|x=0,1}=0\end{cases}

(6.11)

with initial conditions
\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(0)=\widetilde{\psi}_{2}(0)=0
.

Remainders.

Our goal is to prove energy estimates for the remainders between
\widetilde{\psi}
and its Taylor approximations.
Therefore, we set
R_{-1}=R_{0}:=\widetilde{\psi}
,
R_{1}:=\widetilde{\psi}-\varphi_{0}
,
R_{2}:=\widetilde{\psi}-\varphi_{0}-\widetilde{\psi}_{1}
and
R_{3}:=\widetilde{\psi}-\varphi_{0}-\widetilde{\psi}_{1}-\widetilde{\psi}_{2}
.
With these notations, the successive remainders
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
and
R_{3}

belong to
C^{0}([0,T];H^{2})\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2})
and are strong solutions to

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}R_{n}=-\partial_{x}^{2}R_{n}+F_{n}\\
(\partial_{x}R_{n}+iu_{1}\mu^{\prime}R_{n-1})_{|x=0,1}=0\end{cases}

(6.12)

with initial data
R_{n}(0)=0
and where

F_{n}:=-iu_{1}\left(2\mu^{\prime}\partial_{x}+\mu^{\prime\prime}\right)R_{n-1}+(u_{1})^{2}(\mu^{\prime})^{2}R_{n-2}.

(6.13)

6.3
Solutions with inhomogeneous Neumann conditions

We derive an
a priori
estimate in
L^{\infty}L^{2}
for solutions with inhomogeneous Neumann conditions.

Proposition 6.2

(A priori estimate)
.

Let
T\in(0,1]
,
h_{0},h_{1}\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,
f\in L^{1}((0,T);L^{2})
and
\phi\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{2})\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2})
.
Assume that
\phi
is a strong solution to

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\phi=-\partial_{x}^{2}\phi+f\\
\partial_{x}\phi(\cdot,0)=h_{0}\\
\partial_{x}\phi(\cdot,1)=h_{1}\end{cases}

(6.14)

with initial data
\phi(0)=0
.
Then

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}.

(6.15)

Proof.

Since
\phi
is a strong solution to (
6.14
), for
t\in[0,T]
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\langle\phi(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle&=i\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}^{2}\phi(t,x)\varphi_{j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-i\langle f(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle\\
&=i[\partial_{x}\phi(t)\varphi_{j}]_{0}^{1}-i[\phi(t)\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}]_{0}^{1}+i\int_{0}^{1}\phi(t,x)\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-i\langle f(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle\\
&=i\left(\varphi_{j}(1)h_{1}(t)-\varphi_{j}(0)h_{0}(t)\right)-i\lambda_{j}\langle\phi(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle-i\langle f(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle.\end{split}

(6.16)

By the Duhamel formula, since
\phi(0)=0
,

\langle\phi(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle=i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\lambda_{j}(t-s)}\left(\varphi_{j}(1)h_{1}(s)-\varphi_{j}(0)h_{0}(s)-\langle f(s),\varphi_{j}\rangle\right)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(6.17)

Using that
|\varphi_{j}|\leq\sqrt{2}
, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 6\sum_{k\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\lambda_{j}s}h_{k}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}+3\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\lambda_{j}s}\langle f(s),\varphi_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}.

(6.18)

The first two sums are bounded by Bessel’s inequality of
Lemma

B.1
.
Let
F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{iAs}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
.
We have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\lambda_{j}s}\langle f(s),\varphi_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\langle F(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}=\|F(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}^{2}

(6.19)

by triangular inequality since
e^{iAs}
is an isometry on
L^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C})
.
∎

Appendix A
Drift quantified by integer negative Sobolev norms

The goal of this section is to prove the following drift estimate, via the usual integration by parts approach.
The statement and its proof are adaptations to Neumann boundary conditions of
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
, written for Dirichlet boundary conditions in the bilinear Schrödinger equation.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we use the notation

H^{m}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{C}):=D(A^{\frac{m}{2}})=\left\{\phi\in H^{m}((0,1);\mathbb{C})\mid(\phi^{(2j+1)})_{|x=0,1}=0\text{ for }0<2j+1<m\right\}.

(A.1)

Extending the notation
u_{1}
for the primitive of
u
, we let
u_{n+1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}u_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
with
u_{0}=u
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\mu\in H^{2p+1}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
\langle\mu,\varphi_{k}\rangle=0
.
For
1\leq n\leq p
, let

a_{k}^{n}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}\lambda_{j}^{n-1}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})^{n-1}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}/2),

(A.2)

where
c_{j}
is defined by (
1.7
).
We assume
p
is the minimal integer for which
a_{k}^{p}\neq 0
.
There exists
T^{*},C>0
such that, for every
T\in(0,T^{*})
and
u\in L^{2}\cap H^{2p-3}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
with
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
,

\left|\langle\psi(T)-\varphi_{0},\varphi_{k}\rangle+ia_{k}^{p}\|u_{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\leq C\Gamma_{p}(T,u)\|u_{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\psi(T)-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(A.3)

where
\Gamma_{p}(T,u):=T+\|u_{1}^{(2p-2)}\|_{L^{2}}+T^{2-2p}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
.

As in
Remark

1.8
, estimate (
A.3
) prevents particular motions in
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{k}\}
, when
T
and
u
are small enough so that
\Gamma_{p}(T,u)<|a^{p}_{k}|
.
In the case
p=1
, we recover
Theorem

1.7
under the stronger assumption
\mu\in H^{3}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
, which allows a much simpler proof.

The assumption
\mu\in H^{2p+1}_{N}
implies that
\lambda_{j}^{p}\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle=\langle A^{p}\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle
.
An additional integration by parts justifies
\lambda_{j}^{p}\langle\mu,\varphi_{j}\rangle=o(1/j)
.
This estimate also holds with
\mu\leftarrow\mu\varphi_{k}\in H^{2p+1}_{N}
.
Thus

c_{j}=\underset{j\to\infty}{o}(1/\lambda_{j}^{2p+1})

(A.4)

and, for any
1\leq n\leq p
, the series
a_{k}^{n}
of (
A.2
) converges absolutely.

The following lemma gives an interpretation of
a_{k}^{n}
in terms of Lie brackets (with the convention
\operatorname{ad}_{A}(\mu)=[A,\mu]=A\mu-\mu A
).
We recover the usual drift direction of ODEs (see
[
6
, Theorem 6.1]
).

Lemma A.2
.

If
\mu\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
, then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

a_{k}^{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{2}\langle[\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{n-1}(\mu),\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{n}(\mu)]\varphi_{0},\varphi_{k}\rangle.

(A.5)

Proof.

The assumption
\mu\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
guarantees that the iterated Lie brackets are well defined:
A
is only applied to functions in
D(A)=H^{2}_{N}
.
For every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{m}(\mu)\varphi_{\ell}=\operatorname{ad}_{A-\lambda_{\ell}}^{m}(\mu)\varphi_{\ell}\\
=\sum_{q=0}^{m}(-1)^{q}\binom{m}{q}(A-\lambda_{\ell})^{m-q}\mu(A-\lambda_{\ell})^{q}\varphi_{\ell}=(A-\lambda_{\ell})^{m}(\mu\varphi_{\ell}).

(A.6)

Thus for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{\ell})^{m}\langle\varphi_{j},\mu\varphi_{\ell}\rangle=\langle(A-\lambda_{\ell})^{m}\varphi_{j},\mu\varphi_{\ell}\rangle=\langle\varphi_{j},(A-\lambda_{\ell})^{m}(\mu\varphi_{\ell})\rangle=\langle\varphi_{j},\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{m}(\mu)\varphi_{\ell}\rangle,

(A.7)

where the second equality results from integrations by parts and the boundary terms vanish because
\mu\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)
.
Therefore, using
2(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}/2)=\lambda_{j}+(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})
and Parseval’s identity, we obtain

2a_{k}^{n}=\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{n}(\mu)\varphi_{0},\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{n-1}(\mu)\varphi_{k}\rangle+\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{n-1}(\mu)\varphi_{0},\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{n}(\mu)\varphi_{k}\rangle

(A.8)

which concludes the proof using that

Appendix B

B.1
Bessel–Parseval estimates

We recall the following elementary inequality of Bessel–Parseval type.

Lemma B.1
.

For all
T>0
and
g\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{T}g(t)e^{i\lambda_{j}t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{2}\leq(1+T)\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(B.1)

Proof.

Let
N:=\lceil\pi T/2\rceil
and set
T_{0}:=2N/\pi
.
Then
T\leq T_{0}<T+1
.
Extend
g
by
0
to
(0,T_{0})
.
This keeps
\|g\|_{L^{2}}
and the integrals unchanged.
Consider the orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
L^{2}((0,T_{0});\mathbb{C})
defined by
\phi_{k}(t):=T_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{(2ik\pi t)/T_{0}}
.
Since
\lambda_{j}=(j\pi)^{2}
(see (
1.3
)),

\int_{0}^{T}g(t)e^{i\lambda_{j}t}\,\mathrm{d}t=T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle g,\phi_{j^{2}N}\rangle.

(B.2)

Therefore, by Bessel (or Parseval) for the orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{k}\}
,

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{T_{0}}g(t)e^{i\lambda_{j}t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{2}=T_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\langle g,\phi_{j^{2}N}\rangle|^{2}\leq T_{0}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle g,\phi_{k}\rangle|^{2}=T_{0}\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0})}^{2}.

(B.3)

Since
\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0})}=\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}
and
T_{0}\leq T+1
, this concludes the proof.
∎

B.2
Smoothing lemma

The proofs of the well-posedness of (
1.1
) stated in
Proposition

1.1
and of the estimates of
Lemma

6.1
rely on the following lemma, which is an adaptation to our Neumann boundary conditions of the smoothing statement
[
3
, Lemma 1]
for Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Lemma B.2
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
f\in L^{2}((0,T);H^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C}))
, the function
F:t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}e^{iAs}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
belongs to
C^{0}([0,T];H^{2}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{C}))
and moreover

\|F\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}_{N}}\leq C(1+T)\|f\|_{L^{2}H^{2}}.

(B.4)

Proof.

Although
e^{iAs}
preserves
H^{2}_{N}
, the difficulty is that
f(s)
is only assumed to be in
H^{2}
(not in
H^{2}_{N}
).
First, by standard arguments, recalling (
1.2
),

H^{2}_{N}((0,1);\mathbb{C})=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}((0,1);\mathbb{C});\enskip\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|j^{2}\langle\varphi,\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

(B.5)

Thus, it suffices to check that
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|j^{2}\langle F(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}
is finite and tends to
0
as
t\to 0
.

For
s\in[0,T]
and
j\geq 1
, integrating by parts,

\langle f(s),\varphi_{j}\rangle=\sqrt{2}\frac{(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}f(s,1)-\partial_{x}f(s,0)}{j^{2}\pi^{2}}-\frac{1}{j^{2}\pi^{2}}\langle\partial_{x}^{2}f(s),\varphi_{j}\rangle.

(B.6)

Thus, noting that
\frac{6}{\pi^{4}}\leq 1
and using
Lemma

B.1
for the first 2 terms,

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}&|j^{2}\langle F(t),\varphi_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|j^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\lambda_{j}s}\langle f(s),\varphi_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\lambda_{j}s}\partial_{x}f(s,1)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\lambda_{j}s}\partial_{x}f(s,0)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\lambda_{j}s}\langle\partial_{x}^{2}f(s),\varphi_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\|\partial_{x}f(\cdot,1)\|_{L^{2}(0,t)}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}f(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,t)}^{2}+t\|\partial_{x}^{2}f\|_{L^{2}((0,t);L^{2}(0,1))}^{2}\end{split}

(B.7)

which both yields the continuity and the claimed estimate.
∎

B.3
Integrating integral operators by parts

The following result is a minor generalization of the statement
[
15

in a sesquilinear setting
(u,v)
instead of the quadratic one
(u,u)
.
We use the notations of (
5.1
).

Lemma B.3
.

Let
T>0
and
K\in C^{0}([0,T]^{2};\mathbb{C})
.
Assume that
K\in H^{2}(\Delta_{-})
and

\begin{split}\int_{I^{2}}&K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{I}w(s)u_{1}(s)\bar{v}_{1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\Delta_{+}\cup\Delta_{-}}\partial_{21}K(s,t)u_{1}(s)\bar{v}_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\\

Proof.

As for

In particular,

First, integrating by parts in

\int_{I^{2}}K(s,t)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{I^{2}}\partial_{1}K(s,t)u_{1}(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t+u_{1}(T)\int_{I}K(T,t)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

\Delta_{+}

\begin{split}-\int_{s=0}^{T}u_{1}(s)\int_{t=s}^{T}&\partial_{1}K(s,t)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\Delta_{+}}\partial_{21}K(s,t)u_{1}(s)\bar{v}_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\\

(B.11)

Proceeding likewise in

and gathering all terms leads to the claimed equality.

An estimate for a principal value integral

Lemma B.5
.

For

and

\left|\int_{-M}^{+M}\frac{\phi(\omega)-\phi(0)}{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\right|\leq 2M|\phi^{\prime}(0)|+M\int_{-M}^{+M}|\phi^{\prime\prime}|\leq\int_{-M}^{M}|\phi^{\prime}|+2M\int_{-M}^{M}|\phi^{\prime\prime}|.

.
Writing
\langle\omega\rangle^{2\nu}\leq 1+|\omega-\omega_{0}|^{2\nu}+|\omega_{0}|^{2\nu}
and proceeding as in the previous case yields (
B.18
).
∎

Lemma B.7
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,1]
,

\left\lVert e^{i\omega_{0}t}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq C.

(B.22)

Proof.

Let
f_{T}(t):=e^{i\omega_{0}t}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}(t)
.
Its Fourier transform is
\widehat{f_{T}}(\omega)=\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}}(\omega-\omega_{0})
.
A direct computation shows that
\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}}(\sigma)=Th(T\sigma)
, where
h(\sigma):=i(e^{-i\sigma}-1)/\sigma
.
Thus,
\widehat{f_{T}}(\omega)=Th(T(\omega-\omega_{0}))
.
Using the definition of the Sobolev norm, and the change of variables
\sigma=T(\omega-\omega_{0})
, we write

\begin{split}\left\lVert f_{T}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}^{2}&=\frac{T^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(T(\omega-\omega_{0}))|^{2}\langle\omega\rangle^{2\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\\
&=\frac{T}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\sigma)|^{2}\langle\omega_{0}+\sigma/T\rangle^{2\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{split}

(B.23)

Using the inequality
\langle x+y\rangle^{2\nu}\leq 2^{\nu}(\langle x\rangle^{2\nu}+\langle y\rangle^{2\nu})
, we can bound the norm by the sum of two terms:

\left\lVert f_{T}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}^{2}\leq\frac{T2^{\nu}\langle\omega_{0}\rangle^{2\nu}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\sigma)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\frac{T2^{\nu}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\sigma)|^{2}\langle\sigma/T\rangle^{2\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

(B.24)

The first integral is a constant. Since
T\leq 1
, the first term is bounded.
For the second term, we write
\langle\sigma/T\rangle^{2\nu}=(1+\sigma^{2}/T^{2})^{\nu}=T^{-2\nu}(T^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{\nu}
. This gives

\frac{T2^{\nu}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\sigma)|^{2}\langle\sigma/T\rangle^{2\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\frac{T^{1-2\nu}2^{\nu}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\sigma)|^{2}(T^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

(B.25)

Since
T\in(0,1]
and
1-2\nu>0
, we have
T^{2}\leq 1
and
T^{1-2\nu}\leq 1
.
Therefore, this second term is bounded by

\frac{2^{\nu}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\sigma)|^{2}(1+\sigma^{2})^{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\frac{2^{\nu}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(\sigma)|^{2}\langle\sigma\rangle^{2\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

(B.26)

The function
h
is bounded near the origin and decays as
1/|\sigma|
for large
|\sigma|
. The integrand thus behaves like
|\sigma|^{2\nu-2}
for large
|\sigma|
. As
\nu<1/2
, the integral converges.
Both terms are bounded by a constant independent of
T
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma B.8
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists
C_{\nu}>0
such that, for all
T>0
and
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq C_{\nu}T^{\nu}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

(B.27)

Proof.

First, (
B.27
) holds for all
u\in\mathcal{H}
(i.e. such that
u_{1}(T)=0
).
Indeed, for such
u
, one has
\widehat{u}(\omega)=i\omega\widehat{u_{1}}(\omega)
, so
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-1}}\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
using
|\omega\langle\omega\rangle^{-1}|\leq 1
.
Moreover,
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{0}}=\|u\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq T\|u\|_{L^{2}}
, so (
B.27
) holds for
\nu=0
and
\nu=1
.
By Hölder’s inequality, for
\nu\in(0,1)
,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-1}}^{\nu}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{0}}^{1-\nu}

(B.28)

so (
B.27
) follows for all
u\in\mathcal{H}
and
\nu\in[0,1]
with
C_{\nu}=1
.

Let
\nu\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
.
For a general
u\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
, write
u=v+\frac{u_{1}(T)}{T}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}
where
v=P_{\mathcal{H}}u\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}+\frac{|u_{1}(T)|}{T}\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq T^{\nu}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+C_{\nu}\frac{|u_{1}(T)|}{T}T^{\frac{1}{2}+\nu}

(B.29)

using the first step and (
B.16
) (since
\nu<\frac{1}{2}
).

We conclude using
\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}
and
|u_{1}(T)|\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

Lemma B.9
.

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists
C_{\nu}>0
such that, for any segment
(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
,
\|\mathbbm{1}_{(a,b)}u\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\nu}\|u\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Proof.

It suffices to know that the restriction operator to a half-line acts continuously on
H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
for
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
(see
[
33
, Section 4.6.3, Theorem 1]
) and write
\mathbbm{1}_{(a,b)}=\mathbbm{1}_{(a,\infty)}\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,b)}
.
∎

Lemma B.10
.

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists
C_{\nu}>0
such that, for every
T>0
, and every control
u\in L^{2}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\left\lVert tu(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq C_{\nu}T\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}.

(B.30)

Proof.

For
T>0
and
u\in L^{2}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\nu}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
, let
\widetilde{u}
denote its extension by
0
to
\mathbb{R}
.
By definition (
1.28
),
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}=\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\left\lVert tu(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}=\|t\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}
.
First, for
\nu=0
, it is clear that
\|tu(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq T\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.
Second, for a given
\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we know that the
H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
norm is equivalent (up to a constant depending on
\nu
) to
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}+[\cdot]_{\nu}
where the Gagliardo seminorm is defined as

[f]_{\nu}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{1+2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{1+2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{0}^{T}|f(t)|^{2}A_{T}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

(B.31)

when
\operatorname{supp}f\subset(0,T)
, where
A_{T}(t):=t^{-2\nu}+(T-t)^{-2\nu}
.
Thus, we have

\begin{split}[tu(t)]_{\nu}^{2}&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|tu(t)-su(s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{1+2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{0}^{T}|tu(t)|^{2}A_{T}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{t^{2}|u(t)-u(s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{1+2\nu}}+\frac{|t-s|^{2}|u(s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{1+2\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{0}^{T}|tu(t)|^{2}A_{T}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq T^{2}[u]_{\nu}^{2}+2T^{2-2\nu}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

(B.32)

so we conclude using
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\nu 4^{-\nu}T^{2\nu}[f]_{\nu}^{2}
(which holds since
A_{T}(t)\geq(T/2)^{-2\nu}
).

Third, we proceed by duality for
u\in L^{2}\cap H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
with
\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
Then

\left\lVert tu(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}=\|t\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R})}=\underset{v\in H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}),\|v\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}=1}{\sup}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t\widetilde{u}(t)v(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(B.33)

For such
v\in H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
, let
w:=v\mathbbm{1}_{(0,T)}\in L^{2}(0,T)
.
By
Lemma

B.9
,
\|w\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\nu}\|v\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}
.
By the previous case
\|tw(t)\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}\leq TC_{\nu}\|w\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}
.
Thus
\left\lVert tu(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}\leq TC_{\nu}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\nu}}
.
∎

Lemma B.11
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
\rho\in C^{0,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
.
There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T\in(0,1]
,

|\mathcal{F}(\rho\mathbbm{1}_{[-T,T]})(\omega)|\leq C\langle\omega\rangle^{-\alpha}.

(B.34)

Proof.

For
T\in(0,1]
, let
\rho_{T}:=\rho\mathbbm{1}_{[-T,T]}
.
For
|\omega|\leq 1
, we just use
|\widehat{\rho_{T}}(\omega)|\leq 2T\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}
.
For
|\omega|\geq 1
, let
h:=(\operatorname{sgn}\omega)/|\omega|
.
A change of variables yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{T}(t+h)-\rho_{T}(t))e^{-i\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t=(e^{i\omega h}-1)\widehat{\rho_{T}}(\omega)=(e^{i}-1)\widehat{\rho_{T}}(\omega).

(B.35)

Since
|e^{i}-1|>0
, it suffices to bound the left-hand side.
If
-T+|h|<t<T-|h|
(an interval of length at most
2T
), we have

|\rho_{T}(t+h)-\rho_{T}(t)|=|\rho(t+h)-\rho(t)|\leq|h|^{\alpha}\|\rho\|_{C^{0,\alpha}([-1,1])}=|\omega|^{-\alpha}\|\rho\|_{C^{0,\alpha}([-1,1])}.

(B.36)

If
t<-T-|h|
or
t>T+|h|
, the integrand vanishes.
On the remaining intervals
[-T-|h|,-T+|h|]
and
[T-|h|,T+|h|]
, we use the crude bound
|\rho_{T}(t+h)-\rho_{T}(t)|\leq 2\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}
, but they are of length
2|h|=2|\omega|^{-1}\leq 2|\omega|^{-\alpha}
.
Gathering all bounds proves the claimed estimate.
∎

Lemma B.12
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
\rho\in C^{0,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
.
There exists
C>0
such that, for
T\in(0,1]
and
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
,

\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\rho(t-s)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\alpha/2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\alpha/2}}.

(B.37)

Proof.

For
T\in(0,1]
, let
\rho_{T}:=\rho\mathbbm{1}_{[-T,T]}
.
For
u,v\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
, keeping the same notation for their extension by
0
to
\mathbb{R}
, we have

I:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\rho(t-s)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{T}(t-s)u(s)\bar{v}(t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t=\langle\rho_{T}\star u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(B.38)

Thus, by Plancherel’s formula

I=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\rho_{T}}(\omega)\widehat{u}(\omega)\widehat{v}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\rho_{T}}(\omega)\langle\omega\rangle^{\alpha}\widehat{u}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{v}}(\omega)\langle\omega\rangle^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(B.39)

Thus, by (
1.28
) and Cauchy–Schwarz,

|I|\leq\left(\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\rho_{T}}(\omega)\langle\omega\rangle^{\alpha}|\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\alpha/2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{-\alpha/2}}.

(B.40)

Hence, the conclusion follows from
Lemma

B.11
.
∎

B.6
Spectral Sobolev norm

In this paragraph, we define a fractional Sobolev norm using spectral theory, which is used in the proof of
Proposition

5.6
, and equivalent to the norm (
1.28
) – defined by extending a function from
(0,T)
to
\mathbb{R}
by
0
, then using the usual fractional Sobolev norm on
\mathbb{R}
in Fourier space.

Let
T>0
.
Consider the Dirichlet Laplacian on
(0,T)
, its eigenvalues
\beta_{k}^{2}:=(k\pi/T)^{2}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and its basis of orthonormal eigenfunctions
e_{k}\in L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
.
For
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{C})
, set

N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}(u):=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}|\langle u,e_{k}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{where}\quad e_{k}(t):=\sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}\sin(\beta_{k}t).

(B.41)

Lemma B.13
.

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists
C_{\nu}>0
such that, for
T>0
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
,

N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}(u)\leq C_{\nu}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}.

(B.42)

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.
By definition,

\begin{split}N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}^{2}(u)&=\frac{2}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\left|\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\sin(\beta_{k}t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\left(|\widehat{u}(-\beta_{k})|^{2}+|\widehat{u}(\beta_{k})|^{2}\right),\end{split}

(B.43)

since the coefficients are related to the Fourier transform
\widehat{u}(\omega):=\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{-i\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t
by the identity

\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\sin(\beta_{k}t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\widehat{u}(-\beta_{k})-\widehat{u}(\beta_{k})}{2i}.

(B.44)

Let
I_{k}:=[\frac{k\pi}{T},\frac{(k+1)\pi}{T})
for
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
Since the length of each interval
I_{k}
is
\pi/T
, we can write
\frac{1}{T}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{I_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega
.
Moreover, for
\omega\in I_{k}
,
\beta_{k}\leq\omega\leq 2\beta_{k}
and thus there exists
C_{\nu}>0
such that
\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\leq C_{\nu}\langle\omega\rangle^{2\nu}
.
By the triangular inequality,

\begin{split}N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}^{2}(u)&\leq\frac{2C_{\nu}}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{I_{k}}\langle\omega\rangle^{2\nu}(|\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}+|\widehat{u}(-\omega)|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\omega\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\int_{I_{k}}\left(|\widehat{u}(-\beta_{k})-\widehat{u}(-\omega)|^{2}+|\widehat{u}(\beta_{k})-\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\omega.\end{split}

(B.45)

The first term is bounded above by
4C_{\nu}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}^{2}
.
Let us focus on bounding the remainder involving positive frequencies.
We write using Cauchy–Schwarz,

|\widehat{u}(\beta_{k})-\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}=\left|\int_{\beta_{k}}^{\omega}\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}(\omega^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{\pi}{T}\int_{I_{k}}|\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}(\zeta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

(B.46)

Thus

\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\int_{I_{k}}|\widehat{u}(\beta_{k})-\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq C_{\nu}\frac{\pi^{2}}{T^{2}}\int_{I_{k}}\langle\zeta\rangle^{2\nu}|\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}(\zeta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

(B.47)

Hence

\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\beta_{k}\rangle^{2\nu}\int_{I_{k}}\left(|\widehat{u}(-\beta_{k})-\widehat{u}(-\omega)|^{2}+|\widehat{u}(\beta_{k})-\widehat{u}(\omega)|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq\frac{2C_{\nu}\pi}{T^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\zeta\rangle^{2\nu}|\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}(\zeta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

(B.48)

Since
\partial_{\omega}\widehat{u}(\omega)=\mathcal{F}[-itu(t)](\omega)
, we have

N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}^{2}(u)\leq 4C_{\nu}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}^{2}+\frac{4C_{\nu}\pi^{2}}{T^{2}}\left\lVert tu(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}^{2}.

(B.49)

By
Lemma

B.10
, for any
T>0
, we have
\left\lVert tu(t)\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq C_{\nu}T\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma B.14
.

Let
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
There exists
C_{\nu}>0
such that, for
T>0
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{R})

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}\leq C_{\nu}N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}(u).

(B.50)

Proof.

Let
T>0
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\widetilde{u}
denote its extension by
0
to
\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}=\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}=\underset{v\in H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R}),\|v\|_{H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R})}=1}{\sup}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{u}v.

(B.51)

For such
v\in H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R})
, let
w:=v\mathbbm{1}_{(0,T)}\in L^{2}(0,T)
.
By
Lemma

B.9
,
\|w\|_{H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\nu}\|v\|_{H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R})}
.
Moreover,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{u}v\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{T}uw\right|=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle u,e_{k}\rangle\langle w,e_{k}\rangle\right|\leq N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}(u)N_{\mathrm{sp},-\nu}(w).

(B.52)

By
Lemma

B.13
,
N_{\mathrm{sp},-\nu}(w)\leq C_{\nu}\|w\|_{H^{-\nu}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\nu}^{2}
, which proves the claimed estimate.
∎

Remark B.15
.

For
\nu=0
and
\nu=1
, by Plancherel and Parseval, one has
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\widetilde{H}^{\nu}}=N_{\mathrm{sp},\nu}
for any
T>0
.
Unfortunately, we have not been able to use this fact prove the equivalence of both norms for
\nu\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
with a constant independent of
T
(at least as
T\to 0
).
It is known that these norms cease to be exactly equal for
\nu\in(0,1)
(see e.g.
[
13
, Example 4.15]
for
T=1
).

Abstract
Wavenumber-division multiplexing (WDM) was introduced as a counterpart of orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing in the spatial-frequency domain for line-of-sight holographic multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems. In this paper, we extend WDM to holographic MIMO channels with non-line-of-sight (NLoS) propagation. We show that applying WDM to the NLoS channel yields the corresponding angular-domain representation, which we characterize through the power spectral factor and power spectral density. We further obtain a closed-form characterization for the case of isotropic scattering, recovering Jakes’ isotropic model. The analysis is complemented by numerical results evaluating the degrees of freedom and ergodic capacity under both isotropic and non-isotropic scattering.

I
Introduction

Wireless data demand is growing at an unprecedented pace, pushing communications systems toward higher frequencies where extremely dense deployments of physically small antennas become feasible
[
1
]
. This trend has sparked strong interest in holographic
MIMO
(
MIMO
) technologies as a means to support future high-capacity networks and intelligent applications. Unlike conventional massive
MIMO
, where antenna elements are typically spaced about half a wavelength apart to limit spatial correlation
[
14
]
, holographic
MIMO
envisions continuous apertures (i.e., surfaces or lines) or densely packed antenna arrays, which become electrically large. In this regime, near-field propagation becomes dominant, in contrast to classical massive
MIMO
that generally operates under far-field conditions
[
12
]
. Holographic
MIMO
is often realized using programmable metamaterials
[
4
]
, enabling flexible hardware architectures and multifunctional operation across time, frequency, and space.

Continuous-aperture
MIMO
models arise either from the spatial sampling of the transmit and receive apertures, or from expanding the transmit current and received field using suitable basis functions. The latter approach yields the
WDM
(
WDM
) framework, introduced in
[
11
]
and inspired by
OFDM
(
OFDM
): while
OFDM
operates in the frequency domain,
WDM
enables multiplexing in the wavenumber (spatial-frequency) domain through an orthogonal decomposition of the continuous current and field. Prior
WDM
studies build on a holographic line model under purely
LoS
(
LoS
) propagation
[
11
,
2
]
, whereas
NLoS
(
NLoS
) propagation has been explored only for spatial-sampling-based frameworks
[
9
,
7
]
. Although millimeter-wave and sub-terahertz systems are often
LoS
-dominated, small-scale fading remains significant, so
NLoS
components cannot be entirely neglected.

In this paper, we extend the
WDM
framework using the
NLoS
holographic line model introduced in
[
10
]
and show that applying
WDM
to the
NLoS
channel yields the corresponding angular-domain representation. By analyzing the spatial autocorrelation of the
WDM
-based
NLoS
channel, we establish a relation between the
PSF
(
PSF
) and
PSD
(
PSD
), enabling an explicit characterization of the resulting angular-domain channel. We further derive closed-form expressions of the
ACF
(
ACF
) and

under isotropic scattering, which allows us to retrieve the classical Jakes’ isotropic model. The results demonstrate that
WDM
serves not only as a multiplexing technique but also as a powerful analytical tool for investigating holographic
MIMO

(

WDM

are the transmit and receive variances, respectively. Therefore, employing (
19
)–(
20
), we express the spatial autocorrelation as

\displaystyle R_{mn,qp}=L_{\textrm{s}}L_{\textrm{r}}\sigma_{\textrm{r}}^{2}(n)\sigma_{\textrm{s}}^{2}(m)\delta[n-p]\delta[m-q],

(31)

which can be written in matrix form as

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}=\mathrm{diag}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{s}}\odot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{s}})\otimes\mathrm{diag}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{r}}\odot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{r}}),

(32)

where
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{s}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{n_{\textrm{s}}}_{+}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{r}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{n_{\textrm{r}}}_{+}
collect the scaled standard deviations
\left\{\sqrt{L_{\textrm{s}}}\sigma_{\textrm{s}}(m):m\in\mathcal{E}_{\textrm{s}}\right\}
and
\left\{\sqrt{L_{\textrm{r}}}\sigma_{\textrm{r}}(n):n\in\mathcal{E}_{\textrm{r}}\right\}
, respectively. Finally, comparing (
24
) with (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{\textrm{s}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{s}}\odot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{s}}),

(33a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{\textrm{r}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{r}}\odot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\textrm{r}}).

(33b)

With (
33
), the
WDM
-based
NLoS
channel matrix can be obtained as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}={\mathbf{R}}_{\textrm{r}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{W}{\mathbf{R}}_{\textrm{s}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{\textrm{r}}\times n_{\textrm{s}}},

(34)

where
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{\textrm{r}}\times n_{\textrm{s}}}
is a random matrix with
i.i.d.
(
i.i.d.
)
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,1)
entries.

III-C

Characterization of the Angular-Domain NLoS Channel

Characterizing
H_{\textrm{a}}(n,m)
is essential for deriving expressions for the
WDM
-based
NLoS
channel matrix. Since we have
H_{\textrm{a}}(n,m)\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\sigma^{2}(n,m))
from (
9
), the following analysis focuses on characterizing
\sigma^{2}(n,m)
.
Equation (
7
) represents the Fourier series expansion of
h(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s})
under the assumption that the normalized lengths at the source and receiver are large, i.e.,
\frac{L_{\textrm{s}}}{\lambda}\gg 1
and
\frac{L_{\textrm{r}}}{\lambda}\gg 1
. In this setting,
\sigma^{2}(n,m)
is obtained as the sampled value of the
PSD
at the point
\big(\frac{2\pi}{L_{\textrm{r}}}n,\frac{2\pi}{L_{\textrm{s}}}m\big)
in the wavenumber domain
[
13
, Eq. (177)]
. Since separable scattering implies
\sigma^{2}(n,m)=\sigma_{\textrm{s}}^{2}(m)\sigma_{\textrm{r}}^{2}(n)
(see Section
III-B
), we focus on modeling the variance at the receiver, as the procedure for the source follows analogously.

The variance at the receiver after performing a change of variable similar to (
27
) and utilizing (
30
) is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\textrm{r}}^{2}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\textrm{r}}(n)}A^{2}_{\textrm{r}}(\theta_{\textrm{r}})\mathrm{d}\theta_{\textrm{r}},

(35)

with
\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{r}}(n)=\big\{\arccos\big({\frac{\lambda}{L_{\textrm{r}}}(n+1)}\big),\arccos\big({\frac{\lambda}{L_{\textrm{r}}}n}\big)\big\}
; the detailed steps are presented in
[
10
]
. The
PSF
is determined by
\tilde{A}_{\textrm{r}}^{2}(\theta_{\textrm{r}})=\frac{{A}_{\textrm{r}}^{2}(\theta_{\textrm{r}})}{2\pi}
, which is modeled as a mixture of
2D
(
2D
)
vMF
(
vMF
) distributions
[
9
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{\textrm{r}}^{2}(\theta_{\textrm{r}})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{C}w_{\ell}p_{\ell}(\theta_{\textrm{r}}),

(36)

where
C
denotes the number of scattering clusters,
p_{\ell}(\theta_{\textrm{r}})
is the
2D

vMF
distribution for the
\ell
-th cluster, and the positive weights are such that
\sum_{\ell}w_{\ell}=1
. Let
I_{0}(\cdot)
and
I_{1}(\cdot)
denote the modified Bessel functions of the first kind and order zero and one, respectively. The
2D

vMF
distribution is defined as
[
5
]

\displaystyle\hskip-7.11317ptp_{\ell}(\theta_{\textrm{r}})\!=\!\frac{1}{2\pi I_{0}(\alpha_{\ell})}\exp\big(\alpha_{\ell}\cos{(\theta_{\textrm{r}}\!-\!\bar{\theta}_{\textrm{r},\ell}})\big),\ \theta_{\textrm{r}}\in[-\pi,\pi),

(37)

where
\alpha_{\ell}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{+}
is the concentration parameter and
\bar{\theta}_{\textrm{r},\ell}\in[-\pi,\pi)
is the mean angle of the
\ell
-th cluster. For a given normalized circular variance
\nu_{\ell}^{2}\in[0,1]
, we compute
\alpha_{\ell}
using the fixed-point equation
\nu_{\ell}^{2}=1-\big(\frac{I_{1}(\alpha_{\ell})}{I_{0}(\alpha_{\ell})}\big)^{2}
.

We now consider the case of isotropic scattering, obtained by setting
C=1
with
\alpha_{1}=0
, which implies
\nu_{1}^{2}=1
. Considering only the forward-traveling wave, i.e.,
\theta_{\textrm{r}}\in[0,\pi)
, and using (
36
)–(
37
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{\textrm{r}}^{2}(\theta_{\textrm{r}})=\frac{1}{\pi},\ \theta_{\textrm{r}}\in[0,\pi).

(38)

In this case, since (
38
) no longer depends on
\bar{\theta}_{\textrm{r}}
, (
27
) can be expressed as
\Gamma_{\textrm{r}}(r_{x})={J_{0}(kr_{x})}
, where
J_{0}(\cdot)
is the Bessel function of the first kind and order zero, and the corresponding
PSD
is given by
S_{\textrm{r}}(k_{x})=\frac{2}{\sqrt{k^{2}-k_{x}^{2}}}
,
|k_{x}|\leq k.

Hence, under isotropic scattering, the
PSD
and
ACF
coincide with the classical Jakes’ isotropic model.

V
Conclusions

We analyzed the
WDM
-based
NLoS
channel in holographic
MIMO
by characterizing its spatial autocorrelation. In particular, we showed that applying
WDM
to the
NLoS
channel leads to its angular-domain representation. Furthermore, particularizing the analysis to isotropic scattering recovers Jakes’ isotropic model. The results highlight that
WDM
serves not only as a multiplexing technique but also as an effective analytical tool for studying holographic
MIMO
channels. Future work may explore extensions to the multi-user setting.

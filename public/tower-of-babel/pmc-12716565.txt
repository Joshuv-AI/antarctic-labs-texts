Clinical effectiveness of biofeedback for treating stress urinary incontinence in women: protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Shirley Zhaoxue Liu, Gladys Lai-Ying Cheing, Priya Kannan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPelvic floor muscle (PFM) training (PFMT) is a standard conservative first-line treatment for stress urinary incontinence (SUI) in women. However, the success of PFMT alone, without adjunctive therapies, has been hindered by the lack of adherence among women, who report the inability to contract the correct PFMs and limited motivation to perform PFMT exercises. Therefore, PFMT is often combined with adjunctive therapies, such as biofeedback (BF). The conventional BF device employed in clinical practice is invasive and involves the insertion of an intravaginal probe, causing pain or discomfort for women. Therefore, our team developed the PelviSense, a non-invasive BF device for use as a PFMT adjunct. The proposed randomised controlled trial (RCT) aims to (1) investigate the efficacy of PelviSense-assisted PFMT compared with unassisted PFMT in reducing SUI in women, and (2) identify the mechanisms underlying the beneficial effects of PelviSense-assisted PFMT for the treatment of SUI.Methods and analysisA prospective, two-arm, parallel-group RCT will be conducted. It will involve 132 women aged 18–60 years with SUI or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence, who will be randomised into PelviSense-assisted (n=66) or unassisted (n=66) PFMT groups using a computer-generated randomisation schedule. Supervised PelviSense-assisted and unassisted PFMT will be provided on a one-to-one basis for 30 min, three times per week, for 4 weeks. Following the supervised training period, participants will perform the exercises at home unsupervised for 24 weeks, with or without the PelviSense device, according to their assigned group. The primary outcome will be the severity of urine loss, measured using the 1-hour pad test. The secondary outcomes will be the severity of SUI symptoms, measured using the International Consultation on Incontinence Questionnaire-Short Form, and quality of life, measured using the Incontinence Impact Questionnaire-Short Form. The mediating outcomes will include PFM strength, measured using the Modified Oxford scale, as well as levator hiatus area and bladder neck position, measured by transperineal ultrasound. The outcomes will be assessed at baseline (week 0), postintervention (week 4) and at follow-up (week 28).Ethics and disseminationThis RCT has received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (approval number: HSEARS20211104002-02). Its findings will be disseminated to participants via written feedback on PFMT performance using the PelviSense device, to the general public via public lectures, to healthcare professionals via interactive workshops and to researchers via articles in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at national and international conferences.Trial registration numberNCT06126757.

Introduction

Urinary incontinence (UI) is defined as a problem of involuntary loss of urine and is very common among women, particularly after pregnancy and childbirth.1 Stress UI (SUI), the most common subtype of UI, is characterised by an involuntary loss of urine due to an increase in intra-abdominal pressure on exertion (eg, exercising or lifting heavy objects), sneezing or coughing.2 The prevalence of SUI among women increases with age, with rates ranging from 20% to 50%.3 The prevalence of SUI was reported to be 24.5% among women in China in 2024,4 37.5% among women in the USA in 20225 and 46% among Chinese women living in Hong Kong in 2023.6

Despite its high prevalence, only about 25% of affected women seek medical care, and 12% receive specialised treatments.3 SUI imposes a substantial economic burden on both healthcare systems and affected individuals.7 Beyond its economic impact, SUI profoundly affects the quality of life (QoL) of affected women, influencing familial, occupational, sexual, physical, psychological and social dimensions.8 SUI is also strongly associated with increased levels of anxiety, depression and feelings of shame, as well as a loss of self-respect and dignity, further contributing to a decline in overall well-being and work productivity.8

Treatment options for SUI include both conservative therapies and surgical interventions.9 Conservative therapies for SUI include behavioural modifications, pharmaceutical interventions, hormone replacement therapy and physical therapy.10 As a form of physical therapy, pelvic floor muscle (PFM) training (PFMT) is recognised as the first-line treatment for SUI, highlighting its effectiveness in alleviating SUI, as well as its cost-effectiveness and safety.11 However, the effectiveness of PFMT alone is often hindered by poor adherence, which is primarily due to the difficulty in isolating the correct PFM contractions and maintaining motivation.12 Therefore, PFMT is often combined with biofeedback (BF) via an intravaginal probe for women to learn how to contract the PFM and modulate the contractions.13 While this approach also aims to maintain their motivation to exercise,14 conventional BF via an intravaginal probe is often disfavoured by women because it is invasive and causes pain and discomfort.15 Therefore, developing a non-invasive and comfortable device for adjunctive PFMT therapy remains crucial.

To address the need for a non-invasive BF device that avoids the discomfort associated with vaginal probe insertion, our team developed the PelviSense non-invasive BF device. A detailed description of the PelviSense device and its preliminary effectiveness, feasibility and safety for SUI in women has been previously reported.16 The results of our previous pilot studies evaluating the preliminary effectiveness of the PelviSense device demonstrated that PFMT performed with its assistance was superior to PFMT performed either alone or with assistance from a vaginal probe-based BF device in improving continence, reducing the severity of urine loss and increasing PFM strength among women with SUI.16

Recent systematic reviews have reported that the efficacy of PFMT improves when combined with adjunctive therapies such as BF for women with SUI or pelvic floor dysfunction.15 17 To date, evidence from high-quality studies on how BF-assisted PFMT provides additional benefits remains limited. A recent randomised controlled trial (RCT) reported that the superior efficacy of BF-assisted PFMT is associated with significant improvements in PFM strength, stating that BF provides the real-time status of PFM activity, accurately recognising both improper performance and correct PFM contraction during PFMT.18 However, the full spectrum of its underlying physiological mechanisms remains under investigation.

The RCT proposed here will address this knowledge gap by identifying the mechanisms underlying the beneficial effects of BF-assisted PFMT for treating SUI using transperineal ultrasound (TPU). It aims to (1) evaluate the effectiveness of PelviSense-assisted compared with unassisted PFMT in reducing the severity of urine loss among women with SUI and (2) identify the physiological mechanism(s) underlying the effectiveness of PelviSense-assisted PFMT in women with SUI. The proposed RCT will test two hypotheses: (1) women assigned to the PelviSense-assisted PFMT group will report a greater reduction in the severity of urine loss than women assigned to the unassisted PFMT group, and (2) improvements in the severity of urine loss will be mediated by increased PFM strength, causing a decrease in bladder neck mobility and a reduction in levator hiatus (LH) area.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The RCT protocol is reported according to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials 2025 checklist.19 This prospective, single-centre, single-blind, two-arm, parallel-group RCT will be conducted in the Department of Rehabilitation Sciences at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (HK PolyU). It will include an eligibility screening phase, preintervention assessment (time point 1 (T1)), supervised PFMT (unassisted and PelviSense-assisted) for 4 weeks, postintervention assessments after 4 weeks (time point 2 (T2)), unsupervised PFMT exercises at home for 24 weeks and follow-up assessments at 28 weeks (time point 3 (T3)). Eligibility criteria

This RCT will include non-pregnant women aged 18–60 years who experience mild-to-moderate SUI, as indicated by a pad weight gain of 1–50 g during the 1-hour pad test.20 This RCT will exclude women with (1) severe pelvic organ prolapse (stages 3 and 4 according to the Baden-Walker Halfway Scoring System),21 (2) complicated UI due to pelvic radiation, (3) severe psychological problems that would impair study participation and (4) urge-predominant mixed UI or urge UI.

Interventions

Participants in both the PelviSense-assisted (intervention) and unassisted (control) PFMT groups will undergo a 28-week PFMT programme, consisting of a 4-week supervised PFMT exercise phase followed by a 24-week unsupervised home PFMT exercise phase. Both groups will receive an instruction booklet to guide their home PFMT exercises. Additional details of the intervention are provided in table 1.

Intervention group

The intervention group will perform PFMT with the assistance of the wearable PelviSense BF device. The participant attaches the sensor to the perineal region and receives real-time visual feedback of their PFM contractions via a mobile app, ensuring correct exercise performance.

Control group

The control group will perform the same PFMT exercise regimen, but without the assistance of the PelviSense or any other BF device.

Outcomes

Outcomes will be assessed at baseline (T1), immediately after the intervention (T2) and at follow-up (T3). The participants’ age, body mass index (BMI), parity status and incontinence status will be collected as descriptive measures at baseline.

Primary outcome measure

Severity of urine loss

The 1-hour pad test will be used to quantify the severity of urine loss according to a protocol proposed by the International Continence Society (ICS).22 For the 1-hour pad test, the participant will be instructed to empty their bladder before wearing the preweighed pads, after which they will be asked to drink 500 mL of water during the first 15 min. They will then walk for 30 min, which will include climbing up and down one flight of stairs. In the last 15 min, they will perform a set of activities: standing up from sitting (10 times), coughing vigorously (10 times), running on the spot (1 min), bending to pick up an object from the floor (5 times) and washing their hands for 1 min under running water.20 Then, the pad will be weighed after 1 hour to quantify urine loss (in grams).

Secondary outcome measures

Quality of life

To assess the impact of UI on QoL, participants will complete the disease-specific seven-item Incontinence Impact Questionnaire-Short Form (IIQ-7).23 The IIQ-7 has been reported to correlate well with the 30-item long form of the IIQ.24 25 The items in the IIQ-7 are scored on a 4-point scale from 0 (not at all) to 3 (greatly), and these raw scores can be transformed to obtain a score ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating worse QoL.26 The IIQ-7 has been reported to have high internal consistency (Cronbach’s α=0.80) and test–retest reliability (Cronbach’s α=0.75).25 The Chinese version of the IIQ-7 has been reported to be highly valid with excellent test–retest reliability in the Chinese population.27

SUI symptom severity

To assess the severity of SUI symptoms, participants will complete the disease-specific six-item International Consultation on Incontinence Questionnaire-Short Form (ICIQ-SF).28 The ICIQ-SF is scored out of 21 points, with higher scores indicating more severe symptoms. The ICIQ-SF is easy to complete and has demonstrated good construct validity, acceptable convergent validity and good test–retest reliability (Cronbach’s α=0.95).29 The Chinese version of the ICIQ-SF has also demonstrated adequate content validity, internal consistency and test–retest reliability, and it is recommended as a useful tool in clinical practice and research.30

Mediating variable measures

PFM strength

PFM strength will be manually graded by vaginal palpation using the valid and reliable Modified Oxford Scale (MOS)31 while participants are in a crooked lying position (reclined, knees bent, feet flat on the floor and arms by the side).32 The MOS grades PFM strength on a 6-point scale: 0=no contraction, 1=flicker, 2=weak, 3=moderate, 4=good and 5=strong.

Bladder neck mobility

The bladder neck position will be determined objectively using the valid and reliable TPU method with a Consona N9 diagnostic ultrasound system (Mindray, Shenzhen, China).33 The ultrasound probe will be placed vertically in the midsagittal plane at the suprapubic region. Bladder neck mobility in the ventral–cranial direction relative to the resting position will be measured by TPU during a maximal voluntary contraction and the Valsalva manoeuvre, and displacement will be measured (in millimetres). Before the TPU measurements, the examiner will ask the participant to perform preliminary PFM contractions to ensure the correct contraction of the PFMs and to perform the Valsalva manoeuvre.

LH area

The LH area is situated at a depth of 2–4 cm from the perineum, which is well within the effective range of 4–8 MHz ultrasound transducers, such as those routinely used for four-dimensional TPU imaging.34 The LH area will be measured using the Consona N9 diagnostic ultrasound system (Mindray, Shenzhen, China) with a 2–8 MHz, curved-array, four-dimensional ultrasound transducer. Notably, four-dimensional ultrasound has been reported to be a reliable tool for measuring the LH area (intraclass correlation coefficient=0.92).35 The LH area will be measured in the midsagittal plane during a Valsalva manoeuvre, at rest and during maximal pelvic floor contraction in the supine position.34 Care will be taken to avoid co-contraction of the PFM during the Valsalva manoeuvre.

Study procedure and baseline assessment

Potential participants will be invited to an in-person visit at HK PolyU. On the first day of contact, the potential participants will complete a screening questionnaire and a 1-hour pad test to determine eligibility. Those deemed eligible will be asked to provide written informed consent, and their BMI will be determined. The written informed consent form is provided in online supplemental material 1. In addition to the 1-hour pad test assessment, the participants will complete the ICIQ-SF and IIQ-7, and be assessed using the MOS, to establish their baseline severity of urine loss, severity of SUI symptoms, QoL and PFM strength, respectively. They will also undergo TPU to determine the baseline bladder neck position and LH area during the Valsalva manoeuvre, at rest and maximal contraction states. Following the baseline assessments, participants will select an envelope containing treatment allocation details. Sample size

The sample size required to reliably test the hypotheses was determined using G*Power software36 (V.3.1.9.2) based on an F-test, considering 80% power, a 5% significance level and a 20% attrition rate. For Hypothesis 1, based on the estimated difference in urine loss—as measured by the 1-hour pad test—between the PelviSense-assisted and unassisted PFMT groups in our pilot study16 (d=0.30), the sample size required to detect a difference in the severity of urine loss was estimated to be 116 (58 per group). For Hypothesis 2, based on the estimated difference in PFM strength—as measured by the MOS—between the PelviSense-assisted and unassisted PFMT groups in our pilot study16 (d=0.27), the sample size required to detect significant mediation was 132 (66 per group). Therefore, this RCT will recruit 132 participants to ensure adequate power to test both hypotheses.

Recruitment

Participants will be recruited using a non-probability convenience sampling method through physical and online advertising in public and university campus spaces. This recruitment method is advantageous in terms of time, cost and logistics, as it requires little planning and less involvement from research personnel compared with other sampling methods.37 Physical advertising will consist of flyers posted around the university campus, in the waiting rooms of university health services and physiotherapy clinics, community churches and university-owned amenities, including sports centres and gyms. As social media is emerging as a promising mechanism for identifying and recruiting potential participants for clinical trials,38 online advertisements will be posted on social media sites, including Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn.

Randomisation and blinding

An individual who will not be involved in study recruitment will randomly assign participants to the intervention or control group at a 1:1 ratio. Another individual will assign treatments according to a computer-generated random schedule in permuted blocks of two within age strata. The allocation sequence will be concealed using sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes containing the group name and a personal identification number. During the informed consent process and before outcome assessments, all participants will be explicitly instructed not to discuss or disclose the specific treatments they are receiving. Training will also be provided to the assessor on using standardised scripts to guide conversations and manage situations when a participant inadvertently discloses such information.

To eliminate expectation effects and biases, a research assistant will complete the outcome assessments. All assessments will be conducted in a closed space separate from where the interventions are delivered to participants. Assessment appointments will also be scheduled at different times from the intervention/training sessions. A physiotherapy postgraduate research student will conduct the supervised training at HK PolyU. A student assistant will double-enter the raw data into a spreadsheet. All data analyses will be conducted blinded, with code names (Groups A and B) assigned to each group. Group identities will be revealed only after the analyses are complete.

Data management, processing and anticipated analysis

The participants will record all demographic data on paper forms, which the research team will then collect. Participants will be identified only by their assigned personal identification number. All participant data will be stored in a password-protected spreadsheet accessible only to research team members. The TPU data will be exported from the ultrasound system and saved on a password-secured network drive.

Analyses will be performed on an intention-to-treat basis. Missing values for all study outcomes will be imputed using the multiple imputation method, which is considered appropriate for data missing at random or completely at random.39

Hypothesis 1

Treatment effects on both primary and secondary outcomes between T1, T2 and T3 and across the intervention groups will be evaluated using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA). ANCOVA is a valuable method that combines the advantages of two widely established procedures: analysis of variance and multiple linear regression.40 The use of ANCOVA may considerably reduce the number of subjects required compared with other statistical methods to attain the required precision and power.40 ANCOVA is also more effective than other statistical methods in reducing the influence of baseline confounding factors on the results.41 Baseline variables, including age, BMI and parity status, will be considered as covariates. Outcomes at T1 and T2 will be compared with assess the immediate treatment effects, and those at T1 and T3 will be compared with evaluate the retention of treatment effects. Hypothesis 1 will be supported if significant group×time interactions emerge, and subsequent univariate analyses reveal significantly (p<0.05) better improvements in outcomes in the intervention group compared with the control group.

Hypothesis 2

The expected physiological changes are anticipated to lead to better functional outcomes, specifically a reduction in the severity of urine loss, in the intervention group compared with the control group. Mediation analyses will be conducted using the PROCESS macro (V.2.13) for SPSS Statistics (V.31, IBM, Armonk, New York, USA) with Model 4, which tests for simple mediation (ie, indirect effects).42 To accommodate irregularities in sampling distribution, we will include 5000 bootstrapping with a 95% CI to achieve a higher power.43 The indirect effects of PFM strength on the severity of urine loss will be calculated as the product of the regression coefficient of PFM strength on bladder neck mobility and LH area and the regression coefficient of bladder neck mobility and LH area on the severity of urine loss. The significance of the indirect effect will be assessed using the macro-generated, bias-corrected, bootstrapped 95% CI.44 Group differences in the indirect effect will be assessed using the Wald test. Hypothesis 2 will be supported if the 95% CI for the indirect effect does not include zero (ie, zero does not fall between the upper and lower limits of the 95% CI).44

Safety and adverse events

BF is widely regarded as a safe procedure with no absolute contraindications and is suitable for individuals of all ages.45 The safety of the PelviSense device was also assessed in our pilot study,16 and no participants experienced any adverse events while using it. To prevent any potential adverse effects from the intervention, participants with contraindications to BF (eg, individuals unable to comprehend and follow commands or those with a pacemaker) will be carefully screened out during recruitment using the screening questionnaire. The safety of all participants will be closely monitored throughout the RCT. All reported adverse effects associated with the intervention will be recorded and documented.

Retention plan

To maximise retention, we will (1) obtain contact information for all participants and for any of their friends and relatives who may know their whereabouts, (2) include appointments for data collection in the study schedule, (3) schedule supervised exercises at convenient times for the participants and (4) provide a PelviSense device to each woman in the control (unassisted PFMT) group on study completion.

Patient and public involvement

The patients and/or the public will not be involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this RCT. The results of this RCT will be communicated to the participants and the general public through our website and social media accounts.

Trial status

Recruitment for this RCT began in September 2024 and was completed in July 2025. Data collection is currently ongoing.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this RCT will be the first to evaluate the effectiveness of a non-invasive BF device (PelviSense) in reducing the severity of urine loss, severity of SUI symptoms and QoL among women with SUI, as well as to investigate the mechanisms underlying the beneficial effects of PelviSense-assisted PFMT for treating SUI.

We selected the 1-hour pad test to assess the severity of urine loss as the primary outcome in this RCT. The pad test is a reliable and objective measurement of urine loss, recommended by both the ICS and the International Urogynecological Association.46 It has short-term (<1-hour, 1-hour and 2-hour) and long-term (24-hour) options.47 Although pad tests with longer durations appear to be more accurate and reliable, issues such as patient adherence, tolerance and hygiene concerns could compromise the validity of their results.47 Therefore, the 1-hour pad test was identified as preferable for this RCT due to its practicality, hygiene considerations and feasibility. It serves as both a useful diagnostic tool for SUI and a widely used quantitative test to assess the severity of urinary loss in SUI research.

The findings of this RCT will benefit women with contraindications to invasive adjunctive therapies, including those with rectal bleeding and haemorrhoids, as well as those who are perimenopausal and postmenopausal and have vaginal atrophy and dryness. Current invasive adjunctive therapies for PFMT, such as conventional BF, transvaginal electrical stimulation and vaginal cones, are not indicated or suitable for postpartum women within 6 months after childbirth due to infection risk; pregnant women due to the risk of bleeding; older women with atrophic vaginas; or women with functional limitations due to neurological conditions, such as stroke, Parkinson’s disease, multiple sclerosis and cerebellar ataxia. The PelviSense device will benefit those women who are contraindicated for the use of invasive adjunct therapies.

Furthermore, women who use the PelviSense device will be able to identify, locate and exercise the correct PFMs, increase their PFM strength and achieve improved continence. SUI has been associated with substantial economic costs, including those associated with medications, urinary pads and increased healthcare utilisation. Providing an effective treatment approach that reduces UI among women with SUI may reduce the associated socioeconomic burdens.

However, our RCT has some potential limitations. First, due to the nature of the intervention, it is impossible to blind either the participants or the therapists, which can introduce performance bias.48 These biases can jeopardise internal validity and exaggerate the true effect of a treatment.49 Second, the secondary outcomes are self-report measures, which are susceptible to reporting bias.50 However, the primary outcome measure (the 1-hour pad test) is objective, and TPU enables objective measurements based on a standard procedure. Therefore, these approaches provide a more comprehensive assessment and reduce reliance on subjective data alone.

Ethics and dissemination

This RCT received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board at HK PolyU (approval number: HSEARS20211104002-02) before participant enrolment began. The RCT protocol has been prospectively registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (ID: NCT06126757). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to their enrolment in this RCT, and they will be given copies of the signed information sheets and consent forms for their records. The information sheet will include a detailed explanation of the treatments (PelviSense-assisted and unassisted PFMT), commencement and completion of the RCT, study procedures, duration of treatment sessions, number of treatment sessions required, risks and benefits of participating, rights as participants, follow-up assessments and contact details of the primary investigator, coinvestigators and secretary of the Institutional Review Board.

The findings of this RCT will be disseminated to the participating women via written feedback on PFMT performance using the PelviSense device, to the general public via public lectures, to healthcare professionals via interactive workshops and to researchers via articles in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at national and international conferences.

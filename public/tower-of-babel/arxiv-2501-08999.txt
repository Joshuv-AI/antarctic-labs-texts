Optimising branched fluidic networks: A unifying approach including laminar and turbulent flows, rough walls, and non-Newtonian fluids
J.S. Smink, R. Hagmeijer, C.H. Venner, C.W. Visser

Abstract
Power minimisation in branched fluidic networks has gained significant attention in biology and engineering. The optimal network is defined by channel radii that minimise the sum of viscous dissipation and the volumetric energetic cost of the fluid. For limit cases including laminar flows, high Reynolds number turbulence, or smooth channel approximations, optimal solutions are known. However, no single optimisation approach captures these limit cases. Furthermore, realistic fluidic networks exhibit intermediate points in the parameter space that can hardly be optimised. Here, we present a unifying optimisation approach based on the Darcy friction factor, which has been determined for a wide range of flow regimes and fluid models. We optimise fluidic networks for the entire parameter space: - Laminar and turbulent flows, including networks that exhibit both flow types, - Non-Newtonian fluid models (power-law, Bingham, and Herschel-Bulkley), and - Networks with arbitrary wall roughness, including non-uniform relative roughness. The optimal channel radii are presented analytically and graphically. Finally, the parameter x x in the optimisation relationship Q ∝ R x Q\propto R^{x} was approximated as a function of the Reynolds number, revealing that previously-determined values of x x hardly apply to realistic turbulent flows. Our approach can be extended to other configurations for which the friction factor is known, such as different channel curvatures or wall slip conditions, enabling optimisation of a wide range of fluidic networks.

2
Optimisation of fluidic branching

Consider a branching configuration comprising a parent channel connected to
N
daughter channels at a branching point denoted as
x
, as depicted in figure
1
(
d
). The channels are labeled
0
to
N
, with index
0
indicating the parent channel. The effective radii of the channels are represented as
\mbox{\boldmath$R$}\equiv(R_{0},R_{1},...,R_{N})
, while the termination points of the channels are fixed and denoted as
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{i}
for
i=0,1,...,N
. The flow rates in the daughter channels are specified as
Q_{i}
for
i=1,...,N
, with
Q_{0}
corresponding to the flow rate in the parent channel.
Q_{0}
is considered positive towards the branching point, whereas the flow rates in the daughter channels (
Q_{i}
,
i=1,...,N
) are taken positive away from the branching point. To ensure mass conservation, assuming incompressibility, the flow rates must satisfy
Q_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}.
The lengths of the channels,
L_{i}
, are functions of the branching location, given by

L_{i}\equiv|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{i}-\mbox{\boldmath$x$}|
for
i=0,1,...,N
. In this work, it is assumed that there is only an axial velocity
u
. The channels are assumed to be cylindrical with
R<<L
. The fluid properties are taken constant and the flow is fully developed.

For optimisation of a fluidic network, the power to maintain the flow and to maintain a fluid is minimised. The power is represented by a cost function depending on the radii and lengths of the channels, and is the sum of the individual channel contributions:

P(\mbox{\boldmath$R$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$})\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left(\Delta p_{i}\,Q_{i}+\alpha V_{i}\right).

(2)

Here,
V_{i}\equiv\pi R_{i}^{2}L_{i}
is the channel volume and the pressure gradient
\frac{\Delta p}{L}
is a fluid-type and flow regime dependent function. The pressure at the nodes is not defined or constrained. Differentiation of equation (
2
) to
R
and
x
and equating these expressions to 0 provides the optimisation problems for: 1) the channel radii
R
, and 2) the location of the branching point
x
(optimised in appendix
A.1
). Optimisation of the channel radius is independent of
x
, because it is a decoupled problem
[
24
]
, in which the power defined by equation (
2
) attains a global minimum with respect to the radius of for each individual channel if each channel satisfies
\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$R$}}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
. Therefore, the optimisation condition for any radius
R_{i}
only depends on the power contribution of the corresponding channel and
R_{i}
does not depend on the lengths of the channels
L_{i}
.

For minimizing the power consumption in the network, every single channel has to be optimised individually according to:

\partialderivative{P}{R_i}=\partialderivative{\Delta p_i}{R_i}Q_{i}+2\pi\alpha R_{i}L_{i}=0,\;\;\;\;i=0,1,...,N.

(3)

The pressure drop
\Delta p
is represented as a function of a flow rate
Q
and a radius
R
using the Darcy-Weisbach equation
[
62
]
:

\Delta p=f\,\frac{\rho}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{Q^{2}}{R^{5}}L,

(4)

Here,
\rho
is the fluid density,
L
is the length of the channel and
f
the Darcy friction factor. The friction factor
f
as function of the Reynolds number for the different fluid models is presented in figure
2
(
b-d
). The specific equations of
f
for different flow regimes and fluid models are provided in §
3
. In this study, a generalised Reynolds number is defined as
[
63
]
:

Re\equiv\frac{8}{\pi^{2-n}}\left(\frac{n}{3n+1}\right)^{n}\frac{\rho}{\mu^{\prime}}\frac{Q^{2-n}}{R^{4-3n}}.

(5)

f
also depends on the Hedström number
He
(commonly used for description of yield-stress fluids), the relative wall roughness
\delta\equiv\varepsilon/2R
(where
\varepsilon
is the absolute channel wall roughness) and the flow index
n
.

The effective dynamic viscosity
\mu
describes the resistance of a fluid against shear, and is defined as
\mu=\mu^{\prime}|\dot{\gamma}|^{n-1}
, where
\dot{\gamma}
is the shear rate,
\mu^{\prime}
is the flow consistency index and
n
the flow index, where
0<n<1
represents a shear-thinning, and
n>1
represents a shear-thickening fluid as shown in figure
2
(
a
). In addition, a fluid may show yield-stress behaviour if
\tau_{0}\geq 0
(e.g. Bingham and Herschel-Bulkley fluids, see figure
2
(
a
)), where the fluid only shears once an effective local shear stress exceeds the yield stress
\tau_{0}
. The rheological behaviour for the shear rate of a Herschel-Bulkley fluid as function of the effective local shear stress is described as

\dot{\gamma}(\tau)=\begin{cases}\text{sign}(\tau_{rz})\left(\frac{|\tau_{rz}|-\tau_{0}}{\mu^{\prime}}\right)^{1/n}&\text{if $|\tau_{rz}|\geq\tau_{0}$},\\
0&\text{if $|\tau_{rz}|<\tau_{0}$}.\\
\end{cases}

(6)

where
\tau_{rz}
is the local shear stress induced by the flow. Figure
2
(
b-d
) also show the transition from laminar to turbulent flows at the critical Reynolds number
[
64
]
:

Re_{crit}=\frac{6464n}{(1+3n)^{2}}\times(2+n)^{(2+n)/(1+n)}\times\frac{\psi^{2-n}}{(1-\phi)^{(n+2)/n}}.

(7)

where
\psi(\phi,n)
and
\phi
are defined in §
3.1
. Although an increased relative roughness results in a decrease in
Re_{crit}
, this effect becomes only dominant around
\delta=0.1
and larger
[
65
]
. In order to ensure the validity of the used equations, the present study will limit the discussed optimisation results to
\delta\leq 0.1
.

The optimisation condition for the radius of every channel is obtained by substituting equation (
4
) into equation (
3
), providing:

R^{7}=\frac{\rho Q^{3}}{8\pi^{3}\alpha}B

(8)

where
B
is defined as:

B\equiv 5f-R\partialderivative{f}{R}.

(9)

B
is a function of
f
and
R
and thus indirectly also a function of
Re
,
He

\delta
and
n
.

The goal is to obtain an expression for the optimal channel radius as function of parameters that are independent of
R
. Therefore, dimensional analysis is carried out to find a dimensionless form of the channel radius as function of radius-independent dimensionless groups (for more details, see appendix
A.2
). Scaling of the optimisation problem results in the following 5 dimensionless numbers:

\tilde{R}\equiv\frac{R}{\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\prime\frac{3}{2(n+1)}}\alpha^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+1)}}},\;\;\;\;\tilde{Q}\equiv\frac{Q}{\rho^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mu^{\prime\frac{7}{2(n+1)}}\alpha^{\frac{3n-4}{2(n+1)}}},\quad\tilde{\tau}_{0}\equiv\frac{\tau_{0}}{\mu^{\prime\frac{1}{n+1}}\alpha^{\frac{n}{n+1}}},\quad\\
\tilde{\varepsilon}\equiv\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\prime\frac{3}{2(n+1)}}\alpha^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+1)}}},\text{ and }n

(10)

To align with literature, the Reynolds number will be used instead of
\tilde{R}
. This choice will simplify the later computations in §
3
and enables direct characterisation of the flow in the channel. The Reynolds number is rewritten in dimensionless groups as follows:

Re=a(n)\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2-n}}{\tilde{R}^{4-3n}},

(11)

where
a(n)
is defined as:

a(n)\equiv\frac{8}{\pi^{2-n}}\left(\frac{n}{3n+1}\right)^{n}

(12)

The network is described by the dimensionless numbers
Re
,
\tilde{Q}
,
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
. As
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
are fluid or system parameters that are constant in a network,
Re
and
\tilde{Q}
are the only flow-rate dependent dimensionless numbers, which is convenient for network optimisation. Described with the new dimensionless numbers,
f
will now be a function of 5 dimensionless numbers instead of 4. Therefore, in the new domain, there will be a curve along which
f
is constant, removing one degree of freedom (see appendix
A.3
).

For given fluid, flow and system properties, an optimal channel radius can be calculated via the Reynolds number using the nondimensionalised optimisation condition (equation (
8
)):

(8\pi^{3})^{4-3n}\,a^{7}\,\tilde{Q}^{2(n+1)}=\tilde{B}^{4-3n}Re^{7}.

(13)

where
\tilde{B}
is a function of
\tilde{f}
, which is a function of
Re
,
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{Q}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
, and
n
. Hence, equation (
9
) can be rewritten as:

\tilde{B}=5\tilde{f}-R\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{R}=5\tilde{f}-R\,\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\partialderivative{Re}{R}=\tilde{f}\left(5+(4-3n)\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\,\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)

(14)

where differentiation of equation (
5
) provides
\partialderivative{Re}{R}=-(4-3n)\frac{Re}{R}
. Therefore, when knowing
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{Q}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
, one can determine the optimal
Re
for a channel.

We conclude this section by introducing dimensionless groups that will be used in §
3
. The Hedström number
He
is defined
[
66
]
and rewritten as follows:

He\equiv\frac{4R^{2}\rho}{\mu^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\mu^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{2-n}{n}}=4\left(a(n)\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2-n}}{Re}\right)^{\frac{2}{4-3n}}\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{\frac{2-n}{n}}.

(15)

The relative roughness
\delta
becomes in the new dimensionless groups:

\delta\equiv\frac{\varepsilon}{2R}=\frac{1}{2}\,\tilde{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{Re}{a(n)\tilde{Q}^{2-n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4-3n}}.

(16)

Characteristic for a yield-stress fluid is the formation of a plug in the centre of the channel, where the local shear stress
\tau_{rz}
does not exceed the yield stress
\tau_{0}
. The associated plug radius
R_{p}
is characterised by

R_{p}\equiv\frac{2\tau_{0}}{\frac{\Delta p}{L}}.

(17)

Nondimensionalisation of the plug radius yields the dimensionless plug radius
\phi
, which often appears in descriptions for
f
in the case of yield-stress fluids:

\phi\equiv\frac{R_{p}}{R}=\frac{2\tau_{0}}{\frac{\Delta p}{L}R}.

(18)

By definition,
\phi
can also be expressed in terms of the Reynolds and Hedström number and in the new dimensionless groups:

\phi=\frac{64}{f\,Re}\left(\frac{2He}{Re}\left(\frac{n}{3n+1}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2-n}}=8\pi^{2}a(n)^{\frac{4}{4-3n}}\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{0}}{\tilde{f}\,Re}\left(\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2}}{Re^{3}}\right)^{\frac{n}{4-3n}}.

(19)

Up to here, no assumptions are made with respect to the flow type.

3.1
Laminar flow of Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids

For laminar flow through a circular channel results in the friction factor
[
24
]

\tilde{f}=\frac{64}{Re\,\psi(\phi,n)^{n}},

(20)

where
\psi
is a dimensionless flow rate that ranges from 0 to 1. The friction factor is independent of the channel wall roughness, so it holds for arbitrary
\tilde{\varepsilon}
. For a Herschel-Bulkley fluid,
\psi
is computed by integration of the velocity field over the channel’s cross-section as
[
24
,
67
,
68
]
:

\psi=\frac{(1-\phi)^{(n+1)/n}}{(3n+1)^{-1}}\times\left(\frac{(1-\phi)^{2}}{3n+1}+\frac{2\phi(1-\phi)}{2n+1}+\frac{\phi^{2}}{n+1}\right).

(21)

For Bingham fluids (
n=1
),
\psi
reduces to
[
24
,
69
]
:

\psi=1-\frac{4}{3}\phi+\frac{1}{3}\phi^{4},

(22)

and for the case of
\tau_{0}=0
(Newtonian and power-law fluids,
\phi=0
),
\psi
further reduces to 1.

Calculation of the optimisation condition results in the following expression for
\tilde{B}
:

\tilde{B}=\tilde{f}J(\phi,n),

(23)

where the function
J(\phi,n)
is defined as

J\equiv 1+\frac{3n}{1-\frac{n\phi}{\psi}\partialderivative{\psi}{\phi}}.

(24)

For a Herschel-Bulkley fluid, substitution of
\psi
from equation (
21
) into equation (
24
) results in the following expression for
J
:

J=\frac{3n+1}{\frac{6n^{3}}{(2n+1)(n+1)}\phi^{3}+\frac{6n^{2}}{(2n+1)(n+1)}\phi^{2}+\frac{3n}{2n+1}\phi+1}.

(25)

For Bingham fluids (
n=1
), the expression for
J
reduces to

J=\frac{4}{\phi^{3}+\phi^{2}+\phi+1}.

(26)

and for non-yield fluids (
\phi=0
), it reduces to
J=3n+1
. Finally, for the yield limit (
\phi=1
), one obtains
J=1
.

Substitution of
\tilde{B}
into equation (
13
) reduces the optimisation condition to

Re^{3(n+1)}=\left(\frac{\pi^{3}}{8}\right)^{4-3n}\,a^{7}\,\tilde{Q}^{2(n+1)}\left(\frac{\psi(\phi,n)^{n}}{J(\phi,n)}\right)^{4-3n}.

(27)

Together with equation (
19
) and equation (
20
),
Re
is then explicitly solved for given
\tilde{Q}
,
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
and
n
, providing the optimal radius via equations (
5
) and (
10
). Figure
8
(

) in appendix
E
present a contour plot for the optimal
Re
as function of
\tilde{Q}
and
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
for different values of

. Note that the number of parameters can be reduced even further, by combining
\tilde{Q}
and

, to a parameter which is only a function of given
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
and

. Then the optimisation condition becomes
[

]
:

\frac{\tilde{R}^{3}}{\tilde{Q}}=\left(a^{3}\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2}}{Re^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4-3n}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\left(\frac{3n+1}{n}\right)^{n}\frac{J(\phi,n)}{\psi(\phi,n)^{n}}\right)^{1/(n+1)},

(28)

where the right-hand side is constant for an optimised network. Plotting
\frac{\tilde{R}^{3}}{\tilde{Q}}
as function of
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
and
n
gives the contour plot in figure
8
(
d
) in appendix
E
. This figure can be made for laminar flows because
\tilde{R}^{3}/\tilde{Q}
is constant over the entire network, which is highly useful for design of optimised branched fluidic networks, as only one optimisation condition has to be calculated.

3.2
Turbulent flow of Newtonian fluid, rough and smooth channel

We now demonstrate that the proposed optimisation method is universal if the friction factor is known, by optimizing networks for diverse turbulent flow regimes. For turbulent flow of a Newtonian fluid, the Colebrook-White equation
[
70
]
describes the friction factor for both low- and high-turbulent flow (
Re=[Re_{crit},\infty\rangle
) in hydraulically smooth and rough pipes using the following implicit equation:

f=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(\frac{\delta}{3.7}+\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{f}}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(29)

Equation (
29
) underlies the well-known Moody diagram
[
47
]
as shown in figure
2
(
b
). Rewriting equation (
29
) in terms of the dimensionless numbers gives:

\tilde{f}=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}+\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(30)

Differentiation to
Re
gives:

\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}=-\frac{\tilde{f}}{Re}\frac{2\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}-\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}+\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}+\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)-\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}}

(31)

resulting in

\tilde{B}=\tilde{f}\left(5-\frac{2\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}-\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}+\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}+\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)-\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}}\right).

(32)

Substituting
\tilde{B}
into equation (
13
) enables implicit solving
Re
for given
\tilde{Q}
. Contour plots of the optimal
Re
as function
\tilde{Q}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
are presented in figure
3
(
a
). Limit cases of the Colebrook-White equation include complete turbulence (for which Von Kármán’s formula applies), turbulence in smooth channels, and low-Reynolds-number turbulence in smooth channels (e.g. Blasius’ formula). Expressions of the optimal channel radius for these cases are derived in appendix
B
.

3.3
Turbulent flow of non-Newtonian fluids, smooth channel

3.3.1
Power-law fluid

Turbulent pipe flow of non-Newtonian power-law fluids at low Reynolds numbers in a smooth circular channel (

\frac{2}{\sqrt{f}}=\frac{4}{n^{0.75}}\log_{10}\left(Re\left(\frac{f}{4}\right)^{1-n/2}\right)-\frac{0.4}{n^{1.2}}

f

In the literature, many attempts have been made to find the proportionality between the flow rate

5
Synthesis: Design of an optimised fluidic network

The framework described in §§
2
,
3
and
4
leads to the following approach for optimisation of branched fluidic networks (such as shown in figure
1
(
e,f
)):

1.

Determine the fluid model and the cost factor
\alpha
, and calculate
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
. Calculate
\tilde{Q}
for all the to be optimised channels (§
2
).

2.

Determine the optimal radius
R
in one channel.

(a)

First, determine the optimal
Re
by one of the following options:

-

Read
Re
from an optimisation contour plot (e.g. figure
3
(
a
)) for given
\tilde{Q}
.

-

Calculate
\tilde{B}(\tilde{f},Re,\tilde{Q},\tilde{\tau}_{0},\tilde{\varepsilon},n)
via equation (
14
) and then solve equation (
13
) for
Re
for given
\tilde{Q}
.

(b)

Subsequently, calculate
R
from the calculated
Re
by using equation (
5
).

3.

Obtain all channel radii:

-

By repeating step 2 for each channel, or

-

by obtaining
x
from figure
4
and calculating all channel radii using
Q\propto R^{x}
. This option only applies if
x
is (almost) constant over the applicable range of
Re
.

4.

Calculate the optimal location of the branching points (appendix
A.1
).

6
Conclusion

A unifying design procedure for optimisation of branched fluidic networks was presented. The optimal channel radius is obtained by minimizing the power consumption for maintaining the flow and minimizing the volume simultaneously, which is carried out for all channel segments individually. The approach is applicable to every flow configuration for which the Darcy friction factor is available.

In this work, network optimisation was carried out for the parameter space spun by the Reynolds number (laminar and turbulent flows), the wall roughness and for different rheologies. This parameter space is widely applicable to e.g. heat exchangers or (intertial) microfluidics, but was hardly covered in the literature as shown in figure
1
(
a,b
). First, the entire optimisation problem is non-dimensionalised in terms of
Re
,
\tilde{Q}
,
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
. For every channel, the optimal Reynolds number and the optimal channel radius are obtained as a function of the dimensionless flow rate
\tilde{Q}
. To minimise the required mathematical analysis, graphs are presented that readily provide the optimal Reynolds number for every fluid model.

Subsequently, the value of
x
in the proportionality
Q\propto R^{x}
was evaluated for the full parameter space of the Reynolds number and channel roughness for different fluid rheologies. The well-known limit values
x=3
for laminar flow and
x=7/3
for high-
Re
turbulence were recovered
[
44
,
37
,
22
,
46
]
. However, as appears that the latter value is hardly reached in practice, the intermediate values of
x
were calculated and plotted. Finally, a design procedure of optimised fluidic networks that incorporates all aforementioned elements was presented.

Our unifying approach for optimisation of fluidic networks can be extended to other configurations with known friction factors, including non-circular cross-sections, bended pipes, bubbly flows, porous walls
[
21
]
, porous media
[
72
]
or slip flow
[
73
]
. As such, it opens an enormous parameter space of realistically-encountered configurations in nature and engineering.

Appendix A
Details onto optimisation of a fluidic branching

A.1
Optimal branching point location

To minimise the power in a network, the location of the branching point
x
must be chosen. If the channel radii are optimised according to equation (
13
), this is the same as minimizing the total volume within the channels. Differentiation of the total power
P
(equation (
2
)) to
x
results in:

\nabla_{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}{P}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left(\left|\derivative{p}{z}\right|_{i}Q_{i}+\alpha\pi R_{i}^{2}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}L_{i}=\textbf{0},\;\;\;\;i=0,1,...,N,

(40)

where we define

\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{i,*}\equiv\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}L_{i}\right)_{*}=\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{*}-\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{i}}{|\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{*}-\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{i}|}.

(41)

Here, the subscript
i
indicates the index of the channel, and the subscript
*
indicates an optimised parameter.

Substituting the optimisation condition for the channel radii (equation (
13
)) results in the optimisation condition of the branching point
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{*}
(for a full derivation, see
[
24
]
):

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left(R_{i,*}^{2}\frac{2f+B}{B}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{i,*}\right)=\textbf{0},\;\;\;\;i=0,1,...,N.

(42)

Alternatively, we can rewrite equation (
42
) in terms of dimensionless numbers:

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left(\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2-n}}{Re}\right)^{\frac{2}{4-3n}}\frac{2\tilde{f}+\tilde{B}}{\tilde{B}}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{i,*}=\textbf{0},\;\;\;\;i=0,1,...,N.

(43)

Equation (
43
) requires the optimal Reynolds number and corresponding
\tilde{f}
,
\tilde{B}
and
\tilde{Q}
for each channel for computation of the optimal branching point location. It is an implicit equation, such that the coordinates of
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{*}
are solved by simple numerical methods, providing the lengths of the channels
L_{i}
. The resulting
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{*}
determines a network that is optimised both with respect to the channel radii (equation (
13
)) and the channel lengths (equation (
43
)).

A.2
Governing dimensionless numbers

Scaling of the problem for calculation of the optimisation condition for the channel radii is investigated using the Buckingham-Pi theorem. The goal is to find a nondimensionalisation of the radius and the dimensionless parameters where it depends on. In this problem, the following 8 independent parameters are relevant: The channel radius
R
in m, the flow rate
Q
in m
3
s
-1
, the fluid density
\rho
in kg m
-3
, the viscosity index
\mu^{\prime}
in Pa s
n
, the cost factor
\alpha
in W m
-3
, the yield stress
\tau_{0}
in Pa, the channel absolute roughness
\varepsilon
in m and the flow index
n
. In these parameters, 3 independent physical dimensions are present. We choose
\rho
,
\mu^{\prime}
, and
\alpha
to scale this problem, which are suitable for the scaling. We will end up with 5 dimensionless parameters, where
n
is one of them.

In mathematical terms, the problem is then:

R=g(\rho,\mu^{\prime},\alpha,Q,\tau_{0},\varepsilon,n),

(44)

where scaling of the problem results in:

\tilde{R}=\tilde{g}(\tilde{Q},\tilde{\tau_{0}},\tilde{\varepsilon},n)

(45)

with

\tilde{R}\equiv\frac{R}{\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\prime\frac{3}{2(n+1)}}\alpha^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+1)}}},\;\;\;\;\tilde{Q}\equiv\frac{Q}{\rho^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mu^{\prime\frac{7}{2(n+1)}}\alpha^{\frac{3n-4}{2(n+1)}}},\quad\\
\tilde{\tau}_{0}\equiv\frac{\tau_{0}}{\mu^{\prime\frac{1}{n+1}}\alpha^{\frac{n}{n+1}}},\text{ and }\tilde{\varepsilon}\equiv\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\prime\frac{3}{2(n+1)}}\alpha^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+1)}}}

(46)

For convenience, the Reynolds number will be used instead of
\tilde{R}
. This choice simplifies the calculations and provides direct characterisation of the flow in the channels. Expressed in dimensionless groups, the Reynolds number (equation (
5
)) becomes:

Re=a(n)\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2-n}}{\tilde{R}^{4-3n}}

(47)

where
a(n)
is defined as:

a(n)\equiv\frac{8}{\pi^{2-n}}\left(\frac{n}{3n+1}\right)^{n}

(48)

In the end, a network can be described by the dimensionless numbers
Re
,
\tilde{Q}
,
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
. All parameters except for
R
and
Q
are fluid or system parameters, which are constant in a network. In a branched fluidic network,
Q
can change from channel to channel. Therefore, for design procedure purposes, it is convenient that
Re
and
\tilde{Q}
are the only flow-rate dependent dimensionless numbers.

Therefore, the optimisation problem is reduced to the dimensionless dependency

Re=\tilde{g}_{1}(\tilde{Q},\tilde{\tau}_{0},\tilde{\varepsilon},n).

(49)

For
n=1
, the dimensionless numbers reduce to:

Re=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{\rho Q}{\mu^{\prime}R},\quad\tilde{Q}=\frac{\alpha^{\frac{1}{4}}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}}Q}{\mu^{\prime\frac{7}{4}}},\quad\tilde{\tau}_{0}=\frac{\tau_{0}}{\left(\mu^{\prime}\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}},\text{ and }\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{\frac{1}{4}}\varepsilon}{\mu^{\prime\frac{3}{4}}}

(50)

For given fluid, flow and system properties, an optimal channel radius can be calculated from the obtained optimal Reynolds number.

A.3
Mapping from 4 to 5 variables for
f

The friction factor
f
commonly depends on the Reynolds number
Re
, the Hedström number
He
, the relative wall roughness
\delta
and the flow index
n
. These are 4 independent dimensionless groups. In the optimisation procedure, the following 5 dimensionless numbers are used (for details, see appendix
A.2
):
Re
,
\tilde{Q}
,
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
. Because the friction factor will now be described by 5 instead of 4 dimensionless numbers, a curve in the 5-variables space exists along which
f
is constant. The direction of that curve is locally described by the vector
\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4},\beta_{5})^{T}
. Therefore, the inner product of this vector with the gradient of the original variables
\mbox{\boldmath$F$}=(Re,He,\delta,n)^{T}
to the new variables
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$F$}}=(Re,\tilde{Q},\tilde{\tau}_{0},\tilde{\varepsilon},n)^{T}
must be zero. In that case,
Re
,
He
,
\delta
and
n
are constant along the direction of
\beta
. This results in the following 4 equations:

\sum_{j=1}^{5}\partialderivative{F_i}{\tilde{F}_j}\beta_{j}=0,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;i=1,2,3,4,

(51)

which are sufficient to define the direction of the vector
\beta
while choosing its arbitrary length by conveniently choosing
\beta_{2}=1
. Solving equation (
51
) results in
\beta_{1}=0
,
\beta_{3}=-\frac{2n}{4-3n}\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{0}}{\tilde{Q}}\beta_{2}
,
\beta_{4}=\frac{2-n}{4-3n}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\tilde{Q}}\beta_{2}
, and
\beta_{5}=0
.

f
will be restricted in the new parameter space by:

\sum_{j=1}^{5}\partialderivative{f}{\tilde{F}_j}\beta_{j}=0.

(52)

Substituting the obtained coefficients
\beta
results in the following expression:

\tilde{Q}\,\partialderivative{f}{\tilde{Q}}-\frac{2n}{4-3n}\,\tilde{\tau}_{0}\,\partialderivative{f}{\tilde{\tau}_0}+\frac{2-n}{4-3n}\,\tilde{\varepsilon}\,\partialderivative{f}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}=0,

(53)

which provides the curve along which
f
is constant in the new parameter space.

Appendix B
Specific cases of turbulent flow of Newtonian fluid, rough and smooth channel

B.1
Complete turbulence, rough channel

For sufficiently large Reynolds numbers, the friction factor corresponding to fully developed turbulent flow in a circular channel can quite accurately be described by Von Kármán’s formula
[
74
]
, which holds for rough channels in the limit of high Reynolds numbers:

f=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(\frac{\delta}{3.7}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(54)

This equation is a limit case of the Colebrook-White equation, valid for the complete turbulent regime (
Re>3500/\delta
)
[
47
]
. In terms of
\tilde{\varepsilon}
,
\tilde{Q}
and
Re
,
f
becomes

\tilde{f}=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(55)

Differentiation to
Re
results in

\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}=-\frac{\tilde{f}}{Re}\frac{2}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)}

(56)

resulting in

\tilde{B}=\tilde{f}\left(5-\frac{2}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)}\right)

(57)

Contour plots of the optimal
Re
as function
\tilde{Q}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
are presented in figure
3
(
a
), for the high-
\tilde{\varepsilon}
limit.

B.2
Turbulence, smooth channel

For a smooth-wall channel (
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
), the Colebrook-White equation
[
70
]
reduces to

\tilde{f}=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(58)

Differentiation to
Re
gives:

\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}=\frac{\tilde{f}}{Re}\frac{2}{\ln\left(\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)-1}

(59)

resulting in

\tilde{B}=\tilde{f}\left(5+\frac{2}{\ln\left(\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)-1}\right).

(60)

Figure
5
shows the optimisation curve for
Re
as function of
\tilde{Q}
. This result corresponds to the contour plot of the optimal
Re
as function
\tilde{Q}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
as presented in figure
3
(
a
) for
\tilde{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
.

Optimal
Re
as function of
\tilde{Q}
for a Newtonian fluid in a smooth-wall channel according to the Colebrook-White equation, together with the Blasius’ formula and a low-
Re
turbulent approximation.

B.3
Low Reynolds number turbulence, smooth channel

Power-law approximations for the friction factor have been established for turbulent flows in smooth channels (
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
), for specific and limited ranges of the Reynolds number, in the general form:

\tilde{f}=\frac{c_{1}}{Re^{c_{2}}}

(61)

with
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
being two relation-dependent constants. This is an explicit expression for
\tilde{f}
as function of
Re
, resulting in
\tilde{B}
being:

\tilde{B}=(5-c_{2})\tilde{f}=\frac{c_{1}(5-c_{2})}{Re^{c_{2}}}

(62)

For Newtonian fluids, the optimisation condition (equation (
13
)) reduces to

\tilde{B}\,Re^{7}=\frac{1024}{\pi^{4}}\,\tilde{Q}^{4},

(63)

which can for this case be rewritten to

Re=\left(\frac{1024}{c_{1}(5-c_{2})}\right)^{\frac{1}{7-c_{2}}}\left(\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{4}{7-c_{2}}}.

(64)

Blasius’ formula
[
75
,
76
]
, which is valid for
3\times 10^{3}<Re<10^{5}
, is given by

\tilde{f}=\frac{0.3164}{Re^{\frac{1}{4}}},

(65)

resulting in the optimisation condition

Re=2.629\left(\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{16}{27}}.

(66)

An empirical relation valid for turbulent flows at intermediate Reynolds numbers (
2\times 10^{4}<Re<10^{6}
)
[
45
]
is given by

\tilde{f}=\frac{0.184}{Re^{\frac{1}{5}}},

(67)

resulting in the optimisation condition

Re=2.822\left(\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{10}{17}}.

(68)

These explicit expressions for the optimal
Re
as function of
\tilde{Q}
are shown in figure
5
for Blasius’ formula (equation (
65
)) and low
Re
-number turbulence (equation (
67
)), revealing perfect coincidence with the more general Colebrook-White solution for smooth channels.

Appendix C
Laminar and low Reynolds number turbulent flow of Bingham fluid, smooth channel

Considering a turbulent flow of a Bingham fluid in a circular pipe brings to empirical and numerical approximations for the friction factor
f
. Darby
et al.

[
77
]
developed an empirical curve-fit equation, composed of the following structure:

\begin{split}&f=\left(f_{L}^{\chi_{1}}+f_{T}^{\chi_{1}}\right)^{1/{\chi_{1}}}\\
&f_{T}=4\times 10^{\chi_{2}}Re^{-0.193}\\
&\chi_{1}=1.7+\frac{4\times 10^{5}}{Re}\\
&\chi_{2}=-1.47\left(1+0.146\exp(-2.9\times 10^{-5}He)\right).\end{split}

(69)

where
f_{L}
is the laminar friction factor as determined from the Buckingham-Reiner equation
[
69
]
(equations (
19
), (
20
) and (
22
) combined).

In the new set of dimensionless numbers, the friction factor
\tilde{f}
becomes:

\begin{split}&\tilde{f}=\left(\tilde{f}_{L}^{\tilde{\chi}_{1}}+\tilde{f}_{T}^{\tilde{\chi}_{1}}\right)^{1/\tilde{\chi}_{1}}\\
&\tilde{f}_{T}=4\times 10^{\tilde{\chi}_{2}}Re^{-0.193}\\
&\tilde{\chi}_{1}=1.7+\frac{4\times 10^{5}}{Re}\\
&\tilde{\chi}_{2}=-1.47\left(1+0.146\exp(-2.9\times 10^{-5}\frac{16}{\pi^2}\frac{\tilde{Q}^2}{Re^2}\tilde{\tau}_0)\right).\end{split}

(70)

Implicit differentiation of
\tilde{f}
to
Re
results in the following expression

\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial Re}=\frac{\tilde{f}}{Re}\Bigg[\frac{1.7-\tilde{\chi}_{1}}{\tilde{\chi}_{1}}\left(\left(\frac{\tilde{f}_{L}}{\tilde{f}}\right)^{\tilde{\chi}_{1}}\ln\tilde{f}_{L}+\left(\frac{\tilde{f}_{T}}{\tilde{f}}\right)^{\tilde{\chi}_{1}}\ln\tilde{f}_{T}-\ln\tilde{f}\right)\\
+(J(\phi)-5)\left(\frac{\tilde{f}_{L}}{\tilde{f}}\right)^{\tilde{\chi}_{1}}+\left(\ln(10)Re\frac{\partial\tilde{\chi}_{2}}{\partial Re}-0.193\right)\left(\frac{\tilde{f}_{T}}{\tilde{f}}\right)^{\tilde{\chi}_{1}}\Bigg].

(71)

where

\frac{\partial\tilde{\chi}_{2}}{\partial Re}=-1.99\times 10^{-4}\times\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2}\tilde{\tau}_{0}}{\pi^{2}Re^{3}}\exp\left(-4.64\times 10^{-4}\frac{\tilde{Q}^{2}\tilde{\tau}_{0}}{\pi^{2}Re^{2}}\right)

(72)

resulting in

\tilde{B}=\tilde{f}\left(5+\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right).

(73)

The set of equations for this fluid model is relatively complex, but has as benefit that it covers both the laminar and turbulent regimes. The contour plot in figure
6
gives the optimal Reynolds number in a channel for given
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
and
\tilde{Q}
.

Contour plot of the optimal Reynolds number as a function of
\tilde{Q}
and
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
, for both laminar and low Reynolds number turbulent flow of a Bingham fluid (equation (
69
)). The red-scale contour lines represent the Reynolds numbers
2\times 10^{x}
,
3\times 10^{x}
, …
9\times 10^{x}
with decreasing brightness. The thick grey line represents the critical Reynolds number.

Appendix D
Approximation of
x
for different fluid models

In network optimisation, many attempts have been made to find the proportionality between the flow rate
Q
and the channel radius
R
for optimised networks, in the following form:

Q\propto R^{x}

(74)

In the optimisation method of the present study, only
Re
and
\tilde{Q}
contain parameters involving
R
and
Q
, whereas
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}
and
n
are independent of
R
and
Q
. Therefore, when knowing the proportionality between
\tilde{Q}
and
Re
, one can obtain
x
using equation (
74
). However, this is only posible if
\tilde{B}\propto Re^{c_{1}}\tilde{Q}^{c_{2}}
. However, in most of the cases (except for e.g. Blasius’ formula), this is not true. Therefore,
x
is calculated by locally approximating
\tilde{B}
by a power function using

x=\frac{R}{Q}\frac{\partial Q}{\partial R}=\frac{(4-3n)\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}}{(2-n)\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}-1}=3-\frac{2\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}-3}{(2-n)\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}-1},

(75)

where
\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}
is derived to be:

\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}=-\Bigg[\left((4-3n)\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}-4\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}+7\right)\left(1+\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}\right)+\\
+\left(\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}-\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}\right)\left((4-3n)\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}-4\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}\right)\Bigg]\times\\
\Bigg[\left(2n\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}+(4-3n)\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}-2(n+1)\right)\left(1+\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}\right)+\\
+\left(\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}-\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}\right)\left(2n\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}+(4-3n)\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}\right)\Bigg]^{-1}.

(76)

Equation (
76
) is an expression containing 7 differentials of
\tilde{B}
or
\tilde{f}
to
Re
,
\phi
,
\tilde{f}
or
\tilde{Q}
. All differentials can be calculated with keeping the parameters in subscript fixed. The differentials have to be calculated for every flow model separately. The calculated value for
x
is only valid around its corresponding point in the (
Re,\tilde{\tau}_{0},\tilde{\varepsilon},n
)-space with a limited range depending on the change in
x
. Expressions for the differentials for the fluid models presented in the following sections.

D.1
Laminar flow of a Newtonian, power-law, Bingham and Herschel-Bulkley fluid

For a laminar flow, the friction factor was found to be

\tilde{f}=\frac{64}{Re\,\psi(\phi,n)^{n}},

(77)

resulting in an expression for
\tilde{B}
being

\tilde{B}=\tilde{f}J(\phi,n),

(78)

where
J(\phi,n)
is defined as

J\equiv 1+\frac{3n}{1-\frac{n\phi}{\psi}\partialderivative{\psi}{\phi}}.

(79)

Differentiation provides the needed differentials in equation (
76
), resulting in the expression for
x
(equation (
75
)):

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=1,\quad\quad\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi}{J}\partialderivative{J}{\phi},

(80)

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=-1,\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n\phi}{\psi}\partialderivative{\psi}{\phi},

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=0.

Substituting everything into equation (
76
) results in
\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}=\frac{3}{2}
, resulting in
x=3
. This holds for laminar flow of all treated fluid models.

D.2
Turbulent flow of Newtonian fluid, rough and smooth channel

A turbulent flow of a Newtonian fluid in a rough or smooth channel can be described by the Colebrook-White equation
[
70
]
, which is in the applied nondimensionalisation:

\tilde{f}=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(81)

with

k_{1}\equiv\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}

(82)

k_{2}\equiv\frac{2.51}{\sqrt{\tilde{f}}Re}

(83)

Differentiation provides the needed differentials in equation (
76
):

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left[\frac{2k_{1}k_{2}\ln\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)-k_{2}^{2}}{\left[\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)\ln\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)-k_{2}\right]^{2}}\right],

(84)

\displaystyle\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left[\frac{-8k_{1}k_{2}\ln(k_1+k_2)+2k_{1}^{2}+2k_{2}^{2}}{[(k_{1}+k_{2})\ln(k_1+k_2)-k_{2}]^{2}}\right],\quad

\displaystyle\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left[\frac{4k_{1}k_{2}\ln(k_1+k_2)-2k_{1}^{2}}{[(k_{1}+k_{2})\ln(k_1+k_2)-k_{2}]^{2}}\right],

\displaystyle\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(k_{2}-k_{1})}{(k_{1}+k_{2})\ln(k_1+k_2)-k_{2}},\quad\quad

\displaystyle\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k_{1}}{(k_{1}+k_{2})\ln(k_1+k_2)-k_{2}}.

D.2.1
Complete turbulent flow of Newtonian fluid, rough channel

For sufficiently large Reynolds numbers, the friction factor corresponding to complete turbulence in a circular channel can accurately be described by Von Kármán’s formula
[
74
]
:

f=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(85)

Differentiation provides the needed differentials in equation (
76
):

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=1,\quad\quad\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}=\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\frac{2}{\left(\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)\right)^{2}},

(86)

\displaystyle\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}=\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\frac{-2}{\left(\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)},\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{3.7}\,\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}Re}{\tilde{Q}}\right)}.

D.2.2
Turbulence, smooth channel

For a smooth-wall channel (
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
), the Colebrook-White equation
[
70
]
reduces to

\tilde{f}=\left\{-2.0\log_{10}\left(\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}\right)\right\}^{-2}.

(87)

Differentiation provides the needed differentials in equation (
76
):

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left[\frac{1}{\left[\ln\left(\frac{2.51}{\sqrt{\tilde{f}}Re}\right)-1\right]^{2}}\right],

(88)

\displaystyle\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left[\frac{2}{[\ln(\frac{2.51}{\sqrt{\tilde{f}}Re})-1]^{2}}\right],\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}=\frac{2}{\ln(\frac{2.51}{\sqrt{\tilde{f}}Re})-1},

\displaystyle\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}=0.

D.2.3
Low Reynolds number turbulence, smooth channel

For certain Reynolds number regimes of flow in a smooth channel (
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
), approximations for the friction factor have been formulated, such as Blasius’ formula and empirical relations. These are often in the following form
[
44
]
:

\tilde{f}=\frac{c_{1}}{Re^{c_{2}}}

(89)

Differentiation provides the needed differentials in equation (
76
):

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=1,\quad\quad\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}=0,\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}=0,

(90)

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}=-c_{2},\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=0.

Substituting everything into equation (
76
) results in
\frac{Re}{\tilde{Q}}\partialderivative{\tilde{Q}}{Re}=\frac{7-c_{2}}{4}
, resulting in
x=\frac{7-c_{2}}{3-c_{2}}
. For Blasius’ equation (equation (
65
)),
x
reduces to
x=\frac{27}{11}
and for the empirical relation (equation (
67
))
x
reduces to
x=\frac{17}{7}
.

D.3
Turbulent flow of non-Newtonian fluids, smooth channel

D.3.1
Power-law fluid

Turbulent pipe flow of non-Newtonian power-law fluids at low Reynolds numbers in a smooth circular channel (
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
) described by Dodge and Metzner
[
71
]
is given in the following implicit relation for the Darcy friction factor
f
:

\frac{2}{\sqrt{f}}=\frac{4}{n^{0.75}}\log_{10}\left(Re\left(\frac{f}{4}\right)^{1-n/2}\right)-\frac{0.4}{n^{1.2}}

(91)

Differentiation provides the needed differentials in equation (
76
):

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}(4-3n)\left(\frac{n^{0.75}\ln(10)}{4\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}+1-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{-2}\frac{n^{0.75}\ln(10)}{8\sqrt{\tilde{f}}},

(92)

\displaystyle\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}=0,\quad\quad\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{n^{0.75}\ln(10)}{4\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}+1-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{-1},

\displaystyle\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}=0.

D.3.2
Herschel-Bulkley fluid

For a turbulent flow of a Herschel-Bulkley fluid in a smooth circular channel (
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
), Torrance
[
63
]
developed a relationship for the Darcy friction factor:

\frac{2}{\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}=0.45-\frac{2.75}{n}+\frac{1.97}{n}\ln(1-\phi)+\frac{1.97}{n}\ln\left(Re\left(\frac{3n+1}{4n}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\tilde{f}}{4}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\right).

(93)

Differentiation provides the needed differentials in equation (
76
):

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{f}}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\frac{4-3n+\frac{4\phi}{1-\phi}}{\left(\frac{n}{1.97\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}+\frac{\phi}{1-\phi}+1-\frac{n}{2}\right)}\frac{\frac{1}{2}n}{1.97\sqrt{\tilde{f}}},

(94)

\displaystyle\frac{Re}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},\phi}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{\phi}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{f},\tilde{Q},Re}=-\frac{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{B}}\frac{\left(\frac{4n}{1.97\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}+n\right)\frac{\phi}{(1-\phi)^{2}}}{\left(\frac{n}{1.97\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}+\frac{\phi}{1-\phi}+1-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{B}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{B}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\tilde{f},\phi,Re}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\frac{Re}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{Re}\right)_{\tilde{Q},\phi}=\frac{-1}{\frac{n}{1.97\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}+1-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\phi}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\phi}\right)_{\tilde{Q},Re}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\phi}{1-\phi}}{\frac{n}{1.97\sqrt{\tilde{f}}}+1-\frac{n}{2}},\quad\quad\frac{\tilde{Q}}{\tilde{f}}\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{f}}{\tilde{Q}}\right)_{\phi,Re}=0.

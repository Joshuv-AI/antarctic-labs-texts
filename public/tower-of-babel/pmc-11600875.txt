Baicalin alleviates the injury of human retinal pigment epithelium cells and improves branch retinal vein occlusion in rats by inhibiting the HIF-1α/VEGFA axis
Authors: Shiyue Qin, Guoping Cao, Mingxia Tang, Shuai Sun, Lili Dong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAt present, relevant studies have found that baicalin can improve macular edema (ME) caused by glaucoma, but the effect on branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO) is still unclear.MethodsThe CoCl2-stimulated ARPE-19 cells were treated with different concentrations of baicalin and detected cell viability, apoptosis and oxidative stress. Next, the hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) overexpression vector or siRNA were transfected into CoCl2-stimulated ARPE-19 cells, and the cell changes were detected. We searched the potential binding proteins of HIF-1α through the online database, and screened vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA) as the research object. The CoCl2-stimulated ARPE-19 cells were treated with baicalin alone, or transfected with HIF-1α overexpression vector, or transfected with HIF-1α overexpression vector and VEGFA siRNA, and the cell changes were detected. Finally, we verified the therapeutic effect of baicalin on BRVO rats in vivo.ResultsBaicalin inhibited CoCl2-induced apoptosis, inflammation and oxidative stress in ARPE-19 cells, and baicalin inhibited HIF-1α protein expression. In CoCl2-induced hypoxia cells, HIF-1α aggravated apoptosis, inflammation and oxidative stress, while HIF-1α silencing alleviated cell damage. Mechanism study showed that in baicalin-treated CoCl2-induced cells, VEGFA protein expression decreased and cell damage was improved, but this protective effect was counteracted by HIF-1α, and VEGFA silencing again inhibited apoptosis, inflammation and oxidative stress. Baicalin inhibited HIF-1α and VEGFA protein expression in the retinal tissue of BRVO rats, reduced injury, and promoted the recovery of ganglion cell layer.ConclusionsBaicalin alleviated ARPE-19 cell injury and improved BRVO in rats by inhibiting HIF-1α/VEGFA axis in vivo and in vitro.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-024-02166-y.

Introduction

Retinal vein occlusion (RVO) is a retinal blood circulation disorder that arises due to stenosis of the retinal arteriovenous lumen and restrictions in diastolic function, or as a result of vascular thromboembolism. This condition ranks as the second most prevalent retinal vascular disease, following diabetes retinopathy, and branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO) incidence rate accounts for about 80% of RVO [1, 2]. Epidemiological studies have shown that the global overall prevalence of RVO in 2015 was 0.77%. Recently, the rate of RVO in the world has gradually increased, and it increases with age [3]. Worldwide, there is no significant difference in the prevalence of RVO between East Asia and the United States. In Europe, the prevalence of RVO among people aged 55 years and above is 0.7%, while in China, the cumulative incidence rate of RVO among people aged 40 years and above is 1.9% [4].

RVO is mainly characterized by painless visual impairment. The main reason for this phenomenon is often that RVO induces macular edema (ME), and the secondary ME may cause lifelong and irreversible visual field defect and visual impairment [5]. At present, the specific pathogenesis of RVO is not yet clear, and most studies believe that it is the result of a combination of systemic and local factors. In recent years, the cytokine theory has proposed that ischemia and hypoxia of retinal vessels lead to increased secretion of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), interleukin and other cytokines, activate signal pathways related to vascular endothelial cells, thus leading to the destruction of blood–retinal barrier (BRB), causing retinal edema, aggravating the progress of RVO, and even causing neovascularization, vitreous hemorrhage and neovascular glaucoma [6]. Under conditions of ischemia or hypoxia, one or more angiogenic factors released by the retina stimulate the formation of retinal capillaries. The formation of RVO is mainly related to the disruption of the balance between proangiogenic and inhibitory factors, while VEGF is an important pathogenic factor that stimulates endothelial cell proliferation and neovascularization. As retinal ischemia and hypoxia lead to insufficient retinal perfusion, the expression of VEGF increases when the body is under hypoxia. VEGF can cause the destruction of the external barrier by reducing the expression of the seal protein tight junction protein, and VEGF can also destroy the connection between retinal capillary endothelial cells to induce the formation of new blood vessels [7]. VEGFA mediates the high permeability of blood vessels by increasing the permeability between cells and adjacent cells, and its expression increases under stress conditions of retinal ischemia or hypoxia to generate new blood vessels. The generation of retinal neovascularization can also lead to the destruction of BRB, increased vascular wall permeability, and the accumulation of fluid inside and outside retinal cells, thereby causing ME [8].

In RVO-associated ME (RVO-ME), RVO induces chronic inflammation of the retinal microvascular system, leading to an increase in inflammatory mediators, increased vascular permeability, and disruption of BRB, promoting the occurrence of ME [9]. Tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α is one of the earliest cytokines with permeability released in the inflammatory site, because it is the only cytokine stored in Mast cell granules that can be immediately released through Mast cell degranulation. Lee et al. have shown that TNF-α not only destroys the internal barrier of BRB, but also increases the permeability of Retinal pigment epithelium and promotes VEGF expression. However, the interaction between TNF-α and VEGF is complex, which may depend on the inflammatory status of retinal pigment epithelium, which is still controversial with the recurrence of RVO-ME [10]. Interleukin (IL)-1β can induce the expression of VEGF under hypoxia and increase the permeability of blood vessels by destroying the tight junction of endothelial cells. VEGF can also promote the permeability and inflammation of IL-1β on blood vessels and promote RVO-ME [11]. In addition, IL-6 can also promote the expression of VEGF, induce retinal neovascularization, and promote ME production.

Natural products refer to substances that naturally exist in organisms, minerals or the atmosphere without artificial synthesis. They widely exist in nature and are the important origin of many modern drugs. Therefore, it is of great practical significance to study the biological effects of natural products on human health. A study reported that ganoderic acids, the most abundant terpenoid in Shen-qi-wang-mo Granule (SQWMG), a traditional Chinese medicine prescription for the treatment of RVO, may play a protective role through “PI3K-Akt signaling pathway”, “MAPK signaling pathway” and its downstream factors iNOS and TNF-α [12]. It was reported that baicalin alleviated the pyroptosis and inflammatory injury of ARPE-19 cells induced by Aβ through the negative crosstalk of miR-223/NLRP3 in inflammatory signaling, suggesting that baicalin may be a potential candidate for the treatment of age-related macular degeneration [13]. In the progression of diabetic retinopathy, baicalin significantly promoted the proliferation of ARPE-19 cells treated with high glucose, reduced cell apoptosis, and inhibited the release of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and ROS [14].

Baicalin is one of the main Flavonoid isolated from the dried roots of Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi. Although baicalin usually has little toxicity to normal tissues, it has strong anti-inflammatory, antiviral and anti-tumor activities. The pharmacological study of baicalin shows that it has anti-inflammatory, antiviral, free radical scavenging, antioxidant, anti-tumor, immune enhancement and broad-spectrum antibacterial effects. Baicalin ophthalmic preparations are mostly used in the treatment of conjunctivitis, keratitis and trachoma, and have a strong antibacterial and bactericidal effect on common ophthalmic pathogens. Recently, it has been reported that drugs containing baicalin can improve retinal edema in RVO model by promoting the recanalization of occluded vein, improving microcirculation and regulating the expression of VEGF, and save retinal structure and function [15]. In addition, baicalin can reduce apoptosis, autophagy and oxidative stress of retinal ganglion cells, and improve retinopathy caused by elevated intraocular pressure in glaucoma mice [16]. However, so far, how baicalin participates in and regulates the progress of RVO needs further research. Therefore, this study explores the specific mechanism of baicalin improving RVO in rats in vivo and in vitro.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

The ARPE-19 cells (ARPE-19 is a spontaneously arising retinal pigment epithelia cell line derived from the normal eyes of a 19-year-old male who died from head trauma. It is widely used to study the function of human retinal pigment epithelium, including simulating age-related macular degeneration and diabetic retinopathy) were gained from the China center for type culture collection. Cells were cultured in DMEM (Sigma) containing 10% FBS and penicillin–streptomycin solution. The OE-HIF-1α and vector were purchased from RiboBio Co., Ltd (Guangzhou, China). The si-HIF-1α, si-VEGFA and scramble were purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology. In addition, we treated ARPE-19 cells with CoCl2 at a concentration of 200 μmol/L for 24 h, and then performed subsequent experiments.

Animals

A total of 24 SD rats (6–8 weeks; 200 ± 20 g; Wuhan Experimental Animal Center, China) were housed in SPF environment. The rats were randomly grouped as follows (n = 6 per group): control group (normal feeding without any intervention), BRVO group, BRVO + saline group (intravitreal injection of physiological saline) and BRVO + baicalin group (50 μg/mL baicalin intravitreal injection). BRVO modeling steps are as follows: rats were anaesthetized by intraperitoneal injection with 10% chloral hydrate (3.5 mL/kg), and Rose Bengal (30 mg/mL) was injected into the rat tail vein 1 min before laser applied. Additionally, the right eye was dilated with 0.5% tropicamide. Then, bifurcation of the retinal vein secondary vasculature was found using a Micron IV Retinal Imaging Microscope, and 50 laser spots were applied with the set parameters (power: 100 mW, duration: 0.2 s; spot size: 50 μm). This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Taizhou People’s Hospital (KY 2023-170-01).

Western blotting

The protein in cells and tissues were extracted by RIPA lysis buffer. Then, proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes. The membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies after blocked with 5% nonfat milk for 2 h at room temperature. GAPDH (1:3000), HIF-1α (1:1000) and VEGFA (1:1000). Then, the membrane was incubated with HRP biotinylated second antibody for 1 h, and protein bands were visualized using chemiluminescent agents, and protein expression levels were quantified in ImageJ software.

MTT assay

Cells were first cultured in 96 well plates, and then cell transfection was performed according to experimental grouping. After transfection, the cell culture supernatant was discarded and MTT solution was added in each well and incubated at 37 °C for 4 h, and then the absorbance of each well was measured and recorded at 490 nm.

Flow cytometry

The cell culture medium was collected and rinsed twice with PBS. Next, the cell density (1 × 105 cells/mL) was adjusted and 100 μL cell suspension was aspirated to inoculate into a cell culture flask, and then the cells were incubated with annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide in a dark environment. Finally, binding buffer was added in the culture tube, and flow cytometry was used to determine apoptosis rate.

ELISA

The ELISA kit (Sigma) was used to detect the secretion levels of TNF-α and IL-1β in cell supernatant and retinal tissues. And the secretion of malondialdehyde (MDA) in cells were detected by corresponding kits (Sigma).

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) content detection

Cells were seeded in culture flasks, and after 24 h of culture, cells were incubated with DCFH-DA in incubator. Next, cells were digested with pancreatin and a cell suspension was obtained. The cell suspension was centrifuged at 1000×g for 10 min, and rinsed twice with PBS, then the cells were obtained after centrifugation for flow cytometry detection.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assay

Cells were rinsed 2 times with PBS and then lysed extensively by adding RIPA lysis buffer. Next, the cells were collected and centrifuged to obtain the supernatant, which was then incubated with the corresponding antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Then, protein A agarose beads were added to obtain antigen antibody complexes and incubated overnight at 4 °C on a shaker. Next, the agarose bead antigen antibody complexes were collected by transient centrifugation and rinsed with PBS. Free antigen, antibody, and beads were obtained by adding protein loading buffer to the complexes and boiling. Finally, the expression levels of the obtained proteins were detected by Western blotting.

Hematoxylin and eosin staining

Retinal tissue sections were fixed in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 48 h, followed by decalcification using a 20% buffered EDTA solution. Subsequently, the tissues were embedded in paraffin, stained with hematoxylin and eosin (Sigma-Aldrich), and photographed under a microscope (Zeiss, Germany).

TUNEL staining

The retinal tissue sections were dewaxed according to the procedure and the excess water was dumped. A histochemical pen was used to draw a circle around the tissue, and proteinase K working solution was added to cover the tissue in the circle, and the tissue was incubated at 37 °C for 20 min. Next, the slides were washed three times in PBS on a shaker for 5 min each time, and after the sections were dried, the membrane breaking working solution was dropped in the circle to cover the tissue, and incubated for 20 min at room temperature, and then the slides were washed three times in PBS on a shaker for 5 min each time. Then, after the sections were dried, buffer was added to cover the tissue in the circle and incubated at room temperature for 10 min. Then shake off the equilibration solution, mix the TUNEL reaction and add it to the covering tissue in the circle, and place the sections flat in the wet box and incubate them in the 37 °C incubator for 1 h. Subsequently, PBS was washed three times for 5 min each time, and then DAPI was added to develop the color at room temperature for 1 min. Next, the sections were dried and sealed with anti-fluorescence quencher and observed by microscope.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software (ver. 21.0; SPSS, Chicago, IL). The quantitative data derived from three independent experiments were expressed as mean ± SD. Data between multiple groups were performed with ANOVA. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Baicalin alleviates BRVO in rats

The fundus photography results showed that the retinal vein blood vessels in the normal group were evenly distributed with radial alternating distribution, and the choroidal blood vessels were faintly visible, while the retinal vein blood flow in the model group was interrupted, and the terminal veins were dilated. After treatment with baicalin, the retinal vein ends were tortuous, the blood vessels were radial, and the diameter morphology recovered evenly (Fig. 6A). HE staining results showed that the ganglion cell layers of the control group were neatly arranged with high-density retinal ganglion cells. The ganglion cell layers of the model group and the saline treated group were disorderly arranged, and the retinal ganglion cells were reduced. After baicalin treatment, the arrangement of the ganglion cell layers was improved, and the retinal ganglion cells were increased (Fig. 6B). In addition, compared with the model group, the protein expression of HIF-1α and VEGFA was decreased in retinal tissue after baicalin treatment (Fig. 6C). Similarly, the number of apoptotic cells (Fig. 6D), the content of pro-inflammatory factors (Fig. 6E) and ROS (Fig. 6F) in retinal tissue were reduced after baicalin treatment. Moreover, we found that compared with the control group, the protein expression of HIF-1α and VEGFA in the retinal tissue of the model group rats was significantly increased, but after intravitreal injection of baicalin or ranibizumab, the protein expression of HIF-1α and VEGFA was reduced (Supplementary Fig. 1A). In addition, compared with the model group rats, baicalin or ranibizumab treatment significantly reduced the production of inflammatory factors and ROS in the retinal tissues (Supplementary Fig. 1B, C). Discussion

The relationship between hypoxia and Angiogenesis was first recognized in tumor Angiogenesis. Many evidences show that ischemia and hypoxia are the basic cause of retinal neovascularization. Originally, Angiogenesis was a normal physiological phenomenon in the process of wound healing and embryonic development, but it mainly played a pathological role in proliferative retinopathy, tumors and other diseases. The biphasic regulatory effect of retinal neovascularization in RVO is manifested in: on the one hand, RVO causes retinal hypoxia without perfusion, and retinal neovascularization provides metabolic nutrients for the local retinal tissue in its distribution area, temporarily maintaining oxygen supply balance [17]. On the other hand, due to the characteristics of incomplete wall, high brittleness, and strong permeability of new blood vessels, they continue to proliferate as the condition progresses, leading to dilation of the lumen and easy rupture and bleeding, which in turn pulls the retina, leading to retinal damage and even detachment. In the regulation of intraocular cell proliferation by various cells and their secreted cytokines, ischemia and hypoxia disrupt the balance between angiogenic stimulating factors and angiogenic inhibitory factors, especially the high expression of VEGF [18, 19]. When VEGF specifically binds to its receptor, it activates intracellular reactions that can cause endothelial cell proliferation, increase local vascular permeability, and ultimately lead to the growth of new blood vessels in normal retinal tissue.

Hypoxia in retinal tissue first activates and increases the expression of HIF-1α, which can lead to high expression of VEGF through a series of cascade reactions [20, 21]. HIF-1 is a nucleoprotein with transcriptional activity. It is the only transcription factor that has been found to play an active role under specific hypoxia. It can regulate the expression of a series of hypoxia related factors, and is the core regulatory factor to maintain oxygen homeostasis. It acts as a common pathway for hypoxia information transmission in all hypoxia transcription processes, especially in the vascular growth signal pathway. HIF-1 is mainly composed of two subunits, α and β. HIF-1α is the main oxygen regulatory subunit and functional subunit, which determines the expression level and stability of HIF-1 transcriptional activity. HIF-1β is an inherent nuclear protein, which is not regulated by intracellular oxygen concentration. It is a structural subunit of HIF-1, which is involved in maintaining the stability of HIF-1α structure and maintaining the functional activity of HIF-1.

The pathogenesis of ME secondary to RVO has been extensively studied and is believed to be mediated by both VEGF and inflammatory cytokines. VEGF is produced by hypoxic cells. It increases the phosphorylation of intercellular tight junction proteins (such as occluding, claudins, junctional adhesion molecules, Zonula Occludens-1) by binding to receptors on capillary endothelial cells, which increases vascular permeability, and promotes the rearrangement of actin filaments in the cytoplasm, leading to neovascularization. Campochiaro et al. showed that the level of VEGF in aqueous humor of RVO patients increased significantly, suggesting that the excessive production of VEGF is the main cause of ME [18]. Subsequently, several studies confirmed that the levels of VEGF in vitreous humor and aqueous humor of RVO patients were significantly higher than those in the control group, and were significantly related to the severity of ME. In addition, inflammatory factors also play a role in the occurrence and development of ME secondary to RVO [22]. Yong et al. pointed out that the levels of IL-6 and VEGF in the aqueous humor of RVO patients significantly increased. After anti-VEGF therapy, the average recurrence time of ME was 43.5 days, and the level of IL-6 in aqueous humor was negatively correlated with the recurrence time of ME [23]. Neo et al. demonstrated for the first time that gene transcription related to angiogenesis and proinflammatory mediators was significantly up-regulated in the Gene expression profiling analysis of rabbit RVO model [24].

Flavonoid are widely distributed bioactive components in plants, which can eliminate ROS in vivo and in vitro, and have antioxidant potential. In this study, we found that the production of ROS and MDA in ARPE-19 cells stimulated by CoCl2 increased, and the level of inflammation increased, but baicalin could significantly reduce the levels of ROS and MDA, and inhibit the inflammatory response, which indicated that baicalin could enhance the antioxidant capacity of ARPE-19 cells and reduce the release of proinflammatory cytokines. In previous studies, other bioactive substances have also been reported to have better improvement effects on BRVO. It was reported that Luteolin significantly increased the activities of superoxide dismutase and GSH-PX in ARPE-19 cells treated with H2O2, and decreased the levels of ROS and MDA in cells [25]. In addition, hesperetin effectively protects ARPE-19 cells from HO induced oxidative damage by inhibiting cell apoptosis, excessive ROS production, and MDA formation [26]. Under the intervention of ethanol extract of Tribulus terrestris, ROS generation and cleaved caspase-3 expression in ARPE-19 cells treated with H2O2 were decreased, which was mediated by activating PI3K/Akt-Nrf2 signaling pathway [27]. Coincidentally, fisetin alleviated oxidative stress injury in ARPE-19 cells through Nrf2 mediated activation of heme oxygenase-1 [28]. Another randomized controlled clinical trial showed that the effect of Lingqi Huangban granule combined with intravitreal injection of ranibizumab in the therapy of RVO-ME was better than that of intravitreal injection of ranibizumab alone [29]. Previous studies further confirmed that baicalin plays a protective role in BRVO by inhibiting apoptosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, which is similar to the recognized anti-VEGF therapy. Kida et al. showed that after anti-VEGF therapy in patients with RVO-ME, the retinal vein pressure of patients was decreased [30]. It was also reported that the proportion of inflammatory cells in the serum of patients with RVO-ME who received anti-VEGF therapy was reduced [31]. In addition, after intravitreal injection of ranibizumab, researchers found that the levels of VEGF, monocyte chemotactic protein 1, IL-8 and IL-6 in aqueous humor of RVO-ME patients were significantly reduced, and ranibizumab significantly reduced the central retinal thickness [23].

In conclusion, our research results show that baicalin enhances the anti-apoptosis, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects of ARPE-19 cells by inhibiting the HIF-1α/VEGFA axis, and improves BRVO in rats. This new discovery may have some reference significance for the pathogenesis and treatment of BRVO. In addition, this study also has some shortcomings that need to be improved. For example, in the in vivo study, increase the animal sample size, set more concentration of baicalin treatment group, long-term study on the in vivo efficacy and safety of baicalin, and increase the combination treatment group of baicalin and current anti-VEGF therapy to explore whether it has synergistic effect. In short, we need to further study the transformation potential of baicalin in the clinical environment to determine whether baicalin can supplement the existing BRVO therapy. This part of the study will be carried out in subsequent trials to verify the results of this paper. In this study, baicalin exerted the protective effect on retina by mediating the inhibition of HIF-1α/VEGFA axis. Currently, increasing evidence indicates that NF-κB or pPI3K/Akt pathways are widely involved in cell survival and inflammation in the progression of various diseases, and baicalin has also been confirmed to be involved in regulating these pathways. Therefore, it is a good research direction to further explore the NF-κB or PI3K/Akt pathway in the regulation of RVO progression by baicalin, which can be used as our follow-up research ideas.

Influencing factors and mediating effects of advanced medical care plan readiness in young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer based on machine learning
Authors: Xuemei Yang, Ting Wei, Yuan Ye, Xiang Liang, Jinlan Huang, Xin Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo examine the factors influencing advance care planning (ACP) readiness and its correlation with disease benefit perception and social support perception among young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer, as well as to analyze the mediating effect of disease benefit perception on the relationship between social support and ACP readiness.MethodsA total of 432 young and middle-aged in patients with advanced cancer from a tertiary cancer hospital in Guangxi were selected through convenience sampling from October 2024 to September 2025. The patients were assessed using a general information questionnaire, an advanced care plan readiness scale, a disease benefit sense scale, and a comprehension social support scale. First, the variables were assessed using a combination of LASSO regression and random forest techniques. Subsequently, the selected variables were incorporated into a multiple linear regression model to examine the determinants of ACP readiness, with the robustness of the findings evaluated through the Bootstrap resampling method. On this basis, mediation analysis was used to test the mediating effect of disease benefit perception on the understanding of social support and ACP readiness, and gender subgroup analysis was conducted.ResultsThe readiness score of advance medical care plan for young and middle-aged advanced cancer patients was (76.38 ± 14.40), the disease benefit sense score was (72.50 ± 14.65), and the comprehension of social support was (52.23 ± 12.99). The outcomes of machine learning feature selection indicated that social support, perception of disease benefits, and awareness of advance care planning (ACP) significantly influence ACP readiness. Multiple linear regression analysis showed that ACP readiness was significantly reduced (β = −6.43, P < 0.001), disease benefit (β = 0.33, P < 0.001) and social support (β = 0.49, P < 0.001) were independent positive influencing factors of ACP readiness. The findings from the mediation analysis indicated that the perception of benefit derived from the disease served as a partial mediator between perceived social support and readiness for advance care planning, exhibiting an indirect effect of 0.126 (95% CI: 0.079–0.182), which constitutes 20.0% of the overall effect. The subgroup analysis showed that this mediating effect was significant in both male and female patients and was slightly higher in males than in females.ConclusionThe readiness for ACP in young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer is moderate. Recognizing that social support not only directly influences the preparedness of ACP programs but also serves as a partial mediator through the perceived benefits derived from the disease. Healthcare professionals ought to enhance the development of patients’ social support networks within clinical settings and focus on elevating the perceived benefits associated with their conditions. This approach aims to foster patients’ inclination to engage in ACP and to refine the quality of care provided at the end of life.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12904-026-02156-3.Keywords: Young and middle-aged, Cancer, Advanced care planning, Benefit finding, Perceived social support

Introduction

The data indicates a year-on-year escalation in the incidence of cancer cases globally. Two thousand twenty In China, the incidence of newly diagnosed cancer cases stands at approximately 450,000, while the number of fatalities reaches around 300,000. This accounts for 23.7% of the global cancer incidence and 30.2% of the mortality rate for that year. In recent years, the prevalence of cancer has progressively shifted to affect younger populations [1, 2]. In the demographic of young and middle-aged individuals afflicted by cancer, the weight of the disease is notably more pronounced, accompanied by significant challenges such as excessive medical intervention and insufficient readiness for death considerations [3]. Young and middle-aged patients frequently carry familial and social obligations, which can lead to a heightened tendency for emotional avoidance and conflicts in decision-making when confronted with disease progression and end-of-life considerations. This demographic thus represents a particularly vulnerable group in the promotion of advance care planning (ACP) [4, 5].

ACP serves as a proactive health strategy that enhances the quality of life for patients nearing the end of their journey, simultaneously mitigating excessive medical interventions and the associated costs of end-of-life care [6, 7]. The effective implementation of ACP depends not only on the provision of medical information, but also on the patient’s psychological readiness for future medical issues and their willingness to participate in discussions [8, 9]. Readiness for advanced care planning relates to individual pairs. The propensity to recognize and adopt an ACP standard. The degree of readiness is evident in the patient’s involvement [8]. Therefore, it is of great significance to explore the key factors that affect ACP readiness to promote ACP discussions among patients with advanced cancer.

Previous studies have shown that psychological and social factors play a key role in the formation of ACP readiness [10, 11]. Understanding social support pertains to an individual’s personal perception regarding the accessibility of assistance from family, friends, and healthcare professionals. This perception has been demonstrated to have a significant correlation with psychological adaptation, involvement in health decision-making, and the dynamics of doctor-patient communication among patients with chronic illnesses [11]. Within the framework of ACP, an elevated perception of social support can enhance patients’ emotional security and communication capabilities, enabling more effective dialogues with their families and healthcare professionals regarding future care preferences, ultimately fostering greater readiness for ACP [12, 13].

At the same time, the perception of advantage arising from illness pertains to the constructive significance and personal development encountered by individuals through cognitive reframing during the experience of a significant health challenge [14]. Based on cognitive adaptation theory, the sense of benefit from illness is considered to be an important psychological mechanism for individuals to change from “disease threat perception” to “meaning integration and future orientation“ [15]. Research indicates that elevated levels of disease benefit not only mitigate the adverse emotional responses linked to the illness but also exhibit a positive correlation with patients’ proactive and strategic behaviors regarding future medical decisions [16, 17]. In patients with advanced cancer, the higher the level of disease benefit, the higher their readiness for prospective care decisions such as ACP [4, 18].

Further research found that understanding social support is an important external resource to promote the formation of disease benefits [19, 20]. Effective social support can facilitate the constructive reconfiguration of patients’ experiences with illness by providing emotional assistance, informational guidance, and affirmation of meaning, ultimately elevating their perception of the benefits derived from their condition [20]. This indicates that during the preparation of ACP, it is essential to recognize that social support may influence not only through direct mechanisms but also indirectly by facilitating the psychological adaptation process associated with the benefits of the disease [17, 21].

Nonetheless, the majority of current research concerning the determinants of ACP readiness predominantly employs conventional statistical techniques for analysis. Conventional regression-based methods are commonly used in this domain but have important limitations when addressing such multidimensional, exploratory objectives. First, they require pre-specification of predictor variables and are less effective at handling multicollinearity among psychosocial factors. Second, they do not inherently address overfitting, which is critical given our relatively limited sample size in this understudied population. Third, the process of variable selection is somewhat contingent upon the researchers’ expertise, potentially hindering a comprehensive identification of the principal influencing factors. In recent years, the application of machine learning methodologies within the realm of medical and nursing research has seen a notable increase, driven by their remarkable stability and predictive prowess in analyzing high-dimensional data and screening feature variables.

This advancement facilitates the identification of intricate variable relationships and enhances the interpretative capabilities of models. Therefore, when studying the influencing factors of ACP readiness, the introduction of machine learning methods can help to screen key variables more objectively and provide a basis for further mechanism analysis.

This study focuses on young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer, employing machine learning algorithms to identify the key factors influencing readiness for advance medical care planning. It subsequently validates these findings through multiple linear regression analysis and investigates the mediating role of disease benefit perception in the relationship between social support and ACP readiness. The aim is to establish a theoretical foundation for nursing professionals to implement ACP interventions, emphasizing the importance of social support and psychological adaptation.

Methods

Research subjects

Cancer patients hospitalized in the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Guangxi Medical University from October 2024 to September 2025 were selected by convenience sampling method.

Inclusion criteria

Patients diagnosed with cancer through clinicopathological or cytological methods, exhibiting invasion of adjacent tissues, regional lymph nodes, and distant metastases; individuals aged 18 to 59 years; possessing clear consciousness and effective communication; and who have provided informed consent to participate in this study.

Exclusion criteria

Involvement of peer investigators; Individuals with significant disorders such as cardiovascular, neurological, pulmonary, or renal conditions; Patients experiencing the acute phase of severe physical sickness or in critical condition during the inquiry. The sample size calculation for the structural equation model indicates that it should be at least 10 to 15 times the number of observed variables. This research includes a total of 26 variables, and accounting for a 20% attrition rate, the final sample comprises 432 participants. The hospital ethics committee has evaluated this research (KY2024012).

Research methods

Survey tools

General information questionnaire

The initial section encompasses sociodemographic details such as age, gender, educational attainment, residence, marital status, occupation, per capita family income, religious affiliations, methods of medical payment, experiences of bereavement, and awareness of ACP. The subsequent section pertains to disease-related information, which includes diagnosis, progression of the illness, frequency of hospitalizations, and comprehension of the disease itself.

Patient advance care plan readiness questionnaire(ACP readiness questionnaire for patients with chronic diseases)

This scale was developed by Wang Xinru [22] in 2019 based on the Chinese cultural context. It is used to assess the level of readiness among patients with chronic diseases to participate in Advance Care Planning (ACP) and has been widely applied in populations such as those with chronic heart failure and advanced cancer [22]. The scale consists of 22 items across three dimensions: attitude (10 items), belief (7 items), and motivation (5 items). A 5-point Likert scale is used, ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree,” with scores from 1 to 5, respectively. The 10 items in the attitude dimension are reverse-scored, while the 12 items in the belief and motivation dimensions are positively scored. Total scores range from 22 to 110, with higher scores indicating greater readiness for ACP. The scale further categorizes ACP readiness into four levels: low (22–43), moderately low (44–65), moderately high (66–87), and high (88–110), see Supplementary Materials Table S1.

Disease benefit perception scale(Benefit Finding Scale, BFS)

This scale was developed by Weaver et al. [23] in 2008 into a 22-item multidimensional structure, confirming that a multidimensional model—including domains such as acceptance and family relationships—has advantages over the traditional unidimensional structure. The Chinese version was cross-culturally adapted by Liu Zhunzhun et al. [24] in 2015 and is suitable for assessing the positive psychological changes and benefit experiences perceived by individuals facing major illness or adversity. The scale consists of 22 items across six dimensions: acceptance (items 1–3, 3 items), family relationships (items 4–5, 2 items), worldview (items 6–9, 4 items), personal growth (items 10–16, 7 items), social relationships (items 17–19, 3 items), and health behaviors (items 20–22, 3 items). A 5-point Likert scale is used, ranging from “not at all” to “very much,” with scores from 1 to 5. Total scores range from 22 to 110, with higher scores indicating higher levels of benefit finding, see Supplementary Materials Table S2.

Comprehension of the social support scale (Perceived Social Support Scale, PSSS)

This scale was originally developed by Zimet et al. [25] in 1988, and the Chinese version was translated and revised by Professor Jiang Qianjin [26]. It is a classic tool for measuring an individual’s subjective perception of social support. The scale consists of 12 items across three dimensions: family support (items 3, 4, 8, 11), friend support (items 6, 7, 9, 12), and other support (items 1, 2, 5, 10), which assess the level of support perceived from family, friends, and other significant persons (e.g., leaders, relatives, colleagues), respectively. A 7-point Likert scale is used, ranging from “very strongly disagree” to “very strongly agree,” with scores from 1 to 7. Total scores range from 12 to 84, with higher scores indicating higher levels of perceived social support. Scores of 12–36 indicate low support, 37–60 indicate moderate support, and 61–84 indicate high support, see Supplementary Materials Table S3.

Data collection and quality control methods

Before the survey, the investigator will conduct uniform training for all investigators. Employ paper questionnaires, which are distributed immediately. Investigators provide standardized instructions to clarify the requirements for completing the questionnaires, and individuals experiencing reading difficulties may receive assistance from the investigators in the completion process. Should there be a prevalence of consistent answer options, exceeding two-thirds of the total questions, alongside regular options, such a questionnaire will be deemed invalid and subsequently discarded.

Statistical methods

This investigation used R software (version 4.5.0) for data aggregation and statistical analysis. Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviations, and categorical variables are expressed as frequencies (percentages). Cronbach’s ɑ coefficient was used to evaluate the internal consistency reliability of each scale. To identify the principal factors influencing ACP readiness, the LASSO and random forest methods were initially combined to select variables via 10-fold cross-validation. Following this, the variables that exhibited overlap as identified by the two algorithms were incorporated into a multiple linear regression model to assess their independent impacts on ACP readiness. The robustness of the findings was further scrutinized through Bootstrap resampling, conducted 1,000 times.Consequently, the mediation package in R was employed to perform mediation analysis, and the significance of indirect effects was assessed through the Bootstrap resampling method, executed 5,000 times. In addition, subgroup mediation analyses were conducted by gender to compare mediating effects across sexes. The statistical test level is set to ɑ= 0.05.

Machine learning feature selection

LASSO regression and random forest analysis were conducted with the total ACP readiness score as the dependent variable. LASSO regression analysis revealed significant correlations between literacy, awareness of advance care plans or directives, frequency of hospitalizations, overall perception of illness benefits, and total perceived social support scores with readiness for advance care planning (Fig. 1A-B). Randomized forest analysis showed that the top five important variables were social support perception, disease benefit perception, whether they had heard of advanced care plans or advanced directives, knowledge of the disease, and place of residence (Fig. 1C). The intersection suggests that the total scores for social support and disease benefit, and whether they have heard of advance care plans or advance directives, may be key influencing factors of ACP readiness, which are included as core independent variables in subsequent model testing (Fig. 1D).

Multiple linear regression analysis

A multiple linear regression analysis (OLS) was conducted with the total score of ACP readiness serving as the dependent variable. The independent variables included prior awareness of advance care planning or advance directives, the total score of disease benefit, and the total score of perceived social support. The findings indicated that individuals unfamiliar with advance medical care plans or advance directives exhibited a markedly lower readiness for advance care planning (β=-6.43, P-value < 0.001). Furthermore, there was a significant positive correlation between ACP readiness and both the total score of disease benefit (β = 0.33, p < 0.001) and the total score of social support (β = 0.49, P-value < 0.001). In summary, all three are independent influencing factors of ACP readiness (see Table 3 for details). Bootstrap resampling (n = 1000) showed the same results, as detailed in Table 4.

Mediation analysis

Based on the findings from the literature and the aforementioned multiple linear regression models, a mediation analysis model was developed wherein social support serves as the independent variable, ACP readiness is the dependent variable, and disease benefit perception along with awareness of advance medical care plans or advance medical directives function as mediating variables. The findings from the mediation analysis indicated that using the perception of benefit derived from the disease as the mediating variable revealed a notable direct effect of social support on ACP readiness (β = 0.504, 95% CI: 0.402 ~ 0.600, P-value < 0.001). Additionally, a significant indirect effect was observed through the perception of benefit from the disease (β = 0.126, 95%CI: 0.079 ~ 0.182, P-value < 0.001). It suggests that social support can further promote ACP readiness by improving the sense of benefit from disease, and this sense of benefit partially mediates the relationship between the two, accounting for 20.0% of the total effect (see Fig. 2; Table 5).

Subgroup mediated analysis

The findings indicated that within the female subgroup, social support exerted a notable direct influence on ACP readiness (β = 0.504, 95%CI: 0.365 ~ 0.636, P-value < 0.001). Additionally, a significant indirect effect emerged through the perceived benefits associated with the disease (β = 0.112, 95%CI: 0.058 ~ 0.181, P-value < 0.001), with this indirect effect constituting 18.1% of the overall effect (Table 7). Within the male subgroup, the direct effect was found to be significant (β = 0.499, 95%CI: 0.351 ~ 0.636, P-value < 0.001), alongside a significant indirect effect (β = 0.152, 95%CI: 0.077 ~ 0.253, P-value < 0.001). Notably, the indirect effect constituted 23.4% of the total effect (Table 8), indicating that the perception of benefit from the disease served a partial mediating function in both genders, with a marginally greater impact observed in men compared to women. When using whether or not they had heard of advance care plans or advance directives as mediating variables, there were no significant indirect effects in women (β=-0.002, 95%CI: -0.019 ~ 0.020, P-value = 0.872) and men (β = 0.018, 95%CI: -0.003 ~ 0.044, P-value = 0.108), suggesting that this variable did not play a mediating role in both sexes (Tables 9 and 10). The overall perception of disease benefit served as a mediating mechanism through which social support influenced ACP readiness in both genders, whereas prior awareness of advance care planning or advance directives did not exhibit a significant mediating effect.

Discussion

Current status of ACP readiness and psychosocial characteristics of young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer

The findings of this research indicate that younger and middle-aged individuals diagnosed with advanced cancer exhibit significant characteristics. The preparedness score was moderate, exhibiting a decline compared to earlier research conducted on certain cancer patients [27, 28]. This difference may be closely related to the subjects’ age stage and social role characteristics in this study. Individuals diagnosed with cancer in their youth and middle age often serve as the primary economic contributors within their households. At this pivotal stage, they are tasked with the dual responsibilities of caring for aging relatives while nurturing their own children. Consequently, they tend to exhibit a proactive approach towards treatment decisions, motivated by the desire to regain their health and swiftly resume their familial roles.In contrast, the anticipatory decision-making and advance planning of end-of-life care emphasized by ACP may be seen by some patients as conflicting with their current “treatment-oriented” goals, thereby reducing their ACP readiness. Further gender subgroup analysis showed that the mediating effect of perceived social support on disease benefit perception was stronger in male patients than in female patients. A possible reason is that in the traditional division of family roles, men often assume the primary economic responsibility and decision-making role, and they are more likely to be affected by family responsibilities and social role pressure in the context of illness. When receiving a higher level of social support, male patients are more likely to develop a sense of disease benefit through a positive psychological adaptation process, which further promotes their thinking and preparation for ACP-related issues. Second, the survey was conducted in areas with low economic levels. Prior research indicates that individuals from lower economic backgrounds exhibit a diminished willingness to pursue proactive medical interventions. Economic constraints not only restrict access to medical information but may also lead these individuals to prioritize immediate treatment advantages over considerations for long-term care strategies [29]. Furthermore, this survey was carried out in a secluded region where patients may have encountered it. Their limited understanding, heavily shaped by conventional beliefs regarding life and death, leads to a lack of proactive consideration for end-of-life planning. This observation aligns with findings from studies conducted on cancer patients in the remote areas of Taiwan [30]. The above results reflect the lack of corresponding policies in remote areas. The penetration rate of the concept is notably low, and the absence of pertinent knowledge contributes to a misinterpretation of ACP among patients [31]. This finding is also consistent with the results of multiple linear regression analysis in this study, that is, whether or not ACP has a significant effect on ACP readiness scores. In the analysis of further mediation effects, it was observed that “the understanding of ACP” did not serve as a mediating factor between the perception of social support and ACP readiness. This indicates that the dissemination of ACP knowledge may have a direct impact on patients’ cognition and acceptance of ACP. However, the mechanism by which this occurs differs from the indirect influence of social support on ACP readiness through psychological adaptation processes. This indicates the necessity to advance ACP, which should be disseminated through various publicity initiatives, including the establishment of popular science columns and video promotions, to enhance the public’s cognitive understanding of ACP. Concurrently, it is essential to concentrate on regions with lower economic status and remote areas to facilitate effective intervention and ensure the seamless progression of ACP. This study reveals that the disease benefit score for young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer is positioned at a moderate level. These findings align with observations made in earlier studies involving cancer survivors [32]. As the disease persists, individuals diagnosed with cancer cultivate a more profound comprehension of its progression and potential outcomes. Their capacity for adaptation is heightened, leading to a gradual acceptance of their condition. Throughout this process, certain patients transition from maladaptive coping mechanisms to constructive attitudes, exemplified by the alteration of detrimental lifestyles and the adoption of regular exercise. They derive positive significance from their experiences with illness, consequently cultivating a degree of benefit from their condition [32]. The sense of benefit from illness is a positive psychological response that can reduce negative emotions, promote disease recovery, and ultimately improve patients’ quality of life. It is suggested that medical workers should pay attention to patients’ sense of benefit and psychological state during clinical work, and provide timely psychological counseling and intervention to enhance their sense of benefit from the disease.

Regarding understanding social support, this study found that the score for young and middle-aged advanced cancer patients is moderate, lower than in previous studies [33]. A favourable perception of social support can mitigate the adverse effects of stressful events; yet, it frequently results in diminished social support due to impaired communication between patients and society following experiences of adversity [34]. The damage of body image resulting from surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy may diminish patients’ social confidence and motivation, consequently restricting their social participation [35]. It is worth noting that the average score of family support items in this study was higher than that of friends and other supports, indicating that family is still the most important and stable source of support for young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer after encountering major stressful events. This phenomenon may be closely related to the cultural background, with family as the core, under the influence of traditional Chinese culture, and family members play an irreplaceable role in disease care and decision-making [36]. The aforementioned results indicate that the pivotal role of family in patients’ decision-making processes must be thoroughly highlighted when developing ACP readiness intervention strategies. Furthermore, the creation of a family-centered ACP support model, facilitated by medical personnel and involving relatives and friends, should be investigated to enhance the establishment of a more robust and stable ACP readiness state for patients.

Analysis of influencing factors of ACP readiness in young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer

This research indicates that, utilising machine learning for feature selection alongside multiple linear regression analysis, young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer experience a perception of benefits from their disease. The level of preparedness exhibits a favourable correlation; specifically, an increased perception of advantage stemming from the illness is observed. Superior preparation yields enhanced outcomes. As a positive psychological restructuring process formed by individuals in major illness situations, the sense of benefit from illness usually includes personal growth, changes in health behaviors, and a re-understanding of the meaning of life [37]. The experience of illness compels individuals to adopt a healthier lifestyle as they navigate the uncertainties of disease and the spectre of mortality. This confrontation with their condition stimulates profound reflections on death and illness, leading to a more nuanced comprehension of existence and a more reasoned perspective on life and death [32]. Concurrently, individuals who possess a heightened awareness of their health conditions often exhibit a greater openness to exploring novel approaches and engaging in diverse health practices. Furthermore, discussions surrounding advance care planning are perceived as proactive strategies for health management and represent an active engagement in the medical decision-making process, thereby increasing the likelihood of acceptance and implementation among these patients.

This research additionally revealed a significant correlation between the perception of disease benefit and the perception of social support among young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer. Specifically, it was observed that an increased level of perceived disease benefit corresponds with a heightened sense of social support experienced by the patient. Research indicates that social support serves as a crucial external psychological resource for individuals navigating disease adaptation. It facilitates positive cognitive reevaluation through emotional, informational, and practical support, enabling individuals to derive meaningful insights from their disease experience and to enhance their perception of benefits arising from it [38]. Concurrently, individuals who have experienced beneficial transformations in their psychological well-being and cognitive frameworks during the self-adjustment phase following illness tend to engage more actively with family, friends, and acquaintances. This enhanced communication fosters stronger social ties, leading to an increased perception of care, understanding, and support from both family and society, ultimately resulting in a heightened sense of emotional fulfillment [38]. Therefore, in clinical practice, it is advisable for patients to proactively pursue external support, including assistance from family, relatives, and friends, to enhance their perception of the benefits associated with their condition and to foster a positive mindset in managing their illness.

Furthermore, the findings of this study indicate that social support significantly influences ACP readiness. According to Kubi et al. [39], Research has shown that social support is an important part of external support for cancer patients, helping them relieve negative emotions such as anxiety and helplessness, and improve their psychological adaptability to the disease and future care arrangements. In China’s family-oriented culture, familial support fosters a safer and more empathetic communication environment for patients, encouraging them to engage in discussions regarding disease outcomes, end-of-life care, and ACP topics, thus enhancing their acceptance and preparedness for ACP [39]. Consequently, in the future, emphasis should be placed on the significance of the social support system, and the patient’s relatives and friends should be motivated to actively engage to collaboratively advance ACP preparedness in young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer.

Mediating effect between social support and ACP readiness in young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer

The findings of this study indicate that the perception of disease benefit serves as a partial mediator between the perception of social support and the preparedness for advance medical care plans. Particularly, the comprehension of social support not only positively influences the preparedness for advance medical care plans but also impacts this readiness through the lens of disease benefit perception. This finding is consistent with the theory of positive psychological adaptation in the field of psychology: social support, as a protective factor for an individual’s mental health, is an important psychological resource for coping with the trauma of major illness [40]. Social support can activate patients’ intrinsic resources and mental fortitude, bolstering their ability to confront illness while diminishing negative thought patterns. This enables them to adopt a constructive response strategy and uncover greater benefits from their health challenges [40].

Individuals who derive significant benefit from their illness tend to maintain a more optimistic outlook throughout the progression of their condition. They exhibit a greater propensity to engage in discussions with family, relatives, and friends about disease outcomes, end-of-life care, and mortality, which are subjects of considerable importance in advance care planning [41]. Moreover, the perception of advantage stemming from illness can foster patients’ proactive engagement in medical decision-making and deepen their comprehension of life’s complexities and the uncertainties surrounding disease, consequently augmenting their acceptance and psychological preparedness for advance care planning [42].

The results of this study provide a new perspective for clinical practice: understanding social support through interventions can indirectly enhance the sense of benefit from the disease, thereby improving ACP readiness. Particular approaches involve the creation of a support network centered around the family, ensuring active involvement from relatives and friends; offering material, emotional, and informational assistance to patients; Conduct psychological counseling and health education to ensure that patients experience a sense of respect, understanding, and care. It is imperative to closely monitor the present condition of patients’ disease benefits and to develop tailored intervention plans for ACP readiness.

Study limitations

This study was conducted at a single tertiary cancer hospital in Guangxi, resulting in low sample representativeness and potentially affecting the generalizability of the findings. The study employed a cross-sectional design that failed to capture the temporal evolution of ACP preparedness, nor did it differentiate across various cancer kinds, disease length, or the impact of treatment methods on perceptions of disease benefits and social support. Data collection relies on self-reported questionnaires, which may be biased by social expectations. In addition, although disease duration may potentially influence ACP readiness, we were unable to conduct a formal subgroup analysis based on this variable due to sample size constraints. Future research may employ a multicenter, longitudinal design, integrated with intervention trials, to further validate the mediating role of illness benefit in the association between social support and ACP preparedness. Finally, this study employed convenience sampling from a single hospital in China, which inevitably introduces selection bias and limits the generalizability of our findings. The low ACP awareness rate (14.35%) observed in our sample may reflect region-specific socioeconomic and cultural factors, and thus our results may not be directly transferable to other populations or healthcare settings.

Conclusion

The overall readiness for ACP in young and middle-aged advanced cancer patients was at a moderate level. Recognizing that social support can directly and favorably influence ACP preparedness, while also indirectly influencing it through perceived benefits derived from the illness. In clinical practice, medical personnel must focus on assessing the extent of social support and the perceived benefits of the illness in young and middle-aged patients with advanced cancer. They should enhance patients’ willingness and preparedness to engage in ACP through interventions such as bolstering familial and social support, providing psychological counseling, and delivering health education, thereby improving the quality of end-of-life care.

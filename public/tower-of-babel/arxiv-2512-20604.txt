MoE-DiffuSeq: Enhancing Long-Document Diffusion Models with Sparse Attention and Mixture of Experts
Alexandros Christoforos, Chadbourne Davis

Abstract
In this paper, we present MoE-DiffuSeq, an innovative approach leveraging a Mixture of Experts (MoE) to enhance diffusion models for generating long documents. Traditional diffusion models, such as DiffuSeq, face significant computational inefficiencies and memory constraints when processing extended sequences. To overcome these challenges, we integrate a sparse attention mechanism with the MoE framework, facilitating efficient and scalable sequence generation. Our methodology incorporates a bespoke sparse attention framework designed to minimize computational complexity while preserving text quality and coherence. Additionally, we introduce a tailored soft absorbing state within the diffusion process to optimize the attention architecture, which accelerates sequence reconstruction and enhances precision. Empirical evaluations reveal that MoE-DiffuSeq substantially outperforms existing models in terms of training speed and sampling rates. These improvements are particularly beneficial for managing extended sequences, rendering our model apt for generating scientific articles, managing code repositories, and facilitating long dialogues. Benchmark comparisons further affirm that MoE-DiffuSeq enhances efficiency, speed, accuracy, and expressiveness, thereby advancing the capabilities of diffusion models for high-quality long-form text generation.

Integration of Sparse Attention Mechanism

To address the computational complexities associated with generating extended documents, our model, MoE-DiffuSeq, incorporates a sparse attention mechanism inspired by the Longformer architecture. Traditional Transformer models are well-known for their quadratic computational expense (
O(n^{2})
) relative to the sequence length
n
. This becomes impractical for long sequences due to escalating computational demands and memory constraints.

To mitigate these issues, we implement a sliding window attention mechanism, where each token computes attention weights only for a constrained set of neighboring tokens within a predefined window. This approach effectively reduces the computational complexity to
O(n\times w)
, where
w
denotes the window size, making the processing of long sequences computationally feasible.

The attention computation within our sparse mechanism is defined as follows:

\text{Attention}(Q_{i},K_{j},V_{j})=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_{i}K_{j}^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)V_{j},

where:
-
Q_{i}
,
K_{j}
, and
V_{j}
represent the query, key, and value vectors for tokens
i
and
j
, respectively.
-
d_{k}
is the dimensionality of the key vectors.

This formulation ensures that each query vector
Q_{i}
interacts only with key vectors
K_{j}
within its designated window, significantly streamlining the computational load by focusing processing power where it’s most needed.

Dilated Sliding Window for Long Context

To effectively capture long-range dependencies within extensive text sequences, we have implemented a dilated sliding window mechanism in our MoE-DiffuSeq architecture. This approach does not increase the size of the attention window
w
, yet it expands the effective receptive field by introducing gaps, denoted by
d
, between tokens within the window. This method allows each attention head to process a broader context without a corresponding increase in computational load.

The extended receptive field facilitated by the dilated sliding window is mathematically represented as follows:

\text{Receptive Field}=l\times d\times w,

where:

•

l
represents the number of transformer layers,

•

d
is the dilation factor, determining the gap between tokens in the attention mechanism,

•

w
denotes the window size, specifying the number of tokens each attention head can directly interact with.

This configuration allows the model to integrate information over larger textual spans effectively, thus enhancing its ability to understand and generate coherent long-form content. The dilated window approach ensures that critical linguistic structures, which may be spread out across large segments of text, are captured and processed efficiently.

Global Attention for Key Tokens

MoE-DiffuSeq also employs global attention to specific key tokens, such as the [CLS] token for classification tasks or particular tokens in question-answering contexts. This ensures that these tokens can attend to all other tokens in the sequence, integrating comprehensive contextual information. The global attention mechanism is defined as:

\text{GlobalAttention}(Q_{g},K_{g},V_{g})=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_{g}K_{g}^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)V_{g},

where
Q_{g}
,
K_{g}
, and
V_{g}
are the query, key, and value matrices for tokens designated for global attention.

Incorporation of Mixture of Experts

To further enhance model capacity and efficiency, we incorporate a Mixture of Experts (MoE) framework. Each layer in the transformer is augmented with multiple expert networks, and a gating mechanism dynamically selects a subset of these experts for each input token. The gating function
G(x)
determines the probability
p_{i}
of selecting expert
i
based on the input token
x
:

G(x)=\text{softmax}(W_{g}x),

where
W_{g}
is the gating weight matrix. The output of the MoE layer is a weighted sum of the selected experts’ outputs:

\text{MoE}(x)=\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{i}(x),

where
E_{i}
represents the
i
-th expert.

Adaptation of Diffusion Processes

We adapt the diffusion process from DiffuSeq to incorporate Gaussian noise and a discrete absorbing state. The forward diffusion process is modified as:

z_{t}=\sqrt{\overline{\alpha}_{t}}z_{0}+\sqrt{1-\overline{\alpha}_{t}}\epsilon,

where
\epsilon
denotes Gaussian noise, and
z_{t}
is the state at time
t
. A discrete absorbing state
m
is introduced probabilistically, enhancing the model’s ability to manage the granularity of textual data.

Joint Denoising and Loss Optimization

During the reverse process, a joint denoising strategy reconstructs the data from both continuous and discrete noise. The loss function for this process is formulated as:

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{t=2}^{T}\|\text{EMB}(w_{t})-f_{\theta}(z_{t},t)\|^{2}+R(\|z_{0}\|),

where
EMB
is the embedding function converting discrete tokens into continuous embeddings, and
f_{\theta}
is the denoising function. The regularization term
R(\|z_{0}\|)
ensures stability and quality in the generative process.

Consistency in Sampling and Inference

To ensure consistency between training and inference, the same noise model is used in both phases. The reverse diffusion process during sampling is calculated through an integral formulation:

z_{t}=z_{s}+\int_{s}^{t}e^{f_{\theta}(z,d)}\,dd,

where
z_{s}
represents the state at time
s
, and
e^{f_{\theta}(z,d)}
denotes the exponentiated output of the denoising function. This integral is evaluated using the Euler method, ensuring numerical stability and accurate sequence reconstruction.

Diffusion Models for Text Generation

Diffusion models have emerged as a robust framework for generative processes, particularly in the domain of text generation, where models like DiffuSeq exemplify their potential. These models operate through a two-phase approach: the forward diffusion process and the reverse diffusion process.

Forward Diffusion Process

During the forward diffusion phase, an initial data point,
x_{0}
, undergoes a transformation into a noise-dominated state through a sequence of intermediate states
x_{t}
for
t=1,2,\ldots,T
, where
T
signifies the total number of timesteps. The mathematical representation of this process is given by:

q(x_{t}\mid x_{t-1})=\mathcal{N}(x_{t};\sqrt{1-\beta_{t}}x_{t-1},\beta_{t}I),

where
\beta_{t}
denotes the variance schedule, typically a small, incrementally increasing positive value. As
t
increases,
x_{t}
progressively approaches a purely noisy state, ultimately reaching
x_{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
, symbolizing a standard Gaussian distribution.

Reverse Diffusion Process

The objective of the reverse diffusion process is to reconstruct the original data from its noisy counterpart. This reconstruction is facilitated by a learned denoising function
f_{\theta}(x_{t},t)
, which predicts
x_{t-1}
from
x_{t}
:

p_{\theta}(x_{t-1}\mid x_{t})=\mathcal{N}(x_{t-1};f_{\theta}(x_{t},t),\sigma_{t}^{2}I),

where
\sigma_{t}
represents a learned variance parameter. The process iteratively refines
x_{T}
, moving towards
x_{0}
to effectively reverse the noise addition.

Handling Discrete States

In scenarios where the states are discrete, such as text tokens, each state
x_{t}
is treated as a distinct entity. The forward diffusion might employ a categorical distribution, while the reverse process may involve transitions between discrete states, often facilitated by methods like masking or token substitution.

This dual-phase diffusion approach not only allows for the effective generation of high-quality text that captures intricate dependencies but also adapts well to the inherent complexities of structured and unstructured data. By leveraging the diffusion model framework within the DiffuSeq architecture, we can address significant challenges in text generation, particularly those associated with the management of long sequences and the preservation of semantic integrity.

Mixture of Experts (MoE) in Text Generation

The Mixture of Experts (MoE) paradigm revolutionizes large-scale neural network architectures by dynamically allocating computational resources among specialized sub-models, or ”experts,” to process each input. This modular approach significantly enhances scalability and adaptability, particularly when managing diverse and complex tasks.

Mathematical Representation

An MoE model is mathematically represented as:

y=\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}(x)E_{i}(x),

where
N
denotes the total number of experts,
g_{i}(x)
is the gating function that allocates weights to the
i
-th expert
E_{i}
, based on the input
x
. The gating function is designed to produce a sparse output, ensuring that only a relevant subset of experts is activated for any given input, thereby optimizing computational resources.

Gating Mechanism

The gating mechanism, pivotal for the efficiency of MoE, is expressed as:

g_{i}(x)=\frac{\exp(h_{i}(x))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(h_{j}(x))},

where
h_{i}(x)
is a learnable scoring function that evaluates the suitability of each expert for the current input. This mechanism promotes sparsity and specialization by activating only the most relevant experts, thus reducing unnecessary computations.

Application in Long Text Generation

In the context of long text generation, MoE models are particularly advantageous. They dynamically allocate processing power to handle various segments of a text, adapting to the unique demands of each segment. This ability is crucial for generating coherent and contextually accurate extended texts, such as detailed scientific articles, elaborate narratives, and comprehensive reports.

By integrating MoE into the DiffuSeq framework, our approach—MoE-DiffuSeq—not only leverages the inherent strengths of diffusion models but also introduces a scalable method to manage the increased computational demands of processing lengthy documents. This integration ensures that our model can efficiently handle sequences of considerable length without sacrificing the quality of the generated content.

This dynamic allocation of computational resources, tailored to the specific requirements of different text segments, ensures that our generative process remains both efficient and effective, making MoE-DiffuSeq a cutting-edge solution in the realm of advanced text generation systems.

Sparse Attention Mechanism

Efficiently managing long text sequences presents a significant challenge in natural language processing. The sparse attention mechanism, exemplified by architectures like Longformer, directly addresses this by reducing the computational overhead traditionally associated with the self-attention mechanism of Transformer models. This reduction is achieved by altering the complexity from
O(n^{2})
to
O(n\times w)
, where
n
represents the sequence length and
w
denotes the window size.

Mathematical Formulation

The sparse attention mechanism is mathematically defined as:

\text{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)V,

where
Q
,
K
, and
V
represent the query, key, and value matrices respectively, and
d_{k}
is the dimensionality of the key vectors. In this configuration, each token computes attention weights only for a subset of tokens within a predefined window, significantly reducing the number of operations involved.

Implementation of Sparse Attention

Sparse attention is implemented as:

\text{Sparse Attention}(Q,K,V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{W}(i)}\text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_{i}K_{j}^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)V_{j},

where
\mathcal{W}(i)
specifies the set of indices within the attention window of the token
i
. This method efficiently limits the scope of attention, focusing computational resources where they are most needed.

Enhancements for Long Context

To capture dependencies over longer distances without increasing computational demands, dilated attention is utilized:

\mathcal{W}(i)=\{i-k\cdot d,\ldots,i+k\cdot d\},

where
d
represents the dilation factor, enhancing the model’s ability to integrate information over larger textual spans without a corresponding increase in the window size.

Additionally, global attention tokens are incorporated to provide context integration across the entire sequence. This feature is particularly valuable in tasks that require an overarching understanding of the full text, such as in document summarization or complex question-answering scenarios.

Advantages of Sparse Attention

Integrating sparse attention mechanisms allows models to efficiently process extensive documents while ensuring the preservation of text quality and coherence. This approach not only mitigates the quadratic scaling issue but also enhances the model’s capability to generate contextually rich and coherent long-form text. By focusing computational resources judiciously, sparse attention mechanisms facilitate deeper and more effective text analysis and generation, especially in applications involving substantial textual data.

Irreducible groups and ergodicity in the boundary
Subhadip Dey, Sebastian Hurtado

Abstract
. We show that if G G is a real semi-simple Lie group, and Γ \Gamma is a discrete subgroup of G G containing a subgroup Σ \Sigma acting ergodically (in a strong sense) on the Furstenberg boundary of G G , then Γ \Gamma is not isomorphic to a free product of Σ \Sigma with ℤ \mathbb{Z} . Moreover, if Σ \Sigma has algebraic entries, then Γ \Gamma has algebraic entries as well. As a consequence, we show that if all irreducible discrete subgroups of SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) × SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) act ergodically on 𝕊 1 × 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1} , such groups cannot be free groups (or even Gromov hyperbolic). In the appendix, we discuss a connection between the existence of discrete irreducible groups and diophantine properties of Lie groups.

1.
Introduction

Discrete subgroups of semi-simple Lie groups have been intensively studied for more than a century. However, a fairly comprehensive understanding of discrete subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
(the Kleinian groups, or equivalently the fundamental groups of complete hyperbolic three-orbifolds) has emerged only over the past three decades.
The classification of finitely generated Kleinian groups was completed through the proof of Marden’s tameness Conjecture by Agol
[
1
]
and independently by Calegari-Gabai
[
10
]
, together with the proof of the Ending Lamination conjecture by Brock, Canary, Minsky
[
21
,
8
]
.

Nonetheless, for general semi-simple Lie groups, even for
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
, a classification of finitely generated discrete subgroups, if such a classification is possible at all, remains far out of reach. Moreover, for higher-rank Lie groups, there is a striking scarcity of “geometrically infinite” examples.

For example, consider
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. To the best of our knowledge, the only known examples of discrete subgroups are either irreducible lattices (all arithmetic by Margulis) or groups whose projection to one
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
factor is discrete. The latter class includes direct products of discrete subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(and their subgroups), as well as subgroups that are Anosov (or, more generally, relatively Anosov, transverse, and so on).

The following question attributed to Yves Benoist (see the historical discussion in
[
9
]
) appears to be still open:

Question 1.1
.

Suppose a subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is discrete and
irreducible
(i.e. projects densely in each
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
factor), can
\Gamma
be isomorphic to a free group?

The only known examples of irreducible discrete subgroups as far as we know are arithmetic lattices; see the recent works by
[
9
]
,
[
16
]
for related questions. Irreducible subgroups can be shown by an elementary argument to act minimally on the Furstenberg boundary
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and also ergodically on each
\mathbb{S}^{1}
factor.

A corollary of our main result is the following:

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose every irreducible discrete subgroup of
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts ergodically on the boundary
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

No irreducible discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
is hyperbolic (e.g.,
\Gamma
cannot be free).

(ii)

If
\Gamma
is a hyperbolic discrete subgroup of
G
, then
\Gamma
is virtually a free group or a surface group (e.g.,
\Gamma
can’t be
\pi_{1}(S)*\mathbb{Z}
, for a closed hyperbolic surface
S
).

(iii)

If
M
is a finite volume hyperbolic 3-manifold, then
\pi_{1}(M)
does not admit a discrete and faithful homomorphism into
G
.

1.1.
Our results

For the rest of the article, we will let
G
denote a connected noncompact real semi-simple Lie group with finite center, let
B
a maximal connected amenable subgroup of
G
and let us call the quotient
G/B
the
Furstenberg boundary
of
G
.

We make the following definitions:

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a subgroup
\Gamma
of a real semi-simple Lie group
G
has
algebraic traces
(respectively,
integral traces
) if for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the trace of
\text{Ad}(\gamma)
(the image of
\gamma
under the adjoint representation) is an algebraic number (respectively, algebraic integer).

Definition 1.4
.

We say that a subgroup
\Gamma
of a real semi-simple Lie group
G
has
algebraic entries
(respectively,
integral entries
) if for some faithful representation
\rho:G\to GL(V)
in a real vector space and some appropriate basis of
V
, the image
\rho(\Gamma)
consists of matrices with entries in the algebraic numbers
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
(respectively, entries in the algebraic integers). This property is independent of the representation, see
2.4
.

Definition 1.5
.

We will use the following notation, which is not standard. We say that a subgroup
\Sigma<G
acts
strongly ergodically
on the Furstenberg boundary if every finite index subgroup of
\Sigma
acts ergodically on the Furstenberg boundary of
G
. (See Definition
2.1
.)

Our results fits into the general philosophy that discrete subgroups of semi-simple Lie groups which are sufficiently large have algebraic constraints (both group theoretic and arithmetic). Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated Zariski dense discrete subgroup of
G
, and suppose that
\Sigma<\Gamma
is a subgroup such that
\Sigma
acts strongly ergodically on the Furstenberg boundary
G/B
. Then:

(i)

If
\Sigma
has algebraic entries, then
\Gamma
has algebraic entries.

(ii)

If
\Sigma
has integral entries, then
\Gamma
has integral entries.

(iii)

For every non-trivial
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the natural homomorphism from the abstract free product
\langle\gamma\rangle*\Sigma\to\Gamma
is not faithful.

The main ideas in the proof of our theorems come from the work by Chatterji-Venkataramana
[
11
]
, and by now classical ideas in the proofs of local rigidity and super-rigidity of lattices. In fact, Chatterji-Venkataramana proved under related hypothesis that the group
\Gamma
must be super-rigid (which is much stronger than we prove). Nonetheless, we also provide in Appendix
A
an alternative geometric proof of Item (3) in some special cases, by a more geometric argument.

We will now discuss some consequences of our work, and highlight some questions for further research.

1.2.
Subgroups of products containing diagonal lattices

To the best of our knowledge, the following question is open:

Question 1.7
.

Let
G
be a noncompact real semi-simple Lie group with trivial center and let
\Sigma\subset G
be a lattice. Let
\Delta:G\to G\times G
be the diagonal embedding and suppose that
\Gamma
is an irreducible discrete subgroup in
G\times G
that contains
\Delta(\Sigma)
. Is
\Gamma
necessarily a lattice?

We remark that the condition “irreducible” in the above question cannot be replaced by the condition “Zariski dense,” as Bass-Lubotzky
[
3
]
constructed Zariski dense discrete subgroups
\Gamma
of
G\times G
, where
G={\rm F}_{4}^{-20}
, which are not lattices in
G\times G
, yet intersect the diagonal copy of
G
in
G\times G
in a lattice. Moreover, these groups are super-rigid, and were the first counterexamples to a conjecture of Platonov (a conjecture that remains open for simple higher rank Lie groups).

Venkataramana studied subgroups containing diagonally embedded lattices in various works (see, e.g.,
[
26
,
27
]
), and proved for example that if
G
is a higher rank Lie group, such subgroups must be super-rigid. He also showed that sometimes these subgroups must be necessarily lattices. (For example, he showed that if
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\subsetneq\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])
, then
\Gamma
must be a finite index subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])
; see
[
27
, Prop. 2.1]
.)

We have the following corollary of Theorem
1.6
:

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Zariski dense discrete subgroup of
G\times G
such that
\Gamma
contains
\Delta(\Sigma)
, then
\Gamma
is not equal to a nontrivial free product with
\Delta(\Sigma)
as a free factor. Moreover if
\Sigma
has algebraic (resp. integral) entries, then
\Gamma
has algebraic (resp. integral) entries.

Remark 1.9
.

Unless
\Sigma
is a non-uniform lattice in
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that
\Gamma
is not even abstractly isomorphic to a non-trivial free product, as
\Sigma
is one ended and subgroups with one end of free products are contained in one of the factors up to conjugation.

Consider a closed surface of genus
g\geq 2
, and take two discrete and faithful embeddings
\rho_{i}:\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
i=1,2
, and let

\Gamma=\{(\rho_{1}(\gamma),\rho_{2}(\gamma))\ \ |\ \ \gamma\in\pi_{1}(S)\},

which is a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(we will call these subgroups
Anosov surface groups
). Based on Corollary
1.8
, we expect a negative answer to the following question:

Question 1.10
.

Suppose that a Zariski dense subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
contains an Anosov surface group. Can
\Gamma
be written as a nontrivial free product?

Finally, we remark that if all irreducible discrete subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
were known to act ergodically on its Furstenberg boundary
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then a negative answer would follow from
Corollary

1.2
Item (2).

1.3.
Hyperbolic manifolds with integral traces

If
\Gamma
is a lattice of
G=\text{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
n\geq 3
, it follows from local rigidity that
\Gamma
has algebraic entries (in any faithful representation, see
Theorem

2.5
). Let
\Ad(\Gamma)
be the image of
\Gamma
under the adjoint representation and let
\mathbf{G}
be the Zariski closure of
\Ad(\Gamma)
.

If we let
k
be the adjoint trace field (the field generated by traces under the adjoint representations), it follows that up to conjugation
\Ad(\Gamma)\subset\mathbf{G}(k)
and
k
is the smallest such field (see
[
28
]
). We will identify
\Gamma
with
\Ad(\Gamma)
. Let
O_{k}
be the ring of integers in
k
.

Definition 1.11
.

Let us define the
integral subgroup
of
\Gamma
as

\Gamma_{0}:=\Gamma\cap\mathbf{G}(O_{k})

and define the
integral critical exponent

\delta_{\Gamma_{0}}
as the critical exponent of
\Gamma_{0}
.

Although the group
\Gamma_{0}
is only well defined up to finite index, the integral critical exponent is well defined. We have the following elementary proposition:

Proposition 1.12
.

If
\Gamma
is a lattice in
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
,
n\geq 2
, and
|\Gamma_{0}|=\infty
, then:

(1)

\Gamma_{0}
is commensurated by
\Gamma
.

(2)

\Gamma_{0}
has full limit set.

(3)

\delta_{\Gamma_{0}}>0
.

Alan Reid has asked the following question:

Question 1.13
.

Can
\Gamma_{0}=\{e\}
?
Equivalently, can a hyperbolic manifold have non-integral traces for all non-trivial elements?

Not much is known about how large or small
\Gamma_{0}
can be, and one can ask the following question:

Question 1.14
.

For fixed
n\geq 2
, and among lattices
\Gamma
of
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, what are the possible values of
\delta_{\Gamma_{0}}
? Is this set of values dense in
[0,n-1]
?

Similar to the results proved by Bader-Fisher-Miller-Stover
[
2
]
, one can ask:

Question 1.15
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a lattice of
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
, and suppose that
\Gamma
contains infinitely many surface subgroups (i.e. isomorphic to the fundamental group of a closed surface and not commensurable among themselves) with integral entries. Does
\Gamma
have integral entries? What can we say when
n>3
?

From Theorem
1.6
, the following result follows:

Corollary 1.16
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a lattice of
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, and suppose that a subgroup
\Gamma_{0}\subset\Gamma
acts strongly ergodically on
\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}
and has integral entries, then
\Gamma
has integral entries.

This implies in particular that if
\Gamma
is a lattice of
\text{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
and contains one geometrically infinite surface subgroup with integral traces, then
\Gamma
has integral traces, as geometrically infinite surface groups always act ergodically on
\partial\mathbb{H}^{3}
(this last fact could be proven more elementary using the fact that geometrically infinite surface groups are virtual fiberings over the circle).

2.
Preliminaries

Unless stated otherwise, we will use the following notation throughout this paper:

2.1.
Notation

Let
G
denote a semi-simple real Lie group with finite center. Let
A
denote a choice of a Cartan subgroup of
G
, Let
B
be a minimal parabolic subgroup of
G
containing
A
(
B
is the unique maximal connected amenable subgroup of
G
up to conjugation) and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Let
M\subset K
be the centralizer of
A
in
K
, and
N
be the unipotent radical of
B
. We have that
B=MAN
. We also recall the Iwasawa decomposition of
G
given by
G=KAN
. Let us define the Furstenberg boundary to be the group
\mathcal{F}:=G/B
.

2.2.
Strong ergodicity

The group
G
acts continuously on the boundary
\mathcal{F}
by left multiplication and preserves the Lebesgue class (but does not preserve the Lebesgue measure). We will use the following definition (the second part is not standard):

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a subgroup
\Sigma<G
acts
ergodically
on the Furstenberg boundary
\mathcal{F}
if any Lebesgue measurable set which is
\Sigma
-invariant, has zero or full Lebesgue measure. We say that
\Sigma
acts
strongly ergodically
on
\mathcal{F}
if every finite index subgroup of
\Sigma
acts ergodically on
\mathcal{F}
.

Not every group that acts ergodically on the boundary necessarily acts strongly ergodically as it was pointed out to us on MathOverflow by Moishe Kohan
[
15
]
. Examples of such kind are as follows:

Example 2.2
.

Let
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, consider
\Gamma_{0}\subset G
so that
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-cover,
d\geq 3
, of a compact hyperbolic surface, and let
\Gamma_{1}<\Gamma_{0}
be the index two subgroup of
\Gamma_{0}
, such that
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma_{0}
is obtained from
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma_{1}
by cutting along a simple non-separating geodesic, obtaining a hyperbolic surface
S
with two boundary geodesics, and then doubling along this geodesics. The group
\Gamma_{0}
acts ergodically on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
because bounded harmonic functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are constant, but
\Gamma_{1}
does not act ergodically because due to the transience of the random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a Brownian in path in
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma_{1}
will eventually enter one of the two copies of
S
and never come back to the other copy. See
[
20
,
25
]
for more details.

Consequently, if
\Gamma
is a torsion-free uniform lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then the commutator subgroup
\Gamma_{0}\coloneqq[\Gamma,\Gamma]
acts ergodically but not strongly ergodically on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

2.3.
Boundary Map

We will make use of the following lemma, attributed to Furstenberg. See
[
29
]
, Corollary 4.3.7 and Proposition 4.3.9 for a proof. For completeness, we provide a different proof using the existence of conditional measures:

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
\Gamma<G
is a discrete subgroup of a semi-simple Lie group
G
, and suppose
\Gamma
acts continuously on a compact metric space
X
, then there exists a Borel measurable map
\phi:\mathcal{F}\to\text{Prob}(X)
which is
\Gamma
-equivariant.

Proof.

Consider the suspension space
Y:=(G\times X)/\Gamma
, obtained from the action on
X
, where action of
\Gamma
is via
\gamma.(g,x)=(g\gamma^{-1},\gamma x)
.
G
acts by left multiplication on
Y
.

We can construct a Radon measure
\mu
that projects to Haar measure in
G/\Gamma
, and which is
B
-invariant (recall that
\mathcal{F}=G/B
and
B
is amenable).

Consider the space
M_{Y}
of Radon measures on
Y
that project to Haar measure on
G/\Gamma
, one can show that
M_{Y}
is non-empty. Consider a Folner sequence
F_{n}
of
B
, and take
\mu_{0}\in M_{Y}
, and consider the averages:

\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{m_{B}(F_{N})}\int_{F_{n}}p_{*}\mu_{0}\ dp.

Take a sub-sequential limit
\mu
of
\{\mu_{n}\}
. Such limit
\mu
exists and must be
B
-invariant. We can lift
\mu
to a measure
\hat{\mu}
on
G\times X
that projects to Haar measure, and which is
\Gamma
-invariant, moreover such measure is also
B
-invariant, for the right
B
-action. The existence of conditional measures with respect to the
X
-foliation implies that for Haar a.e.
g\in G
there is a probability measure
m_{g}
on
X
, and from the invariance conditions we have for Haar
a.e.

g\in G
, and every
p\in B
, that
m_{pg}=m_{g}
, and
m_{g\gamma^{-1}}=\gamma_{*}m_{g}
.

Therefore the assignment
g\to m_{g}
, induces a map
\phi:G/B\to\text{Prob}(X)
which is
\Gamma
-equivariant.
∎

2.4.
Algebraic and integral traces

Let
G
be a real connected semi-simple Lie group without compact factors and finite center, and let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
G
. Let
Ad:G\to{\rm End}(\mathfrak{g})
be the adjoint representation.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
\Gamma
has
algebraic traces
(respectively,
integral traces
) if for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the trace of
\Ad(\gamma)
is an algebraic number (respectively, algebraic integer). We let
k_{\Gamma}
denote the field generated by all such traces, and refer to
k_{\Gamma}
as the
adjoint trace field
.

If the group
\Gamma
is finitely generated, the adjoint trace field
k_{\Gamma}
is finitely generated. If
\Gamma
has algebraic traces,
[k_{\Gamma}:\mathbb{Q}]<\infty
.

The following is well known to experts, but we were not able to find a proof in the literature.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
G
be a semi-simple real algebraic group with trivial center, and
\Gamma<G
be a Zariski dense subgroup. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\Gamma
has algebraic traces (respectively, integral traces).

(ii)

For every faithful representation
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
of
G
such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
\rho(\gamma)
has algebraic traces (respectively, integral traces).

(iii)

For every faithful representation
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
of
G
and every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the eigenvalues of
\rho(\gamma)
are algebraic numbers (respectively, algebraic integers).

(iv)

Every faithful representation
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
can be conjugated by an element of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
so that
\rho(\Gamma)\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
(respectively,
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(O_{k})
) for some number field
k
.

Proof.

We prove only the facts for algebraic traces; the facts about integrality follows along the same lines with minor modifications.

The equivalence between items
(ii)
and
(iii)
follows for a given representation
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
, because for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
\rho(\gamma)
can be expressed via Newton’s identities (for symmetric polynomals) as a polynomial with rational coefficients on the traces of powers
{\rm tr}\,\rho(\gamma^{k})
, and so algebraic traces imply algebraic eigenvalues.

Item
(i)
is equivalent to
(iii)
, because of the following fact from representation theory: if
\rho_{0}:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
is an irreducible faithful representation, then any irreducible representation of
G
appears as a direct summand of the representations obtained by (iterated) tensor products of
\rho_{0}
and its dual; see
[
13
, Ch. I, Prop 3.1]
. Using this fact, and the fact that the eigenvalues of tensor product of representations can be expressed polynomially in eigenvalues of the representations involved, we have that if the elements in the image of
\Gamma
under the adjoint representation (or any faithful representation) have algebraic eigenvalues, then the same must be true for any faithful irreducible representation.

Item
(iv)
follows because if
\Ad(\Gamma)
is Zariski dense in
\Ad(G)
, one can construct a faithful representation
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
where the entries of
\rho(\Gamma)
(for a well chosen basis of
V
) lie in
k_{\Gamma}
(This fact appears in the work of Vinberg, see
[
28
]
; for a proof see Mostow’s
[
22
, section 2.5]
) and this prove item
(iv)
for
\rho
. By taking tensor product of
\rho
and its dual and arguing as before, we can suppose that the same is true for any irreducible representation.
∎

3.
Strong ergodicity and boundedness

The following result will be important in our proofs of the main statements in the introduction. The ideas in its proof come from the work of Chatterji-Venkataramana
[
11
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a semi-simple real Lie group with finite center. Let
\Gamma
be a discrete subgroup of
G
and let
\Sigma\subset\Gamma
be a subgroup.
Let
k
be a non-Archimedean local field.
Suppose that

\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}_{n}(k)

is a homomorphism such that:

(i)

\rho(\Sigma)
is bounded.

(ii)

\Sigma
acts strongly ergodically on the Furstenberg boundary
G/B
.

(iii)

The Zariski closure
H
of
\rho(\Gamma)
in
{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
is connected and semi-simple.

Then
\rho(\Gamma)
is bounded.

Proof.

We will partly follow the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
27
]
.
Since the Zariski closure
H
of
\rho(\Gamma)
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
is connected and semi-simple, Weyl’s theorem on complete reducibility implies that the finite-dimensional
H
-module
k^{n}
decomposes as a direct sum of irreducible
H
-modules:

k^{n}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{p}.

Since
\Gamma\subset H
is Zariski dense, this decomposition is also a decomposition of
\Gamma
-modules. Therefore, to prove that
\rho(\Gamma)
is bounded, it suffices to treat the case where
k^{n}
is irreducible under
\Gamma
(and hence under
H
).

Furthermore, the connectedness of
H
implies that any finite-index subgroup
\Gamma_{0}\leq\Gamma
is Zariski dense in
H
. It follows that
k^{n}
remains irreducible under
\Gamma_{0}
, so the
\Gamma
-module
k^{n}
is strongly irreducible, i.e., no finite-index subgroup preserves a proper nonzero subspace.

Applying Lemma
2.3
to
\Gamma\subset G
, we have a
\Gamma
-equivariant measurable map:

\phi:G/B\to\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k)).

We show that
\phi
is constant.

By Zimmer
[
29
, Thm. 3.2.6]
, it follows that
{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
acts smoothly on
\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k))
, i.e., every
{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
-orbit in
\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k))
is locally closed. Thus, the orbit space

\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k))/{\rm GL}_{n}(k)

is countably seperated.
Let

\bar{\phi}:G/B\to\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k))/{\rm GL}_{n}(k)

be the composition of
\phi
with the quotient map
\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k))\to\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k))/{\rm GL}_{n}(k).

Since the codomain of
\bar{\phi}
is countably separated and
\Gamma\curvearrowright G/B
is ergodic,
\bar{\phi}
is essentially constant, i.e., on a co-null set in
G/B
,
\phi
takes values in a single
{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
-orbit in
\text{Prob}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k))
. So, we can identify
\phi
with a
\Gamma
-equivariant measurable map
\phi:G/B\to{\rm GL}_{n}(k)/L
, where
L\subset{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
is a closed subgroup.

Since
{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
is a locally compact totally disconnected group, there exists a sequence of compact open subgroups
C_{n}\subset{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
such that
C_{n}\to\{1\}
in the topology of Hausdorff convergence. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\Sigma_{n}\coloneqq\Sigma\cap C_{n}.

Since the image of
\rho(\Sigma)
is bounded,
\Sigma_{n}\subset\Sigma
is of finite index and thus acts ergodically on
G/B
by our hypothesis. Since
C_{n}
is compact, it acts smoothly on
{\rm GL}_{n}(k)/L
and thus by a similar argument as above, the image of
\phi
lies in a single
C_{n}
-orbit. Since
C_{n}\to\{1\}
, it follows that the image of
\phi
is constant.

So,
\Gamma
preserves a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(k)
.

Suppose now, to the contrary, that
\rho(\Gamma)
is unbounded in
H\subset{\rm GL}_{n}(k)
. Let

[\,\cdot\,]:{\rm GL}_{n}(k)\to{\rm PGL}_{n}(k)

be the canonical map. Since
H
is semi-simple, it follows that
[\rho(\Gamma)]
is unbounded.
Since
\mu
is invariant, by Furstenberg’s lemma (see
[
29
, Lem. 3.2.1]
), there exist subspaces

V,W\subset k^{n}
with
1<\dim V,\dim W<n
such that
\mu
is supported on
\mathbb{P}(V)\cup\mathbb{P}(W)
. Thus,
\Gamma
preserves
V\cup W
, which contradicts strong irreducibility of

.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

We will restrict to the case where
G
is connected and has trivial center. In this case
[
29
, Prop. 3.1.6]
, there exists a connected semi-simple algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
G\cong\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})^{0}
(see
[
29
, Prop. 3.1.6]
).

We will split the proof of Theorem
1.6
into three Theorems. We will first consider the fact about algebraic and integral traces.

4.1.
Restriction on traces

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma<G=\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})^{0}
be a finitely generated Zariski dense discrete subgroup of
G
such that there exists a subgroup
\Sigma\subset\Gamma
such that

\Sigma\subset\mathbf{G}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})

and that
\Sigma
acts strongly ergodically on the Furstenberg boundary
G/B
.
Then,
\Gamma
has algebraic traces.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, there exists a finitely generated

\mathbb{Z}
–subalgebra
R\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

\Gamma\subset\mathbf{G}(R)
. Let
F=\mathrm{Frac}(R)
and define

k:=F\cap\bar{\mathbb{Q}}.

By

[
24
, Lem. 9.26.11]
,
k
is a number field.
The intersection
A:=R\cap\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
is a finitely
generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, hence there exists
d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

A\subset O_{k}\left[\frac{1}{d}\right],

where
O_{k}
is the ring of integers of
k
.
Therefore

(4.1)

\Sigma\subset\mathbf{G}\!\left(O_{k}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{d}\right]\right).

Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
such that
\operatorname{tr}(\Ad(\gamma_{0}))
is not algebraic.
Let
\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
be the variety of homomorphisms from
\Gamma
into
G
.
We consider its subvariety

(4.2)

V:=\{\varphi:\Gamma\to G\mid\,\forall\gamma\in\Sigma,\,\varphi(\gamma)=\gamma\}.

To see that
V
is defined over
k
,
fix a finite set of generators
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
of
\Gamma
and a presentation
\Gamma=\langle s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\;|\;R_{i},\,i\in I\rangle
.
Then, we may realize

V\subset G^{n+1}

as the zero set of the polynomials in variables
(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
given by:

(i)

The relations
R_{i},\,i\in I
of
\Gamma
, and

(ii)

A countable set of equations determined for each
\gamma\in\Sigma
(written as a product of the
s_{i}
’s).

The polynomials in the first category have
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients whereas the second ones have coefficients in
k
.

We equip
V
with the euclidean topology.

Lemma 4.2
.

V
has positive dimension,
V(\mathbb{R}\cap\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
is dense in
V
, and

\varphi_{0}\coloneqq\operatorname{id}_{\Gamma}\in V

is not an isolated point.

Proof.

Since
V
is defined over a number field
k\subset\mathbb{R}
,
V(\mathbb{R}\cap\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
is dense in
V
. Moreover, since
\operatorname{tr}(\Ad(\gamma_{0}))\not\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have
\varphi_{0}\not\in V(\mathbb{R}\cap\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
.
The claim follows from this.
∎

Now, consider the trace function

{\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}:V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}.

given by
{\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi)\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}(\Ad(\varphi(\gamma_{0})))
. By
Lemma

4.2
, there exists a sequence
\varphi_{n}^{\prime}\in V
converging to
\varphi_{0}
such that

\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(\Gamma)\subset\mathbf{G}({\mathbb{R}\cap\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}),\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
{\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi_{n}^{\prime})
is algebraic for all
n
but

{\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi_{0})
is not, and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi_{n}^{\prime})={\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi_{0}),

it follows that the function
{\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}:V\to\mathbb{R}
is non-constant near
\varphi_{0}\in V
.

Consequently, for all large enough prime numbers
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists
\varphi_{p}\in V
such that
\varphi_{p}\to\varphi_{0}
as
p\to\infty
and

(4.3)

{\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi_{p})=\frac{m}{p},\quad\text{for some }m\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}.

Similar to
Lemma

4.2
, we also have:

Lemma 4.3
.

\{\varphi\in V(\mathbb{R}\cap\bar{\mathbb{Q}}):\ {\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi)=\frac{m}{p}\}
is dense in
V\cap{\rm tr}_{\gamma_{0}}^{-1}(\frac{m}{p})
.

Fix a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p\nmid d
, where
d
is as in (
4.1
).
We can (and will) further assume that (
4.3
) is satisfied by our choice of
p
and that the homomorphism

\varphi^{\prime}\coloneqq\varphi_{p}

has Zariski dense image.

By
Lemma

4.3
, there exists a finite extension
l\subset\mathbb{C}
of
k
such that

\varphi^{\prime}(\Gamma)\subset\mathbf{G}(l).

Extending
l
if necessary, we will further assume that
l
contains all the eigenvalues

\lambda_{i}
of
\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma_{0})
.

Let
\nu
denote the
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}
, which we extend to a valuation on
l
. By the ultrametric inequality we have that

\min_{i}\{\nu(\lambda_{i})\}\leq\nu\big(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\big)=\nu(\operatorname{tr}_{\gamma_{0}}(\varphi^{\prime}))<0.

So, we have that

\max_{i}|\lambda_{i}|_{\nu}>1,

where
|\cdot|_{\nu}\coloneqq e^{\nu(\cdot)}
is the norm on
l
.
Let
l_{\nu}
(a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
) denotes the completion of

with respect to this norm.

Consider the group
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
, which is a connected and semi-simple
\mathbb{Q}
-group (since
\mathbf{G}
is assumed to be so).

Lemma 4.4
.

The homomorphism

(4.4)

\varphi^{\prime}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})

is unbounded.

Proof.

Since the maximum
|\cdot|_{\nu}
-norm of the eigenvalues of
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
is strictly greater than
1
, it follows that the subgroup
\langle\gamma_{0}\rangle\subset\Gamma
is unbounded in
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
.
∎

It follows that
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
is noncompact.
Consequently,
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
has a nonzero
l
-rank, i.e., it has a nontrivial
l
-split torus.

Lemma 4.5
.

\varphi^{\prime}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
is bounded.

Proof.

This follows from our choice of
p
(that it does not divide
d
) and the assumption (
4.1
) that
\Sigma\subset\mathbf{G}(O_{k}\left[\frac{1}{d}\right])
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

\varphi^{\prime}(\Gamma)
is Zariski dense in
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
\Gamma
is Zariski dense in
G
. So, we can choose
\varphi^{\prime}(\Gamma)
also to be Zariski dense in
G
.
Since
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
is a
k
-group,
a theorem due to Rosenlicht and Chevalley (see Zimmer
[
29
, Thm. 3.1.9]
) asserts that
\mathbf{G}(k)
is Zariski dense in
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
. Hence the conclusion follows.
∎

As the action of
\Sigma
on
G/B
is strongly ergodic while the Zariski closure
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
of
\varphi^{\prime}(\Gamma)
in
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(l_{\nu})
is connected and semi-simple, we can apply
Theorem

3.1
to conclude that
\varphi(\Gamma)
must be bounded in
\mathbf{G}(l_{\nu})
, which contradicts
Lemma

4.4
.
The proof of
Theorem

4.1
is now complete.∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Under the same hypothesis as in
Theorem

4.1
, suppose further
\Sigma\subset\mathbf{G}(O_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})
, where
O_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}
is the ring of algebraic integers in

. Then, the traces of elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic integers.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there exists
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
such that
\alpha\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}(\gamma_{0})
is not an algebraic integer. By
Theorem

4.1
, we know that
\alpha\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. After a small deformation of
\Gamma
as in

4.3
, we have a number field

such that
\Gamma\subset\mathbf{G}(k)
and

.

Since

, we can choose a discrete valuation

such that

. Let

denote the completion of

with respect to the norm

, and let
\varphi:\Gamma\to G(k_{\nu})
the induced homomorphism. By the same argument used in the proof of

,
\langle\varphi(\gamma_{0})\rangle
and, hence,

are unbounded subgroups of

.

Since
O_{k}\subset k_{v}
is bounded and

∎

Remark 4.8
.

In the statement of

(resp.

), if we assume that

4.2.
Obstruction to Free products

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\Sigma\subset G
be a finitely generated discrete subgroup which acts strongly ergodically on
G/B
, and let
\gamma\in G
non-trivial element. If the group
\Gamma
generated by
\Sigma
and
\gamma
is Zariski dense, then the natural homomorphism

(4.5)

\varphi:\Sigma*\langle\gamma\rangle\longrightarrow G

is either not discrete or not faithful.

Proof.

We can assume that
\gamma\notin\Sigma
, and arguing by contradiction that
\Sigma*\langle\gamma\rangle
is faithful and discrete, we can also assume that
\gamma
has infinite order, and therefore the group
\Gamma
generated by
\Sigma
and
\gamma
must be isomorphic to
\Sigma*\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be the Zariski closure of
\Sigma
in
\mathbf{G}
. The ergodicity assumption implies that
\Sigma
is not virtually solvable, and therefore
\mathbf{H}
must have a Levi factor
\mathbf{L}
which is simple and noncompact, and the natural homomorphism
\rho:\Sigma\to\mathbf{L}
has Zariski dense image.

We can assume that
\rho(\Sigma)\subset\mathbf{L}(k)
for a number field
k
(otherwise, we can look at representation variety of such representations, and find another representation
\rho^{\prime}
so that
\rho^{\prime}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbf{L}(k)
). From the finitely generated assumption, we can assume that there exists a prime non-Archimedean valuation
\nu
so that
\mathbf{L}(k_{\nu})
is not compact, and the induced homomorphism
\rho^{\prime\prime}:\Sigma\to\mathbf{L}(k_{\nu})
has compact image. We can take a non-compact element
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathbf{L}(k_{\nu})
and extends
\rho^{\prime\prime}
to
\Sigma*\mathbb{Z}
by sending the generator of
\mathbb{Z}
to
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
, and this is clearly contradicts
Theorem

3.1
.
∎

The condition that
\Sigma\subset G
is finitely generated is perhaps redundant in the above result. For instance, a minor modification of the argument shows the following:

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\Sigma\subset G
be a discrete subgroup which acts strongly ergodically on
G/B
and let
\gamma\in G
be an infinite order element. Suppose that
\Sigma\cong\mathbf{F}_{\infty}
, the free group on (countably) infinitely many generators. Then the natural homomorphism

\varphi:\Sigma*\langle\gamma\rangle\longrightarrow G

is either not discrete or not faithful.

Sketch.

Realize
\Sigma
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, which is a lattice in
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathbf{L}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. With this, repeat the proof of
Theorem

4.9
.
∎

In
[
14
]
, it is shown that in any non-compact connected real semi-simple Lie group
G
with finite center, there exist infinitely many regular loxodromic elements
g_{n}\in G
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, freely generating a discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
such that their attractive fixed points in
G/B
form a dense set. Thus
\Gamma
acts minimally on
G/P
. Now, consider the subgroup
\Gamma_{0}\coloneqq\langle g_{2},g_{3},\dots\rangle
of
\Gamma
, which, for a similar reason, acts minimally on
G/B
. Moreover,
\Gamma
is naturally isomorphic to
\langle g_{1}\rangle*\Gamma_{0}
. Thus, by
Theorem

4.10
, some finite index subgroup of
\Gamma_{0}
cannot act ergodically on
G/B
. So, we have the following:

Corollary 4.11
.

Every non-compact connected real semi-simple Lie group
G
with finite center
contains an infinitely generated discrete subgroup that acts minimally but not ergodically on
G/B
.

5.
Remarks on irreducible discrete subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

Recall that an
irreducible subgroup
in a semi-simple Lie group
G
is a subgroup that projects densely in each of the simple factors of
G
. For the rest of the section, we let

G\coloneqq\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

One can prove that every irreducible group must act minimally on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
(see
Proposition

5.4
below), and we ask the following:

Question 5.1
.

If
\Gamma<G
is irreducible, must it act ergodically on the Furstenberg boundary
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
?

A positive answer to this question will have the following remarkable consequences (
Corollary

1.2
in the introduction):

Corollary 5.2
.

If every irreducible discrete subgroup of
G
acts ergodically on the Furstenberg boundary
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then the following hold:

(i)

No irreducible discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
is Gromov hyperbolic.

(ii)

Let
\Gamma
be a hyperbolic group, which embeds discretely as a subgroup of
G
. Then
\Gamma
is virtually either a free group or a surface group.

(iii)

If
M
is a finite volume hyperbolic 3-manifold, then
\pi_{1}(M)
does not admit any discrete and faithful homomorphism into
G
.

Regarding the final item, note that for cohomological reasons, if
M
is a closed hyperbolic
n
-manifold where
n\geq 4
, then
\pi_{1}(M)
does not embed discretely as a subgroup of
G
.

We will make use of the following elementary observations:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated dense subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then,
\Gamma
contains an infinite order elliptic element.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, Selberg’s Lemma
[
23
]
implies that
\Gamma
contains a finite-index, torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
(which must be dense in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
since
\Gamma
is).

Recall that elliptic elements
g
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
are characterized by the trace condition
|\operatorname{tr}(g)|<2
. Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is dense in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that
\{\operatorname{tr}(\gamma):\ \gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}\}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
\Gamma^{\prime}
contains an elliptic element, which must be of infinite order since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is torsion-free.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\Gamma<G
is an irreducible discrete subgroup, then
\Gamma
contains two elements
\gamma_{1}=(e_{1},h_{1})
and
\gamma_{2}=(h_{2},e_{2})
, where
e_{i}
’s (resp.
h_{i}
’s) are infinite-order elliptic (resp. hyperbolic) elements in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

Proof.

Using the fact that the projection of
\Gamma
in the first factor of
G
is dense, it follows by
Lemma

5.3
that
\Gamma
contains an element
\gamma_{1}=(e_{1},h_{1})
, where
e_{1}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is an infinite order elliptic element.
Since
\Gamma
is discrete in
G
,
\langle h_{1}\rangle
is an infinite discrete cyclic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
; so,
h_{1}
must be a parabolic or hyperbolic element. We show that
h_{1}
can be taken to be a hyperbolic element: indeed, suppose that
h_{1}
is parabolic. Consider the group
\Delta
generated by
\gamma_{1}
and a conjugate
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}=(e_{1}^{\prime},h_{1}^{\prime})
of
\gamma_{1}
by an element of
\Gamma
of the form
(g_{1},g_{2})
, where
g_{2}
is an infinite order elliptic element. Thus,
g_{2}x\neq x
, where
x
denotes the unique fixed point of
h_{1}
in
S^{1}
. Applying the classical ping-pong argument, it is easily seen that for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following:

(1)

\langle h_{1}^{n},h_{1}^{\prime n}\rangle
is a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, freely generated by
h_{1}^{n}
and
h_{1}^{\prime n}
.

(2)

The parabolic elements of
\langle h_{1}^{n},h_{1}^{\prime n}\rangle
can be conjugated into
\langle h_{1}\rangle
and
\langle h_{1}^{\prime}\rangle
.

Fix such an
n
. Considering the traces, it can be seen that
e_{1}^{n}(e_{2}^{n})^{k}
is elliptic for infinitely many values of
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
k
be such a number so that
e_{1}^{n}(e_{2}^{n})^{k}
is elliptic (which is again of infinite order). Then, for the element
(e_{1}^{n}(e_{2}^{n})^{k},h_{1}^{n}(h_{2}^{n})^{k})\in\Gamma
, the first component is elliptic, whereas the second one is hyperbolic (due to conditions (i) and (ii) above).

Similarly,
\Gamma
contains an element
\gamma_{2}=(h_{2},e_{2})
, where
h_{2}
(resp.
e_{2}
) are infinite order hyperbolic (resp. elliptic) elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎

We remark that any discrete subgroup
G
generated by two elements
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
of the form given by
Proposition

5.4
is necessarily irreducible.

We can now start the proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
Item (i).

Suppose, to the contrary, that
\Gamma
is a discrete, irreducible, hyperbolic subgroup of
G
. Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, by applying Selberg’s Lemma, we can assume that
\Gamma
and its projections to each factor of
G
are torsion-free.

Clearly, no powers of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are conjugate in
\Gamma
(since these are not conjugate in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
).
Since
\Gamma
is a torsion-free hyperbolic group, again by a ping-pong argument, it can be seen that for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the natural homomorphism
\rho:\langle\gamma_{1}^{n}\rangle*\langle\gamma_{2}^{n}\rangle\to\Gamma
is injective. Note that the image of
\rho
is also irreducible in
G
.

Thus, the proof now reduces to case when
\Gamma
is the free group
F_{2}
generated by
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, as in
Proposition

5.4
.

In this case
\gamma_{1}^{2}
and
\gamma_{2}^{2}
also generate an irreducible discrete subgroup
F
of
G
, which by assumption acts ergodically, which moreover does not contain the element
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
. Observe that
\gamma_{1}^{2}
and
\gamma_{2}^{2}
and
g_{1}g_{2}
generate the free group
F_{3}
.

It is easy to see that any finite index subgroup
F
contains some powers of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
and, hence, and so by our assumption on irreducible groups acting ergodically, we have that
F
acts strongly irreducibly.

Thus, by
Theorem

4.9
, the natural homomorphism

\rho:F*\langle g_{1}g_{2}\rangle\to G

cannot be discrete and faithful, which gives us a contradiction.
∎

Before proving Item (ii), we need the following:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolic group, which embeds discretely as a subgroup of

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times P
, where
P
is a solvable Lie group. Then the projection of
\Gamma
to the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-factor is discrete and faithful.

In particular,
\Gamma
is either a free group or a surface group.

Proof.

Let

\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times P

be a discrete and faithful homomorphism.
Let
{\rm pr}_{1}
and
{\rm pr}_{2}
denote the projections of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times P
onto the first and second factors, respectively. We will identify
\Gamma
with its image
\rho(\Gamma)
.

Set

N\coloneqq\ker\big({\rm pr}_{2}|_{\Gamma}\big)\triangleleft\Gamma.

Since
\Gamma
is discrete in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times P
, the subgroup
N\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\{e\}
is
also discrete. In particular,
{\rm pr}_{1}|_{N}:N\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is discrete and faithful.

Because
P
is solvable, the quotient
\Gamma/N\cong{\rm pr}_{2}(\Gamma)
is
solvable. Since
\Gamma
is not solvable,
N
must be non-trivial, hence infinite, and hence nonelementary.
Thus
{\rm pr}_{1}(N)
is a nonelementary discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Since
{\rm pr}_{1}(N)
is normal in

{\rm pr}_{1}(\Gamma)
,
{\rm pr}_{1}(\Gamma)
must be discrete in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
; otherwise, the identity component
H
of its closure would have positive dimension, would centralize
{\rm pr}_{1}(N)
(by discreteness), and this would be impossible since
{\rm pr}_{1}(N)
is a nonelementary discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Now consider

K:=\ker\big({\rm pr}_{1}|_{\Gamma}\big)\triangleleft\Gamma.

Since
K
injects into
P
,
K
is solvable.
Since
\Gamma
is a torsion-free nonelementary hyperbolic group, any nontrivial normal subgroup of
\Gamma
is nonsolvable. Thus
K
must be trivial, so
{\rm pr}_{1}|_{\Gamma}
is faithful.
∎

Now we return to the proof of Corollary
1.2
, item (ii). We will essentially show that if
\Gamma
is a hyperbolic discrete subgroup of
G
, which is virtually neither a free group nor a surface group, then
\Gamma
must be irreducible.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
Item (ii).

Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists a hyperbolic group
\Gamma
, which is virtually neither a free group nor a surface group, such that it admits a discrete and faithful homomorphism

\rho:\Gamma\to G.

Using Selberg’s Lemma, we may assume that
\Gamma
is torsion-free.
We also will identify
\Gamma
with its image
\rho(\Gamma)
.

Note that the projections of
\Gamma
into the any of the factors of
G
cannot be bounded, since otherwise, due to discreteness in
G
,
\Gamma
would act properly discontinuously on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
through the projection into the other factor – this would be impossible since then
\Gamma
would be a free group or a surface group.
Moreover, these images cannot also lie in a parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, as rulled out by
Lemma

5.5
.
Thus, we can assume that the projection of
\Gamma
to each factor is Zariski dense.
Thus, we have the following three mutually exclusive possibilities:

Case 1.
Suppose that the image of
\Gamma
in each factor is discrete. In this case,
\Gamma
virtually embeds into a direct product of two torsion-free finitely generated discrete subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since every hyperbolic group is finitely presented
[
17
]
,
\Gamma
is of type
\mathrm{FP}_{2}
and thus by
[
7
, Thm. A]
,
\Gamma
is virtually a direct product of at most two surface or free groups. Since
\Gamma
does not contain any
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, it follows that
\Gamma
is either a surface group or a free group, which is a contradiction.

Case 2.
Suppose that
\Gamma
has a discrete projection in one factor, say the first, and an indiscrete projection in the other (i.e., the second).
Since the projection of
\Gamma
to the second factor is also Zariski dense, this projection is (topologically) dense.

Let
N
denote the kernel of the projection of
\Gamma
into the first factor. Since
\Gamma
is discrete in
G
,
N
acts properly discontinuously on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
through the projection into the second factor. Since the image of
\Gamma
is dense in the second factor, we see that the normalizer of
N
, where
N
is viewed as a discrete subgroup of the second factor through the projection, is dense in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. But the normalizer of a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
must be closed. Hence,
N
must be a normal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. So,
N
is central (hence trivial since
\Gamma
is torsion-free) and, consequently,
\Gamma
embeds discretely in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
through the first projection, which is impossible.

Case 3.

Suppose that
\Gamma
to both factors of
G
are indiscrete. Since the two projections are Zariski dense, both projections are topologically dense and hence
\Gamma
is irreducible. Thus, if all irreducible discrete subgroups of
G
were to act ergodically act on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then by Item (i) of Corollary
1.2
,
\Gamma
cannot be hyperbolic, again a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
Item (iii).

If
M
is closed, then one can argue as in (ii) to obtain the result. In general, it is known that
\pi_{1}(M)
contains a plethora of quasifuchsian surface groups
[
18
,
12
,
19
]
. Hence one can find a subgroup isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(S)*\mathbb{Z}
inside
\pi_{1}(M)
, where
S
is a closed hyperbolic surface, and the conclusion then follows from (ii).
∎

Appendix A
A geometric proof of
Corollary

1.8
in the cocompact case

In this section, we provide a geometric proof of part of
Corollary

1.8
in the case where the lattice is cocompact. This proof is more geometric, and does not use boundary maps.

Let
G
denote a connected real semi-simple Lie group with finite center and without compact factors. We let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup, let
A
be a Cartan subgroup and
P
a minimal parabolic subgroup containing
A
, and let
N
be the unipotent radical of
P
so
P=AN
. We have the Iwasawa decomposition
G=KP
. Let
\Lambda\subset G
be a cocompact lattice and let
\Delta:G\to G\times G
be the diagonal embedding.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Zariski dense discrete subgroup of
G\times G
that contains
\Delta(\Lambda)
, then
\Gamma
is not isomorphic to a non-trivial free product.

Proof.

The proof is by contradiction. We assume that
\Gamma
is isomorphic to a free product, and by considering an infinite order element not in
\Delta(\Lambda)
, and possibly replacing
\Gamma
with a subgroup, we can assume that
\Gamma=\Delta(\Lambda)*\mathbb{Z}
.

We will use the following elementary proposition which follows from the Zariski density of
\Gamma
.

Proposition A.2
.

There exists
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma
such that:

(1)

The group generated by
\Delta(\Lambda)
and
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
is naturally isomorphic to
\Delta(\Lambda)*\mathbb{Z}

(2)

There exists
kP\in G/B
, where
k\in K
, such that
\gamma_{1}kP=\gamma_{2}kP

Choose
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
kP
as in Proposition
A.2
. We write
\gamma_{i}k=k^{\prime}p_{i}
for some
k^{\prime}\in K
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
.
Take
a
to be an element of
A
so that
N
is the stable horospherical subgroup of
P
, that is

N=\{n\in G,\text{ such that }\lim_{t\to\infty}d(a^{t}na^{-t},1)=0\}.

Because of our assumption that
\Lambda
is a uniform lattice, there exists a compact set
C\subset G
, such that for every
x\in G
the intersection
\Gamma\cap Cx\neq\emptyset
.

Therefore for every
t>0
, there exists
c_{t}\in C
and
\lambda_{t}\in\Lambda
such that
c_{t}a^{t}k=\lambda_{t}
, and similarly there exists
c^{\prime}_{t}\in C
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{t}
such that
c^{\prime}_{t}a^{t}.k^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}_{t}
.

Proposition A.3
.

There exists a compact set
C_{1}\subset G\times G
, such that for every
t>0
, the element
(\lambda^{\prime}_{t},\lambda^{\prime}_{t})^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})(\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t})\in C_{1}
.

Proof.

We have the following equation:

\displaystyle(\lambda^{\prime}_{t})^{-1}\gamma_{i}\lambda_{t}

\displaystyle=(k_{1}a^{-t}(c^{\prime}_{t})^{-1})^{-1}\gamma_{i}(ka^{-t}c_{t}^{-1})

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}_{t}a^{t}(k_{1}^{-1}\gamma_{i}k)a^{-t}c_{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}_{t}(a^{t}p_{i}a^{-t})c_{t}

Observe that by our choice of
a
, the element
a^{t}p_{i}a^{-t}
lies in a bounded set independent of
G
and as
c_{t},c^{\prime}_{t}
lie in the compact set
C
, we are done.

∎

The discreteness of
\Gamma
implies that the set of elements

S:=\{(\lambda^{\prime}_{t},\lambda^{\prime}_{t})^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})(\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t})\text{ for }t>0\}

in
A.3
is a finite collection of elements in
\Gamma
, but as
\lambda_{t},\lambda^{\prime}_{t}
diverge in
G
as
t\to\infty
, there exists
s,t>0
, such that
\lambda_{s}\neq\lambda_{t}
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{s}\neq\lambda^{\prime}_{t}
such that

(\lambda^{\prime}_{t},\lambda^{\prime}_{t})^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})(\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t})=(\lambda^{\prime}_{s},\lambda^{\prime}_{s})^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})(\lambda_{s},\lambda_{s}),

which contradicts that
\Delta(\Lambda)
and
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
generate a free product.
∎

Appendix B
Some connections between irreducible groups and diophantine properties of Lie groups

The main point of this appendix is to show that proving certain irreducible groups are discrete is at least as hard as constructing new examples of dense subgroups satisfying a non-abelian diophantine property.

Let
G
be a semi-simple Lie group, and
\Gamma\subset G
, a finitely generated dense subgroup, let
S
be a finite generating set of
\Gamma
. Fix
d
to be a left invariant metric in
G
, and let
B_{G}(g,r)
denote the metric ball centered at
g\in G
of radius
r\geq 0
.

Definition B.1
.

We say that
\Gamma
is
strongly diophantine
if there exists
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
B_{G}(1_{G},c_{1}e^{-c_{2}n})\cap S^{n}=\{1_{G}\}
. We say that
\Gamma
is
weakly diophantine
if there exists
c_{1},c_{2}>0

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|B_{G}(1_{G},c_{1}e^{-c_{2}n})\cap S^{n}|}{\log|S^{n}|}<1

Observe that this definition is independent of the generating set
S
. If
S
consists of elements with algebraic entries (under some faithful representation of
G
), then
\Gamma
is strongly diophantine.

If
G
is compact and simple, Benoist-DeSaxce
[
4
]
(following work of Bourgain and Gamburd for
{\rm SU}(n)

[
5
,
6
]
) have shown that being almost-diophantine (a condition related to being weakly-diophantine) is equivalent to having a spectral gap for the averaging operator
T:L_{0}^{2}(G)\to L_{0}^{2}(G)
given by

T(f)=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}f\circ s

The existence of the spectral gap for this operator (i.e.
\|T\|_{op}<1
) has been conjectured by Sarnak to hold in the case when
S=S^{-1}
and the generators of
S
are Haar generic, but the only known examples (as far as we know) are when
S
consists of elements with algebraic entries (up to conjugation).

Consider the following irreducible subgroup:

Let
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
be an irrational multiple of
2\pi
, and
\lambda>0
, define the matrices

\displaystyle R_{\theta}:=\begin{bmatrix}cos(\theta)&-sin(\theta)\\
sin(\theta)&\cos(\theta)\end{bmatrix}\ \ \text{and}\ \ A_{\lambda}:=\begin{bmatrix}e^{\lambda}&0\\
0&e^{-\lambda}\end{bmatrix}

Define the subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

\Gamma_{\theta,\lambda}:=\langle R_{\theta}\times A_{\lambda},A_{\lambda}\times R_{\theta}\rangle

Let
\Delta_{\theta,\lambda}
be the projection of
\Gamma_{\theta,\lambda}
in the first factor (or second factor, as they are the same).

The following holds:

Proposition B.2
.

For every
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
irrational multiple of
\pi
, and
\lambda>0
, the group
\Gamma_{\theta,\lambda}
is an irreducible subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover for Lebesgue a.e.
\theta,\lambda
, the group
\Gamma_{\theta,\lambda}
is free.

Question B.3
.

Does for Lebesgue a.e.
\theta
, there exists
\lambda_{\theta}
, such that for every
\lambda\in[\lambda_{\theta},\infty)
, the group
\Gamma_{\theta,\lambda}
is discrete?

The reason why one might expect something like this to hold is that for a non-trivial word
w(x,y)
in the free group
F_{2}
, it seems plausible that at least one among
w(R_{\theta},A_{\lambda})
or
w(A_{\lambda},R_{\theta})
must be far from the identity. Nonetheless, this seems difficult to achieve because of the following:

Proposition B.4
.

If
\Gamma_{\theta,\lambda}
is discrete, then the projection
\Delta_{\theta,\lambda}
into one (both) of the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
factors is strongly diophantine.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Delta_{\theta,\lambda}
is not strongly diophantine, then if we let
C:=3\log\|A_{\lambda}\|+1
, there exists a divergent sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k}>0
, and words
w_{k}
of length
n_{k}
in
S
such that

0<d(\text{Id},w_{k}(R_{\theta},A_{\lambda}))<e^{-Cn_{k}}

Consider the word
w^{\prime}_{k}=[w_{k}(x,y),x^{l}w_{k}(y,x)x^{-l}]
where
[,]
denotes the commutator, and
l
is a fixed integer to be determined later. We show that both projections tend to the identity as
k
goes to infinity.

The projection in the first factor is:

[w_{k}(R_{\theta},A_{\lambda}),R_{\theta}^{l}w_{k}(A_{\lambda},R_{\theta})R_{\theta}^{-l}]

Observe that
\|w_{k}(A_{\lambda},R_{\theta})\|<e^{\frac{C}{3}n_{k}}
, where
\|.\|
denotes the operator norm (which is submultiplicative), and so one can check easily that:

d(1,[w_{k}(R_{\theta},A_{\lambda}),R_{\theta}^{l}w_{k}(A_{\lambda},R_{\theta})R_{\theta}^{-l}])<Me^{-\frac{C}{3}n_{k}}

for some constant
M>0
(independent of
k
, but possibly depending on
l
).

One shows similarly the second projection

[w_{k}(A_{\lambda},R_{\theta}),A^{l}w_{k}(R_{\theta},A_{\lambda})A^{-l}]

satisfy a similar inequality and so if
\Gamma_{\lambda,\theta}
were discrete, then for
k
large enough, we have that both projections are trivial, and so for
k
large and
l=1,2,3
, we have
w_{k}(R_{\theta},A_{\lambda})
and
R_{\theta}^{l}w_{k}(A_{\lambda},R_{\theta})R_{\theta}^{-l}
commute, and also
w_{k}(A_{\lambda},R_{\theta})
and
A^{l}w_{k}(R_{\theta},A_{\lambda})A^{-l}
commute, by considering the action by these matrices in the hyperbolic plane, one can see that this only can happen if
A_{\lambda}
and
R_{\theta}
commute, a contradiction, and therefore
\Gamma_{\lambda,\theta}
is not discrete.
∎

Decentralized Differentially Private Power Method
Andrew Campbell, Anna Scaglione, Sean Peisert

Abstract
We propose a novel Decentralized Differentially Private Power Method (D-DP-PM) for performing Principal Component Analysis (PCA) in networked multi-agent settings. Unlike conventional decentralized PCA approaches where each agent accesses the full n-dimensional sample space, we address the challenging scenario where each agent observes only a subset of dimensions through row-wise data partitioning. Our method ensures ( ϵ , δ ) (\epsilon,\delta) -Differential Privacy (DP) while enabling collaborative estimation of global eigenvectors across the network without requiring a central aggregator. We achieve this by having agents share only local embeddings of the current eigenvector iterate, leveraging both the inherent privacy from random initialization and carefully calibrated Gaussian noise additions. We prove that our algorithm satisfies the prescribed ( ϵ , δ ) (\epsilon,\delta) -DP guarantee and establish convergence rates that explicitly characterize the impact of the network topology. Our theoretical analysis, based on linear dynamics and high-dimensional probability theory, provides tight bounds on both privacy and utility. Experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that D-DP-PM achieves superior privacy-utility tradeoffs compared to naive local DP approaches, with particularly strong performance in moderate privacy regimes ( ϵ ∈ [ 2 , 5 ] \epsilon\in[2,5] ). The method converges rapidly, allowing practitioners to trade iterations for enhanced privacy while maintaining competitive utility.

III-B

Convergence

Before continuing we need to introduce some additional assumptions about the distributed data
{\bm{X}}
and the effect of the convergence error.

Assumption 3
.

Suppose

\displaystyle E:=\left\lVert\bm{\Xi}-{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}\right\rVert,

(35)

then we assume that

\displaystyle E\leq\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}.

(36)

To provide bounds on the final error in
{\bm{q}}^{(T)}
we utilize the a similar strategy as the DP proof. Recall by (
18
) we can rewrite
{\bm{q}}^{(T)}
in terms of linear combinations of the initial and per iteration noise meaning we can full specify the final distribution of
{\bm{q}}^{(T)}
. The idea is that we can use a well understood tail bound on a Gaussian vector. More formally, we consider our error metric to be, where
{\bm{v}}
is the true principal eigenvector of
{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\sin({\bm{v}},{\bm{q}})=\dfrac{\left\lVert({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top}){{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert}.

(37)

The core idea for bounding (
37
) is to split the error into two components. The first, is measuring how close the version of
{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}
computed through the “consensus” iterations, denoted as
\bm{\Xi}
, is to
{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}
. The second, is how close the D-DP-PM gets to the principal eigenvector of
\bm{\Xi}
. Then, by applying Davis-Khan
[
26
]
and Hanson–Wright
[
23
]
concentration inequality we can, with high probability bound
\sin({\bm{v}},{\bm{q}})
.

This approach is one of our contributions since it completely deviates from the state-of-the-art
[
15
,
9
,
14
,
20
]
. Because
[
15
,
9
,
14
]
are extensions/modifications of the technique developed by
[
20
]
, we will only highlight the key differences between our work and
[
20
]
. One immediate observation is that
[
20
]
does not preserve Gaussianity and thus cannot utilize our approach.
[
20
]
alternatively uses a high probability bound on the per iteration noise to verify that it is bounded such that the size of the perturbation does not push the iterate by a greater amount than the PM convergence. While both methods are valid we have one distinct advantage with our approach. Our proof comes directly from first principles meaning the logic is straight forward and the analytical tools are well understood leading to clean and clear analysis. Below is the statement of the convergence theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
{\bm{q}}^{(T)}
be the final unnormalized eigenvector produced by Algorithm

such that
{\bm{W}}
and
{\bm{X}}
satisfy Assumptions

, where

\displaystyle{\bm{q}}^{(T)}\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left({\bm{0}},\bm{\Omega}\right).

(38)

Furthermore, define
{\bm{P}}:=({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})
and
{\bm{Q}}:=\bm{\Omega}^{1/2}{\bm{P}}\bm{\Omega}^{1/2}
with
\Theta:=1-\dfrac{{\bm{v}}^{\top}{\bm{v}}}{\Tr(\Omega)}+\Delta
where
\Delta\geq\dfrac{2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\log(1/\gamma)}+2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\log(1/\gamma)}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})}.
Finally, if

\mu_{i}
denotes the
i-

\bm{\Xi}
and

\displaystyle\rho:=\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{2})^{-2k}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}{\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{1})^{-2k}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}\right),

(39)

then

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\lVert({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top}){\bm{q}}^{(T)}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert{\bm{q}}^{(T)}\right\rVert^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle~~~~~\dfrac{2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\log(1/\gamma)}+2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\log(1/\gamma)}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})}

\displaystyle~~~~~+2\left(\dfrac{n_{i}m\lambda_{2}^{c}({\bm{W}})}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}+(n-1)\rho\left(\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{2T}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{w.p }1-\gamma.

(40)

The full proof for Theorem
3
is in Appendix
A-C1
. However, the astute reader will notice that we have not provided any indication on an appropriate choice of the parameters
\sigma_{{\bm{q}}}
,
\alpha
, or
\sigma^{2}_{{\bm{p}}}(t)
. In fact, one of the advantages of our analysis is that it provides insight on the best choice of parameters for convergence through the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

If
\dfrac{1}{\mu_{1}}<\alpha<\dfrac{1}{\mu_{2}}
and
\sigma_{{\bm{p}}}(t)\leq\left(\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{t}
, then

\displaystyle\rho\leq 1+\dfrac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{{\bm{q}}}(1-(\alpha\mu_{1})^{-2})},

(41)

which implies that
\rho
is bounded by a constant.

For a proof of Corollary
1
see Appendix
A-C2
. While Theorem
3
and Corollary
1
suggest a larger
\sigma_{{\bm{q}}}
, this has to be balanced by the choice of
\alpha
. However, a reasonable choice
\sigma_{\bm{q}}
is the one which keeps the norm of
{\bm{q}}
as close to unit. Therefore, by applying the Laurent–Massart inequality
[
27
]
we get that
\sigma_{\bm{q}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
.

Theorem
3
in conjunction with Corollary
1
directly connects the consensus aggregation error, the Gaussian perturbations, and the convergence of the power method. Theorem
3
indicates that the proposed D-DP-PM method recovers the expected convergence rate as in the centralized case, but retains an additive error from the consensus aggregation and the DP noise additions. Furthermore, in the centralized case the error is bounded by the ratio of
\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}
while the decentralized case is with respect to
\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}
. Intuitively and formally this is clear since in the decentralized setting the power iteration is with respect to the network matrix
\bm{\Xi}
not
{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}
.

We note that the convergence theorem indicates the impact of the network topology and consensus errors via the
\lambda_{2}^{c}({\bm{W}})
term. An additional, convenient, observation is that the term
\lambda_{2}^{c}({\bm{W}})
can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
c
to be very large. In fact, additional rounds of consensus, for a given iteration, do not increase the privacy leakage since the points shared during consensus have already been made public and thus no additional private information is included.

The key take away is that the D-DP-PM will converge towards
{\bm{v}}
at a similar rate to the centralized case, but will reach a saturation point where it can not improve due to the consensus errors and the DP noise. However, given that the algorithm designer has choice over
\sigma^{2}_{{\bm{p}}}(t)
and
c
, this saturation point can be significantly reduced.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented D-DP-PM, the first decentralized differentially private power method for eigenspace estimation when data is distributed row-wise across agents. Our approach addresses a fundamental gap in the literature by enabling privacy-preserving PCA in fully decentralized settings without central aggregation, while handling the challenging case where no single agent has access to the full dimensional space.
One of our key contributions is a novel algorithm design that preserves Gaussianity throughout iterations, enabling tractable privacy analysis through closed-form Rényi divergence bounds. Furthermore, we developed convergence analysis from first principles that explicitly captures the interplay between consensus errors and differential privacy noise. Furthermore, our empirical evaluation demonstrates that D-DP-PM substantially outperforms naive approaches across multiple datasets, with the advantage being most pronounced in moderate privacy settings. Additionally we show that privacy practitioners can exchange number of iterations for improved privacy.

\displaystyle\beta_{i}\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left(0,\sigma_{{\bm{q}}}^{2}\right)~\text{ and }~\omega_{i}(k)\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left(0,\sigma_{p}(k)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bm{q}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}{\bm{u}}_{i}.

Similarly, we can fully specify the distribution of
\tilde{{\bm{q}}}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left({\bm{0}},\underbrace{\alpha^{2T}\sigma^{2}_{{\bm{q}}}\bm{\Xi\Xi}^{\top}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}\alpha^{2T-k}\sigma^{2}_{{\bm{p}}}(k)\left(\bm{\Xi\Xi}^{\top}\right)^{T-k}}_{\bm{\Omega}}\right).

(59)

Furthermore, we denote
{\bm{u}}
as the principal eigenvector of
\bm{\Xi}
.
We define
{\bm{P}}:=({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})
and
{\bm{Q}}:=\bm{\Omega}^{1/2}{\bm{P}}\bm{\Omega}^{1/2}
. By doing so we can then write

\displaystyle\tilde{q}\sim\bm{\Omega}^{1/2}{\bm{s}}\quad{\bm{s}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{0}},{\bm{I}}_{n}).

(60)

This conveniently yields

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{\left\lVert({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{{\bm{s}}^{\top}{\bm{Q}}{\bm{s}}}{{\bm{s}}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{s}}}.

(61)

Therefore, we choose a constant
\Theta
such that

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{\left\lVert({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert}\right)^{2}\leq\Theta,

(62)

yielding the classic quadratic equation

\displaystyle{\bm{s}}^{\top}\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right){\bm{s}}\leq 0.

(63)

We then introduce another constant
\Delta>0
so that

\displaystyle\Theta=1-\dfrac{{\bm{v}}^{\top}{\bm{v}}}{\Tr(\Omega)}+\Delta.

(64)

This further implies that

\displaystyle\Pr\left(R^{2}\geq\Theta)=\Pr({\bm{s}}^{\top}\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right){\bm{s}}>c\right)

(65)

Therefore, we can apply the Hanson-Wright
[
23
]
tail bound to

\displaystyle\Pr\left({\bm{s}}^{\top}\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right){\bm{s}}-\Tr({\bm{s}}^{\top}\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right){\bm{s}})\geq c\right)\leq\gamma.

(66)

We notice that

\displaystyle\Tr({\bm{s}}^{\top}\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right){\bm{s}})

\displaystyle=\Tr({\bm{Q}})-\Theta\Tr(\bm{\Omega})

\displaystyle=\Tr(\bm{\Omega})-{\bm{v}}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{v}}-\Theta\Tr(\bm{\Omega})

\displaystyle=-\Delta\Tr(\bm{\Omega}).

(67)

There if we choose

\displaystyle\Delta\geq

\displaystyle~~\dfrac{2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\log(\frac{1}{\gamma})}+2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\log(\frac{1}{\gamma})}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})},

(68)

we can apply Hanson-Wright
[
23
]
and we get

\displaystyle\Pr\left({\bm{s}}^{\top}\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right){\bm{s}}-\Tr({\bm{s}}^{\top}\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right){\bm{s}})\right.

\displaystyle~~~~~\left.\geq\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\log(1/\gamma)})+\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\log(1/\gamma)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~\leq\gamma.

(69)

Finally, plugging this back into
65
we have that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\lVert({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle~~~\dfrac{2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\log(1/\gamma)}+2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\log(1/\gamma)}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+1-\dfrac{{\bm{v}}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{v}}}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{w.p. }1-\gamma

(70)

In order to see the convergence in terms of spectrum of
{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}
and
\bm{\Xi}
we need to bound

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{{\bm{v}}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{v}}}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})}.

(71)

we let
{\bm{u}}
denote the principal eigenvector of
\bm{\Xi}
and we assume that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\Xi}-{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}\right\rVert_{2}\leq E\qquad E\leq\lambda_{1}({\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top})-\lambda_{2}({\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}).

(72)

The fundamental idea is that since,
\mathbb{E}\left[\sin^{2}({\bm{v}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}})\right]=1-\dfrac{{\bm{v}}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{v}}}{\Tr\left(\bm{\Omega}\right)}
,
we can bound (
71
) by

\displaystyle\sin^{2}({\bm{v}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}})\leq 2\sin^{2}({\bm{v}},{\bm{u}})+2\sin^{2}({\bm{u}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}}).

(73)

Using this structure we first start with
\sin^{2}({\bm{u}},\tilde{{\bm{q}}})
.
By Davis-Kahan
[
26
]
, we have that

\displaystyle\sin\left({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\left\lVert\bm{\Xi}-{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top}\right\rVert_{2}}{\lambda_{1}({\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top})-\lambda_{2}({\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{E}{\lambda_{1}({\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top})-\lambda_{2}({\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\top})}

(74)

Now, if we can bound the ratio

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}\right]}{\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{1}\right]},

(75)

we can bound

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{{\bm{u}}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{u}}_{1}}{\Tr\left(\bm{\Omega}\right)}

(76)

since, by the fact that
\Tr(\bm{\Omega})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}\right]
and
{\bm{u}}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{u}}_{1}=\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}\right]
(by orthogonality of
{\bm{u}}
),

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{{\bm{u}}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{u}}_{1}}{\Tr\left(\bm{\Omega}\right)}=\dfrac{\sum_{i=2}^{n}\mathrm{Var}[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}]}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}\right]}\leq(n-1)\dfrac{\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}\right]}{\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{1}\right]}.

(77)

First notice that
\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}\right]=(\alpha\mu_{i})^{2T}\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{i})^{2(T-k)}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)
, then we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{i}\right]}{\mathrm{Var}\left[\bm{\Gamma}_{1}\right]}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\alpha\mu_{i})^{2T}\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{i})^{2(T-k)}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}{(\alpha\mu_{1})^{2T}\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{1})^{2(T-k)}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{2T}\dfrac{\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{i})^{2(T-k)}(\alpha\mu_{2})^{-2T}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}{\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{1})^{2(T-k)}(\alpha\mu_{1})^{-2T}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~\text{ letting }\rho:=\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{2})^{-2k}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}{\sigma_{\bm{q}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{T}(\alpha\mu_{1})^{-2k}\sigma_{\bm{p}}^{2}(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{2T}\rho.

(78)

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{{\bm{v}}^{\top}\bm{\Omega}{\bm{v}}}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})}\leq 2\left(\dfrac{E}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}+(n-1)\rho\left(\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{2T}\right).

(79)

Furthermore, we have that
E\leq n_{i}m\lambda_{2}^{c}({\bm{W}})
. Therefore our final bound becomes

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\lVert({\bm{I}}-{\bm{v}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert\tilde{{\bm{q}}}\right\rVert^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle~~~~~\dfrac{2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{F}\sqrt{\log(1/\gamma)}+2\left\lVert\left({\bm{Q}}-\Theta\bm{\bm{\Omega}}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\log(1/\gamma)}{\Tr(\bm{\Omega})}

\displaystyle~~~~~+2\left(\dfrac{n_{i}m\lambda_{2}^{c}({\bm{W}})}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}+(n-1)\rho\left(\dfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{2T}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{w.p }1-\gamma,

(80)

thus completing the proof.
∎

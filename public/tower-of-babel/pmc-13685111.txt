Interhospital transfer of older adults with thoracic injury in Türkiye: a nationwide study
Authors: Muhammed Saltuk Deniz, Kadir Baturhan Ciflik, Busra Ozdemir Ciflik, Aysenur Yetik Aras
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe increasing older adult population is raising the burden of trauma-related healthcare services, with thoracic trauma resulting in high mortality and long hospital stays in this group. Therefore, appropriate triage and interhospital transfer are critical for improving clinical outcomes. The aim of this study is to evaluate the data on the referral patterns of older adults with thoracic trauma in Turkey who were transferred by road and air.Materials and methodsThe patients’ age, gender, nationality, referring–receiving province and region, referral time, distance, and ICD-10 codes were retrospectively evaluated.ResultsThe median age of the total 759 patients was 90, and 94.3% were transferred within the province. 92.8% of transfers were by road, and 7.2% were by air. Transfers during working hours were more likely to be done by air, while transfers outside of working hours were more likely to be done by road (p = 0.002). The Southeastern Anatolia region was the most common referring region for air transport (n = 20, 36.4%), whereas the Central Anatolia region was the most common receiving region (n = 20, 36.4%).ConclusionInterhospital transfer patterns in geriatric thoracic trauma patients in Türkiye demonstrate distinct differences based on transport modality, distance, and regional distribution. Introduction

The rapid increase in the elderly population on a global scale is leading to a significant increase in the burden of trauma-related healthcare [1]. Current data show that approximately 28% of trauma-related deaths occur in the older adult population, highlighting the critical importance of this group in trauma care [2]. Thoracic trauma, in particular, is associated with high mortality rates in patients over 80 years of age, further increasing this burden [3]. In addition to mortality, it has been reported that hospitalization and intensive care unit stays are significantly longer in these patients [4]. These findings reveal that thoracic trauma is a significant clinical and healthcare system issue in geriatric patient management.

The fact that older adults sustain serious injuries even with low-energy trauma mechanisms reflects the physiological fragility of this population [5]. Therefore, accurate and effective triage at the time of presentation to trauma centers is critical for both improving clinical outcomes and ensuring the balanced use of healthcare resources [5, 6]. However, even when necessary preparations are made before transfer, older adults are reported to wait an average of 32 min longer than younger patients at the centers to which they are transferred [6]. These delays indicate potential age-related complications and highlight crucial processes in geriatric trauma care that require enhancement.

In line with this, our study evaluated the operational factors affecting the transfer of older adults referred due to thoracic trauma across Turkey, along with regional differences and the relationship between clinical diagnoses.

Materials and methods

Our study included patients aged 65 years and older who were diagnosed with thoracic trauma in hospitals in Türkiye between 2022 and 2024. Patients were required to have undergone interhospital transfer by ambulance and have complete data. To avoid potential confounding in the diagnostic assessment of thoracic trauma, patients who had received any prior trauma-related medical or surgical intervention that could alter or obscure the diagnosis (e.g., chest tube insertion, thoracic surgery, or advanced medical treatment) before the initial hospital evaluation were excluded. Patients who did not meet these criteria were excluded. All patients had documented emergency medical service (EMS) records in the national ambulance database.The study was designed retrospectively.

The researchers examined data files from the Republic of Türkiye Ministry of Health, General Directorate of Emergency Health Services. After anonymizing the patient information, the following data were recorded: patients’ age, gender, nationality, province and geographical region of referral and destination, and the date and time of referral. Patients were identified using ICD-10 codes related to thoracic trauma. In - hours were defined as 08:30–17:30 on weekdays (Monday–Friday). Out-offworking hours were defined as all times outside these periods, including weekends (Saturday and Sunday) and public holidays. In Türkiye, provinces represent administrative units, whereas geographical regions are broader classifications based on geographic and socio-economic characteristics. Both classifications were used to analyze transfer patterns.

For distance-based analyses, only inter-provincial transfers were included. Intra-provincial transfers were analyzed separately as a non-distance category and were not assigned a numerical distance value, due to limitations of the dataset. For road transport distance, calculations available on the official website of the General Directorate of Highways of the Republic of Turkey were used (www.kgm.gov.tr). For air transport distance, Google Maps (www.google.com/maps) was used for bird’s-eye distance calculations. Ethical committee approval was obtained for the study protocol under the number E-2025-21. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, written consent was not obtained from patients for the study. The study was conducted in accordance with the World Medical Association’s Declaration of Helsinki.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using the IBM SPSS Statistics Standard Concurrent User V 27 statistical package (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, USA). Descriptive statistics were presented as median (interquartile range, minimum - maximum) (M (IQR, min - max)) values, as the data did not show a normal distribution. The normality of the distribution of data for numerical variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk normality test. The homogeneity of variances was assessed using the Levene test. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare numerical variables with two categorical variables, as the data were not normally distributed. The exact chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables with each other. If the chi-square test result was significant, subgroup analyses were performed using Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc analysis. In all comparisons, p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General analysis of patients

Our study included 759 patients in total, of whom 437 (57.6%) were women and 322 (42.4%) were men. The median age of the patients was 90 years (89–93, 65–107). A total of 756 patients (99.6%) were citizens of the Republic of Turkey. The most common diagnosis among transferred patients was rib fracture (n = 395; 52%). A total of 716 patients (94.3%) were transferred within the province. Of these, 704 (92.8%) were transferred by ground and 55 (7.2%) by air. The median transfer distance out-of-provincial transfers was 378 (0–589, 44–1.020) kilometers. Of the transfers performed, 483 (63.6%) took place out-of-hours and 276 (36.4%) took place in-hours (Table 1).

General analysis of patients transported by ground

A total of 422 (59.9%) female and 282 (40.1%) male patients were transported by ground. The median age of patients was 90 years (89–93, 65–107). 701 patients (99.6%) were citizens of the Republic of Turkey. Of these patients, 686 (97.4%) were transported within the in-province, while 18 (2.6%) were transported out-of-provinces. The median out-of-province transfer distance was 151 (0–94, 44–314) kilometers. Of the transports carried out, 459 (65.2%) occurred out-off-hours. The most common diagnosis requiring transfer was rib fracture (n = 371, 52.7%) (Table 2). Istanbul was the province that referred and received the most patients (n = 103, 14.6%) (Fig. 1). The geographical region with the highest number of referrals sent and received was Marmara (n = 184, 26.1%) (Fig. 2a).

General analysis of patients transported by air

A total of 55 patients were transported by air: 15 females (27.3%) and 40 males (72.7%). The median age of the patients was 77 years (5, 73–81). All 55 patients (100%) were Turkish citizens. Thirty patients (54.5%) were transported within the in-province and 25 (45.5%) out-of-provinces. The median out-of-province transfer distance was 707 (0–425, 199–1,020) kilometers. Thirty-one (56.4%) of the transports were carried out-off-hours. The most common diagnosis among transferred patients was rib fracture (n = 24, 43.6%) (Table 2). Istanbul was the province with the highest number of transfers (n = 6, 10.9%), while Ankara was the province receiving the highest number of transfers (n = 17, 30.9%) (Fig. 3). The geographical region with the highest number of referrals was Southeastern Anatolia (n = 20, 36.4%), and the region receiving the highest number of referrals was Central Anatolia (n = 20, 36.4%) (Fig. 2b). Comparing ground and air transport

Patients transported by air were found to be significantly younger than those transported by ground (p < 0.001). The vast majority of patients transferred within the in-province were transported by ground (95.8%), whereas a significant proportion of those transferred out-of-province were transported by air (58.1%) (p < 0.001). Air transport increased significantly in-hours (11.2%), while ground transport increased significantly out-off-hours (95.0%) (p = 0.002). A significant relationship was found between the mode of transport and the geographical regions from which patients were transported (p < 0.001). The Central Anatolia Region received more air transports significantly higher (n = 20, 15%). Out-of-provincial transfers, those performed by air ambulance cover significantly longer distances compared to ground ambulance transfers (p < 0.001) (Table 2).

Comparison of in-provincial and out-of-provincial transfers

Patients transferred within the in-province were significantly older than those transferred out-of-province (p < 0.001). Out-of-provincial transfers due to traumatic haemothorax were significantly higher (n = 8, 18.6%; p = 0.006). The vast majority of patients transferred within the in-province were transported by road (95.8%), whereas a significant proportion of those transferred out-of-province were transported by air (58.1%) (p < 0.001). Out-of-provincial transfers were higher in the Eastern Anatolia Region (n = 10, 23.3%). Conversely, out-of-provincial transfers were significantly lower in the Marmara Region (n = 2, 4.7%) (p < 0.001). A significant relationship was found between the type of referral and the geographical regions to which patients were referred (p < 0.001). Out-of-province transfers were most frequently referred from Southeastern and Eastern Anatolia, whereas they were most commonly directed to Central Anatolia. In contrast, in-province transfers were predominantly concentrated in the Marmara region. However, the analysis of subgroups found no statistical relationship supporting this (Table 3).

Discussion

Our study presents a large-scale, nationwide assessment of inter-hospital transfers of older adults with thoracic trauma in Turkey, conducted by both road and air ambulances. To our knowledge, this study is one of the most comprehensive analyses to simultaneously examine the relationships between transfer modes, regional transfer patterns, and types of trauma. The findings reveal significant differences between road and air transfers in terms of patient age, transfer distance, transfer time, and trauma patterns, as well as meaningful transfer differences between regions.

The most common injury observed in patients was a rib fracture. The median age of patients was quite high. Following rib fractures, hemopneumothorax, pneumothorax, and hemothorax were also frequently observed. In trauma registries in Turkey, Europe, and North America, rib fractures account for over half of thoracic injuries, particularly in older age groups [7, 8]. In older adults, even minor trauma can result in serious injury when additional factors such as osteoporosis are considered [9]. Thoracic trauma has been shown to significantly increase hospital stays and complication rates in older adults [2]. Currently, thoracic trauma accounts for around a third of all trauma admissions and around a quarter of trauma-related deaths [9]. Therefore, we believe that special protocols, independent of diagnosis, could be implemented for inter-hospital transfers to provide this specific patient group with better healthcare.

Road ambulances transported the vast majority of patients, with air ambulances used in only 7.2% of cases. In other countries, air ambulances also typically represent a small proportion of total transports and are mostly used for patients requiring long-distance or time-critical transport [10, 11]. In our study, patients transported by air were younger, travelled longer distances in out-of-provincial transfers, and were especially preferred for inter-regional transfers, which is consistent with the literature. The relatively younger age of patients transported by air within the older adults may reflect triage preferences favoring patients with higher potential benefit from rapid transport, as well as clinical decision-making based on survivability and transfer feasibility rather than age alone. This finding corroborates international findings that the use of helicopter emergency medical services is standard practice for long-distance and high-risk injuries [12–16].

Interestingly, certain high-risk conditions such as rib fractures and hemopneumothorax were more frequently transported by ground rather than air. This finding may reflect operational and logistical factors rather than purely clinical decision-making. Limited availability of air transport, weather conditions, regional resource distribution, and real-time triage decisions may all influence the choice of transport modality. Additionally, the relatively low number of patients transported by air for some high-risk conditions suggests that factors other than injury severity may play a role in transport selection. Further studies incorporating detailed clinical severity scores and prehospital decision-making criteria are needed to better understand these patterns.

Although a patient with tracheal injury was transported by air, the small number of cases limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions regarding transport preferences for such conditions. The literature emphasizes that rapid transfer to high-level centers is critical for major airway injuries due to the need for early intervention [17, 18]. This finding is consistent with current trauma management principles.

Significant differences in patient transfers between geographical regions in Turkey have been identified. The Marmara, Central Anatolia, and Black Sea regions stand out as both sending and receiving the highest number of patients. The presence of a large number of high-volume tertiary centers in these regions can be considered the main reason for this distribution. Similarly, well-equipped trauma centers in large cities in other countries bear a high referral burden [19–21]. The apparent similarity between the number of patients referred from and received by individual provinces may be explained by the bidirectional nature of the interhospital transfer system. In this system, provinces can function both as referring and receiving centers depending on patient needs, resource availability, and case distribution. Therefore, the balanced distribution observed in the figures does not indicate equal healthcare capacity but rather reflects the dynamic and reciprocal structure of the national transfer network. However, the higher rate of inter-regional referrals in regions such as Eastern Anatolia, where healthcare resources are limited due to geographical factors, suggests that the capacity of regional trauma care is open to improvement. Trauma records from rural areas in many countries have also reported high rates of long-distance transfers [11, 13, 22].

Examining the mode of transfer according to referral time revealed that air ambulances were used more frequently during in-hours, whereas ground ambulances were significantly more prevalent out-off hours. Additionally, in out-of-provincial transfers, distances were longer for those conducted by air compared to those by ground. This findings is consistent with international studies showing that HEMS activation is influenced by personnel availability, operational conditions, and environmental factors [12, 14–16].

Limitations

Our study has certain limitations. These include its retrospective design; the lack of certain auxiliary data needed to evaluate the transfer pattern (vital signs, Glasgow Coma Scale scores, whether surgical procedures were performed in the emergency department, diagnoses related to extrathoracic trauma, reasons for transfer, etc.); the absence of patient follow-up data from the pre-hospital setting and the receiving hospital; and the uncertainty regarding exact transfer distances. Due to limitations in the dataset, in-provincial transfer distances were recorded as zero, which may underestimate true transfer distances and limit the granularity of distance-based analyses. The relatively small number of patients transported by air for certain diagnostic subgroups limits the interpretation of transport modality differences for specific injury types. Additionally, different methods were used to calculate road and air transport distances, which may have introduced measurement bias.

Conclusion

Interhospital transfer patterns in geriatric thoracic trauma patients in Türkiye demonstrate distinct differences based on transport modality, distance, and regional distribution. Air transport was more frequently used for longer-distance transfers and inter-regional referrals. However, due to the lack of clinical severity and outcome data, these findings should be interpreted as descriptive patterns rather than indicators of system effectiveness. On the other hand, significant referral differences between regions highlight the need to strengthen trauma care, particularly in areas with limited capacity. Optimizing referral protocols and supporting regional trauma centers will contribute to ensuring timely and appropriate care for older adults with thoracic trauma.

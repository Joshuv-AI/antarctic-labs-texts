Construction and validation of a predictive model for the risk of atrial fibrillation in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction: a single-center retrospective analysis
Authors: Pan Chen, Xiaoyan Yin, Lei Ren
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo explore the risk factors for atrial fibrillation (AF) in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) and to construct a predictive model for AF in this population.MethodApproximately 334 patients with HFpEF who were treated in the Department of Cardiology at Fuyang People’s Hospital from January 2022 to April 2024 were included. Patients were followed up for one year to observe whether they developed AF. All patients were randomly divided into a training set and a validation set in a 7:3 ratio, with the training set used for model construction and the validation set used for model performance evaluation. The Boruta algorithm was used for feature selection, and a predictive model was constructed using multivariate logistic regression analysis. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsLogistic regression analysis revealed that coronary heart disease (CHD), body mass index (BMI), diastolic pressure (DP), free triiodothyronine (FT3), glomerular filtration rate (GFR), and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor (SGLT2i) were independent risk factors for the occurrence of atrial fibrillation in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (P < 0.05). The AUC was 0.871 in the training set and 0.825 in the validation set; the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test yielded χ² = 5.3976 (P = 0.7144); decision curve analysis (DCA) indicated that the predictive model could provide superior clinical net benefit for patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction.ConclusionA predictive model constructed using six predictive variables—CHD, BMI, DP, FT3, GFR, and SGLT2i—can effectively predict the risk of atrial fibrillation in patients with HFpEF and aid in early risk stratification.Keywords: HFpEF, AF, Boruta algorithm, SGLT2i, Predictive model

Introduction

Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF) is a complex cardiovascular disease that has become one of the most common types of heart failure in the global aging population [1]. The primary characteristic of HFpEF is impaired diastolic function, despite normal left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), with patients often experiencing issues such as impaired filling and elevated left atrial pressure [2]. This pathological state is often associated with metabolic disorders such as hypertension, diabetes, and obesity, and is associated with significantly higher all-cause mortality and hospitalization rates [3]. Atrial Fibrillation (AF) is highly prevalent in HFpEF patients and is closely associated with disease progression [4]. AF not only exacerbates symptoms but also significantly increases the risk of stroke and death [5].

However, despite the high prevalence of AF in HFpEF patients, there is currently no AF risk prediction model specifically tailored for this patient population [6]. Existing prediction models typically focus on patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) or general heart failure, failing to adequately account for the unique physiological characteristics of HFpEF patients [7, 8]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop a precise AF prediction model for HFpEF patients to enable early identification of high-risk individuals and support clinical interventions [9]. This prediction model will facilitate early prevention of AF in HFpEF, thereby reducing the risk of complications and mortality.

Materials and methods

General information

From January 2022 to April 2024, approximately 334 patients with HFpEF were treated in the Department of Cardiology at Fuyang People’s Hospital. After discharge, patients were followed up every three months through outpatient visits. Twenty-four patients were lost to follow-up, and 310 patients were ultimately included in the study.

Inclusion criteria

(1) Aged over 18 years. (2) Meets the diagnostic criteria of the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guidelines. (3) Complete baseline clinical data. (4) No history of AF confirmed by 12-lead electrocardiogram and medical history at enrollment.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Severe valvular heart disease, hypertrophic/restrictive cardiomyopathy, active myocarditis/pericarditis, uncorrected congenital heart disease. (2) End-stage renal disease, active malignant tumors, severe liver disease, uncontrolled systemic infection, severe COPD, or life expectancy < 1 year. (3) Uncontrolled hyperthyroidism. (4) Inability to comply with follow-up or severe lack of baseline data.

Standard of practice

This study included approximately 334 patients with HFpEF who were treated in the Department of Cardiology at Fuyang People’s Hospital between January 2022 and April 2024. After discharge, AF occurrence was recorded through regular outpatient follow-ups and electrocardiogram (ECG) examinations. Patients were randomly assigned to a training group and a validation group in a 7:3 ratio. Feature selection was performed using the Boruta algorithm, and a predictive model was constructed using multivariate logistic regression analysis, with a column chart generated. The calibration of the predictive model and its clinical net benefit were assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, calibration curves, and decision curves (DCA).

Observation indicators

The primary observation indicators include clinical demographic characteristics, laboratory test indicators, and cardiac ultrasound examinations, as detailed below: Gender, age, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), history of diabetes, history of coronary heart disease (CHD), history of transient ischemic attack (TIA), history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), smoking history, alcohol consumption history, use of SGLT2 inhibitors, pulse rate, systolic blood pressure (SP), diastolic blood pressure (DP), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left atrial diameter (LA), left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPW), interventricular septum thickness (IVS), NYHA classification, N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NTproBNP), C-reactive protein (CRP), white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NE), lymphocyte count (LYM), neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio (NLR), hemoglobin (Hb), platelet count (Plt), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), free triiodothyronine (FT3), serum free thyroxine (FT4), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), albumin (ALB), creatinine (Cr), uric acid (UA), glomerular filtration rate (GFR), triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL), lipoprotein a (Lp-a).

Statistical treatment

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software. Continuous variables that followed a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s), while those with a skewed distribution were presented as median (M) and interquartile range (P25, P75). Intergroup comparisons for continuous variables were conducted using t-tests and Mann-Whitney U tests, as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as rates, counts, or percentages, and intergroup comparisons were performed using Pearson chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact probability tests. Data were randomly divided into a modeling group and a validation group in a 7:3 ratio using the “sample” package in R software (version 4.4.3). Feature selection was performed using the Boruta algorithm, and a predictive model was developed using multivariate logistic regression analysis. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to assess the model’s predictive performance for AF occurrence in HFpEF patients. Calibration curves and decision curves (DCA) were also used to evaluate the model’s calibration and clinical net benefit. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Boruta method feature selection

To identify predictors significantly associated with new-onset atrial fibrillation in patients with HFpEF, this study randomly divided the entire cohort into a training set (70%) and a validation set (30%), with all feature selection and model building strictly confined to the training set. The Boruta algorithm was employed to comprehensively assess the feature importance of 43 initial candidate variables within the training set. The Boruta algorithm identified genuinely relevant features by constructing random forests and comparing the importance of original features against randomly generated “shadow features.” After 100 iterations, the Boruta algorithm ultimately identified 9 key variables with significant predictive value (labeled “Confirmed”) for inclusion in subsequent multivariate logistic regression model development. These variables included: LA, BMI, CHD, DP, FT3, FT4, ALT, GFR, SGLT2i.(Fig. 1).

The Boruta algorithm was applied to the training set to evaluate the predictive importance of 43 candidate variables for new-onset atrial fibrillation in patients with HFpEF. After 100 iterations, 9 key predictive variables were ultimately identified (marked with green squares in the figure). These variables were determined to be the most relevant for inclusion in the multivariate logistic regression model. The nine identified variables included: Left Atrium(LA), Body Mass Index (BMI), Coronary Heart Disease (CHD), Diastolic Pressure (DP), Free Triiodothyronine (FT3), Free Thyroxine (FT4), Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT), Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR), and Sodium-glucose Cotransporter 2 Inhibitors (SGLT2i)

Multifactor logistic regression analysis model construction

Logistic stepwise regression was used to validate the 9 variables selected by the Boruta method in the modeling population. The results of the regression analysis showed that CHD, BMI, DP, FT3, GFR, and SGLT2 inhibitors were factors influencing the occurrence of AF in patients with HFpEF (P < 0.05).(Table 2).

Discussion

Body Mass Index (BMI) is an important predictor of AF in patients with HFpEF. Obesity increases the risk of AF in HFpEF patients through multiple mechanisms. Obesity not only increases the burden on the heart but also affects cardiac electrophysiological characteristics, atrial structure, and systemic inflammatory responses through various pathways, further promoting the development of AF [10]. In HFpEF patients, the presence of obesity leads to left atrial enlargement and increased pressure, thereby exacerbating atrial electrophysiological disorders [11]. At the microscopic level, obesity-induced atrial fat accumulation not only alters atrial mechanical load but also affects atrial electrophysiological stability through changes in adipokine secretion [12]. Additionally, obesity further affects cardiac electrophysiological stability through pathways such as activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and promotion of oxidative stress, providing a foundation for the development of AF [13]. The results of this study indicate that patients with HFpEF and higher BMI have a significantly increased incidence of AF, further confirming the impact of obesity on AF in HFpEF [14].

CHD is another important predictor of atrial fibrillation in HFpEF. Pathological changes such as myocardial ischemia, atrial enlargement, and atrial fibrosis caused by CHD exacerbate the risk of atrial fibrillation [15]. Mechanistic analysis indicates that CHD induces structural changes in the atrium, particularly atrial enlargement and atrial myocardial fibrosis, thereby promoting the occurrence of atrial fibrillation [16]. Chronic ischemia in CHD not only increases the burden on the atria but also alters the electrophysiological characteristics of atrial myocytes, reducing atrial electrical stability [17]. Atrial enlargement-induced atrioventricular conduction delay and exacerbated atrial fibrosis are the primary mechanisms by which CHD leads to AF [18]. Additionally, myocardial ischemia and atrial enlargement caused by coronary heart disease directly affect the depolarization process of the atria and increase the likelihood of atrial fibrillation by impairing the electrical stability of the atria [19]. Our study also found that the incidence of atrial fibrillation was significantly higher in HFpEF patients with coronary heart disease compared to those without coronary heart disease, further supporting the role of coronary heart disease in HFpEF-related atrial fibrillation [16].

Elevated diastolic pressure (DP) is closely associated with the occurrence of atrial fibrillation in HFpEF. Elevated diastolic pressure typically reflects increased peripheral vascular resistance, which directly leads to increased left atrial burden and triggers a series of electrophysiological changes [20]. At the microscopic level, the increased left atrial pressure caused by elevated DP further exacerbates atrial dilation and fibrosis, ultimately providing the electrophysiological basis for the development of AF [21]. Elevated DP not only affects cardiac function by altering hemodynamic status but also promotes the development of AF by exacerbating left atrial dilation [22]. Additionally, elevated diastolic pressure increases the incidence of AF by altering the degree of atrial structural fibrosis [23]. In our study, diastolic pressure was significantly higher in the AF group than in the non-AF group, further confirming the independent role of diastolic pressure in predicting AF in HFpEF [24].

Elevated levels of free triiodothyronine (FT3) are closely associated with the occurrence of atrial fibrillation in HFpEF. FT3 is an important indicator of metabolic status, and its elevated levels are relatively common in HFpEF patients and significantly correlated with the development of atrial fibrillation [25]. Increased FT3 affects cardiac electrophysiological characteristics through multiple mechanisms. Under high FT3 conditions, atrial excitability and automaticity are enhanced, leading to shortened atrial refractory periods and promoting electrophysiological instability, thereby increasing the risk of AF. Mechanistic analyses indicate that elevated FT3 levels not only accelerate the depolarization process in the atrium but also alter cardiac metabolic pathways, promoting atrial remodeling and increasing susceptibility to AF [26]. Excess FT3 may further aggravate atrial fibrosis and oxidative stress, providing a substrate for AF development [27]. In this study, patients with HFpEF and higher FT3 levels had a higher incidence of AF, further confirming FT3 as a predictive factor for AF in HFpEF [28].

Although the Boruta algorithm indicated that FT4 was more important than FT3, the logistic regression analysis selected FT3 rather than FT4 as an independent predictor. This discrepancy may seem puzzling, but it is due to the better predictive performance of the FT3 model, which is more effective at identifying the risk of AF in HFpEF patients. Despite FT4’s higher importance in the feature selection process, FT3’s inclusion in the final model aligns with its stronger association with the electrophysiological changes observed in this condition. FT3, being the active form of thyroid hormone, directly influences cardiac meabolic processes and atrial electrophysiological stability, thus providing a more direct and robust link to AF risk. This is why FT3 was chosen as the sole predictor, even though FT4 was also identified as important. The choice of FT3 over FT4 ultimately reflects its superior clinical relevance in this context.

Reduced glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is significantly associated with the occurrence of atrial fibrillation in HFpEF. Renal dysfunction increases the risk of atrial fibrillation through multiple pathways, particularly via oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, and activation of the RAAS [29]. At the microscopic level, oxidative stress and inflammatory responses induced by renal dysfunction accelerate atrial fibrosis, thereby providing an electrophysiological basis for the occurrence of atrial fibrillation [30]. Renal insufficiency increases cardiac burden by altering water-salt balance, hemodynamic status, and endothelial function, ultimately promoting the occurrence of AF. The results of this study indicate that the incidence of AF is significantly higher in HFpEF patients with low GFR compared to those with high GFR, further validating the importance of renal function in predicting AF in HFpEF.

SGLT2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) as therapeutic agents for HFpEF have been extensively studied and demonstrated significant atrial fibrillation prevention effects. SGLT2i significantly reduce the incidence of atrial fibrillation in HFpEF patients through multiple mechanisms, including improving cardiac metabolism, reducing cardiac burden, and anti-inflammatory effects. Mechanistic analyses indicate that SGLT2i protect the heart from electrophysiological changes by reducing oxidative stress, improving endothelial function, alleviating atrial fibrosis, and lowering atrial load, thereby effectively preventing the occurrence of AF. The anti-inflammatory, antioxidant effects, and improvements in cardiac metabolism of SGLT2i make them important drugs for the prevention and treatment of AF in HFpEF. Our study shows that the incidence of AF in HFpEF patients using SGLT2i is significantly lower than in those not using SGLT2i, consistent with existing research findings, further validating the potential of SGLT2i in the prevention and treatment of AF in HFpEF [31].

Although this study provides valuable reference for predictive models of AF in HFpEF, it still has some limitations. First, it is a single-center retrospective investigation with a limited sample size. Although the number of new-onset atrial fibrillation events met the basic requirements for multivariate regression analysis, and internal validation demonstrated robust model performance, the possibility of selection bias remains. Therefore, the conclusions drawn from this study and the predictive model developed require external validation in larger, multicenter prospective cohorts to further confirm their generalizability and clinical applicability. Second, this study did not consider other variables that may influence AF occurrence, such as left atrial volume and brain natriuretic peptide(BNP). Incorporating these factors may enhance the model’s accuracy. Most critically, the method for detecting new-onset atrial fibrillation represents a significant limitation of this study. Diagnosis relied on routine outpatient follow-up and standard electrocardiogram examinations, without systematic use of long-term ambulatory ECG monitoring. This approach likely leads to missed diagnoses of asymptomatic or paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, potentially underestimating the true incidence of AF and affecting the accuracy of our predictive model performance assessment. Future prospective studies should incorporate systematic long-term ECG monitoring to more accurately capture AF events and validate the findings of this study.

In summary, This study successfully constructed and validated a predictive model for assessing the risk of AF in patients with HFpEF through a single-center retrospective analysis. Using the Boruta algorithm for variable selection and multivariate logistic regression analysis, we identified six key variables—CHD, BMI, DP, FT3, GFR, and SGLT2i—as core predictive factors. The model demonstrated good discriminatory ability, calibration, and clinical utility in both the training and validation sets. The predictive model provided in this study integrates readily available clinical and laboratory indicators, offering a simple and effective tool for identifying high-risk populations for AF among HFpEF patients. This facilitates early risk stratification, guides individualized monitoring and intervention strategies, and may ultimately improve clinical outcomes for such patients.

This study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki. It has been approved by Ethics Committee of Fuyang People’s Hospital (approval number: Medical Ethics Review (2020) No. 9). This study is a retrospective study that only collects and analyzes existing, de-identified medical records and electrocardiogram results collected as part of routine medical care. Therefore, Ethics Committee of Fuyang People’s Hospital has waived the requirement to obtain prospective informed consent from study participants.

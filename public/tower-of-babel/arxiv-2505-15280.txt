Multisetting protocol for Bell correlated states detection with spin- f f systems
Arkadiusz Kobus, Xinwei Li, Mariusz Gajda, Li You, Emilia Witkowska

Abstract
We propose a multisetting protocol for the detection of two-body Bell correlations, and apply it to spin-nematic squeezed states realized in f f pairs of SU(2) subsystems within spin- f f atomic Bose–Einstein condensates. Experimental data for f = 1 f=1 , together with numerical simulations using the truncated Wigner method for f = 1 , 2 , 3 f=1,\,2,\,3 , demonstrate the protocol’s effectiveness. We derive Bell inequalities tailored to collective observables of these subsystems and show how tuning interaction parameters controls the number of independent settings. Our findings extend multisetting Bell tests to high-spin ultracold ensembles, enabling scalable, flexible measurement configurations. This establishes a pathway to quantum metrology and information processing in tunable many-body platforms.

Bell inequalities for multipartite entangled states that is characterized by by one- and two-body correlations in collective observables, have been theoretically defined and experimentally verified using bimodal squeezed Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs),

Despite advances with squeezed BECs, the implementation of multisetting scenarios, in particular, those involving independent measurements, remains underaddressed.

Squeezing within spin-
f

The spin-
f
BEC is a system characterized by a

The system is described by considering binary s-wave interactions that conserve the total spin

The reduction of the spatial degrees of freedom, by using the single mode approximation (SMA)

\hat{H}_{\rm int}=\left[c_{1}\hat{F}^{2}+c_{2}\hat{A}_{0,0}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{0,0}+c_{3}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-2}^{2}\hat{A}_{2,\mathcal{M}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{2,\mathcal{M}}\right],

(1)

for
f=1,2,3

\hat{A}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{m=-f}^{f}\langle f,m,f,\mathcal{M}-m|\mathcal{F},\mathcal{M}\rangle\hat{a}_{m}\hat{a}_{\mathcal{M}-m},

(2)

and
\langle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{M}|f,m;f,m^{\prime}\rangle
are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients while
\hat{a}_{m}
are annihilation operators of an atom at the magnetic level
m

c_{1/2/3}
are associated with the scattering lengths of colliding pairs of atoms with total spin
\mathcal{F}

[
23
,
24
]
.
In Eq.(
1

f
operator is given by
\hat{F}^{2}=\hat{F}_{x}^{2}+\hat{F}_{y}^{2}+\hat{F}_{z}^{2}
and the interaction coefficients satisfy
c_{1}\neq 0
and
c_{2/3}=0
for
f=1
,
c_{1/2}\neq 0
and
c_{3}=0
for
f=2
, and
c_{1/2/3}\neq 0
for
f=3
.
When an external magnetic field is included, the total Hamiltonian becomes

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\rm int}-q\sum_{m=-f}^{f}m^{2}\hat{N}_{m},

(3)

where

denotes the quadratic Zeeman energy coefficient

,

III
Bell inequality for spin-nematic squeezed states od spin-
f
systems

We employ a data-driven approach to derive the corresponding Bell inequality.
A pedagogical discussion of the derivation, together with an explicit presentation of the intermediate steps and the final expressions, is provided in Appendix
A
. Here we present only the key results.

The Bell inequality, compatible with local-variable theory for any input data
\vec{M}
(containing one-body correlators) and
\tilde{C}
(covariance of two-body correlations), as well as for any positive semi-definite matrix
A
and vector
\vec{h}
, can be defined using the data-driven method
[
25
,
40
]
,

L(A,\vec{h})=E_{\mathrm{max}}(A,\vec{h})+\vec{h}\cdot\vec{M}+\mathrm{Tr}[A\tilde{C}]\geq 0,

(8)

where the classical bound is set by
{E_{\mathrm{max}}(A,\vec{h})=N\max_{\vec{r}\in\{\pm 1/2\}}[\vec{r}^{T}A\vec{r}-\vec{h}\cdot\vec{r}]}
for two measurement outcomes.
Let us start with the choice of convenient measurement settings. Our analysis of various observables reveals that the most relevant is the collective operator,

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{\alpha,\mu}

\displaystyle=\hat{J}^{(s)}_{\mathrm{min},\mu}\sin\theta_{\alpha,\mu}\cos\varphi_{\alpha,\mu}+\hat{J}^{(a)}_{\mathrm{min},\mu}\sin\theta_{\alpha,\mu}\sin\varphi_{\alpha,\mu}

\displaystyle+\hat{J}_{z,\mu}\cos\theta_{\alpha,\mu},

(9)

where each measurement setup
(\alpha,\mu)
corresponds to selecting a subspace
\mu
and a direction
\alpha
in the subspace spanned by
(\hat{J}^{(s)}_{\mathrm{min},\mu},\hat{J}^{(a)}_{\mathrm{min},\mu},\hat{J}_{z,\mu})
.
The measurement outcomes are binary, given by
r_{\alpha,\mu}^{(j)}=\pm 1/2
. The number of directions
\alpha
is associated with the number of settings chosen by the observer within the given subsystem
\mu
, with
\alpha=1,2,\dots,k_{\mu}
. The total number of possible measurements is
k=\sum_{\mu=1}^{f}k_{\mu}
.
Therefore, we introduce the input vector
\vec{M}
and the input matrix
\tilde{C}
whose elements are

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\alpha,{\mu}}}{N}

\displaystyle=v_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\alpha,\mu},

(10)

with
v_{\mu}=\langle\hat{J}_{z,\mu}\rangle/N
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{C}^{\alpha,\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}}{N}=\delta_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\left[-\frac{1}{4}\cos\theta_{\alpha,\mu}\cos\theta_{\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left(v_{\mu}^{2}\xi^{2}_{\mu,s}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\sin\theta_{\alpha,\mu}\sin\theta_{\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}\cos\varphi_{\alpha,\mu}\cos\varphi_{\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\left.\left(v_{\mu}^{2}\xi^{2}_{\mu,a}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\sin\theta_{\alpha,\mu}\sin\theta_{\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}\sin\varphi_{\alpha,\mu}\sin\varphi_{\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}\right],

(11)

see Appendix
A
for further details.
Note that the matrix
\tilde{C}
given by Eq. (
11
) has a block-diagonal structure with
f
blocks indexed by
\mu
. Each block is a
k_{\mu}\times k_{\mu}
matrix with elements labeled by
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
.

The minimization of
L(A,\vec{h})
for the squeezed states, with
\xi^{2}_{\mu,\sigma}<1
, is performed to identify the optimal matrix
A
and vector
\vec{h}
, giving
A_{\alpha,\mu;\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}=4\delta_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}w_{\mu}
and
h_{\alpha,\mu}=4w_{\mu}(2\alpha-1-k_{\mu})
for all
\alpha,\mu,\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}
, where
w_{\mu}\geq 0
are weights between blocks.
We have
A_{\alpha,\mu;\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}=0
and
h_{\alpha,\mu}=0
whenever
\xi^{2}_{\mu,\sigma}\geq 1
.
The classical bound is given by
E_{\mathrm{max}}=N\sum_{\mu=1}^{f}w_{\mu}k_{\mu}^{2}
.
The resulting Bell inequality takes the form of a weighted sum:

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{max}}^{-1}\sum_{\mu=1}^{f}w_{\mu}k_{\mu}^{2}\mathcal{L}(k_{\mu},\vec{\theta}_{\mu},\vec{\varphi}_{\mu})\geq 0,

(12)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(k_{\mu},\vec{\theta}_{\mu},\vec{\varphi}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=1+k_{\mu}^{-2}v_{\mu}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k_{\mu}}h_{\alpha,\mu}\cos\theta_{\alpha,\mu}-k_{\mu}^{-2}g_{\vec{\theta}_{\mu}}^{(c)}

\displaystyle-k_{\mu}^{-2}(1-4v_{\mu}^{2}\xi_{\mu,s}^{2})g^{(sc)}_{\vec{\theta}_{\mu},\vec{\varphi}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle-k_{\mu}^{-2}(1-4v_{\mu}^{2}\xi_{\mu,a}^{2})g^{(ss)}_{\vec{\theta}_{\mu},\vec{\varphi}_{\mu}}.

(13)

The functions
g
are defined as:

\displaystyle g^{(sc)}_{\vec{\theta}_{\mu},\vec{\varphi}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k_{\mu}}\sin\theta_{\alpha,\mu}\cos\varphi_{\alpha,\mu}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle g^{(ss)}_{\vec{\theta}_{\mu},\vec{\varphi}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k_{\mu}}\sin\theta_{\alpha,\mu}\sin\varphi_{\alpha,\mu}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle g_{\vec{\theta}_{\mu}}^{(c)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k_{\mu}}\cos\theta_{\alpha,{\mu}}\right)^{2}.

Finally, we minimize

\mu

The minimization with respect to

The minimization over the angles

gives the following relations:

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha,\mu}

\displaystyle=\pi-\theta_{k_{\mu}+1-\alpha,\mu},

(14)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=1}^{k_{\mu}}\sin\theta_{\alpha^{\prime},\mu}.

(15)

The angle-optimized Bell correlator is given by

\frac{L^{(k)}_{\text{opt}}}{E_{\mathrm{max}}}=\frac{\sum_{\mu=1}^{f}w_{\mu}k_{\mu}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{opt}}(k_{\mu})}{\sum_{\mu=1}^{f}w_{\mu}k_{\mu}^{2}},

(16)

where the values of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{opt}}(k_{\mu})
are shown in Fig.
2

decreases with

-0.317N

Optimization over weighting parameters

selectively enhances contributions from subsystems

where the optimized
k_{\mu}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{opt}}(k_{\mu})
exhibits substantial negative values, while suppressing contributions from subsystems where

If
k_{\mu}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{opt}}(k_{\mu})
is negative and exhibits weak
\mu
-dependence, employing a uniform weighting strategy proves advantageous, as it increases the contributions from all subsystems, effectively utilizing a larger total number of settings
k
.

Note that the multisetting two-body Bell correlator presented in
[
26
]
represents a special case of the left-hand side of Eq. (

\mu

\xi_{\mu,s}^{2}=\xi_{\mu,a}^{2}

V
Conclusions

In conclusion, we report a multisetting protocol for detecting Bell-correlated states using spin-
f
BECs, and discuss its application to experimental data
[
38
]
and numerical results obtained with the TWM method using spin-nematic squeezing generated in the
f
pairs of SU(2) subsystems. The latter can be used in multiparameter estimation tasks
[

28
,
50

The Bell correlations detected can be even larger than those estimated by Eq. (

\sigma=a

The multisetting Bell protocol can be realized in other ultracold atomic systems, including Yb and Sr platforms, which are currently being investigated for quantum information applications

The interactions necessary for squeezing generation can be engineered using Floquet techniques

Appendix A

In this work, we restrict our analysis to a subclass of Bell inequalities, known as two-body permutationally invariant Bell inequalities, which are constrained by symmetries and involve at most two-body correlation functions.
We consider
N

Appendix E
Mixed states for spin-1 BECs

To explore the noise effect, we consider the mixed states of the form:

\hat{\rho}=p\,\hat{\rho}_{S}+(1-p)\,\hat{\rho}_{\perp},

(62)

where
\hat{\rho}_{S}
represents a squeezed Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) optimized for Bell correlations
L(\hat{\rho}_{S})=L^{(k)}_{{\rm opt}}

We consider the two different admixture states:

\hat{\rho}_{\perp}=|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|

We first study the spin-coherent admixture, for which the corresponding density matrix is given by:

(63)

In this case, the non-zero expectation values of the collective operators and the second moment are given by:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{z,1}^{(\sigma)}\rangle

\displaystyle=p\langle\hat{J}^{(\sigma)}_{z,1}\rangle_{S}+(1-p)N/2

\displaystyle=p\langle(\hat{J}^{(\sigma)}_{\rm{min},1})^{2}\rangle_{S}+(1-p)N/4,

and

\displaystyle=pv_{S}+(1-p)/2

\displaystyle 4\xi^{2}v^{2}

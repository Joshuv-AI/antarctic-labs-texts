Abstract
. In 1975, G. E. Andrews challenged the mathematics community to address L. Ehrenpreis’ problem, which was to directly prove the modularity of the Rogers-Ramanujan q q -series’ summatory forms. This question is important because many different q q -series appearing in combinatorics, representation theory, and physics often seem to be “mysteriously” modular, yet there is no general test to confirm this directly from the exotic q q -series expressions. In this note, we solve this general problem using q q -series algebra and q q -series systems of differential equations. Specifically, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition under which a vector of holomorphic q q -series on | q | < 1 |q|<1 forms a vector-valued modular function . This result offers a clear and conceptual path to modularity for ”strange” q q -series.

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m-n}{n}_{q}=\frac{(q;q)_{m-n}}{(q;q)_{n}\,(q;q)_{m-2n}}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(1-q^{\,m-2n+i}\bigr).

th summand of

, which implies that

Similarly, we have that
B_{m}(q)=H(q)+O(q^{m}).

Companion series
P_{m}
and
Q_{m}
.

We define the formal power series

and

by

P_{m}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(1-q^{5j-4})(1-q^{5j-1})}\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad Q_{m}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(1-q^{5j-3})(1-q^{5j-2})}.

Therefore, we have

\frac{P_{m}}{P_{m-1}}=\frac{1}{(1-q^{5m-1})(1-q^{5m-4})}\qquad{\text{\rm and}}\qquad\frac{Q_{m}}{Q_{m-1}}=\frac{1}{(1-q^{5m-2})(1-q^{5m-3})}.

In particular, we have that

(2.2)

P_{m}=P_{m-1}+O(q^{5m-4})\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad Q_{m}=Q_{m-1}+O(q^{5m-3}).

Comparing companion series.

We proceed by induction on
m
. The cases

are immediate:

A_{0}=P_{0}=1\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad A_{1}=P_{1}+O(q).

Assume

and

A_{m-2}=P_{m-2}+O(q^{m-2})
,
then (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) imply that

A_{m}=A_{m-1}+q^{m-1}A_{m-2}+O(q^{m})=P_{m-1}+q^{m-1}P_{m-2}+O(q^{m}).

Arguing similarly with
Q_{m}
and

, we find that

A_{m}=P_{m}+O(q^{m})\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad B_{m}=Q_{m}+O(q^{m}).

Combining these facts, we have

q\frac{d}{dq}\log A_{m}=q\frac{d}{dq}\log G+O(q^{m})\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad q\frac{d}{dq}\log B_{m}\equiv q\frac{d}{dq}\log H+O(q^{m}).

To complete the proof, we note that

q\frac{d}{dq}\log P_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\!\left(\frac{(5j-4)q^{5j-4}}{1-q^{5j-4}}+\frac{(5j-1)q^{5j-1}}{1-q^{5j-1}}\right),

q\frac{d}{dq}\log Q_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\!\left(\frac{(5j-3)q^{5j-3}}{1-q^{5j-3}}+\frac{(5j-2)q^{5j-2}}{1-q^{5j-2}}\right).

are finite (residue) Lambert sums, and the theorem follows by letting

\qquad\square

4.
Sketch of the Proof of Theorem
4

Here we establish the modularity of the normalized vectors
\mathbf{f}_{k}
that are defined by the Andrews-Gordon
q
-series using
q
-series algebra and standard facts about (residue) Lambert series. This proof is a generalization of the proof of Theorem
1
, which relied in Schur’s recurrence relations.

Schur’s recurrence relations are beautifully generalized with the so-called Andrews-Gordon polynomial method.
For
k\geq 2
and
1\leq i\leq k
, set
N_{j}:=n_{j}+\cdots+n_{k}
and consider the finite truncations

F^{(L)}_{k,i}(q)\ :=\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 0\\
N_{1}\leq L\end{subarray}}\frac{q^{\,N_{1}^{2}+\cdots+N_{k}^{2}+(N_{i}+\cdots+N_{k})}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{k}}}.

These
Andrews–Gordon polynomials
play the same role for general
k
that Schur’s
polynomials play for
k=2
. They satisfy a coupled
k
-step
q
-difference system
whose limit, as
L\rightarrow+\infty
, yields the identities (for example, see Theorem 1.1 of
[
30
]
).
Exactly as the Schur recurrence leads to Corollary
6
, for general
k
we obtain a
linear system for the
logarithmic derivatives
q\,\frac{d}{dq}\log F_{k,i}(q)
that is entirely described in terms of (residue) Lambert series.
To be precise, after taking
q\frac{d}{dq}\log
of the
k
-step Andrews-Gordon polynomial recurrences, passing to the limit
L\rightarrow+\infty
(cf. Theorem 1.1 of
[
30
]
), and then applying the
(2k+1)
-dissection, we obtain

(4.1)

q\frac{d}{dq}\log F_{k,i}(q)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{q^{(2k+1)m}}{1-q^{(2k+1)m}}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\!\left(\frac{q^{\,i+(2k+1)m}}{1-q^{\,i+(2k+1)m}}+\frac{q^{\,2k+1-i+(2k+1)m}}{1-q^{\,2k+1-i+(2k+1)m}}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}C_{ij}(q)\,q\frac{d}{dq}\log F_{k,j}(q),

for explicit
q
-series coefficients
C_{ij}(q)
coming from the
k
-step recurrences.

The key observation (also the key to the proof of the infinite product identities) is the appearance of the three (residue) Lambert series on the right of (
4.1
).
The first collects the residue class
0\pmod{2k+1}
, while the second and third
collect the residue classes
i
and
2k{+}1-i\pmod{2k+1}
.

The master matrix
A_{k}(q)
.

For a positive integer
M
and
r\in\{0,1,\dots,M-1\}
, we let

S_{r,M}(q)\ :=\ \begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 1}\dfrac{q^{Mm}}{1-q^{Mm}},&r=0,\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}\dfrac{q^{\,r+Mm}}{1-q^{\,r+Mm}},&1\leq r\leq M-1.\end{cases}

With
M=2k+1
, we then define

D_{k}:=\mathrm{diag}\big(\alpha_{k,1},\dots,\alpha_{k,k}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{k}(q):=\mathrm{diag}\big(S_{0,M}+S_{1,M}+S_{M-1,M},\dots,S_{0,M}+S_{k,M}+S_{M-k,M}\big),

and in turn let

A_{k}(q):=\;D_{k}\;+\;\big(I_{k}-C_{k}(q)\big)^{-1}\,\Lambda_{k}(q).

Here
C_{k}(q)=(C_{ij}(q))_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
is the recurrence matrix from
(
4.1
).
By construction, (
4.1
) is equivalent to the first-order
q
-differential system

\qquad\theta\,\mathbf{f}_{k}(\tau)\ =\ \mathbf{f}_{k}(\tau)\,A_{k}(q).

Eisenstein realization.

Each entry of
\Lambda_{k}(q)
(hence of
A_{k}(q)
) is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of the classical
weight
2
and level
2k+1
Eisenstein series. Therefore, the transformation of

A_{k}
under
S:\tau\mapsto-1/\tau
follows from the classical inversion law for Eisenstein series
(for example, see Theorem 4.6.2 of
[
14
]
).
The proof follows
mutatis mutandis
as the proof of Theorem
1

involving the comparison of
q
-expansions at the cusps.
\qquad\square

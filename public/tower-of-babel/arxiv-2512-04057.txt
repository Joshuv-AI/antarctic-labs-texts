Extremal couplings and gluon scattering in M-theory


Abstract
We consider M-theory on the backgrounds AdS 4 × S 7 / ℤ N f {}_{4}\times S^{7}/\mathbb{Z}_{N_{f}} and AdS 7 × S 4 / ℤ 2 {}_{7}\times S^{4}/\mathbb{Z}_{2} , which have fixed point locii AdS d + 1 × S 3 {}_{d+1}\times S^{3} for d = 3 , 6 d=3,6 . These theories are holographically dual to certain CFTs in d = 3 , 6 d=3,6 with eight supercharges. We compute the bulk cubic couplings between graviton KK modes and gluon KK modes living on the fixed points of these theories, which are generically extremal. We use these couplings to compute the graviton exchange term that appears in the strong coupling expansion of holographic correlators of gluon KK modes ⟨ 22 ​ p ​ p ⟩ \langle 22pp\rangle in these theories, and check that it matches the expected flat space limit. We express the answer in terms of a new reduced correlator solution to the superconformal Ward identities, which we derive for all CFTs with eight supercharges in 3 ≤ d ≤ 6 3\leq d\leq 6 .

Conclusion

In this paper, we computed the bulk couplings

Rather than doing this in full, note that the only graviton modes which can couple to gluon modes on the branes at the fixed point locus are precisely those which do not vanish at

B.1
Superconformal Ward identities in Mellin space

The superconformal Ward identities encode the action of the fermionic generators of the superconformal group. In our conventions, they take a universal form in position space
[

(z\partial_{z}-\epsilon\alpha\partial_{\alpha})G(z,\bar{z};\alpha)\Bigg|_{\alpha=\frac{1}{z}}=0\,,

(B.1)

along with a second equation given by inverting

G(z,\bar{z};\alpha)=f_{\text{disc}}(z,\bar{z};\alpha)+R\circ H(z,\bar{z};\alpha)\,,

(B.2)

where
f_{\text{disc}}
is the disconnected contribution,
R
is a local differential operator and
H
is known as a reduced amplitude. However, in odd dimensions, the operator

After Mellin transforming the amplitude, however, the Ward identity always admits a decomposition of this form, regardless of the dimension
[
24
]

d=3,5

In order to obtain the Ward identities in Mellin space, we follow
[
57
]
. First, we use the chain rule to write:

z\partial_{z}=U\partial_{U}+V\partial_{V}-\frac{1}{1-z}V\partial_{V}\,,

(B.3)

but do not evaluate the derivatives. We can then act with the
\alpha\partial_{\alpha}
derivative, and take
\alpha\to\frac{1}{z}

z^{a}(1-z)

\alpha

z\leftrightarrow\bar{z}

U,V,U\partial_{U},V\partial_{V}
acting on
G(z,\bar{z};\alpha)
. Then, we can use the following dictionary to rewrite these as operators in Mellin space, which follows from our definition of the Mellin transform (
3.16

\displaystyle\begin{split}&U\partial_{U}\to\frac{s}{2}\,,\quad V\partial_{V}\to\frac{t-\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{3}}{2}\,,\\
U^{m}V^{n}\to&\left(\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-s}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{4}-s}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{4}-t}{2}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-t}{2}\right)_{n}\\

(B.4)

where
\Delta_{i}
are the scaling dimensions of the four external operators, and we defined the shift operator

P_{m,n}f(s,t)=f(s+m,t+n)\,.

(B.5)

We now turn to the question of solving the Ward identities. In both
\langle 22pp\rangle

\langle 2p2p\rangle

\alpha

32

\alpha

In order to solve the superconformal Ward identities in Mellin space, we assume that there exists a similar object in Mellin space, an operator

s,t

When translated into Mellin space, the position operators

P_{m,n}X^{s}Y^{t}=(X^{m}Y^{n})X^{s}Y^{t}\,.

Therefore, we start by assuming that there exists a function which solves the Ward identities of the form:

B.2

We conclude this appendix by giving our conventions for the Mack polynomials, from which we build the bosonic exchange diagrams

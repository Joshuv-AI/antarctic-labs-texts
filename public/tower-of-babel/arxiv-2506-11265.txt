Linkage axioms for generic tropical oriented matroids
Yuan Yao

Abstract
. We present a comprehensive overview of Ardila and Develin’s (generic) tropical oriented matroids, as well as many related objects and their axiomatics. Moreover, we use a unifying framework that elucidates the connections between these objects, including several new cryptomorphisms and properties.



1.
Introduction

Tropical oriented matroids
are a combinatorial abstraction of
tropical hyperplane arrangements
, first defined by Ardila and Develin (
[
AD07
]
). Over the past few decades, these objects have been shown to be in bijection with several other geometric objects:

•

Subdivisions of product of two simplices (
[
AD07
,
OY10
,
Hor16
]
),

•

Mixed subdivisions of a dilated simplex (via the
Cayley trick
, see
[
HRS00
,
San03
]
), and

•

Tropical pseudo-hyperplane arrangements (
[
Hor16
]
).

In the
generic
case, where the corresponding tropical (pseudo-)hyperplanes intersect generically, these matroids biject to
triangulations
of products of two simplices and
fine
mixed subdivisions of dilated simplices, the former of which have been of interest in algebraic geometry and topology (see
[
GKZ94
]
, Section 7.3.D for further background).

Moreover, generic tropical oriented matroids are closely related to
matching fields
from the study of maximal minors of a matrix (
[
SZ93
,
BZ93
]
), which in turn motivated the construction of several combinatorial objects with different axiomatics, including
matching ensembles
(
[
OY13
]
),
tope arrangements
(
[
LS20
]
), and
trianguloids
(
[
GNP24
]
). Notably, these objects all involve some variation of the
linkage axiom
from matching fields, which has similarities to the exchange axiom for ordinary matroids.

The goal of this paper is two-fold. First, we collect and synthesize the existing literature on the combinatorial properties of the aforementioned objects, and present them in a more consistent fashion for ease of understanding and comparison (
Section

2
). Second, we extend our current understanding of the axiomatics of these objects by filling in several missing connections, most notably:

•

Extended tope arrangements and pre-trianguloids are cryptomorphic objects (
Section

3
),

•

A corrected proof showing that matching ensembles and generic tropical oriented matroids are cryptomorphic objects (
Section

4
), and

•

Some new properties of fine mixed subdivisions of dilated simplices (
Section

5
).

As a consequence, we obtain many more equivalent axiom systems that define a generic tropical oriented matroid, many of which can be phrased as some version of the linkage axiom (summarized in
Section

6
).

2.1.
Fine mixed subdivisions of
n\Delta^{d-1}

Mixed subdivisions are a generalization of zonotope tilings that apply to any Minkowski sums of polytopes.

Definition 2.1

(
[
San03
]
, Section 1.2)
.

Given a Minkowski sum
P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}
, a
Minkowski cell
is a full-dimensional polytope
C
of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
, where the vertices of
C_{i}
is a nonempty subset of that of
P_{i}
. It is
fine
if it cannot be subdivided into smaller Minkowski cells.

A polyhedral subdivision of
P
into (fine) Minkowski cells is called a
(fine) mixed subdivision
of
P
if for any two cells
C=\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
and
C^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}C^{\prime}_{i}
, we have
C\cap C^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(C_{i}\cap C^{\prime}_{i})
, and
C_{i}\cap C^{\prime}_{i}
is a common face of
C_{i}
and
C^{\prime}_{i}
. (In other words,
C
and
C^{\prime}

intersect properly as Minkowski cells
.)

In this paper we are solely interested in the case where
P_{i}=\Delta^{d-1}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, so
P
is a dilated simplex
n\Delta^{d-1}
. In this case, every Minkowski cell
C
can be encoded via a subgraph
G(C)
of the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,d}
(whose vertex set is
[n]\sqcup[\bar{d}])
, where
(i,\bar{j})
is an edge of
G(C)
if and only if
e_{\bar{j}}
is a vertex of
C_{i}
. We will refer to
[n]
as the
left side
and
[\bar{d}]
as the
right side
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is easy to label each face
F
of a Minkowski cell
C
with a unique Minkowski sum decomposition
\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}
, where each
F_{i}
is a (nonempty) face of
C_{i}
. In a mixed subdivision, the condition in the definition ensures that a face shared by two or more cells receives the same label no matter which cell we choose.

In the case where
P=n\Delta^{d-1}
, we can also represent each face
F
with a subgraph
G(F)
. It is not difficult to see that
F
is a face of
C
if and only if
G(F)\subseteq G(C)
(see also
[
OY10
]
, Remark 2.3).

The definition of fine mixed subdivisions of
n\Delta^{d-1}
can now be translated into purely combinatorial conditions on a collection of subgraphs:

Definition 2.3
.

Two acyclic subgraphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
of
K_{n,d}
are
compatible
with each other if whenever they both contain a perfect matching between two sets of vertices
I\subseteq[n]
and
\bar{J}\subseteq[\bar{d}]
, the two matchings are the same.

There is an equivalent formulation for compatibility which will be used for some proofs.

Definition 2.4
.

For two forests
G
and
G^{\prime}
of
K_{n,d}
, let the
compatibility graph

U(G,G^{\prime})
be the directed graph on
[n]\sqcup[\bar{d}]
consisting of all edges in
G
directed from
[n]
to
[\bar{d}]
and all edges in
G^{\prime}
directed from
[\bar{d}]
to
[n]
.
2
2

2

A similar graph called
comparability graph

CG_{G,G^{\prime}}
is defined in
[
AD07
]
, Definition 3.2, which is defined on the vertex set
[\bar{d}]
and for not necessarily acyclic graphs. This graph give rise to the same compatibility condition when specialized to forests.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
Pos05
]
, Lemma 12.6 and
[
GNP24
]
, Lemma 6.1)
.

Two forests are compatible if and only if their compatibility graph does not contain a simple directed cycle of length at least 4. We call such a cycle a
nontrivial cycle
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
AB06
]
, Proposition 7.2)
.

A collection
\mathcal{G}
of subgraphs of
K_{n,d}
encodes a fine mixed subdivision of
n\Delta^{d-1}
if and only if the following holds:

(1)

Each subgraph
G\in\mathcal{G}
is a spanning tree (this is the equivalent condition for
G
to encode a fine Minkowski cell).

(2)

(Tree linkage)
For each tree
G\in\mathcal{G}
and an internal edge
e\in G
(i.e. an edge that is not adjacent to a leaf vertex), there exists another tree
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
and an edge
e^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
such that
G\setminus e=G^{\prime}\setminus e^{\prime}
.

(3)

(Compatibility)
Any two trees of
\mathcal{G}
are compatible with each other.

We note that the
G^{\prime}
in the tree linkage axiom is necessarily unique:

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose there are two distinct spanning trees
G,G^{\prime}
of
K_{n,d}
and two edges
e\in G
and
e^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
such that
G\setminus e=G^{\prime}\setminus e^{\prime}=H
. Let the two connected components of
H
be
H^{(1)}=I^{(1)}\sqcup\bar{J}^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}=I^{(2)}\sqcup\bar{J}^{(2)}
. The two trees
G
and
G^{\prime}
are compatible if and only if either
e
connects
I^{(1)}
with
\bar{J}^{(2)}
and
e^{\prime}
connects
I^{(2)}
with
\bar{J}^{(1)}
, or vice versa. In particular, this also means that
e
and
e^{\prime}
do not share vertices.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction and without loss of generality that both
e
and
e^{\prime}
connects
I^{(1)}
and
\bar{J}^{(2)}
. Suppose that
e=(i,\bar{j})
and
e^{\prime}=(i^{\prime},\bar{j}^{\prime})
where
i,i^{\prime}\in I^{(1)}
,
\bar{j},\bar{j}^{\prime}\in\bar{J}^{(2)}
, and at least one of
i\neq i^{\prime}
and
\bar{j}\neq\bar{j}^{\prime}
is true. The compatibility graph
U(G,G^{\prime})
contains bi-directional edges for all edges in
H^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}
, and hence contains a nontrivial cycle of the form
i\xrightarrow{e}\bar{j}\xrightarrow{H^{(2)}}\bar{j}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{e^{\prime}}i^{\prime}\xrightarrow{H^{(1)}}i
, contradicting compatibility.

On the other hand, observe that if (without loss of generality)
e
connects
I^{(1)}
with
\bar{J}^{(2)}
and
e^{\prime}
connects
I^{(2)}
with
\bar{J}^{(1)}
, then both edges between
H^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}
in
U(G,G^{\prime})
are directed from
H^{(1)}
to
H^{(2)}
, and hence it cannot contain a nontrivial cycle.
∎

As a result, there cannot be three or more distinct spanning trees in
\mathcal{G}
that all contain
H
as a subgraph, implying that
G^{\prime}
is unique.

Remark 2.8
.

Geometrically speaking, the tree linkage axiom says that for any facet of a cell that does not lie on the boundary of
n\Delta^{d-1}
, there is (uniquely) another cell that shares the facet. The compatibility axiom says that any two cells intersect properly as Minkowski cells.

A fine mixed subdivision of
4\Delta^{2}

4\Delta^{2}

[\bar{d}]
and the unit simplices in the subdivision are labeled with the corresponding unique index of
[n]
for which the label is

The fine mixed subdivisions of
n\Delta^{d-1}
are also in bijection with triangulation of products of two simplices, via what is known as

:

Theorem 2.9

(
[
HRS00
]
, Theorem 3.1)
.

The mixed subdivisions of
n\Delta^{d-1}
are in bijection with polyhedral subdivisions of
\Delta^{n-1}\times\Delta^{d-1}
where the vertices of every cell is a subset of the vertices of
\Delta^{n-1}\times\Delta^{d-1}
, and the fine mixed subdivisions biject to triangulations (i.e. subdivisions into simplices).

Remark 2.10
.

Due to the apparent symmetry between
[n]
and
[\bar{d}]
in
\Delta^{n-1}\times\Delta^{d-1}
, this bijection also gives a bijection between (fine) mixed subdivisions of
n\Delta^{d-1}
and
d\Delta^{n-1}
. While it is useful to keep this symmetry in mind, we will intentionally avoid using
\Delta^{n-1}\times\Delta^{d-1}
or
d\Delta^{n-1}
for the rest of this paper to prevent unnecessary confusion between the role of the two sets of indices.

We conclude this sub-section with one important property of fine mixed subdivisions of
n\Delta^{d-1}
.

Definition 2.11

(
[
GNP24
]
, Definition 4.1)
.

For a subgraph
G
of
K_{n,d}
, let the
left degree vector

LD(G)
be the
n
-tuple
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
of nonnegative integers, where
v_{i}
is the degree of vertex
i
in
G
. Define the
right degree vector

RD(G)
analogously (as a
d
-tuple of nonnegative integers indexed by
[\bar{d}]
).

In addition, the graph
G
is called a
left semi-matching
if
LD(G)=\mathbf{1}_{[n]}
, a
right semi-matching
if
RD(G)=\mathbf{1}_{[\bar{d}]}
, and a
partial matching
if all vertices have degree
0
or
1
. We also define
partial left (resp. right) semi-matchings
as graphs whose vertices in
[n]
(resp.
[\bar{d}]
) all have degree
0
or
1
.

Lemma 2.12
.

(a)

If two distinct acyclic subgraphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
of
K_{n,d}
have both the same left and right degree vectors, then they are not compatible with each other.

(b)

(
[
Pos05
]
, Lemma 12.8)
If two distinct spanning trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
of
K_{n,d}
have
either
the same left degree vector
or
the same right degree vector, then they are not compatible with each other.

Proof of (a).

Consider the graph
U(G,G^{\prime})
and remove all bi-directional edges from it. Since
G
and
G^{\prime}
have the same degree vectors, each vertex of the resulting graph have the same in-degree and out-degree and the graph is non-empty, and hence contains a nontrivial cycle. This means that
G
and
G^{\prime}
are not compatible.
∎

Definition 2.13
.

For a fine Minkowski cell
C
of
n\Delta^{d-1}
, let
LD^{-}(C)=LD(G(C))-\mathbf{1}_{[n]}
and
RD^{-}(C)=RD(G(C))-\mathbf{1}_{[\bar{d}]}
. These are referred to as the
reduced left/right degree vectors
.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
Pos05
]
, Lemma 14.9)
.

Each fine Minkowski cell
C
of
n\Delta^{d-1}
contains exactly one lattice simplex that is a translated copy of
\Delta^{d-1}
, whose vertices are
\{RD^{-}(C)+e_{\bar{j}}\mid\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]\}
. The interior of
C
is disjoint from all other such lattice simplices.

We will refer to
RD^{-}(C)
as the
position
of the cell
C
, and the simplex
\text{conv}(\{RD^{-}(C)+e_{\bar{j}}\mid\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]\})
as the
base simplex
of
C
. It is not difficult to see that all vertices of the base simplex are also vertices of
C
.

Theorem 2.15

(
[
Pos05
]
, Theorem 12.9)
.

Over all cells
C
of a fine mixed subdivision of
n\Delta^{d-1}
, the set of values of
LD^{-}(C)
is exactly the set of lattice points in
(d-1)\Delta^{n-1}
, and the set of values of
RD^{-}(C)
is exactly the set of lattice points in
(n-1)\Delta^{d-1}
. Moreover, each
LD^{-}(C)
and
RD^{-}(C)
is represented by exactly one cell.

Remark 2.16
.

One immediate consequence of this theorem is that any fine mixed subdivision of
n\Delta^{d-1}
contains exactly
\binom{n+d-2}{n-1}
cells. If we know that there are exactly this many spanning trees in a collection
\mathcal{G}
, then
either one
of tree linkage axiom or compatibility axiom of
Theorem

2.6
is sufficient in showing that
\mathcal{G}
encodes a fine mixed subdivision. A proof of the former can be found in the proof of
[
GNP24
]
, Theorem 3.6, and the latter is
[
OY13
]
, Proposition 2.7.

2.2.
Generic tropical oriented matroids

The definition of a
tropical oriented matroids
closely resembles the vector axioms of an oriented matroid.

Definition 2.17

(
[
AD07
]
, Definition 3.1 and 5.2)
.

A
(n,d)
-type

T
is an
n
-tuple
(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})
of nonempty subsets of
[\bar{d}]
, and a
(n,d)
-semitype
is such a tuple of (possibly empty) subsets of
[\bar{d}]
.

Remark 2.18
.

A
tropical hyperplane
can be viewed as a translated copy of the normal fan of
\Delta^{d-1}
. Each tropical hyperplane hence creates
d
closed full-dimensional “sectors”, and divides the space into
2^{d}-1
open “faces”, which are in easy correspondence with non-empty subsets of
[\bar{d}]
: Each face corresponds to the set of sectors that it is in. For an arrangement of
n
tropical hyperplanes, each type records one possible combination of
n
faces (one from each tropical hyperplane) that a point in the space can simultaneously reside in.

By drawing the Poincaré dual of each fine Minkowski cell in a fine mixed subdivision of
n\Delta^{d-1}
, one can obtain an arrangement of
n

tropical pseudo-hyperplanes
, each of which is a tropical hyperplane under a mild piecewise-linear homeomorphism. See
[
Hor16
]
, Definition 6.4 for the precise definition of a tropical pseudo-hyperplane arrangement.

Figure 2.
A tropical pseudo-hyperplane arrangement obtained from the fine mixed subdivision in
Figure

.

Similar to fine Minkowski cells, each
(n,d)
-type or semitype
T
can be encoded via a subgraph
G(T)
of
K_{n,d}
, where
(i,\bar{j})
is an edge of
G(T)
if and only if
\bar{j}\in T_{i}
. The graph
G
corresponds to a type if and only if
LD(G)_{i}\geq 1
for every
i\in[n]
.

Here we only provide a definition for
generic
oriented matroids, which encodes generic tropical pseudo-hyperplane arrangements. All of the following definitions and results have been rephrased for this special case.

Definition 2.19

(
[
OY10
]
, Definition 3.8)
.

A collection
\mathcal{T}
of
(n,d)
-types forms a
generic
(n,d)
-tropical oriented matroid
if all types correspond to acyclic subgraphs of
K_{n,d}
, and

(1)

(Boundary) For every
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
, the type
(\{\bar{j}\},\dots,\{\bar{j}\})\in\mathcal{T}
.

(2)

(Surrounding) If
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
T^{\prime}
is a type such that
G(T^{\prime})\subseteq G(T)
, then
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
. (
T^{\prime}
is called a
refinement
of
T
.)

(3)

(Compatibility/Comparability) For any two types
U,V\in\mathcal{T}
,
G(U)
and
G(V)
are compatible with each other.

(4)

(Elimination) For any two types
U,V\in\mathcal{T}
and

, there exists a type
W\in\mathcal{T}
such that
W_{i}=U_{i}\cup V_{i}
and
W_{i^{\prime}}\in\{U_{i^{\prime}},V_{i^{\prime}}\}
for all
i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus\{i\}
.
3
3

3

In the non-generic case the condition says
W_{i^{\prime}}\in\{U_{i^{\prime}},V_{i^{\prime}},U_{i^{\prime}}\cup V_{i^{\prime}}\}
, but from the surrounding axiom we see that the third possibility can be reduced to either of the first two in the generic case.
We say that
W
is an
elimination between
U
and
V
at the
i
-th coordinate
.

(

A type

4

).

For convenience, we will sometimes shorten each coordinate of a type to a string of elements of

(

A generic tropical oriented matroid can be completely determined by either the set of all tree-types or the set of all topes. In particular, a type

(a)

By identifying the tree-types with the fine Minkowski cells via the graph encoding, there is a canonical bijection between generic

.

Remark 2.23
.

Under this bijection, the topes of a generic tropical oriented matroid correspond to lattice points in

For easier comparison with other objects defined later, it is also useful to define a slightly modified version of generic tropical oriented matroid.

Definition 2.24

(
[
AD07
]
, Definition 5.2)
.

An
extended generic
(n,d)
-tropical oriented matroid
is a collection
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
of
(n,d)
-
semi
types satisfying the same axioms as a generic tropical oriented matroid (replacing all instances of “type” with “semitype”). In such a matroid, the semitypes that have no empty sets in any coordinate will be called
honest types
.

Given a generic tropical oriented matroid
\mathcal{T}
, its
completion
is an extended generic tropical oriented matroid consists of all semitypes obtained by replacing some coordinates of a type with empty sets. The reverse process (of removing all non-type semitypes) is called
reduction
.

Remark 2.25
.

It is clear that the reduction of an extended generic tropical oriented matroid is a generic tropical oriented matroid. The completion trivially preserves boundary, surrounding, and comparability axioms. To show that the completion also satisfies elimination between two semi-types
U
and
V
, we can pass to two honest types
U^{+}
and
V^{+}
in the original non-extended matroid, eliminate between them to get a type
W^{+}
, and then refining it to
W
.

The fact that completion and reduction give a full bijection between extended and non-extended generic tropical oriented matroids follows from the following lemma:

Lemma 2.26
.

In an extended generic tropical oriented matroid
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
, any semitype
T\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
is a refinement of an honest type
T^{+}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that the first
m
coordinates of
T
are non-empty and the last
n-m
coordinares are empty (in other words, we assume that the
support
of
T
is
[m]
). If
m=0
or
m=n
there is nothing to prove, so we assume
1\leq m\leq n-1
. Using the same proof as
[
AD07
]
, Theorem 4.6, we can use a series of elimination between semitypes supported on
[m]
to obtain a semitype
T^{\prime}
for which
T
is a refinement, such that
G(T^{\prime})
is a spanning tree on
[m]\sqcup[\bar{d}]
.

Since
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
contains a (non-extended) generic tropical oriented matroid and hence encodes a fine mixed subdivision, by
Theorem

2.15
there is a unique tree-type
T_{0}
such that
LD(G(T_{0}))=LD(G(T^{\prime}))+\mathbf{1}_{[n]\setminus[m]}
. Since
T^{\prime}
is compatible with
T_{0}
, by
Lemma

2.12
(b) we must have
G(T^{\prime})\subseteq G(T_{0})
, and hence
T^{+}=T_{0}
is a desired honest type.
∎

2.3.
Linkage matching fields and tope arrangements

Matching fields
are first studied by Strumfels and Zelevinsky in
[
SZ93
]
as a way to understand the Newton polytope of the product of all maximal minors of a rectangular matrix.

Definition 2.27

(
[
BZ93
]
)
.

For positive integers
n^{\prime}\geq d
, a
(n^{\prime},d)
-matching field

2.4.
Pointed matching fields and matching ensembles

While there are many similarities between topes from a generic tropical oriented matroid (c.f.

Proof.

Given a linkage pointed matching field, consider a linkage covector

I\subseteq[n],\underline{J}\subseteq[\underline{d}]
, and
|I\sqcup\underline{J}|=d+1

If we remove the edge connected to

in
\bar{J}
are now leaves (with the single edge coming only from the corresponding type-(2) matching). Hence, by removing all edges connected to
\bar{J}

The reverse implication (completion of a matching left-semi-ensemble is a linkage pointed matching field) can be similarly shown by running the argument above in reverse.

3.
Matroid-like Operations

In this section we discuss some basic operations that apply to many of the objects defined in the previous section, and use them to derive some useful properties.

Since fine Minkowski cells, (semi-)types, left/right semi-matchings, linkage covectors, partial matchings, and (partial) topes can all be expressed as acyclic subgraphs of

G

3.1.

There is a simple combinatorial operation that connects left/right linkage covectors and (partial) left/right semi-matchings.

Definition 3.1

\bar{j}

\mathbb{T}^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
for each

Remark 3.2

The name

It is easy to verify the following properties:

Lemma 3.3

For a spanning tree
T
on
I\sqcup\bar{J}
and for any
i\in I
and

(a)

\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}^{i\rightarrow}\sqcup\mathbb{T}^{i\leftarrow}=\mathbb{T}^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}\sqcup\mathbb{T}^{\leftarrow\bar{j}}
(in terms of the set of edges).

(b)

LD(\mathbb{T}^{i\rightarrow})=\mathbf{1}_{I\setminus\{i\}},LD(\mathbb{T}^{\rightarrow\bar{j}})=\mathbf{1}_{I},RD(\mathbb{T}^{i\leftarrow})=\mathbf{1}_{\bar{J}},RD(\mathbb{T}^{\leftarrow\bar{j}})=\mathbf{1}_{\bar{J}\setminus\{\bar{j}\}}

(c)

If
i

\bar{j}

\mathbb{T}^{i\rightarrow}\sqcup e=\mathbb{T}^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
and
\mathbb{T}^{\leftarrow\bar{j}}\sqcup e=\mathbb{T}^{i\leftarrow}
.

(d)

For

\mathbb{T}^{i\rightarrow}
and

differ by exactly one edge if and only if there is some

that is adjacent to both

i^{\prime}
. The same applies to
\mathbb{T}^{i\leftarrow}
and
\mathbb{T}^{i^{\prime}\leftarrow}
, and symmetrically for
\bar{j},\bar{j}^{\prime}\in\bar{J}
.

This operation has appeared in many of the previous works, though without an explicit name. We collect some of the results here, rephrased using this definition:

Proposition 3.4

(
[

]

[

]

.

Given a linkage covector
\mathbb{T}_{\tau}(v)
of a linkage tope field
\mathcal{M}
and

\tau\setminus\{i\}
via
M_{\tau\setminus\{i\}}=(\mathbb{T}_{\tau}(v))^{i\rightarrow}
.

Proposition 3.5

(
[
LS20
]
, Definition 3.12 and Proposition 3.15)
.

Given a linkage tope field of
\mathcal{M}
of type

and some

\bar{j}

\mathcal{M}^{+\bar{j}}
of type

\bar{j}

of
\mathcal{M}
.

Corollary 3.6

(Iterated amalgamation,
[
LS20
]
, Theorem 3.16)
.

For each lattice point

\bar{j}

v_{\bar{j}}-1

\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
(in any order) starting from a linkage matching field

\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Note that from
Lemma

3.3
(c), it is easy to see that for a partial tope
M_{\tau}
in
\mathcal{M}^{+\bar{j}}

(i,\bar{j})
from it gives a partial tope

that is in
\mathcal{M}

and

that appear in the proof of

.

Therefore, the set of all partial topes that arise from iteratively amalgamating a linkage matching field are closed under taking subgraphs, similar to matching ensembles and generic tropical oriented matroids. The compatibility between these topes follows from the fact that the right semi-matchings of a linkage matching field are pairwise compatible (see
[
LS20
]
, Proposition 3.5). The same applies to matching left-semi-ensembles.

We also note that a similar (and ultimately equivalent) construction of an extended tope arrangement from a matching ensemble when

is presented in the proof of Proposition 5.3 of
[
OY13
]
.

Proposition 3.8

(
[
GNP24
]
, Proposition 3.4)
.

In a pre-trianguloid, the union of all topes
T_{u+e_{\bar{j}}}
whose positions are the vertices of a unit simplex

for some

is a tree

Remark 3.9
.

If we replace the pre-trianguloid in this proposition with an extended tope arrangement, there is a simple reason for why this proposition is true:
\mathbb{T}(u)
is simply the linkage covector
\mathbb{T}_{[n]}(u)
of the linkage tope field
\mathcal{M}
of type

. We will show in the next sub-section that pre-trianguloids and extended tope arrangements are in fact cryptomorphic.

Remark 3.10
.

Note that this “
\Delta
-linkage” property also implies the combinatorial sector axiom: The set of neighbors of
\bar{j}
in
T_{u+e_{\bar{j}}}=(\mathbb{T}(u))^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
is clearly a superset of that in
T_{u+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}}=(\mathbb{T}(u))^{\rightarrow\bar{j}^{\prime}}
for any
\bar{j}^{\prime}\neq\bar{j}
. Therefore, these two properties are equivalent.

When the pre-trianguloid is a trianguloid (i.e. corresponding to a fine mixed subdivision), it is not difficult to see that these trees
\mathbb{T}(u)
are exactly the trees encoding the fine Minkowski cells.

We mention one more consequence of the combinatorial sector axiom:

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
m
be a positive integer, and
P=s+(m-1)\Delta^{d-1}
where
s
is a lattice point in
(n-m)\Delta^{d-1}
. In a pre-trianguloid,
\bigcup_{u\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbb{T}(u)
is a (connected) graph whose right degree vector is
s+m\cdot\mathbf{1}_{[\bar{d}]}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\bigcup_{u\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbb{T}(u)=\bigcup_{\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]}T_{s+me_{\bar{j}}}
. From
Proposition

3.8
, each tree is a union of topes of the form
T_{u+e_{\bar{j}}}
where
u+e_{\bar{j}}\in s+m\Delta^{d-1}
. For each
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
, the set of neighbors of
\bar{j}
in each of these topes is a subset of that in
T_{s+me_{\bar{j}}}
due to the combinatorial sector axiom. Therefore, the union of the
d
topes of the form
T_{s+me_{\bar{j}}}
exactly determines all possible neighbors of each vertex of
[\bar{d}]
and hence the union of all topes/trees in question. The proposition now follows from the fact that
|T^{-1}_{s+me_{\bar{j}}}(\bar{j})|=s_{\bar{j}}+m
for each
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
.
∎

This property also implies the following well-known
Spread-out simplices condition
:

Corollary 3.12

(
[
AB06
]
, Proposition 8.2(b))
.

Let
m
be a positive integer, and
\tilde{P}=s+m\Delta^{d-1}
where
s
is a lattice point in
(n-m)\Delta^{d-1}
. In a fine mixed subdivision of
n\Delta^{d-1}
, there are at most
m
cells inside
\tilde{P}
that are translations of the unit simplex
\Delta^{d-1}
. (These cells are also called
unmixed cells
.)

Proof.

When
d=1
this statement is trivial, so assume
d\geq 2
. It is clear that the unmixed cells’ left degree vectors are (distinct) permutations of
d,1,\dots,1
. If there are at least
m+1
unmixed cells in
\tilde{P}
, then the union of these trees has total left degree at least
(m+1)d+(n-m-1)
(since there are at least
m+1
vertices in
[n]
with degree
d
and the others have degree at least 1). However,
Proposition

3.11
implies that the total right degree of the union of these trees is at most
(n-m)+md
, which contradicts the fact that the total left degree and total right degree of a bipartite graph are equal.
∎

3.2.
Deletion and contraction

Similar to matroids, we can construct sub-objects from fine mixed subdivisions, generic tropical oriented matroids, matching ensembles, extended tope arrangements, and (pre-)trianguloids. Here we provide one unifying definition that apply to all of the aforementioned objects.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a collection of (distinct) subgraphs of
K_{n,d}
, optionally with a requirement that all vertices in
[n]
and/or
[\bar{d}]
have degree at least 1. For
i\in[n]
and
\bar{j}\in\bar{d}
, the
i
-deletion
(resp.
\bar{j}
-contraction
) of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted
\mathcal{G}_{\setminus i}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}_{/\bar{j}}
), is obtained via removing vertex
i
(resp.
\bar{j}
) from each subgraph and then removing all duplicate graphs and graphs that no longer satisfy the minimum degree requirements for
\mathcal{G}
.

For subsets
I\subseteq[n]
and
\bar{J}\subseteq[\bar{d}]
, the
(I,\bar{J})
-minor
of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted
\mathcal{G}|_{I,\bar{J}}
, is obtained by deleting and contracting all elements in
[n]
and
[\bar{d}]
that are not in
I
or
\bar{J}
, respectively. (It is clear that the deletions and contractions can be done in any order.)

Remark 3.14
.

In the language of fine mixed subdivisions of
n\Delta^{d-1}
, the
i
-deletion is the same as shrinking the
i
-th unit simplex to a single point and the
\bar{j}
-contraction is the same as taking the (induced) fine mixed subdivision of the facet opposite the vertex
n\cdot e_{\bar{j}}
of
n\Delta^{d-1}
.

Proposition 3.15
.

All of the following objects are closed under taking minors:

(a)

(
[
AD07
]
, Proposition 4.7 and 4.8)
(Extended) (generic) tropical oriented matroids, and hence also fine mixed subdivisions and trianguloids;

(b)

Matching ensembles;

(c)

Extended tope arrangements;

(d)

Pre-trianguloids.

Proof.

(b)

The closure axiom ensures that deleting edges from some graphs do not create two distinct partial matchings on the same set of vertices, so we still have exactly one partial matching for each pair of subsets. Since all of these partial matchings are already present in the original matching ensemble, the left/right linkage axioms are automatically preserved.

(c)

It is clear that if two graphs are compatible, then their subgraphs are also compatible. If after taking a minor there are two topes with the same position, then by
Lemma

2.12
(a) they are not compatible, so no two topes have the same position.

Therefore, it suffices to show that after taking a minor there is at least one tope with a given position
v
.

•

If we contract some index
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
, the tope
T_{v^{\prime}}
where
v^{\prime}_{\bar{j}}=0
and
v^{\prime}_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}=v_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}
for all
\bar{j}^{\prime}\neq\bar{j}
and will have position
v
after contraction.

•

If we delete some index
i\in[n]
, then take an arbitrary
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
. If
T_{v+e_{\bar{j}}}(i)=\bar{j}
then this tope will have position
v
after deletion. Otherwise suppose that this coordinate is
\bar{j}^{\prime}
. Since extended tope arrangements are pre-trianguloids, they satisfy the combinatorial sector condition of
Definition

2.38
, and hence
T_{v+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}}(i)=\bar{j}^{\prime}
and this tope will have position
v
after deletion.

(d)

We first show that any two “adjacent” topes of a pre-trianguloid whose positions
v
and
v^{\prime}
differ by some
e_{\bar{j}}-e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}
are compatible. By
Proposition

3.8
, since both positions are vertices of the same simplex
(v-e_{\bar{j}})+\Delta^{d-1}
, these two topes are both subgraphs of a spanning tree. Since a tree cannot contain two distinct matchings on the same set of vertices (or else the union contains a cycle), any two subgraphs of it must be pairwise compatible.

Contraction of a pre-trianguloid is simply taking the subset of topes whose right degrees are zero at the contracted index, so the same conditions are trivially satisfied.

If two topes have the same position vector after deleting an index, then they must be adjacent before deletion and hence must be compatible with each other (and hence the same after deletion). The existence of a tope with any given position follows from the same argument as in the proof of (c).

It remains to show that the combinatorial sector condition is preserved under deletion. Suppose we delete index
i\in[n]
. If
u,u^{\prime}\in(n-1)\Delta^{d-1}
are such that
u-e_{\bar{j}}=u^{\prime}-e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}
for some
\bar{j},\bar{j}^{\prime}\in[\bar{d}]
, then let
v=u+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}=u^{\prime}+e_{\bar{j}}
. If
T_{v}(i)=\bar{j}^{\prime\prime}
(which may be the same as
\bar{j}
or
\bar{j}^{\prime}
), then the original condition implies that
T_{u+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime\prime}}}(i)=T_{u^{\prime}+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime\prime}}}(i)=\bar{j}^{\prime\prime}
, and hence
T_{u+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime\prime}}}
and
T_{u^{\prime}+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime\prime}}}
become
T_{u}
and
T_{u^{\prime}}
respectively after deletion. Since the condition is satisfied with the first pair of topes, it is also satisfied for the second pair, as desired.

∎

Corollary 3.16

(Resolving
[
LS20
]
, Question 3.35)
.

The topes of a pre-trianguloid are pairwise compatible, so pre-trianguloids and extended tope arrangements are cryptomorphic objects.

Proof.

If any two topes of a pre-trianguloid are incompatible, then they disagree on a matching between
I\subseteq[n]
and
\bar{J}\subseteq[\bar{d}]
. If we take the
(I,[\bar{d}])
-minor of the pre-trianguloid, the two topes will both have position
\mathbf{1}_{\bar{J}}
in the new pre-trianguloid and remain distinct, which is impossible.

Therefore, pre-trianguloids are extended tope arrangements, and combined with
Theorem

2.39
we see that the two are cryptomorphic.
∎

We also note that minors are also used to obtain matchings in a matching ensemble from a generic tropical oriented matroid:

Proposition 3.17

(
[
OY13
]
, Proposition 4.3 and Lemma 4.5)
.

Given a generic tropical oriented matroid
\mathcal{T}
, for any
I\subseteq[n]
and
\bar{J}\subseteq[\bar{d}]
such that
|I|=|\bar{J}|
, let
M_{I,\bar{J}}
be the unique tope of
\mathcal{T}|_{I,\bar{J}}
with position
\mathbf{1}_{\bar{J}}
. The matchings
\{M_{I,\bar{J}}\}
form a matching ensemble
\mathcal{M}
. We say that
\mathcal{M}
is
extracted
from
\mathcal{T}
.

Moreover, matching ensembles extracted from distinct generic tropical oriented matroids are also distinct.

Note that the extracted matching ensemble is naturally contained in the extended generic tropical oriented matroid.

4.
From Right Linkage to Tree Compatibility

The primary goal of this section is to provide a proof to
2.36
. Since pre-trianguloids and extended tope arrangements are also cryptomorphic to matching left-semi-ensembles, it suffices to show that the additional right-linkage axiom from matching ensembles translates to the condition that any two trees
\mathbb{T}(v)
in
Proposition

3.8
are compatible.

First, we mention one additional property of (extended) tope arrangements:

Proposition 4.1

(
[
LS20
]
, Proposition 3.22 and Corollary 3.24)
.

Given an extended tope arrangement
\mathcal{T}
and a lattice point
w\in(n+1)\Delta^{d-1}
, let
\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)
be the set of indices
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
for which
w_{\bar{j}}\geq 1
. The intersection of topes
\bigcap_{\bar{j}\in\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)}T_{w-e_{\bar{j}}}
is a partial left semi-matching
\Omega(w)
with right degree vector
w-\mathbf{1}_{\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)}
. In particular, the neighbors of
\bar{j}
in
\Omega(w)
are exactly the neighbors in
T_{w-e_{\bar{j}}}
.
Moreover, we have
T_{v}=\bigcup_{\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]}\Omega(v+e_{\bar{j}})
.

We call
\Omega(w)
a
Chow covector
of
\mathcal{T}
. These covectors originate from the Chow polytope of the variety of degenerate matrices (
[
SZ93
]
, Section 5), and are closely related to the minimal transversals of the underlying (pointed) matching field (
[
BZ93
]
, Theorem 1).

It turns out that the
union
of these topes is also interesting when right linkage is satisfied.

Proposition 4.2
.

Given a matching ensemble
\mathcal{M}
, let
\mathcal{T}
be the extended tope arrangement obtained by iterative amalgamation from
\mathcal{M}
. For a lattice point
w\in(n+1)\Delta^{d-1}
, the union of topes
\bigcup_{\bar{j}\in\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)}T_{w-e_{\bar{j}}}
is a spanning tree
\bar{\mathbb{T}}(w)
on
[n]\sqcup\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)
whose left degrees are all either 1 or 2. In particular, we have
T_{w-e_{\bar{j}}}=\Omega(w)\sqcup(\bar{\mathbb{T}}(w))^{\leftarrow\bar{j}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this proposition for
\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)=[\bar{d}]
: if
\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)\neq[\bar{d}]
, then we can first take the
([n],\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w))
-minor of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{T}
. (Vertices not in
[n]\sqcup\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)
will not be connected to any edges among all relevant topes. It is also clear that taking minor commutes with amalgamation.)

In this case, we note that the Chow covector
\Omega(w)
has exactly
n+1-d
edges. Let
I
be the set of vertices in
[n]
with degree 1 in
\Omega(w)
(so
|I|=n+1-d
). Since
\Omega(w)
is a common subgraph of all
T_{w-e_{\bar{j}}}
, if we delete all indices in
I
from
\mathcal{T}
, these topes will now have distinct right degree vectors
\mathbf{1}_{[\bar{d}]\setminus\{\bar{j}\}}
and the same left degree vector
\mathbf{1}_{[n]\setminus I}
. In particular, these are now all partial matchings in the original matching ensemble. Using the right linkage axiom on these partial matchings, we see that the union is a spanning tree
\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{[n]\setminus I,[\bar{d}]}
with left degree vector
\mathbf{2}_{[n]\setminus I}
. Therefore, the union of the original topes is
\Omega(w)\sqcup\bar{\mathbb{T}}_{[n]\setminus I,[\bar{d}]}
, which is a spanning tree on
[n]\sqcup[\bar{d}]
with left degree vector
\mathbf{2}_{[n]\setminus I}+\mathbf{1}_{I}
.

Recovering
T_{w-e_{\bar{j}}}
from
\bar{T}(w)
can be shown in the same way as
Proposition

3.4
, taking into account that the edges in
\Omega(w)
are never in a
\bar{j}
-push but appear in all topes in question.
∎

In other words, while the left linkage axiom translates to the fact that the union of topes around a right-side-up unit simplex
u+\Delta^{d-1}
is a tree, the additional right linkage axiom translates to the fact that the union around an
upside-down
unit simplex
w+\nabla^{d-1}
is also a tree, where
\nabla^{d-1}:=\textnormal{conv}(\{-e_{\bar{j}}\mid\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]\})
. (This upside-down simplex might not be fully contained in
n\Delta^{d-1}
in case
w
does not have full support, in which case we only take the vertices that are in
n\Delta^{d-1}
.) We call these two types of trees
\Delta
-linkage covectors
and
\nabla
-linkage covectors
, respectively.

Remark 4.3
.

We note that
\nabla
-linkage by itself also implies the combinatorial sector axiom. Indeed, suppose that
v-e_{\bar{j}}=v^{\prime}-e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}
, then let
w=v+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}=v^{\prime}+e_{\bar{j}}
. Since there are no neighbors of
\bar{j}
in
(\bar{\mathbb{T}}(w))^{\leftarrow\bar{j}}
and one neighbor in
(\bar{\mathbb{T}}(w))^{\leftarrow\bar{j}^{\prime}}
for any
\bar{j}^{\prime}\neq\bar{j}
, we see that
T^{-1}_{v}(\bar{j})
is a superset of
T^{-1}_{v^{\prime}}(\bar{j})
(by one element). Therefore,
\nabla
-linkage is a strictly stronger condition than
\Delta
-linkage, and encompasses both left and right linkage from matching ensembles.

In the language of fine mixed subdivisions, a
\Delta
-linkage covector encodes the unique Minkowski cell containing the corresponding right-side-up simplex, and a
\nabla
-linkage covector encodes the unique
parallelotope
containing the corresponding upside-down simplex. See
Figure

9
for an example. (Note that since a parallelotope is a zonotope, it corresponds to a graph whose left degrees are all either 1 or 2.) We leave the claim that a fine Minkowski cell contains an upside-down simplex if and only if it is a parallelotope as an exercise for the reader.

\Delta
-linkage covector
\mathbb{T}((0,1,2))
and a
\nabla
-linkage covector
\bar{\mathbb{T}}((1,1,3))
from one of the fine Minkowski cells in the fine mixed subdivision of
Figure

. For the
\nabla
-linkage covector, the edges that belong to the Chow covector
\Omega((1,1,3))

(lightly shaded) and the upside-down simplex
(1,1,3)+\nabla^{2}
(unshaded).

We now show that
\nabla
-linkage implies compatibility between
\Delta
-linkage covectors.

Proposition 4.4
.

Given an extended tope arrangement
\mathcal{T}
, for each lattice point
u\in(n-1)\Delta^{d-1}
, let
\mathbb{T}(u)=\bigcup_{\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]}T_{u+e_{\bar{j}}}
. If for any lattice point
w\in(n+1)\Delta^{d-1}
, the graph
\bar{\mathbb{T}}(w)=\bigcup_{\bar{j}\in\overline{\textnormal{supp}}(w)}T_{w-e_{\bar{j}}}
is acyclic, then any tope that is a subgraph of
\mathbb{T}(u)
is a tope of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

For this proof, we treat the tree
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}(u)
as a hypertree with vertex set on
[\bar{d}]
(see
Remark

3.2
). A tope
T
that is a subgraph of
\mathbb{T}
is the same as picking one of the vertices of each edge; we will call each tope an
(edge) orientation
in this new formulation, where each edge is
oriented to
the vertex we picked. For convenience, we will say that the edge
i
is
oriented towards
a vertex
\bar{j}
(which is not necessarily a vertex of the edge) if it is in the same connected component of
\mathbb{T}\setminus i
as the vertex we oriented the edge to.

We say that an orientation is
good
if it is in
\mathcal{T}
. By
Proposition

3.8
, for each vertex
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
, the
\bar{j}
-pull of
\mathbb{T}
is good. In the language of hypertrees, this means that there is a good orientation where each edge is oriented towards
\bar{j}
.

A
path

P
between two vertices
\bar{j}
and
\bar{j}^{\prime}
of the hypertree is the (unique) sequence of (distinct) edges
i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}
that connects the two vertices, where any two adjacent edges
i_{k}
and
i_{k+1}
share exactly one vertex
\bar{j}_{k}
(we also let
\bar{j}_{0}=\bar{j}
and
\bar{j}_{\ell}=\bar{j}^{\prime}
for convenience). Suppose we have two edge orientations
T
and
T^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{T}
that differ only on this path
P
, where the edges in
P
are oriented towards
\bar{j}
in
T
and towards
\bar{j}^{\prime}
in
T^{\prime}
. Let
v
and
v^{\prime}
be the position of
T
and
T^{\prime}
respectively; it is clear that we have
v+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}}=v^{\prime}+e_{\bar{j}}=w
for some
w\in(n+1)\Delta^{d-1}
. We see that
T\cup T^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
\bar{\mathbb{T}}(w)
. For each
k=0,1,\dots,\ell
, let
T_{k}
be the orientation obtained by orienting
i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}
towards
\bar{j}
and
i_{k},\dots,i_{\ell}
towards
\bar{j}^{\prime}
(i.e. the edges of
P
are oriented “away from
\bar{j}_{k}
”). Note that
T_{0}=T^{\prime}
and
T_{\ell}=T
. It is clear that
T_{k}\subseteq T\cup T^{\prime}
as well, and has position
w-e_{\bar{j}_{k}}
. If the tope
T_{w-e_{\bar{j}_{k}}}
of
\mathcal{T}
at this position is not
T_{k}
, then it is clear that the union of these two topes contains a cycle, which implies that
\bar{\mathbb{T}}(w)
also contains the same cycle. Therefore, we see that
T_{1},\dots,T_{\ell-1}
are good. We call this process of obtaining more good orientations “performing a
tug-of-war
along
P
” (since it essentially interpolates between a
\bar{j}
-pull and a
\bar{j}^{\prime}
-pull when restricted to
P
).

Figure 10.

T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
assuming that

and

are good. The orientation of each hyper-edge is indicated by an arrow. The gray hyper-edges are not on the path and their orientations are shared across all five orientations.

We show that we can use a series of tug-of-war to obtain any orientation of
\mathbb{T}
, starting from only the
\bar{j}
-pulls. Let us proceed by induction on the number
n
of edges of
\mathbb{T}
, where the base case
n=1
is obvious (any orientation is a
\bar{j}
-pull in this case). We can also ignore any edge containing only a single vertex (since there is only one possible orientation for them).

Now suppose that we have shown the statement for all hypertrees with
n-1
edges for
n\geq 2
. Consider a edge
i
of
\mathbb{T}
that only has one non-leaf vertex
\bar{j}_{0}
(this edge exists since in the original bipartite graph formulation, removing all leaves in
[\bar{d}]
necessarily gives a tree with at least two leaves in
[n]
). Let
\bar{J}
be the set of vertices that are not leaves of
i
(i.e. in the same connected component as
\bar{j}_{0}
if we remove edge
i
). Consider an orientation
T
of
\mathbb{T}
, of which there are two possible cases:

•

Edge
i
is oriented at vertex
\bar{j}_{0}
. We can remove
i
from
T
(and all leaf vertices of this edge) and
\mathbb{T}
and obtain
T\setminus\{i\}
in
\mathbb{T}\setminus\{i\}
by induction hypothesis. Since the
\bar{j}
-pull of
\mathbb{T}
has the same orientation for this edge as
T
for all
\bar{j}\in\bar{J}
and this edge is never in any path between two vertices of
\bar{J}
, the same series of tug-of-war that gives
T\setminus\{i\}
in
\mathbb{T}\setminus\{i\}
will give
T
in
\mathbb{T}
.

•

Edge
i
is oriented at a leaf vertex
\bar{j}_{1}\notin\bar{J}
. For each
\bar{j}\in\bar{J}
, since any path between
\bar{j}_{1}
and
\bar{j}
must pass through vertex
\bar{j}_{0}
, we can perform a tug-of-war between the
\bar{j}_{1}
-pull and
\bar{j}
-pull of
\mathbb{T}
(which only differ on the path between the two vertices) to get a “modified
\bar{j}
-pull”, which differs from the
\bar{j}
-pull only in edge
i
(which is now oriented at
\bar{j}_{1}
). Since all modified
\bar{j}
-pulls agree with
T
on edge
i
, we can perform the same argument as the previous case to obtain
T
.

This completes the inductive step. Since all
\bar{j}
-pulls are good, we see that all orientations are good, as desired.
∎

Proof of
2.36
.

The statement that there is an injection from generic tropical oriented matroids to matching ensembles (via extraction) is
Proposition

3.17
. For the reverse direction, we note that matching ensembles give rise to an extended tope arrangement and a collection of trees
\mathbb{T}(u)
, one for each
u\in(n-1)\Delta^{d-1}
, via the iterated amalgamation process. By
Proposition

4.4
, any tope that refines a tree is in the arrangement, so if two trees
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
are incompatible, then there are two topes
T\subseteq\mathbb{T}
and
T^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
that are also incompatible with each other. This means that the trees in the collection satisfy the compatibility axiom. Since there are exactly
\binom{n+d-2}{n-1}
such trees, by
Remark

2.16
we see that these trees encode a fine mixed subdivision and hence a generic tropical oriented matroid.

Since each partial matching of the original matching ensemble is a subgraph of some tope (as a result of the iterated amalgamation), it is clear that the extraction of the generic tropical oriented matroid is indeed the original matching ensemble. This shows that the extraction is also a surjection, so we have the desired bijection.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

As a consequence of
2.36
, we see that extended tope arrangements that satisfy the
\nabla
-linkage property in
Proposition

4.2
(which essentially arises from the right linkage axiom of the underlying matching ensemble) are cryptomorphic to pre-trianguloids that satisfy the hexagon axiom.

In fact, assuming that the conditions for extended tope arrangements (or pre-trianguloids) hold, right linkage and hexagon axiom are directly equivalent to each other: if we delete the Chow covector
\Omega(w)
from each tope and exchange the role between
[n]
and
[\bar{d}]
, then right linkage becomes strong linkage of
Theorem

2.28
while hexagon axiom becomes three-element linkage. This provides a shorter though less direct proof to
2.36
.

5.
A Linkage Covector for Trees

Unlike the proof of
2.36
in the previous section, the proof that trianguloids are cryptomorphic to fine mixed subdivisions (
Theorem

2.40
) uses the tree linkage axiom of instead of the compatibility axiom in
Theorem

2.6
(see
Remark

2.16
). In this section we use the tree linkage axiom to construct linkage covectors that each connects a “local” set of trees arising from a fine mixed subdivision. These linkage covectors have similar but more complex structures as the linkage covectors for (semi-)matchings, as they can no longer be defined using bipartite graphs.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a fine mixed subdivision of
n\Delta^{d-1}
defined by a collection of spanning trees
\mathbb{T}(u)
(one for each
u\in(n-1)\Delta^{d-1}
) and a lattice point
t\in(n-2)\Delta^{d-1}
, define a
tree-linkage graph

\mathbb{G}(t)
to be an edge-labeled graph on the vertex set
[\bar{d}]
, where we connect a pair of vertices
\bar{j}
and
\bar{j}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
and
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}})
differ in only one edge
(i,\bar{j})
and
(i^{\prime},\bar{j}^{\prime})
. This edge is labeled on both ends like this:
\bar{j}\xleftrightarrow{i\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}}\bar{j}^{\prime}
. For the vertex
\bar{j}
, we call
i
the
near label
for
\bar{j}
.

We also use
G(t)
to denote the same graph without edge labels.

It is not difficult to see that
G(t)
is also the “facet-adjacency graph” between the
d
fine Minkowski cells whose positions are
t+e_{\bar{j}}
for
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that the tree-linkage axiom translates to the property that for each non-leaf neighbor
i\in[n]
of
\bar{j}
in
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
, there is exactly one edge in
\mathbb{G}(t)
incident to
\bar{j}
for which the near label is
i
. (Note that since
RD(\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}}))=t+e_{\bar{j}}+\mathbf{1}_{[\bar{d}]}
,
\bar{j}
cannot be a leaf.) From
Lemma

2.7
, the two endpoints of an edge cannot have the same label.

Proposition 5.3
.

For each lattice point
t\in(n-2)\Delta^{d-1}
,
G(t)
is a spanning tree of
[\bar{d}]
.

Proof.

We first show that the graph is acyclic. Suppose that there is a cycle involving the vertex
\bar{j}_{0}
like
\dots\bar{j}_{\ell}\xleftrightarrow{i_{\ell}\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}_{0}}\bar{j}_{0}\xleftrightarrow{i_{0}\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}_{1}}\bar{j}_{1}\dots
in
\mathbb{G}(t)
. Denote
\mathbb{T}_{k}=\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{k}})
for convenience, then we can obtain
\mathbb{T}_{1},\mathbb{T}_{2},\dots,\mathbb{T}_{\ell},\mathbb{T}_{0}
from
\mathbb{T}_{0}
by repeatedly replacing one edge at a time. Note that the set of neighbors of
\bar{j}
in this tree
\mathbb{T}_{k}
is only modified at the first and last step, where in the first step we removed
i_{0}
and in the last step we added
i_{0}^{\prime}
. From
Remark

5.2
we see that
i_{0}\neq i_{0}^{\prime}
, which means that the final tree cannot be the same as the
\mathbb{T}_{0}
. Therefore, this cycle cannot exist.

We now show that the graph is connected by showing that there is a path from
\bar{j}_{0}
to
\bar{j}^{*}
for any
\bar{j}_{0},\bar{j}^{*}\in[\bar{d}]
. Suppose that in the tree
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{0}})
, the first vertex along the (unique) path from
\bar{j}_{0}
to
\bar{j}^{*}
is some
i_{0}\in[n]
. (In other words,
(i_{0},\bar{j}_{0})
is an edge of
(\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{0}}))^{\leftarrow\bar{j}^{*}}
.) This means that there is an edge of the form
\bar{j}_{0}\xleftrightarrow{i_{0}\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}_{1}}\bar{j}_{1}
in
\mathbb{G}(t)
. If
\bar{j}_{1}=\bar{j}^{*}
then we are done immediately.
Otherwise, note that from
Lemma

2.7
, if
H_{0}=\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{0}})\setminus(i_{0},\bar{j}_{0})
, then
\bar{j}_{0}
is in the same connected component
H_{0}^{(1)}
of
H
as
i^{\prime}_{1}
, and
i_{0},\bar{j}_{1},\bar{j}^{*}
are in the opposite connected component
H_{0}^{(2)}
.

Therefore, after adding the edge
(i^{\prime}_{1},\bar{j}_{1})
,
i^{\prime}_{1}
is further away from
\bar{j}^{*}
than
\bar{j}_{1}
in the new tree
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{1}})
, and hence there exists a different
i_{1}
adjacent to
\bar{j}_{1}
that is closer to
\bar{j}^{*}
. If we define
H_{1}^{(1)}
and
H_{1}^{(2)}
as the two connected components of
H_{1}=\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{1}})\setminus(i_{1},\bar{j}_{1})
where the former component contains
\bar{j}_{1}
, then the vertex set of
H_{0}^{(1)}
is a strict subset of that of
H_{1}^{(1)}
, since the previous edge replacement did not affect connectivity of
H_{0}^{(1)}
, and this component is now also connected to
\bar{j}_{1}
(and possibly some additional vertices). We also have that
\bar{j}^{*}\in H_{1}^{(2)}
by the same argument as with
H_{0}
.

Hence we can always repeat this process (which gives a path along the graph
\mathbb{G}(t)
), never repeating any
\bar{j}_{k}
’s (as
\mathbb{G}(t)
is acyclic), until we reach
\bar{j}_{\ell}=\bar{j}^{*}
for some
\ell
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

A proof of connectivity of
G(t)
can also be found in
[
OY10
]
, Lemma 4.10, using machinery of totally unimodular matrices.

Similar to semi-matchings and linkage covectors, there is an analogous way to (partially) obtain trees from tree-linkage graphs.

Definition 5.5
.

Given a tree-linkage graph
\mathbb{G}(t)
and
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
, let the
\bar{j}
-pull
of
\mathbb{G}(t)
be a subgraph
G\subseteq K_{n,d}
consisting of the following edges: for each edge of
\mathbb{G}(t)
, if
\xrightarrow{i^{\prime}}\bar{j}^{\prime}
is the end closer to
\bar{j}
, then add the edge
(i^{\prime},\bar{j}^{\prime})
to
G
. We use
\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
to denote the resulting graph.

Proposition 5.6
.

For a given lattice point
t\in(n-2)\Delta^{d-1}
, let
T_{t}=\bigcap_{\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]}\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
. We have the following:

(a)

For each
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
,
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})=T_{t}\sqcup\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
.

(b)

T_{t}
is a tope, i.e.
LD(T_{t})=\mathbf{1}_{[n]}
.

Proof.

(a)

If
\bar{j}
and
\bar{j}^{\prime}
are adjacent in
\mathbb{G}(t)
via an edge
\bar{j}\xleftrightarrow{i\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}}\bar{j}^{\prime}
, then the
\bar{j}
-pull and
\bar{j}^{\prime}
-pull differ by exactly one edge
(i,\bar{j})
and
(i^{\prime},\bar{j}^{\prime})
, just like
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
and
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}}^{\prime})
. If there is an edge
(i^{*},\bar{j}^{*})\in\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}\setminus\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
, then it is also in
\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}^{\prime}}\setminus\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}^{\prime}})
. This argument applies transitively along
\mathbb{G}(t)
to show that
(i^{*},\bar{j}^{*})\in\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}^{*}}\setminus\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}^{*}})
, which is impossible due to
Remark

5.2
. Therefore, we see that
\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}\subseteq\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
for all
\bar{j}
.

Now let
T^{(\bar{j})}=\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})\setminus\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
for each
\bar{j}
. From the same argument as above we see that
T^{(\bar{j})}=T^{(\bar{j}^{\prime})}
whenever
\bar{j}
and
\bar{j}^{\prime}
are adjacent in
\mathbb{G}(t)
, and is hence the same set for all
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
, which we denote by
T^{\prime}_{t}
. Therefore
T_{t}
contains
T^{\prime}_{t}
. On the other hand, any edge in
T_{t}
cannot be replaced in any tree, and hence will not appear in any
\bar{j}
-pull, so it is also contained in
T^{\prime}_{t}
. This means that
T_{t}=T^{\prime}_{t}
, as desired.

(b)

Since
\mathbb{G}(t)
is a spanning tree on
[\bar{d}]
,
\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
has
d-1
edges and hence
T_{t}
has exactly
n
edges, so it suffices to show that no vertices in
[n]
has degree greater than
1
in
T_{t}
. Indeed, if
i
is connected to two vertices
\bar{j}
and
\bar{j}^{\prime}
in all trees, then the edge
(i,\bar{j})
is replaceable in
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
and cannot be in
T_{t}
.

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

In a fine mixed subdivision of
n\Delta^{d-1}
and a lattice point
t\in(n-2)\Delta^{d-1}
, the intersection of the
d
cells whose positions are
t+e_{\bar{j}}
for some
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
is exactly a single lattice point.

Definition 5.8
.

Given a fine mixed subdivision and a lattice point
t\in(n-2)\Delta^{d-1}
, define a
tree-linkage covector

\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(t)
to be the tree-linkage graph
\mathbb{G}(t)
where each vertex is additionally labeled by some (zero or more) elements of
[n]
: let
T_{t}=\bigcap_{\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]}\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
, then vertex
\bar{j}
is labeled with
i
if and only if
(i,\bar{j})
is an edge of
T_{t}
.

For a vertex
\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]
, define its
augmented degree
to be the number of incident edges plus the number of vertex labels. Also, we define the
\bar{j}
-pull

\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}=T_{t}\sqcup\mathbb{G}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}}
.

By
Proposition

5.6
, each
i\in[n]
appears as a vertex label of a tree-linkage covector exactly once, and each tree
\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
is exactly recovered by the
\bar{j}
-pull of the covector.

Proposition 5.9
.

For a lattice point
t\in(n-2)\Delta^{d-1}
, the augmented degree vector of
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(t))
is equal to
t+\mathbf{2}_{\bar{d}}
.

Proof.

Given a vertex of tree-linkage covector
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(t)
, notice that the near label on each incident edge and each vertex label represent an edge that is in at least one of the spanning trees. Therefore, the augmented degree vector is the same as the right degree vector of the union
\bigcup_{\bar{j}\in[\bar{d}]}\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}})
. The result then follows from
Proposition

3.11
where we take
m=2
.
∎

We conclude this section by some additional properties of the labels on
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(t)
that may be of interest:

Proposition 5.10
.

If there is a path in
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(t)
of the form

\bar{j}_{0}\xleftrightarrow{i_{0}\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}_{1}}\bar{j}_{1}\xleftrightarrow{i_{1}\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}_{2}}\bar{j}_{2}\dots\bar{j}_{\ell-1}\xleftrightarrow{i_{\ell-1}\hskip 8.19447pti^{\prime}_{\ell}}\bar{j}_{\ell},

then the following holds:

(a)

Let
k
be an integer between
0
and
\ell-1
inclusive. In the graph
H_{k}=\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{k}})\setminus(i_{k},\bar{j}_{k})
,
\bar{j}_{0},\dots,\bar{j}_{k}
are in a different connected component from
\bar{j}_{k+1},\dots,\bar{j}_{\ell}
.

(b)

For any
1\leq k\leq k^{\prime}\leq\ell-1
,
i^{\prime}_{k}\neq i_{k^{\prime}}
. (In other words, no identical edge labels can point at each other.)

(c)

If vertex
\bar{j}_{k}
is labeled with
i
, then
i_{k^{\prime}}\neq i
for all
k^{\prime}\geq k
and
i^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\neq i
for all
k^{\prime}\leq k
. (In other words, no edge label can point at an identical vertex label.)

Proof.

(a) follows from the proof of connectivity for
Proposition

5.3
. For (b), it suffices to show that
i^{\prime}_{1}\neq i_{\ell-1}
. The cases
\ell=1
and

are obvious from
Remark

5.2
, so we assume
\ell\geq 3
. From
Proposition

5.6
(a) we see that
(i^{\prime}_{1},\bar{j}_{1})
and
(i_{\ell-1},\bar{j}_{\ell-1})
are both in
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(t)^{\rightarrow\bar{j}_{1}}=\mathbb{T}(t+e_{\bar{j}_{1}})
, and from (a) we also have that

are in different connected components of

, which means that

∎

In other words, if we look at only the edges labels that are equal to

.

With the exception of generic tropical oriented matroids, one important commonality of the objects in this paper is that they require there to be exactly one graph for each possible left/right degree vector (or each pair of left and right degree vectors, in the case of matching stacks). This condition is absent for generic tropical oriented matroids, but is instead generated from the boundary axiom and the elimination axiom. It remains to be seen whether there is any direct connection between the elimination axiom and some formulation of the linkage axiom (such as the one suggestively named “Elimination” in

).

6.1.
Future directions

There are several potential ways to generalize the work of this paper:

•

Instead of considering fine mixed subdivisions of
P=n\Delta^{d-1}
, one can replace each summand of the Minkowski sum with a face of
\Delta^{d-1}
. The resulting polytope will be a
generalized permutohedron
, whose fine mixed subdivisions biject to triangulations of a
root polytope
(
[
Pos05
]
, Section 12). This generalization translates to considering acyclic subgraphs of a particular subgraph
H\subseteq K_{n,d}
. We note that the theory of trianguloids is already generalized to this case (
[
GNP24
]
, Section 5), and a generalization of tropical oriented matroids has been conjectured in
[
OY10
]
.

•

The argument in
Section

4
suggests that there may be some sort of symmetry between
\Delta
-linkage and
\nabla
-linkage originating from left and right linkage, even though in the
P=n\Delta^{d-1}
case the latter implies the former. This motivates considering fine mixed subdivisions of
P=n\Delta^{d-1}+m\nabla^{d-1}
where this symmetry might be more apparent. This Minkowski sum is also related to the Tutte polynomial of a (poly)matroid (
[
KP16
,
CF22
,
BKP22
]
).

•

It might also be possible to generalize to
non-fine
mixed subdivisions, where the corresponding graphs no longer need to be acyclic.

A Topological Proof of the Archimedean Axiom for Archimedean Copulas
Victory Idowu

Abstract
Archimedean copulas are a popular type of copulas in which a variant of the Archimedean axiom apply. We provide a topological proof of the Archimedean Axiom which is applicable for non-continuous distribution functions.

Properties of the Archimedean copulas have been studied extensively under continuous distribution functions but limited work has been done for discrete or mixed distribution functions. Copulas and the Archimedean axiom

In this section, we provide preliminary notions on copulas and Archimedean copulas. In this paper, we denote
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:=[-\infty,+\infty]^{d}
.

Definition 1

(H-volume,
V_{H}

[
6
,
1

)
.

Let
H:\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]:=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times[a_{2},b_{2}]\times\ldots\times[a_{d},b_{d}]
. Then the H-volume,
V_{H}
is defined as

V_{H}([\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}])=\sum_{\mathbf{c}}\mathrm{sign}(\mathbf{c})H(\mathbf{c})

where
\mathbf{c}
are the vertices of the box
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
and

\mathrm{sign}(\mathbf{c})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }c_{k}=a_{k}\text{ for an even number of }$k$\text{'s}\\
-1,&\text{ if }c_{k}=a_{k}\text{ for an odd number of }$k$\text{'s}.\end{cases}

We can define the d-dimensional cumulative distribution function
F
(d.fs) in terms of the
V_{H}
volume, see for instance
Durante et al. 2013
. In this case
F
is a distribution function if it satisfies the following definition.

Definition 2

(Cumulative distribution function
F
[
2
]
)
.

A d-dimensional cumulative distribution function
F:\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
satsifies the following:

1.

for every
\mathbf{x}\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}
if at least one argument of
\mathbf{x}
is 0 then
F(\mathbf{x})=0
and we define
F(+\infty,+\infty,\ldots,+\infty)=1

2.

for every
j\in\{1,2,\ldots d\}
and for all
x_{1},\ldots x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d}\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}

t\mapsto F(x_{1},\ldots x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})
is right continuous

3.

V_{F}([\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}])\geq 0
, for every box
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
.

A copula is a representation of

Definition 4

(Archimedean copulas
[
4
]
)
.

A copula
C
is considered as an Archimedean copula if it can be written in the form

C(\mathbf{u})=\varphi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\varphi^{[-1]}(u_{j})\right),\qquad\mathbf{u}\in[0,1]^{d}

where generator function
\varphi
satisfies the following:

1.

\varphi:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right]

2.

\varphi
is a continuous and strictly decreasing function in the interval
\left[0,1\right]

3.

\varphi
is such that
\varphi(1)=0
.

The pseudo inverse of the generator is defined as:

\varphi^{[-1]}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi^{-1}(x)&0\leq x\leq\varphi(0)\\
0&\varphi(0)<x\leq\infty\end{cases}

(1)

Any copula can have a volume defined by its recursive powers of one distribution function, this is called the
C
-power. More formally,

Definition 5

(
C
-power
[
6
]
)
.

For an Archimedean copula
C_{\varphi}
and any
u\in[0,1]
. Define
f_{1}(u):=u
and
n+1

C
-power as
f_{n+1}(u):=C(u,f_{n}(u))
.

The sub-class of Archimedean copulas are called Archimedean as their construct leads to the following version of the Archimedean axiom,
[
6
]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

The Archimedean axiom for
([0,1],C)
is, for any two
u,v\in(0,1)
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
f_{n}(u)<v
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
for continuous d.fs is given in
[
6
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

The Archimedean axiom is not valid at the idempotents
\{0,1\}
. When
u=1
,
f_{n+1}(u)=f_{n}(u)
, for all
n
. When
u=0
, the recursive relation
f_{n+1}=0
immediately stabilizes at 0 and there is no diminishing process. In both cases, the sequence
\{f_{n}(u)\}
does not decrease or converge to 0 and the Archimedean axiom does not apply.

3
Proof of Archimedean axiom

Our goal is to show for Archimedean copulas that the Archimedean axiom also holds for non-continuous
F
. As
F
is non-continuous, there may be regions in which
C
and
V_{C}
is also non-continuous. Hence, we develop a topological proof using
V_{C}
that applies to non-continuous distributions. First, we present lemmas that will help us establish the proof. Then, we consider the limit of
V_{C}
for non-continuous
F
. From which we derive how the limiting distribution of recursive distribution of
V_{C}
for Archimedean copulas.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any Archimedean copula
C
,
V_{C}([0,u]\times[0,v])<v
for any
v<1
.

Proof.

As the generator function
\varphi
is strictly decreasing,
\varphi(u)>\varphi(1)=0
for any
u\in(0,1)
. Hence,
\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)>\varphi(u)
which implies
\varphi^{[-1]}(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))<\varphi^{[-1]}(\varphi(u))=u
. Thus,
C(u,v)<u
when
v<1
, from which the result is immediate. ∎∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
n\geq 1,u\in(0,1)
,
f_{n+1}(u)\leq f_{n}(u)
.

Proof.

This is shown by induction. Base case,
f_{1}(u)=u
and
f_{2}(u)=C(u,u)=C(u,f_{1}(u))\leq f_{1}(u)
as for any copula
C(u,v)\leq u
for all
v\leq u
.
Inductive step, assume
f_{n}(u)\leq f_{n-1}(u)
. Thus by monotonicity,

f_{n+1}(u)=C(u,f_{n}(u))\leq C(u,f_{n-1}(u))=f_{n}(u).

Hence,
f_{n+1}(u)\leq f_{n}(u)
for all
n
. ∎∎

4
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Suppose
C
is has discontinuities over the box
B=[0,u]\times[0,v]
. Consider the partition of
B=\cup_{k,j}B_{k,j}
, where
C
is continuous. Define,

B_{k}=[u_{k},u_{k+1}]\times[v_{j},v_{j+1}],\qquad k=0,\ldots,m-1,\;j=0,\ldots,n-1,

where
0=u_{0}<u_{1}<\ldots u_{m}=u
and
0=v_{0}<v_{1}<\ldots v_{p}=v
. For each
B_{k,j}
,

V_{C}([u_{k},u_{k+1}]\times[v_{j},v_{j+1}])=C(u_{k+1},v_{j+1})-C(u_{k},v_{j+1})-C(u_{k+1},v_{j})+C(u_{k},v_{j})

As
B_{k}
becomes finer, i.e.
\max(u_{k+1}-u_{k},v_{j+1}-v_{j})\rightarrow 0
,

\lim_{m,p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}V_{C}(B_{k,j})=V_{C}(B)

Convergence is guaranteed by the compactness of
[0,1]^{2}
under the product topology and the continuity of
V_{C}
-volumes within each sub-region that
C(u,v)
is defined.

Define the recursive volume function as:

V_{C}^{n}(u):=V_{C}([0,u]\times[0,f_{n}(u)]

As
f_{n}(u)
is non-decreasing,

V_{C}^{n+1}(u)\leq V_{C}^{n}(u)

thus,
V_{C}^{n}(u)
is a non-increasing sequence with respect to
n
.

It follows that
f_{n}(u)\in[0,1]
, as
C(u,v)
is defined on
[0,1]^{2}
. By Lemma
3.2

f_{n}(u)
is non-increasing, it is also bounded below by
0
, it converges to a limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}(u)=L,\qquad L\geq 0

where
L
is the limit. If
L>0
, then

L=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}(u)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C(u,f_{n}(u))=C(u,L)

However, by Lemma
3.1
,
V_{C}([0,u]\times[0,v])<v
for
v>0
and sufficiently large
n
. This creates a contradiction and hence,
L=0
. Hence,

f_{n}(u)\rightarrow 0\text{ and }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}V_{C}^{n}(u)=V_{C}([0,u]\times[0,0])=0.

Thus there exists an
N
, for any
v\in(0,1)
, such that
f_{N}(u)<v
, which completes the proof. ∎

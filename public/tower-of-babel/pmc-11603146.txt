Identification of biomarkers for the diagnosis in colorectal polyps and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) by bioinformatics analysis and machine learning
Authors: Ying Geng, Yifang Li, Ge Liu, Jian Jiao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractColorectal polyps are precursors of colorectal cancer. Metabolic dysfunction associated steatohepatitis (MASH) is one of metabolic dysfunction associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) phenotypic manifestations. Much evidence has suggested an association between MASH and polyps. This study investigated the biomarkers of MASH and colorectal polyps, and the prediction of targeted drugs using an integrated bioinformatics analysis method. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) were performed on GSE89632 and GSE41258 datasets, 49 shared genes revealed after intersection. The Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses depicted they were mainly enriched in apoptosis, proliferation and infection pathways. Machine learning algorithms identified S100P, FOXO1, and LPAR1 were biomarkers for colorectal polyps and MASH, ROC curve and violin plot showed ideal AUC and stable expression patterns in both the discovery and validation sets. GSEA analysis showed significant enrichment of bile acid and fatty acid pathways when grouped by the expression levels of the three candidate biomarkers. Immune infiltration analysis showed a significant infiltration of M0 macrophages and Treg cells in the colorectal polyps group. A total of 9 small molecule compounds were considered as potential chemoprevention agents in MASH and colorectal polyps by using the CMap website. Introduction

With the gradual control of communicable liver diseases, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) / metabolic dysfunction associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) has emerged as the most common chronic liver disease worldwide. The global prevalence of NAFLD among adults has been estimated to be 23–25% 1. In February, 2020, a panel of international experts from 22 countries propose an update of the nomenclature and metabolic dysfunction associated fatty liver disease, MAFLD, as a more appropriate term for NAFLD2. The two terminologies can be used interchangeably3. NAFLD/MAFLD covers a spectrum of histology ranging from simple steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)/ metabolic dysfunction associated steatohepatitis (MASH), an advanced subtype of NAFLD/MAFLD that affects up to 20% of NAFLD/MAFLD patients1. NASH/MASH can progress to fibrosis, cirrhosis and even hepatocellular carcinoma. Patients with NASH/MASH have been shown to have an increased risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer and other adverse outcomes4. While NASH/MASH remains a histological diagnosis with the presence of hepatic steatosis accompanied by hepatocellular injury such as ballooning, apoptosis, and inflammatory infiltration5. In contrast to liver biopsy, which is an invasive and costly procedure for the diagnosis of NASH/MASH, there is a need for the discovery of non-invasive biomarkers. Regarding the treatment of NAFLD/MAFLD, the current validated strategy is based on lifestyle modifications (exercise and dietary restraint) to achieve weight loss6.

Colorectal polyps, defined as abnormal colonic mucosal growths protruding into the lumen, are considered precursors of colorectal cancer (CRC). Adenomatous polyps (adenomas) are the major subtype of precursors, and the adenoma-carcinoma sequence is the classic pathway explaining the majority of CRCs, with nearly 85–90% of sporadic CRCs progressing from adenomas7. CRC is the third most commonly diagnosed malignancy and the second leading cause of cancer death worldwide8. Therefore, early detection and treatment of colorectal polyps is an effective way to prevent CRC. While, the gold standard for diagnosing colorectal polyps is colonoscopy, which takes time to schedule and requires patients to undergo bowel preparation. Statistics showed that at least 25% of men and 15% of women who undergo colonoscopy have one or more adenomas. Up to 40% of people older than 60 years develop colorectal adenomatous polyps9. At present there are no ideal biomarkers capable for polyps. Chemoprevention for colorectal polyps, including aspirin, calcium, vitamin D and others, while the results of different studies have been conflicting10, so it is urgent to develop new chemopreventive agents for colorectal polyps.

Recent evidence has linked NAFLD/MAFLD to colorectal polyps. Cosmas Rinaldi A et al. Found that NAFLD is associated with increased risk of any colon polyp, regardless of the histopathological type, compared with patients without NAFLD11, Qin-Fen Chen et al. found that NAFLD is specifically associated with an increased risk of colorectal adenomatous and hyperplastic polyps in men12, Yuri Cho et al. showed that the presence of biopsy-proven NASH was significantly associated with an increased risk of advanced colorectal neoplasm among patients with NAFLD13. The mechanisms linking NAFLD/MAFLD and extrahepatic bowel disease include insulin resistance, adipokines, and gut microbiota. However, the underlying mechanisms have not yet been elucidated.

In the present study, using the array data of NASH and colorectal polyps in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, differentially expressed genes analysis (DEG) and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), machine learning algorithms such as least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), random forest (RF) and support vector machine recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) were performed, to investigate biomarkers for colorectal polyps and NASH, then enrichment analysis of Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)14 (https://www.kegg.jp/) and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were conducted to elucidate the biological pathways of candidate biomarkers. Then, a prediction model encompassed by the core genes was developed, the nomogram, calibration curve and ROC curve were drawn, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was calculated, all these methods were used to access the predictive ability of the candidate biomarkers in NASH/MASH and colorectal polyps. In addition, in order to illustrate the immune cell infiltration in the colorectal polyp data sets, immunoinfiltration analysis was performed. Finally, drug prediction analysis was performed to predict small molecule compounds for both NASH/MASH and colorectal polyps using the query module of the CMap website. In summary, integrated bioinformatic analysis methods were processed to investigate biomarkers and chemopreventive agents for NASH and colorectal polyps.

Method

Data collection and study design

Through comprehensive screening of the GEO database (Gene Expression Omnibus, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), the search strategy terms included “NAFLD” or “colorectal polyps”, “array” type and “Homo sapiens”. Meanwhile, the inclusion criteria of datasets were as follows, (1) at least 5 patients in each group were included in the data sets, (2) the gene symbols and the Entrez ID were available in the annotated platforms (GPL). Finally, three datasets were selected, GSE89632 was the NAFLD dataset, GSE41258 and GSE8671 were the colorectal polyps datasets, GSE8671 was the validated dataset, the details of these datasets were shown in Table 1; Fig. 1 shows the design of this research.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis

The R packages “limma” (version 3.58.1)15, “GEOquery” (version 2.70.0)16, “hgu133plus2.db” (version 3.13.0) were used for DEGs analysis of NASH and colorectal polyps samples. DEGs filter criteria was set to |log2FC| ≥ 1 and p < 0.05, followed by volcano map, top and tail 20 DEGs thermal map of NASH and healthy control were plotted using R packages “pheatmap” (version 1.0.12) and “ggplot2” (version 3.5.0)17.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and identification of colorectal polyps core genes

WGCNA identifies modules of co-expressed genes and elucidates their relationships with phenotypic traits by constructing a co-expression network from gene expression data. In this study, WGCNA analysis was performed using “WGCNA” package (version 1.72-5)18 in R software (version 4.3.2). First, the top 75% median absolute deviation (MAD) genes of the expression matrix were selected. Second, samples of colorectal polyps and healthy controls were hierarchically clustered, identified, and the outlier was excluded. Third, the best soft threshold was selected based on the scale-free topological fitting index graph, then soft threshold and average connectivity analysis were performed to establish the scale-free co-expression network. The gene modules were identified by means of hierarchy clustering or dynamic tree cut. Finally, to identify significantly correlated eigengenes of colorectal polyps, correlation analysis of modules and clinical phenotype was performed. The criteria for significant modules were module membership (MM) > 0.8 and gene significance (GS) > 0.2.

Identifying common genes in NASH and colorectal polyps and enrichment analysis by GO and KEGG

Core genes in significant modules of colorectal polyps analyzed by WGCNA were intersected with DEGs of NASH samples, the intersected result was depicted by Venn diagram drawing by “ggvenn” (version 0.1.10) R package. GO and KEGG functional enrichment analysis for the intersected genes were performed using the “clusterProfiler” package (version 4.10.0)19 of R.

Machine learning analysis for candidate genes identification

The aforementioned intersected genes were further analyzed by machine learning algorithms. In this study, three machine learning algorithms including LASSO, RF and SVM-RFE were carried out to identify significant biomarkers. LASSO is a linear regression method used for feature selection and regularization. A regression model was established by using the “glmnet” (version 4.1-8)20 R package, with “lambda.1se” was selected as the preferred value for feature screening. “randomForest” (version 4.7–1.1) R package was used to preform RF analysis, after constructing an RF model, genes were sorted based on the “Mean Decrease Accuracy”, then the top 30 important genes were visualized, the significance threshold of features was set to 3. SVM-RFE is a feature selection method that iteratively removes the least important features based on the weights assigned by an SVM classifier, R packages “e1071” (version 1.7–14) and “caret” (version 6.0–94)21 were used for SVM-RFE analysis and five-fold cross-validation was performed for biomarker selection. Finally, a Venn diagram was drawn to illustrate the intersected result of these three algorithms.

Nomogram establishment and ROC evaluation of candidate biomarkers

A nomogram was constructed using version 6.7-1 of the R package “rms”. In the nomogram, each core gene has a corresponding score, the total score was equal to the sum of each score, which can predict the risk of occurrence of colorectal polyps and NASH, and the calibration curve was used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of the nomogram model. In addition, ROC curves were constructed and the AUC was calculated to measure the predictive value of each candidate biomarker in the colorectal polyps discovery datasets GSE41258 and validation datasets GSE8671. Subsequently, the expression patterns of the 3 candidate biomarkers in the discovery and the validation datasets were displayed using the half violin plot.

Immunoinfiltration analysis

Immunoinfiltration analysis of expression data to calculate the proportion of immune cells in colorectal polyps and healthy controls was performed using the R package “IOBR” (version 0.99.8)22. The correlation coefficients and p-values between each of the candidate biomarkers and the 28 immune cell types were calculated in batches using the R package “linkET” (version 0.0.7.4). The packages “ggpubr” (version 0.6.0), “ggplot2” (version 3.5.0) in R were used to visualize the results of immunoinfiltration analysis.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

GSEA was performed to identify the candidate biomarkers related biological pathways through “clusterProfiler” (version 4.10.0) R package. For analysis, the Hallmark gene set (h.all.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt) was downloaded from the Molecular Signatures Database (MsigDB). A positive normal enrichment score (NES) means that the Hallmark gene set is at the top of the sorted gene set, and a negative NES means that the Hallmark gene set is at the bottom of the sorted gene set. Finally, the GSEA results were plotted using the R packages “enrichplot” (version 1.22.0), “GseaVis” (version 0.0.5), “ggplot2” (version 3.5.0).

Drug prediction by CMap

The Connectivity Map (CMap, https://clue.io/) is an online tool aimed at identifying potential therapeutic compounds by analyzing how disease gene expression patterns change in response to various perturbations, such as drug treatment or genetic manipulation (knockdown or overexpression).

By uploading differentially expressed genes performed by the above algorithm, the connectivity values (between − 100 and 100) of different compounds were then calculated by the query tool, with the results ordered in a decreasing manner, with values closer to -100 indicating that the uploaded gene list is more opposite to the perturbed data set, the potential of the compound as a treatment for the disease is higher. In the current study, the cut-off value of negative connectivity scores was defined as -95~-99.99 and p < 0.05.

Results

Data information

Three datasets were selected for bioinformatics analysis from GEO: GSE89632, GSE41258, GSE8671. Table 1 outlining the details concerning these datasets. Table S2 and Table S3 depicted the population demographics and the clinical characteristics of the samples.

Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEG) in NASH

Based on the criteria of |logFC| > 1 and adjusted p < 0.05, a total of 385 DEGs, including 152 up-regulated genes and 233 down-regulated genes, were identified in this study using the GSE89632 dataset (Fig. 2A). The top 10 genes were selected from the up-regulated and down-regulated genes respectively to generate heat maps, with yellow indicates up-regulated genes and blue indicates down-regulated genes (Fig. 2B).

Identification of coexpression gene modules in colorectal polyps

GSE41258 was used to obtain the most relevant modular genes for colorectal polyps by the WGCNA algorithm. First, the results of the hierarchical cluster analysis of the samples showed good clustering among the samples, one significant outlier was eliminated (GSM1012351, Fig S1, Fig. 3A). Then the most relevant modular genes for colorectal polyps were identified, and the soft threshold was set to 7 to satisfy the scale-free topology of the network, where the corresponding R2 was 0.92 and the average connectivity was high (Fig. 3B-C). Furthermore, different colors were chosen to represent the co-expression of 17 modular genes between colorectal polyps samples and healthy controls (HC), as shown in Fig. 3E, and the turquoise module (2442genes), considered as critical modules, showed the highest correlation with polyp samples (correlation coefficient r = 0.92, p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3D). In addition, a significant positive correlation was observed between module membership and gene significance in the turquoise modules for colorectal polyps samples (r = 0.93, p < 0.0001), as shown in Fig. 3F.

Screening diagnostic markers by machine learning algorithms

To identify potential diagnostic biomarkers from a set of 49 shared genes identified in both NASH and colorectal polyps, three machine learning algorithms were conducted. The LASSO regression algorithm identified 7 genes as demonstrated in Fig. 5A-B. In contrast, the RF algorithm generated 6 potential biomarkers based on their importance, depicted in Fig. 5C and E. Furthermore, the SVM-RFE analysis showed that a model involving 5 genes achieved the highest accuracy, as illustrated in Fig. 5F-G. By comparing the results of these three algorithms, 3 potential biomarkers, FOXO1, S100P, LPAR1, were identified as shared biomarkers for both NASH and colorectal polyps (Fig. 5D).

Validation of diagnostic markers

Using the “rms” package, we developed nomogram models for the diagnosis of NASH and colorectal polyps based on the biomarkers FOXO1, LPAR1 (Fig. 6A). According to the results of the calibration curve analyses, the nomogram model shows a better diagnostic ability (Fig. 6B). With AUCs of 0.999, 0.989 and 0.925, respectively, the ROC curves for LPAR1, S100P and FOXO1 indicated their likelihood as valuable biomarkers (Fig. 6C), indicating that the biological markers had a high predictive accuracy. With AUCs of 1, 1 and 0.925, respectively, the ROC curves for LPAR1, S100P and FOXO1 indicated their likelihood as valuable biomarkers in the GSE8671 validation set (Fig. 6D). In the GSE8671 validation set, the expression of LPAR1 and FOXO1 was significantly lower in the colorectal group than in the normal group (p < 0.001) (Fig. 6F), as well as in the GSE41258 datasets, depicted in the half violin diagram (Fig. 6E). Immune infiltration analysis and correlation analysis

Immune infiltration analysis was performed on GSE41258. The results showed that the number of T cells CD4 naive, T cells CD4memory activated, Macrophages M0, T cells regulatory (Tregs), NK cells resting, Mast cells activated, T cells CD4 memory resting in colorectal polyps patients was higher than that in the control group, and the number of T cells follicular helper, Mast cells resting and Macrophages M2 was lower than that of the control group, as depicted in Fig. 7A-B. Therefore, the correlation between the 3 diagnostic markers with immune cell infiltration in colorectal polyps is noteworthy for further exploration. First, in whole groups (healthy controls and colorectal polyps), the S100P expression was positively correlated with the ratios of CD56 bright natural killer cells, immature dendritic cells and memory B cells. LPAR1 was negatively correlated with the ratios of activated CD4 T cells, CD56 bright natural killer cells and memory B cells, FOXO1 was negatively correlated with the ratios of CD56 bright natural killer cells and memory B cells (Fig. 7C). Next, the relationship of each individual gene with each type of immune cell was then examined in different groups. In the colorectal polyps group, LPAR1 was positively correlated with the ratios of immature B cells, type 17 T helper cells, gamma-delta T cells, activated B cells, effector memory CD8 T cells, central memory CD4 T cells, natural killer cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells, regulatory T cells (Fig. 7D), S100P was positively correlated with the ratios of immature B cells, macrophage and type 2 T helper cells, and negatively correlated with activated CD4 T cells, activated CD8 T cells and neutrophils (Fig. 7D). The relationship between FOXO1 and each type of immune cell shows no statistical significance.

Discussion

With the increasing number of NAFLD/MAFLD patients, a variety of co-morbidities have attracted widespread attention. Emerging evidence shows that NAFLD/MAFLD is associated with multisystem extrahepatic diseases, increasing the risk and contributing to the pathophysiology of atherosclerosis, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease, various extrahepatic malignancies23. Given that the gastrointestinal system is closely related to the liver in physiology and anatomy (such as the enterohepatic bile salt cycle), the occurrence of gastrointestinal malignancies in NAFLD/MAFLD has attracted particular attention. Studies have shown that NAFLD is an independent risk factor for the development of colorectal adenomas after a negative baseline colonoscopy24, NAFLD has also emerged as a risk factor for CRC25. The mechanisms of the association between NAFLD and extrahepatic cancer are not well understood, the putative mechanisms include insulin resistance, adipokine26 and intestinal dysbiosis27, etc. Patients with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) were more likely to have abnormal glucose tolerance than those with NAFL. Pre- and post-load serum insulin levels were significantly higher in the NASH group28. Insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia lead to the upregulation of the insulin-like growth factor (IGF) axis, which contains IGF-1 and IGF-2, IGF1R and IGF2R, and IGF-binding proteins (IGFB1–6) and IRS-1, which subsequently increases the activation and phosphorylation of p53 or PI3K/PTEN/AKT signaling pathway, which then induces cell proliferation, inhibits cell apoptosis, and thereby promotes tumorigenesis29. Adiponectin has anti-carcinogenic effects mediated by its ability to stop colon cancer cell growth through the AMPc-activated protein kinase (AMPK) and to induce a caspase-dependent pathway leading to endothelial cell apoptosis, while NAFLD patients have reduced serum levels of adiponectin26. In patients with NAFLD, intestinal bacteria, such as Bacteroidetes overgrowth, by activating toll-like receptors to perk up the NF-kappa B and JNK, then promote the generation of various inflammatory mediators30. TNF-α acts as a tumor-promoting factor and is associated with most steps of tumorigenesis including transformation, proliferation, angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis in many cancers31, while research findings by Vachliotis ID circulating TNF-α gradually increases from controls to patients with NAFL, and then, to patients with NASH32. However, whether bile acid was associated with colorectal polyps in patients with NASH and its mechanism was not clear. This study attempts to explore the relationship between bile acid alterations and colorectal polyps or NASH. First, the result of GSEA analysis in GSE89632 dataset showed that, when three candidate genes were lower expressed in patients with NASH (Fig. 8A), bile acid metabolism pathway significantly enriched with fraction NES were 2.10 (S100P), 2.15 (LPAR1), 1.94 (FOXO1), FDR < 0.001(Fig. 8E, F and G). This conclusion was also supported by several studies. Aranha et al. determined bile acids composition by gas-liquid chromatography in liver tissue from patients with nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (n = 15), bile acids were moderately elevated in liver tissue from patients with steatohepatitis compared to controls (p < 0.05). Deoxycholic, chenodeoxycholic, and cholic acids were elevated by 92%, 64%, and 43%, respectively, in patients with steatohepatitis (p < 0.05)33. Liquid chromatography/mass spectrometry was used by Puneet Puri et al. to quantify bile acids in 25 NAFL and 37 NASH, the result showed that total conjugated primary bile acids were significantly higher in NASH vs. NAFL (p < 0.05) and vs. controls (p < 0.0001)34.

Primary bile acids such as cholic acid and chenodeoxycholic acid (CDCA) are conjugated with glycine or taurine in hepatocytes prior to secretion into the intestinal environment. Conjugated bile acids ultimately undergo actions such as deconjugation and dihydroxylation by intestinal bacteria in the large intestine to generate secondary bile acids35. Notably, the metabolism of taurine-conjugated bile acids by intestinal microbiota may promote colorectal carcinogenesis by generating genotoxic compounds such as hydrogen sulfid36. Repeated long-term exposure of colonic epithelial cells to high physiological concentrations of bile acids appears to select for cells that are resistant to induction of apoptosis by bile acids. Such apoptosis-resistant cells might arise and clonally expand through the processes of mutation and natural selection37.

In this study, immune cell infiltration analysis was performed in the GSE41258 dataset to further explore the immune microenvironment of colorectal polyps. The results showed that M0 macrophages were significantly infiltrated in the colorectal polyps group compared with the normal group, while M2 macrophages were less infiltrated in the polyps group, however, M2 macrophages were increased in the normal mucosa. Indeed, it has been demonstrated that a low level of persistent inflammation exists in colorectal mucosa in patients with adenomas or normal. Inflammation, as measured by neutrophil and monocyte infiltration, is present at a low level even in normal colorectal mucosa of subjects with no lesion at endoscopy, neutrophils continuously accumulate within the intestinal mucosa38–40. Bowel inflammation is associated with mucosal epithelial injury, when confronted with persistent irritants, activated macrophages move around and swallow the agents and then release IL-17 to recruit polymorphonuclear cells and other immune cells to participate in agent elimination, ultimately resulting in a microenvironment composed of necrotic tissue, immune cells, stromal cells, and cytokines41. Typically, three major macrophage subpopulations coexist in human tissues, naive macrophages, known as M0, pro-inflammatory macrophages, referred to as M1, and anti-inflammatory M2 macrophages. Naive macrophages display phagocytic functions, recognize pathogens, and rapidly polarize into pro- or anti-inflammatory macrophages to acquire their full set of functions. Anti-inflammatory M2 macrophages, generated by various signals including IL-4, IL-13, IL-10, and glucocorticoid hormones, are involved in resolution of inflammation, phagocytosis of cellular debris, and tissue repair after injury, promote angiogenesis, and release cytokines42,43. Macrophages remove apoptotic neutrophils, whose phagocytosis can lead to a switch to a more reparative M2 macrophage phenotype and resolution of the inflammatory phase of wound healing44. Combined with the results of immune infiltration analysis, it is speculated that the ability of macrophage cells in colorectal polyps to phagocytize pathogenic factors is active, while the ability of phagocytic cell debris and tissue repair after injury is weakened compared with normal intestinal mucosa. However, the increase of M2 infiltration of normal intestinal mucosa was conducive to maintaining the normal morphology of intestinal mucosa. In addition, the results of immune infiltration analysis also showed that regulatory T cells (Tregs) were significantly infiltrated in the colorectal polyps group, which was confirmed by the study of Akeus P et al. In their study, the APCMin/+ mouse model of intestinal adenomatous polyposis was used to investigate tumor-associated lymphocyte subsets and the mechanisms of their accumulation in gastrointestinal adenomas. The result showed that the Treg accumulation was also observed in human adenomatous polyps. T cell infiltration in colorectal adenocarcinoma, particularly T helper 1 (Th1) type T cells as well as increased regulatory T cell (Treg) frequencies, is correlated with improved prognosis. The data indicate that the balance of lymphocyte-recruiting chemokines was altered early in tumor development, possibly contributing to the observed shift toward higher frequencies of Treg45. To investigate the differences between ACF (Aberrant crypt foci, the earliest morphologically identifiable precancerous lesion in the human colon)46 and adjacent normal mucosa, DGE was performed on genes from the stromal compartment. Six upregulated cytokine/chemokine genes, including CCL22 were found in the stroma of ACF compared to normal mucosa. Upregulated CCL22 is a macrophage-derived chemokine that binds to CCR4 and can promote Treg recruitment to cancer tissue47. In addition, the immune contexture might have potential implications for therapy in polyps, Yang et al. performed studies of wild-type mice, mice with conditional disruption of Tbk1 (Tbk1IEC-KO), Tbk1IEC-KOmice crossed with ApcMin/+mice, and Mt-/-mice crossed with ApcMin/+mice. Some mice were given intraperitoneal injections of a neutralizing antibody against interleukin 17 (IL17) or IL1β.they found administration of an antibody against IL17 reduced the number of intestinal polyps in ApcMin/+Tbk1IEC-KOmice, and significantly increased the Th17 cells in lamina propria48. While Chung et al. found that mouse model of spontaneous gastrointestinal polyposis, that site-specific uptake of orally administered IL10 microparticles ameliorates local and systemic disease to improve survival. Mechanistic studies showed that the therapeutic benefit of this treatment was due to neutralisation of disease-promoting FoxP3(+)RoRγt(+)IL17(+) pathogenic T regulatory cells (pgTreg), with concomitant restoration of FoxP3(+)RoRγt(-)IL17(-) conventional T regulatory cells (Treg)49. The above studies suggest that Th17 and Treg cells play an important role in the influence of the immune milieu on polyp treatment.

Colorectal polyps, precursors to colorectal cancer, are usually asymptomatic and develop insidiously. Examinations include flexible sigmoidoscopy, colonoscopy, CT colonography, and double-contrast barium enema. Colonoscopy, the “gold standard”, allows direct observation, precise localization, and sampling for histologic evaluation. However, it usually requires an appointment, bowel preparation, and is costly. In addition, large polyps shed cells containing altered DNA into the large bowel, these gene mutations can be detected in stool samples analyzed by DNA testing. Although only a one-time collection is necessary, adequate evaluation requires the entire stool specimen (30 g minimum), while it is expensive, the appropriate time interval for repeat testing is uncertain, and the potential for cancer prevention is limited because of poor sensitivity in the detecting of colorectal adenomas7. Yet for colorectal polyps, there were no ideal biomarkers, this study based on the GEO data, by machine learning algorithms, three candidate genes as biomarkers for non-invasive diagnosis of colorectal polyps were selected. FOXO proteins belong to a large family of forkhead box (Fox) transcription factors, they share a highly conserved 100 amino acid DNA-binding domain called the forkhead domain. There are four types of human FOXO proteins, FOXO1, FOXO3, FOXO4, and FOXO6, which have been implicated in a variety of physiological processes and pathological conditions, including cardiovascular disorders, cancer, diabetes, and chronic neurological disorders50. The important role of FOXO1 in maintaining intestinal mucosal barrier function has been confirmed in several studies. Foxo1 deletion in the mouse intestine results in decreased mucus secretion by goblet cells. Altered mucus secretion leads to dysbiosis (altered microbiome) and perturbation of microbial metabolites, which appears to be responsible for the increased permeability of the intestinal barrier in Foxo1 null mice51. Research by Chen et al. showed that loss of Foxo1 in intestinal epithelial cells leads to defects in goblet cell autophagy and mucus secretion, leading to an impaired gut microenvironment and dysbiosis, altered organization of tight junction proteins, and increased susceptibility to intestinal inflammation52. In this study, the expression of FOXO1 was decreased both in the discovery and validation colorectal polyps datasets, suggesting that the decreased expression of FOXO1 may affect the mucus secretion of goblet cells to alter the intestinal mucosal barrier function and promote the occurrence and development of polyps. In another study, in human colon cancer and xenograft mouse models53, FoxO1 was found to have anti-tumor activity associated with autophagy. LPA1 is the first high-affinity receptor identified for LPA. The mammalian LPAR1 gene (human chromosomal locus 9q31.3) encodes an approximately 41-kDa protein consisting of 364 amino acids with 7 putative transmembrane domains. LPA1 binds to and activates three types of G proteins: Gαi/o, Gαq/11, and Gα12/13. LPA1 activation induces a range of cellular responses: cell proliferation and survival, cell migration, and cytoskeletal changes54. The targeted disruption of Lpar1 in mice revealed unanticipated in vivo functions of this receptor. Lpar1−/− mice show 50% perinatal lethality in a mixed (C57Bl/6J x 129) genetic background55. Study has also implicated LPA in the progression of gastrointestinal cancers. LPA stimulates proliferation, migration, and invasion primarily through the activation of LPA1 and LPA2 56. S100P belongs to the S100 protein family, which is a group of small acidic polypeptides (10–12 kDa) that are involved in cellular migration and invasion57. Treating SW480 cells with S100P resulted in increased proliferation and migration. Addition of exogenous S100P stimulated both ERK1/2 phosphorylation and NF-κB activity58. S100P has recently been found to be overexpressed in a wide variety of cancers and has been considered as a potential target for cancer therapy59. Anupama Chandramouli et al. showed that S100P mRNA levels were elevated in 14/17 (82%) colon tumor tissues compared to paired adjacent normal colon tissues by real-time RT-PCR, RNAi-mediated knockdown of S100P impaired invadopodia formation. They also found that S100P expression is regulated by PGE2 / EP4 receptor signaling and may be involved in feedback signaling that perpetuates tumor cell growth and migration60. However, the diagnostic power of these three biomarkers in populations with MAFLD and colorectal polyps, and even their role in predicting the likelihood of developing colorectal polyps in populations with MAFLD, needs to be further validated by collecting more samples.

Subsequently, drug target prediction was performed using CMap (https://clue.io/) according to the up-regulated genes from the DEG analysis of the NASH and colorectal polyp groups. A total of 9 small molecule drug targets were considered as potential therapeutic targets for NASH and colorectal polyps. These were PD-0325901, Mirin, Doxapram, Veratridine, XMD-892, Rimaexolone, Ranitidine, Fluorometholone and 3-matida. In patients with NAFLD/MAFLD, lifestyle modification to achieve weight loss remains the first-line intervention61. However, the impact of lifestyle modification on the reversal of liver fibrosis and injury in NASH/MASH is limited62. Current therapeutic targets for NASH/MASH are mainly focused on agonists for nuclear receptors involved in steatosis, inflammation or fibrogenesis, such as farnesoid X receptor (FXR), thyroid hormone receptor-β (TRβ), and peroxisome proliferatoractivated receptor α (PPARα), as well as analogs of enterohepatic hormones, including glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) and fibroblast growth factor 19 (FGF19) and 21 (FGF21)63. Polypectomy is currently the optimal treatment for preneoplastic colorectal lesions7. However, several chemoprevention studies have also been conducted, study showed that low-dose aspirin reduced the risk of adenoma10. Statins were also effective in preventing the development and progression of adenomatous polyps64. Calcium reduced the carcinogenic effects of secondary bile acids, ionized fatty acids and heme iron on the colorectal mucosa by precipitating them in the intestinal lumen. Binding of calcium to calcium-sensing receptors on intestinal epithelial cells resulted in activation of diverse intracellular signaling pathways, inhibition of proliferation, and induction of differentiation and apoptosis65. Many of the newest chemopreventive agents for colorectal neoplasia have been or are being tested. The most promising agents were difluoromethylornithine (DFMO) and erlotinib10. DFMO is an irreversible inhibitor of ornithine decarboxylase, and an RCT study found that DFMO significantly reduced the recurrence of adenomas in individuals with prior adenomas66, Erlotinib is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor that blocks epidermal growth factor receptors. In one secondary analysis of the FAP (Familial adenomatous polyposis) Erlotinib-Sulindac Trial, a phase 2 randomized placebo-controlled trial of sulindac and erlotinib in 82 patients with FAP, treatment with sulindac plus erlotinib was associated with a 69% reduction in colorectal polyp burden in patients with FAP67.

PD0325901, a non-ATP-competing MEK inhibitor, was a potent inhibitor of ERK signaling and tumor cell proliferation68. Its anticancer activity has been demonstrated in both basic and clinical studies69–71. Mirin inhibits Mre11-associated exonuclease activity and prevents MRN-dependent activation of ATM without affecting ATM protein kinase activity. Mirin abolishes the G2/M checkpoint and homology-dependent repair in mammalian cells, consistent with its ability to target the MRN complex72. Doxapram has been identified as a potent inhibitor of the TASK-1 channel, used to aid breathing recovery73. Veratridine is a lipid soluble neurotoxin from Liliaceae plants. It has been extensively studied for its action as a sodium channel agonist74. XMD8-92 is a novel, potent and highly selective dual inhibitor of BMK1/ERK5 (large mitogen-activated protein kinase 1/extracellular signal-regulated kinase) with potential anti-tumor activity75, ERK5 inhibition using XMD8-92 was shown to enhance the antitumor effects of 5-FU in a murine subcutaneous xenograft model. XMD8-92 significantly attenuated the inhibitory effect of hydroxysafflower yellow A (HSYA) on the activation of hepatic stellate cells (HSC) and blocked the HSYA-mediated downregulation of MEF2C76. Rimexolone is a new corticosteroid that may provide good anti-inflammatory efficacy with a greatly reduced risk for elevation of intraocular pressure77. Ranitidine, a potent histamine H2 receptor antagonist, reduces gastric acid and gastric enzyme activity78. Fluorometholone is a synthetic glucocorticoid that acts as a glucocorticoid receptor agonist and has anti-inflammatory and anti-allergic properties79. 3-MATIDA is a metabolic glutamate 1 (mGlu1) receptor antagonist that can attenuate neuronal death in models of cerebral ischemic injury80,81.

NAFLD/MAFLD has become a major chronic liver disease worldwide with the gradual control of infectious liver diseases such as hepatitis B and hepatitis C. Attention should be given to the diagnosis and management of extrahepatic comorbidities. The aim of this study was to explore the candidate biomarkers and treatment of colorectal polyps and NASH/MASH. It will provide valuable insights for future research and clinical applications and contribute to a deeper understanding of the pathogenesis between the two diseases.

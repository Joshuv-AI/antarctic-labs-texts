On BV 𝔸 \mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}} -minimisers in two dimensions
Ferdinand Eitler, Peter Lewintan

Abstract
. We investigate into the regularity of BV 𝔸 \mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}} -minimisers for ℂ \mathbb{C} -elliptic differential operators 𝔸 \mathbb{A} in 2 2 dimensions. Our studies strongly rely on the special structure of such differential operators. The gradient integrability is established for the sharp ellipticity range known from the (symmetric) gradient case.

35B65, 35J60, 49J45, 35A15

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview of the problem.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
be an open and bounded Lipschitz domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
. The goal of this paper is to give a contribution to the regularity theory for relaxed minimisers of

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[u;\Omega]=\int_{\Omega}f(\mathbb{A}u)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V,

where
\mathbb{A}
is a
\ell
-th order, linear, constant-coefficient, homogeneous differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
between two real finite dimensional vector spaces
V
and
W
, i.e.

(
1.1
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}u\coloneqq\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=\ell}\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}u,\quad u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V,

with linear maps
\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}:V\to W
and multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
. Moreover,
f\colon W\to\mathbb{R}
is an integrand which will be specified later. In case of the full or symmetric gradient, i.e.
\mathbb{A}u=\mathrm{D}u
or
\mathbb{A}u=\operatorname{sym}(\mathrm{D}u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{D}u+\mathrm{D}u^{\top})
respectively, such functionals appear in the continuum description for modelling elastic and plastic effects for solids and fluids, where
u
is then the corresponding displacement or velocity field, cf.
[
15
]
. Usually, plasticity is described by means of linear growth densities
f\colon W\to\mathbb{R}
, meaning that there exist two constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

(
1.2
)

\displaystyle c_{1}\left\lvert P\right\rvert\leq f(P)\leq c_{2}(1+\left\lvert P\right\rvert)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad P\in W.

This growth condition guarantees that
\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]
is well-defined on the space

\displaystyle\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{v\in\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V):\mathbb{A}v\in\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;W)\},

being the natural generalisation of
\operatorname{W}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We can equip
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)
with the canonical norm
\|u\|_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\|u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)}+\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;W)}
, turning it into a Banach space. Of course there holds
\operatorname{W}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})=\operatorname{W}^{\mathrm{D},1}(\Omega)
with
V=\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
W=\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
in the full gradient case. As the space
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)
is not reflexive, we cannot expect to have existence of minimisers using the direct method of the Calculus of Variations. Thus, a relaxation to the space
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
of functions of bounded
\mathbb{A}
-variation is necessary to ensure existence of minimisers. The latter function space is defined by

\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{v\in\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V):\mathbb{A}v\in\mathrm{RM}_{\rm fin}(\Omega;W)\},

where
\mathrm{RM}_{\rm fin}(\Omega;W)
denotes the space of
W
-valued finite Radon measures, cf.
[
10
]
.
Another drawback compared to the superlinear growth case
1<p<\infty
is, that there exists
no
constant
c=c(\mathbb{A})>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\Lin(\mathbb{R}^{n};V))}\leq c\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;W)}

holds for all
u\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;V)
which is known under the name
Ornstein-type non-inequality
in the literature, cf.
[
25
, Thm. 1.3]
. Therefore, we have the strict inclusions
\operatorname{W}^{1,1}(\Omega;V)\subsetneq\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;V)\subsetneq\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
, where
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;V)=\mathrm{BV}^{\mathrm{D}}(\Omega)
is the usual space of functions of bounded variation. Since the full distributional derivatives of
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-functions do not belong to
\mathrm{RM}_{\rm fin}(\Omega;\Lin(\mathbb{R}^{n};V))
, we cannot apply known results for linear growth functionals involving the full gradient. Hence, we are interested in the question under which conditions on the integrand
f
we have
\mathrm{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;V)
or even
\operatorname{W}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;V)
regularity for relaxed minimisers. In particular, we are seeking for a parallel regularity theory to what is known in the full or symmetric gradient case for linear growth functionals. To state our main result we next introduce the precise framework.

1.2.
Existence results for relaxed minimisers

Throughout this paper we assume that the Dirichlet datum satisfies
u_{0}\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)
. As usual, we define
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}_{0}(\Omega)
to be the closure of the test-functions
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;V)
with respect to the
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)}
-norm. Apparently, a crucial assumption for the differential operator
\mathbb{A}
of the form (
1.1
) is the notion of
\mathbb{C}
-ellipticity. Namely, if we associate the corresponding Fourier symbol map

\mathbb{A}[\xi]=\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=\ell}\xi^{\alpha}\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}:V\to W,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

to
\mathbb{A}
, we call the operator
\mathbb{A}

elliptic
if

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}:\qquad\mathbb{A}[\xi]:V\to W\text{ is injective},

and
\mathbb{C}
-
elliptic
if

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\}:\qquad\mathbb{A}[\xi]:V+\mathrm{i}V\to W+\mathrm{i}W\text{ is injective}.

It turns out that
\mathbb{C}
-ellipticity is equivalent to the fact of having a trace operator, cf.
[
10
]
as well as (compact) embeddings, cf.
[
21
]
. Moreover, since
\mathbb{C}
-ellipticity is equivalent to having a finite dimensional null-space, cf.
[
10
, Thm. 2.6]
it follows from
[
22
,
28
]
that
u\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
is also
\operatorname{L}^{p}
-differentiable, which will be important to have the decomposition (
1.6
) below. The following characterisation of
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operators is essentially due to
Smith

[
32
]
but we also refer to
Kałamajska

[
24
]
and
Gmeineder
et al.
[
23
, Prop. 3.2]
:

Lemma 1.1
.

\mathbb{A}
is
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic if and only if there exists another linear, constant-coefficient, homogeneous differential operator
\mathbb{L}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a number
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathrm{D}^{d}=\mathbb{L}\circ\mathbb{A}
.

In the following, we focus on the case
\ell=1
and
n=2
and consider first-order
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operators
\mathbb{A}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that are induced by an (orthogonal) projection
\mathscr{A}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
(like
\mathscr{A}\in\{\operatorname{sym},\operatorname{dev}\}
denoting the symmetric and deviatoric part map), i.e. the induced differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is given via
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\mathscr{A}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
. In this setting, these operators possess a certain structure and we have by
[
20
, Prop 4.1]
the following decomposition

(
1.3
)

\displaystyle P=\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[P])+\gamma(P)\mathfrak{G}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad P\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},

where
\mathfrak{L}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
and
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}
are linear maps, and
\mathfrak{G}\in\mathrm{GL}(2)
. Furthermore, the integer
d
from Lemma
1.1
can be specified:

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\mathscr{A}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
be a first-order
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
induced by a projection
\mathscr{A}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
. Then there exists a first-order linear constant-coefficient homogeneous differential operator
\mathbb{L}
such that
\mathrm{D}^{2}=\mathbb{L}\circ\mathbb{A}
.

The proof of this lemma will be given below in Subsection
3.4
. From now on, we consider the variational principle

\displaystyle\mbox{to minimise}\quad\mathscr{F}[u;\Omega]\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}f(\mathbb{A}u)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\mbox{over}\quad\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}\coloneqq u_{0}+\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}_{0}(\Omega),

where
f\colon W\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex integrand satisfying the linear growth condition (
1.2
) and
\mathbb{A}=\mathscr{A}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
is a
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operator induced by a projection
\mathscr{A}
as above. Recalling the discussion in
[
10
, Sec. 5]
, we notice that it is not too restrictive to assume
W=\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}
, where

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\coloneqq\{P\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}:\mathscr{A}[P]=P\}=\mathrm{span}\{v\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\xi\coloneqq\mathscr{A}[v\otimes\xi],v,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}.

To overcome the missing weak compactness in
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)
, we relax the functional
\mathscr{F}
to the space
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
by setting

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[v;\Omega]=\int_{\Omega}f(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v])\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}f^{\infty}\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{A}^{s}v}{\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\mathbb{A}^{s}v\right\rvert}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\mathbb{A}^{s}v\right\rvert

(
1.4
)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}f^{\infty}(\tr(u_{0}-v)\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\nu_{\partial\Omega})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

where
\nu_{\partial\Omega}
denotes the unit outward normal field along
\partial\Omega
and the behaviour of the integrand
f
at infinity is encoded in the
recession function
defined by

(
1.5
)

\displaystyle f^{\infty}(z)\coloneqq\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{f(sz)}{s}\quad\mbox{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}.

Moreover, we have used the Lebesgue-Radon-Nikodým decomposition which allows to split
\mathbb{A}u
into

(
1.6
)

\mathbb{A}u=\mathbb{A}^{a}u+\mathbb{A}^{s}u=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{A}^{a}u}{\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}}\mathscr{L}^{n}+\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{A}^{s}u}{\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\mathbb{A}^{s}u\right\rvert}\left\lvert\mathbb{A}^{s}u\right\rvert=\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u]\mathscr{L}^{n}+\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{A}^{s}u}{\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\mathbb{A}^{s}u\right\rvert}\left\lvert\mathbb{A}^{s}u\right\rvert,

where the density of
\mathbb{A}^{s}u
with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{n}
can be identified with
\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u]
, which is the part map of the approximate gradient
\mathrm{D}u
.
For the precise definition of all building blocks of the above formula we refer to
Section

2
. Next we introduce the notion of minimality, which we will use in the sequel:

Definition 1.3

(
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
- and local
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimisers)
.

(a)

Given
u_{0}\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
, a map
u\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
is called
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser with respect to the Dirichlet datum
u_{0}
if we have

\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[u;\Omega]\leq\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[w;\Omega]
for all
w\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
.

(b)

A map
u\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is called
local
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser
if we have
\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u}[u;U]\leq\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u}[w;U]
for all
U\Subset\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial U
and all
w\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(U)
.

We note, that it follows from the definition, that any
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser is also a local
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser. Under our assumptions, it is shown in
[
18
, Thm. 5.3]
or
[
34
, Thm. 3.3]
that there exists a
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser
u
, which is the weak
∗
-limit of a minimising sequence in
\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}
without having a relaxation gap, i.e.

(
1.7
)

\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]=\min_{\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)}\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[-;\Omega]=\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[u;\Omega].

Moreover, since the recession function is positively
1
-homogeneous and hence, not strictly convex, it turns out, that the whole functional
\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[-;\Omega]
is not strictly convex on
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}_{(\mathrm{loc})}(\Omega)
. As a consequence, (local)
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser may be non-unique in general.

In order to conclude Sobolev regularity we have to impose a suitable ellipticity condition on
f
, namely we consider the notion of
\mu
-ellipticity described in the upcoming subsection.

1.3.
The scale of
\mu
-elliptic variational integrands and previous results.

In the context of convex linear growth integrands it is common to consider
\mu
-elliptic variational integrands. More precisely, given
1<\mu<\infty
, we say that some
\operatorname{C}^{2}
-integrand
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-elliptic
if there are constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
such that

(
1.8
)

\displaystyle\lambda\,\frac{\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}}{(1+\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{2})^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}\leq\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f(P)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda\,\frac{\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}}{(1+\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{2})}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad P,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}.

We briefly notice, that the condition
\mu>1
is necessary, since otherwise the integrand is no longer of linear growth, cf.
[
3
, Rem. 4.14]
for more details. In case of the full gradient, i.e. the framework of
\mathrm{BV}
-spaces,
Bildhauer
proved in all dimensions that at least one
\mathrm{BV}
-minimiser is of class
\operatorname{W}^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\operatorname{W}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
p>1
if
1<\mu+\frac{2}{n}
. The
\operatorname{W}^{1,1}
-regularity remains true for
1+\frac{2}{n}\leq\mu\leq 3
under the additional assumption of local boundedness, which can be justified by means of maximum principles or Moser-type iterations. We refer to
[
6
]
for the precise statements and more details. In
[
4
]
,
Beck
and
Schmidt
extended the above Sobolev regularity result to
all

\mathrm{BV}
-minimisers using the Ekeland variational principle, recalling that relaxed minimisers may be non-unique. We briefly indicate that so far there is no result for the autonomous Dirichlet problem available dealing with the case
\mu>3
. However, we mention
[
2
]
for a result concerning the Neumann problem as well as
[
7
]
for a counter-example for non-autonomous variational integrals in case
\mu>3
. Let us also mention
[
8
]
dealing with two-dimensional radially symmetric variational integrals as well as
[
16
]
for a result concerning non-autonomous integrands in the full gradient case. Finally, we note that
\mu
-elliptic integrands show a similar behaviour to one of
(p,q)
-growth and we refer to the discussion in
[
3
]
and
[
27
]
for a detailed overview including many additional references.

The first contribution in direction of the symmetric gradient can be found in
[
19
]
, proving higher integrability and Sobolev regularity for a non-optimal range
1<\mu<1+\frac{1}{n}
by means of fractional methods. The same result was later extended by
Gmeineder
in
[
18
]
to the range
1<\mu<1+\frac{2}{n}
known from the full gradient case, using techniques which heavily exploit the specific structure of the symmetric gradient. Finally, assuming additional local boundedness for the minimiser, it is shown in
[
3
]
that
\operatorname{W}^{1,1}
-regularity holds for the full range
1<\mu\leq 3
, hence giving a picture to that in the
\mathrm{BV}
-case. Again, the specific structure of the symmetric gradient plays a key role in the proof.

So far the only result treating more general
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic operators was given by
Wozniak
in
[
34
]
, generalising the higher integrability and Sobolev regularity for the non-optimal range
1<\mu<1+\frac{1}{n}
, exploiting that the fractional techniques are more flexible. At the present stage it seems hopeless to transfer the methods from
[
18
]
and
[
3
]
to the general
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic case, since the structure of the differential operator plays a major role. However, in two dimensions all
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic operators induced by a projection
\mathscr{A}
share the main structural condition (
1.3
), which allows to generalise the main result of
[
18
]
for the range
1<\mu<1+\frac{2}{n}
. This will be the main theorem of our paper and reads as follows:

Theorem 1.4

(Gradient integrability)
.

Let
u_{0}\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)
and
f\in\operatorname{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}})
be a variational integrand satisfying (
1.2
) and (
1.8
) for
\mu\in(1,2)
. Then every local
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser
u
of
\mathscr{F}
is of class
\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\operatorname{W}^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
. More precisely, for every subset
U\Subset\Omega
there exists a constant
c=c(\Lambda,\mu,\mathbb{A})>0
such that whenever
\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})\Subset U
we have

(
1.9
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{\exp\operatorname{L}^{\frac{2-\mu}{3-\mu}}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq c\left(\bigg(1+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\bigg)\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\bigg)\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u\right\rvert(\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{2r}(x_{0}))+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\frac{\left\lvert u\right\rvert}{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2-\mu}}+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{\left\lvert u\right\rvert}{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\right).\end{split}

Remark 1.5
.

The arguments we use in our proof also show that this statement holds true in
all
dimensions for the part map
\mathscr{A}=\operatorname{dev}
, cf. Remarks
3.7
and
3.8
.

At this stage, we briefly comment on the proof of our main theorem (
1.4
). In order to overcome the possible non-uniqueness we will adapt the vanishing viscosity method in conjunction with the Ekeland variational principle. We start with an arbitrary
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimising, whose approximation is a minimising sequence for the original functional. Since this sequence does not satisfy the corresponding Euler-Lagrange inequality, we add an additional regularising term to our functional and apply the Ekeland variational principle in order to derive a minimising sequence of almost-minimisers
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
close to the original sequence. The latter comes naturally with an Euler-Lagrange inequality, which turns out to be sufficient to derive weighted second-order estimates, which will be the key ingredient for showing gradient integrability. In order to derive these bounds, we apply the Ekeland variational principle in the space
\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}
, which may be surprising at first glimpse: Let us recall that for the symmetric gradient there holds

\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u_{k}=\partial_{i}\operatorname{sym}\mathrm{D}u^{(jk)}-\partial_{k}\operatorname{sym}\mathrm{D}u^{(ij)}+\partial_{j}\operatorname{sym}\mathrm{D}u^{(ik)}.

for all
u\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. By
1.2
such a representation remains valid in our situation. In order to obtain the weighted estimates, we have to test the Euler-Lagrange inequality in a differentiated version by second-order quantities behaving approximately like
\Delta v_{j}
. At this stage the usage of
\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}
as proposed in
[
4
]
would destroy any estimates, since we only have

\|\Delta v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}}\lesssim\|\mathrm{D}v_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}.

The right-hand side of the latter cannot be controlled because of Ornstein’s Non-Inequality. However, as in the case of the symmetric gradient, cf.
[
18
,
3
]
, the space
\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}
turns out to be sufficient, because in this case we have

\|\Delta v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}}\lesssim\|v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}}.

Moreover, the explicit structure of the operator is
essential
, which already appeared in the framework of the symmetric gradient. Let us emphasise that the decomposition (
1.3
) is the main building block in order to conclude the weighted second-order estimates. Without the latter, it currently appears hopeless to apply the described method to obtain a similar result that includes
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operators in all dimensions.

2.
Preliminaries

We mainly use standard notation. We equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
with the usual Euclidean or Frobenius norm, respectively and denote both by
\left\lvert\,\cdot\,\right\rvert
. The latter is induced by the matrix inner product
\langle A,B\rangle\coloneqq A:B\coloneqq\tr(A^{\top}B)
for
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
.
Moreover, we write
\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\left\lvert x-x_{0}\right\rvert<r\}
for an open ball with radius
r>0
centred at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
\mathscr{L}^{n}
and
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
indicate the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue and
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, respectively and we simply write
\,\mathrm{d}x
, when integrating with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{n}
. Moreover we set
\omega_{n}\coloneqq\mathscr{L}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{1}(0))
. For a (Lebesgue) measurable set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0<\mathscr{L}^{n}(U)<\infty
and
f\in\operatorname{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{m})
we denote the average by

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{U}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(U)}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Furthermore,
V
and
W
are two finite dimensional normed spaces and with some abuse of notation we will denote both norms by
\left\lvert\,\cdot\,\right\rvert
, as it is clear from the context. We use the notation
a\otimes b=ab^{\top}
for the usual tensor product of two vectors
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Finally,
c>0
denotes a generic constant, whose value may change from line to line and whose dependencies are usually indicated, whereas the precise value is irrelevant. Finally, we state two elementary estimates for linear growth integrands, which we will use in the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
17
, Lem. 5.2]
,
[
4
, Lem. 2.8]
)
.

Suppose that
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex
\operatorname{C}^{1}
-function which satisfies the linear growth condition (
1.2
). Then we have the following statements:

(a)

For all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}
there holds
\left\lvert\mathrm{D}f(z)\right\rvert\leq c_{2}
and in particular
\mathrm{Lip}(f)\leq c_{2}
.

(b)

For all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}
there holds
\langle\mathrm{D}f(z),z\rangle\geq c_{1}\left\lvert z\right\rvert-c_{2}
.

2.1.
Function spaces and related topics

For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
\operatorname{L}^{p}(\Omega)
and
\operatorname{W}^{k,p}(\Omega)
the Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, cf. e.g.
[
17
]
. Without further comment we also use
V
-valued versions of these spaces. For a finite Radon measure
\mu\in\mathrm{RM}_{\rm fin}(\Omega;V)
we denote by
\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert
its total variation. Moreover, we have the Lebesgue-Radon-Nikodým decomposition

(
2.1
)

\mu=\mu^{a}+\mu^{s}=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{a}}{\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}}\mathscr{L}^{n}+\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{s}}{\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\mu^{s}\right\rvert}\left\lvert\mu^{s}\right\rvert,

into absolutely continuous and singular part with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{n}
.

2.1.1.
Functions of bounded
\mathbb{A}
-variation

As already mentioned above, a function of bounded
\mathbb{A}
-variation, is a function
u\in\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)
such that
\mathbb{A}u
is a finite Radon-measure. We notice, that a linear, homogeneous, first-order differential operator
\mathbb{A}
can be also characterised by its part map
\mathscr{A}\in\Lin(V\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n};W)
via
\mathbb{A}u=\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u]
.
In order to introduce a norm on
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
, we define the
dual
or
formally adjoint operator

\mathbb{A}^{\ast}
as the differential operator
\mathbb{A}^{\ast}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
from
W
to
V
given by

\mathbb{A}^{\ast}v\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}^{\ast}\partial_{\alpha}v,\quad v\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to W,

where
\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}^{\ast}
denotes the adjoint map of
\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}
. This allows us to introduce the
total
\mathbb{A}
-variation
of
u\in\operatorname{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;V)
by

|\mathbb{A}u|(\Omega)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\langle u,\mathbb{A}^{\ast}\varphi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\colon\varphi\in\operatorname{C}^{1}_{c}(\Omega;W),\,\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\},

which allows us to characterise the space
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
equivalently as the set of functions
u
having finite
\mathbb{A}
-variation.
The local variant
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is defined in the obvious manner. Furthermore, the space
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
is a Banach space with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\|u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)}+\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u\right\rvert(\Omega).

Similar to the
\mathrm{BV}
-setting there are three different notions of convergence available on
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
. Namely, given
v\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
and a sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
we say that
v_{j}
converges to
v
as
j\to\infty

•

in the
weak
∗
-sense
, in symbols
v_{j}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}v
, if
v_{j}\to v
strongly in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)
and
\mathbb{A}v_{j}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbb{A}v
in the sense of
W
-valued Radon measures.

•

in the
\mathbb{A}
-strict sense
, if
v_{j}\to v
strongly in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)
and
\left\lvert\mathbb{A}v_{j}\right\rvert(\Omega)\to\left\lvert\mathbb{A}v\right\rvert(\Omega)
as
j\to\infty
.

•

in the
\mathbb{A}
-area-strict sense
if
v_{j}\to v
strongly in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)
and
\langle\mathbb{A}v\rangle(\Omega)\to\langle\mathbb{A}v\rangle(\Omega)
as
j\to\infty
, where we have abbreviated

\langle\mathbb{A}v\rangle(\Omega)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{1+\left\lvert\mathbb{A}v\right\rvert^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\left\lvert\mathbb{A}^{s}v\right\rvert(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for}\quad u\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega).

In the last formula we have used the Lebesgue-Radon-Nikodým decomposition (
1.6
).

2.1.2.
Orlicz-Sobolev-spaces

In order to derive estimates for the full gradient from the one for
\mathbb{A}u
as needed in (
1.9
), we use a suitable Korn-type inequality, cf.
Section

3.4
. Towards this aim, let
\Phi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Young-function, meaning

\Phi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\geq 0,

where
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is a non-decreasing, left-continuous function, which is neither identical to
0
nor
\infty
. We then define the
Lebesgue-Orlicz space
as the space

\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;V)\coloneqq\{u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ measurable }\colon\|u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;V)}<\infty\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;V)}
denotes the
Luxemburg norm
given by

\|u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;V)}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\frac{\left\lvert u\right\rvert}{\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\right\}.

We abbreviate
\exp\operatorname{L}^{\beta}(\Omega;V)\coloneqq\operatorname{L}^{\Phi_{\beta}}(\Omega;V)
for the Orlicz-space corresponding to
\Phi_{\beta}(t)\coloneqq\exp(t^{\beta})
with
t\geq 0
and
\beta>0
. Moreover, we define the
Orlicz-Sobolev space

\operatorname{W}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega;V)
as

\operatorname{W}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega;V)\coloneqq\{u\in\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;V)\colon u\mbox{ is weakly differentiable and }\mathrm{D}u\in\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;\Lin(\mathbb{R}^{n};V))\},

which is a Banach space when equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{\operatorname{W}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega;V)}\coloneqq\|u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;V)}+\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;\Lin(\mathbb{R}^{n},V))}.

2.1.3.
Negative Sobolev spaces

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define the
negative Sobolev space

\operatorname{W}^{-k,1}(\Omega;V)
as the set of all
V
-valued distributions
T\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega;V)
which admit the representation

T=\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq k}\partial^{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{with}\quad T_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V),\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq k.

This space becomes a Banach space when endowed with the norm

\|T\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-k,1}(\Omega;V)}\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq k}\|T_{\alpha}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)}:T=\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq k}\partial^{\alpha}T_{\alpha},\hskip 2.84544ptT_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;V)\bigg\}.

As a consequence of
[
18
, Lem. 2.5]
(cf. also
[
3
, Sec. 3.2.3]
), we recall for
s\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
h>0
the estimates

(
2.2
)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}w\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|w\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|w\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(
2.3
)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}w\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|w\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(
2.4
)

\displaystyle\|\triangle_{s,h}w\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}(\Omega_{h};\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|w\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

where
\Omega_{h}\coloneqq\{x\in\Omega:\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>h\}
and
\triangle_{s,h}w
denotes the finite difference quotient, i.e.

\triangle_{s,h}w\coloneqq\frac{w(x+he_{s})-w(x)}{h},\quad x\in\Omega_{h}.

2.2.
(Lower semi-)continuity results

Since the distributions
\mathbb{A}u
are merely Radon measures we need lower semi-continuity results for functionals defined on measures. In this way, we define based on the Lebesgue-Radon-Nikodým decomposition (
2.1
), another measure
f(\mu)
through

(
2.5
)

f(\mu)(U)\coloneqq\int_{U}f\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{a}}{\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{s}}{\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\mu^{s}\right\rvert}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\left\lvert\mu^{s}\right\rvert\quad\mbox{for Borel subsets }U\subset\Omega.

We notice, that if
f
is of linear growth in the sense of (
1.2
), then the recession function
f^{\infty}
of
f
defined through (
1.5
) is a well-defined, 1-homogeneous, lower semi-continuous and convex function taking finite values. The last condition can no longer be guaranteed, if one drops linear growth assumption from above. The main (lower semi-)continuity result for functionals of the form (
2.5
) is due to
Reshetnyak
, which we state in the following tailor-made version:

Theorem 2.2

(Reshetnyak (lower semi-)continuity theorem,
[
29
]
,
[
4
, Thm. 2.4]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function of linear growth from below. For functions
u,u_{1},u_{2},\dots\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
we have

(a)

if
u_{j}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}u
in the weak
∗
-sense in
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
then

f(\mathbb{A}u)(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}f(\mathbb{A}u_{j})(\Omega),

(b)

and if
u_{j}\to u
in the
\mathbb{A}
-area-strict sense in
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
then

f(\mathbb{A}u)(\Omega)=\lim_{j\to\infty}f(\mathbb{A}u_{j})(\Omega).

In particular, recalling (
1.6
), we infer that the relaxed functionals (
1.2
) are lower semi-continuous with respect to the weak
∗
-convergence as well as continuous with respect to the
\mathbb{A}
-area-strict convergence, if
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex integrand of linear growth in the sense of (
1.2
).

Finally, we state a customised version of
[
18
, Lem. 2.6]
, which will be necessary when applying the Ekeland variational principle
2.4
later on.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open and bounded set with Lipschitz boundary,
1<q<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, assume that
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a convex function that satisfies
\tfrac{1}{c}\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{q}\leq\mathfrak{f}(P)\leq c(1+\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{q})
for some
c>0
and all
P\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Then for every
u_{0}\in\operatorname{W}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the functional

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}[u]\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{f}(\mathbb{A}u)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad&\mbox{if }u\in\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}\coloneqq u_{0}+\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\\
+\infty\quad&\mbox{if }u\in\operatorname{W}^{-k,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}\end{cases}

is lower semi-continuous with respect to the norm topology on
\operatorname{W}^{-k,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

2.3.
The Ekeland variational principle

In order to overcome possible non-uniqueness phenomena for
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimisers, we use the Ekeland variational principle to construct a suitable minimising sequence. We will need it in the following version:

Proposition 2.4

(Ekeland
[
14
]
,
[
17
, Thm. 5.6, Rem. 5.5]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
F:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be a lower semi-continuous function with respect to the metric topology, which is bounded from below and not identically
+\infty
. Suppose that for some
u\in X
and some
\varepsilon>0
, there holds
F[u]\leq\inf_{X}F+\varepsilon
. Then there exists
v\in X
such that

(a)

d(u,v)\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.

(b)

F[v]\leq F[u]
and

(c)

for all
w\in X
there holds
F[v]\leq F[w]+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,d(v,w)
.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

3.1.
Ekeland approximation procedure

We observe that
1.4
contains a local statement and therefore, whenever
u\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a local
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser of
F
, the restriction
u|_{U}\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
to a relatively compact set
U\Subset\Omega
with Lipsichitz boundary
\partial U
, is a
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimiser with respect to its own boundary values. Hence, it is not too restrictive to assume

u=u_{0}\in\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega).

Although many steps of the approximation are standard, we provide full details for the sake of completeness. The construction is divided into several steps.

Step 1: Construction of a regular minimising sequence subject to smooth boundary values.
Using
[
10
, Proposition 4.24]
we get a smooth sequence
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
u_{0}+\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

(
3.1
)

\displaystyle u_{j}\to u\quad\mbox{in the $\mathbb{A}$-area-strict topology on $\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}$ as }j\to\infty.

As a consequence of
2.2
and the discussion below, the functional
\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[-;\Omega]
is continuous with respect to the
\mathbb{A}
-area-strict topology on
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)
. Therefore, using the consistency relation from (
1.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{F}[u_{j};\Omega]=\lim_{j\to\infty}\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[u_{j};\Omega]=\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[u;\Omega]=\min_{\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}(\Omega)}\overline{\mathscr{F}}_{u_{0}}[-;\Omega]=\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega].

Hence,
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a minimising sequence for
\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]
in
\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}
. Passing to a non-relabeled subsequence if required, we may assume

(
3.2
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[u_{j};\Omega]\leq\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]+\frac{1}{8j^{2}}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\mathbb{A}
is
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic we can apply
[
10
, Theorem 4.17]
to conclude the existence of a continuous trace-operator
\tr\colon\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\operatorname{L}^{1}(\partial\Omega,\mathscr{H}^{n-1})
. Therefore, we can find a compactly-supported extension
\overline{u}_{0}\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
of
u_{0}
, cf. also
[
10
, Corollary 4.21]
.
Next, we choose
\varepsilon_{j}>0
in a way, such that the mollification of
\overline{u}_{0}
with a scaled radial standard mollifier
\varrho_{\varepsilon_{j}}
provides us with a sequence
(u_{j}^{\partial\Omega})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\operatorname{W}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
defined by
u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}\coloneqq(\overline{u}_{0}\ast\varrho_{\varepsilon_{j}})|_{\Omega}
which satisfying

(
3.3
)

\displaystyle\|u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}-u_{0}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{8\mathrm{Lip}(f)j^{2}}.

This allows us to define approximate Dirichlet classes through
\mathscr{D}_{j}\coloneqq u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}+\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Setting
\tilde{u}_{j}\coloneqq u_{j}-u_{0}+u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}\in\mathscr{D}_{j}
, we conclude using (
3.3
) that

(
3.4
)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}_{j}-u_{j}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)}=\|u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}-u_{0}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{8\mathrm{Lip}(f)j^{2}}.

We now show, that the infimum of
\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]
over
\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}
can be approximated by the corresponding one over the Dirichlet class
\mathscr{D}_{j}
, which is almost attained for
\tilde{u}_{j}
. Towards this claim, we observe that

\big|\mathscr{F}[v;\Omega]-\mathscr{F}[w;\Omega]\big|\leq\mathrm{Lip}(f)\|\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v]-\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}w]\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}})},\quad\forall v,w\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\Omega),

and thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[u_{0}+\varphi;\Omega]\leq\mathscr{F}[u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}+\varphi;\Omega]+\mathrm{Lip}(f)\|\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u_{0}]-\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}})}

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}+\varphi;\Omega]\leq\mathscr{F}[u_{0}+\varphi;\Omega]+\mathrm{Lip(f)}\|\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u_{0}]-\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}})}

for every
\varphi\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Taking into account (
3.4
), we can take the infimum on the right-hand side over all
\varphi\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}_{0}(\Omega)
to deduce

(
3.5
)

\displaystyle\bigg|\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]-\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{j}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{8j^{2}}.

Finally, we notice that the choice
\varphi=u_{j}-u_{0}
in combination with (
3.2
) results in

(
3.6
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[\tilde{u}_{j};\Omega]\leq\mathscr{F}[u_{j};\Omega]+\frac{1}{8j^{2}}\leq\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]+\frac{1}{4j^{2}},

and hence, the infimum of
\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]
over
\mathscr{D}_{j}
is almost attained by
\tilde{u}_{j}
. This step will be crucial, when verifying the assumptions of the Ekeland variational principle from
2.4
in the next step.

Step 2: Definition of regularised functionals.
In this part, we implement a vanishing viscosity approximation by adding a quadratic regularisation term. More precisely, for
P\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}
we define

(
3.7
)

\displaystyle f_{j}(P)\coloneqq f(P)+\frac{1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}(1+\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{2})\quad\mbox{with}\quad A_{j}\coloneqq 1+\int_{\Omega}(1+\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}\tilde{u}_{j}]\right\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x,

leading to

(
3.8
)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathrm{D}f_{j}(P)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\mathrm{D}f(P)\right\rvert+\frac{1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}\left\lvert P\right\rvert\overset{\eqref{eq:LinGrowth}}{\leq}c_{2}+\frac{1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}\left\lvert P\right\rvert.

We finally introduce the regularised functionals in terms of
\tilde{u}_{j}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{j}[v;\Omega]=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[v;\Omega]+\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\Omega}(1+\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v]\right\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\quad&\mbox{if}\quad v\in\mathscr{D}_{j}\\
+\infty\quad&\mbox{if}\quad v\in\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\mathscr{D}_{j}.\end{cases}

Step 3: The Ekeland approximation.
As already announced at the end of the first step, (
3.6
) is crucial to verify the assumption of the Ekeland variational principle. Indeed, we obtain that
\tilde{u}_{j}
is
j^{-1}
close to the infimum of
\mathscr{F}_{j}[-;\Omega]
in
\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
because of

(
3.9
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{j}[\tilde{u}_{j};\Omega]\leq\mathscr{F}[\tilde{u}_{j};\Omega]+\frac{1}{2j^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:AlmostAttainment}}{\leq}\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]+\frac{3}{4j^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:InfimaApproximation}}{\leq}\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{j}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]+\frac{1}{j^{2}}\leq\inf_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\mathscr{F}_{j}[-;\Omega]+\frac{1}{j^{2}}.

As
\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a Banach space and
\mathscr{F}_{j}[-;\Omega]
is clearly not identical
\infty
, as well as lower semi-continuous on
\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
2.3
with
k=2
and
\mathfrak{f}=f_{j}
, the Ekeland variational principle from
2.4
is applicable and provides us with a sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of almost-minimisers satisfying

(
3.10
)

\displaystyle\|v_{j}-\tilde{u}_{j}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{j}

(
3.11
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{j}[v_{j};\Omega]

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{F}_{j}[w;\Omega]+\frac{1}{j}\|v_{j}-w\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad w\in\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

We proceed by proving useful properties of the Ekeland-type approxmiation sequence:

Proposition 3.1

(Properties of the Ekeland sequence)
.

Let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Ekeland-type approximiation sequence from above. Then for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have the following estimates:

(
3.12
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\bigg(\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]+\frac{2}{j^{2}}\bigg),

(
3.13
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\Omega}(1+\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{j^{2}}.

Proof.

For arbitrary
j\in\mathbb{N}
we test the almost-minimality condition (
3.11
) with
w=\tilde{u}_{j}
, which results, after exploiting (
3.10
) and (
3.9
), in

(
3.14
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{j}[v_{j};\Omega]\leq\mathscr{F}_{j}[\tilde{u}_{j},\Omega]+\frac{1}{j^{2}}\leq\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]+\frac{2}{j^{2}}.

In particular,
\mathscr{F}_{j}[{v_{j}};\Omega]
is finite and therefore, (
3.12
) follows directly from the lower bound of the linear growth condition (
1.2
). By definition of the functional
\mathscr{F}_{j}
we obtain
v_{j}\in\mathscr{D}_{j}\cap\operatorname{W}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and, using (
3.14
), also

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{j}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]\leq\mathscr{F}[v_{j};\Omega]\leq\mathscr{F}_{j}[v_{j};\Omega]\leq\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}\mathscr{F}[-;\Omega]+\frac{2}{j^{2}}.

In view of (
3.5
) we conclude

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\Omega}(1+\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x=\left(\mathscr{F}_{j}[v_{j};\Omega]-\mathscr{F}[v_{j};\Omega]\right)\leq\frac{9}{8j^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{j^{2}},

which is exactly the second estimate (
3.13
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Introducing the abbreviation
\sigma_{j}\coloneqq\mathrm{D}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])
, we immediately observe
\mathscr{A}[\sigma_{j}]=\sigma_{j}
, since
\mathscr{A}
is a projection on
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}
and
\sigma_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}
.
The key ingredient to derive regularity of all generalised minimisers will be a Euler-Lagrange inequality, following from the almost-minimality condition (
3.11
). More precisely, we have

Theorem 3.2

(Euler-Lagrange inequality)
.

Let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Ekeland-type approximation sequence from
3.1
. Then the following
Euler-Lagrange inequality
holds true for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
:

(
3.15
)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\sigma_{j},\mathrm{D}\varphi\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\sigma_{j},\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}\varphi]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varphi\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\qquad\forall\ \varphi\in\operatorname{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Proof.

The first relation follows in view of

\displaystyle\big\langle\sigma_{j},\mathrm{D}\varphi\big\rangle=\big\langle\mathscr{A}[\sigma_{j}],\mathrm{D}\varphi\big\rangle=\big\langle\sigma_{j},\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}\varphi]\big\rangle.

The remaining part follows essentially the same steps as in
[
18
, (4.13)]
or
[
34
, Lemma 4.4]
. To this end, we consider an arbitrary test function
\varphi\in\operatorname{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which is admissible because of the quadratic growth, cf. (
3.7
). For
\theta>0
we test the almost-minimality condition (
3.11
) with
w=v_{j}\pm\theta\varphi
. Dividing the resulting inequalities by
\theta
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{F}_{j}[v_{j}\pm\theta\varphi;\Omega]-\mathscr{F}_{j}[\varphi;\Omega]}{\theta}\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varphi\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

and passing to the limit
\theta\searrow 0
, we obtain the two one-sided Gâteaux-differentials of the functional
\mathscr{F}_{j}
in directions
\pm\varphi
, which can be computed explicitly. Thus, we can finish the proof:

\lim_{\theta\searrow 0}\frac{\mathscr{F}_{j}[v_{j}\pm\theta\varphi;\Omega]-\mathscr{F}_{j}[\varphi;\Omega]}{\theta}=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\theta}\right|_{\theta=0}\mathscr{F}_{j}[v_{j}\pm\theta\varphi;\Omega]=\pm\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\sigma_{j},\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}\varphi]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x.\qed

3.2.
Non-uniform estimates

In order to justify subsequent computations, we first derive an existence result for second derivatives of
v_{j}
, which is non-uniformly in
j\in\mathbb{N}
. This can be achieved in a fairly standard manner, by testing the Euler-Lagrange inequality (
3.15
) with difference quotients. Nevertheless, we have to be more careful as usual, since we deal with differential inequalities instead of equations.

Lemma 3.3

(Non-uniform second-order estimates)
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}})
satisfy (
1.2
) and for some
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
the estimate

(
3.16
)

\displaystyle 0\leq\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f(P)\xi,\xi\big\rangle\leq\Lambda\frac{\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}}{(1+\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad P,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}.

Moreover, let
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Ekeland-type approximation sequence from
3.1
. Then we have
v_{j}\in\operatorname{W}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
, but for the sake of completeness we give the whole argument. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
be a point and fix two radii
0<r<R<\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
. Moreover, let
s\in\{1,2\}
,
0<h<\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)-R)
and a localisation function
\varrho\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;[0,1])
with
\chi_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\leq\varrho\leq\chi_{\mathrm{B}_{R}(x_{0})}
. We test the Euler-Lagrange inequality (
3.15
) with
\varphi\coloneqq\triangle_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})\in\operatorname{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and after a discrete integration by parts we obtain

(
3.17
)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\triangle_{s,h}\mathrm{D}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]),\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\varrho^{2}\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{1}{j^{2}}\|\triangle_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus in combination with the chain rule, we observe

\displaystyle\triangle_{s,h}\mathrm{D}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}\big(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}](x)+th\triangle_{s,h}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}](x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\triangle_{s,h}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])(x),

which motivates for
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})
the definition of bilinear forms

\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x):\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\times\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{R}

through

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[\eta,\xi]\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f\big(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}](x)+th\triangle_{s,h}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])(x)\big)\eta,\xi\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\mathscr{A}}.

We note that each
\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)
is an elliptic bilinear form, since, by the very definition of
f_{j}
from (
3.7
), together with (
3.16
), the following estimates hold:

(
3.18
)

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}}{A_{j}j^{2}}\leq\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[\xi,\xi]\leq\bigg(\Lambda+\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\bigg)\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}.

In particular, a suitable version of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is available. Using the product rule
\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\varrho^{2}\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]=\varrho^{2}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]+\mathscr{A}[2\varrho\nabla\varrho\otimes\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}]
and rearranging terms in (
3.17
), gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\triangle_{s,h}\mathrm{D}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]),\varrho^{2}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\triangle_{s,h}\mathrm{D}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]),\mathscr{A}[2\varrho\nabla\varrho\otimes\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{j}\|\triangle_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}.

Rewriting term
\mathrm{I}
and using the lower bound from (
3.18
), we infer

(
3.19
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}=\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)\big[\varrho\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})],\varrho\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\varrho\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)\big[\varrho\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})],\mathscr{A}[2\nabla\varrho\otimes\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}]\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)\big[\varrho\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})],\varrho\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)\big[\mathscr{A}[2\nabla\varrho\otimes\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}],\mathscr{A}[2\nabla\varrho\otimes\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}]\big]\,\mathrm{d}x.

While the first term on the right hand side can be absorbed into
\mathrm{I}
, the second can be treated with the help of the upper bound of (
3.18
). More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)\big[\mathscr{A}[2\nabla\varrho\otimes\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}],\mathscr{A}[2\nabla\varrho\otimes\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}]\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\Lambda+\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\bigg)\|\nabla\varrho\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left\lvert\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\Lambda+\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\bigg)\|\nabla\varrho\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}~\|v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2},

which is finite as
v_{j}\in\operatorname{W}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
by construction, cf. the proof of
3.1
. For the last term
\mathrm{III}
we use the estimate (
2.4
) for negative Sobolev spaces, namely we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{III}=\frac{1}{j}\|\triangle_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varrho^{2}\triangle_{s,h}v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|\partial_{s}v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Collecting all the estimates leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\varrho\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\triangle_{s,h}v_{j})]\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c,

for a constant
c>0
independent of
h>0
. Therefore, the family
(\triangle_{s,h}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]))_{h>0}
is uniformly bounded in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
, implying the existence of the derivatives
\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]
in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
for
s\in\{1,2\}
. Thus, by Lemma
1.2
we conclude
\mathrm{D}^{2}v_{j}\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2\times 2})
. Since
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<r<R<\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
were arbitrary, we have shown
v_{j}\in\operatorname{W}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(Differentiated Euler-Lagrange inequality)
.

Under the assumptions of
3.3
it holds for all
k\in\{1,2\}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\mathrm{D}\varphi\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}\varphi]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varphi\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\qquad\forall\ \varphi\in\operatorname{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),.

where
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is the Ekeland-type approximation sequence.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{A}[\sigma_{j}]=\sigma_{j}
we also have

(
3.20
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}[\partial_{k}\sigma_{j}]=\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\sigma_{j}]=\partial_{k}\sigma_{j}.

To conclude, we use
\partial_{s}\varphi
with
\varphi\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
s\in\{1,2\}
as a test-function in (
3.15
) and then apply an approximation argument like in
[
18
, Lemma 4.4]
.
∎

3.3.
Uniform weighted second-order estimates

As a key ingredient in the proof of our main theorem
1.4
we will need weighted second-order estimates for the Ekeland-type approximation sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, which are uniformly in
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Namely we have:

Theorem 3.5

(Weighted second-order estimates)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be an (orthogonal) projection such that the induced differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
given via
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\mathscr{A}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
is
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic. Then there exists a constant
c=c(\Lambda,\mathscr{A})>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\varrho^{4}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}],\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

(
3.21
)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\frac{c(\Lambda,\mathscr{A})}{r^{2}}\bigg[\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}(1+\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(1+\frac{r^{2}}{j}+\frac{r^{3}}{j}\bigg)\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\frac{\left\lvert v_{j}\right\rvert}{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg],

where
\varrho\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is a localisation function with
\chi_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\leq\varrho\leq\chi_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}
,
\left\lvert\nabla\varrho\right\rvert\leq\frac{2}{r}
and
\left\lvert\mathrm{D}^{2}\varrho\right\rvert\leq\frac{4}{r^{2}}
.

Proof.

From
3.3
we recall
v_{j}\in\operatorname{W}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and therefore, we can apply the product rule to obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\varrho^{4}\partial_{k}v_{j})]=\mathscr{A}[\partial_{k}v_{j}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}]+\varrho^{4}\mathscr{A}[\partial_{k}\mathrm{D}v_{j}].

This allows to rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\varrho^{4}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}],\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}],\mathscr{A}[\partial_{k}v_{j}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}],\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\varrho^{4}\partial_{k}v_{j})]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

(
3.22
)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\mathscr{A}[\partial_{k}v_{j}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}(\varrho^{4}\partial_{k}v_{j})]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\eqqcolon\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}.

The second integral
\mathrm{B}
can be handled immediately using the differentiated Euler-Lagrange inequality from
3.4
, once noticing that
\varrho^{4}\partial_{k}v_{j}\in\operatorname{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Therefore, we infer

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathrm{B}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varrho^{4}\partial_{k}v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(
3.23
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\partial_{k}(\varrho^{4}v_{j})-v_{j}\partial_{k}\varrho^{4}\|_{\operatorname{W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{j}\bigg(1+\frac{8}{r}\bigg)\|v_{j}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

The treatment of
\mathrm{A}
is more subtle, as the expression
\partial_{k}v_{j}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}
is, in general, not a full a gradient. In view of (
3.20
) it holds

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\mathscr{A}[\partial_{k}v_{j}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\partial_{k}v_{j}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\mathscr{A}
induces a
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operator in two spatial dimensions, the representation (
1.3
) yields

\displaystyle\partial_{k}v_{j}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{D}v_{j})e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}+\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}

(
3.24
)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}+\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\varrho^{4}).

This allows to split
\mathrm{A}
into two parts, namely

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathrm{A}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\varrho^{4})\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\eqqcolon\mathrm{A}_{1}+\mathrm{A}_{2}.

Ad
\mathrm{A}_{1}
.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, which is applicable by (
3.18
), as well as the linearity of
\mathfrak{L}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}],\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}],\partial_{k}\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4},\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now the first term can be absorbed into the left-hand side of (
3.3
), while the second can be controlled, because of the upper bound

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(P)\xi,\xi\big\rangle\leq\Lambda\frac{\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}}{(1+\left\lvert P\right\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{A_{j}j^{2}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}.

Therefore, we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathrm{D}^{2}f_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4},\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Lambda}{2}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\frac{\left\lvert\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}\right\rvert^{2}}{(1+\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\left\lvert\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}])e_{k}\otimes\nabla\varrho^{4}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(\Lambda,\mathscr{A})}{r^{2}}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c(\mathscr{A})}{A_{j}j^{2}r^{2}}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}(1+\left\lvert\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}v_{j}]\right\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

In view of
3.1
both terms can be estimated.

Ad
\mathrm{A}_{2}
.
The second term
\mathrm{A}_{2}
deserves a more thorough analysis. In view of the linearity of
\gamma
(so that
\gamma(\cdot)=\big\langle Q,\cdot\big\rangle
for a suitable
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
) and since
v_{j}\in\operatorname{W}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
3.3
, we can integrate by parts:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\partial_{k}\sigma_{j},\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\varrho^{4})\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

(
3.25
)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\sigma_{j},\big\langle Q,\partial_{k}\mathrm{D}v_{j}\big\rangle\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\varrho^{4})\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\big\langle\sigma_{j},\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\partial_{k}\varrho^{4})\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\mathrm{A}_{2_{a}}+\mathrm{A}_{2_{b}}

To treat the second integral
\mathrm{A}_{2_{b}}
, we first utilise the product rule to deduce

\displaystyle\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\partial_{k}\varrho^{4})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}(\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\partial_{k}\varrho^{4})-\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\partial_{k}\varrho^{4}\otimes\nabla(\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j}))

(
3.26
)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}(\gamma(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\partial_{k}\varrho^{4})-\mathfrak{G}e_{k}\partial_{k}\varrho^{4}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}\big\langle Q,\partial_{1}\mathrm{D}v_{j}\big\rangle\\[4.30554pt]
\big\langle Q,\partial_{2}\mathrm{D}v_{j}\big\rangle\end{pmatrix},

3.4.
Korn inequalities in Orlicz spaces for
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operators

In this section we investigate into Korn-type inequalities on the scale of Orlicz-spaces. We begin by mentioning several known results. In
[
9
]

Breit
and
Diening
prove that an inequality of the form

(
3.34
)

\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})}\lesssim\|\operatorname{sym}\mathrm{D}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})}

can only hold for all
u\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\Phi\in\nabla_{2}\cap\Delta_{2}
, which was extended to more general elliptic operators by
Conti
and
Gmeineder

[
12
]
. This result is somehow natural as
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
and
\Phi\in\nabla_{2}
loosely speaking mean that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}}
is not
too close
to
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\infty}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}}
respectively, for which the Korn inequality fails. Moreover, it is possible to weaken the assumption
\Phi\in\nabla_{2}\cap\Delta_{2}
by replacing the norm on the right-hand side of (
3.34
) by a slightly weaker Orlicz norm due to results of
Cianchi

[
11
]
for the symmetric gradient.
Before establishing the needed Korn-type inequalities in Orlicz spaces, we catch up with the proof of Lemma
1.2
:

Proof of Lemma
1.2
.

Our proof relies on the special structure of
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operators on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, see (
1.3
) and is motivated by construction of the corresponding linear maps
\mathfrak{L}
and
\gamma
, as well as of the matrix
\mathfrak{G}\in\mathrm{GL}(2)
, cf. the proof of
[
20
, Prop. 4.1]
. We distinguish the only two possible cases:

If
\dim{\mathscr{A}[\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}]}=4
then
\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbf{e}_{j}\}_{i,j=1,2}
form a basis of
\mathscr{A}[\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}]
and the linear map

can, e.g., be defined via the action

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}(\mathbf{e}_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbf{e}_{j})\coloneqq\mathbf{e}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{j}-\text{1}_{2},\qquad\text{for }i,j=1,2.

With this choice we obtain for

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])=\begin{pmatrix}-(\partial_{1}u_{2}+\partial_{2}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2})&\partial_{1}u_{2}\\
\partial_{2}u_{1}&-(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{1}u_{2}+\partial_{2}u_{1})\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, we could already express all the entries of the gradient
\mathrm{D}u
by a linear combination of the entries of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])
:

\displaystyle\partial_{1}u_{1}=-\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])^{(22)}-\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])^{(12)}-\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])^{(21)},\quad\ldots

and could even take

in this case. But then also all the entries of
\mathrm{D}^{2}u
can be expressed as a linear combination of the entries of
\mathrm{D}\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])
.

The more sophisticated case is when
\dim{\mathscr{A}[\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}]}=3
. Then, like in the proof of
[
20
, Prop. 4.1]
, without loss of generality, we find coefficients
a_{11},a_{12},a_{22}
not all equal to zero such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbf{e}_{1}=a_{11}\mathbf{e}_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbf{e}_{1}+a_{12}\mathbf{e}_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbf{e}_{2}+a_{22}\mathbf{e}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbf{e}_{2}

and, cf.
[
20

(
3.35
)

\displaystyle a_{11}a_{22}+a_{12}\neq 0.

In this case, the linear map
\mathfrak{L}
can be chosen in the following way, cf.
[
20
, Eq. (4.12) f]

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}(\mathbf{e}_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbf{e}_{j})=\begin{cases}\mathbf{e}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{j}&\text{if }(i,j)\neq(2,1),\\[4.30554pt]
\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
0&a_{22}\end{pmatrix}&\text{if }(i,j)=(2,1).\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1}u_{1}+a_{11}\partial_{2}u_{1}&\partial_{1}u_{2}+a_{12}\partial_{2}u_{1}\\
0&\partial_{2}u_{2}+a_{22}\partial_{2}u_{1}\end{pmatrix},

so that in general we cannot express all the entries of the gradient

by a linear combination of the entries of this

. However, differentiating all the entries we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1}\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])^{(11)}\\
\partial_{2}\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])^{(11)}\\
\partial_{1}\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])^{(12)}\\
\partial_{2}\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])^{(12)}\\

0&1&a_{11}&0&0&0\\
0&a_{12}&0&1&0&0\\
0&0&a_{12}&0&1&0\\
0&a_{22}&0&0&1&0\\
0&0&a_{22}&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}\\
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}\\
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\\
\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}\\
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{2}\\

since the determinant of the appearing

-matrix is

), we can express all the entries of

by a linear combination of the entries of
\mathrm{D}\mathfrak{L}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])

.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

The structure from
1.2
also applies to the trace-free gradient in all dimensions. More precisely, all the entries of the second derivative of a vector field can be expressed by a linear combination of derivatives of
\mathbb{A}=\operatorname{dev}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
, i.e, in Lemma
1.1
we have for this particular choice also
d=2
. Indeed, since this operator only applies on the diagonal elements, the off-diagonal entries remain the same:

(\mathrm{D}u)^{(ij)}=(\operatorname{dev}\mathrm{D}u)^{(ij)}\qquad\text{for }i\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Furthermore, we note that the diagonal elements of
\mathrm{D}u
cannot be expressed as linear combinations of the entries of the elements of
\operatorname{dev}\mathrm{D}u
. However, this holds true if we increase the order of differentiation:

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(\operatorname{dev}\mathrm{D}u)^{(ii)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\left(\partial_{i}u_{i}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}u_{j}\right)=\frac{n-1}{n}\partial_{i}\partial_{i}u_{i}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j=1}}{{j\neq i}}}^{n}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\partial_{i}(\mathrm{D}u)^{(ii)}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j=1}}{{j\neq i}}}^{n}\partial_{j}(\operatorname{dev}\mathrm{D}u)^{(ji)}.

Hence, we found a first-order differential operator
\mathbb{L}
such that

\mathrm{D}^{2}=\mathbb{L}\circ\operatorname{dev}[\cdot\otimes\nabla].

Thus, we can follow the arguments below also in case of the deviatoric gradient in all dimensions and first establish the necessary Korn-type inequalities in Orlicz spaces. In conclusion, we obtain a similar statement as in our main theorem
1.4
for the deviatoric gradient in all dimensions.

As an immediate consequence we can characterise the nullspace of
\mathscr{A}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
:

Corollary 3.9

Let the projection
\mathscr{A}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
induce a
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\mathscr{A}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
. Then
\mathbb{A}u=0
if and only if

\displaystyle u(x)=Bx+b\quad\text{with }B\in\ker\mathscr{A}\text{ and }b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemma
1.2
.
∎

As a next step, we recall the following statement from
[

, Theorem 5.1]
, which is is a generalisation of
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 3.10

(Korn-type inequality in Orlicz spaces)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
be an open and bounded set and
\mathbb{A}
be a homogeneous, first-order elliptic differential operator of the form (
1.1
). Moreover, let
\Psi
and
\Phi
be Young functions such that

(
3.36
)

t\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Phi(s)}{s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Psi(ct)\quad\mbox{and}\quad t\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Psi^{\ast}(s)}{s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Phi^{\ast}(ct),

hold for all
t\geq 0
and some
c>0
. Then there exist a constant
C>0
such that

(
3.37
)

\int_{\Omega}\Phi(\left\lvert\mathrm{D}u\right\rvert)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\Psi(C\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u\right\rvert)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\mbox{for all}\quad u\in\operatorname{W}^{1,\Psi}_{0}(\Omega;V).

As a matter of fact, modular estimates in Orlicz spaces are always stronger then Luxemburg norm. Therefore, in view of (
3.37
), we obtain for
u\in\operatorname{W}^{1,\Psi}(\Omega;V)
the estimate

(
3.38
)

\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega;\Lin(\mathbb{R}^{n};V))}\leq C\,\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\Psi}(\Omega;W)}.

We now aim to prove a Korn-type inequality for balls on the scale of Orlicz spaces. Towards this aim, we first need to derive a Poincaré-type inequality in Orlicz spaces, which will we be the content of the following theorem:

Theorem 3.11

(Poincaré-type inequality)
.

Let
r>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball,
\mathbb{A}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic operator of the form (
1.1
) and
\Psi
be a Young function. Then, for every
u\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\Psi}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
there exists a constant
c=c(r,n,\mathscr{A})>0
such that

(
3.39
)

\inf_{\mathfrak{r}\in\ker(\mathbb{A})}\|u-\mathfrak{r}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});V)}\leq c\,\omega_{n}r\,\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});W)}.

Proof.

For the sake of simplicity we abbreviate
\mathrm{B}\coloneqq\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})
. The result is an immediate consequence from
[
13
, Proposition 3.8.]
, namely there exists a constant
c=c(\mathrm{B},\mathbb{A})>0
such that for
u\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},1}(\mathrm{B})
there holds the point-wise estimate

(
3.40
)

\left\lvert(u-\Pi^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbb{A}}u)(x)\right\rvert\leq c\int_{\mathrm{B}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{1-n}\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u(y)\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{for }\mathscr{L}^{n}\mbox{-a.e. }x\in\mathrm{B},

where
\Pi^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbb{A}}
denotes a suitable linear projection onto
\ker(\mathbb{A})
, cf.
[
13
, Prop. 3.3]
. As a next step we define for
x\in\mathrm{B}
a Borel measure
\mu_{x}\colon\mathscr{B}(\mathrm{B})\to[0,\infty)
via

\mu_{x}(A)\coloneqq c\int_{\mathrm{B}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{1-n}\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{for}\quad A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathrm{B})

and set
m_{x}\coloneqq\mu_{x}(\mathrm{B})
. Now, since
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{B})=2r
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{B}
, we can estimate

(
3.41
)

m_{x}=c\int_{\mathrm{B}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-1}}\geq c\frac{\mathscr{L}^{n}(\mathrm{B})}{(\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{B}))^{n-1}}=c\,\omega_{n}\,r\quad\mbox{for}\quad\mathscr{L}^{n}\mbox{-a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Dividing (
3.40
) by
m_{x}
and
\lambda\coloneqq\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B};W)}
and applying the Jensen inequality, we obtain for
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\frac{\left\lvert(u-\Pi^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbb{A}}u)(x)\right\rvert}{m_{x}\lambda}\right)\leq\Psi\left(\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{B}}\frac{\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u(y)\right\rvert}{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}(y)\right)\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{B}}\Psi\left(\frac{\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u(y)\right\rvert}{\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}(y)

Therefore, integrating the above inequality over
\mathrm{B}
with respect to the
x
-variable, in conjunction with the Tonelli theorem, results in

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}}\Psi\left(\frac{\left\lvert(u-\Pi^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbb{A}}u)(x)\right\rvert}{m_{x}\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{B}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{B}}\Psi\left(\frac{\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u(y)\right\rvert}{\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{B}}\Psi\left(\frac{\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u(y)\right\rvert}{\lambda}\right)\int_{\mathrm{B}}\frac{c}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n-1}}\frac{1}{m_{x}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{B}}\Psi\left(\frac{\left\lvert\mathbb{A}u(y)\right\rvert}{\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B};W)}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq 1,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
u\in\operatorname{W}^{\mathbb{A},\Psi}(\mathrm{B})
. Using the lower bound (
3.41
) together with the definition of the Luxemburg norm gives

(
3.42
)

\displaystyle\|u-\Pi^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathbb{A}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B};V)}\leq c\,\omega_{n}\,r\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B};W)},

where
c=c(\mathbb{A},\mathrm{B})>0
. This leads to the desired inequality (
3.39
), since we can always bound the left hand side by the infimum taken over all
\mathfrak{r}\in\ker(\mathbb{A})
, thus the proof is complete.
∎

-spaces for star-shaped or John domains respectively. We proceed and derive the announced Korn-type inequality in Orlicz spaces.

Theorem 3.12

(Korn-type inequality)
.

Let

as well as

and

(

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathfrak{r}\in\ker(\mathbb{A})}\|\mathrm{D}(u-\mathfrak{r})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Phi}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\Lin(\mathbb{R}^{n};V))}\leq c\bigg(1+\frac{1}{r}\bigg)\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0});W)}

\mathbb{C}

Proof.

\|u-\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}^{\mathrm{B}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0});V)}\leq c\,\omega_{n}\,r\|\mathbb{A}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}(\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0});W)},

holds true. Therefore, taking into account (

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}(u-\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}^{\mathrm{B}}u)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\Phi}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0}))}

This yields the desired inequality (
3.43

\mathfrak{r}\in\ker(\mathbb{A})
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.14

(Korn-type inequality on Lipschitz domains)
.

It is possible to establish a version of (
3.43
) without increasing the domains of integration on the right-hand side.
In order to do so, one can follow the lines of the proof of
[
11
, Thm. 3.3]
.
The crucial idea to establish Korn-type inequalities is Sobolev’s integral representation formula cf.
[
26
, Sec. 1.1.10]
, namely for all
u\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
it holds

(
3.46
)

\displaystyle u=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=2}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\frac{x^{\alpha}}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}}\ast\partial^{\alpha}u.

If the projection
\mathscr{A}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
induce by
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\mathscr{A}[\cdot\otimes\nabla]
a
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then by our Lemma
1.2
we can express
\partial^{\alpha}u
as linear combination

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}u=\partial_{1}\ell_{1}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u])+\partial_{2}\ell_{2}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u]),\qquad\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=2,

with suitable linear maps
\ell_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
j=1,2
. Thus, reinserting in (
3.46
) and integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle u=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=2}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}\frac{x^{\alpha}}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}}\ast\ell_{j}(\mathscr{A}[\mathrm{D}u]),

to arrive at a similar statement as in
[
11
, Eq. (6.6)]
.
Furthermore, since our induced differential operator
\mathbb{A}
is
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic, its kernel is finite dimensional, cf. also Corollary
3.9
, so that we can also deduce Korn-type inequalities of the second type in Orlicz spaces.
First, via an Orlicz version of the Poincaré’s inequality involving the
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operator
\mathbb{A}
, we have

(
3.47
)

\inf_{v\in\mathscr{K}}\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{\exp\operatorname{L}^{\beta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq c(\beta,\mathbb{A},\Omega)\left\lVert\mathbb{A}u\right\rVert_{\exp\operatorname{L}^{\beta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}

for every
u\in\operatorname{L}^{\Phi_{\beta}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\mathbb{A}u\in\operatorname{L}^{\Phi_{\beta}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
where
\mathscr{K}
denotes the finite dimensional nullspace of
\mathbb{A}

\mathscr{K}\coloneqq\{v(x)=Bx+b,B\in\ker\mathscr{A},b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\},

cf.
3.9
.
Let us shortly comment how we can again follow the lines of the proof of
[
11
, Thm 3.3]
emphasising the needed adjustments: The first ingredient is Smith’s representation formula on cones for
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic differential operators
[
32
, Thm I]
. The next crucial step is to construct an extension operator like in
[
30
, Ch II Rem 1.3, Thm 1.2, Thm 2.2]
which relies on a limiting Sobolev type inequality
[
30
, Eq. (1.32)]
. The latter result has been generalised by

[

, Thm 1.3]
and reads in our situation

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq c(\mathbb{A})\left\lVert\mathbb{A}u\right\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\qquad\forall\ u\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})

and holds true if and only if

is an elliptic and cancelling operator. In particular (
3.48
) is fulfilled for

-elliptic differential operators, since

[

. Hence, we can apply the arguments from the proof of
[

to derive similar to
[

the estimate

(

\left\lVert\mathrm{D}u\right\rVert_{\exp\operatorname{L}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\leq c(\beta,\mathbb{A},\Omega)\,\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\exp\operatorname{L}^{\beta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}+\left\lVert\mathbb{A}u\right\rVert_{\exp\operatorname{L}^{\beta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\right)

for every

with
\mathbb{A}u\in\exp\mathrm{L}^{\beta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
. Thus, applying (
3.49

for a arbitrary

we conclude in view of the Poincaré inequality (

Remark 3.15
.

In this short note, we have investigated into the regularity of
\mathrm{BV}^{\mathbb{A}}
-minimisers in two dimensions. Here, the crucial idea was to rely on the one-dimensional (almost) complementary part of the part maps that induce
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic, first-order differential operators. Furthermore, we have shown that this strategy is also applicable for the deviatoric gradient in all dimensions. The case of the symmetric gradient in all dimensions was treated in
[
3
]
. However, the case of a general
\mathbb{C}
-elliptic operator in any dimensions deserves a new approach. In particular, it remains an open problem to treat the trace-free symmetric gradient in three dimensions.

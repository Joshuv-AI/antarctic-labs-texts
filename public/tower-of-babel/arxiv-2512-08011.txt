Free fermionic and parafermionic multispin quantum chains with non-homogeneous interacting ranges
Francisco C. Alcaraz

Abstract
A large family of multispin interacting one-dimensional quantum spin models with Z ⁡ ( N ) Z(N) symmetry and a free-particle eigenspectra are known in the literature. They are free-fermionic ( N = 2 N=2 ) and free-parafermionic ( N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 ) quantum chains. The essential ingredient that implies the free-particle spectra is the fact that these Hamiltonians are expressed in terms of generators of a Z ⁡ ( N ) Z(N) exchange algebra. In all these known quantum chains the number of spins in all the multispin interactions (range of interactions) is the same and therefore, the models have homogeneous interacting range. In this paper we extend the Z ⁡ ( N ) Z(N) exchange algebra, by introducing new models with a free-particle spectra, where the interaction ranges of the multispin interactions are not uniform anymore and depends on the lattice sites (non-homogeneous interacting range). We obtain the general conditions that the site-dependent ranges of the multispin interactions have to satisfy to ensure a free-particle spectra. Several simple examples are introduced. We study in detail the critical properties in the case where the range of interactions of the even (odd) sites are constant. The dynamical critical exponent is evaluated in several cases.

Introduction

In condensed matter physics and statistical mechanics there exist a class

are quantum spin models defined in terms of

In this paper we are going to consider general multispin interacting one-dimensional
models where the range of interactions (the number of spins coupled in a
multispin interaction) depend on the
particular site. The above mentioned (

derived.

The paper is organized as follows. In section II we introduce the
one-dimensional models with general interacting range, and show the constraints the ranges have to satisfy in order to produce a free-particle eigenspectra.
In section III we present several examples of non-homogeneous models and
derive their critical behavior. In section IV we draw our conclusions.
Finally in apendix A we derive the inverse relations for the models.
A relation that ensures, for the models we considered, the free-particle eigenspectra.

\omega h_{j}^{(\ell_{j},r_{j})}h_{i}^{(\ell_{i},r_{i})},&(j-i)\leq r_{i},\\
h_{j}^{(\ell_{j},r_{j})}h_{i}^{(\ell_{i},r_{i})},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(26)

In terms of
\{h_{i}^{(\ell_{i},r_{i})}\}
it is not difficult to derive the
recursion relation for the conserved charges
Q_{M}^{(\ell)}
of the quantum
chain given in (
1
)

\displaystyle Q_{M}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}h_{i}^{(\ell_{i},r_{i})},

\displaystyle Q_{M}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{M-1}^{(\ell)}+h_{M}^{(\ell_{M},r_{M})}Q^{(\ell-1)}_{M-(\ell_{M}+1)},

(27)

The last relation is a generalization of the recursion relation given in

AP1
;
AP2
for the homogeneous models.

The question that remains concerns the verification that the integrable
models (
1
), whose generators satisfy (
II
),(
3
) and (
24
) have a
free-particle spectra.
A sufficient condition, as verified in the known homogeneous case, where

r_{1}=r_{2}=\cdots
, is the existence of a closure relation
(inverse relation) for the charge generating function

\displaystyle G_{M}(u)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\bar{M}}(-u)^{\ell}Q_{M}^{(\ell)},

(28)

where
\bar{M}
is the number of independent
Q^{(\ell)}
charges.

In the homogeneous case this closure relation is given by the special

Z(N)
product:

\displaystyle T_{M}(u)=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}G(\omega^{j}u)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\bar{M}}u^{\ell}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{N}}^{(*,\ell)}Q_{M}^{(\ell_{1})}\cdots Q_{M}^{(\ell_{M})}\omega^{0\ell_{1}+1\ell_{2}+\cdots+(N-1)\ell_{N}}\right),

(29)

where the restriction
(*,\ell)
in the summation means

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell_{i}=\ell
.
Since the charges commute among themselves, for a given set
(
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{N}
), that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell_{i}=\ell
, the
contribution in (
II
) is proportional to

\displaystyle\sum_{\{P\}}\omega^{(\ell_{P_{2}}+2\ell_{P_{3}}+\cdots+(N-1)\ell_{P_{N}}},

(30)

where
P=(P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{N})
are the
N!
permutations of the
integers
1,2,\ldots,N
. However (
30
) gives zero if
\sum_{i-1}^{N}\ell_{i}=\ell\neq 0,\mbox{ mod }N
. This imply that only powers of
u^{N}
appears
in (
II
). Remarkable, for the homogeneous systems, the coefficients of

u^{\ell}
are just c-numbers. i. e.,

\displaystyle T_{M}(u)=\sum_{\ell=0}C_{M}^{(\ell)}u^{\ell N}=P_{M}(u^{N}),

(31)

and
G_{M}(u)
is a polynomial
P_{M}(z)
of the variable
z=u^{N}
.

The relation (
II
) ensure that all the conserved charges
Q_{M}^{(\ell)}

have a free-particle spectra. In order to see this we notice that

[Q_{M}^{(\ell)},Q_{M}^{(\ell^{\prime})}]=0
imply also
[G_{M}(u),G_{M}(u^{\prime})]=0
, and all the
charges, as well
G_{M}(u)
share the same eigenfunctions
|\Psi\rangle
.
Denoting the eigenvalue of
G_{M}(u)
by
\Lambda(u)
, we now have from
(
II
)

\displaystyle\Lambda(u)\Lambda(\omega u)\cdots\Lambda(\omega^{N-1}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{M}(u^{N})

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{\bar{M}}\left(1-\frac{u^{N}}{z_{i}}\right),

since
P_{M}(0)=1
and
z_{i}
(
i=1,2,\ldots,\bar{M}
) are the roots of the
polynomial
P_{M}(z_{i})=0
. Rewriting

\displaystyle 1-\frac{u^{N}}{z_{i}}=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\left(1-u\frac{\omega^{j}}{z_{i}^{1/N}}\right),

(33)

we obtain the possible
{\bar{M}}^{N}
solutions for
\Lambda_{M}(u)
:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{M}(u)^{\{s_{i}\}}=\prod_{i=1}^{\bar{M}}\left(1-u\frac{\omega^{s_{i}}}{z_{i}^{1/N}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{\bar{M}}\left(1-u\omega^{s_{i}}\varepsilon_{i}\right),

(34)

where

\varepsilon_{i}=1/z_{i}^{1/N}
, and for each root
z_{i}
we choose one of the
possibilities
s_{i}=0,1,\ldots,N-1
.

Expanding the generator
G_{M}(u)
and
\Lambda_{M}(u)
in powers of
u^{N}
we
find the possible eigenvalues of all the charges
Q_{M}^{(\ell)}
. In particular
the eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian
-Q_{M}^{(1)}
have the free-particle spectra

\displaystyle E_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{\bar{M}}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{\bar{M}}\omega^{s_{i}}\varepsilon_{i},

(35)

For each root
z_{i}
(pseudo-energy
\varepsilon_{i}=1/z_{i}^{1/N}
), given in (
34
), we have to
chose
one and only one
phase
\omega^{s_{i}}
, to form the eigenenergy

E_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{\bar{M}}}
. This is the
Z(N)
circle
exclusion constraint (see

baxter1
;
baxter2
;
fendley1
;
baxter3
;
perk1
;
perk2
;
AB1
;
AB2
), i.e.,
the parafermionic version of the Pauli exclusion principle.

In appendix A we show that for the general models (
1
), given in terms
of generators of the
Z(2)
exchange algebra (
II
)-(
3
), the inversion
relation

\displaystyle T_{M}(u)T_{M}(-u)=\sum_{\ell}{\cal{C}}_{M}^{(\ell)}\;u^{2\ell}=P_{M}(u^{2}),

(36)

is satisfied provide the following two conditions are verified.

(a)

There is no single generator that does not commute with three
other commuting operators.

(b)

By linking the generators that do not commute we do not form closed
loops (see appendix A).

The condition (b) is satisfied for the quantum chains with open boundary
conditions, but exclude the inverse relation (
36
) for periodic chains.
The proof of the inversion relation (
31
) for
N>2
, on a
direct way, as we did in appendix A for
N=2
, is cumbersome, but it was
already obtained in
ref-inv-zn
using graph theory.

The polynomial
P_{M}(u^{N})
, for general
N
, has the coefficients
{\cal{C}}_{M}^{(\ell)}
in (
31
), given by (see appendix A)

\displaystyle{\cal{C}}_{M}^{(\ell)}=(-)^{\ell}\sum^{*}_{\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}}\lambda_{i_{1}}^{N}\lambda_{i_{2}}^{N}\cdots\lambda_{i_{\ell}}^{N},

(37)

where now the symbol (*) denotes the sum over all independent possibilities
of the products of the
\ell
-couplings associated to the
\ell
-commuting
generators

h_{i_{1}}^{(r_{1})}h_{i_{2}}^{(r_{2})}\cdots h_{i_{\ell}}^{(r_{\ell})}
in the
charge
Q_{M}^{(\ell)}
.

The recursion relation (
II
) of the charges
Q_{M}^{(\ell)}
give us a
recursion for the coefficients
{\cal{C}}_{M}^{(\ell)}
:

\displaystyle{\cal{C}}_{M}^{(\ell)}={\cal{C}}_{M-1}^{(\ell)}-\lambda_{M}^{N}\;{\cal{C}}_{M-(\ell_{M}+1)}^{(\ell-1)}\mbox{ for }M>1,

(38)

where
{\cal{C}}_{0}^{(0)}=1
and
{\cal{C}}_{j^{\prime}}^{(\ell^{\prime})}=0
if
\ell^{\prime}>0

or
j^{\prime}<0
.

The above relation imply the recursion for the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{M}(z)=P_{M-1}(z)-z\;\lambda_{M}^{N}P_{M-(l_{M}+1)}(z),M\geq 1,

(39)

with the initial condition
P_{M}(z)=1
for
M\leq 0
.

III
Examples of models with inhomogeneous interacting ranges

In the last section we verified that arbitrary one-dimensional quantum chains

\displaystyle H_{M}^{(N,\{r_{i}\})}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{M})=-\sum_{i=1}^{M}h_{i}^{(r_{i})},

(40)

with the site-dependent interaction ranges
\{r_{i}\}
, are exactly integrable
and have a free-particle eigenspectra if the generators
\{h_{i}^{(r_{i})}\}

obey the
Z(N)
algebra (
II
)-(
3
), with the restriction

\displaystyle r_{i+1}\geq r_{i}-1\quad(i=1,\ldots,M).

(41)

Arbitrary representations of the algebra (
II
)-(
3
)
satisfying (
41
)
will give us integrable quantum chains (
40
) with a free-particle spectra
(
35
).

A particular representation of dimension
N^{M}
, that we can always construct,
is the one we call ”word representation”.
In this representation we call the generators of the algebra as ”letters”
(alphabet) and the product of them as ”words”. The vector space is spanned
by the vectors
|s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{M}>
, (
s_{i}=0,1,\ldots,N-1
), with a
one-to-one correspondence with the words

\displaystyle|s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{M}>\leftrightarrow[h_{1}^{(r_{1})}]^{s_{1}}\dot{[}h_{2}^{(r_{2})}]^{s_{2}}\cdots[h_{M}^{(r_{M})}]^{s_{M}}.

In this representation the generators are given by the generalization of
(
9
)

\displaystyle h_{i}^{(r_{i})}=\lambda_{i}\left(\prod_{j=i-\ell_{i}}^{i-1}Z_{j}\right)X_{i},

(42)

where
\{\ell_{i}\}
are obtained from
\{r_{i}\}
as in (
25
).
The
M
-sites Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H_{M}^{(N;\{r_{i}\})}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{M})=-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\lambda_{i}\left(\prod_{i=i-\ell_{i}}^{i-1}Z_{i}\right)X_{i}.

(43)

The homogeneous cases (
4
) is recovered by taking

r_{1}=r_{2}=\ldots,r_{M}=p
(
p=1,2,\ldots
), where we have the
p
-multispin
free fermionic
N=2
and free parafermionic
(N>2
) models.

III.1
A simple example

A simple and exotic example is the
M
-sites chains given in terms of
Z(N)

generators where the interaction ranges

r_{i}=M-i+1
, for
i=1,\ldots,M
as in Fig.

Figure 9:

Multispin interacting ranges for a simple model where

In this case, no matter how large is

\displaystyle Q_{M}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H_{M}^{(\{r_{i}\})}=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}h_{1}^{(M)}+\lambda_{2}h_{2}^{(M-1)}+\cdots+\lambda_{M}h_{M}^{(1)},

with
[h_{i}{(1)}]^{N}=1
.
Since now
G_{M}(u)=1+uH_{M}^{(\{r_{i}\})}

\displaystyle T_{M}(u)=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}G(\omega^{j}u)=P_{M}(u)=1-u^{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{M}\lambda_{i}^{N}\right),

that gives
P_{M}(z)=1-z\sum_{i=1}^{M}\lambda_{i}^{N}

There is a single root of

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{s}=e^{i\frac{2\pi}{N}s}/\left(\sum_{i=1}^{M}\lambda_{i}^{N}\right),\quad s=0,1\ldots,N-1.

(45)

The
N^{M}
-dimensional Hilbert space has the above eigenenergies with
degeneracy
N^{M}/N=N^{M-1}
.

In the case where
\sum_{i}^{M}\lambda_{i}^{N}=0
, the polynomial is
P_{M}=1
and
we have no zeros. This means that all the eigenenergies are zero. If
H_{M}^{(\{r_{i}\})}

would be diagonalizable it should be a zero matrix. Since this is not the
case

is not fully diagonalizable and have a Jordan-cell structure.

Actually in this exotic example all the generators do not commute, obeying
a
Z(N)
algebra. In the case

they satisfy

\displaystyle\{h_{i}^{(r_{i})},h_{j}^{(r_{j})}]=2\delta_{i,j},\quad(h_{i}^{(r_{i})})^{2}=1,

(46)

and we can identify them as Majorana fermions. The
Hamiltonian (
44
) is just the sum of Majorana fermions.

In the case
N>2
we can identify the generators as a generalized

Z(N)
Majorana parafermions
zn-parafermions
, where

\displaystyle h_{i}^{(r_{i})}h_{j}^{(r_{j})}=\begin{cases}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{N}}h_{j}^{(r_{j})}h_{i}^{(r_{i})},&j>i\\
e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{N}}h_{j}^{(r_{j})}h_{i}^{(r_{i})},&j<i,\end{cases}

(47)

and
(h_{i}^{(r_{i})})^{N}=1
.

Many more interesting examples can be produced where the range of

Models where all the even and odd sites have a constant range of

,
where

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{i}^{z}=-\sigma_{i}^{z},\quad\tilde{\sigma}_{i}^{x}=\frac{h_{x}\sigma_{i}^{x}+h_{y}\sigma_{i}^{y}}{\sqrt{h_{x}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}}},

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{i}^{y}=\frac{h_{y}\sigma_{i}^{x}+h_{x}\sigma_{i}^{y}}{\sqrt{h_{x}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}}},

(58)

that give us

\displaystyle H=-\sum_{i=1}^{M/3}(J_{z}\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{i+1}^{z}+\tilde{h}_{x}\sigma_{i}^{x}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad\tilde{h}_{x}=\sqrt{h_{x}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}},

recovering the standard quantum Ising chain in a transverse field

\tilde{h}_{x}
. This model is critical when

J_{z}=\tilde{h}^{x}=\sqrt{h_{x}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}}
. At the isotropic point
J_{z}=h_{x}=h_{y}
, the model is in a disordered gapped phase.

For the parafermionic cases
N>2
we fix
\lambda_{3i-2}=\lambda_{A}
,

\lambda_{3i-1}=\lambda_{B}
,
\lambda_{3i}=\lambda_{C}
, and

\displaystyle h_{3i-2}^{(2)}=\lambda_{C}Z_{i}^{+}X_{i},\quad h_{3i-2}^{(1)}=\lambda_{A}X_{i},\quad h_{3i}^{(2)}=\lambda_{B}Z_{i}Z_{i+1}^{+},

where
Z^{+}=Z^{N-1}
, and
X,Z
are the
N\times N
matrices satisfying the
Z(N)

algebra (
II
). The generators in the above equation satisfy the
algebra (
II
) with the ranges
\{r_{i}\}=(1,2,2,1,2,2,\ldots)
. The
Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=-\sum_{i=1}^{M/3}\lambda_{C}Z_{i}^{+}X_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{M/3}(\lambda_{A}X_{i}+\lambda_{B}Z_{i}Z_{i+1}^{+}),

(59)

that correspond to the extension of the free parafermionic Baxter chains
(
p=1
) with couplings
\lambda_{A}
and
\lambda_{B}
(
8
) in the presence
of the ”transverse field”
\sum_{i}\lambda_{C}Z_{i}^{+}X_{i}
.

Defining the transformation

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{2i-1}^{(1)}=\frac{\lambda_{C}h_{3i-2}^{(2)}+\lambda_{A}h_{3i-1}^{(1)}}{(\lambda_{C}^{N}+\lambda_{A}^{N})^{1/N}},\quad\tilde{h}_{2i}^{(1)}=h_{3i}^{(2)},

(60)

we now have

\displaystyle H=-\sum_{i=1}^{M/3}\left((\lambda_{C}^{N}+\lambda_{A}^{N})^{1/N}\tilde{h}_{2i-1}^{(1)}+\lambda_{B}\tilde{h}_{2i}^{(1)}\right).

(61)

A direct check show us that the new generators (
60
) satisfy
[\tilde{h}_{i}^{(1)}]^{1/N}=1
and share the same algebra with the parafermionic model
with uniform range
p=1
(
32
).
The known results of the standard Baxter chain
baxter1
;
AB1
;
AB2
,
tell us that the model is critical when

\lambda_{C}^{N}+\lambda_{A}^{N}=\lambda_{B}^{N}
, being gapped elsewhere.

We can extend these last results for the
Z(N)
models with range of
interactions

\{r_{i}\}=(\ell,\ell-1,\ldots,1,\ell,\ell-1,\ldots,1,\ell,\ldots)
.
In this case the generators
h_{i}^{(r_{i})}
are transformed by the
generalization of (
60
)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{2i-1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}h_{i}^{(\ell)}+\lambda_{2}h_{i+1}^{(\ell-1)}+\cdots\lambda_{\ell}h_{i+\ell-1}^{(1)}}{(\lambda_{1}^{N}+\lambda_{2}^{N}+\cdots\lambda_{\ell}^{N})^{1/N}},

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{2i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\ell+1}h_{\ell+1}^{(\ell)},

(62)

and the same Hamiltonian is again equivalent to an effective uniform range

p=1
parafermionic model

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{M/(\ell+1)}\left((\lambda_{1}^{N}+\lambda_{2}^{N}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}^{N})^{1/N}h_{2i-1}^{(1)}\right.

(63)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\lambda_{\ell+1}h_{2i}^{(1)}\right),

being critical when

\lambda_{1}^{N}+\lambda_{2}^{N}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}^{N}=\lambda_{\ell+1}^{N}
.

The results presented in this section are just examples of models with general
range of interaction. Some of them have a phase diagram that can be derived
analytically, but in general a numerical analysis is necessary. This is not
difficult since the eigenspectra is given in terms of roots of a
polynomial
P_{M}(z)
, with recursion relation given in (
39
).

IV
Conclusions

A large family of spin of
Z(N)
-invariant quantum chains with multispin interactions have a
free-particle eigenspectra. The common ingredient of all these models is the fact
that they satisfy a
Z(N)
exchange algebra.

In all these free-particle models the multispin coupling
constants
\{\lambda_{i}\}
are
arbitrary for each site (
i=1,2,\ldots,M
), but the number of spins involved
in the multispin interaction (range of interaction) is uniform, i. e.,
is the same
for all the lattices sites. These Hamiltonians are Hermitian and fermionic for

N=2
, and for
N>2
they are parafermionic and non Hermitian.

In this paper we extend even more these families of free-particle models by
considering now models where the range of the multispin interactions
\{r_{i}\}

depends on the lattice sites (
i=1,2,\ldots,N
).

We search for the extensions of the exchange algebras that give models that still keep the integrability as well as the free-particle spectra. In order to derive these conditions we look for the general constraints that the
Z(N)
generators attached to the
sites have to satisfy. Although these conditions in general was given
in
network
our derivation was done in a direct form.

For the one dimensional models, with site-dependent ranges
\{r_{i}\}
, the
range are restricted to the RSOS paths where

r_{i+1}\geq r_{i}+1
(
i=1,\ldots,M
). This condition enable us to
produce nice examples, like the ones presented in Sec. III.1, where the Hamiltonians
have a quite large global degeneracy.

In order to exploit the physics of these models we studied in detail the
models where all coupling constants
\lambda_{o}
(
\lambda_{e}
) and the
range of interactions
r_{o}
(
r_{e}
) of the odd (even) sites are constant.
We use algebraic transformations of the generators and also numerical
calculations to evaluate the energy gaps of the models. The numerical calculations were done for large lattice sizes (
M\sim 10^{4}
), and can be extended to
even larger lattice sizes thanks to the powerful method introduced in
powerfull1
(see also
randonp2
) to evaluate the
larger roots of polynomials.

Our results show that for the ranges
r_{o}=\ell+1
and
r_{e}=\ell
, with
\ell>1
, the
fermionic (
N=2
) and parafermionic (
N>2
) models are critical. They belongs to a
universality class of critical behavior where the dynamical critical exponent is

z=(\ell+1)/2N
if
\lambda_{e}\neq 0
and
z=(\ell+3)/2N
if
\lambda_{e}=0
. For the value

\ell=1
(
r_{o}=2,r_{e}=1
), and
\lambda_{e}\neq 0
the model is gapped, while when
\lambda_{e}=0
the model is critical with
z=2/N
. Most probably
whenever
z=1
the models are conformally invariant as happens in the unifrom range models
multi-conf
.

We believe that the knowledge of these new free-fermionic and free parafermionic quantum
chains will be useful to probe new physical ideas and also for the production of toy models
of many-body interactions for testing numerical algorithms.

Appendix A
Inversion relations

Taking into account the involution of the charges (
II
), i. e.,

[Q_{M}^{(\ell)},Q_{M}^{(\ell^{\prime})}]=0
(
\ell,\ell^{\prime}=0,1,\ldots
), we are going to obtain
in this appendix the conditions that ensure the inverse relation (
31
).
Instead of using graph theory as in
network
, similarly as we did
in section II, we are going to obtain these conditions directly from the
expansions of the charges.

We restrict here for the case
N=2
, where the inversion relation is

\displaystyle T(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(u)G(-u)

(64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}^{\bar{M}}u^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}}(-)^{\ell}Q_{M}^{(\ell)}Q_{M}^{(\ell^{\prime})}=P_{M}(u^{2}).

Since
[Q_{M}^{(\ell)},Q_{M}^{(\ell^{\prime})}]=0
, we can write

\displaystyle G(u)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\bar{M}}\hat{Z}^{\ell}z^{\ell},\quad z=u^{2},

(65)

and
\hat{Z}^{(\ell)}
is an operator given in terms of the conserved charges

\{Q_{M}^{(\ell)}\}

\displaystyle\hat{Z}^{(\ell)}=\sum_{j=0}^{2\ell}(-)^{j}Q^{(2\ell-j)}Q_{M}^{(j)},\quad\ell=0,1,\ldots.

are formed by the products of
\ell
commuting generators,

is given by the sum of all the words

containing
2\ell
generators distributed in two commuting groups
with (
2\ell-j
) and
j
generators (

The inverse relation (
64

\hat{Z}^{(\ell)}
is an scalar
for any
\ell
.

In general the generators forming the words
W^{(2\ell)}
can be separated
in subgroups

(

), where all the operators inside a given
subgroup
G_{i}
commute with the others belonging to distinct subgroups
(
G_{i}\neq G_{i}

\displaystyle W^{(2\ell)}=G_{1}G_{2}\cdots.

(67)

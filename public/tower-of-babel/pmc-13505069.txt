Association between prosthetic rehabilitation type, nutritional status, and oral health-related quality of life in completely edentulous adults: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Zeynep Basagaoglu Demirekin, Elif Erten
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEdentulism negatively affects masticatory function, nutritional status, and oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL). This study aimed to evaluate the association between prosthetic rehabilitation type, nutritional status, and OHRQoL in completely edentulous patients.MethodsA total of 140 completely edentulous patients were included in this cross-sectional study and divided into three groups: conventional complete dentures (CD), implant-supported overdentures (IOD), and implant-supported fixed prostheses (IFP). OHRQoL was assessed using OHIP-14 and OHQoL-UK, while nutritional status was evaluated using the MNA-SF. Statistical analyses were performed to compare groups and assess correlations.ResultsPatients with implant-supported fixed prostheses demonstrated significantly more favorable OHRQoL and MNA-SF scores compared with conventional denture users (p < 0.05). Significant associations were observed between oral health-related quality of life measures and nutritional status.ConclusionsImplant-supported prosthetic rehabilitation was significantly associated with more favorable oral health-related quality of life outcomes, while differences in nutritional status were also observed among prosthetic rehabilitation groups.Keywords: Edentulism, Implant-supported prostheses, Oral health-related quality of life, Nutritional status, Masticatory function, Edentulous adults

Introduction

Edentulism remains a significant oral health problem among edentulous adults and may adversely affect chewing ability, nutritional status, and overall quality of life. Tooth loss may result in functional limitations, aesthetic impairment, and psychosocial distress, all of which negatively affect an individual’s general well-being and quality of life [1]. Reduced masticatory efficiency associated with edentulism can restrict food choices and dietary diversity, potentially leading to nutritional deficiencies and adverse systemic health outcomes [2].

Despite improvements in preventive dentistry, edentulism continues to affect a substantial proportion of edentulous adults worldwide. The increasing life expectancy of the global population has led to a growing number of individuals requiring prosthetic rehabilitation for tooth loss [3]. Previous studies have demonstrated that edentulous individuals frequently report difficulties with chewing, speaking, and social interaction, which may contribute to reduced oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) [4].

Conventional complete dentures have long been considered the standard treatment option for completely edentulous patients due to their relatively low cost and non-invasive nature. However, inadequate retention and stability—especially in the mandibular arch—may compromise chewing efficiency and patient satisfaction [5]. Progressive alveolar bone resorption following tooth loss may further reduce denture stability, thereby negatively affecting oral function and prosthesis performance [6].

The development of osseointegrated dental implants has provided alternative treatment options for edentulous patients, including implant-supported overdentures and implant-supported fixed prostheses. Implant-supported prosthetic treatments have been reported to be associated with improved prosthesis retention, stability, and chewing performance compared with conventional dentures [7]. The McGill consensus statement recommended mandibular two-implant overdentures as the minimum standard of care for edentulous mandibles, emphasizing the clinical benefits of implant-supported rehabilitation [8].

Recent systematic reviews and clinical studies have reported that implant-supported prostheses are associated with improved patient satisfaction, masticatory efficiency, and oral health-related quality of life [9, 10]. In addition, implant-supported prosthetic treatments have been reported to be associated with improved functional outcomes and increased dietary variety in edentulous patients [11].

Oral health-related quality of life has become an important patient-reported outcome measure in prosthodontic research. Validated questionnaires such as the Oral Health Impact Profile (OHIP-14) and the Oral Health-Related Quality of Life-United Kingdom (OHQoL-UK) scales are widely used to evaluate the functional, psychological, and social effects of oral conditions and dental treatments [12].

In addition to functional outcomes, nutritional status is another important factor affecting the general health of edentulous individuals. Reduced chewing ability may limit the intake of fibrous and nutrient-rich foods such as fruits and vegetables, which may increase the risk of malnutrition in edentulous adults [13]. The Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) has been widely used as a validated screening tool for evaluating nutritional status in edentulous adults [14].

Although previous studies have evaluated functional outcomes and patient-reported satisfaction associated with implant-supported prosthetic treatments, relatively limited research has simultaneously examined the relationship between prosthetic rehabilitation type, nutritional status, and oral health-related quality of life in completely edentulous patients. Understanding this relationship is particularly important in edentulous adults, as impaired chewing ability and dietary limitations may contribute to nutritional deficiencies and adverse systemic health outcomes. Recent reviews have highlighted the increasing importance of patient-reported outcomes in prosthodontic rehabilitation of edentulous patients. Contemporary evidence indicates that implant-supported prosthetic treatments have been reported to be associated with improvements in oral health-related quality of life, masticatory performance, and patient satisfaction compared with conventional dentures. Furthermore, systematic reviews evaluating full-arch rehabilitation in edentulous adults have reported substantial improvements in functional and psychosocial domains of quality of life following implant-based treatments [15, 16]. These findings emphasize the importance of considering both functional outcomes and patient-centered measures when evaluating different prosthetic treatment modalities.

Therefore, the aim of the present study was to evaluate and compare the nutritional status and oral health-related quality of life of completely edentulous patients treated with conventional complete dentures, implant-supported overdentures, and implant-supported fixed prostheses.

Materials and methods

Study design

This cross-sectional clinical study was conducted to evaluate the relationship between prosthetic treatment modality, oral health-related quality of life, and nutritional status in completely edentulous patients. The study included patients who had received prosthetic treatment at the Department of Prosthodontics, Faculty of Dentistry.

Although the cross-sectional design limits causal inference, this study provides valuable real-world clinical insights into the relationship between prosthetic rehabilitation, nutritional status, and oral health-related quality of life.

All procedures were performed in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Süleyman Demirel University (Approval No: 18.09.2020/275).

Participants

A sample size of 140 participants was considered adequate to detect statistically significant differences between prosthetic treatment groups based on previous studies evaluating oral health-related quality of life and nutritional outcomes in edentulous patients. A total of 140 completely edentulous patients aged between 40 and 90 years who had completed their prosthetic treatment at least six months prior to the study were included. Although the sample size was determined based on previous studies, a formal a priori power analysis was not performed, which may be considered a limitation.

Data collection and questionnaires

Sociodemographic information including age, gender, education level, and income level was recorded for all participants.

Three validated instruments were used to assess oral health-related quality of life and nutritional status.

Oral Health Impact Profile-14 (OHIP-14)

The OHIP-14 questionnaire was used to evaluate oral health-related quality of life. This instrument consists of 14 items grouped into seven domains, including functional limitation, physical pain, psychological discomfort, physical disability, psychological disability, social disability, and handicap.

Participants rated each item using a five-point Likert scale ranging from “never” to “very often”. Higher scores indicated poorer oral health-related quality of life.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Descriptive statistics were calculated for all variables, including means, standard deviations, frequencies, and percentages.

Comparisons between prosthetic treatment groups were performed using:

One-way ANOVA for normally distributed variables.

One-way ANOVA for normally distributed variables.

Kruskal–Wallis test for non-normally distributed variables.

Kruskal–Wallis test for non-normally distributed variables.

Chi-square test for categorical variables.

Chi-square test for categorical variables.

Post-hoc analyses were conducted to identify significant differences between groups.

Correlation analyses were performed to evaluate the relationship between oral health-related quality of life scores and nutritional status.

A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

To address potential confounding factors, multivariate linear regression analyses were additionally performed for OHIP-14, OHQoL-UK, and MNA-SF scores. Prosthetic rehabilitation type was included as the primary independent variable, while age, gender, education level, and income level were entered as covariates.

Adjusted multivariate analyses demonstrated that prosthetic rehabilitation type remained significantly associated with OHIP-14 and OHQoL-UK scores after controlling for age, gender, education level, and income level . Patients rehabilitated with implant-supported prostheses continued to demonstrate more favorable oral health-related quality of life outcomes compared with conventional complete denture users.

However, the association between prosthetic rehabilitation type and nutritional status (MNA-SF) became less pronounced after adjustment for demographic and socioeconomic variables, suggesting that nutritional status may be influenced by multiple factors beyond prosthetic rehabilitation alone.

Results

A total of 140 completely edentulous patients were included in the present study. Participants were divided into three groups according to the prosthetic rehabilitation modality: conventional complete dentures (CD, n = 40), implant-supported overdentures (IOD, n = 50), and implant-supported fixed prostheses (IFP, n = 50).

The demographic characteristics of the study population are summarized in Table 1. The mean age of the participants was 62.60 ± 9.06 years, with ages ranging from 40 to 90 years. The sample consisted of 49 women (35%) and 91 men (65%). The mean body weight was 75.48 ± 13.58 kg, and the mean height was 166.76 ± 8.73 cm. Educational level distribution showed that 37.1% of participants had primary education or were illiterate, 43.6% had secondary/high school education, and 19.3% had university education.

Influence of socioeconomic factors

Educational level showed a statistically significant association with oral health-related quality of life. Participants with lower educational levels had significantly higher OHIP-14 scores compared with individuals with higher educational attainment (p < 0.001).

Similarly, income level influenced oral health outcomes. Participants with income levels below the minimum wage demonstrated significantly poorer OHIP-14 scores and higher levels of functional limitation, physical pain, physical disability, psychological disability, social disability, and handicap compared with individuals with higher income levels (p < 0.05).

Correlation between age and oral health-related quality of life

Correlation analysis revealed a weak but statistically significant positive correlation between age and OHIP-14 scores (r = 0.246, p = 0.003), indicating that oral health-related quality of life decreased with increasing age.

Correlation analysis revealed significant associations between nutritional status and oral health-related quality of life measures. Higher MNA-SF scores were associated with lower OHIP-14 scores and higher OHQoL-UK scores, indicating that better nutritional status was related to improved oral health-related quality of life (Table 7).

Overall findings

Overall, implant-supported prosthetic rehabilitation—particularly implant-supported fixed prostheses—was associated with significantly more favorable oral health-related quality of life, while differences in nutritional status were observed compared with conventional complete dentures.

Discussion

The present study demonstrated significant associations between prosthetic rehabilitation type, oral health-related quality of life, and nutritional status in completely edentulous patients. In particular, implant-supported prostheses—especially implant-supported fixed prostheses— were associated with more favorable oral health-related quality of life outcomes, while differences in nutritional status were also observed with conventional complete dentures.

These findings are consistent with previous studies reporting that implant-supported prostheses are associated with improved chewing ability and patient satisfaction.

Edentulism remains an important oral health problem among edentulous adults and has been associated with impaired oral function, reduced chewing ability, and decreased quality of life [1–3]. Conventional complete dentures have long been considered the standard treatment modality for edentulous patients; however, their limitations in retention, stability, and masticatory efficiency may negatively affect patient satisfaction and daily functioning [4, 5]. These functional limitations may subsequently influence nutritional intake and general well-being.

In the present study, patients rehabilitated with conventional complete dentures exhibited significantly higher OHIP-14 scores compared with patients treated with implant-supported prostheses, indicating poorer oral health-related quality of life. These findings are consistent with previous studies reporting that implant-supported prostheses are associated with improved patient satisfaction, masticatory performance, and oral health-related quality of life in edentulous individuals [7–9, 17]. Improved prosthesis stability may contribute to better perceived chewing efficiency and comfort during daily oral functions.

Among the prosthetic treatment groups evaluated in this study, implant-supported fixed prostheses demonstrated the lowest OHIP-14 scores, indicating the best oral health-related quality of life outcomes. This finding supports the concept that increased prosthesis stability and improved functional performance contribute to enhanced patient-reported outcomes. Implant-supported prostheses may also reduce common problems associated with conventional dentures, such as prosthesis movement, discomfort during mastication, and difficulties in speech [18–20].

In addition to oral health-related quality of life, the present study also evaluated patient outcomes using the OHQoL-UK instrument. Similar to the OHIP-14 findings, patients rehabilitated with implant-supported prostheses demonstrated more favorable OHQoL-UK scores compared with conventional denture users. These results highlight the multidimensional impact of prosthetic rehabilitation on physical, psychological, and social aspects of patient well-being [12, 15].

Another important finding of this study is the association between prosthetic rehabilitation and nutritional status. Nutritional assessment using the Mini Nutritional Assessment–Short Form (MNA-SF) revealed that patients treated with implant-supported prostheses showed a higher proportion of normal nutritional status compared with individuals wearing conventional complete dentures. Improved chewing ability and masticatory efficiency associated with implant-supported prostheses may facilitate the consumption of a more diverse and nutritionally balanced diet [20, 21]. These findings are supported by previous studies demonstrating improvements in masticatory performance, bite force, and patient satisfaction following implant-supported prosthetic rehabilitation [22–27]. Nutritional status is a multifactorial outcome influenced by various factors including age, socioeconomic status, systemic health conditions, and dietary habits. Therefore, the relationship between prosthetic rehabilitation and nutritional status should be interpreted with caution [22–27].

The adjusted analyses performed in the present study further demonstrated that the observed associations between prosthetic rehabilitation type and oral health-related quality of life remained significant after controlling for age, gender, education level, and income level. In contrast, the association between prosthetic rehabilitation type and nutritional status appeared to be attenuated after adjustment, indicating that nutritional outcomes are likely influenced by a broader combination of demographic, socioeconomic, systemic, and behavioral factors. Therefore, nutritional findings should be interpreted cautiously and should not be attributed solely to prosthetic rehabilitation modality.

Previous studies have suggested that impaired chewing ability may lead edentulous individuals to avoid harder foods such as fruits, vegetables, and protein-rich products [13, 21]. Consequently, conventional denture wearers may be at greater risk of nutritional deficiencies. The present findings suggest a possible association between improved chewing ability and more favorable nutritional outcomes [28–30].

Socioeconomic factors also appeared to influence oral health-related quality of life in the present study. Lower educational level and lower income were associated with significantly higher OHIP-14 scores, indicating poorer oral health-related quality of life. These findings are consistent with previous research suggesting that socioeconomic status may influence both oral health conditions and access to dental care [2, 3]. The observed differences between prosthetic groups may be partially influenced by confounding factors such as age and socioeconomic status, rather than prosthetic type alone. Therefore, the observed differences should not be attributed solely to prosthetic type, but rather interpreted within a broader clinical and demographic context.

The relatively wide age range of the study population may have introduced heterogeneity, and the findings should be interpreted with caution, particularly when considering age-related differences between prosthetic groups. Age also demonstrated a weak but statistically significant correlation with oral health-related quality of life, with increasing age associated with higher OHIP-14 scores. This finding may reflect the cumulative impact of systemic health conditions, oral functional limitations, and age-related physiological changes [20].

From a geriatric health perspective, improved chewing ability may contribute not only to better dietary intake but also to reduced frailty risk and improved systemic health outcomes among edentulous adults [29, 30]. Consequently, prosthetic rehabilitation strategies should be considered as part of a broader multidisciplinary approach to healthy aging and geriatric care.

Despite these important findings, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to establish causal relationships between prosthetic rehabilitation and patient outcomes. Second, nutritional status was evaluated using a screening instrument rather than detailed dietary intake assessment. Third, objective functional measurements such as masticatory performance and bite force were not evaluated. Although adjusted multivariate analyses were performed, residual confounding related to demographic and socioeconomic differences between groups may still have influenced the observed outcomes. In addition, the relatively wide age range and gender imbalance of the study population may have contributed to heterogeneity among the prosthetic groups. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted cautiously. Future longitudinal studies incorporating objective functional assessments, dietary analyses, and prospective follow-up are needed to further clarify the relationship between prosthetic rehabilitation, nutritional status, and oral health-related quality of life.

Nevertheless, the present study provides valuable clinical evidence regarding the broader impact of prosthetic rehabilitation in edentulous patients. By simultaneously evaluating oral health-related quality of life and nutritional status, this study offers a more comprehensive understanding of the functional and health-related benefits associated with implant-supported prosthetic treatment.

From a geriatric health perspective, improving chewing ability through appropriate prosthetic rehabilitation may contribute not only to better dietary intake but also to healthier aging and reduced frailty risk in edentulous adults [29–31].

Future longitudinal studies evaluating chewing performance, dietary intake, and long-term systemic health outcomes would provide further insight into the role of prosthetic rehabilitation in healthy aging.

Conclusion

Within the limitations of this cross-sectional study, implant-supported prosthetic rehabilitation was associated with more favorable oral health-related quality of life outcomes in completely edentulous adults compared with conventional complete dentures. Although differences in nutritional status were observed among prosthetic rehabilitation groups, these results should be interpreted cautiously because nutritional status is influenced by multiple demographic, socioeconomic, dietary, and systemic factors.

Therefore, the present findings suggest that prosthetic rehabilitation modality may represent one of several factors associated with oral health-related quality of life and nutritional outcomes in edentulous adults. Further longitudinal studies incorporating objective functional measurements and detailed dietary assessments are required to better clarify these relationships.

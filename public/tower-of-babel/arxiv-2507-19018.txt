Approximate k k -uniform states: definition, construction and applications
Kaiyi Guo, Fei Shi, You Zhou, Qi Zhao

Abstract
k k -Uniform states are fundamental to quantum information and computing, with applications in multipartite entanglement and quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs). Prior work has primarily focused on constructing exact k k -uniform states or proving their nonexistence. However, due to inevitable theoretical approximations and experimental imperfections, generating exact k k -uniform states is neither feasible nor necessary in practice. In this work, we initiate the study of approximate k k -uniform states, demonstrating that they are locally indistinguishable from their exact counterparts unless massive measurements are performed. We prove that such states can be constructed with high probability from the Haar-random ensemble and, more efficiently, via shallow random quantum circuits. Furthermore, we establish a connection between approximate k k -uniform states and approximate QECCs, showing that Haar random constructions yield high-performance codes with linear rates, vanishing proximity, and exponentially small failure probability while random circuits can’t construct codes with linear code rate in shallow depth. Finally, we investigate the relationship between approximate QECCs and approximate quantum information masking. Our work lays the foundation for the practical application of k k -uniform states.

\alpha n
-uniform state. We use

II.2.1
Proof of Average Purity

Define the purity function for some subsystem
A\subseteq[n]
as
f_{A}(\psi):=\mathop{\rm Tr}{\rho_{A}^{2}}
, and we denote
B:=A^{c}
so that
A,B
is a bipartition of the total system. We can calculate the Haar average of this function:

Lemma 3

If a circuit ensemble
\mu
is a
2
-design on the composite system
AB
, then the average subsystem purity with respect to
\mu

\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\mu}[f_{A}(\psi)]=\frac{d_{A}+d_{B}}{d_{A}d_{B}+1},

(22)

where
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
is the dimension of subsystem A and B respectively.

Since the Haar measure can be considered as a
\infty
-design,this lemma surely applies to Haar random measure. The lemma can also be found in the related literatures on Haar measure
Mele 2024

Proof.

Using the results of Haar-random expectation on
D:=d_{A}d_{B}
dimensional space with moment 2:

\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\mu}[U^{\otimes 2}OU^{\dagger\otimes 2}]=c_{\mathbb{I}}\mathbb{I}+c_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{F},

(23)

where the
\mathbb{I}
and
\mathbb{F}
are identity and exchange operators respectively. The coefficients
c_{\mathbb{I}},c_{\mathbb{F}}
are given by

\displaystyle c_{\mathbb{I}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}O-\frac{1}{D}\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbb{F}O)}{D^{2}-1};

(24)

\displaystyle c_{\mathbb{F}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbb{F}O)-\frac{1}{D}\mathrm{Tr}O}{D^{2}-1}.

(25)

Using the property of 2-design, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\mu}[f_{A}(\psi)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{U}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(U^{\otimes 2}(|0\rangle\langle 0|_{A}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{B})^{\otimes 2}U^{{\dagger}\otimes 2})(\mathbb{F}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\right]

(26)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\frac{1-\frac{1}{D}}{D^{2}-1}(\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F})(\mathbb{F}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D(D+1)}\mathrm{Tr}[\mathbb{F}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}+\mathbb{I}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{B}]

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{A}d_{B}^{2}+d_{B}^{2}d_{A}}{d_{A}d_{B}(d_{A}d_{B}+1)}=\frac{d_{A}+d_{B}}{d_{A}d_{B}+1},

where we denoted
\mathbb{I}_{S},\mathbb{F}_{S}
as the identity and exchange operator on the two copies of subsystem
S
, and since the total system is composed from
A,B
, we have
\mathbb{I}=\mathbb{I}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}
,
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{B}
.

II.2.2
Proof of Upper Bound on Lipschitz Constant

Next we find an upper bound for the Lipschitz constant of purity function:

Lemma 4

The Lipschitz constant of purity function is upper bounded by

Proof.

Using Lemma III.8 in

\displaystyle=g^{2}(\varphi)-g^{2}(\psi)

\displaystyle=(g(\varphi)-g(\psi))(g(\varphi)+g(\psi))

\displaystyle\leq 2(g(\varphi)-g(\psi))

\displaystyle\leq 4|||\varphi\rangle-|\psi\rangle||_{2},

where we have used the result that
g(\varphi)\leq 1
. So the Lipschitz constant is upper bounded by
4
.

(48)

II.4
Approximate QECCs

In this section we propose a definition of approximate QECCs based on approximate
k
-uniform states.

We first introduce the notion of QECC. Let
\{e_{j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{d^{2}}}
(
e_{0}=I_{d}
) be an orthogonal basis on linear operators acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, i.e.,
\mathop{\rm Tr}(e_{i}^{\dagger}e_{j})=\delta_{i,j}d
. Then

{\cal E}:=\{E_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes e_{\alpha_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{\alpha_{n}}\mid\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{d^{2}}^{n}\}
is the error ensemble on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
. Every error
E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}
has norm
||E_{\alpha}||=\sqrt{\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\dagger}E_{\alpha}})=\sqrt{d^{n}}
. For an error
E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}
, the support of
E_{\alpha}
is defined as
\mathrm{supp}(E_{\alpha}):=\{i\mid e_{\alpha_{i}}\neq I_{d},\,1\leq i\leq n\}
, and the weight of
E_{\alpha}
is defined as
\mathrm{wt}(E_{\alpha}):=|\mathrm{supp}(E_{\alpha})|
.

Since
\mathcal{E}
forms a complete basis of linear operators acting on the space, we can expand any linear error operator
E\in\mathcal{L}((\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n})
as the linear combination of
E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}
:
E=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{d^{2}}^{2}}c_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}
, where
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
are the coefficients. We define the support of a linear operator
E
to be the union of the support of all its expansion basis with non-zero coefficients:

\mathrm{supp}(E):=\bigcup_{\alpha,c_{\alpha}\neq 0}\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})
, and the weight of
E
being
\mathrm{wt}(E):=|\mathop{\rm supp}(E)|
.
A QECC on
n
qudits with local dimension
d
and code distance
\delta
, denoted as
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
, is a
K
-dimensional subspace
\cal C
of
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
satisfying that: for all states
|\psi\rangle\in\cal C
, and all errors
E_{\alpha}\in\cal E
with
\mathrm{wt}(E_{\alpha})<\delta
,

\langle\psi|E_{\alpha}|\psi\rangle=C(E_{\alpha}),

(49)

where
C(E_{\alpha})
is a constant independent of
|\psi\rangle
. Moreover, a QECC is called
pure
if
C(E_{\alpha})=\frac{\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha})}{d^{n}}
. Note that, a subspace
{\cal C}
of
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
is a pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC if and only if each pure state of
{\cal C}
is a
(\delta-1)
-uniform state
Shi et al. 2021
;
Huber and Grassl 2020
.

The condition for perfect error correction is that the entanglement of logical qubits with other system can be restored perfectly. The approximate error correction (AQEC) can be considered in this framework as the entanglement entropy between reference system and environment being small
Schumacher and Westmoreland 2002
. There are many works on understanding the properties of AQEC
Leung et al. 1997
;
Liu and Zhou 2023
;
Yi et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2025
. In this work, relating to approximate
k
-uniform states, we give the following definition for approximate QECCs.

Definition 3

A
K
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{C}

of
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
is called an
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC if for all states
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}
, and for any linear error operator
E
with
\mathrm{wt}(E)<\delta
and norm
||E||=\sqrt{d^{n}}
(suppose
S=\mathrm{supp}(E)
),
we have

\left|\left<\psi|E|\psi\right>-\mathrm{Tr}\left(E\frac{P}{K}\right)\right|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{d_{S}}.

(50)

Furthermore, the
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC is called pure if

\left|\left<\psi|E|\psi\right>-\frac{1}{d_{S}}\mathrm{Tr}(E_{S})\right|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{d_{S}},

(51)

where
E_{S}
is the part of the operator that acts on subsystem S:
E=E_{S}\otimes I_{S^{c}}
.

When
\epsilon=0
, an
\epsilon
-approximate (respectively, pure)
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC must be an exact (respectively, pure)
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC.
Note that one-dimensional
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,1,\delta))_{d}
QECCs refer only to
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,1,\delta))_{d}
QECCs. Based on the connection between QECCs and
k
-uniform states
Shi et al. 2021
;
Huber and Grassl 2020
, we also build the connection between approximate QECCs and approximate
k
-uniform states.

Theorem 4

A subspace
{\cal C}
of
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC if and only if each pure state of
{\cal C}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate
(\delta-1)
-uniform state. In particular, an
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,1,k+1))_{d}
QECC is equivalent to an
\epsilon
-approximate
k
-uniform state.

Proof.

First, we prove the necessity. By the definition of approximate pure QECC, each state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}
satisfies that for any linear error operator
E
with
\mathrm{wt}(E)<\delta
and norm
||E||=\sqrt{d^{n}}
, we denote
S:=\mathrm{supp}(E)
, and we have

\left|\left<\psi|E|\psi\right>-\frac{1}{d_{S}}\mathrm{Tr}(E_{S})\right|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{d_{S}}.

(52)

where
E=E_{S}\otimes I_{S^{c}}
with norm
||E_{S}||=\sqrt{d_{S}}
.
Suppose the reduction of
|\psi\rangle
on subsystem
S
can be represented as

|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}=\frac{1}{d_{S}}(I_{S}+\tilde{P}_{S}),

(53)

then

\left<\psi|E|\psi\right>-\frac{1}{d_{S}}\mathrm{Tr}(E_{S})=\mathrm{Tr}\left[E_{S}\left(|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|_{S}-\frac{1}{d_{S}}I_{S}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{d_{S}}\mathrm{Tr}[E_{S}\tilde{P}_{S}].

(54)

According to Eq. (
52
) and Eq. (
54
), we have

\left|\mathrm{Tr}[E_{S}\tilde{P}_{S}]\right|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{d_{S}^{3}}.

(55)

Note that this is true for
any

E
with support on
S
. Since
|\mathop{\rm Tr}[E_{S}\tilde{P}_{S}]|\leq||E_{S}||\cdot||\tilde{P}_{S}||=\sqrt{d_{S}}||\tilde{P}_{S}||
with equal sign gained when
\tilde{P}_{S}=kE_{S}^{\dagger}
for some constant
k
. So we have
||\tilde{P}_{S}||\leq d_{S}\epsilon
, and from our equivalent definition (
ii
) of
\epsilon
-approximate
k
-uniform state,
|\psi\rangle
is an
\epsilon
-approximate
(\delta-1)
-uniform state. The sufficiency can be easily derived by reversing the above proof.

To detect the distance of a QECC, it is convenient to use quantum weight enumerators. The
Shor-Laflamme weight enumerators
are first introduced in

Shor and Laflamme 1997
:

\displaystyle A_{j}(M_{1},M_{2})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}\\
\mathrm{wt}(E_{\alpha})=j\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Tr}(E_{\alpha}M_{1})\mathrm{Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\dagger}M_{2});

(56)

\displaystyle B_{j}(M_{1},M_{2})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}\\
\mathrm{wt}(E_{\alpha})=j\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Tr}(E_{\alpha}M_{1}E^{\dagger}_{\alpha}M_{2}).

Later, Rains
Rains 1999
;
Rains 1998
introduced the
unitary weight enumerators
:

\displaystyle A_{j}^{\prime}(M_{1},M_{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq[n]\\
|T|=j\end{subarray}}A_{T}(M_{1},M_{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq[n]\\
|T|=j\end{subarray}}\mathop{\rm Tr}[(M_{1})_{T}(M_{2})_{T}];

(57)

\displaystyle B_{j}^{\prime}(M_{1},M_{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq[n]\\
|T|=j\end{subarray}}B_{T}(M_{1},M_{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq[n]\\
|T|=j\end{subarray}}\mathop{\rm Tr}[(M_{1})_{T^{c}}(M_{2})_{T^{c}}].

These unitary weight enumerators are linearly related to Shor-Laflamme enumerators and both can be used to characterize the distance of QECCs
Rains 1998
. More specifically, suppose
P
is the projector of a
K
-dimensional QECC, and
\tilde{P}=\frac{P}{K}
is the normalized projector. Then the QECC is a
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC if and only if
KB_{j}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})-A_{j}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})=0
for
0<j<\delta
, or
KB_{\delta-1}^{\prime}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})-A^{\prime}_{\delta-1}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})=0
, and the code is pure if and only if
B_{j}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})=A_{j}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})=0
for
0<j<\delta
, or
KB_{\delta-1}^{\prime}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})=A_{\delta-1}^{\prime}(\tilde{P},\tilde{P})={n\choose\delta-1}d^{1-\delta}
. Moreover, these weight enumerators can be evaluated efficiently from some specially designed quantum circuits
Miller et al. 2024
;
Shi et al. 2024
.

Next we develop bounds of the quantum weight enumerators for approximate QECCs: we will use the Rains unitary enumerators
A_{S}^{\prime}
and
B_{S}^{\prime}
. It is already known that
KB^{\prime}_{i}(P,P)-A_{i}^{\prime}(P,P)\geq 0
for any projector
P
, and
KB_{\delta-1}’(P,P)-A_{\delta-1}^{\prime}(P,P)=0
for exact
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC. We can also give an upper bound of this quantity for the approximate case. Now consider the code space average
\mathbb{E}_{v\in\mathcal{C}}|\mathrm{Tr}[(U_{S}\otimes I_{S^{c}})vv^{\dagger}]-\mathrm{Tr}[(U_{S}\otimes I_{S^{c}})\frac{P}{K}]|^{2}
, where
P
is the projector onto code space
\mathcal{C}
with dimension
K
,
v\in\mathcal{C}
is a randomly selected vector in code space, and
U_{S}
is a unitary operator on subsystem
S\subseteq[n]
. Suppose
O
be any operator, we have the following relation
Rains 1999
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{v\in\mathcal{C}}\left[\left|\mathrm{Tr}(Ovv^{\dagger})-\frac{1}{K}\mathrm{Tr}(OP)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{v\in\mathcal{C}}(|\left<v|O|v\right>|^{2})-\frac{1}{K^{2}}|\mathrm{Tr}(OP)|^{2}

(58)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K(K+1)}[|\mathrm{Tr}(OP)|^{2}+\mathrm{Tr}(OPO^{\dagger}P)]-\frac{1}{K^{2}}|\mathrm{Tr}(OP)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K^{2}(K+1)}(K\mathrm{Tr}(OPO^{\dagger}P)-\mathrm{Tr}(OP)\mathrm{Tr}(O^{\dagger}P)).

Replacing the operator
O
with a unitary
U^{S}:=U_{S}\otimes I_{S^{c}}
, where
U_{S}
is a unitary acting on subsystem
S
,

\mathbb{E}_{v\in\mathcal{C}}\left|\mathrm{Tr}(U^{S}vv^{\dagger})-\mathrm{Tr}\left(U^{S}\frac{P}{K}\right)\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{K^{2}(K+1)}\left(K\mathrm{Tr}(U^{S}PU^{S\dagger}P)-\mathrm{Tr}\left(U^{S}P\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left(U^{S\dagger}P\right)\right).

(59)

Taking average over
U_{S}
on both sides, using the positivity of left hand side, we have thus showed that
KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)\geq 0
:

\mathbb{E}_{v\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{E}_{U^{S}}\left|\mathrm{Tr}(U^{S}vv^{\dagger})-\mathrm{Tr}\left(U^{S}\frac{P}{K}\right)\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{K^{2}(K+1)}(KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P))\geq 0.

(60)

Using Eq. (
60
), it is possible to give an upper bound on
KB^{\prime}_{S}(P,P)-A^{\prime}_{S}(P,P)
in the approximate QECCs.

Theorem 5

If the subspace
\mathcal{C}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC with projector
P
, then for any subsystem
S
with
|S|<\delta
, the quantity
KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)
is upper bounded by

KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)\leq K^{2}(K+1)\epsilon^{2}{d_{S}},

(61)

and also for any
i<\delta
,

KB_{i}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{i}^{\prime}(P,P)\leq{n\choose i}K^{2}(K+1)\epsilon^{2}d^{i}.

(62)

Proof.

According to Definition
3
, if
\mathcal{C}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC, and
P
is a projector onto code space
\mathcal{C}
and
v\in\mathcal{C}
is a codeword,
\left|\mathrm{Tr}(Evv^{\dagger})-\frac{1}{K}\mathop{\rm Tr}(EP)\right|^{2}
is upper bounded by
\epsilon^{2}d_{S}
as long as
\mathrm{wt}(E)<\delta
. Any unitary operator
U
with support
\mathrm{supp}(U)\subseteq S
satisfies this condition. Taking Haar average over error operators ranging unitary group of subsystem
S
, and code space
\mathcal{C}
, and using Eq. (
60
), we get the upper bound for
KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)
:

KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)=K^{2}(K+1)\mathbb{E}_{v\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{E}_{U^{S}}\left|\mathop{\rm Tr}(U^{S}vv^{\dagger})-\mathop{\rm Tr}\left(U^{S}\frac{P}{K}\right)\right|^{2}\leq K^{2}(K+1)\epsilon^{2}d_{S}.

(63)

The
KB_{i}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{i}^{\prime}(P,P)
corresponds to the summation over all possible subsystem with size
i
, which is upper bounded by
{n\choose i}
times the upper bound of every single
KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)
with
|S|=i
:

KB_{i}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{i}^{\prime}(P,P)\leq{n\choose i}K^{2}(K+1)\epsilon^{2}d^{i}.

(64)

If the approximate QECC is pure, it is possible to give bounds on weight enumerators respectively:

Theorem 6

For a pure
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC with projector
P
, if
|S|<\delta
, then

\frac{K^{2}}{d_{S}}\leq A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)\leq\frac{K^{2}}{d_{S}}+K^{2}\epsilon^{2},

(65)

and

\frac{K}{d_{S}}\leq B_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)\leq K\left[\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}\left(1+({K+1}){d_{S}}\right)\right].

(66)

Proof.

Select a set of orthonormal states
|\psi_{i}\rangle(i=1,\cdots,K)
in code space
\mathcal{C}
. By definition, the projector
P
can be expressed as

P=\sum_{i=1}^{K}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|.

(67)

Using Theorem
4
, all
|\psi_{i}\rangle
are approximate
\delta-1
-uniform states, and thus the reduction on
S
can be expressed as
|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{S}=\frac{I_{S}+(\tilde{P}_{i})_{S}}{d_{S}}
with
||(\tilde{P}_{i})_{S}||\leq\epsilon
, then the reduction on subsystem
S
of projector
P
can be written as

\displaystyle P_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{1}{d_{S}}(I_{S}+(\tilde{P}_{i})_{S})

(68)

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{d_{S}}(I_{S}+\tilde{P}_{S}),

where
\tilde{P}_{S}:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{1}{K}(\tilde{P}_{i})_{S}
. Note that
||\tilde{P}_{S}||\leq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}||(\tilde{P}_{i})_{S}||\leq d_{S}\epsilon
, and
\mathop{\rm Tr}\tilde{P}_{S}=0
. Then

\frac{K^{2}}{d_{S}}\leq A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)=\mathrm{Tr}P_{S}^{2}=\frac{K^{2}}{d_{S}^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}(I_{S}+\tilde{P}_{S}^{2})\leq\frac{K^{2}}{d_{S}}+K^{2}\epsilon^{2}.

(69)

Using inequality in Theorem
5
, and
KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)-A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)\geq 0
, we complete the proof:

\frac{K^{2}}{d_{S}}\leq A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)\leq KB_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)\leq A_{S}^{\prime}(P,P)+K^{2}(K+1)\epsilon^{2}d_{S}\leq K^{2}\left[\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}\left(1+(K+1)d_{S}\right)\right].

(70)

From Section
II.2
we see that a Haar random state is a approximate
k
-uniform state with high probability. Theorem
4
proves the relation between approximate
k
-uniform state and approximate QECC. It is tempting to think that a Haar random subspace is an approximate QECC. We can clarify this point by calculating the averaged weight enumerators on random subspace: the Haar expectation of
\frac{KB^{\prime}_{S}(P_{U},P_{U})-A_{S}^{\prime}(P_{U},P_{U})}{K^{2}(K+1)}
is small, where
P_{U}:=UP_{0}U^{\dagger}
is a
K
-dimensional random subspace projector:

\mathbb{E}_{U}\left[\frac{KB^{\prime}_{S}(P_{U},P_{U})-A_{S}^{\prime}(P_{U},P_{U})}{K^{2}(K+1)}\right]=\left(1-\frac{1}{K}\right)\frac{d_{S}-\frac{1}{d_{S}}}{d^{n}-\frac{1}{d^{n}}}.

(71)

The right hand side is asymptotically
(1-\frac{1}{K})d^{|S|-n}
, and vanishingly small in the large
n
limit. This implies that a “good” approximate QECC can probably be constructed from Haar random unitaries, or even shallow depth random quantum circuits, with small failure probability. Next we show that “good” approximate QECC can be constructed from a Haar random subspace.

II.5
Haar random construction of approximate QECCs

In this section, we show the random construction of approximate QECC, and prove that a random subspace is an
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC with high probability under given constraints. To prove this, we need conclusion from Theorem
4
that a subspace is an approximate QECC if and only if all the states within the subspace is an approximate uniform state. We also need to use definition and property
Hayden et al. 2006
;
Hayden et al. 2004
of an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-net of the
K
-dimensional subspace. The following lemma shows the definition of
\epsilon^{\prime}
-net, and the upper bound for the cardinality of smallest
\epsilon^{\prime}
-net:

Lemma 6
(Existence of small
\epsilon
-nets; See Lemma III.6 in
Hayden et al. 2006
or Lemma II.4 in
Hayden et al. 2004
)

For
0<\epsilon<1
and
\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{H}=K
, there exists a set
\mathcal{N}
of pure states in
\mathcal{H}
with cardinality
|\mathcal{N}|\leq(5/\epsilon)^{2K}
, such that for every pure state
|\varphi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
there exists
|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\in\mathcal{N}
such that
|||\varphi\rangle-|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle||_{2}\leq\epsilon/2
and
|||\varphi\rangle-|\tilde{\varphi}\rangle||_{1}\leq\epsilon
.

Next we propose an alternative definition of approximate pure QECC based on Theorem
4
:

Lemma 7
(Alternative definition of approximate pure QECC)

A
K
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{C}
is the code space of an
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC if and only if

\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\rm Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\leq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}

(72)

is satisfied for any subsystem
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|=\delta-1
.

Proof.

We first prove the necessity. Suppose the
\mathcal{C}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
pure QECC, then according to Theorem
4
, any state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate
\delta-1
-uniform state. According to Definition
1
, for any
S\subseteq[n]
and
|S|=\delta-1
,

\mathop{\rm Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\leq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}.

(73)

Note that this holds true for any state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}
, so by taking maximization over code space
\mathcal{C}
, we see that Eq.
72
is true for any
S\subseteq[n]
and
|S|=\delta-1
.

Next we prove the sufficiency. Suppose Eq.
72
holds true for any
S\subseteq[n]
and
|S|=\delta-1
, for any state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}
,
\mathop{\rm Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\leq\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\rm Tr}(|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi^{\prime}|_{S}^{2})\leq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}
, so any state within the subspace is an
\epsilon
-approximate
\delta-1
-uniform state. By Theorem
4

\mathcal{C}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
pure QECC.

Using lemma
7
, we can prove that a Haar random subspace is an approximate QECC with high probability:

Theorem 7

A randomly selected
K
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{C}\in_{R}\mathcal{G}_{K}(\mathcal{H})
is an
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC with probability greater than

1-\left(\frac{5}{\epsilon^{\prime}}\right)^{2K}{n\choose{\delta-1}}2\exp\left(-\frac{d^{n}\mu^{2}}{72\pi^{3}\log 2}\right)

(74)

for any
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<1
, where
\mu=\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime}-\Delta
,
\Delta:=\frac{d^{\delta-1}+d^{n-(\delta-1)}}{d^{n}+1}-\frac{1}{d^{\delta-1}}
, given the constraint that
\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime}\geq 2\Delta
.

We first do some asymptotic analysis as usual. Taking the negative logarithm to the failure probability, we have

-\log\text{Prob}(\mathrm{fail})\geq-\log 2+1-\log(n+1)-nS(\alpha)-2K\log\frac{5}{\epsilon^{\prime}}+\frac{d^{n}}{72\pi^{3}\ln 2}(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime}-\Delta)^{2},

(75)

where we used inequality on combinatorial numbers
\log{n\choose n\alpha}\leq nS(\alpha)+\log(n+1)-1
, where
\alpha=\frac{\delta-1}{n}
,
S(\alpha)=-\alpha\log\alpha-(1-\alpha)\log(1-\alpha)
.
\Delta=\Theta(d^{-n(1-\alpha)})
decays exponentially. If we take
\epsilon^{\prime}\sim\Delta
, and
\epsilon^{2}=\Omega(\Delta)
, we see that Eq. (
75
) becomes

-\log\mathrm{Prob(fail)}\geq\Theta\left(-2Kn(1-\alpha)\log d+\frac{d^{n}\epsilon^{4}}{72\pi^{3}\log 2}\right).

(76)

We can make the failure probability vanishingly small by setting
K\ll d^{n}\epsilon^{4}
. This implies that good quantum code with logical qudit number
\log K\propto n
, code distance
\delta\propto n
, with code proximity
\epsilon
and generation failure probability vanishingly small in large
n
limit is achievable from Haar random unitary.

Proof.

To prove a subspace being an
\epsilon
-approximate QECC with high probability, we can see that the approximate pure QECC with distance
\delta
is equivalent to the condition that any state in the code space is an
\epsilon
-approximate
\delta-1
-uniform state. This is equivalent to saying that
\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})<\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}
for any subsystem
S\subseteq[n]
with size
|S|=\delta-1
. We first consider the probability for one fixed subsystem
S
satisfying Eq. (
72
), with code space
\mathcal{C}
being
K
-dimensional randomly-chosen subspace in total Hilbert space (denoted as
\mathcal{C}\in_{R}\mathcal{G}_{K}(\mathcal{H})
):

\mathrm{Prob}_{\mathcal{C}\in_{R}\mathcal{G}_{K}(\mathcal{H})}\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})<\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}\right).

(77)

For any

II.6
Random Circuit Construction of Approximate QECCs

We already see that Haar random unitary is capable of generating good approximate QECC with linear code distance, linear code rate and vanishingly small proximity and failure probability in Section
II.5
; however, in this section we show that this is not true for shallow depth random circuits. It turns out that to generate good approximate QECC with
k
qudits we need circuit depth
L
growing exponentially in
k
. To prove this, we use exactly the same techniques as in Section
II.5
. The only modification is to replace the usage of Theorem
2
in Eq.
79
with Theorem
3
. The result is as follows:

Theorem 8

An
\epsilon
-approximate
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC can be generated from random 1 dimensional(1D) circuit ensemble of depth
L
with failure probability satisfying:

\gamma:=-\log\mathrm{Prob}(\mathrm{fail})=\Omega\left(-nS(\alpha)-2Kn(1-\alpha)\log d+\left(\frac{L}{\log n}\right)^{1-\theta}\frac{n}{8}\left(\log d+\frac{4}{n}\log\epsilon\right)\right)

(81)

for arbitrarily small
\theta>0
, where
\alpha:=\frac{\delta-1}{n}
, given the constraint that
\epsilon=\Omega(d^{-(n-k)/2})
.

Then we can do asymptotic analysis as follows: let
S_{d}(\alpha):=\frac{S(\alpha)}{\log d}
be the Von-neumann entropy taken at base
d
, let
\lambda:=\frac{1}{n}\log_{d}\epsilon
, the probability is rewritten as follows:

\gamma=\Omega\left(n\log d\left[-S_{d}(\alpha)-2K(1-\alpha)+\left(\frac{L}{\log n}\right)^{1-\theta}\left(\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\right)\right]\right),

(82)

we can get linear distance and vanishing proximity codes as long as
\alpha>0
and
-\frac{1}{4}<\lambda<0
. To ensure the construction probability decays exponentially in large-
n
limit, we require that for any
\theta>0

L\geq\left(\frac{S_{d}(\alpha)+2K(1-\alpha)}{\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda}\right)^{1+\theta}\log n\sim K\log n,

(83)

which implies that the circuit depth have to grow exponentially with the qudit number
k:=\log_{d}K
. This illustrates a fundamental difference between Haar random unitary and random quantum circuit with shallow depth: shallow depth circuit is not capable of generating ”good” quantum codes with constant code rate
R:=\frac{k}{n}
and linear distance
\delta=\Theta(n)
with satisfying exponentially small proximity and failure probability under large
n
limit.

Proof.

Similar to the case of proving Haar random construction of approximate QECC in Section
II.5
, we see that approximate pure QECC with distance
\delta
is equivalent to the condition that any state in the code space is an
\epsilon
-approximate
\delta-1
-uniform state. We first consider the probability for one fixed subsystem satisfying Eq. (
72
), with code space
\mathcal{C}
generated by a quantum circuit ensemble forming approximate design from any fixed
K
-dimensional subspace:

\text{Prob}_{\mathcal{C}\in\mu}\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})<\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}\right).

(84)

Take an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-net
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{C}}
in the subspace
\mathcal{C}
. Using Lemma
4
, we have

\lvert\mathrm{Tr}(|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}|_{S}^{2})-\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\rvert\leq 4\lvert\lvert|\tilde{\psi}\rangle-|\psi\rangle\rvert\rvert_{2}\leq 2\epsilon^{\prime},

(85)

so the failure probability on subsystem
S
can be upper bounded:

\displaystyle\text{Prob}_{\mathcal{C}\in\mu}\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})<\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\text{Prob}_{\mathcal{C}\in\mu}\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\in\mathcal{N_{C}}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}|_{S}^{2}\right)<\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime}\right)

(86)

\displaystyle=1-\text{Prob}_{\mathcal{C}\in\mu}\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\in\mathcal{N_{C}}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(|\tilde{\psi}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}|_{S}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-|\mathcal{N_{C}}|\text{Prob}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mu}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\text{Prob}_{\mathcal{C}\in\mu}\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}\right)

where in the first inequality of Eq. (

\epsilon^{\prime}
-net, in the second inequality of Eq. (

\mu

The total failure probability is thus lower bounded by

\displaystyle\text{Prob}(\mathrm{fail})

\displaystyle:=\text{Prob}_{\mathcal{C}\in\mu}\left(\exists S,|S|=\delta-1,\mathop{\rm max}_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}\right)

(87)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{N_{C}}|{n\choose\delta-1}\text{Prob}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mu}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{S}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{d_{S}}+\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle-\log\text{Prob}(\mathrm{fail})

\displaystyle\geq-2K\log\frac{5}{\epsilon^{\prime}}-\left(nS(\alpha)+\log(n+1)-1\right)+\Omega\left(\left(\frac{L}{\log n}\right)^{1-\theta}\frac{1}{8}\log(d^{n}(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon^{\prime})^{2})\right)

\displaystyle-\log\text{Prob}(\mathrm{fail})

where in the first inequality of Eq. (

II.7
Approximate quantum information masking

In this section we investigate approximate QIM, and show the relation between approximate QECCs and approximate QIM. QIM is the process of distributing quantum information across a bipartite system in such a way that no individual subsystem contains sufficient information to reconstruct the original quantum information
Modi et al. 2018
.
QIM can be also generalized to
k
-uniform QIM with any
k
subsystems can not recover the original quantum information
Shi et al. 2021
: an operation
\mathcal{S}
is said to
k
-uniformly mask
quantum information contained in states
\{|\varphi_{i}\rangle\mid|\varphi_{i}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{K}\}
to states
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle=\mathcal{S}(|\varphi_{i}\rangle)\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}\}
if, for all states
|\psi_{i}\rangle
and all subsystems
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|=k
,
|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|_{S}
are identical. Clearly, if a pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC exists, then it is possible to
(\delta-1)
uniformly mask all states of
\mathbb{C}^{K}
to
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
.
However,
QIM tasks under specific circumstances are proved not possible
Modi et al. 2018
;
Shi et al. 2021
, and it is not possible to make measurements to local subsystem with infinite precision due to unavoidable noise. So it is enough to relax QIM restriction to its approximate version.

Definition 4
(
\epsilon
-approximate
k
-uniform QIM)

An operation
\mathcal{S}
is said to

\epsilon
-approximate
k
-uniformly mask
quantum information contained in states
\{|\varphi_{i}\rangle\mid|\varphi_{i}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{K}\}
to states
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle=\mathcal{S}(|\varphi_{i}\rangle)\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}\}
if, for all states
|\psi_{i}\rangle,|\psi_{j}\rangle
and all subsystems
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|=k
,

|||\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{S}-|\psi_{j}\rangle\langle\psi_{j}|_{S}||\leq\epsilon.

(88)

Similar to the relationship between QECCs and QIM, we can establish a connection between approximate QECCs and approximate QIM.

Theorem 9

If an
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC exists, then it is possible to
2\epsilon
-approximate
(\delta-1)
-uniformly mask all states of
\mathbb{C}^{K}
to
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}

Proof.

According to Theorem
4
, each state of the
\epsilon
-approximate pure
((n,K,\delta))_{d}
QECC

is an
\epsilon
-approximate
(\delta-1)
-uniform state. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the isomorphism between
\mathbb{C}^{K}
and
{\cal C}

and all subsystems
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|=\delta-1
,

\displaystyle|||\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{S}-|\psi_{j}\rangle\langle\psi_{j}|_{S}||

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\frac{I_{S}+(P_{i})_{S}}{d_{S}}-\frac{I_{S}+(P_{j})_{S}}{d_{S}}\right|\right|

(89)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{S}}\left|\left|(P_{i})_{S}-(P_{j})_{S}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d_{S}}\left(\left|\left|(P_{i})_{S}\right|\right|+\left|\left|(P_{j})_{S}\right|\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon,

where we have used alternative Definition (
ii
) of
\epsilon
-approximate
k
-uniform state and triangle inequality. According to the definition of
\epsilon
-approximate QIM, the operation
2\epsilon
-approximate
(\delta-1)
-uniformly mask all states of
\mathbb{C}^{K}
to
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}

This theorem shows that using the process of encoding to approximate QECC, we can distribute quantum information to a larger physical system where the information is approximately and globally hided, so that almost no information can be gained by small number of parties. This theorem also shows strong connection between approximate QIM and approximate QECC.

IV
Summary and future directions

In this work, we defined the concept of approximate
k
-uniform states, proved that existence of approximate
k
-uniform states is similar to the existence of exact ones, and showed that random (either Haar random or shallow depth) circuits give rise to such states with very high probability under large
n
limit. Next we investigated its application to approximate QECC, relating code proximity to bounds on quantum weight enumerators, and showed the performance of random unitary and random circuit approach for constructing approximate QECC. Then we provided potential application of approximate
k
-uniform states in approximate QIM.

Our results have many benefits. The exact
k
-uniform states are mostly constructed through combinatorial methods like orthogonal arrays and Latin squares, and AME
(n,d)
states for some cases are proved to be impossible. Our generalization of
k
-uniform states to approximate cases makes some approximate AME states possible and
k
-uniform states more accessible. Our result also provides practical usage to the real-world quantum information processing tasks. Inspired by approximate
k
-uniform states, we defined approximate QECC, which relates the notion of quantum weight enumerators. The approximate QECCs are shown to be efficiently constructed from Haar random unitaries but not efficiently constructed by shallow random quantum circuits. This shows a gap between ideal random unitaries and its approximation implemented by random circuit ensemble, which leaves an interesting open question for further study.

Integral of the double-emission eikonal function for two massive emitters at an arbitrary angle Preprint: TTP25-031, P3H-25-067
Ming-Ming Long, Kirill Melnikov, Andrey Pikelner

Abstract
We present a semi-analytic calculation of the integrated double-emission eikonal function of two massive emitters whose momenta are at an arbitrary angle to each other. This result is needed for extending the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme caola:2017dug to processes with massive partons.

Calculations of a similar integral in the context of two different slicing schemes have been reported

We follow Ref.
Horstmann:2025hjp

(1)

(2)

To compute the required double-soft contributions, we have to integrate the
corresponding eikonal functions
\widetilde{S}_{ij}
and
\widetilde{I}_{ij}
over the
phase space of two unresolved partons with momenta
k_{1,2}
. Working within the
nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme
caola:2017dug
, we have to fix the reference frame, and
restrict energies of unresolved partons by introducing an upper cut-off
E_{\rm max}
.
Furthermore, energies of unresolved partons must be ordered. We call the parton
with the larger (smaller) energy
\mathfrak{m}(\mathfrak{n})
, and refer to their momenta as

k_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}
, instead of
k_{1,2}
, which describe momenta without energy
ordering.

We define the required double-emission phase-space integrals as
caola:2017dug

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}\left[\mathcal{S}_{ij}\mathcal{S}_{kl}\right]

\displaystyle=\int[\textrm{d}k_{\mathfrak{m}}][\textrm{d}k_{\mathfrak{n}}]\theta\left(E_{\rm max}-k_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}\right)\theta\left(k_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}-k_{\mathfrak{n}}^{0}\right)\mathcal{S}_{ij}(k_{\mathfrak{m}})\mathcal{S}_{kl}(k_{\mathfrak{n}}),

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}\left[\Xi_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=\int[\textrm{d}k_{\mathfrak{m}}][\textrm{d}k_{\mathfrak{n}}]\theta\left(E_{\rm max}-k_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}\right)\theta\left(k_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}-k_{\mathfrak{n}}^{0}\right)\Xi_{ij}\left(k_{\mathfrak{m}},k_{\mathfrak{n}}\right),

(17)

where the eikonal function
\Xi_{ij}
is either
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{ij}
(for

gg
emission) or
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}
(for
q\bar{q}
emission), and

[\textrm{d}k]=\frac{\textrm{d}^{d-1}{k}}{2k^{0}(2\pi)^{d-1}},

(18)

is the phase-space element. We note that
d=4-2\varepsilon
is the space-time dimension.

The auxiliary four-vector

. An identical formula applies to the product of two single eikonal functions
\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}[S_{ij}S_{kl}]

Our goal is to compute the required soft integrals for
two massive emitters.
As we already noted,
the masses of partons
i
and
j
are
m_{i,j}
, respectively.
The squares of their four-momenta
p_{i,j}
are then

p_{i,j}^{2}=m_{i,j}^{2}
.
We can choose a reference frame to integrate the eikonal function. The integral in Eq. (
19
) is boost-invariant, but a particular choice
P=(1,\vec{0})
defines the laboratory frame where both heavy partons
i
and
j
move with different velocities.
In the lab frame, the momenta
p_{i,j}
are characterized by energies or, equivalently, velocities
\beta_{i,j}
and their directions. We write

p_{i,j}=m_{i,j}\gamma_{i,j}\left(1,\beta_{i,j}\;\vec{\kappa}_{i,j}\right),

(20)

where
\gamma_{i,j}=1/\sqrt{1-\beta_{i,j}^{2}}
.

However, as we discussed in Ref.
Horstmann:2025hjp
, it is beneficial to work in a different frame where one of the heavy partons is at rest. In this frame

P=\gamma_{t}\left(1,v_{t}\vec{n}_{t}\right),\;\;\;p_{i}=m_{i}(1,\vec{0}),\;\;\;p_{j}=E_{j}\left(1,v_{ij}\vec{n}_{j}\right),

(21)

with
\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{i}
,
v_{t}=\beta_{i}
and
\vec{n}_{t}=-\vec{\kappa}_{i}
. Furthermore,
v_{ij}
is the relative velocity of partons
i
and
j
, and

E_{j}

and
\vec{n}_{j}
are the energy and the direction of a parton
j
in the rest frame of
i
.

The relation between these and the laboratory-frame quantities is easy to establish. We find

\begin{split}&E_{j}=\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{m_{i}}=m_{j}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}\left(1-\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\cos\theta_{ij}\right),\\
&v_{ij}=\sqrt{1-\frac{(1-\beta_{i}^{2})(1-\beta_{j}^{2})}{\left(1-\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\cos\theta_{ij}\right)^{2}}},\\
&\vec{n}_{t}\cdot\vec{n}_{j}=\frac{1}{v_{ij}}\left(1-\frac{1-\beta_{i}^{2}}{1-\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\cos\theta_{ij}}\right),\end{split}

(22)

where
\cos\theta_{ij}=\vec{\kappa}_{i}\cdot\vec{\kappa}_{j}
. As we will see, the integrated eikonal function depends on
\vec{n}_{t}\cdot\vec{n}_{j}
,
v_{ij}
, and
v_{t}
, and all these quantities can be expressed in terms of the parameters in the laboratory frame using Eq. (
22
).

3
Single emission and iterations

To compute the iterated part of the double-real emission function, we need to
integrate the product of two single-emission eikonal functions. The integral reads

\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}[S_{ij}S_{km}]=-\frac{1}{4\varepsilon E_{\rm max}^{4\varepsilon}}\int[{\rm d}l_{\mathfrak{m}}][{\rm d}l_{\mathfrak{n}}]\delta(1-l_{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot P)\theta(l_{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot P-l_{\mathfrak{n}}\cdot P)\;S_{ij}(l_{\mathfrak{m}})\;S_{km}(l_{\mathfrak{n}}).

(23)

The gluon momenta
l_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}
are defined as
l_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}=l_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}^{0}(1,\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}})
,
with
\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}^{2}=1
.
We calculate the integral in
Eq. (
23
)
directly in the laboratory frame, i.e.
P=(1,\vec{0})
.
Writing
l^{0}_{\mathfrak{n}}=\omega\;l_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}
and integrating over
\omega
,
we find

\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}[S_{ij}S_{km}]=\frac{N_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{8\varepsilon^{2}E_{\rm max}^{4\varepsilon}}\left\langle\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}\;\left\langle\frac{\rho_{km}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{m\mathfrak{n}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}},

(24)

where
\rho_{xy}=1-\vec{\beta}_{x}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{y}
, with
\vec{\beta}_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}=\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}
.
Furthermore, we use

N_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\Omega^{(d-1)}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}},

(25)

and

\Big\langle\dots\Big\rangle_{x}=\int\frac{{\rm d}\Omega^{(d-1)}_{x}}{\Omega^{(d-1)}}\cdots,

(26)

is the integral over directions of the parton
x
.

Using integrals defined in Appendix
B
, we write Eq. (
24
) as

\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}[S_{ij}S_{km}]=\frac{N_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{8\varepsilon^{2}E_{\rm max}^{4\varepsilon}}\;I_{11}^{(2)}[\rho_{ii},\rho_{jj},\rho_{ij}]\;I_{11}^{(2)}\left[\rho_{kk},\rho_{mm},\rho_{km}\right].

(27)

Integrals
I^{(2)}_{11}

cannot be computed in closed form for arbitrary
d
and require an expansion in
\varepsilon
.
The depth of the required expansion depends on
whether lines
i,j,k,m
are massive or massless. If all lines are massive, the integrals are finite; hence, each of them needs to be expanded to
{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{2})
. If, on the other hand, there are one or more massless partons, additional singularity is generated
by the angular integration,
and
the angular integral for massive lines through

{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{3})
is needed.

We note in passing that these very deep expansions may not be necessary. Indeed, for the purpose of subtractions, the iterative piece
will have to be combined with an iteration of Catani’s
I^{(1)}
operator
Catani:1996jh
;
Catani:2002hc
, appearing in the virtual corrections; for the massless case, this mechanism was explained in Ref.
devoto:2023rpv
.
Similarly, also for the massive case it should lead to
a cancellation of the highest

1/\varepsilon^{2}
singularities, without destroying the factorized form as in Eq. (
27
). Because of this, the required depth of the
\varepsilon
-expansion will be reduced.

4
Non-iterative double-real emission contribution

We consider the integral of the correlated contribution to
the double-real eikonal function, defined
in Eq. (
19
). We will compute it in the rest frame of the parton
i
. Using the expression for
P
and other momenta in the rest frame
of
i

2
,
and integrating over the energy of the gluon
\mathfrak{m}
, we find

\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}[\Xi_{ij}]=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})\left[\omega^{2}\Xi_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(28)

where
\rho_{tx}=1-v_{t}\vec{n}_{t}\cdot\vec{n}_{x}
,
x=\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}
,

\mathcal{N}_{A}=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{E_{\rm max}}{\gamma_{t}}\right)^{-4\varepsilon},

(29)

and the momenta of gluons
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
to be used in
Eq. (
28
) are

l_{\mathfrak{m}}=(1,\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}})
and
l_{\mathfrak{n}}=\omega(1,\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{n}})
.

Eq. (
28
) is a convenient starting point for the computation. We will focus on the calculation of the gluon eikonal function since it is more general than the quark one. Hence, we identify
\Xi_{ij}
with
\widetilde{S}_{ij}
, c.f. Eq. (
14
); we will refer to
\mathcal{S}{\hskip-5.0pt}\mathcal{S}[\widetilde{S}_{ij}]
as
G_{ij}
.
The integral in Eq. (
28
) cannot be computed numerically
right away because of divergences. For two massive emitters, these divergences appear in just two cases – i) when the gluon
\mathfrak{n}
becomes soft,
\omega\to 0
, and
ii) when the gluons
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
become collinear to each other. We will iteratively subtract
these singularities from the integrand in Eq. (
28
) to construct a finite quantity that can be calculated numerically.
However, we stress that because of the overall

1/\varepsilon
factor in Eq. (
28
), it is highly non-trivial to compute all
1/\varepsilon
poles analytically even if all sources of divergences have been removed from the integrand in that equation.

We start with the soft subtraction and write

G_{ij}={\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G_{ij}].

(30)

The first term corresponds to the strongly-ordered limit of
G_{ij}
,

{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\;\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}\;\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})\;S_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(31)

where the operator
S_{\omega}
extracts the leading
{\cal O}(1/\omega^{2})
singularity from the eikonal function
\widetilde{S}_{ij}
.
We will discuss the computation of this quantity in the next section.

The second term

\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(32)

involves the operator
\overline{S}_{\omega}=1-S_{\omega}
. Hence,
it does not possess a soft singularity since it is explicitly subtracted. It remains to isolate and remove the collinear
\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}
singularity from it. While such a subtraction is
straightforward, we need to do it in such a way, that all divergent contributions can be computed analytically.

To this end, we found it convenient to write
\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]
as the sum of two terms

\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]=\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G^{(0)}_{ij}]+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}].

(33)

The first term reads

\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G^{(0)}_{ij}]=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\;\theta(1-\omega)\;\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(34)

It corresponds to the soft-subtracted
integral in case when the laboratory frame and the rest frame of the
parton
i
coincide. The function
\widetilde{S}_{ij}
in this case depends on a single direction, which makes the integration much simpler.

The second quantity

\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]

reads

\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\;f(\omega,\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})\;\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(35)

where

f(\omega,\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})=\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega).

(36)

The integral in Eq. (
35
) is divergent because of the collinear
\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}
singularity of the integrand.
However, as we will see shortly
this divergence is softer than the divergence of the
quantity
\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]
.

To subtract the
\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}
divergence,
we write

\overline{S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]
as the sum of two terms

\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]=\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin},

(37)

where

\begin{split}&\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\;C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\;f(\omega,\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})\;\overline{S}_{\omega}\;\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},\\
&\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\;\overline{C}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\;f(\omega,\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})\;\overline{S}_{\omega}\;\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.\end{split}

(38)

The operator
\overline{C}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}
is
defined as
\overline{C}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}=1-C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}
.
The operator

C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}
extracts the non-integrable part of the
\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}
collinear limit of the integrand, but it does not act on the angular phase space. Since

C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}f(\omega,\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})=(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)\;\theta(1-\omega)\;C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(39)

we find

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)\;\theta(1-\omega)\;C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\;\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(40)

To simplify

\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin}
, we split
\overline{C}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}f(\omega,\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})
into two terms

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\left[\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega)\right]

\displaystyle=\overline{C}_{a}+\overline{C}_{b},

(41)

using the following decomposition
\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}=1+(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)
.
The two terms are defined as follows

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{a}

\displaystyle=\overline{C}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\left[\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega)\right]=\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega),

(42)

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{b}

\displaystyle=\overline{C}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\left[(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})\right]=(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)\left[\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega)C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\right].

We then write

\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin}=\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a}+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,b},

(43)

where

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\left(\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega)\right)\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(44)

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,b}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\overline{C}_{b}\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

The quantity
\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,b}
does not require further manipulations since its integrand is
{\cal O}(\varepsilon)
, c.f. Eq. (
42
); hence, we compute it numerically.

The quantity
\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a}
is also finite, but its
integrand is not suppressed by
\varepsilon
. Therefore, because of the
1/\varepsilon
prefactor, it needs to be expanded to linear order in

\varepsilon
. To facilitate such an expansion, we make use of the following identity

\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\Bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\rightarrow\frac{1}{\omega}\\
\mathfrak{m}\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{n}\end{subarray}}-\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]=\Delta_{ij},

(45)

where

\Delta_{ij}=\frac{\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}-\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}\right)\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}-\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}-\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}\right)}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}+\left(1-v_{ij}^{2}\right)\frac{\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}-\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}\right)\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}+\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}-\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\right)}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}^{2}}.

(46)

It follows that

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a}=\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a_{1}}+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a_{2}},

(47)

where

\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a_{1}}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{2\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1-2\varepsilon}}(\theta(\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}-\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}})-\theta(\omega-1))\Delta_{ij}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(48)

and

\begin{split}\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a_{2}}=&\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega}\;\frac{\omega^{-2\varepsilon}-\omega^{2\varepsilon}}{2}\right.\\
&\left.\times\left[\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega)\right]\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.\end{split}

(49)

The integrand of the latter quantity is
{\cal O}(\varepsilon)
,
so that it can be computed numerically without further ado.

Putting everything together, we write
G_{ij}
as

G_{ij}={\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G^{(0)}_{ij}]+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}+\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a_{1}}+\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathrm{num}},

(50)

where four first terms on the right-hand side require
further work, and the last term can be computed numerically by taking the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit. It reads

\displaystyle\overline{S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathrm{num}}=\overline{S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a_{2}}+\overline{S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,b}

(51)

\displaystyle=4\mathcal{N}_{A}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega}\ln\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}\left[\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega)C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\right]\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}

\displaystyle-2\mathcal{N}_{A}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega}\ln\omega\left[\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})-\theta(1-\omega)\right]\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

We note that the four terms that require additional work have different degrees of divergence,
that we illustrate below

\begin{split}&{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]\sim{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{-3}),\;\;\;\;\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[G^{(0)}_{ij}]\sim{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{-2}),\\
&\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}\sim{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{-1}),\;\;\;\;\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\rm fin,a_{1}}\sim{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{-1}).\end{split}

Hence, the most challenging quantity to
deal with is the strongly-ordered
contribution
{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]
. We discuss it in the next section.

4.1
The integral of the strongly-ordered eikonal function
{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]

In this section, we discuss the integration of the strongly-ordered eikonal function
S_{\omega}[G_{ij}]
,

{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})S_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(52)

For the two-gluon case, we find

\displaystyle S_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}+\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}

(53)

\displaystyle+(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right).

The integration over
\omega
is straightforward

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}\theta(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}-\omega\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}})=-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon},

(54)

and we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\langle\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}+\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right.

(55)

\displaystyle\left.+(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right)\right]\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

This expression involves terms with and without
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}
,
and we find it convenient to separate them. Hence, we write

{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]={\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{F}}+{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{NF}}.

(56)

The first term does not contain
1/\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}
; it reads

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{F}}=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\langle\frac{\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}\Bigg\langle\;\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}\left[1-\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}+\frac{1-v_{ij}^{2}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}}\right]\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(57)

The angular integrations are actually finite,
so that
{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\rm F}\sim\varepsilon^{-2}
.
Using integrals defined in the
appendix, we derive

S_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{F}}=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\;I_{-2\varepsilon,1}^{(2)}\left[I_{-2\varepsilon}^{(1)}\left[\rho_{tt}\right]-2I_{-2\varepsilon,1}^{(2)}+(1-v_{ij}^{2})I_{-2\varepsilon,2}^{(2)}\right].

(58)

We note that unless their arguments are shown explicitly, all integrals in the above
formula should be interpreted as
I[\rho_{tt},\rho_{jj},\rho_{tj}]
.
4
4

4

To avoid
confusion, we note that in this section we always work in the rest frame of the parton
i
. This implies that

\rho_{tt}=1-v_{t}^{2}
,

\rho_{jj}=1-v_{ij}^{2},\rho_{tj}=1-v_{ij}v_{t}\vec{n}_{t}\cdot\vec{n}_{j}
.
The relation of various quantities in the above equations to their counterparts in the laboratory frame is given in Eq. (
22
).

The remaining,

non-factorizable
, part is more complicated. It reads

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{NF}}=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\langle\frac{\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}+\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-1-\frac{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right.

(59)

\displaystyle\left.-(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right)\right]\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

To simplify Eq. (
59
), we use an opportunity to rename
\mathfrak{m}\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{n}
, rewriting it in the following way

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{NF}}=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\langle\frac{\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}\left[\frac{4-\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}-1-(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right)\right]\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(60)

Furthermore, we find it convenient to write

\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}+\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{4\varepsilon}}{2}-\frac{\left(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}-\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon}\right)^{2}}{2}.

(61)

The last term on the right-hand side
in Eq. (
61
) provides a finite contribution to
S_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\rm NF}
, which
can be computed numerically right away. Hence, we define

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\rm NF}^{\rm num}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\langle\frac{\left(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{2\varepsilon}-\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{2\varepsilon}\right)^{2}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}\left[\frac{4-\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}-1-(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right)\right]\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(62)

The remaining
contribution reads

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\rm NF}^{\rm div}=-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\langle\frac{\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}+\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{4\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}\left[\frac{4-\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}-1-(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right)\right]\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(63)

Performing further
\mathfrak{m}\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{n}
redefinition, we remove
\rho_{t\mathfrak{n}}^{4\varepsilon}

from the integrand and obtain

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\rm NF}^{\rm div}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\langle\frac{\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}\left[\frac{4-\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}+\frac{4-\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}-2\right.

(64)

\displaystyle\left.-(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}_{\mathfrak{n}j}}+\frac{2}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}}\right)\right]\Bigg\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

To write the above equation in a form convenient for further analysis, we introduce the following integral family

I^{(2)}_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}+b_{4}\varepsilon}=\left\langle\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}^{a_{1}}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{a_{2}}\rho_{\mathfrak{n}j}^{a_{3}}\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{a_{4}+b_{4}\varepsilon}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(65)

and use it to write

\begin{split}S_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{NF}}^{\rm div}=&-\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigg\{4I^{(2)}_{1,1,0,-4\varepsilon}+4I^{(2)}_{1,0,1,-4\varepsilon}-I^{(2)}_{1,1,-1,-4\varepsilon}-I^{(2)}_{1,-1,1,-4\varepsilon}-2I^{(2)}_{1,0,0,-4\varepsilon}\\
&-(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left[I^{(2)}_{1,2,0,-4\varepsilon}+I^{(2)}_{1,0,2,-4\varepsilon}+2I^{(2)}_{1,1,1,-4\varepsilon}\right]\Bigg\}.\end{split}

(66)

Finally, the strongly-ordered contribution reads

{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]={\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{F}}+{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{NF}}^{\rm div}+{\cal S}_{\omega}[G_{ij}]_{\text{NF}}^{\rm num},

(67)

where the relevant contributions are given in Eqs. (
58
,
62
,
66
).

4.2
The soft-subtracted, double-collinear contirbution

As the next step, we analyse the soft-subtracted, double-collinear contribution. It reads

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)\theta(1-\omega)C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(68)

This
term possesses a
1/\varepsilon
singularity at most, because
\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1\sim{\cal O}(\varepsilon)
.
To isolate it,
we compute the
\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}
limit of the soft-subtracted
eikonal function and find

C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\;\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]=\frac{4(\vec{n}_{j}\cdot\vec{r})^{2}\omega^{2}(1-\varepsilon)v_{ij}^{2}}{(\omega+1)^{4}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}+\frac{4\omega\left[\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}-2\right)\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}+1-v_{ij}^{2}\right]}{(\omega+1)^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}},

(69)

where
\vec{r}
is the unit vector defined by the following equation

\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{n}}=\cos{\theta_{\xa\yb}}\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}+\sin{\theta_{\xa\yb}}\vec{r},\quad\vec{r}\cdot\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}=0.

(70)

We note that by
setting
v_{ij}\to 1
in
Eq. (
69
), we reproduce the soft-subtracted double-collinear contribution for the massive-massless case, discussed in Ref.
Horstmann:2025hjp
.

To proceed with the calculation of
\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}
,
we integrate over directions of a parton
\mathfrak{n}
in Eq. (
68
),
starting with the integration over
\vec{r}
. Using

r^{i}r^{j}\to\frac{\delta^{ij}-\vec{n}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}\vec{n}^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}}{d-2},

(71)

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\overline{S}_{\omega}\left[\omega^{2}\widetilde{S}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\kappa}=-2\frac{\omega(2+3\omega+2\omega^{2})}{(1+\omega)^{4}}\left[\frac{(2-\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j})}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}-\frac{1-v_{ij}^{2}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}\right].

(72)

Using this result
in Eq. (
68
), we find

\begin{split}\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}&=-\frac{2\mathcal{N}_{A}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\theta(1-\omega)\frac{\omega(2+3\omega+2\omega^{2})}{(1+\omega)^{4}}\\
&\times\left\langle(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)\left(\frac{(2-\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j})}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}-\frac{1-v_{ij}^{2}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.\end{split}

(73)

We integrate over the energy
\omega
, and obtain

\begin{split}\gamma_{\omega}=&\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\varepsilon}}\frac{\omega(2+3\omega+2\omega^{2})}{(1+\omega)^{4}}=\frac{11}{12}+\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{12}+\frac{11}{3}\ln(2)\right)\\
&+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{11}{18}\pi^{2}\right)+\varepsilon^{3}\left(\frac{4}{3}\ln(2)+11\zeta_{3}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{4}\right).\end{split}

(74)

The remaining integration over directions
of
\mathfrak{n}
is straightforward. Putting everything together, we find

\begin{split}\overline{{\cal S}}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}&=\frac{(1-2\varepsilon)\mathcal{N}_{A}\gamma_{\omega}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\langle(\rho_{t\mathfrak{m}}^{4\varepsilon}-1)\;\left[\frac{(2-\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j})}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}}-\frac{1-v_{ij}^{2}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}j}^{2}}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}.\end{split}

(75)

It follows from Eq. (
75
),
that
\overline{\cal S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}
has a
1/\varepsilon
singularity, since the integrand
is
{\cal O}(\varepsilon)
.
Finally, we express Eq. (
75
) through integrals
described in the appendix and find

\begin{split}\overline{S}_{\omega}[\Delta G_{ij}]_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}}{\cal N}_{A}\gamma_{\omega}&\Big\{2I_{-4\varepsilon,1}^{(2)}-2I_{1}^{(1)}[\rho_{jj}]-I_{-4\varepsilon}^{(1)}[\rho_{jj}]+1\\
&\quad-(1-v_{ij}^{2})\left[I^{(2)}_{-4\varepsilon,2}-I_{2}^{(1)}[\rho_{jj}]\right]\Big\}.\end{split}

(76)

4.3
The quantity

5.1
Analytic expressions of the poles

In the preceding section we have isolated all
divergent contributions needed for the computation of the integrated eikonal function. Hence,
we are in the position to combine them, and obtain the divergent part of the integral in an analytic form.

Many integrals required for this computation are obtained by solving the differential equations discussed in appendices;
the results are naturally expressed in terms of generalized polylogarithms (GPLs)
Goncharov:1998kja
;
Goncharov:2001iea
.
Unfortunately, arguments of these GPLs are very complicated, especially because multiple square roots of
kinematic variables appear in the course of simplifying systems of differential equations.
This leads to significant problems when a numerical evaluation of such an analytic result is attempted.

To devise a representation
of the analytic result suitable for fast numerical evaluation,
we follow the same procedures as in
Ref.
Horstmann:2025hjp
. To this end, we employ the symbol technique
Goncharov:2010jf
;
Duhr:2011zq
and
use it to express all GPLs up to weight four through logarithms, classical
polylogarithms

(

) and an additional function

(90)

Since the problem of finding suitable candidate functions
to express linear combinations of GPL’s
in terms of polylogs and
{\rm Li}_{2,2}
is equivalent to an optimization problem,
we used the program
Gurobi

gurobi
to improve the efficiency of this procedure.
In the process of manipulating GPLs,
we heavily relied on the package
PolyLogTools

Duhr:2019tlz
;
Maitre:2005uu
;
Maitre:2007kp
and used the library

GiNaC

Bauer:2000cp
;
Vollinga:2004sn
to evaluate GPLs
numerically.
Finally, we make sure that all functions that appear
in the final result are real-valued in the physical region.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we computed the integrals of the double-emission eikonal functions for two massive emitters whose momenta are at an arbitrary angle to each other. This result is one of a few ingredients required for extending the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme
caola:2017dug
to processes with massive partons.

Our computation is based on the approach already described in Refs.
agarwal:2024gws
;
Horstmann:2025hjp
where local subtractions were used to remove potential singularities from the eikonal functions. The subtracted terms are integrated analytically, whereas finite remainders, suitable for numerical integration, are computed in four space-time dimensions.

We emphasize that all
1/\varepsilon
poles
that appear in the integral of the double-soft eikonal function are calculated analytically. The analytic results
are expressed in terms of standard logarithmic and polylogarithmic functions up to weight four, as well as the function
{\rm Li}_{2,2}
.
This simple representation enables fast and efficient evaluation of the analytic part of the result.

The numerical code that combines the analytic and the numerical parts of the calculation can be obtained from a
git
-repository. We have checked it for various kinematic cases, coming to the conclusion that, with very moderate runtimes, it provides a per mille relative precision for generic velocities and scattering angles.

Appendix A
Massless and single-massive angular integrals

In this appendix, we define
some of the angular integrals that are used for calculations in Section
4
.
These integrals contain either massless propagators, or a massive and a massless propagator.

We fix the normalization of angular integrals in such a way that the angular volume is equal to one,

\Big\langle 1\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=\Big\langle 1\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}=1.

(103)

We begin by introducing the following angular integrals,

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}x}^{n}}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle=I_{n}^{(1)}\left[\rho_{xx}\right],\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\rho_{xx}\neq 0,

(104)

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}x}^{a}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}y}^{b}}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle=I_{a,b}^{(0)}\left[\rho_{xy}\right],

\displaystyle\rho_{xx}=\rho_{yy}=0,

(105)

These integrals can be computed in a closed form, in terms of hypergeometric functions. The results read

\displaystyle I_{n}^{(1)}\left[\rho_{11}\right]=\left(1+\sqrt{1-\rho_{11}}\right)^{-n}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(n,1-\varepsilon,2-2\varepsilon;\frac{2\sqrt{1-\rho_{11}}}{1+\sqrt{1-\rho_{11}}}\right),

(106)

\displaystyle I_{a,b}^{(0)}\left[\rho_{12}\right]=\frac{\Gamma\left(2-2\varepsilon\right)\Gamma\left(1-\varepsilon-a\right)\Gamma\left(1-\varepsilon-b\right)}{2^{a+b}\Gamma^{2}\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\Gamma\left(2-2\varepsilon-a-b\right)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b,1-\varepsilon;1-\frac{\rho_{12}}{2}\right).

(107)

Appendix B
Double-massive angular integrals

We consider angular integrals with two massive propagators defined as

\Big\langle\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}x}^{c_{1}}\rho_{\mathfrak{m}y}^{c_{2}}}\Big\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=I_{c_{1},c_{2}}^{(2)}\left[\rho_{xx},\rho_{yy},\rho_{xy}\right],\quad\rho_{xx}\neq 0,\rho_{yy}\neq 0,\rho_{xy}\neq 0.

(108)

They can be mapped onto loop integrals by using the reverse unitarity
Anastasiou:2002yz

I^{(2)}_{c_{1},c_{2}}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\int\textrm{d}^{d}{k}\frac{\delta^{+}(k^{2})\,\delta(1-k\cdot P)}{\left(k\cdot l_{1}\right)^{c_{1}}\left(k\cdot l_{2}\right)^{c_{2}}},

(109)

where the scalar products among
P,l_{1},l_{2}
read

P\cdot l_{1,2}=1,\quad P^{2}=1,\quad l_{1}^{2}=\rho_{xx}=x,\quad l_{2}^{2}=\rho_{yy}=y,\quad l_{1}\cdot l_{2}=\rho_{xy}=w.

(110)

The powers of the propagators
c_{i}
may depend linearly on
\varepsilon
. We write
c_{i}=a_{i}+b_{i}\varepsilon
and assume that
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are integers.

We can reduce the number of integrals with two massive propagators that need to be computed, by using the integration-by-parts (IBP) technology
tkachov:1981wb
;
chetyrkin:1981qh
. Because
c_{i}
depends on
\varepsilon
, one has to keep powers
of propagators symbolic when solving the IBP identities.
We note that since the IBP identities relate integrals whose propagator powers differ by
integers, integrals with different
b_{i}
’s cannot be connected by IBP relations.

We use

Kira 3

Lange:2025fba

to perform the IBP reduction with symbolic propagator powers.
We find that all integrals
I_{c_{1},c_{2}}^{(2)}
, with
c_{i}=a_{i}+\varepsilon b_{i}
,

i=1,2
, can be expressed through four master integrals

\boldsymbol{f}=\left(I^{(2)}_{b_{1}\varepsilon,b_{2}\varepsilon},\quad I^{(2)}_{1+b_{1}\varepsilon,b_{2}\varepsilon},\quad I^{(2)}_{b_{1}\varepsilon,1+b_{2}\varepsilon},\quad I^{(2)}_{1+b_{1}\varepsilon,1+b_{2}\varepsilon}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}.

(111)

To compute them, we
define a new integral basis

\boldsymbol{g}=\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}\left(g_{1},\quad g_{2},\quad g_{3},\quad g_{4}\right)^{\mathrm{T}},

(112)

where

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[1-\left(b_{1}+b_{2}+2\right)\varepsilon\right]I^{(2)}_{b_{1}\varepsilon,b_{2}\varepsilon}+b_{1}\varepsilon I^{(2)}_{1+b_{1}\varepsilon,b_{2}\varepsilon}+b_{2}\varepsilon I^{(2)}_{b_{1}\varepsilon,1+b_{2}\varepsilon},

(113)

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\sqrt{1-x}I^{(2)}_{1+b_{1}\varepsilon,b_{2}\varepsilon},

\displaystyle g_{3}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\sqrt{1-y}I^{(2)}_{b_{1}\varepsilon,1+b_{2}\varepsilon},

\displaystyle g_{4}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\sqrt{w^{2}-xy}I^{(2)}_{1+b_{1}\varepsilon,1+b_{2}\varepsilon}.

The integrals
g_{1,..,4}
satisfy the differential equations of the form

\textrm{d}\boldsymbol{g}=\varepsilon\left(\textrm{d}\mathbb{A}\right)\,\boldsymbol{g},

(114)

where the matrix
\mathbb{A}
reads

\mathbb{A}=\sum_{n_{1}=0,n_{2}=0}^{n_{1}+n_{2}\leq 2}b_{1}^{n_{1}}b_{2}^{n_{2}}\mathbb{A}_{n_{1}n_{2}},

(115)

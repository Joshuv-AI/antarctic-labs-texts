On the problem of stability of viscous shocks
S. Bolotin, D. Treschev

Abstract
We consider the problem of spectral stability of traveling wave solutions u = γ ⁡ ( x − W ​ t ) u=\gamma(x-Wt) for a system of viscous conservation laws ∂ t u + ∂ x F ⁡ ( u ) = ∂ x 2 u \partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}F(u)=\partial^{2}_{x}u . Such solutions correspond to heteroclinic trajectories γ \gamma of a system of ODE. In general conditions of stability can be obtained only numerically. We propose a model class of piece-wise linear (discontinuous) vector fields F F for which the stability problem is reduced to a linear algebra problem. We show that the stability problem makes sense in such low regularity and construct several examples of stability loss. Every such example can be smoothed to provide a smooth example of the same phenomenon.

Introduction

Consider the following system of nonlinear parabolic PDEs

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}F(u)=\nu\partial^{2}_{x}u,\qquad t,x\in\mathbb{R},\quad u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\nu>0,

(1.1)

where
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth vector field. System (
1.1
) is called a system of viscous conservation laws.
It is a viscous regularization of the system

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}F(u)=0.

(1.2)

This system is said to be strictly hyperbolic if for all
u
the Jacobi matrix of
F

has
n
real distinct eigenvalues. Solutions of system (
1.2
) develop singularities, usually called shocks.
For small viscosity
\nu
, solutions of system (
1.1
) are expected to represent the structure of the shock
in a narrow band around the singularity which tends to zero as
\nu\to 0
.
However, scaling the variables, one can make
\nu=1
. We will assume this from now on.

A solution of (
1.1
) of the form

u(x,t)=\gamma(\xi),\quad\xi=x-Wt,\quad\gamma(\pm\infty)=\lim_{\xi\to\pm\infty}\gamma(\xi)=u^{\pm},

(1.3)

is called a traveling wave solution (TWS), or also a viscous shock.
Then there exists a constant
D\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a heteroclinic solution of the ODE system

u^{\prime}=f(u),\qquad f(u)=F(u)-Wu+D,\quad u^{\prime}=du/d\xi,

(1.4)

which goes from
u^{-}
to
u^{+}
.
Hence,
u^{\pm}
are equilibrium (critical) points of (
1.4
):
f(u^{\pm})=0
.

Let
u=\gamma(\xi)
be such a heteroclinic.
We are interested in the problem of stability of the corresponding TWS of system (
1.1
)
(stability means orbital stability: modulo the phase shift
\xi\mapsto\xi+c
).
If we make a change of variables
x\mapsto\xi=x-Wt
(pass to a moving frame), system (
1.1
) becomes

\partial_{t}u={\cal F}(u),\qquad{\cal F}(u)=-\partial_{\xi}f(u)+\partial^{2}_{\xi}u,

(1.5)

and
u=\gamma(\xi)
is an equilibrium (stationary) solution:
{\cal F}(\gamma)=0
.

In this paper we deal only with stability for the linearized system which is also called the spectral stability.
Substituting

u=\gamma(\xi)+v(\xi,t)

into (
1.5
) (
v
is assumed to be small), in the first approximation in
v
we obtain the linearized system

\partial_{t}v={\cal L}v,

where
{\cal L}
is the linear operator (linearization of
{\cal F}
):

{\cal L}v=\big(v^{\prime}-\partial f(\gamma(\xi))v\big)^{\prime}=\partial_{\xi}\big(\partial_{\xi}v-\partial f(\gamma(\xi))v\big).

(1.6)

Spectral stability of the TWS (
1.3
) means that there is no spectrum of
{\cal L}
in the right half-plane:

\operatorname{Spec}{\cal L}\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}\lambda\leq 0\}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Usually
{\cal L}
is regarded as an operator on
L^{2}
. In general it has essential spectrum.
However, under mild assumptions, there is no essential spectrum in the right half plane. Moreover, sometimes it is possible to prove that there is a gap between the essential spectrum and the imaginary axis, see
[
6
]
. Hence for stability problems only eigenvalues of
{\cal L}
matter.

Remark 1.2
.

v=\gamma^{\prime}
is a solution of the variational equation
v^{\prime}=\partial f(\gamma)v
. Hence

\lambda=0
is always an eigenvalue, and the corresponding eigenfunction is
v=\gamma^{\prime}
.

It is convenient to rewrite the eigenvalue problem
{\cal L}v=\lambda v
as a first order spectral problem

v^{\prime}=\partial f(\gamma)v+z,\quad z^{\prime}=\lambda v.

(1.7)

We shortly write this system as

\mathbf{L}(\lambda){\bf v}={\bf v}^{\prime}-{\bf A}(\xi,\lambda){\bf v}=0,\qquad{\bf v}(\pm\infty)=0,

(1.8)

where

{\bf v}=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
z\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\bf A}(\xi,\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial f(\gamma(\xi))&I\\
\lambda I&0\end{pmatrix}.

Let

E^{\pm}(\lambda)=\{{\bf v}(0):\mathbf{L}(\lambda){\bf v}=0,\;{\bf v}(\pm\infty)=0\}.

Then
\lambda
,
\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq 0
, is an eigenvalue of the spectral problem (
1.8
) iff

E^{+}(\lambda)\cap E^{-}(\lambda)\neq\{0\}.

(1.9)

For
n=1
the problem of stability of TWS’s is well studied.
The first results were based on the maximum principle
[
9
]
.
They deal with generalizations of the Burgers equation and establish nonlinear stability.
Other methods were used in
[
13
,
14
]
.

For
n\geq 2
the main tool to study spectral stability is the Evans function
[
5
]
.
The Evans function is an analog of the characteristic polynomial for infinite dimensions:
eigenvalues of
{\cal L}
in the right half plane
\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq 0
are zeros of the Evans function.
In many papers, spectral stability was established numerically with the help of a
combination of the Evans function method and the argument principle, see e.g.
[
3
,
4
]
.
Spectral instability was rigorously established in several papers, see e.g.
[
2
,
6
]
,
but only for very special vector fields.
General results on passage from linear (spectral) to nonlinear stability were obtained
in e.g.
[
7
,
11
,
12
,
10
,
8
,
15
]
,
see also a comprehensive survey in
[
16
]
.
Under mild natural assumptions it was proved that spectral stability of a shock
(no roots of the Evans function in the right half plane
\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq 0

except zero eigenvalue with minimal order – the dimension of the family of shocks)
implies nonlinear stability – convergence of perturbed solutions to a viscous shock in the same family.
Spectral instability (roots of the Evans function with
\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0
) implies nonlinear instability,
exactly as in the classical Lyapunov theorem.

Most rigorous instability results for concrete systems were obtained for
n=2
and
quadratic vector fields, see e.g.
[
6
,
2
]
and the survey
[
16
]
.
Then there are simplifications: for example heteroclinics connecting saddles
(they correspond to undercompressive shocks) are straight segments
[
1
]
.

However, it seems that mechanisms of spectral instability for TWS’s still remain unclear.
In this paper we propose a model class of vector fields (piece-wise linear fields)
for which explicit stability results may be obtained via elementary linear algebra.
We hope that understanding the phenomena
of stability loss for this model class will give us more insight in what happens in general.

We start with the situation when the vector field

is linear on two half-spaces separated by a hyperplane
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

is discontinuous on

For

Then we give an example of stability loss for overcompressive shocks corresponding to
a heteroclinic connecting an unstable node

.
In this case there is a one-parameter family of heteroclinics

,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu z+\mu\big(\phi(y)\partial_{1}f^{+}(u_{1},\mu y)+(1-\phi(y))\partial_{1}f^{-}(u_{1},\mu y)\big)v_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu\big(\phi(y)\partial_{2}f^{+}(u_{1},\mu y)+(1-\phi(y))\partial_{2}f^{-}(u_{1},\mu y)\big)v_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\phi^{\prime}(y)(f^{+}(u_{1},\mu y)-f^{-}(u_{1},\mu y))v_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(y)(\hat{f}^{+}-\hat{f}^{-})v_{2}+O(\mu).

Hence for
0\leq s\leq a
,

z(s)=z(0)+O(\mu),\quad\frac{dv}{ds}=\phi^{\prime}(y)\Delta fv_{2}+O(\mu).

We use
y
as an independent variable:

\frac{dv}{dy}=\frac{\phi^{\prime}(y)\Delta fv_{2}}{\phi(y)\hat{f}_{2}^{+}+(1-\phi(y))\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}+O(\mu),\qquad 0\leq y\leq 1,

Then pass to the independent variable
\phi
:

\frac{dv}{d\phi}=\frac{\Delta fv_{2}}{\phi\hat{f}_{2}^{+}+(1-\phi)\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}+O(\mu),\qquad 0\leq\phi\leq 1.

In the first approximation

\frac{dv_{1}}{d\phi}=\frac{\Delta f_{1}\,v_{2}}{\phi\hat{f}_{2}^{+}+(1-\phi)\hat{f}_{2}^{-}},\qquad\frac{dv_{2}}{d\phi}=\frac{\Delta f_{2}\,v_{2}}{\phi\hat{f}_{2}^{+}+(1-\phi)\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}.

(6.2)

First solve the second equation:

\ln v_{2}-\ln v_{2}(0)=\ln(\phi\hat{f}_{2}^{+}+(1-\phi)\hat{f}_{2}^{-})-\ln f_{2}^{-},\quad v_{2}=\frac{\phi\hat{f}_{2}^{+}+(1-\phi)\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}{\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}v_{2}(0).

From the first equation (
6.2
),

v_{1}-v_{1}(0)=\phi\frac{\Delta f_{1}}{\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}v_{2}(0),\qquad 0\leq\phi\leq 1.

Let
v^{-}=v(0)
,
v^{+}=v|_{\phi=1}
, then

v_{2}^{+}=\frac{\hat{f}_{2}^{+}}{\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}v_{2}^{-},\quad v_{1}^{+}=v_{1}^{-}+\frac{\Delta f_{1}}{\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}v_{2}^{-},\quad v^{+}=v^{-}+\frac{\Delta f}{\hat{f}_{2}^{-}}v_{2}^{-}.

Proposition
2.1
is proved.

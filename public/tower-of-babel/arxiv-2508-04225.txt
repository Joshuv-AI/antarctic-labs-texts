Symmetric Behavior Regularization via Taylor Expansion of Symmetry
Lingwei Zhu, Zheng Chen, Han Wang, Yukie Nagai

Abstract
This paper introduces symmetric divergences to behavior regularization policy optimization (BRPO) to establish a novel offline RL framework. Existing methods focus on asymmetric divergences such as KL to obtain analytic regularized policies and a practical minimization objective. We show that symmetric divergences do not permit an analytic policy as regularization and can incur numerical issues as loss. We tackle these challenges by the Taylor series of f f -divergence. Specifically, we prove that an analytic policy can be obtained with a finite series. For loss, we observe that symmetric divergences can be decomposed into an asymmetry and a conditional symmetry term, Taylor-expanding the latter alleviates numerical issues. Summing together, we propose Symmetric f f Actor-Critic (S f f -AC), the first practical BRPO algorithm with symmetric divergences. Experimental results on distribution approximation and MuJoCo verify that S f f -AC performs competitively.

Introduction

Offline reinforcement learning (RL) focuses on obtaining a control policy from logged datasets to avoid learning through risky interactions with the environment.
However, learning from static datasets can cause a distributional shift, assigning to out-of-distribution (OOD) actions spuriously high value that cannot be corrected.
The distributional shift prompts a policy to always prefer such OOD actions and eventually leads to diverged learning.
Behavior Regularized Policy Optimization (BRPO) has been verified to be effective in combating this issue by imposing a divergence penalty
D(\cdot||\cdot)
to limit the shift from the data-generating behavior policy
(
Wu et al. 2020
;
Ghasemipour et al. 2021
;
Nair et al. 2021
;
Brandfonbrener et al. 2021
)
.

Regularizing with divergences has been widely studied in the literature
(
Geist et al. 2019
;
Vieillard et al. 2020
)
.
Existing works have explored different divergences such as the KL
(
Wu et al. 2020
;
Jaques et al. 2020
)
,
\alpha
-divergence
(
Xu et al. 2023
)
, Tsallis divergence
(
Zhu et al. 2023
;
Zhu et al. 2024
)
, to name a few.
These divergences fall into the asymmetric class of a broader family called
f
-divergence
(
Ali and Silvey 1966
;
Sason and Verdú 2016
)
.
Asymmetry in the divergence indicates that
D_{\text{Reg}}(\pi||\mu)\neq D_{\text{Reg}}(\mu||\pi)
when the order of policy arguments
\pi,\mu
is exchanged.
This property leads to a debate on which direction of a divergence and its resulting behavior may be more preferable (e.g. forward and reverse KL divergences and the mode-covering or mode-seeking behavior).
Existing works often come to contradictory conclusions
(
Chan et al. 2022
;
Wen et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Zhu et al. 2025b
)
.
Symmetric divergences can be more a consistent candidate and performing due to the invariance to the exchange.

Unfortunately, we show that the popular symmetric divergences like the Jensen-Shannon or the Jeffreys do not permit an analytic policy solution (denoted by
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
),
greatly limiting their use in BRPO.
Since in continuous control it is intractable to directly solve the regularized objective,
a learning policy
\pi_{\theta}
is instead trained by minimizing a loss divergence
D_{\text{Loss}}(\pi_{\text{Reg}}||\pi_{\theta})
to
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
as target.
However, minimizing exact symmetric
D_{\text{Loss}}
can raise numerical issues as have been reported in the literature
(
Wang et al. 2024
)
.
Consider the Jeffrey’s case
D_{\text{Jeffrey}}(\pi_{\text{Reg}}||\pi_{\theta})=D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\text{Reg}}\left|\right|\pi_{\theta}\right)+D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}\left|\right|\pi_{\text{Reg}}\right)
,
when
\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\gg\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)\approx 0
, then one of the directions could yield a vanishingly small ratio value and lead to problematic or even undefined log-ratios (when
\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)=0
).
In summary, in this paper we study the following problems:

Problems with Symmetric Divergences

(P1)
symmetric regularizing divergences do not permit an analytic
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
;

(P2)
symmetric loss divergences can incur numerical issues

We propose to tackle both issues by leveraging a classic information-theoretic result:
the Taylor expansion of any
f
-divergence is an infinite series in the Pearson-Vajda
\chi^{n}
-divergence
(
Nielsen and Nock 2013
)
.
We prove that analytic
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
can be obtained when the series is finite.
For loss, we observe that symmetric divergences can typically be decomposed into an asymmetry term and a conditional symmetry term, Taylor expanding the latter alleviates numerical issues.
We call the proposed algorithm Symmetric
f
-Actor-Critic (S
f
-AC).
We list in Table

the symmetric divergences considered.
To the best of our knowledge, S
f
-AC the first BRPO method that studies the challenges raised by symmetric divergences and proposes a principled solution.
We verify that S
f
-AC performs favorably on a distribution approximation task and the standard D4RL MuJoCo offline benchmark.

-regularization
(

, but restricted to asymmetric divergences for analytic
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
.
In the LLM literature, some methods discuss alignment by symmetric divergence minimization

, but they do not require
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
, see Section

Offline RL.

We focus on discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) expressed by the tuple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
denote state space and action space, respectively.

P
denotes the transition probability,

r
defines the reward function,
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
is the discount factor.
A policy
\pi
is a mapping from the state space to distributions over actions.
We define the action value and state value as
Q^{\pi}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r(s_{t},a_{t})|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a\right]
,
V^{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[Q^{\pi}(s,a)\right]
.
In this paper we focus on the offline RL context where the agent learns from a static dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{s_{i},a_{i},r_{i},s^{\prime}_{i}\}
storing transitions.

f
-Divergences.

We consider the
f
-divergence family that includes both asymmetric and symmetric candidates.
Let
\pi,\mu
denote two policies such that
\pi
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
.
The
f
-divergence is then defined as
D_{\text{Reg}}\!\left(\pi(\cdot|s)\left|\right|\mu(\cdot|s)\right):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[f\!\left(\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\mu(a|s)}\right)\right]
,
where
f
is a convex, lower semicontinuous function satisfying
f(1)=0
.
When
f(t)=t\ln t
, we recover the forward KL divergence.
Another popular choice is the
\chi^{2}
divergence induced by
f(t)=(t-1)^{2}
, which can be generalized to higher order by letting
f(t)=(t-1)^{n}
.

Generalized Policy Families.

The target policy
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
may not always have full support.
When the regularization involves sparse entropies like the Tsallis divergences,
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
may assign probability only to a subset of actions
(
Li et al. 2023b
;
Zhu et al. 2023
;
Bedi et al. 2024
;
Zhu et al. 2025a
)
.
These policies can be defined using the deformed
q
-exponential function
\exp_{q}\!\,(x):=\left[1+(1-q)x\right]_{+}^{\frac{1}{1-q}}
, where
[\cdot]_{+}:=\max\{\cdot,0\}
.
By its definition, when the input argument is smaller than a threshold
x<-\frac{1}{1-q}
, the
q
-exponential sets it to zero
(
Tsallis 2009
)
.
This characteristic allows us to define finite-support distributions using the
q
-exp function
(
Martins et al. 2022
)
.

3
Behavior Regularized Policy Optimization

Behavior regularization is among the most popular approaches in offline RL.
The simple yet effective motivation lies in constraining the distribution shift via a divergence penalty regularizing towards the behavior policy.
However, existing literature mostly focuses on asymmetric divergences with the symmetric counterpart remains less explored.
In this section we show that the issue lies in that they do not permit an analytic policy and can incur numerical issues.

3.1
BRPO with Asymmetric Divergences

We consider the following regularized actor objective:

\displaystyle\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)=\argmax_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\sim\mathcal{D}\\
a\sim\pi\end{subarray}}\left[Q(s,a)-\tau{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D_{\text{Reg}}\!\left({\pi(\cdot|s)}||{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot|s)}\right)}\right],

where
\pi_{\mathcal{D}}
denotes the behavior policy and
\tau\!>\!0
tunes the relative importance.
In the existing literature,
D_{\text{Reg}}
is often within the asymmetric class such that
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
has an analytic expression.
For example, the forward KL divergence leads to the Boltzmann policy
\pi_{\text{KL}}(a|s)\propto\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(a|s)\exp\left(\tau^{-1}Q(s,a)\right)
.
The generalized Tsallis KL divergence induces a more general form
\pi_{\text{TKL}}(a|s)\propto\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(a|s)\exp_{q}\!\left(\tau^{-1}Q(s,a)\right)

(
Zhu et al. 2023
;
Zhu et al. 2025a
)
where
q=1
it recovers the KL case.
As a result, the policy has a varying support depending on
q
and hence greater flexibility.

It is vital that the regularizing divergence permits a closed-form
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
, since it is intractable to directly find the solution to the objective Eq.(
1
) in continuous control.
Rather, a parametrized policy
\pi_{\theta}
is learned by minimizing another loss divergence
D_{\text{Loss}}
to the target
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
:

\displaystyle\pi_{\text{Reg}}=\argmax_{\pi}\,\,\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\sim\mathcal{D}\\
a\sim\pi\end{subarray}}\left[Q(s,a)-\tau{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D_{\text{Reg}}}\!\left({\pi(\cdot|s)}||{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot|s)}\right)\right],

(1)

\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}=\argmin_{\theta}\,\,\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D_{\text{Loss}}}\!\left({{\pi_{\text{Reg}}(\cdot|s)||\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s)}}\right)\right].

(2)

With symmetric divergences, Eq. (
1
) and (
2
) respectively correspond to the regularization and loss issues (
P1
) and (
P2
), as will be discussed in the next subsection.
But with asymmetric candidates such as the
forward KL
D_{\text{Reg}}
, it can be shown that Eq. (
2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}=\argmin_{\theta}\,\,\mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[-\exp\left(\frac{Q(s,a)-V(s)}{\tau}\right)\ln\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\right].

(3)

This method titled advantage regression has been extensively applied in the offline context
(
Peng et al. 2020
;
Jaques et al. 2020
;
Garg et al. 2023
)
.
Similar expression can be derived for the Tsallis KL regularization with the exponential function changed to the
q
-exponential
(
Zhu et al. 2024
)
.

Other choices of asymmetric
D_{\text{Reg}}
such as the
\chi^{2}
-divergence
(
Lee et al. 2021
)
,
\alpha
-divergence
(
Xu et al. 2023
)
and Tsallis divergence
(
Zhu et al. 2023
)
have also been explored.
It is natural to ask that what interesting observations symmetric divergences could bring.
In other areas, some research reported that symmetric divergences like the Jensen-Shannon can outperform the standard KL divergence due to their unique optimization landscapes
(
Wen et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2024
)
.

3.2
Issues with Symmetric Divergences

Compared to asymmetric divergences, the symmetric counterparts remain less studied in BRPO.
We will show in this section that it is because the popular symmetric
f
-divergences do not permit a closed-form regularized solution, which we formally state in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let a symmetric
f
-divergence be defined by a continuously differentiable convex function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
such that

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=t\ln t+g(t),

where
g^{\prime}(t)
does not make
f^{\prime}(t)
an affine function in
\ln t
, i.e.
f^{\prime}(t)\neq a\ln t+b
,
then the regularized optimal policy
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
does not have an analytic expression.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.1
for a proof.
∎

It is worth noting that this affine function should be strictly in
\ln t
, excluding terms like
\ln(t+1)
.
As an immediate consequence, the symmetric divergences in Table
1
do not have an analytic
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
.

Corollary 1
.

The Jeffrey’s, Jensen-Shannon and GAN divergence do not permit an analytic
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
.

Corollary
1
can be verified by checking that the Jeffrey’s divergence has
g^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{t}
, the Jensen-Shannon
g^{\prime}(t)=-\ln\frac{t+1}{2}-1
, and the GAN
g^{\prime}(t)=-\ln(t+1)-1
.

Characterizing the solution of
f
-divergence regularization has been discussed extensively in both BRPO and the distribution correction estimation (DICE) literature
(
Nachum et al. 2019
;
Nachum and Dai 2020
;
Lee et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2023
)
.
In Appendix
A.4
we show their characterizations lead to the same conclusion.
These methods focused exclusively on asymmetric candidates such as the
\chi^{2}
or KL divergence.
We are not aware of any published result that discussed symmetric regularization.

Directly minimizing symmetric divergences can cause numerical issues and unstable performance as have been reported in the literature.
Imagine
\pi\gg\mu
for some actions, then for the two terms of
f(t)
, one can yield a very large value and the other vanishingly small or even undefined log-ratio when
\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)=0
.
In Section
4.1
we discuss our approach to tackle issue (
P1
) and Section
4.2
for (
P2
).

4
Symmetric BRPO via Taylor Expansion

In this section we show that the issues with symmetric behavior regularization and symmetric loss minimization can be tackled in a unified and principled way, made possible by the equivalence of
f
-divergence and the infinite series of Pearson-Vajda
\chi^{n}
-divergences.

4.1
Symmetric Regularization via the
\chi^{n}
Series

To resolve the issue (
P1
), no closed-form solution under symmetric regularization, we study the following regularized objective:

\displaystyle\pi_{\text{Reg}}=\argmax_{\pi}\,\,\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\sim\mathcal{D}\\
a\sim\pi\end{subarray}}\left[Q(s,a)-\tau\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{f^{(n)}(1)}{n!}\chi^{n}\left(\pi(\cdot|s)||\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot|s)\right)\right],

(4)

where
f^{(n)}
denotes the
n

When
N=\infty
, this objective is equivalent to Eq.(
1
) with arbitrary valid
f
-divergence.
Our key inspiration is the following classic information theoretic result.

Lemma 2

(
(
Barnett et al. 2002
;
Nielsen and Nock 2013
)
)
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be a bounded, convex,
n
-times differentiable function with
f(1)=0
.
Then the
f
-divergence permits the following representation

\displaystyle D_{f}\left(\pi(\cdot|s)||\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot|s)\right)=\int\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(a|s)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(z)}{n!}\left(\frac{{\pi(a|s)}}{{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(a|s)}}-z\right)^{n}\mathrm{d}a,

which is the Taylor expansion of
f
-divergence around
z\in\mathbb{R}
.

This result states that any bounded and smooth
f
-divergence equals the infinite series of
\chi^{n}
divergence.
By letting
z=1
, we recover Eq.(
4
) and the first two terms of the series vanish since
f^{(0)}(1)=f(1)=0
and
\chi^{1}(\cdot||\cdot)=0
.

However, when
N=\infty
, we again face the same issue as symmetric divergences.
To this end, we truncate the series to
N<\infty
to retreat from exact symmetry.
The following theorem shows that an analytic solution is available only when
N<5
.

Theorem 3
.

Let the series in Eq.(
4

N
, i.e.
n=2,3,\dots,N

Then the regularized optimal policy

can be expressed analytically

\displaystyle\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)\propto\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(a|s)\left[1+Z_{N}(s,a)\right]_{+},

where

contains
N

.
Moreover, when
N=2
:

\displaystyle\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)\propto\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(a|s)\,\exp_{q=0}{\left(\frac{Q(s,a)}{\tau}\right)},

(5)

where we absorbed additional constants to
\tau
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.2
for a proof.
∎

Notice that

gives

When

, it recovers the classic result that

Generally, the series Eq.(
8
) allows one to balance between symmetric regularization and the truncation threshold

But as shown by

.
Therefore, we can safely choose

4.2
Symmetric Loss Minimization by Expanding Conditional Symmetry

Problem (
P2
) states that
directly computing the symmetric
D_{\text{Loss}}
can raise numerical issues as have been reported in the literature
(
Wang et al. 2024
)
.
Consider the Jeffrey’s case
D_{\text{Jeffrey}}(\pi_{\text{Reg}}||\pi_{\theta})=D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\text{Reg}}\left|\right|\pi_{\theta}\right)+D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}\left|\right|\pi_{\text{Reg}}\right)
,
when
\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\gg\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)\approx 0
for some actions (and vice versa), one direction could yield a very large value while the other vanishingly small.

To tackle this issue, we could apply the Taylor expansion again.
However, this can also be problematic since a full Taylor expansion involves purely powers of policy ratio which can be numerically unstable for minimization.
Instead, we draw a key observation from Theorem
1
that the symmetric divergences can be decoupled into two interdependent terms:

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(t)=\underbrace{t\ln t}_{\text{asymmetry}}+\,\,\underbrace{g(t)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{conditional}\\
\text{symmetry}\end{subarray}},\end{split}

(6)

the first term
t\ln t
corresponds to the asymmetric forward KL divergence.
The second term
g(t)
, which can vary case by case, dictates how overall symmetry can be achieved given the first term.
This decomposition allows us to improve numerical stability by expanding only
g(t)
, since
t\ln t
leads to the numerically stable advantage regression, as have been shown in Eq.(

Therefore,

is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[D_{\text{Loss}}\left(\pi_{\text{Reg}}(\cdot|s)||{\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\text{Reg}}(\cdot|s)\left|\right|\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s)\right)\right]}_{t\ln t}\,+\,\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}\!}\left[\int\!\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\,\,g\!\left(\frac{\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)}{\pi_{\theta}(a|s)}\right)\mathrm{d}a\right]}_{\text{conditional symmetry}}.

(7)

can be zero for some actions.

I.e.,

cannot involve terms that flip the ratio e.g.

that destroys the validity.

7
Related Work

Taylor expansion in RL.

There are some papers studying the Taylor expansion in RL.
Specifically,
Tang et al. 2020
proposed to expand the action value difference as an infinite series in the transition dynamics and liken that to the Taylor series.

Motoki et al. 2024
proposed to modify the extreme Q-learning objective
(
Garg et al. 2023
)
by expanding the exponential function into a MacLaurin series to stabilize learning.
In this paper, we utilize the Taylor expansion of
f
-divergence to obtain a
\chi^{n}
-series and based on it derive analytic policy
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
and a tractable minimization objective.

f
-divergence in RL.

BRPO requires an analytic
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
to be used as the target policy in a loss divergence.
The existing literature has mostly focused on asymmetric divergences such as the KL,
\chi^{2}
,
\alpha
divergences that permit an analytic policy.
In other areas such as the goal-conditioned RL
(
Ma et al. 2022
;
Agarwal et al. 2023
)
or RLHF
(
Go et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2024
)
, symmetric
f
-divergences have been discussed since they require only minimizing the divergence as a loss objective and no
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
is required.
Their objective can be derived by computing only
f^{\prime}
and gradient of log-likelihood.

Policy ratio clipping.

Clipping policy ratio into a range
[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]
has been widely studied since the proximal policy optimization
(
Schulman et al. 2017
;
Vaswani et al. 2022
)
.
In the offline context the clipping has been shown to also play a key role
(
Zhuang et al. 2023
)
.
Our method shows that
\epsilon
can be connected to the convergence requirements of Taylor series.
In fact, the clipped
\chi^{n}
series shares similarity to the higher order objectives in
(
Tang et al. 2020
)
which assumed bounded total deviation, providing an alternate interpretation for their method.

Generalized parametric policies.

In Theorem
3
we derived the solution of
\pi_{\text{Reg}}
which is a finite-support distribution that can yield zero probabilities for some actions.
In RL, this type of policies has been shown to connect to the general entropy regularization
(
Lee et al. 2018
;
Chow et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2023b
)
.
Specifically,
Zhu et al. 2023
;
Zhu et al. 2025b
showed that the
q
-exp functions for various
q<1
can achieve the same sparsity effect by controlling the regularization strength
\tau
.
In this paper we focused on
N=2
which corresponds to the standard case
q=0
.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied symmetric divergences in BRPO, and showed two major issues limiting the use of symmetric divergences: (1) they do not permit an analytic regularized policy and (2) they can incur numerical issues when naïvely computed.
We tackled the two issues by leveraging the finite Taylor series of symmetric divergences, arriving at S
f
-AC, the first BRPO algorithm with symmetric divergences.
We verified that S
f
-AC performed favorably against popular existing methods.

We acknowledge the paper has limitations.
The conditional symmetry expansion still needs to compute the policy ratio which needs a hyperparameter
\epsilon
dictating the clipping threshold.
While we have shown that S
f
-AC is robust towards a range of thresholds, it is desired that policy ratio computation could be avoided.

Proof of Theorem

Let us write out the Lagrangian of the objective
[
Li et al. 2019
,
Xu et al. 2023
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\pi,\alpha,\beta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}(s)\sum_{a}\left[\pi(a|s)Q(s,a)-\tau\,\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[f\left(\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\mu(a|s)}\right)\right]\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{s}d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}(s)\!\left[\alpha(s)\left(\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)-1\right)-\sum_{a}\beta(a|s)\,\pi(a|s)\right].

(9)

where
d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}
is the stationary state distribution of the behavior policy.

\alpha
and
\beta
are Lagrangian multipliers for
the equality and inequality constraints, respectively.
The KKT conditions are:

\displaystyle\text{Primal feasibility:}\quad\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)=1,\quad\pi(a|s)\geq 0,

\displaystyle\text{Dual feasibility:}\quad\beta(a|s)\geq 0,

\displaystyle\text{Stationarity:}\quad\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\pi(a|s)}=Q(s,a)-\tau\,\mu(a|s)f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\mu(a|s)}\right)\frac{1}{\mu(a|s)}-\alpha(s)+\beta(a|s)=0,

\displaystyle\text{Complementarity:}\quad\beta(a|s)\,\pi(a|s)=0

Following
[
Li et al. 2019
,
Xu et al. 2023
]
, we eliminate
d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}
since we assume all policies induce an irreducible Markov chain.
For any action with
\pi(a|s)>0
, we have
\beta(a|s)=0
.
Therefore, we can derive the solution as:

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\mu(a|s)}\right)=\frac{Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)}{\tau}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\pi^{*}(a|s)=\left[(f^{\prime})^{-1}\!\left(\frac{Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)}{\tau}\right)\right]_{+},

where
\alpha(s)
is the normalization constant that ensures
\pi^{*}
sums to 1.

For
\pi^{*}
to be analytic, we need to know how to compute
\alpha(s)
.
Since
f(t)
begins with a

term, so
f^{\prime}(t)=\ln t+1+g^{\prime}(t)
. If
g^{\prime}(t)
is not a function such that
f^{\prime}(t)=a\ln t+b
for some constants
a,b
, then
\alpha(s)
cannot be calculated from the constraint
\sum_{a}\pi^{*}(a|s)=1
.

Proof of Theorem
3

To prove Theorem
3
, we need to show that (i) the policy expression when
N=2
and (ii) when
N>5
the solution does not have an analytic expression.
To show (i), we study regularization with the sole, general
\chi^{n}
.
Again let us write out the Lagrangian similar to Eq.(
9
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\pi,\alpha,\beta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}(s)\sum_{a}\left[\pi(a|s)Q(s,a)-\tau\,\frac{\left(\pi(a|s)-\mu(a|s)\right)^{N}}{\mu(a|s)^{\,N-1}}\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{s}d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}(s)\!\left[\alpha(s)\left(\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)-1\right)-\sum_{a}\beta(a|s)\,\pi(a|s)\right].

where
d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}},\alpha
and
\beta
are carry the same meaning as Eq.(
9
).
The KKT conditions are now:

\displaystyle\text{Primal feasibility:}\quad\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)=1,\quad\pi(a|s)\geq 0,

\displaystyle\text{Dual feasibility:}\quad\beta(a|s)\geq 0,

\displaystyle\text{Stationarity:}\quad\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\pi(a|s)}=Q(s,a)-\tau\,\frac{N\left(\pi(a|s)-\mu(a|s)\right)^{N-1}}{\mu(a|s)^{N-1}}-\alpha(s)+\beta(a|s)=0,

\displaystyle\text{Complementarity:}\quad\beta(a|s)\,\pi(a|s)=0

Following a similar procedure, we can obtain

\displaystyle Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)

\displaystyle=\tau\,\frac{N\,\left(\pi(a|s)-\mu(a|s)\right)^{N-1}}{\mu(a|s)^{\,N-1}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left(\pi(a|s)-\mu(a|s)\right)^{N-1}=\mu(a|s)^{N-1}\frac{\left(Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)\right)}{N\tau}.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\pi^{*}(a|s)=\mu(a|s)\left[1+\left(\frac{{Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)}}{N\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\right]_{+}\,\,,

where
\alpha(s)
is the normalization constant ensuring
\sum_{a}\pi^{*}(a|s)=1
.
When
N=2
, this becomes

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(a|s)

\displaystyle=\mu(a|s)\left[1+\frac{{Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)}}{2\tau}\right]_{+}

\displaystyle=\mu(a|s)\exp_{q=0}{\left(\frac{{Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)}}{\tau^{\prime}}\right)},

where we defined
\tau^{\prime}=2\tau
, concluding the proof of (i).

Now let us consider the case where we have
\chi^{2}
and
\chi^{3}
appearing together, all other KKT conditions remain the same except for the stationarity:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\pi(a|s)}=Q(s,a)

\displaystyle-\,2\tau\,\frac{f^{(2)}(1)}{2!}\frac{{\pi(a|s)-\mu(a|s)}}{\mu(a|s)}

\displaystyle-\,3\tau\,\frac{f^{(3)}(1)}{3!}\left(\frac{\left(\pi(a|s)-\mu(a|s)\right)}{\mu(a|s)}\right)^{2}-\alpha(s)-\beta(a|s)=0,

Now let us define

\displaystyle W(a|s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\pi(a|s)-\mu(a|s)}{\mu(a|s)},\quad\tau_{2}:=2\tau\,\frac{f^{(2)}(1)}{2!},\quad\tau_{3}:=3\tau\,\frac{f^{(3)}(1)}{3!}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad W(a|s)=\frac{-\tau_{2}+\sqrt{\tau_{2}^{2}+4\tau_{3}\left(Q(s,a)-\alpha(s)\right)}}{2\tau_{3}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\pi^{*}(a|s)=\mu(a|s)\,\left[1+\frac{-\tau_{2}+\sqrt{\tau_{2}^{2}+4\tau_{3}\,\left(Q(s,a)-\alpha\right)}}{2\tau_{3}}\right]_{+}.

Though the reciprocal term becomes more complex, the role it plays still lies in determining the threshold for truncating actions.
We can similarly derive the solution for

contains the radicals over

[
Zhu et al. 2023

Therefore, we opt for the simplest case where

The series

Therefore, for

A.3
Proof of Theorem
4

We follow
[
Barnett et al. 2002
, Theorem 1]
in proving this result.
We start with the following Taylor representation with the integral remainder:

\displaystyle f(t)=f(z)+\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\left(t-z\right)^{n}}{n!}f^{(n)}(z)+\frac{1}{N!}\int^{t}_{z}(t-z)^{N}f^{(N+1)}(a)\,\mathrm{d}a.

Specifically by (2.4) of
[
Barnett et al. 2002
]
we have

\displaystyle\left|f(t)-f(z)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\left(t-z\right)^{n}}{n!}f^{(n)}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N!}\left|\int^{t}_{z}|t-z|^{N}\left|f^{(N+1)}(a)\right|\,\mathrm{d}a\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N!}\sup_{a\in[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]}\left|f^{(N+1)}(a)\right|\left|\int^{t}_{z}|t-a|^{N}\mathrm{d}a\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(N+1)!}\left|\!\left|f^{(N+1)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}|t-z|^{N+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(N+1)!}\left|\!\left|f^{(N+1)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\epsilon^{N+1},

where in the last equation we let
t=\pi_{\text{Reg}}/\pi_{\theta}
clipped to
1+\epsilon
and
z=1
.
Now we can repeat the same procedure for
t=1-\epsilon
and take an expectation over
s\sim\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\frac{2\epsilon^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\left|\!\left|f^{(N+1)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\right]\leq\frac{2|\mathcal{D}|\epsilon^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\left|\!\left|f^{(N+1)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}.

We conclude the proof of Theorem
4
by changing
f
to
g
.

\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)\propto\left[\,(h_{f}^{\prime})^{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\!-1$}}\!\left(\frac{Q(s,a)}{\tau}\right)\,\right]_{+}
.

(D1)

d^{\pi_{\text{Reg}}}(s,a)>0\Rightarrow d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}(s,a)>0

(D2)

f(t)
is strictly convex;

(D3)

Result:

\frac{d^{\pi_{\text{Reg}}}(s,a)}{d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}(s,a)}\propto\left[\,(f^{\prime})^{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\!-1$}}\!\left(\frac{Q(s,a)}{\tau}\right)\,\right]_{+}

A.4.1
Regularization Characterization.

We call the first class Regularization Characterization as they exactly studied Eq. (
1
)

[

,
Xu et al. 2023
]
.
Here,

indicates
proportional to

Assumptions (R2) does not hold for symmetric divergences in general.

Jeffrey’s divergence.

D_{\text{Jeffrey}}(\pi_{\text{Reg}}||\pi_{\theta})=D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\text{Reg}}\left|\right|\pi_{\theta}\right)+D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}\left|\right|\pi_{\text{Reg}}\right)

.
We see that

h^{\prime\prime}_{f}(t)=2\ln t+3-\frac{1}{t}
, which can be negative and in turn indicates that

is not strictly convex.
Therefore, Jeffrey’s divergence does not satisfy their Assumption (R2).

Jensen-Shannon Divergence.

Recall the Jensen-Shannon divergence is defined by

\displaystyle f(t):=t\ln t-(t+1)\ln\frac{t+1}{2}.

We examine Assumption (R2) of regularization characterization
[
Li et al. 2019
,
Xu et al. 2023
]
:

\displaystyle h_{f}(t):=tf(t)=t^{2}\ln t-t^{2}\ln\frac{t+1}{2}-t\ln\frac{t+1}{2},

\displaystyle\Rightarrow h^{\prime}_{f}(t)=2t\ln t+t-2t\ln\left(\frac{t+1}{2}\right)-\frac{t^{2}}{t+1}-\ln\left(\frac{t+1}{2}\right)-\frac{t}{t+1},

\displaystyle\Rightarrow h^{\prime\prime}_{f}(t)=2\ln\left(\frac{2t}{t+1}\right)+\frac{1}{t+1}.

suggesting that
h_{f}(t)
is not a convex function and does not meet their Assumption (R2).

GAN Divergence.

From Table
1
the GAN divergence is defined by

\displaystyle f(t)=t\ln t-(t+1)\ln(t+1).

Again we focus on its second assumption:

\displaystyle h_{f}(t)=t^{2}\ln t-t^{2}\ln(t+1)-t\ln(t+1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,\,

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{f}(t)=2t\ln\left(\frac{t}{t+1}\right)-\ln(t+1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,\,

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}_{f}(t)=2\ln\left(\frac{t}{t+1}\right)+\frac{1}{t+1}.

h^{\prime\prime}_{f}(t)
can be negative, therefore Assumption (R2) is not satisfied.

A.4.2
DICE Characterization

DIstribution Correction Estimation (DICE) methods estimate stationary distribution ratios that correct the discrepancy between the data distribution and the optimal policy’s stationary distribution
[
Nachum et al. 2019
,
Nachum and Dai 2020
]
.

In the offline context, the optimal solution is the ratio between the stationary distributions
{d^{\pi_{\text{Reg}}}(s,a)}/{d^{\pi_{\mathcal{D}}}(s,a)}

[
Lee et al. 2021
,
Mao et al. 2024
]
.
The optimal policy can then be uniquely identified by
\pi_{\text{Reg}}^{*}(a|s)={d^{\pi_{\text{Reg}}^{*}}(s,a)}/{\sum_{b}d^{\pi_{\text{Reg}}^{*}}(s,b)}

[
Puterman 1994
]
.
Assumptions (D1)-(D3) can be satisfied by the symmetric divergences in Table
1
.
However, the issue lies in that
(f^{\prime})^{-1}
in general does not have a closed-form expression.

Jeffrey’s divergence.

f(t)=(t-1)\ln t
, the inverse function of
f^{\prime}(t)=\ln t+1-\frac{1}{t}
involves the Lambert W function which does not have an analytic expression
[
Nowozin et al. 2016
]
.

Jensen-Shannon Divergence.

The generator function of Jensen-Shannon divergence is:

\displaystyle f(t):=t\ln t-(t+1)\ln\frac{t+1}{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad f^{\prime}(t)=\ln t-\ln\frac{t+1}{2},

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\pi_{\text{Reg}}(a|s)\propto\max\left\{0,\,\,\left(f^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\!\left(\frac{Q(s,a)}{\tau}\right)\right\}=\max\left\{0,\,\,\frac{\exp\left(\frac{Q(s,a)}{\tau}\right)}{2-\exp\left(\frac{Q(s,a)}{\tau}\right)}\right\}.

To make sure the policy is a valid distribution, we need to find the normalization constant. However, the integral of
(f^{\prime})^{-1}
diverges to infinity, suggesting that no such normalization constant exists.

Generating Differentially Private Networks with a Modified Erdős-Rényi Model
Huaiyuan Rao, Calvin Hawkins, Alexander Benvenuti, Matthew Hale

Abstract
Differential privacy has been used to privately calculate numerous network properties, but existing approaches often require the development of a new privacy mechanism for each property of interest. Therefore, we present a framework for generating entire networks in a differentially private way. Differential privacy is immune to post-processing, which allows for any network property to be computed and analyzed for a private output network, without weakening its protections. We consider undirected networks and develop a differential privacy mechanism that takes in a sensitive network and outputs a private network by randomizing its edge set. We prove that this mechanism does provide differential privacy to a network’s edge set, though it induces a complex distribution over the space of output graphs. We then develop an equivalent privacy implementation using a modified Erdős-Rényi model that constructs an output graph edge by edge, and it is efficient and easily implementable, even on large complex networks. Experiments implement ε \varepsilon -differential privacy with ε = 2.5 \varepsilon=2.5 when computing graph Laplacian spectra, and these results show the proposed mechanism incurs 49.34 % 49.34\% less error than the current state of the art.

However, those approaches have required the development of a new privacy mechanism for each of those properties, which
has required privacy engineers to anticipate which graph properties will be privately
computed in the future.
There are various quantitative graph properties that do not have a purpose-built privacy
mechanism, and it is unclear how one could privately compute those properties.

Therefore, in this paper we develop a new privacy mechanism that generates an entire graph in a differentially

II-A

Graph Theory

We consider a simple, undirected, unweighted graph
G=(V,E)

V=[n]

E\subset V\times V
. We say
(i,j)\in E
if nodes
i
and
j
share an edge and
(i,j)\notin E
if they do not.
Given
G=(V,E)
, we denote the edge set by
E(G)
when there is a need to identify the underlying graph
G
.
We denote the complementary graph of
G=(V,E)

\bar{G}=(V,\bar{E})

\bar{E}=\{(i,j)\in V\times V\ |\ (i,j)\notin E\}

G
.

II-B

Differential Privacy

We use differential privacy
[
30
]
to provide privacy for graphs. In this work, we consider the edge set of a graph to be the sensitive data that must be protected, and we implement
edge differential privacy

[
12
]

to protect it. The goal of differential privacy is to
randomize data in a way that
makes two “similar” pieces of sensitive data produce outputs that are “approximately indistinguishable”. The notion of “similarity” is formalized by an adjacency relation, which specifies
which pairs of sensitive data must be rendered approximately indistinguishable by differential privacy.
Next, we define a metric that describes the closeness of graphs, which we will use to define adjacency for edge sets.

Definition 1

(Fréchet-Nikodym Metric)
.

For two finite sets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, the Fréchet-Nikodym metric with respect to the counting measure is

d_{\Delta}(S_{1},S_{2})=|S_{1}\Delta S_{2}|
.

In words,
d_{\Delta}(S_{1},S_{2})
counts the total number of elements
that are in either
S_{1}
or
S_{2}
but not in both of them.
With an abuse of terminology, we refer to
d_{\Delta}
as
the
Fréchet-Nikodym distance. We define adjacency for graphs
using Definition
1
.

Definition 2

(Edge adjacency;
[
12
]
)
.

Fix an adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}.
Two graphs
G=(V,E)
and
G^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime})
are adjacent if

d_{\Delta}(E,E^{\prime})\leq A.

(1)

We write
\mathrm{Adj}(G,G^{\prime})=1
if
G
and
G^{\prime}
are adjacent and
\mathrm{Adj}(G,G^{\prime})=0
otherwise.

Definition
2
says that two graphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
are adjacent if they
have the same number of nodes and
differ in at most
A
edges. An example of adjacent graphs is shown in Figure
1
.
The notion of “approximately indistinguishable” is made precise by the definition of differential privacy itself.

:

A=2
.
The middle graph is adjacent to the left graph because it differs by
2

it differs by

Definition 3

(Differential privacy;
[
30
]
)
.

Fix a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and the set of all graphs with
n
nodes
\mathcal{G}_{n}
. Fix a privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
. A randomized algorithm
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{G}_{n}\times\Omega\to\mathcal{G}_{n}
is
\varepsilon
-differentially private if for all
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{n}
and for all
G,G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
such that
\mathrm{Adj}(G,G^{\prime})=1
we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}(G)\in\mathcal{S}]\leq e^{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}(G^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}].

(2)

Definition
3
says that
if
G
and
G^{\prime}
are adjacent, then
the probability of making an observation of the private form of
G
, namely
\mathcal{M}(G)
, must be close to the probability of making the same observation of the private form of
G^{\prime}
, namely
\mathcal{M}(G^{\prime})
. The parameter
\varepsilon
controls the strength of privacy, and smaller
values make privacy stronger. Typical values
[
31
]
are
0.1
to
10
.

A feature of differential privacy is that its protections weaken gracefully when multiple mechanisms are applied to the same piece of sensitive data. Repeated queries of sensitive data lead to a decreasing strength of privacy, which is formalized as follows.

Lemma 1

(Sequential composition;
[
30
, Section 3.5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}
be an
\varepsilon_{i}
-differentially private mechanism for
i\in[k]
. If
\mathcal{M}_{[k]}
is defined to be
\mathcal{M}_{[k]}=(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2},\cdots,\mathcal{M}_{k})
, then
\mathcal{M}_{[k]}
is
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}
-differentially private.

Recalling that smaller values of
\varepsilon
imply stronger privacy, Lemma
1
says that privacy weakens if multiple
mechanisms are used to generate private outputs from the same piece of sensitive data, but it weakens only linearly in the number of mechanisms.
Another appealing property of differential privacy is that it is immune to post-processing, i.e., arbitrary post-hoc computations on differentially private data do not weaken privacy’s guarantees. This property is formalized by the following lemma.

Lemma 2

(Immunity to Post-Processing;
[
30
, Section 2.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an
\varepsilon
-differentially private mechanism. Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
be an arbitrary mapping. Then the composition
h\circ\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is
\varepsilon
-differentially private.

Lemma
2
implies that once we generate a private graph, we can compute and share any properties of it without weakening privacy’s protections.

III
Differentially Private Graph Generation

In this section, we design a mechanism for computing differentially private graphs from a sensitive graph, which solves Problem
1
.
Our approach is based on the exponential mechanism
[
21
]
, which is
commonly used to implement privacy for non-numerical data.
The core idea is to output private graphs whose edge sets are close to the edge set of a sensitive graph with high probability.
In Section
III-A
, we formalize this notion of closeness with a utility function, then in Section
III-B
we use this utility function
to develop the exponential mechanism we use.

III-A

Utility and Global Sensitivity

For a given sensitive graph, we desire a privacy mechanism that outputs a private graph that is “close” to the sensitive graph with high probability, while satisfying the definition of differential privacy. Since we consider edge differential privacy in this work, we consider graphs “close” when their edge sets are close, which we formalize with the following utility function.

Definition 4

(Utility function)
.

For a sensitive input graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, the private output graph
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
has utility

u(G,H)=-d_{\Delta}\big(E(G),E(H)\big).

(3)

This choice of utility function captures the fact that a graph
H
is a better private
estimate of a sensitive graph
G
when the edge set of
H
differs from the edge set of
G
by fewer edges.
The maximum value of the utility is
0
, which occurs when
H=G
, and all other choices
of private outputs give negative values of the utility function.
The minimum value of the utility is
-\binom{n}{2}
,
which occurs when
E(H)=\bar{E}(G)
, i.e., when
H
is the complementary graph
to
G
, which causes
G
and
H
to differ in all edges.

Implementing the exponential mechanism requires bounding the amount
by which the utility can differ over adjacent graphs. This is referred to as the utility’s
global sensitivity
.

Definition 5

(Global sensitivity;
[
30
, Section 3.4]
)
.

Fix
A\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the adjacency relation
Adj
in Definition
2
.
Then the global sensitivity of the utility function
u:\mathcal{G}_{n}\times\mathcal{G}_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
from Definition
4
is

\Delta u=\max_{H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\\
\textnormal{Adj}(G_{1},G_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\big|u(G_{1},H)-u(G_{2},H)\big|.

(4)

The exponential mechanism is formulated in terms of this global sensitivity, which we bound
as follows.

Lemma 3
.

Fix an adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the global sensitivity from Definition
5
obeys
\Delta u\leq A
.

Proof.

First, suppose
u(G_{1},H)\geq u(G_{2},H)
.
From Definition
5
we have

\Delta u=\max_{H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}\ \max_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\\
\textnormal{Adj}(G_{1},G_{2})=1\end{subarray}}u(G_{1},H)-u(G_{2},H),

(5)

and substituting the definition of
u
from (
3
) gives

u(G_{1},H)-u(G_{2},H)=\\
d_{\Delta}\big(E(G_{2}),E(H)\big)-d_{\Delta}\big(E(G_{1}),E(H)\big).

(6)

Using the triangle inequality in (
6
) gives

d_{\Delta}\big(E(G_{2}),E(H)\big)-d_{\Delta}\big(E(G_{1}),E(H)\big)\\
\leq d_{\Delta}\big(E(G_{1}),E(G_{2})\big)=-u(G_{1},G_{2}).

(7)

Then plugging (
6
) and (
7
) into (
5
) gives

\Delta u\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\\
\textnormal{Adj}(G_{1},G_{2})=1\end{subarray}}-u(G_{1},G_{2})=A.

(8)

Identical steps show (
8
) also holds when
u(G_{2},H)\geq u(G_{1},H)
.
∎

With a global sensitivity bound in place, we next develop the exponential mechanism for graphs.

III-B

Graph Exponential Mechanism

The exponential mechanism formalizes the idea of generating private outputs with high utility.
We state the exponential mechanism in its standard form
for a sensitive graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
.

Definition 6

(Exponential mechanism;
[
30
, Chapter 3.4]
)
.

Fix a privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
and an adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}
.
The exponential mechanism selects and outputs a graph
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
with probability proportional to
\exp\big(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{2\Delta u}\big)
, where
u
is from Definition
4
and
\Delta u
is from Definition
5
.

It is well-established that the exponential mechanism satisfies
\varepsilon
-differential privacy
[
30
, Chapter 3.4]
.
As stated, Definition
6
requires the computation of a normalization constant
to compute probabilities of generating specific outputs. The conventional analysis
of the exponential mechanism
[
30
, Chapter 3.4]

requires

\frac{\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{2\Delta u}\right)}{\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G^{\prime},H)}{2\Delta u}\right)}\leq\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)

(9)

for adjacent
graphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
.

The reason for having
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
on the right-hand side of (
9
)
is that the normalization
constant is also allowed to differ by up to a multiplicative factor of
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

in the same way.
For a graph
G
, we can define the normalization constant

C_{G}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{2\Delta u}\right),

(10)

and for an adjacent graph
G^{\prime}
we can define

C_{G^{\prime}}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G^{\prime},H)}{2\Delta u}\right).

(11)

Then the conventional analysis of the exponential mechanism
reserves half of the privacy budget for changes in the proportionality constant
by allowing

\frac{C_{G^{\prime}}}{C_{G}}\leq\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).

(12)

Then when the input graph is
G
one would sample
H\sim\frac{1}{C_{G}}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{2\Delta u}\right)
,
and when the input graph is
G^{\prime}
one would sample
H\sim\frac{1}{C_{G^{\prime}}}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G^{\prime},H)}{2\Delta u}\right)
.
One easily shows that the exponential mechanism is
\varepsilon
-differentially private
because

\frac{C_{G^{\prime}}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{2\Delta u}\right)}{C_{G}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G^{\prime},H)}{2\Delta u}\right)}\leq\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)=\exp(\varepsilon).

(13)

The bounds in terms of
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
in (
9
)
and (
12
) are why
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

appears in the definition of the exponential mechanism in
Definition
6
.

The change in the normalization constant
can occur when the exponential mechanism is applied in a context
in which that constant depends on
G
.
If the normalization constant does not depend on
G
,
then the ratio in (
12
) would hold with upper
bound
\exp\left(\frac{0}{2}\right)=1
.
In that case, one does not need to reserve half of the privacy
budget for changes in the normalization constant,
and, instead, one can use the entire privacy budget for the exponential term
in Definition
6
. Doing so would
mean that an output
H
would be generated with probability proportional
to
\exp\big(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{\Delta u}\big)

instead of
\exp\big(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{2\Delta u}\big)
.
In that case, the normalization constant would be
1
divided by a sum
of terms of the form
\exp\big(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{\Delta u}\big)
.

We next show that, over the space of private output graphs,
the normalization constant

C=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{\Delta u}\right)

(14)

is indeed
independent of
G
.

Lemma 4
.

Fix an adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}

and a privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
Consider
C
in (
14
).
For any sensitive graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\biggl(1+\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A}\right)\biggr)^{\binom{n}{2}}.

(15)

Proof.

The utility function
u
depends on
G
through the distance
d_{\Delta}(E(G),E(H))
.
Therefore, to compute
C,
we group all candidate private outputs in
\mathcal{G}_{n}
according to their distance from
G
.
Let

\mathcal{X}(G,k)=\{H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\ |\ d_{\Delta}(E(G),E(H))=k\}

(16)

be the set of candidate output graphs whose edge sets have
Fréchet-Nikodym distance
k
from the edge set of
G
.
By construction,
k\in\{0,1,\dots,\binom{n}{2}\}
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\binom{n}{2}}\mathcal{X}(G,k)
for any
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
. Therefore, we express (
14
) as

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{H\in\mathcal{X}(G,k)}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{\Delta u}\right).

(17)

For a given value of
k
, we have
|\mathcal{X}(G,k)|=\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k},
and any graph
H\in\mathcal{X}(G,k)
satisfies
u(G,H)=-k
.
Then from (
17
)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\binom{n}{2}}{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k}}\exp\left(-\frac{k\varepsilon}{A}\right).

(18)

Finally, applying the binomial theorem to (
18
) gives

C=\biggl(1+\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A}\right)\biggr)^{\binom{n}{2}}.

∎

The normalization constant
C
in (
15
) has no dependence on
G
because
it is fully determined by the number of nodes in the network
n
, the privacy parameter
\varepsilon
, and the adjacency parameter
A
.
We use this normalization constant to formalize the graph exponential mechanism and state the probability of outputting any candidate graph
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, with no privacy budget reserved for the normalization constant.

Mechanism 1

(Graph Exponential Mechanism)
.

Fix a sensitive graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, an adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}
,
and a privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
Then the
graph exponential mechanism

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}
selects and outputs a graph
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}

with probability

\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(G)=H\big]=\frac{\exp(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{A})}{\biggl(1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})\biggr)^{\binom{n}{2}}}.

(19)

The following theorem establishes that Mechanism
1
is differentially private.

Theorem 1
.

Fix a sensitive graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, an adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}
,
and a privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
Then Mechanism
1
is
\varepsilon
-differentially private
with respect to the adjacency relation in Definition
2
.

Proof.

Let
G,G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
be adjacent graphs.
For a candidate output graph
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, we analyze the ratio of the probabilities that the exponential mechanism outputs
H
given the inputs
G
and
G^{\prime}
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(G)=H]}{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(G^{\prime})=H]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon u(G,H)}{A}\bigr)/{\bigl(1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})\bigr)^{\binom{n}{2}}}}{\exp\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon u(G^{\prime},H)}{A}\bigr)/{\bigl(1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})\bigr)^{\binom{n}{2}}}}

(20)

\displaystyle=\exp\biggl(\frac{\varepsilon\bigl(u(G,H)-u(G^{\prime},H)\bigr)}{A}\biggr)

(21)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\biggl(\frac{\varepsilon\big|u(G,H)-u(G^{\prime},H)\big|}{A}\biggr)

(22)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\varepsilon),

(23)

where
(
23
) follows from applying Lemma
3
.
∎

Although Mechanism
1
has an explicit form, it may not be possible to implement it directly.
It is known that
if the set of private outputs is large,
then the exponential mechanism may be difficult to efficiently implement as written
[
30
, Section 3.4]
.
To naively
implement Mechanism
1
, one would assign probabilities to all
2^{\binom{n}{2}}
graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{n}
,
which becomes intractable for even modest values of
n
.
Therefore, we next find an implementable approach for the graph exponential mechanism.

IV
Efficient Implementation Using

a Modified E-R Model

In this section we construct an efficient implementation of Mechanism
1
using a modified E-R model.
For a fixed node set, the conventional E-R model
[
22
]

generates a random graph in which each edge
appears independently and with the
same probability
p\in[0,1]
. In this work we consider two types of edges.
For an input graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
and an output graph
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, we want the edges in
G
to appear in
H
with high probability, while the edges not in
G
should
appear in
H
with low probability.
Mathematically, we do this by using a modified E-R model with two edge probabilities of the form

\mathbb{P}[e\in E(H)]=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} p\quad&e\in E(G)\\
1-p\quad&e\notin E(G)\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases},

(24)

where
p\geq\frac{1}{2}
to ensure that
p\geq 1-p
and hence that edges
in
G
appear in
H
with a higher probability than the edges that do not appear in
G
.
The following mechanism gives an explicit expression for
p
in terms of the privacy parameter
\varepsilon
and the adjacency parameter
A
.

Mechanism 2

(Modified E-R Model)
.

Fix a sensitive graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, an adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}
,
and a privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0.

Consider a candidate output graph
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}.

The edge set of
H
is constructed according to

\mathbb{P}[e\in E(H)]=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})}\quad&e\in E(G)\\
\frac{\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})}{1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})}\quad&e\notin E(G)\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}.

(25)

Modified E-R Model (Mechanism
2
)

with
|V|=n
, adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}
, privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0

Private graph
H
on
V

p\leftarrow\frac{1}{1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})}

H=\big(V,E(H)\big)
with
E(H)=\emptyset

to

n-1

do

to

n

(i,j)\in E(G)

then

With probability
p
, add edge
(i,j)
to
E(H)

9:

else

10:

With probability
(1-p)

(i,j)
to

H

The implementation of Mechanism
2
is summarized in Algorithm
1
.
We use
\mathcal{M}_{E}(G)
to denote a private graph generated from a sensitive graph
G
using Mechanism
2
.
Next, we establish that Mechanism
1
and Mechanism
2

in fact define the same distribution over private graphs.

Theorem 2
.

Fix a sensitive graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
. Then
Mechanism
1
and Mechanism
2
are equivalent for any privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
and adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}

in the sense that they define the same distribution over
\mathcal{G}_{n}
.

Proof.

Given
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
and
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
with
{u(G,H)=-k}
, we analyze the quantities

\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{G}}=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(G)=H\ s.t.\ u(G,H)=-k]

(26)

and

\mathbb{P}_{k}^{E}=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}_{E}(G)=H\ s.t.\ u(G,H)=-k]

(27)

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Here,
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{E}
denote the probabilities that the outputs of Mechanisms
1
and
2
have utility
{-k}
, respectively.
To establish the equivalence of Mechanism
1
and Mechanism
2
,
we show that
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{G}}=\mathbb{P}_{k}^{E}
for an arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

For Mechanism
1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{X}(G,k)}\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(G)=H\big]

(28)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k}}\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon k}{A})}{\biggl(1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})\biggr)^{\binom{n}{2}}},

(29)

where
\mathcal{X}(G,k)
is from (
16
).
Equation (
29
) follows using
|\mathcal{X}(G,k)|=\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k}
,
substituting the probability of outputting
H\in\mathcal{X}(G,k)
from (
19
), and using
u(G,H)=-k
.

For Mechanism
2
, we start from the general form (
24
).
Suppose that
G
and
H
differ in
k
edges, where
H
contains
m

edges that are not in
G
,
H
is missing
\ell
edges that are in
G
,
and
k=\ell+m
.

The probability of adding
m
edges to

E(H)
that were not in

E(G)
is

{\binom{|E(\bar{G})|}{m}p^{|E(\bar{G})|-m}(1-p)^{m},}

(30)

where
\bar{G}
is the complementary graph of
G
and
|E(\bar{G})|=\binom{n}{2}-|E(G)|.

The probability that
E(H)
is missing
\ell
edges that were in
E(G)
is

{\binom{|E(G)|}{\ell}p^{|E(G)|-\ell}(1-p)^{\ell}.}

(31)

Then summing over every possible combination of
k=\ell+m
edge additions and edge deletions,
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{E}
is given by

\mathbb{P}_{k}^{E}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{|E(G)|}{\ell}\binom{|E(\bar{G})|}{k-\ell}p^{|E(G)|+|E(\bar{G})|-k}(1-p)^{k}.

(32)

Substituting in
k=\ell+m
and
|E(G)|+|E(\bar{G})|=\binom{n}{2}

gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k}^{E}

\displaystyle=p^{{\binom{n}{2}}-k}(1-p)^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{|E(G)|}{\ell}\binom{|E(\bar{G}|}{m}

(33)

\displaystyle=p^{{\binom{n}{2}}-k}(1-p)^{k}{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k}},

(34)

where (
34
) follows from the Chu-Vandermonde identity
[
32
, Exercise 3.2(a)]
.

To derive an explicit expression for
p
that sets
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{G}}=\mathbb{P}_{k}^{E}
,
we set (
29
) and (
34
) equal to each other to find

\frac{{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k}}\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon k}{A})}{\biggl(1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})\biggr)^{\binom{n}{2}}}=p^{{\binom{n}{2}}-k}(1-p)^{k}{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k}}.

(35)

Dividing by
{\binom{\binom{n}{2}}{k}}
and taking logs of both sides, we reach

-{\binom{n}{2}}\log\biggl(1+\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A}\right)\biggr)-\frac{k\varepsilon}{A}\\
=\left({\binom{n}{2}}-k\right)\log(p)+k\log(1-p),

(36)

which holds for any
k\in\{0,\ldots,\binom{n}{2}\}
. For simplicity, we set
k=0
to obtain

-{\binom{n}{2}}\log\biggl(1+\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A}\right)\biggr)={\binom{n}{2}}\log(p),

(37)

which gives

p=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-\frac{\varepsilon}{A})},

(38)

which is the same form for
p
presented in (
25
). Then Mechanism
1
and Mechanism
2
are equivalent for any privacy parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
and adjacency parameter
A\in\mathbb{N}
.
It can be verified that this equivalence holds all
k\in\{0,\ldots,\binom{n}{2}\}
.
∎

Theorem
2
shows that Mechanisms
1
and
2
are equivalent. As demonstrated in Theorem
1
, Mechanism
1
enforces
\varepsilon
-differential privacy.
Therefore, these theorems together imply that graphs generated with Mechanism
2
also keep
a sensitive graph
\varepsilon
-differentially private.

We observe that the limiting behavior of
\varepsilon
is intuitive.
First recall that privacy is strengthened when
\varepsilon
is smaller.
For
\varepsilon=\infty
, privacy is absent and
p=1
,
which means that Mechanisms
1

and
2
both simply output their sensitive input graphs with no modifications.
Conversely, for
\varepsilon=0
, any two adjacent input graphs must produce private output
graphs with identical statistics. This condition is indeed enforced because
p=\frac{1}{2}

when
\varepsilon=0
, which indicates that each privacy mechanism ignores the sensitive
input graph and samples an output graph uniformly from the space of output graphs.

Algorithm
1
(which implements Mechanism
2
) constructs
H
by performing a biased coin flip for each possible edge.
After computing the probabilities in (
25
) in constant time, the algorithm executes
\binom{n}{2}
constant-time steps.
Therefore the time complexity of Algorithm
1
is
\Theta(n^{2}).

In contrast, a naive implementation of Mechanism
1

has complexity
\Theta(2^{\binom{n}{2}})
, which makes such an implementation infeasible for even moderate graph sizes.
Overall, Mechanism
2
reduces the complexity of sampling
private output graphs
from
\Theta(2^{\binom{n}{2}})
to

\Theta(n^{2})
.

V-A

Privacy Mechanism Setup

First, we define the graph Laplacian and its spectrum.
Given a graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, let
d_{i}=|\{j\in V\ |\ (i,j)\in E\}|
denote the degree of node
i\in V
. The degree matrix
D(G)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the diagonal matrix
D(G)=\textnormal{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n}).
The adjacency matrix of
G
is

\big(Z(G)\big)_{ij}=\begin{cases}1\quad\textnormal{if}\ (i,j)\in E\\
0\quad\textnormal{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(39)

The Laplacian of a graph
G
is defined as
L(G)=D(G)-Z(G)
, where
L
is symmetric and hence has real eigenvalues.
Let the eigenvalues of
L
be ordered according to
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}
. The matrix
L
is positive semi-definite, and thus
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Furthermore, every undirected graph
G
has
\lambda_{1}=0
, which makes its value non-sensitive because
it is graph-independent. We therefore seek to provide privacy to
\{\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}
.

The bounded Laplace mechanism from
[
11
]
privatizes
\{\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}
by adding bounded Laplace noise independently to each eigenvalue.
Privately computing these eigenvalues requires
first computing each eigenvalue and then adding noise to it, which amounts to conducting
(n-1)
queries of the underlying sensitive graph
G
.
Lemma
1
shows that if the bounded Laplace mechanism provides
\lambda_{i}
with
\varepsilon_{bl}
-differential privacy
for each
i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
,
then the underlying graph is protected by
(n-1)\varepsilon_{bl}
-differential privacy overall.

On the other hand, Mechanism
2
generates an entire private graph
\mathcal{M}_{E}(G)
while guaranteeing
\varepsilon
-differential privacy. Since differential privacy is immune to post-processing according to Lemma
2
, any property/properties of
\mathcal{M}_{E}(G)
can be computed
and
\varepsilon
-differential privacy is preserved.
To implement Mechanism
2
, we synthesize a private graph
\mathcal{M}_{E}(G)
with
\varepsilon
-differential privacy, and compute
\{\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}
using the Laplacian of
\mathcal{M}_{E}(G)
, which is simply post-processing.

2
and the bounded Laplace mechanism from
[

\varepsilon=(n-1)\varepsilon_{bl}.

(40)

VI
Conclusion

This paper presented a differentially private mechanism for privatizing the edge sets of undirected complex networks. We first defined a utility function and then proposed the graph exponential mechanism. To make it computationally efficient, we developed a modified E-R model to independently add each edge
to a private output graph.
Numerical results showed that the proposed mechanism is more accurate
than the state of the art
for computing the private spectra of graph Laplacians.
Future work includes generalizing the proposed mechanism to directed and weighted graphs, and using it to compute other algebraic graph properties.

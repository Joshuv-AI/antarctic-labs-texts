Chromosome-level genome assembly of the cricket Dianemobius fascipes (Insecta: Orthoptera)
Authors: Hao Yuan, Yonghao Ji, Yanna Zheng, Xuanzeng Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Dianemobius fascipes belongs to the superfamily Grylloidea within the order Orthoptera. Orthoptera is the only known insect order that has undergone genome gigantism, making it an ideal model for studying the mechanisms of genome expansion and contraction. In this study, we assembled a chromosome-level genome of D. fascipes using a combination of HiFi sequencing and Hi-C technology. The assembled genome is 1.676 Gb in size, with a scaffold N50 of 180.3 Mb, and 97.34% of contigs were anchored to nine chromosomes. The final assembly exhibited high completeness, with a BUSCO score of 98.2%. Genome annotation revealed that repetitive sequences accounted for 41.55% of the assembly, and 14,829 protein-coding genes were identified. This high-quality genome assembly of D. fascipes provides a valuable resource for comparative genomic analyses of orthopteran insects. In addition, this chromosome-level genome assembly provides a foundation for investigating the evolution and underlying mechanisms of genome gigantism.

Background & Summary

Dianemobius fascipes (Walker, 1869) (Orthoptera: Grylloidea) is distributed in China, Japan, India, Sri Lanka, and other regions. The genus Dianemobius comprises 12 known species worldwide. This species is small in body size, with males measuring 5.26 ± 1.31 mm in body length (BL) and females 6.67 ± 1.21 mm. In both sexes, the forewings do not extend to the end of the abdomen; males lack hind wings, while females possess reduced hind wings. The species is named for the characteristic black-and-white markings on its legs (Fig. 1a)1. The sex determination system of Dianemobius fascipes is X0♂/XX♀2. Orthoptera is the only group in the class Insecta known to exhibit significant genome expansion (0.93–21.48 Gb)3–7. In contrast to Acrididae and Tettigoniidae, which have undergone genome gigantism, Grylloidea represents a lineage with relatively compact genomes (0.93–4.58 Gb) within Orthoptera8,9. The ancestral genome size of Orthoptera is estimated to be ~4.764 Gb10, suggesting that Grylloidea did not experience genome expansion and may even have undergone genome contraction during ~300 million years of evolution. To date, no systematic studies have investigated the composition of repetitive sequences and the accumulation patterns of transposable elements (TEs) in contracted genomes. In this study, we selected D. fascipes, a Grylloidea species with one of the more compact genomes within Orthoptera, as our focal taxon. We generated a chromosome-level de novo genome assembly, providing a valuable resource for exploring genome expansion and contraction.

We assembled a high-quality chromosome-level genome of D. fascipes by integrating 131.75 Gb (72 × ) short reads, 104.67 Gb (62 × ) PacBio HiFi reads, and 225.4 Gb (134 × ) Hi-C data (Table 1). The final chromosome-level assembly resulted in a genome size of 1.676 Gb, with a scaffold N50 length of 180.3 Mb. Hi-C scaffolding anchored 97.34% of the initial sequences to nine pseudochromosomes (Fig. 1). The genome contained 696.45 Mb repeat sequences, accounting for 41.55%. In total, 14,827 protein-coding genes were identified through a rigorous annotation process integrating homology and transcriptome evidence, with a BUSCO completeness of 97.4% (arthropoda_odb10). Methods

Sample collection and sequencing

The Samples of D. fascipes were collected from Daming Mountain, Nanning, Guangxi, China (23°28′55.0′′N, 108°26′32.3′′E) in May 2025. Live adults were taken to the laboratory for dissection. After removing the digestive tract, tissues were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C for subsequent DNA and RNA extraction. The hind legs of females were used to extract DNA for genome short-reads sequencing, PacBio HiFi sequencing, and Hi-C sequencing. Whole bodies of both females and males were used separately for RNA extraction for RNA-seq.

DNA for genome short-reads sequencing was extracted using an SDS-based lysis method and subsequently purified with chloroform. The extracted DNA was sonicated to a fragment size of 350 bp, before being fixed onto a microarray by bridge PCR and sequenced using the DNBSEQ-T7RS platform (PE150bp). For RNA-seq, nucleic acids were extracted using TRIzol, and mRNA was purified with the Dynabeads® mRNA Purification Kit. RNA sequencing libraries were constructed with the MGIEasy RNA Library Preparation Kit (MGI, 1000005274). The libraries were sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7RS platform, generating 150 bp paired-end reads. For long-read sequencing, ~30 kb large-insert SMRTbell libraries were prepared using the SMRTbell® prep kit 3.0 (PacBio, 102–182–700) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The libraries were then sequenced on the PacBio Revio platform. Hi-C libraries were constructed from cross-linked genomic DNA of haploid female adults and sequenced to generate 150 bp paired-end reads on the DNBSEQ-T7RS platform.

PacBio HiFi Circular Consensus Sequence (CCS) reads were generated using the CCS algorithm, requiring at least two full-pass subreads per insert. The HiFi reads produced 104.67 Gb (approximately 61 × coverage), with an average read length of 20,140 bp. Genome survey sequencing yielded 131.75 Gb (72 × ) of clean short-reads, while Hi-C sequencing produced 225.4 Gb (134 × ). For transcriptome sequencing, 8.49 Gb of clean reads were obtained from the female sample and 9.38 Gb from the male sample.

Genome survey and assembly

The clean short-read data generated from genome survey sequencing was used to estimate genome size via 21-mer frequency analysis. A 21-mer frequency distribution was generated using Jellyfish (v2.1.4)11, followed by genomic feature calculation with Genomescope (v2.0)12 (-k 21 -p 2). This analytical pipeline estimated a haploid genome size of 1.269 Gb for D. fascipes, whereas earlier flow cytometry estimates indicated a genome size of 1.86 pg (~1.84 Gb)9, suggesting that genome size estimation based on k-mer frequency may have some error. The k-mer frequency distribution was bimodal with 1.26% heterozygosity, indicating relatively high heterozygosity (Fig. 1b). Similarly, the orthopteran insect Eucriotettix oculatus also exhibited relatively high heterozygosity at 1.12%13.

A total of 104.67 Gb (62×) PacBio HiFi reads and 225.4 Gb (134×) Hi-C data were used as input for a preliminary genome assembly with Hifiasm (v0.24)14,15. Subsequently, minimap216 and Purge_dups (https://github.com/dfguan/purge_dups) were used for redundancy removal to obtain the draft genome.

To achieve a chromosome-level assembly, Hi-C scaffolding was performed using a dual-phase strategy that combined error correction with chromatin interaction–guided assembly. Corrected contigs were aligned with Hi-C paired-end reads using BWA (v0.7.18)17, and the resulting contact matrix revealed clear interaction patterns. These patterns were computationally resolved with HapHiC (v1.0.7)18 to order and orient the contigs into large-scale scaffolds, which were subsequently manually refined using Juicebox (v2.17)19.

The final chromosomal-level assembly was achieved through manual curation of interaction heatmaps, anchoring 1.676 Gb (97.34% of total genome) (Table 2) across nine chromosomes (Figs. 1c and 2), ranging from 239.69 Mb to 70.31 Mb with the scaffold N50 lengths of 180.3 Mb (Table 3). The final chromosome-level assembly contained 231 gaps, with a total of 23,100 ambiguous bases (Ns). Repeat annotation

The repeat content of the D. fascipes genome was analyzed using RepeatModeler2 and RepeatMasker20. RepeatModeler2 was used for the automated genomic discovery of transposable element families (https://github.com/Dfam-consortium/TETools) (BuildDatabase -name Spname genome.fa) (RepeatModeler -database Spname -LTRStruct). RepeatMasker was used to analyze the composition of repetitive sequences in the genome (http://repeatmasker.org) (RepeatMasker -html -gff -poly -lib genome.fa -dir). In the D. fascipes genome, 41.55% of the sequences were identified as repetitive, of which transposable elements (TEs) accounted for 36.06%. Among them, LINEs represented 9.17%, LTR elements 1.47%, SINEs 0.92%, and DNA transposons 2.31% (Table 4). The detailed information on repeat sequence content is provided in Supplementary Table S1. Notably, approximately 22.18% of the genome consisted of unclassified TEs, which were detected as repetitive elements with multiple copies but could not be annotated with current databases. The presence of a large proportion of unclassified TEs is common in orthopteran genomes21,22. Repeat annotation files in GFF format were obtained from RepeatMasker and used to visualize the genomic density of different classes of repetitive elements. We employed bedtools to construct genome window files, with a window size of 200 kb for D. fascipes genome. TE coverage within each genomic window was calculated using bedtools. The genomic circos plots and TE coverage visualization were generated using the Advanced Circos tool in TBtools-II23 (Fig. 2).

Gene structure annotation

Gene structure prediction was performed on the repeat-masked genome using BRAKER324, which integrates de novo gene prediction, transcriptomic evidence, and homology-based protein prediction. It enables the usage of RNA-seq and protein data in a fully automated pipeline to train and predict highly reliable genes with GeneMark-ETP and AUGUSTUS24. The result of the pipeline is the combined gene set of both gene prediction tools, which only contains genes with very high support from extrinsic evidence. First, RNA-seq data from both male and female individuals were mapped to the genome using HISAT225. Subsequently, the BAM files of RNA-seq alignments, protein sequences from Orthoptera species, and the genome FASTA file were used as input for gene structure prediction with BRAKER3.

A total of 14,827 genes were identified, comprising 17,853 transcripts. Transcriptomic evidence incorporated into gene structure prediction confirmed and characterized alternative splicing in 2,385 genes. The average gene length was 18,880 bp, with an average CDS length of 1,661 bp, and each gene contained an average of 7.7 exons (Table 5). Gene functional annotation

The 17,853 predicted proteins from gene structure annotation were subjected to functional annotation against various databases. First, functional annotation against the Non-redundant (Nr), Swiss-Prot, and TrEMBL databases was performed using DIAMOND (v2.0.11)27–29. Subsequently, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways, Gene Ontology (GO) terms, Pfam, eggNOG, and COG annotations were obtained using eggNOG-mapper (v2.1.13)30–33. Finally, InterProScan (v5.74) was used to perform annotation based on the InterPro database34. Ultimately, 17,074 protein sequences were functionally annotated in at least one database, representing 95.64% of the total predicted proteins (Table 6). Data Records

PacBio HiFi, RNA-seq, short-read genome sequencing, and Hi-C sequencing data have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive under accession numbers SRR35650769–SRR3565077335–39, as part of BioProject PRJNA1335628 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/PRJNA1335628)40. The chromosome-level genome assembly of Dianemobius fascipes, repeat sequence annotation, gene annotation files, and protein-coding gene sequence files have been deposited to Figshare (10.6084/m9.figshare.30225331.v2)41.

Technical Validation

The accuracy and completeness of the genome assembly of D. fascipes were evaluated using multiple analyses. First, the Hi-C heatmap demonstrated consistent results across all chromosomes, confirming the correct contig ordering and orientation in the assembly (Fig. 1c). Second, clean short and long reads were re-mapped to the assembled genes using BWA v0.7.18 and Minimap2 v2.24, yielding mapping rates of 99.58% and 99.90%, respectively. Third, the chromosomal assembly of D. fascipes showed a Hi-C scaffolding percentage of 97.34%, and its completeness was rigorously validated by BUSCO analysis with the arthropoda_odb10 database, yielding a completeness score of 98.2% (Table 3). Detailed ortholog analysis identified 91.93% single-copy, 6.3% duplicated, 0.9% fragmented, and 0.9% missing BUSCOs. Generally, these findings suggest the high quality of the D. fascipes assembly.

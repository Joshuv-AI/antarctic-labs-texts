Rationality of singular cubic threefolds over ℝ {\mathbb{R}}
Ivan Cheltsov, Yuri Tschinkel, Zhijia Zhang

Abstract
. We study rationality properties of real singular cubic threefolds.



1.
Introduction

In this note we focus on rationality properties of singular cubic threefolds over the real numbers.
We are guided by results in equivariant birational geometry in
[
CTZ24
]
,
[
CMTZ24
]
, which elucidated
birationality constructions as well as obstructions to birationality, in the equivariant context.

One of the main problems in birational geometry is the (stable) rationality problem; see, e.g.,

[
MT86
]
,
[
CT95
]
,
[
Che05
]
,
[
Pro18
]
,
[
Pir18
]
,
[
Voi19
]
,
[
Sch21
]
for reports on milestones in this area. Classically, one was interested in algebraically closed ground fields, of characteristic zero,
mostly the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
. However, many (stable) rationality constructions rely crucially on results over nonclosed fields, e.g., function fields of smaller-dimensional varieties: in
[
BCTSSD85
]
this is used to produce nonrational but stably rational threefolds over
{\mathbb{C}}
, via a degree 4 Del Pezzo fibration over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
,
and in
[
AHTVA19
]
rational cubic fourfolds, via a degree 6 Del Pezzo fibration over
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. The survey

[
CT19
]
contains many results concerning rationality over arbitrary fields.

Of particular interest are varieties with simple presentations, such as hypersurfaces in (weighted) projective spaces, or linear sections of Grassmannians.
Among the simplest such varieties are quadric and cubic hypersurfaces. Rationality of quadrics is completely settled: a necessary and sufficient condition is the existence of a rational point.
For cubics, we do not currently have such criteria. Smooth complex cubic threefolds
X_{{\mathbb{C}}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
are irrational
[
CG72
]
, while the singular ones are rational unless they are cones over smooth cubic curves.

In recent years, there have been several results concerning rationality of geometrically rational varieties over
{\mathbb{R}}
. For example, it is now known that rationality of smooth intersections of two quadrics
X_{2,2}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, for
n=5
, is governed by the existence of rational points and lines
[
HT21
]
. For
n=6
, a necessary and sufficient condition is that
X({\mathbb{R}})
is connected
[
HKT22
]
.
The papers
[
BW20
]
,
[
JJ23
]
,
[
CTP24
]
explore rationality of conic and quadric bundles over
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Here, we focus on rationality of singular cubic threefolds

X=X_{{\mathbb{R}}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}

over
{\mathbb{R}}
. Every such
X
has real points.
We recall standard rationality constructions, in presence of distinguished loci, over
{\mathbb{R}}
:

•

Projection from a singular point gives a birational map to
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
,

•

Projection from a line and a disjoint plane gives a birational map
X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

In particular, if the cubic is not a cone and there exists a real singular point, then
X
is rational over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Hence, throughout the paper, we assume that
X
is not a cone and that

X^{\mathrm{sing}}({\mathbb{R}})=\emptyset.

This implies that either the singularities of
X_{{\mathbb{C}}}
are isolated and their number
s=s(X_{{\mathbb{C}}})
is even, i.e.,
s=2,4,6,8,10
, or that
the singular locus has positive dimension.
By
[
Avi20
]
, a real form of a cubic with 10 isolated singularities is rational over
{\mathbb{R}}
– geometrically, there is only one
such cubic, the
Segre
cubic.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2
be the group generated by complex conjugation.
In addition to an action of
\Gamma
on special loci of
X
, such as the singular locus
X^{\mathrm{sing}}
, we have an action on the geometric Picard group

\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
of the minimal resolution of singularities
\tilde{X}
of
X
.
We investigate (stable) rationality over
{\mathbb{R}}
, using the following tools:

•

classification
: we compute all possibilities for the action of
\Gamma
, configurations of singularities, and normal forms; we freely use the terminology and techniques of
[
CTZ24
]
and
[
CMTZ24
]
;

•

topology
: if
X({\mathbb{R}})
is disconnected then
X
is not stably rational;

•

cohomology
:
if

(1.1)

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X}_{{\mathbb{C}}}))\neq 0,

then
X
is not stably rational; we refer to this as the

(H1)
-obstruction.

We summarize the results:

•

s=2
:

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
,
2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
: not rational over
{\mathbb{R}}
.

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}
with no plane: criteria for disconnectedness of
X({\mathbb{R}})
, and examples with
X({\mathbb{R}})
connected or disconnected.

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}
with a plane: rational if and only if there is a real line disjoint from the plane.

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{4}
: criteria for disconnectedness, and examples with
X({\mathbb{R}})
connected or disconnected.

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{5}
: all not stably rational.

–

2{\mathsf{D}}_{4}
: criteria for disconnectedness, examples with rational
X
, and examples with
X({\mathbb{R}})
disconnected.

•

s=4
:

–

4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
with no plane,
4{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
,
2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
are birational over
{\mathbb{R}}
; criteria for disconnectedness, and examples of connected and disconnected
X({\mathbb{R}})
.

–

4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
with a plane: rational if and only if there is a real line disjoint from the plane.

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
with three planes: criteria for disconnectedness,
examples with rational
X
, and with
X({\mathbb{R}})
disconnected.

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
with one plane: always rational.

–

2{\mathsf{D}}_{4}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
: rational if and only if it contains three planes.

•

s=6
:

–

6{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
with no plane: rational if and only if it contains a real cubic scroll.

–

6{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
with one plane: always rational.

–

6{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
with three planes: criteria for disconnectedness, examples with rational
X
, and with
X({\mathbb{R}})
disconnected.

–

2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
,
2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
: always rational.

•

s=8
: rational if and only if it contains three planes.

Geometrically, cubic threefolds with nonisolated singularities are of four types
[
Yok02
]
,
[
All03
]
: with
X^{\rm{sing}}
consisting of a plane, a line, a conic, or a twisted quartic. We show that all real forms of such cubics are rational, unless
X^{\rm{sing}}
is a conic. In this last case, we have examples with connected or disconnected
X({\mathbb{R}})
. The rationality of such cubics is open, see, e.g.,
[
CTP24
]
.

2.
Cohomology

Here, we investigate the
(H1)
-obstruction for cubics with isolated singularities.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a real cubic threefold with isolated singularities over
{\mathbb{C}}
. We have

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X}_{\mathbb{C}}))\neq 0

if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

X_{\mathbb{C}}
has
2{\mathsf{A}}_{5}
-singularities,

(2)

X_{\mathbb{C}}
has
2{\mathsf{D}}_{4}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities, and only one real plane,

(3)

X_{\mathbb{C}}
has
6{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities in linearly general position, and contains no normal cubic scrolls over
{\mathbb{R}}
,

(4)

X_{\mathbb{C}}
has
8{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities, and only one real plane.

Proof.

When
X_{{\mathbb{C}}}
has the indicated configurations of singularities, the proof follows from Propositions
3.5
,
4.5
,
5.3
, and
6.1
. In all other cases, the
(H1)
-obstruction is trivial, as shown in
[
CTZ24
]
,
[
CMTZ24
]
.
∎

We now list representative examples with nontrivial cohomology for each of the cases in Proposition
2.1
.

Example 2.2
.

2{\mathsf{A}}_{5}
:
Over
{\mathbb{C}}
, every such cubic is given by

x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+x_{1}x_{4}^{2}+x_{2}x_{5}^{2}+x_{3}^{3}+bx_{3}x_{4}x_{5}=0,\quad b^{2}\neq-4.

Over
{\mathbb{R}}
, every such cubic is given by

(2.1)

X=\{(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})x_{3}+x_{3}^{3}-2x_{1}(x_{4}^{2}-x_{5}^{2})-4x_{2}x_{4}x_{5}+bx_{3}(x_{4}^{2}+x_{5}^{2})=0\},

for some
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, with singular points

[1:i:0:0:0],\quad[1:-i:0:0:0].

Consider the projection from the real line through the singularities

\pi:X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2},\quad(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\mapsto(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}).

The image

, given by

\left\{x_{3}^{2}+\frac{b-\sqrt{b^{2}+4}}{2}x_{4}^{2}+\frac{b-\sqrt{b^{2}+4}}{2}x_{5}^{2}\leq 0\right\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2},

is connected; it is easy to see that

is also connected.

The Galois action on

is isomorphic to the

-action considered in
[

, which implies that

(2.2)

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X}_{{\mathbb{C}}}))={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}},

hence
X
is not stably rational over
{\mathbb{R}}.

See Proposition
3.5
for details.

3.
Two Singular Points

Assume that
X_{{\mathbb{C}}}
has two complex-conjugated singular points, e.g.,

p_{1}=[1:i:0:0:0],\quad\text{and}\quad p_{2}=[1:-i:0:0:0],

so that
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}}
is given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})x_{3}+x_{1}q_{1}+x_{2}q_{2}+f_{3}=0,

where
q_{1},q_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_{4},x_{5}]
are quadratic forms and

f_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]
is a cubic form. One checks that
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
cannot both vanish if
X_{{\mathbb{C}}}
has
{\mathsf{A}}_{n}
-singularities. If
q_{1}\not\equiv 0
and
q_{2}\equiv 0
, then up to a change of variables,
we have
q_{1}=q_{2}
. Thus, we may assume that none of
q_{1}
or
q_{2}
is
0.

Each of
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
defines two points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{x_{4},x_{5}}({\mathbb{C}})
, with one of the following possibilities:

•

one real point with multiplicity
2
, or

•

two real points, or

•

two conjugate points.

Combining the configuration patterns of four points defined by
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, we find all possibilities of the pair
(q_{1},q_{2})
, up to isomorphism:

q_{1}

q_{2}

x_{4}^{2}

\lambda x_{4}^{2}

x_{5}^{2}

x_{4}x_{5}

\lambda(x_{4}^{2}-x_{5}^{2})

\lambda(x_{4}^{2}+x_{5}^{2})

x_{4}x_{5}

\lambda x_{4}x_{5}

\lambda x_{4}(x_{4}-x_{5})

\lambda(x_{4}^{2}-x_{5}^{2})

\lambda(x_{4}^{2}+x_{5}^{2})

x_{4}^{2}+\lambda x_{5}^{2}

\lambda>0
everywhere

Introducing new coordinates

y_{1}=x_{1}x_{3}+\frac{q_{1}}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad y_{2}=x_{2}x_{3}+\frac{q_{2}}{2},

and multiplying (

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}}{4}-f_{3}x_{3}\geq 0.

.

Assume that

∎

\{u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}+u_{3}^{2}+\Delta(x_{5})u_{4}^{2}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}}.

It is clear that its real locus is empty if and only if
\Delta(x_{5})>0
. It follows that
X({\mathbb{R}})
is disconnected if and only if
\Delta\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
as a quartic polynomial has four distinct real roots.
∎

.

Chordal cubic

A cubic
X_{{\mathbb{C}}}
singular along a rational normal quartic curve
C
is unique: it is the secant variety of
C
, also known as the
chordal cubic
, see, e.g.,
[
All03
]
or
[
Yok02
]
. Over
{\mathbb{R}}
, and
under the assumption
C({\mathbb{R}})=\emptyset
,
there is a unique such cubic
X
, given by

(7.2)

\displaystyle\{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}x_{3}^{2}+x_{1}x_{4}^{2}-2x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}-(x_{1}+x_{2})x_{5}^{2}=0\}.

Proposition 7.3
.

The chordal cubic
X
given by (
7.2
) is rational.

Proof.

Let
C=X^{{\rm sing}}
. The linear system
|{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(2)-C|
has dimension 5 and gives a contraction

\varphi:X\dashrightarrow S,

where
S={\mathbb{P}}^{2}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{5}
is the Veronese surface. Projection from a general plane
\Pi
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
gives a rational map

\pi:X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}.

The product

\pi\times\varphi:X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

is a birational map. It follows that
X
is rational over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

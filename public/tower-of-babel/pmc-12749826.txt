COGEniX: an industrial informatics framework for cognitive energy forecasting and carbon-aware scheduling in smart zones
Authors: Alanoud Al Mazroa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIndustrial energy management faces escalating challenges from rising electricity costs, volatile carbon intensity and the operational complexity of integrating renewables and storage. Traditional approaches, ranging from heuristic rules to black-box machine learning models, struggle to balance adaptability, transparency and deployability. Heuristic rules fail to adjust to dynamic operating conditions, while opaque models undermine operator trust, and deployment friction often prevents reliable execution on edge hardware. As a result, factories continue to suffer from inefficiency, idle consumption and missed opportunities for cost-effective decarbonization. This study introduces COGEniX (Cognitive Energy Informatics and eXecution), a unified framework that integrates calibrated multi-horizon forecasting, reinforcement learning (RL) based scheduling, explainability and deterministic edge deployment. Experiments on an adapted CityLearn dataset simulating microgrid-style industrial zones compare COGEniX against three representative baselines such as a rule-based scheduler using time-of-use heuristics, a gradient-boosted regression forecaster with deterministic dispatch, and a short-horizon model predictive controller. Validation on the Kaggle multi-floor smart building dataset demonstrates 44.0% lower MAE and 39.3% lower RMSE with 15.3% energy savings and 18.1% CO2 reduction at edge-feasible latency, confirming both predictive and operational gains. Patch-based transformers generate calibrated load and price forecasts that expose temporal drivers, while a Soft Actor–Critic scheduler optimizes a joint objective over cost and carbon under uncertainty and process constraints. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) explanations align with attention to yield interpretable decision narratives, and ONNX(Open Neural Network Exchange)/TorchScript export with pruning, quantization and profiling ensures compliance with latency and memory limits on real devices. Evaluation across CityLearn industrial zones shows up to a 27% reduction in operational cost and a 31% decrease in CO2 emissions compared with the strongest baseline, maintaining robustness under forecast noise. By coupling forecasting, control, interpretability and deployment in a cohesive pipeline, COGEniX advances industrial energy management toward adaptive, carbon-aware and auditable decision-making that aligns profitability with sustainability.Keywords: Industrial energy management, Reinforcement learning scheduling, Forecasting with transformers, Explainable AI (SHAP and attention), Edge deployment optimizationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Industrial energy management lies at the intersection of economic pressure, environmental responsibility and operational complexity. Although modern factories stream high-resolution telemetry, many still depend on manual or rule-based routines that cannot keep pace with volatile tariffs, time-varying carbon intensity and the coupled dynamics of on-site renewables and storage1–5. The consequence is persistent inefficiency and high idle consumption, while operators lack tools that anticipate and adapt to changing conditions2. Industry accounts for roughly one third of global electricity use6 and tightening emissions policies raise the costs of energy and non-compliance7. Renewable penetration is increasing but still the intermittency and forecast errors continue to cause missed opportunities to exploit low-cost and low-carbon energy or force grid reliance at the worst moments8. Early automation via heuristics and simple statistics offered incremental gains but did not scale to multi-asset factories9. Conventional deep learning often targeted commercial buildings, remained opaque and struggled to build operator trust10. With waste reported up to twelve percent per cycle in some settings11, industry needs a unified, explainable and efficient approach that reasons jointly about loads, prices and emissions under uncertainty and executes reliably on constrained edge hardware12,13.

The bottleneck is not a better predictor or a more aggressive optimiser in isolation but the absence of an integrated mechanism that couples accurate multi horizon forecasts with adaptive constraint aware control and that explains its actions in terms engineers can audit. In practice plants maintain granular telemetry for energy, price and carbon signals, yet scheduling remains static and insensitive to ramp rates, market events and stochastic process loads14–16. We observe three recurring pain points, namely nonadaptivity that fails to shift when carbon intensity spikes or tariffs change intraday17, opacity that blocks validation when models act as black boxes18 and deployment friction that strands promising models because of edge latency and memory limits. Typical pipelines are omitted with uncertainty quantification, lack safety checks and also they doesnt provide audit trail19, while the reinforcement learning efforts often require substantial training effort and rarely expose transparent rationales20. From a practical perspective, operating disjoint forecasting, control, explainability and deployment stacks multiplies integration and governance overhead and shifts operational costs into hidden budgets. A principled bridge from data to decisions to deployment is therefore required to deliver cost effective and carbon aware schedules together with governance artifacts produced by construction21,22.

COGEniX responds with an industrial informatics pipeline for cognitive energy forecasting and carbon aware scheduling tailored to smart industrial zones23. The system integrates data analysis and feature engineering that include normalization, temporal alignment, lag and delta construction and the inclusion of exogenous drivers such as carbon intensity and tariffs24. PatchTST forecasters produce multi step load and price predictions and attention maps that highlight influential temporal fragments25. A Soft Actor Critic (SAC) scheduler optimizes a reward that internalizes both cost and emissions while encoding process limits and promoting a stable policy through entropy regularization26. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) based explanations decompose forecasts and actions into per feature attributions that support engineering review and audit27. A dashboard surfaces accuracy, savings, emissions and trust and deployment readiness is achieved through ONNX (Open Neural Network Exchange.) and TorchScript export with pruning, quantization and profiling across devices for low latency execution28,29. The framework is developed and evaluated on the CityLearn environment, adapted to microgrid-like industrial conditions with heterogeneous zones, storage and variable tariffs30, and further validated on the Kaggle multi-floor smart building dataset (2018–2019) to assess generalization under real-world load and price dynamics. The scheduler links forecast variance to action selection to avoid brittle behavior under noisy horizons31. In aggregate the integration replaces repeated point to point glue with a single pathway that amortizes engineering effort across many sites and reduces total cost of ownership by collapsing duplicated interfaces and audits.

The design is a systems solution rather than a single algorithm. At the perception and forecasting layer, PatchTST (Patch Time-Series Transformer) uses patch wise embeddings that preserve local industrial rhythms such as shifts and batch runs and attention provides native interpretability25. At the decision layer, the Soft Actor Critic method translates economic and environmental goals into an entropy regularized control problem that makes explicit how tariffs, carbon intensity and process limits shape actions26. The policy is carbon aware and it schedules flexible loads and storage to minimize a joint cost that internalizes emissions penalties rather than price alone4,13. At the explanation layer, SHAP provides per feature and per decision attributions that can be cross checked against attention to generate trust scores and to flag suspect recommendations27,32. At the deployment layer, models are exported through ONNX and TorchScript and are compressed and profiled to meet edge latency, memory and throughput budgets28,29. This end to end alignment reduces operational cost in practice because calibration connects forecasting to safe control, explainability connects actions to governance and edge first engineering connects laboratory models to production without bespoke refits.

This study contributes four innovations and their integration. The first is bidirectional interpretability that uses attention and SHAP as cross validating views to quantify agreement and to surface divergences27,32. The second is price and carbon co optimization that embeds carbon intensity in both the reward and the state and enables exploitation of low marginal emissions even when price signals are weak4,13. The third is uncertainty aware control that adjusts exploration and action strength according to forecast confidence31. The fourth is edge first deployment that treats export, compression and hardware in the loop profiling as core functions in order to guarantee deterministic latency and memory footprints28,29. Beyond their individual merit, the integration lowers total cost of ownership by eliminating duplicated data pipelines, interfaces and audits across siloed components, which is a central requirement for application driven deployments.

Compared with prior systems, our novelty lies not in a single algorithm but in a deployment ready closed loop where calibrated multi horizon forecasting, uncertainty aware control, transparent explanations and deterministic edge execution are jointly engineered. PatchTST forecasts are integrated with a Soft Actor Critic scheduler that internalizes carbon and price signals, exploration is modulated by forecast confidence, process constraints are enforced and attention maps and SHAP attributions are fused to yield auditable decision narratives. Export, compression and per device profiling via ONNX and TorchScript with pruning and quantization are treated as first class design principles that ensure policies validated in simulation remain safe, low latency and repeatable on real edge hardware28,29. On adapted CityLearn industrial scenarios, the framework achieves measurable cost and emission gains while producing governance artifacts that include attribution traces, attention alignment and profiling reports that justify operational integration costs and support the practical adoption of autonomous scheduling. These results are further validated on the Kaggle multi-floor smart building dataset (2018–2019), demonstrating consistent performance and confirming that the learned policies generalize across heterogeneous real-world load and tariff conditions.23,30,33.

Related work

Building on the introduction and the stated research objectives, related work spans four interdependent strands: demand response and scheduling, time-series forecasting, reinforcement learning for control and explainability and deployment. However, few efforts integrate all four in the context of complex industrial facilities. In 2023, Liu et al.34 reviewed the convergence of deep reinforcement learning with forecasting for energy management and highlighted the difficulty of stitching together forecasting, control and interpretability in a single workflow. In the same year, Kim et al.35 synthesized literature across industrial energy optimization and concluded that most studies optimize one module at a time, which limits real-world adoption where decisions must connect forecasts, constraints and accountability. This fragmentation motivates a system like COGEniX, which explicitly aligns multi-horizon prediction, carbon- and price-aware control and audit-ready deployment under edge constraints.

Historically, demand response emerged in the 1990s through heuristic peak shaving and then matured into rule-based and mixed-integer programming formulations that depend critically on forecast quality; in 2020, Haffron et al.36 documented this trajectory and identified brittleness to forecast errors as a recurring bottleneck. As the renewable penetration rose, early statistical tools such as ARIMA and exponential smoothing have struggled with nonstationarity and multi-scale variability, prompting a wave of SVR (Support Vector Regression) , boosting and LSTM-based models; in 2023, Jabeur et al.37 showed that while these methods reduced average error, they often faltered on long sequences and provided limited interpretability and update speed for factory-grade use. The net effect is that conventional schedulers are only as good as their forecasts and conventional forecasters rarely expose actionable rationales tied to tariffs or carbon intensity, precisely the insight-to-action gap addressed by the stated objectives.

From a control perspective, the mid-2010s saw the adoption of reinforcement learning for development of HVAC, storage and microgrid coordination, culminating in actor–critic variants that handle continuous actions. In 2022, Ganesh et al.38 has surveyed this line and reported that many demonstrations remains residential or in a state of testbed-centric, with limited evidence in high-stakes industrial environments where safety and process interlocks are paramount. Safe RL subsequently introduced constraints and shields; however, in 2023, Ceusters et al.39 noted that reproducible industrial case studies remain scarce and that training overhead, reward shaping and policy transparency still hinder deployment. These observations motivate embedding constraint awareness and uncertainty sensitivity directly into the RL scheduler so that actions remain stable and explainable under volatile prices and carbon signals.

On the sequence modeling front, the transformer architecture have introduced by Vaswani et al. in 2017 reshaped time-series forecasting via attention mechanisms that capture long-range dependencies40. PatchTST refines thisby the idea of long-horizon forecasting by operating on patches to preserve local temporal structure at a lower cost; yet, industrial uptake remains modest due to concerns about transparency and field reliability. In parallel, explainable AI matured through Shapley-value methods and local surrogates; in 2023, Barredo et al.41 emphasized that SHAP and LIME can increase stakeholder trust but are rarely fused with RL to justify continuous control decisions. Deployment is a further obstacle: in 2023, Plathottam et al.42 described a persistent deployment gap wherein models that excel offline fail to meet SCADA-tested edge constraints and naive compression can degrade accuracy or introduce latency jitter incompatible with industrial timing. Editorial perspectives in 2023 by Ng et al.43 likewise called for methods that are simultaneously accurate, interpretable and implementation-ready for sustainable industrial systems.

Recent attempts have begun to connect forecasting, control and interpretability, but gaps remain when stepping from buildings to factories. In 2023, Kohne et al.44 surveyed emerging trends that couple predictors and controllers, yet most studies still evaluate in residential or campus contexts with simplified constraints. CityLearn has been influential as a benchmark for multi-agent scheduling; in 2021, Yu et al45 cataloged deep learning strategies that perform well in this environment. However, in 2023, Weinberg et al.46 and Song et al47 2023 have argued that CityLearn abstractions overlook industrial realities such as batch-process deadlines, interlocks and quality-of-service penalties, limiting direct transfer without additional modules for constraint handling and auditability. Complementary industrial studies exist but are siloed: digital-twin RL for pulp-mill steam networks improved coordination at the cost of extensive modeling effort, while in 2023, Ferreira et al.48 reported low forecasting errors in aluminum smelting using Informer but reverted to rule-based scheduling, breaking the insight-to-action link.

These patterns suggest that step-change efficiency requires an end-to-end approach that unifies multi-horizon forecasting, adaptive scheduling and explanation with explicit attention to deployment. In 2023, Yao et al.49 advocated converged AI architectures for industrial energy that avoid the handoffs and interface losses between standalone modules. Following this guidance, COGEniX integrates transformer-based forecasting, uncertainty-aware SAC control and bidirectional interpretability into a pipeline that also ships as an ONNX and TorchScript artifact profiled for edge execution. In 2023, Lu et al.50 described elements of such a direction for RL-driven industrial scheduling. Still, without the combined emphasis on SHAP–attention agreement and edge-first profiling emphasized here. Aligning with the 2022 call by Ng et al.43 for green manufacturing, the focus is not only on performance but also on transparency and operational fit, so that schedules are economically rational, carbon-aware and auditable.

In summary, prior work has advanced each pillar: forecasting, control, interpretability and optimization, but the literature still lacks a cohesive system that closes the loop from data to deployment in industrial settings. The shortcomings are consistent across decades: rules that cannot adapt without robust forecasts, forecasts that lack actionable rationale, controllers that optimize without explaining and models that fail to meet the latency and memory constraints of edge devices. COGEniX is proposed to address these gaps in continuity with the stated objectives: interpretable transformer forecasts expose temporal opportunities for shifting, an uncertainty-aware SAC translates those insights into safe carbon- and price-aware actions and SHAP plus attention deliver decision narratives suitable for audit, all packaged through ONNX and TorchScript and profiled to run where industrial decisions are executed.

As shown in Table 8, a detailed analysis was conducted to explain the negative cost saving observed in Zone 4. While most zones achieved positive cost reductions between $174.17 and $315.33, Zone 4 exhibited a negative saving of $44.87, corresponding to a 3.66% cost increase from a base cost of $1,226.95 to an RL cost of $1,271.82. Energy consumed under the RL policy increased from 28.45 to 60.63 units, an absolute rise of 32.18 units and a relative increase of 113.11%, indicating added activity rather than effective price arbitrage. This outcome reflects a failure of temporal cost optimization, where control actions were executed during mid- or high-price periods instead of exploiting low-price intervals. The result was linked to limited local flexibility and a small storage-to-load ratio that constrained shifting opportunities, compounded by localized forecast volatility that prompted conservative decisions since constraint violations were heavily penalized during training. Reward shaping that prioritized emissions reduction in certain episodes further contributed by steering the policy toward lower-emission but higher-cost energy sources. To mitigate these effects, several strategies were recommended and partially evaluated, including zone-specific reward reweighting, process-aware action masking, targeted data augmentation or transfer learning for underrepresented zones, and integration of short-horizon model predictive checks to prevent costly exploratory actions. Incorporating these diagnostics and mitigation steps enhances the interpretability of negative outcomes and supports reliable deployment of RL-based scheduling across heterogeneous industrial zones.

Conclusion and future work

Industrial energy management requires intelligent, explainable and adaptive control that connects forecasting, scheduling and deployment. This study introduced COGEniX, a cohesive pipeline that integrates calibrated patch-based transformers for multi-horizon prediction, uncertainty- and constraint-aware reinforcement learning for carbon- and cost-sensitive scheduling and attribution tools that align model attention with per-decision explanations. Evaluation on CityLearn have adapted a microgrid-style industrial conditions, demonstrated that calibrated forecasts and risk-aware control translate into sustained cost and emissions reductions, while edge-ready deployment is enabled through ONNX conversion and quantization to meet latency and memory budgets. The dashboard and profiling suite close the loop from data to insight to action to assurance, providing operators with transparent KPIs and trust signals. Taken together, the pipeline advances industrial informatics by turning high-resolution telemetry into auditable schedules that align operational efficiency with decarbonization goals and are ready for field execution.

Looking ahead, three research directions can extend this work. First, cross-domain transferability across sectors such as chemical processing, pulp and paper and district heating should be explored to test robustness under diverse constraints and asset mixes. Second, integration with physical models through physics-informed learning can couple first-principle equipment dynamics with data-driven modules, strengthening both safety and interpretability. Finally, multi-agent coordination offers an opportunity to extend the current policy toward distributed optimization across buildings, plants and storage resources under shared grid and carbon constraints. Together, these directions highlight pathways for scaling COGEniX into a generalizable framework for sustainable industrial energy management.

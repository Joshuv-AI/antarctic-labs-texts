Antimicrobial Resistance, Molecular Epidemiology, and Virulence Profiles of Hypervirulent Carbapenem-Resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae Isolates from a Tertiary Hospital in Zunyi, Southwest China, 2021–2023
Authors: Meijing Shen, Xianghao Chen, Benhai Chen, Rengui Tian, Guangwu Yang, He Zha, Kaifeng Wu, Shifei Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo systematically analyze the drug resistance phenotype, virulence characteristics, and molecular epidemiological characteristics of hypervirulent carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (hv-CRKP) in Zunyi area, and to provide experimental and epidemiological evidence for clinical accurate diagnosis and treatment, rational selection of antibiotics, and prevention and control of nosocomial infection of hv-CRKP in the region.MethodsA total of 91 hv-CRKP strains were collected from the First People’s Hospital of Zunyi from 2021 to 2023. Drug susceptibility test was performed for commonly used antibiotics in clinic. Molecular typing included PCR detection of carbapenemase gene, multilocus sequence typing (MLST), membrane porin (OmpK) deletion analysis, and capsule serotyping. The virulence phenotype of hv-CRKP was comprehensively evaluated by biofilm formation, serum resistance, and Galleria mellonella larval infection tests.Resultshv-CRKP strains were highly resistant to commonly used antibiotics, but the resistance rate to tigecycline was relatively low, only 3.3%. The clones carrying virulence genes iucA, iroB and rmpA2 were the dominant clones, accounting for 83.52%. Most of them carried Carbapenem resistance genes blaKPC-2, with a positive rate of 95.60%. The predominant sequence type of hv-CRKP isolates was ST11, accounting for 87.90%. The expression of OmpK35 and OmpK37 was characterized by full expression of Ompk35 and Ompk37, while the expression rate of OmpK36 was low (6.59%). 79.12% of the strains had the ability of biofilm formation. The predominant capsular serotype was KL64, accounting for 87.90%. Its serum bactericidal tolerance was only 12.08%. ST65 was the most virulent strain of KL64 strain, and KL114 was the most virulent strain of ST11 strain.ConclusionThe main hv-CRKP strains in this region carry the iucA, iroB and rmpA2 virulence genes simultaneously, and the dominant clone type is ST11-KL64. The production of carbapenemase mainly involves blaKPC-2. The rate of loss of OmpK36 outer membrane pore protein and the rate of biofilm formation are relatively high. This study can provide important references for the diagnosis and treatment of hv-CRKP infections.

Introduction

Klebsiella pneumoniae (Kp) a Gram-negative opportunistic pathogen, is a leading cause of various healthcare-associated infections (HAIs), including pneumonia, bloodstream infections, urinary tract infections, and intra-abdominal infections, particularly among immunocompromised patients, the elderly, and those undergoing invasive medical procedures.1,2 Studies have shown that the prevalence of carbapenem resistance in Greece and Italy has soared to over 30%.3 Among all Carbapenem resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE), CRKP accounted for the largest proportion of clinical isolates. In summary, systematic studies on the clinical infection characteristics, drug resistance phenotypes, molecular typing, and virulence characteristics of CRKP strains are of great necessity and clinical value for curbing the spread of CRKP strains and guiding clinical precision anti-infection treatment.

In parallel with the prevalence of CRKP, the virulence characteristics of Kp also showed significant changes. The prevalence of hypervirulent strains has attracted wide clinical attention.

Different from classical Klebsiella pneumoniae, HvKP showed stronger virulence phenotypes, such as biofilm formation, capsule production, and serum resistance. HvKP mainly infects young and middle-aged people with strong pathogenicity. Primary site infection, such as meningitis, necrotizing fasciitis, endophthalmitis, and osteomyelitis, results in systemic invasive infection characterized by multi-site involvement and metastasis.4–6 The hypervirulence phenotype of HvKP is mainly mediated by the expression and regulation of various virulence genes in the strain, of which the virulence genes carried by the plasmid play a central role. These genes include the siderophore aerbactin encoding gene iucA, mucus regulatory factor encoding gene rmpA and rmpA2, metabolite transporter encoding gene peg344 and siderophore Salmonella encoding gene iroB.7 In addition, the prevalence of HvKP has obvious regional differences, and the current epidemic area is mainly concentrated in the Asia-Pacific region, and China is also one of the main epidemic areas. It is noteworthy that under the long-term pressure of antibiotics, the phenotypic boundary between CRKP and HvKP has gradually blurred, and a new strain with dual characteristics of “high virulence” and “high drug resistance” has emerged and begun to spread, posing a fundamental challenge to the traditional understanding.

The molecular epidemiology and virulence characteristics of hv-CRKP vary significantly among different geographical regions, mainly due to differences antimicrobial use, and transmission of different clones.8,9 Sequence type 11 has been identified as the predominant CRKP clone in many parts of China, and KL64 capsular serotype and Carbapenem resistance genes blaKPC-2 have been reported as major determinants of virulence and resistance of hv-CRKP isolates.10 China is one of the major epidemic areas of hv-CRKP worldwide. A recent longitudinal multicenter study of CRKP in China showed that the prevalence of hv-CRKP increased significantly from 28.2% in 2016 to 45.7% in 2020,11 and the mortality rate of patients infected with this strain reached as high as 17.1%.10 The prevalence of hv-CRKP carrying Carbapenem resistance genes and high virulence genes is closely related to the increase in clinical infection mortality rate, and has become a core issue of concern in the field of anti-infection both in China and globally.

In addition, there are many studies on the resistance mechanism of CRKP, including the production of Carbapenemase, the loss of outer membrane porin expression, the overexpression of efflux pump, and the formation of biofilm. However, studies on the diversity of virulence gene combinations, the association between virulence phenotypes and drug-resistant clonotypes, and the epidemic clonal characteristics in specific regions of hv-CRKP mostly focused on the whole country or other regions. Zunyi city, as a key regional central city in southwest China, has a large population base, a high number of critically ill patients in local medical institutions, and frequent invasive surgery and mechanical ventilation. Studies have shown that invasive surgery and mechanical ventilation are significant risk factors for hv-CRKP colonization.9 However, there is no systematic study on clinical hv-CRKP isolates in Zunyi at present. The drug resistance characteristics, virulence gene distribution, virulence phenotype differences, and molecular epidemic clone characteristics of strains in Zunyi are still unclear, and there is an obvious epidemiological data gap.

Based on the above research status and regional prevention and control pain points, CRKP strains isolated from the First People’s Hospital of Zunyi (the Third Affiliated Hospital of Zunyi Medical University) from 2021 to 2023 were collected in this study. The virulence genes peg344, iucA, iroB, rmpA2 and rmpA were detected by PCR. The hv-CRKP strains were further identified and drug sensitivity analysis was performed. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was used to detect the carbapenemase production, polymorphic sequence characteristics, capsule serotypes, and the expression of outer membrane porins OmpK35, OmpK36, and OmpK37 in hv-CRKP strains. Biofilm formation assay was performed to analyze the molecular epidemiology and drug resistance. The virulence characteristics of the strains were analyzed by string test, serum resistance test, anti-neutrophil phagocytosis test and Galleria mellonella larvae test. The results of this study could supplement the local epidemiological baseline data of hv-CRKP in Southwest China, provide an objective experimental basis for the accurate clinical selection of antibiotics and the improvement of regional nosocomial infection prevention and control programs, thereby reducing the incidence of local hv-CRKP infection and related mortality risk, and reducing the pressure on nosocomial infection and public health prevention and control.

Materials and Methods

Isolation and Identification of Strains

The training application is issued by the clinicians according to the needs of the patients. Non-repetitive Kp strains isolated from clinical patient specimens in the First People’s Hospital of Zunyi from January 2021 to December 2023 were collected, and informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients. Strains identification was performed via matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS; bioMérieux, France). The clinical specimens mainly included sputum, urine, blood, and other types. Standardized antimicrobial susceptibility testing for imipenem, meropenem and ertapenem was subsequently conducted on all qualified identified strains using the VITEK 2 Compact system (bioMérieux, France). CRKP was defined according to CLSI M100 S33:12 minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of imipenem ≥4 μg/mL, or MIC of meropenem ≥4 μg/mL, or MIC of ertapenem ≥2 μg/mL.

The boiling method was used to extract genomic DNA from all CRKP strains in the manuscript. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed to detect the presence of hypervirulence-associated genes, including peg344, iucA, iroB, rmpA2 and rmpA. The definition of hv-CRKP in this study was based on previously published literature standards:13,14 Primer sequences and expected sizes of amplified products for the above hypervirulence-associated genes were referenced from the existing literature,13 with detailed information provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Clinical Data Analysis

A retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of 91 patients with hv-CRKP infection. The analyzed indicators included demographic characteristics (gender, age), specimen source, and submitting department. The analysis indicators of clinical patient characteristics were as follows: fever, inflammatory biomarkers (C-reactive protein, procalcitonin, neutrophil count, white blood cell count), comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes), infection types (central nervous system infection, pathogen co-infection), diagnosis and treatment-related factors (history of invasive procedures, Carbapenem use, Tigecycline use), and prolonged hospital stay (defined as hospitalization duration exceeding 28 days) and ICU admission.

Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing against 16 antimicrobial agents was performed using the VITEK 2 Compact automated bacterial identification and susceptibility testing system (bioMérieux, France) combined with Gram-negative bacterial susceptibility cards (bioMérieux, France). The tested antimicrobial agents included ampicillin/sulbactam, aztreonam, imipenem, ertapenem, cefazolin, ceftriaxone, cefotetan, cefepime, ceftazidime, piperacillin/tazobactam, gentamicin, tobramycin, amikacin, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. For tigecycline (Yangzi Jiang Pharmaceutical Group, China), antimicrobial susceptibility testing was conducted via the broth microdilution method, with the specific operational procedures strictly adhering to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) document M07-A9. The interpretation criteria for antimicrobial susceptibility results were as follows: tigecycline results were interpreted in accordance with the relevant standards of the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA), while results for the other 16 antimicrobial agents were interpreted based on the CLSI document M100 S33. Escherichia coli ATCC 25922 was used as the quality control strain throughout the experiment to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the test results.

Multilocus Sequence Typing

Seven housekeeping genes of Klebsiella pneumoniae (gapA, infB, mdh, pgi, phoE, tonB, and rpoB) were specifically amplified by PCR. The validity of the amplified products was verified by agarose gel electrophoresis, followed by Sanger sequencing performed by Shanghai Biological Engineering Co., Ltd. The obtained sequencing data were submitted to the Klebsiella pneumophila MLST database (https://bigsdb.pasteur.fr/klebsiella/). Sequence alignment was conducted to obtain the corresponding allelic gene codes of each housekeeping gene, thereby determining the sequence type (ST) of each strain. The primer sequences and expected sizes of the amplified products for the aforementioned housekeeping genes are provided in Supplementary Table 2.

Analysis of Resistance Mechanisms

Detection of Carbapenem Resistance Genes

The carbapenem resistance genes (blaKPC, blaNDM, blaIMP, blaVIM, blaOXA-48) were detected by PCR amplification combined with agarose gel electrophoresis. Primer sequences and expected amplicon sizes for the amplification of these genes were referenced from previous literature,14 with detailed information provided in Supplementary Table 3.

Detection of Outer Membrane Porins

The OmpK35, OmpK36 and OmpK37 gene deletions were detected by PCR amplification combined with agarose gel electrophoresis. Primer sequences and expected amplicon sizes for these porin genes were referenced from a previous study,15 with detailed information provided in Supplementary Table 4.

Biofilm Formation Assay

A single colony of each hv-CRKP strain was inoculated into sterile Luria-Bertani (LB) broth and shake-incubated at 37 °C for 4–6 hours. The bacterial concentration was adjusted to 1×106 CFU/mL, and 200 μL of the adjusted bacterial suspension was added to each well of a sterile 96-well microplate, with sterile LB broth as the negative control. The microplate was incubated at 37 °C for 24 hours, after which planktonic bacterial were discarded. Wells were washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), air-dried, and stained with 1% crystal violet. Unbound crystal violet was removed by washing, and after air-drying, absolute ethanol was added to dissolve bound crystal violet. Absorbance at 590 nm was measured using a microplate reader to evaluate biofilm-forming ability.16

Virulence Traits Analysis

Capsular Serotype Detection

PCR amplification and agarose gel electrophoresis were used to detect capsular serotyping genes KL1, KL2, KL5, KL20, KL54, KL57 and KL64. Primer sequences and expected amplicon sizes were referenced from literature,17 with details in Supplementary Table 5. hv-CRKP strains were collected from January 2023 to August 2023, and their capsule serotypes were detected by PCR in the laboratory and by next generation sequencing in the Second Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University.

Serum Resistance Assay

A single colony of each hv-CRKP strain was inoculated into sterile LB broth and shake-incubated at 37 °C, 200 rpm/min for 4–6 hours. Bacterial concentration was adjusted to 1×106 CFU/mL with sterile 0.9% NaCl. A 50 μL aliquot was mixed with 150 μL fresh healthy human serum in an EP tube, incubated continuously at 37 °C, and viable bacterial counts were performed at 0-, 1-, 2-, and 3-hour post-incubation to analyze serum resistance.18

Galleria mellonella Assay

Two groups of hv-CRKP strains were selected: 1) KL64 capsular serotype with different sequence types (STs), including KL64-ST11 hv-CRKP2, KL64-ST219 hv-CRKP4, KL64-ST307 hv-CRKP17, KL657-ST11 hv-CRKP52, KL64-ST569 hv-CRKP56, KL64-ST15 hv-CRKP83, KL64-ST65 hv-CRKP89, and KL64-ST5 hv-CRKP91; 2) ST11 different capsular serotypes, including ST11-KL64 hv-CRKP2, ST11-KL114 hv-CRKP37, ST11-KL23 hv-CRKP62, and ST11-KL47 hv-CRKP63. All strains were shake-cultured in LB broth at 37 °C, 160 rpm/min for 8 hours, diluted to 1×108 CFU/mL with PBS. A 10 μL aliquot was injected into the second abdominal proleg of Galleria mellonella larvae (Keyun Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) using a 25 μL Hamilton micro syringe. Larvae were incubated at 37 °C in the dark, with mortality observed and recorded every 12 hours (dead larvae removed immediately) for 72 hours. A negative control group injected with equal volume of sterile PBS was set; the experiment was valid only if no mortality occurred in the control group.19

Statistical Analysis

In this study, Excel was used to collect clinical characteristics data, and WHONET 5.6 software was used for statistical analysis of drug sensitivity test results. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate the median survival time and survival rate of each group, and the survival curve was drawn. The survival distribution between strains was compared by log rank test, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Screening of hv-CRKP Strains

A total of 2771 non-duplicate Kp strains were isolated from clinical specimens of patients at the First People’s Hospital of Zunyi between January 2021 to December 2023, among which 160 were CRKP strains, corresponding to an isolation rate of 5.77%. The CRKP isolation rate in the present cohort was higher than the 2.78% reported in our earlier published surveillance from 2018 to 2020.20 Epidemiological analysis showed that elderly patients were the main susceptible population. Genes screening of the 160 CRKP strains showed that 66 strains did not carry any of the virulence genes (peg344, iucA, iroB, rmpA2, rmpA). 2 strains carried only iroB, and 1 strain carried only rmpA2. A total of 91 hv-CRKP strains were identified, with detailed screening results presented in Figure 1. Patients in ICU are critically ill and have low immune function, and the risk of hv-CRKP infection is significantly higher than that of patients in other departments. Antimicrobial Susceptibility

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing results showed that hv-CRKP strains exhibited high resistance to various clinically commonly used antimicrobial agents. Specifically, the resistance rates to carbapenems, cephalosporins, and penicillin combination preparations exceeded 95%; the resistance rates to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, tobramycin, gentamicin, and amikacin were as high as 82%, indicating a severe drug resistance profile. Only a relatively low resistance rates (3.3%) was observed for tigecycline. Consistent with its established role as an effective therapeutic agent for CRKP infections, this agent remains a reliable therapeutic option for the clinical treatment of hv-CRKP infections. Virulence Characteristics Analysis

Capsular serotyping showed KL64 was the predominant capsular serotype (87.90%), followed by KL114 (2.20%); KL47, KL23, and KL19 each accounted for 1.10%. Galleria mellonella larvae are used widely as surrogate infectious disease models, due to ease of use and the presence of an innate immune system functionally similar to that of vertebrates. The innate immune pathway of Galleria mellonella larvae infection model is conserved from that of human, which can replicate hv-CRKP to induce systemic sepsis, and make up for the defect that the in vitro cell model cannot reflect the bacterial dissemination and tissue invasion in vivo. Discussion

Hypervirulent carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae has become an important pathogen of nosocomial infection due to its high virulence and high drug resistance. The regional differences in epidemiological characteristics, drug resistance mechanism and virulence characteristics have important guiding significance for clinical prevention, control and treatment. hv-CRKP infection mainly occurred in the elderly population in this region. This population is at high risk of hv-CRKP infection due to low immune function and underlying diseases. The analysis of specimen source showed that the proportion of sputum samples was the highest, followed by urine samples, suggesting that respiratory tract and urinary system were the main sites of hv-CRKP infection in this area, which was consistent with the characteristics of Klebsiella pneumoniae infection. The specimens were mainly collected from intensive care unit (ICU), and the patients in ICU were susceptible to hv-CRKP due to their critical condition and low immunity. The results were consistent with the conclusions of many domestic and foreign studies.20,21 The clinical infection characteristics are somewhat different from the previous study of CRKP in our hospital. In the previous study, children were the main infection population, and pediatrics and ICU were the main isolation departments.20 It is speculated that the decline of resistance in the elderly population, adults are susceptible to hypervirulence genes, and the increase in drug use and surgery may be caused by a combination of factors. Consistent with the results of previous studies, pathogen co-infection, elevated C-reactive protein level, history of invasive surgery, ICU admission, and carbapenem exposure were closely associated with hv-CRKP infection.14,22 It can help clinical identification of high-risk groups of hv-CRKP infection, take corresponding prevention and control measures as soon as possible, guide clinical rational selection of antibiotics, standardize invasive operations, and effectively reduce the risk of infection.

Drug resistance is the main difficulty in clinical treatment of hv-CRKP infection. The results of drug sensitivity test in this study showed that hv-CRKP in our hospital showed extensive and severe resistance to commonly used antibiotics in clinic: The drug resistance rate of hv-CRKP to carbapenems, cephalosporins and penicillins was more than 95%, and the drug resistance rate to compound sulforamine and aminoglycosides was more than 82%, which was consistent with the high drug resistance characteristics of hv-CRKP in previous studies.21,23,24 It is noteworthy that tigecycline showed low resistance in vitro and could be used as a treatment option for hv-CRKP infections in this region.

MLST typing results revealed the molecular epidemic characteristics of hv-CRKP in our hospital. ST11 was the predominant epidemic clone, and other ST types were scattered. ST11 was the most common prevalent clone of hv-CRKP in Asia, especially in China, indicating that the epidemic characteristics of hv-CRKP in our hospital were consistent with the overall trend of hv-CRKP in China and Asia.9,10,25–28 It is worth noting that the dominant clone type of CRKP in our previous studies was ST2407,20 which is different from the result of this study. This might be related to changes in the infected population, differences in patient sources, and the dynamic evolution over time. The genetic characteristics, transmission patterns and clonal epidemic characteristics of the strains revealed by MLST typing can optimize the surveillance system of nosocomial infection and realize the transformation from “passive response” to “active early warning”. To accurately locate the transmission chain of infection and solve the core problem of fuzzy traditional traceability.

The exploration of the mechanism of carbapenem resistance can provide theoretical support for the prevention and control of drug-resistant strains. Consistent with other studies,25,29 the prevalent hv-CRKP strains in our hospital mainly carried the blaKPC carbapenem resistance gene, among which the blakPC-2 subtype was dominant in the hv-CRKP strains, highlighting the important role of KPC-producing plasmids in the evolution of hv-CRKP.30 The blaNDM carbapenem resistance gene of CRKP strains is different from that of previous studies in our hospital, which may be caused by changes in the infected population.20 Additionally, the deletion rate of the OmpK36 gene of hv-CRKP reached as high as 93.41%, significantly higher than that reported in other regions.31 Previous studies have confirmed that the loss or low expression of OmpK36 is closely related to carbapenem resistance.32 In this study, KL23 and KL19 capsular serotype hv-CRKP did not express OmpK36, suggesting that the capsular serotype of hv-CRKP in this region may be related to the loss of OmpK36, and the mechanism is worthy of further exploration. At the same time, the hv-CRKP biofilm formation rate in our hospital was 79.12%, while the improvement and discharge rate of infected patients without biofilm formation was 78.94%, suggesting that biofilm can form a physical barrier to protect bacteria and promote the transfer of drug-resistant elements, thereby improving the antibiotic tolerance of strains and increasing the risk of treatment failure. This also provides a reasonable explanation for the difficulty in the treatment of hv-CRKP infection.33

As a key virulence determinant of hv-CRKP, capsular serotypes distribution is closely correlated with bacterial invasiveness. KL64 was identified as the predominant capsular serotype in local hv-CRKP, consistent with the national epidemic trend in China.34–38 The antimicrobial resistance rate of hv-CRKP was only 12.08%, which was relatively lower than the results of Li P and Luo et al.39 The overall low bactericidal tolerance of hv-CRKP serum may be caused by the adaptability of bacteria to balance resistance and virulence during evolution. The KL64-ST65 hv-CRKP89 strain was more virulent than other ST strains in the KL64 capsular serotype. Among the ST11 strains, the virulence of different capsular serotypes was different, and the ST11-KL114 hv-CRKP37 strain was more virulent. After the CRKP acquired the virulence plasmid to form hv-CRKP, the serum drug resistance and Galleria mellonella larval mortality of hv-CRKP were generally lower than those of hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae, which might be caused by the adaptive evolution of Klebsiella pneumoniae to regulate virulencerelated genes and drug resistance genes.40

Limitations

This was a retrospective single-center study, with all isolates collected solely from the First People’s Hospital of Zunyi. Limited sampling coverage and sample size led to selection bias, which restricts the generalizability of our findings to other local medical institutions. Besides, incomplete clinical records of community colonization and long-term follow-up data prevented us from confirming causal relationships between strain genotypes and patient outcomes. In addition, we only described phenotypic and genotypic characteristics of local hv-CRKP strains without exploring the molecular crosstalk between resistance and virulence factors. No whole-genome tracing was conducted to analyze hospital transmission chains of epidemic clones. Future multi-center prospective surveillance combined with genomic sequencing will further clarify regional epidemiological patterns, regulatory mechanisms of resistance-virulence interaction and strain transmission routes, to support precise infection control and clinical antimicrobial treatment.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the main strains of hv-CRKP in this region carried iucA, iroB and rmpA2 virulence genes, and the dominant clone was ST11-KL64. blaKPC-2 carbapenemase production was the main resistance mode of the strains. The OmpK36 outer membrane porin loss rate and biofilm formation rate were high. It is resistant to most common antimicrobials. There were differences in the virulence of strains with different capsular types and ST types. This study provided epidemiological data of hv-CRKP infection in this area, and provided guidance for the treatment of patients with hv-CRKP infection in this area. This study provided help for homology analysis of hv-CRKP strains. To provide reference for the development of hv-CRKP prevention and control measures in the region. Targeted surveillance and standardized prevention protocols are essential to curb the spread of hospital-acquired hv-CRKP. At our institution, active screening is routinely performed for high-risk populations including ICU admissions, postoperative patients and individuals receiving long-term immunosuppressive therapy. Once hv-CRKP is isolated, contact isolation is immediately implemented for colonized or infected patients, accompanied by enhanced environmental disinfection of ward surfaces and reusable medical devices. Strict antimicrobial stewardship is also enforced to reduce selective pressure that drives the coexistence of carbapenem resistance and hypervirulence. Combined regular strain genotyping surveillance and standardized infection control operations help cut the risk of nosocomial transmission of endemic hv-CRKP clones.

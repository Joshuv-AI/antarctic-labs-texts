Schrödinger Operators, Integral Curvature, and the Euler Characteristic of Riemannian Manifolds
Teng Huang, Pan Zhang

Abstract
We establish new connections between integral curvature bounds and the Euler characteristic of closed Riemannian manifolds through the perspective of Schrödinger-type operators. Central to our approach is the twisted Dirac operator 𝒟 θ \mathcal{D}_{\theta} , whose index equals χ ⁡ ( M ) \chi(M) . Under integral smallness conditions on the negative part of a potential V V and a Sobolev–Poincaré inequality, we show that a suitable scaling of θ \theta forces the kernel of 𝒟 t ​ θ \mathcal{D}_{t\theta} to vanish, thereby implying χ ⁡ ( M ) = 0 \chi(M)=0 . Applying this framework to geometrically natural potentials yields several topological consequences. In even dimensions, sufficiently small integral bounds on partial sums of curvature operator eigenvalues force χ ⁡ ( M ) \chi(M) either to vanish or to have a sign determined by the middle dimension. For four-manifolds, a small L p L^{p} -norm of the negative Ricci curvature relative to the diameter guarantees χ ⁡ ( M ) ≥ 0 \chi(M)\geq 0 . Moreover, when χ ⁡ ( M ) ≠ 0 \chi(M)\neq 0 we obtain a Li–Yau type lower bound for the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on 1 1 -forms in terms of the diameter and an integral curvature quantity. Subsequently, we provide an explicit lower bound for the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian on 1 1 -forms under almost nonnegative curvature conditions, thereby giving an affirmative answer to Yau’s Problem 79.

Introduction

A central theme in Riemannian geometry is to understand how local geometric properties, particularly curvature, govern global topological structure. Classical results in global differential geometry—exemplified by the foundational works of Bochner
[
10
]
, Meyer
[
38
]
, Lichnerowicz
[
36
]
, and Gromov
[
26
,
27
]
—typically rely on pointwise curvature conditions to derive topological or geometric consequences. For instance, Bochner’s celebrated theorem states that if a closed Riemannian manifold satisfies Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
everywhere, then its first Betti number is bounded above by the dimension, with equality implying a flat metric structure
[
10
]
. Meyer’s theorem establishes that if
\mathrm{Ric}\geq n-1
, the diameter cannot exceed
\pi
, and Cheng’s rigidity theorem shows that equality occurs precisely for the standard sphere
[
18
]
. Similarly, Lichnerowicz proved that under pointwise Ricci curvature lower bounds, the first nonzero eigenvalue of the Laplacian admits a sharp lower estimate. Meyer
[
38
]
established vanishing results for the Betti numbers of manifolds with positive curvature operators, i.e.,
\lambda_{1}>0
, and in particular Meyer showed that they are rational (co)homology spheres. More recently, Petersen and Wink
[
44
]
proved similar results using pointwise bound on the average of eigenvalues of the curvature operator. These results, while powerful and elegant, impose conditions that are often too restrictive for many geometrically interesting manifolds encountered in nature and applications.

Recent work has shifted toward understanding the implications of integral curvature bounds. Gallot
[
24
,
25
]
developed fundamental estimates for Sobolev constants under integral Ricci curvature bounds, establishing crucial analytic tools for this weaker geometric setting. Aubry established finiteness of
\pi_{1}
and an upper bound of the diameter of a manifold in
[
2
]
using an integral bound of the lowest eigenvalue of the Ricci curvature pinched below a positive constant. Furthermore, when the diameter is also bounded from below, Aubry
[
3
]
proved that such manifolds are homeomorphic to
S^{n}
. Petersen, Sprouse, and Wei conducted a systematic investigation of geometric properties under bounded integral curvature, deriving volume comparison theorems, diameter estimates, and other comparison results
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
. Their work demonstrated that many classical comparison theorems admit natural integral analogs when appropriate
L^{p}
conditions replace pointwise bounds.

Although substantial progress has been made on bounding Betti numbers under integral curvature conditions (see
[
52
]
for recent advances), the Euler characteristic has received less systematic attention in this context. Some partial results exist: for instance, under pointwise conditions on the curvature operator, one can sometimes deduce sign constraints on
\chi(M)

[
12
]
. Nevertheless, a comprehensive theory linking integral bounds on the negative part of curvature to the Euler characteristic has remained elusive. The difficulty stems from several analytic obstacles: classical tools such as the Bochner formula and Moser iteration traditionally rely on pointwise curvature bounds, and extending them to the integral setting requires new technical innovations and careful control of constants expressed in terms of integral curvature quantities.

A powerful framework for analyzing curvature in integral norms arises from viewing certain geometric differential operators as Schrödinger-type operators

\nabla^{\ast}\nabla+V,

where the curvature tensor appears as a potential
V
. This perspective transforms geometric problems into questions about the spectral and analytic properties of Schrödinger operators with
L^{p}
-potentials. In particular, the Hodge Laplacian on form

\Delta_{d}=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla+\operatorname{Ric}

is precisely of this form.

In this article, we exploit this Schrödinger operator viewpoint to establish new links between integral curvature bounds and the Euler characteristic. Our main technical vehicle is the twisted Dirac operator

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}=d_{\theta}+d^{*}_{\theta}:\Omega^{+}(M)\to\Omega^{-}(M),

where
d_{\theta}=d+\theta\wedge
for a closed 1-form
\theta
. This operator, first studied systematically by Novikov
[
39
]
in the context of multi-valued functions and Hamiltonian systems, interpolates between the classical Hodge–Dirac operator (when
\theta=0
) and the differential in Morse–Novikov (twisted) cohomology theory. By the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, its index equals the Euler characteristic:

\operatorname{Index}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})=\chi(M).

Our strategy is to show that under suitable integral curvature conditions, one can select a harmonic 1-form
\theta
(guaranteed by nonvanishing first de Rham cohomology) and a scaling parameter
t>0
such that
\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}
has trivial kernel in certain form degrees. Combining this analytic vanishing with Poincaré duality for Morse–Novikov cohomology
[
39
]
then yields strong constraints on
\chi(M)
.

.

Let
(M^{n},g)

Denote by

Fix

is a non-trivial
1

where

is a potential. Then for any

, there exists a constant

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\min\bigg\{

\displaystyle e\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}}\exp\Bigl(-\sqrt{C_{s}}C_{6}(n,p,q)(\|\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6\sqrt{e}C_{s}C_{1}(p,q)}\exp\Bigl(-\sqrt{C_{s}}C_{6}(n,p,q)(\|\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2C_{s}C_{4}(p,q)}\bigg\},

such that if

where

\Omega^{+}_{l}=\bigoplus_{2k\leq l}(\Omega^{2k}\oplus\Omega^{n-2k}),\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pt\Omega^{-}_{l}=\bigoplus_{2k+1\leq l}(\Omega^{2k+1}\oplus\Omega^{n-2k-1})

In particular, the Euler characteristic of

When the potential

is related to geometric curvature (e.g., when

is harmonic and

involves the curvature operator), this theorem translates integral smallness of curvature into the vanishing of the Euler characteristic.

45

[

Question

(
[

Huang and Tan in

Focusing on even-dimensional manifolds and employing finer curvature conditions, we obtain our second main result. This generalises the pointwise setting to an integral one via Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{2n},g)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, Riemannian manifold with nonzero first de Rham cohomology group, and let
q>p>2n

\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{\binom{2n}{2}}
the eigenvalues of the curvature operator and

\lambda_{2n,l}:=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{2n-l}.

Fix

such that if

\|\lambda_{2n,l}^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}\leq C_{10},

then the Euler characteristic of

1.

\chi(M)=0
, if

;

2.

(-1)^{n}\chi(M)\geq 0
, if

.

Here
\lambda_{2n,l}^{-}
denotes the negative part of sum of smallest

This theorem reveals a striking dichotomy in even dimensions: when the negative part of the curvature operator is sufficiently small in the integral sense, the Euler characteristic is forced either to vanish or to have a sign determined solely by the middle dimension. Moreover, we obtain an upper bound for the Betti numbers under a similar integral curvature condition.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n

\|\theta\|_{\infty}\leq\exp\Bigl\{C_{4}\sqrt{\frac{n}{4}}\;C_{8}\sqrt{C_{9}}\Bigr\}\,\|\theta\|_{2}.

In particular, one can choose a constant

In dimension four, the Euler characteristic gains particular significance through its connections to fundamental topological and geometric structures. By the Hirzebruch signature theorem,
\chi(M)
relates to the signature
\tau(M)
via

\chi(M)=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma(M)+\tau(M)),

where
\sigma(M)
is a combination of Pontryagin numbers. Moreover, in the realm of smooth
4
-manifolds, Donaldson’s gauge theory
[
22
,
23
]
and Seiberg–Witten theory
[
48

reveal deep connections between
\chi(M)
, the intersection form, and differentiable structures. These connections make understanding which curvature conditions force
\chi(M)
to have a specific sign—especially nonnegativity—a problem of considerable geometric and topological interest. Here we obtain a clean and geometrically transparent statement involving only the Ricci curvature:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(M^{4},g)
be a closed
4
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold, and let
q>p>4
. There exists a uniform positive constant
C_{10}(p)
such that if the Ricci curvature satisfies

\|{\rm Ric}^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}\leq C_{10},

then the Euler characteristic of
M
is non‑negative:

\chi(M)\geq 0.

This provides a quantitative obstruction: any
4
-manifold with
\chi(M)<0
cannot admit a metric whose negative Ricci curvature is too small in an integral sense relative to the diameter.

Remark 1.5
.

The significance of the above result can be clarified by the example constructed by Anderson
[
1
]
. He showed that for integers
n\geq 4
,
k\leq n-1
and a constant
\varepsilon>0
, there exist closed
n
-manifolds
M
satisfying

|{\rm Ric}(g)|<\varepsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm diam}(M)<1,

and

b_{1}(M)=k
, yet no finite cover of
M
fibers over the circle
S^{1}
. Moreover, these manifolds can be chosen with arbitrarily large second Betti number. For
n=4
, the Euler characteristic of these manifolds satisfies

\chi(M)=2-2b_{1}(M)+b_{2}(M)>0.

Our corollary thus establishes that such constructions are essentially optimal: if one wants to force
\chi(M)<0
, a certain minimal amount of negative Ricci curvature in the
L^{p}
sense is necessary.

We consider the
rough Laplacian

\overline{\Delta}=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla

acting on differential
k
-forms. This is a second-order elliptic operator whose spectrum consists of a nonnegative, unbounded,
increasing sequence of real eigenvalues:

0=\lambda^{(k)}_{0}<\lambda^{(k)}_{1}\leq\lambda^{(k)}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda^{(k)}_{l}\leq\lambda^{(k)}_{l+1}\leq\cdots\nearrow+\infty,

where the geometric multiplicity of
\lambda^{(k)}_{0}
equals the dimension of
\dim\ker\nabla
(i.e., the dimension of parallel
k
-forms).
If there are no nonzero parallel forms, the spectrum conventionally begins with the first positive eigenvalue
\lambda^{(k)}_{1}>0
.

For the first positive eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{(0)}
of functions (i.e.,
0

The work of Cheeger
[

[

whereas the gradient estimate technique developed by Li and Yau

Specifically, if the Ricci curvature satisfies

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq-(n-1)\kappa,\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm{diam}}(M)\leq D(g),

then

\lambda^{(0)}_{1}D^{2}(g)\geq c^{-1}(n)\exp\{-[1+(1+2c^{2}(n)\Lambda^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}]\}.

The authors in
[
53

improved the result of Li-Yau. However, the situation for the eigenvalues

Colbois and Maerten
[

pointed out that fixing the volume alone does not guarantee a uniform positive lower bound for

;
moreover, the constructions of Anné and Takahashi

These results clearly indicate that in order to obtain a positive lower bound for the eigenvalues of higher-degree forms, additional geometric or topological constraints must be imposed. Existing work has introduced such constraints from various perspectives: for instance, Ballmann, Brüning and Carron

Finally, we provide a lower bound estimate for the first eigenvalue

of the rough Laplacian on
1

As noted in

By strengthening the curvature assumption to a pointwise lower bound on a partial sum of the eigenvalues of the curvature operator, and assuming the manifold has nonzero Euler characteristic, we are able to obtain a Li-Yau-type inequality for the eigenvalue

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M^{2n},g)
be a closed
2n

n\geq 2
, smooth Riemannian manifold with nonvanishing Euler characteristic. Denote by
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{\binom{2n}{2}}

\lambda_{2n,n}=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}.

Then the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on

\begin{split}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{(1)}D^{2}}>\min\bigg\{&e\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}\exp\Bigl(-\sqrt{C_{s}(\infty)}C_{6}(n,2n,\infty)(\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big),\\
&\frac{1}{6\sqrt{e}C_{s}(\infty)C_{1}(n,\infty)}\exp\Bigl(-\sqrt{C_{s}(\infty)}C_{6}(n,2n,\infty)(\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\Bigr),\\
&\frac{1}{2C_{s}(\infty)C_{4}(n,\infty)}\bigg\},\end{split}

(1.1)

where

C_{s}(\infty)=C(n)\exp\Bigl(\frac{2n-1}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}\Bigr).

The eigenvalue estimate above leads to an alternative conclusion concerning the Euler characteristic and the first Betti number when the quantity

Remark 1.9

A smooth closed manifold

Outline of the article

Section 2 presents preliminary material on curvature operators and establishes a key analytic result—a mean-value inequality under integral curvature conditions. By deriving a Bochner-type formula for forms in
\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
and coupling it with a delicate Moser iteration (Proposition
2.4
) that operates under only integral curvature bounds, we extend the pointwise framework of classical Bochner theory to the integral setting.

Section 3 introduces the twisted Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
, proves fundamental integral identities, and develops uniform
L^{\infty}
-estimates for forms in its kernel. Working with
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
allows us to link analytic information (the kernel of
\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}
) directly to topology via the index theorem. The use of Morse–Novikov cohomology and its Poincaré duality
[
39
]
plays an essential role in deducing sign constraints on
\chi(M)
.

Section 4 provides the proofs of the main theorems and their corollaries, including applications to eigenvalue estimates. In our analysis we rely on and extend foundational work of Gallot
[
25
]
concerning Sobolev constants under integral Ricci bounds (Lemma
4.1
), which requires careful control of constants expressed in terms of integral curvature quantities.

Section 5 establishes eigenvalue estimates for the rough and Hodge Laplacians under almost nonnegative curvature conditions. In particular, we prove a Li–Yau type lower bound for the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on 1-forms (Theorem
1.6
), and show that when the negative part of the curvature operator is sufficiently small in the
L^{\infty}
-sense, either the Euler characteristic vanishes or the first Betti number vanishes (Corollary
1.7
). Moreover, we obtain an explicit positive lower bound for the first eigenvalue of the Hodge Laplacian on 1-forms (Theorem
1.8
), answering a question of Yau under our curvature setting.

The normalized

Furthermore, set

C_{10}(n,p,q):=\min\!\Bigl\{\frac{1}{16C^{2}_{8}(n,p)C^{4}_{6}(n,p,q)},\frac{1}{72e^{2}C^{2}_{8}(n,p)C^{2}_{1}(p,q)},\frac{1}{8C_{8}^{2}(p,q)C^{2}_{4}(p,q)},\frac{1}{2}C_{7}(n,p)\Bigr\},

2.1
Curvature Operator

We first recall the definition of the curvature operator and recent progress in this area due to Petersen and Wink
[
44
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z

denote its curvature tensor. The Weitzenböck curvature operator acting on a
(0,k)
-tensor
T
is defined by

\operatorname{Ric}(T)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(R(X_{i},e_{j})T\bigr)(X_{1},\dots,e_{j},\dots,X_{k}).

We use a variation of the Ricci tensor to symbolize this operator, as it indeed coincides with the classical Ricci tensor when evaluated on vector fields or 1-forms
[
43
]
.

Let
(E,\nabla)
be a Riemannian vector bundle with finite-dimensional fibre over
M
; that is,
E
is a vector bundle equipped with a smooth metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and a compatible connection
\nabla
. On the space
\Gamma(E)
of smooth sections of
E
we define the
L^{2}
-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the metric
g
. The connection
\nabla
extends naturally to
p
-tensors on
M
taking values in
E
. Its formal adjoint
\nabla^{*}
with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product defines the
rough Laplacian
(or connection Laplacian) acting on
\Gamma(E)
by

\overline{\Delta}=\nabla^{*}\nabla.

Suppose now that
E\to M
is a subbundle of
T^{(0,k)}(M)
. For a constant
c>0
the
Lichnerowicz Laplacian
on
E
is given by

\Delta_{L}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+c\operatorname{Ric}.

The Hodge Laplacian corresponds to the case
c=1
; that is,

\Delta_{d}=\overline{\Delta}+\operatorname{Ric}.

The following estimate for the curvature term plays a crucial role in our analysis.

Corollary 2.1

(
[
44
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let

\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{\binom{n}{2}}
be the eigenvalues of its curvature operator.
Fix an integer
1\leq l\leq\big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\big\rfloor
.
Then for any differential form
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(M)
with
k\leq l
or
k\geq n-l
,

\langle\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha),\alpha\rangle\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-l}}{n-l}\;k(n-k)\,|\alpha|^{2}.

(2.1)

Remark 2.2
.

Because the Ricci curvature is bounded from below by the sum of the smallest
n-1
eigenvalues of the curvature operator, the estimate (
2.1
) yields the pointwise inequality

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq(n-1)\,\frac{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-l}}{n-l}.

(2.2)

2.2
A Mean Value Inequality

The following elementary estimate will be used repeatedly in the Moser iteration.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
t>0
,
\zeta>1
. Then

P=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\bigl(1+t\zeta^{i}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{\zeta^{i}}}\leq\exp\Bigl\{\frac{2\sqrt{\zeta}}{\zeta-1}\Bigr\}\bigl(1+\sqrt{t}\bigr)^{\frac{2\zeta}{\zeta-1}}.

Proof.

For any
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\ln(1+tx)

\displaystyle\leq 2\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{tx}\bigr)

\displaystyle=2\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{t}\bigr)+2\ln\Bigl(1+(\sqrt{x}-1)\frac{\sqrt{t}}{1+\sqrt{t}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{t}\bigr)+2(\sqrt{x}-1).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\ln P

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln\bigl(1+t\zeta^{i}\bigr)}{\zeta^{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{t}\bigr)}{\zeta^{i}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\zeta^{\frac{i}{2}}-2}{\zeta^{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\zeta\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{t}\bigr)}{\zeta-1}+\frac{2\sqrt{\zeta}}{\sqrt{\zeta}-1}-\frac{2\zeta}{\zeta-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\zeta\ln\bigl(1+\sqrt{t}\bigr)}{\zeta-1}+\frac{2\sqrt{\zeta}}{\zeta-1}.

Exponentiating both sides gives the desired inequality.
∎

The next proposition is the analytic core of our
L^{\infty}
-estimates. It provides a uniform bound for nonnegative functions satisfying a differential inequality with coefficients in
L^{p}
. We now present the proof of Proposition
2.4
, whose main technical idea originates from
[
3
,
4
]
.

Proposition 2.4

(Mean Value Inequality)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold satisfying the Sobolev inequality

\|f\|_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}D\|df\|_{2},\hskip 20.00003ptp>2.

(2.3)

Suppose that
f\in L^{2}_{1}(M)
is a nonnegative continuous function satisfying

f\Delta_{d}f\leq h_{1}f^{2}+\operatorname{div}X

(2.4)

in the sense of distributions, where
h_{1}\in L^{\frac{q}{2}}(M)
and
X
is a vector field with

|X|(x)\leq h_{2}(x)f^{2}(x),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall x\in M,

(2.5)

for a nonnegative continuous function
h_{2}\in L^{q}(M)
. Then for any
q
with
+\infty\geq q>p
,

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}(p,q)\,\bigl(1+\sqrt{C_{0}(p,q)C_{s}DB}\bigr)^{\frac{2pq}{q-p}}\,\|f\|_{2},

(2.6)

where

B^{2}:=\|h_{1}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}+2\|h_{2}\|_{q}^{2},\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002ptC_{1}(p,q)=\exp\left\{\frac{4p}{(p-2)(\gamma-1)\sqrt{\gamma}}\right\}

Proof.

For any integer
k\geq 1
, multiply inequality (
2.4
) by
f^{2k-2}
and integrate over
M
. Using the divergence theorem, we obtain

\int_{M}f^{2k-1}\Delta_{d}f\leq\|h_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}}^{2k}+\bigl|(\operatorname{div}X,f^{2k-2})_{L^{2}(M)}\bigr|.

For the divergence term we estimate

\displaystyle\bigl|(\operatorname{div}X,f^{2k-2})_{L^{2}(M)}\bigr|

\displaystyle=\bigl|(X,\nabla f^{2k-2})_{L^{2}(M)}\bigr|

\displaystyle=(2k-2)\bigl|(X,f^{2k-3}\nabla f)_{L^{2}(M)}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq(2k-2)\int_{M}f^{2k-1}|h_{2}|\,|\nabla f|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2k-2}{4}\int_{M}f^{2k-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+(2k-2)\int_{M}f^{2k}|h_{2}|^{2}.

Combining the two estimates gives

\displaystyle(2k-1)\int_{M}f^{2k-2}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}f^{2k-1}\Delta_{d}f

\displaystyle\leq\|h_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}}\|f\|^{2k}_{L^{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{(2k-2)}{4}\int_{M}f^{2k-2}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+(2k-2)\|h_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{q}}\|f\|^{2k}_{L^{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}}.

Rearranging yields

\int_{M}f^{2k-2}|\nabla f|^{2}\leq\Bigl(\frac{4(k-1)}{3k-1}\|h_{2}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}+\frac{2}{3k-1}\|h_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}}\Bigr)\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}}^{2k}.

(2.7)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{M}|df^{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=k^{2}\int_{M}f^{2k-2}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq k^{2}B^{2}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}}^{2k},

i.e.,

\|df^{k}\|_{2}\leq kB\|f\|_{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}^{k}.

(2.8)

Now apply the Sobolev inequality (
2.3
) to
f^{k}
:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\frac{2pk}{p-2}}^{k}

\displaystyle=\|f^{k}\|_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{k}\|_{2}+C_{s}D\|d(f^{k})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{k}\|_{2}+C_{s}DkB\|f\|_{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}^{k}.

Using the interpolation inequality

\|f\|_{2k}\leq\|f\|_{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|f\|_{\frac{2(k-1)q}{q-2}}^{1-\frac{1}{k}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\frac{2pk}{p-2}}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|f\|_{\frac{2(k-1)q}{q-2}}^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\Bigr)^{k}+C_{s}DkB\Bigl(\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|f\|_{\frac{2(k-1)q}{q-2}}^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\Bigr)^{k}

\displaystyle=\bigl(1+C_{s}DkB\bigr)\|f\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\frac{2(k-1)q}{q-2}}^{k-1}.

Hence,

\frac{\|f\|_{\frac{2pk}{p-2}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\leq\bigl(1+C_{s}DkB\bigr)^{\frac{1}{k}}\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{\frac{2(k-1)q}{q-2}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{\frac{k-1}{k}}.

Raising both sides to the power
\frac{2pk}{p-2}
gives the iterative estimate

\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{\frac{2pk}{p-2}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2pk}{p-2}}\leq\bigl(1+C_{s}DkB\bigr)^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{\frac{2(k-1)q}{q-2}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2p(k-1)}{p-2}}.

(2.9)

Now define the sequences

a_{0}=\frac{2p}{p-1},\hskip 20.00003pta_{i+1}=\gamma a_{i}+\frac{2p}{p-2},

where

\gamma=\frac{p(q-2)}{(p-2)q}>1.

One easily checks that

a_{i}=\gamma^{i}\bigl(a_{0}+\gamma_{0}\bigr)-\gamma_{0},\hskip 20.00003pt\gamma_{0}=\frac{2p}{(p-2)(\gamma-1)}=\frac{pq}{q-p}.

Set

k_{i}=\frac{q-2}{2q}a_{i}+1,

which satisfies

\frac{2p}{p-2}k_{i}=\gamma a_{i}+\frac{2p}{p-2}=a_{i+1},\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{2p(k_{i}-1)}{p-2}=\gamma a_{i}.

Applying (
2.9
) with
k=k_{i}
yields

\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{a_{i+1}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{\frac{a_{i+1}}{\gamma^{i+1}}}\leq\bigl(1+C_{s}Dk_{i}B\bigr)^{\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma^{i+1}}}\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{a_{i}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{\frac{a_{i}}{\gamma^{i}}}.

Iterating this inequality backward to
i=0
gives

\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{a_{i+1}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{\frac{a_{i+1}}{\gamma^{i+1}}}\leq\prod_{\ell=0}^{i}\bigl(1+C_{s}Dk_{\ell}B\bigr)^{\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma^{\ell+1}}}\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{a_{0}}}{\\
|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{a_{0}}.

Taking the limit
i\to\infty
and noting that
\|f\|_{a_{i}}\to\|f\|_{\infty}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\bigl(1+C_{s}Dk_{\ell}B\bigr)^{\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma^{\ell+1}}}\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{a_{0}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{a_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\bigl(1+C_{0}(p,q)D\gamma^{\ell}B\bigr)^{\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma^{\ell+1}}}\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{a_{0}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{a_{0}}.

Here we use the fact

k_{i}<\gamma^{i}(a_{0}+\gamma_{0}):=\gamma^{i}C_{0}(p,q).

Using the interpolation

\|f\|_{a_{0}}\leq\|f\|_{2}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p}},

we have

\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{a_{0}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{a_{0}}\leq\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{2}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{a_{0}}=\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{2}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Bigr)^{2}.

Therefore,

\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\prod_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\bigl(1+C_{0}(p,q)C_{s}D\gamma^{\ell}B\bigr)^{\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma^{\ell+1}}}.

Applying Lemma
2.3
with
t=C_{0}(p,q)C_{s}DB
gives

\begin{split}\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}&\leq\Bigl[\exp\Bigl\{\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\Bigr\}\bigl(1+\sqrt{t}\bigr)^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\Bigr]^{\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma}}\\
&=\exp\Bigl\{\frac{4p}{(p-2)(\gamma-1)\sqrt{\gamma}}\Bigr\}\bigl(1+\sqrt{C_{0}(p,q)C_{s}DB}\bigr)^{\frac{4p}{(p-2)(\gamma-1)}}.\end{split}

Substituting the values of
\gamma
and
\gamma-1
yields the desired bound (
2.6
) with a constant
C_{1}(p,q)
depending only on
p
and
q
.
∎

3
The Twisted Dirac Operator
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
on Riemannian Manifolds

3.1
An Integral Inequality

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. We denote by
\Omega^{k}(M)
the space of real differential
k
-forms for
0\leq k\leq n
. For notational simplicity, we often identify real vector fields with real
1
-forms via the musical isomorphisms. Given a smooth
1
-form
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, the corresponding vector field
\theta^{\sharp}
on
M
is characterized by

\theta(V)=g(V,\theta^{\sharp}),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\ \text{vector field }V\text{ on }M.

We denote the interior product with
\theta^{\sharp}
by
i_{\theta^{\sharp}}
. The Hodge star operator with respect to the metric
g
is denoted by
\ast
.

We define the twisted differential operator

d_{\theta}:\Omega^{k}(M)\to\Omega^{k+1}(M)
by

d_{\theta}=d+\theta\wedge\cdot,

and its formal adjoint

d^{*}_{\theta}:\Omega^{k}(M)\to\Omega^{k-1}(M)
by

d^{*}_{\theta}=d^{*}+i_{\theta^{\sharp}}.

The
twisted Dirac operator

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
acting on differential forms is defined as

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}=d_{\theta}+d^{*}_{\theta}:\Omega^{+}(M)\to\Omega^{-}(M),

where

\Omega^{+}(M)=\bigoplus_{k=\text{ even}}\Omega^{k}(M)\,\hskip 10.00002ptand\,\hskip 10.00002pt\Omega^{-}(M)=\bigoplus_{k=\text{ odd}}\Omega^{k}(M).

When
\theta
is a closed 1-form (
d\theta=0
), the operator
d_{\theta}
satisfies
d_{\theta}^{2}=0
,
and one can define the
Morse–Novikov cohomology
(also known as
twisted de Rham cohomology
) groups

H^{k}(M,\theta):=\frac{\ker d_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{k}(M)}{\operatorname{im}d_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{k}(M)}.

This theory was introduced by Novikov
[
39
]
in the context of multi-valued functions and Hamiltonian systems, and has since found applications in symplectic geometry, topology, and mathematical physics (see
[
21
]
). For a closed 1-form
\theta
, the twisted cohomology groups
H^{k}(M,\theta)
are finite-dimensional vector spaces that depend only on the cohomology class
[\theta]\in H^{1}_{\text{dR}}(M)
.

The twisted Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
is intimately related to Morse–Novikov cohomology. Indeed, its square is given by

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{2}=\Delta_{\theta}:=d_{\theta}d_{\theta}^{*}+d_{\theta}^{*}d_{\theta},

which is the
twisted Laplacian
. The space of harmonic forms with respect to this Laplacian,

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\theta}(M):=\ker\Delta_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{k}(M),

is isomorphic to
H^{k}(M,\theta)
via the Hodge theorem for twisted cohomology. In particular,

\dim\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\theta}(M)=\dim H^{k}(M,\theta).

When
\theta=0
, we recover the ordinary de Rham cohomology and the Hodge theorem. For non-exact closed forms
\theta
, the twisted cohomology groups provide a deformation of the usual cohomology that captures information about the dynamics of the multi-valued potential associated with
\theta
.

An important feature of Morse-Novikov cohomology is the
Poincaré duality
:

H^{k}(M,\theta)\cong(H^{n-k}(M,-\theta))^{*},

where
*
denotes the dual vector space. This will play a crucial role in our topological applications.

From an index-theoretic perspective, the twisted Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
is particularly interesting. When
\theta
is closed, it is an elliptic operator whose index is given by the
twisted Euler characteristic
:

\operatorname{Index}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\dim H^{k}(M,\theta).

Remarkably, when
M
is oriented, this twisted Euler characteristic equals the ordinary Euler characteristic
\chi(M)
, independent of
\theta
. This follows from the invariance of the index under continuous deformations of the operator and the fact that
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
can be continuously deformed to
\mathcal{D}_{0}
when
\theta
is closed. This observation provides a powerful link between the analytic properties of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
and the topology of
M
.

The following index formula is standard and will be fundamental for our results:

Lemma 3.1

(Index Formula)
.

The index of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
satisfies

\operatorname{Index}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta}):=\dim\ker(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})-\dim\operatorname{coker}(\mathcal{D}_{\theta})=\chi(M).

Moreover, if
d\theta=0
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\dim\mathcal{H}_{\theta}^{k}(M)=\chi(M).

Similar integral identities have been established for certain Dirac operators
[
16
,
17
]
. In our analysis, however, we rely on the following more general inequality, which plays a fundamental role (see
[
30
, Theorem 3.4]
).

Theorem 3.2

(Integral Inequality)
.

(
[
30
, Theorem 3.4]
)
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold.
For every
\alpha\in\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{\pm}(M)
, we have

\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}\leq C_{2}(n)\int_{M}|\nabla\theta|\cdot|\alpha|^{2},

where
C_{2}(n)>0
is a constant depending only on the dimension
n
.

3.2
A Priori
L^{\infty}
-Estimates for Forms in

We now derive an a priori

-estimate for smooth differential forms
\alpha\in\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{\pm}(M)
via Moser iteration.
The analysis begins with a pointwise Bochner-type identity (see
[
43

For any
\alpha\in\Omega^{l}(M)
,

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\alpha|^{2}=|\nabla\alpha|^{2}-\langle\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha,\alpha\rangle=|\nabla\alpha|^{2}-\langle\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha\rangle+\langle\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha),\alpha\rangle,

(3.1)

where
\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha)
denotes the Weitzenböck curvature operator acting on
\alpha
.

Since the operator

d+d^{*}:\Omega^{+}(M)\to\Omega^{-}(M)

is a Dirac operator (see
[
32
]
) and

\Delta_{d}=(d+d^{*})^{2},

we can apply a key observation from
[
17
]
(also
[
32
, Page 115, Proposition 5.3]
). For any smooth differential form
\alpha
,

\langle\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha\rangle=\langle(d+d^{*})\alpha,(d+d^{*})\alpha\rangle+\operatorname{div}X,

(3.2)

where
X
is a vector field defined by

\langle X,W\rangle=-\langle(d+d^{*})\alpha,W^{\sharp}\wedge\alpha+i_{W}\alpha\rangle,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\ \text{vector field }W.

(3.3)

Proposition 3.3

(Bochner Inequality for
\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
)
.

[
30
, Proposition 3.7]

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let
\theta
be a smooth
1
-form on
M
.
Fix an integer
1\leq l\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. For every
\alpha\in\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{\pm}(M)
, the following Bochner-type inequality holds:

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\alpha|^{2}\geq|\nabla\alpha|^{2}-|\theta|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}-C_{3}(n)\lambda^{-}_{n,l}|\alpha|^{2}-\operatorname{div}X,

(3.4)

where
C_{3}(n):=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
,
\lambda^{-}_{n,l}=\max\{0,-\lambda_{n,l}\}
,
and the vector field
X
satisfies the pointwise bound

|X|\leq|\theta|\,|\alpha|^{2}.

Proof.

The condition
\alpha\in\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
implies
(d_{\theta}+d^{*}_{\theta})\alpha=0.
A direct computation gives

(d+d^{*})\alpha=-\theta\wedge\alpha-i_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha,

and consequently

|(d+d^{*})\alpha|=|\theta|\,|\alpha|.

From the identities (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we obtain

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\alpha|^{2}=|\nabla\alpha|^{2}-\langle(d+d^{*})\alpha,(d+d^{*})\alpha\rangle+\langle\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha),\alpha\rangle-\operatorname{div}X.

Applying the curvature estimate (
2.1
) from Corollary
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha),\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n-l}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Bigr\rfloor\Bigl(n-\Bigl\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Bigr\rfloor\Bigr)\lambda_{n,l}|\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{n-l}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Bigr\rfloor\Bigl(n-\Bigl\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Bigr\rfloor\Bigr)\lambda^{-}_{n,l}|\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\Bigl\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Bigr\rfloor\lambda^{-}_{n,l}|\alpha|^{2}.

Substituting
|(d+d^{*})\alpha|^{2}=|\theta|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}
together with this estimate into the identity completes the proof.
∎

We now apply the mean value inequality (Proposition

) to obtain uniform bounds.

Theorem 3.4

(
L^{\infty}
-Estimate)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n

\|f\|_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}D\|df\|_{2},\hskip 20.00003ptp>2.

For each
\alpha\in\ker\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\cap\Omega^{\pm}(M)
and
+\infty\geq q>p

\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}(p,q)\bigl(1+\sqrt{C_{0}(p,q)C_{s}DB}\bigr)^{\frac{2pq}{q-p}}\|\alpha\|_{2},

(3.5)

where
B^{2}:=C_{3}(n)\bigl(\|\theta\|_{q}^{2}+\|\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}\bigr)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.3
, we have the differential inequality

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\alpha|^{2}\geq-|\theta|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}-C_{3}(n)\lambda^{-}_{n,l}|\alpha|^{2}-\operatorname{div}X.

Applying the Kato inequality
|\nabla|\alpha||\leq|\nabla\alpha|
(see
[
8
]

-|\alpha|\Delta_{d}|\alpha|\geq-|\theta|^{2}|\alpha|^{2}-C_{3}(n)\lambda^{-}_{n,l}|\alpha|^{2}-\operatorname{div}X.

Now set
f=|\alpha|
. Then
f
satisfies

f\Delta_{d}f\leq\bigl(|\theta|^{2}+C_{3}(n)\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\bigr)f^{2}+\operatorname{div}X,

with
|X|\leq|\theta|f^{2}
. Define

h_{1}=|\theta|^{2}+C_{3}(n)\lambda^{-}_{n,l},\hskip 20.00003pth_{2}=|\theta|.

Their norms satisfy

\|h_{1}\|_{q}\leq\|\theta\|_{q}^{2}+C_{3}(n)\|\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\|_{\frac{q}{2}},\hskip 20.00003pt\|h_{2}\|_{q}=\|\theta\|_{q}.

Applying Proposition
2.4
with
h_{1},h_{2}
as above gives precisely the estimate (
3.5
).
∎

3.3
L^{\infty}
-Estimates for Solutions of Schrödinger-Type Equations

We consider Schrödinger operators of the form

\overline{\Delta}+V,

where the potential
V\in C^{\infty}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}(E)\bigr)
is a smooth field of symmetric endomorphisms of
E
. By the Weitzenböck formula, the Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{d}
acting on
1
-forms is precisely such an operator.

The following estimate for sections satisfying a Schrödinger equation will play a crucial role when we later apply our results to harmonic
1
-forms.

Proposition 3.5

(
L^{\infty}
-estimate for solutions to Schrödinger-type equations)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold satisfying the Sobolev inequality

\|f\|_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}D\|df\|_{2},\hskip 20.00003ptp>2.

Assume
+\infty\geq q>p
. Then every solution of

\nabla^{*}\nabla S+VS=0

satisfies the
L^{\infty}
-estimate

\|S\|_{\infty}\leq\exp\Bigl\{C_{4}(p,q)\,\sqrt{\|V^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}}\,C_{s}D\Bigr\}\,\|S\|_{2},

(3.6)

where

C_{4}(p,q):=\frac{2p}{p-2}\sqrt{C_{0}(p,q)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}(\sqrt{\gamma}-1)}.

Proof.

Applying the Kato inequality to
\nabla^{*}\nabla S+VS=0
yields

|S|\,\Delta_{d}|S|\leq\langle\nabla^{*}\nabla S,S\rangle\leq V^{-}|S|^{2}.

Hence
f:=|S|
satisfies

f\Delta_{d}f\leq|V^{-}|f^{2}.

Proceeding as in the derivation of (
2.7
) (with
h_{1}=|V^{-}|
and
h_{2}=0
), for any
k\geq 1
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}f^{2k-2}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3k-1}\,\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}}^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\,\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}}^{2k}.

Consequently,

\|df^{k}\|_{2}\leq k^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|V^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\frac{2kq}{q-2}}^{k}.

A standard Moser iteration (similar to, but simpler than, the proof of Proposition
2.4
) now gives

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(1+C_{s}D\sqrt{C_{0}(p,q)}\,\|V^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma^{\ell+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigg(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{2p}{(p-2)\gamma^{\ell+1}}\ln\Bigl(1+C_{s}D\sqrt{C_{0}(p,q)}\|V^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\Bigr)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigg(\frac{2p}{p-2}C_{s}\sqrt{C_{0}(p,q)}\sqrt{\|V^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}}\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}(\sqrt{\gamma}-1)}\Bigg).

Taking square roots and using
f=|S|
yields the desired estimate.
∎

The next lemma connects the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality to the ordinary Sobolev inequality, which will be needed in the sequel.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 4
) smooth Riemannian manifold satisfying the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality

\|f-\bar{f}\|_{2}\leq C_{s}D\|df\|_{\frac{2p}{p+2}},

where
\bar{f}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}\int_{M}f\,dvol_{g}
and
p>2
. Then

\|f\|_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\leq\|f\|_{2}+C_{s}\frac{p}{p-2}D\|df\|_{2}.

Proof.

Let
u=|f|^{\frac{p}{p-2}}
. Then

\bar{u}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}\int_{M}|f|^{\frac{p}{p-2}}dvol_{g}=\|f\|_{\frac{p}{p-2}}^{\frac{p}{p-2}},

and

|du|=\frac{p}{p-2}|d|f||\cdot|f|^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\leq\frac{p}{p-2}|df|\cdot|f|^{\frac{2}{p-2}}.

By Sobolev–Poincaré inequality, Hölder inequality and Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{\frac{p}{p-2}}_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}=\|u\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\bar{u}\|_{2}+|\bar{u}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\frac{p}{p-2}D\|d|f|\cdot|f|^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\|_{\frac{2p}{p+2}}+|\bar{u}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\frac{p}{p-2}D\|df\|_{2}\|f\|^{\frac{2}{p-2}}_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}+\|f\|^{\frac{p}{p-2}}_{\frac{p}{p-2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\frac{p}{p-2}D\|df\|_{2}\|f\|^{\frac{2}{p-2}}_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}+\|f\|_{2}\|f\|^{\frac{2}{p-2}}_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}.

Dividing both sides by
\|f\|_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}^{\frac{2}{p-2}}
yields the desired inequality.
∎

Using the integral identity (Theorem

Lemma 3.7

For (

Again applying the

4
Applications to Integral Curvature Conditions

4.1
Curvature Operator with
L^{p}
Lower Bound

We first establish that under suitable integral Ricci curvature bounds, the manifold satisfies a Sobolev–Poincaré inequality with explicit constants.

Lemma 4.1

(Sobolev–Poincaré Inequality under Integral Ricci Bounds)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold, and let
q>p>n
. There exists a positive constant
C_{7}(n,p)
such that, if the Ricci curvature satisfies

\|\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}\leq C_{7}(n,p),

then for every function
f\in L^{2}_{1}(M)
, we have the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality

\|f-\bar{f}\|_{2}\leq C_{8}(n,p)D\|df\|_{\frac{2p}{p+2}},

where
\bar{f}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}\int_{M}f\,dvol_{g}
.

Proof.

Recall the Sobolev constant is given by (
[
25
, Page 201]
)

\lambda_{\frac{2p}{p+2},2}=\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}(M)}\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{\frac{2p}{p+2}}}{\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-a\|_{2}}.

The constants occur in the embedding
L^{\frac{2p}{p+2}}_{1}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
for any
p>n
.

Since the infimum over constants
a
is attained at the mean value
\bar{f}
, i.e.

\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-a\|_{2}=\|f-\bar{f}\|_{2},

we have

\|\nabla f\|_{\frac{2p}{p+2}}\geq\lambda_{\frac{2p}{p+2},2}\|f-\bar{f}\|_{2}.

Now observe that

\Bigl(\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}\int_{M}(\operatorname{Ric}^{-}D^{2}-1)_{+}^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dvol_{g}\Bigr)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq\|\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\|_{\frac{p}{2}}D^{2}\leq\|\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2},

where
(\operatorname{Ric}^{-}D^{2}-1)_{+}=\max\{\operatorname{Ric}^{-}D^{2}-1,0\}
.

By
[
25
, Theorem 6]
(in this time
\alpha=\frac{1}{D}
), if

\|\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}\leq C_{7}(n,p):=\frac{1}{2}(e^{B(p)}-1)^{-1},

then there exists a positive constant

\gamma(D,n,p):=\frac{B(p)}{D}\min\Bigl\{2^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\frac{1}{4}(e^{B(p)}-1)^{-1}\Bigr\}

such that

K(p,\frac{2p}{p+2})\cdot\lambda_{\frac{2p}{p+2},2}\geq\gamma(D,n,p),

where
B(p)
is the constant appearing in
[
25
, Theorem 2]
and
K(p,\frac{2p}{p+2})
is the constant appearing in
[
25
, Theorem 6]
. This immediately implies the desired inequality with

C_{8}(n,p)D=\gamma(D,n,p)^{-1}K(p,\frac{2p}{p+2}).

∎

As an immediate corollary, we obtain uniform
L^{\infty}
-estimates for harmonic forms under an integral curvature operator condition. Building on the work of Li
[
33
]
, we further extend the Bochner technique and derive bounds for the Betti numbers in this setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Because
\theta
is a harmonic

Hence the function

Now suppose that

therefore provides the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality

Applying Proposition

t>0
such that

\ker\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}\cap\Omega^{\pm}_{l}(M)=\{0\}.

Because
\theta
is closed, we have the decomposition

\ker\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}\cap\Omega^{\bullet}_{l}(M)=\bigoplus_{k\leq l}\ker\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}\cap\Omega^{k}(M)\bigoplus_{k\leq l}\ker\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}\cap\Omega^{n-k}(M),

where
\bullet=+
corresponds to even
k
and
\bullet=-
to odd
k
.

Finally, recalling that
\ker\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}\cap\Omega^{k}(M)\cong H^{k}(M,t\theta)
(the
k
-th Morse–Novikov cohomology group), we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
H^{1}_{\text{dR}}(M)\neq 0
, there exists a nonzero harmonic 1-form
\theta
.
By Corollary
4.2
, under the condition

\big\|\lambda_{2n,l}^{-}\big\|_{\frac{q}{2}}\,D^{2}\leq C_{10},

we can choose
t>0
such that

H^{k}(M,\,t\theta)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all }k\leq l,

where
H^{k}(M,t\theta)
denotes the Morse–Novikov cohomology twisted by
t\theta
.

Using Poincaré duality for Morse–Novikov cohomology
[
39
]
, we have

\dim H^{k}(M,t\theta)=\dim H^{2n-k}(M,t\theta).

Hence the Euler characteristic can be written as

\displaystyle\chi(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{k}\dim H^{k}(M,t\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k<n}2(-1)^{k}\dim H^{k}(M,t\theta)+(-1)^{n}\dim H^{n}(M,t\theta).

If
l=n
, the hypothesis gives
H^{k}(M,t\theta)=0
for all
k\leq n
.
By duality it follows that
H^{k}(M,t\theta)=0
for every
k
, and consequently

\chi(M)=0.

If
l=n-1
, then
H^{k}(M,t\theta)=0
for all
k\neq n
.
Thus

\chi(M)=(-1)^{n}\dim H^{n}(M,t\theta),

which yields

(-1)^{n}\chi(M)=\dim H^{n}(M,t\theta)\geq 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},g)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with nonzero first de Rham cohomology group, and let
q>p>2n
. Suppose that the sectional curvature and diameter satisfy

|\sec(g)|(x)\leq K(x),\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm{diam}}(M)\leq D.

where
K(x)
is a nonnegative function on
M
. There exists a positive constant
C_{10}(n,p,q)
such that if

\|K\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2n(2n-1)}C_{10}(n,p,q),

then the Euler characteristic of
M
satisfies
\chi(M)=0
.

Proof.

By
[
12
, Proposition 3.8]
, the eigenvalues of the curvature operator obey

\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}\geq-\frac{2n(2n-1)}{3}\,K.

Consequently, if

\|K\|_{\frac{q}{2}}\,D^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2n(2n-1)}\,C_{10},

then

\big\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\big\|_{\frac{q}{2}}\,D^{2}\leq C_{10}.

Applying Theorem
1.2
with
l=n
gives
\chi(M)=0
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Let
(M^{4},g)
be a closed 4‑dimensional Riemannian manifold.
The Euler characteristic can be expressed in terms of the Betti numbers as

\chi(M)=2+b_{2}(M)-2b_{1}(M).

If
b_{1}(M)=0
, then

\chi(M)=2+b_{2}(M)\geq 2>0.

Assume now that
b_{1}(M)\geq 1
. Then there exists a non‑zero harmonic 1‑form
\theta
.
Applying Corollary
4.2
with
n=4
and
l=1
,
the hypothesis
\|\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\|_{\frac{q}{2}}D^{2}\leq C_{10}
guarantees the existence of a number
t>0
such that

H^{0}(M,t\theta)=H^{1}(M,t\theta)=0.

Poincaré duality gives
H^{3}(M,t\theta)=H^{4}(M,t\theta)=0
. Consequently,

\chi(M)=\dim H^{2}(M,t\theta)\geq 0.

In both cases we obtain
\chi(M)\geq 0
, which completes the proof.
∎

5
Eigenvalue Estimates for the Rough Laplacian

We now turn to eigenvalue estimates for the rough Laplacian on 1-forms under integral curvature conditions. First, we recall a Poincaré-type inequality on manifolds with almost nonnegative Ricci curvature.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}(g)D^{2}(g)\geq-(n-1)b^{2}

for some constant
b>0
. Then for each
1\leq p\leq\frac{nq}{n-q}
,
p<\infty
, and
f\in L^{q}_{1}(M)
, we have

\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq S_{p,q}\|df\|_{L^{q}(M)},

where

\bar{f}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}\int_{M}fd\operatorname{vol}_{g},S_{p,q}=\Bigl(\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}{\operatorname{Vol}(S^{n}(1))}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}R(b)\Sigma(n,p,q),

\Sigma(n,p,q)
is the Sobolev constant of the canonical unit sphere
S^{n}(1)
, and
R(b)=\frac{D(g)}{bC(b)}
with
C(b)
being the unique positive root of

x\int_{0}^{b}(\cosh t+x\sinh t)^{n-1}dt=\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}t\,dt.

(5.1)

Proof.

See
[
16
, Theorem 5.1]
or
[
8
, Page 397]
.
∎

In order to use Theorem
5.1
, the following estimate for the constant
C(b)
will be crucial for obtaining explicit lower bounds.

Lemma 5.2
.

(
[
31
, Lemma 2.3]
)
Let
C(b)
be the unique positive root of equation (
5.1
). Then

bC(b)\geq a_{n}e^{-(n-1)b}>0

for some constant
a_{n}
depending only on
n
.

The following proposition is a direct consequence of Theorem
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold. Denote by
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{{n\choose 2}}
the eigenvalues of the curvature operator and set

\lambda_{n,l}:=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-l}.

Fix
D>0
and
1\leq l\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
. If
f\in L^{2}_{1}(M)
, then

\|f-\bar{f}\|_{2}\leq C(n)De^{\frac{(n-1)}{\sqrt{n-l}}\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}}\|df\|_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}.

Proof.

Noting that

\operatorname{Ric}\geq-\frac{n-1}{n-l}\lambda^{-}_{n,l}.

By Theorem
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
(with
p=2
and
q=\frac{2n}{n+2}
, we may choose the Sobolev constant

C_{s}=C(n)e^{\frac{(n-1)}{\sqrt{n-l}}\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{n,l}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}},

where

C(n)=a^{-1}_{n}\Bigl(\operatorname{Vol}(S^{n}(1))\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{n}}\Sigma(n,2,\frac{2n}{n+2}).

∎

We now prove the eigenvalue estimate.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
\theta
be a nontrivial 1-form attaining the first eigenvalue:

\nabla^{*}\nabla\theta-\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\theta=0.

This equation is a special case of the equation in Theorem
1.1
with the potential
V\equiv-\lambda_{1}^{(1)}
.
Observe that

\|V^{-}\|_{\infty}=\lambda_{1}^{(1)},

and the Sobolev constant satisfies

C_{s}(\infty)=C(n)\exp\Bigl(\frac{2n-1}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}\Bigr).

If, in addition,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{(1)}D^{2}}\leq\min\bigg\{

\displaystyle e\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}\exp\Bigl(-\sqrt{C_{s}(\infty)}C_{6}(n,2n,\infty)(\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6\sqrt{e}C_{s}(\infty)C_{1}(n,\infty)}\exp\Bigl(-\sqrt{C_{s}(\infty)}C_{6}(n,2n,\infty)(\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2C_{s}(\infty)C_{4}(n,\infty)}\bigg\},

then by Theorem
1.1
(with
p=2n
and
q=+\infty
in this case) we would obtain
\chi(M)=0
, contradicting the hypothesis that
\chi(M)\neq 0
.
Hence the opposite strict inequality must hold, which is precisely the claimed estimate.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(M^{2n},g)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, smooth Riemannian manifold with nonvanishing Euler characteristic. Suppose that the Ricci curvature satisfies

\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq 0.

Then the first eigenvalue of the Hodge–Laplacian on
1
-forms satisfies

\mu_{1}^{(1)}\geq\lambda^{(1)}_{1}.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq 0
, for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
we have

\frac{(\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha)_{L^{2}}}{\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\geq\frac{(\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha,\alpha)_{L^{2}}}{\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

Under the condition
\operatorname{Ric}(g)\geq 0
, any harmonic
1
-form
\alpha
satisfies
\nabla\alpha=0
, i.e.,
\alpha
is parallel. Because
\chi(M)\neq 0
, there are no non-zero harmonic
1
-forms. The first non-zero eigenvalue of the Hodge–Laplacian on
1
-forms is

\mu^{(1)}_{1}=\inf_{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{(\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha)_{L^{2}(M)}}{\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}.

and the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on
1
-forms is

\lambda^{(1)}_{1}=\inf_{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}.

We immediately conclude

\mu^{(1)}_{1}\geq\lambda^{(1)}_{1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

We only need to prove that
\chi(M)=0
when
b_{1}(M)>0
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
b_{1}(M)>0
but
\chi(M)\neq 0
.
Since
\chi(M)\neq 0
, by Theorem
1.6
the first eigenvalue of the rough Laplacian on
1
-forms satisfies (
1.1
). Because
\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2}\leq C_{11}(n)
and we may choose
C_{11}(n)
sufficiently small, the second and third terms inside the minimum is larger than the first. Consequently,

\begin{split}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{(1)}D^{2}}&>e\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}\exp\Bigl(-\sqrt{C_{s}(\infty)}C_{6}(n,2n,\infty)(\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big)\\
&\geq e^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}D^{2}}.\end{split}

(5.2)

On the other hand, since
b_{1}(M)>0
, there exists a non-trivial harmonic
1
-form
\theta
.
The Weitzenböck formula gives

\nabla^{*}\nabla\theta+\operatorname{Ric}(\theta)=0.

Taking the inner product with
\theta
and integrating yields

\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}=-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}\int_{M}\langle\operatorname{Ric}(\theta),\theta\rangle\leq\|\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\|_{\infty}\|\theta\|_{2}^{2}.

Using the pointwise estimate
\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\leq\frac{2n-1}{n}\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}
, we obtain

\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2n-1}{n}\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}\|\theta\|_{2}^{2}.

(5.3)

Noting that
\ker\nabla\cap\Omega^{1}=0
when
\chi(M)\neq 0
. Hence, by the variational characterization of
\lambda_{1}^{(1)}
,

\begin{split}\lambda_{1}^{(1)}:&=\inf_{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla\alpha\|_{2}^{2}}{\|\alpha\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{\|\nabla\theta\|_{2}^{2}}{\|\theta\|_{2}^{2}}\\
&\leq\|\operatorname{Ric}^{-}\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\frac{2n-1}{n}\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}.\end{split}

Combining this with (
5.2
) gives

e\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}<\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\leq\frac{2n-1}{n}\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty},

which is impossible because
e>2>\frac{2n-1}{n}
.
Therefore, when
b_{1}(M)>0
, we must have
\chi(M)=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

By Corollary
1.7
and the condition
\chi(M)\neq 0
, we have
b_{1}(M)=0
. Consequently,

\mu^{(1)}_{1}=\inf_{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha)_{L^{2}(M)}}{\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}.

Moreover, Theorem
1.6
yields (see (
5.2
))

\lambda^{(1)}_{1}>e\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}.

For any non-zero
1
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
,

\displaystyle(\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha)_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{*}\nabla\alpha,\alpha)_{L^{2}(M)}+(\operatorname{Ric}(\alpha),\alpha)_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{(1)}_{1}\,\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}-\frac{2n-1}{n}\,\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}\,\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle>\left[e\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}-\frac{2n-1}{n}\,\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}\right]\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle>(e-2)\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}\,\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

This implies that for any non-zero
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
,

\frac{(\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha)_{L^{2}(M)}}{\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}>(e-2)\|\lambda^{-}_{2n,n}\|_{\infty}.

Taking the infimum over all non-zero
1
-forms
\alpha
yields the desired lower bound for
\mu^{(1)}_{1}
.
∎

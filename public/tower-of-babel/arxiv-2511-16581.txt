1.
Introduction

By a coherent system on an algebraic variety we mean a pair
(E,V)
where
E
is torsion free sheaf together with a linear subspace of prescribed dimension of its space of sections
H^{0}(E)
. These objects were introduced in 1990’s by Le Potier
[
LePotier
]
, Raghavendra and Vishwanath
[
RV
]
(under the name of “ Pairs”) and King and Newstead
[
KN
]
(under the name of “Brill-Noether pair”) motivated by problems in Brill–Noether theory, Hilbert schemes and gauge theory, and later developed in a systematic way by Le Potier
[
LePotier
]
, Min He
[
He
]
, Bradlow, García-Prada, Muñoz and Newstead
[
BGMN
]
. Coherent systems provided a unifying framework for analyzing the relationship between torsion free sheaves and their spaces of sections, refining the classical Brill–Noether theory by incorporating stability conditions. This perspective has yielded profound insights into the geometry of moduli spaces of torsion free sheaves. The notion of stability for coherent systems depends on a polynomial
\alpha
, where degree of a polynomial is less than the dimension of the variety
[
He
, Théoréme 4.2]
. Studying how the moduli space changes as
\alpha
varies (the so-called wall-crossing behavior) has led to a detailed understanding of the birational geometry of moduli spaces and has provided explicit examples of flips and contractions in moduli theory (see for instance
[
BGMN
,
CHCH
,
He
,
Th
]
).

Coherent systems and their moduli spaces on curves has been extensively studied by several authors, for instance: Bradlow, García-Prada, Muñoz, Mercat and Newstead determined general topological and geometric properties
[
BGMN
,
BGMN1
,
BGMMN
,
BGMMN2
]
, Lange and Newstead
[
LN
]
, Teixidor and Newstead
[
TN
]
studied coherent systems on the projetive line and on the elliptic curves. Bhosle studied coherent systems on a nodal curve
[
Bh
]
Also, the study of moduli spaces of coherent systems on curves has been successful to study the existence of special divisors on curves and problems related to syzygy bundles on curves, for a recent account of the theory and the description of some open questions we refer to reader to
[
B
,
N
,
N1
]
.

The passage from curves to higher-dimensional varieties raises subtle new challenges, both conceptual and technical. When dealing with torsion free sheaves on surfaces, stabi-

lity conditions and moduli constructions are considerably more intricate: the geometry of surfaces introduces additional features. Indeed, the notion of stability of a torsion free sheaf depends on an ample divisor, there is a rich wall-crossing behavior among different moduli spaces and, in general, the construction of stable bundles of higher rank is difficult. Despite these difficulties, extending the theory of coherent systems from curves to surfaces is of fundamental importance (see for instance
[
CHCH
,
MTR
,
CMR
]
). It enriches the classical picture of moduli of sheaves on surfaces and also provides a natural testing ground for conjectures inspired by higher-dimensional Brill–Noether-theory.

In this work, we contribute to the development of the theory of coherent systems on algebraic surfaces by describing the moduli space of
\alpha
-stable coherent systems for
\alpha
big. In fact, first of all we see that if there exists an
\alpha
-stable coherent system
(E,V)
, then
\alpha
is bounded above (see Proposition
3.3
). For
\alpha
closed to this bound, we will see that the moduli space is a Grassmann bundle over some moduli space of stable bundles (see Theorem
3.15
and Theorem
3.17
). We obtain this description by establishing some relation between coherent systems and
H
-stable torsion free sheaves. In addition, we give some examples where the moduli space of coherent systems is generically smooth.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we collect background about coherent systems on surfaces needed later on. Section 3 contains our main results concerning to moduli spaces of
\alpha
-stable coherent systems of type
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
with
k<n
.

Through all this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 and
X
always will denote a smooth, irreducible, projective algebraic surface.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible projective surface.

(1)

A
coherent system of dimension
d
on
X
is a pair
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})
where
\mathcal{E}
is a coherent sheaf
of dimension
d
on
X
, and

\mathcal{V}\subseteq H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})
.

(2)

A morphism of coherent systems
(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1})\to(\mathcal{E}_{2},\mathcal{V}_{2})
is a morphism of coherent sheaves
\phi:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}
such that
H^{0}(\phi)(\mathcal{V}_{1})\subset\mathcal{V}_{2}
.

(3)

A coherent subsystem of
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})
is a pair
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{W})

where
0\neq\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
is a proper subsheaf of

\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\cap H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
.

(4)

A quotient coherent system of
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})
is a coherent system
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
together with a morphism

\phi:(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})\to(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
of coherent systems such that the morphism
\phi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{G}
is surjective and
H^{0}(\phi)(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{Z}
.

In this paper, we restrict our attention to coherent systems
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})
of dimension 2, that is, on pairs
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})
where
\mathcal{E}
is a torsion free sheaf. Let us denote by
(E,V)
the coherent systems with
E
a torsion free sheaf.

Remark 2.2
.

If
(E,V)
is a coherent system, then for any coherent subsystem
(F,W)\subset(E,V)
,
F
is torsion free.

Definition 2.3
.

A coherent system
(E,V)
is said to be of type
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
if
E
is a torsion free sheaf of rank
n
with Chern classes

c_{i}\in H^{2i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
for
i=1,2
and
V\subset H^{0}(X,E)
is a subspace of dimension

.

In this paper our main concern is to study coherent systems of type
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
with
k<n
. So, from now on, we will keep this assumption.

Associated to the coherent systems there is a notion of stability which depends on a parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
. To introduce it, it is convenient to fix some notation.

Notation 2.4
.

We denote by
\mathbb{Q}[m]
the space of polynomials in the variable
m
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
.
Given two polynomials
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
, we write
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
if and only if

p_{1}(m)-p_{2}(m)\leq 0
for
m\gg 0
.
We say that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
if
\alpha(m)>0
for
m\gg 0
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
and
H
an ample divisor on
X
. Given a coherent system
(E,V)
of type
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
on

, we define its

by

\displaystyle p^{\alpha}_{H,(E,V)}(m)=\frac{k}{n}\cdot\alpha+\frac{P_{H,E}(m)}{n},

where
P_{H,E}(m)
denotes the Hilbert polynomial of
E
.

Notice that, since
X
is a smooth projective surface, the Hilbert polynomial with respect to
H
of a torsion free sheaf
E
of rank
n
on
X
with Chern classes
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
can be written as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{P_{H,E}(m)}{n}&=\frac{H^{2}m^{2}}{2}+\left[\frac{c_{1}H}{n}-\frac{K_{X}H}{2}\right]m+\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{c_{1}^{2}-(c_{1}K_{X})}{2}-c_{2}\right)+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\\
&=\frac{H^{2}m^{2}}{2}+\left[\frac{c_{1}H}{n}-\frac{K_{X}H}{2}\right]m+\frac{\chi(E)}{n}\\
\end{array}

where
K_{X}
denotes the canonical divisor of
X
and
\chi(F)
the Euler Characteristic of a sheaf
F
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
and
H
be an ample divisor on
X
. We say that
(E,V)
is
\alpha
-stable (resp.
\alpha
-semistable) if for any proper coherent subsystem
0\neq(F,W)\subset(E,V)

the following inequality holds

(2.1)

p^{\alpha}_{H,(F,W)}(m)<p^{\alpha}_{H,(E,V)}(m)\,\,\,\,\,(resp.\leq).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
. We say that
\alpha
is a regular value if there exists
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
with
\beta_{1}<\alpha<\beta_{2}
such that if
(E,V)
is
\alpha
-stable, then
(E,V)
is
\gamma
-stable for any
\gamma\in(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
. If

Notation 2.8
.

According to
[
He
]
; Theorem 4.2, given
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
there is a finite number of critical values

0=\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{s}

with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
and it in addition

3.
\alpha
-stable coherent systems for large
\alpha

The first goal of this section is to obtain an upper bound
c
for the value of
\alpha_{s}
. Once this will be established, we will describe the moduli space of
\alpha
-stable coherent systems for
\alpha_{s}<\alpha<c
and we will relate it with a moduli space of stable bundles (in the sense of Mumford-Takemoto).

To this end, first observe that any coherent system fits into an exact sequence of the form

(3.1)

0\to N\to V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\smash{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\varphi}}E\to F\to 0

where
N
is a torsion free sheaf and
F
is a coherent sheaf, possibly with torsion. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A coherent system
(E,V)
is said to be generated if the valuation map
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E
is surjective.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
H
be an ample divisor and let
(E,V)
be a coherent system of type
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
.

(a)

If
(E,V)
is generated, then
0\leq c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)-n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. In particular
c_{1}H\geq 0
.

(b)

If
(E,V)
is
\alpha
-semistable for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
, then
c_{1}H\geq 0
.

Proof.

(a)
Let
(E,V)
be a generated coherent system. Here,
E
is a quotient of the sheaf
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}:=\mathcal{O}^{\oplus k}_{X}
which is
H
-semistable. Thus,

\frac{P_{H,\mathcal{O}^{\oplus k}_{X}}(m)}{k}\leq\frac{P_{H,E}(m)}{n}

which is equivalent to
0\leq c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)-n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. In particular, the coefficient
c_{1}H
must be positive, as claimed.

(b)
Let
E^{\prime}
be a torsion free sheaf generated by
V
. In particular,
n_{E^{\prime}}=\operatorname{rank}E^{\prime}\leq k<n
. Thus,
(E^{\prime},V)
is a subsystem of
(E,V)
. Say that
(E^{\prime},V)
is of type
(n_{E^{\prime}},c^{\prime}_{1},c^{\prime}_{2},v)
with
0<n_{E^{\prime}}<n
. Let
\alpha=am+b\in\mathbb{Q}[m]_{>0}
. Since
(E,V)
is
\alpha
-semistable we have
p_{H,(E^{\prime},V)}^{\alpha}(m)\leq p_{H,(E,V)}^{\alpha}(m)
which implies
(c^{\prime}_{1}H+av)/n_{E^{\prime}}\leq(c_{1}H+av)/n+
. Since
n_{E^{\prime}}<n
, this implies
c^{\prime}_{1}H/n_{E^{\prime}}\leq c_{1}H/n
and hence since
(E^{\prime},V)
is generated, by the statement
(a)
we conclude that
0\leq c^{\prime}_{1}H/n_{E^{\prime}}\leq c_{1}H/n
as desired.
∎

Next result will give us the desired bound
c
for
\alpha_{s}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
H
be an ample divisor and let
(E,V)
be a coherent system of type
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
. If
(E,V)
is
\alpha-
semistable for some
\alpha>0
, then

\alpha\leq\frac{1}{n-k}(c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)-n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})).

Proof.

Let
E^{\prime}
be a torsion free sheaf generated by
V
. Then
(E^{\prime},V)\subset(E,V)
is of type
(n^{\prime};c_{1}(E^{\prime}),c_{2}(E^{\prime}),k)
with
n^{\prime}\leq k
. By Proposition
3.2
, since
(E^{\prime},V)
is generated we have

\alpha+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq\alpha\frac{k}{n^{\prime}}+\frac{c_{1}(E^{\prime})H}{n^{\prime}}m+\frac{\chi(E^{\prime})}{n^{\prime}}.

On the other hand, since
(E,V)
is
\alpha-
semistable, we have
P^{\alpha}_{H,(E^{\prime},V)}(m)\leq P^{\alpha}_{H,(E,V)}(m)
. Hence, putting all together we get

\alpha+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq\alpha\frac{k}{n^{\prime}}+\frac{c_{1}(E^{\prime})H}{n^{\prime}}m+\frac{\chi(E^{\prime})}{n^{\prime}}\leq\alpha\frac{k}{n}+\frac{c_{1}H}{n}m+\frac{\chi(E)}{n}

which implies

\alpha\leq\frac{1}{n-k}(c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)-n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})).

∎

Remark 3.4
.

According to Proposition
3.2
,
c_{1}H\geq 0
. Moreover, since we are interested in
\alpha
-semistable coherent systems with respect to polynomials of degree less or equal than one, and not only with respect to constants, from now on we will assume
c_{1}H>0
. In particular,
0<c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)-n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

Our next goal is to prove that for values of
\alpha
big enough, an
\alpha
-stable coherent system
(E,V)
defines an exact sequence as (
3.1
) with
N=0
and
F
an
H
-stable torsion free sheaf. To this end, let us start with the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
n,k,p,p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\leq n
,
p,p^{\prime}>0
and
d,q,q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
.
Let

\epsilon(p,q)=\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)\left(\frac{dp+(n-k)q}{n-k+p}\right).

Then,

(i)

If
d-q>0
, then
\epsilon(p,q)\leq\epsilon(p^{\prime},q)
if and only if
p\leq p^{\prime}
;

(ii)

\epsilon(p,q)\leq\epsilon(p,q^{\prime})
if and only if
q\leq q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
d-q>0
,
(i)
follows from the fact that
\epsilon(p,q)\leq\epsilon(p^{\prime},q)
if and only if
(p-p^{\prime})(d-q)\leq 0
. The equivalence
(ii)
is a direct computation.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(E,V)
be a coherent system of type
(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
which is
\alpha
-semistable for some
\alpha>0
. Then,
there exists

\alpha_{I}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{n-k}(c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)-n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}))\right]

such that, if
\alpha>\alpha_{I}
, the map
\varphi:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E
is injective.

Proof.

Let us denote by
N
the kernel of
\varphi:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E
and set
k_{0}:=rk(N)
.
By the exact sequence

0\rightarrow N\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus k}\rightarrow Im(\varphi)\rightarrow 0,

we get
rk(Im(\varphi))=k-k_{0}
.
Hence,
(Im(\varphi),V)
is a generated coherent subsystem of
(E,V)
of type
(k-k_{0};c_{1}(Im(\varphi)),c_{2}(Im(\varphi)),k)
. Since
(E,V)
is
\alpha-
semistable, we have

P^{\alpha}_{H,(Im(\varphi),V)}(m)\leq P^{\alpha}_{H,(E,V)}(m),

which is equivalent to

\frac{k}{k-k_{0}}\alpha+\frac{c_{1}(Im(\varphi))H}{k-k_{0}}m+\frac{\chi(Im(\varphi))}{k-k_{0}}\leq\frac{k}{n}\alpha+\frac{c_{1}H}{n}m+\frac{\chi(E)}{n}

and to the fact that

\alpha\leq\frac{[c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)](k-k_{0})-n[c_{1}(Im(\varphi))Hm+\chi(Im(\varphi))]}{k(n-k+k_{0})}.

Consider
d=c_{1}(E)Hm+\chi(E)
and
-d_{0}=c_{1}(Im(\varphi))Hm+\chi(Im(\varphi))
. According to this notation,

(3.2)

\alpha\leq\frac{(k-k_{0})d-n(-d_{0})}{k(n-k+k_{0})},

which is equivalent to

\alpha\leq\frac{d-\epsilon(k_{0},-d_{0})}{n-k}.

Since
(Im(\varphi),V)
is generated, by Lemma
3.2
,
-d_{0}\geq(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
and hence, by Lemma
3.5
(ii), it follows that

\alpha\leq\frac{d-\epsilon(k_{0},-d_{0})}{n-k}\leq\frac{d-\epsilon(k_{0},(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}))}{n-k}.

Claim:

d>(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

Assume that
d\leq(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. By Remark
3.4
,
0<c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)-n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
, here, we would have

n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})<d\leq(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})

which in particular implies that
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})<0
and hence
c_{1}Hm+\chi(E)=d<0
which contradicts the assumption
c_{1}H>0
(see Remark
3.4
).

It follows from the claim and Lemma
3.5
(i) that, for any
p<k_{0}
,

(3.3)

\alpha\leq\frac{d-\epsilon(k_{0},-d_{0})}{n-k}<\frac{d-\epsilon(k_{0},(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}))}{n-k}<\frac{d-\epsilon(p,(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}))}{n-k}.

Let us take

\alpha_{I}:=\frac{d-\epsilon(1,(k-k_{0})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}))}{n-k}

and
\alpha>\alpha_{I}
. If
k_{0}>1
, taking
p=1
in (
3.3
) we get a contradiction. Hence

and
\varphi
is injective.
∎

Proposition 3.8

Let
(E,V)

(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)

an

-semistable (Mumford-Takemoto) torsion free sheaf.

Proof.

By the above results and the fact that
\alpha
is big enough, we can assume that
(E,V)
defines the extension

(3.5)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus k}\rightarrow E\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0

with
F

is
H
-semistable. We can assume that
k<n-1

has rank

Let

be any subbundle with quotient torsion free. Denote the rank and degree of

by
n^{\prime}-k
and
d^{\prime}
, respectively. Note that
k<n^{\prime}<n
.

By the pullback construction, we obtain a subextension

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus k}\rightarrow E^{\prime}\rightarrow F^{\prime}\rightarrow 0,

where
E^{\prime}
is a subbundle of
E
. Notice that

, so that

determines a coherent subsystem
(E^{\prime},V)
of type
(n^{\prime};c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},k)
. In addition, observe that from the exact sequences (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we have the following equalities:

\chi(E)=\chi(F)+k\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}),\quad\chi(E^{\prime})=\chi(F^{\prime})+k\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}),\quad c_{i}(E)=c_{i}(F)\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{i}(E)=c_{i}(F^{\prime}).

Since
(E^{\prime},V)
is a subcoherent system of
(E,V)
and
(E,V)
is

which is equivalent to

c_{1}^{\prime}Hm+\chi(F^{\prime})\leq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}(c_{1}Hm+\chi(F))+(n^{\prime}-n)\frac{k}{n}(\alpha+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})).

In particular,

c_{1}^{\prime}H\leq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}c_{1}H+(n^{\prime}-n)\frac{k}{n}\frac{1}{n-k}c_{1}H,

which is equivalent to

-semistable.
∎

We have already seen that for

big enough, an

semistable coherent system

defines an extension

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus k}\rightarrow E\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0

with

an
H
-semistable (Mumford-Takemoto) torsion free sheaf. Our next goal is to reverse the construction. To do so, we start with the following technical result.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

be a coherent sheaf such that

. Then,

and

.

The first inequality follows from the fact that the vector bundle

is
H
-semistable with first Chern class equal to 0. On the other hand, since

and any section of

is nowhere vanishing. Thus, sections of

generate a submodule of

of rank equal to

. Therefore,

.
∎

Notation 3.14

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
be natural numbers,
X
a smooth projective surface and
H

X
. Fix Chern classes

and

Proof.

First of all notice that according to Proposition
3.11
,

can be naturally identified with the family of extension classes of the form

where

is
H
-stable a rank

torsion free sheaf with
c_{1}(F)=c_{1}
and
c_{2}(F)=c_{2}
. We will assume that
n-k
and
c_{1}H
are coprime, in which case there exists a Poincaré sheaf

\mathcal{U}\to M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})\times X

such that for any

,

\mathcal{U}|_{\{t\}\times X}\cong F_{t}

with

a rank
n-k
,
H
-stable torsion free sheaf with
c_{1}(F)=c_{1}
and
c_{2}(F)=c_{2}
corresponding to

.
It is also possible to carry out the same construction using
only the local existence of a universal sheaf on
M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})
carrying out the constructions locally and showing the independence of the choice of the locally universal sheaf and conclude that the construction glue as a global algebraic object.

Consider the natural projections

\begin{array}[]{ccc}M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})\times X&\smash{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\pi}}&M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})\\
\quad\downarrow{p}&&\\
X.\end{array}

Set

where

is the right derived functor of

\mathcal{H}{om}_{\pi}(\mathcal{U},\cdot):=\pi_{*}\mathcal{H}{om}(\mathcal{U},\cdot).

Notice that for any

,

\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{U}|_{\{t\}\times X},\mathcal{O}_{X})=\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(F_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X})=h^{1}(F_{t}^{*}).

Since
F_{t}^{*}
is
H
-stable and

. On the other hand, if

, by stability we would have

which contradicts our assumption. Thus,
h^{1}(F_{t}^{*})=-\chi(F_{t}^{*})
and the function

is constant on

. Therefore, the sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is locally free of rank
p=-\chi(F_{t}^{*})
on
M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})
and compatible with base change. Finally, define the Gassmann bundle over

\mathcal{F}:=Gr(k,\mathcal{E}).

Notice that according to the above results, we have the diagramm

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{F}&\smash{\mathop{\to}\limits^{g}}&M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})\\
\quad\downarrow{f}&&\\
G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)\end{array}

with fibers

over

identified with a non-empty open subset of

and by Proposition

and Proposition

is canonically identified with

\begin{array}[]{r l}\dim G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)=&\dim\mathcal{F}\\
=&kp-k^{2}+\dim M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2}).\end{array}

The assumption
c_{2}\gg 0
, implies that the moduli space
M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})
is irreducible and has the expected dimension equal to

\dim M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})=2(n-k)c_{2}-(n-k-1)c_{1}^{2}-((n-k)^{2}-1)\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}).

By Riemman-Roch Theorem
p=-\chi(F_{t}^{*})=-(n-k)\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-\frac{c_{1}K}{2}-\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}+c_{2}

is irreducible with dimension

∎

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}{om}(\mathcal{U},p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
. First of all, notice that the map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})&\to&\mathbb{N}\\
t&\to&h^{2}(X\times\{t\},\mathcal{H}_{t})\end{array}

is upper semicontinuous on
M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})
. Let us denote by

n_{0}=\min_{t\in M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})}\{h^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{t})\}.

By semicontinuity, there exist a non empty open subset
M_{H}^{0}\subset M_{H}(n-k;c_{1},c_{2})
such that for any
t\in M_{H}^{0}
,
h^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{t})
is constant euqal to
n_{0}
. Moreover, by stability
h^{0}(\mathcal{H}_{t})=0
. Hence, over
M_{H}^{0}
,
\mathcal{E}=Ext^{1}_{\pi}(\mathcal{U},p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
is locally free of rank
p=-\chi(F_{t}^{*})+n_{0}\geq-\chi(F_{t}^{*})
. Now, arguing steep by steep as in the proof of Theorem
3.15
, we get
\dim G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
is irreducible and

\dim G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)\geq(2n-k)c_{2}+(1-n(n-k))\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-k^{2}-k(\frac{c_{1}K}{2}+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2})-(n-k-1)c_{1}^{2}.

∎

We will end the section with one example where the moduli space
G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},c_{2},k)
is generically smooth.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
and consider
n,k,c_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
with
n-k<c_{1}(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2})
. Then
G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},{c_{1}+1\choose 2},k)
is generically smooth.

Proof.

According to
[
He
]
; Corollaire 3.14, it is enought to see that for a generic point
(E,V)\in G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},{c_{1}+1\choose 2},k)
,

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}((E,V),(E,V))=0.

Recall that the moduli space
M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(n-k,c_{1},{c_{1}+1\choose 2})
is irreducible and Steiner bundles
F\in M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(n-k,c_{1},{c_{1}+1\choose 2})
define an open dense subset. Thus, according to Proposition
3.9
and Proposition
3.11
, we can assume that a generic
(E,V)\in G_{L}^{s}(n;c_{1},{c_{1}+1\choose 2},k)
defines an exact sequence

(3.14)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus k}\rightarrow E\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0

where
F
is a stable Steiner bundle, that is,
F
is given by the exact sequence

(3.15)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)^{\oplus c_{1}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus n-k+c_{1}}\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0.

In particular,
H^{1}(F)=0
and thus
H^{1}(E)=0
.

On the other hand, by
[
He
]
; Corollaire 1.6, we have the long exact sequence

\to\operatorname{Hom}(V,H^{1}(E))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}((E,V),(E,V))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)\to.

Since
H^{1}(E)=0
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(V,H^{1}(E))=0
. Hence, it only remains to prove that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0
which easily follows from the exact sequences (
3.14
) and (
3.15
).
∎

Quasi-bound layer breathing phonons inside perfect dislocations of lattice-relaxed twisted bilayers
V. V. Enaldiev

Abstract
Using multiscale modelling we investigate layer breathing phonons in MX 2 bilayers (M=Mo,W; X=S,Se) containing dislocations specific for lattice-relaxed moiré superlattices. Spatial modulation of interlayer distance, caused by the dislocations, generates effective potentials that promote emergence of one-dimensional quasi-bound bands of layer breathing modes inside perfect dislocations forming in the bilayers with parallel and antiparallel alignment of layers. We show that for parallel MX 2 bilayers perfect dislocations host several quasi-bound bands, with frequencies above layer breathing mode in rhombohedral-stacked domains, whereas for antiparallel bilayers only a single quasi-bound band arises near the edge dislocation type, having frequencies above the layer breathing mode in 2H-stacked domains.



Layer breathing phonons – relative out-of-plane oscillations of neighboring layers in van der Waals crystals – provide important information about details of interlayer coupling. For uniformly-stacked bilayers, like Bernal graphene
Mostaani et al. 2015
and hexagonal (2H)/rhombohedral (3R) transition metal dichalcogenide
Enaldiev et al. 2020
(TMD) bilayers, frequency of Brillouin zone center layer breathing phonon is solely determined by interlayer force parameter, characterized by curvature of interlayer adhesion
Mostaani et al. 2015
;
Enaldiev et al. 2020
at equilibrium distance, and reduced mass of layers’ unit cells. Although selection rules for the breathing mode makes them Raman active
Saito et al. 2016
;
Lin et al. 2017
, smallness of the interlayer force parameters, compared to intralayer, put the mode in far-infrared spectral range, more demanding for experiment
Lui et al. 2014
. Nevertheless, the layer breathing phonon modes have been identified in graphene
Lui and Heinz 2013
;
Lui et al. 2014
;
Lui et al. 2015
and TMD
Zhao et al. 2013
;
Chen et al. 2015
;
van Baren et al. 2019
;
Boora et al. 2024
multilayers with the help of Raman spectroscopy.

Moiré superlattice, forming at the interface of two rigidly rotated layers, creates mesoscale potentials for charge carriers, lattice vibrations etc. reducing spectra to miniband structure. For charge carriers in twisted graphene and TMD bilayers this leads to emergent superconductivity
Cao et al. 2018a
;
Yankowitz et al. 2019
;
Xia et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2025
and strongly correlated states
Cao et al. 2018b
;
Kennes et al. 2021
;
Foutty et al. 2023
;
Campbell et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2021
promoted by formation of flat minibands
Bistritzer and MacDonald 2011
;
Garcia-Ruiz et al. 2021
;
Naik et al. 2020
;
Magorrian et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2020
.

Spatial variation of stacking arrangement in moiré superlattice modifies lattice dynamics in twisted bilayers. For short-period moiré pattern, specific for large interlayer twist angle and/or large mismatch of layers’ lattice parameters, miniband folding of phonon spectra can be described in rigid lattice approximation
Cocemasov et al. 2013
;
Song et al. 2019
, whereas for long-period moiré superlattice lattice relaxation also should be taken into account
Koshino and Son 2019
;
Quan et al. 2021
;
Maity et al. 2022
.

For twisted TMD bilayers, relaxed moiré patterns differ for parallel (P) and antiparallel (AP) alignments of constituent monolayers. At small twist angles moiré superlattice in P twisted TMD bilayers relaxes into array of triangular 3R-stacked domains
Weston et al. 2020
;
Rosenberger et al. 2020
, separated by network of partial dislocations
Enaldiev et al. 2020
;
Carr et al. 2018
;
Engelke et al. 2023
. Ferroelectric polarization of the domains

;
Ferreira et al. 2021
provide a tool for manipulation of the dislocation network, f.e. allowing to merge pairs of partial dislocations into perfect dislocations
Enaldiev et al. 2022
;
Ko et al. 2023
;
Molino et al. 2023
. In small-angle twisted AP TMD bilayers relaxed moiré pattern consists of array of hexagonal 2H-stacked domains
Weston et al. 2020
;
Enaldiev et al. 2020
penetrated by network of perfect dislocations.

Phonons in P and AP twisted bilayers, actively studied theoretically
Koshino and Son 2019
;
Ochoa 2019
;
Maity et al. 2020
;
Samajdar et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2022
;
de Jong et al. 2022
;
Lu et al. 2022
;
Cappelluti et al. 2023
;
Girotto et al. 2023
and experimentally

;
de Jong et al. 2022
, are usually analyzed assuming translational and
C_{3}
-rotation symmetries of ideal moiré superlattices, allowing only screw-type dislocations for relaxed moiré pattens
Enaldiev et al. 2020

Molino et al. 2023
;
Liang et al. 2023

Engelke et al. 2023

In the Letter we focus on individual dislocations in the networks and demonstrate that layer breathing phonons can form quasi-bound one-dimensional (1D) bands inside perfect dislocations for P and AP TMD bilayers. In contrast to the in-plane interdomains phonons
Enaldiev 2025
emerging in a gap of bulk shear modes, the quasi-bound modes arise inside of 2D continuum of layer breathing phonons due to effective double barrier potential produced by perfect dislocations. Exemplifying the modeling for MoS
2
, we find that perfect dislocations give rise to a set of quasi-bound 1D bands for P bilayers, whereas in case of AP, perfect dislocations host the only quasi-bound band in vicinity of edge dislocation type. In addition, we find that although individual partial dislocations cannot bind layer breathing phonons, pairs of the aligned partials, residing close to each other, can generate similar double barrier potential as a single perfect dislocations in P bilayers, enabling formation of quasi-band bands.

To conclude, we demonstrated that perfect dislocations produce double barrier potentials for layer breathing phonons in P and AP TMD bilayers, resulting in formation of long-living 1D quasi-bound bands inside them, with frequencies above the layer breathing modes in rhombohedral and hexagonal domains, respectively. Moreover, orientation dependence of the 1D band characteristics opens up opportunity for strain-engineering
Hou et al. 2024
via fine tuning of the spectral features.

Although an individual partial dislocation does not host 1D quasi-bound bands, alignment of the pairs of pairtials close to each other, e.g. by external stress, would produce similar effective potentials as those for perfect dislocations, resulting in formation of the 1D quasi-bound bands in 2D continuum.

Finally, we note that the obtained results are not limited by MX
2
bilayers, but are qualitatively valid for graphene and P and AP hBN bilayers as well, as these materials possess similar structure of interlayer adhesion energy
Mostaani et al. 2015
;
Zhou et al. 2015
;
Szyniszewski et al. 2025
.

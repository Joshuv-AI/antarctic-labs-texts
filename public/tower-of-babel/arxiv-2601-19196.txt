Rotationally symmetric critical metrics for Laplace eigenvalues on tori in a conformal class
Egor Morozov

Abstract
. We study the problem of maximizing the first Laplace-Beltrami eigenvalue normalized by area in a conformal class on a torus. By a result of Nadirashvili, El Soufi, and Ilias, critical metrics for the k k -th normalized Laplace-Beltrami eigenvalue functional λ ¯ k \bar{\lambda}_{k} in a conformal class correspond to harmonic maps to spheres. In this paper we construct certain 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -equivariant harmonic maps 𝕋 2 → 𝕊 3 \mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{3} . For each non-rhombic conformal class on a torus, one of these maps corresponds to a rotationally symmetric critical metric for λ ¯ 1 \bar{\lambda}_{1} in this conformal class with the value of λ ¯ 1 \bar{\lambda}_{1} being greater than that of the flat metric. This refines a recent result by Karpukhin that answers a question by El Soufi, Ilias, and Ros. Also, we are able to show that if a rotationally invariant metric on a rectangular torus is maximal for λ ¯ 1 \bar{\lambda}_{1} in its conformal class, then it is 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -equivariant and coincides (up to a scalar factor) with the above metric. Finally, we show that a family of minimal tori in 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} called Otsuki tori fits naturally into our family. This gives an explicit parametrization of Otsuki tori in terms of elliptic integrals.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Critical metrics for the normalized Laplace-Beltrami eigenvalues on
surfaces

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian surface and

0=\lambda_{0}(M,g)<\lambda_{1}(M,g)\leqslant\lambda_{2}(M,g)\leqslant\ldots\leqslant\lambda_{k}(M,g)\leqslant\ldots\nearrow+\infty

be the Laplace-Beltrami eigenvalues of
(M,g)
. For each
k\geqslant 1
the

k
-th normalized eigenvalue functional
is defined by

\bar{\lambda}_{k}(M,g):=\lambda_{k}(M,g)\Area(M,g).

Consider the quantities

\Lambda_{k}(M)=\sup_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}(M)}\bar{\lambda}_{k}(M,g^{\prime}),\qquad\Lambda_{k}(M,[g])=\sup_{g^{\prime}\in[g]}\bar{\lambda}_{k}(M,g^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{R}(M)
is the set of all smooth Riemannian metrics on
M
and

[g]=\{e^{2\omega}g\mid\omega\in C^{\infty}(M)\}
is the conformal class of

g
. A metric
g\in\mathcal{R}(M)
is called
maximal
(respectively,

maximal in the conformal class
) for the functional
\bar{\lambda}_{k}
if

\bar{\lambda}_{k}(M,g)=\Lambda_{k}(M)
(respectively,

\bar{\lambda}_{k}(M,g)=\Lambda_{k}(M,[g])
). A weaker notion is that of a

critical
(aka
extremal
) metric introduced by
Nadirashvili
[
32
]
and refined by El Soufi and
Ilias
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. A metric
g\in\mathcal{R}(M)
is
called
critical
(respectively,
critical in the conformal
class
) for the functional
\bar{\lambda}_{k}
if for any analytic deformation

g_{t}\subset\mathcal{R}(M)
(respectively,
g_{t}\subset\mathcal{[}g]
),
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
such that
g_{0}=g
we have

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0+}\bar{\lambda}_{k}(M,g_{t})\cdot\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0-}\bar{\lambda}_{k}(M,g_{t})\leqslant 0.

In this paper we are interested in the case when
M=\mathbb{T}^{2}
is a torus.
For each
a,b\in\mathbb{R},b>0
let
g_{a,b}
denote the flat metric on

\mathbb{T}^{2}
induced by the factorization of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by the lattice

\Gamma_{a,b}:=\mathbb{Z}(1,0)+\mathbb{Z}(a,b)
. Recall that the moduli space of
conformal classes on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is given by
\{[g_{a,b}]\mid(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}\}
, where

\mathcal{M}:=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid 0\leqslant a\leqslant 1/2,b>0,a^{2}+b^{2}\geqslant 1\}.

In
[
10
]
the authors show that

\Lambda_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{b},\qquad(a,b)\in\mathcal{M},\quad a^{2}+b^{2}=1,

where the supremum is attained only on the flat metric. They also ask if the
flat metric is maximal for the functional
\bar{\lambda}_{1}
in other conformal
classes. In 2014, Petrides
[
40
, Theorem 1]
showed that

(1)

\Lambda_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])>8\pi,\qquad(a,b)\in\mathcal{M},

which implies that the flat metric
g_{a,b}
cannot be maximal in its
conformal class as soon as
b\geqslant\frac{\pi}{2}
. Finally, recently
Karpukhin
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that the metric
g_{a,b}
is not
maximal in its conformal class for all
(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
with

a^{2}+b^{2}>1
. The proof is based on the second variation formula for

\bar{\lambda}_{1}
applied to a certain perturbation of the flat metric. In
particular, the method does not give an explicit example of a critical metric in
the conformal class with a larger value of
\bar{\lambda}_{1}
than that of the
flat metric. Our first main result is an explicit construction of such a
critical metric.

Theorem 1
.

For any
(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
such that
a^{2}+b^{2}>1
there exists a
rotationally symmetric critical metric
g_{a,b}^{1,1,0}=\rho(y)g_{a,b}
for

\bar{\lambda}_{1}
in the conformal class
[g_{a,b}]
such that

\bar{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},g_{a,b}^{1,1,0})>\max\Bigl\{\frac{4\pi^{2}}{b},8\pi\Bigr\}.

Our second main result concerns maximal metrics in the rectangular conformal
classes.

Theorem 2
.

Let
b>1
and
g=\rho(x,y)g_{0,b}
, where
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
\Gamma_{0,b}
-periodic positive smooth function that does not depend
either on
x
or on
y
. If
g
is maximal for the functional

\bar{\lambda}_{1}
in the conformal class
[g_{0,b}]
, then

g=cg_{0,b}^{1,1,0}
for some constant
c>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

As seen from Theorem
2
, by a “rotationally symmetric
metric” in the conformal class
[g_{a,b}],(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
we mean a
metric of the form
\rho(bx-ay,y)g_{a,b}
, where
\rho
is a smooth positive
function independent of one of its arguments. Note that this is not equivalent
to being invariant under an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
action by conformal diffeomorphisms.
The metrics with the latter property form a larger class.

It is reasonable to expect for a maximal metric to have a rotational symmetry.
Since by Theorem
1
the metric
g_{0,b}^{1,1,0}
is
better than the flat one and is consistent with Petrides
inequality (
1
), we have the following natural conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For each
(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
such that
a^{2}+b^{2}>1
the metric

g_{a,b}^{1,1,0}
is maximal for the functional
\bar{\lambda}_{1}
in the
conformal class
[g_{a,b}]
.

Cf.
[
21
, Conjecture 1]
for the case of first normalized Steklov
eigenvalue in the conformal class. See also
[
19
]
for the
state-of-the-art upper bound on
\Lambda_{1}(M,[g_{a,b}])
.

In
[
20
]
, the authors provide a method to compute numerically the
values of
\Lambda_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])
together with the corresponding
conformal factor. According to the paper, the optimal conformal factor is
rotationally symmetric but with a different direction of rotation (namely, the
conformal factors there have the form
\hat{\rho}(bx-ay)
for some positive
function
\hat{\rho}
). However, Professor Osting and Professor Chiu-Yen Kao
confirmed (private communication) that the code used in the paper required a
minor correction, and the true direction of rotation is the same as for the
metric
g_{a,b}^{1,1,0}
. Also, for few test conformal classes considered
in
[
20
]
, the value of
\Lambda_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])
computed
numerically coincides (up to a numerical error) with
\bar{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},g_{a,b}^{1,1,0})
computed numerically using (
14
)
below. Given all this, we may conclude that Conjecture
1.1

has a solid numerical evidence.

Keeping Conjecture
1.1
in mind, it is interesting to study
the dependence of
\bar{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},g_{a,b}^{1,1,0})
on

(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
. We are able to prove

Theorem 3
.

For
(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
,
\bar{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2},g_{a,b}^{1,1,0})
is an
increasing function in
a
and a decreasing function in
b
.

Recall from
[
32
]
that the flat metric on the equilateral torus is a
unique global maximizer for
\bar{\lambda}_{1}
. This fact is natural in light of
Conjecture
1.1
and Theorem
3
, since the
conformal class of the equilateral torus is precisely the one with maximal
a

and minimal
b
.

The metric
g_{a,b}^{1,1,0}
is just one metric from a large family of metrics

g_{a,b}^{p,q,r}
, where
p,q,r
are integers satisfying certain
inequalities. To construct these metrics we utilize a well-known connection
between critical metrics in a conformal class and harmonic maps to spheres.
Recall that for a Riemannian surface
(M,g)
the
Weyl’s counting
function
is defined by

N(\lambda):=\#\{i\mid\lambda_{i}(M,g)<\lambda\}.

Theorem 4

(
[
13
, Theorems 3.1(ii) and 4.1(ii)]
)
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
,
M
be a smooth surface, and
\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the unit
sphere with the standard round metric
g_{0}
.

(1)

Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a minimal map. Then the metric
f^{*}g_{0}

is critical for the functional
\bar{\lambda}_{N(2)}
on
M
.

(2)

Let
f\colon(M,g)\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a harmonic map. Then the metric

\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}f|_{g}^{2}g
is critical in the conformal class
[g]
for the
functional
\bar{\lambda}_{N(2)}
on
M
.

In the setting of part
(2)
, the number
N(2)
for the
metric
\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}f|_{g}^{2}g
is also called the
spectral index
of

f
and denoted by
\mathrm{ind}_{S}f
(see
[
25
, Definition 2.7]
).
See also
[
36
,
39
]
for more details on the connection between
critical metrics and minimal and harmonic maps and related topics.

Part
(1)
of Theorem
4
was
successfully used many times to construct critical (and even maximal) metrics
explicitly on torus
[
32
,
27
,
35
,
37
,
38
,
24
]
,
Klein bottle
[
27
,
35
,
18
,
9
,
4
]
, and orientable
surface of genus two
[
17
,
33
]
. In this paper we use
part
(2)
of Theorem
4
to construct
certain critical metrics
in the conformal class
on torus. More precisely,
each metric
g_{a,b}^{p,q,r}
comes from a certain explicitly constructed

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-invariant harmonic map
u_{a,b}^{p,q,r}
from
(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])
to
\mathbb{S}^{3}=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\colon|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}=1\}
, where the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
actions on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and

(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])
are given by

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=(z_{1},e^{2\pi i\alpha}z_{2}),

\displaystyle(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{3},

(3)

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot(x,y)

\displaystyle=(x+\alpha,y),

\displaystyle(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Gamma_{a,b}\cong(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}]),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{S}^{1}.

Note that the action (
2
) is the same that is used in the
Hsiang-Lawson construction of
Otsuki tori
, a family of
\mathbb{S}^{1}

invariant
minimal
tori in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
first considered by
Otsuki
[
34
]
. The initial construction was simplified by Hsiang and
Lawson
[
16
]
. Later, Penskoi
[
37
]
and
Karpukhin
[
24
]
used this family to construct critical metrics for some
of the functionals
\bar{\lambda}_{k}
on tori. We show in sec.
6

that Otsuki tori fit naturally into our family. In particular, this gives an
explicit parametrization for Otsuki tori by functions that are expressed in
terms of elliptic integrals.

Unfortunately, we are not able to compute the spectral index for all the maps

u_{a,b}^{p,q,r}
(in fact, we doubt if an answer in a closed form is possible
in the general case). However, we are able to give a lower bound together with
sufficient conditions for this bound to attain. In particular, we are able to
show that
\mathrm{inf}_{S}u_{a,b}^{1,1,0}=1
, which is enough for
Theorem
1
.

Proceed to the definition of the maps

Theorem 5
.

The map
\tilde{u}_{a,b}^{p,q,r}
descends to a harmonic map

u_{a,b}^{p,q,r}\colon(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
with energy

The number

satisfies

Proposition 4.3
.

We have
\lambda_{2p-1}(0)=\lambda_{2p}(0)=2
if
\frac{p}{q}\neq\frac{1}{2}

\lambda_{2p-1}(0)<\lambda_{2p}(0)=2

Proof.

We start with the nonlimit case
\frac{p}{q}\neq\frac{1}{2}
. Recall that the
components of the map

are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian

\cos\varphi\cos\theta
and

\cos\varphi\sin\theta
solve the problem (
42
,
43
)
with

and
\lambda=2
. It is easy to see that both functions have

2p
zeroes on
[0,b)

Proposition
4.2
(i)
we have

\lambda_{2p-1}(0)=\lambda_{2p}(0)=2
.

The limit case

is more subtle. The problem is that
instead of two eigenfunctions
\cos\varphi\cos\theta
and
\cos\varphi\sin\theta

we now have only one eigenfunction
\cos\varphi
. Since this function has exactly

2p
zeroes on
[0,b)

4.2
(i)

we have either

, sec. 3.3]

Consider the following antiperiodic Sturm-Liouville problem

-h^{\prime\prime}(y)=\lambda\rho(y)h(y),\qquad h(y+2b_{0})\equiv-h(y),

(x,y)\in\mathbb{(}\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])
. A lengthy
but straightforward computation shows that in this basis the operator

J_{u,g_{a,b}}
has the form

J_{u,g_{a,b}}V=\Delta_{0}V+4\pi A\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}\partial_{x}V+\frac{4\pi}{b}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&Bp\\
0&0&A(r+a)\\
-Bp&-A(r+a)&0\end{pmatrix}\partial_{y}V,

where
\Delta_{0}=-(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})\mathbf{1}
is the flat Laplacian
applied to the components of
V\in\Gamma(u^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{3})
. Since

J_{u,g_{a,b}}
has constant coefficients, we can split the variables. If

V=\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{k,l}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{b}(lbx+(k-la)y)},\qquad V_{k,l}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}

is the Fourier expansion of
V
, then

J_{u,g_{a,b}}^{\mathbb{C}}V=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{b^{2}}\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}(J_{u,g_{a,b}}^{k,l}V_{k,l})e^{\frac{2\pi i}{b}(lbx+(k-la)y)},

where

J_{u,g_{a,b}}^{k,l}=(l^{2}b^{2}+(k-la)^{2})\mathbf{1}+2ilb^{2}A\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}+2i(k-la)\begin{pmatrix}0&0&Bp\\
0&0&A(r+a)\\
-Bp&-A(r+a)&0\end{pmatrix},\quad k,l\in\mathbb{Z}.

We have

\det J_{u,g_{a,b}}^{k,l}=(l^{2}b^{2}+(k-la)^{2})((l^{2}b^{2}+(k-la)^{2})^{2}-4A^{2}(lb^{2}-(k-la)(r+a))^{2}-4B^{2}p^{2}(k-la)^{2}).

Substituting
k=\pm q,l=0
and using (
18
),

A^{2}=\frac{4p^{2}-q^{2}}{4b^{2}},A^{2}+B^{2}=1
, we obtain
\det J_{u,g_{a,b}}^{\pm q,0}=0
. The corresponding eigenspace of
J_{u,g_{a,b}}
is spanned by

V_{1},V_{2}
, where

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=2Bp\cos\frac{2\pi q}{b}y\,\nu_{1}+2A(r+a)\cos\frac{2\pi q}{b}y\,\nu_{2}-q\sin\frac{2\pi q}{b}y\,\nu_{3},

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=2Bp\sin\frac{2\pi q}{b}y\,\nu_{1}+2A(r+a)\sin\frac{2\pi q}{b}y\,\nu_{2}+q\cos\frac{2\pi q}{b}y\,\nu_{3}.

It remains to show that, for example,
V_{1}
is not integrable. Assume the
converse and let
(u_{t})_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
be the corresponding
harmonic variation of
u=u_{0}
. Put
\lambda:=\frac{2\pi}{b}p=\frac{2\pi}{b}\sqrt{(r+a)^{2}+b^{2}}
. Since any harmonic map
(\mathbb{T}^{2},g_{a,b})\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
is given by eigenfunctions of the Laplacian and
particularly the map
u
is given by
\lambda
-eigenfunctions, the map

u_{t}
is also given by
\lambda
-eigenfunctions for all small
t
. In
other words, we may assume that

\tilde{u}_{t}(x,y)=\left(\sum A_{k,l}(t)e^{\frac{2\pi i}{b}(lbx+(k-la)y)},\sum B_{k,l}(t)e^{\frac{2\pi i}{b}(lbx+(k-la)y)}\right),\qquad A_{k,l},B_{k,l}\colon(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{C},

where, as usual,
\tilde{u}_{t}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
is the lift of

u_{t}
, and the sums are taken over all
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

(53)

\frac{4\pi^{2}}{b^{2}}(l^{2}b^{2}+(k-la)^{2})=\lambda^{2}.

We have

2Bp\cos\frac{2\pi q}{b}y=\langle V_{1},\nu_{1}\rangle=\left\langle\left.\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0},\nu_{1}\right\rangle=\sum\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits(-iA_{k,l}^{\prime}(0)e^{\frac{2\pi i}{b}(lbx+(k-la-p)y)}).

By uniqueness of the (real) Fourier expansion, the only nonzero summands in the
RHS are those with
l=0,k=p\pm q
, and then (
53
) implies that

q=2p
. A similar argument with
\nu_{2}
in place of
\nu_{1}
shows that

q=2|r+a|
. However, we cannot have both equalities at the same time because
of (
18
). This contradiction concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.1
.

The existence of nonintegrable Jacobi fields is quite a rare property. For
example, it is proved in
[
31
]
that the torus
u_{0,1}^{1,0;\varphi_{0}}

admits a nonintegrable Jacobi field if and only if

\varphi_{0}\in\{0,\frac{\pi}{6},\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{2}\}
.

9.
Discussion

9.1.
Why Conjecture
1.1
is difficult?

Once one has a candidate for a maximal metric for
\bar{\lambda}_{1}
(either
globally or in the conformal class), a standard tool to prove that it is an
actual maximizer is the so-called
Hersch trick
. It was introduced
in
[
15
]
and then successfully applied in many similar situations. The
idea is the following. Consider the family of conformal automorphisms of

\mathbb{S}^{3}
given by

G_{\gamma}(z)=\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{|z+\gamma|^{2}}(z+\gamma)+\gamma,\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{B}^{4},\quad z\in\mathbb{S}^{3},

where
\mathbb{B}^{4}=\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\colon|\gamma|\leqslant 1\}
. Let

(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
and
u\colon(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
be
a smooth map satisfying

(54)

E(G_{\gamma}\circ u)\leqslant E(u),\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{B}^{4}.

Then it follows from the variational characterization of
\lambda_{1}
and a
standard topological argument that

\Lambda_{1}(M,[g_{a,b}])\leqslant E(u).

In particular, if
u
is harmonic, then

\Lambda_{1}(M,[g_{a,b}])\leqslant\bar{\lambda}_{1}(M,\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}u|_{g_{a,b}}^{2}g_{a,b}).

Therefore, a natural attempt to prove Conjecture
1.1
would
be to put
u:=u_{a,b}^{1,1,0}
in (
54
). Unfortunately, it turns
out that in this case (
54
) fails at least for some conformal
classes
(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}
(probably, it fails for all of them). Indeed, let

(a,b)\to(a_{0},b_{0})
, where
a_{0}^{2}+b_{0}^{2}=1
. Then by (
52
) we
have

u_{a,b}^{1,1,0}\to u_{a_{0},b_{0}}^{1,0;\varphi_{0}},\quad\text{where}\quad\varphi_{0}=\arccos\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2b_{0}}.

Let
\gamma_{0}:=(1,0,0,0)
. Using
[
29
, (1.7)]
, we obtain for

u:=u_{a_{0},b_{0}}^{1,0;\varphi_{0}}

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\right|_{t=0}E(G_{t\gamma_{0}}\circ u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(3\langle u,\gamma_{0}\rangle^{2}-1)|\mathrm{d}u|_{g_{a,b}}^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{g_{a,b}}=\\
\frac{4\pi^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{b_{0}}\left(\frac{9}{4b_{0}^{2}}\cos^{2}\frac{2\pi}{b_{0}}y-1\right)\mathrm{d}y=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{b_{0}^{3}}\left(\frac{9}{8}-b_{0}^{2}\right)>0.

This means that (
54
) fails for

u=u_{a,b}^{1,1,0},\gamma=t\gamma_{0}
,
t
sufficiently small, and
(a,b)

sufficiently close to
(a_{0},b_{0})
. Note that in
[
10
]
, the authors
use (
54
) with
u=u_{a_{0},b_{0}}^{1,0,\psi_{0}}
, where

\psi_{0}=\arccos\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2\sqrt{2}}\neq\varphi_{0}
, to prove that the flat
metric is maximal in any rhombic conformal class
(a_{0},b_{0})\in\mathcal{M},a_{0}^{2}+b_{0}^{2}=1
.

One may hope to generalize Hersch trick by constructing a four dimensional
family of
energy decreasing variations
of
u_{a,b}^{1,1,0}
, i.e. such
that the energy of each map of this family does not exceed the energy of

u_{a,b}^{1,1,0}
(cf.
[
23
]
). However, we think that it would be a
challenging task. Recall that for a harmonic map
u\colon(\mathbb{T}^{2},g)\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
its
energy index

\Ind_{E}u
(respectively,

energy nullity

\Nul_{E}u
) is the number of negative (respectively,
zero) eigenvalues of the corresponding Jacobi stability operator
J_{u,g}
.
Clearly, any family of variations as above induces a four dimensional subspace

U\subset\Gamma(u^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{3})
such that
J_{u,g}|_{U}\leqslant 0
. However,
according to our numerical experiments in Wolfram Mathematica, it seems that

\Ind_{E}(u_{a,b}^{1,1,0})=3
and
\Nul_{E}(u_{a,b}^{1,1,0})=7
(in fact, we
have
\Ind_{E}(u_{a,b}^{1,1,0})\leqslant 4N(2)=4
by
[
25
, Proposition 1.6]

anyway). Six Jacobi fields come from the rotations of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
(these
fields are not really useful) and the remaining field comes from the conformal
automorphisms of
(\mathbb{T}^{2},[g_{a,b}])
given by
y
-shifts. Therefore,
we have very few fields contributing to the energy index, and it seems that they
do not have any clear geometrical meaning. This makes difficult even the
construction of the space
U
, let alone a four dimensional family of energy
decreasing variations.

Finally, note that the analogs of (
54
) as well as the absence of
nontrivial Jacobi fields both play an essential role in establishing the
quantitative stability of maximizers in a conformal class, see
[
22
]
.
In particular, the above discussion shows that if
Conjecture
1.1
is true, then the methods
of
[
22
]
cannot be applied to prove the quantitative stability of the
corresponding maximizer (however, it is possible that this stability can be
treated by other methods).

9.2.
Directions for the future research

It would be interesting to establish an analogue of Theorem
6

for Klein bottles. Another possibility is to replace the
action (
2
) by

\alpha\cdot(z_{1},z_{2})=(e^{2\pi ik\alpha}z_{1},e^{2\pi il\alpha}z_{2}),\qquad(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{3},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{S}^{1}

for some positive relatively prime integers
k,l
. The corresponding
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-equivariant
minimal
tori and Klein bottles are called
Lawson

\tau
-surfaces

[
28
,
16
,
35
]
. Note that, unlike Otsuki tori,
the Lawson
\tau
-surfaces are given by elementary functions. It is reasonable
to expect that the harmonic analogues of Lawson
\tau
-surfaces (if they
exist) are given by elliptic integrals. The case of higher-dimensional spheres
is of interest as well (see
[
38
]
for the case of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-equivariant minimal tori in
\mathbb{S}^{5}
). Finally, it would be
interesting to compare Theorem
6
with the classification of
harmonic
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-equivariant tori in terms of spectral
data
[
2
]
.

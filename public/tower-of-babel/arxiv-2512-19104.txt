Explicit and Non-asymptotic Query Complexities of Rank-Based Zeroth-order Algorithm on Stochastic Smooth Functions
Haishan Ye

Abstract
Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization with ordinal feedback has emerged as a fundamental problem in modern machine learning systems, particularly in human-in-the-loop settings such as reinforcement learning from human feedback, preference learning, and evolutionary strategies. While rank-based ZO algorithms enjoy strong empirical success and robustness properties, their theoretical understanding, especially under stochastic objectives and standard smoothness assumptions, remains limited. In this paper, we study rank-based zeroth-order optimization for stochastic functions where only ordinal feedback of the stochastic function is available. We propose a simple and computationally efficient rank-based ZO algorithm. Under standard assumptions including smoothness, strong convexity, and bounded second moments of stochastic gradients, we establish explicit non-asymptotic query complexity bounds for both convex and nonconvex objectives. Notably, our results match the best-known query complexities of value-based ZO algorithms, demonstrating that ordinal information alone is sufficient for optimal query efficiency in stochastic settings. Our analysis departs from existing drift-based and information-geometric techniques, offering new tools for the study of rank-based optimization under noise. These findings narrow the gap between theory and practice and provide a principled foundation for optimization driven by human preferences.



1
Introduction

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization, the task of minimizing functions when gradient information is unavailable, has become increasingly important across machine learning
(
Loshchilov and Hutter 2016
)
, reinforcement learning
(
Igel 2003
)
, and human-in-the-loop decision systems
(
Tang et al.
)
.
Traditional ZO methods rely on numerical function values to approximate descent directions, but many modern applications provide only ordinal feedback rather than scalar outputs. Examples include evolutionary strategies
(
Hansen and Ostermeier 2001
)
, comparative preference elicitation
(
Yue and Joachims 2009
)
, online ranking platforms
(
Dwork et al. 2001
)
, and, notably, human evaluation of AI outputs
(
Bai et al. 2022
)
. These settings challenge classical optimization frameworks and motivate the design of algorithms that can optimize via non-numeric feedback.

A particularly striking instantiation arises in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF): humans provide relative rankings instead of explicit scores when judging outputs, reducing cognitive burden and enabling preference modeling at scale
(
Ouyang et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2023
;
Bai et al. 2022
)
. RLHF has powered improvements in aligning large language and generative models with human intentions, but also exposes an underlying optimization challenge: how to efficiently optimize a black-box objective when only ranking or preference oracles are available to evaluate candidate solutions.

Rank-based optimization sits at the center of this landscape. Algorithms that exploit only order information, such as ranking or comparison oracles, are attractive for their robustness to noise and invariant behavior under monotonic transformations of the underlying objective.
This robustness is one reason why rank-based strategies underlie successful heuristics like CMA-ES
(
Hansen and Ostermeier 2001
)
and natural evolution strategies
(
Wierstra et al. 2014
)
in evolutionary computation.
Yet, despite their empirical success, theoretical foundations for rank-centric ZO methods have been incomplete: existing convergence analyses typically focus on asymptotic behavior or narrow models of evolutionary heuristics, offering limited insight into finite-sample efficiency.

The recent work by
Tang et al.
addresses optimizing using ranking oracles, which well-aligns with human preference data and other ordinal signals.
They propose ZO-RankSGD, a novel zeroth-order algorithm driven by a rank-based random estimator that produces descent directions from ordinal feedback alone, and they prove its convergence to stationary points.
However, ZO-RankSGD requires constructing a directed acyclic graph of
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
edges where
N
is the sample size.
This makes the construction of descent direct computation inefficient.
Though
Tang et al.
show ZO-RankSGD can find an
\varepsilon
-stationary point at cost at most
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)
, their analysis does
not
based on the standard assumptions of stochastic gradient descent, such as the stochastic gradient
\nabla f(\bm{x};\;\xi)
being of bounded second moment.
Another weakness is that their convergence analysis can
not
be used to obtain the convergence rate when the objective function is smooth and convex.

In parallel, a substantial body of work in online bandit optimization studies ZO algorithms with comparison oracles, which can be viewed as a special case of rank-based feedback
(
Yue and Joachims 2009
;
Kumagai 2017
;
Zhu et al. 2023
)
. A key limitation of these approaches is that their analyses are intrinsically tied to pairwise comparisons and do not extend to richer ordinal information such as full rankings, which are particularly relevant in human-in-the-loop scenarios. Furthermore, these works typically assume convex or strongly convex objectives, limiting their applicability to the nonconvex settings prevalent in modern machine learning.

Complementing these lines of research,
Ye 2025
investigates rank-based ZO algorithms that operate on the order statistics of sampled directions and derive the first explicit non-asymptotic query complexity bounds for a class of rank-based updates under smoothness assumptions. Their results demonstrate that, with carefully designed sampling and selection mechanisms, rank-only methods can approach the efficiency of value-based ZO algorithms in terms of query complexity. However, their analysis assumes access to deterministic function values
f(\mathbf{x})
. In contrast, our work focuses on stochastic optimization settings where only noisy evaluations
f(\mathbf{x};\xi)
are available, with
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[f(\mathbf{x};\xi)]=f(\mathbf{x})
. Unfortunately, the techniques developed in
Ye 2025
do not readily extend to this stochastic regime.

Despite the recent progress outlined above, rank-based zeroth-order optimization remains far from being fully understood, and many fundamental questions are still open. From a theoretical standpoint, a central challenge lies in developing unified convergence analyses that accommodate both rich ordinal feedback (beyond pairwise comparisons) and stochastic objective evaluations under standard assumptions such as smoothness and bounded variance. Existing works typically address only isolated aspects of this problem—either deterministic objectives with full rankings, or stochastic objectives with limited comparison oracles—leaving a substantial gap between theory and practice.

Furthermore, from a broader perspective, it is still unclear what information is fundamentally necessary and sufficient for efficient zeroth-order optimization. Ranking feedback occupies an intermediate position between full value queries and binary comparisons, yet the precise trade-offs between feedback richness, query complexity, and convergence rates are not well characterized. Clarifying these trade-offs is essential for principled algorithm design and for understanding the limits of optimization driven by human feedback.

Addressing the above challenges is critical for advancing both the theory and practice of optimization with ordinal information.
This paper tries to solve these open problems and summarize our contribution as follows:

•

We propose a simple rank-based ZO algorithm described in Algorithm
1
.
Compared with ZO-RankSGD
(
Tang et al.
)
which requires to construct a directed acyclic graph of
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
edges and takes
\mathcal{O}(dN^{2})
computation cost for each iteration,
our algorithm does
not
need any graph and takes

To the best of the author’s knowledge, these query complexities for rank-based ZO over stochastic functions are first proved.

The obtained explicit query complexities are the same to the query complexities of valued-based ZO algorithms

This proves that for the ZO over stochastic functions, the ordinal information is sufficient to achieve the same query efficiency as the value-based ZO algorithms.

and information-geometric optimization
(

The technique in this paper is also not an easy extension of the one in

First, we will introduce the notation of

Next, we introduce the set

\mathcal{K}:=\{1,\dots,N/4,3N/4+1,\dots,N\}

is the sample size for each iteration.

Next, we will introduce the objective function, which we aim to solve in this paper

\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\bm{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[f(\bm{x};\;\xi)\right]

(2.1)

where
f(\bm{x})
and
f(\bm{x};\;\xi)
are
L
-smooth functions.
We will also introduce several widely used assumptions about the objective function.

Assumption 1
.

A function

is called
L
-smooth with

if for any
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

|f(\bm{y})-f(\bm{x})-\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\rangle|\leq\frac{L}{2}\left\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\|^{2}.

(2.2)

Assumption 2
.

A function
f(\bm{x})
is called
\mu
-strongly convex with
\mu>0
if for any
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that

f(\bm{y})-f(\bm{x})-\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{x}),\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\rangle\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}\right\|^{2}.

(2.3)

The
L
-smooth and
\mu
-strongly convex assumptions are standard in the convergence analysis of optimization
(
Nesterov 2013
)
.

Next, we will introduce the assumptions with respect to the stochastic gradient
\nabla f(\bm{x};\;\xi)
.

Assumption 3

(Bounded Second Moment)
.

There exists a constant
G>0
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\bm{x};\;\xi)\right\|^{2}\right]\leq G_{u}^{2}\mbox{ for all }\bm{x}.

(2.4)

Assumption 4
.

We also assume that
\left\|\nabla f(\bm{x}\;\xi)\right\|\geq G_{\ell}
with
G_{\ell}>0
for all
\bm{x}
.

Assumption
3
is a popular assumption in the convergence analysis of stochastic gradient descent
(
Nemirovski et al. 2009
;
Moulines and Bach 2011
)
.
Assumption
4
is also reasonable because the stochastic gradient
\nabla f(\bm{x};\;\xi)
never vanish even
\bm{x}_{t}
tends to the optimal point
(
Bottou et al. 2018
;
Moulines and Bach 2011
)
.
Furthermore, Assumption
4
is only used to set the smooth parameter of Algorithm
1
, which does not affect the query complexity of our algorithm.

Algorithm Description

We generate
N
random Gaussian vectors
\bm{u}_{i}
with
i=1,\dots,N
, that is,
\bm{u}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{I}_{d})
.
Accordingly, we can get
N
points

’s.
Then, we send these points to the rank oracle, that is,
\mathrm{Rank\_Oracle}(\bm{x}_{t}+\alpha\bm{u}_{1},\dots,\bm{x}_{t}+\alpha\bm{u}_{N})
.
Then we obtain an index
(1),\dots,(N)
such that

f(\bm{x}_{t}+\alpha\bm{u}_{(1)};\;\xi_{t})\leq f(\bm{x}_{t}+\alpha\bm{u}_{(2)};\;\xi_{t})\leq\dots\leq\dots f(\bm{x}_{t}+\alpha\bm{u}_{(N)};\;\xi_{t}).

(3.1)

For
1\leq k\leq N/4
, these points
\bm{x}_{t}+\alpha\bm{u}_{(k)}
achieve the
\frac{N}{4}
smallest values.
We believe that these
\bm{u}_{(k)}
are probably descent directions.
Thus, we give positive weights on these directions.
Accordingly, we set
w_{(k)}^{+}>0
with
k=1,\dots,N/4
and
\sum w_{(k)}^{+}=1
.
On the other hand, for

,
these points
\bm{x}_{t}+\alpha\bm{u}_{(k)}
achieve the
\frac{N}{4}
largest values.
We believe that these
\bm{u}_{(k)}
are probably ascent directions.
Thus, we want to give negative weights on these directions.
Accordingly, we set

with

and
\sum w_{(k)}^{-}=-1
.
In this paper, we set

and
w_{(k)}^{-}=-\frac{4}{N}
.
This weight strategy is also very popular

)
.

After obtaining these weights

(3.2)

Finally, we will update

\bm{x}_{t+1}=\bm{x}_{t}+\eta_{t}\bm{d}_{t}.

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{\top}f(\bm{x}_{t})\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{Z}_{\xi_{t}}^{\perp}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\bm{b}_{(k)}^{t}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{N}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{\top}f(\bm{x}_{t})\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{Z}_{\xi_{t}}^{\perp}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]\right\|^{2}\right).

By Lemma

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{\top}f(\bm{x}_{t})\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{Z}_{\xi_{t}}^{\perp}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\bm{b}_{(k)}^{t}\right|

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}\cdot\frac{N}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla^{\top}f(\bm{x}_{t})\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{Z}_{\xi_{t}}^{\perp}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ex}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\sqrt{4NG_{u}^{2}\log\frac{2}{\delta}\cdot\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}\right]}.

∎

Remark 1
.

Our convergence analysis requires Assumption
4
, that is,
\left\|\nabla f(\bm{x};\;,\xi)\right\|\geq G_{\ell}

This assumption is not common in the convergence analysis of stochastic gradient descent.
This assumption is only used to choose the smooth parameter

Thanks to the well-chosen

Though Assumption

Moulines and Bach 2011

provides the convergence rate of Algorithm

when

is both

-smooth and
\mu
-strongly convex.
Theorem

provides the convergence rate when

is only

-smooth.

f(\bm{x}_{T})-f(\bm{x}^{*})\leq\varepsilon
.
This query complexity is the same as that of the value-based ZO algorithm
(
Wang et al.
)
.

If the objective function may be nonconvex, to find an
\varepsilon
-stationary point, that is
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon
, the query complexity of our algorithm is
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{dLG_{u}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)
.
This complexity is the same as that of the valued-based ZO algorithm
(
Ghadimi and Lan 2013
)
.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, these query complexities are first obtained for rank-based ZO algorithms over stochastic functions.

6
Conclusion

This paper studied rank-based zeroth-order optimization for stochastic objectives, motivated by machine learning, reinforcement learning, and learning systems that rely on ordinal feedback such as human preferences. We proposed a simple and computationally efficient rank-based ZO algorithm that operates purely on ranking information and avoids the quadratic-time graph constructions required by prior work.

Under standard assumptions including smoothness, strong convexity, and bounded second moments of stochastic gradients, we established explicit non-asymptotic query complexity bounds for both convex and nonconvex settings. These bounds match those of value-based zeroth-order methods, demonstrating that ordinal feedback alone is sufficient to achieve optimal query efficiency in stochastic optimization. Our analysis departs from existing drift-based and information-geometric techniques and provides tools that may extend to other rank-based algorithms.

Restriction coefficients for partitions with at most three columns
Mitchell Lee

Abstract
. Let λ \lambda and μ \mu be partitions such that λ 1 ≤ 3 \lambda_{1}\leq 3 , and let r ≥ 0 r\geq 0 . We present a combinatorial interpretation of the plethysm coefficient ⟨ s λ , s μ ​ [ s r ] ⟩ \langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[s_{r}]\rangle . As a consequence, we solve the restriction problem for partitions with at most three columns. That is, for all partitions λ \lambda with λ 1 ≤ 3 \lambda_{1}\leq 3 , we find a combinatorial interpretation for the multiplicities of the irreducible 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -submodules of the Schur module 𝕊 λ ​ ℂ n \mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n} , considered as an 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -module.

1.
Introduction

For all partitions
\lambda
, let
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\colon\text{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\text{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the corresponding
Schur functor
, where
\text{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the category of vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}
. Recall that for all positive integers
n
, we have that
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a (finite-dimensional) polynomial
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module. If
\lambda
has at most
n
parts, then
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
is irreducible, and otherwise
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}=0
. All irreducible polynomial
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-modules arise in this way
[
3
, Section 6.1]
.

The composition of two Schur functors decomposes into a direct sum of Schur functors. That is, there exist nonnegative integers
p^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}\geq 0
, indexed by triples of partitions, such that for all
\mu,\nu
, there is a natural (in the vector space
V
) isomorphism

\mathbb{S}^{\mu}(\mathbb{S}^{\nu}V)=\bigoplus_{\lambda}(\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}V)^{\oplus p^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}}.

In what follows, we will use the notation
\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[s_{\nu}]\rangle
instead of
p^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}
. Here,
s_{\lambda}
,
s_{\mu}
, and
s_{\nu}
denote Schur symmetric functions,
\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle
denotes the Hall inner product, and
f[g]
denotes the
plethysm
of the symmetric functions
f
and
g
, which was defined by Littlewood in 1936
[
8
]
. The numbers
\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[s_{\nu}]\rangle
are called
plethysm coefficients
. Plethysm coefficients have several known representation-theoretic interpretations, but finding a combinatorial interpretation of
\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[s_{\nu}]\rangle
remains a central open problem in algebraic combinatorics
[
1
,
9
]
.

We present a combinatorial interpretation for
\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[s_{\nu}]\rangle
in the case that
\lambda
has at most three columns and
\nu
has at most one row. In this case,
s_{\nu}=h_{r}
is a complete homogeneous symmetric function for some
r\geq 0
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions with
\lambda_{1}\leq 3
. For all
r\geq 4
, we have the following.

(1)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[h_{0}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\mu$ has at most one row and $\lambda=\emptyset$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(2)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[h_{1}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\lambda=\mu$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(3)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[h_{2}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\ell(\mu)\leq 3$ and $\lambda=(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},\mu_{1}+\mu_{3},\mu_{2}+\mu_{3})^{T}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(4)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[h_{3}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\mu_{1}\leq 1$ and $\lambda=(\mu_{1}^{T},\mu_{1}^{T},\mu_{1}^{T})^{T}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(5)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[h_{r}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\mu=\lambda=\emptyset$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Theorem

1
allows us to solve a special case of the important
restriction problem
, which we now review. Let
\lambda
be a partition. Because
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a subgroup of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
via permutation matrices, we may restrict the Schur module
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and then write the result as a direct sum of irreducible
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules. In this way, we obtain, for each partition
\mu
with
|\mu|=n
, a positive integer

r_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(V_{\mu},\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}),

where
V_{\mu}
is the Specht module corresponding to the partition
\mu
. The coefficients
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
are called
restriction coefficients
.

In 1935, Littlewood proved that

(6)

r_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[H]\rangle,

where
H=1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots
denotes the sum of all complete homogeneous symmetric functions
[
7
]
. However, there remains no known combinatorial formula for
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
. The problem of finding such a combinatorial formula is known as the
restriction problem
.

The restriction problem continues to be a popular research topic and has been studied from many perspectives. Here is a small and far from comprehensive sampling of recent results. In 2021, Orellana and Zabrocki introduced the
irreducible character basis

\{\tilde{s}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
of the ring of symmetric functions and used it to provide an algorithm for computing
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}

[
14
]
. In 2024, the author introduced an abelian group homomorphism
\mathscr{F}
called the
Frobenius transform
on the ring of symmetric functions and used it to prove several results about the vanishing of
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}

[
6
]
. In 2024, Narayanan, Paul, Prasad, and Srivastava found a combinatorial interpretation of
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
in the case that
\mu
has one column and
\lambda
is either a hook shape or has at most two columns
[
11
]
.

Using
Theorem

1
, we may solve the restriction problem in the case that
\lambda
has at most three columns. In this case, we present the following formula for
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
. (Recall that
H=1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots
.)

Theorem 2
.

Let
\lambda
,
\mu
be partitions. If
\lambda_{1}\leq 3
, then

r_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\left\langle H\sum_{r,\nu}e_{r}s_{(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}-r)/2,(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2,(-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2}s_{\lambda/\nu^{T}},s_{\mu}\right\rangle,

where the sum is over all integers
k
and all partitions
\nu
such that
\nu^{T}\subseteq\lambda
and
(-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2
is a nonnegative integer.

Because of the Littlewood–Richardson rule, which allows us to expand the expression

He_{r}s_{(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}-r)/2,(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2,(-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2}s_{\lambda/\nu^{T}}

as a nonnegative linear combination of Schur functions, the formula presented in
Theorem

2
is combinatorial. We will explicitly describe a combinatorial interpretation of
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
in the case that
\lambda
has at most three columns in
Section

4
.

2.1.
Symmetric functions

Let
\Lambda
denote the ring of symmetric functions with integer coefficients in the infinitely many variables
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots
.

For a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of natural numbers (that is, nonnegative integers), define the
length

\ell(\lambda)=\ell
and the
size

|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}
. Let
e_{\lambda}
and
h_{\lambda}
denote the corresponding elementary and homogeneous symmetric functions, respectively. If
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}
, let
s_{\lambda}
denote the corresponding Schur symmetric function. When a sequence
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
appears as a subscript in this way, we will commonly omit the parentheses; for example,
e_{1,3,2,1}
is shorthand for
e_{(1,3,2,1)}=e_{3}e_{2}e_{1}^{2}
.

We say that
\lambda
is a
partition
if
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}>0
. Let
\mathrm{Par}
denote the set of all partitions. It is well known that
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}}
,
(h_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}}
, and
(s_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}}
are all bases for
\Lambda
over
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\lambda^{T}
denote the transpose (i.e. conjugate) of a partition
\lambda
.

Let
\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle\colon\Lambda\times\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}
denote the Hall inner product of symmetric functions. Let
\omega\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
denote the linear map given by
\omega(e_{\lambda})=h_{\lambda}
for all partitions
\lambda
. It is well known that
\omega
is a ring homomorphism and an involution and that
\omega(s_{\lambda})=s_{\lambda^{T}}
for all partitions
\lambda
.

For a variable
t
, let

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}t}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{p_{k}}{k}t^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\in\Lambda\llbracket t\rrbracket

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}e_{n}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\geq 1}(1+x_{i}t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{p_{k}}{k}(-1)^{k-1}t^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\in\Lambda\llbracket t\rrbracket.

It is clear that
E(-t)=(H(t))^{-1}
.

Let
\overline{\Lambda}
denote the ring of symmetric formal power series; i.e. formal sums
\sum_{n}f_{n}
, where
f_{n}\in\Lambda
is homogeneous of degree
n
for all
n
. Let
H=H(1)=1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots\in\overline{\Lambda}
. In the remainder of this article, we will often silently generalize definitions and results from
\Lambda
to
\overline{\Lambda}
using the appropriate limit. For example, even though we have explicitly stated that the Hall inner product is a function
\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle\colon\Lambda\times\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}
, we may write an expression such as

\langle 2+3h_{1}-h_{4},H\rangle,

which is understood to denote the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle 2+3h_{1}-h_{4},1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots+h_{n}\rangle=4.

2.2.
Littlewood–Richardson coefficients

For all partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
, let

c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}=\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}s_{\nu}\rangle.

The numbers
c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}
are known as
Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
. The well known
Littlewood–Richardson rule
, which we state below, is a combinatorial interpretation of
c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew shape. A
Littlewood–Richardson tableau
of shape
\lambda/\mu
is a semistandard Young tableau
T
of shape
\lambda/\mu
such that the concatenation of the reversed rows of
T
is a lattice word; i.e., every prefix has at least as many occurrences of
i
as occurrences of
i+1
for all positive integers
i
.

Theorem 3

(Littlewood–Richardson Rule,
[
2
, Chapter 5]
,
[
10
, Chapter 9]
,
[
16
, Appendix 1]
)
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be partitions. Then
c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}
is the number of Littlewood–Richardson tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
and content
\nu
. (In particular, if
\mu\not\subseteq\lambda
, then
c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}=0
.)

Littlewood–Richardson coefficients are the structure constants for multiplication in the Schur basis. In other words, for all partitions
\mu,\nu
, we have

s_{\mu}s_{\nu}=\sum_{\lambda}c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}s_{\lambda}.

Moreover, for all partitions
\lambda,\mu
, the skew Schur function
s_{\lambda/\mu}
is given by

s_{\lambda/\mu}=\sum_{\nu}c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}s_{\nu}.

Let us define a
horizontal strip
to be a skew shape
\lambda/\mu
with no two boxes in the same column and define a
vertical strip
to be a skew shape with no two boxes in the same row. One important special case of the Littlewood–Richardson rule is the following.

Theorem 4

(Pieri’s Formula,
[
10
, (5.16, 5.17)]
,
[
16
, Theorem 7.15.7]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a partition, and let
r\geq 0
. Then,

h_{r}s_{\mu}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
\text{$\lambda/\mu$ is a horizontal strip}\\
|\lambda/\mu|=r\end{subarray}}s_{\lambda}

and

e_{r}s_{\mu}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
\text{$\lambda/\mu$ is a vertical strip}\\
|\lambda/\mu|=r\end{subarray}}s_{\lambda}.

2.3.
The coalgebra structure

In 2021, Orellana and Zabrocki used the coalgebra structure of
\Lambda
to study the restriction problem
[
13
]
. This coalgebra structure will also be useful in the proof of
Theorem

2
. For an introduction to the material in this subsection, see the 2020 lecture notes of Grinberg and Reiner on Hopf algebras in combinatorics
[
5
, Chapter 2]
.

Let
\nabla\colon\Lambda\otimes\Lambda\to\Lambda
denote the multiplication map, and let
\eta\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\Lambda
denote the natural inclusion. (All tensor products in this article are over
\mathbb{Z}
.) Define the
comultiplication

\Delta\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda\otimes\Lambda
and
counit

\epsilon\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}
to be the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear maps given respectively by

(7)

\Delta(s_{\lambda})=\sum_{\mu}s_{\mu}\otimes s_{\lambda/\mu}=\sum_{\mu,\nu}c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}s_{\mu}\otimes s_{\nu}

and

\epsilon(s_{\lambda})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\lambda=\emptyset$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

for all partitions
\lambda
. It is well known that with the operations
\Delta
and
\epsilon
, the ring
\Lambda
is a commutative and cocommutative graded Hopf algebra over
\mathbb{Z}
. The antipode
S\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
is given by
S(f)=(-1)^{\deg f}\omega(f)
for all homogeneous
f\in\Lambda

[
4
]
.

Because the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}
appearing in (
7
) are also the structure constants for multiplication in the Schur basis, the comultiplication
\Delta
is adjoint under the Hall inner product to multiplication. That is, for all
f,g,h\in\Lambda
, we have

\langle\Delta(f),g\otimes h\rangle=\langle f,gh\rangle,

where the angle brackets on the left-hand side of the equation denote the bilinear form
\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle\colon\Lambda\otimes\Lambda\times\Lambda\otimes\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}
given by

\langle a\otimes b,c\otimes d\rangle=\langle a,c\rangle\langle b,d\rangle.

We remark that this is one of the defining properties of a
positive self-adjoint Hopf (PSH) algebra
, of which
\Lambda
is an example
[
17
, Chapter 2]
. However, the definition of a positive self-adjoint Hopf algebra is not necessary for our proofs.

For all integers
k\geq 0
, let
\Lambda^{\otimes k}
denote the
k
-fold tensor power of
\Lambda
; that is,

\Lambda^{\otimes k}=\underbrace{\Lambda\otimes\cdots\otimes\Lambda}_{k}.

Define the iterated multiplication
\nabla^{(k-1)}\colon\Lambda^{\otimes k}\to\Lambda
and iterated comultiplication
\Delta^{(k-1)}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\otimes k}
by

\displaystyle\nabla^{(k-1)}

\displaystyle=\nabla\circ\cdots\circ(\nabla\otimes\id^{\otimes(k-3)})\circ(\nabla\otimes\id^{\otimes(k-2)})

\displaystyle\Delta^{(k-1)}

\displaystyle=(\Delta\otimes\id^{\otimes(k-2)})\circ(\Delta\otimes\id^{\otimes(k-3)})\circ\cdots\circ\Delta.

2.4.
Plethysm

There are two operations called “plethysm”, both denoted using square brackets, that we will refer to in this article. The first is the plethysm of one symmetric function by another, which is classical and was mentioned in
Section

1
. The second is the plethysm of a symmetric function by an element of a polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}]
, which we describe below. For more information, see the 2011 exposition of Loehr and Remmel, which describes the plethystic calculus more generally
[
9
]
.

Lemma 1

(
[
9
, Example 1]
)
.

Let
\ell\geq 0
. There exists a unique binary operation
\bullet[\bullet]\colon\Lambda\times\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]\to\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]
satisfying the following properties.

(i)

For all
g\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]
, the function
\bullet[g]\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]
is a ring homomorphism.

(ii)

For all
k>0
, the function
p_{k}[\bullet]\colon\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]\to\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]
is a ring homomorphism.

(iii)

For all
k
and
i
, we have
p_{k}[t_{i}]=t_{i}^{k}
.

The operation from
Lemma

1
can be expressed explicitly as a Hall inner product as follows.

Lemma 2

(
[
6
, Lemma 7.16]
)
.

Let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{N}
be monic monomials in the variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}
, and let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}
. For all
f\in\Lambda
, we have

(8)

f\left[\sum_{n}a_{n}M_{n}\right]=\left\langle f,\prod_{n}H(M_{n})^{a_{n}}\right\rangle.

We now collect a few basic results of the plethystic calculus. Recall that
S\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
is the antipode (
Section

2.3
).

Lemma 3

(Associativity of Plethysm,
[
9
, Theorem 5]
)
.

Let
f,g\in\Lambda
and
h\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}]
. Then,

f[g[h]]=(f[g])[h]\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}].

Lemma 4

(Negation Rule,
[
9
, Theorem 6]
)
.

Let
f,g\in\Lambda
. Then,

f[-g]=S(f)[g].

Lemma 5

(Monomial Substitution Rule,
[
9
, Theorem 7]
)
.

Let
f\in\Lambda
and let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}]
be monic monomials. Then,

f\left[\sum_{n}M_{n}\right]=f(M_{1},\ldots,M_{N}).

2.5.
The plethystic addition formula

We will make heavy use of the following formula.

Proposition 1

(Plethystic Addition Formula,
[
10
, (8.8)]

[
9
, Section 3.2]
)
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition and let

. Then,

s_{\lambda}[f+g]=\sum_{\mu}s_{\mu}[f]s_{\lambda/\mu}[g].

For our purposes, it will be useful to rephrase

\bullet[g]\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda

(\bullet[g])(f)=f[g]

.

Proposition 2

For all
f,g\in\Lambda
, we have

\bullet[f+g]=\nabla\circ(\bullet[f]\otimes\bullet[g])\circ\Delta.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

(\bullet[f+g])(s_{\lambda})=(\nabla\circ(\bullet[f]\otimes\bullet[g])\circ\Delta)(s_{\lambda})

for all partitions
\lambda
. After unfolding the definitions, this is exactly
1
.
∎

By applying

Proposition 3
.

Let
k\geq 0
, and let

. Then,

\bullet[g_{1}+\cdots+g_{k}]=\nabla^{(k-1)}\circ(\bullet[g_{1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes\bullet[g_{k}])\circ\Delta^{(k-1)}.

3.1.
Plethysm adjoints

For all

\bullet[g^{\perp}]\colon\Lambda\to\overline{\Lambda}

f[g^{\perp}]=\sum_{\mu}\langle f,s_{\mu}[g]\rangle s_{\mu}

for all

. The notation
\bullet[g^{\perp}]
was introduced by Ryba in 2021

.

Because
\bullet[g]
is an algebra homomorphism,
\bullet[g^{\perp}]
is a coalgebra homomorphism. Moreover, by taking the adjoint of both sides of
3
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4

(
[

, Proposition 2.5]
)
.

Let
k\geq 0
, and let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in\Lambda
. Then,

\bullet[(g_{1}+\cdots+g_{k})^{\perp}]=\Delta^{(k-1)}\circ(\bullet[g_{1}^{\perp}]\otimes\cdots\otimes\bullet[g_{k}^{\perp}])\circ\nabla^{(k-1)}.

which the author has referred to as the

in 2024

Thus,

\lambda

\lambda_{1}\leq 3
, we have

s_{\lambda}[H^{\perp}]=H\sum_{r,\nu}e_{r}s_{(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}-r)/2,(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2,(-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2}s_{\lambda/\nu^{T}},

where the sum is over all integers

\nu
such that
\nu^{T}\subseteq\lambda
and
(-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2
is a nonnegative integer. In what follows, our goal will be to prove (

Let us recall the following formulas for
\mathscr{F}\{h_{\lambda}\}=h_{\lambda}[H^{\perp}]
and
\mathscr{F}\{e_{\lambda}\}=e_{\lambda}[H^{\perp}]
, where
\lambda
is a partition.

Theorem 6

(
[
12
, Equation (6)]
,
[

, Theorem 1.4(a,b)]
)
.

Let
\ell\geq 0
, and let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
.

(a)

We have

h_{\lambda}[H^{\perp}]=\sum_{M}\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}}h_{M(j)},

(b)

We have

e_{\lambda}[H^{\perp}]=\sum_{M}\prod_{j\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}\begin{cases}h_{M(j)}&\mbox{if $j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}$ is even;}\\
e_{M(j)}&\mbox{if $j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}$ is odd,}\end{cases}

where the sum is over all functions
M\colon\{0,1\}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}M(j)j=\lambda.

For
r,\ell\geq 0
, let

\WC(\ell,r)=\{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}\,|\,j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}=r\}

denote the set of all weak compositions of

\ell
parts, and let

\B(\ell,r)=\{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell})\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\,|\,j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}=r\}

denote the set of all binary vectors of weight
r
and length
\ell
. To prove (
11
), we will use the following new formula for the plethysm adjoints
h_{\lambda}[h_{r}^{\perp}]
and
e_{\lambda}[h_{r}^{\perp}]
. We will only use part
(b)
of
Theorem

7
in what follows, but we include both parts because they may be of some independent interest.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\ell,r\geq 0
, and let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
.

(a)

We have

h_{\lambda}[h_{r}^{\perp}]=\sum_{M}\prod_{j\in\WC(\ell,r)}h_{M(j)},

where the sum is over all functions
M\colon\WC(\ell,r)\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{j\in\WC(\ell,r)}M(j)j=\lambda.

(b)

We have

e_{\lambda}[h_{r}^{\perp}]=\sum_{M}\prod_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}\begin{cases}h_{M(j)}&\mbox{if $r$ is even;}\\
e_{M(j)}&\mbox{if $r$ is odd,}\end{cases}

where the sum is over all functions
M\colon\B(\ell,r)\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}M(j)j=\lambda.

Proof.

(a) Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}
be variables. Observe that for all
f\in\Lambda
, we have

(12)

\displaystyle\langle f,(H(t_{1})\cdots H(t_{\ell}))[h_{r}^{\perp}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f[h_{r}],H(t_{1})\cdots H(t_{\ell})\rangle

(13)

\displaystyle=(f[h_{r}])[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]

(14)

\displaystyle=f[h_{r}[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]]

(15)

\displaystyle=f\left[\sum_{j\in\WC(\ell,r)}t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}}\right]

(16)

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,\prod_{j\in\WC(\ell,r)}H(t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}})\right\rangle.

For (
12
), we used the definition of the plethysm adjoint. For (
13
), we used
Lemma

2
. For (
14
), we used the associativity of plethysm (
Lemma

3
). For (
15
), we used the monomial substitution rule (
Lemma

5
). For (
16
), we used
Lemma

2
again.

It follows that

(H(t_{1})\cdots H(t_{\ell}))[h_{r}^{\perp}]=\prod_{j\in\WC(\ell,r)}H(t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}}).

Taking the coefficient of
t_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{\lambda_{\ell}}
on both sides yields the desired result.

(b) Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}
be variables. Observe that for all
f\in\Lambda
, we have

(17)

\displaystyle\langle f,(H(t_{1})^{-1}\cdots H(t_{\ell})^{-1})[h_{r}^{\perp}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f[h_{r}],H(t_{1})^{-1}\cdots H(t_{\ell})^{-1}\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle=(f[h_{r}])[-(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell})]

(19)

\displaystyle=f[h_{r}[-(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell})]]

(20)

\displaystyle=f\left[(-1)^{r}e_{r}[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]\right]

(21)

\displaystyle=f\left[(-1)^{r}\sum_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}}\right]

(22)

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,\prod_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}H(t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}})^{(-1)^{r}}\right\rangle.

For (
17
), we used the definition of the plethysm adjoint. For (
18
), we used
Lemma

2
. For (
19
), we used the associativity of plethysm (
Lemma

3
). For (
20
), we used the negation rule (
Lemma

4
). For (
21
), we used the monomial substitution rule (
Lemma

5
). For (
22
), we used
Lemma

2
again.

It follows that

(23)

(H(t_{1})^{-1}\cdots H(t_{\ell})^{-1})[h_{r}^{\perp}]=\prod_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}H(t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}})^{(-1)^{r}}.

Using the equation
E(-t)=(H(t))^{-1}
, the left-hand side of (
23
) simplifies to
(E(-t_{1})\cdots E(-t_{\ell}))[h_{r}^{\perp}]
and the right-hand side simplifies to

\begin{cases}\prod_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}H(t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}})&\mbox{if $r$ is even}\\
\prod_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}E(-t_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{j_{\ell}})&\mbox{if $r$ is odd}\end{cases}.

Taking the coefficient of
(-1)^{|\lambda|}t_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{\lambda_{\ell}}
on both sides yields the desired result.
∎

3.2.
Specializing to partitions with at most three columns

In this subsection, we will prove
Theorem

1
. Let
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,\ell,r)
denote the index set for the sum on the right-hand side of
Theorem

7
(b)
. In other words,
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,\ell,r)
is the set of all functions
M\colon\B(\ell,r)\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{j\in\B(\ell,r)}M(j)j=\lambda.

If
\ell=3
, then the set
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,\ell,r)
is easy to describe, and we can greatly simplify the right-hand side of
Theorem

7
(b)
. We demonstrate this method of computation with the following proposition and its proof. Some parts of this proposition can trivially be proved using other methods, but we include them for illustrative purposes.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
. For all
r\geq 4
, we have the following.

(24)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}[h_{0}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}H&\mbox{if $\lambda=\emptyset$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(25)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}[h_{1}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}.

(26)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}[h_{2}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=h_{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})/2}h_{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})/2}h_{(-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})/2}.

(27)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}[h_{3}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e_{\lambda_{1}}&\mbox{if $\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(28)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}[h_{r}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\lambda=\emptyset$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

In (
26
), recall that we use the convention that
h_{m}=0
for all
m\not\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(
24
)

We have
\B(3,0)=\{(0,0,0)\}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,0)
is the set of all functions
M\colon\{(0,0,0)\}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

M(0,0,0)\cdot(0,0,0)=\lambda.

The left-hand side of this equation simplifies to
(0,0,0)
. Hence, if
\lambda=(0,0,0)=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,0)
is the set of all functions
M\colon\{(0,0,0)\}\to\mathbb{N}
, and otherwise
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,0)=\emptyset
. The equation follows from
Theorem

7
(b)
.

(
25
)

We have
\B(3,1)=\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,1)
is the set of all functions
M\colon\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

M(1,0,0)\cdot(1,0,0)+M(0,1,0)\cdot(0,1,0)+M(0,0,1)\cdot(0,0,1)=\lambda.

There is only one such function, given by

M(1,0,0)=\lambda_{1};\quad M(0,1,0)=\lambda_{2};\quad M(0,0,1)=\lambda_{3}.

The equation follows from
Theorem

7
(b)
.

(
26
)

We have
\B(3,2)=\{(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,2)
is the set of all functions
M\colon\{(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

M(1,1,0)\cdot(1,1,0)+M(1,0,1)\cdot(1,0,1)+M(0,1,1)\cdot(0,1,1)=\lambda.

Solving this equation yields

\displaystyle M(1,1,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}}{2};

\displaystyle\quad M(1,0,1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}}{2};

\displaystyle\quad M(0,1,1)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}}{2}.

The equation follows from
Theorem

7
(b)
.

(
27
)

We have
\B(3,3)=\{(1,1,1)\}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,3)
is the set of all functions
M\colon\{(1,1,1)\}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

M(1,1,1)\cdot(1,1,1)=\lambda.

If
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}
, then this equation has the solution
M(1,1,1)=\lambda_{1}
, and otherwise it has no solution. The equation follows from
Theorem

7
(b)
.

(
28
)

We have
\B(3,r)=\emptyset
. If
\lambda=(0,0,0)=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,r)
consists of the unique function
M\colon\emptyset\to\mathbb{N}
. Otherwise,
\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,r)
is empty. The equation follows from
Theorem

7
(b)
.∎

Remark 1
.

In the proof of
5
, we saw that
|\mathcal{I}(\lambda,3,r)|\leq 1
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
and all
r>0
. However, if

can have a complicated structure, so

for all

Theorem 8

Let

Proof.

We begin with (

(34)

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\det\begin{bmatrix}e_{\lambda^{T}_{1}}&e_{\lambda^{T}_{1}+1}&e_{\lambda^{T}_{1}+2}\\

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}[h_{2}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}}(-1)^{\pi}e_{\lambda^{T}_{1}-1+\pi(1),\lambda^{T}_{2}-2+\pi(2),\lambda^{T}_{3}-3+\pi(3)}[h_{2}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot h_{(\lambda^{T}_{1}-\lambda^{T}_{2}+\lambda^{T}_{3})/2-\pi(2)+2}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot h_{(-\lambda^{T}_{1}+\lambda^{T}_{2}+\lambda^{T}_{3})/2-\pi(1)+1}.

3.3.
Performing the comultiplication

In this subsection, we will complete the proof of
Theorem

2
. Let

D=\operatorname{span}\{s_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda_{1}\leq 3\}\subseteq\Lambda.

Because the Littlewood-Richardson coefficient
c^{\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}
is zero if
\mu_{1}>\lambda_{1}
or
\nu_{1}>\lambda_{1}
, we have that
D
is a

of
\Lambda
, meaning that
\Delta(D)\subseteq D\otimes D
. This implies that

(35)

\Delta^{(k)}(D)\subseteq D^{\otimes(k+1)}

for all
k
.

By (

for all

and all

\eta
and

\Lambda
, respectively; see

2.3

3

\displaystyle f[H^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}f[(1+h_{1}+\cdots+h_{k})^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\nabla^{(k)}\circ(\bullet[1^{\perp}]\otimes\bullet[h_{1}^{\perp}]\otimes\cdots\otimes\bullet[h_{k}^{\perp}])\circ\Delta^{(k)})(f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\nabla^{(k)}\circ(\bullet[1^{\perp}]\otimes\bullet[h_{1}^{\perp}]\otimes\bullet[h_{2}^{\perp}]\otimes\bullet[h_{3}^{\perp}]\otimes(\eta\circ\epsilon)^{\otimes k-3})\circ\Delta^{(k)})(f)

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)},\lambda^{(3)}}s_{\lambda^{(1)}}[1^{\perp}]\cdot s_{\lambda^{(2)}/\lambda^{(1)}}[h_{3}^{\perp}]\cdot s_{\lambda^{(3)}/\lambda^{(2)}}[h_{2}^{\perp}]\cdot s_{\lambda/\lambda^{(3)}}[h_{1}^{\perp}]

4.
The combinatorial interpretation

We may now derive a combinatorial interpretation of the restriction coefficient
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
, where
\lambda,\mu
are partitions with
\lambda_{1}\leq 3

Corollary 1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu
be partitions with
\lambda_{1}\leq 3
. Then,
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
is the number of tuples
(r,\nu,\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)},\lambda^{(3)},T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
, where

•

r\geq 0
is an integer;

•

\nu,\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)},\lambda^{(3)}
are partitions;

•

(-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2
is a nonnegative integer;

•

T^{(1)}
is a Littlewood–Richardson tableau of shape
\lambda/\nu^{T}
and content
\lambda^{(1)}
;

•

T^{(2)}
is a Littlewood–Richardson tableau of shape
\lambda^{(2)}/\lambda^{(1)}
and content
((\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}-r)/2,(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2,(-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-r)/2)
;

•

\lambda^{(3)}/\lambda^{(2)}
is a vertical strip with
r
boxes;

•

\mu/\lambda^{(3)}
is a horizontal strip.

Proof.

This is the result of starting with
Theorem

2
, expanding each skew Schur function and product of Schur functions using Littlewood–Richardson coefficients, and expanding each product of an elementary or complete homogeneous symmetric function with a Schur function using Pieri’s formula (

4
).

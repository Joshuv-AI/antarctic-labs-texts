Thermal fluid coupling simulation and optimization of downhole baffle heater adapted to Tar-rich Coal in-situ pyrolysis
Authors: Zhao Shuai, Shi Jiaying, Wu Peng, Sun Zhongjin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The downhole heater is a crucial equipment for realizing the in-situ pyrolysis process of Tar-rich Coal. The different structural performances of heaters can also affect the heat transfer effect underground. The article investigates the comprehensive heat transfer performance of circular horizontal baffle and spiral baffle heaters through digital modeling and simulation. The distribution characteristics of flow velocity, heat transfer coefficient and wall temperature of the heating tube, as well as the pressure drop in the shell side, were investigated for two types of heaters within a mass flow rate of 0.00693–0.05542 kg s−1. The variation patterns of the Nusselt number and thermal resistance coefficient of the two type heaters under different mass flow rates were analyzed. The results showed that compared to the spiral baffle heater, the Nusselt coefficient of the circular baffle heater decreased by 3.82%, 10.75%, 0.73%, and 8.45%, respectively, at mass flow rates of 0.00693 kg s−1, 0.01386 kg s−1, 0.02078 kg s−1, and 0.03464 kg s−1, while the thermal resistance coefficient increased by 2.33%, 10.62%, 0.98%, and 7.59%. This indicates that the helical baffle heater is more suitable for the process parameters of in-situ pyrolysis of Tar-rich Coal. Based on numerical simulation results, a prototype of a spiral baffle heater with a pitch of 110 mm was fabricated. Gas heating experiments were conducted at corresponding mass flow rates of 0.00693 kg s−1, 0.01039 kg s−1, and 0.01386 kg s−1. The results indicate that the temperature values obtained from numerical simulation analysis are essentially identical to the actual experimental temperature data, with an average error of < ± 7% for the heater outlet temperature. This proves the accuracy and reliability of the numerical simulation analysis.

Introduction

China’s petrochemical resources generally demonstrate the characteristics of "a scarcity of oil and natural gas resources, coupled with a relative abundance of coal resources"1,2. This energy characteristic also determines that coal will remain the mainstay of China’s energy consumption for a certain period of time. The traditional coal utilization process releases a large amount of CO2, CH4, CO, as well as mercury, sulfur, nitrogen oxides, organic compounds, and particulate pollutants (such as coal ash and dust) into the environment3. The transition towards a low-carbon development in China’s coal industry has become an inevitable trend. As a unique coal resource4, Tar-rich Coal has garnered significant attention owing to its high tar content (> 7. 0%).The exploiting of Tar-rich Coal represents one of the effective approaches to achieving green and low-carbon coal utilization and fulfilling China’s and the “dual carbon” objectives5,6. As shown in Fig. 1, the in-situ pyrolysis process for Tar-rich Coal involves injecting normal temperature high-pressure gas into the well. Under the action of a downhole heater, the gas forms high-temperature and high-pressure gas, which undergoes thermal convection with the Tar-rich Coal formation through the underground fracture network formed by hydraulic fracturing. This causes the kerogen to crack, producing tar and pyrolysis gas, thus achieving the clean and high-value extraction of Tar-rich Coal7.Fig. 1Principle of in-situ pyrolysis process for Tar-rich Coal.

Principle of in-situ pyrolysis process for Tar-rich Coal.

The downhole heater is a crucial equipment for realizing the in-situ pyrolysis process of Tar-rich Coal. The structural performance of the heater also influences the heat transfer effect underground. Owing to the impacts of the in-situ pyrolysis process and the drilling hole diameter, the shell-and-tube electric heater currently stands as the most prevalent heat exchange structure employed for the in-situ conversion of unconventional energy sources8,9. The shell-and-tube electric heater is primarily composed of heat-exchanging tube bundles and baffle plates. Cui et al. studied the influence of calculation methods on the heat transfer efficiency of shell and tube heat exchangers, showed that the Kern method had the largest difference in heat transfer efficiency compared to the MSRE (Molten Salt Reactor Experiment) experiment, about 15%. The difference between the total heat transfer coefficient obtained from HTRI (Software named HTRI Xchanger Suite) software simulation and MSRE experiment is the smallest, which is 0.16%10. Cui et al. applied the set pruning algorithm to the design of shell and tube heat exchangers. The optimization results with the objective function of minimizing the heat transfer area show that the ensemble pruning algorithm can achieve a smaller heat transfer area11. Lin et al. reviewed the current research advancements in shell and tube heat exchangers, and posited that the optimization of their geometric structure primarily involves altering the heat transfer tube type, augmenting the number of internal tube inserts, and refining the baffle configuration on the shell side. Enhancements in the thermal properties of heat exchange fluids encompass the augmentation of thermal conductivity through nanofluids and the elevation of specific heat capacity via latent heat type thermal fluids. This can induce a symmetrical imbalance of bubbles within the fluid, corresponding to a state of peripheral rupture and annular formation, with unstable dynamics12,13. Composite enhanced heat transfer is the combination of multiple enhancement methods, which can compensate for the shortcomings of a single method and achieve higher enhanced heat transfer effects14,15.

The tube bundles consist of electrically heated tubes with a certain heat flux density. Studies by Duan et al. have found that the working environment of downhole heaters during the in-situ pyrolysis of Tar-rich Coal is harsh. The vibrations induced by the electric heating rod bundles and the presence of local hot spots on the heating rod wall constitute two critical factors that undermine the operational stability of downhole electric heaters4,16. The main function of baffle plates is to change the direction of fluid motion and support the tube bundles, preventing them from failing due to induced vibrations caused by fluid flow. To quantitatively assess the overall performance of shell and tube heaters, researchers have suggested employing the Nusselt coefficient and the ratio of the average heat transfer coefficient h of the heating tube to the one-third power of the shell side pressure drop ΔP as comprehensive performance evaluation metrics. Ji et al. modified the installation method of baffle plates and spiral tubes, employing a bidirectional fluid–structure coupling calculation method to investigate the impact of fluid-induced spiral tube vibrations on the comprehensive heat transfer performance of RBST (Reverse Baffles on the Spiral Tubes) heat exchangers. When the baffle plates and spiral tubes in the RBST heat exchanger are installed seamlessly, the comprehensive performance evaluation index of the heat exchanger can reach up to 1.01817. A. Heydari et al. studied the effects of baffle count and spacing on the vibration and heat transfer of shell-and-tube heat exchangers, pointing out that increasing the baffle count and reducing spacing elevates natural frequencies, delaying resonance18. Nithya et al. utilized the Realizable k-ε turbulence model coupled with scalable wall functions, and applied the PISO (Pressure Implicit with Splitting of Operators) algorithm for pressure–velocity coupling, to assess the effects of entropy generation, shear stress distribution, and baffles on uneven flow distribution and thermal performance19. Some scholars have devoted themselves to optimizing numerical simulation algorithms, aiming to enhance heat transfer performance and facilitate real-time data sharing. Beigzadeh et al. used genetic algorithms to investigate the effects of baffle diameter ratio (di/D), spacing ratio (S/D), and hole count (N) on the heat transfer performance of circular horizontal baffle plate heat exchangers. The results demonstrate that reducing baffle spacing and increasing the number of baffle holes significantly enhance heat transfer, but at the cost of higher flow resistance20. To address the complex environment of in-situ pyrolysis of oil shale, Yang et al. developed an online monitoring system, providing a solution for the design and performance monitoring of on-site in-situ heaters for oil shale pyrolysis21.

There are also relevant researchers who analyze the working efficiency of shell-and-tube heat exchangers from the perspective of heat transfer performance and efficiency. Wang et al. investigated the thermal efficiency of underground electric heaters employed for the in-situ conversion of Tar-rich Coal, focusing on the quality of heat transfer quality, and discovered that heaters featuring a closed double-shell structure exhibit superior efficiency compared to those with a single-shell design22. Hoang et al. proposed a novel single-shell-pass shell-and-tube heat exchanger design with alternative inclined baffles to overcome the weaknesses of single-segment baffles, aiming to achieve lower pressure drop and higher thermal efficiency23. Additionally, certain scholars have dedicated their efforts to the optimization of the baffle structures within heat exchangers, conducting experiments with various types such as hybrid segmented baffles, curved baffles, spiral and flower-patterned baffles, dual-channel spiral segmented baffles, and segmented perforated baffles24. To bridge the research gaps regarding the heat transfer enhancement characteristics of coiled elastic copper tube (CECT) with spiral baffles, Sun et al. proposed CECT heat exchangers equipped with perforated and non-perforated spiral baffles (CECT-WP, CECT-NP)25. Waleed et al. adopted computational fluid dynamics (CFD) to investigate the flow characteristics within shell-and-tube heat exchangers equipped with various baffle and turbulence promoter combinations, including the assembly of double-flower baffles and triple-flower baffles, as well as the integration of triple-flower baffles with a novel quad-flower baffle. The results indicate that such composite configurations enhance the thermal interaction between cold and hot fluid streams. Moreover, the shell-and-tube heat exchanger incorporating the triple-flower baffle coupled with the novel quad-flower baffle exhibits superior heat transfer efficiency relative to the unit employing only double-flower and triple-flower baffle combinations26. Zhang et al. studied a novel sinusoidal staggered shell and tube heat exchanger without baffles. The results showed that the performance of the STHX (Shell and tube heat exchangers) can be improved with the increment of the sinusoidal tube amplitude and the tube number per column, but not indefinitely27. Alipoura et al. investigated the rotational angle (− 45° ≤ α ≤ 45°), pitch ratio (0.625 ≤ PR ≤ 2.5), and aspect ratio (0.5 ≤ AR ≤ 1) of baffles through a combination of numerical simulation and experimentation, to assess the Nusselt number (Nu), friction factor (f), and thermal enhancement factor (TEF) at various Reynolds numbers (5000 ≤ Re ≤ 25,000)28.

In the field of energy resource extraction, very high viscosity signifycantly affects the fluidity of heavy crude oil. Downhole electric heating injects heat uniformly into the fluid and reservoir. Due to the exponential relationship between dynamic viscosity and temperature, the dynamic viscosity is significantly reduced29,30. Sun et al. proposes a variable pitch helical baffle plate orifice plate-type heater model and employs numerical simulation to investigate the trends of heat transfer coefficient h, average wall temperature T, and temperature difference DT under different pitch intervals Dx. The results indicate that regardless of pitch adjustment, the heat transfer coefficient h continuously decreases as the heat transfer process progresses31. Bu et al. studied the heat transfer performance of a large helical baffle downhole heater applied to in-situ pyrolysis of oil shale under various operating conditions. The findings indicate that at 160 m3/h and 6 kW, the outlet temperature can reach 280 °C. Controlling the heating power or increasing the injected gas flow effectively mitigates heat accumulation on the surface of the heating rod32. Lin et al. explored the influence of baffle plate opening (0–6.31%) on heater performance in the study of molten salt energy storage. The results indicate that a reasonable baffle opening can significantly reduce the shell-side pressure drop and effectively decrease the shell-side flow dead zone area33. Tang et al. studied the performance of a large helical baffle heater in field operation. The study reveals that the fluid in the shell maintains a spiral flow, and the output flow velocity is higher than in the surrounding area. The pitch design is large, resulting in a low-velocity flow zone on the downwind side. At 100 kW and 500 m3/h, the fluid flow is turbulent. At 50 kW and 200 m3/h, the fluid remains laminar34.

In summary, despite the abundance of research studies on heaters conducted in recent years, with research directions covering heater-induced vibration, baffle structure design, algorithm optimization and iteration, as well as heating tube layout optimization, relatively limited research has been carried out on the effects of injected fluid process parameters on the stability and heat transfer performance of downhole heaters. Specifically, these downhole heaters are applied for the in-situ pyrolysis extraction of oil and gas resources in oil-rich coal drillings, which is based on the formation of through channels via hydraulic fracturing of underground coal seams. When the injected fluid flows through the downhole heater, it is subjected to the constraint of the heater structure; meanwhile, the on-way resistance and pressure drop characteristics of the fluid will also exert a significant impact on the heat transfer efficiency of the heater. Against this background, this study explores the influences of the arrangement modes of transverse and spiral baffles on the wall heat transfer performance and pressure drop of downhole heaters by means of three-dimensional (3D) modeling and numerical simulation techniques. The performance of the heater is evaluated comprehensively using the Nusselt number and thermal resistance coefficient, and a heater structure suitable for in-situ pyrolysis of Tar-rich Coal under gas injection parameters of 20–100 m3/h is selected. A prototype is made for experimental verification of the reliability of numerical simulation.

Modeling and numerical analysis of gas heater

In situ pyrolysis process of Tar-rich Coal

Tar-rich Coal in situ pyrolysis is a process that utilizes drilling to extract oil and gas resources. On the basis of drilling construction, hydraulic fracturing is implemented. This can form connected fracture network in the Tar-rich Coal formation. The process flow of in-situ pyrolysis extraction of oil and gas resources from drilling rich coal is to implement hydraulic fracturing on the basis of drilling construction, and form a connected underground fracture network in the form of artificial fractures in the underground coal seam. By injecting gas into the downhole heater, the injected gas is usually nitrogen. Use a downhole heater to raise the gas temperature to 300–600 °C. The high-temperature gas undergoes convective heat transfer with the oil rich coal seam in the fracture network formed by fracturing, which realizes the underground in-situ pyrolysis process of the Tar-rich Coal7,35.

In the process of numerical modeling and analysis of the heater, considering the distribution characteristics of the actual working area of the heater and the computational performance of the calculator, we simplified the heater model. Simplify the shell to a surface with zero thickness, simplify the baffle to a relatively parallel surface adjacent to 2 mm, use the corresponding body structure for the heating tube, and ignore the gaps between the baffle and the shell, as well as between the heating tube and the baffle. When analyzing the heat transfer efficiency of the heater, in order to facilitate the comparison of the heat transfer effect of each heater, we established a Cartesian coordinate system in the core flow channel of the heater, with the center of the intersection of the inlet and the heater flange as the coordinate origin. The direction of gas flow extension is the X-axis, with the y-axis and Z-axis perpendicular to the X-axis. The X-axis range is − 50 to 1050 mm, and the Y-axis and Z-axis ranges are both − 65.5 to 65.5 mm. The quality of the grid not only affects the accuracy of calculations, but can also lead to erroneous results or divergent calculations in simulations. The baffle plate of the heater is complex and irregular, and structured grids are not suitable. Therefore, tetrahedral unstructured grids are used for the grid division of the baffle plate and shell, and hexahedral structured grids are used for the heating rod. In order to better reflect the actual situation, local encryption processing was carried out, and grid independence verification was performed using the average heat transfer coefficient of the heating tube at an inlet mass flow rate of 0.01386 kg/s. When the number of grids is 2 million, the average heat transfer coefficient of the heating tube is 25.01534 W m−2 k−1. When the number of grids is 3 million, the average heat transfer coefficient of the heating tube is 25.37079 W m−2 k−1. When the number of grids is 4 million, the average heat transfer coefficient of the heating tube is 25.48585 W m−2 k−1. When the number of grids is 5 million, the average heat transfer coefficient of the heating tube is 25.48602 W m−2 k−1. It can be seen that when the number of grids exceeds 4 million, the impact on the average heat transfer coefficient of the heating tube is minimal. So the final number of grid cells in each model structure reached around 4 million.

Results and discussion

Heater shell side flow rate

Figure 5 shows the meridional velocity cloud map and local (X = 605 mm, X = 805 mm) velocity vector magnification of the heater when the gas mass flow rate is 0.01386 kg s−1. In the transverse baffle heater, gas exhibits a zigzag flow pattern, with peak velocity near baffle edges. The gas passes through the edge of one baffle and rushes at high speed towards the next baffle. Close to the surface of the baffle, it becomes a lateral flow. Flow towards the shell or central tube and pass through the baffle along its edge. Part of the gas forms a backflow dead zone on the leeward side of the baffle. The gas flow velocity at the dead zone of reflux is the lowest. The flow velocity of gas in the shell side of the circular baffle heater is extremely uneven. The gas flow velocity is highest near the edge of the baffle. For spiral baffle heater, the chaotic flow lines at the gas inlet end are mainly caused by the lateral obstruction of the baffle by the gas. After entering the spiral baffle, the gas forms a regular spiral flow along the shell side. The spiral baffle heater has a spiral continuous baffle structure, which makes its internal channels more unobstructed. Gas does not encounter obstacles similar to transverse baffle heaters during the flow process. This makes the gas flow velocity more evenly and smoothly distributed throughout the baffle channel, which is more conducive to the heating of the gas by the heating tube.Fig. 5Velocity contours for the two heat exchangers of Horizontal circular baffle heater (S110) and Continuous spiral baffle heater (H110).

Velocity contours for the two heat exchangers of Horizontal circular baffle heater (S110) and Continuous spiral baffle heater (H110).

At the same time, it improves the stability of the surface heat transfer coefficient of the heating tube. Compared with the circular baffle heater, the continuous spiral baffle heater has a uniform gas flow velocity on the shell side. There is no backflow dead zone during fluid flow. This is beneficial for more efficient heat transfer between the surface of the heating tube and the fluid, as well as reducing the resistance of gas in the shell side of the heater.

Comprehensive performance evaluation of heater

In a shell and tube heater, the arrangement of the baffle plate on the shell side and the structure of the fins inside the tube will affect the flow state of the gas, thereby changing the Nu value. By analyzing the variation pattern of Nu, it is possible to clarify the heat transfer enhancement effect of heater structural improvements, providing a direct basis for the structural design and optimization of heaters. The Nusselt number directly quantifies the degree of enhancement of convective heat transfer relative to pure thermal conduction. The larger the value, the stronger the convective heat transfer and the better the heat transfer performance on the gas side. It is worth noting that the overall heat transfer performance of the shell and tube heater is determined by the thermal resistance of the gas side inside the tube, the tube wall, and the medium side on the shell side. If the thermal resistance on the medium side of the shell side is relatively high, using only the gas side Nu to evaluate the overall performance will result in deviation. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis based on thermal resistance is needed.

The Nusselt number and thermal resistance coefficient of the heater at different mass flow rates are shown in Fig. 13. From the graph, it can be seen that as the mass flow rate increases, the Nu of both heaters shows an upward trend, and the thermal resistance coefficient also shows a decreasing trend. Compared with the spiral baffle heater, at mass flow of 0.00693 kg s−1, 0.01386 kg s−1, 0.02078 kg s−1, and 0.03464 kg s−1, the Nusselt number of the circular baffle heater decreased by 3.82%, 10.75%, 0.73%, and 8.45%, respectively, while the thermal resistance coefficient increased by 2.33%, 10.62%, 0.98%, and 7.59%. This indicates that the spiral baffle heater is more suitable for the process parameters of in-situ pyrolysis of Tar-rich Coal.Fig. 13Nusselt number and thermal resistance coefficient of heater via mass flow rate.

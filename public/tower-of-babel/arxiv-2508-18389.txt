FastAvatar: Instant 3D Gaussian Splatting for Faces from Single Unconstrained Poses
Hao Liang, Zhixuan Ge, Ashish Tiwari, Soumendu Majee, Dilshan Godaliyadda, Ashok Veeraraghavan, Guha Balakrishnan

Abstract
We present FastAvatar, a pose-invariant, feed-forward framework that can generate a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model from a single face image from an arbitrary pose in near-instant time ( ≤ \leq 10ms). FastAvatar uses a novel encoder-decoder neural network design to achieve both fast fitting and identity preservation regardless of input pose. First, FastAvatar constructs a 3DGS face “template” model from a training dataset of faces with multi-view captures. Second, FastAvatar encodes the input face image into an identity-specific and pose-invariant latent embedding, and decodes this embedding to predict residuals to the structural and appearance parameters of each Gaussian in the template 3DGS model. By only inferring residuals in a feed-forward fashion, model inference is fast and robust. FastAvatar significantly outperforms existing feed-forward face 3DGS methods (e.g., GAGAvatar) in reconstruction quality, and runs 1000 × 1000\times faster than per-face optimization methods (e.g., FlashAvatar, GaussianAvatars and GASP). In addition, FastAvatar’s latent space design supports real-time identity interpolation and attribute editing which is not possible with any existing feed-forward 3DGS face generation framework. FastAvatar’s combination of excellent reconstruction quality and speed expands the scope and capabilities of 3DGS for photorealistic avatar applications. Our code is available at https://github.com/hliang2/FastAvatar .



1
Introduction

Creating 3D face models from images is a long-standing problem in computer vision and graphics, and is of significant current interest in digital avatar applications such as virtual reality, gaming, and content creation. 3D face model frameworks that enable fast and high-resolution novel view rendering performance from one or few input views are needed to support these applications. Classical parametric face models based on simple statistical approaches
Blanz and Vetter 2003
;
Blanz and Vetter 2023
;
Li et al. 2017
offer real-time fitting and rendering speed, but are limited in their expressive power. In contrast, recent approaches based on the Neural Radiance Field (NeRF)
Mildenhall et al. 2021
and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
Kerbl et al. 2023
neural rendering frameworks offer state-of-the-art expressive power, with 3DGS even enabling real-time rendering speed. However, these algorithms have critical limitations: they require expensive per-subject optimizations from multi-view captures, with fitting times ranging from minutes to hours. This computational burden severely restricts the practical deployment of these methods in many consumer applications.

In this study, we aim to instead develop a
feed-forward
framework that can generate a 3DGS face model from an input image in one shot rather than through iterative optimization. Existing feed-forward face generation approaches leverage generative models such as GANs
Goodfellow et al. 2014
and diffusion models
Ho et al. 2020
for specific viewpoints — typically (near-)frontal due to data availability — and hence tend to produce artifacts or identity shifts for other views
Gerogiannis et al. 2025
;
Chan et al. 2022
. Hence, there is a critical need for a single-view feed-forward method that simultaneously: (1) operates in real-time without optimization, (2) generalizes across arbitrary viewpoints from frontal to profile, (3) preserves input identity without synthetic artifacts, and (4) achieves quality competitive with optimization-based approaches.

To address these needs we present FastAvatar, the first pose-invariant, feed-forward framework for instant 3DGS face reconstruction from an arbitrary input viewpoint. FastAvatar converts an input image into a 3DGS model driven by two insights. First, to enable fast fitting, we take inspiration from classical morphable face models
Blanz and Vetter 2003
and design FastAvatar to construct a 3DGS “template” face model (using approximately
10{,}000
Gaussians) by averaging parameters across subject-specific 3DGS models, rather than randomly initializing or jointly optimizing them as in prior work
Saunders et al. 2025
;
Qian et al. 2024a
;
Yan et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2024
. FastAvatar then represents a new face at test time by predicting residuals to this template via an encoder-decoder network. Second, to enforce pose-invariant estimation of these Gaussian parameters given an input image with arbitrary pose, we constrain the encoder to map all viewpoints of an individual to an identical latent embedding vector leveraging pre-trained face recognition features
Deng et al. 2019
and contrastive learning. The decoder then maps a given latent vector into Gaussian parameter residuals for the template model. At inference time, an input image can be converted to a full 3DGS model in one feed-forward pass through the encoder and decoder.

We demonstrate FastAvatar’s utility with comprehensive quantitative and qualitative results mainly using the Nersemble test dataset
Kirschstein et al. 2023
. First, given three distinct input poses (frontal and two other extreme poses) we evaluated reconstruction quality on 15 novel viewpoints. FastAvatar achieves 21.3 dB PSNR in under
10
ms, significantly outperforming GAGAvatar
Chu and Harada 2024
(12.99 dB) while being
4,500\times
faster than optimization-based methods such as GaussianAvatars
Qian et al. 2024a
, FlashAvatar
Xiang et al. 2024
, DiffusionRig
Ding et al. 2023

and Arc2Avatar
Gerogiannis et al. 2025
. Critically, FastAvatar maintains this high quality from frontal to extreme profile views, while prior feed-forward methods show catastrophic degradation for non-frontal inputs. Furthermore, FastAvatar’s learned latent space exhibits semantic structure that enables smooth identity interpolation and controlled attribute editing, which are capabilities not demonstrated by existing 3DGS face methods. FastAvatar’s combination of high reconstruction quality and real-time speed from a single image enables new capabilities of photorealistic avatar creation in consumer and interactive application systems.

Related Work

Recovering a high-quality 3D face from images is a long-standing problem in computer vision and graphics. The seminal 3D Morphable Model (3DMM) model ushered in a wave parametric morphable face methods

;

Li et al. 2017

While 3DMM- and FLAME-based methods are computationally efficient and robust under constrained conditions, their low-rank basis assumptions limit their expressive capabilities for complex, high-frequency patterns (e.g., subtle skin variations and hair patterns).

These methods typically attach Gaussians to vertices (or surface patches) of a fitted parametric head mesh and optimize per-Gaussian parameters (means, scales, appearances) to the observed image(s). While these hybrid 3DGS approaches can produce detailed reconstructions and can fit scenes far faster than NeRFs (e.g., NeRFs often take tens of hours, whereas optimized 3DGS avatars can converge in 30 minutes to 1 hour per subject), their fitting times are still far from real-time due to their reliance on per-face iterative optimization.

In contrast to iterative optimization approaches, feed-forward methods directly map input images to outputs — either 3D models or novel views — resulting in real-time fitting speed. Several feed-forward methods directly predict parameters to NeRF or 3DGS models given one or more input images

These approaches work well on frontal or near-frontal inputs with limited pose variation, but often exhibit pose-dependent inconsistencies; reconstructions from non-frontal inputs can differ noticeably in identity and geometry compared to frontal inputs. Another family of feed-forward methods use generative models such as diffusion models

FastAvatar, the proposed algorithm in this study, integrates several ideas developed in these existing lines of work to perform high-quality, real-time novel view synthesis given only a single input face image. FastAvatar uses a feed-forward architecture to predict deformations to a template 3DGS model, taking inspiration from morphable models. The architecture also maps the input image into a pose-invariant embedding, enabling better identity-pose disentanglement given an input image from any arbitrary pose.

3
Method

Given a single face image
I\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}
taken from an arbitrary pose, our goal is to reconstruct a complete 3DGS model
\mathcal{G}
that enables high-quality novel view synthesis. We also assume a training set of
N
prior subjects with images simultaneously taken from different poses. The fundamental challenge of this problem is to learn a mapping from 2D pixels to thousands of 3D Gaussian parameters in real-time while maintaining robustness to extreme pose variations. We address this challenge with FastAvatar: a feed-forward framework that learns to deform a “template” 3DGS face model in appearance and structure to fit the subject in
I
.

In particular, FastAvatar uses an encoder-decoder design to map an input image to 3DGS parameters (Figure
2
b) with key novelties to handle the challenge described above. FastAvatar first constructs a 3DGS “template” model by averaging parameters across subject-specific 3DGS models (Figure
2
a), each initialized with Gaussians placed at consistent FLAME
Li et al. 2017
landmarks. FastAvatar’s encoder is then trained to map the input image into an identity-specific (pose-independent) latent vector, while its decoder maps this vector into per-Gaussian parameter residuals with respect to the template model. Estimating residual parameter values with respect to the template makes the learning problem tractable and fast because the template provides strong geometric and appearance priors. At inference time, FastAvatar yields a full 3DGS model for an input image in under 10ms. We now present a brief background of 3DGS, followed by further details of FastAvatar’s design.

3.1
Preliminaries: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)

A 3DGS
Kerbl et al. 2023
model
\mathcal{M}=\{G_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
is a set of
K
anisotropic Gaussian primitives that can be efficiently rendered via differentiable rasterization to provide novel views of a given scene. Each Gaussian
G_{k}
is fully characterized by its geometric and appearance parameters: position
\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
defining the center in 3D space, covariance matrix
\Sigma_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
controlling shape and orientation, opacity
\alpha_{k}\in[0,1]
determining transparency, and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients
c_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times(L+1)^{2}}
for view-dependent color, where
L
is the SH degree.

To ensure positive semi-definiteness for efficient optimization, the covariance matrix is factorized as
\Sigma_{k}=R_{k}S_{k}S_{k}^{T}R_{k}^{T}
, where
R_{k}
is a rotation matrix parameterized by quaternion
q_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
S_{k}
is a diagonal scaling matrix with scale vector
s_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. During rendering, each pixel color is computed through alpha-blending of
D
ordered Gaussians along the viewing ray:

C=\sum_{d=1}^{D}c_{d}\alpha_{d}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}(1-\alpha_{j}),

(1)

where
c_{d}
is the color contribution evaluated from SH coefficients for the viewing direction, and
\alpha_{d}
combines the Gaussian’s opacity with its 2D projection weight.

3.2
Template 3DGS Face Model Construction

Directly inferring parameters of a 3DGS model from one image is challenging due to the number of parameters involved. In our design, each Gaussian has 59 parameters: 10 for geometry (center, scale, rotation), 48 for appearance (spherical harmonic coefficients), and 1 for opacity. Instead, inspired by 3DMM
Blanz and Vetter 2023
, we design FastAvatar to simply predict the
residual deformations
needed to modify a data-driven template 3DGS model
\mathcal{T}
to fit the input image.
\mathcal{T}
encodes strong geometric priors on facial structure to reduce learning complexity.

We first optimize
N
individual 3DGS models
\{\mathcal{M}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
for each of the subjects in the training set, each model consisting of
K
Gaussians.
K
determines the balance between representation capacity for fine details and computational efficiency. We initialize the positions of these Gaussians at consistent 3D landmarks for each subject using the FLAME
Li et al. 2017
mesh to ensure a one-to-one correspondence of Gaussians across subjects when computing the average template model. After initialization, we use the standard 3DGS optimization routine
Ye et al. 2025
to generate the person-specific models. We then construct the 3DGS template model
\mathcal{T}
by simply computing the statistical average of each parameter of each Gaussian of the subject-specific models. Unlike prior work that randomly initializes or jointly optimizes the template
Saunders et al. 2025
;
Qian et al. 2024a
;
Yan et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2024
, we find the proposed approach to require fewer parameters and yield smaller deformations to fit new faces.

3.3
Encoder-Decoder Network

The core of our framework is an encoder-decoder architecture that maps a 2D input image
I
to residuals for each parameter of each Gaussian in template model
\mathcal{T}
. Given that
I
can be taken from any pose, we additionally design the encoder of this model to return a pose-agnostic latent code that only captures identity-specific information.

We found that training the encoder and decoder simultaneously in an end-to-end fashion results in a degenerate mapping that always produces an average, blurry face. Instead, we adopt a multi-stage training strategy. We first establish a well-structured latent space by only training the decoder along with per-subject optimizable latent codes. Next, we freeze the decoder and train the encoder to map images to these pretrained codes. In line with this training strategy, we first describe the FastAvatar decoder design followed by the encoder design below.

3.3.1
Decoder Design and Optimization

The decoder
h_{\theta}
takes as input a subject identity code
w\in\mathbb{R}^{|w|}
and a Gaussian identity code
e_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{|e|}
for Gaussian
k
and outputs
\Delta G_{k}=h_{\theta}(w,e_{k})
, where
\Delta G_{k}=\{\Delta\mu_{k},\Delta s_{k},\Delta q_{k},\Delta\alpha_{k},\Delta c_{k}\}
denotes parameter offsets for position, scale, rotation, the (third-degree) spherical harmonics parameters and opacity . The identity code
w
encodes subject-specific properties such as identity, expression and geometry, while Gaussian embeddings
e_{k}
provides highly localized context for a specific Gaussian. We obtain the final subject-specific 3DGS model by applying the residuals in
\Delta G_{k}
to the parameters in
\mathcal{T}
:

\displaystyle\mu_{k}

(2)

\displaystyle s_{k}

\displaystyle=s_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\exp(\Delta s_{k}),

(3)

\displaystyle q_{k}

\displaystyle=\text{norm}(q_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}+\Delta q_{k}),

(4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma(\text{logit}(\alpha_{k}^{\mathcal{T}})+\Delta\alpha_{k}),

(5)

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle=c_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}+\Delta c_{k},

(6)

\mathcal{T}

[0,1]

We implement the decoder with a shallow MLP. We initialize each Gaussian embedding
e_{k}
using a sinusoidal positional encoding of its position in the template
\mathcal{T}
. We initialize a set of random identity codes
\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

N

h_{\theta}

\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}

h_{\theta}

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSIM}}(I,\hat{I})+\lambda_{4}\mathcal{L}_{2}(\Delta\mu)+\lambda_{5}\mathcal{L}_{2}(\Delta s),

3.4
Encoder Design and Optimization

We design FastAvatar’s encoder
g_{\phi}
to predict a pose-invariant latent embedding of the face depicted in
I
such that a full 3DGS representation of the face may be inferred from this embedding by the decoder. In particular, we construct FastAvatar’s encoder to be a functional composition:
g=g_{FR}\circ g_{MLP}
, where
g_{FR}
is a pretrained face recognition model backbone and
g_{MLP}
is a shallow projection network. The face recognition model is trained on millions of images to extract identity features invariant to pose, which meets our purpose, while the lightweight MLP projects these features to a latent code
w
amenable to 3D reconstruction. Given pre-computed identity codes
\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from decoder training (see previous section), we train
g_{\phi}
to map all ground truth images of the same training subject to the same embedding using the following loss function:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{enc}}(w,\hat{w})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(w,\hat{w})+\lambda_{\text{cos}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cos}}(w,\hat{w}),

(9)

where
\hat{w}=g_{\phi}(I)
is an embedding predicted from the encoder and
\lambda_{\text{cos}}
measures cosine distance.

3.5
Inference and Optional Further Refinement

At inference time, given an input image
I
from arbitrary viewing pose, we perform a single forward pass through the FastAvatar pipeline to produce a subject-specific 3DGS model.
To further improve subject-specific fidelity, we offer an optional test-time refinement stage. Starting from the feed-forward predicted model
\mathcal{M}
, we jointly optimize the latent code
w
predicted by the encoder along with the decoder parameters.
In our experiments, we find that running this joint optimization for 300 iterations (approximately 3 seconds) works well to adapt the latent code and decoder to subject-specific details such as high-frequency facial features and accessories (See Fig.
3
. This optimization routine only affects fitting speed, while rendering speed remains equivalently fast.

3.6
Identity and Attribute Manipulation

A key advantage of FastAvatar’s learned representation is its well-structured latent space which can support real-time identity and attribute manipulation. For example, to interpolate between two identities encoded by embeddings
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, we can simply perform linear interpolation:
\tilde{w}=(1-\alpha)w_{1}+\alpha w_{2}
with
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

To edit a facial attribute (e.g., expression, hair length), we can use a latent space traversal technique most famously used with GANs
Radford et al. 2015
;
Shen et al. 2020
;
Liang et al. 2023
. For a given binary face attribute
y\in\{-1,+1\}
, we obtain a pretrained classifier to obtain a binary label per subject (the continuous case is also easily handled with a regression model). We then train a linear Support Vector Machine (SVM) to find the optimal hyperplane separating positive and negative attribute samples in the latent space. The normal vector of the hyperplane
\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\mathbf{n}/\|\mathbf{n}\|
encodes a direction in latent space highly correlated with the target attribute. To edit the attribute, we can simply traverse along this direction:
\tilde{w}=w+\lambda\hat{\mathbf{n}}
, where
\lambda
controls the magnitude of the change. The direction
\hat{\mathbf{n}}
is
global
, i.e., not identity-specific, such that it enables meaningful edits across subjects and poses.

4
Experiments & Results

We evaluated FastAvatar on single-view 3D face reconstruction using the popular Nersemble dataset
Kirschstein et al. 2023
consisting of 417 identities captured with 16 simultaneous calibrated cameras covering poses from frontal to profile views. Each identity is recorded with multiple expressions, providing challenging variations for reconstruction. We used 410 identities for training and held out 7 identities for testing.

Baselines.
We compare FastAvatar’s performance against a number of state-of-the-art methods: (1)
Optimization-based
: GaussianAvatars
Qian et al. 2024a
, FlashAvatar
Xiang et al. 2024
; (2)
Feed-forward
: GAGAvatar
Chu and Harada 2024
; (3)
Diffusion-based
: Arc2Avatar
Gerogiannis et al. 2025
, DiffusionRig
Ding et al. 2023
. We used official, publicly available implementations of all baselines with their recommended settings. For DiffusionRig, Arc2Avatar and GAGAvatar, we estimated camera intrinsics/extrinsics via Perspective-n-Point (PnP) registration of their meshes/point clouds to our template (see Supplementary).

Metrics.
We quantified reconstruction accuracy using PSNR, SSIM
Wang et al. 2004
, LPIPS
Zhang et al. 2018
, and MAE (L1 distance) metrics. For each test identity, we used one view as input and evaluated performance on the remaining 15 views, and repeat for all 16 views. All times are reported using a single NVIDIA A100 (40 GB) GPU.

Implementation Details.
We constructed the template model
\mathcal{T}
from the 410 3DGS models (one for each training subject) optimized for 5,000 iterations using all 16 views. We used VHAP
Qian 2024
to track head motion to extract FLAME parameters and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. We placed two Gaussians at each vertex position of the resulting mesh model, with scales proportional to local edge lengths and rotations aligned to surface normals, which leads to a total of
K=10,144
Gaussians. Please see Supplementary for further implementation details.

5
Discussion and Conclusion

Results show that FastAvatar significantly outperforms existing baselines in single-image 3D face reconstruction from arbitrary input poses. FastAvatar achieves higher accuracy across all metrics while operating in real-time (without additional optimization), and near real-time with additional subject-specific inference optimization. The additional optimization enhances subtle details related to expressions or textures while sacrificing roughly 2-3 seconds in fitting speed only (while having the same rendering speed).

The clear separation between FastAvatar and existing approaches suggests that these design choices — 3DGS template models, learned deformations, and view-invariant encodings — improve the quality–speed trade-off in feed-forward 3D face reconstruction, and can be crucial building blocks in virtuar avatar research moving forward. Our ablation studies further underscore the impact of the template design: simply averaging 3DGS parameters across subjects yields a stable, semantically consistent initialization that makes offset learning easier and more robust. In addition, the coherent latent space learned by FastAvatar enables attribute interpolations and traversals directly in 3DGS space—a capability not typically demonstrated in prior 3DGS avatar frameworks—and underscores the value of disentangled, view-invariant embeddings. We find that simple
linear
traversals in latent space are effective despite the highly nonlinear mapping from Gaussian parameters (positions, scales, appearance SHs) to pixels; we hypothesize that implicit biases of the shallow FastAvatar decoder encourage a simple, low-dimensional structure conducive to such linear edits.

This work has a few limitations. First, our training set exhibits demographic imbalance, with an over-representation of middle-aged Caucasian males and under-representation of women, elderly, and diverse ethnic backgrounds. Second, FastAvatar does not explicitly model hair, clothing, or lighting: long hairstyles, fine hair strands, clothing details, and illumination variations tend to be smoothed out, especially during template construction. These factors may be important in applications requiring fine-grained head-and-shoulder reconstructions. This challenge is common in related 3D avatar reconstruction works
Saunders et al. 2025
;
Qian et al. 2024a
;
Chu and Harada 2024
and is an active area of future research in the community. Overall,
FastAvatar establishes a new standard for real-time, single-image 3D face reconstruction
significantly pushing the frontier of reconstruction quality and fitting speed compared to existing works.

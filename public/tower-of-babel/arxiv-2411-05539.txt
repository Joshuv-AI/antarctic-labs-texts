2.
Ditkin sets for some functional spaces

Throughout this work we will assume that
G
is a locally compact Abelian group with dual group
\widehat{G}
and Haar measures
d\mu
and
\hat{d}\mu
respectively.

Assume that
X
is a Banach ideal in
L^{1}_{\omega}(G).
In the spirit of
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
7
]
we call a closed set
E\subset\widehat{G}
is a Ditkin set for
X
if for every
f\in X
such that the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
of the function
f
vanishes on
E
can be approximated in
X
with functions
f\ast F
such that
\hat{F}
vanishes in some neighbourhood on
E.

Authors also used names such as C-set and Wiener-Ditkin set in some sources.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
L^{1}_{\omega}(G)
be a Beurling algebra and let
X
be a Banach ideal in
L^{1}_{\omega}(G).
Then every Ditkin set for
X
is a Ditkin set for
L^{1}_{\omega}(G).

Proof.

Since
X
is a Banach ideal in
L^{1}_{\omega}(G),X
is continuously embedded in
L^{1}_{\omega}(G).
Then there exists
C>0
such that
\|.\|_{1,w}\leq C\|.\|_{X}.
Assume that a closed subset
E\subset\widehat{G}
is a Ditkin set for
X.
It is known that the Beurling’s algebra
L^{1}_{\omega}(G),
admits a bounded approximate identity
(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I},
(see
[
14
]
,
[
9
]
). Let
f\in X
such that
\hat{f}
vanishes on
E,
and let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Then there exists
\alpha_{0}\in I
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|f-f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{0}}\|_{1,w}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Here
f\ast e_{\alpha_{0}}\in X
and by the properties of Fourier transform,
(f\ast e_{\alpha_{0}}\widehat{)}=\hat{f}\hat{e}_{\alpha_{0}}=0
on
E.
Since
E
is a Ditkin set for
X,
there exists
F_{1}\in X
such that
\hat{F}_{1}
vanishes on a neighbourhood of
E
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{0}}-F_{1}\ast(f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{0}})\|_{X}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2C}.

If we set
F=F_{1}\ast e_{\alpha_{0}},
we have
F\in X
and
\hat{F}=0
on a neighbourhood of
E.
Thus from (2.1) and (2.2) we obtain

\displaystyle\|f-f\ast F\|_{1,w}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{0}}\|_{1,w}+\|f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{0}}-f\ast F\|_{1,w}

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+C\|f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{0}}-F_{1}\ast(f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{0}})\|_{X}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+C\frac{\varepsilon}{2C}=\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
L^{1}_{\omega}(G)
be a Beurling algebra and let
X

Then every Ditkin set for
L^{1}_{\omega}(G)

Proof.

Assume that a closed subset

is a Ditkin set for

Also let

\alpha\in I

[
14
]
,
[

]

is an essential Banach ideal in

then for every

(2.3)

\displaystyle lim_{I}g\ast e_{\alpha}=g

in X, (see Corollary 15.3 in
[
2
]
).
Let

such that
\hat{f}
vanishes on
E,
and let
\varepsilon>0
be given. From (2.3), there exists
\alpha_{1}\in I
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|f-f\ast\ e_{\alpha_{1}}\|_{X}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Since
E
is a Ditkin set for
L^{1}_{\omega}(G),
there also exists

such that
\hat{F}_{1}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|f-f\ast{F_{1}}\|_{1,w}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2M}.

If we set

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume that
X
is an essential Banach ideal in
L^{1}_{\omega}(G).
Then a closed subset
E\subset\widehat{G}
is a Ditkin set for
X
if and only if
E
is a Ditkin set for
L^{1}_{\omega}(G).

Proof.

The proof is clear from Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.2.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Assume that
X
is an essential Banach ideal in
L^{1}(G).
Then closed subgroups of
\widehat{G}
are Ditkin sets for
X.

Proof.

It is known by Theorem 7.4.1 in
[
12
]
that closed subgroups of
\widehat{G}
are Ditkin sets for
L^{1}(G).
Thus by Corollary 2.3, closed subgroups of
\widehat{G}
are Ditkin sets for
X
.
∎

3.
Applications

3.1.
Ditkin sets for the generalized grand Lebesgue space
L^{p),\theta}(G)

In this part we will investigate the Ditkin sets for generalized grand Lebesgue space
L^{p),\theta}(G).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Abelian group with dual group
\widehat{G}
and Haar measures
d\mu
and
\hat{d}\mu
respectively. If
\mu(G)
is finite, then every Ditkin set for the generalized grand Lebesgue space
L^{p),\theta}(G)
is a Ditkin set for
L^{1}(G).

Proof.

Since
\mu(G)
is finite, then
G
is compact (see Theorem 4 in
[
10
]
). It is known by Theorem 5 in
[
5
]
that the generalized grand Lebesgue space
L^{p),\theta}(G)
is a Banach convolution module over
L^{1}(G).
It is also proved in Theorem 4 in
[
5
]
that the generalized grand Lebesgue space
L^{p),\theta}(G)
does not admit an approximate identity, bounded in
L^{1}(G).
Thus
L^{p),\theta}(G)
is not essential Banach module over
L^{1}(G)
by Corollary 15.3 in
[
2
]
. Since we have the embedding
L^{p),\theta}(G)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(G),
then
L^{p),\theta}(G)
is a Banach ideal in
L^{1}(G)
but not essential. Finally from Thorem 2.1 we observe that every Ditkin set for
L^{p),\theta}(G)
is a Ditkin set for
L^{1}(G)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Abelian group with dual group
\widehat{G}
and Haar measures
d\mu
and
\hat{d}\mu
respectively. Assume that
\mu(G)
is finite. It is known by Theorem 7.4.1 in
[
12
]
that closed subgroups of
\widehat{G}
are Ditkin sets for
L^{1}(G).
Thus by Theorem 3.1, every Ditkin set for the generalized grand Lebesgue space
L^{p),\theta}(G)
is a closed subgroups of
\widehat{G}.

3.2.
Ditkin sets for the space
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}

It is known that the subspace
{C_{c}}(\Omega)
of the space
L^{p),\theta}\left(\Omega\right)
is not dense in
L^{p),\theta}\left(\Omega\right)
. Its closure
[L^{p}(\Omega)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
consists of functions
f\in L^{p)}\left(\Omega\right)
such that

(3.1)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{\theta}{p-\varepsilon}}\left\|f\right\|_{p-\varepsilon}=0,

(see
[
1
]
).

Now we will discuss the Ditkin sets for the space
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Abelian group with dual group
\widehat{G}
and Haar measures
d\mu
and
\hat{d}\mu
respectively. Assume that
\mu(G)
is finite. Then a closed subset
E\subset\widehat{G}
is a Ditkin set for
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
if and only if
E
is a Ditkin set for
L^{1}(G).

Proof.

Since
\mu(G)
is finite then
G
is compact. It is known by Theorem 6 in
[
5
]
that
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
admits an approximate identity bounded in
L^{1}(G).
Also it is known by Theorem 5 in
[
5
]
that
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
is Banach convolution module over
L^{1}(G).
Since
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}\hookrightarrow L^{1}(G),
from Theorem 5 in
[
5
]
,
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
is a Banach ideal in
L^{1}(G).
Then
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
is essential Banach convolution ideal in
L^{1}(G)
by Corollary 15.3 in
[
2
]
. Finally by Corollary 2.3, a closed subset
E\subset\widehat{G}
is a Ditkin set for
[L^{p}(G)]_{L^{p),\theta}}
if and only if
E
is a Ditkin set for
L^{1}(G).

∎

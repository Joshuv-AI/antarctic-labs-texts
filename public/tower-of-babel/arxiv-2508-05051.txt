Betti Numbers and Formal Local Cohomology Modules behruz.sadeqi@iau.ac.ir
Behruz Sadeqi

Abstract
This article investigates the relationship between Betti numbers of finitely generated modules over a Noetherian local ring ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,\mathfrak{m}) and the structure of formal local cohomology modules. We establish a connection between the vanishing of formal local cohomology and the depth of a module, generalizing classical results. The main theorem links the Betti numbers to the Artinian property of formal local cohomology modules, and we provide applications to Cohen-Macaulay rings. Original proofs are given for all stated results.

1
Introduction

Betti numbers and local cohomology are fundamental tools in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. While Betti numbers
\beta_{i}(M)
measure the complexity of a module
M
via its minimal free resolution, local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
capture local properties at an ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Formal local cohomology, introduced by P. Schenzel, generalizes this by considering inverse limits of local cohomology modules of quotients
M/\mathfrak{a}^{n}M
.

This article explores the relationship between these invariants. We prove that for a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, the formal local cohomology modules
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
vanish below the depth of
M
and are Artinian in general. Moreover, we establish a duality between the Betti numbers of
M
and the Bass numbers of
\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
when
R
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d
.

Throughout,
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
denotes a Noetherian local ring, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module.

Definition 1.1

(Betti Numbers)
.

The
i
-th Betti number of
M
is
\beta_{i}(M)=dim_{k}\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(k,M)
. These are the ranks of the free modules in a minimal free resolution of
M
.

Definition 1.2

(Local Cohomology)
.

The local cohomology modules of
M
with support in
\mathfrak{m}
are defined as:

H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)=\varprojlim_{n}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a}^{n},M).

Definition 1.3

(Formal Local Cohomology)
.

The formal local cohomology modules of
M
are:

\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M).

Remark 1.4
.

Unlike ordinary local cohomology,
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
depends on the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology. When
i=0
,
\mathfrak{F}^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\widehat{M}
, the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion.

2
Main Results

We first establish vanishing properties of formal local cohomology related to the depth of
M
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
t=\operatorname{depth}M
. Then:

1.

\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for all
i<t
.

2.

\mathfrak{F}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\neq 0
.

Proof 2.2
.

(i) For
i<t
,
\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(k,M)=0
for all
j<t
. Consider the spectral sequence:

E^{p,q}_{2}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{q}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M))\implies\operatorname{Ext}^{p+q}_{R}(k,M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M).

Since
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{q}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)
is
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion,
E^{p,q}_{2}=0
for
p>0
.
Thus,
E^{0,q}_{2}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{q}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M))
converges to
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{R}(k,M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)
. For
q<t
,
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{R}(k,M)=0
, so
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{R}(k,M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
for all
n
. Hence,
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{q}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M))=0
, implying
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{q}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
for all
n
. Thus,
\mathfrak{F}^{q}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\varprojlim_{n}0=0
.

(ii) For
i=t
,
\operatorname{Ext}^{t}_{R}(k,M)\neq 0
. The isomorphism
\operatorname{Ext}^{t}_{R}(k,M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M))
and the left-exactness of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(k,-)
yield
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M)\neq 0
. The exact sequences:

0\to\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\to M\to M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\to 0

induce maps
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M)\to H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)
. Since
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M)\neq 0
and the system is Mittag-Leffler,
\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)\neq 0
.

We now relate formal local cohomology to Betti numbers.

Theorem 2.3
.

\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
is Artinian for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof 2.4
.

Each
M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
has finite length, so
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)
is Artinian (as
R/\mathfrak{m}^{n}
-modules). The inverse limit of Artinian modules is Artinian.

The Artinian property allows us to link Betti numbers to the structure of
\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
when
R
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose
R
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d
with canonical module
\omega_{R}
. Then:

\beta_{i}(M)=dim_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\left(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),\omega_{R}\right)\quad\text{for all }i\geq 0.

Proof 2.6
.

By local duality,
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-i}_{R}(M,\omega_{R}),E(k))
, where
E(k)
is the injective hull of
k
. For
\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
, we have:

\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)\cong\varprojlim_{n}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M,\omega_{R}),E(k)).

Since
\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M,\omega_{R})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M,\omega_{R})
, and
\omega_{R}
is finitely generated, passing to limits gives:

\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})},E(k)).

The minimal free resolution of
M
dualizes to a resolution of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})
, so
\beta_{i}(M)=\mu_{i}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R}))
. The result follows by applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,E(k))
and noting that
\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})}/\mathfrak{m}\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})\otimes_{R}k
.

For Cohen-Macaulay modules, we refine Theorem
2.1
.

Corollary 2.7
.

If
M
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d
, then
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for
i\neq d
, and
\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\neq 0
.

Proof 2.8
.

For a Cohen-Macaulay module,
\operatorname{depth}M=dimM=d
. Apply Theorem
2.1
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
R=k[[x,y]]
and
M=R/(xy)
. Then
\operatorname{depth}M=1
,
dimM=1
. We compute:

\mathfrak{F}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M/(x^{n},y^{n})M).

Since
M/(x^{n},y^{n})M\cong k[x,y]/(xy,x^{n},y^{n})
, its local cohomology is concentrated in degree 1, and
\mathfrak{F}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\neq 0
. Betti numbers are
\beta_{0}=1
,
\beta_{1}=1
,
\beta_{i}=0
for
i\geq 2
, consistent with Theorem
2.5
.

This section presents additional original results connecting Betti numbers, formal local cohomology, and homological invariants of modules. We provide complete proofs and illustrative examples.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be Cohen-Macaulay with
\dim R=d
, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. The following are equivalent:

1.

\operatorname{projdim}M<\infty

2.

\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
has finite length for all
i

3.

\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
is injective

Proof 2.11
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): If
\operatorname{projdim}M=p<\infty
, consider the minimal free resolution:

0\to R^{b_{p}}\to\cdots\to R^{b_{0}}\to M\to 0

For each
n
,
M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
has a filtration with quotients
\mathfrak{m}^{k}M/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}M
. Since
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(k,M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
for
i>p
,
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)
has finite length. The inverse limit
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)
then has finite length by the Mittag-Leffler condition.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): When
R
is Cohen-Macaulay,
\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
is Artinian by Theorem 2.3. Finite length Artinian modules over local rings are injective.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): If
\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
is injective, apply local duality:

\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})},E(k))

The injectivity implies
\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})}
is flat, hence free since
R
is local. Thus
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})
is free, so
M
has finite projective dimension.

Definition 2.12

(Lyubeznik Numbers)
.

For a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
, the Lyubeznik numbers are
\lambda_{i,j}(R)=\dim_{k}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{j}(R))
.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
R
be a complete Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
. Then:

\beta_{i}(\omega_{R})=\sum_{j=0}^{\dim R}\lambda_{i,j}(R)

Proof 2.14
.

Consider the Grothendieck spectral sequence:

E^{p,q}_{2}=\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{q}(R))\implies\operatorname{Ext}^{p+q}_{R}(k,R)

Since
R
is Cohen-Macaulay,
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{q}(R)=0
for
q\neq d=\dim R
. The spectral sequence collapses, giving:

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(k,R)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i-d}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(R))

By local duality,
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(k,R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(k,\omega_{R}),k)
, so:

\dim_{k}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(k,R)=\beta_{i}(\omega_{R})

Combining these isomorphisms:

\beta_{i}(\omega_{R})=\dim_{k}\operatorname{Ext}^{i-d}_{R}(k,H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(R))=\lambda_{i-d,d}(R)

The result follows by noting that
\lambda_{i,j}(R)=0
for
j\neq d
.

Proposition 2.15
.

For any finitely generated
R
-module
M
:

\operatorname{depth}M=\min\{i\mid\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\neq 0\}=\max\{i\mid\mathfrak{F}^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0\ \mathfrak{F}orallj<i\}

Proof 2.16
.

By Theorem 2.1,
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for
i<\operatorname{depth}M
and
\mathfrak{F}^{\operatorname{depth}M}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\neq 0
. Suppose
\operatorname{depth}M=t
. If
\mathfrak{F}^{t}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
, then
\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
. But
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)
surjects onto
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M)
, so
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
for large
n
. This contradicts
\operatorname{depth}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=t
for all
n
.

Example 2.17

(Hypersurface Singularity)
.

Let
R=k[[x,y,z]]/(xy-z^{2})
with
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y,z)
. Then
\dim R=2
and
\operatorname{depth}R=2
. Compute formal cohomology:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}\cong R

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)

\displaystyle=\varprojlim_{n}H^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{n})

The minimal free resolution of
R
is:

0\to R\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}z\\
-x\\
y\end{pmatrix}}R^{3}\xrightarrow{(y,z,x)}R\to R/(xy-z^{2})\to 0

Betti numbers:
\beta_{0}=1,\beta_{1}=3,\beta_{2}=1
. Lyubeznik numbers:
\lambda_{0,2}=1,\lambda_{1,2}=0,\lambda_{2,2}=1
. By Theorem 4.2:

\beta_{i}(\omega_{R})=\beta_{i}(R)=\lambda_{i,2}(R)

Example 2.18

(Non-Cohen-Macaulay Ring)
.

Let
R=k[[x,y,z]]/(xz,yz)
with
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y,z)
. Then
\dim R=2
,
\operatorname{depth}R=1
. Formal cohomology:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}\cong R

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)

\displaystyle=\varprojlim_{n}H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{n})

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)

\displaystyle=\varprojlim_{n}H^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{n})\neq 0

Minimal resolution:

0\to R^{2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\
-x&z\\
0&-x\end{pmatrix}}R^{3}\xrightarrow{(z,0,y)}R\to R/(xz,yz)\to 0

Betti numbers:
\beta_{0}=1,\beta_{1}=3,\beta_{2}=2
. Applying Proposition 4.3:

\operatorname{depth}R=1=\min\{i\mid\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\neq 0\}=\min\{1,2\}

since
\mathfrak{F}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
and
\mathfrak{F}^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
are both nonzero.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be complete Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d
,
M
finitely generated. Then:

\operatorname{id}\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\operatorname{projdim}M

Proof 2.20
.

By local duality and completion:

\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})},E(k))

Since
R
is complete,
\widehat{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})
. Thus:

\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R}),E(k))

The injective dimension of the right-hand side equals the projective dimension of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})
, which equals
\operatorname{projdim}M
since
\omega_{R}
is dualizing.

Proposition 2.21
.

For any finitely generated
R
-module
M
:

\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0\quad\text{for all }i>\dim R

Proof 2.22
.

For each
n
,
\dim(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
, so
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
for
i>0
. Thus
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)=0
for
i>\dim R
.

Theorem 2.23
.

For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
:

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M\mid dimR/\mathfrak{p}=d\}

where
d=dimM
.

Proof 2.24
.

Consider the short exact sequences:

0\to\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)\to M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\to B_{n}\to 0

where
B_{n}
has no
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion. Then
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)\cong H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(B_{n})
. Since
\Supp B_{n}\subseteq\Supp M
, and
\dim B_{n}\leq d
, we have
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(B_{n})\neq 0
iff
\dim B_{n}=d
. Taking inverse limits:

\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\varprojlim_{n}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(B_{n})

The associated primes correspond to minimal primes of dimension
d
in
\Supp M
.

Summary of Results

We established:

•

Vanishing of
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
below
\operatorname{depth}M
(Theorem
2.1
).

•

Artinianness of
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
(Theorem
2.3
).

•

Duality between Betti numbers and
\mathfrak{F}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
for Cohen-Macaulay rings (Theorem
2.5
).

•

Characterization of finite projective dimension via formal cohomology (Theorem
2.10
)

•

Lyubeznik-Betti number relations (Theorem
2.13
)

•

Depth characterization through formal cohomology (Proposition
2.15
)

•

Injective dimension duality (Theorem
2.19
)

•

Vanishing beyond dimension (Proposition
2.21
)

•

Associated primes identification (Theorem
2.23
)

Future work could extend these results to non-maximal ideals or investigate numerical invariants of
\mathfrak{F}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
in non-Cohen-Macaulay cases.

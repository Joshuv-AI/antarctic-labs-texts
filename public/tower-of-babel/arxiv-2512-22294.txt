A Three-Level Alignment Framework for Large-Scale 3D Retrieval and Controlled 4D Generation Thanks: Corresponding author.
Philip Xu, David Elizondo, Raouf Hamzaoui

Abstract
This paper introduces Uni4D, a comprehensive framework for large-scale open-vocabulary 3D retrieval and 4D generation based on a structured three-level alignment across text, 3D models, and image modalities. Built on the extensive Align3D-130 dataset, Uni4D employs a 3D-Text Multi-head Attention and Search (ATMS) model, optimizing text-to-3D retrieval through enhanced semantic alignment. The framework further strengthens alignment by incorporating (i) precise text-to-3D retrieval, (ii) multi-view 3D-to-image alignment, and (iii) image-to-text alignment for generating temporally consistent 4D assets. Experimental results highlight Uni4D’s effectiveness in achieving high-quality 3D retrieval and controlled 4D generation, advancing capabilities in dynamic multimodal understanding and application.

To further enhance the quality of the dataset, we utilized GPT-4o to generate approximately 1.3 million high-quality text descriptions. This approach not only addressed the lack of text annotations in the original datasets but also significantly improved semantic consistency and accuracy. Compared to traditional automated annotation methods, GPT-4 excels at generating semantically rich, contextually relevant descriptions. We also implemented a multi-round generation and human review strategy to ensure that the generated descriptions accurately reflect the characteristics of the 3D models and to screen out harmful 3D models, preventing data contamination.

The Align3D-130 dataset contains over 1.3 million single-object 3D assets, ranging from simple geometric shapes to complex mechanical structures. This scale and diversity ensure the datasets applicability across a wide range of scenarios, meeting various research needs. Additionally, the multimodal nature of the datasetwhere each 3D model is paired with a high-quality text descriptiongives it a unique advantage in multimodal learning and cross-modal retrieval tasks, advancing 3D model understanding and generation.

Furthermore, we developed a GPU-accelerated 3D model conversion method that quickly converts common 3D model formats, such as GLB and OBJ, into point clouds. By leveraging parallel computation, this method significantly reduces model conversion and point cloud encoding time, accelerating the model inference and training process. This not only improves data processing efficiency but also provides technical support for large-scale dataset construction, enhancing Align3D-130s efficiency and scalability in handling extensive 3D models and ensuring compatibility with our ATMS model discussed later.

In the data preprocessing stage, we applied Euclidean norm normalization
26
to the feature vectors of the 3D models to ensure consistency when comparing features. The text data was cleaned to improve the models processing efficiency. To train the model without explicit labels, we employed a self-supervised contrastive learning framework. By constructing positive and negative sample pairs (pairing the correct 3D model with the text as a positive sample and pairing incorrect 3D models with the text as negative samples), the model learns to pull positive pairs closer and push negative pairs further apart in the embedding space. The core contrastive loss function is as follows:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{contrastive}}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\frac{\exp\left(\text{sim}\left(z_{\text{text}_{i}},z_{\text{3D}_{i}}\right)/\tau\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp\left(\text{sim}\left(z_{\text{text}_{i}},z_{\text{3D}_{j}}\right)/\tau\right)}

Where
\boldsymbol{\tau}
represents our temperature hyperparameter

This approach enables the model to learn the alignment between text and 3D models with only a limited amount of manual annotations.This approach also reduces the burden of sample annotation, enabling more efficient alignment between text annotations and 3D data.

3.3
RL strategy based on Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) during training:

We utilize a Monte Carlo Tree-based reinforcement learning strategy during training to optimize ATMS training process.

Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) is a decision-making optimization method based on random sampling, widely used in reinforcement learning and games. Since its introduction by Coulom (2006), MCTS has been built around a core framework of selection, expansion, simulation, and backpropagation, addressing complex problems by balancing exploration and exploitation (Browne et al., 2012). In the AlphaGo series
33
, MCTS was combined with deep neural networks to guide the search process, significantly improving the performance of Go-playing algorithms. Recently, Moerland et al.
25
leveraged the state representation capabilities of deep reinforcement learning to further enhance the search performance of MCTS, opening up new directions for tackling complex continuous control tasks. Additionally, Liu et al. (2023) proposed the WU-UCT algorithm
22
, which achieves linear speedup with negligible performance loss by tracking unfinished simulation queries, significantly improving the efficiency of MCTS. The success of MCTS lies in its deep integration with deep learning and reinforcement learning, showcasing broad application prospects.

In the context of reinforcement learning, the training process is viewed as an environment where the state and actions collectively determine the dynamic adjustment of the model’s parameters. The state
s_{t}
at time step
t
includes a set of information, such as the current learning rate
\text{lr}_{t}
, epoch
\text{epoch}_{t}
, the temperature
\text{temperature}_{t}
used in contrastive loss, and performance metrics on the training and validation sets. These metrics include the current validation accuracy
\text{val\_acc}_{t}
, previous validation accuracy
\text{prev\_val\_acc}_{t}
, previous validation loss
\text{prev\_val\_loss}_{t}
, previous training accuracy
\text{prev\_train\_acc}_{t}
, previous training loss
\text{prev\_train\_loss}_{t}
, and the current average reward
\text{avg\_reward}_{t}
. This collection of state information reflects the model’s current training status.

The action
a_{t}
is selected from a predefined action space, which includes operations such as increasing the learning rate
lr_up
, decreasing the learning rate
lr_down
, making no changes
no_change
, increasing the temperature
temp_up
, and decreasing the temperature
temp_down
. At each time step
t
, based on the current state
s_{t}
and the chosen action
a_{t}
, the model’s hyperparameters are adjusted accordingly, influencing the subsequent training process. Through this iterative procedure, reinforcement learning continuously optimizes the training process to improve the model’s performance.

Reward Function:
At time step
t
, after taking action
a_{t}
, the reward function
r_{t}
evaluates how the model’s performance changes, focusing on improvements in validation accuracy and loss, while applying penalties to prevent overfitting and control for large gradients. The reward function is defined as:

\displaystyle r_{t}=

\displaystyle\;0.3\times\Delta\text{Val\_Acc}_{t}+0.2\times\Delta\text{Val\_Loss}_{t}

\displaystyle+0.1\times\mathbb{I}(\text{Val\_Acc}_{t}>\text{Best\_Val\_Acc})-\text{Penalty}_{t}

Specifically, for the penalty function, we constructed it with two components: gradient-based penalties and overfitting penalties.The specific formula is as follows:

\text{Penalty}_{\text{overfit}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0.5\times(\Delta\text{Train\_Acc}_{t}-\Delta\text{Val\_Acc}_{t})\\
&\quad\textit{if }\Delta\text{Train\_Acc}_{t}>0\textit{ and }\Delta\text{Val\_Acc}_{t}<0\\[10.0pt]
&0\\
&\quad\textit{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

\Delta\text{Train\_Acc}_{t}=\text{Train\_Acc}_{t}-\text{Prev\_Train\_Acc}_{t}

\text{Penalty}_{\text{grad}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0.1\times(\|\nabla_{t}\|-\text{Threshold})\\
&\quad\textit{if }\|\nabla_{t}\|>\text{Threshold}\\[10.0pt]
&0\\
&\quad\textit{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

\|\nabla_{t}\|=\sqrt{\sum_{i}\|\nabla_{t}^{(i)}\|_{2}^{2}}

\nabla_{t}^{(i)}
is the gradient of the
i
-th parameter of the model. The threshold is a preset value (e.g., 1.0).

The sum of the penalty function is expressed as
\text{Penalty}_{t}=\text{Penalty}_{\text{overfit}}+\text{Penalty}_{\text{grad}}
, meaning that at each time step
t
, the total penalty is the combination of these two terms.By simultaneously penalizing overfitting and excessive gradient descent, we achieved stable learning rates while avoiding issues of gradient explosion and overfitting.

The objective of the reinforcement learning agent is to maximize the cumulative reward by selecting the optimal actions
a_{t}
.This objective is represented as
\max_{\{a_{t}\}}\sum_{t}r_{t}
, which means the agent aims to choose a sequence of actions that maximizes the total reward
r_{t}
at each time step.

Monte Carlo Tree Search:
In our method, Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) is used to efficiently explore the action space and select the action that maximizes the expected reward. MCTS consists of four stages:
Selection
, where the child node with the highest UCT value is recursively chosen until an unexplored node is found;
Expansion
, where a new child node is generated for nodes that are not fully expanded;
Simulation
, where a random policy is used to simulate from the new node until a terminal state or maximum depth is reached; and
Backpropagation
, where the simulation result is propagated back up the tree, updating each node’s visit count and total reward.

Selection with UCT:
During the Selection phase, nodes are recursively selected based on the Upper Confidence Bound (UCT) formula to balance exploration and exploitation:

\text{UCT}(v_{i})=\frac{W_{i}}{N_{i}}+c\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2\ln N_{p}}{N_{i}}},

where
v_{i}
is the
i
-th child node,
W_{i}
is the total reward accumulated by
v_{i}
,
N_{i}
is the number of visits to
v_{i}
,
N_{p}
is the number of visits to the parent node, and
c
is the exploration parameter (e.g.,
c=1.4
). In the code, the selection weight for each child node is calculated as:

\text{Weight}_{i}=\frac{\text{TotalReward}_{i}}{\text{Visits}_{i}}+c\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(\text{ParentVisits})}{\text{Visits}_{i}}},

3.4
Three-level alignment of text, 3D, and image

Since high-quality 4D generation requires comprehensive control over text, image, and corresponding 3D model information, we propose a three-level alignment of text, 3D, and image modalities using the ATMS(3D-Text Multi-head Attention & Search) and CAM(CrOSSMA) models. This alignment provides a novel approach for integrating the three distinct embedding spaces.This alignment is governed by three distinct loss functions that integrate the three embedding spaces, thereby enhancing the quality of text-to-4D generation. The overall alignment loss is defined as:

S_{\text{total}}=\alpha_{1}S_{\text{text-3D}}+\alpha_{2}S_{\text{3D-image}}+\alpha_{3}S_{\text{image-text}}

where
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
, and
\alpha_{3}
are weighting coefficients for each alignment loss, and each subterm is calculated based on the cosine similarity of its respective two components.

Text-to-3D Alignment:
We first leverage the text-to-3D retrieval capability of the ATMS model to achieve alignment between text and 3D assets. For raw text inputs with temporal information, such as ’A red bag of potato chips is spinning,’ we apply a customized tokenization process to extract key terms describing the object (e.g., ’A red bag of potato chips’). This extracted hot text is then fed into the ATMS model, where we compute the corresponding text embeddings and retrieve the top-k most similar 3D models using cosine similarity, achieving high-quality text-to-3D alignment.

3D-to-Image Alignment:
Next, we process the 3D models by capturing multiple photos of each sample, creating a set of images to achieve alignment between 3D models and images. Specifically, we use Blender and Xorg on a headless Linux server to render and photograph our 3D models, saving multi-angle images for controlling the subsequent 4D generation.

Image-to-Text Alignment:
The final layer of alignment between images and text is achieved using our CAM (CrOSSMA) model, which aligns the encoding of Blender-rendered images with the original text input. CAM is constructed with a cross-attention mechanism followed by a linear layer: the text is encoded via the CLIP model to obtain text embeddings, which serve as the query, while image embeddings, obtained by encoding the images with CLIP, act as the keys and values in the cross-attention layer. The outputs are then forwarded through an FFN (feed-forward network). The multimodal embeddings obtained from this alignment layer are subsequently fed into video generation models to generate temporally coherent videos, which are further used in 4D Gaussian processing.

Temporal Video Generation:
Using the aligned images and text from our previous three-level alignment, we use video generation models (such as CongVideoX
38
and Kling et al.) to generate temporally coherent videos that match the sequential information in the text, supporting subsequent 4D generation. These models effectively aligns text-based temporal information with video frames, enabling the generation of high-quality videos that conform to both image and text content. This completes the latent alignment among text, image, and video. Next, we use the generated video as part of the input to the 4D Gaussian model to guide 4D generation.

3.5
4D Generation

Utilizing the embeddings from the three-layer alignment, Uni4D generates the target 4D model through a 4D Gaussian process. The models state at a given time
t
,
\mathbf{M}(t)
, is represented as:

\mathbf{M}(t)=G_{\text{4D}}(\mathbf{x},t|z_{\text{text}},z_{\text{3D}},z_{\text{image}},z_{\text{video}})

Here, the Gaussian process takes embeddings from text, 3D models, images, and videos as conditional inputs, with the generation guided by the overall alignment score
S_{\text{total}}
.

Conclusion

In this work, we introduced Uni4D, a framework that advances large-scale 3D retrieval and 4D generation through a novel three-level alignment strategy across text, 3D models, and images. By addressing the challenges of cross-modal alignment and efficient retrieval, Uni4D achieves state-of-the-art performance, enhancing capabilities for open-vocabulary applications in dynamic, multimodal environments. Despite its promising results, the framework faces limitations in computational efficiency due to the large scale of data and complexity in multimodal alignment. Future work will focus on optimizing the models efficiency and exploring adaptive learning techniques to better handle diverse real-world scenarios, thereby extending the reach of 3D and 4D understanding in complex spatiotemporal contexts.

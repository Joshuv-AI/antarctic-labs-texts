Computing Linear Combinations of φ \varphi -Function Actions for Exponential Integrators
Awad H. Al-Mohy

Abstract
We propose a matrix-free algorithm for evaluating linear combinations of φ \varphi -function actions, w i := ∑ j = 0 p α i j ​ φ j ​ ( t i ​ A ) ​ v j w_{i}:=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\alpha_{i}^{\,j}\,\varphi_{j}(t_{i}A)v_{j} for i = 1 : r i=1\colon r , arising in exponential integrators. The method combines the scaling and recovering method with a truncated Taylor series, choosing a spectral shift and a scaling parameter by minimizing a power-based objective of the shifted operator. Accuracy is user-controlled and ultimately limited by the working precision. The algorithm decouples the stage abscissae t i t_{i} from the polynomial weights α i j \alpha_{i}^{j} , and a block variant enables simultaneous evaluation of { w i } i = 1 r \{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r} . Across standard benchmarks, including stiff and highly nonnormal matrices, the algorithm attains near-machine accuracy (IEEE double precision in our tests) for small step sizes and maintains reliable accuracy for larger steps where several existing Krylov-based algorithms deteriorate, providing a favorable balance of reliability and computational cost.

In this section we present the theoretical framework of our algorithm for computing the linear combinations (

We begin with the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let

(2.2)

(2.3)

,
2\leq j\leq p+1
.
Consequently, we have
\mathrm{e}^{A}v_{0}=AF_{s}(:,1)+v_{0}

Proof.

We have
e^{z}=\left(\mathrm{e}^{z/s}\right)^{s}
for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
and any natural number
s
.
Applying the linear operator
\mathcal{D}
defined in
[
1

at the ordered pair
(A,J)
in direction

using the chain rule
[
1
, Lem. 1.2 (6)]

\mathcal{D}_{\exp}(A,J,V)=\mathcal{D}_{z^{s}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{A/s},\mathrm{e}^{J/s},S\right),

(2.4)

where
S=\mathcal{D}_{\exp}(A/s,J/s,V/s)
. We have

\mathrm{e}^{z}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}/k!
.
Thus,

S=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{z^{k}}\left(A/s,J/s,V/s\right)/k!.

Since
z^{k}=z\cdot z^{k-1}
, we have

D_{k}:=\mathcal{D}_{z^{k}}\left(A/s,J/s,V/s\right)=\left(\frac{A}{s}\right)\mathcal{D}_{z^{k-1}}+\left(\frac{V}{s}\right)\left(\frac{J}{s}\right)^{k-1},

obtained from the product rule of the operator

. Thus, the product rule applied again to the right hand side of (

) yields

\mathcal{D}_{\exp}(A,J,V)=\mathrm{e}^{A/s}\mathcal{D}_{z^{s-1}}+S\left(\mathrm{e}^{J/s}\right)^{s-1}=:F_{s}

It remains to clarify the relation (
2.3
). By
[
1

,
\mathcal{D}_{\exp}(A,J,V)
is the
(1,2)
block of

\exp\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&V\\
0&J\end{bmatrix}\right)
. Reblocking the matrix as

\begin{bmatrix}A&V\\
0&J\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A&v_{0}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}&\begin{matrix}v_{p}&v_{p-1}&\cdots&v_{1}\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\\
0&J_{p}(0)\end{bmatrix},

taking the exponential of both sides, and applying
[
3
, Thm. 2.1]
on the right hand side for the matrix

\begin{bmatrix}A&v_{0}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

and the vectors
\begin{bmatrix}v_{j}\\
0\end{bmatrix}

\varphi_{\ell}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&v_{0}\\

This theorem leads to the following important corollary.

Corollary 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
with the matrix
J

\alpha\,J

\alpha\,J=\Delta^{-1}J\Delta

∎

3
Error analysis and parameter selection

Theorem
1
forms the basis for our algorithm. We use a truncated Taylor series,
T_{m}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}z^{k}/k!
, to approximate the exponential function
\mathrm{e}^{z}

s

S

as small as possible. Al-Mohy and Higham

propose shifting the diagonal entries of the matrix

is accessible via matrix-vector product only, the evaluation of

Therefore we develop a new approach to shift

\left\|\mathrm{e}^{A/s}v-T_{m-1}(A/s)v\right\|\leq\frac{\|A^{m}v\|}{s^{m}m!}+\left\|\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{A^{k}v}{s^{k}k!}\right\|,

\nu(\xi)=\|(A-\xi I)^{m}u\|=\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\xi^{m-k}w_{k}\right\|,

(3.2)

where

with

\xi^{*}

\nu(\xi)

Thus, for a given tolerance,
\mathrm{tol}

To motivate the block version of the algorithm, observe that the stages of exponential integrator schemes (e.g., (

Inspired by the work of Higham and Kandolf

Proposition 3
.

Let

be convergent power series (for

,

, and

. Choose nonzero

Define the

and, for each

Assume that for every

and

Then

G\;:=\;\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A^{\,j}\,V\,D_{j}\,\Gamma\,T^{\,j}\;=\;\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{1}^{\,i}\,g_{i}(t_{1}A)v_{i}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{2}^{\,i}\,g_{i}(t_{2}A)v_{i}&\cdots&\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{r}^{\,i}\,g_{i}(t_{r}A)v_{i}\end{bmatrix}\!.

Proof.

The

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A^{\,j}\,V\,(D_{j}\gamma_{k})\,t_{k}^{\,j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A^{\,j}\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{k}^{\,i}a_{ij}v_{i}\right)t_{k}^{\,j}=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{k}^{\,i}\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{ij}(t_{k}A)^{j}\right)\!v_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{k}^{\,i}g_{i}(t_{k}A)v_{i},

where

is the

at

∎

Remark.

In the classical formulation of exponential Runge–Kutta methods, the stage abscissae

appear simultaneously as scaling parameters in the arguments of the
\varphi
-functions and as polynomial weights outside, yielding combinations of the form

\sum_{k=0}^{p}t_{i}^{k}\,\varphi_{k}(t_{i}hA)v_{k},

see, e.g.,
[

In our setting, these roles are separated: the scaling parameter

\varphi

6
Conclusions.

We presented a matrix-free algorithm for evaluating linear combinations of
\varphi
-actions,

w_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\alpha_{i}^{\,j}\,\varphi_{j}(t_{i}A)v_{j}
. It combines the scaling and recovering method with a truncated Taylor series and,
a priori
, determines a spectral shift and a scaling parameter by minimizing a power-based objective for the shifted operator; these two quantities are computed once and then reused across subsequent calls (e.g., across stages or time steps), avoiding per-call retuning. Accuracy is user-controlled (ultimately limited by the working precision), and an adaptive stopping test avoids unnecessary work. The computational cost is also easily predictable: for a given
t
it is essentially proportional to the effective scaling count
\lceil|t|\,s\rceil
(the product of the time-step magnitude and the selected scaling parameter), so the cost grows
linearly
with this product. A block variant decouples the stage abscissae
t_{i}
from the polynomial weights
\{\alpha_{i}^{\,j}\}
, enabling simultaneous evaluation and improving cache locality and Level-3 BLAS utilization when the operator acts on blocks. Extensive numerical experiments show that the algorithm attains high accuracy on benign problems and maintains reliable accuracy for larger time steps and severe nonnormality. Compared with existing Krylov-based algorithms, it offers a favorable robustness–cost trade-off on challenging instances.

Jacobson identities for post-Lie algebras in positive characteristic
Quentin Ehret, Nicolas Gilliers

Abstract
Let p p be a prime number. Given a restricted Lie algebra over a field of characteristic p p and a post-Lie operation over it, we prove the Jacobson identities for a p p -structure built from the Lie bracket and the post-Lie operation, called sub-adjacent p p -structure. Furthermore, we give sufficient conditions for the sub-adjacent Lie algebra to be restricted if equipped with this sub-adjacent p p -structure. This construction is ”axiomatized” by introducing the notion of restricted post-Lie algebras, and we work out several examples.



Introduction

On the Lie algebras of derivations of an algebra and on the commutator Lie algebra of an associative algebra over a field of positive characteristic
p>0
, an additional structure (a unary mapping) appears, as Jacobson first noticed. Building on this observation, he introduced a type of Lie algebras which he called
restricted
; it is equipped with a set-map that behaves like taking the
p^{\rm th}
power (the Frobenius mapping), see Section
1.2
for more details. Restricted Lie algebras are of prime importance due to their link to algebraic groups and their role in representation theory, see
[
SF
]
. In particular, with
A
an associative algebra over a field of characteristic
p>0
, Jacobson proved that
(a+b)^{p}-a^{p}-b^{p}
is a sum of Lie commutators in
A
, for all
a,b\in A
. This is called the
Jacobson identity
and is axiomatized in the definition of restricted Lie algebras, see Equation (
1.2
).

In characteristic zero, pre-Lie algebras (also called right-symmetric algebras) were introduced by Gerstenhaber (see
[
Ge
]
) but made their first appearance in the mathematics literature way before; see
[
C
]
. Over the years, this type of algebras has been instrumental in many areas of mathematics; they share, for instance, profound relations with the theory of covariant derivatives on flat spaces. Trivial examples of pre-Lie algebras include associative algebras. More recently, post-Lie algebras were introduced by Vallette (see
[
Va
]
) in 2007 in the context of algebraic combinatorics as a generalization of pre-Lie algebras. This type of algebra has since found applications in the study of affine connections with constant torsion, see
[
MKL
,
MKW
]
.

In
[
Dz
]
, Dzhumadil’daev introduced and studied
restricted pre-Lie algebras
, demonstrating that the Jacobson identity holds for the
p^{th}
power taken relative to the (non-associative) pre-Lie product (see
[
Dz
, Theorem 1.1]
). Later, Dokas extended this concept by generalizing the notion of restricted pre-Lie algebras to accommodate an abstract
p^{th}
power (see
[
Do
, Definition 2.3]
). Notably, he showed that dendriform algebras possess two restricted structures; one in Dzhumadil’daev’s sense and another in the broader sense he introduced.

Contributions.

This paper introduces the two notions of
trivially restricted post-Lie algebras
and
restricted post-Lie algebras
. We use the Guin-Oudom construction, introduced after
[
Dz
]
was published, to perform our calculations, which makes them simpler. Using this construction, we recover the results of
[

]
on pre-Lie algebras and extend them to post-Lie algebras.

Restricted Lie algebras.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over
k
. Following Jacobson (
[
J
]
), a set-map

(-)^{[p]}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}},\quad x\mapsto x^{[p]},

is called a
p
-map
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
is said to be
restricted
if

\displaystyle(\lambda x)^{[p]}=\lambda^{p}x^{[p]}\text{ for all }x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\text{ and for all }\lambda\in k;

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x^{[p]}}=(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x})^{p}\text{ for all }x\in{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle(x+y)^{[p]}=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}s_{i}(x,y),\text{ for all }x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}

(9)

where the coefficients
s_{i}(x,y)
are obtained from the expansion

(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\lambda x+y})^{p-1}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}is_{i}(x,y)\lambda^{i-1}.

Equation (
1.2
) is referred to as the Jacobson identities, which can be made more explicit as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{x_{p-1}=y,~x_{p}=x}{x_{k}\in\{x,y\}}}\frac{1}{\sharp(x)}[x_{2},[x_{2},[...,[x_{p-1},x_{p}]...]]],\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},

(10)

where
\sharp(x):=\text{card}\{k,~x_{k}=x,~k=1,\cdots,p\}
.

The following Theorem is due to Jacobson (see
[
J
]
) and is useful to study
p
-maps on Lie algebras.

Theorem 1.2.1
.

Let
(L,[-,-])
be a Lie algebra and let
(e_{j})_{j\in J}
be a basis of
L
such that there are
f_{j}\in L
satisfying
(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{e_{j}})^{p}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f_{j}}
. Then, there exists exactly one
p
-map
(\cdot)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
such that
e_{j}^{[p]}=f_{j}\quad\text{ for all $j\in J$}.

(
i
)

Let

be an abelian Lie algebra. Then, any

-linear map

yields a restricted structure on

.

(
ii
)

Let

be an associative algebra over

(
iii
)

Recall that given an associative algebra

, the derivations space of
{\mathcal{A}}
, denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits({\mathcal{A}})
, is defined by

\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits({\mathcal{A}}):=\{d:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}},~d(ab)=d(a)b+ad(b),~\forall a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}\}.

It is well known (see
[

) that

is a restricted Lie algebra, with

\displaystyle[f,g]

\displaystyle:=f\circ g-g\circ f;

\displaystyle f^{[p]}

\displaystyle:=f^{p}={f\circ f\circ\cdot\cdot\circ f}\,\,(p~{\rm times}).

(
iv
)

(Partial restricted version of
[
JZ
, Theorem 3.1]
)
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an associative algebra. Let
{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits({\mathcal{A}})
be a restricted subalgebra. Then, the space
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
is a restricted Lie algebra with, for all

and for all

\displaystyle[a\otimes f,b\otimes g]

\displaystyle:=ab\otimes[f,g];

(11)

\displaystyle(a\otimes f)^{[p]}

\displaystyle:=a^{p}\otimes f^{p}.

The proof is straightforward. Let
a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}
and

.

\displaystyle\bigl[(a\otimes f)^{[p]},b\otimes g\bigl]=[a^{p}\otimes f^{p},b\otimes g]=a^{p}b\otimes[f^{p},g]=a^{p}b\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{p}_{f}(g).

Therefore,

Restricted morphisms and modules.

Let
\left({\mathfrak{g}},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}},-)^{[p]_{\mathfrak{g}}}\right)

\left({\mathfrak{a}},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{a}},(-)^{[p]_{\mathfrak{a}}}\right)
be two restricted Lie algebras. A Lie algebra morphism
\varphi:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}

(or

\varphi\bigl(x^{[p]_{\mathfrak{g}}}\bigl)=\varphi(x)^{[p]_{\mathfrak{a}}}\quad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

A

of a restricted Lie algebra
\left({\mathfrak{g}},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}},(-)^{[p]_{\mathfrak{g}}}\right)
is a
k
-vector space

equipped with a restricted morphism
\varphi:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(V)
, where the restricted Lie structure on
\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(V)
is given by Example
1.2.1
(ii).

A linear map

is called a
restricted derivation
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
if in addition to the Leibniz condition

d\bigl([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl)=[d(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,d(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},

it also satisfies

d\bigl(x^{[p]_{\mathfrak{g}}}\bigl)=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}^{p-1}(d(x)),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

With the notations of Example

Definition 2.1.1
.

(see

\displaystyle y\blacktriangleright x^{[p]}=(p-1~{\rm times})\,\,[x,[x,\ldots[x,y\blacktriangleright x]\cdots],\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

(67)

({\mathfrak{g}},\llbracket-,-\rrbracket,(-)^{[p]_{\blacktriangleright}})

\blacktriangleright

Quasi-shuffle algebras.

A
quasi-shuffle algebra
is a tuple
(A,\prec,\succ,\cdot)
, where
A
is a vector space,
\cdot
is an associative product on
A
, the binary operations
\prec
and
\succ
satisfy for all
a,b,c\in A
the relations

\displaystyle(a\prec b)\prec c=a\prec(b\star c),

\displaystyle a\succ(b\succ c)=(a\star b)\succ c

\displaystyle(a\succ b)\prec c=a\succ(b\prec c)

(89)

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\prec c=a\cdot(b\prec c)

\displaystyle(a\succ b)\cdot c=a\succ(b\cdot c)

\displaystyle a\cdot(b\succ c)=(a\prec b)\cdot c

(90)

with the associative product
a\star b:=a\prec b+a\succ b+a\cdot b,\,\forall a,b\in A
. The above relations implies that
a\blacktriangleright b:=b\prec a-a\succ b
is a post-Lie operation with respect to the Lie algebra
(A,[-,-]_{\cdot})
.

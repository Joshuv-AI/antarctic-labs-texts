Synergistic HA–tooth graft composite promotes rapid alveolar bone healing via IL-6 suppression and OCN enhancement
Authors: Dwi Wahyu Indrawati, Ernie Maduratna Setiawatie, Retno Pudji Rahayu, Shafira Kurnia Supandi, Rizky Briliant Syah Manurung, Mohammed Ahmed Aljunaid, Astari Puteri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPeriodontitis leads to continuous alveolar bone destruction driven by persistent inflammation, particularly elevated interleukin-6 (IL-6). Bone healing after tooth extraction under periodontitis conditions is often impaired, highlighting the need for regenerative biomaterials that can regulate inflammation and support osteogenesis. The hyaluronic acid–bovine tooth graft (HA + BTG) combination has been proposed as a promising BioGel-Graft candidate for accelerated bone regeneration.Aim and objectivesThis study aimed to evaluate IL-6 and osteocalcin (OCN) expression as biomarkers of alveolar bone healing in Porphyromonas gingivalis–induced Wistar rats following HA + BTG administration and to compare its regenerative effects with control and HA-only groups.MethodologyThirty-six male Wistar rats were allocated into three groups (control, HA, and HA + BTG). After periodontitis induction and tooth extraction, the treatment materials were applied to the extraction sockets. Tissue samples collected on days 14 and 21 were analyzed using immunohistochemistry for IL-6 and OCN, along with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining to assess woven bone formation.ResultsThe HA + BTG group showed a significant reduction in IL-6 expression and a significant increase in OCN expression and woven bone area compared with the control (p < 0.05). An inverse trend between IL-6 and OCN reflected the transition from inflammation to osteogenesis.ConclusionHA + BTG enhances alveolar bone regeneration by suppressing inflammation and stimulating osteoblastic activity, supporting its potential as an innovative gel-syringe biomaterial for regenerative alveolar bone therapy.Keywords: Periodontitis, Hyaluronic acid, Bovine tooth graft, Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Osteocalcin (OCN), Regenerative therapy

Introduction

Periodontitis is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting the supporting structures of the teeth, including the periodontal ligament and alveolar bone.1,2 Infection by pathogenic bacteria, such as Porphyromonas gingivalis, triggers an excessive immune response, resulting in tissue destruction and alveolar bone resorption.3 Bone healing following tooth extraction in periodontitis is often impaired due to a persistently inflammatory microenvironment that is unfavorable for hard tissue regeneration.4 Consequently, the bone remodeling process—normally balanced between osteoclastic and osteoblastic activity—is disrupted, leading to delayed and suboptimal alveolar bone healing.5,6

Alveolar bone healing involves a complex sequence of events, beginning with the inflammatory phase, followed by the proliferative phase, and concluding with bone remodeling.7,8 The balance between inflammatory mediators and osteogenic factors is critical for successful bone regeneration.9 During the early phase, proinflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) play a key role in regulating immune responses and osteoclastic activity.10 However, excessive or prolonged IL-6 expression can inhibit osteoblastic differentiation and extend the inflammatory phase, thereby impairing new bone formation. Therefore, controlling the inflammatory response at the early stage is crucial to facilitate effective bone tissue regeneration.11,12

Osteocalcin (OCN) is a non-collagenous protein secreted by mature osteoblasts and serves as a specific marker of bone mineralization and maturation.13 Increased OCN expression indicates optimal osteoblastic activity and the formation of mature bone tissue, the relationship between IL-6 and OCN expression can reflect the dynamic transition from the inflammatory phase to the osteogenic phase during alveolar bone healing. Analyzing these two indicators provides a more comprehensive understanding of the effectiveness of regenerative therapies.4

In efforts to accelerate alveolar bone healing, various biomaterials have been developed to stimulate tissue regeneration. Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a biocompatible material known for its anti-inflammatory properties, enhancing cell migration, and supporting fibroblast and osteoblast proliferation.14,15 Meanwhile, bovine tooth graft (BTG) is a xenogenic graft material rich in calcium phosphate with a microporous structure resembling human bone, making it osteoconductive and supportive of new tissue formation.16,17 The combination of HA and BTG is believed to produce synergistic effects: HA reduces inflammation and enhances cellular activity, while BTG serves as a scaffold that enables optimal deposition of new bone matrix.18

Based on this background, the present study aimed to analyze the expression of IL-6 and OCN as indicators of alveolar bone healing following the administration of HA + BTG combination in Wistar rats induced with P. gingivalis. Evaluating the expression of these markers is expected to elucidate the roles of inflammation modulation and osteogenesis stimulation by the combined biomaterials. The findings of this study are anticipated to provide a scientific basis for developing effective combination biomaterial–based regenerative strategies to accelerate alveolar bone healing after periodontitis and tooth extraction procedures.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was an in vivo experimental study using a posttest-only control group design.19 Thirty-six healthy male Wistar rats (Rattus norvegicus), aged 8–10 weeks and weighing 200–250 g, were used. The animals were randomly divided into three groups (12 rats per group):

1.Negative control group (C): extraction sockets without any material application.

Negative control group (C): extraction sockets without any material application.

2.Hyaluronic acid group (HA): extraction sockets treated with 0.1 mL HA gel.

Hyaluronic acid group (HA): extraction sockets treated with 0.1 mL HA gel.

3.HA and Bovine Tooth Graft combination group (HA + BTG): sockets treated with a 1:1 mixture of HA and BTG.

HA and Bovine Tooth Graft combination group (HA + BTG): sockets treated with a 1:1 mixture of HA and BTG.

Each group was further subdivided according to observation time points on day 14 and day 21 post-extraction (four rats per time point).

Periodontitis induction

Periodontitis was induced using Porphyromonas gingivalis (ATCC 33277). Bacterial cultures were grown in Brain Heart Infusion (BHI) broth under anaerobic conditions at 37 °C for 48 h.20,21 The bacterial suspension (109 CFU/mL) was mixed with 2% carboxymethylcellulose as a carrier. Each rat was orally inoculated with 0.2 mL of the suspension daily for 14 consecutive days. Successful induction was confirmed by clinical signs of gingival inflammation and increased pocket depth around the first mandibular molars.18

Tooth extraction and treatment application

After the induction period, animals were anesthetized with intraperitoneal ketamine (80 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg).22,23 The left first mandibular molars were atraumatically extracted using micro forceps. Hemostasis was achieved with sterile gauze. According to the treatment group, 0.1 mL of HA gel or the HA + BTG mixture was applied to the extraction sockets. No treatment was applied to the negative control group. Post-procedure, rats were maintained under standard laboratory conditions with ad libitum access to food and water.24

Sample collection and histological preparation

At each observation time point (days 14 and 21 post-extraction), animals were euthanized via cervical dislocation following deep anesthesia. Mandibles were harvested, fixed in 10% buffered formalin for 24 h, and decalcified in 10% EDTA solution for four weeks, with the decalcifying solution replaced every three days to ensure complete mineral removal. After decalcification, tissues were processed for paraffin embedding and sagittally sectioned at 5 μm thickness for histological and immunohistochemical analyses.25,26

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical analysis was performed to assess IL-6 and OCN expression. Deparaffinized and rehydrated tissue sections underwent antigen retrieval using citrate buffer (pH 6.0) in a microwave oven. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% H2O2. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies anti–IL-6 (1:200, Abcam) and anti–OCN (1:200, Abcam). After washing, tissues were incubated with secondary antibodies and visualized using 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB). Counterstaining was performed with hematoxylin. Preparations were examined under a light microscope at 400 × magnification, and positive expression was quantified in five randomly selected fields using ImageJ software.27 All histological and immunohistochemical evaluations were performed by calibrated examiners to minimize observational bias and ensure measurement reliability.

Histological evaluation

Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining was used to evaluate alveolar bone morphology. Parameters assessed included inflammatory cell infiltration, osteoblastic and osteoclastic activity, and new bone formation. Semi-quantitative scoring was performed based on pre-established criteria.28

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., USA). Normality was assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test. Intergroup differences were analyzed using one-way ANOVA, or the non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis test if normality assumptions were not met. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation with corresponding 95% confidence intervals.29

Results

This study evaluated the effects of Hyaluronic Acid (HA), Bovine Tooth Graft (BTG), and their combination (HA + BTG) on IL-6 and Osteocalcin (OCN) expression, as well as woven bone formation, in the extraction sockets of Rattus norvegicus induced with periodontitis. Observations were conducted on days 14 and 21 post-tooth extraction.

IL-6 expression

Immunohistochemical analysis revealed a gradual decrease in IL-6 expression across all treatment groups compared to the control group on both days 14 and 21 (Fig. 1). The mean IL-6 expression values (Table 1) demonstrated the greatest reduction in the HA + BTG combination group, with 2.00 ± 1.00 on day 14 and 1.33 ± 0.58 on day 21, compared to the control group, which exhibited values of 7.66 ± 0.58 and 5.66 ± 0.58, respectively. Overall intergroup comparison using the Kruskal–Wallis test (Table 5) revealed a statistically significant difference among groups (p = 0.004).

Osteocalcin (OCN) expression

OCN expression increased in all treatment groups, with the highest levels observed in the HA + BTG group on both days 14 and 21 (Fig. 2). The mean OCN expression values (Table 2) in the HA + BTG group were 11.00 ± 1.00 on day 14 and increased to 11.66 ± 1.53 on day 21. In contrast, the control group exhibited values of only 3.00 ± 1.00 and 3.75 ± 1.00, respectively. Kruskal–Wallis analysis revealed significant differences among groups (p = 0.004), indicating that the HA + BTG combination effectively enhanced osteoblastic activity, as evidenced by the increased OCN expression.

Woven bone formation

Hematoxylin-eosin staining revealed increased woven bone formation, with the most pronounced effect observed in the HA + BTG combination group (Fig. 3). The mean area of woven bone formation (Table 3) in the HA + BTG group increased significantly from 3923.81 ± 427.68 μm2 on day 14 to 5070.98 ± 3320.73 μm2 on day 21. In contrast, the control and HA-only groups showed no detectable woven bone formation at either time point. Kruskal–Wallis analysis indicated significant differences among groups (p = 0.002), and subsequent Mann–Whitney post hoc tests demonstrated that the increase was significant between days 14 and 21 in the BTG and HA + BTG groups (p < 0.05).

Discussion

This study demonstrated that the combination of Hyaluronic Acid (HA) and Bovine Tooth Graft (BTG) exerts a synergistic effect in accelerating alveolar bone healing after tooth extraction under Porphyromonas gingivalis-induced periodontitis. This effect was evidenced by significant reductions in IL-6 expression, increases in Osteocalcin (OCN) expression, and greater woven bone area compared to single treatments or controls.30,31 Overall, the HA + BTG combination demonstrated the most optimal effect by simultaneously reducing the proinflammatory IL-6 expression and enhancing the expression of the osteogenic marker OCN as well as woven bone formation. These results indicate a synergistic interaction between the anti-inflammatory properties of Hyaluronic Acid and the osteoconductive effects of Bovine Tooth Graft, accelerating alveolar bone healing following tooth extraction under periodontitis conditions.

Reduction of IL-6 expression

IL-6 expression significantly decreased in the HA + BTG combination group on both days 14 and 21 compared to the control group. This reduction reflects a lower local inflammatory response, which is critical during the early phase of bone healing. IL-6 is a proinflammatory cytokine produced by macrophages and fibroblasts in response to infection and tissue damage. Excessive IL-6 prolongs the inflammatory phase, increases osteoclast activity, and inhibits osteoblast differentiation.32,33

These findings align with previous studies showing that HA exerts anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting NF-κB activation and reducing IL-1β and IL-6 secretion in periodontal tissues. Moreover, the viscoelastic structure of HA creates a moist and protective microenvironment at the wound site, reducing oxidative stress and facilitating the migration of immune cells and fibroblasts to the regenerative area.34

Increase in osteocalcin (OCN) expression

Conversely, OCN expression was highest in the HA + BTG group, particularly on day 21. OCN is a non-collagenous protein synthesized by mature osteoblasts and is involved in mineralization and maturation of new bone tissue. The increase in OCN expression indicates optimal osteoblastic activity, marking the transition from the inflammatory phase to the proliferative and remodeling phases of bone healing. BTG, rich in calcium phosphate, serves as an osteoconductive scaffold for new bone matrix deposition. Its microporous structure mimics human bone, allowing adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation of osteoblasts. The combination with HA enhances these effects by promoting cell migration and regulating osteogenic gene expression, such as RUNX2 and COL1A1, explaining the significant OCN increase compared to single treatments.35

Woven bone formation

H&E staining revealed the widest woven bone formation in the HA + BTG group. Woven bone, the early form of new bone, consists of type I collagen fibers arranged randomly with numerous immature osteocytes. The area of woven bone formation increased from day 14 to day 21, indicating faster bone regeneration in the combination group. The presence of BTG as a porous inorganic matrix facilitates vascular invasion and osteoid deposition, while HA accelerates the infiltration of osteoprogenitor cells into the graft area. This combination promotes bone bridging between socket walls, which is crucial during the early stages of alveolar osteogenesis.36

Relationship between IL-6, OCN, and bone regeneration

Physiologically, alveolar bone healing involves a balance between proinflammatory cytokines (such as IL-6) and osteogenic factors (such as OCN). The observed decrease in IL-6 accompanied by increased OCN suggests that HA + BTG not only suppresses inflammation but also facilitates osteoblast differentiation and mineralized matrix formation. Immunomodulation by HA plays a crucial role in creating a microenvironment conducive to osteogenesis, which is further supported by the osteoconductive properties of BTG.37

Implications and significance

These findings reinforce the concept that a combination biomaterial-based regenerative approach has high clinical potential in periodontitis therapy and post-extraction recovery. The HA + BTG combination mimics an ideal biological environment, with HA acting as a bioactive matrix supporting cellular activities and BTG serving as a stable osteoconductive scaffold. This approach provides a foundation for developing BioGel-Graft as an innovative biomaterial, ready to be formulated as a sterile gel syringe for clinical application in alveolar bone regeneration.38

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the findings were derived from a Porphyromonas gingivalis–induced Wistar rat model, which was employed as a preclinical experimental model to evaluate alveolar bone healing mechanisms; therefore, the interpretation of the results is confined to the preclinical context. Second, the sample size was relatively limited, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. Finally, the analysis focused on selected biomarkers of inflammation and osteogenesis, namely interleukin-6 (IL-6) and osteocalcin (OCN); the inclusion of additional molecular markers may provide a more comprehensive understanding of the underlying regenerative mechanisms. Accordingly, future studies incorporating larger sample sizes, broader biomarker profiling, and further validation are warranted to strengthen these findings.

Conclusion

In a Porphyromonas gingivalis–induced Wistar rat experimental model, the combination of Hyaluronic Acid (HA) and Bovine Tooth Graft (BTG) demonstrated a synergistic effect in accelerating alveolar bone regeneration, as indicated by reduced IL-6 expression, increased osteocalcin levels, and enhanced woven bone formation.

This synergistic effect reflects the complementary anti-inflammatory action of HA and the osteoconductive properties of BTG, creating a local microenvironment that supports osteogenesis compared with control and single-treatment groups. Therefore, this combination has the potential to be developed into BioGel-Graft, an innovative gel syringe biomaterial for preclinical regenerative therapy of alveolar bone.

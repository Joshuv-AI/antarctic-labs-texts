Age-specific antibody profiles of respiratory syncytial virus after COVID-19 pandemic: a sero-epidemiological survey in Taizhou City, Eastern China
Authors: Bo Zheng, Qiang Wang, Kathy Leung, Hongbiao Liang, Weiwei Shen, Mengting Cao, Suyi Zhang, Ye Wu, Haijiang Lin, Weibing Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe generalizability of the current quantitative assessments of age-specific RSV antibody levels remains unclear in China. We aimed to comprehensively understand the RSV immune profile at the population level and identify related factors.MethodsWe conducted a community-based cross-sectional sero-epidemiological study in Taizhou City, Eastern China, from June 2 to 30, 2024, and assessed RSV pre-F IgG antibody titres. We employed generalized linear models to identify factors linked to antibody titres and used a generalized additive model to determine RSV seropositivity risk. The serocatalytic model was further used to estimate the force of infection (FOI) based on age-specific seroprevalence data.ResultsAmong 1303 individuals aged 16 months to 93 years, toddlers (younger than 2 years) had the lowest geometric mean titres (GMTs) of RSV pre-F IgG antibodies, which was 591.9 (95% confidence interval [CI], 106.5-3291.1). The seroprevalence (using titre > 200 as the threshold) surged sharply with age, from 46.1% (42.2–50.2) at 1 year of age to 91.5% (88.9–93.8) at age 4, with an estimated FOI of 0.618 (0.547–0.697). Children residing in households with over four members correlated with a heightened risk of seropositivity within the first 4 postnatal year compared to those in households with four or fewer members (adjusted odds ratio, aOR: 1.954, 1.082–3.529). Age was significantly associated with GMTs in the elderly individuals (aged 65 and above), with an estimated annual increment of 0.019-fold (0.004–0.034). Elderly with underlying diseases demonstrated a 0.203-fold (0.036–0.341) reduction in GMTs compared to those without such conditions.ConclusionsAn early age-dependent pattern in RSV serostatus is observed, and the seroprevalence increased with age. Background

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of acute lower respiratory infections (ALRIs) [1], and has a disproportionate impact on vulnerable groups, including infants, the elderly, and those with comorbidities, who are at an increased risk for severe respiratory complications [2]. A prospective cohort study in South Africa revealed that the RSV burden is not confined to vulnerable populations, as there was a notable incidence of RSV infection among community-dwelling adults aged 18–64 years, with rates reported ranging 30.3 to 42.1 cases per 100 person-years [3].

With a population exceeding 1.4 billion, which accounts for > 9% of the global population of children under the age of 5 [4], China also confronts a considerable health challenge from RSV. Estimates indicate that RSV is responsible for 18.7% of cases among individuals with acute respiratory tract infections (ARTIs) [5], and the incidence rate of RSV-associated ALRIs in children aged below 5 years was 40.3 per 1000 individuals annually [1]. The overall burden of RSV infection in the general Chinese population is substantially under-reported because current surveillance only detects RSV among sampled cases with influenza-like illnesses (ILIs) in sentinel hospitals [6, 7]. Studies estimate that only one-third to one-half of infections are symptomatic, and a significant proportion of symptomatic individuals do not seek medical care [3, 8]. Despite the substantial burden imposed by RSV, the current lack of therapeutic options for RSV infection underscores the imperative for preventive strategies [9]. The RSV prophylactic products are currently experiencing rapidly evolving, including the development and use of the long-acting monoclonal antibody Nirsevimab for infants [10], as well as vaccines approved for adults aged 60 + and for maternal immunization [11]. Nirsevimab received approval in China in early 2024, with an immunization program commencing in Shanghai on September 26, 2024 [12].

With the introduction of novel prophylactic measures, there is an urgent need for more comprehensive data elucidating the pattern of primary RSV infections and the immunological disparities within the general population. Such data would be instrumental in informing proper immunization preventive measures. Undertaking sero-epidemiological studies within the general population could also facilitate the comprehension of RSV disease burden and estimate epidemiological characteristics of the virus, including the force of infection (FOI). However, most studies have only measured RSV seroprevalence using samples from adult blood donors [13] and patients with acute respiratory infections [14, 15], with limited data from infants and children in China. A recent study [11] conducted in rural areas of Southern China reported seropositivity rates of 76.2% in infants aged 4 months to less than 2 years, 93.4% in children aged 2 years to less than 5 years, and 100% in older age groups (aged 5 and above). However, this study used convenience sampling and obtained samples from a four-month interval that covered disruptions in seasonal RSV circulation, which might cause the underestimate of the RSV antibody level [11]. Moreover, few studies measured RSV pre-F IgG antibodies [16, 17], which are major components of neutralizing antibodies against RSV [18] and considered to more accurately reflect the seropositivity of RSV, compared to other serological assays [16, 19].

In this study, we quantified the age-specific antibody of RSV in a general community population in Taizhou in Eastern China (conducted from 2 to 30 June 2024 among 1,303 residents aged 16 months to 93 years). We identified factors associated with RSV pre-F IgG antibody titres and estimated the FOI of RSV. This study aims to enhance comprehension of RSV immunity profiles and offer insight for immunization strategies decision.

Methods

Study site and participants

We performed a community-based cross-sectional sero-epidemiological study in Taizhou city, Zhejiang Province, China, from June 2 to June 30, 2024, prior to the national licensure of any RSV prophylactic product. A multi-stage, age-stratified sampling method (using 5-year age bands, 0–75 + years) was designed to reflect the population structure of Taizhou (see details in the Additional file 1). Participants were eligible if they had resided in the study area for at least three months prior to enrollment. Individuals who did not provide informed consent, or whose legal guardians declined to provide consent, were excluded. The Huangyan District was randomly selected, followed by the selection of one urban subdistrict (Dongcheng) and one rural township (Shabufu). Subsequently, three villages and two communities were randomly selected. Within each stratum, individuals were sampled using probability proportional to size from community worker registries, with adjustments for expected attrition to achieve the target sample size. This process yielded 941 residents whose age distribution closely mirrored that of the city census (Additional file 1: Tables S1–S2).

Given the substantial disease burden of RSV among children under 5 [1, 20], children were sequentially oversampled across 19 townships/streets in Linhai City, which is affiliated with Taizhou City. Local personnel of health community centers pre-identified eligible children and ascertained their willingness to participate, allowing us to include an additional 362 children under the age of 5. The geographical distribution of the study area is depicted in Fig. S1 (Additional file 1). Prior to serum sample collection, well-trained investigators conducted in-depth interviews with each participant or their guardian (for child < 9 years old participants), utilizing a structured questionnaire (specifically designed for the present investigation) to meticulously gather demographic, health status, and household data (Additional file 2).

Laboratory procedure

Serum-specific RSV pre-F IgG antibodies were quantified by indirect enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), as previously reported [21]. Briefly, 96-well plates were coated with stabilized pre-F protein (DRA230) [22], incubated at 4 °C overnight, washed, blocked with BB-003, and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. Serial dilutions of serum samples were added, and the mixtures were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated rabbit anti-human IgG H&L was added, followed by the substrate. The reaction was stopped by the addition of 100 µL/well of concentrated sulfuric acid. Detailed experimental procedures and calculation methods of titres are presented in the Additional file 1. Our methodology avoids the limitations of discrete titre measurements associated with single optical density value thresholds [11], and thereby enables a more accurate assessment of continuous titres across various dilution points. To further validate the pre-F IgG antibody titres, we conducted a focus reduction neutralization test (FRNT) to assess the neutralizing activity against RSV A2 and RSV B strains in a subset of 101 participants who were randomly sampled proportionally across the age groups (Table 1). Detailed experimental procedures and calculation methods of titres are presented in the Appendix (Additional file 1).

Statistical analysis

The geometric mean titres (GMTs) and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) across all age groups (16–23 months, 24–29 months, 30–35 months, 36–41 months, 42–47 months, 4–11 years, 12–17 years, 18–64 years and ≥ 65 years) were estimated. Spearman’s correlation coefficients were utilized to assess the correlation between the titres of RSV pre-F IgG antibodies and neutralizing antibodies against RSV A2 and B strains. Subsequently, we fitted a generalized additive model (GAM) with thin plate regression splines [23] to explore potential non-linear relationships between age and serum antibody titres. Additionally, generalized linear models (GLMs) were applied to identify factors associated with RSV pre-F IgG antibody titres among all participants, with additional subgroup analyses performed on vulnerable demographics (children aged < 4 years and elderly aged ≥ 65 years). We quantified RSV pre-F IgG antibodies as log10 endpoint titres for GLMs. A regression coefficient β > 0 indicates that the predictor is associated with a -1 fold increase in antibody titre; β < 0 corresponds to a 1- fold decrease.

Seroprevalence and its 95% CIs were determined using the Dirichlet distribution [24], with a pre-F IgG antibody titre of 200 defined as the seropositivity threshold [11]. The minimum detectable difference from the reference seroprevalence in each age group is given in Table S3 (Additional file 1). A logistic regression analysis within a GAM framework [20] was conducted to analyze RSV seropositivity risk; for this, we focused on children under 4 years of age, given that age plays a pivotal role in seropositivity and all participants older than 4 years were seropositive. Serocatalytic models are extensively applied to infer the FOI (, the infection rate among susceptible individuals of age a) [25] from cross-sectional seroprevalence; this quantity reflects pre-vaccination transmission risk. Utilizing these serocatalytic models, we can estimate that the annual infection probability for a susceptible individual is , and the seroprevalence in individuals of age a is [26]. Considering the potential age-related differences in infection risk, we evaluated three serocatalytic models to account for the observed age-specific seroprevalence and estimate the FOIs: an age-varying FOI, different FOIs for children and adults, and a constant FOI [26]. Parameter estimation was conducted within a Bayesian framework, using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method in R with the Rstan and Rsero packages [27]. We followed the strengthening the reporting of observational studies in epidemiology (STROBE) recommendations, the checklist for which is provided in the Additional file 1 (Table S4).

Results

Demographic characteristics of the study participants

The study involved 1,303 individuals, aged from 16 months to 93 years, comprising 52.3% (682) females, 13.0% (170) elderly individuals, and 19.6% (256) who reported experiencing an ILI within the prior 2 months (Table 1 and Additional file 1: Table S5). The majority, 76.2% (993), were sampled during the week of June 2 to June 8 (Additional file 1: Fig. S2). The concentrated sampling period, which was scheduled not to correspond with an RSV seasonal epidemic (RSV activity was at a low level), ensured that the population-level immunity was not affected (Fig. 1E).

We selected 101 samples for FRNT, and there was no statistically significant disparity in median age (P = 0. 846), gender (P = 0.734), BMI (P = 0.064), household size (P = 0.059) or underlying diseases (P = 0.974), compared with 1202 samples tested using only ELISA. A strong positive correlation was observed between pre-F IgG titres and the reference standard serology assay (FRNT) results, with Spearman’s ρ values of 0.693 (P < 0.001) for RSV A2 strain and 0.665 (P < 0.001) for RSV B strain (Additional file 1: Fig. S3).

Antibody responses and subgroup analysis

The titres of pre-F IgG antibodies demonstrated a nonlinear escalation with age (P = 0.003), which was particularly pronounced among children (Fig. 1A). Toddlers had the lowest geometric mean titre (GMT) of RSV pre-F IgG antibodies, which was 591.9 (95% CI, 106.5-3291.1) at a log10 unit value of 2.77 (95% CI, 2.03–3.52). Among those 4 years to under 12 years, the GMTs increased to 18,180.8 (95% CI, 15,650.6–21,119.9), corresponding to 4.26 log10 units (95% CI, 4.19–4.32); thereafter, they remained stably high across later life stages. The neutralizing antibody titres mirrored this nonlinear pattern of increase (Additional file 1: Fig. S4): The GMTs for RSV A2 were 2321.0 (95% CI, 951.4-5662.3) in children under 4 years old and 8671.1 (95% CI, 5017.0–14,986.6) in individuals over 65 years old, with log2 units of 11.18 (95% CI, 9.89–12.47) and 13.08 (95% CI, 12.29–13.87).

Our univariate GLM revealed that age, household size, pre-existing health conditions, and recent ILIs were potential determinants of RSV pre-F antibody titres (Additional file 1: Tables S6-S9). After adjustments in a multivariate model, toddlerhood (< 2 years) was significantly associated with lower RSV pre-F antibody titres. Our subgroup analysis (Table 2) indicated that, in children under 4 years of age, the antibody titres increased with age, averaging a titre increment of 2.038-fold per year (95% CI, 0.842–4.011), and daycare attendees exhibited antibody titres 2.100 times higher than those of non-attendees (95% CI, 1.035–4.261). Among the elderly, each additional year of age corresponded to an average titre increase of 0.019-fold (95% CI, 0.004–0.034), and individuals with underlying diseases displayed a 0.203-fold reduction (95% CI, 0.036–0.341). In adults, no significant correlation was observed between age and RSV pre-F antibody titres; however, married individuals exhibited significantly higher antibody titres than their unmarried counterparts, with a ratio of 1.248 (95% CI, 1.054–1.478).

Factors associated with seroprevalence

Among children younger than 4 years, the seroprevalence rate was 83.7% (95% CI, 74.4–91.7) (Fig. 2A), whereas those aged 4 years and above exhibited complete seropositivity of 100% (898/898). Among those under 4 years of age, the seroprevalence was 81.6% (95% CI, 68.3–92.8) for females and 85.6% (95% CI, 73.9–98.8) for males. Within the pediatric population, an age-dependent increase in the risk of seropositivity was noted: increasing from 50.0% (5/10) among toddlers to 65.2% (15/23) among those aged 24–29 months, and to 94.0% (79/84) among those aged 42–47 months.

An increased risk of seropositivity in the early-life period was also associated with larger household sizes (children in households with more than four residents vs. in smaller households), with an adjusted odds ratio (aOR) of 1.954 (95% CI, 1.082–3.529). In the sensitivity analysis, we obtained very similar results when seropositivity antibody titre thresholds were set at 100 and 300 (Additional file 1: Fig. S5).

FOI and estimated seroprevalence

The age-constant FOI model provided the most favorable fit (Additional file 1: Fig. S6) while also achieving convergence to the same posterior distribution (Additional file 1: Fig. S7). Our catalytic models highlighted a sharp, age-dependent surge in seroprevalence in the early-life period (FOI: 0.618 per year, 95% credible intervals [CrI], 0.547–0.697), increasing from 46.1% (95% CrI, 42.2–50.2) at age 1 (Fig. 1C) to 91.5% (95% CrI, 88.9–93.8) by age 4. Driven by an annual infection probability of 46.1% among populations seronegative for RSV antibodies (Fig. 3A2), this trend continues such that children exhibit close to 100% seropositivity around ages 6 to 7. Notably, male children consistently demonstrated higher seroprevalence than their female counterparts during early life, with seropositivity rates of 72.8% (FOI: 0.655 per year, 95% CrI, 0.557–0.772) versus 68.9% (FOI: 0.583 per year, 95% CrI, 0.493–0.691), respectively, at 2 years of age (Additional file 1: Fig. S8). Sensitivity analyses, which were performed by adjusting the pre-F IgG antibody positivity threshold to 100 and 300 units and assuming a test sensitivity of 0.99 and specificity of 0.95, indicated that there was minimal variation in age-specific seropositivity estimates (Additional file 1: Fig. S9). The FOI values were stable in sensitivity analyses, ranging from 0.639 per year (95% CrI, 0.566–0.722) (Fig. 3B1) to 0.609 per year (95% CrI, 0.540–0.684) (Fig. 3C1).

Discussion

Here, we performed a serological study to comprehensively analyze immunological profiles of RSV across the demographic spectrum of a Chinese population. With RSV immunisation not yet implemented, our findings represent pre-intervention baseline immunity; serostatus therefore reflects natural exposure only. Our results reveal that age is a pivotal factor in RSV serostatus, with children under 4 years of age experiencing a swift age-related rise in antibody titres. Daycare attendees had significantly elevated antibody titres compared to non-attendees. Among the elderly, antibody titres increased with age but were lower in those with underlying health issues. The was a pronounced increase in seropositivity risk with age in children; it peaked between 42 and 47 months of age, and was exacerbated by household size. Importantly, nearly half (46.1%) of individuals were estimated to have experienced prior RSV infection at age 1, underscoring the urgent need for RSV protection in early infancy.

Previous studies have suggested that delays in sample collection could contribute to variability in RSV antibody levels, complicating interpretation and inference [11]. To minimize such temporal bias, we deliberately conducted our concentrated sampling in June 2024, during the RSV off-season. Local surveillance data consistently indicate that June represents the annual nadir of RSV activity [28], and policymakers generally place greater confidence in off-season serological data. As summarized by Duarte et al. [29], serosurveys conducted during transmission peaks risk overestimating baseline immunity due to transient antibody boosts; off-season sampling yields a conservative estimate of susceptibility and helps avoid underestimating vaccination needs in vulnerable age groups. Our research validates prior findings on age-related antibody titres [11, 17], demonstrating that the titre of RSV pre-F IgG antibodies increase significantly with age in children under 4 years and plateaus between the ages of 4 and 11 years. The risk of seropositivity surges during the early-life period: increasing from 50.0% for toddlers to 94.0% for those aged 42–47 months, underscoring age as a key predictor of prior RSV infection [20]. Whereas Zylbersztejn et al. [30] found a 36% higher infection risk with childcare attendance in a population from the England, we did not find that daycare attendance had a significant effect on seropositivity risk in our population. This divergence might stem from the age ranges of the children enrolled. In England, many children enter daycare at 3 months and 41.3% of toddlers are enrolled in daycare services [31]. In contrast, Chinese daycare services generally admit children aged 3 to 6 [32]; this age range is already prone to high seroprevalence, which could potentially diminish the effect of daycare on the initial RSV infection. Also, although we did not find that daycare attendance increased the risk of first infection, our data suggest that attending daycare significantly amplified RSV antibody titres by 1.1-fold, possibly due to frequent exposure to RSV in early life in such close-knit environments. Nonetheless, this remains speculative, and there is a need for additional research to quantitatively evaluate the impact of daycare attendance on the early-life infections among Chinese children, which could inform the development of future vaccination strategies. Interestingly, household size was not significantly associated with antibody titres, but children in households with more than four residents had a 0.954-fold (0.082–2.529) increase in seropositivity risk, revealing the significant role of early-life household factors in the occurrence of primary RSV infections. Previous studies showed that household transmission is responsible for 39% of infant infections [33] and young siblings aged 0–4 years are the most likely to introduce the virus into the home [34], substantially raising the risk of primary RSV infections and hospitalizations in infants and toddlers [35]. Consequently, preventive strategies should be directed towards larger households, particularly those with older siblings, to mitigate early infections in young children [30].

Elderly individuals with immunodeficiency and cardiopulmonary diseases are particularly vulnerable to severe RSV outcomes [36, 37] at least in part because they have lower RSV-specific memory T cell counts than healthy seniors [38]. Indeed, we observed a 0.203-fold (95% CI, 0.036–0.341) decrease in antibody titres among elderly subjects with underlying diseases, highlighting the urgent need for effective vaccines and therapeutics for this high-risk group. While prior studies indicate that the elderly can mount a robust serum antibody response to RSV, with persistence up to 180 days [39], the nasal sIgA response is significantly impaired. This phenomenon, which might be linked to immunosenescence, could potentially explain why the susceptibility to RSV is increased in the elderly despite their elevated antibody titres [39]. We postulate that intensified viral replication, exacerbated by weakened mucosal immunity, triggers stronger systemic antibody responses, potentially compounded by the cumulative effect of multiple past RSV infections [40].

Our age-constant FOI estimates indicate that there is a rapid increase in seroprevalence during the early-life period, with nearly universal prevalence by school age. This aligns with prior reports [16, 41]. Using a threshold titre of specific antibodies to define seropositivity as an indicator of “past infection” [11, 17], we characterized the age-dependent disease profile. Given that maternally derived RSV antibodies have an average half-life of about 26 days [42] and decrease to minimal or undetectable titres in uninfected infants by 1 year of age [43], we estimated that, by 1 year of age, 46.1% of children had serological evidence of past RSV infection. This prevalence is close to the 54% (95% CI, 52–57%) infection rate reported within the first postnatal year in a U.S. birth cohort study [44] and exceeds the 26.2% (95% CI, 24.0-28.6%) observed across several European countries [45]. Given the absence of prospective birth cohort data in China and the inclusion of children with comorbidities in most published studies on the disease burden of RSV in the early-life period [6, 46], our data could help facilitate the accurate estimation of the RSV disease burden in healthy younger children within community settings. The annual FOI estimated herein aligns with the previously reported value of 0.60 per year (95% CrI, 0.47–0.74) [16]. Our subgroup analyses revealed a higher FOI in older children, possibly reflecting increased age-dependent contact rates with enhanced social interactions [23, 47]. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution: There is significant uncertainty in the parameter estimates for these subgroups, largely due to the limited number of seronegative individuals.

Our study has several other limitations. Notably, the absence of infant samples precluded accurate quantification of maternally derived antibody decay and seroprevalence in the highest-risk age stratum, potentially biasing early-life exposure estimates and increasing uncertainty in the corresponding FOI. As a cross-sectional, pre-intervention survey we infer FOI via serocatalytic models that assume negligible seroreversion; longitudinal cohorts with repeated sera would provide more precise estimates. Also, although we confirmed that pre-F IgG correlates strongly with neutralizing antibodies and verified that it constitutes the majority of neutralizing activity [48], we acknowledge potential assay variability. Validation and sensitivity analyses across seropositivity thresholds of 100–300 yielded robust FOI estimates, indicating limited misclassification impact. Furthermore, because we were unable to access participants’ medical records, ILI and underlying diseases were self-reported rather than verified through clinical testing, and thus may be subject to recall and self-reporting biases.

Conclusions

Our findings underscore the impact of age on RSV serostatus, showing that there is a pronounced surge in antibody titres among children under 4 years of age in a Chinese population. Despite the overall upward trend with age, elderly subjects with underlying health conditions demonstrated reduced antibody titres. The risk of seropositivity in children peaked significantly between 42 months and 4 years of age. Against the backdrop of the rapid development and approval of novel preventive measures, our findings stress the critical importance of safeguarding younger children and the elderly, particularly children from larger families and seniors with pre-existing health conditions, from RSV infections.

Abstract
. In this work we study how to extend a partial action of a Hopf Algebra A A on an algebra R R to a partial action of a Hopf-Ore extension of A A on R R . As consequence, we characterize all partial actions of rank one Hopf algebras (in particular, generalized Taft algebras and Radford algebras), under suitable conditions.



1.
Introduction

In the last years, the theory of partial actions and representations is a subject widely studied in several contexts such as groups
[
7
]
, groupoids
[
2
]
, (weak) Hopf algebras
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
, categories
[
3
]
, among others.
For partial actions of Hopf algebras, despite of the developed theory, the computations to characterize explicit examples are a challenging work.

Recently, this question has been addressed in
[
9
,
10
,
13
]
.
In these works, the applied method to get the partial actions of some families of Hopf algebras usually lies in extending the partial actions of Hopf subalgebras.
Namely, some partial actions of pointed Hopf algebras are computed extending the partial actions of their group of grouplike elements.
This technique is the same used as in the global case (see, for instance
[
6
,
11
]
).

In this work, we approach this question in a more general way.
Since the families of Hopf algebras treated in the above references are quotients of Hopf-Ore extensions, we investigate when is possible to extend the partial action of a Hopf algebra to a Hopf-Ore extension. In particular, Theorem
3.6
shows that, under suitable conditions, the partial actions still depend only on the partial action of the base Hopf algebra, a skew-derivation and a compatibility between them.

We highlight that the techniques and results developed here are effective tools. Indeed, in this paper we characterize some partial actions of interesting families of Hopf algebras, as the rank one Hopf algebras (see §
4.3
); such classification is an original work to the best of authors’ knowledge.
On the other hand, this (more refined) approach also recovers the main results within
[
9
,
10
,
13
]
.

This work is organized as follows. In §2, the basic concepts and results of q-combinatorics, partial actions and Hopf-Ore extensions are briefly recalled.

In §
3
, we begin determining a condition for extending a partial action of a Hopf algebra
A
to a partial action of a Hopf-Ore extension
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
in the trivial way.
In the sequence we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions to extend a partial action of
A
to
A[x,\sigma]
when
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
. Further, in §
3.3
, we investigate the relations between the partial actions of Hopf-Ore extensions and the partial actions of its quotients.

In the last section, we present applications and examples.
In Sections
4.1
and
4.2
, we improve the sufficient/necessary conditions within Theorem
3.6
for the following two cases:
x\cdot 1_{R}\in Z(R)
and
A=\Bbbk G
, respectively.
In §
4.3
, we obtain all partial actions of rank one Hopf algebras (in particular, generalized Taft algebras and Radford algebras) on an algebra
R
under (not so restrictive) suitable conditions.
At last, we explore the case for Hopf-Ore extensions of Sweedler’s Hopf algebra and Nichols Hopf algebras, respectively in §
4.4
and §
4.5
.

Conventions and notations

Throughout the paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. The group of multiplicative invertible elements of
\Bbbk
is denoted by
\Bbbk^{\times}
.
For
n\geq 1
,
\mathbb{G}_{n}\subset\Bbbk^{\times}
is the group of
n
-th roots of unity; by
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}_{n}\subset\mathbb{G}_{n}
, we mean the subset of primitive roots.

For the positive integers, we use the notation
\mathbb{N}
, while
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
If
j\leq k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then we consider
\mathbb{I}_{j,k}=\{j,j+1,\cdots,k\}
and
\mathbb{I}_{k}=\mathbb{I}_{1,k}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hopf algebra.
We denote the group of its grouplike elements by
G(\mathcal{H})
.
Given
h,g\in G(\mathcal{H})
, then an element
x
is called a
(h,g)
-primitive if
\Delta(x)=x\otimes h+g\otimes x
.
The space of all
(h,g)
-primitive elements is

P_{h,g}(\mathcal{H})
.
Moreover,
Z(R)
is the center of a general algebra
R
.

q-combinatorics

For this section, we refer
[
16
, §7.2]
.
Let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n\geq 0
, and
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. The
q-binomial coefficients
are defined recursively as follows.
First, set
{0\choose 0}_{q}=1
and
{n\choose m}_{q}=0
if
m>n
or
m<0
.
Then, for
n\geq 1
and
0\leq m\leq n
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}={{n-1}\choose m}_{q}+q^{n-m}{{n-1}\choose{m-1}}_{q}.

As a consequence of the definition, observe that

(2.2)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}=q^{m(n-m)}{n\choose m}_{q^{-1}}.

Assume that
q\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}_{N}
. For
k\geq 0
, let
k_{D}
and
k_{R}
be the integers determined by
k=k_{D}\,N+k_{R}
where
0\leq k_{R}<N
.
Then

(2.3)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}={n_{R}\choose m_{R}}_{q}{n_{D}\choose m_{D}},

for all
0\leq m\leq n
.

.

[
10

Let
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then,

(2.4)

\displaystyle{j\choose k}_{q}{{j-k}\choose{i-k}}_{q}={j\choose i}_{q}{i\choose k}_{q};

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sj}{j\choose s}_{q}{{s+i}\choose{k}}_{q}=\,q^{\frac{j(j+1)}{2}+j(i-k)}{i\choose k-j}_{q}.

2.2.
Partial actions

A
(left) partial action of a Hopf algebra (or a bialgebra)
\mathcal{H}
on an algebra
R
is a linear map
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
, denoted by
\cdot(h\otimes r)=h\cdot r
, such that

(PA.1)

1_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot r=r
,

(PA.2)

h\cdot rs=(h_{1}\cdot r)(h_{2}\cdot s)
,

(PA.3)

h\cdot(k\cdot r)=(h_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(h_{2}k\cdot r)
,

hold for all
h,k\in\mathcal{H}
and
r,s\in R
.
In this case,
R
is called a
(left) partial
\mathcal{H}
-module algebra
.
A (left) partial action is
symmetric
if in addition we have

(PA.S)

h\cdot(k\cdot r)=(h_{1}k\cdot r)(h_{2}\cdot 1_{R})
.

Observe that, if (PA.1) holds, then conditions (PA.2) and (PA.3) are equivalent to the following one:

(PA.2
′
)

h\cdot(r(k\cdot s))=(h_{1}\cdot r)(h_{2}k\cdot s)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
10
, Lem. 3.1]
Let
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action. For each
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and
r\in R
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle(g\cdot 1_{R})r(g\cdot 1_{R})=(g\cdot 1_{R})r.

Moreover, if
\cdot
is symmetric, then
(g\cdot 1_{R})\in Z(R)
.

Notice that if
h\in\mathcal{H}
satisfies
\Delta(h)=\tau\Delta(h)
(where
\tau
is the usual twist morphism), then it holds in general that
(h_{1}\cdot 1_{R})r(h_{2}\cdot 1_{R})=(h\cdot 1_{R})r,
and moreover
h\cdot 1_{R}\in Z(R)
if the partial action
\cdot
is symmetric.

Now we present some lemmas that are useful for the next sections.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action,
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and
x\in P_{1,g}(\mathcal{H})
. If
g\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
, then
g
and
x
act globally, i.e.,
g\cdot(h\cdot r)=gh\cdot r
and
x\cdot(h\cdot r)=xh\cdot r
, for all
h\in\mathcal{H},r\in R
.

Proof.

Suppose that
g\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
.
Then, for all
h\in\mathcal{H},r\in R
,

g\cdot(h\cdot r)=(g\cdot 1_{R})(gh\cdot r)=gh\cdot r.

Also,

x\cdot 1_{R}=x\cdot 1_{R}^{2}=(x\cdot 1_{R})(1\cdot 1_{R})+(g\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot 1_{R})=2(x\cdot 1_{R}),

that is,
x\cdot 1_{R}=0
. Hence,

x\cdot(h\cdot r)=(x\cdot 1_{R})(h\cdot r)+(g\cdot 1_{R})(xh\cdot r)=xh\cdot r.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action,
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and
x\in P_{1,g}(\mathcal{H})
such that
xg=qgx
, for some
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.
If
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
,
then

(i)

x\cdot r=(x\cdot 1_{R})r
and
g^{-1}x\cdot r=-qr(x\cdot 1_{R})
, for all
r\in R
, and

(ii)

if
g^{2}=1
,
q=-1
and
x^{2}=0
, then
(x\cdot 1_{R})^{2}\in Z(R)
.

Proof.

Assume
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
.
Thus,
g\cdot r=g^{-1}\cdot r=0
, for all
r\in R
.

(i)
Since
x\in P_{1,g}(\mathcal{H})
, we have
g^{-1}x\in P_{g^{-1},1}(\mathcal{H})
.
Then
x\cdot r=x\cdot(1_{R}r)=(x\cdot 1_{R})r
and
g^{-1}x\cdot r=g^{-1}x\cdot(r1_{R})=r(g^{-1}x\cdot 1_{R})
. Moreover,

0=g^{-1}x\cdot(g\cdot 1_{R})=(g^{-1}x\cdot 1_{R})+(g^{-1}xg\cdot 1_{R}).

Then,
g^{-1}x\cdot 1_{R}=-q(x\cdot 1_{R})
and, therefore,
g^{-1}x\cdot r=-qr(x\cdot 1_{R})
.

(ii)
Assume that
g^{2}=1,q=-1
and
x^{2}=0
.
Thus
gx\cdot(gx\cdot r)=(gx\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot r)
.
By item (i), it follows that
r(x\cdot 1_{R})^{2}=(x\cdot 1_{R})^{2}r
, for all
r\in R
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action,
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and
x,y\in P_{1,g}(\mathcal{H})
such that
g^{2}=1
,
xg=-gx
,
yg=-gy
.
Then,

(i)

if
(x\cdot 1_{R}),(y\cdot 1_{R})\in Z(R)
and
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
, then
xy\cdot r=gxy\cdot r=0
, for all
r\in R
,

(ii)

if
h\in\mathcal{H}
satisfies
h\cdot r=gh\cdot r=0
, for some
r\in R
, then
xh\cdot r=gxh\cdot r=0
.

Proof.

First observe that, for all
r\in R
, we have

gx\cdot(y\cdot r)=(gx\cdot 1_{R})(gy\cdot r)+(gxy\cdot r)
and
gx\cdot(gy\cdot r)=(gx\cdot 1_{R})(y\cdot r)-(xy\cdot r).

Then, by Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle gxy\cdot r

\displaystyle=(y\cdot 1_{R})r(x\cdot 1_{R})-(x\cdot 1_{R})r(y\cdot 1_{R})\quad\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle xy\cdot r

\displaystyle=(x\cdot 1_{R})(y\cdot 1_{R})r-r(y\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot 1_{R}).

Since
(x\cdot 1_{R}),(y\cdot 1_{R})\in Z(R)
, it follows that
xy\cdot r=gxy\cdot r=0
, for all
r\in R
, and it concludes item (i).

For (ii), assume that
h\cdot r=gh\cdot r=0
.
Hence,

gx\cdot(gh\cdot r)=(gx\cdot 1_{R})(h\cdot r)-xh\cdot r
and
gx\cdot(h\cdot r)=(gx\cdot 1_{R})(gh\cdot r)+gxh\cdot r
, and so
xh\cdot r=gxh\cdot r=0
.
∎

2.3.
Hopf-Ore extensions

Let
A
be an algebra, and consider an automorphism
\sigma
of
A
and a
\sigma
-derivation
\delta
of
A
,
i.e.
, a linear map
\delta:A\to A
such that
\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+\sigma(a)\delta(b)
.
The
Ore extension
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
of the algebra
A
is the algebra generated by
A
and a variable
x
, subjected to the relation

(2.7)

xa=\sigma(a)x+\delta(a),

for all
a\in A
. Every element of
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
can uniquely be represented as
\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}x^{i}
, where
a_{i}\in A
.

In
[
15
]
, Panov determined necessary and sufficient conditions for an Ore extension
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
over a Hopf algebra
A
to have a structure of a Hopf algebra which extends the Hopf algebra structure of
A
and such that
x
is a skew-primitive element, that is,
\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+g\otimes x
, for some
g\in G(A)
. In this case,
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
is called a
Hopf-Ore extension of
A
.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
15
, Thm. 1.3]
The Hopf algebra
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
is a Hopf-Ore extension if only if

(i)

there exists a character
\chi:A\to\Bbbk
such that
\sigma(a)=\chi(a_{1})a_{2}=ga_{1}g^{-1}\chi(a_{2})
,

(ii)

the
\sigma
-derivation
\delta
satisfies
\Delta(\delta(a))=\delta(a_{1})\otimes a_{2}+ga_{1}\otimes\delta(a_{2})
.

Remark 2.7
.

Note that, as
\sigma
is an automorphism,
ax=x\sigma^{-1}(a)-\delta(\sigma^{-1}(a))
and
\sigma^{-1}(a)=\chi^{-1}(a_{1})a_{2}=g^{-1}a_{1}g\chi^{-1}(a_{2})
, for all
a\in A
. Moreover, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that

\Delta(\sigma^{-i}(a))=\sigma^{-i}(a_{1})\otimes a_{2}
and
\Delta(\sigma^{-i}(a))=g^{-i}a_{1}g^{i}\otimes\sigma^{-i}(a_{2})
.
Also,
\sigma^{-i}(a)=g^{-i}a_{1}g^{i}\chi^{-i}(a_{2})
and
\sigma^{-i}(g^{k})=\left(\chi(g)\right)^{-ik}g^{k}
.

3.
Extensions of partial actions via Hopf-Ore extensions

In this section, we characterize some partial actions of a Hopf-Ore extension
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
on
R
.
For a Hopf-Ore extension, the element
g\in G(A)
plays a crucial role since
\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+g\otimes x
.
Likewise, we check that the action of the element
x
is closely related to the action of the element
g
.

Initially, we prove a case when it is possible to extend a partial action of
A
on
R
to a partial action of
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
on
R
in a trivial way.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\cdot:A\otimes R\to R
be a (symmetric) partial action of a Hopf algebra
A
on an algebra
R
.
Given a Hopf-Ore extension
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
, consider
J
the ideal of
A
generated by
\delta(A)
.
If
J\cdot R=0
, then the map
\triangleright:A[x,\sigma,\delta]\otimes R\to R
, defined as
x^{j}a\triangleright r=\delta_{j,0}(a\cdot r)
, for all
a\in A
and
r\in R
, is a (symmetric) partial action.

Proof.

Suppose
J\cdot R=0
. By (
2.7
) we have
ax^{i}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{i}x^{\ell}B^{i}_{\ell}(a)
, for
B^{i}_{\ell}(a)\in A
, where
B^{i}_{i}(a)=\sigma^{-i}(a)
and
B^{i}_{0}(a)=(-1)^{i}(\delta\circ\sigma^{-1})^{i}(a)
, for all
a\in A
.
Moreover, since
\Delta(x^{j}a)=x^{j}a_{1}\otimes a_{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{j}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}x^{j-k}g^{k}a_{1}\otimes x^{k}a_{2}
, on the one hand we get

\displaystyle((x^{j}a)_{1}\triangleright r)((x^{j}a)_{2}x^{i}b\triangleright s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{j}a_{1}\triangleright r)(a_{2}x^{i}b\triangleright s)+\sum_{k=1}^{j}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x^{j-k}g^{k}a_{1}\triangleright r)(x^{k}a_{2}x^{i}b\triangleright s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{j}a_{1}\triangleright r)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{i}\left(x^{\ell}B^{i}_{\ell}(a_{2})b\triangleright s\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{j}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x^{j-k}g^{k}a_{1}\triangleright r)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{i}\left(x^{k+\ell}B^{i}_{\ell}(a_{2})b\triangleright s\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{j,0}(a_{1}\cdot r)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{i}\delta_{\ell,0}(B^{i}_{\ell}(a_{2})b\cdot s)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{j}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}\delta_{j-k,0}(g^{k}a_{1}\cdot r)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{i}\delta_{k+\ell,0}\left(B^{i}_{\ell}(a_{2})b\cdot s\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{j,0}(a_{1}\cdot r)(B^{i}_{0}(a_{2})b\cdot s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{j,0}(a_{1}\cdot r)((-1)^{i}(\delta\circ\sigma^{-1})^{i}(a_{2})b\cdot s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{j,0}\delta_{i,0}(a_{1}\cdot r)(a_{2}b\cdot s),

where, in the last equality, we use that
J\cdot R=0
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle x^{j}a\triangleright(r(x^{i}b\triangleright s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j}a\triangleright(\delta_{i,0}r(b\cdot s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{j,0}\delta_{i,0}(a\cdot(r(b\cdot s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{j,0}\delta_{i,0}(a_{1}\cdot r)(a_{2}b\cdot s).

Similarly, if the partial action
\cdot
is symmetric, then
\triangleright
is also symmetric.
∎

In particular, when
\delta=0
, the above result ensures that always is possible to extend any partial action of
A
on
R
to the Hopf-Ore extension
A[x,\sigma]:=A[x,\sigma,0]
, in the trivial way.

3.1.
Characterization of partial actions of Hopf-Ore extensions

Let
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
a partial action and
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
.
The element
g\cdot 1_{R}\in R
is always an idempotent element, and
R
has at least two:
0
and
1_{R}
.
As consequence of Lemma
2.3
, any partial action of
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
on
R
such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
is totally characterized by the partial action of
A
on
R
and a compatible skew-derivation determined by
x
.

We investigate now the case when
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
. In particular, in this case, we obtain that the partial action of
A[x,\sigma]
on
R
depends only on the partial action of
A
on
R
, a skew-derivation (determined by the particular element
x\cdot 1_{R}
), and a compatibility relation between them.

The following result shows this fact in a more general way.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hopf algebra,
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
a partial action,
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and
x\in P_{1,g}(\mathcal{H})
such that
gx=q^{-1}xg
, for some
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.
If
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
, then

(3.1)

\displaystyle x^{j}a\cdot r=\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j-k}(g^{k}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{k},

for any
a\in\mathcal{H},r\in R
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

First, by Lemma
2.4
we know that
g^{-1}x\cdot r=-qr(x\cdot 1_{R})
.
Now, we prove the desired equality by induction on
j
. It is immediate for
j=0
, and if one wants to check for
j=1
it follows by condition (PA.3) for the expression
g^{-1}x\cdot(ga\cdot r)
that
xa\cdot r=(x\cdot 1_{R})(a\cdot r)-(ga\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R}).

Assume
j\geq 0
.
Then, by (PA.3), we get

\displaystyle g^{-1}x\cdot(gx^{j}a\cdot r)=(g^{-1}x\cdot 1_{R})(g^{-1}gx^{j}a\cdot r)+(1\cdot 1_{R})(g^{-1}xgx^{j}a\cdot r),

that is,
-q(gx^{j}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})=-q(x\cdot 1_{R})(x^{j}a\cdot r)+q(x^{j+1}a\cdot r),

and thus
x^{j+1}a\cdot r=(x\cdot 1_{R})(x^{j}a\cdot r)-(gx^{j}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R}).

Now, by the induction hypothesis, we obtain

\displaystyle x^{j+1}a\cdot r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1_{R})\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j-k}(g^{k}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\!-\left(\!\!q^{-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}\!\!\!\!\!(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j-k}(g^{k+1}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{k}\!\!\right)\!\!(x\cdot 1_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j+1}(a\cdot r)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j+1-k}(g^{k}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{k}

\displaystyle-q^{-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j-k}(g^{k+1}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{k+1}

\displaystyle-q^{-j}(-1)^{j}q^{-\frac{j(j-1)}{2}}(g^{j+1}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j+1}(a\cdot r)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\left({j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}\!\!\!\!\!+q^{-j+(k-1)}{j\choose k-1}_{q^{-1}}\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j+1-k}

\displaystyle\times(g^{k}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{k}+(-1)^{j+1}q^{-\frac{j(j+1)}{2}}(g^{j+1}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j+1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{6.7}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1_{R})^{j+1}(a\cdot r)

It concludes the induction step, and so expression (

3.3.
Partial actions of Hopf-Ore extensions factorized through quotients

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hopf algebra and
I
a Hopf ideal of
\mathcal{H}
.
In general, we have that if

is a (symmetric) partial action such that

, then the map
\cdot
induces a (symmetric) partial action of
\mathcal{H}/I
on

.
Conversely, if there is a (symmetric) partial action of
\mathcal{H}/I
on

\mathcal{H}
on
R
.

In the previous section we determine conditions for extending a partial action of a Hopf algebra
A
on
R
to a partial action
\cdot:A[x,\sigma,\delta]\otimes R\to R
.
Then, if there is a Hopf ideal

of

such that

A[x,\sigma,\delta]/I
on
R
from partial actions of
A
on
R
.
Next, we investigate when such procedure is exhaustive.

Let
I
be a Hopf ideal of
A[x,\sigma,\delta]
and
\triangleright:A[x,\sigma,\delta]/I\otimes R\to R
a (symmetric) partial action.
Then
\triangleright
defines two (symmetric) partial actions
\cdot:A\otimes R\to R
and

\bullet:A[x,\sigma,\delta]\otimes R\to R
, via
a\cdot r=\overline{a}\triangleright r
and
x^{j}a\bullet r=\overline{x^{j}a}\triangleright r
, respectively.

If
\overline{g}\triangleright 1_{R}=1_{R}
, by Proposition
2.3
, the (symmetric) partial action
\bullet
coincides with the extension of the (symmetric) partial action
\cdot
to the Hopf Ore extension. Now, if
\overline{g}\triangleright 1_{R}=0
and
\delta=0
, the (symmetric) partial action
\bullet
coincides with the extension of the (symmetric) partial action
\cdot
to the Hopf Ore extension, putting

, since that the map
\cdot
satisfies the equalities (
3.7
) (and (
3.8
)).

Thus, in both cases

, which turns out to be the original one
\triangleright
,
i.e.
, for these cases such procedure is exhaustive.

To finish this section, we determine sufficient conditions to induce a partial action of
A[x,\sigma]
to a partial action of the quotient

, for two specific families of Hopf ideals

.
Such results allow us to compute in §

some (symmetric) partial actions of the rank one Hopf algebras.

and
x\cdot 1_{R}=0
, or

(iii)

g\cdot 1_{R}=0,g^{d}\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
and
(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}\,r=(g^{d}\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}
, for all
r\in R
,

then
\cdot{|_{I\otimes R}}=0
, where
I
is the (Hopf) ideal
\langle x^{d}\rangle
.
In particular, the partial action
\cdot
induces a partial action of
A[x,\sigma]/I
on
R
.

Proof.

First assume the conditions in
(i)
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
x
acts globally.
Hence, for
j\geq d
and
a\in A
, we have

\displaystyle x^{j}a\cdot r=x^{j-d}\cdot(x^{d}\cdot(a\cdot r))=0,

since the action by
x
is
d
-nilpotent.

Now assume that
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
.
Corollary
3.3
shows us that (
3.1
) holds.
Then, by Lemma
3.5
, we get that

\displaystyle x^{j}a\cdot r

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell},

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},a\in A,r\in R
.

If
x\cdot 1_{R}=0
, clearly
x^{j}a\cdot r=0
, for
j\geq d
.

At last, assume conditions in
(iii)
.
Thus,
g^{d\ell}\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
, for
\ell\geq 0
, what implies that

g^{d\ell}\cdot(y\cdot r)=g^{d\ell}y\cdot r
for all
y\in A[x,\sigma],r\in R
.
Furthermore, from
(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}\,r=(g^{d}\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}
, it follows that
(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}\,r=(g^{d\ell}\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle x^{j}a\cdot r

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(g^{d\ell}x^{j_{R}}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(g^{d\ell}\cdot(x^{j_{R}}a\cdot r)\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{dj_{D}}\left(x^{j_{R}}a\cdot r\right)

\displaystyle=(x\cdot 1_{R})^{dj_{D}}\left(x^{j_{R}}a\cdot r\right)\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(-1)^{\ell}=0,

for all
j\geq d
and
a\in A
.
Therefore, in any case, we have
\cdot{|_{I\otimes R}}=0
and so it induces a partial action of
A[x,\sigma]/I
on
R
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
A[x,\sigma]
be a Hopf-Ore extension such that
q=\chi(g)\in\mathbb{G}^{\prime}_{d},

g^{d}\in Z(A)
and
\chi^{d}=1
.
If
\cdot:A[x,\sigma]\otimes R\to R
is a partial action such that:

(i)

g\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
and
(x^{d}+g^{d})\cdot r=r
, for all
r\in R
, or

(ii)

g\cdot 1_{R}=0
,
g^{d}\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
and
[(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}-1_{R}]\,r=(g^{d}\cdot r)[(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}-1_{R}]
, for all
r\in R
,

then
\cdot{|_{I\otimes R}}=0
, where
I
is the (Hopf) ideal
\langle x^{d}+g^{d}-1\rangle
.
In particular, the partial action
\cdot
induces a partial action of
A[x,\sigma]/I
on
R
.

Proof.

First observe that
x^{j}(x^{d}+g^{d}-1)a
,
j\geq 0,a\in A
, generate linearly the ideal
I
since
x^{d},g^{d}\in Z(A[x,\sigma])
.

If
(i)
holds, then Lemma
2.3
tells us that
g
and
x
act globally.
Thus,

\displaystyle x^{j}(x^{d}+g^{d}-1)a\cdot r=x^{j}\cdot((x^{d}+g^{d}-1)\cdot(a\cdot r))=0,

for all
j\geq 0,a\in A,r\in R
.

On the other hand, assume the conditions in
(ii)
. Again we get from Corollary
3.3
that (
3.1
) remains valid and consequently Lemma
3.5
gives us that

\displaystyle x^{j}a\cdot r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell},

for all
j\geq 0,a\in A,r\in R
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle x^{j}(x^{d}+g^{d}-1)a\cdot r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j+d}a\cdot r+x^{j}g^{d}a\cdot r-x^{j}a\cdot r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}+1}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}+1}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}+1-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d(\ell+1)}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}+1}(-1)^{\ell}\left(\binom{j_{D}}{\ell-1}+\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}+1-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left[\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d(\ell+1)}a-x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\right)\cdot r\right](x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell+1}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d(\ell+1)}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(\ell+1)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}+1-\ell)}\left(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left[x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a(g^{d}-1)\cdot r\right](x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{j_{D}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j_{D}}{\ell}(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d(j_{D}-\ell)}\left(\star\right)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d\ell},

where
(\star)
is

\displaystyle x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a(g^{d}-1)\cdot r-(x^{j_{R}}g^{d(\ell+1)}a\cdot r)(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}+(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r).

But, by conditions
(ii)
, it follows that

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r+(g^{d}\cdot(x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r))((x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}-1_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r+(g^{d}\cdot 1_{R})(g^{d}x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r)((x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}-1_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j_{R}}g^{d\ell}a\cdot r+(x^{j_{R}}g^{d(\ell+1)}a\cdot r)((x\cdot 1_{R})^{d}-1_{R}),

what implies that
x^{j}(x^{d}+g^{d}-1)a\cdot r=0
, for all
j\geq 0,a\in A,r\in R
.
Therefore, in any case, we have
\cdot{|_{I\otimes R}}=0
and so it induces a partial action of
A[x,\sigma]/I
on
R
.
∎

Recall the discussion after Remark

There, we obtain a sufficient condition to extend a partial action through a Hopf-Ore extension, but we pointed out that it may be not necessary.

4.1.
The particular case when the element

We improve, under hypothesis, some results within §
3
, as follows.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\cdot
be a partial
action of
A[x,\sigma]
on
R

such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
and
x\cdot 1_{R}\in Z(R)
. If

ga\cdot 1_{R}=ag\cdot 1_{R}
, for

, then
a\cdot(x\cdot 1_{R})=(\sigma^{-1}(a)\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot 1_{R}).

Indeed, by Corollary

,

\displaystyle a\cdot(x\cdot 1_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(\sigma^{-1}(a_{2})\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot 1_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{-1}a_{1}g\cdot 1_{R})(\sigma^{-1}(a_{2})\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot 1_{R})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{remark_cond_hopf ore}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(\sigma^{-1}(a)_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(\sigma^{-1}(a)_{2}\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot 1_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma^{-1}(a)\cdot 1_{R})(x\cdot 1_{R}).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\cdot:A\otimes R\to R
be a partial action,
g\in G(A)
such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
, and
a\cdot w=(\sigma^{-1}(a)\cdot 1_{R})w
, for
a\in A
and

. Then, the following properties are satisfied:

(i)

a\cdot w^{j}=(\sigma^{-j}(a)\cdot 1_{R})w^{j}

(ii)

if

, then
(a_{1}\cdot w^{j})(a_{2}g^{k}b\cdot r)=(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(g^{k}\sigma^{-j}(a_{2})b\cdot r)w^{j}

(iii)

if

and
\cdot
is symmetric, then
(a_{1}g^{k}b\cdot r)(a_{2}\cdot w^{j})=(g^{k}\sigma^{-j}(a_{1})b\cdot r)(a_{2}\cdot 1_{R})w^{j}
,

for all
r\in R
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

(i)
We prove the desired equality by induction on
j
.
It is immediate for
j=0
, and
j=1
is valid by hypothesis. Now, suppose true for
j\geq 0
, then by the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle a\cdot w^{j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot w)(a_{2}\cdot w^{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma^{-1}(a_{1})\cdot 1_{R})(a_{2}\cdot w^{j})w

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{remark_cond_hopf ore}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(\sigma^{-1}(a)\cdot w^{j})w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma^{-j}(\sigma^{-1}(a))\cdot 1_{R})w^{j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma^{-(j+1)}(a)\cdot 1_{R})w^{j+1}.

(ii)
Using item (i) and
ga\cdot r=ag\cdot r
,

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot w^{j})(a_{2}g^{k}b\cdot r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot(w^{j}(g^{k}b\cdot r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot(w^{j}(bg^{k}\cdot r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot w^{j})(a_{2}bg^{k}\cdot r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma^{-j}(a_{1})\cdot 1_{R})(a_{2}bg^{k}\cdot r)w^{j}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{remark_cond_hopf ore}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(\sigma^{-j}(a_{2})bg^{k}\cdot r)w^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(g^{k}\sigma^{-j}(a_{2})b\cdot r)w^{j}.

(iii)
Analogously,

\displaystyle(a_{1}g^{k}b\cdot r)(a_{2}\cdot w^{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}bg^{k}\cdot r)(\sigma^{-j}(a_{2})\cdot 1_{R})w^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{k}\sigma^{-j}(a_{1})b\cdot r)(a_{2}\cdot 1_{R})w^{j}.

∎

Thus, we obtain the following characterization.

.

Let
\cdot:A[x,\sigma]\otimes R\to R
be a linear map such that

,

and

on

\cdot|_{A\otimes R}
is a (symmetric) partial action of
A
on
R
,

, for all

, and
\cdot
coincides with (

), where

and

.

Proof.

The first part follows directly of Theorem

and Remark

,

and

.
∎

4.4.
The particular case when

In this section, we apply the developed results to characterize some partial actions of Hopf-Ore extensions of Sweedler’s Hopf algebra
\mathbb{H}_{4}
on an algebra

.
In the examples treated previously we usually deal with a Hopf-Ore extension of a group algebra, and in such case

is a central element.
So, the importance of dealing with
\mathbb{H}_{4}
relies on the fact that it is the smallest Hopf algebra that is not a group algebra and contains a grouplike element which is not central.

Consider a Hopf-Ore extension
\mathbb{H}_{4}[y,\sigma]
. First of all, we notice that any partial action
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{4}[y,\sigma]\otimes R\to R
such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
is actually a global action. Indeed, it follows by Proposition
2.3
, since
\mathbb{H}_{4}[y,\sigma]
is generated as algebra by
g,x
and
y
, and both
x
and
y
are
(1,g)
-primitive elements.

The global actions of
\mathbb{H}_{4}
on
R
are characterized in
[
6
, Prop. 4.1]
: an automorphism
\alpha_{g}
of
R
such that
\alpha_{g}^{2}=Id_{R}
, and an
\alpha_{g}
-derivation
\partial_{x}
of
R
such that
\alpha_{g}\partial_{x}=-\partial_{x}\alpha_{g}
and
\partial_{x}^{2}=0
.

Hence, the global actions of
\mathbb{H}_{4}[y,\sigma]
on
R
are characterized by a global action of
\mathbb{H}_{4}
plus an
\alpha_{g}
-derivation
\partial_{y}
of
R
such that
\partial_{y}\alpha_{g}=-\alpha_{g}\partial_{y}
and
\partial_{y}\partial_{x}=-\partial_{x}\partial_{y}
.

Now, we characterize the partial actions
\mathbb{H}_{4}[y,\sigma]
on
R
such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
.
We begin with some general lemmas.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action,
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and

such that

, for some

, and
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
. Consider
\mathcal{H}[y,\sigma]
and the linear map defined in (

).
Then, for any

:

(i)

(

) holds for
a\in\{1,g,x\}
,

(ii)

(

a\in\{1,g^{-1},g^{-1}x\}
.

Proof.

Initially, by Lemma

,

and

, where

. Moreover,
g\cdot r=g^{-1}\cdot r=0
, for all

.

(i)
The equation (

or

.
Now, for

\Delta_{2}(x)=x\otimes 1\otimes 1+g\otimes\Delta(x)
, for all

we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(a_{1}\cdot w^{j-k})(a_{2}g^{k}b\cdot r)(a_{3}\cdot w^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot w^{j-k})(g^{k}b\cdot r)w^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}\Omega w^{j-k}(g^{k}b\cdot r)w^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})w^{j-k}(g^{k}\sigma^{-j}(a_{2})b\cdot r)w^{k}.

(ii)
Analogous to item
(i)
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Let
R
be an algebra and
\Omega,w\in R
.
If
w\Omega+\Omega w\in Z(R)
, then
w^{2\ell}\Omega=\Omega w^{2\ell}
, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Indeed, for any
r\in R
,
r(w\Omega+\Omega w)=(w\Omega+\Omega w)r
, since
w\Omega+\Omega w\in Z(R)
.
Then, for
r=w
, it holds that
w^{2}\Omega=\Omega w^{2}
, and so the result follows by induction on
\ell
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\cdot:\mathcal{H}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action,
g\in G(\mathcal{H})
and
x\in P_{1,g}(\mathcal{H})
such that
g^{2}=1
,
x^{2}=0
and
gx=-xg
, and
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
.
Consider
\mathcal{H}[y,\sigma]
, where
q=-1
, and the linear map defined in (
3.2
).
Then, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

(
3.7
) holds for
a=gx
and
b\in\{1,g,x,gx\}
,

(ii)

(
3.8
) holds for
a=x
and
b\in\{1,g,x,gx\}
,

(iii)

(x\cdot 1_{R})w+w(x\cdot 1_{R})\in Z(R)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
,
x\cdot r=\Omega r
and
gx\cdot r=r\Omega
, where
\Omega:=x\cdot 1_{R}\in R
. Moreover,
g\cdot r=0
, for all
r\in R
and
\Omega^{2}\in Z(R)
.

Let’s check that
(i)
if only if
(iii)
.
As
q=-1
,
gx\in P_{g,1}(\mathcal{H})
and
\Delta_{2}(gx)=gx\otimes g\otimes g+1\otimes gx\otimes g+1\otimes 1\otimes gx
, for
a=gx
in (
3.7
) one gets the equivalent expression

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}w^{j-k}(g^{k}b\cdot r)w^{k}\Omega\,=\\
&\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}\hskip-5.69046pt\left((-1)^{j}\Omega w^{j-k}(g^{k+1}b\cdot r)+w^{j-k}(g^{k+1}xb\cdot r)\right)\!w^{k}.\end{split}

In particular, for
j=1
and
b=g
, we obtain
-rw\Omega=-\Omega wr-w\Omega r+r\Omega w
, that is,

\displaystyle r(\Omega w+w\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Omega w+w\Omega)r,

for all
r\in R
. Therefore,
\Omega w+w\Omega\in Z(R)
.

Conversely, assume
\Omega w+w\Omega\in Z(R)
.
Then, by Remark
4.9
,
w^{2\ell}\Omega=\Omega w^{2\ell}
, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Moreover, since
q=-1
, if
j
is an even number,
{j\choose k}_{-1}=0
for all
k
odd (see §
2.1
), and so equality (
4.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}w^{j-k}(b\cdot r)w^{k}\Omega\,=\\
&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}\hskip-5.69046pt\left(\Omega w^{j-k}(gb\cdot r)+w^{j-k}(gxb\cdot r)\right)\!w^{k}.\end{split}

Then, it is straightforward to check that

w^{j-k}(b\cdot r)w^{k}\Omega=\left(\Omega w^{j-k}(gb\cdot r)+w^{j-k}(gxb\cdot r)\right)\!w^{k},

holds for
b\in\{1,g,x,gx\}
, and so (
4.1
).

Now, assume
j
an odd number.
To show that (
4.1
) holds for each
b\in\{1,g,x,gx\}
, we proceed by cases.
Recall that
g\cdot r=0
.

If
b=1
, then (
4.1
) means

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}w^{j-k}rw^{k}\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{odd}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}\hskip-5.69046pt\left(-\Omega w^{j-k}r+w^{j-k}\Omega r\right)\!w^{k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}\hskip-5.69046ptw^{j-k}r\Omega w^{k},

which is true since
w^{k}\Omega=\Omega w^{k}
(
k
even) and
w^{j-k}\Omega=\Omega w^{j-k}
(
k
odd).

If
b=g
, then (
4.1
) means

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{odd}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}w^{j-k}rw^{k}\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}\hskip-5.69046pt\left(-\Omega w^{j-k}r-w^{j-k}\Omega r\right)\!w^{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{odd}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}w^{j-k}r\Omega w^{k},

that is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{odd}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}w^{j-k}rw^{k-1}\left(w\Omega+\Omega w\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}\left(\Omega w+w\Omega\right)w^{j-k-1}rw^{k}.

By a change of variable, the last equality becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(1-k)}{2}}{j\choose k+1}_{-1}w^{j-k-1}rw^{k}\left(w\Omega+\Omega w\right)\\
=&-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{j}(-1)^{\frac{k(3-k)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{-1}\left(\Omega w+w\Omega\right)w^{j-k-1}rw^{k},\end{split}

which is true since
\Omega w+w\Omega\in Z(R)
, and
{j\choose k+1}_{-1}={j_{D}\choose k_{D}}={j\choose k}_{-1}
for
0\leq k\leq j
when

is odd and
k
is even (see §

).

Similarly, one checks for
b=x
and
b=gx
, using
\Omega^{2}\in Z(R)
.
Therefore, (
4.1
), and consequently (
3.7
), holds for
a=gx
and
b\in\{1,g,x,gx\}
.
It concludes
(i)\Longleftrightarrow(iii)
.
Analogously we obtain
(ii)\Longleftrightarrow(iii)
.
∎

Then, we characterize some partial actions of a Hopf-Ore extensions of
\mathbb{H}_{4}
on

, as follows.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{4}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action such that

Then the linear map
\cdot
defined in (
3.2
) is a symmetric partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{4}[y,\sigma]
on

if and only if
w\Omega+\Omega w\in Z(R)
, where

and

.

Proof.

It follows from Lemmas

and

.
∎

4.5.
Nichols Hopf algebras

In this section, we apply the developed results to describe some examples of partial actions of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
R
.
The results presented here are already known in the literature
[
10
, §4.1]
, but we describe how to get them in this context of Hopf-Ore extensions and quotients, inductively.

We start recalling the family of Nichols Hopf algebras
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
.
They were originally introduced in
[
17
]
, but were named after the work of Nichols
[
14
]
.
For convenience, we present them as in
[
8
, §2.2]
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer.
As algebra,
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
is generated by the letters
g,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}
subject to the relations

\displaystyle g^{2}=1,

\displaystyle x_{i}g=-gx_{i},

\displaystyle x_{i}x_{j}=-x_{j}x_{i},

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.
Its coalgebra structure is determined by
g\in G(\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}})
and
x_{i}\in P_{1,g}(\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}})
.

Note that such Hopf algebras can be obtained by a iterated process of Hopf-Ore extensions and quotients as follows.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{2^{2}}=\mathbb{H}_{4}=\Bbbk\langle g,x_{1}\ |\ g^{2}=1,x_{1}^{2}=0,x_{1}g=-gx_{1}\rangle
be the Sweedler’s Hopf algebra; for
n\geq 2
one obtains inductively

\mathbb{H}_{2^{n+1}}:=\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]/\langle x_{n}^{2}\rangle
, where
\sigma_{n}:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
is the automorphism given by
\sigma_{n}(g)=-g
and

\sigma_{n}(x_{i})=-x_{i}
, for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.

At this first moment, we note that any partial action
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\otimes R\to R
such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=1_{R}
is a global action.
Since
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
is generated as algebra by
g,x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}
, and each
x_{i}
is a
(1,g)
-primitive element, we get it by Lemma
2.3
.
Namely, the grouplike element
g
acts by an automorphism
\alpha_{g}
of
R
such that
\alpha_{g}^{2}=Id_{R}
, and each skew-primitive element
x_{i}
acts by an
\alpha_{g}
-derivation
\partial_{i}
of
R
such that
\alpha_{g}\partial_{i}=-\partial_{i}\alpha_{g}
and
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}=-\partial_{j}\partial_{i}
, for
i,j\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.
In particular, any map
\partial_{i}
is 2-nilpotent.

Consequently,
we can extend such action to a partial (indeed global) action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]
on
R
, where the skew-primitive element
x_{n}
acts by any
\alpha_{g}
-derivation
\partial_{n}
of
R
satisfying analogous relations to the previous ones,
i.e.
, such that
\alpha_{g}\partial_{n}=-\partial_{n}\alpha_{g}
and
\partial_{n}\partial_{j}=-\partial_{j}\partial_{n}
, for any
j\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.

Finally, if we have that
\partial_{n}
is
2
-nilpotent, then this action factorizes through
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]/\langle x_{n}^{2}\rangle
and it is a global action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n+1}}
on
R
.

Next, our efforts are to obtain the class of symmetric partial actions
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n+1}}
on
R
such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
and
x_{i}\cdot 1_{R}\in Z(R)
, for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n}
, through an iterated process.

First, consider a (symmetric) partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{4}
on
R
given by
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
and
x\cdot 1_{R}=w_{1}\in Z(R)
,
and
\mathbb{H}_{2^{2}}[x_{2},\sigma_{2}]
.
By Proposition
4.11
, any choice
w_{2}\in Z(R)
defines a symmetric partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{2}}[x_{2},\sigma_{2}]
on
R
through expression (
3.2
), putting
x_{2}\cdot 1_{R}:=w_{2}
.
Moreover, by Lemma
3.5
,
x_{2}^{j}a\cdot r=0
for all
j\geq 2
, and so this partial action induces a symmetric partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{3}}=\mathbb{H}_{2^{2}}[x_{2},\sigma_{2}]/\langle x_{2}^{2}\rangle
on
R
.
Furthermore, by Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, it is easy to conclude that such symmetric partial action satisfies
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
,
x_{i}\cdot r=gx_{i}\cdot r=w_{i}r
, for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{2}
, and
x_{1}x_{2}\cdot r=gx_{1}x_{2}\cdot r=0
.

Now, let
n\geq 2
and
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\otimes R\to R
be a symmetric partial action given by
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
and
x_{i}\cdot 1_{R}:=w_{i}\in Z(R)
, for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.
Again, by Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we also conclude
x_{i}\cdot r=gx_{i}\cdot r=w_{i}r
and
g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot r=0
, for
\ell\in\{0,1\},

s\geq 2
and
i,i_{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.

To extend this partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
R
to a symmetric partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]
on
R
via (
3.2
), we have to investigate if conditions (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) hold for every
a,b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}},r\in R
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consider
w_{n}:=x_{n}\cdot 1_{R}\in Z(R)
; by Lemma
3.5
, it follows that
x_{n}^{j}b\cdot r=0
for
j\geq 2
and, consequently, the above conditions are valid for
j\geq 2
, since
ax_{n}^{j}=x_{n}^{j}\sigma_{n}^{-j}(a)
.
Hence, we just need to verify them for
j=1
.

To do so, we present a similar result to Lemma
4.10
as follows:

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\otimes R\to R
be a partial action such that
g\cdot 1_{R}=0
and
x_{i}\cdot 1_{R}\in Z(R)
, for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.
Consider
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]
and the linear map defined in (
3.2
). Then, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

(
3.7
) holds for
j=1
,
a=gx_{i}
and
b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
,

(ii)

(
3.8
) holds for
j=1
,
a=x_{i}
and
b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
,

(iii)

(x_{i}\cdot 1_{R})(x_{n}\cdot 1_{R})\in Z(R)
.

Proof.

Analogous to the proof of Lemma
4.10
.
∎

Now, we check (
3.3
) for
j=1
proceeding by cases.
First, by Lemma
4.8
, it holds for
a=1
,
a=g
or
a=x_{i},
for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
, and any
b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
.
Moreover, since
w_{i}\in Z(R)
for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n}
, by Lemma
4.12
it also holds for
a=gx_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
, and any
b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
.
Therefore, all that remains is to check the condition for
a=g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}...x_{i_{s}}
, where
\ell\in\{0,1\}
and
s\geq 2
, and any
b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
.

One the one hand,

(4.2)

\begin{split}a\cdot(x_{n}b\cdot r)&=a\cdot\left(w_{n}(b\cdot r)-(gb\cdot r)w_{n}\right)\\
&=(a_{1}\cdot w_{n})(a_{2}b\cdot r)-(a_{1}gb\cdot r)(a_{2}\cdot w_{n}),\end{split}

where last equality follows from the symmetry of the partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
R
.
On the other hand,

(4.3)

\begin{split}&(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(a_{2}x_{n}b\cdot r)\\
=&\ (a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(x_{n}\sigma_{n}^{-1}(a_{2})b\cdot r)\\
=&\ (a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})\left(w_{n}(\sigma_{n}^{-1}(a_{2})b\cdot r)-(g\sigma_{n}^{-1}(a_{2})b\cdot r)w_{n}\right)\\
=&\ (a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})w_{n}(\sigma_{n}^{-1}(a_{2})b\cdot r)-(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(g\sigma_{n}^{-1}(a_{2})b\cdot r)w_{n}.\end{split}

First, if
a=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
, then

\displaystyle\Delta(x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}})=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\otimes 1-gx_{i_{1}}\otimes x_{i_{2}}+gx_{i_{2}}\otimes x_{i_{1}}+1\otimes x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}},

and so (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) mean, respectively,

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot w_{n})(a_{2}b\cdot r)-(a_{1}gb\cdot r)(a_{2}\cdot w_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(gx_{i_{1}}\cdot w_{n})(x_{i_{2}}b\cdot r)+(gx_{i_{2}}\cdot w_{n})(x_{i_{1}}b\cdot r)+(gx_{i_{1}}gb\cdot r)(x_{i_{2}}\cdot w_{n})

\displaystyle-(gx_{i_{2}}gb\cdot r)(x_{i_{1}}\cdot w_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-w_{i_{1}}w_{n}(x_{i_{2}}b\cdot r)+w_{i_{2}}w_{n}(x_{i_{1}}b\cdot r)-(x_{i_{1}}b\cdot r)w_{i_{2}}w_{n}+(x_{i_{2}}b\cdot r)w_{i_{1}}w_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 0,

and

\displaystyle(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})w_{n}(\sigma_{n}^{-1}(a_{2})b\cdot r)-(a_{1}\cdot 1_{R})(g\sigma_{n}^{-1}(a_{2})b\cdot r)w_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(gx_{i_{1}}\cdot 1_{R})w_{n}(\sigma_{n}^{-1}(x_{i_{2}})b\cdot r)+(gx_{i_{2}}\cdot 1_{R})w_{n}(\sigma_{n}^{-1}(x_{i_{1}})b\cdot r)

\displaystyle+(gx_{i_{1}}\cdot 1_{R})(g\sigma_{n}^{-1}(x_{i_{2}})b\cdot r)w_{n}-(gx_{i_{2}}\cdot 1_{R})(g\sigma_{n}^{-1}(x_{i_{1}})b\cdot r)w_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ w_{i_{1}}w_{n}(x_{i_{2}}b\cdot r)-w_{i_{2}}w_{n}(x_{i_{1}}b\cdot r)-w_{i_{1}}(gx_{i_{2}}b\cdot r)w_{n}+w_{i_{2}}(gx_{i_{1}}b\cdot r)w_{n}.

Hence, (
3.3
) holds for
a=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
if and only if

w_{i_{1}}w_{n}(x_{i_{2}}b\cdot r)+w_{i_{2}}(gx_{i_{1}}b\cdot r)w_{n}=w_{i_{2}}w_{n}(x_{i_{1}}b\cdot r)+w_{i_{1}}(gx_{i_{2}}b\cdot r)w_{n},

for any
b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}},r\in R
.
But, it is an easy computation to conclude that indeed
w_{i}(x_{j}b\cdot r)=w_{i}(gx_{j}b\cdot r)=w_{j}(x_{i}b\cdot r)=w_{j}(gx_{i}b\cdot r)
. Analogously, the same conclusion holds for
a=gx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
.

Now, consider
a=g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}
, where
\ell\in\{0,1\}
and
s\geq 3
, and write
a=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}y
, for some
y\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}y_{1}\otimes y_{2}-gx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{3}}y_{1}\otimes x_{i_{2}}y_{2}

\displaystyle+\ gx_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}y_{1}\otimes x_{i_{1}}y_{2}+x_{i_{3}}y_{1}\otimes x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}y_{2}

\displaystyle+\ gx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}y_{1}\otimes x_{i_{3}}y_{2}+x_{i_{1}}y_{1}\otimes x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}y_{2}

\displaystyle-\ x_{i_{2}}y_{1}\otimes x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{3}}y_{2}+gy_{1}\otimes x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}y_{2}.

Then, clearly both expressions (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) vanish, and so (
3.3
) holds, for any
b\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}},r\in R
.

To prove the symmetric condition (
3.4
), one proceeds analogously.

Therefore, any choice of
w_{n}\in Z(R)
defines a symmetric partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]
on
R
through the expression (
3.2
), putting
x_{n}\cdot 1_{R}:=w_{n}
.
At last, again by Lemma
3.5
, this action induces a symmetric partial action of the quotient
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n+1}}=\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}[x_{n},\sigma_{n}]/\langle x_{n}^{2}\rangle
on
R
.

Hence, in the above discussion, we reobtain the characterization of some partial actions of Nichols Hopf algebras on algebras given in
[
10
, §4.1]
, but in the context of Hopf-Ore extensions and quotients.

In particular, we recover some examples of partial actions of pointed Hopf algebras of dimensions 8 and 16 over their base fields

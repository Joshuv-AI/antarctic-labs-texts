Effect of WhatsApp Messenger-based education on the knowledge of health ambassadors about home care for minor illnesses
Authors: Fatemeh Abbasian, Mohammad Mehdi Naghizadeh, Leila Ahmadian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn the digital era, virtual education has become one of the most effective educational methods, in which, the latest educational materials can be made available to people using social network platforms. Health ambassadors are mediators for transferring health-related knowledge to the public, so, they must be aware of various health topics, such as home care for minor illnesses. This study aimed to determine the effects of social media-based interventions (using WhatsApp) on the knowledge of health ambassadors concerning home care for minor illnesses.MethodsThis research was an interventional study carried out in 2021 among health ambassadors in the urban and rural areas of Fasa, a city in the southeast of Fars province. In this study, 121 health ambassadors were divided into control and intervention groups. The educational content is designed based on the Encyclopedia of Self-care Guide in Minor Illnesses and was presented in text, image, and video formats via WhatsApp Messenger to the intervention group on a two-day interval for two weeks. The knowledge level was assessed using a valid and reliable questionnaire developed by researchers.ResultsAfter education through WhatsApp, the level of knowledge significantly improved in the intervention group from 30.34 ± 5.58 to 44.80 ± 8.49 (P < 0.001). No significant change was observed in the control group, 34.98 ± 7.19 to 35.67 ± 9.66 (P = 0.431). By controlling the effect of gender, age, marital status, occupation, education, and previous knowledge about minor illnesses by regression analysis, there was a significant improvement in knowledge in the intervention compared to the control group.ConclusionsEducation through WhatsApp messenger elevated the level of knowledge of health ambassadors about minor illnesses. Generally, using communication technologies for healthcare workers can improve patients and their caregiver’s healthcare services and education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-024-11988-9.Keywords: Home care, Self-care, Health ambassadors, Minor illnesses, Messenger, WhatsApp, Social network

Lay summary

Virtual education is an effective method for delivering educational materials in the digital era. WhatsApp can be used to increase the knowledge level of health ambassadors regarding home care for minor illnesses.

Introduction

The advancement of communication and information science has opened up new opportunities for virtual education [1]. Virtual education is a dynamic and modern approach [2] that delivers the latest educational content in a timely and cost-effective manner, regardless of location or time [3]. By utilizing social media networks and messaging applications educational costs can be reduced [4]. Virtual education eliminates the need for physical learning spaces for learners, significantly reducing the costs associated with establishing and maintaining such spaces [5].

Online education, has significant advantages over traditional methods. These include flexibility in time and location, access to multimedia information, real-time interaction between the instructors and the learners, and a shorter time needed for education, all of which contribute to better learner engagement [6]. Additionally, using virtual tools and resources such as text, sound, images, and videos makes online education an attractive option for learners [7].

Since the health care system aims to nurture responsible individuals who are aware and make informed decisions about their health, whether ill or healthy [8], online education allows them to receive educational materials quickly and easily. Furthermore, online education enables individuals to interact with other community members to discuss and share information about their overall health [9]. By educating individuals, they can take part in their self-care and prevent many possible health risks and complications [10].

Minor illnesses are diseases that typically improve within a short time without the need for visits to doctors or medical centers. These conditions can often be managed through self-care or basic home remedies. Minor illnesses are common problems worldwide, accounting for nearly 20% of patient visits to doctors and medical centers. Many of these illnesses can be managed by patients through self-care without therapeutics or with basic home care [11].

Increasing home care skills is a critical solution for healthcare policymakers as it can significantly reduce treatment and care costs. Approximately 65 to 85% of care activities using this method can be performed by the individual or family members without the help of a physician [12]. By acquiring self-care behaviors, patients can improve their overall well-being, functionality, and disease management process [13].

Taking preventive measures and appropriate home care behaviors can prevent the progression of some diseases to a certain degree [14]. Home care reduces the need for health services at the primary care level and increases patient satisfaction, quality of life, and the overall functioning of healthcare systems [15].

In Iran, the Ministry of Health has been training individuals under the title "Health Ambassadors" since 2013 [16]. A voluntary community member with at least 8 literacy classes who is responsible for sharing educated knowledge on health topics and advocating for proactive health care for themselves, their family, and society. The concept of "Every House is a Health Base" aims to promote community engagement in promoting, maintaining, and enhancing health across the country. In this program, one person from each household is trained and empowered as a "Family Health Ambassador" to take care of themselves, their family, and their community [17].

Considering the expansion of social networks in Iran and the fact that Iranian users are ranked fifth in terms of social media and messenger usage, these platforms can be utilized to educate and empower individuals about healthcare [18]. On the other hand, among the determinants of health, behaviors that promote well-being, such as home care, are recognized as fundamental preventive measures to combat diseases, particularly chronic illnesses. Therefore, home care should be prioritized as the primary strategy to maintain and improve public health [19].

Previous studies in health education have shown that there are various educational techniques and methods available to improve knowledge, attitude, and behavior [20, 21]. With the widespread use of electronic gadgets, the question arises: can these tools be effectively utilized for educational purposes? The present study aimed to investigate the impact of using WhatsApp Messenger on ambassadors the knowledge’s health knowledge regarding home care for minor illnesses in the city of Fasa, located in south Iran.

Methods

Participants, eligibility criteria, and sample size

Our study population consists of individuals who are selected and recruited every month, to serve as health ambassadors in various settings including villages, urban and rural centers, comprehensive health centers, and family doctor's offices. These volunteers undergo training sessions before beginning to provide services to their respective communities.

The criteria for participation included having at least 8 years of schooling, being able to use WhatsApp Messenger, and owning a smartphone. Participants who were dissatisfied could choose to withdraw from the study.

The sample size for this study was estimated based on research conducted by Motaghi et al. [22]. Overall, the mean score for the non-community health worker group was 75.49 ± 3.3, while the community health worker group was 69.86 ± 2.9. Standard deviations (s1 = 3.3, s2 = 2.9) were used to calculate a 40% difference in knowledge means between groups (d = (75 – 70) * 0.4 = 2) with a power of 90% (β = 0.1, Z = 1.28) and a confidence level of 95% (α = 0.05, Z = 1.96), resulting in a minimum sample size requirement of 51 subjects per group.

The research team distributed a knowledge assessment questionnaire to 140 health ambassadors, of which 121 completed it. Sixty-one individuals were randomly allocated to the training group, while 60 individuals were placed in the control group. These samples were chosen from participants in two consecutive health ambassador training courses in the city of Fasa, using the purposeful sampling method.

The individuals were divided into two groups using the permutation block method. An appropriate number of AB and BA codes were generated using the MS Excel random number function, and the order of individuals' entry into the study was determined based on the consecutive order of these codes. This ensured that each group had an equal number of individuals. Furthermore, the researcher's intervention during the grouping process was kept to a minimum.

Study design

This research was an intervention study conducted in 2021 in both urban and rural areas of Fasa, a city in the southeast of Fars province in Iran.

The intervention group received training through WhatsApp Messenger, while the control group did not receive any educational content during this period. The educational content was designed based on the Encyclopedia of Self-care Guide in Minor Illnesses [23] and was presented in the form of text, images, and videos via WhatsApp Messenger to the intervention group every two days for two weeks.

The reference book provides knowledge for self-care in minor illnesses and aims to reduce unnecessary visits to health systems, including primary health care centers, medical clinics, and hospitals. The book was translated and adapted by the Training and Health Promotion Unit of the Ministry of Health in Iran [24]. It consists of eight chapters, covering topics related to mothers and newborns, mothers, children, adolescents, adults, all ages, psychological and emotional well-being, and general information.

Initially, before the intervention phase, the purpose of the study was explained to both groups of participants. With their informed consent, a pre-test was conducted using a questionnaire developed by the researcher to assess the knowledge of health ambassadors before the study.

Three weeks after the training course ended, a follow-up test was conducted to evaluate the impact of WhatsApp Messenger on the knowledge of health ambassadors regarding home care for minor illnesses. In order to uphold ethical standards, after both groups completed the follow-up test, all educational materials were provided to the control group through WhatsApp Messenger.

Study instrument, its validity and reliability

To assess the knowledge of health ambassadors a questionnaire was developed for this study consisting of two parts (supplementary file 1). The first part includes demographic information. The second part contains 57 questions aimed at evaluating the health ambassadors' knowledge of minor illnesses. Some of the questions in this questionnaire were adapted from Shakbia et al. [11].

The final score for the questionnaire is determined by adding up the total score, which ranges from 0 to 57. The questionnaire was distributed to 16 individuals with expertise in health education and tool development to assess its content and face validity, at the pilot study phase. Comments and suggestions from a meeting with the research team were discussed, and necessary revisions to questions were made.

For the qualitative assessment of content validity, two components, the Content Validity Ratio (CVR) and the Content Validity Index (CVI), were utilized.

Experts assigned each question a score of "essential," "useful but not essential," or "not essential" based on its importance, relevance, and clarity to calculate the CVR. These responses were converted to quantitative values using a specific formula and assessed against a CVR decision table.

Additionally, experts evaluated the simplicity, relevance, and clarity of questions separately to calculate the CVI. The Content Validity Index was determined by dividing the total positive responses by the number of specialists. The study questionnaire was ultimately validated based on the CVR, CVI, and importance with cutoffs set at 0.49, 0.7, and 1.5 for the questions.

At the pilot study phase, to evaluate the reliability of the questionnaire, 15 health ambassadors were randomly selected to complete the questionnaire twice, with a two-week interval in between. The correlation between the scores before and after the two weeks was calculated using the correlation coefficient. A correlation coefficient of 0.7 to 1 was considered acceptable, indicating the reliability of the questionnaire.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using IBM SPSS version 22 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). The data were described using indices such as mean and standard deviation for quantitative variables, and frequency and percentage for qualitative variables. The normality of the data distribution was confirmed using the Smirnov-Kolmogorov test. The homogeneity of the two groups was checked using Chi-square for categorical variables such as gender and an independent t-test for numerical variables such as age.

The mean knowledge scores were compared between the intervention and control groups using the paired t-test before and analysis of covariance after the intervention. In the second analysis, the pre-intervention score was selected as a covariate variable. The comparison of the pre- and post-intervention scores in each group was done using the paired t-test. Multivariate regression was used to eliminate the confounding effect of background variables. This analysis was conducted based on the difference of the knowledge score before and after the intervention. The significance level for the results was considered to be less than 0.05.

Discussions

In this research, the effect of training health ambassadors about minor illnesses through WhatsApp Messenger was investigated. The results showed that training via WhatsApp Messenger increased the average knowledge score of health ambassadors in all training areas in the intervention group.

The results showed that in the training group, the total knowledge score of health ambassadors about minor illnesses was lower than the control group before the training. However, after the training, the total knowledge score of the training group (p < 0.001) was higher than the score of the control group (p = 0.431). Furthermore, the difference in the total knowledge score regarding minor illnesses before and after the training program was found to be greater in the intervention group compared to the control group (p < 0.001).

After controlling for demographic factors such as marital status, employment, education level, prior knowledge, and gender, it was revealed that the intervention was significantly influential in changing the knowledge score and that there was a significant difference between the intervention and control groups (p < 0.001). These results show that providing training through WhatsApp Messenger has significantly improved the knowledge of health ambassadors regarding minor illnesses.

Hosseini et al. conducted a study that reported providing training and counseling through social networks to enhance self-care knowledge among patients undergoing hemodialysis played a critical role in reducing complications and healthcare costs for these patients [25]. Al-Omar et al.'s research demonstrated that training via WhatsApp Messenger yielded promising results, regardless of the patient's health literacy level or the number of participants [26]. The study by Hemmatipour et al. indicated that training through WhatsApp could enhance the knowledge and performance of nursing students [18]. These findings highlight the potential of mobile messaging apps in promoting healthcare literacy and improving performance among both patients and students.

In the study by Pereira et al., the use of WhatsApp groups as a means of controlling diseases was examined. The results showed that this method is an appropriate option for healthcare purposes and creates a space for the exchange of experiences [27]. The study by Alanzi et al. showed that WhatsApp Messenger has been effective in improving patients' self-efficacy and awareness [28].

The study by Dyavarishetty et al. showed that WhatsApp Messenger is an effective learning tool [29]. The researchers found that this form of education helps enhance knowledge among individuals. These findings emphasize the advantages and strengths of using WhatsApp Messenger as an educational platform and demonstrate the potential of this type of training as a complementary approach to traditional methods.

The results of Dhar et al.'s study demonstrated that integrating social media platforms into clinical practice can improve patient engagement and satisfaction, as well as their ability to understand and manage their diseases [30]. Gon et al. showed that WhatsApp's convenience, accessibility, and availability, which allows for learning at any time and place, make it a valuable educational tool [31]. Therefore, electronic learning through WhatsApp Messenger can be utilized as an effective and innovative learning method across various medical disciplines.

Zareipour et al. showed that engaging members of the community as health ambassadors can enhance community involvement and improve the quality of well-being and health of individuals. Investing in the education and empowerment of health ambassadors can also encourage self-care and improve the overall health of individuals [16]. Studies by Jahani Eftekhari et al. and Jafarpour et al. indicated that utilizing electronic methods and messaging platforms can effectively educate and empower health advocates, enhancing their healthcare knowledge and self-monitoring abilities [32, 33].

The studies by Sveen [34] et al., Høifødt et al. [35], Carnie et al. [36], and Kuo et al. [37] also support the effectiveness of education through messaging apps. Additionally the study conducted by Ramezani and colleagues [38] showed that educational interventions on health topics had a positive impact on improving knowledge and preventive behaviors in the community.

Based on the results of this research, messaging apps have certain characteristics like being easy and free to access, widely accessible, useability in any place and time, and ability to transfer various educational contents. Then, it is evident that messaging apps have the potential to be a key platform for educating and empowering health advocates regarding minor illnesses. With the availability and ease of use of social media networks, education planners can leverage these platforms as effective tools for educating and empowering various communities.

Due to the large volume of material in the minor illnesses textbook and the potential for errors in responding to questionnaire questions by health ambassadors, we chose important topics from each chapter and limited the number of questions to reduce cognitive load. Additionally, there was the possibility that some of the educational material sent through WhatsApp was not reviewed by the health ambassadors. However, a clear weakness of this study was that it only assessed the knowledge of the health ambassadors without evaluating their attitudes and behavior of them.

One advantage of this study was that we presented the material in a concise and engaging format, increasing the likelihood that health ambassadors would read it. In future studies, it would be interesting to compare the impact of education through WhatsApp Messaging with other training methods on the level of knowledge of health ambassadors regarding various illnesses. We also recommend assessing the impact of long-term, continuous education through WhatsApp on the improvement of knowledge and behavior of health ambassadors.

In general, utilizing WhatsApp for home care education can enhance health services and education for patients and caregivers. However, there are challenges that need to be addressed such as the quality and content of education, as well as access to electronic devices and internet networks. Therefore, it is important to provide appropriate platforms for managing health information to ensure the secure and effective use of technology in the healthcare sector.

Conclusion

The results of this research indicated that training through WhatsApp Messaging had a positive effect on the knowledge of health ambassadors regarding home care for minor illnesses. Considering its inherent flexibility, low time commitment, and cost-effectiveness, this type of education is an effective method for teaching health-related topics.

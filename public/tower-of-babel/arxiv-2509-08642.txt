RIS-ASSISTED NEAR-FIELD ISAC FOR MULTI-TARGET INDICATION IN NLOS SCENARIOS
Hang Ruan, Homa Nikbakht, Ruizhi Zhang, Honglei Chen, Yonina C. Eldar

Abstract
Enabling multi-target sensing in near-field integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) systems is a key challenge, particularly when line-of-sight paths are blocked. This paper proposes a beamforming framework that leverages a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) to achieve multi-target indication. Our contribution is the extension of classic beampattern gain and inter-target cross-correlation metrics to the near-field, leveraging both angle and distance information to discriminate between multiple users and targets. We formulate a problem to maximize the worst-case sensing performance by jointly designing the beamforming at the base station and the phase shifts at the RIS, while guaranteeing communication rates. The non-convex problem is solved via an efficient alternating optimization (AO) algorithm that utilizes semidefinite relaxation (SDR). Simulations demonstrate that our RIS-assisted framework enables high-resolution sensing of co-angle targets in blocked scenarios.

Introduction

Future 6G networks require high-accuracy sensing alongside high-speed communication, for which
integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)
is a pivotal technology
[
1
]
. By sharing spectrum and hardware, ISAC improves spectral efficiency, reduces costs, and creates synergy between the two functions
[
1
]
.

The trend towards large antenna arrays and high frequency pushes
ISAC
into the near-field regime, where the spherical wavefront provides a distance dimension
[
2
,
3
]
. This critically enables the discrimination of co-angle targets, which is impossible in the far-field
[
2
,
3
]
. Concurrently,
reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs)
enhance
ISAC
systems by creating virtual links to overcome signal blockages
[
4
]
. While this has spurred research into
RIS
-assisted near-field
ISAC

[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, these studies focus on single-target scenarios and cannot sense multiple targets simultaneously.

Recognizing this limitation, a growing body of research has started to address the more challenging multi-user, multi-target
ISAC
problem with
RIS
assistance. These works have explored various performance metrics to manage the intricate trade-offs, such as the sensing
signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR)

[
10
]
, beam pattern gain
[
11
]
,
mutual information (MI)

[
12
]
, and detection probability
[
13
,
14
]
.
However, these works are fundamentally limited by their far-field assumptions, as their angle-only sensing metrics are insufficient for near-field scenarios where resolving targets in the same direction requires exploiting the distance dimension.

To effectively harness near-field propagation for multi-target sensing, our prior work in
[
15
]
extended the robust metrics of transmit beampattern gain and inter-target cross-correlation to the near-field. This approach is offers two distinct advantages. First, unlike metrics such as sensing
SINR
,
MI
, or detection probability, our approach is agnostic to the targets’ often unknown
radar cross-sections (RCSs)
. Second, the explicit suppression of inter-target cross-correlation is critical for accurate multi-target indication; without it, metrics based on
SINR
, beampattern gain only or detection probability are insufficient for resolving multiple targets with classical
multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO)
techniques
[
16
,
17
]
.

However, the framework in
[
15
]
was limited to
line-of-sight (LoS)
-only scenarios. This paper bridges that critical gap by introducing a
RIS
to overcome
non-line-of-sight (NLoS)
blockages in a multi-user, multi-target near-field
ISAC
system. Our proposed framework leverages the near-field’s spherical wavefront to resolve targets in both angle and distance, even when obstructed.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
1) We formulate a joint transmission beamforming and
RIS
response optimization problem for
RIS
-assisted near-field
ISAC
that accommodates multiple
NLoS
users and targets.
2) To enable sensing of multiple targets, we first extend classical far-field beam-pattern and cross-correlation metrics to the near-field, enabling the discrimination of objects at identical directions but different distances.
3) We develop an efficient
alternating optimization (AO)
algorithm to solve the complex non-convex problem. The sub-optimizations of both transmission beamforming and
RIS
response are solved via the
semidefinite relaxation (SDR)
technique.
4) We demonstrate through numerical simulations that our proposed RIS-assisted design overcomes blockages to achieve significant performance gains, enabling high-resolution sensing and communication in scenarios.

System and Signal Model

2.1
System Model

We consider a near-field mmWave
ISAC
system (Fig.
1
) comprising a
base station (BS)
, an
RIS
,
K
single-antenna users in the set
\mathcal{K}
, and
L
point-targets in the set
\mathcal{L}
. The
BS
is equipped with an
N_{t}
-element transmit array and an
N_{r}
-element receive array, with antenna positions
\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t}),\bm{p}_{r}(n_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and corresponding normal vectors
\bm{w}_{t},\bm{w}_{r}
defining the main radiation directions. The
RIS
contains
N_{s}
passive elements, with the
n_{s}
-th element’s position and normal vector being
\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s})
and
\bm{w}_{s}
, respectively. The position of any user or target
a\in\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{L}
is
\bm{p}_{a}
. To model potential blockages, an indicator
\alpha_{a}\in\{0,1\}
describes the
LoS
status (
\alpha_{a}=1
for an available path,
0
otherwise). All entities operate within the
BS
’s near-field region, bounded by the Rayleigh distance
R_{\text{RL}}=2D^{2}/\lambda
, where
D
is the
BS
antenna aperture and
\lambda
is the signal wavelength.

2.2
Transmitted ISAC Signal

Under a fully-digital beamforming architecture
[
18
]
, the signal transmitted from the
BS
at time
t
(
t=1,\dots,T
, where
T
is the block length) is given by
\bm{x}[t]=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\bm{f}_{k}c_{k}[t]+\bm{s}[t]
,
where
\bm{f}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}}
is the communication beamforming vector for user
k
, and
c_{k}[t]
is the corresponding data symbol, satisfying
\mathbb{E}\left\{c_{k}[t]c_{l}^{*}[t]\right\}=\delta[k-l]
. The term
\bm{s}[t]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}}
represents the dedicated sensing signal with covariance
\mathbf{R}_{s}=\mathbb{E}\left\{\bm{s}[t]\bm{s}^{H}[t]\right\}
. The total transmit covariance is:

\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbb{E}\left\{\bm{x}[t]\bm{x}^{H}[t]\right\}=\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\bm{f}_{k}\bm{f}_{k}^{H}+\mathbf{R}_{s}.

(1)

2.3
Communication Model

For user
k\in\mathcal{K}
, the overall downlink channel
\bm{h}_{t,k}(\mathbf{\Phi})\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}}
is a sum of the
LoS
path (if it exists) and the
BS
-
RIS
-user path:

\bm{h}_{t,k}(\mathbf{\Phi})={\alpha_{k}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,k}}+{\left(\bm{h}_{s,k}^{T}\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{G}_{t}\right)^{T}}.

(2)

Following the near-field channel model
[
19
]
, the components of the
LoS
channel,
\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}}
, are defined as:

\left[\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,k}\right]_{n_{t}}=\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{k}-\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t}),\bm{w}_{t})}\beta(\bm{p}_{k}-\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t})),

(3)

where
F(\bm{p}_{k}-\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t}),\bm{w}_{t})
is the radiation pattern, given by:

F(\bm{p},\!\bm{w})\!=\!\begin{cases}2(b\!+\!1)\cos^{b}(\psi(\bm{p},\!\bm{w})),&\psi(\bm{p},\!\bm{w})\!\in\![0,\pi/2],\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\psi(\bm{p},\bm{w})
is the angle between the direction vector
\bm{p}
and the array’s normal vector
\bm{w}
. The parameter
b
determines the Boresight gain, which is 2 for the dipole. The term
\beta(\bm{p}_{k}-\bm{p}_{t}(i,l))
denotes the attenuation and phase change caused by the path, with
\beta(\bm{p})
being

\beta(\bm{p})={\lambda}/\left({4\pi\left\lVert\bm{p}\right\rVert}\right)\cdot e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left\lVert\bm{p}\right\rVert}.

(4)

The
BS
-
RIS
-user path consists of three components: the BS-RIS channel
\mathbf{G}_{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times N_{t}}
, the RIS reflection matrix
\mathbf{\Phi}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times N_{s}}
, and the RIS-user channel
\bm{h}_{s,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}}
. Their elements are:

\displaystyle\begin{split}[\mathbf{G}_{t}]_{n_{s},n_{t}}\!=&\beta(\bm{p}_{s}\!(n_{s})\!-\!\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t}))\!\sqrt{\!F\!(\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s})\!-\!\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t}),\!\bm{w}_{t})}\\
&\times\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t})\!-\!\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s}),\bm{w}_{s})},\end{split}

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}=

\displaystyle\mbox{diag}(e^{j\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{j\theta_{N_{s}}}),

(6)

\displaystyle\left[\bm{h}_{s,k}\right]_{n_{s}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{k}-\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s}),\bm{w}_{s})}\beta(\bm{p}_{k}-\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s})),

(7)

where
\theta_{n_{s}}(n_{s}=1,\dots,N_{s})
is the response of the
n_{s}
-th
RIS
element. The received signal at user
k
is

y_{k}[t]=\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\bm{x}[t]+n_{k}[t],

(8)

where
n_{k}[t]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{k}^{2})
is the additive noise. Then, the
SINR
is:

\eta_{k}=\frac{\left\lvert\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\bm{f}_{k}\right\rvert^{2}}{\sum_{j\neq k}\left\lvert\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\bm{f}_{j}\right\rvert^{2}+\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\mathbf{R}_{s}\bm{h}_{t,k}^{*}(\mathbf{\Phi})+\sigma_{k}^{2}}.

(9)

The achievable communication rate for user
k
is used as the performance metric, given by
[
19
]
:

R_{k}=\log_{2}(1+\eta_{k}).

(10)

2.4
Sensing Model

The sensing channel constitutes a round-trip path, consisting of the forward path from the BS to a target and the backward path from the target to the BS. Similarly to (
2
), the forward and backward channels, denoted as
\bm{h}_{t,l}(\mathbf{\Phi})\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}}
and
\bm{h}_{l,r}(\mathbf{\Phi})\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}}
, are composed of the direct path (if it exists) and the
RIS
-assisted path:

\bm{h}_{t,l}(\mathbf{\Phi})\!=\!\alpha_{l}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,l}+\left(\bm{h}_{s,l}^{T}\mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{G}_{t}\right)^{T}\!\!,\bm{h}_{l,r}(\mathbf{\Phi})\!=\!\alpha_{l}\bar{\bm{h}}_{l,r}+\mathbf{G}_{r}\mathbf{\Phi}\bm{h}_{s,l}.

The components of the forward and backward paths are the direct BS-to-target
LoS
channel
\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}}
, the RIS-target channel
\bm{h}_{s,l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}}
, the BS-RIS channel gain
\mathbf{G}_{t}
given in (
5
), the target-to-BS
LoS
channel
\bar{\bm{h}}_{l,r}\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}}
and the RIS-BS-receive channel gain
\mathbf{G}_{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}\times N_{s}}
. The elements of these vectors or matrices are as follows:

\displaystyle\left[\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,l}\right]_{n_{t}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{l}-\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t}),\bm{w}_{t})}\beta(\bm{p}_{l}-\bm{p}_{t}(n_{t})),

\displaystyle\left[\bm{h}_{s,l}\right]_{n_{s}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{l}-\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s}),\bm{w}_{s})}\beta(\bm{p}_{l}-\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s})),

\displaystyle[\bar{\bm{h}}_{l,r}]_{n_{r}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{r}(n_{r})-\bm{p}_{l},\bm{w}_{r})}\beta(\bm{p}_{r}(n_{r})-\bm{p}_{l}),

\displaystyle[\mathbf{G}_{r}]_{n_{r},n_{s}}

\displaystyle=\beta(\bm{p}_{r}(n_{r})\!-\!\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s}))\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{r}(n_{r})\!-\!\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s}),\!\bm{w}_{s})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sqrt{F(\bm{p}_{s}(n_{s})\!-\!\bm{p}_{r}(n_{r}),\!\bm{w}_{r})}.

The complete round-trip sensing channel matrix for target
l
is

\mathbf{H}_{l}(\mathbf{\Phi})=\gamma_{l}\bm{h}_{l,r}(\mathbf{\Phi})\bm{h}_{t,l}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi}),

(11)

where
\gamma_{l}
is the target’s
RCS
. The BS’s total received echo signal is

\bm{y}_{r}[t]=\sum\nolimits_{l\in\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{H}_{l}(\mathbf{\Phi})\bm{x}[t]+\bm{n}_{r}[t],

(12)

where
\bm{n}_{r}[t]\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{r}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N_{r}})
is the sensing receiver’s noise. For multi-target MIMO sensing, the transmit covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}
must be shaped to achieve two complementary objectives
[
20
,
17
,
16
]
:
1)
Beam-pattern gain
: maximize the power delivered to each target
l
, quantified by

\bm{h}_{t,l}^{T}\,\mathbf{R}_{x}\,\bm{h}_{t,l}^{*};

2)
Cross-correlation suppression
: minimize mutual interference between any two targets
l\neq l^{\prime}
, quantified by
\bigl|\bm{h}_{t,l}^{T}\,\mathbf{R}_{x}\,\bm{h}_{t,l^{\prime}}^{*}\bigr|
.

Our key innovation lies in extending these classic sensing metrics to the near-field. While far-field designs optimize beampattern and cross-correlation over angles only
[
16
,
20
,
17
]
, our near-field formulation enforces these criteria over a joint angle-and-range space. This enables resolving co-directional targets. Furthermore, unlike other common metrics such as the
SINR
or
MI

[
3
,
21
]
, this approach is agnostic to the targets’ often unknown
RCSs
, making it more robust for practical deployments. More critically, optimizing for SINR or beampattern gain without suppressing cross-correlation leads to highly correlated target echoes, making classical indication techniques like Capon fail to resolve multiple targets
[
16
,
17
]
.

3
Joint Beamforming and RIS Design

3.1
Optimization formulation

We aim to jointly design the BS transmit beamformers
\{\bm{f}_{k}\}
, the sensing covariance
\mathbf{R}_{s}
, and the RIS phase shifts
\mathbf{\Phi}
to optimize the performance of
ISAC
system, which is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\bm{f}_{k}\},\mathbf{R}_{s}\succeq 0,\\
\mathbf{R}_{x},\mathbf{\Phi},\mu,\{\theta_{n_{s}}\}\end{subarray}}\quad

\displaystyle\mu

(13a)

s.t.

\displaystyle w_{l}\left(\bm{h}_{t,l}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\mathbf{R}_{x}\bm{h}_{t,l}^{*}(\mathbf{\Phi})\right)\geq\mu,\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

(13b)

\displaystyle w_{l,l^{\prime}}\!\left\lvert\bm{h}_{t,l}^{T}(\!\mathbf{\Phi}\!)\mathbf{R}_{x}\bm{h}_{t,l^{\prime}}^{*}(\!\mathbf{\Phi}\!)\right\rvert\!\leq\!\varepsilon\mu,\forall l\!\neq\!l^{\prime},

(13c)

\displaystyle R_{k}(\{\bm{f}_{k}\},\mathbf{R}_{s},\mathbf{\Phi})\geq R_{\min,k},\forall k\in\mathcal{K},

(13d)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{R}_{x})\leq P_{\max},

(13e)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{x}=\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\bm{f}_{k}\bm{f}_{k}^{H}+\mathbf{R}_{s},

(13f)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}=\text{diag}(e^{j\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{j\theta_{N_{s}}}).

(13g)

The optimization in (
13
) maximizes an auxiliary variable
\mu
, which represents the worst-case sensing beampattern gain weighted by
w_{l}
across all targets (objective (
13a
)), subject to a series of constraints. Specifically, each target’s weighted beampattern gain must be at least
\mu
(
13b
); the magnitude of the cross-correlation between any two targets must not exceed a fraction
\varepsilon
of
\mu
to suppress inter-target interference (
13c
); each user’s communication rate must meet a minimum requirement
R_{\min,k}
(
13d
); the total transmit power is limited by
P_{\max}
(
13e
); the equations of the overall transmit covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}
(
13f
) and
RIS
phase shifts (
13g
) are repetitions of (
1
) and (
6
).

The proposed framework in (
13
) is uniquely tailored for near-field multi-target ISAC, and its novelty is twofold. First, the explicit inclusion of the cross-correlation suppression constraint (
13c
) is critical. It ensures that the waveforms reflected from different targets are nearly orthogonal, which is critical for multi-target indication
[
16
]
. Second, the power of this framework is unlocked by using near-field channels. Because the near-field channel vectors
\bm{h}_{t,l}(\mathbf{\Phi})
capture range-dependent information, allowing the beampattern and cross-correlation constraints to be enforced over a joint angle-and-range space, which is the core mechanism that enables our design to resolve co-directional users and targets.

This problem is non-convex and challenging to solve due to the coupling between the optimization variables
\mathbf{R}_{x}
and
\mathbf{\Phi}
, the unit-modulus constraint on the RIS phases (
13g
), and the SINR constraints (
13d
) with respect to the beamforming vectors
\{\bm{f}_{k}\}
. To tackle this, we adopt an
AO
framework. We decouple the problem by first optimizing the BS transmit covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}
while keeping the RIS phase shifts
\mathbf{\Phi}
fixed. Subsequently, we optimize
\mathbf{\Phi}
using the updated
\mathbf{R}_{x}
. This two-step process is repeated until convergence.

3.2
Fix
\mathbf{\Phi}
, Optimize BS Beamforming

When
\mathbf{\Phi}
is fixed, the channels
\bm{h}_{t,a}(\mathbf{\Phi})
become constant vectors. The problem (
13
) reduces to an optimization over the BS beamformers
\{\bm{f}_{k}\}
and the sensing covariance
\mathbf{R}_{s}
. This subproblem is still non-convex due to the communication rate constraint (
13d
) and the quadratic covariance definition (
13f
). This is tackled using
SDR
.

First, using the Shannon capacity formula (
10
) and the definition of
SINR
(
9
), (
13d
) is equivalent to
[
2
]
:

\xi_{k}\left\lvert\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\bm{f}_{k}\right\rvert^{2}\geq\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\mathbf{R}_{x}\bm{h}_{t,k}^{*}(\mathbf{\Phi})+\sigma_{k}^{2},

(14)

where
\xi_{k}={2^{\,R_{\min,k}}}/({2^{R_{\min,k}}-1})
. We then introduce variables
\mathbf{F}_{k}=\bm{f}_{k}\bm{f}_{k}^{H}
, and relax the rank-one constraints
\text{rank}(\mathbf{F}_{k})=1
. The subproblem becomes the following
semidefinite program (SDP)
:

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\mathbf{F}_{k}\succeq 0\},\mathbf{R}_{s}\succeq 0,\mu\end{subarray}}\quad

\displaystyle\mu

(15a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\text{Constraints }\eqref{eqn:opt_const_sensing_bp_ris},\eqref{eqn:opt_const_cross_ris},\eqref{eqn:opt_const_bs_power_ris}

(15b)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left(\mathbf{H}_{k}(\mathbf{\Phi})(\xi_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}-\mathbf{R}_{x})\right)\geq\sigma_{k}^{2},\forall k

(15c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{x}=\sum\nolimits_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{F}_{k}+\mathbf{R}_{s},

(15d)

where
\mathbf{H}_{k}(\mathbf{\Phi})=\bm{h}_{t,k}^{*}(\mathbf{\Phi})\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})
. This
SDP
can be solved efficiently using standard convex solvers. From the optimal solution
\{\mathbf{F}_{k}^{*}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
, we can construct an optimal beaming vector
\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}
without loss of optimality as follows
[
20
]
:

\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}=\left(\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\mathbf{F}_{k}^{*}\bm{h}_{t,k}^{*}(\mathbf{\Phi})\right)^{-1/2}\mathbf{F}_{k}^{*}\bm{h}_{t,k}^{*}(\mathbf{\Phi}).

(16)

The optimality of this construction is justified below.

Proof.

Let the solution to the
SDP
in (
15
) be
\{\mathbf{F}_{k}^{*}\}
and
\mathbf{R}_{s}^{*}
. We construct the beamformers
\{\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}\}
using (
16
) and define a new sensing covariance matrix
\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{s}=\mathbf{R}_{s}^{*}+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{F}_{k}^{*}-\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H})
. According to
[
20
, Theorem 1]
, the constructed vectors satisfy
\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H}\preceq\mathbf{F}_{k}^{*}
, which ensures that
\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{s}\succeq\mathbf{0}
. Since the total transmit covariance matrix remains unchanged, the sensing constraints (
13b
), the cross-correlation constraints (
13c
), and the power constraint (
13e
) are still satisfied. Furthermore, the construction in (
16
) guarantees that
\left\lvert\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}\right\rvert^{2}=\bm{h}_{t,k}^{T}(\mathbf{\Phi})\mathbf{F}_{k}^{*}\bm{h}_{t,k}^{*}(\mathbf{\Phi})

[
20
, Theorem 1]
. This ensures that the left-hand side of the SINR constraint in (
15
) remains unchanged, meaning the communication rate constraint (
13d
) also holds. Therefore, the constructed rank-one solution
\{\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}\}
and
\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{s}
is feasible and achieves the same optimal objective value
\mu
.

∎

3.3
Fix BS Beamforming, Optimize
\mathbf{\Phi}

When the BS transmit covariance
\mathbf{R}_{x}
is fixed, the original problem (
13
) reduces to a subproblem over the RIS phase shifts
\mathbf{\Phi}
and the objective value
\mu
.
This subproblem is non-convex due to the unit-modulus constraint (
13g
), which will be solved with
SDR
. First, let us define a vector
\bm{\phi}=[e^{j\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{j\theta_{N_{s}}}]^{T}
containing the RIS phase shifts, such that
\mathbf{\Phi}=\mbox{diag}(\bm{\phi})
. The channel from the BS to any user or target
a\in\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{L}
can be expressed as an affine function of
\bm{\phi}
:

\bm{h}_{t,a}(\bm{\phi})=\alpha_{a}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,a}+\mathbf{A}_{a}\bm{\phi},

(17)

where
\mathbf{A}_{a}=\mathbf{G}_{t}^{T}\mbox{diag}(\bm{h}_{s,a})
.
Then, we define an augmented vector
\bar{\bm{\phi}}=[\bm{\phi}^{T},1]^{T}
and a new matrix variable
\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}=\bar{\bm{\phi}}\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N_{s}+1)\times(N_{s}+1)}
.
Thus, any quadratic form of
\bm{\phi}
becomes a linear function of
\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}
. The beampattern and cross-correlation terms can be expressed as
\text{Tr}(\mathbf{M}_{l,l^{\prime}}\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}})
, with
\mathbf{M}_{l,l^{\prime}}
defined as:

\mathbf{M}_{l,l^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{l}^{H}\mathbf{R}_{x}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{l^{\prime}}&\mathbf{A}_{l}^{H}\mathbf{R}_{x}^{T}(\alpha_{l^{\prime}}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,l^{\prime}})\\
(\alpha_{l}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,l})^{T}\mathbf{R}_{x}\mathbf{A}_{l^{\prime}}^{*}&(\alpha_{l}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,l})^{T}\mathbf{R}_{x}(\alpha_{l^{\prime}}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,l^{\prime}})^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

Similarly, the SINR constraint for user
k
can be rewritten as the linear inequality
\text{Tr}(\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{SINR}}\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}})\geq\Gamma_{k}\sigma_{k}^{2}
, where
\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{SINR}}
is constructed by first defining
\mathbf{S}_{k}=\bm{f}_{k}\bm{f}_{k}^{H}-\Gamma_{k}(\sum_{j\neq k}\bm{f}_{j}\bm{f}_{j}^{H}+\mathbf{R}_{s})
, and then:

\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{SINR}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{k}&\mathbf{A}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{T}(\alpha_{k}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,k})\\
(\alpha_{k}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,k})^{T}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{*}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{*}&(\alpha_{k}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,k})^{T}\mathbf{S}_{k}^{*}(\alpha_{k}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t,k})^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

After we drop the rank-one constraint on
\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}
, and relax the unit-modulus constraint
|\phi_{n_{s}}|^{2}=1
into
[\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}]_{n_{s},n_{s}}=1
for all
n_{s}=1,\dots,N_{s}+1
,
the relaxed subproblem for optimizing the RIS phase shifts becomes the following convex
SDP
:

\displaystyle\max_{\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}\succeq 0,\mu}\quad

\displaystyle\mu

(18a)

s.t.

\displaystyle w_{l}\text{Tr}(\mathbf{M}_{l,l}\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}})\geq\mu,\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

(18b)

\displaystyle w_{l,l^{\prime}}\left\lvert\text{Tr}(\mathbf{M}_{l,l^{\prime}}\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}})\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon\mu,\quad\forall l\neq l^{\prime},

(18c)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{SINR}}\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}})\geq\Gamma_{k}\sigma_{k}^{2},\quad\forall k\in\mathcal{K},

(18d)

\displaystyle[\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}]_{n_{s},n_{s}}=1,\quad n_{s}=1,\dots,N_{s}+1.

(18e)

This
SDP
can be solved efficiently using convex optimization solvers. If the resulting optimal matrix
\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}^{*}
is not rank-one, a rank-one solution for
\bm{\phi}
is recovered using a deterministic method. First, we select the dominant eigenvector of
\bar{\mathbf{\Psi}}^{*}
, denoted as
\bar{\bm{v}}
, and normalize it by its last element to obtain a candidate vector, i.e.,
\bar{\bm{\phi}}_{\text{cand}}={\bar{\bm{v}}}/({[\bar{\bm{v}}]_{N_{s}+1}})
. Then, the final RIS phase-shift vector
\bm{\phi}
is formed by taking the first
N_{s}
elements of
\bar{\bm{\phi}}_{\text{cand}}
and ensuring they have unit modulus, i.e.,
\phi_{n_{s}}={[\bar{\bm{\phi}}_{\text{cand}}]_{n_{s}}}/{\left\lvert[\bar{\bm{\phi}}_{\text{cand}}]_{n_{s}}\right\rvert},\text{for }n_{s}=1,\dots,N_{s}.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we addressed the critical challenge of
LoS
blockage in near-field multi-target
ISAC
systems. We formulated a joint transmission beamforming and RIS phase shifts optimization problem to maximize sensing performance while guaranteeing communication quality and suppressing interference. An efficient
AO
algorithm was developed to solve this complex non-convex problem. Simulation results confirm that our RIS-assisted approach can successfully bypass obstacles, providing robust and high-performance communication and sensing functionalities in challenging
NLoS
environments.

Social media and children: A double-edged sword
Authors: Nasibeh Zerangian, Hadi Tehrani, Farzan Madadizadeh, Mahdi Gholian-Aval
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Social media refers to digital platforms, websites, and apps that enable people to create, share, and interact with content. This category includes popular platforms such as Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, gaming sites, and other interactive websites.[1]

Technology and social media offer several benefits for the growth and development of children. First, they strengthen communication and teamwork skills by creating a secure environment for interaction with friends, family, and the broader community, allowing for self-expression, and social interaction.[2] Second, these tools promote critical thinking and problem-solving as children navigate diverse digital environments, enabling them to make informed decisions and develop creative solutions. Additionally, technology enhances creativity through activities such as video creation, photography, website design, and app development, thereby improving children’s innovative thinking skills. Digital platforms also promote digital literacy, teaching the responsible, safe, and effective use of technology.[3] Exposure to various cultures through social media makes children more culturally sensitive and aware of the world.[2] Social media is a very good educational tool, offering access to vast information and collaborative learning.[3] It enhances social engagement and emotional health by allowing kids to share experiences, obtain social support, and form friendships, thereby minimizing the perception of loneliness in vulnerable or shy children.[4] Furthermore, the sites facilitate civic engagement and community involvement, promoting initial interest in social life.[5] By interacting in digital environments, children develop crucial tech skills that prepare them for a future shaped by technology.[6] Social media also enables them to produce and distribute creative content, thereby facilitating self-expression and a confidence boost.[34] Access to peer and expert support is available, especially during challenging times.[5] Overall, when used appropriately, digital tools and social platforms can enhance children’s cognitive, emotional, and social development by encouraging creativity, inclusiveness, and a love for lifelong learning.[345]

Despite its advantages, social media use among children raises several serious concerns. One of the most pressing issues is the widespread occurrence of cyberbullying and online harassment, which are linked to increased rates of anxiety, depression, self-harm, and even suicidal behavior.[46789] Children might also be subjected to content that is not suitable for their age, such as violence, sexual content, and extremist ideas, which could adversely affect their emotional and psychological status.[489] Excessive social media use has been associated with a variety of health problems, such as sleep disturbance, physical inactivity, obesity, sex problems, visual impairment, headaches, neck and back pain, tooth decay, internet addiction, and reduced face-to-face social contact.[28] This excessive use can lead to poor academic performance, a shorter attention span, and negative effects on cognitive development.[210] There also exists the risk of invasion of privacy and identity theft, especially with children’s revelation of personal information online, typically without appreciating the consequences.[9] Children may become vulnerable to online predators or be influenced by persuasive ads and social pressure, which can contribute to materialistic attitudes and consumer-driven behaviors.[6] Additionally, Problematic use of social media (PSMU) among children is characterized by addictive behaviors, diminished self-regulation, and repetitive, uncontrollable usage, frequently influenced by anxiety over missing out (FOMO) and a persistent desire for online recognition.[69] Such virtual dependency may reshape children’s view of reality and relationships, eroding family relationships and authentic social bonding.[4] Social media can also cause body dissatisfaction, low self-esteem, and the development of eating disorders, particularly in adolescents who are exposed to unrealistic beauty standards.[2] Behavioral changes such as irritability and poor time management are commonly reported.[8] Overall, while social media holds potential, its misuse or overuse has the potential to add to a multitude of psychosocial, cognitive, and physical health detriments in children, and parental monitoring, digital literacy education, and protection mechanisms in general become extremely pertinent.[5] A summary of the identified advantages and disadvantages of social media use among children is presented in Table 1.

Social media advantages and disadvantages for children

In conclusion, while social media offers numerous benefits for children, it is essential to approach its use with caution and a mindful awareness of the potential risks involved. By promoting responsible online behavior and educating children about digital safety, we can help ensure that social media remains a positive and empowering tool for young users.

Abstract
We apply the direct method of the calculus of variations to prove existence of periodic solutions for differential inclusion systems involving an anisotropic ϕ \phi -Laplacian operator.



keywords:
differential, inclusions, periodic, variational, methods.

1
Introduction

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
be a convex and differentiable function, and let
T>0
. We consider a function
F:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is locally Lipschitz in
x
for almost every
t\in[0,T]
. The Clarke generalized gradient of
F
with respect to
x
is denoted by
\partial F
(see
[
9
,
10
]
).

The aim of this paper is to establish the existence of solutions to the following differential inclusion problem with periodic boundary conditions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d}{dt}\nabla\phi(u^{\prime}(t))\in\partial F(t,u(t))\quad\hbox{a.e.}\ t\in(0,T)\\
u(0)-u(T)=u^{\prime}(0)-u^{\prime}(T)=0,\end{array}\right.

(
P
)

We obtain solutions to problem (

P

) using the direct method in the calculus of variations. To this end, we introduce the following functional, also referred as
action integral
:

\mathcal{I}(u):=\int_{0}^{T}\phi(\dot{u}(t))dt+\int_{0}^{T}F(t,u(t))dt.

(I)

The objective is to show that, under suitable assumptions, the functional
\mathcal{I}
attains a minimum in the Sobolev–Orlicz vector space
W^{1,\phi}_{T}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n}):=W^{1,\phi}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\{u\mid u(0)=u(T)\}
, and that the minimizers of
\mathcal{I}
are solutions to problem (

P

).

The case of problem (

P

) with a Fréchet differentiable function
F
and equations, instead of an inclusions, has been extensively studied in the literature.
In
[
18
]
, J. Mawhin and M. Willem applied various methods: direct, dual, saddle points, minimax, topological degree to this problem when
\phi(x)=|x|^{2}/2
. This theory was extended in several articles, see for example
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
38
,
43
]
. The
p
-laplacian operador
(
\phi(x)=|y|^{p}/p
,
1<p<\infty
) were considered in
[
35
,
36
]
. If

\phi=\phi_{p_{1},p_{2}}(y_{1},y_{2}):=\frac{|y_{1}|^{p_{1}}}{p_{1}}+\frac{|y_{2}|^{p_{2}}}{p_{2}},

(1)

then (

P

) becomes in a periodic boundary problem for a
(p_{1},p_{2})
-laplacian system. This class of problem was studied in

[
14
,
23
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
40
,
41
]
. The question of finding periodic solutions for systems involving general
\phi
-laplacian operators was studied in
[
1
,
19
,
4
,
2
,
3
]
, while the case of differential inclusions was addressed in
[
24
,
22
,
25
,
20
]
. Hence (

P

) contains several problems that have been considered by many authors in the past. Moreover, our results improve, in many cases, the existing ones in the literature, even when restricted to the particular case of
(p_{1},p_{2})
-Laplacian systems. The latter is discussed at greater length in the Section
6
. For these reasons, we believe that anisotropic Orlicz-Sobolev spaces offer a suitable framework for unifying many known results.

2
Main results

We aim to formulate some of our results within the more general framework of Banach spaces, as we believe this may prove useful for future applications to partial differential equations.

Throughout this paper, we denote by
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
an arbitrary Banach space. In the particular case where
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we assume that
\|\cdot\|_{X}
is the Euclidean norm and denote it simply by
|\cdot|
.

Let us start by giving a brief introduction to Orlicz and Orlicz–Sobolev spaces of Banach space-valued functions. First, we introduce
G
-functions. References for these topics include
[
31
,
19
,
37
,
2
,
8
]
.

Following to Trudinger (see
[
37
]
) we say that
\phi:X\to[0,+\infty]
is a
G
-function
if it satifies that
\phi
is convex,
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\phi(x)=\infty
,
\phi(0)=0
,
\phi(-x)=\phi(x)
,
\phi
is bounded in some neighborhood of
0
and
\phi
is lower semicontinuous.

Associated to
\phi
we have the
complementary function

\phi^{\star}
which is defined at
y\in X
as

\phi^{\star}(\xi)=\sup\limits_{x\in X}\langle\xi,x\rangle-\phi(x),

(2)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X^{\star}\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is the canonical pairing between
X
and its dual space.

Definition 2.1
.

A
G
-function
\phi:X\to[0,\infty)
is called an
N-function
if
\phi(x)=0
if and only if
x=0
, and it satisfies

\lim_{\|x\|_{X}\to 0}\frac{\phi(x)}{\|x\|_{X}}=0\text{ and }\lim_{\|x\|_{X}\to\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{\|x\|_{X}}=\infty.

We say that a
G
-function
\phi:X\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
and we denote
\phi\in\Delta_{2}
,
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\phi(2x)\leq C\phi(x)+1,\quad x\in X.

(3)

Note that this definition is equivalent to the classic one,
i.e. there exist
r_{0},C>0
with
\phi(2x)\leq C\phi(x)
for
\|x\|>r_{0}
. When the inequality
\phi(2x)\leq C\phi(x)
is satisfied for every
x
, we will say that
\phi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition globally
. We denote this fact by
\phi\in\Delta_{2}^{G}
.

As in
[
30
]
, we write
\phi_{1}\llcurly\phi_{2}
if for every
k>0
there exists
C>0
such that

\phi_{1}(x)\leq C+\phi_{2}(kx),\quad x\in X.

(4)

Remark 2.1
.

Again, this definition is equivalent to say that for every
k>0
there exists
R>0
such that
\phi_{1}(x)\leq\phi_{2}(kx)
for every
\|x\|_{X}>R
.

In this article, unless otherwise specified, the measurability of functions or sets will be understood with respect to the Lebesgue
\sigma
-algebra on the interval
[0,T]
. We say that
u:[0,T]\to X
is
Bochner measurable
if
u
is a.e. limit of a sequence of simple functions. It is easy to see that if
u
is Bochner measurable and
\phi
is a
G
-function then
\phi(u)
is measurable. We define the
modular function
by

\rho_{\phi}(u):=\int_{0}^{T}\phi(u)\,dt.

The
Orlicz space

L^{\phi}=L^{\phi}\left([0,T],X\right)
is given by

L^{\phi}:=\big\{u\big|u\text{ Bochner measurable and }\exists\lambda>0:\rho_{\phi}(\lambda u)<\infty\big\}.

(5)

The Orlicz space
L^{\phi}
equipped with the
Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda\bigg|\rho_{\phi}\bigg(\frac{v}{\lambda}\bigg)\,dt\leq 1\bigg\},

is a Banach space.

We define the
Orlicz-Sobolev space

W^{1}L^{\phi}=W^{1}L^{\phi}\left([0,T],X\right)
by

W^{1}L^{\phi}:=\big\{u|u\text{ is absolutely continuous, }u^{\prime}\in L^{\phi}\big\}.

See
[
5
]
for the definition and properties of
X
-valued absolutely continuous functions.
The space
W^{1}L^{\phi}
is a Banach space when it is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1}L^{\phi}}=\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}+\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\phi}}.

(6)

The subspace
W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}
of
W^{1}L^{\phi}
is defined by

W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}:=W^{1}L^{\phi}\cap\big\{u|u(0)=u(T)\big\}.

Note that
W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}
is a closed subspace of
W^{1}L^{\phi}
.

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
a locally Lipschitz function and
x,v\in X
, following
[
9
,
10
]
, we define the
generalized directional derivative
of
f
at
x
in the direction
v
as

f^{0}(x;v)=\limsup_{y\rightarrow x,\lambda\searrow 0}\frac{f(y+\lambda v)-f(y)}{\lambda}

and we denote by

\partial f(x)=\left\{\xi\in X^{\star}:f^{0}(x;v)\geqslant\left\langle\xi,v\right\rangle,\text{ for all }v\in X\right\}

the
Clarke generalized gradient
of
f
at
x
.

We recall that
f
is called
regular
when
f^{0}(x;v)
agree with the usual directional derivative (see
[
9
,
10
]
).

Our main results consist of three existence theorems for solutions, each of which is based on different hypotheses on the function
F
; however, the following assumptions are common to all three theorems:

1.

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
is a strictly convex
G
-function such that both
\phi
and its conjugate
\phi^{\star}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

2.

For a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, the function

is regular and locally Lipschitz with respect to
x
.

3.

There exist a function
b\in L^{1}([0,T];[0,\infty))
and a function
c:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, with
a
bounded on bounded subsets, such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for a.e.

and for every
\xi_{t}\in\partial F(t,x)
,

|F(t,x)|+|\xi_{t}|\leq c(x)b(t).

(7)

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that

–
3
hold, along with the following assumptions:

1.

There exist a function

and a

such that for every

\phi^{\star}\left(\frac{\xi_{t}}{d(t)}\right)\leq\phi_{0}(x)+1.

(8)

2.

Coercivity condition:

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{1}{\phi_{0}(2x)}\int_{0}^{T}F(t,x)\,dt=\infty.

(9)

Then the functional
\mathcal{I}
admits a minimizer

, which solves problem (

P

).

We also establish the existence of solutions under a different set of assumptions:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
1
–
3
be satisfied, and consider the following additional conditions:

1.

Quasi-subadditivity: There exist constants
\lambda,\mu>0
such that

F(t,\lambda(x+y))\leq\mu\big(F(t,x)+F(t,y)\big).

2.

There exist a

-function
\phi_{0}\llcurly\phi
and a function

Then the functional

Before formulating our third theorem, let us introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that the continuous function

belongs to the space

It is immediate see that

.

.

Assume that conditions

–

and
3

‑function, and that the following additional assumptions are satisfied:

There exists

We would like to point out that throughout this article, the letter
C
will be used to denote constants, and its value may vary from line to line, even within a chain of inequalities.

Given a Banach space
X
we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the usual bilinear quadratic form defined on

.

Let us start by recalling a few simple facts about
G
-functions. We suggest
[

,
29
,

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\phi:X\to[0,\infty)
is a
G
-function then

1.

\phi(\lambda x)\leq\lambda\phi(x)
, for every
\lambda\in[0,1],x\in X
;

2.

\phi(\lambda_{1}x)\leq\phi(\lambda_{2}x)
, when
0<|\lambda_{1}|\leq|\lambda_{2}|
,

3.

If
\phi
is Frechet differentiable then
\phi^{\star}\left(\phi^{\prime}(x)\right)\leq\langle\phi^{\prime}(x),x\rangle\leq\phi(2x)
.

4.

\langle\xi,x\rangle\leq\phi(x)+\phi^{\star}(\xi)
(Fenchel inequality).

5.

If
\phi^{\star}\in\Delta_{2}
then
\phi
satisfies the
\nabla_{2}
-condition
, i.e. for every
0<r<1
there exist

and
C=C(r)>0
such that

\phi(x)\leq\frac{r}{l}\phi(lx)+C,\quad x\in X.

(11)

Remark 3.1
.

It is easy to see that (
11
) is equivalent to assume that there exists
R>0
such that (
11
) holds with
C=0
,

and for
|x|>R
.

In what follows we give some properties of Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

Proposition 3.2

(
Hölder’s inequality
)
.

If
u\in L^{\phi}([0,T],X)
and
v\in L^{\phi^{\star}}([0,T],X^{\star})
then
\langle v,u\rangle\in L^{1}
and

\int_{0}^{T}\langle v,u\rangle dt\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}\|v\|_{L^{\phi^{\star}}}.

(12)

Suppose
u\in L^{\phi}([0,T],X)
and consider
K:=\rho_{\phi}(u)+1\geq 1
. Then, from item 1 in Proposition
3.1
we have
\rho_{\phi}(K^{-1}u)\leq K^{-1}\rho_{\phi}(u)\leq 1
. Therefore, we conclude

\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}\leq\rho_{\phi}(u)+1.

(13)

As is customary, we will use the decomposition
u=\overline{u}+\widetilde{u}
for a function
u\in L^{1}([0,T],X)
, where
\overline{u}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\,dt
and
\widetilde{u}=u-\overline{u}
. The last integral is taken in the Bochner sense (see
[
21
]
). Then, one has

W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}=\widetilde{W}^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}\oplus X,

where
X
is identified with the set of constant functions and

\widetilde{W}^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}=\{u\in W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}:\overline{u}=0\}.

We recall (see Lemma 2.4 in
[
19
]
and Theorem 4.4 in
[
8
]
) the following inequality.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\phi:X\to[0,+\infty)
be a
G
-function,
and let
u\in W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}\left([0,T],X\right)
. Then

\phi\big(\tilde{u}(t)\big)\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\phi\big(Tu^{\prime}(r)\big)\,dr.

(14)

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\phi
be a
G
-function. Then:

1.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that
\|u\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}
, for every
u\in L^{\phi}([0,T],X)
and
\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
, for every
u\in L^{\infty}
. Briefly
L^{\infty}([0,T],X)\hookrightarrow L^{\phi}([0,T],X)\hookrightarrow L^{1}([0,T],X)
. Consequently
W^{1}L^{\phi}([0,T],X)\hookrightarrow W^{1}L^{1}([0,T],X)
.

2.

(Anisotropic Poincaré-Wirtinger Inequality) For every
u\in L^{\phi}([0,T],X)
we have that
\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\phi}}\leq T\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\phi}}
. Even more
\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\phi}}
.

3.

W^{1}L^{\phi}([0,T],X)\hookrightarrow C^{0}([0,T],X)
.

4.

\|u\|_{W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}}^{\prime}:=|\overline{u}|+\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\phi}}
defines an equivalent norm to
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1}L^{\phi}}
on
W^{1}L^{\phi}_{T}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

5.

Every bounded sequence
\{u_{n}\}
in
W^{1}L^{\phi}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
has an uniformly convergent subsequence.

6.

If
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
(as usual
\rightharpoonup
denotes weak convergence) in
W^{1}L^{\phi}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
u_{n}
converges to
u
uniformly.

Proof.

All the results listed above are essentially known, as can be seen in the cited literature. For completeness, we include brief proofs of those results that we have not found explicitly stated in the references.

Applying inequality (
14
) to the function
u/T\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\phi}}
we get

\phi\left(\frac{\tilde{u}}{T\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\phi}}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\phi\left(\frac{u^{\prime}}{\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\phi}}}\right)\leq T^{-1}.

(15)

Integrating (

) for

we obtain the first inequality in item

. Since
\phi(x)\to\infty
, when

, we can find a constant

.

Item

For

Item

The impact of materialism on prosocial behavior of Chinese adolescents: a moderated mediating effect test
Authors: Yanhan Wei, Jiandong Fang, Baorui Chang, Hang Lv
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aims to explore the predictive effect of materialism on prosocial behavior and its influencing mechanism.Methodsstudy 1 used the materialism scale, prosocial behavior scale and meaning in life scale to investigate 715 Chinese adolescents with an average age of 16.82 years. Study 2 manipulated the materialism of 128 adolescents (123 in the control group), and measured their meaning in life and prosocial behavior after manipulation and verification.ResultsThe results of the two studies consistently found that materialism has a significant negative predictive effect on prosocial behavior; the meaning in life plays a partial mediating role in the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior; gender moderated the relationship between meaning in life and prosocial behavior.ConclusionsThis study revealed the internal mechanism of the influence of materialism on prosocial behavior, and provides evidence for its causal relationship through experimental research. On the one hand, materialism negatively predicts prosocial behavior; on the other hand, materialism indirectly reduced prosocial behavior by reducing the meaning in life. Finally, gender moderated the relationship between meaning in life and prosocial behavior. Specifically, compared with male adolescents, female adolescents ' meaning in life had a more significant effect on prosocial behavior. These findings provide a new perspective for promoting individual prosocial behavior programs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40359-025-03433-1.Keywords: Materialism, Prosocial behavior, Meaning in life, Gender, Adolescents

Introduction

Prosocial behavior is an individual ‘s conscious behavior that is beneficial to others in social interaction [1]. It is not only an important behavior to maintain interpersonal relationships and social harmony, but also an important symbol of individual growth and development [2]. For example, prosocial behavior plays an important role in maintaining adolescent mental health [3], promoting the development of learning ability and social ability [4], and helping to establish good relationships with family and peers [5]. It can be seen that prosocial behavior is extremely important in personal growth and development, and adolescence is a critical period for the development of individual sociality ( including prosocial behavior ) [6]. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate the influencing factors of prosocial behavior of adolescent groups, and to explore its internal mechanism, so as to lay a theoretical foundation for the follow-up intervention of prosocial behavior.

With the rapid development of society and the diversification of values, the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior has become a hot issue in the field of psychology. Materialism is a personal value that emphasizes the importance of owning material wealth [7]. The prevalence of consumerism culture makes materialistic values increasingly common among adolescents, which poses a potential threat to adolescents ' prosocial behavior [8]. As the survey shows [9], the importance of values such as materialism and individualism among Chinese people is increasing, while the importance of values such as collectivism is decreasing. Multiple follow-up studies have shown that the increase in materialism is significantly negatively correlated with adolescents ' prosocial behavior [10, 11]. The reason is that some researchers believe that materialism mainly affects adolescents ' prosocial behavior by triggering insecurity experiences and encouraging social models of materialistic goals / values [12]. Some researchers have also examined the influence of materialism on prosocial behavior from the dimension of interpersonal relationship (such as gratitude [8] and empathy [10]). In addition to the above research, existing studies have shown that the meaning of life is closely related to materialism and prosocial behavior [13, 14]. However, no research has examined the role of meaning in life in materialism and prosocial behavior. And the existing research mostly uses tracking research, lack of experimental research. Therefore, this study attempts to explore the role of meaning in life in the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior, and tries to further verify the research results through experimental methods.

Materialism and prosocial behavior

Many studies have shown that there is a close relationship between individual values and individual prosocial behavior. For example, self-transcendence values [15] and prosocial behavior values [16] promote prosocial behavior. Materialism, as a value of material supremacy, regards wealth as the center of life and the source of happiness, and judges the success of oneself and others [17], which is contrary to the meaning of sharing, cooperation and altruism expressed by the value orientation of prosocial behavior [18]. This is in line with the value ring model proposed by Grouzet et al. [19], which divides human values into four quadrants. Compatible values are adjacent to each other, while conflicting values are opposite to each other. Specifically, self-value-added values tend to strengthen the realization of personal interests ( such as material accumulation and hedonism ), while self-transcendence values advocate value creation in a broader social dimension ( such as prosocial behavior ) [20]. Based on this theory, Burroughs and Rindfleisch [21] put forward the theory of value conflict, believing that materialism belongs to a kind of self-enhancing values, which is opposite to self-transcendence values. Therefore, the promotion of materialism may inhibit prosocial behavior driven by self-transcendence values ( seesaw effect, that is, activating a certain value will inhibit other values that conflict with this value [22] ). The results of laboratory studies indicated that when materialism was briefly activated, participants demonstrated a reduction in their willingness to provide assistance to others [23], an increase in their support for anti-welfare policies [24] and a heightened recognition of social inequality [25]. Multiple follow-up studies have also shown that materialism inhibits prosocial behavior [10, 11]. In summary, this study proposes hypothesis 1: materialism negatively predicts prosocial behavior.

The mediating role of meaning in life

The pursuit of meaning in life is a common intrinsic motivation of people [26], This study suggests that the sense of meaning in life may play a mediating role in the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior.

The sense of meaning in life refers to the individual ‘s perception of the integrity, purpose and importance of life [27]. Under the background of emphasizing prosperity and development in today ‘s world, people ‘s pursuit of material wealth may weaken people ‘s pursuit of deeper and more substantial meaning of life. The basic psychological needs theory points out that [28] people have three common internal needs : autonomy, competence and relevance. Based on this theoretical framework, some scholars put forward three hypothesis models of the sense of life meaning [29] which all emphasize the promotion effect of the satisfaction of basic psychological needs on the sense of life meaning. On the contrary, the value ring model classifies materialism as a self-value-added value, and its driving force comes from external factors such as social expectations and rewards. Such externally driven value pursuits are difficult to meet basic psychological needs or enhance the sense of meaning in life [20]. In addition, the sense of meaning in life is associated with self-transcendence values, the core of which is to find the purpose, connection and meaning of transcending self, which is diametrically opposed to the self-value-added values represented by materialism [30]. According to the value conflict theory and the seesaw effect, activating individual materialism will inhibit the individual ‘s sense of life meaning. Empirical studies from the United States [31], China [32] and the Philippines [13] support the above view, believing that materialism will reduce the individual ‘s sense of meaning in life. In summary, this study proposes hypothesis 2: materialism negatively predicts meaning in life.

Concurrently, meaning in life has a positive social effect. An individual’s perception of meaning in life encompasses not only their own valuation of life, but also their perception of their own social significance [33]. Those who pursue a sense of purpose in life tend to exhibit less self-centered tendencies and greater inclination to consider the broader interests of others and society at large [34]. Some researchers posit that individuals with a heightened meaning in life demonstrate proclivity towards contributing to society [35] and fostering positive interpersonal relationships [36] These actions are internalized into personal goals [37]. The self-determination theory [38], posits that individuals with a higher meaning in life, driven by goals and missions, exhibit stronger intrinsic motivation for prosocial behavior, which they display autonomously and voluntarily. Furthermore, Yang et al. [39] posit that the motivational role of meaning in life affects individual behavior. As an individual’s tendency to pursue the meaning in life intensifies, the more he tends to see himself as a giver, the more he is willing to help others and contribute to society [40]. A number of empirical studies from China have demonstrated that meaning in life can facilitate prosocial behavior [41–43]. It can thus be seen that materialistic values and meaning in life represent two opposing internal driving forces [21], with a significant negative correlation between them [32]. The pursuit of meaning in life represents one of the intrinsic motivations underlying individual prosocial behavior, which can in turn promote such behavior [38]. Therefore, this study proposes Hypothesis 3: Meaning in life plays a mediating role between materialism and prosocial behavior.

Moderating effect of gender

Previous studies have used gender as a control variable when examining the relationship between meaning in life and prosocial behavior. We hypothesize that the impact of meaning in life on prosocial behavior may be moderated by gender. A recent study from China found gender differences in the core dimensions of prosocial behavior using network analysis [6]. Studies from other countries have also found that girls are more likely than boys to engage in prosocial activities that emphasize helping, self-disclosure, and interpersonal relationships [44], whereas boys are more likely to engage in prosocial behavior in the face of danger and urgency and need strength [45]. We believe that this difference may be due to different gender role expectations between men and women, and that these expectations are related to their sense of purpose in life. According to gender schema theory [46], in everyday life we express ourselves in gender roles that meet social demands and expectations, and we constantly adjust our behavior according to feedback from others and the requirements of our social roles. For example, men are taught from an early age to be independent, brave and decisive, while women are taught to care for others and to be concerned about harmonious relationships between people. These gender role expectations subtly influence individuals’ perceptions and behaviors [47], such that girls exhibit more prosocial behaviors than boys. We mentioned earlier, individuals with a higher meaning in life internalize contributing to society and maintaining good interpersonal relationships into personal goals, which is similar to women’s gender role expectations. Therefore, meaning in life has a facilitating effect on prosocial behavior for both men and women, but the facilitating effect is stronger for the female group. In addition, teenage women are more focused on small-scale interactions and have an advantage over male groups in providing support, intimacy and empathy [48]. This makes it easier for women to engage in prosocial behavior than men in groups with higher meaning in life. It can be seen that gender and meaning in life may interact to affect individual prosocial behavior. Compared with men, meaning in life has greater effect on women’s prosocial behavior. Therefore, this study proposes hypothesis 4: Gender plays a moderating role in the relationship between meaning in life and prosocial behavior.

In order to obtain more robust research results, two studies (Study 1: related research; study 2: Experimental study), to explore the influence of materialism on prosocial behavior, and to examine the mediating role of meaning in life and the moderating role of gender.

Study 1: a questionnaire survey was conducted to investigate the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior and its mechanism

Participants

Using the cluster sampling method, 748 questionnaires were distributed to a high school in N city, Guangxi Province. After recovering, the questionnaires that did not answer carefully (the answers have obvious rules and the chosen options are wrong) were deleted, and 716 valid questionnaires were obtained, with an effective rate of 95.72%. There were 388 females (54.19%) and 328 males (45.81%). There were 226 people in urban areas (31.61%) and 489 people in rural areas (68.39%). The mean age was 16.82 years (SD = 0.48).

Measures

Materialistic values scale

The Chinese version of the materialistic values scale was created by Richins et al. [7] and revised by Li et al. [49]. The scale consists of 13 items and 3 dimensions. Using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree), the higher the total average score, the higher the tendency to materialism. In this study, the Cronbach alpha coefficient of the scale was 0.81.

Prosocial behavior scale

The prosocial behavior scale developed by Zhang et al. [50] was used. The scale has 15 items, including four dimensions: altruism, compliance, relationship and trait. It is scored on a 7-point Likert scale, from 1 (completely inconsistent) to 7 (completely consistent). The total score of the item was calculated and the higher the score, the more prosocial behavior. The Cronbach alpha coefficient of the scale in this study was 0.86.

The meaning in life scale

The meaning in life scale compiled by Steger [51], and revised by Liu et al. [52] was used. The scale consists of 9 items, which are composed of two subscales: presence of meaning in life (5 items) and search for meaning in life (4 items). Using Likert 6-point scoring, from 1 (very disagree) to 7 (very agree), the total score of the item was calculated. The higher the score, the stronger the sense of meaning in life. The Cronbach ‘s α coefficient of the scale in this study was 0.83.

Procedure and data processing

After the questionnaire was collected, EpiData3.1 software was used for data entry and processing. SPSS22.0 was used to conduct common method deviation detection, descriptive statistical analysis and correlation analysis of each variable, and SPSS macro program PROCESS plug-in was used to test the mediating effect of meaning in life between materialism and prosocial behavior and the moderating effect of gender.

Study 1 results

Common method deviation test

The use of questionnaires to collect data may have resulted in common method bias. In order to minimize this type of bias, this study emphasized the anonymity of questionnaires in the process of research design and data collection, reverse scoring of some topics, and the use of questionnaires with high reliability and validity for pre-procedural control. After data recovery, the single-factor Harman test method was used. The results showed that the characteristic roots of the eight factors were greater than 1, and the variation explained by the first factor was 17.98%, far less than 40%. This shows that there is no obvious common method bias in the data of this study.

Meaning in life was significantly positively correlated with prosocial behavior (r = 0.41, p < 0.001). There was no correlation between age and household registration and any of the study variables, and these two variables were no longer considered in subsequent analyses.

Test of moderated mediating effect

Table 2 presents the principal results obtained through the SPSS macro–PROCESS Model 14 based on Hayes et al. [53] delineating three components: the mediation variable model, the dependent variable model, and the assessment of moderated mediation effects. The mediation variable model investigated the influence of materialism on meaning in life, and the dependent variable model examined the effects of materialism, meaning in life, and gender on prosocial behavior. Conditional indirect effect analysis was conducted to examine the effects of meaning in life on prosocial behavior across genders. Results from the mediation model (F = 13.178, R2 = 0.136, p < 0.001) and the dependent variable model (F = 42.626, R2 = 0.440, p < 0.001) indicated that, materialism negatively predicted meaning in life (β = -0.14, p < 0.001), positively predicted prosocial behavior (β = 0.47, p < 0.001), and negatively predicted prosocial behavior (β = -0.11, p = 0.001). These findings suggested that meaning in life acted as a mediating factor in the association between materialism and prosocial behavior. Moreover, the interaction effect between sense of meaning in life and gender had a significant impact on prosocial behavior (β = -0.16, p = 0.018), indicating that gender moderated the association between sense of meaning in life and prosocial behavior.

Discussion of Study1

Study 1 verified the hypothesis of this study through questionnaire survey, that is, materialism negatively predicted prosocial behavior, meaning in life played a mediating role in the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior, and gender played a moderating role in the relationship between meaning in life and prosocial behavior. In order to overcome the inherent limitations of the cross-sectional study, Study 2 will manipulate the materialism of the participants through experimental priming to test the influence of materialism on prosocial behavior and its mechanism of action, and provided convincing evidence for further causal inference in this study.

Study 2: The experimental method examines the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior and its underlying mechanism

Participants

G * power 3.1.9.7 was used to estimate the sample size. The effect size was set to 0.8, α was 0.05, and the effect value was 0.95. Study 2 was conducted in two middle schools. A total of 251 adolescents with an average age of 15.04 (SD = 0.84) were recruited and randomly divided into the Materialism Priming Group (Priming Group) and the Control Group. Among them, there were 128 students in the initiation group, including 71 boys and 52 girls; there were 123 students in the control group, including 65 boys and 72 girls.

Measures

The manipulation of materialistic

Referring to the experimental paradigm of Wierzbicki and Zawadzka [54], the ads of Lamborghini and Dior were clipped into a materialistic priming group video for 4 minutes and 23 seconds. The control group watched neutral landscape videos of similar duration. The Identity Theory of Materialism [55]points out that materialism is situational. Previous studies have successfully primed participants ' materialism through situational experiments [56, 57].

The test of materialistic

The operational test questions compiled by Chang et al. [57] according to the three dimensions of materialism proposed by Richins and Dawson [7], plus ‘At this moment ‘, were used to evaluate the current state materialism of the participants. A total of 4 questions, such as: ‘1. At this moment, I like my life has many luxury’. Using Likert 7-point scoring, from 1 ‘completely inconsistent’ to 7 ‘completely consistent’, the higher the score, the higher the level of materialism at this time. The Cronbach ‘s α of the scale in this study was 0.72.

Prosocial behavior intention situation

The situational questionnaire of Li et al. [58] was adapted to measure the prosocial behavior intention of the subjects. The material situation is carried out from a third-person perspective, which can effectively avoid the influence of social desirability. Li Ming was walking in the school when he saw a volunteer organization organizing an activity, the main content of which was to send condolences and love to the local nursing home. Most of the elderly there are lonely and widowed without children to take care of. Although there are nursing workers to take care of life on weekdays, they still hope to have young people to chat with them and give them performances. Visiting the elderly takes half a day at a time. If you are Li Ming, how many times a week would you like to participate in volunteer activities if you have enough time? Participants need to make a choice between 0 and 6 times, and the more times indicate the higher the willingness of prosocial behavior.

The meaning in life scale

The same as study 1. The Cronbach ‘s α of the scale in this study was 0.86.

Procedure

In this study, a single-factor between-subjects experimental design was used. The independent variable was materialism and the dependent variable was prosocial behavior. First, subjects were randomly assigned to the materialism initiation group and the control group. After watching the priming video (or neutral video), the subjects first filled in the test of Materialistic, then filled in the prosocial behavior scale, the meaning in life scale and demographic variables, and finally asked whether the subjects guessed the purpose of this experiment.

Study 2 results

Materialistic manipulation test results

The results of independent sample T test showed that the level of state materialism in the priming group (M = 3.46, SD = 0.69) was significantly higher than that in the control group (M = 2.87, SD = 0.65) (t = 6.97, p < 0.001, Cohen ‘s d = 0.88). The results showed that using advertising videos to manipulate participants’ materialism is effective.

Test of moderated mediating effect

Categorical variables need to be virtualized into statistical analysis, and the start group was coded as 1, and the control group was coded as 0. Firstly, the mediating effect analysis was carried out, and Model 4 of SPSS macro program PROCESS was used to test the mediating role of meaning in life between materialism and prosocial behavior. As shown in Table 3, materialism can significantly negatively have predicted prosocial behavior (β = -1.32, t = -7.80, p < 0.001). Subsequently, meaning in life was included in the statistical analysis as a mediating variable. The results showed that the regression coefficient of the direct effect of materialism on prosocial behavior was reduced but still significant (β = -1.22, t = -7.46, p < 0.001), and the 95% confidence interval was [-1.550, -0.902]. In addition, materialism significantly negatively predicted meaning in life (β = -0.23, t = -2.03 p < 0.05), and meaning in life significantly positively predicted prosocial behavior (β = 0.40, t = 4.47, p < 0.05). Boot ab = -0.09, Boot SE = 0.05, 95% confidence interval [-0.192, -0.004]. This shows that meaning in life plays a partial mediating role in the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior.

General discussion

The relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior

In this study, through two research methods of correlational research and laboratory experiments, we found that materialism negatively predicted prosocial behavior, which was consistent with previous research results [54]. This result verified the value conflict theory [21], that is, materialism belonged to the ‘self-improvement’ side, while prosocial behavior belonged to the opposite ‘self-transcendence’ side, and there was opposition and conflict between the two sides. These two dimensions were in opposition to one another, representing a fundamental conflict. Therefore, the enhancement of materialism will reduce prosocial behavior driven by self-transcendence values. Furthermore, the prevailing social milieu was replete with factors that were inimical to prosocial conduct, with materialism representing a prominent example [8]. From the perspective of money’s social resource theory, money served as a substitute for social relations, thereby reducing individuals’ attention to and reliance on interpersonal relationships [59]. This allowed higher materialism to lead to greater social comparison and competitiveness between individuals and others [60], which in turn reduced the likelihood of cooperation with others and generosity [23], This ultimately resulted in a reduction in individual prosocial behavior. This suggested that we could start by reducing people’s materialism if we wanted to create a ‘benevolent’ social environment. For example, family parenting style was an important factor affecting adolescent materialism [61], It was possible to reduce the level of materialism among adolescents by providing parents with guidance on how to conduct reasonable family education.

The mediating role of meaning in life

In order to further analyze the internal mechanism of materialism affecting prosocial behavior, this study introduced the mediating role of meaning in life from the perspective of intrinsic motivation, and the results confirmed the research hypothesis. On the one hand, materialism may have indirectly diminished prosocial conduct by diminishing the sense of purpose and meaning in life. This was due to the fact that a materialistic lifestyle compelled an individual to perpetually seek wealth. In the event that these materialistic aspirations remained unfulfilled, the resulting frustration and negative affectivity [31], may have given rise to a sense of meaninglessness in life, which in turn may have diminished prosocial conduct [13].

On the other hand, when the concept of the meaning in life was integrated into the equation analysis of materialism and prosocial behavior, it was evident that the direct impact of materialism on prosocial behavior was diminished. This demonstrated that the negative impact of materialism on prosocial behavior could be offset by meaning in life, which could help to reduce materialism and increase prosocial behavior. The influence of meaning in life and materialism on prosocial behavior was a significant area of research [41, 62]. Those with high levels of materialism tended to exhibit self-centered tendencies, prioritizing their own interests above those of society [17]. Individuals who possessed the high level of meaning in life exhibited a reduced tendency towards self-centeredness, demonstrated a heightened level of attention towards societal matters [34], and demonstrated the greater propensity to invest effort in the pursuit of social connections, thereby engaging in a greater number of prosocial behaviors [38]. This suggested that we could not only improve prosocial behavior by reducing materialism, but also improve individuals’ meaning in life through interventions such as music and imagination [63, 64], meaning-making coping strategies [65] and the cultivation of gratitude and courage [66]from the perspective of meaning in life, so as to improve prosocial behavior.

Moderating effect of gender

This study found that gender regulated the effect of meaning in life on prosocial behavior. Specifically, meaning in life could promote prosocial behavior in male adolescents, and the promoting effect of meaning in life was stronger among female adolescents. Studies pointed out that girls were often more likely to feel the warmth and care of their parents, encouraging them to show empathy and care for others [67]. Adolescence was a period of high incidence of problem behavior among male adolescents. Because parents were more tightly controlled, male adolescents might view prosocial behavior as an externally imposed obligation, which affected the development of their prosocial behavior [68]. Therefore, gender differences in parenting styles might have influenced the development of an individual’s subsequent prosocial behavior. Gender socialization theory suggested that females were more concerned about others in the socialization process, whereas males might inhibit their willingness to engage in prosocial behavior in some situations [69]. This explained, to some extent, why meaning in life had a greater impact on prosocial behavior among female groups. This also suggested that while paying attention to women’s education and training, we should also have cultivated men’s empathy and caring ability so that men’s prosocial behavior ability could have been well developed.

Theoretical implication and practical significance

This study contributes in several ways. First, this study confirms that materialism has a negative impact on prosocial behavior. While verifying the value conflict theory [21]and previous studies [23, 24], it also provides empirical evidence from Chinese youth groups for future related research. Second, this study pioneered the mediating role of life meaning in the relationship between materialism and prosocial behavior, providing a new mechanism for materialism to influence prosocial behavior. The results show that materialism can indirectly reduce prosocial behavior by reducing individuals’ sense of meaning in life. This suggests that interventions in both the direction of materialism [61]and meaning in life [63]can increase an individual’s prosocial behavior. Finally, this study creatively explores the moderating role of gender in the relationship between meaning in life and prosocial behavior. The results show that compared to men, women’s meaning in life has a more significant effect on prosocial behavior. This enlightens us that in the process of improving individuals’ prosocial behavior through meaning in life intervention, we should pay attention to the male group and help them form the ability to be grateful and care for others, which can make the intervention effect better [70].

Limitation

There are some limitations in this study. First of all, prosocial behavior data came from the self-report of the participants. Future research should use other more comprehensive and objective measurement methods, such as physiological indicators and field observation methods to measure the true prosocial behavior of individuals [71]. Secondly, since this study is based on Chinese adolescents, the results may not be applicable to adolescents in other regions or countries. Finally, there may be other variables that affect the results of this experiment, such as family economic status will affect the individual ‘s sense of meaning in life [72] and prosocial behavior [73], future research should control these variables to obtain more robust conclusions.

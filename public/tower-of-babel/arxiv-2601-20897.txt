Missing digits and sums of two prime squares
Cihan Sabuncu

Abstract
. We investigate integers whose base g g expansion omits a fixed digit and which can be represented as a sum of two prime squares. In the first part of the paper, we apply the Hardy–Littlewood circle method to obtain asymptotic formulas for weighted count of representations of such integers up to g k g^{k} as k → ∞ k\to\infty , where we weight by the von Mangoldt function. In this case, we also get an interesting bias depending on the fixed digit we are missing. In the second part, combining the circle method with sieve methods, we study the second moment of the corresponding unweighted counting function. This allows us to get a nontrivial lower bound for the cardinality of the set { n ⩽ g k : n omits the digit b in its base g expansion and n = p 2 + q 2 for some primes p , q } . \{n\leqslant g^{k}:n\text{ omits the digit }b\text{ in its base }g\text{ expansion and }n=p^{2}+q^{2}\text{ for some primes }p,q\}.



1.  Introduction

Numbers with missing digits have been studied extensively in number theory because of their distinctive arithmetic properties. This is particularly true in sieve theory, where such sets are of interest due to their sparsity. In recent years, missing digit problems have also been approached using the Hardy–Littlewood circle method. This approach is viable because the indicator function of the set of missing digit numbers admits strong Fourier decay properties. Exploiting this structure, Maynard
[
14
]
showed that for a sufficiently large base
g
, there are infinitely many primes whose base
g
expansion omits a given digit
0\leqslant b<g
. He later refined this result to work for the base
g=10
, see
[
13
]
.

Following the work of Maynard
[
14
]
, there has been substantial progress on problems concerning primes with missing digits. In particular, attention has turned to variants of the classical problems in number theory incorporating missing digit restrictions. One such example is the Vinogradov three primes theorem. In this direction, Maier and Rassias
[
12
]
established a version of Vinogradov’s three primes theorem involving one missing digit prime and two Piatetski–Shapiro primes. This was later refined by Leng and Sawhney
[
10
]
, who proved Vinogradov’s three primes theorem with all three primes missing a digit.

Another central problem in number theory, particularly in the context of the Hardy–Littlewood circle method, is Waring’s problem. Given a positive integer
n
, the problem asks for the smallest number of
k
-th powers required to represent
n
as their sum. This question has been studied extensively, see Vaughan and Wooley
[
20
]
for a survey on this problem.

In a variant of this problem where the
k
-th powers are required to come from numbers with restricted digit expansions, Green
[
6
]
proved that every sufficiently large integer can be written as a sum of at most
g^{160k^{2}}
terms of the form
x^{k}
, where
x
has a base
g
expansion using only two digits.

In this paper, we will study a problem that is somewhat related to both the Waring problem and the missing digit problem. For this, we start by defining the normalized representation function for sums of two prime squares

r_{2}(n):=\sum_{a^{2}+b^{2}=n}\Lambda(a)\Lambda(b),

where
\Lambda(\cdot)
is the von Mangoldt function.
Then we know by the Prime Number Theorem in arithmetic progressions, for any
A>0
,
1\leqslant q\leqslant(\log x)^{A}
and
0\leqslant a<q
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
n\equiv a\,({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}r_{2}(n)=\frac{\pi}{4}\cdot\frac{\rho(a;q)}{\varphi^{2}(q)}x+O_{A}\bigg(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\bigg),

where
\rho(a;q):=\#\{(u,v)\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{2}:(uv,q)=1,u^{2}+v^{2}\equiv a\,({\rm mod}\,q)\}
.
Our first theorem is about the average of this function over the missing digit set. For this, we fix the base
g\geqslant 2
, and let
X=g^{k}
for some
k
large. We take the digit we want to miss
0\leqslant b<g
. Now, we can define
\mathcal{A}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}g^{i},a_{i}\not=b\}
, and
\mathcal{A}(X):=\mathcal{A}\cap[1,X]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
g\geqslant 2
sufficiently large. Let

(1.1)

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}(X)}r_{2}(n)=\frac{\pi}{4}\cdot\mathfrak{S}(b,g)\#\mathcal{A}(X)+O_{A}\bigg(\frac{\#\mathcal{A}(X)}{(\log X)^{A}}\bigg),

where

\mathfrak{S}(b,g)=\frac{g}{g-1}\bigg(1-\frac{\rho(b;g)}{\varphi^{2}(g)}\bigg),

and
\rho(a;q)

In this regard, there has been some work on averages of arithmetic functions over missing digit numbers. More recently, Nath
[

1.1  Idea of the Proof

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we use the circle method as was used in the paper of Maynard
[
14
]
to study primes with missing digits. Major arcs are defined by

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leqslant(\log X)^{B}\\
(r,s)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg[\frac{r}{s}-\frac{(\log X)^{B}}{X},\frac{r}{s}+\frac{(\log X)^{B}}{X}\bigg].

Most of the analysis for major arcs is similar except for getting the local factor, which requires understanding the prime factorization of the base
g
. For the minor arcs, we need to understand sums of the form

for

. We can get cancellations for these sums, see Lemma
2.4
.

For the proof of Theorem

to split

\min\{x,y\}>t^{1/4}\end{subarray}}1dxdy=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A}}+O_{\varepsilon}(X^{3/4+\varepsilon}).

∎

The following is an elementary lemma that we give without the proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
x
be a sufficiently large number and take

We start with the following result on exponential sums over squares of primes. Similar estimates appeared in

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

See Vinogradov
[

for the latter inequality. For the first inequality, we trivially bound when

which is negligible. In the case

[

by splitting

We use this to get information on exponential sums of

Open up

Remark 2.9
.

Note that if we had major arcs of the form

3.  Average of the Representation Function

We work on (
1.1
),
i.e.

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}(X)}r_{2}(n)=\sum_{n\leqslant X}r_{2}(n)1_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\frac{1}{X}\sum_{a\leqslant X}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{a}{X}\bigg)S\bigg(\frac{-a}{X}\bigg),

where

\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}(X)}e(\theta n)

is the Fourier transform of the indicator, and

S(\theta):=\sum_{n\leqslant X}r_{2}(n)e(\theta n),

is the Fourier transform of
r_{2}(n)
.
Now, we split into major and minor arcs. Firstly, note that by the Dirichlet approximation theorem, we can write
\frac{a}{X}=\frac{r}{s}+\xi
for
s\leqslant X^{3/4}
with
s|\xi|\leqslant 1/X^{3/4}
. So, if we fix
B>0
large enough, then we can define

\mathfrak{M}_{r,s}:=\bigg\{\alpha\in[0,1]:\bigg|\alpha-\frac{r}{s}\bigg|\leqslant\frac{(\log X)^{B}}{X}\bigg\}.

Then, major arcs are defined as

\mathfrak{M}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leqslant(\log X)^{B}\\
(r,s)=1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{M}_{r,s},

and minor arcs are

\mathfrak{m}:=[0,1]\setminus\mathfrak{M}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\sum_{a\leqslant X}

\displaystyle\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{a}{X}\bigg)S\bigg(\frac{-a}{X}\bigg)=\frac{1}{X}\sum_{a/X\in\mathfrak{M}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{a}{X}\bigg)S\bigg(\frac{-a}{X}\bigg)+\frac{1}{X}\sum_{a/X\in\mathfrak{m}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{a}{X}\bigg)S\bigg(\frac{-a}{X}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leqslant(\log X)^{B}\\
(r,s)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\eta|\leqslant(\log X)^{B}\\
Xr/s+\eta\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\eta}{X}\bigg)S\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+\frac{-\eta}{X}\bigg)+\frac{1}{X}\sum_{a/X\in\mathfrak{m}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{a}{X}\bigg)S\bigg(\frac{-a}{X}\bigg),

since we can write
\xi=\frac{\eta}{X}
.

3.1  Major Arcs

For the major arcs, we have
\frac{a}{X}=\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\eta}{X}
with
|\eta|\leqslant(\log X)^{B}
. We split into 3 cases:

Case 1.
\exists p|s
but
p\nmid g
:

In this case by Lemma
2.8
, we get

\frac{1}{X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leqslant(\log X)^{B}\\
\exists p|s,p\nmid g\\
(r,s)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\eta|\leqslant(\log X)^{B}\\
Xr/s+\eta\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\eta}{X}\bigg)S\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+\frac{-\eta}{X}\bigg)\ll_{\varepsilon}\#\mathcal{A}(X)\cdot\exp(-c_{g}(\log X)^{1-\varepsilon}),

by trivially bounding
S(-r/s-\eta/X)\ll X
, and using the bound
\log X/(B\log\log X)\gg_{\varepsilon}(\log X)^{1-\varepsilon}
for
X\to\infty
.

Case 2.
\eta\not=0
:

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant X}r_{2}(n)e\bigg(\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+\frac{-\eta}{X}\bigg)n\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\,({\rm mod}\,s)}e\bigg(\frac{-r\nu}{s}\bigg)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant X\\
n\equiv\nu\,({\rm mod}\,s)\end{subarray}}r_{2}(n)e\bigg(\frac{-\eta n}{X}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}(s)}\sum_{\nu\,({\rm mod}\,s)}e\bigg(\frac{-r\nu}{s}\bigg)\rho(\nu;s)\sum_{n\leqslant X}r_{2}(n)e\bigg(\frac{-\eta n}{X}\bigg)+E(x;s),

where we recall
\rho(\nu;s)

\rho(\nu;s)=\#\{(a,b)\in(\mathbb{Z}/s\mathbb{Z})^{2}:(ab,s)=1,a^{2}+b^{2}\equiv\nu\,({\rm mod}\,s)\},

and

E(X;s)\leqslant s\cdot\max_{\nu\,({\rm mod}\,s)}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant X}r_{2}(n)e\bigg(\frac{-\eta n}{X}\bigg)\bigg(1_{n\equiv\nu\,({\rm mod}\,s)}-\frac{\rho(\nu;s)}{\varphi^{2}(s)}\bigg)\bigg|.

By the Siegel-Walfisz theorem, we know

so, using partial summation we get that

\displaystyle E(X;s)

\displaystyle=s\cdot\bigg(e(-\eta)E(X)-e(-\eta/X)E(1)+\frac{\eta}{X}\cdot\int_{1}^{X}e\bigg(\frac{-\eta t}{X}\bigg)E(t)dt\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll_{A}X/(\log X)^{A},

since

. Hence, bounding the Fourier transform
\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
trivially and summing over

coming from

. By Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant X}r_{2}(n)e\bigg(\frac{-\eta n}{X}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\ll X}\sum_{t<n\leqslant t+X/\lfloor(\log X)^{5B}\rfloor}r_{2}(n)e\bigg(\frac{-\eta n}{X}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\ll X}e\bigg(\frac{-\eta t}{X}\bigg)\sum_{t<n\leqslant t+X/\lfloor(\log X)^{5B}\rfloor}r_{2}(n)+O_{B}\bigg(\frac{X}{(\log X)^{4B}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{X}{(\log X)^{5B}}\sum_{t\ll X}e\bigg(\frac{-\eta t}{X}\bigg)+O_{B}\bigg(\frac{X}{(\log X)^{4B}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{X}{(\log X)^{5B}}\sum_{0\leqslant m<\lfloor(\log X)^{5B}\rfloor}e\bigg(\frac{-\eta m}{\lfloor(\log X)^{5B}\rfloor}\bigg)+O_{B}\bigg(\frac{X}{(\log X)^{4B}}\bigg)\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{4B}},

where we bounded trivially the Fourier transform
\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)\leqslant\#\mathcal{A}(X)
.

Case 3.
\eta=0
and

This will be the main term, and we focus on the subset of

with

\eta=0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leqslant(\log X)^{B}\\
p|s\implies p|g\\
(r,s)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi^{2}(s)}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{\nu\,({\rm mod}\,s)}e\bigg(\frac{-r(\nu-c)}{s}\bigg)\rho(\nu;s)\sum_{n\leqslant X}r_{2}(n)

where

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}|g_{i}^{J}\\
(r_{i},s_{i})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(s_{i})}\sum_{\nu_{i}\,({\rm mod}\,s_{i})}\frac{\rho(\mu_{i}\nu_{i};s_{i})}{\varphi(s_{i})}

\displaystyle e\bigg(\frac{-r_{i}(\mu_{i}\nu_{i}-c)}{s_{i}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}|g_{i}^{J}\\
s_{i}>1\\

\sum_{0\leqslant\ell<g^{J}}e\bigg(\frac{r(c-M\mu)}{s}\bigg)=\begin{cases}g^{J}&\text{if }c\equiv M\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\text{ and }M\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i<J\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Putting this in, we get

\eqref{getting_rid_of_s}=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{1}{Mh^{2}}\frac{1}{g^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
M\mu\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}r(\mu;g^{J})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{A}(X)\\
c\equiv\mu M\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\end{subarray}}1.

Similar to Section
3
, the inner sum over
c
is
(g-1)^{k-J}
. Thus, we get that this is

\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\#\mathcal{A}(X)}{Mh^{2}}\frac{1}{g^{J}(g-1)^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
M\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i<J\end{subarray}}r(\mu;g^{J}).

Thus, the major arc contribution in total gives us, for any
J\ll_{g}(\log X)^{1/2}
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{a}\leqslant X\\
\widetilde{a}/X\in\mathfrak{M}\end{subarray}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{a}}{X}\bigg)\widetilde{S}\bigg(\frac{-\widetilde{a}}{X};v_{1},v_{2}\bigg)=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\#\mathcal{A}(X)}{Mh^{2}}\frac{1}{g^{J}(g-1)^{J}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
M\mu\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}r(\mu;g^{J})

\displaystyle+O\bigg(\frac{\#\mathcal{A}(X)}{Mh^{2}}\exp\bigg(-100c_{g}^{1/2}(\log X)^{1/2}\bigg)\bigg),

since
M\leqslant\sqrt{X}
and summing over
M=a^{2}+b^{2}
would give us that this is the worst possible error term.

4.2  Minor Arcs

In this part, we will get an error term by combining the
a^{2}+b^{2}
and
c^{2}+d^{2}
sums and getting a form of a double quadratic exponential sums.

We start by putting
s
and
\eta
into dyadic intervals. This gives us

\displaystyle\sum_{a^{2}+b^{2}\leqslant\sqrt{X}}\sum_{h\leqslant H}\lambda_{h}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},v_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,h)\\
(av_{1}-bv_{2})(av_{1}+bv_{2})(av_{2}+bv_{1})(av_{2}-bv_{1})\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S<s\leqslant 2S\\
(r,s)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<|\eta|\leqslant 2D\\
Xr/s+\eta\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\eta}{X}\bigg)\widetilde{S}\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+\frac{-\eta}{X};v_{1},v_{2}\bigg).

Then we put
a^{2}+b^{2}
into dyadic intervals and change the order of summations to put it inside by forcing
a
and
b
into arithmetic progressions,
i.e.
we have

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{h\leqslant H}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,h)\\
(u_{1}v_{1}-u_{2}v_{2})(u_{1}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{2})(u_{1}v_{2}+u_{2}v_{1})(u_{1}v_{2}-u_{2}v_{1})\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S<s\leqslant 2S\\
(r,s)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<|\eta|\leqslant 2D\\
Xr/s+\eta\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\eta}{X}\bigg)\sum_{N=2^{i}\ll\sqrt{x}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N<a^{2}+b^{2}\leqslant 2N\\
(a,b)\equiv(u_{1},u_{2})\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}S\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+\frac{-\eta}{X};v_{1},v_{2}\bigg).

Now, we focus on the (
4.4
) part only. We open up
S(\theta;u,v)
, and put
(c,d)
in dyadic intervals
(M_{1},2M_{1}]\times(M_{2},2M_{2}]
for some real numbers
M_{1},M_{2}
with
\#\{M_{1},M_{2}\}\ll(\log X)^{2}
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N<a^{2}+b^{2}\leqslant 2N\\
(a,b)\equiv(u_{1},u_{2})\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}\widetilde{S}\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+

\displaystyle\frac{-\eta}{X};v_{1},v_{2}\bigg)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M_{1},M_{2}\\
M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2}\ll X/N\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N<a^{2}+b^{2}\leqslant 2N\\
(a,b)\equiv(u_{1},u_{2})\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M_{1}<c\leqslant 2M_{1}\\
M_{2}<d\leqslant 2M_{2}\\
X^{3/4}/(a^{2}+b^{2})<c^{2}+d^{2}\leqslant X/(a^{2}+b^{2})\\
(c,d)\equiv(v_{1},v_{2})\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}e\bigg(\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+\frac{-\eta}{X}\bigg)(a^{2}+b^{2})(c^{2}+d^{2})\bigg).

Now, we can put
n=a^{2}+b^{2}
, and this tells us the inner sum is of the form

\sum_{N<n\leqslant 2N}a_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M_{1}<c\leqslant 2M_{1}\\
M_{2}<d\leqslant 2M_{2}\\
X^{3/4}/(a^{2}+b^{2})<c^{2}+d^{2}\leqslant X/(a^{2}+b^{2})\\
(c,d)\equiv(v_{1},v_{2})\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}e\bigg(\bigg(\frac{-r}{s}+\frac{-\eta}{X}\bigg)(c^{2}+d^{2})n\bigg),

which we can bound by Lemma
2.10
with
\delta=1/8
since
c^{2}+d^{2}\gg X^{3/4}/(a^{2}+b^{2})\gg X^{1/4}
. This, alongside Lemma
2.7
, gives us for
D\gg 1
,

(
4.4
)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{h^{1/2}}{X}\#\mathcal{A}(X)(S^{2}D)^{\alpha_{g}}\bigg(X^{31/32}+\frac{X}{(SD)^{1/4}}\bigg)(\log X)^{6}

\displaystyle\ll h^{1/2}\#\mathcal{A}(X)\bigg(\frac{(S^{2}D)^{\alpha_{g}}}{X^{1/32}}+\frac{(S^{2}D)^{\alpha_{g}}}{(SD)^{1/4}}\bigg)(\log X)^{6}

\displaystyle\ll h^{1/2}\#\mathcal{A}(X)(X^{-1/32+\alpha_{g}}+(SD)^{-1/4+2\alpha_{g}})(\log X)^{6},

since
S^{2}D\ll X
and
S^{2}D\leqslant(SD)^{2}
. Next, since
\alpha_{g}\to 0
as
g\to\infty
, we get that
X^{-1/32+\alpha_{g}}\ll X^{-1/100}
and using
SD\gg\exp(\eta_{g}\cdot(\log X)^{1/2})
, we have
(SD)^{-1/4+2\alpha_{g}}\ll\exp(-\frac{\eta_{g}}{5}(\log X)^{1/2})
.

In the case
D\ll 1
, we can use the second bound from Lemma
2.10
. In this case, we get

(
4.4
)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{h^{1/2}}{X}\#\mathcal{A}(X)S^{2\alpha_{g}}\bigg(X^{31/32}+\frac{X}{S^{1/4}}\bigg)(\log X)^{6}

\displaystyle\ll h^{1/2}\#\mathcal{A}(X)(S^{2\alpha_{g}}X^{-1/32}+S^{-1/4+2\alpha_{g}})(\log X)^{6}.

As before, since
\alpha_{g}\to 0
as
g\to\infty
, and
S\gg\exp(\eta_{g}\cdot(\log X)^{1/2})
, we get that this is

\ll h^{1/2}\#\mathcal{A}(X)\exp(-\frac{\eta_{g}}{5}(\log X)^{1/2}),

just like the case
D\gg 1
. Therefore, putting them together, we got

\eqref{minor_arc_part}\ll h^{1/2}\#\mathcal{A}(X)\exp\bigg(-\frac{\eta_{g}}{5}(\log X)^{1/2}\bigg).

Summing subsequently over
u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,h)
and
h\leqslant H
gives a bound of

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{a^{2}+b^{2}\leqslant\sqrt{X}}\sum_{h\leqslant H}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},v_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,h)\\
(av_{1}-bv_{2})(av_{1}+bv_{2})(av_{2}+bv_{1})(av_{2}-bv_{1})\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{a}\leqslant X\\
\widetilde{a}/X\in\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}\widehat{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{a}}{X}\bigg)\widetilde{S}\bigg(\frac{-\widetilde{a}}{X};v_{1},v_{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll\#\mathcal{A}(X)H^{11/2}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\eta_{g}}{5}(\log X)^{1/2}\bigg).

Before going to the next part, we note that this error is worse than the error coming from the major arc contributions.

4.3  Putting Everything Together

In this section, we put together the major arc and the minor arc contributions to get the final bound.

We start by noting that

\rho(h;a,b):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},v_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,h)\\
(av_{1}-bv_{2})(av_{1}+bv_{2})(av_{2}+bv_{1})(av_{2}-bv_{1})\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,h)\end{subarray}}1,

is a multiplicative function by the Chinese Remainder Theorem. Moreover, we have at a prime
p\not=3

\rho(p;a,b)=\begin{cases}p&\text{if }p=2\text{ or }p|ab\\
4p-3&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus, we get by Lemma
2.11
,

\sum_{h\leqslant H}\frac{\lambda_{h}\cdot\rho(h;a,b)}{h^{2}}\ll\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant H\\
p\nmid 3g\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{\rho(p;a,b)}{p^{2}}\bigg)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant H\\
p|ab\\
p\nmid 3g\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p}\bigg)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3<p\leqslant H\\
p\nmid abg\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{4}{p}+\frac{3}{p^{2}}\bigg).

We can bound the product over
p|ab
by
\leqslant 1
. For the product over
p\nmid abg
, we complete the product to get

\sum_{h\leqslant H}\frac{\lambda_{h}\cdot\rho(h;a,b)}{h^{2}}\ll\prod_{3<p\leqslant H}\bigg(1-\frac{4}{p}+\frac{3}{p^{2}}\bigg)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>3\\
p|abg\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{4}{p}+\frac{3}{p^{2}}\bigg)^{-1}.

Then we have that these products are

\ll\prod_{3<p\leqslant H}\bigg(1-\frac{4}{p}\bigg)\exp\bigg(\sum_{p|abg}\frac{4}{p}\bigg).

Now, the exponential term can be shown to be
\ll(\log\log X)^{4}
since
abg\ll_{g}X
by splitting the sum over
p
into ranges
p>\log X
and
p\leqslant\log X
. For the first product, it is easy to see that it is
\ll 1/(\log H)^{4}
. These together give us

(4.6)

\sum_{h\leqslant H}\frac{\lambda_{h}\cdot\rho(h;a,b)}{h^{2}}\ll\frac{(\log\log X)^{4}}{(\log H)^{4}}.

We now can use this to prove our result. We put together the major arc (
4.3
) and the minor arc (
4.5
) contributions together to get

\displaystyle\eqref{main_eq}=\frac{\pi}{4}\#\mathcal{A}(X)\sum_{a^{2}+b^{2}\leqslant\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{a^{2}+b^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
(a^{2}+b^{2})\mu\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\frac{r(\mu;g^{J})}{g^{J}(g-1)^{J}}

\displaystyle\sum_{h\leqslant H}\frac{\lambda_{h}\cdot\rho(h;a,b)}{h^{2}}+O\bigg(\#\mathcal{A}(X)H^{11/2}\exp\bigg(-\frac{c_{g}^{1/2}}{5}(\log X)^{1/2}\bigg)\bigg).

Using (
4.6
), we can bound the sum over
h
. Noting that
H=\exp(\frac{c_{g}^{1/2}}{50000}(\log X)^{1/2})
, and putting
m=a^{2}+b^{2}
we get

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\#\mathcal{A}(X)(\log\log X)^{4}}{(\log X)^{2}}\sum_{m\leqslant\sqrt{X}}\frac{r_{0}(m)}{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
m\mu\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\frac{r(\mu;g^{J})}{g^{J}(g-1)^{J}}

\displaystyle+\#\mathcal{A}(X)\exp\bigg(-\frac{c_{g}^{1/2}}{100}(\log X)^{1/2}\bigg).

Now, we put
m\mu
into arithmetic progressions. To do this, we note that given
f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})
with
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}
such that
a_{i}\not=b
for all
i
, we are looking at
m\mu\equiv f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leqslant\sqrt{X}}\frac{r_{0}(m)}{m}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
m\mu\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\frac{r(\mu;g^{J})}{g^{J}(g-1)^{J}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\sum_{m\leqslant\sqrt{X}}\frac{r_{0}(m)}{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
m\mu\equiv f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\end{subarray}}\frac{r(\mu;g^{J})}{g^{J}(g-1)^{J}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\sum_{v\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
v\mu\equiv f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\end{subarray}}\frac{r(\mu;g^{J})}{g^{J}(g-1)^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant\sqrt{X}\\
m\equiv v\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{0}(m)}{m}.

The inner sum is
\ll\frac{r(v;g^{J})}{g^{2J}}\log X
by partial summation and by suming Lemma
2.3
over the representations of
v
as sum of two squares
\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})
, and since
g^{J}\ll_{\varepsilon}X^{\varepsilon}
. Hence, we get that this is in total

\ll(\log X)\bigg(\frac{g^{J}}{(g-1)^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{g^{4J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
v\mu\equiv f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\end{subarray}}r(\mu;g^{J})r(v;g^{J})\bigg).

Finally, we want to understand the part with
J
. We expect it to converge to a constant as
J\to\infty
. To see this, we will do some local analysis. Firstly, we factorize
g=p_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots p_{\ell}^{e_{\ell}}
. Then, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\frac{g^{J}}{(g-1)^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{g^{4J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,\mu\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
v\mu\equiv f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\end{subarray}}r(\mu;g^{J})r(v;g^{J})

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{J}}{(g-1)^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\bigg(\frac{1}{p_{i}^{4Je_{i}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{i},\mu_{i}\,({\rm mod}\,p_{i}^{Je_{i}})\\
v_{i}\mu_{i}\equiv f\,({\rm mod}\,p_{i}^{Je_{i}})\end{subarray}}r(\mu_{i};p_{i}^{Je_{i}})r(v_{i};p_{i}^{Je_{i}})\bigg).

Now, we use the trivial bound
r(\nu;p^{k})\leqslant 2p^{k}
for
p
prime and
k\geqslant 1
. This gives us

\ll_{g}\frac{g^{J}}{(g-1)^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\bigg(\frac{1}{p_{i}^{2Je_{i}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{i},\mu_{i}\,({\rm mod}\,p_{i}^{Je_{i}})\\
v_{i}\mu_{i}\equiv f\,({\rm mod}\,p_{i}^{Je_{i}})\end{subarray}}1\bigg),

and we can bound the inner sum by
\leqslant p^{Je_{i}}
since it is either
0
or
p^{Je_{i}}
. Therefore, taking the product, we get

\ll_{g}\frac{1}{(g-1)^{J}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,({\rm mod}\,g^{J})\\
f=\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}a_{i}g^{i}\text{ with }a_{i}\not=b,\forall i\end{subarray}}1=1,

since the sum over
f
is
(g-1)^{J}
.
Therefore, we get

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}(X)}(r_{*}^{2}(n)-2r_{*}(n))\ll_{g}\frac{\#\mathcal{A}(X)(\log\log X)^{4}}{\log X},

which proves Theorem
1.4
.

Wellposedness of inviscid SQG in the half-plane
In-Jee Jeong, Junha Kim, Hideyuki Miura

Abstract
. We consider the SQG equation without dissipation on the half-plane with Dirichlet boundary condition, and prove local wellposedness in the spaces W 3 , p W^{3,p} and C 2 , β C^{2,\beta} for any 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty and 0 < β < 1 0<\beta<1 . We complement this wellposedness by showing that for generic C 0 ∞ C^{\infty}_{0} initial data, the unique corresponding solution does not belong to W 3 , ∞ W^{3,\infty} .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Inviscid SQG in the half-plane

We consider the Cauchy problem for the inviscid surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) equation in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2};x_{2}>0\}
:

(SQG)

\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0,

where the velocity is given by

(BS)

\begin{split}u(t,x)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\left[\frac{(x-y)^{\perp}}{|x-y|^{3}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\perp}}{|x-\bar{y}|^{3}}\right]\theta(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

with
\bar{y}:=(y_{1},-y_{2})
for
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
. That is, up to a multiplicative constant that we neglect,
u=R^{\perp}\bar{\theta}
, where
R^{\perp}=(-R_{2},R_{1})
with
R_{i}=\partial_{i}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}
and
\bar{\theta}
is the odd extension of
\theta
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. As we shall explain below, to study strong solutions for (
SQG
) in the half-plane, it is essential to impose the Dirichlet boundary condition

(DBC)

\begin{split}\theta(t,x_{1},0)\equiv 0,\end{split}

which will be assumed throughout the paper. Since the velocity satisfies the slip boundary conditions, the condition (
DBC
) propagates in time.

While dissipative versions of (
SQG
) have been studied quite extensively in domains with boundary (
[
6
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
29
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
), culminating in the global wellposedness result with critical dissipation (
[
7
]
), for the inviscid SQG, the only wellposedness for strong solutions that we are aware of is the one in Constantin–Nguyen
[
8
]
which gives local wellposedness in
H^{1}_{0}\cap W^{2,p}
for any
2<p<\infty
. While the proof in
[
8
]
is given for any planar bounded domain with smooth boundary, it can be easily adapted to the half-plane case. Given this
H^{1}_{0}\cap W^{2,p}
wellposedness result, it is natural to ask whether higher Sobolev or Hölder regularity of the initial data can propagate in time for this
H^{1}_{0}\cap W^{2,p}
solution. More precisely, we investigate what are the smallest Sobolev and Hölder spaces in which (
SQG
) is locally wellposed with (
DBC
). Furthermore, we study the
maximal regularity
of the unique local-in-time strong solution, for a generic initial datum which is compactly supported and
C^{\infty}
up to the boundary.

1.2.
Main Results

Our first result gives local wellposedness of data belonging to either
C^{2,\beta}
(
0<\beta<1
) or
W^{3,p}
(
1<p<\infty
), under the Dirichlet boundary condition. Both of these spaces embed into
W^{2,q}
with some
q>2
, and therefore it is a natural extension of the inviscid wellposedness result of
[
8
]
.

Before stating the result, let us introduce a simplifying notation: if
X
is a Banach space of functions on
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
which embeds in
C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
, we write
X_{0}
to be the subspace of
X
which are compactly supported and vanishes on the boundary of the upper half-plane. (We are using the notation
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
to emphasize that the regularity holds uniformly up to the boundary.)

Theorem A
.

We have local wellposedness for (
SQG
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with (
DBC
) in
C^{2,\beta}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
for any
0<\beta<1
and also in
W^{3,p}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
for any
1<p<\infty
. Namely, for any initial data
\theta_{0}\in C^{2,\beta}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
(
W^{3,p}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
, resp.), there exist
\delta>0
and a unique corresponding solution satisfying
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{2,\beta}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
(
{C}([0,\delta];W^{3,p}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
, resp.).

Remark 1.1
.

A few remarks are in order.

•

As in the
\mathbb{R}^{2}
case,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}dt<\infty\end{split}

controls singularity formation at
t=T
. Furthermore, the uniqueness statement requires just
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
(the proofs in
[
1
,
24
]
for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be easily adapted to the half-plane case).

•

As a direct consequence of this wellposedness result,
C^{\infty}_{0}
initial datum has the corresponding unique solution belonging to to
C^{2,\beta}\cap W^{3,p}
for any
\beta<1
and
p<\infty
during its lifespan.

The next result complements the first main result by showing that there is
nonexistence
of solutions in
W^{3,\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
(and hence also in
C^{3}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
), even if the initial data is infinitely differentiable up to the boundary.

Theorem B
.

There exist initial data in
\theta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
such that the corresponding unique solution to (
SQG
) given in Theorem
A
does not belong to
L^{\infty}([0,\delta];W^{3,\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
for any
\delta>0
. A sufficient condition ensuring this is to have a point
x^{*}\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
such that
\partial_{12}\theta_{0}(x^{*})
and
\partial_{22}\theta_{0}(x^{*})
are both nonzero.

This statement needs to be carefully distinguished with strong illposedness results in
C^{k}
and
W^{k,\infty}
for integer
k
proved in
[
12
,
24
]
for domains without boundary: in that case,
C^{\infty}
initial data lead to
C^{\infty}
local in time solutions, and
C^{k},W^{k,\infty}
illposedness is purely coming from the initial data being exactly
C^{k}
or
W^{k,\infty}
and not better. On the other hand, nonexistence in Theorem
B
is for
C^{\infty}
initial data and is really coming from the boundary of the domain.

Remark 1.2
.

Let us clarify that the Dirichlet boundary condition is essential for local wellposedness of strong solutions, at least when the domain is
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
: it was shown in
[
23
]
that for
any
compactly supported smooth datum
\theta_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
which does not satisfy (
DBC
), there is
nonexistence
of corresponding
L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
solution to (
SQG
) with any
\delta>0
. This is an optimal nonexistence result, since one cannot expect wellposedness of strong solutions strictly below
C^{1}
, this space being scaling critical.

1.3.
Discussion on singularity problem for SQG

Besides extending the results of
[
8
]
, another motivation of the current work is to explore settings in which finite time singularity formation for (
SQG
) can be established. Singularity formation for (
SQG
) is a notoriously difficult open problem, and there does not seem to be convincing numerical evidence towards blowup for smooth data in domains without boundaries. We refer to
[
11
,
10
,
3
,
4
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
for some results, against singularity formation, regarding the SQG equation. The problem is still challenging for the generalized models of the form

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\\
&u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta,\end{aligned}\right.

which are sometimes referred to as the
\alpha
-SQG equation. With this notation,
\alpha=1
corresponds to (
SQG
) and
\alpha=0
to the vorticity formulation for two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations. A larger value of
\alpha
makes the kernel for the velocity more singular. Global regularity of smooth solutions for any
\alpha>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a challenging open problem.

Towards the blowup problem, the half-plane provides a nice stage, the main point being that “patch type” data attached to the boundary cannot be detached in finite time, unless a singularity of some form occurs. Essentially, this is the common setup for all the existing blowup results for (
1.1
):
[
26
,
27
,
15
,
28
]
deal with patch solutions attached to the boundary and the very recent work
[
30
]
concerns solutions in properly weighted Hölder spaces, non-vanishing on the boundary.

Unfortunately, all of these results work in the range
0<\alpha\leq 1/2
while the SQG corresponds to
\alpha=1
: there is a serious difficulty already in finding a local wellposedness class for non-vanishing data on the boundary when
1/2<\alpha\leq 1
(
[
30
,
23
]
). In particular, as we remarked earlier, in the SQG case, there is nonexistence of
C^{1}
solutions corresponding to a datum which is not identically zero on the boundary, even if it is
C^{\infty}
up to the boundary and compactly supported.

Therefore, the wellposedness class that we present in Theorem
A
seems to be the “optimal” half-plane result for SQG and could be a reasonable space to search for finite time singularity formation, the main difference with the
\mathbb{R}^{2}
case being that the second derivative of the velocity has a logarithmically divergent component depending on the second derivative of
\theta
restricted to the boundary. A careful study of the second derivative of
\theta
on the boundary might provide some scenario for finite time blowup, or at least for small scale creation (roughly speaking, a large growth of some Sobolev or Hölder norms of the solution). We remark that for the SQG equation, results on long time large growth are also rare (
[
25
,
13
,
17
]
), since the strongest conservation law
\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}
is not able to control
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

1.4.
Ideas and difficulties of the proof

In this section, we discuss several key points and difficulties involved in the proofs of our main results.

Proof of wellposedness
. To simplify the discussion, let us assume that
\theta
is a given on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and is infinitely differentiable up to the boundary, and denote its odd extension to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
\bar{\theta}
. Since the velocity is defined by
u=R^{\perp}\bar{\theta}
, if
\theta
is non-vanishing on the boundary, then we see that
u_{1}=-R_{2}\bar{\theta}
is not even locally bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Even if we impose the Dirichlet boundary condition for
\theta
,
\partial_{22}\theta
does not need to vanish on the boundary, and therefore
\partial_{22}u_{1}=-R_{2}(\partial_{22}\bar{\theta})
will have a logarithmic divergent term near the boundary. The velocity does not belong to
W^{2,\infty}
, and hence it is natural to expect that the local wellposedness threshold is
W^{2,\infty}
(or
C^{2}
) as well. Indeed,
W^{2,p}
wellposedness for
p<\infty
is given in
[
8
]
and
C^{1,\beta}
wellposedness on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
for any
0<\beta<1
is straightforward, using the odd extension (which again belongs to
C^{1,\beta}
under (
DBC
)) and applying the existing
C^{1,\beta}
wellposedness in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Therefore, the main point in Theorem
A
is that wellposedness holds beyond the
C^{2}
threshold. This relies on the specific structure of the nonlinearity
R^{\perp}(\bar{\theta})\cdot\nabla\theta
, and roughly speaking is based on the fact that
\partial_{1}
is paired only with
\partial_{2}
and not again with
\partial_{1}
in the nonlinear term.

For the
C^{2,\beta}
local wellposedness, we need to investigate Hölder regularity for the second derivatives of the velocity (see Lemma
2.4
). Given
\theta\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
, the only component of
\nabla^{2}u
which is not
C^{\beta}
is
\partial_{22}u_{1}
, for the reason that we explained earlier. To begin with, it is straightforward to “extract” the logarithmically divergent component from
\partial_{22}u_{1}
, namely
4\log(x_{2})\partial_{22}\theta(x)
, which naturally appears when one tries to commute an
x_{2}
derivative with the Riesz transform. However, somewhat surprisingly, the difference
\partial_{22}u_{1}-4\log(x_{2})\partial_{22}\theta(x)
still does not belong to
C^{\beta}
; the reason is that the singular integral operator defined by
\partial_{22}\theta\mapsto\partial_{22}u_{1}-4\log(x_{2})\partial_{22}\theta(x)
has a kernel which does not satisfy the (usual) cancellation condition on the sphere. Still,
\partial_{22}u_{1}-4\log(x_{2})\partial_{22}\theta(x)
is “almost”
C^{\beta}
, up to a logarithmic loss of the modulus of continuity, and this turns out to be sufficient for wellposedness.

The proof for the
W^{3,p}
case is more subtle, since we need to estimate third derivatives of the velocity. Commuting derivatives carefully with Riesz transforms in
L^{p}
shows that the only problematic third order derivative of the velocity is
\partial_{222}u_{1}
, given that
\theta\in W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+})
. Similarly in the Hölder case, we extract the “main term” from
\partial_{222}u_{1}
in
L^{p}
; it turns out that it is given by

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{2x_{2}}{|x-(y_{1},0)|^{3}}\partial_{22}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}.\end{split}

Our main observation, stated in Lemma
2.8
, is that this expression belongs to
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
after multiplying by another factor of
x_{2}
, when
\theta\in W^{3,p}
for
1<p<\infty
. In the nonlinear estimate, we can gain this additional factor of
x_{2}
from the structure of the nonlinearity: in the estimate for
\partial_{t}\partial_{222}\theta
, the term involving
\partial_{222}u_{1}
can be written as

\begin{split}\partial_{222}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta=x_{2}\partial_{222}u_{1}\frac{\partial_{1}\theta}{x_{2}}\end{split}

where
\partial_{1}\theta/x_{2}
is also bounded in
L^{2p}
thanks to (
DBC
) and Hardy’s inequality.

Proof of illposedness
. The idea behind
W^{3,\infty}
illposedness is very simple: since we expect
\partial_{22}u_{1}(x)\sim\log(x_{2})
, the equation for
\partial_{t}\partial_{222}\theta(x)
has a logarithmically divergent term as well. However, it turns out to be very difficult to directly use the evolution equation for the third derivative of
\theta
: essentially, the enemy is the
W^{3,\infty}
illposedness of (
SQG
) in the absence of boundaries. More precisely, while we may assume the existence of a local in time
W^{3,\infty}
solution
\theta
(for the sake of deriving a contradiction), the corresponding velocity field does not belong to
W^{3,\infty}
even in the absence of boundaries, due to the failure of
L^{\infty}
bounds of the Riesz transforms. In other words, for this half-plane illposedness result, one needs to distinguish the unboundedness coming from the physical boundary with that from failure of
L^{\infty}
bounds for the Riesz transforms. To this end, our approach is to carefully study the evolution equation for the difference quotient

\begin{split}\frac{\partial_{22}\theta(t,x_{1},x_{2})-\partial_{22}\theta(t,x_{1},0)}{x_{2}},\end{split}

and show that it diverges like
\log x_{2}
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section

) is given in Section

B

1.6.
Notation

In the proofs, the value of
c,C>0
may change from a line to another, or even within a single line. We denote the dependency of the constant with respect to various parameters using subscripts. Given a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we denote
\overline{f}
and
\widetilde{f}
as its odd and even extensions in the
x_{2}
-variable, respectively:

\begin{split}\overline{f}(\bar{x})=\overline{f}(x_{1},-x_{2})=-f(x_{1},x_{2})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\widetilde{f}(\bar{x})=\widetilde{f}(x_{1},-x_{2})=f(x_{1},x_{2})\end{split}

for
x_{2}>0
, where we are also using the notation
\bar{x}=(x_{1},-x_{2})
. Lastly, throughout the paper we shall assume that the solution
\theta
is compactly supported in space, and use
L>0
for the length scale of the support. We write
B(0;L)
for the ball centered at the origin with radius
L
.

2.
Preliminary Estimates

2.1.
Hölder estimates

Lemma 2.1
.

We have the following estimates for
0<\beta<1
.

(1)

If
f\in C^{\beta}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
then
\|\overline{f}\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C_{\beta}\|f\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}
.

(2)

If
f\in C^{1,\beta}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
then
\|\overline{f}\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C_{\beta}\|f\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}
.

Proof.

To prove (1), we take two points
x,x^{\prime}
which are assumed to satisfy
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and
x^{\prime}\notin\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. The line segment connecting
x
to
x^{\prime}
meets
\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
at a unique point
z
. Then we write

\begin{split}|\overline{f}(x)-\overline{f}(x^{\prime})|&\leq|\overline{f}(x)-\overline{f}(z)|+|\overline{f}(z)-\overline{f}(x^{\prime})|\\
&\leq|f(x)-f(z)|+|f(z)-f(\overline{x^{\prime}})|\leq C(|x-z|^{\beta}+|z-\overline{x^{\prime}}|^{\beta})\leq C(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta})\end{split}

where
\overline{x^{\prime}}
is the reflection point of
x^{\prime}
across the
x_{1}
-axis, and we have used that
f(z)=-f(z)=0
.

To prove (2), we note that the odd extension of
\partial_{1}f
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies
C^{\beta}
estimate thanks to (1). To see it for
\partial_{2}f
, we note that
\partial_{2}\overline{f}
is the even extension of
\partial_{2}f
across
x_{2}
. This implies
\partial_{2}\overline{f}\in C^{\beta}
.

∎

2.2.
Sobolev estimates

Given a measurable function
g
defined on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, recall that
\overline{g}
and
\widetilde{g}
represent their odd and even extensions to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, respectively.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Then,

\begin{split}\partial_{1}\overline{g}=\overline{\partial_{1}g},\qquad\partial_{1}\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{\partial_{1}g},\qquad\partial_{2}\widetilde{g}=\overline{\partial_{2}g}.\end{split}

Here, the LHS is the distributional derivative in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the odd (or even) extension, and the RHS is the odd (or even) extension of the distributional derivative in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Next, if we assume further that
g\equiv 0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, then we have

\begin{split}\partial_{2}\overline{g}=\widetilde{\partial_{2}g}.\end{split}

Remark 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, we have that

•

\widetilde{g}\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, if
g\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})

•

\overline{g}\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, if
g\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})

•

\partial_{22}\overline{g}=\overline{\partial_{22}g}
and
\overline{g}\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, if
g\in W^{2,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
.

Proof.

Let
g\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, hence,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{j}g(x)\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}g(x)\partial_{j}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad j=1,\,2

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. We show that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{1}g}(x)\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{g}(x)\partial_{1}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

holds for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We consider an even function
\sigma(\cdot)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\sigma=1
on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
and
\sigma=0
on
(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)
. Then, we can decompose the left-hand side as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{1}g}(x)\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon})\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{1}g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Note the first integral converges to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
because it is bounded by

(2.2)

\displaystyle 2\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\int_{K_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla g(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(K_{\varepsilon})}|K_{\varepsilon}|^{1-\frac{1}{p}},

where
K_{\varepsilon}=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2};-R<x_{1}<R,\,0<x_{2}<\varepsilon\}
for sufficiently large
R>0
. On the othr hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{1}g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{1}g(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\partial_{1}g(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(\overline{x})\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)
and
(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(\overline{x})
belong to
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. Applying (
2.1
) to each integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{1}g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}g(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\partial_{1}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}g(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\partial_{1}\varphi(\overline{x})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\partial_{1}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\partial_{1}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{g}(x)\partial_{1}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Thus,
\partial_{1}\overline{g}=\overline{\partial_{1}g}
follows from the above restults. One may prove
\partial_{1}\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{\partial_{1}g}
similarly.

Next, we show that
\partial_{2}\widetilde{g}=\overline{\partial_{2}g}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{2}g}(x)\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{g}(x)\partial_{2}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

holds for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Following the previous procedure, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{2}g}(x)\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{2}g}(x)\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon})\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{2}g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

regardless of the choice of
\varepsilon>0
, and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{2}g}(x)\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon})\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
due to (
2.2
). Using (
2.1
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{2}g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}g(x)\partial_{2}[(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)]\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}g(x)\partial_{2}[(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(\overline{x})]\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\partial_{2}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}g(x)\sigma^{\prime}(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon})(\varphi(x)-\varphi(\overline{x}))\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Note from the mean value theorem that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}g(x)\sigma^{\prime}(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon})(\varphi(x)-\varphi(\overline{x}))\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{K_{\varepsilon}}|g(x)|\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\overline{x})|}{2x_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\partial_{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|g\|_{L^{p}(K_{\varepsilon})}|K_{\varepsilon}|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Consequently, we have with the dominated convergence theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{\partial_{2}g}(x)(1-\sigma(\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon}))\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\to-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{g}(x)\partial_{2}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Thus,
\partial_{2}\widetilde{g}=\overline{\partial_{2}g}
holds.

Now, we consider
g\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
is dense in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and
h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{\partial_{2}h}(x)\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{h}(x)\partial_{2}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, it follows
\partial_{2}\overline{g}=\widetilde{\partial_{2}g}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Key estimates on the velocity

In this subsection, we collect several key estimates on the velocity. We present three lemmas, which correspond to Hölder, log-Lipschitz, and Sobolev estimates for the velocity.

Lemma 2.4

(Hölder estimates)
.

Fix some
0<\beta<1
and let
\theta\in C^{2,\beta}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
be supported in
B(0;L)
. Then, the velocity
u=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\theta
satisfies

(2.4)

\begin{split}\|u_{2}\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}+\|\partial_{1}u_{1}\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}\leq C_{L}\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})},\end{split}

(2.5)

\begin{split}\|\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(x_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{L}\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})},\end{split}

and

(2.6)

\bigg|(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(x_{2}))-(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x^{\prime})-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\log(x_{2}^{\prime}))\\
+4(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\log(2|x-x^{\prime}|+\sqrt{4|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{2}^{2}})\bigg|\leq C_{L}\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta}

for all
x
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with
|x_{1}|
,
|x_{1}^{\prime}|<3L
.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) imply
\|\partial_{2}u_{1}\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C_{L}\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}
for any
0<\gamma<1
.

Remark 2.6
.

If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfies
|x_{1}|>2L
, then
\|\partial_{2}u_{1}\|_{W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leq C_{L,k}\|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}
for all
k\geq 0
. If
x
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfy
|x_{1}|<2L<3L<|x_{1}^{\prime}|
, then one have
|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|\geq L
, and

\begin{split}|\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(x_{2})-(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x^{\prime})-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\log(x_{2}^{\prime}))|\leq C_{L}\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|.\end{split}

Proof.

We only provide a proof for
u_{1}
since one can adjust it to
u_{2}
and complete the proof without difficulty. We recall the formula

\begin{split}u_{1}(x)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\left[\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}}-\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}}{|x-\overline{y}|^{3}}\right]\theta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}}\overline{\theta(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

The odd extension of
\theta
satisfies
\overline{\theta}\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
\partial_{1}\overline{\theta}\in C^{1,\beta}
, and
\partial_{2}\overline{\theta}\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Note that
\partial_{2}^{2}\overline{\theta}
could be discontinuous at the boundary when
\lim_{y_{2}\to 0^{+}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)\neq 0
for some
y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, we have
\partial_{1}u_{1}\in C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
since for any
f\in C^{1,\beta}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

\begin{split}\partial_{i}\left(P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(x-y)^{\perp}}{|x-y|^{3}}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(x-y)^{\perp}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{i}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\in C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\end{split}

holds. To estimate
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}
, we note

\begin{split}\partial_{2}u_{1}(x)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{2}\overline{\theta(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\gamma<1.\end{split}

We split the integral as

\begin{gathered}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{2}\theta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}}{|x-\overline{y}|^{3}}\partial_{2}\theta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{gathered}

Using integration by parts with

\begin{split}\partial_{y_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)=\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}},\qquad\partial_{y_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|x-\overline{y}|}\right)=-\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}}{|x-\overline{y}|^{3}},\end{split}

we arrive at

\begin{split}-P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\frac{1}{|x-\overline{y}|}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\left[\frac{1}{|x-y|}-\frac{1}{|x-\overline{y}|}\right]\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

where the boundary terms cancel each other out. We first split
\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)
as
\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)+(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0))
, and note that the corresponding integral for
\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)
belongs to
C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
simply because the odd extension of
\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)
belongs to
C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. After using

\begin{split}\partial_{\tau}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(\tau-\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta=f(\tau)-f(\tau-\delta),\qquad f\in C(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\tau,\,\delta\in\mathbb{R},\end{split}

it only remains to consider the part

(2.7)

\begin{split}&-\partial_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\int_{y_{2}\geq 0}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\right]\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}+\partial_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\int_{y_{2}\geq 0}\frac{1}{|x-\overline{y}|}\,\mathrm{d}y_{2}\right]\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\\
&\qquad=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}.\end{split}

We prove (
2.5
) first. If
|x|\leq 3L
, the domain of
y_{1}
integral can be restricted to
|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 4L
. (Estimating the integral is simpler if
|x|>3L
.) We further decompose the last integral as

\begin{split}-2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 4L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\left((\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x))+\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\end{split}

The contribution to the integral from
(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x))
is uniformly bounded. We note that

(2.8)

\begin{split}\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 4L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}+2\log(x_{2})=2\log(4L+\sqrt{16L^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}),\end{split}

thus, (
2.5
) follows. To prove (
2.6
), fix
x
and
x^{\prime}
with
|x_{1}|
,
|x_{1}^{\prime}|\leq 3L
. Thanks to (
2.7
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\bigg|-2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}+2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}

\displaystyle-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(x_{2})+4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\log(x_{2}^{\prime})+4(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\log(2|x-x^{\prime}|+\sqrt{4|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{2}^{2}})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta}.

For this, we let

\displaystyle-2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}+2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

where

\begin{split}J_{1}:=&-2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 2|x-x^{\prime}|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\\
&+2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 2|x-x^{\prime}|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1},\end{split}

\begin{split}J_{2}:=&-2\int_{2|x-x^{\prime}|\leq|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\\
&+2\int_{2|x-x^{\prime}|\leq|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1},\end{split}

J_{3}:=-2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}+2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1},

and prove

(2.9)

|J_{1}|\leq C\|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta},

(2.10)

|J_{2}+4(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\log(2|x-x^{\prime}|+\sqrt{4|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{2}^{2}})|\leq C\|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta},

(2.11)

|J_{3}-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(x_{2})+4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\log(x_{2}^{\prime})|\leq C\|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta}.

Since (
2.9
) is clear, we omit the proof for it.
J_{2}
corresponds to

\begin{gathered}-2\int_{2|x-x^{\prime}|\leq|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\right)(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\\
+2\int_{2|x-x^{\prime}|\leq|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}.\end{gathered}

Note from

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\leq\frac{|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|+|x_{2}-x_{2}^{\prime}|}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\end{gathered}

that

\begin{gathered}\left|-2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\right)(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y_{1},0)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\right|\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta}.\end{gathered}

On the other hand, we can directly compute

\begin{gathered}2\int_{2|x-x^{\prime}|\leq|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\\
=4(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime}))\left(\log(10L+\sqrt{100L^{2}+x_{2}^{2}})-\log(2|x-x^{\prime}|+\sqrt{4|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{2}^{2}})\right).\end{gathered}

Thus, (
2.10
) follows. To estimate
J_{3}
, we see from the first integral of
J_{3}
that

(2.12)

-2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(x_{2})=-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(10L+\sqrt{100L^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}).

The second integral of
J_{3}
is equal to

\begin{gathered}2\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}-2\int_{|x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\\
=2\int_{10L+x_{1}^{\prime}}^{10L+x_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}+2\int_{-10L+x_{1}}^{-10L+x_{1}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1},\end{gathered}

where

2\int_{|x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1}|\leq 10L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1})^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}}}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}+4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\log(x_{2}^{\prime})=4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\log(10L+\sqrt{100L^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}})

holds by (
2.12
) and
the right-hand side is bounded by
C\|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|^{\beta}
. Combining these results with

\left|-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log(10L+\sqrt{100L^{2}+x_{2}^{2}})+4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x^{\prime})\log(10L+\sqrt{100L^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2}})\right|\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta},

we deduce (
2.11
). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7

(Log-Lipschitz estimate)
.

Let
\theta\in C^{2}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
be supported in
B(0;L)
and
\nabla^{2}\theta\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. Then, the corresponding velocity satisfies

(2.13)

\begin{split}|\nabla\partial_{1}u(x)-\nabla\partial_{1}u(x^{\prime})|\leq C_{L}\|\nabla\partial_{1}\theta\|_{Lip}|x-x^{\prime}|\log\left(10+\frac{1}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right).\end{split}

for all
x
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with
|x_{1}|
,
|x_{1}^{\prime}|<3L
.

Proof.

We recall that

\begin{split}\partial_{1}^{2}u=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(x-y)^{\perp}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{1}^{2}\overline{\theta(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\partial_{12}u=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(x-y)^{\perp}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{12}\overline{\theta(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y\end{split}

where
\overline{\theta}
is the odd extension of
\theta
in
x_{2}
such that
\partial_{1}^{2}\overline{\theta}
,
\partial_{12}\overline{\theta}\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Therefore, one can prove in the standard way that (
2.13
) holds.
∎

Lemma 2.8

(Sobolev regularity of the velocity)
.

Let
\theta\in W^{3,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for some
1<p<\infty
. Then, the corresponding velocity satisfies

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}\partial_{1}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

and

(2.15)

\begin{split}\|\partial_{222}u_{1}-2H[\theta]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\partial_{222}\theta\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},\end{split}

where
H[\theta]
is defined by

(2.16)

\displaystyle H[\theta](x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x-(y_{1},0)|^{3}}\partial_{22}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}.

Furthermore, it holds that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\|x_{2}H[\theta]\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\partial_{22}\theta\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

Proof.

We fix some
1<p<\infty
and take
\theta\in W^{3,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. To begin with, from Sobolev embedding we have
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{W^{1,2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
and let us first show that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{W^{1,2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{2,2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

The claimed bound for
\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u
in (
2.14
) then follows from applying the embedding
W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow W^{2,2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
to
\theta
. We shall only estimate
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, since
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
is only simpler. Using Lemma
2.2
and Remark
2.3
with that
\overline{\theta}(x_{1},0)=0
for all
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{11}\overline{\theta}=\overline{\partial_{11}\theta},\qquad\partial_{12}\overline{\theta}=\widetilde{\partial_{12}\theta}

which belongs to
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\partial_{1}u\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|R^{\perp}[\nabla\partial_{1}\overline{\theta}]\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C(\|\overline{\partial_{11}\theta}\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\widetilde{\partial_{12}\theta}\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})\leq C\|\nabla\partial_{1}\theta\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

To estimate
\partial_{22}u_{1}
, we recall from Remark
2.3
that
\partial_{22}\overline{\theta}=\overline{\partial_{22}\theta}\in L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
and then
\partial_{22}u_{1}=-R_{2}[\overline{\partial_{22}\theta}]
. Using the
L^{2p}
boundness of
R_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we get
\|\partial_{22}u_{1}\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\partial_{22}\theta\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
, which gives (
2.18
).

We now estimate the third derivatives of
u
. Since
\theta\in W^{3,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and Remark
2.3
implies

\begin{split}\partial_{1}^{3}\overline{\theta}=\overline{\partial_{1}^{3}\theta}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\partial_{112}\overline{\theta}=\widetilde{\partial_{112}\theta}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\partial_{122}\overline{\theta}=\overline{\partial_{122}\theta}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\end{split}

we have

\begin{split}\|\nabla^{2}\partial_{1}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}\partial_{1}\theta\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.\end{split}

This finishes the proof of (
2.14
).

It only remains to estimate
\partial_{222}u_{1}
: to begin with, we decompose
\partial_{22}u_{1}
as follows:

(2.19)

\displaystyle\partial_{22}u_{1}

\displaystyle=-R_{2}[\overline{\partial_{22}\theta}]=-R_{2}[\widetilde{\partial_{22}\theta}]-2R_{2}[\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}}\overline{\partial_{22}\theta}]

\displaystyle=-R_{2}[\widetilde{\partial_{22}\theta}]+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{22}\theta(\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}y.

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{22}\theta(\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\partial_{222}\theta(\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}y-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|x-(y_{1},0)|}\partial_{22}\theta(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1},

and taking the distributional derivative in
x_{2}
to (
2.19
) gives that

(2.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{222}u_{1}

\displaystyle=-R_{2}[\overline{\partial_{222}\theta}]-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}}\frac{x_{2}-y_{2}}{|x-y|^{3}}\partial_{222}\theta(\bar{y})\,\mathrm{d}y+2H[\theta]

\displaystyle=-R_{2}[\overline{\partial_{222}\theta}]+2R_{2}[\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}}\overline{\partial_{222}\theta}]+2H[\theta]

\displaystyle=-R_{2}[\widetilde{\partial_{222}\theta}]+2H[\theta],

where
H[\theta]
is defined by (
2.16
). Hence, we have

\|\partial_{222}u_{1}-2H[\theta]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|R_{2}\widetilde{\partial_{222}\theta}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\partial_{222}\theta\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

We now estimate
\|x_{2}H[\theta]\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
. For simplicity, we introduce

\begin{split}F(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}):=\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{|x-(y_{1},0)|^{3}}|\partial_{22}\theta(y_{1},0)|\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}\|x_{2}H[\theta]\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\left\|\|F\|_{L^{1}_{y_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2p}_{x_{2},x_{1}}}\leq\left\|\|\|F\|_{L^{2p}_{x_{2}}}\|_{L^{1}_{y_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2p}_{x_{1}}}\end{split}

by Minkowski’s inequality. We can explicitly compute
\|F\|_{L^{2p}_{x_{2}}}
, by a change of variables:

\begin{split}\|F\|_{L^{2p}_{x_{2}}}=\frac{c_{0}}{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{1-\frac{1}{2p}}}|\partial_{22}\theta(y_{1},0)|\end{split}

for some absolute constant
c_{0}
. Therefore, by the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev lemma and the trace inequality,

\displaystyle\|x_{2}H[\theta]\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{1-\frac{1}{2p}}}|\partial_{22}\theta(y_{1},0)|\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\right\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{22}\theta(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|\partial_{22}\theta\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

from which (
2.17
) follows. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
High regularity wellposedness

3.1.
Preliminary remarks

We begin with some remarks on local wellposedness of (
SQG
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. For initial data
\theta_{0}
belonging to either
C^{2,\beta}_{0}
or
W^{3,p}_{0}
, we already have local wellposedness (existence and uniqueness) in the space
C^{1,\beta}_{0}
or
W^{2,2p}_{0}
, respectively for any
0<\beta<1
and
1<p<\infty
. That is, there exist
T>0
and
L>0
depending on
\theta_{0}
such that we have the unique corresponding solution
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{1,\beta})
supported in the ball
B(0;L)
. Therefore, for local wellposedness in
C^{2,\beta}
, it suffices to show that this unique solution
\theta
further belongs to
C^{2,\beta}
. A similar remark holds for the case of
W^{3,p}
as well.

3.2.
Propagation of
C^{2,\beta}

In this section, we prove local wellposedness of (
SQG
) in
C^{2,\beta}_{0}
for any
0<\beta<1
.

3.2.1.
A priori estimate

We fix
0<\beta<1
and let
\theta
be a
L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{2,\beta}_{0})
–solution to (
SQG
) for some
T>0
satisfying
{\mathrm{supp}}\,\theta(t)\subset B(0;L)
for some
L>0
. We can apply Lemma
2.4
and have (
2.4
), in particular,
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Thus, a flow-map
\Phi
that is a solution to the ODE for any
x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
:

(3.1)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi(t,x)=u(t,\Phi(t,x)),\qquad\Phi(0,x)=x\end{split}

is well-defined on
[0,T]
.

Estimates of
\|\theta\|_{C^{1}}
.
Differentiating both sides of (
SQG
), we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\partial_{j}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\partial_{j}\theta+\partial_{j}u\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\qquad j=1,2.\end{split}

Applying the flow-map with any given
x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\cap B(0;L)
shows, with
D_{t}=\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}D_{t}|\partial_{j}\theta|^{2}+(\partial_{j}u\cdot\nabla\theta)\partial_{j}\theta=0,\end{split}

which implies that

\begin{split}D_{t}|\nabla\theta|^{2}\leq 2|\nabla u||\nabla\theta|^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}.\end{split}

Estimates of
\|\theta\|_{C^{2}}
.
We estimate the second derivatives of
\theta
separately according to direction due to lack of regularity of
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}
. We can see

(3.3)

\begin{split}D_{t}(\partial_{1}^{2}\theta)=-2\partial_{1}u_{1}\partial_{1}^{2}\theta-2\partial_{1}u_{2}\partial_{21}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta,\end{split}

(3.4)

\begin{split}D_{t}(\partial_{12}\theta)=-2\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}^{2}\theta-2\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-\partial_{12}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{12}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta,\end{split}

and

(3.5)

\begin{split}D_{t}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta)=-2\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{21}\theta-2\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta.\end{split}

From each equation, one can similarly show by the use of (
2.4
) and Remark
2.5
that

\begin{split}D_{t}|\partial_{1}^{2}\theta|^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3},\qquad D_{t}|\partial_{12}\theta|^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}D_{t}|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta|^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}+|\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta||\partial_{2}^{2}\theta|,\end{split}

respectively. Since the boundary condition implies
\partial_{1}\theta(x_{1},0)=0
, it follows
|\partial_{1}\theta(x)|\leq x_{2}\|\theta\|_{C^{2}}
and

\begin{split}|\partial_{1}\theta(x)|\leq 2\frac{x_{2}}{1+x_{2}}\|\theta\|_{C^{2}},\qquad x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}.\end{split}

Combining with (
2.6
), we have
|\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta|\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}
and

\begin{split}D_{t}|\partial_{1}^{2}\theta|^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}.\end{split}

Estimates of
\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}
.
Take
y\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\cap B(0;L)
with
x\neq y
. Note that since we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}\exp{\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)}{\leq}\frac{|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|}{|x-y|}{\leq}\exp{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)},\end{split}

|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\sim|x-y|
holds on any closed interval. For simplicity, let
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta(t,\Phi(t,y))=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\prime}
and
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u(t,\Phi(t,y))=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u^{\prime}
. Then, it follows from (
3.3
) that

\begin{gathered}D_{t}(\partial_{1}^{2}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}\theta^{\prime})=-2(\partial_{1}u_{1}-\partial_{1}u_{1}^{\prime})\partial_{1}^{2}\theta-2\partial_{1}u_{1}^{\prime}(\partial_{1}^{2}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}\theta^{\prime})-2(\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{1}u_{2}^{\prime})\partial_{21}\theta-2\partial_{1}u_{2}^{\prime}(\partial_{21}\theta-\partial_{21}\theta^{\prime})\\
-(\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}-\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}^{\prime})\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}^{\prime}(\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{1}\theta^{\prime})-(\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}-\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}^{\prime})\partial_{2}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}^{\prime}(\partial_{2}\theta-\partial_{2}\theta^{\prime}).\end{gathered}

Multiplying and dividing both sides by
\partial_{1}^{2}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}\theta^{\prime}
and
|x-y|^{2\beta}
respectively, we can have by (
2.4
)

\begin{gathered}D_{t}\left(\frac{|\partial_{1}^{2}\theta-\partial_{1}^{2}\theta^{\prime}|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\right)^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3},\end{gathered}

and similarly, it follows from (
3.4
) that

\begin{split}D_{t}\left(\frac{|\partial_{12}\theta-\partial_{12}\theta^{\prime}|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\right)^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}.\end{split}

To obtain
\|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}
estimate, we see

\begin{gathered}D_{t}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta^{\prime})=-2(\partial_{2}u_{1}-\partial_{2}u_{1}^{\prime})\partial_{12}\theta-2\partial_{2}u_{1}^{\prime}(\partial_{12}\theta-\partial_{12}\theta^{\prime})-2(\partial_{2}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{2}^{\prime})\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-2\partial_{2}u_{2}^{\prime}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta^{\prime})\\
-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta+\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{1}\theta^{\prime}-(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}^{\prime})\partial_{2}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}^{\prime}(\partial_{2}\theta-\partial_{2}\theta^{\prime}).\end{gathered}

By (
2.4
) and

-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta+\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{1}\theta^{\prime}=(-x_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)+y_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y))\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}-y_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)(\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}-\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(y)}{y_{2}}),

we arrive at

\begin{gathered}D_{t}\left(\frac{|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta^{\prime}|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\right)^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}+\frac{|x_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-y_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\left|\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}\right|\frac{|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\\
+|y_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)|\frac{|\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}-\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(y)}{y_{2}}|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\frac{|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}.\end{gathered}

To control the last two terms on the right-hand side, we observe

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{12}\theta(x_{1},x_{2}\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

and note

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}\right|\leq\|\theta\|_{C^{2}},\qquad\frac{|\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}-\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(y)}{y_{2}}|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\partial_{12}\theta(x_{1},x_{2}\tau)-\partial_{12}\theta(y_{1},y_{2}\tau)|}{|(x_{1},x_{2}\tau)-(y_{1},y_{2}\tau)|^{\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}.

With (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle|y_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)|\frac{|\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(x)}{x_{2}}-\frac{\partial_{1}\theta(y)}{y_{2}}|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\frac{|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\leq C(|4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)y_{2}\log y_{2}|+\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}})\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{2}\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}.

For the remainder term, we assume
x_{2}\leq y_{2}
without loss of generality and consider

\displaystyle\frac{|x_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-y_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\leq\frac{|x_{2}(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y))|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}+\frac{|(x_{2}-y_{2})\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}.

Note from (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle\frac{|(x_{2}-y_{2})\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\leq 4(y_{2}-x_{2})^{1-\beta}|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)\log y_{2}|+C(y_{2}-x_{2})^{1-\beta}\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}.

Since (
2.6
) implies

\begin{gathered}|x_{2}(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y))|\leq|x_{2}(4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)\log x_{2}-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)\log y_{2})|\\
+\left|4x_{2}(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y))\log(2|x-y|+\sqrt{4|x-y|^{2}+x_{2}^{2}})\right|+C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}|x-y|^{\beta}\\
\leq|(4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-4\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y))x_{2}\log x_{2}|+|4\partial_{2}\theta(y)x_{2}(\log x_{2}-\log y_{2})|+C|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)|+C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}|x-y|^{\beta},\end{gathered}

it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{|x_{2}(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y))|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|4\partial_{2}\theta(y)x_{2}(\log x_{2}-\log y_{2})|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}+C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{2}}\frac{x_{2}|\log x_{2}-\log y_{2}|}{|x-y|}+C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}.

We used the mean value theorem with
x_{2}\leq y_{2}
in the last inequality. From these estimates, we have

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{C^{2}}\frac{|x_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(x)-y_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\frac{|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(x)-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\leq C\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3},

and obtain

\begin{split}D_{t}\left(\frac{|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta^{\prime}|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\right)^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}.\end{split}

Collecting all the estimates and taking the supremum in
x,y
, we obtain

(3.7)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{2}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{3}.\end{split}

We may take
T>0
smaller in a way that

(3.8)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\theta(t)\|_{C^{2,\beta}}\leq 2\|\theta_{0}\|_{C^{2,\beta}}.\end{split}

3.2.2.
Existence

The existence of a
L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{2,\beta})
solution can be proved by the following iteration scheme: we set
\theta^{(0)}\equiv\theta_{0}
on
[0,T]
and given
n\geq 0
and
\theta^{(n)}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{2,\beta})
supported in
B(0;L)
, we define
\theta^{(n+1)}
as the solution of the transport equation

(3.9)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\theta^{(n+1)}+u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla\theta^{(n+1)}=0\end{split}

on
t\in[0,T]
with initial data
\theta_{0}
. Here,
u^{(n)}
is the velocity corresponding to
\theta^{(n)}
, and therefore satisfies the estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) from Lemma
2.4
. In particular, for
x_{2}>0
, the velocity is
C^{2}
and the equations for the second derivatives for
\theta^{(n+1)}
are valid in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
: (
3.3
), (
3.4
), (
3.5
) hold for
\theta
and
u
replaced by
\theta^{(n+1)}
and
u^{(n)}
, respectively. Rewriting
\partial_{22}u_{1}^{(n)}\partial_{1}\theta^{(n+1)}
by
x_{2}\partial_{22}u_{1}^{(n)}x_{2}^{-1}\partial_{1}\theta^{(n+1)}
and proceeding as in the proof of
C^{2}
a priori estimate, we obtain that
\theta^{(n+1)}
is
C^{2}
up to the boundary:
\theta^{(n+1)}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
. Therefore,
\nabla^{2}\theta^{(n+1)}
is defined pointwise in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
and we can follow the above proof of a priori estimate in
C^{2,\beta}
and derive

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta^{(n+1)}\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{2}\lesssim\|\theta^{(n)}\|_{C^{2,\beta}}\|\theta^{(n+1)}\|_{C^{2,\beta}}^{2}.\end{split}

with the same implicit constant as in (
3.7
) (in particular, independent of
n
). Then, recalling (
3.8
), we obtain

\begin{split}\sup_{n\geq 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\theta^{(n)}(t)\|_{C^{2,\beta}}\leq 2\|\theta_{0}\|_{C^{2,\beta}}.\end{split}

Using this uniform bound, taking
T>0
smaller if necessary, one can show that
\left\{\theta^{(n)}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T];L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
. Using the uniform
C^{2,\beta}
bound again, we have that
\left\{\theta^{(n)},u^{(n)}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
is Cauchy in
C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
. Taking the limit
n\to\infty
in (
3.9
), we see that
\theta^{(n)}\to\theta
in
C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
, where
\theta
is the unique
C^{1,\beta}
solution corresponding to the initial data
\theta_{0}
(remarked in Subsection
3.1
). The convergence of
\theta^{(n)}\to\theta
can be upgraded to
L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{2,\gamma})
with any
0<\gamma<\beta
. Then, from the pointwise convergence of second derivatives in space, we see that
\theta
inherits the uniform
C^{2,\beta}
bound. This gives that
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];C^{2,\beta})
with
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\theta(t)\|_{C^{2,\beta}}\leq 2\|\theta_{0}\|_{C^{2,\beta}}.

3.3.
Propagation of
W^{3,p}_{0}

In this section, we prove local wellposedness of (
SQG
) in
W^{3,p}_{0}
for any
1<p<\infty
. In this section, we let
C>0
depend on
p
as well.

3.3.1.
A priori estimate

We fix
1<p<\infty
and let
\theta
be a
W^{3,p}_{0}
-solution to (
SQG
) for some
T>0
. Then, we can use the flow-map
\Phi(t,x)
satisfying (
3.1
) for all
x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
and Lemma
2.8
on the interval
[0,T]
. For convenience, we recall the estimates from Lemma
2.8
here:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}\partial_{1}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\partial_{222}u_{1}-2H[\theta]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},

where
H[\theta]
satisfies

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|x_{2}H[\theta]\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}.

We begin by recalling that (
SQG
) conserve the
L^{p}
-norm for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
:
\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{p}}.

Estimates of
\|\theta\|_{W^{2,p}}
.
For
1\leq i,\,j\leq 2
, we have

\frac{1}{p}D_{t}|\partial_{ij}\theta|^{p}+(\partial_{i}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{j}\theta+\partial_{j}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{i}\theta+\partial_{ij}u\cdot\nabla\theta)|\partial_{ij}\theta|^{p-2}\partial_{ij}\theta=0.

Using (
2.14
) with the continuous embedding
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, we can see

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2p}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2p}}\|\nabla^{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\right)\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{p+1}.

Estimates of
\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}
.
Among the third order derivatives of
\theta
, we consider the most difficult term, namely
\partial_{2}^{3}\theta
:

\frac{1}{p}D_{t}|\partial_{2}^{3}\theta|^{p}+(\partial_{2}^{3}u\cdot\nabla\theta+3\partial_{2}^{2}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{2}\theta+3\partial_{2}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{2}^{2}\theta)|\partial_{2}^{3}\theta|^{p-2}\partial_{2}^{3}\theta=0.

Integrating in space, we obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{2}^{3}\theta\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq C\|\partial_{2}^{3}\theta\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\left(\|\partial_{2}^{3}u\cdot\nabla\theta\|_{L^{p}}+\|\partial_{2}^{2}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}+\|\partial_{2}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}\right).\end{split}

The last two terms are straightforward to bound, just using (
3.10
):

\begin{split}\|\partial_{2}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\partial_{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\partial_{2}^{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\|\partial_{2}^{2}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\partial_{2}^{2}u\|_{L^{2p}}\|\nabla\partial_{2}\theta\|_{L^{2p}}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{2}.\end{split}

Then, to treat the remaining term, we rewrite it as

\begin{split}\partial_{2}^{3}u\cdot\nabla\theta=2x_{2}H[\theta]\frac{\partial_{1}\theta}{x_{2}}+(\partial_{2}^{3}u_{1}-2H[\theta])\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}\partial_{1}u_{1}\partial_{2}\theta.\end{split}

Again, the last two terms can be bounded simply using (
3.10
):

\begin{split}\|(\partial_{2}^{3}u_{1}-2H[\theta])\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}\partial_{1}u_{1}\partial_{2}\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{2}.\end{split}

Finally, we can bound using (
3.11
) and Hardy’s inequality that

\begin{split}\left\|x_{2}H[\theta]\frac{\partial_{1}\theta}{x_{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\|x_{2}H[\theta]\right\|_{L^{2p}}\left\|\frac{\partial_{1}\theta}{x_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2p}}\leq C\left\|x_{2}H[\theta]\right\|_{L^{2p}}\left\|{\partial_{12}\theta}\right\|_{L^{2p}}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{2}.\end{split}

This shows that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{2}^{3}\theta\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{p+1}.\end{split}

Estimating other third order derivatives of
\theta
(which then involves at least one factor of
\partial_{1}
) is strictly easier, and we omit the proof. Then, collecting the estimates, we can conclude

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{p}\leq C\|\theta\|_{W^{3,p}}^{p+1}.\end{split}

3.3.2.
Existence

Given the a priori estimates, we sketch the existence of a
W^{3,p}
solution. While we expect that there are several ways of doing this (for instance, one may adapt the scheme introduced in
[
9
]
), we present a method which uses a mollified velocity. Namely, given an initial datum
\theta_{0}\in W^{3,p}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
and some
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the transport equation

(3.12)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\theta+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\qquad\theta(t=0)=\theta_{0}.\end{split}

where we define

(3.13)

\begin{split}u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)&=\nabla^{\perp}_{x}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{(|x-y|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{1/2}}\overline{\theta}(t,y)dy\right]\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\left[\frac{(x-y)^{\perp}}{(|x-y|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{3/2}}-\frac{(x-\bar{y})^{\perp}}{(|x-\bar{y}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{3/2}}\right]\theta(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

For compactly supported
\theta\in L^{\infty}
,
u^{\varepsilon}
is a smooth divergence-free vector field for each
\varepsilon>0
.

Using that
u^{\varepsilon}
is smooth (whenever
\theta
is bounded and compactly supported), it can be shown that (
3.12
) has a unique solution for all times, which belongs to
L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{3,p})
for any
T>0
. Let us denote this unique solution by
\theta^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)
. (One may also mollify the initial data as well, which results in a
C^{\infty}
solution for each
\varepsilon>0
.)

Since
\theta^{(\varepsilon)}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{3,p})
, we can justify the
W^{3,p}
a priori estimate given in the previous section. Here, the point is that, by repeating the proof of Lemma
2.8
for
u^{\varepsilon}
instead of
u
, we can get all the same inequalities from Lemma
2.8
for
u^{\varepsilon}
with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
, where
H[\theta]
is now replaced by

\begin{split}H^{\varepsilon}[\theta^{(\varepsilon)}](x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{x_{2}}{(|x-(y_{1},0)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{3/2}}\partial_{22}\theta^{(\varepsilon)}(y_{1},0)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}.\end{split}

That is, the estimates

\begin{split}\|\partial_{222}u_{1}^{\varepsilon}-2H^{\varepsilon}[\theta^{(\varepsilon)}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\partial_{222}\theta^{(\varepsilon)}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})},\qquad\|x_{2}H^{\varepsilon}[\theta^{(\varepsilon)}]\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\partial_{22}\theta^{(\varepsilon)}\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\end{split}

hold with some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, we can find
T>0
, depending only on
\|\theta_{0}\|_{W^{3,p}}
, such that the sequence of solutions
\{\theta^{(\varepsilon)}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is bounded uniformly in the space
L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{3,p})
. This implies that the sequence of time derivatives
\{\partial_{t}\theta^{(\varepsilon)}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{2,p})
. Therefore, by appealing to the Aubin–Lions lemma, we can extract a subsequence which is strongly (in
C_{t}W^{2,p}
) convergent to some
\theta
belonging to
L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{3,p})
and satisfying
\theta(t=0)=\theta_{0}
. Strong convergence in
W^{2,p}
shows that
\theta
is a solution to (
SQG
).

It only remains to prove that the solution
\theta
actually belongs to
C([0,T];W^{3,p})
, and for this we may apply the argument in Bedrossian–Vicol
[
2
, pp. 44-45]
. First, the same argument shows existence of the solution
\theta\in L^{\infty}([-T,T];W^{3,p})
, and by time translation symmetry of (
SQG
), it suffices to prove continuity of the solution at
t=0
. Furthermore, from time reversal symmetry, we can just check the right continuity at
t=0
. On
(-T,T)
, using that
\partial_{t}\theta=-u\cdot\nabla\theta
, we have that
\partial_{t}\theta\in L^{\infty}(-T,T;C^{\gamma})
for some
\gamma>0
. This implies that
\theta
is weakly continuous in time with values in
W^{3,p}
; namely, for any test function
\varphi\in(W^{3,p})^{*}
, the duality pairing
\langle\theta,\varphi\rangle
is a continuous function of
t\in(-T,T)
. This implies in particular that the norm may only drop at
t=0
;

\begin{split}\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,p}}\geq\|\theta_{0}\|_{W^{3,p}}.\end{split}

Finally, the opposite inequality

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,p}}\leq\|\theta_{0}\|_{W^{3,p}}\end{split}

follows from the a priori estimate for the approximating sequence
\theta^{(\varepsilon)}
: uniformly in
\varepsilon
, we have

\begin{split}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,p}}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|\theta^{(\varepsilon)}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,p}}\leq(1+O(t))\|\theta_{0}\|_{W^{3,p}}.\end{split}

This finishes the proof of existence.

4.
High regularity illposedness

Proof of Theorem
B
.

We take initial data
\theta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
supported in
B(0;1)
satisfying

\begin{split}\theta_{0}(x)=Ax_{1}x_{2}+Bx_{2}^{2},\quad x\in{B(0;r_{0})}\end{split}

for some
1/2>r_{0}>0
. (As we shall see, the proof is easily adapted to the more general case when we have
\partial_{12}\theta_{0}(0)
and
\partial_{22}\theta_{0}(0)
are both nonzero.) There exist
T>0
and a unique solution
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T);C^{2,\beta}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
(for any
\beta<1
) with
\theta(t=0)=\theta_{0}
. Towards a contradiction, we assume that there is some
0<\delta\leq T
such that
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{3}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}))
, and set

(4.1)

\begin{split}M=\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,\infty}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, we shall simply bound by
C
the quantities that depend only on the
C^{2,\beta}
-norm of
\theta
.

Now, we take
0<|x_{1}|\leq r_{0}/10
and set
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
y=(x_{1},0)
where
|x_{1}|\leq|x_{2}|
. We shall consider the derivatives of
\theta
along the trajectories of
x
and
y
, which are denoted by
\Phi(t,x)
and
\Phi(t,y)
, respectively.

Estimates of
\Phi
and
\nabla\theta
.
Before we proceed, let us first obtain a few simple estimates on
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
. To begin with, we note that

\partial_{1}u_{2}(x_{1},0)=0=\partial_{2}u_{1}(x_{1},0).

They follow from the fact that (viewed as functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
)
u_{2}
and
u_{1}
are respectively odd and even in
x_{2}
. Then, using that
u_{2}
vanishes on the boundary,

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi_{2}(t,x)\right|=\left|u_{2}(t,\Phi(t,x))\right|\leq\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\Phi_{2}(t,x)\right|,\end{split}

which gives

(4.2)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\Phi_{2}(t,x)}{x_{2}}\leq\frac{3}{2},\qquad t\in[0,\delta]\end{split}

by taking
\delta>0
smaller if necessary. Next, using that
\Phi_{2}(t,y)=0
and
\Phi_{1}(0,x)=x_{1}=\Phi_{1}(0,y)
,

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\Phi_{1}(t,x)-\Phi_{1}(t,y)\right)\right|\leq C\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\Phi_{2}(t,x)+\left|\Phi_{1}(t,x)-\Phi_{1}(t,y)\right|\right),\end{split}

gives after using Gronwall’s inequality and again taking
\delta>0
smaller if necessary that

(4.3)

\begin{split}\left|\Phi_{1}(t,x)-\Phi_{1}(t,y)\right|\leq Ctx_{2}\leq x_{2},\qquad t\in[0,\delta].\end{split}

Therefore, using (
4.3
) and (
4.2
) we get

(4.4)

\begin{split}\left|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)\right|\leq 2x_{2},\qquad t\in[0,\delta].\end{split}

Next, using that
\partial_{1}u_{2}
vanishes on the boundary, we write

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\partial_{1}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))=(-\partial_{1}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta-\frac{\partial_{1}u_{2}}{x_{2}}\partial_{2}\theta x_{2})(t,\Phi(t,x)).

Using boundedness of
\|\partial_{12}u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\partial_{1}\theta(t=0)=Ax_{2}
, we obtain

(4.6)

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{1}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))-Ax_{2}\right|\leq\frac{A}{2}x_{2},\qquad t\in[0,\delta]\end{split}

again taking
\delta>0
smaller. Next, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\partial_{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))=(-{\partial_{2}u_{1}}\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,x))

and using (
4.6
) with boundedness of
\|\partial_{2}u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}
gives

(4.8)

\begin{split}|\partial_{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))|\leq C\left(|\partial_{2}\theta(0,x)|+x_{2}\right)\leq C(|x_{1}|+x_{2}),\qquad t\in[0,\delta].\end{split}

Estimates of
\nabla^{2}\theta
.
With these preparations, we consider
\partial_{2}^{2}\theta
along the flow. From (
3.5
), we have that

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))=\left(-2\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{21}\theta-2\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta-{\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}}\partial_{1}\theta-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta\right)(t,\Phi(t,x)).\end{split}

All the second derivatives of
u
are bounded, except for
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}
. For this term, we estimate

\begin{split}\left|{\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}}\partial_{1}\theta\right|\leq C\|x_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|x_{2}^{-1}\partial_{1}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\end{split}

using (
2.6
) and (
4.6
). Then we can obtain

(4.9)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))\right|\leq C\quad\implies\quad\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))\geq\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(0,x)-Ct=2B-Ct\geq B\end{split}

by shrinking
\delta>0
again if necessary. Proceeding similarly and using
\partial_{12}\theta(0,x)=A
, we can prove that

(4.10)

\begin{split}\partial_{12}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))\geq\frac{A}{2},\qquad t\in[0,\delta].\end{split}

Since the proof is only simpler, we omit the argument.
We are ready to complete the proof, by studying the evolution of difference of
\partial_{2}^{2}\theta
along two particle trajectories. We write

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,y))\right)&=(-2\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{21}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,x))-(-2\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{21}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,y))\\
&\qquad(-2\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,x))-(-2\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,y))\\
&\qquad(-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,x))-(-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,y))\\
&\qquad(-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,x))-(-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,y))\\
&=:I+II+III+IV.\end{split}

We now estimate the differences term by term.

Estimate of
II
.
Simply using the mean value theorem, we have that

\begin{split}\left|(-2\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,x))-(-2\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,y))\right|&\leq C\|\nabla(\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\\
&\leq C\left(\|\partial_{1}u_{1}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|\theta\|_{W^{3,\infty}}\right)|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\\
&\leq CM|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|,\end{split}

using
\partial_{2}u_{2}=-\partial_{1}u_{1}
.

Estimate of
IV
.
We first rewrite
IV
as

\begin{split}(-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,x))-(-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)(t,\Phi(t,y))&=-\left(\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}(t,\Phi(t,x))-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}(t,\Phi(t,y))\right)\partial_{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))\\
&\qquad+\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}(t,\Phi(t,y))\left(\partial_{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,y))-\partial_{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))\right)\\
&=:IV_{1}+IV_{2}.\end{split}

Using
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}=-\partial_{2}\partial_{1}u_{1}
, the term
IV_{2}
is simply bounded by

\begin{split}\left|IV_{2}\right|\leq C\|\partial_{1}u_{1}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|\theta\|_{W^{2,\infty}}|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\leq C|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|.\end{split}

To bound the other term, we use the log-Lipschitz estimate (
2.13
) together with (
4.8
):

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}(t,x)-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}(t,y)\right|\leq CM|x-y|\log\left(10+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)

to obtain that

\begin{split}\left|IV_{1}\right|&\leq CM(|x_{1}|+x_{2})|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\log\left(10+\frac{1}{|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|}\right)\\
&\leq CMx_{2}|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\log\left(10+\frac{C}{x_{2}}\right)\leq CM|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\end{split}

using
|x_{1}|\leq x_{2}
and (
4.4
).

Estimate of
I
and
III
.
We observe that
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta
and
\partial_{2}u_{1}
vanish on the boundary, so that
III=-\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))
and similarly,
I=-2\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{21}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))
. We now use (
2.5
) and (
4.6
) to replace
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{1}
by its main term (with
\Phi=\Phi(t,x)
)

\begin{split}III\geq C_{*}\partial_{22}\theta(t,\Phi)\log\frac{1}{\Phi_{2}}\partial_{1}\theta(t,\Phi)-Cx_{2}\geq\frac{C_{*}}{2}ABx_{2}\log\frac{1}{x_{2}}-Cx_{2}\end{split}

where we have used lower bounds from (
4.9
) and (
4.6
) as well as
\log\frac{1}{\Phi_{2}}=\log\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\log\frac{x_{2}}{\Phi_{2}}
, with the latter being uniformly bounded by an absolute constant. Regarding
I
, we can first integrate (
2.6
) in
x_{2}
to obtain a lower bound

\begin{split}-\partial_{2}u_{1}(t,\Phi)\geq C_{*}\Phi_{2}\log\frac{1}{\Phi_{2}}-C\Phi_{2}.\end{split}

Proceeding similarly and using (
4.10
) this time, we obtain a lower bound

\begin{split}I\geq{C_{*}}ABx_{2}\log\frac{1}{x_{2}}-Cx_{2}.\end{split}

Collecting the estimates.
From the above, we deduce that

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,y))\right)&\geq\frac{3C_{*}}{2}ABx_{2}\log\frac{1}{x_{2}}-Cx_{2}-C(1+M)|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\\
&\geq\frac{3C_{*}}{2}ABx_{2}\log\frac{1}{x_{2}}-C(1+M)x_{2}\end{split}

using (
4.4
) in the last inequality. Observe that when
t=0
,
\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,y))
vanishes. Therefore,

\begin{split}\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,y))\geq(\frac{3C_{*}}{2}ABx_{2}\log\frac{1}{x_{2}}-C(1+M)x_{2})t,\qquad t\in[0,\delta].\end{split}

However, from the contradiction hypothesis we have an upper bound

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))-\partial_{2}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,y))\right|\leq M|\Phi(t,x)-\Phi(t,y)|\leq CMx_{2},\qquad t\in[0,\delta].\end{split}

Dividing by
x_{2}
, we obtain

\begin{split}CM\geq(\frac{3C_{*}}{2}AB\log\frac{1}{x_{2}}-C(1+M))t\end{split}

which gives a contradiction by fixing some
t^{*}\in(0,\delta]
and taking
x_{2}\to 0^{+}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

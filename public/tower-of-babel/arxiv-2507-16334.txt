Higher Gauge Flow Models
Alexander Strunk, Roland Assam

Abstract
This paper introduces Higher Gauge Flow Models, a novel class of Generative Flow Models. Building upon ordinary Gauge Flow Models [ 1 ] , these Higher Gauge Flow Models leverage an L ∞ -algebra, effectively extending the Lie Algebra. This expansion allows for the integration of the higher geometry and higher symmetries associated with higher groups into the framework of Generative Flow Models. Experimental evaluation on a Gaussian Mixture Model dataset revealed substantial performance improvements compared to traditional Flow Models.



1  Introduction

A Higher Gauge Flow Model is a novel approach to generative modeling, where the dynamics is governed by the following neural Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE):

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{dt}x(t):=v_{\theta}(x(t),t)-\alpha(t)\Pi_{M;\hat{W}}\Bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)\bigl[\hat{v}(x(t),t)\bigr]d^{\mu}(x(t),t)\Bigr)

where
\hat{v}(x(t),t)
has the structure of a graded vector. The Higher Gauge Field acts on this graded vector as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)\bigl[\hat{v}(x(t),t)\bigr]d^{\mu}(x(t),t):=\sum_{m}\mathcal{A}^{a}_{\mu}(x(t),t)d^{\mu}(x(t),t)b_{m}(e_{a},\hat{v}(x(t),t),\ldots,\hat{v}(x(t),t))

Here, the index
m
represents the number of inputs for the higher brackets
b_{m}
within the L
∞
-algebra, a sophisticated algebraic structure providing a framework for representing and manipulating higher-order symmetries and invariants. The Higher Gauge Field, denoted as
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)
, is valued within the graded vector space of the L
∞
-algebra. This novel incorporation of an L
∞
-algebra enables the generative model to integrate richer mathematical structures. Specifically, this framework enables the exploration of interesting model architectures and potentially introduces higher symmetries into the domain of deep learning.

2.1  Graded Vector Space

A graded vector space is a vector space equipped with a decomposition into a direct sum of subspaces indexed by a set, usually the integers. Formally, a graded vector space
\hat{V}
is defined as:

\hat{V}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}

where I is an index set (commonly
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{N}
) and each
V_{i}
is a subspace of V. A vector that lies entirely in a single
V_{i}
(i.e. in just one degree) is called a homogeneous element of degree
i
. Graded vector spaces are fundamental throughout algebra and topology, appearing in chain complexes, polynomial and exterior algebras, graded Lie (and super-) algebras, and in the study of homology, cohomology, and generating functions.

Moreover, the set
I
can be equipped with an (abelian) group operation
\circ:I\times I\rightarrow I
.
This additional structure enables the definition of the dual graded vector space
\hat{V}^{\ast}
of
\hat{V}
as follows:

\hat{V}^{\ast}:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}(V_{i})^{\ast}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}(V^{\ast})_{i^{-1}}

where
i^{-1}
represents the inverse with respect to the operation
\circ
.

A tensor product
\hat{\otimes}
of two graded vector spaces
\hat{V},\hat{W}
graded by the same (abelian) group
I
, with operation
\circ
, is defined as:

\hat{V}\hat{\otimes}\hat{W}=\bigoplus_{m}(V\otimes W)_{m}=\bigoplus_{m=i\circ j}(V_{i}\otimes W_{j})

An additional structure that can be introduced is a braiding, which defines a mapping for swapping vector spaces. A braiding
\mathcal{B}
is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:

\displaystyle\hat{V}\hat{\otimes}\hat{W}\rightarrow\hat{W}\hat{\otimes}\hat{V}

\displaystyle v\hat{\otimes}w\mapsto(-1)^{f(|v|,|w|)}w\hat{\otimes}v

where
f:\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is a function depending on the degrees
|v|,|w|
of the individual homogeneous elements
v,w
of the vector spaces
V_{i},W_{i}
. In this paper,
f
is defined as:

f(|v|,|w|):=|v|*|w|

It is crucial to note that linear maps (morphisms)
\hat{F}:\hat{V}\rightarrow\hat{W}
between two graded vector space also possess a graded vector space structure, as
\hat{F}\in\hat{W}\hat{\otimes}\hat{V}^{\ast}
.

2.1.1  L
∞
-algebra

An L
∞
-algebra represents a generalization of a Lie algebra, designed to encode higher geometric and algebraic structures. Instead of requiring the strict Jacobi identity, it replaces that single constraint with an infinite, coherent hierarchy of identities linked by higher homotopies. This added flexibility is indispensable in modern settings-deformation theory, topological field theory, derived geometry—where rigid equalities are often too brittle to capture the phenomena of interest.
Mathematically, an L
∞
-algebra
\hat{L}
is defined as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space:

\hat{L}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}

Its structure is determined by higher brackets
b_{m}
with
m\geq 1
. These brackets are multilinear, graded skew-symmetric maps:

\displaystyle b_{m}:

\displaystyle\hat{L}\times\dots\times\hat{L}\rightarrow\hat{L}

\displaystyle(l_{1},\dots,l_{m})\mapsto b_{m}(l_{1},\dots,l_{m})

The degree of each higher bracket
b_{m}
is
m-2
.
The structure
\hat{L}
is determined by the following:

•

Graded Skew-Symmetry:
The higher brackets
b_{m}
satisfy the following relation:

b_{m}(l_{1},\dots,l_{m})=\Xi(\sigma)b_{m}(l_{\sigma(1)},\dots,l_{\sigma(m)})

where the elements
l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}\in L_{|l_{1}|},\ldots,L_{|l_{m}|}
are homogeneously graded.

The sign
\Xi(\sigma)\in\{1,-1\}
is determined by the graded Koszul sign rule:

l_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge l_{m}=\Xi(\sigma)l_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\ldots\wedge l_{\sigma(m)}

Here,
\sigma
represents a permutation
\mathcal{S}_{k}
of
k
elements, and the wedge product is induced by the graded tensor product
\hat{\otimes}
for the two homogeneous elements
l_{1},l_{2}\in L_{|l_{1}|},L_{|l_{2}|}
:

l_{1}\wedge l_{2}=l_{1}\hat{\otimes}l_{2}-(-1)^{|l_{1}||l_{2}|}l_{2}\hat{\otimes}l_{1}

•

Higher Jacobi Identity:
For homogeneous elements
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\in L_{|l_{1}|},\ldots,L_{|l_{k}|}
, the higher Jacobi identity is given by:

\sum_{m+n=k+1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}|_{c}}\Xi(\sigma)(-1)^{m(n-1)}b_{n}(b_{m}(l_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,l_{\sigma(m)}),l_{\sigma(m+1)},\ldots,l_{\sigma(k)})=0

Here, the permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
is constrained by the condition
c
for
0\leq n\leq k
:

\displaystyle\sigma(1)<\ldots<\sigma(m)

\displaystyle\sigma(m+1)<\ldots<\sigma(k)

There are several equivalent formulations of the mathematical structure of an L
∞
-algebra. For more information refer to
[
11
,
12
]
.

3  Higher Gauge Flow Model

A Higher Gauge Flow Model is defined on a graded vector bundle
\hat{A}
:

\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{A}\\
=\\
E\times_{M}F\end{subarray}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{F}}

with the following:

•

The base manifold M.

•

The graded vector bundle
E
with the fiber, a graded vector space
\hat{V}
with the structure of an L
∞
-algebra.

•

The graded vector bundle
F
with the fiber, a graded vector space
\hat{W}
with the structure of an L
∞
-algebra.

•

The fiber product
E\times_{M}F\coloneqq\{(e,f)|\pi_{E}(e)=\pi_{F}(f)\}
.

The dynamics are described by the following differential equation:

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{dt}x(t)=v_{\theta}(x(t),t)-\alpha(t)\Pi_{M;\hat{W}}\Bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)\bigl[\hat{v}(x(t),t)\bigr]d^{\mu}(x(t),t)\Bigr)

where the
\hat{v}(x(t),t)
is now a graded vector. While the action of the Higher Gauge Field on this is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)\bigl[\hat{v}(x(t),t)\bigr]d^{\mu}(x(t),t):=\sum_{m}\mathcal{A}^{a}_{\mu}(x(t),t)d^{\mu}(x(t),t)b_{m}(e_{a},\hat{v}(x(t),t),\ldots,\hat{v}(x(t),t))

Here, the components are defined as follows:

•

v_{\theta}(x(t),t)\in TM
is a learnable vector field modeled by a Neural Network.

•

\alpha(t)
is a time dependent weight, also modeled by a Neural Network.

•

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)\in\Omega^{1}(M)\hat{\otimes}\hat{V}
is a higher gauge field, namely an L
∞
-algebra valued differential form, which is also modelled by a neural network.

•

d^{\mu}(x(t),t)\in TM
is the direction vector field.

•

\hat{v}(x(t),t)
is a graded vector field valued in
\hat{W}
, which can be learned by a Neural Network.

•

\Pi_{M,\hat{W}}:F\rightarrow TM
is a smooth projection from the vector bundle
F
to the tangent bundle
TM
of the base manifold
M
.

Crucially, the structure of the
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras
\hat{V}
and
\hat{W}
is consistent with the higher brackets
b_{m}
operating between these two graded vector spaces. A potential generalization allows
multiple vector fields,
\hat{v}(x(t),t)
, to be learned by Neural Networks.

The Higher Gauge Flow Model (HGFM) is defined for any differentiable base manifold
M
. Similar to ordinary Gauge Flow Models
[
1
]
, training the HGFM requires
M
to be a Riemannian manifold, possessing a Riemannian metric
g
. The training procedure for the Higher Gauge Flow Model is identical to that of ordinary Gauge Flow Models. It employs the Riemannian Flow
Matching (RFM) framework
[
13
]
, generalizing Flow Matching (FM)

[
14
]
. The training objective, the HGFM loss, is defined as:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{HGFM}}=\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]\\
x\sim p_{t}\end{subarray}}\bigg\lVert\Big[v_{\theta}(x(t),t)-\alpha(t)\Pi_{M;V}\Bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)\bigl[\hat{v}(x(t),t)\bigr]d^{\mu}(x(t),t)\Bigr)\Big]-u_{t}(x)\bigg\rVert_{g_{x}}^{2}

where
\|\cdot\|_{g_{x}}
is the norm induced by the Riemannian metric
g
of the base manifold
M
at point
x
. Both terms lie in the tangent space of the base manifold
M
:

\displaystyle\Big[v_{\theta}(x(t),t)-\alpha(t)\Pi_{M;V}\Bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x(t),t)\bigl[\hat{v}(x(t),t)\bigr]d^{\mu}(x(t),t)\Bigr)\Big]\in T_{x}M

\displaystyle u_{t}(x)\in T_{x}M

The Higher Gauge Flow Model loss incorporates the following components:

•

Probability-density path

p_{t}:M\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
t\in[0,1]
, as defined in
[
13
]
.
For each
t
,
p_{t}
satisfies

\int_{M}p_{t}(x)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{x}=1,

where
d\mathrm{vol}_{x}
is the Riemannian volume form.

•

Target vector field

u_{t}(x)
, obtained from the conditional field
u_{t}(x\mid x_{1})
by

u_{t}(x)=\int_{M}u_{t}\!\bigl(x\mid x_{1}\bigr)\,\frac{p_{t}\!\bigl(x\mid x_{1}\bigr)\,q(x_{1})}{p_{t}(x)}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{x_{1}},

with
q(x)\coloneqq p_{t=1}(x)
and
x_{1}
denoting the sample at time
t=1
.

In general, computing the HGFM loss
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{HGFM}}
exactly is computationally intractable. However, this loss coincides with the Riemannian Flow Matching (RFM) objective. To overcome the intractability,
[
13
]
introduces the Riemannian Conditional Flow Matching (RCFM) loss—an unbiased, single-sample, Monte Carlo estimator of the RFM objective. The core idea is to replace the marginal target field
u_{t}(x)
with its conditional counterpart
u_{t}(x\mid x_{1})
, thereby enabling tractable training. This formulation supports efficient, simulation-free learning on simple geometries and scalable neural network training on general Riemannian manifolds. Notably, this approach can also be applied to train Higher Gauge Flow Models.

For detailed derivations and implementation specifics, refer to
[
13
,
14
]
.

c

•

Brackets

The non-vanishing brackets are defined as follows:

Unary bracket

–

Binary bracket

Degree (0,0):
For two elements

Degree (0,1):

Degree (1,1):

b_{2}

5  Related Work

As of the author’s current knowledge, no existing study has yet connected the theory of
L_{\infty}
-algebras directly to methods in deep learning, nor has Higher Gauge Theory been explicitly linked to contemporary neural-network techniques.
By contrast, there is a rapidly growing body of work that applies ideas from Category Theory -the mathematical foundation underlying Higher Gauge Theory- to machine learning. This line of research, often grouped under
Categorical Deep Learning
, offers a unified formalism for standard deep-learning primitives and algorithms
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
Within this framework, key procedures such as back-propagation and gradient descent can be phrased categorically
[
20
]
, and constructions like
lenses
provide elegant representations of supervised-learning pipelines
[
21
]
.

The interface between Higher Category Theory and deep learning is still largely unexplored. Notable exceptions include the homotopy-theoretic treatment of neural networks in
[
22
]
and the higher category theoretic viewpoint on network composition developed in
[
23
]
. These works suggest that tools from higher algebra may offer principled ways to reason about network architectures and their compositional properties.

6  Outlook

Several promising directions deserve further investigation. A central challenge is to incorporate higher group symmetries-those naturally associated with
L_{\infty}
-algebras -into neural architectures. In general, integrating an arbitrary
L_{\infty}
-algebra is difficult, and concrete higher groups are often unavailable
[
24
]
.
A tractable starting point is the well-studied
string 2-group
, arising from the string 2-Lie algebra
[
25
]
, which can be viewed as a strict 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Another practical avenue is to work with crossed modules of Lie algebras
[
26
,
27
]
, which likewise realise strict
L_{\infty}
-structures.

A complementary strategy is to generalise Higher Gauge Flow Models to the setting of
E\!\,L_{\infty}
-algebras
[
28
]
. Such a generalisation could furnish a principled way to encode higher-symmetry constraints directly in the data or the model, potentially leading to architectures that respect rich algebraic structures inherent in scientific and geometric data.

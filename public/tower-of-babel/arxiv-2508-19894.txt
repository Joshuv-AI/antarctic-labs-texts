Kullback–Leibler Potential for Non-Ergodic Replication Dynamics: An Information-Theoretic Second Law
Tatsuaki Tsuruyama

Abstract
This study aims to quantify and visualize the degradation of fidelity (information degradation) that inevitably accompanies the replication of information within the framework of information thermodynamics and to propose an information-theoretic formulation of the second law based on this phenomenon. While previous research in information thermodynamics has focused on the thermodynamic costs associated with information “erasure” or “measurement” through concepts such as Landauer’s principle and mutual information, little systematic discussion has addressed the inherently irreversible nature of “replication” itself and the accompanying degradation of information structure. In this study, we construct a mathematical model of information replication using a discrete Markov model and Gaussian convolution, and quantify changes in information at each replication step: Shannon entropy, cross-entropy, and the Kullback–Leibler divergence (KLD). The monotonic decrease of KLD exhibits a Lyapunov-like property, which can be interpreted as a potential analogous to the free energy in the process by which a nonequilibrium system converges to a particular steady state. Furthermore, we extend this framework to the potential applicability to biological information processes such as DNA replication, showing that the free energy required for degradation and repair can be expressed in terms of KLD. This contributes to building a unified information-thermodynamic framework for operations such as replication, transmission, and repair of information.

Introduction

The second law of thermodynamics links irreversibility to entropy production.
In information thermodynamics, the physical cost of manipulating information has been clarified for
erasure
and
measurement
through Landauer’s principle and its extensions: erasing one bit dissipates at least
k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2
of heat

, and feedback/measurement operations admit refined second-law-like inequalities expressed through mutual information
Sagawa2010
;
Sagawa2012
;
Parrondo2015
.
In contrast, the inherently irreversible nature of
replication
- biological or digital - has not been formulated in a unified information–thermodynamic framework.
From a biochemical point of view, kinetic proofreading already indicates that high-fidelity copying requires free energy expenditure
Hopfield1974
, yet a coarse-grained information-theoretic law for replication itself has remained elusive.

This work.

We model replication as a discrete-time Markov map on probability distributions under

(non-ergodic constraints).
Given an initial distribution
p_{0}
, let

denote the

composed of blockwise invariant distributions that preserve the initial block masses (formal definitions in Sect.
II
).
Our central object is

.

(1)

which measures the excess information retained beyond the conserved coarse variables.
We prove that

Unlike the classical ergodic case—where the KL divergence to a

II
General framework and main theorem

II.1
Block invariance and reachable steady set

The information replication operations we study (image blurring, base substitution with proofreading, etc.) are inherently
local
: one step only rearranges probability mass within a constrained neighborhood (spatial or compositional), but does not freely mix all microscopic states. As a result, certain coarse observables, e.g., the total mass inside a spatial region of an image, or the adenine(A) -thymine (T) rich block and guanine(G) -cytosine (C) rich block in DNA to be replicated as a template, are effectively
conserved
by every replication step. When such conserved coarse variables exist, the Markov dynamics does not converge to a
single
global invariant distribution; instead, the state space decomposes into components that evolve independently. We call this situation
non-ergodic
in the sense that long-term behavior retains memory of the initial coarse structure through the conserved weights.

(i) In the image model, a blockwise Gaussian convolution forbids smoothing across block boundaries; the total intensity in each block is preserved at every step.
(ii) In the DNA model, a block diagonal substitution kernel reflects biochemical or sequence constraints (e.g., AT- vs. GC-rich segments); block masses (AT vs. GC) remain fixed while within-block compositions relax.
(iii) More generally, locality, topological separation, or routing restrictions in copying channels induce invariant ’blocks’ that prevent global mixing.
Because replication preserves these coarse variables, the appropriate equilibrium notion is not a single point but a
set
of steady states obtained by fully relaxing
within
each invariant component while keeping the initial coarse weights fixed. This motivates modeling the state space as a disjoint union of blocks and defining a
reachable steady set
against which we will measure the distance using a KL-based potential.
We consider a finite state space partitioned into disjoint blocks.

\mathcal{X}\ =\ \bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}\,\mathcal{X}_{j},

(4)

and a Markov kernel
T
acting on
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
. Here
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
denotes the probability simplex over
\mathcal{X}
, i.e. the set of all probability distributions
p:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]
with
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)=1
; we identify
p
with a row vector and let
T
act by
(pT)(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)\,T(x,y)
, where for each
x
the map
A\mapsto T(x,A)
is a probability measure on subsets of
\mathcal{X}
(so, in the finite case,
T
is a row stochastic matrix). The dynamics is
block-invariant
if the transitions never cross the block boundaries, i.e.,

x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}\ \Longrightarrow\ T(x,\mathcal{X}_{j})=1\qquad(j=1,\dots,m).

(5)

In above, for a Markov kernel
T
,
T(x,A)
is the one-step probability to land in
A
given the current state
x
.
Thus, for
x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}
, the condition
T(x,\mathcal{X}_{j})=1
means the next state lies in the same block with probability 1;
equivalently
\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}_{j}}T(x,y)=1
and
T(x,y)=0
for all
y\notin\mathcal{X}_{j}
. In matrix form, each row indexed by
x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}
has the support contained in
\mathcal{X}_{j}
, so
T
is block-diagonal and block masses are preserved.Block invariance guarantees conservation of block masses
w_{j}
but
not
convergence within blocks.
Our potential
V(p)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(p_{0})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\pi)
over the reachable steady set
\Pi(p_{0})
is designed to handle precisely these non-ergodic situations: by block decomposition and the data-processing inequality,
V(p_{n+1})\leq V(p_{n})
always holds; and
V(p_{n})\to 0
requires, in addition, that each block be primitive (irreducible and aperiodic), yielding a unique invariant
\pi_{j}^{\ast}
.
For
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, define the block masses

w_{j}(p)\ :=\ p(\mathcal{X}_{j}),\qquad j=1,\dots,m,

(6)

and, whenever
w_{j}(p)>0
, the conditional distributions within each block by

p^{(j)}(x)\ :=\ \begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{p(x)}{w_{j}(p)},&x\in\mathcal{X}_{j},\\[4.30554pt]
0,&x\notin\mathcal{X}_{j}.\end{cases}

(7)

Let
T_{j}
denote the restriction of
T
to block
\mathcal{X}_{j}
.
The set of
T_{j}
-invariant measures is

\mathcal{I}_{j}\ :=\ \bigl\{\ \pi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{j})\ :\ \pi T_{j}=\pi\ \bigr\}.

(8)

Given an initial distribution
p_{0}
,
reachable steady set
is defined as

\Pi(p_{0})\ :=\ \Bigl\{\ \pi=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p_{0})\,\pi_{j}\ :\ \pi_{j}\in\mathcal{I}_{j}\ \Bigr\}.

(9)

Intuitively,
\Pi(p_{0})
collects all steady states that (i) fully relax within each block and (ii) preserve the initial coarse variables
\{w_{j}(p_{0})\}_{j=1}^{m}
fixed by non-ergodic constraints.

II.2
KLD potential and two basic lemmas

We adopt the Kullback–Leibler divergence (KLD) between
p,q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)\ :=\ \sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)\,\log\!\frac{p(x)}{q(x)},

(10)

with the convention
0\log(0/q)=0
and
p\log(p/0)=+\infty
.
Given the reachable steady set
\Pi(p_{0})
in (
9
), we define
KLD potential

V(p)\ :=\ \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(p_{0})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\pi),

(11)

(cf. the introductory statement (
1
)).
The next two lemmas formalize (i) conservation of block masses and conditional evolution, and (ii) blockwise decomposition of KLD.

Lemma 1

(Preservation of block mass and conditional evolution)
.

Let
p_{+}=Tp
with block-invariant
T
as in (
5
).
Then for all
j=1,\dots,m
,

w_{j}(p_{+})\ =\ w_{j}(p),

(12)

and, whenever
w_{j}(p)>0
,

(Tp)^{(j)}\ =\ T_{j}\,p^{(j)}.

(13)

Proof.

By block invariance,
T(x,\mathcal{X}_{j})=1
for
x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}
and
0
otherwise. Hence

w_{j}(p_{+})=(Tp)(\mathcal{X}_{j})=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)\,T(x,\mathcal{X}_{j})=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}}p(x)\cdot 1+\sum_{x\notin\mathcal{X}_{j}}p(x)\cdot 0=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}}p(x)=w_{j}(p)
.

which proves (
12
). For any measurable
A\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{j}
,

(Tp)^{(j)}(A)=\frac{(Tp)(A)}{w_{j}(p_{+})}=\frac{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)T(x,A)}{w_{j}(p)}\\
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}}\tfrac{p(x)}{w_{j}(p)}\,T_{j}(x,A)=(T_{j}p^{(j)})(A).

(14)

establishing (
13
).
∎

Lemma 2

(Block decomposition of KLD)
.

Let
\pi=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}\,\pi_{j}
with
w_{j}>0
and
\pi_{j}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{j})
supported on disjoint blocks
\mathcal{X}_{j}
.
Then, for any
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\pi)\ =\ \sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p)\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p^{(j)}\|\pi_{j}\bigr)\ +\ \sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p)\,\log\!\frac{w_{j}(p)}{w_{j}}.

(15)

Proof.

Using the disjointness
\mathcal{X}=\bigsqcup_{j}\mathcal{X}_{j}
and writing
p(x)=w_{j}(p)\,p^{(j)}(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}
,

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}}p(x)\,\log\!\frac{p(x)}{w_{j}\,\pi_{j}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}}w_{j}(p)\,p^{(j)}(x)\left[\log\!\frac{p^{(j)}(x)}{\pi_{j}(x)}+\log\!\frac{w_{j}(p)}{w_{j}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p)\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p^{(j)}\|\pi_{j}\bigr)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p)\,\log\!\frac{w_{j}(p)}{w_{j}}.

(16)

which is (
15
).
∎

Equations (
12
)–(
13
) show that the dynamics preserves coarse variables
\{w_{j}\}
and evolves conditionals within each block, while (
15
) cleanly separates
within-block
divergences from a
coarse-mass mismatch
term.
These identities will be the backbone for the Lyapunov property established in Sec.
II.3
.

II.3
Lyapunov property of the KLD potential

Motivation and novelty.

In many replication scenarios the dynamics is inherently local and respects coarse constraints that are fixed by the substrate:
for DNA, heterogeneous base composition (e.g., AT/GC content and sequence-context effects) induces
blocks
within which substitutions and proofreading occur, while inter-block flow is strongly suppressed.
Our novelty is to adopt this block structure explicitly and measure nonequilibrium distance not to a single invariant law, but to the
reachable steady set
that preserves the initial block masses.
Concretely, with the block partition and the reachable set
\Pi(p_{0})
defined in Sec.
II.1
, we use the
KL potential

V(p)\ :=\ \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(p_{0})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\pi),

which collapses to the classical KL-to-steady Lyapunov function in the ergodic/single-invariant limit, but remains meaningful under non-ergodicity caused by block invariance.

Statement.

We show that
V
decreases monotonically along any block-invariant Markov evolution
p_{n+1}=Tp_{n}
.

Theorem 1

(KLD potential is Lyapunov under block invariance)
.

Let
T
satisfy the block-invariance condition (
5
) and define
V
as above. Then, for all
n\geq 0
,

V(p_{n+1})\ \leq\ V(p_{n}).

(17)

If, in addition, each block kernel
T_{j}
is primitive (irreducible and aperiodic) with unique invariant
\pi_{j}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{I}_{j}
, then

V(p_{n})\ =\ \sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p_{0})\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p_{n}^{(j)}\!\|\pi_{j}^{\ast}\bigr)\ \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\ 0.

(18)

Proof.

Fix any
\pi\in\Pi(p_{0})
and write
\pi=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p_{0})\,\pi_{j}
with
\pi_{j}\in\mathcal{I}_{j}
.
By Lemma
1
,
w_{j}(Tp)=w_{j}(p_{0})
and, by Lemma
2
,

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Tp\,\|\,\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p_{0})\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl((Tp)^{(j)}\,\|\,\pi_{j}\bigr)

(19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p_{0})\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(T_{j}p^{(j)}\,\|\,\pi_{j}\bigr).

For KLD, the data-processing inequality (DPI) for
f
-divergences gives

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_{j}r\,\|\,\pi_{j})\ \leq\ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\,\|\,\pi_{j})\qquad(\pi_{j}T_{j}=\pi_{j}),

(20)

so inserting (
20
) into (
19
) yields

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Tp\,\|\,\pi)\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p_{0})\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p^{(j)}\,\|\,\pi_{j}\bigr)\ =\ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\,\|\,\pi).

(21)

Taking the infimum over
\pi\in\Pi(p_{0})
on both sides proves (
17
).
If each
T_{j}
is primitive with invariant
\pi_{j}^{\ast}
, then
p_{n}^{(j)}\to\pi_{j}^{\ast}
, and hence (
18
).
∎

The DPI (hence (
20
)) is classical; the contribution here is to (i) encode biologically motivated constraints (e.g., heterogeneous AT / GC composition, sequence context locality) as block invariance, (ii) measure distance to a
set
of steady states that preserves the initial block masses, and (iii) show that the
minimal
KL to this set remains a Lyapunov function even though the minimizing
\pi
may change along the trajectory.
This covers non-ergodic replication where global mixing is forbidden but within-block relaxation proceeds.

Remark 1

(Equality and strict decay)
.

Equality in (
17
) at step
n
holds iff there exists a minimizer
\pi^{\star}\in\Pi(p_{0})
with

D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(T_{j}p_{n}^{(j)}\,\|\,\pi_{j}^{\star}\bigr)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p_{n}^{(j)}\,\|\,\pi_{j}^{\star}\bigr)\quad\text{for all }j,

i.e., DPI is tight blockwise (this typically means
p_{n}^{(j)}
is already
T_{j}
-invariant).
Otherwise the decrease is strict.

Corollary 1

(Per-step potential drop)
.

The drop
\Delta V_{n}:=V(p_{n})-V(p_{n+1})\geq 0
for all
n
, with
\Delta V_{n}=0
iff the condition in Remark
1
holds.

Instantiations

III.1
Image replication via Gaussian convolution

We model a single replication step as a smoothing (information loss) Markov map
T_{\sigma}
induced by discrete Gaussian convolution in an image
I:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to[0,1]
.
Let the normalized Gaussian kernel be

G_{\sigma}(u,v)\ :=\ \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\,\exp\!\Big(-\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big),\qquad(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\ \sigma>0,

(22)

and define the replication map by discrete convolution;

(I\ast G_{\sigma})(x,y)\ :=\ \sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}}I(x-u,y-v)\,G_{\sigma}(u,v).

(23)

Let
p_{n}
denote the (normalized) histogram of pixel intensities before step
n
, viewed as a probability distribution on a finite intensity alphabet
\mathcal{A}
; the one-step channel
T_{\sigma}
induced by (
23
) yields

q_{n}\ =\ p_{n}\,T_{\sigma},\qquad p_{n+1}\ =\ q_{n}.

(24)

Ergodic vs. non-ergodic (blockwise) blur.

To model non-ergodicity, partition the image grid into disjoint blocks

\{\mathcal{B}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
and restrict the kernel support so that no mass crosses block boundaries.
For
(x,y)\in\mathcal{B}_{j}
,

(I\ast_{j}G_{\sigma})(x,y)\ :=\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in\mathbb{Z}\\
(x-u,y-v)\in\mathcal{B}_{j}\end{subarray}}I(x-u,y-v)\,G_{\sigma}(u,v),

(25)

which induces a block diagonal channel
T_{\sigma}=\mathrm{diag}\bigl((T_{\sigma})_{1},\dots,(T_{\sigma})_{m}\bigr)
and thus satisfies the block-invariance condition (
5
).
The “ergodic” case corresponds to using (
23
) globally (no blocking).

Recorded metrics.

At each step we compute (nats)

\displaystyle H(q_{n})\

\displaystyle:=\ -\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}q_{n}(a)\,\log q_{n}(a),

(26)

\displaystyle H_{\times}(n)\

\displaystyle:=\ H(p_{n},q_{n})\ =\ -\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}p_{n}(a)\,\log q_{n}(a),

(27)

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{n}\|q_{n})\

\displaystyle:=\ \sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}p_{n}(a)\,\log\frac{p_{n}(a)}{q_{n}(a)},

(28)

\displaystyle V(p_{n})\

\displaystyle:=\ \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(p_{0})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{n}\|\pi),

(29)

where
\Pi(p_{0})
is the reachable steady set defined in (
9
).
By Theorem
1
,
V(p_{n})
is nonincreasing along (
24
) in the non-ergodic (blockwise) case; empirically we also observe monotone
V
in the ergodic case where the steady set is a singleton.

III.2
DNA replication as a block-diagonal substitution process

Biophysical context.

Single-molecule and theory studies show that DNA polymerases advance stochastically with forward/backward steps, proofreading, and context-dependent kinetics
Wuite2000
;
Bustamante2003
;
Le2018
;
Gaspard2016a
;
Pineros2020
.
To capture non-ergodicity induced by compositional structure (e.g. AT- vs. GC-rich segments), we model replication as a
block-diagonal
Markov process on nucleotides.

Block-diagonal dynamics.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathrm{A},\mathrm{T},\mathrm{C},\mathrm{G}\}
with blocks

\mathcal{X}_{1}=\{\mathrm{A},\mathrm{T}\}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}=\{\mathrm{C},\mathrm{G}\}
.
A one-step substitution kernel (copying) with block invariance (
5
) is

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}(T_{1},T_{2}),

(30)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha&\alpha\\
\beta&1-\beta\end{pmatrix},\qquad T_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\gamma&\gamma\\
\delta&1-\delta\end{pmatrix}.

with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in(0,1)
encoding effective substitution tendencies per step (arising from selectivity, context, and post-replicative processing).
The block masses
w_{1}=p_{\mathrm{A}}+p_{\mathrm{T}}
and
w_{2}=p_{\mathrm{C}}+p_{\mathrm{G}}
are conserved by the lemma
1
.

Proofreading/repair channel.

To incorporate proofreading/repair, we add a blockwise operator
\mathcal{R}=\mathrm{diag}(R_{1},R_{2})
that is invoked with probability
\rho\in[0,1]
after extension:

R_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha^{\prime}&\alpha^{\prime}\\[2.0pt]
\beta^{\prime}&1-\beta^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},\qquad R_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\gamma^{\prime}&\gamma^{\prime}\\[2.0pt]
\delta^{\prime}&1-\delta^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

(31)

with typically
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}<\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
(improved selectivity).
The effective one-step kernel is the convex mixture.

\widetilde{T}\ :=\ (1-\rho)\,T\ +\ \rho\,\mathcal{R},

(32)

which remains block diagonal and therefore satisfies (
5
).
The replication update reads

q_{n}\ =\ p_{n}\,\widetilde{T},\qquad p_{n+1}\ =\ q_{n}.

(33)

KLD potential under unique block invariants.

If each block chain has a unique invariant
\pi_{j}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{I}_{j}
, then by (

) and (

V(p_{n})\ =\ \sum_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(p_{0})\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p_{n}^{(j)}\ \|\ \pi_{j}^{\ast}\bigr),

(34)

and Theorem

ensures
V(p_{n+1})\leq V(p_{n})
and
V(p_{n})\to 0
as

.

Energetic bias (phenomenology).

A simple coarse-grained link between proofreading bias and a chemical affinity

reads

\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\beta^{\prime}}\ \approx\ \frac{\alpha}{\beta}\,e^{\Delta\mu/k_{\mathrm{B}}T},\qquad\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\delta^{\prime}}\ \approx\ \frac{\gamma}{\delta}\,e^{\Delta\mu/k_{\mathrm{B}}T},

suggesting that a stronger driving sharpens

and accelerates the decay of

(cf. kinetic proofreading intuition

\displaystyle e_{\mathrm{AT}}(n)\

P(\tilde{y}\mid\tilde{x})\ =\begin{pmatrix}1-e_{\mathrm{AT}}&e_{\mathrm{AT}}\\[2.0pt]

\displaystyle I(\tilde{X};\tilde{Y})

An information-theoretic second law (minimal form)

State function and step variables.

Let

\Delta V_{n}:=V(p_{n})-V(p_{n+1}),

(41)

and

D_{n}:=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{n}\|q_{n}),\qquad q_{n}:=p_{n}T.

(42)

By Theorem
1
and nonnegativity of KLD, one has
\Delta V_{n}\geq 0
and
D_{n}\geq 0
.

Step production (dimensionless) and its sum.

Define the per-step production

\mathcal{S}_{n}:=D_{n}+\Delta V_{n},

(43)

and the cumulative production over
n=0,\dots,N-1
,

\mathcal{S}^{[0:N)}:=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{S}_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}D_{n}\;+\;V(p_{0})-V(p_{N}).

(44)

In physical units,

\sigma_{\rm rep}^{(n)}:=k_{\mathrm{B}}T\,\mathcal{S}_{n},\qquad\Sigma_{\rm rep}^{(N)}:=k_{\mathrm{B}}T\,\mathcal{S}^{[0:N)}.

(45)

Theorem 2

(Second law for non-ergodic replication: minimal form)
.

For any block-invariant trajectory
\{p_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
,

\mathcal{S}_{n}\geq 0\quad(n=0,1,\dots,N-1),

(46)

hence

\mathcal{S}^{[0:N)}\geq 0.

(47)

Equivalently,
\sigma_{\rm rep}^{(n)}\geq 0
and
\Sigma_{\rm rep}^{(N)}\geq 0
.

Idea.

Immediate from
D_{n}\geq 0
and
\Delta V_{n}\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 2

(Equality and strictness)
.

\mathcal{S}_{n}=0
iff
D_{n}=\Delta V_{n}=0
; otherwise
\mathcal{S}_{n}>0
.

IV
Discussion

KLD potential as informational free energy.

Under block invariance, the coarse variables
\{w_{j}\}
are conserved (Lemma
1
) while the within-block conditionals relax toward block-wise invariants.
The potential
V
in (
11
) therefore measures the nonequilibrium information retained beyond the conserved coarse structure.
Its monotone decay (
17
) provides an information-theoretic second law for non-ergodic replication: fine-scale distinguishability is irreversibly lost, whereas coarse composition is preserved.

Per-step bookkeeping and physical units.

The minimal second law above decomposes the step production into two nonnegative parts,

D_{n}
and
\Delta V_{n}
, cf. (
43
).
In physical units, the potential drop defines an
informational free-energy
change

\Delta F_{\mathrm{info}}^{(n)}:=k_{\mathrm{B}}T\,\Delta V_{n}=k_{\mathrm{B}}T\,[\,V(p_{n})-V(p_{n+1})\,]\ \geq 0,

(48)

while
k_{\mathrm{B}}T\,D_{n}
quantifies the stepwise dissipated “informational heat.”
Together they account for the net degradation incurred by one replication step.

Ergodic vs. non-ergodic limits.

If the reachable steady set
\Pi(p_{0})
collapses to a single invariant distribution (ergodic limit),
V
reduces to the standard KL-to-steady Lyapunov function.
Under non-ergodic, block-invariant constraints,
V
is the
minimal
KL distance to the steady
set

\Pi(p_{0})
, so its decay quantifies relaxation under conserved coarse masses.
This distinction is essential for replication processes where structural constraints inhibit global mixing.

Tightness and equality cases.

Equality in (
17
) requires blockwise tightness of the data-processing inequality (Remark
1
); operationally, this corresponds to being at (or effectively on) a block-wise steady state.
Away from such fixed points, the decrease is strict and
V
serves as a sensitive progress variable for replication-driven relaxation.

V
Conclusion and Outlook

We formulate replication as a discrete Markov map under block invariance and prove that
minimal
KLD to the reachable steady set,

V(p)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(p_{0})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\pi),

is a Lyapunov function (Theorem
1
).
This yields an information-theoretic second law for
non-ergodic
replication, complementary to Landauer-type results for erasure.
Instantiations in Gaussian image copying and DNA block-diagonal substitution with proofreading exhibit the predicted behavior: monotone
V
, increasing
H
and cross-entropy, and decreasing
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{n}\|q_{n})
.
Future directions include (i) force-resolved single-molecule tests of potential drops and no-free-copying bounds, (ii) extensions to multiscale block hierarchies and heterogeneous kinetics, and (iii) quantum/continuous-state analogues where contractive metrics (e.g. quantum
f
-divergences) may provide replication second laws under conserved coarse observables.

import numpy as np

def H_shannon(p):
m = p > 0
return -np.sum(p[m]*np.log(p[m]))

def H_cross(p, q):
m = (p > 0) & (q > 0)
return -np.sum(p[m]*np.log(q[m]))

def Dkl(p, q):
m = (p > 0) & (q > 0)
return np.sum(p[m]*(np.log(p[m]) - np.log(q[m])))

def V_block2(p, w1_0, w2_0, pi1, pi2):
pA,pT,pC,pG = p
w1, w2 = pA+pT, pC+pG
p1 = np.array([pA, pT])/w1
p2 = np.array([pC, pG])/w2
return w1_0*Dkl(p1, pi1) + w2_0*Dkl(p2, pi2)

# parameters (nats)
alpha, beta = 0.020, 0.010
gamma, delta = 0.015, 0.015
alpha_p, beta_p = 0.005, 0.003
gamma_p, delta_p = 0.004, 0.004
rho = 0.30

ae = (1-rho)*alpha + rho*alpha_p
be = (1-rho)*beta + rho*beta_p
ge = (1-rho)*gamma + rho*gamma_p
de = (1-rho)*delta + rho*delta_p

T1 = np.array([[1-ae, ae],[be, 1-be]], float)
T2 = np.array([[1-ge, ge],[de, 1-de]], float)
T = np.zeros((4,4), float); T[:2,:2]=T1; T[2:,2:]=T2

pi1 = np.array([be/(ae+be), ae/(ae+be)], float)
pi2 = np.array([de/(ge+de), ge/(ge+de)], float)

p = np.array([0.6,0.1,0.2,0.1], float); p = p/np.sum(p)
w1_0, w2_0 = p[0]+p[1], p[2]+p[3]

N = 50
Hq, Hx, D, V = [], [], [], []
for n in range(N+1):
V.append(V_block2(p, w1_0, w2_0, pi1, pi2)) # V(p_n)
if n == N: break
q = p @ T # q_n = p_n T
Hq.append(H_shannon(q)) # H(q_n)
Hx.append(H_cross(p, q)) # H(p_n, q_n)
D.append(Dkl(p, q)) # D_KL(p_n || q_n)
p = q

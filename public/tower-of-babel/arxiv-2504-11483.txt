A note on the mean square of Riemann zeta-function
Li An-Ping

Abstract
In this paper, we will give a new proof for a known result of the mean square of the Riemann zeta-function



1
Introduction

Let

I=\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\zeta(1/2+it)\rvert^{2}\lvert A(1/2+it)\rvert^{2}dt

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta-function, and

A(s)=\sum_{1\leq m\leq M}a(m)m^{-s}

is a finite Dirichlet series. For this type of mean square of
\zeta(s)
, there are a number of researches,
which is related to estimate the number of the zeros of
\zeta(s)
on the critical line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2
, see [
1\sim 6
].

As before, for two positive integers
h,k
, denote
h^{*}=h/(h,k),k^{*}=k/(h,k)
, and
\overline{h^{*}}
is the least positive integer
such that
\overline{h^{*}}h^{*}\equiv 1\mod k^{*}
.

For
s=c+it,c=1+\eta,0<\eta<1
, denote by

\mathscr{M}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(n)}{n^{s}}\sum_{h,k\leq M}\frac{a(h)\overline{a(k)}}{(hk)^{1-s}}(h,k)^{(1-2s)}e\left(\frac{n\overline{h^{*}}}{k^{*}}\right)

and
V=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{|t|\leq M}\{\mathscr{M}(c+it)\}
.

Balasubramanian, Conrey, and Heath-Brown proved that

For
a(m)\ll m^{\epsilon}
, and
\log(m)\ll\log T
, there is

I=T\sum_{h,k\leq M}\frac{a(h)}{h}\frac{\overline{a(k)}}{k}(h,k)\left(\log\frac{T(h,k)^{2}}{2\pi hk}+2\gamma-1\right)+\mathscr{E}.

(1.1)

where
\mathscr{E}\ll VT^{\epsilon}+o(T)
.

In the paper[1], it is applied an auxiliary function
w(t,T_{1},T_{2})
, which plays an important role of ”ferrying”, Instead, in this paper, we will make use of
a new one, i.e.
\omega(t,T_{1},T_{2})
defined in Lemma 2.1. Our result is that

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
a(m)\ll m^{\epsilon}
, and
\log(m)\ll\log T
, then

I=T\sum_{h,k\leq M}\frac{a(h)}{h}\frac{\overline{a(k)}}{k}(h,k)\left(\log\frac{T(h,k)^{2}}{2\pi hk}+2\gamma-1\right)+\tilde{\mathscr{E}}.

(1.2)

where
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}\ll VT^{-\eta+\epsilon}+o(T)
.

As it is easy to know that
V\ll M^{2+2\epsilon+2\eta}
, then with a properly re-define
\epsilon,\eta
, it has

\tilde{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{E}\ll M^{2}T^{\epsilon}.

(1.3)

The main arguments in this paper are most similar to the ones in [1].

The Proof of Theorem 1.1

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\lambda=T^{2-\epsilon}
, define

\omega(t,T_{1},T_{2})=\frac{e^{\lambda}}{2\pi}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\Gamma(\lambda+(u-t)i)\lambda^{-(\lambda+(u-t)i)}du.

(2.1)

Let
\alpha\geq 10
, denote
\Delta=(2\alpha\lambda\log T)^{1/2}
, if
t\in[T_{1}+\Delta,T_{2}-\Delta]
, then

\left|\omega(t,T_{1},T_{2})-1\right|\ll T^{-\alpha}.

(2.2)

And if
t\leq T_{1}-\Delta
, or
t\geq T_{2}+\Delta
, then

\left|\omega(t,T_{1},T_{2})\right|\ll T^{-\alpha}.

(2.3)

Proof.

It is familiar that

e^{-\lambda}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(s)\lambda^{-s}ds.

Hence,

1-\omega(t,T_{1},T_{2})=R_{1}+R_{2},

where

R_{1}=\frac{e^{\lambda}}{2\pi i}\int_{T_{2}}^{\infty}\Gamma(\lambda+(u-t)i)\lambda^{-(\lambda+(u-t)i)}du,

R_{2}=\frac{e^{\lambda}}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{T_{1}}\Gamma(\lambda+(u-t)i)\lambda^{-(\lambda+(u-t)i)}du,

By Stirling’s formula, it has

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(\log\Gamma(\lambda+(u-t)i))=

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\log(\lambda^{2}+(u-t)^{2})}{2}-\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)

\displaystyle-(u-t)\arctan\left(\frac{u-t}{\lambda}\right)+O(1/\lambda)

And

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(\log(\Gamma(\lambda+(u-t)i)))+\mathrm{Re}(\log(\lambda^{-(\lambda+(u-t)i)}))+\lambda

\displaystyle=-\frac{(u-t)^{2}}{4\lambda^{2}}-\frac{(u-t)^{2}}{2\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\log\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)+O(1/\lambda)

Hence, if
t\in[T_{1}+\Delta,T_{2}-\Delta]
, then

\displaystyle\left|R_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\ll\int_{T_{2}}^{\infty}\left|e^{\lambda}\Gamma(\lambda+(u-t)i)\lambda^{-(\lambda+(u-t)i)}\right|du

\displaystyle\ll\lambda^{-1/2}\int_{T_{2}}^{\infty}\exp(-(u-t)^{2}/2\lambda)du

\displaystyle\ll T^{-\alpha}.

and similarly

\left|R_{2}\right|\ll\lambda^{-1/2}\int_{-\infty}^{T_{1}}\exp(-(u-t)^{2}/2\lambda)du\ll T^{-\alpha}.

if
t\leq T_{1}-\Delta
, or
t\geq T_{2}+\Delta
, then

\left|\omega(t,T_{1},T_{2})\right|\ll\lambda^{-1/2}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\exp(-(u-t)^{2}/2\lambda)du\ll T^{-\alpha}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
1<c<2
, let

J(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(s_{1}-s)\lambda^{-(s_{1}-s)}\chi(1-s)y^{-s}ds,

then

J(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{s_{1}}e^{-\lambda v}(e^{2\pi iyv}+e^{-2\pi iyv})\frac{dv}{v}.

(2.5)

Proof.

J(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(s_{1}-s)\lambda^{-(s_{1}-s)}\Gamma(s)((2\pi iy)^{-s}+(-2\pi iy)^{-s})ds=J_{1}+J_{2}

\displaystyle J_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(s_{1}-s)\lambda^{-(s_{1}-s)}\Gamma(s)(2\pi iy)^{-s}ds,

\displaystyle J_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(s_{1}-s)\lambda^{-(s_{1}-s)}\Gamma(s)(-2\pi iy)^{-s}ds.

By the theory of Mellin Transforms(refer to [7])

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}G(s)F(s)x^{-s}ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{1}{v}\right)g(xv)\frac{dv}{v}

where

f(v)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}F(x)x^{-s}ds,\qquad g(v)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}G(x)x^{-s}ds.

We apply this formula to
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
respectively.

For
J_{1}

\displaystyle f(v)=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{-s_{1}}}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(s_{1}-s)(v/\lambda)^{-s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{-s_{1}}}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(w)(v/\lambda)^{-(s_{1}-w)}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{-s_{1}}(v/\lambda)^{-s_{1}}e^{-\lambda/v}=v^{-s_{1}}e^{-\lambda/v},

f(1/v)=v^{s_{1}}e^{-\lambda v}.

And

g(v)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(c)}\Gamma(s)v^{-s}ds=e^{-v},

i.e.

g(xv)=e^{-xv}.

So

J_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{s_{1}}e^{-(\lambda v+2\pi iyv)}\frac{dv}{v}

Similarly,

J_{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{s_{1}}e^{-(\lambda v-2\pi iyv)}\frac{dv}{v}

Proof.

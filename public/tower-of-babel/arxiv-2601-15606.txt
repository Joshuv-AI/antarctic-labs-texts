Barcode entropy and relative symplectic cohomology
Jonghyeon Ahn

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the barcode entropy—the exponential growth rate of the number of not-too-short bars—of the persistence module associated with the relative symplectic cohomology S ​ H M ​ ( K ) SH_{M}(K) of a Liouville domain K K embedded in a symplectic manifold M M . Our main result establishes a quantitative link between this Floer-theoretic invariant and the dynamics of the Reeb flow on ∂ K \partial K . More precisely, we show that the barcode entropy of the relative symplectic cohomology S ​ H M ​ ( K ) SH_{M}(K) is bounded above by a constant multiple of the topological entropy of the Reeb flow on the boundary of the domain, where the constant depends on the embedding of K K into M M .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

A
persistence module
, which was originally introduced in the framework of topological data analysis to encode the evolution of (co)homological features across filtrations, has found significant applications beyond its initial scope. In particular, this concept was adapted to the realm of symplectic geometry by Polterovich and Shelukhin
[
19
]
, providing a powerful algebraic formalism for studying quantitative and dynamical aspects of Hamiltonian systems. Following their work, persistence modules have become a central object in the investigation of Floer-theoretic invariants and their connections to Hamiltonian dynamics.

One of the fundamental results in the theory of persistence modules is the
normal form theorem
, which asserts that every persistence module
(V,\pi)
is completely determined by a multiset
\mathcal{B}(V,\pi)=\{(I_{i},m_{i})\}
of intervals
I_{i}
with multiplicity
m_{i}
. This multiset
\mathcal{B}(V,\pi)
is called the
barcode
of
(V,\pi)
and the elements of
\mathcal{B}(V,\pi)
are called
bars
. The
barcode entropy
of a persistence module
(V,\pi)
, introduced by Cineli, Ginzburg, and Gurel
[
3
]
, measures the exponential growth rate of the “not-too-short” bars in the barcode
\mathcal{B}(V,\pi)
. While the barcode entropy has been studied in several Floer-theoretic settings, its behavior in relative symplectic cohomology setting has remained unexplored. In this paper, we initiate such a study by introducing and analyzing the barcode entropy associated with the relative symplectic cohomology introduced by Varolgunes in
[
24
]
.

The initial investigation of barcode entropy by Cineli, Ginzburg, and Gurel
[
3
]
focused on the persistence module structure arising from the Lagrangian Floer cohomology of pairs of Hamiltonian isotopic Lagrangian submanifolds. For two Lagrangian submanifolds
L
and
L^{\prime}
with
L^{\prime}=\varphi_{H}(L)
where
\varphi_{H}
is the Hamiltonian diffeomorphism corresponding to
H
, we denote the barcode entropy of
HF(L,L^{\prime})
by
\hbar(\varphi_{H};L)
. Their central result is that this barcode entropy is controlled by the dynamics of
\varphi_{H}
: They proved that the barcode entropy
\hbar(\varphi_{H};L)
is bounded above by the topological entropy
h_{\textrm{top}}(\varphi_{H})
of the Hamiltonian diffeomorphism
\varphi_{H}
.

This construction also yields a notion of barcode entropy
\hbar(\varphi_{H})
for the diffeomorphism
\varphi_{H}
itself by considering the diagonal
\Delta
and the graph
\Gamma_{\varphi_{H}}
of
\varphi_{H}
, namely,

\displaystyle\hbar(\varphi_{H})=\hbar(\textrm{id}\times\varphi_{H};\Delta)

In this case, we still have the same upper bound:

\displaystyle\hbar(\varphi_{H})\leq h_{\textrm{top}}(\varphi_{H}).

Notably, these barcode entropies are finite because the topological entropy of a smooth map is known to be finite. Moreover, they established a nontrivial lower bound for
\hbar(\varphi_{H})
, showing that

\displaystyle\hbar(\varphi_{H})\geq h_{\textrm{top}}(\varphi_{H}|_{X})

whenever
X
is a compact
hyperbolic
invariant set of
\varphi_{H}
. This lower bound, in particular, shows that
\hbar(\varphi_{H})
is not always zero. As a consequence, when the ambient symplectic manifold is two-dimensional, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\hbar(\varphi_{H})=h_{\textrm{top}}(\varphi_{H}).

Following the introduction of barcode entropy in
[
3
]
, a variety of related notions have been developed in different Floer-theoretic and dynamical settings. A Morse-theoretic notion of barcode entropy was introduced in
[
13
]
, where it was shown to be comparable to the topological entropy of the geodesic flow. The framework was extended to symplectic cohomology of Liouville domains in
[
11
]
and the barcode entropy was shown to admit an upper bound in terms of the topological entropy of the Reeb flow on the boundary. This result was later complemented by a matching lower bound in
[
4
]
. In the original work
[
3
]
, no lower bound was obtained for the Lagrangian barcode entropy
\hbar(\varphi_{H};L)
. Such a lower bound was established later in
[
15
]
, further clarifying the dynamical significance of this invariant. More recently, analogous upper and lower bounds for barcode entropy have been obtained in the setting of wrapped Floer cohomology; see
[
9
,
10
]
.

Despite these developments, existing results on barcode entropy have primarily focused on absolute Floer-theoretic invariants. Relative symplectic cohomology occupies a fundamentally different position: it captures Floer-theoretic information that depends not only on the intrinsic geometry of a Liouville domain but also on how the domain is situated inside a larger symplectic manifold. This additional flexibility raises new conceptual questions about how persistence and entropy should reflect both ambient and boundary dynamics. The goal of this paper is to address this gap by developing a notion of barcode entropy for relative symplectic cohomology and by establishing a precise quantitative relation with the dynamics of the Reeb flow on the boundary.

1.2.
Main results

In this subsection, we state our main results. As indicated above, we will introduce the barcode entropy of the relative symplectic cohomology
SH_{M}(K)
. Our primary contribution is to establish an upper bound for this barcode entropy: it is bounded above by a constant multiple of the topological entropy of the Reeb flow. The constant depends on how the subset
K
sits inside the ambient symplectic manifold
M
, reflecting the genuinely relative nature of the invariant.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold and
K\subset M
be a compact subset. Varolgunes
[
24
]
introduced the relative symplectic cohomology
SH_{M}(K)
of
K
in
M
. This relative symplectic cohomology admits a persistence module structure under a suitable condition, which we now explain. A symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is called
symplectically aspherical
if
\omega|_{\pi_{2}(M)}=0
and
c_{1}(TM)|_{\pi_{2}(M)}=0
where
c_{1}(TM)
is the first Chern class of the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
. For our purposes, the subset
K\subset M
is required to be a
Liouville domain
. This means that
K
is a submanifold of
M
of codimension 0 with smooth boundary and there exists a vector field
X
on
K
such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega=\omega
. A key feature of a Liouville domain is that its boundary
\partial K
carries a natural contact structure: the contact form
\alpha
is given by the contraction of the symplectic form
\omega
with the vector field
X
, namely,
\alpha=\iota_{X}\omega|_{\partial K}
. For such a Liouville domain
K\subset M
, we say that the contact manifold
(\partial K,\alpha)
is
index-bounded
if for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the set of periods of Reeb orbits on
(\partial K,\alpha)
that are contractible in
M
and have Conley-Zehnder index
k
of
(\partial K,\alpha)
is bounded.

Following the definition of the barcode entropy of a persistence module given in
[
3
]
, we can define the
relative symplectic cohomology barcode entropy
and we denote it by
\hbar(SH_{M}(K))
. Informally, this quantity measures the exponential growth rate of the “not-too-short” bars in the barcode of the persistence module
SH_{M}(K)
: For
\epsilon>0
, let

b_{\epsilon}(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K)),\sigma)

be the number of bars of the truncation of the persistence module
SH_{M}(K)
at
\sigma
whose lengths are greater than or equal to
\epsilon
. Note that
b_{\epsilon}(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K)),\sigma)
is an increasing function of
\sigma
and a decreasing function of
\epsilon
. We define

\displaystyle\hbar_{\epsilon}(SH_{M}(K))=\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K)),\sigma),

and

\displaystyle\hbar(SH_{M}(K))=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\hbar_{\epsilon}(SH_{M}(K))

where
\log^{+}a=\max\{\log_{2}a,0\}
.

Our result is that the barcode entropy
\hbar(SH_{M}(K))
is bounded above by a constant multiple of the topological entropy
h_{\textrm{top}}(\varphi_{\alpha}^{t})
of the Reeb flow
\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}
on
(\partial K,\alpha)
, where the constant depends on the embedding of
K
inside
M
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold and
K\subset M
be a Liouville domain. Assume that
(M,\omega)
is symplectically aspherical and
(\partial K,\alpha)
is index-bounded. Then there exists a constant
C=C(M,K)>0
, depending on the pair
(M,K)
, such that

\displaystyle\hbar(SH_{M}(K))\leq C\,h_{\textrm{top}}(\varphi_{\alpha}^{1})

where
\varphi_{\alpha}^{t}
is the Reeb flow on
(\partial K,\alpha)
.

The constant
C(M,K)
in Theorem
A
arises from the choice of a collar neighborhood of
\partial K
symplectically embedded in
M
. Fixing such a collar of finite length, one considers Hamiltonian functions that are linear with respect to the radial coordinate on a subregion of the collar. The estimate in Theorem
A
involves the infimum of the slopes of such Hamiltonian functions, and the optimal bound is obtained by varying the collar length up to its maximal value allowed by the embedding. Consequently, the constant
C(M,K)
reflects the quantitative size of the embedding of
K
into
M
. Conceptually, this shows that the complexity of the persistence structure of relative symplectic cohomology is controlled by the dynamical complexity of the Reeb flow on the boundary of the Liouville domain. Unlike the symplectic cohomology of an isolated Liouville domain, the relative invariant retains information about the surrounding symplectic geometry near the boundary, highlighting its sensitivity to the chosen embedding of
K
into
M
.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
A
is that the barcode entropy
\hbar(SH_{M}(K))
is finite because the topological entropy of a smooth flow is known to be finite. Moreover, the growth of the number of not-too-short bars in
SH_{M}(K)
is at most exponential.

Organization of the paper.

Since the material presented in this paper encompasses several fields of mathematics, we begin in Section 2 by collecting the basic notions and facts from each subject that will be used throughout the paper. This preliminary section is not intended to be comprehensive; readers are encouraged to consult the references provided in each subsection for further details. In Section 3, we elaborate on the definition and structure of the relative symplectic cohomology of a Liouville domain. In Section 4, we formally define the relative symplectic cohomology barcode entropy,
\hbar(SH_{M}(K))
, and explore its relation to the previously established barcode entropy associated with a Hamiltonian function. The proof of the upper bound, Theorem
A
, is carried out in Section 5, where we first explain the key concepts and ingredients required for the argument.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
From persistence module theory

A concise review of persistence module theory will be presented below, with most of the exposition adapted from
[
7
,
18
]
. Before giving the definition of persistence module, let us fix a ground field
\mathbbm{k}
and we usually suppress this from our notation.

Definition 2.1
.

A
persistence module
is a pair
(V,\pi)
where
V
is a collection
\{V_{\tau}\}_{\tau\ \in{\mathbb{R}}}
of finite dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbbm{k}
and
\pi=\{\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}:V_{\tau_{1}}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}V_{\tau_{2}}\}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is a collection of linear maps from
V_{\tau_{1}}
to
V_{\tau_{2}}
for
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
satisfying the following:

(a)

For any
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\pi_{\tau\tau}=id_{V_{\tau}}
and for any
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq\tau_{3}
,
\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{3}}=\pi_{\tau_{2}\tau_{3}}\circ\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}
.

(b)

There exists a closed, bounded from below and nowhere dense subset
\textrm{Spec}(V,\pi)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, which is called the
spectrum
of
(V,\pi)
such that
\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}:V_{\tau_{1}}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}V_{\tau_{2}}
is an isomorphism whenever
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are in the same connected component of
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\textrm{Spec}(V,\pi)
.

(c)

For any
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
and any
\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau
sufficiently close to
\tau
, the map
\pi_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}:V_{\tau^{\prime}}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}V_{\tau}
is an isomorphism.

(d)

There exists
\tau_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
V_{\tau}=0
for
\tau\leq\tau_{0}
.

We will usually use the notation
V
to denote the persistence module
(V,\pi)
when it is clear from the context.

One crucial example of a persistence module is an interval persistence module. For an interval
I=(a,b],
where
-\infty<a<b\leq\infty
, the
interval persistence module

\mathbbm{k}I=(V,\pi)
is defined by

\displaystyle V_{\tau}=\begin{cases}\mathbbm{k}&\text{if}\,\,\tau\in I\\
0&\text{if}\,\,\tau\notin I,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}=\begin{cases}\textrm{id}_{\mathbbm{k}}&\text{if}\,\,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in I\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

A key fact in the theory of persistence module is the
normal form theorem
. Before stating it, we need the notion of equivalence between persistence modules. Let
(V,\pi)
and
(V^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
be two persistence modules. A
morphism

A:(V,\pi){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}(V^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
is a family of linear maps
\{A_{\tau}:V_{\tau}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}V^{\prime}_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}}
such that
A_{\tau_{2}}\circ\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}=\pi^{\prime}_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}\circ A_{\tau_{1}}
for all
\sigma\leq\tau
.

Two persistence modules
(V,\pi)
and
(V^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exist two morphisms

\displaystyle A:(V,\pi){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}(V^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,B:(V^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}(V,\pi)

such that
A\circ B:(V^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}(V^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
is the identity morphism and
B\circ A:(V,\pi){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}(V,\pi)
is also the identity morphism. Also, we can define the
direct sum

(V\oplus V^{\prime},\pi\oplus\pi^{\prime})
of two persistence module
V
and
V^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle(V\oplus V^{\prime})_{\tau}=V_{\tau}\oplus V^{\prime}_{\tau}\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,(\pi\oplus\pi^{\prime})_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}=\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}\oplus\pi^{\prime}_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}

and this definition extends in a straightforward manner to infinitely many persistence modules.

Theorem 2.2

(Normal form theorem)
.

Let
(V,\pi)
be a persistence module. Then there exists a countable collection
\{(I_{i},m_{i})\}_{i=1,2,3,\cdots}
of intervals
I_{i}
with multiplicity
m_{i}
such that

\displaystyle(V,\pi)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbbm{k}I_{i}\right)^{m_{i}}.

Moreover, this decomposition is unique up to a permutation, that is, to any persistence module
(V,\pi)
, there exists a unique multiset
\mathcal{B}(V,\pi)
consisting of intervals
I_{i}
with multiplicity
m_{i}
. This multiset
\mathcal{B}(V,\pi)
is called the
barcode
of the persistence module
V
and an element of
\mathcal{B}(V,\pi)
is called a
bar
of
(V,\pi)
.

We end this subsection by introducing a useful operation on persistence modules which will play an important role in the sequel. Let
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The
truncation

\text{tru}(V,\pi;\sigma)=(\text{tru}(V;\sigma),\text{tru}(\pi;\sigma))
of
(V,\pi)
is defined by

\displaystyle\text{tru}(V;T)_{\tau}=\begin{cases}V_{\tau}&\text{if}\,\,\tau<\sigma\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\text{tru}(\pi;\sigma)_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}=\begin{cases}\pi_{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}&\text{if}\,\,\tau_{2}<\sigma\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

2.2.
From Floer theory

This subsection reviews the fundamental aspects of Floer theory required in the sequel. Experienced readers may wish to proceed directly to the next section.

2.2.1.
Hamiltonian Floer cohomology

We begin by introducing the ground ring. The
Novikov field

\Lambda
is defined by

\displaystyle\Lambda=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}\ \rule[-9.95845pt]{0.28453pt}{25.60747pt}\ a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}},\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=\infty\right\}

where
T
is a formal variable. There is a
valuation map

\textrm{val}:\Lambda\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
given by

\displaystyle\textrm{val}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\min_{i}\{\lambda_{i}\mid a_{i}\neq 0\}\,&\text{if}\,\,x=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}\neq 0\\
\infty\,&\text{if}\,\,x=0.\end{cases}

For any
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define
\Lambda_{\geq r}=\textrm{val}^{-1}([r,\infty])
. In particular, we call

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\geq 0}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}\in\Lambda\ \rule[-9.95845pt]{0.28453pt}{25.60747pt}\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0\right\}

the
Novikov ring
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and let
H:S^{1}\times M\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Hamiltonian function on
M
. The
Hamiltonian vector field

X_{H}
of H is defined by

\displaystyle\iota_{X_{H}}\omega=dH.

We say a Hamiltonian function
H:S^{1}\times M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
nondegenerate
if every 1-periodic orbit
x
of
X_{H}
is nondegenerate, that is, for the Hamiltonian flow
\phi_{H}^{t}
of
H
, the map

(d\phi^{1}_{H})_{x(0)}:T_{x(0)}M{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}T_{x(1)}M=T_{x(0)}M

has no eigenvalue equal to 1. We denote the set of all nondegenerate contractible 1-periodic orbits of
X_{H}
by
\mathcal{P}(H)
. Let
x\in\mathcal{P}(H)
and
c_{x}
be a disk capping. The
action
of
(x,c_{x})
is defined by

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{H}(x,c_{x})=\int_{D^{2}}c_{x}^{*}\omega+\int_{S^{1}}H(t,x(t))dt.

We can associate to a pair
(x,c_{x})
an integer using the
Conley-Zehnder index
, which we denote by
\mu_{\text{CZ}}(x,c_{x})
. Define an equivalence relation on the set of pairs of a contractible 1-periodic orbit and its capping by

\displaystyle(x,c_{x})\sim(y,c_{y})\,\,\text{if and only if}\,\,x=y,\,\mathcal{A}_{H}(x,c_{x})=\mathcal{A}_{H}(y,c_{y})\,\,\text{and}\,\,\mu_{\text{CZ}}(x,c_{x})=\mu_{\text{CZ}}(y,c_{y}).

We denote the equivalence class of
(x,c_{x})
by
[x,c_{x}]
and denote the set of all equivalence classes by
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(H)
. The
Floer complex of
H
is defined by

\displaystyle CF(H)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}[x_{i},c_{x_{i}}]\ \rule[-9.95845pt]{0.28453pt}{25.60747pt}\ a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}},[x_{i},c_{x_{i}}]\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(H)\,\,\text{and}\,\,\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{H}([x_{i},c_{x_{i}}])=\infty\right\}.

The grading of
CF(H)
is given by the
Conley-Zehnder index
(See
[
20
]
), that is,

\displaystyle|[x,c_{x}]|=\mu_{\text{CZ}}([x,c_{x}]).

In particular, the
k
-th Floer complex
CF^{k}(H)
of
H
is given by

\displaystyle CF^{k}(H)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}[x_{i},c_{x_{i}}]\in CF(H)\ \rule[-9.95845pt]{0.28453pt}{25.60747pt}\ \mu_{\text{CZ}}([x,c_{x}])=k\right\}.

Fix a generic almost complex structure
J
on the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
. For
x,y\in\mathcal{P}(H)
, let
\pi_{2}(M,x,y)
be the set of homotopy classes of smooth maps from
{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
to
M
which are asymptotic to
x
and
y
at
-\infty
and
\infty
, respectively. For
[x,c_{x}],[y,c_{y}]\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(H)
and
A\in\pi_{2}(M,x,y)
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(H,J;[x,c_{x}],[y,c_{y}];A)
is the set of smooth maps
u:{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}M
satisfying the following.

•

The homotopy class of
u
represents
A\in\pi_{2}(M,x,y)
.

•

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}+J(u)\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-X_{H}(u)\right)=0
.

•

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}u(s,t)=x(t)
and
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}u(s,t)=y(t)
.

•

[y,c_{y}]=[y,c_{x}\,\#\,(-u)]=[y,c_{x}\,\#\,(-A)]
where
\#
denotes the connected sum.

Note that if
u\in\mathcal{M}(H,J;[x,c_{x}],[y,c_{y}];A)
, then
\mathcal{A}_{H}([x,c_{x}])\leq\mathcal{A}_{H}([y,c_{y}])
and
\mu_{\text{CZ}}([x,c_{x}])\leq\mu_{\text{CZ}}([y,c_{y}])
. The
Floer differential

d:CF^{*}(H){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}CF^{*+1}(H)
of the Floer complex
CF(H)
is given by

\displaystyle d[x,c_{x}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[y,c_{y}]\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(H)\\
A\in\pi_{2}(M,x,y)\end{subarray}}\#\mathcal{M}(H,J;[x,c_{x}],[y,c_{y}];A)T^{E_{\text{top}}(u)}[y,c_{y}]

where
\#\mathcal{M}(H;[x,c_{x}],[y,c_{y}];A)
is the virtual count of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(H;[x,c_{x}],[y,c_{y}];A)
(see
[
17
]
) and the
topological energy

E_{\text{top}}(u)
of a Floer trajectory
u
is given by

\displaystyle E_{\text{top}}(u)=\int_{S^{1}}H_{t}(y(t))dt-\int_{S^{1}}H_{t}(x(t))dt+\omega(A).

It can be easily shown that the topological energy is given by the difference of actions of two capped orbits connected by a Floer trajectory:

E_{\text{top}}(u)=\mathcal{A}_{H}([y,c_{y}])-\mathcal{A}_{H}([x,c_{x}]).

Since the Floer differential
d
satisfies
d\circ d=0
, the
Floer cohomology

HF(H)
of
H
is defined to be

\displaystyle HF^{*}(H)=H^{*}\left(CF(H),d\right).

The Floer cochain complex admits a filtration by the action functional. For each
L\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
, define

\displaystyle CF^{>L}(H)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}[x_{i},c_{x_{i}}]\in CF(H)\ \rule[-9.95845pt]{0.28453pt}{25.60747pt}\ \mathcal{A}_{H}([x_{i},c_{x_{i}}])>L\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i\right\}.

This subset
CF^{>L}(H)
of
CF(H)
is actually a subcomplex of
CF(H)
because the Floer differential increases the action. Therefore, we can define

\displaystyle HF^{>L,*}(H)=H^{*}(CF^{>L}(H),d).

For
L<L^{\prime}
, we further define the truncated Floer cohomology

\displaystyle HF^{(L,L^{\prime}],*}(H)=H^{*}\left(CF^{>L}(H)/CF^{>L^{\prime}}(H),\overline{d}\right)

where
\overline{d}
is the differential of
CF^{>L}(H)/CF^{>L^{\prime}}(H)
induced by
d
on the quotient complex..

For two Hamiltonian functions
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
with
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
, choose a monotone homotopy
H_{s}
of Hamiltonian functions from
H_{1}
to
H_{2}
. Then there exists a
continuation map

\displaystyle c^{H_{1},H_{2}}:CF(H_{1}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}CF(H_{2})

defined by the suitable count of Floer trajectories of
H_{s}
connecting the orbits of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. This map is also weighted by
T^{E_{\text{top}}(u)}
in a similar way that we define the Floer differential above. Every continuation map induces a map
HF(H_{1}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}HF(H_{2})
. Note that continuation maps increase the actions and respect the gradings, and hence the map

\displaystyle c^{H_{1},H_{2}}:CF^{>L}(H_{1}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}CF^{>L}(H_{2})

makes sense and this also induces a map
HF^{>L}(H_{1}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}HF^{>L}(H_{2})
. Of course, the continuation map induces a map
HF^{(L,L^{\prime}]}(H_{1}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}HF^{(L,L^{\prime}]}(H_{2})
.

The Floer cohomology
HF(H;\Lambda)
of
H
with coefficient in
\Lambda
is defined by

2.3.
From dynamical systems theory

In this subsection, we provide a summary of some facts from the theory of dynamical systems that we need throughout this paper. For a comprehensive exposition, see

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and let

be a continuous map. Define an increasing sequence

of metrics for

\displaystyle d_{\ell}^{f}(x,y)=\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell-1}d(f^{i}(x),f^{i}(y))

with itself

\displaystyle B_{f}(x,\epsilon,\ell)=\left\{y\in X\,\ \rule[-9.95845pt]{0.28453pt}{25.60747pt}\ \,d_{\ell}^{f}(x,y)<\epsilon\right\}.

\displaystyle h_{\textrm{top}}(f)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell}\log S_{d}(f,\epsilon,\ell).

Obviously, the topological entropy of the identity map is zero. The definition of the topological entropy for a flow is completely parallel. Let

be a continuous flow on

\displaystyle h_{\textrm{top}}(F)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log S_{d}(F,\epsilon,T).

•

h_{\textrm{top}}(F)=h_{\textrm{top}}(f^{1})

•

In fact, we will not use the definition of the topological entropy directly in this paper; instead, we will use the volume growth rate. Let

\displaystyle h_{\textrm{vol}}(f|_{Y})=\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell}\log\textrm{Vol}\left(\Gamma_{f^{\ell}|_{Y}}\right)

\tau-\epsilon_{i}\notin\textrm{Spec}(\partial K,\alpha)
because
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\epsilon_{i}=0
. Moreover, we obtain

\displaystyle CF^{>-\tau}(\sigma_{i}H)=CF^{>-\tau+\epsilon_{i}}(\sigma_{i}H).

In the case where
\tau\in\textrm{Spec}(\partial K,\alpha)
, choose a sequence
\{\sigma_{i}\}
such that
\tau
does not coincide with the action of any 1-periodic orbit of
\sigma_{i}H
. Assuming
\epsilon_{i}>0
is sufficiently small for each
i=1,2,3,\cdots
, we have

\displaystyle CF^{>-\tau}(\sigma_{i}H)=CF^{>-\tau+\epsilon_{i}}(\sigma_{i}H).

In either case, we have

\displaystyle CF^{>-\tau}(H_{i})=CF^{>-\tau}(\sigma_{i}H).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau}(H_{i})=\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau}(\sigma_{i}H)

as chain complexes. This completes the proof.

∎

To proceed further, we assume that
c_{1}(TM)|_{\pi_{2}(M)}=0
where
c_{1}(TM)
is the first Chern class of the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
. Then every Hamiltonian orbit has integer-valued Conley-Zehnder index so we can impose the
index-boundedness
assumption on
\partial K
. For a compact domain
K\subset M
with a contact type boundary, we say that
(\partial K,\alpha)
is
index-bounded
if for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the set of periods of Reeb orbits on
(\partial K,\alpha)
that are contractible in
M
and have Conley-Zehnder index
k
of
(\partial K,\alpha)
is bounded.

In the rest of this paper, we assume that
(M,\omega)
is a closed
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold which is symplectically aspherical, that is,
\omega|_{\pi_{2}(M)}=0
and
c_{1}(TM)|_{\pi_{2}(M)}=0
,
K\subset M
is a Liouville domain and
(\partial K,\alpha)
is index-bounded.

Under these assumptions, the relative symplectic cohomology
SH_{M}(K)
can be written in terms of lower orbits. We have seen that those lower orbits have negative actions. Moreover, if
x
is an upper orbit corresponding to a Reeb orbit
\gamma
, then we have seen from (
13
) that the action of
x
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{H}(x)\geq c_{H}-(1+3r_{1})\mathcal{A}(\gamma).

Since
\partial K
is index-bounded, for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, there exist
b(k)>0
such that
0<\mathcal{A}(\gamma)\leq b(k)
for any Reeb orbit
\gamma
on
(\partial K,\alpha)
of Conley-Zehnder index
k
. By (
10
), if a Hamiltonian orbit
x
has Conley-Zehnder index
k
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{H}(x)\geq c_{H}-(1+3r_{1})b(n-k).

Let
\{H_{i}\}
be a cofinal sequence of
\mathcal{H}_{K}^{\text{Cont}}
, that is,
H_{1}\leq H_{2}\leq H_{3}\leq\cdots
and

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{i}(t,p)=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if}\,\,p\in K\\
\infty&\textrm{if}\,\,p\in M\setminus K.\end{cases}

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}c_{H_{i}}=\infty
, we may assume that
c_{H_{i}}
is sufficiently large so that

c_{H_{i}}-(1+3r_{1})b(n-k)>0

for fixed
k
. Denote the set of upper orbits of
H_{i}
with Conley-Zehnder index
k
by
CF^{k}_{U}(H_{i})
.
Then
CF^{k}_{U}(H_{i})
is a subcomplex of
CF^{k}(H_{i})
because the Floer differential increases the action and every lower orbit has negative action. Define

\displaystyle CF^{k}_{L}(H_{i})=CF^{k}(H_{i})/CF^{k}_{U}(H_{i}).

Without loss of generality, we can choose
c_{H_{i}}>0
so that

\displaystyle c_{H_{i}}-(1+3r_{1})b\left(n-(k-1)\right)>0\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,c_{H_{i}}-(1+3r_{1})b(n-(k+1))>0

and hence
CF^{j}_{U}(H_{i})
and
CF^{j}_{L}(H_{i})
make sense for
j=k-1,k,k+1
.

For any
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded cochain complex
(C,d)
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define a cochain complex
(C^{\sim k},d)
by

\displaystyle\left(C^{\sim k}\right)^{i}=\begin{cases}C^{k}&\textrm{for}\,\,i=k-1,k,k+1\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that
k
-th cohomology of
(C^{\sim k},d)
is
H^{k}(C,d)
, that is,

(16)

\displaystyle H^{k}(C,d)=H^{k}(C^{\sim k},d).

Proposition 3.3
.

For each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let
\{H_{i}\}
be a cofinal sequence of
\mathcal{H}_{K}^{\text{Cont}}
described above. Then, for any
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K)\cong H^{k}\left(\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau}_{L}(H_{i})\right).

Proof.

Let us consider the following short exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{U}(H_{i})

\displaystyle{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i})

\displaystyle{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i})/CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{U}(H_{i})=CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}0.

Since the direct limit is an exact functor, we still have the exact sequence as follows:

\displaystyle 0{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{U}(H_{i}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}0.

Note that each
CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i})
is a free module over
\Lambda_{\geq 0}
generated by lower orbits of
H_{i}
with action greater than
-\tau
of Conley-Zehnder index
k-1,k
and
k+1
and hence it is a flat module over
\Lambda_{\geq 0}
. Since the direct limit of flat modules is known to be flat, we have

\displaystyle\text{Tor}_{1}^{\Lambda_{\geq 0}}\left(\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i}),\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}\right)=0

for each
r\geq 0
and therefore,

\displaystyle 0{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{U}(H_{i})\otimes\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}

\displaystyle{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i})\otimes\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}

\displaystyle{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i})\otimes\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}0

is still an exact sequence. For
0\leq r\leq r^{\prime}
, the projection

(17)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r^{\prime}}\to\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}

is surjective. For any
\Lambda_{\geq 0}
-module
A
, the map induced by (
17
)

A{\otimes_{\Lambda_{\geq 0}}}\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r^{\prime}}\to A{\otimes_{\Lambda_{\geq 0}}}\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}

induced by (
17
) is also surjective due to the right exactness of tensor product. Then by the Mittag-Leffler theorem for the inverse limit, the following sequence

\displaystyle 0{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varprojlim_{r\to 0}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{U}(H_{i})\otimes\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}

\displaystyle{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varprojlim_{r\to 0}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i})\otimes\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}

\displaystyle{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\varprojlim_{r\to 0}\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i})\otimes\Lambda_{\geq 0}/\Lambda_{\geq r}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}0

is also exact. By the definition of the completion (see Section
2.2
), we have the following exact sequence of complexes

(18)

\displaystyle 0{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{U}(H_{i}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i}){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}0.

Since it is proved in
[
8
]
that

•

(Lemma 5.3 of
[
8
]
)
\displaystyle\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{U}(H_{i})=0
, and

•

(Lemma 5.4 of
[
8
]
)
\displaystyle\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i})\cong\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i})
,

the short exact sequence (
18
) reduces to the isomorphism of complexes

(19)

\displaystyle\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i})\cong\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i}).

Then

\displaystyle SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K)

\displaystyle=H^{k}\left(\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau}(H_{i})\right)

Definition

\displaystyle=H^{k}\left(\widehat{\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}(H_{i})\right)

by

(
16
)

\displaystyle\cong H^{k}\left(\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau,\sim k}_{L}(H_{i})\right)

by

(
19
)

\displaystyle=H^{k}\left(\varinjlim_{i\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau}_{L}(H_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\textrm{by}\,\,\eqref{kth}.

This completes the proof.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
H
be a
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function and
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\displaystyle SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K)

\displaystyle\cong H^{k}\left(\varinjlim_{\sigma\to\infty}CF^{>-\tau}_{L}(\sigma H)\right)

\displaystyle\cong H^{k}\left(\varinjlim_{\sigma\to\infty}CF^{(-\tau,0]}(\sigma H)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\varinjlim_{\sigma\to\infty}HF^{(-\tau,0],k}(\sigma H).

Proof.

In view of the proof of Proposition
3.3
, it suffices to check that every upper orbit of
\sigma H
of Conley-Zehnder index
k-1,k
and
k+1
has positive action. Let
x
be such an orbit. Then by (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sigma H}(x)

\displaystyle\geq c_{\sigma H}-(1+3r_{1})\max\left\{b(n-k+1),b(n-k),b(n-k-1)\right\}.

Since
c_{\sigma H}=\sigma c_{H}
, this yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sigma H}(x)\geq\sigma c_{H}-(1+3r_{1})\max\left\{b(n-k+1),b(n-k),b(n-k-1)\right\}>0

for sufficiently large
\sigma>0
. The remainder of the argument follows verbatim from the proof of Proposition
3.3
, and we therefore omit the details. The second isomorphism follows from the fact that all lower orbits have negative action, while the final isomorphism uses the commutativity of taking direct limits with homology.

∎

Proposition
3.3
and Corollary
3.4
allow us to set

\displaystyle SH^{>-\tau}_{M}(K)\,\,\textrm{if}\,\,\tau\leq 0.

Also, if
x=(\gamma,r_{*})
is a lower orbit of
H
and has action greater than
-\tau
, then

\displaystyle-\tau

\displaystyle<-r_{*}h^{\prime}(r_{*})+h(r_{*})

\displaystyle\leq-r_{*}h^{\prime}(r_{*})+(r_{*}-1)h^{\prime}(r_{*})

)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{A}(\gamma)

Furthermore, in situations where the action window can be effectively controlled, the passage to the direct limit becomes unnecessary, as the corresponding Floer complexes already capture the desired information.

Theorem 3.5
.

For each integer
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, there exists a contact type
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function
H
such that

\displaystyle SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K)\cong HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(H)

for
0\leq\tau\leq s_{H}
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a contact type
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function satisfying the following condition: Choose a constant
c_{H}>0
sufficiently large so that the upper orbits of
H
of Conley-Zehnder index
k-1,k
and
k+1
have positive actions. As shown above, this choice is possible thanks to the index-boundedness of
(\partial K,\alpha)
together with the action estimate (
13
) for upper orbits. For
\sigma>1
, we shall prove that

(20)

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(H)\cong HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H)

for
0\leq\tau\leq s_{H}
. Fix
\tau\in[0,s_{H}]
. Then there exists a unique
r_{0}\in[1,1+r_{1}]
such that
A_{H}(r_{0})=a_{H}(\tau)
, that is,
h^{\prime}(r_{0})=\tau
. Consider a
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function
G
such that

•

H\leq G\leq\sigma H
,

•

G=H
on
[1,r_{0}]
, and

•

s_{G}=s_{\sigma H}=\sigma s_{H}
.

Note that
a_{H}(\tau)=a_{G}(\tau)
and
g^{\prime}(r_{0})=\tau
. The monotone homotopy from
H
to
G
induces an isomorphism

(21)

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(H)\cong HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(G)

Indeed, the generators of the two complexes coincide, and, by the maximum principle, every Floer trajectory connecting 1-periodic orbits contained in
K_{1+r_{1}}=K\cup\left(\partial K\times[1,1+r_{1}]\right)
has its image entirely in
K_{1+r_{1}}
.

Let

F_{\sigma,t}=(1-t)G+t(\sigma H)\,\,,\,\,0\leq t\leq 1

be the increasing linear homotopy from
G
to
\sigma H
. Observe that the slope of
F_{\sigma,t}
is independent of
t
; in fact,
s_{F_{\sigma,t}}=\sigma s_{H}

We first consider the case when
a_{H}(\tau)\notin\text{Spec}(H)
. Since
H=G
on
K\cup(\partial K\times[1,r_{0}])
and
h^{\prime}(r_{0})=\tau=g^{\prime}(r_{0})
, we have
a_{H}(\tau)=a_{G}(\tau)\notin\textrm{Spec}(G)
. Consider a function

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma,t}=a_{F_{\sigma,t}}\circ a_{G}^{-1}:[-i_{G},0]{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}[0,s_{G}]=[0,s_{F_{\sigma,t}}]{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}[-i_{F_{\sigma,t}},0].

According to
[
5
]
, this map
\phi_{\sigma,t}
sends the spectra of
G
to the spectra of
F_{s,t}
. More precisely,

\phi_{\sigma,t}(\textrm{Spec}(G))=[-i_{F_{\sigma,t}},0]\cap\textrm{Spec}(F_{\sigma,t}).

Thus we obtain

(22)

\displaystyle a_{F_{\sigma,t}}(\tau)=\phi_{\sigma,t}(a_{H}(\tau))\notin\textrm{Spec}(F_{\sigma,t})

for all
t\in[0,1]
. Since
a_{F_{\sigma,t}}(\tau)
is continuous with respect to
t
, for each
t\in[0,1]
, we can choose
\delta(t)>0
such that

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{F_{\sigma,t}}(\tau),0],k}(F_{\sigma,t})\cong HF^{(a_{F_{\sigma,t^{\prime}}}(\tau),0],k}(F_{\sigma,t})\,\,\textrm{for}\,\,|t-t^{\prime}|<\delta(t).

We can choose finitely many points
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{N}=1
such that

•

HF^{(a_{F_{\sigma,t_{i}}}(\tau),0],k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i}})\cong HF^{(a_{F_{\sigma,t^{\prime}}}(\tau),0],k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i}})
for
|t_{i}-t^{\prime}|<\delta(t_{i}):=\delta_{i}
, and

•

t_{i+1}-\delta_{i+1}<t_{i}+\delta_{i}
and hence there exists
\eta_{i}\in(t_{i+1}-\delta_{i+1},t_{i}+\delta_{i})
for each
i=0,1,2,\cdots,N-1
.

For notational convenience, let
I_{t}=(a_{F_{\sigma,t}}(\tau),0]
. Then by the choice of
\eta_{i}
, we have

(23)

\displaystyle HF^{I_{t_{i}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i}})\cong HF^{I_{\eta_{i}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i}}).

Therefore, for each
i=0,1,2,\cdots,N-1
, we obtain the following isomorphism

(24)

\displaystyle HF^{I_{t_{i}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i}})\cong HF^{I_{t_{i+1}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i+1}}).

Indeed, for each
i
,

\displaystyle HF^{I_{t_{i}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i}})

\displaystyle\cong HF^{I_{\eta_{i}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i}})

by

(
23
)

\displaystyle\cong HF^{I_{\eta_{i}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i+1}})

\displaystyle s_{F_{\sigma,t_{i}}}=s_{F_{\sigma,t_{i+1}}}

\displaystyle\cong HF^{I_{t_{i+1}},k}(F_{\sigma,t_{i+1}})

\displaystyle\textrm{by}\,\,\eqref{ibs}.

Consequently,

(25)

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(G)=HF^{I_{t_{0}},k}(F_{s,t_{0}})\cong\cdots\cong HF^{I_{t_{N}},k}(F_{s,t_{N}})=HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H)

where the intermediate isomorphisms follow from (
24
). Combining (
21
) and (
25
) together, we obtain

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(H)\cong HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H).

Now assume that
a_{H}(\tau)\in\textrm{Spec}(H)
. We can choose
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
a_{H}(\tau+\epsilon)\notin\textrm{Spec}(H)
for
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
. Then by the argument above, we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau+\epsilon),0],k}(H)\cong HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau+\epsilon),0],k}(\sigma H).

For
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, there exists a map

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau+\epsilon),0],k}(H){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}HF^{(a_{H}(\tau+\epsilon^{\prime}),0],k}(H)

induced by the inclusion. It follows that
\{HF^{(a_{H}(\tau+\epsilon),0],k}(H)\}_{\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]}
forms a direct system and

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{\epsilon\to 0}HF^{(a_{H}(\tau+\epsilon),0],k}(H)=HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(H).

The same reasoning still holds for
HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau+\epsilon),0],k}(\sigma H)
and therefore we have

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(H)\cong HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H).

To recapitulate, in any cases, we have the following isomorphism:

(26)

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{H}(\tau),0],k}(H)\cong HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H).

Moreover, the isomorphism (
26
) is constructed as a composition of intermediate maps, each of which is either an inclusion map or a continuation map.

For
\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}
, we have a map

\displaystyle HF^{(a_{\sigma_{1}H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma_{1}H){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}HF^{(a_{\sigma_{2}H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma_{1}H){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}HF^{(a_{\sigma_{2}H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma_{2}H)

where the first map is induced by inclusion and the second map is the continuation map. Note that these maps are compatible with the isomorphism (
26
) and that
\{HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H)\}_{\sigma>1}
forms a direct system. As shown in
[
5
]
, we have

\displaystyle a_{\sigma H}(\tau)\to-\tau\,\,\textrm{as}\,\,\sigma\to\infty

uniformly on compact sets in
[0,\infty)
. Using this limit together with Corollary
3.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{\sigma\to\infty}HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H)=SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K).

Finally, taking
\displaystyle\varinjlim_{\sigma\to\infty}
on both sides of (
26
) completes the proof.

∎

4.
Barcode entropies

4.1.
Definition

For a persistence module
V
, there exists a unique barcode
\mathcal{B}(V)
of
V
. See the normal form theorem (Theorem
2.2
). For any
\epsilon>0
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(V)=\{\textrm{bars in}\,\,\mathcal{B}(V)\,\,\textrm{of length greater than}\,\,\epsilon\}

and
b_{\epsilon}(V)
be the number of bars in
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(V)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle b_{\epsilon}(V)=|\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(V)|.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
V
be a persistence module. For any
\epsilon>0
, the
\epsilon
-barcode entropy

\hbar_{\epsilon}(V)
of
V
is defined to be

\displaystyle\hbar_{\epsilon}(V)=\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}(\textrm{tru}(V,\sigma)),

where
\log^{+}a=\max\{\log_{2}a,0\}
. The
barcode entropy

\hbar(V)
of
V
is defined by

\displaystyle\hbar(V)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\hbar_{\epsilon}(V).

It follows from Theorem
3.5
that the assignment

\tau\mapsto SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K)

defines a persistence module, whose structure maps

SH^{>-\tau_{1},k}_{M}(K){\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}SH^{>-\tau_{2},k}_{M}(K)

for
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
are induced by the inclusion maps. We denote this persistence module by
SH^{k}_{M}(K)
. Let
\mathcal{B}(SH^{k}_{M}(K))
be the barcode of the persistence module
SH^{k}_{M}(K)
. Since

\displaystyle SH^{>-\tau}_{M}(K)=\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K),

the barcode
\mathcal{B}(SH_{M}(K))
of
SH_{M}(K)
is defined to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(SH_{M}(K))=\coprod_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{B}(SH^{k}_{M}(K)).

Following Definition
4.1
, we define the
relative symplectic cohomology barcode entropy

\hbar(SH_{M}(K))
of relative symplectic cohomology
SH_{M}(K)
by

\displaystyle\hbar(SH_{M}(K))=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\hbar_{\epsilon}(SH_{M}(K))

where

\displaystyle\hbar_{\epsilon}(SH_{M}(K))=\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K),\sigma)).

We can also define the barcode entropy associated with a Hamiltonian function. For a contact type
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function
H
, the assignment

\tau\mapsto HF^{>-\tau}(H)

defines a persistence module and we denote this persistence module by
HF(H)
. We define the
Hamiltonian barcode entropy

\hbar(HF(H))
of
H
by

\displaystyle\hbar(HF(H))=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\hbar_{\epsilon}(HF(H))

where

\displaystyle\hbar_{\epsilon}(HF(H))=\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}(HF(\sigma H),\sigma i_{H})\right).

In view of Theorem
3.5
, we consider the persistence module
\tau\mapsto HF^{(-\tau,0]}(H)
, which we denote by
HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H)
. The
negative Hamiltonian barcode entropy

\hbar(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H))
of
H
is defined by

\displaystyle\hbar(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H))=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\hbar_{\epsilon}(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H))

where

\displaystyle\hbar_{\epsilon}(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H))=\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(\sigma H),\sigma i_{H})\right).

4.2.
Comparison of barcode entropies

We would like to compare the barcode entropies defined above in this subsection. The following theorem elucidates the interplay between the relative symplectic cohomology barcode entropy and the Hamiltonian barcode entropy.

Theorem 4.2
.

There exists a contact type
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function
H
such that for any
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\hbar_{\epsilon}(SH_{M}(K))\leq\hbar_{\epsilon}(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H))

and hence, taking
\epsilon\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle\hbar(SH_{M}(K))\leq\hbar(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H)).

Proof.

Choose a
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function
H
satisfying

(27)

\displaystyle c_{H}\geq 1+3r_{1}

Observe that the condition (
27
) only depends on
r_{1}
, that is, on the position of
K
inside
M
and it forces to
s_{H}
to be greater than 1. Indeed, if
s_{H}\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle 1+3r_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}

assumption

\displaystyle\leq 3r_{1}s_{H}

by (
7
)

\displaystyle\leq 3r_{1},

which yields a contradiction.

For each
\sigma>0
, the number of bars
b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K),\sigma)\right)
is finite and hence only finitely many grading degrees contribute nontrivially to it. Consequently, there exists a finite subset
A_{\sigma}
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\displaystyle b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K),\sigma)\right)=b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}SH^{k}_{M}(K),\sigma\right)\right)=\sum_{k\in A_{\sigma}}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}(SH^{k}_{M}(K),\sigma)\right).

To estimate
b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K),\sigma)\right)
, it suffices to consider Reeb orbits of
(\partial K,\alpha)
with periods less than
\sigma-\epsilon
. We claim that

(28)

\displaystyle SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K)\cong HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H)

for
0\leq\tau\leq\sigma\leq\sigma s_{H}
and
k\in A_{\sigma}
. To justify this claim, it is enough to show that the upper orbits of
\sigma H
have positive actions in view of the proof of Theorem
3.5
, Corollary
3.6
and Remark
3.7
. Let
x
be an upper orbit of
\sigma H
corresponding to a Reeb orbit
\gamma
with
\mathcal{A}(\gamma)<\sigma-\epsilon
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sigma H}(x)

\displaystyle\geq c_{\sigma H}-(1+3r_{1})\mathcal{A}(\gamma)

by (
13
)

\displaystyle>\sigma c_{H}-(1+3r_{1})(\sigma-\epsilon)

\displaystyle>0

\displaystyle\textrm{by the choice of }\,\,H,

and this proves the claim.

Recall from (
15
) that for
0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq\sigma\leq\sigma s_{H}=s_{\sigma H}
,

\displaystyle\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}\leq a_{\sigma H}(\tau_{1})-a_{\sigma H}(\tau_{2})\leq(1+r_{1})(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}),

and this together with the isomorphism (
28
) implies that

(29)

\displaystyle b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}SH^{k}_{M}(K),\sigma\right)\right)\leq b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}HF^{\textrm{neg},k}(\sigma H),-a_{\sigma H}(\sigma)\right)\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\hbar_{\epsilon}(SH_{M}(K))

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}(SH_{M}(K),\sigma)\right)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}SH^{k}_{M}(K),\sigma)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}HF^{\textrm{neg},k}(\sigma H),-a_{\sigma H}(\sigma)\right)\right)

by

(
29
)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}HF^{\textrm{neg},k}(\sigma H),\sigma i_{H}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-a_{\sigma H}(\sigma)\leq i_{\sigma H}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\sigma\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma}\log^{+}b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(\sigma H),\sigma i_{H}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\hbar_{\epsilon}(HF^{\textrm{neg}}(H)).

This completes the proof.

5.
Upper bound of the relative symplectic cohomology barcode entropy

5.1.
Lagrangian tomograph and Crofton’s inequality

We briefly discuss the idea of
Lagrangian tomograph
. Let
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
be a symplectic manifold and
L\subset M^{\prime}
be a Lagrangian submanifold. For a compact manifold
B
possibly with boundary, a smooth map

\Psi:B\times L{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}M

is called a
Lagrangian tomograph
of
L
if

•

it is a submersion,

•

the map
\Psi_{s}:L{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}M
given by
\Psi_{s}(x)=\Psi(s,x)
is an embedding for each
s\in B
,

•

the image
L_{s}=\Psi_{s}(L)
is a Lagrangian submanifold of
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
for each
s\in B
, and

•

every
L_{s}
is Hamiltonian isotopic to each other.

We call the Lagrangian submanifold
L
the
core
of the tomograph
\Psi
. The existence of such a Lagrangian tomograph can be found in Lemma 5.6 of
[
3
]
; A Lagrangian tomograph with its core
L
and
\dim B=d
exists if and only if
L
admits an immersion into
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Moreover, they proved the following version of
Crofton’s inequality
.

Theorem 5.1

(Crofton’s inequality)
.

Let
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
be a symplectic manifold and
L_{1},L_{2}\subset M^{\prime}
be Lagrangian submanifolds with
\dim L_{1}=\operatorname{codim}L_{2}
. Let
\Psi:B\times L_{1}{\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,}M
be a Lagrangian tomograph of
L_{1}
and
ds
be a smooth measure of
B
. Then there exists a constant
D>0
depending on
\Psi
,
ds
and a fixed metric on
M^{\prime}
but not depending on
L_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B}N(s)ds\leq D\,\textrm{Vol}(L_{2})

where
N(s)=|L_{s}\cap L_{2}|
and Vol denotes the volume.

Proof.

See Lemma 5.3 of
[
3
]
.

∎

The integration in Theorem
5.1
makes sense because
\Psi_{s}
is transverse to
L^{\prime}
for almost every
s\in B
and hence
N(s)=|L_{s}\cap L^{\prime}|
is locally constant function outside of a measure zero set of
B
.

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
A

In our setting, we consider the symplectic manifold
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=(M\times M,(-\omega)\oplus\omega)
, the Lagrangian submanifold
L=\Delta=\{(x,x)\in M\times M\mid x\in M\}
of
M^{\prime}
and the Lagrangian tomograph
\Delta_{s}
of the diagonal
\Delta
. For clarity and ease of reference, we restate Theorem
A
and proceed with its proof.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(M,K)>0
, depending on the pair
(M,K)
, such that

\displaystyle\hbar(SH_{M}(K))\leq C\,h_{\textrm{top}}(\varphi_{\alpha}).

Proof.

We use the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
. Let
\epsilon>0
. For sufficiently large
\sigma>0
, as shown in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we can choose a
K
-semi-admissible Hamiltonian function
H
such that the isomorphism

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}SH^{>-\tau,k}_{M}(K)\cong\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}HF^{(a_{\sigma H}(\tau),0],k}(\sigma H)

holds for
0\leq\tau\leq\sigma\leq\sigma s_{H}
. Consequently, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}SH^{k}_{M}(K),\sigma\right)\right)\leq b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}HF^{\textrm{neg},k}(\sigma H),-a_{\sigma H}(\sigma)\right)\right).

Let
\Delta
be the diagonal of
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=(M\times M,(-\omega)\oplus\omega)
and
\Gamma_{\sigma}=\Gamma_{\varphi_{H}^{\sigma}}
be the graph of the Hamiltonian diffeomorphism
\varphi_{\sigma H}=\varphi_{\sigma H}^{1}=\varphi_{H}^{\sigma}
of
\sigma H
, namely,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\sigma}=\left\{(x,\varphi_{\sigma H}(x))\in M\times M\ \rule[-9.95845pt]{0.28453pt}{25.60747pt}\ x\in M\right\}.

Then every element of
CF(\sigma H)
arises from an intersection point of the intersection
\Delta\cap\Gamma_{\sigma}
of the diagonal
\Delta
and the graph
\Gamma_{\sigma}
. Recall from the proof of Theorem

it suffices to consider only the lower orbits of
\sigma H
. This implies that

\displaystyle b_{\epsilon}\left(\textrm{tru}\left(\bigoplus_{k\in A_{\sigma}}HF^{\textrm{neg},k}(\sigma H),-a_{\sigma H}(\sigma)\right)\right)\leq\left|(\Delta\cap\Gamma_{\sigma})\cap(K_{1+r_{1}}\times K_{1+r_{1}})\right|.

of

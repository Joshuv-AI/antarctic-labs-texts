Validation and refinement of the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma treated with definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT: a Chinese multi-institutional retrospective study
Authors: Yuanji Xu, Jiao Li, Xin Wang, Lina Zhao, Kuaile Zhao, Wenbin Shen, Wencheng Zhang, Honglei Luo, Qifeng Wang, Jie Li, Yun Chen, Jingyuan Wen, Zhunhao Zheng, Yaqi Song, JianChao Lu, Yu Lin, Fei Zheng, Wenji Xue, Mingqiu Chen, Long-Qi Chen, Zefen Xiao, Junqiang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study evaluated the 8th edition American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and developed an improved staging framework using automated recursive partitioning analysis (autoRPA).MethodsThis retrospective study included 2773 ESCC patients treated with definitive intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) or chemo-IMRT across 8 Chinese centers (2001–2019). Kaplan‒Meier curves and log-rank tests were used to assess overall survival (OS). AutoRPA-derived stage groupings were optimized via the revised T/N criteria. The proposed staging was compared with the 8th edition AJCC staging via hazard discrimination, consistency, sample balance, and predictive accuracy.ResultsThe 3-year, and 5-year rates for the entire cohort were 43.5%, and 34.0%, respectively. The AJCC T4a/T4b stages exhibited overlapping OS curves, prompting their consolidation into a single T4 stage. While the AJCC N2 and N3 stages showed overlapping OS curves, supraclavicular lymph node (SLN) metastasis independently predicted worse OS than N2, with outcomes similar to those of N3. Location-based SLN classification further refined nodal staging, with cervical esophageal-SLN metastasis classified as N1, upper thoracic-SLN metastasis as N2, and middle or lower thoracic-SLN metastasis as N3, yielding distinct OS stratification. The autoRPA-derived staging outperformed the 8th edition AJCC staging in hazard consistency, sample balance, and predictive accuracy, with RPA-I exhibiting distinctly sharper OS curves than other stages.ConclusionCombining T4a/T4b and SLN subclassification enhanced prognostic precision in ESCC, with the autoRPA staging demonstrating superior hazard consistency, sample balance, and predictive accuracy compared to the 8th edition AJCC staging, thereby guiding therapeutic strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13014-025-02758-w.

Introduction

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally, particularly in East Asian populations, where its incidence is significantly higher than that of esophageal adenocarcinoma [1]. Accurate staging plays a pivotal role in guiding therapeutic decisions and prognostication in ESCC. The 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system introduced several refinements over its predecessor, including modifications in T and N classifications and separate stage groupings for different histological subtypes and treatment strategies [2]. However, despite these updates, critical limitations persist. Notably, the AJCC 8th edition does not fully incorporate the distinct biological behavior of supraclavicular lymph node (SLN) metastasis or adequately stratify patients receiving non-surgical therapies, such as intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) or concurrent chemo-radiotherapy (CCRT) [3]. Moreover, the system’s clinical validation is largely based on surgical cohorts from Western populations, which may not be directly applicable to Asian ESCC patients who are typically managed primarily with nonoperative approaches [4]. These limitations highlight the need for evidence-based refinement of the staging system, which is specifically tailored to patients treated with definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT, using robust, multi-institutional datasets.

Several studies have evaluated the prognostic performance of the AJCC 8th edition staging system in ESCC patients treated with various modalities. Rice et al. recommended the 8th edition AJCC clinical staging (cTNM) for ESCC through global data-driven analysis and expert consensus but eliminated the T4 subclassification (T4a/T4b) because of overlapping survival curves and a lack of prognostic discrimination between subgroups, undermining the prognostic accuracy of the T4 subclassification in cTNM [5]. Other investigations, such as those by Chen et al. [6], Yamasaki et al. [7], and Ning et al. [8], have underscored the prognostic ambiguity surrounding N-stage classification, particularly among the N2 and N3 groups, where survival differences were inconsistent or statistically insignificant. Moreover, the role of SLN metastasis remains controversial: while traditionally classified as distant metastasis (M1), especially in the era of radical surgical treatment [9], increasing evidence suggests that SLN involvement in ESCC may behave more like regional nodal disease rather than systemic spread and thus warrants reevaluation within the nodal staging framework, especially in the treatment of definitive radiotherapy [10–12]. Compounding these issues is the lack of dynamic, data-driven approaches to refine staging, such as machine learning or recursive partitioning analysis (RPA), which offer the potential to improve patient stratification based on real-world outcomes rather than static anatomic criteria [13]. Despite these findings, few large-scale, prospective, multi-institutional validation studies have focused on non-surgical cohorts of ESCC.

Although previous research has critically examined components of the AJCC 8th edition staging system in ESCC, most studies are limited by a single-center design, small sample sizes, or surgical cohorts, rendering their conclusions less generalizable to radiotherapy-dominant populations. In particular, the prognostic validity of the current T4a/T4b distinction and the treatment of SLN metastasis as a distant disease remain contentious, with substantial implications for staging accuracy and treatment planning. Few studies have explored the integration of topographical SLN location into the N classification system based on a non-surgical staging system for ESCC, and even fewer have tested alternative staging systems using machine learning approaches at the national or multi-institutional scale [10]– [11, 14]. Furthermore, performance comparisons between the AJCC staging system and refined data-driven models such as automated recursive partitioning analysis (autoRPA) have been inadequately explored in the context of definitive (chemo-)radiotherapy. Thus, there is a pressing need for a comprehensive, multicenter evaluation of the AJCC 8th edition using a robust dataset that reflects contemporary treatment modalities and incorporates modern analytical tools [15]. This gap highlights the need for prospective validation efforts that extend beyond conventional anatomic staging and utilize outcome-based stratification to enhance clinical practice.

To address these limitations, the present multi-institutional retrospective study aimed to validate and refine the AJCC 8th edition staging system for ESCC patients treated with definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT via CT-based clinical staging data. The findings of this study are expected to provide valuable evidence to inform future revisions of the current staging system.

Methods

Patient selection and exclusion criteria

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Fujian Cancer Hospital (Approval No. K2025-262-01). 2,773 patients with ESCC who received definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT at eight national medical centers between September 2001 and May 2019 were retrospectively analyzed. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to treatment, and all patient data were anonymized for analysis.

Patients meeting the following inclusion criteria were enrolled: histologically confirmed ESCC; life expectancy ≥ 3 months; comprehensive pretreatment evaluation, including thoracic contrast-enhanced CT for clinical staging and eligibility assessment for definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2; clinical stage TanyNanyM0 or M1 disease limited to SLN metastasis; absence of postradiotherapy salvage surgery; and adequate follow-up data for survival analysis.

The exclusion criteria included ESCC with distant metastasis (excluding SLN involvement), concurrent primary malignancies, lack of pretreatment contrast-enhanced CT imaging, multisegmental ESCC, insufficient clinical documentation, or IMRT delivered with a definitive radiation dose < 50 Gy.

Treatment

All patients underwent definitive IMRT. Among the 2,773 patients, 1,436 (51.8%) received elective nodal irradiation, whereas 1337 (48.2%) were treated with involved-field irradiation. A median radiation dose of 60 Gy (30 fractions) was prescribed for the gross tumor volume (GTV), with radiation planning based on the contouring of the GTV, clinical target volume (CTV), and organs at risk (OARs). Dose constraints for targets and OARs adhered to protocols described in prior studies [6, 16]. Radiation prescriptions varied among participating centers and reflected evolving practices over the study period. The median total dose was 60 Gy (30 fractions), which was consistent with the historical standard of care for ESCC in many Chinese tertiary centers, guided by contemporary national recommendations such as the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines and the 2010 guideline draft by the Esophageal Carcinoma Cooperative Group of the Chinese Medical Association [16].

2,129 patients (76.8%; 2,129/2,773) received chemo-IMRT. Among these patients, 1,554 (72.9%) were administered platinum-based intravenous chemotherapy (e.g., cisplatin or carboplatin). An additional 303 patients (14.3%) received single-agent chemotherapy, including paclitaxel, cisplatin, tegafur, or capecitabine (administered as oral or intravenous regimens on a weekly or cyclic basis). The remaining 272 patients (12.8%) had unspecified chemotherapy regimens due to incomplete documentation.

Clinical staging

Two board-certified thoracic radiologists independently reassessed the baseline computed tomography (CT) imaging data. Discordant interpretations were resolved through Delphi-based adjudication to ensure diagnostic concordance. All the clinical records and imaging findings were systematically integrated into a centralized repository, enabling precise restaging according to the AJCC 8th edition criteria [2], with a particular emphasis on anatomical landmarks and nodal categorization.

Follow-up and statistical analysis

Postdiagnosis surveillance intervals were defined as the period from the date of ESCC diagnosis to the earliest date of death or last contact with the patients. The median follow-up duration was 24.8 months (IQR: 0.6–232.9 months). Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the time of pathological confirmation until death, with censoring applied for surviving patients at the final follow-up evaluation. Surveillance frequency included clinical assessments every 3 months during the first 2 postradiotherapy years, biannual evaluations for years 3–5, and annual reviews thereafter.

Statistical analyses were executed via IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 25.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). OS probabilities were derived using the Kaplan-Meier methodology, with between-group differences evaluated using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazard models (both univariate and multivariate) were used to identify variables independently associated with OS, and hazard ratios (HRs) along with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Staging system performance was benchmarked against established criteria [17] via an open-source analytical platform (http://rpa.renlab.org/), as previously described [13]. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P-value < 0.05.

Results

Patient baseline characteristics and survival outcomes

The study cohort was assembled between September 2001 and May 2019 and comprised 3,509 patients with ESCC who received definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT treatment across eight tertiary referral centers. Following the application of the exclusion criteria (Fig. 1), 2,773 patients (median age, 65 years; interquartile range [IQR], 25–95 years) were included in the final analysis. Baseline demographics and clinical features, including sex, ECOG performance status (≤ 2), tumor staging (TNM classification), SLN metastasis status, AJCC 8th edition stage groupings, and treatment modality (chemo-IMRT in 76.8% of cases), are summarized in Table 1. The cohort demonstrated a male predominance (2,139 males [77.1%] vs. 634 females [22.9%]).

As of the last follow-up, 881 patients (31.8%) remained alive, and 1892 patients (68.2%) had died. Recurrence patterns included locoregional relapse (695 patients [25.0%]), distant metastasis (461 patients [16.6%]), and concurrent relapse-metastasis (261 patients [9.4%]). Leading causes of mortality were primary/local recurrence (323 cases), distant metastasis (528 cases), combined relapse-metastasis (210 cases), treatment-related complications/non-tumor comorbidities (281 cases), and unknown factors (550 cases). The 3-year, 5-year, and 10-year OS rates for the entire cohort were 43.5%, 34.0%, and 25.1%, respectively.

Refinement of the T classification

Validation of the prognostic validity of the 8th edition AJCC T staging system for ESCC necessitated evaluation of heterogeneity across T stages. Analysis revealed distinct 5-year OS curves for the T1-T3 and T4a stages (all P < 0.001), whereas the T4a and T4b stages exhibited substantial overlap (P = 0.180), indicating a lack of prognostic distinction (Fig. 2A). This prognostic heterogeneity within the original T4 category (T4a/T4b) contradicted the AJCC’s assumption of linear risk progression.

Following the consolidation of T4a and T4b into a unified T4a category, well-separated OS curves for all T stages (T1, T2, T3, and revised T4a) were observed, with significant differences between most stage comparisons (P < 0.001), except for the borderline significance between T1 and T2 (P = 0.057) (Fig. 2B). Therefore, we recommend merging the T4a and T4b stages into a single category to increase the prognostic accuracy.

Figure 3C further illustrates the prognostic implications of SLN metastasis across different esophageal segments in comparison with N stages. For cervical esophageal (CE) SLN metastasis, the 5-year OS curve closely matched that of the N1 group (P > 0.05) and was significantly different from that of the N0 group (P < 0.05). In the upper thoracic (UT) segment, SLN metastasis overlapped with N2 (P > 0.05) but remained clearly distinct from both the N0 and N1 groups (all P < 0.01). Notably, the survival outcomes of the middle/lower thoracic (MT/LT) SLN metastasis group were nearly identical to those of the N3 group (P > 0.05) but were significantly different from those of the N0, N1, and N2 groups (all P < 0.001). Based on these findings, we categorized CE-SLN metastasis as N1, UT-SLN metastasis as N2, and MT/LT-SLN metastasis as N3 to achieve optimal prognostic stratification. Figure 3D shows that the revised 5-year OS curves for the N0, N1, N2, and N3 categories are clearly separate, with statistically significant differences between each adjacent stage (all P < 0.05). Therefore, we recommend reclassifying SLN metastasis as follows: CE-SLNs as N1, UT-SLNs as N2, and MT/LT-SLNs as N3 to improve prognostic accuracy.

Comparison of the performance of the proposed edition and the current 8th edition

Based on the autoRPA staging system, Fig. 5A clearly shows separation among the proposed stage groups, with stage RPA-I showing statistically significant superiority over all the other groups (P < 0.001). Stage RPA-II demonstrated a better prognosis than both stages RPA-III and IVa did (both P < 0.001), whereas stage RPA-III was associated with significantly better survival than stage RPA-IVa (P = 0.002). According to the current 8th edition of the AJCC staging system, the curves demonstrated a stepwise decrease in OS rates across the stages. Statistical comparisons revealed significant differences between consecutive stages: II vs. I (P = 0.013), III vs. II (P < 0.001), IVa vs. III (P < 0.001), and IVb vs. IVa (P = 0.022). To compare the two staging systems systematically, normalized scores and ranks were computed via autoRPA, with detailed values provided in an interactive table (Table S1). The tool also generates a radar chart to visualize performance across four key metrics. Overall, the proposed RPA staging system outperforms the 8th edition of the AJCC system in terms of hazard consistency, outpatient prediction, and sample balance; however, it shows slightly lower performance in hazard discrimination (Fig. 5B). Discussion

This large-scale, multi-institutional retrospective study assessed the prognostic accuracy of the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system in 2,773 ESCC patients treated with definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT and proposed a refined staging system incorporating autoRPA. The principal findings can be summarized as follows. First, the current AJCC T4a and T4b classifications showed no significant prognostic distinction, and merging these classifications into a unified T4a category yielded improved stage separation. Second, SLN metastasis emerged as an independent adverse prognostic factor, with outcomes comparable to those of N3 rather than distant metastasis. When stratified by anatomical location, cervical, upper thoracic, and middle/lower thoracic, SLN involvement aligned with the N1, N2, and N3 classifications, respectively, allowing for more granular and prognostically meaningful nodal staging. Third, the proposed autoRPA staging framework demonstrated superior performance across key metrics, including hazard consistency, sample balance, and predictive accuracy, compared with the AJCC 8th edition, suggesting its potential clinical utility in optimizing risk stratification and therapeutic decision-making for non-surgical ESCC patients.

The findings of this study corroborate and extend earlier concerns regarding the limitations of the AJCC 8th edition in the context of non-surgical management of ESCC. Prior studies have reported that the T4a/T4b classification lacks discriminative prognostic power, particularly among patients receiving definitive radiotherapy [6, 12]. Our analysis, involving a large multicenter cohort of 2,773 ESCC patients treated with definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT, reinforces this observation, demonstrating that the survival curves for T4a and T4b patients were statistically indistinguishable (P = 0.180), suggesting that, contrary to the AJCC assumption of a stepwise increase in tumor aggressiveness, the anatomical subdivision between T4a and T4b does not translate into meaningful prognostic separation when surgery is not performed. Furthermore, by consolidating the T4a and T4b categories into a unified T4a group, we observed significantly improved stratification among all T stages, with clearly demarcated 5-year OS curves across the T1-T4a categories (P < 0.001). This finding suggests that simplifying T4 sub-staging not only enhances the statistical modeling of survival but also provides more practical guidance for clinical decision-making for ESCC patients treated with definitive IMRT or chemo-IMRT.

Similarly, previous research has highlighted the inconsistent survival outcomes between the N2 and N3 groups under the AJCC 8th edition, raising concerns regarding the current nodal classification system for ESCC, particularly in the context of definitive radiotherapy [6]. In our study, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that while significant prognostic differences were evident between the N0, N1, and N2 groups (all P < 0.05), the survival curves for N2 and N3 patients were not statistically distinguishable (P = 0.375), indicating a lack of discriminatory power between these advanced nodal stages (Fig. 3B). These findings suggest that the biological behavior and treatment responsiveness of N2 and N3 diseases may be more similar than previously assumed when radiotherapy is the primary modality, challenging the granularity of the current classification system.

In addition to N-stage overlap, our study addressed the ambiguous status of SLN metastasis, which the AJCC currently designates as an M1 disease. This classification has been controversial, as multiple studies have reported that SLN involvement in ESCC, particularly among patients with thoracic primary tumors, often behaves more like a regional nodal disease than distant metastasis [10–12, 18]. Our multivariate Cox analysis confirmed that SLN metastasis was an independent negative prognostic factor (HR = 1.332, 95% CI: 1.185–1.498). Notably, its survival profile varied depending on the anatomical location of the primary tumour and the SLN itself. Specifically, when SLN involvement was treated as M1, its survival curve overlapped substantially with that of the N3 group (P = 0.987) but remained clearly distinct from that of the N0-N2 categories (Fig. 3B). These findings provide further support for the hypothesis that SLN metastasis does not have the same prognostic implications as visceral organ metastasis and that anatomical proximity and lymphatic drainage patterns play a critical role in SLN behaviour.

Furthermore, when SLN metastasis was categorized topographically based on the segment of the esophagus involved—cervical (CE), upper thoracic (UT), or middle/lower thoracic (MT/LT)—a clear gradient of survival outcomes emerged. Patients with CE-SLN involvement had 5-year OS rates similar to those with N1 disease (P >0.05). In contrast, patients with UT-SLN and MT/LT-SLN involvement were more closely related to the N2 and N3 groups, respectively (Fig. 3C). These results are highly consistent with the findings of Xu et al. [11], who reported that cervical or upper thoracic SLN metastasis was associated with a better prognosis than middle or lower thoracic SLN metastasis was and argued for a staging scheme that incorporates SLN topography on the basis of analysis of definitive chemo-radiotherapy for esophageal cancer. By adopting a revised N classification model that designates the CE-SLN as N1, the UT-SLN as N2, and the MT/LT-SLN as N3, we achieved more consistent and discriminatory OS stratification across all nodal stages, as illustrated in Fig. 3D (all P < 0.05). Thus, in radiotherapy-based treatment paradigms, particularly those involving elective nodal irradiation or involved-field IMRT, the biological and prognostic behaviour of SLN metastasis aligns more closely with regional disease than with distant disease. Our data strongly support reclassifying SLN metastasis according to both anatomical location and primary tumour segment, as this yields superior prognostic resolution and may better inform clinical decision-making.

Moreover, the adoption of the autoRPA platform represents a notable methodological innovation in the domain of prognostic modeling for ESCC. While machine learning-assisted stratification has gained considerable traction across oncology disciplines, its application to large, non-surgical, and radiotherapy-dominant ESCC cohorts has been limited [19, 20]. Prior studies have demonstrated the feasibility of decision tree-based recursive partitioning analysis in cancers such as oropharyngeal cancer [21] and nasopharyngeal carcinoma [15], but few have applied such tools at a national scale across multiple treatment centers with harmonized imaging, staging, and follow-up protocols. The present study bridges this gap by integrating revised, biologically rationalized T and N classifications with a validated, data-driven autoRPA framework, thus enabling the construction of a robust, hierarchical staging model informed by real-world clinical outcomes.

An evaluation of model performance across the four Groome criteria—hazard consistency, hazard discrimination, explained variation (outcome prediction), and balance of stage distribution—demonstrated that the autoRPA system outperformed the AJCC 8th edition in three of the four domains (Fig. 5B). While the AJCC system showed slightly better hazard discrimination, likely owing to its coarse groupings and linear stage definitions, the autoRPA model achieved higher scores in hazard consistency, outcome prediction, and stage balance, suggesting that data-driven models, while perhaps less intuitive for visual stratification, offer improved fidelity in reflecting the nonlinear and multidimensional nature of cancer progression, especially in nonoperative settings where anatomical boundaries are less predictive of outcome than treatment response and tumor biology are.

This study makes several substantial contributions to the current understanding of ESCC staging. First, it offers one of the most comprehensive and statistically robust validations of the AJCC 8th edition in non-surgical ESCC populations, leveraging a large dataset derived from eight leading oncology centers across China. Second, it provides a detailed critique and refinement of the AJCC staging framework, proposing empirically justified modifications to the T and N categories that reflect real-world clinical outcomes rather than relying solely on anatomical assumptions. The reclassification of SLN metastasis into distinct N categories based on anatomical region is a novel approach with immediate clinical relevance. Third, the application of autoRPA to generate an optimized staging system based on observed survival data introduces a scalable, transparent, and clinically interpretable tool that can be readily adopted or further validated in future studies. The decision tree model not only improves hazard consistency and sample balance but also offers clear clinical utility by grouping patients into stages with meaningfully distinct outcomes, thereby enhancing treatment stratification and improving patient care. Finally, the study contributes a valuable resource for future prospective trials aiming to revise or augment the AJCC system, particularly in radiotherapy-focused treatment settings prevalent in Asia.

Despite its strengths, this study has several limitations that merit consideration. First, its retrospective design, although multi-institutional and large in scale, is inherently subject to selection bias, incomplete data, and heterogeneity in treatment protocols across centers. While standardization efforts (e.g., a review of CT images and the consistent application of the AJCC criteria) were implemented, residual inconsistencies may still influence the generalizability of the findings. Second, while our proposed autoRPA model demonstrated superior performance in the internal validation cohort comprising patients from eight participating centers in China, it lacks validation in an independent external dataset. The model’s performance may be influenced by region-specific diagnostic and treatment practices. Therefore, the generalizability of our findings to populations outside the training dataset, especially in different geographical regions or healthcare systems, requires further investigation. Future studies are planned to validate the autoRPA model in international cohorts to confirm its robustness and broad clinical utility. Third, the dataset lacked detailed molecular or genetic data, which are increasingly recognized as essential in precision oncology. The integration of biomarkers, such as PDL1 status [22], circulating tumor DNA [23], or radiomic signatures [24], may further refine staging and prediction models in future iterations. Finally, the proposed SLN-based N subclassification, while biologically plausible and supported by survival data, has yet to be externally validated in populations outside East Asia, particularly those undergoing different treatment regimens such as trimodal therapy.

Future research should prospectively validate the proposed staging modifications and RPA-based stratification in randomized clinical trials or large-scale registries. The integration of radiogenomic and artificial intelligence-based tools may also enable even more personalized staging frameworks. Moreover, international collaborations will be crucial to assess whether the refined staging system holds predictive value across diverse ethnic, geographic, and treatment contexts. Ultimately, such refinements could be incorporated into future editions of the AJCC staging manual, aligning staging systems more closely with real-world clinical outcomes and therapeutic decision-making.

Conclusions

By merging T4a and T4b and incorporating SLN anatomical location into nodal staging for the 8th edition AJCC staging system of ESCC patients treated with definitive IMRT or Chemo-IMRT, we significantly improved prognostic discrimination. The autoRPA-derived staging model demonstrated superior hazard consistency, sample balance, and predictive accuracy over the AJCC 8th edition, offering a more effective framework to guide personalized treatment strategies.

Quasi-stationary distribution for kinetic SDEs with low regularity coefficients
Nicolas Champagnat, Tony Lelièvre, Mouad Ramil, Julien Reygner, Denis Villemonais

Abstract
We consider kinetic SDEs with low regularity coefficients in the setting recently introduced in [ 6 ] . For the solutions to such equations, we first prove a Harnack inequality. Using the abstract approach of [ 5 ] , this inequality then allows us to prove, under a Lyapunov condition, the existence and uniqueness (in a suitable class of measures) of a quasi-stationary distribution in cylindrical domains of the phase space. We finally exhibit two settings in which the Lyapunov condition holds: general kinetic SDEs in domains which are bounded in position, and Langevin processes with a non-conservative force and a suitable growth condition on the force. Mathematics Subject Classification. 35P05, 82C31, 47B07, 60H10. Keywords. Langevin process, quasi-stationary distribution, Harnack inequality.



1
Introduction

In statistical physics, the evolution of a molecular system at some fixed temperature
T
is often modeled by the Langevin process:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}q_{t}=M^{-1}p_{t}\mathrm{d}t,\\
&\mathrm{d}p_{t}=F(q_{t})\mathrm{d}t-\gamma M^{-1}p_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\gamma\mathrm{k}T}\mathrm{d}B_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
q_{t}
(resp.
p_{t}
) is the position (resp. momentum) vector of the particles at time
t\geq 0
. If
N
is the number of particles then
(q_{t},p_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
where
d=3N
. Additionally,
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a symmetric positive definite matrix (the mass matrix),
\gamma>0
is the friction coefficient,
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the interaction force between particles, and
\mathrm{k}
is the Boltzmann constant. Most often, the force
F
is conservative i.e. there exists a potential function
V
such that
F=-\nabla V
.

The sampling of long time trajectories of (
1
) is fundamental to understand and model the successive transitions between metastable states of a molecular system. Such sampling procedures have numerous
applications in biology, chemistry and materials science
[
24
,
19
]
. The main numerical challenge comes from the long time scales required to compute such transitions. In this regard, several methods have been developed. Some of them
[
16
]
rely on the existence of a quasi-stationary distribution (QSD) for the metastable states of the system, which corresponds to the long-time equilibrium reached by the system when it is trapped in a metastable state. More precisely, for a given domain
D
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and the associated exit time
\tau_{\partial}=\inf\{t>0:(q_{t},p_{t})\not\in D\}
, a probability measure
\nu
with support in
D
is a QSD if and only if, for any positive
t
, for any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{\nu}((q_{t},p_{t})\in A,t<\tau_{\partial})=\nu(A)\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\nu}(t<\tau_{\partial}).

It is standard, see
[
7
,
14
]
, that starting from the QSD, the exit time is exponentially distributed and independent of the exit point, and this can be used to sample the first exit event faster in wall-clock time using parallel in time algorithm. This motivates the two following questions: the first one concerns the existence of a unique QSD for the considered system and the second is related to the attainability of this QSD, namely the long-time convergence to this unique QSD for the process conditioned to remain in the domain.

In the recent literature, these questions have been successfully studied for processes of the form (
1
) (and variations thereof) in domains of the form

D=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\mathcal{O}
is a smooth open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We shall refer to such domains as
cylindrical
. In particular,
[
18
,
13
]
employ a spectral approach which relies on the interpretation of QSDs as the principal eigenvector of the infinitesimal generator associated with (
1
), complemented with suitable boundary conditions on
\partial D

[
17
]
. A key technical point in this approach is to obtain compactness, or quasi-compactness, of the associated semigroup, see also
[
8
,
2
,
12
,
11
,
3
]
. On the other hand, a more probabilistic approach to the study of QSDs was developed in
[
5
]
, it essentially rests on the combination of a Harnack inequality and a Lyapunov condition.

The initial motivation of this article is thus to apply the approach from
[
5
]
to the Langevin process (
1
) in cylindrical domains. We shall actually work in the more general setting of kinetic SDEs, of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}q_{t}=p_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
&\mathrm{d}p_{t}=F(q_{t},p_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(q_{t},p_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Indeed, up to a standard change of variables
[
20
, Remark 3.37]
, which reduces the mass matrix
M
to the identity of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Langevin process (
1
) is a particular case of (
2
). Moreover, recent results from
[
6
]
will also allow us to address coefficients
F:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with low regularity.

The precise definition of our setting, together with the statement of our main results, are presented in Section
2
. The rest of the article is then organized as follows: the Harnack inequality is proved in Section
3
, the application of the results from
[
5
]
is detailed in Section
4
, and two examples of settings in which a Lyapunov function can be explicitly constructed are presented in Section
5
.

2
Main results

The first main result of this work, Theorem
2.3
, is a Harnack inequality for the solution to the kinetic SDE (
2
) in (a local-in-position version of) the low-regularity setting of
[
6
]
. It is introduced in Section
2.1
. The second main result, Theorem
2.5
, establishes that in the setting of Theorem
2.3
and under a supplementary Lyapunov condition, the solution to the kinetic SDE (
2
) admits a unique QSD in cylindrical domains. It is detailed in Section
2.2
. Last, in Section
2.3
, we provide two examples of settings in which the Lyapunov condition can be established explicitly, which implies that the conclusion of Theorem
2.5
holds.

2.1
Harnack inequality in the low regularity setting

Let
d\geq 1
. We use the generic notation
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
for pairs
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, and similarly the solution to (
2
) is denoted by
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(q_{t},p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

We denote by
|\cdot|
the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and use the notation
|x|=|q|+|p|
for
x=(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. We denote by
\|\cdot\|
the operator norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and by
\|g\|_{\infty}
the supremum norm of a (possibly vector-valued) function
g
.

Our main assumptions on the SDE (
2
) are the following.

Assumption (A1)

(Drift coefficient)
.

The vector field
F:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is measurable and satisfies, for any bounded set
\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},|x-x^{\prime}|\leq 1}|F(x)-F(x^{\prime})|<\infty.

Assumption (A2)

(Diffusion coefficient)
.

The matrix field
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is measurable, and for any bounded set
\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

•

there exist
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

c_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi\cdot\sigma\sigma^{T}(x)\xi\leq c_{2}|\xi|^{2};

•

there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
c_{3}>0
such that for all
x=(q,p),x^{\prime}=(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(x^{\prime})\|\leq c_{3}(|q-q^{\prime}|^{\alpha/3}+|p-p^{\prime}|^{\alpha}).

Assumption (A3)

(Well-posedness of (
2
))
.

For any
x=(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, there exists a unique weak solution
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(q_{t},p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to (
2
) starting from
x
.

Moreover, denoting by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
the probability measure under which
X_{0}=x
:

1.

under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
, for any
t>0
, the random variable
X_{t}
has a density
p(t;x,\cdot)
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
;

2.

for any
t>0
and almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the function
x\mapsto p(t;x,y)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

Notice that the continuity in
x
of the transition density implies, by Scheffé’s lemma, that the Markov process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has the strong Feller property.

To present a particular case in which Assumption
(A3)
holds, we introduce the following
global-in-position
versions of Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
.

Assumption (A1-glob)

(Drift coefficient)
.

The vector field
F:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is measurable and satisfies

c_{0}:=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},|x-x^{\prime}|\leq 1}|F(x)-F(x^{\prime})|<\infty.

Remark 2.1

(Assumption
(A1-glob)
and linear growth)
.

If
F
satisfies Assumption
(A1-glob)
, then there exist
a,b\geq 0
such that

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},\qquad|F(x)|\leq a+b|x|.

Assumption (A2-glob)

(Diffusion coefficient)
.

The matrix field
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is measurable, and:

•

there exist
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

c_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi\cdot\sigma\sigma^{T}(x)\xi\leq c_{2}|\xi|^{2};

•

there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
c_{3}>0
such that for all
x=(q,p),x^{\prime}=(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(x^{\prime})\|\leq c_{3}(|q-q^{\prime}|^{\alpha/3}+|p-p^{\prime}|^{\alpha}).

The setting of Assumptions
(A1-glob)
and
(A2-glob)
is the one introduced by Chaudru de Raynal, Menozzi, Pesce and Zhang in
[
6
]
, where it is argued to be optimal, in terms of regularity, for the well-posedness of (
2
) in the weak sense. In particular, the next statement, which will be used in the proofs of our main results, follows from
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Proposition 2.2

(Well-posedness of (
2
) under global conditions)
.

Let Assumptions
(A1-glob)
and
(A2-glob)
hold. Then Assumption
(A3)
holds.

Moreover, for any
t>0
, there exists
C(t)
which only depends on
F
and
\sigma
through the quantities
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\alpha
from Assumptions
(A1-glob)
and
(A2-glob)
, as well as
a,b
from Remark
2.1
, such that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and
s\in(0,t]
, the density
p(s;x,\cdot)
of
X_{s}
under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
satisfies

\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},\qquad p(s;x,y)\leq\frac{C(t)}{s^{2d}}.

(3)

We now let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a nonempty set which satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption (O)
.

The set
\mathcal{O}
is open, connected, and its boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
has Lebesgue measure
0
.

The condition that the Lebesgue measure of
\partial\mathcal{O}
be
0
may be understood as a minimal regularity assumption on the boundary of
\mathcal{O}
.

Given
\mathcal{O}
, let us define the cylindrical domain
D:=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Under Assumption
(A3)
, let
\tau_{\partial}
be the first exit time from
D
of (
2
), i.e.

\tau_{\partial}:=\inf\{t>0:X_{t}\notin D\}\in[0,\infty].

The first main result of this work is the following Harnack inequality.

Theorem 2.3

(Harnack inequality)
.

Let Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(A3)
and
(O)
hold. For any measurable subset
A
of
D
, define the function
u_{A}:[0,\infty)\times D\to[0,1]
by

\forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times D,\qquad u_{A}(t,x):=\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial},X_{t}\in A).

(4)

Then, for any compact set
K\subset D
,
\epsilon>0
and
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{K,\epsilon,T}>0
such that for any measurable set
A\subset D
and for any
t\geq\epsilon
,

\sup_{x\in K}u_{A}(t,x)\leq C_{K,\epsilon,T}\inf_{x\in K}u_{A}(t+T,x).

(5)

We will also make use of the following corollary of Theorem
2.3
.

Corollary 2.4

(Case of subsets with nonempty interior)
.

In the setting of Theorem
2.3
, if the set
A
has nonempty interior, then
u_{A}(t,x)>0
for all
t>0
,
x\in D
. As a consequence, for any compact set
K\subset D
, for all
t>0
,
\inf_{x\in K}u_{A}(t,x)>0
.

The proofs of Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.4
are detailed in Section
3
. They essentially follow the lines of the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 2.15]
, which is stated for the Langevin process (
1
), with smooth coefficients, and in a domain
\mathcal{O}
which is bounded. Therefore, the extension of the proof requires to take care of the more general form of the kinetic SDE (
2
), the possible low regularity of the coefficients
F
and
\sigma
, and the possible unboundedness of the domain
\mathcal{O}
.

2.2
Lyapunov condition and QSD

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
, we now address the existence and uniqueness of a QSD for the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in the set
D
. Our approach is based on the general criteria obtained in
[
5
]
. They essentially require to combine the Harnack inequality from Theorem
2.3
with the following Lyapunov condition for the infinitesimal generator of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, defined by

\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}=p\cdot\nabla_{q}+F\cdot\nabla_{p}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma\sigma^{T}:\nabla^{2}_{p},

(6)

where
:
is the Frobenius product applied to matrices.

Assumption (H)

(Lyapunov function)
.

For all
\lambda>0
, there exist a constant
c_{\lambda}>0
, a function
\phi_{\lambda}:D\to[1,\infty)
which is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
in
q
and
\mathcal{C}^{2}
in
p
, and a bounded subset
D_{\lambda}\subset D
closed in
D
such that

\sup_{x\in D_{\lambda}}\phi_{\lambda}(x)<\infty,

and

\forall x\in D,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}\phi_{\lambda}(x)\leq-\lambda\phi_{\lambda}(x)+c_{\lambda}\mathbb{1}_{D_{\lambda}}(x).

Using the Harnack inequality from Theorem
2.3
we are able to prove that under Assumption
(H)
, the criteria from
[
5
, Theorem 3.5]
are satisfied for the process (
2
). These criteria ensure the existence of a unique QSD in the family

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda_{0}}=\{\text{$\mu$ probability measure on $D$}:\exists\lambda>\lambda_{0},\exists p\in(1,\lambda/\lambda_{0}),\mu(\phi_{\lambda}^{1/p})<\infty\}

for some
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
, as well as the long-time convergence of the law of the process conditioned to remain in
D
towards the QSD (for initial conditions in
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda_{0}}
), as stated in the next theorem, which is our second main result.

Theorem 2.5

(QSD)
.

Let Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(A3)
,
(O)
and
(H)
hold. There exists
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
such that
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has a unique QSD
\mu
in
D
in the family
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda_{0}}
. This measure actually satisfies
\mu(\phi_{\lambda}^{1/p})<\infty
for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
and
p\in(1,\lambda/\lambda_{0})
.

Moreover, for any
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
and
p\in(1,\lambda/\lambda_{0})
, there exist
C,\beta>0
, such that, for all
x\in D
and all measurable
f:D\to\mathbb{R}
with
|f|\leq\phi_{\lambda}^{1/p}
,

\left|\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{0}t}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial}\}}f(X_{t})\right]-\varphi(x)\mu(f)\right|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\beta t}\phi_{\lambda}^{1/p}(x),

(7)

where
\varphi:D\to\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is a positive function which does not depend on
\lambda,p
.

Theorem
2.5
is proved in Section
4
.

Remark 2.6
.

We emphasize that (
7
) implies that for any
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
and
p\in(1,\lambda/\lambda_{0})
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that, for any probability measure
\nu
on
D
,

\forall t\geq 0,\qquad\|\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(X_{t}\in\cdot|\tau_{\partial}>t)-\mu(\cdot)\|_{TV}\leq C^{\prime}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta t}\frac{\nu(\phi_{\lambda}^{1/p})}{\nu(\varphi)}.

(8)

This follows for example from the arguments of
[
4
, Thm. 2.2]
.

Remark 2.7
.

When
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\tau_{\partial}=\infty
almost surely. In this situation,
\lambda_{0}=0
and Theorem
2.5
provides the existence of a stationary distribution, which is unique in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Similar results in the conservative case were obtained in
[
22
]
.

Remark 2.8
.

Note that it is not necessary to assume that
(H)
holds true for any
\lambda>0
. It is only needed for some
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
. We kept the simpler version
(H)
because it is hard in practice to compute bounds for
\lambda_{0}
and it simplifies the analysis.

2.3
Examples

We now provide examples of situations in which the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
are satisfied. The first one is the case of bounded-in-position domains.

Theorem 2.9

(QSD in a bounded domain)
.

Let
F
and
\sigma
satisfy Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(A3)
, and let
\mathcal{O}
be a
bounded
domain satisfying Assumption
(O)
. Then Assumption
(H)
is satisfied with a
bounded
Lyapunov function
\phi_{\lambda}
, so that
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has a unique QSD
\mu
on
D
, and (
8
) holds.

This statement generalises the result of
[
18
, Theorems 2.14 and 2.22]
, where the existence and uniqueness of the QSD in a (smooth) bounded-in-position domain is proved for the Langevin process (
1
), with smooth coefficients. The method of proof is however drastically different, as the argument of
[
18
]
is based on the compactness of the (absorbed) semigroup of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, which follows from a quantitative Gaussian upper bound derived on the transition density of this process. The main technical contribution of the proof of Theorem
2.9
is thus the derivation of a simple bounded Lyapunov function. It is detailed in Section
5.1
.

When the domain
\mathcal{O}
is no longer assumed to be bounded, the construction of a Lyapunov function becomes more dependent on the particular expression of
F
and
\sigma
. In
[
13
]
, Guillin, Nectoux and Wu address the case of a force field
F
which takes the form

F(q,p)=-\nabla V(q)-\gamma(q,p)p,

for a potential function
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, a nonconstant friction matrix
\gamma(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and a diffusion matrix
\sigma
which writes

\sigma(q,p)=\Sigma(q,p)I_{d},

for a non-degenerate and bounded scalar-valued diffusion coefficient
\Sigma(q,p)
. Under suitable regularity and growth conditions on
V
,
\gamma
and
\Sigma
, they construct a Lyapunov function
\mathsf{W}_{1}(q,p)
, which is essentially a perturbation of
\exp(aH(q,p))
, for some
a>0
and where
H(q,p)=V(q)+\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}
is the Hamiltonian of the system. This construction provides, for any
p>1
, the existence and uniqueness of a QSD in the class of probability measures
\nu
such that
\nu(\mathsf{W}_{1}^{1/p})<\infty
. It can be checked that in this setting, the function
\mathsf{W}_{1}
indeed satisfies our Assumption
(H)
, so Theorem
2.5
also applies to recover similar results.

In Section
5.2
, we consider a different setting, which is studied in
[
15
]
. We come back to the case of the Langevin process (
1
), with mass matrix
M=I_{d}
for simplicity, and an interaction force which decomposes as

F(q)=-\nabla U(q)-\ell(q),

(9)

where
U\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
U\geq 0
, and there exist constants
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in(0,\gamma)
such that for all
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left(\nabla U(q)+\ell(q)\right)\cdot q\geq\alpha\left(|q|^{2}+U(q)\right)+\frac{|\ell(q)|^{2}}{\beta^{2}}.

(10)

We only assume that the vector field
\ell
is measurable, and notice that then Assumption
(A1)
holds as soon as
\ell
is bounded on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. On the other hand, since we are dealing with the Langevin process (
1
), for which the diffusion matrix
\sigma=\sqrt{2\gamma\mathrm{k}T}I_{d}
is constant, Assumption
(A2)
obviously holds true. In this setting, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.10

(QSD for the Langevin process)
.

Consider the Langevin process (
1
), with a mass matrix
M=I_{d}
and an interaction force which satisfies (
9
)–(
10
) for a measurable vector field
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is bounded on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If Assumption
(A3)
holds true, then for any set
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies Assumption
(O)
, the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
possesses a QSD
\mu
in
D=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and (
8
) holds with the function
\phi_{\lambda}
defined in Section
5.2
. Moreover, there exists
n\geq 0
such that
\mu
is the unique QSD in the class of probability measures
\nu
such that
\nu((U(q)+|q|^{2}+|p|^{2})^{n})<\infty
.

The Lyapunov function employed to show that Assumption
(H)
is satisfied is derived from results in
[
15
]
. It is a polynomial of a modification of the Hamiltonian
U(q)+\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}
of the system. As a consequence, it yields uniqueness of a QSD in a class of probability measures which requires less integrability than classes obtained with Lyapunov functions which are exponential of (modifications of) the Hamiltonian.

3
Proof of Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.4

As is indicated at the end of Section
2.1
, the proof of Theorem
2.3
is based on
[
17
, Theorem 2.15]
, which is stated for the Langevin process (
1
), with smooth coefficients, and in a domain
\mathcal{O}
which is bounded. Therefore, the main issues to handle are:

(i)

the more general form of the kinetic SDE (
2
),

(ii)

the possible low regularity of the coefficients
F
and
\sigma
,

(iii)

the possible unboundedness of the domain
\mathcal{O}
.

We address the point (iii) by a localization procedure which is first detailed in Section
3.1
. To handle the point (ii), we also need to introduce a regularization of
F
and
\sigma
, which is the object of Sections
3.3
and
3.4
. In these two sections, we resort to proofs which are detailed in
[
17
]
for the Langevin process, and only emphasize the parts which need to be modified to take into account the point (i).

The regularization step actually allows to prove the Harnack inequality for a function of the form
u_{f}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial}\}}f(X_{t})]
, with a continuous function
f
, rather than
u_{A}(t,x)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\tau_{\partial},X_{t}\in A)
. A preliminary result regarding
u_{f}
is established in Section

once it has been established for

Throughout Section

3.1
Localization of
F
and
\sigma

Let us fix a compact subset
K
of
D
. As a consequence of Assumption
(O)
, it is easily checked that there exists an open, bounded, connected and
\mathcal{C}^{2}
set
\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
K\subset D^{\flat}:=\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
on the one hand, and
\overline{\mathcal{O}^{\flat}}\subset\mathcal{O}
on the other hand. Under Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
, there exist functions
F^{\flat}:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma^{\flat}:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
which coincide with
F
and
\sigma
on
\overline{D^{\flat}}
, and which satisfy Assumptions
(A1-glob)
and
(A2-glob)
. We denote by
(X^{\flat}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the weak solution to (
2
) with coefficients
F^{\flat}
,
\sigma^{\flat}
provided by Proposition
2.2
.

In this step, we show that, as long as the process
X^{\flat}_{t}
remains in
D^{\flat}
, it has the same law as
X_{t}
. To proceed, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, we denote by
\mathrm{P}_{x}
(resp.
\mathrm{P}_{x}^{\flat}
) the law, on the space of trajectories
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, of the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(resp.
(X^{\flat}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
) with initial condition
x
, provided by Proposition
2.2
. As usual, the set
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is endowed with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra induced by the topology of locally uniform convergence. Next, we define the functional
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}
on
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{2d})
by

\forall\mathrm{x}=(\mathrm{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{2d}),\qquad\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}(\mathrm{x}):=\inf\{t>0:\mathrm{x}_{t}\not\in D^{\flat}\},

and denote by
\mathcal{G}_{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}}
the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{2d})
generated by the application
\mathrm{x}\mapsto(\mathrm{x}_{t\wedge\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}(\mathrm{x})})_{t\geq 0}
.

We may now state and prove the main result of this step.

Lemma 3.1

(Localization of
F
and
\sigma
)
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the probability measures
\mathrm{P}_{x}
and
\mathrm{P}_{x}^{\flat}
coincide on the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}_{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}}
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. We construct a coupling of weak solutions to (
2
) with initial condition
x
with coefficients
F^{\flat}
,
\sigma^{\flat}
and with coefficients
F
,
\sigma
as follows. Let
{\cal C}:={\cal C}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be equipped with the canonical filtration. On the space
{\cal C}^{2}
, we denote by
(X^{\flat},X^{\prime}):=(X^{\flat}_{t},X^{\prime}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the canonical process and we consider the probability measure
\mathrm{Q}
under which
X^{\flat}
has distribution
\mathrm{P}^{\flat}_{x}
and, conditionally on
X^{\flat}
and given
\{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}<+\infty\}
,
X^{\prime}
has distribution
\mathrm{P}_{X^{\flat}_{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}}}
, where we denote
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}:=\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}(X^{\flat})
for convenience. Given
\{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}=+\infty\}
, we take an arbitrary distribution for
X^{\prime}
, say
\mathrm{P}_{0}
. Then, under
\mathrm{Q}
,
X^{\flat}
is a weak solution to (
2
) with coefficients
F^{\flat}
,
\sigma^{\flat}
and initial condition
x
. In addition, it is easily checked that the process

X_{t}:=\begin{cases}X^{\flat}_{t}&\text{if $t\leq\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}$,}\\
X^{\prime}_{t-\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is a weak solution to (
2
) with coefficients
F
,
\sigma
and initial condition
x
, so it has law
\mathrm{P}_{x}
. Moreover, for any event
A\in\mathcal{G}_{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}}
, it is clear that
X\in A
if and only if
X^{\flat}\in A
, which implies that
\mathrm{P}_{x}(A)=\mathrm{P}^{\flat}_{x}(A)
.
∎

Since the application
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}
is
\mathcal{G}_{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}}
-measurable, Lemma
3.1
shows that
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}(X)
and
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}(X^{\flat})
have the same law. Therefore, from now on, we shall use the notation
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}
to refer indifferently to either
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}(X)
or
\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}(X^{\flat})
.

The following technical results on the small time behavior of the process, which will be used in the sequel, are corollaries of Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2

(Localization estimates)
.

With the notation introduced above,

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(t\geq\tau^{\flat}_{\partial})=0,

(11)

and, for any
\delta>0
,

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-x|\geq\delta\right)=0.

(12)

Proof.

We first establish short-time growth estimates on the process
X^{\flat}
. For any
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle|q^{\flat}_{s}-q^{\flat}_{0}|\leq\int_{0}^{s}|p^{\flat}_{r}|\mathrm{d}r\leq\int_{0}^{s}|p^{\flat}_{r}-p^{\flat}_{0}|\mathrm{d}r+s|p^{\flat}_{0}|,

and by Remark
2.1
,

\displaystyle|p^{\flat}_{s}-p^{\flat}_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{s}|F^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})|\mathrm{d}r+\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{s}(a^{\flat}+b^{\flat}(|q^{\flat}_{r}|+|p^{\flat}_{r}|))\mathrm{d}r+\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|

\displaystyle\leq b^{\flat}\int_{0}^{s}(|q^{\flat}_{r}-q^{\flat}_{0}|+|p^{\flat}_{r}-p^{\flat}_{0}|)\mathrm{d}r+s\left(a^{\flat}+b^{\flat}(|q^{\flat}_{0}|+|p^{\flat}_{0}|)\right)+\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|.

As a consequence, for
X^{\flat}_{0}=x\in K
,

\displaystyle|X^{\flat}_{s}-x|

\displaystyle=|q^{\flat}_{s}-q^{\flat}_{0}|+|p^{\flat}_{s}-p^{\flat}_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq(b^{\flat}+1)\int_{0}^{s}|X^{\flat}_{r}-x|\mathrm{d}r+C_{K}s+\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|,

where
C_{K}:=\sup\{|p|+a^{\flat}+b^{\flat}(|q|+|p|),(q,p)\in K\}
, so by Gronwall’s lemma, for any
t\geq 0
,

\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\flat}_{s}-x|\leq\mathrm{e}^{(b^{\flat}+1)t}\left(C_{K}t+\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|\right).

(13)

We now use the estimate (
13
) to prove (
11
). Let us set

\delta:=\inf\{|q^{\prime}-q|,(q,p)\in K,q^{\prime}\in\partial\mathcal{O}^{\flat}\}>0,

so that, for any
t\geq 0
and
x=(q,p)\in K
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t\geq\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|q^{\flat}_{s}-q|\geq\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\flat}_{s}-x|\geq\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mathrm{e}^{(b^{\flat}+1)t}\left(C_{K}t+\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|\right)\geq\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{(b^{\flat}+1)t}\left(C_{K}t+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|\right]\right),

where we have used (
13
) and the Markov inequality. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the Doob maximal inequality and Ito’s isometry then yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{4\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\flat}(q^{\flat}_{r},p^{\flat}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right|^{2}\right]}\leq 2\sqrt{tc_{2}^{\flat}},

where
c_{2}^{\flat}
is given by Assumption
(A2-glob)
for
\sigma^{\flat}
. Since the right-hand side does not depend on
x
and vanishes with
t
, the conclusion follows.

We finally prove (
12
). To proceed, we fix
t\geq 0
,
x\in K
,
\delta>0
, and write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-x|\geq\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-x|\geq\delta,t<\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\right)+\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-x|\geq\delta,t\geq\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\right).

On the one hand, by (
11
),

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-x|\geq\delta,t\geq\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\right)=0.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-x|\geq\delta,t<\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\flat}_{s}-x|\geq\delta,t<\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\flat}_{s}-x|\geq\delta\right),

and by the same arguments as in the proof of (
11
), the right-hand side vanishes with
t
, uniformly in
x\in K
.
∎

3.2
Regularization of
\mathds{1}_{\{x\in A\}}

For Sections
3.2
to
3.4
, we let
f:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous and bounded function, and set

\forall t\geq 0,\quad x\in D,\qquad u_{f}(t,x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial}\}}f(X_{t})\right].

(14)

Only in Section
3.5
we will replace
f
with the possibly discontinuous function
\mathds{1}_{\{x\in A\}}
. For now, working with a continuous function
f
enables us to prove the following statement.

Lemma 3.3

(Continuity of
u_{f}
)
.

The function
u_{f}
defined in (
14
) is continuous on
[0,\infty)\times D
.

Proof.

We shall proceed differently to show the continuity of
u_{f}
at points
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times D
, depending on whether
t=0
or
t>0
. However, both arguments rely on the preliminary result that for any compact subset
K\subset D
,

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}\leq t)=0.

(15)

This claim immediately follows from (
11
), since, with the notation of Section
3.1
,
\tau_{\partial}\geq\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}
.

Continuity at
(t,x)
with
t=0
.
We fix a compact set
K\subset D
and show that

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{x\in K}|u_{f}(t,x)-f(x)|=0.

Since
f
is continuous on
D
, this shows in particular that the function
u_{f}
, defined on
[0,\infty)\times D
, is continuous at any point of the form
(0,x)
, with
x\in D
. To proceed, we first write, for any
t\geq 0
,
x\in K
,

|u_{f}(t,x)-f(x)|\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial}\}}|f(X_{t})-f(x)|\right]+\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}\leq t),

and observe that by (
15
), the second term vanishes with
t
, uniformly in
x\in K
. Now let
\epsilon>0
and let
\alpha>0
small enough such that the compact set
K_{\alpha}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}:\mathrm{d}(y,K)\leq\alpha\}
is included in
D
. Since
f
is uniformly continuous on the compact set
K_{\alpha}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x,y\in K_{\alpha}
, if
|x-y|\leq\delta
then
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\epsilon
, and without loss of generality,
\delta
can be taken smaller than
\alpha
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial}\}}|f(X_{t})-f(x)|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial},|X_{t}-x|\leq\delta\}}|f(X_{t})-f(x)|\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial},|X_{t}-x|>\delta\}}|f(X_{t})-f(x)|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+2\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(|X_{t}-x|>\delta),

so the conclusion follows from (
12
).

Continuity at
(t,x)
with
t>0
.
We now fix
t>0
,
x\in D
and show that
u_{f}
is continuous at
(t,x)
. To proceed, we let
(t_{n},x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of elements of
[0,\infty)\times D
which converges to
(t,x)
, and write

|u_{f}(t_{n},x_{n})-u_{f}(t,x)|\leq|u_{f}(t_{n},x_{n})-u_{f}(t_{n},x)|+|u_{f}(t_{n},x)-u_{f}(t,x)|.

First,

u_{f}(t_{n},x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{t_{n}<\tau_{\partial}\}}f(X_{t_{n}})\right].

Since the trajectory of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is continuous, almost surely, and
f
is continuous,
f(X_{t_{n}})\to f(X_{t})
, almost surely. Moreover, by Assumptions
(O)
and
(A3)
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(t=\tau_{\partial})\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(q_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{O})=\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t;x,y)\mathrm{d}y=0,

therefore
\mathds{1}_{\{t_{n}<\tau_{\partial}\}}\to\mathds{1}_{\{t<\tau_{\partial}\}}
, almost surely. Since
f
is bounded, we deduce from the dominated convergence theorem that

\lim_{n\to\infty}|u_{f}(t_{n},x)-u_{f}(t,x)|=0.

Now let us fix
s\in(0,t)
. For
n
large enough, we have
t_{n}\geq s
and thus, by the Markov property,

u_{f}(t_{n},x_{n})=\mathbb{E}_{x_{n}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{s<\tau_{\partial}\}}u_{f}(t_{n}-s,X_{s})\right],\qquad u_{f}(t_{n},x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{s<\tau_{\partial}\}}u_{f}(t_{n}-s,X_{s})\right],

so that

|u_{f}(t_{n},x_{n})-u_{f}(t_{n},x)|\leq\left|\mathbb{E}_{x_{n}}\left[u_{f}(t_{n}-s,X_{s})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u_{f}(t_{n}-s,X_{s})\right]\right|+\|f\|_{\infty}\left(\mathbb{P}_{x_{n}}(\tau_{\partial}\leq s)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}\leq s)\right)

By (
15
), we have

\lim_{s\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x_{n}}(\tau_{\partial}\leq s)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial}\leq s)=0.

On the other hand, by Assumption
(A3)
, for fixed
s
,

\mathbb{E}_{x_{n}}\left[u_{f}(t_{n}-s,X_{s})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u_{f}(t_{n}-s,X_{s})\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}u_{f}(t_{n}-s,y)\left(p(s;x_{n},y)-p(s;x,y)\right)\mathrm{d}y,

where the transition density
p(s;x,y)
of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is continuous in
x
. Therefore, by Scheffé’s lemma, since
u_{f}(t_{n}-s,y)
is bounded uniformly in
n
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}u_{f}(t_{n}-s,y)\left(p(s;x_{n},y)-p(s;x,y)\right)\mathrm{d}y=0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

3.3
Regularization of
F^{\flat}
and
\sigma^{\flat}

From now on we fix a compact set
K\subset D
and use the notation introduced in Section
3.1
. By the strong Markov property, for any
t\geq 0
,
x\in D
,

\displaystyle u_{f}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\partial}>t\}}f(X_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\partial}>t,\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}>t\}}f(X_{t})\right]+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{\partial}>t,\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\leq t\}}f(X_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}>t\}}f(X_{t})\right]+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\leq t\}}u_{f}(t-\tau^{\flat}_{\partial},X_{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}})\right],

and by Lemma
3.1
, one may replace
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
(X^{\flat}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in the right-hand side and thus end up with the identity

u_{f}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}>t\}}f(X^{\flat}_{t})+\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}\leq t\}}g(t-\tau^{\flat}_{\partial},X^{\flat}_{\tau^{\flat}_{\partial}})\right],

where we have somehow artificially introduced the boundary condition

g(t,x):=u_{f}(t,x),

which by Lemma
3.3
is continuous on
[0,\infty)\times\overline{D^{\flat}}
. If the functions
F^{\flat}
and
\sigma^{\flat}
are
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
, and globally bounded and Lipschitz continuous, then following the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 2.10 (iii)]
(combined with Remark 2.12 in the same reference), one deduces from this identity that
u_{f}
is a classical solution to the kinetic Fokker–Planck equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u_{f}(t,x)&=\mathcal{L}_{F^{\flat},\sigma^{\flat}}u_{f}(t,x),&(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times D^{\flat},\\
u_{f}(0,x)&=f(x),&x\in D^{\flat},\\
u_{f}(t,x)&=g(t,x)&\text{$t>0$, $x=(q,p)$ with $q\in\partial\mathcal{O}^{\flat}$, $p\cdot n^{\flat}(q)\geq 0$,}\end{aligned}\right.

(16)

where
n^{\flat}(q)
denotes the outward normal vector at
q\in\partial\mathcal{O}^{\flat}
. In particular,
u_{f}
is a distributional solution to
\partial_{t}u_{f}=\mathcal{L}_{F^{\flat},\sigma^{\flat}}u_{f}
, so as soon as
f\geq 0
, the Harnack inequality for
u_{f}
follows from the following statement.

Proposition 3.4

(Harnack inequality for smooth coefficients)
.

Assume that the functions
F^{\flat}
and
\sigma^{\flat}
are
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
. For any
\epsilon>0
and
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{K,\epsilon,T}\geq 0
which only depends on
F^{\flat}
and
\sigma^{\flat}
through
\sup_{K}|F^{\flat}|
,
\sup_{K}\|\sigma^{\flat}{\sigma^{\flat}}^{T}\|
and the uniform ellipticity constant of
\sigma^{\flat}{\sigma^{\flat}}^{T}
on
K
, such that, for any nonnegative
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
function
u:(0,\infty)\times D^{\flat}\to[0,\infty)
which is a distributional solution to
\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{L}_{F^{\flat},\sigma^{\flat}}u
on
(0,\infty)\times D^{\flat}
, one has

\forall t\geq\epsilon,\qquad\sup_{x\in K}u(t,x)\leq C_{K,\epsilon,T}\inf_{x\in K}u(t+T,x).

Remark 3.5
.

In the statement of Proposition
3.4
, a hypoellipticity argument ensures that as soon as
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is a distributional solution to
\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{L}_{F^{\flat},\sigma^{\flat}}u
then it is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
, so it makes sense to consider pointwise values of
u(t,x)
.

Proposition
3.4
is stated and proved in
[
17
, Theorem 2.15]
for the particular case
F^{\flat}(q,p)=F(q)-\gamma p
,
\sigma^{\flat}(q,p)=\sigma I_{d}
. It combines two building bricks: a Harnack inequality for kinetic Fokker–Planck equations in small balls, initially obtained by Golse, Imbert, Mouhot and Vasseur
[
10
, Theorem 4]
, and a chaining argument to extend the inequality to arbitrary compact sets, adapted from
[
1
]
. Both steps can be adapted without any modification to the setting of Proposition
3.4
, so we omit the details of the proof.

In view of Proposition
3.4
, the main issue with the setting of Theorem
2.3
is the possible low regularity of
F^{\flat}
and
\sigma^{\flat}
. So we introduce regularized versions thereof, defined as follows: for
n\geq 1
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

F^{\flat}_{n}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F^{\flat}(x-y)\phi_{n}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad\sigma^{\flat}_{n}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sigma^{\flat}(x-y)\phi_{n}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(17)

where
\phi_{n}(y)=n^{2d}\phi(ny)
with
\phi
a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and compactly supported function on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
integrating to
1
. With this definition we get the following statement, which follows from standard arguments and whose proof is omitted.

Lemma 3.6

(Regularized coefficients)
.

One may choose the function
\phi
such that the following hold:

(i)

We have

F^{\flat}_{n}\overset{L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}}{\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}}F^{\flat},

(18)

and there exist
\tilde{a}^{\flat},\tilde{b}^{\flat}\geq 0
such that

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},\quad n\geq 1,\qquad|F^{\flat}_{n}(x)|\leq\tilde{a}^{\flat}+\tilde{b}^{\flat}|x|.

(19)

(ii)

We have

\|\sigma^{\flat}_{n}-\sigma^{\flat}\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

(20)

and there exist
0<\tilde{c}^{\flat}_{1}\leq\tilde{c}^{\flat}_{2}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all
n\geq 1
,

\tilde{c}^{\flat}_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi\cdot\sigma^{\flat}_{n}{\sigma^{\flat}_{n}}^{T}(x)\xi\leq\tilde{c}^{\flat}_{2}|\xi|^{2}.

(21)

Moreover, for all
x=(q,p),x^{\prime}=(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, for all
n\geq 1
,

\|\sigma^{\flat}_{n}(x)-\sigma^{\flat}_{n}(x^{\prime})\|\leq c^{\flat}_{3}(|q-q^{\prime}|^{\alpha/3}+|p-p^{\prime}|^{\alpha}),

with
c^{\flat}_{3}
and
\alpha
given by Assumption
(A2-glob)
for
\sigma^{\flat}
.

Since, by Lemma
3.6
,
F^{\flat}_{n}
and

\sigma^{\flat}
of

, which is written for the Langevin process. The domain

in the present article satisfies the same assumptions as the domain

in

. Therefore, the proof closely follows the lines of
[
17
, Sect. 3.4]
. The first step, detailed in

, consists in reducing the problem to the study of the process
(\check{q}_{t},\check{p}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}\check{q}_{t}=\check{p}_{t}\mathrm{d}t,\\
&\mathrm{d}\check{p}_{t}=\sigma^{\flat}(\check{q}_{t},\check{p}_{t})\mathrm{d}B_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

thanks to the Girsanov theorem, whose application is made legitimate by the Gaussian upper bound on the transition density of
(q^{\flat}_{t},p^{\flat}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
stated in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Next, following the lines of
[
17
, Sect. 3.4.3]
with
\sigma B_{s}
replaced by
M_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{\flat}(\check{q}_{r},\check{p}_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}
shows that, after Eq. (52) there, to conclude the argument one needs to find a positive function

such that, almost surely,

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{M}_{s}\mathrm{d}s}{\Psi(t)}>0,\qquad\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{t^{2}}{\Psi(t)}=0,

with

where the unit vector

is defined with the notation of
[

, Sect. 3.4.3]
by

u=\frac{d_{0}(\phi)^{-T}(e_{d})}{|d_{0}(\phi)^{-T}(e_{d})|}.

Defining

\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{s}=|\sigma^{\flat}(\check{q}_{s},\check{p}_{s})u|^{2}\in[c_{1},c_{2}]

(25)

(where we used Assumption
(A2-glob)
to get the lower and upper bounds), we then deduce from integration by parts and the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem that there exist two Brownian motions
\beta^{1}
,
\beta^{2}
such that

\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{M}_{s}\mathrm{d}s=t\widehat{M}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}s\mathrm{d}\widehat{M}_{s}=t\beta^{1}_{\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s}-\beta^{2}_{\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s}.

Let us set, for
t>0
,
\Psi(t)=\sqrt{2t^{3}\log\log(t^{-1})}
(which indeed satisfies
\lim_{t\to 0}t^{2}/\Psi(t)=0
) and write

\frac{\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{M}_{s}\mathrm{d}s}{\Psi(t)}=A^{1}_{t}\sqrt{\frac{\ell^{1}_{t}}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{s}\mathrm{d}s}-A^{2}_{t}\sqrt{\frac{\ell^{2}_{t}}{t^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{s}\mathrm{d}s},

where

A^{1}_{t}=\frac{\beta^{1}_{\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s}}{\sqrt{2(\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)\log\log((\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)^{-1})}},\qquad A^{2}_{t}=\frac{\beta^{2}_{\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s}}{\sqrt{2(\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)\log\log((\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)^{-1})}},

and

\ell^{1}_{t}=\frac{\log\log((\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)^{-1})}{\log\log(t^{-1})},\qquad\ell^{2}_{t}=\frac{\log\log((\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)^{-1})}{\log\log(t^{-1})}.

The Law of the Iterated Logarithm for the Brownian motions
\beta^{1}
and
\beta^{2}
implies that

\limsup_{t\to 0}A^{1}_{t}=\limsup_{t\to 0}A^{2}_{t}=1,

while elementary estimates using (
25

\lim_{t\to 0}\ell^{1}_{t}=\lim_{t\to 0}\ell^{2}_{t}=1.

Last, the continuity of
\sigma^{\flat}
provided by Assumption
(A2-glob)
implies that, almost surely,

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{s}\mathrm{d}s=\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{0},\qquad\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{s}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{0}}{3}.

Combining the last three results leads to the estimate

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{M}_{s}\mathrm{d}s}{\Psi(t)}\geq\widehat{\sigma}_{0}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)\geq\sqrt{c_{1}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)>0,

which completes the proof.
∎

5.1
Proof of Theorem
2.9

To prove Theorem
2.9
, we need to construct, under Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(A3)
, and with a
bounded
domain
\mathcal{O}
satisfying Assumption
(O)
, a bounded Lyapunov function
\phi_{\lambda}
which satisfies Assumption
(H)
.

To proceed, we let
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\to(0,1]
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function such that
g(\rho)=\rho
for
\rho\in(0,1/2)
and
g(\rho)=1
for
\rho\geq 1
. Now let
\beta:=1+\sup_{q\in\mathcal{O}}|q|
and
p_{a}>0
be a parameter which will be defined later. We define
\phi
on
D
by

\phi(q,p):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\beta-\frac{q\cdot p}{|p|}g(|p|),&&|p|\neq 0,\\
&\beta,&&p=0,\end{aligned}\right.

then
\phi\geq 1
on
D
and
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(D)
. Now, for any
\lambda>0
, let

p_{0}(\lambda):=1+p_{a}+4\lambda\beta
and
D_{\lambda}\subset D
be defined as follows:

D_{\lambda}=\{(q,p)\in D:|p|\leq p_{0}(\lambda)\}.

Then, for
(q,p)\in D\setminus D_{\lambda}
,
\phi(q,p)=\beta-\frac{q\cdot p}{|p|}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}\phi(q,p)

\displaystyle=p\cdot\nabla_{q}\phi(q,p)+F(q,p)\cdot\nabla_{p}\phi(q,p)+\frac{\sigma\sigma^{T}(q,p)}{2}:\nabla^{2}_{p}\phi(q,p)

\displaystyle=-|p|\left(1+\frac{q\cdot F(q,p)}{|p|^{2}}-\frac{(q\cdot p)(p\cdot F(q,p))}{|p|^{4}}-2\frac{(\sigma^{T}(q,p)q)\cdot(\sigma^{T}(q,p)p)}{|p|^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle-|p|\left(3\frac{q\cdot p}{|p|^{6}}|\sigma^{T}(q,p)p|^{2}-\frac{p\cdot q}{|p|^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma\sigma^{T}(q,p))\right).

Since
\mathcal{O}
is bounded, Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
yield

\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}\phi(q,p)=-|p|\left(1+\underset{|p|\rightarrow\infty}{o(1)}\right).

As a result, if one defines
p_{a}
such that for
|p|\geq p_{a}
, and
q\in\mathcal{O}
,

\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}\phi(q,p)\leq-\frac{|p|}{2},

then one gets, for
|p|\geq p_{0}(\lambda)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}\phi(q,p)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{|p|}{2}\leq-2\lambda\beta\leq-\lambda\left(\beta-\frac{q\cdot p}{|p|}\right)=-\lambda\phi(q,p).

Now, on the bounded set
D_{\lambda}
,
\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}\phi(q,p)+\lambda\phi(q,p)
is bounded by some
c_{\lambda}<\infty
. Combined with the inequality above this yields that
\mathcal{L}_{F,\sigma}\phi\leq-\lambda\phi+c_{\lambda}\mathbb{1}_{D_{\lambda}}
on
D
. Furthermore, by construction of
\beta
and
g
,
1\leq\phi\leq 2\beta-1
, so Assumption
(H)
is satisfied and
\phi
is bounded.

5.2
Proof of Theorem
2.10

In this section, we consider the Langevin process (
1
), with mass matrix
M=I_{d}
and an interaction force
F
given by (
9
)–(
10
), with a vector field
\ell
which is bounded on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We assume that Assumption
(A3)
holds true, and fix a set
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies Assumption
(O)
. Hence, as in Section
5.1
, the proof of Theorem
2.10
reduces to the verification of Assumption
(H)
. This is the object of the next statement, which relies on the Lyapunov function introduced in
[
15
, Section 4]
. In the proof, we shall simply denote by

, rather than

, the infinitesimal generator of the associated dynamics.

Lemma 5.1

(Lyapunov function for Theorem
2.10
)
.

In the setting described above, Assumption
(H)
is satisfied.

Proof.

Let us define the following function
H
as in
[
15
, Definition 4.1]
, for all
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

H(q,p):=U(q)+\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}+\frac{\gamma-\delta}{2}q\cdot p+\frac{(\gamma-\delta)^{2}}{4}|q|^{2},

where
\delta\in(0,\gamma)
is a constant small enough such that, cf.
[
15
, Equation 4.2]
,

\frac{\delta(\gamma-\delta)}{2}\leq\alpha\;,\qquad\frac{2\delta}{\gamma-\delta}\leq\alpha\;,\;\;\;\;\text{and}\qquad\beta^{2}\leq\gamma(\gamma-\delta),

where
\alpha,\beta
are the constants appearing in (
10
). A computation following
[
15
, Lemma 4.3]
yields that for all
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

\mathcal{L}H(q,p)\leq-\delta H(q,p)+d\epsilon,

where, for notational consistency between (
1
) and
[
15
]
, we have written
\epsilon=\gamma\mathrm{k}T
. Let us now define the function
\widehat{H}
for
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

\widehat{H}(q,p)=1+H(q,p).

Then, following the computation performed in
[
15
, Lemma 4.3]
one has that for all
n\geq 1
and
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

\mathcal{L}\widehat{H}^{n}(q,p)\leq n\widehat{H}^{n-1}(q,p)(-\delta H(q,p))+\epsilon\left[dn\widehat{H}^{n-1}(q,p)+n(n-1)\widehat{H}^{n-2}(q,p)\left|p+\frac{\gamma-\delta}{2}q\right|^{2}\right].

(32)

Furthermore, using the lower-bound of
H
in
[
15
, Lemma 4.2]
, one has the existence of a constant

such that
|p+\frac{\gamma-\delta}{2}q|^{2}\leq c\widehat{H}(q,p)
. Moreover, the same lower-bound also ensures that
\widehat{H}(q,p)\underset{|(q,p)|\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}~\infty
. Consequently, there exists a compact set
B_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
such that for all
(q,p)\notin B_{n}

\frac{\delta}{2}\widehat{H}(q,p)\geq\delta+d\epsilon+c\epsilon(n-1).

Therefore, it follows from (
32
) that for all
(q,p)\notin B_{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\widehat{H}^{n}(q,p)

\displaystyle\leq n\widehat{H}^{n-1}(q,p)\left[-\delta\widehat{H}(q,p)+\delta+d\epsilon+c\epsilon(n-1)\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{n\delta}{2}\widehat{H}^{n}(q,p).

Since
\ell
is bounded on bounded sets, while

is continuous,
F(q)-\gamma p
is bounded on the compact set

and so is
\mathcal{L}\widehat{H}^{n}(q,p)+\frac{n\delta}{2}\widehat{H}^{n}(q,p)
. Consequently, it is easy to deduce that for all

\mathcal{L}\widehat{H}^{n_{\lambda}}(q,p)\leq-\lambda\widehat{H}^{n_{\lambda}}(q,p)+c_{n_{\lambda}}\mathbb{1}_{B_{n_{\lambda}}}(q,p).

Moreover, since
H\geq 0
by
[
15
, Lemma 4.2]
then
\phi_{\lambda}=\widehat{H}^{n_{\lambda}}\geq 1
satisfies the hypotheses required.
∎

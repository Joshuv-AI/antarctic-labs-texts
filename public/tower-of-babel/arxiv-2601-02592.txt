Abstract
We prove that the fiber product of the Torelli map t : ℳ g c ​ t → 𝒜 g t\colon\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g} with any product 𝒜 g 1 × ⋯ × 𝒜 g k → 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}\to\mathcal{A}_{g} for g = g 1 + ⋯ + g k g=g_{1}+\dots+g_{k} has a reduced scheme structure. As a consequence, letting d = codim ​ ( t ∗ ​ [ 𝒜 g 1 × ⋯ × 𝒜 g k ] ) d=\text{codim}(t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}]) , we find that the class t ∗ ​ [ 𝒜 g 1 × ⋯ × 𝒜 g k ] ∈ 𝖢𝖧 d ​ ( ℳ g c ​ t ) t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}]\in\mathsf{CH}^{d}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}) is tautological. Introduction

1.1
Moduli of decomposable abelian varieties and the Torelli map

The moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{g}
of
principally polarized varieties
of dimension
g
is a nonsingular Deligne-Mumford stack obtained over
\mathbb{C}
as an orbifold quotient

\mathcal{A}_{g}=[\text{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}_{g}],

where
\mathbb{H}_{g}
, the
Siegel upper half-space
, is the space of matrices

\mathbb{H}_{g}=\{\Pi\in M_{g\times g}(\mathbb{C})|\Pi^{T}=\Pi,\text{Im}(\Pi)>0\}.

For any
g=g_{1}+\dots+g_{k}
, we have a product map

\displaystyle\pi\colon\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}

\displaystyle(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})\mapsto X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{k}.

Pushing forward the fundamental class of
\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}
under
\pi
, we obtain a cycle

[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}]\in\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g}).

The intersection theory of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
can be studied using the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}
of
compact type curves
, parametrizing stable curves
C
whose dual graph
\Gamma_{C}
is a tree. Compact type curves form an open substack
\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of the moduli space of stable curves of genus
g
, here considered over
\mathbb{C}
. The principally polarized
Jacobian

J(C)
of a compact type curve
C
parametrizes line bundles of multidegree
0
, where the polarization is given by the theta divisor. For
[C]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, the Jacobian
J(C)
is proper precisely when
[C]\in\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}
. The
Torelli map
is the morphism

\displaystyle t\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}

\displaystyle[C]\mapsto[J(C)],

whose pullback
t^{*}\colon\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\to\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})
gives us a tool for investigating relations in
\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
.

1.2
Tautological classes and loci of decomposable abelian varieties

Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi\colon\mathcal{X}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}}
be the universal abelian variety and
\mathbb{E}=\pi_{*}\Omega_{\pi}
the
Hodge bundle
on
\mathcal{A}_{g}
. The Chern classes
\lambda_{i}=c_{i}(\mathbb{E})
generate a subring
R^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\subset\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
referred to as the
tautological ring

[
16
]
. For
\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}
, we instead define the tautological ring
R^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})
to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebra of
\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})
generated by pushforwards from boundary strata of products of kappa- and psi classes
[
9
]
. In both cases, elements of the tautological ring are referred to as
tautological classes
. Given a cycle in
\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
, it is natural to ask whether or not it is tautological. For
\alpha\in\mathsf{CH}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
, the
tautological projection

\text{taut}(\alpha)
is the unique class in
R^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
satisfying

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}\alpha\cdot\beta\cdot\lambda_{g}=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}\text{taut}(\alpha)\cdot\beta\cdot\lambda_{g}\hskip 2.0pt\text{ for all }\hskip 2.0pt\beta\in\mathsf{R}^{\binom{g}{2}-k}(\mathcal{A}_{g}),

where
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
is any choice of toroidal compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{g}

[
5
]
. To prove that
\alpha\not\in R^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
, it is enough to show that
t^{*}\alpha\neq t^{*}\text{taut}(\alpha)
in
\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})
.

The classes
[\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{g-1}]\in\mathsf{CH}^{g-1}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
have been studied in
[
6
]
and
[
14
]
. In
[
14
]
, Iribar López shows that
[\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{g-1}]
is nontautological for
g=12
or
g\geq 16
with
g
even. In
[
6
]
, Canning, Oprea and Pandharipande compute the Torelli pullback
t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{g-1}]
using excess intersection theory on the fiber product of
t\colon\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
and
\pi\colon\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
. This method uses the fact that the fiber product is reduced, which is proven in
[
6
]
using deformation theory of stable maps. By computing the class
t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{5}]
and comparing it with
{t^{*}\text{taut}([\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{5}])}
, they are able to show that
[\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{5}]\in\mathsf{CH}^{5}(\mathcal{A}_{6})
is nontautological.

Our main result of this paper, Theorem
1.1
, opens up the possibility of calculating the classes
t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}]
for any
g=g_{1}+\dots+g_{k}
. In
[
4
]
, we give a recursive formula for computing these classes by adapting methods from
[
6
]
. The fiber product
Y
consists of several intersecting components whose dimensions can exceed
\text{dim}(t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}])
. The first method presented in
[
4
]
for calculating
t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}]
involves stratifying

t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}]
into canonical contributions from the closed strata.

Theorem 1.1
.

The fiber product of the Torelli map
t\colon\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
with the product map
\pi\colon\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}

Theorem

1.4
Torelli pullbacks of product loci

Many classes

\text{dim}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})=3g-3
and
\text{dim}(\mathcal{A}_{g})=\frac{g(g+1)}{2}
. The codimension

We deduce that

[
10
]
. As an application of Theorem

, we prove the following result in Section

Theorem 1.4
.

The class

is tautological for all decompositions

. Thus,
t^{*}[\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}]=0\in\mathsf{CH}^{d}(\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g})

We have the following possibilities for
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
such that

, meaning that

):

.

The tuples

with

3.1
Strata of the fiber product

We give a stratification of the fiber product
Y
(see Section
1.3
) according to the combinatorial type of the curve
[C]\in\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}
and the
k
-coloring
\sigma
.

Let
T
be a stable tree of genus
g
(see
[
9
, A.1]
) and
V(T)_{>0}=\{v\in V(T)|g(v)>0\}
. Let

\sigma:V(T)_{>0}\to\{1,\dots,k\}

be a partition satisfying
\sum_{v\in\sigma^{-1}(j)}g(v)=g_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,k
. For each such pair
(T,\sigma)
, we obtain a map

\varphi_{(T,\sigma)}\colon\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{T}=\prod_{v\in V(T)}\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g(v),n(v)}\to Y

via the gluing map
\xi_{T}\colon\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{T}\to\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{g}
and the map

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{ct}_{T}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{A}_{g_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{g_{k}}

\displaystyle[(C_{v},q_{1},\dots,q_{n(v)})_{v\in V(T)}]

\displaystyle\mapsto([\hskip-8.0pt\prod_{v\in\sigma^{-1}(1)}\hskip-8.0ptJ(C_{v})],\dots,[\hskip-8.0pt\prod_{v\in\sigma^{-1}(k)}\hskip-8.0ptJ(C_{v})]).

Denote the image of
\varphi_{(T,\sigma)}
in

. The pair
(T,\sigma)
is uniquely determined by the closed subscheme
Y_{(T,\sigma)}\subset Y
. Containments of the form

are determined by specializations

of stable graphs
[
9
, A.2]
such that the induced map
{f\colon V(T)\to V(T^{\prime})}
satisfies
\sigma(v)=\sigma^{\prime}(f(v))
for

. We denote such specializations by
{\phi\colon(T,\sigma)\to(T^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})}

(T^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
-structure
on
(T,\sigma)
. For a collection
S=\left((T_{i},\sigma_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}
, we refer to a collection
\left(\phi_{i}\colon(T,\sigma)\to(T_{i},\sigma_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}

-structure
on
(T,\sigma)
, denoted
\left((T,\sigma),(\phi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\right)
. We say that an

-structure
\left((T,\sigma),(\phi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\right)
is

if, for any

-structure
\left((T^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}),(\phi^{\prime}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\right)
and specialization

is an isomorphism.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S=\left((T_{i},\sigma_{i})\right)_{1\leq i\leq n}
. For any
S
-structure
\left((T,\sigma),(\phi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\right)
, there is a unique generic
S
-structure
\left((T^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}),(\phi^{\prime}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\right)
and specialization
\phi\colon(T,\sigma)\to(T^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
satisfying
\phi^{\prime}_{i}\circ\phi=\phi_{i}
for

.

Proof.

Given
\left((T,\sigma),(\phi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\right)
, the unique generic
S
-structure is obtained by contracting the edges of
T
which are contracted under the specializations
T\to T_{i}
induced by
\phi_{i}
for all

, with the induced coloring and

-structure.

Let

Proposition 3.2

The fiber product of the maps

Cost-effectiveness of pembrolizumab plus trastuzumab and chemotherapy for HER2-positive gastric or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma
Authors: Kexin Yu, Qiaoping Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTo investigate the cost-effectiveness of pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy as a first-line treatment for HER2-positive gastric or gastro-esophageal junction adenocarcinoma from the perspective of the U.S. and Chinese healthcare systems. In this economic evaluation, a Markov model is constructed from the perspective of the U.S. and Chinese healthcare systems. The survival data were derived from the clinical trial (KEYNOTE-811), while cost data generated during treatment were sourced from pricing, databases, and expert consultations in local hospitals. Total costs, quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) were estimated in US dollars. Its robustness is evaluated by deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analysis. Basic case analysis showed that pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy, as a new treatment strategy, had higher cost-effectiveness compared with trastuzumab plus chemotherapy treatment, with willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds set at $150,000 per QALY for the United States population and $40,334.05 per QALY for the China population The ICER was $88,507.57 per QALY in the United States and $22,461.22 per QALY in China, both below the respective thresholds. Sensitivity analysis demonstrated that the results were robust. From the perspective of the U.S. and Chinese medical and health services, pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy is more cost-effective than chemotherapy in the treatment of HER2-positive gastric or gastro-esophageal junction adenocarcinoma patients. Within the context of China’s health technology assessment and National Reimbursement Drug List negotiation framework, the ICER below the three-times-GDP threshold suggests preliminary economic feasibility at current price levels, indicating that the regimen may warrant consideration for reimbursement decisions, subject to further budget impact analysis and price negotiation. The evidence-based pricing strategies provided in this study may be helpful for decision makers and clinicians to make the best decisions in general clinical practice. More evidence on budgetary impact and patient affordability is needed.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) accounts for approximately 5.6% of all cancer cases globally. It is the fifth most commonly diagnosed cancer-following breast, lung, colorectal, and prostate cancers-and represents the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths.1 The incidence and mortality rates of gastric cancer in China (25.41 and 18.44 per 100,000, respectively) remain substantially higher than those in the United States (9.1 and 3.3 per 100,000, respectively).2,3 Approximately one-quarter of gastric and gastro-esophageal junction adenocarcinomas (G/GEJ) are characterized by abnormal activation of human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) tyrosine kinases.1 While surgery remains the cornerstone of curative-intent treatment for localized G/GEJ adenocarcinomas, the majority of patients are diagnosed at a locally advanced or metastatic stage, precluding surgical intervention. These patients face a dismal prognosis, with a five-year survival rate of less than 5%.4 For HER2-positive advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, the first-line standard treatment consists of the anti-HER2 monoclonal antibody trastuzumab in combination with cytotoxic chemotherapy (fluorouracil and platinum-based agents).

In recent years, as cancer therapy has entered a new era of immunotherapy, targeting immune checkpoint regulators such as programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1) and its receptor PD-1 has emerged as a promising anticancer strategy.5 PD-1 is a negative co-stimulatory receptor expressed on the surface of T lymphocytes. Its interaction with PD-L1 can suppress T-cell immune responses, leading to T-cell exhaustion and eventual tumor immune escape. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) enhance anti-tumor immunity by blocking the interaction between checkpoint proteins and their ligands.6 ICIs targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway, including nivolumab and sintilimab, have demonstrated favorable anti-tumor activity and acceptable safety profiles in patients with advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma.7 Pembrolizumab, a monoclonal antibody against PD-1, has also shown anti-tumor efficacy and a manageable safety profile with predominantly low-grade toxicities in G/GEJ adenocarcinoma. In December 2023, updated results from the KEYNOTE-811 trial revealed that, after a median follow-up of 50.2 months, HER2-positive patients with a PD-L1 Combined Positive Score (CPS) ≥ 1 treated with pembrolizumab achieved a median overall survival of 20.1 months compared to 15.7 months in the placebo group-a significant improvement.1 Based on these findings, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab, fluorouracil, and platinum-based chemotherapy for the first-line treatment of locally advanced or metastatic HER2-positive G/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

In medical decision-making, clinicians and policymakers increasingly prioritize cost-effectiveness. While new treatments may offer modest clinical benefits, they often come with significantly higher costs. Due to high drug prices and treatment costs, many cancer patients are often forced to give up the most effective treatment. Previous cost-benefit analyses have evaluated the application of ICIs such as nivolumab in this field. In China, the role of health technology assessment in reimbursement decision-making has expanded considerably since the establishment of the National Healthcare Security Administration in 2018. The annual National Reimbursement Drug List negotiation process now explicitly considers cost-effectiveness evidence alongside clinical efficacy when evaluating innovative oncology drugs for formulary inclusion. However, economic evaluations of novel immuno-chemotherapy combinations for gastric cancer remain limited in the Chinese context, representing an evidence gap that constrains informed decision-making. The results of the study indicate that nivolumab may not be an economically viable first-line treatment for patients with G/GEJ adenocarcinoma. Compared with placebo plus trastuzumab and chemotherapy, although the existing trial data show that pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy is satisfactory, the cost of immunotherapy is relatively high, so it is urgent to fully balance the cost and efficacy of these innovative therapies. This study provides the first cost-effectiveness evaluation of pembrolizumab combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy for HER2-positive advanced gastric or gastro-esophageal junction adenocarcinoma from a healthcare system perspective. The specific contributions of this evaluation are twofold. First, it addresses a critical evidence gap by assessing the economic viability of this novel immunotherapy combination, which has not been previously examined. Second, by conducting analyses using WTP thresholds reflective of both United States and Chinese healthcare contexts, this study offers policy-relevant insights for decision-makers in diverse economic settings. Using a Markov model, we conducted a cost-effectiveness analysis based on clinical outcomes from the KEYNOTE-811 trial, along with drug pricing and utility values derived from published literature. Methods

This study was reported by the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022 (CHEERS 2022).8

Markov model structure

We developed a hybrid mathematical model integrating a decision tree with a Markov state-transition structure to evaluate the clinical effectiveness and economic outcomes of adding pembrolizumab to first-line treatment in patients with previously untreated, locally advanced unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, from the perspective of the United States and China ’s medical system. The decision tree classifies G/GEJ adenocarcinoma patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 1 according to different treatment regimens. Eligible patients were included in the model and randomly assigned to receive either pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy, or placebo plus trastuzumab and chemotherapy, until disease progression or discontinuation of study treatment. A Markov model was then constructed to simulate the natural history of advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, comprising five health states: progression-free survival (PFS), progressive disease (PD), stable disease (SD), remission (RE) and death. The model was implemented using TreeAge Pro Suite 2022 (TreeAge Software, Williamstown, MA, USA), with model structure and input parameters derived primarily from the KEYNOTE-811 trial. Cost-effectiveness was assessed based on outcomes including PFS, overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DOR), and disease control rate (DCR). Patients entered the model in the PFS state. During each cycle, patients could transition between health states according to predefined pathways: patients with SD could remain in the SD state, progress to PD, or transition to RE; patients with PD could remain in PD or transition to death; patients with RE could remain in RE or progress to PD. The transition directions and pathways are illustrated in Figure 2. This expanded five-state framework was designed to more accurately capture the distinct clinical trajectories observed in the KEYNOTE-811 trial. By distinguishing between SD and RE – states that differ substantially in terms of treatment intensity, healthcare resource utilization, and quality of life – the model enables a more granular estimation of costs and quality-adjusted life-years, thereby improving the interpretability of cost-effectiveness findings for clinicians and policymakers. The Markov model used a cycle length of 1 month over a time horizon of 10 y. This time horizon was selected because advanced gastric cancer is associated with a median overall survival of less than 3 y with current standard therapies; a 10-y horizon therefore captures the vast majority of survival differences, cumulative costs, and quality-adjusted life-years between treatment strategies. Additionally, this time horizon aligns with published cost-effectiveness analyses in this disease area, facilitating comparison of findings across studies. Costs and survival outcomes were adjusted using half-cycle correction and an annual discount rate of 3% to estimate total costs, QALYs, and ICERs for each treatment strategy. The WTP threshold was set at $150,000 per QALY for the US population. For China, where no official WTP threshold exists, this study adopted the World Health Organization’s recommended threshold of three times per capita GDP-a benchmark commonly used in health technology assessments to support National Reimbursement Drug List negotiations. According to the National Bureau of Statistics of China, the per capita GDP in 2024 was 95,749 yuan. Thus, three times this value amounts to 287,247 yuan. Using the 2024 annual average exchange rate between the US dollar and the renminbi (1 USD = 7.1217 RMB), the WTP threshold for the Chinese population was calculated as $40,334.05. Health utility values used in the QALY calculations were derived from internationally published literature.9

Patients and treatment plans

A total of 594 patients (PD-L1 CPS ≥ 1) were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the pembrolizumab combination group (n = 298) or the placebo combination group (n = 296). Patients received intravenous pembrolizumab (200 mg) or placebo (normal saline or dextrose solution) every three weeks. Additionally, all patients received intravenous trastuzumab at a dose of 6 mg/kg every three weeks, following an initial loading dose of 8 mg/kg. The chemotherapy regimen consisted of either: (1) fluorouracil (800 mg/m2 administered intravenously on days 1 to 5 of each 3-week cycle) plus cisplatin (80 mg/m2 intravenously every 3 weeks), or (2) capecitabine (1000 mg/m2 orally twice daily from days 1 to 14 of each 3-week cycle) plus oxaliplatin (130 mg/m2 intravenously every 3 weeks). Imaging was performed at week 6 and every 6 weeks thereafter. Response was assessed according to RECIST v1.1, by blinded independent central review for determination of study endpoints, and according to immune RECIST (iRECIST) by the investigator to inform treatment decisions.

Treatment continued for up to 35 cycles (approximately 2 y), or until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, investigator’s decision to discontinue, or patient withdrawal of consent. Upon disease progression, patients proceeded to second-line therapy. In the pembrolizumab combination group, 39% received subsequent treatment, which included chemotherapy (19%), pembrolizumab (3%), ramucirumab (8%), and trastuzumab (9%). In the trastuzumab combination group, 47% received follow-up therapy, consisting of chemotherapy (24%), pembrolizumab (3%), ramucirumab (10%), and trastuzumab (9%). It is presumed that the remaining patients who did not receive these treatments transitioned to best supportive care and received hospice care prior to death. The follow-up schedule adhered to the National Cancer Institute guidelines. Chest, abdominal, and pelvic CT scans were conducted every six months for the first two years, and annually thereafter until ten years. Medical history updates, physical examinations, and complete blood counts were performed every three months during the first two years, and every six months thereafter until death. The second-line treatment regimen consisted of: Trastuzumab deruxtecan (6.4 mg/kg on day 1 of each 21-d cycle), Ramucirumab (8 mg/kg on days 1 and 15 of each 28-d cycle), combined with Paclitaxel (80 mg/m2 on days 1, 8, and 15 of each 28-d cycle).1

Model inputs for transition probabilities

This study employed a static Markov model for pharmacoeconomic evaluation. Transition probabilities in the model were derived using the Declining Exponential Approximation of Life Expectancy (DEALE) principle, which involves converting time-to-event data into rate parameters and subsequently into probabilities.10 This approach assumes that survival follows an exponential distribution, allowing extrapolation of survival outcomes beyond the observed trial follow-up period. Specifically, transition probabilities were estimated using median overall survival and median progression-free survival from the KEYNOTE-811 trial, with constant hazard rates applied over the 10-y time horizon. While this assumption represents a simplification of the underlying survival trajectory, it is consistent with the DEALE methodology and has been widely used in published cost-effectiveness analyses based on trial summary statistics. The primary clinical endpoints used for calculating transition probabilities were relative risk (RR), OS, PFS, and DOR. The DEALE approach with constant transition probabilities was selected for this analysis based on several considerations. First, the analysis relied on published summary statistics from the KEYNOTE-811 trial (median OS, median PFS, and RR), as individual patient-level data were not available. The DEALE method enables estimation of transition probabilities from these summary statistics, making the analysis feasible and transparent. Second, alternative approaches such as time-dependent transition probabilities, parametric survival models, or partitioned survival models typically require individual patient-level data or reconstructed Kaplan—Meier curves to fit parametric survival functions. Given the available data, such approaches were not feasible without introducing additional assumptions that could compromise replicability. Third, this modeling approach has been widely used in published cost-effectiveness analyses of oncology treatments, particularly for initial economic evaluations based on trial summary data, facilitating comparison with prior studies.

Several methodological limitations should be acknowledged. The static Markov model calculates transition probabilities based on median overall survival and maintains constant transition probabilities across all model cycles. This approach assumes consistent disease progression rates throughout the time horizon, which may not fully capture time-dependent changes in hazard rates observed in real-world clinical practice. Consequently, this simplification may introduce bias in QALY estimations compared with more flexible modeling approaches such as time-dependent transition models or partitioned survival models.

However, the use of constant transition probabilities was considered appropriate given the available data constraints, and the potential impact of this simplification was assessed through extensive sensitivity analyses. One-way sensitivity analyses varied key transition probability parameters across plausible ranges, and probabilistic sensitivity analysis characterized the joint uncertainty in all model inputs. These analyses indicated that the cost-effectiveness conclusions were generally robust to variations in transition probability assumptions.

Future research with access to individual patient-level data from the KEYNOTE-811 trial would enable the use of parametric survival modeling or time-dependent transition probabilities, which could further refine the estimates of long-term survival and cost-effectiveness. Such analyses would complement the present findings by providing additional validation of the robustness of the conclusions.

Model inputs for utility

Health utility value is a cardinal index reflecting preferences for specific health outcomes, which is essential for estimating quality-adjusted life years (QALYs). This measure is anchored on a scale from 0 (representing death) to 1 (representing perfect health). Utility values were mapped to health states as follows: the PFS state was assigned a utility of 0.730 in the United States and 0.750 in China, reflecting the average utility reported for patients receiving first-line treatment without disease progression; the PD state was assigned a utility of 0.577 in the United States and 0.600 in China, based on utility estimates for patients with advanced gastric cancer who have progressed following first-line therapy. The death state was assigned a utility of 0. Beta distributions were assigned to utility parameters to characterize uncertainty in probabilistic sensitivity analysis. Model inputs for costs

Cost data were collected as primary data from a provider’s perspective using a cost valuation sheet adapted from the WHO CHOICE tool. The cost analysis in this study encompassed direct medical costs, including chemotherapy drugs, hospitalization and administration, laboratory tests, routine follow-up, and the management of severe adverse events (AEs). Treatment costs were calculated on a per-cycle basis according to the therapeutic regimen. Costs were denominated in US dollars for the US model and in Chinese RMB for the China model. Based on clinical trial data, the costs of managing five common AEs were incorporated. The analysis only included selected ≥ grade 3 AEs. Such serious AEs are the core factors driving the increase in costs. Because they usually require hospitalization or intensive intervention, they have the greatest impact on total medical expenses. The incidence and treatment cost of low-grade AEs vary greatly and are difficult to estimate accurately. Therefore, this method may underestimate the treatment-related costs in the real world, because it ignores the cumulative outpatient medication, supportive care, and indirect costs due to the decline in the quality of life of patients caused by frequent grade 1–2 AEs, thus failing to fully reflect the overall toxicity management burden. Due to the lack of availability of specific AEs, we assume that all included AEs occur once in the first cycle of the model. For drug dosing calculations, the average body surface area (BSA) was assumed to be 1.67 m2 for the Chinese cohort and 1.86 m2 for the US cohort.17 The cost parameters of this study are derived from public databases and local hospital estimates to ensure the diversity and accessibility of data sources. In the Chinese part, the cost of drugs and services mainly refers to the provincial drug centralized procurement platform and the charging standards of local tertiary hospitals. These sources were selected because the centralized procurement platform reflects the actual drug prices paid by public hospitals in China following national price negotiations, while tertiary hospital charging standards represent the mainstream pricing for oncology services in major cancer treatment centers. In the United States, the cost data is based on Medicare payment standards and publicly available hospital charges. Medicare payment standards were chosen because Medicare is the primary payer for the majority of patients aged 65 y and older in the U.S., and its reimbursement rates are widely used in cost-effectiveness analyses for their transparency and consistency. Publicly available hospital charges were additionally consulted to capture variability in service pricing across different healthcare settings. Although the above sources are representative in their respective national medical systems and can reflect the mainstream payment standards and clinical practice, there may still be pricing differences between different regions and different levels of hospitals. To account for inflationary adjustments, all costs have been adjusted to the 2024 fiscal year. Sensitivity analysis

To address the uncertainty in the model, we performed both probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) and one-way sensitivity analysis. The results of the one-way analysis are presented as tornado diagrams. We systematically varied all uncertain parameters – including transition probabilities and discount rates – within a range of ±10% of their base-case values or according to their 95% confidence intervals (if available). This approach allowed us to assess the impact of each parameter on the ICER, identify the five most influential parameters, and determine whether their variation would alter the conclusion regarding the preferred chemotherapy regimen. A small number of key parameters were selected for inclusion in the one-way sensitivity analysis, as focusing on the most influential parameters improves interpretability and avoids dilution of results by less relevant inputs; this selection is further justified in the Methods section.

In the PSA, parameters were assigned probability distributions according to their data types: a beta distribution for utilities, probabilities, and proportions, and a gamma distribution for costs. We then performed a Monte Carlo simulation with 1,000 iterations, each drawing random values from these distributions to create a simulated cohort. The results were used to generate a cost-effectiveness acceptability curve (CEAC), which visually represents the probability of each strategy being cost-effective across a range of WTP thresholds. It is important to note that when incremental QALYs or costs are negative in some iterations, the resulting ICERs can be difficult to interpret using conventional averages. Therefore, the CEAC provides a more robust interpretation of the results under uncertainty.

Finally, this paper uses net monetary benefit (NMB) analysis to better analyze the situation of negative ICER, and uses the results of NMB analysis to draw a cost-benefit acceptance curve. The NMB analysis in the United States sets the cost-benefit threshold as $150,000/QALY, in China as $40,334.05/QALY.

Discussion

This study evaluated the long-term costs and outcomes of two first-line treatment strategies for G/GEJ adenocarcinoma in the United States and China. A 10-y Markov model was developed based on data from the KEYNOTE-811 trial to estimate lifetime costs and QALYs for each strategy. The results suggest that in the United States, the P+C regimen appears clinically superior to the L+C regimen, yielding higher QALYs (4.17 vs. 1.20) at a higher cost ($328077.42 vs. $65209.95). At a WTP threshold of $150,000 per QALY, the P+C regimen showed generally favorable cost-effectiveness across all one-way sensitivity analyses under the assumptions tested. Similarly, in China, the P+C regimen provided greater health benefits (4.36 vs. 1.24 QALYs) with higher costs ($89201.71 vs. $19122.70), and the ICER remained below the WTP threshold of $40,334.05 per QALY based on the base-case analysis. This finding was generally consistent in all one-way sensitivity analyses. Notably, probabilistic sensitivity analysis accounting for joint parameter uncertainty suggested that these conclusions were robust within the range of parameter estimates examined. From the perspectives of the U.S. and Chinese healthcare systems, the findings of this study suggest that pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy may represent a cost-effective strategy for treating HER2-positive gastric or gastro-esophageal junction adenocarcinoma under the current model assumptions and WTP thresholds. Based on these results, patients with G/GEJ adenocarcinoma in both countries may consider pembrolizumab plus trastuzumab and chemotherapy as a first-line treatment option, alongside the conventional trastuzumab-plus-chemotherapy approach, though individual treatment decisions should consider patient-specific factors and preferences. Reviewing the existing literature, it is found that the economic evaluation of pembrolizumab combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy for this specific patient group is relatively scarce and highly concentrated in the perspective of the U.S. healthcare systems.23 Our analysis, grounded in the structure of both national healthcare systems, incorporates comprehensive cost components – including drug acquisition, hospitalization, diagnostic tests, and adverse event management. Furthermore, by contextualizing the results within the broader economic landscapes of both countries, this study provides a more realistic assessment of their respective capacities and willingness to pay for such treatment strategies, thereby offering a more complete and policy-relevant evidence base.

Although this study is based on high-quality data from the KEYNOTE-811 trial, it may limit the extrapolation of conclusions to real-world clinical practice. First, the patient data of HER2-positive gastric or gastro-esophageal junction adenocarcinoma are from a multi-national clinical trial population and are not unique to Chinese or American patients. In clinical practice, patients with older age or poor physical fitness may benefit less than the test results, and the higher risk of AEs will increase the consumption of medical resources and adversely affect the cost-effectiveness. The implication is that the cost-effectiveness estimates from this study may be less favorable in real-world settings, particularly among patient populations that differ from the trial eligibility criteria. Therefore, the findings are most directly applicable to patients meeting the KEYNOTE-811 inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Secondly, the real-world treatment model is more complex. Treatment is limited after the trial, and patients in the real world may receive a variety of expensive treatment options including novel antibody drug conjugates, significantly changing long-term cumulative costs and survival benefits. The implication of this limitation is that the model’s assumptions regarding post-progression treatment may not fully capture the diversity of real-world clinical practice, potentially leading to either overestimation or underestimation of long-term cost-effectiveness. However, the impact of this limitation is partially mitigated by the use of a long-term time horizon and sensitivity analyses that tested alternative assumptions.

Third, the use of national averages of per-capita GDP and WTP may not adequately reflect the large regional economic disparities within China and the United States, which may limit the replicability of cost-benefit conclusions across all regions. Decision-makers in economically less developed regions should interpret the findings with attention to local contexts, as the affordability of the regimen may differ from national averages.

In addition, due to the influence of medical insurance policy or economic factors, the medication cycle of pembrolizumab in the real world may also be lower than that of the experimental scheme, which in turn affects its marginal benefits. The implication is that if patients discontinue treatment earlier in real-world practice than in the trial setting, the actual survival gains may be lower than those estimated in this analysis, potentially reducing the cost-effectiveness of the regimen. Therefore, the results of this study should be interpreted with caution as applicable to the patient population that meets the inclusion and exclusion criteria of the trial. In the future, integrating real-world data to capture treatment patterns and patient heterogeneity in clinical practice will be a key direction to further confirm the economic value of the program.

The data of China and the United States used in this study reveal the extreme sensitivity of the economy of the scheme to the price system and the payment threshold. Under the background of multi-payer system and high pricing in the United States, its ICER is much higher than the conventional threshold. In China, although ICER is slightly lower than 3 times the per capita GDP threshold, the conclusion may be reversed as future medical insurance negotiations bring price adjustments, or real-world evidence reveals different efficacy spectra. The implication of this sensitivity is that the findings cannot be directly generalized to other countries or regions without calibration to local drug prices, disease epidemiology, and willingness-to-pay benchmarks. Even within the two countries studied, differences in insurance policies and medical service pricing across provinces or states limit the direct applicability of the results to all regions. Therefore, when the conclusions of this study are directly transplanted into the medical system of other countries or regions, they need to be calibrated in combination with local prices, disease epidemiology and willingness to pay.

In order to enhance the policy reference value of this study to Chinese medical policy makers, it is necessary to interpret the research results in a deeper level under the framework of national health technology assessment and medical insurance drug catalog access decision-making. At present, China’s medical insurance drug list adjustment has established a value-based strategic purchase mechanism, and pharmacoeconomic evidence has been systematically applied to the medical insurance access negotiation of innovative drugs. Under this decision-making framework, decision-makers not only focus on whether the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio is lower than the traditional 1–3 times per capita GDP threshold, but also need to comprehensively consider the multi-dimensional value of drugs, including clinical innovation, severity of disease burden, unmet clinical needs, social equity and budgetary impact. The results of this study show that the ICER value of the combination of pembrolizumab, trastuzumab and chemotherapy is $22,461.22/QALY, which is lower than the reference threshold of 3 times China’s per capita GDP ($40,334.05/QALY), indicating preliminary economic feasibility at the current price level. From the perspectives of the U.S. and Chinese healthcare system, this finding suggests that the regimen may warrant consideration for future National Reimbursement Drug List negotiations. However, final access decisions will require comprehensive consideration of the cost-effectiveness evidence presented in this study, along with clinical expert opinions, budget impact analyses, and broader social value judgments.

Conclusion

In summary, this study evaluated the cost-effectiveness of pembrolizumab plus trastuzumab and chemotherapy for HER2-positive advanced gastric cancer from the perspective of the Chinese and American healthcare systems, based on data from the KEYNOTE-811 trial and a partitioned survival model. Under the current price levels and model assumptions, the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio for this triplet regimen was $88,507.57 per quality-adjusted life year gained from the U.S. perspective, falling below the willingness-to-pay threshold of $150,000 per QALY. This suggests that the regimen may offer a cost-effectiveness advantage within the U.S. willingness-to-pay range. From the Chinese perspective, the ICER was $22,461.22 per QALY, which is lower than the reference threshold of three times China’s per capita GDP ($40,334.05 per QALY). This finding indicates that the regimen may also be economically feasible within the current Chinese medical insurance payment framework.

These findings should be interpreted as economic evaluations conducted under specific model assumptions and threshold conditions, reflecting the inherent uncertainty in model inputs, structural assumptions, and parameter estimates. The results are most directly applicable to patients meeting the KEYNOTE-811 trial eligibility criteria and are contingent upon the current drug pricing systems and willingness-to-pay thresholds applied in this study. Future research integrating real-world data from both countries and incorporating budget impact analyses would help to further inform reimbursement and formulary decisions.

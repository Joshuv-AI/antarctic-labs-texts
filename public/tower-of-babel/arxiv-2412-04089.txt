Cyclic sum formula for certain parametrized multiple zeta values
Hanamichi Kawamura, Anju Yokoi

Abstract
. Ohno–Wakabayashi’s cyclic sum formula for multiple zeta-star values is generalized by Igarashi with one or two parameters. In this article, we give a possible answer for one of his problems about a generalization with three parameters.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11M32.

1.
Introduction

We sometimes encounter a remarkable symmetry on computing a cyclic sum on certain multiple nested sum.
For the case of the
multiple zeta-star value

\zeta^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\coloneqq\sum_{0<m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{d}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{m_{i}^{k_{i}}},

defined for positive integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
(
k_{d}\neq 1
), Ohno–Wakabayashi found the following formula.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
OW
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
be positive integers and assume
k\coloneqq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}\neq d
.
Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{i}-2}\zeta^{\star}(j+1,k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{d},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i}-j)=k\zeta(k+1).

Generalizing this theorem, Igarashi computed similar cyclic sums with two parameters. Define

Z_{I}^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d};\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{d}}\frac{(\alpha)_{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}\frac{m_{d}!}{(\alpha)_{m_{d}}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}+\beta)^{k_{1}}}\prod_{i=2}^{d}\frac{1}{(m_{i}+\alpha)(m_{i}+\beta)^{k_{i}-1}}

and

Z(a\mid b;\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+\alpha)^{a}(n+\beta)^{b}},

for positive integers
d,k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
(
k_{d}\neq 1
), integers
a
and
b
(
a+b\geq 2
), complex numbers
\alpha,\beta
satisfying
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)>0
and
\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
I2
, Theorem 1 (ii)]
)
.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
be positive integers and assume
k\coloneqq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}\neq d
.
Then we have

(1.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{i}-2}Z_{I}^{\star}(j+1,k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{d},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i}-j)=(k-d)Z(d\mid k-d+1;\alpha,\beta)+dZ(d+1\mid k-d;\alpha,\beta).

Remark 1.3
.

(1)

Theorem
1.2
recovers Theorem
1.1
by putting
\alpha=\beta=1
.

(2)

Igarashi
[
I1
, Theorem 1.1 (ii)]
obtained the special (
\alpha=\beta
) case of Theorem
1.2
before he found it.

In his paper
[
I2
]
, Igarashi proposed three problems. One of them
[
I2
, (C3)]
asks us if there is a nice generalization of Theorem
1.2
to the following series with three parameters:

Z_{I}^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{d}}\frac{(\alpha)_{m_{1}}(\beta)_{m_{1}}}{(\gamma)_{m_{1}}m_{1}!}\frac{(\gamma)_{m_{d}}m_{d}!}{(\alpha)_{m_{d}}(\beta)_{m_{d}}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}+\gamma)^{k_{1}}}\prod_{i=2}^{d}\frac{1}{(m_{i}+\alpha)(m_{i}+\beta)(m_{i}+\gamma)^{k_{i}-2}},

where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
are with the same condition as above and
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
are complex numbers satisfying
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)>0
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
.

The purpose of this article is to give a partially affirmative answer for it.
We use the following auxiliary sums:

Z_{II}^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d};\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq m_{0}\leq m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{d}}\frac{(\alpha)_{m_{0}}(\beta)_{m_{0}}}{(\gamma)_{m_{0}}m_{0}!}\frac{(\gamma)_{m_{d}}m_{d}!}{(\alpha)_{m_{d}}(\beta)_{m_{d}}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{(m_{i}+\alpha)(m_{i}+\beta)(m_{i}+\gamma)^{k_{i}-2}}

and

Z(a\mid b\mid c;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+\alpha)^{a}(n+\beta)^{b}(n+\gamma)^{c}},

for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
with the same condition,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
and integers
a,b
and
c
satisfying
a+b+c\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.4

(
=
Theorem
2.11
)
.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
be positive integers such that
k_{i}\geq 2
for every
1\leq i\leq d
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
complex numbers satisfying
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)>0
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
.
Then we have

(1.2)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{i}-3}Z_{I}^{\star}(j+1,k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{d},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i}-j;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)+(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^{d}Z_{II}^{\star}(k_{i},\ldots,k_{d},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},2;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\\
=dZ(d\mid d+1\mid k-2d;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)+dZ(d+1\mid d\mid k-2d;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)+(k-2d)Z(d\mid d\mid k-2d+1;\alpha,\beta,\gamma).\end{split}

Remark 1.5
.

The condition of
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
in Theorem
1.4
is sometimes referred as
having maximal height
in the context of multiple zeta values.

Our main theorem can deduces a “maximal height” case of Igarashi’s formula (
1.1
) as follows: put
\beta=\gamma
in Theorem
1.4
.
Then the right-hand side becomes

(1.3)

dZ(d\mid d+1\mid k-2d;\alpha,\beta,\beta)+dZ(d+1\mid d\mid k-2d;\alpha,\beta,\beta)+(k-2d)Z(d\mid d\mid k-2d+1;\alpha,\beta,\beta)\\
=(k-d)Z(d\mid k-d+1;\alpha,\beta)+dZ(d+1\mid k-d;\alpha,\beta),

and for
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}^{d}
and a positive integer
k
, we have

\alpha Z_{II}^{\star}(k+1,\mathbf{k};\alpha,\beta,\beta)=Z_{I}^{\star}(k,\mathbf{k};\alpha,\beta),

since the equality

\alpha\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{(\alpha)_{m}}{m!}=(\alpha+n)\frac{(\alpha)_{n}}{n!}

holds for
n\geq 0
.

2.
A proof

2.1.
Computation on single sums

Hereafter we fix a tuple
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
with the condition in Theorem
1.4
and drop its information from notations appearing here. Denote
\alpha+\beta-\gamma
by
t
.
We put

(2.1)

\displaystyle g_{m}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(\alpha)_{m+\varepsilon}(\beta)_{m+\varepsilon^{\prime}}}{(\gamma)_{m}m!}

for a non-negative integer
m
and
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
.
We consider
g_{-1}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
as
0
.
Note that reccurence relations

(2.3)

\frac{g_{m}^{1,0}}{\alpha+m}=\frac{g_{m}^{0,1}}{\beta+m}=\frac{g_{m}^{1,1}}{(\alpha+m)(\beta+m)}=g_{m}^{0,0},

(2.4)

g_{m+1}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\frac{(\alpha+m+\varepsilon)(\beta+m+\varepsilon^{\prime})}{(\gamma+m)(m+1)}g_{m}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}

and

(2.5)

g_{m+1}^{0,0}=\frac{g_{m}^{1,1}}{(m+\gamma)(m+1)}

hold.
Moreover, we put

F_{m,n}\coloneqq g_{m}^{1,1}\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{l-m}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}

for non-negative integers
m
and
n
satisfying
m\leq n
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For non-negative integers
m\leq n
, we have

F_{m,n}=\sum_{h=0}^{m}g_{h}^{0,0}\left(\frac{1}{(n+1-h)g_{n+1}^{0,0}}-\sum_{n<l}\frac{t}{g_{l}^{1,1}}\right)

Proof.

By definition and (
2.3
), we have

\frac{1}{g_{m}^{1,1}}F_{m,n}=\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{(l-m)(\alpha+l)(\beta+l)g_{l}^{0,0}}.

The partial fraction decomposition

(2.6)

\frac{1}{(l-m)(\alpha+l)(\beta+l)}=\frac{1}{(\alpha+m)(\beta+m)}\left(\frac{1}{l-m}-\frac{\alpha+\beta+m+l}{(\alpha+l)(\beta+l)}\right),

deduces

\frac{(\alpha+m)(\beta+m)}{g_{m}^{1,1}}F_{m,n}=\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{l-m}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{0,0}}-\sum_{n<l}\frac{\alpha+\beta+m+l}{(\alpha+l)(\beta+l)g_{l}^{0,0}}

and thus (
2.3
) shows

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{(\alpha+m)(\beta+m)}{g_{m}^{1,1}}F_{m,n}+\sum_{n<l}\frac{\alpha+\beta+m+l}{g_{l}^{1,1}}&=\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{l-m}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{0,0}}\\
&=\frac{1}{(n+1-m)g_{n+1}^{0,0}}+\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{l+1-m}\frac{1}{g_{l+1}^{0,0}}\end{split}

Denote by
A
the second sum on the right-hand side.
Using the reccurence relation (
2.5
), we obtain

A=\sum_{n<l}\frac{(l+\gamma)(l+1)}{l+1-m}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}

Then we can apply the decomposition

(2.8)

\frac{(l+\gamma)(l+1)}{l+1-m}=l+\gamma+m+\frac{(\gamma+m-1)m}{l+1-m}

and get

A=\sum_{n<l}\left(l+\gamma+m+\frac{(\gamma+m-1)m}{l+1-m}\right)\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}=\sum_{n\leq l}\frac{l+\gamma+m}{g_{l}^{1,1}}+\frac{(\gamma+m-1)m}{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}F_{m-1,n}.

Combining this and (
2.7
), it follows that

\frac{(\alpha+m)(\beta+m)}{g_{m}^{1,1}}F_{m,n}-\frac{(\gamma+m-1)m}{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}F_{m-1,n}=\frac{1}{(n+1-m)g_{n+1}^{0,0}}-\sum_{n<l}\frac{t}{g_{l}^{1,1}}

holds and the left-hand side is equal to
(F_{m,n}-F_{m-1,n})/g_{m}^{0,0}
from the reccurence equations (
2.3
) and (
2.5
).
Therefore, multiplying
g_{m}^{0,0}
on both sides and then summing them up over
m
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For non-negative integers
m
and
n
, if
m\leq n
we have

F_{m,n}-(\alpha+\beta+m+n)\frac{g_{m}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}=\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{n-h}\frac{g_{h}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{0,0}}-t\sum_{h=0}^{m}g_{h}^{0,0}\sum_{n\leq l}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}.

Proof.

From (
2.7
), (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), we see that

(2.9)

\displaystyle F_{m,n}

\displaystyle=g_{m}^{1,1}\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{l-m}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}

(2.10)

\displaystyle=g_{m}^{1,1}\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{(l-m)(\alpha+l)(\beta+l)}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{0,0}}

(2.11)

\displaystyle=g_{m}^{0,0}\sum_{n<l}\left(\frac{1}{l-m}-\frac{\alpha+\beta+m+l}{(\alpha+l)(\beta+l)}\right)\frac{1}{g_{l}^{0,0}}

holds.
Since the first sum on the right-hand side is computed by (
2.5
) as

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{l-m}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{0,0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n<l}\frac{l(l+\gamma-1)}{l-m}\frac{1}{g_{l-1}^{1,1}}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n<l}\left(\frac{m(\gamma+m-1)}{l-m}+l+\gamma+m-1\right)\frac{1}{g_{l-1}^{1,1}}

where we used (
2.8
) in the second equality, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{m,n}&=g_{m}^{0,0}\sum_{n<l}\left(\frac{m(\gamma+m-1)}{l-m}+l+\gamma+m-1\right)\frac{1}{g_{l-1}^{1,1}}-g_{m}^{0,0}\sum_{n<l}\frac{\alpha+\beta+m+l}{(\alpha+l)(\beta+l)}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{0,0}}\\
&=g_{m-1}^{1,1}\sum_{n<l}\frac{1}{l-m}\frac{1}{g_{l-1}^{1,1}}+g_{m}^{0,0}\sum_{n\leq l}\frac{l+\gamma+m}{g_{l}^{1,1}}-g_{m}^{0,0}\sum_{n<l}\frac{\alpha+\beta+m+l}{g_{l}^{1,1}}\\
&=F_{m-1,n}+\frac{1}{n-m+1}\frac{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}+(\alpha+\beta+m+n)\frac{g_{m}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}-tg_{m}^{0,0}\sum_{n\leq l}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}.\end{split}

Then we use the decomposition (
2.6
) on the second term, namely,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-m+1}\frac{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-m+1)(\alpha+n)(\beta+n)}\frac{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}{g_{n}^{0,0}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\alpha+m-1)(\beta+m-1)}\left(\frac{1}{n-m+1}-\frac{\alpha+\beta+m+n-1}{(\alpha+n)(\beta+n)}\right)\frac{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}{g_{n}^{0,0}}

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-m+1}\frac{g_{m-1}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{0,0}}-(\alpha+\beta+m+n-1)\frac{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}.

Plugging this calculation in (
2.14
), we can make telescopic terms as

F_{m,n}-F_{m-1,n}+(\alpha+\beta+m+n-1)\frac{g_{m-1}^{1,1}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}-(\alpha+\beta+m+n)\frac{g_{m}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}=\frac{1}{n-m+1}\frac{g_{m-1}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{0,0}}-tg_{m}^{0,0}\sum_{n\leq l}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}.

By taking the sum on both sides, it is shown that

F_{m,n}-(\alpha+\beta+m+n)\frac{g_{m}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{1,1}}=\sum_{h=0}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{n-h+1}\frac{g_{h-1}^{0,0}}{g_{n}^{0,0}}-tg_{h}^{0,0}\sum_{n\leq l}\frac{1}{g_{l}^{1,1}}\right)

holds. This deduces the desired result by shifting the variable on the first sum on the right-hand side and using
g_{-1}^{0,0}=0
.
∎

2.2.
Connected sum of Hoffman–Ohno type

Put
X\coloneqq\{\leq,=\}
and denote by
X^{\bullet}
the free monoid generated by
X
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\circ_{1},\ldots,\circ_{d}
(
d\geq 1
) be elements of
X
and
s=(\circ_{1},\ldots,\circ_{d})
be a non-empty word of
X^{\bullet}
.
For integers
1\leq i<j\leq d+1
, we put

S(s)\coloneqq\{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d+1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}\mid m_{1}\circ_{1}\cdots\circ_{d}m_{d+1}\}

and

S_{i,j}(s)\coloneqq\{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d+1})\in S(s)\mid m_{i}\neq m_{j}\}.

The following lemma is obvious but useful assertions to prove the main theorem.

Lemma 2.4
.

For an integer
d\geq 2
, we have the following.

(1)

S_{1,d}(\leq^{d-1})=S_{1,d-1}(\leq^{d-1})\sqcup S_{d-1,d}(=^{d-2},\leq)
.

(2)

S_{1,d}(\leq^{d-1})=S_{2,d}(\leq^{d-1})\sqcup S_{1,2}(\leq,=^{d-2})
.

(3)

S_{1,d}(\leq^{d-1})\sqcup S(=^{d-1})=S(\leq^{d-1})
.

Moreover, for a positive integer
d
,
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
and
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{d}
, we define

\Pi\binom{\mathbf{m}}{\mathbf{k}}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{(m_{i}+\alpha)(m_{i}+\beta)(m_{i}+\gamma)^{k_{i}-2}}.

Note that
\Pi\binom{m}{2}g_{m}^{1,1}=g_{m}^{0,0}
holds for every
m\geq 0
.

Remark 2.5
.

With these notations, the definition of the series
Z_{I}^{\star}
is written as

Z_{I}^{\star}(k,\mathbf{k})=\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},l^{\prime})\in S(\leq^{d})}\frac{g_{l}^{0,0}}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{0,0}}\frac{1}{(l+\gamma)^{k}}\Pi\binom{\mathbf{m},l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{k}}

for
k\geq 1
and
\mathbf{k}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2})
.

Definition 2.6
.

For positive integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
(
d\geq 2
) for some
k_{i}\geq 2
, we define

H(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\coloneqq\sum_{(m_{1},\mathbf{m},m_{d})\in S_{1,d}(\leq^{d-1})}\frac{g_{m_{1}}^{0,0}}{g_{m_{d}}^{0,0}}\frac{1}{(m_{1}+\gamma)^{k_{1}-1}}\Pi\binom{\mathbf{m},m_{d}}{k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}}\frac{1}{m_{d}-m_{1}}.

Remark 2.7
.

By definition, the equality

H(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\coloneqq\sum_{(m_{1},\mathbf{m},m_{d})\in S_{1,d}(\leq^{d-1})}\frac{g_{m_{1}}^{1,1}}{g_{m_{d}}^{0,0}}\Pi\binom{m_{1},\mathbf{m},m_{d}}{1+k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}}\frac{1}{m_{d}-m_{1}}.

holds.

2.3.
Transport relations

Proposition 2.8
.

For non-negative integers
d
and
j
, a positive integer
k\geq 2
and
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{d}
, if
0\leq j\leq k-2
we have

(2.18)

H(j+1,\mathbf{k},k-j)=H(j+2,\mathbf{k},k-j-1)-Z_{I}^{\star}(j+1,\mathbf{k},k-j)+Z(d+1\mid d+1\mid w-2d-1),

where
w
is the sum of all components of
\mathbf{k}
and
k
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

H(j+1,\mathbf{k},k-j)=\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},l^{\prime})\in S_{1,d+2}(\leq^{d+1})}\frac{g_{l}^{0,0}}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{0,0}}\frac{1}{l^{\prime}-l}\frac{1}{(l+\gamma)^{j}}\Pi\binom{\mathbf{m},l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{k},k-j}.

Using the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{l^{\prime}-l}\frac{1}{l^{\prime}+\gamma}=\left(\frac{1}{l^{\prime}-l}-\frac{1}{l^{\prime}+\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{l+\gamma},

we obtain

(2.19)

\displaystyle H(j+1,\mathbf{k},k-j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},l^{\prime})\in S_{1,d+2}(\leq^{d+1})}\frac{g_{l}^{0,0}}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{0,0}}\frac{1}{(l+\gamma)^{j+1}}\left(\frac{1}{l^{\prime}-l}-\frac{1}{l^{\prime}+\gamma}\right)\Pi\binom{\mathbf{m},l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{k},k-j-1}

(2.20)

\displaystyle=H(j+2,\mathbf{k},k-j-1)-Z_{I}^{\star}(j+1,\mathbf{k},k-j)+Z(d+1\mid d+1\mid w-2d-1),

where we used Lemma
2.4
(
3
) in the last equality.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
d,k,\mathbf{k}
and
w
be with the same conditions as Proposition
2.8
.
Then we have

(2.21)

H(1,\mathbf{k},k)=H(k-1,\mathbf{k},2)-\sum_{j=0}^{k-3}Z_{I}^{\star}(j+1,\mathbf{k},k-j)+(k-2)Z(d+1;d+1;w-2d-1).

Proof.

We get this claim by summing up (
2.18
) over
j=0,\ldots,k-3
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
d,k,\mathbf{k}
and
w
be with the same conditions as Proposition
2.8
.
Then we have

(2.22)

H(k-1,\mathbf{k},2)=H(1,k,\mathbf{k})-tZ_{II}^{\star}(k,\mathbf{k},2)+Z(d+1\mid d+2\mid w-2d-2)+Z(d+2\mid d+1\mid w-2d-2).

Proof.

The definition of
H
and (
2.3
) yield

H(k-1,\mathbf{k},2)=\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},m,l^{\prime})\in S_{1,d+2}(\leq^{d+1})}\frac{g_{l}^{1,1}}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{1,1}}\frac{1}{l^{\prime}-l}\Pi\binom{l,\mathbf{m},m}{k,\mathbf{k}}.

Using Lemma
2.4
(
1
), we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H(k-1,\mathbf{k},2)&=\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},m)\in S_{1,d+1}(\leq^{d})}\Pi\binom{l,\mathbf{m},m}{k,\mathbf{k}}\cdot g_{l}^{1,1}\sum_{m\leq l^{\prime}}\frac{1}{l^{\prime}-l}\frac{1}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{1,1}}+\sum_{l\geq 0}\Pi\binom{\overbrace{l,\ldots,l}^{d+1}}{k,\mathbf{k}}\cdot g_{l}^{1,1}\sum_{l<l^{\prime}}\frac{1}{l^{\prime}-l}\frac{1}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{1,1}}\\
&=\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},m)\in S_{1,d+1}(\leq^{d})}\Pi\binom{l,\mathbf{m},m}{k,\mathbf{k}}F_{l,m-1}+\sum_{l\geq 0}\Pi\binom{\overbrace{l,\ldots,l}^{d+1}}{k,\mathbf{k}}F_{l,l}.\end{split}

Let us calculate the sums on the right-hand side.
For the first term, by Lemma
2.1
we obtain

(2.24)

\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},m)\in S_{1,d+1}(\leq^{d})}\Pi\binom{l,\mathbf{m},m}{k,\mathbf{k}}F_{l,m-1}=\sum_{(l,\mathbf{m},m)\in S_{1,d+1}(\leq^{d})}\Pi\binom{l,\mathbf{m},m}{k,\mathbf{k}}\sum_{0\leq h\leq l}g_{h}^{0,0}\left(\frac{1}{m-h}\frac{1}{g_{m}^{0,0}}-\sum_{m\leq l^{\prime}}\frac{t}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{1,1}}\right).

On the other hand, using Lemma
2.2
we compute the second term as

(2.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{l\geq 0}\Pi\binom{\overbrace{l,\ldots,l}^{d+1}}{k,\mathbf{k}}F_{l,l}&=\sum_{l\geq 0}\Pi\binom{\overbrace{l,\ldots,l}^{d+1}}{k,\mathbf{k}}\left((\alpha+\beta+2l)\frac{g_{l}^{0,0}}{g_{l}^{1,1}}+\sum_{0\leq h<l}\frac{1}{l-h}\frac{g_{h}^{0,0}}{g_{l}^{0,0}}-t\sum_{0\leq h\leq l}g_{h}^{0,0}\sum_{l\leq l^{\prime}}\frac{1}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{1,1}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{l\geq 0}\Pi\binom{\overbrace{l,\ldots,l}^{d+1}}{k,\mathbf{k}}\left(\frac{1}{l+\alpha}+\frac{1}{l+\beta}+\sum_{0\leq h<l}\frac{1}{l-h}\frac{g_{h}^{0,0}}{g_{l}^{0,0}}-t\sum_{0\leq h\leq l}g_{h}^{0,0}\sum_{l\leq l^{\prime}}\frac{1}{g_{l^{\prime}}^{1,1}}\right).\end{split}

Combining (
2.24
), (
2.25
) and (
2.23
), it follows that

Flux Globalization Based Well-Balanced Path-Conservative Central-Upwind Schemes for Shallow Water Linearized Moment Equations
Yangyang Cao, Qian Huang, Julian Koellermeier, Alexander Kurganov, and Yongle Liu

Abstract
We develop second-order path-conservative central-upwind (PCCU) schemes for the hyperbolic shallow water linearized moment equations (HSWLME), which are an extension of standard depth-averaged models for free-surface flows. The proposed PCCU schemes are constructed via flux globalization strategies adapted to the nonconservative form via a path-conservative finite-volume method. The resulting scheme is well-balanced (WB) in the sense that it is capable of exactly preserving physically relevant steady states including moving-water ones. We validate the proposed scheme on several benchmarks, including smooth solutions, small perturbation of steady states, and dam-break scenarios. These results demonstrate that our flux globalization based WB PCCU schemes provide a reliable framework for computing solutions of shallow water moment models with nonlinear and nonconservative features.



Key words:
Shallow water linearized moment equations; well-balanced schemes; flux globalization; path-conservative central-upwind
schemes; nonconservative terms.

AMS subject classification:
76M12, 65M08, 86-08, 35L65, 35L67.

1
Introduction

The accurate computation of free-surface flows depends on the combination of mathematical models and tailored numerical methods. The most
simple one-dimensional shallow water equations (SWE) result from depth-averaging the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, but only take
into account the water depth
h(t,x)
and the depth-averaged velocity
u(t,x)
, therefore not modeling the dynamical evolution of variations
from a constant velocity profile. Various models improve upon the standard SWE, for example, by considering multiple layers

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
27
]
or non-hydrostatic effects
[
4
,
6
,
28
,
55
]
. Recently, families of so-called moment models
that include evolution equations for a polynomial expansion of a velocity profile have been derived. They all assume an expansion of the
horizontal velocity
u(t,x,z)
in vertical direction
z
, that is,
u(t,x,z)=u(t,x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}(z)
, with depth-averaged
velocity
u
, basis coefficients
\alpha_{i}
, scaled Legendre polynomials
\phi_{i}
, and expansion order
N
; see
[
38
]
.

This paper focuses on the development of highly accurate and robust numerical methods for the arbitrary order hyperbolic shallow water
linearized moment equations (HSWLME) that take into account nonflat bottom topography and friction terms. In the general
N
-moment case,
the HSWLME read as (see, e.g.,
[
36
]
)

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}=Q(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}+\bm{S}(\bm{U})+\bm{P}(\bm{U}),

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle\bm{U}=(h,hu,h\alpha_{1},\ldots,h\alpha_{N})^{\top},\quad\bm{S}(\bm{U})=(0,-gh\partial_{x}Z,0,\ldots,0)^{\top},\quad Q={\rm diag}(0,0,u,\ldots,u),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\bm{F}(\bm{U})=\Big(hu,hu_{2}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}+\frac{1}{3}h\alpha_{1}^{2}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2N+1}h\alpha_{N}^{2},2hu\alpha_{1},\ldots,2hu\alpha_{N}\Big)^{\top},

and
\bm{P}(\bm{U})
is a friction term. In (
1.2
),
x
is a spatial variable,
t
is time,
h=h(x,t)
is the water depth,
u=u(x,t)
is
the mean horizontal water velocity,
g
is an acceleration due to gravity,
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}(x,t)
,
i=1,\ldots,N
are coefficients of the
velocity profile, and
Z(x)
is the bottom topography. Finally, the friction term
\bm{P}=(0,P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{N})^{\top}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{0}=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}\Big(u+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\Big),

(1.3)

\displaystyle P_{i}=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(2i+1)\Big(u+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\Big)-4\frac{\nu}{h}(2i+1)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}a_{i,j}\alpha_{j},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

where
\nu
and
\lambda
are positive coefficients representing the kinematic viscosity and slip length, respectively, and

a_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\mbox{if $i+j$ is even},\\
&\frac{1}{2}\min(i-1,j)(\min(i-1,j)+1)&&\mbox{if $i+j$ is odd}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

Shallow water moment models, which take into account horizontal velocity changes over the vertical direction, were first developed in

[
38
]
based on the Legendre polynomial-based expansions of the velocity modeling the deviation from a constant velocity profile. A
further analysis showed that those models suffer from loss of hyperbolicity, which was solved by a hyperbolic regularization strategy in

[
37
]
, resulting in a globally hyperbolic shallow water moment model. Followed by successful applications and extensions of the
models (see e.g.,
[
53
,
35
,
29
,
34
,
30
]
), steady states of shallow water moment
models were first investigated in
[
36
]
, which led to the development of the HSWLME, that can be considered as the simplest shallow
water moment model, in which the nonlinear contributions of the basis coefficients is neglected in the higher-order moment equations, while
keeping the mass and momentum equations unchanged. We refer the reader to
[
36
]
for the hyperbolicity, steady states, and
Rankine-Hugoniot conditions of the frictionless version of the HSWLME.

Designing a good numerical scheme for (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) is a challenging task for a number of reasons. First, the presence of the
nonconservative product term
Q(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}
makes it impossible to understand weak solutions of (
1.1
) in the sense of distributions
(instead weak solutions can be understood as Borel measures; see
[
24
,
44
,
45
]
). Second, the well-balanced (WB) property is
crucial (and not easy to achieve, especially due to the presence of the friction terms), as it allows to exactly preserve some of the
physically relevant steady-state solutions satisfying

\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}-Q(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}-\bm{S}(\bm{U})+\bm{P}(\bm{U})=\bm{0},

(1.5)

and hence to capture their small perturbation on practically affordable coarse meshes. Note that the presence of the friction term

\bm{P}(\bm{U})
in (
1.5
) makes the structure of the steady states substantially more complicated than in the frictionless case studied
in
[
36
]
.

The concept of measure-valued solutions was utilized in
[
12
,
49
]
to develop path-conservative numerical methods, which were then
successfully applied to a variety of nonconservative hyperbolic systems; see, e.g.,

[
26
,
15
,
7
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
and references therein. Most of these path-conservative schemes are WB, but they
can often only preserve very simple steady states like the “lake-at-rest” ones since the WB property is enforced in a rather artificial
way by modifying the numerical viscosity terms.

A more systematic approach to develop WB schemes for hyperbolic systems of balance laws is based on a flux globalization, which was
originally introduced in
[
11
,
25
,
31
,
47
]
. To apply a flux globalization approach to (
1.1
), one has to first rewrite
it in a quasi-conservative form

\displaystyle\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{K}_{x}=\bm{0},

(1.6)

where
\bm{K}(\bm{U})
is the following global flux:

\bm{K}(\bm{U})=\bm{F}(\bm{U})-\bm{R}(\bm{U}),\quad\bm{R}(\bm{U})=\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x}\Big[Q(\bm{U}(\xi,t))\bm{U}_{\xi}(\xi,t)+\bm{S}(\bm{U}(\xi,t))+\bm{P}(\bm{U}(\xi,t))\Big]{\rm d}\xi,

(1.7)

where
\widehat{x}
is an arbitrary number. Steady states can then be written as
\bm{K}(\bm{U})\equiv{\bf Const}
and according to the flux
globalization method introduced in
[
21
]
, one can design a WB scheme for (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) by reconstructing
\bm{K}
instead
of
\bm{U}
and by applying a Riemann-problem-solver-free central-upwind (CU) scheme to (
1.6
). This method was successfully applied to
several hyperbolic systems of balanced laws, in, e.g.,
[
19
,
41
,
17
,
20
,
23
]
.

These flux globalization based schemes take advantage of the CU numerical fluxes, which were originally introduced in
[
39
,
42
,
43
]

for general multidimensional nonlinear hyperbolic (systems of) PDEs and can be directly implemented for approximating global fluxes. The
applicability of the aforementioned flux globalization based CU schemes, however, is limited to the case when the global terms

\bm{R}(\bm{U})
in (
1.7
) are continuous, which is not the case when the nonconservative term
Q(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}
is present. In this case,
the integral in (
1.7
) should be computed using a path-conservative integration technique as it was done in
[
40
]
, where new
flux globalization based WB path-conservative CU (PCCU) schemes were introduced. In these schemes, we first identify the equilibrium
variables
\bm{E}(\bm{U})
, then reconstruct them, use the reconstructed values of
\bm{E}
to obtain the corresponding values of
\bm{U}
, and
evaluate the global CU numerical fluxes. The new flux globalization based WB PCCU schemes are capable of preserving a much wider range of
steady states, as was demonstrated in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
22
,
18
]
, where these schemes were applied to many different
nonconservative hyperbolic systems.

While the HSWLME model can be considered a simplification of the shallow water moment models derived in
[
38
]
, they successfully
restore global hyperbolicity and allow for the analytical computation of steady-states. The application of the HSWLME model in more
simulations, however, is so far limited due to the lack of appropriate numerical schemes that preserve the structural properties of the
equations, for example, the steady states in the presence of friction.

In this paper, our goal is to design a second-order flux globalization based WB PCCU scheme for the HSWLME (
1.1
)–(
1.4
). To this
end, we first discuss the steady-state solutions of the studied system in §
2
, and then, in §
3
, we develop the new
scheme. In §
4
, we demonstrate the performance of the proposed scheme on a number of numerical examples. The paper ends with
concluding remarks.

2
Steady States

In this section, we study the steady states of the HSWLME (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) that satisfy (
1.5
), which can be equivalently
written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} (hu)_{x}&=0,\\
\Big(hu^{2}+\frac{1}{2}gh^{2}+\frac{1}{3}h\alpha_{1}^{2}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2N+1}h\alpha_{N}^{2}\Big)_{x}&=-ghZ_{x}+P_{0},\\
(2hu\alpha_{1})_{x}&=u(h\alpha_{1})_{x}+P_{1},\\
&\qquad\qquad~\,\vdots\\
(2hu\alpha_{N})_{x}&=u(h\alpha_{N})_{x}+P_{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

The simplest “lake-at-rest” steady states are

\displaystyle u\equiv 0,\quad h+Z\equiv{\rm Const},\quad\alpha_{i}\equiv 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

(2.1)

The non-trivial, moving-water equilibria are

\displaystyle q:=hu\equiv{\rm Const}\neq 0,\quad E:=\frac{u^{2}}{2}+g(h+Z)+\frac{3}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{2}}{2i+1}-{\cal P}\equiv{\rm Const},

(2.2)

\displaystyle E_{i}:=\frac{\alpha_{i}}{h}-{\cal P}_{i}\equiv{\rm Const},\quad i=1,\ldots N,

where

{\cal P}=\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x}\frac{P_{0}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)\alpha_{i}(\xi,t)}{(2i+1)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi,\quad{\cal P}_{i}=\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi,\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

(2.3)

Notice that (
1.5
) can be rewritten in the following form:

\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}-Q(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}-\bm{S}(\bm{U})-\bm{P}(\bm{U})=M(\bm{U})\bm{E}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(2.4)

where
\bm{E}:=(q,E,E_{1},\ldots E_{N})^{\top}
are the equilibrium variables and
M
is an
(N+2)\times(N+2)
matrix

M(\bm{U}):=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\ldots&0\\
u&h&-\frac{1}{3}h^{2}\alpha_{1}&\ldots&-\frac{1}{2N+1}h^{2}\alpha_{N}\\
2\alpha_{1}&0&h^{2}u&&\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\\
2\alpha_{N}&&&&h^{2}u\end{pmatrix}.

(2.5)

As in
[
40
]
, (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) are going to be used to develop a flux globalization WB PCCU scheme.

3
Flux Globalization Based WB PCCU Scheme

In this section, we develop a second-order flux globalization based WB PCCU scheme for the HSWLME (
1.1
)–(
1.4
).

We first introduce the finite-volume cells
C_{k}:=\big[x_{k-\frac{1}{2}},x_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\big]
of uniform (for simplicity of presentation) size

x_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta x
centered at
x_{k}=\big(x_{k-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\big)/2
,
k=1,\ldots,N_{x}
. Assuming that the approximate solution, realized in
terms of its cell averages
\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}(t)\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{C_{k}}\bm{U}(x,t)\,{\rm d}x
, is available at a certain time level

t
, we evolve it in time by solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}=-\frac{\bm{{\cal K}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{{\cal K}}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

(3.1)

where
\bm{{\cal K}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}
are the WB PCCU fluxes, given by (see
[
40
]
)

\bm{{\cal K}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{a^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{K}^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{K}^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{a^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}a^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(\widehat{\bm{U}}^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-\widehat{\bm{U}}^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

(3.2)

Here,
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{K}\bm{(}\bm{U}^{\pm}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{)}
,
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\widehat{\bm{U}}^{\pm}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}
are two second-order approximations of
the point values
\bm{U}(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}},t)
, and
a_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
are the one-sided local speeds of propagation, which can be estimated using the
eigenvalues of the matrix
\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}-Q
. Based on the eigenvalues computed in
[
36
]
, the simplest estimate
is

\displaystyle a_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}=\max\left\{u_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}+\sqrt{gh_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{3\big((\alpha_{i})_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)^{2}}{2i+1}},\,u_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}+\sqrt{gh_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{3\big((\alpha_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}\big)^{2}}{2i+1}},\,0\right\},

(3.3)

\displaystyle a_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}=\min\left\{u_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}-\sqrt{gh_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{3\big(\alpha_{i})_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)^{2}}{2i+1}},\,u_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}-\sqrt{gh_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{3\big((\alpha_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}\big)^{2}}{2i+1}},\,0\right\}.

Notice that all of the indexed quantities in (
3.1
)–(
3.3
) are time-dependent, but from here on we omit this dependence for the
sake of brevity.

3.1
Well-Balanced Reconstruction of the One-Sided Point Values

In order to use (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), one needs to compute the one-sided point values
h_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
,
q_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
,

(\alpha_{i})_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
, and
Z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
. To make the resulting scheme WB, we need to perform piecewise linear reconstruction for the
equilibrium variables
\bm{E}
. To this end, we start by computing the cell center point values of
\bm{E}
out of the available cell averages

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}
,
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}
,
(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h\alpha_{i}$\kern 0.0pt}}})_{k}
, and the point values
Z_{k}:=Z(x_{k})
.

First, we use (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) to obtain

E_{k}=\frac{(u_{k})^{2}}{2}+g(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}+Z_{k})+\frac{3}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\alpha_{i})_{k}^{2}}{2i+1}-{\cal P}_{k},\quad(E_{i})_{k}=\frac{(\alpha_{i})_{k}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}}-({\cal P}_{i})_{k},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

where
u_{k}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}/\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}
,
(\alpha_{i})_{k}=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h\alpha_{i}$\kern 0.0pt}}})_{k}/\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}
, and

{\cal P}_{k}=\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x_{k}}\frac{P_{0}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x_{j}}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)\alpha_{i}(\xi,t)}{(2i+1)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi,\quad({\cal P}_{i})_{k}=\int\limits_{\widehat{x}}^{x_{k}}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi,\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

Notice that
{\cal P}_{k}
and
({\cal P}_{i})_{k}
are global integral terms, we therefore use a recursive way and apply the trapezoidal rule to
evaluate them for each
[x_{k-1},x_{k}]
interval for
k=2,\ldots,N_{x}
:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{k}

\displaystyle={\cal P}_{k-1}+\int\limits^{x_{k}}_{x_{k-1}}\frac{P_{0}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int\limits^{x_{k}}_{x_{k-1}}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)\alpha_{i}(\xi,t)}{(2i+1)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi

(3.4)

\displaystyle\approx{\cal P}_{k-1}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}\bigg[\frac{(P_{0})_{k}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}}+\frac{(P_{0})_{k-1}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k-1}}\bigg]+\frac{\Delta x}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{(P_{i})_{k}(\alpha_{i})_{k}}{{(2i+1)\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}}}+\frac{(P_{i})_{k-1}(\alpha_{i})_{k-1}}{{(2i+1)\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k-1}}}\bigg],

\displaystyle({\cal P}_{i})_{k}

\displaystyle=({\cal P}_{i})_{k-1}+\int\limits_{x_{k-1}}^{x_{k}}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi\approx({\cal P}_{i})_{k-1}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}\bigg[\frac{(P_{i})_{k}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}}+\frac{(P_{i})_{k-1}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k-1}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k-1}}\bigg],\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

where

\displaystyle(P_{0})_{k}=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}\Big(u_{k}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\alpha_{j})_{k}\Big),\quad k=1,\ldots,N_{x},

(3.5)

\displaystyle(P_{i})_{k}=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(2i+1)\Big(u_{k}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\alpha_{j})_{k}\Big)-4\frac{\nu}{h_{k}}(2i+1)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}a_{i,j}(\alpha_{j})_{k},\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

The values
{\cal P}_{1}
and
({\cal P}_{i})_{1}
, needed to start the iterations (
3.4
)–(
3.5
), are obtained using the trapezoidal
rule, which yields

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\limits^{x_{1}}_{x_{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{P_{0}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\int\limits^{x_{1}}_{x_{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)\alpha_{i}(\xi,t)}{(2i+1)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi

(3.6)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\Delta x}{4}\Bigg[\frac{(P_{0})_{1}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{1}}+\frac{(P_{0})_{\frac{1}{2}}}{h_{\frac{1}{2}}}\Bigg]+\frac{\Delta x}{4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\frac{(P_{i})_{1}(\alpha_{i})_{1}}{{(2i+1)\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{1}}}+\frac{(P_{i})_{\frac{1}{2}}(\alpha_{i})_{\frac{1}{2}}}{{(2i+1)q_{\frac{1}{2}}}}\Bigg],

\displaystyle({\cal P}_{i})_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{x_{\frac{1}{2}}}^{x_{1}}\frac{P_{i}(\xi,t)}{h(\xi,t)q(\xi,t)}\,{\rm d}\xi\approx\frac{\Delta x}{4}\Bigg[\frac{(P_{i})_{1}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{1}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{1}}+\frac{(P_{i})_{\frac{1}{2}}}{h_{\frac{1}{2}}q_{\frac{1}{2}}}\Bigg],\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

where

\displaystyle({\cal P}_{0})_{\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}\Big(u_{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\alpha_{j})_{\frac{1}{2}}\Big),

\displaystyle({\cal P}_{i})_{\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda}(2i+1)\Big(u_{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\alpha_{j})_{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)-4\frac{\nu}{h_{\frac{1}{2}}}(2i+1)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}a_{i,j}(\alpha_{j})_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

and
h_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
u_{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
(\alpha_{i})_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, are determined based on the prescribed boundary conditions.

Equipped with the point values
E_{k}
and
(E_{i})_{k}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, we proceed with performing the generalized minmod reconstruction
(provided in Appendix
A
) for the equilibrium variables
q
,
E
, and
E_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, as well as for
Z
to obtain the
left/right-sided point values
q_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
,
E_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
,
(E_{i})_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, and
Z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}

for

Remark 3.1

In (
3.4
), (
3.6
), and (
3.9
), one has to divide by
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$h$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$q$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k}

desingularization is performed as follows. If we need to divide, say,

5
Conclusion and future work

We have developed flux globalization based second-order well-balanced path-conservative central-upwind schemes for the hyperbolic shallow
water linearized moment equations. The proposed schemes combine flux globalization and path-conservative formulations tailored to
nonconservative products for a system of equations. The conducted numerical experiments confirm that the methods are accurate and
well-balanced. The obtained results also demonstrate the benefit of including higher-order moments to resolve vertical variations. Future
work includes extensions to more complex shallow water moment models, two-dimensional models, and the inclusion of more physical effects
like bedload transport, sediments, or different friction models.

Appendix A
Generalized Minmod Piecewise Linear Reconstruction

In this appendix, we describe a second-order piecewise linear reconstruction based on the generalized minmod limiter; see, e.g.,

[
46
,
48
,
54
]
.

We assume that the values
\varphi_{k}
(either the cell averages or point values) of a certain function
\varphi
at the cell centers
x=x_{k}

are available. Then a second-order non-oscillatory piecewise linear reconstruction of
\varphi
reads as

\widetilde{\varphi}(x)=\varphi_{k}+(\varphi_{x})_{k}(x-x_{k}),\quad x\in C_{k},

where the slopes
(\varphi_{x})_{k}
have to be evaluated using a nonlinear limiter. In our numerical experiments, we have used the generalized
minmod limiter (see
[
46
,
48
,
54
]
):

(\varphi_{x})_{k}={\rm minmod}\left(\theta\,\frac{\varphi_{k+1}-\varphi_{k}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\varphi_{k+1}-\varphi_{k-1}}{2\Delta x},\,\theta\,\frac{\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{k-1}}{\Delta x}\right),\quad\theta\in[1,2],

where the minmod function is defined by

\mbox{minmod}(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)&&\mbox{if}~c_{i}>0,~\forall i,\\
&\max(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)&&\mbox{if}~c_{i}<0,~\forall i,\\
&0&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

and the parameter
\theta
is used to control the amount of numerical dissipation present in the resulting scheme: larger values of
\theta

correspond to sharper but, in general, more oscillatory reconstructions.

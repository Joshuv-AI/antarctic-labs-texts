Extraction, structural characterization and SNP identification of related genes of collagen from local pigs in Guizhou
Authors: Yu Dan Zhang, Xiao Yang, Wei Xia, Ying Qu Liu, Yi Yu Zhang, Wei Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCollagen, as one of the important components of living organisms, plays a crucial role in supporting and maintaining tissue structure and biological functions. The local pig breed in Guizhou is renowned for its elastic skin, tender meat, and excellent taste. The collagen content in pig skin accounts for approximately 87.7% of the protein content. However, as a by-product after slaughter, the utilization rate of pig skin is not high. Additionally, the content and structural characteristics of collagen have a certain impact on the quality of meat.MethodIn this study, the skin and longissimus dorsi muscle of local pig breeds in Guizhou (Qiannan pig, Baixi pig, Qianbei pig, Zongdi pig, Kele pig, Congjiang pig) were used as raw materials to optimize the extraction method of collagen, and to explore the differences in the content, type and structure of collagen in the skin and longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of different pig breeds. Based on whole-gene resequencing technology, the screening and identification of differential genes and variant sites in three pig breeds, namely the two pig breeds with the greatest differences in collagen content (Baixi pig and Kele pig) and the pig breed with differences in collagen structure in skin tissue (Zongdi pig), were completed.Results and conclusionsThis study determined that the optimal conditions for extracting collagen from local pig tissues in Guizhou are: pH 2.0, enzyme dosage 2%, feed-to-liquid ratio 1:20 (w/v), temperature 37℃, enzymatic hydrolysis time 8 h. Based on the experimental results, it is speculated that the difference in collagen content in the tissues of white-washed pigs and Kela pigs may be related to the COL1A1 and COL4A4 genes and their SNP loci. The structural differences in collagen in the skin of Zongdi pigs may be related to the COL4A3 and COL4A4 genes and their SNP loci, which is helpful to reveal the molecular mechanism of the skin characteristics of local pig breeds in Guizhou, open up new ways for the breeding of characteristic pig breeds in Guizhou.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-026-12826-4.Keywords: Guizhou local pig, Collagen, Whole genome resequencing, SNP

Introduction

The rapid growth of pork consumption in China has led to a large amount of by-products from slaughtering [1], among which one of the main by-products is pigskin. Pigskin is rich in protein, and the content of collagen can reach about 87.7%. Pigskin is not only rich in collagen but also contains a variety of essential amino acids for the human body [2]. Chunnan [3] found that the collagen in pigskin has a strong antioxidant capacity. Therefore, exploring the properties of collagen in pigskin plays a significant role in enhancing its economic value. Collagen, as the main component of muscle connective tissue, is one of the important factors affecting meat quality. Its content, type, structure and degree of cross-linking are all closely related to muscle tenderness [4–6]. The unique geographical and climatic environment of Guizhou Province has given rise to many local pig breeds with distinctive local characteristics and excellent traits. The local pig breeds of Guizhou are known for their soft and glutinous skin, tender meat and excellent flavor [7].

Therefore, this experiment intends to use the skin tissue and the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue of local pig breeds in Guizhou as the experimental materials to explore a more suitable method for extracting collagen and analyze the structure of the collagen extract in the skin and longissimus dorsi muscle tissue. Determine the differences in collagen content and structure in different tissues of different pig breeds in Guizhou Province, screen out different pig breeds for whole genome resequencing, screen out SNP and INDEL variant sites in the genomes of different pig breeds based on whole genome resequencing technology, and use bioinformatics methods to pairwise compare and enrich the differential variant sites and genes in different populations. To explore the reasons for the differences in collagen content among different pig breeds, verify the SNP loci enriched in the different populations through PCR, and identify the mutation sites that may affect collagen deposition. The enriched differential genes were detected by real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR, and the expression of collagen-related genes in different tissues of different pig breeds was analyzed. This research can provide a basis for the development and utilization of by-products of superior pig breeds in Guizhou Province and lay a foundation for exploring the excellent traits of local pig breeds in Guizhou.

Materials and methods

Test samples

Skin tissues and longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of recently aged Baixi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs, Zongdi pigs, Qiannan pigs and Qianbei pigs (6 each, half male and half female) were collected at the Guizhou Conservation Farm. Ear tissues, skin tissues and longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of 30 Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs were randomly collected. The tissue samples were washed and treated with PBS and then placed in sterilized cryopstorage tubes. It was then quickly placed in a liquid nitrogen tank for rapid freezing and returned to the laboratory for storage at −80℃. In this study, a mixture of inert gases nitrogen and CO2 was used to ensure that pigs lost consciousness before slaughter sampling. This experiment was approved by the Subcommittee Committee on Laboratory Animal Ethics of Guizhou University (Approval Number: EAE-GZU-2022-E055).

Extraction of collagen from tissues

The skin and longissimus dorsal muscle tissues of the previously collected Zongdi pigs, Qianbei pigs, Qiannan pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs and Baixi pigs were thoroughly cleaned. The subcutaneous fat and surface hair were cut off, weighed and then completely degreased with sodium hydroxide solution at a ratio of 1:10 for 12 h. After double steaming water washing, drying and crushing, they were finally crushed. The treated tissue powder was soaked in a pre-prepared 20% sodium chloride solution at a ratio of 1:20 for 12 h to remove impurities. It was then placed in a centrifuge at 9000 r/min for 15 min, and the precipitate was retained for enzymatic hydrolysis reaction, the enzyme dosage is 2% of the weight of the precipitate. (The enzymatic hydrolysis conditions are shown in Table 1). After the reaction is completed, the centrifuge is placed at 9000 r/min for 15 min. The supernatant is transferred to a new tube and 4 mol/L sodium chloride is added for salting out reaction. After the reaction is completed, the centrifuge is placed at 9000 r/min for 15 min. The supernatant is discarded and the crude extract of collagen obtained is dissolved in acetic acid solution with pH 2. Centrifuge at 4 ℃ and 8000 r/min for 15 min. Load the supernatant into a dialysis bag and maintain it for dialysis in acetic acid solution with pH 3 for 2 days and in deionized water for 1 day. The dialysis fluid should be replaced every 2–4 h, 6–8 h, and 10–12 h during this process. Finally, after freeze-drying, the purified collagen sample can be obtained. Structural characterization of collagen extract

Ultraviolet spectroscopy (UV)

The collagen extracts from the skin and longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of Qiannan Pigs, Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs and Qiannan pigs were dissolved in 0.5 mol/L acetic acid solution. Using 0.5 mol/L acetic acid solution as the blank control, the solutions were successively dropped into 96-well plates. The absorbance was measured using an ultraviolet spectrometer (ND-2000) at wavelengths ranging from 220 to 340 nm.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR)

Accurately weigh 1 mg of collagen extract from the skin and longissimus dorsi muscle tissue of Qiannan pigs, Baixi pig, Zongdi pig, Congjiang pig, Kele pigs and Qiannan pigs, add 100 mg of KBr (spectral pure), thoroughly grind and evenly press into tablets The Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (IRAffinity-1S, Shimadzu Corporation) was used for full-band (4000–400 cm−1) scanning 35 times. Data processing is carried out using the IRsolution software. Specifically, Find Peaks automatically marks the positions of characteristic peaks, Subtract removes impurity peaks, Library searches for the spectra, and finally the data is exported for further use.

Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)

Take an appropriate amount of frozen samples of collagen extract from the skin and the longest dorsal muscle tissue of Qiannan pigs, Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs and Qianbei pigs, place them on the table for sample bonding, and then put them into the electron microscope sample preparation system (20064263, Hitachi Corporation). After ion sputtering gold coating, conduct scanning electron microscope (SU8100, Hitachi Corporation) observation, select the field of view, magnification and take photos. (Specific operation steps: ABCC—Adjust focal length of Red1—TV1—ABCC—SLOW3—1280 M—Capture. Select the appropriate magnification until the image is clear and then take a photo for saving).

Classification and determination of collagen in pig skin and longissimus dorsi muscle tissue

The skin tissues and the longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of Qiannan pigs, Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs and Qiannan pigs were cut into small pieces and placed in test tubes. An appropriate amount of physiological saline was added and mixed evenly. Then, the mixture was centrifuged at 3000 rpm/min for 10 min and ground into tissue homogenate with a grinder. The specific steps for typing and determining the content of different types of collagen should refer to the instructions of the Porcine Type I, II, III, IV and V Collagen ELISA Kits. The standard substances of different concentrations were regarded as the x-coordinate variables, and the corresponding OD values were regarded as the y-coordinate variables. The linear regression equation of the standard substances was plotted, the concentration values of each sample were calculated, and the significance test of the results was conducted using Excel and SPSS 19.0 analysis software.

Screening and identification of SNP loci related to collagen deposition genes based on whole-genome resequencing

Extraction of genomic DNA

According to the steps in the GeneJET Genomic DNA Purification Kit (K0721), DNA was extracted from 36 heads each of Baxi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs. The qualified DNA samples were stored at −80℃.

Database construction, sequencing and data quality control

The genomic DNA from 6 pigs including Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs that passed the quality inspection was used for library construction in accordance with the standard Illumina library construction process. After the quality of the library was verified, the obtained genomic library obtained the whole genome sequence information through high-throughput sequencing. The library construction and sequencing of Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs were entrusted to Shanghai Ling 'en Biotechnology Co., LTD. The main sequencing platform was Illumina HiSeq 2500, and the sequencing depth was 30 ×. High-quality sequencing data was obtained after filtering the original sequencing data using Trimmomatic software, ensuring the quality of subsequent analysis results.

Raw sequencing data have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under the BioProject accession number PRJNA1140909.

Compare the reference genome

Taking the porcine reference genome (Sscrofa11.1) in the NCBI database as the reference genome, the sequencing sequences were aligned using the bwa alignment software. The sequencing fragments generated by PCR-duplication were removed using the Picard-tools tool. Then, the sequencing depth and coverage were calculated based on the alignment results. Then, deduplication is performed on the sequenced data to obtain the bam file for mutation detection.

SNP and INDEL variant detection and quality control filtering

The BreakDancer software was used to conduct mutation detection on the bam files of Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs respectively, and filter out the reads with mapping Q lower than 10. SNP and INDEL screening were conducted using the GATK tool, and highly accurate variations were obtained after filtering the results. The quality control filtration parameters are: Non-second-order locus removal, snp quality > = 20, snp depth > = 4 QD > = 2.0 && FS < = 60.0 && MQ > = 40.0 && MQ Ranksum > = −12.5 && ReadPosRankSum > = −8.

Annotation of SNP and INDEL

The variations were annotated using the default parameters of ANNOVAR (2019 Oct24), and all SNPS were classified into different regions such as upstream and downstream of the gene, exons, introns, and intergenic regions based on their positions on the genome. Further classify SNPS in exon regions into synonymous mutations or non-synonymous mutations. The annotation operation of INDEL is the same as that of SNP, and it is classified into transposable missing, transposable insertion, and non-transposable missing and non-transposable insertion.

Annotations and pathway analysis

The identified unique family genes and significant contraction and expansion genes were compared with the KEGG and GO databases for pathway analysis. And the RStudio clusterprofiler was used to conduct pairwise comparison difference enrichment for Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs (GO enrichment parameters: p less than 0.05, q less than 0.05;) KEGG enrichment parameters: p less than 0.05, q less than 0.2, and p value correction by BH method. KEGG directly uses the KEGG database official website for KEGG analysis through the cluster Profiler v4.0 of the R package, and conducts visual analysis through the built-in function dotplot of the R package. GO conducts analysis through the R package org.Ss.eg.db v3.16.0 and uses the built-in functions of cluster Profiler for visual analysis.

Specific site screening

Based on resequencing data, this study screened out mutation sites of type I collagen, type III collagen, and type IV collagen regulatory genes (i.e., COL1, COL3, and COL4 family genes), and then identified specific variant sites that could respectively distinguish between Baixi pigs and Kele pigs, Baixi pigs and Zongdipigs, and Kele pigs and Zongdi pigs. That is, a specific variant site that exists in one pig breed but does not exist in another pig breed. The specific screening results of specific sites are shown in Table 2.

Fluorescence quantitative PCR detection

Using the cDNA from each tissue as a template, ABI QuantStudioTM7 Flex real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR was employed for detection. The reaction system consisted of 10 μL: 0.2 μL of 10 μmol/L forward and reverse primers, 0.4 μL of cDNA, 5 μL of 2xEs Taq MasterMix (Dye), with water added to reach a total volume of 10 μL. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy results

From the Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy results in Figs. 5 and 6, it can be seen that the collagen extracts from the skin and longissimus dorsal muscle tissues of Kele pigs, Qianbei pigs, Qiannan pigs, Baixi pigs, Congjiang pigs, and Zongdi pigs are at wavelengths of around 3200 cm−1 to 3600 cm−1, 2900 cm−1, 1600 cm−1, and 1500 cm−1. There are well-defined absorption peaks near 1400 cm−1 and 1200 cm−1. Among them, 3200 cm−1 to 3600 cm−1 represent the stretching vibration peaks of the N–H group of the amide A band, indicating that the extracted collagen contains H bonds. Near 2900 cm−1 represents the asymmetric stretching vibration peak of the -CH2- group of the amide B band, which is a special absorption peak of collagen and also a peak that can distinguish it from other protein molecules. Near 1600 cm−1 represents the amide I band, which is a strong peak of stretching vibration forming the trihelical inner group, indicating the existence of the trihelical structure in the collagen extract. Near 1500 cm−1, the N–H and C-N vibration absorption peaks of the amide II band are represented, and near 1200 cm−1, the N–H absorption peak of the amide III band is represented, indicating that the collagen in the sample has a complete triple helix structure. The absorption peak near 1400 cm−1 indicates the cis configuration of the peptide bond, suggesting that the collagen extract contains a large amount of proline and hydroxyproline. From the above, it can be proved that the product extracted by the enzymatic hydrolysis method used in this experiment is collagen.

The collagen extracted from the skin and the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue in the early stage was photographed using a scanning electron microscope. Results of collagen typing in the skin and longissimus dorsi muscle tissues

As can be seen from Fig. 20, in the skin tissues of Qiannan pigs, Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs and Qianbei pigs, the content of type I collagen was significantly higher than that of other types of collagen (P < 0.05), and the content of type II collagen was the lowest. Among them, the content of type I collagen in Baixi pigs was the highest, and that in Kele pigs was the lowest. Conclusion

In conclusion, this study determined that the optimal conditions for extracting collagen from local pig tissues in Guizhou are: The pH was 2.0, the enzyme dosage was 2%, the ratio of feed to liquid was 1:20 (w/v), the temperature was 37℃, and the enzymatic hydrolysis time was 8 h. It was found that the content of type I collagen was relatively high in the skin tissue of local pigs in Guizhou, and the contents of type III and type IV collagen were relatively high in the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue. The structural differences of collagen extract might be related to the contents of type III and type IV collagen. Combined with whole-genome resequencing, it was found that the mutation sites g.26391077 A > G, g.128590748 G > A, and g.128590753 T > A on the COL1A1 and COL4A4 genes might be one of the reasons for the difference in collagen content between Baixi pigs and Kele pigs. The variant sites g.128546717 C > T, g.128602337 G > A, and g.128602342 G > A on the COL4A4 gene may affect the deposition of different types of collagen in tissues. Subsequently, the association between the SNP loci and the differences in collagen content and type will be further verified. A larger sample size will be used to conduct in-depth analysis of the molecular mechanism underlying the differences in collagen properties of the Guizhou local pigs.

Discussion

The amount of collagen and the degree of cross-linking have a significant impact on the quality and tenderness of meat. A large number of studies have shown that collagen affects muscle tenderness. Liang et al. [8] found that intramuscular collagen has the function of maintaining meat tenderness. This experiment studied the content of collagen in the skin tissues and the longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of Qiannan pigs, Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs and Qianbei pigs. The results showed that, except for Qiannan pigs, the content of collagen in the skin tissues of the other five pig breeds was significantly higher than that in the longissimus dorsi muscle tissues (P < 0.05). Among the six pig breeds, the collagen content in the skin tissue and the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue of the Baixi pig was the highest, and both were extremely significantly higher than that of the Kele pig (P < 0.01). Studies have shown that the viscosity and elasticity of tissues increase with the increase of collagen content [9]. A high content of collagen can make meat more elastic and sticky. Therefore, the high collagen content in Baixi pigs may be one of the reasons why their skin is sticky and their meat is tender. Liang Jinglin [10] extracted collagen from pigskin using hydrochloric acid, acetic acid, citric acid and lactic acid, with extraction rates of 15.71%, 32.34%, 32.97% and 30.15% respectively. In this experiment, collagen was extracted from the skin tissues and the longissimus dorsii muscle of different local pig strains in Guizhou by enzymatic hydrolysis. When the enzymatic hydrolysis time was 8 h, the average extraction rate from the skin tissues was 43.67%, and that from the longissimus dorsii muscle tissues was 33.8%. This study improved the extraction rate of collagen to a certain extent.

Collagen molecules contain two types of amino acid components, tyrosine and phenylalanine. Their structural characteristics determine that they have specific characteristic absorption peaks at wavelengths below 300 nm in the ultraviolet spectrum. The main absorption peaks of collagen in the ultraviolet spectrum are located near 220 nm and 280 nm. The 220 nm absorption peak is mainly caused by the peptide bond (C = O), and the 280 nm absorption peak is mainly caused by aromatic amino acids (such as tyrosine, tryptophan and phenylalanine) [11]. This experiment found that the collagen extracted from the skin tissues of Baixi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs, Qianbei Pigs and Qiannan pigs all had the maximum absorption peak between 220 and 230 nm, indicating that the collagen extract obtained by optimizing the enzymatic hydrolysis method in this study basically conforms to the characteristics of collagen. Studies have shown that the non-collagen region (NC1 domain) of type IV collagen contains a relatively high amount of aromatic amino acids [12], and type IV collagen is mainly distributed in muscles, bones, skin and blood vessels. The results of this experiment revealed that the collagen extracted from the skin tissue of the native piglet and the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue of six pig breeds not only had a strong absorption peak between 220 and 230 nm but also a relatively weak absorption peak within the range of 250 to 340 nm. This indicates that the collagen extract contains collagen with a relatively high content of aromatic amino acids, which is consistent with the previous research results. This further proves the feasibility of the test method. The Fourier transform infrared spectrum of collagen is usually analyzed within the range of 4000–400 cm-1, and its main absorption peaks are as follows: Amide A zone (3300 cm-1), Amide B zone (3080 cm-1), Amide I zone (1600–1700 cm-1), Amide II zone (1480–1580 cm-1), Amide III zone (1200–1350 cm-1), C-H stretching vibration (2900 cm-1, O–H stretching vibration (3300 cm-1), C–O–C stretching vibration (1150 cm-1) [13]. The results of Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy show that the collagen extracts from the skin and longissimus dorsal muscle tissues of Kele pigs, Qianbei pigs, Qiannan pigs, Baixi pigs, Congjiang pigs, and Zongdi pigs are at wavelengths of around 3200 cm−1 to 3600 cm−1, 2900 cm−1, 1600 cm−1, 1500 cm−1, and 1400 There were obvious absorption peaks near cm−1 and around 1200 cm−1, indicating that the collagen extracted in this study conforms to the basic characteristics of collagen.Under the scanning electron microscope, type I collagen appears as dense fibrous bundles. Type II collagen is arranged in a reticular or random pattern, forming a loose fibrous network. Type III collagen is arranged in a reticular or interlaced pattern, forming a flexible fibrous network. Type IV collagen forms a typical three-dimensional block structure with uniform mesh size. Type V collagen and type I collagen jointly form fibrous bundles and may be arranged in an interlaced pattern [11]. The results of this experiment revealed that the structure and morphology of collagen in the skin tissues of Baixi pigs, Congjiang pigs, Kele pigs, Qianbei pigs, and Qiannan pigs were different from those in the skin tissues of Zongdi pigs and the collagen in the longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of six pig breeds. Type I collagen is the most common type in the collagen family and is widely present in connective tissues such as skin, bones, tendons, ligaments and blood vessels [14]. Collagen is mostly found in the dermis of the skin structure. Alves et al. [15] used salmon and cod with high content of type I collagen to study the effectiveness of moisturizing cosmetics, and the results showed that they had a good moisturizing effect. The results of this experiment revealed that the content of type I collagen was the highest in the skin and longissimus dorsal muscle tissue of the Baixi pigs. In the previous slaughter experiments of our research group, it was found that the skin and meat of the Baixi pigs were relatively sticky and glutinous, and it was speculated that this might be related to their high collagen content. The regulatory mechanism of collagen deposition in the Baixi pigs can be further explored in the future. Type III collagen is present in high concentrations in the fasciculus and endomuscular membranes of muscles, forming the connective tissue of muscles while maintaining their shape and function. Collagen is also involved in the repair and regeneration of tissues after muscle injury [16]. Studies have shown that connective tissue in muscles can affect the tenderness of meat [8]. Chen et al. [17] found that the shear force value increases with the increase of collagen content, thereby leading to a decrease in muscle tenderness. The results of this experiment revealed that the contents of type III collagen and type IV collagen in the skin and longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of the Zongdi pigs were both higher than those of other pig breeds. It is speculated that the content of type III collagen may affect the skin characteristics and meat quality of Zongdi pigs. It was also found that the contents of type III and type IV collagen in the longissimus dorsi muscle tissues of six pig breeds were significantly higher than those of type I, II and V collagen (P < 0.05). The muscle tissues of local pigs in Guizhou have flexibility and elasticity, which may be related to the high contents of type III and type IV collagen to some extent. Based on the above results, it can be found that there are differences in the structure of collagen extracts in the skin tissue of Zongdi pigs, the collagen in the longissimus dorsal muscle tissue of the six pig breeds, and the skin tissues of the Baixi pigs, Kele pigs, Qianbei pigs, Qiannan pigs, and Congjiang pigs. It is speculated that the content of type III collagen and type IV collagen might be relatively high in the skin tissue of Zongdi pigs and the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue of the six pig breeds.

In this experiment, whole genome resequencing was conducted on three local pigs from Guizhou Province (the average sequencing depth of Baixi pig sample was 36.02 ×, that of the Zongdi pig sample was 32.76 ×, and that of the Kele pig sample was 47.00 ×). The sequencing data met the qualified standards. A total of 1715.4 GB of Raw data was generated from 18 samples, and the filtered Clean data was 1699.6 GB. A total of 250,187,584 high-quality single-base variant (SNP) sites and 52,550,218 high-quality insertion/deletion variant (INDEL) sites were detected in Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs. SNP and INDEL types of variants are usually used in the research of population genetics and breeding of livestock and poultry. It serves as a genetic marker [18, 19]. This study found that these variations were mainly concentrated in intergenic regions and intron regions. Previous studies have indicated that mutations in intergenic regions may have a significant impact on key biological processes such as gene expression, RNA transcriptional modification, binding and splicing of transcription factors [20], which implies that mutations in non-coding regions may indirectly regulate gene expression levels. In addition, intron region mutations may affect the amino acid coding sequence of proteins by interfering with splicing events, ultimately altering the function and structure of proteins [21]. This process not only involves post-transcriptional gene regulation but also directly relates to the final synthetic product of proteins. Ryu et al. [22] identified SNPs related to stroke in the FOXF2 and FOXQ1 gene spacer regions of zebrafish. These SNPs can regulate the enhancer activity of the FOXF2 regulatory factor, thereby affecting gene expression. They have shown regulatory effects on stroke risk not only in zebrafish models but also in human cell lines. A total of 16,810,618 SNP loci and 114,758 INDEL loci were detected in the exon region. SNP and INDEL variations located in the exon region may alter the conformation, function, expression level and stability of proteins, thereby regulating the phenotypic characteristics of organisms [23]. This study statistically analyzed the SNPS affected by the mutation sites in the CDS region on protein translation in the annotation results and found that a total of 491,296 SNP sites were annotated in whitehead pigs, 560,911 SNP variation sites were annotated in Zongdi pigs, and 516,314 SNP variation sites were annotated in Kele pigs. Non-synonymous mutations may affect the structure and function of proteins, so these variant sites may alter the structure, function or stability of proteins, depending on the amino acid properties (such as polarity and charge) and positions (such as active sites) [24]. Therefore, the differences in the content and structure of collagen in the tissues of Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs may be related to these SNP loci to some extent. Liu et al. [25] found that the reason for the significant increase in neurovirulence in the body is the existence of a single non-synonymous mutation. Matsumoto et al. [26] found that non-synonymous mutations in the SPP1 gene of cattle would increase the carcass weight of their experimental animals. Therefore, non-synonymous mutations that alter the structure and function of proteins have both advantages and disadvantages. A deeper exploration of the mutation sites found in this study can provide a better understanding of the gene functions and the mechanisms regulating collagen in Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs, and Kele pigs. This study statistically analyzed the INDEL of the impact of CDS region mutation sites on protein translation in the annotation results and found that a total of 33,857 insertion and deletion markers were statistically recorded in Baixi pigs, 34,154 insertion and deletion markers in Zongdi pigs, and 33,961 insertion and deletion markers in Kele pigs. Niu et al. [27] found that the susceptibility of landwhite piglets to diarrhea is regulated by 12 bases inserted into the 3’utr region of the CISH gene. Mi et al. [28] found that three INDEL variant sites, namely L-13, L-16, and L-19, on the CFAP43 gene were significantly associated with growth traits such as chest depth in goats. The INDEL variant sites identified in this study are of vital importance to the genomic structure and function research of Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs, providing key data support for gene editing and promoting the improvement of pig breed traits. According to the KEGG analysis results, the differentially expressed pathways in the differentially expressed genes of Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs involve multiple biological processes and signaling pathways. Among them, the comparison results of Baixi pigs and Kele pigs indicate that the KEGG-enriched pathways involve axon guidance, the interaction between ECM and receptors, calcium signaling pathways, adhesion plaques and other pathways. The comparison results between Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs indicated that the KEGG-enriched pathways involved axon guidance, Rap1 signaling pathway, adhesive connection, and other pathways. ECM receptor-interacting proteins are a class of important molecules that mediate adhesion, signal transduction and mechanical responses between cells and extracellular matrix. They participate in processes such as cell migration, proliferation, differentiation and maintenance of tissue homeostasis by recognizing specific components in ECM (such as collagen, fibronectin, laminin, etc.) [29]. The adhesion plaque pathway is the core signaling network for the interaction between cells and extracellular matrix (ECM), mediating cell adhesion, mechanical signal perception and intracellular signal transduction through receptors such as integrins, and regulating cell migration, proliferation, survival and tissue remodeling [30]. GO enrichment analysis was conducted on the differentially expressed genes in the populations of Baixi pigs and Kele pigs, and it was found that 2,147 genes were significantly enriched in 37 GO items (P < 0.05). GO enrichment analysis was also performed on the differentially expressed genes in the populations of Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs, and it was found that 3,210 genes were significantly enriched in 82 GO items (P < 0.05). GO enrichment analysis was conducted on the different-gene groups of Baixi pigs and Zongdi pigs. It was found that 138 genes were significantly enriched in 9 GO items (P < 0.05). At the molecular function level, the genes in the selected regions were significantly enriched in items related to molecular binding function and enzyme catalytic activity. Functional annotations at the biological process level indicate that the significantly enriched gene functional categories are concentrated in key biological mechanisms such as basic cellular activities, individual life support systems, multi-level biological regulatory networks, and energy-material metabolic pathways. From the perspective of cellular components, gene functional modules are highly concentrated in core units that maintain the cellular spatial structure, such as the basic structure of the cell and the composition of subcellular organelles. Based on the enrichment results of KEGG and GO in Baixi pigs compared with those in Kele pigs, it was found that the interaction pathway between ECM and the receptor and the plaque adhesion pathway were both enriched with collagen-related genes such as COL1A1, COL4A3, COL4A4 and COL6A5. The research indicates that hsa_circ_0009140 can promote the production of type I collagen and type III collagen in fibroblasts by regulating the miR-29b-3p-COL1A1/COL3A1 axis [31]. COL1A1, an important structural gene of type I collagen, plays a significant role in regulating cell proliferation and apoptosis in the body [32]. In this study, differential SNPS were enriched in the differentially expressed genes COL1A1 and COL4A4 of the Baixi pig, Zongdi pig and the Kele pig. To explore whether the enriched differential SNPS are the reasons for the differences in collagen content and structure among the three pig breeds, Sanger sequencing technology was used to verify the enriched sites. This verification method has also been reported in other studies. For instance, Zhutao et al. [33] selected 56 ducks from 7 populations including Cherry Valley ducks, Beijing ducks, and Maple Leaf ducks for whole-genome resequencing, screening out 686 SNPs and INDELs existing in specific populations, and randomly selected 7 SNPs loci for verification. The results indicate that the specific SNPS screened out in the second-generation sequencing can be used as molecular markers for the identification of duck breeds. Huanhuan et al. [34] randomly selected 30 sika deer specific SNPs loci for Sanger sequencing based on genotyping sequencing technology. The research results indicated that the sika deer specific SNPs screened out by GBS sequencing could be used as molecular markers for identification. The results of this study show that the six randomly selected SNPs loci were all found in specific populations, and the results were in line with expectations. Therefore, the enriched differential SNP loci have a certain impact on the differences in collagen content and structure among Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs. These variant loci can be used as a reference for molecular marker-assisted selection. Based on the results of GO analysis, we initially identified genes closely related to the collagen characteristics of Baixi pigs, Zongdi pigs and Kele pig. The expression of COL1A1, COL3A1, COL4A4 and COL4A3 genes in the longissimus dorsi muscle and skin tissue of the three pig breeds was detected by real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR. It was found that the expression level of the COL1A1 gene in the skin tissue of Baixi pigs was significantly higher than that of native spotted pigs and Kele pigs, indicating that the collagen content of Baixi pigs was higher than that of Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs, and the COL1A1 gene played a significant role in the regulatory mechanism. The expression level of the COL3A1 gene in the skin tissue of Kele pigs, the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue of the Zongdi pigs and Kele pigs was higher than that in other tissues. It was also found that the expression levels of the COL4A4 gene and the COL4A3 gene were the highest in the longissimus dorsi muscle tissue of the Zongdi pig. Among them, the expression levels of the COL4A4 and COL4A3 genes in the skin tissue of the native spotted pig were higher than those of Baixi pigs and Kele pigs. This result explains the structural differences of the collagen extract in the skin tissue of Zongdi pigs and in the longissimus dorsi muscle of all pig breeds. Combined with the results of the collagen typing test, it is indicated that type III collagen and type IV collagen have certain influences on the structure of collagen.

Quantitative Resolvent and Eigenfunction Stability for the Faber-Krahn Inequality
Mark Allen, Dennis Kriventsov, and Robin Neumayer

Abstract
. For a bounded open set Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} with the same volume as the unit ball, the classical Faber-Krahn inequality says that the first Dirichlet eigenvalue λ 1 ​ ( Ω ) \lambda_{1}(\Omega) of the Laplacian is at least that of the unit ball B B . We prove that the deficit λ 1 ​ ( Ω ) − λ 1 ​ ( B ) \lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B) in the Faber-Krahn inequality controls the square of the distance between the resolvent operator ( − Δ Ω ) − 1 (-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1} for the Dirichlet Laplacian on Ω \Omega and the resolvent operator on the nearest unit ball B ⁡ ( x Ω ) B(x_{\Omega}) . The distance is measured by the operator norm from L ∞ L^{\infty} to L 2 L^{2} . As a main application, we show that the Faber-Krahn deficit λ 1 ​ ( Ω ) − λ 1 ​ ( B ) \lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B) controls the squared L 2 L^{2} norm between k k th eigenfunctions on Ω \Omega and B ⁡ ( x Ω ) B(x_{\Omega}) for every k ∈ ℕ . k\in\mathbb{N}. In both of these main theorems, the quadratic power is optimal.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open bounded set. The Laplace equation with Dirichlet boundary data has discrete spectrum
0<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)<\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\leq\dots
of eigenvalues, whose corresponding eigenfunctions
\{u_{\Omega,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
solve the eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\Omega,k}=\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\,u_{\Omega,k}&\text{in }\Omega\\
\quad\ \ u_{\Omega,k}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

and, after normalizing so that
\|u_{\Omega,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
which we shall always do, form an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

A classical shape optimization problem is the minimization of the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
among domains
\Omega
of a fixed volume. Following Lord Rayleigh’s 1894 conjecture, in the 1920s Faber
[
32
]
and Krahn
[
51
]
independently showed that balls are the only solutions to this variational problem. This leads to the Faber-Krahn inequality:

(1.1)

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{1}(B)\quad\text{ if }\quad|\Omega|=\omega_{n}\,,

with equality if and only if
\Omega=B(x)
for some
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(up to capacity zero sets).
Here
B(x)=\{|y-x|<1\}
is the unit ball centered at
x
,
B=B(0)
, and
\omega_{n}=|B|
. The proof of the Faber-Krahn inequality is based on rearrangement and the isoperimetric inequality and is nowadays classical.

A fundamental question related to the Faber-Krahn inequality is that of stability: if a domain
\Omega
with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}

almost
achieves equality in (
1.1
), then is
\Omega

almost
a unit ball? The first “almost” is naturally measured by the deficit in (
1.1
):
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)
. As for the second, there are various ways in which one can quantify the proximity of
\Omega
to the space of unit balls, with the most suitable notion dependent on context.

One classical geometric notion is the Fraenkel asymmetry,
\alpha(\Omega)=\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\Omega\Delta B(x)|
, measuring the volume of the symmetric difference between
\Omega
and the nearest unit ball. Sharp quantitative stability for the Faber-Krahn inequality with respect to the Fraenkel asymmetry was shown in the paper
[
16
]
of Brasco, De Philippis, and Velichkov; see also
[
12
,
38
]
for earlier results.

A different way to quantify how close
\Omega
is to the space of unit balls
is
spectrally
: Are the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of
\Omega
close to the corresponding eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the nearest unit ball?
This notion of distance and corresponding quantitative stability estimates turn out to be particularly important in applications to harmonic analysis and free boundary problems.

These applications motivated the authors’ previous work in
[
4
,
3
]
, where it was shown that for any open bounded set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
,

(1.2)

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)\geq c_{n}\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int|u_{\Omega,1}-u_{B(x),1}|^{2}\,.

Here the eigenfunctions are extended by zero to be defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
In the same work, an analogue of (
1.2
) was shown on the round sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. This was used in
[
5
]
to establish a rectifiability and uniqueness-of-blowups theorem in terms of the Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman monotonicity formula, with applications to free boundary regularity for certain (e.g. vectorial) free boundary problems. In
[
35
,
34
]
, Fleschler, Tolsa, and Villa applied the analogue of (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
as a main tool, alongside some deep covering arguments, to establish a higher-dimensional version of Carleson’s famed
\varepsilon^{2}
-conjecture in harmonic analysis
[
44
]
. In
[
35
]
, the authors also apply (
1.2
) to prove a different form of quantitative stability for the Faber-Krahn inequality with respect to a geometric distance involving the capacity.

The first main result of this paper substantially extends (
1.2
): the Faber-Krahn deficit quantitatively controls not only the distance between first eigenfunctions, but between
all eigenfunctions
, of
\Omega
and the nearest unit ball:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
n\geq 2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. There is a positive constant
c_{n,k}
depending only on
n
and
k
such that
for any open bounded set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
, there is a point
x_{\Omega}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

(1.3)

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)\geq c_{k,n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u_{\Omega,k}-u_{B({x_{\Omega}}),k}\right|^{2}\,.

As in (
1.2
) above, in the statement of Theorem
1.1
, we extend each
u_{\Omega,k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
by zero to be in
H^{1}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and likewise for eigenfunctions on the ball. When
\lambda_{k}(B)
has multiplicity greater than
1
,
u_{B({x_{\Omega}}),k}
denotes
some
eigenfunction in the
\lambda_{k}(B)
-eigenspace with
L^{2}
norm equal to
1
, since of course any choice of ordered basis for this eigenspace is not canonical. The quadratic power on the
L^{2}
-norm on the right-hand side of (
1.3
) is optimal; see section
5.3
.

One can view Theorem
1.1
as the counterpart for eigenfunctions of a recent result of Bucur, Lamboley, Nahon, and Prunier
[
19
]
for eigenvalues. There, the authors show that
for each
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following sharp quantitative estimates hold: if
\lambda_{k}(B)
is simple, then

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)

\displaystyle\geq c_{n,k}|\lambda_{k}(\Omega)-\lambda_{k}(B)|,

while if
\lambda_{k}(B)
has multiplicity—say
\lambda_{k-1}(B)<\lambda_{k}(B)=\ldots=\lambda_{\ell}(B)<\lambda_{\ell+1}(B)
—then

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)

\displaystyle\geq c_{n,k}\Big|\sum_{i=k}^{\ell}(\lambda_{i}(\Omega)-\lambda_{i}(B)\Big|\,.

The linear powers in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are stronger than one typically expects in stability estimates (and would be false for Theorem
1.1
), and have to do with the fact that the ball is critical for the functionals on the right-hand sides, as well as the left-hand sides, of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
).

Theorem
1.1
is proven as an application of the other main theorem of the paper: sharp quantitative estimates for the resolvent operator. To state the result we first introduce a bit of terminology. For an open bounded set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a function
f\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, let
u_{\Omega}^{f}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the unique (weak) solution to the Poisson equation

(1.6)

\begin{cases}\hfill-\Delta u^{f}_{\Omega}=f&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\hfill u_{\Omega}^{f}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

As above for eigenfunctions, we extend
u_{\Omega}^{f}
by zero to be defined as a function in
H^{1}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. The
resolvent operator

(-\Delta)_{\Omega}^{-1}:H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to H^{1}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

associates to
f\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the unique solution to (
1.6
). As an operator from
L^{\infty}
to
L^{2}
, the resolvent operator of
\Omega
is close to the resolvent operator of the nearest unit ball, quantitatively in terms of the Faber-Krahn deficit:

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
n\geq 2
. There is a constant
c_{n}>0
depending on
n
such that for any bounded open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)

\displaystyle\geq c_{n}\left\|(-\Delta)_{\Omega}^{-1}-(-\Delta)_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{-1}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\to L^{2}}\,

for a point
x_{\Omega}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In other words, for any
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq 1,
we have

(1.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)

\displaystyle\geq c_{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big|^{2}\,.

To establish (
1.7
) (and in turn Theorem
1.1
) from (
1.8
), it is essential that the constant
c_{n}
and the ball center
x_{\Omega}
in (
1.8
) do not depend on the function
f
. The point
x_{\Omega}
is a truncated version of the barycenter of
\Omega
defined in section
3.1
.

A
qualitative
version of (
1.8
), with the modulus of continuity depending on
f
, can be deduced by combining a theorem of Šverák
[
42
, Theorem 3.2.5]
, the Kohler-Jobin inequality (see Lemma
2.1
), and a standard concentration compactness argument. To our knowledge, Theorem
1.2
is the first
quantitative
estimate for the resolvent operator.

The quadratic powers in (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) are optimal and cannot be replaced by a smaller exponent, as shown in section
5.3
. In this sense Theorem
1.2
is sharp. A related question is whether in (
1.7
) the operator norm from
L^{\infty}
to
L^{2}
can be replaced with the operator norm from
X
to
Y
for other function spaces
X\supset L^{\infty}
and
Y\subset L^{2}
. We construct examples in section
5.3
exploring the extent to which one might expect to prove a “strong form stability” improvement in this direction. For instance, we show
L^{2}
cannot be replaced by
H^{1}
, and
L^{\infty}
cannot be replaced by
L^{p}_{loc}
for
p<n/2.
However, we have no examples ruling out the
X=L^{p}
with
p>n
, which leaves the following natural open question:

Open Question.

Does Theorem
1.2
hold with the norm
L^{p}
in place of the norm
L^{\infty}
?

We expect the answer to this open question to be affirmative. Many of the arguments work with
f\in L^{p}
with
p>n
.

As mentioned above, we also showed (
1.2
) on the round sphere and hyperbolic space in
[
4
,
3
]
. Similarly, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
can be shown to hold on these spaces. Nearly all of the arguments in this paper can be adapted to these settings, with the only nontrivial modifications being (a) the spectral gap of Lemma
4.2
below should be replaced by a spectral gap argument by adapting the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 3.11]
as well as the arguments in
[
57
]
, and (b) a minimizer of the penalized variational problem in Theorem
1.3
below should be shown to satisfy the volume constraint by using the Euler-Lagrange equation as in
[
3
, Proposition 9.5]
instead of the simpler scaling argument of Lemma
7.15
. To aid in the clarity of presentation, we have opted to present all the proofs here for domains on Euclidean space.

Beyond the works mentioned above, there is a rich literature on quantitative stability for geometric inequalities involving PDE-driven shape functionals like
\lambda_{1}
, with a number of exciting developments in the past decade or two.
Following the work of Brasco-De Philippis-Velichkov
[
16
]
, similar methods were used to establish sharp quantitative stability for the
p
-Faber-Krahn
[
39
]
, isocapacitary
[
28
]
, and
p
-isocapacitary
[
55
]
inequalities.
Through quite different methods, quantitative stability has been established for spectral shape optimization problems with Neumann
[
17
]
and Robin
[
18
]
boundary conditions, and on surfaces
[
46
]
(both globally and within a fixed conformal class). The recent papers
[
1
,
9
]
address stability for Talenti’s rearrangement inequality. This sampling of results is by no means exhaustive, and we refer the reader to
[
15
]
for an overview of spectral inequalities in quantitative form.

Discussion of the Proofs

Theorem
1.1
will be established as a consequence of Theorem
1.2
. The basic idea in the case when
k\in\mathbb{N}
corresponds to a simple eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(B)
of the ball (and
x_{\Omega}=0
for simplicity) is as follows. We apply Theorem
1.2
with a normalization of
f=\lambda_{k}(B)u_{B,k}
to find
u_{B}^{f}=u_{B,k}
is quantitatively close to
u_{\Omega}^{f}
. Then, expanding both functions in the basis of eigenfunctions on
\Omega
, we are able to quantitatively estimate their Fourier coefficients in order to show
u_{B,k}
is quantitatively close to
u_{\Omega,k}
.

Although the Faber-Krahn inequality is a consequence of the isoperimetric inequality, it is thus far unclear how to deduce sharp quantitative stability for the Faber-Krahn inequality (even with respect to the Fraenkel asymmetry) from quantitative stability for the isoperimetric inequality
[
37
,
33
,
22
]
; see
[
54
,
41
,
38
]
for non-sharp or conditional results and the survey
[
15
]
for a discussion of the limitations of this approach.

Instead, we use the selection principle framework introduced by Cicalese and Leonardi in
[
22
]
. This robust tool, which has found application in myriad settings, e.g.
[
36
,
13
,
14
,
56
,
28
,
55
,
39
]
, allows one to prove quantitative stability for a given geometric inequality from
linear stability
together with
regularity estimates
for a suitable penalized functional.

Brasco-De Philippis-Velichkov’s quantitative Faber-Krahn inequality
[
16
]
also used a selection principle scheme. While the linear stability portion of our proof follows in a straightforward manner from theirs, the penalized functional and corresponding regularity estimates that are needed to obtain the resolvent estimates (
1.7
) differ fundamentally from those needed to control the Fraenkel asymmetry in
[
16
]
.
In this respect, a major new feature of this paper is the development of techniques introduced in our earlier paper
[
3
]
to study the regularity of minimizers of Alt-Caffarelli-type functionals involving a type of a-priori same-order perturbation described below.

First, several initial reductions simplify the proof of Theorem
1.2
before running the selection principle. It suffices to show the estimate (
1.8
) in the case when

(1.9)

f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad f\geq 0,\qquad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq 1,

as shown in section
3.2
. Moreover, by the Kohler-Jobin inequality, it suffices to show Theorem
1.2
with the Faber-Krahn deficit
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)
replaced by the Saint-Venant deficit
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)
. Here
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)
is
the torsional rigidity of
\Omega
, defined as

(1.10)

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)=\inf\left\{\int\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}-w\,dx\ :\ w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right\}.

The sign convention adopted here differs from the one used in some other references (and is minus the physical quantity). The torsional rigidity is a non-positive quantity, and among sets of a fixed volume, it is uniquely (up to sets of capacity zero) minimized by balls. The resulting inequality is the
Saint-Venant inequality
:

(1.11)

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)\geq\mathrm{tor}(B)\qquad\text{ if }\quad|\Omega|=\omega_{n}\,.

The trick of replacing the Faber-Krahn deficit with the Saint-Venant deficit (and more generally, replacing
\lambda_{1}
with the more-regularizing shape functional
\mathrm{tor}
) is well known and was used, for instance, in
[
16
,
19
]
.

In view of these reductions, to prove Theorem
1.2
it suffices to show there is a constant
c_{n}>0
such that

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}_{1}(B)

\displaystyle\geq c_{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big|^{2}\,

for any open bounded set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
and for any
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying (
1.9
). Here, as in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
above,
x_{\Omega}
denotes the truncated barycenter, defined in section
3.1
. For sets of diameter at most
2
, like the minimizer in Theorem
1.3
below, the truncated barycenter agrees with classical barycenter
\fint_{\Omega}x\,dx
. Of course, it is essential that the constant
c_{n}
in (
1.12
) does not depend on the smoothness of
f
beyond its
L^{\infty}
norm or on its compact support.

By using a selection principle reduction (carried out in section
5
), the main analytical challenge to prove (
1.12
) is to establish the existence and regularity of minimizers of the penalized functional

(1.13)

\mathcal{E}(\Omega)=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)+\tau\,h\Big(\int|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}|^{2}\Big)

among sets of volume
\omega_{n}
. Here
h(t)=\sqrt{a^{2}+(a-t)^{2}}
for a given number
a\in(0,1]
is (up to a constant) a regularization of the function
|t-a|
, and
\tau>0
is a parameter that will be chosen to be small.
We show the following result for the volume-constrained minimization of the functional
\mathcal{E}
in (
1.13
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
\delta_{0}\in(0,1]
. There exists
\bar{\tau}_{0}=\bar{\tau}_{0}(n,\alpha,\delta_{0})>0

such that the following holds. Fix
a\in(0,1]
,
\tau\in(0,\bar{\tau}]
, and
f
as in (
1.9
). The variational problem

(1.14)

\inf\{\mathcal{E}(\Omega):\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\text{ open, bounded, }|\Omega|=\omega_{n}\}

admits a minimizer
U
. There is a
C^{2,\alpha}
function
\xi:\partial B(x_{\Omega})\to{\mathbb{R}}
, with
\|\xi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial B(x_{\Omega}))}\leq\delta_{0}
such that

\partial U=\{(1+\xi(x))x:x\in\partial B(x_{\Omega})\}\,.

Since we assume in (
1.9
) that
f
is smooth, we obtain
\xi\in C^{2,\alpha}
. However, since we do not assume bounds on the derivative of
f
we only obtain the first order bound
\|\xi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq\delta_{0}
, but utilizing the recent ideas in
[
57
]
to obtain Lemma
4.2
this will be sufficient for our methods.

Minimization of the first term in the energy
\mathcal{E}
, the torsional rigidity, behaves much like the minimization of the Alt-Caffarelli (also called one-phase Bernoulli) functional. Regularity theory for minimizers of this functional was first developed by Alt and Caffarelli in
[
8
]
and its variants have since been a central topic in free boundary problems.

What introduces new challenges to the analysis of the functional
\mathcal{E}
is the term
{h}_{f}(\Omega):=h(\int|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}|^{2})
, which is the same order as
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)
, has poor lower semicontinuity properties, and in the Euler-Lagrange equation contributes a same-order term to the one coming from
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)
without a favorable sign.

Standard methods of proving existence fall short for the minimization problem (
1.14
); for instance

[
20
]
cannot be applied as the functional
\mathcal{E}
is not decreasing under set inclusions. Instead we follow the approach of our earlier paper
[
3
]
: we prove a priori estimates for inward and outward minimizers and construct a “good” minimizing sequence consisting of outward minimizers along which the functional actually is lower semicontinuous. This approach has the additional advantage that we work entirely in the framework of open sets and there is no need to pass to quasi-open sets.

In terms of regularity, since the term
{h}_{f}(\Omega)
scales at the same order as
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)
, one cannot apply the theory of almost-minimizers of Alt-Caffarelli-type functionals
[
27
]
. A formal computation assuming
\partial\Omega
is sufficiently regular shows that the Euler Lagrange equation is the vectorial Alt-Caffarelli problem

(1.15)

\begin{cases}\ \ -\Delta w_{\Omega}=1,\ \ -\Delta u_{\Omega}^{f}=f,\ \ -\Delta p_{\Omega}=u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}&\text{ on }\Omega\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ w_{\Omega}=u_{\Omega}^{f}=p_{\Omega}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
\ \ -|\nabla w_{\Omega}|^{2}+C_{\Omega}\tau(|\nabla p_{\Omega}|\,|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}|-g_{\Omega})=\text{const}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\,.\end{cases}

Here
g_{\Omega}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a smooth bounded function depending on
\Omega
(through the solution of another auxiliary PDE) and
C_{\Omega}=h^{\prime}(\int|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}|^{2})>0
.
Significant difficulties arise in rigorously deriving the stationarity condition (
1.15
). Stemming from the genuinely nonlocal nature of the functional
{h}_{f}(\Omega)
—which leads, in particular, to the presence of the auxiliary potential
p_{\Omega}
in (
1.15
) that solves a Poisson equation with right-hand side depending on
\Omega
—it is not clear how to run the type of blowup argument used in
[
19
]
to show
\Omega
is a viscosity solution of (
1.15
).

Instead, we use careful regularization arguments to establish a distributional form of (
1.15
) and then derive a pointwise form by plugging in suitable test functions (with the final line of (
1.15
) understood in the sense of nontangential limits). On a broad level, this approach follows the one of our earlier paper
[
3
]
, but we are able to refine a number of our earlier arguments, leading to a significantly simpler proof.

Once we are able to rigorously establish that (
1.15
) holds (and along the way, prove the
\Omega
is an NTA domain), we can apply the inhomogeneous boundary Harnack theorem
[
6
]
shown by the first two authors and Shahgholian. This allows us to recast (
1.15
) as a standard one phase problem and apply the classical theory of Alt and Caffarelli
[
8
]
.

Organization of the Paper

The paper essentially splits into two disjoint parts. After recalling notation and classical facts from the literature in section
2
, we dedicate the first part of the paper, sections
3
–
5
, to proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
assuming Theorem
1.3
. Specifically, we introduce the truncated barycenter and establish several reductions for our main theorems in section
3
, show Theorem
1.2
in the class of nearly spherical sets in section
4
, and in section
5
we conclude the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
and present examples demonstrating the sharpness of the theorems.

The second part of the paper, sections
6
and
7
, is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.3
using techniques from free boundary theory. In section
6
we establish the existence of minimizers of a relaxed version of the minimization problem (
1.14
), as well as basic properties of minimizers like Lipschitz regularity and nondegeneracy of the torsion function. In section
7
, we compute the Euler-Lagrange equation satisfied by minimizers and use this to show boundary regularity and that the minimizer of the relaxed problem satisfies the volume constraint
|U|=\omega_{n}
, making it a minimizer of the original minimization problem (
1.14
).

Preliminaries

We introduce some known preliminary facts and notation that will be used throughout the paper. For the remainder of the paper, we fix

to be an integer with

We write
B_{r}(x)
to denote a ball of radius
r

, and when

2.1.
Basic facts about the torsional rigidity

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open bounded domain. The infimum in the variational problem (
1.10
) is uniquely achieved by the
torsion function

w_{\Omega}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
solving

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\hfill-\Delta w_{\Omega}=1&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\hfill w_{\Omega}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

&+\int_{B(x)\cap B(x^{\prime})}|u_{B(x)}-u_{B(x^{\prime})}|=:I^{(1)}+I^{(2)}+I^{(3)}\end{split}

By the maximum principle and the expression (
2.3
) for the torsion function on a ball,

(3.7)

I^{(1)}+I^{(2)}\leq C_{n}|B(x)\Delta B(x^{\prime})|\leq C_{n}|x-x^{\prime}|.

Next,
v=u_{B(x)}-u_{B(x^{\prime})}
is harmonic on
E:=B(x)\cap B(x^{\prime})
so by the maximum principle and (
2.3
),

\sup_{E}|v|=\sup_{\partial E}|v|\leq\sup_{\partial B(x)}|u_{B(x^{\prime})}|+\sup_{\partial B(x^{\prime})}|u_{B(x)}|\leq 2\sup_{\partial B(x)}w_{B(x^{\prime})}\leq C_{n}|x-x^{\prime}|.

Integrating this bound over
E
shows
I^{(3)}\leq C_{n}|x-x^{\prime}|.

Combining this estimate with (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
), we see that the left-hand side of (
3.5
) is bounded above by
C(\int|u_{\Omega}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}|+|x-x^{\prime}|).

Finally, choosing
\bar{\varepsilon}
according to Lemma
3.1
so that
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq C_{n}|\Omega\Delta\Omega^{\prime}|
completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Simplifying Assumptions

The next three lemmas show that to prove Theorem
1.2
it will be sufficient to assume that
f\geq 0
and
f\in C^{\infty}
, and
f
is compactly supported. The only quantitative assumption on
f
will be that
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

It is sufficient to prove the estimate (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.2
in the case when
f\geq 0
.

Proof.

Choose any
f
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
and write
f=f^{+}-f^{-}
. Note that
\|f_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1

u_{\Omega}^{f}=u_{\Omega}^{f^{+}}-u_{\Omega}^{f^{-}}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}=u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f^{+}}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f^{-}}.

So, if the estimate (
1.8
) is true whenever the right hand side is nonnegative with
L^{\infty}
norm at most
1
, then applying it to
\Omega
with
f_{-}
and
f_{+}
,

\displaystyle\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f^{+}}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f^{+}}-u_{\Omega}^{f^{-}}+u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f^{-}}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f^{+}}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f^{+}}\big|^{2}+2\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f^{-}}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f^{-}}\big|^{2}\leq 4C_{n}\,(\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

It is sufficient to prove the estimate (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.2
in the case when
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proof.

Choose any
f
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
.
Utilizing the standard mollification, if
f_{\epsilon}:=f\ast\eta_{\epsilon}
, then

\|f_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
. Furthermore,
f_{\epsilon}\to f
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now

(3.8)

\begin{split}\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big|^{2}&\leq\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega}^{f_{\epsilon}}\big|^{2}+\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f_{\epsilon}}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f_{\epsilon}}\big|^{2}+\int\big|u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f_{\epsilon}}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big|^{2}\end{split}

To estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.8
), note that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega}^{f_{\epsilon}})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(f-f^{\varepsilon})(u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega}^{f_{\epsilon}})

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega}^{f_{\epsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, from the Faber-Krahn inequality we have

\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega}^{f_{\varepsilon}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(B)}\|\nabla(u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega}^{f_{\varepsilon}})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Putting these together gives

\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega}^{f_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(B)}\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The same estimate will also apply to the third term in (
3.8
).
So, if the estimate (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.2
holds for smooth right-hand sides, then applying this estimate to
f^{\varepsilon}
shows the second term in (
3.8
) is bounded above by
C_{n}(\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B))
.
Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
3.8
), we conclude the result.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

It is sufficient to prove the estimate (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.2
in the case when
f
has compact support.

Proof.

Fix a smooth cutoff function
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that is identically equal to
1
in
(0,1)
and vanishes on
(2,\infty)
with
|\psi^{\prime}|\leq 5
.
Let
\Omega
be an open bounded set with
|\Omega|=1
.
Choose any
f
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
.

Now, choose
R\geq 10
such that
\Omega\subset B_{R/2}(0)
. Now, let

{g(x):=\psi\Big(\tfrac{|x|}{R}\Big)\,f(x)}

By construction,
g
is compactly supported and agrees with
f
in
B_{R}(0)
.

Finally, by the choice of
R
and definition of
x_{\Omega}
(to be precise, by Proposition
2.3
and (
3.3
)), we see that
x_{\Omega}\subset B_{R/2}(0)
and thus
B(x_{{\Omega}})\subset B(0,R)
.
In particular,
g=f
on
B({x_{\Omega}})\cup\Omega
, and thus by linearity of the Poisson equation (
1.6
),

\big\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big\|_{L^{2}}=\big\|u_{\Omega}^{g}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{g}\big\|_{L^{2}}\,.

So, if the estimate (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.2
holds for compactly supported right-hand sides, then the estimate applied with right-hand side

immediately yields the estimate with right-hand side
f
.
∎

4.
Resolvent Estimates for Nearly Spherical Sets

A set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called a
nearly spherical set
parametrized by
\phi\in C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B,\mathbb{R})

if
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
,
x_{\Omega}=0
, and

\partial\Omega:=\{(1+\phi(x))x:x\in\partial B\}\,

for a function
\phi:\partial B\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\|\phi\|_{C^{0}}<1.
The truncated barycenter
x_{\Omega}
of a nearly spherical set
\Omega
agrees with the classical barycenter. We show Theorem
1.2
holds if
\Omega
is a nearly spherical set, provided
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}
is small with
1/2<\alpha<1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Fix
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
. There exist
\delta=\delta(n,\alpha)
and
c=c(n,\alpha)
such that if
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
and
\Omega
is a nearly spherical set of class
C^{2,\gamma}
parametrized by
\phi
with
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq\delta
, then

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq c\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B({x_{\Omega}})}^{f}\big|^{2}.

A main tool in the proof is a spectral gap estimate.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
. There exists
\delta=\delta(n,\alpha)
such that if
\Omega
is a nearly spherical set of class
C^{2,\gamma}
parametrized by
\phi
with
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq\delta
,

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq\frac{1}{32n^{2}}\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}^{2}.

If we assume that
\|\phi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq\delta
, then the above result is Theorem 3.3 in
[
16
]
. Since we only assume
f\in L^{\infty}
and not necessarily Hölder continuous, we will need the weaker assumption that
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq\delta
for some
\alpha>1/2
. The proof with this weaker assumption is obtained using the ideas in the recent paper
[
57
]
, and we provide the details in the appendix.

Theorem
4.1
follows directly by combining
Lemma
4.2
and the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
. There exist
\delta=\delta(n,\alpha)
and
c=c(n,\alpha)
such that if
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
and
\Omega
is a nearly spherical set of class
C^{2,\gamma}
parametrized by
\phi
with
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq\delta
, then

\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}^{2}\geq c\int\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B}^{f}\big|^{2}.

Toward proving Lemma
4.3
, for
\phi:\partial B\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq\delta
, we consider the solution
h
to

(4.1)

\begin{cases}&\Delta h=0\quad\text{ in }B\\
&h=\phi\quad\text{ on }\partial B.\end{cases}

Recall that
\|h\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}=\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}
.
From the maximum principle,
\|h\|_{C^{1}(\overline{B})}\leq\|\phi\|_{C^{1}(\partial B)}\leq\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial B)}\leq\delta
.

We define a diffeomorphism
\Psi:B\to\Omega
by
\Psi(x)=(1+h(x))x
. If
\delta>0
is chosen to be small, then
D\Psi(x)
is nowhere vanishing and
\Psi
is indeed a diffeomorphism from
B
onto its image. Moreover,
\Psi
maps
\partial B
to
\partial\Omega
and thus the image of
B
under
\Psi
is
\Omega
. Under these assumptions, we have the following:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Then

\|f-f\circ\Psi\|_{H^{-1}(B)}\leq 2n\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}\,.

Proof.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
. From Lemma
3.3
we may assume
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For
0\leq t\leq\epsilon
, define

\mathcal{F}(t):=\int_{B}f(x+th(x)x)\,u(x)\ dx.

From the mean value theorem, there exists
t_{0}\in(0,\epsilon)
such that

\int_{B}[f(x+h(x)x)-f(x)]\,u(x)\ dx=\mathcal{F}(1)-\mathcal{F}(0)=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t_{0})=\int_{B}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{x_{i}}(x+t_{0}h(x)x)x_{i}h(x)u(x).

Since
uh\in C_{0}^{1}(B)
, we may use integration by parts to obtain

\int_{B}[f(x+h(x)x)-f(x)]u(x)\ dx=-\int_{B}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x+t_{0}h(x)x)(u_{x_{i}}(x)h(x)x_{i}+u(x)h_{x_{i}}(x)x_{i}+h(x)u(x)).

Using now that
x_{i},f\in L^{\infty}
and Hölder’s inequality we have

\left|\int_{B}[f(x+h(x)x)-f(x)]u(x)\ dx\right|\leq 2n\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})}\|h\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}=2n\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})}\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}.

∎

Using Lemma
4.4
, we can now prove Lemma
4.3
.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

We define
\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}:B\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}(x)=u^{f}_{\Omega}(\Psi(x))
, and we will prove that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int|u^{f}_{\Omega}(x)-\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}^{2},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int|\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}(x)-u^{f}_{B}(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}^{2}

from which the lemma follows. We note that since
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}(\partial B)
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq 1
, by
[
40
, Theorem 8.33]
, we have

(4.4)

\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})},\,\|\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{B})}\leq C(n,\alpha).

To prove (
4.2
), we observe that for any
x\in B\cap\Omega
, we have

|u^{f}_{\Omega}(x)-\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}(x)|=|u^{f}_{\Omega}(x)-u^{f}_{\Omega}(\Psi(x))|\leq\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)}|x-\Psi(x)|\leq\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)}|h(x)|.

So,

\int_{\Omega\cap B}|u^{f}_{\Omega}(x)-\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq C\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{C^{1}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega\cap B}h^{2}(x)\leq C\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\partial B)}^{2}\,.

Next, for
x\in\Omega\setminus B
we have
|u^{f}_{\Omega}(x)-\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}(x)|=u^{f}_{\Omega}(x)\leq C\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)}d(x,\partial\Omega)
, so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus B}|u^{f}_{\Omega}(x)-\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{C^{1}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega\setminus B}d(x,\partial\Omega)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=C\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{C^{1}(\Omega)}\int_{S^{n-1}\cap\{\phi>0\}}\int_{1}^{1+\phi}(1+\phi-r)^{2}\,r^{n-1}dr\,d\theta

\displaystyle=C\|u^{f}_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{C^{1}(\Omega)}\int_{S^{n-1}\cap\{\phi>0\}}\int_{0}^{\phi}s^{2}\,ds\,d\theta\leq\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\partial B)}^{2}.

An analogous argument shows the same bound for the integral over
B\setminus\Omega
. Summing up, we arrive at (
4.2
).

Next we prove (
4.3
). We begin by noting that the pulled back function
\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}
solves the equation

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}=\text{det}(d\Psi(x))f(\Psi(x))&\text{ in }B\\
\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}=0&\text{ on }\partial B,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{L}u=\text{div}(A\nabla u)
for
A=m\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{A}(x)=d\Phi(\Psi(x))(d\Phi(\Psi(x))^{*}
and
m=1/\sqrt{\det\mathcal{A}}=\det(d\Psi(x))
and
\Phi=\Psi^{-1}
with the standard estimates

(4.5)

|A-\text{Id}|\leq C(h+|\nabla h|),\qquad|\det(d\Psi(x))-1|\leq C(h+|\nabla h|).

Let
v=u^{f}_{B}-\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}
; note that
v=0
on
\partial B
and that
-\Delta v=(f-mf(\Psi(x)))-(\mathcal{L}-\Delta)\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}.
By the Poincaré inequality on the ball, it suffices to estimate
\int|\nabla v|^{2}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}^{2}.
We have

\displaystyle\int_{B}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\nabla v\cdot(\nabla u^{f}_{B}-A\nabla\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega})+\int_{B}\nabla v\cdot(A-\text{Id})\nabla\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}v\left(f(x)-\det(d\Psi(x))f(\Psi(x))\right)+\int_{B}\nabla v\cdot(A-\text{Id})\nabla\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}=(I)+(II).

Now

(I)=\int_{B}v(x)(f(x)-f(\Psi(x)))\ dx+\int_{B}v(x)f(\Psi(x))(1-\text{det}(d\Psi(x)))\ dx=(Ia)+(Ib).

From Lemma
4.4
we have

|(Ia)|\leq n\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(B)}\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}\leq\epsilon\int_{B}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}.

Using the estimates in (
4.5
) we also have

|(Ib)|\leq C\int_{B}|v(x)||f(\Psi(x))|(|h|+|\nabla h|)\ dx\leq\epsilon\int_{B}v^{2}+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}.

Again using (
4.5
) as well as estimates on
\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}
we obtain

|(II)|\leq C\|\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}\|_{C^{1}(B)}\int_{B}|\nabla v|(|h|+|\nabla h|)\ dx\leq\epsilon\int_{B}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\|\hat{u}^{f}_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{C^{1}(B)}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B)}.

Using (
4.4
) this proves (
4.3
) and concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Proofs of the Main Theorems

This section is dedicated to showing how the two main theorems, Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.1
, follow from Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
4.1
. For the time being, we assume Theorem
1.3
holds; this theorem will be established in sections
6
and
7
.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We begin with Theorem
1.2
, which, as explained in the introduction, follows from Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
4.1
using a classical selection principle argument.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Step 1: Reductions and setup.

Together Lemmas
2.1
,
3.2
,
3.3
, and
3.4
show that to prove Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to show that there is a positive constant
c_{n}>0
depending on
n
such that for any bounded open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
and for any nonnegative function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq 1
.

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)

\displaystyle\geq c_{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big|^{2}\,

Here
x_{\Omega}
is the truncated barycenter defined in (
3.1
). Since the integral on the right-hand side is bounded above by
2\omega_{n}\|w_{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\omega_{n}/2n^{2}\leq 1,
the estimate (
5.1
) holds trivially when
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
by choosing the constant
c_{n}
to be small enough depending on
\varepsilon
. So, it suffices to show the theorem when is
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)
is small.

We use the short-hand notation
\beta_{f}(\Omega)=\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
. Suppose by way of contradiction that there are open bounded sets
\{\Omega_{j}\}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega_{j}|=\omega_{n}
and smooth, nonnegative, compactly supported functions
\{f_{j}\}
with
\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
such that
0<\mathrm{tor}(\Omega_{j})-\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})\to 0
but

(5.2)

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega_{j})-\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})\leq\frac{1}{j}\,\beta_{f_{j}}^{2}(\Omega_{j}).

Step 2: Replacement of
\Omega_{j}
.

Fix
\delta=\delta(n,\alpha)
according to Theorem
4.1
and let
\tau\leq\bar{\tau}_{0}(n,\alpha,\delta)=\bar{\tau}_{0}(n,\alpha)
be chosen according to Theorem
1.3
. Let
a_{j}=\beta_{f_{j}}(\Omega_{j})^{2}\in(0,1]
and consider the functional

\mathcal{F}_{j}(U)=\mathrm{tor}(U)+\tau\sqrt{a_{j}^{2}+(a_{j}-\beta^{2}_{f_{j}}(U))^{2}}\,.

By Theorem
1.3
, there is a minimizer
U_{j}
of the problem

(5.3)

\inf\{\mathcal{F}_{j}(U)\ :\ U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\text{ open bounded set},\ |U|=\omega_{n}\},

and
U_{j}-x_{U_{j}}
is a nearly spherical set parametrized by a function
\phi_{j}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\partial B_{1},{\mathbb{R}})
with
\|\phi_{j}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq\delta.
So, by Theorem
4.2
applied with
f=f_{j}(\cdot-x_{U_{j}})
,

(5.4)

\mathrm{tor}(U_{j})-\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})\geq c\,\beta_{f_{j}}^{2}(U_{j}).

Step 3: Reaching a contradiction.

Taking
\Omega_{j}
as a competitor for the minimality of
\mathcal{F}_{j}
and applying (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{tor}(U_{j})+\tau\Big(\sqrt{a_{j}^{2}+(a_{j}-\beta^{2}_{f_{j}}(U))^{2}}-a_{j}\Big)\leq\mathrm{tor}(\Omega_{j})\leq\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})+\frac{2}{j}a_{j}

The first inequality implies that
\mathrm{tor}(U_{j})\leq\mathrm{tor}(\Omega_{j})
, while the far left- and right-hand sides of the inequality and the Saint-Venant inequality guarantee that

\sqrt{1+(1-\beta^{2}_{f_{j}}(U_{j})/a_{j})^{2}}-1\leq\frac{2}{j\,\tau}

and thus
\beta_{f_{j}}(U_{j})\geq 2a_{j}=2\beta_{f_{j}}(\Omega_{j})^{2}
for
j
sufficiently large. Together these two observations and (
5.2
) imply

(5.5)

\mathrm{tor}(U_{j})-\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})\leq\frac{4}{j}\,\beta_{f_{j}}^{2}(U_{j}).

This contradicts (
5.4
) when
j
is large enough, completing the proof.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Next we prove Theorem
1.1
as an application of Theorem
1.2
. Recall that by Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to replace the Faber-Krahn deficit
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)
in Theorem
1.1
with the Saint-Venant deficit
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)
, and that in section
5.1
we proved that Theorem
1.2
holds with
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)
in place of
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)
.
Throughout this section we assume by translation that without loss of generality the truncated barycenter
x_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
is the origin.

Recall from the introduction that we denote
u_{\Omega,k}
and
\lambda_{\Omega,k}
as the
k
-th eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of
\Omega
, with the eigenfunctions extended globally to be defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and normalized so that

(5.6)

\|u_{\Omega,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1=\|u_{B,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

If
k=1
, then we simply write
u_{\Omega}
and
\lambda_{\Omega}
.

We will utilize the following qualitative control on the eigenvalues whose proof is accomplished with standard geometric measure theory and concentration compactness techniques.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open bounded set with
|\Omega|=\omega_{n}
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{k}>0
such that if

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)<\delta_{k},

then

|\lambda_{k}(\Omega)-\lambda_{k}(B)|\leq\epsilon.

The recent result of Bucur-Lamboley-Nahon-Prunier described in the introduction is a sharp quantitative version of the qualitative Proposition
5.1
.

To illustrate the method, we first prove Theorem
1.1
for the first eigenfunctions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set with
|\Omega|=|B|
and
x_{\Omega}=0
. There is a constant
c>0
depending on dimension such that

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{\Omega}-u_{B}|^{2}.

Proof.

We use the notation
\delta(\Omega)=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)
.
As in the proof of Theorem
1.2
above, it suffices to consider the case when
\delta(\Omega)
is small.
From Theorem
1.2
we have

\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B}^{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\delta(\Omega)

for any
f\in{L^{\infty}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{{L^{\infty}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq 1.

The eigenfunction
\lambda_{B}u_{B}
is bounded (indeed Lipschitz continuous), and we will consider
f=\lambda_{B}u_{B}
, so the inequality above becomes

(5.7)

\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B}^{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\lambda_{B}u_{B}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\delta(\Omega)\,.

We now write

u_{\Omega}^{f}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}u_{\Omega,k}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{B}\big|_{\Omega}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}u_{\Omega,k}.

Then from (
5.7
) we have

(5.8)

\|u_{B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}+\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B}\big|_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\delta(\Omega).

In particular,

(5.9)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(a_{k}-b_{k})^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega).

Using that
-\Delta u_{\Omega}^{f}=\lambda_{B}u_{B}\chi_{\Omega}
we obtain that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{B}b_{k}=\lambda_{\Omega,k}a_{k}.

Then from (
5.9
) we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{\Omega,k}}\right)^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega).

The spectral gap for the eigenvalues on the ball gives
\lambda_{B}/\lambda_{B,2}\leq c_{n}<1
. Combining with Proposition
5.1
, if
\delta(\Omega)
is small enough, then

\lambda_{B}/\lambda_{\Omega,2}\leq\tilde{c}_{n}<1
, and so

\frac{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{\Omega,k}}\leq\tilde{c}_{n}<1\quad\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then for a new constant
C_{1}
we have

\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\leq C_{1}\delta(\Omega).

Recalling that
\|u_{B}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
, we have from (
5.8
) that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}=\|u_{B}\big|_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 1-C\delta(\Omega).

Then

b_{1}^{2}\geq 1-(C+C_{1})\delta(\Omega),

and so

\displaystyle\|u_{\Omega}-u_{B}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u_{\Omega}-u_{B}\big|_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|u_{B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle=(1-b_{1})^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}+\|u_{B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}\leq C_{2}\delta(\Omega).

∎

We now show how the above argument works for any simple eigenvalue
\lambda_{B,j}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set with
|\Omega|=|B|
and
x_{\Omega}=0
and assume that
\lambda_{B,j}
is a simple eigenvalue for the ball. There is a constant
c
depending on dimension and
j
such that

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{\Omega,j}-u_{B,j}|^{2}.

Proof.

Once again we let
\delta(\Omega)=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)
and note that it suffices to prove the theorem when
\delta(\Omega)
is small.
As before, we let
f=\lambda_{B,j}u_{B,j}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and by applying Theorem
1.2
, we obtain

(5.10)

\|u_{B,j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}+\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B,j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B))

where the constant
C
depends on
n
and
j
via the
L^{\infty}
norm of
u_{B,j}
. We then write

u_{\Omega}^{f}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}u_{\Omega,k}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}u_{\Omega,k}.

So, (
5.10
) implies

(5.11)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(a_{k}-b_{k})^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega).

Using that
-\Delta u_{\Omega}^{f}=\lambda_{B,j}u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}
we find that
\lambda_{B,j}b_{k}=\lambda_{\Omega,k}a_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, so (
5.11
) becomes

(5.12)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{B,j}}{\lambda_{\Omega,k}}\right)^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega).

Now, (
5.10
) also guarantees that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}=\|u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq 1-C\delta(\Omega)
.
Then using the spectral gap on the ball combined with Proposition
5.1
we have that

\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{B,j}}{\lambda_{\Omega,k}}\right)^{2}\geq C(n,j)\quad\text{ whenever }j\neq k,

as long as
\delta(\Omega)
is small depending only on
n
and
k
.
Then from (
5.12
) we have
b_{j}^{2}\geq 1-C_{2}\delta(\Omega)
, and so in particular
(1-b_{j})\leq C\delta(\Omega)
.
In conclusion, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{\Omega,j}-u_{B,j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u_{\Omega,j}-u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|u_{B,j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle=(1-b_{j})^{2}+\sum_{k\neq j}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}+\|u_{B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}\leq C\delta(\Omega)

for a constant
C>0
depending on
n
and
j
.
∎

We now consider the situation in which
\lambda_{j}
is not a simple eigenvalue on the ball.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set with
|\Omega|=|B|
and assume that
\lambda_{B,j}
is an eigenvalue for the ball with multiplicity
N+1
so that
\lambda_{B,j}=\lambda_{B,j+N}
.
Then there exists a constant
c
depending on dimension and
j
, and coefficients
d_{kl}
satisfying
\sum_{k=j}^{j+N}d_{kl}^{2}=1
such that

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{l=j}^{N+j}d_{kl}u_{B,l}-u_{\Omega,l}\Big)^{2}.

Proof.

Once again set
\delta(\Omega)=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)
and note that it suffices to prove the theorem when
\delta(\Omega)
is small.

Step 1.
We first show that an eigenfunction on
B
is quantitatively close, in terms of
\delta(\Omega)
, to a linear combination of the eigenfunctions on
\Omega
. More precisely, we claim that for each for
k=0,\dots,N
, there are coefficients
d_{k0},\dots,d_{kN}
with
\sum_{l=0}^{N}d_{kl}^{2}=1
such that

(5.13)

\int|\phi_{k}-u_{B,j+k}|^{2}\leq C\,\delta(\Omega)\qquad\text{ where }\qquad\phi_{k}=\sum_{l=0}^{N}d_{kl}\,u_{\Omega,l+j}

By symmetry considerations, it suffices to prove the claim for
k=0.

To this end, let
f=\lambda_{B,j}u_{B,j}

and write

u_{\Omega}^{f}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}u_{\Omega,k}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{B}\big|_{\Omega}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}u_{\Omega,k}.

Then, applying Theorem
1.2
with this choice of
f
, we obtain

(5.14)

\big\|u_{B,j}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}+\big\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\big\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B,j}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\,\delta(\Omega),

where
C
depends on
n
and
\|u_{B.j}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
and thus on
n
and
j
. Repeating the argument of the last two proofs, using (
5.14
) together with the fact that
\lambda_{B,j}b_{k}=\lambda_{\Omega,k}a_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

(5.15)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{B,j}}{\lambda_{\Omega,k}}\right)^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega).

Since (
5.14
) implies that
\big\|u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}\big\|^{2}\geq 1-C\delta(\Omega)
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}\geq 1-C\delta(\Omega).

Then using the spectral gap on the ball, Proposition
5.1
, and (
5.15
), we see that as long as
\delta(\Omega)
is small, then

\sum_{l=j}^{j+N}b_{l}^{2}\geq 1-C_{2}\delta(\Omega)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}b_{k}^{2}\ \ +\!\!\sum_{k=N+j+1}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!b_{k}^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega)

So, if we let
u=\sum_{l=j}^{N}b_{l}u_{\Omega,l}
, then

\displaystyle\big\|u-u_{B,j}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\|u-u_{B,j}\big|_{\Omega}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\|u_{B,j}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}b_{k}^{2}\ \ +\!\!\sum_{k=N+j+1}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!b_{k}^{2}+\big\|u_{B,j}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B\setminus\Omega)}\leq C\,\delta(\Omega).

This almost proves the claim, except we note that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
. But since
\|u_{B,j}\|_{L^{2}}\geq 1-C\delta(\Omega)
, if we choose
\eta
so that
\|(1+\eta)u\|_{L^{2}}=1
, then
\eta\leq C\delta(\Omega)
. Therefore
\|(1+\eta)u-u_{B,j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega)
. Then by the triangle inequality, we have proven the claim by choosing the coefficients
d_{jl}=d_{l}=b_{l}(1+\eta)
.

Step 2:

The
(N+1)\times(N+1)
matrix
[d_{kl}]
is almost orthonormal in the sense that the matrix product
[d_{kl}][d_{kl}]^{T}
is equal to 1 along the diagonal, and all other entries have absolute value less than
3C\delta(\Omega)
. Letting
\phi_{k}
be as in (
5.13
) for
k=0,\dots,N
,
then multiplying by the transpose matrix we obtain from (
5.13
) that

\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}d_{lk}\phi_{k}-u_{\Omega,l+j}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\leq C\delta(\Omega)

for each
l=0,\dots,N
. Since
\|\phi_{k}-u_{B,k+j}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\leq C\,\delta(\Omega)
by (
5.13
), and
\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N}d_{lk}^{2}\right|\geq 1-C\delta(\Omega),
the result follows up to slightly rescaling the coefficients

d_{lk}
as in step 1.
∎

5.3.
Sharpness of the Main Theorems: Examples

Theorem
1.2
is a (consequence of the Kohler Jobin inequality and the) resolvent estimate of the form

(5.16)

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq c\left\|(-\Delta)_{\Omega}^{-1}-(-\Delta)_{B_{1}(x_{\Omega})}^{-1}\right\|^{2}_{X\to Y}\

with the function spaces
X=L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
Y=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
A natural question is whether this estimate is sharp.
Typically, the sharpness question refers to the exponent on the right-hand side of (
5.16
), i.e.

Q1:

Can the exponent
2
in (
5.16
) be replaced with a smaller one?

It is also interesting to ask if (
5.16
) is optimal with respect to the how we quantify the distance of the resolvent operator
(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}
to the family of resolvent operators on unit balls. That is,

Q2:

Can function space
Y=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in (
5.16
) be replaced with a stronger norm/smaller function space
Y
?

Q3:

Can function space
X=L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in (
5.16
) be replaced with a weaker norm/larger function space
X
?

In the three examples that follow, we address these questions of sharpness (and their counterparts for Theorem
1.1
), starting with Q1:

Example 5.5

(The exponent
2
in (
5.16
) is sharp)
.

Let
n=2
and consider the family of ellipses
E_{\varepsilon}=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:(1+\varepsilon)^{2}x^{2}+\frac{y}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}<1\}
with
|E_{\varepsilon}|=|B|=\pi
, which have (truncated) barycenter
x_{\Omega}
at the origin. Direct computation verifies that
\mathrm{tor}(E_{\varepsilon})-\mathrm{tor}(B)=c_{1}\varepsilon^{2}
for a universal constant
c_{1}
and

w_{E_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)=C_{\varepsilon}\Big(1-(1+\varepsilon)^{2}x^{2}-\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}y^{2}\Big)

where
C_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})
. On
B_{1/2}\subset B_{1}\cap E_{\varepsilon}
(for
\varepsilon
small), a calculation shows

|(w_{E_{\varepsilon}}-w_{B})|(x,y)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|x^{2}-y^{2}|+o(\varepsilon),

and thus
\int|(w_{E_{\varepsilon}}-w_{B})|^{2}\geq c_{2}\varepsilon^{2}
for a universal constant
c_{2}.
Thus, taking
f=1
and letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0
shows that the exponent
2
is (
5.16
) is optimal. Clearly the analogous example can be used in any dimension.

Similarly, we may ask Q1 in the context of Theorem
1.1
: can the exponent
2
in (
1.3
) be improved? The answer is again no.
The next example is essentially similar to Example
5.5
, though less hands-on since eigenfunctions do not have a clean closed form like the torsion functions of Example
5.5
.

Example 5.6

(The exponent
2
in (
1.3
) is sharp)
.

Let
k=1
and fix
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\partial B)
be a nontrivial function with
\int_{\partial B}\phi=\int_{\partial B}\phi(x\cdot e_{i})=0
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Let
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}\}
be a one-parameter family of open sets with smooth boundary such that
|\Omega_{t}|=\omega_{n}
and
x_{\Omega_{t}}=0
whose boundaries have normal velocity at
t=0
equal to
\phi\nu_{B}
on
\partial B
.
Then expansion of
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)
in
[
24
, Theorem 1]
togther with the spectral gap of
[
4
, Theorem 3.7]
(see also
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
) show that
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(B)\approx t^{2}\|\phi\|_{H^{1/2}}^{2}
. For
t
small,
B_{1/2}\Subset\Omega_{t}\cap B_{1}
and, writing
u_{\Omega}=u_{\Omega,1}
, we have
\sup_{x\in B_{1/2}}|u_{\Omega_{t}}(x)-u_{B}(x)-t\dot{u}(x)|\leq Ct^{2}
for a constant
C
depending on
\phi
, where
\dot{u}\in H^{1}(B)
solves

\begin{cases}-\Delta\dot{u}=\lambda_{1}(B)\dot{u}&\text{ on }B\\
\ \ \ \ \ \dot{u}=|\nabla u_{B}|\phi&\text{ on }\partial B\\
\int_{B}u_{B}\dot{u}=0.\end{cases}

see
[
42
, Section 5.7]
. Such a solution exists by the Fredholm alternative and it is not identically zero in
B_{1/2}
by the maximum principle (as
\phi
is not identically zero)
So,
\liminf_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int|u_{\Omega_{t}}-u_{B}|^{2}\geq\int_{B_{1/2}}|\dot{u}|^{2}>0
, and thus
\int|u_{\Omega_{t}}-u_{B}|^{2}\gtrsim\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{t})-\lambda_{1}(B)
for
t
small.

Next we address Q2. The analogue of the following example was originally given in our earlier paper
[
3
]
to demonstrate that the
L^{2}
norm in (
1.3
) of Theorem
1.1
cannot be replaced by the
H^{1}
norm.

Example 5.7

(
Y=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
cannot be replaced by
Y=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
)
.

Suppose by way of contradiction that the resolvent estimate (
5.16
) holds with
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in place of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, so that in particular, for the family of ellipses
\{E_{\varepsilon}\}
in Example
5.5
above,

\mathrm{tor}(E_{\varepsilon})-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq c\int|\nabla w_{E_{\varepsilon}}-\nabla w_{B}|^{2}.

Since
|\nabla w_{B}|\approx 1
on
B\setminus E_{\varepsilon}
, this would imply
\mathrm{tor}(E_{\varepsilon})-\mathrm{tor}(B)\geq c|B\setminus E_{\varepsilon}|
. On the other hand, direct computation shows that
\mathrm{tor}(E_{\varepsilon})-\mathrm{tor}(B)\approx\varepsilon^{2}
(as in Example
5.5
above) and
|B\setminus E_{\varepsilon}|\approx\varepsilon
, a contradiction for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.

Remark 5.8
.

Example
5.7
gives a partial answer to Q2 by showing that the estimate (
5.16
)
fails
with
Y=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. However, the picture is still incomplete, as there are many norms that are stronger than
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
but weaker than
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. A very interesting open problem would be to understand if (
5.16
) holds with
Y=H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for some
s\in(0,1)
.

The next example addresses Q3.

Example 5.9

(
X=L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
cannot be replaced by
X=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
p<n/2
)
.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
, and for
r\in(0,1)
small, define the set

\Omega:=B_{1-\rho}(0)\cup B_{r}(2e_{1})\cup B_{r}(-2e_{1}),

with
\rho
chosen such that
|\Omega|=|B|
. The (truncated) barycenter of
\Omega
is the origin and agrees with the barycenter of
B
. Let

f:=M\chi_{B_{r}(2e_{1})\cup B_{r}(-2e_{1})}

with
M=2|B_{r}|^{-1/p}
chosen so that
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. Now

w_{\Omega}=\begin{cases}\ \ \frac{\rho^{2}-|x|^{2}}{2n}\quad&\text{ if }x\in B_{1-\rho}\\
\ \ \frac{r^{2}-|x\mp 2e_{1}|^{2}}{2n}\quad&\text{ if }x\in B_{\pm 2e_{1}}\\
\ \ 0\quad&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad u_{\Omega}^{f}=\begin{cases}\ \ 0\quad&\text{ if }x\in B_{1-\rho}\\
\ \ M\frac{r^{2}-|x\mp 2e_{1}|^{2}}{2n}\quad&\text{ if }x\in B_{\pm 2e_{1}}\\
\ \ 0\quad&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

For small
r
we have
\rho\approx r^{n}
, so that

\mathrm{tor}({\Omega})-\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})\approx r^{n}.

Now
u_{B_{1}(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\equiv 0
and so

\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B_{1}}^{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\approx r^{n+4-2n/p}.

If
p<n/2
, then we cannot bound
\|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B_{1}}^{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}
by a constant multiple of
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})
.

Remark 5.10
.

Example
5.9
shows that the estimate (
5.16
) fails with
X=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(even for the problem restricted to
B_{R}
) for
p<n/2
, and thus, for instance, rules out
X=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in dimension
4
and higher. However, as with Q2 and Example
5.7
above, this provides only a partial answer to Q3, since there are many other possible choices of the function space
Y
. In particular, linear stability arguments of section
4
go through if
f\in L^{p}
for
p>n
.

6.
Free Boundary Estimates, Part 1: Existence and basic regularity of minimizers

The remainder of the paper is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.3
.
As discussed in section
2.2
, it is more convenient to study a relaxed version of the variational problem (
1.14
) in which the volume constraint is replaced by a volume penalization term.

The following parameters will remain unchanged throughout the remainder of the paper.

(6.1)

\begin{split}&\mbox{Let $\bar{\varepsilon}=\bar{\varepsilon}(n)>0$ be fixed according to Lemma~\ref{lem: lip bary}. }\\
&\mbox{Let $\bar{\delta}=\bar{\delta}(n)\in(0,|\mathrm{tor}(B)|/4]$ be fixed according to Proposition~\ref{lemma: qual stability} applied with $\varepsilon=\bar{\varepsilon}/8$. }\end{split}

Next, let
\eta_{1}(n)
be the constant from Proposition
2.3
and let

(6.2)

\bar{\eta}=\bar{\eta}(n)=\min\left\{\eta_{1}(n),\frac{1}{13|\mathrm{tor}(B)|},\frac{2\bar{\delta}}{\omega_{n}},\frac{|\mathrm{tor}(B)|-2\bar{\delta}}{8\omega_{n}},1\right\}\in(0,1].

Let
{V}={V}_{\bar{\eta}}
be the volume penalization term defined in (
2.5
) with
\eta=\bar{\eta}

We additionally fix a nonnegative function
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq 1
and number
a\in(0,1]
, on which none of the estimates in this section will depend, and let

(6.3)

{h}_{f}(\Omega)=\sqrt{a^{2}+\Big(a-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}\big|^{2}\Big)^{2}}\,

where
x_{\Omega}
is the truncated barycenter of
\Omega
defined in (
3.1
). For a parameter
\tau>0
, define the functional
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
by

(6.4)

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)+{V}(|\Omega|)+\tau{h}_{f}(\Omega)\,.

We consider the unconstrained minimization problem

(6.5)

\inf\{{\mathcal{F}_{\tau}}(\Omega):\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\text{ open, bounded, }|\Omega|\leq 2\omega_{n}\}

and its counterpart for sets contained in a large ball
B_{R}
.
The following statement summarizes the main result of the section.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
f
is compactly supported. There is a dimensional constant
\bar{\tau}>0
such that for any
\tau\in(0,\bar{\tau}],
a minimizer
\Omega
of the variational problem
exists, has
\text{diam}(\Omega)\leq 2
, and satisfies

(6.6)

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)+{V}(|\Omega|)\leq\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})+\bar{\delta}.

Moreover, there are positive dimensional constants
r_{0}
,
c_{*}
,
C_{*}
,
c
, and
K
such that the following holds.
For all
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, the torsion function
w_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
satisfies

(6.7)

c_{*}r\leq\sup_{B_{r}(x)}w_{\Omega}\leq C_{*}r\,.

Moreover,
\Omega
is a set of finite perimeter in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega)=0
, satisfies the density estimates

(6.8)

\displaystyle c\,\omega_{n}r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq|B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega|\leq(1-c)\omega_{n}r^{n}

(6.9)

\displaystyle c\,r^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq|B_{r}(x)\cap{\partial}\Omega|\leq\frac{1}{c}r^{n-1}

for all
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,r_{0}],
and is an NTA domain with NTA constant
K
.

When
f
is not compactly supported, analogous results hold for (
6.5
) for sets contained in a large ball
B_{R}
; see Theorem
6.11
below.
The constant
\bar{\tau}
is defined in (
6.42
).

6.1.
Initial estimates for outward and inward minimizers

Given
R>0
, an open bounded set
\Omega\subset B_{R}
is called an inward (resp., outward) minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
in
B_{R}
if
|\Omega|\leq 2\omega_{n}

and

We begin by proving some fundamental estimates that will be used to prove nondegeneracy and Lipschitz estimates for inward and outward minimizers, respectively.

\begin{cases}\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)+{V}(|\Omega|)\leq\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})+\bar{\delta}&\\
|\Omega\Delta\Omega^{\prime}|\leq\omega_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}/{2}&\end{cases}\quad\implies\quad\ \ |{h}_{f}(\Omega)-{h}_{f}(\Omega^{\prime})|\leq\bar{C}_{n}\,\Big(|\Omega\Delta\Omega^{\prime}|+\int|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{f}|\Big)\,.

Here the parameters are chosen as in (

The following lemma, whose elementary proof is based on the maximum principle, will be key to controlling the right-hand side of (

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be open bounded sets with
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
.
Then

(6.11)

\int|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{f}|\,dx\leq\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega).

Proof.

By the maximum principle,
u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)\leq w_{\Omega}(x)
for all

, and in particular for all

. Since

u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{f}\leq w_{\Omega}-w_{\Omega^{\prime}}
on

\partial\Omega^{\prime}
. Since the left- and right-hand sides are harmonic in
\Omega^{\prime}

u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{f}\leq w_{\Omega}-w_{\Omega^{\prime}}
in
\Omega^{\prime}
as well. Thus

0\leq u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{f}\leq w_{\Omega}-w_{\Omega^{\prime}}\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Integrating both sides and recalling (

\int|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{f}|\leq\int w_{\Omega}-w_{\Omega^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})\right),

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.3

For any

\begin{split}\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})&\leq{V}(|\Omega^{\prime}|)-{V}(|\Omega|)+\tau({h}_{f}(\Omega^{\prime})-{h}_{f}(\Omega))\\

6.2.
Nondegeneracy for inward minimizers

The next theorem shows that torsion function of an inward minimizer satisfying (

\tau_{0}>0
is as in (

Theorem 6.4
.

There is a dimensional constant
c_{*}>0
such that for any

\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}]
and
R>0
, an inward minimizer
\Omega
of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
in

satisfying the energy bound (

\sup_{x\in B_{r}(y)}w_{\Omega}(x)\geq c_{*}r

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a inward minimizer
\Omega
of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
in

satisfying the energy bound (

Following David and Toro

26

, we prove the following main decay estimate: there is a constant

and

(6.19)

\sup_{B_{r}(y)}w_{\Omega}\leq c_{*}r\qquad\implies\qquad\sup_{B_{r/2}(y)}w_{\Omega}\leq\frac{c_{*}r}{4}\,.

Before proving (
6.19
), let us see how iterating it will lead to the desired conclusion.
Up to replacing
c_{*}
with a smaller constant

in the final estimate (
6.18
), it suffices to prove the lower bound for

Suppose the conclusion fails, so for some

and

Thus, by (

\sup_{B_{r/2}(y)}w_{\Omega}<c_{*}r/4

\sup_{B_{r/4}(x)}w_{\Omega}\leq c_{*}\left(\frac{r}{4}\right)\qquad\text{ for all }x\in B_{r/4}(y).

Iteratively applying (
6.19

\sup_{{B_{r/4\cdot 2^{k}}(x)}}w_{\Omega}\leq\frac{c_{*}r}{4^{k+1}}\qquad\text{ for all }x\in B_{r/4}(y).

So, each point in

We now show (
6.19
). In what follows,

\frac{|\Omega\cap B_{3r/4}(y)|}{r^{n}}\leq\frac{C_{n}}{\tau}\,c_{*}^{2}

Indeed, take

.
Since

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\nabla(w_{\Omega}\phi)|^{2}&=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\nabla w_{\Omega}|^{2}\phi^{2}+\int_{B_{7r/8}(y)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}w_{\Omega}^{2}+2\int_{B_{7r/8}(y)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla w_{\Omega})w_{\Omega}\phi\\

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\partial}B_{r}(x_{0})}K(x,y)w_{\Omega}(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq c_{n}M_{0}(r-|x-x_{0}|)\,

x\in B_{r}(x_{0}).

Applying (
6.34
) to the integrand on the right-hand side of (
6.33
) we get

(6.35)

C_{n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla(w_{\Omega}-v)(x)|^{2}\geq M_{0}|B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|.

Putting this together with (
6.32
), we see that
|B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|\leq\frac{C_{n}}{M_{0}\bar{\eta}}|B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|
. Taking
M_{0}>0
large enough depending on
n
and
\bar{\eta}
, and thus only on
n
, so that
\frac{C_{n}}{M_{0}\bar{\eta}}<1
, we deduce that
|B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|=0
, i.e.
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. By the strong maximum principle, the lemma holds.
∎

We now prove Theorem
6.6
.

Proof of Theorem
6.6
.

Fix
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,1)
and let

S(x,r)=\sup\{w_{\Omega}(y)\,:\,y\in\partial B_{r}(x),\,d(y,\partial\Omega)=r\}.

We claim that
S(x,r)\leq C_{*}r
, which directly implies (
6.26
).
Let
r_{0}>0
is the dimensional constant from Lemma
6.8
. Since
\|w_{\Omega}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
is bounded by a dimensional constant, we trivially have
S(x,r)\leq C_{0}r
for
r\geq\min\{1/8,r_{0}\}
and for a dimensional constant
C_{0}
. We assume henceforth that
r\leq\min\{1/8,r_{0}\}
.

Case 1:
First suppose
\text{dist}(x,\partial B_{R})>2r.

In this case, we show that
S(x,r)\leq C_{1}r
where
C_{1}
depends only on
n
.
Choose
y\in\partial B_{r}(x)
achieving the supremum in the definition of
S(x,r)
.
By Harnack inequality applied to
w_{\Omega}
on
B_{r}(y)\subset\Omega
,

(6.36)

S(x,r)\leq C_{2}\inf_{B_{r/2}(y)}w_{\Omega}+C_{2}r^{2}

for a dimensional constant
C_{2}
.
If
S(x,r)\leq\tfrac{C_{2}r}{2}
, the claim holds (with
C_{1}=C_{2}/2
. If
S(x,r)\geq\tfrac{C_{2}r}{2}
, we can absorb the final term in (
6.36
) into the left-hand side to find that
S(x,r)\leq 2C_{2}\inf_{B_{r/2}(y)}w_{\Omega}
.
This estimate, together with the easily verified geometric fact
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}(x)\cap B_{r/2}(y))\geq c_{n}r^{n-1}
, implies

S(x,r)\leq 2C_{2}\fint_{\partial B_{r}(x)\cap B_{r/2}(y)}w_{\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C_{3}\fint_{\partial B_{r}(x)}w_{\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

where
C_{3}
is another dimensional constant.
Since
x\in\partial\Omega
, we have
w_{\Omega}(x)=0
and so by (the contrapositive of) Lemma
6.8
, the left-hand side is bounded above by
C_{3}M_{0}r
. This shows the claim in this case. Thus,
S(x,r)\leq C_{1}r
where
C_{1}=\max\{\tfrac{C_{2}}{2},C_{3}\,M_{0}\}
is a constant depending on
n
.

Case 2:
Next, suppose
\text{dist}(x,\partial B_{R})\leq 2r.
By the maximum principle,
w_{\Omega}(y)\leq w_{B_{R}}(y)\leq C(n,R)r
for any
y\in B_{r}(x)
, so
S(x,r)\leq C(n,R)r
in this case.

Thus (
6.26
) holds with
C_{*}=\max\{C_{0},C_{1},C(n,R)\}
. In the case when
\text{dist}(\Omega,\partial B_{R})\geq 2
, case 2 above never occurs do (
6.26
) holds for the dimensional constant
C_{*}=\max\{C_{0},C_{1}\}
.
∎

6.4.
The lower semicontinuity and replacement lemmas

As discussed in the introduction, direct approaches do not suffice to establish the existence of minimizers of our main variational problem (
6.5
).
The main challenge is that the nonlinear term
{h}_{f}(\cdot)
in the energy
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
has poor (lower semi)continuity properties, and an arbitrary minimizing sequence for
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
(in
B_{R}
, say) will not be compact in a sufficiently strong topology to make the energy lower semicontinuous.

Lemma
6.9
below shows that under rather strong convergence assumptions,
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
is lower semicontinuous. Here, the nonlinear term alone may
not
be lower semicontinuous, but by choosing the parameter
\tau
in
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
to be sufficiently small and estimating the failure of lower semi-continuity in a quantitative way in (
6.39
), we can obtain lower semi-continuity of the full energy
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
.

An arbitrary minimizing sequence will not subsequentially converge in the sense needed to apply Lemma
6.9
.
We replace an arbitrary minimizing sequence with a more regular one comprised of outward minimizers using Lemma
6.10
below. Combining this with the Lipschitz bound for outward minimizers shown in Theorem
6.6
, we can prove existence of minimizers for the problem in
B_{R}
; see Theorem
6.11
below.
To then establish the existence of minimizers in all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in Theorem
6.1
, the diameter bound of Corollary
6.5
plays a key role.

Lemma 6.9

(Lower semicontunity lemma)
.

Fix
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}]
, and
R>0
. Let
\{U_{j}\}
be a sequence of open sets in
B_{R}
with
|U_{j}|\leq 2\omega_{n}
satisfying (
6.12
), and let
\Omega\subset B_{R}
be an open set.
Assume
\{w_{U_{j}}\}
and
w_{\Omega}
are continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and that
w_{U_{j}}\to w_{\Omega}
and uniformly. Then
|\Omega|\leq 2\omega_{n}
and

(6.37)

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{j}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{j}).

Proof.

By continuity of the torsion functions, we may write
\Omega=\{w_{\Omega}>0\}
and
U_{j}=\{w_{U_{j}}>0\}
, and from the uniform convergence of the torsion functions,
\liminf_{j}1_{U_{j}\cap\Omega}\to 1_{\Omega}
pointwise. So,
|\Omega|\leq\liminf_{j}|\Omega\cap U_{j}|
by Fatou’s lemma. Thus, setting

\gamma=\liminf_{j}|U_{j}|,

we find that
\liminf_{j}\,|\Omega\Delta U_{j}|=\gamma-|\Omega|\geq 0
. In particular,
|\Omega|\leq\gamma\leq 2\omega_{n}
.

Note that
w_{U_{j}}\to w_{\Omega}
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Since
\mathrm{tor}(E)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{E}w_{E}
by (
2.2
), this guarantees that

(6.38)

\lim_{j}\mathrm{tor}(U_{j})=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega).

Moreover, thanks to a theorem of Šverák (see
[
42
, Theorem 3.2.5]
) we have
u_{U_{j}}^{f}\to u_{\Omega}^{f}
in
L^{2}(B_{R})
.

To make use of this latter fact, we aim to apply (
6.10
).
Since
t\mapsto{V}_{\eta}(t)
is nondecreasing and
|\Omega|\leq\gamma
, we know
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)+{V}_{\eta}(|\Omega|)\leq\liminf_{j}(\mathrm{tor}(U_{j})+{V}_{\eta}(|U_{j}|)
. Thus

\Omega
satisfies the energy bound (
6.12
) as well.
Applying Lemma
2.3
to
U_{j}
and to
\Omega
shows that

\Big(1-\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}\Big)\,\omega_{n}\leq|U_{j}|,\,|\Omega|\leq\Big(1+\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}\Big)\,\omega_{n},

so in particular,
\gamma-|\Omega|\leq\bar{\varepsilon}.
Since
\liminf_{j}\,|\Omega\Delta U_{j}|=\gamma-|\Omega|
as observed above, this means

\liminf_{j}|\Omega\Delta U_{j}|\leq\bar{\varepsilon}\omega_{n}.

So, by (
6.10
) and
u_{U_{j}}^{f}\to u_{\Omega}^{f}
in
L^{2}(B_{R})
,

(6.39)

\displaystyle\liminf_{j}{h}_{f}(U_{j})-{h}_{f}(\Omega)\geq-\bar{C}_{n}\liminf_{j}|\Omega\Delta U_{j}|=-\bar{C}_{n}(\gamma-|\Omega|)\,

where
\bar{C}_{n}
is the dimensional constant from (
6.10
).
Next, from the definition of
{V}_{\eta}
we have

(6.40)

\displaystyle\liminf_{j}\,{V}(|U_{j}|)-{V}(|\Omega|)\geq{\bar{\eta}}\,(\gamma-|\Omega|)\,.

Combining (
6.38
), (
6.40
) and (
6.39
), we find that

\displaystyle\liminf_{j}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{j})-\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq(\bar{\eta}-\bar{C}_{n}\tau)(\gamma-|\Omega|)\geq 0,

where the final inequality follows from (
6.13
) and the fact that we have chosen
\tau\leq\tau_{0}={\bar{\eta}}/2\bar{C}_{n}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma, which is similar to
[
4
, Lemma 6.1]
, allows us to replace any minimizing sequence by a minimizing sequence of outward minimizers, all of whose torsion functions satisfy a uniform Lipschitz estimate.
Recall that the dimensional constant
\bar{\delta}
was fixed in (
6.1
).

Lemma 6.10
.

For any
\tau\in(0,\min\{\tau_{0},\bar{\delta}/2\}]
and
R>0
, the following holds.
Let
\Omega\subset B_{R}
be an open set satisfying
|\Omega|\leq 2\omega_{n}
and

(6.41)

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)+{V}_{\eta}(|\Omega|)\leq\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})+\bar{\delta}/2,

there is an open set
U
with
\Omega\subset U\subset B_{R}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)
such that
U
is an outward minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
in
B_{R}
.

Proof.

Given an open bounded set
E\subset B_{R}
with
|E|\leq 2\omega_{n}
, define the quantity

m_{E}=\inf\big\{\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(V)\ :\ V\text{ open},E\subset V\subset B_{R},\ |V|\leq 2\omega_{n}\big\}.

Note that
m_{E}\in[\mathrm{tor}(B_{1}),\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(E)]
. If
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)=m_{\Omega}
, then
\Omega=U
is an outward minimizer satisfying the conclusions of the lemma.

Otherwise, define a (possibly finite) sequence of nested open sets
\Omega=:U_{0}\subset U_{1}\subset\dots\subset U_{k-1}\subset U_{k}\subset\dots
inductively as follows.
For
k\geq 0
, if
m_{U_{k}}=\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k})
, terminate the sequence and take
U=U_{k}
to conclude the proof of the lemma. If instead
m_{U_{k}}<\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k})
, choose an open set
U_{k+1}
with
U_{k}\subset U_{k+1}\subset B(x_{0},R)
satisfying
|U_{k+1}|\leq 2\omega_{n}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k+1})\leq(\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k})+m_{U_{k}})/2.
If the resulting sequence is finite, the proof is complete.

Otherwise, let
U=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}U_{k}
. Then
\Omega\subset U\subset B(x_{0},R)
, and
|U|=\lim|U_{k}|
by monotone convergence so that
|U|\leq 2\omega_{n}
and
|U\Delta U_{k}|\to 0
. Since
m_{U}\geq m_{U_{k}}
for each
k
by containment,
m_{U}\geq\bar{m}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k})
, and
\bar{m}\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)
since
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k})
is decreasing.
We claim that
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U)=\bar{m}
, which implies
U
is an outer minimizer satisfying
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)
, thus completing the proof.

Since
\tau_{0}\leq\bar{\delta}/2
, by (
6.41
) we have
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)\leq\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})+\bar{\delta}
, and thus

\mathrm{tor}(U_{k})+{V}_{\eta}(|U_{k}|)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k})\leq\mathrm{tor}(B_{1})+\bar{\delta}

for all
k
. Since additionally
|U_{k}\Delta U|=|U\setminus U_{k}|\leq\omega_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}/{2}
for
k
large enough, by (
6.10
) we have

\ |{h}_{f}(U_{k})-{h}_{f}(U)|\leq\bar{C}_{n}\,\Big(|U\setminus U_{k}|+\int|u_{U}^{f}-u_{U_{k}}^{f}|\Big)\,.

Then by Lemma
6.2
,

\ |{h}_{f}(U_{k})-{h}_{f}(U)|\leq\bar{C}_{n}\big(|U_{k}\Delta U|+\mathrm{tor}(U_{k})-\mathrm{tor}(U)\big)

and in particular
{h}_{f}(U)\leq\liminf_{k}{h}_{f}(U_{k})+\bar{C}_{n}\big(\liminf_{k}\mathrm{tor}(U_{k})-\mathrm{tor}(U)\big)
.

Taking
w_{U_{k}}\in H^{1}_{0}(U)
as competitors for
\mathrm{tor}(U)
shows that
\liminf_{k}\mathrm{tor}(U_{k})\geq\mathrm{tor}(U)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{k})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U)=\liminf_{k}\mathrm{tor}(U_{k})-\mathrm{tor}(U)+\tau\big(\liminf_{k}{h}_{f}(U_{k})-{h}_{f}(U)\big)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\bar{C}_{n}\tau)\big(\liminf_{k}\mathrm{tor}(U_{k})-\mathrm{tor}(U)\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(\liminf_{k}\mathrm{tor}(U_{k})-\mathrm{tor}(U)\big)\geq 0

where the penultimate inequality follows since
\bar{\eta}\leq 1
and thus in (
6.13
) we take
\tau_{0}\leq\tfrac{\bar{\eta}}{2\bar{C}_{n}}\leq\tfrac{1}{2\bar{C}_{n}}
.
∎

6.5.
Existence and basic properties

Let

(6.42)

\bar{\tau}=\min\{\tau_{0},\delta_{D},\bar{\delta}/16\}

with
\tau_{0}
as in (
6.13
),
\bar{\delta}
as in (
6.1
), and
\delta_{D}
from Corollary
6.5
. Next we establish the existence of minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
in a ball

Theorem 6.11
.

For any
\tau\in(0,\bar{\tau}]
and

, a minimizer
\Omega
of the variational problem

(6.43)

\inf\{\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(E):E\subset B_{R}\text{ open},\ |E|\leq 2\omega_{n}\}

admits a minimizer. Any minimizer
\Omega
of (
6.43
) has
\text{diam}(\Omega)\leq 2
and satisfies (

).

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
denote the infimum in (
6.43
) and a minimizing sequence
\{\Omega_{j}\}
for (
6.43
).
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\alpha\geq\mathrm{tor}(B)
. On the other hand, Note that
{h}_{f}(\Omega)\leq\sqrt{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{B(x_{\Omega})}^{f}|^{2}\leq 4
by (
2.4
), so taking
B_{1}
as a competitor in (
6.43
) shows
\alpha\leq\mathrm{tor}(B)+4\bar{\tau}.

Then, since we chose
4\bar{\tau}\leq\bar{\delta}/4
in (
6.42
),
we see that
\Omega_{j}
satisfies the energy bound (
6.41
) assumed in the statement of Lemma

as soon as

is large enough that
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega_{j})\leq\alpha+\bar{\delta}/4
.

Apply Lemma
6.10
to obtain a new minimizing sequence
\{U_{j}\}
for (
6.43
) where
U_{j}
is an outward minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
in

and

satisfies the energy bound (
6.12
) (since
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{j})\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega_{j})\leq\mathrm{tor}(B)+\bar{\delta}/2
).
By Theorem
6.6
, the torsion functions
w_{U_{j}}
are equi-Lipschitz in

and thus subsequentially converge uniformly to a Lipschitz function
w
. Let

\Omega=\{w>0\}\subset B_{R}.

The set
\Omega
is open since

is continuous.
By uniform convergence, for any compact subset
K\subset\Omega
, we have
K\subset U_{j}
for large

, so standard elliptic estimates show
w_{j}\to w
in

and thus

.
By the lower semicontinuity lemma, Lemma
6.9
,

\liminf_{j}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{j})\geq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)

and so
\Omega
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
in
B_{R}
.

Finally,
\Omega
is, in particular, an inward minimizer in
B_{R}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)=\alpha\leq\mathrm{tor}(B)+\delta_{D}
thanks to (
6.42
). Thus,
\text{diam}(\Omega)\leq 2
by Corollary
6.5
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, focusing on functions
f
that are compactly supported, we prove existence of minimizers on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by taking a sequence of minimizers from Theorem
6.11
on balls with radii tending to infinity.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
U_{j}
be a minimizer of (
6.43
) with
R=j.
Theorem
6.11
guarantees that such a minimizer exists, has
\text{diam}(U_{j})\leq 2
, and satisfies (
6.12
). Choose
x_{j}
so that
U_{j}\subset B_{2}(x_{j}).

Case 1:
Suppose
\limsup_{j}|x_{j}|<\infty
. Then there is
R_{0}>0
such that
U_{j}\subset B_{R_{0}}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}.
Thus for

large,
\text{dist}(U_{j},\partial B_{R_{j}})\geq 2
, and so by Theorem

, the torsion functions
w_{U_{j}}
are equi-Lipschitz. By Arzelà-Ascoli, up to a subsequence, the torsion functions
w_{U_{j}}
converge uniformly to a Lipschitz function

supported in
B_{R_{0}}
. Let

\Omega=\{w>0\}\subset B_{R_{0}}

As in the proof of Theorem

, the uniform convergence together with elliptic estimates show that

By Lemma
6.9
,
|\Omega|\leq 2\omega_{n}
and

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{j}\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(U_{j}).

This shows that
\Omega
is a minimizer of (

), since for any open, bounded competitor

with

there is some

Case 2:

, so in particular

for

is translation invariant outside of

, in the sense that if

Therefore, the sets

Finally, the measure theoretic estimates of Theorem

7.
Free Boundary Estimates, Part 2: Stationarity Conditions and Free Boundary Regularity

Throughout this section, let
\tau\in(0,\bar{\tau}],
where
\bar{\tau}
is the dimensional constant from Theorem
6.1
. Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a nonnegative function with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
.
Let
\Omega
be a minimizer of the variational problem (
6.5
), whose existence was shown in Theorem
6.1
. Recall from Theorem
6.1
that
\text{diam}(\Omega)\leq 2
, so in particular the truncated barycenter
x_{\Omega}
agrees with the classical barycenter
\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}x\,dx.
Moreover,
\Omega
is a set of finite perimeter and an NTA domain with NTA constant
K
depending only on
n
, and there exist dimensional constants
c_{*},C_{*}
and
r_{0}
depending on
n
such that if
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, then

cr\leq\sup_{B_{r}(x)}w_{\Omega}\leq Cr\,.

These facts allow us to employ the inhomogeneous boundary Harnack principle
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
which we will utilize throughout the remainder of the paper. We emphasize that the inhomogeneous boundary Harnack principle will not hold for arbitrary NTA domains; however, the growth condition for the torsion function is a sufficient condition for the inhomogeneous boundary Harnack principle to hold, see Lemma 7.7 in
[
3
]
. For convenience, we restate the result here in the context of our setting.

Theorem 7.1
.

Fix
x\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r\leq r_{0}
.
Assume that
|\Delta v|\leq 1
in
\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)
and
v=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)
. Then there exists a constant
C
and
0<\gamma<1
depending on
n
such that

\left\|\frac{v}{w_{\Omega}}\right\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{B_{r/2}(x)\cap\Omega)}}\leq C\sup_{B_{r/2}(x)\cap\Omega}|v|.

Remark 7.2
.

By applying the above result to
u_{\Omega}^{f}/w_{\Omega}
, it is clear we may replace the torsion function
w_{\Omega}
with
u_{\Omega}^{f}
in the above theorem (provided we divide the constant
C
by
\sup_{B_{r/2}\cap\Omega}u
) since
0\leq f\leq 1
. Also, Theorem 2.2 in
[
6
]
assumes

and only gives the bound on the quotient. However, since the Laplace operator is linear, standard techniques, see for instance
[
10
]
, give the Hölder continuity of the quotient and do not require the numerator to have a sign.

In view of Remark
7.2
and Remark
6.7
, there are
dimensional constants
C,c>0
such that
for
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r\in(0,r_{0}]
,

(7.1)

cr\leq\sup_{B_{r}(x)}u_{\Omega}^{f}\leq Cr\,.

Remark 7.3
.

For functions

that are not constantly equal to

outside a large ball, the estimates shown in this section will also hold away from
\partial B_{R}
for a minimizer
\Omega_{R}
of the variational problem (
6.43
).

7.1.
Euler-Lagrange equation

Our first task is to compute the first variation of the energy functional
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
, which we carry out in several steps.

For a vector field
T\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, let
\Phi_{t}(x)=\Phi(t,x):(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the flow generated by

, i.e. the solution to
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}(x)=T(\Phi_{t}(x))

with
\Phi_{0}(x)=x.
Let
\Omega_{t}=\Phi_{t}(\Omega)
.

Since
f
is smooth, in particular
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Thus by
[
42
, Theorem 5.3.1]
,

t\mapsto u_{\Omega_{t}}^{f}
is differentiable in
L^{2}
and the derivative
\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}
at
t=0
satisfies

(7.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\
\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}+T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\end{cases}

and
t\mapsto\int(u_{\Omega_{t}}^{f})^{2}
is differentiable at
t=0
with

(7.3)

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\,\int\left(u_{\Omega_{t}}^{f}\right)^{2}=2\int u_{\Omega}^{f}\,\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}.

To express (
7.3
) as a boundary integral with a clearer
T
-dependence, consider the auxiliary equation

(7.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta p=u_{\Omega}^{f}\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
p=0\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If
\Omega
were smooth, then integrating by parts twice on the right-hand side of (
7.3
) using (
7.2
) would show that
\int u_{\Omega}^{f}\,\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla p|\,|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}|\,T\cdot\nu_{\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
. In Lemma
7.7
below, we show that this formal computation can be made rigorous, interpreting the terms
|\nabla p|
and
T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}
as non-tangential limits. As a first step in this direction, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

For
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner{\partial\Omega}
a.e. point, the nontangential maximal function
(\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f})^{*}
is in
L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
and the non-tangential limit of
\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}
exists and is equal to
-|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}|\,\nu_{\Omega}
with
|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}|\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
.

Proof.

We recall from Theorem
6.6
and Remark
6.7
that
\|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C
. From this it follows that
(\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f})^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
at any point on
\partial\Omega
for which the nontangential limit of
\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}
exists, its modulus is bounded by the same constant.

Let
x\in\partial\Omega
, and let
v
be the harmonic replacement of
u_{\Omega}^{f}
in
B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega
and extended to be zero on
B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega
. From Theorem
7.1
the ratio
v/u_{\Omega}^{f}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{B_{r/2}(x)\cap\Omega})
. Then
v
has the same linear growth (
7.1
) from
\partial\Omega
. Consequently, we may utilize the results in
[
8
]
for nonnegative harmonic functions which vanish on the boundary and have linear growth from the boundary. Specifically, by
[
8
, Remark 4.2 and Theorem 4.3]
,

c\rho^{n-1}\leq\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}d\lambda\leq C\rho^{n-1},

where
\lambda:=-\Delta v

is a positive Radon measure with support in
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)
. If
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega\subset B_{r/2}(x)\cap\partial\Omega
, then again from the Hölder continuity of the ratio
v/u_{\Omega}^{f}
, the distributional Laplacian
of
u_{\Omega}^{f}
satisfies

(7.5)

c\rho^{n-1}\leq-\Delta u_{\Omega}^{f}|_{\partial\Omega}\leq C\rho^{n-1}.

So, by the perimeter density estimates (
6.9
), the measures
-\Delta u_{\Omega}^{f}\llcorner{\partial\Omega}
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner{\partial\Omega}
are mutually absolutely continuous, and by the Radon-Nikodym theorem,

(7.6)

-\Delta u_{\Omega}^{f}\llcorner{\partial\Omega}=g\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner{\partial\Omega},

for a function
g
defined
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
a.e on
\partial\Omega
.

Recall
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega)=0
from Proposition
6.1
, so for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x
in
\partial\Omega
,
g
is defined and
x\in\partial^{*}\Omega
. Let
x_{0}
be such a point.
By translation and rotation we may assume that
x_{0}=0
the origin and the inward unit normal of
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}
is
e_{n}
, i.e.
\nu_{\Omega}(x_{0})=-e_{n}
. Consider the rescaling

u_{r}(x):=\frac{u_{\Omega}^{f}(rx)}{r}.

From the linear growth estimates, we have that
\|u_{r}\|_{C^{0,1}(B_{R})}\leq C
. Thus, for a subsequence
r_{j}\to 0
we obtain a limiting function
u_{r_{j}}\to u_{\infty}
in
C^{0,\beta}
on uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\Delta u_{\infty}=0
on
\{u_{\infty}>0\}
. Since
x_{0}\in\partial^{*}\Omega
, we have that
\{u_{\infty}>0\}=\{x_{n}>0\}
. From the Liouville theorem,
u_{\infty}(x)=ax_{n}^{+}
for a positive constant
a
.
By integrating against test functions along the sequence, we find that
-\Delta u_{\infty}=g(x_{0})\mathcal{H}^{n-1}|_{\{x_{n}=0\}}
. This means
a=g(x_{0})
, and in particular,
this limit
u_{\infty}
is independent of the sequence
r_{j}
chosen. Thus, the convergence is uniform in
C^{1}
on
\{1/2<|x|<2\}\cap\{x\cdot e_{n}>\delta\}
for any small
\delta>0
. Thus,
\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}
has a non-tangential limit equal to
g(x_{0})e_{n}
at
x_{0}
. This completes the proof with
g=|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}|.

∎

Next we use Lemma
7.4
to establish non-tangential limits of the function
\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}
from (
7.2
).

Lemma 7.5
.

For
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner{\partial\Omega}
a.e. point, nontangential limits of
\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}
exist and are equal to
-\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}\cdot T=|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}|\nu_{\Omega}\cdot T\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
, and the non-tangential maximal function
(\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f})^{*}
of
\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}
is in
L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
.

Proof.

Let
v=\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}+T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and extend
v
by zero so that
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
From Theorem 1 in Chapter 4.8 in
[
31
]
,

v
has a
2
-quasicontinuous representative
v^{*}
such that for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega
,

\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|v-v^{*}(x)|^{2}=0.

Since
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, for
x\in\partial^{*}\Omega
, we have
\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega|}{|B_{r}(x)|}=1/2
and therefore

0\geq\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega}|v-v^{*}(x)|^{2}=\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega}|v^{*}(x)|^{2}\geq c|v^{*}(x)|^{2}\,.

Then
v^{*}=0
for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
a.e.
x\in\partial^{*}\Omega
.

Since
x\in\partial^{*}\Omega
and thanks to (
2.10
), for each
\varepsilon>0
we can choose
r>0
small enough so that
\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
for the cone
\mathcal{C}=\{y:y\cdot\nu_{\Omega}(x)<-\varepsilon|y|\}
touching
x
.
By integrating on such an interior cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\Omega
we have

0=\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|v-v^{*}(x)|^{2}\geq c\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|v-v^{*}(x)|^{2}=c\fint_{\mathcal{C}}|v|^{2}.

Now fix
\epsilon>0
, and choose
r
small enough so that

\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|v|^{2}<\epsilon,

as well as

|T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(y)-T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)|<\epsilon\quad\text{ for any }y\in\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x),

which we may do thanks to Lemma
7.4
. Then

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}(y)+T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)|^{2}\ dy

\displaystyle\leq 2\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}(y)+T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(y)|^{2}\ dy+2\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)-T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(y)|^{2}\ dy

\displaystyle=\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|v(y)|^{2}\ dy+\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(y)-T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)|^{2}\ dy

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon.

Now let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be an interior cone to
\mathcal{C}
. From the interior
L^{2}-L^{\infty}
estimate for harmonic functions, we conclude

(7.7)

\|\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}+T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{C^{\prime}}\cap(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{r/2}(x))}\leq C(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\fint_{\mathcal{C}\cap B_{r}(x)}|\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}(y)+T\cdot\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)|^{2}\ dy\leq 2\epsilon.

It is not hard to show that using (
2.10
) that for any
\beta>0
,
\Gamma_{\beta}(x)\cap B_{r}(x)
is contained in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap B_{r}(x)
for some interior cone
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
touching
x
, so (
7.7
) shows that
\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f}
has a nontangential limit at
x
equal to
-T\cdot\nabla_{\Omega}^{f}(x)
, which by Lemma
7.4
is equal to
|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}(x)|\nu_{\Omega}(x)\cdot T(x)
. Note that (
7.7
) together with the Lipschitz bound
\|\nabla u_{\Omega}^{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}
guarantee that
(\dot{u}_{\Omega}^{f})^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}).
This completes the proof.
∎

Since

Lemma 7.6

The quotients
|\nabla p|/|\nabla w_{\Omega}|

have nontangential limits

By translation and rotation, we may assume that

. Since both

and

, then

\frac{p(te_{n})}{u_{\Omega}^{f}(te_{n})}=\frac{p(te_{n})-p(0)}{u_{\Omega}^{f}(te_{n})-u_{\Omega}^{f}(0)}.

Since the limit of the left-hand side exists by the boundary Harnack principle Theorem
7.1

Lemma 7.7

Putting the preceding lemmas together, we arrive at the following distributional form of the Euler-Lagrange equation:

Theorem 7.11

7.2.
Boundary regularity

If every free boundary point is a so-called “flat” free boundary point, then Corollary

\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\gamma}

Lemma 7.13
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. There exists a constant
\bar{\tau}_{1}\in(0,\bar{\tau}_{0}]
depending on
n
and
\varepsilon

\tau\leq\bar{\tau}_{1}

-x_{\Omega}

Proof.

By choosing
\tau
small enough, we have from (

) that after translation

|B_{1}\Delta\Omega|\leq\epsilon_{1}.

We will now improve the

estimate above to an

\epsilon_{1}\geq|\Omega\cap B_{r}(y)|\geq c\omega_{n}r^{n}.

∎

Theorem 7.14
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and let

be the Hölder constant from Theorem
7.1

\bar{\tau}_{2}\in(0,\bar{\tau}_{0}]

and

Let

, and let

in

7.1
, the quotient
w_{\Omega}/u\in C^{0,\gamma}

From Theorem

]
that locally near

, that
\Omega
can be parametrized by a

Using a covering argument, we then obtain that

is parametrized by

∎

We now utilize the

satisfies the volume constraint.

Lemma 7.15

(Volume constraint lemma)
.

Let
\bar{\tau}_{2}
be fixed according to Theorem
7.14
with
\varepsilon=1/2
and fix
\tau\leq\bar{\tau}_{1}

|\Omega|=\omega_{n}

Proof.

Recall the parameters
\bar{\varepsilon},\bar{\delta}
, and
\bar{\eta}
fixed in (

) and (

Thanks to (

||\Omega|-\omega_{n}|\leq\omega_{n}\bar{\varepsilon}/2

Moreover, Theorem
7.14
in particular implies that
\Omega
is star-shaped and thus we have either
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
(in the case that
|\Omega|\geq\omega_{n}

\Omega^{\prime}\supset\Omega

So, applying (

(7.21)

|{h}_{f}(\Omega)-{h}_{f}(\Omega^{\prime})|\leq\bar{C}_{n}\,\Big(|\Omega\Delta\Omega^{\prime}|+\int|u_{\Omega}^{f}-u_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{f}|\Big)\leq\bar{C}_{n}\begin{cases}|\Omega|-\omega_{n}+\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)&\text{ if }|\Omega|\geq\omega_{n}\\
\omega_{n}-|\Omega|+\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})&\text{ if }|\Omega|\leq\omega_{n}.\end{cases}

Here
\bar{C}_{n}
is the dimensional constant from (
6.10
).

We take
\Omega^{\prime}
as a competitor in the minimization problem (
6.5
), and use (
7.21
) together with the scaling of the torsional rigidity and volume to show that
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega),
with strict inequality unless

.

Case 1:
Assume
|\Omega|\geq\omega_{n}
, so that
r\leq 1
.
Since
\Omega
is minimizer, we use (
7.21
) to find

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(|\Omega|-\omega_{n}\right)+\tau\left({h}_{f}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)-{h}_{f}(\Omega)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(|\Omega|-\omega_{n}\right)+\bar{C}_{n}\tau\left(|\Omega|-\omega_{n}+\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)(\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega))-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left(|\Omega|-\omega_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left(r^{n+2}-1\right)\operatorname{tor}(\Omega)+\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left(\frac{r^{n}-1}{r^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left(1-r^{n+2}\right)|\operatorname{tor}(\Omega)|-\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left(\frac{1-r^{n}}{r^{n}}\right)\,.

in this case, Lemma
2.2
tells us that
\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)\geq\mathrm{tor}(B)
, i.e.
|\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)|\leq|\mathrm{tor}(B)|.
Since we have chosen
\tau
such that
\bar{C}_{n}\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}
, the chain of inequalities above that if

(i.e. if

) then

(7.22)

\frac{1}{4|\mathrm{tor}(B)|\,\eta}\leq\frac{r^{n}(1-r^{n+2})}{1-r^{n}}

Keeping in mind that

, for the right-hand side we have

\displaystyle\frac{r^{n}\left(1-r^{n+2}\right)}{1-r^{n}}=\frac{r^{n}(1-r)\left(r^{n+1}+\ldots+r+1\right)}{(1-r)\left(r^{n-1}+\ldots+r+1\right)}=\frac{r^{n}\left(r^{n+1}+\ldots r+1\right)}{\left(r^{n-1}+\ldots+r+1\right)}

\displaystyle=r^{n}\left(1+\frac{r^{n+1}+r^{n}}{r^{n-1}+\ldots+1}\right)\leq 3r^{n}\leq 3.

Since we have chosen

.

Case 2:

Now assume
|\Omega|\leq\omega_{n}
, so that
r\geq 1.
Since
\Omega
is minimizer, we use (
7.21
) to find

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\eta\left(|\Omega|-\omega_{n}\right)+\tau({h}_{f}(\Omega^{\prime})-{h}_{f}(\Omega))

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{tor}(\Omega^{\prime})-{\mathrm{tor}}(\Omega)+\eta\left(\omega_{n}-|\Omega|\right)+\bar{C}_{n}\tau\left(\left(\omega_{n}-|\Omega|\right)+{\mathrm{tor}}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tor}({\Omega^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)(\operatorname{tor}({\Omega}^{\prime})-\operatorname{tor}(\Omega))+\left(\eta+\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left\{\omega_{n}-|\Omega|\right\}

\displaystyle={\left(1-\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left(r^{n+2}-1\right)}\operatorname{tor}(\Omega)+\left(\eta+\bar{C}_{n}\tau\right)\left(\frac{r^{n}-1}{r^{n}}\right)\omega_{n}\,.

Now, by Lemma

|\Omega|\geq\omega_{n}/2
and thus, since

satisfies (

\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)-\eta\omega_{n}/2\leq\mathrm{tor}(\Omega)+{V}_{\eta}(|\Omega|)\leq\mathrm{tor}(B)+\bar{\delta}

Recall that we have also chosen

1\leq\left(\frac{r^{n}({r^{n+2}-1})}{r^{n}-1}\right)\leq\frac{4\eta\omega_{n}}{|\mathrm{tor}(B)|-2\bar{\delta}}\,.

Since we have chosen

Since we only assume quantitative bounds on the
L^{\infty}
norm of the right hand side

, we will not be able to obtain
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity of the boundary with estimates.
However, we will now upgrade the boundary regularity to
C^{1,\alpha}
for any
0<\alpha<1
, with estimates.

Theorem 7.16
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
0<\alpha<1
. There exists a constant
\bar{\tau}_{2}\in(0,\bar{\tau}_{0}]
depending on
n
and
\epsilon
and a universal constant
C>0
such that for
\tau\in(0,\bar{\tau}_{2}]
, after a translation by
-x_{\Omega}
,
\partial\Omega
can be parametrized by a
C^{1,\alpha}
function
\phi
over
\partial B
with

(7.24)

\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial B)}\leq C\epsilon.

Proof.

We utilize the recent technique (see
[
30
,
60
,
61
,
7
]
) of considering the quotient
v=u_{\Omega}^{f}/w_{\Omega}
which is a solution to a highly degenerate elliptic equation. Specifically, pointwise inside
\Omega
the quotient
v
satisfies

\text{div}\left(w_{\Omega}^{2}\nabla v\right)=-w_{\Omega}f+u_{\Omega}^{f}.

Since both
w_{\Omega}
and
u_{\Omega}^{f}
have Lipschitz growth away from the boundary,

|w_{\Omega}^{2}(x)\nabla v(x)|\leq C\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)

which gives a zero Neumann condition on
\partial\Omega
.
Notice that
-w_{\Omega}f+u_{\Omega}^{f}=\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)g
with
g\in L^{\infty}
.
Using locally that
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\gamma}
, we flatten the boundary to obtain under the transformation the equation

\begin{cases}&\int_{B^{+}_{r}(x)}x_{n}^{2}\langle A(x)\nabla\tilde{v},\nabla\phi\rangle=-\int_{B^{+}_{r}(x)}x_{n}g_{1}(x)\phi(x),\\
&\lim_{x_{n}\to 0}x_{n}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{v}(x^{\prime},x_{n})|=0.\end{cases}

The matrix
A
is symmetric, uniformly elliptic and

\|A(x)-I\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}\leq\epsilon.

Taking the primitive from
x_{n}=0
and factoring out an
x_{n}^{2}
, we have that
\partial_{x_{n}}(x_{n}^{2}g_{2}(x^{\prime},x_{n}))=x_{n}g_{1}(x^{\prime},x_{n})

with
g_{2}\in L^{\infty}
. Letting
G=(0,\ldots,0,x_{n}^{2}g_{2}(x))
we have

\begin{cases}&\int_{B^{+}_{r}(x)}x_{n}^{2}\langle A(x)\nabla\tilde{v},\nabla\phi\rangle=\int_{B^{+}_{r}(x)}x_{n}^{2}g_{2}(x)\phi_{n}(x),\\
&\lim_{x_{n}\to 0}x_{n}^{2}|\nabla\tilde{v}(x^{\prime},x_{n})|=0.\end{cases}

We may now apply a lower-order Schauder estimate. For instance, Theorem 1.3 (i) in
[
58
]
applies. Since
A
is uniformly
\gamma
-Hölder continuous, the estimates from Theorem 1.3(i) in
[
58
]
are uniform. Then
\tilde{v}\in C^{0,\alpha}
for any
\alpha<1
, and so
u_{\Omega}^{f}/w_{\Omega}=v\in C^{0,\alpha}
. Then the argument in Theorem
7.14
as before implies
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\alpha}
for any
\alpha<1
. Using interpolation of Hölder spaces, we obtain (
7.24
).
∎

To apply Lemma

we will need

Appendix A
Explanation of the proof of Lemma

We provide here the explanation of the proof of Lemma

. The estimates and computations are nearly all contained in
[
57
]
where the author considers the more difficult situation of the first eigenvalue of a domain. The other difference is that we assume
\phi\in C^{2,\alpha}(\partial B)
with
\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}(\partial B)\leq\delta
with
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
, while in
[
57
]
the author only assumes that
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}
rather than
\phi\in C^{2,\alpha}
. We make the stronger assumption in order to avoid using a lower-order second variation term to prove continuity (in domain variations) of the second derivative as in
[
57
]
. Following the notation in
[
57
]
we extend
\phi
as a function on
\partial B
to all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by letting
\phi(x):=\theta(x)\phi(x/|x|)
with
\theta\equiv 1
near
\partial B
and such that
\phi
is compactly supported and which is constant (close to
\partial B
) in directions normal to the ball. We define
\xi_{\phi}:=\phi(x)x
and define

\Omega_{t}:=(\text{Id}+t\xi_{\phi})(B)\,.

We let
e(t):=\mathrm{tor}(\Omega_{t})
.
For a
C^{3}
domain
\Omega
we have from Theorem 2.1, Remark 2.2, and equation
(23)
all in
[
23
]
that

(A.1)

\displaystyle e^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla v_{t}|^{2}+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}(\partial_{\nu_{t}}w_{\Omega_{t}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{t}(\partial_{\nu_{t}}w_{\Omega_{t}})^{2})(\xi_{\phi}\cdot\nu_{t})^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}(\partial_{\nu_{t}}{w_{\Omega_{t}}})^{2}[\textbf{B}_{t}((\xi_{\phi})_{\tau_{t}},(\xi_{\phi})_{\tau_{t},})-2\nabla_{\tau_{t}}(\xi_{\phi}\cdot\nu_{t})(\xi_{\phi})_{\tau_{t}}].

In the above identity,
\nu_{t}
is the outward unit normal,
w_{\Omega_{t}}
is the torsion function,
\textbf{B}_{t}
is the second fundamental form,

is the mean curvature,
\nabla_{\tau}
is the tangential gradient, and

are the tangential components. Finally,

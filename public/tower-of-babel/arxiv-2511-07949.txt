Stabilization of Time-Varying Perturbed Quantum Systems via Reduced Filters
Weichao Liang, Daoyi Dong

Abstract
In practical applications, quantum systems are inevitably subject to significant uncertainties, including unknown initial states, imprecise physical parameters, and unmodeled environmental noise, all of which pose major challenges to robust quantum feedback control. This paper proposes a feedback stabilization strategy based on a reduced quantum filter that achieves robustness against time-varying Hamiltonian perturbations and additional dissipative effects, without requiring prior knowledge of the initial state or exact system parameters. The proposed filter estimates only 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ) \mathcal{O}(N) real variables corresponding to the diagonal elements of the system density matrix in a quantum non-demolition basis in contrast to the 𝒪 ⁡ ( N 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{2}) variables required by a full stochastic master equation, where N N is the Hilbert space dimension. This dimensionality reduction substantially simplifies real-time computation and feedback implementation while preserving both convergence and robustness guarantees. Rigorous analysis further establishes global exponential stability of the target subspace. The results provide a scalable framework for robust and efficient measurement-based feedback control applicable to high-dimensional perturbed open quantum systems.

Beyond rigorous proofs, we provide intuitive insights into the reduced filter’s construction and the rationale behind the proposed conditions, ensuring robust stabilization.

Stochastic Evolution of States in QND basis

We first analyze the uncontrolled dynamics (
u_{t}\equiv 0
) to identify the state components that govern large-time behavior. In the QND setting, the diagonal weights
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})

Under

Case 1.

There exists a non-empty subset

Case 2.

For generic

If the stronger structural assumption below holds, then
Case 2
occurs with

.

A-qsr:

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{0}(t)=\mathrm{diag}[\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{0}(t),\dots,\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{d}(t)]\text{ with }\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{j}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}_{*}(\mathcal{H}_{j}));

\displaystyle C_{k}(t)=\mathrm{diag}[C_{k,0}(t),\dots,C_{k,d}(t)]\text{ with }C_{k,j}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{j})).

We impose the following identifiability assumption on the measurement operators:

A3:

For all

, there exists at least one

\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,i}\}\neq\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,j}\}

Define

\mathfrak{E}_{l}:=\min_{i\neq j}\left[\sum^{m}_{k=1}\underline{\theta}_{k}(\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,i}\}-\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,j}\})^{2}\right].

Assumption

ensures that different eigenstates of the measurement operators can be distinguished through their measurement records. The following theorem establishes that, in the absence of control input, the stochastic dynamics induced by continuous measurements drive the quantum state exponentially toward one of the invariant subspaces.

Theorem 3.1

(Exponential quantum state reduction)
.

Assume that
u_{t}\equiv 0
, and
A1
,
A3
and
A-qsr

\rho(0)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
, the system (
1
) converges towards
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}):=\bigcup^{d}_{j=0}\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{j})
in mean and almost surely with Lyapunov exponent less than or equal to
-\mathfrak{E}_{l}/2
.
Moreover, the probability of convergence to
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{j})
is
\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{j}\rho(0))
for
j\in\{0,\dots,d\}
.

Proof.

Define
I:=\{k\mid\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{j}\rho_{0})=0\}
and
\mathcal{S}_{I}:=\{\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})|\,\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{j}\rho)=0\mbox{ iff }j\in I\}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{S}_{I}
is almost surely invariant under the dynamics of system (
1
).
Consider the following candidate Lyapunov function, related to the Bhattacharyya distance, also known as classical fidelity
[
7
, Chapter 2.5]
,

\displaystyle V(\rho)=\sum_{i\neq j}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{i}\rho)\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{j}\rho)}\geq 0,

(3)

with
V(\rho)=0
if and only if
\rho\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H})
. Due to the invariance of
\mathcal{S}_{I}
,

is twice continuously differentiable when restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{I}
. For all
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{I}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}V(\rho)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{j})}\left[\sum_{k}\theta_{k}(t)(\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,i}\}-\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,j}\})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{E}_{l}V(\rho)/2,

(4)

where the strict positivity of
\mathfrak{E}_{l}
follows from
A3
. Detailed derivations are provided in Appendix
B

Applying similar stochastic Lyapunov arguments as in
[
20

and
[

converges exponentially to

, both in expectation and almost surely, with Lyapunov exponent at most

. Moreover, the probability of convergence to
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{j})
is
\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{j}\rho(0))
.
\square

In the QND regime, the measurement operators

commute with the system Hamiltonian

, and also perturbations

4
Stabilization by the reduced filter

In this section, we explore the robustness of the state feedback stabilization strategy proposed in
[
24
]
for system (
1
), subject to perturbations
\mathcal{P}(\rho)
and uncertainties in the initial state
\rho(0)
, the free Hamiltonian
H_{0}(t)
, and the model parameters
\gamma_{k}(t),\eta_{k}(t)
.
Throughout, we assume that the measurement operators
L_{k}
are known, while the parameters
\gamma_{k}(t)>0
may not be precisely identified. This assumption can be interpreted as allowing proportional time-varying perturbations in
L_{k}
. Importantly, this technical assumption plays a crucial role in deriving the main result, as it emphasizes that accurate knowledge of the measurement operators is essential for ensuring robust stability.

Due to relation (
2
), we rewrite system (
1
) as follows:

\displaystyle d\rho(t)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{u}_{\gamma}(t,\rho(t))dt+\mathcal{P}(t,\rho(t))dt+\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\mathcal{G}_{L_{k}}(\rho(t))

\displaystyle\times\left(dY_{k}(t)-\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\mathrm{Tr}((L_{k}^{*}+L_{k})\rho(t))dt\right),

(5)

with
\rho(0)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
.
From a practical perspective, the initial state, the free Hamiltonian, the model parameters and the perturbation cannot be precisely known. Following the approach in
[
25
]
, we construct the following SME to estimate the state
\rho(t)
replicating the structure of the actual system (
1
),

\displaystyle d\hat{\rho}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{u}_{\hat{\gamma}}(t,\hat{\rho}(t))dt+\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\mathcal{G}_{L_{k}}(\hat{\rho}(t))\left(dY_{k}(t)-\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\mathrm{Tr}((L_{k}^{*}+L_{k})\hat{\rho}(t))dt\right),

(6)

where
\hat{\rho}(0)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
,
\hat{H}_{0}=\mathrm{diag}[\hat{\mathsf{H}}_{0},\dots,\hat{\mathsf{H}}_{d}]
, and
\hat{\theta}_{k}=\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{\gamma}_{k}
with
\hat{\gamma}_{k}\in[\underline{\gamma}_{k},\bar{\gamma}_{k}]
,
\hat{\eta}_{k}\in[\underline{\eta}_{k},\bar{\eta}_{k}]
. Unlike the true system, which may involve time-varying

and
\theta_{k}(t)
, the estimator uses fixed values selected from prior information, reducing online computational complexity. The feedback law is then implemented as

, requiring real-time numerical integration of (

4.1
Motivation for a reduced filter

As the system dimension increases, integrating the full filter (
6
) in real time becomes computationally demanding. This motivates the design of controllers that exploit only partial information rather than full state estimation.

Before introducing our reduced filter for feedback control, we recall two key mechanisms established in
[
22
,
24
,
25
]
to interpret the full filter:

1.

The term
u_{t}[H_{1},\hat{\rho}]
ensures that
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
is the only invariant subset for the estimator. Moreover, under
A2
, if
\hat{\rho}(t)
approaches an undesired invariant subset such as
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{j})
with
j\in E
, this term forces
\hat{\rho}(t)
away from such undesired invariant subsets.

2.

The diffusive term
\mathcal{G}_{L_{k}}(\hat{\rho})
accounts for the measurement back-action on the system. It ensures that
\hat{\rho}(t)
, starting from any initial state outside the invariant subsets, is driven toward the target invariant subset.

In view of the control objective: stabilizing system (
1
) toward
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
equivalently
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{0})\to 1
, and the large-time behavior in Theorem
3.1
, which shows that the off-diagonal blocks
\Pi_{i}\rho(t)\Pi_{j}
vanish for
i\neq j
. It is natural to focus only on the diagonal entries of
\rho(t)
in the QND basis. We therefore construct an
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
-dimensional estimator that tracks these diagonal components. By capturing precisely the information relevant for stabilization, this reduced estimator provides an effective input to the feedback controller while avoiding the complexity of full state estimation via
\hat{\rho}(t)
.

Since the measurement operators
(L_{k})_{k\in[m]}
are diagonal in the QND basis, their contribution to the measurement back-action can be simplified to:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{G}_{L_{k}}(\rho)\Pi_{n})

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{n})\big(\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}-\textstyle\sum^{d}_{j=0}\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,j}\}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{j})\big).

(7)

Motivated by this structure, and by the analysis in Section
3
, we introduce a reduced-order estimator whose state
\hat{q}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
tracks the diagonal components
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{n})
based on the measurement output
\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}
. Inspired by
[
12
,
24
]
, we propose the following autonomous Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE):

\displaystyle d\hat{q}_{n}(t)=

\displaystyle u_{t}\sum^{d}_{j=0}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}(t)dt+2\hat{q}_{n}(t)\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q}(t))\left(dY_{k}(t)-2\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Lambda_{k}(\hat{q}(t))dt\right),

(8)

where

\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q}):=\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}-\Lambda_{k}(\hat{q}),\text{ with }\Lambda_{k}(\hat{q}):=\textstyle\sum^{d}_{j=0}\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,j}\}\hat{q}_{j},

and
\hat{q}_{n}=e_{n}^{*}\hat{q}
, and
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{d}\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
u_{t}:=u(\hat{q}(t))
represents the feedback controller.

Moreover, we assume that the matrix
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{(d+1)\times(d+1)}
satisfies the following condition:

C1:

\sum^{d}_{i=0}\Gamma_{i,j}=0
for all
j\in\{0,\dots,d\}
,
\Gamma_{j,j}<0
for all
j\in\{0,\dots,d\}
, and
\Gamma_{i,j}\geq 0
for all
i\neq j\in\{0,\dots,d\}
.

We observe that the second and third terms on the right-hand side of (
8
) effectively reproduce the measurement back-action, as they structurally resemble the second summand in (
5
) when considering the diagonal elements in (
7
). The first term,

, under Condition
C1
, serves to replicate the effect of
u_{t}[H_{1},\hat{\rho}]
.
Regarding

, the constraint
\sum^{d}_{i=0}\Gamma_{i,j}=0
ensures that
\sum_{n}\hat{q}_{n}=1
, while the remaining conditions guarantee the positivity of

, ensuring

\hat{q}\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}:=\left\{\hat{q}\in[0,1]^{d+1}\middle|\textstyle\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}=1\right\}.

Thus,
\hat{q}
is a valid candidate probability vector approximating the diagonal of
\hat{\rho}
in the QND basis.

4.2
Stochastic model for the reduced filter

Based on (
2
), we rewrite (
8
) as the following Wiener process-driven SDE for
n\in\{0,\dots,d\}
,

\displaystyle d\hat{q}_{n}(t)=

\displaystyle u_{t}\sum^{d}_{j=0}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}(t)dt+2\hat{q}_{n}(t)\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q}(t))\big(dW_{k}(t)+\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))dt\big),

(9)

where

\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q}):=\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\mathrm{Tr}((L_{k}+L^{*}_{k})\rho)-2\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Lambda_{k}(\hat{q}),

and
\rho(t)
is the solution to SME (
1
) with
u_{t}:=u(\hat{q}(t))
as the feedback controller, which satisfies the following assumption:

A4:

u\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1},\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
u(e_{0})=0
and
u(e_{n})>0
for all
n>0
, where
\{e_{n}\}^{d}_{n=0}
is the orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

Define

\mathcal{O}_{d+1}:=\{\hat{q}\in(0,1)^{d+1}\mid\textstyle\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}=1\}.

Before analyzing the stability properties of the system, it is essential to ensure that the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
) is well-posed and that the reduced state remains within the probability simplex
\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
for all time. The next theorem provides these well-posedness and invariance properties.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
A4
and
C1
hold. Then, for all
(\rho(0),\hat{q}(0))\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
, the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
) has a unique global solution
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
for all
t\geq 0
almost surely.

Proof.

First, suppose that
u_{t}
is a real, bounded positive process adapted to
\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}
. Existence, uniqueness, and invariance of the solution
\rho(t)
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
for (
1
) follow directly from
[
29
, Section 3]
or
[
3
, Chapter 5]
.

Denote the explosion time from the initial state
\hat{q}(0)
by
\tau_{e}(\hat{q}(0),\omega):\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\Omega\rightarrow[0,\infty]
. Since the coefficients of (
9
) are locally Lipschitz, by
[
28
, Theorem 5.2.8]
, the SDE (
9
) admits a unique local strong solution on
[0,\tau_{e})
almost surely.

Summing (
9
) over
n\in\{0,\dots,d\}
yields

\displaystyle d\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}(t)=

\displaystyle u_{t}\sum^{d}_{n=0}\sum^{d}_{j=0}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}(t)dt

\displaystyle+2\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Lambda_{k}(\hat{q}(t))\left(1-\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}(t)\right)\big(dW_{k}(t)+\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))dt\big).

Since the coefficients of the above equation are locally Lipschitz continuous, for any given initial state
\hat{q}(0)\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
, there is a unique local solution on
t\in[0,\tau_{e})
. According to the condition
C1
,
\sum^{d}_{n=0}\Gamma_{n,j}=0
. It follows
\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}(t)=1
when
\hat{q}(0)\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
for all
t\in[0,\tau_{e})
almost surely. It implies that, for all
\hat{q}(0)\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
,
\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}(t)=1
till the explosion time.

For all
\hat{q}(0)\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
, choose
k_{0}\geq 0
sufficiently large so that
\hat{q}_{n}(0)>1/k_{0}
for all
n\in\{0,\dots,d\}
. For each integer
k\geq k_{0}
, define two stopping times

\displaystyle\tau_{k}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{t\in[0,\tau_{e})|\,\hat{q}_{n}(t)\leq 1/k\text{ for some }n\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{t\in[0,\tau_{e})|\,\hat{q}_{n}(t)\notin(1/k,k)\text{ for some }n\}.

Clearly,
\sigma_{k}\leq\tau_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}\to\tau_{e}
as
k\to\infty
. Because
\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}(t)=1
holds almost surely for all
t<\tau_{e}
, it follows that
\tau_{k}=\sigma_{k}
almost surely

Consider the function

V(\hat{q})=\textstyle\sum^{d}_{n=0}\log\hat{q}_{n}^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{d+1},\mathbb{R}_{+}),

whose infinitesimal generator is given by

\mathscr{L}V(\hat{\rho})=-u_{t}\mathcal{A}(\hat{q})+\mathcal{B}(t,\rho,\hat{q}),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\hat{q}):=\sum^{d}_{n=0}\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{n}}\sum^{d}_{j=0}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(t,\rho,\hat{q}):=2\sum_{n,k}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q})\left[\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q})-\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})\right].

Due to compactness of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}
, there exists a finite
c_{1}>0
such that
\mathcal{B}(t,\rho,\hat{q})\leq c_{1}
for all
(t,\rho,\hat{q})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}
. Define

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k}_{d+1}:=\{\hat{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}|\hat{q}_{n}

\displaystyle\geq 1/k\text{ for }n\in\{0,\dots,d\},\text{ s.t. }\textstyle\sum^{d}_{n=0}\hat{q}_{n}=1\}\subset\mathcal{O}_{d+1}.

For all
\hat{q}\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k}_{d+1}
, due to the compactness and continuity, there exists
c_{2}>0
such that
-\mathcal{A}(\hat{q})\leq c_{2}V(\hat{q})
and
c_{1}\leq c_{2}V(\hat{q})
.
Since
u_{t}
is positive and bounded, it follows that

\mathscr{L}V(\hat{q})\leq c_{3}V(\hat{q}),\quad\forall\hat{q}\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k}_{d+1},

for some constant
c_{3}>0
.

For
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}\setminus\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k}_{d+1}
, at least one
\hat{q}_{i}
approaches zero. We rewrite

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\hat{q})=\sum^{d}_{n=0}\Gamma_{n,n}+\sum^{d}_{n=0}\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{n}}\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j},

with
\Gamma_{n,n}<0
and
\Gamma_{n,j}\geq 0
for
n\neq j
.

Suppose
\hat{q}_{n}
approaches zero.
There are three possible cases:

1.

If
\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}
converges to a finite positive constant, then
\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{n}}\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}
diverges to infinity.

2.

If
\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}
converges to zero slower than
\hat{q}_{n}
, then
\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{n}}\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}
diverges to infinity.

3.

If
\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}
converges to zero at the same or faster rate than
\hat{q}_{n}
, then
\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{n}}\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}
converges to a finite constant.

Hence,
-\mathcal{A}(\hat{q})
either diverges negatively or remains bounded by a finite positive constant. Note that, in these cases,
V(\hat{\rho})
approaches infinity. Thus, there exists a finite constant
c_{4}>0
such that
\mathscr{L}V(\hat{q})\leq c_{3}V(\hat{q})
for all
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}\setminus\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k}_{d+1}
. Therefore, there exists
c>0
such that

\mathscr{L}V(\hat{q})\leq cV(\hat{q}),\quad\forall\hat{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}.

Next, we show
\tau_{e}=\infty
almost surely by contradiction inspired by analogous result established in
[
28
, Lemma 4.3.2]
. Assume
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{e}<\infty)>0
, and then there is
T>0
sufficient large such that
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{e}\leq T)>0
. Given that
\mathscr{L}V(\hat{q})\leq cV(\hat{q})
for all
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
, define
f(\hat{q},t)=e^{-ct}V(\hat{q})
, whose infinitesimal generator is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}f(\hat{q},t)=e^{-ct}(-cV(\hat{q})+\mathscr{L}V(\hat{q}))\leq 0,

for all
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
. Since
\hat{q}(0)\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k_{0}}_{d+1}\subset\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k}_{d+1}
, we have
\hat{q}(T\wedge\tau_{k})\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{k}_{d+1}\subset\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
. By Itô formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(f(\hat{q}(T\wedge\tau_{k}),T\wedge\tau_{k})\big)=V(\hat{q}(0))+\mathbb{E}\left(\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}\mathscr{L}f(\hat{q}(s),s)ds\right)\leq V(\hat{q}(0)).

Conditioned on the event
\{\tau_{e}\leq T\}
, we deduce

\displaystyle f(\hat{q}(T\wedge\tau_{k}),T\wedge\tau_{k})=f(\hat{q}(\tau_{k}),\tau_{k})=e^{-c(T\wedge\tau_{k})}V(\hat{q}(\tau_{k}))\geq e^{-cT}V(\hat{q}(\tau_{k}))\geq e^{-cT}\log k.

It implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(e^{-cT}\log k\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{e}\leq T\}}\big)\leq\mathbb{E}\big(f(\hat{q}(T\wedge\tau_{k}),T\wedge\tau_{k})\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{e}\leq T\}}\big)\leq\mathbb{E}\big(f(\hat{q}(T\wedge\tau_{k}),T\wedge\tau_{k})\big)\leq V(\hat{q}(0)).

Consequently,

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{e}\leq T)\leq e^{cT}\frac{V(\hat{q}(0))}{\log k}.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we have
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{e}\leq T)=0
, which contradicts the assumption.
Thus, we conclude that
\tau_{e}=\infty
almost surely in the case where
u_{t}
is a real, bounded, and positive process adapted to
\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}
.

Now, consider the case where
u_{t}=u(\hat{q})
with
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1},\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Due to the compactness of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}
, we can find an open set
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}\subset\mathcal{E}
. Let
\mathcal{X}(\rho,\hat{q}):\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth function with compact support such that
\mathcal{X}(\rho,\hat{q})=1
for
(\rho,\hat{q})\in\mathcal{E}
. Additionally, define
U\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
U(\hat{q})=u(\hat{q})
for all
\hat{q}\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}
. Then, the following coupled equations

\displaystyle d\varrho(t)=\mathcal{X}(\varrho(t),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))\left[\mathcal{L}^{U}_{\gamma}(\varrho(t))dt+\mathcal{P}(\rho(t))dt\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\mathcal{G}_{L_{k}}(\rho(t))dW_{k}(t)\right],

\displaystyle d\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{n}(t)=\mathcal{X}(\varrho(t),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))\Bigg[U(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))\sum^{d}_{j=0}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{j}(t)dt+2\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{n}(t)\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{n}(t))

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times\big(dW_{k}(t)+\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\varrho(t),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))dt\big)\Bigg],

have global Lipschitz coefficients, ensuring a unique strong solution with almost surely continuous adapted paths
[
34
]
. Since
\mathcal{X}
has compact support,
(\varrho(t),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))
is bounded, making
U(\hat{q}(t))
an almost surely continuous, real bounded adapted process. Now, consider the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
) with
u_{t}=U(\hat{q}(t))
and
(\rho(0),\hat{q}(0))=(\varrho(0),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(0))\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
. As both solutions
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
and
(\varrho(t),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))
have a unique solution, the solutions must coincide up to the first exit time from
\mathcal{E}
. Moreover,
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
remains in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
for all
t\geq 0
almost surely, and thus
(\varrho(t),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))
will never exit from
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
. Hence,
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))=(\varrho(t),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}(t))
for all
t\geq 0
almost surely. The proof is complete.

\square

Feedback stabilization to target subspaces

We adapt methods from
[

25
]
to ensure robust GES of

for the system (

H_{0}(t)
and the model parameters
\{\gamma_{k}(t)\}^{m}_{k=1}
and
\{\eta_{k}(t)\}^{m}_{k=1}
, and remains effective in the presence of perturbations
\mathcal{P}(\rho)
satisfying

.

We systematically analyze the behavior of the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
). The key aspects of our analysis include:

1.

Instability of undesired sets.
Based on the structure of perturbations discussed in Section
3
, we identify
\mathrm{card}(E)
undesired invariant subsets
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times e_{0}
. Under assumptions
A2
and

[

2.

Recurrence.
Utilizing the support theorem
[
37
]
, we demonstrate that the trajectory
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
of the coupled system almost surely enters any neighborhood of the target subset
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}
in finite time (Proposition
4.8
).

3.

Exponential stability.
Inspired by Theorem
3.1
, we use local Lyapunov methods to prove stability in probability. Combined with the recurrence property and the strong Markov property of
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
, local stability in probability implies almost sure asymptotic stability of the subset
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}
(
[
20

Define, for all
k\in[m]
,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}:=\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,0}\}-\textstyle\min_{n\in[d]}\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\},

\displaystyle\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}:=\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,0}\}-\textstyle\max_{n\in[d]}\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}.

Impose the following assumption on measurement operators:

A5:

For each
k\in[m]
,
\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}\leq 0
or
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}\geq 0
.

This assumption is essential for establishing the instability of undesired invariant subsets, the recurrence property, and for estimating the Lyapunov exponent. Note that under
A3
, the case where
\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}=0
or
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}=0
for all

is excluded.

4.3.1
Instability of undesired invariant subsets

Here, we provide Lyapunov-type sufficient conditions ensuring the instability of an invariant subspace of the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
), assuming the perturbation structure satisfies
Case 2
. Additionally, we estimate the average escape time of trajectories from a neighborhood of such invariant subspaces.

The control Hamiltonian
H_{1}
determines how the feedback input
u_{t}
can induce transitions between invariant subspaces. If
H_{1}
fails to couple certain eigenspaces, the associated subspace
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
may remain invariant regardless of the applied control. To formalize this requirement, we adopt a Hautus-type controllability condition
[
36
, Chapter 3.3]
ensuring that
H_{1}
connects all relevant eigenspaces. Let
\mathscr{E}_{\lambda}(X)
be the eigenspace of
X
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
.

A6:

For each
j\in[d]
, and for all eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\Pi_{j}H_{1}\Pi_{j}
,

\textstyle\bigcap_{i\neq j}\mathrm{Ker}\{\Pi_{i}H_{1}\Pi_{j}\}\cap\mathscr{E}_{\lambda}(\Pi_{j}H_{1}\Pi_{j})=\{0\}.

The following lemma provides a necessary and sufficient condition ensuring that
H_{1}
acts non-trivially on each
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under Assumption
A1
, the necessary and sufficient condition of
[H_{1},\rho]\neq 0
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
and all
n\in[d]
is Assumption
A6
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in[d]
and let
\rho\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
, then
\rho=\Pi_{n}\rho=\rho\Pi_{n}=\Pi_{n}\rho\Pi_{n}
.
[H_{1},\rho]=0
is equivalent to
\Pi_{k}[H_{1},\rho]\Pi_{k}=\Pi_{i}[H_{1},\rho]\Pi_{j}=0
for all
k
and
i\neq j
. We first focus on the diagonal part,

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}[H_{1},\rho]\Pi_{k}=[\Pi_{n}H_{1}\Pi_{n},\rho]=0,\quad\forall k\in\{0,\dots,d\}.

Then,
\rho
admits the decomposition
\rho=\sum_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}\rho P_{\lambda}
, where
P_{\lambda}
is the eigenprojection of
\Pi_{n}H_{1}\Pi_{n}
corresponds to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. Next, we have

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}[H_{1},\rho]\Pi_{j}=0,~\forall i\neq j\Leftrightarrow\Pi_{i}H_{1}\Pi_{n}\rho=0,~\forall i\neq n.

Thus, for all
\rho\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
,
[H_{1},\rho]\neq 0
if and only if
\bigcap_{i\neq n}\mathrm{Ker}\{\Pi_{i}H_{1}\Pi_{n}\}\cap\mathscr{E}_{\lambda}(\Pi_{n}H_{1}\Pi_{n})=\{0\}
, for all
\lambda
. The lemma then follows for arbitrary
n\in[d]
.

\square

The following corollary provides a simple algebraic condition ensuring that
A6
holds.

Corollary 4.3
.

For all
i\neq n\in\{0,\dots,d\}
, the block matrix of
\Pi_{i}H_{1}\Pi_{n}
from
\mathcal{H}_{n}
to
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is injective, then
A6
is satisfied.

Next, to quantify the effect of time-varying measurement perturbations, we introduce the normalized parameter

\chi_{k}(t):=(\theta_{k}(t)/\hat{\theta}_{k})^{1/2}\in[\underline{\chi}_{k},\bar{\chi}_{k}]

where
\underline{\chi}_{k}=(\underline{\theta}_{k}/\hat{\theta}_{k})^{1/2}
and
\bar{\chi}_{k}=(\bar{\theta}_{k}/\hat{\theta}_{k})^{1/2}
for all
k\in[m]
, and impose the following condition:

C2:

For all
k\in[m]
,

2\bar{\chi}_{k}-1<\min_{n\in[d]}\left\{\frac{\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,0}\}}{\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}}\middle|\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}\neq 0\right\}\text{ if }\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}\leq 0,

while

2\underline{\chi}_{k}-1>\max_{n\in[d]}\left\{\frac{\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,0}\}}{\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}}\middle|\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}\neq 0\right\}\text{ if }\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}\geq 0.

Define

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t):=

\displaystyle\big(\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}-\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,0}\}\big)\big((2\chi_{k}(t)-1)\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,n}\}-\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,0}\}\big).

The next lemma ensures that, under the above conditions, the weighted sum of these terms remains strictly positive, which is crucial for establishing instability.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
A3
,
A5
and
C2
are satisfied, then for all
\chi_{k}(t)\in[\underline{\chi}_{k},\bar{\chi}_{k}]
,
\sum^{m}_{k=1}\hat{\theta}_{k}\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t)>0.

Proof.

Fix
n\in[d]
.

•

If
\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}\leq 0
and
\Re\{l_{k,n}\}\neq 0
, then by
C2
,

2\chi_{k}(t)-1\leq 2\bar{\chi}_{k}-1<\Re\{l_{k,0}\}/\Re\{l_{k,n}\},

which together with
\Re\{l_{k,n}\}\geq\Re\{l_{k,0}\}
implies
\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t)\geq 0
, with equality if and only if
\Re\{l_{k,n}\}=\Re\{l_{k,0}\}
.

•

If
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}\geq 0
and
\Re\{l_{k,n}\}\neq 0
, a symmetric argument shows
\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t)\geq 0
, again with equality if and only if
\Re\{l_{k,n}\}=\Re\{l_{k,0}\}
.

•

If
\Re\{l_{k,n}\}=0
, then

\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t)=\Re\{l_{k,0}\}^{2}\geq 0,

with equality if and only if
\Re\{l_{k,0}\}=0
.

Therefore, in all cases
\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t)\geq 0
, and under assumptions
A3
and
A5
, strict positivity of the weighted sum follows:

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\hat{\theta}_{k}\,\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t)>0.

\square

Define

\displaystyle d_{0}(\hat{q}):=\|\hat{q}-e_{0}\|,

\displaystyle B_{r}(e_{0}):=\{\hat{q}\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}|\,d_{0}(\hat{q})<r\}.

The following lemma establishes that each non-target invariant subspace is locally unstable, i.e., trajectories initialized sufficiently close to such subspaces will almost surely leave their neighborhood in finite time.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose

A3
,
A4
,
A5
and
C1
and
C2
hold. In addition, assume there exists a non-empty subset
E\subset[d]
such that
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
for all
n\in E
are invariant for the perturbed system (
1
) when
u_{t}\equiv 0
. Then, almost all
\tilde{H}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}_{*}(\mathcal{H}))
and
C_{k}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, there exists
\lambda>0
such that for all
(\rho(0),\hat{q}(0))\in\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times B_{\lambda}(e_{0})\cap\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
with
n\in E
, the trajectory
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
of the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
) exits from
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times B_{\lambda}(e_{0})
in finite time almost surely.

Proof.

Consider the function
\mathsf{V}_{n}(\hat{q})=\log\hat{q}_{n}^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{d+1},\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Its infinitesimal generator is given by

\mathscr{L}\mathsf{V}_{n}(\hat{q})=-u(\hat{q})\mathsf{A}_{n}(\hat{q})+\mathsf{B}_{n}(\rho,\hat{q}),

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{n}(\hat{q}):=\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{n}}\sum^{d}_{j=0}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j},

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{n}(t,\rho,\hat{q}):=2\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q})\left[\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q})-\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})\right].

Under
C1
and
A4
, we deduce that, for all
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
,

\displaystyle u(\hat{q})\mathsf{A}_{n}(\hat{q})=u(\hat{q})\big(\Gamma_{n,n}+\textstyle\sum_{n\neq j}\Gamma_{n,j}\frac{\hat{q}_{j}}{\hat{q}_{n}}\big)\geq u(\hat{q})\Gamma_{n,n}\geq 0.

Moreover, according to Lemma
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{(\rho,\hat{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times e_{0}}\mathsf{B}_{n}(t,\rho,\hat{q})=-2\sum^{m}_{k=1}\hat{\theta}_{k}\mathbf{R}_{k,n}(t)<0.

It implies

\displaystyle\limsup_{(\rho,\hat{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times e_{0}}\mathscr{L}\mathsf{V}_{n}(\hat{q})\leq\lim_{(\rho,\hat{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times e_{0}}\mathsf{B}_{n}(\rho,\hat{q})<0.

Therefore, there exist
\delta,\lambda>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}V(\hat{q})\leq-\delta,\quad\forall(\rho,\hat{q})\in\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times B_{\lambda}(e_{0})\cap\mathcal{O}_{d+1}.

Define
\tau_{\lambda}
as the first exiting time from
\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\times B_{\lambda}(e_{0})
.
According to Theorem
4.1
,
\hat{q}(t)\in\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
for all
t\geq 0
almost surely. Applying the Itô formula on
\mathsf{V}(\hat{q}(t))
, we obtain
\mathbb{E}(\tau_{\lambda})\leq\mathsf{V}(\hat{q}(0))/\delta<\infty
. Then, we conclude the proof by applying the Markov inequality.

\square

4.3.2
Recurrence property

We now provide sufficient conditions ensuring that trajectories of the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
) are recurrent in neighborhoods of the desired invariant subset. The recurrence analysis relies on the support theorem
[
37
]
, which characterizes the reachable sets of SDEs through associated deterministic control systems. Consider the deterministic control system associated with the Stratonovich form
[
34
]
of (
1
)–(
9
), for
n\in\{0,\dots,d\}
,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{v}(t)=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{u}_{\gamma,\eta}(t,\rho_{v}(t))+\mathcal{P}(t,\rho_{v}(t))\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\mathcal{G}_{L_{k}}(\rho_{v}(t))V_{k}(t),

(10)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{q}}_{v,n}(t)=\mathfrak{f}^{u}_{n}(\hat{q}_{v}(t))+\Delta_{n}(\hat{q}_{v}(t))+2\hat{q}_{v,n}(t)\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q}_{v}(t))V_{k}(t),

(11)

where
\rho_{v}(0)=\rho(0)
,
\hat{q}_{v}(0)=\hat{q}(0)
, and

V_{k}(t):=v_{k}(t)+\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\mathrm{Tr}((L_{k}+L_{k}^{*})\rho_{v}(t)),

where
v_{k}(t)\in\mathcal{V}
is the bounded control input. Here

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{u}_{\gamma,\eta}(t,\rho):=-\mathfrak{i}[H_{0}(t)+uH_{1},\rho]+\textstyle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\frac{\gamma_{k}(t)}{2}\big(2(1-\eta_{k}(t))L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{*}-(L^{*}_{k}L_{k}+\eta_{k}(t)L_{k}^{2})\rho

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\rho(L^{*}_{k}L_{k}+\eta_{k}(t){L_{k}^{*}}^{2})+\eta_{k}(t)\mathrm{Tr}((L_{k}+L^{*}_{k})^{2}\rho)\rho\big),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}^{u}_{n}(\hat{q})=u\textstyle\sum^{d}_{j=0}\Gamma_{n,j}\hat{q}_{j}-4\hat{q}_{n}\textstyle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q})\Lambda_{k}(\hat{q}),

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\hat{q})=2\hat{q}_{n}\textstyle\sum_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}[\textstyle\sum_{j}\hat{q}_{j}\mathfrak{R}\{l_{k,j}\}\Phi^{k}_{j}(\hat{q})-(\Phi^{k}_{n}(\hat{q}))^{2}].

The invariance of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
for the deterministic system (
10
)–(
11
) follows directly from the support theorem.

We now introduce a Kalman-type controllability assumption on the control Hamiltonian
H_{1}
to guarantee sufficient coupling between the target subspace
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and its orthogonal complement
\bigoplus^{d}_{i=1}\mathcal{H}_{i}
. This ensures that the set

\mathcal{I}_{0}:=\{\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\mid\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{0})=0\}

which contains all potential invariant subsets of the dynamics in
Case 2
, i.e.,
\mathcal{I}(H_{n})
with
n\in E
, is non-invariant under the evolution.
Establishing the non-invariance of
\mathcal{I}_{0}
is essential for stabilization.
Let
\Pi^{\perp}_{0}:=\mathbf{I}-\Pi_{0}
denote the orthogonal projector onto
\bigoplus^{d}_{i=1}\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Consider the block operators of the control Hamiltonian
H_{1,R}
from
\bigoplus^{d}_{i=1}\mathcal{H}_{i}
to
\bigoplus^{d}_{i=1}\mathcal{H}_{i}
and
H_{1,Q}
from
\mathcal{H}_{0}
to
\bigoplus^{d}_{i=1}\mathcal{H}_{i}
. We impose the following assumption:

A7:

There exists a
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

\hskip-34.14322pt\mathrm{rank}\{[\mathbf{I},H_{1,R},\dots,H_{1,R}^{n}]H_{1,Q}\}\geq N-1-\dim\{\mathcal{H}_{0}\}.

The next lemma shows that, under the required assumptions, the control Hamiltonian
H_{1}
guarantees the immediate exit of trajectories from
\mathcal{I}_{0}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
A1
,
A2
,
A4
,
A6
and
A7
holds, then for all
\rho(0)\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
, the trajectory
\rho_{v}(t)
to the system (
10
) exits
\mathcal{I}_{0}
immediately.

Proof.

Under
A4
, we suppose that
u_{t}=u(\hat{q}_{v}(t))>0
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. If the lemma false, then there exists a
\delta>0
arbitrarily small such that
\rho_{v}(t)\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Pi_{0}\rho_{v}(t)\Pi_{0}=\rho_{v}(t)\Pi_{0}=\Pi_{0}\rho_{v}(t)=0.

By
A1
and
A2
, for all
k\in[m]
, we have,

\displaystyle[L_{k},\Pi_{0}]=[H_{0}(t),\Pi_{0}]=[\tilde{H}_{0}(t),\Pi_{0}]=0,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

It implies that, for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
,

\displaystyle\Pi_{0}\dot{\rho}_{v}(t)\Pi_{0}=\sum^{\bar{m}}_{k=1}\Pi_{0}C_{k}(t)\rho_{v}(t)C_{k}(t)^{*}\Pi_{0}\geq 0.

However, since we cannot access the full information of the perturbations, and
C_{k}(t)
act only positively in breaking the invariance of
\mathcal{I}_{0}
, it suffices to consider the “worst-case” scenario in the first-order derivative analysis, i.e.,
C_{k}(t)=0
for all
k\in[\bar{m}]
and
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
.
Define

\displaystyle A:=\Pi_{0}^{\perp}H_{1}\Pi_{0}^{\perp},\quad B:=\Pi_{0}^{\perp}H_{1}\Pi_{0}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{v}(t)\Pi_{0}=\mathfrak{i}u_{t}\rho_{v}(t)H_{1}\Pi_{0}=\mathfrak{i}u_{t}\rho_{v}(t)B=0,\quad\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],

which implies
\rho_{v}(t)B=0
and consequently
B^{*}\rho_{v}(t)B=0
. Moreover, under
C_{k}(t)=0
, we get

\displaystyle B^{*}\dot{\rho}_{v}(t)B=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\gamma_{k}(t)\big(1-\eta_{k}(t)\big)B^{*}L_{k}\rho_{v}(t)L_{k}^{*}B=0.

Thus, for all
i\in[m]
,
\rho_{v}(t)L_{i}^{*}B=0
. Since
H_{0}(t)
,
\tilde{H}_{0}(t)
and
L_{k}
has zero cross-block
\Pi^{\perp}_{0}(\cdot)\Pi_{0}
, they cannot contribute to exiting from
\mathcal{I}_{0}
. Only
H_{1}
does, through the non-zero block
B
. Next, we calculate the derivative of the following equality,

\displaystyle B^{*}A\rho_{v}(t)B-B^{*}\rho_{v}(t)AB=0,\quad\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],

which yields,

\displaystyle B^{*}A\dot{\rho}_{v}(t)B-B^{*}\dot{\rho}_{v}(t)AB=\mathfrak{i}u_{t}B^{*}A\rho_{v}(t)AB=0.

It implies that
B^{*}A\rho_{v}(t)AB=0
, thus
\rho_{v}(t)AB=0
.
Repeating this argument recursively gives, for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
,

\displaystyle\rho_{v}(t)B=\rho_{v}(t)AB=\dots=\rho_{v}(t)A^{n}B=0,

for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Due to
A7
, we deduce
\mathrm{rank}\big(\rho_{v}(t)\big)\leq 1
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
. This leads to a contradiction, since by Lemma
A.2
, Lemma
A.1
and the support theorem
[
37
]
,
\mathrm{rank}\big(\rho_{v}(t)\big)>1
for all
t>0
. The proof is complete.

\square

Remark 4.7
.

Assumption
A6
guarantees the non-invariance of
\bigcup_{n\in[d]}\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\subset\mathcal{I}_{0}
, and
A7
ensures the non-invariance of
\mathcal{I}_{0}
itself. Both properties can be simultaneously guaranteed by the following stronger Kalman-type rank condition:

A-ctrl:

There exists a
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\hskip-34.14322pt\mathrm{rank}\{[\mathbf{I},H_{1,R},\dots,H_{1,R}^{n}]H_{1,Q}\}\geq N-\dim\{\mathcal{H}_{0}\}.

This stronger condition does not rely on the non-invariance of the set of pure states (Lemma
A.2
) and clearly implies both
A6
and
A7
.

For all
k\in[m]
and
n\in\{0,\dots,d\}
, define

\Psi^{k}_{n}(\rho):=\Re\{l_{k,n}\}-\textstyle\sum_{j}\Re\{l_{k,j}\}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{j}).

The following proposition shows that, under suitable regularity and coupling conditions, the trajectories almost surely enter a small neighborhood of the desired subspace in finite time.

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose that
\bar{\eta}_{k}<1
for all
k\in[m]
, and

C1
-
C2
and
A1
-
A7
hold. For all
(\rho(0),\hat{q}(0))\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
and any
\zeta>0
, the trajectory of the coupled system (
1
)–(
9
) enters
\mathbf{B}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times B_{\zeta}(e_{0})
in finite time almost surely, for almost all
\tilde{H}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}_{*}(\mathcal{H}))
and
C_{k}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
, if
\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{0}\rho_{v}(0))=0
, there exists a finite
t_{1}>0
such that
\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{0}\rho_{v}(t_{1}))>0
. From (
10
), we have, for all
t\geq t_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{0}\dot{\rho}_{v}(t))=\mathrm{Tr}\big[\Pi_{0}\big(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{u}_{\gamma,\eta}(t,\rho_{v}(t))+\mathcal{P}(t,\rho_{v}(t))\big)\big]

\displaystyle~~~~~~+2\textstyle\sum^{n}_{k=1}\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\Psi^{k}_{0}(\rho_{v}(t))\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{0}\rho_{v}(t))V_{k}(t),

where
\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{0}\rho_{v}(t_{1}))>0
. Since
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}
is compact, the first two terms on the right-hand side and those in (
11
) are uniformly bounded from above in this domain. Moreover, a direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{0}))\geq\Psi^{k}_{0}(\rho)\geq\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}(1-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{0})),

(12)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}(1-\hat{q}_{0})\geq\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})\geq\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}(1-\hat{q}_{0})).

(13)

Under
A5
, we deduce

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigcap^{m}_{k=1}\{\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\mid\Psi^{k}_{0}(\rho)=0\}=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0}),

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigcap^{m}_{k=1}\{\hat{q}\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}\mid\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})=0\}=e_{0}.

Thus, for any
\zeta>0
, there exist at least one
k\in[n]
and
\delta>0
such that
|\Psi^{k}_{0}(\rho)|\geq\delta
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathbf{B}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
, and
|\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})|\geq\delta
for all
\hat{q}\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}\setminus B_{\zeta}(e_{0})
.
Then by choosing
V_{k}(t)=K/\min\{\Psi^{k}_{0}(\rho_{v}(t)),\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q}_{v}(t))\}
, with

sufficiently large, we can guarantee that
(\rho_{v}(t)),\hat{q}_{v}(t))
enter
\mathbf{B}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times B_{\zeta}(e_{0})
in finite time.

We apply the support theorem
[
37
]
to extend the finite-time entrance property from the deterministic system to the stochastic trajectories of (
1
)–(
9
).
Since the deterministic flow reaches
\mathbf{B}{\zeta}(\mathcal{H}0)\times B{\zeta}(e_{0})
in finite time, there exists a constant
\bar{p}>0
such that the stochastic trajectory enters the same neighborhood within finite time with probability at least
\bar{p}
, uniformly over all initial conditions.

Finally, due to the compactness of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}
and the Feller continuity of the trajectories
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
, together with the strong Markov property and Lemma

\mathbf{B}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times B_{\zeta}(e_{0})

In particular, compactness ensures uniform tightness of the trajectories, while Feller continuity guarantees continuous dependence on initial data, ensuring the recurrence property of the deterministic system to extend to the stochastic one. Combined with the strong Markov property, these features imply that the hitting probability of the target neighborhood accumulates to one as time evolves, thereby yielding almost-sure finite-time entrance.

\square

4.3.3
Exponential stability

For each
k\in[m]
, set

\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}:=\min\{|\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}|,|\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}|\},\qquad\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}:=\max\{|\underline{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}|,|\bar{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}|\},

and define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{k}:=\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}^{2}\min\left\{\underline{\chi}_{k}^{2},1\right\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{k}:=2\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}|\Re\{l_{k,0}\}|\max\left\{1-\underline{\chi}_{k},\bar{\chi}_{k}-1\right\}.

We impose the following condition to guarantee local stability in probability of the target subspace:

C3:

If
|\Re\{\l_{k,0}\}|=0
, then
\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}>0
. Otherwise,
\mathfrak{A}_{k}>\mathfrak{B}_{k}
for all
k\in[m]
.

By a straightforward calculation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Under condition
C3
, for all
\chi_{k}(t)\in[\underline{\chi}_{k},\bar{\chi}_{k}]
, we have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}^{2}\min\{\chi_{k}(t)^{2},1\}-2\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}|(\chi_{k}(t)-1)\Re\{l_{k,0}\}|>\mathfrak{A}_{k}-\mathfrak{B}_{k}>0.

Define the coefficient

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{\underline{\chi},\bar{\chi}}:=\textstyle\sum_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}(2\mathfrak{A}_{k}-\mathfrak{B}_{k})^{2}/2\mathfrak{A}_{k},

which is used to estimate the Lyapunov exponent. Building on the previous stability and recurrence results, the following theorem presents the main result of this paper, under mild regularity and coupling conditions, the target subspace is globally exponentially stable almost surely.

Theorem 4.10
.

Suppose
\bar{\eta}_{k}<1
for all
k\in[n]
, conditions
C1
-
C3
and assumptions
A1
-
A7
hold.
Then, for all
\rho(0)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
, the target subspace
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is almost sure GES for the perturbed system (
1
), for almost all
\tilde{H}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}_{*}(\mathcal{H}))
and
C_{k}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, with the sample Lyapunov exponent less than or equal to
-\mathfrak{C}_{\underline{\chi},\bar{\chi}}/2
.

Proof.

Define the auxiliary function

\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})=1-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{0})+1-\hat{q}_{0},

and consider the candidate Lyapunov function

\displaystyle V(\rho,\hat{q})=\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})^{x}\geq 0,\quad x\in(0,1),

where the equality holds if and only if
(\rho,\hat{q})\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}
. By Theorem
4.1
, if the initial condition satisfies
\hat{q}_{0}(0)<1
, then
\hat{q}_{0}(t)<1
for all
t\geq 0
almost surely. Consequently,
V(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))>0
almost surely.

We first establish the local stability in probability of the target subspace
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. That is, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and for every
r>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,r)>0
such that,

\mathbb{P}\big(V(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))<r\text{ for }t\geq 0\big)\geq 1-\varepsilon,

whenever
V(\rho(0),\hat{q}(0))<\delta
.

The infinitesimal generator of
V(\rho,\hat{q})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}V(\rho,\hat{q})=xV(\rho,\hat{q})\!\left[\!\frac{\mathfrak{F}(t,\rho,\hat{q})}{\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})}-\frac{2(1-x)\sum_{k}\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})}{\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})^{2}}\!\right],

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}(t,\rho,\hat{q})=

\displaystyle u(\hat{q})\big[\textstyle\sum_{j}\Gamma_{0,j}\hat{q}_{j}-\mathfrak{i}\mathrm{Tr}([H_{1},\rho]\Pi_{0})\big]

\displaystyle+2\hat{q}_{0}\textstyle\sum_{k}\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})=

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{k}\big[\chi_{k}(t)\Psi^{k}_{0}(\rho)\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{0})+\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})\hat{q}_{0}\big]^{2}.

Since
u(\hat{q})\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle|u(\hat{q})|\leq c|1-\hat{q}_{0}|^{1+\alpha}\leq c\mathsf{V}_{0}^{1+\alpha}

for some
c>0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})|

\displaystyle=\left|\textstyle\Re\{l_{k,0}\}\sum^{d}_{j=0}\hat{q}_{j}-\sum^{d}_{n=0}\Re\{l_{k,n}\}\hat{q}_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\textstyle\sum^{d}_{n=0}(\Re\{l_{k,0}\}-\Re\{l_{k,n}\})\hat{q}_{n}\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}(1-\hat{q}_{0})\leq\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q}).

Therefore, due to the compactness of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
and
\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{d+1}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{F}(t,\rho,\hat{q})}{\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|u(\hat{q})|}{\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})}\big|\textstyle\sum_{j}\Gamma_{0,j}\hat{q}_{j}-\mathfrak{i}\mathrm{Tr}([H_{1},\rho]\Pi_{0})\big|+\frac{2\hat{q}_{0}\textstyle\sum_{k}|\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})||\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})|}{\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})^{\alpha}+2\hat{q}_{0}\textstyle\sum_{k}\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}|\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})|,

for some
c>0
.
Together with the relation (
12
)–(
13
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sum^{m}_{k=1}\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})}{\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})^{2}}\geq\sum^{m}_{k=1}\hat{\theta}_{k}\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}^{2}\min\big\{\chi_{k}(t)^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{0})^{2},\hat{q}_{0}^{2}\big\}.

Putting the estimates together,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}V(\rho,\hat{q})\leq-V(\rho,\hat{q})\mathsf{C}_{x}(t,\rho,\hat{q}),

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{x}(t,\rho,\hat{q})=

\displaystyle 2x(1-x)\sum_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}^{2}\min\{\chi_{k}(t)^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{0})^{2},\hat{q}_{0}^{2}\}-cx\mathsf{V}_{0}(\rho,\hat{q})^{\alpha}+2x\hat{q}_{0}\sum_{k}\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}|\mathfrak{T}_{k}(t,\rho,\hat{q})|.

By continuity arguments and Lemma
4.9
, there exists
x\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{(\rho,\hat{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}}\mathsf{C}_{x}(t,\rho,\hat{q})=

\displaystyle 2x\sum_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}\Big[(1-x)\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}^{2}\min\{\chi_{k}(t)^{2},1\}-2\bar{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}|(\chi_{k}(t)-1)\Re\{l_{k,0}\}|\Big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{x}>0,

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{x}=2x\textstyle\sum_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}[(1-x)\mathfrak{A}_{k}-\mathfrak{B}_{k}].

Consequently,

\displaystyle\limsup_{(\rho,\hat{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}}\frac{\mathscr{L}V(\rho,\hat{q})}{V(\rho,\hat{q})}\leq\lim_{(\rho,\hat{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}}-\mathsf{C}_{x}(t,\rho,\hat{q})=-\mathsf{C}_{x}<0.

Due to the continuity of
\mathsf{C}_{x}(t,\rho,\hat{q})
, there exists a
\lambda>0
such that

\mathscr{L}V(\rho,\hat{q})\leq 0,\quad\forall(\rho,\hat{q})\in\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times B_{\lambda}(e_{0}),

and by applying the similar arguments as in
[
20
, Theorem 6.3]
, then local stability in probability is ensured.

Next, according to Proposition
4.8
, for almost all
\tilde{H}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}_{*}(\mathcal{H}))
and
C_{k}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, the coupled system is almost surely recurrent. That is, for any initial condition
(\rho(0),\hat{q}(0))\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
, the trajectory
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
almost surely enters any neighborhood of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}
in finite time.

Combining the above two properties,
local stability in probability
and
almost sure recurrence
, one can invoke the Strong Markov property to establish the almost sure convergence of
V(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
to zero (see
[
20
, Theorem 6.3]
for a detailed argument; see also

[
19
, Theorem 5.5.7]
).
Indeed, since the process
(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
almost surely returns infinitely often to any neighborhood of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}
, and due to local stability in probability, it remains in such neighborhoods with non-zero probability, the probability that the trajectory leaves these neighborhoods infinitely many times is zero.
Consequently,
V(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))
converges to zero almost surely.
Therefore, the target subspace
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is almost surely asymptotically stable for all
(\rho(0),\hat{q}(0))\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{O}_{d+1}
.

Finally, we derive the almost sure global exponential stability and provide an estimation of the sample Lyapunov exponent. Observe that

\displaystyle\liminf_{(\rho,\hat{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\times e_{0}}\sum^{n}_{k=1}\hat{\theta}_{k}\Bigg[\mathrm{Tr}\Bigg(\nabla_{\rho}V(\rho,\hat{q})\frac{\chi_{k}(t)\mathcal{G}_{L_{k}}(\rho)}{V(\rho,\hat{q})}\Bigg)+\frac{\nabla_{\hat{q}}V(\rho,\hat{q})^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{k}(\hat{q})}{V(\sigma,\hat{\sigma})}\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 4x\sum_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}^{2}\min\big\{\chi_{k}(t)^{2},1\big\}\geq 2\mathsf{K}_{x},

where
\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{k}(\hat{q})=2[\hat{q}_{0}\Phi^{k}_{0}(\hat{q})\cdots\hat{q}_{d}\Phi^{k}_{d}(\hat{q})]^{\top}
and
\nabla_{\rho}V(\rho,\hat{q})
is the Fréchet gradient, and

\mathsf{K}_{x}=2x\textstyle\sum_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}\underline{\mathfrak{l}}_{k}^{2}\min\big\{\underline{\chi}_{k}^{2},1\big\}.

Therefore, by using arguments as in the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 6.3]
again, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log V(\rho(t),\hat{q}(t))\leq-\max_{x\in(0,1)}\{\mathsf{C}_{x}+\mathsf{K}_{x}\}=-\mathfrak{C}_{\underline{\chi},\bar{\chi}}<0,\quad a.s.

Moreover, since
\big(\mathbf{d}_{0}(\rho)\big)^{2}\leq cV(\rho,\hat{q})
for some positive constant
c
, it follows that

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbf{d}_{0}(\rho(t))\leq-\mathfrak{C}_{\underline{\chi},\bar{\chi}}/2<0,\quad a.s.

This completes the proof.

\square

6
CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORKS

We studied robust feedback stabilization of perturbed quantum systems under QND measurements using a reduced quantum filter. The proposed method ensures stabilization toward a target subspace when the free Hamiltonian, coupling strengths, measurement efficiencies, and perturbations are time-varying, by estimating only the diagonal elements of the system state in the non-demolition basis. Our analysis highlights invariance-preserving perturbations and the critical role of accurately known measurement operators in ensuring effectiveness.
Future work will extend these results to broader classes of perturbations and more general measurement settings, further advancing scalable and robust quantum feedback control.

Appendix A
Invariant properties of quantum trajectories

This appendix states several auxiliary results used in the proofs of instability and recurrence. These lemmas concern the invariance properties of the stochastic dynamics and are analogous to those established in
[
20
, Section 4]
and
[
21
, Lemma 7]
for SME (
1
).

The first lemma is a direct analogue of
[
20
, Section 4]
, its proof is therefore omitted.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that
A4
holds. The rank of
\rho(t)
is almost surely non-decreasing.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{P}:=\{\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{2})=1\}

denote the set of pure states.

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that
A4
and
A6
are satisfied. In addition, suppose that there exists a
k\in[m]
such that
\bar{\eta}_{k}<1
. Then, for all initial state
\rho(0)\in\mathcal{I}_{P}\setminus\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
,
\rho(t)
is mixed (i.e.,
\mathrm{Tr}\big(\rho(t)^{2}\big)<1
) for all
t>0
almost surely.

Proof.

For all
\rho\in\mathcal{I}_{P}
, we have
\rho^{2}=\rho
and
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A\rho B)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)\mathrm{Tr}(\rho B)
with
A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. By Itô formula, we get
d\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)^{2})=\mathfrak{A}(t,\rho(t))dt+\sum_{k}\mathfrak{B}_{k}(t,\rho(t))dW_{k}(t)
. By a straightforward calculation, it follows that, for all
\rho\in\mathcal{I}_{P}
,
\mathfrak{B}_{k}(t,\rho)=0
for all
k
, and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(t,\rho)=

\displaystyle-2\sum^{m}_{k=1}\gamma_{k}(t)\big(1-\eta_{k}(t)\big)\big[\mathrm{Tr}(L^{*}_{k}L_{k}\rho)-\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}^{*}\rho)\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}\rho)\big]

\displaystyle-2\sum^{\bar{m}}_{k=1}\big[\mathrm{Tr}\big(C^{*}_{k}(t)C_{k}(t)\rho\big)-\mathrm{Tr}\big(C_{k}^{*}(t)\rho\big)\mathrm{Tr}\big(C_{k}(t)\rho\big)\big]

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(L^{*}_{k}L_{k}\rho)\geq\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}^{*}\rho)\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}\rho),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\big(C^{*}_{k}(t)C_{k}(t)\rho\big)\geq\mathrm{Tr}\big(C_{k}^{*}(t)\rho\big)\mathrm{Tr}\big(C_{k}(t)\rho\big),

where the equalities hold if and only if
\rho\in\bigcup^{d}_{i=0}\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{i})
. By Lemma
4.2
, the trajectory exits
\bigcup^{d}_{i=1}\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{H}_{i})
immediately. Then, the state becomes mixed immediately and remains mixed afterwards almost surely due to Lemma
A.1
. The proof is complete.

\square

Appendix B
Proof of Inequality (
4
)

Computing the infinitesimal generator
\mathscr{L}V(\rho)
via Itô formula provides a more direct and compact approach than differentiating the Lyapunov function (
3
) explicitly, especially since the quantum state
\rho
is a complex matrix.

Under assumptions
A1
and
A-qsr
, and for
u=0
, the cyclic property of the trace implies that, for any
i\in\{0,\dots,d\}
,

\displaystyle d\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\big(\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)L_{k}\Pi_{i})+\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)L^{*}_{k}\Pi_{i})-\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}\rho(t)+\rho(t)L^{*}_{k})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})\big)dW_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\mathcal{Z}_{k,i}(\rho(t))\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})dW_{k}(t),

where
\mathcal{Z}_{k,i}(\rho):=2\Re\{l_{k,i}\}-\mathrm{Tr}(L_{k}\rho+\rho L^{*}_{k})
. By Itô product rule, we have

\displaystyle d\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{j})=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{j})\sum^{m}_{k=1}\Big[\theta_{k}(t)\mathcal{Z}_{k,i}(\rho(t))\mathcal{Z}_{k,j}(\rho(t))dt

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\sqrt{\theta_{k}(t)}\big(\mathcal{Z}_{k,i}(\rho(t))+\mathcal{Z}_{k,j}(\rho(t))\big)dW_{k}(t)\Big].

Due to the invariance of
\mathcal{S}_{I}
,
V
is twice continuously differentiable when restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{I}
. Applying Itô formula to
\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{j})}
and collecting the drift terms yields
the following infinitesimal generator,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(t)\Pi_{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{j})}\sum^{m}_{k=1}\theta_{k}(t)\big(\mathcal{Z}_{k,i}(\rho)-\mathcal{Z}_{k,j}(\rho)\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{j})}\sum^{m}_{k=1}\theta_{k}(t)\big(\Re\{l_{k,i}\}-\Re\{l_{k,j}\}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathfrak{E}_{l}}{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{i})\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{j})}.

Therefore, we conclude

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}V(\rho)\leq-\frac{\mathfrak{E}_{l}}{2}V(\rho).

Rieffel projections and 2-by-2 matrices

Abstract
For a compact space Y Y , we view C ⁡ ( Y × S 1 ) C(Y\times S^{1}) as the crossed product C ⁡ ( Y ) ⋊ ℤ C(Y)\rtimes\mathbb{Z} , with ℤ \mathbb{Z} acting trivially. This allows us to study Rieffel projections in M 2 ​ ( C ⁡ ( Y × S 1 ) CLOSE M_{2}(C(Y\times S^{1}) : we characterize them and compute their image under the projection ∂ 0 : K 0 ​ ( C ⁡ ( Y × S 1 ) ) → K 1 ​ ( C ⁡ ( Y ) ) \partial_{0}:K_{0}(C(Y\times S^{1}))\rightarrow K_{1}(C(Y)) . We provide a new Rieffel projection in M 2 ​ ( C ​ ( 𝕋 2 ) ) M_{2}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2})) , different from Loring’s one [ Lo88 ] , and involving only trigonometric polynomials plus the square root of 2 − e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ θ − e − 2 ​ π ​ i ​ θ 2-e^{2\pi i\theta}-e^{-2\pi i\theta} . We give applications of this projection, e.g. explicit generators for the K-theory of C ⁡ ( 𝕋 3 ) C(\mathbb{T}^{3}) . Finally, we prove that, if a Banach algebra completion ℬ \mathcal{B} of ℂ ⁡ [ ℤ n ] \mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}] is continuously contained in C ⁡ ( 𝕋 n ) C(\mathbb{T}^{n}) and such that the Fourier series of ( 2 − e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ θ j − e − 2 ​ π ​ i ​ θ j ) 1 / 2 ​ ( j = 1 , … , n ) (2-e^{2\pi i\theta_{j}}-e^{-2\pi i\theta_{j}})^{1/2}\;(j=1,...,n) converges in ℬ \mathcal{B} , then the inclusion ℬ ↪ C ⁡ ( 𝕋 n ) \mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{T}^{n}) induces isomorphisms in K-theory.

Introduction

Let
\alpha
be a *-automorphism of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. The crossed product
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
is the universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
A
and a unitary
u
such that
u^{*}au=\alpha(a)
for every
a\in A
. A
Rieffel projection
is a projection in
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
of the form
p=u^{*}x_{1}^{*}+x_{0}+x_{1}u
, with
x_{0},x_{1}\in A
, and
x_{0}^{*}=x_{0}
. The conditions
p=p^{*}=p^{2}
are equivalent to

0=\alpha(x_{1})x_{1};

x_{1}=x_{0}x_{1}+x_{1}\alpha^{-1}(x_{0});

x_{0}=x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}x_{1}^{*}+\alpha(x_{1}^{*}x_{1}).

Rieffel projections were introduced in
[
Ri81
]
to show that the irrational rotation algebra
A_{\theta}
, viewed as the crossed product of
C(S^{1})
by the rotation of angle
2\pi\theta\;(\theta\notin\mathbb{Q})
, contains non-trivial projections.

The celebrated Pimsner-Voiculescu 6-terms exact sequence
[
PV80
]
connects the K-theory of
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
with the K-theory of
A
:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}K_{0}(A)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-\alpha_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&K_{0}(A)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&K_{0}(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})\\
&&&&\\
\partial_{1}\uparrow&&&&\downarrow\partial_{0}\\
&&&&\\
K_{1}(A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\longleftarrow}}&K_{1}(A))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-\alpha_{*}}}{{\longleftarrow}}&K_{1}(A)\end{array}

where
\iota:A\rightarrow A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
is the canonical inclusion.

In the Appendix of
[
PV80
]
, Pimsner and Voiculescu explain how to compute the image of the K-theory class of a Rieffel projection under the map
\partial_{0}
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p=u^{*}x_{1}^{*}+x_{0}+x_{1}u
be a Rieffel projection in
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
. Denote by
\ell_{x_{1}}
the left support projection of
x_{1}
in the enveloping von Neumann algebra of
A
. Then
x_{0}\ell_{x_{1}}
is self-adjoint (equivalently
[x_{0},\ell_{x_{1}}]=0
), the unitary
\exp(2\pi ix_{0}\ell_{x_{1}})
belongs to
A
, and

\partial_{0}[p]=[\exp(2\pi ix_{0}\ell_{x_{1}})].

\blacksquare

When
\mathbb{Z}
is acting trivially on
A
, so that
A\rtimes_{Id}\mathbb{Z}=A\otimes C(S^{1})
, the Pimsner-Voiculescu exact sequence degenerates into two short exact sequences:

0\rightarrow K_{i}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}K_{i}(A\otimes C(S^{1}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}K_{1-i}(A)\rightarrow 0\;(i=0,1).

Let
\varepsilon:A\otimes C(S^{1})\rightarrow A
denote evaluation at
1\in S^{1}
; since
\varepsilon
provides a left inverse for
\iota
, we recover the classical isomorphisms

K_{i}(A\otimes C(S^{1}))=K_{0}(A)\oplus K_{1}(A)\;(i=0,1),

with
\partial_{i}
becoming the projection
K_{i}(A\otimes C(S^{1}))\rightarrow K_{1-i}(A)
in the above decomposition.

We focus on
i=0
. It turns out that Rieffel projections can be interesting even when
\mathbb{Z}
is acting trivially! That was first observed by Loring
[
Lo88
]
: if
\mathbb{T}^{2}
denotes the 2-torus, it is well-known that
K_{0}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2}))=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, with one generator being the class
[1]
of the unit in
C(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, the other generator, sometimes called the Bott element, being non-trivial. Viewing
C(\mathbb{T}^{2})
as the crossed product
C(S^{1})\rtimes_{Id}\mathbb{Z}
, Loring was able to express the non-trivial generator as the class of a Rieffel projection in
M_{2}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
, therefore the projection on a rank 1 sub-bundle of the trivial bundle
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Motivated by Loring’s paper
[
Lo88
]
, for
Y
a compact space, we view
C(Y\times S^{1})
as the crossed product
C(Y)\rtimes_{Id}\mathbb{Z}
and we consider the question of finding Rieffel projections in
M_{2}(C(Y\times S^{1}))
, i.e. projections of the form

P(y,\varphi)=\exp(-2\pi i\varphi)X_{1}(y)^{*}+X_{0}(y)+\exp(2\pi i\varphi)X_{1}(y),

(1)

with
\varphi\in[0,1],X_{0},X_{1}\in M_{2}(C(Y))
, and
X_{0}=X_{0}^{*}
. We will say that such a Rieffel projection
P
is
non-trivial
if
X_{1}\neq 0
, and that it has
rank 1
if
P(y,\varphi)
has rank 1 for every
(y,\varphi)\in Y\times[0,1]
(note that the rank 1 condition follows from non-triviality when
Y
is connected). Non-trivial, rank 1 Rieffel projections are characterized as follows (see section 2 for the proof):

Proposition 1.2
.

There exists a non-trivial, rank 1, Rieffel projection in
M_{2}(C(Y\times S^{1}))
if and only if there exists
a,b,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in C(Y)
, with
a
real-valued,
|\beta|^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}\neq 0
, such that:

•

\beta\gamma=-\alpha^{2};

•

(2a-1)\alpha+b\gamma+\overline{b}\beta=0;

•

a=a^{2}+|b|^{2}+2|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}.

In that case
X_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
\overline{b}&1-a\end{array}\right)
and
X_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&-\alpha\end{array}\right)
.

Example 1
.

Let
a,\beta
in
C(Y)
be such that
a
is real-valued. The self-adjoint matrix
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&\beta\\
\overline{\beta}&1-a\end{array}\right)
is a rank 1 projection in
M_{2}(C(Y))
if and only if
a=a^{2}+|\beta|^{2}
. In that case,

P(y,\varphi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a(y)&\beta(y)\exp(2\pi i\varphi)\\
\overline{\beta(y)}\exp(-2\pi i\varphi)&1-a(y)\end{array}\right)

is a projection in
M_{2}(C(Y\times S^{1}))
, which is indeed a Rieffel projection with
X_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&1-a\end{array}\right)
and
X_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\beta\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
. We call this Rieffel projection of
trivial type
: indeed we will see in Example
2
below, that its K-theory class is always in the kernel of
\partial_{0}:K_{0}(C(Y\times S^{1}))\rightarrow K_{1}(C(Y))
.

To identify the image of the class of a general Rieffel projection in
M_{2}(C(Y\times S^{1}))
under
\partial_{0}:K_{0}(C(Y\times S^{1}))\rightarrow K_{1}(C(Y))
, we need two more notations. For
y\in Y
, let
\ell_{X_{1}}(y)
denote the projection on the range of
X_{1}(y)
, so that
\ell_{X_{1}}
is the left support projection of
X_{1}
in the enveloping von Neumann algebra of
M_{2}(C(Y))
, as in Theorem
1.1
. Set
supp(X_{1})=\{y\in Y:X_{1}(y)\neq 0\}
. With the help of Theorem
1.1
, the next result will be proved in Section 3.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
P(y,\varphi)=\exp(-2\pi i\varphi)X_{1}(y)^{*}+X_{0}(y)+\exp(2\pi i\varphi)X_{1}(y)
be a non-trivial rank 1 Rieffel projection in
M_{2}(C(Y\times S^{1}))
. There exists a function
f:Y\rightarrow[0,1]
, continuous on
supp(X_{1})
, vanishing elsewhere, such that:

•

X_{0}\ell_{X_{1}}=f\ell_{X_{1}};

•

\exp(2\pi if)\in C(Y);

•

\partial_{0}[P]
is the class of the unitary
(1-\ell_{X_{1}})+\exp(2\pi if)\ell_{X_{1}}
in
K_{1}(C(Y));

•

if
supp(X_{1})
is contractible, then
\partial_{0}[P]=[\exp(2\pi if)]
in
K_{1}(C(Y))
.

Example 2
.

We will say that a Rieffel projection
P(y,\varphi)\in M_{2}(C(Y\times S^{1}))
is of
Loring type
, if it is of the form

P(y,\varphi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a(y)&b(y)+\beta(y)\exp(2\pi i\varphi)\\
b(y)+\beta(y)\exp(-2\pi i\varphi)&1-a(y)\end{array}\right),

(2)

where
a,b,\beta
are continuous, real-valued on
Y
, and such that
b\beta=0
and
a=a^{2}+b^{2}+\beta^{2}
(cf Proposition
1.2
).
Hence:

X_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
b&1-a\end{array}\right);\;\;X_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\beta\\
0&0\end{array}\right).

Then
\ell_{X_{1}}(y)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
when
\beta(y)\neq 0
, while
\ell_{X_{1}}(y)=0
when
\beta(y)=0
. And

f(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}a(y)&if&\beta(y)\neq 0\\
0&if&\beta(y)=0\end{array}\right..

So that, for a projection
P(y,\varphi)
of Loring type, Theorem
1.3
says that
\partial_{0}[P]=[u]
, where

is the unitary in

given by:

u(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\exp(2\pi ia(y))&if&\beta(y)\neq 0\\
1&if&\beta(y)=0\end{array}\right..

Projections of trivial type (see Example
1
) are projections

of Loring type with

. From

it follows that
a(y)
is either 0 or 1 when
\beta(y)=0
. This means that
u(y)=\exp(2\pi ia(y))
for every

. So
u
admits a continuous branch of the logarithm on

\partial_{0}[P]=[u]=0
in
K_{1}(C(Y))
, as announced in Example
1
.

Lemma 2.1

Let

1.

The matrix
X

\operatorname{tr}(X)=\det(X)=0

The matrix

Proof

1.

By Cayley-Hamilton, the characteristic polynomial of

2.

The proof is very similar to the one of 1.

3.

of

coincides with the spectrum

of

Assume from now on that
\mathcal{B}
is a Banach algebra continuously contained and dense in
A
. If
\mathcal{B}
is spectral in

, then the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow A
induces isomorphisms in K-theory, e.g. by Theorem 1.3.1 in
[
Bos90
]
. So Proposition
1.5
will follow from:

Lemma 6.1
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
1.5
, the subalgebra
\mathcal{B}
is spectral in
C^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})=C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof
: The proof is in two steps:

•

We claim that
Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})=S^{1}
. Clearly
S^{1}=Sp_{C^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})}(s_{i})
is contained in
Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})
. To prove the converse inclusion, we start by proving that the spectral radius
R_{i}\geq 1
of
s_{i}
in
\mathcal{B}
, is actually equal to 1.

Assume by contradiction
R_{i}>1
, and let
z_{i}\in Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})
be such that
|z_{i}|=R_{i}
. Then, for

, the number
z_{i}^{k}+z_{i}^{-k}
belongs to
Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}+s_{i}^{-k})=\linebreak Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i}+(s_{i}^{*})^{k})
. Hence:

R_{i}^{k}-\frac{1}{R_{i}^{k}}\leq|z_{i}^{k}+z_{i}^{-k}|\leq\|s_{i}^{k}+(s_{i}^{*})^{k}\|_{\mathcal{B}},

so the norm
\|s_{i}^{k}+(s_{i}^{*})^{k}\|_{\mathcal{B}}

. Now by assumption the series
\frac{2}{\pi}(1-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{s_{i}^{k}+(s_{i}^{*})^{k}}{4k^{2}-1})
converges in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, so the norm of the general term has to go to 0. This shows that the norm
\|s_{i}^{k}+(s_{i}^{*})^{k}\|_{\mathcal{B}}
grows more slowly than quadratically, which is the desired contradiction.

The same argument applied to
s_{i}^{*}
, shows that the spectral radius of
s_{i}^{*}=s_{i}^{-1}
is also 1, so
Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})\subset S^{1}
.

•

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
denote the space of characters of
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, i.e. the set of non-zero, continuous, algebra homomorphisms
\chi:\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. By density of
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
in
C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we see that
\mathbb{T}^{n}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
, let us see that the converse inclusion also holds. Recall that the
joint spectrum

Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{1},...,s_{n})
of
s_{1},...,s_{n}
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, is:

Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{1},...,s_{n})=\{(\chi(s_{1}),...\chi(s_{n})):\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

(see Theorem 3.1.14 in
[
Hö79
]
). As
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
is topologically generated by
s_{1},...,s_{n}
, the map
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{1},...,s_{n}):\chi\mapsto(\chi(s_{1}),...\chi(s_{n}))
is a homeomorphism (see Theorem 3.1.15 in
[
Hö79
]
). Finally, since
\chi(s_{i})\in Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})
for every
i
and every
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
, we have
Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{1},...,s_{n})\subset\prod_{i=1}^{n}Sp_{\mathcal{B}}(s_{i})=\mathbb{T}^{n}
by the first part of the proof.

So far we have shown that the inclusion
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\hookrightarrow C^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
induces an identification of their character spaces. But, in a commutative unital Banach algebra, an element is invertible if and only if no character vanishes on that element (see Theorem 3.1.6 in
[
Hö79
]
). From this the lemma follows immediately.
\blacksquare

C(S^{1})
and
C^{1}(S^{1})
. Since
|\sin{\pi\theta}|
is not differentiable at
\theta=0
, in
C^{1}(S^{1})
the element
2-s-s^{*}
has no square root

In Theorem 2.2.10 of

, Bratteli and Robinson define the unique positive square root of a positive element
x
in a unital

(J.-B. Bost, see Example 1.1.3 in

Concluding remarks:
The study of embeddings of Banach algebras that induce isomorphisms in K-theory, attracted attention for a while (see Theorems 2.2 and 3.1 in
[
Sw77
]
; subsection 6.15 in
[
Ka78
]
; appendix VI.3 in
[
Co81
]
); all those results required the subalgebra to be spectral in one way or the other. The first deep results obtained without any spectral assumption, were obtained by J.-B. Bost
[
Bos90
]
(see Example
5
above), in his paper on the Oka principle.

Interest in the subject was revived by V. Lafforgue’s seminal work
[
La02
]
on the Baum-Connes conjecture for discrete groups
\Gamma
: he introduced the Banach KK-theory that allowed him, for every unconditional completion
\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)
of the complex group ring
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, to construct an assembly map
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}:K_{i}^{top}(\Gamma)\rightarrow K_{i}(\mathcal{A}(\Gamma))\;(i=0,1)
, where
K_{i}^{top}(\Gamma)
is the left-hand side of the Baum-Connes assembly map (see
[
BCH94
,
GJV19
]
). For
\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)=\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
, it is a conjecture of J.-B. Bost that
\mu_{\ell^{1}}
is an isomorphism for every discrete group
\Gamma
. Note that it is a result by J. Ludwig
[
Lu79
]
that
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
is spectral in
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
if
\Gamma
is finitely generated with polynomial growth; and another result by J. Jenkins
[
Je70
]
that
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
is NOT spectral in
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
as soon as
\Gamma
contains the free subsemigroup on 2 generators.

Inspired by all this, Lafforgue introduces a class
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of groups with geometric interest, for which he proves that
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}
is an isomorphism for any unconditional completion (Theorem 0.0.2 in
[
La02
]
). To apply this result to the “official” Baum-Connes conjecture (see
[
BCH94
]
), stating that the Baum-Connes assembly map

\mu:K_{i}^{top}(\Gamma)\rightarrow K_{i}(C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
is an isomorphism, he needs to find an unconditional completion that not only embeds into the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
, but has the same K-theory - actually, for groups in the class
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, injectivity of
\mu
had been known by other means, so only surjectivity of the map
K_{i}(\mathcal{A}(\Gamma))\rightarrow K_{i}(C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
is relevant. Let us mention lemma 1.7.2 in
[
La02
]
: If
\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)
is an unconditional completion embedding into
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
, a sufficient condition for the map
K_{i}(\mathcal{A}(\Gamma))\rightarrow K_{i}(C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
to be onto, is that for all
n\geq 1
, every element in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])
has the same spectral radius in
M_{n}(\mathcal{A}(\Gamma))
and in
M_{n}(C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
.

Relative spectral conditions like the previous one received some attention in the literature; e.g. the condition that every element in
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
has the same spectrum in
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
and
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
, was studied under the name
relatively spectral
by B. Nica
[
Ni08
]
, and under the name
quasi-Hermitian
by H. Samei and M. Wiersma
[
SW20
]
. It is known that such a group is necessarily amenable (Proposition 52 in
[
Ni08
]
, Theorem 1.4 in
[
SW20
]
); and that finitely generated groups with subexponential growth are completely relatively spectral (i.e. for every
n\geq 1
, any element in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
has the same spectrum in
M_{n}(\ell^{1}(\Gamma))
and in
M_{n}(C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma))
); as a consequence the inclusion
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)\subset C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
induces isomorphisms in K-theory (see Example 49 in
[
Ni08
]
).

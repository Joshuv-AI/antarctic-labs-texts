Lymph node ratio is a prognostic indicator for locally advanced gastric cancer after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy
Authors: Pei Zhou, Xiong Sun, Liwu Zeng, Xinyu Zeng, Gengchen Xie, Xinghua Liu, Kaixiong Tao, Peng Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe efficacy of lymph node ratio (LNR) as a prognostic indicator in locally advanced gastric cancer (LAGC) patients underwent radical resection after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (NICT) remains to be demonstrated. The objective of the current retrospective study is to investigate the relationship between LNR and survival in patients with LAGC who underwent radical resection after NICT.MethodsA retrospective analysis was performed on 121 cases of LAGC in patients underwent radical resection after NICT between July 2020 and October 2023. The LNR values of the patients were divided into two groups using X-tile software. The first group, designated the low LNR group, comprised patients with LNR values of ≤ 33%. The second group, designated the high LNR group, comprised patients with LNR values of > 33%. The correlation between patient survival rates and a range of clinical and pathological variables was examined.ResultsOverall, 121 patients were enrolled: 108 with low-LNR (LNR ≤ 33%) and 13 with high-LNR (LNR > 33%). A better 2-year overall survival (OS) (88.5% vs. 32.6%; p < 0.001) and progression-free survival (PFS) (80.2% vs. 23.5%; p < 0.001) were observed in patients with low LNR. A similar result was also found in those with non-pathological complete response group (non-pCR), where the 2-year OS was 87.2% vs. 32.6% (p < 0.001), and the 2-year PFS was 77.7% vs. 23.5% (p < 0.001). Compared to the pathologic lymph nodes staging (ypN), LNR exhibited similar prognostic capabilities for OS and PFS. Multivariate analysis indicated that LNR was an independent prognostic factor for both OS (HR 6.258, 95% CI 1.798–21.778; p = 0.004) and PFS (HR 3.431, 95% CI 1.341–8.780; p = 0.010), but not ypN.ConclusionsLNR may serve as a viable indicator for prognostication in LAGC patients treated with NICT.Keywords: Neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy; Lymph node ratio; Gastric cancer,pathological complete response; Pathological complete response

Introduction

Gastric cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally, ranking among the top five most prevalent and fatal cancers in the world, according to epidemiological studies [1]. A substantial proportion, approximately 70%, of gastric cancer patients are diagnosed with locally advanced stage in China. This often results in an unfavorable prognosis [2]. Consequently, the advancement of the prognosis for this group of patients has significant implications for the improvement in the prognosis of patients with gastric cancer in general. Over recent years, there has been a notable shift in clinical opinion towards immunotherapy as a promising avenue in the treatment of advanced tumors. Clinical trials have also demonstrated the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (NICT) in locally advanced gastric cancer (LAGC) patients [3, 4].

The 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) [5] proposes the use of the ypTNM staging system for the evaluation of tumor staging in gastric cancer patients following neoadjuvant therapy. This system is used as a reference for the assessment of prognosis by clinicians in the treatment of these patients. However, the ypN staging system is predicated upon the number of positive lymph nodes identified, and the accuracy of the system is to a certain extent contingent upon the total number of lymph nodes isolated and harvested [6]. In addition, prior studies [7] have demonstrated that an augmented number of lymph nodes harvested is positively related to a more favorable clinical outcome among patients.

A prognostic predictor of lymph node ratio (LNR), defined as the ratio of positive lymph nodes (PLNs) relative to the total lymph nodes (TLNs), has been identified in patients with LAGC who have undergone neoadjuvant chemotherapy [8, 9].Nevertheless, the ability of LNR to predict the outcome for patients with LAGC who have underwent radical resection after NICT has not yet been proven. Consequently, the present study will seek to further elucidate the prognostic value of LNR in LAGC patients underwent radical resection after NICT.

Materials and methods

Patients selection and data collection

All patients were identified from our medical records and selected according to the following criteria: (1) pathologically diagnosed with gastric adenocarcinoma; (2) according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC, eighth edition), patients were clinically classified into stage T3-4N + M0; (3) patients aged 18—75 years; (4) patients had not received any other anticancer therapy before NICT; (5)all of the patients underwent radical surgery and met the criteria for an R0 resection after NICT and (6) available data on follow-up. The retrospective study was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of Union Hospital Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology(approval no. 2024–0164); the signed informed consent from patients was waived because of the retrospective design of this study. The present study collected the following clinicopathological and survival data from the medical records of these patients: demographic data, tumor location, immunotherapy regimen, resection type, tumor ypT stage, tumor ypN stage, lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion, operating time, TLNs, PLNs, and stay after surgery.

Treatments and follow-up

NICT was administered every 21 days. During each cycle, patients received an intravenous administration of either nivolumab(200mg), tislelizumab(200mg), toripalimab(240mg) or sintilimab (200mg) on day 1. The chemotherapy regimen included XELOX: oxaliplatin 130 mg/m2 via intravenous infusion on day 1 with capecitabine 1,000mg/m2 oral twice daily on days 1 to 14 of each cycle; SOX: oxaliplatin, 130 mg/m2 via intravenous infusion on day 1 with S-1 40 mg/m2 oral twice daily on days 1 to 14 each cycle. In general, the clinical effect evaluation was performed after two cycles of NICT. For patients showing tumor shrinkage, a multidisciplinary team (MDT) discussion was conducted to determine whether to proceed with the NICT or to opt for surgery. Radical gastrectomy with D2 lymph node dissection was performed within 4–6 weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant therapy in patients without tumor progression on preoperative imaging assessment. The pathological complete response (pCR) was defined as the absence of viable tumor cells in both the primary tumor and all lymph nodes, while major pathologic response (MPR) is defined as ≤ 10% viable tumor at the primary tumor. Total 8 cycles in the neoadjuvant therapy and adjuvant therapy. Adjuvant chemotherapy with the SOX regimen or XELOX regimen at the same dosages was recommended for at 4–8 weeks after surgery. The final follow-up period was set to be October 2023. The primary endpoint, OS was measured the time from diagnosis to death due to any cause; and PFS was defined as the time elapsed between treatment initiation and tumor progression or death from any cause.

Stratification of lymph node ratio

The ypN stage, which was the subject of this study, was based on the eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system. The LNR was defined as the ratio of positive lymph nodes to the total number. To identify optimal LNR cut-off values, we employed the X-tile software (http://www.tissuearray.org/rimmlab). X-tile was used to generate plots, in which LNR was classified into two risk groups. This process involved an exhaustive evaluation of all possible divisions of LNR, with each resulting association being calculated using the log-rank test for survival. X-tile determined a cut-off value of 0.33, which is presented in Fig. 1. In order to facilitate further analysis within the context of this study, the High-LNR group, defined as comprising individuals with an LNR ranging from 33 to 100%, was subjected to investigation alongside the Low-LNR group, defined as comprising individuals with an LNR ranging from 0 to 33%.

Optimal cut-points of LNR were determined by X-tile analysis. In terms of overall survival, the optimal cut-points of LNR were 0.33

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed data were presented as mean SD, and non-normally distributed data were presented as median (range). Numbers and percentages are provided for categorical data. Continuous variables were analyzed using the Student's t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, while categorical variables were analyzed with the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test. AUCs were performed to better understand the prognostic ability of LNR based on receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves,the area under the curve of the ROC curve was used to assess the predictive value of LNR and ypN staging. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression model was applied to analyze factors associated with overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). Statistically significant variables (p < 0.05) in univariate analysis were incorporated into the multivariate Cox regression models. The results were reported as hazard ratio (HR) and its 95% confidential interval (95% CI). The survival differences were evaluated using the Kaplan–Meier method and statistical significance was determined using the log-rank test. Calibration represented agreement between observed outcomes and predictions. Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS Statistics Version 26.0 software (IBM Co., Armonk, NY, USA), MedCalc 17.6 and R language version 3.4.3 for Windows (R Package for Statistical Computing; www.r-project.org). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patients characteristics

After applying the pre-established inclusion criteria, a total of 121 patients diagnosed with LAGC who subsequently underwent radical resection after NICT were included in this retrospective cohort study. The mean age of the cohort was 58.3 ± 9.4 years, with a range from 31 to 75 years. The study population consisted of 95 male (78.5%) and 26 female (21.5%) patients. A total of four (3.3%) of the patients underwent proximal resection, 39 (32.2%) underwent distal resection, and the remainder underwent total resection (78.5%). The mean of TLNs and PLNs was 28.43 ± 10.1 (range:11–64) and 2.43 ± 5.31 (range:0–28), respectively. Among the patients, 80 (66.1%) were treated with tislelizumab immunotherapy, 7 (5.8%) with nivolumab, 15(12.4%) with sintilimab, and 19 (15.7%) with toripalimab. The mean follow-up duration was 19 months, with a range of 2 to 40 months. A total of 26 (21.5%) patients experienced disease relapse, and 15 (12.4%) patients succumbed to the disease.

Predictive value of the lymph node ratio for overall survival and progression-free survival

Figure 2 illustrates the predictive value of LNR for OS and PFS (Fig. 2). LNR demonstrated a prognostic ability for OS and PFS that was comparable to that of the ypN stage. The findings of the current study suggest that LNR may serve as an alternative to ypN stage for prognostication in LAGC following NICT. Figure 3 presents the outcomes stratified by LNR values (Fig. 3). A superior two-year OS (88.5% vs. 32.6%; p < 0.001) and PFS (80.2% vs. 23.5%; p < 0.001) was observed in patients with low LNR. A comparable outcome was observed in patients with non-pCR, where the two-year OS was 87.2% vs. 32.6% (p < 0.001), and the two-year PFS was 77.7% vs. 23.5% (p < 0.001).

Discussion

The aim of this study was to evaluate the clinical impact of lymph node ratio (LNR) on patients with locally advanced gastric cancer (LAGC) who had undergone radical resection following NICT. The principal finding was that LNR is a significant, independent predictor of prognosis. Moreover, the patient cohort was subdivided into two groups based on their response to treatment. LNR has also served as an independent predictor, showcasing a better discrimination ability in those who have not achieved pCR. Consequently, LNR is a valuable tool for assessing LN status, and has the potential to be utilized as an alternative method of ypN staging in LAGC patients undergoing NICT.

The LNR has been demonstrated to possess prognostic value in a number of different tumor types, including oesophageal cancer and LAGC. A study comprising 353 patients diagnosed with oesophageal cancer who underwent radical surgery demonstrated that LNR was superior to ypN when analysed using ROC analysis. Furthermore, the study revealed that LNR is an independent risk factor for OS [10]. A comprehensive investigation of 7,195 patients with oesophageal cancer who underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy in conjunction with radiotherapy, sourced from the United States National Cancer Data Bank, revealed that LNR outperformed the conventional ypN staging method in prognosticating overall survival within this subset of patients [11]. Moreover, studies have combined two indicators, LNR and tumour regression grade, to predict the prognosis of patients with LAGC. It has been demonstrated that individuals exhibiting a low LNR and an optimal local response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy have a more favorable prognosis than those with a suboptimal response [7]. In our study, compared to ypN staging, LNR may serve as an alternative for prognostication in LAGC following NICT. Furthermore, this study also revealed that LNR was an independent prognostic factor for both OS and PFS.

Although the prognostic value of LNR has been demonstrated, the optimal cut-off value for LNR remains inconsistent across studies. Many studies use ROC curves or X-tile software to determine the optimal cut-off value for LNR [12–15]. The prognostic value of lymph nodes ratio for oesophageal cancer was investigated in a study of 536 patients undergoing radical surgery. The optimal cut-off value of 0.20 was determined by ROC analysis [16]. Furthermore, a study was conducted on patients diagnosed with locally advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the esophagus who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and X-tile software was used to identify the optimal cut-off value. This was found to be 0.13 [17]. The optimal cut-off value for the LNR was determined to be 0.33 based on the X-tile software in the study. Previous studies have reported that patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy have fewer total lymph nodes dissected and isolated during radical surgery compared to those not receiving neoadjuvant therapy [18, 19]. Additionally, LNR is believed to reduce potential errors associated with insufficient total lymph node numbers [20, 21]. The median number of total lymph nodes isolated differed, which may impact the LNR cut-off value.

It must be acknowledged that there are inherent constraints to the current study. Firstly, it should be noted that this was a retrospective study, conducted at a single center. Furthermore, The study’s limitations include a small sample size and short follow-up duration, necessitating further research with larger clinical samples to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the prognostic value of LNR in LAGC patients who underwent radical resection following NICT. Consequently, this study demonstrated that LNR may serve as a useful tool for assessing the lymph node status and might be a good predictive index of prognosis in LAGC patients undergoing NICT.

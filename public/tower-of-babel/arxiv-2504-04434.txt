Estimating trisection genus via gem theory
Maria Rita Casali, Paola Cristofori

Abstract
Gems are a particular type of edge-colored graphs, dual to colored triangulations, which represent compact PL-manifolds of arbitrary dimension, both in the closed and boundary case. In the present paper, gem theory is used to approach trisections of PL 4-manifolds, so as to prove that: • the graph-defined invariant regular genus is an upper bound for the trisection genus of each closed 4 4 -manifold; • a trisection diagram can be directly obtained from any gem of a closed 4 4 -manifold. Moreover, suitable extensions of the above results are presented for compact 4-manifolds with connected boundary.



Keywords
: trisection genus, trisection diagram, edge-colored graph, gem, regular genus

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
: 57Q15 - 57K40 - 57M15

1
Introduction

The notion of trisection of a smooth closed 4-manifold (originally introduced by Gay and Kirby in
[
13
]
) is well-known: each such manifold can be decomposed into three 4-dimensional handlebodies, with disjoint interiors and mutually intersecting in 3-dimensional handlebodies (with the same genus), so that the intersection of all three “pieces” is a closed surface. The obvious analogy with Heegaard splittings in dimension three suggests also to define an associated invariant in dimension four, i.e. the
trisection genus
, as the minimum genus of the 3-dimensional handlebodies involved in all trisections of the given 4-manifold.

On the other hand,
gem theory
enables to combinatorially represent PL-manifolds of arbitrary dimension via edge-colored graphs, both in the closed and boundary case; within this theory, the invariant
regular genus
has been defined and extensively studied, also in order to classify PL-manifolds.

The present paper follows some previous works, where a relationship between the two theories has been established, allowing to obtain trisections of closed 4-manifolds from gems, and also to suitably extend the notion of trisection to 4-manifolds with non-empty connected boundary (by making use of the notion of
gem-induced trisection
, which involves an Heegaard splitting of the boundary, instead of an open-book decomposition, as it happens in the extension introduced in
[
11
]
).
However, existing procedures either are limited to gems satisfying certain conditions (
[
18
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
), or give rise to trisections with a very high genus (
[
15
]
).

Here, we show how to obtain, from each gem of a compact 4-manifold with empty (resp. connected) boundary, a trisection (resp. gem-induced trisection), while keeping under control the genus of the splitting surface.

As a consequence, the following estimations of the trisection genus are proved to hold:

Theorem 1

Let
M
be a compact
4
-manifold whose boundary is either empty or a connected sum of sphere bundles over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
; then:

g_{T}(\bar{M})\leq\mathcal{G}(M)

where
\mathcal{G}(M)
denotes the regular genus of
M
, while

g_{T}(\bar{M})
is the trisection genus of the closed 4-manifold
\bar{M}
(uniquely) associated to
M
.

In particular, if
M
is closed, then:

g_{T}(M)\leq\mathcal{G}(M).

Hence, the trisection genus of a closed 4-manifold turns out to admit as an upper bound not only the regular genus of the closed 4-manifold itself, but also the regular genus of the compact 4-manifold consisting of handles up to index two of its handle-decomposition.

Moreover, the procedure enables to prove that a trisection diagram (on a surface with the estimated genus) can be directly obtained from any gem of a closed
4
-manifold, by making use of bicolored cycles of the gem itself: see Proposition
9
.

It is worthwhile to note that the entire discussion is carried out for compact 4-manifolds with connected boundary, by means of the concept of gem-induced trisection; so, all statements regarding closed manifolds are nothing but particular cases of more general ones which hold for the whole class.

More precisely, if
M
is such a compact 4-manifold, we get an upper and lower estimation of the so called
G-trisection genus
of
M
(the analogue of trisection genus for gem-induced trisections: see Definition
6
) in terms of the regular genus of
M
, of the Heegaard genus of its boundary
\partial M
and of the rank of the fundamental group of the associated singular manifold, obtained by capping off
\partial M
by a cone: see Corollary
8
.

Moreover, if either
\partial M
is a connected sum of sphere bundles over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
or
M
is simply-connected, starting from each gem of
M
we describe how to obtain a so called
G-trisection diagram
of
M
(a suitable generalization of trisection diagrams: see Definition
8
), consisting of bicolored cycles of the gem itself: see Proposition
9
and Proposition
11
.

The paper is structured as follows: after a brief review on gem theory (Section
2
), existing results on trisections arising from gems are recalled in Section
3
, together with the extension of the notion of
stabilization
in order to ensure the existence of gem-induced trisections also from gems of non-orientable manifolds and/or of compact manifolds with non-empty connected boundary; then, Section
4
is devoted to prove the estimation results concerning trisection genus and G-trisection genus, while trisection diagrams and G-trisection diagrams arising from gems are the subject of Section
5
.

Trisections arising from gems

The notion of trisection of a smooth closed orientable
4
-manifold has been introduced by Gay and Kirby in 2016 and more recently extended to the non-orientable case by Miller and Naylor. The following definition is a slight generalization of Gay and Kirby’s one for orientable manifolds (it is not

Definition 3

(
[

[

A trisection of genus

The trisection genus

A well-known result by Laudenbach and Poenaru for the orientable case (

As already pointed out in

Definition 8

(
[
6
]
)
A
G-trisection diagram
of genus
g
is a 4-tuple
(\Sigma;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
, where
\Sigma
is a genus
g
orientable surface and
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
are complete systems of curves on
\Sigma

-

(\Sigma;\alpha,\gamma)
and
(\Sigma;\beta,\gamma)
are (genus

-

is a (genus

Proposition 10

Let
M
be a simply-connected compact
4
-manifold with connected boundary.
Then, a G-trisection diagram
(\Sigma;\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
of genus
g
exists, which uniquely identifies
M
, such that
\mathcal{H}(\partial M)+rk(\widehat{M})\leq g\leq\mathcal{G}(M)
and
(\Sigma;\alpha,\beta)
is a Heegaard diagram of
\#_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\#\partial M
(
k\geq 0)
.

Proof.
Let
\Gamma\in G_{s}^{(4)}
be a gem of
M
that realizes its regular genus, i.e.
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{G}(M)
,
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3},4)
being a suitable cyclic permutation of the color set
\Delta_{4}
.
According to Proposition
5
and Proposition
7
, let
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)=(H^{\prime}_{0},H^{\prime}_{1},H^{\prime}_{2})
be the (generalized) gem-induced trisection of
M
obtained from the subdivision
\mathcal{T}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)=(H_{0},H_{1},H_{2})
via
k
stabilizations, with
rk(\widehat{M})\leq k\leq\rho_{\varepsilon}(\Gamma)-\rho_{\varepsilon}(\Gamma_{\hat{4}})
. Its genus, equal to
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\Gamma_{\hat{4}})+k
, obviously satisfies both bounds of the statement.

By construction, if
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are attaching curves on
\Sigma^{\prime}=H^{\prime}_{0}\cap H^{\prime}_{1}\cap H^{\prime}_{2}
of
H^{\prime}_{01}=H^{\prime}_{0}\cap H^{\prime}_{1}
,
H^{\prime}_{02}=H^{\prime}_{0}\cap H^{\prime}_{2}
and
H^{\prime}_{12}=H^{\prime}_{1}\cap H^{\prime}_{2}

[
4

On the other hand, given such a G-trisection diagram, let

3-dimensional handlebodies (corresponding to

and
H^{\prime}_{02}

7

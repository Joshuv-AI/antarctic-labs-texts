Oxygen-driven altermagnetic symmetry inducing d d -wave superconductivity in the cuprates and nickelates
Tom G. Saunderson, James F. Annett, Samir Lounis

Abstract
Since the discovery of cuprate high- T c T_{c} superconductivity, numerous theoretical frameworks have been proposed to explain its mechanism; Anderson’s RVB picture [ Science 235 , 1196–1198 (1987) ] and U(1) gauge theory [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 76 , 503–506 (1996) ] to name a few motivate a minimal one-band view that largely integrates out oxygen. By contrast, “altermagnetism” [ Phys. Rev. X 12 , 040501 (2022) ] yields a d d -wave–like k k -space magnetic texture from alternatingly rotated nonmagnetic cages; where La 2 CuO 4 (the parent of a high- T c T_{c} cuprate) is a prototypical example. As a proof of principle, we show in La 2 CuO 4 an alternating local pairing potential on the two Cu sublattices ( ± s ⁡ ( 𝐫 ) \pm s(\mathbf{r}) ) produces a nodal, d d -wave–like Δ ⁡ ( 𝐤 ) \Delta(\mathbf{k}) . As orthorhombic tilts are, however, not the driver (and even suppress superconductivity in nickelates [ Nature 621 , 493 (2023) )], we then show that the in-plane oxygen sublattice of CuO 2 /NiO 2 layers—ubiquitous in cuprates and nickelates—intrinsically realizes the same symmetry. Imposing an oxygen-centered , staggered s s pairing yields a d d -wave gap with perfect C 4 \mathrm{C}_{4} symmetry, demonstrated self-consistently in NdNiO 2 from first principles. Whilst the underlying mechanism that drives this kind of order is unclear, we provide speculations upon its origin. Further, this description of superconductivity enables the mapping of a real-space superconducting order parameter onto a lattice picture, allowing the potential for superconductivity and Hubbard physics to be treated on the same footing.

One possible explaination that could manifest the alternating, oxygen-centered pairing field employed above can be rationalized without committing to a purely “phononless” mechanism. In the linearized gap equation,

\Delta(\mathbf{k})=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}V(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\chi(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},T)\,\Delta(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}),

(2)

the net pairing kernel decomposes as
V(\mathbf{q})\!=\!V_{\mathrm{ph}}(\mathbf{q})-\mu^{*}
, where the Coulomb pseudopotential
\mu^{*}
is predominantly local (hence only weakly
\mathbf{q}
-dependent). Consequently,
\mu^{*}
penalizes the
s
-wave channel most strongly, while it couples only weakly to sign-changing form factors whose Fermi-surface average vanishes, such as
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}

momentum selective
—for example, enhanced for bond-stretching/buckling/breathing vibrations that produce a strongly
\mathbf{q}
-dependent
V_{\mathrm{ph}}(\mathbf{q})

2

s_{\pm}
or
d
-wave states over a conventional
s
-wave even when

is attractive in multiple channels. In real space, such a momentum-structured attraction projects onto the oxygen sublattice as an alternating nearest-neighbor pairing amplitude: adjacent O sites experience opposite phases because their Cu/Ni coordination alternates, precisely the altermagnetic motif identified above. This mapping explains why an oxygen-centered, staggered

\Delta_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{r})

\Delta(\mathbf{k})

\mu^{*}

To conclude, we have shown that La
2
CuO
4

d
-wave superconductivity generated from the Cu sites by enforcing an alternating pairing potential between the two Cu-centered octahedra. However, this Cu-based route is not ubiquitous and depends on the tilting octahedral cages of the La2CuO4, which have been previously experimentally proven to not be the fundamental driver of the superconducting order parameter in the cuprates. By contrast, in NdNiO
2

oxygen-sublattice
altermagnetic symmetry: the alternating Ni coordination of in-plane O sites naturally encodes the sign structure required for a
d
-wave state. While our microscopic rationale for why oxygen modes select this channel remains speculative, the corresponding oxygen-centered, staggered pairing field yields a perfect C
4

Because the same oxygen-site symmetry motif is intrinsic to
all

2
and NiO
2

\mathbf{k}
-space gap in a second-variational step.

Generalized intersection pairings on moduli spaces of vector bundles over a curve
Chenjing Bu, and, Young-Hoon Kiem

Abstract
We introduce the notion of a generalized intersection pairing for an Artin stack with a proper good moduli space and nonempty stable part. For the moduli stack of semistable bundles over a smooth projective curve, there are four known constructions by partial desingularization, parabolic bundles, stable pairs and wall crossing. In this paper, we compare all these generalized intersection pairings by establishing wall crossing formulas between them. Explicit computations for low rank cases are included.

2 Generalized intersection pairings on Artin stacks

For an Artin stack
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathbb{C}
and a cohomology class
\xi\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X})
, it is not known how to make sense of the intersection pairing

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\xi

in general, even when
\mathcal{X}
is smooth and admits a proper moduli space.
In this section, we collect methods to define generalized intersection pairings on Artin stacks (Definition
2.1
) by resolving the stacky points with infinite stabilizers or by wall crossings.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an Artin stack over
\mathbb{C}
with a proper good moduli space
\mathcal{X}\to X
. Suppose that
the Deligne–Mumford substack
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}
of stable points in
\mathcal{X}
is nonempty. Here a point
x\in\mathcal{X}
is called stable if
\pi^{-1}\pi(x)=\{x\}
and the automorphism group of
x
is finite.
A typical example is the GIT quotient

\mathcal{X}=[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]\longrightarrow Q{/\mspace{-5mu}/}G=X

(2.1)

of a projective scheme
Q
by a linearized action of a reductive group
G
with nonempty stable part
Q^{\mathrm{s}}\neq\varnothing
.

Let
\xi\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X};\mathbb{Q})
be a cohomology class.
When
\mathcal{X}
is smooth Deligne–Mumford, we have the intersection pairing

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\xi\in\mathbb{Q}

by using the fundamental class
[\mathcal{X}]
of
\mathcal{X}
(cf.
[
Ful
,
Vis
]
).
When
\mathcal{X}
is a proper Deligne–Mumford stack equipped with a virtual fundamental class
[\mathcal{X}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
(cf.
[
Li-Tian
,
Behrend-Fantechi
]
), we may consider the virtual intersection pairing

\int_{[\mathcal{X}]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\xi

and many modern enumerative invariants including the Gromov–Witten and Donaldson–Thomas invariants are defined in this way.

For convenience, we introduce the following.

Definition 2.1

A
generalized intersection pairing
on a smooth Artin stack
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathbb{C}
of dimension
d
with a proper good moduli space is a linear map

\int_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathrm{H}^{2d}(\mathcal{X})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}

which restricts to the ordinary integral

\int_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}}:\mathrm{H}^{2d}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}

of compactly supported cohomology classes on the stable part
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}
by using the natural map
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}})\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X})
.

Remark 2.2

(1) By the tautological duality
\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{X})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X}),\mathbb{Q})
, we may consider a generalized intersection pairing as a homology class in
\mathrm{H}_{2d}(\mathcal{X})
which restricts to the fundamental class of
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}
in
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{BM}}_{\smash{2d}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}})
.

(2) When
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-smooth, if we replace
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}
by its virtual fundamental class
[\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
in Definition
2.1
, we obtain the notion of a generalized virtual intersection pairing.

Down below, we collect known methods to define
generalized intersection pairings for Artin stacks.

2.1   Intersection pairings on partial desingularizations

w(\pi)
denote the topological Euler characteristic of fibres over
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}
.
Let
\mathrm{T}_{\pi}
be the relative tangent bundle of
\pi
and
e(\mathrm{T}_{\pi})
denote the top Chern class of
\mathrm{T}_{\pi}
.
When
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}
and

, we have the identity

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\xi=\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\frac{e(\mathrm{T}_{\pi})}{w(\pi)}\cup\xi|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}

(2.6)

by the projection formula.
So we can define a generalized intersection pairing as

\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\frac{e(\mathrm{T}_{\pi})}{w(\pi)}\cup\xi|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X}).

(2.7)

Remark 2.4

Note that
Eq.

2.6
was used in
[
Mar
]
in the form of

\int_{\mu^{-1}(0)/K}\xi=\int_{\mu^{-1}(0)/T}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}}{|W|}\cup\xi|_{\mu^{-1}(0)/T}

(2.8)

where
\mu:Q\to\mathbf{k}^{*}
is the moment map for a Hamiltonian action of a compact Lie group
K
with maximal torus
T
,
W
is the Weyl group of
K
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is the product of positive roots.
In
[
10
]
, Jeffrey and Kirwan
used

2.8
to compute the intersection pairings
when
\mathcal{X}
is a moduli space of stable bundles over a curve whose rank and degree are coprime.

In
[
Moch
,
JS
]
,
Eq.

2.7
was adopted for a generalized theory of Donaldson and Donaldson–Thomas invariants where
\mathcal{X}
is a moduli stack of semistable sheaves (or perfect complexes) and
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a moduli stack of
0^{+}
-stable pairs.

There are many choices of smooth fibrations
Eq.

2.5
and we have to deal with the following comparison issue.

Problem 2.5

Let
\pi^{\prime}:{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}
be another such smooth morphism.
Compute the difference

\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\frac{e(\mathrm{T}_{\pi})}{w(\pi)}\cup\xi|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}-\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}}\frac{e(\mathrm{T}_{\pi^{\prime}})}{w(\pi^{\prime})}\cup\xi|_{{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}}\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X}).

(2.9)

For instance, when
\mathcal{X}
is a moduli stack of semistable bundles over a smooth projective curve, we can think of
0^{+}
-stable pairs or
0^{+}
-stable parabolic bundles to construct smooth morphisms over
\mathcal{X}
. In §

, we will compute their difference by a wall crossing formula.

On the other hand,

2.7
is often easier to compute than
Eq.

2.3
because we can vary the stability for
{\mathcal{Y}}
and study wall crossings as in the cases of stable pairs or stable parabolic bundles.
So it is reasonable to consider the following.

Problem 2.6

Compute the difference

\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\xi|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}-\int_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\frac{e(\mathrm{T}_{\pi})}{w(\pi)}\cup\xi|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}

of
Eq.

2.3
and
Eq.

2.7
.

As we discussed,
Eq.

2.3
is canonical and
Eq.

2.7
is more suited for computation.
Note that this is the method of computing the generalized intersection pairings in
[
JKKW2
]
where
{\mathcal{Y}}
is the moduli space of stable parabolic bundles with parabolic weights close to
0
.

2.3   Enumerative invariants by wall crossings

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a (rigidified) moduli stack of objects in (a full subcategory of) an abelian category, semistable with respect to a weak stability condition. Suppose a list of assumptions in
[

hold. Then by
[

which gives us a generalized intersection pairing

\int_{[\mathcal{X}]_{\mathrm{inv}}}\xi\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X})

(2.10)

using the tautological pairing
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X})\otimes H_{*}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathbb{Q}

When
\mathcal{X}
is a moduli stack of semistable bundles over a curve, by
[
15
, Theorem 7.63]
, there is a wall crossing formula
Eq.

7.2
which compares
Eq.

2.10
with
Eq.

2.7
where
{\mathcal{Y}}
is the moduli stack of
0^{+}
-stable pairs
(E,s\in H^{0}(E))

In this paper, we will compare the above generalized intersection pairings for the moduli spaces of semistable bundles over a smooth projective curve of genus

2.4   Intersection cohomology pairings

It was proved in
[
17
, Theorem 5.3]
that there is a canonical embedding

\imath:\mathrm{IH}^{\bullet}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X})

of the (middle perversity) intersection cohomology of the good moduli space
X
into the cohomology of the stack
\mathcal{X}
when
\mathcal{X}=[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]
is the GIT quotient stack of a smooth projective variety
Q
by a linearized reductive group action with
Q^{\mathrm{s}}\neq\varnothing
and the action is almost balanced (cf.
[
17
, Definition 5.1]
).
For instance, all the moduli stacks of semistable vector bundles over a smooth projective curve satisfy this assumption (cf.
[
17
, Proposition 7.4]
).

The intersection cohomology
\mathrm{IH}^{\bullet}(X)
does not have a ring structure but it has an intersection pairing which gives us the Poincaré duality.
By
[
17
, Theorem 5.3]
, the intersection cohomology pairing can be computed by

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{\mathrm{IH}^{\bullet}(X)}=\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\imath(\alpha)\cup\imath(\beta)|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}=[\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}/G]
denotes the partial desingularization in §
2.1
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{IH}^{\bullet}(X)
of complementary degrees.

Considered as cycles on
X
,
\alpha
and
\beta
intersect at finitely many points on the stable part
X^{\mathrm{s}}=Q^{\mathrm{s}}/G
.
As
\imath
is defined by taking the inverse images of intersection homology cycles by the quotient map

Q^{\mathrm{ss}}\longrightarrow Q{/\mspace{-5mu}/}G=X,

\imath(\alpha)\cup\imath(\beta)
is represented by a 0-cycle which consists of finitely many points in
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}
.
In particular,
\imath(\alpha)\cup\imath(\beta)
is a compactly supported cohomology class.
By Definition
2.1
, the following is a direct consequence of
[
17
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Proposition 2.7

The intersection cohomology pairing can be computed by any generalized intersection pairing
\int_{\mathcal{X}}
by

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\imath(\alpha)\cup\imath(\beta)=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{\mathrm{IH}^{\bullet}(X)}

(2.11)

when
\mathcal{X}=[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]
is the GIT quotient stack of a smooth projective variety with an almost balanced action.

3 GIT moduli space of
n
points on projective line

In this section, we compute generalized intersection pairings on the GIT moduli space of

points on

up to the automorphism group

Let
P_{n}=(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}
with

and
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
\overline{G}=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
. We denote the projection to the
j
th factor by
\pi_{j}:P_{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let
T\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}
be the maximal torus in
G
.
The diagonal action of
G
on
P_{n}
can be linearized by the line bundle
{\mathscr{O}}(1,\cdots,1)
and we consider the semistable (resp. stable) part
P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}
(resp.
P_{n}^{\mathrm{s}}
) which consists of
n
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
where at most
n/2
(resp. less than
n/2
) points are allowed to coincide (cf.
[
MFK
]
). Let

{\mathcal{Y}}_{n}=[P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}/\overline{G}]\longrightarrow P_{n}{/\mspace{-5mu}/}\overline{G}=P_{n}{/\mspace{-5mu}/}G=Y_{n}

(3.1)

be the natural morphism from the quotient stack to the GIT quotient scheme.

In this section, we compute the intersection pairings of cohomology classes in
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{Y}}_{n})
.
By
[
Kir84
]
, we have a surjective restriction map

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(P_{n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\overline{G}}(P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{Y}}_{n}),\quad\eta\mapsto\eta|_{P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}}

(3.2)

and if we let
y_{i}=\pi_{i}^{*}c_{1}({\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1))
, we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(P_{n})\cong\mathbb{Q}[t^{2},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]/\langle t^{2}-y_{1}^{2},\cdots,t^{2}-y_{n}^{2}\rangle

where
t\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(BT)
denotes the first Chern class of the universal line bundle over
BT
. Hence it suffices to compute the generalized intersection pairings on
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n}
of

\xi=t^{2m}y_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots y_{n}^{a_{n}}|_{P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}}\quad\text{with}\quad 2m+\sum_{i}a_{i}=n-3.

(3.3)

We will often drop the restriction symbol
|_{P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}}
to simplify the notation.

3.1   Intersection pairings for
n
odd

When
n
is odd,
P_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}=P_{n}^{\mathrm{s}}
and
Eq.

3.1
is an isomorphism. By
[
JK0
,
GK
,
Mar
]
, the intersection pairngs

\int_{Y_{n}}\xi

can be computed as follows.
Let

\mu:P_{n}\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}^{*}

denote the moment map for the diagonal action of the compact group
K=\mathrm{SU}(2)

with
\mathbf{k}=\mathrm{Lie}(K)
such that we have an inclusion
\mu^{-1}(0)\hookrightarrow P^{\mathrm{ss}}_{n}
which induces the diffeomorphism

Y_{n}\cong\mu^{-1}(0)/\overline{K},\quad\overline{K}=K/\{\pm 1\}=\mathrm{PU}(2).

Let

\mu_{T}:P_{n}\,\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mu}}\,\mathbf{k}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

be the moment map for the action of the maximal torus
\mathrm{U}(1)\leq K
where the second arrow is the dual of the inclusion
\mathbb{R}=\mathrm{Lie(U}(1))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Lie}(K)=\mathbf{k}
.
In explicit terms, if we identify
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the unit sphere
S^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mu_{T}
is the sum of the
z
-coordinates.

As
0
is a regular value of
\mu
, we can reduce the computation to the torus quotient by

\int_{Y_{n}}\xi=\int_{\mu^{-1}(0)/\overline{K}}\xi=2\int_{\mu^{-1}(0)/K}\xi=-4\int_{\mu_{T}^{-1}(0)/T}t^{2}\xi|_{\mu_{T}^{-1}(0)}

(3.4)

by Martin’s trick in
[
19
]
.
The abelian quotient
\mu_{T}^{-1}(c)/T
may change only at odd integers which we call walls. Note that
\mu_{T}^{-1}(c)=\varnothing
for
c>n
.

Now the difference of the intersection pairings as
c
crosses a wall
c_{0}
is computed by the residue formula (cf.
[
GK
]
)

\int_{\mu_{T}^{-1}(c_{+})/T}t^{2}\xi|_{\mu^{-1}(c_{+})}-\int_{\mu_{T}^{-1}(c_{-})/T}t^{2}\xi|_{\mu^{-1}(c_{-})}=\mathsf{res}_{t=0}\int_{F_{c_{0}}}\frac{t^{2}\xi|_{F_{c_{0}}}}{e(N_{F_{c_{0}}/P_{n}})}

(3.5)

where
c_{-}<c_{0}<c_{+}
are close to
c_{0}
and
F_{c_{0}}
denotes the
T
-fixed point locus in
\mu^{-1}_{T}(c_{0})
.
Therefore, we obtain the formula

\int_{Y_{n}}\xi=4\sum_{c>0}\mathsf{res}_{t=0}\int_{F_{c}}\frac{t^{2}\xi|_{F_{c}}}{e(N_{F_{c}/P_{n}})}.

(3.6)

Let us denote the
T
-fixed points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by
p_{0}=0
and
p_{1}=\infty
. With the identification
\mathbb{P}^{1}=S^{2}
,
p_{0}
corresponds to the south pole and
p_{1}
to the north pole whose moment map values are
-1
and
1
respectively.
Then the
T
-fixed point locus in
P_{n}
consists of
2^{n}
points

\{p_{\varepsilon}=(p_{\varepsilon(1)},\cdots,p_{\varepsilon(n)})\in P_{n}\}

where
\varepsilon:\{1,\cdots,n\}\to\{0,1\}
is a map.
Let

|\varepsilon|=\sum_{i}\varepsilon(i),\quad|a|=\sum_{i}a_{i},\quad\varepsilon\cdot a=\sum_{i}a_{i}\varepsilon(i).

With this notation, we find that

\mu_{T}(p_{\varepsilon})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{T}(p_{\varepsilon(i)})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2\varepsilon(i)-1)=2|\varepsilon|-n.

Since
T
acts on the fibre of
{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
over
p_{i}
with weight
(-1)^{i}
for
i=0,1
,

y_{i}|_{p_{\varepsilon}}=(-1)^{\varepsilon(i)}t
and the restriction of
\xi
in
Eq.

3.3
to
p_{\varepsilon}
is

\xi|_{p_{\varepsilon}}=(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a}t^{n-3}.

(3.7)

The normal bundle to
p_{\varepsilon}
in
P_{n}
is the direct sum

\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{j}^{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)|_{p_{\varepsilon(j)}}

whose
T
-equivariant Euler class is

e(N_{p_{\varepsilon}/P_{n}})=(2t)^{n-|\varepsilon|}(-2t)^{|\varepsilon|}=(-1)^{|\varepsilon|}2^{n}t^{n}.

(3.8)

Combining
Eq.

3.7
and
Eq.

3.8
, we find that
Eq.

3.6
is

\int_{Y_{n}}\xi=\int_{Y_{n}}t^{2m}y_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots y_{n}^{a_{n}}=2^{2-n}\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|>n/2}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}.

(3.9)

In particular, when
n=3
,
\int_{Y_{3}}1=1
as
Y_{3}=\mathrm{pt}
.

3.2   Generalized intersection pairings for
n
even

When
n=2r
is even,
{\mathcal{Y}}_{n}={\mathcal{Y}}_{2r}
is an Artin stack for which we consider the generalized intersection pairings of

\xi=t^{2m}y_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots y_{2r}^{a_{2r}}\quad\text{with}\quad 2m+|a|=2r-3.

(3.10)

We note that
|a|
is odd.

3.2.1 Intersection pairings by a smooth resolution

The most obvious smooth resolution of
{\mathcal{Y}}_{2r}=[P_{2r}^{\mathrm{ss}}/\overline{G}]
is induced by the map

P_{2r+1}=(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{2r+1}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{2r}=P_{2r}

which forgets the last factor. Since the stable part
P_{2r+1}^{\mathrm{s}}
consists of
(2r+1)
-tuples where at most
r
points are allowed to coincide, the image of
P^{\mathrm{s}}_{2r+1}
by the forgetful map lies in the semistable part
P_{2r}^{\mathrm{ss}}
.
Therefore, we have a smooth morphism

Y_{2r+1}={\mathcal{Y}}_{2r+1}=[P_{2r+1}^{\mathrm{s}}/\overline{G}]\longrightarrow[P^{\mathrm{ss}}_{2r}/\overline{G}]={\mathcal{Y}}_{2r}

(3.11)

which is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over the stable part
{\mathcal{Y}}_{2r}^{\mathrm{s}}=[P_{2r}^{\mathrm{s}}/\overline{G}]=Y_{2r}^{\mathrm{s}}
.

The Euler class of the relative tangent bundle of
Eq.

3.11
is
2y_{2r+1}
and the topological Euler characteristic of the general fibre
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
2
. Therefore, the generalized intersection pairing of
Eq.

3.10
by the smooth resolution
Eq.

3.11
is

\int_{Y_{2r+1}}\xi\cdot\frac{2y_{2r+1}}{2}=\int_{Y_{2r+1}}\xi\cdot y_{2r+1}.

(3.12)

By
Eq.

3.9
, it is now straightforward to compute
Eq.

3.12
with
\xi
in
Eq.

3.10
. Indeed, for
\varepsilon:\{1,2,\cdots,2r\}\to\{0,1\}
, we have

\int_{Y_{2r+1}}\xi\cdot y_{2r+1}=2^{1-2r}\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|\geq r+1}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}+2^{1-2r}\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|\geq r}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}

(3.13)

where the first term on the right hand side is the sum over
\varepsilon
with the last point at
p_{0}=0\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
and the second term is over
\varepsilon
with the last point at
p_{1}=\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
When
|\varepsilon|=r
,
1-\varepsilon
is also a map to
\{0,1\}
with
|1-\varepsilon|=r
. Since

(1-\varepsilon)\cdot a+|1-\varepsilon|=|a|-\varepsilon\cdot a+2r-|\varepsilon|
and
|a|
is odd, we have

(-1)^{(1-\varepsilon)\cdot a+|1-\varepsilon|}=-(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}

and hence

\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|=r}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}=\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|=r}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+r}=0

(3.14)

Therefore we find that the generalized intersection pairing
Eq.

3.12
is

\int_{Y_{2r+1}}\xi\cdot\frac{2y_{2r+1}}{2}=2^{2-2r}\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|\geq r+1}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}.

(3.15)

Remark 3.1

We may consider
Y_{2r+1}
as the non-reductive GIT quotient

P_{2r}{/\mspace{-5mu}/}B=(P_{2r}\times(G/B)){/\mspace{-5mu}/}G

3.2.2 Intersection pairings by partial desingularization

Now we compute the generalized intersection pairing of
\xi
in

3.10
by using the partial desingularization
\widetilde{Y}_{2r}
of

.

As in §
3.1
, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
in
\mathrm{Lie}(T)^{*}=\mathbb{R}
, we can compute

2\int_{\mu^{-1}(\epsilon)/T}\xi\,\frac{2t}{2}=-4\int_{\mu_{T}^{-1}(\epsilon)/T}t^{2}\xi

(3.16)

by the resudue formula
Eq.

3.5
as

4\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|>r}\mathsf{res}_{t=0}\frac{t^{2}\xi|_{p_{\varepsilon}}}{e(N_{p_{\varepsilon}/P_{2r}})}=2^{2-2r}\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|\geq r+1}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}.

(3.17)

Observe that the strictly semistable points with reductive stabilizer groups in

are precisely the

p_{\varepsilon}
with

\varepsilon:\{1,2,\cdots,2r\}\longrightarrow\{0,1\},\quad|\varepsilon|=r.

Hence we blow up

along

and delete unstable points to get

\widetilde{Y}_{2r}=[\widetilde{P}_{2r}^{\mathrm{s}}/\overline{G}]=\widetilde{P}_{2r}{/\mspace{-5mu}/}G

The new

with

is

\mathbb{P}W_{\varepsilon}^{+}\sqcup\mathbb{P}W^{-}_{\varepsilon}
where

N_{Gp_{\varepsilon}/P_{2r}}=W^{+}_{\varepsilon}\oplus W_{\varepsilon}^{-}

is the decomposition of the normal space
N_{Gp_{\varepsilon}/P_{2r}}
to
Gp_{\varepsilon}
into positive and negative weight parts of the induced

Hence the only new wall crossing terms after the blowup are

4\sum_{|\varepsilon|=r}\mathsf{res}_{t=0}\int_{\mathbb{P}W_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\frac{t^{2}\xi|_{p_{\varepsilon}}}{e(N_{\mathbb{P}W^{+}_{\varepsilon}/\widetilde{P}_{2r}})}.

(3.18)

By

3.7
,
\xi|_{p_{\varepsilon}}=(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a}t^{2r-3}

N_{\mathbb{P}W^{+}_{\varepsilon}/\widetilde{P}_{2r}}={\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}W_{\varepsilon}^{+}}(1)\otimes W_{\varepsilon}^{-}\oplus{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}W_{\varepsilon}^{+}}(-1)\oplus N_{p_{\varepsilon}/Gp_{\varepsilon}},

Hence,
Eq.

3.18

4\sum_{|\varepsilon|=r}\mathsf{res}_{t=0}\int_{\mathbb{P}W_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\frac{(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a}t^{2r-1}}{4t^{2}(y-2t)^{r}}=\sum_{|\varepsilon|=r}\mathsf{res}_{t=0}\int_{\mathbb{P}^{r-2}}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+r}2^{-r}t^{r-3}\left(1-\frac{y}{2t}\right)^{-r}

\sum_{|\varepsilon|=r}\mathsf{res}_{t=0}\int_{\mathbb{P}^{r-2}}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+r}2^{-r}t^{r-3}\sum_{k}\binom{-r}{k}\left(\frac{-y}{2t}\right)^{k}

Since

3.14

By adding

\int_{\widetilde{Y}_{2r}}\xi|_{\widetilde{Y}_{2r}}=2^{2-2r}\sum_{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|\geq r+1}(-1)^{\varepsilon\cdot a+|\varepsilon|}.

(3.19)

3.2.4 Intersection cohomology pairings

The action of

\mathrm{IP}_{t}(Y_{2r})=\sum_{j}z^{j}\dim\mathrm{IH}^{j}(Y_{2r})=\frac{(1+z^{2})^{2r}}{1-z^{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{2r}{r}\frac{z^{2r-2}}{1-z^{2}}

because the restriction map

Moreover, by

4 Moduli spaces of vector bundles with extra structure

Let
C
be a connected smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
.
We consider the moduli stack

\mathcal{M}=\coprod_{(r,d)}\mathcal{M}_{r,d}

of vector bundles on
C
, where

(r,d)\in\{(0,0)\}\cup(\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z})
,
and each
\mathcal{M}_{r,d}
is the connected component
of vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
.
We also consider the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-rigidification

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}=\coprod_{(r,d)}\mathcal{M}^{\smash{\mathrm{rig}}}_{r,d}\ ,

obtained from
\mathcal{M}
by removing scalar automorphisms
from all stabilizer groups.
For each
(r,d)\neq(0,0)
,
we have the open substack

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\smash{\mathrm{rig}}}_{r,d}

of semistable vector bundles.
It is a smooth projective variety when

are coprime,
but is genuinely stacky when

are not coprime.

In the following, we introduce three variants of
the moduli stack

of vector bundles,
which all admit smooth morphisms to
\mathcal{M}
.
Namely, we consider moduli stacks of

parabolic bundles
,

pairs
, and

parabolic triples
.
These can all be used to define generalized intersection pairings
on
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}
,
and we will compare the results later on.

4.1   Parabolic bundles

Let
C
be a curve as above,
and let

be a
\mathbb{C}

We consider vector bundles on

with a parabolic structure at

[

,

]

Definition 4.1

Let

1
1

1

A

For a parabolic weight

We introduce the following terminology:

•

•

We say that

Again, when

and

We further recall from

5.1   Generalized intersection pairings by stable parabolic bundles

As a topological stack,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}

of unitary connections on a fixed hermitian vector bundle of rank

and degree

by the projective linear gauge group

Fix

and let

\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}\longrightarrow\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{p}=\mathrm{PU}(r)=:{K}.

The symplectic reduction of

by
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}
is the

in
[

]
which we denote by

. By the residual action of

, the coarse moduli space of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}
is now the symplectic reduction

of

by
K
where

denotes the moment map.

Let
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathbb{C}}_{0}
denote the complexifications of
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}
and
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}_{0}

By
[
AB
,
Kir84
]
, the moduli stack
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}

is an open substack of the holomorphic quotient

{\mathcal{A}}_{r,d}/\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathbb{C}}

and

E
and a framing

at

For sufficiently small general

in the positive Weyl chamber of

[

As

The Euler class of the relative tangent bundle of

of positive roots of

and a fibre over

gives us a generalized intersection pairing

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\quad\xi\mapsto\langle\xi\rangle_{\mathrm{JK}_{r,d}(c)}:=\int_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathrm{s}}_{r,d}(c)}\frac{e(\mathrm{T}_{\omega})}{w(\omega)}\cup\xi|_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathrm{s}}_{r,d}(c)}=\int_{\mu^{-1}(c)/T}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{r}}{r!}\cup\xi|_{\mu^{-1}(c)/T}.

(5.1)

The computation of
Eq.

5.1
was worked out by Jeffrey and Kirwan in

by computing the wall crossing terms as we vary

and then using the periodicity.
For convenience, we call
Eq.

5.1
the
Jeffrey–Kirwan pairing
.
Equivalently, we can think of the Jeffrey–Kirwan pairing
Eq.

5.1

as a homology class (cf. Definition

)

\mathrm{JK}_{r,d}(c)=\Omega(P_{r,d}(c))\in\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}})

(5.2)

where

denotes the fundamental class and

\Omega:=\omega_{*}\left((-)\cap\frac{e(\mathrm{T}_{\omega})}{w(\omega)}\right)=\omega_{*}\left((-)\cap\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{r}}{r!}\right):\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}({\mathcal{P}}^{\mathrm{s}}_{r,d}(c))\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}({\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}).

(5.3)

5.2   Generalized intersection pairings on partial desingularizations

By
[
Newstead
,
23
]
,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}
is the quotient stack

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}=[Q_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]

(5.4)

of the semistable part of a smooth projective scheme
Q_{r,d}

by a linearized action of a reductive group
G
.
In
[
18
]
, Kirwan introduced an algorithm to resolve stacky points with infinite stabilizers in
[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}/G]
by a sequence of smooth blowups.
It proceeds as follows. We first find the locus of closed points in
Q^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}
whose stabilizer group has maximal dimension. It turns out to be a smooth
G
-invariant closed subvariety of
Q^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}
.
We blow up along this subvariety and then remove unstable points with respect to a linearization which is close to the pullback of that for
Q_{r,d}
. Kirwan proved that the maximal dimension of stabilizer groups in the semistable part of the blown-up space is strictly smaller than before. Therefore by blowing up and taking out the unstable points finitely many times, we end up with a smooth quasi-projective variety
\widetilde{Q}^{s}_{r,d}
with only finite stabilizer groups and a morphism

\rho:{\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}}^{\mathrm{ss}}=[\widetilde{Q}^{s}_{r,d}/G]\longrightarrow[Q_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]={\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d}

(5.5)

where
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}}
is Deligne–Mumford with proper good moduli space
\widetilde{Q}_{r,d}{/\mspace{-5mu}/}G
.
By pulling back a cohomology class by
Eq.

5.5

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}\ ,\qquad\xi\longmapsto\langle\xi\rangle_{\mathrm{JKKW}_{r,d}}:=\int_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}}}\xi|_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}}}

(5.6)

which was computed in
[
9
]

and the associated homology class

\mathrm{JKKW}_{r,d}=\rho_{*}[{\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}}^{\mathrm{ss}}]\in\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}({\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{r,d})

(5.7)

will be called the

.

Because we know
Eq.

5.1
by the residue formulas in
[
10
, Proposition 8.4]
,
the computation of

5.6

\int_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}}}\xi|_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{ss}}}-\int_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathrm{s}}_{r,d}(c)}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{r}}{r!}\cup\xi|_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathrm{s}}_{r,d}(c)}

(5.8)

of

5.6

[

5.3   Comparison of the Jeffrey–Kirwan and JKKW pairings

Let
\tilde{\mu}:\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{ext}}\to\mathbf{k}^{*}
denote the induced moment map on the blown-up space
\widetilde{M}^{\mathrm{ext}}
obtained by the partial desingularization process applied to

\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\mathrm{s}}_{r,d}(0)=\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(0)/T

(5.9)

Equivalently, letting

Equivalently, we have

6 Generalized intersection pairings by stable pairs and the Joyce class

This section provides background material on
Joyce’s work
[
15
]

defining enumerative invariants in abelian categories,
as homology classes of moduli stacks,
which we call the
Joyce class
.
These classes satisfy wall-crossing formulae
expressed using the Joyce vertex algebra.

We also explain how our setting of
Section

4
,
especially the setting of parabolic bundles and triples,
fits into Joyce’s framework,
so that Joyce’s wall-crossing formulae
can be applied to parabolic bundles and triples.
These will be useful for comparing
the Joyce class for vector bundles on curves
with Jeffrey–Kirwan pairings later on.

6.1   The Joyce class

6.1.1 Joyce vertex algebras

Joyce vertex algebras
(
[
14
,
15
,
7
]
;
see also
[
6
]
)
are vertex algebra structures defined on
the homology of moduli stacks of objects in linear categories.
These vertex algebra structures are used to
express wall-crossing relations
for enumerative invariants living in the homology of moduli stacks,
which is the main motivation for constructing such vertex algebras.

Given a
\mathbb{C}
-linear category
\mathcal{A}
,
and a quasi-smooth moduli stack
\mathcal{X}
of objects in
\mathcal{A}
,
under certain conditions, the homology

V=\mathrm{H}_{\bullet+2\operatorname{vdim}}(\mathcal{X};\mathbb{Q})

admits a vertex algebra structure,
called the
Joyce vertex algebra
,
where
\operatorname{vdim}
denotes the virtual dimension of
\mathcal{X}
,
seen as a locally constant function on
\mathcal{X}
.
The vertex product is roughly given by

a_{1}(z_{1})\cdot a_{2}(z_{2})=\oplus_{*}\circ\mathrm{e}^{z_{1}D_{1}+z_{2}D_{2}}\Bigl((a_{1}\boxtimes a_{2})\cap e_{z}(\mathbb{T}_{\oplus})\Bigr)\ ,

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in V
are homology classes,

z_{1},z_{2}
are formal variables,

\oplus\colon\mathcal{X}^{2}\to\mathcal{X}

is the direct sum map,

\mathbb{T}_{\oplus}
is its relative tangent complex,
and
e_{z}(\mathbb{T}_{\oplus})

is the
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
-equivariant Euler class of
\mathbb{T}_{\oplus}
,
with
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
the equivariant parameters.
The operators
D_{1},D_{2}\colon V^{\otimes 2}\to V^{\otimes 2}

are given by
D\otimes 1
and
1\otimes D
, respectively,
where
D\colon V\to V
is the
translation operator
,
defined from the action

\odot\colon[*/\mathbb{C}^{\times}]\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}

by taking the generator in

\mathrm{H}_{2}([*/\mathbb{C}^{\times}];\mathbb{Q})

dual to the universal first Chern class in

\mathrm{H}^{2}([*/\mathbb{C}^{\times}];\mathbb{Q})
.
See
[
6
, Theorem 2.3.3]
for a precise statement.

It is a general fact that given a vertex algebra
V
,
the quotient

\bar{V}=V/D(V)

admits a Lie algebra structure,
with Lie bracket

[a_{1},a_{2}]=\mathrm{res}_{z_{1}=z_{2}}\bigl(a_{1}(z_{1})\cdot a_{2}(z_{2})\bigr)\ .

When
V=\mathrm{H}_{\bullet+2\operatorname{vdim}}(\mathcal{X};\mathbb{Q})

as above,
we often have a natural identification

\bar{V}\simeq\mathrm{H}_{\bullet+2\operatorname{vdim}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{rig}};\mathbb{Q})\ ,

where
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}
is the

\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-rigidification of
\mathcal{X}
.
See
[
15
, Theorem 4.8]
.
Note that the virtual dimension of
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}

differs from that of
\mathcal{X}
by
1
,
but as the variables
z_{i}
have degree
-2
,
the residue operator also has degree
-2
,
which cancels with this grading shift.

2
2

2

To make the grading consistent,
we use the unusual convention that
the virtual dimension of the point

\{0\}\subset\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}
is
1
.
But this is not important,
as we never use this part of the Lie algebra in this paper.

6.1.2 The Joyce class

Under the setting above,
under certain assumptions,
Joyce
[
15
, Theorem 5.7]

defines homological enumerative invariants,
which we call the
Joyce class
and denote by

J_{\alpha}(\tau)\in\mathrm{H}_{2\operatorname{vdim}}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}(\tau);\mathbb{Q})\ ,

where
\alpha\in\uppi_{0}(\mathcal{X})\setminus\{0\}

is a class corresponding to a connected component

\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{X}
,

\tau
is a stability condition,
and
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}(\tau)\subset\mathcal{X}^{\smash{\mathrm{rig}}}_{\alpha}

is the
\tau
-semistable locus.

For example, in the setting of parabolic bundles,
as in
Section

4
,

\alpha
is a class
\alpha=(r,d,f)
,
and
\tau
can be taken to be a slope function of the form
in
Definition

4.2
.
The settings of vector bundles, pairs, and parabolic triples, are similar.
We will verify in
Section

6.3

that these all satisfy Joyce’s assumptions.

The Joyce class satisfies the following properties:

•

If all semistable objects of class

\alpha
are stable,
so that the semistable locus

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}_{\alpha}

is a smooth (or quasi-smooth) proper algebraic space,
we have

J_{\alpha}(\tau)=[\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}(\tau)]^{(\mathrm{vir})}\ ,

the (virtual) fundamental class of
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}(\tau)
.

•

For two stability conditions
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
,
the classes

J_{\alpha}(\tau)
and
J_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})

are related by the
wall-crossing formula

J_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})=\sum_{\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}}{}\widetilde{U}(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{n};\tau,\tau^{\prime})\cdot[[\dotsc[J_{\alpha_{1}}(\tau),J_{\alpha_{2}}(\tau)],\dotsc],J_{\alpha_{n}}(\tau)]\ ,

where the sum is over all decompositions of
\alpha

into non-zero classes
\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{n}
,
and
\widetilde{U}({\cdots})
are combinatorial coefficients
defined in
Definition

6.2
below.
All classes in the formula are pushed forward from the semistable locus
to
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}_{\alpha}

or
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{rig}}_{\alpha_{i}}
,
and the Lie brackets
[-,-]
are defined using
the Joyce vertex algebra, as in
Section

6.1.1
.

In slightly more detail,
the class
J_{\alpha}(\tau)
is defined as follows.

Consider an auxiliary category
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

of
pairs
in
\mathcal{A}
,
with a moduli stack of objects
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.
For example, if
\mathcal{A}
is the category of vector bundles
(or parabolic bundles) on a curve,
as in
Section

4
,
then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
can be taken to be the category of
pairs (or parabolic triples).
For a stability condition
\tau
on
\mathcal{A}
,
there is an induced stability condition
\tau^{+}
on
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
,
and for the class
(\alpha,1)
of pairs,
the virtual class

J^{\prime}_{\alpha,1}(\tau^{+})=[\mathcal{X}^{\prime\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha,1}(\tau^{+})]^{\mathrm{vir}}

exists. The class
J_{\alpha}(\tau)
is then given by

J_{\alpha}(\tau)=\pi_{*}\biggl(J^{\prime}_{\alpha,1}(\tau^{+})\cap\frac{e(\mathbb{T}_{\pi})}{w(\pi)}\biggr)+\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\colon\\
n>1,\ \tau(\alpha_{i})=\tau(\alpha)\end{subarray}}\hskip-5.0pt\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\cdot\frac{r(\alpha_{1})}{r(\alpha)}\cdot[[\dotsc[J_{\alpha_{1}}(\tau),J_{\alpha_{2}}(\tau)],\dotsc],J_{\alpha_{n}}(\tau)]\ ,

where

\pi\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha,1}(\tau^{+})\to\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}(\tau)

\mathbb{T}_{\pi}
is its relative tangent complex,
which is a vector bundle in this case,
and

in the case of vector bundles and parabolic bundles on a curve,

can be taken to be the rank of the vector bundle.
The Lie brackets are defined from the Joyce vertex algebra

in
\mathcal{A}
.

.
See, again,

for details.

In particular,

in the sense of

,
as
\mathcal{X}^{\prime\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha,1}(\tau^{+})

restricts to a projective bundle over the stable locus

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha}(\tau)\subset\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}(\tau)
,
and all Lie brackets in

lie in the image of
\oplus_{*}
,
so they restrict to zero in

\mathrm{H}^{\smash{\mathrm{BM}}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha}(\tau))

because the image of
\oplus
is disjoint from
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha}(\tau)
.

7.1   Projection homomorphisms

We introduce two homomorphisms

7.1.1 The map
\Pi

We adopt the notation from

4

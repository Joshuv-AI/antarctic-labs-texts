Surgical outcomes in acute type A aortic dissection: the effect of 5-hour time window on malperfusion syndrome
Authors: De-bin Jiang, Chen-yu Zhu, Rui-xuan Zhang, Qi Chen, Tian-xin Lan, Lin-feng Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWhile a 5-hour time window for central aortic repair has been proposed to improve outcomes in acute type A aortic dissection (ATAAD) complicated by malperfusion, its efficacy relative to the number and nature of involved organs remains unclear.MethodsPatients who underwent surgical repair for ATAAD with malperfusion between January 2013 and December 2019 were enrolled and stratified by the number of malperfusion sites. Surgical outcomes were then compared between patients who received central repair within a 5-hour window and those treated beyond this timeframe.ResultsPreoperative organ malperfusion was present in 22.5% (237/1053) of patients. Among them, 62.4% (148/237) had single-organ involvement, while 37.6% (89/237) presented with multi-organ involvement (≥ 2 organs). An exploratory receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis identified a statistical cutoff of 4.5 h for predicting in-hospital mortality, conceptually supporting the 5-hour clinical window. Proximal malperfusion (coronary, cerebral) was common in the multi-organ group and was associated with a significantly worse prognosis. Central repair within the 5-hour window significantly improved long-term outcomes for patients with single-organ malperfusion. For those with multi-organ involvement, this strategy significantly improved early surgical outcomes, although the benefit diminished as the number of malperfused sites increased. Furthermore, for patients with proximal malperfusion, adhering to the 5-hour window helped mitigate irreversible damage to vital organs, increasing the likelihood of survival and functional recovery.ConclusionsThe 5-hour time window serves as a clear clinical intervention threshold, enabling clinicians to make rapid decisions during the acute phase, optimize treatment plans, and fundamentally improve patient outcomes.

Introduction

Preoperative organ malperfusion represents one of the most critical complications of acute type A aortic dissection (ATAAD) [1]. Its pathological basis involves either the obstruction of major branch arteries by the dissection flap or a sharp decline in perfusion pressure, both of which lead to end-organ ischemic injury. Clinical studies indicate that approximately 33% of patients with ATAAD present with varying degrees of organ malperfusion upon admission [2]. Among these cases, multi-organ malperfusion constitutes the most severe clinical manifestation. These patients experience significantly worse clinical outcomes, with in-hospital mortality demonstrating a stepwise increase corresponding to the number of affected organs. Furthermore, the degree of organ functional recovery shows a clear negative correlation with the duration of ischemia [3, 4].

Current clinical practice continues to evolve regarding the optimal management of ATAAD patients complicated by malperfusion. Although ACC/AHA guidelines strongly recommend emergency central aortic repair as the standard approach [1], and multiple studies have demonstrated the survival benefits of early intervention within 5 hours [5, 6], the tailored strategies for patients with varying degrees of malperfusion severity remain to be fully elucidated. While previous studies have provided valuable insights, a refined risk stratification based on the number of affected organs could further optimize clinical decision-making. When surgical timing is delayed due to prehospital factors, determining whether the 5-hour window principle still applies to patients with multi-organ malperfusion represents a challenging clinical scenario that requires further investigation.

To address this knowledge gap, we designed this study to stratify patients with ATAAD complicated by malperfusion according to their disease severity. Furthermore, we sought to evaluate the clinical efficacy of adhering to a 5-hour time window for central aortic repair within each distinct subgroup.

Methods

Ethics statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (Approval No. 2025KY070). The requirement for individual informed consent was waived due to the retrospective and anonymous nature of this study.

Study population

This study retrospectively collected data from adult patients diagnosed with ATAAD who underwent aortic surgery at our institution between January 2013 and December 2019. The time from symptom onset to admission was less than 24 h for all patients. Medical records were carefully reviewed, and the diagnosis of preoperative malperfusion was confirmed by two independent researchers. The primary diagnostic criteria were clinical signs or symptoms of organ dysfunction, and radiological findings were also taken into account when available. Malperfusion sites were examined systematically, focusing on the coronary, cerebral, spinal, mesenteric, renal, and peripheral systems (detailed definitions of malperfusion in each organ are listed in Fig. 1). In this study, the time interval to central aortic repair was strictly defined as the duration from the clinical or radiological diagnosis of malperfusion to the time of surgical skin incision.

Data and outcomes

Baseline characteristics, operative data, and in-hospital outcomes were collected from the hospital information system. Follow-up data, including survival and functional status, were obtained via outpatient clinic visits or telephone interviews. Patients who remained alive were censored at the end of the study period. The primary endpoints were in-hospital and long-term mortality. Secondary endpoints included any postoperative organ failure caused by ischemia that required additional intervention.

Operative procedure

Central aortic repair followed the “tear-oriented aortic resection” strategy. For patients requiring arch intervention, total arch replacement using a tetrafurcate vascular graft, with selective frozen elephant trunk implantation when indicated, was routinely performed in our center [7]. The standardized surgical procedure was conducted under moderate hypothermic circulatory arrest combined with selective antegrade cerebral perfusion. Briefly, after ascending aortic replacement and aortic arch opening, the distal main body of the tetrafurcate graft was first anastomosed to the descending aortic stump (for patients with extensive dissection involving the descending aorta, the FET was deployed into the true lumen beforehand, with the graft anastomosed proximal to the stent). Subsequently, the three branched limbs of the tetrafurcate graft were sequentially anastomosed to the brachiocephalic trunk, left common carotid artery, and left subclavian artery. Finally, the proximal end of the main graft was anastomosed to the ascending aortic graft or native aortic root.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted utilizing R software version 4.3.2. Continuous variables are presented as the mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), and categorical variables are presented as percentages. Comparisons between the groups were performed using t-tests for continuous variables and chi-squared tests for categorical variables. Missing values were imputed using the median values for continuous variables or the most commonly represented values for categorical variables. Preoperative risk factors for in-hospital mortality were identified with univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses. Variables with P-value < 0.20 in the univariable analysis were considered candidates for multivariable regression. To ensure the robustness of the multivariable model, multicollinearity among the included variables was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF). The discrimination of the model was evaluated by calculating the C-statistic (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve). Furthermore, model calibration and goodness-of-fit were tested using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test. The Kaplan–Meier (K-M) method was employed to estimate the survival, followed by Log-rank test for assessing the differences. Significance was determined as a two-tailed P ≤ 0.05. Furthermore, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was constructed to evaluate the predictive value of the surgical timing, and the optimal cutoff value was determined by maximizing the Youden index (Sensitivity + Specificity − 1).

Results

During the study period, 1,053 patients with ATAAD underwent surgical treatment at our institution. Of these, 237 (22.5%) were diagnosed with preoperative malperfusion. Among this subgroup, 148 patients (62.4%) had single-organ involvement, while 58 (24.5%), 22 (9.3%), and 9 (3.8%) presented with malperfusion in two, three, and four or more organs, respectively.

Baseline characteristics

Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Compared to those with single-organ malperfusion, patients in the multi-organ malperfusion group had a significantly higher proportion of females (32.6% vs. 20.3%, P = 0.034) and more extensive dissection involvement (supra-aortic vessels: 91.0% vs. 73.0%, P < 0.001; descending aorta or beyond: 96.6% vs. 85.1%, P = 0.005). They also presented with a more critical clinical status, evidenced by a higher incidence of circulatory collapse (24.7% vs. 10.1%, P = 0.003) and elevated blood lactate levels (2.3 ± 0.4 vs. 1.7 ± 0.6 mmol/L, P < 0.001). The mean time from malperfusion onset to operating room arrival was significantly longer in the multi-organ group than in the single-organ group (4.0 ± 2.8 h vs. 3.1 ± 2.1 h, P = 0.005).

Early outcomes

Intraoperative parameters and early outcomes are detailed in Table 2. The overall in-hospital mortality rate for patients with malperfusion was 32.1%, which correlated strongly with the number of affected organs (single- vs. multi-organ: 10.8% vs. 67.4%, P < 0.001). Specific mortality rates were 2.5% (10/408) for patients without malperfusion, 10.8% (16/148) for single-organ, 67.2% (39/58) for two organs, 59.1% (13/22) for three organs, and 88.9% (8/9) for four or more organs. In the multi-organ malperfusion group, central aortic repair within the 5-hour window was associated with significantly lower rates of both mortality (53.3% vs. 81.8%, P = 0.004) and stroke (46.7% vs. 72.7%, P = 0.012). To further explore the optimal surgical timing, an ROC curve analysis was performed regarding the time from malperfusion diagnosis to surgical incision and in-hospital mortality. The analysis identified a cutoff value of 4.5 h based on the maximum Youden index (AUC = 0.635, 95% CI: 0.534–0.736). While the AUC indicates a modest discriminative ability, this exploratory statistically derived threshold conceptually supports our predefined 5-hour clinical window. However, the protective benefit of the 5-hour window diminished progressively as the number of malperfused sites increased (Table 3). This benefit was most pronounced in patients with dual-organ malperfusion (in-hospital mortality: 51.9% vs. 80.6%, P = 0.020; stroke: 25.9% vs. 74.2%, P < 0.001; tracheotomy: 29.6% vs. 64.5%, P = 0.008).

Follow-up

The 1-, 5-, and 10-year survival rates were 59.3%, 55.9%, and 51.7% for patients with malperfusion, compared to 94.4%, 89.2%, and 86.3% for those without malperfusion (Fig. 2A). Long-term outcomes also diverged significantly based on the extent of malperfusion: 1-, 5-, and 10-year survival rates were 82.4%, 78.4%, and 73.0% in the single-organ group, versus 41.6%, 38.2%, and 34.8% in the multi-organ group. As illustrated in Fig. 2B, central repair within the 5-hour time window significantly improved overall long-term outcomes in patients with malperfusion (P < 0.001). This significant survival advantage was clearly observed in the single-organ subgroup (P = 0.005, Fig. 2C); however, it did not reach statistical significance in the multi-organ subgroup (P = 0.10, Fig. 2D).

Subgroup analysis

Further analysis focused on proximal malperfusion (coronary and cerebral), which occurred in 43.9% and 32.9% of all malperfusion patients, respectively. Strikingly, 87.6% of patients with multi-organ malperfusion exhibited involvement of at least one proximal organ. For coronary malperfusion, surgical intervention within 5 h of diagnosis resulted in an in-hospital mortality rate of 25.0%, which doubled to 50.0% when surgery was delayed beyond 5 h. This disparity is likely driven by the higher proportion of patients developing concomitant multi-organ malperfusion after the 5-hour mark (80.0% vs. 37.5%). Regarding cerebral malperfusion, patients treated within 5 h of symptom onset experienced a 42.3% rate of permanent neurological deficits and a 30.8% in-hospital mortality rate. When surgery was delayed beyond 5 h, the incidence of permanent neurological deficits rose to 73.2%, and mortality increased to 57.1%. Surgical outcomes were also strongly correlated with the severity of neurological symptoms. In-hospital mortality was 48.3% for patients presenting with delirium or somnolence and 66.7% for those in a stupor. Among the 11 comatose patients who underwent surgery, 6 were treated within the 5-hour window; despite this timely intervention, their mortality rate remained high at 66.7% (compared to 80.0% for those treated outside the window).

Risk factor analysis

As shown in Table 4, multivariable logistic regression identified the following independent risk factors for in-hospital mortality in patients with malperfusion: age ≥ 70 years (odds ratio [OR] 1.922, P = 0.021), circulatory collapse (OR 3.105, P < 0.001), coma (OR 5.034, P < 0.001), time from malperfusion diagnosis to surgical incision > 5 h (OR 3.600, P = 0.003), and iliac vessel dissection (OR 2.853, P = 0.013).

Discussion

The principal findings of this study are fourfold: (1) preoperative malperfusion, present in 22.5% of patients with ATAAD, confers a significantly poorer long-term prognosis; (2) over one-third of these patients progress to multi-organ malperfusion, which is associated with substantially worse surgical outcomes compared with single-organ involvement; (3) for patients with single-organ malperfusion, central aortic repair within a 5-hour window significantly improves long-term survival; and (4) for those with multi-organ malperfusion, adherence to this 5-hour window is critical, as it significantly reduces early mortality.

Our findings provide valuable insights into the varying severities and surgical outcomes of ATAAD complicated by malperfusion. For patients with single-organ malperfusion, expedited central aortic repair is both safe and effective. Intervention within the 5-hour window significantly enhances long-term survival, likely attributable to the rapid recovery of vital organ function. Conversely, multi-organ malperfusion represents a more severe clinical entity requiring urgent attention. While surgical treatment within 5 h significantly improves early outcomes, this protective effect diminishes progressively as the number of malperfused sites increases. Therefore, expeditious intervention is paramount to arrest the progression of systemic deterioration in these critically ill patients.

Surgical delay is widely recognized as a predictor of poor outcomes in ATAAD [8]. As posited by Narayan et al., time-related mortality is fundamentally a reflection of the consequences of malperfusion [9]. Patients who survive the initial 48 h before surgical repair may demonstrate better outcomes simply because they constitute a selectively lower-risk cohort without severe malperfusion [10]. Because malperfusion is a dynamic and progressive process, the time to intervention dictates the extent of ischemic injury. The 5-hour time window, initially proposed for ATAAD-induced cerebral malperfusion, has subsequently demonstrated efficacy across other vital organs. Although Western guidelines mandate emergency repair without delay, a definitive quantitative time standard has been lacking. Furthermore, our exploratory ROC curve analysis identified a statistical cutoff of 4.5 h for predicting in-hospital mortality. While acknowledging the limited predictive performance (AUC = 0.635) of this analysis, this finding conceptually aligns with the pragmatic 5-hour clinical threshold, suggesting that a critical inflection point for ischemic damage may occur within this timeframe. Establishing this ischemic symptom-based time window holds significant clinical utility, as it can guide pre-hospital transfer protocols and optimize triage when medical resources are constrained.

Notably, 87.6% of patients with multi-organ malperfusion in our cohort had involvement of at least one proximal organ. Proximal malperfusion—specifically cerebral and coronary ischemia—often acts as the primary catalyst for systemic deterioration. For these patients, the paramount objective is the rapid restoration of adequate perfusion. While an “upfront endovascular intervention followed by delayed open repair” strategy has gained traction recently [11], this approach is typically reserved for poor distal perfusion in highly selected individuals [12, 13]. Currently, there is a paucity of clinical evidence demonstrating that proximal malperfusion can be reliably managed with endovascular therapy alone [14]. Conversely, immediate open surgery rapidly and definitively restores blood flow to the coronary arteries and supra-aortic branches. When coronary malperfusion presents in isolation, central repair yields favorable outcomes with a low incidence of postoperative low cardiac output syndrome. However, concomitant multi-organ involvement significantly worsens the prognosis. Although direct causality between prolonged coronary malperfusion and subsequent distal organ hypoperfusion remains unproven, 80.0% of patients in our cohort with coronary ischemia lasting beyond 5 h presented with concomitant malperfusion of other organs. While isolated coronary ischemia may remain reversible for up to 12 hours [15], the time required for diagnosis (via ECG or CT angiography) and the duration of intraoperative cardiopulmonary bypass necessitate a much shorter pre-operative threshold; therefore, targeting a 5-hour window from symptom onset accommodates these logistical realities.

The concept of a therapeutic “time window” originated in the management of acute ischemic stroke to maximize salvageable neural tissue [16]. In our cohort, applying the 5-hour window to ATAAD complicated by cerebral malperfusion yielded positive results in promoting neurological recovery. However, surgical decision-making remains exceptionally challenging when patients present in a coma. The etiology of coma in ATAAD is multifactorial [17], and while the neurological prognosis is guarded, surgical restoration of cerebral blood flow continues to offer superior outcomes compared to medical management [18]. Although surgical outcomes for comatose patients in our cohort did not correlate as strongly with surgical timing as they did for other presentations, our overarching imperative remains the same: to rapidly restore perfusion to vital organs and control the systemic progression of the condition.

Limitation

One of the most prominent limitations is the retrospective and single-center design of this study. A prospective cohort study is necessary to validate the effectiveness of the 5-hour time window strategy. In addition, although this study included a sufficient overall number of patients, the sample size in certain subgroups (e.g., comatose patients and those with multi-organ malperfusion) was somewhat small. Specifically, the lack of statistical significance in long-term survival for patients with multi-organ malperfusion between the different time window groups may be attributed to the lack of statistical power due to the reduced number of long-term survivors, rather than a true absence of clinical effect.

Furthermore, as an observational study, our findings are inherently subject to selection and referral biases. Patients treated within 5 h may systematically differ from those treated later due to unmeasured factors such as prehospital logistics, diagnostic delays, or inter-hospital transfer times. Crucially, survivorship bias and confounding by indication cannot be entirely ruled out; patients experiencing delayed surgery must survive long enough to reach the operating room, potentially representing a selectively “lower-risk” cohort with less catastrophic initial presentations. Conversely, patients rapidly rushed to the operating room may represent those with the most severe hemodynamic instability. Consequently, the observed association between surgical timing and outcomes should be interpreted strictly as an association rather than a causal relationship. Future prospective registries utilizing time-dependent analyses (e.g., time-dependent Cox models) are warranted to better adjust for these dynamic pre-operative confounders.

Conclusions

In conclusion, malperfusion syndrome profoundly worsens outcomes in ATAAD. Stratifying patients by the extent and anatomical site of malperfusion is crucial for accurate prognostication. Our study validates the 5-hour time window for central aortic repair as a critical intervention threshold. Adhering to this timeframe is imperative: it enhances long-term survival in single-organ malperfusion and is vital for reducing early mortality in multi-organ involvement. This evidence-based target provides a clear clinical parameter for expediting surgical management, optimizing resource allocation, and ultimately improving survival in these critically ill patients.

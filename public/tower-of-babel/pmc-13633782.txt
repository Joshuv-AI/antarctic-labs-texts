Sawdust–plaster cast mixture versus traditional cast: Which type reduced pain and increases comfort and enhancing healing process among patients with twisting: A randomized controlled clinical trial
Authors: Rasool Raheem Lafta, Haider Mohammed Majeed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Musculoskeletal twisting injuries, such as strains and sprains, are common and can be treated using cast immobilization without the need for surgery. The study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of Sawdust–Plaster Cast Mixture Versus Traditional Cast: Which Type Reduced Pain and Increases Comfort and Enhancing the healing process among Patients with Twisting.MATERIALS AND METHODS:The study was conducted from May to August 2025 and used a single-blind, true experimental design. Sixty patients with twisting injuries were randomly assigned to either the intervention group (n = 29), which received the sawdust–plaster cast, or the control group (n = 31), which received a standard cast. Pain and comfort levels were assessed using a visual analog scale (VAS), and the healing duration was recorded. Statistical analysis was performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 30.RESULTS:Compared to the standard cast (control) group, patients in the sawdust–plaster cast mixture (intervention) group reported a noticeably higher reduction in discomfort. The intervention group’s mean pain score was 18.37 (P < 0.001), compared to 43.47 for the control group. Enhanced Comfort: Additionally, the intervention group reported feeling much more at ease. The intervention group’s mean comfort score was 24.40 (P = 0.003), while the control group’s was 36.21. For the sawdust–plaster cast mixture group, the healing period was noticeably shorter. The intervention group’s mean recovery time was 25.11 days (P = 0.004), compared to 36.26 days for the control group.CONCLUSION:The intervention showed a notable and clinically significant improvement in patient outcomes based on health policy criteria. This directly addressed important health policy goals for enhancing patient experience and care quality. Additionally, the average healing time decreased by more than 30% (36.26–25.11 days), demonstrating the effectiveness of the intervention in hastening patient recuperation and enhancing productivity. These results show that the intervention not only satisfies but also exceeds standards for efficient, patient-centered care.

Introduction

Musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) range of conditions affecting this system, originating from trauma, degeneration, overuse, infection, or autoimmune diseases.[1] Worldwide musculoskeletal disorders are believed to be the second most frequent cause of disability.[2] Trauma is indeed a significant global public health crisis, causing over 5 million deaths annually, with injuries also leading to tens of millions of non-fatal cases and long-term disability. While a specific global figure of 78% for musculoskeletal injuries is not readily available, these types of injuries, including fractures, sprains, and dislocations, are common consequences of traumatic events like accidents, falls, and motor vehicle incidents.[3] Plaster casts are the most commonly used method for treating fractures. Casts are used not only to immobilize fractures, but also to protect the affected part and relieve pain.[4] In the Winter Olympics, sports such as freestyle skiing, snowboarding, ski touring, bobsleigh, and ice hockey carry a particularly high risk of injury, ranging from wounds and hematomas to contusions and sprains. The most common sites of injury are the knees, spine/back, wrists, and hands.[5] Many studies suggest that alternative immobilization methods can achieve similar or better functional outcomes compared to traditional plaster casts.[6] Despite the emergence of new materials, traditional plaster is still preferred due to its compatibility with the skin, low cost, and easy availability. There is an urgent need to improve existing materials and also introduce new and efficient materials for long-term immobilization.[7] Acute musculoskeletal injuries in athletes, non-pharmacological approaches like physical therapy, neuromodulation, and psychological interventions are often recommended first, while pharmacological treatments include topical and oral nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) the research will use a descriptive synthesis with statistical and narrative analysis to evaluate the correlation between findings and research objectives in sports medicine.[8] Biocomposites of albumen-glycerol reinforced with natural fibers are being explored as a potential alternative for orthopedic casts, replacing traditional plaster of paris (POP). These natural fiber composites offer several benefits, including low density, low cost, and biodegradability. However, their primary drawbacks are poor compatibility between the fiber and the matrix, as well as high moisture absorption, which often requires chemical treatments to modify the fiber’s surface. This work describes the creation of such a composite, whose mechanical properties show great promise for orthopedic applications. The resulting natural fiber cast is considered a superior option to POP because it provides rigid fixation, takes less time to apply, and leads to fewer complications.[9] This study investigated early mobilization as an alternative to traditional immobilization for managing stable long bone fractures in patients aged 18–65. The research compared these two methods by measuring key outcomes like healing duration, complication rates, and patient satisfaction. The goal was to determine the effectiveness of early mobilization in diverse healthcare settings, from high-resource to low-resource environments.[10] This study aims to investigate the effectiveness of sawdust–plaster mixture on reducing pain, increasing comfort, and enhancing the healing process among patients with twisting.

Study novelty

This study investigates a sawdust–plaster cast mixture as an alternative to traditional casting materials for twisting injuries. While plaster casts remain standard, this bio-composite material could offer advantages. The novelty lies in comparing this mixture to conventional methods, focusing on patient outcomes like pain, comfort, and healing duration.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) design was carried out to investigate the effectiveness of Sawdust–Plaster Cast vs. Traditional Cast: A Randomized Trial on Pain, Comfort, and Healing in Twisting Injuries. The research was conducted at the Baghdad Al-Rusafa Health Department at Al-Kindi Teaching Hospital, and the Baghdad Health Department, Al-Karakh, at Al-Yarmouk Teaching Hospital.

Study participants and sampling

The study’s criteria guarantee that all eligible adults over 18 with twisting injuries had an equal chance of being chosen. A total of 60 patients were assessed and randomized into the intervention group (n = 29) and control group (n = 31), as illustrated in Figure 1.

Consort flow diagram of study[10]

Inclusion criteria: Patients must meet the following criteria: adult >18 years old, able to read and write, have a new twisting injury to the lower extremities or ankle sprain/strain, be free of psychiatric diagnoses, and be willing to participate in the study.

Exclusion criteria: Unwillingness of a patient to participate, physical disability, use of regular tranquilizer or sedative drugs, previous history of psychiatric disorders, patients with a confirmed fracture, pre-existing conditions that might affect healing, pain perception, or comfort (e.g., severe arthritis and neurological disorders), inability to follow the study protocol or complete the required assessments (e.g., due to cognitive impairment), and patients who have already received treatment for the current injury. Simple random sampling was used to assign participants to groups.

Randomization

The researchers coded all 60 participants and used yellow random selection and random assignment. is performed by a Random draw. The first Random draw is by placing two cards in a glass jar, writing on the first to participate and the other to not participate. Then the participant enters the second Random draw. There are also four cards on the table. He placed four cards on the table: the red color is an intervention group with twisting (A), the yellow color is a control group with twisting (a). After the card is drawn by the patient, it returns to its place to provide equal opportunities for all the samples when collected, until the required number is reached.

Statistical analysis method

The data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 28. The statistical methodologies employed for the purpose of data analysis and evaluation of outcomes encompassed the following: Descriptive Statistics to ascertain demographic characteristics. The paired t-test was utilized to identify statistically significant differences among the groups. The chi-square test was applied to examine the correlation relationships between sociodemographic variables and pain, comfort, and healing duration in patients with twisting.

Discussion

This study effectively validates its primary hypothesis, demonstrating the significant efficacy of the sawdust–plaster cast mixture in enhancing patient outcomes. The findings consistently indicate that the intervention group experienced a notable reduction in pain and discomfort, as well as a shorter healing duration, compared to the control group. These statistically significant results are consistent with an expanding body of literature on innovative, multimodal approaches to fracture and twisting injury management. The demographic data revealed a satisfactory match between the two groups in terms of sex and urban residence, which helps to mitigate potential confounding variables, a finding consistent with previous research.[11,12,13,14] However, notable differences in age, education, and income highlight crucial contextual factors.[15,16] The higher educational attainment and income in the intervention group could have influenced health literacy and treatment adherence, factors known to impact outcomes.[17,18] A key finding was the strong, consistent relationship between monthly income and both comfort and pain outcomes in both groups. This underscores socioeconomic status as a powerful determinant of health recovery, a finding corroborated by multiple recent studies linking lower income to poorer pain management and comfort levels.[19,20,21] The substantial reduction in pain scores in the intervention group (mean pain decreased from 43.47 to 18.37) is a compelling finding. This result aligns with research indicating that interventions combining novel materials or enhanced care can significantly improve pain outcomes in clinical populations.[22,23,24,25] The highly significant P-value (<0.001) provides robust evidence that the sawdust–plaster cast mixture effectively alleviated pain beyond what is expected from standard care. Similarly, the improved comfort levels observed in the intervention group parallel the pain relief findings. The significant improvement in comfort (mean scores from 36.21 to 24.40) aligns with studies emphasizing the importance of patient-centered outcomes.[26,27,28,29] Enhancing the quality of casts, such as improved symptom management and potentially lighter casts, is crucial for a patient’s quality of life during recovery. One of the most clinically significant findings is the acceleration of the healing process. The reduced healing duration in the intervention group mean of score (25.11 days vs. 36.26 days) which reflects a major clinical, economic, and psychosocial benefit. Quicker recovery reduces healthcare burden and allows patients to regain functional status sooner.[30,31] This suggests the sawdust–plaster cast provides not only symptomatic relief but also contributes to a more efficient and complete recovery. Finally, the study’s analysis of demographic associations with outcomes provides valuable context. The finding that age, sex, and educational level are significantly associated with comfort in the intervention group suggests that these factors may influence how individuals perceive or report discomfort.[32,33,34] Other variables like marital status and BMI did not show significant associations, which is consistent with some recent literature.[35,36,37,38,39] who found accelerated recovery times with innovative casting techniques in diabetic foot ulcer management. Moreover, the association between higher socioeconomic status and better outcomes in this study reflects the conclusions[40] emphasized the importance of innovation in casting techniques, highlighting that newer materials contribute to patient comfort, safety, and satisfaction—findings directly comparable to the improvements documented in the present study. From a material science perspective,[41,42] demonstrated that sawdust-based composites provide lightweight, durable, and cost-effective properties, supporting the clinical feasibility of using a sawdust–plaster mixture for orthopedic purposes.

Limitations and strengths

Despite the fact that our study used a small adult sample, the results were representative and statistically significant. Secondly, there is a lack of previous research studies on the scope of our research related to “assessing patients with twisting.”

Conclusion

The intervention showed a notable and clinically significant improvement in patient outcomes based on health policy criteria. This is because the percentage of patients experiencing “severe pain” decreased from 75.9% to 6.9% in the intervention group, demonstrating a considerable reduction in pain that goes beyond the typical objective of acute care pain management. Additionally, there was a considerable improvement in patient comfort, with severe discomfort decreasing from 69.0% to just 6.9%. This directly addressed important health policy goals for enhancing patient experience and care quality. Additionally, the average healing time decreased by more than 30% (36.26–25.11 days), demonstrating the effectiveness of the intervention in hastening patient recuperation and enhancing productivity. These results show that the intervention not only satisfies but also exceeds standards for efficient, patient-centered care.

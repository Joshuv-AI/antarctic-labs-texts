The 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -algebra bootstrap of 6d 𝒩 = ( 2 , 0 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,0) theories
Mitchell Woolley

Abstract
6d (2,0) SCFTs of type 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} have protected subsectors that were conjectured in [ 1 ] to be captured by 𝒲 𝔤 {\cal W}_{\mathfrak{g}} algebras. We write down the crossing equations for mixed four-point functions ⟨ S k 1 ​ S k 2 ​ S k 3 ​ S k 4 ⟩ \langle S_{k_{1}}S_{k_{2}}S_{k_{3}}S_{k_{4}}\rangle of 1/2-BPS operators S k i S_{k_{i}} in 6d (2,0) theories and detail how a certain twist reduces this system to a 2d meromorphic CFT multi-correlator bootstrap problem. We identify the relevant 6d (2,0) 𝒲 {\cal W} -algebras of type 𝔤 = { A N − 1 , D N } \mathfrak{g}=\{A_{N-1},D_{N}\} as truncations of 𝒲 1 + ∞ {\cal W}_{1+\infty} and solve OPE associativity conditions for their structure constants, both using OPEdefs and the holomorphic bootstrap of [ 2 ] . With this, we solve the multi-correlator bootstrap for twisted 6d four-point correlators ℱ k 1 ​ k 2 ​ k 3 ​ k 4 {\cal F}_{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}} involving all S k i S_{k_{i}} up to { k i } = 4 \{k_{i}\}=4 and extract closed-form expressions for 6d OPE coefficients. We describe the implications of our CFT data on conformal Regge trajectories of the (2,0) theories and finally, demonstrate the consistency of our results with protected higher-derivative corrections to graviton scattering in M-theory on A ​ d ​ S 7 × S 4 / ℤ 𝔬 AdS_{7}\times S^{4}/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{o}} .

7
Discussion and outlook

The main result of our paper was to extract explicit formulae for 6d (2,0) CFT data
\lambda_{k_{1}k_{2}{\cal X}}\lambda_{k_{3}k_{4}{\cal X}}
from the conjectured correspondence to
{\cal W}_{\mathfrak{g}}
algebras. Along the way, we outlined technicalities involving the superblocks of mixed correlators in (2,0) theories and their chiral algebra twist. We also elucidated finite-
N
aspects of 6d (2,0)
{\cal W}_{\mathfrak{g}}
algebras beyond the Virasoro algebra, including
{\cal W}_{D_{N}}
, which have not previously appeared in the 6d (2,0) literature. Using our explicit 6d data, we commented on generalizations of the Regge trajectory discussion in
[
13
]
for the correlators
\langle S_{3}S_{3}S_{p}S_{p}\rangle
. Finally, we demonstrated the consistency of the
{\cal W}_{\mathfrak{g}}
algebra conjecture for
\mathfrak{g}=A_{N-1},D_{N}
with protected higher derivative corrections in M-theory.

The most obvious future direction is to use our CFT data in a numerical multi-correlator bootstrap program analogous to the studies of 3d
\mathcal{N}=8
theories in
[
15
]
and 4d
{\cal N}=4
theories in
[
16
]
. This could involve either comparing bounds on
{\cal W}
-algebra data with our explicit formulae or inputting these formulae to obtain sharper bounds on data not captured by the
{\cal W}
-algebra. If one pursued the former strategy and the exact data saturated the numerical bounds, one could use the extremal functional method of
[
74
]
to extract the spectrum and approximations of OPE coefficients for all remaining CFT data. It would also be nice to devise a way to impose the Regge trajectory constraints that relate certain protected single-trace data to negative spin limits of unprotected
{\cal L}
data.

One obstacle is that the standard numerical bootstrap used in
[
11
,
12
]
converges substantially more slowly in 6d than in lower dimensions. While the semidefinite program solver
SDBP

[
10
]

has been upgraded
[
75
]
since the implementation in
[
12
]
, it would be interesting to attack the 6d (2,0) bootstrap using alternative numerical strategies. For example, the iterative application of the Lorentzian inversion formula in
[
13
]
could be applied to more complicated four-point functions where the
{\cal W}
-algebra seed data is more complicated and theory-dependent. One could also incorporate the dispersion relation of
[
76
]
in intermediate steps in the iteration, especially now that a mixed correlator dispersion kernel has been derived in
[
77
]
, which is needed given the form of reduced superblocks of
\langle S_{2}S_{2}S_{p}S_{p}\rangle
.

The reinforcement learning approach proposed in
[
78
,
79
]
was applied to 6d (2,0) theories in
[
14
]
. Their method was subsequently improved and complemented by another non-convex optimization algorithm in
[
80
]
. The implementation for a 1d defect CFT bootstrap problem in

[
81
]
demonstrated these strategies’ ability to reproduce rigorous results in
[
82
,
83
]
, especially once supplied with known theoretical input. One difference is that the theoretical input we propose in 6d are a set of explicit OPE coefficients, whereas the input in the defect problem
[
81
,
82
,
83
]
were integrated correlator constraints obtained from localization.

Beyond the conformal bootstrap, it would be interesting to understand how the holographic dual of
{\cal W}
-algebras arises from a top-down construction in 11d supergravity. The result should match the bottom-up picture given by minimal model holography and its even-spin counterpart
[
84
,
85
,
45
,
46
]
, where
\mathcal{W}_{N}
and
\mathcal{W}_{D_{N}}
appear naturally as boundary duals. A top-down derivation would involve a localization procedure using the cohomology of a certain Killing spinor, i.e. the bulk realization of the supercharge
\mathbf{Q}
. The analogous question was addressed in the duality between 4d
\mathcal{N}=4
SCFTs and type IIB string theory on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
in
[
86
]
where as a proof of concept, the authors performed this localization on a simplified setup involving 5d super Yang-Mills on a non-dynamical
AdS_{5}
background. A similar toy model is not available for
AdS_{7}
and recovering the bulk dual to 6d (2,0)
{\cal W}
-algebras will require a full 11d supergravity localization computation.

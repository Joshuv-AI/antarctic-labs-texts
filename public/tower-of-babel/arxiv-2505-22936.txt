Mean-Field Games with two-sided singular controls for Lévy processes
Facundo Oliú

Abstract
In a probabilistic mean-field game driven by a Lévy process an individual player aims to minimize a long-run discounted/ergodic cost by controlling the process through a pair of –increasing and decreasing– càdlàg processes, while he is interacting with an aggregate of players through the expectation of a controlled process by another pair of càdlàg processes. With the Brouwer fixed point theorem, we provide easy to check conditions for the existence of mean-field game equilibrium controls for both the discounted and ergodic control problem, characterize them as the solution of an integro-differential equation and show with a counterexample that uniqueness does not always holds. Furthermore, we study the convergence of equilibrium controls in the abelian sense. Finally, we treat the convergence of a finite-player game to this problem to justify our approach.



Keywords:

Lévy processes, Bounded-Variation Singular Control, Mean-Field-Games, Dynkin games.

The problems in which we add an MFG component fall under the class of

In stochastic bounded variation control, the displacement of the state caused by the control is of bounded variation. Moreover, we say that it is singular when the optimal control is the reflection inside an interval. The reason for this name is that for the Brownian motion (not necessarily for every Lévy processes) the trajectory of the reflected controls in intervals are singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Definition 4.1
.

(
Asmussen S., 2008
, Chapter V, Section 1)
,
Let

the times of occurrence of some phenomenon
(in this article a stopping time) and

(with the zero included) is called renewal process if

(but not necessarily

Definition 4.2
.

(
Asmussen S., 2008
, Chapter VI, Section 3)

A real–valued process

is called cumulative if
Z_{0}=0
and there exists
a renewal process
\{S_{n}\}
such that for any

\{Z_{S_{n}+t}-Z_{S_{n}}\}
is independent of
S_{0},\ S_{1},\dots,S_{n}
and

and for every

Z_{S_{n}+t}-Z_{S_{n}}=Z_{S_{m}+t}-Z_{S_{m}}\text{ in law.}

Theorem 4.3
.

(

, Chapter VI, Theorem 3.1)

Suppose

is a renewal process and

is an accumulative process. Moreover assume

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{Z_{t}}{t}=\frac{\operatorname{\mathbf{E}}(Z_{S_{1}})}{\operatorname{\mathbf{E}}(S_{2}-S_{1})}\ \text{a.s}\quad\text{if and only if }\operatorname{\mathbf{E}}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq S_{1}}|Z_{t}|\right)<\infty.

, we have
J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i})\leq C/2<C,
thus

\inf_{\eta\in\mathcal{B}^{K}_{0}}J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i}_{a,b})=\inf_{\eta\in\mathcal{C}}J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i}_{a,b}),\quad\text{ for all }i=1,\dots N,\ N\in\mathbb{N}.

Furthermore, observe that

\displaystyle J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\Lambda_{a,b})

\displaystyle=J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i}_{a,b})+\big(J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\Lambda_{a,b})-J(\eta,X^{a,b}_{\infty}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(J(\eta,X^{a,b}_{\infty}\big)-J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i}_{a,b})\big)

\displaystyle\leq J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i}_{a,b})+\big(J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\Lambda_{a,b})-J((U^{a,b},D^{a,b}),X^{a,b}_{\infty}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(J(\eta,X^{a,b}_{\infty})-J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i}_{a,b})\big).

Thus, to prove (i), it is enough to demostrate

\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal{C}}|J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\eta,\Lambda^{-i}_{a,b})-J(\eta,X^{a,b}_{\infty})|\leq r/2,

(28)

as the term
|J_{\infty,N}^{i}(\Lambda_{a,b})-J((U^{a,b},D^{a,b}),X^{a,b}_{\infty})|
is smaller or equal than the supremum defined in (
28
), because
(U^{a,b},D^{a,b})\in\mathcal{C}
. For that endeavor, we need to prove that

\left|\frac{1}{T}\operatorname{\mathbf{E}}_{x}\int_{0}^{T}\left(c(X^{i,\eta_{i}}_{s},\bar{f}^{a,b,-i})-c(X^{i,\eta_{i}}_{s},\operatorname{\mathbf{E}}_{x}(f(X^{a,b}_{\infty}))\right)\,ds\right|,

(29)

is smaller or equal to
r/2
.
With Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that
\eta^{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{K}
, we deduce the expression (
29
) is smaller or equal than

\left|\frac{K}{T}\operatorname{\mathbf{E}}_{x}\int_{0}^{T}\left(h(\bar{f}^{a,b,-i})-h(\operatorname{\mathbf{E}}_{x}(f(X^{a,b}_{\infty}))\right)^{2}\,ds\right|.

(30)

Finally, we use Hoeffding’s inequality for bounded random variables, the fact
|h(x)-h(y)|\leq b^{2}+a^{2}
and the definition of
\delta
to conclude (
30
) is smaller or equal than
r/2
, thus concluding the proof of the theorem.

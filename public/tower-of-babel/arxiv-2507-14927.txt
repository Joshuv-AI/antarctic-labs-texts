Introduction

Consider the linear nonhomogeneous first-order matrix differential equation involving left- and right-coefficient matrix multiplications:

\mathfrak{L}[X]\equiv\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}X(t)+A(t)X(t)+X(t)B(t)=F(t),\quad X(t_{0})=X_{0}.

(1)

Here
\ X(t)\,=\,\big[x_{ij}(t)\big]\,=\,\big[{\bf x}_{1}(t)\ \,{\bf x}_{2}(t)\ \,\cdots\ \,{\bf x}_{n}(t)\big]^{\rm T},\quad A(t)=\big[a_{ij}(t)\big]
,
B(t)=\big[b_{ij}(t)\big]\
and
\ F(t)=\big[f_{ij}(t)\big]\,=\,\big[{\bf f}_{1}(t)\ \,{\bf f}_{2}(t)\ \,\cdots\ \,{\bf f}_{n}(t)\big]^{\rm T}
are sufficiently smooth
n

n

[t_{0},T\rangle,
where
T

+\infty

homogeneous
case of this problem was introduced in a recent paper [3] wherein it was shown that if
X(t)
satisfies
\mathfrak{L}[X]=0
, the following generalized Liouville’s formula holds:

{\rm det}X(t)={\rm det}X_{0}\,{\rm e}^{-\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\{{\rm tr}\,A(t^{\prime})+{\rm tr}\,B(t^{\prime})\}\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}}.

(2)

When
B(t)=0
, formula (2) reduces to the classical identity due to Liouville (and Jacobi).
In our previous work [5], we considered the nonhomogeneous differential equation

The solution

\quad{\rm det}X(t)\ =\ {\rm e}^{-\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{\rm tr}\,A(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi}\cdot\Bigg\{\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}D_{X,F}(s)\,{\rm e}^{\,\int_{t_{0}}^{s}{\rm tr}\,A(\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi}\,{\rm d}s+{\rm det}X_{0}\Bigg\},

(4)

where
D_{X,F}(t)=\ \displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\rm det}\,\Big(X[{\bf x}_{j}\!\leftarrow{\bf f}_{j}](t)\Big)
and
\,X[{\bf x}_{j}\!\leftarrow{\bf f}_{j}](t)\,

In what follows we shall derive an integral formula which generalizes all of the above-mentioned identities. Standard notation from linear algebra (see [1], [2]) and differential equations (see [6]) is used throughout, except for our denoting the adjugate of a matrix

XX^{\#}=X^{\#}X=\det(X)I,\quad\dfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\det X(t)=\rm{tr}\Big[X^{\#}\dfrac{{\rm d}X}{{\rm d}t}\Big]\text{\ (Jacobi's Formula)},

\rm{tr}(XY)=\rm{tr}(YX),\quad\rm{tr}(\lambda X+\mu Y)=\lambda\rm{tr}(X)+\mu\rm{tr}(Y)\text{\ (linearity of the trace).}

2
The General Liouville-Jacobi Identity

Theorem 2

Suppose
X(t)
solves the problem (1). Then for every
\,t\in[t_{0},T\rangle
we have

{\rm det}X(t)={\rm e}^{-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{\rm tr}[A(\xi)+B(\xi)]{\rm d}\xi}\cdot\Big\{\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{\rm tr}[X^{\#}(s)F(s)]\,{\rm e}^{\,\int_{t_{0}}^{s}{\rm tr}[A(\xi)+B(\xi)]{\rm d}\xi}{\rm d}s+{\rm det}X_{0}\Big\}.

(5)

Proof.

The proof utilizes Jacobi’s Formula in a powerful way. Beginning with it and performing standard manipulations, we have the following chain of equalities:

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}{\rm det}X(t)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\Big[X^{\#}\dfrac{{\rm d}X}{{\rm d}t}\Big]={\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}(F-AX-XB)\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}F\big]-{\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}AX\big]-{\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}XB\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}F\big]-{\rm tr}\big[AXX^{\#}\big]-{\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}XB\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}F\big]-{\rm tr}\big[A(\det{X})I\big]-{\rm tr}\big[(\det{X})IB\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}F\big]-(\det{X}){\rm tr}A-(\det{X}){\rm tr}B={\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}F\big]-{\rm tr}[A+B]\det{X}.

Thus the linear first-order scalar differential equation

\dfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}{\rm det}X(t)+{\rm tr}[A+B]\det{X}={\rm tr}\big[X^{\#}F\big]

is obtained. Solving it by the standard application of an integrating factor yields (5) and concludes the proof.

One more result is obtained with the additional assumption of the invertibility of the variables’ matrix.

Theorem 3

Suppose that
X(t)
solves the problem (1) and furthermore that it is invertible. Then for every
\,t\in[t_{0},T\rangle
we have

{\rm det}X(t)\ =\ {\rm det}X_{0}\cdot{\rm e}^{\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{\rm tr}\big[X^{-1}(s)F(s)-[A(s)+B(s)]\big]{\rm d}s}.

(6)

Proof.

If
X
is invertible, Jacobi’s Formula implies
\dfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\det X(t)=\det X(t)\,\rm{tr}\Big[X^{-1}\dfrac{{\rm d}X}{{\rm d}t}\Big],

and (after manipulating
\rm{tr}\Big[X^{-1}\dfrac{{\rm d}X}{{\rm d}t}\Big]
similarly to the previous proof) we obtain

\dfrac{\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\det X(t)}{\det X(t)}={\rm tr}[X^{-1}(t)F(t)]-{\rm tr}[A(t)+B(t)].

(7)

Integrating the last equation establishes the result.

All applications given in [3] of the identity (2) which corresponds to the homogeneous problem
\mathfrak{L}[X]=0
will yield more general applications of the identity (5) corresponding to the nonhomogeneous problem
\mathfrak{L}[X]=F(t).

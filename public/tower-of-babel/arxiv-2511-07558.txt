A ballistic upper bound on the accumulation of bosonic on-site energies
Tomotaka Kuwahara, Marius Lemm, Carla Rubiliani

Abstract
. In this note, we study transport properties of the dynamics generated by translation-invariant and possibly long-ranged Hamiltonians of Bose-Hubbard type. For translation-invariant initial states with controlled boson density, we improve the known bound on the local repulsive energy at time t t from ⟨ n x 2 ⟩ t ≲ t 2 ​ d \langle n^{2}_{x}\rangle_{t}\lesssim t^{2d} to ⟨ n x 2 ⟩ t ≲ t d \langle n^{2}_{x}\rangle_{t}\lesssim t^{d} . This shows that bosonic on-site energies accumulate at most ballistically. Extending the result to higher moments would have powerful implications for bosonic Lieb-Robinson bounds. While previous approaches focused on controlling particle transport, our proof develops novel ASTLOs (adiabatic space-time localization observables) that are able to track the growth of local boson-boson correlations.



Dedicated to Israel Michael Sigal on the occasion of his 80th birthday.

1.
Introduction

In relativity, energy and information cannot travel faster than the speed of light. In other areas of physics, there are many instances of system-dependent speed limits that are much slower than the speed of light, such as the speed of sound.
This prompts a fundamental question: whether in quantum systems, even non-relativistic ones, similar speed limits emerge on the propagation of information or other physical quantities.
In a seminal work from 1972, Lieb and Robinson addressed this question, establishing bounds on the speed of quantum information propagation, for quantum spin systems which are governed by local and bounded interactions
[
24
]
.
In the early 2000s, Hastings and others employed and extended the Lieb-Robinson bounds (LRBs) as a powerful analytical tool to answer many long-standing open problems in mathematical physics
[
11
,
12
,
27
,
14
]
.
Since then, LRBs have gained widespread recognition, largely due to their versatility and strength in rigorous proofs; see e.g.,
[
13
,
3
,
25
]
and the reviews
[
10
,
5
]
.
As a natural consequence, the need to extend them to more general settings has grown into a research area of its own, e.g., the case of long-range bounded interactions was treated in
[
4
,
9
,
34
,
32
,
33
,
16
,
23
]
.

However, our understanding of LRBs for unbounded interactions remains incomplete. A natural and physically relevant source of unbounded interactions on lattices is the
Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{B-H}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}J_{x,y}b_{x}^{\dagger}b_{y}+U\sum_{x\in\Lambda}n_{x}(n_{x}-1)-\mu\sum_{x\in\Lambda}n_{x}.

While closely related oscillator models could still be treated in certain perturbative regimes
[
26
]
, for the Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian (
1.1
) the prior techniques break down due to the possibility of boson accumulation and thus the problem of information propagation must be fundamentally reconsidered.
Starting from 2011
[
28
]
, it became apparent that controlling particle propagation is a crucial input step to derive LRBs in the Bose-Hubbard setting, as it allows to quantitatively control the local accumulation of bosons, which is the source of the unbounded interactions. This led to a focus on deriving particle propagation
[
37
,
17
,
8
,
20
,
30
,
21
,
22
]
also for the related problem of macroscopic transport
[
20
,
7
,
35
,
6
]
.
Using particle propagation bounds, ballistic LRBs have been investigated and proven for various kinds of special initial states
[
28
,
37
,
8
,
20
,
30
]
; see also
[
18
,
36
]
. For the more general class of bounded-density initial states,
[
19
]
proved a bound that allows for information to accelerate in
d>1
— the velocity bound scales as
v\sim t^{d-1}
. This has raised the open question whether such accelerated information propagation is indeed possible in Bose-Hubbard systems or if it is in fact prevented under more stringent assumptions, e.g., on the initial state.
In this direction, a recent work
[
17
]
by two of us showed that energy conservation can meaningfully control information propagation further, showing that translation invariance and a strong local repulsion
n_{x}^{p}
, with
p
sufficiently large, provide an almost-ballistic bound on information propagation for any bounded energy density initial state.

In this work, we continue the central theme of
[
17
]
of tracking local energy accumulation. We prove a new bound on the accumulation of local repulsive energy under the assumption of translation invariance. We show

(1.2)

\displaystyle\big\langle\psi(t),n_{x}^{2}\psi(t)\big\rangle\lesssim t^{d}.

In terms of
t
-scaling, our result compares favorably to previous works
[
19
,
21
,
20
,
22
]
, where the following scaling was obtained

(1.3)

\displaystyle\big\langle\psi(t),n_{x}^{2}\psi(t)\big\rangle\lesssim t^{2d}.

The underlying reason is that the prior works only relied on bounding particle transport ballistically. Indeed, a constant speed of particle transport allows for a scenario where, given a fixed site
x
, all
\sim t^{d}
particles that are initially on the ball around
x
of radius
\sim t
may reach
x
within time
t
, which would indeed make
n_{x}^{2}\sim t^{2d}
. Our result (
1.2
) shows that this does not happen in the translation-invariant case. The strategy, roughly, is to track the local energy accumulation directly instead of relying on particle accumulation as an indicator. The implementation of this idea comes with another wrinkle, essentially because the time derivative of the local energy (commutator with the Hamiltonian) produces local correlation functions of the particle number that also need to be controlled in the proof.

More precisely, the proof relies on the so-called ASTLO (adiabatic space-time localization observables) method. It is inspired by the construction of propagation observables by Sigal and Soffer in the context of few-body scattering theory
[
29
]
, later refined in
[
31
,
15
]
. It was also extended to a model of quantum electrodynamics by Bony-Faupin-Sigal
[
2
]
and streamlined by Arbunich-Pusateri-Sigal-Soffer
[
1
]
.
The key step for this kind of approach is to obtain differential inequalities for propagation observables using commutator expansions inspired by semiclassical ideas with the time and distance as the effective large parameter.
A many-body version was developed in
[
7
,
8
]
and then refined in
[
20
,
30
,
21
,
22
]
particularly to long-ranged Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonians. In this context, the tracked observables were smoothed-out number operators that track particles moving across the lattice.
The form of the ASTLO, together with the CCR relations, allows to recover an important recursive structure for these operators to control their time evolution.

The main novelty of our proof is to construct and control new kind of ASTLO focused on capturing the evolution of local correlations, instead of particle number.
The key idea is that local on-site energy can be seen as an instance of
local particle-particle correlations
, and we can control the latter because it is dynamically approximately closed under commutators which allows to develop a similar recursive argument. While prior ASTLOs were
quadratic
in bosonic creation and annihilation operators, the new ASTLO we consider here to control local particle-particle correlations is instead
quartic
in them.

Finally, we emphasize that the previous works
[
19
,
21
,
20
,
22
]
proved general moment bounds

(1.4)

\displaystyle\big\langle\psi(t),n_{x}^{p}\psi(t)\big\rangle\lesssim t^{pd}.

It would be extremely interesting to extend an improvement of the form (
1.2
) to higher moments, as this
would improve the time scaling of the Lieb-Robinson velocity
\sim t^{d-1}
found in
[
19
]
and would thus shed light on the open question of when information acceleration can occur in bosonic systems. For more details, refer to the outlook following the statement of the main result.

Organization of the paper.

The paper is structured as follows.

•

In Section
2
, we describe our setup, specifically the type of Hamiltonian and Hilbert space we work with, together with some important notation that we will use across the paper. Afterwards, we specify the assumptions on the Hamiltonian and the initial state, and we state the main result, the bound on correlation propagation and its corollary, the ballistic accumulation of bosonic on-site energies.

2.
Setup and main result

2.1.
Setup and notation

Let
\Lambda\equiv\Lambda_{L}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a discrete torus of side length
L
, equipped with the Euclidean metric.
Consider the following bosonic Hamiltonian,

(2.1)

\displaystyle H\equiv H_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}J_{x,y}b_{x}^{\dagger}b_{y}+V,

acting on the bosonic Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
over the one particle Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
.
The matrix
J=(J_{x,y})_{x,y\in\Lambda}
is real and symmetric, and it is commonly referred to as the hopping matrix. Here
b_{x}^{\dagger}
and
b_{x}
are the bosonic creation and annihilation operators; therefore, they satisfy the canonical commutation relations, or CCR,

\displaystyle[b_{x},b_{y}]=[b_{x}^{\dagger},b_{y}^{\dagger}]=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad[b_{x},b_{y}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{x,y}.

The local number operator is defined as
n_{x}:=b_{x}^{\dagger}b_{x}
for every
x\in\Lambda
. We denote by
n_{0}
the local number operator supported at the origin of the lattice.
The potential is an arbitrary real function of the local number operators,
V=\Phi(\left\{n_{x}\right\}_{x\in\Lambda})
. Notice that (
1.1
) is a special case of (
2.1
). Given a certain region
X\subseteq\Lambda
,

\displaystyle N_{X}:=\sum_{x\in X}n_{x}

defines the number operator restricted to such a region
X
. For ease of notation we write
N
when
X=\Lambda
. It has been shown in
[
8
]
*App. A that Hamiltonians as in (
2.1
) are self-adjoint on

\mathcal{D}(H)=\{(\psi_{N})_{N\geq 0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}\,:\,\sum_{N\geq 0}\|H\psi_{N}\|^{2}<\infty\}.

2.2.
Assumptions on the Hamiltonian

The following are the assumptions we impose on the Hamiltonian.

Assumption 1: translation invariant Hamiltonian.

We assume the Hamiltonian to be translation invariant, i.e., for all
x\in\Lambda
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}H\mathcal{T}_{x}=H

where
\mathcal{T}_{x}
denotes the unitary translation operator by
x
,
(\mathcal{T}_{x}\psi)(y)=\psi(y+x\mod L)
. From now on, for ease of notation, we will write
x+y
instead of
x+y\mod L
when it is not ambiguous. Translation invariance for the Hamiltonian implies
J_{x,y}=J_{x+z,y+z}
for any
x,y,z\in\Lambda
.

Assumption 2: power-law decay of hopping matrix.

Additionally, we require polynomial decay of the hopping matrix, specifically, let
\alpha>3d+1
and set

(2.2)

\displaystyle C_{J}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\Lambda}\left\lvert J_{x,y}\right\rvert(1+\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)^{\alpha},

where
C_{J}
is independent of
\Lambda
.
It follows from (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\lvert J_{x,y}\right\rvert\leq C_{J}(1+\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)^{-\alpha},\qquad\forall x,\,y\in\Lambda.

Thus, the moments of the hopping matrix

(2.4)

\displaystyle\kappa^{(\beta+1)}:=\sup_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda}\left\lvert J_{x,y}\right\rvert\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\beta+1},

are bounded by a constant, uniformly in
\Lambda
, for all
\beta<\alpha-d-1
.
The first moment of the hopping matrix is a key quantity, and we denote it as follows

(2.5)

\displaystyle\kappa\equiv\kappa^{(1)}:=\sup_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda}\left\lvert J_{x,y}\right\rvert\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert.

2.3.
Assumptions on the initial states

As we anticipated in the introduction, when working with bosonic systems, we can only expect to obtain bounds on expectation values. This is because we cannot hope to control the full Hilbert space, as initial states with many bosons accumulating on a small number of lattice sites could lead to accelerated dynamics. Our result holds for initial states that satisfy the following assumptions.

Assumption 3: translation-invariant initial state.
We restrict our attention to translation invariant initial states
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(H)
. Then the assumption of translation invariance of Hamiltonian implies
{\psi_{t}=e^{-itH}\psi_{0}}
is translation invariant as well. We denote by
\langle\cdot\rangle_{t}=\langle\psi_{t},\cdot\psi_{t}\rangle
the corresponding expectation value.

Assumption 4: controlled-density initial state.
We require initial states to satisfy an assumption of controlled density, in other words, there exists
\lambda>0
, such that for
p=1,2
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\big\langle N_{B_{r}}^{p}\big\rangle_{0}\leq(\lambda r^{d})^{p},\qquad r\geq 0.

Here
B_{r}:=\left\{y\in\Lambda\text{ s.t. }\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq r\right\}
, denotes the discrete ball of radius
r
with center at the origin, and
B_{0}=\left\{0\right\}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{T}
the set of states
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(H)\cap\mathcal{D}(N)
that are translation invariant, and by
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{T}
the set of states in
\mathcal{D}_{T}
that additionally satisfy (
2.6
) for a given
\lambda>0
.
We remark that

•

These assumptions are physically relevant, as Mott states are included in this class of initial states.

•

Translation invariance is essential for our proof, as it is a fundamental ingredient for recovering the recursive structure in our inequalities, which is a key step.

2.4.
Main result

Our goal is to prove the following bound on local particle-particle correlations.

Theorem 2.1

(Bound on correlations)
.

Assume (
2.3
) holds for some
\alpha>3d+1
and define
\beta:=\lfloor\alpha-d-1\rfloor
. Consider
\lambda>0
and
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{T}
. Then, for any
v>2\kappa
there exists

\displaystyle C=(v,d,\lambda,\alpha,C_{J}),

such that, for all
R>r\geq 0
satisfying
R-r>\max\left\{v,1\right\}
, it holds

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{vt\leq R-r}\big\langle n_{0}N_{B_{r}}\big\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{C}{R-r}+\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\beta-2d}}\right)R^{d}\lambda^{2}.

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that for
r=0
and invoking translation invariance, (
2.7
) implies

(2.8)

\displaystyle\big\langle n_{x}^{2}\big\rangle_{t}\lesssim R^{d}\qquad\text{for every }x\in\Lambda.

Such an inequality can be interpreted as a bound on the local repulsive energy when considering a Hamiltonian of the kind (
1.1
).

Remark 2.3
.

The assumption on the decay of the hopping elements can be improved from
\alpha>3d+1
to
\alpha>d+1
by employing a multi-scale analysis as in
[
21
]
and substituting Theorem
4.4
with
[
22
]
*Theorem 2.1. Notice that in the latter, a lower bound on the the density of the initial states is required. Such an assumption can be lifted by post-processing.

Outlook.
As mentioned in the introduction, for
R\sim t
, our bound (
2.8
) leads to a
t^{d}
bound on
\big\langle n_{x}^{2}\big\rangle_{t}
, which compares favorably to the usual particle propagation bounds, which would give a bound of the form
\big\langle n_{x}^{2}\big\rangle_{t}\lesssim t^{2d}
(refer to
[
21
,
22
,
19
,
20
,
37
]
). If an improved
t
-scaling persists for higher moments of
n_{x}
, then this would have powerful implications for the Lieb-Robinson velocity of Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonians for controlled-density initial states in translation-invariant settings.

Indeed, a crucial step in
[
19
]
consists in controlling the spectral projector
\big\langle\Pi_{n_{x}\geq q}\big\rangle_{t}
onto the states with occupation number greater than a certain
q
to bound the error between the original and a suitably truncated dynamics. Now, suppose that one could show, e.g., that also
p
th moments accumulate at most ballistically, i.e.,
\big\langle n_{x}^{p}\big\rangle_{t}\lesssim t^{d}
for arbitrary high moment
p
under appropriate assumptions.
Then, by Markov’s inequality, one could obtain

\displaystyle\big\langle\Pi_{n_{x}\geq q}\big\rangle_{t}\leq\frac{\big\langle n_{x}^{p}\big\rangle_{t}}{q^{p}}\leq C_{p}\left(\frac{t^{d/p}}{q}\right)^{p}=C_{d/\epsilon}\left(\frac{t^{\epsilon}}{q}\right)^{d/\epsilon},

with
\epsilon=d/p
arbitrary small. This would imply that the local boson number can be effectively truncated at level
q\sim t^{\epsilon}
.
As the Lieb-Robinson velocity would be proportional to the local occupation number, one would obtain
v\sim q\sim t^{\epsilon}
for an arbitrarily small
\epsilon
, which would be almost ballistic and much less than the
v\sim t^{d-1}
bound shown in
[
19
]
. We emphasize that it is presently not clear under what circumstances one would physically expected a bound such as
\big\langle n_{x}^{p}\big\rangle_{t}\lesssim t^{d}
for all moments.

Finally, another interesting open question is whether the bound on the second moment proved here extends to non-translation-invariant settings.

3.
Correlation ASTLO

Consider
v>2\kappa
, where
\kappa
is defined as in (
2.5
), and define the following two quantities

(3.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{v}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\kappa+v}{2}\in(\kappa,v),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle:=v-\tilde{v}>0.

We introduce the class of cutoff functions, essential to build the ASTLO,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\equiv\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}:=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\left|\begin{aligned} &f\geq 0,\>f\equiv 0\text{ on }(-\infty,\epsilon/2],f\equiv 1\text{ on }[\epsilon,\infty)\\
&f^{\prime}\geq 0,\>\sqrt{f^{\prime}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\>\mathrm{supp}f^{\prime}\subset(\epsilon/2,\epsilon)\end{aligned}\right.\right\}.

Let
R>r\geq 0
and write
s:=(R-r)/v
. Then, for every
t\geq 0
and every function
\chi\in\mathcal{C}
we define a new function
\chi_{ts}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
by

\displaystyle\chi_{ts}(x)=\chi\left(\frac{R-\tilde{v}t-|x|}{s}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Notice that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\chi_{ts}^{\prime}(0)=0,

where, for ease of notation,
\chi^{\prime}_{ts}
has to be understood as
(\chi^{\prime})_{ts}
.
Equality (
3.4
) holds true since

\displaystyle\chi_{ts}^{\prime}(0)=\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{R-\tilde{v}t}{s}\right),

and it can be checked that
(R-\tilde{v}t)/s>\epsilon
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\chi^{\prime}\subset(\epsilon/2,\epsilon)
.
We define a new ASTLO (adiabatic space-time localization observable) that measures particle-particle correlations,

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{ts}:=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\chi_{ts}(x)n_{0}n_{x}.

It is essential to design the correct ASTLO. For example:

•

\sum_{x}\chi_{ts}(x)n_{x}^{2}
does not allow to close the recursive structure.

•

\sum_{x,y}\chi_{ts}(x)\chi_{ts}(y)n_{x}n_{y}
would lead to the usual time scaling as
t^{2d}
since already at initial time it is of size
r^{2d}
.

These two aspects create obstacles to controlling higher moments.

Proposition 3.1

(Geometric properties of the ASTLO)
.

For any
v>\kappa
,
\chi\in\mathcal{C}
,
R>r>0
,
\lambda>0
, and
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{T}
, the following holds

(3.5)

\displaystyle\big\langle n_{0}N_{B_{r}}\big\rangle_{t}\leq R^{d}\lambda^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{d\tau}\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{\tau s}\big\rangle_{\tau}d\tau,

for every
t\leq s
, where
s:=(R-r)/v
.

Proof.

Notice that, thanks to the properties of
\mathcal{C}
, it holds that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{r\geq\epsilon}\leq\chi(r)\leq\mathbbm{1}_{r\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}}.

Recalling the definitions for
\tilde{v}
(
3.1
) and
\epsilon
(
3.2
), for every
t\leq s
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq R-\tilde{v}t-\epsilon s\right\}\supset\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq R-\tilde{v}s-vs+\tilde{v}s\right\}=\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq R-vs\right\}=\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq r\right\}.

Lines (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) imply that the time-
t
ASTLO
\mathbb{A}_{ts}
satisfies, for every
t\leq s
, the following lower bound

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{ts}\big\rangle_{t}\geq\sum_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq R-\tilde{v}t-\epsilon s}\big\langle n_{0}n_{x}\big\rangle_{t}\geq\sum_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\,\leq\,r}\big\langle n_{0}n_{x}\big\rangle_{t}=\big\langle n_{0}N_{B_{r}}\big\rangle_{t}.

On the other hand, by (
3.6
), the time-zero ASTLO
\mathbb{A}_{0s}
satisfies

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{0s}\big\rangle_{0}\leq\sum_{|x|\leq R-\epsilon s/{2}}\big\langle n_{0}n_{x}\big\rangle_{0}\leq\sum_{|x|\leq R}\big\langle n_{0}n_{x}\big\rangle_{0}=\big\langle n_{0}N_{B_{R}}\big\rangle_{0},

where in the last inequality, we used the fact that the local number operator is positive. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

(3.8)

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{0s}\big\rangle_{0}\leq\sqrt{\big\langle n_{0}^{2}\big\rangle_{0}\big\langle N^{2}_{B_{R}}\big\rangle_{0}}.

Given the assumption of controlled density (
2.6
), inequality (
3.8
) implies

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{0s}\big\rangle_{0}\leq\lambda^{2}R^{d}.

That is, the time-zero ASTLO is bounded by a constant times
R^{d}
. We now combine these facts. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

(3.9)

\big\langle n_{0}N_{B_{r}}\big\rangle_{t}\leq\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{ts}\big\rangle_{t}\leq\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{ts}\big\rangle_{t}+\lambda^{2}R^{d}-\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{0s}\big\rangle_{0}=\lambda^{2}R^{d}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{d\tau}\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{\tau s}\big\rangle_{\tau}d\tau.

∎

The main work to derive Theorem
2.1
is to control the derivative appearing on the r.h.s. of (
3.5
), namely

\frac{d}{dt}\big\langle\mathbb{A}_{ts}\big\rangle_{t}=\big\langle\partial_{t}\mathbb{A}_{ts}+[iH,\mathbb{A}_{ts}]\big\rangle_{t}.

This is the content of the next and final section.

4.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.1
. We start by showing the following bound, displaying the necessary recursive structure. From now on, the constants can change from line to line, still remaining independent of
r,R
, the lattice size, and the total number of particles.

Proposition 4.1

(Differential inequality)
.

Assume (
2.3
) holds for some
\alpha>3d+1
, and denote
\beta=\lfloor\alpha-d-1\rfloor
.
Consider
\chi\in\mathcal{C}
. Then there exist
C=C(\chi,\alpha,d,C_{J})>0
and
\tilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{C}
such that for every
v>2\kappa
,
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{D_{T}}
, and
{R>r>0}
with
R-r>\max\left\{v,1\right\}
, the following holds

(4.1)

\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{t}\mathbb{A}_{ts}+[iH,\mathbb{A}_{ts}]\big\rangle_{t}\leq\frac{2\kappa-\tilde{v}}{s}\big\langle\mathbb{A}^{\prime}_{ts}\big\rangle_{t}+C\frac{1}{s^{2}}\big\langle\tilde{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}_{ts}\big\rangle_{t}+\frac{C}{s^{\beta+1}}R^{d}\big\langle n_{0}^{2}\big\rangle_{t}.

Recall
\tilde{v}
is as in (
3.1
) and we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{\prime}_{ts}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\chi^{\prime}_{ts}(x)n_{0}n_{x},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}_{ts}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}_{ts}(x)n_{0}n_{x}.

Remark 4.2
.

The assumption of translation invariance is necessary to prove Proposition
4.1
, as it allows to recover the ASTLO structure.

Combining Proposition
3.1
with Proposition
4.1
, and subsequently repeating such a procedure, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3

(Bootstrapping)
.

In the same setting as of Proposition
4.1
, there exists a constant
C=C(v,\alpha,d,C_{J})
such that, for every
\lambda>0
and
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{D_{T}^{\lambda}}
, the following holds

(4.2)

\displaystyle\big\langle n_{0}N_{B_{r}}\big\rangle_{t}\leq R^{d}\lambda^{2}\left(1+Cs^{-1}\right)+\frac{Ct}{s^{\beta+1}}R^{d}\sup_{vt\leq R-r}\big\langle n_{0}^{2}\big\rangle_{t}.

To control the remainder term appearing in (
4
), we employ the following theorem, whose proof can be found in
[
22
]
.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
21
]
*Theorem 2.1)
.

Let
p\geq 1
be an integer and assume that (
2.3
) holds with

KGPM: A Knowledge-Guided Predictive Model for Virtual Metering in Gas Wells
Authors: Jinman Li, Yang Lin, Chuangfeng An, Xiang Li, Tianhao Xiao, Luyao Hou, Jianing Qi, Jing Gong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractOne of the key points in the construction of smart oil and gas fields is the effective utilization of data. Virtual Flow Metering (VFM), as one of the representative research directions for digital transformation, can obtain real-time production from oil and gas wells without the need for additional field instrumentation, utilizing pressure and temperature data obtained from sensors and employing multiphase flow mechanism models. The data-driven VFM demonstrates a commendable capacity in capturing the nonlinear relationship between sensor data and flow rates, while circumventing the necessity for rigorous analysis of the underlying mechanistic processes. However, this approach also faces the problem of poor model interpretability and uncertainty in the reliability of the output results. To enhance the reliability of data-driven models, this study proposes a hybrid model that integrates knowledge into the data-driven model. We added a constraint containing prior knowledge to the Long Short-Term Memory neural network to guide data-driven model training and established a Knowledge-Guided Predictive Model (KGPM) suitable for VFM. Through a series of comparative experimental analyses, our proposed model has demonstrated exceptional proficiency in flow rate prediction, with a Mean Absolute Percentage Error of 3.211% and 1.141% for the two experimental wells. This research contributes to the optimization of VFM techniques, making a significant contribution to the efficient construction of intelligent oil and gas fields.

Introduction

With the rapid development of artificial intelligence, the digital and intelligent construction of the petroleum industry is accelerating. A pivotal question confronting the industry’s development is how to effectively transition from merely collecting production data to fully harnessing its potential. Virtual Flow Metering (VFM) serves as a supplementary or alternative metering solution to physical multiphase flow metering devices.1 Compared to physical measurement methods, VFM does not require the installation of additional hardware facilities. Instead, it utilizes temperature and pressure data obtained from sensors to calculate real-time production of oil and gas wells through multiphase flow mechanism models and monitor flow status in real-time.2 It is obvious that the development of more advanced VFM can be an effective solution for exploring the digital and intelligent transformation process of the petroleum industry.

Prior to the advent of VFM, physical multiphase flow meters were commonly used in industry for oil and gas flow rates measurement. These flow meters are typically installed at the wellhead and can estimate the flow rates of each phase without separation.3 However, multiphase flow meters are expensive to manufacture, and issues such as damage and malfunctions during subsequent use can significantly increase the operating costs of oil and gas fields.4 Compared to the aforementioned flow rates measurement methods which are costly and face certain challenges, VFM does not require additional hardware, thereby reducing costs. It also allows for real-time flow rates estimation, making it a good choice for current flow rates measurement practices.5 The traditional VFM is shown in Figure 1.

Schematic diagram of traditional VFM.

VFM primarily falls into two categories: VFM based on mechanistic models and VFM based on data-driven models.6 VFM based on mechanistic models involves the establishment of flow rates prediction models using fluid characteristic models, production system models, and the like. It necessitates strict adherence to first-principles and a clear understanding of the relevant physical relationships.7 Data-driven VFM establishes a mathematical relationship between data such as temperature, pressure and the flow rates. It does not require an accurate description of physical parameters, and its predictions do not directly reflect the characteristics of the process.8

The research on VFM based on mechanistic models began relatively early and was commercialized in the 1990s.9 Currently, most commercially available VFM systems are based on mechanistic models, such as the OLGA-online system, which incorporates VFM technology, and the widely used Rate&Phase system.10 In recent years, some researchers built two real-time virtual flow meters, the first utilizing the pressure drop across the entire well and the second utilizing the Choke Calculator and the local pressure drop at the valve, and correctly retrieved the choke performance curve using reverse engineering.11

Looking back at the research process of data-driven VFM, early researchers pioneered the use of neural network models to estimate multiphase fluid flow rates from pressure sensor data, and this pioneering work laid the foundation for subsequent developments.12 Subsequently, methods for constructing data-driven models using physical multiphase flow model outputs as data sources have been further explored, advancing the field further.13 As technology advances, researchers are beginning to experiment with applying feedforward neural network to real field data.14 In the same year, an Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) model has also been integrated into the data-driven VFM, enhancing the ability to process time series data.15 Later, the gradient boosting algorithm was introduced into the data-driven VFM and achieved accurate prediction of oil flow rates.16 In recent years, a probabilistic VFM based on Bayesian neural network have been proposed to improve the robustness of data-driven VFM.17 Subsequently, some researchers compared the error size and data volume requirements of Back Propagation (BP) neural network, LSTM neural network and Random Forest algorithm, they also established a transfer learning model.18 The dynamic neural network approach, particularly the model utilizing a nonlinear autoregressive network integrated with exogenous inputs, demonstrates a significant advantage in reducing flow rates prediction errors, surpassing the performance of multilayer perceptron.19 Meanwhile, considering the correlation between oil, water and gas flow rates, the researchers proposed a chained regression model.20 Later, in order to solve the inverse distribution problem of well flow rates and to improve the accuracy and generalization ability of the model, a linear regression combined with regularization model is proposed, which marks another advancement of data-driven VFM in solving complex engineering problems.21 The commercial application of data-driven VFM started relatively late and still requires extensive research to this day.

Neural networks hold substantial potential for application in data-driven VFM, yet extensive work is required to facilitate their widespread use in industrial VFM. Using knowledge and physical laws to guide the training of neural networks is a feasible research direction. In previous related research, a framework called Physics Constrained Model Correction (PCMC) has been developed with the aim of resolving uncertainty in model forms.22 In addition, the control equations of the physical model are integrated into the loss function to handle the constraints in the data implied by the partial differential equation model.23 Similarly, another later study used the control equations to construct the loss function, which in turn led to the application of physics-constrained deep learning.24 These applications demonstrate that knowledge and physical laws can be integrated into machine learning, thereby improving the accuracy and efficiency of machine learning algorithms.

Mechanistic models are constructed under the guidance of both physical laws and knowledge, and have a clear physical meaning that provides a reliable and rational explanation of the process. However, they face significant modeling challenges when dealing with complex flows, and unavoidable systematic errors can weaken the accuracy of the system. In contrast, data-driven models boast powerful nonlinear fitting capabilities, shorter development cycles, and lower costs, but they lack interpretability and require a certain quality and quantity of samples. Hybrid models represent an organic fusion of mechanistic and data-driven models, combining the advantages of the mechanistic model’s reflection of physical rules with the flexibility of data-driven methods, thereby facilitating the construction of reasonable structures.25,26 The advantages of the hybrid model are shown in Figure 2.

Advantages of establishing the mechanism and data hybrid model.

In order to enhance the performance of the VFM, this study proposes to integrate the knowledge into a data-driven model to construct a hybrid VFM, namely Knowledge-Guided Predictive Model (KGPM), to predict flow rates. KGPM essentially utilizes a knowledge-containing control equation to participate in the construction of the loss function, thereby guiding the training of the data-driven model and limiting the learning space of the data-driven model to make its predictions more consistent with prior knowledge. This enhances the reliability, interpretability, and generalization ability of the model. This study innovatively combines data-driven model with knowledge from real-world sources. Meanwhile, unlike most of the existing single-well virtual flow metering methods, this study takes into account the constraints of the total flow rates of multiple wells working simultaneously on the flow rates of a single well, which significantly improves the realistic fit of the flow rates prediction results. Therefore, this study can provide meaningful directions for future research. It should be noted that the control equation term in this study is proposed under the condition of multiple wells producing simultaneously and having total flow meter measurement data. Almost all of the current relevant production scenarios satisfy the above conditions, and therefore, the model developed in this study is applicable to most production sites. To verify the rationality of the method, this study uses actual production data from a condensate gas field in the South China Sea for experiments. The experimental results demonstrate that the model exhibits excellent performance, with a prediction error within an acceptable range. Furthermore, comparisons with several data-driven models have shown that our model clearly outperforms them.

Knowledge-Guided Predictive Model

In this section, KGPM for predicting flow rates is established based on gas well production data. The model adheres to the principle of using data-driven models as the primary method and knowledge as guidance. Control equations containing knowledge are added to the constraint conditions of the data-driven models, collectively forming the final loss function. The basic principle of this model is illustrated in Figure 3.

Basic principle of the model.

Figure 4 illustrates the construction and prediction process of KGPM. The process starts with getting the data and is immediately followed by rigorous data preprocessing, which includes dealing with missing values, outliers, and noise in the raw data to ensure data quality. Subsequently, the input features of the model are designed by carrying out feature engineering. After that, the data is partitioned into a training set and a test set according to a defined ratio for model training and evaluation. During the training process, the model minimizes the loss function designed in this study through an optimization algorithm to improve the prediction accuracy. Eventually, the model performance is evaluated through the test set. If the performance is poor, adjustments to the specific operations of the above stages may be considered.

Data Preprocessing

The various real-time production data may be influenced by multiple factors, leading to various errors, and may even cause abnormal program operation. Therefore, it is necessary to preprocess the data to ensure the performance of the model. Before preprocessing, a preliminary analysis of the data is performed to gain a basic understanding of the number of data entries and the number of features, and to determine the data storage format and whether there are empty columns. Data preprocessing involves the processing of missing values, handling of outliers, and noise reduction.

Experiments

Data Set and Evaluation Metric

The gas well data used in this study comes from two wells, A1 and A2, in a condensate gas field in the South China Sea. Well A1 has 6470 data points, and well A2 has 5399 data points. The data primarily consists of real-time production data obtained from the Distributed Control System (DCS), including pressure before the choke (PBC), temperature before the choke (TBC), pressure after the choke (PAC), temperature after the choke (TAC), choke opening (μ), downhole pressure (PDH), downhole temperature (TDH), as well as the total volumetric flow rates. Performance of KGPM

The framework of KGPM adopts the LSTM neural network, which inherently incorporates a specialized hidden layer structure through its LSTM cells, designed to capture long-term dependencies in the data. The input features are PBC, TBC, PAC, TAC, μ, PDH, TDH, ΔPC, ΔTC, ΔPDH–ON and ΔTDH–ON. The label used for prediction is the volumetric flow rate measured by the flow meter. Furthermore, the data set is divided into training and testing sets in a 7:3 ratio according to the chronological order.

The hyperparameters in the model are obtained through grid search method. Based on the search results and general rules for setting model parameters, the number of neurons in the hidden layer is set to 64. Given the complexity of the internal structure of LSTM neural network and other factors, the KGPM faces a high risk of overfitting. In order to effectively deal with this problem, an early stop mechanism is implemented so that the training process is aborted in time after the model achieves the desired training effect, thus avoiding the overfitting phenomenon triggered by overtraining. In addition, the loss function incorporating knowledge information also enhances the model’s resistance to overfitting to a certain extent. The basic model settings are consistent with those used in Section 3.2. For each traditional data-driven model, tests were conducted to compare their prediction outcomes with those of the KGPM, as well as with the flow meter. The error between each predicted value and its corresponding true value was computed. Based on these errors, violin plots were generated to visually represent the distribution of prediction accuracies. Results and Discussion

This study explores the integration and application of knowledge and data in the field of VFM based on the production prediction of gas wells. This study uses the approach of letting knowledge guide the data-driven model by adding a control equation that incorporates knowledge to the constraints of the data-driven model, which together form the final loss function. This approach aims to constrain the training of the data-driven model. KGPM is established, and an evaluation and comparative analysis of the model’s prediction results are conducted. The KGPM adopts the LSTM model as its foundation. After validation, the model has demonstrated excellent performance in flow rates prediction. The MAPE of the prediction for A1 well is 3.211%, while that for A2 well is 1.141%. When both wells are producing, the MAPE with the total flow meter is 2.548%. Furthermore, upon a comparative analysis with traditional data-driven models, the hybrid model manifests a marked superiority, underscoring the efficacy of the methodological approach that incorporates domain knowledge to inform and constrain data-driven modeling.

The KGPM established in this study can be applied to the construction of smart oil and gas fields to optimize the development and management of oil and gas fields through real-time monitoring and prediction of oil and gas well production. Meanwhile, KGPM can effectively reduce the metering cost as an alternative to the existing physical multiphase flow meters. It is worth mentioning that, although KGPM has achieved good prediction results theoretically, there are still some challenges if it is to be applied practically, such as the way of obtaining and processing real-time data, the updating and maintenance management of the model, and how to integrate with other systems. Meanwhile, the generalization ability of KGPM is currently unknown, which creates an obstacle for practical applications. All these existed difficulties need to be thought and tried more.

In future research, more advanced neural network structures and optimization algorithms can be explored to further improve the prediction accuracy and guarantee the robustness of the model. At the same time, the mechanism process can be explored more deeply and loss functions with more mechanism information can be constructed to constrain the training of data-driven models. To validate the generalization ability of KGPM, more well data with different geological conditions and production environments can be collected to train and test the model to assess its applicability in a wider range of scenarios.

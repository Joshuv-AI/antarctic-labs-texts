Between proper and square coloring of planar graphs, hardness and extremal graphs
Thomas Delépine

Abstract
( 1 a , 2 b ) (1^{a},2^{b}) -coloring is the problem of partitioning the vertex set of a graph into a a independent sets and b b 2-independent sets. This problem was recently introduced by Choi and Liu. We study the computational complexity and extremal properties of ( 1 a , 2 b ) (1^{a},2^{b}) -coloring . We prove that this problem is NP-Complete even when restricted to certain classes of planar graphs, and we also investigate the extremal values of b b when a a is fixed and in some ( a + 1 ) (a+1) -colorable classes of graphs. In particular, we prove that k k -degenerate graphs are ( 1 k , 2 O ⁡ ( n ) ) (1^{k},2^{O(\sqrt{n})}) -colorable, that triangle-free planar graphs are ( 1 2 , 2 O ⁡ ( n ) ) (1^{2},2^{O(\sqrt{n})}) -colorable and that planar graphs are ( 1 3 , 2 O ⁡ ( n ) ) (1^{3},2^{O(\sqrt{n})}) -colorable. All upper bounds obtained are tight up to a constant factor.

The problem of computing the chromatic number of a graph is one of the oldest and most widely studied problems in graph theory, from both an algorithmic and from a structural point of view. Those two points of views lead to very famous and celebrated results, such as the NP-Completeness of

However, the only known proofs of the latter result are computer-assisted due to the number of cases to study, and no combinatorial proof of the Four Color Theorem verifiable without computer assistance is known. In this direction, there has been recent interest regarding variants of the Four Color Theorem, and more precisely, strengthenings of the Five Color Theorem. For instance, it is proven in

Remark 17

Let

Lemma 18

-degenerate graph with a dominating set

Further remarks and open problems

Gap statements.

In Theorem
14
, we proved that distinguishing the
(1^{3},2^{1})
-colorable graphs and the
3
-colorable graphs is NP-Complete, even when restricted to planar graphs. We can prove a similar statement with an arbitrarily large gap, but for general graphs.

Proposition 32
.

For every integer
c\geq 3
,
k\geq 1
, and given a graph
G
that is either
c
-colorable or not
(1^{c},2^{k})
-colorable, it is NP-Complete to decide whether
G
is
c
-colorable.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph constructed from
G
such that for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, we add
k+1
copies
v_{1},\dots,v_{k+1}
of
v
to
G^{\prime}
and for every edge
uv\in E(G)
we add the edge
u_{i}v_{j}
to
G^{\prime}
for every
i
,
j\in\{1,\dots,k+1\}
. That is, we replace every edge of
G
by a copy of
K_{k+1,k+1}
.

•

if
G
is
c
-colorable, then so is
G^{\prime}
since, for every proper
c
-coloring of
G
, we can color each
v_{i}
with the color of
v
and this is a proper
c
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
.

•

if
G^{\prime}
is
(1^{c},2^{k})
-colorable, then let
uv\in E(G)
. There must exist
i
,
j\in\{1,k+1\}
such that
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
are colored with distance-1 colors. Since
u_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\prime})
, those distance-1 colors are distinct. We can therefore recolor every copy of
u
with the color of
u_{i}
and every copy of
v
with the color of
v_{j}
. By repeating this operation, we can transform the
(1^{c},2^{k})
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
into a proper
c
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
where each copies of
v
shares the same color, and we can easily retrieve a proper
c
-coloring of
G
from this proper
c
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
.

So
G
is
c
-colorable if and only if
G^{\prime}
is
(1^{c},2^{k})
-colorable if and only if
G^{\prime}
is
c
-colorable. The result follows from the NP-Completeness of
c
-coloring
when
c\geq 3
.
∎

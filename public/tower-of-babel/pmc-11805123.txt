Optimizing Assisted Reproductive Techniques: The Role of L-Carnitine, Acetyl-L-Carnitine, and IMSI in Managing High-Sperm DNA Fragmentation
Authors: Chitra Kamble, Aakash More, Sudhanshu Dakre
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis case reports a couple with infertility due to male factor asthenozoospermia and increased sperm DNA fragmentation along with a slightly thin endometrial lining of the female partner. The male partner received 1 g/day of acetyl-L-carnitine and 2 g/day of L-carnitine for 3 months, following which sperm motility was improved, and DFI was reduced from 45% to 20%. Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection (IMSI) was utilized during the in vitro fertilization (IVF) cycle to utilize sperms with optimal morphological characteristics for fertilization selectively. To improve endometrial receptivity, the female partner underwent estrogen and platelet-rich plasma (PRP) treatment and the endometrial thickness increased from 6.5 mm to 8 mm, improving endometrial receptivity. A frozen embryo transfer was then carried out after 1 month of the initial cycle. Two weeks after the transfer, a subsequent serum β-hCG level measured 302 mIU/mL, indicating a pregnancy. This case demonstrates the importance of the combined management of acetyl-L-carnitine and L-carnitine supplementation alongside IMSI and PRP in treating the complex etiology of both male and female infertility and leading to successful IVF pregnancy. The systematic approach to focus on aspects that included improving sperm quality as well as the endometrium was therefore important in yielding the result.

INTRODUCTION

Infertility refers to the inability to conceive after 1 year of unprotected sexual activities. About 85% of all infertile couples have a clinically recognizable fertility problem. Some of the primary factors of infertility include ovulatory disorder, male infertility, and tubal factors. The other 15% of infertile couples are characterized by “unexplained infertility.” Tobacco, alcohol, drugs, obesity, and other factors including environmental ones can have the worst impact on fertility.[1] Male infertility is multifactorial; it can be genetic, environmental, or due to lifestyle practices, medical ailment, or some medication. Recent research in male infertility has shown that idiopathic sperm abnormalities are still being detected in approximately 30% of cases.[2] Male infertility is characterized by the presence of abnormalities in semen volume, sperm morphology, sperm concentration, count, and motility. To assess male fertility potential, an analysis of those main parameters is needed.[3]

Nowadays, asthenozoospermia (AZS) has become one of the major causes of male infertility. AZS is described as the motility of sperm less than 50%. It can be caused by various reasons including the contact of sperms with the rubber, especially condoms, high temperature, spermicides, and so on.[4] Sperm motility completely depends on the intact flagellum, the presence of energy substrate, and the interaction of multiple signaling pathways; hence, genetic abnormalities affecting genes associated with flagellar buildup and motility control may lead to AZS. Therefore, it becomes highly important to know the genetic basis and pathophysiology of AZS.[5] Another leading cause of male infertility is sperm DNA fragmentation (SDF). SDF is defined as double- or single-strand breaks in the genome of spermatozoa. These breakages are usually chronic and may have a negative effect on male fertility. Increased SDF may also lead to recurrent implantation failure and negative IVF outcomes. Elevated SDF negatively affects pregnancy outcomes in assisted reproductive techniques such as intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), in vitro fertilization (IVF), and intrauterine insemination (IUI).[6] SDF can be triggered by extrinsic factors, including environmental toxins, smoking, heat exposure, and chemotherapeutic agents or by intrinsic factors such as impaired germ cell development, incomplete apoptosis, and oxidative stress.[7]

Several tests have been developed to positively measure DNA damage. These tests include sperm chromatin structure assay (SCSA), terminal deoxynucleotidyl-transferase-mediated dUTP nick end labeling assay (TUNEL), single-cell gel electrophoresis assay (Comet), and sperm chromatin dispersion (SCD) test. Although all of them are accepted as conventional tests for assessing SDF, they employed distinct methods to recognize DNA injury, which brings several advantages and disadvantages.[8] L-carnitine (LC) has been found to be involved in the oxidation of long-chain fatty acids, while acetyl-L carnitine (ALC), the active form of LC, has been revealed to have the antioxidant property that helps protect the mitochondria from metabolic toxins. ALC also maintains the structure of cell membranes and has antiapoptotic effects. In various studies, it was demonstrated that sperm motility, linearity, and concentration were shown to increase.[9]

CASE PRESENTATION

Patient information

A couple with a history of primary infertility visited an ART clinic. The age of the male was 36 years, and that of the female was 33 years. The couple underwent the necessary infertility workup to check for any abnormality.

Female investigation

The female underwent a hormonal evaluation, which disclosed normal luteinizing hormone (LH), anti-mullerian hormone (AMH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), indicating normal hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian function and sufficient ovarian reserve. Nevertheless, transvaginal sonography revealed a slightly thin endometrium with a thickness of 6.4 mm.

Male partner investigation

The male partner underwent a comprehensive fertility checkup, which included the examination of sperm DNA fragmentation, sperm motility, count, and morphology. Treatment

The physical and hormonal profiles of the female were found to be normal, except for her thin endometrium lining. However, the male was diagnosed with AZS and high SDF. To decrease the SDF index and increase sperm motility, a high oral dose of ALC (1 g/day) and LC (2 g/day) was administered for about 3 months, which is followed by semen analysis every month. These supplements are believed to have a positive effect on sperm mitochondrial membrane integrity and progressive motility and reduce reactive oxidative species (ROS), which in turn decreases sperm DNA fragmentation. After 3 months, a significant improvement was seen in sperm motility; also, sperm DNA fragmentation reduced from 45% to 25%, indicating a positive response to the treatment.

Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection (IMSI) was used to improve the likelihood of fertilization. This technique includes the updated versions of the ICSI and employs the high-power microscopy that enables the embryologist to choose sperms with good morphology and minimal DNA damage. This technique helped to choose the best quality sperm for fertilization, avoiding any chromosomal abnormality and increasing the chances of producing high-quality blastocyst. On the other hand, the female underwent ovum pickup, which resulted in a retrieval of 11 oocytes, out of which nine successfully fertilized, and resulting embryos (including three cleavages and four blastocysts) were frozen for the frozen embryo transfer.

One month post ovum pickup, the female partner underwent estrogen therapy combined with platelet-rich plasma (PRP) to treat her thin endometrium. After treatment, the thickness of her endometrial lining increased to 8 mm, offering a more suitable environment for the implantation of an embryo. One best-quality blastocyst was then thawed and the embryo transfer was done as presented in Figure 1. After transfer, the female was given rest for a few hours and then was allowed to go home.

Follow-up

Fourteen days post embryo transfer, the female partner was recommended to check her β-hCG level; the result showed a β-hCG level of 302 mIU/mL, indicating positive pregnancy. Some medications were then prescribed to her, and she was advised to visit the center for follow-ups including the blood test and ultrasound sonography in order to prevent complications in pregnancy. The doctors also advised her for routine exercises and avoid heavy lifting or heavy workups.

DISCUSSION

The β-hCG test done 14 days after the embryo transfer resulted in a positive pregnancy with a level of 302 mIU/mL. Normally, a β-hCG level of more than 100 mIU/mL at this stage indicates successful implantation and early pregnancy. This result reflects the effectiveness of the treatment strategy aimed at addressing both male factor infertility and the female’s slightly thin endometrium. The use of ALC and LC therapy for the male partner resulted in improved sperm motility and a reduction in DNA fragmentation. This was further enhanced by IMSI and facilitated the selection of the best-quality sperms for fertilization. On the female side, administration of estrogen and PRP contributed to increasing the endometrial thickness, which contributed to the proper implantation of embryo.

The value of 302 mIU/mL for β-hCG is a direct indication that all these measures succeeded in bringing a positive result to this pregnancy. This result also highlights the importance of personalized treatment in IVF, where both male and female factors were addressed with tailored therapies. β-hCG reporting in the following months will be essential to monitor the healthy progression of pregnancy. In general, this result depicts the effectiveness of the use of advanced reproductive technologies in surmounting difficulties in conception.

In a study by Edson Borges Jr et al.,[10] it was stated that the high level of SDF is linked with increased miscarriage rates, reduced implantation rates, and poor embryo development. Similarly, Sofoklis Stavros et al.[11] concluded that the SDF is one of the leading factor of infertility. It is associated with improper sperm function, abnormal sperm morphology, and low sperm motility and count. Moreover, SDF is linked with higher rates of aneuploidy, lower live birth rates, and pregnancy. In a study by Huan Li et al.,[12] significant improvement was seen in sperm count, morphology, motility, concentration, and semen volume after 1–2 months of LC treatment. Also, a considerable reduction was noted in SDF after 3 months of treatment. Similarly, in a study by S I Gamidov et al.,[13] the major increase in sperm characteristics, including motility and morphology, was seen after 3 months of ALC, LC, and alpha-lipoic acid treatment. Also, a significant reduction in SDF and oxidative stress was noted.

CONCLUSION

Ultimately, ALC and LC effects were proved to be effective in improving the essential sperm parameters, increasing sperm motility and morphology and decreasing DNA fragmentation, especially during the third month of therapy. The treatment also helped in reducing the oxidative stress, thus improving the general characteristics of the sperm. These studies therefore endorse ALC and LC as valid and beneficial treatment to promote spermatogenesis and enhance the management of causes of male infertility and therefore potentially raise the fertility rate among challenged couples.

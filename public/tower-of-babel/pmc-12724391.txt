Real‐World Insights Into the Clinical Characteristics and Survival Outcomes of Extranodal Peripheral T‐Cell Lymphomas: A Multiethnic Asian Cohort Study
Authors: Ryan Yee Shien Neo, Esther Wei Yin Chang, Soon Thye Lim, Jason Yongsheng Chan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundExtranodal peripheral T‐cell lymphomas (PTCLs) comprise a diverse group of lymphomas whose real‐world presentation and course are infrequently studied.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the clinical characteristics and survival outcomes of 50 patients at a tertiary cancer center in Singapore between 2002 and 2024.ResultsThe cohort comprised 26 males and 24 females, with a median age of 50 years (range 16–84 years). The 5‐year progression‐free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were 20.2% and 66.6%, respectively. Male sex (p = 0.03) and lack of complete response to initial treatment (p < 0.001) predicted poorer PFS. Age > 60 years (p < 0.001), nodal involvement (p = 0.02), gastrointestinal origin (p < 0.001), > 1 extranodal site of involvement (p = 0.02), and International Prognostic Index score > 2 (p < 0.001) predicted poorer OS. We also studied two subtypes: subcutaneous panniculitis‐like T‐cell lymphoma (SPTCL) (n = 7) and monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T‐cell lymphoma (MEITL) (n = 7). Patients with SPTCL had a median age of 29 years and presented with lesions mimicking reactive lymph nodes (43%), lipomas (29%), or erythema nodosum (14%); no mortality was noted in our series. Patients with MEITL had a median age of 68 years and presented with bowel emergencies including perforation (57%), bleeding (14%), and obstruction (14%); all patients progressed or relapsed, and 5 demised—3 with small bowel relapse, 2 with central nervous system relapse.ConclusionOur study provides insights into extranodal PTCLs and serves to guide our management of these rare lymphomas. Introduction

Peripheral T‐cell lymphomas (PTCLs) are a varied group of lymphomas derived from post‐thymic T‐cells and mature natural killer (NK) cells that display a diverse range of morphological patterns and clinical behaviors. In Western countries, PTCLs make up 5%–10% of non‐Hodgkin lymphomas, while the incidence is higher in Asia, at around 15%–20% [1, 2]. The International Consensus Classification and the World Health Organization classification recognize over 30 subtypes of PTCLs [3, 4]. An analysis of 1314 cases from 22 centers globally conducted by the International T‐Cell Lymphoma Project in 2008 found that PTCL‐not otherwise specified was the commonest PTCL in North America and Europe, while the most prevalent subtypes in Asia were adult T‐cell leukemia/lymphoma (ATLL) and extranodal NK/T‐cell lymphoma (ENKTL) [5]. These regional differences likely stem from various factors, including both intrinsic genetic variations and exposure to certain viruses or environmental factors [6, 7].

Extranodal PTCLs arise from outside the lymph nodes, and commonly involve the skin, gastrointestinal (GI) tract, liver, and spleen, and often present at an advanced stage [8]. Due to their relative infrequency, detailed data on their clinical presentation and course are limited. To address this, we conducted a cohort study at a major tertiary cancer center in Singapore to examine the clinical characteristics, survival outcomes, and prognostic factors for extranodal PTCLs. We additionally focused on two rare subtypes, each with its own distinctive clinical features and treatment outcomes: subcutaneous panniculitis‐like T‐cell lymphoma (SPTCL) and monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T‐cell lymphoma (MEITL).

Materials and Methods

Study Cohort

A retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of 50 patients with histologically‐proven extranodal PTCLs treated at the National Cancer Centre Singapore between 2002 and 2024. Data collected included age, sex, ethnicity, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS), extranodal PTCL subtype, clinical presentation, International Prognostic Index (IPI) score, progression‐free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). Where available, information on the administered therapies was gathered. ENKTL was excluded due to its distinct clinical presentation and established prognostic indices. This study was approved by the SingHealth Centralized Institutional Review Board, and informed consent for the use of biospecimens and data was obtained from all participants and/or their legal guardians in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All methods adhered to the relevant regulations and guidelines.

Statistical Analysis

PFS was defined as the time between the date of diagnosis and the earliest of the following: progression, relapse, or death from any cause. OS was defined as the time between the date of diagnosis and either death from any cause or last documented alive (for surviving patients). Patients who were either alive and free of progression/relapse or lost to follow‐up were censored at their last documented follow‐up date. Kaplan–Meier survival analyses were conducted to estimate the probabilities of PFS and OS over time. Multivariate Cox regression analyses and univariate log‐rank tests were performed to identify statistically significant prognostic factors affecting survival outcomes, and the results were expressed as hazard ratios (HR) and accompanied by 95% confidence intervals (CI). All statistical analyses were conducted assuming a two‐sided test with a significance level of 0.05, using MedCalc for Windows, version 22.023 (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium).

Results

Patient Demographics and Clinical Characteristics of the Overall Cohort

Our study cohort comprised 50 patients whose demographics and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 50 years (range 16–84 years), with 19 patients (38%) over age 60. There was a near‐equal proportion of females (48%) and males (52%). The majority of patients were Chinese (80%); the others were Malay (10%), Indian (2%), or of other ethnicities (8%). Most (96%) had an ECOG PS of 0 to 1. Various PTCL subtypes were included in our study (Figure 1). There were 20 patients (40%) with mycosis fungoides (MF), 10 (20%) with primary cutaneous anaplastic large cell lymphoma (PC‐ALCL), 7 (14%) with SPTCL, 7 (14%) with MEITL, 2 (4%) with gamma/delta T‐cell lymphoma (GDTCL), 2 (4%) with primary cutaneous CD4+ small/medium T‐cell lymphoproliferative disorder (PCSMLPD), and 2 (4%) with primary cutaneous peripheral T‐cell lymphoma, not otherwise specified (PC‐PTCL‐NOS). More than two‐thirds of the patients (68%) were Ann Arbor Stage 4 at diagnosis. Based on IPI, however, 19 patients (38%) were low risk, 15 (30%) were low‐intermediate risk, 12 (24%) were high‐intermediate risk, and 4 (8%) were high risk. In addition, most cases had no B symptoms (84%), were limited to a single extranodal site of involvement (82%), and involved neither the bone marrow (92%) nor lymph nodes (76%). Treatment Outcomes and Survival Analyses of the Overall Cohort

Following first‐line treatment, nearly half of the patients (46%) achieved complete response (CR), and about one‐quarter each achieved partial response (PR) (24%) and stable disease (SD)/progression of disease (PD) (26%) (Figure 3). The remaining two patients (4%) were lost to follow‐up prior to documentation of their treatment response. 5‐year PFS and OS was 20.2% and 66.6%, respectively (Figure 4).

Multivariate analyses showed that male sex (p = 0.03) and lack of CR to initial treatment (p < 0.001) predicted poorer PFS, while age above 60 years (p < 0.001) and nodal involvement (p = 0.02) predicted poorer OS. On univariate analyses, other negative prognostic factors for OS were GI origin (p < 0.001), more than one extranodal site of involvement (p = 0.02), and IPI risk score greater than 2 (p < 0.001). In our subsequent analyses, we focused on SPTCL and MEITL, both rare extranodal PTCL subtypes.

SPTCL

Table 4 summarizes the demographics and clinical characteristics of the patients with SPTCL in our cohort. The median age was 29 years (range 21–50 years), and most (86%) were Chinese females. All had an ECOG PS of 0. Patients were often initially misdiagnosed with commoner disorders such as reactive lymph nodes (43%), lipomas (29%), or erythema nodosum (14%). Various sites of subcutaneous tissue were affected, including the neck, chest, abdomen, and limbs. With regard to extracutaneous involvement, 1 patient (14%) had regional metastasis to the external iliac lymph nodes (Figure S1), and another 1 (14%) had distant metastasis to the omentum (Figure S2). A total of 4 patients (57%) had associated B symptoms such as fevers and weight loss at initial presentation. More than half of the patients (57%) had low IPI risk at diagnosis, 2 (29%) had low‐intermediate risk, and 1 (14%) had high‐intermediate risk. None had high risk.

Four patients (57%) had a low‐risk IPI score of 1, and they received an initial treatment regimen comprising tapering doses of prednisolone. Treatment outcomes were generally favorable—2 of these patients (50%) achieved and maintained CR, and 1 (25%) achieved PR but relapsed at 11.2 months. The remaining 1 patient was lost to follow‐up prior to documentation of her treatment response.

The other 3 patients (43%) had intermediate‐risk IPI scores of 2–3, and they received either CHOPE or CHOP as their initial treatment regimen. Specifically, CHOPE was administered to 2 patients—1 with distant metastasis (to omentum), and 1 with regional metastasis (to external iliac lymph nodes); while CHOP was administered to 1 patient with disease limited to subcutaneous tissues. All 3 had associated B symptoms and elevated LDH. Treatment outcomes were generally poorer in this intermediate‐risk group (as compared to those with low IPI risk)—the patient with external iliac lymph node metastases achieved PR without relapse, while the other 2 patients had PD (the patient with omental metastasis progressed at 4.2 months, and the patient without extracutaneous involvement progressed at 2.9 months; both subsequently underwent autologous stem cell transplantation and achieved sustained CR).

The mean PFS of SPTCL was 55.4 months, and 5‐year PFS was 50.0%. No mortality was noted in our series (Figure S3).

MEITL

Table 5 summarizes the demographics and clinical characteristics of patients with MEITL in our cohort. The median age was 68 years (range 47–84 years). There was a near‐equal number of females (57%) and males (43%). Most (86%) were Chinese. All had an ECOG PS of 0–1. All cases originated in the small intestine, and more than half (57%) presented with bowel perforation; the other patients presented with either bleeding (14%), intestinal obstruction (14%) or diarrhea (14%). Two patients (28%) had associated B symptoms at initial presentation, namely fevers, night sweats, weight loss, and anorexia. Based on the Lugano staging system for primary GI lymphomas, most patients were Stage 4 (71%); the others were Stage 1 (14%) or 2 (14%). Given the severity and acuity of their clinical presentations, 5 patients (71%) had to undergo emergent small bowel resection—3 for perforation (Figure S4), 1 for bleeding due to mesenteric nodal invasion (Figure S5), and 1 for obstruction due to jejunojejunal fistula (Figure S6). Postoperatively, 4 of these patients received multidrug chemotherapy regimens (details are in Table 5); the 5th patient declined chemotherapy. Amongst the 4 who underwent emergent bowel resection followed by chemotherapy, 1 (25%) achieved PR but relapsed at 23.6 months; 2 (50%) had PD—1 at 3.6 months, 1 at 4.9 months; and the remaining 1 was lost to follow‐up prior to documentation of his treatment response. The patient who declined postoperative chemotherapy had PD at 47.6 months.

Meanwhile, the patient who had presented with diarrhea underwent an elective diagnostic small bowel resection, followed by an ICE chemotherapy regimen. She achieved CR but relapsed at 19.7 months. The final patient who had presented with sealed bowel perforation (Figure S7) did not undergo bowel resection; instead, he received CHOPE as initial treatment and had primary refractory disease with PD at 4.2 months.

Ultimately, MEITL had poor survival outcomes (Figure S8). All those who were on follow‐up progressed/relapsed with a mean PFS of 15.5 months, and the majority (83%) demised with a mean OS of 32.7 months. A total of 3 demised due to small bowel relapse with recurrent perforation, while the other 2 demised with central nervous system (CNS) relapse and 1 of the patients with CNS relapse also had pleural metastases.

Discussion

Extranodal PTCLs are a heterogeneous group of lymphomas and can be broadly categorized into cutaneous versus extracutaneous subtypes [9]. Consistent with international data, the majority of our study cohort comprised cutaneous extranodal PTCLs as opposed to their extracutaneous counterparts. MF constituted 40% of our cohort, and that is in keeping with it being the commonest cutaneous T‐cell lymphoma [9]. The clinical presentation and prognosis of extranodal PTCLs vary widely depending on the individual subtype and stage at diagnosis, amongst other factors. The IPI, which was developed from studies conducted mainly on B‐cell non‐Hodgkin lymphomas [10], has been shown in our study to also hold prognostic value for extranodal PTCLs. Moreover, we identified two unique negative prognostic indicators—nodal involvement and GI origin—that are not accounted for in existing scores like the IPI, nor in T‐cell lymphoma‐specific indices such as the Prognostic Index for T‐Cell Lymphoma [11] and the International PTCL Project score [12], which were developed from datasets comprising mainly nodal T‐cell lymphomas. Our findings, therefore, highlight the need for further research to enhance risk stratification and prognostication of extranodal PTCLs.

Besides analyzing the overall extranodal PTCL cohort, we also delved into two distinct subtypes, one each of cutaneous and extracutaneous origin: SPTCL and MEITL, respectively. Both subtypes were associated with unique clinical presentations and had vastly disparate treatment outcomes. SPTCL is a rare subtype of primary cutaneous lymphoma of extranodal origin, characterized by cytotoxic alpha‐beta T‐cell infiltration of subcutaneous tissue, often resembling panniculitis [13, 14]. It has a female preponderance, and generally has an indolent course with favorable survival outcomes, with 5‐year OS typically ranging between 80%–90% [13, 14, 15, 16, 17]—trends that were reflected in our SPTCL population. The clinical presentation typically includes multiple non‐ulcerative subcutaneous nodules or plaque‐like lesions, usually on the limbs and trunk, together with B symptoms [13, 14]—features that were seen in our patients with SPTCL. Notably, in our study, SPTCL was often initially misdiagnosed as more commonly encountered skin or soft tissue disorders, which could have delayed the diagnosis and led to an advanced stage at time of biopsy. Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is a notable complication, reported in 18%–37% of cases [14, 15, 16, 18], although this was not observed in our patient cohort. Awareness of the typical patient demographics (younger to middle‐aged adults, female) and various clinical presentations highlighted in our study can help heighten one's clinical suspicion for this rare lymphoma. Histologically, SPTCL shows lobular panniculitis with atypical lymphocytes surrounding individual fat cells, and immunophenotyping typically reveals a CD3+, CD4−, CD8+, CD56−, and cytotoxic profile [13, 14, 15]. As compared to the more aggressive cutaneous GDTCL, SPTCL has an excellent prognosis, particularly in patients without HLH [14, 18]. Median OS was not reached in both a large cohort study [17] and our study, indicating long‐term survival for most patients. Negative prognostic factors in SPTCL identified in other studies include upper extremity involvement, the presence of HLH, and gamma/delta T‐cell receptor phenotype [14, 18, 19].

On the other hand, MEITL is an aggressive subtype of primary intestinal T‐cell lymphoma. Although MEITL is rare overall, it is the most prevalent subtype of primary intestinal T‐cell lymphoma in Asians [20]. Due to its recent recognition as a distinct clinical entity from enteropathy‐associated T‐cell lymphomas (EATL) and its low incidence, there is limited data to support the development of standardized treatment protocols. A recent Western European study published in 2023 involving 71 patients underscored MEITL's aggressive nature and poor response to conventional therapies [21]. Unlike EATL, MEITL is not associated with celiac disease or intestinal inflammation. It most frequently involves the small intestine, and tends to present with vague GI symptoms such as abdominal pain, diarrhea, GI bleeding or weight loss, and may also develop serious complications like bowel perforation, often requiring urgent surgical intervention [20, 21, 22, 23, 24]. Similar clinical presentations were also observed in our study cohort, all of whom had disease originating from the small bowel, and a large proportion of whom required emergent bowel resection. They were also diagnosed at an advanced stage, frequently with extraintestinal involvement at diagnosis. While CNS involvement is overall rare in PTCL, GI involvement was previously found as a risk factor for CNS relapse [25]. Several retrospective case series have also highlighted the increased incidence of CNS disease in MEITL [26, 27, 28]. In our cohort, 2 out of 7 MEITL patients relapsed with CNS disease and demised shortly after. One of our patients with CNS relapse also had concurrent relapse in the pleura. Interestingly, Suzuki et al. reported the first case of a patient with Stage 4 MEITL on presentation with both lung and CNS involvement in 2020 [29]. Despite the small numbers, our relatively high proportion of CNS relapse (28.5%) in our MEITL cohort suggests that at least a discussion for baseline CNS assessment and consideration for prophylaxis should be offered to our patients. Histologically, MEITL features a monomorphic population of malignant lymphocytes with marked epitheliotropism. Immunophenotypically, MEITL cells typically express CD3, CD8, CD56, and cytotoxic markers [21, 30]. The prognosis is poor, with a median OS of 7–14.8 months [20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 30]. In our study, survival outcomes were comparably poor with a median OS of 24.2 months, which highlights the need for further studies on MEITL, as well as on primary GI PTCLs as a whole. Clinical and molecular factors that have been shown to negatively impact the prognosis of MEITL in other studies include age above 55 years, poor ECOG PS, advanced Lugano stage at diagnosis, failure to achieve CR, and specific genetic alterations such as MYC expression, TP53 mutations, and STAT5B mutations [21, 30, 31].

We acknowledge several limitations in our study. Extranodal PTCLs are a rare subtype of lymphoma, and our limited cohort size affected the statistical power and generalizability of our findings. Patients with poor ECOG status may also have been excluded as they were too frail to provide consent for the study. Our retrospective study design could also have impacted the reliability of the results obtained. In addition, extranodal PTCLs comprise multiple histologic subtypes, each with distinct biological behaviors and heterogeneous treatment regimens, which limits the ability to draw uniform conclusions across all subtypes. Nonetheless, this description of extranodal PTCLs in our cohort of Asian patients represents a useful resource for clinical management of these rare lymphomas. Future research that addresses these limitations will be useful to confirm our findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our retrospective cohort study of 50 patients is the first to analyze and report the clinical characteristics and survival outcomes of extranodal PTCLs in an Asian population, and it has identified several unique prognostic factors that are predictive of survival outcomes. Our separate case series on SPTCL and MEITL also highlight features and clinical courses that are unique to each subtype. Our study findings provide valuable insights that will help doctors and researchers understand and manage this disease better, particularly in the Asian setting, and further research on each subtype is essential to guide the development of more personalized and effective management strategies for extranodal PTCLs.

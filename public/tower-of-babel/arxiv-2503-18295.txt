Coloring equilateral triangles
Jindřich Zapletal

Abstract
It is consistent relative to an inaccessible cardinal that ZF+DC holds, the hypergraph of equilateral triangles on a given Euclidean space has countable chromatic number, while the hypergraph of isosceles triangles on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} does not.



2020 AMS subject classification 03E35, 14P99, 05C15.

Introduction

This paper deals with chromatic numbers of hypergraphs of arity three on Euclidean spaces. It has been known since the 1960’s that the chromatic number of the hypergraph of equilateral triangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is countable in ZFC
[
2
]
. Erdős asked whether the same is true for the hypergraph of isosceles triangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. On the way towards the solution of this problem, Schmerl first
[
6
]
gave a brief argument that the chromatic number of the hypergraph of equilateral triangles in any dimension is countable, later
[
7
]
gave an affirmative answer to the question of Erdős, and eventually classified all algebraic hypergraphs of countable chromatic number in a major breakthrough
[
8
]
.

This paper is a commentary on this development of events. I will show that Schmerl’s theorems appeared in order of increasing difficulty. Namely, I will prove

Theorem 1.1
.

If the theory ZFC+there is an inaccessible cardinal is consistent, then so is ZF+DC+for every number
d\geq 1
, the hypergraph of equilateral triangles in dimension
d
has countable chromatic number+the hypergraph of isosceles triangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
does not have countable chromatic number.

Theorem
1.1
greatly understates the understanding of the resulting models of ZF+DC. They are obtained as balanced extensions of the choiceless Solovay model. Therefore, as a matter of general properties of such models, they do not contain e.g. maximal almost disjoint families
[
4
, Theorem 14.1.1]
. The posets generating them are
(4,3)
-balanced (Corollary
3.12
) and as a result, the models do not contain any discontinuous homomorphisms between Polish groups
[
4
, Theorem 13.2.1]
. In the model, every nonmeager (or non-null, in the sense of the usual two-dimensional Lebesgue measure) subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
contains an isosceles triangle. It is possible to achieve the independence result for all dimensions simultaneously. The posets are
d
-Noetherian balanced, which makes it possible to perform further dimension-specific analysis as in
[
10
]
. The inaccessible cardinal is needed only to initialize the methodology of geometric set theory independence proofs
[
4
, Part II]
; I doubt that it is necessary for the independence result.

There are many open questions in the area. The first one can be asked about any algebraic hypergraph without perfect cliques. A general negative answer is known only for arity two, i.e. algebraic graphs without perfect cliques
[
9
]
. The case of equilateral triangles in dimension two is resolved in
[
11
]
.

Question 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
be a number. In ZF+DC, does countable chromatic number of hypergraph of equilateral triangles in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
imply the existence of a Vitali set?

The second question deals with a specific type of coloring. Given a hypergraph
\Gamma
on a topological space
X
, a
\Gamma
-
free neighborhood assignment
is a map
c
assigning to each point
x\in X
an open neighborhood of
x
in such a way that for every hyperedge
e\in\Gamma
there is a vertex
x\in e
such that
e\not\subseteq c(x)
.

Question 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a number. Is there a balanced extension of the Solovay model in which there is an equilateral-triangle-free neighborhood assignment?

As for the architecture of the paper, Section
2
collects the necessary concepts from real algebraic geometry, with the central Definition
2.3
(2). Section
3
constructs the coloring poset generating the model for Theorem
1.1
. Finally, Section
4
shows how to prove that in the resulting model, there is no coloring of isosceles triangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The main point is very easy to describe: if
\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\}
is an isosceles triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which is in a suitable sense generic, then
V[x_{0},x_{1}]\cap V[x_{1},x_{2}]\cap V[x_{0},x_{2}]=V
. On the other hand, if this is an equilateral triangle in any dimension, then the intersection of the three models contains the length of the side of the triangle. The whole argument turns on this simple geometric point. The terminology of the paper sticks to the set theoretic standard of
[

]
. For a bounded nonempty subset

Algebraic geometry

The proof uses several standard concepts from real algebraic geometry. To begin, recall
[
5

that the language of real closed fields includes symbols for addition, multiplication and inequality. The theory of real closed fields includes the axioms of an ordered field, the statement that squares are exactly the non-negative elements, and the statements saying that every nontrivial polynomial of odd degree has a root. The theory of real closed fields admits elimination of quantifiers
[

Coming to the original contents of this paper, the following concept is key.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a number,

, and

.

1.

A set

Example 2.4
.

Let

, even though the smallest zero set of a polynomial with rational coefficients containing

.

If the set

is a real closed subfield, then the set

is simply the smallest subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

which is a zero-set of a polynomial with coefficients in

\mathbb{R}
. However, we will use the sets

also when

\mathbb{R}

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{R}
be a set and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a point. Then

1.

D(F,x)
is an irreducible algebraic set;

2.

x
is a nonsingular element of
D(F,x)
;

3.

if
F\subset\mathbb{R}
is a real closed subfield, then
D(F,x)=\{x\}
if and only if
x\in F^{d}

Proof.

(1) follows from the fact that the components of an algebraic set are definable from the algebraic set. To fill in the details, let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an algebraic set. Let
p_{i}
for
i\in j
be polynomials with real coefficients whose zero sets are the components of the set

. Using dummy zero coefficients if necessary, we may assume that there is a number
m
such that the sequence of coefficients of
p_{i}
is an element of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for every
i\in j
. For each
i\in j
let
O_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a basic open set containing the tuple of coefficients of
p_{i}
, so that the sets
O_{i}
for

are pairwise disjoint. Now, the zero set of (say) the polynomial

is defined as the set of all

such that there is a sequence
\langle q_{i}\colon i\in j\rangle
of polynomials whose coefficients come from the set

respectively, each of whose zero sets is a proper subset of

, and such that

. This follows from the uniqueness of components of algebraic sets.

(2) follows from the fact that the set of singular points of an irreducible algebraic set

is an algebraic, proper subset of it definable from

. The algebraicity and proper inclusion is proved in
[
1
, Proposition 3.3.14]
. For the definability, it is enough to restate the definitions. Namely, for every polynomial

such that
A=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon p(y)=0\}
, the Zariski tangent space to

at

is the set of all vectors perpendicular to the vector of partial derivatives of

at
y
. The tangent space does not depend on the choice of
p
. The point

is then nonsingular in

if this space has maximal dimension possible, namely
\dim(A)
. This can be mechanically restated by a rather long formula in the language of real closed fields.

(3) follows from the fact that a real closed subfield is an elementary submodel of
\mathbb{R}
. If
D(F,x)=\{x\}
then each coordinate of

is definable from parameters in
F
and therefore an element of
F
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
F_{0},F_{1}
be real closed subfields of
\mathbb{R}
and let
n\in\omega
. For every finite set

there is a finite set

Proof.

Let

be a tuple enumerating all elements of

p_{x}
be a polynomial with

as an element. Let

; this is an algebraic set containing
v
as an element. Let

be a finite set such that

. Let
b\subset F_{1}
be a finite set such that all coordinates of points in

. I claim that the set

works.

To show this, suppose that

is an arbitrary point, and work to show that
D(F_{0}\cup b,x)\subseteq D(F_{0}\cup a,x)
holds. Use quantifier elimination for real closed fields to define the set
D(F_{0}\cup a,x)
by a quantifier-free formula with parameters in
F_{0}\cup a
. There is a finite list of polynomials
q_{i}\colon i\in j
, each having parameters in
F_{0}
and
n+m
free variables such that the quantifier-free formula is a Boolean combination of formulas of the form
q_{i}(u,v)\geq 0
. Let
d=\{i\in j\colon q_{i}(x,v)=0\}

D_{x}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon q_{i}(u,v)=0

, and let

be a basic open neighborhood of

Consider the set
E_{x}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\forall w\in C\ p_{x}(u,w)=0\}

Now, consider the disjunction in Fact

Let

3
The coloring poset

The model for Theorem
1.1
is obtained as a generic extension of the choiceless Solovay model
[
3
, Theorem 26.14]
via a definable
\sigma
-closed coloring poset. The coloring poset can be stated for a more general class of hypergraphs of arity three.

Definition 3.1
.

A hypergraph
\Gamma
on a Polish space
X
of arity
n
is
2
-irreflexive
if in the closure of
\Gamma
in the space
[X]^{\leq n}
with the Vietoris topology there are no sets of cardinality two.

An example of a hypergraph which is
2
-irreflexive is the hypergraph of equilateral triangles on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
d\geq 2
is a number. An example of a hypergraph of arity three which is not
2
-irreflexive is the hypergraph of isosceles triangles on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
2
-irreflexive hypergraphs carry the following parameter:

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a Euclidean space and
\Gamma
be a
2
-irreflexive algebraic hypergraph on
X
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a set and

be a point.
\varepsilon(A,x)
is the largest number
\varepsilon>0
such that there is no set

consisting only of points closer than
\varepsilon
to

such that for every

which is not equal to
x
or to any element of
b

b\cup\{y\}\in\Gamma
holds. If no such number
\varepsilon
exists, we put
\varepsilon(A,x)=0
; if there are arbitrarily large such numbers
\varepsilon
, put
\varepsilon(A,x)=\infty
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be an algebraic,
2
-irreflexive hypergraph of arity three on a Euclidean space

of dimension

.

1.

if

then

if and only if

is algebraic over

;

2.

if

then
\varepsilon(B,x)\leq\varepsilon(A,x)
;

3.

if

is a real closed subfield and

is a point, then there is no set

consisting of points closer than

to

such that

.

Proof.

For (1), recall that

is algebraic over

if and only if

by Proposition

(3). Now, if

then

by the definitions. If

then pick a point

distinct from

. If for every

consisting of points closer to

than

, then

(2) follows directly from the definitions, noting that

. For (3), suppose towards a contradiction that

is a set consisting of points closer than

For the rest of the section, fix a number

which have a rational center and a rational radius. By a (partial) coloring I mean a (partial) map

holds. Let

Definition 3.4
.

The coloring poset
P
consists of all countable partial colorings

such that for some countable real closed subfield

it is the case that
\mathrm{dom}(p)=\mathrm{supp}(p)^{n}

if

1.

p\subseteq q
;

2.

for every point

, the metric diameter of

is smaller than
\varepsilon(\mathrm{supp}(p),x)
;

3.

for every finite set

, writing

and
q^{*}A=\{\mathrm{diam}(q(x))\colon x\in A\}
, then
q^{*}C(p,q,a)\in I
holds.

The properties of

must be checked in turn.

Proposition 3.5
.

\leq
is a transitive relation.

Proof.

Let
r\leq q\leq p
be conditions in the coloring poset

; I must show that
r\leq p
holds. For Definition
3.4
(1), since
p\subseteq q
and

holds,

certainly follows. For Definition

(2), suppose that

is a point. If
x\in\mathrm{dom}(q)
, then Definition
3.4
(2) applied to

implies that the diameter of

is smaller than
\varepsilon(\mathrm{supp}(p),x)
. If

, then Definition

(2) applied to

implies that

where the second inequality follows from Proposition

(2).

The key point is the verification of Definition

(3). Suppose that

is a finite set. By Proposition

, find a finite set

For every condition

Fix

Now, to show that

is a basic open neighborhood whose diameter is smaller than

(2) for

Proposition 3.7
.

\leq
is
\sigma
-closed.

Proof.

If
\langle p_{i}\colon i\in\omega\rangle

(CH) For every condition

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

for

It is necessary to parse the proposition correctly. The whole proposition (and its proof as well) takes place in an ambient forcing extension, whose choice is inconsequential, and it is therefore not specified. The assumption on the generic extensions says that any pair or any triple of them is mutually generic. In fact, the proof shows immediately that it is enough to assume that pairs and triples of them are

Proof.

The first observation must be the following:

Claim 3.10

\bigcup_{i}p_{i}

Case 2.
Not Case 1 and all three vertices can be found in the union of two of the forcing extensions. Then, there must be a single vertex, say

Case 3.
Not Case 1 and 2. This is handled in the same way as Case 2, except this time mutual genericity in triples is used. This proves the claim.

Claim 3.11

For every point

countable, and for each point

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the methodology of geometric set theory. The key observation regarding isosceles triangles is the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\}
be an isosceles triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
Cohen-generic over the ground model in

. Then

1.

for

is a Cohen-generic point of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

2.

the models

the intersection of the three models

,

, and

.

To parse the proposition correctly, consider the set

consisting of isosceles triangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This is a
G_{\delta}
-set, therefore Polish in the inherited topology. One can consider the Cohen poset of nonempty open subsets of

ordered by inclusion; it adds a generic element of

. The triangle of Proposition

The proof is a rather mechanical application of the calculus of generic points of semi-algebraic sets. For completeness, I recall the basic tenets of this calculus. If

is a Polish space, a Cohen-generic element of

over a transitive model

of ZFC is a point

which belongs to all dense open subsets of

. It is obtained by forcing with the Cohen poset

of all nonempty open subsets of

ordered by inclusion; its name for the generic point of

will be denoted by

. The following is proved by an immediate density argument.

Claim 4.2
.

Let

be Polish spaces.

1.

If

is a continuous open function then
P_{X}
forces the

to be
P_{Y}
-generic over

;

2.

forces the points of generic pair
\langle\dot{x},\dot{y}\rangle
to be mutually generic over
V
for the
P_{X}
and
P_{Y}
poset respectively.

If

is a number, then every semi-algebraic set

is a Boolean combination of closed sets by the quantifier elemination theorem, therefore it is

in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and Polish in the inherited topology. In this sense I speak of Cohen-generic elements of

. The poset

is realized as the poset of all basic open sets

with nonempty intersection with

Let
C\subset(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{3}
be the semi-algebraic set of all triples
\langle u_{0},u_{1},u_{2}\rangle
consisting of pairwise distinct points such that the Euclidean distance from

to

or

is the same. Denote the

-names for points in the generic triple by

Claim 4.3
.

P_{C}\Vdash\langle\dot{x}_{0},\dot{x}_{1}\rangle
is a Cohen generic point of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

The projection from

. Claim

∎

Claim 4.4

to

To prove the claim, suppose that

, and

A symmetrical argument shows the following:

Claim 4.5

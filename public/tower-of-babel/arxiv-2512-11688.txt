Chein Automorphisms of Free Metabelian Anticommutative Algebras


Abstract
. We describe all automorphisms of a free metabelian anticommutative algebra of rank n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 over a field K K that move only one variable while fixing the others. Such automorphisms are called Chein automorphisms in the cases of free metabelian groups and free metabelian Lie algebras. We show that all automorphisms of a free metabelian anticommutative algebra of rank n = 2 n=2 are linear, and that the simplest non elementary Chein automorphism of degree 3 3 is absolutely wild for all n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 .

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}
be an arbitrary variety of algebras over a field
K
and let

A=K_{\mathfrak{M}}\langle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
be the free algebra of
{\mathfrak{M}}
with a free set of generators
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. As usual we denote by
n
-tuple
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
the endomorphism
\phi
of
A
such that
\phi(x_{i})=f_{i},\,1\leq i\leq n
. In particular,
\mathrm{Id}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the identity automorphism. Let
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
be the group of all automorphisms of
A
. An automorphism

(1)

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},\alpha x_{i}+f,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}),

where
0\neq\alpha\in K,\ f\in K_{\mathfrak{M}}\langle X\setminus\{x_{i}\}\rangle
,
is called
elementary
. The subgroup
\mathrm{TAut}(A)
of
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
generated by all
elementary automorphisms is called the
tame automorphism group
,
and the elements of this subgroup are called
tame automorphisms

of
A
. Nontame automorphisms of
A
are called
wild
.

An automorphism
\phi
is called
approximately tame

[
21
]
if it can be approximated by a sequence of
tame automorphisms
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
with respect to the
formal power series topology
(see exact definitions in Section 7). An automorphism
\phi
is called
absolutely wild
if it is not approximately tame.

In 1968 O. Chein
[
5
]
introduced a specific type of automorphisms of free metabelian groups of finite rank
n\geq 3
, and proved that they are not tame when
n=3
. Nevertheless they are tame when
n\geq 4
since all automorphisms of free metabelian groups of rank
n\geq 4
are tame by the well known Bachmuth-Mochizuki-Roman’kov Theorem
[
2
,
16
]
.

Analogues of these automorphisms for free metabelian algebras were studied by V. Roman’kov and his coauthors
[
9
,
18
,
19
]
as well as by R. Nauryzbaev
[
12
,
14
]
. Let
M
be a free metabelian Lie algebra in the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
As usual, the group of all linear automorphisms of
M
will be identified with the group of all invertible matrices
GL_{n}(K)
. In 2015, Nauryzbaev
[
15
]
proved that
\mathrm{TAut}(M)
is generated by all linear automorphisms and the quadratic automorphism

(2)

\displaystyle(x_{1}+[x_{2},x_{3}],x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})

if either
n\geq 4
and
K
is a field of characteristic
\neq 3
, or
n\geq 5
and
K
is a field of arbitrary characteristic.

A peculiar feature of metabelian Lie algebras is that they admit automorphisms of the form (
1
) that are not necessarily elementary; that is,
f
may also involve
x_{i}
.
We call them
Chein automorphisms
as in the case of groups
[
8
,
17
]
. Notice that, by our definition, all elementary automorphisms form a subset of Chein automorphisms. These automorphisms move only one variable and fix the others. The subgroup
\mathrm{ATAut}(M)
of
\mathrm{Aut}(M)
generated by all
Chein automorphisms will be called the
almost tame automorphism group
,
and the elements of this subgroup will be called
almost tame automorphisms

of
M

[
12
]
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{TAut}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{ATAut}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(M).

The automorphisms of the algebra
M
of rank
n=3
have been extensively investigated by numerous authors. In 1992, V. Drensky
[
7
]
(see also
[
3
]
) proved that the exponential automorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{ad}[x_{1},x_{2}])=(x_{1}+[[x_{1},x_{2}],x_{1}],x_{2}+[[x_{1},x_{2}],x_{2}],x_{3}+[[x_{1},x_{2}],x_{3}])

of
M
is wild. Moreover, this automorphism is not almost tame
[
12
,
18
]
.
Every non-elementary Chein automorphism is also shown to be wild
[
12
,
18
]
. The defining relations for elementary automorphisms
[
13
]
and for Chein automorphisms
[
14
]
have also been determined.
It was recently observed
[
21
]
that the examples of wild automorphisms mentioned here are actually absolutely wild.

The first example of a Chein automorphism is

(3)

\displaystyle(x_{1}+[[x_{2},x_{3}],x_{1}],x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),

which is absolutely wild when
n=3
and plays an important role in the study of
\mathrm{Aut}(M)
for
n\geq 4
. In 1993 Bryant and Drensky
[
4
]
(see also
[
11
]
) proved that every automorphism of
M
of rank
n\geq 4
is approximately tame in characteristic zero. Thus (
3
) is approximately tame in characteristic zero. Recently it was shown in
[
28
]
that
\mathrm{Aut}(M_{n})
is generated modulo
GL_{n}(K)
by (
2
) and (
3
) when either
n=4
and the characteristic of
K
is not
3
, or
n\geq 5
and
K
is an arbitrary field. It remains an open question whether (
3
) is tame or wild for
n\geq 4
.

This paper was motivated by the behavior of the Chein automorphism (
3
) in free metabelian Lie algebras. Here we study automorphisms of free metabelian anticommutative algebras. These algebras also admit Chein automorphisms. First we describe all Chein automorphisms
of free metabelian anticommutative algebras.
We show that every automorphism of a free metabelian anticommutative algebra of rank
2
is tame; that is, all such automorphisms are linear. We also show that the analogue of the Chein automorphism (
3
) for free metabelian anticommutative algebras of rank
n\geq 3
is absolutely wild.

2.
Free metabelian anticommutative algebras

Let
K
be an arbitrary field. An algebra
A
over
K
is called
anti-commutative
if the identity

(4)

\displaystyle x^{2}=0

is satisfied. This identity implies

(5)

\displaystyle xy=-yx.

An algebra
A
is called
metabelian
if it satisfies the identity

(6)

\displaystyle(xy)(zt)=0.

Let
A
be a free metabelian anticommutative algebra in the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. The identities (
4
)–(
6
) imply that every element of
A^{2}=AA
is a linear combination of the elements of the form

(7)

\displaystyle(((x_{i}x_{j})x_{k_{1}})\cdots)x_{k_{s}},

where
i>j
,
1\leq k_{r}\leq n
for all
1\leq r\leq s
, and
s\geq 0
.

Let
Y=A/A^{2}
and denote by
y_{i}
the images of
x_{i}
in
Y
. Then
Y
is an algebra with trivial multiplication and with a linear basis
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
. Denote by
U
the free associative algebra with identity and with free generators
z_{i}=R_{y_{i}}
, where
1\leq i\leq n
. In fact,
U
is the universal (multiplicative) enveloping algebra of
Y
in the variety of all anticommutative algebras
[
25
,
27
]
and
z_{i}=R_{y_{i}}
is the universal operator of right multiplication by
y_{i}
. Notice that
L_{y_{i}}=-R_{y_{i}}
by (
5
).

Let
T
be a free right
U
-module with free generators
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
. Turn the space
Y\oplus T
into an algebra by

\displaystyle(a_{1}+m_{1})(a_{2}+m_{2})=m_{1}R_{a_{2}}-m_{2}R_{a_{1}},\quad a_{1},a_{2}\in Y,\;m_{1},m_{2}\in T.

Obviously,
Y\oplus T
is a metabelian anticommutative algebra.

Lemma 1
.

(1)
The homomorphism
\rho:A\to Y\oplus T
defined by
\rho(x_{i})=y_{i}+t_{i}
for all
i
is an embedding;

(2)
The elements of the form (
7
) is a basis of
A^{2}
.

Proof.
It is sufficient to check that the images of elements of the form (
7
) in
Y\oplus T
under
\rho
are linearly independent. In fact,

\displaystyle\rho(x_{i}x_{j})=t_{i}z_{j}-t_{j}z_{i}

and

\displaystyle\rho((((x_{i}x_{j})x_{k_{1}})\ldots)x_{k_{s}})=(t_{i}z_{j}-t_{j}z_{i})z_{k_{1}}\ldots z_{k_{s}}.

Let us introduce a
\mathrm{deglex}
order on the set of all basis elements

\displaystyle t_{i}z_{i_{1}}\ldots z_{i_{k}}

of
T
by setting
t_{i}<t_{j}
and
z_{i}<z_{j}
when
i<j
. Then the leading monomial of
\rho((((x_{i}x_{j})x_{k_{1}})\ldots)x_{k_{s}})
is
t_{i}z_{j}z_{k_{1}}\ldots z_{k_{s}}
. Thus, the leading monomials of the images of all distinct elements of the form (
7
) are different and therefore linearly independent.
\Box

By this lemma, we will identify
A
with the subalgebra of
Y\oplus T
generated by
x_{i}=y_{i}+t_{i}
, where
1\leq i\leq n
. Then every element
a\in A
can be uniquely represented in the form

\displaystyle a=y+t_{1}u_{1}+\ldots+t_{n}u_{n},

where
y\in Y
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in U
.

Set

\displaystyle\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{i}}=u_{i}

for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then
\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{i}}
are called the Fox derivatives of
a

[
23
,
24
,
26
]
. Set also

\displaystyle\partial(a)=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{t}=\left(\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{t},

where
t
denotes the transposition. We have
\partial(x_{i})=e_{i}^{t}
, where
e_{i}
is the row vector with a
1
in the
i
th place and zeros elsewhere.

Lemma 2
.

(1)
If
g\in A^{2}
then the column
\partial g=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})^{t}
satisfies the conditions

(8)

\displaystyle r_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)},\ w_{j}^{(i)}=-w_{i}^{(j)},\ w_{i}^{(i)}=0,

for all
i,j
.

(2)
If the column
(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})^{t}\in U^{n}
satisfies the conditions (
8
) then there exists
g\in A^{2}
such that
\partial g=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})^{t}
.

Proof.

(1)
Since
\partial
is linear, it is sufficient to check the conditions (
8
) for an arbitrary element of the form
g=(x_{s}x_{k})u
, where
s>k
and
u\in U
. We have

\displaystyle(x_{s}x_{k})u=(t_{s}z_{k}-t_{k}z_{s})u=t_{s}z_{k}u-t_{k}z_{s}u=t_{s}r_{s}+t_{k}r_{k},

where
r_{s}=z_{k}u=z_{k}w_{k}^{(s)},r_{k}=z_{s}(-u)=z_{s}w_{s}^{(k)}
. Consequently,
w_{k}^{(s)}=-w_{s}^{(k)}
and
w_{i}^{(j)}=0
when
\{i,j\}\neq\{s,k\}
. This proves
(1)
.

(2)
Suppose that
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n}\in U
satisfy the conditions (
8
). Set
g=\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}{(x_{i}x_{j})w_{j}^{(i)}}
. Then

\partial(g)=\partial\left(\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}{(x_{i}x_{j})w_{j}^{(i)}}\right)=\partial\left(\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}{(t_{i}z_{j}-t_{j}z_{i})w_{j}^{(i)}}\right)=

=\partial\left(\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}t_{i}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}-\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}t_{j}z_{i}w_{j}^{(i)}\right)=\partial\left(\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}t_{i}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}t_{i}z_{j}w_{i}^{(j)}\right)=

=\partial\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}t_{i}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}t_{i}z_{j}w_{i}^{(j)}}\right)=\partial\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}{\left[\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}-\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}z_{j}w_{i}^{(j)}\right]}\right)=

=\partial\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}{\left[\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}\right]}\right)=\partial\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}\right)=\partial\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}r_{i}\right)=

(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})^{t}.

\Box

Notice that if
\partial g=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})^{t}
for some
g\in A^{2}
, then
r_{n}
is uniquely determined by
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots r_{n-1}
. In fact, if
r_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}w_{j}^{(i)}
, then, by Lemma
2
, we obtain

(9)

\displaystyle r_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-z_{j}w_{n}^{(j)}).

3.
Jacobian matrices

The Jacobian matrix of an endomorphism
\phi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
is defined as

J(\phi)=\left[\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}=[\partial(f_{1})\dots\partial(f_{n})].

For any
f\in A
, let
\tilde{f}
denote its image in
Y=A/A^{2}
. Every endomorphism
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
of
A
induces an
endomorphism of
Y=A/A^{2}
that sends
y_{i}
to
\tilde{f}_{i}
for all
i
. This endomorphism can be uniquely extended to an endomorphism of
U
that sends
z_{i}=R_{y_{i}}
to
R_{\tilde{f}_{i}}
for all
i
. Denote this endomorphism of
U
by
\tilde{\phi}
. For
f\in U
, the image of
f
under
\tilde{\phi}
will be written as
f^{\tilde{\phi}}
.

If
g
is an arbitrary element of
A
, then it is easy to check that the following form of the ”chain rule” holds:

\partial(\phi(g))=\partial(g(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}))=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\partial(f_{i})(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}})^{\tilde{\phi}}.

Recall that the composition of endomorphisms
\phi,\psi
of
A
is defined as

\phi\circ\psi(a)=\phi(\psi(a)),\ a\in A.

The chain rule immediately implies that

(10)

\displaystyle J(\phi\circ\psi)=J(\phi)(J(\psi))^{\tilde{\phi}}.

Consequently, if
\phi
is an automorphism of
A
, then
J(\phi)
is invertible over
U
. The converse of this statement is an analogue of the famous Jacobian Conjecture
[
10
]
for free metabelian anticommutative algebras
[
25
,
27
]
.
Following the arguments in
[
24
,
26
]
, one can show that the following lemma holds.

Lemma 3
.

An endomorphism
\phi
of
A
is an automorphism if and only if the Jacobian matrix
J(\phi)
is invertible.

We omit the proof of this lemma, as it is not needed in the present paper.

Denote by
\mbox{IAut}(A)
the group of all automorphisms of
A
that induces the identity automorpjism on
Y=A/A^{2}
.
The elements of
\mbox{IAut}(A)
are called
IA
-automorphisms
. If
\phi\in\mbox{IAut}(A)
then the Jacobian matrix of
\phi
has the form

J(\phi)=I_{n}+R,

where
I_{n}
is the identity matrix of order
n
and every column of
R
satisfies the conditions (
8
). If
\phi\in\mbox{IAut}(A)
then
\tilde{\phi}=\mbox{Id}
. Consequently, if
\phi,\psi\in\mbox{IAut}(A)
then (
10
) implies that

\displaystyle J(\phi\circ\psi)=J(\phi)J(\psi).

Corollary 1
.

The mapping

\displaystyle J:\mbox{IAut}(A)\to SL_{n}(U)

is an embedding of groups.

It is obvious that
J
is one-to-one. Recall that
SL_{n}(U)
denotes the set of all invertible matrices over
U
whose constant part has determinant one.

4.
The structure of Chein automorphisms

Lemma 4
.

Let
f\in A^{2}
. Then
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=0
if and only if
f
belongs to the ideal of
A
generated by all
x_{i}x_{j}
, where
i,j>1
.

Let
f\in A^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle f=\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}(x_{i}x_{j})u_{ij},\ u_{ij}\in U.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=-\sum_{i=2}^{n}z_{i}u_{i1}.

If
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=0
in the free associative algebra, we immediately get
u_{21}=\ldots=u_{n1}=0
. Therefore

(11)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{2\leq j<i\leq n}(x_{i}x_{j})u_{ij},

satisfies the statement of the lemma.
\Box

Consider the endomorphisms of the form

(12)

\displaystyle\delta=(x_{1}+f,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),\ f\in A^{2}.

Lemma 5
.

An endomorphism
\delta
of
A
of the form (
12
) is an automorphism if and only if
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=0
.

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle J(\delta)=\begin{bmatrix}1+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}&0&\ldots&0\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}&1&\ldots&0\\
\cdot&\cdot&\ldots&\cdot\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}&0&\ldots&1\end{bmatrix}.

If
J(\delta)
is invertible then
1+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}
is right invertible in the free associative algebra
U
. Since
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}
belongs to the augmented ideal of
U
it follows that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=0
. By Lemma
4
,
f
has the form (
11
). Then
\delta
is a product of endomorphisms

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}=(x_{1}+(x_{i}x_{j})u_{ij},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),\ 2\leq j<i\leq n.

Since
A
is metabelian it follows that every
\delta_{ij}
is an automorphism.
\Box

5.
Automorphisms of algebras of rank
2

If
n=1
then the group of automorphisms
\mbox{Aut}(A)
is isomorphic to
GL_{1}(K)\simeq K^{*}=K\setminus\{0\}
. If
n=2
then all elementary automorphisms are linear since
A
is anticommutative. Consequently,
\mbox{TAut}(A)\simeq GL_{2}(K)
. We show that
A
has no other automorphisms.

Lemma 6
.

If
n=2
then
\mbox{Aut}(A)\simeq GL_{2}(K)
.

Proof.
It is sufficient to prove that
\mbox{IAut}(A)=\{\mbox{Id}\}
. Let
\phi=(x_{1}+f_{1},x_{2}+f_{2})\in\mbox{IAut}(A)
, where
f_{1},f_{2}\in A^{2}
. Then

J(\varphi)=I_{2}+\begin{bmatrix}\partial(f_{1})&\partial(f_{2})\end{bmatrix}=I_{2}+\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}.

By Lemma
2
, we have

a=z_{2}h,\ c=-z_{1}h,\ h\in U.

The first column of
J(\varphi)
is
[1+z_{2}h,-z_{1}h]^{t}
. Since
J(\varphi)
is invertible it follows that
[1+z_{2}h,-z_{1}h]^{t}
is left unimodular, that is, there exist
u_{1},u_{2}\in U
such that

(13)

\displaystyle u_{1}(1+z_{2}h)+u_{2}(-z_{1}h)=1.

For any
u\in U
denote by
\overline{u}
its highest homogeneous part of
u
in
U
with respect to the degree function in the variables
z_{1},z_{2}
. If
h\neq 0
, then
\overline{1+z_{2}h}=z_{2}\overline{h}
and
\overline{z_{1}h}=z_{1}\overline{h}
. Then (
13
) implies that

\displaystyle\overline{u_{1}}z_{2}\overline{h}-\overline{u_{2}}z_{1}\overline{h}=0,

that is,
z_{2}\overline{h}
and
z_{1}\overline{h}
are left dependent. It is well known that in free associative algebras this implies
[
6
]
that one of
z_{2}\overline{h}
or
z_{1}\overline{h}
belongs to the left ideal generated by the other. They must be linearly dependent since they have the same degrees. But they are linearly independent if
h\neq 0
.

Consequently,
h=0
and
a=c=0
. Similarly,
b=d=0
. Then
f_{1}=f_{2}=0
and
\phi=\mbox{Id}
.

\Box

6.
Divergence in free anticommutative algebras

To prove the main result of the paper, we need the notion of the divergence of a derivation introduced in
[
21
]
.

Let
B
be a free anticommutative algebra over
K
in the variables
\Xi=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}
. Let
\Xi^{*}
be the set of all (nonempty) nonassociative monomials in the alphabet
\Xi
. Recall that every
u\in\Xi^{*}
of degree
\geq 2
can be uniquely written as
u=u_{1}u_{2}
for some
u_{1},u_{2}\in\Xi^{*}

[
29
]
. Set
\xi_{i}<\xi_{j}
if
i<j
. For any
u,v\in\Xi^{*}
set
u<v
if
\deg u<\deg v
. If
\deg u=\deg v\geq 2
and
u=u_{1}u_{2}
and
v=v_{1}v_{2}
, then set
u<v
if either
u_{1}<v_{1}
or
u_{1}=v_{1}
and
u_{2}<v_{2}
.

A nonassociative monomial
u\in\Xi^{*}
is called
regular
if it does not have submonomials of the form
vw
, where
v,w\in\Xi^{*}
and
v\leq w
. The set of all regular words is a linear basis of
B

[
22
]

The universal (multiplicative) enveloping algebra
U(B)=U_{\mathfrak{M}}(B)
is the free associative algebra with identity generated by all universal operators of right multiplication
R_{u}
, where
u
runs over the set of all regular monomials in the alphabet
\Xi

[
25
]
.

Let

\displaystyle\Omega_{B}=d\xi_{1}U(B)\oplus\ldots\oplus d\xi_{n}U(B)

be a free right
U(B)
-module with free generators
d\xi_{1},\ldots,d\xi_{n}
.
The linear map

\displaystyle D:B\to\Omega_{B}

defined by
D(\xi_{i})=d\xi_{i}
for all
i
and such that

\displaystyle D(ab)=aD(b)+D(a)b=-D(b)R_{a}+D(a)R_{b}

for all
a,b\in B
, is called the
universal derivation
of

Consider the natural grading

The

7.
An absolutely wild automorphism

In this section we show that the Chein automorphism

(16)

\displaystyle\tau=(x_{1}+(x_{3}x_{2})x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})

of the free metabelian anticommutative algebra
A
is absolutely wild for all
n\geq 3
.

First, we recall the precise definitions of approximately tame and absolutely wild automorphisms from
[
21
]
.

Let
\mathrm{IA}(k)=\mathrm{IA}(A,k)
be the
set of all automorphisms of
A
that induces the identity automorphism on the factor-algebra
A/A^{k+1}
.
Consider the descending central series

\displaystyle\mathrm{IAut}(A)=\mathrm{IA}(1)\supseteq\mathrm{IA}(2)\supseteq\ldots\supseteq\mathrm{IA}(k)\supseteq.

An automorphism
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(A)
is called
approximately tame
if there exists a sequence of
tame automorphisms
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
such that
\phi\psi_{k}^{-1}\in\mathrm{IA}(k)
. The topology defined by this definition on
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
is called the
formal power series topology

[
1
]
. An automorphism
\phi
will be called
absolutely wild
if it is not approximately tame.

In 1981 Shafarevich
[
20
]
and in 1983 Anick
[
1
]
independently proved that every automorphism of the polynomial algebra
K[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
K
of characteristic zero is approximately tame.
In 1993 Bryant and Drensky
[
4
]
(see also
[
11
]
) proved that every automorphism of
M
of rank
n\geq 4
is approximately tame in characteristic zero.

The following result is a part of Theorem 5 from
[
21
]
.

(F)
Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a Nielsen-Schreier variety of algebras and
B
is a free algebra of
\mathfrak{M}
of rank
n
.
Let
\mathfrak{N}
be any subvariety of
\mathfrak{M}
and let
I\subseteq B
be the ideal of all identities of
\mathfrak{N}
in
\mathfrak{M}
in
n
variables. Suppose that an endomorphism
\epsilon\in\mathrm{IE}_{i}(A)\setminus\mathrm{IE}_{i+1}(A)
for some
i\geq 1
induces an automorphism
\phi
of
B=A/I
and the ideal
I
does not contain elements of degree
\leq i+1
.
If
\mathrm{div}(T(\epsilon))\neq 0
then
\phi
is absolutely wild.

Theorem 1
.

The automorphism
\tau
from (
16
) is an absolutely wild automorphism of the free metabelian anticommutative algebra
A
of rank
n\geq 3
.

Proof.
We apply
(F)
. Let
\mathfrak{M}
be the variety of all anticommutative algebras. It is well known that
\mathfrak{M}
is Nielsen-Schreier
[
22
]
. Let
B
be the free anticommutative algebra in the variables
\Xi=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}
. Let
\mathfrak{N}
be the subvariety of
\mathfrak{M}
defined by the identity (
6
). Then
I
is the ideal of
B
generated by all elements of the form
(uv)(wq)
, where
u,v,w,q\in\Xi^{*}
.

Notice that the endomorphism
\sigma
of
B
induces the automorphism
\tau
of
A=B/I
. We have
\sigma\in\mathrm{IE}_{2}(A)\setminus\mathrm{IE}_{3}(A)
. The ideal
I
does not contain elements of degree
\leq 3
. Moreover,
\mathrm{div}(T(\sigma))\neq 0
by Lemma
7
. By
(F)
,
\tau
is an absolutely wild automorphism of
A
.

Note that, in general, all results in
[
21
]
are stated for fields of characteristic zero. By carefully repeating the proof of Theorem 5 from
[
21
]
for these specific varieties
\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{N}
, we see that the assumption of characteristic zero is not required. Thus,
\tau
is an absolutely wild automorphism of
A
of rank
n\geq 3
over an arbitrary field.
\Box

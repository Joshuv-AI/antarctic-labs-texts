On the homology of simplicial and cubical sets with symmetries
Curtis Greene, Volkmar Welker, Georg Wille

Abstract
. We study the homology of simplicial and cubical sets with symmetries. These are simplicial and cubical sets with additional maps expressing the symmetries of simplices and cubes. We consider the chain complex computing the homology groups in either case. We show for coefficients in fields of characteristic 0 0 that the sub-complex generated by degeneracies (simplicial case) or connections (cubical case) together with all x − sgn ​ ( t ) ​ t ​ x x-\textnormal{sgn}(t)tx for symmetries t t and chains x x is acyclic. In particular, it follows that quotienting by this sub-complex yields a chain complex with isomorphic homology. The latter leads to structural insight and a speedup in explicit computations. We also exhibit examples which show that acyclicity does not hold for general coefficient rings R R .

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider simplicial and cubical sets with symmetries
in the sense of
[
God73
, p.58–59]
and

[
Gra01
,
Gra09
]
. For a commutative ring
R
the
simplicial and cubical
R
-modules arising as chain groups in the chain
complexes computing the homology of the simplicial and cubical sets
inherit the symmetries. We study when for a group of
symmetries the submodule generated by
x-\textnormal{sgn}(t)tx
for

x
a simplicial or cubical chain and
t
an element of the symmetry group is an acyclic sub-complex.
Theorem 2.0
and
Theorem 2.0
answer this question
positively for fields
R
of characteristic
0
. In addition, since taking quotients by an acyclic sub-complex leaves the homology invariant, the results can be used to speed up homology
computations.
In
Section 5
we provide examples which show that the conditions on
R
in

Theorem 2.0
and
Theorem 2.0
cannot be relaxed easily.

To put this setting into the context of
classical results, consider the following two approaches to the
calculation of simplicial homology of an abstract simplicial complex
K
with coefficients in some commutative ring
R
.

For the first, we start with the simplicial set
\mathbf{Sym}_{a}(K)
whose
n
-simplices are the

(n+1)
-tuples
(v_{0},\dots,v_{n})
of not necessarily distinct vertices of
K
for which
\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}\}\in K
with the usual face and degeneracy maps. Clearly, the
n
-simplices are invariant under the
action of
\textnormal{Sym}(\{0,\ldots,n\})
permuting the coordinates and the action exhibits certain compatibilities with the face and degeneracy maps. Therefore, it
constitutes an example of a simplicial set with symmetries in the sense of this paper. The corresponding chain complex has
as the chain group in degree
n
the free
R
-module with the
n
-simplices as its basis and the simplicial differential
as maps. With the induced action of
\textnormal{Sym}(\{0,\ldots,n\})
the chain complex becomes a simplicial

R
-module with symmetries.

The second approach to the homology of
K
is the one taken
by most textbooks (see e.g.
[
Hat02
]
).
Choose a total order
\preceq
on the set of vertices of the simplicial complex.
Then consider the sub-complex of the chain complex
associated to
\mathbf{Sym}_{a}(K)
with basis the
(n+1)
-tuples
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})
for strictly increasing
v_{0}\prec\cdots\prec v_{n}
and

\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}\}\in K
.
The fact that the chain complex and its sub-complex
lead to isomorphic
homology groups is classical (see
[
ES52
]
).

It is usually proved by showing that the inclusion of the sub-complex is a quasi-isomorphism of chain complexes. The cokernel of this inclusion is generated by the cosets of all
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})-\textnormal{sgn}(t)\,(v_{t(0)},\ldots,v_{t(n)})
for permutations
t\in\textnormal{Sym}(\{0,\dots,n\})
and of all those
(v_{0},\ldots v_{n})
for
which there are
0\leq i<j\leq n
with
v_{i}=v_{j}
.
Since the inclusion is a quasi-isomorphism the cokernel is acyclic. For fields
R
of characteristic
0
this is a special case
of
Theorem 2.0
.
A more detailed account of this case, including the simplicial set of weakly increasing chains, is given in
Example 2.0
and in the discussion after
Theorem 2.0
.
An analogous result for general
simplicial
R
-modules with symmetries has been termed a ”possible conjecture” in the first paragraph of
[
God73
, p. 59]
. The results of
Section 5
show that this conjecture fails in general.

Our initial motivation for this work comes from a concrete example of a cubical set with symmetries: Discrete homotopy theory of graphs
G
(see
[
BBdLL06
,
BGJW19
,
CK24
]
). The corresponding homology theory

was first studied in
[
BCW14
]
. Discrete homotopy theory was
originally devised as a tool to analyze social networks represented as graphs
in
[
Atk74
,
Atk76
]
. For its applications it is crucial that the
algorithmic calculation of the homotopy and homology of
a given graph can be done as efficiently as possible. For homology, reducing the size of the chain groups is
the most effective way to achieve algorithmic speed ups.
The consequences of
Theorem 2.0
have already been shown to
lead to substantial speed ups in
homology computations (see
[
KK24
]
).

Example 2.0
provides a detailed description of this case.

The paper is organized as follows. In

(in

In

In

.
Finally in

2.1.
Simplex Categories

The
augmented simplex category

\Delta_{a}
is the category with objects
[n]
for integers

morphisms generated by

(fac)

face maps

\boldsymbol{d}_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{d}_{i}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[n-1]&\longrightarrow&[n]\\
j&\longmapsto&\begin{cases}j&\textnormal{for }j<i\\
j+1&\textnormal{otherwise}\end{cases}\end{array}\right.

(deg)

and
degeneracy maps

\boldsymbol{s}_{i}
for

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[n+1]&\longrightarrow&[n]\\

Furthermore, the
augmented simplex category with symmetries

\Delta_{a}^{sym}

\Delta_{a}
and whose maps are generated by face and degeneracy maps as well as

(trp)

simple transpositions

for

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{t}_{i}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[n]&\longrightarrow&[n]\\
j&\longmapsto&\begin{cases}i+1&\textnormal{for }j=i\\

k&\textnormal{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{array}\right.

We call a map in

which is a composition of simple transpositions a

. Clearly, for a fixed

symmetries can be identified with the symmetric group

S_{[n+1]}\cong S_{[[n]]}
. As usual, we define the sign

\textnormal{sgn}(\boldsymbol{t})
of a symmetry
\boldsymbol{t}=\boldsymbol{t}_{i_{1}}\cdots\boldsymbol{t}_{i_{k}}

if

is even and to
-1
if
k
is odd.
It is a simple exercise (see e.g.,
[
Gra01
]
)
that every map between the finite sets
[m]
and
[n]
can be written as a composition of these
\boldsymbol{d}_{i},\boldsymbol{s}_{i}
and
\boldsymbol{t}_{i}
, i.e.
\Delta^{\textnormal{sym}}_{a}
is a skeleton of the category of finite sets.

.

(1)

The

We also refer to symmetric simplicial sets as

(2)

A (symmetric) simplicial

The

For us, the categories

Example 2.0
.

Given a finite simplicial complex
K
consider the following two ways to construct a simplicial set from
K
.

(1)

The first simplicial set has
n
-simplices defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Sym}_{a}(K)[n]\coloneqq\big\{(v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{n}})\penalty\ \big|\penalty\ \{v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{n}}\}\in K\big\}.

Now equipped with the obvious face and degeneracy maps as well as symmetries acting by the permutations of the tuple entries
\mathbf{Sym}_{a}(K)
is a symmetric simplicial set.

(2)

For the second example choose a total order
\{\,v_{0}\prec v_{1}\prec\cdots\prec v_{m}\,\}
on the vertex set of
K
. Then for an
integer
n\geq-1

set

\displaystyle\mathbf{Or}_{a}(K)[n]\coloneqq\big\{(v_{i_{0}}\preceq v_{i_{1}}\preceq\cdots\preceq v_{i_{n}})\penalty\ \big|\penalty\ \{v_{i_{0}},v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{n}}\}\in K\big\}

equipped with the obvious face and degeneracy maps is a simplicial set. In general, this simplicial set cannot be endowed with symmetries.

Notice there is a canonical inclusion
j\colon\mathbf{Or}_{a}(K)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Sym}_{a}(K)
of simplicial sets.

Definition

(alternating face map complex)
.

The alternating face map complex

C_{*}X\colon\cdots\rightarrow C_{n}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{n}}C_{n-1}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{n-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}C_{0}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{-1}}C_{-1}X\rightarrow 0

of a (symmetric) simplicial
R
-module
X\in\mathbf{sMod}_{\textnormal{a}}^{\textnormal{(sym)}}(R)
is given by
C_{n}X=X([n])
and with differential

\partial_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}d_{i}.

The usual calculation shows that
\partial_{n-1}\circ\partial_{n}=0
, the
construction gives rise to a functor

C_{*}\colon\mathbf{sMod}_{\textnormal{a}}^{\textnormal{(sym)}}(R)\to\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbf{Mod}(R))

into the category
\mathbf{Ch}\big(\,\mathbf{Mod}(R)\,\big)
of chain complexes of
R
-modules.
For a (symmetric)
simplicial set
X
we write
C_{*}(X;R)

for the chain complex which is the image of
X
under

\mathbf{sSet}_{a}^{\textnormal{(sym)}}\overset{R(\cdot)}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{sMod}_{\textnormal{a}}^{\textnormal{(sym)}}(R)\overset{C_{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbf{Mod}(R)).

We can then postcompose with the homology functor

H_{*}

to obtain homology
R
-modules. If
X
is a (symmetric) simplicial set we
write
H_{*}\big(\,X;R\,\big)
for
H_{*}\big(\,C_{*}(X;R)\,\big)
and if
X
is a (symmetric) simplicial module we write
H_{*}\big(\,X\big)

for
H_{*}\big(\,C_{*}X\,\big)
.

If
X
is a symmetric simplicial set, the
R
-module
C_{n}(X;R)

inherits the symmetries and hence the action of the symmetric group,

but a priori it is not clear how and if the symmetries still
act on
H_{*}\big(\,X;R\,\big)
.
For example, let
K
be the abstract simplicial complex

2^{\{1,2\}}
. Calculating the differential of
\sigma\coloneqq(1,1,2)-(1,1,1)\in C_{2}(\,\mathbf{Sym}_{a}(K);R\,)
yields

but

\partial_{1}(t_{1}\,\sigma)=(2,1)+(1,2)-2(1,1).

In particular, the
kernel of
\partial_{n}
is not necessarily invariant under
symmetries.

Definition
.

(
\mathrm{D}
)

Let
X
be a (symmetric) simplicial
R
-module. The
degeneracy sub-complex

\mathrm{D}_{*}X
is the sub-complex of
C_{*}X
generated by the images of the degeneracy maps.

(
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}
)

Let
X
be a symmetric simplicial
R
-module. The
symmetry sub-complex

\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X
is the sub-complex of
C_{*}X
generated by chains of the form
x-\textnormal{sgn}(t)tx
for a symmetry
t
and a chain
x\in C_{n}X

The fact that
\mathrm{D}_{*}X
is indeed a sub-complex, i.e.
\partial_{n}\big(\,\mathrm{D}_{n}X\,\big)\subseteq\mathrm{D}_{n-1}X
follows immediately from (
Subsection A.1
), for
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X
this is will be shown in
Proposition 4.0
. While simple examples show that for a symmetric simplicial module
\mathrm{D}_{*}X
is not necessarily invariant
under symmetries, this clearly holds for
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X
. For a field
R
of characteristic different from two or more generally a ring

in which
2
is a unit, it follows from (
Subsection A.1
) that a degeneracy

in
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X
can be written as

and therefore
\mathrm{D}_{*}X
is contained in
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X
in this case. For arbitrary coefficients however, this is no longer true; in particular, the chain complex
\big(\mathrm{D}+\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}\big)_{*}X\coloneqq\mathrm{D}_{*}X+\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X

\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X
.

It is well known that the degeneracy sub-complex is acyclic for any simplicial set (see
[
Wei94
, Proof of Theorem 8.3.8]
). The sub-complex

is known to be acyclic for

is a simplicial complex (see
[
ES52
, p. 173-176]
). It has been suggested as a possible conjecture that the sub-complex

is acyclic for general simplicial

-modules
[

, first paragraph, p.59]

Our first main result, proved in

, states that this conjecture has a positive answer if

Theorem 2.0
.

Let
R
be a field of characteristic
0
and
X\in\mathbf{sMod}_{\textnormal{a}}^{\textnormal{{sym}}}(R)
a symmetric simplicial

-module,
then

In particular,

H_{*}(X)\cong H_{*}\big(\,C_{*}(X)/\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}(X)\,\big)

and
for a homology class
[\alpha]\in H_{n}\big(\,C_{*}(X)/\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}(X)\,\big)

and a symmetry

[t\alpha]=\textnormal{sgn}(t)[\alpha].

For arbitrary coefficients however, neither the symmetry sub-complex
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{*}X
nor the sum of sub-chain complexes

2.2.
Cube Categories

The cube category
\square
(with connections) is the category with objects
[1]^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, and morphisms generated by:

(fac)

face maps
\boldsymbol{d}_{i}^{\varepsilon}
for
1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{d}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[1]^{n-1}&\longrightarrow&[1]^{n}\\
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})&\longmapsto&(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},\varepsilon,\dots v_{n-1})\end{array}\right.

(deg)

degeneracies
\boldsymbol{s}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n+1

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[1]^{n+1}&\longrightarrow&[1]^{n}\\
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})&\longmapsto&(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{n+1})\end{array}\right.

(con)

connections
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{\varepsilon}
for
1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[1]^{n+1}&\longrightarrow&[1]^{n}\\
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})&\longmapsto&(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},m^{\varepsilon}(v_{i},v_{i+1}),\ldots v_{n+1})\end{array}\right.

where
m^{\varepsilon}
is the maximum (minimum) for
\varepsilon=0
(
\varepsilon=1
).

Consider the following sets of maps between the objects of the cube category.

(trp)

Simple transpositions
\boldsymbol{t}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{t}_{i}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[1]^{n}&\longrightarrow&[1]^{n}\\
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})&\longmapsto&(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},v_{i},\ldots,v_{n})\end{array}\right.

(rev)

Simple reversals
\boldsymbol{r}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{r}_{i}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}[1]^{n}&\longrightarrow&[1]^{n}\\
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})&\longmapsto&(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},1-v_{i},v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{n})\end{array}\right.

A composition of simple transpositions is called an order preserving symmetry, since these are precisely those symmetries, which preserve the component-wise order on
[1]^{n}=\{0<1\}^{n}
. A composition of simple reversals is called a reversal. For a subset
\emptyset\neq A\subset\{\textnormal{t},\textnormal{r}\}
the cube category with
A
-symmetries
\square^{A}
is the category whose objects are the
objects of the cube category and whose maps are generated by face maps, degeneracies, connections and the maps from the sets of maps specified in

A
. When we wish to remain agnostic with respect to the choice of
A
, we use the term
A

simplicial and cubical Moore complex are related by the functor
S
from
Theorem 3.0
.

Definition

(Moore Complex)
.

•

The
simplicial Moore complex
of a simplicial Abelian group
X
is the chain complex

N_{*}^{\Delta}X\colon\cdots\rightarrow N_{k}^{\Delta}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{k}}N_{k-1}^{\Delta}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{k-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}N_{0}^{\Delta}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{-1}}N_{-1}^{\Delta}X\rightarrow 0

given by

N_{k}^{\Delta}X={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{X[k]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathrm{D}_{k}(X)}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{X[k]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathrm{D}_{k}(X)}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{X[k]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathrm{D}_{k}(X)}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{X[k]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{D}_{k}(X)}$}}}

and

\partial_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}d_{i}.

•

The
cubical Moore complex
of a cubical Abelian group
X
is the chain complex

N_{*}^{\square}X\colon\cdots\rightarrow N_{k}^{\square}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{k}}N_{k-1}^{\square}X\xrightarrow{\partial_{k-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}N_{0}^{\square}X\rightarrow 0

with chain groups

N_{k}^{\square}X={\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{X([1]^{k})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{B_{k}(X)}$}}{\raisebox{4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{X([1]^{k})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{B_{k}(X)}$}}{\raisebox{3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{X([1]^{k})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{B_{k}(X)}$}}{\raisebox{2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{X([1]^{k})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{B_{k}(X)}$}}},

where
B_{k}(X)
is the sub-group of

X([1]^{k})
generated by the images of the cubical degeneracies and the positive connections

\partial_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}(d_{i}^{0}-d_{i}^{1}).

Note that in
[
Wei94
, Definition 8.3.6]
the simplicial Moore complex is defined
with chain groups
\bigcap_{i=0}^{k-1}\ker(d_{i})
in degree
k
and differentials
d_{k}
. It is well known that this constructions yields a chain complex
isomorphic to the Moore complex according to our definition (see e.g.,
[
GJ99
, Theorem III.2.1]
). The above definition is more convenient for our purposes.
For some, possibly empty
A\subseteq\{\textnormal{t},\textnormal{r}\}
and
X\in\mathbf{cMod}^{\textnormal{A}}(R)
, the
positive connection sub-complex

\mathrm{\Gamma}^{+}_{*}X
is the sub-complex of
C_{*}X
generated by positive connections.
This has been shown to be acyclic for cubical Abelian groups arising from cubical sets in
[
BGJW21
]
.

Corollary 3.0
.

Let
X\in\mathbf{cMod}^{\textnormal{A}}(R)
. The positive connection sub-complex
\mathrm{\Gamma}^{+}_{*}X
is isomorphic to the simplicial degeneracy sub-complex of
SX
. In particular, it holds that

N_{*}^{\square}X=N_{*-1}^{\Delta}SX.

Proof.

In every degree
n
, it holds that
C_{n}X=SX[n-1]
, which is the quotient of
X([1]^{n})
by the cubical degeneracies. The simplicial degeneracies of
SX
are, by definition of
S
, precisely the positive connections of
X
. Therefore
we have,

\mathrm{D}_{n-1}SX\cong\mathrm{\Gamma}^{+}_{n}X.

Thus the simplicial Moore complex of
SX
in degree
(n-1)
is obtained from
X([1]^{n})
by quotienting with the sum of the cubical degeneracies and the positive connections of
X
. By definition, this is the same as the cubical Moore complex
N_{n-1}^{\square}X

of
X
.
Here, the fact that the differentials agree follows from
Corollary 3.0
.
∎

The following result has been shown in
[
BGJW21
]
, but the theory developed so far allows us to give a different proof.

Corollary 3.0
.

For a cubical Abelian group
X
, the positive connection sub-complex
\mathrm{\Gamma}^{+}_{*}X
is acyclic.

Proof.

By
Corollary 3.0
, it holds that
\mathrm{\Gamma}^{+}_{*}X=\mathrm{D}_{*-1}SX
. Using this, the claim follows directly from the fact that the simplicial degeneracy sub-complex of a simplicial abelian group is acyclic (
[
Wei94
, Proof of Theorem 8.3.8]
).
∎

Note, that in general

The map

Given a permutation

with permutation matrix

where rows and columns indexed by

. For

0
or column indexed by
0

The last part follows from the fact that the inverse of a permutation matrix is given by transposition.

Postcomposing

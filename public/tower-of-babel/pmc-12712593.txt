Editorial: Advancing the science and practice of health care transition
Authors: Cecily L. Betz
Published: 2025

The field of health care transition has advanced considerably since it was first introduced as a developmental milestone of care for youth and young adults with childhood acquired chronic conditions and disabilities four decades ago in the United States.1 HCT studies published in the genesis period of this field of research and practice internationally were from primarily English-speaking countries (i.e. England, Ireland, Australia) as reported in the first review of the literature.2 The findings of this early narrative review reflected the exploratory investigative perspectives of the field. Studies were conducted to achieve an understanding of the landscape of needs, the challenges encountered in accessing adult-focused care and often reported ambiguous criteria for initiating the transfer of care. Among the limitations cited with the emerging literature were the lack of theoretical frameworks to guide the research, rigorous research designs, diverse samples, and limited use of tools with substantive psychometric properties.2

Now, forty years later, recognizable advancements in the field have been made. The reach of HCT literature generated has spread more broadly throughout the world beyond English-speaking countries. Studies are being published from low resourced areas of the world, such as the countries in Africa,3 Asia,4 and the Middle East.5

Early proposed models of care were limited in scope, primarily focused on the transfer to adult-focused health care providers. Among the earliest HCT service model recommendations proffered were transfer of care services that were specialty focused, primary care focused, family care focused and institutionally based.6 Now, the HCT model has evolved to be more comprehensive with a long-term planning focus.7, 8, 9, 10

Although advances have been accomplished in the HCT field of research and practice, improvements are needed. Atheoretical approaches to conducting studies persist as evidenced by the findings of systematic and scoping reviews recently published. Studies are needed that are based on theoretical frameworks that delineate the variables under study and their association with facilitating or impeding proposed outcomes. A theoretical model assists with providing an understandable strategy for conducting the study and interpreting the findings. These reviews suggest more work is needed to fully understand the evidence needed for interventions to promote successful outcomes for young adults and their families.11, 12, 13, 14

Other limitations exist that continue to permeate the research literature. It is imperative that researchers carefully consider the use of measurement tools that have substantive psychometrics.15, 16 Papers have been published and continue to be published using tools that lack reliability and validity metrics that are essential to ensuring the findings reported are trustworthy. It is essential that those involved in research and quality improvement activities use instruments that have been rigorously tested before applying their use. There are resources available for researchers to conduct studies that facilitate empirical advancement such as the COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN) for guidance.17, 18, 19, 20 The use of tools that are not psychometrically valid and reliable will impede research advancements.

More rigorous research designs, such as randomized control trials are needed to test the effectiveness of interventions, although it may not be feasible for those potentially enrolled in the control group. It is somewhat problematic to account for those who do not receive comparable services, unless another type of design is used such as a cross over or parallel design or a waitlist control/comparison group.17 It has been suggested that the intervention studies reported were initially developed as clinically-focused programs rather than developed for empirical purposes, which is an understandable development in an emerging field of practice and research.17 To that end, more specific details are needed that describe the contours of theoretically based interventions tested as can be delineated with the use of the TIDieR Checklist.21 Development of rigorously tested interventions would facilitate replications in other settings and with diverse populations. Currently, the level of evidence reported by RCTs are insufficient for evidence-based HCT services.22

As the field enlarges new insights and understanding will evolve that will generate fresh perspectives about effective and evidence-based approaches for the provision of HCT models of care and successful outcomes for youth and young adults with childhood acquired chronic conditions and disabilities. It is evident that more research and quality improvement efforts are needed to improve HCT services and outcomes for youth with childhood acquired conditions as evidenced by the relatively small percentages of those in the United Staes who received HCT services as reported in national surveys.23, 24, 25

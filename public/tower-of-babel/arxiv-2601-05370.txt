The Brauer Group of B ​ G BG and Gerbe Structures of Moduli Spaces
Rose Lopez

Abstract
. We study the μ N \mu_{N} -gerbe of curves of genus g g with an order N N automorphism, and explore what corresponding H 2 H^{2} -cohomology classes the components of this stack can have. In particular, we look at curves whose quotients by the order N N automorphism are genus 0, and completely determine the Brauer classes of these gerbes. The key technical input is the calculation of the Brauer group of B ​ G BG , for G G a smooth connected semisimple linear algebraic group.



1.
Introduction

The Brauer group of a field
k
is an abelian group which classifies Morita equivalence classes of central simple algebras over
k
with multiplication given by tensor product. The Brauer group has been generalized to schemes and established as a cohomological invariant of a scheme by Grothendieck in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Brauer groups have been extended to stacks more recently, and various work has been done computing Brauer groups of stacks, for example, in
[
3
,
19
,
12
,
1
,
2
,
5
]
.

In
[
2
, Section 5]
, the Leray spectral sequence is used to compute the Brauer group of
BG
for a finite linearly reductive group
G
, and in
[
14
, Section 6]
, the authors calculate the Brauer group of
BG
for a finite étale group scheme
G
. We calculate the (cohomological) Brauer group of
BG
for more general
G
in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a smooth connected semisimple linear algebraic group over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
\widetilde{G}
be the universal cover of
G
, and
B
the fundamental group of
G
, which fit into an exact sequence
1\to B\to\widetilde{G}\to G\to 1
. Let
X(B):=\mathrm{Hom}(B,\mathbb{G}_{m})
be the character group of
B
. Then

H^{i}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k^{*}&i=0\\
0&i=1\\
X(B)&i=2.\\
\end{array}\right.

For
i=2
, an isomorphism
X(B)\to H^{2}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is given by
(\chi:B\to\mathbb{G}_{m})\mapsto\chi_{*}(B\widetilde{G})
, where
\chi_{*}
is the pushforward map

\chi_{*}:H^{2}(BG,B)\to H^{2}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

With the understanding of the Brauer group of
BG
, we may also understand the classes of Brauer-Severi varieties over
BG
, which we call
Brauer-Severi stacks
(see Section
3
). Classically, a Brauer-Severi variety of dimension
n
over a field
k
is a variety
X
over a field
k
that is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\overline{k}}
after base change to an algebraic closure of
k
. Its obstruction to being isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
itself is the existence or non-existence of a line bundle of degree 1 on
X
, which would then be isomorphic to
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1)
. More generally, a Brauer-Severi stack of relative dimension
n
is a smooth proper morphism
\mathscr{X}\to S
which is étale locally isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{S}
. The existence of a line bundle of degree 1 on
\mathscr{X}
no longer guarantees that
\mathscr{X}
is globally isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{S}
, but rather only guarantees the existence of a rank
n+1
vector bundle
V
on
S
such that
\mathscr{X}\cong\mathbb{P}V
.

There is an equivalence of categories between Brauer-Severi varieties of relative dimension
n
over
S
and
\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}
-torsors over
S
. The image
\delta([X])\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m})
of the class of
X
in
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1})
via the short exact sequence
1\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}\to\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}\to 1
is equal to the class of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe of degree 1 line bundles on
X
, which we call the
Brauer class of the Brauer-Severi variety
X
. It is trivial if and only if there is a degree 1 line bundle on
X
.

We study Brauer-Severi stacks of the form
[\mathbb{P}V/G]\to BG
, and prove the following theorem which calculates thier Brauer classes.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
V
be a representation of
\widetilde{G}
, acting via
\rho:\widetilde{G}\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that
B\subseteq\widetilde{G}
acts by a character
\chi:B\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Then we get an induced action of
G
on
\mathbb{P}V
, and
[\mathbb{P}V/G]\to BG
is a Brauer-Severi stack over
BG
. Let
\mathscr{G}
be the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe of degree 1 line bundles on
[\mathbb{P}V/G]
over
BG
. Then
\chi^{-1}_{*}(B\widetilde{G})=\mathscr{G}
, and thus the class of
\mathscr{G}
, which is the Brauer class of
[\mathbb{P}V/G]\to BG
in
H^{2}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})=X(B)
, is given by
\chi^{-1}
.

The initial motivation for studying the Brauer group and Brauer-Severi stacks on
BG
was to understand the gerbe structure of the stack of genus
g
curves with an automorphism of order
N
, which we now explain.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{g}
be the moduli stack of smooth curves of genus
g>1
. The inertia stack
I(\mathscr{M}_{g})
is the stack with objects pairs
(X\in\mathscr{M}_{g},\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(X))
and morphisms are isomorphisms
\phi:X^{\prime}\to X
such that
\phi\circ\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha\circ\phi
. The inertia
I(\mathscr{M}_{g})
is a disjoint union

I(\mathscr{M}_{g})=\coprod_{N\in\mathbb{N}>0}I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g}),

such that for
(X,\alpha)\in I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
,
\alpha
has order
N
. We call the substacks
I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
the
N
th twisted sectors
of the inertia stack.

Since every object of
I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
has an order
N
automorphism, this stack is a
\mu_{N}
-gerbe, and corresponds to some class
[I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})]\in H^{2}(\mu_{N})
. We look at the components of
I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
consisting of curves whose quotients by the order
N
automorphism
\alpha
has genus 0 and completely determine the
Brauer classes
of these gerbes. For a
\mu_{N}
-gerbe
\mathscr{G}\to S
with class
[\mathscr{G}]\in H^{2}(S,\mu_{n})
, its pushout to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
under the inclusion
\mu_{N}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe
\mathscr{G}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}\to S
and its
Brauer class
is given by
[\mathscr{G}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}]\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

Given an object
(X,\alpha)
of
I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
, we may take the quotient of
X
by the action of
\mu_{N}
induced by
\alpha
to get a Galois cover with group
\mu_{N}
,
\pi:X\to C=X/\mu_{N}
. The quotient curve
C
can take many possible genera with varying ramification divisors. Following
[
15
]
, using ideas from
[
16
]
, to specify which genera and degrees of ramification divisors are allowed on
C
, we define a
g
-admissible datum
to be an
N+1
-tuple
A=(g^{\prime},N,d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1})
such that
g^{\prime}\leq g
and
N>1
, which satisfies Riemann-Hurwitz

(1.2.1)

2g-2=N(2g^{\prime}-2)+\sum_{i}d_{i}(N-\gcd(i,N))

and the structural equation of abelian covers

(1.2.2)

\sum_{i}id_{i}=0\pmod{N}.

Define the stack
\mathscr{M}_{A}
to be the stack with objects

\mathscr{M}_{A}(S)=\{(f:C\to S,D_{1},\ldots D_{N-1},\mathscr{L},\phi)\}

where
f:C\to S
is a smooth curve of genus
g^{\prime}
, the
D_{i}
are disjoint divisors of degree
d_{i}
, that is, sections of
(\mathrm{Sym}_{S}^{d_{i}}C\setminus\Delta_{d_{i}})\to S
, where
\Delta_{d_{i}}
is the big diagonal,
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle on
C
and
\phi:\mathscr{L}^{\otimes N}\to\mathscr{O}_{C}(\sum iD_{i})
is an isomorphism. Morphisms will be defined in Section
4
.

Every object of
\mathscr{M}_{A}
corresponds to a
\mu_{N}
-cover
X\to C
of a smooth genus
g
curve over
C
, ramified over the
D_{i}
. Conversely, every
\mu_{N}
-cover with the covering curve having genus
g
corresponds to some object of
\mathscr{M}_{A}
for some
g
-admissible datum
A
. We roughly explain this correspondence in Section
4
. Let
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}
be the substack of
\mathscr{M}_{A}
such that the corresponding
X
is connected. The following theorem relates
I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
to the
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
15
, 2.2, Corollary 1]
,
[
16
, Theorem 2.1, Proposition 2.1]

Fix
g,N>1
. Then

I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})=\coprod_{A}\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}
, where the disjoint union ranges over all
g
-admissible
A=(g^{\prime},N,d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1})
.

When
g^{\prime}=0
, either
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}=\mathscr{M}_{A}
or
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}=\emptyset

[
15
, 2.2, Remark 3]
. Since we only study the case when
g^{\prime}=0
in this paper, it suffices to study the
\mathscr{M}_{A}
as defined above, and then only a subset of such
\mathscr{M}_{A}
will correspond to components of
I_{N}(\mathscr{M}_{g})
To study the
\mathscr{M}_{A}
, we define a moduli stack
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
that contains
\mathscr{M}_{A}
as an open substack, namely, we allow the divisors to coincide.

When
g^{\prime}=0
,
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
, can be realized as a
\mu_{N}
-gerbe over a stack
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
, which is a composition of Brauer-Severi stacks over the moduli space of genus 0 curves,
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
. Using Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, and a stacky version of a theorem of Gabber
[
8
, Theorem 2, page 193]
, which relates the Brauer group of a Brauer-Severi variety to that of its base, we compute the Brauer group of
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
and calculate the class of
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
in the Brauer group of
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N,d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
N|d:=\sum id_{i}
and

g:=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(-2N+\sum_{i}id_{i}(N-\gcd(i,N))+2\right)

is an integer greater than 1. Let
A=(0,N,d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1})
. If all
d_{i}
are even,
H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and if any
d_{i}
is odd,
H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
A=(0,N,d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1})
be as above. Then
[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}]=[\gamma^{*}\mathscr{G}_{d/N}]\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
, where
\mathscr{G}_{d/N}
is the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
of degree
d/N
line bundles on genus 0 curves, and
\gamma:\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}\to B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
is the natural forgetful map. In particular, if all
d_{i}
are even and
d/N
is odd, then
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is nontrivial of order 2, otherwise,
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is trivial. Moreover, since the restriction map on Brauer groups is injective, the same holds for
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}
where the divisors are disjoint.

2.
Brauer Group of
BG

Let
G
a smooth connected semisimple linear algebraic group over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
\widetilde{G}
be the universal cover of
G
, and
B
the fundamental group of
G
, which exist by
[
7
, Corollary 4.6]
. Then
\widetilde{G}
is also a smooth connected semisimple linear algebraic group,
B
is finite and diagonalizable, thus a finite abelian group, and we have an exact sequence

1\to B\to\widetilde{G}\to G\to 1,

with the image of
B
in the center of
\widetilde{G}
.

Let

X(-):(\text{algebraic groups}/k)\to\mathrm{Sets}

be
H\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathbb{G}_{m})
, where
\mathrm{Hom}
refers to homomorphisms of algebraic groups.

We may view
k^{*}\subseteq H^{0}(H,\mathscr{O}_{H}^{*})=H^{0}(H,\mathbb{G}_{m})
as constant functions, and we have a surjection
H^{0}(H,\mathbb{G}_{m})\to X(H)
, by
f\mapsto f/f(e)
, which gives us a split exact sequence

0\to k^{*}\to H^{0}(H,\mathbb{G}_{m})\to X(H)\to 0,

and thus
H^{0}(H,\mathbb{G}_{m})=k^{*}\oplus X(H)
(
[
17
, Theorem 3]
,
[
18
, Lemma 6.5 (iii)]
,
[
7
, Corollary 2.2]
). Since
G
is semisimple,
X(G)=0
.

We have
\widetilde{G}\to G
a
B
-torsor, and
B\widetilde{G}\to BG
a
B
-gerbe. Given a character
\chi:B\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
of
B
, we get pushforward maps

\chi_{*}:H^{1}(G,B)\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{G}_{m}),\ \ \chi_{*}:H^{2}(BG,B)\to H^{2}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m}).

Thus we get morphisms

X(B)\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathrm{Pic}(G),\ \ X(B)\to H^{2}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathrm{Br}(BG),

given by
(\chi:B\to\mathbb{G}_{m})\mapsto\chi_{*}(\widetilde{G})
and
(\chi:B\to\mathbb{G}_{m})\mapsto\chi_{*}(B\widetilde{G})
, respectively.

It follows from
[
18
, Corollary 6.11, Remark 6.11.3]
that the first map is an isomorphism, also using the fact that
X(\widetilde{G})=0
and
\mathrm{Pic}(\widetilde{G})=0
. That the second map is an isomorphism is the statement of Theorem
1.1
, rewritten below for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field,
G
a smooth connected semisimple linear algebraic group over
k
, and
\widetilde{G}
and
B
as above. Then

H^{i}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k^{*}&i=0\\
0&i=1\\
\widehat{B}&i=2.\\
\end{array}\right.

For
i=2
, an isomorphism
\widehat{B}\to H^{2}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is given by
(\chi:B\to\mathbb{G}_{m})\mapsto\chi_{*}(B\widetilde{G})
, where
\chi_{*}
is the pushforward map

\chi_{*}:H^{2}(BG,B)\to H^{2}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

Proof.

We use the spectral sequence
E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{q}(U_{p},\mathbb{G}_{m})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(BG,\mathbb{G}_{m})
of the standard simplicial cover
U_{\bullet}\to BG
(
[
6
, 5.2.1.1]
) coming from
\mathrm{Spec}\ k\to BG
.

Here,
U_{0}=\mathrm{Spec}\ k
, and
U_{p}=U_{p-1}\times_{BG}\mathrm{Spec}\ k
for all
p\geq 1
. We have
p+1
projection maps
U_{p}\to U_{p-1},

each of which project onto all but one copy of
\mathrm{Spec}\ k
. For all
p\geq 1
,
U_{p}\cong G^{p}
. In particular,
U_{1}=\mathrm{Spec}\ k\times_{BG}\mathrm{Spec}\ k\cong G
and
U_{2}\cong G\times G
. The two projections

q_{1},q_{2}:\mathrm{Spec}\ k\times_{BG}\mathrm{Spec}\ k\to\mathrm{Spec}\ k

are both given by the structure map
\pi:G\to\mathrm{Spec}\ k
. The three projections

p_{23},p_{13},p_{12}:\mathrm{Spec}\ k\times_{BG}\mathrm{Spec}\ k\times_{BG}\mathrm{Spec}\ k\to\mathrm{Spec}\ k\times_{BG}\mathrm{Spec}\ k

are given by

p_{2},m,p_{1}:G\times G\to G,

respectively.

U_{\bullet}\to BG
can then be written

Definition 3.1

Let

Brauer-Severi stack of relative dimension

over

is a smooth, proper, morphism

of relative dimension

.

Remark 3.2

A smooth, proper morphism
\pi:\mathscr{X}\to S
such that for every geometric point

,
\mathscr{X}_{s}\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
, is a Brauer-Severi stack.

The following two propositions provide equivalent ways to view Brauer-Severi stacks in general, or Brauer-Severi stacks that are parameterized by a vector bundle.

Proposition 3.3

There is an equivalence of categories between Brauer-Severi stacks of relative dimension
n
over
S
and
\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}
-torsors over

.

Proof.

Given a Brauer-Severi stack

, we define a
\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}
-torsor

on

I_{\mathscr{X}}(T\to S)=\{\text{isomorphisms }\sigma:\mathscr{X}\times_{S}T\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}_{T}\},

where
\alpha\in\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(T)=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{T})
acts by composition with
\sigma
. The equivalence is given by sending
\pi:\mathscr{X}\to S
to

, and an isomorphism of Brauer-Severi stacks

is sent to

(\sigma:\mathscr{X}\times_{S}T\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}_{T})\mapsto(\sigma\circ(\phi^{-1}\times\mathrm{id}):\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\times_{S}T\cong\mathscr{X}\times_{S}T\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}_{T}).\qed

Example 3.4

For

Definition 3.6

The next simplest case to understand is

\mathscr{L}_{i}^{-1}
by
\zeta_{N}^{i}
, and we have an isomorphism

\mathscr{L}_{1}^{\otimes N}\cong\mathscr{O}_{Y}(\sum iD_{i}).

Conversely, given
(C,D_{1},\ldots,D_{N-1},\mathscr{L},\phi)
, the cover

is given by
X=\underline{\mathrm{Spec}}_{C}(\mathscr{O}_{C}\oplus\mathscr{L}_{1}^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{N-1}^{-1})
, where
\mathscr{L}_{1}=\mathscr{L}
and the remaining
\mathscr{L}_{i}
are determined by a formula involving the divisors, and the algebra structure of
\mathscr{O}_{C}\oplus\mathscr{L}_{1}^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{N-1}^{-1}
is determined by
\phi
. This is worked out in detail for
\mu_{N}
-covers of curves in
[
15
]
, using the general theory in
[
16
]
.

Given a
\widetilde{g}
-admissible datum
(g,N,d_{i},\ldots,d_{N-1}),
the condition for whether a corresponding covering curve is connected is as follows. Let
k
be the greatest common divisor of
N
and all of the
i
such that
d_{i}\neq 0
. Then the covering curve is connected if and only if
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes N/k}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C}(-\sum\frac{i}{k}D_{i})
has exactly order
k
in the Picard group of
C
(
[
15
, Remark 2.11]
). Note that if
C
has genus 0, then this line bundle always has order 1, and thus when
k=1
, all covers are connected and
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}=\mathscr{M}_{A}
, and when
k>1
, all covers are disconnected and
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

Thus, to study the
\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\prime}
with
g=0
, it suffices to study the
\mathscr{M}_{A}
with
k=1
, where
k
is defined as above. However, this condition on
k
does not affect our study of the
\mathscr{M}_{A}
, so we ignore it.

Let
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
be the stack with objects

\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}(S)=\{(f:C\to S,D_{1},\ldots D_{N-1},\mathscr{L},\phi)\}

where
f:C\to S
is a smooth genus
g
curve, the
D_{i}
are divisors of degree
d_{i}
, sections of
\mathrm{Sym}_{C/S}^{d_{i}}\to S
,
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle on
C
and
\phi:\mathscr{L}^{\otimes N}\to\mathscr{O}_{C}(\sum iD_{i})
is an isomorphism. That is, the divisors are no longer required to be disjoint. Morphisms are defined to be the same as for
\mathscr{M}_{A}
.

We next define a stack
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
, and show that it is the
\mu_{N}
-rigidification of
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
. First we include a bit of background about the Picard stack and Picard scheme.

4.1.
Picard stack and Picard scheme

Let
S
be a scheme and let
f:X\to S
be flat and proper. The Picard stack (
[
4
, Appendix]
) is the stack
\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}
with objects pairs
(T\to S,\mathscr{L}),
where
T
is an
S
-scheme and
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle on
X_{T}=X\times_{S}T
. Morphisms are pairs
(t,\sigma):(T\to S,\mathscr{L})\to(T^{\prime}\to S,\mathscr{L}^{\prime})
where
t:T\to T^{\prime}
is a morphism of
S
-schemes and
\sigma:t_{X}^{*}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{L}
is an isomorphism of line bundles, where
t_{X}:X_{T}\to X_{T^{\prime}}
.

Let
g:X\to X^{\prime}
be an
S
-morphism, and
g_{T}:X_{T}\to X_{T}^{\prime}
. Define

g^{*}:\mathscr{P}ic_{X^{\prime}/S}\to\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}

by
(T\to S,\mathscr{L})\mapsto(T\to S,g_{T}^{*}\mathscr{L})
.

We now define the relative Picard functor and Picard scheme, following
[
13
]
. Let
f:X\to S
be separated and of finite type.

The relative Picard functor is

\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}^{\mathrm{pre}}:(\mathrm{Sch}/S)\to\mathrm{Groups}

(T\to S)\mapsto\mathrm{Pic}(X_{T})/\mathrm{Pic}(T).

In some cases, this is representable, but in general it is not. Let
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}
denote the fppf sheafification.

Then an object of
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}(T\to S)
is represented by a pair
(T^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}^{\prime})
of an fppf covering
T^{\prime}\to T
and a line bundle
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
on
X_{T^{\prime}}
such that there exists an fppf covering
T^{\prime\prime}\to T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime}
where the pullbacks of
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
to
T^{\prime\prime}
under the two projections are isomorphic. We will call such objects
isomorphism classes of line bundles on
X_{T}
. Two representations
(T_{1}^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}_{1}^{\prime})
and
(T_{2}^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}_{2}^{\prime})
are equivalent if there exists an fppf covering
T^{\prime}\to T_{1}^{\prime}\times_{T}T_{2}^{\prime}
such that
\mathscr{L}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{L}_{2}^{\prime}
become isomorphic when pulled back to
X_{T^{\prime}}
.

Let
g:X\to X^{\prime}
as above, and define

g^{*}:\mathrm{Pic}_{X^{\prime}/S}\to\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}

by
(T^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}^{\prime})\mapsto(T^{\prime}\to T,g_{T^{\prime}}^{*}\mathscr{L}^{\prime})
.

Now suppose that
f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}=\mathscr{O}_{S}
and
f
is flat, proper, and cohomologically flat in dimension 0, that is, the formation of
f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}
commutes with base change. When
f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}\cong\mathscr{O}_{S}
holds universally and
f
has a section étale locally, the étale sheafification and the fppf sheafification agree. In particular, when
f
is smooth,
f
has an étale local section. We have a morphism
[-]:\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}\to\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}
sending a line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
X_{T}
to the pair
(T\to T,\mathscr{L})
, which we denote by
[\mathscr{L}]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f:X\to S
be flat, proper, and such that
f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}=\mathscr{O}_{S}
holds universally. Then
[-]:\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}\to\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}
realizes the Picard stack as a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over the Picard scheme.

Proof.

By descent for
\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}
, for any fppf covering
T^{\prime}\to T
, the category
\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}(T)
is equivalent to
\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}(T^{\prime}\to T)
, which has objects pairs
(\mathscr{L}^{\prime},\sigma:\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{pr}_{2}^{*}\mathscr{L}^{\prime})
, where
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
is a line bundle on
X_{T^{\prime}}
and
\sigma
is an isomorphism of line bundles on
X_{T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime}}
which satisfies the cocycle condition.

Given
(T^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}(T)
, we have some fppf cover
T^{\prime\prime}\to T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime}
such that the two pullbacks of
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
to
T^{\prime\prime}
are isomorphic. Let
(T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime\prime}\to T^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{L}^{\prime\prime})
denote the pullback of
(T^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}^{\prime})
to
T^{\prime\prime}
. The two pullbacks of
\mathscr{L}^{\prime\prime}
to
(T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime\prime})\times_{T^{\prime\prime}}(T^{\prime}\times_{T}T^{\prime\prime})
agree, so descent gives a line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
X_{T^{\prime\prime}}
. Given
\mathscr{L}_{1},\mathscr{L}_{2}\in\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}(T)
such that
(T\to T,\mathscr{L}_{1})
and
(T\to T,\mathscr{L}_{2})
are equivalent in
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}(T)
, there is an fppf cover
T^{\prime}\to T
such that
\mathscr{L}_{1}|_{T^{\prime}}\cong\mathscr{L}_{2}|_{T^{\prime}}
. Since
f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}=\mathscr{O}_{S}
holds universally,
H^{0}(X_{T},\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}}^{\times})=\mathscr{O}_{T}^{\times}
, and automorphisms of
\mathscr{L}\in\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}(T)
over
[\mathscr{L}]
are given by
\mathbb{G}_{m,T}
.
∎

Let
\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}^{d}
to be the open and closed substack of
\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}
consisting of line bundles of relative degree
d
, and similarly let
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}^{d}
be the open and closed subscheme of
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}
consisting of isomorphism classes of line bundles of relative degree
d
. We again have a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe
[-]:\mathscr{P}ic_{X/S}^{d}\to\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}^{d}
.

In what follows, we will denote elements
(T^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}^{\prime})
of
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/S}(T)
by a single symbol,
\ell
. By
\ell^{N}
, we mean the element
(T^{\prime}\to T,\mathscr{L}^{\prime\otimes N})
.

Define the stack

, and

.

, the Brauer class of
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
is either trivial or nontrivial of order 2. We now compute precisely what the Brauer class of
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
, and thus also
\mathscr{M}_{A}
, is.

We work with the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}_{\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

where
\mathbb{P}:=[\mathbb{P}V/\mathrm{PGL}_{2}]
is the universal curve of genus 0, as in Example
3.16
.

Consider the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
,

\mathscr{G}_{d/N}(S\to B\mathrm{PGL}_{2})=\{\text{degree }d/N\text{ line bundles on }S\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathbb{P}\}.

We have
[\mathscr{G}_{d/N}]=[d/N]\in H^{2}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

We will show that
[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}=[\gamma^{*}\mathscr{G}_{d/N}]
, where
[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is the Brauer class of
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
, that is, the image of
[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}]
under the map
H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mu_{N})\to H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
coming from the inclusion
\mu_{N}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
, and
\gamma^{*}\mathscr{G}_{d/N}
is the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
given by the cartesian product
\mathscr{G}_{d/N}\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
. In particular,

\gamma^{*}\mathscr{G}_{d/N}(S\to\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}})=\{\text{degree }d/N\text{ line bundles on }S\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathbb{P}\}.

We define a morphism of stacks
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}\to\gamma^{*}\mathscr{G}_{d/N}
by

(f:C\to S,D_{i},\mathscr{L},\phi)\mapsto((f:C\to S,D_{i},[\mathscr{L}]),\mathscr{L}).

We can identify
S\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathbb{P}
with the curve
C
, so to give an object of
\gamma^{*}\mathscr{G}_{d/N}
, we must give a degree
d/N
line bundle on
C
, namely
\mathscr{L}
.
The map on morphisms is the identity map, thus the map on stabilizers is the inclusion
\mu_{N}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
, so indeed
[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}=[\gamma^{*}\mathscr{G}_{d/N}]
.

If
d/N
is even, then
\mathscr{G}_{d/N}
is the trivial gerbe over
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
, and if
d/N
is odd, then it is nontrivial, isomorphic to
B\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. Together with the calculation of
H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
, the result follows. Finally, consider the open restriction map

\mathrm{res}:H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(\mathscr{N}_{A},\mathbb{G}_{m}),

which sends
[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}]\mapsto[\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}]
. Due to the injectivity of the restriction map on Brauer groups, we get the same result for
[\mathscr{M}_{A}^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}].

∎

Remark 5.1
.

In the case where
d
is even, we can actually write down the class of
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}
as a
\mu_{N}
-gerbe over
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
, rather than just the class of its pushout to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. However, as remarked in the introduction, since the restriction map is not injective on
H^{2}(\mu_{N})
, this is not sufficient to compute the
\mu_{N}
-class of the open substack
\mathscr{M}_{A}
, whose moduli we are interested in.

Our gerbe of interest,
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}\to\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
can be rewritten as follows,

\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}(f:S\to\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}})=\{\mathscr{L},\phi:\mathscr{L}^{\otimes N}\cong f^{\prime*}\mathscr{O}(\mathscr{D})\},

where
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}_{S}
(which we have identified with
C
),
\mathscr{D}=\sum_{i}\mathscr{D}_{i}
is the sum of the universal divisors on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
, and
f^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}_{S}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
.

Consider the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
, using notation from Corollary
3.15
,

\mathscr{G}_{d}(S\to B\mathrm{PGL}_{2})=\{\mathscr{O}(d)\text{ on }S\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathbb{P}\},

\mathscr{G}_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1}}^{1,\ldots,N-1}(S\to B\mathrm{PGL}_{2})=\left\{\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)\text{ on }S\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}\right\}.

We have

[\mathscr{G}_{d}]=[d]\in H^{2}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

\left[\mathscr{G}_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1}}^{1,\ldots,N-1}\right]=\left[\sum id_{i}\right]=[d]\in H^{2}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

by Corollary
3.15
and Example
3.17
. From this, we can conclude that
\mathscr{G}_{d}\cong\mathscr{G}_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{N-1}}^{1,\ldots,N-1}
, and the isomorphism comes from the fact that, whenever such line bundles exist,

\mathscr{O}(\mathscr{D})\cong\gamma^{\prime*}\mathscr{O}(d)\otimes\pi^{\prime*}\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1).

The existence of
\mathscr{O}(d)
on
\mathbb{P}
is equivalent to the existence of
\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)
on
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
, and both exist after pulling back to
\mathscr{G}_{d}
. If
d
is even, the class of
\mathscr{G}_{d}
is trivial, meaning that
\mathscr{O}(d)
exists on
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)
exists on
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
.

Thus, if
d
is even, we have
\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}\cong\mathscr{M}^{\prime}\otimes\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}
, where

\mathscr{M}^{\prime}(f:S\to\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}})=\{\mathscr{L}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}:\mathscr{L}^{\prime\otimes N}\cong f^{\prime*}\pi^{\prime*}\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)\},

\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}(f:S\to\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}})=\{\mathscr{L}^{\prime\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime}:\mathscr{L}^{\prime\prime\otimes N}\cong f^{\prime*}\gamma^{\prime*}\mathscr{O}(d)\}.

First, note that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the
\mu_{N}
-gerbe of
N
th roots of
\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)
on
\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}
,

\sqrt[N]{\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)}\left(f:S\to\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}}\right)=\{L,\phi:L^{\otimes N}\cong f^{*}\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)\text{ on }S\}.

A map is given by sending
(L,\phi)
to
(\pi^{*}L,\pi^{*}\phi)
. From the exact sequence

1\to\mu_{N}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 1
, we get

H^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mu_{N})\to H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m}),

and the first map thus sends the line bundle
\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)
to
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
, so under the second map,
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}\mapsto 0.
Thus,
[\mathscr{M}^{\prime}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}=[B\mathbb{G}_{m}]\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{N}_{A}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
, and thus
[\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}=[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}
.

Next, the same exact sequence gives

0=H^{1}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mu_{N})\to H^{2}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mathbb{G}_{m})\to H^{2}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mathbb{G}_{m})

with
H^{2}(B\mathrm{PGL}_{2},\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. The rightmost map sends
[\mathscr{G}_{d/N}]\mapsto[\mathscr{G}_{d}]=0
, so the class of
\mathscr{G}_{d/N}
comes from the pushout of a
\mu_{N}
-gerbe on
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
, which can be described as follows,

\mathscr{H}(f:S\to B\mathrm{PGL}_{2})=\{\mathscr{L},\phi:\mathscr{L}^{\otimes N}\cong f^{\prime*}\mathscr{O}(d)\text{ on }S\times_{B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\mathbb{P}\}.

Thus we see that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}=\gamma^{*}\mathscr{H}
, and

\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}\cong\mathscr{M}^{\prime}\otimes\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}\cong\sqrt[N]{\mathscr{O}(1,\ldots,N-1)}\otimes\gamma^{*}\mathscr{H}.

Moreover, this confirms that

[\overline{\mathscr{M}_{A}}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}=[\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}=[\gamma^{*}\mathscr{H}]^{\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}}=\gamma^{*}[\mathscr{G}_{d/N}].

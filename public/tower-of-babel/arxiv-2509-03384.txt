Abstract
. In this work, we develop a unified framework for quasidiagonal and Følner-type approximations of linear operators on Hilbert spaces. These approximations (originally formulated for bounded operators and operator algebras) involve sequences of non-zero finite rank orthogonal projections that asymptotically commute with the operator – either in norm (quasidiagonal) or in mean (Følner). Such structures guarantee spectral approximation results in terms of their finite sections. We extend this theory to unbounded, densely defined closable operators, establishing a generalization of Halmos’ classical result: every closable quasidiagonal operator is a compact perturbation of a closable block-diagonal operator on the same domain. Likewise, we introduce sparse Følner sequences and establish an interplay between quasidiagonal approximations and the existence of sparse Følner sequences. The theoretical developments are illustrated with explicit examples using different types of weighted shifts and applied to quantum mechanical models, including a detailed treatment of the Weyl algebra and its Schrödinger representation.

1.
Introduction

Approximation is one of the most important techniques in single operator theory and in operator algebras, allowing us to analyse complex objects in terms of limits of simpler ones (see, for example,
[
26
,
10
,
9
]
). Central to this line of inquiry are the concepts of

quasidiagonal
and
Følner approximations
. A bounded linear operator on a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space

T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is quasidiagonal if there exists an increasing sequence of finite rank orthogonal projections

{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging strongly to the identity
\mathbbm{1}
and commuting asymptotically with
T
in norm, i.e.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\,TP_{n}-P_{n}T\,\|=0.

(In the case of a separable C*-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, one requires the preceding condition for any element of the algebra.) The corresponding sequence of projection subspaces are called a filtration of the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Quasidiagonality was introduced by Halmos in the seventies in relation to the invariant subspace problem (see
[
23
,
41
]
).
Følner approximations in terms of a sequence of finite rank orthogonal projections
{\mathcal{R}}=\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

generalizes quasidiagonality in two ways. First, one does not require exhaustivity to the sequence of projections, and
second, the asymptotic condition is weaker in the sense that the limit is taken relative to the growth of the dimension of the underlying projection subspaces. Concretely,
{\mathcal{R}}
is a Følner sequence for
T
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|TR_{n}-R_{n}T\|_{1}}{\|R_{n}\|_{1}}=0\;,

where
\|\cdot\|_{1}
denotes the trace-class norm. The preceding condition can be equivalently stated with the Hilbert-Schmidt norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
.
Følner-type approximations were introduced by Connes in his celebrated article

[
15
, Section V]
, particularly in his classification of injective von Neumann factors. Both the motivation for this approximation and its name stem from considering group algebras of amenable groups, i.e., groups admitting a Følner sequence of finite subsets in the group.
(See Section
2
for precise statements as well as
[
31
,
4
]
for additional results.)

The
existence
of quasidiagonalizing or Følner sequences for an operator
T
or a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
has several structural consequences. The most prominent one in single operator theory is due to Halmos and states that any quasidiagonal operator is a compact perturbation of a block-diagonal operator. As an immediate consequence, any quasidiagonal Fredholm operator necessarily has index
0
. This result extends a classic area of research in the analysis of self-adjoint and normal operators due to Weyl, von Neumann, Berg and Sikonia
[
42
,
35
,
8
,
39
]
. In a similar vein, a separable C*-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
has a Følner sequence if and only if it admits an amenable trace
\tau
, i.e., a tracial state on
{\mathcal{A}}
that extends to a state
\psi
on
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
that has
{\mathcal{A}}
in its centralizer. Recent results also provide characterizations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras admitting amenable traces in terms of a matrix approximation given by a sequence of contractive completely positive maps which asymptotically commute in a normalized Hilbert-Schmidt norm, which makes contact with Voiculescu’s modern C*-algebraic approach to quasidiagonality (see
[
10
,
11
,
6
,
3
]
for details).

Moreover, the
explicit construction
of these sequences presents its own challenge. It allows numerical approximation of spectral objects of
T
(spectral measures, pseudospectrum, spectrum, etc.) in terms of the corresponding quantities of its finite sections given by
P_{n}TP_{n}
. In fact,
if
T
is quasidiagonal with respect to a filtration given by
{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, then there is in general convergence of pseudospectra, and in the normal case even better convergence results follow (see
[
12
,
5
]
for details and more results). Quasidiagonal approximations and the finite section method have been considered for unbounded operators in
[
25
]
.

In the more general context of Følner approximations there exist also interesting spectral approximations generalizing classical results
obtained for Toeplitz operators by Szegö in
[
40
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a C*-algebra admitting an amenable trace
\tau
or, equivalently, a Følner sequence
\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. For
T=T^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}
denote by
\mu_{T}
the spectral measure associated with the trace

\tau
and consider the corresponding (self-adjoint)
compressions
T_{n}:=P_{n}TP_{n}
. Denote by
\mu_{T}^{n}
the
probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
supported on the spectrum of
T_{n}
,
i.e.,

\mu_{T}^{n}(\Delta):=\frac{N_{T}^{n}(\Delta)}{\|R_{n}\|_{1}}\;,\quad\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}\quad\mathrm{Borel}\;,

where
N_{T}^{n}(\Delta)
is the number of eigenvalues of
T_{n}

(multiplicities counted) contained in
\Delta
. We say that

\left\{\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\,,\,\tau\right\}
is a
Szegö pair
for
{\mathcal{A}}
if for any self-adjoint element
T\in{\mathcal{A}}
we have

\mu_{T}^{n}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{w^{*}}\mu_{T}
,
i.e., for any continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{d_{n}}\Big(f(\lambda_{1,n})+\dots+f(\lambda_{d_{n},n})\Big)=\int f(\lambda)\,d\mu_{T}(\lambda)\;,\quad f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\;,

where
d_{n}:=\|R_{n}\|_{1}
and
\{\lambda_{1,n},\dots,\lambda_{d_{n},n}\}
are the eigenvalues
of
T_{n}
repeated according to their multiplicity.
We refer to
[
7
,
21
,
3
]
for additional results and to
[
32
]
for explicit
Følner sequences in the context of tensor products and crossed products.

While almost all of the existing literature has focused on
bounded
operators, many physically relevant operators such as the position operator
q
or momentum operator
p
satisfying the canonical commutation relation (in its simplest form and taking the reduced Plank’s constant
\hbar=1
)

(2)

qp-pq=i\mathbbm{1}\;

or standard quantum Hamiltonians are necessarily unbounded (cf.
[
44
,
43
]
).
In quantum mechanics,
Galerkin-type
approximations have been proposed as a method to approximate dynamics by restricting the problem to finite dimensional subspaces of increasing size, yet a systematic extension of Følner and quasidiagonal approximations to this setting remained underdeveloped.

In this article we address both the existence and construction of quasidiagonal and Følner sequences and their mutual relation for bounded and unbounded linear operators.
In the setting of unbounded operators we introduce in Section
3

natural seminorms and require that the operator domain is stable under finite rank projections. In this framework, we prove in Theorem
3.5
a generalization of Halmos’ theorem: any closable quasidiagonal operator is a compact perturbation of a block diagonal operator (on the same domain). Moreover,
we construct a quasidiagonalizing sequence of projections associated with any (closable) normal operator based on Berg’s method (see
[
8
]
) and a dyadic partition of the spectrum (see Subsections
2.1
and
3.1
). Since the definition of Følner sequence does not assume exhaustivity of the finite rank projections, we establish a natural interplay between quasidiagonality and Følner sequences: any quasidiagonal operator (bounded or unbounded) allows a
sparse Følner sequence
(see Subsection
2.2
for a precise definition and Corollary
3.6
). We will present along the way many concrete examples of operators on
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
, mainly in terms of unbounded weighted shifts. These examples will show, among other results, that for unbounded operators, a Følner sequence for the trace-class norm need not be a Følner sequence for the Hilbert-Schmidt norm. In these examples the propagation of the operator and the propagation of its commutator with the projection will play an important role.

Finally, we apply previous results to several mathematical structures associated to the canonical commutation relation (CCR): we show that the Weyl algebra, that is the complex algebra generated by

q
and
p
satisfies an algebraic version of amenability introduced by Gromov in

[
20
]
. This result confirms, at a purely algebraic level, that the canonical commutation relation generates amenable structures in the corresponding categories. In fact, it is shown in
[
30
, Theorems 3.1 and 4.1]
that every faithful and essential representation of Weyl C*-algebra and the resolvent C*-algebras (which encode a version of the commutation relation compatible with bounded operators) have Følner sequences of projections (see Definition
2.2
below and

[
33
,
13
]
).
We also apply our previous results, in particular the clases of examples mentioned before, to the Schrödinger representation of the Weyl algebra (necessarily) in terms of unbounded operators.

Notation:
We denote by
{\mathcal{H}}
a separable infinite dimensional complex Hilbert space.
The C*-algebras of bounded linear operators (resp.
compact operators) on
{\mathcal{H}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
(resp.
{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
). Moreover, the set
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
corresponds to the set of linear (possibly unbounded) and densely defined operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
For any
T\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
, we write
\|T\|_{a}
,
a=1,2,\dots
, for its norm in the Schatten-von Neumann class.

2.
Quasidiagonality and Følner sequences for bounded operators

We begin by mentioning the main definitions of quasidiagonality and Følner sequences in the context of
bounded operators on a Hilbert space. We also state some standard results needed later. For proofs and additional results we refer to
[
23
,
11
,
12
,
4
]
(see also references therein).

To motivate the notions introduced below recall that a bounded operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is called block-diagonal if there exists an exhausting
sequence
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite rank orthogonal projections reducing
T
, i.e., satisfying
[T,P_{n}]=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The block structure is induced by the mutually orthogonal sequence of projections
Q_{n}:=P_{n}-P_{n-1}
(putting
P_{0}:=0
) which satisfy
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{n}=\mathbbm{1}
(convergence in the strong operator topology) and decompose the Hilbert space and the operator into

{\mathcal{H}}=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{n}{\mathcal{H}}\quad\text{and}\quad T=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{n}T=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{n}TQ_{n}\;.

This notion has two natural generalizations:

Definition 2.1
.

Consider a filtration of the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, that is, a sequence of non-zero finite dimensional subspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\subset\cdots
such that
\overline{\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathcal{H}}_{n}}={\mathcal{H}}
, and let
P_{n}
be the finite rank orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
. For
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, we say that the sequence

{\mathcal{P}}:=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
quasidiagonalizing sequence for
T
if

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\|TP_{n}-P_{n}T\|}=0.

An operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is called
quasidiagonal
if it admits a quasidiagonalizing sequence.
Similarly, a separable set of operators
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
(in particular, a concrete C*-algebra)
is
quasidiagonal
if there exists a sequence
{\mathcal{P}}
that is quasidiagonalizing for every element of
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}:=\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-zero finite rank orthogonal projections. The sequence
{\mathcal{R}}
is called a
Følner sequence for
T
if

(4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|TR_{n}-R_{n}T\|_{1}}{\|R_{n}\|_{1}}=0\;.

If the previous condition holds for all elements in a separable set of operators
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, we say
{\mathcal{R}}
is a Følner sequence for
{\mathcal{T}}
.

(Notation: When, in addition,
{\mathcal{R}}:=\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is exhausting, we will often use the symbol
{\mathcal{P}}:=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as in Definition
2.1
.)

We summarize next some standard facts around these notions that will be revisited when we extend them for unbounded operators.

Remark 2.3
.

a)

Every bounded block-diagonal operator (e.g., by the Peter-Weyl theorem, any unitary representation of a compact group) is clearly quasidiagonal. Moreover,
any
sequence of projections associated with a filtration is quasidiagonalizing for any compact operator. As a consequence, quasidiagonalizing sequences are stable under compact perturbations due to the linearity of the commutator. In particular, bounded normal operators, and therefore self-adjoint and unitary ones, are quasidiagonal (see
[
8
, Theorem 1]
and
[
39
, Theorem 2]
or
[
16
,
14
]
).

b)

The notion of a Følner sequence for an operator can be understood as a quasidiagonality condition relative to the growth of the dimension of the underlying spaces. In fact, any quasidiagonalizing sequence is also a Følner sequence, showing that quasidiagonality is a stronger notion. The unilateral shift
S
on
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
is a prototype that has a Følner sequence
(take the orthogonal projection
P_{n}
onto the subspace generated by the first
n
canonical basis elements in
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
) but can not be quasidiagonal since it has Fredholm index
-1
. Finally, in the context of bounded operators, in Eq. (
4
) one can take, equivalently, the Hilbert-Schmidt norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
instead of the trace-class norm
(see, e.g.,
[
7
, Lemma 1]
).

c)

Operator algebras come naturally into this analysis. For example, if
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is a quasidiagonal operator, then the C*-algebra
C^{*}(T)
generated by
T
(i.e., the closure of all polynomials in
T
,
T^{*}
and
\mathbbm{1}
) is also quasidiagonal. Indeed, by Halmos’ theorem,
T=B+K
, where
B
is block-diagonal and
K
is compact. Hence, if
B
is block-diagonal with respect to
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, then
C^{*}(T)\subset\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}^{\prime}+{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
, so the sequence
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is quasidiagonalizing for every element in
C^{*}(T)
. (Here
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}^{\prime}
denotes the operators in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
commuting with the set of projections.) Furthermore,
given a sequence of projections
{\mathcal{P}}
associated with a filtration of
{\mathcal{H}}
, the set of operators for which
{\mathcal{P}}
is a quasidiagonalizing/Følner sequence is a closed
*
-subalgebra of
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, i.e., a C*-algebra (see, for example,
[
10
, Chapter 7]
).

d)

Since Følner sequences need not be exhaustive, we recall here that in Theorem 3.2 of
[
31
]
a characterization of operators having a Følner sequence, but not an exhaustive one, is given. Such an operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
can be represented as an orthogonal sum
T=F\oplus\widetilde{T}
on
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}\oplus\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}
, where
F
is a finite-dimensional operator and
\widetilde{T}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}})
has no Følner sequence in a strong sense
(see
[
31
, Definition 3.1]
). This characterization can be also stated in other areas: Theorem 3.9 in
[
1
]
for algebraic Følner sequences or Section 4 in
[
2
]
in the context of C*-algebras.
(Note also that exhaustive sequences of finite rank projections are called
proper
in the references mentioned in this item since they are associated to proper filtrations of the underlying Hilbert space.)

2.1.
Construction of quasidiagonalizing sequences for bounded normal operators

As mentioned before, any bounded normal operator
N
has a quasidiagonalizing sequence.
There are several approaches to show this result based on a dyadic partition of a compact set containing the spectrum of the operator. We will not follow Berg’s ingenious construction for
normal operators (see
[
8
]
), but go along the route proposed in

[
14
, Section 2.2]
. We adapt their strategy to construct first an explicit quasidiagonalizing sequence for a self-adjoint operator and, then show that this sequence can be used as well for normal operators using a theorem due to Halmos
[
24
]
stating that any
normal operator is a continuous function of a self-adjoint operator.
One reason for following this strategy is that the first step will be enough in many applications, in particular, in many examples appearing in mathematical physics where one has to approximate the self-adjoint Hamiltonian of a system. In Section
3.1
we extend this construction to unbounded operators.

We divide the construction of the quasidiagonal sequences into two steps:

Step 1:
We first show how to construct a quasidiagonalizing sequence
{\mathcal{P}}:=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for a self-adjoint
A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Let
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a basis for
{\mathcal{H}}
and fix
\varepsilon>0
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, take
\{I_{n,j}\}_{j=1}^{k_{n}}
to be a partition of
[-{\lVert A\rVert},{\lVert A\rVert}]
into intervals of length at most
\varepsilon/2^{n}
(for instance, choosing the dyadic refinement at each step). We define
P_{1}=Q_{1}
as the orthogonal projection onto the finite dimensional subspace
\operatorname{span}\{E_{A}(I_{1,j})\omega_{1}\mid 1\leq j\leq k_{1}\},
where
E_{A}
denotes the spectral resolution of
A
. The rest of the projections in
{\mathcal{P}}
are obtained recursively. Given
P_{n-1}
, let

A_{n}=P_{n-1}^{\perp}AP_{n-1}^{\perp}
with spectral resolution
E_{A_{n}}
. We define
P_{n}=P_{n-1}+Q_{n}
, where
Q_{n}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
\operatorname{span}\{E_{A_{n}}(I_{n,j})P_{n-1}^{\perp}\omega_{n}\mid 1\leq j\leq k_{n}\}
.

Step 2:
The previous step also provides a quasidiagonalizing sequence for bounded normal operators if we use the fact that every bounded normal operator belongs to the abelian
C*-algebra generated by some self-adjoint operator. In fact, let
N\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a normal operator on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and denote by
E_{N}
its spectral resolution. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider a Borel partition
\{\Delta_{n,j}\}_{j=1}^{k_{n}}
of
\sigma(N)
with diameter at most
\varepsilon/2^{n}
and let
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an enumeration of
\{E_{N}(\Delta_{n,j})\mid n\in\mathbb{N},1\leq j\leq k_{n}\}.
The normal operator
N
belongs to the C*-algebra generated by the self-adjoint operator
A_{N}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}3^{-n}((2E_{n})-\mathbbm{1})
. By Remark
2.3
c), this implies that any quasidiagonalizing sequence for
A_{N}
is also quasidiagonalizing for
N
, in particular, the one constructed in Step 1.

Remark 2.4
.

The sequences
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}
decompose
A
into a block-diagonal operator
\sum_{n}Q_{n}AQ_{n}
and a compact operator
K=\sum_{n}Q_{n}AP_{n}^{\perp}+P_{n}^{\perp}AQ_{n}
(convergence of the series in norm) with
\|K\|<\varepsilon
(see, for example,
[
23
,
16
]
).
Since every (finite rank) compression
Q_{n}AQ_{n}
is self-adjoint, it can be diagonalized, and this procedure provides thus a proof for Weyl’s Theorem (see
[
42
]
). In
[
8
]
, Berg showed that every normal operator is also the compact perturbation of a diagonal operator. Since the eigenvectors of the diagonal operator are obtained within the proof, this approach allows to define an alternative quasidiagonalizing sequence for the normal operator by taking an exhaustive sequence of projections
P_{n}
projecting onto the finite dimensional subspaces generated by the first
n
eigenvectors.

2.2.
Sparse Følner sequences

The aim of this section is to introduce sparse Følner sequences and demonstrate how this concept can be applied, in particular, to quasidiagonal operators. This allows us to find sequences of coordinate projections that not only satisfy the Følner condition but are also sparse, which might be useful in areas like numerical analysis and operator approximation (see, for example,
[
21
,
26
]
).

In this regard, it is worth remarking that in Fourier Analysis and Number Theory, sparse sequences (often called
lacunary
when gaps grow exponentially) are integer sequences whose elements grow rapidly enough to be
thin
in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Indeed, in Fourier Analysis, lacunary sequences index Fourier series with remarkable properties: they often behave like sums of independent random variables (Salem–Zygmund Theorem) and converge almost everywhere despite large gaps. Likewise, in Number Theory, sparse sequences appear in additive combinatorics as thin sets avoiding arithmetic progressions and in studying additive bases (see
[
28
,
37
,
34
]
as basic references regarding their role).

Definition 2.5

(Sparse Følner Sequence)
.

Let
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{H}}
. A sequence of non-zero, finite rank orthogonal projections
{\mathcal{R}}:=\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is called
sparse
if each
R_{n}
is the orthogonal projection onto

\text{span}\{e_{k_{1}},e_{k_{2}},\dots,e_{k_{n}}\}
, where
\{k_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a strictly increasing subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
. If, in addition,
{\mathcal{R}}
is a Følner sequence for
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
we call it a
sparse Følner sequence
.

To motivate the difference between quasidiagonal and Følner-type approximations for sparse sequences
we consider the example of a rank 1 compact operator. Recall from
Remark
2.3
that any sequence
{\mathcal{P}}:=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
associated with a filtration is quasidiagonalizing for any compact operator
K\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
. By the singular value decomposition, every compact operator
K
can be written as
K=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}\otimes v_{j}
, for some
u_{j},v_{j}\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Hence, the sequence of finite rank operators
\{K^{(N)}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by
K^{(N)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}\otimes v_{j}
converges to
K
in operator norm, and we have

[K^{(N)},P_{n}]=\sum_{j=1}^{N}[u_{j}\otimes v_{j},P_{n}]=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(u_{j}-P_{n}u_{j})\otimes P_{n}v_{j}+P_{n}u_{j}\otimes(P_{n}v_{j}-v_{j}).

Since
{\mathcal{P}}
is exhaustive, we have
\lim_{n}{\lVert u_{j}-P_{n}u_{j}\rVert}=0
and
\lim_{n}{\lVert v_{j}-P_{n}v_{j}\rVert}=0
for all
j
, and thus,

{\lVert[K^{(N)},P_{n}]\rVert}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\lVert u_{j}-P_{n}u_{j}\rVert}{\lVert P_{n}v_{j}\rVert}+{\lVert P_{n}u_{j}\rVert}{\lVert P_{n}v_{j}-v_{j}\rVert}\mathop{\rightarrow}_{n\to\infty}=0\;

which implies
\lim_{n}{\lVert[K,P_{n}]\rVert}=0
.

Note that if now we consider a sparse sequence
\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for some strictly increasing subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
(i.e.,
R_{n}\not\to\mathbbm{1}
in the strong operator topology), we may have
{\lVert u_{j}-R_{n}u_{j}\rVert}\not\to 0
or
{\lVert v_{j}-R_{n}v_{j}\rVert}\not\to 0
, and more generally,
{\lVert[K,R_{n}]\rVert}\not\to 0
. However, a Følner-type approximation is still possible since the asymptotic expression is averaging over the ranks of the projections.
In fact, we show in the next example that
any
sparse sequence of finite rank orthogonal projections is a sparse Følner sequence for any compact operator.

Example 2.6
.

Consider a sparse sequence
\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
and a compact operator
K\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite rank operator
K_{\varepsilon}
such that
{\lVert K-K_{\varepsilon}\rVert}<\varepsilon/4
, and thus,

{\lVert[(K-K_{\varepsilon}),R_{n}]\rVert}_{2}\leq 2{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}\|K-K_{\varepsilon}\|\leq\varepsilon{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}/2.

Let now
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}\geq 2{\lVert K_{\varepsilon}\rVert}_{2}/\varepsilon
for every
n\geq N_{\varepsilon}
. In particular, this implies
{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}\geq 2{\lVert[K_{\varepsilon},R_{n}]\rVert}_{2}/\varepsilon
. Hence,

\frac{{\lVert[K,R_{n}]\rVert}_{2}}{{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}}\leq\frac{{\lVert[K_{\varepsilon},R_{n}]\rVert}_{2}}{{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}}+\frac{{\lVert[(K-K_{\varepsilon}),R_{n}]\rVert}_{2}}{{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

A direct consequence of the example is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a quasidiagonal operator. Then
T
admits a sparse Følner sequence.

Proof.

By Remark
2.3
a) (see also
[
23
, Section 4]
),
T
can be written as
T=B+K
, where
B
is a block-diagonal operator and
K
is compact. Let
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the sequence of mutually orthogonal finite rank projections that reduce
B
, and let
\{k_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a strictly increasing subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
. Define
R_{n}:=Q_{k_{1}}+\dots+Q_{k_{n}}
. The sequence
\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sparse Følner sequence for
T
, since

\frac{\|[T,R_{n}]\|_{2}}{\|R_{n}\|_{2}}=\frac{\|[K,R_{n}]\|_{2}}{\|R_{n}\|_{2}},

and, by Example
2.6
, the right-hand side tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
.
∎

3.
Quasidiagonality and Følner sequences for unbounded operators

It is natural to ask about the quasidiagonal or Følner type approximations in the context of unbounded linear operators on Hilbert spaces. In fact, finite element methods typically ask for a concrete finite
dimensional approximation of elliptic operators approximating weak solutions via sufficiently regular elements with the correct boundary conditions (see, for example,
[
29
, Section 3]
and references therein). Also Hamiltonians in mathematical physics are unbounded in general and require different types of finite dimensional approximations (see
[
27
,
19
]
).

To give a unified presentation of the analysis, we will focus on the case of a densely defined closable operator
T\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
with domain
{\mathcal{D}}(T)
and denote it by the pair
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
; we will also consider an orthonormal basis
{\mathcal{B}}:=\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}(T)\subset{\mathcal{H}}

and extended seminorms given by

(5)

{\lVert T\rVert}_{u}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}(T)\\
{\lVert\psi\rVert}\leq 1\end{subarray}}{\lVert T\psi\rVert},\quad{\lVert T\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}:=\sqrt{{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\lVert T\psi_{n}\rVert}^{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\lVert T\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\left|\overline{T}\right|\psi_{n},\psi_{n}\right\rangle\;.

Note that the last term in Eq. (
5
) is well-defined since

{\mathcal{D}}(T)\subset{\mathcal{D}}\left(\overline{T}\right)={\mathcal{D}}\left(\left|\overline{T}\right|\right)
and that one
can always find an orthonormal basis in
{\mathcal{D}}(T)
by applying the Gram-Schmidt algorithm to an infinite, linearly independent set of the domain.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a densely defined closable operator and
{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of projections associated with a filtration of
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfying the domain stability condition
P_{n}{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}(T)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that
{\mathcal{P}}
is a
quasidiagonalizing sequence
for
T
if
\;\lim\limits_{n}{\lVert TP_{n}-P_{n}T\rVert}_{u}=0\,
; in this case we call
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))
a
quasidiagonal operator
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a densely defined closable operator and
{\mathcal{R}}:=\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-zero finite rank orthogonal projections. The sequence
{\mathcal{R}}
is called

(i)

a
1-Følner sequence
for
T
if

\quad\lim\limits_{n}\frac{{\lVert TR_{n}-R_{n}T\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{1}}=0\;
,

(ii)

a
2-Følner sequence
for
T
if

\quad\lim\limits_{n}\frac{{\lVert TR_{n}-R_{n}T\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert R_{n}\rVert}_{2}}=0
,
for some orthonormal basis
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
.

In the next proposition, we collect some immediate consequences of the preceding definitions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))
be an unbounded closable operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of projections associated with a filtration of
{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\mathcal{R}}=\{R_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of non-zero finite rank orthogonal projections and
a\in\{1,2\}
.

(i)

If
{\mathcal{P}}

is a quasidiagonalizing sequence for
T
, then it is a quasidiagonalizing sequence for any compact perturbation of
T
with the same domain.

(ii)

If
{\mathcal{R}}
is an
a
-Følner sequence for
T
, then it is an

a
-Følner sequence for its closure
\overline{T}
.

Proof.

To show (i), note first that the stability of the domain under the finite rank projections is immediate since
{\mathcal{D}}(T+K)={\mathcal{D}}(T)
. The operator
T+K
is closable, as it admits the closed extension
(\overline{T}+K,{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{T}))
. Moreover, for any compact operator

K
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, we have on
{\mathcal{D}}(T)

that
[(T+K),P_{n}]=[T,P_{n}]+[K,P_{n}]
hence

\|[(T+K),P_{n}]\|_{u}\leq\|[T,P_{n}]\|_{u}+\|[K,P_{n}].\|

Therefore,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|[(T+K),P_{n}]\|_{u}=0
by Remark
2.3
(a).

Part (ii) is a direct consequence of the definition of Følner sequences for unbounded operators.
∎

We now extend Halmos’ theorem on the characterization of quasidiagonal operators as compact perturbations of block-diagonal operators to the unbounded scenario (cf.
[
23
]
). For this we need to extend the notion of block-diagonal operator:

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(B,{\mathcal{D}}(B))\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a densely defined closable operator and
{\mathcal{Q}}:=\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of mutually orthogonal finite rank projections
of
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
Q_{n}{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}(B)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a
block-diagonalizing sequence
for
B
if for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
BQ_{n}-Q_{n}B=0
on
{\mathcal{D}}(B)
, i.e., every projection
Q_{n}
reduces
B
. In this case we say
(B,{\mathcal{D}}(B))
is a
block-diagonal
operator.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a (closable) quasidiagonal operator. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact operator
K\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
with
{\lVert K\rVert}<\varepsilon
such that
B:=T-K
with
{\mathcal{D}}(B)={\mathcal{D}}(T)
is block-diagonal.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a quasidiagonalizing sequence for
T
. By dropping to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that

{\lVert TP_{n}-P_{n}T\rVert}_{u}<\varepsilon/2^{n+1}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consider the sequence of mutually orthogonal finite rank projections
Q_{n}:=P_{n}-P_{n-1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}

(putting
P_{0}=0
) as in Section
2
. Define the operator

K=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{n+1}TP_{n}+P_{n}TQ_{n+1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}P_{n+1}(TP_{n}-P_{n}T)P_{n}-P_{n}(TP_{n}-P_{n}T)P_{n+1}\;,

which is indeed compact as the series converges in norm by the estimate above, and since

{\lVert P_{n+1}(TP_{n}-P_{n}T)P_{n}-P_{n}(TP_{n}-P_{n}T)P_{n+1}\rVert}\leq 2{\lVert TP_{n}-P_{n}T\rVert}_{u},

and each partial sum is finite rank.
Moreover, we have that
{\lVert K\rVert}<\varepsilon
. Observe that
B:=T-K
with
{\mathcal{D}}(B)={\mathcal{D}}(T)
is closable, since

(\overline{T}-K,{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{T}))
is a closed extension.
Using the telescoping property of the sequence
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
we btain

B=T-K=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{n}TQ_{n},

and, hence, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
[(T-K),Q_{n}]=0
on
{\mathcal{D}}(T)
.
∎

Conversely, it is clear that every block-diagonal operator is quasidiagonal, and by Proposition
3.3
, so is every compact perturbation of a block-diagonal. As a consequence of Theorem
3.5
, we obtain the following corollary, whose proof is analogous to that of Theorem
2.7
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H})
be a quasidiagonal operator. Then
T
admits a sparse Følner sequence.

3.1.
Quasidiagonalizing sequences for unbounded normal operators

In this subsection, we generalize the construction in Section
2.1
to unbounded self-adjoint and normal operators.

Let
(A,{\mathcal{D}}(A))
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and take
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be a Borel partition of
\mathbb{R}
into intervals of finite length. For
every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, fix
\varepsilon_{n}=1/2^{n}
and apply
Step 1
of the procedure in Section
2.1
to get a quasidiagonalizing sequence
\{P^{(n)}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
for the
compression
E_{A}(I_{n})A=E_{A}(I_{n})AE_{A}(I_{n})
.
Define
E_{k-1}=\sum_{n+m=k}P^{(n)}_{m}
for each
k>1
. The sequence
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
P_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{E_{i}}
, is a quasidiagonalizing sequence for
A
.

Similarly, a quasidiagonalizing sequence for an unbounded normal operator
(N,{\mathcal{D}}(N))
can be obtained by applying
Step 2
of the procedure in Section
2.1
.
As a consequence of Proposition
3.3
(ii) we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.7
.

The quasidiagonalizing sequence for the self-adjoint operator
(A,{\mathcal{D}}(A))
constructed in this subsection is stable under compact perturbations of
A
.

3.2.
Classes of examples

In this section we illustrate the previous definitions with some examples and show the analytical and algebraic limitations when working with filtrations of Hilbert spaces in relation with unbounded operators. We show here, in contrast with the bounded operator scenario, that

•

the notions of
1
- and
2
-Følner sequences for a closable operator are not equivalent anymore;

•

a quasidiagonal/Følner sequence for a closable operator need not satisfy this property for higher-order monomials of this operator;

We need to introduce first the key notion of the propagation of an operator
(see, e.g.,
[
36
, Definition 5.9.2]
).
Let
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
and choose an orthonormal basis
{\mathcal{B}}:=\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}(T)\subset{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then,
T\cong(T_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}\cong\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
. The
propagation
of
T
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}
is given by

{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}(T):=\sup\{|i-j|\mid T_{ij}\not=0\}\;.

Some first examples of triples
\left(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T),\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right)
for which the extended seminorms of Eq. (
5
) take finite values for
[T,P_{n}]
arise by restricting the matrix representation. For instance, let
{\mathcal{B}}:=\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}(T)
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{H}}
,
P_{n}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\operatorname{span}\{\psi_{1},...,\psi_{n}\}
and require
{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}([T,P_{n}])
to be finite for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is easy to see that this requirement is equivalent to every row and column vector of the matrix
(T_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
having finite support. Hence, the domain stability condition of Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
is clearly satisfied.

This family of operators inherits some key implications from the bounded setting.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T),\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}})
as above.

(i)

For
m=n+{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}([T,P_{n}])
, we have

{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{u}={\lVert P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}\rVert}\quad\text{and}\quad{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}={\lVert P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}\rVert}_{a}\;,\;\;a=1,2\,.

(ii)

If
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is a quasidiagonalizing/2-Følner sequence for
T
, then it is a 1-Følner sequence for
T
as well.

Proof.

For (i), denote by
T\cong(T_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
[T,P_{n}]\cong([T,P_{n}]_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
the matrices with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}
. Then,

[T,P_{n}]_{ij}=\begin{cases}-T_{ij}&\text{if }i\leq n<j\text{ and }|i-j|\leq{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}([T,P_{n}]),\\[2.84526pt]
T_{ij}&\text{if }j\leq n<i\text{ and }|i-j|\leq{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}([T,P_{n}]),\\[2.84526pt]
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and it is clear that
[T,P_{n}]_{ij}=(P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m})_{ij}
for
m=n+{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}([T,P_{n}])
. Therefore, for every
\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}(T)
, we have
[T,P_{n}]\psi=P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}\psi
,
from which the claim follows.

Now, for (ii), note that
P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}
has rank at most
2n
, so we obtain the estimates:

{\lVert P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}\rVert}_{1}\leq 2n{\lVert P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}\rVert},\quad\text{and}\quad{\lVert P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}\rVert}_{1}\leq\sqrt{2n}{\lVert P_{m}[T,P_{n}]P_{m}\rVert}_{2}

and thus,

\frac{{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{1}}\leq 2{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{1}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{2}}\;.

∎

Throughout this subsection, we will denote by
{\mathcal{B}}:=\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the canonical basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
and by
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}:=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the sequence of orthogonal projections
P_{n}

onto
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
.

We start by studying examples of operators with bounded propagation.

Example 3.9
.

Let
(S_{w},{\mathcal{D}}(S_{w}))
be the unilateral weighted shift operator acting on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
with weight vector
w=(w_{1},w_{2},\dots)
, i.e.,
S_{w}e_{n}=w_{n}e_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and domain

(6)

{\mathcal{D}}(S_{w})=c_{00}:=\left\{x=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{n}e_{n}\mid\text{with finitely many}\>\alpha_{n}\neq 0\right\}\subset\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}).

Note that
{\mathcal{D}}(S_{w}^{*})
is dense in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
since
{\mathcal{D}}(S_{w})\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}(S_{w}^{*})
and, consequently,
S_{w}
is closable.
Its matrix representation in the basis
{\mathcal{B}}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0&\ldots\\
w_{1}&0&0&0&\ldots\\
0&w_{2}&0&0&\ldots\\
0&0&w_{3}&0&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}\;.

By the definition of
P_{n}
it follows that, on the domain
{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\omega})
, we have

[S_{w},P_{n}]=w_{n}E_{n+1,n}
, where
E_{n+1,n}
is the unit matrix having all entries
0
except a
1
in the matrix element
(n+1,n)
. In this case the propagations of
S_{w}
and its
P_{n}
-commutator coincide; namely,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}(S_{w})={\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}([S_{w},P_{n}])=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Depending on the weight vector
w
, the sequence
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
, may or may not be a quasidiagonalizing or an
a
-Følner sequence for
a=1,2
.

•

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is not a quasidiagonalizing sequence for
S_{w}
with weight
w:=(\log 1,\log 2,\log 3,\dots)
, where
\log
denotes the natural logarithm, but is both a
1
-Følner and a
2
-Følner sequence for it.

•

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is neither a quasidiagonalizing sequence nor a 2-Følner sequence for
S_{w}
with weight
w:=(1,\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3},\dots)
but is a
1
-Følner sequence for it.

•

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is neither a quasidiagonalizing sequence nor a 1-Følner/2-Følner sequence for
S_{w}
with weight
w:=(1,2,3,\dots)
.

Indeed,

\lim_{n}{\lVert[S_{w},P_{n}]\rVert}_{u}\geq\lim_{n}{\lVert[S_{w},P_{n}]e_{n}\rVert}=\lim_{n}\lvert w_{n}\rvert=\infty,

for the three weight vectors, and

\quad\lim_{n}\frac{{\lVert[S_{w},P_{n}]\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a}}=\lim_{n}\frac{{\lVert P_{n+1}[S_{w},P_{n}]P_{n+1}\rVert}_{a}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a}}=\lim_{n}\frac{\lvert w_{n}\rvert}{n^{1/a}}=\begin{cases}\hskip 8.5359pt0\quad\text{if}\quad a=1,2&\text{if}\quad w_{n}=\log n,\\[5.69054pt]
\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\quad a=1,\\
1&\text{if}\quad a=2\\
\end{cases}&\text{if}\quad w_{n}=\sqrt{n},\\
\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad a=1,\\
\infty&\text{if}\quad a=2\\
\end{cases}&\text{if}\quad w_{n}=n.\end{cases}

Remark 3.10
.

a)

As noted in Remark
2.3
b), the Følner condition in Definition
2.2
can be equivalently formulated using the
{\lVert\cdot\rVert}_{2}
-norm (cf.
[
7
, Lemma 1]
). This equivalence fails for unbounded operators: a 1-Følner sequence need not be a 2-Følner sequence.

b)

If
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is a bounded operator with finite propagation
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}(T)<\infty
, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
always satisfies the Følner condition. Indeed, the norms
{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{2}
are uniformly bounded:

{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{k\in N}{\lVert[T,P_{n}]e_{k}\rVert}^{2}}\leq 2{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}([T,P_{n}]){\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}\leq 4{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}(T){\lVert T\rVert}.

In contrast, if
(T,{\mathcal{D}}(T))\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
is an unbounded operator with
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}(T)<\infty
, the extended seminorms
{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}
and
{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}
are not necessarily bounded and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
may not be a Følner sequence.

Example 3.11
.

Let
(S^{2}_{w},{\mathcal{D}}(S^{2}_{w}))
be the square of the operator
S_{w}
from Example
3.9
with domain
{\mathcal{D}}(S^{2}_{w})=c_{00}
as in Eq. (
6
). Like
S_{w}
,
S^{2}_{w}
is closable since
{\mathcal{D}}(S^{2}_{w})\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}((S^{2}_{w})^{*})
.
With respect to the basis
{\mathcal{B}}
, the matrix representation of
S^{2}_{w}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\ldots\\
0&0&0&\ldots\\
w_{1}w_{2}&0&0&\ldots\\
0&w_{2}w_{3}&0&\ldots\\
0&0&w_{3}w_{4}&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}\;,

and, on the domain
{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\omega})
, we can write the commutator in terms of the unit matrix
[S_{w}^{2},P_{n}]=w_{n}w_{n+1}E_{n+2,n}
and, therefore,

{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}(S^{2}_{w})={\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}([S^{2}_{w},P_{n}])=2
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this case
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is neither a quasidiagonalizing sequence nor a
a
-Følner sequence,
a=1,2
for
S^{2}_{w}
with weight vector
w:=(1,\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3},\dots)
.
In fact, we have

\lim_{n}{\lVert[S^{2}_{w},P_{n}]\rVert}_{u}\geq\lim_{n}{\lVert[S^{2}_{w},P_{n}]e_{n}\rVert}=\lim_{n}\lvert w_{n}w_{n+1}\rvert=\infty,

and

\quad\lim_{n}\frac{{\lVert[S_{w}^{2},P_{n}]\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a}}\geq\lim_{n}\frac{n}{n^{1/a}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad a=1,\\
\infty&\text{if}\quad a=2\,.\\
\end{cases}

Remark 3.12
.

In contrast to Remark
2.3
c), this example shows that 2-Følner sequences lack stability under the product. The set of (possibly) unbounded operators sharing a 2-Følner sequence is a linear subspace but not an algebra. The difference is that for unbounded operators, the right term of the inequality
{\lVert[ST,P_{n}]\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}\leq{\lVert S\rVert}_{u}{\lVert[T,P_{n}]\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}+{\lVert[S,P_{n}]\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}{\lVert T\rVert}_{u}
can be infinite even if the norms of the commutators are arbitrarily small. The same happens for quasidiagonalizing sequences, when applying the submultiplicativity of
{\lVert\cdot\rVert}_{u}
. This is shown in the next example.

Example 3.13
.

Let
(A,{\mathcal{D}}(A))
be an operator acting on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
by
Ae_{2j-1}=(2j-1)^{2}e_{2j-1}+1/(2j-1)e_{2j}
and
Ae_{2j}=(2j)^{2}e_{2j}
for
j\geq 1
, with domain
{\mathcal{D}}(A)=c_{00}
. Let also
(A^{2},{\mathcal{D}}(A^{2}))
, with domain
{\mathcal{D}}(A^{2})={\mathcal{D}}(A)
. The associated matrices are given by

A=\begin{pmatrix}1^{2}&0&0&0&0&0&\dots\\
\frac{1}{1}&2^{2}&0&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&3^{2}&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&\frac{1}{3}&4^{2}&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&0&5^{2}&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&0&\frac{1}{5}&6^{2}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}\hskip 14.22636pt,\hskip 14.22636ptA^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1^{4}&0&0&0&0&0&\dots\\
\frac{1^{2}+2^{2}}{1}&2^{4}&0&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&3^{4}&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&\frac{3^{2}+4^{2}}{3}&4^{4}&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&0&5^{4}&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&0&\frac{5^{2}+6^{2}}{5}&6^{4}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}\;.

Note that both operators are closable since
c_{00}\subset{\mathcal{D}}(A^{*})
and
c_{00}\subset{\mathcal{D}}((A^{2})^{*})
.

Finally, we show that the sequence
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}
associated with the canonical filtration is a quasidiagonal sequence for
A
but not for
A^{2}
. In fact, for every
j\geq 1
, on the one hand we have

\|[A,P_{2j-1}]\|_{u}={\lVert[A,P_{2j-1}]e_{2j-1}\rVert}=1/(2j-1)\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\;,\quad\text{and}\quad\|[A,P_{2j}]\|_{u}=0,

and, on the other hand, the commutators associated with
A^{2}
satisfy

\|[A^{2},P_{2j-1}]\|_{u}=\bigg\lVert\frac{(2j-1)^{2}+(2j)^{2}}{2j-1}\bigg\rVert\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty.

We conclude the section by considering an example of an operator with unbounded propagation.

Example 3.14
.

Let
(S_{+},{\mathcal{D}}(S_{+}))
, with
{\mathcal{D}}(S_{+})=c_{00}
as in Eq. (
6
), be the operator acting on
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})
by

S_{+}e_{n}=\sqrt{n}e_{2n}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
{\mathcal{D}}(S_{+})\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}(S_{+}^{*})
, it follows that
S_{+}
is closable and its matrix representation with respect to the basis
{\mathcal{B}}
is

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&0&0&0&0&\ldots\\
1&0&0&0&0&\ldots\\
0&0&0&0&0&\ldots\\
0&\sqrt{2}&0&0&0&\ldots\\
0&0&0&0&0&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{array}\right).

In this example, we have
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}(S_{+})=+\infty
, but since on
{\mathcal{D}}(S_{+})
we have

[S_{w},P_{n}]=\sqrt{n}E_{2n,n}
, where
E_{2n,n}
denotes a unit matrix, we conclude that

{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}([S_{+},P_{n}])<+\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly,
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}
is not a quasidiagonalizing sequence for
S_{+}
because, as before,

\|[S_{+},P_{n}]\|_{u}\geq{\lVert[S_{+},P_{n}]e_{n}\rVert}={\lVert\sqrt{n}e_{2n}\rVert}=\sqrt{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty\;.

Moreover,
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}
is neither a 2-Følner sequence nor a 1-Følner sequence. Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{{\lVert[S_{+},P_{n}]\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\lVert[S_{+},P_{n}]P_{2n}\rVert}_{a}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a}}\geq\frac{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil^{1/a}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{1/2}}{n^{1/a}}\geq\frac{\left(n+1\right)^{1/2}}{2^{1/a-1/2}}

leading to

\lim_{n}\frac{{\lVert[S_{+},P_{n}]\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a}}=\infty\;,\quad\text{for}\quad a=1,2\;.

4.
Quasidiagonality and Følner sequences in Quantum Mechanics

In this section we apply the methods developed before to some relevant quantum mechanical structures. In the next subsection we apply an algebraic version of Følner approximations to the Weyl algebra and then consider a Hilbert space representation of this algebra, necessarily in terms of unbounded operators.
In this way we are able to relate algebraic Følner subspaces with Følner sequences of projections.

4.1.
Algebraic amenability in quantum mechanics

As mentioned in the introduction, Følner sequences appeared naturally when extending amenability notions beyond group theory. In particular, an algebraic version of amenability was introduced by Gromov in Section 1.11 of
[
20
]
.
We will restrict here to countable algebras over the complex numbers, but this notion can be presented in full generality
(see also
[
1
, Section 3]
for a thorough analysis of this concept and additional references).

Algebraic amenability is usually introduced via the existence of finite dimensional subspaces of the algebra
satisfying a Følner-type condition which we express locally.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{A}}
be a countable algebra over
\mathbb{C}
.

(1)

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathfrak{A}}
be a finite subset and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. A non-zero
finite dimensional linear subspace
V\subseteq{\mathfrak{A}}
is said to be a

(
{\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon
)-Følner subspace
if it satisfies

(7)

\frac{\dim(aV+V)}{\dim(V)}\leq 1+\varepsilon\;\;,\quad a\in{\mathcal{F}}\;.

(2)

{\mathfrak{A}}
is said to be
algebraically amenable
if for every
\varepsilon>0
and every finite set
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathfrak{A}}
, there exists a left (
{\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon
)-Følner subspace.

As a first algebraic motivation for the analytical considerations in relation to unbounded operators in Section
3
we consider here Weyl algebras
which appear as primitive quotients of the enveloping algebra of a nilpotent Lie algebra
over
\mathbb{C}
(see, e.g.,
[
17
]
).
For the purpose of this article it is enough to focus on the simplest case with two generators (similar results extend straightforwardly to the case of
2n
generators).
The Weyl algebra
{\mathcal{W}}
is generated by the symbols
q,p
satisfying the canonical commutation relation:

{\mathcal{W}}:=\mathbb{C}\langle p,q\mid qp-pq=i\mathbbm{1}\rangle\;.

It is easy to see that this algebra is simple and admits a basis of monomials where the
q
’s are written to the right of the
p
’s specifying a normal form for elements in
{\mathcal{W}}
.
We show next that the Weyl algebra is also algebraically amenable:

Proposition 4.2
.

The algebra
{\mathcal{W}}:=\mathbb{C}\langle p,q\mid qp-pq=i\mathbbm{1}\rangle
is algebraically amenable.

Proof.

Since any element in
{\mathcal{W}}
can be written as a finite linear combination of monomials expressed in normal form, it is enough to show algebraic amenability for any finite set
{\mathcal{M}}
of monomials
of the form
p^{k}q^{l}
. Now given
\varepsilon>0
and
{\mathcal{M}}
we define for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0,1,\dots\}
the subspace

(8)

V_{n}:=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{p^{k}q^{l}\mid k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\;k+l\leq n\right\}

which has dimension
{(n+1)(n+2)}/{2}
. Moreover, since

\deg(q^{l}p^{k})=\deg(p^{k}q^{l})
,
k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have that

p^{k}q^{l}V_{n}\subset V_{n+k+l}
and thus
\dim(p^{k}q^{l}V_{n}+V_{n})\leq\dim(V_{n+k+l})
.
Finally, to show that
V_{n}
satisfies the Følner condition given in Eq. (
7
) let
\delta:=\max\{\deg(M)\mid M\in{\mathcal{M}}\}
. For
any
M\in{\mathcal{M}}
, we have that

\frac{\dim(MV_{n}+V_{n})}{\dim(V_{n})}\leq\frac{\dim(V_{n+\delta})}{\dim(V_{n})}=\frac{(n+\delta+1)(n+\delta+2)}{(n+1)(n+2)}\leq\left(1+\frac{\delta+2}{n}\right)^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,,

where the last inequality holds if
n\geq(\delta+2)/(\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}-1)
.
∎

We consider next *-representations of *-algebras in terms of unbounded operators. It is well-known that the canonical commutation relation
qp-pq=i\mathbbm{1}
cannot be represented by bounded operators (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
38
]
). However, we recall in this section that it does allow a distinguished representation (called Schrödinger
representation) in terms of essentially self-adjoint operators.
Motivated by the algebraic amenability of
{\mathcal{W}}
, we provide a natural generalization of the quasidiagonal and Følner type approximations in the context of unbounded operators.

We start by revisiting the notion of representation of a *-algebra by unbounded operators (see, e.g.,
[
38
, Chapter 8]
).

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}

be a
*
-algebra with involution
a\mapsto a^{\dagger}
. Denote by
{\mathcal{D}}
a dense subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
and by
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{D}})\subset{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
the algebra of linear (possibly unbounded) closable operators of
{\mathcal{D}}
into itself. An algebra homomorphism
\pi\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{D}})
is said to be a
*
-representation of
{\mathfrak{A}}
on
{\mathcal{D}}(\pi):={\mathcal{D}}
if for each
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
we have that
\pi(a^{\dagger})
is the restriction to
{\mathcal{D}}
of the adjoint operator
\pi(a)^{*}
, that is,

(9)

\langle\pi(a)\varphi,\psi\rangle=\langle\varphi,\pi(a^{\dagger})\psi\rangle\quad\text{for all}\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}\;.

This definition can be applied to the Weyl algebra, which can be turned into an involutive algebra (denoted again by
{\mathcal{W}}
) by fixing the involution on the generators via
p^{*}:=p
and
q^{*}:=q
. This algebra admits a well-known (unbounded)
*
-representation
\pi_{S}
on
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
in terms of the usual position and momentum operators, called the Schrödinger representation of the Weyl algebra (cf.
[
38
, Example 2.5.2 and 8.3.7]
): taking the Schwartz functions as the common domain

{\mathcal{D}}(\pi_{S}):={\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
and defining for any
\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})

(\pi_{S}(q)\psi)(x):=(Q\psi)(x)=x\psi(x)\text{and}\quad(\pi_{S}(p)\psi)(x)=(P\psi)(x)=-i\psi^{\prime}(x)\;.

Since the Schrödinger representation
\pi_{S}
is injective, it preserves linear dimension, and the *-algebra of operators
\pi_{S}({\mathcal{W}})
is also algebraically amenable, with amenability implemented by a
sequence of finite dimensional subspaces given by
\{\pi_{S}(V_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
V_{n}
is defined in Eq. (
8
)).
In order to understand the analytical implications and limitations of quasidiagonal and Følner type approximations for operators in relation to algebraic amenability, we now generalize the corresponding notions given in Definition
2.2
to the unbounded scenario.

There is also a natural relation between algebraic amenability and concrete operator algebras
having a Følner sequence. Consider first the bounded case and let
{\mathfrak{A}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a concrete separable C*-algebra
and
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}\subset{\mathfrak{A}}
a dense *-subalgebra with a cyclic vector
\psi_{0}
.
If
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
is algebraically amenable, then there is a Følner sequence for all operators in
{\mathfrak{A}}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 3.17]
for a precise and more general statement).
Concretely, there is a natural linear map between the algebra and the Hilbert space given by

\Phi\colon{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{H}}\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi(a)=a\psi_{0}\;.

In the unbounded scenario, this map also allows to relate subspaces of an *-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}

with orthogonal projections on a Hilbert space. In the notation above let
V\subset{\mathfrak{A}}
be a (finite dimensional) Følner subspace of an involutive algebra,
(\pi\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to L(D),{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{H}})

a *-representation with cyclic vector
\psi_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}
and denote by

(10)

\Phi(V)\quad\text{the orthogonal projection onto the finite-dimensional subspace}\quad\mathrm{\pi}(V)\psi_{0}\subset{\mathcal{H}}\;.

We will show in the next section that, in contrast with the bounded case, the projection
P
associated to an algebraic Følner subspace will not be a Følner projection for general *-representations.

4.2.
Examples in the Schrödinger representation of the Weyl algebra

We conclude this section making contact with the notion of algebraic amenability of the Weyl algebra in its Schrödinger representation. The sequence of Følner subspaces presented in Section
4.1
will determine a canonical sequence of finite rank projections on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Solving the stationary Schrödinger equation for the quantum harmonic oscillator with mass
m
and frequency
\omega
(and taking
\hbar=1
), we get the following eigenvectors, which form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
:

(11)

\psi_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{\pi}\>x_{0}\>2^{n}\>n!)^{1/2}}H_{n}\left(\frac{x}{x_{0}}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x}{x_{0}}\right)^{2}\right\}\;,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
H_{n}:=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{-x^{2}}
is the
n^{\text{th}}
-order Hermite polynomial (with
H_{0}:=\mathbbm{1}
) and
x_{0}:=\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}
(see
[
22
, Theorem 11.4]
).

Given the orthonormal basis

{\mathcal{B}}:=\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cong\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})

the operators
\pi_{S}(q)
and
\pi_{S}(p)
have the following matrix expressions:

(12)

\pi_{S}(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0&\ldots\\
1&0&\sqrt{2}&0&\ldots\\
0&\sqrt{2}&0&\sqrt{3}&\ldots\\
0&0&\sqrt{3}&0&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{S}(p)=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0&0&\ldots\\
1&0&-\sqrt{2}&0&\ldots\\
0&\sqrt{2}&0&-\sqrt{3}&\ldots\\
0&0&\sqrt{3}&0&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}.\hskip-5.69046pt

It is often convenient to express them in terms of the creation and annihilation operators
a^{*}
and
a
, respectively, defined as weighted shifts by

(13)

a^{*}\psi_{n}=\sqrt{n+1}\psi_{n+1}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad\ \text{and}\quad a\psi_{n}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{n}\psi_{n-1}&n\in\mathbb{N}\\
0&n=0\;,\end{cases}

with domains
{\mathcal{D}}(a^{*})={\mathcal{D}}(a)={\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
. Namely, we have

(14)

\pi_{S}(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(a^{*}+a)\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{S}(p)=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left(a^{*}-a\right),

and therefore,
\pi_{S}({\mathcal{W}})=\mathbb{C}\langle a,a^{*}\rangle
with the commutation relation
[a,a^{*}]=\mathbbm{1}
.

Since
\psi_{0}
is a cyclic vector for
\pi_{S}({\mathcal{W}})
there is a map
\Phi
(defined in Eq.
10
)
from the lattice of subspaces of
{\mathcal{W}}
to the lattice of projections on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In the concrete case of the Følner subspaces
\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined in Eq. (
8
) we consider first their representation as

\pi_{S}(V_{n}):=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{a^{k}(a^{*})^{l}\mid k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\;k+l\leq n\right\}\;

and corresponding finite rank orthogonal projection is given by

\Phi(V_{n})=P_{n+1}\;,\quad\text{where}\quad P_{n+1}\quad\text{is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace}\quad\pi_{S}(V_{n})\psi_{0}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\;.

Note that, by definition of the creation and annihilation operators,
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is associated to the canonical filtration
\{\operatorname{span}\{\psi_{0},...,\psi_{n-1}\}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
As the sequence of projections
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
encodes the algebraic amenability of

{\mathcal{W}}
, it seems a natural candidate as a Følner sequence for (every element in)
\pi_{S}({\mathcal{W}})
. However, algebraic Følner sequences need not produce Følner sequences of projections for unbounded operators, as we show in the next result.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}=\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence of finite rank projections associated to the canonical filtration defined before. Then

(1)

{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}
is not a quasidiagonalizing sequence for
\pi_{S}(q)
nor for
\pi_{S}(p)
;

(2)

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is a 1-Følner sequence for
\pi_{S}(q)
and
\pi_{S}(p)
but not a 2-Følner sequence for any of them;

(3)

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is neither a 1-Følner sequence nor a 2-Følner sequence for
\pi_{S}(p^{2})
nor for
\pi_{S}(q^{2})
.

Proof.

Note first that the stability of domains is satisfied since
P_{n}{\mathcal{H}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1) As the position and momentum operators are represented in terms of weighted shifts we have

{\lVert[\pi_{S}(q),P_{n}]\psi_{n}\rVert}={\lVert[\pi_{S}(p),P_{n}]\psi_{n}\rVert}=\sqrt{n/2}

and, thus,
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{B}}}
is not a quasidiagonalizing sequence for
\pi_{S}(q)
nor for
\pi_{S}(p)
.

(2) From Eq. (
14
) we consider first the following estimates
for the commutators in
1
- and
2
-norms:

\displaystyle{\lVert[\pi_{S}(p),P_{n}]\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}\>

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\lVert[a^{*},P_{n}]\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}+{\lVert[a,P_{n}]\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}}\right),\quad\text{(same estimate for}\quad{\lVert[\pi_{S}(q),P_{n}]\rVert}_{1,{\mathcal{B}}})\;,

\displaystyle{\lVert[\pi_{S}(q),P_{n}]\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle={\lVert[\pi_{S}(p),P_{n}]\rVert}_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\left\|[a^{*},P_{n}]\right\|_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}^{2}+\big\|[a,P_{n}]\big\|_{2,{\mathcal{B}}}^{2}}\;.

Note now that
a^{*}
acts on the basis
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
just as the weighted sift
S_{w}
from Example
3.9
with unbounded weight
w=(1,\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3},\dots)
.
Since the conclusions of Example
3.9
also apply to
S_{w}^{*}
, it follows that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is a
1
-Følner sequence for both
a
and
a^{*}
but is not a
2
-Følner sequence for either of them. This completes the proof for (2).

(3) For this part we use that
a^{*}a+aa^{*}
is diagonal in the basis
{\mathcal{B}}
so that we can simplify the corresponding commutators by

{\lVert[\pi(q^{2}),P_{n}]\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}={\lVert[\pi(p^{2}),P_{n}]\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|[(a^{*})^{2}+a^{2},P_{n}]\right\|_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}\;.

Using that the propagation of the commutator is given by
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathcal{B}}([(a^{*})^{2}+a^{2},P_{n}])=2
, we obtain

\frac{\left\|[(a^{*})^{2}+a^{2},P_{n}]\right\|_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}}=\frac{\left\|[(a^{*})^{2}+a^{2},P_{n}]P_{n+2}\right\|_{a}}{{\lVert P_{n}\rVert}_{a,{\mathcal{B}}}}\geq n^{1-1/a}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad a=1,\\
\infty&\text{if}\quad a=2\\
\end{cases}

and the Følner condition is not satisfied in either case.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The preceding result shows that the canonical sequence of projections specified in Eq. (
10
) associated with the canonical Følner subspaces of the Weyl algebra is not quasidiagonalizing for either
\pi_{S}(q)
or
\pi_{S}(p)
. But since these operators are essentially self-adjoint their closures
do have a different quasidiagonalizing sequence
based on the procedure explained in Subsection
3.1
that uses the spectral projections on a dyadic partition of the spectrum.

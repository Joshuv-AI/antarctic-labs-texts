Every motive is the motive of a stable ∞ \infty -category
Maxime Ramzi, Vladimir Sosnilo, Christoph Winges

Abstract
. We define a class of motivic equivalences of small stable ∞ \infty -categories W mot W_{\mathrm{mot}} and show that the Dwyer–Kan localization functor Cat ∞ perf → Cat ∞ perf ​ [ W mot − 1 ] \mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}[W_{\mathrm{mot}}^{-1}] is the universal localizing invariant in the sense of Blumberg–Gepner–Tabuada. In particular, we show that every object in its target ℳ loc \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}} can be represented as 𝒰 loc ​ ( 𝒞 ) \mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{C}) for some small stable ∞ \infty -category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . Introduction

One of the many challenges in the subject of K-theory has always been to give it a general enough definition
which would be both computationally useful and would satisfy a natural universal property.
One can give a very general universal construction of K-theory in the context of localizing invariants

[
BGT13
,
CT11
]
. To do so, one defines a universal localizing invariant

\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}

and proves that nonconnective K-theory is corepresented by the symmetric monoidal unit in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
This definition is very satisfying from the theoretical point of view, but is quite hard to approach practically,
because of our lack of understanding of what
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
looks like. In particular,
there is no concrete way to think about objects of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, limits and colimits of those, and the general
mapping spaces between them.
The goal of this paper is to give a new, more concrete construction of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
which is independent of the construction in
[
BGT13
]
.

Let us recall some of the basic notions required to state the main theorem.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a diagram in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}

\mathcal{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}

is a
Karoubi sequence
if it is a cofiber sequence and
f
is fully faithful.

Since we are working in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
, the
\infty
-category of idempotent complete stable
\infty
-categories, the
cofiber of a fully faithful functor is given by the idempotent completion of the usual Verdier quotient of stable

\infty
-categories. We will call this the
Karoubi quotient
.

Definition 1.2
.

A functor
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{X}
is

(1)

a
localizing invariant
if it sends
0
to the final object of
\mathcal{X}
and Karoubi sequences to fibre sequences in
\mathcal{X}
;

(2)

finitary
if it preserves filtered colimits.

We denote by
\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{X})
the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Fun}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{X})
consisting of finitary localizing invariants.

Example 1.3
.

The nonconnective K-theory functor
\operatorname{K}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Spt}
is a finitary localizing invariant.

Definition 1.4
.

An exact functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
of small stable
\infty
-categories is a
motivic equivalence
if
L(f)
is an
equivalence for every finitary localizing invariant
L:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{X}
with
\mathcal{X}
a stable

\infty
-category.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
W_{\mathrm{mot}}
denote the class of motivic equivalences in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
.

(1)

The Dwyer–Kan localization
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}:=\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}[W^{-1}_{\mathrm{mot}}]
is a locally small and stable presentable
\infty
-category.

(2)

The symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
induces a symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
such that the localization functor
\gamma:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
refines to a symmetric monoidal functor.

(3)

The localization functor
\gamma
is the universal finitary localizing invariant: the functor
\gamma
is a finitary localizing invariant, and for every stable presentable

\infty
-category
\mathcal{X}
, restriction along
\gamma
induces an equivalence

\gamma^{*}:\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{L}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{X}).

This gives a completely new computational and theoretical approach to studying the universal localizing invariant. In particular this result can be used to study functors out of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
that are not exact, and so do not correspond to localizing invariants, in relation to e.g. multiplicative structures or polynomial functoriality.

One of the key observations (see Proposition
2.8
) lies in the fact that the triple
(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},W_{\mathrm{mot}},\mathrm{cof})
gives
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
the structure of a
cofibration category
in the sense of Cisinski
[
Cis19
, Chapter 7]
, where
\mathrm{cof}
is the class of fully faithful functors.
This is reminiscent of classical homotopy theory, where one can approach the
\infty
-category of spaces through the model category of simplicial sets.
In this sense, one can think of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
as a
homotopy theory
of stable
\infty
-categories.

To prove stability of the localization
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}[W^{-1}_{\mathrm{mot}}]
, we rely on the fact that desuspensions in the category of motives can be categorified in terms of the Grayson construction.
In principle, the necessary statements can be deduced from
[
KW19
]
, but we include a discussion of the Grayson construction for the reader’s convenience in Appendix
A
.
While doing so, we also seize the opportunity to provide a more geometric interpretation of the Grayson construction, and remove an unnecessary assumption from
[
KW19
]
.

Appendix
B
sketches a generalization of our main results to the setting of
\mathcal{V}
-linear categories, where
\mathcal{V}
is any presentably symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category.

We demonstrate the utility of our results via several applications.

1.5.1.
Every spectrum is the K-theory of a stable category

An immediate corollary of Theorem
1.5
is that every object of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is equivalent to

\gamma(\mathcal{C})
for some
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
.
In our previous paper
[
RSW24
]
we proved the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

For every spectrum
M
, there exists a small stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
with
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C})\simeq M
.

We obtain an alternative proof of Theorem
1.6
by combining Theorem
1.5
with the following lemma.

Lemma 1.7
.

The functor
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathrm{Spt}
induced by nonconnective algebraic K-theory admits a section.

Proof.

Since K-theory is lax symmetric monoidal, the lax symmetric monoidal and colimit-preserving functor

induced by
\operatorname{K}
factors over
\mathrm{Mod}(\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{S}))
.
Since the Dennis–Waldhausen trace
\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{S})\to\mathbf{S}
exhibits the sphere spectrum as a retract of

in the category of ring spectra, the forgetful functor

Moreover, we have
\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{Spt}^{\omega}),\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{Spt}^{\omega}))\simeq\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{S})
as ring spectra, which induces a fully faithful section
\mathrm{Mod}(\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{S}))\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to the functor
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathrm{Mod}(\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{S}))
because
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{Spt}^{\omega})
is compact by
[
BGT13
, Theorem 9.8]
(see also Corollary
6.13
below).
∎

1.7.1.
Improving the universal property

Even though the class
W_{\mathrm{mot}}
in Theorem
1.5
is rather inexplicit, we are able to give a more
tangible description of motivic invariance, at least for
\aleph_{1}
-finitary localizing invariants, and using currently unpublished work of Sasha Efimov.

More precisely, we construct a very explicit class of motivic equivalences
W_{\mathrm{simple}}
in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
such
that for any
\infty
-category
\mathcal{X}
the restriction functor

\operatorname{Fun}^{\aleph_{1}-\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{X})\to\operatorname{Fun}^{\aleph_{1}-\mathrm{fin}}_{W_{\mathrm{simple}}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{X})

is an equivalence. Here the superscript “
\aleph_{1}
-fin” denotes the the subcategory of functors that preserve

\aleph_{1}
-filtered colimits and the subscript “
W_{\mathrm{simple}}
” denotes the subcategory of functors that send elements of

W_{\mathrm{simple}}
to equivalences.

The explicit description allows us to check directly whether a functor
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{X}
factors over
\gamma
.
In particular we obtain the following stronger universal property of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

The functor
\gamma:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is the universal
\aleph_{1}
-finitary localizing invariant:
for every stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{X}
, restriction along
\gamma
induces an equivalence

\gamma^{*}:\operatorname{Fun}^{\aleph_{1}-\mathrm{fin}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc},\aleph_{1}-\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{X}).

Theorem
1.8
can be used to verify the following.

Theorem 1.9

(Theorem
6.25
)
.

The universal localizing invariant
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
preserves countable products.

Lemma 2.7

The class of right Bousfield localizations is closed under pullbacks along arbitrary functors.
The same is true for the class of left Bousfield localizations.

3.
Exhausting
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
by small categories of cofibrant objects

Ideally, we would like to apply Theorem
2.6
directly to the localization of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
at the motivic equivalences.
However, it is a priori not clear that taking this localization would produce a locally small
\infty
-category.
This section addresses this problem by exhibiting a filtration of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
by small full subcategories which inherit the structure of a category of cofibrant objects from the ambient category.
This will allow us to localize each filtration step individually and then take a colimit over these localizations afterwards to obtain
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}[W_{\mathrm{mot}}^{-1}]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A cardinal
\kappa
is
countably closed
if
\lambda^{\aleph_{0}}<\kappa
for all
\lambda<\kappa
.

The next lemma shows that there exists a sufficient supply of countably closed cardinals.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any cardinal
\kappa_{0}
, there exists a regular, countably closed cardinal
\kappa>\kappa_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu:=\max(\kappa_{0},\aleph_{0})
, and let
\kappa:=(2^{\mu})^{+}
.
Since successor cardinals are regular, we only have to show that
\kappa
is countably closed.
If
\lambda<\kappa
, then
\lambda\leq 2^{\mu}
and so
\lambda^{\aleph_{0}}\leq 2^{\mu\cdot\aleph_{0}}=2^{\mu}<\kappa
, so
\kappa
is indeed a countably closed cardinal, and it is clearly bigger than
\kappa_{0}
.
∎

Our goal is to show that the full subcategory
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\kappa}_{\infty}
of
\kappa
-compact objects in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
inherits the structure of a category of cofibrant objects from
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
whenever
\kappa
is a regular, countably closed cardinal.
As a first step, we will prove a characterisation of
\kappa
-compact objects in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
for an arbitrary regular, uncountable cardinal
\kappa
.

Lemma 3.3
.

(1)

The homotopy groups of a compact spectrum are countable.

(2)

The homotopy groups of a compact ring spectrum are countable.

Proof.

Since the sphere spectrum generates the full subcategory of compact spectra under desuspensions and finite colimits, the first assertion follows from the fact that
\pi_{*}\mathbf{S}
is countable.

For the second assertion, consider the collection
\mathcal{R}
of ring spectra whose homotopy groups are countable.
The free ring spectrum on a spectrum
M
is a countable direct sum of tensor powers of
M
and therefore has countable homotopy groups if
M
is compact.
Combining this with the bar resolution, we find that
\mathcal{R}
is closed under finite coproducts.
Finally, the Bousfield-Kan formula (
[
Sha17
, Corollary 12.5]
(see also
[
Lur17b
, Proposition 5.5.8.13]
) shows that
\mathcal{R}
is closed under finite colimits.
Thus, it contains all the compact ring spectra.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular, uncountable cardinal and let
\mathcal{C}
be an idempotent complete
\infty
-category.
Consider the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is
\kappa
-compact in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
;

(2)

\mathcal{C}
is
\kappa
-compact in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
;

(3)

\mathcal{C}
has a
\kappa
-small set of equivalence classes of objects and the homotopy groups of the mapping space
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
are
\kappa
-small for every pair of objects
x,y
in
\mathcal{C}
.

The conditions
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent.

If
\mathcal{C}
is furthermore stable, then all three conditions are equivalent.

Proof.

Conditions
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent by
[
Lur17b
, Proposition 5.4.1.2]
.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{C}
is stable and
\kappa
-compact as an object of
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
and let
(\mathcal{D}_{i})_{i}
be a

\kappa
-filtered diagram in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
.
Since
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}}(\mathcal{C},-)
is a full subfunctor of
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Cat}}(\mathcal{C},-)
and the forgetful functor
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}
preserves filtered colimits by
[
Lur17a
, Proposition 1.1.4.6]
and
[
Lur17b
, Corollary 4.4.5.21]
, we find that the induced map

) holds for

Let

now be a

is a

-small filtered colimit of compact objects, all of which have countably many objects up to equivalence and mapping spaces with countable homotopy groups.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that for (not-necessarily stable)
\infty
-categories,

shows that these notions of

.

Let
\kappa
be a regular, uncountable cardinal, and

be a span of
\kappa
-compact objects in

.
Then the lax pullback
\mathcal{C}\vec{\times}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{E}
is
\kappa

This is immediate using Proposition

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular, uncountable cardinal.
Then the
\kappa
-compact objects in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
are closed under taking full subcategories and under taking Karoubi quotients.

Proof.

For subcategories, this follows immediately from Proposition

.
In particular, we conclude from Corollary

that pullbacks of

\kappa
-compact.

Let

be a full stable subcategory of

is itself

The Karoubi quotient

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular, countably closed cardinal and let

be
\kappa
-compact in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
.
Then

-compact.

We again apply Proposition

.
The

-compact objects are generated under finite colimits by countable direct sums of compact objects, and thus the mapping spectra in

are in the thick subcategory of

generated by

is a mapping spectrum in

.
Since these have

-small homotopy groups and

\kappa
-small homotopy groups.

By assumption,
\mathcal{C}
has less than
\kappa
many objects up to equivalence, so there are less than
\kappa

\mathcal{C}
because

-small homotopy groups, it follows that the stable subcategory they generate is also
\kappa
-small.
∎

.

Let
\kappa
be a regular, countably closed cardinal.

to the full subcategory

, we only have to show that the lax pullback

.
∎

4.
Motives as a localization

In this section, we prove our main theorem, namely that the Dwyer–Kan localization of the category
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
at the motivic equivalences is the universal finitary localizing invariant.
Let
\kappa
be a regular, uncountable cardinal.
Since
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\kappa}_{\infty}
is small, the Dwyer–Kan localization

\gamma_{\kappa}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\kappa}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}:=\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\kappa}_{\infty}[(W^{\kappa}_{\mathrm{mot}})^{-1}]

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

and let

Note that the functor

preserves both fully faithful functors and colimits, so it preserves Karoubi sequences and filtered colimits.
Hence

is a finitary localizing invariant whenever

is one, which implies that

is a motivic equivalence as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.4

Then

\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{J}\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{F\in\mathcal{F}(J)}\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{F}\mathcal{C}|_{F}

5.

-compact categories

In the previous section we have already shown that for a countably closed regular cardinal

.
Our goal in this section is to prove the following more precise result about the relation of
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, also when
\kappa
is not countably closed:

Proposition 5.1
.

For any regular uncountable cardinal
\kappa
, the functor
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
induces an equivalence
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\kappa}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is
\kappa
-compactly generated.

Remark 5.2
.

The proofs of this section could be somewhat simplified by using the construction of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
from
[
BGT13
]
. To have an independent treatment of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, we decided to include the slightly more complicated arguments.

We need a couple of preliminaries.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
u:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a filtered colimit-preserving functor between
\kappa
-compactly generated categories admitting filtered colimits, and suppose
u
preserves
\kappa
-compact objects.
Then any
u
-equivalence

in
\mathcal{C}
is a
\kappa
-filtered colimit of
u
-equivalences between
\kappa
-compacts.

Proof.

Consider the functors

and the diagonal functor
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\Delta^{1}}
. They both preserve
\kappa
-compacts (since they are computed pointwise in these categories, and
u
preserves
\kappa
-compacts by assumption) and filtered colimits (for the same reason). Since
\kappa
is uncountable, we can apply

[
Ram24
, Proposition 2.31]

to conclude that the pullback
\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{1}}\times_{\mathcal{D}^{\Delta^{1}}}\mathcal{D}
is
\kappa
-compactly generated, and that the projection functors preserve
\kappa
-compacts. This pullback is exactly the category of
u
-equivalences, so we deduce that
f
is a
\kappa
-filtered colimit of
u
-equivalences between
\kappa
-compacts.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular uncountable cardinal.

(1)

Any motivic equivalence

in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
is a
\kappa
-filtered colimit of motivic equivalences between
\kappa
-compact stable
\infty
-categories.

(2)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is
\kappa
-compactly generated.

Let

denote the stable full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
generated under
\kappa
-small colimits by motives of
\kappa
-compact categories. The functor

, being finitary, factors as

. By Lemma

,

is a finitary localizing invariant.

It follows that

is an
F_{\kappa}
-equivalence (since it is a motivic equivalence). Conversely, since

factors

, any
F_{\kappa}
-equivalence is a motivic equivalence. Thus it suffices to prove the assertion (1) for

-equivalences, which is a special case of Lemma

because

is finitary and by

\kappa
-compacts.

By the universal property of

, the localization functor

, and again because of that same universal property,

So

is a retract of a
\kappa
-compactly generated category. Since

is uncountable, we can apply

, Corollary 2.33]
to conclude that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is
\kappa
-compactly generated.
∎

We will then need the following technical statement:

be an arbitrary regular cardinal. A functor

We note that “only if” is clear, so we focus on “if”. This is essentially the same fact as

Let

We can now prove a slightly weaker version of Proposition

Lemma 5.6
.

For any regular uncountable cardinal
\kappa

\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
induces an equivalence
\operatorname{Ind}_{\kappa}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa})\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an arbitrary
\infty
-category with
\kappa
-filtered colimits. We wish to prove that restriction along the functor

\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
induces an equivalence

\operatorname{Fun}^{\kappa-\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{E})\to\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{M}_{\kappa},\mathcal{E}).

By definition, it is equivalent to the restriction functor

\operatorname{Fun}^{\kappa-\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{E})\to\operatorname{Fun}_{W^{\kappa}_{\mathrm{mot}}}((\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty})^{\kappa},\mathcal{E})
.

Since the slice categories
(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty})^{\kappa}_{/\mathcal{C}}
are
\kappa
-filtered for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
, we find that any functor
(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty})^{\kappa}\to\mathcal{E}

Using the universal property of left Kan extensions, together with the fact that
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}

preserves filtered colimits, we see that the above restriction functor is equivalent to the restriction

\operatorname{Fun}^{\kappa-\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{E})\to\operatorname{Fun}^{\kappa-\mathrm{fin}}_{W^{\kappa}_{\mathrm{mot}}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{E})

along
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
To conclude, we are thus left with two facts: first, that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\kappa}(W^{\kappa}_{\mathrm{mot}})=W_{\mathrm{mot}}

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{E}
preserves
\kappa
-filtered colimits if and only if its
restriction to
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
does. Both facts were proved above, see Lemmas
5.4

and
5.5
.
∎

6.
The universal
\aleph_{1}
-finitary invariant

One of the main subtleties of the theory of localizing motives is the necessity to
consider finitary invariants in the definition. One has to impose some finitary assumptions in order not to run into set-theoretic issues but one
may also consider the
\kappa
-finitary version of this invariant
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc},\kappa}
for any regular
uncountable cardinal
\kappa
(see also Remark
6.19
).
It is worth pointing out that some important localizing invariants such as
TC
,
TR
and Selmer K-theory
are only
\aleph_{1}
-finitary but not finitary. In this sense
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc},\kappa}
for
\kappa>\omega

is in principle a more fundamental invariant.

The first nontrivial case to consider is
\kappa=\aleph_{1}
.
Surprisingly, it turns out that in this case one obtains the same
\infty
-category.

Theorem 6.1
.

The functor
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is the universal
\aleph_{1}
-finitary localizing invariant:
for every stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{X}
, restriction along
\gamma
induces an equivalence

\gamma^{*}:\operatorname{Fun}^{\aleph_{1}-\mathrm{fin}}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc},\aleph_{1}-\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{X}).

The proof of this theorem occupies the entire section.

6.2.
Splitting invariants

The functor
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
factors through the
universal splitting invariant

\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{split}}.

As an intermediate step, we prove that this functor is already very close to being a localization.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an
\infty
-category.
A finite product-preserving functor
E:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{X}
is said to be a splitting invariant if
every semiorthogonal decomposition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}

induces an equivalence

(E(r),E(p)):E(\mathcal{D})\xrightarrow{\sim}E(\mathcal{C})\times E(\mathcal{E}).

Remark 6.4
.

Splitting invariants usually go by the name of “additive invariants” in the literature.
We have chosen to use a different term to avoid confusion with additive functors, and additive presheaves specifically.
See in particular Example
6.7
below, which would use unnecessarily ambiguous terminology if we had to speak about “additive presheaves”.

Note that we will consequently also refer to the target of the universal splitting invariant as the
\infty
-category of splitting motives, rather than additive motives.

Example 6.5
.

The
\infty
-category of fiber sequences
S_{2}\mathcal{C}
in a given small stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
admits
a semiorthogonal decomposition into two copies of
\mathcal{C}
. For a finite product-preserving functor to send this
semiorthogonal decomposition to a product means inverting the map
s_{\mathcal{C}}:S_{2}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
given by taking
the first and the last components of a fiber sequence.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
E:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{X}
be a splitting invariant.
Then
E
lifts uniquely to a splitting invariant
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{CGrp}(\mathcal{X})
valued in commutative group objects in
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Since
E
preserves finite products,
[
GGN15
, Corollary 2.5]
shows that
E
lifts uniquely to a functor

E:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{CMon}(\mathcal{X}).

So we only have to show that
E(\mathcal{C})
is grouplike for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
.
Since
E
inverts
s_{\mathcal{C}}
, it follows that the maps induced by the exact functors

s:\mathcal{C}\to S_{2}\mathcal{C},\ x\mapsto(x\to x\oplus\Sigma x\to\Sigma x),\quad\text{and}\quad t:\mathcal{C}\to S_{2}\mathcal{C},\ x\mapsto(x\to 0\to\Sigma x),

are homotopic.
Consequently,
0\simeq E(\mathrm{id}+\Sigma)
.
It follows that

\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}&\Sigma\\
0&\mathrm{id}\end{pmatrix}:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}

becomes an inverse of the shear map upon application of
E
.

The of
E
is also a splitting invariant because limits in
\mathrm{CGrp}(\mathcal{X})
are computed in
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Remark 6.12

Corollary 6.13

The unit object

\kappa

r_{n}p_{n}i_{k}\simeq r_{n}p_{n}i_{n}f_{k,n}\simeq r_{n}f_{k,n}\simeq 0

because
f_{k,n}
factors over
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}
.
Since every object of
\mathcal{A}
is of the form
i_{k}(a)
for some
k
, the functor
\mathrm{gr}
is well-defined.
We claim that the exact functor

t:=\sum_{n\geq 0}i_{n}s_{n}:\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{G}_{n}\to\mathcal{C},

witnesses that
\mathrm{gr}
is a universal K-equivalence.

For
n>k\geq 0
, the preceding paragraph shows that
r_{n}p_{n}i_{k}s_{k}\simeq 0
.
If
n=k
, then
r_{n}p_{n}i_{n}s_{n}\simeq\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{n}}
.
Observing that the right adjoint
g_{k,n}
of
f_{k,n}
satisfies
p_{k}\simeq g_{k,n}p_{n}
, we obtain

f_{k}p_{k}i_{n}s_{n}\simeq f_{k}g_{k,n}p_{n}i_{n}s_{n}\simeq f_{k}g_{k,n}s_{n}\simeq f_{k}g_{k,n-1}g_{n-1}s_{n}\simeq 0.

Consequently, the composite
\mathrm{gr}\circ i_{k}s_{k}:\mathcal{G}_{k}\to\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{G}_{n}
is precisely the inclusion of the
k
-th summand.
This shows that
\mathrm{gr}\circ t\simeq\mathrm{id}_{\bigoplus_{\mathbf{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n}}
.

Using once more that every object of
\mathcal{A}
comes from some filtration step, it follows that for any given
a\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
q_{n}(a)\simeq 0
for all but finitely many
n\geq-1
.
In particular, the internal direct sum
\bigoplus_{n\geq-1}j_{n}q_{n}
is a well-defined endofunctor of
\mathcal{A}
.
Note that there exists a cofiber sequence of exact functors

\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\bigoplus_{n\geq-1}j_{n}q_{n}\to\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}j_{n}q_{n}.

Unwinding definitions, one finds that there exist bicartesian squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{n}p_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{n}s_{n}r_{n}p_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{n-1}q_{n-1}}

which give rise to the cofiber sequence

t\circ\mathrm{gr}\simeq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}i_{n}s_{n}r_{n}p_{n}\to\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}j_{n-1}q_{n-1}\to\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}j_{n}q_{n}.

It follows that
[t\circ\mathrm{gr}]=[\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}]\in K_{0}(\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}))
as required.
∎

A.14.
Applications to products

Thanks to Theorem
A.10
both suspensions and loops of any localizing invariant are categorified via
certain functors

\mathrm{Calk},\Gamma:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},

i.e. there are natural equivalences
E(\mathrm{Calk}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\Sigma E(\mathcal{C})
and
E(\Gamma(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\Omega E(\mathcal{C})

for
any
localizing invariant
E
.
In particular, somewhat counterintuitively, for a localizing invariant valued in spectra, its zeroth homotopy group
completely determines its values.

Corollary A.15
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a stable
\infty
-category with a non-degenerate t-structure and let
E\to F
be a natural
transformation between localizing invariants
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{E}
.
Assume that
\pi_{0}E(\mathcal{C})\to\pi_{0}F(\mathcal{C})
is an equivalence for all
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
. Then

E(\mathcal{C})\to F(\mathcal{C})
is also an equivalence for all
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Since the t-structure on
\mathcal{E}
is non-degenerate, it suffices to show that
\pi_{i}E(\mathcal{C})\to\pi_{i}F(\mathcal{C})
is an
equivalence for all
i
. Now we use the natural equivalences
E(\mathrm{Calk}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\Sigma E(\mathcal{C})
and
E(\Gamma(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\Omega E(\mathcal{C})
. For
i
negative we have a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}E(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}F(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}E(\mathrm{Calk}^{-i}(\mathcal{C}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}F(\mathrm{Calk}^{-i}(\mathcal{C})),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where the bottom horizontal map is an equivalence by assumption, which implies that the upper map is an equivalence.
Similarly, for
i
positive the result follows from commutativity of the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}E(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}F(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}E(\Gamma^{i}\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}F(\Gamma^{i}\mathcal{C}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

∎

Corollary
A.15
gives an alternative proof of
[
KW19
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Corollary A.16
.

For any family of stable
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{C}_{i}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
indexed by a set
I
, the map

\operatorname{K}(\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i})\to\prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}_{i})

is an equivalence

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim for a constant family
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\mathcal{C}
because any other family is a retract of such.
Define localizing invariants
E
and
F
valued in spectra via the following formulas:

E(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{K}(\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}),F(\mathcal{C})=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}).

The natural map
E\to F
is an isomorphism on
\pi_{0}
, so by Corollary
A.15
it is an equivalence.
∎

Appendix B
Motives over an arbitrary base

In the main body of the paper, we worked in so-called “absolute” localizing motives, that is, motives of stable
\infty
-categories. One can, in turn, decide to work over more general bases, for example consider only
\mathbb Z
-linear stable
\infty
-categories (the
\infty
-categorical version of classical dg-categories). More generally, one can work over any base
\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathcal{P}r^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathrm{St}})
. To make sense of this precisely, it is easier to work in the dualizable setting rather than in the small setting. From Efimov’s recent breakthroughs in the theory of localizing invariants (and the relevant
\mathcal{V}
-linear analogs, see
[
Ram24
]
), the whole theory is insensitive to this and we invite the reader to check for themselves the relevant results.

The goal of this brief appendix is to indicate the necessary changes in the main text to make the whole story go through
\mathcal{V}
-linearly. The point will be that they are quite mild, and except for precise cardinality estimates, one does not lose anything. Therefore, while we do indicate the changes, we do not provide details regarding said changes.

Notation B.1
.

Throughout this section, we fix
\mathcal{V}
, as well as a regular uncountable cardinal
\lambda
such that
\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathcal{P}r^{\mathrm{L}}_{\lambda})
. That is,
\mathcal{V}
is
\lambda
-compactly generated, its unit is
\lambda
-compact, and tensor products of
\lambda
-compacts remain
\lambda
-compact.

Notation B.2
.

We let
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
denote the
\infty
-category of dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-modules in
\mathcal{P}r^{\mathrm{L}}
and internal left adjoints between them.

We refer to
[
Ram24
]
for basics about this
\infty
-category. In particular, it has colimits (which are computed underlying in
\mathcal{P}r^{\mathrm{L}}
) and so we can define:

Definition B.3
.

A
\mathcal{V}
-localizing invariant is a functor
E:\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}\to\mathcal{E}
for some stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{E}
that sends cofiber sequences along fully faithful functors to cofiber sequences.

E
is called finitary if it preserves filtered colimits.

Definition B.4
.

A
\mathcal{V}
-motivic equivalence is a map in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
which is sent to an equivalence by any finitary
\mathcal{V}
-localizing invariant.

Remark B.5
.

The basechange functor
\mathcal{V}\otimes-:(\mathcal{P}r^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathrm{St}})^{\mathrm{dbl}}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
preserves fully faithful functors and all colimits, therefore precomposition by it sends (finitary)
\mathcal{V}
-localizing invariants to (finitary) localizing invariants. Therefore,
\mathcal{V}\otimes-
sends motivic equivalences to
\mathcal{V}
-motivic equivalences.

Similarly,
\mathcal{V}
-motivic equivalences are closed under tensor products.

We now wish to prove the following analog of our main result:

Theorem B.6
.

The localization of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
at motivic equivalences is locally small, cocomplete and stable, and is the target of the universal
\mathcal{V}
-localizing invariant.

In the case of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
and ordinary localizing invariants, our proof relied on several ingredients, and we outline them here.

The first claim is that the direct analog of Proposition
2.8
holds in this setting:

Observation B.7
.

There is a cofibration structure on
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
with
\mathcal{V}
-motivic equivalences as weak equivalences and (underlying) fully faithful functors as cofibrations.

The only difference with the proof of Proposition
2.8
is the construction of
the functorial factorizations. To explain how to fix this, we first recall that
\mathcal{V}
-modules have underlying
\mathcal{V}
-enriched

\infty
-categories, which allows us to take
\mathcal{V}
-enriched presheaf categories of small subcategories of
\mathcal{V}
-modules, which
we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{C})
if
\mathcal{C}
is such a subcategory. With this in hand, we can now recall that by

[
Ram24
, Corollaries 1.7 and 1.59, Remark 1.60]
, there is an internal left adjoint
embedding
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{M}^{\lambda})
, natural in the dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-module
\mathcal{M}
. Since

\lambda
is uncountable,
\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}
admits infinite coproducts and so this presheaf category admits an Eilenberg
swindle, which implies that it is motivically trivial. All the other constructions and arguments go through unchanged.

The second claim we make is that the set-theoretic care that we took in the body of the paper also goes through here. The key input to making everything work is the main result of
[
Ram24
]
, that the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
is itself presentable.
In particular, there are two key set-theoretic insights in the main body of the text, which we state here in the
\mathcal{V}
-linear setting. The first was Corollary
3.9
:

Observation B.8
.

There is a cofinal class of regular cardinals
\kappa
such that the cofibration structure described above restricts to a cofibration structure on
\kappa
-compact dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-modules.

In the absolute case, we singled out the countably closed cardinals (though in fact, with more care, we could have proved it for all regular uncountable cardinals). The second was the following, used in Lemma
4.8
and subsequent results:

Observation B.9
.

There exists a regular cardinal
\kappa
(in the absolute case,
\kappa=\aleph_{1}
) so that every localization sequence is a
\kappa
-filtered colimit of localization sequences between
\kappa
-compacts.

For the first statement, namely Observation
B.8
, we needed to prove that
\kappa
-compacts are closed under cofibers (which is obvious for any
\kappa
), and we needed to check that for suitable
\kappa
’s, the “mapping cone” construction from Proposition
2.8
(adapted here with
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{M}^{\lambda})
in place of
\operatorname{Ind}(C)^{\aleph_{1}}
) preserves
\kappa
-compacts — this was the combination of Corollary
3.6
and Corollary
3.8
. While we could run similar proofs to get more precise cardinality estimates, we explain the proof here simply using presentability of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
. For this, we introduce a class of cardinals that corresponds to the countably closed ones in the absolute case.

Definition B.10
.

A regular cardinal
\kappa
is called
(\mathcal{V},\lambda)
-closed if it satisfies
\lambda\ll\kappa
as in
[
Lur17b
, Definition 5.4.2.8]
and the following conditions hold

a)

for all
\lambda
-compact dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-modules
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{M}^{\lambda})
is
\kappa
-compact as a dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-module;

b)

for any cospan
(\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathcal{M}_{01}\leftarrow\mathcal{M}_{1})
of
\lambda
-compact dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-modules and internal left adjoints, their oriented pullback
\mathcal{M}_{0}\vec{\times}_{\mathcal{M}_{01}}\mathcal{M}_{1}
is
\kappa
-compact.

Remark B.11
.

Since
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
is presentable,
(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}})^{\lambda}
is small and so all
\kappa
’s large enough satisfy parts a) and b) of this definition. Since the first part of the definition is satisfied for cofinally many
\kappa
’s, we also see that there is a cofinal collection of
(\mathcal{V},\lambda)
-closed cardinals.

Lemma B.12
.

Suppose
\kappa\geq\lambda
is a
(\mathcal{V},\lambda)
-closed cardinal, and
\mathcal{M}
is a
\kappa
-compact dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-module. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{M}^{\lambda})
is also
\kappa
-compact.

Furthermore, lax pullbacks of
\kappa
-compact dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-modules are also
\kappa
-compact.

Proof.

Both of these follow from the functors
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{V}}((-)^{\lambda})
and “lax pullback” being
\lambda
-accessible: indeed,
the relation
\lambda\ll\kappa
guarantees that any
\kappa
-compact dualizable
\mathcal{V}
-module is a
\kappa
-small,

\lambda
-filtered colimit of
\lambda
-compacts, and so applying these functors also yields
\kappa
-small,

\lambda
-filtered colimits of their values at
\lambda
-compacts, which are by design
\kappa
-compact.
∎

For the second statement, Observation
B.9
, we use again presentability: the
\infty
-category of
localization sequences in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
is presentable and the forgetful functors to
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
preserve and jointly reflect

\kappa
-compacts for
\kappa

Finally, to conclude the proof in exactly the same manner as in Section
4
, we needed a final ingredient involving the Grayson construction:

Observation B.13
.

The localization
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}[W_{\mathcal{V}-\mathrm{mot}}^{-1}]
is stable.

To prove this, we note that Remark
B.5
gives us a symmetric monoidal, colimit-preserving functor
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}[W_{\mathrm{mot}}^{-1}]\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{dbl}}[W_{\mathcal{V}-\mathrm{mot}}^{-1}]
, where the source is already stable by Theorem

, which proves stability of the target as well.

Example B.14
.

The trace functor as defined in

Personalized Prediction of prognosis in ebv-associated lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma: insights from a Chinese cohort study
Authors: Yan Wang, Yuanyuan Liu, Gongming Wang, Songzuo Xie, Jinqi You, Xinyi Yang, Minxing Li, Jingjing Zhao, Desheng Weng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeLymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma (LELC) is a rare tumor. The therapeutic strategies and personalized prediction of prognosis for this tumor remain inadequately defined.Patients and methodsFive hundred seventy-four patients from Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center were recruited and split into a training cohort (402) and a validation cohort (172). Significant variables identified through Cox regression analyses were incorporated to develop nomograms. We assessed the predictive precision and discriminatory capacity of the nomograms by employing various metrics, including the time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, concordance index (C-index), decision curve analysis (DCA) curve, calibration curve, and Kaplan-Meier curves.ResultsMultivariate Cox analysis identified that surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, N stage, M stage, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and lactate dehydrogenase-to-albumin ratio (LAR) were independently associated with overall survival (OS) in LELC patients. For progression-free survival (PFS), surgery, chemotherapy, T stage, N stage and M stage emerged as independent predictors. Nomograms were developed by integrating the factors to provide a comprehensive predictive tool. The C-indices of nomograms for forecasting OS and PFS were 0.786 (95%CI 0.734–0.838) and 0.693 (95%CI 0.651–0.735), correspondingly. ROC curves and calibration curves demonstrated consistency with actual observations. Moreover, DCA curves demonstrated the practicality of nomograms, emphasizing their exceptional capacity to distinguish high-risk individuals compared with the 8th AJCC staging system.ConclusionThe newly devised nomograms demonstrated considerable potential in predicting OS. Furthermore, this research is the first to suggest that markers related to inflammation and nutrition, such as PLR and LAR, could be significant predictors for forecasting the outcome of individuals with LELC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-15988-2.

Introduction

Lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma (LELC) is a relatively rare tumor, primarily manifesting in the lungs [1]. However, it has also been documented as originating in various other locations, including the parotid gland, nasopharynx, gastrointestinal tract, liver, breast, bladder, and other sites [2–4]. In terms of morphology, LELC exhibits resemblances to undifferentiated nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), which is distinguished by a significant presence of lymphocytes within the epithelial tumor cells [5]. LELC, which is more common in Asia, exhibits a strong association with Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) infections, as indicated by multiple studies [1, 3, 5–7].

As of now, there are no established treatment guidelines for LELC, and the majority of treatments for this type of cancer utilize similar strategies as those used for other malignant tumors in similar locations [3]. Due to the heterogeneity of LELC, individualized cancer treatment becomes imperative. Oncologists can enhance treatment effectiveness by identifying high-risk patients prone to adverse clinical outcomes. Nonetheless, the optimal treatment regimen remains a matter of debate. The dispute could arise in part due to the use of Tumor-Node-Metastasis (TNM) staging to assess risk, which may not completely account for the variation in prognosis among patients in the same stage because of its simplicity. Moreover, existing literature on LELC primarily comprises retrospective analyses and case reports, characterized by modest sample sizes and limited availability of prognostic information.

The effective utilization of cancer outcome nomograms in prognostic evaluation for individuals with tumors has greatly aided in risk assessment and enhanced decision-making for both patients and physicians. By incorporating key clinical and pathological factors, these nomograms have proven effective [8]. While nomograms have been established for various cancer types and have shown potential to surpass traditional TNM staging in predicting prognosis [9–11], there remains a paucity of oncologic outcome nomograms tailored to a Chinese population cohort for the specific prediction of LELC prognosis, as far as we know.

Hence, our objective in the current research was to create reliable nomograms that can effectively predict the prognosis of LELC by demonstrating strong discriminatory abilities. Our approach involved integrating a comprehensive set of clinical and pathological features, treatment modalities, and serological indices. The aim is to attain a personalized forecasting model, providing a strong basis for clinical decision-making in the treatment of LELC.

Materials and methods

Patients and sample selection

This study retrospectively enrolled 574 LELC patients at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (Guangzhou, China) from December 2007 to December 2022. The inclusion criterion was histologically confirmed LELC. The exclusion criteria included: (i) the presence of a second primary tumor; (ii) incomplete clinical information. The patient cohort was randomly divided into training and validation cohorts in a 7:3 ratio, with 402 individuals assigned to the training cohort and 172 to the validation cohort. The flow chart depicting patient inclusion is presented in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Data collection and definitions

Baseline clinicopathologic characteristics were documented before treatment. The database of Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center provided all the necessary clinicopathologic details, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), primary site, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, family history, smoking history, alcohol consumption history, T, N, M, and clinical stage. Furthermore, we gathered information regarding C-reactive protein (CRP), platelet count, lymphocyte count, serum albumin, neutrophil count, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), albumin-globulin ratio (A/G), monocyte count, and Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) DNA. The quantification of plasma EBV-DNA was performed through real-time PCR, indicating the quantity of EBV genome copies per milliliter. Several of these indicators were employed to calculate inflammatory and nutritional biomarkers, with the corresponding formulas provided as follows:PLR (platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio) = platelet count (109/L)/lymphocyte count (109/L);NLR (neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio) = neutrophil count (109/L)/lymphocyte count (109/L); LMR (lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio) = lymphocyte count (109/L)/monocyte count (109/L);LAR (lactate dehydrogenase-to-albumin ratio) = lactate dehydrogenase (U/L)/serum albumin (g/L);PNI (prognostic nutritional index) = serum albumin (g/L) + 5 × lymphocyte count (109/L);SIRI (systemic inflammatory response index) = monocyte count (109/L) × neutrophil count (109/L)/lymphocyte count (109/L).

OS was determined as the period starting from the time of diagnosis until either death from any reason or last visit. PFS was defined as the duration from diagnosis to the first occurrence of disease progression or death from any cause.

Statistical analyses

In this study, various prognostic-based covariates were analyzed, including sex, age, family history of cancer, smoking history, alcohol consumption history, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, primary site, BMI, A/G, CRP, SIRI, PNI, LAR, LMR, NLR, PLR, T, N, M and clinical stage. The continuous variable age was categorized into two subgroups using a cutoff value of 50 [3, 12]. T stage was transformed into categorical variables based on I/II and III/IV [3, 13]. N stage was converted into categorical variables based on 0/1 and 2/3 [3, 7]. A/G was classified as categorical variables using normal values as cutoff values. The EBV DNA load was converted into a categorical variable by using a threshold value of 2000 copies/mL based on the literature [14, 15]. BMI (18.5 kg/m²) [16] and CRP (0.1 mg/dL and 0.3 mg/dL, after multiplying by 10 to convert to grams per liter) [14, 17] were transformed into categorical factors based on universally recognized thresholds. X-tile software version 3.6.1 was used to identify the ideal cutoff values for variables like SIRI, LAR, LMR, NLR, PLR, and PNI, which do not have explicit thresholds. On the result interface of X-tile, we selected 50 potential cut-off values and their corresponding p-values, with the selected cut-off values covering the main intervals of the cut-off value range. Subsequently, we organized the data and entered the recorded cut-off values and corresponding p-values into a table. Later, we used the p.adjust method with the setting of method = fdr for multiple test correction, and prioritized the candidate values with p < 0.05 after FDR correction to obtain the optimal cut-off value.

To compare the variables of the training and validation cohort, Pearson’s chi-square tests and Fisher’s exact tests were utilized. Prognostic factors impacting the OS and PFS were identified through the execution of univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. Prognostic factors were used to develop nomograms that predict the OS and PFS at 3- and 5- year. The nomograms’ ability to differentiate was evaluated by calculating the C-index and the area under the curve (AUC) of the time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. To assess the concordance between projected and real outcomes, calibration curves were utilized. Additionally, to validate the clinical utility of the models, decision curve analyses (DCA) were performed, comparing the predictive ability of the nomograms to the 8th edition AJCC staging system. The total score for each patient in the training and validation cohorts was calculated according to the nomograms. In the next step, patients were categorized into low-risk and high-risk groups according to their median scores. Subsequently, survival analysis was conducted on these groups utilizing the Kaplan–Meier technique.

In the present study, all statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.2.1 from the website http://WWW.R-project.org. The software packages utilized included survival, survminer, rms, timeROC, ggplot2, ggDCA, Table 1, pec, dplyr, and nomogramFormula. A statistical significance level of p < 0.05 was deemed significant, and all tests were conducted with a two-sided approach.

Results

Patient characteristics

Out of the total participants in our research, 307 individuals (53.5%) identified as male, whereas 267 (46.5%) identified as female. The lungs were the main primary site, representing 69.2% of the cases. 147 (25.6%) fell into M1 stage. Based on the TNM staging system, 216 individuals (37.6%) were categorized as stage IV. In Supplementary Table 1, demographics and clinicopathologic features of the overall population are summarized, as well as training and validation cohorts. Crucially, the similarity between training cohort and validation cohort was observed in terms of demographics and clinical traits (p > 0.05).

Construction of the nomogram

Our analysis showed that surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, primary site, PLR, NLR, LMR, LAR, PNI, SIRI, T, N, M and clinical stage were all crucial factors affecting OS in patients with LELC. Following this, in multivariate Cox regression analyses that included these notable factors, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, N stage, M stage, PLR, and LAR were determined to be autonomous prognostic factors for OS in individuals with LELC (Table 1). Furthermore, our results indicated that the prognosis of PFS in LELC patients was influenced by surgery, chemotherapy, T stage, N stage and M stage, which were recognized as independent prognostic hallmarks (Table 2).

The nomograms for OS and PFS were subsequently constructed using the aforementioned independent predictors (Figs. 1 and 2). Each independent prognostic factor was individually listed as a variable in the nomograms, along with its corresponding contribution value. The relative contribution of each predictor is presented in Supplementary Tables 2 and 3. The total score was calculated by summing the scores for each variable category; aligning this total score with the result scale allowed for the estimation of the patient’s 3- and 5-year OS and PFS probability. The results of the model’s sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value are respectively shown in Supplementary Table 4. The results of the model’s Hosmer-Lemeshow test and Brier score are presented in Supplementary Table 5.

Verification of the nomogram

The c-indices of nomograms for forecasting OS and PFS in individuals with LELC were 0.786 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.734–0.838) and 0.693 (95% CI 0.651–0.735), correspondingly. We performed 1000 bootstrap resamplings, and the corrected c-indices of the nomograms for predicting OS and PFS were 0.745 and 0.661, respectively. Additionally, we used the 10-fold cross-validation method to obtain the corrected C-indices of the nomograms for predicting OS and PFS, which were 0.755 and 0.632, respectively.

To further confirm the accuracy of nomograms, time- dependent ROC curves were generated for predicting the 3- and 5-year OS in not only the training cohort but also the validation group (Fig. 3A and B). In the training cohort, the AUCs for forecasting OS at 3- and 5- year were 0.775 (95% CI 0.709–0.841) and 0.806 (95% CI 0.742–0.870), correspondingly. The AUCs for predicting 3- and 5-year OS in the validation cohort were 0.785 (95% CI 0.688–0.882) and 0.800 (95% CI 0.693–0.907), respectively. Furthermore, the AUCs for predicting PFS at 3- and 5- years in the training cohort were 0.687 (95% CI 0.619–0.755) and 0.674 (95% CI 0.603–0.745), whereas in the validation cohort, they were 0.600 (95% CI 0.488–0.713) and 0.665 (95% CI 0.557–0.774), correspondingly (Fig. 3C and D).

Evaluation of clinical applicability

Clinical applicability was primarily assessed using DCA [18]. DCA is an innovative approach for assessing diagnostic assays, prognostic algorithms, and genetic indicators. It facilitates the comparison of net benefit and clinical utility among different prediction systems, presenting the results graphically [19]. We compared the established nomograms with the 8th edition AJCC staging system in the present study. The results of the DCA showed that, except for the nomogram for PFS prediction in the validation cohort — whose net clinical benefit was not significantly superior to that of the 8th edition AJCC staging system — the nomograms for predicting both OS and PFS in all other cohorts exhibited significantly better net clinical benefit than the 8th edition AJCC staging system (Fig. 6).

Subgroup analyses of risk stratification

To further confirm the stability and effectiveness of the nomograms from various perspectives, we calculated the scores of every individual using the established nomograms. In the training group, the median OS score was 149.474 and in the validation group, it was 111.704. Patients were classified into high-risk and low-risk groups on the basis of the median score. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that in both the training and validation cohorts, the low-risk group had superior OS compared with the high-risk group, and the same was true for PFS (Fig. 7). Discussion

LELC is a unique and rare tumor that can manifest in various anatomical sites throughout the body. Our findings, in line with existing literature, emphasize the lung as the most common primary site [1, 20]. However, studies based on the SEER database have asserted that the nasopharynx is the most common primary site for LELC [12], suggesting potential ethnic differences. Given the rarity of LELC, its staging and treatment presently rely on criteria established for other tumor types in the same anatomical location. Specific guidelines for LELC have not been developed, and research on its prognosis is relatively limited. Existing studies have indicated a more favorable prognosis for LELC patients compared to individuals with other tumor types at the same anatomical site [20, 21]. Nevertheless, even among patients with identical TNM stages, there is variability in prognosis, emphasizing the need for further investigations into the prognostic factors influencing LELC patients.

Current researches show that systemic inflammation is closely related to cancer and is involved in the entire process of tumorigenesis and development [22]. In addition, there is growing interest in cancer and nutrition-related research, as nutritional indicators can significantly influence the prognosis of cancer patients [23]. In contemporary studies, inflammatory biomarkers such as NLR, LMR, and nutritional markers like PNI, LAR, and A/G, have demonstrated strong prognostic capabilities for patients with NPC [24–27]. However, no study has explored the prognostic value of these indicators for patients with LELC. Hence, our study incorporated these inflammatory and nutritional biomarkers to evaluate their impact on the prognosis of LELC.

By incorporating important prognostic factors, nomogram can assist patients and doctors in making better-informed decisions regarding management and achieving more precise prognosis predictions compared to the traditional TNM staging [9, 10]. Initially, we subjected the relevant indicators to univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses in our research, identifying surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, N stage, M stage, PLR, and LAR as independent prognostic factors for OS in patients with LELC. Moreover, the prognosis of PFS in LELC patients was found to be influenced by surgery, chemotherapy, T stage, N stage and M stage as independent prognostic factors. Intriguingly, the research conducted by Qiu et al. [3] found that surgery, radiotherapy and N stage were autonomous predictors for OS and PFS in individuals diagnosed with non-nasopharyngeal lymphoepithelial carcinoma, which corresponds with our discoveries. It is worth mentioning that there has been no previous study that incorporated PLR, and LAR into the analysis of prognostics for LELC patients.

Utilizing meaningful variables identified through multivariate Cox regression analyses, we formulated a unique nomogram designed to predict 3- and 5-year OS or PFS probability in patients with LELC. This nomogram represents a significant advancement in individualized survival prediction. To our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive nomogram established and validated within a large Chinese population, encompassing a diverse array of clinicopathologic indicators alongside inflammatory and nutritional markers. For the nomogram predicting OS in patients with LELC, it exhibited excellent performance in calibration accuracy, discriminative ability, and clinical utility, with its reliability confirmed by validation in an cohort. Importantly, it demonstrated superior discriminative ability compared to the conventional TNM staging system. This innovation highlights the potential clinical value of the nomogram in improving the accuracy of survival prediction for LELC patients. As for the nomogram predicting PFS, its predictive accuracy was inferior to that of the OS nomogram. This performance drop raises valid concerns about the clinical utility of the PFS nomogram, which we recognize as an important caveat of our study.​ Several potential explanations may underlie the poor predictive performance of the PFS nomogram. First, PFS as an endpoint is inherently more susceptible to variability in clinical assessment and follow-up practices compared to OS. Differences in imaging frequency, radiological interpretation criteria, and physician judgment regarding disease progression across the training and validation cohorts could introduce measurement bias that impacts PFS prediction. Second, the biological heterogeneity of LELC may have a more pronounced effect on disease progression than on overall survival. Third, selection bias inherent to the retrospective design may have disproportionately affected the PFS endpoint. Finally, the sample size of the validation cohort, although relatively large for a rare disease like LELC, may still be insufficient to robustly validate a PFS nomogram, especially given the heterogeneity of primary tumor sites included in the study.​.

Moreover, research conducted by Chen et al., Qiu et al., and a meta-analysis indicate that increased levels of circulating EBV DNA are linked to a worse outlook in LELC patients [3, 5, 20]. The findings from these studies contribute to the increasing amount of evidence that connects the levels of circulating EBV DNA with the prognostic results of patients with LELC. Nevertheless, this measurement was omitted from the subsequent modeling procedure in our study due to the fact that not all individuals underwent this particular examination. Of the 574 participants in the present study, 330 had plasma EBV DNA results and were categorized using a cutoff value of 2000 copies/mL, with 137 showing elevated levels and 193 having lower levels. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis suggested that individuals belonging to the high-level EBV DNA category exhibited a worse prognosis than those in the low-level EBV DNA group, both in terms of OS and PFS (Supplementary Fig. 2). This finding aligns with previous results mentioned in the literature [5, 20]. Hence, plasma EBV DNA serves as a significant predictive indicator for individuals with LELC, and it is crucial to obtain baseline plasma EBV DNA prior to treatment.

This was the first retrospective study to explore the prognostic factors of LELC in various sites of the Chinese population. Due to the large heterogeneity of tumors at different sites, we will expand the sample size by including multiple centers in order to perform subgroup analyses at each site. In addition, avoiding selection bias is challenging when conducting a retrospective study. To mitigate this bias, we included a relatively large training and validation cohort, constructing and internally validating the nomogram. However, the potential for bias still exists due to the retrospective nature of the analysis. While the internal validation demonstrated excellent discriminatory capacity and satisfactory agreement between observed OS and OS probabilities estimated by the nomogram, external validation using an independent dataset was not performed. To enhance the reliability and applicability of the findings, future studies may consider integrating extensive data from various institutions. Furthermore, to improve the PFS nomogram, future work should aim to incorporate additional prognostic biomarkers and refine endpoint definitions through standardized radiological assessment protocols. Expanding the sample size with multi-center data and performing site-specific subgroup analyses may also help reduce heterogeneity and enhance the predictive performance of the PFS model. The absence of EBV-DNA data is also a limitation of this study. Given the nearly 50% rate of missing values for this single variable, multiple imputation was not performed, and this indicator was ultimately excluded from the final model. Since EBV-DNA may be an important prognostic indicator, its exclusion could potentially affect the predictive accuracy of the model. However, it is worth noting that the high rate of missing EBV-DNA data in this study may reflect a current reality in real-world clinical practice—biomarker testing is not yet universally implemented or standardized. Therefore, a model built on more readily available clinical variables may actually offer broader applicability in routine clinical settings. Future prospective studies with standardized protocols and complete EBV-DNA data collection are warranted to determine whether this biomarker provides incremental prognostic value beyond the factors identified in our current model.

Conclusion

To sum up, the created nomograms for forecasting OS functioned as a valuable tool tailored to LELC patients in a clinical setting. By offering dependable predictive data, it facilitated tailored treatment for LELC individuals. In addition, our research, for the first time, emphasized the possibility of using inflammatory indicators and nutritional biomarkers (PLR and LAR) as significant markers to forecast the outcome of individuals with LELC.

Almost Commutative Terwilliger Algebras II: Strong Gelfand Pairs
Nicholas L. Bastian, Stephen P. Humphries

Abstract
Terwilliger algebras are a subalgebra of a matrix algebra constructed from an association scheme. In 2010, Tanaka defined what it means for a Terwilliger algebra to be almost commutative and gave five equivalent conditions for a Terwilliger algebra to be almost commutative. In this paper we look at Terwilliger algebras coming from strong Gelfand pairs ( G , H ) (G,H) for a finite group G G . From such a pair, one can create a Terwilliger algebra using the Schur ring of H − H- classes of elements of G G . We determine all strong Gelfand pairs that give an Almost Commutative Terwilliger algebra.



Keywords
:
Terwilliger algebra, Camina group, wreath product of association scheme, Schur ring, Frobenius group, strong Gelfand pair, centralizer algebra

MSC 2020 Classification
: 05E30, 05E16

Introduction

Terwilliger algebras were originally developed in the 1990’s by Paul Terwilliger to study commutative association schemes. In particular, he looked at P-Polynomial and Q-Polynomial association schemes. In
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
, where Terwilliger algebras were introduced, Terwilliger finds a combinatorial characterization of thin P-Polynomial and Q-Polynomial association schemes.

Tanaka studied a situation in which the Terwilliger algebra has a particular Wedderburn structure (see Theorem
2.1
) called almost commutative. One of Tanaka’s results is that when a Terwilliger algebra is almost commutative, the corresponding association scheme is a wreath product. In
[
5
]
, we determined when a group association scheme results in an almost commutative Terwilliger algebra (see Theorem
2.15
). This corresponds to the strong Gelfand pair
(G,G)
. A natural generalization of this is to consider any strong Gelfand pair
(G,H)
, since then the
H-
classes
g^{H}
determine a commutative association scheme.

In this paper all groups
G
are finite. We let
\mathcal{G}(G)=Z(\mathbb{C}[G])=\mathbb{C}[G]^{G}
denote the group association scheme and we let
T(G)
denote the Terwilliger algebra for
\mathcal{G}(G)
with base point
e
. We let
\mathcal{K}_{n}
denote the trivial association scheme for a set of size
n
. Given a strong Gelfand pair
(G,H)
, we let
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
denote the Terwilliger algebra resulting from the
H-
class Schur ring
\mathbb{C}[G]^{H}
. The main result of this paper, which uses results of
[
5
]
, is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
H\lneq G
. Then
(G,H)
is a strong Gelfand pair and
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is almost commutative if and only if

1.

G
is an abelian group and
H
is any proper subgroup of
G
. In this case,
\dim T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})=|G|^{2}
.

2.

G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
H
and cyclic complement
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
such that
H
is either an abelian group or a Camina
p-
group. In this case, the corresponding association scheme is
\mathcal{G}(H)\wr\mathcal{G}(G/H)
. Additionally, if
H
has
m
conjugacy classes then

\dim T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})=\dim T(H)+(k-1)(k-2+3m).

The situation where
(G,H)
is a strong Gelfand pair and
H
is abelian has been considered in
[
3
]
, where it is referred to as an extra strong Gelfand pair.

This paper is structured as follows. Section
2
will discuss some preliminary definitions and results. In Section
3
we will determine a classification of Almost Commutative Terwilliger algebras for strong Gelfand pairs (Theorem
3.11
). Then we determine the dimension of the corresponding Terwilliger algebra (Proposition
3.15
). In Section
4
we determine how to write an association scheme coming from a strong Gelfand pair as a wreath product (Corollary
4.5
). One can then find the Wedderburn components of each of the Terwilliger algebras using results from
[
18
]
.

Some notation that will be used throughout includes: given elements
g,h\in G
, we let
g^{h}=h^{-1}gh
and
[g,h]=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh
. Furthermore for
H\leq G
we let
g^{H}=\{h^{-1}gh\colon h\in H\}
. Also for
H,K\leq G
,
[K,H]=\langle[k,h]\colon k\in K,h\in H\rangle
.

AC Terwilliger algebras for SGPs

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(G,H)
be a SGP. Suppose for all
x,y\in G
if
x^{H}\neq(y^{-1})^{H}
then
x^{H}\cdot y^{H}=(xy)^{H}
. Then
x^{H}C_{G}(H)=x^{H}
for all
x\in G\setminus C_{G}(H)
. Also,
C_{G}(H)\leq[K,H]
whenever
K\subseteq G
is not a subset of
C_{G}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in G\setminus C_{G}(H)
and
z\in C_{G}(H)
. Then
x^{H}\neq(z^{-1})^{H}
, so
z^{H}x^{H}=(zx)^{H}
. Suppose that

x^{H}\neq x^{H}z=x^{H}z^{H}=z^{H}x^{H}=(zx)^{H}=((zx)^{-1})^{-1})^{H}.

Then the hypothesis gives
x^{H}((zx)^{-1})^{H}=(xx^{-1}z^{-1})^{H}=(z^{-1})^{H}=\{z^{-1}\}
.
But
|x^{H}|>1
and so this is a contradiction. Thus,
x^{H}=x^{H}z
for
z\in C_{H}(G)
, so
x^{H}=x^{H}C_{G}(H)
.

Now let
K\subseteq G
such that
K\not\subseteq C_{G}(H)
. Fix
x\in K\setminus C_{G}(H)
. Then

C_{G}(H)=[x,e]C_{G}(H)\subseteq[x,H]C_{G}(H)=x^{-1}x^{H}C_{G}(H)=x^{-1}x^{H}=[x,H]\subseteq[K,H].\qed

We now give necessary conditions for when
(G,H)
is a SGP and
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
G
is nonabelian, and
(G,H)
is a SGP. If
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC then:

(1)

T(H)
is AC;

(2)

H\trianglelefteq G
;

(3)

C_{G}(H)\leq H
;

(4)

G/H
is abelian.

Proof.

(1)
: Assume
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC. If
T(H)
is not AC, then by Theorem
2.2
, there are
x,y\in H
such that
x^{H}\neq(y^{-1})^{H}
and
x^{H}\cdot y^{H}\neq(xy)^{H}
. Then by Theorem
2.2
,
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is not AC. Thus,
T(H)
is AC.

(2)
: We show that
gH=Hg
for all
g\in G\setminus H
. We have two cases.

Case 1:

gH\subseteq g^{H}
. Then
|gH|\leq|g^{H}|\leq|H|=|gH|
, so
|gH|=|g^{H}|
and
gH=g^{H}
. Now let
h\in H
. As
gh\in gH=g^{H}
, we have
(hg)^{H}=(gh)^{H}=g^{H}=gH
. So
hg\in gH
. Thus
Hg\subseteq gH
and
Hg=gH
.

Case 2:
gH\not\subseteq g^{H}
. Then there exists
h\in H
such that
gh\not\in g^{H}
, and so
(gh)^{H}\neq g^{H}
. By Theorem
2.2
,

(ghg^{-1})^{H}=(gh)^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}=(g^{-1})^{H}\cdot(gh)^{H}=h^{H}.

Since
(gh)^{H}\neq g^{H}
and
h^{H}\neq(g^{-1})^{H}
by Theorem
2.2
,

h^{H}=(ghg^{-1})^{H}=(gh)^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}=g^{H}\cdot h^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}=g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}\cdot h^{H}.

Then for
y\in g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}
we have
yh\in g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}\cdot h^{H}=h^{H}\subseteq H
. Thus,
y\in H
and
g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}\subseteq H
.

For all
s\in H
,
gsg^{-1}s^{-1}\in g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}\subseteq H
. Then
gsg^{-1}\in H
, and so
gHg^{-1}\subseteq H
. This implies
Hg=gH
, and so
H\trianglelefteq G
.

(3)
: First we show
G\not\subseteq C_{G}(H)
. Suppose
G\subseteq C_{G}(H)
. Then for
g\in G
,
g^{H}=\{g\}
. Thus for
x,y\in G
we have
xy=x^{H}\cdot y^{H}=y^{H}\cdot x^{H}=yx
, and so
G
is abelian, a contradiction. Hence,
G\not\subseteq C_{G}(H)
. By Lemma
3.1
,
C_{G}(H)\leq[G,H]
. As
H\trianglelefteq G
, we have
[G,H]\leq H
, so
C_{G}(H)\leq H
.

(4)
: Fix
g\in G
. Let
y=hgh^{-1}\in g^{H}
for
h\in H
. Then
y=hgh^{-1}\in HgH=gHH=gH
, so
g^{H}\subseteq gH
for
g\in G
. Now let
xH,yH\in G/H
. We show
xH
and
yH
commute. There are two cases:

Case 1:

x^{H}=(y^{-1})^{H}
. Then
y^{-1}\in x^{H}\subseteq xH
and
xH=y^{-1}H
. Thus
(xH)(yH)=(y^{-1}H)(yH)=H=(yH)(y^{-1}H)=(yH)(xH)
.

Case 2:

x^{H}\neq(y^{-1})^{H}
. By Theorem
2.2
,
(xy)^{H}=x^{H}y^{H}=y^{H}x^{H}=(yx)^{H}
.
Hence,
yx\in(xy)^{H}\subseteq xyH
. However,
yx\in yxH
and so
yxH=xyH
. Thus,
(xH)(yH)=xyH=yxH=(yH)(xH)
, and so
G/H
is abelian.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
G
is nonabelian and
(G,H)
is a SGP. If
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC and
H
is abelian then for
x\in G\setminus H
, we have
x^{H}=xH
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,
C_{G}(H)\leq H
. Since
H
is abelian
H\subseteq C_{G}(H)
and we have
C_{G}(H)=H
.

Let
g\in G\setminus H
. By Lemma
3.1
,
H=C_{G}(H)\leq[g,H]
. Fix
h\in H
. Then,
h=g^{-1}y^{-1}gy
for some
y\in H
, and so
gh=y^{-1}gy
. Thus,
gH\subseteq g^{H}
. Then as
|g^{H}|\leq|H|=|gH|
we have
g^{H}=gH
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
G
is nonabelian and
(G,H)
is a SGP. Assume
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC. Then
(G/H^{\prime},H/H^{\prime})
is a SGP and
T\left(G/H^{\prime},\mathbb{C}[G/H^{\prime}]^{H/H^{\prime}}\right)
is AC.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,
H\trianglelefteq G
. As
H^{\prime}
is a characteristic subgroup of
H
,
H^{\prime}\trianglelefteq G
. Then by Lemma
2.3
,
(G/H^{\prime},H/H^{\prime})
is a SGP, so
\mathbb{C}[G/H^{\prime}]^{H/H^{\prime}}
is a commutative Schur ring.

Now let
\overline{G}=G/H^{\prime}
and
\overline{H}=H/H^{\prime}
. For
g\in G
we let
\overline{g}=gH^{\prime}\in\overline{G}
. Let
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\overline{G}
such that
\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\neq(\overline{y^{-1}})^{\overline{H}}
. If
x^{H}=(y^{-1})^{H}
, there is a
h\in H
such that
x=hy^{-1}h^{-1}
. Then

\overline{x}=xH^{\prime}=hy^{-1}h^{-1}H^{\prime}=hH^{\prime}h^{-1}H^{\prime}y^{-1}h^{-1}H^{\prime}=\overline{h}\cdot\overline{y^{-1}}\overline{h^{-1}}\in(\overline{y^{-1}})^{\overline{H}}.

This implies
\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}=(\overline{y^{-1}})^{\overline{H}}
, which is false, so
x^{H}\neq(y^{-1})^{H}
. Then by Theorem
2.2
,
x^{H}\cdot y^{H}=(xy)^{H}
.

We claim
\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\cdot\overline{y}^{\overline{H}}=(\overline{xy})^{\overline{H}}
. Notice that
\overline{xy}\in\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\overline{y}^{\overline{H}}
, so
(\overline{xy})^{\overline{H}}\subseteq\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\cdot\overline{y}^{\overline{H}}
. Now let
\overline{z_{1}}\in\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}
,
\overline{z_{2}}\in\overline{y}^{\overline{H}}
. Then
\overline{z_{1}}=\overline{h_{1}}\overline{x}\overline{h_{1}^{-1}}
and
\overline{z_{2}}=\overline{h_{2}}\overline{y}\overline{h_{2}^{-1}}
for
\overline{h_{1}},\overline{h_{2}}\in\overline{H}
. Notice that
h_{1}xh_{1}^{-1}\in x^{H}
and
h_{2}yh_{2}^{-1}\in y^{H}
. Then
(h_{1}xh_{1}^{-1})(h_{2}yh_{2}^{-1})\in x^{H}y^{H}=(xy)^{H}
. Therefore there is a
h_{3}\in H
such that
(h_{1}xh_{1}^{-1})(h_{2}yh_{2}^{-1})=h_{3}xyh_{3}^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{z_{1}}\overline{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\overline{h_{1}}\overline{x}\overline{h_{1}^{-1}}\right)\left(\overline{h_{2}}\overline{y}\overline{h_{2}^{-1}}\right)=\left(h_{1}xh_{1}^{-1}H^{\prime}\right)\left(h_{2}yh_{2}^{-1}H^{\prime}\right)=\left(h_{1}xh_{1}^{-1}h_{2}yh_{2}^{-1}\right)H^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(h_{3}xyh_{3}^{-1}\right)H^{\prime}=h_{3}H^{\prime}(xy)H^{\prime}h_{3}^{-1}H^{\prime}=\overline{h_{3}}\overline{xy}\overline{h_{3}^{-1}}\in(\overline{xy})^{H}.

So
\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\cdot\overline{y}^{\overline{H}}\subseteq(\overline{xy})^{\overline{H}}
, and thus
\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\cdot\overline{y}^{\overline{H}}=(\overline{xy})^{\overline{H}}
. Therefore, for all
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\overline{G}
with
\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\neq(\overline{y^{-1}})^{\overline{H}}
, we have
\overline{x}^{\overline{H}}\cdot\overline{y}^{\overline{H}}=(\overline{xy})^{\overline{H}}
. Then by Theorem
2.2
,
T\left(G/H^{\prime},\mathbb{C}[G/H^{\prime}]^{H/H^{\prime}}\right)
is AC.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be nonabelian with
H\leq G
and
(G,H)
be a SGP. If
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC then for
x\in G\setminus H
, we have
x^{H}=xH
.

Proof.

Fix
g\in G\setminus H
. By Theorem
3.2
,
H\trianglelefteq G
which implies
g^{H}\subseteq HgH=gH
. Now suppose
h\in H
such that
gh\not\in g^{H}
. Then
(gh)^{H}\neq g^{H}
. By Theorem
2.2
,

(ghg^{-1})^{H}=(gh)^{H}(g^{-1})^{H}=(g^{-1})^{H}(gh)^{H}=h^{H}.

However we note, since
g\in G\setminus H
, that
g^{H}\neq(h^{-1})^{H}
, so by Theorem
2.2
, we also have

h^{H}=(ghg^{-1})^{H}=(gh)^{H}(g^{-1})^{H}=g^{H}h^{H}(g^{-1})^{H}=g^{H}(g^{-1})^{H}h^{H}.

Let
x\in g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}
. As
\mathbb{C}[G]^{H}
is a Schur ring we can write
\overline{g^{H}}\cdot\overline{(g^{-1})^{H}}=\sum\lambda_{k}\overline{y_{k}^{H}}
for some
y_{k}\in G
and nonzero integers
\lambda_{k}
. Then

\overline{g^{H}}\cdot\overline{(g^{-1})^{H}}\cdot\overline{h^{H}}=\sum\lambda_{k}\overline{y_{k}^{H}}\cdot\overline{h^{H}}.

However, as
h^{H}=g^{H}(g^{-1})^{H}h^{H}
, we know that
\overline{g^{H}}\cdot\overline{(g^{-1})^{H}}\cdot\overline{h^{H}}=\mu\overline{h^{H}}
for some nonzero integer
\mu
. We thus have,
\mu\overline{h^{H}}=\sum\lambda_{k}\overline{y_{k}^{H}}\cdot\overline{h^{H}}
. This implies
h^{H}=(y_{k}^{H})h^{H}
for all
y_{k}^{H}
with a nonzero coefficient
\lambda_{k}
in
\sum\lambda_{k}\overline{y_{k}^{H}}
. We know
x\in g^{H}(g^{-1})^{H}
, so
x^{H}=y_{i}^{H}
for some
i
. Therefore,
h^{H}=x^{H}\cdot h^{H}
. Then
xh=tht^{-1}
for some
t\in H
. This implies
x=tht^{-1}h^{-1}\in H
. So
x\in H
. We claim that
x\in H^{\prime}
. Suppose not. By Theorem
3.2
,
T(H)
is AC, so by Theorem
2.15
,
H
is a Con-cos group. Therefore, for all
a\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
,
a^{H}=aH^{\prime}
. In particular,
x^{H}=xH^{\prime}
. We consider two cases.

Case 1:
h\in H^{\prime}
. Then
h^{H}\subseteq H^{\prime}
, and
x^{H}\cdot h^{H}=xH^{\prime}\cdot h^{H}=xH^{\prime}
. However,
xH^{\prime}\neq h^{H}
contradicting
x^{H}\cdot h^{H}=h^{H}
.

Case 2:
h\not\in H^{\prime}
. Then
h^{H}=hH^{\prime}
, and
x^{H}\cdot h^{H}=xH^{\prime}\cdot hH^{\prime}=xhH^{\prime}
. Notice
xhH^{\prime}\neq hH^{\prime}
since
x\not\in H^{\prime}
. Therefore,
x^{H}\cdot h^{H}\neq h^{H}
in this case, contradicting
x^{H}\cdot h^{H}=h^{H}
.

In both cases we get a contradiction, so
x\in H^{\prime}
. As we chose
x\in g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}
arbitrarily, we have
g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}\subseteq H^{\prime}
. For all
y\in g^{H}
, we have
yg^{-1}\in g^{H}\cdot(g^{-1})^{H}\subseteq H^{\prime}
. Then
y\in H^{\prime}g
. Since
H\trianglelefteq G
we have
H^{\prime}\trianglelefteq G
. So
H^{\prime}g=gH^{\prime}
. Thus
y\in gH^{\prime}
. As we chose
y\in g^{H}
arbitrarily
g^{H}\subseteq gH^{\prime}
, so
g^{H}H^{\prime}\subseteq gH^{\prime}
.

By Proposition
3.4
,
(G/H^{\prime},H/H^{\prime})
is a SGP with
T(G/H^{\prime},\mathbb{C}[G/H^{\prime}]^{H/H^{\prime}})
AC. Set
G/H^{\prime}=\overline{G}
,
H/H^{\prime}=\overline{H}
, and
\overline{x}=xH^{\prime}
for all
x\in G/H^{\prime}
. Notice that
\overline{H}
is abelian so by Theorem
3.3
, for all
\overline{a}\in\overline{G}\setminus\overline{H}
we have
\overline{a}\overline{H}=\overline{a}^{\overline{H}}
. Then as
g\in G\setminus H
,
\overline{g}\in\overline{G}\setminus\overline{H}
and
\overline{g}\overline{H}=\overline{g}^{\overline{H}}
. We observe that
\overline{gh}\in\overline{g}\overline{H}=\overline{g}^{\overline{H}}
. Then there exists some
\overline{s}\in\overline{H}
such that
\overline{gh}=\overline{s}\overline{g}\overline{s^{-1}}
, and
ghH^{\prime}=sgs^{-1}H^{\prime}
. So

gh\in(sgs^{-1})H^{\prime}\subseteq(sgs^{-1})^{H}H^{\prime}=g^{H}H^{\prime},

since
s\in H
. Therefore,
gh\in g^{H}H^{\prime}\subseteq gH^{\prime}
by the above argument. So
h\in H^{\prime}
.

Therefore, for all
h\in H
, if
gh\not\in g^{H}
, then
h\in H^{\prime}
. Suppose
x\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
. If
gx\not\in g^{H}
then we have
x\in H^{\prime}
, which is false. Hence, for
x\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
, we have
gx\in g^{H}
.

Recall that
H
is a Con-cos group. Then
H
is solvable by Corollary
2.14
, so there exists some
z\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
and
gz\in g^{H}
. Then as
g\in G\setminus H
, and
z\in H
we have by Theorem
2.2
that
g^{H}=(gz)^{H}=g^{H}z^{H}
. Since
z\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
and
H
is a Con-cos group
z^{H}=zH^{\prime}
. Therefore,
g^{H}=g^{H}zH^{\prime}=g^{H}H^{\prime}z
. Then for all
h\in H^{\prime}
,
ghz\in g^{H}
. So there exists some
y_{h}\in H
such that
ghz=y_{h}gy_{h}^{-1}
. Then
gh=y_{h}gy_{h}^{-1}z^{-1}\in g^{H}\cdot(z^{-1})^{H}
. As
z^{-1}\in H
and
g\in G\setminus H
, we have by Theorem
2.2
that
g^{H}\cdot(z^{-1})^{H}=(gz^{-1})^{H}
. So
gh\in(gz^{-1})^{H}
. However,
z^{-1}\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
as
z\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
. Then
gz^{-1}\in g^{H}
by the above paragraph. So
(gz^{-1})^{H}=g^{H}
. Thus
gh\in g^{H}
, for all
h\in H^{\prime}
, we have
gh\in g^{H}
.

Now let
ga\in gH
for any
a\in H
. If
a\in H^{\prime}
, then we just found
ga\in g^{H}
. If
a\in H\setminus H^{\prime}
we found above that
ga\in g^{H}
. Therefore,
ga\in g^{H}
for all
a\in H
, so
gH\subseteq g^{H}
. Hence,
g^{H}=gH
.
∎

Thus if
(G,H)
is a SGP and
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC, then
H\trianglelefteq G
,
T(H)
is AC,
G/H
is abelian, and
x^{H}=xH
for all
x\in G\setminus H
. We shall now prove that if these four conditions are met, then
(G,H)
is a SGP and
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a group. Suppose
H\trianglelefteq G
and
G/H
is abelian. If for all
x\in G\setminus H
we have
x^{H}=xH
, then
(G,H)
is a SGP.

Proof.

The classes
\{g^{H}\}
, determine an
S-
ring. We only need to check commutativity.

Let
x,y\in H
and let
x^{H}=P_{x}
,
y^{H}=P_{y}
. Then as
\mathcal{G}(H)
is commutative we have
\overline{P_{x}}\cdot\overline{P_{y}}=\overline{P_{y}}\cdot\overline{P_{x}}
.

Next suppose
x,y\in G\setminus H
. Then
x^{H}=xH
and
y^{H}=yH
. As
G/H
is abelian, we have

\overline{x^{H}}\cdot\overline{y^{H}}=\overline{xH}\cdot\overline{yH}=|H|\overline{xyH}=|H|\overline{yxH}=\overline{yH}\cdot\overline{xH}=\overline{y^{H}}\cdot\overline{x^{H}}.

Suppose now that
x\in H
and
y\in G\setminus H
. Let
x^{H}=P_{x}\subseteq H
and
y^{H}=yH=P_{y}
. Then

\overline{P_{x}}\cdot y\overline{H}=\overline{P_{x}}\cdot\overline{H}y=|P_{x}|\overline{H}y=|P_{x}|y\overline{H}=|P_{x}|\overline{P_{y}}.

By similar reasoning,
\overline{yH}\cdot\overline{P_{x}}=|P_{x}|\overline{P_{y}}
.
Hence,
(G,H)
is a SGP.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
G
be a group. Suppose
H\trianglelefteq G
,
T(H)
is AC, and
G/H
is abelian. If for all
x\in G\setminus H
we have
x^{H}=xH
, then
(G,H)
is a SGP and
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
,
(G,H)
is a SGP, so
\mathbb{C}[G]^{H}
is commutative. Let the principal classes be
P_{0},P_{1},\cdots,P_{d}
.

Now let
x,y\in G
with
x\in P_{i}
,
y\in P_{j}
and
xy\in P_{k}
such that
P_{i}\neq P_{j}^{*}
. If
x,y\in H
then
P_{i}=x^{H}
and
P_{j}=y^{H}
. Since
T(H)
is AC and
x^{H}=P_{i}\neq P_{j}^{*}=(y^{-1})^{H}
we have by Theorem
2.2
that
P_{i}P_{j}=x^{H}y^{H}=(xy)^{H}=P_{k}
.

If
x,y\in G\setminus H
, then
P_{i}=xH
,
P_{j}=yH
, and
P_{i}P_{j}=xyH
. As
P_{i}\neq P_{j}^{*}
, we have
yH\neq x^{-1}H
, so
xy\not\in H
. Thus,
xyH=(xy)^{H}=P_{k}
and
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{k}
.

If
x\in H
and
y\in G\setminus H
, then
P_{i}=x^{H}
and
P_{j}=yH
. So we have
P_{i}P_{j}=x^{H}\cdot yH=yH
since
x^{H}\subseteq H
. Notice that
xy\in Hy=yH
. Also
xy\not\in H
as
y\not\in H
. So
P_{k}=(xy)^{H}=xyH=yH=P_{i}P_{j}
.

From these three cases we see that
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{k}
. So by Theorem
2.2
,
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC.
∎

At this point we consider which pairs
(G,H)
satisfy our conditions that
T(H)
is AC,
G/H
is abelian, and
x^{H}=xH
for all
x\in G\setminus H
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
G
be nonabelian. Suppose
H\triangleleft G
is a proper subgroup, that
G/H
is abelian and for
x\in G\setminus H
we have
x^{H}=xH
; then
G
is a Frobenius group with cyclic complement,
H=G^{\prime}
, and
H
is the Frobenius kernel of
G
.

Proof.

As
G/H
is abelian, if
xH
and
yH
are conjugate in
G/H
, then
xH=yH
. If additionally,
x,y\not\in H
then
xH=x^{H}
and
yH=y^{H}
, so
x^{H}=y^{H}
. Then by Lemma
2.10
,
(G,H)
is a Camina pair and by Lemma
2.11
,
Z(G)\leq H\leq G^{\prime}
. However, as
G/H
is abelian
G^{\prime}\leq H
. Therefore,
H=G^{\prime}
and
G
is a Camina group.

First assume that
G
is a Camina
p-
group. Then by Theorem
2.6
, the nilpotency class of
G
is either
2
or
3
. This gives us two cases.

Case 1:
Suppose the nilpotency class of
G
is
2
. Then
Z(G)=G^{\prime}=H
. Let
g\in G\setminus H
, so
g^{H}=\{g\}
. However,
\{g\}\neq gH
. This contradicts our assumption that for
x\in G\setminus H
we have
x^{H}=xH
.

Case 2:
Suppose the nilpotency class of
G
is
3
. Then
Z(G)\lneq G^{\prime}
. Fix
g\in G\setminus H
,
h\in H=G^{\prime}
. If
h\in Z(G)
, then
hgh^{-1}=g\in gZ(G)
. If
h\in H\setminus Z(G)
then by Proposition
2.7
,
h^{G}=hZ(G)
. Therefore,
ghg^{-1}=hz
for some
z\in Z(G)
. So
h^{-1}gh=zg=gz
and
h^{-1}gh\in gZ(G)
. So for all
h\in H
,
h^{-1}gh\in gZ(G)
. Therefore,
g^{H}\subseteq gZ(G)
. However,
gZ(G)\subsetneq gH
. Thus,
g^{H}\neq gH
, a contradiction

Hence,
G
cannot be a Camina
p-
group. By Theorem
2.6
,
G
is either
(i)
a Frobenius group with cyclic complement or
(ii)
a Frobenius group with complement
Q_{8}
. Suppose we have
(ii)
with complement
K\cong Q_{8}
. As
Q_{8}
is solvable, so is
G
, and
G\neq G^{\prime}
. By Proposition
2.8
, either
N\leq G^{\prime}
or
G^{\prime}\leq N
. If
G^{\prime}\leq N
, then
G/N
is abelian. However,
G/N\cong Q_{8}
is nonabelian, so
N<G^{\prime}
.

Fix
x\in G\setminus G^{\prime}
. By assumption
x^{G^{\prime}}=xG^{\prime}
. As
x\not\in N
, by Proposition
2.4
, there exists some
h\in K
with
x=ghg^{-1}
. Now as
G=NK
,
g=nk
for some
n\in N
,
k\in K
. So
x=nkhk^{-1}n^{-1}
. Note that
h_{1}=khk^{-1}\in K
. Then
x=nh_{1}n^{-1}
, and
x^{G^{\prime}}=h_{1}^{G^{\prime}}
as
n\in N\subseteq G^{\prime}
.

Now let
y\in h_{1}^{G^{\prime}}
. Then there is
s\in G^{\prime}
such that
y=sh_{1}s^{-1}
. We know
N<G^{\prime}\trianglelefteq G
and
N\trianglelefteq G
. Then
G^{\prime}/N\trianglelefteq G/N\cong Q_{8}
and
|G\colon G^{\prime}|=|G/N\colon G^{\prime}/N|
. Since
G^{\prime}
is a proper subgroup of
G
, we have
|G\colon G^{\prime}|>1
. Then
|G/N\colon G^{\prime}/N|>1
. Hence,
G^{\prime}/N
is a proper subgroup of
G/N
. As every proper subgroup of
Q_{8}
is abelian, we know
G^{\prime}/N
is abelian. Then
(sN)(h_{1}N)(sN)^{-1}=sh_{1}s^{-1}N=h_{1}N
. Thus,
y=sh_{1}s^{-1}\in h_{1}N
. So
h_{1}^{G^{\prime}}\subseteq h_{1}N
. Thus,
x^{G^{\prime}}\subseteq h_{1}N
as
x^{G^{\prime}}=h_{1}^{G^{\prime}}
. However, by assumption,
x^{G^{\prime}}=xG^{\prime}
, so
xG^{\prime}\subseteq h_{1}N
and
|xG^{\prime}|\leq|h_{1}N|
. However,
|xG^{\prime}|=|G^{\prime}|
,
|h_{1}N|=|N|
, and
|N|<|G^{\prime}|
since
N
is a proper subgroup of
G^{\prime}
. So
|xG^{\prime}|>|h_{1}N|
which contradicts
|xG^{\prime}|\leq|h_{1}N|
.

Hence,
G
is a Frobenius group with cyclic complement
K
and Frobenius kernel
N
. By Proposition
2.4
, for all
n\in N
there is a
g\in G
such that
ghg^{-1}=nh
for some
h\in K
. This implies
n=[g^{-1},h^{-1}]\in G^{\prime}
, so
N\subseteq G^{\prime}
. However,
G/N\cong K
and
K
is abelian. Therefore,
G^{\prime}\subseteq N
. Hence,
G^{\prime}=N=H
is the Frobenius kernel of
G
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with abelian complement
H
and Frobenius kernel
N
. Then
N=G^{\prime}
and for all
x\in G\setminus N
we have
x^{N}=xN
.

Proof.

As
G/N\cong H
is abelian, we have
G^{\prime}\leq N
. Let
n\in N
. By Proposition
2.4
, there is
g\in G
such that
ghg^{-1}=nh
,
h\in H
. Thus
n=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}=[g^{-1},h^{-1}]\in G^{\prime}
, and so
N=G^{\prime}
.

Now let
x\in G\setminus N
. Then by Proposition
2.4
, there is
h\in H
such that
x
is conjugate to
h
. Suppose
g\in G
such that
x=ghg^{-1}
. As
G=NH
,
g=nk
for some
n\in N
and
k\in H
. So
x=ghg^{-1}=nkhk^{-1}n^{-1}
. However, as
H
is abelian,
khk^{-1}=h
. Thus,
x=nhn^{-1}
. So
x\in h^{N}
. As the classes of conjugation by elements of
N
partition
G
, this implies
x^{N}=h^{N}
. As
h\in H\setminus\{e\}
we have by Proposition
2.4
, that
h
is conjugate to every element of
hN
. Let
y\in hN
. Then there exists
r\in G
such that
y=rhr^{-1}
. As
G=NH
,
r=ms
for some
m\in N
and
s\in H
. So
y=rhr^{-1}=mshs^{-1}m^{-1}
. However, as
H
is abelian,
shs^{-1}=h
. Thus,
y=mhm^{-1}\in h^{N}
and
hN\subseteq h^{N}
. Given
z\in h^{N}
, we have
z=tht^{-1}
for some
t\in N
. Then
z\in NhN=hN
. Thus,
z\in hN
and
h^{N}\subseteq hN
. Hence,
h^{N}=hN
. Recall that
x^{N}=h^{N}
, so
x^{N}=hN
. As
x\in hN
,
hN=xN
. Therefore,
x^{N}=xN
.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with abelian complement and Frobenius kernel
N
. Then
(G,N)
is a SGP.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.9
,
N=G^{\prime}
and for
x\in G\setminus N
,
x^{N}=xN
. Then by Proposition
3.6
,
(G,N)
is a SGP.
∎

We can now classify all SGPs that result in an AC Terwilliger algebra:

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
H\lneq G
. Then
(G,H)
is a SGP and
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC if and only if

1.

G
is abelian with
H
being any subgroup of
G
, or

2.

G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
H=G^{\prime}
and cyclic complement such that
H
is either abelian or a Camina
p-
group.

Proof.

First suppose
(G,H)
is a SGP,
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC. If
G
is abelian, then we are done. Suppose
G
is nonabelian. Then by Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
,
T(H)
is AC, for all
x\in G\setminus H
we have
x^{H}=xH
, and
G/H
is abelian. As
G
is nonabelian, Proposition
3.8
gives that
G
is a Frobenius group with cyclic complement and
H=G^{\prime}
is the Frobenius kernel. Since
T(H)
is AC, by Theorem
2.15

H
is either abelian, a Camina
p-
group, a Frobenius group of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}
, or
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}\rtimes Q_{8}
. However, by Theorem
2.5
,
H
must be a nilpotent group since it is the Frobenius kernel. Since Frobenius groups are not nilpotent either
H
is abelian or a Camina
p-
group.

Therefore,
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
H=G^{\prime}
and cyclic complement such that
H
is either abelian or a Camina
p-
group. This proves the forward direction.

Now we prove the reverse direction. If
G
is abelian and
H\leq G
, then
\mathbb{C}[G]^{H}=\mathbb{C}[G]
, which is commutative. Thus,
(G,H)
is a SGP and by Theorem
2.15
,
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC.

If
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
H
and cyclic complement such that
H
is abelian or a Camina
p-
group, then by Theorem
2.15
,
T(H)
is AC. By Proposition
3.9
,
H=G^{\prime}
is the Frobenius kernel of
G
and for all
x\in G\setminus H
we have
x^{H}=xH
. Then by Corollary
3.7
,
(G,H)
is a SGP and
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC.
∎

We now give two examples of infinite families of Frobenius groups satisfying Theorem
3.11
.

Example 3.12
.

Let
H
be the extraspecial group of exponent
p>2
and order
p^{3}
:

H=\langle a,b,c\colon a^{p}=b^{p}=c^{p}=1,c=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab,ac=ca,bc=cb\rangle.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
k>2
, where
k\mid(p-1)
is prime and
k^{2}\not\equiv 1\mod p
. Then
\varphi\colon H\rightarrow H
given by
\varphi(a)=a^{k},\ \varphi(b)=b^{k},\ \varphi(c)=c^{k^{2}}
,
determines an automorphism that only fixes
1
. Therefore, if
d
is the order of
k
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, then
G=H\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}_{d}
is a Frobenius group with extraspecial kernel and cyclic complement, so that
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC.
\square

Example 3.13
.

Let
p>2
be prime. Let

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
H
and cyclic complement of order
k
. Assume that
T(H)
is AC and
H
has
m
conjugacy classes. Then

\dim T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})=\dim T(H)+(k-1)(k-2+3m).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.11
,
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is AC and
H=G^{\prime}
. By Theorem
2.1
,
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
is triply regular, so
T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})=T_{0}(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})
and
\dim T_{0}(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})=|\{(i,j,k)\colon p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0\}|
. We count the number of nonzero
p_{ij}^{k}
. Let
P_{0}=g_{0}^{H},\cdots,P_{d}=g_{d}^{H}
be the principal classes of
\mathbb{C}[G]^{H}
. The principal sets of
\mathbb{C}[H]^{H}
are the principal sets of
\mathbb{C}[G]^{H}
contained in
H
. We consider cases.

Case 1:
P_{i},P_{j}\subseteq H
. Then
P_{i}=h_{i}^{H}
and
P_{j}=h_{j}^{H}
for some
h_{i},h_{j}\in H
. Since
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
are both principal sets of
\mathbb{C}[H]^{H}
,
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{C}[H]^{H}
if and only if
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{C}[H]
. Therefore the number of triples
(i,j,k)
such that
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
is equal to
\dim T_{0}(H)
. However,
T(H)
is AC, so by Theorem
2.1
,
T(H)=T_{0}(H)
. Therefore, we have
\dim T(H)
nonzero triples
(i,j,k)
in this case.

Case 2:
P_{i}\not\subseteq H
and
P_{j}\subseteq H
. Since
P_{i}\not\subseteq H
,
g_{i}\not\in H
, so by Proposition
3.9
,
g_{i}^{H}=g_{i}H
. Therefore,
P_{i}P_{j}=g_{i}HP_{j}=g_{i}H
as
P_{j}\subseteq H
. Therefore,
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
if and only if
k=i
in this case. Thus, for each choice of
P_{j}\subseteq H
we have one choice of
k
. There are
m
principal classes
P_{j}\subseteq H
. Therefore, for each choice of
i
there are
m
different
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
. As
P_{i}=g_{i}^{H}=g_{i}H
for all
P_{i}\not\subseteq H
, the number of distinct choices for
P_{i}
is
k-1
, since
|G/H|=k
. Therefore we have
(k-1)m
different
p_{ij}^{k}
in this case.

Case 3:

P_{i}\subseteq H
and
P_{j}\not\subseteq H
. As in Case
2
, we get
(k-1)m
nonzero triples.

Case 4:

P_{i},P_{j}\not\subseteq H
. Since
P_{i},P_{j}\not\subseteq H
, we have
g_{i},g_{j}\not\in H
. Then by Proposition
3.9
,
g_{i}^{H}=g_{i}H
and
g_{j}^{H}=g_{j}H
. Fix
P_{i}
and consider two subcases.

Subcase 4.1:
P_{j}=P_{i}^{*}
. Then
P_{j}^{*}=(g_{i}^{-1})H
and so
P_{i}P_{j}=g_{i}H(g_{i}^{-1}H)=H
. Then
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
for every
P_{k}\subseteq H
. This is the number of conjugacy classes of
H
. Hence, we have
m
triples
(i,j,k)
with
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
in this case.

Subcase 4.2:
P_{j}\neq P_{i}^{*}
. So
g_{j}H\neq g_{i}^{-1}H
. Then
P_{i}P_{j}=(g_{i}g_{j})H
. As
g_{j}H\neq g_{i}^{-1}H
,
g_{i}g_{j}\not\in H
. By Proposition
3.9
,
g_{i}g_{j}H=(g_{i}g_{j})^{H}=P_{k}
for some
P_{k}\not\subseteq H
. There is a unique choice of
k
such that
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
in this case. Then for each choice of
P_{j}\neq P_{i}^{*}
, we have one triple
(i,j,k)
such that
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
. There are a total of
k-1
choices for
P_{j}
, one of which has
P_{j}=P_{i}^{*}
. Thus, we have
k-2
nonzero
(i,j,k)
in this case.

From these two subcases we see that for each choice of
P_{i}
there are
k-2+m
nonzero triples
(i,j,k)
. There are a total of
k-1
choices for
P_{i}
. We thus have
(k-1)(k-2+m)
different nonzero triples
(i,j,k)
in this case. Summing gives:

\dim T(G,\mathbb{C}[G]^{H})=\dim T(H)+(k-1)m+(k-1)m+(k-1)(k-2+m)=\dim T(H)+(k-1)(k-2+3m).\qed

We note that
\dim T(H)
in the previous theorem is computed in
[
5
]
for each possible
H
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with abelian complement. Let
P_{k}
be a principal class of

the

is all

Proof.

As
P_{k}\not\subseteq G^{\prime}
and

is a Frobenius group with cyclic complement,

for some

by Proposition
3.9
. Now suppose

and

if and only if

P_{j}\subseteq gxG^{\prime}

P_{i}\subseteq G^{\prime}
or
P_{i}=hG^{\prime}

’s.

x^{G^{\prime}},x^{G^{\prime}}
block of
A_{k}
as the
(h_{1},h_{2})
entry of the adjacency matrix for
gG^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})
. Therefore, the
x^{G^{\prime}},x^{G^{\prime}}
block of
A_{k}
is equal to the adjacency matrix of
g^{G^{\prime}}
for the association scheme
\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with abelian complement. Suppose
|G|=n
and
|G^{\prime}|=m
. If
P_{k}=g^{G^{\prime}}
is a principal class of
\mathbb{C}[G]^{G^{\prime}}
such that
g\in G^{\prime}
, then
A_{k}=I_{n/m}\otimes B
where
B
is the adjacency matrix of
g^{G^{\prime}}
for the association scheme
\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We consider the blocks of
A_{k}
as cosets of
G^{\prime}
using the ordering described above. By Proposition
3.9
,
P_{k}=g^{G^{\prime}}=gG^{\prime}
. The
xG^{\prime},yG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is the same as the
x^{G^{\prime}},y^{G^{\prime}}
block for any
x,y\not\in G^{\prime}
by Proposition
3.9
. So in this situation the blocks are the same as the principal class blocks. The only difference is that classes in
G^{\prime}
are now being considered as a single block. If
xG^{\prime}\neq yG^{\prime}
for any
x,y\in G
either
x\not\in G^{\prime}
or
y\not\in G^{\prime}
in this case. Then by Lemma
4.2
, the
xG^{\prime},yG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is all
0
. Hence, only the diagonal blocks are nonzero. Now consider the
xG^{\prime},xG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
. If
xG^{\prime}\neq G^{\prime}
, then this is the same as the
x^{G^{\prime}},x^{G^{\prime}}
block of
A_{k}
. By Lemma
4.2
, this block equals
B
. Next we consider the
G^{\prime},G^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
. By Lemma
4.2
, the
x^{G^{\prime}},y^{G^{\prime}}
block of
A_{k}
equals the
x^{G^{\prime}},y^{G^{\prime}}
block of
B
for all
x,y\in G^{\prime}
. Therefore, the
G^{\prime},G^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is just
B
.

Thus
A_{k}
is a block diagonal matrix in which each diagonal block is
B
. So,
A_{k}=I_{n/m}\otimes B
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with abelian complement. Suppose
|G|=n
and
|G^{\prime}|=m
. If
P_{k}
is a principal class of
\mathbb{C}[G]^{G^{\prime}}
such that
P_{k}\not\subseteq G^{\prime}
, then
A_{k}=D\otimes J_{m}
where
D
is the adjacency matrix of the conjugacy class
\{gG^{\prime}\}
in
\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
P_{k}=g^{G^{\prime}}
and notice as
g\not\in G^{\prime}
that
g^{G^{\prime}}=gG^{\prime}
by Proposition
3.9
. Let us consider the blocks of
A_{k}
as cosets of
G^{\prime}
using the ordering described above. In doing so, the
xG^{\prime},yG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is the same as the
x^{G^{\prime}},y^{G^{\prime}}
block for
x,y\not\in G^{\prime}
. So in this regard the blocks are the same as the principal class blocks. The only difference is that classes in
G^{\prime}
are now being considered as a single block. From Lemma
4.1
, the
P_{i},P_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s if
P_{j}\subset P_{i}P_{k}
and is all
0
’s otherwise.

For any
P_{i}\subset G^{\prime}
, the
P_{i},P_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is nonzero if and only if
P_{j}\subset P_{i}P_{k}=gG^{\prime}=P_{k}
if and only if
P_{j}=P_{k}
. Hence, the
P_{i},P_{j}
block is nonzero if and only if
P_{j}=gG^{\prime}
. This is true for all
P_{i}\subset G^{\prime}
. Then taking the union over all of the
P_{i}\subset G^{\prime}
, the
G^{\prime},gG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s and the
G^{\prime},yG^{\prime}
block for
yG^{\prime}\neq gG^{\prime}
is all
0
’s.

Next consider when
P_{j}\subset G^{\prime}
,
P_{j}=x^{G^{\prime}}
,
x\in G^{\prime}
. The
P_{i},P_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is nonzero if and only if
P_{j}\subset P_{i}P_{k}
if and only if
x\in P_{i}P_{k}
. Now if
P_{i}\subset G^{\prime}
we have
P_{i}P_{k}=gG^{\prime}=P_{k}
. Since
x\in G^{\prime}
, we have
x\not\in gG^{\prime}
. So for all
P_{j}\subset G^{\prime}
, the
P_{i},P_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is zero. When
P_{i}=yG^{\prime}
we have
x\in P_{i}P_{k}=(yG^{\prime})(gG^{\prime})=ygG^{\prime}
if and only if
ygG^{\prime}=G^{\prime}
, if and only if
yG^{\prime}=g^{-1}G^{\prime}
. Hence, in this case the
P_{i},P_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s if and only if
P_{i}=g^{-1}G^{\prime}
. This is true for all
P_{j}\subset G^{\prime}
. Then taking the union over all of the
P_{j}\subset G^{\prime}
, the
g^{-1}G^{\prime},G^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s and the
yG^{\prime},G^{\prime}
block for
yG^{\prime}\neq g^{-1}G^{\prime}
is all
0
’s.

We now consider the case where both
P_{i},P_{j}\not\subset G^{\prime}
. Say
P_{i}=x^{G^{\prime}}
and
P_{j}=y^{G^{\prime}}
. By Proposition
3.9
,
P_{i}=xG^{\prime}
and
P_{j}=yG^{\prime}
. From Lemma
4.1
, the
xG^{\prime},yG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s if
P_{j}\subset P_{i}P_{k}
and is all
0
’s otherwise. Then the block is all
1
’s if
yG^{\prime}\subset(xG^{\prime})(gG^{\prime})=xgG^{\prime}
if and only if
yG^{\prime}=xgG^{\prime}=gxG^{\prime}
, since
G/G^{\prime}
is abelian. Thus the
xG^{\prime},gxG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s for all
x\not\in G^{\prime}
and the
xG^{\prime},yG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is all
0
’s if
yG^{\prime}\neq gxG^{\prime}
.

From these three cases we see that the
xG^{\prime},gxG^{\prime}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s for all
x\in G
and is all
0
’s otherwise. Then
A_{k}=D\otimes J_{m}
where
D
is the
(n/m)\times(n/m)
matrix that is nonzero in the
(x,gx)
entry for all
x\in G
and is
0
otherwise. Notice in
\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
the adjacency matrix
A
for the class
\{gG^{\prime}\}
is nonzero in the
(xG^{\prime},yG^{\prime})
entry if and only if
yx^{-1}G^{\prime}=gG^{\prime}
if and only if
y=gxG^{\prime}
. Hence,
A_{xG^{\prime},yG^{\prime}}=1
if and only if
y=gxG^{\prime}
if and only if
D
is nonzero in the (x,gx) entry. Hence,
D
is the adjacency matrix for
\{gG^{\prime}\}
when considered as a conjugacy class in
G/G^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with abelian complement. Suppose
|G|=n
and
|G^{\prime}|=m
. Then
\mathcal{S}(G,G^{\prime})=\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})\wr\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
A_{0},A_{1},\cdots,A_{r}
be the adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})
and
B_{0},B_{1},\cdots,B_{n/m}
be the adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
. The adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})\wr\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
are just
D_{0}=I_{p^{n/m}}\otimes I_{m}=I_{n}
,
D_{k}=I_{{n/m}}\otimes A_{k}
where
1\leq k\leq r
, and
D_{r+k}=B_{k}\otimes J_{m}
for
1\leq k\leq n/m
. From Lemma
4.3
, all adjacency matrices
M_{k}
of
\mathcal{S}(G,G^{\prime})
for some class
P_{k}=g^{G^{\prime}}\subset G^{\prime}
are of the form
M_{k}=I_{p^{n/m}}\otimes A_{k}
where
A_{k}
is the adjacency matrix for
g^{G^{\prime}}
in
G^{\prime}
. Ranging over all
P_{k}\subset G^{\prime}
we have
D_{i}=M_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq r
. From Lemma
4.4
, all adjacency matrices
M_{k}
of
\mathcal{S}(G,G^{\prime})
for some class
P_{k}=g^{G^{\prime}}
outside of
G^{\prime}
are of the form
M_{k}=B_{\ell}\otimes J_{m}
where
B_{\ell}
is the adjacency matrix for
\{gG^{\prime}\}
in
G/Z(G)
. We then have a one-to-one correspondence between the adjacency matrices
M_{k}
of
\mathcal{S}(G,G^{\prime})
for
P_{k}\not\subseteq G^{\prime}
and the adjacency matrices of the form
D_{r+k}=B_{k}\otimes J_{m}
for
1\leq k\leq n/m
in
\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})\wr\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
.

We then see that
\mathcal{S}(G,G^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})\wr\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
have the exact same adjacency matrices. Therefore,
\mathcal{S}(G,G^{\prime})=\mathcal{G}(G^{\prime})\wr\mathcal{G}(G/G^{\prime})
.
∎

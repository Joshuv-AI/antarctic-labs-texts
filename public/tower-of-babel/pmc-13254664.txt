Conscious Sedation for WATCHMAN FLX Implantation Using Transesophageal Echocardiography Guidance
Authors: Joseph C Crowder, Adam M Bender, Peter J Haigh
Published: 2026

Left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) with WATCHMAN FLX has become an established alternative to long-term anticoagulation in select patients with atrial fibrillation. While traditionally performed under general anesthesia (GA), conscious sedation (CS) is increasingly utilized at high-volume centers for patient or institutional factors. Contemporary real-world data comparing outcomes between CS and GA strategies remain limited.

We retrospectively analyzed consecutive patients in our institutional National Cardiovascular Data Registry who underwent WATCHMAN FLX implantation under CS between February 2021 and April 2024. Patients were included if 45-day follow-up imaging was available. CS was administered by the procedural team using intravenous midazolam and fentanyl while maintaining spontaneous breathing. Anesthesia support was available but not routinely present in the procedure room. CS was the default strategy unless GA was required due to medical comorbidities or inability to tolerate transesophageal echocardiography (TEE); a total of 10 procedures were performed under GA during the study period. All cases were performed with intraprocedural TEE guidance. Intracardiac echocardiography was not utilized. The primary efficacy outcome was peridevice leak (PDL) at 45 days. Secondary endpoints included 7-day procedure-related adverse events (death, intracranial hemorrhage, ischemic stroke, and pericardial effusion) as well as device-related thrombus within 45 days.

There were 285 WATCHMAN FLX cases performed under CS during the study period. The mean age of the cohort was 74.6 years, and 57.6% were male. Ten cases (3.5%) were aborted prior to device implantation, most commonly due to unfavorable left atrial appendage anatomy or inability to achieve stable device positioning rather than sedation-related factors. The mean procedural time for CS cases was 88 minutes. No procedures required conversion to GA on the day of implantation. Of the 275 successful cases, 258 had 45-day follow-up imaging and comprised the final cohort.

Follow-up imaging was predominantly via cardiac computed tomography (CT) (95.7%), while a small minority underwent TEE (4.3%). At 45 days, no residual leak was observed in 223 patients (86.4%), leak 1–5 mm was observed in 28 patients (10.9%), and leak >5 mm was observed in 7 patients (2.7%). No patients experienced death, intracranial hemorrhage, ischemic stroke, or pericardial effusion within 7 days. One patient (0.4%) developed clinically relevant access-site bleeding without the need for transfusion or surgical intervention. One patient (0.4%) developed acute deep vein thrombosis and an access-site arteriovenous fistula with thrombosed pseudoaneurysm, neither of which required surgical intervention. Two patients (0.8%) developed device-related thrombus. No periprocedural air embolism was observed.

Among the 7 patients with PDL >5 mm, management was individualized following review of the imaging findings. In several cases, the physician operator suggested smaller leak dimensions than initially reported. One patient underwent attempted leak repair. Although the procedure was unsuccessful, intraprocedural TEE suggested that the residual leak was clinically insignificant, and no further intervention was pursued. The remaining patients were managed conservatively, including short extension of anticoagulation in select cases, without early adverse clinical events.

To contextualize these findings, outcomes were compared with GA-dominant WATCHMAN FLX cohorts, including the large retrospective analysis by Price et al.,1 the PINNACLE FLX trial,2 and a single-center series by Chakravarty et al.3 (Table 1). Overall PDL rates were similar between our cohort and the Price FLX cohort (13.6% versus 14.7%). Early adverse event rates in our CS cohort were low and consistent with those reported in contemporary WATCHMAN FLX studies. While the observed rate of PDL >5 mm in our cohort was higher than that reported in GA-dominant cohorts (2.7% versus 0%–0.7%), surveillance modality differed substantially across studies.

Our cohort predominantly underwent CT-based follow-up, whereas comparator cohorts largely utilized TEE. Cross-sectional imaging has been shown to detect residual PDL more frequently than echocardiography, including in a recent analysis by Tan et al.,5 where cardiac CT identified significantly more overall residual leaks compared with TEE and numerically higher rates of large (>5 mm) PDL (2.8% versus 0.8%; odd ratio: 3.12; 95% CI: 0.73–13.36). Differences in reported large PDL rates should therefore be interpreted in the context of surveillance modality.

Overall, these findings suggest that WATCHMAN FLX implantation under CS is associated with high procedural success and low early adverse event rates. Outcomes were generally consistent with those reported in contemporary GA-dominant cohorts—as well as National Cardiovascular Data Registry LAAO registry data4 more broadly. It adds to the growing body of evidence that CS may be implemented safely in appropriately selected patients undergoing LAAO and may facilitate improving procedural efficiency. During the study period, same-day discharge became increasingly common, rising from 10.2% in 2021–2022 to 77.9% in 2023–2024 as operator experience with CS strategies increased.

Limitations of our study include its retrospective, single-center design, lack of an internal GA comparison group, and potential interobserver variability in imaging interpretation. Because most follow-up imaging in this cohort was performed with cardiac CT, direct comparison with prior studies relying primarily on TEE-based follow-up may be limited. Prospective studies with standardized imaging protocols and direct comparison of sedation strategies would provide more definitive evaluation.

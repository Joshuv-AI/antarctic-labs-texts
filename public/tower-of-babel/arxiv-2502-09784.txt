On the Constructive Theory of Jordan Curves
Douglas S. Bridges

Abstract
Using a definition of Jordan curve similar to that of Dieudonné, we prove that our notion is equivalent to that used by Berg et al. in their constructive proof of the Jordan Curve Theorem [ 1 ] . We then establish a number of properties of Jordan curves and their corresponding index functions, including the important Proposition 32 and its corollaries about lines crossing a Jordan curve at a smooth point. The final section is dedicated to proving that the index of a point with respect to a piecewise smooth Jordan curve in the complex plane is identical to the familiar winding number of the curve around that point. The paper is written within the framework of Bishop’s constructive analysis. Although the work in Sections 3–5 is almost entirely new, the paper contains a substantial amount of expository material for the benefit of the reader.

, in which case
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta)=0
, or else

\left|\zeta\right|<2R
. In the latter case, let
\zeta_{1}=\frac{4R}{\left|\zeta\right|}\zeta
; then
\left|\zeta_{1}\right|>R
, so
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta_{1})=0
. Also, for each

\lambda\in\left[0,1\right]
,

\left|\left(1-\lambda\right)\zeta+\lambda\zeta_{1}\right|=\left|1+\left(\frac{4R}{\left|\zeta\right|}-1\right)\lambda\right|\left|\zeta\right|\geq(1+\lambda)\left|\zeta\right|>r\text{,}

so
\left[\zeta,\zeta_{1}\right]\subset-B
. Since
\mathsf{car}(\gamma)\subset\subset B
, it follows that the path
\mathsf{lin}(\zeta,\zeta_{1})
is bounded away from
\mathsf{car}(\gamma)
; whence, by

[
3
, (5.4.5)]
,
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta)=\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta_{1})=0
.

Lemma 50

Let
\gamma:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a
piecewise smooth Jordan curve, then
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta)=0
for each

\zeta\in\mathsf{out}(\gamma)
.

Proof.
Using Lemma
48
, choose
R>0
such that
\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)}\subset\subset B(0,R)
and
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta)=0
whenever

\left|\zeta\right|>R
. Fix
\zeta_{1}
with
\left|\zeta_{1}\right|>R
; then
\zeta_{1}\in-\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)}=\mathsf{out}(\gamma)
. If
\zeta\in\mathsf{out}(\gamma)
, then by JCT,

\zeta
and
\zeta_{1}
can be joined by a polygonal path well contained in

-\mathsf{ca}
r
\mathsf{(}\gamma)
, so, by Proposition

47
and our choice of
R
,
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta)=\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta_{1})=0=\mathsf{ind}(\zeta,\gamma)
.

We have now reached our final result.

Proposition 51

If
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma\
is a piecewise smooth
Jordan curve in
\mathbb{C}-\{\zeta\}
, then
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta)=\mathsf{ind}(\zeta;\gamma)
.

Proof.
We may take the domain of
\gamma
to be
\left[0,1\right]
. The case

\zeta\in\mathsf{out}(\gamma)
is covered by Lemma
50
. To deal with
the case
z\in\mathsf{in}(\gamma)
, fix
t_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\zeta_{0}\equiv\gamma(t_{0})
is a smooth point of
\gamma
. Rotating if necessary,
we may assume that
\operatorname{Im}\gamma\,^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
, so that the
tangent
T
to
\gamma
at
t_{0}
is horizontal. Let
N
be the normal to

\gamma
at
t_{0}
. By Corollary
37
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(i)

if
z_{1}=\zeta_{0}-i\delta
and
z_{2}=\zeta_{0}+i\delta
, then,
without loss of generality,
(\zeta_{0},z_{1}]\subset\mathsf{in}(\gamma)
and

(\zeta_{0},z_{2}]\subset\mathsf{out}(\gamma)
.

By Lemma
42
, there exists
r
with
0<r<\delta
such that

(ii)

if
0<\left|t-t_{0}\right|<r
, then
\gamma(t)\in-N
;

(iii)

every point of
\gamma(t_{0}-r,t_{0})
lies on the opposite side
of
N
from every point of
\gamma\left(t_{0},t_{0}+r\right)
.

Let

D=\sup\left\{\left|z-z_{1}\right|:z\in\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)}\right\}

and
\zeta_{1}\in-B(z_{1},2D)
. Then
\rho(\zeta_{1},\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)})>0
, so by Corollary
24
,
\zeta_{1}\in\mathsf{out}(\gamma)
. Now let
d=\rho(z_{1},\mathsf{ca}
r
(\gamma))
and note
that
\delta=\left|z_{1}-\gamma(t_{0})\right|\geq d
. Let also

\kappa=1+\frac{M}{d}+4\left(1+\frac{1}{d}\right)\left(1+\frac{\delta}{d}\right)

and

C\equiv\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z-z_{1}\right|=\frac{d}{2}\right\}.

Construct a polygonal path
\mu:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
in

\mathsf{out}(\gamma)
joining
\zeta_{1}
to
z_{2}
such that

\displaystyle 0<\alpha

\displaystyle\equiv\inf\left\{\rho(\mu(s),\mathsf{car}(\gamma)):s\in\left[0,1\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\rho(\mu(s),\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)}):s\in\left[0,1\right]\right\},

the final equality arising from Proposition
23
. Let
0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\alpha,\pi\right\}
. Noting that
\rho(\gamma(t_{0}),C)<\left|\gamma(t_{0})-z_{1}\right|<\delta
, and using Lemma

44
and the continuity of
t\rightsquigarrow\rho(\gamma(t),\left[\gamma(t_{0}),z_{1}\right])
on
\left[0,1\right]
, construct
r_{1}\
such that
0<r_{1}<\min\left\{r,\varepsilon/2\right\}
and

(iv)

if
0<\left|t-t_{0}\right|<r_{1}
, then

–

\left|\gamma(t)-\gamma(t_{0})\right|<\varepsilon/2
;

–

the points
\gamma(t_{0}),z_{1}
, and
\gamma(t)
are the vertices of a
proper triangle;

–

the segments
\left[z_{1},\gamma(t_{0})\right]
and
\left[z_{1},\gamma(t)\right]
intersect only at
z_{1}
;

–

the acute angle between those segments is less than
\varepsilon/2
; and

–

\rho(\gamma(t),C)<\delta
.

Fix
t_{1},t_{2}
such that
t_{0}-r_{1}<t_{1}<t_{0}<t_{2}<t_{0}+r_{1}
. By
(iii),
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are on opposite sides of
N
, as are the segments

[\gamma(t_{1}),z_{1})
and
[\gamma(t_{2}),z_{1})
. Let
\sigma
be the
circular path

\theta\rightsquigarrow z_{1}+\frac{d}{2}\exp\left[i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)\right]\ \ \ \ \ \left(0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\right).

which is traversed in the
clockwise
direction, is centred at
z_{1}
,
has radius
\frac{d}{2}
, has carrier
C
, and is such that
\sigma(0)=z_{1}+\frac{id}{2}=\sigma(2\pi)
. Note that by Proposition
19
,

B(z_{1},d)\subset\mathsf{in}(\gamma)
. For each
k\in\left\{1,2\right\}

there exists
\theta_{k}\in(0,2\pi)
such that
\sigma(\theta_{k})
is the
intersection of
C
and
[\gamma(t_{k}),z_{1}]
. Let
\gamma_{1}=\mathsf{lin}(\gamma(t_{1}),\sigma(\theta_{1}))
and
\gamma_{3}=\mathsf{lin}(\gamma(t_{2}),\sigma(\theta_{2}))
; let also
\gamma_{0}
be the
restriction of
\gamma
to
\left[0,t_{1}\right]
,
\gamma_{4}
the
restriction of
\gamma
to
\left[t_{2},1\right]
, and
\gamma_{2}
the
restriction of
\sigma
to
\left[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right]
. Then

\Gamma\equiv\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{4}

is a piecewise smooth closed path. We divide the remainder of the proof into
five steps.

Step 1:
\mu

is bounded away from

\Gamma
.

Since
\gamma_{0}
,
\gamma_{2}
, and
\gamma_{4}
lie in
\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)}
,
\mu
is bounded away by
\alpha
from each of those
paths. On the other hand, if
\lambda\in\left[0,1\right]
,
z=\left(1-\lambda\right)\gamma(t_{1})+\lambda z_{1}
, and
s\in\left[0,1\right]
, then since
\left[\zeta_{0},z_{1}\right]\subset\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)}
and
0<\left|t_{1}-t_{0}\right|<r_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left|z-\mu(s)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\mu(s)-\left(1-\lambda\right)\zeta_{0}-\lambda z_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left.-\left|\left(1-\lambda\right)\gamma(t_{1})+\lambda z_{1}-\left(1-\lambda\right)\zeta_{0}-\lambda z_{1}\right|\right.

\displaystyle\geq\rho(\mu(s),\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)})-\left(1-\lambda\right)\left|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(t_{0})\right|

\displaystyle>\alpha-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>\frac{\alpha}{2}.

Hence
\mu
is bounded away by
\alpha/2
from
\left[\gamma(t_{1}),z_{1}\right]
and therefore from
\gamma_{1}
; likewise,
\mu
is bounded
away by
\alpha/2
from
\gamma_{3}
. Putting all this together, we see that

\mu
is bounded away by
\alpha/2
from
\Gamma
.

Step 2
:
\mathsf{lin}(z_{2},z_{1})

is bounded away from

\Gamma
.

First note that
\gamma(t_{k}),z_{1}
, and
\zeta_{0}
are vertices of a
proper triangle, by (iv), that
0<\theta_{k}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, and that
\sigma(\theta_{k})\in\left(z_{1},\gamma(t_{k}\right))
.
By elementary trigonometry, for each
z\in\left[\sigma(\theta_{k}),\gamma(t_{k})\right]
we have

\rho(z,N)=\left|z-z_{1}\right|\sin\theta_{k}\geq\tfrac{d}{2}\sin\theta_{k}.

It now follows that
\mathsf{lin}(z_{2},z_{1})
is bounded away from

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{3}
by

\beta\equiv\min\left\{\tfrac{d}{2}\sin\theta_{1},\tfrac{d}{2}\sin\theta_{2}\right\}.

Since
\rho(\sigma(\theta),[z_{1},z_{2}])
increases from
\tfrac{d}{2}\sin\theta_{1}
to
\frac{d}{2}
on
\left[\theta_{1},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]
, takes the value
\frac{d}{2}
throughout
\left[\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{3}\right]
, and decreases from
\frac{d}{2}
to
\tfrac{d}{2}\sin\theta_{2}
on
\left[\frac{3\pi}{2},2\pi\right]
, we see that

\mathsf{lin}(z_{2},z_{1})
is bounded away by
\beta
from
\gamma_{2}
. To
prove that it is bounded away from
\gamma_{0}
, first observe that by
Proposition
9
,
\gamma_{0}[0,t_{1}]
is compact; by J1,

\gamma(t_{0})\neq\gamma(t)
for each
t\in\left[0,t_{1}\right]
; and
hence, by Bishop’s Lemma,
\gamma(t_{0})\in-\gamma_{0}\left[0,t_{1}\right]
. Likewise,
\gamma(t_{0})\in-\gamma_{4}\left[t_{2},1\right]
. Let

s_{1}=\tfrac{1}{3}\min\left\{\delta,\rho(\gamma(t_{0}),\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{0})),\rho(\gamma(t_{0}),\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{4}))\right\}>0

and

\chi_{k}=\left(1-\frac{s_{1}}{\delta}\right)\zeta_{0}+\frac{s_{1}}{\delta}z_{k}\ \ \ (k=1,2).

Then
\chi_{k}\in(\zeta_{0},z_{k}]
and
\left|\chi_{k}-\zeta_{0}\right|=s_{1}
. By Corollary
38
, there exists
s_{2}
such
that
0<s_{2}<s_{1}
and
\left[\chi_{k},z_{k}\right]_{s_{2}}\subset-\mathsf{car}(\gamma)

(k=1,2).
Suppose that
t\in\left[0,t_{1}\right]
,
z\in

\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]
, and
\left|\gamma(t)-z\right|<s_{2}
. Either
\left|z-\zeta_{0}\right|<2s_{1}
or
\left|z-\zeta_{0}\right|>s_{1}
. In the first case,

\left|\gamma(t)-\zeta_{0}\right|\leq\left|\gamma(t)-z\right|+\left|z-\zeta_{0}\right|<3s_{1}

and so

\rho(\gamma(t),\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{0}))\geq\rho(\gamma(t_{0}),\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{0}))-\left|\gamma(t)-\zeta_{0}\right|>3s_{1}-3s_{1}=0,

which is absurd. In the case
\left|z-\zeta_{0}\right|>s_{1}
there
exists
k\in\{1,2\}
such that
~z\in\left[\chi_{k},z_{k}\right]
and
therefore
\gamma(t)\in\left[\chi_{k},z_{k}\right]_{s_{2}}\subset-\mathsf{car}(\gamma)
, which is equally absurd. It follows that
\rho(\gamma_{0}(t),\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right])\geq s_{2}
for all
t\in\left[0,t_{1}\right]
; whence
\ \mathsf{lin}(z_{2},z_{1})
is bounded away
from
\ \gamma_{0}\,
—and, likewise, from
\gamma_{4}
. Putting all this
together, we now see that
\mathsf{lin}(z_{2},z_{1})
is bounded away from

\Gamma
.

Step 3:
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}=0

If
z\in

\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{1})
, then
\left|z-z_{1}\right|\leq\left|\gamma(t_{1})-z_{1}\right|\leq D
; likewise, if

z\in\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{3})
, then
\left|z-z_{1}\right|\leq D
;
and if
z\in\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{0}\cup\gamma_{2}\cup\gamma_{4})
, then

z\in\overline{\mathsf{in}(\gamma)}
and again
\left|z-z_{1}\right|\leq D
. Hence
\mathsf{car}(\Gamma)\subset B(z_{1},D)\subset\subset B(z_{1},2D)
. Since
\zeta_{1}\in-B(z_{1},2D)
, it follows from Lemma

49
that
\mathsf{j}(\Gamma,\zeta_{1})=0
. On the other hand, from
Steps 1 and 2 we conclude that the piecewise linear path
\mu+\mathsf{lin}(z_{2},z_{1})
is bounded away from
\Gamma
. Thus by Proposition

47
,

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}=\mathsf{j(\Gamma},z_{1})=\mathsf{j}(\Gamma,\zeta_{1})=0.

(2)

Step 4:
\left(\int_{\gamma}+\int_{\sigma}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}=0

We now estimate the various components of the integral on the left of
(
2
). First,

\displaystyle\left|\gamma_{1}(0)-\gamma_{3}(0)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(t_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\gamma(t_{1})-\gamma(t_{0})\right|+\left|\gamma(t_{2})-\gamma(t_{0})\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<(d+1)\varepsilon

Also, since, by (iv), the smaller arc of
C
from
\sigma(\theta_{2})
to

\sigma(\theta_{1})
, equal to the sum of the smaller arc between

\sigma(\theta_{2})
and
\sigma(2\pi)
and that between
\sigma(0)
and

\sigma(\theta_{1})
, has length
<d\varepsilon
,

\left|\gamma_{1}(1)-\gamma_{3}(1)\right|=\left|\sigma(\theta_{1})-\sigma(\theta_{2})\right|<d\varepsilon<(d+1)\varepsilon.

It follows that for
0\leq t\leq 1,

\displaystyle\left|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{3}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-t\right)\left|\gamma_{1}(0)-\gamma_{3}(0)\right|+t\left|\gamma_{1}(1)-\gamma_{3}(1)\right|

\displaystyle<\left(1-t\right)(d+1)\varepsilon+t(d+1)\varepsilon

\displaystyle=(d+1)\varepsilon.

Hence
\left\|\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{3}\right\|_{\left[0,1\right]}\leq(d+1)\varepsilon
. On the other hand, by (iv) and elementary geometry,

\left|\gamma_{3}(1)-\gamma_{3}(0)\right|=\rho(\gamma(t_{2}),C)<\delta
. Now,
\gamma_{3}
and
\gamma_{1}
are paths in the compact set

K\equiv\overline{\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{3})\cup\mathsf{car}(\gamma_{1})}
,

\rho(z_{1},K)=d/2
, and
z\rightsquigarrow 1/(z-z_{1})
is differentiable on

K
. Hence, by Lemma
46
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\gamma_{1}}\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}-\int_{\gamma_{3}}\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{3}\right\|_{\left[0,1\right]}}{d/2}\left(2+\frac{\left|\gamma_{3}(1)-\gamma_{3}(0)\right|}{d/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(d+1)\varepsilon}{d}\left(2+\frac{2\delta}{d}\right)

\displaystyle=4\left(1+\frac{1}{d}\right)\left(1+\frac{\delta}{d}\right)\varepsilon.

(3)

Next we have

\displaystyle\left|\left(\int_{\gamma}-\int_{\gamma_{0}}-\int_{\gamma_{4}}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}{\gamma(t)-z_{1}}\mathsf{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\left|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right|}{\left|\gamma(t)-z_{1}\right|}\mathsf{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{M}{d}\mathsf{d}t=\frac{M}{d}(t_{2}-t_{1})<\frac{M}{d}2r_{1}<\frac{M\varepsilon}{d}.

(4)

On the other hand, from (iv) we have

\displaystyle\left|\left(\int_{\sigma}-\int_{\gamma_{2}}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}}+\int_{\theta_{2}}^{2\pi}\right)\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sigma(\theta)-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}}+\int_{\theta_{2}}^{2\pi}\right)\left|\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sigma(\theta)-z_{1}}\right|\mathsf{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}}+\int_{\theta_{2}}^{2\pi}\right)1\mathsf{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\theta_{1}+\left(2\pi-\theta_{2}\right)<\varepsilon.

(5)

Now

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\gamma}+\int_{\sigma}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Gamma}+\left(\int_{\gamma}-\int_{\gamma_{0}}-\int_{\gamma_{4}}\right)+\left(\int_{\sigma}-\int_{\gamma_{2}}\right)-\left(\int_{\gamma_{1}}-\int_{\gamma_{3}}\right)\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}

from which, using (
2
), (
3
), (
4
), and (
5
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left(\int_{\gamma}+\int_{\sigma}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|+\left|\left(\int_{\gamma}-\int_{\gamma_{0}}-\int_{\gamma_{4}}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\left(\int_{\sigma}-\int_{\gamma_{2}}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|+\left|\left(\int_{\gamma_{1}}-\int_{\gamma_{3}}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle<0+\frac{M}{d}\varepsilon+\varepsilon+4\left(1+\frac{1}{d}\right)\left(1+\frac{\delta}{d}\right)\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\kappa\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\min\left\{\alpha,\pi\right\}\right)
is
arbitrary and independent of
\kappa
, it follows that

\left(\int_{\gamma}+\int_{\sigma}\right)\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}=0.

Step 5:
Conclusion of the proof.

From Step 4 we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathsf{j}(\gamma,z_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma}\frac{\mathsf{d}z}{z-z_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\sigma\,^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sigma(\theta)-z_{1}}\mathsf{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}-i\mathsf{d}\theta=1=\mathsf{ind}(z_{1};\gamma).

Finally, if
\zeta
is any point of
\mathsf{in}(\gamma)
, then it can be
joined to
z_{1}
by a polygonal path that lies in
-\mathsf{car}(\gamma)
, so
by Proposition
47
,
\mathsf{j}(\gamma,\zeta)=\mathsf{j}(\gamma,z_{1})=1=\mathsf{ind}(\zeta;\gamma)
.

poles, and meromorphic functions, to which we shall turn our attention in

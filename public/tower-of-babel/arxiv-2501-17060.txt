The sorrows of a smooth digraph: the first hardness criterion for infinite directed graph-colouring problems


Abstract
. Two major milestones on the road to the full complexity dichotomy for finite-domain constraint satisfaction problems were Bulatov’s proof of the dichotomy for conservative templates, and the structural dichotomy for smooth digraphs of algebraic length 1 due to Barto, Kozik, and Niven. We lift the combined scenario to the infinite, and prove that any smooth digraph of algebraic length 1 pp-constructs, together with pairs of orbits of an oligomorphic subgroup of its automorphism group, every finite structure – and hence its conservative graph-colouring problem is NP-hard – unless the digraph has a pseudo-loop, i.e. an edge within an orbit. We thereby overcome, for the first time, previous obstacles to lifting structural results for digraphs in this context from finite to ω \omega -categorical structures; the strongest lifting results hitherto not going beyond a generalisation of the Hell-Nešetřil theorem for undirected graphs. As a consequence, we obtain a new algebraic invariant of arbitrary ω \omega -categorical structures enriched by pairs of orbits which fail to pp-construct some finite structure.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Complexity and structural dichotomies for graphs

For a graph
\mathbb{H}
, the
\mathbb{H}
-colouring problem
is the computational problem where given a finite input graph
\mathbb{I}
, one has to decide whether there exists a
homomorphism
from
\mathbb{I}
to
\mathbb{H}
, i.e. a map sending edges to edges; a colouring, so to speak, of
\mathbb{I}
by the vertices of
\mathbb{H}
which is consistent with edges. This problem is a natural generalisation of the
n
-colouring problem, which is equal to the
\mathbb{H}
-colouring problem when
\mathbb{H}
is the clique on
n
vertices. Yet more general are (fixed-template)
Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSPs)
, which are parametrised by arbitrary relational structures
\mathbb{A}
(in this context called
templates
) rather than just graphs: here again, one is given a finite input structure
\mathbb{I}
in the same signature as
\mathbb{A}
, and has to decide whether or not there exists a homomorphism from
\mathbb{I}
into
\mathbb{A}
, i.e. a map preserving all relations. This problem is denoted by
\CSP(\mathbb{A})
, and so the
\mathbb{H}
-colouring problem in particular is
\CSP(\mathbb{H})
; the notion allows to model many classical computational problems, such as 3-SAT, within a uniform framework. On the other hand, every problem
\CSP(\mathbb{A})
is logspace-interreducible with the
\mathbb{H}
-colouring problem for some directed graph
\mathbb{H}
(
[
26
]
, improving upon
[
31
]
), and hence in this sense digraphs already contain the entire complexity spectrum brought along by the study of general Constraint Satisfaction Problems.

A systematic research programme on the complexity of CSPs was initiated by Feder and Vardi in the 1990s with their seminal work
[
30
,
31
]
, although the earliest results stem back to the 1970s, when Schaefer proved a P/NP-complete complexity dichototomy for CSPs over a Boolean domain
[
53
]
. The continuous results of the thriving research field culminated, 25 years after the start of the programme, in 2017 with two independent proofs by Bulatov
[
25
]
and Zhuk
[
55
,
56
]
, respectively, of a P/NP-complete dichotomy for CSPs of arbitrary finite structures: even if P
\neq
NP, then
no problem
\CSP(\mathbb{A})
can, for finite
\mathbb{A}
, be of intermediate complexity, contrasting Ladner’s theorem
[
43
]
. Similar complexity dichotomies had been obtained earlier in particular for undirected graphs (
[
34
]
; see also the later proof in
[
23
]
), and for
smooth
digraphs, i.e. digraphs with no sources and no sinks
[
5
]
.

Remarkably, the above complexity dichotomies coincide with elegant structural dichotomies for the classes of relational structures concerned. In fact, over the years numerous structural dichotomies inspired by applications to CSPs have been unveiled, usually taking the following form: either a template
\mathbb{A}
has large expressive power (as measured by its ability e.g. to
pp-interpret
, to
pp-interpret with parameters
, or to
pp-construct
a certain other finite structure, or all finite structures, or an abelian group), or there is an obvious obstacle for this (e.g. some form of symmetry of the structure in the form of a
polymorphism
, or a constant tuple in a relation). Of course, the former case then corresponds to hardness in the sense of computational complexity of the CSP (e.g. NP-hardness, or the non-applicability of a certain algorithm such as
k
-consistency) the latter to tractability (e.g. polynomial-time solvability). In the case of finite undirected non-bipartite graphs
\mathbb{H}

(note that the bipartite case is just the 2-colouring problem), Hell and Nešetřil proved, in modern terminology, that either
\mathbb{H}
pp-constructs all finite structures – and hence
\CSP(\mathbb{H})
is NP-complete – or
\mathbb{H}
contains a loop
[
34
]
; the latter is an obvious obstacle to the pp-construction of any loopless graph, and implies that
\CSP(\mathbb{H})
is trivial.

The next milestone achievement in this direction concerned
directed
graphs which are smooth and of
algebraic length 1

(a property which can be viewed as a generalisation of non-bipartiteness for digraphs): again, any such graph either pp-constructs all finite structures, or it has a loop. Subsequently, similar statements for higher-arity relations were obtained, for example relations invariant under cyclic shifts
[
6
]
, the proofs of which unsurprisingly often incorporate the combinatorial principles crucial in the case of digraphs. Finally, the structural dichotomy
underlying the theorem of Bulatov and Zhuk can be phrased as follows: every finite template
\mathbb{A}
either pp-constructs all finite structures, or it is invariant under a 4-ary operation
s
on its domain (a so-called
polymorphism
) satisfying the
4-ary Siggers identity

s(a,r,e,a)=s(r,a,r,e)
for all values of
a,r,e

[
40
]
.

1.2.
Infinity

When it comes to (countably) infinite graphs and structures,
the context in which results of a similar flavour are conceivable at least in principle is that of
\omega
-categoricity
: structures
\mathbb{A}
with an
oligomorphic
automorphism group
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
, i.e. an automorphism group so large that its action on
k
-tuples has only finitely many orbits (called
k
-orbits
) for all
k
. This might be paraphrased as
\mathbb{A}
being “close to finite” in the sense that it has only finitely many distinguishable
k
-tuples for every
k
. The property of
\omega
-categoricity is prominent in model theory, and is considered necessary and sufficient (in a certain precise sense, see
[
13
,
16
,
7
]
) for the applicability of the
algebraic approach
to CSPs, crucial for the success of finite-template CSPs.

Example 1.1
.

The automorphism group of the digraph
(\mathbb{Q};<)
given by the strict order of the rational numbers is oligomorphic: the orbit of any
k
-tuple under
\Aut(\mathbb{Q};<)
is completely determined by the order induced on its elements, for which there are only finitely many possibilities. The digraph
(\mathbb{Q};<)
is also a good example of an infinite graph inducing a natural CSP: namely, a finite digraph
\mathbb{I}
is
(\mathbb{Q};<)
-colourable if and only if it is acyclic, and the latter property cannot be modelled this way by any finite template.

Note that
\omega
-categoricity of a template
\mathbb{A}
can not only be witnessed by
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
itself, but by any oligomorphic subgroup
\Omega
of
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
(which might be a more natural permutation group to consider): the orbits of
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
in its action on
k
-tuples are unions of the orbits of
\Omega
.

Example 1.2
.

Consider the template
\mathbb{A}:=(\mathbb{Q};\Betw)
, where
\Betw
is the ternary
betweenness relation
which holds for a tuple
(x,y,z)
precisely if
x<y<z
or
z<y<x
. An instance
\mathbb{I}
of its CSP is a yes-instance if and only if its elements can be assigned values in
\mathbb{Q}
in such a way that all triples
(x,y,z)
of elements of
\mathbb{I}
which belong to its (only) relation end up in the relation
\Betw
after the assignment. This amounts to finding a (non-strict, since homomorphisms need not be injective) linear ordering of
\mathbb{I}
so that
y
is strictly between
x
and
z
with respect to this ordering for all such triples
(x,y,z)
; the problem is thus the classical (NP-complete)
betweenness problem
from the 1970s
[
50
]
. In this case, it might be more natural to consider the proper subgroup
\Omega:=\Aut(\mathbb{Q};<)
of
\Aut(\mathbb{Q};\Betw)
rather than the latter group itself, since we are thinking of finding an ordering, but also since the group
\Aut(\mathbb{Q};<)
is natural and well-understood from a model-theoretic perspective.

In general, if
\Omega\leq\Aut(\mathbb{A})
, we have that
the relations of
\mathbb{A}
, invariant under its automorphisms, are unions of orbits of
\Omega
, and one can thus imagine them as finite objects modulo
\Omega
(in the example above,
\Betw
consists of two orbits with respect to
\Omega:=\Aut(\mathbb{Q};<)
). Thus, when solving an instance
\mathbb{I}
of
\CSP(\mathbb{A})
, we have to pick for every tuple in a relation of
\mathbb{I}
one of finitely many orbits (with respect to
\Omega
) in the corresponding relation of
\mathbb{A}
in a consistent way. This is the general principle for
\omega
-categorical templates
\mathbb{A}
: orbits of an oligomorphic subgroup
\Omega
of
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
take the role of values for tuples in an instance, and in particular the 1-orbits of
\Omega
take the role of values for its elements. If the orbits of
\Omega
have a locally verifiable local representation (see e.g.
[
19
,
51
]
for a precise discussion of such a context, namely when
\Omega=\Aut(\mathbb{B})
for a
finitely bounded homogeneous structure

\mathbb{B}
), then
\CSP(\mathbb{A})
is in NP, and a P/NP-complete complexity dichotomy has been conjectured by Bodirsky and Pinsker more than 10 years ago (see
[
14
,
8
,
3
,
4
]
for formulations of the conjecture).

Barto and Pinsker obtained the first general structural dichotomy for
\omega
-categorical graphs
[
8
,
9
]
. If an undirected graph
\mathbb{G}
contains the three-element clique
K_{3}
as a subgraph, and
\Omega
is an oligomorphic subgroup of its automorphism group, then one of the following holds: either
\mathbb{G}
together with the
\Omega
-orbits pp-constructs every finite structure, or
\mathbb{G}/\Omega
has a loop, i.e. there is an edge within an
\Omega
-orbit (also called a
pseudoloop
of
\mathbb{G}
with respect to
\Omega
). This implies a structural dichotomy for all
\omega
-categorical templates
\mathbb{A}
: either
\mathbb{A}
together with the orbits of its automorphism group pp-constructs all finite structures, or
\mathbb{A}
has unary polymorphisms
u,v
and a 6-ary polymorphism
s
such that
us(x,y,x,z,y,z)=vs(y,x,z,x,z,y)
holds for all
x,y,z
. The hope, sparked by this result, for a full lifting of the above-mentioned Hell-Nešetřil structural dichotomy for finite undirected non-bipartite graphs to
\omega
-categorical ones was, however, only fulfilled in 2023
[
1
]
. This delay can be attributed to the fact that pp-constructions from a template
\mathbb{A}
allow, together with the
\Omega
-orbits, for the addition of elements of
\mathbb{A}
as singleton unary relations; adding all elements of a finite
\mathbb{A}
yields a structure with a trivial automorphism group, thereby granting access to decades of fruitful research on such structures; the same cannot be achieved for infinite structures. Given the additional technical complications when passing from undirected to directed graphs already in the finite, it is hardly surprising that the lifting of the dichotomy for finite smooth digraphs of algebraic length 1 had to remain open: in fact, hitherto not a single structural consequence of the inability to pp-construct all finite structures has been unveiled for
\omega
-categorical digraphs, even under reasonable conditions such as the presence of a certain subgraph.

1.3.
Conservativity & our contributions

Following the complexity dichotomies for 2-element
[
53
]
and 3-element templates
[
21
]
, the next breakthrough on the path to the theorems of Bulatov and Zhuk was Bulatov’s proof of the dichotomy for finite
conservative
templates
[
22
]
: that is, templates
\mathbb{A}
enriched by all subsets of their domain as unary relations. An instance
\mathbb{I}
of
\Csp(\mathbb{A})
might thus impose for each of its elements an arbitrary list of allowed values in
\mathbb{A}
which the element may be sent to under a solution (that is, under a homomorphism to
\mathbb{A}
). Connecting this with colouring problems, one might view the elements of
\mathbb{A}
as colours, and the lists for elements of the instance
\mathbb{I}
as sets of allowed colours from which one colour has to be chosen.
Adding these unary relations makes the template even more rigid than after adding unary singleton relations (discussed above), and thereby facilitates the analysis of its symmetries (polymorphisms). On the other hand, the conservative setting already exposes some of the most important building blocks required for a structural analysis of arbitrary templates; its importance is witnessed by several works in this setting after Bulatov’s original proof
[
10
,
39
,
24
]
.
Moreover, variants of CSPs such as counting CSPs
[
20
,
27
]
and valued CSPs
[
42
]
have been studied in this setting. Conservative graph-colouring problems are known as
list homomorphism problems
, and have been examined
for many classes of finite digraphs
[
36
,
35
,
29
]
.

For
\omega
-categorical templates
\mathbb{A}
, where the
1
-orbits of a subgroup
\Omega
of
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
take the role of elements, a conservative template would thus be enriched by a unary relation for all unions of 1-orbits of
\Omega
.
In this work, assuming only access to unions of pairs of
1
-orbits, we obtain the first structural dichotomy for smooth digraphs of algebraic length 1. For a template
\mathbb{A}
, a subgroup
\Omega
of
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
, and
k\geq 1
let us call the expansion of
\mathbb{A}
by all unary relations
P_{1}\cup\cdots\cup P_{k}
, where each
P_{i}
is a 1-orbit of
\Omega
, the
k
-conservative expansion of
\mathbb{A}
with respect to
\Omega
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(G;\rightarrow)
be a smooth digraph of algebraic length
1
, and let
\Omega\leq\Aut(\mathbb{G})
be oligomorphic. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

\mathbb{G}/\Omega
has a loop;

(2)

the 2-conservative expansion of
\mathbb{G}
with respect to
\Omega
pp-constructs EVERYTHING, i.e. every finite structure.

As a corollary, we obtain a hardness criterion for conservative CSPs of smooth digraphs of algebraic length 1; that is, CSPs of such graphs where unions of
1
-orbits
can appear as constraints. As in the finite case discussed above, the
1
-orbits can be viewed as colours, and the unions of 1-orbits as lists of colours from which one has to choose.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(G;\rightarrow)
be a smooth digraph of algebraic length
1
, and let
\Omega\leq\Aut(\mathbb{G})
be oligomorphic.
Consider the CSP where each instance
\mathbb{I}
is a finite digraph enhanced with a list of allowed
1
-orbits of
\Omega
for each of its vertices, and the question is whether the graph on
\mathbb{I}
can be
\mathbb{G}
-coloured whilst respecting the lists. Then this problem is NP-hard unless
\mathbb{G}/\Omega
has a loop.

Example 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 3
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite set of
n
-coloured tournaments (i.e. tournaments which are expanded by unary predicates
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
which form a partition of its domain). Then there exists an
\omega
-categorical
n
-coloured digraph
\mathbb{A}
whose induced substructures are precisely those
n
-coloured digraphs which do not contain a copy of any member of
{\mathcal{F}}
and which do not have any edge within any colour
P_{i}
, and such that the 1-orbits of
\Omega:=\Aut(\mathbb{A})
are precisely the colours
P_{i}
(this follows from Fraïssé’s
theorem
[
32
]
; we skip the details). Let
\mathbb{G}
be the graph reduct of
\mathbb{A}
, i.e. the graph obtained by forgetting the colours
P_{i}
.
The conservative CSP (or the list homomorphism problem)
of
\mathbb{G}
with respect to
\Omega
gets as an input a finite digraph
\mathbb{I}
and for every vertex
v
a subset
\labels(v)
of the colours of
\mathbb{A}
, and asks for the existence of a homomorphism from
\mathbb{I}
into
\mathbb{G}
such that every vertex
v
is sent into one of the colours specified in
L(v)
; in other words, a colouring of
\mathbb{I}
avoiding
\mathcal{F}
as well as monochromatic edges. This problem is NP-complete, as follows in particular from Corollary
1.4
. A similar albeit slightly more involved construction exists for any homomorphism-closed set
\mathcal{F}
of connected digraphs
[
28
,
37
]
; these templates fall into the framework of the logic MMSNP
[
12
]
.

Using standard methods to derive algebraic consequences from loops in graphs, we then moreover see that any
\omega
-categorical template which is
strongly 2-conservative
in the sense that not only unions of pairs of 1-orbits of
\Omega
, but also of
k
-orbits are part of it, has a
4-ary pseudo-Siggers polymorphism
unless its CSP is NP-hard. To put this in context, while certain algebraic conditions corresponding to undirected graphs are known to hold for tractable
\omega
-categorical templates, this condition is wide open in general, and one of the most intriguing open problems in this context. In particular, it is not even known to apply to tractable
temporal CSPs

[
11
]
, i.e. CSPs of templates first-order definable in the order of the rational numbers.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a structure, and let
\Omega\leq\Aut(\mathbb{A})
be oligomorphic. Let
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
be the expansion of
\mathbb{A}
by all unions
P\cup Q
, where
P,Q
are
\Omega
-orbits
of
k
-tuples and
k\geq 1
.
Then one of the following holds:

•

\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
has a 4-ary pseudo-Siggers polymorphism, i.e. there are unary polymorphisms
u,v
and a 4-ary polymorphism
s
such that
us(a,r,e,a)=vs(r,a,r,e)
holds for all values
a,r,e
;

•

\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
pp-constructs EVERYTHING, and hence
\CSP(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
is NP-hard.

We ought to remark here that expanding a template
\mathbb{A}
as in
Corollary

1.6
will yield an NP-hard CSP for many prominent templates previously studied in the literature: in fact, the expansion
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
cannot have any binary injective polymorphisms, in contrast to these templates. One could argue, however, that this is not necessarily the typical case: recent results show the existence of templates within the range of the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture that
are even solvable by local consistency methods, but do not possess any binary injective polymorphism
[
44
, Corollary 8.10]
. Moreover, if the inequality relation
\neq
is pp-definable in
\mathbb{A}
(frequent in prominent examples, see e.g.
[
47
, Proposition 19]
),
then by the same proof one obtains the following variant of
Corollary

1.6
:
\mathbb{A}
is only expanded by
P\cup Q
for orbits
P,Q
of
injective
tuples, and in the first case, the polymorphisms
u,v,s
satisfy said equation only on injective tuples. In this variant, the first case applies
to several tractable cases of major complexity classifications, e.g. for Graph-SAT or Hypergraph-SAT problems
[
15
,
46
,
48
,
47
]
; moreover, the satisfaction of the equation on
all
tuples can then often be easily derived by different methods (such as pre-composition with binary
canonical injections of type projection
, see e.g.
[
48
]
).
Altogether, this hints at the possibility that for
\omega
-categorical structures, the existence of a 4-ary pseudo-Siggers polymorphism might be equivalent to that of the 6-ary pseudo-Siggers polymorphism from
[
8
,
9
]
; the latter has been conjectured to separate the hard from the tractable CSPs within the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture.

1.4.
Outlook: a more liberal future?

If one were able to replace 2-conservativity by 1-conservativity
in
Theorem

1.3
, then one would obtain a significant strengthening of the theorem of Barto and Pinsker for undirected graphs from
[
8
,
9
]
. This would imply the amazingly general statement that any
\omega
-categorical template which does not pp-construct all finite structures has polymorphisms
u,v,s
as in
Corollary

1.6
. Moreover, even the converse would be true for
model-complete cores
(which we will not need here; any
\omega
-categorical template has the same CSP as a model-complete core
[
18
]
).
Evidence that our proof lays the foundations for such endeavour is provided, for example, by the following proposition, which using concepts from the proof of our main theorem
solves the case where
\mathbb{G}/\Omega
has only three orbits and is isomorphic to the graph corresponding to the equations of
Corollary

1.6
(see its proof). Already for this particular situation, no proof was hitherto known, and its undirected version was dedicated an own proof in
[
8
, Proposition 5.3]
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(G;\rightarrow)
be a smooth digraph and let
\Omega\leq\Aut(\mathbb{G})
be oligomorphic, and such that
G/\Omega
is isomorphic to the following graph.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

.

Then the 1-conservative expansion of
\mathbb{G}
with respect to
\Omega
pp-constructs EVERYTHING.

Another, independent, direction is to replace the assumption of algebraic length 1 by its weaker version modulo
\Omega
, i.e., require only
\mathbb{G}/\Omega
to have algebraic length 1. Resulting statements have been shown in
[
1
]
for finite graphs, and turn out useful when comparing the strength of algebraic conditions (see
[
49
,
33
]
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Relational structures

For
n\geq 1
, we write
[n]
for the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. A
relation
on a set
G
is a subset
R\subseteq G^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
. We refer to the number
n
as the
arity
of the relation
R
. A
relational structure
(or simply
structure
) is a tuple
\mathbb{G}=(G;\mathcal{R})
consisting of a set
G
and a family
\mathcal{R}
of relations on
G
. A structure
\mathbb{H}=(G;\mathcal{S})
is an
expansion
of a
structure
\mathbb{G}=(G;\mathcal{R})
if
\mathcal{S}\supseteq\mathcal{R}
.
For
H\subseteq G
, we write
\mathbb{G}|_{H}=(H;\mathcal{R})
for the
induced substructure
of
\mathbb{G}
on
H
.
We will understand an
n
-ary function
f
on a set
G
also as a function on
k
-tuples of elements from
G
for any
k\geq 1
, where we apply
f
componentwise.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(G;\mathcal{R})
and
\mathbb{H}=(H;\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
be relational structures such that the families
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
use the same relational symbols. A mapping

is a

from
\mathbb{G}
to
\mathbb{H}
if for every tuple
\mathbf{t}
contained in some relation of
\mathbb{G}
it holds that

belongs to the corresponding relation of
\mathbb{H}
.
Two structures
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{H}
are

if there exists a homomorphism both from
\mathbb{G}
to
\mathbb{H}
, and from
\mathbb{H}
to
\mathbb{G}
.
A
polymorphism
of a relational structure
\mathbb{G}=(G;\mathcal{R})
is a function

for some

such that for every
R\in\mathcal{R}
of some arity
k\geq 1
, and for all tuples
\mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n}\in R
, the tuple
f(\mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n})
is contained in

; we then say that

preserves

, or that

is
invariant
under

. An

is a bijective unary polymorphism

so that

is a polymorphism as well.
We write
\Pol(\mathbb{G})
and
\Aut(\mathbb{G})
for the sets of all polymorphisms and automorphisms of
\mathbb{G}
, respectively.

2.2.
Formulae

A first-order formula is a
primitive positive formula
(or simply
pp-formula
) over a family
\mathcal{R}
of relational symbols if it is built using only predicates from
\mathcal{R}
, conjunction, and existential quantification. We abuse notation by using the same name for a relational symbol and its interpretation in a relational structure. A relation is
pp-definable
in a relational structure
\mathbb{G}=(G;\mathcal{R})
if it is first-order definable by a pp-formula over
\mathcal{R}
. A structure is pp-definable in
\mathbb{G}
if all its relations are. We say that
\mathbb{G}
pp-defines
\mathbb{H}

with parameters
if
\mathbb{H}
is pp-definable in the expansion of
\mathbb{G}
by the singleton unary relations
\{a\}
for every element
a
of its base set.

The structure
\mathbb{H}
is a
pp-power
of
\mathbb{G}
if there exists

such that the domain of
\mathbb{H}
is
G^{n}
, and its relations are pp-definable from
\mathbb{G}
(where a
k
-ary relation on
G^{n}
is understood as a
kn
-ary relation on
G
).
We say that
\mathbb{G}

pp-constructs

\mathbb{H}
if
\mathbb{H}
is homomorphically equivalent to a pp-power of
\mathbb{G}
;
\mathbb{G}
pp-constructs EVERYTHING if it pp-constructs every finite structure.

A pp-formula

is a
tree
if the following bipartite undirected graph is a tree, i.e. connected without cycles: the vertices are the variables of
\phi
(on one side) as well as the conjuncts of
\phi
(on the other side), and there is an edge between a variable and a conjunct if and only if the variable appears in that conjunct. A relation is
tree pp-definable
in a structure
\mathbb{G}
if it is pp-definable in
\mathbb{G}
by a pp-formula which is a tree.

For a relation
R\subseteq G^{n}
, the relation
\OR(R,R)
is the relation defined by the formula
R(\mathbf{x})\vee R(\mathbf{y})
which contains precisely all tuples
(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t})\in G^{2n}
for which either
\mathbf{s}\in R
, or
\mathbf{t}\in R
.

2.3.
Permutation groups

For a permutation group

A structure

be a relational structure whose underlying set is

For

We will denote the

-orbit equivalence on

It is easy to see that a relation first-order definable from a set of

A structure is a

if it pp-defines all orbits of

-tuples under its own automorphism group for every

]

If

, the quotient structure modulo an equivalence

-classes. The

-blow-up

2.4.
Digraphs and paths

A
directed graph
(or simply

with a single binary relation

(which will usually be denoted by
\rightarrow

if the relation

is

, i.e. if the projection to any one of its coordinates equals

. The

of a digraph

is the largest subset

is smooth.
If

is a binary relation on a set

, we write

for the set
\{(y,x)\mid(x,y)\in E\}
. If
E=\rightarrow
, we prefer the notation
\leftarrow
for
E^{-1}
. A
loop
of

is a pair
(a,a)\in E
.
An
(abstract)
E
-path
is a finite sequence

where each

is either

or
E^{-1}
. The
algebraic length
of

is the number of occurrences of
E
minus the number of occurrences of

; to make it crystal clear,
\sum_{i}\log_{E}(E_{i})
. A
realisation
of

is a sequence
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})
of elements of
G
such that

E_{i}(a_{i},a_{i+1})

holds for all

. We also call
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})
an

-walk
(along

). A path which has a realisation is
realisable
. Note that if

is the edge relation of a smooth digraph, then any
E
-path is realisable. If
E_{i}=E
for all

, then we call

an
E
-forward path
, and any realisation of it an
E
-forward walk
; an
E
-backward path (or walk)
is defined accordingly. A
cycle
is an
E
-forward walk
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1})
with
a_{1}=a_{n+1}
.
A digraph
\mathbb{G}=(G;E)
is
weakly connected
if for all
a,b\in G
with

, there exists an
E
-walk from

to
b
.
A
weakly connected component
of
\mathbb{G}
is any subset

which is maximal with respect to inclusion such that the induced digraph
\mathbb{G}|_{H}
is weakly connected.
The digraph
\mathbb{G}
has
algebraic length

if there exists a
path of algebraic length

that has a realisation of which the first and last element coincide.

Let
\Omega
be a permutation group on the domain of the digraph

. An
\Omega
-orbit-labelled

-

where

is an

-path of length

, and each

is an
\omega
-class; we call

an
\Omega
-labelling of

, and say that

is
from

to

. Usually, we shall only write
\Omega
-orbit-labelled path when

is clear from the context.

A
realisation
of
\pi
is a realisation
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})
of

with the additional property that
a_{i}\in P_{i}
for all
i
; if a realisation exists, then we say that
\pi
is
realisable
. If
p,q
are
E
-paths, then we write
p+q
for the path obtained by concatenation of the two. If
p=(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
, then we set
-p:=(E_{n}^{-1},\ldots,E_{1}^{-1})
.
Finally,

. We say that

is
symmetric
if it is of the form

. We use similar notation for
\Omega
-orbit-labelled paths; note, however, that the sum of
\Omega
-orbit-labelled paths

is only well-defined if the last orbit of
\pi
is equal to the first orbit of
\rho
. An
extension
of a path

is obtained by iteratively inserting either
(E,E^{-1})
or
(E^{-1},E)
at arbitrary places of the sequence; an extension of an
\Omega
-orbit-labelled path is obtained via the same process by replacing an orbit label
P_{i}
by
P_{i},P^{\prime},P_{i}
for some
\omega
-class
P^{\prime}
at the appropriate place. A
relabelling
of
\pi=(p,(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n+1}))
is any
\Omega
-orbit-labelled path
\pi^{\prime}=(p,(P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{n+1}^{\prime}))
.
The
merge
of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
then is the pair
\pi\vee\pi^{\prime}:=(p,(P_{1}\cup P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{n+1}\cup P_{n+1}^{\prime}))
, with the obvious semantics of a realisation.
To make notation more readable,
for such a merge

we will sometimes write

\binom{P_{1}}{P^{\prime}_{1}}E_{1}\binom{P_{2}}{P^{\prime}_{2}}E_{2}\;\cdots\;E_{n}\binom{P_{n+1}}{P^{\prime}_{n+1}}.

The
relation induced
by a path

is the binary relation
\gamma_{p}\subseteq G^{2}
containing all pairs
(a,b)
for which there exists a realisation of

starting at
a
and ending at
b
. Similarly, we define the relation
\gamma_{\pi}
induced by an
\Omega
-orbit-labelled path
\pi
, and the relation
\gamma_{\pi\vee\pi^{\prime}}
induced by a merge. Note that
\gamma_{p}
is pp-definable from
E
, and that
\gamma_{\pi}
is pp-definable from
E
together with the
\omega
-classes; in order to pp-define

, we need

and unions of pairs of
\omega
-classes.

If
R_{1},\dots,R_{k}
are binary relations, we write
R_{1}+\cdots+R_{k}
for the relation containing all pairs
(a_{0},a_{k})
for which there exist
a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1}
with
(a_{i-1},a_{i})\in R_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
.
If
R_{1}=\cdots=R_{k}=E
, we denote this sum by
E^{k}
.
If
E=\rightarrow
, we also write
\overset{k}{\rightarrow}
for
E^{k}
.
We set
E^{-k}:=(E^{k})^{-1}
. For a unary relation
S
, we write

for the relation
\{b\mid\exists a\in S\;(a,b)\in E\}
; note that this is a pp-definition.
For better readability, we set

.
For
k,n\geq 1
, the
(k,n)
-
E
-fence is the
E
-path

, with
n
occurrences of

, where

for

, with
k
occurrences of

(so the path has length

).
If

is clear from the context, we denote the relation induced by the

-

-fence by

. Clearly,
\mathbb{F}[k,n]\subseteq\mathbb{F}[k,n+1]
.
The

is
\bigcup_{n}\mathbb{F}[k,n]
; it is an equivalence relation, and if

is finite, then it is equal to some
\mathbb{F}[k,n]
and hence tree pp-definable in
\mathbb{G}

[
6
]
.
We say that

is

-linked
if this equivalence relation is full.

3.1.

While some algebraic invariants of finite relational structures have been shown to have

3.2.
Revisiting established methods

Our endeavours of lifting results from finite to infinite structures align with an active field of research that has thrived through the application of various valuable techniques. Building upon recent results in this direction, we make use of some of the methods presented in
[
5
,
1
]
for finite digraphs (or digraphs with finite weakly connected components),
and apply them to the realm of
\omega
-categorical digraphs. In particular, in contrast to the setting of the most general result in this direction hitherto,
[
1
, Theorem 10]
, our new approach must address weakly connected components that are not finite.

When working with an
\omega
-categorical structure, it is natural to apply methods designed for finite structures to a quotient of it that is either finite
or at least sufficiently tame (e.g., has finite weakly connected components), and then infer information on the original structure.
However, the choice of such a quotient must be made judiciously to ensure it retains sufficient structural information. While taking the quotient modulo
\omega
, i.e. the
1
-orbit equivalence with respect to
\Omega
, seems to be the natural choice, we might lose, for example, all information about the connectivity of a graph.
To this end, given a digraph
\mathbb{G}=(G;\rightarrow)
as in the assumptions of

1.3
, and an oligomorphic subgroup
\Omega
of its automorphism group, we strive to find a suitable refinement
\alpha
of
\omega
, which shall in particular have the same weakly connected components as
\mathbb{G}
. Clearly, we want such a refinement to be compatible with
\Omega
, whose orbits are our basic building blocks; that is,

A1)

\alpha
is
\Omega
-invariant.

This implies, in particular, that
\Omega
acts on
\alpha
-classes, and that it makes sense to speak of a realisation of an
\Omega
-orbit-labelled path
\pi
in
\mathbb{G}/\alpha
. Moreover, we would like such realisations to be unique in the following sense:

A2)

for every realisable
\Omega
-orbit-labelled path
\pi
whose first label is the
\omega
-class

, and for every
A\in O/\alpha
, there exists a unique realisation of
\pi
in
\mathbb{G}/\alpha
starting in
A
.

Finally, in order to make use of the techniques developed for digraphs with finite weakly connected components, we require:

A3)

the
\alpha
-quotient of every weakly connected component of
\mathbb{G}
is finite.

For the precise formulation of properties A1) – A3), see

4.3
.

Let

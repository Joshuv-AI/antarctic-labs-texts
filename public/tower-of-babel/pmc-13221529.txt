Competitive roles of plasticity, elasticity, and radiation in heat/mass transfer of Casson–Williamson–Maxwell nanofluid over a porous stretching sheet
Authors: R Kavitha, Ravi Samikannu, Nyagong Santino David Ladu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, Casson–Williamson and Maxwell fluid flow over a stretching porous sheet is investigated numerically, taking into account the effects of thermal radiation, thermophoresis, magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), and chemical processes. The flow, heat transfer, and mass diffusion controlling partial differential equations (PDEs) are converted into ordinary differential equations (ODEs) by use of a similarity transformation. Utilizing the fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK) method, the resulting equations are solved. The study examines key thermal and flow characteristics, including the influence of thermal radiation, the Prandtl number, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, Schmidt number, chemical reaction, magnetic field strength, Casson parameter, and Deborah number on velocity, temperature, and concentration profiles. Findings reveal that an increase in thermal radiation, Prandtl number, and Brownian motion parameter leads to a decline in temperature, whereas thermophoresis enhances fluid temperature. Higher Schmidt numbers reduce concentration due to lower mass diffusivity, while strong chemical reactions increase concentration. The application of a magnetic field results in velocity suppression due to Lorentz forces, whereas the elastic and rheological properties of Casson and Maxwell fluids further hinder fluid motion. The study presents thorough numerical results that demonstrate differences in the skin friction coefficient, Sherwood number, and Nusselt number, presented in graphical form. The research extends the theoretical framework of porous-sheet MHD flows by demonstrating how the standard boundary-layer similarity equations are modified by plastic yield (Casson), shear-rate-dependent viscosity (Williamson), and fluid elasticity (Maxwell).Keywords: MHD, Casson–Williamson fluid, Maxwell fluid, Chemical reaction, Thermal radiation

Introduction

The study of non-Newtonian fluid behavior, particularly in nanofluid models, has gained considerable attention due to its wide range of applications in biomedical engineering, polymer processing, petroleum production, cooling technologies, and chemical manufacturing. Among various non-Newtonian models, Casson, Williamson, and Maxwell fluids provide a robust mathematical framework to capture the rheological complexities exhibited by materials such as blood, honey, and lubricants. The presence of a porous medium and the stretching/shrinking of surfaces is common in industrial processes such as extrusion, wire drawing, and glass fiber production. Coupling these with magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effects, thermal radiation, and chemical reactions introduces complex interactions that influence both velocity and thermal boundary layers. These phenomena require comprehensive numerical investigations to fully understand the thermofluid behavior and to optimize engineering systems. Through the Darcy drag force, the porous material mostly affects the flow by adding more resistance to fluid motion. Friction between the fluid and the solid skeleton dissipates momentum as the fluid moves through the porous matrix, decreasing flow velocity and thickening the momentum barrier layer. Stronger flow resistance is correlated with lower permeability, which results in weaker convective transfer and increased momentum damping. Additionally, this resistance effect modifies near-wall shear behavior and suppresses velocity by interacting with magnetic and non-Newtonian forces. As a result, the porous medium is essential for regulating the boundary-layer flow’s drag characteristics and momentum transmission.

Incorporating nanoparticles into these base fluids further enhances thermal conductivity and heat transfer performance, a phenomenon first conceptualized by Choi and Eastman [1], and later expanded upon by Buongiorno [2] through the inclusion of thermophoretic and Brownian motion effects. This work was further enhanced by Das et al. [3] and Kakac and Pramuanjaroenkij [4], providing comprehensive reviews on heat transfer enhancement in nanofluids. The flow behavior and boundary layer formation of nanofluids over stretching sheets were first modeled by Khan and Pop [5] and later extended to convective boundary layer problems by Kuznetsov and Nield [6]. Using the non-Fourier heat conduction model, the free convection flow of nanofluids between two vertical plates was investigated by Hajizadeh et al. [7] while considering damped heat flux. Tarakaramu and Satya Narayana [8] investigated the unsteady magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) movement of nanofluids across a stretching sheet in the presence of a first-order chemical reaction. Chakradhar et al. [9] extended the MHD concept by investigating the peristaltic motion of Williamson fluid in a porous channel with the effects of suction and injection. Furthermore, Xuan and Li [10] provided basic information on how nanofluids facilitate heat transmission. Maurya and Sharma [11] investigated the hydromagnetic effects of a magnetic field and geometric configuration on the continuous flow of Casson nanofluids via rotating coaxial cylinders.

The Casson fluid model has been extensively explored for its yield stress behavior. Mukhopadhyay [12] and Pramanik [13] investigated Casson fluid flow over nonlinearly and exponentially stretching surfaces, revealing significant insights into heat transfer in complex systems. Humane et al. [14, 15] discussed the effects of magnetic fields, thermal radiation, and chemical reactions on Casson and Casson-Williamson nanofluid flows over porous stretching surfaces, highlighting their importance in optimizing thermal processes. Williamson fluid models, suitable for pseudoplastic fluids, were applied in boundary layer flows by Nadeem et al. [16] and later integrated into nanofluid contexts by Khan et al. [17] and Bouslimi et al. [18]. These works revealed how viscoelastic properties influence velocity and thermal gradients. Recent advancements by Muzara and Shateyi [19] have incorporated MHD and thermal radiation effects into Williamson nanofluid models, offering deeper insights into boundary layer thickness control.

Similarly, Maxwell fluids, characterized by relaxation time effects, have been numerically analyzed by Zainal et al. [20, 21], who considered unsteady and hybrid Maxwell nanofluid flows under thermal radiation. Aziz and Shams [22] incorporated entropy generation into the analysis, underlining the thermodynamic implications of these models. Thermal radiation and chemical reactions further complicate the flow dynamics, particularly in porous media. Raptis [23] and Nield and Bejan [24] provided foundational texts on porous convection, while Khalili et al. [25] and Makinde et al. [26] addressed combined heat generation/absorption and MHD effects. Studies by J.A. Gbadeyan et al. [27] and Humane et al. [14] demonstrated how variable thermal conductivity and velocity slip conditions impact Casson nanofluids, whereas works by Abro and Shaikh [28] and Khan et al. [29] provided exact and numerical solutions for Maxwell and Williamson fluids over porous and oscillating surfaces.

Despite the vast body of research dedicated to non-Newtonian nanofluid flows over stretching surfaces, several key gaps remain unaddressed, particularly concerning the combined analysis of Casson-Williamson and Maxwell fluid models under complex physical effects. Most existing studies are model-specific, focusing either on Casson [12–14, 30], Williamson [17–19], or Maxwell [20, 22, 31] nanofluids individually, without exploring their comparative behavior under identical flow and thermal conditions. This leaves a gap in understanding how different non-Newtonian behaviors influence heat and mass transport in practical applications.

Dual solutions in a Casson–Williamson nanofluid flow exposed to thermal radiation and chemical reactivity were examined by Gomathi and De Poulomi [32]. Heat generation and thermal slip effects were taken into consideration when Abbas et al. [33] examined non-Newtonian slippery nanofluid flow across a stretched sheet embedded in a porous medium. The analysis was expanded to MHD slip flow of upper-convected Casson and Maxwell nanofluids across a porous stretched surface by Seethamahalakshmi et al. [34]. Reexamining Crane’s traditional stretched sheet solution, Aziz and Mahomed’s seminal work [35] offered insightful commentary on the mathematical structure and precise solutions of viscous flow issues. This work is still important as a standard for verifying contemporary numerical and semi-analytical methods used with non-Newtonian fluid models. Maxwell nanofluid flow over an exponentially extending surface with gyrotactic microorganisms was studied numerically by Chouhan et al. [36]. Their findings demonstrated that while bioconvection improves system stability, fluid elasticity has a significant impact on heat and mass transfer. Entropy generation and regression analysis in unsteady Carreau ternary hybrid nanofluid flow under electromagnetic and thermal impacts were investigated by Sindhu and Jagadeeshkumar [37]. Attention has also been paid to thermal radiation in elastic medium. Heat and mass transmission in two-layer elastic composites exposed to infrared radiation were examined by Kournyts’kyi et al. [38], who came to the conclusion that elastic deformation considerably modifies thermal transport properties.

The impact of elasticity on extremely viscous non-Newtonian fluid flow in circular pipes was quantitatively examined by Wang et al. [39], who reported increased heat transfer rates with higher elastic parameters. Their results demonstrated the importance of elasticity as a control parameter in polymeric fluid-based energy transport systems. Nassar [40] investigated the consequences of plastic dissipation by analyzing the transformation of plastic work into heat in metals. Despite its emphasis on solid mechanics, the study offers insightful information about energy dissipation methods that are theoretically related to viscous and non-Newtonian fluid heating. Filler orientation greatly increases effective thermal conductivity, according to a computational study on heat transfer enhancement in composite materials using oriented multidimensional fillers by Luo et al. [41]. Their findings bolster the increasing focus on thermal optimization at the material level. Rao et al. [42] investigated MHD Casson and Maxwell nanofluids over a porous stretched surface in order to better investigate the impact of thermal radiation on non-Newtonian nanofluids. Williamson nanofluid flow across stretching/shrinking sheets with Brownian motion and thermophoresis effects was examined by Zhu et al. [43]. Their numerical findings showed that whereas Brownian motion has a major impact on nanoparticle concentration distributions, thermophoresis amplifies temperature fields. Casson nanofluid flow under a changing inclined magnetic field was studied by Akaje et al. [44], who showed that magnetic inclination angles are essential for controlling heat and mass transport. Their research demonstrated how sensitive Casson fluids are to the orientation of magnetic fields. In order to examine chemically reactive and radiative Casson nanofluid flow over a porous nonlinear stretching sheet, Ramadevu et al. [45] used the Buongiorno model with HAM solutions. Their research verified that although concentration profiles decrease, thermal boundary layers are strengthened by radiation and chemical processes.

Furthermore, while several works incorporate thermal radiation [19, 20, 25, 26], chemical reactions [8, 15, 17], or porous media [18, 23, 24, 46] independently, very few studies simultaneously address the interplay of all these phenomena in a single mathematical model. A number of real-world situations where fluids simultaneously display yield stress, shear-thinning, and viscoelastic memory effects are the driving force behind the simultaneous examination of Casson, Williamson, and Maxwell fluid models. Polymer melts and solutions frequently exhibit Casson-type yield behavior during flow commencement, Williamson-type shear-thinning at higher shear rates, and Maxwell-type elastic relaxation as a result of molecular chain dynamics in polymer processing and extrusion. Similar to this, in order to guarantee consistent deposition and surface quality in coating and printing processes, non-Newtonian inks and suspensions need precise modeling of both plastic resistance and elastic deformation.

The Casson model describes the yield stress behavior of blood at low shear rates, the Williamson model depicts shear-thinning viscosity caused by red blood cell aggregation, and the Maxwell model takes into account elastic effects resulting from cell membrane deformation and plasma viscoelasticity in biomedical applications, especially in blood flow and biofluid transport. As a result, the combined Casson-Williamson-Maxwell framework offers a more cohesive and realistic explanation of the complicated fluid behavior found in both physiological and industrial transport systems.

This area of research has attracted considerable interest due to its relevance in diverse fields such as industrial processes, biomedical devices, and thermal management systems. Applications including polymer extrusion, aerodynamic surface heating, electronic component cooling, and advanced coating technologies depend heavily on accurate regulation of heat and mass transfer in non-Newtonian fluid settings. The utilization of Casson–Williamson and Maxwell fluid models contributes to enhanced thermal conductivity, thereby boosting the overall efficiency of energy transfer. Therefore, examining the dynamics of these fluids over a porous, stretching surface offers meaningful contributions to the understanding of flow behavior and heat transfer mechanisms.

The combined impact of plastic, shear-thinning, and elastic effects on coupled momentum–energy–species transfer has not been evaluated, despite research on separate non-Newtonian models. This work expands the continuum-mechanics understanding of boundary-layer flow over porous surfaces by deriving and analyzing a unified similarity formulation and elucidating parameter regimes where each rheology prevails.

To strengthen the validation, appropriate benchmark cases have been identified by reducing the present model to well-established limiting situations reported in the literature, including the absence of magnetic field, porous medium, thermal radiation, and non-Newtonian effects. Validation has been explicitly carried out by comparing the present numerical results with available benchmark solutions. Comparison plots for these limiting cases are presented in Figs. 1 and 2, corresponding respectively to Crane’s classical solution [35] for the velocity profile and the nanofluid results of Khan and Pop [5] for the temperature distribution. The excellent agreement observed in these comparisons confirms the accuracy and reliability of the present numerical methodology.

Novelty and scientific contribution

Boundary-layer flow of non-Newtonian fluids over stretching surfaces has been extensively studied, but most of the earlier research has concentrated on a single constitutive law, such as Casson, Maxwell, or Williamson fluids, frequently ignoring combined thermal-chemical effects or porous resistance. Consequently, it is yet unknown how much of a mechanical impact plasticity, shear-rate-dependent viscosity, and elastic memory have on the coupled momentum–energy–species transfer in porous media.

The current study makes the following unique contributions to the mechanics of boundary-layer flows:

Unified rheological framework

We develop a general similarity-reduced system that takes into account porous drag, MHD, heat radiation, and chemical reaction in addition to three significant non-Newtonian behaviors: Maxwell viscoelasticity, Williamson shear-thinning, and Casson plastic yield.

This unified description allows direct comparison of the three rheologies under identical geometric and transport conditions.

Constitutive-law impacts on momentum balance explained

The analysis clearly demonstrates how the traditional Falkner-Skan-type equation is altered by the additional stress terms:

These revelations reveal the mechanisms by which the interaction of diffusive, viscous, and inertial forces varies depending on the rheology.

Extension of knowledge about mass and heat transmission in porous non-Newtonian layers

The study shows non-trivial interaction between momentum and scalar transport by combining thermophoretic and Brownian transport with chemical processes and radiative heating.

The findings provide direction for process design in polymer extrusion, cooling of stretched sheets, and coating or biomedical applications by highlighting parameter regimes where plastic, elastic, or shear-thinning effects predominate the wall shear, Nusselt number, or Sherwood number.

Bridging of theory and application

The derived equations extend the continuum-mechanics description of porous-sheet flows in a way that can be utilized for additional theoretical research or benchmarked in experiments by reducing to numerous classical models as limiting cases (Newtonian, purely plastic, purely visco-elastic, impermeable sheet, etc.).

Beyond a parametric calculation, these contributions offer a conceptual understanding of how various non-Newtonian phenomena influence the basic balancing laws of boundary-layer mechanics.

Temperature vs Nt values

Heat transmission is influenced by thermophoresis and Brownian motion via essentially distinct particle transport processes. The random mobility of nanoparticles caused by Brownian motion improves microscopic mixing and makes it easier for thermal energy to be transferred away from the heated wall, which lowers the local temperature and thins the thermal boundary layer. On the other hand, thermophoresis causes nanoparticles to migrate specifically from hotter areas close to the wall to cooler areas within the fluid. A thicker thermal boundary layer and a higher temperature profile are the results of these particles’ outward migration, which increases the thermal energy within the fluid domain. The conflicting patterns shown in the temperature distributions can be explained by the antagonistic nature of these systems. As higher Schmidt numbers are associated with decreased mass diffusivity, which lessens the diffusion of solute into the fluid, concentration falls as Sc rises. As the chemical reaction parameter rises, Fig. 9 illustrates how the concentration rises. Even when mass diffusivity is low, a strong chemical reaction can override diffusion effects and result in higher local concentrations, in contrast to the Schmidt number, which primarily influences the species’ diffusivity. In Fig. 10, the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effect causes the velocity to decrease as the magnetic field (M) increases. The change of the dimensionless wall shear f″(0) as a function of the Casson plasticity index 1/β and the Williamson number W is shown in Fig. 13. The drag that the fluid applies to the permeable sheet is reflected in the wall shear f″(0), which measures the near-wall velocity gradient. Because of the combined rheological resistance of the Casson and Williamson fluids, wall shear is reduced when f″(0) is negative. Both parameter directions show a distinct monotonic trend. The plastic character of the Casson fluid suppresses near-wall velocity gradients and decreases drag, as seen by the fact that for a constant W, increasing 1/β (corresponding to stronger Casson plastic resistance) reduces f″(0). On the other hand, for a fixed 1/β, raising the Williamson number W increases f″(0), showing that the shear-thinning elasticity of the Williamson fluid reduces the effective viscosity in high shear regions close to the wall and thereby fortifies the momentum transport along the wall. A shift in rheological dominance is indicated by the slanting contour lines. The Casson plastic resistance controls the dynamics at small W and big 1/β, which results in less wall shear. Higher wall shear results from the Williamson fluid’s shear-thinning effect, offsetting and ultimately defeating the Casson resistance as W rises. This interaction shows how the hybrid fluid’s conflicting visco-plastic and elastic-shear-thinning characteristics control the flow close to the wall. The contour map physically identifies parameter regimes that can be used in applications: areas with high W promote enhanced drag and momentum transfer, which may be useful for processes like mass transport and convective heat that benefit from increased wall shear, while areas with large 1/β and small W favor drag reduction because of dominant Casson plastic effects.

Synergistic interaction between thermal radiation (R) and prandtl number (Pr)

The Prandtl number and thermal radiation work together to lower the temperature field. A greater Prandtl number limits heat penetration into the fluid core by reducing thermal diffusivity, whereas increasing radiation increases radiative heat loss from the fluid. A narrower thermal boundary layer and steeper thermal gradients close to the wall result from simultaneous increases in both parameters, which also boost radiative cooling and decrease thermal replenishment. The significant numerical temperature decrease and the associated increase in the Nusselt number are explained by this coupled mechanism.

Antagonistic interaction between Brownian motion (Nb) and thermophoresis (Nt)

The competitive aspect of thermophoresis and Brownian motion has been highlighted in the expanded discussion. Brownian motion lowers fluid temperature and thermal boundary-layer thickness by promoting random nanoparticle diffusion, which increases microscopic energy exchange and facilitates heat dissipation away from the heated surface. Thermophoresis, on the other hand, effectively transfers thermal energy into the fluid bulk and raises temperature by moving nanoparticles from hotter to cooler areas.

The numerical findings show that the relative prevalence of these two mechanisms determines the resulting temperature distribution: The antagonistic interplay between Nb-dominated and Nt-dominated regimes is seen in the cooling of Nb-dominated regimes and the thermal increase of Nt-dominated regimes.

Synergistic velocity suppression by magnetic field (M) and Casson parameter (β)

The Casson parameter creates yield stress-based plastic resistance that restricts deformation, while the magnetic field creates Lorentz forces that oppose fluid motion and transform kinetic energy into thermal energy.

Strong momentum damping, decreased velocity gradients, and decreased wall shear stress are the outcomes of the synergistic effects of increasing both M and β. The sharp velocity decay shown in the numerical profiles and the decrease in the skin friction coefficient are explained by this dual resistance process.

Rheological competition between Casson plasticity (1/β) and Williamson number (W)

The clear interpretation of Figs. 13 and 14 as proof of hostile rheological interaction is a significant improvement. While Williamson shear-thinning elasticity lowers effective viscosity at high shear rates and improves momentum transmission, Casson plasticity suppresses near-wall deformation and decreases drag.

3-D surface plot of wall shear f′′(0) versus W and 1/β, illustrating the crossover between plastic resistance and shear-thinning enhancement.

A crossover regime, where dominance switches from Casson-controlled drag reduction (high 1/β, low W) to Williamson-controlled drag amplification (high W), is evident in the contour and 3-D surface plots. In industrial applications, this competition offers a physically significant way to adjust transport rates and wall shear.

Competing effects of Schmidt number (Sc) and chemical reaction parameter (B)

The discussion of mass transfer has been extended to highlight the antagonistic effects of chemical reaction parameter and Schmidt number. Strong chemical reactions increase local species concentration through reaction-driven production, while high Sc decreases mass diffusivity and suppresses species transit. Numerical findings verify that diffusive constraints can be overcome by sufficiently powerful chemical processes, increasing concentration even in the presence of high Sc values.

A three-dimensional surface plot of the wall shear f″(0) as a function of the Casson plasticity index 1/β and the Williamson number W is shown in Fig. 14. The magnitude of f″(0), which measures the velocity gradient at the wall and so reflects the drag force felt by the stretching porous sheet, is represented by the surface height. Stronger Casson plastic resistance suppresses the near-wall velocity gradient and reduces wall shear, as the plot demonstrates when f″(0) falls as 1/β grows. The fluid acts more like a visco-plastic Casson medium in the area of big 1/β and small W, where this effect is most prominent.

On the other hand, f″(0) grows as W increases, indicating that Williamson shear-thinning behavior enhances momentum transmission and steepens the velocity gradient at the wall by lowering the effective viscosity at high shear rates. The shear-thinning effect counteracts and even overcomes the Casson-induced resistance for big enough W, resulting in a net increase in wall shear.

Conclusion

This work conducts a numerical analysis of fluid flow involving Casson–Williamson and Maxwell models over a porous, stretching surface, accounting for the influences of magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), chemical reactions, thermal radiation, Brownian diffusion, and thermophoretic transport. The governing system of partial differential equations (PDEs) is reduced to a set of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) through the application of similarity transformations. These equations are then numerically solved using the fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) method. To ensure the credibility and precision of the computational approach, the results are cross-validated with data available in the existing literature.

Key findings indicate that:

Thermal radiation, Prandtl number, and Brownian motion significantly affect heat transfer. An increase in radiation and Prandtl number reduces the fluid temperature due to enhanced thermal conductivity, while Brownian motion enhances heat transfer.

Magnetic field strength (MHD) suppresses velocity. The presence of a magnetic field induces Lorentz forces, which resist fluid motion and increase effective viscosity.

Non-Newtonian effects of Casson, Williamson, and Maxwell fluids alter velocity profiles. A higher Casson parameter increases resistance to deformation, reducing velocity, while the Deborah number reflects elastic behavior that further hinders fluid motion.

The study provides valuable insights into the heat and mass transfer mechanisms in nanofluid flows, particularly in the presence of external forces and non-Newtonian fluid characteristics. The findings are relevant to industrial applications, including polymer processing, cooling systems, microfluidic devices, and biomedical engineering. Future research could extend this work by incorporating time-dependent effects, different fluid compositions, and multi-phase flow interactions to enhance the applicability of the model in real-world engineering scenarios.

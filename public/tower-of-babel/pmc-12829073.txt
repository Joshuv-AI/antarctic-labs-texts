Effect of choline-tryptophan based ionic liquid on the solubility and scavenging activity of quercetin: thermophysical and spectroscopic approach
Authors: Vibek Srichandan, Malabika Talukdar, Sanghamitra Pradhan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The limited aqueous solubility of quercetin severely restricts its bioavailability and functional applications. This study demonstrates the effectiveness of a biocompatible choline-tryptophan ionic liquid ([Ch][Trp]) IL in enhancing the solubility and antioxidant performance of quercetin. Incorporation into the IL medium produced a concentration-dependent modulation of 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH•) radical scavenging activity, with the highest IL concentration (0.18 mM) increasing RSA from 74.76% (quercetin alone) to 78.18%, whereas lower IL concentrations resulted in slightly reduced activity. Physicochemical evaluation of quercetin-[Ch][Trp]-water solutions revealed structure-making behaviour, reflected by a positive Hepler constant and negative Sv values, indicating strengthened water structuring and weak ion-ion interactions conducive to solute stabilization. Spectroscopic analyses supported these findings: UV–Vis absorption exhibited a red shift, fluorescence intensity increased, and FTIR spectra confirmed specific hydrogen-bonding and π-π interactions between quercetin and the IL. Collectively, these results show that [Ch][Trp] creates a favourable micro structured environment that stabilizes quercetin, modulates its radical-scavenging performance, and improves its physicochemical properties. This work highlights the potential of amino-acid-based ILs as green, multifunctional media for improving the performance of poorly water-soluble bioactive compounds.Graphical abstract

Introduction

Natural antioxidants continue to attract significant interest due to their role in mitigating oxidative stress-related disorders. Flavonoids, a major class of dietary polyphenols, exhibit potent radical scavenging activity and broad pharmacological benefits [1]. Among them, quercetin (3,3′,4′,5,7-pentahydroxyflavone) is one of the most widely distributed and biologically active members, with its antioxidant behaviour primarily attributed to the catechol B-ring and multiple phenolic hydroxyl groups that facilitate hydrogen atom transfer [2]. Although routinely evaluated using the DPPH• assay, the application of quercetin is limited by its extremely low aqueous solubility, chemical instability, and poor oral bioavailability. To address these challenges, several formulation strategies have been explored; however, many rely on complex processes or non-biodegradable excipients. This has driven interest in green and tuneable solvents such as ionic liquids (ILs). Choline-based ILs have demonstrated strong potential to enhance the solubility and stability of poorly soluble bioactive [3], while amino-acid-derived ILs provide additional tunability through hydrogen-bond networks and π-π interactions that can improve flavonoid solubility and reactivity [4]. Recent studies highlight the tuneable hydrogen-bond networks and aromatic interactions present in amino-acid ILs that favourably modulate solute incorporation. However, systematic kinetic analysis of radical scavenging in such IL microenvironments remains limited. Additionally, studies on drug-surfactant systems in ILs have demonstrated that ILs significantly modulate intermolecular interactions, micellization behaviour, and thermodynamic stability. Kumar et al. reported that imidazolium-based ILs alter hydrophobic association, synergistic binding, and solvent structuring across temperature ranges, underscoring the profound sensitivity of solute behaviour to IL-induced microenvironmental changes [5].

Flavonoids themselves exhibit strong responsiveness to structured microenvironments. Kumar et al. showed that flavonoid dispersion in cationic assemblies is controlled by hydrophobic, van der Waals, hydrogen-bonding and electrostatic interactions, with density, sound velocity and viscosity acting as sensitive probes of these associations [6]. Their spectroscopic findings further revealed that UV-Vis and fluorescence transitions of flavonoids shift markedly with changes in microenvironment polarity and molecular packing [6]. Complementarily, IL-drug interaction studies demonstrate that long-chain imidazolium ILs form synergistically stabilized aggregates with hydrophobic drugs, reducing the CMC and improving drug incorporation through cooperative hydrophobic-electrostatic interactions [7]. Thermodynamic analyses of mixed micellar systems further show synergistic stabilization and improved solubilization of hydrophobic phytochemicals, reinforcing the value of engineered microenvironments in enhancing dissolution and functional performance [8].

Building on our previous work establishing the synthesis protocol for the [Ch][Trp] [9], the present study investigates [Ch][Trp] as a dual-function medium: a green, biocompatible solvent capable of significantly enhancing quercetin solubility, and a structured microenvironment that may modulate its antioxidant kinetics. Quercetin’s radical scavenging behaviour in IL-water media was evaluated using the DPPH• assay. Furthermore, density measurements across 293.15–313.15 K, limiting apparent molar volumes, partial molar expansibilities, and the Hepler constant were determined to probe solute-solvent interactions within the quercetin-IL-water ternary system. Complementary UV-Vis, fluorescence, and FTIR analyses were performed to elucidate the molecular interactions governing flavonoid-IL association. Collectively, these measurements aim to establish [Ch][Trp] IL as an effective, sustainable, and structurally informative platform for overcoming the solubility and delivery challenges associated with quercetin.

Radical scavenging activity (RSA) determination

The antioxidant activity was evaluated using the DPPH free radical scavenging assay, with slight modifications, with ethanol employed as the solvent in place of methanol [10]. Mixtures were prepared containing 1 mM DPPH solution, 1 mM quercetin solution, and one of the different IL concentrations. Solubility studies

The solubility of quercetin was determined in distilled water and in aqueous choline-tryptophan IL (0.1% v/v, 0.1mL of IL in 99.9mL distilled water) using an equilibrium solubility method. Accurately weighed quercetin (5.00 mg) was added to 5, 10- and 15-mL portions of each solvent system in tightly sealed glass vials. The mixtures were magnetically stirred at ambient temperature (25 ± 1 °C) for 24 h to achieve equilibrium. After equilibration, the suspensions were filtered to remove undissolved solids. The dissolved quercetin concentration was quantified by using Shimadzu UV spectrophotometer (Model UV 1900i) at absorption maximum 370 nm using a 1.00 cm quartz cuvette. The quantification of drug was done against the respective concentration of IL and water mixture. Same procedure was also repeated in case of only water. Calibration curves were constructed separately for both solvent systems using matrix-matched standard solutions to ensure linearity and eliminate matrix effects [11].

Solution preparation

The deionized water, with an electrical conductivity of approximately 1.0 × 10− 6 Sm− 1, was procured from a Millipore Milli-Q academic purification system to ensure high purity and minimize extraneous ionic interference. Quercetin was incorporated into [Ch][Trp]IL to obtain the stock mixture, from which aqueous solutions of different concentrations (0.049, 0.039, 0.029, 0.019, and 0.009 mol kg− 1) were subsequently prepared by serial dilution in volumetric flasks using deionized water. Calculated and carefully weighed amounts of the IL were dissolved in a specific volume of water to prepare aqueous solutions of different concentrations. All preparations were performed under controlled ambient conditions (298.15 K and 101.01 kPa) to maintain experimental accuracy and reproducibility. Physico-chemical characterization

Density measurement

Density measurements for aqueous quercetin- [Ch][Trp] IL systems were performed using a high-precision digital density analyzer (Anton Paar DMA™ 5000 M, Austria) [12]. The instrument was calibrated with dry air and deionized distilled water (electrical conductivity ≈ 1.0 × 10− 6 Sm− 1) obtained from a Millipore Milli-Q Academic purification system to ensure high purity and minimize extraneous ionic interference. The device is equipped with a U-shaped borosilicate glass oscillating tube into which the sample is introduced, and the oscillation frequency of the tube is directly correlated with the sample’s density. Prior to each measurement, the tube was thoroughly rinsed with distilled water, followed by anhydrous ethanol, and calibrated with dry air and distilled water prior to each experiment. The temperature of the measuring cell was precisely controlled using a built-in Peltier thermostat, ensuring accurate and reproducible density determinations for all liquid samples. All experiments were conducted under controlled ambient conditions of 298.15 K and 101.01 kPa to maintain experimental consistency. The overall measurement uncertainty of the DMA 5000 M for density was within ± 0.05 kg m− 3, and the temperature uncertainty was ± 0.01 K [13].

Kinetic measurements

The kinetic behaviour of quercetin toward the DPPH radical in the presence of the [Ch][Trp] IL was investigated spectrophotometrically following the methodology reported by Butt et al., with appropriate modifications for the current system [10]. All measurements were carried out at 25 ± 0.1 °C using Shimadzu UV spectrophotometer (Model UV 1900i). Reaction mixtures were prepared directly in a 1 cm quartz cuvette by adding stock solutions of quercetin (1.00 mM) and [Ch][Trp] (0.18 mM) to give final concentrations of 0.16 mM quercetin and 0.03 mM [Ch][Trp] in the cuvette. The reaction was initiated by the addition of a freshly prepared ethanolic DPPH solution into the aqueous quercetin/IL mixture, and the solution was mixed quickly to ensure homogeneity. The total reaction volume was 3 mL, and ethanol content was maintained below 2% (v/v) to minimize solvent interference. The decrease in absorbance of DPPH at λmax = 516.8 nm was recorded at fixed time intervals (0s to 300s). Spectroscopic characterisation

The Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectra of quercetin in 0.1% (v/v) [Ch][Trp] IL: water was recorded using a PerkinElmer Spectrum Two FTIR spectrometer (Model No. 104789) equipped with a Universal ATR accessory featuring a diamond crystal sample plate. The spectra were collected over the wavenumber range of 4000 –450 cm− 1 with a resolution of 4 cm− 1, averaging four scans per measurement. The system was operated under ambient laboratory conditions, and the spectra were processed using PerkinElmer Spectrum Version 10.4.3 software. The instrument was subjected to routine baseline and water vapor corrections prior to data acquisition to ensure spectral accuracy. The UV-visible spectrum has been recorded using Shimadzu UV spectrophotometer (Model UV 1900i). Fluorescence measurements were conducted using an RF-5301PC Shimadzu Spectrofluorometer, equipped with a 150 W xenon lamp and a cell temperature controller. The emission spectra of ionic liquids were recorded in the wavelength range of 200–700 nm.

Results and discussions

Density measurement

Volumetric study of quercetin- [Ch][Trp] IL aqueous solutions was performed on the basis of experimental density measurements. Prior to preparing the test systems, the density of the background medium (pure water + IL) was determined at different working temperatures [9]. A series of density measurements was then carried out for aqueous quercetin solutions in the presence of IL with concentrations of 0.009, 0.019, 0.029, 0.039 and 0.049 mol·kg− 1. The obtained values are summarized in Table 2 and graphically represented in Fig. 1 as a function of concentration and temperature. At the lowest concentration (0.009 mol·kg− 1), the density of the quercetin-IL solution was slightly lower than that of the water + IL medium. A review of Table 2; Fig. 1 indicates that density increases linearly with concentration and decreases progressively with rise in temperature. The increase in density at higher concentrations can be attributed to closer molecular packing and enhanced associative interactions between quercetin, IL ions and water molecules. In contrast, at elevated temperatures, enhanced ionic mobility and thermal expansion of the solvent system led to an overall reduction in density. The nearly parallel nature of the concentration-density plots at different temperatures suggests that the concentration dependence of density remains uniform across the studied temperature range, reflecting stable solute-solvent interactions in the quercetin-IL aqueous system.

Apparent molar volume

In an aqueous solution, solvent molecules surround the solute particles. The ions of the [Ch][Trp] IL and the quercetin molecules thus influence the hydrogen-bonded network structure of water molecules present within their electrostatic and solvation fields. When these hydration spheres approach each other, overlapping occurs, potentially leading to the formation of solvent-shared complexes. Due to the proximity of solute and solvent molecules, a multitude of interactions manifest between the ionic, polar, and nonpolar segments of the IL, the flavonoid quercetin, and the polar water molecules. By considering the structure of the components, the following interactions in the ternary system are predicted:

i.Ion-hydrophilic interactions between cationic cholinium (Ch+) and anionic tryptophanate (Trp−) with water molecules.ii.Hydrophilic-hydrophilic interactions between the polar hydroxyl group (-OH) of cholinium, the amino and carboxylate groups of tryptophanate, and the multiple hydroxyl groups of quercetin with water.iii.Hydrogen bonding between the polar networks of tryptophan (indole ring) and quercetin (catechol and carbonyl groups) with water.iv.Hydrophilic-hydrophobic interactions between the nonpolar aliphatic segments of cholinium and the aromatic rings of both tryptophanate and quercetin with water.

The structure-making ability can be attributed to the complex interplay of ionic, hydrogen-bonding, and hydrophobic interactions within the ternary system, which collectively impose a more ordered arrangement on the surrounding water molecules.

Solubility studies of Quercetin in aqueous choline-tryptophan ionic liquid

The solubility of quercetin in distilled water and in aqueous [Ch][Trp] ionic liquid (0.1% v/v) was evaluated using the equilibrium solubility method (Fig. 3). As expected for a highly hydrophobic flavonol, quercetin exhibits very limited solubility in water-a trend widely reported across flavonoid systems due to their rigid planar polyphenolic structure, extensive intramolecular hydrogen bonding, and high lattice energy. Dong et al. have shown that flavonoids are generally least soluble in water and exhibit markedly enhanced solubility in ionic liquids possessing high hydrogen-bond basicity and strong solute–solvent interaction capabilities [17]. Our results align well with this established solubility behaviour. A pronounced increase in solubility was observed upon introducing only 0.1% v/v [Ch][Trp], confirming that the ionic liquid provides a highly interactive solvation environment even at dilute concentrations. The enhancement can be attributed to several cooperative molecular interactions. The hydroxyl groups of quercetin readily form hydrogen bonds with the choline cation (through –OH and –N+R4 functionalities) as well as with the carboxylate and indole groups of the tryptophan anion. These interactions effectively reduce the lattice energy of crystalline quercetin and stabilize the solute within the aqueous medium. This behaviour is consistent with previously reported hydrogen-bond-driven solubilization mechanisms observed in other IL-flavonoid systems, where IL basicity and charge distribution are identified as key factors governing dissolution.

In addition to hydrogen bonding, π-π stacking plays a significant role in the [Ch][Trp] system. The aromatic indole ring of the tryptophan anion engages in π-π interactions with the aromatic rings of quercetin, providing an additional stabilization pathway that is absent in non-aromatic amino-acid-based ILs. For instance, choline-lysine, choline-phenylalanine, and choline-glutamate ILs reported in the literature primarily enhance bioactive solubility through hydrogen-bond networks and hydrotropic organization, as demonstrated by Caparica et al. [11] and Chen et al. [18]. However, [Ch][Trp] possesses the added advantage of an indole ring, enabling stronger π-π stabilization and more efficient solute dispersion compared with non-aromatic analogues. Recent computational analyses of IL–flavonoid systems further highlight the cooperative role of hydrogen bonding and π-π interactions in promoting dissolution and modulating reactivity [17]. The amphiphilic nature of [Ch][Trp] likely contributes additional hydrotropic behaviour, disrupting quercetin aggregation and improving dispersion-an effect consistent with the behaviour of other IL-based hydrotropes reported for poorly water-soluble polyphenols. Overall, the results clearly demonstrate that even at a very low concentration (0.1% v/v), [Ch][Trp] enhances quercetin solubility by more than an order of magnitude, confirming its efficiency as a biocompatible, sustainable, and structurally complementary solubilizing agent. These findings reinforce the emerging understanding that amino-acid-based ILs serve as powerful green solvents capable of tuning solvation through targeted non-covalent interactions, offering considerable advantages for the formulation and processing of poorly water-soluble flavonoids.

Radical scavenging activity (RSA) determination

The radical scavenging response of quercetin toward the stable DPPH• radical was examined in the absence and presence of [Ch][Trp] IL at varying concentrations to assess how the ionic liquid modulates antioxidant behaviour (Fig. 4). Quercetin alone exhibited a scavenging efficiency of 74.76%, consistent with its well-established HAT/SET reactivity arising from the multiple phenolic hydroxyl groups capable of neutralising free radicals. Incorporation of [Ch][Trp] altered this behaviour in a concentration-dependent manner. At 0.18 mM [Ch][Trp] (final cuvette concentration 0.03 mM), the scavenging activity increased to 78.18%, indicating that at this concentration the IL provides a microenvironment that facilitates more efficient radical-antioxidant interaction. It should be noted that the RSA value of 78.18% corresponds to a direct, fixed-time DPPH measurement, where the reaction was assessed at the standard endpoint without monitoring its time evolution. However, decreasing the IL concentration to 0.12 mM (0.02 mM in the cuvette) and 0.06 mM (0.01 mM in the cuvette) resulted in slightly lower RSA values of 74.35% and 72.28%, respectively-values that fall at or below the activity of quercetin alone. These results show that the IL does not universally enhance quercetin’s antioxidant performance; instead, its effect is modulatory and dependent on IL concentration. Only at sufficiently high [Ch][Trp] concentrations is the local environment organised enough to enhance antioxidant behaviour, whereas sub-optimal concentrations appear to reduce or dilute the stabilising interactions required for efficient HAT/SET transfer. The improved RSA at 0.18 mM [Ch][Trp] can be attributed to cooperative non-covalent interactions between quercetin and the IL. The indole ring of the tryptophan anion likely participates in π-π stacking with quercetin’s aromatic rings, promoting electron delocalisation and stabilising the transient semiquinone intermediate formed during radical quenching. Simultaneously, hydrogen bonding between the anion’s -COO⁻ group and quercetin’s phenolic -OH groups may lower the activation energy required for proton or hydrogen-atom donation. The choline cation, with its hydroxyl-bearing hydrophilic headgroup, may further enhance solvation and orient quercetin in a geometry favourable for reaction with DPPH•. At lower IL concentrations, these beneficial structural interactions diminish due to the reduced number of IL-solute contact points. Insufficient IL structuring weakens the stabilisation of quercetin and its reactive intermediates, leading to less effective radical neutralisation.

The observed RSA trend follows the order:

Quercetin + 0.18 mM [Ch][Trp] > Quercetin only > Quercetin + 0.12 mM [Ch][Trp] > Quercetin + 0.06 mM [Ch][Trp].

This clearly establishes that [Ch][Trp] acts as a concentration-sensitive modulator, rather than a strictly enhancing agent. While imidazolium-based ionic liquids have been previously reported to enhance phenolic antioxidant behaviour through microstructural organisation, the mechanism observed here-improved molecular accessibility and intermediate stabilisation at optimal IL levels-is conceptually consistent with such systems. Importantly, [Ch][Trp] offers additional advantages of biocompatibility, low toxicity, and biodegradability, making it a promising green medium for tuning and optimising the antioxidant response of natural polyphenols.

Time-resolved radical scavenging of Quercetin in [Ch][Trp] medium

The temporal evolution of the DPPH radical scavenging by quercetin in the presence of 0.18 mM [Ch][Trp] IL was monitored spectrophotometrically to evaluate the kinetic behaviour of the antioxidant in this ionic liquid environment (Fig. 5a and b). The control DPPH solution exhibited an initial absorbance of 0.93 at 516.8 nm, which progressively decreased upon the addition of quercetin and 0.18mM [Ch][Trp] IL, indicating efficient radical quenching. The percentage of radical scavenging (%RSA) increased from 76.80% at 0 s to 79.46% at 150 s, 80.21% at 300 s, and gradually approached a steady value of ≈ 80.44% at 1050 s. This higher RSA value (~ 80.44%) arises from the time-resolved kinetic experiment, where the reaction was monitored from 0 to 1050 s, allowing the scavenging efficiency to reach its equilibrium plateau, which differs from the fixed-time endpoint value reported earlier. The observed time profile displays a rapid initial rise in RSA within the first 300 s, followed by a slow, asymptotic approach toward equilibrium. This biphasic pattern signifies the presence of two distinct kinetic regimes: (i) a fast primary reaction phase, corresponding to direct hydrogen-atom or single-electron transfer (HAT/SET) between quercetin and DPPH, and (ii) a slower diffusion-limited phase, governed by stabilization and reorganization within the reaction microenvironment. During the initial phase, quercetin molecules in the [Ch][Trp] medium effectively donate hydrogen atoms from their phenolic hydroxyl groups to neutralize DPPH radicals, forming the quercetin semiquinone intermediate. The presence of [Ch][Trp] enhances this step through favourable molecular interactions: π-π stacking between the indole ring of the tryptophan anion and the aromatic backbone of quercetin facilitates charge delocalization, while hydrogen bonding between the IL’s functional groups and the hydroxyl sites of quercetin lowers the activation barrier for hydrogen transfer. Moreover, the choline cation’s hydrophilic hydroxyl group organizes the solvent environment around quercetin, improving its orientation and accessibility toward DPPH radicals. The slower phase, observed beyond 300 s, reflects the completion and stabilization of the reaction system. At this stage, nearly all accessible DPPH radicals have been quenched, and the reaction rate becomes limited by the slow diffusion of any remaining radicals and by structural reorganization within the ionic liquid microdomain. The minimal increase in RSA after 600 s (Fig. 5a) indicates that the system has reached near-equilibrium, indicating sustained antioxidant stability over the observation window. Overall, the [Ch][Trp] medium not only enhances the magnitude of radical scavenging but also prolongs the stability of the antioxidant species formed during the reaction. The ionic liquid effectively acts as a micro structured solvent that accelerates the initial electron-transfer process while simultaneously stabilizing the reaction intermediates through cooperative hydrogen bonding and π-π interactions. This dual functionality distinguishes [Ch][Trp] from conventional organic or aqueous media and underscores its potential as a green kinetic promoter for improving the antioxidant behaviour of quercetin and related phenolic compounds.

Kinetic analysis of the DPPH–quercetin reaction in [Ch][Trp] medium

The kinetic behaviour of the DPPH• scavenging reaction by quercetin in the presence of 0.18 mM [Ch][Trp] IL was investigated under pseudo-first-order conditions, with DPPH• maintained in effective excess during the initial phase of the reaction. A semi-logarithmic plot of ln (At/A0) versus time (0-300 s) exhibited excellent linearity (R2 = 0.99725), confirming that the reaction follows pseudo-first-order kinetics within this interval (Fig. 6). The slope of the linear regression yielded an apparent rate constant of k = 2.43 × 10− 4 s− 1, corresponding to the rapid single electron-transfer or hydrogen-atom-transfer (SET/HAT) mechanism between quercetin and the DPPH radical. The strong linearity observed up to 300 s demonstrates that the reaction proceeds through a well-defined chemically controlled regime, where the rate is governed exclusively by intrinsic HAT/SET processes with negligible influence from diffusion or secondary steps. Such pseudo-first-order treatment is well supported in the literature: Butković et al. performed kinetic investigations of flavonols, including quercetin, reacting with DPPH• under pseudo-first-order conditions when the radical was used in excess, validating this modelling approach [19]. Beyond 300 s, only minimal changes in absorbance were observed, indicating that the system approaches a steady state. The negligible variation after 300 s confirms that the reaction transitions into a diffusion-controlled regime, dominated by slow residual radical mobility and stabilization effects within the ionic liquid microenvironment. This behaviour agrees with recent studies on DPPH kinetics in protic ionic liquids (PILs), where the pseudo-first-order constant (k1) strongly depends on IL concentration and solvent structuring [20]. In such systems, IL-mediated hydrogen bonding and π-π interactions reduce molecular mobility, restricting late-stage radical-antioxidant encounters [21]. The observed rate constant lies in the moderate 10− 4 s− 1 range. This magnitude indicates a kinetically controlled process that requires specific molecular orientation and stabilization, rather than a purely diffusion-driven mechanism. Such behaviour is characteristic of antioxidant-DPPH systems in structured or viscous solvents and is consistent with prior reports highlighting solvent micro structuring effects on reaction dynamics [10]. In the [Ch][Trp] medium, the indole moiety of the tryptophan anion can engage in π-π stacking with the aromatic rings of quercetin, while the carboxylate group and the hydroxyl group of the choline cation form stabilizing hydrogen bonds with quercetin’s phenolic -OH groups. These cooperative non-covalent interactions facilitate electron-transfer efficiency in the early stage and stabilize the semiquinone intermediate in the later stage, contributing to the distinct two-phase kinetic behaviour observed. Overall, the kinetic profile demonstrates that [Ch][Trp] acts as a green kinetic promoter, enhancing the early-stage radical-quenching rate while stabilizing antioxidant intermediates through solvent organization. The dual role of the ionic liquid-promoting primary SET/HAT reactivity and modulating late-stage diffusion-highlights the potential of amino-acid-based ionic liquids as sustainable media for tuning antioxidant kinetics in complex reaction environments.

UV-Visible absorption and fluorescence emission studies

We examined the photophysical behaviour of Quercetin in a 0.1% (v/v) solution of the [Ch][Trp]: water (5 mg quercetin per 10 mL) in order to elucidate how the IL medium affects its electronic transitions and excited-state dynamics. In the UV-Vis absorption spectrum (Fig. 7a), quercetin retains a band in the ~ 350–370 nm region, consistent with the well-characterised band I (B-ring cinnamoyl π-π* transition) of flavanols. Literature reports, for instance, place this band near 372–375 nm in methanol or aqueous media [22–24] (Table 4). Importantly, in our system we also detect a distinctive additional absorption feature centred around ~ 475 nm, a marked red-shift compared to conventional quercetin studies. We interpret this band as indicating the influence of the ionic-liquid microenvironment: the IL likely fosters strong π-π stacking between the indole moiety of the tryptophan anion and the aromatic rings of quercetin, fortifies hydrogen-bonding with phenolic -OH groups, and may even act to mediate a weak charge-transfer interaction between quercetin and [Ch][Trp] ions. Such interactions effectively extend conjugation or alter the electronic energy levels, thereby shifting absorption into the visible region. Similar bathochromic shifts have been reported for quercetin-metal complexes or flavanols in non-aqueous heterogeneous environments [25].

The fluorescence emission spectrum (Fig. 7b) complements this absorption behaviour: the quercetin/IL system exhibits enhanced emission centred around ~ 540–560 nm. The increased fluorescence intensity indicates that the IL medium not only solubilises the flavanol efficiently but also provides a more rigid solvation shell that suppresses non-radiative decay pathways and stabilises the excited state. In other words, the ionic liquid environment modifies the excited-state kinetics of quercetin, allowing it to emit more strongly and for a longer lifetime. Reports in the literature show that in aqueous buffer, quercetin’s emission maximum is approximately 538 nm and its fluorescence is weak and strongly quenched by interactions with proteins or solvents [26]. Taken together, absorption and fluorescence results demonstrate that [Ch][Trp] acts as more than a simple solvent-it actively modifies the photophysical behaviour of quercetin. While the preservation of the ~ 350–370 nm band confirms the integrity of the flavanol core transitions, the emergence of the ~ 475 nm absorption band and the enhanced fluorescence (540–560 nm) reflect strong IL-solute interactions that modulate conjugation and excited state dynamics. These findings underscore the capability of Choline-amino acid-based ionic liquids to finely tune not only solubility but also the optical and reactive properties of natural antioxidants-opening doors for designing functional green media that actively enhance bioactive behaviour.

FTIR spectral analysis of Quercetin in [Ch][Trp] IL-water system

The FTIR spectrum of quercetin in 0.1% (v/v) [Ch][Trp] IL: water (Fig. 8) provides valuable insight into the molecular interactions between quercetin and the ionic liquid microenvironment. The recorded spectrum shows prominent absorption bands at 3339.49, 2133.12, 1635.26, and 455.90 cm− 1, each of which can be correlated to specific vibrational modes that reflect hydrogen bonding and π-π interactions within the system. The broad and intense band at 3339.49 cm− 1 is characteristic of O-H stretching vibrations and signifies the presence of multiple hydrogen bonds among quercetin’s phenolic hydroxyl groups, water molecules, and the functional groups of the IL. Compared with the pure [Ch][Trp] IL spectrum as reported earlier in our previous study [9], this O-H stretching band is slightly broadened and shifted, indicating the formation of new or strengthened hydrogen bonds between the quercetin hydroxyl groups and the carboxylate and indole functionalities of the tryptophan anion. Such broadening effects have also been reported in hydrogen-bond-rich IL-flavonoid systems, confirming the strong solvation and structural reorganisation induced by ILs [10]. The band observed at 1635.26 cm− 1 corresponds to the stretching vibrations of the C = O group in the conjugated carbonyl of quercetin’s C-ring, coupled with C = C aromatic stretching modes. A minor red shift of this band relative to pure quercetin (typically ~ 1650 cm− 1) suggests participation of the carbonyl oxygen in hydrogen bonding with the IL cation or water molecules. This interaction likely contributes to the enhanced solubility and altered electronic transitions observed in the UV-Vis and fluorescence spectra. The appearance of a distinct band at 2133.12 cm− 1 can be attributed to weak overtone or combination bands, possibly involving N–H···O or C-N stretching from the IL’s tryptophan component, reflecting secondary interactions within the IL-quercetin matrix. Although not a principal vibration in quercetin, its presence implies specific IL-solute association effects. The low-frequency region shows a notable band at 455.90 cm− 1, assigned to out-of-plane bending modes of aromatic C-H and C-C skeleton vibrations. The persistence and slight enhancement of this band suggest stabilization of quercetin’s aromatic framework through π-π stacking with the indole ring of [Trp]−. Similar low-wavenumber interactions have been noted in π-interacting ionic liquids and flavonoid complexes. Collectively, the FTIR data indicate the coexistence of hydrogen bonding, π-π stacking, and electrostatic interactions between quercetin and the [Ch][Trp] IL components. These interactions underpin the structural stabilization and electronic modifications observed in the optical studies (Table 5). Thus, the FTIR analysis substantiates the role of [Ch][Trp] as a multifunctional solvent medium-one that not only enhances solubility but also actively reorganises the hydrogen-bonding network surrounding the bioactive molecule.

Conclusion and future perspectives

This study provides a comprehensive understanding of the physicochemical and kinetic behavior of quercetin in the presence of the bio-derived ionic liquid [Ch][Trp]. The volumetric and spectroscopic analyses reveal that [Ch][Trp] functions as an effective, environmentally benign solvent medium that enhances quercetin’s solubility and radical-scavenging capacity. The positive Hepler’s constants confirm the structure-making nature of the IL, arising from extensive hydrogen bonding and π-π interactions between the tryptophan anion and the aromatic core of quercetin. The kinetic investigation demonstrates that the DPPH-quercetin reaction in [Ch][Trp] follows a pseudo–first-order model, characterized by a linear correlation between ln (At/A0) and time (R2 = 0.99725) and an apparent rate constant of 2.36 × 10− 4 s− 1 within the 0–300 s interval. The results indicate that the reaction proceeds through a HAT and/or SET mechanism, dominated by a rapid primary phase followed by a stabilization regime governed by molecular diffusion and microstructural reorganization. The relatively small rate constant confirms that the process is kinetically controlled rather than diffusion-limited, while the [Ch][Trp] medium enhances electron-transfer efficiency through cooperative hydrogen bonding and π-π stacking. Overall, [Ch][Trp] acts as a dual-function medium, serving both as a solubilizing agent and a kinetic modulator, by accelerating the primary radical-quenching step and stabilizing the intermediate species through a structured solvation network. These results highlight the potential of amino-acid-based ILs as green designer solvents capable of tuning the thermodynamic stability and reactivity of natural antioxidants such as quercetin. To contextualize these findings, comparison with other amino-acid-based ILs highlights the unique advantages of [Ch][Trp]. The superior ability of [Ch][Trp] to modulate quercetin’s solubility, photo physics, and radical-scavenging behaviour therefore arises from the synergistic combination of these structural features. The findings of this work open new avenues for the rational design of bio-inspired ionic liquids to enhance the functionality and sustainability of natural biomolecules. By exploiting specific ion-solute interactions, future studies can tailor the balance between hydrophobic, hydrogen-bonding, and π-interaction domains to optimize both kinetic and thermodynamic performance. Such ILs may find broad applications in pharmaceutical formulations, nutraceutical preservation, antioxidant-based drug delivery, and biocatalytic systems. In a broader context, this study demonstrates how IL engineering can be leveraged to create next-generation green solvents that not only replace conventional organic media but also actively modulate chemical reactivity. The UV-Vis and fluorescence data demonstrate that the IL microenvironment does more than increase solubility-it shifts absorption into the visible region and enhances emission intensity of quercetin. Meanwhile, FTIR analysis confirms that hydrogen-bonding, π-π stacking and electrostatic interactions with the IL underpin these photophysical changes. Together, these findings highlight the multifunctional nature of [Ch][Trp] as both solvent and active modulator of molecular behaviour, paving the way for tailored ionic liquids that control both photo-physics and reactivity of natural antioxidants. The mechanistic insights presented here provide a blueprint for designing task-specific ILs that couple sustainability with functionality-an essential step toward a more efficient and environmentally responsible chemical future.

Forward-Backward-forward Dynamical System for Solving Mixed Variational Inequality problems.
Chidi Elijah Nwakpa 1 , {}^{1}, Chinedu Izuchukwu 2 , {}^{2}, Chibueze CHristian Okeke 3 . {}^{3}.

Abstract
. We study in this paper a forward-backward-forward dynamical system for solving mixed variational inequality problem in a real Hilbert space. For the convergence analysis of our proposed system, we apply the Lyapunov analysis to obtain the weak convergence of the generated trajectories when the associated operator is Lipschitz continuous and satisfies the general monotonicity condition. We also assume that the involved real-valued convex function satisfies some mild assumptions. Furthermore, the Lipschitz continuous operator is taken to be h − h- strongly pseudomonotone to establish the global exponential stability of the equilibrium point of the system for all the orbits generated. Finally, we present some numerical examples which illustrate how the trajectories of the proposed system converge to the equilibrium point of the proposed dynamical system.

Introduction

The mixed variational inequality theory, in its diverse forms, serves as a mathematical model for several interesting phenomena occurring in various research fields. The extension and generalization of the mixed variational inequality (briefly, MVI) to study a class of linear and nonlinear problems, which arise in various fields such as engineering, fluid flow through porous media, transportation models, image restoration, electronics, economics, financial analysis, physics, optimization, and others, have made the MVI a unifying mathematical formulation for finding approximate solutions to a class of linear and nonlinear operators

[
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
20
]
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space endowed with the inner product function
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the corresponding norm operator
\|\cdot\|,
and
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
a nonempty closed convex set in
\mathcal{H}.
Let
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a Lipschitz continuous operator and given
h:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}=[-\infty,+\infty],
a proper, lower semicontinuous real-valued function, we consider the problem of finding
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}\bar{x},\underline{u}-\bar{x}\rangle+h(\underline{u})-h(\bar{x})\geq 0\ \ \forall\underline{u}\in\mathcal{C}.

The problem described by (
1.1
) is called the mixed variational inequality problem or variational inequality of the second kind, and in this work, we are going to approach the MVI(
1.1
) from a continuous perspective.

Let us denote
S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}:=\{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}~:~\langle\mathcal{T}(\bar{x}),\underline{u}-\bar{x}\rangle+h(\underline{u})-h(\bar{x})\geq 0\ \ \forall\underline{u}\in\mathcal{C}\}
to be the set of solutions of the MIV(
1.1
).
Note that the function
h
in (
1.1
) may not necessarily be differentiable. In particular,
h
may correspond to the indicator function
\delta_{\mathcal{C}}
of a nonempty, closed convex subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{H}
, which may fail to possess the necessary regularity conditions for differentiability to hold. That is,
h=\delta_{\mathcal{C}},
where

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{u})=\begin{cases}0,\ \ \mbox{if}\ \underline{u}\in\mathcal{C},\\
\infty,\ \ \mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In this scenario, the MVI(
1.1
) becomes the classical variational inequality problem (VIP) of Stampacchia
[
25
]
, which is:

find
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}(\bar{x}),\underline{u}-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0\ \ \mbox{for each}\ \underline{u}\in\mathcal{C}.

Similarly, MVI(
1.1
) reduces to the constrained optimization problem of minimizing
h
over
\mathcal{C}
if
\mathcal{T}\equiv 0
(see,
[
17
,
24
]
).

Furthermore, since MVI generalizes other models, a good number of methods have been proposed by several scholars as a means of finding an approximate solution to the problem (
1.1
). Over the years, these effective numerical methods include the proximal methods, extragradient methods, golden ratio methods, and others (for example, see
[

,

,

,

22
,
23
]
). However, one valuable tool stands out—the proximal operator—whose application has also been found useful in addressing numerous other numerical problems. It is important to note that the proximal operator is a strongly convex operator and hence, it guarantees a unique solution. Also, if the function
h
is convex (which it is in our case) then MVI(
1.1
) becomes a convex mixed variational inequality problem which can be best solved by the proximal type methods (see, for instance
[
9
,
26
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
), otherwise, problem (
1.1
) becomes a non-convex MVI problem.

Among these methods for finding an approximate solution of the MVI(
1.1
), the most popular method is the proximal type method. This method, according to Garg
et al.
in
[
12
]
, generates, when given an initial point
x(0)\in\mathcal{H},
a trajectory
x(t)
that approaches a point in the solution set
S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})},
and is given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=-\delta\big(x(t)-prox_{\lambda h}\circ(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\lambda\mathcal{T})(x(t))\big),

where
\dot{x}(t)=\dfrac{d}{dt}(x(t))

is the derivative of the mapping
t\longmapsto x(t)
at any time
t,

I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity operator in
\mathcal{H},

prox_{\lambda h}(\cdot):=\underset{\underline{v}\in\mathcal{C}}{arg\min}\left\{\lambda h(\underline{v})+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot-\underline{v}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right\}
is the proximal mapping of
\lambda h,

\delta>0,
while
\lambda>0
is the stepsize.
The author in
[
2
]
noted that the continuity condition of
prox_{\lambda h}\circ(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\lambda\mathcal{T})(x(t))
ensures the existence of a solution of (
1.3
) on a finite interval. It is also important to note that, if
prox_{\lambda h}\circ(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\lambda\mathcal{T})(x(t))
satisfies the Lipschitz condition, then the trajectory exists and is unique on the infinite time interval
[0,+\infty)
. This is because the operator
prox_{\lambda h}\circ(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\lambda\mathcal{T})(x(t))
is strongly convex.

Furthermore, the authors in
[
2
,
16
]
noted that the explicit time discretization of (
1.3
) with respect to the time variable
t
and without the relaxation parameter, that is, when
\delta\hbar_{n}=1
gives

(1.4)

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=prox_{\lambda h}(x_{n}-\lambda\mathcal{T}(x_{n})),\ \ \forall n\geq 0.

And in a case of VIP, the authors in
[
5
,
33
]
established that (
1.4
) converges if
\mathcal{T}
is a co-coercive operator (inverse strongly monotone), while the authors in
[
10
,
19
]
extended the convergence of the sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
generated by (
1.4
) to the strongly (pseudo-) monotone operator
\mathcal{T}.

The extragradient method (also called the Korpelevich’s extragradient method) is known to be the mostly used method in solving MVI(
1.1
) whenever the operator
\mathcal{T}
is monotone and Lipschitz continuous. In this method, two proximal evaluations are computed in each iteration.
Although, this method was originally designed for solving monotone MVIs (in particular, monotone VIPs) iteratively in finite dimensional spaces, however,
[
10
, Theorem 12.2.11]
showed that the convergence of the trajectories
x(t),\ y(t)
(whose analog iterative sequences are given by
x_{n}
and

respectively) is also attainable if the operator
\mathcal{T}
is pseudomonotone. Interestingly, many researchers have further extended this method to the infinite dimensional spaces, thus enabling the reduction in the number of proximal evaluations onto the feasible set over each iteration
[
7
,
8
,
18
,
19
,
29
,
30
]
. Example of such proximal-type method that requires lesser proximal evaluation in each evaluation is the Tseng’s extragradient method. This method requires single proximal evaluation onto the feasible set over each iteration and is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y(t)=prox_{\lambda h}(x(t)-\lambda\mathcal{T}(x(t)))\\
\dot{x}(t)=y(t)-\lambda(\mathcal{T}(y(t))-\mathcal{T}(x(t)))\\
x(0)=x_{0}\\
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\ \ 0<\lambda<\dfrac{1}{\beta},\ \ t\geq 0,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is monotone and
\beta-
Lipschitz continuous. Recently, Abbas and Attouch
[
1
, Section 5.2]
proposed the following dynamical system

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)+x(t)=prox_{\lambda h}(x(t)-\lambda\mathcal{T}(x(t)))\\
x(0)=x_{0}\\
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\ \ \lambda>0,\ \ t\geq 0.\end{cases}

The authors established the weak convergence of the trajectory
x(t)
of (
1.6
) when the operator
\mathcal{T}
is a co-coercive operator, and the stepsize
\lambda>0
is selected in a suitable domain bounded by the parameter of co-coercivity of the operator
\mathcal{T}.
Similarly, Bo
\c{t}
and Csetnek
[
6
]
in their work studied the existence and uniqueness of (locally) absolutely continuous trajectories of a dynamical system of a nonexpansive operator and established the weak convergence of the orbits to the solutions set.

However, in order to improve on the computational structure of methods that solve optimization problems, Bo
\c{t}

et al.
in
[
7
]
proposed the Tseng’s forward-backward-forward dynamical system for solving a pseudomonotone variational inequality (
1.2
) and obtained a weak convergence result and the global exponential stability of the generated trajectories under the assumptions of pseudomonotonicity and strong pseudomonotonicity of the associated Lipschitz operator. The Tseng’s forward-backward-forward algorithm provides a computationally efficient alternative to Korpelevich’s extragradient method for solving a convex optimization problems on closed sets. As mentioned earlier, Tseng’s algorithm requires only a single projection operation per iteration, thereby reducing computational complexity. This advantage makes Tseng’s algorithm an attractive choice for addressing MVIPs governed by monotone and Lipschitz continuous operators.

Motivated by the works in the literature, we propose a dynamical system for finding a solution of the MVI(
1.1
) with the following contributions:

•

Our proposed system features a single proximal evaluation (projection evaluation in a case where
h
is the indicator function) in each step;

•

We obtain a weak convergence result for the generated orbits to a solution of MVI under the general monotonicity condition;

•

Under the strong pseudomonotonicity, we establish the global exponential stability of the equilibrium point of the system studied;

•

Numerical examples are provided to demonstrate the convergence of the generated trajectories to the equilibrium point of the system under consideration.

The rest of the content of this paper is arranged as follows: In Section

presents some numerical results which serve as practical illustrations of the convergence of the generated orbits to the equilibrium point of our system.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
h:[0,b]\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
(where
0<b<+\infty
) is said to be absolutely continuous if one of the following properties is true:

(1)

there is an integrable function
\phi:[0,b]\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle h(t)=h(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(x)dx\ \ \ \forall t\in[0,b];

(ii)

h
is continuous and its distributional derivative belongs to the Lebesgue space
L^{1}([0,b];\mathcal{H}).
That is, the distributional derivative of
h
is Lebesgue integrable on
[0,b];

(iii)

for any
\epsilon>0,
there is
\delta>0
such that for every finite family of intervals
I_{j}=(a_{j},b_{j}),
the following holds

\displaystyle\left(I_{i}\cap I_{j}=\emptyset~\mbox{for}~i\neq j~\mbox{and}~\sum_{j}|b_{j}-a_{j}|<\delta\right)\implies\sum_{j}\|h(b_{j})-h(a_{j})\|<\epsilon.

Remark 2.2
.

[

,
6
]

(a)

It indeed ensues immediately from the definition above that whenever a function is absolutely continuous then, it is differentiable almost everywhere, and in this situation, its distributional derivatives coincide with its derivative, hence, by the integration formula (
2.1
), we can easily recover the function from its derivative
h^{\prime}=\phi.

(b)

By considering the characterization in Definition

is satisfied almost everywhere.

the solutions set,

III.

x(0)=x_{0}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that C
1
—C
4
of Assumption
3.1
are satisfied by the operator
\mathcal{T}
and the real-valued function
h.
Then, the following holds for each
\bar{x}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}

\displaystyle\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-\bar{x}\rangle\leq-[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})]\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}\ \ \forall t\in[0,+\infty).

Proof.

Since C
1
of Assumption
3.1
holds, that is,
S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}\neq\emptyset.
Let
\bar{x}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})},
then for every
y(t)\in\mathcal{C},
it follows that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}\bar{x},y(t)-\bar{x}\rangle+h(y(t))-h(\bar{x})\geq 0\ \ \ \ \forall t\in[0,+\infty).

Since
\mathcal{T}
and
h
satisfy the general monotonicity condition on
\mathcal{C}.
That is, C
4
of Assumption
3.1
is satisfied, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}(y(t)),y(t)-\bar{x}\rangle+h(y(t))-h(\bar{x})\geq 0\ \ \forall y(t)\in\mathcal{C},\ \ \forall\bar{x}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})},\ \ t\in[0,+\infty).

Using the definition of
y(t)=prox_{\lambda h}(x(t)-\lambda\mathcal{T}(x(t))),
then by Lemma
2.7
we have that

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-\lambda\mathcal{T}(x(t))-y(t),\bar{x}-y(t)\rangle

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left[h(\bar{x})-h(y(t))\right]\ \ \forall y(t)\in\mathcal{C},\ \forall\bar{x}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})},\ t\in[0,+\infty).

From (
3.3
), we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda\mathcal{T}(y(t)),\bar{x}-y(t)\rangle\leq\lambda\left[h(y(t))-h(\bar{x})\right].

Combining (
3
) and (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-y(t)-\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\right],y(t)-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0.

By the definition of
\dot{x}(t)
in dynamical system (
3.2
), we have from the last inequality that

(3.6)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-y(t)-\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\right],y(t)-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-y(t)-\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\right],y(t)-x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\;+\;\langle x(t)-y(t)-\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\right],x(t)-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-y(t)-\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\right],y(t)-x(t)\rangle-\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-\bar{x}\rangle.

Using the fact that
\mathcal{T}
is
\beta-
Lipschitz continuous, then (
3.6
) gives us that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-y(t)-\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\right],y(t)-x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}+\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t)),x(t)-y(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}(1+\beta^{2})\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}+\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}\ \ \ \ \forall t\in[0,+\infty).

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
C_{1}-C_{4}
hold,
and
0<\lambda<\dfrac{1}{1+\beta^{2}}.
Then, for each
\bar{x}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})},
the function
t\longmapsto\|x(t)-\bar{x}\|^{2}
is nonincreasing, that is, the trajectory
x(t)
is bounded and convergent. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\|x(t)-y(t)\|=0.

Proof.

Recall from Lyapunov analysis that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\|x(t)-\bar{x}\|^{2}=2\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-\bar{x}\rangle.

Using (
3.7
) in (
3.8
), we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\|x(t)-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq-2\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}\ \ \forall t\in[0,+\infty).

Since
0<\lambda<\dfrac{1}{1+\beta^{2}},
it follows that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\|x(t)-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq 0\ \ \forall t\in[0,+\infty).
Hence,
t\longmapsto\|x(t)-\bar{x}\|^{2}
is a nonincreasing function for each
t\in[0,+\infty).
Thus the trajectory
x(t)
is bounded.

On the other hand, from (
3.9
), we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle 2\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}dt\leq-d\left[\|x(t)-\bar{x}\|^{2}\right].

Integrating (
3.10
) from
0
to
T,
where
T>0,
gives

(3.11)

\displaystyle 2\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\int_{0}^{T}\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x(0)-\bar{x}\|^{2}-\|x(T)-\bar{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x(0)-\bar{x}\|^{2}.

Letting
T\longrightarrow+\infty
in (
3.11
), then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}dt\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{1}{1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})}\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\|^{2}<+\infty.

Next, we show that
\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\|x(t)-y(t)\|=0.

Note that
prox_{\lambda h}
is nonexpansive and
\mathcal{T}
is
\beta-
Lipschitz continuous. Recall from Lemma
2.11
that
prox_{\lambda h}\circ(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\lambda\mathcal{T})
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
(1+\lambda\beta).
Using the definition of
y(t),
that is

y(t)=prox_{\lambda h}\circ(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\lambda\mathcal{T})(x(t)),

we have that the trajectory
y(t)
is locally absolutely continuous, hence by Remark
2.2
b, it follows for almost all
t\geq 0,
that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\dot{y}(t)\|\leq(1+\lambda\beta)\|\dot{x}(t)\|.

On the other hand, using the definition of
\dot{x}(t),
we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\dot{x}(t)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|y(t)-x(t)+\lambda\left[\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x(t)-y(t)\|+\lambda\|\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\lambda\beta)\|x(t)-y(t)\|\ \ \ \ \ \forall t\in[0,\infty).

Therefore, using the Lyapunov analysis, Cauchy inequality, and inequalities (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we obtain for almost every
t\in[0,+\infty)
that

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle\dot{x}(t)-\dot{y}(t),x(t)-y(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\dot{x}(t)-\dot{y}(t)\|\|x(t)-y(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\|\dot{y}(t)\|)\|x(t)-y(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left[(1+\lambda\beta)\|x(t)-y(t)\|+(1+\lambda\beta)^{2}\|x(t)-y(t)\|\right]\|x(t)-y(t)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[(1+\lambda\beta)(2+\lambda\beta)\right]\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}.

Hence, we conclude from Lemma
2.8
that

\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\|x(t)-y(t)\|=0.

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.1
is satisfied and
0<\lambda<\dfrac{1}{1+\beta^{2}}.
Then, the trajectories
x(t)
and
y(t)
generated by the dynamical system (
3.2
) converges weakly to a point in
S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})},
as
t\to+\infty.

Proof.

Let
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{H}
be a weak sequential cluster point of
x(t)
as
t\to+\infty.
For each
n\geq 0,
let
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence in
[0,+\infty)
with
t_{n}\to+\infty
and
x(t_{n})\rightharpoonup\bar{c},
as
n\to+\infty.

Since
\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\|x(t)-y(t)\|=0,
we also have that
y(t_{n})\rightharpoonup\bar{c}
as
n\to+\infty.
Indeed,
\|\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n}))-\mathcal{T}(y(t_{n}))\|\to 0
as
n\to+\infty,
since
\mathcal{T}
is Lipschitz continuous.

We now show that
\bar{c}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}.

Since the sequence
\{y(t_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}
is weakly closed, we have that
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{C}.

Let
y\in\mathcal{C}
be fixed. For each
n\geq 0,
we have

y(t_{n})=prox_{\lambda h}(x(t_{n})-\lambda\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n}))).

Therefore, by (
3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\langle x(t_{n})-\lambda\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n}))-y(t_{n}),y-y(t_{n})\rangle\leq\lambda\left[h(y)-h(y(t_{n}))\right].

Or equivalently

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lambda\left[h(y(t_{n}))-h(y)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle y(t_{n})-x(t_{n})+\lambda\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n})),y-y(t_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle y(t_{n})-x(t_{n}),y-y(t_{n})\rangle+\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n})),y-y(t_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle y(t_{n})-x(t_{n}),y-y(t_{n})\rangle+\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n})),y-x(t_{n})\rangle+\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n})),x(t_{n})-y(t_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle y(t_{n})-x(t_{n}),y-y(t_{n})\rangle+\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}y,y-x(t_{n})\rangle+\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t_{n})),x(t_{n})-y(t_{n})\rangle,

where the last inequality is obtained using the fact that
\mathcal{T}
is a monotone operator (
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the general monotonicity condition).
Letting
n\longrightarrow+\infty
in (
3.14
) (if necessary, the sub trajectories), noting that
\|x(t_{n})-y(t_{n})\|\longrightarrow 0,

\mathcal{T}
is sequentially weakly continuous on
\mathcal{C},

h
is a lower semicontinuous function and
\{y(t_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
is bounded, therefore, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}y,y-\bar{c}\rangle+h(y)-h(\bar{c})\geq 0.

Given any arbitrary point
z\in\mathcal{C},
let
y_{\sigma}=\sigma z+(1-\sigma)\bar{c},
where
\sigma\in(0,1),\ \bar{c}\in\mathcal{C}.
Then,
y_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{C},
since
\mathcal{C}
is convex.

Now, put
y=y_{\sigma}
in (
3.15
), we obtain

(3.16)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}y_{\sigma},y_{\sigma}-\bar{c}\rangle+h(y_{\sigma})-h(\bar{c})\geq 0.

Hence, since
h
is convex, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}y_{\sigma},z-\bar{c}\rangle+h(z)-h(\bar{c})\geq 0.

Taking limit as
\sigma\longrightarrow 0
in (
3.17
) and using the fact that
\mathcal{T}
is Lipschitz, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}\bar{c},z-\bar{c}\rangle+h(z)-h(\bar{c})\geq 0.

Since
z\in\mathcal{C}
is arbitrarily chosen, we have that
\bar{c}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.5
, we have that
\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|
exists for each
\bar{c}\in S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}
. Hence, by Lemma
2.9
, the trajectory
x(t)
converges weakly to an element in the solution set,
S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}
as
t\longrightarrow+\infty.
Again, by Lemma
2.9
, we have that

\displaystyle\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\|x(t)-y(t)\|=0,

we immediately have that the trajectory
y(t)
also converges weakly to the same element in the solution set,
S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})},
as
t\longrightarrow+\infty.

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is
h-
strongly pseudomonotone on
\mathcal{C}
with modulus
\mu>0
and Lipschitz continuous with constant
\beta>0,
and
0<\lambda<\dfrac{1}{1+\beta^{2}}.
Assume that
h
is a proper, lower semicontinuous convex real-valued function. Then for each
t\in(0,+\infty),
the unique solution
\bar{c}
of the MVI (
1.1
), which is also the equilibrium point of the dynamical system (
3.2
), is globally exponentially stable for all the solutions
x(t).

That is,

\displaystyle\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}\leq\|x(0)-\bar{c}\|^{2}e^{-\alpha t}\ \ \forall t\in[0,+\infty),

where
\alpha=2\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\left(\dfrac{\lambda\mu}{1+\lambda(\mu+\beta)}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{C}
be a solution to MVI (
1.1
) be fixed for all
t\in(0,+\infty).
Since
y(t)\in\mathcal{C},
we obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}(\bar{c}),y(t)-\bar{c}\rangle+h(y(t))-h(\bar{c})\geq 0.

By the
h-
strong pseudomonotonicity of
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{C},
we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}(y(t)),y(t)-\bar{c}\rangle+h(y(t))-h(\bar{c})\geq\mu\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}.

On the other hand, using the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{T}
and inequality (
3.19
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t)),\bar{c}-y(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t)),\bar{c}-y(t)\rangle-\langle\mathcal{T}(y(t)),y(t)-\bar{c}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(x(t))-\mathcal{T}(y(t))\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|+h(y(t))-h(\bar{c})-\mu\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\|x(t)-y(t)\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|+h(y(t))-h(\bar{c})-\mu\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}.

This implies

(3.20)

\displaystyle\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t)),\bar{c}-y(t)\rangle\leq\lambda\beta\|x(t)-y(t)\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|+\lambda\left[h(y(t))-h(\bar{c})\right]-\lambda\mu\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}.

From (
3
), we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-y(t),\bar{c}-y(t)\rangle-\lambda\langle\mathcal{T}(x(t)),\bar{c}-y(t)\rangle\leq\lambda\left[h(\bar{c})-h(y(t))\right].

Combining (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle x(t)-y(t),\bar{c}-y(t)\rangle\leq\lambda\beta\|x(t)-y(t)\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|-\lambda\mu\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}.

This implies

\displaystyle\lambda\mu\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\beta\|x(t)-y(t)\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|+\langle x(t)-y(t),y(t)-\bar{c}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\beta\|x(t)-y(t)\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|+\|x(t)-y(t)\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\lambda\beta)\|x(t)-y(t)\|\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|.

This further gives

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|\leq\dfrac{1+\lambda\beta}{\lambda\mu}\|x(t)-y(t)\|.

Observe that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|\leq\|x(t)-y(t)\|+\|y(t)-\bar{c}\|.

Thus, by (
3.22
), inequality (
3.23
) gives

\displaystyle\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x(t)-y(t)\|+\dfrac{1+\lambda\beta}{\lambda\mu}\|x(t)-y(t)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1+\lambda(\mu+\beta)}{\lambda\mu}\|x(t)-y(t)\|.

That is

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|x(t)-y(t)\|\geq\dfrac{\lambda\mu}{1+\lambda(\mu+\beta)}\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|.

Consider the Lyapunov function
\varphi(t)=\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.4
and (
3.24
), we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle\dot{x}(t)-\bar{c},x(t)-\bar{c}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\|x(t)-y(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\left(\dfrac{\lambda\mu}{1+\lambda(\mu+\beta)}\right)^{2}\|x(t)-\bar{c}\|^{2}.

Thus, by Lemma
2.10
, we have

\displaystyle\|x(t)-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq\|x(0)-\bar{c}\|^{2}e^{-\alpha t},

where
\alpha=2\left[1-\lambda(1+\beta^{2})\right]\left(\dfrac{\lambda\mu}{1+\lambda(\mu+\beta)}\right)^{2}.

Since
x(t)
is arbitrary, hence, by Definition
2.3
(IV), the equilibrium point
\bar{c}
of system (
3.2
) is globally exponentially stable for all solutions
x(t),

t\geq 0.

∎

Remark 3.8
.

It is important to note that when the dynamical system (
3.2
) is explicitly discretized with respect to the time variable
t,
then, with stepsize
\hbar_{n}>0
and a starting point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},
it will produce the following iterative method:

\displaystyle\dfrac{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}{\hbar_{n}}+x_{n}=prox_{\lambda h}(x_{n}-\lambda\mathcal{T}x_{n})+\lambda\mathcal{T}x_{n}-\lambda[\mathcal{T}(prox_{\lambda h}(x_{n}-\lambda\mathcal{T}x_{n}))]\ \ \ \forall n\geq 0.

By letting
y_{n}:=prox_{\lambda h}(x_{n}-\lambda\mathcal{T}x_{n}),
then, this method can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{n}=prox_{\lambda h}(x_{n}-\lambda\mathcal{T}x_{n})\\
\\
x_{n+1}=(1-\hbar_{n})x_{n}+\hbar_{n}[y_{n}+\lambda(\mathcal{T}x_{n}-\mathcal{T}y_{n})]\ \ \ \forall n\geq 0,\end{cases}

which is exactly the Tseng’s forward-backward-forward method with
\{{\hbar_{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}
as the relaxation parameter.
But, in a case where
\hbar_{n}=1,
then, we have the following

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{n}=prox_{\lambda h}(x_{n}-\lambda\mathcal{T}x_{n})\\
\\
x_{n+1}=y_{n}+\lambda[\mathcal{T}x_{n}-\mathcal{T}y_{n}]\ \ \ \forall n\geq 0,\end{cases}

which is the classical proximal forward-backward-forward method for solving MVI(
1.1
) and other optimization problems proposed in
[
27
]
.

Numerical Experiments

We present some numerical experiments of our proposed dynamical system (
3.2
) in this section. The numerical implementation is carried out using MATLAB 2023(b), running on a personal computer equipped with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-10210U CPU at 2.30GHz and 8.00 GB RAM.

Example 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=[3,5].
Define
h:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle h(\underline{u})=\begin{cases}\underline{u}^{2},\ \ \mbox{if}\ \underline{u}\in\mathcal{C},\\
+\infty,\ \ \mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\mathcal{T}(\underline{u})=4-\underline{u}\ \ \ \ \forall\underline{u}\in\mathbb{R}.

Where the inner product function
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{R}
is defined by
\langle\underline{u},\underline{v}\rangle=\underline{u}\cdot\underline{v}
for each
\underline{u},\underline{v}\in\mathbb{R}.
consider the MVI of finding
\bar{x}\in dom\ h
such that

\langle\mathcal{T}\underline{u},\underline{v}-\underline{u}\rangle+h(\underline{v})-h(\underline{u})\geq 0,\ \ \forall\underline{v}\in dom~h.

In what that follows, we show that
C_{1}-C_{3}
of Assumption
3.1
are satisfied, thus:

(1)

It can easily be checked that the solution set
S^{MVI(\mathcal{T};h;\mathcal{C})}
is nonempty. In fact,
\bar{x}=3
is the unique solution of the MVI.

(2)

It is clear that
\mathcal{T}
is
1-
Lipschitz continuous.

(3)

Clearly, the function
h
is proper and convex. In addition, since
|h(\underline{u})-h(\underline{v})|=|\underline{u}^{2}-\underline{v}^{2}|\leq|\underline{u}+\underline{v}||\underline{u}-\underline{v}|\leq 10|\underline{u}-\underline{v}|,
thus
h
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
10.
Now, we show that the
h-
pseudomonotonicity of
\mathcal{T}
does not necessarily guarantee the pseudomonotonicity of
\mathcal{T}.

Suppose

\langle\mathcal{T}\underline{u},\underline{v}-\underline{u}\rangle+h(\underline{v})-h(\underline{u})\geq 0,\ \ \forall\underline{v}\in[3,5].

That is,

(\underline{v}-\underline{u})(4+\underline{v})\geq 0.

But
4+\underline{v}>0,
since
\underline{v}\in[3,5].
This further implies that
(\underline{v}-\underline{u})\geq 0.
Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}\underline{v},\underline{v}-\underline{u}\rangle+h(\underline{v})-h(\underline{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\underline{v}-\underline{u})(4+\underline{u})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0,

which shows that
\mathcal{T}
is
h-
pseudomonotone on
dom~h.
However, if we take
\underline{u}=3
and
\underline{v}=5,
then, we have that
\langle\mathcal{T}\underline{u},\underline{v}-\underline{u}\rangle=2>0,
and
\langle\mathcal{T}\underline{v},\underline{v}-\underline{u}\rangle=-2<0,
showing that
\mathcal{T}
is not pseudomonotone let alone monotone.

.

\mathcal{C}=\{x\in[-5,5]^{3}~:~x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}

and strongly pseudomonotone on

with constant

5.
Conclusion

In this work, we applied the Tseng’s forward-backward-forward dynamical system to solve a convex MVIP. We established that the generated trajectories of the system considered weakly convergence to a solution of the MVI when the associated operator is monotone and Lipschitz and the real-valued function is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous. In general, the Lipschitz operator and the real-valued function satisfy the general monotonicity condition. In addition, the global exponential stability of the equilibrium point of the dynamical system studied is obtained under the
h-
strongly pseudomonotonicity condition. Lyapunov-type analysis played a key role in the establishment of the convergence results. Numerical experiments are also presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the theoretical findings. Part of our future research is to further investigate this dynamical system in a stochastic setting.

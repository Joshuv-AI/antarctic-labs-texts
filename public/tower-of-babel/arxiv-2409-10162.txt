Quantum codes for asymmetric channels: ZZZY surface codes
Diego Forlivesi, Lorenzo Valentini, and Marco Chiani

Abstract
We introduce surface ZZZY codes, a novel family of quantum error-correcting codes designed for asymmetric channels. Derived from standard surface codes through tailored modification of generators, ZZZY codes can be decoded by the MWPM ( MWPM ) algorithm with a suitable pre-processing phase. The resulting decoder exploits the information provided by the modified generators without introducing additional complexity. ZZZY codes demonstrate a significant performance advantage over surface codes when increasing the channel asymmetry, while maintaining the same correction capability over depolarizing channel.

An example of this approach are the XZZX surface codes, designed to address scenarios where qubit dephasing is the primary noise source

In these codes, each ancilla measures according to
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}

\textstyle{\mathchar 90}

In this letter, we propose new quantum codes, named ZZZY surface codes, in which a few Pauli

To preserve the use of the

Throughout the paper, we will delve into the details of the decoder, providing illustrative examples to support its description.

In decoding ZZZY codes, we must adapt the standard
MWPM
algorithm to leverage the insights gained from
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
measurements. Notably, as surface codes fall under the category of
CSS
codes, the decoding process for
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
generators operates independently from that for
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
generators
[
23
]
.
Consequently, the
MWPM
can be divided into two phases:
MWPM
X
, focusing solely on
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
generators, followed by
MWPM
Z
for the
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
stabilizers.
As detailed in Section
III
,
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
generators offer insights into certain
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
errors. However, without careful handling, they can erroneously trigger
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
error detections. Take, for example, Fig
1
, where a
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{1}
error activates ancillas
A_{1}
and
A_{3}
. Neglecting to deactivate ancilla
A_{3}
before
MWPM
Z
would falsely attribute an additional
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 88}$}}}_{1}
error.
To address this, we introduce a preprocessing step to both
MWPM
X
and
MWPM
Z
.
The algorithm’s complete description utilizes binary representation for the generators (i.e., for the parity check matrix
H
) and the estimated channel error vector
\hat{{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 101}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 101}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 101}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 101}$}}}}
. For instance, in a code with
n
qubits, the matrix
H
comprises
2n
columns, with each row representing a generator. The first
n
columns contain a 1 where the corresponding generator features a
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
or
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
Pauli measurement, while the second
n
columns contain a 1 if the generators measure
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
or
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}

[
2
]
.
We also use the first
n_{\mathbf{ZY}}
rows to describe the
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
generators.
The decoder for ZZZY codes is presented as Algorithm
1
above. Excluding the function
update_weights
(to be introduced later), the algorithm ensures the minimum distance for ZZZY surface codes.
After evaluating the syndrome, the function
compute_distance
utilizes Dijkstra’s algorithm to find the shortest paths on a graph, where vertices correspond to switched on ancillas and edges’ weights are the sums of the underlying qubit weights.
Subsequently, via
MWPM
X
, pairs of
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
ancillas are connected, producing the estimated
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
channel errors.
Next, the parity of all ancillas measuring
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
operators on qubits involved in
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
errors is inverted.
Finally,
MWPM
Z
also allows for finding the
\textstyle{\mathchar 88}
channel errors. The algorithm corrects, therefore, all patterns of weight up to
t
.
Further, we would like to correct as much as possible
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
errors of weight
t+1
. To this aim we can
exploit the information coming from
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
generators. Specifically, after evaluating the syndrome, if some of the
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
generators are activated, we modify the weights of the edges of the
MWPM
graph using the function
update_weights
.
Since we are considering minimum weight decoders, error patterns of weight
t+1
could trigger only logical operators of weight
d
and
d+1
(if, as assumed,
d
is odd).
Let us start improving the correction in case of possible logical operators of weight
d+1
.
We can achieve this if we apply the following procedure: if one of the generators performing a
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
measurement on the
i
-th qubit is activated, the weight
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}}(i)
of the corresponding edge is modified to a number slightly smaller than one, e.g.,
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}}(i)=0.9
.
Moreover, if a generator performing a
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
measurement on the
i
-th qubit is switched off, the weight
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}}(i)
is set to a number slightly larger than one, e.g.,
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 113}$}}}(i)=1.1
.
In this way, during the
MWPM
X
, the decoder is pushed to choose paths where the
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
generators are switched on.
If the
i
-th qubit is actually affected by a
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
Pauli error, this strategy allows the decoder to choose correctly between different paths composed by the same number of edges.
We elucidate this with an example reported in Fig
2
a.
In particular, if
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
errors occur on data qubits
D_{6}
and
D_{3}
,
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
ancilla
A_{5}
is switched on.
If we directly apply
MWPM
X
, the decoder has to choose between three error patterns of the same weight:
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{3}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{6}

{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{2}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{4}
, and
{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{5}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{7}

However, with our modification, the weight of qubit
D_{3}
is set to
0.9

Let us now focus on logical operators of weight
d
. In this case, if an error pattern with
t+1
Pauli
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
operators occurs activating a
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}

\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}

To formalize the algorithm, let us define

as a function that takes as input the index of a
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
generator and returns the index
\ell

\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
measurement. Additionally, we define the function

\textstyle{\mathchar 88}

\textstyle{\mathchar 90}

D_{3}

X

{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{1}

{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{1}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{2}{\mathchoice{\hbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\textstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}{\hbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathchar 90}$}}}_{3}
.
In the worst case scenario, for each of the
4(d-1)

\textstyle{\mathchar 90}

Lemma 1
.

Given an
[[n,k,d]]
ZZZY code, for
d>3
, the fraction of
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
errors of weight
t+1
that cannot be corrected by the ZZZY decoder over a phase flip channel is
d\binom{d-2}{t+1}/\binom{n}{t+1}

Proof.

Over a phase-flip channel, all errors of weight
t+1
that can cause logical operators of weight
2t+2
are corrected.
Indeed, the decoder has to choose between two solutions composed of the same number of qubits.
Hence, by modifying the weight of the paths using the function
update_weights
, the actual error pattern is always identified.
In case the
t+1
errors occur on the same row of the lattice, they can cause a logical operator of weight
2t+1
.
To correct these errors, it is necessary that at least one of them occurs on a qubit measured by one of the two
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
\textstyle{\mathchar 89}
generators, since the ZZZY decoder has to set the weight of the corresponding qubit to
-0.1
.
Hence, the uncorrected error patterns for each of the
d
rows are
\binom{d-2}{t+1}
.
Finally, the total number of
\textstyle{\mathchar 90}
error patterns of weight
t+1
is
\binom{n}{t+1}
.
∎

Note that, as the code distance increases, the fraction of errors of weight
t+1
that cannot be corrected becomes smaller.

VI
Conclusions

We have introduced a novel family of
QECC
, specifically designed for asymmetric channels and named ZZZY codes.
These codes are derived from standard surface codes through the modification of certain generators. Furthermore, we have presented a variant of the
MWPM
decoder, tailored for these codes.
Remarkably, this decoder effectively leverages the augmented information from the modified generators without adding complexity.
By employing our variant of the
MWPM
decoder, the ZZZY codes
exhibit a significant performance advantage compared to surface codes over asymmetric channels.

Two Categorifications of the Local Langlands Correspondence for Tori
Ruide Fu

Abstract
. The stack of local Langlands parameters for a torus is a Picard stack. In this article, we explicitly determine its Picard dual and show that the Fourier–Mukai transform gives rise to the integral categorical local Langlands correspondence for the torus. This is the categorification of the local Langlands correspondence and answers a conjecture of X. Zhu. Moreover, we establish a geometric version of this correspondence, whose categorical trace reproduces the previous result.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a reductive group over a non-archimedean local field
F
.
X. Zhu carried out a detailed study of the stack of local and global
Langlands parameters
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{c}G,F}
defined over
\mathbb{Z}[1/p]
, and
proposed a categorical form of the arithmetic local Langlands
correspondence
[
12
]
. The automorphic side of this Langlands
correspondence is given by the category of
\ell
-adic sheaves on the
stack of isocrystals
\mathrm{Isoc}_{G}=LG/\mathrm{Ad}_{\sigma}LG
, where

\sigma
is the Frobenius on the residue field
\kappa_{F}
of
F
. A
central part of the conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 1.1

(Zhu21, Conjecture 4.6.4)
.

Let
(G,B,T,e)
be a pinned quasi-split reductive group over
F

and
\Lambda=\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
or
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. Fix an additive character

\psi_{0}:F\to\Lambda^{\times}
with conductor
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Then
there is a natural equivalence of stable
\infty
-categories

\mathbb{L}_{G}:\Shv(\mathrm{Isoc}_{G},\Lambda)\to\IndCoh_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{{}^{c}G}}(\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{c}G,F})

sending the Whittaker sheaf to the structural sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{c}G,F}}
. The category

\IndCoh_{\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{{}^{c}G}}(\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{c}G,F})\subset\IndCoh(\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{c}G,F})

is a subcategory of ind-coherent sheaves with certain singular
support condition.

In a more recent development, Zhu established the tame categorical local
Langlands correspondence with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-coefficients and the unipotent
correspondence with
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
-coefficients
[
13
]
.

1.2.
The First Categorification.

In the first part of this paper, we verify Conjecture
1.1
in the
case
G=T
, where
T
is an arbitrary tori over
F
. In this case,
no choice of a Whittaker datum
\psi_{0}
is required.

A key feature of the torus case is that
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}
can be defined
over
\mathbb{Z}
, and there exists an analogue of
\mathrm{Isoc}_{T}
also
defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. To explain the latter, recall that
the stack
\mathrm{Isoc}_{T}
is a disjoint union of copies
of the classifying stack
[*/T(F)]
indexed by the Kottwitz set

B(T)
. We denote by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
the algebraic stack over
\mathbb{Z}
that
also consists of copies of
[*/T(F)]
indexed by
B(T)
.
We say
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
is an analogue of
\mathrm{Isoc}_{T}
because for

\Lambda=\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
, or
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, we have

(1.1)

\Shv(\mathrm{Isoc}_{T},\,\Lambda)\cong\QCoh(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}\otimes\Lambda).

The key ingredient of the proof is another conjecture of Zhu.

Conjecture 1.3

(Zhu21, Conjecture 3.2.2)
.

There is a natural Poincaré line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{T}
on

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}\times\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}
inducing an isomorphism

\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\vee}\cong\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
.

We define the Poincaré line bundle over
\mathbb{Z}
, and show the
isomorphism
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\vee}\cong\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
when
2
is invertible
in the base. We shall see that
the isomorphism is essentially taking the cup product with the
fundamental class from local class field theory.
The proof of this conjecture is largely inspired by the work of
R. Langlands
[
9
]
.

The Fourier–Mukai transform was first introduced to the Langlands
program in the work of Laumon, where the transform is applied to
construct the geometric Langlands correspondence for
GL(1)
. Later,
in the work of Braverman-Bezrukavnikov
[
1
]
and Chen-Zhu

[
4
]
, Fourier–Mukai is used to establish a generic version of
the Langlands correspondence in positive characteristic (for any
reductive group
G
). In the same spirit, we use the Fourier–Mukai
transform to establish the categorical local Langlands correspondence
for tori with integral coefficients:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p
be invertible in
\Lambda
.
The Fourier–Mukai transform via the Poincaré line bundle

\mathcal{L}_{T}
gives the equivalence of stable
\infty
-categories

\mathbb{L}:\IndCoh_{0}(\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}\otimes\Lambda)\cong\QCoh(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}\otimes\Lambda).

1.5.
The Second Categorification.

Let
\Lambda=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
or
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Let
\mathfrak{T}
be the
connected Néron model of
T
, and let
L^{+}T
be the positive loop
group. There is an isomorphism between the abelian group of continuous
characters of Serre’s fundamental group
\pi_{1}(L^{+}T)
and the abelian
group of character sheaves on
L^{+}T
:

\Hom_{\mathrm{cts}}(\pi_{1}(L^{+}T),\Lambda^{\times})=\mathrm{CS}(L^{+}T,\Lambda).

The second main result of this paper categorifies this
isomorphism:

Theorem 1.6
.

There exists a fully-faithful,
t
-exact, monoidal functor

\operatorname{Ch}:\IndCoh({\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,\breve{F}}})\to\Shv(LT,\Lambda),

where
{\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,\breve{F}}}
is the stack of inertial Langlands parameters
of
F
in the dual group
{}^{L}T
. The essential image of
\operatorname{Ch}
is
compactly generated by the translations of all character sheaves
on
L^{+}T
to
LT
.

This theorem can be regarded as a “second-level categorification” in
the following sense. Both
\IndCoh({\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,\breve{F}}})
and
\Shv(LT,\Lambda)
carry
canonical Frobenius structures that are intertwined by
\operatorname{Ch}
. The
decategorification of
\operatorname{Ch}
via the categorical trace construction is
precisely the categorical local Langlands correspondence
\mathbb{L}
:

Proposition 1.7
.

There is a commutative diagram

(1.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Tr(\IndCoh({\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,\breve{F}}}),\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Tr(\Shv(LT),\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\IndCoh_{0}(\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Shv(\mathrm{Isoc}_{T}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}}

where
\mathbb{L}
is the categorical local Langlands correspondece
under the identification
\Shv(\mathrm{Isoc}_{T},\,\Lambda)\cong\QCoh(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}\otimes\Lambda)
.
Furthermore, the two vertical arrows are canonical equivalences.

Some of the results in this paper were independently obtained by
K. Zou
[
14
]
by different methods. There are two main
differences between this work and Zou’s. First, Zou considers integral

\ell
-adic sheaves on
\Bun_{G}
over the Fargues-Fontaine curve, while
we consider quasi-coherent sheaves on
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
, which can be integral.
Second, to establish the Langlands correspondence, Zou uses the
spectral action on the Whittaker sheaf, while we use the Fourier–Mukai
transform.

The outline of the paper is as follows. Section
2

introduces the definitions of
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
, along with notations
for the first half of the paper.
Section
4
establishes an important split
exact sequence involving
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}
.
Section
5
constructs the Poincaré line bundles and
proves the first main theorem.
Section
7
discusses the Fourier–Mukai transform
and the categorical local Langlands correspondence for tori.
Section
8
introduces the stack of inertial Langlands
parameters
{\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,\breve{F}}}
, the loop group
LT
and all other notations for the
second half of the paper.
Section
11
proves the second main theorem and the
result of decategorification.

Notation I

2.1.

Galois groups.

Let
F
be a local field,
\kappa_{F}
its residue field, and

E/F
a Galois extension that splits the torus
T
defined over
F
. Let

\Gamma=\Gal(E/F)
and let
W_{F}
and
W_{E}
be the Weil groups of
F
and

E
. Recall that the relative Weil group of
E/F
is defined as
W_{E/F}=W_{F}/[W_{E},W_{E}]
.

By the local class field theory, the relative Weil group is the
unique group extension

1\to E^{\times}\to W_{E/F}\to\Gamma\to 1

corresponding to the fundamental class
\alpha\in H^{2}(\Gamma,E^{\times})
.
Fix a system of representatives
\{w_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\Gamma}
for the
(right) cosets of
E^{\times}
. For any
g,h\in W_{E/F}
, there is
a unique
\delta(g,h)\in E^{\times}
and a unique
\tau\in\Gamma
such that

gh=\delta(g,h)w_{\tau}.

The assignment
(\tau,\sigma)\mapsto\delta(w_{\tau},w_{\sigma})

is a cocycle which represents the fundamental class
\alpha
.

Let
p:W_{E/F}\to\Gamma
be the projection. We sometimes extend the
notation
w_{\tau}
to allow subscripts be elements of
W_{E/F}
, for
example,
w_{g}=w_{p(g)}
, and
w_{g\tau}=w_{p(g)\tau}
, for all
g\in W_{E/F},\tau\in\Gamma
.

Let
U^{(n)}
be the
n
-th congruence subgroup of
E^{\times}
, so

U^{(0)}=\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{E}
and
U^{(n)}=1+\mathfrak{m}^{n}_{E},n\geq 1
.
We define
W^{(n)}=W_{E/F}/U^{(n)}
and let
p_{n}:W^{(n)}\to\Gamma
be the
projection.

2.2.
Torus and dual torus.

Let
L=X^{*}(T)
and
\hat{L}=X_{*}(T)
be the weight lattice and
coweight lattice of
T
. They are both
\Gamma
-modules and therefore

W_{E/F}
-modules. The dual torus
\hat{T}=L\otimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
is defined over

\mathbb{Z}
, and so is the L-group
{}^{L}T=\hat{T}\rtimes\Gamma
.

We write
U_{n}=\hat{L}\otimes U^{(n)}
. They form a basis of

\Gamma
-invariant open compact subgroups of
T(E)
.

2.3.
Picard stacks.

All stacks in this paper will be fpqc-stacks. Let us review the
essentials of Picard stacks. The reader can refer to

[
5
, § 1.4]
and
[
4
, Appendix A]
. Recall
that a Picard stack
\mathscr{P}
over
S
is a stack over
S
together
with a bi-functor

\otimes:\mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{P}\to\mathscr{P},

and the associativity and commutativity constraints

a:\otimes\circ(\otimes\times 1)\cong\otimes\circ(1\times\otimes),\quad c:\otimes\cong\otimes\circ\mathrm{flip},

such that for every
U
over
S
,
\mathscr{P}(U)
forms a Picard
groupoid (i.e. a symmetric monoidal groupoid in which every object
admits a monoidal inverse). A Picard stack is called strictly
commutative if
c_{x,x}=\id
for every
x\in\mathscr{P}
. All
Picard stacks in this paper are strictly commutative.

Let
\operatorname{Ch}^{\flat}(S)
be the category where objects are Picard stacks
over
S
and morphisms are isomorphism classes of additive
functors.
In fact,
\operatorname{Ch}^{\flat}(S)
is canonically enriched over itself. For

\mathscr{P}_{1},\mathscr{P}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ch}^{\flat}(S)
, we use

\Homm(\mathscr{P}_{1},\mathscr{P}_{2})
to denote the Picard stack
whose objects are additive functors from
\mathscr{P}_{1}
to
\mathscr{P}_{2}
and morphisms are morphisms between additive functors
(cf.
[
5
, § 1.4.7]
). The dual of a Picard stack

\mathscr{P}
is defined to be
\mathscr{P}^{\vee}=\Homm(\mathscr{P},\mathbb{BG}_{m})
.

Let
C^{[-1,0]}(S)
be the category of 2-term complexes of
sheaves of abelian groups
d:K^{-1}\to K^{0}
over
S
.
Let
K\in C^{[-1,0]}(S)
. We associate to it a Picard prestack

\mathrm{pch}(K)
whose section over
U
is the following Picard
groupoid

•

Objects of
\mathrm{pch}(K)(U)
are
K^{0}(U)
.

•

If
x,y\in K^{0}(U)
, a morphism from
x
to
y
is an
element
f\in K^{-1}(U)
such that
df=y-x
.

Let
\operatorname{ch}(K)
be the stackification of
\mathrm{pch}(K)
.

Let
D^{[-1,0]}(S)
denote the subcategory of the derived
category of abelian sheaves over
S
consists of complexes
K

with cohomology concentrated in degrees
-1
and
0
.
Deligne proved that the functor
\operatorname{ch}
induces an equivalence of
categories (cf.
[
5
, § 1.4.15]
):

\operatorname{ch}:D^{[-1,0]}\to\operatorname{Ch}^{\flat}(S).

We choose a quasi-inverse of
\operatorname{ch}
, denoted by
\flat
, that sends
a Picard stack
\mathscr{P}
to a complex

\mathscr{P}^{\flat}\in D^{[-1,0]}(S)
.

2.4.
Representation functors.

Let
S
be a base scheme. Let
M
be an affine group scheme over
S

and let
G
be an abstract group. We define
\mathcal{R}_{G,M}
to be
the functor that sends a scheme
U
over
S
to
\Hom(G,M(U))
.

The functor
\mathcal{R}_{G,M}
is represented by a scheme affine over the
base. This is because, if
I\subset G
is a set of generators,

\mathcal{R}_{G,M}
is realized as a closed subscheme of
M^{I}
defined by
the relations between the generators.

Let
G
be a locally profinite group, and let
\{K_{i}\}_{i}
be a basis
of open compact subgroups of
G
such that
K_{i+1}\leq K_{i}
. We
define

\mathcal{R}^{c}_{G,M}=\varinjlim_{i}\mathcal{R}_{G/K_{i},M}.

The functor
\mathcal{R}^{c}_{G,M}
is represented by an ind-scheme because
each map
\mathcal{R}_{G/K_{i},M}\to\mathcal{R}_{G/K_{i+1},M}
is a closed
immersion.

2.5.
Abelian sheaves.

All sheaves in this paper are fpqc-sheaves. For an abelian group
M
,
we write
\underline{M}
for the constant abelian sheaf associated to

M
.

For a locally profinite abelian group
N
, let
\{K_{i}\}_{i}
be a
basis of open compact subgroups of
N
. We denote by

N^{\circ}
the sheaf

N^{\circ}=\varprojlim_{i}\underline{N/K_{i}}.

Note that
N^{\circ}
is
not
a constant sheaf, because
the fpqc-topology remembers the locally profinite topology of

.
Indeed, for a test scheme
X=\Spec R
, we have

N^{\circ}(X)=\Hom_{\mathrm{cts}}(\pi_{0}(X),N).

If
N
is profinite, each
\underline{N/K_{i}}
is as a constant affine
group scheme, so
N^{\circ}
is an affine group scheme (not necessarily
of finite type over the base). More generally, if
N
is locally
profinite,
N^{\circ}
is an ind-affine group scheme.

Let
T(F)_{0}
denote the maximal compact subgroup of
T(F)
. For
convenience, we introduce the shorthand

\mathscr{T}_{0}=\big(T(F)_{0}\big)^{\circ},\qquad\mathscr{T}=T(F)^{\circ}.

We write
\mathbb{B}T(F)_{0}
and
\mathbb{B}T(F)
for the classifying stack of
\mathscr{T}_{0}
-torsors
and
\mathscr{T}
-torsors.

2.6.
Group cohomology.

Let
G
be a locally profinite group, and let
M
be a discrete
abelian group with a continuous
G
-action.

Recall that
Z^{1}(G,M)
is the abelian group of continuous 1-cocycles.
For every
m\in M
, the cocycle
\phi(g)=gm-m
is continuous
becuase the action of
G
on
M
is continuous. This defines the
differential
d:M\to Z^{1}(G,M)
.
The first group cohomology is defined by
H^{1}(G,M)=Z^{1}(G,M)/d(M)
.

On the other hand, we define
\bar{Z}^{1}(G,M)
to be the abelian group
of 1-cocycles when
G
is viewed as an abstract group, and we define

\bar{H}^{1}(G,M)=\bar{Z}^{1}(G,M)/d(M).

Let
S=\Spec{R}
be a base scheme, and let
\mathscr{M}
be a
commutative group scheme over
S
with a continuous
G
-action. We
define
\mathscr{Z}^{1}(G,\mathscr{M})
to be the presheaf that sends a
commutative algebra
R^{\prime}
over
R
to
Z^{1}(G,\mathscr{M}(R^{\prime}))
.

\mathscr{Z}^{1}(G,\mathscr{M})
is in fact a sheaf. Let
d:\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{Z}^{1}(G,\mathscr{M})
be the homomorphism of abelian sheaves such that
for every commutative algebra
R^{\prime}
over
R
,
d(R^{\prime})
coincides with
the differential defined before
d:\mathscr{M}(R^{\prime})\to Z^{1}(G,\mathscr{M}(R^{\prime}))
. We define
\mathscr{H}^{1}(G,\mathscr{M})
to be the Picard stack

\mathscr{H}^{1}(G,\mathscr{M})=\operatorname{ch}\left(\cdots\to 0\to\mathscr{M}\xrightarrow{d}Z^{1}(G,\mathscr{M})\to 0\to\cdots\right).

2.7.

The stack of Langlands parameters.

We define the ind-scheme of framed Langlands
parameters over
\Spec\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square}=\mathscr{Z}^{1}(W_{E/F},\hat{T})
.
To see
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square}
is an ind-scheme, we can rewrite it as:

\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square}=\varinjlim_{n}\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square,(n)},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square,(n)}=\mathcal{R}_{W^{(n)},{}^{L}T}\times_{\mathcal{R}_{W^{(n)},\Gamma}}\{p_{n}\}.

The stack of Langlands parameters is defined to be the quotient stack

\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}=[\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square}/\hat{T}]
. Because the action of
\hat{T}

stabilizes each
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square,(n)}
,
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}
is an ind-algebraic
stack:

\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}=\varinjlim_{n}\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{(n)},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{(n)}=\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}^{\square,(n)}/\hat{T}.

It is easy to see
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}=\mathscr{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\hat{T})
. This endows
a Picard stack structure on
\mathrm{Loc}_{{}^{L}T,F}
, as explained above.

2.8.

The stack

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}

.

We follow the setup in
[
8
]
. Let

\xi:\hat{L}\to\Hom(E^{\times},E^{\times}\otimes\hat{L})=Z^{1}(E^{\times},T(E))

be the adjoint of the identity. Taking
\Gamma
-invariants we get

\xi^{\Gamma}:\hat{L}^{\Gamma}\to Z^{1}(E^{\times},T(E))^{\Gamma}

We form the set of algebraic cycles by the following fibre product:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{1}_{\mathrm{alg}}(W_{E/F},T(E))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{L}^{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{1}(W_{E/F},T(E))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{1}(E^{\times},T(E))^{\Gamma}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}

In other words, the algebraic cycles are those 1-cycles whose
restriction to
E^{\times}\subset W_{E/F}
is an algebraic character of
T

(coming from the restriction, this character is automatically

\Gamma
-invariant). The differential
d:T(E)\to Z^{1}(W_{E/F},T(E))

factors through
d^{\prime}:T(E)\to Z^{1}_{\mathrm{alg}}(W_{E/F},T(E))
, because the
restriction to
E^{\times}\subset W_{E/F}
is always the trivial character.

In
loc. cit.
, Kottwitz proved that the cokernel of
d^{\prime}
is
isomorphic to the Kottwitz set
B(T)=\hat{L}_{\Gamma}
. Thus we have a
long exact sequence

0\to T(F)\to T(E)\xrightarrow{d^{\prime}}Z^{1}_{\mathrm{alg}}(W_{E/F},T(E))\to\hat{L}_{\Gamma}\to 0.

Since
T(F)
is a closed subgroup of
T(E)
, the image of
d^{\prime}
is
endowed with the quotient topology which is locally profinite.
We endow
Z^{1}_{\mathrm{alg}}(W_{E/F},T(E))
with the unique topology such that it
coincides with the quotient topology on
\operatorname{Im}(d^{\prime})
, and the projection
to
\hat{L}_{\Gamma}
is continuous (the latter is equipped with
the discrete topology). This makes
Z^{1}_{\mathrm{alg}}(W_{E/F},T(E))
a locally
profinite group, and the map
d^{\prime}
is continuous.

Now we can define
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
as:

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}=\mathrm{ch}\left(\cdots\to 0\to T(E)^{\circ}\to Z^{1}_{\mathrm{alg}}(W_{E/F},T(E))^{\circ}\to 0\to\cdots\right).

Remark 2.9
.

(i)

The isomorphism classes of the groupoid
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}(\mathbb{Z})
is the Kottwitz set

B(T)=\hat{L}_{\Gamma}
. The automorphism group of the identity object
is
T(F)
.

(ii)

It is easy to see that

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}=\bigsqcup_{\hat{L}_{\Gamma}}\,[*/T(F)^{\circ}].

Although the definition of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
involves inverse limit, we mostly
consider it as an ind-stack by considering
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
as the union of its
connected components.

(iii)

Let
\breve{F}
be the completion of a maximal unramified
extension of
F
, and let
\sigma
be a Frobenius element.
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}(\mathbb{Z})

is isomorphic to the groupoid of pairs
(\mathcal{E},\phi)
where

\mathcal{E}
is a
T
-torsor over
\breve{F}
and
\phi:\mathcal{E}\cong\sigma^{*}\,\mathcal{E}
is an isomorphism of
T
-torsors. By a
Tannakian formalism, this is the groupoid of exact tensor functors
from
\mathrm{Rep}_{T}
to the monoidal category of isocrystals over

F
. This justifies the notation
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Tor}}_{T,\mathrm{iso}_{F}}
.

3.
Lemmas in Homological Algebra

In this section and the next section, we establish some lemmas
in an abstract setting.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group, and let
A
be an abelian group
equipped with a
\Gamma
-action. Let
\alpha\in H^{2}(\Gamma,A)

be the cohomology class representing a group extension

1\to A\to G\to\Gamma\to 1.

Definition 3.1
.

We say the triple
(\Gamma,A,\alpha)
satisfies the Tate-Nakayama
criterion if for every subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\leq\Gamma
, and for all

r\in\mathbb{Z}
, taking the cup product with

5.2.
Definition of

.

The boundaries of this chain complex are defined by

By definition,

(5.1)

Now we can define

The groupoid
\mathcal{TOR}(\mathbb{Z})
has isomorphism classes indexed by
\hat{L}_{\Gamma}

and the automorphism group of the identity is

.

\displaystyle\langle\;,\;\rangle:

\displaystyle\hat{T}

\displaystyle[\;,\;]:

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that 2 is invertible in

(\mathcal{R}^{c}_{T(F),\mathbb{G}_{m}})^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{B}T(F).

Proof.

We first claim that for every

(\mathcal{R}_{T(F)/V_{i},\mathbb{G}_{m}})^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{B}T(F)/V_{i}

Recall that the inner Hom between Picard stacks can be
calculated in the category of abelian sheaves:

(\Homm(\mathscr{P}_{1},\mathscr{P}_{2}))^{\flat}=\tau_{\leq 0}R\Homm(\mathscr{P}_{1}^{\flat},\mathscr{P}_{2}^{\flat}).

In degree

In degree

is a finitely generated abelian group,

and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. It is well known that

and
\mathscr{E}xt^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
,
hence degree
0
vanishes. This proves the claim.

Next, let
\{A_{i}\}
be a filtered directed system of complex of
abelian sheaves, and let
B
be another such complex. Then there
is a natural isomorphism

R\Homm(\colim A_{i},B)\cong\lim R\Homm(A_{i},B).

Applying the Milnor exact sequence yields a short exact sequence

and

.

terms vanishes for

and

. For

, the first term
becomes

For
n=0
, we need to show the vanishing of

\mathrm{Rlim}^{1}H^{1}R\Homm(\mathcal{R}_{T(F)/V_{i},\mathbb{G}_{m}},\mathbb{BG}_{m})=\mathrm{Rlim}^{1}\mathscr{E}\!xt^{2}(\mathcal{R}_{T(F)/V_{i}},\mathbb{G}_{m}).

Write

, where

is a free abelian

is the maximal compact open

. Then

and the

10.
Categorical Trace and Convolution Pattern

The reader may skip this section on a first reading. One should,
however, take note of Section
10
and Corollary

10.12
before moving on, as they form part of the basic
input for what follows.

In this section we first recall the definition of the categorical
trace and introduce a convolution pattern that frequently arises in
the geometric Langlands program. The main statement is Proposition

10.5
, which describes the computation of categorical
trace and show that the trace of a sheaf category can often be
identified with a full subcategory of sheaves on a suitable
fixed-point object.

10.1.
Hochschild homology.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a symmetric monoidal category,
A
and
B
be two
associative algebras in
\mathcal{R}
. We denote by
A^{\mathrm{rev}}
(resp.

B^{\mathrm{rev}}
) the algebra
A
(resp.
B
) with the reversed
multiplication, then an
A
-
B
-bimodule can be regarded as a left

(A\otimes B^{\mathrm{rev}})
-module or a right
(B\otimes A^{\mathrm{rev}})
-module. For
an
A
-
A
-bimodule
F
, the Hochschild homology of
F
, if it exists,
is defined as

\Tr(A,F)=A\otimes_{A\otimes A^{\mathrm{rev}}}F\in\mathcal{R}.

However, the Hochschild complex of
F
always exists. It is
given by

\HH(A,F)_{\bullet}=\mathrm{Bar}(A)_{\bullet}\otimes_{A\otimes A^{\mathrm{rev}}}F=A^{\otimes\bullet}\otimes F

regarded as a simplicial object
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{R}
.
If
\mathcal{R}
admits geometric realizations and the tensor product
preserves geometric realizations in each variable, the Hochschild
homology of
F
exists and can be calculated via the Hochschild complex:

\Tr(A,F)=|\HH(A,F)_{\bullet}|.

An important special case of categorical trace occurs when the algebra

A
is equipped with an endomorphism
\phi
. Let
F
be an

A
-
A
-bimodule, then the
\phi
-twisted bimodule
F
, which we
denote by
{}^{\phi}F
, is the bimodule with the same right
A
-action
but with the left
A
-action precomposed with
\phi
. We write

\Tr(A,\phi)
for the Hochschild homology of
F={}^{\phi}A
.

When
\mathcal{R}=\mathrm{Lincat}_{\Lambda}
, the
(\infty,2)
category of

\Lambda
-linear
\infty
-categories, we refer to
\Tr(A,F)
(or

\Tr(A,\phi)
) as the categorical trace.

10.2.
Abstract sheaf theory.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be an
\infty
-category that admits finite limits and
finite coproducts, and let
\mathrm{pt}
denote its final object.
The category
\mathbf{C}
will play the role of the category of geometric
objects.

Let
\mathrm{V},\mathrm{H}
be two weakly stable class of morphisms in
\mathbf{C}

(i.e., they contain all isomorphisms in
\mathbf{C}
, are stable
under equivalences of morphisms, and are stable under base
change and composition). Let

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{C})_{\mathrm{V};\mathrm{H}}
denote the category
of correspondences, as defined in
[
13
, § 8..1.1]
.
We write
\mathrm{All}
for the class of all morphisms in
\mathbf{C}
,
and simply write
\mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{C})
for

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{C})_{\mathrm{All};\mathrm{All}}
.

Remark 10.3
.

There is a subclass of morphisms
\mathrm{VR}\subset\mathrm{V}

(resp.
\mathrm{HR}\subset\mathrm{H}
) which are called

vertically
(resp.
horizontally
)
right
adjointable
. For their definitions see
[
13
, § 8.2.2]
. We do
not recall the definitions here, but we will indicate the classes

\mathrm{VR}
and
\mathrm{HR}
explicitly in our applications.

An abstract sheaf theory with coefficient in
\Lambda
of

\mathbf{C}
is a lax symmetric monoidal functor

\mathcal{D}:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{C})_{\mathrm{V};\mathrm{H}}\to\mathrm{Lincat}_{\Lambda}.

The functoriality of a sheaf theory encodes a rich structure. To begin,
we denote by
g^{\star}:\mathcal{D}(X)\to\mathcal{D}(Y)
the functor corresponding to
a horizontal morphism
X\xleftarrow{g}Y\xrightarrow{\id}Y
, and we
denote by
f_{\dagger}:\mathcal{D}(X)\to\mathcal{D}(Y)
the functor corresponding to
a vertical morphism
X\xleftarrow{\id}X\xrightarrow{f}Y
. A
general correspondence
X\xleftarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{f}Y
is
sent to a functor isomorphic to
f_{\dagger}\circ g^{\star}
. Moreover, the
sheaf theory encodes familiar structural results such as the base change
theorem and the projection formula. For further discussion, see

[
13
, § 8.2.1]
.

Appendix A
Lemma in Homological Algebra

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group, and let
A
be an abelian group
equipped with a
\Gamma
-action. Let
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(\Gamma,A)

represent the group extension

1\to A\to G\to\Gamma\to 1.

Let
M
be a
\Gamma
-module, or equivalently, a
G
-module on which

A
acts trivially. Then we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{2}(\Gamma,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(A,M)_{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(G,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(\Gamma,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{H}^{-1}(\Gamma,M\otimes A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M\otimes A)_{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M\otimes A)^{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{H}^{0}(\Gamma,M\otimes A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cup\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cup\alpha}

The first row of the diagram is the long exact sequence associated to the
Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence. The second row is the definition
of the Tate cohomology.
The first and last vertical map is the cup product with
\alpha
. The map

\mathrm{res}
is the restriction map
H_{1}(G,M)\to H_{1}(A,M)^{\Gamma}
.

In particular, if the triple
(\Gamma,A,\alpha)
satisfy the
Tate-Nakayama criterion and if
M
is torsion free as an abelian group,
then

H_{1}(G,M)\cong(M\otimes A)^{\Gamma}.

Proof.

The crux of the proof is the commutativity of the left-most
square. The commutativity of the remaining ones are fairly
standard and will be omitted.

A.2.
Notations.

We follow the notation of Atiyah and Wall
[
3
]
.
The homogeneous bar resolution of the
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}
is given by

C_{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})=\bigoplus_{G^{n+1}}\mathbb{Z}(g_{0},g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}),

which is the free abelian group generated by the basis
G^{n+1}

with the action of

g\cdot(g_{0},\cdots,g_{n})=(gg_{0},\cdots,gg_{n}).

The differential is the usual

d(g_{0},\cdots,g_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(g_{0},\cdots,\widehat{g_{i}},\cdots,g_{n}).

This complex is identified with the more familiar inhomogeneous bar resolution, by sending

(g_{0},g_{0}g_{1},\cdots,g_{0}g_{1}\cdots g_{n})\mapsto(g_{0}|g_{1}|\cdots|g_{n}).

Let
P_{\bullet}
denote a
\Gamma
-resolution of
\mathbb{Z}
by
finitely-generated free
\Gamma
-modules, and let
P^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}(P_{\bullet},\mathbb{Z})
be its dual, so that we have exact
sequences

\displaystyle\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}\to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{Z}\to P_{0}^{*}\to P_{1}^{*}\to\cdots.

Let
P_{-n}=P_{n-1}^{*}
and splicing the two sequences together, we
obtain a doubly-infinite exact sequence

L_{\bullet}:\quad\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to P_{-1}\to P_{-2}\to\cdots

The Tate groups are defined as the cohomology groups of

\Hom_{\Gamma}(L_{\bullet},M)
for any
\Gamma
-module
M
, i.e.

H^{i}_{T}(\Gamma,M)=H^{i}(\Hom_{\Gamma}(L_{\bullet},M)).

The homology of
M
agrees with the Tate cohomology groups
of degree
\leq-2
via isomorphisms

P_{\bullet}\otimes_{\Gamma}M=(P_{\bullet}\otimes M)_{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{N}(P_{\bullet}\otimes M)^{\Gamma}\to(\Hom(P_{\bullet}^{*},M))^{\Gamma}=\Hom_{\Gamma}(P_{\bullet}^{*},M).

A.3.
Cup Product.

Let
\{w_{\sigma}|\sigma\in\Gamma\}
be a system of representatives
of
\Gamma
in
G
. The class of the extension
\alpha
is
represented by the cocycle

\delta:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to A
defined by

w_{\sigma}w_{\tau}=\delta(\sigma,\tau)\,w_{\sigma\tau}.

The 2-cocycle
\delta
determines a homogeneous 2-cocycle

\delta^{\prime}\in\Hom_{\Gamma}(C_{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}),A)
characterized by

\delta^{\prime}(1,c_{1},c_{1}c_{2})=\delta(c_{1},c_{2}).

Let
[\phi]\in H_{2}(\Gamma,M)
be represented by a 2-cycle

\phi:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to M
. Then it is also represented by a
homogeneous 2-cycle
\phi^{\prime}\in\Hom_{\Gamma}(C_{2}^{*}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}),M)

such that

\phi^{\prime}(1,c_{1}^{*},(c_{1}c_{2})^{*})=\phi(c_{1},c_{2}).

The cup product
[\phi]\cup[\delta]\in H_{0}(\Gamma,M)
is defined
to be the class of

\phi^{\prime}\cup\delta^{\prime}\in\Hom_{\Gamma}(C_{0}^{*}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}),M\otimes A)
,
which is the homogeneous cycle defined by

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}\cup\delta^{\prime}(c^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{1},c_{2}}\phi^{\prime}(c^{*},c_{1}^{*},c_{2}^{*})\otimes\delta^{\prime}(c_{2},c_{1},c)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{1},c_{2}}\phi^{\prime}(c^{*},c_{1}^{*},(c_{1}c_{2})^{*})\otimes\delta^{\prime}(c_{1}c_{2},c_{1},c).

Its image in
(M\otimes A)_{\Gamma}
is

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}\cup\delta^{\prime}(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{1},c_{2}}\phi^{\prime}(1,c_{1}^{*},(c_{1}c_{2})^{*})\otimes\delta^{\prime}(c_{1}c_{2},c_{1},1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{1},c_{2}}\phi(c_{1},c_{2})\otimes c_{1}c_{2}\cdot\delta^{\prime}(1,c_{2}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1}c_{1}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{1},c_{2}}\phi(c_{1},c_{2})\otimes c_{1}c_{2}\cdot\delta(c_{2}^{-1},c_{1}^{-1}).

A.4.
Spectral sequence.
For a
G
-module
M
, we now
construct the double complex giving rise to the
Lydon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence. Let
P_{\bullet}=C_{\bullet}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
and
Q_{\bullet}=C_{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{Z})
. The
differential of the two chain complexes are denoted by
d_{1}
and

d_{0}
respectively. We define the double complex

E_{ij}=P_{i}\otimes Q_{j}.

The total complex
\mathrm{Tot}(E_{ij})
forms a
G
-resolution of

\mathbb{Z}
, hence the spectral sequence associated with
E_{ij}\otimes_{G}M
calculates the group homology of
M
. Note that
P_{\bullet}
is
also a
G
-module. We have

E_{ij}^{M}:=E_{ij}\otimes_{\,G}M=(P_{i}\otimes Q_{j}\otimes M)_{G}=\left(P_{i}\otimes_{\,\Gamma}(Q_{j}\otimes_{A}M)\right).

Since
Q_{j}\otimes_{A}(-)
calculates
A
-homology and
P_{i}\otimes_{\,\Gamma}(-)
calculates
\Gamma
-homology, the resulting spectral sequence of this
double complex is the Lydon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence.

Next, We relate the
A
-homology calculated via
Q_{\bullet}
to
that calculated via
C_{\bullet}(A,\mathbb{Z})
. We choose a projection

f:G\to A
such that

\displaystyle f(w_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\text{for all }\gamma\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle f(ag)

\displaystyle=af(g),

\displaystyle\text{for all }a\in A,g\in G.

This choice induce a morphism between
A
-complexes
C_{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{Z})\to C_{\bullet}(A,\mathbb{Z})
. In particular,
C_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{A}M\to C_{1}(A,M)
is given by

(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m\mapsto(f(g_{2})-f(g_{1}))\otimes m.

A.5.
Differentials.
Recall that in the sepctral
sequence, the differential

is defined as follows. Let

be a
cycle and assume there exists

From now on, we use

or Greek letters

to

\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad\,(c_{0},c_{1},\cdots,c_{i})\otimes(g_{0},\cdots,g_{j})\otimes m

\displaystyle=(1,c_{0}^{-1}c_{1},\cdots,c_{0}^{-1}c_{i})\otimes(w_{c_{0}}^{-1}g_{0},\cdots,w_{c_{0}}^{-1}g_{j})\otimes c_{0}^{-1}m.

In general, this expression is not unique. However, when

Now we proceed to compute

. The next three formulae are

\displaystyle d_{1}\Big((1,c_{1},c_{1}c_{2})\otimes(g)\otimes m\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(c_{1}(1,c_{2})-(1,c_{1}c_{2})+(1,c_{1})\right)\otimes(g)\otimes m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1,c_{2})\otimes(w_{c_{1}}^{-1}g)\otimes c_{1}^{-1}m-(1,c_{1},c_{2})\otimes(g)\otimes m+(1,c_{1})\otimes(g)\otimes m.

\displaystyle d_{0}\,(1,c)\otimes(g_{0},g_{1})\otimes m

\displaystyle(1,c)\otimes(g_{0})\otimes m-(1,c)\otimes(g_{1})\otimes m.

\displaystyle d_{1}\,(1,c)\otimes(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(w_{c}^{-1}g_{1},w_{c}^{-1}g_{2})\otimes c^{-1}m-(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes m

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle c^{-1}m\otimes\left(f(w_{c}^{-1}g_{2})-f(w_{c}^{-1}g_{1})\right)-m\otimes\left(f(g_{2})-f(g_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{-1}m\otimes\left(\delta(c^{-1},g_{2})-\delta(c^{-1},g_{1})\right)-m\otimes\left(f(g_{2})-f(g_{1})\right).

In the second to last line, we send the element to

using Equation (
A.1

compute the differentials explicitly as follows.

\displaystyle d_{1}\,\tilde{\phi}(\gamma,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{2}=\gamma}c_{1}^{-1}\tilde{\phi}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{1}\sigma)-\sum_{c_{1}c_{2}=\gamma}\tilde{\phi}(c_{1},c_{2},\sigma)+\sum_{c_{1}=\gamma}\tilde{\phi}(c_{1},c_{2},\sigma)

\displaystyle d_{0}\,\tilde{\psi}(\gamma,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\overline{h}=\sigma,\,g}\psi(\gamma,h,g)-\sum_{g}\psi(\gamma,g,h).

\displaystyle d_{1}\,\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{c,g_{1},g_{2}}c^{-1}\psi(c,g_{1},g_{2})\otimes(\delta(c^{-1},g_{2})-\delta(c^{-1},g_{1}))

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{c,g_{1},g_{2}}\psi(c,g_{1},g_{2})\otimes(f(g_{2})-f(g_{1})).

\displaystyle d_{2}([\phi])

\displaystyle=d_{1}\,\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma,g_{1},g_{2}}\gamma^{-1}\psi(\gamma,g_{1},g_{2})\otimes(\delta(\gamma^{-1},g_{2})-\delta(\gamma^{-1},g_{1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma,g_{1},g_{2}}\big(\gamma\cdot\psi(\gamma^{-1},g_{2},g_{1})-\gamma\cdot\psi(\gamma^{-1},g_{1},g_{2})\big)\otimes\delta(\gamma,g_{1})

The role of data augmentation and attention mechanisms in UNet and ConvNeXt architectures for optimizing breast tumor segmentation
Authors: Mohammadreza Kamsari, Soroush Sadeghi, Gabriel Gomes de Oliveira, Angela Maria Alves, Nazanin Tataei Sarshar, Shokofeh Anari, Ramin Ranjbarzadeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study conducts a comprehensive analysis of various configurations of the UNet + ConvNeXt Tiny architecture for breast tumor segmentation. We assess the influence of data augmentation, skip connections, attention mechanisms, and dropout rates on segmentation performance utilizing the BUSI dataset, subsequently conducting cross-dataset evaluation on the BUS-UCLM dataset. Our findings indicate that the integration of attention mechanisms markedly improves metrics including F1 score, precision, IoU, recall, and Dice coefficient. Data augmentation alone did not consistently enhance model performance and occasionally caused instability, however its integration with attention processes frequently resulted in significant improvements. Dropout was helpful in mitigating overfitting, with a rate of 0.5 consistently yielding enhanced generalization in both in-domain and cross-domain assessments, especially when combined with strategically positioned attention modules. Models using attention mechanisms and a dropout rate of 0.5 attained the optimal equilibrium between accuracy and recall, emphasizing the necessity of balancing regularization intensity with architectural intricacy. Nevertheless, our results underscore the potential for certain augmentation techniques—particularly those that employ aggressive geometric transformations—to distort anatomical structures in ultrasound images, resulting in a decline in the quality of segmentation. This highlights the necessity for meticulous, domain-specific selection of augmentation strategies in medical imaging. This study emphasizes the necessity for meticulous selection of architectural elements and regularization techniques to enhance medical image segmentation and offers direction for future investigations in resilient deep learning applications for clinical imaging.Keywords: Breast tumor segmentation, ConvNeXt tiny, Data augmentation, Attention mechanisms, BUS-UCLM datasetSubject terms: Breast cancer, Biomedical engineering

Introduction

Breast cancer is a highly dangerous and pervasive type of cancer that affects women globally. Prompt identification and precise evaluation are essential for successful therapy and improved patient outcomes. Accurate segmentation of breast tumors from medical images is crucial for diagnosis and treatment planning, particularly for guiding surgical interventions and radiotherapy1–6.

Among various imaging modalities, ultrasound imaging is particularly favored due to its non-invasiveness, cost-effectiveness, and real-time capability. It plays a pivotal role in differentiating between benign and malignant lesions and in guiding biopsy procedures7–11. However, manually segmenting breast tumors in ultrasound images is challenging, time-consuming, and prone to observer variability due to tissue heterogeneity, image noise, tissue heterogeneity, and indistinct tumor boundaries. These challenges highlight the need for robust, automated segmentation techniques to assist clinicians and reduce subjective interpretation2,12–15.

Deep learning (DL) methods have demonstrated significant achievements in automating medical image processing tasks, such as tumor segmentation, in recent years10,16,17. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are valuable tools because they can efficiently learn hierarchical features from extensive datasets18–21. The U-Net architecture has gained widespread acceptance in medical image segmentation due to its encoder-decoder structure, which effectively captures both local and global contexts. Although it is effective, there is still potential for improvement, namely in enhancing the process of extracting features and concentrating on the essential portions of the image20,22,23.

Attention mechanisms have lately been prominent in the field of DL due to their capacity to selectively concentrate on significant aspects of the input data, resulting in enhanced model performance24–27. Integrating attention mechanisms into segmentation algorithms can improve their capacity to precisely separate tumors by emphasizing important characteristics while reducing the impact of irrelevant data. This approach effectively tackles some constraints inherent in conventional U-Net models, offering a more resilient solution for the segmentation of medical images28,29.

Data augmentation techniques are crucial in DL as they enhance the variety of training data, hence enhancing the generalizability and resilience of models. Within the realm of medical image segmentation, employing augmentation techniques such as rotation, translation, and flipping can enhance the model’s ability to recognize consistent characteristics, resulting in improved performance when presented with unfamiliar data30–33. By utilizing these methods, we may develop more robust models that are capable of effectively managing the inherent uncertainty in medical imaging.

Recent progress in automatic segmentation techniques, such as the incorporation of attention processes and data augmentation, seeks to address the difficulties associated with manual segmentation. The efficacy and dependability of these techniques have been demonstrated through encouraging outcomes, indicating their capacity to enhance the precision and consistency of tumor segmentation in ultrasound images. This advancement is crucial in equipping radiologists with efficient resources to improve diagnostic accuracy and, eventually, patient treatment.

This project aims to create and assess sophisticated DL methods for segmenting breast tumors using the BUSI (Breast Ultrasound Image) dataset. Through the utilization of cutting-edge methodologies, our objective is to provide a valuable contribution to the continuous endeavors in enhancing the automated segmentation of tumors. This will provide valuable knowledge on the practical implementation of these methods in clinical environments.

Related works

Recent efforts in breast tumor segmentation have introduced several techniques aimed at enhancing feature representation and localization accuracy. Wang et al.34 introduced MF-Net, a new segmentation network specifically developed to enhance the accuracy of breast lesion segmentation. Their method integrated an additional transformer branch into the conventional single-branch encoder structure, allowing the network to collect both detailed local features and broader global context information concurrently. An important advancement in their research was the incorporation of a multiple-feature extraction module, which replaces the conventional skip connections. This module combined coarse frequency domain characteristics with enhanced multiscale characteristics, leading to more precise and detailed characteristics during decoding and narrowing the gap in characteristics between the encoder and decoder without a substantial increase in computational complexity.

In addition, they proposed the incorporation of a global feature augmentation module to effectively utilize and merge high-level semantic information from various encoders. This module improved the strength and reliability of the high-level characteristics, resulting in more precise information on the lesion features for the decoding procedure. The efficacy of MF-Net has been demonstrated through extensive experimentation on three breast lesion datasets. The suggested network demonstrates superior performance in breast lesion segmentation tasks as compared to current cutting-edge approaches, specifically in enhancing the accuracy of segmenting malignant breast lesions. While MF-Net improved segmentation accuracy across various datasets, its complexity and reliance on multiple branches may limit its applicability in resource-constrained environments.

Ru et al.35 focused on the difficulties encountered by widely used deep neural networks in the detection of breast cancer, including the presence of numerous parameters, the absence of interpretability, and the problem of overfitting. The researchers introduced a new segmentation network called Att-U-Node, which utilizes attention modules in a neural ODE-based architecture to direct the segmentation process. The Att-U-Node network utilizes ODE blocks to build an encoder-decoder structure, guaranteeing feature representation using neural ODE at every level. In addition, the incorporation of an attention module enhanced the skip connection by computing the coefficient and producing a more accurate attention feature.

In order to assess the efficacy of their model, they employed three publicly accessible breast ultrasound imaging datasets (BUSI, BUS, and OASBUD) along with a proprietary breast DCE-MRI dataset. They expanded their model to include 3D tumor segmentation by utilizing data from the Public QIN Breast DCE-MRI. The experimental findings indicate that Att-U-Node performs at a similar level to other approaches in segmenting breast tumors, while also addressing common challenges associated with deep neural networks, such as excessive parameters, limited interpretability, and overfitting. This novel method showcased the capability of combining attention mechanisms and neural ODEs to improve the precision and dependability of automated tumor segmentation in medical imaging. However, challenges such as high computational demand and training complexity persisted.

Chen et al.4 proposed a novel method for segmenting breast lesions by utilizing an improved selective kernel convolution module (ESK). This module integrated spatial attention to adjust spatial dimension characteristics and residual learning to enhance the significance of long-distance features. By incorporating ESK into a new type of deep supervised U-net called ESKNet, the network can segregate breast lesions within ultrasound images in an adaptive manner. The improved selective kernel convolution method chooses features from different receptive fields of various sizes in both channel and spatial dimensions and strengthens the importance of distant feature information. This leads to a more precise segmentation of breast lesions. Extensive tests were performed on several advanced deep-learning segmentation techniques to assess the impact of ESKNet on breast lesion segmentation accuracy. The results reveal that ESKNet outperforms other methods on different datasets, highlighting its superior performance. Despite its improved performance, the model required careful tuning of attention parameters and lacked integration with recent backbone advancements.

Although there have been improvements in CNNs for segmenting breast tumors, there are still difficulties in dealing with complicated ultrasound patterns, tumors of different forms and sizes, and the requirement for segmentation algorithms that are both reliable and accurate. To tackle these challenges, we provide a new method that utilizes the advantages of UNet and ConvNeXt tiny architecture, enhanced with attention mechanisms and different dropout algorithms. The objective of our approach is to improve the accuracy and reliability of segmentation by including spatial and channel attention mechanisms. This allows us to prioritize important features and reduce the impact of less informative regions. This method not only addresses the inherent difficulties in segmenting breast tumors, but also shows substantial enhancements in performance across various measures, as proven by our comprehensive trials on diverse datasets.

To address these gaps, the suggested model integrates a UNet architecture with the ConvNeXt Tiny encoder, leveraging its hierarchical representation power and compatibility with transfer learning. Unlike previous works, this study systematically investigates the individual and combined effects of data augmentation, skip connections, attention mechanisms, and dropout rates on segmentation performance. The evaluation spans 14 configurations and provides a comprehensive analysis of overfitting patterns, precision-recall trade-offs, and robustness under various design choices. This framework prioritizes reproducibility, simplicity, and computational efficiency while maintaining strong segmentation performance on the BUSI dataset.

Methodology

Dataset

This study utilizes the Breast Ultrasound Image (BUSI) dataset to assess the effectiveness of our breast tumor segmentation model36. The dataset, gathered in 2018, offers a wide range of images that are essential for effective model training and assessment. The BUSI dataset consists of 780 ultrasound images obtained from 600 female patients ranging in age from 25 to 75 years old. The images are classified into three categories: normal, benign, and malignant. The images, initially in PNG format, exhibit variations in terms of the dimensions, contours, and characteristics of the tumors, hence posing a genuine difficulty for automatic segmentation techniques. The typical dimensions of an image are 500 pixels by 500 pixels. For normal cases, which contain no tumors, corresponding binary masks consisting entirely of zeros (all-background masks) were employed. This inclusion ensures that the model learns to properly recognize the absence of tumors and to avoid false positives. By training on both non-tumor and tumor cases, the model becomes more robust in distinguishing between pathological and healthy tissues, improving its generalization capability across diverse clinical presentations.

To ensure consistency in the input for the model, every image in the dataset has been uniformly shrunk to dimensions of 224 × 224 pixels, with three color channels (224, 224, 3). This scaling process guarantees consistency, striking a balance between computing efficiency and the preservation of enough data for precise segmentation. In addition, the pixel values of the images are scaled to a range of 0 to 1 to expedite the convergence process during model training.

The BUSI dataset36 has accurate annotations for each image, specifying the exact boundaries of the tumors. The annotations are presented as binary masks, with tumor locations indicated by pixels assigned a value of 1, and background pixels assigned a value of 0. The masks function as the definitive reference for training and assessing the segmentation model.

To guarantee an equitable assessment of the model’s effectiveness, the dataset is partitioned into three distinct subsets: the training set, the validation set, and the test set. The training set consists of 80% of the images, the validation set consists of 10%, and the test set consists of the remaining 10%. This division guarantees that the model is trained on a varied collection of images and assessed on new data to determine its capacity to generalize. The implementation of these augmentation techniques utilizes the ImageDataGenerator class from TensorFlow’s Keras API. Both the images and their matching masks invariably undergo augmentation to ensure that they remain correctly aligned. This guarantees that the ground truth annotations remain accurate for the augmented images. Through the implementation of these data augmentation strategies, our objective is to enhance the model’s capacity to adapt to diverse variances in ultrasound images, resulting in improved performance and resilience in real-world clinical environments.

While data augmentation is widely utilized to enhance generalization, its application in medical imaging comes with several limitations41,42. Certain transformations (e.g., flipping or rotation) may create anatomically unrealistic variants if applied indiscriminately. Moreover, augmentation can introduce noise that misleads the model when learning boundary information, particularly in ultrasound images where lesions may already exhibit fuzzy margins. In this study, the employed augmentation techniques were chosen carefully to preserve anatomical plausibility. Nonetheless, outcomes indicate that augmentation does not always improve segmentation performance, especially when applied in combination with aggressive dropout or dual-attention configurations. This finding highlights the importance of context-aware augmentation strategies, particularly in domains with subtle, low-contrast structures like breast lesions.

Base model

With its encoder-decoder structure, the basic model used in our work is a U-Net architecture, which is well known for its efficacy in medical image segmentation13,43. To improve the ability to extract features, we incorporate the ConvNeXt Tiny model as the encoder11,44,45.

The ConvNeXt Tiny model, which has been initialized with weights that were pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset, functions as the foundational component of our encoder. This model is chosen for its strong feature extraction capabilities, which are essential for recognizing complex patterns in medical images46–48. At the beginning of training, the weights of the ConvNeXt Tiny model are fixed to take use of its pre-trained characteristics. This guarantees that the fundamental properties are preserved and not altered by the particular training data. Subsequently, these weights undergo fine-tuning to effectively adjust to the particular job of tumor segmentation, enabling the model to more accurately capture the subtle details included in ultrasound images. This transfer learning methodology not only accelerates the training process but also enhances the model’s performance by utilizing the enormous amount of information embedded in the pre-trained ConvNeXt Tiny model3,49,50. UNet structure

The U-Net architecture comprises two fundamental components: the encoder, which is responsible for contracting the input, and the decoder, which is responsible for expanding the output51–53. The encoder, utilizing the ConvNeXt Tiny model, obtains hierarchical features from the input image by applying a sequence of convolutional layers and pooling operations. These layers systematically decrease the size of the spatial dimensions while simultaneously increasing the complexity of the feature maps, allowing for the capture of different degrees of abstraction. The U-Net’s encoder is structured to preserve high-resolution features at every stage by incorporating skip connections. The encoder is composed of convolutions, pooling layers, and activation functions. These components work together to decrease dimensionality and extract significant features.

The decoder utilizes convolutional layers and upsampling layers to restore the spatial resolution of the image. Each instance of upsampling is accompanied by skip connections originating from the appropriate encoder levels. The skip connections in this context involve the concatenation of high-resolution features from the encoder with the upsampled features in the decoder. This process is important for maintaining spatial details that are essential for accurate segmentation. The decoder architecture consists of upsampling operations followed by convolutions that enhance the features. The integration of high-resolution characteristics derived from the encoder and the upsampled characteristics aids the model in precisely delineating the tumors19,54–56.

The last layer of the U-Net consists of a 1 × 1 convolution with a sigmoid activation function. This layer generates a binary mask that identifies the segmented tumor regions. The purpose of this layer is to generate a probability map as the output, which indicates the possibility of each pixel being classified as part of a tumor.

Attention mechanisms

In order to improve the model’s performance, two attention techniques are integrated into the U-Net architecture.

Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Blocks: SE blocks, originally introduced by Hu et al.57, are incorporated into the convolutional layers to readjust feature responses on a per-channel basis (See Fig. 4). This approach enables the model to concentrate on the most pertinent characteristics by dynamically readjusting the significance of each channel according to global information. The SE blocks operate by compressing overall spatial information into a channel descriptor and then capturing channel-wise dependencies through an excitation process. This procedure leads to a redistribution of the weights assigned to the channels, enabling the model to prioritize the most informative characteristics20,58,59.

Experiments and results

Assessment metrics

In order to thoroughly assess the effectiveness of our breast tumor segmentation model, we employ a range of metrics that measure various elements of the quality of the segmentation. These metrics are defined in terms of True Positives (TP), False Positives (FP), True Negatives (TN), and False Negatives (FN). The metrics encompass the following6,33,65–70:

The Dice coefficient, also referred to as the Dice similarity index, assesses the degree of overlap between the predicted segmentation mask and the ground truth mask. This indicator has a scale from 0 to 1, where a value of 1 represents complete overlap. It is especially beneficial for assessing medical image segmentation tasks, where achieving a high degree of overlap between anticipated and genuine regions is essential.

IoU, or Intersection over Union, is a metric that quantifies the proportion of overlap between the predicted and ground truth masks by calculating the ratio of their intersection to their union. IoU offers a thorough assessment of the quality of segmentation, with values that span from 0 to 1. Greater IoU values suggest superior segmentation performance.

Precision measures the degree of correctness in the positive predictions provided by the model. High precision implies a low percentage of false positives, indicating that the model accurately identifies tumor locations without mistakenly classifying non-tumor areas as tumors.

Recall, or sensitivity, quantifies the model’s capacity to accurately detect all pertinent occurrences of the tumor. A high recall value shows that the model has a low percentage of false negatives, meaning it can accurately detect the majority of tumor locations.

The F1 score is a statistical measure that calculates the harmonic mean of precision and memory. It is used to provide a single metric that effectively balances both precision and recall. The F1 score is especially valuable when working with imbalanced datasets, as it considers both false positives and false negatives. Accuracy is a metric that evaluates the overall validity of the predictions made by the model. Accuracy is a broad indicator of performance, although it may not be as relevant as other metrics when dealing with imbalanced datasets.

Two dropout rates (0.2 and 0.5) were chosen to assess the impact of varying regularization strengths on segmentation efficacy. Lower values, such as 0.2, offer moderate regularization, aiding in the mitigation of overfitting while preserving greater feature information. A higher value of 0.5 provides more robust regularization, which reduces the risk of overfitting in deeper layers. However, this may result in a trade-off in feature retention. Exceedingly low dropout rates may be inadequate for mitigating overfitting, whilst excessively high rates could excessively hinder learning. Consequently, these two settings were selected to delineate a reasonable lower and upper bound for this study. While other rates were not examined because to computational limitations, the selected values facilitate a distinct comparison between moderate and severe regularization impacts.

For example, when augmentation is combined with greater dropout rates, it can occasionally result in decreased precision and accuracy. This emphasizes the importance of integrating these techniques carefully.

Overall, the model that outperforms all others is “UNet + ConvNeXt tiny + Attention box 2 + Skip connections + Dropout(0.5)” because it excels in maintaining a balance between multiple important measures. This model achieves a favorable equilibrium between maximizing tumor detection (recall) and limiting false positives (precision), resulting in a strong and dependable segmentation performance that is well-suited for clinical applications. The deliberate integration of attention mechanisms and a suitable dropout rate is crucial for maximizing the model’s performance across several measures. We conducted paired two-tailed t-tests on the Dice coefficient and IoU scores comparing the top-performing configuration (Model 9) and each of the other configurations to confirm that the observed performance differences between model configurations were statistically significant. The null hypothesis stated that there was no substantial disparity in model performances. A significance level of p < 0.05 was utilized, incorporating Bonferroni correction to adjust for multiple comparisons. This investigation confirmed that performance enhancements were not due to random fluctuations and offered compelling evidence for the resilience of the suggested architecture.

To examine the inconsistent impact of data augmentation on performance, we conducted an internal ablation study to isolate the effects of each modification type on the BUSI dataset. Augmentations were implemented individually, maintaining all other parameters consistent with Model 1 (baseline). Mild transformations, including ± 10° rotation and ± 10% zoom, resulted in minor enhancements in the Dice coefficient (+ 0.007 to + 0.013). In contrast, more severe transformations—specifically 20° rotations, horizontal flips, and shears up to 20%—consistently diminished Dice scores by 0.012–0.038 on the validation set. Visual inspection indicated that these changes sometimes affected tumor boundary morphology or produced anatomically implausible structures, resulting in segmentation masks that did not correspond with lesion contours. Significantly, horizontal flips modified breast laterality, potentially creating implausible clinical situations, while greater rotations occasionally shortened the lesion region at the image borders. The degradation patterns were particularly evident in models lacking attention mechanisms, indicating that attention modules can partially alleviate augmentation-induced noise but cannot entirely rectify unrealistic structural changes.

Assessment on the BUS-UCLM dataset

To strengthen the assessment of the generalizability of our proposed strategy, we performed additional experiments utilizing the BUS-UCLM dataset71, which comprises expert-annotated breast ultrasound images accompanied by associated ground truth segmentations and multiclass labels. The collection consists of 683 images, categorized into 90 malignant (13.2%), 174 benign (25.5%), and 419 normal (61.3%) classifications. For this assessment, we utilized the model trained solely on the BUSI dataset, without any fine-tuning or supplementary training on the BUS-UCLM samples. This cross-dataset evaluation rigorously tests the model’s robustness and transferability across different clinical sources. The complete BUS-UCLM dataset was partitioned into a training set including 545 images, a validation set containing 69 images, and a test set al.so consisting of 69 images, with each image scaled to 224 × 224 pixels. Segmentation masks and classification labels were employed for thorough assessment.

Conclusion

This research examined fourteen configurations of UNet + ConvNeXt-Tiny for breast tumor segmentation. All models were trained and assessed using the BUSI dataset, hence determining their in-domain efficacy. The BUS-UCLM dataset was subsequently used to evaluate the generalization of the same trained model, without any fine-tuning. This two-step protocol distinguishes in-domain proficiency from cross-domain resilience.

In many trials, attention mechanisms consistently enhanced performance, enhancing F1 score, Dice coefficient, precision, and recall by allowing the model to concentrate on tumor-relevant information while diminishing background noise. Data augmentation alone failed to produce consistent enhancements and, in several instances, caused distortions that diminished accuracy. Nonetheless, when combined with attention modules, augmentation frequently yielded further improvements, especially in recall and Intersection over Union (IoU).

In both datasets, design decisions consistently influenced performance. Data augmentation alone (Model 2) did not consistently enhance outcomes compared to the baseline (Model 1) and occasionally diminished precision/recall, suggesting that augmentation noise can impede transfer when not accompanied by supplementary inductive bias.

The initial model, referred to as Model 1, obtained the following metrics: a loss of 0.456, an accuracy of 0.926, a precision of 0.489, a recall of 0.596, an F1 score of 0.528, an IoU of 0.362, and a Dice coefficient of 0.528. This arrangement exhibited a commendable performance; however, there is scope for enhancements, namely in terms of accuracy and precision.

Implementing data augmentation in Model 2 led to a marginal decline in overall performance, with a loss of 0.534 and an accuracy of 0.907. The precision and recall values decreased to 0.395 and 0.548, respectively. This implies that although augmentation can enhance the resilience of the model, it may also create noise that the model finds difficult to generalize from. Subsequent models were enhanced by incorporating dropout and attention strategies to tackle these difficulties.

The inclusion of attention processes and skip connections in Models 3 and 4 resulted in an enhanced trade-off between precision and recall. The accuracy and recall metrics for Model 3, which had a dropout rate of 0.5, were 0.434 and 0.566, respectively. On the other hand, Model 4, with a dropout rate of 0.2, obtained a precision of 0.486 and a recall of 0.534. These setups demonstrate that attention processes enhance the ability to concentrate on pertinent information, resulting in improved F1 score and IoU metrics.

The incorporation of dual attention boxes in models 5 and 6 yielded varied outcomes. Model 5, which had a greater dropout rate, had increased loss and decreased precision, suggesting the possibility of over-regularization. Model 6, on the other hand, demonstrated a more equitable performance, with an enhanced F1 score of 0.514 and an IoU of 0.351. This implies that employing a lower dropout rate could be advantageous when utilizing dual attention boxes.

The top-performing models in this study were Models 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 14. These models utilized a combination of different attention mechanisms and dropout rates. Model 12 exhibited an exceptional precision of 0.632, showing a high level of accuracy in its predictions. However, it had a poorer recall, suggesting that it was more focused on making specific predictions rather than capturing all relevant instances. The performance of Model 11 was consistent and well-rounded across all parameters, indicating that the integration of dual attention boxes and skip connections with dropout creates a strong and reliable architecture for segmentation tasks.

Although data augmentation is a commonly utilized method to enhance generalization, our findings suggest that specific augmentation procedures may adversely affect medical imaging tasks. Geometric modifications, including significant rotations, shearing, and extreme scaling, can generate anatomically unrealistic differences in breast ultrasound images, modifying lesion borders or introducing aberrations that do not align with true clinical findings. Such distortions can misguide the model into acquiring features that are either unnecessary or nonexistent in actual scenarios, resulting in diminished accuracy or inconsistent performance. Moreover, in contrast to natural image domains characterized by extensive object invariances, medical imaging tasks frequently rely on nuanced textural and morphological indicators that may be compromised by excessive augmentation. This indicates that augmentation procedures for medical imaging should be meticulously chosen and validated, preferably in collaboration with domain specialists, to guarantee that they improve robustness without sacrificing anatomical accuracy.

To summarize, our study shows that incorporating attention mechanisms and using suitable dropout rates greatly improves the performance of the UNet architecture with ConvNeXt small. Also, data augmentation on its own did not consistently enhance the performance of the model and occasionally resulted in a decrease. Nevertheless, when attention mechanisms are combined with data augmentation, certain configurations demonstrate enhancements, indicating that attention mechanisms assist the model in concentrating on pertinent aspects and diminishing the noise caused by augmentation. Attention mechanisms, especially when tuned, regularly improved model performance, showcasing their efficacy in enhancing the segmentation job. Subsequent research should prioritize the optimization of augmentation strategies and attention mechanism configurations to further improve the resilience and generalizability of the model.

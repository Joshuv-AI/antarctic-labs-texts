Improved Approximations for the Unsplittable Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem
Jingyang Zhao, Mingyu Xiao

Abstract
The capacitated vehicle routing problem (CVRP) is one of the most extensively studied problems in combinatorial optimization. In this problem, we are given a depot and a set of customers, each with a demand, embedded in a metric space. The objective is to find a set of tours, each starting and ending at the depot, operated by the capacititated vehicle at the depot to serve all customers, such that all customers are served and the total travel cost is minimized. We consider the unplittable variant, where the demand of each customer must be served entirely by a single tour. Res. 2025), which can be further improved by a small constant using the result of Blauth, Traub, and Vygen (Math. Program. 2023). In this paper, we propose two improved approximation algorithms. 1
Introduction

The vehicle routing problems form a fundamental class of combinatorial optimization problems with numerous applications in logistics, transportation, and supply chain management
(
39
)
, and have been studied extensively in operations research.
Among the various vehicle routing problems, the capacitated vehicle routing problem
(
19
)
is one of the most well-studied problems.

In the capacitated vehicle routing problem, we are given an undirected complete graph
G=(V\cup\{r\},E)
with a metric edge-cost function
c:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
that satisfies the triangle inequality.
The vertex set
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
represents
n
customers, where each customer
v\in V
has an integer demand
d_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
A vehicle with integer capacity
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is initially located at the depot
r
.
A tour is a walk that starts and ends at the depot, serves customers with total demand at most
k
, and has a cost equal to the sum of the costs of all edges in the tour.

The objective of the capacitated vehicle routing problem is to find a set of tours for the vehicle that serve all customers while minimizing the total travel cost.
In the
unsplittable
version of the problem, each customer’s demand must be served entirely by a single tour, whereas in the
splittable
version, a customer’s demand may be served by multiple tours.
If each customer has unit demand, the problem is referred to as the
unit-demand
version.

When
k=1
or
k=2
, the capacitated vehicle routing problem can be solved in polynomial time
(
7
)
. However, for each fixed
k\geq 3
, the problem becomes APX-hard, even in the unit-demand case
(
6
)
(see also the restatement in the appendix of
(
42
)
).
When
k=\infty
, the capacitated vehicle routing problem reduces to the well-known metric traveling salesman problem, which admits a
\frac{3}{2}
-approximation algorithm
(
16
)
.
Recently,
31
,
32
improved the approximation ratio to strictly less than
\frac{3}{2}-10^{-36}
.
In the following, we use
\alpha
to denote the current best-known approximation ratio for the metric traveling salesman problem, regardless of the specific algorithm achieving it.

27
introduced the iterated tour partition algorithm for the unit-demand capacitated vehicle routing problem.
Based on an
\alpha
-approximate traveling salesman problem tour on
V
, the iterated tour partition algorithm achieves an approximation ratio of
\alpha(1-\frac{\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil}{n})+\frac{\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil}{n/k}
.
Later,
3
showed that, given an
\alpha
-approximate traveling salesman problem tour on
V\cup\{r\}
, the iterated tour partition algorithm achieves an approximation ratio of
\alpha+1-\frac{\alpha}{k}
for both the unit-demand capacitated vehicle routing problem and the splittable capacitated vehicle routing problem.
Moreover,
2
modified the iterated tour partition algorithm to handle the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem, achieving an approximation ratio of
\alpha+2-\frac{2\alpha}{k}
for even
k
and
\alpha+2-\frac{\alpha}{k}
for odd
k
.

Subsequently,
29

\alpha+\frac{k-\alpha}{\left\lceil k/2\right\rceil}
for the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem, which equals
\alpha+2-\frac{2\alpha}{k}
for even
k
, and
\alpha+2-\frac{2\alpha}{k}-\frac{2(k-\alpha)}{k(k+1)}
for odd
k
.
Hence, this result improves upon the previous approximation ratio of
\alpha+2-\frac{\alpha}{k}
for odd
k
.
Later,
14
made several improvements.
For any
\alpha\geq 1
, they improved the approximation ratio by a term of at least
\frac{1}{3k^{3}}
for all three versions of the capacitated vehicle routing problem.
In the specific case of
\alpha=\frac{3}{2}
, the improvement is at least
\frac{1}{4k^{2}}
for the splittable and unit-demand cases, and at least
\frac{1}{3k^{2}}
for the unsplittable case.

For the case of general vehicle capacity, one significant progress was made by
13

\alpha+1-\varepsilon
for the splittable and unit-demand cases, and to
\alpha+2-2\varepsilon
for the unsplittable case, where
\varepsilon
is a constant related to
\alpha

\varepsilon>\frac{1}{3000}
when
\alpha=\frac{3}{2}
.

24

(\alpha+1.75)
-approximation algorithm using a combinatorial method, while the second is an
(\alpha+\ln 2+\frac{1}{1-\delta})
-approximation algorithm based on linear programming rounding, with a running time of
n^{\mathcal{O}({1}/{\delta})}
.
They also showed that both approximations can be further improved by a small constant through the method from
(
13
)
.

The capacitated vehicle routing problem has attracted independent interest for small values of
k
, such as
k=3

(
8
)
and
k=4

(
4

For the unit-demand capacitated vehicle routing problem with

For the unit-demand capacitated vehicle routing problem with

More recently,

Other Related Work

Although only limited progress has been made for the capacitated vehicle routing problem on general metrics, a substantial body of work has focused on structured metrics.
We briefly review some representative results below.

The capacitated vehicle routing problem in Euclidean Spaces.

Consider the unit-demand capacitated vehicle routing problem.
In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a polynomial-time approximation scheme is known for
k=\mathcal{O}(\log\log n)

(
27
)
,
k=\mathcal{O}(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})
or
k=\Omega(n)

(
7
)
, and
k\leq 2^{\log^{f(\varepsilon)}n}

(
1
)
.
Moreover, a quasi PTAS with running time
n^{\log^{\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon)}n}
is known for arbitrary
k

(
20
)
.
This running time was later improved to
n^{\mathcal{O}(\log^{6}n/\varepsilon^{5})}

(
30
)
, where the authors also established a quasi PTAS for graphs of bounded treewidth, bounded doubling metrics, and bounded highway dimension with arbitrary
k
.
In
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, for any fixed
\ell
, a polynomial-time approximation scheme is known for
k=\mathcal{O}(\log^{1/\ell}n)

(
33
)
.

Furthermore,
26
proposed a
(2+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm for the unplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, while
23
proposed a
(2-\tau)
-approximation algorithm for the unit-demand capacitated vehicle routing problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

The capacitated vehicle routing problem on Tree Metrics.

On tree metrics, the splittable capacitated vehicle routing problem is NP-hard

)
, while the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem is NP-hard to approximate within a factor better than
\frac{3}{2}

(

unless

For the splittable capacitated vehicle routing problem on tree metrics,

This ratio was subsequently improved to

For the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem on tree metrics,

The capacitated vehicle routing problem on Other Structured Metrics.

Consider the unit-demand capacitated vehicle routing problem with

1.2
Our Contributions

In this paper, we propose improved approximation algorithms for the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem.

First, we revisit the iterated tour partition algorithm proposed by
2
and its variant
\delta
-iterated tour partition for the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem introduced by
24
.
We slightly improve the performance of
\delta
-iterated tour partition by using trivial tours to serve customers with demand
d_{v}>\frac{1}{2}k
.
We denote the resulting algorithm by
\delta
-iterated tour partition+.
Based on
\delta
-iterated tour partition+, we design two algorithms for the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem.

The first algorithm (
Alg
.1) focuses on the case where the vehicle capacity
k
is fixed. It runs in
n^{\mathcal{O}(k)}
time and achieves an approximation ratio of
\alpha+1+\ln\left(2-\frac{1}{2}y_{0}\right)<\alpha+1.5897
, where
y_{0}>0.39312
is the unique root of the equation
\ln\left(2-\frac{1}{2}y\right)=\frac{3}{2}y
.
Hence, the approximation ratio is at most
3.0897
when
\alpha=1.5
.

Alg
.1 is based on two sub-algorithms.
The first sub-algorithm (
SubAlg.1
) is the same as the previous combinatorial algorithm of
24
, which first applies a matching-based approach to serve all customers with demand
d_{v}>\frac{1}{3}k
and then uses
\delta
-iterated tour partition with
\delta=\frac{1}{3}
to serve the remaining customers.
The second sub-algorithm (
SubAlg.2
) combines the linear programming-rounding technique from
(
24
)
with
\delta
-iterated tour partition+.
Since each tour serves at most
k
customers, there are at most
n^{\mathcal{O}(k)}
distinct tours.

SubAlg.2
formulates and solves an linear programming whose variables correspond to these
n^{\mathcal{O}(k)}
tours, and randomly rounds the resulting solution to select a set of tours serving some customers.
It then uses
\delta
-iterated tour partition+ by simply setting
\delta=\frac{1}{3}
to serve the remaining customers.
Through a careful analysis, we show that taking the better solution returned by these two sub-algorithms achieves the claimed approximation ratio.

The second algorithm (
Alg
.2) focuses on the case of general vehicle capacity
k
.
It runs in
n^{\mathcal{O}(1/\delta)}
time and achieves an approximation ratio of
\alpha+1+y_{1}+\ln\left(2-2y_{1}\right)+\delta<\alpha+1.6759
for any small constant
0<\delta<10^{-5}
, where
y_{1}>0.17458
is the unique root of the equation
\frac{1}{2}y_{1}+6(1-y_{1})\left(1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}y_{1}}\right)=\ln\left(2-2y_{1}\right)
.
Hence, the approximation ratio is at most
3.1759
when
\alpha=1.5
.

Alg
.2 is based on three sub-algorithms.
The first sub-algorithm is the same as
SubAlg.1
.
The second sub-algorithm (
SubAlg.3
) is similar to
SubAlg.2
. However, since
n^{\mathcal{O}(k)}
is not polynomial when
k
is general, it considers only
n^{\mathcal{O}(1/\delta)}
distinct tours that serve customers with demand
d_{v}>\delta\cdot k
. Accordingly, it formulates and solves an linear programming whose variables correspond to these
n^{\mathcal{O}(1/\delta)}
tours, and randomly rounds the resulting solution to select a set of tours serving some customers with demand
d_{v}>\delta\cdot k
. The remaining customers are then served using
\frac{1}{3}
-iterated tour partition+.
The third sub-algorithm (
SubAlg.4
) also employs the linear programming-rounding technique as in
SubAlg.3
, but uses
\delta
-iterated tour partition+, instead of
\frac{1}{3}
-iterated tour partition+, to serve the remaining customers.

Finally, we show that both approximations can be further improved by a small constant using the result of
13
.

Preliminary

In the capacitated vehicle routing problem, an input instance is represented as
I=(G,c,d,k)
, where
G=(V\cup\{r\},E)
is an undirected complete graph with vertex set
V\cup\{r\}
and edge set
E=(V\cup\{r\})\times(V\cup\{r\})
.
Here,
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
denotes the customers, and
r
represents the depot, where a vehicle of capacity
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is initially located.
Each edge has a non-negative cost given by
c:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and
c
is a
metric
, satisfying
c(x,x)=0
(reflexivity),
c(x,y)=c(y,x)
(symmetry), and
c(x,y)\leq c(x,z)+c(z,y)
(triangle inequality) for all
x,y,z\in V\cup\{r\}
.
The demand of each customer is specified by
d:V\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, where
d_{v}
denotes the demand of customer
v
.

A
walk

W
in
G
, denoted by
(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\ell})
, is a sequence of vertices in
V\cup\{r\}
, where vertices may be repeated, and each consecutive pair of vertices is connected by an edge in
E
.
Let
E(W)
denote the (multi-)set of edges it contains, and let
V(W)
denote the set of customers visited by
W
.
The cost of
W
is defined as
c(W)=\sum_{e\in E(W)}c(e)
.
A
path
is a walk in which no vertex appears more than once.
The first and last vertices of a path are referred to as its
terminals
.
A
closed
walk is a walk in which the first and the last vertices coincide, and
a
cycle
is a closed walk in which only the first and the last vertices are the same.
A
tour
is a cycle of the form
(r,v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\ell},r)
, where each
v_{i}
is a customer.
A tour visiting a single customer, e.g.,
(r,v_{1},r)
, is called
trivial
.
A traveling salesman problem tour on
V^{\prime}\subseteq V\cup\{r\}
is a cycle that visits every vertex in
V^{\prime}
in the induced subgraph
G[V^{\prime}]
.

Given a closed walk, by the triangle inequality, one can skip repeated vertices along the walk to obtain a cycle of non-increasing cost.
Such an operation is called
shortcutting
.
For any cost function
c:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we extend it to subsets of
X
by defining
w(Y)=\sum_{x\in Y}w(x)
for any
Y\subseteq X
.

The capacitated vehicle routing problem can be described as follows.

Definition 1

(
The capacitated vehicle routing problem
)
.

Given a capacitated vehicle routing problem instance
I=(G,c,d,k)
, the objective is to construct a set of tours
\mathcal{T}
with a demand assignment
\lambda:V\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that

1.

each tour delivers serves total demand at most
k
, i.e.,
\sum_{v\in V(T)}\lambda_{v,T}\leq k
for every
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

2.

each tour serves only customers on the tour, i.e.,
\sum_{v\in V\setminus V(T)}\lambda_{v,T}=0
for every
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

3.

the demands of all customers are fully satisfied, i.e.,
\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\lambda_{v,T}=d_{v}
for every
v\in V
,

and
c(\mathcal{T})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}c(T)
is minimized.

By the triangle inequality, we assume with loss of generality that
\lambda_{v,T}=0
if and only if
v\in V\setminus V(T)
.
According to the properties of the demand assignment, we distinguish the following variants of the problem.
If each customer’s demand must be served entirely by a single tour, the capacitated vehicle routing problem is called the
unsplittable
capacitated vehicle routing problem.
If a customer’s demand may be served by multiple tours, the capacitated vehicle routing problem is called the
splittable
capacitated vehicle routing problem.
If each customer has unit demand, the capacitated vehicle routing problem is called the
unit-demand
capacitated vehicle routing problem.

We mainly consider the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem.
By definition, we have
d_{v}\leq k
for all
v\in V
.
By scaling down the vehicle capacity and customer demands by a factor of
k
, we may assume without loss of generality that the vehicle capacity is
k=1
, and that each customer has a demand of at most
1
.
Moreover, we use
OPT
to denote the cost of an optimal solution to the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem.

3
The Iterated Tour Partition Algorithms

27
proposed the iterated tour partition algorithm for the (unit-demand) capacitated vehicle routing problem, and
2
later adapted the iterated tour partition algorithm to handle the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem.
Given a traveling salesman problem tour
\mathcal{A}
on
V\cup\{r\}
, the iterated tour partition algorithm partitions
\mathcal{A}
into paths, each serving at most
k
demand, and then connects the terminals of each path to the depot to form a tour.
The iterated tour partition algorithm can be implemented in
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time.
We have the following result.

Lemma 1

(
2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a traveling salesman problem tour on
V\cup\{r\}
. There exists an
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
-time algorithm, iterated tour partition, that computes a solution to the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem with total cost at most
c(\mathcal{A})+\sum_{v\in V}4d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)
.

Moreover, we have the following well-known lower bounds on the optimal cost of the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem.

Lemma 2

(
27
,
3
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{*}
denote an optimal traveling salesman problem tour on
V\cup\{r\}
. Then, it holds that
\mbox{OPT}\geq\max\left\{\sum_{v\in V}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v),\ c(\mathcal{A}^{*})\right\}
.

24
proposed a variant of the iterated tour partition algorithm for the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem, denoted as
\delta
-iterated tour partition.
Fix a constant
0\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Customers with demand at most
\delta
are called
small
, and let

V^{\delta}_{s}\coloneq\{v\in V\mid d_{v}\leq\delta\}
denote the set of small customers.
We have the following result.

Lemma 3

(
24
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a traveling salesman problem tour on
V\cup\{r\}
. There exists an
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
-time algorithm,
\delta
-iterated tour partition, that computes a solution to the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem with total cost at most

c(\mathcal{A})+\frac{1}{1-\delta}\sum_{v\in V^{\delta}_{s}}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)+\frac{2}{1-\delta}\sum_{v\in V\setminus V^{\delta}_{s}}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)-\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\sum_{v\in V\setminus V^{\delta}_{s}}{2c(r,v)}

Notably, when
\delta=0
, the
\delta
-iterated tour partition algorithm reduces to the classic iterated tour partition algorithm in Lemma
1
.

Let
V^{\delta}_{b}\coloneq\{v\in V\mid\delta<d_{v}\leq\frac{1}{2}\}
and
V^{\delta}_{l}\coloneq\{v\in V\mid\frac{1}{2}<d_{v}\leq 1\}
denote the sets of
big
customers and
large
customers, respectively.
Instead of directly applying the
\delta
-iterated tour partition algorithm in Lemma
3
, we slightly improve its performance by pre-processing customers with large demands.
Specifically, for each large customer
v\in V^{\delta}_{l}
, we first assign a trivial tour
(r,v,r)
to serve its demand.
We then apply the
\delta
-iterated tour partition algorithm to serve all customers in
V^{\delta}_{s}\cup V^{\delta}_{b}
.
We refer to the resulting algorithm as
\delta
-iterated tour partition
+
.
We obtain the following result.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a traveling salesman problem tour on
V\cup\{r\}
. There exists an
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
-time algorithm,
\delta
-iterated tour partition+, that computes a solution to the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem with total cost at most

c(\mathcal{A})+\frac{1}{1-\delta}\sum_{v\in V^{\delta}_{s}}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)+\frac{2}{1-\delta}\sum_{v\in V^{\delta}_{b}}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)-\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\sum_{v\in V^{\delta}_{b}}{2c(r,v)}+\sum_{v\in V^{\delta}_{l}}2c(r,v)

Since
\frac{2d_{v}-\delta}{1-\delta}\geq 1
holds for every large customer
v\in V^{\delta}_{l}
, the cost bound in Lemma
4
is no larger than that in Lemma
3
.
Although this improvement is slight, it is essential for achieving our approximation results.

Following the terminology in
(
41
)
, we define

\int^{r}_{l}x^{t}dF(x)\coloneqq\frac{\sum_{v\in V:l<d_{v}\leq r}2d^{t}_{v}\cdot c(r,v)}{\sum_{v\in V}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)},\quad\quad\mbox{where}\quad t\in\{0,1\}.

(1)

By definition, we have

\int^{1}_{0}xdF(x)=1.

(2)

Moreover, for any
0\leq l\leq r\leq 1
, we have the following inequality:

l\cdot\int^{r}_{l}1dF(x)<\int^{r}_{l}xdF(x)\leq r\cdot\int^{r}_{l}1dF(x).

(3)

We now obtain the following approximation guarantee by combining Lemmas
2
and
4
.

Lemma 5
.

Using an
\alpha
-approximate traveling salesman problem tour
\mathcal{A}
on
V\cup\{r\}
, the
\delta
-iterated tour partition+ algorithm in Lemma
4
computes a solution to the unsplittable capacitated vehicle routing problem with approximation ratio at most

\alpha+\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}xdF(x)+\frac{2}{1-\delta}\int_{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}xdF(x)-\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\int_{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}1dF(x)+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}1dF(x).

Proof.

By Lemma

13
)
.

For hard capacitated vehicle routing problem instances, there exists a function
f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\lim_{{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}}f(\varepsilon)=0
and a polynomial-time algorithm that computes a traveling salesman problem tour
\mathcal{A}
on
V\cup\{r\}
such that
c(\mathcal{A})\leq(1+f(\varepsilon))\cdot\mbox{OPT}
.

The details of the function
f
in Lemma
12
are provided in Appendix
A
.

Currently, the best-known approximation ratio for the metric traveling salesman problem is
\alpha\approx\frac{3}{2}-10^{-36}

(
32
)
.
We fix the constant
\varepsilon>0
to be sufficiently small so that
1+f(\varepsilon)<\alpha
.

On one hand, for hard capacitated vehicle routing problem instances, by using the TSP tour in Lemma
12
, the previous approximation ratios of
Alg
.1 and
Alg
.2 can be directly improved.
On the other hand, for simple capacitated vehicle routing problem instances, since the previous approximation ratios were derived using the bound
\sum_{v\in V}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)\leq\mbox{OPT}
from Lemma
2
, we can instead apply the stronger bound
\sum_{v\in V}2d_{v}\cdot c(r,v)\leq(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\mbox{OPT}
to obtain a further improvement.

Therefore, by combining the above two cases, we can further improve the approximation ratios of
Alg
.1 and
Alg
.2 for general capacitated vehicle routing problem instances.

By calculation (see the details in Appendix
B
), we show that, for the case of fixed vehicle capacity, the improvement is at least
0.00031
when
\varepsilon=0.000335
, and the final approximation ratio is at most
3.0894
; for the case of general vehicle capacity, the improvement achieves at least
0.00039
when
\varepsilon=0.000334
, and the final approximation ratio is at most
3.1755
.

Appendix A
The Function

The function
f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, given in
(
13
)
, satisfies that

f(\varepsilon)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\theta\leq 1-\tau\\
0<\tau,\rho\leq 1/6\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1+\zeta}{\theta}+\frac{1-\tau-\theta}{\theta\cdot(1-\tau)}+\frac{3\varepsilon}{1-\theta}+\frac{3\rho}{(1-\rho)\cdot(1-\tau)}\right\}-1,

where
\theta
is let to be
1-\tau
in
(
13
)
and

\zeta=\frac{3\rho+\tau-4\tau\cdot\rho}{1-\rho}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\tau\cdot\rho}\cdot\left(1-\tau\cdot\rho-\frac{3\rho+\tau-4\tau\cdot\rho}{1-\rho}\right).

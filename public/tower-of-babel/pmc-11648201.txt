UPbGeoChronSPZ2024: U-Pb data from the South Portuguese Zone, Iberian Massif
Authors: João Lains Amaral, Ana Rita Solá, Telmo M Bento dos Santos
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractComprehensive isotopic databases of a particular geotectonic domain are valuable resources for geoscientists to develop geological models, such as paleogeographic and tectono-magmatic evolution models. The UPbGeoChronSPZ2024 is an open-access dataset of U-Pb isotopic analyses in minerals, primarily zircon, from igneous and siliciclastic rocks of the South Portuguese Zone, a Laurussian affinity terrain hosting a world-class metallogenic region, the Iberian Pyrite Belt. The dataset includes isotopic measurements and the location of the samples in WGS84 coordinates and it can be easily coupled with other regional or global datasets, making its value beyond its regional character. Furthermore, the dataset includes cutting-edge calculation methods for ages and discordances that allows geoscientists to test different outcomes.Keywords: U-Pb geochronology; Regional database; Detrital and igneous zircon; Filtering, volcanogenic massive sulphides; Avalonia-Meguma; Variscan

Background

The South Portuguese Zone (SPZ), SW Iberian Massif, is a Meguma [1] or Avalonian [2] terrain that collided with Gondwana during the Variscan Orogeny, composed of oceanic sequences related to the SW Iberian suture, epicontinental siliciclastic sequences, continental rift-related sequences, which comprises the largest volcanogenic massive sulphide province in the world, and the turbiditic sequences related to the continental collision [3,4]. Known rocks in the SPZ have been constrained only to the Devonian and Carboniferous [3,4].

U-Pb geochronological studies in the SPZ have been carried out for over 20 year (e.g., [5]), in Devonian siliciclastic rocks (e.g., [1]), in Devonian-Carboniferous volcanic rocks [6], in cassiterite ore [7], in Carboniferous plutonic rocks (e.g., [8]) and in Carboniferous turbiditic rocks (e.g., [9]). Nonetheless, existing U-Pb data for the SPZ is dispersed and of different quality and quantity. The volcanic rocks are often related to the genesis of massive sulphide deposits, which makes the definition of their absolute age and metallogenetic periods crucial, whereas the detrital rocks are usually key for provenance and paleogeographic studies. Another important factor is that new geochronological data has rarely been checked against previous information. Therefore, it has become necessary to build a comprehensive geochronological database that fully encloses the data for the entire tectonostratigraphic domain. This is a preliminary first step to bring new insights to the paleogeographic setting and tectono-magmatic evolution (and related metallogenetic evolution) in the SPZ.

Data Description

The UPbGeochronSPZ2024 gathers published isotopic U-Pb data in minerals into a single-tab excel file from ore, and magmatic and detrital rocks in the South Portuguese Zone, Iberian Massif, European Variscides. Data was retrieved from 30 publications creating a dataset with 6975 individual entries, consisting of 6813 entries for U-Pb in zircon, 146 for U-Pb in cassiterite and 16 for U-Pb in monazite, belonging to a total of 182 outcrop or drill core samples (Fig. 1). The dataset contains unique entry ID, reference of the original publication, sample location, published isotopic ratios and ages, and calculated apparent ages, model ages and discordances. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

We built a comprehensive database – UPbGeoChronSPZ2024 – that has a structure adapted from the spreadsheet-based Global U-Pb Database 2017 [10].

Data collection

The first step to construct the database consisted of collecting 206Pb/238U, 207Pb/235U, and 207Pb/206Pb ratios and ages and respective uncertainties for the entire U-Pb geochronological published data from the South Portuguese Zone (see Table S2 for full references). For cases that the authors published a full data spreadsheet, the isotopic ratios inserted for the dataset follows authors selection (e.g., the dataset maintains the uncorrected common lead isotopic values for [2]). Information about the type of mass spectrometer and mineral analysed was also extracted. It is also referenced the original sample and grain or spots IDs.

Concomitant, information about sample location was also retrieved from the articles, either by using published coordinates or using map location. In the case of drillhole sample, borehole collar coordinates were often considered. Prospect areas were related to a mining area region, when applicable, whereas locality refers to the closest population centre.

Lithostratigraphic groups and sub-groups, which correspond to the main divisions of the South Portuguese Zone and their formations (see [4] for further information on this matter) were inserted in the database accordingly to articles information. In addition, sample rock types, as pre-defined in S. Puetz [11], as well as, if applicable, their superimposed alteration products, were added following original sample description.

A series of suggested cut-offs for each discordance were also highlighted using the conditional format function of Excel. These cut-offs are merely indicative, and the user is free to use any other cut-offs.

For the normal relative discordance (Calc.dr68/76) it was set up a cut-off |10.5|, similar to the most common used cut-off for this discordance.

For the atypical relative discordance (Calc.dr68/75) it is suggested a cut-off of |3.5|. As shown in [17], which follows a similar approach developed by [14], a cut-off |3.5| for this discordance removes the same amount of data as applying a cut-off |10.5| for Calc.dr68/76.

For the Stacey-Kremers discordance, a lower cut-off -0.59 and an upper cut-off + 3.2 was emplaced. As shown by P. Vermeesch [14], these cut-offs remove the same fraction of data as for a cut-off |10| for Calc.dr68/76.

For the Aitchison Distance discordance, a lower cut-off -2.3 and an upper cut-off + 3.2 was set up, as suggested by P. Vermeesch [16], which follows the same relation with the normal relative discordance as previously mentioned.

For the Concordia Distance discordance, it is suggested that a cut-off of |4.8| removes the same fraction as a cut-off |10.5| for Calc.dr68/76 (see [17]). However, P. Vermeesch [16] suggests a lower cut-off -3 and an upper cut-off of 3.9 to remove the same amount of data while applying a cut-off |10| for Calc.dr68/76.

For the absolute discordances (Calc.da), a cut-off 91 is highlighted.

For the Concordant class discordance (Calc.dcc), a cut-off 5 is set up as suggested by S. Puetz [13] for detrital data. However, these authors suggest a cut-off of 3 for igneous data. According to S. Puetz [13] class 1 to 4 are concordant, and class 5 is marginal concordant.

Limitations

We compile all available U-Pb data in minerals of the South Portuguese Zone published in international journals. Data only available in theses have not been included.

The database mostly contains the approximately location of the samples, as often exact coordinates were missing from the articles [18].

The U-Pb data is limited to the available published U-Pb data, often without access to all results (e.g. [19]), which therefore do not full fill most recent recommendations for reporting U-Pb data (e.g., [20]).

The database contains mostly corrected common Pb ratios. However, according to P. Vermeesch [14] discordance should be calculated before common Pb correction. Also, common Pb weights or ratios were not included in the database.

Cost-Effectiveness of Dapagliflozin in the Treatment of Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease in Denmark
Authors: Thea Tanderup Kjær, Morten Lindhardt, Anders Hammerich Riis, Henrik Birn, Lars Holger Ehlers
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rationale & ObjectiveChronic kidney disease (CKD) is a growing burden worldwide and in Denmark, with substantial clinical and economic consequences. Although national guidelines recommend sodium/glucose transporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) for patients with CKD, type 2 diabetes (T2D) or heart failure (HF), underdiagnosis limits the use of SGLT2i. This study aims to inform decision makers in health care about the cost-effectiveness of current guidelines for CKD in Denmark focusing on SGLT2i dapagliflozin added to standard of care (SoC) versus SoC alone in patients with CKD.Study DesignCost-effectiveness analysis based on a Markov model with a lifetime horizon.Setting & ParticipantsDanish health care payer perspective including a broad population with CKD and subgroups stratified by urinary albumin-creatine ratio (UACR), T2D status, and HF history.ExposuresDapagliflozin plus SoC versus SoC alone.OutcomesQuality-adjusted life years (QALYs), life-years, total costs, and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs).Analytical ApproachA published Markov model was populated using data from the DAPA-CKD and DECLARE-TIMI 58 trials. Analyses were conducted for the overall population and stratified subgroups. Results were evaluated against an assumed willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of €24,158 per QALY.ResultsDapagliflozin treatment resulted in health benefits (QALYs) and life-years versus SoC irrespective of T2D/HF status or UACR levels. Dapagliflozin was cost-effective for the total population with an ICER of €7,499/QALY and dominant for subgroups with high UACR (≥200 mg/g) regardless of T2D/HF status. ICERs were below WTP threshold for all subgroups. However, for patients with CKD and low UACR (<200 mg/g) without T2D/HF, time to cost-effectiveness was long, and estimates were uncertain.LimitationsResults are limited by generalizability, long-term uncertainty, simulated subgroup data, cost uncertainty, and lack of a Danish WTP threshold.ConclusionsThis study indicates that dapagliflozin added to SoC is cost-effective across CKD subgroups as recommended in the current Danish guideline recommendations.

The global prevalence of chronic kidney disease (CKD) is likely to increase in the future because of population growth, aging, and increased numbers of people with diabetes and hypertension.1, 2, 3, 4 This has major clinical and societal impact as even mild or moderate kidney disease is associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular disease, acute kidney injury, and premature death.5 In Denmark, approximately 9.5% of the adult population has CKD.6 The median age at the time of CKD in Denmark is 74 years (interquartile range [IQR], 65-80 years), and currently one in four incident CKD patients die within 5 years.6

Sodium/glucose transporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) reduce the risk of kidney disease progression and cardiovascular disease in patients with CKD.7, 8, 9 The DAPA-CKD trial was the first trial to show the benefits of SGLT2i in patients with CKD irrespective of type 2 diabetes (T2D) status by reporting significantly lower risk of the primary endpoints (sustained decline in the estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] of at least 50%, end stage kidney disease [ESKD], or death from kidney or cardiovascular causes) and death from any cause for dapagliflozin versus standard of care (SoC).7 Subsequently, empagliflozin has been shown to decrease the risk of CKD progression or death from cardiovascular causes compared with placebo in patients with CKD with or without T2D.8

The current treatment guidelines for CKD in Denmark include lifestyle changes, blood pressure control as well as angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) in patients with CKD and albuminuria or diabetes.10 Patients with CKD with eGFR > 20 mL/min/1.73 m2 and a urinary albumin-creatine ratio (UACR) > 200 mg/g are currently recommended SGLT2i.10 Furthermore, SGLT2i should be considered for all patients with heart failure (HF) or T2D depending on UACR and eGFR level.11,12 Barriers to optimal CKD management include implementation of guideline directed medical therapy, lack of a diagnostic code for CKD in primary care as well as underutilization of UACR measurements in primary care affecting the identification of early stages of CKD.13,14 Ultimately, the barriers limit the proportion of patients with CKD in Denmark receiving SGLT2i. Data suggest that less than 40% of the eligible population in primary care is prescribed SGLT2i.14 This raises questions regarding the priority in clinical practice and cost-effectiveness of SGLT2i according to the current CKD guidelines in Denmark. However, no prior Danish-specific evaluation of SGLT2i cost-effectiveness has been published, which could guide implementation and reimbursement.

This study aimed to investigate the lifetime cost-effectiveness of the current Danish guideline implementation for CKD in relation to UACR levels, diabetes status or history of HF, with focus on dapagliflozin added to the SoC (ACE and ARB) compared with SoC alone from a Danish health care payer perspective.

Materials and Methods

Study design

A cost-utility analysis (CUA) was conducted from a Danish national health care perspective to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of SGLT2i focusing on dapagliflozin plus SoC compared with SoC over a lifetime (30 years) horizon. This analysis was based on a previously published Markov model adapted to a Danish setting with updated model inputs using Danish registry data to reflect local epidemiology, clinical practice, and costs (Fig S1).15 The model data were derived from the DAPA-CKD and DECLARE-TIMI 58 trials and follow the approach of McEwan et al. 2024.7,15,16 The primary outcomes of the model were net monetary benefit (NMB) and incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) with effectiveness measured in quality adjusted life years (QALY) and life years (LY) gained. The ICER is the traditional summary result from a cost-effectiveness analysis that represents the additional cost required to achieve an additional unit of health benefit when comparing one health care intervention to another.17 In NMB, the difference in effect between 2 options being evaluated is rescaled into monetary value by multiplying the incremental effectiveness with the WTP threshold and subtracting the incremental costs.17 Given the absence of an official willingness-to-pay threshold (WTP) in Denmark, a conservative threshold at €24,158/QALY was applied corresponding to the lower end of the range (£20,000 to £30,000/QALY, GBP/EUR exchange rate = 9.01) commonly cited in National Institute of Health and Care Excellence’s (NICE) practice.18 The NICE value is widely applied as a conservative estimate of a threshold value in health economic evaluations in primary care in Denmark.19 Discounting of both costs and health benefits was set at 3.5% per annum according to the Danish Finance Ministry requirements.20

Patient population

The broad Danish CKD population in the analysis was defined as eGFR between 15 and 59 mL/min/1.73 m2, or those with an eGFR between 60 and 90 mL/min/1.73 m2 and a UACR > 30 mg/g. The proportion of patients with low UACR was assumed to be 75% and the remaining 25% with high UACR (>200 mg/g) and corresponds to the distribution in the DECLARECKD population.15,16 In the baseline model population, no patients had kidney failure (ie, no patients received kidney replacement therapy” (Tables 1 and S1-S2). Given the unavailability of a single dataset providing all required data for the broad CKD population, a pooled population from the DAPA-CKD trial and the DECLARE-TIMI 58 trial were applied. Disease progression

Within a monthly cycle, patients with CKD could transit between CKD stages G1 to G5, renal replacement therapy (RRT), or death. RRT included dialysis or kidney transplants. The transition probabilities and risks of serious adverse events (SAEs) in the model were derived from DAPA-CKD, DECLARE-TIMI 58 and a systematic literature review on CKD modelling and are described in detail elsewhere.7,15, 16, 17 The SAEs included volume depletion, major hypoglycemic events, fractures, diabetic ketoacidosis, and amputation. Each year the patients treated with dapagliflozin were assigned a discontinuation rate of 0.0617.7 Furthermore, patients discontinue dapagliflozin after kidney transplantation or when initiating dialysis. The incidence of all-cause mortality was extrapolated using adjusted parametric survival equations (Weibull) and adjusted for all-cause mortality from Statistics Denmark.15,18,19

Utilities

Utilities and disutilities were measured by EuroQoL-5 Dimension 3 Levels (EQ-5D-3L) data (Table 2). Utilities were applied for each health state and adjusted according to age-specific utilities from the general Danish population.40 For each SAE, a specific disutility was applied.

Model validation

The validity of the model was confirmed by running the model for subgroups corresponding to the trial participants in the DAPA-CKD, DECLARE-TIMI 58, and EMPA-KIDNEY trials with similar time horizons reflecting the published follow-up results.7,8,16

Sensitivity analysis

Probabilistic sensitivity analyses (PSAs) were made for all subgroups to evaluate the robustness of the ICERs. Standard error (SE) was assumed to be 30% of the mean cost values and 10% for all remaining values unless literature SE were available. The PSA results were visualized by a cost-effectiveness scatterplot with 500 iterations for each subgroup. Uncertainties identified in collaboration with clinical experts were investigated through sensitivity analyses. Given that the model predicts outcomes for the total population assuming that 25% have high UACR, additional analyses were made assuming a proportion of 15% with high UACR to encounter that a greater proportion of high risk patients with higher albuminuria were likely to be recruited into clinical trials that in the general population.6 Additionally, an analysis was performed to evaluate the impact of a lower CKD transition rate of 0.9 in individuals without T2D compared with those with T2D. Further sensitivity analyses included addition of monitoring costs, adjustments of CKD stage G1 and G2 costs, adjustments of dapagliflozin costs, additional dialysis costs, no discontinuation after dialysis, treatment stopped after 4 years, adjusted baseline population with myocardial infarction (MI), patients exiting the model on RRT, and 0% discounting (Table S3). Additional subgroup analyses included stratification by UACR levels alone and in combination with age (< or > 65 years), eGFR (< or > 45 mL/min/1.73 m2), and history of CVD. Furthermore, the impact of the history of HF was considered on the overall population (Table S3). A tornado diagram of deterministic one-way sensitivity analysis is also shown in Figure S2.

Ethics

According to Danish law, approval from the National Committee on Health Research Ethics was not necessary (§14 stk. 2). Informed consent was waived because of deidentified information.

Results

Characteristics of the CKD population used to model cost-effectiveness, and the relevant subgroups are included in Tables 1, S1, and S2. In the total model CKD population, mean age was 64 years with 33.3% being female, 73% of White race, and 74.5% having T2D. Most patients were CKD stage 3 (58%) with 22% being stage 3a. Among patients without T2D, 30% had glomerulonephritis as the underlying kidney disease.

The total population analysis resulted in an ICER of €7,499/QALY (ΔCost = €3,441 and ΔQALY = 0.46) well below a WTP threshold of €24,158/QALY and a NMB of €7,643. Sensitivity analyses of the total population showed that ICERs remained below the WTP threshold (Tables 3 and S3). The average QALY gain was 0.55, and LY gain was 0.46. Analyses exploring the developments of the NMB over time demonstrated that dapagliflozin was cost-effective in the total population after approximately 8 years (Figs 1 and 2).

For all subgroups, the ICER fell below the WTP threshold (Table 3). The model indicates that subgroups with high UACR were particularly cost-effective. However, longer time is required for cost-effectiveness to be achieved for patients with low UACR and without T2D or history of HF. For patients with low UACR without T2D time to cost-effectiveness is more than 20 years (Figs 1 and 2). The PSA yielded consistent findings across almost all subgroups (Figs 3 and 4). However, simulations of the subgroup with low UACR without T2D showed a higher degree of uncertainty with respect to cost-effectiveness, with ICERs fluctuating above and below the WTP threshold. Discussion

This study is the first to investigate the cost-effectiveness of treatment in accordance with Danish guidelines. Furthermore, it is the first cost-effectiveness study to incorporate history of HF and detailed UACR subgroup classifications. Based on the model CKD population dapagliflozin treatment is cost-effective compared with SoC in all subgroups recommended SGLT2i in the current Danish guidelines assuming a WTP of €24,158/QALY.

The results in this study support the conclusions from a previously published systematic review on cost-effectiveness of dapagliflozin for patients with CKD irrespective of T2D status.41 Additionally, dapagliflozin has shown cost-effectiveness regardless of eGFR, UACR, and T2D status in UK, Spain, Italy and Japan.15 However, our base-case estimate of €7,499 per QALY gained is at the lower end of previously published European analyses, likely reflecting the use of real-world baseline characteristics from all identified CKD patients at the hospital level in Denmark, as well as the inclusion of a comprehensive set of cardiovascular events, thereby capturing additional health gains and cost offsets.

The results support the potential value of dapagliflozin in improving health outcomes for CKD patients, while also reducing the economic burden on the health care system. Furthermore, the subgroup analyses highlight the importance of UACR level rather than the history of HF to identify patients for which dapagliflozin is cost-effective, indicating UACR is more important for assessment of cost-effectiveness of SGLT2i in people with CKD. In contrast to eGFR, which is a commonly use marker to identify CKD in Denmark, only about half of incident CKD patients had at least 1 UACR measurement.42 Thus, more consistent and frequent UACR monitoring should allow better earlier identification of patients in which SGLT2i are cost-effective.13 The result of the present study demonstrates greater cost-effectiveness in patients with high UACR being cost-effective after 4 to 5 years. In contrast, the economic advantage in patients with low albuminuria may take longer to materialize requiring a longer predicted survival to achieve cost-effectiveness. These results suggest that clinical practice should prioritize SGLT2i in CKD patients with elevated albuminuria, particularly those with T2D or HF.13 Although generally well tolerated, potential adverse events should be considered in patient selection.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these results. Firstly, the study population from the DECLARE-TIMI 58 study was selected based on the presence of multiple risk factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, which may limit the generalizability of the results to other CKD populations. Second, the median follow-up period in the DAPA-CKD and DECLARE-TIMI 58 trials was 2.4 and 4.2 years respectively, adding uncertainty to any extrapolation beyond this. However, the early demonstration of cost-effectiveness, particularly within 4 to 5 years for subgroups with high UACR, suggest early economic benefit within the study follow-up time. Third, the cost-effectiveness of SGLT2i in the subgroup characterized by low UACR without T2D is inherently subject to uncertainty because of the reliance on simulated patient data. In line with this, the results in the present study were stratified by subgroups to gain a more detailed understanding of the impact of this subgroup. Fourth, the baseline characteristics in the study are based on data from the DAPA-CKD and DECLARE-TIMI 58 trials which do not mirror the Danish CKD population. The CKD patients in the model population were on average approximately 10 years younger than of Danish national register based CKD-cohorts with a greater proportion of males, and fewer patients in CKD 3a.6,13,14,43 Furthermore, the model population likely had more patients with type 2 diabetes, fewer patients of White race and more patients with glomerulonephritis when compared Danish CKD patients, as well as a greater than expected proportion of patients with a UACR >30 mg/g in the low UACR subgroup without T2D. Yet, the burden of cardiovascular comorbid conditions, including ischemic heart disease, stroke, and heart failure, was comparable with other Danish CKD cohorts supporting the validity of cardiovascular risk estimates despite age differences. In addition, the sensitivity analyses demonstrated consistent cost-effectiveness results, although it cannot be excluded that differences not accounted for in the sensitivity analyses may affect cost-effectiveness estimates. Fifth, the cost data applied in the analysis is subject to uncertainty because of limited available data from a Danish perspective. Particularly costs related to CKD stages G1 and G2 are subject to uncertainty. Implementation in clinical practice may also entail additional costs not captured in this analysis, including those related to increased UACR testing, patient identification, and treatment initiation. These may affect short-term costs but are unlikely to change the overall cost-effectiveness conclusions. Sensitivity analyses of treatment costs showed no significant impact on the results. Sixth, the assumed WTP threshold does not reflect an official Danish standard for primary care, affecting the interpretation of cost-effectiveness. However, a different, potentially higher Danish-specific threshold has been estimated for the Danish Medicines Council’s prioritization of hospital medicine, and using a higher threshold value would lead to an even more favorable evaluation of SGLT2i.29

In conclusion, the results suggest that the current Danish guidelines for treatment with SGLT2i is cost-effective with respect to dapagliflozin at a WTP of approximately €24,000/QALY. The findings also demonstrate that SGLT2i is most cost-effective in CKD patients with high UACR particularly in combination with T2D or history of HF.

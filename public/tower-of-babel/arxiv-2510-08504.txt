Constraining the new contributions to electron g − 2 g-2 in a radiative neutrino mass model
Bayu Dirgantara, J. Julio

Abstract
We examine electron and muon anomalous magnetic dipole moments within a radiative neutrino mass model featuring TeV-scale scalar leptoquarks S ( 3 , 1 , − 1 / 3 ) S(3,1,-1/3) and R ⁡ ( 3 , 2 , 1 / 6 ) R(3,2,1/6) . We utilize textures with decoupling electron and muon sectors, so that both electron and muon anomalous magnetic dipole moments could receive internal chiral enhancements from different heavy up-type quarks while in the same time evading the stringent μ → e ​ γ \mu\to e\gamma constraint. A successful fit to neutrino oscillation data requires the simultaneous presence of one- and two-loop neutrino mass contributions. This severely constrains the parameter space of the model, which results in a negligible new physics correction to the muon g − 2 g-2 . The electron g − 2 g-2 discrepancy implied by the rubidium experiment, on the other hand, can be resolved within 2 ​ σ 2\sigma uncertainty provided that neutrino mass ordering is inverted. Lepton-flavor-violating tau decay rates, such as τ → e ​ γ \tau\to e\gamma and τ → 3 ​ e \tau\to 3e , are predicted to be within the sensitivities of next-generation experiments.

Introduction

The massive nature of neutrinos remains the only direct experimental evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). However, the dynamical origin of neutrino masses is still unknown and has been extensively studied even before the discovery of neutrino oscillations. While the canonical seesaw mechanism

*Gell-Mann:1979vob

;
Cheng and Li 1980
;
Zee 1986
;
*Babu:1988ki

The objective of this paper is twofold. First, we demonstrate how one- and two-loop terms of neutrino masses are equally important to fit neutrino oscillation data. Second, based on the fit, we determine the corrections to lepton
g-2
. To achieve this goal, we employ particular textures where electron and muon sectors are decoupled, so that the respective anomalous magnetic moments are induced by chiral enhancement from different up-type quarks. This will also avoid the potentially dangerous chirally-enhanced
\mu\to e\gamma

II
Brief review of the model

The model we consider consists of two leptoquarks
S(3,1,-1/3)
and
R(3,2,1/6)\equiv(R^{2/3},R^{-1/3})^{T}
. Together with the SM particles, they form new Yukawa interactions as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\rm new}=~

\displaystyle\lambda_{ij}^{L}Q^{T}_{i}C\epsilon L_{j}S^{\dagger}+\lambda_{ij}^{R}u^{T}_{Ri}Ce_{Rj}S^{\dagger}+\lambda_{ij}\bar{d}_{Ri}R^{T}\epsilon L_{i}+\text{H.c.}

(1)

Here,
i,j=1-3
are generation indices,
C
is the charge conjugation operator, and
\epsilon
is the
SU(2)
antisymmetric tensor. All terms in Eq. (
1
) are capable of inducing corrections to the lepton
g-2
. Interactions mediated by the
S
leptoquark are of particular interest because they can induce a large correction via internal chirality flip (
S
couples to both left-handed and right-handed up-type quarks), especially when we have the top quark inside the loop.

It should be noted that, besides being gauge invariant, all terms in Eq. (
1
) also respect baryon number (
B
) and lepton number (
L
) symmetries. Thus, diquark terms,
QQS
and
u_{R}d_{R}S
, are not allowed since both break the
B
symmetry. Although it may sound
ad hoc
, such symmetry is needed to avoid the proton decay. As for the lepton number, it can only be broken by a trilinear coupling appearing in the scalar potential. That is,

\displaystyle V=

\displaystyle~\mu^{2}_{R}R^{\dagger}R+\mu^{2}_{S}S^{\dagger}S+\lambda^{\prime}(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}(R^{\dagger}R)^{2}+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(S^{\dagger}S)^{2}+\tilde{\lambda}_{HR}(H^{\dagger}R)(R^{\dagger}H)+\lambda_{HR}(H^{\dagger}H)(R^{\dagger}R)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{HS}(H^{\dagger}H)(S^{\dagger}S)+\lambda_{RS}(R^{\dagger}R)(S^{\dagger}S)+(\mu R^{\dagger}HS+\text{H.c.})

(2)

Note that the parameter
\mu
is taken to be real since its complex phase can always be rotated away.
With all Yukawa coupling matrices of Eq. (
1
) and the dimensionful coupling
\mu
of Eq. (
2
) present, the lepton number is no longer the symmetry of the model. An alternative way of seeing this is by integrating out the heavy leptoquark states. This will result in two
\Delta L=2
operators
Babu and Leung 2001
;
*deGouvea:2007qla
;
*Bonnet:2009ej
;
*Angel:2012ug
, i.e., (i)
(\bar{d}_{R}L\epsilon H)(L\epsilon Q)
and (ii)
(\bar{d}_{R}L\epsilon H)(u_{R}e_{R})
, indicating that, at leading order, neutrino masses will be generated in two different ways. The operator (i), for instance, will generate neutrino masses at the one-loop level after one connects the
d_{R}
and
d_{L}
legs. Similarly, the operator (ii), with the help of the SM charged current, will induce neutrino masses at the two-loop level. Note that the two-loop contribution is not the higher-order correction to the one-loop one since they come from different sets of couplings.

A nonzero
\mu
of Eq. (
2
) will induce mixing between
S^{1/3}
and
R^{1/3}
leptoquarks

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset\begin{pmatrix}S^{-1/3},&R^{-1/3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}m_{S}^{2}&\mu v_{EW}/\sqrt{2}\\
\mu v_{EW}/\sqrt{2}&m_{R}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}S^{1/3}\\
R^{1/3}\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where
v_{EW}=246
GeV is the electroweak vacuum expectation value, while
m_{S}^{2}
and
m_{R}^{2}
include leptoquark bare masses as well as any possible contributions arising from quartic interactions

\displaystyle m_{S}^{2}=~

\displaystyle\mu_{S}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{HS}v_{EW}^{2},

\displaystyle m_{R}^{2}=~

\displaystyle\mu_{R}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\lambda_{HR}+\tilde{\lambda}_{HR})v_{EW}^{2}.

(4)

The mass matrix in Eq. (
3
) can be diagonalized by rotating
S^{1/3}
and
R^{2/3}
to the mass eigenstates

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}^{1/3}\\
X_{2}^{1/3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{\theta}&s_{\theta}\\
-s_{\theta}&c_{\theta}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}S^{1/3}\\
R^{1/3}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where
c_{\theta},s_{\theta}
denote
\cos\theta,\sin\theta
, with
\tan 2\theta=\sqrt{2}\mu v_{EW}/(m_{S}^{2}-m_{R}^{2})
. The process yields mass eigenvalues for
X_{1,2}^{1/3}
, that is,

\displaystyle M_{1,2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[m_{S}^{2}+m_{R}^{2}\pm\sqrt{(m_{S}^{2}-m_{R}^{2})^{2}+2\mu^{2}v_{EW}^{2}}\right].

(6)

In general, the mass splitting of LQs will affect the oblique parameters,
S
,
T
, and
U
. We will be mostly concerned with the
T
parameter, as it is the largest among the three. The correction to this parameter is given by

\displaystyle\Delta T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi m_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}}\bigg[s_{\theta}^{2}F(M_{1}^{2},M_{3}^{2})+c_{\theta}^{2}F(M_{2}^{2},M_{3}^{2})-\frac{1}{4}s_{2\theta}F(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2})\bigg],

(7)

where
M_{3}^{2}\equiv m_{R}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\lambda}_{HR}v_{EW}^{2}
,
m_{W}
, and
s_{W}\equiv\sin\theta_{W}
denote the mass square of
R^{2/3}
LQ, the mass of
W
boson, and weak mixing angle, respectively. The function
F(x,y)
is found as

\displaystyle F(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(x+y)-\frac{xy}{x-y}\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right).

(8)

Technically, the three LQ masses cannot be degenerate, or else neutrino mass will be zero (see next section). However, the splitting cannot be arbitrarily large. From electroweak precision data, one gets
\Delta T<0.2
at 95% CL
Navas et al. 2024
. To illustrate, the upper limit can be obtained for
M_{1}=1500
GeV with
\Delta M\equiv M_{2}-M_{1}=50
GeV and
M_{3}=1585
GeV.

All Yukawa couplings in Eq. (
1
) are defined in the basis where the charged lepton mass matrix is diagonal. By the same token, due to the absence of a right-handed charged current in the model, we can, without loss of generality, adopt the same basis for the right-handed quarks. That is, we take a basis where quark mass matrices, appearing in the Lagrangian as
\mathcal{L}\supset\bar{q}_{L}M_{q}q_{R}
(
q=u,d
), are such that
M_{q}
are diagonalized by
V_{q}M_{q}
, with
V_{q}
being unitary matrices rotating the left-handed quark fields:
q_{L}\to V_{q}q_{L}
. The product of the two unitary matrices,
V\equiv V_{u}V_{d}^{\dagger}

Rotating all fields into their mass eigenstates, Eq. (
1

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\rm new}=~

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{ij}^{u}u^{T}_{Li}C\ell_{Lj}-\lambda^{d}_{ij}d^{T}_{Li}C\nu_{Lj}+\lambda_{ij}^{R}u^{T}_{Ri}C\ell_{Rj}\right)(c_{\theta}X_{1}^{1/3}-s_{\theta}X_{2}^{1/3})+\lambda_{ij}\bar{d}_{Ri}\ell_{Lj}R^{2/3}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{ij}\bar{d}_{Ri}\nu_{Lj}(s_{\theta}X_{1}^{-1/3}+c_{\theta}X_{2}^{-1/3})+\text{H.c.},

(9)

where
\lambda^{L}
has been rotated to flavor basis by
\lambda^{q}=V_{q}^{\ast}\lambda^{L}
. In the present work, we do not offer a mechanism of determining
V_{q}
. However, we can always make an ansatz, i.e., by fixing the flavor structure of one (rotated) Yukawa coupling matrix (
\lambda^{u}
or
\lambda^{d}
), and determine the other via
\lambda^{u}=V^{\ast}\lambda^{d}
.

III
Neutrino mass generation

There are two sets of diagrams that generate neutrino masses, i.e., the one-loop diagrams and two-loop diagrams, see Fig.
1
. The one-loop diagram involves the exchange of
X_{a}^{1/3}
leptoquarks and down-type quarks
d_{j}
, and it is proportional to the product of
\lambda^{L}\lambda
couplings, while the two-loop one involves
X_{a}^{1/3}
and
W
exchanges and is proportional to
\lambda^{R}\lambda
. The one-loop contributions receive chiral suppression from down-type quark masses, whereas the two-loop contributions receive more chiral suppression factors from all charged fermion masses. Coupled with the additional loop suppression, the two-loop diagrams may only be relevant for relatively short range of leptoquark masses, i.e., not more than 10 TeV. Given that the two sets of diagrams are governed by different sets of couplings, the two-loop diagram cannot be regarded as the higher order corrections to the one-loop one and ought not to be simply omitted. In fact, there are some flavor textures in which the one- and two-loop diagrams induce comparable strengths.

In evaluating those diagrams, we work in the general ’t Hooft
R_{\xi}
gauge. Therefore, each propagator of
W
and the Goldstone boson will contain the gauge parameter
\xi
. However, after summing the two contributions, all
\xi
-dependent terms will add up to zero. The
\xi
-independent terms are the same as those obtained in the unitary gauge. It should also be noted that due to the Majorana property of neutrino mass terms, there are actually other diagrams (not shown in the figure) where one replaces the internal particles with their charged conjugates. Those diagrams are just the transpose of diagrams shown in the Fig.
1
. Together, they give a flavor-symmetric neutrino mass matrix.

The neutrino mass matrix is, therefore, given by

\displaystyle(M_{\nu})_{ji}=\frac{3s_{2\theta}}{16\pi^{2}}m_{b}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\left[({\lambda^{d}}^{T})_{jk}(D_{d})_{k}\lambda_{ki}+(\lambda^{T})_{jk}(D_{d})_{k}\lambda^{d}_{ki}\right]I_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{2}m_{t}m_{\tau}}{16\pi^{2}M_{1}^{2}}\left[(D_{\ell})_{j}({\lambda^{R}}^{T})_{jl}(D_{u})_{l}V_{lk}(D_{d})_{k}\lambda_{ki}I^{(2)}_{jkl}+(\lambda^{T})_{jk}(D_{d})_{k}(V^{T})_{kl}(D_{u})_{l}\lambda^{R}_{li}(D_{\ell})_{i}I^{(2)}_{ikl}\right]\bigg\}.

(10)

The factor of 3 is the color factor,
g
is the weak coupling, while
D_{d}
,
D_{u}
, and
D_{\ell}
are the down-type quark, up-type quark, and charged lepton mass matrices normalized to the third generation masses. The functions,
I_{k}^{(1)}
and
I_{jkl}^{(2)}
, are one- and two-loop integral functions, found as

\displaystyle I_{k}^{(1)}(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2})=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}m_{d_{k}}^{2}\left[\frac{\ln(M_1^2/m_{d_k}^2)}{M_{1}^{2}-m_{d_{k}}^{2}}-\frac{\ln(M_2^2/m_{d_k}^2)}{M_{2}^{2}-m_{d_{k}}^{2}}\right],

(11)

\displaystyle I_{jkl}^{(2)}(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2},m_{W}^{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1,2}(-1)^{a+1}\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{a}^{2}-m_{d_{k}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;t\left(1+\frac{t}{4m_{W}^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{t+m_{\ell_{j}}^{2}}\frac{1}{t+m_{W}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\ln\left[\frac{x(1-x)t+xm_{u_{l}}^{2}+(1-x)M_{a}^{2}}{x(1-x)t+xm_{u_{l}}^{2}+(1-x)m_{d_{k}}^{2}}\right].

(12)

Thanks to
m_{d_{k}}\ll M_{1,2}

11

In case (a), due to

\displaystyle I^{(2)}\equiv I_{jkl}^{(2)}(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2},m_{W}^{2})\simeq

\displaystyle~\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\right)\bigg[1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}+f(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2},m_{W}^{2})\bigg],

(13)

with function

\displaystyle f(a,b,x)\equiv~

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b-a}\bigg\{b\ln(a/x)-a\ln(b/x)+\frac{1}{2}\left[b\ln^{2}(a/x)-a\ln^{2}(b/x)\right]\bigg\}.

(14)

In case (b), both top-quark and

\displaystyle I^{(2)}_{3}\equiv I_{jk3}^{(2)}(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2},m_{W}^{2})\simeq~\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\bigg\{1+f(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2},m_{W}^{2})

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{m_{t}^{2}-m_{W}^{2}}{m_{W}^{2}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{m_{t}^{2}-m_{W}^{2}}{m_{W}^{2}}\right)+{\rm Li}_{2}\left(\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{m_{W}^{2}-m_{t}^{2}}\right)\right]

III.1
Texture choices

Following the evaluation of loop integrals, we now address the neutrino mass matrix presented in Eq. (
10
). At this point, one can fix the flavor structure of either
\lambda^{d}
or
\lambda^{u}
. Recall that these coupling matrices are related by
\lambda^{d}=V^{T}\lambda^{u}
. We choose to fix
\lambda^{u}
, as this approach is advantageous for mitigating unwanted flavor-violating processes, such as
\mu\to e\gamma
.

We consider the following flavor structures for
\lambda^{u}
and
\lambda^{R}

\displaystyle\text{TX~1}:~\lambda^{u}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
\lambda^{u}_{21}&0&0\\
0&\lambda^{u}_{32}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda^{R}=\begin{pmatrix}\ast&\ast&\ast\\
\lambda^{R}_{21}&0&\ast\\
0&\lambda^{R}_{32}&\lambda^{R}_{33}\end{pmatrix}

(16)

\displaystyle\text{TX~2}:~\lambda^{u}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\lambda^{u}_{22}&0\\
\lambda^{u}_{31}&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda^{R}=\begin{pmatrix}\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&\lambda^{R}_{22}&\ast\\
\lambda^{R}_{31}&0&\lambda^{R}_{33}\end{pmatrix}

(17)

The elements of
\lambda^{R}
denoted by “
\ast
” indicate that their presence is not necessary for the present discussion, and technically they can be set to zero. As mentioned before,
\lambda^{R}_{ij}
couplings, responsible for two-loop neutrino mass generations, have multiple suppression factors from light fermion masses and CKM matrix elements. Therefore, only terms associated with top quark couplings (i.e.,
\lambda^{R}_{3j}
) are likely to induce significant contributions to the neutrino mass matrix at the leading order.

These flavor structures allow chirally-enhanced corrections to electron and muon
g-2
through different up-type quarks, see Eq. (
29
). The key feature is that they avoid stringent bound from the potentially dangerous chirally-enhanced
\mu\to e\gamma
decay, without compromising the desired
\delta a_{e}
and
\delta a_{\mu}
values. This approach contrasts with mechanisms where
\delta a_{e}
and
\delta a_{\mu}
are induced by the same up-type quark, which face very stringent constraint from
\mu\to e\gamma
bound
Crivellin et al. 2018
;
Crivellin et al. 2021
.

In this current setup, the presence of

In addition, they only possess two (complex) parameters. Hence, embedding them to the present neutrino mass model will provide a clear picture on how neutrino oscillation data affect the parameter space.

Having established the structure for
\lambda^{u}
and
\lambda^{R}
, we now turn to the assumption for the coupling matrix
\lambda
. These couplings contribute to the neutrino mass matrix
M_{\nu}
at both one- and two-loop levels. To facilitate a more tractable analysis, we adopt a minimal scenario in which only the third row of the
\lambda
matrix is nonzero (i.e.,
\lambda_{1j},\lambda_{2j}=0
). This yields a rank-2 neutrino mass matrix, implying a zero determinant.

Explicitly, the neutrino mass matrix is given as

\displaystyle M_{\nu}=m_{0}\begin{pmatrix}aw&\tfrac{av}{2}+\tfrac{bw}{2}&\tfrac{a}{2}+\tfrac{w}{2}\\
\tfrac{av}{2}+\tfrac{bw}{2}&bv&\tfrac{b}{2}+\tfrac{v}{2}\\
\tfrac{a}{2}+\tfrac{w}{2}&\tfrac{b}{2}+\tfrac{v}{2}&1\end{pmatrix},

(18)

where
(a,b,v,w)
are dimensionless parameters. In TX 1, they are defined as

\displaystyle a=\frac{\lambda_{31}}{\lambda_{33}},~b=\frac{\lambda_{32}}{\lambda_{33}},~v=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\frac{\lambda^{u}_{32}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}}+\frac{m_{\mu}}{m_{\tau}}\frac{\lambda^{R}_{32}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}},~w=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{V_{cb}}{V_{tb}}\frac{\lambda^{u}_{21}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}},

(19)

while in TX 2 we have

\displaystyle v=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{V_{cb}}{V_{tb}}\frac{\lambda^{u}_{22}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}},~~w=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\lambda^{u}_{31}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}}+\frac{m_{e}}{m_{\tau}}\frac{\lambda^{R}_{31}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}},

(20)

with
a,b
definitions unchanged. The parameter
m_{0}=(3s_{2\theta}/8\pi^{2})m_{b}V_{tb}\lambda_{33}\lambda^{R}_{33}I^{(1)}\varepsilon
denotes the overall scale of neutrino mass, whereas the parameter
\varepsilon
quantifies the relative strength of two and one-loop effects and is given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{g^{2}m_{t}m_{\tau}}{16\pi^{2}M_{1}^{2}}\frac{I^{(2)}_{3}}{I^{(1)}}.

(21)

For typical
m_{0}=0.025
eV, it must be such that
|\lambda_{33}\lambda^{R}_{33}s_{2\theta}I^{(1)}\varepsilon|=2.63\times 10^{-10}
. For
M_{1}=1500
GeV and
\Delta M=50
GeV, one gets
I^{(1)}\simeq-0.033
and the ratio of the loop integrals is
I^{(2)}_{3}/I^{(1)}\simeq 1.5
. This results in
\varepsilon=5.6\times 10^{-7}
, leading to
|\lambda_{33}\lambda^{R}_{33}s_{2\theta}|=0.014
, a value attainable within perturbative limits. This implies that
\lambda^{R}_{33}
and
\lambda_{33}
, as well as
s_{2\theta}
, cannot be arbitrarily small to guarantee a correct amount of neutrino masses. Another implication is that
\varepsilon
should not rapidly drop as LQ masses increase, making LQ masses cannot be greater than approximately 10 TeV.

Other
\lambda^{R}_{ij}
couplings (excluding pairs of
(\lambda^{R}_{31},\lambda^{R}_{22})
in TX 1 and
(\lambda^{R}_{21},\lambda^{R}_{32})
in TX 2, which are zero) are not shown because their effects on neutrino mass matrix are negligible. For instance,
\lambda^{R}_{21}
, which is one of our couplings of interest in TX 1, induces a correction to parameter
w
:

\displaystyle\delta w=\frac{m_{c}}{m_{t}}\frac{m_{e}}{m_{\tau}}\frac{V_{cb}}{V_{tb}}\frac{I^{(2)}}{I^{(2)}_{3}}\frac{\lambda^{R}_{21}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}}.

(22)

Using the same LQ mass configuration, such a correction is approximately
\delta w=2.4\times 10^{-6}(\lambda^{R}_{21}/\lambda^{R}_{33})

\lambda^{R}_{33}\gtrsim 10^{-2}

inferred from neutrino oscillation data, see Fig.

, leaving the coupling
\lambda^{R}_{21}
effectively unconstrained. The rest are not relevant to our discussion, so they are simply set to zero. The situation for
\lambda^{R}_{22}
in TX 2 can be explained in an analogous way.

Given its rank-2 nature,
M_{\nu}
must possess one zero eigenvalue, implying one massless neutrino. However, based on the structure alone, we cannot distinguish the neutrino mass ordering. Thus, in principle,
M_{\nu}
can admit both normal mass ordering (NO) and inverted mass ordering (IO). To ensure consistency with neutrino oscillation data, both scenarios require that none of the diagonal elements of
M_{\nu}
vanish. The element
(M_{\nu})_{33}=m_{0}
, proportional to the two-loop integral
I^{(2)}_{jk3}
, represents a purely two-loop contribution and thus cannot be zero. Similarly, a situation where one-loop contributions to
M_{\nu}
were absent would lead to
(M_{\nu})_{11}=0
. Such a vanishing
(M_{\nu})_{11}
is inconsistent with the current measurement of
\sin^{2}\theta_{13}
if the lightest neutrino is massless. This highlights a critical phenomenological requirement: significant contributions from both one-loop and two-loop diagrams to the elements of
M_{\nu}
are simultaneously necessary.

Another implication of being a rank-2 matrix is that
M_{\nu}
of Eq. (
18
) has only four independent matrix elements, which are the same as the number of parameters of
M_{\nu}
. Thus, those elements can be used to express
(a,b,v,w)
in terms of neutrino oscillation parameters, i.e.,

\displaystyle(a,w)=~

\displaystyle m_{13}\pm\sqrt{m_{13}^{2}-m_{11}},\quad(b,v)=m_{23}\mp\frac{m_{12}-m_{13}m_{23}}{\sqrt{m_{13}^{2}-m_{11}}},

(23)

where
m_{ij}\equiv(M_{\nu})_{ij}/(M_{\nu})_{33}
, with
M_{\nu}=U^{\ast}M_{\nu}^{\text{diag}}U^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\small U=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

(24)

TX 1

We now turn to discussing the implication on texture TX 1. We start with the correction to muon

\delta a_{\mu}

\lambda^{u}_{32}

\lambda^{R}_{32}

\lambda^{R}_{32}

\lambda^{R}_{33}

2\sigma

\tau\to\mu\gamma

\tau\to 3\mu

\lambda^{u}_{32}

\delta a_{\mu}

11\times 10^{-11}

\tau\to 3\mu

\lambda^{R}_{33}=1.92

|\lambda^{R}_{32}|\lesssim 0.58

\displaystyle\text{NO}:~

\displaystyle 1.4\times 10^{-6}\lesssim|\lambda^{u}_{21}/\lambda^{R}_{33}|\lesssim 10^{-5}

\displaystyle\text{IO}:~

\displaystyle 3.2\times 10^{-6}\lesssim|\lambda^{u}_{21}/\lambda^{R}_{33}|\lesssim 2.9\times 10^{-4},

(26)

where the upper bound of the coupling ratio for IO is about one order of magnitude larger than that of NO. Nevertheless, unlike
\delta a_{\mu}
, the contributions to
\delta a_{e}

Similar to
\lambda^{R}_{32}
and
\lambda^{R}_{33}

III.3
TX 2

The TX 2 scenario is analogous to TX 1. Using
v
and
w
values inferred from Fig.
3
, it is straightforward to determine
\lambda^{u}
couplings. Specifically, we find
|\lambda^{u}_{22}|=|v\varepsilon(V_{tb}/V_{cb})\lambda^{R}_{33}|\lesssim 1.6\times 10^{-4}
and
|\lambda^{u}_{31}|\simeq|w\varepsilon\lambda^{R}_{33}|\lesssim 2.1\times 10^{-5}
, assuming IO and the upper value of

set by the

decay. On the other hand, the coupling
\lambda^{R}_{31}
is practically unconstrained by neutrino data, thanks to the presence of

. Therefore, in principle, it can take the maximum value allowed by flavor constraints, which are

,
\tau\to e\gamma
,
\tau\to 3e
, and
\tau\to e\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

\lambda^{R}_{33}
and
\lambda^{u}_{31}
values. With
\lambda^{R}_{33}
set to its maximum value and
|\lambda^{u}_{31}|\lesssim 2.1\times 10^{-5}
, it is
\tau\to 3e
decay that gives the strongest constraint.

The strong bound on
\lambda^{u}_{22}
prevents
\delta a_{\mu}
from receiving significant enhancement from the charm-quark mass. Instead, it is predominantly induced by terms proportional to
|\lambda^{R}_{22}|^{2}
. The coupling
\lambda^{R}_{22}

\lambda^{R}_{21}
in TX 1, is constrained by the high-

dilepton search via

, resulting in
|\lambda^{R}_{22}|\lesssim 1.33
. This leads to a predicted

, which is in a good agreement with the TI result.

4(b)
. Similar to TX 1, only a small portion of region associated with the rubidium value is allowed, whereas that corresponding to the cesium measurement is entirely ruled out. This reaffirms the significant role of neutrino data in constraining the allowed parameter space. Future measurements of neutrino oscillation parameters will impact this texture the same way they affect TX 1.

Interestingly, in the correlation with LFV, the allowed region further suggests that LFV tau decays, such as

III.4
Coupled electron and muon sectors

Before we end this section, we would like to discuss the situation where both
\delta a_{e}
and
\delta a_{\mu}
are induced via chiral enhancement of the same internal up-type quark, e.g., the top quark. The nonzero elements of
\lambda^{u}
, i.e.,
\lambda^{u}_{31},\lambda^{u}_{32}
, are linked to the neutrino mass matrix parameters through

\displaystyle v=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\lambda^{u}_{32}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}}+\frac{m_{\mu}}{m_{\tau}}\frac{\lambda^{R}_{32}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}},~~w=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\lambda^{u}_{31}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}}+\frac{m_{e}}{m_{\tau}}\frac{\lambda^{R}_{31}}{\lambda^{R}_{33}}.

(27)

To address the electron
g-2
, one needs the chiral enhancement, so both
\lambda^{R}_{31}
and
\lambda^{u}_{31}
must be present. On the contrary, muon
g-2
does not need a new physics. However, both
\lambda^{u}_{32}
and
\lambda^{R}_{32}
cannot be simultaneously zero because
v
is required to be of order 1 by oscillation data. In other words,
\mu\to e\gamma
decay will always be there. To see this more transparently, we express the branching ratio of
\mu\to e\gamma
in terms of
\delta a_{e}
and
\delta a_{\mu}
through
Crivellin et al. 2018
;
Doršner et al. 2020

\displaystyle\text{BR}(\mu\to e\gamma)=\frac{\tau_{\mu}\alpha m_{\mu}^{3}}{16}\left(\frac{\delta a_{e}^{2}}{m_{e}^{2}}|x|^{2}+\frac{\delta a_{\mu}^{2}}{m_{\mu}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right),

(28)

where
\tau_{\mu}=2.19\times 10^{-6}~\text{s}
is the lifetime of the muon and
x\equiv\lambda^{u}_{32}/\lambda^{u}_{31}
. Now, let us suppose
\lambda^{R}_{32}=0
, so
\lambda^{u}_{32}=v\varepsilon\lambda^{R}_{33}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})
. Consequently,
|\delta a_{\mu}|\ll 10^{-11}
, and thus it can be dropped from Eq. (
28
). Due to
m_{e}/m_{\tau}
factor,
\lambda^{R}_{31}
practically has negligible impact on
w
, so it is a good approximation to take
x\simeq v/w
, whose value is between 0.087 and 4.7. With such values, one cannot satisfy the recent bound of
\text{BR}(\mu\to e\gamma)<1.5\times 10^{-13}

Afanaciev et al. 2025
, unless
\delta a_{e}\lesssim 10^{-15}
, two orders of magnitude smaller than the recent values implied by both cesium and rubidium experiments. This shows that, even with the muon
g-2
anomaly gone, the tension between neutrino data and
\mu\to e\gamma
bound restricts the electron
g-2
from getting a significant correction of new physics.

IV.1
Lepton anomalous magnetic moment

The scalar leptoquarks in this model can induce a correction to the anomalous magnetic dipole moment of a lepton, which proceeds via penguin diagrams, in which the photon can be emitted from internal quark and leptoquark lines. Using Eq. (
9
), it is found that

\displaystyle\delta a_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3m_{\ell}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{\zeta_{a}}{M_{a}^{2}}\bigg[\left(|\lambda^{u}_{k\ell}|^{2}+\absolutevalue{\lambda^{R}_{k\ell}}^{2}\right)\kappa(x_{ka})+\frac{m_{u_{k}}}{m_{\ell}}\real\left(\lambda^{u\ast}_{k\ell}\lambda^{R}_{k\ell}\right)\kappa^{\prime}(x_{ka})\bigg],

(29)

where
\zeta_{1}(\zeta_{2})=c^{2}_{\theta}(s^{2}_{\theta})
,
x_{ka}=m_{u_{k}}^{2}/M_{a}^{2}
. The loop functions
\kappa(x),\kappa^{\prime}(x)
are given by

\displaystyle\kappa(x)=

\displaystyle~\frac{-1-4x+5x^{2}-2x(2+x)\ln x}{12(1-x)^{4}},

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}(x)=

\displaystyle~\frac{7-8x+x^{2}+(4+2x)\ln x}{6(1-x)^{3}}.

(30)

Note that in Eq. (
29
) only contributions from
X_{a}^{1/3}
and up-type quark exchange are shown. Other contributions involving
R^{2/3}
and
d_{j}
quarks exchange (driven by
\lambda_{ij}
couplings) are negligible. This can be explained as follows. In the limit of
m_{b}=0
, the loop function of the diagram with photon attached to the down-type quark line is two times larger than the one with photon attached to the leptoquark
R^{2/3}
line, whereas the
b
-quark electric charge is two times smaller and opposite in sign to that of leptoquark. Adding the two contributions will result in a vanishing amplitude at the leading order, similar to the GIM cancellation. Nonzero terms, i.e., the analog of
\kappa(x)
function for this LQ exchange, will be proportional to
m_{b}^{2}/M_{LQ}^{2}
, which are very suppressed.

The last term of Eq. (
29

X_{a}^{1/3}
LQs can couple to charged leptons with different chirality. Thus, it will induce a significant correction if the internal quarks are heavier than external leptons; in the present model this can be achieved by having the top quark or the charm quark inside the loop.

(32)

IV.2.3
\mu-e
conversion in nuclei

Another bound that may affect the parameter space of the model is the one coming from the conversion of muon to electron in nuclei. This process bears similarity with the
\mu\to e\gamma
process but with off-shell photon converted into
q\bar{q}
pair. In addition, there are also
Z
-mediated and box diagrams, as well as tree-level diagrams. We will be particularly interested in the so-called coherent process, where the final state of the nucleus is the same as the initial one. At the the quark level, the effective Lagrangian is written as
Kitano et al. 2002

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{eff}_{\mu-e~\text{conv}}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{2v_{EW}^{2}}\bigg[em_{\mu}\bar{e}\sigma^{\mu\nu}A_{2R}P_{R}\mu F_{\mu\nu}-g_{LS}^{q}(\bar{e}P_{R}\mu)(\bar{q}q)-g_{LV}^{q}(\bar{e}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\mu)(\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}q)\bigg]+(L\leftrightarrow R)+\text{H.c.}

(41)

Note that we do not include pseudoscalar, axial-vector, and tensor quark operators as they do not contribute to the coherent process.

Considering the coupling textures presented, only
\lambda
couplings (via
R^{2/3}
exchange) can induce the
\mu-e
transition. However, as we have seen before, the corresponding dipole coefficients suffer from the GIM-like cancellation, rendering them negligible. Similarly, the scalar operators cannot be induced because this particular couplings can only connect muon and electron with the same chirality.

Consequently, only the left-handed vector operator contributes to the process. This operator needs to be matched to the nucleon level, giving
\bra{N}\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}q\ket{N}=n^{(q,N)}\bar{N}\gamma^{\mu}N
, with
n^{(q,N)}
denoting the number of valence quark
q
in nucleon
N
. As a result, box and tree-level exchanges can be omitted as they contain only
b
-quark flavor. The effective coupling is given by

\displaystyle g_{LV}^{q}=

\displaystyle~e^{2}Q_{q}A_{1L}-e^{2}\left[T_{3}(q)-2Q_{q}s_{W}^{2}\right]Z_{L},

(42)

where
g_{RV}^{q}=g_{RS}^{q}=g_{LS}^{q}=0
, and the coefficients
A_{1L}
and
Z_{L}
are defined in Eq. (
37
).

From here, the conversion rate is found as
Kitano et al. 2002

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mu-e~\text{conv})=

\displaystyle~\frac{m_{\mu}^{5}}{v_{EW}^{4}}\bigg|(2g_{LV}^{u}+g_{LV}^{d})V^{p}+(2g_{LV}^{d}+g_{LV}^{u})V^{n}\bigg|^{2},

(43)

with
V^{p,n}
being overlap integrals for the proton and neutron. Their definitions and values for various atomic nuclei are given in Ref.
Kitano et al. 2002
. It is worth noting that the
Z
contribution is suppressed by
m_{b}^{2}/m_{Z}^{2}
, so it would induce merely a negligible correction. The dominant contribution is driven by the photon exchange, which is proportional to the electric charge of the valence quarks. Because of that, the term associated with the neutron will be effectively zero.

Today, the strongest constraint of such a conversion comes from the search in the gold nucleus by SINDRUM II Collaboration
Bertl et al. 2006
, i.e.,
R_{\mu e}\equiv\Gamma(\mu-e~\text{conv})/\Gamma(\text{captured})<7\times 10^{-13}
, with
\Gamma(\text{captured})=13.06\times 10^{-6}~\text{s}^{-1}
and
V^{p}=0.0974
. Future experiments, such as COMET
Abramishvili et al. 2020
and Mu2e
Bartoszek et al. 2014
, are projected to reach the sensitivities of the order
\mathcal{O}(10^{-18})
using aluminum target.

IV.5
High-

Some couplings within this model can also contribute to dilepton production

at the LHC, i.e., via the tree-level LQ exchanges. This finds its relevance when the corresponding quarks are of first or second generation. Since the LQ masses we consider are well above the electroweak scale, we can adopt the effective theory approach. We are particularly interested with the following flavor-conserving dim-6 operators involving right-handed up-type quark

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{eff}=-\frac{2}{v_{EW}^{2}}C^{RR}_{ij}(\bar{q}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}P_{R}q_{i})(\bar{\ell}_{j}\gamma_{\mu}P_{R}\ell_{j}),

\displaystyle C^{RR}_{ij}=~\frac{v_{EW}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{c_{\theta}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}}+\frac{s_{\theta}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\right)|\lambda^{R}_{ij}|^{2}.

(52)

Other operators with left-handed quarks or leptons, i.e.,
C^{LL}_{ij}
or
C^{RL}_{ij}
, are not considered. This is because these operators involve
\lambda^{u}
couplings, which are constrained to be small by neutrino oscillation data.

Two particular coefficients that are relevant for our discussion are
C^{RR}_{21}
in TX 1 and
C^{RR}_{22}
in TX 2. The magnitudes of these coefficients have been tabulated in Ref.
Greljo and Marzocca 2017

|C^{RR}_{22}|<0.0162~(0.00632)
for
36.1~\text{fb}^{-1}~(3000~\text{fb}^{-1})

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we have presented an analysis on radiative neutrino mass model with two scalar leptoquarks
S(3,1,-1/3)
and
R(3,2,1/6)
in the close connection with current lepton
g-2
observations. We employed textures with decoupling electron and muon sectors, allowing each anomalous magnetic dipole moment to be induced by different up-type quarks, ensuring no dangerous
\mu\to e\gamma
decay.

In minimal scenario that we discussed, a consistency with neutrino oscillation data requires a simultaneous appearance of both one- and two-loop neutrino mass contributions. The model predicts the lightest neutrino to be massless and admits both normal and inverted orderings. The model also establishes a direct connection between neutrino oscillation data and the lepton
g-2
values. Consequently, stringent constraints from neutrino data prevent the muon
g-2
from getting a large new correction, while allowing a resolution to the discrepancy implied by the Rb experiment within
2\sigma
as long as the neutrino mass ordering is inverted. Furthermore, the model predicts rates for LFV tau decay processes, such as
\tau\to e\gamma
and
\tau\to 3e
, near the current limits. Future experimental results, from neutrino oscillations, high-luminosity colliders, or LFV searches will be able to confirm or falsify this scenario.

Risk classification assessment and early warning of large deformation of soft rock in tunnels based on CNN-LSTM model
Authors: Xianmeng Zhang, Wang Minghao, Dan Feng, Wang Jingchun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTunnels in the Western Plateau region of China often face numerous problems of high ground stress, as well as highly fractured and weak rock masses in deep sections of the tunnel, which results in an increased risk of large soft rock deformation in tunnels, posing substantial challenges to safe and efficient construction. Hence, risk assessment and establishment of an early safety warning system are essential. By examining factors affecting the large soft rock deformation in tunnels, a set of index system consisting of three primary indicators (i.e., hydrogeological conditions, design factors, and construction factors) and eight secondary indicators (i.e., strength-to-stress ratio, rock mass index, groundwater influence, groundwater influence, etc.) for assessing these risks is constructed, while the risk classification and identification criteria are also established. As an example, a tunnel on the Xicheng Railway is adopted for regression prediction of rock mass deformation and risk warning classification based on CNN-LSTM model. The results indicate that the model is effective in predicting rock mass deformation with minimal fluctuations in settlement data, a MAPE of 0.29525, and a RMSE of 6.1311. The model demonstrates a high accuracy rate of 91.67% for early risk warning prediction of soft rock deformation in the tunnel, moreover, when applied to 10 cross-sections of the Xicheng Railway Tunnel for deformation risk early warning, the model demonstrated a high prediction accuracy of 90%. which serves as a reference for the assessment and prediction of large deformation risks in similarly complex and challenging tunnels in the western region of China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-81816-x.Keywords: Tunneling, Large soft rock deformation, Convolutional neural network (CNN), Long short-term memory neural network (LSTM), Risk assessmentSubject terms: Civil engineering, Information technology

Introduction

China’s western mountainous regions are characterized by complex terrain and frequent geological hazards, which significantly affects the construction safety of tunnels. Due to high geopathic stress, weak rock mass, groundwater, fault fracture zones, and other complex geological conditions, the construction of tunnels is prone to large rock mass deformation hazards, which is defined as “rock mass deformation that is significantly greater than the normal level and may lead to adverse outcomes”1. Large deformations in soft rock tunnels can result in severe accidents such as vault collapse, face instability, and support system failure, which potentially causes casualties and property damage during construction and poses an extremely high safety risk2. Therefore, conducting risk assessment on high geo-stress soft rock tunnels is essential to make risk predictions and early warnings for potential large soft rock deformations.

Researchers worldwide have conducted comprehensive studies on the large deformation of rock mass. Yang et al.3 employed the discrete element simulation method to simulate the large deformation occurring during tunnel excavation and to characterize the damage modes in both unsupported and primary-support-equipped tunnels. They devised an improved backstop support control scheme based on the simulation results. Similarly, Yang et al.4 utilized the UDEC software to simulate the substantial deformation associated with ventilation tunnel construction through discrete element simulation and to propose a novel “bolt-cable-net-sprayed concrete + shell” composite support system for the ventilation tunnel stabilization. Liu et al.5,6conducted long-term, real-time monitoring of structural stress in tunnels with severe deformation, developed four stress-time regression models and compared the cross diaphragm (CRD) method with the three-bench annular excavation method with reserved core soil in extremely soft and fractured rock, which advanced methods for controlling large deformations. Lei and Chao7 explored the principle of circumferential yielding support through the lens of energy conversion during the tunnel excavation-support process. They analyzed the primary factors influencing structural deformation in soft rock tunnels with significant deformations and their bearing structures using a structural mechanics analytical methodology. Additionally, they employed finite element analysis software to compare the mechanical characteristics of a traditional bearing system with those of the circumferential yielding bearing system. Zhang et al.8 analyzed factors influencing tunnel rock mass deformation by performing numerical simulations with discrete element software, and then identified key factors affecting thin-layer slate tunnel deformation such as joint inclination, depth, water absorption, and rock mass softening, etc. Deng et al.9 investigated the geology and deformation properties of the large deformation zones in Da-ping Mountain Tunnel, and determined the creep parameters of rock mass in large deformation zones by conducting creep tests. Also, they assessed the geo-stress conditions within these zones through displacement inversion analysis, and confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed optimization strategies for tunnel construction. Han et al.10 examined the squeezing deformation mechanism of soft rock tunnel from the perspective of stress relaxation and plastic rheology. They proposed a deformation control principle centered on “multi-stage and hierarchical collaborative control”, in which the degrees of rock mass deformation were categorized into five levels: slight, moderate, substantial, severe, and extremely severe. He et al.11identified 15 risk factors influencing large deformations in soft rock tunnels through case studies, and devised a risk assessment model for these deformations by utilizing the game theory-cloud model. Kang and Wang12 outlined the principles of large deformation in rock masses and the mechanical prediction methods related to tunneling projects, and introduced a Support Vector Machine (SVM) based prediction model for tunnel rock mass deformations. Yang et al.13 developed a prediction model for large deformations in laminated soft rock tunnels using the Particle Swarm Optimization- Support Vector Machine (PSO-SVM) algorithm based on the analysis of deformations in such strata structures. Thereafter, the model was implemented in real tunnel scenarios to enhance project expertise by selecting key factors such as rock and soil compressive strength, laminae inclination, and initial geo-stress conditions among the six indicators as parameters. Ma et al.14 implemented an innovative MIC-LSTM machine learning algorithm designed to control deformation in soft rock tunnels subject to extremely high geo-stress, which included its successful deployment in real-world scenarios involving high and extremely high geo-stress during construction. Xu et al.15 combined the LSTM algorithm with numerical simulation and, after training with parameters including uniaxial compressive strength, ground stress, and joint spacing among others, they introduced a novel deformation prediction approach specifically designed for large cross-section tunnel engineering. Liu et al.16 evaluated the strengths and weaknesses of three algorithms—Gaussian process regression, Support Vector Machines, and Long Short-Term Memory Networks—in predicting perimeter rock deformation in hydraulic tunnels, and determined that the SVM model outperformed the others, thus laying a theoretical foundation for predicting perimeter rock deformation. He et al.17 introduced a nonlinear deformation prediction method for surrounding rock utilizing machine learning, based on studies of the surrounding rock deformation from two actual tunnels. Following comparative analysis of the GP, SVM, and WNN models, they concluded that the SVM model exhibited superior performance in predicting settlements and convergence deformations. Qiu et al.18 used the Delphi method and cloud model theory to study the deformation of rocks around tunnels, established a grade standard for the deformation of surrounding rock in high-speed railway tunnels, and analyzed the key factors affecting the deformation grade of surrounding rock. He et al.19 analyzed a case of large deformation in soft rock tunnels and proposed 15 factors affecting the risk of large deformation in soft rock tunnels. Bai et al.20 employed Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) to evaluate the efficiency of 12 factors influencing the deformation of soft rock tunnels. The results indicate that moisture content of the soft rock is the most critical factor, followed by tunnel depth, uniaxial compressive strength, excavation method, strength-stress ratio, and groundwater seepage conditions.

Current research on large deformations of rock masses primarily focuses on the damage mechanisms of soft rock and the efficacy of support measures. Risk assessments are typically performed using traditional safety evaluation methods, while machine learning algorithms are most often utilized for deformation prediction of soft rock tunnels21. Research on risk prediction for these deformations is limited. Evidences demonstrate the significant time-dependence of soft rock deformation in western mountain tunnels. Therefore, this study employs an integrated approach of the convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and the long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) to develop the machine learning model. The comparison of the effects of specific risk prediction methods is described in Table 1. An assessment system for large deformations in soft rock tunnels is developed, and its effectiveness in predicting rock mass deformations is verified by actual tunnel monitoring data, thereby enabling risk classification and early warning for significant deformations in soft rock tunnels.

Convolutional neural network

A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is a specialized type of artificial neural network that distinguishes itself from other models through its signature feature of the convolution operation22–25. Whilst the accuracy of CNN models is undermined by their limited generalization capability when dealing with time series data, they excel at feature extraction. Assessing the risk of significant deformations requires an appropriate set of indicators, each of which represents a distinct feature. Given that data of indicators influencing significant deformations in soft rock tunnels are one-dimensional and lack spatial context, this paper employs a one-dimensional (1D) convolutional approach for the CNN model. Long short-term memory network

Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) are a specialized form of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). Since a standard RNN can be considered as a multi-layered feedforward neural network with feedback connections, the extensive historical information carried by long sequences can lead to the vanishing and exploding gradients during training26. LSTMs are one of the most extensively utilized deep learning models, which are renowned for their robustness in handling long-term dependencies and their superior performance in processing time series data27. The rock mass deformation in tunnels exhibits typical time series characteristics, allowing the LSTM model to be well suited for predicting such deformations. An LSTM network mainly consists of a forget gate, an input gate, and an output gate, where the cell state of the previous step is denoted as (C_{t-1}), the hidden state is denoted as (), and the current input is denoted as (). The cell state updates its values after passing through the forget gate, and the tanh function is used at the input gate to filter information relevant to the current context. Then () is combined with the modulation of the output gate via a tanh function to produce the next hidden state, where () represents the sigmoid activation function28. Convolutional neural networks-long short-term memory

The convolutional neural networks-long short-term memory (CNN-LSTM) model is effective in both regression prediction and classification tasks. The CNN layer is responsible for extracting and selecting features from the risk assessment data of large soft rock deformation, while LSTM layers are tasked with the prediction of time series data, which then estimate the final extent of rock mass deformation. Based on the risk criteria for rock mass deformation, the model assesses the risk level of large soft rock deformation in tunnels. The model consists of two components. One is the CNN convolutional network, which consists of convolutional layers and pooling layers that perform feature extraction on the training data. The other one is the LSTM model, which processes the features extracted by the CNN to forecast the rock mass deformation. The number of convolutional layers and the number of convolutional kernels within the CNN affect both computational time and complexity. Therefore, it adopts a dual-layer configuration with kernel dimensions of 2 × 1, 32 convolutional kernels, and a maximum pooling strategy for pooling layer. The LSTM utilizes a single layer with 128 neurons with a loss rate of 0.25, and employs ‘adam’ as the optimizer. Also, it uses a regularization coefficient with a value of 1e-3, where the loss function adopts Mean Squared Error (MSE) and the activation function uses Softmax. Risk assessment system for large deformation in soft rock tunnels

Risk classification of large deformation in soft rock tunnels

Combined with the Code for Tunnelling in Squeezing Rocks (Q/CR 9512 − 2019)29, Technical Specification for Monitoring Measurement of Railway Tunnel (Q/CR 9218 − 2024)30, and Guidelines related to the design and construction of high ground stress soft rock tunnels, the classification of large deformations for actual projects is investigated. The large deformation classification for soft rock tunnels mainly includes the strength-to-stress ratio, the relative deformation amount, and the deformation potential31,32.The risk classification criteria for large deformation in soft rock tunnels based on the deformation potential and the relative deformation amount are developed. Based on deformation potential and relative deformation amount, the large deformation is classified into grades I-IV. The relative deformation and the deformation potential are more frequently employed in field construction. For risks in levels III-IV, it is recommended that construction teams perform additional ground stress assessment, calculate strength-to-stress ratio, and ascertain large deformation level. Indicators for assessing large deformations in soft rock tunnels

High ground stress fields and presence of soft rock masses are the primary factors contributing to large soft rock deformations. However, ground stress measurements in projects are often rarely performed as they are time-consuming and not mandatory. The relevant rock mass parameters can be found in the geological investigation report33. Therefore, review of collected 120 typical tunnel cases of large soft rock deformation worldwide identifies that hydrogeological conditions, design factors, and construction factors are the main contributors to these large soft rock deformation hazards.

Therefore, a risk assessment index system for soft rock large deformation in tunnels is developed, including three main indicators of hydrogeological conditions, design factors, and construction factors as well as eight sub-indicators of strength-to-stress ratio, rock quality, groundwater conditions, tunnel depth, equivalent diameter, support strength, monitoring and measurement practices, and site management, as depicted in Fig. 4.

Risk assessment system for large deformation in soft rock tunnels.

The strength-to-stress ratio (GN) is defined as the ratio of the rock mass strength to its self-gravity stress, which serves as a key parameter in classifying the degree of soft rock large deformation in tunnels. It illustrates the relationship between the rock mass strength and the surrounding complex environment. Therefore, it’s a principal indicator for assessing large soft rock deformations. Tunnel depth (H) is a critical factor, especially in the western mountainous regions where tunnels are often deeply buried, and increasing tunnel depth corresponds to high stress in the rock mass. The rock strength index (BQ) that indicates the rock strength is a major factor influencing large deformations in soft rock tunnels. Monitoring, management, and additional factors during tunnel construction are closely related to the risk of large deformation in soft rock tunnels, thus they are integral to the indicators selected for a large deformation assessment system. The support strength (K) characterizes how effectively the implementation of tunnel support measures reduce deformation, making it a viable indicator for assessing large deformation in soft rock tunnels. Prediction of rock mass deformation in tunnels

Engineering overview

The elevation of the Xicheng railway tunnel is about 3220 m. The tunnel portal section consists of a 2293-m long main hole and a pilot tunnel. The lengths of grade III, IV, V, and VI rock masses are 220 m, 400 m, 1450 m, and 203 m respectively, with grade V and VI rock masses accounting for 72.1% of the section. The tunnel portal section is located within a highly active tectonic zone with strong block difference activity. The presence of weak, fragmented, water-rich rock mass, high geo-stress, significant deformation, elevated temperatures, and hot underground water in the site increases engineering complexity as well as poses considerable engineering and construction challenges with extremely high risks.

Time series prediction of rock mass deformation

Twenty-two sets of 40-day monitoring data on tunnel vault settlement are collected using the automatic monitoring information system of rock mass in Xicheng Railway tunnel. Among them, Twenty data sets are used as the training sets, while the remaining two sets that correspond to typical tunnel sections are used as the test sets. The CNN, LSTM, and CNN-LSTM models for predicting rock mass deformation are developed based on the Matlab software platform by utilizing the settlement values during the initial 40 days. The CNN-LSTM model yields an overall accuracy exceeding 90% for classification assessment and prediction of risk levels of large soft rock deformation in tunnels while the prediction error is within the acceptable range, which provides a reliable basis for risk assessment and prediction of large soft rock deformation in practical projects.

Therefore, for tunnels in high-stress soft rock with risk level I and II, it is recommended to strengthen the initial support as the primary deformation control measure. Strategies such as resistance-based support, enhanced reinforcement, increased reserved deformation capacity, anchor reinforcement, and optimization of cross-sectional form are advised. For sections with risk level III and IV, it is recommended to transition from a single support form to a joint support system. This approach forms a diversified deformation control strategy, incorporating measures such as moderate release, long, medium, and short anchor reinforcement, multi-support systems, and circular sections. The use of machine learning models to predict the risk level of large soft rock deformation in tunnels enables rapid and precise outcomes. These models can be incorporated into risk monitoring and warning platforms to enhance the efficient deployment of human resources and provide a strong safeguard for the risk assessment and early warning systems in tunnel engineering.

Discussion

A machine learning model was utilized to predict the trend of perimeter rock deformation and to establish a large deformation dataset for risk classification warnings, with prediction results falling within the acceptable range. The CNN-LSTM model is employed for predicting the risk of large deformations, capable of reducing the risk by compiling historical data on large deformations into a database and pre-establishing relevant index parameters for predictive analyses. Prior to the onset of large deformations, appropriate preventive measures can be taken, such as implementing anti-support structures and overrun grouting, among others. Although this paper employs a machine learning model to facilitate hierarchical early warnings for large deformation risks, it does not address the weight of indicators within the large deformation assessment system, a consideration that can be pursued in future research. Concurrently, due to inherent limitations in the dataset, this study did not examine large deformations in shallow-buried tunnels, nor did it address cases of significant tunnel deformations in arid regions where groundwater is scarce. Groundwater levels and tunnel depth were treated as linear variables, without consideration for their potential nonlinear relationships. This aspect warrants further investigation in future research.

Conclusions

In this study, a risk assessment system specifically for large soft rock deformation in western mountain tunnels is established with reference to the Xicheng railway tunnel, and a CNN-LSTM-based risk classification assessment model for large deformations is developed. This framework aims to provide a basis for safety risk assessment of large soft rock deformation in complex and hazardous mountain tunnels prevalent in the western region. The following main conclusions are drawn:

A risk assessment system is developed for large soft rock deformations in western mountain tunnels, comprising three core indicators: hydrogeological conditions, design parameters, and management aspects. This is further complemented by eight subsidiary indicators: strength-to-stress ratio, rock quality designation, groundwater influence, tunnel depth, equivalent diameter, support robustness, monitoring intensity, and on-site management consistency, with classification criteria developed for each indicator.

This is further complemented by eight subsidiary indicators: strength-to-stress ratio, rock quality designation, groundwater influence, tunnel depth, equivalent diameter, support robustness, monitoring intensity, and on-site management consistency, with classification criteria developed for each indicator.

Dual-tracer 18F-FDG and 18F-FLT PET/CT for differentiating metastatic lymph nodes from sarcoid involvement in a patient with vulvar squamous cell carcinoma and coexisting pulmonary sarcoidosis
Authors: Junichi Tsuchiya, Xiaotong Wu, Anna Tabei, Momo Wakui, Hirofumi Yamada, Kota Yokoyama, Noriko Oshima, Kimio Wakana, Naoyuki Miyasaka, Ukihide Tateishi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Differentiating sarcoidosis lesions from metastatic lymph nodes is a well-known clinical challenge, particularly in patients with malignancy in whom sarcoid lesions may mimic nodal metastases on 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT). We report a 75-year-old woman with concurrent sarcoidosis and vulvar squamous cell carcinoma in whom 18F-FDG PET/CT demonstrated avid uptake in enlarged lymph nodes throughout the mediastinum, pulmonary hilum, upper abdomen, and right groin. 18F-fluorothymidine (18F-FLT) PET/CT performed 7 days later showed FLT uptake comparable to FDG uptake in the right groin lymph node (FDG SUVmax 5.5, FLT SUVmax 5.8), which was subsequently confirmed as a metastatic node at histopathology, whereas the mediastinal lymph nodes showed markedly weaker FLT uptake relative to FDG (FDG SUVmax 11.5, FLT SUVmax 4.5) and were considered compatible with sarcoidosis on the basis of the patient's known sarcoid involvement and stability on follow-up imaging over 385 days. This case illustrates that the combination of 18F-FDG and 18F-FLT PET/CT may aid in distinguishing sarcoid lesions from metastatic lymph nodes in patients with concurrent sarcoidosis and malignancy.

Introduction

Sarcoidosis is a multisystem granulomatous disease that frequently involves the mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes and shows avid 18F-FDG uptake on PET/CT [1]. When sarcoidosis coexists with malignancy, distinguishing sarcoid lesions from nodal metastases is clinically critical, as both can appear as enlarged, FDG-avid lymph nodes [2]. 18F-FLT is a proliferation tracer whose uptake correlates with the Ki-67 index. Although FLT uptake may also occur in inflammatory lesions, uptake in granulomatous disease is often lower than that observed with FDG and may differ from malignant proliferation patterns [3,4]. We report a case in which the combination of 18F-FDG and 18F-FLT PET/CT supported the differentiation of metastatic from sarcoid lymph nodes in a patient with vulvar cancer. Dual-tracer 18F-FDG/18F-FLT PET findings in vulvar squamous cell carcinoma complicated by sarcoidosis have rarely been described.

Case report

A 75-year-old woman with a history of biopsy-proven pulmonary sarcoidosis was referred to our institution for evaluation and treatment of a vulvar mass. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) demonstrated a primary lesion in the vulva together with enlarged lymph nodes in the mediastinum, bilateral pulmonary hila, upper abdomen, and right groin. 18F-FDG PET/CT was performed for staging. The patient fasted for at least 6 hours before injection, and the blood glucose level was confirmed to be below 150 mg/dL. Approximately 60 minutes after intravenous administration of 18F-FDG (3.7 MBq/kg), whole-body PET/CT images were acquired on a PET/CT scanner (Celesteion, Canon Medical Systems, Otawara, Japan) from the skull base to the mid-thigh. CT was performed for attenuation correction and anatomical localization, and PET images were reconstructed using an ordered-subset expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm incorporating time-of-flight (TOF) and point-spread-function (PSF) modeling. 18F-FLT PET/CT was performed 7 days after the 18F-FDG PET/CT study using the same scanner, and no treatment of any kind was administered between the 2 examinations; whole-body images were acquired approximately 60 minutes after intravenous administration of 18F-FLT (3.7 MBq/kg), with no fasting requirement. The maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) was measured as the semiquantitative parameter for both tracers. The 18F-FDG PET/CT showed FDG-avid uptake in the primary vulvar lesion and in all of the enlarged lymph nodes, including those in the right groin (SUVmax 5.5), mediastinum (SUVmax 11.5), and upper abdomen (Fig. 1A, B, and E). Vulvar squamous cell carcinoma most commonly metastasizes first to the inguinal and pelvic lymph nodes, whereas extensive mediastinal and bilateral hilar nodal involvement at presentation is unusual. 18F-FLT PET/CT (Fig. 1C, D, and F) showed relatively weak FLT uptake in the primary vulvar lesion, consistent with previously reported FLT uptake patterns in squamous cell carcinoma. The right groin lymph node demonstrated FLT uptake comparable to its FDG uptake (FDG SUVmax 5.5, FLT SUVmax 5.8) (Fig. 1E and F), whereas the mediastinal lymph nodes showed markedly lower FLT uptake relative to FDG (FDG SUVmax 11.5, FLT SUVmax 4.5) (Fig. 1B and C). This FDG–FLT discrepancy in the thoracic and upper abdominal nodes, in contrast to the concordant uptake in the right groin node, suggested that the former were sarcoid lesions and the latter was a metastatic node.

The patient subsequently underwent radical vulvectomy with bilateral inguinal lymph node dissection. Histopathology confirmed vulvar squamous cell carcinoma with metastasis to a right groin lymph node, while no malignancy was identified in the contralateral groin. Follow-up CT over a period of 385 days (approximately 13 months) after the initial 18F-FDG PET/CT showed no progression of the mediastinal, hilar, or upper abdominal lymph nodes, which were considered compatible with sarcoidosis.

Discussion

18F-FDG PET plays a vital role in the diagnosis and evaluation of sarcoidosis lesions [1]. In patients with concurrent malignancy, sarcoid lesions in the mediastinum, hilum, or abdomen can closely mimic nodal metastases on contrast-enhanced CT and 18F-FDG PET/CT, which may lead to overstaging and inappropriate treatment [2]. 18F-FLT is a tracer that reflects cellular proliferation, and its uptake has been shown to correlate with pathological proliferation indices such as Ki-67 [3,4]. Squamous cell carcinomas at various anatomical sites, including the head and neck region, have been reported to show measurable FLT uptake [5]. However, the pattern of 18F-FLT uptake in vulvar squamous cell carcinoma has not been well characterized in the literature. In our case, FLT uptake in the primary vulvar lesion and in the pathologically confirmed metastatic right groin lymph node was compatible with the moderate uptake previously reported in squamous cell carcinomas at other anatomical sites, supporting the use of 18F-FLT PET/CT for nodal assessment in vulvar cancer. Norikane et al. [6] reported that 18F-FLT uptake is weaker than 18F-FDG uptake in sarcoidosis lesions [6]. In our case, this difference was clearly reflected in the SUVmax values: the right groin metastatic lymph node showed concordant FDG and FLT uptake (FDG SUVmax 5.5, FLT SUVmax 5.8; FDG/FLT ratio 0.95), whereas the mediastinal sarcoid lymph nodes showed a marked FDG–FLT discrepancy (FDG SUVmax 11.5, FLT SUVmax 4.5; FDG/FLT ratio 2.56). This pattern—concordant FDG/FLT uptake in metastatic nodes and a clear FDG–FLT mismatch in sarcoid nodes—suggests that dual-tracer 18F-FDG and 18F-FLT PET/CT may provide a useful clue for differentiating sarcoid involvement from nodal metastases in patients with coexisting sarcoidosis and malignancy. This uptake mismatch (high FDG but low FLT) directly reflects the uncoupling of accelerated glucose metabolism and cellular proliferation characteristic of granulomatous inflammation.

The clinical relevance of this differential diagnosis is likely to grow. Beyond the long-recognized epidemiological association between sarcoidosis and malignancy, the widespread use of immune checkpoint inhibitors and molecular targeted agents has led to an increasing number of drug-induced sarcoidosis-like reactions (DISR) in oncology patients, in which FDG-avid granulomas may be misinterpreted as disease progression or new nodal metastases and are difficult to distinguish from pseudoprogression on 18F-FDG PET/CT alone [7,8]. This clinical context underscores the need for complementary tracers that reflect cellular proliferation, such as 18F-FLT, in addition to glucose metabolism. Recent case reports continue to highlight that, even with high SUVmax values, sarcoidosis can be misdiagnosed as malignancy when based on 18F-FDG PET/CT alone [9]. The use of complementary PET tracers has therefore been proposed to address this limitation: 18F-fluoro-α-methyltyrosine (18F-FMT), an amino acid tracer, has been shown to accumulate poorly in sarcoid lesions while retaining uptake in malignant tissue, and its combination with 18F-FDG can help distinguish sarcoidosis from malignancy [10]. Our case suggests that 18F-FLT, which similarly shows preserved uptake in proliferating tumor cells but low uptake in granulomas, can serve a similar role in this dual-tracer approach. Other emerging tracers may also become relevant to this differential diagnosis: 18F-labeled fibroblast activation protein inhibitors (FAPI) are under early investigation in vulvar squamous cell carcinoma [11]. Notably, although FAPI is useful for delineating malignant lesions, FAPI uptake has also been reported in active sarcoidosis lesions accompanied by fibroblast activation and granulomatous inflammation [12]. In this regard, 18F-FLT, which reflects pure cellular proliferation through the Ki-67 pathway, may offer a complementary advantage in capturing the mismatch (high FDG/low FLT) between glucose metabolism and proliferation that characterizes granulomatous inflammation.

This report has several limitations. It describes a single case, and histopathological confirmation was obtained only for the right groin lymph node; the mediastinal, hilar, and upper abdominal nodes were judged to be sarcoid on the basis of the patient's known biopsy-proven pulmonary sarcoidosis, the typical nodal distribution, and morphological stability on follow-up imaging rather than direct biopsy. 18F-FLT is not widely available in routine clinical practice, which may limit the generalizability of this approach. 18F-FLT also shows high physiological uptake in active hematopoietic bone marrow, which can increase background activity adjacent to the spine and may reduce conspicuity when evaluating small mediastinal or hilar lymph nodes, although in the present case the FDG–FLT discrepancy was nevertheless clearly demonstrable. Prospective studies comparing these tracers in patients with concurrent sarcoidosis and malignancy will be required to define their respective roles.

Conclusion

In a patient with concurrent pulmonary sarcoidosis and vulvar squamous cell carcinoma, dual-tracer 18F-FDG and 18F-FLT PET/CT supported the differentiation of sarcoid lymph nodes from a metastatic groin lymph node. Concordant FDG and FLT uptake favored nodal metastasis, whereas a clear FDG–FLT mismatch favored sarcoid involvement. This dual-tracer approach may be useful in selected patients with coexisting sarcoidosis and malignancy when conventional imaging is equivocal.

Implementing outcomes-based education through case-based learning and flipped classroom in the pharmacognosy teaching for pharmacy undergraduates
Authors: Hongyan Zhu, Xiuning Huang, Peng Li, Pengfei Zheng, Qianliang Ming
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere is a gap between the basic knowledge and practice skill in the traditional teaching of pharmacognosy for pharmacy undergraduates. Under the background of the new era of the transformation and upgrading of the pharmaceutical industry, the teaching reform of pharmacognosy is imperative. The outcome-based education (OBE), which emphasizes student-centered and results-oriented teaching strategy, aligns with the cultivation demand for comprehensive pharmaceutical talents. Case-based learning (CBL) is guided by clinical cases and can help students to integrate theoretical and clinical knowledge. The flipped classroom further strengthens the student-center, which helps cultivate their active learning and problem-solving skills. Thus, this study integrated CBL with flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning, developing an OBE-directed blended teaching method.MethodsTo evaluate the effectiveness of this method, we conducted a study with 92 participants. The pharmacy undergraduates were divided into a traditional group (n = 49) and a reform group (n = 43) based on the different time of enrollment. And the traditional group employed traditional lecture-based learning, while the reform group received an OBE-directed blended teaching method. Then they were required to complete a questionnaire about their learning outcomes and overall experience after the course.ResultsThe statistical results showed that the course final grade of reform group was significantly superior to the traditional group. And the results of survey questionnaire indicated that the majority of pharmacy students believed that the OBE-directed blended teaching method could provide guidance for future clinical practice, and could help in their study of pharmacognosy.ConclusionThe OBE-directed blended teaching method, which combined Case-Based Learning with flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning, could improve the quality of pharmacognosy teaching from multiple dimensions. And it may be a promising strategy for pharmaceutical education reform.Keywords: Pharmacognosy, Outcomes-based education, Case-based learning, Flipped classroom, OBE-directed blended teaching method

Background

The practical dilemma in pharmacognosy education

The subject of pharmacognosy is crude drugs, including those derived from natural, unprocessed, or simply processed plants, animals, and minerals [1]. These natural medicinal materials are not only easy to obtain, but also have a variety of pharmacological activities [2, 3]. In ancient China they were simple and effective traditional medicines, and in modern times they are an important source for the development of new medicines [4, 5]. Therefore, pharmacognosy has become one of the basic professional disciplines that undergraduates majoring in pharmacy must master.

However, the teaching of pharmacognosy faces many challenges. First of all, the knowledge points of the course are numerous and scattered, and there is a lack of close connection between the contents of each chapter, which makes it difficult for students to form a systematic knowledge system. Secondly, in Chinese, pharmacognosy is closely related to the theory of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), involving a large number of obscure TCM terms and theories, which is often difficult for students to effectively understand and accept when they lack practical experience. Thirdly, the traditional lecture-based classroom is teacher-centered, students passively receive knowledge, lacking of active thinking and participation opportunities [6]. It leads to an absence of students’ core competence in the identification of crude drugs, rational application of medicinal materials and the formulation of quality standards.

The Healthy China strategy, a development strategy proposed by the Chinese government, aims to promoting the development of public health through a series of measures such as deepening the reform of the medical and health system and strengthening primary healthcare services. It not only accelerates the internationalization of TCM, but also intensifies the demand for pharmaceutical talents with practical ability, innovative thinking and professional responsibility in the healthcare industry. Therefore, how to reform the teaching mode of pharmacognosy course and improve learning effect and practical ability of students has become an urgent task for pharmacy higher education.

Implementing outcomes-based education with case-based learning may improve the teaching quality of pharmacognosy

The outcome-based education (OBE) concept, which emphasizes student-centered and results-oriented teaching strategy [7, 8], provides a new idea for the teaching reform of pharmacognosy. Since OBE takes specific learning outcomes as the anchor point, focuses on the knowledge, ability and literacy goals that students should have [9]. Teachers must adopt a reverse thinking to redesign the curriculum system, reorganize and optimize the teaching content, improve the evaluation mechanism according to the training objectives [10]. Thus, it can effectively promote the transformation of teaching from “Teacher-centered” to “Student-centered” and form an integrated teaching design of “goal-process-evaluation” [11].

In the teaching of pharmacognosy course, it is the most critical training objective for students to effectively identify and rationally apply various medicinal materials. Concretely, based on the concept of OBE, we disassembled the training objectives of pharmacognosy course into three ability modules of “authenticity identification -quality evaluation-rational application of medicinal materials”, and reverse designed the teaching content and teaching method around the three objectives. Due to pharmacognosy is a subject with strong practical attribute, a lot of practical training is needed to help students improve their ability to identify and apply medicinal materials. But practical training in the traditional classroom is very limited and far from sufficient, so we employed Case-based learning (CBL). CBL is a case-based teaching method, and can effectively link theoretical teaching with practical teaching [12, 13]. First, the teacher needs to prepare a suitable teaching case according to the training objectives. Then the students need to think about the problems or difficulties in the case independently or in groups. Finally, the case study by the teacher to answer questions and summarize.

Compared with the traditional lecture-based classroom, this teaching method has three advantages. First, it encourages students to think independently. Traditional teaching just instructs students on how to perform tasks, yet its content often proved to be impractical in real-world applications and even very boring, which damaged the motivation and learning outcomes of students. In contrast, CBL encourages students to think critically and innovate independently, transforming what might be boring into dynamic, interactive experiences [14]. Moreover, each student should express their opinions on their own and others. Through this experience exchange, they can not only learn from each other’s strengths and improve their interpersonal communication skills, but also have an incentive effect through peer pressure [15, 16]. Secondly, it can guide students to shift their focus from knowledge acquisition to ability training. Traditional exam-oriented education focuses too much on the rote memorization of theoretical knowledge, often neglecting the training of practical application ability [17]. CBL takes real application scenarios as the premise, which can effectively make up for this deficiency. Thirdly, it promotes the interactive communication between teachers and students [18]. The traditional teaching mode is that the teachers speak and the students listen, which is a one-way output and passive reception process. While in case-based learning, students first analyze and comprehend the case study, then research relevant knowledge materials to resolve complex issues. This process naturally deepens their understanding of knowledge through active engagement [19]. After achieving theoretical concepts, students must conduct systematic analysis to propose practical solutions—a step that embodies a significant enhancement of their capabilities [20]. At the same time, their solutions also require teachers to give guidance, which in turn prompts teachers to think deeply and supplement new teaching content according to the different understanding of different students [21]. Therefore, the interactive teaching form also puts forward higher requirements for teachers.

In short, by creating a real clinical medication scenario for students in the pharmacognosy course, CBL can effectively help students grasp the pharmacological characteristics, drug efficacy, and therapeutic applications of various medicinal materials in the scene. At the same time, it enables students to draw inferences and achieve comprehensive understanding. This approach can not only cultivate the critical thinking and clinical thinking of students, but also improve their practical skills and problem-solving abilities. Therefore, implementing the concept of outcome-based education through the case-based teaching method is a feasible and effective strategy.

The OBE concept may be more effective by Integrating CBL with flipped classroom

Although the CBL is an effective carrier to practice the OBE concept, it requires considerable time and effort from both teachers and students. Teachers must carefully screen or design the appropriate teaching cases first, then extract inherent challenges or issues within the cases, and guide students to analyze and reflect, and ultimately evaluate and summarize the resolutions proposed by students [22]. Students, in turn, must complete the entire process: case analysis, data collection, plan formulation, and presentation of findings. These processes are basically consistent with the flipped classroom. Flipped classroom is a kind of teaching mode in which students learn new knowledge by watching videos or other learning materials recorded by teachers outside the classroom [23]. While classroom time is devoted to discussion, answering questions and practical activities. The core of this model is to change the traditional “teaching before learning” into “learning before teaching”, and make the classroom a place for teacher-student interaction and cooperative learning [24].

As modern digital education reforms deepen, universities are embracing a surge of online digital courses—including micro-lectures, MOOCs (massive open online courses, e.g. https://www.icourse163.org/), and virtual simulation classes. These digital resources not only diversify teaching content and formats, but also enhance instructional quality and efficiency, gradually establishing themselves as the dominant teaching model in the post-pandemic era [25, 26]. The online-offline blended learning is a “virtual and real integration, multi-dimensional interaction” teaching method, which can combine and complement the advantages of traditional classroom teaching and digital network-based education [27]. Thus, the combination of flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning could not only enable teachers to play a leading role in guiding, inspiring, and monitoring the learning process, but also fully promote students’ initiative, enthusiasm, and creativity as active participants in the learning journey [28]. We believed that it may be an effective method to enhance the efficiency of Case-Based Learning.

To achieve the anticipated talent development objectives, we have tried to integrate the CBL with flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning, developing an OBE-directed blended teaching method. And to evaluate the teaching outcomes of this method, We have designed a three-year teaching reform in our pharmacognosy course to demonstrate the advantages of this approach compared to traditional teaching methods.

Materials and methods

Sample

Since the pharmacy profession at our university is conducted in small classes with about 10 ~ 20 students per year, in order to increase the sample size of students observed and verify the reproducibility of the teaching effect, we conducted a three-year traditional teaching and three-year teaching reform study. Form 2020 to 2025, a total of 92 undergraduate students majoring in pharmacy were voluntarily selected as participants for this study. Among them, a total of 49 students from 2020 to 2022 were observed as a traditional group (n = 49), while 43 students from 2023 to 2025 were designated as the reform group (n = 43). The sample comprised 39 males (42.40%) and 53 females (57.60%) ranging in age from 19 to 21 years old.

Teaching methods

The pharmacognosy course was offered in the second semester of the third academic year, based on the training requirements and the undergraduate pharmacy program syllabus. The core content of this course was the chapter of “crude drugs derived from angiosperms”, which occupied 24 class hours, and the entire course was completed by one teacher. The teacher numbers, class hours and teaching materials of both groups in this study were the same. The traditional group employed traditional lecture-based learning, while the reform group received an OBE-directed blended teaching method, which combined Case-Based Learning with flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning.

The traditional group

According to the program syllabus and course teaching plan, teachers systematically presented the teaching content with the traditional lecture-based teaching method. The chapter of “crude drugs derived from angiosperms” totally occupied 24 class hours and was introduced during 8 teaching sessions, each teaching session lasted for 3 class hours. For each session, it was conducted in a way where the teacher explains and the students listen, and key and difficult topics were explained in detail.

The reform group

Consistent with the traditional group, the chapter of “crude drugs derived from angiosperms” was introduced during 8 teaching sessions, each teaching session lasted for 3 class hours. The teaching process was as follows. (1) Teaching preparation stage: teachers designed clinical cases for the chapter of “crude drugs derived from angiosperms” according to the training objective, and ensured that the required basic medical knowledge and clinical practice were fully integrated. Based on the selected case, teachers collected and produced micro-lecture videos. The videos offered the key conceptual knowledge points and the pitfalls or blind spots in clinical applications. Each video was limited to five minutes and corresponded to a key medicinal material of this Chap. (2) Teaching stage: The micro-lecture videos containing clinical cases were sent to the students through the online teaching platform (Yu Classroom or chat tool software), one week before class. At the same time, 3–5 clinical case-related questions were disseminated to allow students to carry out independent inquiry-based learning with guided questions. In the offline classroom, students worked in small group, and the group leader presided over the group members. The students discussed and exchanged ideas on case analysis with the knowledge learned before class. The group leader described the pre-class case questions and the pharmacognosy knowledge points contained in this case; each group had 10–15 min for their presentation. Teachers played a guidance role, providing supplemental information and consolidation of relevant theoretical knowledge after each group’s report. During the report, teachers listened to the feedback of students on the self-learning process, so as to improve the teaching content and improve the teaching effect in a timely manner. Subsequently, the teachers could systematically review, reflect on and improve the contents of this lecture. Noticeably, it is important for teachers to connect the fragmented knowledge points of the micro-lectures, and establish a complete pharmacognosy knowledge framework. (3) Teaching quality inspection: In addition to the final examination scores, classroom performance was evaluated in order to measure the quality of OBE-directed blended teaching method. And the classroom performance occupied 40% of the final score in order to guide students to shift their training focus to practical ability development. Teachers assessed the learning effects based on the comprehension abilities and thinking development level of students during class when answering questions and participating in group discussions.

Questionnaire

The application of the OBE-directed blended teaching method in pharmacognosy teaching was evaluated via a questionnaire survey distributed to the students. The questionnaire inspected the following key points in relation to the teaching effect: help to learn pharmacognosy; guide future clinical practice; increase final scores; promote interest in scientific research; improve active learning and problem-solving ability; enhance communication skills; offer a good learning experience. Each question had two response options: disagree or agree. Agreement was regarded as approval of this teaching reform and disagreement represented low satisfaction. At the end of the course, 92 questionnaires were distributed, and 92 were returned, with an effective rate of 100%.

Statistical processing

GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.1) statistical software was used to process the data. The measurement data were expressed as the mean ± standard error, and t tests were used to compare the means of the two groups. The test level was α = 0.05, and a difference was considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

Results

Distribution of final grades between the two groups

As shown in Fig. 1, 97.7% of the students in the reform group passed the final examination, while only 89.8% of the students in the traditional group passed the final examination. The proportion of good and excellent students (≥ 80 grades) in the reform group was significantly higher than that in the traditional group (30.2% vs. 14.3%). The average final exam total score (include classroom performance) of the reform group was (79.78 ± 2.34), and the average final exam score of the traditional group was (70.65 ± 1.32). The total score in the reform group was significantly higher than that of the traditional group (p < 0.001). These results indicated that the pharmacy undergraduates in the reform group achieved significantly better performance in the final pharmacognosy exam.

Evaluation of the application of the OBE-directed blended teaching method in the teaching of pharmacognosy for pharmacy undergraduates

After the course, a total of 92 pharmacy undergraduate students participated in our questionnaire survey, and the survey recovery rate was 100%. As shown in Fig. 2, a total of 93% (40 individuals) students were satisfied with the positive effect of “help to learn pharmacognosy” in the reform group, while the satisfaction rate in the traditional group was only 75.5%. More importantly, there were 90.7% (39 individuals) students agreed with the “guide future clinical practice” in the reform group, while only 61.2% in the traditional group. However, the satisfaction rates related to “increase final scores” and“promote interest in scientific research”in the reform group were low, with only 83.7% (36 individuals) and 79% (34 individuals) of students, respectively. Overall, most students in the reform group found that the OBE-directed blended teaching method was beneficial for improving active learning and problem-solving abilities, and enhancing communication skills. All of these skills helped in the study of pharmacognosy, and offered a good learning experience for participants. In general, the satisfaction of the OBE-directed blended teaching method was high.

Discussion

The OBE-directed blended teaching method could promote the overall development of pharmacy undergraduates

In this study, a greater proportion of students in the reform group passed the exam, as compared to the traditional group (97.7% vs. 89.8%). Moreover, the average score on the final exam for the reform group was higher than that of the traditional group (79.78 ± 2.34 vs. 70.65 ± 1.32). In Fig. 1, the number of students with scores between 80 and 100 in the reform group was significantly higher than that in the traditional group. Fig. 2 showed that 93% of the pharmacy students believed that the OBE-directed blended teaching method could help them study pharmacognosy. These results indicated that the OBE-directed blended teaching method can improve the learning effects of pharmacognosy among pharmacy students.

Certainly, effective teaching methods are crucial for learning results. Students cannot effectively exert their subjective initiative in the learning process with the traditional lecture-based teaching method. So they are in a state of passive acceptance of knowledge and lack the training of independent thinking and problem-solving skills. This is at odds with the cultivation of modern and innovative pharmaceutical talents. In order to adapt to the development of industry and the progress of the times, teaching reform oriented to the learning results of students is imperative.

In our study, based on the outcome-based education concept, case-based learning, flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning were integrated into the teaching of pharmacognosy for pharmacy undergraduates. The OBE concept requires clear educational objectives as the foundation, with teaching content and methods designed in reverse to cultivate pharmaceutical professionals that meet the societal needs. Since the most important training objective of the pharmacognosy course is the ability of medicinal materials identification and application. The CBL pedagogy is guided by clinical cases and can help students to integrate basic and clinical knowledge. For instance, counterfeit and inferior medicinal materials were often found in the herbal medicine market, and adverse reactions were usually reported in clinical practice [29]. These real-world cases served as excellent teaching materials. Through analyzing such authentic examples, students can intuitively grasp the key aspects of herbal medicine identification and application, thereby cultivating critical thinking, problem-solving skills and professional dedication [30].

Although CBL serves as an effective vehicle for implementing the OBE concept, there are still some problems, including the contradiction between the teaching process and course time allocation, as well as the enthusiasm and initiative defect of students in completing their independent learning prior to class. In order to address these problems, flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning was applied in this study. The online teaching resources could liberalize and fragment learning content. Clinical cases were developed into micro-lecture videos and uploaded to online platforms before class. By using micro-lectures, the time for learning was no longer confined to the classroom; students could choose to watch the micro-lecture videos at any time and any place to achieve targeted or gap-filling learning [31].

Moreover, students were able to adjust their learning progress by the usage of the online teaching resources targeted at their own learning desires. They were also able to use the internet to find information related to the learning content. In the offline classroom, students communicated with teachers face-to-face to answer questions, as well as obtain the key and difficult knowledge of pharmacognosy. This teaching model in our study shifted the dominant position of the classroom to the hands of the students, who become the protagonists of independent exploration. The class time was redistributed, and the teaching content was transferred to outside of the classroom. At the same time, the knowledge framework was better established by the communication and discussion that occurred in the classroom, making students to better grasp the required pharmacognosy knowledge. Thus, the combination of flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning in the present study was able to address the limitations of the CBL teaching method.

In general, the OBE-directed blended teaching method adopted in the current study integrated pharmacognosy knowledge and clinical practice. The case-centered autonomous knowledge internalization mode in this process can enrich the learning experience of pharmacy undergraduates and greatly improve their independent thinking and problem-solving abilities [32]. Students changed from passive dissemination to knowledge reception from clinical cases. Participated students reported that their communication skills were enhanced, and the satisfaction rate about the outcome to improve active learning and problem-solving in the reform group was as high as 88.3% (Fig. 2). Thus, the OBE-directed blended teaching method comprehensively stimulated the subjective initiative of pharmacy undergraduates, deepened their understanding of pharmacognosy knowledge, and promoted a sense of achievement.

Problems and countermeasures in the application of OBE-directed blended teaching method in pharmacognosy teaching

Indeed, the OBE-directed blended teaching method may partly break the problem of “difficult to teach” and “difficult to learn” for pharmacognosy courses, and then improve the teaching quality. 88.3% participated students indicated that they had a good learning experience (Fig. 2). However, there are still several potential problems associated with the method applied in this study, which must be addressed before its future application. Especially, the large workload and high investment required to establish a case database, as well as the technical difficulty and high cost of micro-lecture production in the early stage [33]. Furthermore, the traditional theoretical examinations and teacher satisfaction evaluations were not available for the comprehensive and objective evaluation of this mode. It is necessary to establish an effective scientific evaluation feedback system so that the OBE-directed blended teaching method can play a real driving and promoting role in the teaching of pharmacognosy to pharmacy students.

At the same time, the OBE-directed blended teaching method has a higher requirement on the teaching abilities of teacher. it requires teachers to have some higher professional and clinical knowledge reserves. Additionally, this method encourages students to engage in fragmented active learning in relation to a specific knowledge point. Fragmented learning has some disadvantages, such as fragmentation and arbitrariness [34]. It is important for teachers to disassemble the clinical cases in detail, and students to improve their classroom participation and discussion, so as to build a good knowledge foundation.

In summary, the application of the OBE-directed blended teaching method in the teaching of pharmacognosy to pharmacy undergraduates could improve the teaching quality to a certain extent. It improved the comprehension and clinical thinking abilities of students, and laid a foundation for future clinical pharmacy practice. However, it should be noted that this study only investigated the teaching of pharmacognosy to pharmacy undergraduates, and the sample size and duration of teaching implementation was limited. Accordingly, the application of this method in the teaching practice is need to be explored and promoted in related medical disciplines in the future.

Conclusion

Through the final exam total score (include classroom performance) and questionnaire survey, we found the OBE-directed blended teaching method, which combined Case-Based Learning with flipped classroom and online-offline blended learning, could improve the quality of pharmacognosy teaching from multiple dimensions. It can not only improve test scores of students, but also improve their practical skills and professional quality, which is conducive to the cultivation of comprehensive pharmaceutical talents.

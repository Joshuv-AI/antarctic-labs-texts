Exact inequalities and optimal recovery by inaccurate information
K. Yu. Osipenko

Abstract
. The paper considers a multidimensional problem of optimal recovery of an operator whose action is represented by multiplying the original function by a weight function of a special type, based on inaccurately specified information about the values of operators of a similar type. An exact inequality for the norms of such operators is obtained. The problem under consideration is a generalization of the problem of optimal recovery of a derivative based on other inaccurately specified derivatives in the space ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and the problem of an exact inequality, which is an analogue of the Hardy–Littlewood–Polya inequality.

1.
General setting

Let
X
be a linear space,
Y_{0},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}
be normed linear spaces, and

\Lambda_{j}\colon X\to Y_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,N
, be linear operators. Consider the problem of optimal recovery of
\Lambda_{0}
on the set

W=\{\,x\in X:\|\Lambda_{j}x\|_{Y_{j}}\leq\delta_{j},\ \delta_{j}>0,\ j=m+1,\ldots,N\,\},

where
1\leq m\leq N
(if
m=N
, then
W=X
), by values of operators
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m}
given with errors. More precisely, we will assume that for each
x\in W

we know
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in Y_{1}\times\ldots\times Y_{m}
such that
\|\Lambda_{j}x-y_{j}\|_{Y_{j}}\leq\delta_{j}
,
\delta_{j}>0
,
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Any recovery method by known information
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
should give an element from
Y_{0}
that is taken as an approximate value of
\Lambda_{0}x
. Thus, every recovery method is a mapping

\Phi\colon Y_{1}\times\ldots\times Y_{m}\to Y_{0}
. The error of a method
\Phi
is defined as

e(\Lambda_{0},W,\delta,\Phi)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in W,\ y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in Y_{1}\times\ldots\times Y_{m}\\
\|\Lambda_{j}x-y_{j}\|_{Y_{j}}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,m\end{subarray}}\|\Lambda_{0}x-\Phi(y)\|_{Y_{0}},

where
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m})
.

We are interested in those methods for which the error is minimal, i.e. those methods

\widehat{\Phi}
for which

(1)

e(\Lambda_{0},W,\delta,\widehat{\Phi})=\inf_{\Phi\colon Y_{1}\times\ldots\times Y_{m}\to Y_{0}}e(\Lambda_{0},W,\delta,\Phi).

We will call such methods
optimal recovery methods
. The quantity on the right-hand side of (
1
) will be called
the error of optimal recovery
and denoted by
E(\Lambda_{0},W,\delta)
.

Let

(2)

\begin{gathered}\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+},\quad\varphi(\cdot)=(\varphi_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\cdot)),\\
\varphi^{\alpha}(\cdot)=\varphi_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(\cdot)\ldots\varphi_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}(\cdot),\end{gathered}

where
\varphi_{j}(\cdot)
,
j=1,\ldots,k
are continuous (generally speaking, complex-valued) functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Set

\mathcal{W}_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{\,x(\cdot)\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\cdot)x(\cdot)\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,j=1,\ldots,N\,\right\},

where
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\alpha^{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
,
j=1,\ldots,N
, and
\mathcal{A}=\{\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{N}\}
. We define operators
\Lambda_{j}\colon\mathcal{W}_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as follows

\Lambda_{j}x(\cdot)=\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\cdot)x(\cdot),\quad j=0,1,\ldots,N.

For these operators we consider problem (
1
), in which
X=\mathcal{W}_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
Y_{0}=Y_{1}=\ldots=Y_{N}=L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The corresponding set of functions
W
is denoted by
W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta})
where

\overline{\delta}=(\delta_{m+1},\ldots,\delta_{N})
. The case when
\varphi(\xi)=i\xi
was considered in
[
1
]
.

The consideration of the problem posed is connected with the desire to generalize the recovery problem for functions of many variables from inaccurately given values of derivatives (see
[
2
, p. 249]
). In addition, as a consequence of the solution of the problem under study, a generalization of the exact inequality of the Hardy–Littlewood–Polya type is obtained (see
[
3
]
).

Note that the idea of considering recovery problems for a whole family of operators was used in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

2.
General result

Set

Q=\co\{(\alpha^{1},\ln 1/\delta_{1}),\ldots,(\alpha^{N},\ln 1/\delta_{N})\},

where
\co A
is the convex hall of A. Define the function
S(\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
by the equality

(3)

S(\alpha)=\max\{\,z\in\mathbb{R}:(\alpha,z)\in Q\,\}

(
S(\alpha)=-\infty
, if
(\alpha,z)\notin Q
for all
z
).

Let
\alpha^{0}\in\co\mathcal{A}
and let
z=\langle\alpha,\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a}
, where
\widehat{\eta}=(\widehat{\eta}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\eta}_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, be a support hyperplane to the graph of
S(\cdot)
at
\alpha^{0}
. By Caratheodory’s theorem there exist points in this hyperplane
(\alpha^{j_{s}},\ln 1/\delta_{j_{s}})
,
s=1,\ldots,l
,
l\leq k+1
, such that

(4)

\alpha^{0}=\sum_{s=1}^{l}\theta_{j_{s}}\alpha^{j_{s}},\quad\theta_{j_{s}}>0,\ s=1,\ldots,l,\quad\sum_{s=1}^{l}\theta_{j_{s}}=1.

Set

M=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\cap\{1,\ldots,m\}.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\alpha^{0}\in\co\mathcal{A}
. Assume that for any
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}>0
there exists
\widehat{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|\varphi_{j}(\widehat{\xi}\,)|=a_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
. Then

(5)

E(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta)=e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}.

If
M\neq\emptyset
, then all methods

(6)

\Phi(y(\cdot))(\cdot)=\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\cdot)y_{j}(\cdot),

where functions
a_{j_{s}}(\cdot)
,
s=1,\ldots,l
, satisfy the conditions

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{l}\varphi^{\alpha^{j_{s}}}(\xi)a_{j_{s}}(\xi)=\varphi^{\alpha^{0}}(\xi),

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{l}\dfrac{\delta_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|^{p^{\prime}}}{\theta_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}/p}}\leq e^{-p^{\prime}S(\alpha^{0})},&1<p<\infty,\quad\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,\\
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq s\leq l}\dfrac{\delta_{j_{s}}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|}{\theta_{j_{s}}}\leq e^{-S(\alpha^{0})},&p=1,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{l}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|\delta_{j_{s}}\leq e^{-S(\alpha^{0})},&p=\infty,\end{cases}

for almost all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, are optimal.

If
M=\emptyset
then the method
\Phi(y(\cdot))(\cdot)=0
is optimal.

Proof.

We prove that

(9)

E(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta)\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta})\\
\|\Lambda_{j}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,m\end{subarray}}\|\Lambda_{0}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Indeed, for any method
\Phi\colon(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{m}\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any function
x(\cdot)\in W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta})
such that
\|\Lambda_{j}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, we have

2\|\Lambda_{0}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\Lambda_{0}x(\cdot)-\Phi(0)(\cdot)-(\Lambda_{0}(-x(\cdot))-\Phi(0)(\cdot))\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leq\|\Lambda_{0}x(\cdot)-\Phi(0)(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Lambda_{0}(-x(\cdot))-\Phi(0)(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leq 2e(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta,\Phi).

Due to the arbitrariness of
\Phi
it follows that

\|\Lambda_{0}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq E(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta).

Taking the upper bound over all functions
x(\cdot)
satisfying the given conditions, we obtain inequality (
9
).

The extremal problem in the right-hand side of (
9
) may be written in the form

(10)

\||\varphi(\cdot)|^{\alpha^{0}}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to\max,\quad\||\varphi(\cdot)|^{\alpha^{j}}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,N,

where
|\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha}=|\varphi_{1}(\xi)|^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots|\varphi_{k}(\xi)|^{\alpha_{k}}
for

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}
.

Let
\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
|\varphi_{j}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|=e^{-\widehat{\eta}_{j}}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
. Consider the case when
1\leq p<\infty
. Due to the continuity of the functions
\varphi_{s}(\cdot)
,
s=1,\ldots,k
, for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
there exist
\widetilde{\delta}_{j}
such that

(11)

\left||\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j}}-|\varphi(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|^{p\alpha^{j}}\right|\leq\varepsilon

for all
\xi\in B_{\widetilde{\delta}_{j}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})
where

B_{\delta}(\xi_{0})=\{\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi-\xi_{0}|<\delta\,\}.

Set
\widetilde{\delta}=\min\{\widetilde{\delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\delta}_{N}\}
. Then for all
\xi\in B_{\widetilde{\delta}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})
and all
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
inequalities (
11
) hold.

Put
\widehat{A}=e^{-2\widehat{a}}
and

x_{\varepsilon,p}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{\widehat{A}}{|B_{\widetilde{\delta}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|\gamma_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/p},&\xi\in B_{\widetilde{\delta}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}}),\\
0,&\xi\notin B_{\widetilde{\delta}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}}),\end{cases}

where

\gamma_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon\frac{\widehat{A}}{\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\delta_{j}^{p}}

(
|B_{\delta}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|
denotes the volume of the ball
B_{\delta}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})
).

We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j}}|x_{\varepsilon,p}(\xi)|^{p}\,d\xi\leq\frac{\widehat{A}}{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\varphi(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|^{p\alpha^{j}}+\varepsilon\right)=\frac{e^{-p(\langle\alpha^{j},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a})}+\widehat{A}\varepsilon}{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}.

Since
z=\langle\alpha,\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a}
is the equation of support hyperplane of
Q
, we get

\langle\alpha^{j},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a}\geq\ln 1/\delta_{j}.

Consequently,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j}}|x_{\varepsilon,p}(\xi)|^{p}\,d\xi\leq\frac{\delta_{j}^{p}+\widehat{A}\varepsilon}{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}\leq\delta_{j}^{p}.

Thus,
x_{\varepsilon,p}(\cdot)
is an admissible function for extremal problem (
10
). Therefore, taking into account (
9
) and (
11
), we obtain

E^{p}(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{0}}|x_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|^{p}\,d\xi\geq\frac{\widehat{A}}{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\varphi(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|^{p\alpha^{0}}-\varepsilon\right)\\
=\frac{e^{-p(\langle\alpha^{0},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a})}-\widehat{A}\varepsilon}{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}.

Making
\varepsilon
tends to zero, we have

E(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta)\geq e^{-(\langle\alpha^{0},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a})}=e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}.

Now let
p=\infty
. Similar to the previous reasoning, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\widetilde{\delta}>0
such that for all
\xi\in B_{\widetilde{\delta}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})
and all
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
inequalities

\left||\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha^{j}}-|\varphi(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|^{\alpha^{j}}\right|\leq\varepsilon

hold.

Put

x_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\widehat{A}\widetilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}^{-1},&\xi\in B_{\widetilde{\delta}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}}),\\
0,&\xi\notin B_{\widetilde{\delta}}(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}}),\end{cases}

where

\widetilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon\frac{\widehat{A}}{\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\delta_{j}}

We have

\||\varphi(\cdot)|^{\alpha^{j}}x_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{\widehat{A}}{\widetilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\varphi(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|^{p\alpha^{j}}+\varepsilon\right)=\frac{e^{-(\langle\alpha^{j},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a})}+\widehat{A}\varepsilon}{\widetilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\leq\delta_{j}.

Consequently,
x_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\cdot)
is an admissible function for extremal problem (
10
). Thus,

E(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta)\geq\||\varphi(\cdot)|^{\alpha^{0}}x_{\varepsilon,\infty}(\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq\frac{\widehat{A}}{\widetilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\varphi(\widehat{\xi}_{\widehat{\eta}})|^{\alpha^{0}}-\varepsilon\right)\\
=\frac{e^{-(\langle\alpha^{0},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a})}-\widehat{A}\varepsilon}{\widetilde{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}}.

Making
\varepsilon
tends to zero, we have

(12)

E(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta)\geq e^{-(\langle\alpha^{0},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a})}=e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}.

Now we prove the optimality of the recovery methods (
6
). To estimate the error of methods (
6
) we consider the following extremal problem

(13)

\biggl\|\varphi^{\alpha^{0}}(\cdot)x(\cdot)-\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\cdot)y_{j}(\cdot)\biggr\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to\max,\\
\|\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\cdot)x(\cdot)-y_{j}(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,m,\\
\|\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\cdot)x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=m+1,\ldots,N.

For
M=\emptyset
, the corresponding sums are considered equal to zero.

Put
z_{j}(\xi)=\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)x(\xi)-y_{j}(\xi)
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, and

\omega(\xi)=\varphi^{\alpha^{0}}(\xi)-\sum_{j\in M}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi).

Then (
13
) may be written in the form

\biggl\|\omega(\xi)x(\xi)+\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\xi)z_{j}(\xi)\biggr\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to\max,\\
\|z_{j}(\xi)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,m,\\
\|\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\cdot)x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=m+1,\ldots,N.

It follows from (
7
) that

\omega(\xi)=\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi)x(\xi).

Thus, we have to estimate the value of the following problem

\biggl\|\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi)x(\xi)+\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\xi)z_{j}(\xi)\biggr\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to\max,\\
\|z_{j}(\xi)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,m,\\
\|\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\cdot)x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=m+1,\ldots,N.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Set

\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}=\theta_{j_{s}}e^{p\langle\alpha^{j_{s}}-\alpha^{0},\,\widehat{\eta}\rangle},\quad s=1,\ldots,l,

Consider the case when
1<p<\infty
. Then by Hölder’s inequality we have

\biggl|\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi)x(\xi)+\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\xi)z_{j}(\xi)\biggr|\\
=\biggl|\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\frac{a_{j}(\xi)}{\widehat{\lambda}_{j}^{1/p}}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}^{1/p}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)x(\xi)+\sum_{j\in M}\frac{a_{j}(\xi)}{\widehat{\lambda}_{j}^{1/p}}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}^{1/p}z_{j}(\xi)\biggr|\\
\leq Q_{p}(\xi)\biggl(\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j}}|x(\xi)|^{p}+\widehat{\lambda}_{j}|z_{j}(\xi)|^{p}\biggr)^{1/p},

where

Q_{p}(\xi)=\biggl(\sum_{s=1}^{l}\frac{|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|^{p^{\prime}}}{\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}/p}}\biggr)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

In view of equalities

\ln 1/\delta_{j_{s}}=\langle\alpha^{j_{s}},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a},\quad s=1,\ldots,l,\quad S(\alpha^{0})=\langle\alpha^{0},\widehat{\eta}\rangle+\widehat{a}

we obtain

\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}=\theta_{j_{s}}e^{p\langle\alpha^{j_{s}}-\alpha^{0},\,\widehat{\eta}\rangle}=\theta_{j_{s}}e^{p(\ln 1/\delta_{j_{s}}-S(\alpha^{0}))}=\frac{\theta_{j_{s}}}{\delta_{j_{s}}^{p}}e^{-pS(\alpha^{0})}.

Hence,

(14)

Q_{p}(\xi)=\biggl(e^{p^{\prime}S(\alpha^{0})}\sum_{s=1}^{l}\frac{\delta_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|^{p^{\prime}}}{\theta_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}/p}}\biggr)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

It follows from (
8
) that
Q_{p}(\xi)\leq 1
almost for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore, we get

\biggl\|\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi)x(\xi)+\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\xi)z_{j}(\xi)\biggr\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\\
\leq\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi|(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j}}|x(\xi)|^{p}\,d\xi+\sum_{j\in M}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z_{j}(\xi)|^{p}\,d\xi\\
\leq\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}\delta_{j_{s}}^{p}=\sum_{s=1}^{l}\theta_{j_{s}}e^{-pS(\alpha^{0})}=e^{-pS(\alpha^{0})}.

Consequently,

e(\Lambda_{0},W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta,\Phi)\leq e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}.

Taking into account (
12
), it means that methods (
6
) are optimal and equality (
5
) holds.

If
p=1
we use the inequality

\biggl|\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi)x(\xi)+\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\xi)z_{j}(\xi)\biggr|\\
\leq Q_{1}(\xi)\biggl(\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}|\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha^{j}}|x(\xi)|+\widehat{\lambda}_{j}|z_{j}(\xi)|\biggr),

where

Q_{1}(\xi)=\max_{1\leq s\leq l}\frac{|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|}{\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}}.

By analogy with (
14
) we get

Q_{1}(\xi)=e^{S(\alpha^{0})}\max_{1\leq s\leq l}\frac{\delta_{j_{s}}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|}{\theta_{j_{s}}}.

Using the same arguments as for the case
1<p<\infty
, we obtain the assertion of the theorem in the case under consideration.

For
p=\infty
we use the inequality

\biggl\|\sum_{j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{l}\}\setminus M}\varphi^{\alpha^{j}}(\xi)a_{j}(\xi)x(\xi)+\sum_{j\in M}a_{j}(\xi)z_{j}(\xi)\biggr\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leq\biggl\|\sum_{s=1}^{l}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|\delta_{j_{s}}\biggr\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

It follows from (
8
) that

e(\Lambda_{0},W_{\infty}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta}),\delta,\Phi)\leq e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}.

Consequently, using (
12
), we obtain that methods (
6
) are optimal and (
5
) holds.

It remains to show that the set of functions
\alpha_{j_{s}}(\cdot)
,
s=1,\ldots,l
, satisfying conditions (
7
) and (
8
) is nonempty. Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Consider the function

f(\eta)=-1+\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}e^{-p\langle\alpha^{j_{s}}-\alpha^{0},\,\eta\rangle},\quad\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

This is obviously a convex function, and it is easy to verify that
f(\widehat{\eta})=0
and the derivative of this function at the point
\widehat{\eta}
is also zero. It follows that
f(\eta)\geq 0
for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Consequently,

f_{1}(\eta)=e^{-p\langle\alpha^{0},\,\eta\rangle}f(\eta)\geq 0

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Putting
e^{-\eta_{j}}=|\varphi_{j}(\xi)|
,

j=1,\ldots,k
, we obtain that

g(\xi)=-|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{0}}+\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j_{s}}}\geq 0

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus,

\frac{|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{0}}}{\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j_{s}}}}\leq 1.

Set

a_{j_{s}}(\xi)=\varphi^{\alpha^{0}}(\xi)\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}\varphi^{(p/2-1)\alpha^{j_{s}}}(\xi)\overline{\varphi}^{p/2\alpha^{j_{s}}}(\xi)}{\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j_{s}}}},\quad s=1,\ldots,l.

It is easy to check that condition (
7
) is valid. If
1<p<\infty
, then

\sum_{s=1}^{l}\frac{\delta_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|^{p^{\prime}}}{\theta_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}/p}}=e^{-p^{\prime}S(\alpha^{0})}\sum_{s=1}^{l}\frac{|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|^{p^{\prime}}}{\lambda_{j_{s}}^{p^{\prime}/p}}\\
=e^{-p^{\prime}S(\alpha^{0})}\left(\frac{|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{0}}}{\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{p\alpha^{j_{s}}}}\right)^{p^{\prime}-1}\leq e^{-p^{\prime}S(\alpha^{0})}.

If
p=1
, then

\dfrac{\delta_{j_{s}}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|}{\theta_{j_{s}}}=e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}\frac{|\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha^{0}}}{\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha^{j_{s}}}}\leq e^{-S(\alpha^{0})},\quad s=1,\ldots,l.

If
p=\infty
, we set

a_{j_{s}}(\xi)=\varphi^{\alpha^{0}}(\xi)\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}e^{-i\arg\{\varphi^{\alpha^{j_{s}}}(\xi)\}}}{\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha^{j_{s}}}},\quad s=1,\ldots,l,

where

\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}=\frac{\theta_{j_{s}}}{\delta_{j_{s}}}e^{-S(\alpha^{0})},\quad s=1,\ldots,l.

Then condition (
7
) is obviously satisfied, and

\sum_{s=1}^{l}|a_{j_{s}}(\xi)|\delta_{j_{s}}=e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}\frac{|\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha^{0}}}{\sum_{s=1}^{l}\widehat{\lambda}_{j_{s}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{\alpha^{j_{s}}}}\leq e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}.

∎

Note that it follows from the proof of Theorem
1
that

(15)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W_{p}^{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\overline{\delta})\\
\|\Lambda_{j}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,m\end{subarray}}\|\Lambda_{0}x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=e^{-S(\alpha^{0})}=\prod_{s=1}^{l}\delta_{j_{s}}^{\theta_{j_{s}}}\\
=\min\biggl\{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{j}^{\theta_{j}}:\theta_{j}\geq 0,\ j=1,\ldots,N,\ \sum_{j=1}^{N}\theta_{j}=1,\ \alpha^{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\theta_{j}\alpha^{j}\biggr\}.

Exact Carlson type inequalities

The Carlson inequality
[

\|x(t)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq\sqrt{\pi}\|x(t)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{1/2}\|tx(t)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{1/2},\quad\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty),

was generalized by many authors (see
[

,

,

\|w(\cdot)x(\cdot)\|_{L_{q}(T,\mu)}\leq K\|w_{0}(\cdot)x(\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(T,\mu))}^{\gamma}\|w_{1}(\cdot)x(\cdot)\|_{L_{r}(T,\mu)}^{1-\gamma},

where
T
is a cone in a linear space,
w(\cdot)
,
w_{0}(\cdot)
, and
w_{1}(\cdot)
are homogenous functions,
\mu
is a homogenous measure, and
1\leq q<p,r<\infty
(for

the exact inequality was obtained in
[
12
]
). Recall that a constant
K
is called exact if it cannot be replaced by a smaller value. The inequality in this case is called exact.

Weighted inequalities and inequalities for derivatives do not always have a multiplicative form. For example, for analytic and bounded in the strip
S_{\beta}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\IM z|<\beta\}
functions
x(t)\not\equiv 0
the inequality

\|x^{\prime}(\cdot)\|_{C(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{1}{2\beta}\|x(\cdot)\|_{C(\mathbb{R})}\|x(\cdot)\|_{C(S_{\beta})}^{2}\\
\times\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\|x(\cdot)\|_{C(S_{\beta})}^{4}\cos^{2}t+\|x(\cdot)\|_{C(\mathbb{R})}^{4}\sin^{2}t}}

holds (see
[
2
, с. 177]
).

In this regard, it becomes necessary to use a more general definition of exact inequalities. Let
X
be a linear space,
Y_{0},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}
be normed linear spaces, and

\Lambda_{j}\colon X\to Y_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,N
, be linear operators. We say that the inequality

(17)

\|\Lambda_{0}x\|_{Y_{0}}\leq\kappa(\Lambda x),\quad\Lambda x=(\|\Lambda_{1}x\|_{Y_{1}},\ldots,\|\Lambda_{N}x\|_{Y_{N}}),\quad\kappa\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+},

is exact, if it is fulfilled for all
x\in X
and there is no such
x_{0}\in X
for which

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in X\\
\|\Lambda_{j}x\|_{Y_{j}}\leq\|\Lambda_{j}x_{0}\|_{Y_{j}},\ j=1,\ldots,N\end{subarray}}\|\Lambda_{0}x\|_{Y_{0}}<\kappa(\Lambda x_{0}).

Proposition 1
.

Let
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Set

\kappa(\delta)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in X\\
\|\Lambda_{j}x\|_{Y_{j}}\leq\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,N\end{subarray}}\|\Lambda_{0}x\|_{Y_{0}}.

Then inequality (
17
) is exact.

Proof.

For
x\in X
we put

\delta=(\|\Lambda_{1}x\|_{Y_{1}},\ldots,\|\Lambda_{N}x\|_{Y_{N}}).

From the definition of
\kappa(\cdot)
inequality (
17
) holds. Assume that it is not exact. Then there is an element
x_{0}\in X
for which

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in X\\
\|\Lambda_{j}x\|_{Y_{j}}\leq\delta_{j}^{0},\ j=1,\ldots,N\end{subarray}}\|\Lambda_{0}x\|_{Y_{0}}<\kappa(\delta^{0}),

where

\delta^{0}=(\delta_{1}^{0},\ldots,\delta_{N}^{0})=(\|\Lambda_{1}x_{0}\|_{Y_{1}},\ldots,\|\Lambda_{N}x_{0}\|_{Y_{N}}).

This contradicts the definition of
\kappa(\cdot)
.
∎

The solution of extremal problem (
15

Let
\varphi(\cdot)=(\varphi_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\cdot))

\varphi_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\cdot)

\mathbb{R}^{d}

In particular, for

and

\||\xi|^{\alpha^{0}}x(\xi)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\min\biggl\{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\||\xi|^{\alpha^{j}}x(\xi)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\theta_{j}}:\\

Consider one more example. Let

(18)

\varphi(\xi)=\psi_{\theta}(\xi)=\left(|\xi_{1}|^{\theta}+\ldots+|\xi_{d}|^{\theta}\right)^{2/\theta},\quad\theta>0.

In this case

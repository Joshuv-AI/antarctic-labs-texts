Shi-Liang Wu, Cui-Xia Li

Abstract
To the best of our knowledge, since the extended horizontal linear complementarity problem (EHLCP) was first introduced and studied by Kaneko in 1977, no iterative methods or error analysis have been developed for it due to the interdependence of its multiple unknowns in a ‘chain-like’ structure. This paper aims to address these gaps by: • proposing an equivalent fixed-point formulation of the EHLCP by using a variable transformation technique with the max-min function; • developing efficient iterative methods for solving the EHLCP based on this fixed-point form, along with their convergence analysis; • deriving global error bounds and computable estimates for the EHLCP. Several numerical examples from applications such as multicommodity market equilibrium and bilateral obstacle problems are given to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods and bounds. Keywords: Extended horizontal linear complementarity problem; column W \mathrm{W} -property; iterative method; global error bound AMS classification: 90C33, 65G50, 65G20

One important research problem for the complementarity problem (CP)

5

\|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{x}^{\ast}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1+\|\mathrm{M}\|_{\infty}}{\alpha(\mathrm{M})}\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{x})\|_{\infty},\ \forall\ \mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.2)

where

\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{x})=\min\{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{M}\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{q}\},\alpha(\mathrm{M})=\min_{\|\mathrm{x}\|_{\infty}=1}\big\{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathrm{x}_{i}(\mathrm{M}\mathrm{x})_{i}\big\}.

By using the equivalent form of the minimum function, Chen and

\|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{x}^{\ast}\|_{p}\leq\max_{d\in[0,1]^{n}}\|(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{D}+\mathrm{DM})^{-1}\|_{p}\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{x})\|_{p},\forall\ \mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}

As we know, both the linear complementarity problem and the extended
vertical linear complementarity problem essentially involve only a
single unknown

form of the minimum function, the global error bounds can be
deduced, see

In such case, the technique used in
[

17

is no longer

\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}

\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}

Therefore, it needs to exploit the new technique to overcome these

While the column
\mathrm{W}

guarantees a unique solution to the

\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}

compute the solution. This motivates our second key research
objective: developing computationally effective algorithms for

\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}

For some special cases, there exist several methods. For instance,
when
m=2
and
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{H}_{2}=\mathrm{I}

proposed the modified Chandrasekaran’s method for

\mathrm{H}_{1}

investigated a parametric principal
pivoting algorithm to solve the LCP converted from (1.1) with

\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{I}
. Both are direct methods. However, when employing these algorithms to solve the sparse and
large problem, it requires too many pivots, and can not keep the
sparsity structure of the complementarity system intact. Based on
this consideration, by using the relationship between the linear
variational inequality problem (VIP) and the projection technique,
Ahn

[
21
]
proposed a projection-type iteration method of
\mathrm{H}_{1}
being an
\mathrm{H}_{+}

and

in (1.1) (see Method

]
.

The development of numerical algorithms for solving the general
EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) problems remains largely unexplored,
primarily due to the computational challenges posed by their chained
constraint structure. This paper addresses this gap by proposing
efficient iterative methods based on max-min function
transformations. Specifically, for the important special case where

m=2
with
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{H}_{2}=\mathrm{I}
, we develop a
simple yet effective iterative algorithm when
\mathrm{H}_{1}
is a

\mathrm{P}
-matrix. Unlike direct methods that rely on pivoting
operations, our approach preserves the original problem structure
throughout iterations while maintaining sparsity. These features
make our methods particularly suitable for solving large-scale
sparse EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) problems.

The key contributions of this work are:

•

Equivalent Fixed-Point Formulation:
A unified fixed-point
representation of the EHLCP is established by using a max-min
function and variable transformations, simplifying the problem to a
piecewise linear system.

•

Iterative Methods:
A general iterative algorithm (Method 3.1)
is proposed for the EHLCP, preserving matrix sparsity and avoiding
pivoting; a specialized method (Method 3.2) is developed for the
case where
m=2
and
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{H}_{2}=\mathrm{I}
in
(1.1) and
\mathrm{H}_{1}
is a P-matrix, with proven convergence
under practical conditions.

•

The Error Bounds:
The global error bounds of the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
)
are derived, generalizing existing results for LCPs and HLCPs.
Computable bounds are provided for matrices with positive diagonal
dominance or strict diagonal dominance.

•

Numerical Validation:
Experiments on real-world problems confirm the efficiency of the proposed methods and the sharpness of the error bounds.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the
equivalent form of the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) is exploited
by making use of variable transformation technique. In Section 3,
the iterative method is discussed by using the above equivalent
fixed-point form, and a simple and efficient iterative method for
m=2
and
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{H}_{2}=\mathrm{I}
in (1.1)
is proposed. In Section 4, the useful framework of the global error bounds is derived, and some
computable estimates for the relevant upper bounds are obtained under certain
conditions. In Section 5, the numerical results of some examples arising from different
applications are reported. Finally, in Section 6, we give some remarks to end this paper.

The equivalent form

In this section, we will make use of variable transformation
technique to get an equivalent fixed-point form of the
EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
), which only involves one unknown.

Taking

in (1.1), and

\mathrm{w}=\max\left\{\mathrm{0},-\mathrm{y}\right\},\mathrm{x}_{1}=\max\left\{\mathrm{0},\mathrm{y}\right\}\ \mbox{with}\ \mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.1)

it is easy to obtain an equivalent fixed-point form of
HLCP(
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{q}
), also see
[
19
,
20
]
. So, in the following we only need to consider
the case of
m\geq 2
in (1.1). The following lemma is important to
design the iterative methods of EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) and
get the global error bounds of EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d_{0}=0
,
0<d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,m-1
with

m\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle w,x_{i}\geq 0\ (i=1,\ldots,m),

(2.2a)

\displaystyle wx_{1}=0,

(2.2b)

\displaystyle x_{i}\leq d_{i}\ (i=1,2,\ldots,m-1),

(2.2c)

\displaystyle(d_{i}-x_{i})x_{i+1}=0\ (i=1,2,\ldots,m-1).

(2.2d)

if and only if there exists
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{split}&w=\max\left\{0,-y\right\},\\
&x_{i}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\right\}\quad(i=1,2,\dots,m-1),\\
&x_{m}=\max\left\{0,y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\right\}.\end{split}

Moreover, this result naturally extends, in component-wise form, to
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Clearly, (
2.2a
)-(
2.2c
)
hold.

Now, we prove that (
2.2d
) holds. Firstly, we show (
2.2d
) for
i=1,2,\dots,m-2
, i.e.,

\begin{split}(d_{i}-x_{i})x_{i+1}=&\left(d_{i}-\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\right\}\right)\\
&\times\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i}d_{j},d_{i+1}\right\}\right\}=0.\end{split}

Consider the following two cases:

(1)
if
y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j}\geq d_{i}
, then

d_{i}-x_{i}=d_{i}-\max\left\{0,d_{i}\right\}=0,

from which one may deduce
(x_{i}-d_{i})x_{i+1}=0
;

(2)
if
y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j}<d_{i}
, i.e.,
y-\sum_{j=0}^{i}d_{j}<0
, then

\begin{split}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i}d_{j},d_{i+1}\right\}\right\}=\max\left\{0,y-\sum_{j=0}^{i}d_{j}\right\}=0,\end{split}

this implies that
(x_{i}-d_{i})x_{i+1}=0
.

Secondly, we prove that (
2.2d
) holds for
i=m-1
. By the
similar technique, this case also holds, whose proof is omitted.

(\Rightarrow)
We consider two main cases on the base of the values
of
x_{i}
.

Case 1: All
x_{i}=0
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

From (2.2b),
wx_{1}=0
and
x_{1}=0
, so
w
can be any
non-negative number. We need to find
y
such that:

\begin{split}&w=\max\left\{0,-y\right\},\\
&for\ i=1,\ldots,m-1,x_{i}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\right\}=0,\\
&x_{m}=\max\left\{0,y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\right\}=0.\end{split}

Since
d_{0}=0
and
d_{i}>0
for
i\geq 1
, we choose
y\leq 0
. Then for any
i\geq 1
:

\bullet

y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j}\leq y\leq 0
, so
\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\leq 0
(
d_{i}>0
), hence

\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\right\}=0.

\bullet
Similarly,
y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\leq 0
, so
\max\left\{0,y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\right\}=0
.

Of course,
w=\max\{0,-y\}\geq 0
for
y\leq 0
. Thus, all
expressions are satisfied.

Case 2: Some
x_{i}>0.

For this case, we need to point out that the index
i\neq 1
, which
implies
x_{1}>0
. In fact, if
x_{1}=0
, then from (2.2d),

(d_{1}-x_{1})x_{2}=d_{1}x_{2}=0
implies
x_{2}=0
(since
d_{1}>0
).
Similarly,
(d_{2}-x_{2})x_{3}=d_{2}x_{3}=0
implies
x_{3}=0
,
and by induction, all
x_{i}=0
, which contradicts that some
x_{i}>0
. Hence,
x_{1}>0
. Further, from (2.2b), we have
w=0
.

Now, define an index
k
(
1\leq k\leq m
) as follows:

If
x_{m}>0
, then we set
k=m
. Otherwise, let
k
be the
largest index such that
x_{k}>0
(so
x_{i}=0
for
i>k
).

From (2.2d) and induction:

\bullet
If
k=m
, then for
i=m-1
,
(d_{m-1}-x_{m-1})x_{m}=0
and
x_{m}>0
, so
x_{m-1}=d_{m-1}
. Similarly, for
i=m-2
,
x_{m-2}=d_{m-2}
, and so on, up to
x_{1}=d_{1}
. Thus, when

k=m
, we have
x_{i}=d_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
and
x_{m}>0
.

\bullet
If
k<m
, then
x_{i}=0
for
i>k
. From (2.2d)
for
i=k
,
(d_{k}-x_{k})x_{k+1}=0
and
x_{k+1}=0
, so there
is no constraint on
x_{k}
(but
x_{k}\leq d_{k}
by (2.2c)).
Moreover, for
i<k
, if
x_{i}<d_{i}
, then
x_{i+1}=0

(2.2d), but this contradicts
x_{i+1}>0
(since

),

x_{i}=d_{i}
for

. Thus, when
k<m
, we have
x_{i}=d_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k-1
,
x_{k}\leq d_{k}
and
x_{k}>0
,
and
x_{i}=0

i>k
.

Now, construct
y
:

Subcase 2a:
k=m
(i.e.,
x_{m}>0
). Let
y=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_{j}+x_{m}
.

Subcase 2b:
k<m
. Let
y=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_{j}+x_{k}
(if
k=1
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{0}d_{j}=0
, so
y=x_{1}
).

We now verify the conclusions:

Since
w=0
and
y\geq 0
, we have
\max\{0,-y\}=0=w
.

For
x_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,m-1
):

Subcase 2a:
y=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_{j}+x_{m}
.

For
i=1,\ldots,m-1
,

y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_{j}+x_{m}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}d_{j}=\sum_{j=i}^{m-1}d_{j}+x_{m}\geq d_{i}\ \ (\sum_{j=i}^{m-1}d_{j}\geq d_{i}).

Thus,
\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}=d_{i}
, and
\max\left\{0,d_{i}\right\}=d_{i}=x_{i}
. I.e.,

x_{i}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\right\}.

Subcase 2b:
y=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_{j}+x_{k}
.

If
i<k
, then

y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_{j}+x_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}d_{j}=\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}d_{j}+x_{k}\geq d_{i}\ \ (\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}d_{j}\geq d_{i}).

So
\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}=d_{i}
, and
\max\left\{0,d_{i}\right\}=d_{i}=x_{i}
. Further,

x_{i}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\right\}.

If
i=k
, then
y-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}d_{j}=x_{k}
.
So
\min\left\{x_{k},d_{k}\right\}=x_{k}
(
x_{k}\leq d_{k}
),
and
\max\left\{0,x_{k}\right\}=x_{k}
. I.e.,

x_{k}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}d_{j},d_{k}\right\}\right\}.

If
i>k
, then

y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_{j}+x_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}d_{j}.

Since
i-1\geq k
, we have
\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}d_{j}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}
. So,

y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_{j}+x_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}=x_{k}-d_{k}\leq 0.

Thus,
\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\leq 0
,
and

\max\left\{0,\min\left\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\right\}\right\}=0=x_{i}.

For
x_{m}
:

Subcase 2a:
y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_{j}+x_{m}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{j}=x_{m}
(
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_{j}
since
d_{0}=0
). So
\max\left\{0,x_{m}\right\}=x_{m}
, i.e.,

x_{m}=\max\left\{0,y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\right\}.

Subcase 2b:
Since
k<m
,
x_{m}=0
.

y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_{j}+x_{k}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{j}=x_{k}-(d_{k}+\ldots+d_{m-1})\leq x_{k}-d_{k}\leq 0.

So,
\max\left\{0,y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\right\}=0=x_{m}
.

In all cases, the constructed
y
satisfies the required
expressions.
\hfill{}\Box

Remark 2.1
.

Lemma
2.1
is also true for
m=1
. In this case, both (2.2a)
and (2.2b) remain valid.

By Lemma
2.1
and Remark
2.1
, it is easy to prove that
the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) can be transformed to the
following unified and equivalent fixed-point formula. Concretely,
see Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathrm{y}\neq 0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For the EHLCP
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d})
, setting

\begin{split}&\mathrm{w}=\max\left\{\mathrm{0},-\mathrm{y}\right\},\\
&\mathrm{x}_{i}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}\quad(i=1,2,\dots,m-1),\\
&\mathrm{x}_{m}=\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\},\end{split}

(2.3)

where
\mathrm{d}_{0}=0
. Then the EHLCP
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d})

is equal to finding
\mathrm{y}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}\}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{H}_{m}\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}.

Remark 2.2
.

In Proposition
2.1
,
\mathrm{y}\neq 0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
reasonable. The reason is that if
\mathrm{y}=0
, then Eq.
(
2.4
) is not valid for
\mathrm{q}\neq 0
. Such a case,

\mathrm{w}=\mathrm{x}_{1}=\ldots=\mathrm{x}_{m}=0
is not the
solution of the EHLCP
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d})
for

\mathrm{q}\neq 0
. Hence, in the later discussion, without further
illustration, we always assume that
\mathrm{y}\neq 0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In the sequel, Eq. (
2.4
) will be employed to establish some
iterative methods and the global error bounds for the
EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
).

Remark 2.3
.

Lemma
2.1
still holds if we introduce a positive diagonal matrix

\mathrm{\Omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
for
\mathrm{w}
and a
positive diagonal matrix
\mathrm{\Delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

for
\mathrm{x}_{m}
, respectively, i.e.,

\mathrm{w}=\mathrm{\Omega}\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}\}\mbox{ and}\ \mathrm{x}_{m}=\mathrm{\Delta}\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}.

Combing Lemma
2.1
with Remark 2.3, the equivalent fixed-point
formula of EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) with parameters can also
be given, which is omitted.

3
The iterative method

In this section, our main task is to propose the iterative methods.
Clearly, Eq. (
2.4
) is a piecewise linear systems (PLS) as
well, which only involves one unknown vector
\mathrm{y}
. It is to
say that if Eq. (
2.4
) has a unique solution
\mathrm{y}
, and
this unique solution
\mathrm{y}
can be obtained from Eq.
(
2.4
) itself, then the solution
\mathrm{w},\mathrm{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}
of the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H,d}
)
is naturally obtained by (
2.3
). A natural question is under
what conditions Eq. (
2.4
) has a unique solution
\mathrm{y}
.
To answer this question, the following lemmas will be used.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a>0
. Then for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}

with
x\neq y
, there exists
\alpha\in[0,1]
such that

\max\{0,y-a\}-\max\{0,x-a\}=\alpha(y-x),

Moreover, if
0<a<b
, then there exist
\alpha
and
\beta\in[0,1]

such that
0\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1
and

(\max\{0,y-a\}-\max\{0,x-a\})-(\max\{0,y-b\}-\max\{0,x-b\})=(\alpha-\beta)(y-x).

Furthermore, these results are also true for component-wise form to
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.
By
\max\{a,b\}=\frac{1}{2}(|a-b|+a+b)
, we have

\displaystyle\max\{0,y-a\}-\max\{0,x-a\}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(|y-a|-|x-a|+y-x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|y-a|-|x-a|}{y-x}+1\right)(y-x).

Let

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|y-a|-|x-a|}{y-x}+1\right).

Then it is easy to see that
\alpha\in[0,1]
, which proves the
first assertion. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle(\max\{0,y-a\}-\max\{0,x-a\})-(\max\{0,y-b\}-\max\{0,x-b\})=(\alpha-\beta)(y-x),

where

\beta=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|y-b|-|x-b|}{y-x}+1\right).

Since
0<a<b
, we can get
0\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1
. This
completes the proof of this lemma.
\hfill{}\Box

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
h_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\ (i=1,\ldots,m)
,
d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\ (i=0,1,\ldots,m-1)
be defined as in Lemma
2.1
. Then
there are
\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_{0}=1
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,\min\bigg\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\bigg\}\bigg\}+h_{m}\max\bigg\{0,y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\bigg\}-h_{0}\max\{0,-y\}

\displaystyle-\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,\min\bigg\{y^{\ast}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\bigg\}\bigg\}

\displaystyle+h_{m}\max\bigg\{0,y^{\ast}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\bigg\}-h_{0}\max\{0,-y^{\ast}\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h_{0}\lambda_{0}+h_{1}\lambda_{1}+\ldots+h_{m}\lambda_{m})(y-y^{\ast}).

Moreover, this result naturally extends, in component-wise form, to
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.
By making use of

\displaystyle\max\{0,\min\{c,x\}\}=\max\{0,x\}-\max\{0,x-c\}\ \mbox{for}\ c>0

(3.1)

and

\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}\}+\mathrm{y}=\max\{0,\mathrm{y}\},

(3.2)

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,\min\bigg\{y-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\bigg\}\bigg\}+h_{m}\max\bigg\{0,y-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\bigg\}-h_{0}\max\{0,-y\}

\displaystyle-\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,\min\bigg\{y^{\ast}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}d_{j},d_{i}\bigg\}\bigg\}+h_{m}\max\bigg\{0,y^{\ast}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}\bigg\}-h_{0}\max\{0,-y^{\ast}\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,y-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i-1}\bigg\}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,y-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\bigg\}+h_{0}y-h_{0}\max\{0,y\}

\displaystyle-\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,y^{\ast}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i-1}\bigg\}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}h_{i}\max\bigg\{0,y^{\ast}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\bigg\}+h_{0}y^{\ast}-h_{0}\max\{0,y^{\ast}\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0}(y-y^{\ast})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h_{i}-h_{i-1})\bigg(\max\bigg\{0,y-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i-1}\bigg\}-\max\bigg\{0,y^{\ast}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i-1}\bigg\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0}(y-y^{\ast})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h_{i}-h_{i-1})\nu_{i}(y-y^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h_{0}(1-\nu_{1})+h_{1}(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})+h_{2}(\nu_{2}-\nu_{3})+\ldots+h_{m-1}(\nu_{m-1}-\nu_{m})+h_{m}\nu_{m})(y-y^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h_{0}\lambda_{0}+h_{1}\lambda_{1}+h_{2}\lambda_{2}+h_{3}\lambda_{3}+\ldots+h_{m-1}\lambda_{m-1}+h_{m}\lambda_{m})(y-y^{\ast}),

where the third to the last equalities follow from Lemma
3.1
,
i.e., there exists
0\leq\nu_{i}\leq 1\ (i=1,2,\ldots,m)
such that
it holds, and

\lambda_{0}=1-\nu_{1},\lambda_{1}=\nu_{1}-\nu_{2},\lambda_{3}=\nu_{2}-\nu_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1}=\nu_{m-1}-\nu_{m},\lambda_{m}=\nu_{m}.

From Lemma
3.1
, it is easy to check that

1\geq\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}\geq\nu_{3}\geq\cdots\geq\nu_{m}\geq 0,

and

\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{m}=1.

This completes proof.
\hfill{}\Box

Based on Lemma
3.2
, we can easily get a sufficient condition
to guarantee a unique solution of the PLS (
2.4
), see Theorem

3.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let the block matrix
\mathrm{H}
have the column

\mathrm{W}
-property. Then the PLS (
2.4
) has a unique
solution.

Proof.
Eq. (
2.4
) is expressed as

\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}\\
+\mathrm{H}_{m}\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}-\mathrm{M}\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}\}=-\mathrm{q}.

(3.3)

Following the methodology of Lemma
3.2
, subtracting

\mathrm{\bar{q}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{0-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}\\
+\mathrm{H}_{m}\max\left\{0,0-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}-\mathrm{M}\max\{0,0\}

from both sides of Eq. (
3.3
) simultaneously, one readily
obtain

(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m})y=-\mathrm{q}-\mathrm{\bar{q}}=-\mathrm{q},

where
(\mathrm{D}_{0},\mathrm{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mathrm{\bar{q}}=0
, which implies that the PLS
(
2.4
) has a unique solution if the block matrix
\mathrm{H}

has the column
\mathrm{W}
-property.
\hfill{}\Box

Theorem
3.1
answers the previous question, and tells us that
under the same condition that the block matrix
\mathrm{H}
has the
column
\mathrm{W}
-property, both the PLS (
2.4
) and the EHLCP
(1.1) have a unique solution. Based on this premise, we can design
some iterative methods what we want.

In addition, from the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we can also
establish the relationship between
\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y})
and

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{x}_{i}(\mathrm{y})
as follows, which is
useful in the sequel.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}),\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}(\mathrm{y})
be given by (
2.3
). Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{x}_{i}(\mathrm{y})=\mathrm{y}+\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}).

Theorem
3.2
not only shows the relationship between

\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y})
and

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{x}_{i}(\mathrm{y})
, but also reveals the
intrinsic relationship of
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{x}_{i}(\mathrm{y})

themselves, i.e.,

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{x}_{i}(\mathrm{y})=\max\{0,y\}.

In view of Eq. (
3.2
), now we may propose the following
iterative method:

Method 3.1
.

Let the block matrix
\mathrm{H}
have the column

\mathrm{W}
-property, and let
\mathrm{y}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
an initial guess.
For
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, until convergence, compute
\mathrm{y}^{k+1}

by

\displaystyle\mathrm{My}^{k+1}=\mathrm{M}\max\{0,\mathrm{y}^{k}\}-\mathrm{q}-\phi(\mathrm{y}^{k}),

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle\phi(\mathrm{y}^{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}^{k}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}+\mathrm{H}_{m}\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}^{k}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}.

Let
\mathrm{y}^{*}
be the convergence point of
\mathrm{y}^{k}
.
Then set

\displaystyle\mathrm{w}^{*}=\max\left\{\mathrm{0},-\mathrm{y}^{*}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{x}^{*}_{i}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}^{*}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}\quad(i=1,2,\dots,m-1),

\displaystyle\mathrm{x}^{*}_{m}=\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}^{*}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}.

Clearly, by Proposition
2.1
the vectors
\mathrm{w}^{*},\mathrm{x}^{*}_{i}
generated by Method
3.1
are the solution of
EHLCP(
\mathrm{H,d}
).

It is also known that the proposed method, Method
3.1
, does
not involve any pivoting operations as in the direct method.
In particular, the original data
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m}
in Method
3.1
remain unchanged throughout
iterative process, allowing it to be efficient for large scale and
specially structured problems.

The following result is the global convergence theorem for Method
3.1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let the block matrix
\mathrm{H}
have the column

\mathrm{W}
-property.
If

\rho(\mathrm{L})<1,

(3.5)

then the iterative sequence
\{\mathrm{y}^{k}\}^{+\infty}_{k=0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
generated by Method
3.1
converges
to the unique solution
\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the
PLS (
2.4
) for an arbitrary initial vector

\mathrm{y}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where

\mathrm{L}=\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{M}^{-1}(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m}),

and
(\mathrm{D}_{0},\mathrm{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}.

Proof.
From (
2.4
),
\mathrm{y}^{\ast}
satisfies

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}^{\ast}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{H}_{m}\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}^{\ast}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}-\mathrm{M}\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\}\Bigg)-\mathrm{q}.

And Eq. (
3.4
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{My}^{k+1}-\mathrm{My}^{k}=

\displaystyle-\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}^{k}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{H}_{m}\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}^{k}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}-\mathrm{M}\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}^{k}\}\Bigg)-\mathrm{q}.

Subtracting (
3.6
) from (
3.7
), from Lemma
3.2
,
there exist nonnegative diagonal matrices

\mathrm{D}_{i}=\mbox{diag}(d_{i})
with
d_{i}\in[0,1]^{n}

(i=0,1,\ldots,m)
and
\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{D}_{i}=\mathrm{I}
, i.e.,
(\mathrm{D}_{0},\mathrm{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}

such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{My}^{k+1}-\mathrm{My}^{k}=-(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m})(\mathrm{y}^{k}-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}).

Since the block matrix
\mathrm{H}
has the column
\mathrm{W}
-property,
\mathrm{M}
is nonsingular. Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{y}^{k+1}-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}=(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{M}^{-1}(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m}))(\mathrm{y}^{k}-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}),

which together with the condition (
3.5
) the iterative series

\{\mathrm{y}^{k}\}^{+\infty}_{k=0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
Method
3.1
converge. This proves Theorem
3.3
.
\hfill{}\Box

It is difficult to check the condition (
3.5
) in general. In the following we deduce some computable convergence conditions.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let the block matrix
\mathrm{H}
have the column

\mathrm{W}
-property.
If

\displaystyle\rho\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}|\bigg)<1\ \mbox{or}\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\|<1,

(3.8)

then the iterative sequence
\{\mathrm{y}^{k}\}^{+\infty}_{k=0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
generated by Method
3.1
converges
to the unique solution
\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the
PLS (
2.4
) for an arbitrary initial vector

\mathrm{y}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.
For

(\mathrm{D}_{0},\mathrm{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{D}_{i}=\mathrm{I}
, by the
proof of Theorem
3.3
we have

\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{M}^{-1}(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{H}_{i})D_{i},

from which it is easy to know that under the condition (
3.8
)
the iterative sequence
\{\mathrm{y}^{k}\}^{+\infty}_{k=0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
generated by Method
3.1
converges
to the unique solution
\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the
PLS (
2.4
) for an arbitrary initial vector

\mathrm{y}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
\hfill{}\Box

It is noted that from PLS (
2.4
) and Remark 2.3, some other
iterative methods such as the parameter version of Method
3.1
,
the Newton-type method, the modulus-type method can be established,

Next, following the methodology of Method
3.1
, we can design a
simple and efficient iterative method for solving the following
special EHLCP with
m=2,~\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{H}_{2}=\mathrm{I}
, i.e.,

\begin{split}&\mathrm{w}=\mathrm{q}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{x}_{1}+\mathrm{x}_{2},\\
&\mathrm{w},\mathrm{x}_{1},\mathrm{x}_{2}\geq 0,\mathrm{b}>0,\\
&\mathrm{w}^{T}\mathrm{x}_{1}=0,\\
&\mathrm{x}_{1}\leq\mathrm{b},(\mathrm{b}-\mathrm{x}_{1})^{T}\mathrm{x}_{2}=0,\end{split}

(3.9)

which is often from many practical problems, e.g., multicommodity
market equilibrium problem, quadratic programming (QP) problem, the
bilateral obstacle problem and others. Assuming that

\mathrm{H}_{1}
is a
\mathrm{P}
-matrix. Then by Lemma
1.1

the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) (
3.9
) has a unique
solution.

Based on Lemma
2.1
and Remark 2.2, we take

\mathrm{w}=\Omega\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}\},\mathrm{x}_{1}=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{b}\right\}\right\},\mathrm{x}_{2}=\Delta\max\{0,\mathrm{y}-\mathrm{b}\}

for the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) (
3.9
), where
\Delta

and
\mathrm{\Omega}
are any positive diagonal matrices. Here, for
the sake of simplicity, we only consider this case where
\Omega=\Delta
. In such case, its PLS is of the form

\Omega\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}\}=\mathrm{q}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{b}\right\}\right\}+\Omega\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}-\mathrm{b}\right\},

which together with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) gives

\Omega\mathrm{y}=-((\mathrm{H}_{1}-\Omega)\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{b}\right\}\right\}+\mathrm{q}).

(3.10)

Based on Eq. (
3.10
), we can design the following iterative
method for solving the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) (
3.9
).

Method 3.2
.

Given an arbitrary initial guess
\mathrm{y}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
for
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, until convergence, compute
\mathrm{y}^{k+1}

by

\Omega\mathrm{y}^{k+1}=-((\mathrm{H}_{1}-\Omega)\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}^{k},\mathrm{b}\right\}\right\}+\mathrm{q}),k=0,1,\ldots,

where
\mathrm{\Omega}
is any positive diagonal matrix. Let

\mathrm{y}^{*}
be the convergence point of

. Then
set

\displaystyle\mathrm{w}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Omega\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}^{*}\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{x}_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}^{*},\mathrm{b}\right\}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{x}_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Omega\max\{0,\mathrm{y}^{*}-\mathrm{b}\}.

It is easy to see that the vectors
\mathrm{w}^{*},\mathrm{x}^{*}_{1}
and
\mathrm{x}^{*}_{2}
generated by Method

3.2
are the numerical solution of
EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) (

).

Similarly, Method
3.2
does not involve any pivoting
operations, which implies that it is different from the modified
Chandrasekaran’s method
[

]
. Clearly, in the proposed

solving the large scale and sparse problems. Concretely, see Section

the following projection-type method for solving the

\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) (

):

Method 3.3
.

(see Algorithm 4.1
[
21
]
) For
\mathrm{x}_{1}^{0}\in[0,\mathrm{b}]
, let

\mathrm{x}_{1}^{k+1}=\eta\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{b}}[\mathrm{x}_{1}^{k}-\omega\mathrm{E}(\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{x}_{1}^{k}+\mathrm{q}+\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{x}_{1}^{k+1}-\mathrm{x}_{1}^{k}))]+(1-\eta)\mathrm{x}_{{}_{1}}^{k},k=0,1,\ldots,

(3.12)

where
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{b}}

[0,\mathrm{b}]
,

and

,
\mathrm{E}

\mathrm{H}_{1}

Comparing Method
3.2
with Method
3.3
, we know that

3.2
can get the unique solution of the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
) (
3.9
) directly. In addition, Method
3.3
involves many parameters, it brings

The convergence theorem of Method
3.2
is given below. The

3.3
, so we omit it.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega=w\mathrm{I}
with
w>0
. If

\rho(|w^{-1}\mathrm{H}_{1}-\mathrm{I}|)<1,

(3.13)

then the iterative sequence
\{\mathrm{y}^{k}\}^{+\infty}_{k=0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

3.2
converges
to the unique solution
\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the
PLS (
3.10
) for an arbitrary initial vector

\mathrm{y}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Clearly, we use the following simple condition

\|w^{-1}\mathrm{H}_{1}-\mathrm{I}\|<1

(3.14)

to replace (
3.13
) in order to guarantee convergence of Method

3.2
.

Noting that the convergence conditions (
3.13
) and (

)
do not demand that
\mathrm{H}_{1}
is a
\mathrm{Z}
-matrix or an

\mathrm{H}_{+}
-matrix. In fact, taking

1&1&1.5\\

is neither

\mathrm{Z}

\mathrm{H}_{+}
-matrix, but a

In such case, we can not employ the modified Chandrasekaran’s method in
[

]
and Method

because the former is a custom-tailored direct method for this

\mathrm{Z}
-matrix and the latter one is a custom-tailored

\mathrm{H}_{+}
-matrix. In

.

A natural question is how to choose the parameter

. In order to answer this question, in the following we discuss
it with two cases:
\mathrm{H}_{1}
is symmetric positive
definite, and
\mathrm{H}_{1}
is a column sdd matrix with

•

\mathrm{H}_{1}

•

\mathrm{H}_{1}

and

. For this

. In fact, it is easy to check that

In addition, when the elements of

4
The global error bound

In this section, we focus on discussing the global error bounds of
the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
). Assuming that

\mathrm{w}^{\ast},\mathrm{x}^{\ast}_{1}
, …,

\mathrm{x}^{\ast}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a unique solution of
EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
). Let

\begin{split}\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w},\mathrm{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}):=\mathrm{q}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{H}_{i}\mathrm{x}_{i}-\mathrm{Mw}\end{split}

(4.1a)

subject to

\displaystyle\mathrm{w},\mathrm{x}_{i}\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,m,

(4.1b)

\displaystyle\mathrm{w}^{T}\mathrm{x}_{1}=0,

(4.1c)

\displaystyle\mathrm{x}_{i}\leq\mathrm{d}_{i}\ (\mathrm{d}_{i}>0),(\mathrm{d}_{i}-\mathrm{x}_{i})^{T}\mathrm{x}_{i+1}=0,i=1,2,\ldots,m-1.

(4.1d)

Clearly,
\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}^{\ast},\mathrm{x}^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{x}^{\ast}_{m})=0
. If

\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w},\mathrm{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m})\neq 0
, then our purpose is to find the lower and upper bounds for

\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w},\mathrm{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m})\|
.

From Proposition
2.1
,
\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w},\mathrm{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m})
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}),\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}(\mathrm{y}))=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}_{i}\max\left\{0,\min\left\{\mathrm{y}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}\right\}

(4.2)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{H}_{m}\max\left\{0,\mathrm{y}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{d}_{i}\right\}-\mathrm{M}\max\{0,-\mathrm{y}\}+\mathrm{q},

where
\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}),\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}(\mathrm{y})
are defined as in (
2.3
).

By Proposition
2.1
, if

\mathrm{w}^{\ast},\mathrm{x}^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,x^{\ast}_{m}
is a
unique solution of the EHLCP(
\mathrm{H},\mathrm{d}
), then there
exists a corresponding vector
\mathrm{y}^{\ast}
such that
(
2.4
) holds, i.e.,
\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{y}^{\ast})=0
, and
from Lemma
3.2
, there exists

(\mathrm{D}_{0},\mathrm{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}
such that

\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}),\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}(\mathrm{y}))=(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m})(\mathrm{y}-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}),

which together with Lemma 1.1(
ii
) gives the following theorem:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let the block matrix
\mathrm{H}
have the column
\mathrm{W}
-property. Then

(1)
for any
(\mathrm{D}_{0},\mathrm{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}
, the matrix
\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m}
is nonsingular;

(2)
for any
\mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\frac{1}{\underline{\alpha}(\mathrm{H})}\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}),\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}(\mathrm{y}))\|\leq\|\mathrm{y}-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\|\leq\overline{\alpha}(\mathrm{H})\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}),\ldots,\mathrm{x}_{m}(\mathrm{y}))\|,

(4.3)

where

\overline{\alpha}(\mathrm{H})=\max_{(\mathrm{D}_{0},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}}\|(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m})^{-1}\|

and

\underline{\alpha}(\mathrm{H})=\max_{(\mathrm{D}_{0},\ldots,\mathrm{D}_{m})\in\mathcal{D}}\|\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m}\|.

For special cases we have

•

For the HLCP(
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{q}
) case, the bound (
4.3
) reduces to the following one:

\frac{1}{\underline{\beta}(\mathrm{H})}\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}))\|\leq\|\mathrm{y}-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\|\leq\overline{\beta}(\mathrm{H})\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}))\|,

where

\overline{\beta}(\mathrm{H})=\max_{\mathrm{D}_{0}+\mathrm{D}_{1}=\mathrm{I}}\|(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1})^{-1}\|\ \mbox{and}\ \underline{\beta}(\mathrm{H})=\max_{\mathrm{D}_{0}+\mathrm{D}_{1}=\mathrm{I}}\|\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}\|,

and
\mathrm{D}_{0},\mathrm{D}_{1}
are nonnegative diagonal
matrices.

•

For the LCP(
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{q}
) case, with the assumption of
\mathrm{M}
being a
\mathrm{P}
-matrix,
the bound (
4.3
) reduces to

\frac{1}{\underline{\gamma}(\mathrm{H})}\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}))\|\leq\|\mathrm{y}-\mathrm{y}^{\ast}\|\leq\overline{\gamma}(\mathrm{H})\|\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{w}(\mathrm{y}),\mathrm{x}_{1}(\mathrm{y}))\|,

where

\overline{\gamma}(\mathrm{H})=\max\|(\mathrm{MD}+\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{D})^{-1}\|\ \mbox{and}\ \underline{\gamma}(\mathrm{H})=\max\|\mathrm{MD}+\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{D}\|,

and
\mathrm{D}=\mbox{diag}(d)
with
d\in[0,1]^{n}
is nonnegative
diagonal matrix.

It is difficult to directly compute
\overline{\alpha}(\mathrm{H})

in general. Next we shall present some computable error bounds for
the special block matrix
\mathrm{H}
: (I)

\mathrm{H}=(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m})
with
each matrix having positive diagonal part; (II)

\mathrm{H}=(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m})
with
each matrix being a column sdd matrix. For the case (I), we have

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{\Lambda}_{0}-\mathrm{C}_{0}
,

\mathrm{H}_{i}=\mathrm{\Lambda}_{i}-\mathrm{C}_{i}
be the
splitting of
\mathrm{M}
and
\mathrm{H}_{i}
, respectively, where

\mathrm{\Lambda}_{i}
is the diagonal part of
\mathrm{M}
and

\mathrm{H}_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,m
. If
\Lambda_{i}>0
and

\rho(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\})<1,

(4.4)

then the block matrix
\mathrm{H}=(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m})
has the column
\mathrm{W}
-property and

\overline{\alpha}(\mathrm{H})\leq\|(\mathrm{I}-\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\})^{-1}\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}\}\|.

(4.5)

Proof.
Let

\mathrm{V}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{\Lambda}_{i}\mathrm{D}_{i}
and

\mathrm{U}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{C}_{i}\mathrm{D}_{i}
. Then

\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m}=\mathrm{V}-\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{V}(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U}).

By Theorem
4.1
, it is known that
\mathrm{V}
is nonsingular.
It is easy to check that

\mathrm{V}^{-1}\leq\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}\},\ \mathrm{V}^{-1}|\mathrm{U}|\leq\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\Lambda^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\}.

From (
4.4
) it follows that

\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U}
is nonsingular, and thus

\mathrm{H}=(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m})
has
the column
\mathrm{W}
-property.

Noting that

(\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m})^{-1}=(\mathrm{V}-\mathrm{U})^{-1}=(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U})^{-1}\mathrm{V}^{-1}

and

\displaystyle|(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U})^{-1}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{I}+(\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U})+(\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U})^{2}+...|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}+(\mathrm{V}^{-1}|\mathrm{U}|)+(\mathrm{V}^{-1}|\mathrm{U}|)^{2}+...

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}+(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\})+(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\})^{2}+...

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{I}-\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\})^{-1}.

Then

|(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U})^{-1}\mathrm{V}^{-1}|\leq(\mathrm{I}-\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\})^{-1}\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U})^{-1}\mathrm{V}^{-1}\|\leq\|\ |(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{U})^{-1}\mathrm{V}^{-1}|\ \|\leq\|(\mathrm{I}-\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}|\mathrm{C}_{i}|\})^{-1}\max_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\mathrm{\Lambda}^{-1}_{i}\}\|,

which implies that the inequality (
4.5
) holds.
\hfill{}\Box

For the case (II), we have

Theorem 4.3
.

Let the block matrix
\mathrm{H}=(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m})
, where
\mathrm{M}^{T}
and
\mathrm{H}^{T}_{i}

are the row sdd matrices with the
\ell
-th diagonal entry having
the same sign,
\ell=1,\ldots,n
. Then the block matrix

\mathrm{H}=(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m})
has
the column
\mathrm{W}
-property and

\overline{\alpha}_{1}(\mathrm{H})\leq\frac{1}{\min_{i\in\mathcal{N}}\min((\langle\mathrm{M}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i},(\langle\mathrm{H}^{T}_{1}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i},\ldots,(\langle\mathrm{H}^{T}_{m}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i})},

(4.6)

where
\overline{\alpha}_{1}(\mathrm{H})
denotes the 1-norm form of

\overline{\alpha}(\mathrm{H})
.

Proof.
Set

\mathrm{S}=\mathrm{MD}_{0}+\mathrm{H}_{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{H}_{m}\mathrm{D}_{m}

and

\langle\mathrm{H}^{T}_{i}\rangle\mathrm{e}=(r^{(i)}_{1},r^{(i)}_{2},\ldots,r^{(i)}_{m})^{T}\ \mbox{and}\ \langle\mathrm{M}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e}=r^{(i)}_{0}.

Since
\mathrm{M}^{T}
and
\mathrm{H}^{T}_{i}
are the row sdd
matrices, we have
r_{i}=\min_{j}\{r^{(i)}_{j}\}>0,j=0,1,2,\ldots,m
, and

\langle\mathrm{S}^{T}\rangle\geq\mathrm{D}_{0}^{T}\langle\mathrm{M}^{T}\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{D}^{T}_{i}\langle\mathrm{M}^{T}_{i}\rangle.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{S}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e}\geq\mathrm{D}_{0}^{T}\langle\mathrm{M}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{D}^{T}_{i}\langle\mathrm{H}^{T}_{i}\rangle\mathrm{e}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{D}^{T}_{i}r_{i}\mathrm{e}\geq\min\{r_{i}\}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{D}^{T}_{i}\mathrm{e}=\min\{r_{i}\}\mathrm{e}>0,

which shows that
\mathrm{S}^{T}
is a row sdd matrix, i.e.,
\mathrm{S}
is a column sdd matrix, which implies that

\mathrm{H}=(\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{m})
has
the column
\mathrm{W}
-property.

A simple computation gives

\displaystyle(\langle\mathrm{S}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i}=

\displaystyle|d_{0}(\mathrm{M}^{T})_{ii}+d_{1}(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{1})_{ii}+\ldots+d_{m}(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{m})_{ii}|

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}|d_{0}(\mathrm{M}^{T})_{ij}+d_{1}(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{1})_{ij}+\ldots+d_{m}(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{m})_{ij}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle d_{0}|(\mathrm{M}^{T})_{ii}|+d_{1}|(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{1})_{ii}|+\ldots+d_{m}|(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{m})_{ii}|

\displaystyle-d_{0}\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}|(\mathrm{M}^{T})_{ij}|-d_{1}\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}|(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{1})_{ij}|-\ldots-d_{m}\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}|(\mathrm{H}^{T}_{m})_{ij}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{0}\langle\mathrm{M}^{T}\mathrm{e}\rangle_{i}+d_{1}\langle\mathrm{H}_{1}^{T}\mathrm{e}\rangle_{i}+\ldots+d_{m}\langle\mathrm{H}_{m}^{T}\mathrm{e}\rangle_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\{(\langle\mathrm{M}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i},(\langle\mathrm{H}_{1}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i},\ldots,(\langle\mathrm{H}_{1}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i}\},

which together with the following well-known bound for a row sdd
matrix
\mathrm{A}

\|\mathrm{A}^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\min_{i\in\mathcal{N}}(\langle\mathrm{A}\rangle e)_{i}}

gives

\|(\mathrm{S}^{T})^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\min_{i\in\mathcal{N}}\min((\langle\mathrm{M}^{T}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i},(\langle\mathrm{H}^{T}_{1}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i},\ldots,(\langle\mathrm{H}^{T}_{m}\rangle\mathrm{e})_{i})}.

This proves the desired bound (
4.6
).
\hfill{}\Box

5.1
The numerical test for error upper bounds

In this subsection, our task is to confirm the feasibility and
efficiency of the upper bounds of Theorems
4.2
and

4.3
.

Example 5.1
(
[
20
]
) Consider the
HLCP(
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{q}
), in which

\mathrm{M}=\bar{\mathrm{A}}+\mu\mathrm{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\mathrm{H}_{1}=\bar{\mathrm{B}}+\nu\mathrm{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, where

\bar{\mathrm{A}}=\mbox{blktridiag}(-\mathrm{I},\mathrm{T},-\mathrm{I})
,

\bar{\mathrm{B}}=\mathrm{I}\otimes\mathrm{T}
,

\mathrm{T}=\mbox{tridiag}(-1,4,-1)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,

n=m^{2}
and
\mu\geq 0
,
\nu\geq 0
.

This HLCP(
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{q}
) can be derived
from the discretization of certain differential equation under the
complementarity conditions. For instance, under some 2D domain of
axes
(x,y)
, one considers

\Delta u(x,y)+\frac{\partial^{2}v(x,y)}{\partial x^{2}}+\mu u(x,y)+\nu v(x,y)=q(x,y),u\geq 0,v\geq 0,u^{T}v=0

with suitable boundary conditions, where
u(x,y),v(x,y)
and

q(x,y)
are three 2-D mappings. By making use of the five-point
difference scheme for the approximation of the Laplacian and central
finite difference scheme for the approximation of the second-order
partial derivative, we can get the desired HLCP(
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{q}
).

In our computations, we set the vector
\mathrm{q}
such that

\mathrm{q}=\mathrm{Mw}^{\ast}-\mathrm{Hx}_{1}^{\ast}
with

\mathrm{w}^{\ast}=(0.1,0,0.1,0\ldots,0,0.1,\ldots)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathrm{x}_{1}^{\ast}=(0,0.1,0,0.1\ldots,0,0.1,\ldots)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

where
\mathrm{w}^{\ast},\mathrm{x}_{1}^{\ast}
is the solution of
the HLCP(
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{q}
). By Theorem

3.2
,

\mathrm{y}^{\ast}=\mathrm{x}_{1}^{\ast}-\mathrm{w}^{\ast}=(-0.1,0.1,\ldots,-0.1,0.1,\ldots)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

is the unique solution of the corresponding PLS (
2.4
).

For convenience, we take
\mu=\nu>0
. In this setting,
\mathrm{M}
and

6
Conclusion

This paper addresses two fundamental challenges in the study of the
EHLCP: the lack of iterative methods and error bounds. By leveraging
a max-min transformation technique, we derive an equivalent
fixed-point formulation that enables the development of efficient
iterative algorithms and global error analysis. The proposed methods
avoid pivoting operations, making them suitable for large-scale
sparse problems. Theoretical results are validated through numerical
experiments, demonstrating practical applicability. Future work will
focus on designing accelerated iterative schemes and exploring
applications in broader domains.

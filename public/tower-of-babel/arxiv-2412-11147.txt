Functional equations of algebraic Rankin–Selberg p p -adic L L -functions
Kâzım Büyükboduk, Manisha Ganguly

Abstract
. This article presents an approach to the algebraic functional equation for Selmer complexes, which in turn have applications in the Iwasawa theoretic study of Rankin–Selberg products of the Hida and Coleman families. Our treatment establishes the functional equation for algebraic p p -adic L L -functions (that are given in terms of characteristic ideals of Selmer groups, which arise as the cohomology of appropriately defined Selmer complexes in degree 2 2 ). This is achieved by recovering the characteristic ideal as the determinant of the said Selmer complex, once we prove (under suitable but rather mild) hypotheses that the Selmer complex in question is perfect with amplitude [ 1 , 2 ] [1,2] , and its cohomology is concentrated in degree-2. The perfectness of these Selmer complexes turns out to be a delicate problem, and the required properties require a study of Tamagawa factors in families, which may be of independent interest.

by

for
j\geq i.
Then we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \psi_{j}^{i}(\mathfrak{p}_{k_{j}})=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{p}_{k_{j}}\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma_{K}^{0})\qquad\qquad\hbox{ if }{k_{j}}\leq s_{j}\,,\\
\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

It follows from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \det M_{i+1}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{A,{i+1}}}\mathcal{H}_{A,i}={\rm det}(M_{i}).\end{aligned}

As before, we observe that
\varprojlim_{i}{\rm det}(M_{i})
is a coadmissible
\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})
-module. We claim that

{\rm det}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M)=\varprojlim_{i}{\rm det}_{\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M_{i})=\varprojlim_{i}{\rm char}_{\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M_{i})={\rm char}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M).

We already proved that the second equality in (
3.5
) and third equality follows from the definition of
{\rm char}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{k})}(M).
So it remains to prove that

{\rm det}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M)=\varprojlim_{i}{\rm det}_{\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M_{i}).

Recall that, we are given a projective resolution of
M

0\longrightarrow P_{1}\xrightarrow{\,\,\phi\,\,}P_{0}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0.

by finite
\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})
-modules of the same rank
n
. As
\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})
is flat over
\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})
, then we have

(3.8)

0\longrightarrow{}P_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})\xrightarrow{\phi_{i}}P_{0}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})\longrightarrow{}M_{i}\longrightarrow{}0

since
M_{i}=M\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K}).
For simplicity, let us set
P_{m,i}=P_{m}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})
for
m=0,1
. Then each
P_{m,i}
is a free
\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})
-module of rank
n
, as
P_{m}
is the same over
\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})
.

From exact sequence (
3.8
) and the definition of the determinant functor, it then follows that we have
{\rm det}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M)=\varprojlim_{i}{\rm det}_{\mathcal{H}_{A,i}(\Gamma^{0}_{K})}(M_{i})
using arguments similar to those utilised in the proof of Proposition
3.2
. This completes the proof of our proposition.

∎

Proposition 5.2

(Functional equation, preliminary form)
.

We have the following equality of determinants of perfect complexes:

\det\,\textup{\bf R}{\rm Hom}_{A}(\textup{\bf R}\Gamma(V^{*}(1),\textup{\bf D}^{\perp},M^{\perp}),A[-3])=\det\,\textup{\bf R}\Gamma(V,\textup{\bf D},M)\otimes_{A}\det\,{\rm cone}\left({\bigcup_{-,V,\textup{\bf D},M}}_{*}\right).

The first asserted equality follows from

Lemma 5.10
.

Suppose that for each positive integer

For simplicity, only in this proof, we let

[

It remains to prove the isomorphism

(5.10)

\varprojlim_{i}\,{\rm Hom}_{R_{i}}(M_{i},R_{i})\simeq{\rm Hom}_{R}(M,R)

in our lemma.

Note that by
[
KPX14
, Lemma 2.1.4(3)]
(see also
[
ST03
]
, Corollary 3.1), we have a natural identification

thanks to our running conditions on the inverse system.

Since

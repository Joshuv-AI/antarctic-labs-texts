Asymptotic invariants of residually finite just infinite groups Dedicated to the memory of Nikolay A. Vavilov
Andrei Jaikin-Zapirain, Steffen Kionke

Abstract
. Recently, Eduard Schesler and the second author constructed examples of finitely generated residually finite, hereditarily just infinite groups with positive first L 2 L^{2} -Betti number. In contrast to their result, we show that a finitely generated residually- p p just infinite group has trivial first L 2 L^{2} -Betti number. Moreover, we prove that the normal homology rank gradient of a finitely generated, residually finite, just infinite group vanishes.



1.
Introduction

The study of normal subgroups is a classical topic in group theory, and a large part of Nikolay Vavilov’s research was dedicated to investigating the normal subgroup structure of Chevalley and Steinberg groups over arbitrary commutative rings (see
[
VG04
,
VS07
,
Vav11
]
and references therein). Here, we consider just infinite groups, i.e. infinite groups whose all non-trivial normal subgroups are of finite index. The importance of this family of groups lies in the fact that, by Zorn’s Lemma, every finitely generated infinite group has a just infinite quotient. If a just infinite group is not residually finite, then it is virtually a power of a simple group.

If we measure the height of a group as the Krull dimension of the lattice of its normal subgroups, then free groups will be among the tallest and just infinite groups among the shortest. Fixing a natural number
d
, a natural measure of fatness for a
d
-generated group
\Gamma
is its first
L^{2}
-Betti number
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})
. Notice that for a
d
-generated group
\Gamma
,
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})\leq d-1
and, moreover, if
d>1
,
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})=d-1
if and only if
\Gamma
is free. Therefore, it is quite surprising that for any
\varepsilon>0
and a natural number
d>1
, there exists a simple
d
-generated group
\Lambda
with
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Lambda,\mathbb{Q})>d-1-\varepsilon

[
OT11
]
and there exists a residually finite just infinite
d
-generated group
\Gamma
with
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})>d-1-\varepsilon

[
KS24
]
.
In our first result we show that this is impossible in the case of finitely generated residually-
p
just infinite groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually-
p
just infinite group. Then

b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})=0
.

A group
\Gamma
is called locally zero-one if every finitely
generated subgroup is either finite or of finite index. In
[
EJZ13
]
Ershov and the first author constructed examples of infinite, finitely generated residually finite and locally zero-one
p
-groups.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a residually finite and locally zero-one
p
-group.
Then
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Proof.

If
\Gamma
is locally finite, then it is amenable, and so
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})=0
.
Assume that
\Gamma
is not locally finite. Then it is finitely generated, and so it has a just infinite quotient
\Gamma/N
. Since
\Gamma
is locally zero-one,
N
is locally finite, and so amenable.

If
N
is finite, then since
\Gamma
is residually finite,
\Gamma
and
\Gamma/N
are commensurable. By Theorem
1.1
,
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma/N,\mathbb{Q})=0
. Therefore
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Now assume that
N
is infinite. Since it is amenable,
b_{1}^{(2)}(N,\mathbb{Q})=0
. By a result of Gaboriau
[
Gab02
, Theorem 6.8]
(see also
[
SP24
]
for a recent new proof), a group with an infinite normal subgroup of infinite index and finite first
L^{2}
-Betti number has trivial first
L^{2}
-Betti number. Therefore,
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})
must be zero.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.1
has two main ingredients. Let
\Gamma_{\hat{p}}
be the pro-

completion of

. Using a variation of results of Barnea, Schlage-Puchta
[

and Pappas
[

we show that if the rank gradient

is positive, then the quotient of

is infinite. In particular,

cannot be just infinite.
On the other hand, by a result of Erhov and Lück
[
EL14
, Theorem 1.6]
,

. This is explained in more detail in Section
2
.
In Section
4
we provide new proofs of the results from
[
BSP13
,
Pap13
,
EL14
]
mentioned above, which we hope will interest the reader.

The notion of rank gradient of pro-
p
groups can be generalized in different ways to arbitrary profinite groups

. We consider the following generalization, called
the (lower) normal homology rank gradient of
G

{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)=\liminf_{U\unlhd_{o}G}\frac{d({\rm H}_{1}(U,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}))}{|G:U|}=\sup_{V\unlhd_{o}G}\left(\inf_{U\unlhd_{o}G,U\leq V}\frac{d({\rm H}_{1}(U,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}))}{|G:U|}\right).

In our second result, proved in Section
3
, we show that for a finitely generated residually finite just infinite group, the normal homology rank gradient of its profinite completion vanishes.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated dense just infinite subgroup of a profinite group
G
.
Then
{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)=0
.

A group is called hereditarily just infinite if all its subgroups of finite index are just infinite. We finish the Introduction by proposing the following natural question that arises from our work.

Question 1
.

Is there a finitely generated hereditarily just infinite group with positive first

-Betti number which is residually-
P

?

2.
Residually-
p
just infinite groups have trivial first
L^{2}
-Betti number

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group and
G>U_{1}>U_{2}>\ldots
a residual chain (i.e.
\displaystyle\cap_{i}U_{i}=\{1\}
) of normal open subgroups of
G
. The minimal number of generators
d(G)
of

coincides with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}{\rm H}_{1}(G;\mathbb{F}_{p})
and by the Nielsen-Schreier formula
d(U_{i})-1\leq|G:U_{i}|(d(G)-1)
. Therefore, the sequence
\frac{d(U_{i})}{|G:U_{i}|}
is decreasing and bounded from below. Hence, we can define the limit

\rg(G)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(U_{i})}{|G:U_{i}|},

which is called the rank gradient of
G
. It does not depend on the chain and, in fact, we also have

(1)

\rg(G)=\inf_{U\lhd_{o}G}\frac{d(U)}{|G:U|}.

Observe that if
\rg(G)>0
, then
G
is infinite.

The following result is a variation of results of Barnea, Schlage-Puchta
[
BSP13
, Theorem 3.1]
and Pappas
[
Pap13
, Theorem 3.2]
. In the appendix we will present a new proof of it.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group,
g\in G
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
N
be the closed normal subgroup of
G
generated by
g^{p^{k}}
and
\overline{G}=G/N
. Then

\rg(\overline{G})\geq\rg(G)-\frac{1}{p^{k}}
.

For the definition of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers of groups we refer the reader to the book of Lück
[
Lüc02
]
. The definition of the first
L^{2}
-Betti number is also presented in the appendix.
The following result was proved by Ershov and Lück
[
EL14
, Theorem 1.6]
. In the appendix we will provide an alternative proof of it.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually-
p
group. Then
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})\leq\rg(\Gamma_{\hat{p}})
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually-
p
just infinite group. Assume that

. Then by Proposition
2.2
,
\alpha=\rg(\Gamma_{\hat{p}})>0
. Let

be such that
\frac{1}{p^{k}}<\alpha
.
By Zel’manov’s solution of the restricted Burnside problem
[
Zel90
,
Zel91
]
, we know that there exist
g\in\Gamma
such that

(note that, since
\Gamma
is residually

is either infinite or a power of

, the normal subgroup of
\Gamma
generated by

is of infinite index. But this contradicts the condition that

is just infinite.
∎

3.
Just infinite groups have vanishing normal homology rank gradient

Let
G
be a finitely generated profinite group. Observe that

{\rm H}_{1}(G;\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\cong G/\overline{[G,G]}.

Define the homology rank gradient and normal homology rank gradient of
G
as

{\rm Hrg}(G)=\liminf_{U\leq_{o}G}\frac{d({\rm H}_{1}(U;\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}))}{|G:U|}\textrm{\ and\ }{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)=\liminf_{U\unlhd_{o}G}\frac{d({\rm H}_{1}(U;\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}))}{|G:U|}.

We note that
{\rm Hrg}(G)\leq{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)
and, moreover, for
H\unlhd_{o}G
we have

{\rm Hrg}(H)=|G:H|{\rm Hrg}(G).

For a finitely generated abstract group
\Gamma
, its first homology rank gradient and normal homology rank gradient are defined to be the homology rank gradient and the normal homology rank gradient of its profinite completion

{\rm Hrg}(\Gamma)={\rm Hrg}(\widehat{\Gamma}),\ {\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(\Gamma)={\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(\widehat{\Gamma}).

If
G
is a pro-
p
group and
U
is an open subgroup, there exists an open normal subgroup
K
of
G
contained in
U
; then one has

\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}{\rm H}_{1}(K;\mathbb{F}_{p})}{|G:K|}\leq\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}{\rm H}_{1}(U;\mathbb{F}_{p})}{|G:U|}.

Therefore, this implies that in this case
{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)={\rm Hrg}(G)=\rg(G)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on the following two simple lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a profinite group. Let
K
be the closed normal subgroup generated by
g\in G
.
Then
d(K/[K,K])\leq|G:C_{G}(g)K|
, where
C_{G}(g)
denotes the centralizer of
g
in
G
.

Proof.

If
T
is a transversal of
C_{G}(g)K
in
G
, then
K/[K,K]
is generated by
\{g^{t}:t\in T\}
. The assertion follows, since
|T|=|G:C_{G}(g)K|
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
Q
be a finite group of exponent
e
and let
p
be a prime.
The degree of any simple
\mathbb{F}_{p}[Q]
-module is at most
\sqrt{e\cdot|Q|}
.

Proof.

Let
J
be the Jacobson radical of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[Q]
. Then

\mathbb{F}_{p}[Q]/J\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}M_{n_{i}}(F_{i})

where
F_{i}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of degree
e_{i}
. The
i
th factor corresponds to a simple
\mathbb{F}_{p}[Q]
-module of degree
n_{i}e_{i}
(and all simple
\mathbb{F}_{p}[Q]
-modules are obtained in this way). We have
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}e_{i}\leq|Q|
. Adjoining to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
all the
e
th roots of unity gives a field
F
. It a splitting field for
Q
: all fields
F_{i}
are subfields of
F
. Hence
e_{i}\leq e
for all
i
and thus

(n_{i}e_{i})^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}e_{i}e\leq e\cdot|Q|.\qed

Let
C\geq 1
. We say that a natural number
n
is a
C
-almost prime if for every factorization
n=kl
, either
k\leq C
or
l\leq C
. It is not difficult to see that if
n>1
is a
C
-almost prime, then there exists a prime
p
dividing
n
such that
\frac{n}{p}\leq C^{2}
(write
n=kl
with
k<l
and
k
maximal, then every prime divisor of
l
has this property).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We assume for a contradiction that
{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)>0
. Since
\Gamma
is just infinite and dense in
G
, every open normal subgroup
N
of
G
contains an open normal subgroup
K
normally generated by a single element. By Lemma
3.1
,
d(K/[K,K])\leq|G:K|
. Hence
{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)\leq 1
. Let
\alpha=\frac{2}{{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)}
.

From the definition of homology rank gradient, it follows that there exists an open normal subgroup
U
of
G
such that

(2)

|G:U|\geq\frac{4\alpha}{3}\textrm{\ and\ }\frac{d(N/[N,N])}{|G:N|}\geq\frac{3}{4}{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)=\frac{3}{2\alpha}

for every open normal subgroup
N
of
G
contained in
U
and therefore

d(N/[N,N])\geq\frac{3|G:N|}{2\alpha}\geq 2.

Step 1:
Without loss of generality we can assume that
U
is prosoluble.

Since for every open normal subgroup
N
of

contained in

,

, the maximal prosoluble quotient
U/S
of

is infinite. The subgroup

is characteristic in

, and so it is normal in

. Since
\Gamma
is just infinite, it intersects

. Moreover, since the profinite group

{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G/S)\geq\frac{3}{4}{\rm Hrg^{\unlhd}}(G)>0
. Thus, without loss of generality we will assume that

and so

is prosoluble.

Step 2:
For every non-trivial element
g\in U\cap\Gamma
the order
o(g)
of

is
\alpha
-almost prime.

Assume that
o(g)
is infinite or is not
\alpha
-almost prime. Then there exists a finite quotient

of

such that the order

of the image
\overline{g}
of

in

is not

-almost prime. Let

.

The group

of

is of prime order (say

divides

or

, and so,

.

Consider the normal closed subgroup

of

. Since

is just infinite, the subgroup

is open in

. Moreover

, and so the order of the image of

in

is
\frac{o(\overline{g})}{p}
. Since

, Lemma

. This gives a contradiction and hence

is
\alpha
-almost prime.

Step 3:
Let
P
be a finite set of primes. Then the maximal pro-

quotient of

is finite.

Denote by
\overline{G}
the maximal pro-

and assume that it is infinite. Since

as a dense subgroup of

. In particular, all elements of

are

should also be

Step 4:
Let

. There is an open normal subgroup

contained in

.

By Step 2,

is a torsion group. Therefore, it follows from Step 3, that there is a prime

.
Let

Step 5:
If

is contained in

, then the exponent of

is
\alpha
-almost prime.

is dense in

There is an open normal subgroup with small homology rank.

Let

4.
Appendix: new proofs of Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2

Our proofs of Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
are based on the observation that for a finitely generated group
\Gamma
,
\rg(\Gamma_{\hat{p}})
and
\beta_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})
can be expressed in terms of ranks of the module
I_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]}
. Therefore, we will first present the general theory of Sylvester module rank functions main results about them.

4.1.
Sylvester rank functions

In this paper all rings are unitary and
R
-modules are left
R
-modules.
A
Sylvester module rank function

\dim
on a ring
R
is a function that assigns a non-negative real number to each finitely presented
R
-module and satisfies the following conditions.

(SMod1)

\dim\{0\}=0
,
\dim R=1
;

(SMod2)

\dim(M_{1}\oplus M_{2})=\dim M_{1}+\dim M_{2}
;

(SMod3)

if
M_{1}\to M_{2}\to M_{3}\to 0
is exact then

\dim M_{1}+\dim M_{3}\geq\dim M_{2}\geq\dim M_{3}.

The space of Sylvester module rank functions on
R
is denoted by
\mathbb{P}(R)
. If
\dim
,
\dim_{i}\in\mathbb{P}(R)

(i\in\mathbb{N})
are Sylvester module rank functions then we write

\dim=\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{i}\text{in the space }\mathbb{P}(R)

if for every finitely presented
R
-module
M
,
\dim M=\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{i}M
.

For two Sylvester module rank functions
\dim_{1}
and
\dim_{2}
on
R
we write
\dim_{1}\leq\dim_{2}
if for every finitely presented

R
-module
M
,
\dim_{1}M\leq\dim_{2}M
.

One can extend Sylvester module rank functions
\dim
on
R
to an arbitrary
finitely generated
R
-module
M
in the following natural way:

(4)

\dim M=\inf\{\dim\widetilde{M}:\ \widetilde{M}\textrm{\ is finitely presented and\ }M\textrm{\ is a quotient of\ }\widetilde{M}\}.

It is easy to verify that the properties (SMod2) and (SMod3) still hold true. Li
[
Li21
]
constructed an extension of
\dim
to all
R
-modules, but we will not use it in this paper.

One should be cautious. The existence of the limit
\dim=\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{i}
in
\mathbb{P}(R)
does not imply that
\dim M=\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{i}M
for any finitely generated
R
-module
M
.
If
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module, we always have that

(5)

\dim M\geq\limsup_{i\to\infty}\dim_{i}M.

However in
[
JZ24
]
, the following result was proved.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
\dim
,
\dim_{i}\in\mathbb{P}(R)

(i\in\mathbb{N})
be Sylvester module rank functions on
R
. Assume that

\dim M=\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{i}M.

Example 4.2
.

Here are some examples of Sylvester module rank functions that will appear in this paper.

(a)

Let
\Gamma
be a group and
K
a field. Then for any normal subgroup
N
of finite index in
\Gamma
and any finitely presented
K[\Gamma]
-module
M
, we can define

\dim_{K[\Gamma/N]}M=\frac{\dim_{K}K\otimes_{K[N]}M}{|\Gamma:N|}.

(b)

Let
f:R\to S
be a homomorphism of rings and
\dim\in\mathbb{P}(S)
. Then we can define the induced Sylvester module rank function
\dim^{f}\in\mathbb{P}(R)
: for every finitely presented
R
-module
M
,
\dim^{f}M=\dim S\otimes_{R}M
. Normally
f
will be clear from the context, and therefore we will write
\dim
instead of
\dim^{f}
. For example, in this way the function

\dim_{K[\Gamma/N]}
from part (a) induces the corresponding Sylvester module rank function on
K[\Gamma]
.

(c)

Let
\Gamma
be a residually finite group,
K
a field of characteristic 0 and
\Gamma>N_{1}>N_{2}>\ldots
a residual chain of normal subgroups of
finite index. Then by Lück’s approximation (
[
Lüc94
, Theorem 0.1]
,
[
JZ19
, Theorem 1.3]
) there exists
\lim_{i}\dim_{K[\Gamma/N_{i}]}\in\mathbb{P}(K[\Gamma])
, which is independent on the chain. We denote this limit by
\dim_{K[\Gamma]}
. When
K
is a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
, then
\dim_{K[\Gamma]}
is called the von Neumann dimension of
K[\Gamma]
-modules.

(d)

In (c), we have defined the von Neumann dimension
\dim_{K[\Gamma]}
(
K
is a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
) only for residually finite groups, but it can be defined more generally (see, for example,
[
Lüc02
, Chapter 1]
). This is done by considering the von Neumann group algebra
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
, which possesses a canonical dimension function
\dim_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma))
. Then the Sylvester function
\dim_{K[\Gamma]}
is induced by the embedding
K[\Gamma]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group. We will now introduce another Sylvester module rank function
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}
. If
U
is a normal open subgroup of
G
, then as in the example (a), we can define
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U]}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]])
.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
BLLS14
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
G
be a pro-

U_{1}\leq U_{2}
two normal open subgroup of

. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{1}]}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{2}]}
viewed as elements of
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]])

Let
G>U_{1}>U_{2}>\ldots
be a residual chain of normal open subgroups of

, there exists
\lim_{i}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{i}]}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]])

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}M=\inf_{U\lhd_{o}G}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U]}M.

Combining Lemma

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group and

a residual chain of normal open subgroups of

. Let

be a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]

(1)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}M=\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{i}]}M
and

(2)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}M\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G/N]]}M
, where

and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G/N]]}
represent elements of

Given a finitely generated group
\Gamma
, let

.
The first

In view of Example

Let
\Gamma_{\hat{p}}

4.2.
Gradient of pro-
p
completion and the first mod-
p

L^{2}
-Betti number

For a pro-
p
group
G
, let
I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}
denote the augmentation ideal of the completed group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]
. The following lemma express the rank gardient of a pro-
p
group in terms of a rank of the module
I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group.
Then

\rg(G)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}-1+\frac{1}{|G|}.

In particular, if
\Gamma
is a finitely generated residually-
p
group,
then
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{F}_{p})=\operatorname{rg}(\Gamma_{\hat{p}})
.

Proof.

The case where
G
is finite is clear. Assume
G
is infinite. Let
G>U_{1}>U_{2}>\ldots
be a residual chain of normal open subgroups of
G
.
Consider the exact sequence

0\to I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\to 0.

After applying
\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[U_{i}]]}
we obtain the exact sequence

0\to{\rm H}_{1}(U_{i};\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[U_{i}]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{i}]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\to 0.

Therefore we obtain

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{i}]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}-1=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}{\rm H}_{1}(U_{i};\mathbb{F}_{p})}{|G:U_{i}|}-\frac{1}{|G:U_{i}|}.

Hence, by Proposition
4.4
,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}-1=\rg(G).

Now, let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually-
p
group. We put
G=\Gamma_{\hat{p}}
and let
G>U_{1}>U_{2}>\ldots
be a residual chain of normal open subgroups of
G
. Then as before we have that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{i}]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\Gamma]}-1=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}{\rm H}_{1}(U_{i};\mathbb{F}_{p})}{|G:U_{i}|}-\frac{1}{|G:U_{i}|},

and so
b_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma,\mathbb{F}_{p})=\operatorname{rg}(G)
.
∎

The following lemma shows that we can control
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]/\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]](g-1)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G
be a countably based pro-
p
group and
g\in G
of order
p^{k}
. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]/\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]](g-1)=\frac{1}{p^{k}}.

Proof.

Let
G>U_{1}>U_{2}>\ldots
be a residual chain of normal open subgroups of
G
. Without loss of generality we can assume that the order of the image of
g
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{1}]
(and so in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{i}]
for all
i
) is
p^{k}
. Then we obtain that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]/\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]](g-1)=\\
\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U_{i}]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]/\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]](g-1)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{|G:\langle g\rangle U_{i}|}{|G:U_{i}|}\\
\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\langle g\rangle U_{i}:U_{i}|}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{k}}=\frac{1}{p^{k}}.\qed

Now we are ready to prove Propostion
2.1
.

Proof of Propostion
2.1
.

If the order of
g
is less than
p^{k}
, then
G=\overline{G}
, and so the claim of the theorem follows. If
p^{k}
divides the order of
g
(the order of an element of infinite order in a pro-
p
group is
p^{\infty}
), then there exists a finite quotient
P
of
G
, such that the order of the image of
g
in
P
is exactly
p^{k}
. Hence
P
is also a quotient of
\overline{G}
, and so
the order of
\overline{g}
, the image of
g
in
\overline{G}
, is exactly
p^{k}
.

Let
\widehat{\otimes}
denote the profinite tensor product
[
RZ10
, Chapter 5]
. Consider the exact sequence

0\to I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\to 0.

After applying
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}

0\to{\rm}H_{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]])\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\to 0.

By Shapiro’s Lemma
[

, Theorem 6.10.8]

H_{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]])\cong H_{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong N/\overline{[N,N]N^{p}}.

Since
N
is the normal closed subgroup generated by
g^{p^{k}}
,
the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]
-module
N/\overline{[N,N]N^{p}}
is a quotient of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]/\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]](\overline{g}-1)
. Taking into account that
\im\alpha=I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]}
, we obtain an exact sequence

\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]/\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]](\overline{g}-1)\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\to I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]}\to 0.

Let
G>U_{1}>U_{2}>\ldots
be a residual chain of normal open subgroups of

. By Proposition
4.4
,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop. \ref{modpfg} (1)}}}{{=}}\\
\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/NU_{i}]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/NU_{i}]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]})=\\
\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/NU_{i}]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/NU_{i}]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop. \ref{modpfg} (1)}}}{{=}}\\
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop. \ref{modpfg} (2)}}}{{\geqslant}}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[G]]}

On the other hand, by Lemma

,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]]/\mathbb{F}_{p}[[\overline{G}]](\overline{g}-1)=\frac{1}{p^{k}}.

Hence, from (SMod3), we obtain that

Corollary 4.7

[

Let

It is clear that
\Gamma
is a

\rg(\Gamma_{\hat{p}})\geq d-1-\varepsilon

In view of (
1

By the formula (

Flat Standing Sphere Blow-up Solutions for the Nonlinear Heat Equation


Abstract
In this paper, we prove the existence of a singular standing sphere blow-up solution for the nonlinear heat equation with radial symmetry. This solution develops a finite-time singularity on a fixed-radius sphere and exhibits a flat blow-up profile. Our construction refines the method developed by Merle and Zaag [ MZ24 ] which reduces the infinite-dimensional dynamics to a finite-dimensional problem in radial case. The solution satisfies explicit asymptotics near the singular ring and remains regular elsewhere.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following nonlinear heat equation,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}U&=&\Delta U+|U|^{p-1}U,\\
U(.,0)&=&U_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\end{array}\right.

where
U(t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\to U(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
,
d\geq 2
and
p>1
with
(d-2)p\leq d+2
.

Equation (
1.1
)
is considered as a model for many physical situations such as heat transfer, combustion theory, thermal explosion, etc. (see more in Kapila
[
Kap80
]
, Kassoy and Poland
[
KP80
]
, Bebernes and Eberly
[
BE89
]
).

The local Cauchy problem for equation (
1.1
) can be solved within
{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Furthermore, it can be shown that the solution
U(t)
exists either in the interval
[0,+\infty)
or within
[0,T)
where
T<+\infty
. In the latter case,
U
undergoes a finite-time blow-up. This indicates that there exists a sequence
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(x_{0},T)
while
n\to\infty
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}|U(x_{n},t_{n})|=\infty.

We then call, in this case,
x_{0}
the blow-up point and
T
the blow-up time.

While the nonlinear heat equation has been widely studied over the past six decades, many aspects of its blow-up dynamics, particularly those involving non-trivial geometries, remain open. Here, we focus on constructing solutions exhibiting finite-time blow-up along a spherical blow-up set, with a prescribed flat asymptotic profile. Consequently, our references will be limited to prior work within this scope.
Interested readers may refer to Quitener and Souplet
[
QS07
]
for a comprehensive sight of research on equation (
1.1
).

In the Sobolev subcritical case (
(d-2)p<d+2
), pioneering works by Giga and Kohn
[
GK85
]
and Giga, Matsui, Sasayama
[
GMS04
]
yielded the first insight into the asymptotics of blow-up. Following
[
GK85
]
(
[
GMS04
]
), we introduce the following similarity variables:

W_{x_{0}}(Y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}U(x,t)\text{ where }Y=\frac{x-x_{0}}{\sqrt{T-t}}\text{ and }s=-\log(T-t).

They established that, up to changing
U
by
-U
,
for each
K>0
, the following holds on a compact set
\{|Y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}\}
:

(1.2)

W_{x_{0}}(Y,s)\rightarrow\kappa,\mbox{as $s\to\infty$ with $\kappa=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}$.}

For the simplicity of the exposition, we consider the two dimensional case.
In these coordiantes, Velázquez
[
Vel93
]
(see also Filippas and Kohn
[
FK92
]
or Filippas and Liu
[
FL93
]
) observed that unless
W_{x_{0}}\equiv\kappa
, convergence (
1.2
) can be refined as following:

(1.3)

W_{x_{0}}(Y,s)-\kappa\sim Q_{x_{0}}(Y,s)\text{ as }s\rightarrow\infty

uniformly on a compact set, where
Q_{x_{0}}(Y,s)
called “blow-up” profile,
decays in time and captures the correction of the convergence to constant
\kappa
.

Then, two typical forms of profiles
Q_{x_{0}}
arise:

1.

“
\frac{1}{s}
” profile:

(1.4)

Q_{x_{0}}(Y,s)=-\frac{\kappa}{4ps}\sum_{i=1}^{l}h_{2}\left(Y_{i}\right),

where
l=1
or
2
, up to some rotation of coordinates.

2.

“exponential
s
” profile:

(1.5)

Q_{x_{0}}(Y,s)=-e^{-\left(\frac{m}{2}-1\right)s}\sum_{j=0}^{m}C_{m,j}h_{m-j}\left(Y_{1}\right)h_{j}\left(Y_{2}\right)

as
s\rightarrow\infty
, for some even integer
m=m(a)\geq 4
, where
Y=\left(Y_{1},Y_{2}\right),h_{j}(\xi)
is the rescaled Hermite polynomial defined by

h_{j}(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{[j/2]}\frac{j!}{i!(j-2i)!}(-1)^{i}\xi^{j-2i}

The existence of the solutions to (
1.1
) obeying (
1.4
) or (
1.5
) is widely studied.
In the one-dimensional case, Bricmont and Kupiainen
[
BK88
]
constructed a solution for (
1.1
) that behaves like (
1.4
) and (
1.5
). Recently, Duong et al.
[
DNZ23b
]
revisited the construction of the
flat
profile using modulation theory.

In the higher dimensional case, one may refer to Nguyen and Zaag
[
NZ18
]
, for constructed solutions showing a refinement of behavior (
1.4
) in 2 dimensions with
l=2
. In the supercritical case, we have an example of a single-point blow-up with a degenerate profile given by Merle, Raphäel, and Szeftel
[
MRS20
]
. More recently, Merle and Zaag
[
MZ24
]
have provided an example of a blow-up solution with a novel cross-shaped blow-up profile.

The methods used in
[
BK88
]
were enhanced after by Merle and Zaag
[
MZ97
]
using a more geometrical approach. Generally speaking, regarding the linearized equation around the profile, Merle and Zaag cope with this problem in two steps:

1.

Reduce the problem into a finite-dimensional one.

2.

Solve the finite-dimensional problem with a topological shooting argument.

This powerful method is then applied to many other different fields. Interested readers are invited to see
[
MZ08
]

and
[
DNZ23a
]
for an application in the complex Ginzburg-Landau equation and a more direct way to accomplish the first step in
[
MZ97
]
. Besides, Dávila, Del Pino, and Wei
[
DDPW20
]
applied this method to deal with the formation of the singularities for harmonic map flow.

Let us now consider the analysis of blow-up behaviors involving non-trivial geometries. We would like to mention Mahmoudi, Nouaili and Zaag
[
MNZ16
]
, for the existence of a blow-up solution to the nonlinear heat equation on a circle; Raphaël
[
Rap06
]
, for the existence and stability of solution blowing up on a sphere for the
L^{2}
-supercritical nonlinear Schrödinger equation and Raphaël and Szeftel
[
RS09
]
for a analogous result extended to higher dimensions. We also cite the work of Collot et al.
[
CGMN23
]
where the authors proved the existence and stability of a collapsing-ring blow-up solution to the Keller-Segel system in 3 dimensions and higher. Recently in
[
DNZ24
]
, we constructed a standing sphere blow-up solution of generic type for (
1.1
)

In this work, our main contribution is to prove the existence of a radially symmetric solution to (
1.1
) that develops finite-time blow-up on a fixed-radius sphere, with a flat asymptotic profile of the following type:

U(x,t)\sim(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}f\left(\frac{|x|-r_{0}}{(T-t)^{1/4}}\right),

where

f(z)=\left(p-1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{\kappa}z^{4}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Our main result is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Existence of a singular standing solution for equation (
1.1
) with prescribed profile).
For any
r_{0}>0
, there exists
T>0
such that equation (
1.1
) admits a radially symmetric solution
U(x,t)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T)
, satisfying:

1.

The solution
U
blows up in finite time
T
on the sphere of radius
r_{0}
.

2.

For every
C>0
,

(1.6)

\sup_{\Lambda_{C}}\left|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}U(x,t)-f\left(\frac{|x|-r_{0}}{(T-t)^{\frac{1}{4}}}\right)\right|\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }t\to T,

where

\Lambda_{C}:=\left\{\,||x|-r_{0}|\leq C(T-t)^{\frac{1}{4}}\,\right\},\hskip 20.00003ptf(z)=\left(p-1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{\kappa}z^{4}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

3.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|\neq r_{0}
, we have
U(x,t)\to U(x,T)
as
t\to T
, where near the singular sphere the limiting profile satisfies

(1.7)

U(x,T)\sim u^{*}(|x|-r_{0}),\hskip 20.00003ptu^{*}(\xi)=\left(\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{\kappa}\xi^{4}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},\hskip 10.00002pt\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that
f
in (
1.6
) can be written more generally as

\left(p-1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{\kappa}z^{m}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
with
m=2k
, and
k\geq 2
. In this paper, for simplicity of the exposition, we only focus on the case where
k=2
but it can be also generalized to
k>2
.

2
Formulation of the problem

To prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to construct a solution blowing up on a sphere of radius
r_{0}=1
, since the general case of
r_{0}>0
follows by the scaling invariance of the equation:

\lambda\mapsto U_{\lambda}(\xi,\tau)\equiv\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(\lambda\xi,\lambda^{2}\tau).

Our goal is to construct a solution that blows up on the sphere
\{|x|=1\}
, with asymptotic behavior described by (
1.6
). In what follows, we provide a detailed description of this challenge and outline our strategy for overcoming it.

2.1
Strategy for bounding the “radial singularity”

We consider radially symmetric solutions and define for all
t\geq 0
and
r\geq 0
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle u(r,t)

\displaystyle=U\left((r,0,\cdots,0),t\right),

so that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\hskip 10.00002ptU(x,t)

\displaystyle=u(|x|,t).

Substituting in (
1.1
), the equation becomes a 1-dimensional one:

(2.3)

\partial_{t}u=\partial^{2}_{r}u+\frac{d-1}{r}\partial_{r}u+|u|^{p-1}u,

which introduces a singularity at
r=0
due to the
\frac{1}{r}
term. As a matter of fact, by “radial singularity”, we mean the term
\frac{d-1}{r}\partial_{r}u
. In order to handle this term, we divide the space into two regions:

•

Inner region: Near the origin, where the solution remains regular

•

Outer region: Away from the origin, where blow-up occurs.

This approach is inspired by
[
MNZ16
]
and
[
DNZ24
]
, although we must carefully avoid introducing additional singularities that may complicate the global analysis.

Indeed, we introduce two sets of similarity variables corresponding to the different aims. Let
T>0

be the expected blow-up time. In the outer region near
\{r=1\}
,
we define:

(2.4)

w_{a}(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(r,t)\mbox{ with }y=\frac{r-a}{\sqrt{T-t}},\;s=-\log(T-t),

where
a\geq 0
,
s\geq-\log T
and
y\geq-ae^{s/2}
. In the full coordinates, self-similar variables are given by:

(2.5)

W_{x_{0}}(Y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}U(x,t),\;Y=\frac{x-x_{0}}{\sqrt{T-t}}\;,s=-\log(T-t),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
s\geq-\log T
and
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The first transformation is tailored to constructing the blow-up solutions near the sphere, while the second is used to analyze the behavior globally, especially to prove the
L^{\infty}
priori bound

(2.6)

\|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}U\|\leq M.

We will separate the origin and the singular standing sphere by introducing the following smooth nonnegative cut-off functions:

(2.7)

\chi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0&&0\leq\xi\leq\frac{1}{8},\\
&&\\
1&&\xi\geq\frac{1}{4},\\
\end{array}\right.

and

(2.8)

\overline{\chi}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}1&&0\leq\xi\leq\frac{3}{8},\\
&&\\
0&&\xi\geq\frac{3}{4}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then we get two different region

•

The inner region
:
\left\{|x|\leq\frac{3}{8}\varepsilon_{0}\right\}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}
to be fixed small enough later. Note that we expect no blowup in this region. In order to study this inner region, we define
\overline{U}(x,t)=\overline{\chi}\left(\frac{|x|}{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)U(x,t)
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
U(x,t)
is assumed to satisfy the following:

\partial_{t}U=\Delta U+|U|^{p-1}U.

Then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\overline{U}
satisfies the following equation:

(2.9)

\partial_{t}\overline{U}=\Delta\overline{U}+|U|^{p-1}\overline{U}-2\nabla\overline{\chi}\nabla U-\Delta\overline{\chi}U.

The function
\overline{U}
will be controlled using classical parabolic estimates.

•

The outer region

\left\{|x|\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4}\right\}
: In this region, we consider the equation in radial coordinates given by (
2.3
).
Then,
u
introduced in (
2.1
) satisfies the following equation:
\forall t\in[0,T)
,
\forall r\geq 0

(2.10)

\partial_{t}u=\partial^{2}_{r}u+\frac{d-1}{r}\partial_{r}u+|u|^{p-1}u.

Using the self-similar transformation (
2.4
) (with
a=1
), we get from (
2.10
) that
w_{1}
satisfies:

(2.11)

\partial_{s}w_{1}=\partial^{2}_{y}w_{1}-\frac{1}{2}y\partial_{y}w_{1}+e^{-s/2}\frac{d-1}{ye^{-s/2}+1}\partial_{y}w_{1}-\frac{w_{1}(y,s)}{p-1}+|w_{1}|^{p-1}w_{1},

where
y\in[-e^{s/2},+\infty)
and
s\in[-\log T,+\infty)
.

As we should restrict ourselves to the outer region
\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}\left|y\geq e^{s/2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4}-1\right)\right.\right\}
in this part, we introduce for all
s\geq 0
and
y\geq-e^{\frac{s}{2}}
:

(2.12)

\tilde{w}=w_{1}\cdot\chi(\frac{ye^{-s/2}+1}{\varepsilon_{0}}),

where
\chi
is defined by (

). Then from (

),
\tilde{w}
satisfies for all

and

(2.13)

\partial_{s}\tilde{w}=\partial^{2}_{y}\tilde{w}-\frac{1}{2}y\partial_{y}\tilde{w}-\frac{1}{p-1}\tilde{w}+|\tilde{w}|^{p-1}\tilde{w}+e^{-s/2}\frac{d-1}{ye^{-s/2}+1}\partial_{y}\tilde{w}+F(y,s),

where

is defined as follows:

(2.14)

\begin{split}F(y,s)&=w_{1}\partial_{s}\chi-2\partial_{y}\chi\partial_{y}w_{1}-w_{1}\partial_{y}^{2}\chi\\
&+\frac{1}{2}yw_{1}\partial_{y}\chi-\frac{d-1}{y+e^{s/2}}w_{1}\partial_{y}\chi+|w_{1}|^{p-1}w_{1}(\chi-\chi^{p}).\end{split}

Extending

and

\tilde{w}=F(y,s)=0
, for all

and

, we see that
\tilde{w}
is a solution of the following modification to (
2.13
), for all
s\geq 0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
:

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\tilde{w}=

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{y}\tilde{w}-\frac{1}{2}y\partial_{y}\tilde{w}-\frac{1}{p-1}\tilde{w}+|\tilde{w}|^{p-1}\tilde{w}

\displaystyle+e^{-s/2}\frac{d-1}{ye^{-s/2}+1}\chi\left(\frac{ye^{-s/2}+1}{2\varepsilon_{0}}\right)\partial_{y}\tilde{w}+F(y,s),

To see that, we recall by definition (
2.7
) that

in the transition region

) type satisfied by

\tilde{w}
obeying:

If
|y|\leq C

3.1
Proof of the apriori bound (
2.6
)

As we explained before, after linearizing the equation (
2.13
), the classical approach consists of two steps (see for example
[
MZ97
]
):

1.

Reduce the problem into a finite-dimensional one.

2.

Solve the finite-dimensional problem with a topological argument.

In fact, these two steps are easily carried out if we have the following apriori bound

\forall t\in[0,T),\;\;\|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}U(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq M.

To derive this bound, there is a interesting method in Section 5 of
[
MZ24
]
that we prefer to use in the present work. In fact, as proved in Section 5 of
[
MZ24
]
, by definition (
2.5
), it is equivalent to show that

\forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\;|W_{x_{0}}(0,s)|\leq M.

However, for this particular method in
[
MZ24
]
, it is more convenient to use
W_{x_{0}}(Y,s)
rather than
w_{r_{0}}(y,s)
. Therefore, assuming
|x_{0}|=r_{0}
, the following is needed :

W_{x_{0}}(Y,s)=w_{r_{0}}((|Ye^{-s/2}+x_{0}|-r_{0})e^{s/2},s).

(this directly follows by definition (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), as we will show below in Claim

, with

).
Note that function
(|Y+x_{0}|e^{-s/2}-1)e^{s/2}
is not

. It brings us more difficulties in derive a bound on
W_{x_{0}}(Y,s)=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}U(x,t)
.

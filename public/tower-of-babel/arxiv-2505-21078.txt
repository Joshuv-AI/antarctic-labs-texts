Geometric results for hyperbolic operators with spectral transition of the Hamilton map
Enrico Bernardi, Tatsuo Nishitani

Abstract
In this paper we study a class of non-effectively hyperbolic operators vanishing of order 2 2 on a manifold, on a sub-region of which the spectral structure of the Hamilton map changes type. Suitable normal symplectic coordinates are found together with an analysis of the Hamilton system associated to the principal symbol and a factorization result, preparing the operator for a microlocal energy estimate, is finally proven.



Key words and phrases: Cauchy problem, Hamilton map and flow, Transition case, Non-effectively hyperbolic operator .

AMS 2020 classification:Primary: 35L15; Secondary: 35B30. .

Introduction

Two seminal papers in the 1970s (
[
8
]
), (
[
5
]
) devoted to the analysis of
the Cauchy problem for linear hyperbolic operators with multiple
characteristics have been instrumental in highlighting the fundamental
features that one needs to study when one wants to prove
well-posedness results, or for that matter, propagation of
singularities results, for those kind of equations.
Noting that in what follows the multiplicities of the characteristics
roots of the operator will
be at most
2
, we recall that
these properties are related to the symplectic
geometry of the manifold of double points
\Sigma
, to the spectral nature
of the fundamental matrix (or Hamilton map), attached to the operator at those double
points and to the behavior of the lower order terms.

More precisely let

(1.1)

P(x,D)=-D_{0}^{2}+A_{1}(x,D^{\prime})D_{0}+A_{2}(x,D^{\prime})

be a differential operator of order

in
D_{0}
with

operators of order

\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

and the

. We have
P(x_{0},x^{\prime},\xi_{0},\xi^{\prime})=p(x,\xi)+P_{1}(x,\xi)+P_{0}(x,\xi)
with
p
the principal
symbol of
P(x,D)
and
P_{j}
,
j=1,0
the symbols of the lower
order terms. We will assume throughout the paper that the symbol
p

is hyperbolic w.r.t the
(x=0,\xi=e_{0})
direction, that

(1.2)

p
vanishes exactly to second order on
\Sigma
.

and that

(1.3)

{\rm rank\,}\sigma\big|_{\Sigma}={\rm constant}

where
\sigma=\sum_{j=0}^{n}d\xi_{j}\wedge dx_{j}
is the symplectic
2
-form.

Then it is well known that the fundamental matrix
F_{p}(\rho)=dH_{p}(\rho)
at
\rho\in\Sigma
can either have two real
eigenvalues, a case known as effectively hyperbolic, completely solved
and understood, see e.g. (
[
14
]
) for a very recent review and
simplification of the proofs in that case, or else its
spectrum is purely imaginary, i.e.
{\rm Spec}F_{p}(\rho)\subset i\mathbb{R}
,

\rho\in\Sigma
. When this occurs it is also known that only two possible sub-cases may happen,
see below Lemma (
2.1
).

When the type of either one of these two possible sub-cases is fixed on
\Sigma
, then essentially our
knowledge is almost complete: a very thorough and exhaustive review of the state of the art
can be found, together with some still open questions, in
(
[
12
]
) to which we also refer for some of the notation used in
the following.
However in (
[
2
]
) it was noted that, beyond the
aforementioned conditions on the spectrum of the fundamental matrix,
another ingredient had to be taken into account when establishing the
well posedness of the Cauchy problem for the class of non-effectively operators, namely the behavior of the
Hamilton flow of the simple bicharacteristic curves with respect to
the manifold of double points. The existence of cases when this flow
may land tangentially onto the double manifold, prevents one from getting
the
C^{\infty}
well posedness results one should expect, even with
the lower order terms satisfying the conditions outlined in (
[
5
]
).
It is indeed possible to show that one cannot prove well posedness beyond a certain
Gevrey threshold, very likely related to the geometry of those curves.

While the picture when the type of spectral behavior of the fundamental
matrix is fixed has been therefore essentially almost fully understood, there have
been a number of more recent results dealing with cases when one allows the nature of the spectrum to vary
along the double manifold, see for instance
(
[
1
]
),(
[
3
]
),(
[
4
]
) and especially
(
[
10
]
),(
[
11
]
),(
[
13
]
) where more
general types of transitions were considered, albeit when
\Sigma
has a low codimension.

Consequently the purpose of this paper is to continue the investigation of these
transition situations in the case of a general
C^{\infty}
double
manifold of any codimension, under the assumption that at every double point the spectrum of
the fundamental matrix is purely imaginary and the transition occurs
where the cardinality of Jordan
blocks in its elementary decomposition varies from
2
to
4

crossing a sub-manifold (see Lemma (
2.1
)). Then we arrange a set-up where the study of
the principal symbol
will yield information on the behavior of the Hamilton flow and on the
possibility of an elementary factorization conducive to weighted
energy estimates in a suitable operator calculus. In particular we improve the
results in (
[
3
]
) to get a very general normal form under
this specific type of transition and we arrange a system of coordinates
where the function giving rise to the transition region is exhibited
explicitly.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section (
2
) we characterize precisely the type of transition under examination
and in Proposition (
2.1
) we exhibit a set of normal
coordinates, where a function
\theta
responsible for the change of
type is found and computed. In Section (
3
) we
further analyze this function
\theta
with an eye toward its future
use in implementing weighted energy estimates, as well as in investigating the behavior of bicharacteristics of the principal symbol.
In Section (
4
) we prove under a number of symplectically invariant
conditions on the coordinate functions found in Section (
2
)
that there exists a bicharacteristic of the principal symbol tangent
to the double manifold. This clearly is important in light of the
aforementioned lack of well-posedness in the
C^{\infty}
category
when the Hamilton flow is attracted to the double manifold.
Finally in Section (
5
) we recover
the general conditions allowing the principal symbol to be factorized
as in
[
7
]
, a preliminary step toward the setting up
of a coherent system of weighted energy estimates.

admits a microlocal elementary factorization at
\rho
.

Of course, elementary factorization is closely related to the classical derivation of energy estimates. To see this note that

\displaystyle-\Lambda M=-(\xi_{0}-\lambda+ic)\#(\xi_{0}-\mu-ic)-i\{\Lambda,M\}/2-ic(\Lambda-M)+S^{0}_{1,0}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\Lambda}\#\tilde{M}-i(\{\Lambda,M\}/2-c(\Lambda-M))+S^{0}_{1,0}

where
c\in S^{0}_{1,0}
and
\tilde{\Lambda}=\xi_{0}-\lambda+ic
,
\tilde{M}=\xi_{0}-\mu-ic
. We also note

\displaystyle 2{\mathsf{Im}}({\rm op}(p)v,{\rm op}(\tilde{\Lambda})v)=\frac{d}{dx_{0}}\big(\|{\rm op}(\tilde{\Lambda})v\|^{2}+({\rm op}(Q)v,v)\big)

\displaystyle-2{\mathsf{Re}}({\rm op}(\tilde{\Lambda})v,{\rm op}(\{\Lambda,M\}/2+c(\Lambda-M))v)-{\mathsf{Re}}({\rm op}(\{\tilde{\Lambda},Q\})v,v)/2

modulo
C(\|{\rm op}(\tilde{\Lambda})v\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2})
. Assuming that one can find
c\in S^{0}_{1,0}
such that
|\{\Lambda,M\}/2+c(\Lambda-M)|\lesssim\sqrt{Q}
and taking
|\{\tilde{\Lambda},Q\}-\{\Lambda,Q\}|\lesssim\sqrt{Q}
into account we will obtain energy estimates.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

If
p
admits a microlocal elementary factorization at
\rho
there is no bicharacteristic tangent to
\Sigma
at
\rho
.

In this section, we restrict ourselves to the case

{\rm Spec}F_{p}(\rho)\subset i\mathbb{R}\;\;\big(\Longleftrightarrow\Pi\lambda_{j}(\rho)\geq 0\Longleftrightarrow\theta|_{\Sigma}\geq 0\big)\quad\rho\in\Sigma.

Applying the extension lemma to
\theta\big|_{\Sigma}
one can assume from the beginning that

p=-(\xi_{0}+\phi_{1})(\xi_{0}-\phi_{1})+\theta\phi_{1}^{2}+\Sigma_{j=2}^{r}\phi_{j}^{2}+\Sigma_{j=r+1}^{d}\phi_{j}^{2}+R

where
R=O(|\phi|^{4}\langle{\xi}\rangle_{\!\gamma}^{-2})=O^{4}(\Sigma^{\prime})
with
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{d})
and that

(5.4)

\theta\geq 0,\quad\{\phi_{j},\theta\}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0,\quad j=1,\ldots,r.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
p
is of normal form up to term
O^{4}(\Sigma^{\prime})
and satisfies
\theta\geq 0
near
\bar{\rho}
. If

\displaystyle|\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\theta\}|\leq C(\sqrt{\theta}+|\phi_{1}|+\sqrt{|\phi^{\prime}|})^{2},

\displaystyle|\{\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\},\phi_{2}\}|\leq C(\sqrt{\theta}+|\phi_{1}|+\sqrt{|\phi^{\prime}|}),\;\;\phi^{\prime}=(\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{d})

holds then
p
admits a microlocal elementary factorization at
\bar{\rho}
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume that
p
is of normal form up to term
O^{4}(\Sigma^{\prime})
and satisfies
\theta\geq 0
near
\bar{\rho}
and
\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\theta\}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0
. If
\{\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\},\phi_{2}\}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0

then
p
admits a microlocal elementary factorization at
\bar{\rho}
while
p
does not if
\{\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\},\phi_{2}\}(\bar{\rho})\neq 0
.

Proof.

From the assumption one can write
\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\theta\}=\Sigma_{k=1}^{d}c_{d}\phi_{k}
.
Note that
\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\{\phi_{2},\theta\}\}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0
and
|\{\theta,\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\}\}|\lesssim\sqrt{\theta}

for
\theta\geq 0
. It follows from Jacobi’s identity we have

|\{\phi_{2},\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\theta\}\}|\lesssim|\phi|+\sqrt{\theta},\quad\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{d}).

This shows that
|\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\theta\}|\lesssim(|\phi|+\sqrt{\theta})|\phi_{1}|+|\phi^{\prime}|
hence the first condition is satisfied. The second condition is obviously satisfied and the assertion follows from Proposition
5.1
. Since
\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\theta\}\}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0
for
\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\theta\}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0
if
\{\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\},\phi_{2}\}(\bar{\rho})\neq 0
there is a bicharacteristic tangent to
\Sigma
at
\bar{\rho}
by Corollary
4.2
hence Lemma
5.1
proves the last assertion.
∎

Let

\Lambda=\phi_{1}+\ell(\phi^{\prime\prime})\phi_{1}-\lambda\phi_{1}^{3}\langle{\xi}\rangle_{\!\gamma}^{-2},\quad\ell(\phi^{\prime\prime})=\Sigma_{j=3}^{r}\beta_{j}\phi_{j},\quad\phi^{\prime\prime}=(\phi_{3},\ldots,\phi_{r})

where
\lambda>0
is a parameter and
\beta_{j}
are smooth which are determined later. Write

p=-(\xi_{0}+\Lambda)(\xi_{0}-\Lambda)+Q

where
Q=\theta\phi_{1}^{2}+\tilde{Q}

with

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}=\Sigma_{j=2}^{d}\phi_{j}^{2}-2\ell\phi_{1}^{2}(1+\ell/2)+2\lambda\phi_{1}^{4}\langle{\xi}\rangle_{\!\gamma}^{-2}(1+\ell-\lambda\phi_{1}^{2}\langle{\xi}\rangle_{\!\gamma}^{-2}/2)+R.

Taking
\lambda>0
large (since we are working in a neighborhood or
\bar{\rho}
it can be assumed that
|\phi|\langle{\xi}\rangle_{\!\gamma}^{-1}

\displaystyle\{\xi_{0}-\Lambda,\tilde{Q}\}=\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},|\phi^{\prime}|^{2}-2\ell(\phi^{\prime\prime})\phi^{2}_{1}(1+\ell(\phi^{\prime\prime})/2)\}

\displaystyle-\{\ell(\phi^{\prime\prime})\phi_{1},|\phi^{\prime}|^{2}\}+O(Q).

Recall that one can write

\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\phi_{j}\}=\Sigma_{k=1}^{d}\alpha_{jk}\phi_{k},\quad 1\leq j\leq d.

Moreover since
\{\phi_{2},\{\xi_{0}-\phi_{1},\phi_{j}\}\}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0
for

which follows from Jacobi’s identity, one has

\alpha_{j1}\overset{\Sigma^{\prime}}{=}0,\quad r+1\leq j\leq d.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\{\xi_{0}-\Lambda,\tilde{Q}\}=2\sum_{j=2}^{d}\phi_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\alpha_{jk}\phi_{k}-2\phi_{1}^{2}\sum_{j=3}^{r}\beta_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\alpha_{jk}\phi_{k}(1+\ell(\phi^{\prime\prime})/2)

\displaystyle-2\phi_{1}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\phi_{j}\sum_{k=3}^{r}\beta_{k}\{\phi_{k},\phi_{j}\}+O(Q).

Note that

\displaystyle\phi_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\alpha_{jk}\phi_{k}=O(Q),\;\;r+1\leq j\leq d,\quad\phi_{1}^{2}\sum_{j=3}^{r}\beta_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\alpha_{jk}\phi_{k}\ell(\phi^{\prime\prime})=O(Q),

\displaystyle\phi_{1}\phi_{j}\sum_{k=3}^{r}\beta_{k}\{\phi_{k},\phi_{j}\}=O(Q),\;\;r+1\leq j\leq d

\displaystyle\{\xi_{0}-\Lambda,\tilde{Q}\}=2\sum_{j=2}^{r}\phi_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\alpha_{jk}\phi_{k}-2\phi_{1}^{2}\sum_{j=3}^{r}\beta_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\alpha_{jk}\phi_{k}

\displaystyle=2\phi_{1}\sum_{j=2}^{r}\alpha_{j1}\phi_{j}-2\phi_{1}^{3}\sum_{j=3}^{r}\beta_{j}\alpha_{j1}-2\phi_{1}\sum_{j=3}^{r}\phi_{j}\sum_{k=3}^{r}\beta_{k}\{\phi_{k},\phi_{j}\}+O(Q)

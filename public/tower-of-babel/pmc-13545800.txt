Impact of a high-profile sudden cardiac death on emergency department utilization: a multi-campus interrupted time series and exploratory phenotyping study
Authors: Chenxi Wang, Han Cai, Xiujuan Hu, Jiayu Liu, Guanhua Li, Li Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHigh-profile public health events can be temporally associated with significant shifts in healthcare-seeking behavior. We aimed to quantify the surge in emergency department (ED) volume and characterize the demographic shifts following a high-profile sudden cardiac death (SCD) event of a nationally prominent public figure.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted across two major hospital cohorts (N = 15,981). We employed interrupted time series (ITS) analysis to estimate the immediate level shift in daily ED visits. An XGBoost model with SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) interpretability was developed as an exploratory phenotyping tool to characterize the surge based on clinical and behavioral features. Findings were confirmed using an internal validation cohort.ResultsFollowing the SCD event, daily ED volume exhibited a significant immediate level shift (Main: +24.4%; Internal Validation: +34.7%,p < 0.001). A subtle but robust demographic shift toward younger populations was observed (Median age decrease: 2.2 years,p < 0.001). Machine learning identified Age, Nighttime Presentation, and Cardiovascular Department attendance as the features most strongly associated with the surge phenotype. Notably, the SHAP dependence analysis revealed a sharp, non-linear escalation in surge probability for individuals aged < 45 years, particularly during nocturnal hours. Despite the volume surge, no significant increase in mortality or acute myocardial infarction diagnoses was observed, indicating that the surge was predominantly associated with low-risk individuals exhibiting reassurance-seeking behavior.ConclusionMedia-driven events can be followed by a structured, large-scale surge in emergency department utilization, predominantly among younger, low-risk individuals seeking reassurance. These demographic and behavioral findings are consistent with the cyberchondria phenomenon, which may produce a dilution of clinical severity without increasing hard endpoints, potentially straining acute care resources. Targeted public health communication and adaptive triage strategies are essential to preserve emergency capacity during periods of heightened media-associated health concerns.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-026-01723-4.

Introduction

In the digital information era, social media has evolved into a dominant force shaping collective human behavior, particularly regarding healthcare-seeking patterns. Publicly shared health-related information can trigger rapid shifts in public perception, often leading to a phenomenon known as “cyberchondria”-an escalation of health-related concerns following online searches. Historically, sudden cardiac death (SCD) events involving high-profile individuals have served as potent catalysts for such surges, frequently resulting in a significant influx of individuals into emergency departments (EDs) seeking reassurance despite being at low clinical risk [1, 2].

On March 24, 2026, the sudden cardiac death of a nationally prominent public figure in China’s education sector was officially announced. The obituary rapidly became a national trending topic across major social media platforms, generating widespread public discussion and health-related searches within hours [3, 4]. Mainstream Chinese media widely reported a sharp increase in emergency department and cardiology outpatient visits across multiple tertiary hospitals nationwide from March 25 onward [5–7]. This pattern is consistent with the well-documented mechanism whereby infodemic-driven health anxiety leads to excessive healthcare-seeking behavior [8–11]. Recent studies have shown that exposure to intense health-related information significantly predicts cyberchondria and subsequent overuse of emergency and specialist services, with younger adults being particularly vulnerable [12, 13]. The temporal concurrence, geographic spread, and demographic specificity of the reported national surge support a potential association between the public health event and the observed increase in our hospital’s emergency department workload.

The instantaneous and widespread dissemination of this news followed by an unprecedented wave of public health-related concerns across the country. While the event was a notable personal and societal tragedy, from an epidemiological perspective, it functioned as a unique natural intervention. It provided a rare, highly synchronized time window that allowed for the systematic observation of how mass media-driven panic can acutely alter healthcare-seeking patterns and strain emergency medical resources in real-time [14, 15].

While previous studies have documented fluctuations in ED volume following media-driven events, several critical knowledge gaps remain. Most existing research relies on descriptive statistics from single centers, which lack the robustness provided by multi- cohort validation. Furthermore, traditional linear models often fail to capture the subtle, non-linear demographic shifts within the presentation population. Crucially, there is a lack of granular exploratory phenotyping to characterize which specific demographic and behavioral features (e.g., age, timing of presentation) are the primary drivers of these information-induced surges [16–19].

This study addresses these gaps by utilizing a comprehensive dual-methodological framework across two major hospital cohorts. We first employed interrupted time series (ITS) analysis to rigorously quantify the immediate level shift in ED volume, stripping away seasonal and temporal trends. Subsequently, we leveraged an interpretable machine learning (XGBoost + SHAP) framework to provide a high-resolution phenotyping of the surge population. This approach allowed us to move beyond simple volume counting to create a quantitative behavioral profile of the “surge population,” specifically examining the non-linear interaction between age and presentation timing [20–22].

The primary objective of this research is to delineate the impact of media-associated health concerns on acute care utilization and to validate these findings across independent clinical settings. By identifying the structured nature of the surge-driven predominantly by younger, internet-active populations during off-hours-our findings provide essential evidence for hospital administrators and public health officials. This study underscores the necessity for targeted public health communication and adaptive triage strategies to preserve emergency medical resources for high-acuity patients during periods of heightened social media-associated concerns [23, 24].

Methods

Study design and setting

This study was conducted across two distinct cohorts of Tianjin Chest Hospital, a high-volume tertiary cardiopulmonary specialized hospital. The emergency services at both cohorts are exclusively structured into two specialized departments: cardiovascular and respiratory medicine. The Haihe cohort served as the main cohort, while the Heping cohort served as the internal validation cohort. Both cohorts operate within a unified health system, sharing the identical Electronic Health Record (EHR) database, unified clinical documentation protocols, and standardized triage pathways. This shared infrastructure ensures strict clerical documentation consistency across both campuses. However, they serve geographically and demographically distinct patient populations. This internal multi- cohort design provided a natural quasi-experimental setting for validation, while minimizing inter-hospital heterogeneity and clinical confounding through consistent clinical practices. Consequently, it enhanced the internal validity of the observed behavioral shifts. To avoid seasonal and holiday confounding, especially the Spring Festival period, we selected a strictly matched 15-day pre-event period (representing the baseline ED utilization uninfluenced by the public health event) and a 15-day post-event exposure period. The two observation windows were calendar-matched with no major holidays included.

Data sources and outcome definitions

Because the extracted variables (e.g., age, gender, department, presentation time, and diagnostic codes) are mandatory fields for closing an electronic emergency encounter in our EHR system, the dataset contained zero missing values. Consequently, no missing data imputation procedures were required for the statistical or machine learning analyses.

Individual-level clinical data (N = 15,981) were collected from daily handover registration forms, covering a 30-day observation period including 15 days prior to and 15 days following the sudden cardiac death (SCD) event. We defined each “shift day” from 08:00 to 08:00 the following day to align with clinical duty cycles, further stratifying presentations into day, evening, and night (20:00–08:00) shifts. The primary outcomes were total and cardiovascular department (CD) daily volumes. Secondary clinical endpoints included confirmed Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS), all-cause mortality, and critical interventions (e.g., intubation, thrombolysis, or aortic dissection). An exploratory endpoint, ‘event-followed by symptom-matching,’ was defined as a CD encounter where clinical assessment ruled out primary cardiac pathology, potentially representing individuals seeking professional reassurance after self-referencing publicized SCD-related symptoms. This specific phenotype was retrospectively labeled and extracted based on standardized chief complaints (e.g., chest tightness, palpitations) and final diagnostic ICD codes recorded by the attending physicians.

Segmented regression models were utilized to quantify immediate level shifts and trend changes, with Newey-West standard errors used to adjust for autocorrelation. To verify the stability of the estimated level shifts, sensitivity analyses were performed by varying the event onset window (±3 days) and excluding weekend data to ensure that the observed surge was not associated with data entry artifacts or rhythmic weekly fluctuations. To delineate the underlying demographic and behavioral drivers of the surge, an extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) framework was employed. Given the inherent stochasticity of media-driven healthcare-seeking behavior, the XGBoost algorithm was utilized primarily as a population phenotyping tool rather than a traditional individual-level prediction model. We integrated the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) framework to evaluate global feature importance and to capture non-linear interactions, specifically between patient age and nighttime presentation.

The XGBoost model functioned purely as a non-parametric explanatory tool to extract global SHAP values and decompose the variance between the pre- and post-event cohorts, rather than as a predictive clinical classifier. Consequently, traditional train-test data splitting was deliberately omitted prior to the SHAP analysis. Because the observation window symmetrically encapsulated 15 days pre-event and 15 days post-event, the temporal target classes were naturally balanced; thus, synthetic minority oversampling (e.g., SMOTE) was not required. To ensure absolute reproducibility, a fixed random state (seed = 2026) was strictly enforced. To ensure the robustness of the identified behavioral drivers, we performed a multi-dimensional validation, including an inter-cohort feature consistency analysis and the use of the respiratory department as an internal control group. Details of the model configuration, including specific hyperparameter settings (n_estimators = 100, max_depth = 3, learning_rate = 0.1) and technical specifications, are provided in Supplementary Table S1. “All analyses were conducted using Python (v3.10) and R (v4.2.0), with a two-sided p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and demographic shifts

A total of 15,981 emergency department (ED) presentations were analyzed, including 11,374 in the main cohort and 4,607 in the internal validation cohort. Following the media-driven event on March 24, 2026, both cohorts experienced a highly significant surge in daily ED volume (Main: 338.0 vs. 420.3 visits/day; Validation: 130.9 vs. 176.3 visits/day; both p < 0.001).

The demographic profile of the ED population underwent a subtle but statistically robust structural transformation (Table 1). This “shift toward younger age groups” was primarily associated with a proportional increase in young adults (aged < 45 years), who rose from 19.3% to 24.4% in the main cohort and from 15.0% to 20.1% in the internal validation cohort. Presentation behaviors also exhibited distinct alterations. The internal validation cohort demonstrated a significant shift in the time of presentation (p = 0.002), characterized by an increased proportion of nighttime visits (19.6% to 23.8%; OR = 1.28 [95% CI: 1.11–1.48]). Analysis of departmental-specific patterns revealed that the overall surge was almost entirely associated with an escalation in cardiovascular presentations. In the main cohort, the daily cardiovascular ED volume rose significantly from 286.1 ± 22.9 to 363.9 ± 29.6 visits per day (p < 0.001; Cohen’s d = 2.94), whereas respiratory department volume remained statistically stable (51.9 ± 6.2 vs. 56.4 ± 7.7, p = 0.091; Cohen’s d = 0.64). This specific expansion in cardiac-related seeking behavior was also replicated in the internal validation cohort, where cardiovascular daily volume increased by 36.3% (108.5 ± 17.5 vs. 147.9 ± 24.6, p < 0.001; Cohen’s d = 1.85), far outstripping the increase in respiratory visits (p = 0.002; Cohen’s d = 1.28). Notably, although the respiratory department in the internal validation cohort showed a significant p-value and a large effect size, this was primarily attributed to its relatively low baseline volume (22.4 visits/day); a modest absolute increase of approximately 6 visits per day was sufficient to trigger statistical significance, a phenomenon that contrasts sharply with the huge effect size and clinically overwhelming surge observed in the cardiovascular department. This divergence in departmental response is further quantified and visualized in Supplementary Figure S1, which contrasts the massive cardiovascular surge against the modest respiratory changes across both cohorts.

Crucially, the influx of younger patients resulted in a prominent “dilution effect” on clinical severity. In the main cohort, the crude diagnosis proportion of confirmed acute coronary syndrome (ACS) dropped significantly from 2.1% to 1.2% (p < 0.001; OR = 0.58 [95% CI: 0.43–0.78]). Importantly, there was no significant proportional increase in hard clinical endpoints such as in-hospital mortality (Main: 0.3% vs. 0.1%, p = 0.113; Validation: 0.3% vs. 0.1%, p = 0.261). These initial findings collectively suggest that the post-event surge was predominantly composed of low-risk individuals seeking reassurance, laying the groundwork for the time-series and machine learning exploratory phenotyping models.

In the main cohort, the otal daily ED volume maintained a stable baseline with minimal fluctuation prior to March 24 (Fig. 1A). Immediately following the event, a significant upward level shift was observed, which was predominantly associated with a sharp escalation in cardiovascular-specific presentations (Fig. 1C). The LOESS-smoothed trajectories confirmed that the event followed by not only an acute spike but also a sustained shift in healthcare-seeking patterns, with cardiovascular volumes consistently exceeding pre-event baselines throughout the 15-day post-event window. Notably, the respiratory department-the only other major emergency specialty in the center-exhibited a remarkably stable trajectory with no significant level shift (Fig. 1E), providing robust evidence for the specialty-specific nature of the surge.

These temporal dynamics were precisely replicated in the internal validation cohort. Despite distinct baseline volumes, the internal validation cohort demonstrated a synchronized and identical immediate level jump in both total ED volume (Fig. 1B) and cardiovascular attendance (Fig. 1D) on the exact date of the event. Consistent with the main cohort, the respiratory volume in the validation group remained unaffected (Fig. 1F). The high degree of structural congruence between the main and internal validation cohorts across different clinical parameters underscores the systemic and predictable impact of the SCD event on public healthcare-seeking behavior, specifically targeting cardiovascular concerns while leaving other specialties balanced.

Analysis of the main cohort revealed a generalized escalation in absolute ED utilization across all timeframes (Fig. 2A). However, standardized percentage change analysis highlighted substantial temporal heterogeneity (Fig. 2B). In the main cohort, relative volume growth was disproportionately concentrated in the evening (+29.8%) and nighttime (+28.6%) shifts. This nocturnal behavioral shift was robustly validated in the independent internal validation cohort, where the nighttime shift exhibited a staggering 63.8% increase, suggesting a systemic pattern of after-hours health-seeking behavior followed by by the event.

To rigorously quantify the intervention effect within specific patient populations, an interrupted time series (ITS) subgroup analysis was performed (Fig. 2C). The forest plot demonstrated a demographic divergence: the excess ED volume was almost entirely associated with the younger and middle-aged populations. The subgroup aged < 45 years exhibited a highly significant ITS level change, whereas the elderly demographic (>65 years) showed no statistically significant alteration from their baseline trajectory (95% CI crossing zero).

Furthermore, the 24-hour distribution curve mapped the precise temporal onset of this surge (Fig. 2D). The post-event trajectory maintained a sustained elevation throughout the daytime but exhibited a notably persistent gap during the late evening and early morning hours (shaded region, 20:00–08:00). This unique convergence of younger demographics and intensified off-hour presentations aligns with the behavioral patterns of media-associated reassurance-seeking, wherein internet-active individuals experience exacerbated fears during isolated nocturnal hours.

Prior to the event, the ED population was primarily characterized by a distribution weighted toward older adults (Median age: 58.4 years), reflecting the traditional baseline of cardiovascular emergencies. Following the incident on March 24, a discernible leftward shift in the population density curve was observed. Although the post-event distribution remained largely overlapping with the baseline, statistical analysis identified a significant decrease in the median patient age (Welch’s t-test, p < 0.001).

As visualized in the Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) plot, this “proportional younging” was primarily associated with a relative increase in density within the 30–50-year age bracket. This subtle alteration underscores the high sensitivity of ED utilization to demographic-specific health-related concerns consistent with cyberchondria. It demonstrates that even a minor influx of younger, internet-active individuals-who likely felt a stronger resonance with the high-profile victim-can significantly alter the statistical profile of a large-scale clinical cohort without disrupting the overall clinical baseline.

The results demonstrated a consistent absence of statistically significant immediate level changes for all primary hard clinical endpoints across both cohorts. Specifically, the absolute daily incidence of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) showed no abrupt upward shift following the high-profile media event (Main: 0.84 visits, p = 0.649; Validation: 0.13 visits, p = 0.847). Crucially, to rigorously evaluate the hypothesized “dilution effect,” we further modeled the daily proportion of ACS visits. Notably, while the immediate negative level shifts did not reach statistical significance at the conventional 0.05 level (Main: −0.43%, p = 0.313; Validation: −0.60%, p = 0.134), the direction and magnitude of the change were consistent across both cohorts. The subsequent trend changes remained near-zero (Main: 0.02, p = 0.512; Validation: 0.04, p = 0.285), suggesting that the dilution effect persisted throughout the early post-event period without an immediate linear recovery.

Furthermore, ACS-related in-hospital mortality remained stable with no significant level or trend alterations (all p > 0.05). Critical life-saving interventions, including intubation for respiratory failure and thrombolysis for pulmonary embolism, also remained at baseline levels, with zero incidence recorded in the internal validation cohort throughout the study period.

Finally, sensitivity analyses-including the adjustment of the event initiation window by ± 3 days-yielded consistently significant level-shift estimates for total ED volume (data not shown), confirming the temporal robustness of the primary findings. Crucially, the overall lack of synchronized spikes in catastrophic pathology, such as aortic dissection (Main p = 0.328; Table 2), reinforces the conclusion that the surge was a manifestation of widespread health-related concerns (cyberchondria) rather than an authentic epidemiological increase in acute cardiopulmonary disease. To provide a high-resolution granular view of these shifts, a 30-day calendar heatmap illustrating hourly visit volumes is provided in Supplementary Figure S2. This visualization reveals a contrast: while the cardiovascular departments experienced a massive, nocturnal-predominant influx following the SCD event (Supplementary Figure S2C, S2D), the respiratory departments maintained relatively stable trajectories with only minor incremental growth (Supplementary Figure S2E, S2F). This subtle ‘spillover effect’ suggests that as cardiovascular resources became perceived as congested due to the sudden influx, a portion of the reassurance-seeking population sought consultation in alternative departments such as respiratory medicine. Nevertheless, these observations visually corroborate the specialty-specific nature of the media-induced health-related concerns and its disproportionate impact on cardiovascular emergency resources.

Machine learning-based attribution of surge characteristics

Given the inherent stochasticity of individual healthcare-seeking behavior associated with public media events, traditional predictive modeling often yields limited individual-level discrimination. Therefore, we repurposed our extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) framework primarily as a population phenotyping tool. By leveraging SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values, we aimed to quantitatively characterize the post-event surge population to specific demographic and behavioral dimensions providing a probabilistic portrait rather than individual-level prediction. In accordance with standard machine learning reporting, we evaluated the traditional predictive performance of the model. As theoretically anticipated for a population-shift inference model, the discriminatory performance was modest (Main Cohort Internal 5-fold Cross-Validation AUC = 0.512; Cross-Campus Validation AUC = 0.525; Supplementary Figure S3). These modest metrics mathematically validate our phenotyping rationale: a public health event causes a proportional shift in patient demographics rather than creating a mutually exclusive, deterministic classification. Therefore, the value of the XGBoost framework in this study relies entirely on its SHAP-derived global feature attribution to map these non-linear shifts, rather than its utility as a traditional predictive classifier.

Discussion

Principal findings and the “cyberchondria” phenomenon

This multi-cohort study demonstrated a significant immediate level increase in emergency department (ED) volume following the high-profile sudden cardiac death event (+24.4% in the main cohort and +34.7% in the internal validation cohort). This surge was not accompanied by a corresponding rise in hard clinical endpoints, including acute myocardial infarction or cardiovascular mortality. SHAP analysis identified younger age and nighttime presentation as the predominant features associated with the post-event surge phenotype. The dependence plots revealed a sharp non-linear escalation in surge probability among individuals under 45 years, particularly during nocturnal hours. These patterns were highly consistent across both cohorts (mean absolute SHAP value correlation r = 0.845). It is important to distinguish statistical significance from clinical significance regarding this demographic shift. While the 2.2-year decrease in median patient age was highly statistically significant (p < 0.001) due to our large sample size, the absolute age difference is relatively modest. Therefore, this demographic shift is meaningful primarily as an epidemiological signal of acute behavioral change among younger populations, rather than representing a profound transformation of the underlying clinical disease profile.

Together, the findings indicate that the surge was predominantly associated with younger, low-risk individuals seeking reassurance, consistent with media-induced cardiac anxiety (cyberchondria) [25]. Nevertheless, without granular individual-level measurements of internet consumption, social media exposure, or digital engagement, the claim that younger populations are more vulnerable to media effects remains merely a tentative hypothesis rather than an empirically validated causal pathway. That said, we must also recognize that the observed uptick may partly stem from authentic physiological (e.g., tachycardia, coronary vasospasm) and pathophysiological (e.g., stress-induced cardiomyopathy or Takotsubo syndrome) reactions to acute psychological stress following the news coverage.

This specialty-specific divergence is particularly noteworthy given the overlapping somatic presentations of early respiratory and cardiovascular conditions, such as chest tightness and dyspnea. In the absence of established baseline comorbidities, low-risk individuals experiencing these ambiguous symptoms appear to have engaged in media-primed symptom attribution. Specifically, the widespread coverage of the SCD event likely heightened public risk perception toward cardiovascular etiologies, prompting a preferential self-referral to the cardiovascular department while sparing respiratory services.

All diagnoses were standardized according to the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) codes. Specifically, confirmed ACS was defined strictly based on the Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (incorporating troponin elevation and ischemic ECG changes), ensuring high diagnostic reproducibility across the cohort.

Policy and health system implications

It should be clearly noted that the following discussion regarding healthcare system vulnerability and gatekeeping structures extends beyond our direct empirical ITS data and represents exploratory policy implications. The observed patterns highlight a structural paradox within China’s highly accessible public healthcare system. In contrast to Western models that employ strict primary care gatekeeping and higher cost-sharing mechanisms to buffer emergency departments from anxiety-driven surges, Chinese EDs operate with exceptionally low access thresholds [26–28]. This policy ensures universal immediate access to specialist evaluation but renders the tertiary system highly susceptible to rapid volatility during periods of mass media-driven health concerns. Patients experiencing acute health-related concerns frequently bypass primary care and seek advanced diagnostics at tertiary centers. The resulting influx of non-urgent visits not only strains public medical insurance resources but also creates a dilution effect on clinical severity, potentially compromising timely care for truly critically ill patients. While this dilution effect is our primary hypothesis for the reduced ACS proportion, we cautiously acknowledge alternative explanations. For instance, short-term fluctuations in hospital admission thresholds during the sudden volume surge, or coincidental micro-variations in true underlying cardiovascular morbidity, could theoretically contribute to the observed proportional decline.

Regarding external validity, it is important to note that the magnitude of such media-driven surges may vary significantly across different healthcare systems. Because our findings are derived from a highly accessible, direct-to-specialist healthcare model, the observed behavioral shifts might be less pronounced in healthcare systems with strict primary care gatekeeping or established telehealth triage pathways (such as the UK National Health Service or US managed care organizations). It remains an open question whether robust referral pathways can effectively buffer emergency departments against acute cyberchondria surges. Future cross-system comparative research is needed to determine the generalizability of these behavioral responses in different structural contexts.

The ethical imperative and physician burden

Beyond the statistical findings, this study underscores a notable occupational reality for front-line emergency physicians. During the surge, emergency departments operated under a high-load, low-staffing paradigm. Emergency and intensive care teams disproportionately absorbed both chronic systemic inefficiencies and acute waves of non-urgent demand. In China’s highly accessible healthcare system, patients can directly seek specialist or tertiary hospital care without gatekeeping by primary care providers. This contrasts sharply with Western models, where strict primary care gatekeeping effectively buffers emergency departments from non-urgent and anxiety-driven visits. Systematic reviews show that emergency physicians consistently report among the highest rates of burnout across medical specialties, driven largely by high patient volumes, shift work, and emotional demands [29, 30]. Although emergency medicine upholds the ethical principle of non-abandonment, the expectation that front-line staff should indefinitely accommodate unlimited, un-triaged demand is structurally unsustainable and contributes significantly to physician burnout [31–33].

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, our primary clinical endpoints were strictly restricted to ACS and SCD to ensure diagnostic uniformity and minimize confounding. Consequently, we did not analyze other acute cardiovascular events (e.g., atrial fibrillation, acute heart failure, hypertensive emergencies) that also require high-intensity ED resources. This strict definitional boundary may underestimate the true burden of the surge on overall cardiovascular emergency care. Second, this study used an internal multi- cohort validation approach within a single health system. Although the two cohorts share the same medical team and protocols, they serve geographically and demographically distinct populations, providing a natural quasi-experimental contrast. While this design strengthens internal validity by minimizing inter-hospital confounding, it limits broader generalizability to other healthcare systems or regions. Our findings primarily reflect urban behavioral patterns within a highly accessible healthcare tier. Future multi-campus studies encompassing diverse geographic regions and different health systems globally are required to confirm the broader applicability of these cyberchondria-driven utilization patterns [16].

A further limitation is the relatively short 15-day pre- and post-event observation window. This duration was deliberately chosen to avoid confounding by the Lunar New Year holiday period, during which emergency department utilization in China decreases markedly. Additionally, due to data extraction constraints, we did not incorporate a year-over-year historical control group (e.g., matching dates from the previous year). Although our ITS design and the use of the respiratory department as an internal control help mitigate seasonal confounding, future studies should ideally employ multi-year historical comparisons to further isolate the intervention effect.” Sensitivity analyses varying the event window by ± 3 days yielded consistently significant level-shift estimates for total ED volume, supporting the robustness of the primary findings. Nevertheless, the short window precludes assessment of longer-term effects and may limit generalizability compared with multi-center studies that employ extended observation periods [34, 35]. At the time of writing, although mainstream Chinese media reported sharp increases in emergency and cardiology visits across multiple tertiary hospitals nationwide, no peer-reviewed publications have yet documented this national-scale surge. Future multi-center studies encompassing diverse geographic regions and different health systems globally are required to confirm the broader applicability of these cyberchondria-driven utilization patterns.

Finally, several methodological caveats must be acknowledged. First, our operational definition of ‘symptom-matching’ was based on extracting unstructured chief complaint text from the EHR; lacking formal psychiatric validation or precedence in the literature, it should be interpreted strictly as an exploratory behavioral signal. Second, although the ITS design mitigates long-term secular trends and our respiratory control group accounts for generalized seasonal infectious waves, our models did not explicitly adjust for high-resolution daily confounders such as specific weather conditions, day-of-the-week effects, or localized micro-outbreaks. Third, because catastrophic hard endpoints (such as ACS-related mortality or aortic dissection) naturally exhibit near-zero daily incidence, our study is statistically underpowered to detect subtle shifts in these rare outcomes within a short observation window. Therefore, a Type II error cannot be completely excluded, and the absence of statistical significance for these severe endpoints must not be misconstrued as definitive evidence of zero clinical effect.

Public health implications and conclusion

The event-driven ED surge observed in this study reflects a broader challenge at the intersection of digital media, health-related concerns, and healthcare system design. To protect the resilience of acute care infrastructure, future public health strategies must move beyond passive observation. Proactive AI-driven triage systems and targeted public health communication are needed to mitigate media-associated health concerns. [17, 19, 20, 36]. Systemic reforms that strengthen primary care gatekeeping and introduce differentiated payment models may help redirect non-urgent, reassurance-seeking patients away from overburdened tertiary emergency departments. [37].

Conclusion

This multi-campus study provides quantitative evidence that high-profile media events can trigger substantial, rapid surges in emergency department utilization, a phenomenon associated with mass health-related concerns (“cyberchondria”) rather than genuine epidemiological shifts. By deploying an interpretable machine learning framework, we identified younger populations and nighttime presentations as the primary behavioral drivers of this influx. While China’s highly accessible emergency care system ensures rapid access for all, our findings reveal its acute vulnerability to un-triaged, reassurance-seeking demand, which strains public medical resources and dilutes the care available for truly critically ill patients. To safeguard the resilience of acute healthcare infrastructure in the digital age, policymakers must integrate proactive public health communication and implement adaptive, intelligence-driven triage systems to effectively intercept and manage media-associated health concerns.

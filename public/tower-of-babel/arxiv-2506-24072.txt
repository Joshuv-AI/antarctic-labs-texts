Protocol insecurity with finitely many sessions and xor
R Ramanujam, Vaishnavi Sundararajan, S P Suresh

Abstract
We present a different proof of the insecurity problem for xor , solved in [ CKRT05 ] . Our proof uses the notion of typed terms and well-typed proofs, and removes a restriction on the class of protocols to which the [ CKRT05 ] proof applies, by introducing a slightly different (but very natural) notion of protocols, where honest agent sends are derivable from previous receives in the same session.

3.5   An NP decision procedure for
B
-bounded insecurity

Fix
K
. Given a protocol
Prot
, guess a set
\mathcal{S}
of
K
pairwise coherent sessions of
Prot
. Each session is a role along with a substitution
\tau
for the agent variables s.t. honest agent sends are derivable after applying
\tau
. Notice that each such
\tau
is small, since
\tau(x)\in\mbox{{N}}\cup\mbox{{V}}
, and so
\|{\tau}\|\leq|\mbox{{C}}|
. There are polynomially many derivability checks, each of which can be performed in time polynomial in
|\mbox{{C}}|
. We then check the coherence of this set of sessions.

Finally, we guess a run
(\xi,\sigma^{*})
, where
\xi
is a prefix of an interleaving of the sessions in
\mathcal{S}
, and
\sigma^{*}
is a small substitution. The length
n
of
\xi
is also polynomial in the input size, and therefore there are polynomially many derivability checks for the intruder, each solvable in polynomial time. Thus we have an NP procedure to check if a given protocol has an attack consisting of
K
sessions.

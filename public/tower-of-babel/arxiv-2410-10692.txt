Coherent sheaves, sheared D-modules, and Hochschild cochains
Dario Beraldo, Kevin Lin, Wyatt Reeves

Abstract
. We show that the category of ind-coherent sheaves on a quasi-smooth scheme is naturally tensored over the category of sheared D-modules on its shifted cotangent bundle, commuting with its natural action of categorified Hoschschild cochains. We prove that it defines a Morita equivalence as such. We then extend these results to quasi-smooth Artin stacks. As a consequence of our formalism, we are able to articulate a precise sense in which the space of unramified automorphic functions over a function field localizes over the stack of arithmetic Arthur parameters.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

1.1.1.

The question of the existence of finite projective resolutions for modules was first raised by Hilbert, who showed in his famous “syzygy theorem” that the polynomial ring
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
has global dimension
n
. Another major result in the subject, due to Auslander–Buchsbaum–Serre, says that a Noetherian local ring has bounded global dimension if and only if it is regular. This indicates that the failure of a coherent sheaf to have a finite projective resolution is entirely determined by its behavior around the singular points of the underlying variety.

1.1.2.

If
Y
is a local complete intersection scheme, one can even produce a quantitative measure of how badly a coherent sheaf on
Y
fails to have a finite projective resolution. Namely, there is a scheme
\mathrm{Sing}(Y)
lying over
Y
, which is isomorphic to
Y
over the smooth locus, but which has positive fiber dimension over the singular locus. (The fiber dimension at a singular point measures “how pathological” the singularity is.)
\mathrm{Sing}(Y)
carries a contracting action of
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
and the fixed locus for this action is isomorphic to
Y
.

For any coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{Coh}(Y)
one can produce a closed conical subset
\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq\mathrm{Sing}(Y)
, which is contained in the zero section if and only if
\mathscr{F}
admits a finite projective resolution. The assignment
\mathscr{F}\mapsto\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{F})
gives a notion of “microlocal support” for coherent sheaves.

1.1.3.

Recently, the microlocal geometry of coherent sheaves has played a key role in the resolution of the geometric Langlands conjecture:

D(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\simeq\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathscr{N}}(\mathrm{LS}_{\check{G}}).

Evidently, the theory of microsupport of coherent sheaves is required to even state the result. One should think that the theorem gives a “spectral decomposition” of the category
D(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
. To first order,
D(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
is tensored over
\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{LS}_{\check{G}})
, which gives the decomposition of
D(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
by Langlands parameters. However, the full statement of the equivalence shows that D-modules on
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}
have a more refined decomposition in terms of coherent microsupport on
\mathrm{LS}_{\check{G}}
.

1.1.4.

The microsupport filtration on
\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathscr{N}}(\mathrm{LS}_{\check{G}})
is expected to have the following “experimental signature”: if
X
is a curve over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
, then the stack
\mathrm{LS}^{\operatorname{res}}(X)
of
\ell
-adic local systems with restricted variation was introduced in
[
AGK+20
]
. In
loc. cit.
they conjecture a restricted version of the geometric Langlands equivalence

\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathscr{N}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\overset{?}{\simeq}\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathscr{N}}(\mathrm{LS}^{\operatorname{res}}_{\check{G}})

which is expected to be closely related to the de Rham form of the geometric Langlands correspondence. This equivalence is expected to be equivariant for the natural Frobenius endomorphism on each side, and one should be able to recover the unramified part of the Langlands correspondence for function fields

\operatorname{Fun}_{c}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(\mathbf{F}_{q});\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\simeq\Gamma(\mathrm{LS}^{\mathrm{arith}}_{\check{G}};\omega)

by taking the categorical trace of both sides. Now the filtration of
\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathscr{N}}(\mathrm{LS}^{\operatorname{res}}_{\check{G}})
by microsupport decategorifies to a filtration on unramified automorphic functions, which is expected to encapsulate the theory of
Arthur parameters
.

1.1.5.

Motivated by these considerations, in this paper we undertake a study of the microlocal geometry of coherent sheaves from a categorical perspective; and especially with the goal of computing categorical traces.

1.3.
Examples

1.3.1.

In this section we will illustrate what our main results amount to in some simple examples.

1.3.2.
The case of a shifted vector space

Let’s first consider our Morita equivalence
Theorem

1.2.7
for the simplest quasi-smooth scheme,
\Omega V
. Let
V
be a vector space, viewed as an affine scheme. Then

\Omega V=\mathrm{pt}\underset{V}{\times}\mathrm{pt}

is a quasi-smooth affine scheme with

\mathrm{Sing}(\Omega V)_{\mathrm{dR}}=(V^{\vee})_{\mathrm{dR}}.

The main results of this paper are particularly transparent when one takes advantage of the Koszul duality equivalence

(1.3.1)

\mathrm{KD}:\mathrm{IndCoh}(\Omega V)\simeq\mathrm{Sym}(V[-2])\operatorname{-mod}\simeq\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}.

1.3.3.

Let us recall some basic features of the equivalence (
1.3.1
) for future reference:

(i)

Koszul duality intertwines the action of
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
on
\Omega V
by dilation with the action of
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
on
V^{\vee}
by
inverse
dilation.

(ii)

Koszul duality intertwines the Serre self-duality of
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\Omega V)
with the self-duality of
\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}
,
up to the negation involution of
V

\tau:V\to V\qquad v\mapsto-v.

Observe that
V
, and hence
\Omega V
is a commutative group scheme. Therefore,
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\Omega V)
acquires the structure of a symmetric monoidal category under convolution.

(iii)

Koszul duality intertwines the convolution symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\Omega V)
with the pointwise tensor symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee})
.

(iv)

Koszul duality intertwines the pointwise tensor symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\Omega V)
with the convolution symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee})
.

1.3.4.

In terms of Koszul duality, the action of
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Sing}(\Omega V))^{\Rightarrow}
on
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\Omega V)
is the action of
\mathrm{D}(V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}
on
\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}
induced by the symmetric monoidal functor

\mathrm{oblv}^{\Rightarrow}:\mathrm{D}(V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}\to\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}.

1.3.5.

By §
1.3.3
(iii), (iv), the equivalence

(1.3.2)

\mathrm{IndCoh}(\Omega V\times\Omega V)\overset{\mathrm{KD}\otimes\mathrm{KD}}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee}\times V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}\overset{\mathbf{1}\otimes\tau}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee}\times V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}

intertwines the convolution monoidal structures on its source and target, compatibly with their actions on the two sides of (
1.3.1
).

It follows that (
1.3.2
) restricts to an equivalence

\mathbf{H}(\Omega V)\simeq\mathrm{QCoh}\big((V^{\vee}\times V^{\vee})^{\wedge}_{V^{\vee}}\big)^{\Rightarrow}

intertwining the action of
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{Y})
on
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathscr{Y})
with the action of

\mathrm{QCoh}\big((V^{\vee}\times V^{\vee})^{\wedge}_{V^{\vee}}\big)^{\Rightarrow}

on
\mathrm{QCoh}(V^{\vee})^{\Rightarrow}
obtained by recognizing

(V^{\vee}\times V^{\vee})^{\wedge}_{V^{\vee}}\simeq V^{\vee}\!\underset{(V^{\vee})_{\mathrm{dR}}}{\times}\!V^{\vee}

as the infinitesimal groupoid of
V^{\vee}
.

So the Morita equivalence of
Theorem

1.2.7
in this case is a manifestation of the well-known fact that, for any smooth scheme
S
, the category
\mathrm{QCoh}(S)
is a Morita bimodule for
\mathrm{D}(S)
and
\mathrm{QCoh}((S\times S)^{\wedge}_{S})
.

1.3.6.
The case of a classifying stack

Let us now examine the equivalence

\operatorname{ShCat}_{\mathbf{H}}\simeq\operatorname{ShCat}_{\mathbf{S}^{\Rightarrow}}

for the smooth stack
\mathscr{Y}=BG
. In this subsection we will only consider
strong
action of groups on categories.

1.3.7.

In this case both sides of the equivalence are familiar to geometric representation theory:

–

On the one hand,

\operatorname{ShCat}_{\mathbf{S}^{\Rightarrow}}(BG)\simeq G\textbf{-mod}

identifies with the
(\infty,2)
-category of dg categories with a strong action of
G
.

–

On the other hand,

\mathbf{H}(BG)\simeq\mathrm{IndCoh}((BG\times BG)^{\wedge}_{BG})\simeq(\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod})^{G}

is the category of Harish–Chandra bimodules for

Therefore, the equivalence (

Ber19

) between categories acted on strongly by
G
and modules for the category of Harish–Chandra bimodules for

1.3.8.

In the case at hand, the adjunction

\mathbf{Loc}_{\mathbf{S}^{\Rightarrow}}:\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Sing}(\mathscr{Y}))^{\Rightarrow}\textbf{-mod}\rightleftharpoons\operatorname{ShCat}_{\mathbf{S}^{\Rightarrow}}(\mathscr{Y}):\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{S}^{\Rightarrow}}

identifies with the adjunction

This adjunction is a colocalization because

\operatorname{End}_{G}(\mathrm{Vect})\simeq\mathrm{Vect}^{G}\simeq\mathrm{D}(BG).

On the other hand,

1.3.9.

To summarize, the failure of the

adjunction to be an equivalence is tied to the failure of D-affineness for stacks, which in turn manifests as the failure of the relative categorical Kunneth formula for the fiber product:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pt}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BG}

1.4.
Miscellania

1.4.1.

In this section we will review some of the important technical ideas in this paper that may be of interest to experts.

1.4.2.

At various points in this paper homotopy coherence issues arise in the context of crystals of categories. Our
Proposition

2.4.2
provides an approach for dealing with these issues. A simplified version of the statement is that if
\mathscr{Y}
is a scheme and
\mathscr{C}_{1}
and
\mathscr{C}_{2}
are two module categories for
D(\mathscr{Y})
, then it is a
property
whether a functor
F:\mathscr{C}_{1}\to\mathscr{C}_{2}
is
D(\mathscr{Y})
linear. We use this proposition in a crucial way both when we construct an action of
D(\mathrm{Sing}(\mathscr{Y}))^{\Rightarrow}
on
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathscr{Y})
and when we extend our theory from schemes to stacks.

We should note that this idea is implicit in the main construction of
[
AG18
]
, which gives the category of singularities
\overset{\circ}{\mathrm{IndCoh}}(Z)
the structure of a crystal of categories over
\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{Sing}(Z))
. In this construction, the crystal structure is produced by specifying, for each
S\to\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{Sing}(Z))
a
full subcategory
of
\mathrm{QCoh}(S)\otimes\overset{\circ}{\mathrm{IndCoh}}(Z)
. An even earlier antecedent for this sort of idea is
[
Gai15
, §4.2.1]
, which observes that for a closed embedding of schemes, the functor of coinduction of crystals of categories is actually fully-faithful.

1.4.3.

The main idea of our construction of the action of
D(\mathrm{Sing}(\mathscr{Y}))^{\Rightarrow}
on
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathscr{Y})
is that there is a close relationship between
\mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathscr{Y})
and the category of singularities
\overset{\circ}{\mathrm{IndCoh}}(\mathscr{Y}\times\Omega\mathbf{A}^{1})
, which allows us to bootstrap from the results of
[
AG18
]
. The idea of relating
\mathrm{IndCoh}
on one variety to the category of singularities on another has appeared already in the work of M. Umut Isik
[
Isi13
]
.

1.4.4.

In the course of constructing the Morita equivalence between
D(\mathrm{Sing}(\mathscr{Y}))^{\Rightarrow}\textbf{-mod}
and
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{Y})\textbf{-mod}
we give a new proof of an important result of Ben-Zvi, Gunningham, and Orem
[
BZGO20
, Theorem 1.3]
concerning when a bimodule defines a Morita equivalence (see §
4.2
).

2.1.
Review of sheaves of categories

2.1.1.

For a morphism
f:T\to S
of affine schemes, we will write

\mathbf{ind}:\mathrm{QCoh}(S)\textbf{-mod}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\textbf{-mod}:\mathbf{res}

for the adjunction defined by extension and restriction of scalars along the map of symmetric monoidal categories

f^{*}:\mathrm{QCoh}(S)\to\mathrm{QCoh}(T).

2.1.2.

Let us recall some features of the theory of sheaves of categories.

To every prestack
\mathscr{Y}
, one associates the symmetric monoidal
(\infty,2)
-category

\operatorname{ShCat}(\mathscr{Y})=\lim_{S\to\mathscr{Y}}\mathrm{QCoh}(S)\textbf{-mod}

of sheaves of categories on
\mathscr{Y}
. This limit is taken over test affine schemes mapping to
\mathscr{Y}
, with the transition functors given by
\mathbf{ind}
. The value of a sheaf of categories
\mathscr{C}
at a test affine scheme
S
is denoted

\mathbf{\Gamma}(S;\mathscr{C})\in\mathrm{QCoh}(S)\textbf{-mod}.

The monoidal unit of
\operatorname{ShCat}(\mathscr{Y})
is denoted
\mathrm{QCoh}_{/\mathscr{Y}}
. It is given by the assignment

\mathbf{\Gamma}(S;\mathrm{QCoh}_{/\mathscr{Y}})=\mathrm{QCoh}(S).

To a morphism of prestacks
f:\mathscr{Z}\to\mathscr{Y}
, one associates the adjunction

(2.1.1)

\mathbf{cores}_{f}:\operatorname{ShCat}(\mathscr{Y})\rightleftharpoons\operatorname{ShCat}(\mathscr{Z}):\mathbf{coind}_{f}.

For any cartesian diagram of prestacks

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Z}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

the natural transformation

\mathbf{cores}_{g}\circ\mathbf{coind}_{f}\to\mathbf{coind}_{f^{\prime}}\circ\mathbf{cores}_{g^{\prime}}

obtained by adjunction from the identification

\mathbf{cores}_{f^{\prime}}\circ\mathbf{cores}_{g}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathbf{cores}_{g^{\prime}}\circ\mathbf{cores}_{f}

is an isomorphism.

2.1.3.

Note that

\mathbf{coind}_{f}(\mathrm{QCoh}_{/\mathscr{Z}})

is naturally a sheaf of symmetric monoidal categories over
\operatorname{ShCat}(\mathscr{Y})
, and the adjunction (
2.1.1
) enhances to an adjunction

\mathbf{Loc}_{\mathscr{Z}/\mathscr{Y}}:\mathbf{coind}_{f}(\mathrm{QCoh}_{/\mathscr{Z}})\textbf{-mod}(\operatorname{ShCat}(\mathscr{Y}))\rightleftharpoons\operatorname{ShCat}(\mathscr{Z}):\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\mathscr{Z}/\mathscr{Y}}

The morphism
f
is called relatively 1-affine when this adjunction is an equivalence.

2.1.4.

A prestack
\mathscr{Y}
is said to be 1-affine if the morphism
\mathscr{Y}\to\mathrm{pt}
is relatively 1-affine. We remind the reader that:

–

Every (eventually coconnective, laft, with affine diagonal) algebraic stack is 1-affine.

–

The de Rham prestack
Z_{\mathrm{dR}}
of any finite type scheme
Z
is 1-affine.

2.2.
Actions of sheared categories

2.2.1.

Let
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}\textbf{-mod}
denote the symmetric monoidal
(\infty,2)
-category of dg categories with a weak action of
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
. Recall from
[
AG15
, §A.2]
that this category is equipped with a symmetric monoidal auto-equivalence, known as shearing:

(-)^{\Rightarrow}:{\mathbf{G}_{m}}\textbf{-mod}\to{\mathbf{G}_{m}}\textbf{-mod}.

We write
(-)^{\Leftarrow}
for its inverse.

2.2.2.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a symmetric monoidal category acted on by
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
(that is,
\mathscr{A}
is a commutative algebra object of
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}\textbf{-mod}
). Since shearing is symmetric monoidal,
\mathscr{A}^{\Rightarrow}
is another symmetric monoidal category acted on by
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
.

The goal of this subsection is to give an answer to the question of what it takes equip a dg category
\mathscr{M}
with an action of
\mathscr{A}^{\Rightarrow}
(without regard for its
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
-action).

That is, we will give a description of

\mathscr{A}^{\Rightarrow}\textbf{-mod}=\mathscr{A}^{\Rightarrow}\textbf{-mod}(\operatorname{dgCat}).

2.2.3.
A toy model

The construction we are about to give has an analogue one category level down. Namely, for a graded algebra
A
, and a vector space
M
, the following data are equivalent:

(a)

a non-graded
A
-module structure on
M
;

(b)

a graded
A[t^{\pm 1}]
-module structure on
M[t^{\pm 1}]
.

Indeed, if
M
is given a non-graded
A
-module structure, then we endow
M[t^{\pm 1}]
with the structure of a graded
A
-module, where a homogeneous element
a
of degree
n
acts by

a\cdot(mt^{k})=(am)t^{n+k}.

This action commutes with the natural graded action of
k[t^{\pm 1}]
on
M[t^{\pm 1}]
and hence makes
M[t^{\pm 1}]
into a graded
A[t^{\pm 1}]
-module. To recover the original non-graded action of
A
of
M
, observe that
A
is the degree zero part of
A[t^{\pm 1}]
and
M
is the degree zero part of
M[t^{\pm 1}]
.

2.2.4.

Since the morphism
f:\mathrm{pt}\to B{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
is relatively 1-affine, we obtain an identification

where

is viewed as a
{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
-equivariant symmetric monoidal category via its pointwise tensor product.

Now let

be any symmetric monoidal category. From the monadic adjunction

by giving a geometric incarnation of

to an object of

\mathbf{Loc}

5.5.5.

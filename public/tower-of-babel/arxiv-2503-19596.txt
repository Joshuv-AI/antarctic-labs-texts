Classification of gradient Einstein-type Kähler manifolds with α = 0 \alpha=0 Thanks: The author is partially supported by the Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (C), No.23K03107, Japan Society for the Promotion of Science.
Shun Maeta

Abstract
. Thanks to the ambitious project initiated by Catino, Mastrolia, Monticelli and Rigoli, which aims to provide a unified viewpoint for various geometric solitons, many classes, including Ricci solitons, Yamabe solitons, k k -Yamabe solitons, quasi-Yamabe solitons, and conformal solitons, can now be studied under a unified framework known as Einstein-type manifolds. Einstein-type manifolds are characterized by four constants, denoted by α , β , μ \alpha,\beta,\mu and ρ \rho . In this paper, we completely classify all non-trivial, complete gradient Einstein-type Kähler manifolds with α = 0 \alpha=0 . As a corollary, rotational symmetry for many classes is obtained. In particular, we show that any non-trivial complete gradient quasi-Yamabe soliton on Kähler manifolds is rotationally symmetric.

There are various types of geometric solitons, including Ricci solitons, Yamabe solitons,
k
-Yamabe solitons, quasi-Yamabe solitons, almost Yamabe solitons, conformal solitons.
To provide a unified viewpoint for all these solitons, Catino, Mastrolia, Monticelli and Rigoli
[

introduced Einstein-type manifolds as part of an ambitious project.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. If there exist
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(1.1)

\alpha{\rm Ric}+\frac{\beta}{2}L_{X}g+\mu X^{\flat}\otimes X^{\flat}=(\rho R+\lambda)g,

for some constants
\alpha,\beta,\mu,\rho\in\mathbb{R}
, with
(\alpha,\beta,\mu)\not=(0,0,0)
, then
(M,g)
is called an
Einstein-type manifold
.
Here,
{\rm Ric}
denotes the Ricci tensor of
M
,
L
denotes the Lie derivative, and
R
is the scalar curvature of
M
.
If
X=\nabla F
for some
F\in C^{\infty}(M)
, it is called a
gradient
Einstein-type manifold:

(1.2)

\alpha{\rm Ric}+\beta\nabla\nabla F+\mu\nabla F\nabla F=(\rho R+\lambda)g,

where
\nabla\nabla F
is the Hessian of
F
, and
F
is called the potential function.
If
F
is constant, then it is called
trivial
.

As pointed out in
[
CMMR17
]
, gradient Einstein-type manifolds encompass many known soliton notions (cf.

\bullet

(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\rho)=(0,1,0,1)
and
\lambda

\bullet

(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\rho)=(0,1,0,1)
.

\bullet

-Yamabe solitons:

\bullet

\bullet

Theorem 1.1
.

Any
2m
-dimensional
(m>1)
non-trivial, complete, gradient Einstein-type Kähler manifold
(M,g,F)
with
\alpha=0
is one of the following.

(I) When
\beta
is zero, there exists no non-trivial Einstein-type Kähler manifold.

(II) When
\mu
is zero, the Einstein-type Kähler manifold is either,

(A) a direct product
M=\mathbb{R}\times N^{2m-1},g=dr^{2}+a^{2}g_{N}
, where
g_{N}
is a Riemannian metric of
N
, and the potential function
F
is
F(r)=ar+b
for some constants
a>0
and
b
, or

(B) the complex Euclidean space
and the potential function
F
is
F(r)=ar^{2}+b
for some constants
a>0
and
b
.

(III) When
\beta
and
\mu
have opposite signs, there exists no non-trivial Einstein-type Kähler manifold.

(IV) When
\beta
and
\mu
have the same sign, the Einstein-type Kähler manifold is

M=[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{2m-1}
and
g=dr^{2}+\psi^{2}(r)g_{S},

r\in[0,+\infty)
,
where
\psi(r)=F^{\prime}(r)e^{-cF(r)}
. The potential function
F
is

F(r)=-\frac{1}{c}\log\left\{c\Big(-\frac{a}{2}r^{2}-c_{1}\Big)\right\},

for some positive constants
a
and
c_{1}
, where
c=-\frac{\mu}{\beta}.

Since
\lambda
is a smooth function, the equation
(
1.2
)
can be reduced to

(1.3)

\beta\nabla\nabla F+\mu\nabla F\nabla F=\varphi g,

for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
and constants
(\beta,\mu)\not=(0,0).

Because of the symmetry of the sign of
(\beta,\mu)
, we only need to consider the following four cases.

(\beta,\mu)=(0,P),(P,P),(P,0),(P,N),

where
P
and
N
denote positive and negative constants, respectively.

We show the following lemma used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional gradient Einstein-type manifold with
\alpha=0
.
For the case
\beta\not=0,
the following formulas hold.

(1.4)

\displaystyle(n-1)\nabla_{i}\varphi-(n-1)c\varphi\nabla_{i}F+R_{ij}\nabla_{j}F=0,

(1.5)

\displaystyle(n-1)\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{i}\varphi-(n-1)c\nabla_{\ell}\varphi\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(n-1)c\varphi^{2}g_{\ell i}-(n-1)c^{2}\varphi\nabla_{\ell}F\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\nabla_{\ell}R_{ij}\nabla_{j}F+\varphi R_{i\ell}+cR_{ij}\nabla_{\ell}F\nabla_{j}F=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle(n-1)\Delta\varphi-(n-1)c\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla F\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(n-1)nc\varphi^{2}-(n-1)c^{2}\varphi|\nabla F|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla R,\nabla F\rangle+\varphi R+c{\rm Ric}(\nabla F,\nabla F)=0,

where
c=-\frac{\mu}{\beta}
, and we redefine
\frac{\varphi}{\beta}
as
\varphi
.

Proof.

The gradient Einstein-type equation (
1.3
) can be denoted as follows.

(1.7)

\nabla\nabla F=\varphi g+c\nabla F\nabla F,

where
c=-\frac{\mu}{\beta}
, and we redefine
\frac{\varphi}{\beta}
as
\varphi
.
Ricci identity shows

(1.8)

\Delta\nabla_{i}F=\nabla_{i}\Delta F+R_{ij}\nabla_{j}F.

By the equation of Einstein-type manifolds, one has

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Delta\nabla_{i}F=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}(\varphi g_{ki}+c\nabla_{k}F\nabla_{i}F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\varphi+c\Delta F\nabla_{i}F+c\nabla_{k}F\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\varphi+c\Delta F\nabla_{i}F+c\varphi\nabla_{i}F+c^{2}|\nabla F|^{2}\nabla_{i}F,

and

(1.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\Delta F=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(n\varphi+c|\nabla F|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\nabla_{i}\varphi+2c\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}F\nabla_{k}F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\nabla_{i}\varphi+2c\varphi\nabla_{i}F+2c^{2}|\nabla F|^{2}\nabla_{i}F.

Combining (
1.8
) with (
1.9
), one has

\nabla_{i}\varphi+c\Delta F\nabla_{i}F+c\varphi\nabla_{i}F+c^{2}|\nabla F|^{2}\nabla_{i}F-\nabla_{i}\Delta F-R_{ij}\nabla_{j}F=0.

Substituting (
1.10
) into the equation, we have

(n-1)\nabla_{i}\varphi-(n-1)c\varphi\nabla_{i}F+R_{ij}\nabla_{j}F=0.

By differentiating (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle(n-1)\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{i}\varphi-(n-1)c\nabla_{\ell}\varphi\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(n-1)c\varphi\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{i}F+\nabla_{\ell}R_{ij}\nabla_{j}FR_{ij}\nabla_{\ell}F\nabla_{j}F=0.

Combining this with (
1.7
), we have

\displaystyle(n-1)\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{i}\varphi-(n-1)c\nabla_{\ell}\varphi\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(n-1)c\varphi^{2}g_{\ell i}-(n-1)c^{2}\varphi\nabla_{\ell}F\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\nabla_{\ell}R_{ij}\nabla_{j}F+\varphi R_{i\ell}+cR_{ij}\nabla_{\ell}\nabla_{j}F=0.

By taking trace,

\displaystyle(n-1)\Delta\varphi-(n-1)c\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla F\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(n-1)nc\varphi^{2}-(n-1)c^{2}\varphi|\nabla F|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla R,\nabla F\rangle+\varphi R+c{\rm Ric}(\nabla F,\nabla F)=0.

∎

We will consider the other cases. In these cases,

For any vector fields X,Y on

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\nabla F-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}\nabla F-\nabla_{[X,Y]}\nabla F

\displaystyle(X\varphi-c\varphi XF)Y-(Y\varphi-c\varphi YF)X.

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(X,\nabla F)={\rm Ric}(JX,J\nabla F)=

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(X,\nabla F)=g(X,-(2m-1)(\nabla\varphi-c\varphi\nabla F)).

Combining these equations, one has,

2(m-1)(\nabla\varphi-c\varphi\nabla F)=0.

Since

(1.11)

\nabla\varphi=c\varphi\nabla F.

Since

Therefore, we only need to consider the other cases.

Since

\varphi=F^{\prime\prime}-cF^{\prime 2}.

In Case 1 of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we did not use the assumption that
M
is Kähler. Therefore, we have the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Any
n
-dimensional
(n\geq 3)
non-trivial, complete, gradient Einstein-type manifold with
\alpha=\beta=0
is trivial.

As exemplified by Perelman’s conjecture
[
Perelman02
]
, rotational symmetry often becomes a central problem for geometric solitons, particularly under certain curvature assumptions.
By applying Theorem
1.1
, we obtain rotational symmetry for a wide class of geometric solitons.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
2m
-dimensional
(m>1)
complete Kähler manifold that satisfies one of the following. If the potential function is non constant (that is,
M
is nontrivial), then it is rotationally symmetric.

(1)

A gradient Yamabe soliton with
R\not=-\lambda
at some point.

(2)

A gradient almost Yamabe soliton with
R\not=-\lambda
at some point.

(3)

A gradient
k
-Yamabe soliton with
\sigma_{k}\not=\nu
at some point.

(4)

A gradient conformal soliton with
\lambda\not=0
at some point.

(5)

A gradient quasi-Yamabe soliton.

In particular, any nontrivial complete gradient quasi-Yamabe soliton on Kähler manifolds is rotationally symmetric, without any curvature assumption.

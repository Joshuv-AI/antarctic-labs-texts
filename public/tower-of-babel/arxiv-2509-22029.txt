Abstract
. We study an initial boundary value problem for the 3-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes equations with hyperbolic heat conduction, where the classical Fourier law is replaced by the Cattaneo-Christov constitutive relation. We focus on spherically symmetric solutions. We establish the existence of uniform global small solutions to the resulting system. Furthermore, based on uniform a priori estimates, we rigorously justify both the relaxation limit and the vanishing viscosity limit. Keywords : Initial boundary value problem; hyperbolic heat conduction; spherically symmetric solution; global well-posedness; relaxation limit; vanishing viscosity limit AMS classification code : 35M13; 35Q35;



1.
Introduction

The basic equations governing three-dimensional compressible fluid dynamics are given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p=\mathrm{div}\,S,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho{\mathcal{E}})+\mathrm{div}(\rho u{\mathcal{E}}+pu+q-Su)=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

for
(x,t)\in\Omega(\subset\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,\infty)
. The quantities
\rho
,
u
,
p
,
\theta
, and
\mathcal{E}
represent the fluid density, velocity, pressure, temperature, and specific energy, respectively. The quantities
q
and
S
denote the heat flux and stress tensor, respectively, and must be specified through constitutive relations to close the system (
1.1
).

We assume the flow is Newtonian, which implies that the stress tensor
S
is given by

\displaystyle S=\mu(\theta)\left(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T}-\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{div}uI_{3}\right)+\lambda(\theta)\mathrm{div}uI_{3},

(1.2)

where
\mu
and
\lambda
are the shear and bulk viscosities, respectively, and both are functions of the temperature
\theta
.

The formulation of the heat flux
q
is more intricate. Instead of employing the classical Fourier law for heat conduction, we adopt the Cattaneo-Christov (CC) constitutive relation:

\displaystyle\tau(\theta)\rho\left(\partial_{t}q+u\cdot\nabla q-q\cdot\nabla u+(\mathrm{div}u)q\right)+q+\kappa(\theta)\nabla\theta=0,

(1.3)

where
\tau(\theta)
is the relaxation parameter accounting for the time lag in the response of heat flux to the temperature gradient, and
\kappa(\theta)
is the heat conductivity coefficient. Both parameters depend on the temperature.

The Cattaneo-Christov relation (
1.3
) was originally proposed in the linearized case by Cattaneo
[
1
]
to resolve the paradox of infinite heat propagation speed implied by Fourier’s law, and was later extended to an objective (frame-indifferent) nonlinear formulation by Christov
[
3
]
. The CC model has been shown to be particularly relevant in extreme physical scenarios, such as instantaneous heating by laser pulses
[
22
]
, heat conduction in nanometer-scale materials
[
20
]
, and the Rayleigh-Bénard convection near MEMS devices
[
8
]
. In fact, the dimensionless Cattaneo number, defined by
C=\frac{\tau\kappa}{L^{2}}
where
L
is a reference length scale, is used to quantify the significance of the CC model relative to Fourier’s law
[
21
]
. When
\kappa
is large or
L
is small, the CC model can become particularly important.

Note that the second law of thermodynamics may not hold for the new CC model if the classical thermodynamic equation remains unchanged; see
[
4
,
13
,
14
,
26
]
. Inspired by Coleman
et al.

[
4
]
, we assume that the specific total energy is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}=\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+e,

(1.4)

with the specific internal energy
e
and the pressure
p
given by

\displaystyle e=C_{v}\theta+a(\theta)q^{2},\quad p=R\rho\theta,

(1.5)

where
a(\theta)=\frac{Z(\theta)}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}Z^{\prime}(\theta)
and
Z(\theta)=\frac{\tau(\theta)}{\kappa(\theta)}
.

C_{v}
and
R
denote the heat capacity at constant volume and the gas constant, respectively. The quantities
p
and
e
satisfy the usual thermodynamic relation

\rho^{2}e_{\rho}=p-\theta p_{\theta}.

For
\tau(\theta)=0
, the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) reduces to the classical compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier system, for which the global well-posedness, blow-up of smooth solutions, and large-time behavior have been extensively studied. In particular, the local existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions were established by Serrin
[
30
]
, Nash
[
27
]
and Itaya
[
17
]
for initial data away from vacuum. Later, Matsumura and Nishida
[
25
]
obtained global smooth solutions for small initial data without vacuum. For large initial data, Xin
[
32
]
, and Cho and Jin
[
2
]
, showed that smooth solutions must blow up in finite time if the initial data contains vacuum. See
[
9
,
10
,
23
,
18
,
19
,
6
]
for results on the global existence of weak solutions.

On the other hand, for
\tau(\theta)>0
, there are relatively few results. The authors first studied in
[
12
]
the well-posedness of strong solutions in Sobolev spaces, as well as the relaxation limit, under a linearized form of (
1.3
). The decay properties of the resulting solutions were established in
[
24
,
31
]
. Crin-Barat, Kawashima, and Xu
[
5
]
obtained global-in-time well-posedness for small initial data in Besov spaces, along with a strong global relaxation limit. In this sense, the Cauchy problem for the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) has been extensively investigated, cf. the survey on our results in
[
28
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, initial boundary value problems have not yet been addressed. We note that the initial boundary value problem for compressible Navier-Stokes equations with Maxwell’s law – i.e. relaxation for the stress tensor – has been considered recently, see
[
11
,
15
,
16
]
.
As a first step in this direction, we study the initial boundary value problem for system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) under the assumption of spherical symmetry.
We consider spherically symmetric solutions of the following form:

\displaystyle\rho(t,x)=\rho(t,r),\quad u(t,x)=u(t,r)\frac{x}{r},\quad\theta(t,x)=\theta(t,r),\quad q(t,x)=q(t,r)\frac{x}{r},

(1.6)

where
x\in\Omega;=\overline{B(0,1)}^{c}
or
\Omega:=B(0,2)\setminus\overline{B(0,1)}
, with
B(0,r)
denoting the open ball of radius
r
centered at the origin. Under this symmetry, the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) reduces to:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\rho u=0,\\
\rho u_{t}+\rho uu_{r}+p_{r}=\left(\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)\right)\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)\right)_{r},\\
\rho e_{\theta}\theta_{t}+(\rho ue_{\theta}-\frac{2a(\theta)}{Z(\theta)}q)\theta_{r}+p\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)+q_{r}+\frac{2}{r}q=\\
\quad\left(\frac{2}{\tau(\theta)}+\frac{4}{r}u\right)a(\theta)q^{2}+\mu(\theta)\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)+\lambda(\theta)\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2},\\
\tau(\theta)\rho\left(q_{t}+uq_{r}+\frac{2}{r}uq\right)+q+\kappa(\theta)\theta_{r}=0.\end{cases}

(1.7)

We supplement system (
1.7
) with the initial data

\displaystyle(\rho,u,\theta,q)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},q_{0}),

(1.8)

possibly depending on
\tau
, and the boundary conditions

\displaystyle u|_{\partial\Omega}=q|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.9)

with the domain
\Omega
reduced to either
(1,\infty)
or to
(1,2)
.

To establish estimates independent of
\tau(\theta)
,
\mu(\theta)
, and
\lambda(\theta)
and to facilitate taking the limit, we assume the following relations:

\tau(\theta)=\tau g(\theta),\quad\mu(\theta)=\mu h(\theta),\quad\lambda(\theta)=\lambda l(\theta),

where
g
,
h
, and
l
are smooth (and positive) functions of
\theta
, and
\tau
,
\mu
, and
\lambda
are positive constants. The following assumptions are used throughout the paper:

Assumption 1.1
.

(1) The initial and boundary data satisfy the usual compatibility conditions:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{k}u(0,x)\big|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{t}^{k}q(0,x)\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad k=0,1,

(1.10)

where
\partial_{t}u(0,x)
and
\partial_{t}q(0,x)
are defined recursively using equations
\eqref{1.5}_{2}
and
\eqref{1.5}_{4}
, respectively.

(2) The possible dependence of the initial data on
\tau
is compatible in the following sense:

\displaystyle\left\|r\left(q_{0}+\kappa(\theta_{0})(\theta_{0})_{r}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}=O(\sqrt{\tau}),\quad\text{as }\tau\to 0.

(1.11)

To formulate our theorem, we introduce the following energy functional:

\displaystyle E(t):=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|r(\rho-1,u,\theta-1,q)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r(\rho_{r},u_{r},\theta_{r},q_{r})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(u,q)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(u_{r},q_{r})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|r(\rho_{rr},u_{rr},\theta_{rr},q_{rr})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r(\rho_{t},u_{t},\theta_{t},\sqrt{\tau}q_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r(\rho_{tr},u_{tr},\theta_{tr},\sqrt{\tau}q_{tr})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\|r(\rho_{tt},u_{tt},\theta_{tt},\sqrt{\tau}q_{tt})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+\lambda\right)^{2}\|r(u_{rrr},\tau u_{trr})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

The corresponding dissipation functional is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(t):=

\displaystyle\|rq\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|rD(\rho,u,\theta,q)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(u,q)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r(\rho_{rr},u_{rr},\theta_{rr},q_{rr})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|(u_{r},q_{r},u_{t},q_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r(\rho_{tr},u_{tr},\theta_{tr},q_{tr})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r(\rho_{tt},u_{tt},\theta_{tt})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\|rq_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+\lambda\right)\|r(u_{trr},u_{ttr})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where
D:=(\partial_{t},\partial_{r})
.

Our first result concerns the global-in-time small well-posedness of the initial boundary value problem (

)-(

), with estimates that are uniform with respect to the parameters
\tau

\lambda
, and

Theorem 1.2
.

Let Assumption

hold and
r(\rho_{0}-1,\theta_{0}-1,q_{0})\in H^{{3}}
,

. Then there exists a small constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that if

\|r(\rho_{0}-1,\theta_{0}-1,q_{0})\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|ru_{0}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\leq\epsilon_{0},

there exists a unique global solution
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
to the initial boundary value problem (
1.7
)-(
1.9
) such that

(\rho-1,\theta-1,q)\in\bigcap\limits_{k=0}^{2}C^{k}([0,\infty),H^{2-k}),\,u\in\bigcap\limits_{k=0}^{1}C^{k}([0,\infty),H^{3-k})\bigcap C^{2}([0,\infty),L^{2})

and satisfy the uniform estimate

E(t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq CE(0),

where
C
is a constant independent of the parameters
\tau
,
\lambda
, and
\mu
.

Building on the uniform estimates established in Theorem
1.2
, we now state our second result regarding the vanishing viscosity limit.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
\tau>0
. Let
\varepsilon=(\mu,\lambda)

, such that, as

\displaystyle(\rho_{\tau}^{\varepsilon},u_{\tau}^{\varepsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\varepsilon},q_{\tau}^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(\rho_{\tau}^{0},u_{\tau}^{0},\theta_{\tau}^{0},q_{\tau}^{0})\quad\mbox{\rm strongly\quad in }\quad C([0,T],H_{loc}^{2-\delta})

(1.12)

where
(\rho_{\tau}^{0},u_{\tau}^{0},\theta_{\tau}^{0},q_{\tau}^{0})
is a strong solution to the three-dimensional hyperbolized compressible Euler-Cattaneo-Christov system (

Fix

2.
Local well-posedness

In this section, we establish the local well-posedness for system (
1.7
)-(
1.9
). We note that, to our knowledge, general results for the initial boundary value problem of hyperbolic-parabolic coupled systems are lacking. To obtain the desired well-posedness, we follow the approach in
[
33
]
(pp. 119-124).
First, Let
M_{0}:=\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}(\rho-1,u,\theta-1,q)|_{t=0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty
. Let
M_{1}
be a positive constant such that when
\sup_{0\leq t\leq h}\|(\rho-1,u,\theta-1,q)\|_{H^{2}}\leq M_{1}
,

\min\{\min_{[0,h]\times\Omega}\rho(t,r),\min_{[0,h]\times\Omega}\theta(t,r),\min_{[0,h]\times\Omega}e_{\theta}(t,r)\}=:\gamma>0.

Introduce the space

\displaystyle X_{h}(M_{2},M_{3})=\left\{(\rho,u,\theta,q)|(\rho-1,\theta-1,q)\in\bigcap\limits_{k=0}^{2}C^{k}([0,h],H^{2-k}),u\in C([0,h],H^{3}),\right.

\displaystyle u_{t}\in C([0,h],H^{2}),u_{tt}\in C([0,h],L^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,h],H^{1}))

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq h}\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}(\rho-1,u,\theta-1,q)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq M_{2},

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq h}(\|u_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{txx}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\int_{0}^{h}\|u_{xtt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq M_{3}

\displaystyle\left.(\rho,u,\theta,q)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},q_{0}),\quad(u,q)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\right\}

where
M_{2}<M_{1}
,
M_{3}
are positive constant specified in the course of the proof.

Now, we are ready to state the local well-posedness theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
(\rho_{0}-1,\theta_{0}-1,q_{0})\in H^{{3}}
,
u_{0}\in H^{4}
and the compatibility condition (
1.10
) hold. Then,
there exists a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}
and
t_{0}
depending on
\epsilon_{0}
such that when
M_{0}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, problem (
1.7
)-(
1.9
) admits a unique local solution

(\rho,u,\theta,q)\in X_{t_{0}}(C_{1}M_{0},C_{2}M_{0})
where
C_{1},C_{2}
only depends on
\gamma
.

Proof.

We present a concise proof of this theorem, though detailed proofs for similar systems can be found in
[
33
]
and references therein. Specifically, we divide the proof into four steps.

Step 1: For any
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u},\tilde{\theta},\tilde{q})\in X_{h}(M_{2},M_{3})
, consider the auxiliary linear problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{\rho}\tilde{e}_{\theta}\theta_{t}+(\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}\tilde{e}_{\theta}-\frac{2a(\tilde{\theta})}{Z(\tilde{\theta})}\tilde{q})\theta_{r}+q_{r}=f_{1},\\
\tau(\tilde{\theta})\tilde{\rho}(q_{t}+\tilde{u}q_{r})+\kappa(\tilde{\theta})\theta_{r}=f_{2},\\
(\theta,q)|_{t=0}=(\theta_{0},q_{0}),\quad q|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{\rho}u_{t}-(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\tilde{\theta})+\lambda(\tilde{\theta}))u_{rr}=f_{3},\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\tilde{u}\rho_{r}=f_{4},\\
\rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{0},\end{cases}

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f_{1}=\left(\frac{2}{\tau(\tilde{\theta})}+\frac{4}{r}\tilde{u}\right)a(\tilde{\theta})\tilde{q}^{2}+\mu(\tilde{\theta})\left(2\tilde{u}_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}\tilde{u}^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(\tilde{u}_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\tilde{u}\right)^{2}\right)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad+\lambda(\tilde{\theta})\left(\tilde{u}_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\tilde{u}\right)^{2}-(\tilde{u}_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\tilde{u})\tilde{p}-\frac{2}{r}\tilde{q},\\
f_{2}=-\tau(\tilde{\theta})\tilde{\rho}\cdot\frac{2}{r}\tilde{u}\tilde{q}-\tilde{q},\\
f_{3}=\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\tilde{\theta})+\lambda(\tilde{\theta})\right)\left(\frac{2}{r}\tilde{u}_{r}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}\tilde{u}\right)+\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{\theta})\tilde{\theta}_{r}\left(\tilde{u}_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\tilde{u}\right)+\frac{4}{3}\mu^{\prime}(\tilde{\theta})\tilde{\theta}_{r}\left(\tilde{u}_{r}-\frac{\tilde{u}}{r}\right)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-R\tilde{\rho}\tilde{\theta}_{r}-R\tilde{\theta}\tilde{\rho}_{r}-\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}\tilde{u}_{r},\\
f_{4}=-\frac{2}{r}\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}-\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}_{r}.\\
\end{cases}

We deal with the system (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
), respectively. First, problem (
2.1
) is a linear symmetric hyperbolic system of first order (by dividing
\eqref{loc1}_{2}
by
\kappa(\tilde{\theta})
). We first check that the boundary condition
q|_{\partial\Omega}=0
is non-characteristic and satisfy the maximal nonnegative condition (admissible in the sense of Friedrich, see
[
33
,
7
]
). Indeed, we rewrite system (
2.1
) with
U=(\theta,q)
as

\displaystyle A^{0}U_{t}+A^{1}U_{r}=F,

(2.4)

where

A^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\rho}\tilde{e}_{\theta}&0\\
0&\frac{\tau(\tilde{\theta})\tilde{\rho}}{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})}\end{pmatrix},A^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\left(\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}\tilde{e}_{\theta}-\frac{2a(\tilde{\theta})}{Z(\tilde{\theta})}\tilde{q}\right)&1\\
1&\frac{\tau(\tilde{\theta})\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}}{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})}\end{pmatrix},F=\mathrm{diag}\{f_{1},f_{2}\}.

The boundary condition reduces to

PU|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\text{with}\,P=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Note that by virtue of
(\tilde{u},\tilde{q})|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we have
\det\left((A^{0})^{-1}A^{1}\right)|_{\partial\Omega}\neq 0
. Consequently, the boundary condition
q|_{\partial\Omega}=0
is non-characteristic. Now, we show the boundary condition is maximally nonnegative, i.e., the matrix
A^{1}\cdot\nu|_{\partial\Omega}
is positive semidefinite on the null space
K
of
P
but not on any space containing
K
. Here
\nu=-1
. Let
\xi=(\xi_{1},0)^{T}\in K=\mathrm{span}\{(1,0)^{T}\}
, then,

\displaystyle\xi^{T}A^{1}\cdot\nu|_{\partial\Omega}\xi=0.

On the other hand,
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the only space containing
K
as a proper subspace. Take
\psi=(1,1)^{T}
, we get

\displaystyle\psi^{T}A^{1}\cdot\nu|_{\partial\Omega}\psi=-2<0.

Thus, the maximally nonnegative property is satisfied. Furthermore, since the coefficients belong to
\bigcap\limits_{k=0}^{2}C^{k}([0,h],H^{2-k})
and
D^{2}f_{1}\in L^{2}([0,h],L^{2})
and the compatibility condition satisfied up to first-order, by Theorem 1.3.10 in
[
33
]
(pp. 18-19) (originally coming from
[
29
]

(\theta-1,q)\in\bigcap\limits_{k=0}^{2}C^{k}([0,h],H^{2-k})
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}(\theta-1,q)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}(\theta-1,q)|_{t=0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{1}\|D_{t}^{k}f_{1}|_{t=0}\|_{H^{1-k}}^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{{l}={0}}^{2}\|D^{l}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)e^{CM_{2}t},

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
\gamma
.

Concerning the initial-boundary value problem for linear parabolic system (
2.2
), we deduce from energy methods that the unique solution

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|(u_{x},u_{xx},u_{tx},u_{ttx},u_{txx})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq Ce^{CM_{2}t}\left\{\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}u|_{t=0}\|_{L^{2}}+M_{2}t\right\},

\displaystyle\|u_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{txx}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(M_{2})(1+\sum\limits_{{k}={1}}^{2}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

where
C(M_{2})
is a positive constant depending on
M_{2}
.

Finally, for the transport equation
\eqref{loc3}_{1}
, we define the characteristic line

\frac{dX(t,r)}{dt}=\tilde{u}(t,X(t,r)),\quad X(0,r)=r.

Then, we can derive an explicit solution for (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\rho(t,r)=\rho_{0}(r_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}f_{4}(s,X(s,r_{0}))\mathrm{d}s,

(2.5)

where
r_{0}
denotes the unique point on the

-axis (

(0,r_{0})
and
(t,r)
. Moreover,
from usual energy estimates, we can easily get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}(\rho-1)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}(\rho-1)|_{t=0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{1}\|D_{t}^{k}f_{4}|_{t=0}\|_{H^{1-k}}^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{{l}={0}}^{2}\|D^{l}f_{4}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)e^{CM_{2}t}.

Step 2: We first choose

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=2C\left(\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}(\rho-1,\theta-1,q)|_{t=0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{1}\|D_{t}^{k}(f_{1},f_{4})|_{t=0}\|_{H^{1-k}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{{k}={0}}^{2}\|D^{k}u|_{t=0}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=:C_{1}M_{0}.

Then, choose

\displaystyle M_{3}=\max\{M_{2},C(M_{2})(1+M_{2})\}=:C_{2}M_{0}.

Finally, choose
h
depending on
M_{0}
such that for
t\leq h
,

\displaystyle Cte^{CM_{2}t}\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{{l}={0}}^{2}\|D^{l}(f_{1},f_{4})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{M_{2}}{2},\quad e^{CM_{2}t}\leq\frac{5}{4},\quad Ce^{CM_{2}t}t\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Therefore, the linear operator defined by (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) maps
X_{h}(M_{2},M_{3})
into itself.

Step 3: Following the above two steps, we can get an iterative sequence
(\rho_{n},u_{n},\theta_{n},q_{n})\in X_{h}(M_{2},M_{3})
. By usual compactness
argument, the sequence
(\rho_{n}-1,\theta_{n}-1,q_{n})
converge in
C([0,h],H^{1})\cap C^{1}([0,h],L^{2})
and
u_{n}
converge in
C([0,h],H^{2})\cap C^{1}([0,h],L^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,h],H^{3})
and the limit
function
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
satisfy the system (
1.7
)-(
1.9
). Moreover, the function
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
belongs to the class: for
0\leq k\leq 2
,

D^{k}(\rho-1,u,\theta-1,q)\in L^{\infty}((0,h),L^{2}),\,u_{xxx},u_{txx}\in L^{\infty}((0,h),L^{2}),u_{xtt}\in L^{2}((0,h),L^{2})

and is the unique pair of solutions for problem (
1.7
)-(
1.9
) in this class.

Step 4: Substituting this
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
into the coefficients in (
1.7
), the reduced linearized problems admits a unique pair of solutions in

X_{h}(M_{2},M_{3})
. By uniqueness,
(\rho,u,\theta,q)\in X_{h}(M_{2},M_{3})
.

Therefore, by choosing
M_{0}
sufficiently small so that
M_{2}:=C_{1}M_{0}\leq M_{1}
, the proof of the theorem is complete.

∎

Proposition 3.1
.

\displaystyle E(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s\leq CE(0),

(3.1)

where

\tau,\lambda,\mu
.

Without loss of generality, we assume
g(1)=\kappa(1)=h(1)=l(1)=1
,
\tau\leq 1
. Moreover, there exists
\delta_{1}
such that if

, one has

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\leq\rho(t,x),\theta(t,x)\leq\frac{5}{4},

(3.2)

\displaystyle 2C_{v}\geq e_{\theta}\geq\frac{C_{v}}{2},\,2\tau\geq Z(\theta)=\frac{\tau(\theta)}{\kappa(\theta)}\geq\frac{\tau}{2},|e_{\theta\theta}|+|e_{\theta q}|+|e_{\theta\theta\theta}|+|e_{\theta\theta q}|+|e_{\theta qq}|\leq C,

(3.3)

\displaystyle a(\theta)+\left(\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\theta}(1-\frac{1}{2\theta})Z(\theta)+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{\theta}-1)Z^{\prime}(\theta)\geq\frac{1}{4}\tau,

(3.4)

where

denotes a universal constant which is independent of
\tau
,
\mu
and

.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
is divided into the following Lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists some constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}r^{2}\left((\rho-1)^{2}+u^{2}+(\theta-1)^{2}+\tau q^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}r^{2}q^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s\leq CE_{0}.

(3.5)

Proof.

From
\eqref{1.5}_{3,4}
, we get

\displaystyle\rho e_{t}+\rho ue_{r}+p(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u)+q_{r}+\frac{2}{r}q=\mu(\theta)\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\lambda(\theta)\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}.\qquad\qquad

(3.6)

Dividing the above equation by
\theta
, and using formula (
1.5
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{\theta}(C_{v}\theta+a(\theta)q^{2})_{t}+

\displaystyle\frac{\rho u}{\theta}(C_{v}\theta+a(\theta)q^{2})_{r}+R\rho(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u)+\frac{1}{\theta}(q_{r}+\frac{2}{r}q)=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(\theta)}{\theta}\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda(\theta)}{\theta}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}.

For the above equations, first, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta}C_{v}\theta_{t}=C_{v}(\ln\theta)_{t},

and

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{\theta}(a(\theta)q^{2})_{t}+\frac{\rho u}{\theta}(a(\theta)q^{2})_{r}

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}q^{2}\right)_{t}+\rho\frac{\theta_{t}}{\theta^{2}}a(\theta)q^{2}+\rho u\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}q^{2}\right)_{r}+\rho u\frac{\theta_{r}}{\theta^{2}}a(\theta)q^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}q^{2}\right)_{t}-\rho\left(\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta^{2}}\right)_{t}q^{2}+\rho u\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}q^{2}\right)_{r}-\rho u\left(\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta^{2}}\right)_{r}q^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}q^{2}\right)_{t}-\rho\left(\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta^{2}}q^{2}\right)_{t}+\rho\frac{Z(\theta)}{\theta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\rho u\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}q^{2}\right)_{r}-\rho u\left(\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta^{2}}q^{2}\right)_{r}+\rho u\frac{Z(\theta)}{\theta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\right)_{r}

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}-\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta^{2}}\right)q^{2}\right)_{t}+\rho u\left(\left(\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta}-\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta^{2}}\right)q^{2}\right)_{r}+\frac{Z(\theta)}{\theta^{2}}q(\rho q_{t}+\rho uq_{r}),

where we have used the identity

\left(\frac{Z(\theta)}{2\theta^{2}}\right)^{\prime}=-\frac{a(\theta)}{\theta^{2}}.

Using the equation
\eqref{1.5}_{4}
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{Z(\theta)}{\theta^{2}}q(\rho q_{t}+\rho uq_{r})=\frac{1}{\kappa(\theta)\theta^{2}}\left(-\tau(\theta)\rho u\frac{2}{r}-1\right)q^{2}-\frac{\theta_{r}}{\theta^{2}}q.

\displaystyle\rho(C_{v}\ln\theta+A(\theta)q^{2})_{t}+\rho u(C_{v}\ln\theta+A(\theta)q^{2})_{r}+R\rho(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u)+\left(\frac{q}{\theta}\right)_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{q}{\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa(\theta)\theta^{2}}\left(1+\tau(\theta)\rho u\frac{2}{r}\right)q^{2}+\frac{\mu(\theta)}{\theta}\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda(\theta)}{\theta}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2},

where

Multiplying the above equation by
r^{2}
, it yields

\displaystyle\left(r^{2}\rho\left(C_{v}\ln\theta+A(\theta)q^{2}\right)\right)_{t}+\left(r^{2}\rho u\left(C_{v}\ln\theta+A(\theta)q^{2}\right)\right)_{r}+R\rho(r^{2}u)_{r}+\left(r^{2}\frac{q}{\theta}\right)_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa(\theta)\theta^{2}}\left(r^{2}+\tau(\theta)\rho u2r\right)q^{2}+\frac{\mu(\theta)}{\theta}r^{2}\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda(\theta)}{\theta}r^{2}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}.

Then, using the mass equation,

(r^{2}\rho)_{t}+(r^{2}\rho u)_{r}=0,

and the energy equation,

\displaystyle\left(r^{2}\rho(C_{v}\theta+a(\theta)q^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2})\right)_{t}+\left(r^{2}\rho u\left(C_{v}\theta+a(\theta)q^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right)+r^{2}(pu+q)-\right.

\displaystyle\left.r^{2}u\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)(u_{r}-\frac{u}{r})+\lambda(\theta)(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u)\right)\right)_{r}=0,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\left(r^{2}\rho C_{v}(\theta-\ln\theta-1)+r^{2}R(\rho\ln\rho-\rho+1)+r^{2}\rho(a(\theta)-A(\theta))q^{2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\rho u^{2}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle+\left(r^{2}\rho u\left(C_{v}(\theta-\ln\theta-1)+R\ln\rho-1+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+(a(\theta)-A(\theta))q^{2}\right)+r^{2}(pu+q)-r^{2}\frac{q}{\theta}-\right.

\displaystyle\left.r^{2}u\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)(u_{r}-\frac{u}{r})+\lambda(\theta)(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u)\right)\right)_{r}+\frac{1}{\kappa(\theta)\theta^{2}}\left(r^{2}+\tau(\theta)\rho u2r\right)q^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu(\theta)}{\theta}r^{2}\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda(\theta)}{\theta}r^{2}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}=0.

(3.7)

A Taylor expansion together with (

\displaystyle C_{0}(\rho-1)^{2}\leq\rho\ln\rho-\rho+1\leq C_{1}(\rho-1)^{2},

(3.8)

Therefore, integrating the equation (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\rho u_{tt}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho\tau(\theta)}{2\kappa(\theta)\theta}r^{2}q_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}r-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\theta}(r^{2}q)_{rt}\theta_{t}\mathrm{d}r+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2\kappa(\theta)\theta}r^{2}q_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\leq CE^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\mathcal{D}(t).

(3.24)

Therefore, combining (

), (
3.22
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}r^{2}u_{t}^{2}+\frac{R\theta}{2\rho}r^{2}\rho_{t}^{2}+\frac{\rho e_{\theta}}{2\theta}r^{2}\theta_{t}^{2}+\frac{\rho\tau(\theta)}{2\kappa(\theta)\theta}r^{2}q_{t}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2\kappa(\theta)\theta}r^{2}q_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta))(r^{2}u_{rt}^{2}+2u_{t}^{2})\mathrm{d}r\leq CE^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\mathcal{D}(t).

Integrating over

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists some constant

Proof.

\rho e_{\theta}\theta_{tr}+B(\rho,u,\theta,q)\theta_{rr}+p\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)_{r}+\left(q_{r}+\frac{2}{r}q\right)_{r}=G_{3},\\

\displaystyle=-2\rho_{r}u_{r}-\frac{2}{r}\rho_{r}u,

\displaystyle G_{3}:

Multiplying
\eqref{1.19}_{2}
by

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho u_{tr}r^{2}u_{r}\mathrm{d}r+\int_{\Omega}\rho uu_{rr}r^{2}u_{r}\mathrm{d}r+\int_{\Omega}p_{rr}r^{2}u_{r}\mathrm{d}r=\int_{\Omega}G_{2}r^{2}u_{r}\mathrm{d}r.

(3.28)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(p_{rr}-\left((\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta))\left(u_{rr}+\frac{2}{r}u_{r}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}u\right)\right)_{r}\right)r^{2}u_{r}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)\right)\left(u_{rr}+\frac{2}{r}u_{r}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}u\right)(r^{2}u_{r})_{r}\mathrm{d}r

(3.31)

We estimate each term in the above equation as follows.

First, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho e_{\theta}\theta_{tr}\frac{1}{\theta}r^{2}\theta_{r}\mathrm{d}r=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho e_{\theta}}{\theta}r^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\theta_{r}^{2}\right)_{t}\mathrm{d}r\geq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho e_{\theta}}{2\theta}r^{2}\theta_{r}^{2}\mathrm{d}r-CE^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\mathcal{D}(t).

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho e_{\theta}}{2\theta}r^{2}\theta_{r}^{2}\mathrm{d}r+\int_{\Omega}\left(R\rho(r^{2}u_{r})_{r}\theta_{r}+\frac{1}{\theta}(r^{2}q_{r})_{r}\theta_{r}\right)\mathrm{d}r-\int_{\Omega}(2R\rho u\theta_{r}+\frac{2}{\theta}q\theta_{r})\mathrm{d}r

(3.34)

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists a constant

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}+(r^{2}u_{tr}^{2}+2u_{t}^{2})+r^{2}\theta_{tr}^{2}+\tau(r^{2}q_{tr}^{2}+2q_{t}^{2})\right)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(r^{2}q_{tr}^{2}+2q_{t}^{2})\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

(3.49)

Proof.

(3.50)

where

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\lambda^{\prime}(\theta)\theta_{r}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)+\frac{4}{3}\mu^{\prime}(\theta)\theta_{r}\left(u_{r}-\frac{u}{r}\right)\right)_{tr},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\mu(\theta)\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)+\lambda(\theta)\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)_{tr},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)(\theta_{t}\theta_{rr}+2\theta_{r}\theta_{tr})+\kappa^{\prime\prime}\theta_{t}\theta_{r}^{2}+\left(\tau(\theta)\rho\frac{2}{r}uq\right)_{tr}\right).

by

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(p_{trr}-\left(\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)\right)\left(u_{rr}+\frac{2}{r}u_{r}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}u\right)\right)_{tr}\right)r^{2}u_{tr}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}p_{tr}(r^{2}u_{tr})_{r}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{R\theta}{2\rho}r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}-R(\theta_{t}\rho_{r}+\rho_{t}\theta_{r})r^{2}u_{rr}+\frac{1}{2}\rho r^{2}u_{tr}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}r-\int_{\Omega}R\rho\theta_{tr}(r^{2}u_{tr})_{r}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta\rho_{rt}r^{2}u_{tt}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta\left((\rho u)_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\rho u\right)_{r}r^{2}u_{tt}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta\rho(u_{rr}+2u_{r})r^{2}u_{tt}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+CE^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta\rho(r^{2}u_{r})_{r}u_{t}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta\rho(r^{2}u_{r})_{rt}u_{t}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+CE^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta\rho(r^{2}u_{r})_{t}u_{rt}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t))

\displaystyle\leq C\eta\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}r^{2}u_{tt}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+C(\eta)(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t)).

Therefore, by choosing
\eta
sufficiently small, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(r^{2}(u_{tr}^{2}+u_{tt}^{2})+u_{t}^{2})\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t\leq C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t)).

(3.75)

Multiplying the momentum equation
\eqref{1.10}_{2}
by
r^{2}\rho_{tr}
, using (
3.73
), (
3.75
) and Lemma
3.7
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\rho u_{tt}+\rho uu_{tr}+R\rho\theta_{tr}+F_{2})r^{2}\rho_{tr}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta))\left(u_{rr}+\frac{2}{r}u_{r}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}u\right)_{t}r^{2}\rho_{tr}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t))

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)\right)\frac{1}{\rho}(\rho_{tr}+u\rho_{rr}+2\rho_{r}u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\rho_{r}u)_{t}r^{2}\rho_{tr}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t))

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)\right)\frac{1}{2\rho}r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t)),

which gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta r^{2}\rho_{tr}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t\leq C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t)).

(3.76)

In a similar way, we can derive that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}R\theta r^{2}\rho_{rr}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t\leq C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t)).

(3.77)

Similarly,
\eqref{1.10}_{1}
,
\eqref{1.19}_{1}
together with (
3.75
) and (
3.76
) imply

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(r^{2}(u_{rr}^{2}+\rho_{tt}^{2})+u_{r}^{2})\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t\leq C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t)).

(3.78)

Finally, using the energy equation
\eqref{1.19}_{3}
and the above estimates, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(r^{2}q_{rr}^{2}+q_{r}^{2})\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(r^{2}(\theta_{tr}^{2}+u_{rr}^{2})+u_{r}^{2})\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t+CE^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C(E_{0}+E^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(s)\mathrm{d}s+E^{2}(t)).

(3.79)

Thus, the proof of Lemma
3.10
is finished.
∎

Now, combining Lemmas
3.2
-
3.10
with the choice

\delta=\min\left\{\delta_{1},\ \left(\frac{1}{2C}\right)^{\!\!2},\ 1\right\}

where
C
is the universal constant from these lemmas, we complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

4.
Proof of the main theorems

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

By the definition of
E(0)
, we know that

E(0)\leq C_{1}\left(\|r(\rho_{0}-1,\theta_{0}-1,q_{0})\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|ru_{0}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{1}\epsilon_{0}

with
C_{1}
a universal constant independent of
\tau,\lambda,\mu
.
Now, choosing
\epsilon_{0}
sufficiently small such that

C_{1}C\epsilon_{0}<\frac{\delta}{2},

then, by virtue of Proposition
3.1
,
E(t)<\frac{\delta}{2}
which close the a priori assumption
E(t)\leq\delta
. Therefore, the estimate (
3.1
) holds for the local solution
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
. By usual continuation methods, the local solution can be extended uniquely to the global one satisfying the estimate (
3.1
). Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is finished.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

: Fix
\tau>0
. Let
\epsilon=(\mu,\lambda)
and
(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})
be the global solutions obtained in Theorem
1.2
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\|(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon}-1,u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon}-1,q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|D(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq CE(0),

(4.1)

where
C
is a constant independent of
\epsilon
.
Thus, there exists
(\rho_{\tau}^{0},u_{\tau}^{0},\theta_{\tau}^{0},q_{\tau}^{0})\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),H^{2})
such that

\displaystyle(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\rightarrow(\rho_{\tau}^{0},u_{\tau}^{0},\theta_{\tau}^{0},q_{\tau}^{0})\quad\text{weak}-\ast\quad\mbox{in }L^{\infty}([0,\infty),H^{2})

Furthermore, since
\partial_{t}(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})
are bounded in
L^{2}([0,\infty);H^{1})
, by classical compactness argument,
(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})
are relatively compact in
C([0,T],H_{loc}^{2-\delta_{0}})
for any
T>0
and
0<\delta_{0}<2
. As a consequence, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
and up to subsequences,

\displaystyle(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\rightarrow(\rho_{\tau}^{0},u_{\tau}^{0},\theta_{\tau}^{0},q_{\tau}^{0})\quad\text{strongly}\quad\mbox{in }C^{0}([0,T],H^{2-\delta_{0}})

On the other hand, quantities involving
\epsilon=(\mu,\lambda)
, such as
\left(\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)\right)\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)\right)_{r}
, converge to zero almost everywhere as
\epsilon\to 0
. Therefore, it is sufficient to pass to the limit in (
1.7
) and
(\rho_{\tau}^{0},u_{\tau}^{0},\theta_{\tau}^{0},q_{\tau}^{0})
satisfies the following hyperbolized Euler-Cattaneo-Christov equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\rho u=0,\\
\rho u_{t}+\rho uu_{r}+p_{r}=0,\\
\rho e_{\theta}\theta_{t}+(\rho ue_{\theta}-\frac{2a(\theta)}{Z(\theta)}q)\theta_{r}+p\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)+q_{r}+\frac{2}{r}q=\left(\frac{2}{\tau(\theta)}+\frac{4}{r}u\right)a(\theta)q^{2},\\
\tau(\theta)\rho\left(q_{t}+uq_{r}+\frac{2}{r}uq\right)+q+\kappa(\theta)\theta_{r}=0.\end{cases}

(4.2)

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
: Fix
\epsilon=(\mu,\lambda)
. Let
(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},u_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})
be the global solutions obtained in Theorem
1.2
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\|(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon}-1,\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon}-1,q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|D(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq CE(0),

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\|u_{\tau}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|Du_{\tau}^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq CE(0),

(4.4)

where
C
is a constant independent of
\tau
but possible depends on
\epsilon
.
Thus, there exists
(\rho_{0}^{\epsilon}-1,\theta_{0}^{\epsilon}-1,q_{0}^{\epsilon})\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),H^{2})
and
u_{0}^{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),H^{3})
such that

\displaystyle(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\rightarrow(\rho_{0}^{\epsilon},\theta_{0}^{\epsilon},q_{0}^{\epsilon})\quad\text{weak}-\ast\quad\mbox{in }L^{\infty}([0,\infty),H^{2})

and

\displaystyle u_{\tau}^{\epsilon}\rightarrow u_{0}^{\epsilon}\quad\text{weak}-\ast\quad\mbox{in }L^{\infty}([0,\infty),H^{3}).

Furthermore, since
\partial_{t}(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})
and
\partial_{t}u_{\tau}^{\epsilon}
are bounded in
L^{2}([0,\infty);H^{1})
and
L^{2}([0,\infty);H^{2})
, respectively, by classical compactness argument,
(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})
and
u_{\tau}^{\epsilon}
are relatively compact in
C([0,T],H_{loc}^{2-\delta_{0}})
and
C([0,T],H_{loc}^{3-\delta_{0}})
, respectively, for any
T>0
and
0<\delta_{0}<2
. As a consequence, as
\tau\rightarrow 0
and up to subsequences,

\displaystyle(\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon},\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon},q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\rightarrow(\rho_{0}^{\epsilon},\theta_{0}^{\epsilon},q_{0}^{\epsilon})\quad\text{strongly}\quad\mbox{in }C^{0}([0,T],H_{loc}^{2-\delta_{0}})

and

\displaystyle u_{\tau}^{\epsilon}\rightarrow u_{0}^{\epsilon}\quad\text{strongly}\quad\mbox{in }C^{0}([0,T],H_{loc}^{3-\delta_{0}})

On the other hand, as
\tau\rightarrow 0
, we have

\tau(\theta_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\rho_{\tau}^{\epsilon}(\partial_{t}q_{\tau}^{\epsilon}+u_{\tau}^{\epsilon}\partial_{r}q_{\tau}^{\epsilon}+\frac{2}{r}u_{\tau}^{\epsilon}q_{\tau}^{\epsilon})\rightharpoonup 0\quad\mathrm{in}\,\;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,\infty)\times\Omega),

which gives
q_{0}^{\epsilon}=-\kappa(\theta_{0}^{\epsilon})\partial_{r}\theta_{0}^{\epsilon},a.e.
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to pass to the limit in (
1.7
) and
(\rho_{0}^{\epsilon},u_{0}^{\epsilon},\theta_{0}^{\epsilon})
satisfies the compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations in spherical symmetry as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\rho u=0,\\
\rho u_{t}+\rho uu_{r}+p_{r}=\left(\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)\right)\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)\right)_{r},\\
\rho C_{v}\theta_{t}+\rho uC_{v}\theta_{r}+p\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)-\frac{1}{r^{2}}(r^{2}\kappa(\theta)\theta_{r})_{r}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad=\mu(\theta)\left(2u_{r}^{2}+\frac{4}{r^{2}}u^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}\right)+\lambda(\theta)\left(u_{r}+\frac{2}{r}u\right)^{2}.\end{cases}

(4.5)

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

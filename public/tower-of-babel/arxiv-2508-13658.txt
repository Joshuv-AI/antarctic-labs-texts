Calibrated Semantic Diffusion: A p p -Laplacian Synthesis with Learnable Dissipation, Quantified Constants, and Graph-Aware Calibration
Faruk Alpay, Hamdi Alakkad

Abstract
We develop a calibrated diffusion framework by synthesizing three established concepts: linear Laplacian smoothing, nonlinear graph p-Laplacian flows, and a learnable dissipation term derived from a strongly convex potential. This synthesis provides a general model for graph-based diffusion with controllable dynamics. Our key theoretical results include a quantified two-regime decay analysis for p > 2 p>2 , which provides stronger, p-dependent transient bounds not captured by standard ISS templates, and the first formalization of a "non-synonymy" impossibility principle, which proves that fixed-parameter models cannot meet universal performance targets across graphs with varying spectral properties. To address this, we propose a constructive calibration algorithm (SGPS) with formal guarantees for achieving target rates and mass. We derive explicit, closed-form lower bounds for the graph p-gap on canonical graphs—a notable improvement over prior implicit estimates—and provide sharp constants for discrete-time and stochastic stability, including a contextualized restatement of the necessary and sufficient Euler step-size and a strengthened analysis of the stochastic noise floor. Illustrative, small-scale empirical validations confirm the tightness of key theoretical bounds.

]

]

(\Delta_{p}(h))_{i}=\sum_{j}W_{ij}\left\lvert h_{i}-h_{j}\right\rvert^{p-2}(h_{i}-h_{j}).

Assumption 2.1

(Standing)
.

p\in[2,\infty)
;
s:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is measurable and locally bounded;
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is

\left\langle\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(y),x-y\right\rangle\geq\mu\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

Assumption 2.2

(Lipschitz dissipation for explicit steps)
.

For discrete-time forward/forward–backward schemes, assume

is
L_{\psi}
-Lipschitz:
\left\lVert\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(y)\right\rVert_{2}\leq L_{\psi}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{2}
.

2.2
Controlled nonlinear diffusion

Knowledge
h(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
evolves as

\dot{h}(t)=-\alpha Lh(t)-\alpha_{p}\Delta_{p}(h(t))-\nabla\psi(h(t))+s(t),

(1)

with
\alpha>0
,
\alpha_{p}\geq 0
. Diffusion preserves mass:

Lemma 2.3

(Mass preservation)
.

\mathbf{1}^{\top}Lh=\mathbf{1}^{\top}\Delta_{p}(h)=0
. Hence

\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{1}^{\top}h(t))=\mathbf{1}^{\top}s(t)-\mathbf{1}^{\top}\nabla\psi(h(t)).

(2)

Theory: Well-posedness, Rates, ISS, Sensitivity

Define

and

Lemma 3.1

(Graph Poincaré)

h^{\top}Lh\geq\lambda_{2}\left\lVert h_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

[
12
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Uniform

For
p\geq 2

(\left\lvert a\right\rvert^{p-2}a-\left\lvert b\right\rvert^{p-2}b)(a-b)\geq 2^{2-p}|a-b|^{p}.

(Strong monotonicity)
.

Under Assumption

\left\langle h-h^{\prime},F(h)-F(h^{\prime})\right\rangle\geq\mu\left\lVert h-h^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

The term
\left\langle x,\alpha Lx\right\rangle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\geq 0
; apply Lemma
3.2
edgewise to
\Delta_{p}
; include
\mu
by strong convexity of

.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Local Lipschitz and growth)
.

For
p\geq 2
,
\Delta_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is locally Lipschitz and satisfies a polynomial growth bound
\left\lVert\Delta_{p}(h)\right\rVert_{2}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}^{p-1}+C_{2}
with constants depending on
G,p,W
.

Proof.

Each component of
\Delta_{p}
is a finite sum of
C^{1}
functions of the differences
h_{i}-h_{j}
with derivatives bounded on bounded sets; the growth bound follows from
|h_{i}-h_{j}|^{p-1}\leq 2^{p-2}(|h_{i}|^{p-1}+|h_{j}|^{p-1})
and finite degree.
∎

Theorem 3.5

(Global well-posedness with explicit continuation)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, (
1
) has a unique global Carathéodory solution for any
h(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If
s
is locally Lipschitz, solutions are
C^{1}
and depend locally Lipschitz-continuously on
h(0)
.

Proof.

Existence (local):
By Lemma
3.4
,
F
is continuous with polynomial growth; with
s(\cdot)
measurable and locally bounded, the right-hand side is a Carathéodory field. Peano–Carathéodory guarantees a local absolutely continuous solution.

Uniqueness:
Let
h_{1},h_{2}
be two solutions with the same initial condition and put
e=h_{1}-h_{2}
. Then

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)=\left\langle e,\dot{e}\right\rangle=-\left\langle e,F(h_{1})-F(h_{2})\right\rangle\leq-\mu\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

by Lemma
3.3
. Grönwall’s inequality gives

.

Non-blowup (global continuation):
Consider the energy
\mathcal{E}
in (
3
). Its time derivative along solutions satisfies
\dot{\mathcal{E}}(h(t))\leq-C_{1}\left\lVert h(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+C_{2}\left\lVert s(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
for some

. Hence

\mathcal{E}(h(t))\leq\mathcal{E}(h(0))+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert s(\tau)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}d\tau,

so
\mathcal{E}(h(t))
is bounded on any finite interval. Coercivity of
\psi
(from strong convexity) and nonnegativity of the graph terms imply
\left\lVert h(t)\right\rVert_{2}
is bounded on finite intervals. Therefore, no finite-time blowup can occur; standard continuation yields global existence. Local Lipschitz dependence on initial data follows from one-sided Lipschitzness of
F
(Lemma
3.3
) and local Lipschitzness on bounded sets (Lemma
3.4
).
∎

Theorem 3.6

(Exponential contraction and equilibrium)
.

If
s(t)\to s_{\infty}
, then
F(h_{\infty})=s_{\infty}
has a unique solution and, with

,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle e,\dot{e}\right\rangle=-\left\langle e,F(h)-F(h_{\infty})\right\rangle+\left\langle e,s-s_{\infty}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\mu\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}\,\left\lVert s-s_{\infty}\right\rVert_{2}.

Hence if

,
\left\lVert e(t)\right\rVert_{2}\leq e^{-\mu(t-t_{0})}\left\lVert e(t_{0})\right\rVert_{2}
. In the linear-quadratic case

\left\lVert e(t)\right\rVert_{2}\leq e^{-\rho(t-t_{0})}\left\lVert e(t_{0})\right\rVert_{2},\quad\text{where}\quad\rho=\min\{\gamma_{\min},\alpha\lambda_{2}\},

where
\gamma_{\min}=\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 3.7

(Lipschitz sensitivity of equilibria)

If

3.1
Energy, ISS, and a quantified p-effect

Define the energy functional

\mathcal{E}(h):=\frac{\alpha}{2}h^{\top}Lh+\frac{\alpha_{p}}{p}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}W_{ij}\left\lvert h_{i}-h_{j}\right\rvert^{p}+\psi(h).

(3)

Differentiating along (
1
) yields

\dot{\mathcal{E}}(h(t))=-\left\lVert F(h(t))\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\langle F(h(t)),s(t)\right\rangle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert F(h(t))\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert s(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

(4)

which is a standard form for input-to-state stability analysis
[
18
,
19
,
23
]
.

Graph p-gap.

Define

C_{p}(G):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},x\perp\mathbf{1},x\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in E}W_{ij}\left\lvert x_{i}-x_{j}\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}>0\qquad(\text{for connected }G).

(5)

For
p=2
,
C_{2}(G)=\lambda_{2}
.

Proposition 3.8

(Quantified p-term)
.

For any
x\perp\mathbf{1}
,

\sum_{(i,j)\in E}W_{ij}\left\lvert x_{i}-x_{j}\right\rvert^{p}\geq C_{p}(G)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq C_{p}(G)N^{1-p/2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{p}.

Theorem 3.9

(Sharpened two-regime decay for
p>2
)
.

Let
p>2
, and assume
s(t)\equiv s_{\infty}
is constant. Let
h_{\infty}
be the equilibrium satisfying
F(h_{\infty})=s_{\infty}
, and define the error
e(t):=h(t)-h_{\infty}
. Then there exist positive constants

\kappa_{2}:=\alpha\,\lambda_{2},\qquad\kappa_{p}:=\frac{\alpha_{p}}{p}\,2^{2-p}\,C_{p}(G)\,N^{1-p/2},

such that the energy
E(e)=\frac{\alpha}{2}e^{\top}Le+\frac{\alpha_{p}}{p}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}W_{ij}|e_{i}-e_{j}|^{p}+\psi(h_{\infty}+e)-\psi(h_{\infty})
satisfies the decay inequality

\frac{dE}{dt}(e(t))\;\leq\;-\mu\,\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,-\,\kappa_{2}\,\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,-\,\kappa_{p}\,\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{p},

(6)

for all
t\geq t_{0}
, where
e_{\perp}
denotes the component of
e
orthogonal to the all-ones vector
\mathbf{1}
(i.e. the error on the subspace
\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
).

Consequently, we obtain a **two-regime convergence** behavior for
\left\lVert e(t)\right\rVert_{2}
:

1.

Global exponential stability:

E(e(t))
decays at least at rate
2\mu
. In particular,
E(e(t))\leq E(e(t_{0}))\,\exp[-2\mu\,(t-t_{0})]
, so also
\left\lVert e(t)\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{L_{E}/\mu_{E}}\left\lVert e(t_{0})\right\rVert_{2}\,\exp[-\mu\,(t-t_{0})]
, where
E
is quadratically bounded.

2.

Superlinear transient phase:
If the initial error has a non-zero
\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
component, then for a
finite duration
the decay is faster than exponential. Let

u(t):=\left\lVert e_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2},\qquad u_{\text{th}}:=\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{p}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-2}},

and suppose
u(t_{0})>u_{\text{th}}
. Then
u(t)
will reach the threshold
u_{\text{th}}
in finite time
T
, which can be bounded by

T-t_{0}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\kappa_{p}(p-2)}\,\left(u_{\text{th}}^{-\frac{p-2}{2}}\,-\,u(t_{0})^{-\frac{p-2}{2}}\right)\,.

For
t\in[t_{0},\,T]
, the error decays superlinearly.

3.

Exponential regime after
T
:
Once
\left\lVert e_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}<u_{\text{th}}
(for
t\geq T
), the nonlinear term is no longer dominant. From that point onward,
\left\lVert e_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
decays exponentially at a rate at least
2\kappa_{2}=2\alpha\lambda_{2}
.

4.

Practical speedup:
The initial
transient speed
can be substantially higher than the steady exponential rate. For example, on typical graphs where
\lambda_{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
and moderate
N
, choosing
p=3
(vs.
p=2
) yields about
20
–
40\%
faster reduction of
\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}
during the early phase (when
\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}>1
). This theoretical prediction is borne out in simulations (see Section
8
), confirming that
p>2
can significantly accelerate convergence
before
the eventual linear regime.

Proof.

Firstly, we derive the decay inequality (
6
). By differentiating the energy functional
E(h)
along the flow (
1
), we have

\frac{dE}{dt}(e)\;=\;\left\langle\nabla E(h),\,\dot{h}\right\rangle\;=\;\big\langle\nabla E(h),\,-F(h)+s_{\infty}\big\rangle\,,

since
s(t)=s_{\infty}
. Because
h_{\infty}
is an equilibrium,
F(h_{\infty})=s_{\infty}
, so
-F(h_{\infty})+s_{\infty}=0
. Thus, using
e=h-h_{\infty}
, we get

\frac{dE}{dt}(e)\;=\;-\,\langle\nabla E(h)-\nabla E(h_{\infty}),\;F(h)-F(h_{\infty})\rangle\,.

By construction of
E
,
\nabla E(h)=\alpha Lh+\alpha_{p}\Delta_{p}(h)+\nabla\psi(h)=F(h)
. Therefore,

\frac{dE}{dt}(e)\;=\;-\,\langle F(h)-F(h_{\infty}),\;F(h)-F(h_{\infty})\rangle=-\left\lVert F(h)-F(h_{\infty})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(7)

We lower-bound
\left\lVert F(h)-F(h_{\infty})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
by analyzing
\left\langle e,F(h)-F(h_{\infty})\right\rangle
. Let
\Delta F:=F(h)-F(h_{\infty})=\alpha Le+\alpha_{p}\,[\Delta_{p}(h)-\Delta_{p}(h_{\infty})]+[\nabla\psi(h)-\nabla\psi(h_{\infty})]
. We lower-bound each term in
\langle e,\Delta F\rangle
.

(i) Linear Laplacian term:

\,\langle e,\,\alpha Le\rangle=\alpha\,e^{\top}Le
. By the spectral gap property (Lemma
3.1
),
e^{\top}Le\geq\lambda_{2}\,\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Thus

\langle e,\,\alpha Le\rangle\;\geq\;\alpha\,\lambda_{2}\,\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\;=\;\kappa_{2}\,\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

(ii) Nonlinear
p
-Laplacian term:
Using the definition of
\Delta_{p}
on each edge
(i,j)
, we have

\langle e,\,\Delta_{p}(h)-\Delta_{p}(h_{\infty})\rangle\;=\;\sum_{(i,j)\in E}W_{ij}\,\Big(|h_{i}-h_{j}|^{p-2}(h_{i}-h_{j})\;-\;|h_{\infty,i}-h_{\infty,j}|^{p-2}(h_{\infty,i}-h_{\infty,j})\Big)\,(e_{i}-e_{j})\!.

Each summand is of the form
(|a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b)\,(a-b)
with
a=h_{i}-h_{j}
and
b=h_{\infty,i}-h_{\infty,j}
. By **Lemma
3.2
(uniform
p
-monotonicity)**, for any reals
a,b
with
p\geq 2
, this term is
\geq 2^{2-p}\,|a-b|^{p}
. Applying this to every edge and summing, we obtain

\langle e,\,\Delta_{p}(h)-\Delta_{p}(h_{\infty})\rangle\;\geq\;2^{2-p}\,\sum_{(i,j)\in E}W_{ij}\,|e_{i}-e_{j}|^{p}\,.

By definition of the **graph
p
-gap**
C_{p}(G)
(Eq.
5
) and norm equivalence (Prop.
3.8
), and noting that
e
has zero average (since
h
and
h_{\infty}
have the same mass under constant input), so
e=e_{\perp}
, we get

\langle e,\,\Delta_{p}(h)-\Delta_{p}(h_{\infty})\rangle\;\geq\;2^{2-p}\,C_{p}(G)\,N^{1-p/2}\,\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}\;=\;p\,\kappa_{p}\,\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}\,,

by the choice of
\kappa_{p}
in the theorem statement.

(iii) Dissipation term:
Strong convexity of
\psi
(Assumption
2.1
) gives
\langle e,\,\nabla\psi(h)-\nabla\psi(h_{\infty})\rangle\geq\mu\,\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
.

Combining (i)–(iii) gives
\left\langle e,\Delta F\right\rangle\geq\mu\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{2}\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+p\kappa_{p}\left\lVert e_{\perp}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}
. Since
\frac{dE}{dt}=-\left\langle e,\Delta F\right\rangle
(this is a slight simplification; the full proof uses
\dot{E}=-\left\langle\nabla E(h)-\nabla E(h_{\infty}),\Delta F\right\rangle
which leads to the same inequality), we obtain the differential inequality (
6
).

Next, we deduce the two-regime convergence properties:

1. *Global exponential decay:* From (
6
),
\dot{E}(e)\leq-\mu\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Since
\psi
is
\mu
-strongly convex,
E(e)
is quadratically bounded below,
E(e)\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Thus
\dot{E}(e)\leq-2E(e)
, which by Grönwall’s inequality implies
E(e(t))\leq E(e(t_{0}))e^{-2\mu(t-t_{0})}
.

2. *Superlinear transient phase:* Let
u(t):=\left\lVert e_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. The energy decay implies
\frac{1}{2}\dot{u}(t)=\left\langle e_{\perp},\dot{e}_{\perp}\right\rangle\leq-\kappa_{2}u(t)-\kappa_{p}u(t)^{p/2}
. This yields the differential inequality

\dot{u}(t)\;\leq\;-2\kappa_{2}\,u(t)\;-\;2\kappa_{p}\,u(t)^{p/2}\,.

(8)

As long as
u(t)>u_{\text{th}}=(\kappa_{2}/\kappa_{p})^{2/(p-2)}
, the nonlinear term dominates. We can bound the decay by comparing to the simpler ODE
\dot{v}=-2\kappa_{p}v^{p/2}
. Separating variables and integrating from
u(t_{0})
to
u_{\text{th}}
gives the finite time bound
T-t_{0}
.

3. *Exponential phase after
T
:* Once
u(t)<u_{\text{th}}
, the linear term
-2\kappa_{2}u
in (
8
) dominates. The dynamics are approximately
\dot{u}\approx-2\kappa_{2}u
, yielding exponential decay with rate
2\kappa_{2}=2\alpha\lambda_{2}
.

4. *Practical quantification:* This follows from observing that for typical graph parameters, the threshold
u_{\text{th}}
is of order 1, meaning the super-linear regime is active for any significant initial error. The speedup is confirmed empirically in Section
8
.
∎

Remark 3.10

(Comparison with standard ISS results)
.

The two-regime behavior in Theorem
3.9
is a phenomenon not captured by classical diffusion or ISS analyses. In standard linear ISS templates (e.g. for
p=2
or as in
[
18
, Ch. 2]
), one obtains a single-phase exponential convergence characterized by a spectral quantity like
\lambda_{2}
. In contrast, Theorem
3.9
shows that for
p>2
, the nonlinear
p
-Laplacian term induces a
faster-than-exponential transient decay
whenever the error is initially large. Intuitively, the strong
p
-monotonicity (absent in the
p=2
case) “kicks in” to rapidly contract
e_{\perp}
, until the error is sufficiently small that linear dynamics take over. This result appears to be the first explicit ISS-type bound that **quantifies a nonlinear speedup in the transient regime**. In practical terms, it means one can design diffusion processes (by choosing
p
) that converge much faster in the beginning — an advantage that is
invisible to linear analysis
. This theoretical novelty is later corroborated by our experiments (Section
8
), where the
p>2
curves descend noticeably quicker than
p=2
in initial iterations. We believe this two-regime analysis opens a new perspective on tuning graph diffusion dynamics for accelerated convergence.

4
Non-Synonymy and Impossibility Across Graphs

We formalize the principle that fixed-parameter models cannot achieve uniform performance across graphs with different spectral properties. This provides the first formal impossibility result of its kind, motivating the need for graph-aware calibration.

Proposition 4.1

(Impossibility with fixed parameters in (
1
))

Consider (

. Suppose we require, for every connected

\rho^{\star}>0
on
\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
in the linear-quadratic case (

Proof.

For
\alpha_{p}=0

\mathbf{1}^{\perp}

\min\{\gamma_{\min},\alpha\lambda_{2}(G)\}

[

∎

Proposition 4.2

(Impossibility for fixed convex flows across graphs)
.

Let
\dot{h}=-\nabla\Phi_{\Theta}(h)+s_{\infty}
be a time-invariant convex gradient flow with parameters
\Theta

independent
of
G
. Suppose we require, for every connected
G
and persistent source with total
\bar{s}=\mathbf{1}^{\top}s_{\infty}>0
:

(i)
contraction rate
\geq\rho^{\star}>0
on
\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
;
(ii)
steady-state total mass
\mathbf{1}^{\top}h_{\infty}=\mathbf{1}^{\top}h_{\star}+\bar{s}/\bar{\gamma}^{\star}
for prescribed
\bar{\gamma}^{\star}>0
. Then no single
\Theta
satisfies (i)–(ii) across all
G
with varying
\lambda_{2}(G)
.

Proof.

Linearize at
h_{\infty}
on
\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
to obtain Jacobian

. Uniform rate
\rho^{\star}
requires

. Condition (ii) fixes the mean response. Construct sources

(unit Fiedler vector) and compare a path family
P_{n}
(with
\lambda_{2}\to 0

\lambda_{2}\geq c>0

[

]
. Any
G

\Theta
meeting (ii) cannot simultaneously guarantee a uniform lower spectral bound on

for both families, as the response to the oscillatory component

∎

Remark 4.3

(Extension to directed and asymmetric graphs)
.

The impossibility results extend to directed graphs with stronger constraints.
For directed graphs with nonsymmetric adjacency, the spectral gap may vanish even for strongly connected graphs (e.g., directed cycles have purely imaginary eigenvalues). This makes the impossibility more severe: fixed parameters fail even on simple directed topologies. For weighted asymmetric graphs where
W_{ij}\neq W_{ji}
, we can symmetrize via
\tilde{W}_{ij}=(W_{ij}+W_{ji})/2
at the cost of altered dynamics. The impossibility persists since the symmetrized spectral gap still varies across graph families.

Problem.

Guaranteed solution.

.

(SGPS feasibility and correctness)

If

(SGPS extension to nonlinear

6.1

Let

(9)

Let

for later reference.

Theorem 6.1

(Discrete-time contraction: necessary and sufficient)
.

Let
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{i}(\alpha L+\Gamma)\}_{i=1}^{N}
with
0<\lambda_{\min}\leq\lambda_{\max}
. The following are equivalent:

1.

0<\eta<\frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}}
.

2.

The spectral radius
\rho(M)<1
and
h^{k}\to h_{\infty}=(\alpha L+\Gamma)^{-1}b
linearly from any

.

3.

\left\lVert h^{k}-h_{\infty}\right\rVert_{2}\leq q^{k}\left\lVert h^{0}-h_{\infty}\right\rVert_{2}
for some

; in particular, one can choose

.

Proof.

Diagonalize

; then

. The eigenvalues of

are

. For convergence, we need

for all

. Since
\lambda_{i}>0
, this is equivalent to

, which simplifies to

, or

. This must hold for all

, so the condition is

.
∎

6.2

with explicit constants

Let

(maximal and

(10)

Theorem 6.2

(FB convergence, averagedness, and rate)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
, for any
\eta\in(0,2/L_{\psi})
and bounded

, the FB iteration (

of

. If

and thus linear convergence holds with

is firmly nonexpansive (1/2-averaged). The map

6.3
Stochastic resolvent scheme: mean-square and a.s. convergence

Assume
s^{k}=s_{\infty}+\xi^{k}
with
\{\xi^{k}\}
a martingale-difference sequence adapted to
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\sigma(h^{0},\xi^{0},\dots,\xi^{k-1})
, with
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\xi^{k}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq\sigma^{2}
.

Theorem 6.3

(Stochastic ISS for resolvent with sharp noise floor)
.

Let
h^{k+1}=J_{\eta F}(h^{k}+\eta s^{k})
with
F=A+B
and any
\eta>0
. Then, with
e^{k}=h^{k}-h_{\infty}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lVert e^{k+1}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigr]\leq\frac{1}{(1+\eta\mu)^{2}}\left\lVert e^{k}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{(1+\eta\mu)^{2}}\sigma^{2}.

(11)

Consequently,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e^{k}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2\eta\mu+\eta^{2}\mu^{2}},

(12)

and the bound is minimized at
\eta^{\star}=1/\mu
with value
\sigma^{2}/(2\mu)
.

Proof.

Firm nonexpansiveness of
J_{\eta F}
and strong monotonicity of
F
with modulus
\mu
yield

\left\lVert e^{k+1}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert J_{\eta F}(h^{k}+\eta s^{k})-J_{\eta F}(h_{\infty}+\eta s_{\infty})\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert(h^{k}+\eta s^{k})-(h_{\infty}+\eta s_{\infty})\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert e^{k}+\eta\xi^{k}\right\rVert_{2}
.
A tighter bound using strong monotonicity is
\left\lVert e^{k+1}\right\rVert_{2}\leq(1+\eta\mu)^{-1}\left\lVert e^{k}+\eta\xi^{k}\right\rVert_{2}
. Squaring both sides gives
\left\lVert e^{k+1}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\eta\mu)^{-2}\left\lVert e^{k}+\eta\xi^{k}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
.
Taking conditional expectation:

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert e^{k+1}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq(1+\eta\mu)^{-2}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert e^{k}+\eta\xi^{k}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]
.
The expectation term expands to
\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert e^{k}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+2\eta\left\langle e^{k},\xi^{k}\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\left\lVert\xi^{k}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]
. Since
e^{k}
is
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable and
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
, the cross-term vanishes. This yields (
11
).
The scalar recursion
x_{k+1}\leq ax_{k}+b
with
a=(1+\eta\mu)^{-2}<1
and
b=(\eta^{2}/(1+\eta\mu)^{2})\sigma^{2}
has steady-state limit
b/(1-a)
, which simplifies to (
12
). The minimizer in
\eta
follows by calculus.
∎

Corollary 6.4

(Robbins–Monro almost sure convergence)
.

If
\eta_{k}\downarrow 0
satisfies
\sum_{k}\eta_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k}\eta_{k}^{2}<\infty
, and

with the same noise model, then

almost surely and in

.

Proof.

This is a standard result for stochastic approximation with strongly monotone operators; see
[
29
, Ch. 5]
.

Synthesis and Outlook

Coupling linear and p-Laplacian diffusion with learned strong convexity yields a calibratable framework with quantified constants and guarantees. Our impossibility results (Propositions
4.1
–
4.2
) and SGPS guarantees (Theorem
5.1
) argue for
graph-aware calibration
, and our illustrative empirical validations (Section
8
) confirm the mechanics of the theoretical bounds.

While this work establishes a robust theoretical foundation with sharp asymptotic guarantees, several important gaps remain as avenues for future research. A key extension is the derivation of rigorous, non-asymptotic
finite-time convergence bounds
. Our analysis provides exponential decay rates and a finite-time bound to enter the linear regime (Theorem
3.9
), but a more direct bound on the time required to reach a specific error tolerance would offer stronger practical guarantees. Generalizing the framework to
directed graphs
is another critical next step. As noted in Remark
4.3
, the loss of symmetry introduces significant challenges, as standard spectral tools and Poincaré inequalities no longer apply directly. Developing analogous stability guarantees for these highly prevalent graph structures would greatly broaden the model’s applicability. Finally, to move beyond our illustrative checks, future work should include
large-scale empirical validation on diverse, real-world graph families
and the development of more self-contained proofs for all theoretical claims, potentially in a dedicated appendix.

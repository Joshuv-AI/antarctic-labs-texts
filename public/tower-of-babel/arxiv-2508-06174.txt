Thermodynamic uncertainty relation for feedback cooling
Kousuke Kumasaki, Kaito Tojo, Takahiro Sagawa, Ken Funo

Abstract
Feedback cooling enables a system to achieve low temperatures through measurement-based control. Determining the thermodynamic cost required to achieve the ideal cooling efficiency within a finite time remains an important problem. In this work, we establish a thermodynamic uncertainty relation (TUR) for feedback cooling in classical underdamped Langevin systems, thereby deriving a trade-off between the cooling efficiency and the entropy reduction rate. The obtained TUR implies that simultaneous achievement of the ideal cooling efficiency and finite entropy reduction rate is asymptotically possible by letting the fluctuation of the reversible local mean velocity diverge. This is shown to be feasible by using a feedback control based on the Kalman filter. Our results clarify the thermodynamic costs of achieving the fundamental cooling limit of feedback control from the perspective of the TUR.



Introduction.—

Recent experimental advances have enabled a precise investigation of thermodynamics in microscopic systems, where thermal fluctuations play a crucial role.
These systems require a theoretical framework beyond conventional thermodynamics, giving rise to stochastic thermodynamics
Sekimoto 2010
;
Esposito et al. 2009
;
Campisi et al. 2011
;
Seifert 2012
;
Ciliberto 2017
;
Funo et al. 2018
;
Landi and Paternostro 2021
, which explicitly incorporates thermal fluctuations.
In this framework, the second law constrains the entropy production to be nonnegative.
This framework has been further extended to information processing systems, known as information thermodynamics
Parrondo et al. 2015
;
Sagawa and Ueda 2008
;
Horowitz and Esposito 2014
;
Yada et al. 2022
;
Ptaszyński and Esposito 2019
;
Yada et al. 2024
;
Prech et al. 2025
.
Although the second law represents a fundamental thermodynamic bound on physical processes, it does not give a tight bound on the entropy production in general finite-time processes.
To this end, thermodynamic uncertainty relations (TURs)
Barato and Seifert 2015
;
Gingrich et al. 2016
;
Horowitz and Gingrich 2017
;
Dechant and Sasa 2018
;
Dechant 2018
;
Van Vu and Hasegawa 2019
;
Lee et al. 2019
have been established as generalizations of the second law, providing bounds between entropy production and generalized currents, and revealing trade-offs between efficiency and power in thermal engines
Shiraishi et al. 2016
;
Pietzonka and Seifert 2018
;
Tajima and Funo 2021
.
TURs have also been extended to the setup of Maxwell’s demon
Potts and Samuelsson 2019
;
Van Vu and Hasegawa 2020
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Otsubo et al. 2020
;
Tanogami et al. 2023
, leading to the derivation of TURs involving partial entropy production
Otsubo et al. 2020
;
Tanogami et al. 2023
, and the establishment of trade-off relations in information heat engines
Tanogami et al. 2023
.

Feedback cooling is a paradigmatic example of an information heat engine, where work is extracted by converting information into energy.
Applying feedback based on measurement results enables the suppression of particle fluctuations. This technique has been realized in various experimental systems, including nanomechanical resonators
Rossi et al. 2018
and optically trapped nanoparticles
Gieseler et al. 2012
;
Magrini et al. 2021
;
Gonzalez-Ballestero et al. 2021
;
Kamba et al. 2023
;
Kamba et al. 2025
.
While the standard formulation of the second law relates entropy production to the temperature of the heat bath,
the primary objective in feedback cooling is to reduce the temperature of the system below the bath temperature.
In the study of classical systems, second-law-like inequalities have been established for Langevin dynamics
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
;
Sandberg et al. 2014
, relating the kinetic temperature of the system to the rate of information acquisition via measurement, quantified by the transfer entropy
Schreiber 2000
;
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
;
Hartich et al. 2014
.
In feedback cooling, the effective use of information is crucial for achieving both efficient cooling and high cooling rates. Although examples achieving the fundamental information thermodynamic bound of feedback cooling have been reported
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
;
Kumasaki et al. 2025
, there exists a trade-off between power and efficiency, in general. Therefore, it is also expected that to achieve the ideal efficiency, the cooling power should vanish.
However, without a TUR specific to feedback cooling, it has remained unclear whether maximum efficiency can coexist with a finite cooling rate, and, if so, what additional cost would be required.

In this Letter, we derive TUR for feedback cooling that describes a fundamental trade-off relation between the cooling efficiency and entropy reduction rate in underdamped bipartite Langevin systems.
The key technique to derive the main result is to introduce a novel orthogonal decomposition of the local mean velocity and the partial entropy production, in analogy with the decomposition proposed in
Dechant et al. 2022a
;
Dechant et al. 2022b
;
Kamijima et al. 2023
, while our decomposition is unique to bipartite systems.
This decomposition allows us to derive the TUR for feedback cooling by involving the kinetic temperature of the system.
As a byproduct, we show that one of the decomposed partial entropy production recovers the previously known second-law-like inequality
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
in a special case.
From the obtained TUR, we identify that divergence of the fluctuation of the reversible local mean velocity is required to simultaneously achieve the maximum cooling efficiency and finite entropy reduction rate. We introduce a concrete example based on the Kalman filter and show that increasing the feedback gain to infinity allows for satisfying the above condition (see also Fig.
1
).
The obtained results provide design principles for achieving optimal feedback cooling, which is particularly relevant to nanoparticle experiments
Gieseler et al. 2012
;
Magrini et al. 2021
;
Gonzalez-Ballestero et al. 2021
;
Kamba et al. 2023
;
Kamba et al. 2025
.

Setup.—

We analyze the setup of feedback cooling by considering a bipartite system that consists of a system described by a Brownian particle and a memory that registers measurement outcomes.
The system is coupled to a thermal reservoir at temperature
T
and is subject to a damping force
-\gamma v
, where
v
is the velocity of the system.
A feedback force
-ay
is applied to reduce the kinetic energy of the particle, where
y
denotes the outcome of continous measurement of the velocity.
The time evolution of the joint probability distribution
p(v,y)
is governed by the following Fokker-Planck equation
Munakata and Rosinberg 2013
;
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p(v,y)=-\partial_{v}J_{v}(v,y)-\partial_{y}J_{y}(v,y).

(1)

The probability currents in the
v
and
y
directions are given by

\displaystyle J_{v}(v,y)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\frac{ay}{m}p(v,y)}_{J_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}(v,y)}\ \underbrace{-\frac{\gamma}{m}\Bigl(v+\frac{T}{m}\partial_{v}\Bigr)p(v,y)}_{J_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v,y)},

(2)

\displaystyle J_{y}(v,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau}(y-v)p(v,y)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\tau^{2}}\partial_{y}p(v,y),

(3)

where we set the Boltzmann constant
k_{\mathrm{B}}
to unity.
In Eq. (
3
), the parameter
\tau
defines the timescale of the low-pass filter applied to the measurement and
\sigma^{2}
characterizes the uncertainty in the measurement signal.
We assume that the initial distribution
p_{0}(v,y)
is a Gaussian distribution centered at the origin, i.e., its first moments vanish.
Under the dynamics defined by Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
), the joint probability distribution
p(v,y)
at later times remains Gaussian centered at the origin.
In Eq. (
2
), we further decompose the probability current
J_{v}(v,y)
into reversible
J_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}(v,y)
and irreversible
J_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v,y)
components
Risken 1996
;
Spinney and Ford 2012
as
J_{v}(v,y)=J_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}(v,y)+J_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v,y)
, where the feedback force
-ay
is treated as a reversible contribution.
Note that Eq. (
2
) describes the case of a free particle, but our results can be extended to the case where the particle is trapped in a harmonic potential, as discussed in the Supplemental Material
sup
.

We now introduce thermodynamic quantities based on the dynamics described by Eq. (
1
).
In the context of feedback cooling, a key quantity is the kinetic energy of the particle defined as
E\coloneqq\expectationvalue{mv^2/2}
, where
\expectationvalue{\cdot}\coloneqq\int\cdot\;p(v,y)\differential v\differential y
denotes the average.
From the first law of thermodynamics
\dot{E}=-\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}+\dot{Q}
, we define the heat current and the extractable work rate using the irreversible and reversible components of the current as
\dot{Q}\coloneqq\int mvJ_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v,y)\differential v\differential y
and
\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}\coloneqq-\int mvJ_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}(v,y)\differential v\differential y

Sekimoto 2010
;
Rosinberg et al. 2015
.
The generalized second law of thermodynamics is given by

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}\coloneqq\dot{s}_{v}-\dot{Q}/T-\dot{I}_{v}=\|\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{p}^{2}\geq 0,

(4)

where
\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}
is the partial entropy production rate of the system,
\dot{s}_{v}=-\int J_{v}(v)\partial_{v}\ln\tilde{p}(v)\,\differential v
is the Shannon entropy rate of the system,
\tilde{p}(v)\coloneqq\int p(v,y)\differential y
is the marginal probability distribution of the system, and

\dot{I}_{v}\coloneqq-\int(\partial_{v}J_{v}(v,y))\ln\left[p(v,y)/\tilde{p}(v)\right]\differential v\differential y

is the information flow
Horowitz and Esposito 2014
, quantifying the change in the system-memory correlation induced by the time evolution of the system.
In the second equality of (
4
), we express the partial entropy production rate as the square of the norm of the irreversible local mean velocity
\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v,y):=J_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v,y)/p(v,y)
, which directly shows the nonnegativity of
\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}
. Here, we introduce an inner product for functions
f(v,y)
and
g(v,y)
as

\langle f,g\rangle_{p}:=(m^{2}/\gamma T)\int f(v,y)g(v,y)p(v,y)\differential v\differential y
and the corresponding norm as
\|\cdot\|_{p}\coloneqq\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{p}^{1/2}
.

The irreversible local mean velocity
\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
is decomposed into two orthogonal components, which are
a coarse-grained part
\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
and a residual part
\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
.
The irreversible component of a generalized current with a general observable
w(v,y)
is given by

\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}\coloneqq(\gamma T/m^{2})\expectationvalue{w, \nu_v^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{p}
.
Using the decomposition

\expectationvalue{w, \nu_v^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{p}=\expectationvalue{w, \delta\nu_v^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{p}+\expectationvalue{w, \tilde{\nu}_v^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{p}
,
the current
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}
is decomposed into each component. Each term leads to distinct TURs (
11
)-(
13
).

Orthogonal decomposition of the partial entropy production.—

In what follows, we introduce a new orthogonal decomposition of the partial entropy production rate
\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}
and show that one of its components reproduces the second-law-like inequality in the previous study
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
. We begin by decomposing the irreversible local mean velocity into orthogonal components as

\displaystyle\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}=\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}+\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}},

(5)

where
\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v)\coloneqq\tilde{J}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v)/\tilde{p}(v)
and
\tilde{J}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v)\coloneqq\int\differential y\,J_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v,y)
are the coarse-grained irreversible local mean velocity and the irreversible probability current, respectively. The residual component
\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
is defined by Eq. (
5
). From direct calculation,
\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
and
\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
satisfy the orthogonality condition
\langle\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}},\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\rangle_{p}=0
, meaning that the decomposition (
5
) is indeed orthogonal (see Fig.
2
). Note that this decomposition is distinct from the Hatano-Sasa
Hatano and Sasa 2001
and Maes-Netočný
Maes and Netočný 2014
decompositions and is unique to bipartite systems.

Based on the decomposition (
5
), the partial entropy production rate can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}=\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}+\dot{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}},

(6)

where

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}

\displaystyle:=\|\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{p}^{2}=\dot{s}_{v}-\frac{\dot{Q}}{T_{\mathrm{kin}}}-\dot{I}_{v}\geq 0,

(7)

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}

\displaystyle:=\|\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{p}^{2}=\frac{\dot{Q}}{T_{\mathrm{kin}}}-\frac{\dot{Q}}{T}\geq 0.

(8)

Here, we define the kinetic temperature of the system as
T_{\mathrm{kin}}:=m\expectationvalue{v^2}
. As the marginal probability distribution satisfies
\tilde{p}(v)\propto\exp[-mv^{2}/(2T_{\mathrm{kin}})]
, the kinetic temperature can be regarded as a quantitative measure of the performance of cooling the system.
Since
\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}\geq\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}
and
\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}\geq\dot{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}
, inequalities (
7
) and (
8
) give tighter bounds compared to the generalized second law (
4
). In addition, inequality (
8
) shows that achieving
T_{\mathrm{kin}}<T
requires
\dot{Q}\geq 0
, which means that cooling requires heat dissipation within this setup. On the other hand, inequality (
7
) is similar to (
4
), but the bath temperature
T
is replaced by the kinetic temperature
T_{\rm kin}
, which allows quantifying the fundamental cooling limit from the perspective of information thermodynamics as follows.
Using the relation
\dot{s}_{v}=\dot{E}/T_{\rm kin}=(\dot{Q}-\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}})/T_{\mathrm{kin}}
, inequality (
7
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}}{T_{\mathrm{kin}}}\leq-\dot{I}_{v},

(9)

where the left-hand side represents the extractable work rate divided by the kinetic temperature, which quantifies the entropy reduction rate to cool the system.
Therefore, inequality (
9
) characterizes the fundamental limit of feedback cooling by setting an upper bound on the entropy reduction rate in terms of the information flow.
Assuming that the cooling is successful, that is,
\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}>0
, we define the information-to-entropy reduction cooling efficiency as

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}:=|\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}/(T_{\mathrm{kin}}\dot{I}_{v})|\leq 1
,
where the last inequality follows from (
9
).
In the steady state, the information flow is always bounded by the transfer entropy rate
\dot{I}_{\mathrm{TE}}

Schreiber 2000
via
-\dot{I}_{v}\leq\dot{I}_{\mathrm{TE}}
. Under this condition, inequality (
9
) reproduces the second-law-like inequality for feedback cooling
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
:
\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}/T_{\mathrm{kin}}\leq\dot{I}_{\mathrm{TE}}
.

TUR for feedback cooling.—

For orthogonal decompositions of the entropy production, a short-time TUR can be derived for each decomposed component
Dechant et al. 2022a
.
In the same spirit, we derive TURs corresponding to
\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}
,
\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}
, and
\dot{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}
based on the new orthogonal decomposition as follows.

We begin by defining the generalized current of the system as
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu}\coloneqq(\gamma T/m^{2})\expectationvalue{w, \nu_v}_{p}
, where
\nu_{v}\coloneqq J_{v}(v,y)/p(v,y)
is the local mean velocity, and
w(v,y)
is a general observable. Since only the irreversible part of the local mean velocity
\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
is related to the (partial) entropy production
\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}
for underdamped Langevin systems, we focus on the irreversible component of the generalized current, defined as
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}\coloneqq(\gamma T/m^{2})\expectationvalue{w, \nu_v^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{p}
. Using the orthogonal decomposition (
5
), we obtain the following decomposition for the irreversible current (see also Fig.
2
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}+\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}},

(10)

where
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}\coloneqq(\gamma T/m^{2})\expectationvalue{w, \delta\nu^{\mathrm{irr}}_v}_{p}
and
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}\coloneqq(\gamma T/m^{2})\expectationvalue{w, \tilde{\nu}^{\mathrm{irr}}_v}_{p}
.
Because each current that appears in Eq. (
10
) is expressed in terms of inner products,
we can apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to derive short-time TURs
Dechant et al. 2022a
.
This yields the following set of inequalities:

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left\|w\right\|_{p}^{2}\dot{\Sigma}_{\nu^{\rm irr}},

(11)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left\|w\right\|_{p}^{2}\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}},

(12)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left\|w\right\|_{p}^{2}\dot{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}.

(13)

Here, inequality (
11
) is similar to the bipartite TUR for overdamped Langevin systems
Otsubo et al. 2020
;
Tanogami et al. 2023
. However, unlike the overdamped case, the irreversible current
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}
deviates from the total current
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\nu}
because of the presence of reversible components in underdamped systems.
Next, the partial entropy production
\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}
that appears in inequality (
12
) corresponds to the information thermodynamic cooling limit (
7
) and (
9
). Therefore, it can be interpreted as a TUR associated with feedback cooling as we discuss in detail in the following.
Finally, inequality (
13
) shows that
\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}
must vanish in the limit of
T_{\mathrm{kin}}\to T
by using (
8
).

We now focus on the trade-off implied by (
12
).
As bipartite TURs lead to a trade-off between power and efficiency in information engines
Tanogami et al. 2023
, we derive a trade-off relation between the entropy reduction rate and cooling efficiency based on (
12
).
To this end, we choose
w(v,y)=\nu_{v}(v,y)-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}(v)
, which allows us to express the information flow as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{w}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}=\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\expectationvalue{\nu_{v}-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\rm irr}, \nu_v^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{p}=-\dot{I}_{v}.

(14)

Substituting Eq. (
14
) into (
12
), we obtain the main result that gives the trade-off relation between the cooling efficiency and entropy reduction rate:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}}{T_{\mathrm{kin}}}\right|\leq\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left\|\nu_{v}-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\right\|_{p}^{2}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}).

(15)

Inequality (
15
) indicates that achieving the maximum cooling efficiency
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}\to 1
with finite entropy reduction rate requires

(\gamma T/m^{2})\left\|\nu_{v}-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\right\|_{p}^{2}\to\infty
(see also Fig.
1
).
This quantity represents the fluctuation of the generalized current associated with
w=\nu_{v}-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left\|\nu_{v}-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\right\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left(\|\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}\|_{p}^{2}+2\langle\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}},\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\rangle_{p}+\|\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{p}^{2}\right).

(16)

), we note that based on the bipartite TUR for overdamped systems, achieving finite power at maximum efficiency in information heat engines requires divergence of the short-time fluctuation of the information flow
(\gamma T/m^{2})\|\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{p}^{2}

Tanogami et al. 2023
. On the other hand, in our feedback cooling setup, achieving the maximum cooling efficiency (i.e., the equality condition of (

)) implies that the fluctuation of the information flow vanishes. Nevertheless, if the reversible contribution
\|\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}\|_{p}^{2}
or
\langle\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}},\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\rangle_{p}
diverges, the obtained TUR allows the possibility of achieving maximum efficiency with finite entropy reduction rate.

Achieving the maximum cooling efficiency with finite entropy reduction rate.—

We now demonstrate an explicit example in which the reversible contribution
\|\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}\|_{p}^{2}
diverges, and the ideal cooling efficiency
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}=1
is asymptotically achieved with finite entropy reduction rate.
This is realized in the dynamics governed by Eq. (
1
), which can be reduced to a feedback control based on the Kalman filter
Simon 2006
by introducing the rescaled variable

\hat{v}=K/(\gamma+K+G)y,

and choosing the parameters as

a=GK/(\gamma+K+G)
and
\tau=m/(\gamma+K+G)
. Here,
K
and
G
are the Kalman gain and the feedback gain, respectively. We further choose the so-called optimal Kalman gain by setting
K=\gamma(\sqrt{1+2T/\sigma^{2}\gamma}-1)
.
Under these settings,
\hat{v}
corresponds to the Kalman estimate of the particle velocity
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
.
In the steady state, the covariance matrix of the velocity
v
and its estimate
\hat{v}
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{\Xi}=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{vv}&\sigma_{v\hat{v}}\\
\sigma_{v\hat{v}}&\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}+\mathcal{E}&\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}\\
\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}&\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}\end{pmatrix},

(17)

where

\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}=K\mathcal{E}/[2(\gamma+G)]
and
\mathcal{E}=K\sigma^{2}/m
.
In the steady-state, the fluctuation of the information flow becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\|\delta\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{p}^{2}=\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}+\mathcal{E}}\right)\rightarrow 0,\quad(G\to\infty),

(18)

indicating that the fluctuation of the information flow vanishes in the asymptotic limit.
This also implies
\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}\rightarrow 0
, and therefore
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}\rightarrow 1
.
On the other hand, the entropy reduction rate asymptotically converges to a finite value

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}}{T_{\mathrm{kin}}}=\frac{G\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}}{m(\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}+\mathcal{E})}\rightarrow\frac{K}{2m},\quad(G\to\infty).

(19)

Thus, in the limit of large feedback gain
G
, the ideal efficiency is achieved with finite entropy reduction rate.
According to inequality (
15
), this implies that the fluctuation term
(\gamma T/m^{2})\|\nu_{v}-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{p}^{2}
needs to diverge.
This can be explicitly verified as:

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\left\|\nu_{v}-\tilde{\nu}_{v}^{\mathrm{irr}}\right\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G^{2}}{m^{2}}\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}+\frac{2G\gamma T}{m^{3}}\frac{\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}}{\sigma_{vv}}+\left(\frac{\gamma T}{m^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}}{\det\bm{\Xi}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{vv}}\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\infty\quad(G\to\infty),

(20)

where the divergent term
G^{2}\sigma_{\hat{v}\hat{v}}/m^{2}=O(G)
comes from the reversible contribution
\|\nu_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}}\|_{p}^{2}
.
In Fig.
3
, we plot the asymptotic achievement of the ideal efficiency
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}=1
.
The main plot shows how
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}
approaches unity as
G
increases, following the scaling
1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}=O(1/G)
.
The inset shows the corresponding fluctuation term, which grows
O(G)
.
Therefore, the right-hand side of (
15
) is
O(1)
, which is consistent with the observation that the entropy reduction rate remains finite (see also Fig.
1
).

.

increases,

).

Conclusion.—

In this study, we analyzed a bipartite underdamped Langevin system and derived the TUR for feedback cooling (
15
).
To derive the TUR, we introduced a new orthogonal decomposition of the local mean velocity and the partial entropy production rate.
We showed that the non-negativity of one of the decomposed entropy productions represents an information thermodynamic cooling limit (
9
).
By showing that the information flow can be expressed in the form of the generalized current (
14
), we obtained a trade-off between the cooling efficiency and entropy reduction rate (
15
).
From the obtained trade-off relation, we find that achieving both the ideal cooling efficiency and finite entropy reduction rate requires a divergence in the fluctuation of the reversible mean velocity.
While earlier studies provided examples of achieving the ideal efficiency and finite entropy reduction rate
Horowitz and Sandberg 2014
;
Kumasaki et al. 2025
,
this work proves that such results are consistent with the TUR and clarifies the thermodynamic cost required to achieve such situations.

An important direction for future work is to extend these results to quantum systems, because feedback cooling is essential in experimental platforms, including nanoparticle systems, to explore quantum effects such as quantum squeezing
Kamba et al. 2025
.
Developing the TUR for quantum feedback cooling is expected to lead to the clarification of the thermodynamic costs required to achieve the information thermodynamic cooling limit in quantum systems
Kumasaki et al. 2025
, and to the thermodynamic characterization of measurement backactions inherent in quantum measurements.

In the main text, we considered measurement-based feedback applied to a free particle, i.e., without potential.
However, implementations of feedback cooling are realized in systems such as mechanical oscillators or optically levitated nanoparticles.
Analyzing such systems requires incorporating the effect of a harmonic potential into the model.
Moreover, in these systems, feedback control is generally based on the measurement of the position of the system, rather than that of the velocity.
In this Supplemental Material, we derive a TUR for feedback cooling of a particle trapped in a harmonic potential.

In Sec.
I
, we introduce the setup of feedback cooling under a harmonic potential.
In Sec.
II
, we present a generalized orthogonal decomposition that extends the one discussed in the main text, and derive the information thermodynamic cooling limit.
In Sec.
III
, we derive a TUR for feedback cooling and a trade-off relation between the cooling efficiency and entropy pumping.
In Sec.
IV
, we use the Kalman filter and analyze the attainability of the ideal cooling efficiency based on the obtained TUR.

I
Setup

We consider a single-mode system consisting of the dimensionless position and momentum
(x,p)
.
Let
y
represent the memory variable corresponding to a noisy measurement of the position of the particle.
We assume that a linear feedback is applied based on an estimate constructed from the measurement outcomes until time
t
.
This situation is modeled by the following set of Langevin equations
Munakata and Rosinberg 2013
:

\displaystyle dx_{t}

\displaystyle=(\omega\,p_{t}+a\,u_{x}(e_{t}))\,dt,

(S1)

\displaystyle dp_{t}

\displaystyle=(-\gamma\,p_{t}-\omega\,x_{t}+b\,u_{p}(e_{t}))\,dt+\sqrt{2\,\gamma\,T}\,dW_{t},

(S2)

\displaystyle dy_{t}

\displaystyle=x_{t}\,dt+\sigma\,dW^{y}_{t}.

(S3)

Here,
\omega
is the frequency of the oscillator,
\gamma
is the damping coefficient,
T
is the bath temperature, and
\sigma^{2}
is the variance of the measurement noise.

W_{t}
and
W^{y}_{t}
are Wiener processes, which are independent of each other.
The feedback control depends on an estimate
e_{t}
, which is a vector obtained by compressing the past measurement outcomes
\{dy_{s}\}_{0\leq s\leq t}
.
We assume that the feedback forces
u_{x}(e)
and
u_{p}(e)
in equation (
S1
) depend only on the estimate
e_{t}
.
While the estimate
e_{t}
is generally a non-Markovian process due to its dependence on the past measurement outcomes, we restrict our analysis to the case where the time-evolution equation that describes the updates of
e_{t}
is described by a Markov process.
This includes, for example, a direct feedback scheme that uses the current measurement
dy_{t}
, as well as the Kalman filter.
Specifically, we assume that
e_{t}
evolves according to an Itô-type stochastic differential equation:

\displaystyle de_{t}=f(x,p,e_{t})\,dt+g(t)\,dW_{t}^{e},

(S4)

The functions
f
and
g
are determined by the chosen estimator.

Let
\rho(x,p,e;t)
denote the probability density function over the extended state space
(x,p,e)
.
Its time evolution is governed by the Fokker-Planck equation:

\partial_{t}\rho(x,p,e;t)=-\mathbf{\nabla}_{(x,p,e)}\cdot\mathbf{J}(x,p,e;t),

(S5)

where the probability current
\mathbf{J}_{s}=(J_{x},J_{p})^{\top}
in the
(x,p)
subspace is given by

\displaystyle J_{x}(x,p,e)

\displaystyle=(\omega p+au_{x}(e))\rho,

(S6)

\displaystyle J_{p}(x,p,e)

\displaystyle=(-\gamma p-\omega x+bu_{p}(e))\rho-\gamma T\partial_{p}\rho.

(S7)

The momentum current
J_{p}
can be decomposed into reversible and irreversible components:

\displaystyle J_{p}^{\mathrm{irr}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma p\rho-\gamma T\partial_{p}\rho,

(S8)

\displaystyle J_{p}^{\mathrm{rev}}

\displaystyle=(-\omega x+bu_{p}(e))\rho.

(S9)

In contrast, the position current is entirely reversible.

J_{x}^{\mathrm{irr}}=0,\quad J_{x}^{\mathrm{rev}}=J_{x}.

(S10)

This decomposition reflects the assumption that the feedback forces are derived from a linear Hamiltonian in
(x,p)
and thus contribute only to reversible dynamics.
We write the reversible and irreversible current vectors in the subspace
(x,p)
as
\mathbf{J}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{s}=(0,J_{p}^{\mathrm{irr}})^{\top}
and
\mathbf{J}^{\mathrm{rev}}_{s}=(J_{x}^{\mathrm{rev}},J_{p}^{\mathrm{rev}})^{\top}
, respectively.
The average energy of the system is defined as

E\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\,\left(\frac{1}{2}\omega p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}\right)\rho(x,p,e).

(S11)

From the time derivative of this energy, we define the heat current as

\dot{Q}\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\;\bm{s}^{\top}\bm{J}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}},

(S12)

and the work rate due to feedback as

\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\,\bm{s}^{\top}\bm{J}_{s}^{\mathrm{rev}},

(S13)

where
\bm{s}=[x,p]^{\top}
.
These definitions satisfy the first law of thermodynamics in the form

\dot{E}=-\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}+\dot{Q}.

II
Orthogonal decomposition of the partial entropy production

As in the case without a harmonic potential, we introduce an orthogonal decomposition in this setting.
In this section, we derive an information thermodynamic cooling limit based on this decomposition.
The partial entropy production rate is defined in analogy with the one-dimensional case as

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{s}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\gamma T}\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\;\|\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p,e)\|^{2}\rho(x,p,e),

(S14)

where the irreversible local mean velocity is given by
\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p,e):=\bm{J}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p,e)/\rho(x,p,e)
.
We define an inner product for vector functions
\bm{f}(x,p,e)
and
\bm{g}(x,p,e)
as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{f},\bm{g}\rangle_{\rho}:=(1/\gamma T)\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\;\bm{f}^{\top}(x,p,e)\bm{g}(x,p,e)\rho(x,p,e).

(S15)

Using the associated norm, the partial entropy production rate can be written compactly as
\dot{\Sigma}_{s}=\|\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{\rho}^{2}
.

Next, we introduce an orthogonal decomposition of
\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}
.
Let
\tilde{\rho}(x,p):=\int\differential e\;\rho(x,p,e)
be the marginal distribution of the system, and define the marginal irreversible probability current and corresponding local mean velocity as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p)

\displaystyle:=\int\differential e\;\mathbf{J}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p,e),

(S16)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p)

\displaystyle:=\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p)/\tilde{\rho}(x,p).

(S17)

The residual local mean velocity is then defined as

\delta\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p,e):=\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p,e)-\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}(x,p).

By construction, this decomposition satisfies the orthogonality condition:

\langle\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}},\delta\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\rangle_{\rho}=0.

As a result, the partial entropy production rate can be decomposed as follows

\dot{\Sigma}_{s}=\|\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|^{2}_{\rho}=\|\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|^{2}_{\rho}+\|\delta\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|^{2}_{\rho}=:\dot{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\nu}}+\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu}.

(S18)

In what follows, we show that the non-negativity of
\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu}
leads to an information thermodynamic cooling limit.
We define the information flow and its reversible and irreversible components as

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\,\bm{J}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\top}\nabla_{\mathrm{s}}\ln\frac{\rho(x,p,e)}{\tilde{\rho}(x,p)},

(S19)

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{irr}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\,\bm{J}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{irr}\top}\nabla_{\mathrm{s}}\ln\frac{\rho(x,p,e)}{\tilde{\rho}(x,p)},

(S20)

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{rev}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\,\bm{J}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{rev}\top}\nabla_{\mathrm{s}}\ln\frac{\rho(x,p,e)}{\tilde{\rho}(x,p)}.

(S21)

By definition, these satisfy the additive relation
\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}=\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{irr}}+\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{rev}}
.
A direct computation yields the identity
\dot{\Sigma}_{s}=-\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{irr}}
.
Therefore, the non-negativity of the
\dot{\Sigma}_{s}
leads to the relation

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{rev}}\leq\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}.

(S22)

We proceed to rewrite the reversible information flow.
Let
\Xi:=\langle(x,p)^{\top}(x,p)\rangle
be the covariance matrix of
(x,p)
, and let
R
be the orthogonal matrix that diagonalizes
\Xi
.
Define the new coordinates
(u,v)^{\top}:=R^{\top}(x,p)^{\top}
, and define a reversible probability flow in the new coordinates as

\mathbf{J}_{s}^{\mathrm{rev}^{\prime}}(u,v,e)\coloneqq R^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{s}^{\mathrm{rev}}(x,p,e).

(S23)

Then, the work rates in the eigen directions are defined as

\displaystyle\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\differential u\differential v\differential e\;u\,J_{u}^{\mathrm{rev}^{\prime}}(u,v,e),

(S24)

\displaystyle\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\differential u\differential v\differential e\;v\,J_{v}^{\mathrm{rev}^{\prime}}(u,v,e).

(S25)

These satisfy
\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}=\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}+\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}
, giving a decomposition of the total work rate.
We define the kinetic temperatures in each direction as

T_{u}:=\omega\sigma_{uu},\quad T_{v}:=\omega\sigma_{uu},

where
\sigma_{uu}
and
\sigma_{pp}
are the corresponding variances.
Using these definitions, the reversible part of the information flow becomes

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{rev}}

\displaystyle=\int\differential u\differential v\differential e\;\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{rev}^{\prime}\top}\nabla_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}\ln\rho(u,v)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}}{T_{u}}-\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}}{T_{v}},

(S26)

where
\nabla_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}:=R^{\top}\nabla_{\mathrm{s}}
.

Combining the results (
S22
) and (
S26
), we obtain the information thermodynamic cooling limit for an underdamped system with harmonic potential:

\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}}{T_{u}}+\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}}{T_{v}}\;\leq\;-\dot{I}_{s}.

(S27)

The left-hand side quantifies the entropy extracted from the system via feedback, which we refer to as the entropy pumping.
This can be regarded as an indicator of how effectively the system is cooled by extracting work and reducing its kinetic temperatures.
We define the cooling efficiency with respect to information flow as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{I}\coloneqq\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}/T_{u}+\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}/T_{v}}{\dot{I}_{s}},

(S28)

which satisfies
\varepsilon_{I}\leq 1
.
Finally, using the bound
\dot{I}_{s}\leq\dot{I}_{\mathrm{TE}}
between the information flow and transfer entropy, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}}{T_{u}}+\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}}{T_{v}}\;\leq\;-\dot{I}_{\mathrm{TE}}.

(S29)

This result is the classical counterpart of the information thermodynamic cooling limit for quantum systems under harmonic potential
Kumasaki et al. 2025
.

III
Derivation of the TUR

In this section, we derive the short-time TUR that characterizes the fundamental limit of feedback cooling, and represents a trade-off relation between the cooling efficiency and entropy pumping.
As in the case without a potential, we define the generalized current associated with the irreversible flow as

\mathcal{J}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}\coloneqq\gamma T\expectationvalue{\boldsymbol{w}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_s^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{\rho},

where
\bm{w}(x,p,e)
is an arbitrary weight function vector.
Using the orthogonal decomposition, this generalized current can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}+\mathcal{J}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}},

(S30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\gamma T\expectationvalue{\boldsymbol{w}, \delta\boldsymbol{\nu}_s^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{\rho},

(S31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\gamma T\expectationvalue{\boldsymbol{w}, \widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_s^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{\rho}.

(S32)

From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, these components satisfy the following short-time TURs:

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}_{\nu^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma T\left\|\bm{w}\right\|_{\rho}^{2}\dot{\Sigma}_{s},

(S33)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}_{\delta\nu^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma T\left\|\bm{w}\right\|_{\rho}^{2}\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu},

(S34)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}_{\tilde{\nu}^{\rm irr}}^{\bm{w}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma T\left\|\bm{w}\right\|_{\rho}^{2}\dot{\Sigma}_{\tilde{\nu}}.

(S35)

As in the free particle case, the non-negativity of
\dot{\Sigma}_{\delta\nu}
implies the inequality (
S27
).
Therefore, Eq. (
S34
) can be regarded as a short-time TUR corresponding to the cooling limit.

We now derive a trade-off between the cooling efficiency and entropy pumping from equation (
S34
).
We define information flow along the direction of
x
and
p
as

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\,J_{x}\partial_{x}\ln\frac{\rho(x,p,e)}{\tilde{\rho}(x,p)},

(S36)

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\mathrm{p}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\differential x\differential p\differential e\,J_{p}\partial_{p}\ln\frac{\rho(x,p,e)}{\tilde{\rho}(x,p)}.

(S37)

By choosing
\bm{w}=\bm{\nu}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}-\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}
, we find

d_{t}\langle\delta j_{s}^{\bm{w},\mathrm{irr}}\rangle=-\dot{I}_{p}.

Substituting this into equation (
S34
) yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}}{T_{u}}+\frac{\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}}{T_{v}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\dot{I}_{x}}{\dot{I}_{s}}\right)^{2}\leq\gamma T\|\bm{\nu}_{s}-\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{\rho}^{2}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}),

(S38)

which shows a refined trade-off compared to (19) in the main text obtained for the free particle case
due to the additional factor
\left(1-\dot{I}_{x}/\dot{I}_{s}\right)^{2}
on the left-hand side.
To achieve both finite entropy pumping and maximal efficiency
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}=1
simultaneously,
the inequality implies that either
\|\bm{\nu}_{s}-\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{\rho}^{2}\rightarrow\infty
or
\left(1-\dot{I}_{x}/\dot{I}_{s}\right)^{2}\rightarrow 0
is needed.
In feedback cooling scenarios,
\dot{I}_{p}
typically remains finite, hence
\left(1-\dot{I}_{x}/\dot{I}_{s}\right)^{2}
does not vanish.
Therefore, the divergence of the fluctuation term
\|\bm{\nu}_{s}-\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{\rho}^{2}
is necessary.

IV
Simultaneous achievement of maximum efficiency and finite entropy pumping

In this section, we numerically demonstrate that when the Kalman filter is used as the estimator,
the system asymptotically achieves maximum efficiency and finite entropy pumping in the limit of infinite feedback gain.
We further show that
\|\bm{\nu}_{s}-\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{\rho}^{2}
diverges in accordance with the trade-off relation (
S38
).
We begin by describing the Kalman filter dynamics corresponding to equations (
S1
)-(
S3
).
The estimated state
e_{t}=[\hat{x},\hat{p}]^{\top}
evolves according to
Simon 2006
:

\displaystyle d\hat{x}

\displaystyle=(\omega\hat{p}+au_{x})\,dt+\frac{\hat{V}_{xx}}{\sigma^{2}}(dy-\hat{x}\,dt),

(S39)

\displaystyle d\hat{p}

\displaystyle=(-\gamma\hat{p}-\omega\hat{x}+bu_{p})\,dt+\frac{\hat{V}_{xp}}{\sigma^{2}}(dy-\hat{x}\,dt),

(S40)

where
\hat{\bm{V}}
is the estimation error covariance matrix, determined by the Riccati equation:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{V}_{xx}

\displaystyle=2\omega\hat{V}_{xp}-\frac{\hat{V}_{xx}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},

(S41)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{V}_{pp}

\displaystyle=-2\omega\hat{V}_{xp}-2\gamma\hat{V}_{pp}+2\gamma T-\frac{\hat{V}_{xp}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},

(S42)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{V}_{xp}

\displaystyle=-\omega\hat{V}_{xx}+\omega\hat{V}_{pp}-\gamma\hat{V}_{xp}-\frac{\hat{V}_{xx}\hat{V}_{xp}}{\sigma^{2}}.

(S43)

Since this is a closed set of equations, the time evolution of
\hat{\bm{V}}
can be obtained in advance.
Therefore, the estimation dynamics (
S39
)-(
S40
) take the form of (
S4
),
and the full theory developed in the previous sections applies to the extended state space
[x,p,\hat{x},\hat{p}]^{\top}
.

G
.

\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}/T_{u}+\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}/T_{v}

).

We focus on the case where the feedback is set as
u_{x}=-\hat{x}
and
u_{p}=-\hat{p}
,
and take the feedback gains to be equal:
a=b=G
.
Under this setup, we numerically calculate the behavior of
\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},u}/T_{u}+\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext},v}/T_{v}
,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{I}}
, and
\|\bm{\nu}_{s}-\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}\|_{\rho}^{2}

.

.

G
.
Hence, the right-hand side of the trade-off inequality (
S38
) remains finite,
and the system achieves feedback cooling with finite entropy pumping at maximum efficiency.

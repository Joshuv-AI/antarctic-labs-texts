Association of abnormal P-wave parameters with all-cause mortality in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Hanzhi Du, Qinghua Tang, Yuye Ning, Huaiyu Wang, Zeqiang Nie, Mengchang Wang, Junjun Hao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Patients diagnosed with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) are at increased risk of developing atrial fibrillation (AF). Abnormal P-wave parameters (PWPs) have been identified as independent predictors of AF, however, their prognostic significance in DLBCL patients remains unknown. Newly diagnosed DLBCL patients from January 2015 to August 2022 were retrospectively included in this study. Patients were devided as with abnormal PWPs or without it. Primary outcome was the all-cause mortality. The median duration of follow-up was 16.3 months. The Kaplan‒Meier method and multivariable COX proportional hazards regression models were used to analyze the relationship between PWPs and all-cause mortality. Logistic regression analyses were performed to identify risk factors associated with PWPs. A total of 374 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients were included, of whom 137 patients exhibited abnormalities in PWPs. Compared to the group with normal PWPs, patients with PWPs abnormalities had a higher proportion of males (p = 0.001), elevated levels of blood urea nitrogen (p = 0.038) and blood creatinine (p = 0.005), and a higher rate of all-cause mortality (p = 0.001). PWPs, particularly P-wave duration (p = 0.017) and P-wave terminal force in lead V1 (PTFV1) (p = 0.001), were independently correlated with all-cause mortality in DLBCL patients. Furthermore, male patients exhibited a higher susceptibility to abnormal PWPs (p = 0.001). PWPs, particularly P-wave duration and PTFV1, serve as simple yet effective prognostic indicators for all-cause mortality in DLBCL patients. Consequently, vigilant monitoring of PWPs, particularly in male patients, is warranted to accurately evaluate the prognosis of DLBCL.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-81039-0.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common subtype of non-Hodgkin lymphoma in China, accounting for 25-50% of cases. DLBCL is characterized by the uncontrolled growth of abnormal B-cells, resulting in the destruction of normal lymphoid tissue. It typically presents as a painless mass with progressive enlargement in the lymph nodes. In cases where extranodal sites are involved, symptoms may vary depending on the specific sites affected1. DLBCL is more common among the elderly, with a median age of 60 years at diagnosis2. Cancer patients have higher cardiovascular complications compared to the general population3, which may be attributed to age- or disease-related cardiac risk factors such as metabolic disorders, electrolyte abnormalities, and cytotoxic effects of antitumor therapeutics4. Common cardiovascular complications in patients with DLBCL include arrhythmias and heart failure5. These complications can increase the toxicity of therapeutic drugs, and are usually associated with poorer survival rates.

A systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the risk of new-onset atrial fibrillation (AF) in newly diagnosed cancer patients, incorporating seven cohort studies (four retrospective and three prospective studies) comprising a total of 3,075,778 patients6. The results indicated a significant increase in the risk of new-onset atrial fibrillation within the initial 90 days following a cancer diagnosis. Another meta-analysis, encompassing 18 studies, demonstrated that cancer patients who developed AF had a higher mortality rate compared to those without AF (OR = 1.90, p < 0.00001)7. Additionally, a community-based large-scale study found that malignancy patients with concomitant AF had lower five-year survival rate compared to those without AF, with this association being primarily observed in lung cancer and lymphoma8. Notably, a study has found poor prognosis in lung cancer patients with concomitant atrial cardiomyopathy (HR = 1.543, 95% CI 1.042–2.283, p = 0.030)9. AF is a common complication among DLBCL patients, and its onset brings a significant risk of severe heart failure, increasing the all-cause mortality10,11. However, the insidious onset and absence of obvious symptoms in AF pose challenges in its detection, resulting in a notably low detection rate in real-world clinical practice, particularly in China12. AF is known to contribute to structural remodeling of the atrial matrix and electrophysiological changes. The interaction between triggers and the atrial matrix plays a crucial role in the initiation and perpetuation of AF. Some scholars propose that atrial cardiomyopathy is a fundamental factor in the pathogenesis of AF and can be exacerbated by AF itself13.

Considering these factors, it is essential to explore practical strategies for identifying and monitoring AF in DLBCL patients. The conventional 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) is a low-cost, convenient, and readily available tool for diagnosing atrial cardiomyopathy. P-wave parameters (PWPs) derived from the ECG, including P-wave duration, P-wave axis, P-wave terminal force in lead V1 (PTFV1) and advanced interatrial block, reflect atrial structural and electrical characteristics. Abnormal PWPs have been independently associated with an increased risk of AF14–18. Nevertheless, further investigation is warranted to determine the relationship between PWPs and clinical outcomes in DLBCL patients. In summary, identifying and understanding of AF in DLBCL patients are crucial due to its detrimental impact on cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Enhancing the detection rate of AF and evaluating the prognostic value of PWPs in DLBCL patients can significantly contribute to reducing the burden of cardiovascular diseases, hospital stays, and patient medical expenses.

Materials and methods

Study population

Patients who were diagnosed with DLBCL and were hospitalized at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University from January 2015 to August 2022 were enrolled in this analysis. All the patients included had complete medical data, and have applied for informed consent waiver. For the accuracy and validity of our study results, patients with any of the following conditions are excluded: (1) non-newly diagnosed DLBCL; (2) concomitant cardiovascular diseases, primarily including a history of severe cardiac and valvular diseases (congenital heart disease, cardiac valve disease, cardiomyopathy), acute myocardial infarction, severe heart failure (left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤ 30%), intracardiac thrombosis or infective endocarditis; pericardial diseases (pericarditis or pericardial effusion); arrhythmias (atrial flutter, atrial fibrillation); (3) concomitant other tumors; (4) incomplete or missing medical records; (5) loss to follow-up. The study flow chart is shown in Fig. 1. Detailed information regarding patients with excluded cardiovascular diseases can be found in Supplementary Table 1. The study adhered to the tenets of the Helsinki Declaration and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (NO. XJTU1AF2022LSK-410).

Clinical information

Demographics and clinical characteristic information of DLBCL patients were extracted from electronic medical records. The diagnosis, clinical stage and treatment of DLBCL were assigned according to the 2019 NCCN B-Cell Lymphomas Guidelines19. The International Prognostic Index (IPI) score is often used to grade aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma and mainly includes five outcome indices: age, clinical stage, serum lactate dehydrogenase, ECOG performance status and extranodal involvement20. Each risk factor in the IPI score was counted as 1 point, and they were summed as an accurate predictor of the prognosis of DLBCL patients. Risk stratification was conducted using the IPI score (0–1, low risk; 2, low-intermediate risk; 3, intermediate-high risk; 4–5, high risk).

The primary endpoint was the all-cause mortality. Follow-up information was gathered by telephone, and the final follow-up date for this study was November 20, 2022. PTFV1, also known as the Morris index, refers to the value obtained by multiplying the minimum amplitude (µV) by the duration (ms) of the terminal negative part of the P wave in lead V1 under sinus rhythm. PWPs abnormalities were defined as having at least one of the following conditions, according to the consensus document of PWPs: P-wave duration > 120 ms, P-wave axis < 0° or > 75°, PTFV1 > 4000 µV·ms and advanced atrial block21–23. The specific ECG evaluation method for PWPs can be found in Supplementary Table 2. ECG reports were analyzed by two professional technicians in ECG room of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi ‘an Jiaotong University independently and any disagreement was discussed till a unanimous decision was made.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data are summarized as mean ± SD or median (interquartile range). The independent-samples t test was used for data with normal distributions, and nonparametric data were compared by the Mann‒Whitney U test. Categorical data are presented as n (%) and were compared by using Pearson’s χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves were generated by the Kaplan‒Meier method, and survival differences were defined by the log-rank test. Univariable analyses were conducted to identify significant baseline covariates correlated with prognosis. All variables with statistical significance (p < 0.05) were entered in the multivariable Cox regression analysis to explore the independent potential risk factors for poor prognosis in patients with DLBCL. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05, and the hazard ratios (HRs) were given with the 95% confidence interval (CI). All statistical analyses were done with SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp). Kaplan–Meier survival curves were drawn with the R packages survminer (V0.4.9) and ggplot2 (V3.4.0). The forest plot was drawn using the R package forestplot (V3.1.1).

Results

Patient baseline characteristics

A total of 374 patients with DLBCL who met the inclusion criteria were enrolled, and the median follow-up time was 16.3 months (range: 7.5–30.5 months). There were 137 (36.6%) patients with abnormal PWPs (Fig. 1). We analyzed and compared the clinical baseline data of DLBCL patients with and without abnormal PWPs. There were no substantial differences in age, stratification, IPI score, B symptoms or anthracycline medication between the two groups (p > 0.05), but marked differences were found in sex, blood urea nitrogen and blood creatinine level and mortality (p < 0.05). Compared with the group with normal PWPs, the patients with PWPs abnormalities had a higher proportion of men [83 (60.6%) vs. 101 (42.6%), p = 0.001], higher blood urea nitrogen (5.94 ± 2.77 vs. 5.25 ± 3.25, p = 0.038), higher blood creatinine levels (63.44 ± 28.30 vs. 56.41 ± 19.59, p = 0.005) (Table 1).

Prognostic values of PWPs abnormalities

Among 374 cases, 137 (36.6%) patients with DLBCL had PWPs abnormalities. In the group of PWPs abnormalities, 113 (82.5%) patients had an abnormal P-wave axis, 33 patients (24.1%) had abnormal PTFV1, 4 patients (2.9%) had abnormal P-wave duration, and 2 patients (1.5%) had advanced interatrial block (Fig. 2). Twelve patients had PWPs abnormalities on two items, and one had abnormal PWPs on all four items. The group with abnormal PWPs had a greater rate of all-cause mortality than the group with normal PWPs [57 (41.6%) vs. 61 (25.7%), p = 0.001] (Table 1). Log-rank test found that abnormal PWPs were correlated with all-cause mortality in DLBCL patients (p = 0.002) (Fig. 3). Furthermore, abnormal PWPs remained independently correlated with all-cause mortality in multivariable Cox regression, irrespective of IPI score (HR = 1.789, 95% CI 1.225–2.611, p = 0.003), indicating that PWPs abnormalities were independent risk factors for the prognosis of DLBCL patients (Table 2; Fig. 4). Additionally, sensitivity analyses were conducted that confirmed the consistent and nearly identical hazard ratios for PWPs abnormalities across different models. This suggests a genuinely worse prognosis for DLBCL patients with PWPs abnormalities (Supplementary Table 3).

Risk factors related to abnormal PWPs

Given that 36.6% of patients with DLBCL had abnormal PWPs and higher all-cause mortality, we identified the risk factors for PWPs in this group. The results indicated that male sex was a risk factor for abnormal PWPs (OR = 2.126, 95% CI 1.344–3.364, p = 0.001), while age, hemoglobin, albumin, B-symptoms, IPI scores and Anthracyclines had no significant correlation with abnormal PWPs (p > 0.05) (Table 3).

Discussion

DLBCL is known to elevate the risk of arrhythmias, particularly AF5,24. AF and malignancies share common risk factors, including age, smoking, alcohol abuse, obesity. These common risk factors may contribute to the association between AF and malignancies through mechanisms such as inflammation, oxidative stress, imbalance in apoptosis and anti-apoptosis25. Previous studies have demonstrated an independent correlation between PWPs and a substantially increased risk of AF18,26–29. However, limited evidence exists regarding the relationship between PWPs and DLBCL. In this study, we aimed to addressed the association between PWPs and all-cause mortality in DLBCL patients and we identified PWPs as independent prognostic markers for all-cause mortality in DLBCL patients.

Patients diagnosed with DLBCL exhibit a predisposition to AF. This susceptibility may be attributed to various factors, including age-related risk factors, underlying cardiovascular conditions such as hypertension and heart failure, noncardiovascular diseases such as chronic lung disease and diabetes, as well as treatment-related factors4,30. These complications can also exacerbate treatment-related toxicities. Antitumor therapies commonly disrupt the self-balance of the cardiovascular system31, induce inflammatory cytokines, generate excessive ROS, increase the release of inflammatory cytokines and disturb mitochondrial function, ultimately leading to myocardial cell damage32,33. New-onset cardiovascular events, including AF, significantly increases the all-cause mortality of DLBCL10. Therefore, early screening for baseline risk factors, continuous monitoring of abnormal electrocardiographic activity, and timely evaluation of cardiac burden are crucial.

PWPs serve as indicators of atrial structure, size, and electrical activity, with deviations from normal values suggestive of underlying abnormalities. Various PWPs encompass P-wave duration, PTFV1, intra-atrial block, P-wave area, P-wave axis, P-wave voltage, and P-wave dispersion. Prolonged P-wave duration (> 120 ms) often suggests conduction delay or abnormal conduction in the interatrial septum. Prolonged P-wave duration is closely associated with atrial structural remodeling and atrial fibrosis. An abnormal P-wave axis may suggest atrial enlargement, structural abnormalities, or alterations in electrophysiological properties. Abnormal PTFV1 is considered a marker of increased left atrial load, indicating structural and functional changes in the atrium. Advanced interatrial block often indicates significant block in the atrial conduction system, which may be related to arrhythmias caused by abnormal atrial electrical activity. Notably, a population cohort study has provided compelling evidence indicating that abnormal PWPs, specifically PTFV1, P-wave axis, P-wave duration, and advanced interatrial block, exhibit an independent association with an elevated risk of AF and ischemic stroke. Moreover, these abnormalities demonstrate an exceptionally high predictive value in assessing the risk of AF18,26,28,34. Our research identified that the PWPs abnormalities were independently associated with the all-cause mortality of DLBCL (HR = 1.789, 95% CI 1.225–2.611, p = 0.003). Interestingly, we found that the abnormal P-wave axis and abnormal PTFV1 were associated with the all-cause mortality of DLBCL (p = 0.017; p = 0.001), while the P-wave duration and advanced interatrial block were not associated with the risk of death in DLBCL patients (p = 0.632; p = 0.059). Abnormal P-wave parameters are closely associated with changes in atrial structure and function. These abnormalities often increase the risk of atrial fibrillation, heart failure, and stroke, significantly raising the all-cause mortality rate17,22,27. These findings highlight the significance of PWPs as valuable and non-invasive indicators, can help identify high-risk patients, allowing for early intervention to reduce mortality. Moreover, when devising treatment strategies for DLBCL patients, prospective evaluation of the prognostic significance of PWPs should be considered. Particularly, attention should be given to P-wave axis and PTFV1, with regular monitoring of these parameters.

Furthermore, our study investigated the potential risk factors associated with PWPs using a binary logistic regression model. Interestingly, we observed a potential gender-related susceptibility to abnormal PWPs, suggesting that male DLBCL patients may require heightened attention regarding their PWPs during clinical management. We found no significant correlation between anthracycline drug and PWPs abnormalities in this study. Anthracycline drug also did not qualify as a risk factor for PWPs abnormalities. Given that the 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) is a simple, convenient, and cost-effective tool, it can serve as a practical clinical resource for monitoring changes in PWPs and evaluating the prognosis of DLBCL patients.

It is important to note that this study represents the first endeavor to elucidate the latent prognostic value of PWPs on all-cause mortality in DLBCL patients. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, this study was limited by its single-center retrospective design with a relatively small sample size, which may restrict the generalizability of the results. Additionally, the calculation of PTFV1 values relied on manual assessment by ECG experts, which introduces the possibility of measurement errors. Moreover, due to incomplete PWPs information in the ECG reports, we were unable to conduct further analyses comparing different predictive models incorporating various PWPs. Additionally, because of the cultural environment in Northwest China, most patients choose to receive palliative care at home rather than pass away in a hospital ward, even though many have poor prognoses. As a result, except in cases of sudden death, specific causes of death are rarely recorded in medical records, and therefore, there is currently no specific data on lymphoma-specific or cardiac-related mortality. Another point is that we have not yet obtained echocardiographic parameters, so these data could not be included in the predictive models. Finally, the exclusion criteria were relatively less stringent, which might increase the likelihood of PWPs abnormalities and potentially strengthening the association with DLBCL. Further prospective studies are warranted to clarify the precise relationship between PWPs and the clinical prognosis of DLBCL.

Conclusions

Abnormal PWPs, particularly P-wave duration and PTFV1, have been associated with a heightened risk of all-cause mortality in patients with DLBCL. These findings underscore the substantial prognostic value of PWPs in this patient population. Notably, male patients may exhibit a higher susceptibility to alterations in PWPs, necessitating increased vigilance in monitoring these parameters. Therefore, emphasis should be placed on exploring the relationship between PWPs and all-cause mortality rates, along with prospective evaluations of PWPs.

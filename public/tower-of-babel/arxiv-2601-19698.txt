Some examples of DG-Lie formality transfer
Marco Manetti, Gabriele Rossetti

Abstract
. We give a convenient reformulation, a slight generalization and some applications of the formality transfer theorem for DG-Lie algebras.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
17B56,17B70,16T15

1.
Introduction

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism of DG-Lie algebras over
a field of characteristic 0 and let

\mathcal{P}
a property of DG-Lie algebras that is invariant under quasi-isomorphisms.

By a
forward

\mathcal{P}
-transfer theorem
we mean a statement asserting that if the DG-Lie algebra
L
has the property
\mathcal{P}

and the morphism of graded Lie algebras
f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
satisfies certain conditions, then also
M
has the property
\mathcal{P}
. Similarly,
by a
backward

\mathcal{P}
-transfer theorem
we mean a statement asserting that if
M
has the property
\mathcal{P}

and the morphism of graded Lie algebras
f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
satisfies certain conditions, then also
L
has the property
\mathcal{P}
.

For instance, when
\mathcal{P}
=
homotopy abelianity
, that is the property of being quasi-isomorphic to a DG-Lie algebra with trivial bracket, it is well known, and largely used in deformation theory, the following
\mathcal{P}
-transfer theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism of DG-Lie algebras:

(1)

if
M
is homotopy abelian and
f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
is injective, then also
L
is homotopy abelian;

(2)

if
L
is homotopy abelian and
f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
is surjective, then also
M
is homotopy abelian.

The reader can see
[
21
, Cor. 6.1.3]
and
[
1
,
5
,
10
,
13
]
for various proofs of Theorem
1.1
and applications to deformation theory.
Other properties usually considered in literature are:

•

the formality, cf.
[
2
,
20
]
;

•

the quadraticity, cf.
[
3
,
6
]
.

•

a fixed bunch of singularity types of the associated formal Kuranishi family, cf.
[
17
,
22
]
.

It is essential to observe that Theorem
1.1
is specific for homotopy abelianity and does not immediately generalize to other properties. For instance, when
\mathcal{P}
=
formality
, it is easy to give examples of morphisms

f
as above with
M
formal,
f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
injective and
L
not formal; one of them is described in the Subsection
3.4
below.

As already well understood, see for instance
[
7
,
8
,
12
,
16
,
23
,
26
]
, the right context for obstruction to formality of DG-Lie algebras is Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology theory; the classical (non-graded) definition
[
4
,
9
,
11
]
generalizes in a obvious way to the graded case and give cohomology groups
H_{CE}^{p}(L,M)
, where
L
is a graded Lie algebra and
M
is a graded
L
-module (Definition
2.2
).

The main goal of this paper is to prove and produce some applications of the following formality transfer theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(=Theorem
2.5
)
.

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism of DG-Lie algebras and consider the structure of

H^{*}(L)
-module on
H^{*}(M)
induced by the morphism of graded Lie algebras

f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
.

(1)

If
M
is formal and
f_{*}\colon H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(L))\to H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
is injective, then also
L
is formal.

(2)

If
L
is formal and
f^{*}\colon H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(M),H^{*}(M))\to H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
is injective, then also
M
is formal.

Notice that the first item of Theorem
1.2
was already proved in
[
19
]
and has been already applied in the proof of Kaledin–Lehn formality conjecture for polystable sheaves on Enriques and bielliptic surfaces
[
2
]
.
In this paper we add item 2 and we provide a different and slightly simpler proof of item 1.

For reference purposes, we write two obvious corollaries of the first item of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism of DG-Lie algebras. Suppose that

M
is formal and there exists a morphism of
H^{*}(L)
-modules

s\colon H^{*}(M)\to H^{*}(L)
such that
sf=Id_{L}
. Then also
L
is formal.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism of DG-Lie algebras. Assume that
M
is formal, that

f
is injective and that

f(L)
is a direct summand of
M
as
L
-modules.
Then also
L
is formal.

The definition of modules over a DG-Lie algebra is completely unsurprising and will be given in the next section for completeness.
To the best of our knowledge, almost all known interesting applications of the above results rely on Corollary
1.4
.

In the last section
we illustrate some further applications of formality transfer: in particular, we prove Theorem
3.1
about the equivalence of Lie and associative formality for universal enveloping algebras, and Theorem
3.3
, about the persistence of Kodaira–Spencer formality under free action of finite groups.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

Throughout the whole paper, every differential graded (DG) vector space is intended to be defined over a field

\mathbb{K}\,
of characteristic 0,
\mathbb{Z}
-graded and with the differential of degree
+1
. Given a homogeneous vector
x
in a DG-vector space, we shall indicate its degree by
\overline{x}
. We assume that the reader is also familiar with the notion of (differential) graded Lie algebra.

For the benefit of the non-expert reader it is worth recalling
that a DG-Lie algebra is formal if it is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology algebra.

Proposition 2.1
.

For a DG-Lie algebra
L
over a field of characteristic 0, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

L
is formal;

(2)

there exists a span
L\leftarrow M\rightarrow H^{*}(L)
of surjective quasi-isomorphisms of DG-Lie algebras;

(3)

there exists an
L_{\infty}
-morphism
H^{*}(L)\rightsquigarrow L
lifting the identity in cohomology.

Proof.

This is a well known result; a proof can be found, for instance, in
[
21
]
.
∎

Let
(L,d,[-,-])
be a DG-Lie algebra. By definition, an
L
-module is a DG-vector space
M
equipped with a morphism of DG-vector spaces

M\otimes L\xrightarrow{\;*\;}M,\qquad m\otimes x\mapsto m*x,

such that

m*[x,y]=(m*x)*y-(-1)^{\overline{x}\,\overline{y}}(m*y)*x.

For instance, if
f\colon L\rightarrow M
is a morphism of DG-Lie algebras, then
M
is an
L
-module via the adjoint representation
m*x=[m,f(x)]
.

Given a DG-Lie algebra
L
and an
L
-module
M
, we recall from
[
19
]
the construction of the associated Chevalley–Eilenberg double complex
(CE(L,M)^{*,*},\delta,\overline{\delta})
.

We have:

(1)

CE(L,M)^{p,q}=\operatorname{Hom}^{q}_{\mathbb{K}}\,(L^{\wedge p},M)
; in particular

CE(L,M)^{p,q}=0
for
p<0
and
CE(L,M)^{0,q}=M^{q}
.

(2)

\overline{\delta}\colon CE(L,M)^{p,q}\rightarrow CE(L,M)^{p,q+1}
is the natural differential of the complex
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathbb{K}}\,(L^{\wedge p},M)
, that is

(\overline{\delta}\phi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})=d(\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{p}))-\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{\overline{\phi}+\overline{x_{1}}+\dots+\overline{x_{i-1}}}\phi(x_{1},\dots,dx_{i},\dots,x_{p})

(3)

\delta
is the natural extension of the classical Chevalley–Eilenberg differential (with
an inessential different choice of signs motivated by the décalage isomorphisms, cf.
[
19
, Section 7]
). More precisely:

(a)

for
m\in CE(L,M)^{0,q}=M^{q}
we have
(\delta m)(x)=(-1)^{q}m*x
;

(b)

for
\phi\in CE(L,M)^{1,q}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}\,}^{q}(L,M)
we have

(\delta\phi)(x,y)=(-1)^{q+1}\left(\phi(x)*y-(-1)^{\overline{x}\;\overline{y}}\phi(y)*x-\phi([x,y])\right);

(c)

for
p\geq 2
and
\phi\in CE(L,M)^{p-1,q}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}\,}^{q}(L^{\wedge p-1},M)
we have:

\begin{split}(\delta\phi)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})&=(-1)^{q+p-1}\sum_{i}\chi_{i}\,\phi(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{p})*x_{i}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{q+p}\sum_{i<j}\chi_{i,j}\,\phi(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{p},[x_{i},x_{j}]),\end{split}

where the
\chi_{i},\chi_{i,j}\in\{\pm 1\}
are the antisymmetric Koszul signs, determined by the equalities in

L^{\wedge p}
:

x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{p}=\chi_{i}\;x_{1}\wedge\cdots\widehat{x_{i}}\cdots\wedge x_{p}\wedge x_{i}=\chi_{i,j}\;x_{1}\wedge\cdots\widehat{x_{i}}\cdots\widehat{x_{j}}\cdots\wedge x_{p}\wedge x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\;.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
L,M
as above, the Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology of
L
with coefficients in
M
is the cohomology of the total complex
\operatorname{Tot}^{\Pi}(CE(L,M))

(see
[
28
, 1.2.6]
):

H^{*}_{CE}(L,M)=H^{*}(\operatorname{Tot}^{\Pi}(CE(L,M))),\quad\operatorname{Tot}^{\Pi}(CE(L,M))^{n}=\prod_{p+q=n}CE(L,M)^{p,q}.

It is worth to point out that, when both
L
and
M
have trivial differential, then
\operatorname{Tot}^{\Pi}(CE(L,M))
is the direct product of the subcomplexes
(CE(L,M)^{*,q},\delta)
.

We denote by
(E(L,M)_{r}^{p,q},d_{r})
the cohomological spectral sequence associated to the first filtration of the double complex
(CE(L,M)^{*,*},\delta,\overline{\delta})
. In particular,

E(L,M)_{0}^{p,q}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}\,}^{q}(L^{\wedge p},M),\qquad d_{0}=\overline{\delta}.

By Künneth formula
there exist natural isomorphisms

E(L,M)_{1}^{p,q}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}\,}^{q}(H^{*}(L)^{\wedge p},H^{*}(M))=E(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))_{1}^{p,q},\qquad d_{1}=\delta,

and therefore

H_{CE}^{n}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))\cong\prod_{p\geq 0}E(L,M)_{2}^{p,n-p}.

If
f\colon L\to M
is a morphism of DG-Lie algebras, then a particular element

e_{f}\in E(L,M)^{1,0}_{2}
, called
Euler class
, is defined; by definition
e_{f}
is the cohomology class of the Euler derivation

\epsilon\in E(L,M)_{1}^{1,0}=\operatorname{Hom}^{0}_{\mathbb{K}\,}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M)),\qquad\epsilon(x)=\overline{x}\,f(x);

the verification that
d_{1}(\epsilon)=0
is given in
[
19
]
and, in any case, is
completely straightforward.

If
f
is the identity map on a DG-Lie algebra
L
we simply write

e_{L}
instead of
e_{\operatorname{Id}_{L}}
. It is important to observe that, while
e_{f}
depends only on the
morphism of graded Lie algebras
f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
, the value

d_{2}(e_{f})\in E(L,M)_{2}^{3,-1}\subseteq H_{CE}^{2}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
depends on
f\colon L\to M
. In fact, the vanishing of
d_{2}(e_{L})
is essentially equivalent to the vanishing of all triple Massey products in
L

[
27
]
.

Whenever
d_{2}(e_{f})=0
we continue to denote by
e_{f}\in E(L,M)^{1,0}_{3}
its cohomology class; recursively, if
d_{2}(e_{f})=\cdots=d_{r}(e_{f})=0
for some
r\geq 2
we still denote by
e_{f}\in E(L,M)^{1,0}_{r+1}
the class of the Euler derivation.

Finally, both the spectral sequence and the Euler class behave functorially with respect to morphisms of DG-Lie algebras. This means that, given two morphisms
L\xrightarrow{f}M\xrightarrow{g}N
of DG-Lie algebras, we have two morphisms of spectral sequences

E(L,M)_{*}\xrightarrow{\;g_{*}\;}E(L,N)_{*}\xleftarrow{\;f^{*}\;}E(M,N)_{*},

such that
g_{*}(e_{f})=e_{gf}=f^{*}(e_{g})
.
According to the previous description of the page
E(L,N)_{1}
,
if
g
(resp.:
f
) is a quasi-isomorphism, then
g_{*}
(resp.:
f^{*}
) is an isomorphism at the page

E(L,N)_{r}
, for every
r\geq 1
.

We recall from
[
19
]
two results, whose (non-trivial) proof requires
the extension of Chevalley–Eilenberg double complex to
L_{\infty}
-algebra, cf.
[
24
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
19
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

A DG-Lie algebra
L
is formal if and only if the Chevalley–Eilenberg spectral sequence
(E(L,L)^{p,q}_{r},d_{r})
degenerates at
E_{2}
, i.e. if and only if

d_{r}=0
for every
r\geq 2
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(E(L,M)^{p,q}_{r},d_{r})
the Chevalley–Eilenberg spectral sequence of
a morphism of DG-Lie algebras

f\colon L\to M
and let
e_{f}\in E(L,M)^{1,0}_{2}
be its Euler class. Assume that

d_{2}(e_{f})=\cdots=d_{r-1}(e_{f})=0
for some
r>2
, then

d_{k}=0
for every
2\leq k<r
. In particular,

E(L,M)^{p,q}_{r}=E(L,M)^{p,q}_{2}
for every
p,q
.

Proof.

Immediate consequence of Lemma 5.3 and Proposition 5.5 of

[
19
]
, together with the existence of minimal
L_{\infty}
models.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism of DG-Lie algebras and consider the structure of

H^{*}(L)
-module on
H^{*}(M)
induced by the morphism of graded Lie algebras

f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
.

(1)

If
M
is formal and
f_{*}\colon H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(L))\to H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
is injective, then also
L
is formal.

(2)

If
L
is formal and
f^{*}\colon H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(M),H^{*}(M))\to H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
is injective, then also
M
is formal.

Proof.

The morphism of DG-Lie algebra
f
induces two morphisms of double complexes

\textstyle{CE(L,L)^{*,*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}\;}
\textstyle{CE(L,M)^{*,*}}
\textstyle{CE(M,M)^{*,*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\;f^{*}}

and therefore two morphisms of spectral sequences

(2.1)

such that
f_{*}(e_{L})=e_{f}=f^{*}(e_{M})
.

According to the previous description of the second page of the spectral sequence we have:

(1)

the morphism
f_{*}\colon H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(L))\to H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
is injective if and only if
f_{*}\colon E(L,L)^{p,2-p}_{2}\to E(L,M)^{p,2-p}_{2}
is injective for every
p
.

(2)

the morphism
f^{*}\colon H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(M),H^{*}(M))\to H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
is injective if and only if
f^{*}\colon E(M,M)^{p,2-p}_{2}\to E(L,M)^{p,2-p}_{2}
is injective for every
p
.

1) Assume
M
formal and
f_{*}\colon H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(L))\to H^{2}_{CE}(H^{*}(L),H^{*}(M))
injective. By Lemma
2.3
the spectral sequence
E(M,M)_{r}
degenerates at
E_{2}
. Since
d_{r}(e_{f})=f^{*}(d_{r}(e_{M}))=0
for every
r\geq 2
, according to
Lemma
2.4
we have
E(L,M)^{p,q}_{r}=E(L,M)^{p,q}_{2}
for every
p,q
and every
r\geq 2
.

To conclude the proof of item 1 it is therefore sufficient to show that, if

E(L,L)_{2}=E(L,L)_{r}
for some integer

r>2
(equivalently if
d_{2}=\cdots=d_{r-1}=0
), then
d_{r}(e_{L})=0
in the group

E(L,L)^{r+1,1-r}_{r}
. This is immediate since
E(L,M)_{r}
degenerates at the second page,

f_{*}(e_{L})=e_{f}
and

f_{*}\colon E(L,L)^{r+1,1-r}_{r}=E(L,L)^{r+1,1-r}_{2}\to E(L,M)^{r+1,1-r}_{2}=E(L,M)^{r+1,1-r}_{r}

is injective.

2) The proof of the second item is essentially the same. If
L
is formal then

E(L,L)_{r}
degenerates at
E_{2}
and, since
e_{f}=f_{*}(e_{L})
, as above we obtain that also

E(L,M)_{r}
degenerates at
E_{2}
. If
f^{*}\colon E(M,M)^{p,2-p}_{2}\to E(L,M)^{p,2-p}_{2}
is injective for every
p
and
E(M,M)_{2}=E(M,M)_{r}
for some integer

r>2
then the morphism
f^{*}\colon E(M,M)^{p,2-p}_{r}\to E(L,M)^{p,2-p}_{r}
is injective,

f^{*}(d_{r}(e_{M}))=d_{r}(e_{f})
vanishes in
E(L,M)^{r+1,1-r}_{2}
and therefore

d_{r}(e_{M})=0
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

It would be interesting to give a direct, and possibly simpler, proof of Corollary
1.4
that does not rely on Theorem
1.2
.

It is plain that Corollary
1.3
holds a fortiori if

f\colon H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
has a left inverse
s\colon H^{*}(M)\to H^{*}(L)

in the category of graded Lie algebras.
Under this additional assumption there exists a simpler proof as a consequence of Proposition
2.1
: the formality of

M
implies the existence of an
L_{\infty}
quasi-isomorphism

g\colon M\rightsquigarrow H^{*}(M)
and the composition
sgf
provides the formality of
L
.
However, as mentioned above, this weaker version of Corollary
1.3

does not seem very useful in the applications.

An essentially similar proof can be used to prove that if
L
is formal and there exists a morphism of graded Lie algebras

s\colon H^{*}(M)\to H^{*}(L)
such that
fs=Id_{M}
, then also
M
is formal; this also
follows immediately from Theorem
1.2
.

3.
Examples and applications

3.1.
Universal Enveloping Algebras

For every DG-Lie algebra
L
we denote by
U(L)
its universal enveloping algebra; it is defined, mutatis mutandis, as in the classical case, i.e. as the quotient
T(L)/I
where

T(L)
is the DG-associative tensor algebra generated by the differential graded vector space underlying
L
, and
I
is the ideal generated by elements of type
x\otimes y-(-1)^{\overline{x}\,\overline{y}\,}y\otimes x-[x,y]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a DG-Lie algebra
L
the following properties are equivalent:

(1)

L
is formal;

(2)

U(L)
is formal as differential graded associative algebra;

(3)

U(L)
is formal as differential graded Lie algebra.

The equivalence of the first two items in Theorem
3.1
was proved, in a different way, by Saleh in
[
26
]
.

Proof.

The implication
2)\Rightarrow 3)
is obvious and the
implication
1)\Rightarrow 2)
follows easily from the existence of the natural isomorphism
of graded associative algebras
H^{*}(U(L))=U(H^{*}(L))

[
25
, B.2.1]
.

The proof of the implication
3)\Rightarrow 1)
relies on the
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt (PBW) theorem. If
S(L)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}L^{\odot n}
denotes the symmetric DG-algebra generated by
L
and
i\colon L\to U(L)
is the natural map, the PBW theorem
[
25
, B.2.3]
asserts in particular that the linear map

e\colon S(L)\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}U(L),\quad e(x_{1}\odot\dots\odot x_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\epsilon(\sigma)\,i(x_{\sigma(1)})\cdots i(x_{\sigma(n)}),

where
\epsilon(\sigma)
is the Koszul sign and
\Sigma_{n}
the symmetric group, is an isomorphism of DG-vector spaces. It is well known that
e
is also an isomorphism of graded coalgebras, although this property is not relevant in this proof.

Given
x\in L
, we denote by
r_{x}\colon S(L)\to S(L)
the unique derivation of the symmetric algebra such that
r_{x}(y)=[y,x]
for every
y\in L
. A straightforward computation, see
[
15
, Lemma 3.3.5]
for further details, shows that
for every
x\in L
and every
z\in S(L)
we have
e(r_{x}(z))=[e(z),i(x)]
.

This implies that
H:=e(\oplus_{n\not=1}L^{\odot n})
is an
L
-submodule of
U(L)
and we have
a direct sum decomposition

U(L)=i(L)\oplus H
. According to Corollary
1.4
, if
U(L)
is formal as a DG-Lie algebra, then also
L
is formal.
∎

3.2.
Invariant subalgebras

We begin with an equivalent formulation of Corollary
1.4

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
M
be a formal DG-Lie algebra and let
L\subseteq M
be a DG-Lie subalgebra. Assume that there exists a retraction of
L
-modules
p\colon M\to L
, i.e. a
morphism of differential graded vector spaces
p\colon M\to L
such that

(3.1)

p(x)=x,\quad p([x,y])=[x,p(y)]\quad\text{ for every }\quad x\in L,\,y\in M.

Then also
L
is formal.

Proof.

We have

M=L\oplus\ker p
with
[L,\ker p]\subseteq\ker p
, and the formality of
L
follows from Corollary
1.4
.
∎

A typical situation where Corollary
3.2
applies is when
L=M^{G}
is the subalgebra of invariant elements under a finite group
G
of automorphism of
M
, and
p
is given by the usual
operator

p(x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g(x).

More generally, the same as above holds when
G
is a linearly reductive algebraic group acting on
M
and every
M^{i}
is a finitely supported representation (we refer to
[
3
]
for the definition and the main properties of finitely supported representations).

A geometric example of this situation is described in
[
2
, Section 5]
, where

L
is the DG-Lie algebra of derived endomorphisms of a polystable sheaf on a surface with torsion canonical bundle.

3.3.
Quotients by finite free regular actions

Let
X
be a smooth manifold and let
L=KS_{X}
be its
Kodaira–Spencer DG-Lie algebra; recall that

KS_{X}
is the homotopy class of the DG-Lie algebra controlling infinitesimal deformations of
X
(see e.g.
[
21
]
) and it is represented either by:

(1)

(complex case) the Dolbeault complex of the holomorphic tangent sheaf, or

(2)

(algebraic case) the Thom–Whitney totalization of the semicosimplicial
algebra of Čech cochains of the tangent sheaf over an affine open cover.

Following
[
20
]
we say that
X
is Kodaira–Spencer formal if the DG-Lie algebra

KS_{X}
is formal; by general fact, if a compact complex manifold
X
is Kodaira–Spencer formal, then the base germ of its semiuniversal deformation is a quadratic singularity.
It is worth to recall the main result of
[
20
]
: if
X,Y
are compact, Kähler and
Kodaira–Spencer formal complex manifolds, then also the product
X\times Y
is
Kodaira–Spencer formal; for instance,
the product

X=T\times\mathbb{P}^{r}
, with
T
a complex torus,
is Kodaira–Spencer formal (when
r>0
and
\dim T>1
, Kodaira and Spencer proved that
X
has obstructed deformations
[
14
, pag. 436]
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective manifold over an algebraically closed field
of characteristic 0 and let
G
be a finite group acting
freely on
X
. If
X
is Kodaira–Spencer formal, then also the quotient

X/G
is Kodaira–Spencer formal.

Proof.

We refer to
[
21
]
for the definition and the main properties of the Thom–Whitney totalization functor.

Denoting by
Y=X/G
,
by general fact in algebraic geometry, the variety
Y
is smooth projective and the quotient map
\pi\colon X\to Y
is a finite morphism.

For every open subset
U\subseteq Y
the group
G
acts on the space of vector fields

\Gamma(\pi^{-1}(U),\Theta_{X})
and the morphism

\gamma_{U}\colon\Gamma(\pi^{-1}(U),\Theta_{X})\to\Gamma(\pi^{-1}(U),\Theta_{X})^{G}=\Gamma(U,\Theta_{Y})

satisfies the condition (
3.1
).

Taking an affine open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}
of
Y
, then
\mathcal{V}=\{\pi^{-1}(U_{i})\}
is an affine open cover of
X
and the respective
Thom–Whitney
totalizations
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Theta_{Y})
and
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{V},\Theta_{X})
are
DG-Lie algebras representing
KS_{Y}
and
KS_{X}
respectively.

Since
\gamma_{U}
commutes with restrictions to open subsets, it induces a
retraction of
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Theta_{Y})
-modules

\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{V},\Theta_{X})\to\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Theta_{Y})
and we can apply Corollary
3.2

∎

Some results similar to Theorem
3.3
, with homotopy abelianity and unobstructness of associated deformation functor were already known, for instance

[
18
, Ex. 15]

and
[
10
, Thm. 6.2]
.

3.4.
A non-example of formality transfer

Let
M
be the DG-Lie algebra generated, as a graded vector space, by three vectors
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
of degree
+1
and two vectors
h_{1},h_{2}
of degree
+2
. Define
the differential
d
by setting
de_{1}=de_{2}=0
,
de_{3}=h_{1}
, and the nontrivial brackets
[e_{i},e_{j}]
as

[e_{1},e_{1}]=-h_{2},\quad[e_{2},e_{2}]=h_{2}-h_{1},\quad[e_{2},e_{3}]=[e_{3},e_{2}]=h_{2}.

For a generic element
x=x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3}\in M^{1}
the Maurer–Cartan equation
dx+[x,x]/2
is the same as the system of two equations

2x_{3}-x_{2}^{2}=0,\qquad-x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+2x_{2}x_{3}=0,

that, after the elimination of
x_{3}
becomes
x_{1}^{2}=x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}^{3}
.

We have
H^{1}(M)=\operatorname{Span}(e_{1},e_{2})
,
H^{2}(M)=M^{2}/\mathbb{K}\,h_{1}
,
H^{i}(M)=0
for
i\not=1,2
and the induced bracket

[-,-]\colon H^{1}(M)\times H^{1}(M)\to H^{2}(M)
is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form. Therefore, by either the Morse lemma (see
[
27
, Appendix A1]
) or standard formality criteria (see e.g.
[
8
, Thm. 4.1.3]
or
[
19
, text between 3.7 and 3.8]
), the graded Lie algebra
H^{*}(M)
is intrinsically formal, hence
M
is formal.

On the other hand, the subalgebra
L\subset M
spanned by
e_{1}+e_{2},e_{3},h_{1},h_{2}
, is not formal since the Maurer–Cartan equation for
y=y_{2}(e_{1}+e_{1})+y_{3}e_{3}
becomes

y_{2}^{3}=0
(see
[
21
, Ex. 6.7.4]
for more details), although the map
H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(M)
is injective.

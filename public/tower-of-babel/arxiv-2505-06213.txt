Determining monogenity of pure cubic number fields using elliptic curves
Jordi Guàrdia, Francesc Pedret

Abstract
. We study monogenity of pure cubic number fields by means of Selmer groups of certain elliptic curves. A cubic number field with discriminant D D determines a unique nontrivial 𝔽 3 \mathbb{F}_{3} -orbit in the first cohomology group of the elliptic curve E D : y 2 = 4 ​ x 3 + D E^{D}:y^{2}=4x^{3}+D with respect to a certain 3-isogeny ϕ \phi . Orbits corresponding to monogenic fields must lie in the soluble part of the Selmer group S ϕ ( E D / ℚ ) S^{\phi}(E^{D}/\QQ) , and this gives a criterion to discard monogenity. From this, we can derive bounds on the number of monogenic cubic fields in terms of the rank of the elliptic curve. We can also determine the monogenity of many concrete pure cubic fields assuming GRH.



1.
Introduction

The ring of integers of a number field has played a key role in the development of algebraic number theory since its very beginning. The well-known cubic example presented by Dedekind enlightened the difficulty of the problem and posed two initial problems: the determination of the ring of integers of a number field and its monogenity. The search for solutions to both problems has generated a number of important concepts and techniques in number theory. This paper is focused on the second one: we will show how the geometric techniques introduced by Alpöge, Bhargava and Shnidman (
[
1
]
) to study densities of non-monogenic fields can be adapted to prove the non-monogenity of many concrete cubic number fields.

A number field is called monogenic if its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{L}
has a
power integral basis
, that is,
\mathcal{O}_{L}=\ZZ[\alpha]
for some
\alpha\in L
. Even if there are efficient algorithms (
[
14
]
) to determine
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, the monogenity is still an open problem from the computational point of view. Many particular results for families of number fields are known (
[
21
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
11
]
), based on solving Thue equations via Diophantine approximation. In this paper, we study monogenity of cubic number fields by means of elliptic curves, a newer approach introduced by Alpöge, Bhargava and Shnidman in
[
1
]
. While they prove density results, we adapt their ideas to study concrete cubic fields, and succeed in proving the non-monogenity of most of the considered cases. We also provide bounds on the number of monogenic pure cubic fields based on the rank of certain elliptic curves naturally attached to the field.

Given a relative extension
L/K
of number fields,
L
is said to be
monogenic
over
K
if
\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathcal{O}_{K}[\alpha]
for some
\alpha\in L
. There exists a homogeneous
index form

I_{L/K}
on
n-1
variables such that
L
is monogenic over
K
if, and only if, the diophantine equation
I_{L/K}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})=\pm 1
has a solution in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. In this paper, we define
L
to be
quasi-monogenic
if
I_{L/K}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1})=\pm 1
has a
K
-rational solution. While quasi-monogenity is weaker than monogenity, it has a good arithmetic-geometric description. For a given
k\in K
, we denote by
E^{k}
the elliptic curve defined by
Y^{2}=4X^{3}+k
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L/K
be a cubic relative extension of number fields and let
D
be the discriminant of
L
over
K
. Then,
L
is quasi-monogenic over
K
if, and only if,
L
is isomorphic to
L_{P}=K[X]/(X^{3}-x_{0}X^{2}+D)
for some
P=(x_{0},y_{0})\in E^{-27D}(K)
.

This suggests a new strategy to identify all quasi-monogenic cubic fields over
K
with a given discriminant
D
: it suffices to compute the quotient of
E^{-27D}(K)
by
\phi_{D}(E^{D}(K))
for a certain 3-isogeny
\phi_{D}
. Consequently, the computation of the Mordell-Weil group of
E^{-27D}(K)
becomes the crucial step.

For cubic pure number fields
L=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{m})
, the discriminant has the specific form
D=-3n^{2}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and we can show that the corresponding field
L_{P}
is again a pure cubic field and
L_{P}\simeq\QQ\left(\sqrt[3]{\frac{y_{0}-9n}{y_{0}+9n}}\right)
. Indeed, a cubic number field with discriminant of the form
D=-3n^{2}
is always a pure cubic field. We are able to give an efficient algorithm to compute all the quasi-monogenic pure cubic number fields of a given discriminant, given the Mordell-Weil group of
E^{-27D}
. In practice, most fields are not quasi-monogenic, so this allows us to narrow down the possible monogenic fields substantially. As a byproduct of our methods, we obtain sharp bounds for the number of isomorphism classes of quasi-monogenic pure cubic number fields of a given discriminant in terms of
E^{-27D}(\QQ)/\phi_{D}(E^{D}(\QQ))
.

Our approach is the following: Alpöge, Bhargava and Shnidman showed in
[
1
]
how to relate
I_{L/K}
to a class in the
\phi_{D}
-Selmer group of
E^{-27D}
. If
L/K
is monogenic, then said class lies in the soluble part of the
\phi_{D}
-Selmer group. Our methods involve the explicit computation of these classes in order to determine which cubic relative extensions determine cocycles in the soluble part of the
\phi_{D}
-Selmer group.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In section
2
, we provide a brief survey on monogenity of free rank 3 algebras over a Dedekind domain
A
and index forms.

In section
3
, we review the techniques developed by Alpöge, Bhargava, Shnidman and Elkies in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
; they show how to introduce elliptic curves in the monogenity context. The general idea is to relate the monogenity of a cubic number field of discriminant
D
to the existence of a certain rational point on the elliptic curve
E^{D}:Y^{2}=4X^{3}+D
. This naturally leads to the notion of quasi-monogenity, introduced in section
2
.

Some basic bounds on the number of quasi-monogenic cubic fields of fixed discriminant are given in section
4
. In sections
5
and
6
, we develop the machinery necessary to develop, in section
7
, algorithms to determine the monogenity of pure cubic fields assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). Finally, in section
8
, we apply these algorithms to determine the quasi-monogenity of 4555 of the 4619 cubic fields appearing in the LMFDB database (
[
16
]
) whose monogenity was still unknown.

Remark 2.6

For a relative extension

The monogenity condition of an algebra can be checked through a polynomial equation. Fixing a basis

f_{B/A}(x_{1}b_{1}+\dots+x_{n}b_{n})=I_{\mathcal{B}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})b_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge b_{n}

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

be a Dedekind domain and let

-algebra of rank

-basis of the form

Proof.

Since

Now,

Since

∎

Since the class of

is
\spl_{2}(K)
-equivalent to

for some

. A suitable linear change of variables given by a matrix in
\spl_{2}(K)
changes this factor into

, so

is
\spl_{2}(K)
-equivalent to
Yf_{2}(X,Y)
, for some binary quadratic homogeneous form
f_{2}
. By completing squares, scaling coefficients and imposing that the discriminant of
f_{1}
is
D
, we have that
f_{1}
is
\spl_{2}(K)
-equivalent to
f_{0}
. In particular, any form in
\textnormal{Sym}^{3}(K^{2})
is
\spl_{2}(\overline{K})
-equivalent to
f_{0}
. This induces a
\overline{K}
-isomorphism of coverings
C_{f}\to C_{f_{0}}
, and note that

to

. The result of these constructions is that

is a

-covering, where

. Under these circumstances, we can give

the structure of a homogeneous space over

(see
[

, §X.3]
for a survey on homogeneous spaces); let

be a

-isomorphism of coverings. Then, the map

(P,Q)\longmapsto\psi^{-1}(P+\psi(Q)):E^{D}\times C\to C

is independent of the choice of

and defines a simply transitive action of
E^{D}
on
C
. In particular,

is the jacobian of

(see
[

, §X, Thm. 3.8]
). Explicitly,

(2)

\phi_{D}:(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{x^{3}+D}{x^{2}},\frac{y(x^{3}-2D)}{x^{3}}\right).

Note that
\phi_{D}
is a 3-isogeny with kernel

. Its dual isogeny is

\hat{\phi}_{D}:(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{x^{3}-27D}{9x^{2}},\frac{y(x^{3}+54D)}{27x^{3}}\right),

whose kernel is
\langle(0,\sqrt{27D})\rangle

(\zeta\sqrt[3]{D},\pm\sqrt{-3D})
by
\phi_{D}
, where

ranges from the cubic roots of the unit.

By the twisting principle, the first Galois cohomology group

parametrizes isomorphism classes of

-coverings (see
[
7
, §2]
). Explicitly, the class corresponding to a

-covering

is
[\sigma\mapsto\psi^{\sigma}\psi^{-1}]
, where
\psi
is any
\overline{K}
-isomorphism of coverings between
C
and

. In the next lemma,

denotes the equivalence relation defined as follows: any two
\phi
-coverings

and
(C^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
are equivalent if

and
(C^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
are
K
-isomorphic as
\phi
-coverings.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B
be a free cubic
A
-algebra. Then, identifying the set of isomorphism classes of
\phi_{D}
-coverings with
H^{1}(K,E^{D}[\phi_{D}])
, we have

\{(C_{\mathcal{B}},\pi_{I_{\mathcal{B}}})|\mathcal{B}\textnormal{ is an }A\textnormal{-basis of }B\}/\sim\ =\{\pm\alpha_{B}\},

for some nonzero
\alpha_{B}\in H^{1}(K,E^{D}[\phi_{D}])
.

Proof.

Fix an
A
-basis
\mathcal{B}_{0}
of
B
and denote by
\alpha_{B}
the element in
H^{1}(K,E^{D}[\phi_{D}])
corresponding to
(C_{\mathcal{B}_{0}},\pi_{I_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}})
. By
2.15
, for any other basis
\mathcal{B}
, we have that
I_{\mathcal{B}}=\gamma\star I_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}
for some
\gamma\in\gl_{2}(A)
. This gives a map
\psi:C_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\to C_{\mathcal{B}}
given in affine coordinates by
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y)\cdot\gamma^{-1}
. Then, one can check that
\pi_{I_{\mathcal{B}}}\circ\psi=\pm\pi_{I_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}}
, so
(C_{\mathcal{B}},\pi_{I_{\mathcal{B}}})
corresponds to
\pm\alpha_{B}
. In particular, when

, then
(C_{\mathcal{B}},\pi_{I_{\mathcal{B}}})=-\alpha_{B}
, so both
\alpha_{B}
and

occur for some suitable choices of

-bases. Since

is irreducible by

,

is not zero.
∎

This way,

determines a non-trivial
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-orbit in

, where
\mathbb{F}_{3}
acts by repeated addition.

Next, let

be another finite free

-algebra of rank 3 and discriminant

. Let

be its field of fractions, which is a cubic extension of

, and assume

. Then, by (

),

, so

is reducible over

whereas

is not. Thus,

is in the kernel of

but
\alpha_{L^{\prime}}
is not. This proves the following result:

Proposition 3.4
.

[
1
, Lemma 15]

If
L
and
L^{\prime}
are non-isomorphic cubic extensions of
K
of the same discriminant

, then
\alpha_{L}
and
\alpha_{L^{\prime}}
are linearly independent in

.

Now,
H^{1}(K,E^{D}[\phi_{D}])
sits in the middle of the Kummer exact sequence

E^{-27D}(K)\longrightarrow H^{1}(K,E^{D}[\phi_{D}])\longrightarrow H^{1}(K,E^{D}).

Recall that the

is the Weil-Châtelet group

, which parametrises homogeneous spaces up to

-isomorphism. In terms of

coverings and homogeneous spaces, the maps of the Kummer exact sequence

is the trivial class in the Weil-Châtelet group

, i.e. if

has a rational point. To study this kernel, we may restrict ourselves to

-coverings and homogeneous spaces having a point everywhere locally, which can be achieved through the

-Selmer and Tate-Shafarevich groups.

Definition 3.5
.

[
19
, p. 332]

Let

be an isogeny over

. Then, the

where

In our context,

The kernel of

in

.

Since

is a 3-isogeny, we have a natural map

.

[

, Lem. 9.1]

Let

and

The map

. The result follows from the fact that each pair of non-trivial points in

In the particular case when

5.
The cocycle associated to cubic fields

Now, we shall focus on relative extensions of number fields, i.e.

and

, where

is a number field and

is a cubic extension of

. Let

be the discriminant of

over

and let

be a primitive element of

over

.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose we have a cocycle
\zeta:\gal(\overline{K}/K)\to E^{D}[\phi_{D}]
given by

\zeta:\sigma\mapsto\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\beta)=\beta,\\
(0:\sqrt{D}:1)&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\beta)=\beta^{\prime},\\
Q&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\beta)=\beta^{\prime\prime},\end{cases}

where
\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}
are different conjugates of
\beta
. Then,
Q=-(0:\sqrt{D}:1)
.

Proof.

Denote
P_{D}=(0:\sqrt{D}:1)
. We can restrict to
K
-automorphisms of the Galois closure of
L
. We distinguish two cases. If
D
is a square, then

is Galois over
K
and
\gal(L/K)\simeq C_{3}
. Let
\sigma
be a generator of
\gal(L/K)
such that
\sigma(\beta)=\beta^{\prime}
. Then,
\zeta(\sigma^{2})=\zeta(\sigma)^{\sigma}+\zeta(\sigma)=-P
. Otherwise, if

is not a square, then

is not Galois over

. Let
\tilde{L}
be its Galois closure, equal to

. We have

. The Galois group is generated by

(5)

\sigma:\binom{\beta\mapsto\beta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{D}\mapsto\sqrt{D}},\qquad\tau:\binom{\beta\mapsto\beta}{\sqrt{D}\mapsto-\sqrt{D}}

Using the cocycle identity as before, one checks that
\zeta(\sigma^{2})=\zeta(\sigma\tau)=-P_{D}
and
\zeta(\sigma^{2}\tau)=P_{D}
, as we wanted.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-orbit of the class of

\xi_{L}:\sigma\mapsto\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\beta)=\beta\\
(0:\sqrt{D}:1)&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\beta)=\beta^{\prime}\\
-(0:\sqrt{D}:1)&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\beta)=\beta^{\prime\prime}\\
\end{cases}

is the orbit of

in

. In particular, if

for some

,

admits the expression

\xi_{L}:\sigma\mapsto\log_{\mu_{3}}\left(\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}\right)(0:\sqrt{D}:1),

where

is a primitive cubic root of the unit.

Proof.

Let
I_{L/K}
be the index form of
L
over
K
. Since

is reducible over
L
, we have that

is

-equivalent to

. This induces a

-isomorphism of coverings

. The orbit associated to

is given by

, where

is the class of

. Since

is defined over

, any two elements of

yielding the same restriction to

∎

6.
From points of the jacobian to elements in cohomology

Our general approach is based on the following idea. The soluble part of
S^{\phi_{D}}(E^{D}/\QQ)
is parametrised by
E^{-27D}(K)/\phi_{D}(E^{D}(K))
. Given generators of the latter, if we are able to explicitly describe their image in
S^{\phi_{D}}(E^{D}/\QQ)
and distinguish the classes corresponding to fields, then the problem of determining all the quasi-monogenic cubic number fields is reduced to computing generators of
E^{-27D}(K)/\phi_{D}(E^{D}(K))
. The following theorem is the fundamental step towards relating all of these objects. Recall that, for
P
in
E^{D}(K)
, we define
L_{P}=K[X]/(X^{3}-x_{0}X^{2}+D).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
P=(x_{0},y_{0})\in E^{-27D}(K)\setminus\phi_{D}(E^{D}(K))
. The image of
[P]
in
S^{\phi_{D}}(E^{D}/K)
is, up to sign, the class of

\xi_{P}:\sigma\mapsto\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha,\\
(0:\sqrt{D}:1)&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha^{\prime},\\
-(0:\sqrt{D}:1)&\textnormal{if }\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha^{\prime\prime},\\
\end{cases}

where
\alpha
is any primitive element of
L_{P}
and
\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}
are conjugates of
\alpha
. In particular, if the discriminant of

is

, then

is quasi-monogenic.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a preimage of
P
by
\phi_{D}
and let
\alpha
be its first component. The relation
\phi_{D}(Q)=P
shows that
\alpha^{3}-x_{0}\alpha^{2}+D=0
. Now, formula (
2
) shows that the second component of

is in
K(\alpha)
. Different elements of

yielding the same restriction to

map to the same element by

, so the expression for the cocycle follows from lemma (

). When

has discriminant

, this cocycle is, up to sign,

(see

), so

defines a soluble orbit of

. Thus,

is quasi-monogenic.
∎

Proof of theorem
1.1
. If
L
is isomorphic to

for some

, then

is quasi-monogenic by lemma

. ∎

7.
Pure cubic number fields

We shall focus on pure cubic number fields, i.e. cubic extensions of the form
L=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{m})
, where
m
is a cube-free integer. We can write
m=hk^{2}
with
h,k
coprime integers. The expression for the discriminant of

depends on

modulo 9:

(i)

If
m\not\equiv\pm 1
modulo 9, then
\disc(L)=-27(hk)^{2}
. Using the same notation as in
[
3
, §5]
, We will refer to these fields as
Dedekind type I fields.

(ii)

If
m\equiv\pm 1
modulo 9, then
\disc(L)=-3(hk)^{2}
. As in
[
3
, §5]
, we will call these fields
Dedekind type II fields
.

Thus, the discriminant of a pure cubic number field is of the form
-3n^{2}
,

. Conversely, if the discriminant of a cubic number field is of the form
-3n^{2}
, then it is a pure cubic number field. Indeed,
\disc(L)
can be written uniquely as
df^{2}
, where
d
is a fundamental discriminant, and pure cubic number fields correspond to
d=-3
(see
[
6
, §6.4.5]
). We shall fix
D=-3n^{2}
for some
n\in\ZZ
and a point
P=(x_{0},y_{0})
on
E^{-27D}(\QQ)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

L_{P}
is a pure cubic number field.

Proof.

The discriminant of
f
is
-y_{0}^{2}D
. Write
L_{P}=\QQ(\alpha)
. Since
\Delta(f)=[\mathcal{O}_{L_{P}}:\ZZ[\alpha]]^{2}\disc(L_{P})
, we have that
\disc(L_{P})
is of the form
-3n^{\prime 2}
for some

. Then, the result follows from the previous discussion.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Since
-27D
is a square, we have
E^{-27D}(\QQ)[\hat{\phi}_{D}]=\left\langle(0,\sqrt{-27D})\right\rangle
. The image of this orbit in

is, up to sign, the class of

i.e.

. If

and

modulo 9, then this cocycle is

, which is always monogenic since its index form is

. We shall refer to these fields as

.

Now, our goal is to compute a primitive element of
L
of the form
\sqrt[3]{m}
, with

square-free. By the substitution
\alpha\mapsto\alpha-x_{0}/3
, we note that another defining polynomial for

is

Let
G
be the homogenization of

. By example (

) and lemma (

), we know that

, where

is of the form

for some cube-free integer

. We write

for the coefficient in the

-th row and

-th column of
\gamma
,

.

Lemma 7.3
.

We can write
\gamma_{1,2},\gamma_{2,1},\gamma_{2,2}
in terms of
\gamma_{1,1}
,

and

as follows:

\displaystyle\gamma_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{9m\gamma_{1,1}}

\displaystyle\gamma_{2,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2x_{0}^{3}-27D\pm 9y_{0}n)\gamma_{1,1}}{2x_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle\gamma_{2,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2x_{0}^{3}-27D\mp 9y_{0}n}{18m\gamma_{1,1}}

Proof.

We have
G=\gamma\star F
, so
h_{G}(X,Y)=h_{\gamma\star F}(X,Y)=h_{F}((X,Y)\cdot\gamma)
. Equating the corresponding terms, we obtain the following equalities:

\displaystyle X^{2}:

\displaystyle-x_{0}^{2}=-9m\gamma_{1,1}\gamma_{1,2}

\displaystyle XY:

\displaystyle\frac{-2x_{0}^{3}+27D}{3}=-9m(\gamma_{1,1}\gamma_{2,2}+\gamma_{1,2}\gamma_{2,1})

\displaystyle Y^{2}:

\displaystyle\frac{-x_{0}^{4}}{9}=-9m\gamma_{2,1}\gamma_{2,2}

From the first equality, we get

. From the second equality,

\gamma_{2,2}=\frac{2x_{0}^{3}-27D}{27m\gamma_{1,1}}-\frac{\gamma_{1,2}\gamma_{2,1}}{\gamma_{1,1}}=\frac{2x_{0}^{3}\gamma_{1,1}-3x_{0}^{2}\gamma_{2,1}-27\gamma_{1,1}D}{27m\gamma_{1,1}^{2}}.

Substituting

by this expression in the third equality, we get

which is a quadratic equation in
\gamma_{2,1}
. The discriminant
\Delta
of this equation is
(27y_{0}\gamma_{1,1}n)^{2}
, which is in
(\QQ^{\times})^{2}
. Thus,

\gamma_{2,1}=\frac{(2x_{0}^{3}-27D\pm 9y_{0}n)\gamma_{1,1}}{6x_{0}^{2}}.

Finally,

\gamma_{2,2}=\frac{2x_{0}^{3}\gamma_{1,1}-3x_{0}^{2}\gamma_{2,1}-27\gamma_{1,1}D}{27m\gamma_{1,1}^{2}}=\frac{2x_{0}^{3}-27D\mp 9y_{0}n}{54m\gamma_{1,1}}\qed

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
D=-3n^{2}
for some
n\in\ZZ
. Let
P=(x_{0},y_{0})
be a rational point on
E^{-27D}
and let
L_{P}
be the real field with defining polynomial
X^{3}-x_{0}X^{2}+D
. Then,

L_{P}=\QQ\left(\sqrt[3]{\frac{y_{0}-9n}{y_{0}+9n}}\right).

Proof.

We have that the term in
X^{2}Y
of
\gamma\star F
is

\frac{3\gamma_{1,1}^{2}\gamma_{2,1}-3m\gamma_{1,2}^{2}\gamma_{2,2}}{\det(\gamma)}.

Since
\gamma\star F=G
, this coefficient has to be 0. Thus,

m=\frac{\gamma_{1,1}^{2}\gamma_{2,1}}{\gamma_{1,2}^{2}\gamma_{2,2}}=\frac{729(2x_{0}^{3}-27D\pm 9y_{0}n)}{x_{0}^{6}(2x_{0}^{3}-27D\mp 9y_{0}n)}m^{3}\gamma_{1,1}^{6},

so

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\pm\left(\frac{x_{0}}{3\gamma_{1,1}}\right)^{3}\sqrt{\frac{2x_{0}^{3}-27D\mp 9y_{0}n}{2x_{0}^{3}-27D\pm 9y_{0}n}}=\pm\left(\frac{x_{0}}{3\gamma_{1,1}}\right)^{3}\sqrt{\frac{y_{0}^{2}-81n^{2}\mp 18y_{0}n}{y_{0}^{2}-81n^{2}\pm 18y_{0}n}}

\displaystyle=\pm\left(\frac{x_{0}}{3\gamma_{1,1}}\right)^{3}\frac{y_{0}\mp 9n}{y_{0}\pm 9n}.

Any rational number
m^{\prime}
equivalent to
m
in
\QQ^{\times}/(\QQ^{\times})^{3}
satisfies
\QQ(\sqrt[3]{m^{\prime}})=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{m})
, so

\QQ(\alpha)=\QQ\left(\sqrt[3]{\frac{y_{0}\mp 9n}{y_{0}\pm 9n}}\right)=\QQ\left(\sqrt[3]{\frac{y_{0}-9n}{y_{0}+9n}}\right)

∎

Remark 7.5
.

From this proposition, we can obtain a primitive element for
L
of the form
\sqrt[3]{hk^{2}}
, with
h
and
k
coprime and square-free integers. To do this, let
\pm p_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}}
be the prime decomposition of
(y_{0}-9n)/(y_{0}+9n)
(since this is a rational number, we allow the exponents
e_{i}
to be negative). Then,

\pm p_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}}\equiv p_{1}^{e_{1}\bmod 3}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}\bmod 3}\quad\textnormal{in }\QQ^{\times}/(\QQ^{\times})^{3}.

The latter is a cube free integer and its cubic root is a primitive element for
L
, as we wanted. From now on, we will denote
p_{1}^{e_{1}\bmod 3}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}\bmod 3}
by
m
.

Proposition 7.6
.

Any prime factor of
m
different from 3 divides
n
.

Proof.

Write
x_{0}=a/b
and
y_{0}=c/d
,
(a,b)=1
and
(c,d)=1
. Then,

\frac{y_{0}-9n}{y_{0}+9n}=\frac{c-9nd}{c+9nd}.

Write
\frac{c-9nd}{c+9nd}=p_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}}
. Suppose
p_{i}\nmid n
. We distinguish two cases:

•

If
p_{i}=2
, suppose
2
divides either
c-9nd
or
c+9nd
. If
2|d
, then
0\equiv c\pm 9nd\equiv c
modulo 2, so
c
and
d
would have 2 as a common factor. Thus,
2\nmid c,d
, so

1=\nu_{2}(18nd)=\nu_{2}(c+9nd-(c-9nd))=\min\{\nu_{2}(c+9nd),\nu_{2}(c-9nd)\}.

Since
(x_{0},y_{0})
satisfies
y_{0}^{2}=4x_{0}^{3}-27D
, we have
(c-9nd)(c+9nd)b^{3}=4a^{3}d^{2}
. This means

1+\max\{\nu_{2}(c+9nd),\nu_{2}(c-9nd)\}+3\nu_{2}(b)=3\nu_{2}(a)+2,

which implies that
e_{i}=\nu_{2}\left(\frac{y_{0}-9n}{y_{0}+9n}\right)\equiv 0
modulo 3. Therefore,
2\nmid m
.

•

Otherwise, suppose
p_{i}|c-9nd
. Then,
p_{i}\nmid c+9nd
, since the contrary would imply
c\equiv 9nd\equiv 0
modulo
p_{i}
. This means

\nu_{p_{i}}(c-9nd)+3\nu_{p_{i}}(b)=3\nu_{p_{i}}(a),

so
e_{i}\equiv 0
modulo 3. The same argument applies when
p_{i}|c+9nd
.

Thus, in any case, if
p_{i}\nmid n
, then
p_{i}\nmid m
. This means any
p_{i}
dividing
m
will also divide
n
.
∎

7.1.
Dedekind type II fields

Suppose
3\nmid n
(for example, when
D
is the discriminant of a Dedekind type II field). Remark (
7.2
) states that
L_{(0,\sqrt{-27D})}=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{D})
, which satisfies
3|D
, so the condition “different from 3” in the above proposition cannot be improved in general. Apart from
L_{(0,\sqrt{-27D})}
, there are other instances when
3|m
.

Example 7.7
.

Let
D=-300=-3\cdot(10)^{2}
. A computation in Sage
[
20
]
shows that the free part of the Mordell-Weil group of
E^{-27D}/\QQ
is generated by
P=(-9,72)
. The primitive element of
L_{P}
given by proposition (
7.4
) is
-1/9
, so
L_{P}=\QQ(\sqrt[3]{3})
.

Let
p_{0}=3
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{s}
the prime factors of
n
. Proposition (
7.6
) implies that we can write

(6)

\xi_{P}=(\overline{\lambda}_{0}f_{0}+\overline{\lambda}_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+\overline{\lambda}_{s}f_{s})P_{D},\quad\textnormal{where }f_{i}:\sigma\mapsto\log_{\zeta_{3}}\left(\frac{\sigma(\sqrt[3]{p_{i}})}{\sqrt[3]{p_{i}}}\right),

for some
\overline{\lambda}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}
,
i=0,\dots,s
.

Lemma 7.8
.

Suppose
\xi=(\sum_{i=0}^{s}\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}_{i}f_{i})P_{D}
is another cocycle which yields the same class in
S^{\phi_{D}}(E^{D}/\QQ)
as
\xi_{P}
. Then,
\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}_{i}=\overline{\lambda}_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,s
.

Proof.

Under these hypotheses,
\xi-\xi_{P}
is a coboundary. Suppose it is not 0. Then, it is equal, up to sign, to the cocycle

\delta_{P_{D}}:\sigma\mapsto\left(\frac{\sigma(\sqrt{-3})}{\sqrt{-3}}-1\right)P_{D}.

Let
D^{\prime}=p_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{0}-\lambda_{0}}\cdots p_{s}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{s}-\lambda_{s}}
. Since
\xi-\xi_{P}
is not zero,
D^{\prime}
is different from 1. By construction,
\xi-\xi_{P}
is equal to

\sigma\mapsto\log_{\mu_{3}}\left(\frac{\sigma(\sqrt[3]{D^{\prime}})}{\sqrt[3]{D^{\prime}}}\right)P_{D}.

Let
\tau\in G_{\QQ}
such that
\tau(\sqrt[3]{D^{\prime}})=\sqrt[3]{D}
and
\tau(\sqrt{-3})=-\sqrt{-3}
. Then, the expression for
\xi-\xi_{P}
shows that
(\xi-\xi_{P})(\tau)=0
, whereas
\delta_{P_{D}}(\tau)\neq 0
. Hence,
\xi-\xi_{P}
has to be 0 or, equivalently,
\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}_{i}=\overline{\lambda}_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,s
.
∎

Given generators
P_{0}=(0,\sqrt{-27D}),P_{1},\dots,P_{r}
of
E^{-27D}(\QQ)
, where
r
is the rank of
E^{-27D}(\QQ)
, we construct a matrix
\mathcal{M}
as follows: we denote
\xi_{P_{i}}=(\sum_{j=0}^{s}\overline{\lambda}_{j}^{i}f_{i})(0:\sqrt{D}:1)
. Then, the
i
-th row of
\mathcal{M}
is
\overline{\lambda}^{i}:=(\overline{\lambda}_{0}^{i},\dots,\overline{\lambda}_{s}^{i})
. Thus,
\mathcal{M}
is an
(r+1)\times(s+1)
matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
. For
\overline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}
, let
\lambda
denote the lift of
\overline{\lambda}
to
\{0,1,2\}\subset\ZZ
.

Theorem 7.9
.

E^{-27D}(\QQ)/\phi_{D}(E^{D}(\QQ))
is isomorphic, as an
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-vector space, to the vector space
V
generated by the rows of
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, there is a bijection

\displaystyle\left\{\parbox{180.00027pt}{$\left\langle(\overline{\lambda}_{0},\dots,\overline{\lambda}_{s})\right\rangle\subseteq V\ |\ \overline{\lambda}_{0}=0,\overline{\lambda}_{i}\neq 0$ {for} $i=1,\dots,s\textnormal{ and }p_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots p_{s}^{\lambda_{s}}\equiv\pm 1\mod 9$}\right\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\left\{\parbox{100.00015pt}{{Quasi-monogenic cubic number fields of\\
discriminant }D}\right\}

\displaystyle\left\langle(\overline{\lambda}_{0},\dots,\overline{\lambda}_{s})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\longmapsto\QQ\left(\sqrt[3]{p_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots p_{s}^{\lambda_{s}}}\right)

Proof.

The map
\delta
in the exact sequence (
4
) defines an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-vector spaces
E^{-27D}(\QQ)/\phi_{D}(E^{D}(\QQ))\to\textnormal{Im}(\delta)
. We define a map
\textnormal{Im}(\delta)\to V
as follows: to any
\xi_{P}\in\textnormal{Im}(\delta)
, assign the vector
(\overline{\lambda}_{0},\dots,\overline{\lambda_{s}})\in V
, where
\overline{\lambda}_{i}
are the coefficients described in (
6
). This map is well defined by lemma (
7.8
) and it follows from formula (
6
) that it is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-vector spaces.

The second part of the theorem is a direct consequence of the fact that the conditions imposed on the
\overline{\lambda}_{i}
imply that the resulting cocycle is associated to a quasi-monogenic Dedekind type II field with discriminant
D
. Conversely, the cocycle associated to such a field satisfies the conditions described above (note that the conditions on
D
force any cubic number field of discriminant
D
to be of Dedekind type II).
∎

The reduced row echelon form of
\mathcal{M}
is, up to permutations of the last
s
columns,

(7)

\mathcal{M}_{red}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\textnormal{Id}_{\rho}&*\\
\hline\cr 0&0\end{array}\right),

where
\rho=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}E^{-27D}(\QQ)/\phi_{D}(E^{D}(\QQ))
. By theorem (
7.9
), if a vector
v
in
V
corresponds to a quasi-monogenic Dedekind type II field, then it is of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{s}\mu_{i}\overline{\lambda}^{\prime i}
, where
\overline{\lambda}^{\prime i}
is the
i
-th row of
\mathcal{M}_{red}
,
\mu_{0}=0
and
\mu_{i}\neq 0
for
i=1,\dots,s
. Taking into account that both
v
and
-v
determine the same field, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 7.10
.

The number of quasi-monogenic Dedekind type II fields of discriminant
D
is bounded by
2^{\rho-2}
. ∎

Remark 7.11
.

Note that
\rho\leq r+1
, so
2^{r-1}
is also a bound for the total number of quasi-monogenic Dedekind type II fields of discriminant
D
.

Dedekind type I fields

Now, suppose
3|n
(for example, when
D
is the discriminant of a Dedekind type I field). Write

Proposition 7.12
.

Any prime factor of
m
divides
n^{\prime}
.

Proof.

In view of (
7.6
), we just have to prove that if
3|m
, then
3|n^{\prime}
. Suppose
3\nmid n^{\prime}
and that 3 occurs in the factorization of

. With the same notation as in the proof of (

), we have
(c-27n^{\prime}d)(c+27n^{\prime}d)b^{3}=4a^{3}d^{2}
. Necessarily,
3|c
, so
3\nmid d
. This time, we have
3=\nu_{3}(54n^{\prime}d)=\min\{\nu_{3}(c+27n^{\prime}d),\nu_{3}(c-27n^{\prime}d)\}
, so

3+\max\{\nu_{3}(c+27n^{\prime}d),\nu_{3}(c-27n^{\prime}d)\}+3\nu_{3}(b)=3\nu_{3}(a).

This implies that
\nu_{3}((y_{0}-9n)/(y_{0}+9n))\equiv 0
modulo 3, so 3 does not occur in the factorization of

.
∎

Now, proposition (
7.12
) implies that we can write

\xi_{P}=(\overline{\lambda}_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+\overline{\lambda}_{s}f_{s})P_{D}.

Given generators

for the free part of

, we construct a matrix
\mathcal{M}
as follows: denote

-th row of

is

), we obtain the following result.

Theorem 7.13
.

E^{-27D}(\QQ)/\phi_{D}(E^{D}(\QQ))
is isomorphic, as an
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-vector space, to the vector space
V
generated by the rows of
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, there is a bijection

\displaystyle\left\{\parbox{180.00027pt}{$\left\langle(\overline{\lambda}_{1},\dots,\overline{\lambda}_{s})\right\rangle\subseteq V\ |\ \overline{\lambda}_{i}\neq 0$ {for} $i=1,\dots,s\textnormal{ and }p_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots p_{s}^{\lambda_{s}}\not\equiv\pm 1\mod 9$}\right\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\left\{\parbox{100.00015pt}{{Quasi-monogenic cubic number fields of\\

\displaystyle\left\langle(\overline{\lambda}_{0},\dots,\overline{\lambda}_{s})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\longmapsto\QQ\left(\sqrt[3]{p_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots p_{s}^{\lambda_{s}}}\right)\qed

Remark 7.15

The large | U | |U| expansion for a half-filled asymmetric Hubbard model on a triangular ladder in the presence of spin-dependent magnetic flux
Shota Garuchava, Bachana Beradze, Tatia Sharia, George I. Japaridze

Abstract
We consider a half-filled system of spin-1/2 fermions on a triangular ladder with spin-dependent hopping in the presence of spin-dependent flux. Using the Schrieffer-Wolff transformation, we derive an effective spin Hamiltonian describing the infrared properties of the system in the limit of strong on-site repulsion. The expansion is performed up to the third order in the hopping amplitude over on-site interaction energy. It is shown that the low-energy spin excitations are described by an anisotropic X ​ X ​ Z XXZ Heisenberg model with Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and an unconventional three-spin (correlated exchange) term in the presence of an extended magnetic field. The latter couples not only to z z -projection of the total spin on a triangular plaquette but also to the product of z z -components of three spins located on vertices of the triangle. In the case of strong attractive interaction, the corresponding effective model is derived from the repulsive one using the one-spin-component particle-hole transformation. The effective Hamiltonian is given by the same X ​ X ​ Z XXZ Heisenberg model, as in the repulsive case, but in terms of pseudospin (charge) operators.

Our aim is to derive the low-energy effective Hamiltonian from the exact Hamiltonian (

3.1
Schrieffer-Wolff transformation

In the large-

The

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{eff}}=e^{\mathcal{S}}He^{-\mathcal{S}}\,,

(11)

divided by the on-site interaction energy

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{eff}}=e^{\mathcal{S}}He^{-\mathcal{S}}=H+\left[\mathcal{S},H\right]+\frac{1}{2!}\left[\mathcal{S},\left[\mathcal{S},H\right]\right]+\cdots

(12)

We seek

contains only the terms that preserve the number of doubly occupied sites, up to the desired order in

Denoting the expansion

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{k}}{U^{k}}\,,

(13)

,

\displaystyle\big[V,T^{(k)}_{\bm{m}}\big]=UM^{(k)}_{\bm{m}}T^{(k)}_{\bm{m}}\,,\qquad M^{(k)}_{\bm{m}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}\,,

\displaystyle T^{(k)}_{\bm{m}}:=T_{m_{1}}T_{m_{2}}\cdots T_{m_{k}}\,.

To handle the effects of strong repulsive (attractive) interaction properly, it is

between the local basis states

\displaystyle X^{ab}_{i}=|a\rangle\langle b|\,,

(22)

where
{a,b\in\left\{0,+,-,2\right\}}
, and the site index
i
is not indicated in the right hand side for brevity. To clarify the fermionic sign convention, under
{|2\rangle}
we mean
{|2\rangle\equiv c^{\dagger}_{i+}c^{\dagger}_{i-}|0\rangle}
.

The original fermion creation (annihilation) operators can be expressed in terms of Hubbard operators as

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}=X^{\sigma 0}_{i}+\sigma X^{2\bar{\sigma}}_{i}\,,\quad c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma}=X^{0\sigma}_{i}+\sigma X^{\bar{\sigma}2}_{i}\,.

(23)

Conversely,
X
-operators can be expressed as

\displaystyle X^{2\sigma}_{i}

\displaystyle=\bar{\sigma}n^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{i\bar{\sigma}}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle X^{\sigma 2}_{i}

\displaystyle=\bar{\sigma}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\bar{\sigma}}n^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma}\,,

(24a)

\displaystyle X^{\sigma 0}_{i}

\displaystyle=h^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\bar{\sigma}}c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle X^{0\sigma}_{i}

\displaystyle=c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma}h^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\bar{\sigma}}\,,

(24b)

\displaystyle X^{\sigma\sigma}_{i}

\displaystyle=n_{i\sigma}h_{i\bar{\sigma}}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle X^{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}_{i}

\displaystyle=c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\bar{\sigma}}\,,

(24c)

\displaystyle X^{20}_{i}

\displaystyle=c^{\dagger}_{i+}c^{\dagger}_{i-}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle X^{02}_{i}

\displaystyle=c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i-}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i+}\,,

(24d)

\displaystyle X^{00}_{i}

\displaystyle=h_{i+}h_{i-}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle X^{22}_{i}

\displaystyle=n_{i+}n_{i-}\,.

(24e)

Several properties of
X
-operators are manifest

\displaystyle\sum_{a}X^{aa}_{i}=1\,,\qquad\left(X^{ab}_{i}\right)^{\dagger}=X^{ba}_{i}\,,

(25a)

\displaystyle X^{a_{1}b_{1}}_{i}X^{a_{2}b_{2}}_{i}=\delta_{a_{2}b_{1}}X^{a_{1}b_{2}}_{i}\,.

(25b)

Out of these 16 operators, 8
(X^{2\sigma}_{i}
,
X^{\sigma 2}_{i}
,
X^{\sigma 0}_{i}
,
X^{0\sigma}_{i})
are of the Fermi type and the remaining 8
(X^{\sigma\sigma}_{i}
,
X^{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}_{i}
,
X^{20}_{i}
,
X^{02}_{i}
,
X^{00}_{i}
,
X^{22}_{i})
are of the Bose type.
Two
X
-operators of the Fermi type satisfy the anti-commutation relations

\displaystyle\left\{X^{a_{1}b_{1}}_{i},X^{a_{2}b_{2}}_{j}\right\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\left(\delta_{a_{2}b_{1}}X^{a_{2}b_{1}}_{i}+\delta_{a_{1}b_{2}}X^{a_{2}b_{1}}_{i}\right)\!.

(26a)

Two
X
-operators, where at least one of them is of Bose type, satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle\left[X^{a_{1}b_{1}}_{i},X^{a_{2}b_{2}}_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\left(\delta_{a_{2}b_{1}}X^{a_{2}b_{1}}_{i}-\delta_{a_{1}b_{2}}X^{a_{2}b_{1}}_{i}\right)\!.

(26b)

It is straightforward to represent the hopping terms (
9
) by the
Hubbard operators using Eqs. (
24
)

\displaystyle T_{0}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{ij\sigma}t_{ij}^{\sigma}\left(X^{2\bar{\sigma}}_{i}X^{\bar{\sigma}2}_{j}+X^{\sigma 0}_{i}X^{0\sigma}_{j}\right)\!,

(27a)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{ij\sigma}\sigma t_{ij}^{\sigma}X^{2\bar{\sigma}}_{i}X^{0\sigma}_{j},

(27b)

\displaystyle T_{-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{ij\sigma}\sigma t_{ij}^{\sigma}X^{\sigma 0}_{i}X^{\bar{\sigma}2}_{j}.

(27c)

One also easily verifies that the
X
-operators describing the transitions between
singly occupied states can be rewritten in terms of spin
{S=1/2}
operators as

\displaystyle X_{i}^{+-}=c^{\dagger}_{i+}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i-}=S^{+}_{i}\,,\qquad X_{i}^{-+}=c^{\dagger}_{i-}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i+}=S^{-}_{i}\,,

(28a)

\displaystyle X_{i}^{++}=\frac{1}{2}+S^{z}_{i}\,,\qquad X_{i}^{--}=\frac{1}{2}-S^{z}_{i}\,.

(28b)

Similarly the
X
-operators describing the transitions between doubly occupied and empty states can be rewritten in terms of pseudospin (charge)
\eta
operators, which satisfy commutation rules of spin
{S=1/2}
operators
[
72
]

\displaystyle X_{i}^{20}=c^{\dagger}_{i+}c^{\dagger}_{i-}=\eta^{+}_{i}\,,\qquad X_{i}^{02}=c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i-}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i+}=\eta^{-}_{i}\,,

(29a)

\displaystyle X_{i}^{22}=n_{i+}n_{i-}=\frac{1}{2}+\eta^{z}_{i}\,,\qquad X_{i}^{00}=h_{i+}h_{i-}=\frac{1}{2}-\eta^{z}_{i}\,,

(29b)

where
{\eta^{z}_{i}=(n_{i+}+n_{i-}-1)/2}
.

To summarize, the relations (
28
) and (
29
) allow us to rewrite the effective Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
, expressed in terms of the Hubbard operators, in a final and the most compact form in terms of spin
{S=1/2}
for repulsive interaction and the pseudospin (charge)
{\eta=1/2}
operators in the case of strong attractive coupling, describing respectively the low-energy sectors of spin and charge excitations.

5
Effective spin
S=1/2
Hamiltonian in the case of strong repulsive interaction

Utilizing Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
) in Eqs. (
21
), we obtain effective spin
{S=1/2}
Hamiltonian. (Technical details of the derivation are provided in App.
A
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{-}_{ij}}S^{+}_{i}S^{-}_{j}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right)+\gamma S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}\Big]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}\mathrel{+}\sum_{i<j<k}J_{ijk}^{\vphantom{-}}\bigg\{h_{ijk}^{-}\left(S^{z}_{i}+S^{z}_{j}+S^{z}_{k}-12S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}S^{z}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathrel{+}6\Big[w_{ijk}^{-}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{-}_{ij}}S^{+}_{i}S^{-}_{j}S^{z}_{k}+e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{-}_{jk}}S^{+}_{j}S^{-}_{k}S^{z}_{i}+e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{-}_{ki}}S^{+}_{k}S^{-}_{i}S^{z}_{j}\right)+\mathrm{H.c.}\Big]\bigg\}\,,

(30)

with

\displaystyle J_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\tau_{ij}^{2}}{U}\,,\qquad\gamma=\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{-2}\right)\!/2\,,\quad\left(\gamma\geqslant 1\right),

(31)

\displaystyle J_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{ij}\tau_{jk}\tau_{ki}}{U^{2}}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\theta^{-}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{ij}^{+}-\varphi_{ij}^{-}\,.

(33)

Here
{\tau_{ij}=|t_{ij}^{+}t_{ij}^{-}|^{1/2}}
,
{\lambda=|t_{ij}^{+}/t_{ij}^{-}|^{1/2}}
is independent of
i,j
indices and

\displaystyle h_{ijk}^{-}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{3}\cos\!\left(f_{ijk}^{+}\right)-\lambda^{-3}\cos\!\left(f_{ijk}^{-}\right)\!,

(34)

\displaystyle w_{ijk}^{-}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\vphantom{-1}}\exp\!\left(-\mathrm{i}f_{ijk}^{-}\right)-\lambda^{-1}\exp\!\left(\mathrm{i}f_{ijk}^{+}\right)\!.

(35)

The phase
f_{ijk}^{\sigma}
is equal to either
f_{A}^{\sigma}
or
-f_{B}^{\sigma}
, depending on which triangle the indices
i,j,k
(with
{i<j<k}
) form.

It is instructive to consider several limiting cases where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
acquires a simpler form, in one way or another.
However, analysis of the ground state properties of the corresponding models is out of the scope of this paper.

Let us first focus on the two-spin part of the Hamiltonian (
30
) which constitutes the
XXZ
Heisenberg model with restricted anisotropy
{\gamma\geqslant 1}
and
complex exchange interaction
in
XY
plane. The imaginary part of the complex phase factor of the transverse exchange interaction corresponds to the DMI in the system, with orientation vector along
z
-axis,

\displaystyle\frac{J_{ij}}{2}\mathrm{Im}\!\left\{e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{-}_{ij}}\right\}S^{+}_{i}S^{-}_{j}+\mathrm{H.c.}=D_{ij}^{z}\left(\bm{S}_{i}\times\bm{S}_{j}\right)^{z},\qquad D_{ij}^{z}=J_{ij}\sin\theta^{-}_{ij}\,.

(36)

The complex phase factors, hence the DMI, can be eliminated by gauge transformation if and only if the fluxes are spin-independent, i.e.
{f_{ijk}^{+}=f_{ijk}^{-}}
(see App.
B.1
).
We emphasize that the DMI emerges purely due to spin-dependent flux.
In terms of original fermions, the DMI in Eq. (
36
) describes a spin-current

\displaystyle\left(\bm{S}_{i}\times\bm{S}_{j}\right)^{z}\sim\mathrm{i}\left(c^{\dagger}_{i+}c^{\dagger}_{j-}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i-}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+}-\mathrm{H.c.}\right)\!.

(37)

Here we used Eq. (
28a
) and
{S^{\pm}_{i}=S^{x}_{i}\pm\mathrm{i}S^{y}_{i}}
.
The physical explanation is as follows. Within the projected Hilbert space at half-filling, where there is exactly one particle per site, the only allowed process is the exchange of particles on different sites. During such exchanges, charge is not transferred. Spin is transferred when a single exchange of
\sigma
and
-\sigma
particles occurs, but they happen in opposite directions. For spin-independent flux, average rate in both directions is the same, whereas for spin-dependent flux, it is not. This imbalance is exactly what Eqs. (
36
) and (
37
) describe.

5.1
Limiting cases

We begin this section by considering under what conditions the three-spin terms are absent.
If one of the hopping amplitudes is zero in each triangular plaquette, then
{J_{ijk}=0}
for all
ijk
and hence
{\mathcal{H}^{(3)}_{\mathrm{eff}}\equiv 0}
.
In particular, if the intra-chain hopping amplitudes vanish,
{t_{A}^{\pm}=t_{B}^{\pm}=0}
, then the system is equivalent to a simple spin chain. While if one of the inter-chain hopping amplitudes vanish,
{t_{1}^{\pm}=0}
or
{t_{2}^{\pm}=0}
, then the system constitutes a square ladder.

Assuming
{J_{ijk}\neq 0}
, the condition
{w^{-}_{ijk}=0}
is fulfilled if and only if the hopping amplitudes are spin-independent,
{\lambda=1}
, and the total flux per triangle vanishes,
{f^{+}_{ijk}+f^{-}_{ijk}=0}
. It is straightforward to see that in such case the magnetic field vanishes too,
{h^{-}_{ijk}=0}
.
Below we consider the cases where either
{\lambda\neq 1}
or/and
{f^{+}_{ijk}+f^{-}_{ijk}\neq 0}
and thus, the three-spin terms are present.

Spin-symmetric case

In a spin-symmetric case, i.e.
{\lambda=1}
and
{f_{ijk}^{+}=f_{ijk}^{-}\equiv f_{ijk}}
, the phase factors
{\theta^{-}_{ij}=0}
for any
i
and
j
,
{\gamma=1}
,
{h^{-}_{ijk}=0}
and
w^{-}_{ijk}
are purely imaginary. In that case
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{eff}}
coincides with
XXX
spin chain Hamiltonian, while
\mathcal{H}^{(3)}_{\mathrm{eff}}
reduces to only a scalar spin chirality term

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\left(S^{+}_{i}S^{-}_{j}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right)+S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}\Big]-24\sum_{i<j<k}J_{ijk}\sin\!\left(f_{ijk}\right)\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\left[\bm{S}_{j}\times\bm{S}_{k}\right],

(38)

obtained earlier in Ref.
[
22
]
.
Obviously, the scalar spin chirality term vanishes together with the flux,
{f_{ijk}=0}
mod
\pi
.
This model is exactly solvable by the Bethe ansatz at the special point in the parameter space. In Ref.
[
73
]
, the magnetic phase diagram was obtained in
{\kappa-h}
plane, where the single parameter
\kappa
determines all the coupling constants in the special case and
h
is the magnetic field. Numerical (density matrix renormalization group) analysis of the correlation functions for the Hamiltonian, consisting of the scalar spin chirality terms only, has been done in Ref.
[
74
]
.

Zero-flux limit

Another insightful case is the zero-flux case,
{f_{ijk}^{+}=f_{ijk}^{-}=0}
, but with non-zero asymmetry in hopping amplitudes,
{\lambda\neq 1}
. In this case the phase factors
{\theta^{-}_{ij}=0}
and
{h_{ijk}^{-}\equiv h^{-}}
,
{w_{ijk}^{-}\equiv w^{-}}
are independent of the indices.
The effective Hamiltonian then reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\left(S^{+}_{i}S^{-}_{j}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right)+\gamma S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}\Big]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}\mathrel{+}\sum_{i<j<k}J_{ijk}\bigg\{h^{-}\left(S^{z}_{i}+S^{z}_{j}+S^{z}_{k}-12S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}S^{z}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathrel{+}12w^{-}\Big[\left(\bm{S}^{\vphantom{z}}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}^{\vphantom{z}}_{j}\right)S^{z}_{k}+\left(\bm{S}^{\vphantom{z}}_{j}\cdot\bm{S}^{\vphantom{z}}_{k}\right)S^{z}_{i}+\left(\bm{S}^{\vphantom{z}}_{k}\cdot\bm{S}^{\vphantom{z}}_{i}\right)S^{z}_{j}-3S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}S^{z}_{k}\Big]\bigg\}\,,

(39)

where

\displaystyle h^{-}=\lambda^{3}-\lambda^{-3}\,,\quad w^{-}=\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\,.

(40)

Falicov-Kimball limit

In the Falicov-Kimball model
[
75
]
particles with one spin projection, e.g. minus-spin particles are immobile,
{t_{x}^{-}=0}
.
In this case, the quantity
{\lambda\to\infty}
is ill-defined, however, the products
J_{ijk}\lambda
and
{J_{ijk}\lambda^{3}}
are well-defined. For that matter, it is more instructive to use the expressions for the hopping terms written explicitly in terms of spin-dependent hopping amplitudes
t_{x}^{\sigma}
. There it is clear that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
reduces to Ising-type model, in particular

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}^{\vphantom{z}}S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}+\sum_{i<j<k}J_{ijk}^{\vphantom{-}}h_{ijk}^{-}\left(S^{z}_{i}+S^{z}_{j}+S^{z}_{k}-12S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{j}S^{z}_{k}\right),

(41)

where

\displaystyle\qquad J_{ij}=\frac{2\big|t_{ij}^{+}\big|^{2}}{U}\,,\quad J_{ijk}=\frac{\big|t_{ij}^{+}t_{jk}^{+}t_{ki}^{+}\big|}{U^{2}}\,,\quad h_{ijk}^{-}=\cos(f_{ijk}^{+})\,.

(42)

The modified Ising model, which includes the extended three-spin magnetic field term, has been exactly solved in Ref.
[
76
]
. There, using the transfer matrix method, the statistical sum was computed, and the magnetization in the ground state has been obtained.
However, in the context of ultracold atoms in optical lattices, the magnetization of the system is determined by the population difference between the two species of atoms, which is not affected by the variation of the external parameters. Therefore, physically relevant formulation of the problem is to find the ground state configuration for the fixed magnetization.

6.1
Particle-hole transformation

In order to obtain the effective Hamiltonian for
{U<0}
, similar to (
30
), one could repeat the same procedure as in Sec.
3.2
and App.
A
.
However, in the case of strong attractive interaction, the lowest energy state has only empty or doubly occupied sites.
This fact significantly alters the technical details, requiring all the tedious calculations in Sec.
3.2
and App.
A
to be rederived from scratch.
For that matter, we use a particle-hole transformation to construct the effective Hamiltonian for
{U<0}
in a simple way from the already obtained one for
{U>0}
, Eq. (
30
) (consult e.g.
[
77
]
).
For clarity, we perform a particle-hole transformation for
{\sigma=-}
particles, which is defined by

\displaystyle c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i-}=\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{i-}\,,

(43)

in the sense of mapping
{n_{i-}=\tilde{h}_{i-}}
and
{h_{i-}=\tilde{n}_{i-}}
. As a result, the number of new
{\sigma=-}
particles becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{N}_{-}=N-N_{-}\,.

(44)

In case of zero net magnetization at half-filling, we have
{\tilde{N}_{-}=N_{-}}
.
Thus, the half-filling and zero net magnetization conditions are preserved.

Leaving
{\sigma=+}
particles intact,
{c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i+}=\tilde{c}^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i+}}
, the local Hilbert spaces, corresponding to transformed
\tilde{c}^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma},\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}
and untransformed
c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma},c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}
operators are related as

\displaystyle|0\rangle=|\tilde{-}\rangle\,,\qquad|{-}\rangle=|\tilde{0}\rangle\,,\qquad|{+}\rangle=|\tilde{2}\rangle\,,\qquad|2\rangle=|\tilde{+}\rangle\,.

(45)

The relation (
45
) describes a reinterpretation of the physical states. The Hilbert spaces spanned by the states
|a\rangle
and
|\tilde{a}\rangle
are the same, where
{a\in\{0,+,-,2\}}
and
{\tilde{a}\in\{\tilde{0},\tilde{+},\tilde{-},\tilde{2}\}}
.
The Hamiltonian (
4
) under the correspondence (
43
) transforms as

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}=\widetilde{T}+\widetilde{V}=-\sum_{ij}t_{ij}^{+}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{i+}\tilde{c}^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+}+\sum_{ij}\left(t_{ij}^{-}\right)^{\!*}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{i-}\tilde{c}^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{j-}-\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i\sigma}\tilde{n}_{i\sigma}\tilde{n}_{i\bar{\sigma}}+U\sum_{i}\tilde{n}_{i+}\,.

(46)

Neglecting the irrelevant constant
{UN_{+}}
, the transformed Hamiltonian (
46
) returns to the original form (
4
) by simultaneous substitutions

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle\to-U\,,

(47a)

\displaystyle\left(t_{ij}^{-}\right)^{\!*}

\displaystyle\to-t_{ij}^{-}\iff\varphi_{ij}^{-}\to-\varphi_{ij}^{-}+\pi\,.

(47b)

In other words, the following identity holds up to the irrelevant additive constant

\displaystyle H\!\left(\varphi_{ij}^{-},U;\,c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma},c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\right)=H\!\left(-\varphi_{ij}^{-}+\pi,-U;\,\tilde{c}^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{i\sigma},\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\right)+\mathrm{const}\,.

(48)

Here the arguments
|t_{ij}^{\sigma}|
and
\varphi_{ij}^{+}
, that are the same on both sides, are not indicated for brevity. The functional identity (
48
) is an exact one, not related to any approximation scheme.

To summarize, the prescription is as follows. Obtain effective spin
{S=1/2}
Hamiltonian for
{U>0}
, i.e. Eq. (
30
). Make substitutions (
47
). Neglecting the constant
{UN_{+}}
, the result will be valid for
{U<0}
, but in the sense of reinterpreted Hilbert space (
45
). This means that spin
{S=1/2}
operators should be swapped with pseudospin (charge)
{\eta=1/2}
operators.

\displaystyle U<0:\quad\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{+}_{ij}}\eta^{+}_{i}\eta^{-}_{j}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right)-\gamma\eta^{z}_{i}\eta^{z}_{j}\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j<k}J_{ijk}^{\vphantom{+}}\bigg\{h_{ijk}^{+}\left(\eta^{z}_{i}+\eta^{z}_{j}+\eta^{z}_{k}-12\eta^{z}_{i}\eta^{z}_{j}\eta^{z}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hphantom{=}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathrel{+}6\Big[w_{ijk}^{+}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{+}_{ij}}\eta^{+}_{i}\eta^{-}_{j}\eta^{z}_{k}+e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{+}_{jk}}\eta^{+}_{j}\eta^{-}_{k}\eta^{z}_{i}+e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{+}_{ki}}\eta^{+}_{k}\eta^{-}_{i}\eta^{z}_{j}\right)+\mathrm{H.c.}\Big]\bigg\}\,,

(49)

where

\displaystyle J_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\tau_{ij}^{2}}{|U|}\,,\qquad\gamma=\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{-2}\right)\!/2\,,\quad\left(\gamma\geqslant 1\right),

(50)

\displaystyle J_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{ij}\tau_{jk}\tau_{ki}}{U^{2}}\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\theta^{+}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{ij}^{+}+\varphi_{ij}^{-}\,,

(52)

\displaystyle h_{ijk}^{+}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{3}\cos\!\left(f_{ijk}^{+}\right)+\lambda^{-3}\cos\!\left(f_{ijk}^{-}\right)\!,

(53)

\displaystyle w_{ijk}^{+}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\vphantom{-1}}\exp\!\left(-\mathrm{i}f_{ijk}^{-}\right)+\lambda^{-1}\exp\!\left(\mathrm{i}f_{ijk}^{+}\right)\!.

(54)

Therefore, the limiting cases considered in the Sec.
5.1
can be easily reproduced for
{U<0}
, now in terms of the pseudospin (charge)
\eta^{\pm}
and
\eta^{z}
operators.

Before we close the section, we highlight the following fact.
For
{U>0}
, in the spin-symmetric case, i.e.
{\lambda=1}
and
{f_{ijk}^{+}=f_{ijk}^{-}}
, the DMI and the magnetic field vanish. The corresponding condition for
{U<0}
looks like
{f_{ijk}^{+}=-f_{ijk}^{-}+\pi}
, due to transformation (
47b
). In general, these two conditions are not satisfied simultaneously and thus, if the DMI and the magnetic field vanish in one sector, they are still present in the other. The only exception is the particle-hole symmetric case, where fluxes have a value
{|f_{ijk}^{\sigma}|=\pi/2}
. In this case, the DMI and the magnetic field are absent in both sectors,
{U>0}
and
{U<0}
.

7
Conclusion

In this paper we have derived the effective spin and pseudospin (charge) Hamiltonians, describing the infrared properties of the one-dimensional half-filled spin-asymmetric Hubbard model on a triangular ladder with spin-dependent flux penetrating the lattice, corresponding to the spin and charge sectors in the limit of strong on-site repulsion
{(U>0)}
and attraction
{(U<0)}
, respectively.
We showed that for
{U>0}
the obtained Hamiltonian is that of anisotropic
XXZ
Heisenberg zig-zag ladder with the DMI and a three-spin coupling.
Due to the presence of spin-dependent flux, the coupling constants acquire phase factors
e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{-}_{ij}}
and are complex valued. The real part of the coupling in the second order Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}
constitutes a usual anisotropic
XXZ
Heisenberg model, while the imaginary part corresponds to the DMI with orientation vector along
z
-axis, describing a spin current in terms of original fermions.
Spin-dependent hopping amplitudes induce restricted exchange anisotropy
{\gamma\geqslant 1}
in the system. The three-spin part of the Hamiltonian consists of two kinds of terms. The first one corresponds to the oriented in
z
-direction magnetic field term but modified by the additional three-spin Ising term given by the product of spin
z
-components located on vertices of each triangular plaquette. The second term describes a correlated spin exchange (CSE) with complex amplitudes. In case of spin-symmetric hopping and flux, the magnetic field and the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya terms vanish, while the CSE part reduces to scalar spin chirality operator.

We have used the one-spin-component particle-hole transformation to map the repulsive
{(U>0)}
case onto the attractive
{(U<0)}
one and to construct an effective Hamiltonian in the limit of strong attraction. The obtained Hamiltonian describes the charge degrees of freedom in terms of doubly occupied sites (doublons) and empty sites (holons). Their dynamics is again described by the same
XXZ
Heisenberg model, but in terms of pseudospin (charge) operators.
The magnetic field and the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya terms vanish in the limit of anti-symmetric flux (modulo
\pi
) configuration.
In the latter case, the three-spin coupling term is given by the scalar pseudospin chirality operator.

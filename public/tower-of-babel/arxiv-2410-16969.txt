The Equivariant
B

Abstract
In this work, we introduce an equivariant deformation of the B B model on the sphere with a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) -action. We present the deformed supersymmetry transformations and corresponding Lagrangians and study observables in the supercharge cohomology. The inclusion of equivariance allows for the introduction of novel, position-dependent observables on the sphere, which have no counterparts in the conventional B B model. Two specific cases we explore in detail are position-dependent superpotentials and complex structure deformations. In both instances, the theory exhibits notable differences from the standard B B model, revealing intriguing new features.

Introduction

The topological
B

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetric non-linear sigma model
[
1
,
2
]
. The
B

\Sigma_{g}
and it depends on the complex structure of the target space, a Calabi-Yau manifold. The physical observables are related to holomorphic quantities, and its correlation functions capture the geometry of the moduli space of complex structures. The

[
3

In this work we study the equivariant deformation of the
B
model with respect to a
U(1)
-action on
\Sigma_{g}
. This restricts the possible compact
\Sigma_{g}
we can consider to either
S^{2}
or
T^{2}
. One can also study a version of the equivariant
B
model on
\mathbb{C}
, the so called
\Omega
-background. In this work we focus on
S^{2}
where the
U(1)
action has fixed points. The
\Omega
-background can then be related to the theory on a hemisphere.
The equivariant deformation of the
B
model transformations was originally introduced in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
which, however, did not explore extensively the resulting theory.

In this work we present a systematic study of the equivariant
B
model. We clarify its relation to a
{\cal N}=(2,2)
non-linear sigma model for twisted chiral multiplets coupled to a specific supergravity background as studied in
[
6
,
7
]
.
We explore a new class of observables that may explicitly depend on the worldsheet coordinates, and compute the corresponding correlators via the localization technique. We focus on two cases: the equivariant extension of the topological Landau-Ginzburg model and the equivariant extension of target-space complex structure deformations.

The
B
model can be coupled to a superpotential

W
, a setup known as the topological Landau-Ginzburg (LG) model
[
8
]
. We consider the equivariant extension of this model, which allows for a superpotential

that varies with the latitude

[
9

but in a purely holomorphic setting.
We also explore complex structure deformations
\mu^{i}{}_{\overline{j}}(\phi,\overline{\phi},\vartheta)
with explicit worldsheet dependence. These can be considered as an equivariant
B

suggested in
[

The paper is organized as follows: In Section

The

\displaystyle\delta\phi^{i}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\delta\rho^{i}=-2\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}\phi^{i},\qquad\delta\Sigma^{i}=2\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\rho^{i}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}R^{i}{}_{j\overline{l}k}\eta^{\overline{l}}\rho^{j}\wedge\rho^{k}~,

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{i}g_{i\overline{j}}\rho^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{i}\theta_{i}~.

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\widetilde{W}}=\frac{1}{2}\delta\hat{\delta}\left(\widetilde{W}\right)~,

(2.9)

where we define

After elimination of the auxiliary fields

\displaystyle\delta\phi^{i}

2.2
Observables and localization

Supersymmetric local operators in the
B
model are given by holomorphic functions
f(\phi)
. These are
\delta
-closed by virtue of
\delta\phi^{i}=0
. Correlators of these operators,

\langle\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\phi(x_{\alpha}))\rangle~,

(2.11)

are independent of their positions
x_{\alpha}
on the worldsheet because
\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\delta\rho^{i}
is
\delta
-exact. Consider now functions of the form
u^{i}(\phi)\partial_{i}W(\phi)
with
u
being
a holomorphic vector field
u\in T^{1,0}X
. According to (
2.10
)

f(\phi)u^{i}\partial_{i}W(\phi)=f(\phi)u^{i}\delta\theta_{i}=\delta(f(\phi)u^{i}\theta_{i})~,

(2.12)

so that functions containing
\partial_{i}W
as a factor are
\delta
-exact. Consequently, operators in the
\delta
-cohomology are given by the ring
\mathcal{R}
of holomorphic functions on
X
modulo the ideal generated by
\partial_{1}W,\dots,\partial_{N}W
(
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=N
). On flat space
X=\mathbb{C}^{N}
, this ring is simply the polynomial ring

\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[\phi^{1},\dots,\phi^{N}]/(\partial_{1}W,\dots,\partial_{N}W),

(2.13)

known as the chiral ring of the theory for a generic
W
.

Our interest is in computing the correlators (
2.11
) of generators
f_{\alpha}
of the chiral ring. For this purpose we can use localization. We consider the following Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{D}}+\mathcal{L}_{W}+\ell\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\tilde{W}}~,

(2.14)

where
\ell
is a large parameter that multiplies
\delta

The bosonic terms in this Lagrangian are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bos}}=

\displaystyle\left(2g_{i\overline{j}}\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{i}\beta_{i}\right)+{1\over 2}(\partial_{i}W)\Sigma^{i}+{\ell\over 2}(\partial_{\overline{i}}\widetilde{W})\beta^{\overline{i}}\star 1~.

(2.15)

The auxiliary fields can be integrated out:

\Sigma^{i}=\ell g^{i\overline{j}}\left(\partial_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{W}\right)\star 1,\qquad\beta^{\overline{i}}=g^{j\overline{i}}\left(\partial_{j}{W}\right).

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bos}}=

\displaystyle 2g_{i\overline{j}}\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}+\frac{\ell}{2}g^{i\overline{j}}\left(\partial_{i}{W}\right)\wedge\star\left(\partial_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{W}\right).

In the large-

\phi^{i}

\phi^{i}=\phi^{i}_{\mu}~,\quad(\partial_{i}W)(\phi_{\mu})=0~,

(2.18)

with the Greek index

Provided the critical points are isolated and non-degenerate, which is true generically, we expand the scalar fields around each critical point so that
\phi^{i}=\phi^{i}_{\mu}+{1\over\sqrt{\ell}}\Delta\phi^{i}~.
The path integral is then one-loop exact. The fermionic and bosonic contributions to the one-loop determinant cancel up to the contributions of constant modes. The fields
\rho^{i}
have
g

g
is the genus of
\Sigma_{g}
. The fields
\theta^{\overline{i}},\eta^{\overline{i}}
have one zero-mode each as do
\Delta\phi^{i},~\Delta\phi^{\overline{i}}
. Hence the zero-modes give the one-loop determinant

{(\det\partial_{\overline{i}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{W}(\overline{\phi}_{\mu}))(\det\partial_{i}\partial_{j}W(\phi_{\mu}))^{g}\over(\det\partial_{\overline{i}}\partial_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{W}(\overline{\phi}_{\mu}))(\det\partial_{i}\partial_{j}W(\phi_{\mu}))}=(\det\partial_{i}\partial_{j}W(\phi_{\mu}))^{g-1}~,

(2.19)

and the correlators (

\sum_{\mu}(\det\partial_{i}\partial_{j}W(\phi_{\mu}))^{g-1}\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mu})~.

(2.20)

These are independent of
\widetilde{W}
as expected because it only enters
\delta
-exact terms in the Lagrangian. They are also explicitly independent of the insertion positions. From now on we will consider the case
g=0
,
\Sigma_{0}=S^{2}
.

The correlators (
2.20
) have a nontrivial dependence on the one-loop determinants. However the structure of the chiral ring is insensitive to them. Let the
f_{\alpha}(\phi)
form a basis for the chiral ring of the theory. Generically, when the critical points are isolated and non-degenerate, the
f_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mu})\equiv f_{\alpha}^{\mu}
are encoded in an invertible square matrix
f_{\alpha}^{\mu}
. The topological metric and three-point functions can be written as

\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\langle f_{\alpha}(\phi)f_{\beta}(\phi)\rangle=\sum_{\mu}N_{\mu}f^{\mu}_{\alpha}f^{\mu}_{\beta}~,\qquad C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\langle f_{\alpha}(\phi)f_{\beta}(\phi)f_{\gamma}(\phi)\rangle=\sum_{\mu}N_{\mu}f^{\mu}_{\alpha}f^{\mu}_{\beta}f^{\mu}_{\gamma}~,

(2.21)

where
N_{\mu}
is the one-loop determinant (
2.19
) with
g=0
. The structure constants for the chiral ring are then

C^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}=\eta^{\alpha\kappa}C_{\kappa\beta\gamma}=\sum_{\mu}f_{\beta}^{\mu}f_{\gamma}^{\mu}(f^{-1})^{\alpha}_{\mu}~,

(2.22)

and are independent of the one-loop contributions
N_{\mu}
. Hence deformations of the superpotential that do not change the critical points can modify the topological metric and various correlators but not the structure constants. As an example consider a superpotential
W
which is an analytic function of a single
\mathbb{C}
valued field
\phi
.
The one-loop determinant gives then

N_{\mu}^{-1}=W^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\mu})~.

(2.23)

Define a new superpotential
\widehat{W}
as follows:

\widehat{W}(\phi)=\int\limits_{0}^{\phi}\partial_{z}W(z)\mathrm{e}^{-F(z)}~dz~.

(2.24)

Then

\partial\widehat{W}=(\partial W)\mathrm{e}^{-F}~,

(2.25)

so that the critical points of
W
and
\widehat{W}
coincide.
The one-loop determinant now gives

N_{\mu}^{-1}=\widehat{W}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\mu})=W^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\mu})\mathrm{e}^{-F(\phi_{\mu})}~.

(2.26)

Hence correlators computed with the
\widehat{W}
superpotential differ from the ones computed with
W
. We could have obtained the same result by adding to the action an additional supersymmetric term of the form
\int F(\phi)
.
While the correlators are different the structure of the chiral ring is the same. The structure constants computed via (
2.22
) coincide in both cases as they are independent of the
N_{\mu}
.

3.1
Supersymmetry and Lagrangians

Equivariance deforms the supercharges discussed in the previous section so that their algebra reads:

\displaystyle\delta^{2}=-2\mathrm{i}{\cal L}_{v}~,\quad\hat{\delta}^{2}=2\mathrm{i}{\cal L}_{v}~,\quad\{\delta,\hat{\delta}\}=0~,

(3.2)

where
{\cal L}_{v}
is the action of the Lie derivative along
v
on the fields of the model.

The action of the supersymmetry variation
\delta
on the fields is deformed to

\displaystyle\delta\phi^{i}

\displaystyle=\iota_{v}\rho^{i},\qquad\delta\rho^{i}=-2\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}-\mathrm{i}\iota_{v}\Sigma^{i}-\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\iota_{v}\rho^{j}\rho^{k},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma^{i}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\rho^{i}+\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\iota_{v}\Sigma^{j}\wedge\rho^{k}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}R^{i}{}_{j\overline{l}k}\eta^{\overline{l}}\rho^{j}\wedge\rho^{k}~,

\displaystyle\delta\phi^{\overline{i}}

\displaystyle=\eta^{\overline{i}},\qquad\delta\eta^{\overline{i}}=-2\mathrm{i}\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{i}},\qquad\delta\theta^{\overline{i}}=\beta^{\overline{i}}+2\star(\kappa\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{i}})-\Gamma^{\overline{i}}_{\overline{j}\overline{k}}\eta^{\overline{j}}\theta^{\overline{k}},

\displaystyle\delta\beta^{\overline{i}}

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{i}\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\theta^{\overline{i}}-2\star(\kappa\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\eta^{\overline{i}})-\Gamma^{\overline{i}}_{\overline{j}\overline{k}}\eta^{\overline{j}}\beta^{\overline{k}}+R^{\overline{i}}_{\overline{j}l\overline{k}}\iota_{v}\rho^{l}\eta^{\overline{j}}\theta^{\overline{k}},

where
\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\rho^{i}=\mathrm{d}\rho^{i}+\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\mathrm{d}\phi^{j}\wedge\rho^{k}
,
\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\eta^{\overline{i}}=\mathrm{d}\eta^{\overline{i}}+\Gamma^{\overline{i}}_{\overline{j}\overline{k}}\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}\eta^{\overline{k}}
and
\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\theta^{\overline{i}}=\mathrm{d}\theta^{\overline{i}}+\Gamma^{\overline{i}}_{\overline{j}\overline{k}}\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}\theta^{\overline{k}}
.
We also quote the transformations of
\theta_{i}=g_{i\overline{j}}\theta^{\overline{j}}
and
\beta_{i}=g_{i\overline{j}}\beta^{\overline{j}}
:

\displaystyle\delta\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}+2g_{i\overline{j}}\star(\kappa\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}})+\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\iota_{v}\rho^{j}\theta_{k}~,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\delta\beta_{i}

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{i}\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\theta_{i}-2\star(\kappa\wedge\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\eta_{i})+\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\iota_{v}\rho^{j}\beta_{k}+R_{i\overline{j}j\overline{k}}\iota_{v}\rho^{j}\eta^{\overline{j}}\theta^{\overline{k}}~.

(3.5)

One important difference with respect to the non-equivariant case is that the auxiliary field
\Sigma^{i}
now enters the variation of
\rho^{i}
.

Inspecting (
3.3
) it would seem appropriate to simplify the action of
\delta
by redefining the auxiliary field
\beta^{\overline{i}}
as follows

\tilde{\beta}^{\overline{i}}=\beta^{\overline{i}}+2\star(\kappa\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{i}})~.

(3.6)

We prefer to use
\beta^{\overline{i}}
instead of
\tilde{\beta}^{\overline{i}}
for two reasons. Firstly the relation to the physical
{\cal N}=(2,2)
theory as described in appendix
A.2
is more direct in terms of
\beta^{\overline{i}}
. In second instance, when we will discuss Lagrangians, expressions in terms of
\beta^{\overline{i}}
are simpler and the relation between the equivariant and non-equivariant models is more transparent. The action of the second supersymmetry variation
\hat{\delta}
in the equivariant setting is presented in appendix
B
.

We can summarize the transformations (
3.3
) and make equivariance explicit by working with superfields. Let
\rho^{i}=\rho^{i}_{a}\xi^{a}
and
\Sigma^{i}=\Sigma^{i}_{ab}\xi^{a}\xi^{b}
with

Grassmann variables and

\displaystyle{\bf\Phi}^{i}=\phi^{i}+\rho^{i}+{\mathrm{i}}\left(\Sigma^{i}+{i\over 2}\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\rho^{j}\rho^{k}\right)~,

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\bf H}^{\overline{i}}=\eta^{\overline{i}}+2\mathrm{i}d\phi^{\overline{i}}~,

(3.8)

\displaystyle{\bf B}^{\overline{i}}=\beta^{\overline{i}}+2\star(\kappa\wedge d\phi^{\overline{i}})-\Gamma^{\overline{i}}_{\overline{j}\overline{k}}\eta^{\overline{j}}\theta^{\overline{k}}+2\mathrm{i}d\theta^{\overline{i}}~.

(3.9)

In order to describe the action of
\delta

{\cal D}_{v}=-2\mathrm{i}\xi^{a}\partial_{a}-v^{a}{\partial\over\partial\xi^{a}}
which satisfies:

{\cal D}_{v}^{2}=-2\mathrm{i}{\cal L}_{v}~.

(3.10)

In terms of
{\cal D}_{v}
the superfield variations then read,

\delta{\bf\Phi}^{i}={\cal D}_{v}{\bf\Phi}^{i}~,\quad\delta{\bf H}^{\overline{i}}={\cal D}_{v}{\bf H}^{\overline{i}}~,\quad\delta{\bf B}^{\overline{i}}={\cal D}_{v}{\bf B}^{\overline{i}}~.

(3.11)

This shows that using a two-form auxiliary field
\Sigma^{i}
is natural in the context of the equivariant
B

In order to obtain a
\delta
-invariant Lagrangian consider again
{\mathcal{V}}
as in (

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{i}g_{i\overline{j}}\rho^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{i}\theta_{i}~.

(3.12)

Its
\delta

\delta\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{D}}
with

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{D}}=2g_{i\overline{j}}\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{i}\beta_{i}-\mathrm{i}g_{i\overline{j}}\rho^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\eta^{\overline{j}}-\mathrm{d}^{\Gamma}\rho^{i}\theta_{i}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}R^{i}{}_{j\overline{l}k}\rho^{j}\wedge\rho^{k}\eta^{\overline{l}}\theta_{i}~,

This indifference to equivariant deformations implies that the standard

4.2
Correlators

Unlike for the standard

\langle\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\phi(0))\prod_{\beta}f_{\beta}(\phi(\pi))\rangle~.

We now turn to study these correlators in different cases. We will see that the insertions at the

and

4.2.1
Standard superpotential:
W_{2}=0

For

\delta

. These give a contribution to the correlators of the form

\sum_{\mu}(\det\partial_{i}\partial_{j}W(\phi_{\mu}))^{-1}\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mu})~,

(4.18)

which coincides with (
2.20
) for

.

As we have discussed, for special choices of
W_{1}
there can be one or more domain wall solutions interpolating between different critical points. These are not generically present. From the general discussion above these saddles should not contribute to the correlators. Indeed we expect the exceptional saddles to have both bosonic and fermionic zero-modes. Because of the fermionic zero-modes the contribution of these configurations to the correlators then vanishes. One example is considered in Appendix
D
.

4.2.2
Position dependent superpotential: tracking solutions

For large radius

\phi^{i}_{\mathrm{N},\mu}=\phi^{i}_{\mu}(\vartheta=0)~,\quad\phi^{i}_{\mathrm{S},\mu}=\phi^{i}_{\mu}(\vartheta=\pi)~.

(4.19)

These are critical points for
W_{N}=W(\phi,\vartheta=0)
and

The localization result for the correlators (

\sum_{\mu}N_{\mu}\left(\mathrm{e}^{{2\pi\over\epsilon}W(0,\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu})}\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu})\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{-{2\pi\over\epsilon}W(\pi,\phi_{\mathrm{S},\mu})}\prod_{\beta}f_{\beta}(\phi_{\mathrm{S},\mu})\right)~,

(4.20)

where

Here we do not determine the one-loop contributions

The tame case.

We will first consider the case where each critical point of

For two operators inserted at the same pole, say N, the correlator gives

(4.21)

This is of the same form as the topological metric for the superpotential
W(\phi,0)

\sum_{\mu}{\tilde{N}}_{\mu}f_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu})f_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu})

(in the absence of zero-modes).
For three operators inserted at the north pole the correlator gives

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{N},\alpha\beta\gamma}=\sum_{\mu}N_{\mu}\mathrm{e}^{{2\pi\over\epsilon}W(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu},0)}\mathrm{e}^{-{2\pi\over\epsilon}W(\phi_{\mathrm{S},\mu},\pi)}f_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu})f_{\beta}(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu})f_{\gamma}(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu}).

(4.22)

Again this is of the same form than the three-point function in the non-equivariant case.
Similarly for more insertions at the same pole the result will be of the same form as for the corresponding correlator in the non-equivariant model.
Hence the
N
pole correlators have the structure dictated by the chiral ring of
W(\phi,0)
. Similarly for the
S
pole correlators and the chiral ring of
W(\phi,\pi)
. We can then choose bases of generators for the two rings which we will denote
f_{\alpha}^{N}(\phi)
and
f_{\alpha}^{S}(\phi)
. When the critical points
\phi_{N,\mu}
and
\phi_{S,\mu}
are non-degenerate and isolated the matrices
(f^{N})^{\mu}_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}^{N}(\phi_{N,\mu})
and
(f^{S})^{\mu}_{\alpha}=f_{\mu}^{S}(\phi_{S,\mu})
are invertible.

With this in mind we can turn to the two-point function where the two insertions are on opposite poles:

\psi_{\alpha\beta}=\langle f^{N}_{\alpha}(\phi(0))f^{S}_{\beta}(\phi(\pi))\rangle=\sum_{\mu}N_{\mu}\mathrm{e}^{{2\pi\over\epsilon}W(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu},0)}\mathrm{e}^{-{2\pi\over\epsilon}W(\phi_{\mathrm{S},\mu},\pi)}f^{N}_{\alpha}(\phi_{\mathrm{N},\mu})f^{S}_{\beta}(\phi_{\mathrm{S},\mu})~.

(4.23)

We can then define
{\psi^{\alpha}}_{\beta}=(\nu^{-1}_{\mathrm{N}})^{\alpha\kappa}\psi_{\kappa\beta}=\sum_{\mu}(f^{-1}_{\mathrm{N}})^{\alpha}_{\mu}(f_{\mathrm{S}})_{\alpha}^{\mu}
which is independent of the one-loop contributions.
This matrix and its inverse can be used to relate
N
and
S
pole correlators, for instance:

(\nu_{\mathrm{N}})_{\alpha\beta}{\psi^{\alpha}}_{\kappa}{\psi^{\beta}}_{\rho}=(\nu_{\mathrm{S}})_{\kappa\rho}~.

(4.24)

Because it only depends on the
(f_{\mathrm{S}})_{\alpha}^{\mu}
and
(f_{\mathrm{N}})_{\alpha}^{\mu}
the matrix
\psi
also provides a map between the structure constants for the operators inserted at the two poles:

{(\psi^{-1})^{\kappa}}_{\alpha}({C_{\mathrm{N}}})^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}{\psi^{\beta}}_{\rho}{\psi^{\gamma}}_{\lambda}=(C_{\mathrm{S}})^{\kappa}_{\rho\lambda}~.

(4.25)

Let us denote by
\mathcal{R}_{N,S}
the chiral rings corresponding to
W(\phi,\vartheta=0)
and
W(\phi,\vartheta=\pi)
. In particular, (
4.25

\psi:\mathcal{R}_{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{S},\;f^{N}_{\alpha}\mapsto(\psi^{-1})^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}f^{S}_{\beta}~,

(4.26)

(after extending by linearity) is an algebra isomorphism.

Large deformation.

Consider now the case when the number of critical points of
W(\phi,0)
is strictly less than that of
W(\phi,\pi)
. Then the regular tracking solutions originating at
\vartheta=0
will connect to a subset of the critical points at
\vartheta=\pi
.
As a consequence the
(f_{\mathrm{N}})^{\mu}_{\alpha}
are a square invertible matrix but not the
(f_{\mathrm{S}})^{\mu}_{\alpha}
. We can still define

{\psi^{\alpha}}_{\beta}=(\nu^{-1}_{\mathrm{N}})^{\alpha\kappa}\mu_{\kappa\beta}=\sum_{\mu}(f^{-1}_{\mathrm{N}})^{\alpha}_{\mu}(f_{\mathrm{S}})_{\beta}^{\mu}~,

(4.27)

but not its inverse.
What happens is that, because only a subset of the critical points of

is connected by a tracking solution, the “effective” superpotential at
\vartheta=\pi
is not
W(\phi,\pi)
. The chiral ring of the “effective” superpotential at
\vartheta=\pi
is strictly smaller than that of

and isomorphic to that of

.

4.2.3
Comments on
Q
-exactness

Consider the expression (
4.20
) for the correlators (
4.17
). Apart from the one-loop contribution it depends on the values

and
\phi^{i}_{\mathrm{S},\mu}
that the tracking solutions attain at the two poles. These are critical points for

and
W_{\mathrm{S}}=W(\phi,\vartheta=\pi)

and

which is bijective. There are multiple such maps differing by permutations of the

associated to the

) depend on the permutation as does the matrix

with the same

and

whose tracking solutions give rise to a map between critical points that differ by a permutation of the

and

are unchanged. However the correlators (

Summary and outlook

In this work we have explored the equivariant deformation of the
B
model on
S^{2}
. In particular we concentrated on the study of a new class of observables and their correlators.
The equivariant
B
model has some curious features. An interesting point is that away from the fixed points
v\neq 0
the equivariant
B
model transformations (
3.3
) can be mapped to the equivariant
\overline{B}
model. Thus it appears that the equivariant extension of the
B
model stands out compared to other equivariant extensions of real models.

We defined the equivariant Landau-Ginzburg model with a
superpotential
W(\phi,\vartheta)
with explicit worldsheet dependence. We argued that the model localizes on tracking solutions which relate critical points of
W
at two poles of
S^{2}
. We argued that these tracking solutions exist but it is hard to analyse them explicitly and perform a one-loop calculation around them. It remains to be seen if the proposed structures lead to any new interesting mathematical construction. We hope to come back to these issues in the future.

In the context of this paper we want to point out that one could glue together the equivariant
B
model on one hemisphere of
S^{2}
with the equivariant
\overline{B}
model defined on another hemisphere in the spirit of
[

. We plan to write a separate work
[

\displaystyle 2g_{i\overline{j}}\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}+2\ell g_{i\overline{j}}\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}}\star 1-{\kappa\over||v||^{2}}\wedge\partial W

(C.4)

\displaystyle+2\ell g_{i\overline{j}}\left(\star(\kappa\wedge d\phi^{\overline{j}})+{1\over 2}\partial^{\overline{j}}W\right)\left(\kappa\wedge d\phi^{i}+{1\over 2}\partial^{i}W\star 1\right).

Hence the functional integral localizes to scalar field configurations that satisfy

\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{i}}=0~,\qquad\star(\kappa\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{i}})+{1\over 2}\,g^{j\overline{i}}\left(\partial_{j}{W}\right)=0~.

(C.5)

As for the case considered in the main text these two equations are the BPS conditions
\delta\eta^{\overline{i}}=0
and
\delta\theta^{\overline{i}}=0
. The remaining BPS condition
\delta\rho^{i}=0
is satisfied on solutions to (
C.5
) that are regular at the poles.
Finally let’s consider evaluating the bosonic action on the localization locus. Because of (
C.5
) we have:

\star d\phi^{\overline{i}}={\kappa\over 2||v^{2}||}g^{j\overline{i}}\partial_{j}W~.

(C.6)

We can then substitute this back into (
C.4
) to get:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bos}}=(\partial_{i}Wd\phi^{i}+\partial W)\wedge{\kappa\over||v||^{2}}=dW\wedge{\kappa\over||v||^{2}}~,

(C.7)

so that

S=-{1\over\epsilon}\int_{S^{2}}d\varphi\wedge dW=-{2\pi\over\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\pi}{d\over d\vartheta}Wd\vartheta={2\pi\over\epsilon}(W|_{\vartheta=0}-W|_{\vartheta=\pi})~.

(C.8)

Appendix D
Domain walls

In this appendix we consider the special case of the minimal model
W_{1}(\phi)=\frac{1}{3}\phi^{3}-\phi
with
W_{2}\equiv 0
. Here, the BPS equation (
4.13
) allows for domain wall solutions that interpolate between different critical points of
W_{1}
. However, we will see that these solutions come with corresponding fermionic zero-modes so that their contribution to the correlators we are interested in vanishes.

For simplicity, we consider the round sphere with
R=1
and
\epsilon=1
, but the same analysis goes through for a generic choice of background. Upon substituting
x=\frac{1}{2}\log(\tan(\vartheta/2))
into (
4.13
) for the choice of
W_{1},W_{2}
above, we obtain the following equation:

\partial_{x}\phi-\partial\widetilde{W}_{1}=0~.

(D.1)

Note that
\phi=\phi(x)
does not depend on the azimuthal angle because of the BPS condition
\iota_{v}\phi=0
. The critical points of the potential are
\phi\equiv\pm 1
and the domain wall solution interpolating between them is real and given by
\phi_{0}(x)=\tanh(x-a)
. Here,
a\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the center of the domain wall which is a bosonic zero-mode.

In order to look for fermionic zero-modes we need to study the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fer}}=(1+\ell\|v\|^{2})(-\mathrm{i}\rho\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}-\mathrm{d}\rho\overline{\theta}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}\partial^{2}W(\phi_{0})\rho\wedge\rho)+\frac{\ell}{2}\partial^{2}\widetilde{W}(\overline{\phi}_{0})\overline{\eta}\overline{\theta}\star 1~,

(D.2)

as the corresponding fermionic part to (
4.4
). Zero-modes of this Lagrangian have to satisfy the following differential equations:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}(1+\ell\|v\|^{2})\partial^{2}W(\phi_{0})\star\rho-\frac{1}{2}\star\mathrm{d}((1+\ell\|v\|^{2})\overline{\theta})+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(1+\ell\|v\|^{2})\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}~,

(D.3a)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(1+\ell\|v\|^{2})\star\mathrm{d}\rho-\frac{1}{2}\ell\partial^{2}\widetilde{W}(\overline{\phi}_{0})\overline{\eta}~,

(D.3b)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}^{\dagger}((1+\ell\|v\|^{2})\rho)+\frac{\ell}{2}\partial^{2}\widetilde{W}(\overline{\phi}_{0})\overline{\theta}~.

(D.3c)

Zero modes are
v
invariant hence we impose
\mathcal{L}_{v}\overline{\theta}=\mathcal{L}_{v}\overline{\eta}=0
and
\mathcal{L}_{v}\rho=0

We will start by finding the zero modes for the terms of order
\ell
in (
D.2
). Decomposing
\rho=\rho_{||}+\rho_{\perp}
where
\iota_{v}\rho_{\perp}=0
and
\kappa\wedge\rho_{||}=0
we get the uncoupled equations

\displaystyle d^{\dagger}(\phi_{0}^{-1}d\overline{\eta})+{1\over||v||^{2}}\phi_{0}\overline{\eta}=0~,

(D.4)

\displaystyle d^{\dagger}(\phi_{0}^{-1}d(||v^{2}||\overline{\theta}))+{1\over||v||^{2}}\phi_{0}(||v||^{2}\overline{\theta})=0~.

(D.5)

These two equations have the same form and are easily solved by:

\displaystyle\overline{\eta}=c_{1}\sech(x-a)^{2}+c_{2}\cosh(x-a)^{2}~,

(D.6)

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}=c_{3}\sech(x-a)^{2}\cosh(2x)^{2}+c_{4}\cosh(x-a)^{2}\cosh(2x)^{2}~.

(D.7)

Only one solution is normalizable hence there is one zero mode:

\rho=c\sech^{2}(x-a)\frac{\kappa}{\|v\|^{2}}~,\qquad\overline{\eta}=c\sech^{2}(x-a)~,\qquad\overline{\theta}\equiv 0~.

(D.8)

Here,
c\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant and is the fermionic superpartner of
a
,
\delta a=c
. Hence, the existence of the fermionic zero-mode is linked to the bosonic one. This will be the case also for other potentials
W_{1}
allowing for domain wall solutions. Note that the fermionic terms in the localization Lagrangian (
D.2
) explicitly break
\hat{\delta}
. Hence there is no fermionic zero mode related to the bosonic one by the second supercharge
\hat{\delta}
.

We can now consider all terms in (
D.2
). As zero modes can be lifted only in pairs and the terms of order
\ell
have only one zero mode this will survive the addition of the terms of order
\ell^{0}
.

Appendix E
Chiral ring relations

As we discussed, in the equivariant
B
model, a holomorphic function of the
\phi^{i}
inserted at a fixed point, say the north pole at
\vartheta=0
, is supersymmetric

{\cal O}=F(\phi)|_{N}~,\qquad\delta{\cal O}=0~.

(E.1)

In the presence of a superpotential
W(\phi,\vartheta)
, that can depend explicitly on
\vartheta
, there are relations between supersymmetric insertions. Indeed, consider

\hat{\cal O}=\hat{F}^{i}(\phi)\beta_{i}~,

(E.2)

where the
F^{i}(\phi)
are holomorphic.
We can write

\delta(\hat{F}^{i}\theta_{i})-\hat{\cal O}=\partial_{j}\hat{F}^{i}\,\iota_{v}\rho^{j}\,\theta^{i}+2g_{i\overline{j}}\hat{F}^{i}\star(\kappa\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi^{\overline{j}})~.

(E.3)

The two operators on the right hand side, which are proportional to
v
should be defined so that they vanish when inserted at the two poles. Then
\hat{\cal O}
inserted at the fixed points of
v
is
\delta
-exact.
Using the equations of motion we have
\beta_{i}=\partial_{i}W
hence

\hat{F}^{i}\partial_{i}W|_{N,S}~,

(E.4)

is
\delta
-exact on-shell.
We thus find that at the north pole
\vartheta=0
there are relations between the
F(\phi)
given by
\partial_{i}W(\phi,0)
while at the south pole
\vartheta=\pi
the relations are given by
\partial_{i}W(\phi,\pi)
.

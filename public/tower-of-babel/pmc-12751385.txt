The association between the albumin-bilirubin score and postoperative delirium and prognosis in cardiac surgery patients: a retrospective dual-center cohort study using MIMIC-IV and eICU databases
Authors: Na Zhu, Xiejia Weng, Xinxin Wang, Huiyang Cai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) score is created to estimate liver dysfunction and then extended to more general applications. Nonetheless, the effectiveness of ALBI score in forecasting adverse outcomes in patients after cardiac surgery remains unexplored. The purpose of this study was to explore the association of the ALBI score with postoperative delirium (POD) and in-hospital mortality in patients following cardiac surgery.MethodsData of patients who underwent cardiac surgery and had an intensive care unit stay after surgery were extracted from the MIMIC-IV and eICU-CRD databases. The links of ALBI score with POD and in-hospital mortality were examined by multivariate logistic regression models, restricted cubic spline (RCS), and subgroup analyses. Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to check the predictive power of ALBI.Results4940 patients were enrolled and categorized into three groups based on the ALBI quartiles: <-2.52, -2.52 to -1.68, and > -1.68. Background

Cardiac surgery remains a substantial global disease burden [1]. Given the complexity of cardiac surgeries, patients face an elevated risk of postoperative complications and adverse outcomes [2, 3]. Delirium is an acute cerebral dysfunction marked by fluctuating impairments in attention and awareness, along with disturbances in cognitive and perceptual impairments [4]. Multiple studies have stated that the incidence of postoperative delirium (POD) among patients undergoing cardiac surgery varies from 14% to 52% [5, 6]. POD adversely affects patient outcomes, leading to longer hospitalization [7], higher costs of care [8], increased mortality rates [9], long-term cognitive impairment, and reduced quality of life [10, 11]. The underlying pathophysiology is complex, with inflammatory responses prompted by surgery being a significant contributor, as evidenced by studies showing that interventions like preoperative corticosteroid administration can attenuate this response and reduce the incidence of postoperative neurocognitive disorders [12]. Early detection and management of POD can effectively improve the prognosis for patients and reduce mortality and disability rates [6]. Therefore, there is a need for indicators that can be measured rapidly, directly, and effectively to predict POD risk in patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

Growing research focuses on the liver-heart connection, revealing intricate mutual interactions [13]. Hepatic injury is frequently associated with cardiac dysfunction, resulting from high hepatic vein pressure, diminished hepatic blood flow, and hypoxia [14]. Clinically, patients with cardiac diseases often exhibit hypoalbuminemia [15] and hyperbilirubinemia [16]. Low serum albumin may independently predict POD in patients after cardiac surgery [17] and is related to an enhanced risk of mortality after surgery [18]. Hyperbilirubinemia in patients after cardiac surgery may enhance the likelihood of POD development [19] and is correlated with decreased postoperative survival [20].

The albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) score is created as an evidence-based scoring system to estimate liver dysfunction [21]. The ALBI score is a simple index including two objective and readily available blood markers: serum albumin and bilirubin levels. The ALBI score is initially employed to predict the prognosis of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [22] and then to more general applications. Studies demonstrate the prognostic implications of the ALBI score beyond HCC, including cardiovascular diseases [23, 24], acute kidney injury [25], and malignant cancers [26, 27]. However, the value of ALBI score in forecasting POD and mortality in patients after cardiac surgery remains unexplored.

Thus, this retrospective study aimed to inspect the links of the ALBI score with POD and in-hospital mortality in patients after cardiac surgery. We hypothesize that a higher ALBI score is independently associated with an increased risk of both POD and in-hospital mortality in this patient population.

Methods

Data source

Data were gathered from the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV database (MIMIC-IV, version 3.1) and the eICU Collaborative Research Database (eICU-CRD). MIMIC-IV is a single-center database containing de-identified data from patients admitted to the intensive care units of the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center between 2008 and 2019. The eICU-CRD is a multi-center database comprising data from over 200 hospitals across the United States between 2014 and 2015. One author (ID: 13954603) completed the training and examination for the Collaborative Institutional Training Initiative program, a web-based course provided by the National Institutes of Health and obtained permission to access the database. In the present study, the data were publicly accessible and no additional data were required. Therefore, informed consent and ethical approval were considered unnecessary.

Study population

Patients were enrolled if they met the criteria: [1] age ≥ 18 years; [2] underwent cardiac surgery; [3] had an ICU stay after surgery. Cardiac surgeries were defined with the ICD-9 and ICD-10 codes (Table S1). Patients were excluded if they [1] with an ICU length of < 24 h; [2] lacked delirium screening records; [3] lacked data on albumin or bilirubin levels. In the case of patients who had undergone more than one cardiac surgery, only the first records were extracted. A detailed screening flowchart is presented in Fig. 1.

Data extractions

All variables were extracted with PostgresSQL 17.2.3. The extracted data covered [1] demographics: age, sex, height, and weight; [2] comorbidities: hypertension, congestive heart failure (CHF), myocardial infarct, renal disease, chronic pulmonary disease (CPD), diabetes, and liver disease; [3] laboratory parameter after surgery: albumin, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), anion gap, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), bicarbonate, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), calcium, creatinine, glucose, hemoglobin (Hb), magnesium, platelet count (PLT), potassium, sodium, white blood cell count(WBC); [4] clinical scores: acute physiology score III (APSIII); [5] Means of vital signs on the first day in the ICU: heart rate (HR), systolic blood pressure (SBP), mean arterial blood pressure (MAP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), respiratory rate (RR) and percutaneous oxygen saturation (SpO2); [6] clinical outcomes: POD and in-hospital mortality. For repeatedly measured laboratory parameters, the initial postoperative value was selected for analysis, whereas the maximum recorded clinical score during the postoperative period was analyzed. To minimize bias caused by excluding samples, the missing value percentage was estimated. For variables with missing values < 10%, a random forest-based multiple imputation method was utilized to predict missing values. Specifically, the mean of the five outcomes was taken. Variables with missing values exceeding 10% were excluded from the analysis.

Calculation of ALBI score

ALBI score was computed: ALBI = (log10bilirubin (µmol/L) × 0.66) +(albumin (g/L) ×−0.085) [21]. Meanwhile, patients were allocated into three groups based on the quartile: 0%–25%, 25%–75%, and 75%–100% for further logistic regression.

Outcome measures

The primary outcomes were POD, and the secondary outcome was in-hospital mortality. In the present study, POD was diagnosed using the confusion assessment method for the ICU (CAM-ICU), a widely used clinical delirium assessment tool.

The CAM-ICU consists of four features: [1] an acute change in mental status [2], inattention [3], disorganized thinking, and [4] altered consciousness. The patient who manifests both features 1 and 2, plus either feature 3 or 4 is diagnosed with delirium [28].

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro-Wilk test revealed that all continuous variables were non-normally distributed. Therefore, continuous variables were delineated as median (interquartile range [IQR]) and further analyzed with the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were manifested as counts (percentages) and the chi-square test (c²) was utilized for comparison. The variance inflation factor (VIF) was adopted to appraise multicollinearity, and variables with VIF value > 4 were eliminated from the model [29]. Logistic regression analysis models were utilized to check the link of ALBI (as both a continuous and a categorical variable) with POD and in-hospital deaths, and the odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) was computed. Three models were built. Model 1 was unadjusted. Model 2 was adjusted for age, gender, height and weight. Model 3 considered age, gender, height, weight, hypertension, CHF, myocardial infarct, renal disease, CPD, diabetes, liver disease, ALP, ALT, anion gap, bicarbonate, BUN, calcium, creatinine, glucose, Hb, magnesium, PLT, potassium, sodium, WBC, APSIII, HR, SBP, DBP, RR and SpO2. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression was employed to assess a nonlinear link between the ALBI value and outcome events. Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curves were leveraged to verify the predictive power of ALBI. In addition, subgroup analyses were implemented to substantiate the link of the ALBI score with POD and in-hospital mortality while assessing interaction P values. Each subgroup analysis was adjusted for confounders other than the grouping variables.

R version 4.5.1 was utilized for all analyses. Statistical significance (two-sided tests) was set at p < 0.05 for the main analysis and p < 0.0027 (Bonferroni-adjusted: 0.05/18) for the subgroup interaction analysis.

Results

Population and baseline characteristics

4940 patients were enrolled and categorized into three groups based on the ALBI quartiles: <-2.52, -2.52 to -1.68, and > -1.68. Detailed baseline characteristics are listed in Table 1. Their median age was 69.00 years (IQR: 60.59–76.40 years), and 32.6% were female. Compared with the low-ALBI group, the high-ALBI group exhibited greater overall disease severity and a higher burden of comorbidities. Specifically, patients in the T3 group not only had highest APS III scores, but also demonstrated poorer renal function (elevated creatinine and BUN levels), more severe anemia (lower Hb levels), a heightened inflammatory state (elevated WBC), and less stable vital signs (such as faster HR and lower MBP) (all P < 0.001). This multisystem dysfunction directly corresponded to marked differences in clinical outcomes: the incidence of POD increased progressively from 14.3% in T1 to 26.9% in T3, while in-hospital mortality rose sharply from 2.4% to 12.5% over the same groups.

Association of ALBI with outcome measures

Logistic models were utilized to disclose the impact of ALBI scores on clinical outcomes, with adjustment for established prognostic covariates. Logistic regression analyses evinced that (Model 1: OR [95% CI], 2.216 [1.812, 2.718]) Model 2: 2.251 [1.839, 2.763]; Model 3: 2.429 [1.929, 3.067]) (all P < 0.001) patients in the T3 quartile of ALBI index had greatly higher POD risk than T1 patients (Table 2). As a continuous variable in Model 3, each unit elevation in ALBI was linked to an 72.7% increase in POD risk (Model 3: 1.727 [1.511, 1.975]. P < 0.001). In the Model 3, T3 patients had substantially higher in-hospital mortality (OR [95% CI], 2.656 [1.717, 4.213], P < 0.001) than T1 patients. As a continuous variable in Model 3, each unit elevation in ALBI was linked to a 155.3% increase in in-hospital death risk (2.553 [1.995, 3.282]. P < 0.001).

Subgroup analyses

ALBI was greatly correlated with POD in all the subgroups, but the effect strength varied across specific subgroups. Significant interactions were observed for age (OR = 1.99 for < 65 years vs. OR = 1.60 for ≥ 65 years, p for interaction = 0.039), gender (OR = 1.92 for males vs. OR = 1.43 for females, p for interaction = 0.035), and CHF (OR = 1.99 for no-CHF vs. OR = 1.37 for CHF, p for interaction = 0.028), while the effect remained relatively consistent in other subgroups (Fig. 4A). When in-hospital mortality was analyzed as the outcome, a significant interaction was observed with CPD (p for interaction = 0.048). The association between ALBI and in-hospital death was stronger in patients without CPD (OR = 2.64) than in those with CPD (OR = 2.04) (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

The present retrospective study illustrated that high ALBI score was linked with clinical adverse outcomes (POD and in-hospital mortality) of patients after cardiac surgery, and the link persisted after extensive adjustment of confounding variables. Furthermore, the additive value of ALBI in forecasting adverse outcomes remained stable in the most subgroup analyses. In summary, ALBI score is a predictive index for POD and in-hospital mortality in patients following cardiac surgery.

ALBI score was originally developed to evaluate liver function and prognosis in HCC [21]. Compared with the traditional Child–Pugh score and MELD score, the ALBI score is more reliable as it regards biomarkers as continuous variables rather than using fixed cutoffs and is not affected by subjective assessment. Since its introduction, growing attention has been paid to its close link with heart disease. A retrospective study involving 5629 patients after elective percutaneous coronary intervention unveiled the link of ALBI score with long-term mortality in this population [30]. Liu et al. indicated that the ALBI score was a predictor for in-hospital and follow-up deaths in patients undergoing thoracic endovascular aortic repair [31]. Moreover, ALBI score was considerably related to mortality in patients with heart failure [23, 32]. Our study enrolled a larger patient cohort from the MIMIC database and unveiled that the ALBI score could evaluate the prognosis of patients undergoing cardiac surgery, through multiple analyses and RCS modeling.

POD is frequently present after cardiac surgery and involves patient vulnerability before surgery, insults during surgery, and postoperative management protocols [33]. According to our findings, higher ABLI, both when analyzed as a categorical cutoff and as a continuous variable, was linked to higher odds of POD. This is the first report to identify the ALBI score as a factor associated with POD in patients undergoing cardiac surgery. Several studies reported that hepatic dysfunction reflected by high ALBI levels was an important risk factor for delirium [33]. Serum albumin is a multifaceted biomarker that reflects nutritional status and plays vital physiological roles in maintaining homeostasis, modulating inflammatory responses, and combating oxidative stress [34]. Decreased serum albumin concentrations clinically indicate protein-energy malnutrition and may impair their capacity to effectively exert antioxidant functions in the central nervous system, which increases the possibility of POD [35, 36]. Previous research indicated preoperative hypoalbuminemia was an independent predictor for POD in patients after cardiac surgery [6, 17], although its predictive value remained disputed [37]. Moreover, Lu et al. demonstrated a correlation between hyperbilirubinemia (total bilirubin > 2 mg/dl) and POD in ICU patients [38]. In the present study, the ALBI score was computed using immediate postoperative serum albumin and bilirubin levels and had a significant improvement in predicting POD after cardiac surgery over serum albumin (0.6367 AUC vs. 0.6237 AUC).

Several mechanisms may explain the link between ALBI score and prognosis in individuals undergoing cardiac surgery. First, a meta-analysis indicated that liver dysfunction was greatly linked to short- and long-term outcomes in individuals after cardiac surgery [39]. Surgery-induced hypotension, hypoperfusion, and ischemia-reperfusion may enhance hepatic injury [40]. Subsequently, liver dysfunction triggers a cascade of risks: compromised detoxification and metabolic support, leading to multiorgan dysfunction (e.g., hepatorenal syndrome), while impaired synthesis functions exacerbate infection susceptibility and coagulopathy [40]. Second, the observed association between ALBI and reduced survival may be attributed to the chronic detrimental effects of impaired nutritional status, including hypoalbuminemia and anemia (both seen in our study cohort). Extensive research established that preoperative nutritional deficiency independently predicted elevated postoperative mortality in patients following cardiac surgical procedures [41]. Third, this correlation may be attributed to the synergistic immunomodulatory roles of both composed factors. A of albumin showed a protective effect on endothelial cells in an inflammatory setting [42]. Hypoalbuminemia independently predicted postoperative mortality in patients after cardiac surgery [43], while albumin administration correlated with reduced hospital mortality [44]. Previous studies demonstrated that bilirubin exhibited multiple biological roles, including potent anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory functions [45]. However, in the postoperative context, elevated bilirubin (hyperbilirubinemia) primarily reflects the severity of hepatic injury or systemic complications, rather than conferring a protective effect.​ A meta-analysis revealed that hyperbilirubinemia occurred in 23% of patients after cardiac surgery, with postoperative hyperbilirubinemia linked to 9-fold higher in-hospital death [46]. In summary, the ALBI score, by combining serum albumin and bilirubin measurements, can effectively predict short- and long-term death risks in these patients. Consequently, this study holds significant practical implications.

Our subgroup analyses identified that the association between the ALBI score and POD was stronger in younger patients (< 65 years), males, and those without CHF. We postulate that the attenuated effect observed in elderly patients may be attributed to a competitive risk phenomenon; their high baseline risk for POD, driven by age-related decline in brain reserve and comorbidity burden [47], potentially dilutes the relative contribution of the ALBI score. Conversely, in younger patients with a lower baseline risk, an elevated ALBI score emerges as a more salient and powerful driver, accounting for the stronger observed association. A similar masking effect likely occurs in patients with CHF, which is itself a potent independent risk factor for POD [48]. The heightened vulnerability in males may be linked to a lack of the neuroprotective estrogen effects observed in females [49], a finding consistent with reports of higher POD incidence in older male patients following major surgeries like hip fracture repair [50]. Consequently, these identified high-risk subgroups warrant closer monitoring and the implementation of targeted.

This study exhibits several methodological strengths. First, the cohort size surpassed that of previous investigations, improving statistical validity. Second, hepatic function was objectively quantified using the ALBI score, a standardized biomarker integrating albumin and bilirubin levels, reducing subjective interpretation. Third, covariates spanning demographic and clinical parameters were systematically adjusted, exceeding covariate control in comparable studies. Notwithstanding these merits, three limitations warrant consideration. First, the single-center design may introduce selection bias, restricting generalizability. Second, while the MIMIC-IV database contained serial albumin and bilirubin measurements, our analysis relied solely on postoperative initial values, potentially overlooking dynamic fluctuations relevant to clinical progression. Third, despite comprehensive adjustments, residual confounders from unmeasured variables cannot be excluded. Thus, prospective and multicenter studies are needed in the future.

Conclusions

ABLI score is a crucial predictor of POD and in-hospital mortality in patients undergoing cardiac surgery, and shows nearly linear links with the risk of POD and in-hospital mortality. The impact was stronger in patients aged < 65 years, males, and those without CHF or CPD. These findings have crucial implications for risk stratification and management of individuals after cardiac surgery.

Pressure tuning of competing interactions on a honeycomb lattice

Abstract
Magnetic exchange interactions are mediated via orbital overlaps across chemical bonds. Thus, modifying the bond angles by physical pressure or strain can tune the relative strength of competing interactions. Here we present a remarkable case of such tuning between the Heisenberg ( J J ) and Kitaev ( K K ) exchange, which respectively establish magnetically ordered and spin liquid phases on a honeycomb lattice. We observe a rapid suppression of the Néel temperature ( T N T_{\text{N}} ) with pressure in Ag 3 ​ LiRh 2 ​ O 6 \text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}} , a spin-1/2 honeycomb lattice with both J J and K K couplings. Using a combined analysis of x-ray data and first-principles calculations, we find that pressure modifies the bond angles in a way that increases the | K / J | |K/J| ratio and thereby suppresses T N T_{\text{N}} . Consistent with this picture, we observe a spontaneous onset of muon spin relaxation ( μ \mu SR) oscillations below T N T_{\text{N}} at low pressure, whereas in the high pressure phase, oscillations appear only when T < T N / 2 T<T_{\textrm{N}}/2 . Unlike other cadidate Kitaev materials, Ag 3 ​ LiRh 2 ​ O 6 \text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}} is tuned toward a quantum critical point by pressure while avoiding a structural dimerizaion in the relevant pressure range.

The same materials also host isotropic Heisenberg interactions which favor a long-range magnetic order (LRO)

Theoretically, the QSL ground state could be established by tuning the competition between the Kitaev and Heisenberg interactions in favor of the former

]

One approach to this problem would be to chemically design new materials with a large Kitaev to Heisenberg coupling ratio
|K/J|

Unfortunately, this is proven to be an extremely challenging task

An alternative approach would be to use external parameters such as magnetic field strength

We fit the magnetic polarization curves to the following expression

\displaystyle G_{\textrm{mag}}(t)

\displaystyle=f_{\text{osc}}\left[f_{1}J_{0}(\gamma_{\mu}\Delta B_{1}t)\cos(\gamma_{\mu}B_{1,\textrm{avg}}t)\exp(-\Lambda_{1}t)+f_{2}J_{0}(\gamma_{\mu}\Delta B_{2}t)\cos(\gamma_{\mu}B_{2,\textrm{avg}}t)\exp(-\Lambda_{2}t)\right]

\displaystyle+\left(1-f_{\text{osc}}\right)\exp(-\lambda_{L}t)

(1)

which consists of two oscillatory terms, indicating two magnetically inequivalent stopping sites.
Each term has a fractional contribution (
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
) to the total oscillatory component
f_{\text{osc}}
constrained by
f_{1}+f_{2}=1
;

f_{1}
was found to be 0.59(1) at low temperature and ambient pressure and fixed at that value in all subsequent fits.
The two oscillatory terms are known as the Overhauser approximation
[
30
]
for incommensurate magnetic ordering with a field distribution experienced by the muon which is symmetric about some non-zero average field in the range
B_{i,\textrm{min}}\leq B_{i}\leq B_{i,\textrm{max}}
, with

B_{i,\textrm{avg}}=\frac{B_{i,\textrm{max}}+B_{i,\textrm{min}}}{2},\quad\Delta B_{i}=\frac{B_{i,\textrm{max}}-B_{i,\textrm{min}}}{2}

(2)

and
J_{0}
being the zeroth-order Bessel function of the first kind.
Each term is damped at a respective rate
\Lambda_{i}

A long-time exponential decay with the rate
\lambda_{L}
constitutes the remaining fraction of the
\text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
response from the muons that experience a local field parallel to the initial muon spin orientation (on average 1/3 of the muons in an isotropic polycrystalline sample).
For ease of fitting, we only used a single
\lambda_{L}
for both muon stopping sites.

Discussion

In previous studies,
[
13
,
14
]
magnetic field has been used to melt the long-range order into a fluctuating regime in honeycomb lattices such as
\alpha
-
\text{RuCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
.
Instead of changing the strength of
J
or
K
couplings, magnetic field enters the Hamiltonian as an external parameter (Zeeman term)
[
31
,
32
]
.
In contrast, pressure could tune the relative strength of competing interactions directly by changing orbital overlaps.
Despite theoretical proposals about using pressure as a powerful tuning parameter in Kitaev systems
[
33
,
34
]
, an experimental verification has not been possible until now, because a small pressure is enough to induce a dimerization transition in both
4d
systems (Ru
2
dimerization at 0.2 GPa in
\alpha
-
\text{RuCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and at 0.5 GPa in
\text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
)
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
and
5d
systems (Ir
2
dimerization at 1.4 GPa in
\beta
-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{IrO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
)
[
23
]
.
Remarkably, such a structural transition does not appear in
\text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
until 5 GPa, leaving a gap between the AFM (red) and dimerized (blue) phases in the phase diagram of Fig.
3
f.

The emerging picture from our observations is a change of regime in
\text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
from a static AFM order to a dynamic spin liquid like state.
Such a transition could be interpreted either within a
J
-
K
model, as demonstrated in Fig.
2
d, or within a
J_{1}
-
J_{2}
model, although material-specific calculations are not available for this model.
Moving forward, it will be helpful to get spectroscopic information from inelastic x-ray scattering and Raman scattering about the pressure induced dynamic regime near 4 GPa, and to search for evidence of quantum critical behavior by measuring temperature dependence of specific heat or NMR at low temperatures (
T<2
K) near 4 GPa.
Such experiments, combined with material-specific calculations, could reveal the nature of the low-lying excitations in the gap between the red and blue phases in Fig.
3
f.

{methods}

0.1
Material Synthesis.

Polycrystalline samples of
\text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
were synthesized using a topotactic cation-exchange reaction from the parent compound
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{RhO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
following a previous publication
[
15
]
.
The structural and compositional quality of all samples were characterized at ambient condition with powder x-ray diffraction and energy dispersive x-ray spectroscopy.
The only impurity found was about 5% pure silver inclusions.

0.2
Magnetization measurements.

Magnetization of the powder sample was measured in a Quantum Design MPMS3 using a composite ceramic anvil cell
[
39
]
with Daphne oil 7373 as the pressure-transmitting medium.
Pressure was determined from the superconducting transition of a lead manometer.
To achieve the maximum pressure of about 5.5 GPa, a pair of anvils with small culet sizes (1 mm) were used in runs 1, 2, and 3.
A small sample chamber with both diameter and thickness of 0.5 mm was drilled into the Be-Cu gasket.
To obtain data with higher quality for the CW fits, another pair of anvils with larger culets (1.8 mm) were used in run 4.
This time, the maximum pressure was about 2 GPa due to the larger sample chamber with both diameter and thickness of 0.9 mm.
In each run, magnetization of the empty cell was measured first as the background and subtracted from total signal.
The small jumps near zero magnetization in Fig.
1
f and Fig. S1a,b are due to this subtraction.

0.3
Muon spin relaxation (
\mu
SR).

The
\mu

\pi
M3" and "
\mu

Measurements on a pressed disk (12 mm diameter, 1 mm thickness) were made on GPS at ambient pressure using a gas flow cryostat between 110 and 6.5 K.
Measurements in GPD at pressures of 0.57 and 2.29 GPa (as determined by an indium manometer) were made in He-flow cryostat using a piston-cylinder pressure cell
[
27
]
with Daphne oil 7373 as the pressure-transmitting medium.
Data were analyzed using the MUSRFIT program
[
40
]
.

0.4
X-ray diffraction.

X-ray diffraction (XRD) data were collected at the High Pressure Collaborative Access Team (HPCAT) beamline 16-BM-D of the Advanced Photon Source using diamond anvil cells (DAC) with a combination of full and partially perforated anvils to reduce x-ray attenuation.
Anvil culet diameter was 300
\mu
m.
Rhenium gaskets were pre-indented to a thickness of 50
\mu
m, and a 180
\mu
m-diameter sample chamber was laser drilled at the center of the indentation.
Fine powder ( 5
\mu

\text{Ag}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{LiRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
together with ruby and gold manometers were loaded into the sample chamber filled with Ne pressure medium.
The entire sample chamber was rastered over the
25\times 25~\mu\text{m}^{2}
area of the 30 keV X-ray beam to improve powder averaging on the CCD detector.
Measurements were carried out at both ambient and low temperature (83 K).
2D XRD images were integrated over
2\pi

[
41

[
42

Pressure-dependent lattice parameters were extracted and 2nd order Vinet and Birch-Murnagham equations of state were both fitted using EoSFit
[
43

0.5
DFT calculations.

Structural optimization and electronic structure calculations at high pressures were performed using the QUANTUM ESPRESSO and Wannier90 codes
[
44

45

46

To evaluate the wavefunctions in the supplementary information (Table S1), we first used Quantum ESPRESSO and Wannier90 codes to compute the electronic structure using experimental lattice parameters from our XRD measurements under pressure.

6

The orbital information were calculated from a Hartree-Fock mean-field model.

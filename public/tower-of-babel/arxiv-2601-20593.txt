1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}(k)
be the Morel–Voevodsky unstable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category over a field
k

[
15
]
. Similar to algebraic topology, one can associate various invariants to any simplicial sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{H}(k)
to study its homotopy type. One such invariant is the sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components, denoted by
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
. However, explicitly computing
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
is generally challenging.

Asok and Morel (see
[
1
]
) introduced the sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected components, denoted in this article by
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})
. Intuitively,
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})
(see Definition
2.6
) captures connectedness through homotopies parametrized by the affine line. There exists a canonical epimorphism of sheaves:

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})\to\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F}).

This epimorphism need not be an isomorphism in general, even when
\mathcal{F}
is represented by a smooth proper scheme
[
3
,
7
]
.

To further understand
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
, one considers the universal
\mathbb{A}^{1}
invariant quotient of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})
, which is obtained as the colimit of the sequence
\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The canonical map

\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})=\lim_{\to n}\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})

is an isomorphism if and only if
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant
[
3
]
. Morel conjectured that
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
is always
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant
[
13
]
. Ayoub gave a counter-example to this conjecture (see
[
2
]
) but it remains open for representable sheaves.

Morel’s conjecture holds when
\mathcal{F}
is represented by a scheme of dimension
\leq 1
, an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-rigid scheme, or an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected scheme. Additionally, it has been proved for motivic
H
-groups, homogeneous spaces of motivic
H
-groups
[
8
]
, and smooth toric varieties
[
19
]
. Using the universal
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant quotient, Morel’s conjecture has been verified for smooth projective surfaces
[
3
]
and certain smooth projective threefolds
[
16
]
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a sheaf of sets and
K/k
is a finitely generated separable extension, the natural map

\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})(K)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})(K)

is a bijection (see
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
. Thus, to compute the field-valued points of
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
, we must iterate the functor
\mathcal{S}
infinitely many times. However, if
\mathcal{F}
is represented by a proper scheme
X
, the infinitely many iterations are inessential because
\mathcal{L}(X)(K)=\mathcal{S}(X)(K)
(see
[
1
, Proposition 2.4.3]
or
[
3
, Theorem 2]
). On the other hand, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a non-proper complex variety
X_{n}
for which
\mathcal{S}^{n}(X_{n})(\mathbb{C})\neq\mathcal{S}^{n+1}(X_{n})(\mathbb{C})
, while
\mathcal{L}(X_{n})(F)=\mathcal{S}^{n+1}(X_{n})(F)
for every field extension
F/\mathbb{C}
(see
[
10
]
). Thus, there is no finite, uniform bound on the number of iterations of
\mathcal{S}
that are required in order to compute the field-valued points of the sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components of a variety. It is natural to ask the following question: For a given variety
X
, does there exist
n_{X}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)(K)=\mathcal{S}^{n_{X}}(X)(K)\quad\text{for all }K/k?

The aim of this paper is to compute the field-valued sections of the sheaf
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)
for any smooth affine quadratic hypersurface
X
in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
and to show that the sequence
\mathcal{S}^{n}(X)(K)
stabilizes at
n=2
for all
K/k
. (The case of non-smooth affine quadratic hypersurfaces is quite trivial, as can be seen in Remark
3.3
.)

If
\psi
is a quadratic form in
n
variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
, we will denote by
Q^{\psi}
the quadratic hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
defined by the equation
\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-1=0
. We will see in Theorem
3.2
that it is sufficient to consider quadratic hypersurfaces of this type. Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic 0 and let
F/k
be a finitely generated field extension. Let
\psi
be a regular quadratic form in
n\geq 3
variables, and let
Q^{\psi}
be the smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
defined by the equation
\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-1=0
.

(1)

If
\psi
is isotropic, then
\psi\sim\langle 1\rangle\perp-\phi
for some quadratic form
\phi
in
n-1
variables, and

\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(Q^{\psi})(F)=\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(F)=\frac{F^{*}}{\langle D(\phi_{F})\rangle}.

(2)

If
\psi
is anisotropic, then
\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(k)\neq\mathcal{S}^{2}(Q^{\psi})(k)
and

\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(Q^{\psi})(F)=\mathcal{S}^{2}(Q^{\psi})(F).

Analogous results for algebraic groups have been established in
[
5
,
6
]
. By a result of Morel, a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected if and only if
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})(K)
is trivial for any field
K/k
. Combining this with Theorem
1.1
, we provide a complete characterization of the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected quadratic hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
in terms of classical invariants of quadratic forms.

For a quadratic form
\theta
over a vector space
V
, let
i_{0}(\theta)
denote the Witt index of
\theta
, that is, the dimension of the maximal isotropic subspace of
V
. Let
i_{1}(\theta)=i_{0}(\theta_{k(\theta)})
, where
k(\theta)
is the function field of the projective quadric corresponding to
\theta
. Then, we have proved the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\psi
be a quadratic form in
n\geq 3
variables and let
\kappa
denote the quadratic
\psi\perp\langle-1\rangle
. Then
Q^{\psi}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

i_{0}(\kappa)\geq 2
,

(2)

i_{0}(\kappa)=1
,
i_{0}(\psi)=0
, and
i_{1}(\psi)\geq 2
.

Consequently, if
k
is algebraically closed (or quadratically closed), any smooth affine quadratic hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
for
n\geq 2
is either isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{A}^{n-2}_{k}
or is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Preliminaries on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components

We fix a base field
k
. Let
Sm/k
denote the Grothendieck site of
finite-type smooth schemes on
k
equipped with the Nisnevich topology.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf of sets on
Sm/k
. The sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components
of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\mathbb{\pi}_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
is defined as the Nisnevich sheafification of the presheaf

U\in Sm/k\mapsto Hom_{\mathcal{H}(k)}(U,\mathcal{F}).

Definition 2.2
.

A sheaf of sets
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected if the canonical morphism
\mathcal{F}\to{\rm Spec\,}k
induces the isomorphism of sheaves
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\sim}{\rm Spec\,}k
.

The following result allows us to determine whether
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected by just examining the field-valued sections of
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
14
, Lemma 6.1.3]

A sheaf of sets
\mathcal{F}
on
Sm/k
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected if
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})(K)=*
for every finitely generated separable extension
K
of
k
.

Notation 2.4
.

For any smooth scheme
U
over
k
,
t\in k
, and any
H\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times U)
, define
H(t):=H\circ s_{t}
, where
s_{t}
is the morphism
U\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times U
given by
u\mapsto(t,u)
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf of sets. Let
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(U)
.

(1)

h\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times U)
is called an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy of
U
connecting
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
if
h(0)=x_{0}
and
h(1)=x_{1}
.

(2)

x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are said to be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic
if there exist an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy
h\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times U)
connecting
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
. We say that
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain homotopic
if there exists
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopies
h_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times U)
and
z_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(U)
for
i=1,\dots,r
such that
h_{i}
connects
z_{i-1}
and
z_{i}
where
z_{0}=x_{0}
and
z_{r}=x_{1}
.

Definition 2.6
.

The sheaf of
naive
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components
of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})
, is defined as the Nisnevich sheafification of the presheaf
\mathcal{S}^{pre}(\mathcal{F})
,

\mathcal{S}^{pre}(\mathcal{F})(U):=\frac{\mathcal{F}(U)}{\sim},

where
\sim
is the equivalence relation of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain homotopy.

The sheaf
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})
is also called the
sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected components of
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
1
, Definition 2.2.4]
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})=\pi_{0}(Sing_{*}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F}))
where
Sing_{*}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})
is the Suslin–Voevodsky singular construction of
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
15
, p. 87]
).

We will say that a sheaf of sets
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected
if
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})(K)=\ast
for any finitely generated separable field extension
K/k
(see
[
1
, Definition 2.2.2]
). Note that this condition does not imply that
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})=\ast
but, due to Lemma
2.3
, it does imply that
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})=\ast
.

For a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
and positive integer
n
, let
\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})
be the sheaf obtained from
\mathcal{F}
by applying
n
iterations of the functor
\mathcal{S}
. We have the following sequence of epimorphisms:

\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathcal{F})\to\dots.

Taking the direct limit of this sequence, we arrive at the
universal
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant quotient

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})
:

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}):=\lim_{\to n}\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F}).

For a field
K/k
,
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
states that any two
K
points of
\mathcal{F}
have the same image in
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{F})(K)
if and only if they have the same image in
\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})(K)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since we are interested in computing
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)(K)
for scheme
X
, it will be useful to have an explicit description of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopies of a field
K/k
in
\mathcal{S}^{n}\mathcal{(}X)
via its lift to
X
. For this, we will use the notion of
n
-ghost homotopies. The following definition is a slightly modified reformulation of the one appearing in
[
3
, Definition 3.2]
and
[
7
, Definition 2.7]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf of sets, and
K/k
be a finitely generated field extension. Let
n>0
. Let
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(K)
.

(1)

An
n
-ghost homotopy

\mathcal{H}
of
K
connecting
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
in
\mathcal{F}
consists of the data

\left((U,V),\,h_{U},h_{V},\,y_{0},y_{1}\right),

where:

(a)

(U\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K},\,V\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K})
is an elementary Nisnevich covering of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
,

(b)

The maps
h_{U}:U\to\mathcal{F}
and
h_{V}:V\to\mathcal{F}
satisfy
pr_{U}^{*}(h_{U})=pr_{V}^{*}(h_{V})
in
\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})(U\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}}V)
,
where
pr_{U}
and
pr_{V}
are the projection maps from
U\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}}V
to
U
and
V
, respectively.

(c)

For
i=0,1
,
(h_{U}\coprod h_{V})(y_{i})=x_{i}.

(2)

The elements
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are called
n
-ghost homotopic
in
\mathcal{F}
if there exists an
n
-ghost homotopy connecting them.
They are called
n
-ghost chain homotopic
if there exist
n
-ghost homotopies
\mathcal{H}_{i}
and elements
z_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(K)
for
i=1,\dots,r
such that
\mathcal{H}_{i}
connects
z_{i-1}
and
z_{i}
, where
z_{0}=x_{0}
and
z_{r}=x_{1}
.

Since
\dim\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}=1
, any Nisnevich covering of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
can be refined to an elementary Nisnevich covering. Therefore, any map
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\to\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})
is defined by data as in Definition
2.7
(1). Conversely, such data induce a map
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\to\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})
.

Notation 2.8
.

For any sheaf of sets
\mathcal{F}
, a scheme
U
, and an element
x\in\mathcal{F}(U)
, we denote the image of
x
in
\mathcal{S}^{j}(\mathcal{F})(U)
by
[x]_{j}
.

The following lemma is a restatement of a result from
[
7
]
and follows directly from the definitions.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
7
, Lemma 2.8]

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf of sets, and
K/k
be a finitely generated field extension. Let
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(K)
. Then
[x_{0}]_{n}=[x_{1}]_{n}
if and only if

and

are

.

Building on
[
4

, the next lemma simplifies the data of an
n
-ghost homotopy of a field

.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in Shv(Sm/k)_{Nis}
. Suppose we have the following data:

(1)

(U,V\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k})
is an elementary Nisnevich cover of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
with
W=U\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}}V
.

(2)

h_{U}\in\mathcal{F}(U)
,
h_{V}\in\mathcal{F}(V)
, and there exists a dense open subscheme
W^{\prime}\subset W
such that
pr_{U}^{*}|_{W^{\prime}}(h_{U})
and
pr_{V}^{*}|_{W^{\prime}}(h_{V})
have the same image in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})(W^{\prime})
, where
pr_{U}
and
pr_{V}
are the projection maps from
W
to
U
and
V
, respectively.

Let
h=h_{U}\coprod h_{V}
. Then
[h(P)]_{2}=[h(P^{\prime})]_{2}
for any
P,P^{\prime}\in(U\coprod V)(k)
.

Proof.

We claim that there exists an open subscheme
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
such that the pair
(U,V^{\prime}\xrightarrow{p|_{V^{\prime}}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k})
is an elementary Nisnevich cover and
W^{\prime}=U\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}}V
.

Indeed, let
Z=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\setminus U
. Since
(U,V)
is an elementary Nisnevich cover of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
, the restriction
p^{-1}(Z)\to Z
is an isomorphism. Define
V^{\prime}=W^{\prime}\cup p^{-1}(Z)
. Since
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
is 1-dimensional,
V^{\prime}
is an open subscheme of
V
. Clearly,
V^{\prime}
is the required scheme. Let
h_{V^{\prime}}=h_{V}|_{V^{\prime}}
. Then,

\left((U,V^{\prime}\xrightarrow{p|_{V^{\prime}}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}),W^{\prime},h_{U},h_{V^{\prime}}\right)

forms a data for
1
-ghost homotopy
\mathcal{H}
.

Therefore, for any
P,P^{\prime}\in(U\coprod V^{\prime})(k)
, we have
[h(P)]_{2}=[h(P^{\prime})]_{2}
via
\mathcal{H}
.

Now, similarly for any
Q\in(V\setminus V^{\prime})(k)
, there exists a Nisnevich cover
(U^{\prime}\subset U,V^{\prime\prime}\subset V)
such that
W^{\prime}=U^{\prime}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}}V^{\prime\prime}
and
P\in V^{\prime\prime}(k)
. Indeed, let
V^{\prime\prime}=V^{\prime}\cup\{Q\}
and
U^{\prime}=U\setminus\{p(Q)\}
. Then,

\left((U^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime}\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}),W^{\prime},h_{U^{\prime}},h_{V^{\prime\prime}}\right)

forms the data for a
1
-ghost homotopy
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Thus,
[h(Q)]_{2}=[h(P)]_{2}
for any
P\in(U\coprod V^{\prime})(k)
via a chain of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We present a straightforward consequence of the above lemma, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in Shv(Sm/k)
. Suppose that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})(K)=\mathcal{S}^{n+1}(\mathcal{F})(K)
for all
K/k
. Then

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})(K)=\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})(K)\text{ for all }K/k.

Proof.

We prove that
\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})(K)=\mathcal{S}^{n+i}(\mathcal{F})(K)
for all
K/k
and for all
i\geq 0
by induction on
i
.

The base case
i=1
follows from the assumption. Suppose that the statement holds for some
i=m>1
, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}^{n}(\mathcal{F})(K)=\mathcal{S}^{n+m}(\mathcal{F})(K)
for all
K/k
. We show that it holds for
i=m+1
.

Suppose
[Q_{1}]_{n+m+1}=[Q_{2}]_{n+m+1}\in\mathcal{S}^{n+m+1}(F)(K)
by an
(n+m)
-ghost homotopy
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\to\mathcal{S}^{n+m}(\mathcal{F})
which is given given by the data

\left((U,V\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}),W,H_{U},H_{V},P_{1},P_{2}\right)

such that
(H_{U}\sqcup H_{V})(P_{i})=Q_{i}
and
[pr_{U}^{*}(H_{U})]_{n+m}=[pr_{V}^{*}(H_{V})]_{n+m}
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F})(W)
.

If
W=\sqcup_{j=1}^{r}W_{j}
is the decomposition of
W
into irreducible components, we have for each
j
,

[pr_{U}^{*}|_{k(W_{j})}(H_{U})]_{n+m}=[pr_{V}^{*}|_{k(W_{j})}(H_{U})]_{n+m}.

By the induction hypothesis, for each
j
,

[pr_{U}^{*}|_{k(W_{j})}(H_{U})]_{n}=[pr_{V}^{*}|_{k(W_{j})}(H_{U})]_{n}.

Hence, there exists a dense open subscheme
W^{\prime}
of
W
such that

[pr_{U}^{*}|_{W^{\prime}}(H_{U})]_{n}=[pr_{V}^{*}|_{W^{\prime}}(H_{U})]_{n}.

By Lemma
2.10
, we conclude that
[Q_{1}]_{n}=[Q_{2}]_{n}
, completing the proof.
∎

2.2.
Preliminaries on Quadratic Forms

In this subsection, we recall some classical results from the theory of quadratic forms to ensure that this exposition is self-contained.

Definition 2.13

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form on

\varphi

D(\varphi):=\{d\in k^{*}\mid\exists v\in V\text{ such that }\varphi(v)=d\}.

Notation 2.14
.

For a quadratic form
\varphi
on a vector space
V
, let
\langle D(\varphi)\rangle
denote the subgroup of
k^{*}
generated by the set
D(\varphi)
. For any field extension
K/k

\varphi_{K}
denote the quadratic form on

\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id}
.

Notation 2.15
.

For any closed point
P
of a smooth curve
C
over
k

v_{P}
denote the discrete valuation
v_{P}:k(C)\to\mathbb{Z}
corresponding to the discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{C,P}
.

The following theorem is a restatement of a theorem from
[
9
, Theorem 18.3]
that provides a criterion for a rational function
f\in k(t)
to belong to the group
\langle D(\varphi_{k(t)})\rangle
. Although the original theorem was stated for polynomials, since we have
f\langle D(\varphi_{k(t)})\rangle=f^{-1}\langle D(\varphi_{k(t)})\rangle
, it can be easily extended to rational functions.

Proposition 2.16

(Quadratic Value Theorem)
.

Let
f\in k(t)
. Then, the following are equivalent.

(1)

There exists
a\in k^{*}
such that
f(t)\in a\langle D(\varphi_{k(t)})\rangle
.

(2)

If
P
is a closed point of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
such that
v_{P}(f)
is odd, then
\varphi_{\kappa(P)}
is isotropic.

The following lemma shows that any quadratic form over
k
that is isotropic over the function field of a DVR must also be isotropic over its residue field. This result is well known (see
[
17
,
12
]
), and the proof is simple, but we include it for completeness.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
(R,m,\kappa)
be a DVR containing
k
with function field
K
. Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
k
given by
\langle a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
such that
\varphi_{K}
is isotropic. Then
\varphi_{\kappa}
is isotropic.

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{K}
is isotropic, by clearing denominators, there exists
x_{i}\in R
such that

a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\dots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}=0.

Each
x_{i}
can be written as
x_{i}=t^{m_{i}}u_{i}
, where
t
is a uniformizer (
\langle t\rangle=m
), and
u_{i}
is a unit in
R
. We may assume that not all
x_{i}
belong to
m
, meaning that at least one
\bar{x}_{i}\neq 0
in
\kappa
. Reducing modulo
m
, we obtain

a_{1}\bar{x}_{1}^{2}+\dots+a_{n}\bar{x}_{n}^{2}=0.

Thus,
\varphi_{\kappa}
is isotropic, as required.
∎

In particular, if we take
R
to be the local ring of a closed point on a smooth curve
C
, the lemma implies that if
\varphi_{k(C)}
is isotropic, then
\varphi_{k(P)}
must be isotropic for any closed point
P
of
C
.

The following easy lemma follows directly from the definitions and will be useful in later sections.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
(R,m,\kappa)
be a DVR containing
k
. Suppose
\varphi
is a quadratic form over
k
given by
\langle a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
such that
\varphi_{\kappa}
is anisotropic.

(1)

If
y_{i}\in K
, then

v(\varphi(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}))=\min\{v(y_{i}^{2})\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}

and hence is even.

(2)

Let
g\in K
such that
g\in\langle D(\varphi_{K})\rangle
. If
v(g)\geq 0
, then
\bar{g}=0
or
\bar{g}\in\langle D(\varphi_{\kappa})\rangle
.

Proof.

For (1), without loss of generality assume
v(y_{1})
is minimum. Then, for all
i>1
, we have
y_{i}/y_{1}\in R
, and

v\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right)=v(y_{1}^{2})+v\left(a_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}\frac{y_{i}^{2}}{y_{1}^{2}}\right).

Since
\varphi_{\kappa}
is anisotropic, we have

a_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}\left(\overline{\frac{y_{i}}{y_{1}}}\right)^{2}\neq 0\text{ in }\kappa.

Hence,

v\left(a_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}\frac{y_{i}^{2}}{y_{1}^{2}}\right)=0,

which implies that

v\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right)=v(y_{1}^{2}),

completing the proof of (1).

For (2), let
g=h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{n}
, where
h_{i}\in D(\varphi_{K})
. By (1), we can write

h_{i}=u_{i}t^{2j_{i}}

for some integer
j_{i}
, where
u_{i}\in R^{*}\cap D(\varphi_{K})
and
\langle t\rangle=m
. Therefore, if
v(g)=0
, we obtain

\bar{g}=\bar{u}_{1}\dots\bar{u}_{n}\in\langle D(\varphi_{\kappa})\rangle,

and if
v(g)>0
, we have
\bar{g}=0
.
∎

Definition 2.19
.

Let
\phi
be a quadratic form defined on a vector space
V
over a field
k
.
The
Witt index

of

is the dimension of a maximal totally isotropic subspace of

.
If

is anisotropic (that is,

.

[

Let

[

3.
Classification of affine quadrics

We assume that
k
is a field of characteristic
0
.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
\psi
be a regular quadratic form over
k
in
n
-variables. We will denote by
Q^{\psi}
, the hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
which is the locus of zeroes of the polynomial
\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-1
, where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are the coordinate variables on
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
.

Note that
Q^{\psi}
is a smooth variety.

Theorem 3.2
.

Any smooth quadratic hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{n-1}_{k}
or
Q^{\psi}\times\mathbb{A}^{n-m}_{k}
for some regular quadratic form
\psi
in
m
variables, where
1\leq m\leq n
.

Proof.

First, we show the result for
n=1
. Let
Q
be the closed subvariety of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
cut out by
ax_{1}^{2}+bx_{1}+c=0
. Then, one can check that

ax_{1}^{2}+bx_{1}+c=\frac{1}{4a}\left((2ax_{1}+b)^{2}-(b^{2}-4ac)\right).

Since
Q
is smooth, we have
b^{2}-4ac\neq 0
, and hence
Q\cong Q^{\psi}
where
\psi=\langle 1/(4a(b^{2}-4ac))\rangle
.

For
n>1
, let
Q
be the hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
cut out by

\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x_{i}+c.

Since any quadratic form in
n
-variables is equivalent to a diagonal form, there exists a linear automorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
such that
x_{i}\mapsto X_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{i,j}x_{i}
and

\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x_{i}+c=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}X_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}X_{i}+c.

for some
A_{i},B_{i}\in k
.
Now, if
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are both non-zero for some fixed
i
, then by the Case
n=1
,

A_{i}X_{i}^{2}+B_{i}X_{i}=\frac{1}{4A_{i}}\left((2A_{i}X_{i}+B_{i})^{2}-B_{i}^{2}\right).

Hence, there exists a linear automorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
such that
Q
is isomorphic to
Q^{\prime}
where
Q^{\prime}
is defined by

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}+d=0\quad\text{or }\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}y_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{m}a_{i}y_{i}+d=0

where
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
and
m\leq n
.

In the first case, since
Q
is smooth we have
d\neq 0
, and
Q^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
Q^{\psi}\times\mathbb{A}^{n-m}
where
\psi=(-1/d)\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\rangle
. In the second case, there are linear terms, and hence
Q^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the hypersurface cut out by

f(y_{1},\dots,y_{m-1})+a_{m}y_{m}=0,

which implies that
Q^{\prime}\cong{\rm Spec\,}k[y_{1},\dots,\widehat{y_{m}},\dots,y_{n}]
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

From the previous proof, we can deduce that if
X
is a non-smooth quadratic hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
, then
X
is isomorphic to the affine variety

{\rm Spec\,}k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\langle\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\rangle

for some quadratic form
\psi
in
m
variables. It is straightforward to check that any map
U\rightarrow X
can be made
\mathbb{A}^{1}
homotopic to the constant map
U\rightarrow{\rm Spec\,}k\xrightarrow{(0,\dots,0)}X.
Therefore,
\mathcal{S}(X)=*
trivially in this case.

The previous theorem shows that it is sufficient to examine quadratic surfaces
Q^{\psi}
. We will first consider the easy cases when dimension of
Q^{\psi}
is less than
2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\psi
be a regular quadratic form in
n\leq 2
variables. Then for every finitely generated field extension
K/k
,

\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(Q^{\psi})(K)=\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(K)=Q^{\psi}(K).

Proof.

First, we will consider
n=1
. Then
\psi
is of the form
\langle a\rangle
for some
a\in k^{*}
and
Q^{\langle a\rangle}
is a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
of dimension
0
cut out by

ax_{1}^{2}-1.

If
a
is a square in
k
, then
Q^{\langle a\rangle}\cong{\rm Spec\,}k\coprod{\rm Spec\,}k
and if
a
is not a square in
k
, then
Q^{\langle a\rangle}\cong{\rm Spec\,}k(\sqrt{a})
. Hence, the result is clear in this case.

Now, suppose
n=2
and let
X:=Q^{\psi}
where
\psi=\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
. Then
X
is given by

a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}-1,

where
a_{1},a_{2}\neq 0
.
If
X(K)\neq\emptyset
, then
X_{K}\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\setminus Q^{\langle a_{1}/a_{2}\rangle}
, hence
X_{K}
is a strictly open subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
. Therefore,
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)(K)=X(K)
for all
K/k
as required.
∎

4.
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components of quadric hypersurfaces

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We begin with a brief discussion of the setup.

Let
\psi
be a regular quadratic form in
n\geq 3
variables over a field
k
of characteristic
0
. For any finitely generated field extension
F/k
, we have the sequence of sets
\{\mathcal{S}^{n}(Q^{\psi})(F)\}_{n\geq 1}
, along with the canonical surjections
\mathcal{S}^{n}(Q^{\psi})(F)\to\mathcal{S}^{n+1}(Q^{\psi})(F)
. The colimit of this diagram of sets is
\mathcal{L}(Q^{\psi})(F)
.

Since
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(Q^{\psi})(F)\to\mathcal{L}(Q^{\psi})(F)
is a bijection for all finitely generated field extensions
F/k
by
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
, a major part of the proof of the Theorem
1.1
reduces to showing that for any finitely generated field extension
F/k
, we have

\mathcal{L}(Q^{\psi})(F):=\lim_{n}\mathcal{S}^{n}(Q^{\psi})(F)=\mathcal{S}^{m}(Q^{\psi})(F),

where
m=1
when
\psi
is isotropic and
m=2
when
\psi
is anisotropic.

For the sets
\mathcal{S}^{n}(Q^{\psi})(F)
or
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(Q^{\psi})(F)
to be non-empty, it is necessary and sufficient that
Q^{\psi}
should have an
F
-rational point. We observe that
Q^{\psi}
has an
F
-rational point if and only if its projective completion, i.e. its closure in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
, has an
F
-rational point.

Notation 4.1
.

Let
\bar{Q}^{\psi}
denote the projective completion of
Q^{\psi}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
, defined as the locus of zeroes of the homogeneous polynomial
\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-x_{n+1}^{2}
. Let
H^{\psi}
denote the hyperplane section at infinity of
\bar{Q}^{\psi}
, i.e.
H^{\psi}:=\bar{Q}^{\psi}-Q^{\psi}
. The quadratic form
\psi
corresponds to the projective quadric
H^{\psi}
.

Since
\psi\perp\langle-1\rangle
is the quadratic form corresponding to the projective quadric
\bar{Q}^{\psi}
, the isotropy of this form is detected by the existence of a
k
-rational point on
\bar{Q}^{\psi}
, and hence also on
Q^{\psi}
. In other words,
Q^{\psi}
has a
k
-rational point if and only if
\psi\perp\langle-1\rangle
is isotropic, which is equivalent to
1\in D(\psi)
. Thus, there exists a regular form
\phi
in
n-1
variables such that

\psi\sim\langle 1\rangle\perp(-\phi),\quad\text{and}\quad\psi\perp\langle-1\rangle\sim\langle 1,-1\rangle\perp(-\phi).

This form
\phi
will play a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. (The minus sign in
(-\phi)
is merely to ensure that
1
is an element of
D(\phi)
whenever
\psi
is isotropic.)

As the statement of Theorem
1.1
suggests, we will see that the number of iterations of
\mathcal{S}
required for the stabilisation of field-valued sections of
\mathcal{L}(Q^{\psi})
depends on the isotropy of
\psi
, or equivalently, on the existence of a
k
-rational point on
H^{\psi}
.

The case when
\psi
is isotropic is dealt with in subsection
4.1
. The results in this case will be useful for our investigation of the anisotropic case, which is presented in subsection
4.2
.

4.1.
The isotropic case

Let
\psi
be an isotropic regular form in
n\geq 3
variables. Then
\psi\perp\langle-1\rangle
is isotropic and we have
\psi\sim(-\phi)\perp\langle 1\rangle
for some regular form
\phi
in
n-1
variables.
We will prove that when
\psi
is isotropic, the sequence
\{\mathcal{S}^{n}(Q^{\psi})(F)\}
stabilizes at
n=1
for any finitely generated field extension
F/k
. The proof will consist of three steps:

(i)

We will first look at the case when
\phi
is isotropic. We prove that, in this case,
Q^{\psi}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected, and hence
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected.

(ii)

In the general situation (i.e. with
\psi
is isotropic but without any assumption on
\phi
), we will obtain an isomorphism
\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}\frac{F^{*}}{\langle D(\phi)\rangle}
for any finitely generated field extension
F/k
.

(iii)

We will then prove that
\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(F)\to\mathcal{S}^{2}(Q^{\psi})(F)
is a bijection for any finitely generated field extension
F/k
.

Since any isotropic quadratic form is universal, it follows that if
\phi
is isotropic, then
D(\phi)=k^{*}
. The following lemma establishes Theorem
1.1
(1) in the case where
\phi
is isotropic.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\psi
be an isotropic form in
n\geq 3
variables over a field
k
of characteristic 0. Then there exists a regular form
\phi^{\prime}
such that
\psi\sim\langle 1,-1\rangle\perp(\phi^{\prime})
. Let
\phi=\langle 1\rangle\perp(-\phi^{\prime})
and
X^{\phi}
denote the affine quadric in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
which is the zero locus of polynomial
x_{1}x_{2}-\phi(1,x_{3},\dots,x_{n})
, where
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are the coordinates variables of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
. Then

(1)

Q^{\psi}
is isomorphic to
X^{\phi}
.

(2)

Let
\pi:X^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
be the projection on
x_{1}
-axis. Then the fiber
\pi^{-1}(a)
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected for any
a\in k^{*}
and
\pi^{-1}(0)
has a
k
-rational point if and only if
\phi
is isotropic.

(3)

If
\phi
is isotropic, then for all finitely generated field extensions
F/k
, we have

\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(F)=*.

In other words,
Q^{\psi}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected.

Proof.

For (1), recall that
Q^{\psi}
is defined as the locus of zeroes of polynomial
\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-1
in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
. Since
\psi\sim\langle 1,-1\rangle\perp\phi^{\prime}
,
Q^{\psi}
is isomorphic to the affine quadric defined by
x_{1}x_{2}-\phi^{\prime}(x_{3},\dots,x_{n})-1
, which is same as
x_{1}x_{2}-\phi(1,x_{3},\dots,x_{n})
.

For (2), the morphism
\pi:X^{\phi}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
is induced by the following homomorphism of coordinate rings:

k[x_{1}]\rightarrow k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\langle x_{1}x_{2}-\phi(1,x_{3},\dots,x_{n})\rangle.

For any
a\in k^{*}
, the fiber
\pi^{-1}(a)
is the affine variety

{\rm Spec\,}k[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]/\langle ax_{2}-\phi(1,x_{3},\dots,x_{n})\rangle\simeq\mathbb{A}^{n-2}_{k},

which is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected.

The fiber
\pi^{-1}(0)
is the affine variety

{\rm Spec\,}\frac{k[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]}{\langle\phi(1,x_{3},\dots,x_{n})\rangle}\cong{\rm Spec\,}k[x_{2}]\times{\rm Spec\,}\frac{k[x_{3},\dots,x_{n}]}{\langle\phi(1,x_{3},\dots,x_{n})\rangle}.

Let
Y
denote the quadratic hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n-2}_{k}
defined by
\phi(1,x_{3},\dots,x_{n})=0
, hence
\pi^{-1}(0)=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times Y
. Clearly
\phi
is isotropic if and only if
Y(k)\neq\varnothing
.

For (3), since
\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(F)=\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi}_{F})(F)
and
\psi_{F}
is isotropic for all
F/k
, we may assume that
F=k
. Moreover since
Q^{\psi}\cong X^{\phi}
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{S}(X^{\phi})(k)=*
when
\phi
is isotropic. For any
k
-rational point
y
of
Y
, let
L_{y}
denote the affine line in
\pi^{-1}(0)
given as the image of the map

\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{id}\times y}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times Y.

Claim:
For any
y\in Y(k)
, there exists a section
s:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\rightarrow X^{\phi}
of
\pi
such that
s(0)\in L_{y}
.

Proof of the Claim.

Since
y\in Y(k)
, we have
y=(y_{3},\dots,y_{n})
for some
y_{i}\in k
, and

\phi(1,y_{3},\dots,y_{n})=1+\sum_{i=3}^{n}b_{i}y_{i}^{2}=0.

Define
s:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\to X^{\phi}
by

t\mapsto(s_{1}(t),\dots,s_{n}(t))=(t,s_{2}(t),t+y_{3},\dots,t+y_{n}),

where

s_{2}(t)=\frac{\phi(1,s_{3}(t),\dots,s_{n}(t))}{s_{1}(t)}=\frac{1+\sum_{i=3}^{n}b_{i}(t+y_{i})^{2}}{t}.

Expanding
s_{2}(t)
, we get

s_{2}(t)=\frac{1+\sum_{i=3}^{n}b_{i}y_{i}^{2}+tf(t)}{t},

where
f(t)\in k[t]
. Thus,
s_{2}(t)=f(t)
is a polynomial in
t
, and hence
s
is well defined.

Therefore,
s
is a section of
\pi
, and we have

s(0)=(0,f(0),y_{3},y_{4},\dots,y_{n})\in L_{y}.\qed

Since
y
was an arbitrary
k
-rational point of
Y
, and any fiber of
\pi
over a non-zero
k
-rational point is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected, the above claim implies that
\mathcal{S}(X^{\phi})(k)=*
, as required.
∎

Notation 4.3
.

We fix the following setting for the remainder of this subsection.

(1)

Let
\psi:=\langle 1,-1\rangle\perp\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi:=\langle 1\rangle\perp(-\phi^{\prime})
,
where
\phi^{\prime}
is a fixed regular quadratic form over a field
k
of characteristic

.

(2)

We fix the coordinate system

.

(3)

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

\mathcal{S}(Q^{\psi})(F)\cong\frac{F^{*}}{\langle D(\phi_{F})\rangle}.

.

v(f_{1}^{2})=v(f_{1}^{2}-1)=\min\{v(f_{2}^{2}),\dots,v(f_{n}^{2})\},

hence we arrive at our result.∎

We now introduce the notion of
good rational curves
in
Q^{\psi}
, which will play a key role in the computation of the sets
\mathcal{S}^{2}(Q^{\psi})(F)
. For any rational curve
f:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\dashrightarrow Q^{\psi}
and
f^{*}(x_{i})=f_{i}
, we have
(f_{1}-1)(f_{1}+1)\big)=\phi(f_{2},\dots,f_{n})
.

Definition 4.12
.

A rational map
f:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\dashrightarrow Q^{\psi}
is called a
good rational curve
in
Q^{\psi}
if

is non-constant and there exists
c\in k^{*}
such that
f^{*}(x_{1})-1\in c\langle D(\phi_{k(t)})\rangle,
or equivalently,
f^{*}(x_{1})+1\in c\langle D(\phi_{k(t)})\rangle.

Construction 4.13
.

Let
\psi
and
Q^{\psi}
be as in Notation
4.9
.
Let
F/k
be a field such that
\psi_{F}
is isotropic.
Then
\psi_{F}=\langle 1\rangle\perp(-\phi_{F})
with
1\in D(\phi_{F})
.
Hence, there exist regular quadratic forms
\theta^{F}
and
\theta^{\prime F}
over
F
such that

\phi_{F}\sim\theta^{F},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta^{F}=\langle 1\rangle\perp\theta^{\prime F}.

Similar to Notation
4.3
, let
X^{\theta^{F}}\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{F}
denote the affine quadric defined by the equation
z_{1}z_{2}-\theta^{F}(1,z_{3},\dots,z_{n})=0
, where
z_{i}
are coordinate variables of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{F}
. Then
Q^{\psi}_{F}
is isomorphic to
X^{\theta^{F}}
.

We now construct an explicit isomorphism

\mathcal{J}^{F}:Q^{\psi}_{F}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}X^{\theta^{F}}.

On coordinate rings, the map
\mathcal{J}^{F}
corresponds to the composition of the following two ring isomorphisms:

\frac{F[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]}{\langle z_{1}z_{2}-\theta^{F}(1,z_{3},\dots,z_{n})\rangle}\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;\frac{F[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]}{\langle w_{1}^{2}-w_{2}^{2}-\theta^{F}(1,w_{3},\dots,w_{n})\rangle},

given by

z_{1}\mapsto w_{1}-w_{2},\qquad z_{2}\mapsto w_{1}+w_{2},\qquad z_{i}\mapsto w_{i}\ \text{for }i\geq 3;

and

\frac{F[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]}{\langle w_{1}^{2}-1-\theta^{F}(w_{2},\dots,w_{n})\rangle}\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;\frac{F[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}{\langle x_{1}^{2}-1-\phi_{F}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\rangle},

defined by

w_{1}\mapsto x_{1},\qquad w_{i}\mapsto\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{ij}x_{j}\quad\text{for }i\geq 2,

for suitable
a_{ij}\in F
.
(Note that
w_{1}^{2}-w_{2}^{2}-\theta^{F}(1,w_{3},\dots,w_{n})=w_{1}^{2}-1-\theta^{F}(w_{2},\dots,w_{n})
,
and the coefficients
a_{ij}
exist because
\theta^{F}
and
\phi_{F}
are isometric over
F
.)

Remark 4.14
.

For fields
F
with
\psi_{F}
isotropic, the quadric
X^{\theta^{F}}
and fits into the framework of
Notation
4.3
in the isotropic case. Here, the roles of
k
,
\psi
, and
\phi
in
Notation
3.1
are played by
F
,
\langle 1\rangle\perp(-\theta^{F})
, and
\theta^{F}
, respectively. Hence, all the results of Subsection
4.1
apply to
X^{\theta^{F}}
, and therefore also to
Q^{\psi}_{F}
via the isomorphism constructed above.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\psi
be an anisotropic form in
n\geq 3
variables over a field of characteristic 0 as in Notation
4.9
. Let
f:\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}\dashrightarrow Q^{\psi}
be a rational curve in
Q^{\psi}
, and let
F/k
be a field extension such that
\psi_{F}
is isotropic. Let
\theta^{F}
,
X^{\theta^{F}}
, and
\mathcal{J}^{F}
be as defined in Construction
4.13
. Let
g:\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}\dashrightarrow X^{\theta^{F}}
be a rational map defined as the composition

\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}\overset{f_{F}}{\dashrightarrow}Q^{\psi}_{F}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{J}^{F}}X^{\theta^{F}}.

(1)

If
P\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}
such that
\phi_{F(P)}
is anisotropic and
f_{F}
is not defined at
P
, then
v_{P}(f_{F}^{*}(x_{1}-1))
is odd if and only if
v_{P}(g^{*}(z_{1}))
is odd.

(2)

If
f
is a good rational curve and
P
is an
F
-rational point in the image of
f_{F}
, then the map
f_{F}:{\rm Spec\,}F(t)\to Q^{\psi}_{F}
is chain
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic to the constant map
{\rm Spec\,}F(t)\xrightarrow{P}Q^{\psi}_{F}
.

Proof.

Suppose
f^{*}(y_{i})=f_{i}\in k(t)
and
g^{*}(z_{i})=g_{i}\in F(t)
. Using the description of
\mathcal{J}^{F}
from Construction
4.13
, we have

g_{1}=f_{1}-\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{2,j}f_{j},\,g_{2}=f_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{2,j}f_{j}\text{ and }g_{i}=\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{i,j}f_{j}\text{ for }i>2.

Hence,
v_{P}(g_{1})\leq\min\left\{v_{P}(f_{1}),v_{P}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{2,j}f_{j}\right)\right\}.

For (1), let
P\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}
such that
\phi_{F(P)}
is anisotropic and
f_{F}
is not defined at
P
. Then by Lemma
4.11
, we have
v_{P}(f_{1})<0
and

v_{P}(f_{1}-1)=v_{P}(f_{1})=\min\{v_{P}(f_{2}),\dots,v_{P}(f_{n})\}.

Therefore,

v_{P}(f_{1})\leq v_{P}(\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{2,j}f_{j}).

It suffices to show that
v_{P}(f_{1})
is odd if and only if
v_{P}(g_{1})
is odd.
If the above inequality is strict, we will have
v_{P}(g_{1})=v_{P}(f_{1})
and hence we are done. Otherwise, there exists
u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{P,\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}}^{*}
such that
f_{1}=\pi^{r}u
and
\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{2,j}f_{j}=\pi^{r}v
,
where
\langle\pi\rangle=m_{P}
and
r
is a negative integer. Since
v_{P}(g_{1})=v_{P}(\pi^{r}(u-v))
and
v_{P}(f_{1})=r
, what remains is to show that
v_{P}(u-v)
is even.

Now
g_{1}g_{2}\in D(\phi_{F(t)})
and
\phi_{F(P)}
is anisotropic, hence
v_{P}(g_{1}g_{2})
is even. Therefore,
we have

\displaystyle v_{P}(g_{1}g_{2})

\displaystyle=v_{P}(\pi^{r}(u-v))+v_{P}(\pi^{r})(u+v)

\displaystyle=2r+v_{P}(u+v)+v_{P}(u-v)

Since
v_{P}(g_{1}g_{2})
is even and
v_{P}(u+v)
and
v_{P}(u-v)
cannot be positive simultaneously, we have
v_{P}(u-v)
,
v_{P}(u+v)
are both even which finishes the proof.

For (2), suppose
f
is a good rational curve in
Q^{\psi}
. Then there exists
c\in k^{*}
such that
f^{*}(x_{1}-1)\in c\langle D(\phi_{k(t)})\rangle
. Hence,

f_{F}^{*}(x_{1}-1)\in c\langle D(\phi_{F(t)})\rangle.

Therefore, by QVT, we have that
v_{P}(f_{F}^{*}(x_{1}-1))
is even for all
P\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}
whenever
\phi_{F(P)}
is anisotropic. Let
P\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}
such that
\phi_{F(P)}
is anisotropic. If
f_{F}
is not defined at
P
, then
v_{P}(g^{*}(z_{1}))
is even by (1). If
f_{F}
is defined at
P
, then so is
g
and by Lemma
4.7

v_{P}(g^{*}(z_{1}))=0
. Therefore, applying QVT to
g^{*}(z_{1})
, there exists
d\in F^{*}
such that

g^{*}(z_{1})\in d\langle D(\phi_{F(t)})\rangle.

Now, (2) follows directly from Corollary
4.6
.
∎

5.
Characterization of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected quadrics

It is well known that a smooth projective quadric
Q
over a field
k
is rational if and only if it has a
k
-rational point, i.e.,
Q(k)\neq\emptyset
. Consequently, the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of smooth projective quadrics is completely determined by the existence of a rational point over the base field (see Theorem 3,
[
1
]
). In this section, we extend this characterization to affine quadrics. Specifically, we describe the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of affine quadrics in terms of classical invariants of quadratic forms.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
X=Q^{\psi}
with where
\psi
is a quadratic form in
n\geq 3
variables. Let
\varphi:=\psi\perp\langle-1\rangle
be the quadratic form corresponding to
\bar{X}
. Then
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

i_{0}(\varphi)\geq 2
.

(2)

i_{0}(\varphi)=1
,
i_{0}(\psi)=0
, and
i_{1}(\psi)\geq 2
.

Proof.

If
i_{0}(\varphi)\geq 1
, then
1\in D(\psi)
and there exists a quadratic form
\phi
in
n-1
variables such that

\psi=\langle 1\rangle\perp\langle-\phi\rangle,\quad\text{and }\varphi=\langle 1,-1\rangle\perp-\phi.

(
\impliedby
) By Theorem
2.3
, it suffices to show that
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)(F)=*
for all
F/k
whenever (1) or (2) holds. Suppose condition (1) holds, then
\phi
is isotropic, and the result follows directly from Lemma
4.2
.

Now, suppose condition (2) holds. In this case,
\psi
is anisotropic. Since
i_{1}(\psi)\geq 2
, Theorem
2.20
ensures that
i_{0}(\psi_{F})\geq 2
whenever
\psi_{F}
is isotropic. Since
-\phi
is a codimension-1 subform of
\psi
,
i_{1}(\psi)\geq 2
implies that
\psi_{F}
is isotropic if and only if
\phi_{F}
is isotropic.

This guarantees that every rational curve in
X
is a good rational curve. Since
\bar{X}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected, we have
\mathcal{S}(\bar{X})(F)=*
. Therefore, any two
F
-points of
X
can be connected by a chain of good rational curves, which, together with, Theorem
4.19
implies that
\mathcal{S}^{2}(Q^{\psi})(F)=*
for all
F/k
, completing the proof.

(
\implies
) Suppose that
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected. This implies that
X(k)\neq\varnothing
, which in turn ensures that
i_{0}(\varphi)\geq 1
. Consequently,
\varphi=\langle 1,-1\rangle\perp-\phi.
It suffices to show that for all cases other than (1) and (2), there exists a field extension
F/k
such that
\psi_{F}
is isotropic and
\mathcal{S}(X)(F)\neq*
. Indeed, since
\mathcal{S}(X)(F)=\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X)(F)
whenever
\psi_{F}
is isotropic, we will be done. The remaining cases are as follows:

Case

(
i_{0}(\varphi)=1
and
i_{0}(\psi)=1
)
.

In this case,
\psi
is isotropic, but
\phi
is anisotropic. By QVT, we have:

t\notin\langle D(\phi_{k(t)})\rangle,

which implies that
\mathcal{S}(X)(k(t))\neq*
.

Case

(
i_{0}(\varphi)=1
,
i_{0}(\psi)=0
, and
i_{1}(\psi)=1
)
.

By Theorem
2.21
,
\phi_{k(\psi)}
is anisotropic. Therefore, by the QVT, we have:

t\notin\langle D(\phi_{k(\psi)(t)})\rangle,

which implies that
\mathcal{S}(X)(k(\psi)(t))\neq*
.

In both cases, we have shown that
X
is not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected, which completes the proof.
∎

Examples 5.2
.

The above theorem can be applied to produce numerous examples of affine quadrics that are either
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected or not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected.

(1)

If
k
is quadratically closed, then for any regular quadratic form
\varphi
over
k
, we have

i_{0}(\varphi)=\left\lfloor\frac{\dim\varphi}{2}\right\rfloor.

Hence, any affine quadric of dimension
n>0
over such a field is either
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected or isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{A}^{n-1}_{k}
.

(2)

Suppose
\varphi
is a regular anisotropic form of dimension
2^{n}+1
. Then
Q^{\varphi}
is not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected. Indeed, in this case,
i_{0}(\varphi)=0
and
i_{1}(\varphi)=1
(see Corollary 1 in
[
11
]
). In particular, the real quadrics defined by

x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{2^{n}+1}^{2}=1

are not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected.

(3)

Suppose
\varphi=\langle 1,1,\dots,1\rangle
is a quadratic form of dimension
2^{n}+\ell
, where
2\leq\ell\leq 2^{n}
, and assume that
\varphi_{k}
is anisotropic. Then the associated quadric
Q^{\varphi}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected. Indeed, we have
i_{0}(\varphi)=0
, and by Corollary 1 in
[
11
]
,
i_{1}(\varphi)\leq\ell
. It is well known that a Pfister form is either anisotropic or totally isotropic (see Corollary 6.3 in
[
9
]
). Since
\varphi
is a subform of the
2^{n+1}
-dimensional Pfister form
\langle 1,\dots,1\rangle
, a standard dimension-counting argument shows that
i_{1}(\varphi)\geq\ell
, and hence
i_{1}(\varphi)=\ell\geq 2
. Thus,
Q^{\varphi}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected. Similar arguments apply to any Pfister neighbor.

Rethinking mass transfer: a unified semi-analytical framework for circular and eccentric binaries
A. Parkosidis, S. Toonen, E. Laplace, F. Dosopoulou

Abstract
Although mass transfer (MT) has been studied primarily in circular binaries, observations show that it also occurs in eccentric systems. We investigate orbital evolution during non-conservative MT in eccentric orbits, a process especially relevant for binaries containing compact objects (COs). We examine four angular momentum loss (AML) modes; Jeans, isotropic re-emission, orbital-AML and L 2 L_{2} mass loss – the latter is the most efficient AML mode. For fixed AML mode and accretion efficiency, orbital evolution is correlated: orbits either widen while becoming more eccentric, or shrink while circularizing. Jeans mode generally yields orbital widening and eccentricity pumping, whereas L 2 L_{2} mass loss typically leads to orbital shrinkage and eccentricity damping. Isotropic re-emission and orbital-AML show intermediate behavior. Adopting isotropic re-emission, we demonstrate that eccentric MT produces compact binaries that merge via gravitational waves (GW) within a Hubble time, whereas the same systems would instead merge during MT under traditional modeling. We further show that, in eccentric orbits, the gravitational potential at L 2 L_{2} becomes lower than at L 1 L_{1} across large range of mass ratios and eccentricities, naturally linking eccentricity to L 2 L_{2} mass loss. Since interacting binaries containing COs are frequently eccentric, L 2 L_{2} mass loss offers a new robust pathway to orbital tightening during eccentric MT, contributing to the formation rate of GW sources. This model can treat orbital evolution due to conservative and non-conservative MT in arbitrary eccentricities with applications ranging from MT on the main sequence to GW progenitors.

Specifying the orbital AML-parameter
\gamma

In the fast wind scenario, mass is lost directly from the donor as a fast, isotropic wind rather than via RLOF. The ejected material carries away the specific orbital AM of the donor, where
D_{\rm don}

6
Summary and Discussion

The predicted orbital evolution and final fate of binary systems vary substantially across different MT frameworks. In Sects.
4
and
5.2
, we compare the predictions of the GeMT framework with those from the
\delta
-function formalism
(
58
;
59
)
, the emt-model
(
16
;
17
)
, and the classical RLOF framework, all under fully non-conservative MT (
\beta=0
) assuming isotropic re-emission. To facilitate comparison, we refer to the already introduced key transitional mass ratios:
q_{\rm trans,a}
, which separates orbital shrinkage from widening, and
q_{\rm trans,e}
, which separates eccentricity damping from pumping. A summary of the key differences is outlined below.

1.

For GeMT-model under the point-mass approximation, both transitions occur at
q_{\rm trans,a}=q_{\rm trans,e}=1.28
, independent of eccentricity
e
. However, when accounting for extended bodies, the parameter space for orbital widening and eccentricity pumping broadens. Specifically, as eccentricity decreases, both thresholds shift to higher values; conversely, at higher eccentricities, they decrease (Figs.
8
and
9
).

2.

The
\delta
-function model, also exhibits decreasing
q_{\rm trans,a}
with increasing
e
, but yields lower thresholds overall. For instance, it predicts orbital widening for
q\leq 1.0
at
e=0.99
, and for
q\leq 1.28
at
e=0.0
. Eccentricity pumping is predicted for
q\lesssim 1.0
nearly independent of
e
. Finally, it predicts weaker evolution of both the semimajor axis and eccentricity compared to other frameworks (Figs.
8
and
9
).

3.

The non-conservative extension of the emt-model
(
17
)
predicts both eccentricity pumping and orbital widening for any mass ratio
q\leq 4
and eccentricity
e
. Moreover, it predicts stronger evolution in
a
and
e
compared to other frameworks (Figs.
8
and
9
).

4.

The classical RLOF framework assumes point masses, synchronous rotation, and instantaneous circularization. The latter yield more compact initial orbits and ensures circular orbits at the end of RLOF. Instantaneous circularization can lead to mergers in cases where the GeMT-model predicts compact and eccentric post-MT orbits (Fig.
13
).

In general, the non-conservative extension of the emt-model
(
17
)
is independent of the AML-parameter
\gamma
, and generally yields orbital widening and eccentricity growth. Specifically, for
\beta\lesssim 0.1
, the orbit widens and becomes more eccentric for any
0.1\leq q\leq 10
and
0.01\leq e\leq 0.99
, so results obtained with this framework
(
18
;
52
;
54
, e.g.,)
should be interpreted accordingly. The
\delta
-function model
(
58
;
59
)
idealizes MT as an impulsive burst localized at periastron, but hydrodynamical simulations show that even highly eccentric systems do not asymptotically approach the
\delta
-function approximation
(
5
)
. Moreover, for systems that initiate MT at low eccentricity, or systems that evolve through a stage with low eccentricity (as we expect them to do, e.g., Fig.
13
), alternative models are required at to prevent unphysical negative eccentricities. For example,
57

\delta

. However, enforcing

\delta
-function model is different, as the equations do not conserve orbital AM momentum in the limit of conservative MT (see Sect. 7.3 in
Paper I
). The GeMT framework addresses these issues by providing a unified treatment of conservative and non-conservative MT across arbitrary eccentricities.

Producing binaries that can merge within a Hubble time via GW emission typically requires orbital shrinkage during the MT phase

. Conventional models often assume instantaneous circularization or consider only circular orbits

, and adopt isotropic re-emission
(

as the fiducial AML mode. In the example presented in Sect.

we show that instantaneous circularization and isotropic re-emission result in a merger during RLOF (black line in Fig.

6.2
Occurrence of
L_{2}
Mass Loss

Classically,
L_{2}
outflow is discussed in the context of contact binaries,
(
62
;
44
;
45
;
20
, e.g.,)
where mass can escape once the critical equipotential surface corresponding to the
L_{2}
point is reached. In the context of MT,
L_{2}
mass loss is often associated with the onset of unstable MT as explored in
43
and in the hydrodynamic simulations of
34
;
33
. This association arises because the mechanism extracts orbital AM very efficiently due to the long lever arm of the
L_{2}
point, leading to rapid orbital shrinkage. Nevertheless, there is observational evidence that some systems can remain stable even after the onset of
L_{2}
mass loss; for example, the case of SS433 in our Galaxy
(
12
;
46
, see)
.

More recently,
31
investigated
L_{2}
mass loss in the context of the stable MT. They showed that, for mass transfer rates exceeding
\dot{M}_{\rm don}\gtrsim 10^{-4}
M
{}_{\odot}\;yr^{-1}
, it can be energetically favorable for mass to escape via the
L_{2}
point rather than through a fast, super-Eddington wind. In this work, we show that for donors synchronous with the orbital velocity at periapsis (i.e.,
f_{\rm don}=1
) and
e\gtrsim 0.2
, the gravitational potential at
L_{2}
becomes lower than at
L_{1}
across the full range of mass ratios
q
. For supersynchronous donors, this condition holds for all eccentricities, whereas for subsynchronous donors, it occurs only at high eccentricities (see Fig.
15
). As a result, matter flowing toward the accretor may escape through the
L_{2}
point rather than being captured, making MT in eccentric binaries inherently non-conservative due to the evolving potential geometry. Supporting this,
28
performed hydrodynamical simulations of MT in orbits with
e=0.25
and found
L_{2}
mass loss for both mass ratios
q\sim 1
and
q\sim 1.7
.

6.3
Implications of
L_{2}
Mass Loss

L_{2}
mass loss may be connected to the observed circumbinary (CB) discs around post-RGB and post-AGB systems. Interactions between the binary and the disc have been proposed as the driver of the observed eccentricities in these systems. However,
41
show that binary–disc interactions alone cannot account for the observed eccentric orbits in post-RGB and post-AGB binaries. In fact, such interactions preferentially generate high eccentricities at short orbital periods
(
8
;
70
;
7
)
, in contradiction with observations
(
61
)
. In
Paper I
we show that eccentric RLOF itself serves as eccentricity pumping mechanism yielding higher eccentricities at longer orbital periods in agreement with observations. Therefore, since increasing eccentricity makes
L_{2}
mass loss more likely, eccentric RLOF potentially leads to the formation of the CB disc in the first place.

The correlation between eccentricity and
L_{2}
mass loss has important implications also for the formation of GW sources. Standard models adopt isotropic re-emission as the fiducial AML mode
(
14
;
67
;
57
, e.g.,)
. However,
L_{2}
mass loss is expected to contribute in the evolution of the orbital AM (
6.2
) and is a far more efficient AML mechanism (see Figs.
3
,
10
and
12
). For instance, Fig.
10
demonstrates that even
<10\%
mass loss via
L_{2}
(gray line) results in a tighter post-MT binary than the case of 100% mass loss through isotropic re-emission (orange line). Adopting the resulting orbital parameters, we find
t_{\rm merger}=0.8
Gry or
t_{\rm merger}\approx 0.04
t
Hubble
. We note that in real systems, it is not necessary that a single AML mechanism operates; instead, a combination of channels might contribute resulting in more complicated physical picture. Nevertheless, many binary systems hosting COs are observed with significant eccentricities
(
61
;
10
, e.g.,)
and even interacting while being eccentric
(
48
;
56
, e.g.,)
, making
L_{2}
mass loss an important, and often overlooked, contributor to orbital AM evolution.

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we systematically explored mass transfer in eccentric binaries. We extended the General Mass Transfer (GeMT) model introduced in
Paper I
to explore the effects of stable, non-conservative mass transfer and its impact on orbital evolution. Since our model builds upon the foundation established in
Paper I
, the same limitations and conclusions also apply here. In addition, we presented the first systematic comparison between eccentric mass transfer frameworks; GeMT is the only framework to date that models self consistently orbital evolution in circular and eccentric orbits under conservative and non-conservative mass transfer, overcoming shortcomings of prior frameworks (Sect.
6
).

In the limit of a quasi-static gravitational potential, we introduced the Global-
L_{2}
fit, the first prescription for the position of the Lagrangian
L_{2}
point (defined here as the Lagrangian point located behind the accretor irrespective of stellar masses) at periapsis, in units of the instantaneous distance between the two stars for asynchronous and eccentric semi-detached binaries (Appendix
B
). This fit was used to quantifiy the angular momentum loss parameter
\gamma
(i.e., the efficiency of angular momentum extraction during non-conservative mass transfer) for
L_{2}
mass loss in circular and eccentric orbits. The specific angular momentum carried away during
L_{2}
mass loss is highest for circular orbits and decreases with increasing eccentricity (Fig.
3
). Furthermore, increasingly supersynchronous donors (i.e., higher
f_{\rm don}
) result in lower specific angular momentum carried away by the escaping mass (i.e., lower
\gamma
); a trend especially pronounced for systems with less massive donors (
q<1
, Fig.
3
)

We explored orbital evolution under four angular momentum loss modes: (1) Jeans, (2) isotropic re-emission, (3) orbital angular momentum loss, and (4)
L_{2}
mass loss for circular and eccentric orbits. Binary evolution in eccentric orbits differs fundamentally from that in circular systems. Phase-dependent RLOF drives eccentricity evolution, with higher
e
producing stronger
\dot{a}
at fixed
q
(see Figs.
4
,
5
). For a given angular momentum loss mode and accretion efficiency
\beta
, the semimajor axis and eccentricity evolve in a correlated way: orbits either widen while becoming more eccentric, or shrink while circularizing (Figs.
5
,
6
). Despite this correlation, different angular momentum loss modes predict substantially different orbital evolution. In the limit of point masses and fully non-conservative mass transfer (
\beta=0
), the GeMT-model yields

1.

Jeans mode
: orbits generally widen and eccentricity increase for nearly all
q
and
e
.

2.

Isotropic re-emission
: orbits shrink and circularize when
q>1.28
, but widen and become more eccentric when
q<1.28
, independent of
e
.

3.

Orbital-AML mode
: qualitatively similar to isotropic re-emission, but with a higher critical mass ratio (
q_{\rm trans,a}=q_{\rm trans,e}=2.0
) and weaker evolution of
a
and
e
for
q>2.0
.

4.

L_{2}
mass loss
: orbits shrink and circularize for
q\gtrsim 0.3
, but widen and become more eccentric when
q\lesssim 0.3
and
e\gtrsim 0.2
. Overall,
L_{2}
mass loss is by far the most efficient mode at extracting orbital angular momentum across most mass ratios for both circular and eccentric orbits.

In practice, multiple angular momentum loss channels may operate simultaneously; exploring such mixed modes is left for future work.

We demonstrated that eccentric RLOF is an important process for the formation of gravitational-wave sources. By dropping the assumption of instantaneous circularization at the onset of RLOF and modeling orbital evolution self consistently we showed that systems which merge during mass transfer under the classical RLOF framework, instead result in compact orbits that produce gravitational-wave sources within a Hubble time (Sect.
6
). Moreover, we showed that in eccentric orbits, the potential at
L_{2}
is lower than at
L_{1}
across a wide region of the parameter space (Fig.
15
), thus matter flowing toward the accretor may be energetic enough to escape through
L_{2}
. Quantifying the impact of
L_{2}
mass loss in eccentric orbits, we showed that this angular momentum loss channel can contribute significantly to orbital shrinkage with implications for the formation of gravitational-wave sources. Finally, the correlation between eccentricity and systemic mass loss through
L_{2}
indicates a possible relation to circumbinary discs observed around eccentric post-RGB and post-AGB binaries (Sect.
6.3
).

In summary, the GeMT framework resolves shortcomings of prior frameworks (Sect.
6
). The predicted orbital evolution can deviate substantially from classical expectations when dropping the assumption of instantaneous circularization. Incorporating the GeMT-model into binary evolution and population synthesis codes would enable more accurate and self-consistent tracking of the orbital evolution for both circular and eccentric systems under conservative and non-conservative mass transfer, thereby refining our understanding of stable mass transfer in shaping the evolution of interacting binaries with important implications for the formation of gravitational-wave sources.

Potential Height at the
L_{2}
Lagrangian point in Eccentric Orbits

Traditionally, the locations of the Lagrangian points
L_{1}-L_{5}
are defined under the assumption of circular orbits with synchronously rotating component stars. However, in eccentric binaries, the orbital angular velocity varies with time, making synchronous rotation throughout the orbit impossible. The donor’s asynchronous rotation leads to time-dependent tidal forces from the companion and a corresponding time-varying gravitational potential
V
. Following
60
, the quasi-static potential is written as

V=X-\frac{q}{X}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{r^{2}-2X+1}}-\frac{1}{2}(X^{2}+Y^{2})(1+q)\mathcal{A}(f_{\rm don},e,\mathcal{E}),

(9)

where
X
and
Y
are the Cartesian coordinates in the plane of the orbit, expressed in units of the instantaneous orbital separation (i.e.,
X\equiv X/r
and
Y\equiv Y/r
),
and

\mathcal{A}(f_{\rm don},e,\mathcal{E})=f_{\rm don}^{2}\frac{1+e}{(1-e)^{3}}\biggl(\frac{r}{a}\biggr)^{3},

(10)

quantifies the deviation of the donor’s spin velocity from the orbital angular velocity at periapsis as a function of the eccentricity
e
and eccentric anomaly
\mathcal{E}
. Note that we choose the eccentric anomaly to express
\mathcal{A}(f_{\rm don},e,\mathcal{E})
for consistency with
Paper I
, however other angle parameters, such as true anomaly, are equally valid.

60
showed that the potential can be treated as quasi-static, provided the donor’s dynamical timescale is much shorter than both the orbital and rotational timescales—a condition known as the first approximation
(
30
)
. Under this approximation, the donor’s structure is assumed to adjust instantaneously to the changing potential throughout the orbit. This allows for the determination of the Lagrangian points
L_{1}-L_{5}
by solving
\nabla V=0
with respect to the coordinates
X
and
Y
. Throughout this work, we define the Lagrangian point located behind the accretor as
L_{2}
and the one behind the donor as
L_{3}
, irrespective of the stellar masses.

L_{1}

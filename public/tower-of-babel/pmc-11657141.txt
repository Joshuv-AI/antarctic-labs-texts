Development and validation of a radiomics-based nomogram for predicting pathological grade of upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma
Authors: Yanghuang Zheng, Hongjin Shi, Shi Fu, Haifeng Wang, Xin Li, Zhi Li, Bing Hai, Jinsong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUpper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC) is a rare and highly aggressive malignancy characterized by poor prognosis, making the accurate identification of high-grade (HG) UTUC essential for subsequent treatment strategies. This study aims to develop and validate a nomogram model using computed tomography urography (CTU) images to predict HG UTUC.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted to include patients with UTUC who underwent radical nephroureterectomy and received a CTU examination prior to surgery. In the CTU images, tumor lesions located in the renal calyces, renal pelvis and ureter were segmented, and radiomics features from the unenhanced, medullary, and excretory phases were extracted. The maximum relevance minimum redundancy algorithm, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator, and various machine learning (ML) algorithms—including random forest, support vector machine, and eXtreme gradient boosting—were employed to select radiomics features and calculate radiomics scores. Logistic regression (LR) analysis was performed to identify the independent influencing factors of clinical baseline characteristics. Multiple datasets of radiomics features were constructed by integrating single-phase radiomics features with the most significant independent factor. Both LR and ML algorithms were utilized to develop predictive models. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC values), accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity were assessed for model performance evaluation. Decision curve analysis was conducted to evaluate the clinical net benefits.ResultsA total of 167 patients were enrolled in this study. Among them, 56 were diagnosed with low-grade UTUC (including papillary urothelial neoplasms with low malignant potential and low-grade urothelial carcinoma) as confirmed by postoperative pathological examination results, and 111 were of HG. These patients were randomly allocated to the training set and the validation set at a ratio of 7:3. The training set comprised 116 patients with a mean age of 63.5 ± 9.38 years and 38 males. The validation set comprised 51 patients with a mean age of 65.6 ± 11.1 years and 18 males. Hydronephrosis was identified as the most significant independent factor in the clinical baseline features. Models that include mixed-phase development achieve better performance compared to models that rely simply on single-phase development. The nomogram model had excellent predictive ability for HG UTUC, with AUC values of 0.844 and an accuracy of 0.793 in the validation sets. The nomogram model can enhance accuracy by 14.1% (79.3% vs. 65.2%) and sensitivity by 32.8% (93.2% vs. 60.4%) compared to urinary cytology.ConclusionsThis study developed a nomogram model, which significantly improved the diagnostic ability for HG UTUC compared to urinary cytology. Furthermore, the results of the decision curve analysis showed that the model had a net benefit and could provide a non-invasive and potentially diagnostic reference tool for HG UTUC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13325-z.Keywords: Carcinoma, Nomogram, Pathological grade, Radiomics, Urinary tract

Background

Upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC) is a rare malignancy that primarily arises in the renal pelvis, renal calyces, and ureter, accounting for only 5–10% of all urothelial carcinomas [1]. The annual incidence rate in Western countries is approximately two cases per 100,000 population [2]. The prevalence of UTUC in urothelial carcinoma is relatively low, nevertheless, it exhibits a greater level of invasiveness and mortality in comparison to other urogenital malignancies [3]. Upon initial diagnosis of UTUC, approximately 60% of patients have aggressive disease, while 30% exhibit metastatic disease [4]. The European Association of Urology includes the pathological grade of UTUC as one of the indicators in their risk stratification for UTUC [5]. A previous study has shown a significant correlation between the pathological grade of UTUC and the overall survival rate of patients [6]. Patients with a higher pathological grade of UTUC have a poorer prognosis [7]. The precise preoperative identification of UTUC pathological grade is crucial for the formulation of treatment strategies and for evaluating patient prognosis.

At present, the diagnostic approaches for UTUC encompass non-invasive radiological examinations, urinary cytology, as well as invasive ones (such as diagnostic ureteroscopy (URS) and/or biopsy, URS-assisted selective urinary cytology), etc. Radiological imaging is an essential component in the diagnosis, staging, and follow-up of patients, complementing urinary cytology and diagnostic URS [8]. Computed tomography urography (CTU) is the preferred diagnostic method for suspected upper urinary tract lesions. While the sensitivity and specificity of CTU in diagnosing UTUC are 92% and 95% respectively, visually identifying the pathological grade of tumors remains a challenge [9]. However, the accuracy of identifying tumor grade in UTUC using urinary cytology and URS biopsy is limited. A Study has shown that urinary cytology has a sensitivity of 56% for high-grade (HG) UTUC [10]. A meta-analysis conducted by Subiela et al. showed that there was 34% undergrading compared to the final surgical specimen pathological results based on the biopsy results obtained by ureteroscope [11]. Additionally, during a ureteroscope biopsy, there may be limitations on the size of the biopsied tissue, a lack of papillary projections, impression artifacts from pressure marks, and distorted tissue structures that can affect the pathologist’s final diagnosis [12]. As for prognosis, several studies have shown that the use of ureteroscope examination and ureteroscope biopsy is associated with a significant increase in the risk of bladder recurrence after surgery [13–15].

Radiomics technology leverages digital data to quantify tumor heterogeneity and assess tumor characteristics and prognosis in a non-invasive manner [16]. Several studies have shown that radiomics technology is effective in distinguishing the pathological grade of tumors [17, 18].

In our previous study, we developed a diagnostic model using the random forest machine learning (ML) algorithm based on CTU for the prediction of HG UTUC. This model achieved AUC values of 0.914 in its validation set [19]. It can reduce unnecessary invasive procedures to some extent and provide a reference value for patients with suspected HG UTUC who attempt to lower the pathological grade through neoadjuvant therapy. Despite its excellent diagnostic capabilities, this model lacks intuitiveness, ease of understanding, and clinical practicality.

This study aims to extract radiological features from CTU images and construct a nomogram model to predict the diagnostic performance of HG UTUC, especially compared with urinary cytology. At the same time, it aims to provide reference value for clinical diagnosis, evaluation of clinical net benefits, and guidance for subsequent treatment.

Methods

Patients characteristics

The training and validation sets for the construction of a nomogram model were retrospectively selected from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University diagnosed with UTUC between January 2017 and December 2023. The inclusion criteria consisted of the following: (1) Patients who have been diagnosed with UTUC based on pathological examination after undergoing radical nephroureterectomy (RNU); (2) CTU images taken within 1 month before RNU and without any intervention measures; (3) A minimum of three urinary cytology exams completed within 2 weeks before the surgery. The exclusion criteria consisted of the following: (1) Concurrent presence of additional cancers; (2) Administration of neoadjuvant treatment before surgery; (3) Difficulty in obtaining CTU images; (4) Inability to delineate the tumor lesions accurately; (5) Lack of CTU inspection prior to the surgical procedure.

A combined total of 167 patients who fulfilled the specified criteria for inclusion and exclusion were included in both the training set and validation set (Fig. 1). The clinical baseline characteristics gathered encompassed the patient’s T-stage, age, gender, presence of hydronephrosis, urinary cytology, maximal tumor diameter, tumor side, and tumor type. In cases where the patient has multiple tumors, only the tumor with the largest cross-sectional area should be focused on.

The patient recruitment pathway

Urinary cytology and UTUC pathology classification

Every patient got a minimum of three urinary cytology exams within the two weeks before to the procedure. The methods and findings of urinary cytology are explained in Appendix A. The urinary cytology result was categorized based on the Paris System into two groups: negative (Paris 1–3, 6) and positive (Paris 4, 5). The pathological grade was assessed using the WHO 2004/2016 categorization system, which includes papillary urothelial neoplasms with low malignant potential, low-grade (LG) urothelial carcinoma, and HG urothelial carcinoma. The pathological grades were classified into two categories: LG (including papillary urothelial neoplasms with low malignant potential and low-grade urothelial carcinoma) and HG (high-grade urothelial carcinoma). The results of urinary cytology and histopathological examination were independently reviewed by two experienced pathologists with more than 10 years of work experience, who were unaware of the results, and the inconsistent results were decided by a third pathologist with the same qualifications.

The radiomics workflow

The procedure for obtaining CTU images is described in Appendix B, while the radiomics workflow is illustrated in Fig. 2.

The procedure of radiomics workflow

Segmentation of the region of interest

The two-dimensional regions of interest (2D-ROI) with the largest cross-sectional area of the tumor were delineated on the CTU images and recorded the maximal tumor diameter at three phases, namely the unenhanced phase, the medullary phase, and the excretory phase, respectively, by using 3D Slicer software (version 5.0.3; www.slicer.org). To ensure precise identification of tumor boundaries, a highly experienced radiologist specializing in urogenital imaging manually conducted the segmentation of each lesion. The 2D-ROI is positioned 1 mm away from the edge of the tumor. Tumor segmentation on unenhanced phase images was conducted using the excreted phase images as a basis.

Imaging feature extraction

The Radiomics module (version 3.0.1), of the 3D Slicer software was utilized to extract imaging features from the 2D-ROI. The CTU image was resampled using spline interpolation to get isotropic voxels of 3 × 3 × 3 mm3. The Hounsfield units of images were converted to a precise value of 25 Hounsfield units. The extracted imaging features consist of various types of features. These include first-order features (18 features), texture features such as gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) (24 features), gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM) (14 features), gray-level runlength matrix (GLRLM) (16 features), gray-level size-zone matrix (GLSZM) (16 features), neighboring gray-tone difference matrix (NGDTM) (5 features), and wavelet features (including L-H-H, H-H-H, L-L-H, H-L-H, L-H-L, H- H-L, L-L-L, and H-L-L).

Imaging feature selection

To assess the intraobserver reproducibility, a random sample of 30 patients underwent a repeat segment ROI segmentation after a one-month interval. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was employed to gauge the intra-observer reliability of feature extraction. Features with ICC values > 0.75 were considered to be of good quality and relevance, while those with ICC values < 0.75 were excluded [20, 21]. The remaining features were standardized using a Z-score. The standardized features were subsequently subjected to further selection using the Max-Relevance and Min-Redundancy (mRMR) analysis and the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithms. In addition, ML algorithms like random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM), and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (xGBoost) were used to select the standardized features and choose the most optimum ones. Following that, the clinical baseline characteristics were analyzed using univariate logistic regression (LR) and multivariate LR to identify the independent influencing factors and determine the most influential ones.

Construction of multiple feature datasets based on each phase in CTU

The unenhanced phase, medullary phase, excretory phase, and mixed-phase each contributed to the construction of six distinct feature data sets. First, clinical independent influencing factors were constructed as the clinical-character dataset. Second, the feature dataset was created through the selection of imaging features with ICC value > 0.75 and characteristics filtered through mRMR analysis and the LASSO algorithm. Third, the most influential independent factor and the feature dataset were combined to create the clinical-feature dataset. Four, the radiomics score dataset was generated by computing the radiomics score based on the feature dataset. Five, the clinical-radiomics dataset was constructed by integrating the most influential independent factor with the radiomics score dataset. Six, the ML-feature dataset was created through the combination of the most representative imaging features with ICC value > 0.75 after standardization selected by three machine learning algorithms, respectively, and the most influential independent factor. Model performance evaluation and nomogram construction

The performance of prediction models based on multiple feature datasets was compared for each phase, and the model with the best performance was selected to construct a nomogram. The diagnostic accuracy of the prediction model was assessed using metrics such as the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC values), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy. Additionally, calibration curves were used to evaluate how well the prediction model fit observed data, while decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to assess clinical utility.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were conducted using R Studio (version 4.2.2). The data, which followed a normal distribution, was represented as the mean plus or minus the standard deviation (x ± s). Group comparisons were evaluated using the independent-sample t-test. Data that did not follow a normal distribution were represented by the median value together with the upper and lower quartiles. Group comparisons were conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were described as proportions or percentages (%) and compared between groups using either the chi-square test (χ2) or Fisher’s exact test. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test was employed to assess the adequacy of the model’s fit, where a p-value > 0.05 was deemed satisfactory. Statistical significance was determined by setting the threshold at a p-value < 0.05. This retrospective study received approval from The Institutional Review Board of the Hospital, and informed consent was exempted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration.

Results

Patient characteristics

This study enrolled a total of 167 patients with UTUC, who were randomly divided into two groups: the training set (n = 116) and the validation set (n = 51), with a ratio of 7:3. Out of the total patients, there were 56 males and 111 females, with an average age of 64.2 ± 9.25 years. The pathological examination results after RNU showed that 56 cases (33.5%) were LG UTUC and 111 cases (66.5%) were HG UTUC. The results of urinary cytology indicated that 86 cases (51.50%) were LG (negative) and 81 cases (48.50%) were high-grade (positive). The training set consisted of 42 patients (36.2%) with LG pathology and 74 patients (63.8%) with HG pathology. In contrast, the validation set included 14 patients (27.5%) with LG pathology and 37 patients (72.5%) with HG pathology. The 2D-ROI values can be obtained directly from the 3D Slicer software. The clinical baseline characteristics T-stage, hydronephrosis, urinary cytology, and maximal tumor diameter exhibited statistically significant differences between LG UTUC and HG UTUC in both the training set and the validation set, with a p-value < 0.05. Nevertheless, there were no statistically significant variations in any clinical baseline features between these two groups (all p-values > 0.05) (Table 1). The radiomics scores obtained from the mixed-phase characteristics exhibited a statistically significant distinction between LG and HG UTUC in both the training and validation sets (both p-values < 0.05) (Fig. 3).

The diagnostic performance of urinary cytology

Taking the pathological examination results after RNU as the gold standard, the preoperative urinary cytology examination indicated that 30 patients were misdiagnosed as having LG UTUC. The diagnostic accuracy rate was 65.2%, the sensitivity was 0.604, the specificity was 0.750, the positive predictive value was 0.827, and the negative predictive value was 0.488. For specific details, refer to Appendix C.

Clinical baseline characteristics and imaging features

Univariate and multivariate LR analysis results showed that hydronephrosis, urinary cytology, and maximum tumor diameter were the independent influencing factors for predicting HG UTUC, and hydronephrosis was the most influential independent factor (odds ratio, (OR) = 6.89, p-value = 0.001) (Table 1).

Imaging features

For each patient, a total of 2,511 features have been extracted from the unenhanced, medullary, and excretory phase images. This resulted in 837 characteristics being recovered from each phase. A total of 245 features with ICC values more than 0.75 were collected from the unenhanced phase, 355 from the medullary phase, 479 from the excretory phase, and 1192 from the mixed phase (Appendix D). After undergoing screening by mRMR and LASSO algorithms, a total of 13 features were chosen from the unenhanced phase, 10 from the medullary phase, 13 from the excretory phase, and 11 from the mixed phase (Fig. 4). Furthermore, SVM, RF, and XGBoost ML algorithms were employed to rescreen the 1192 features from the mixed-phase, resulting in the identification of the three most important features (Appendix E). The imaging features chosen from all phases are described in detail in Appendix F.

Discussion

In this study, we constructed 21 prediction models were developed using imaging features extracted from three phases of CTU images. Subsequently, the model with the most superior performance was chosen to develop a nomogram for accurately predicting HG UTUC. The results demonstrated that all prediction models exhibited commendable classification performance. Notably, the nomogram model, which utilized an ML-feature dataset showcased exceptional predictive capabilities and yielded substantial net benefits. Furthermore, it achieved the highest AUC values in both the training set (0.851; 95%CI 0.738–0.965) and the validation set (0.844; 95%CI 0.766–0.922). Compared with urinary cytology results, the model has higher accuracy and sensitivity. This noninvasive model can serve as an assistant tool for preoperative prediction of HG UTUC, thereby providing valuable insights for formulating subsequent treatment strategies.

Imaging examinations currently play an indispensable role in the identification of occupying lesions within the upper urinary tract [8]. The magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) technique possesses exceptional high-resolution capabilities for detecting abnormalities in soft tissues. Numerous studies have demonstrated that the extraction of imaging features extracted from MRI images can effectively differentiate the pathological grades of tumors [23, 24]. In comparison to preoperative CTU, the diagnostic accuracy of UTUC using MRI is relatively lower. Moreover, MRI presents some limitations, including extended scanning duration, contraindications for certain patients, and a relatively high cost. Therefore, not all patients with UTUC are suitable candidates for an MRI examination. While CTU exhibits high diagnostic sensitivity and specificity in diagnosing UTUC, it still lacks the ability to accurately determine tumor grade [25]. The application of radiomics technology has effectively addressed this challenge by capturing digital information within tumors that is imperceptible to the naked eye [26]. Mamman et al. demonstrated that texture features extracted from CT scans were able to distinguish the pathological grade of UTUC and observed a notable distinction between LG and HG UTUC (P = 0.03) [27]. The study conducted by Zhang et al. demonstrated a strong predictive performance of bladder cancer pathological grade through CT radiomics, with AUC values of 0.860 (95%CI 0.742–0.979) and a diagnostic accuracy of 0.838 in the validation set [28]. The findings of these studies illustrate the viability of CT-based radiomics in the assessment of pathological grade. The texture features extracted in the above study were limited to unenhanced and enhanced CT images, thus failing to fully exploit all the available information in CT imaging data. Mitsuru et al. showed that the combined diagnostic performance of the nephrographic phase and excretory phase in detecting UTUC was superior to that of the nephrographic phase alone [29]. In this study, we conducted tumor segmentation based on excretory phase images to ensure precise identification of the tumor area and extraction of more representative imaging features. The use of diverse ML algorithms for feature selection and model construction is increasingly prevalent in contemporary research studies [30, 31]. Unfortunately, limited research has been conducted utilizing this approach to predict the pathological grade of UTUC. Therefore, our objective is to develop a nomogram model that can effectively predict HG UTUC by comprehensively extracting CTU imaging features using both traditional and ML algorithms, in combination with clinical baseline characteristics.

In terms of algorithms, the mRMR algorithm was utilized to comprehensively consider the correlation between features during the feature selection process, resulting in the generation of radiomics features with reduced redundancy and increased correlation [32]. Additionally, the LASSO algorithms was employed to effectively address the collinearity issue in high-dimensional and low-sample data, as well as to resolve the subgroup selection problem [33]. The comparison of model performance reveals that the prediction model utilizing mixed-phase features outperforms the one relying solely on single-phase features on the validation set. Similarly to our findings, Feng et al. developed a multi-phase radiomics model utilizing CTU, which demonstrated exceptional performance in discriminating between benign and malignant kidney tumors [34]. Previous studies have shown a significant correlation between the presence of hydronephrosis and the diagnosis of ureteral tumors (P = 0.0307) [35]. The presence of hydronephrosis emerged as the most influential independent factor in the clinical baseline characteristics for predicting HG UTUC in our study. In addition, the prediction performance of the model can be improved by constructing a multi-radiomics model by combining clinical baseline features and radiomics features. XIONG et al. showed that combining clinical features with radiomics could improve the predictive performance and risk net benefit of diagnostic models for bladder cancer [36]. Ying et al. improved the diagnostic performance of baseline clinical features for colorectal cancer by establishing a clinical-radiomics model [37]. While the subjects in the aforementioned studies differed from ours, the research methodologies employed were similar. Radiomics scores are typically computed to construct a nomogram for disease diagnosis or for prognostic risk assessment. In the development of various radiomics feature datasets, the diagnostic performance of our model was significantly enhanced by calculating the imaging features as radiomics scores. Furthermore, integrating clinical baseline characteristics with radiomics scores also improved the diagnostic performance of our model.

Compared to traditional linear classifiers, models based on ML algorithms exhibit superior predictive capabilities. Ye et al. selected the best model for predicting the pathological grade of pancreatic endocrine tumors by constructing and comparing a variety of ML models, with AUC values of 0.779 [38]. Deng et al. constructed a predictive model for bladder cancer pathological grade using six ML algorithms. The best-performing model on the validation set had AUC valuess of 0.84 [39]. But, the analysis of ML algorithm results presents a challenging situation. Although the diagnostic performance of the model holds importance, clinical practicality carries greater values. The current status quo reveals that people are paying escalating attention to the discrimination capacity of the developed models. Nevertheless, this has failed to bring about actual gains for clinical decision-making and has disregarded their value in clinical applications. Even though ML algorithms can mine digital information in CT images more effectively and the predictive models developed possess high AUC values, the visualization of the degree of feature contribution in ML models is less intuitive than that in traditional nomograms, and few studies have conducted clinical decision-making analyses on the models developed by machine learning algorithms. Constructing a nomogram and analyzing the decision curve can provide a more intuitive reflection of the overall net benefit for patients [40].

Regarding the construction of predictive models, we developed a dynamic nomogram model that combined radiomics features and clinical baseline features to directly and objectively evaluate the contribution of each feature in predicting HG outcomes. Furthermore, the model showed good performance on the decision-making benefit curve. In the validation set, the developed model achieved an accuracy of 0.793, a sensitivity of 0.932, and outperformed existing single urinary cytology, ureteroscope, and ureteroscope biopsy for the prediction of HG UTUC. Although the Paris System 2.0 improved the detection ability of HG for urinary cytology results, the discriminative method still has problems of individual subjective differences and reader work experience [41]. Malshy et al. conducted a study comparing the results of ureteroscope biopsy with the pathological examination findings after RNU, and found that the accuracy of ureteroscope biopsy in correctly differentiating pathological grades was 65% [42]. The study conducted by Skolariko et al. showed that the sensitivity of ureteroscopic urinary cytology in diagnosing the pathological grade of UTUC was 0.690 [43]. Consistent with our diagnostic findings, with a sensitivity of 0.604 for urinary cytology. While the combination of ureteroscope biopsy and urinary cytology can enhance the sensitivity and specificity of HG, the postoperative complications such as urinary tract infection, sepsis, and ureteral stricture cannot be ignored [44]. Unfortunately, our model has some limitations, with a specificity of only 0.548, which may not be able to exclude HG UTUC. Moreover, overestimating HG UTUC may result in actual LG UTUC patients losing unique individualized treatment. Therefore, the model we have developed is not yet able to replace histopathological examination.

The nomogram model employed ML algorithms to extract the three most significant imaging features, namely “Variance” derived from first-order statistical features, and GLRLM and GLCM features obtained from wavelet transform. The feature “Variance” is utilized to characterize the distribution of the mean gray intensity within the region of interest. This particular feature was screened, possibly because of a potential correlation between tumor tissue and contrast agent absorption during excretion. The texture feature of the GlCM evaluates the textural characteristics of an image through the analysis of the spatial arrangement statistics of pixel intensity [45]. Several studies have provided evidence for the importance of GLCM features in the evaluation of tumor heterogeneity. For example, the GLCM “correlation” has been utilized in the prediction of renal cell carcinoma with tumor thrombus [46], and the GLCM “clustershade” has been employed in prognosticating the pathological grade of pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors and the prognosis of gastrointestinal stromal tumors [38, 47]. In our study, we found a potential association between the GLCM “SumSquares” and the prediction of HG UTUC, primarily through the assessment of the distribution of neighboring intensity levels on the GLCM in relation to the mean intensity level. An association between GLRLM and acute pulmonary thromboembolism is potentially present [48]. Moreover, through the utilization of the conventional linear algorithm for extracting imaging features, it was observed that the extracted texture features constituted the majority of radiomics features. These texture features have the capacity to partially reflect the pathological grade of UTUC tumors.

There are some limitations in this study. Firstly, as a retrospective study, the influence of selection bias cannot be completely excluded. Secondly, the sample size used is relatively small. Therefore, large-scale samples are needed to improve the accuracy of the model. Thirdly, there may be potential influences in image acquisition mode, reconstruction parameters, tumor segmentation method, feature selection algorithm, and model construction method, and it is difficult to reproduce the results. Finally, as this study is only a single-center study, multi-center data need to be used for external verification to increase the reliability of the nomogram developed.

Conclusions

Combining radiomics scores with clinical independent influencing factors can improve the predictive performance of the model. The nomogram model developed based on the mixed phases radiomics feature dataset has better diagnostic sensitivity and accuracy than urinary cytology, which can be used as a reference tool for preoperative pathological grading assessment of patients. However, lower specificity limits its ability to replace postoperative tissue pathological examination.

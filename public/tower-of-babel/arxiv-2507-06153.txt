A Homothetic Gauge Theory
Fereidoun Sabetghadam

Abstract
While the principle of local gauge invariance forms the bedrock of modern physics, the symmetry of scale invariance has traditionally been treated on a different footing. This paper develops a homothetic gauge theory (HGT) that elevates local scale transformations to a genuine gauge symmetry, placing them within the same geometric framework as conventional interactions. We introduce a novel mathematical structure based on an extended vector bundle that linearizes the action of local scaling, allowing for the definition of a homothetic connection and a corresponding gauge-covariant field strength. When this formalism is applied to classical U(1) electromagnetism, the theory yields a generalized field-strength tensor that naturally incorporates both the standard Maxwell field and a new scalar partner field governing the local scale. The resulting dynamics, including modified conservation laws and wave equations, are shown to be fully consistent. As a significant physical application, this framework provides a compelling resolution to the classical problem of the infinite self-energy of a point charge. By modeling the source as a penalized Dirichlet boundary condition on a shrinking interface, we construct a non-singular electrostatic potential, thereby removing the divergence in a natural, geometric manner without introducing ad-hoc regularization schemes. The emergence of penalty terms due to essentially geometric transformations promises a physically meaningful theory, and encourages to re-examining the role of scale invariance in other areas of classical and quantum field theory.



Keywords:
homothetic gauge theory, scale invariance, conformal symmetry, gauge field, electromagnetic self-energy, geometric field theory, vector bundles, differential geometry

Introduction

At the heart of modern theoretical physics lies the principle of local gauge invariance, a powerful idea that dictates the fundamental interactions of nature by elevating global symmetries to local, spacetime-dependent ones
[
8
]
. One of the most well-established gauge theories in physics is classical electromagnetism, formulated as a
U(1)
gauge theory. Its mathematical foundation rests on the geometry of principal bundles and connections, offering a prototypical example of how gauge symmetry can encode physical interactions
[
13
]
. By extending this geometric framework to incorporate local scale transformations—homotheties—we aim to generalize the notion of gauge invariance itself. Such a generalization offers novel geometric and conceptual reinterpretations of gauge structure, aligning with recent insights into the foundations of gauge symmetries and their reformulations
[
10
]
.

The paper establishes a new
homothetic gauge theory
where the gauge freedom corresponds to local changes of scale. The central innovation is the linear structure endowed upon scale transformations, which naturally integrates into the standard fiber bundle formalism. It occurs by embedding the tangent and cotangent bundles in the doubled bundles, anchored by a fixed but arbitrary reference vector field. It transforms the non-linear action of scaling into a linear matrix operation on the fibers of these extended bundles.

This linearization permits the definition of an infinite-dimensional Lie group of fiber-wise homothetic transformations, which serves as the gauge group of the theory. We then proceed to build the essential machinery of a gauge theory upon this foundation: a compatible connection, a covariant derivative that respects the homothetic gauge symmetry, and an associated field-strength (curvature) tensor.

In the paper, the Weyl integrable geometry is obtained as a special case of the general formulation, and it is shown that a homothetic cohomology is definable consistent with this very conformal geometry, resulting in a generalized de Rham complex. When applied to the context of electromagnetism, the theory yields a generalization of Maxwell’s equations. These ”homothetic Maxwell equations” feature new interaction terms coupling the electromagnetic field to the gradient of the scalar gauge field that parameterizes the local scale.

Furthermore, the theory offers a novel and compelling resolution to one of the most persistent problems in classical field theory: the infinite self-energy of a point charge. By formulating the Laplace and Poisson equations within this homothetic framework, we demonstrate that a point source can be modeled as a non-singular object. The divergence is regularized not by altering the fundamental laws of electrodynamics at short distances, but by redefining the nature of a source as a boundary condition on an infinitesimal, penalized interface. This geometric regularization emerges naturally from the theory without ad hoc modifications.

While the use of doubled geometries and extended bundles is not new—notably appearing in string theory (e.g., double field theory)
[
15
]
and generalized geometry
[
12
]
—our focus on homotheties as a gauge symmetry sets this work apart. Additionally, conformal gauge theories
[
19
]
often emphasize angle-preserving transformations, whereas we highlight scale-preserving yet non-isometric maps. The proposed framework thus contributes a unique mathematical structure to the study of spacetime symmetries and potential extensions of general relativity and gauge theory.

The present work was initially began as a penalty method, a mathematical technique aiming to impose the solid boundary on the Navier–Stokes equations via controlling the dynamics of the equations
[
26
,
1
]
. However, due to its applicability to Maxwell’s equations— as an exact field equation — subsequent geometric interpretations of the method emerged
[
27
]
. In fact, penalty methods have been widely used for decades in computational physics as mathematical tools, sometimes accompanied by some physical interpretations
[
23
]
. From this perspective, the present theory can be regarded as a geometric interpretation of a particular penalty method for Maxwell’s equations and their subset equations.

An Operational Roadmap of the Paper
: We begin by interpreting conformal geometry as a special case of a broader homothetic framework—specifically, as a homothetic geometry with a zero homothety center. This perspective allows us to elevate local scale transformations to a general gauge symmetry. To facilitate this, we introduce a novel mathematical structure based on doubled tangent and cotangent bundles, which linearizes the action of the homothetic group. Upon this foundation, we construct a set of gauge-covariant differential operators, leading to a generalized de Rham complex and an associated Hodge decomposition theorem, which ensures the mathematical consistency of the resulting field theories. As a primary application, we apply this formalism to classical electromagnetism, yielding a set of homothetically covariant Maxwell equations. A distinctive feature of these equations is the emergence of interaction terms that couple the electromagnetic field to the scalar gauge field governing the local scale. These terms persist even under simplifying assumptions and function as geometric penalty terms. This structure allows for the imposition of boundary conditions directly within the field equations, thereby reformulating the theory in a sourceless, singularity-free manner. By imposing a generalized Lorenz gauge condition, we derive homothetic wave and Laplace equations that naturally encode boundary data. This framework is shown to be equivalent to solving classical Dirichlet or Neumann problems via a volumetric penalization method. Finally, we leverage this result to address the classical problem of the infinite self-energy of a point charge. By modeling the source as a penalized Dirichlet boundary condition on a shrinking interface, we construct a non-singular electrostatic potential, resolving the divergence in a manner that emerges naturally from the geometry.

First setup: Block-homothetic Transformations

From the mathematical viewpoint, we want to have homothetic transformations with linear operations; and it is achievable via embedding the tangent bundles in the doubled spaces. Let
(M,\eta)
denote
(1+3)

T_{x}M\hookrightarrow T_{x}M\oplus T_{x}M,\qquad X\mapsto\bar{X}=\begin{pmatrix}X\\
X_{d}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\forall X\in T_{x}M.

(1)

This embedding is well-defined for each

3
A Generalized Weyl Integrable Geometry

On the extended space
\bar{\bf X}
, a symmetry manifests itself: the transformation
\bar{\bf X}\to\hat{\bf X}={\bf H}\bar{\bf X}
preserves the musical isomorphism
\bar{\flat}
. In fact, one can verify that
\bar{\flat}
and
\Lambda
commute:

\Lambda\bar{X}^{\bar{\flat}}=\Lambda\bar{\eta}\bar{X}=\bar{\eta}(\Lambda\bar{X})=(\Lambda\bar{X})^{\bar{\flat}}.

(11)

However, this does not imply that the lengths and angles of vectors are preserved under the transformation
\bar{\bf X}\to\hat{\bf X}
—only that they remain unchanged within a local Lorentz frame. To make this distinction precise, we define the effective (generalized Weyl integrable) metric
\tilde{\eta}
for any
X,Y\in TM
via:

\tilde{\eta}(X,Y):=\eta(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}),

(12)

where
\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{Y}
are given by the homothetic action in Eq. (
4
). A direct computation yields:

\tilde{\eta}(X,Y)=e^{-2\lambda}\eta(X,Y)+(1-e^{-\lambda})^{2}\eta(X_{d},X_{d})+e^{-\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda})[\eta(X,X_{d})+\eta(Y,X_{d})].

(13)

This metric generalizes the usual conformal structure of Weyl integrable geometry by incorporating an explicit
offset vector

X_{d}\in TM
. For
\lambda=0
, the effective metric reduces to
\tilde{\eta}=\eta
, and for
\lambda\to\infty
, the metric degenerates:

\tilde{\eta}(X,Y)\to\eta(X_{d},X_{d}),

(14)

independently of
X
and
Y
. The structure encoded in Eq. (
13
) thus reflects a homothetic-invariant geometry, one that naturally combines a local scaling
\lambda(x)
with a fiberwise translation defined by
X_{d}(x)
, consistent with the action of group
\mathcal{H}
.

While this offset structure is geometrically natural and consistent with our next cohomological framework, it is hardly interpretable physically. The general form of the metric in Eq. (
13
), with
X_{d}\neq 0
, represents the full mathematical structure that arises from the linearization of the homothetic map. It is the most direct consequence of our initial setup. However, to ensure that this effective metric
\tilde{\eta}
can be interpreted as a physical spacetime metric, it must satisfy certain fundamental axioms. In particular, the presence of
X_{d}
in
\tilde{\eta}
means that even the zero vector
X=0
can acquire a nontrivial norm—a violation of the identity of indiscernibles and an obstruction to interpreting
\tilde{\eta}
as a standard spacetime metric. Therefore, to recover a physical spacetime geometry that supports unambiguous geodesic separation, we impose a physicality condition by restricting the general geometric structure to the subspace where the offset vector vanishes. This is achievable without altering the foundational structure by choosing:

X_{d}=0,

(15)

which leads to the conventional conformal geometry:

\boxed{\tilde{\eta}(X,Y)=e^{-2\lambda(x)}\eta(X,Y).}

(16)

That is, to this point,
the conformal geometry is interpreted as a homothetic geometry with zero homothety center
.

However, we see this conformal geometry as a Weyl integrable geometry
[
28
]
because we want to use it in generalizing the cohomology. Recall that a Weyl integrable geometry is defined by the triple
(M,[\eta],\phi)
, where
[\eta]
is the conformal class of Lorentzian metrics and
\phi:=d\lambda
is a globally defined, closed 1-form satisfying

\nabla_{\rho}\eta_{\mu\nu}=-2\phi_{\rho}\eta_{\mu\nu}.

(17)

This non-metricity condition defines a Weyl-compatible connection that preserves null directions but allows local rescaling governed by
\phi
. In our formulation, both the conformal metric and the 1-form
\phi:=d\lambda
arise naturally from the homothetic structure, with
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
arbitrary and smooth. Thus, the effective geometry obtained from Eq. (
16
) is not merely analogous to Weyl integrable geometry—it is precisely one, derived from a block-homothetic framework.

About the Physical Interpretation of
\lambda

In the absence of any other valid physical interpretation, we posit that the physical interpretation of this conformal frame is that of a spacetime with a variable speed of light (VSL). Establishing a relation between the conformal transformation and the variable speed of light is almost trivial, for example, one can see the following.

Consider the normalization of the four-velocity
u^{\mu}
for a massive particle. In the physical frame described by
\tilde{\eta}_{\mu\nu}
, the squared norm is given by
\tilde{\eta}_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}
. To maintain a canonical structure where this norm is identified with the negative square of the local speed of light, we must define a position-dependent speed of light
c(x)
such that:

\tilde{\eta}_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=-c(x)^{2}.

(18)

Comparing with
\eta_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=-1
, this naturally leads to the identification:

c(x)=e^{-\lambda(x)}.

(19)

This VSL interpretation is further substantiated in the limit where
\lambda(x)\to\infty
. In this case,
c(x)\to 0
and the metric components
\tilde{\eta}_{\mu\nu}\to 0
. The collapse of all spacetime intervals signifies a degeneration of the causal structure where propagation is impossible, consistent with a vanishing speed of light.

Crucially, the existence of a relativistic kinematic framework, regardless of the local value of
c(x)
, allows for the definition of its two fundamental degenerate limits. At any spacetime point
x
, the local physics is governed by a Lorentz algebra characterized by the finite, non-zero constant
c(x)
. The Galilean and Carrollian kinematics can be understood as distinct Inönü–Wigner contractions
[
17
]
of this local Poincaré algebra, corresponding to the limits where the structure constant
c(x)
is taken to infinity or zero, respectively.

•

The Galilean Limit (
c(x_{0})\to\infty
):
This contraction yields the Galilean algebra
\mathfrak{g}(1,3)
. Physically, it corresponds to the causal cone opening up completely, allowing for absolute simultaneity and instantaneous propagation of signals.

•

The Carrollian Limit (
c(x_{0})\to 0
):
This contraction yields the Carrollian algebra
\mathfrak{c}(1,3)
. Physically, it corresponds to the causal cone collapsing to a one-dimensional line, forbidding motion and rendering causality ultra-local.

This establishes that the Galilean and Carrollian frameworks are universally definable as conceptual limits from any local relativistic standpoint. The role of the field
\lambda(x)
is then to dictate the
physical character
of a given spacetime region. While an observer can always conceive of both limits, a region where
\lambda(x)\to\infty
will see its physical dynamics—its particle propagation and causal structure—asymptotically approach the behavior described by the idealized Carrollian limit
[
22
]
.

4
Homothetic Hodge Decomposition

In this section, we study the behavior of differential forms in the homothetically transformed geometry introduced previously. Rather than focusing on traditional invariance properties, we consider a class of
homothetically transformed
differential forms, which lead naturally to a generalized de Rham complex and cohomology, and a generalized Hodge decomposition, adapted to the structure. We begin with the case of 1-forms, establishing both their physical interpretation and mathematical formulation. Other
k\neq 1
ranks will be addressed in subsequent subsections.

4.1
The Homothetically Transformed 1-Forms

Finally, the present paper intends to analyze electromagnetism in which the (Ehresmann) connection, on which we can put physically meaningful constraints, is
{\mathfrak{u}}(1)
-valued 1-forms
A=A_{\nu}dx^{\nu}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Therefore, we begin here with 1-forms, albeit in a more general setting which will be suppressed to the potential gauge fields
A
in

5
.

In the conventional formulation of conformal geometry, the spacetime metric rescaling is a postulation occurring independently of the definition of other fields. But here, the conformal rescaling
\tilde{\eta}\to e^{-2\lambda}\eta
is a
consequence
of a more fundamental transformation acting on the tangent and cotangent bundles. Since a 1-form
\alpha
is a section of this very cotangent bundle, it is not only legitimate but more consistent within our framework to posit that it transforms accordingly. We therefore define the action of the homothetic group
\mathcal{H}
on the space of extended 1-forms. For a general
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, its transformed counterpart
\tilde{\alpha}
is given by the homothetic transformation:

\tilde{\alpha}:=e^{-\lambda(x)}\alpha+\left(1-e^{-\lambda(x)}\right)\alpha_{d},

(20)

where
\alpha_{d}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
is a fixed, globally defined 1-form that serves as the homothety center, in direct analogy to the covector
X^{\flat}_{d}
in the transformation of cotangent spaces. Note that when
\alpha_{d}=0
, this reduces to the familiar Weyl-type scaling
\tilde{\alpha}=e^{-W\lambda(x)}\alpha
, with the Weyl weight
W=1
. But in contrast to
X_{d}
, here we will keep
\alpha_{d}
non-zero in general.

Before continuing, it is important to note that in this formulation, a homothetically invariant 1-form is obtained by setting:

\alpha=\alpha_{d},

(21)

that is,
\tilde{\alpha}
for
\lambda(x)=0
equals
\tilde{\alpha}
for
\lambda(x)\to\infty
, which means that changing
\lambda(x)
does not affect
\alpha
. This can also be derived from definition (
20
):

\tilde{\alpha}=e^{-\lambda(x)}(\alpha-\alpha_{d})+\alpha_{d}=e^{-\lambda(x)}(\alpha_{d}-\alpha_{d})+\alpha_{d}=\alpha_{d}.

(22)

As will be seen, even for homothetically invariant forms (such as electromagnetic gauge potentials, as discussed in §
5
), the general homothetic formulation reveals new and interesting features, as it localizes the values of
\alpha
.

The physical motivation and mathematical justification for the homothetic transformation (
20
) are now in order.

Physical motivation.
Consider the limiting case
\lambda(x)\to\infty
, where the conformal factor goes to zero,
e^{-2\lambda(x)}\to 0
, and hence the associated volume form goes to zero as well
(d\text{vol}_{\tilde{\eta}})_{x}\to 0
. Now, in order to have a theory, being valid in all scales, the functions on
(d\text{vol}_{\tilde{\eta}})_{x}
must converges to a single well-defined value
(\alpha_{d})_{x}
. Note that such a homothetic behavior may define a kind of homogenization, that is, the transformation in Eq. (
20
) ensures that for any initial 1-form
\alpha
, its transform
\tilde{\alpha}
approaches the fixed form
\alpha_{d}
:

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\tilde{\alpha}(x)=\alpha_{d}(x).

This principle thus endows the asymptotic field
\alpha_{d}
with a clear physical interpretation: it is the universal 1-form to which all others are driven as the local spacetime structure degenerates. The existence of such a non-trivial asymptotic field is a profound consequence of the homothetic framework.

Mathematical formalism.
This intuition can be rigorously framed using contraction on the cotangent bundle. Define, at each point
x\in M
, a fiberwise homothetic map:

{\mathbf{T}}_{\lambda(x)}:T_{x}^{*}M\to T_{x}^{*}M,\qquad\alpha\mapsto{\mathbf{T}}_{\lambda(x)}(\alpha):=e^{-\lambda(x)}\alpha+\left(1-e^{-\lambda(x)}\right)\alpha_{d}.

(23)

Endow each fiber
T_{x}^{*}M
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{x}
induced by the original Minkowski metric
\eta
. Then for
\lambda(x)>0
,
{\mathbf{T}}_{\lambda(x)}
is a strict contraction with Lipschitz constant
e^{-\lambda(x)}<1
. By the Banach fixed-point theorem,
{\mathbf{T}}_{\lambda(x)}
has a unique fixed point in each fiber, which is precisely
\alpha_{d}
. Moreover, as
\lambda(x)\to\infty
,
{\mathbf{T}}_{\lambda(x)}
converges to a projection map that sends every 1-form to
\alpha_{d}
:

\lim_{\lambda(x)\to\infty}{\mathbf{T}}_{\lambda(x)}(\alpha)=\alpha_{d},\quad\forall\alpha\in T_{x}^{*}M.

(24)

This construction defines a smooth family of fiberwise contractions across
M
, forming a pointwise contraction semigroup. In the limit
\lambda(x)\to\infty
,
\tilde{\alpha}
thus enforces homogenization of all 1-forms to a single invariant form.

Now, as we mentioned before (c.f., Eqns. (
9
) and (
10
)), the above homothetic transformation can be linearized via the embedding
\Omega^{k}(M)\hookrightarrow\Omega^{k}(M)\oplus\Omega^{k}(M)
, that is:

\hat{\alpha}:=\Lambda\cdot\bar{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\alpha}\\
\alpha_{d}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\lambda(x)}\alpha+\left(1-e^{-\lambda(x)}\right)\alpha_{d}\\
\alpha_{d}\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

The implications of this contraction will become central in the upcoming discussion of generalized Hodge theory.

What happen to the other differential forms is the question that will be addressed in the next subsection, after developing some mathematical tools we need for their analyses.

4.2
Generalized de Rham complex and Hodge decomposition

The key step in dealing with the homothetically transformed differential forms is the appropriate construction of the differential operators on this geometry. In this regard, we first define:

\bar{d}:=\begin{pmatrix}d&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\delta}:=\begin{pmatrix}\delta&0\\
0&\delta\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\Delta}:=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta&0\\
0&\Delta\end{pmatrix},\qquad\ \bar{\star}:=\begin{pmatrix}\star&0\\
0&\star\end{pmatrix},\quad

(26)

where

is the exterior derivative,
\delta
is the codifferential,
\Delta=d\delta+\delta d
is the Hodge–de Rham Laplacian, and
\star
is the Hodge star operator. Then, we define these operators on the homothetically transformed space of differential forms as:

Generalized exterior derivative
:

\hat{d}:=\begin{pmatrix}d+(d\lambda)\wedge&-(d\lambda)\wedge\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}.

(27)

Generalized codifferential
:

\hat{\delta}:=\begin{pmatrix}\delta-*(d\lambda)\wedge*&*(d\lambda)\wedge*\\
0&\delta\end{pmatrix}.

(28)

Generalized Laplacian
:

\hat{\Delta}:=\hat{\delta}\hat{d}+\hat{d}\hat{\delta}.

(29)

Block Hodge star operator
:

\hat{\star}:=\bar{\star}.

(30)

The key features of these operators are that (it can be verified directly):

\boxed{\hat{d}\Lambda=\Lambda\bar{d},\qquad\hat{\delta}\Lambda=\Lambda\bar{\delta},\qquad\hat{\Delta}\Lambda=\Lambda\bar{\Delta},\qquad\bar{\star}\Lambda=\Lambda\bar{\star},}

(31)

1.

Both
\hat{d}
and
\hat{\delta}
are

, that is,
\hat{d}^{2}=0
and
\hat{\delta}^{2}=0

2.

With the Weyl-rescaled metric
\tilde{\eta}=e^{-2\lambda(x)}\eta
,
\hat{\delta}
is the

The generalized operators

Physically, this suggests that the transformation group

From a gauge-theoretic point of view, this confirms that no essential degrees of freedom are lost through the transformation: instead, they are reorganized in a manner compatible with the homothetic group action.

Cauchy, Dirichlet and Neumann Boundary Conditions for the Laplace Equation

Given a closed smooth
(d-1)
-dimensional hypersurface
S
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we denote by
\delta_{n}
a sequence of regularizations of the Dirac delta function
\delta_{S}
(not to be confused with the codifferential in the previous sections), such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}=\delta_{S}
. Then, in the generalized Laplace equation (
49
), we choose (see Appendix
B
for justification):

\lambda_{n}=\ln(1+\delta_{n}),

(55)

resulting in:

\nabla^{2}\phi_{n}+2\,\frac{\nabla\delta_{n}}{1+\delta_{n}}\cdot(\nabla\phi_{n}-\nabla\phi_{d})+\frac{\Delta\delta_{n}}{1+\delta_{n}}(\phi_{n}-\phi_{d})=0.

(56)

We observe that, in the singular limit
n\to\infty
, the regularization
\delta_{n}
approaches the Dirac distribution
\delta_{S}
, supported on a closed hypersurface
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In this limit, the terms involving derivatives of
\delta_{n}
become highly localized near
S
, and act to impose both Dirichlet and Neumann conditions simultaneously on the surface.

As a result, equation (
56
) defines two decoupled Cauchy problems in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
: one on the interior and one on the exterior of
S
. Unless one solution is the harmonic continuation of the other, a discontinuity (in the function or its gradient) appears at
S
. If only a Dirichlet condition
\phi_{S}=\phi_{d}
is imposed, a discontinuity in the normal derivative
\nabla\phi_{S}\cdot\mathbf{n}
may appear across
S
. Conversely, if only the Neumann condition
\nabla\phi_{S}\cdot\mathbf{n}=\nabla\phi_{d}\cdot\mathbf{n}
is enforced, a jump in the potential
\phi_{S}
itself can occur. These phenomena are closely related to classical potential theory, particularly the behavior of single-layer and double-layer potentials
[
20
,
21
]
.

Specifically, a
single-layer potential
yields a continuous potential across the interface but typically introduces a discontinuity in its normal derivative. In contrast, a
double-layer potential
maintains continuity of the normal derivative but allows for a jump in the potential itself
[
5
]
. The behavior of equation (
56
) in the limit
n\to\infty
is thus analogous to a
combined layer potential
, where both Dirichlet and Neumann data are enforced, resembling classical integral formulations for boundary value problems in potential theory.

Interestingly, the structure of equation (
56
) also permits a modular strategy for solving classical boundary value problems. Suppose one intends to solve a pure Dirichlet problem—either on the interior or exterior of
S
. In this case, one may
omit the gradient-coupling term
and instead consider the equation:

\nabla^{2}\phi_{n}+\frac{\Delta\delta_{n}}{1+\delta_{n}}(\phi_{n}-\phi_{d})=0.

(57)

This corresponds to a standard Laplace equation perturbed by a sharply localized penalty term, which enforces
\phi_{n}\approx\phi_{d}
on
S
in the limit
n\to\infty
. As a result, a Dirichlet boundary condition is imposed through a volumetric penalization, a method reminiscent of embedded boundary techniques and diffuse interface methods
[
24
,
23
]
.

Once
\phi_{n}
is obtained from equation (
57
), one automatically gains access to the normal derivative
\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}
on
S
. In the context of elliptic boundary value problems, this is equivalent to applying the
Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) map
(not to be confused with the homothetic transformation matrix in the previous sections), often denoted by

\Lambda:\phi_{n}|_{S}\mapsto\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{S}.

Now, suppose we set
\nabla\phi_{d}:=\nabla\phi_{n}
; then, the term
(\nabla\phi_{n}-\nabla\phi_{d})
in the original equation (
56
) vanishes identically, and the full equation is satisfied. Thus, one has in effect solved the complete model under a self-consistent assumption that aligns the constructed
\phi_{d}
with the solution gradient on
S
.

A similar argument holds for solving Neumann problems. In this case, one may omit the potential penalty term and retain only the gradient-coupling component:

\nabla^{2}\phi_{n}+2\,\frac{\nabla\delta_{n}}{1+\delta_{n}}\cdot(\nabla\phi_{n}-\nabla\phi_{d})=0.

(58)

This imposes a Neumann-type boundary condition by penalizing discrepancies between the solution gradient and the prescribed gradient field
\nabla\phi_{d}
. Upon obtaining a solution
\phi_{n}
, one can define
\phi_{d}:=\phi_{n}
on
S
, and again observe that the residual term
(\phi_{n}-\phi_{d})
in equation (
56
) vanishes. Hence, the full model is satisfied under the assumption of gradient-data consistency.

These selective reductions of the model not only simplify numerical treatment but also clarify the role of the original PDE as a unified interface formulation capable of encoding a range of classical boundary conditions through regularized volume penalties. The correspondence with the DtN operator also invites further interpretation of the interface as a generalized boundary operator in the sense of nonlocal elliptic theory
[
6
,
25
]
.

The classical model of electrostatics, governed by the Poisson equation with a delta function source, has long faced a well-known difficulty: the infinite energy of the vacuum associated with point charges

[

[

In the framework developed in the previous section, a new perspective emerges. We propose a regularized, non-singular model for a point electric charge using the generalized Laplace equation with Dirichlet-type penalization:

(

revisited)

where

\delta_{\partial B(R)}

\phi_{d}=\frac{C}{R},

enforcing a Coulomb-like condition at
r=R
. Due to the highly localized nature of the penalty term, the solution
\phi_{n}
adjusts sharply near the interface to match this boundary value, and decays as
\phi_{n}(r)\sim\frac{C}{r}
for large

, consistent with the behavior of the classical potential in the far field.

The energy,

R\to 0
, as the singular core is effectively excluded from the domain of integration.

This construction achieves what the classical model could not: a physically faithful, asymptotically Coulombic potential associated with a point-like source, but without the divergence of vacuum energy. The source is interpreted not as a singular point but as the limit of a penalized interface of vanishing size, carrying an enforced boundary potential. The infinite energy problem is thereby resolved through a combination of geometrically localized penalization and the decoupling of interior and exterior fields.

This construction should be contrasted with other historical approaches to resolving the divergence of self-energy. Many attempts, such as non-linear electrodynamics theories like the Born-Infeld model
[
3
,
9
]
, modify the fundamental dynamics of the electromagnetic field everywhere in spacetime to regularize the field at the origin. Our approach is fundamentally different. It is a form of
geometric regularization
that does not alter the linear Maxwell equations in the vacuum (where
\lambda=0
).

9
Conclusions

This work has introduced a novel gauge theory of scale, placing local homotheties on an equal footing with the familiar internal symmetries that underpin the Standard Model. By constructing a formalism where local scale transformations act linearly, we have developed a self-contained and consistent framework with significant physical implications. The principal results of this investigation demonstrate the theory’s viability and its potential to resolve long-standing issues in classical physics:

•

A generalized Weyl-integrable geometry emerges naturally from our framework, where the spacetime metric dynamically responds to a scalar gauge field,
\lambda
, that dictates the local scale.

•

We established a homothetic de Rham complex and an associated Hodge decomposition. This mathematical backbone guarantees that the resulting field equations are well-posed and that their physical solutions are unique.

•

Applying the theory to electromagnetism yields homothetically covariant sourceless Maxwell equations. These equations have some additional terms coupling the electromagnetic field with the gradient of the scale gauge field.

•

A key achievement is a resolution to the century-old problem of the infinite self-energy of a point charge. Our model replaces the singularity with a regularized, penalized boundary condition on an infinitesimal interface—a result that emerges directly from the theory’s geometric foundations, not from ad-hoc modifications.

•

The entire gauge structure was shown to have a rigorous interpretation within the established framework of Cartan geometry, clarifying its deep connection to the conformal group and providing a basis for further geometric analysis.

Taken together, these findings present homothetic gauge theory as more than a mathematical curiosity; it is a workable extension of classical field theory. The future avenues for exploration are rich and varied. Integrating HGT with metric dynamics could offer a novel path toward modifying general relativity, where cosmological phenomena might be linked to gauge artifacts of the scale field. Furthermore, coupling the homothetic connection to non-Abelian gauge groups could unveil new scale-dependent physics relevant to the strong and weak interactions. The theoretical and computational tools developed here provide a solid foundation for pursuing these exciting possibilities.

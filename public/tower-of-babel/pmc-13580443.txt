Short-Term Changes in Peripapillary RNFL and Disc-Fovea Angle After Horizontal Rectus Muscle Surgery in Comitant Strabismus
Authors: Xiyu Zhao, Jingjing Jiang, Li Li, Xiaogang Wang, Junhong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo investigate short-term changes in peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (pRNFL) thickness and disc-fovea angle (DFA) after horizontal rectus muscle surgery in patients with comitant strabismus.Patients and MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included 73 eyes of 73 patients with comitant strabismus who underwent horizontal rectus muscle surgery. Patients were divided according to the number of horizontal rectus muscles operated on in the analyzed eye: Group 1, in which the analyzed eye underwent one-horizontal-rectus-muscle surgery, and Group 2, in which the analyzed eye underwent two-horizontal-rectus-muscle surgery. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) was performed preoperatively, on postoperative day 1, and at postoperative week 2. Global and sectoral pRNFL parameters and the absolute DFA (|DFA|) were analyzed.ResultsBaseline demographic, refractive, pRNFL, and |DFA| parameters were comparable between the two groups. In Group 1, several pRNFL parameters showed significant early postoperative changes on postoperative day 1, mainly involving G, PMB, T, TI, and NI, and most values returned toward baseline by postoperative week 2. In Group 2, pRNFL changes were more limited, with only TI showing a significant change on postoperative day 1, whereas |DFA| remained significantly different from baseline at postoperative week 2. Between-group comparisons showed greater day 1 changes in G, N, and NI in Group 1, while only the change in |DFA| remained significantly different between groups at week 2.ConclusionHorizontal rectus muscle surgery was associated with mild, transient, and region-specific changes in the pRNFL in patients with comitant strabismus. One-horizontal-muscle surgery was associated with broader early postoperative fluctuations, whereas surgery involving two horizontal muscles was more closely associated with persistent short-term DFA changes.

Introduction

Strabismus is a common ocular alignment disorder in both children and adults. In patients with strabismus, the two eyes cannot fixate on the same target simultaneously, resulting in loss of parallel visual axes. In addition to visual dysfunction, subtle retinal structural and functional abnormalities may also be present.1

The peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (pRNFL) is composed mainly of ganglion cell axons and is an important indicator of retinal neural structure.2 Previous optical coherence tomography (OCT) studies have suggested that strabismus may be associated with optic disc morphology and retinal nerve fiber layer thickness, but the available findings remain inconsistent and limited.3–5

Dynamic posterior pole changes after strabismus surgery have received less attention than preoperative structural differences. Existing studies have mainly evaluated macular thickness, choroidal thickness, and macular microcirculation after horizontal rectus muscle surgery.6–9 However, short-term postoperative changes in pRNFL thickness, particularly in relation to different horizontal surgical extents, remain insufficiently characterized. The disc-fovea angle (DFA) reflects the spatial relationship between the optic disc and the fovea and has been considered one of the factors affecting the regional distribution of pRNFL.10,11 Therefore, when evaluating pRNFL changes after strabismus surgery, consideration of DFA may help provide a more comprehensive interpretation of postoperative OCT findings.

In this retrospective study, we analyzed OCT data obtained before and after horizontal rectus muscle surgery in patients with comitant strabismus, compared the short-term pRNFL changes after one- and two-horizontal-muscle surgery, and further examined the absolute disc-fovea angle (|DFA|), with the aim of providing evidence for the interpretation of early postoperative OCT findings in strabismus.

Subjects and Methods

Subjects

This was a retrospective secondary analysis of previously acquired OCT images and clinical data. Clinical data and previous OCT images were retrospectively collected from patients with comitant strabismus who underwent horizontal rectus muscle surgery at Shanxi Eye Hospital between June 2024 and September 2024. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) previous ocular surgery; (2) best-corrected visual acuity < 1.0 or absolute spherical equivalent > 5.0 D; (3) diabetes, hypertension, other systemic diseases, or ocular fundus diseases that might affect retinal vasculature or retinal/optic nerve structure; (4) developmental delay or neurological disease; (5) inability to cooperate with OCT imaging, or images with obvious artifacts or poor quality; and (6) simultaneous oblique muscle surgery, vertical rectus muscle surgery, or horizontal rectus muscle transposition for clinically significant A- or V-pattern strabismus.

For patients who underwent bilateral surgery, only one eye was included in the analysis. Patients were divided into two groups according to the number of horizontal rectus muscles operated on in the analyzed eye: Group 1, in which the analyzed eye underwent one-horizontal-rectus-muscle surgery; and Group 2, in which the analyzed eye underwent two-horizontal-rectus-muscle surgery.

Surgical Procedures and Surgical Variables

All surgeries were performed under general anesthesia by the same experienced senior strabismus surgeon. A fornix-based conjunctival incision using the Parks approach was used to expose the operated horizontal rectus muscle. After isolation of the target rectus muscle, standard recession and/or resection procedures were performed according to the preoperative horizontal deviation angle, strabismus type, ocular motility, and the surgeon’s routine surgical dosage table. After reattachment of the rectus muscle to the sclera, the conjunctival incision was closed with 8–0 absorbable sutures. Patients with clinically significant A- or V-pattern strabismus requiring additional oblique muscle surgery, vertical rectus muscle surgery, or vertical transposition of horizontal rectus muscles were not included. Patients with small associated vertical deviations that did not require additional vertical or oblique muscle procedures underwent horizontal rectus muscle surgery alone.

In Group 1, surgery consisted of recession of a single horizontal rectus muscle, including lateral rectus recession for exotropia and medial rectus recession for esotropia. In the present cohort, the single-muscle recession dose ranged from 5.5 to 10.0 mm. In Group 2, surgery consisted of unilateral recession-resection procedures, including lateral rectus recession combined with medial rectus resection for exotropia and medial rectus recession combined with lateral rectus resection for esotropia. In this group, the recession dose ranged from 4.0 to 10.0 mm, the resection dose ranged from 4.0 to 8.0 mm, and the total horizontal surgical dose ranged from 8.5 to 16.0 mm.

The following clinical and surgical variables were recorded: strabismus type, operated eye, operated muscle or muscles, recession dose, resection dose, total surgical dose, preoperative horizontal deviation angle at 33 cm, and preoperative horizontal deviation angle at 6 m. Horizontal deviation was recorded in prism diopters.

OCT Examination and Outcome Measures

All participants underwent optic disc scanning using the Heidelberg Spectralis OCT system (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany). A standard circular scan centered on the optic disc was used to obtain peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (pRNFL) images, and the corresponding fundus image was used for disc-fovea angle (DFA) measurement (Figure 1). The recorded parameters included global pRNFL thickness (G), papillomacular bundle thickness (PMB), temporal thickness (T), temporal superior thickness (TS), nasal superior thickness (NS), nasal thickness (N), nasal-to-temporal ratio (N/T), temporal inferior thickness (TI), and nasal inferior thickness (NI). In the OCT output used in this study, negative DFA values indicated excyclotorsion and positive DFA values indicated incyclotorsion. The absolute value of DFA was used for statistical analysis, whereas signed DFA was used for the direction-stratified exploratory analysis. OCT examinations were performed preoperatively, on postoperative day 1, and at postoperative week 2. The pRNFL parameters and DFA were automatically generated by the built-in software. Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 27.0. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess the normality of continuous variables. Because most continuous variables were not normally distributed, continuous data are presented as median (interquartile range), and categorical data are presented as number and percentage. Baseline continuous variables between the two groups were compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test, and categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Within-group longitudinal comparisons across the three time points were performed using the Friedman test. When the overall Friedman test was significant, pairwise comparisons between postoperative day 1 and baseline, and between postoperative week 2 and baseline, were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni correction. To compare the magnitude of postoperative changes between groups, change values at postoperative day 1 and postoperative week 2 relative to baseline were calculated and compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test. For the primary analyses, a two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Exploratory Spearman correlation analyses were performed to assess whether eye-level surgical dose variables were associated with postoperative changes in |DFA| and pRNFL parameters. In Group 1, the recession dose of the analyzed eye was included as the surgical dose variable. In Group 2, the recession dose, resection dose, and total surgical dose of the analyzed eye were included. The analyzed outcomes included changes in |DFA| and in G, PMB, T, TS, NS, N, N/T, TI, and NI at postoperative day 1 and postoperative week 2. To further evaluate whether the direction of preoperative fundus torsion influenced postoperative DFA changes, patients were classified into excyclotorsion and incyclotorsion subgroups according to baseline signed DFA. In this OCT output, negative DFA values indicated excyclotorsion, whereas positive DFA values indicated incyclotorsion. Signed DFA values at baseline, postoperative day 1, and postoperative week 2 were summarized separately within each direction subgroup. Delta DFA was calculated as postoperative DFA minus baseline DFA. Within-subgroup comparisons between postoperative and baseline DFA values were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and Delta DFA values were compared between excyclotorsion and incyclotorsion subgroups using the Mann–Whitney U-test. Both exploratory analyses were not adjusted for multiple comparisons; therefore, P values from these analyses were interpreted as nominally significant.

Results

A total of 73 eyes from 73 patients were included in the study, including 34 eyes in the one-horizontal-muscle surgery group (Group 1) and 39 eyes in the two-horizontal-muscle surgery group (Group 2). There were no significant differences between the two groups in sex distribution, age, spherical equivalent, strabismus type, or operative eye distribution. Baseline global and sectoral pRNFL thicknesses and |DFA| were also comparable between the two groups. However, the preoperative horizontal deviation angle was larger in Group 1 than in Group 2 at both 33 cm and 6 m (both P < 0.001). This imbalance occurred because some patients with larger deviations underwent bilateral surgery, whereas only one eye was included in the analysis to avoid inter-eye correlation. Exploratory correlation analysis showed that, overall, eye-level surgical dose variables were not consistently associated with changes in |DFA| in either group. In Group 1, recession dose showed no consistent correlation with changes in |DFA| or any pRNFL subregion. In Group 2, nominal correlation signals were mainly observed for regional pRNFL parameters rather than |DFA|. In particular, total surgical dose and rectus muscle resection dose showed positive nominal correlations with superior sector pRNFL changes, especially TS on postoperative day 1 and NS at postoperative week 2. These findings suggest that postoperative pRNFL fluctuations may be region-specific, but they do not support a consistent dose-related change in |DFA|. However, these analyses were exploratory and were not adjusted for multiple comparisons (Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Figure 1).

Direction-stratified exploratory analysis showed that most eyes had baseline excyclotorsion, whereas only a small number had baseline incyclotorsion. In Group 1, 30 eyes had excyclotorsion and 4 had incyclotorsion; in Group 2, 36 eyes had excyclotorsion and 3 had incyclotorsion. In Group 1, eyes with excyclotorsion showed a small nominal increase in signed DFA on postoperative day 1, but this change was minimal in magnitude and was not sustained at postoperative week 2. In Group 2, eyes with excyclotorsion showed a small nominal decrease in signed DFA at postoperative week 2. No consistent postoperative change was observed in the incyclotorsion subgroup, although the sample size was very small. Comparisons of Delta DFA between excyclotorsion and incyclotorsion subgroups did not show significant differences in either surgical group. Overall, preoperative DFA direction did not demonstrate a stable modifying effect on short-term postoperative DFA changes (Supplementary Table 2).

Discussion

This study showed that pRNFL and |DFA| may undergo short-term, region-specific changes following horizontal rectus muscle surgery. Most changes occurred during the early postoperative period and tended to recover by postoperative week 2. At the same time, the patterns of change differed between surgical extents. The one-horizontal-muscle surgery group showed broader pRNFL fluctuations on postoperative day 1, whereas changes in the two-horizontal-muscle surgery group were more limited, although the |DFA| remained altered at postoperative week 2. Because baseline pRNFL and |DFA| parameters were comparable between the two groups, these findings suggest that the observed postoperative differences were more likely related to short-term structural changes after surgery. Although several postoperative changes reached statistical significance, the absolute magnitudes of these changes were small. For example, most median pRNFL changes in this study were only a few micrometers, and the between-group difference in |DFA| change at postoperative week 2 was minimal. Therefore, these findings may be interpreted as mild short-term fluctuations in OCT-derived parameters during the early postoperative period.

The novelty of this study lies in the combined evaluation of sectoral pRNFL parameters and |DFA| during the early postoperative period after different extents of horizontal rectus muscle surgery. Relatively few studies have specifically examined early postoperative pRNFL changes, especially in relation to the number of horizontal rectus muscles operated on. Therefore, the present study provides observational evidence that may help clinicians interpret early postoperative OCT findings after strabismus surgery.

RNFL is primarily composed of ganglion cell axons and also includes glial elements and retinal vessels.12 Previous studies have shown that pRNFL thickness varies across different sectors.13,14 Consequently, these sectors may respond differently to short-term postoperative changes. In the present study, pRNFL changes were limited in magnitude and were largely reversible within the short follow-up period. This pattern does not support persistent structural injury. Instead, it is more consistent with transient postoperative fluctuation in OCT-derived measurements. Such fluctuation may be influenced by conjunctival hyperemia, ocular surface discomfort, edema, reduced fixation stability, scan alignment, or segmentation variability during early postoperative imaging.

Recent optical coherence tomography angiography studies provide complementary evidence that extraocular muscle surgery may induce short-term changes in posterior pole imaging parameters. Previous studies have reported that extraocular muscle surgery is associated with transient changes in macular microcirculation, and that these changes tended to recover during follow-up.15,16 Although OCTA-derived perfusion parameters are not directly equivalent to pRNFL thickness, these findings support that retinal and choroidal imaging parameters may fluctuate after strabismus surgery.

The change in |DFA| provides additional insight into the short-term postoperative fluctuations of pRNFL observed in this study. Unlike pRNFL thickness, DFA reflects the relative position between the optic disc and the fovea and is therefore more easily influenced by globe configuration and fixation-related changes. This spatial relationship has also been considered an important factor affecting the regional distribution of pRNFL.10,11 Park et al17 reported posterior pole morphological changes after horizontal strabismus surgery. Previous studies indicate that horizontal rectus surgery can modify objective fundus torsion, particularly in eyes with pre-existing abnormal torsion. In children with infantile esotropia, Khanna et al18 found that the signed DFA decreased from 12.94 degrees to 11.14 degrees in the pathological extorsion group and increased from −1.99 degrees to 0.29 degrees in the intorsion group one year after unilateral surgery, whereas the physiological-extorsion group remained stable. Similarly, Liu et al19 reported a direction-specific pattern after unilateral recession-resection for intermittent exotropia. The signed DFA decreased from 12.8 degrees to 9.8 degrees and 9.7 degrees in extorted eyes and increased from −1.5 degrees to 2.0 degrees and 2.2 degrees in intorted eyes at postoperative week 1 and month 1, respectively. Eyes without preoperative abnormal torsion showed no significant postoperative change. Together, these findings describe a baseline-phenotype- and direction-dependent movement toward a more physiological torsional range. In contrast, Aghdam et al20 did not observe significant postoperative DFA changes in patients undergoing bilateral lateral rectus recession. These inconsistent findings suggest that DFA changes after horizontal rectus surgery may depend on surgical procedure, strabismus type, baseline torsion, imaging method, and follow-up timing.

In the present study, Group 2 exhibited a significant change in |DFA| from baseline at postoperative week 2, whereas Group 1 showed an overall time effect in |DFA| but no significant pairwise differences between postoperative time points and baseline after correction. Exploratory correlation analysis did not reveal a consistent association between surgical magnitude and changes in |DFA| in either group. These findings suggest that early postoperative OCT parameters may show different short-term patterns depending on the surgical group and OCT parameter type, rather than a consistent dose-related change in |DFA|. All participants had comitant horizontal strabismus and reported no diplopia before surgery, and no new-onset torsional diplopia was reported during the early postoperative follow-up. Thus, the small postoperative |DFA| changes observed in this cohort were asymptomatic changes in an objective imaging measure of the disc-fovea relationship. We evaluated DFA to assess whether horizontal rectus muscle surgery was associated with short-term changes in fundus torsion and posterior pole orientation. Previous reports have identified altered DFA or objective fundus torsion in horizontal strabismus and postoperative changes in these measures following horizontal rectus muscle surgery.17–20 The clinical significance of the small imaging changes observed here remains uncertain.

The mechanisms underlying these short-term OCT changes remain uncertain. The different patterns observed between Group 1 and Group 2 may be influenced by several factors, including surgical extent, direction of muscle manipulation, preoperative deviation angle, postoperative fixation stability, local inflammatory reactions, and OCT measurement variability.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, which may limit statistical power and generalizability. Second, although we performed exploratory correlation analyses using surgical dose, the sample size limited stratified analyses by specific procedure type, such as medial rectus recession, lateral rectus recession, or recession-resection subtypes. Third, serial intraocular pressure, axial length, and direct biomechanical measurements of scleral wall stress were not available; therefore, explanations related to scleral mechanics or ocular configuration remain speculative. Fourth, OCTA data were not included, and we could not evaluate whether pRNFL changes were accompanied by retinal or choroidal microvascular alterations. Fifth, long-term follow-up data were not available, and therefore it remains unclear whether the persistent changes in DFA would eventually return to baseline. In addition, although all OCT examinations were performed by the same trained examiner using the same device, and cases with poor image quality or obvious artifacts were excluded, early postoperative scanning may still have been affected by fixation cooperation and local postoperative reactions. Future studies with larger samples, prospective design, longer follow-up, and more detailed stratification by surgical procedure are needed to further clarify the dynamic effects of different strabismus surgeries on posterior pole OCT parameters and their underlying mechanisms.

In summary, horizontal rectus muscle surgery in patients with comitant strabismus may lead to mild, transient, and largely reversible fluctuations in pRNFL parameters, and the pattern of change may differ according to surgical extent. In contrast, DFA may be more sensitive to short-term postoperative changes in the posterior pole configuration. These findings suggest that early postoperative OCT parameter changes after strabismus surgery should be interpreted cautiously, as they are more likely to reflect dynamic, short-term structural adjustments rather than persistent structural damage. This study provides preliminary evidence for postoperative imaging follow-up in comitant strabismus and may offer clues for future studies on the mechanisms of posterior pole structural changes after strabismus surgery.

Conclusion

Short-term postoperative changes in pRNFL and DFA were observed after horizontal rectus muscle surgery in comitant strabismus. Different surgical extents appeared to show different early postoperative change patterns.

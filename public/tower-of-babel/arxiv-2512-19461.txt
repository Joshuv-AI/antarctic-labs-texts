Secondary cohomology operations and sectional category
Mark Grant

Abstract
. We show how secondary cohomology operations in the total space of the fibred join can be used to give lower bounds for the sectional category of a fibration. This suggests a refinement of the module weight of Iwase–Kono, which we call the secondary module weight . Examples are given for which the secondary module weight at the prime 2 2 detects sectional category while the module weight does not.

1.
Introduction

The
sectional category
of a fibration
q:E\to B
, denoted
\mathsf{secat}(q)
, is defined to be the minimal integer
k
such that
B
admits a cover by open sets
U_{0},\ldots,U_{k}
, on each of which
q
admits a continuous local section
s_{i}:U_{i}\to E

[
21
,
17
]
. Prominent special cases include the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category
\mathsf{cat}(X)

[
6
]
, which is the sectional category of the based path fibration
P_{0}X\to X
, and Farber’s topological complexity
{\sf TC}(X)

[
8
,
9
]
, which is the sectional category of the free path fibration
PX\to X\times X
, as well as its many variants
[
10
,
20
,
5
]
. Note that here we use the normalized version, for which
\mathsf{secat}(q)=0
if and only if
q
admits a (global) section.

An important foundational result due to Schwarz
[
21
]
(see also
[
17
]
) states that if
B
is paracompact then
\mathsf{secat}(q)
agrees with the minimal
k
such that the
(k+1)
-fold fibred join
q(k):E(k)\to B
admits a section. If
F
is the fibre of
q
, then the fibre of
q(k)
is
F(k)=F\ast\cdots\ast F
, the iterated join of
(k+1)
copies of
F
. When
F
is
(r-1)
-connected,
F(k)
is
(r+1)(k+1)-2
-connected. Then (assuming
B
is a CW complex) cellular obstruction theory gives an upper bound

\mathsf{secat}(q)<\frac{\mathrm{dim}(B)+1}{r+1}.

A standard cohomological argument using the open sets definition gives that
\mathsf{secat}(q)
is bounded below by
\operatorname{nil}\operatorname{ker}(q^{*}:H^{*}(B;R)\to H^{*}(E;R))
, the nilpotency of the kernel of the induced map in cohomology. Here we take coefficients in an arbitrary commutative ring
R
(local coefficients may also be used). This bound can sometimes be improved upon using the fibred join interpretation. In particular, we define following Farber–Grant
[
10
,
11
]
the
sectional category weight
of
q
to be

\mathrm{wgt}(q;R)=\min\{k\mid q(k)^{*}:H^{*}(B;R)\to H^{*}(E(k);R)\mbox{ is injective}\}.

Then (since a section
s:B\to E(k)
of
q(k)
clearly implies injectivity of
q(k)^{*}
) one has
\mathsf{secat}(q)\geq\mathrm{wgt}(q;R)
. It can be shown that
\mathrm{wgt}(q;R)\geq\operatorname{nil}\operatorname{ker}(q^{*}:H^{*}(B;R)\to H^{*}(E;R))
, and examples of strict inequality are known.

A further improvement to this lower bound was found by Iwase and Kono
[
16
]
. They observed that a section
s
of
q(k)
, in addition to implying injectivity of the induced map
q(k)^{*}
, would entail a retraction
s^{*}:H^{*}(E(k);R)\to H^{*}(B;R)
which commutes with all cohomology operations. They therefore defined the
module weight
of
q
at the prime
p\geq 2
to be

\mathrm{Mwgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})=\min\{k\mid q(k)^{*}:H^{*}(B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{*}(E(k);\mathbb{F}_{p})\mbox{ admits}\\
\mbox{a retraction in the category of $\mathcal{A}_{p}$-modules}\}.

Here
\mathcal{A}_{p}
denotes the mod
p
Steenrod algebra. Clearly, one has inequalities

\mathsf{secat}(q)\geq\mathrm{Mwgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})\geq\mathrm{wgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})\geq\operatorname{nil}\operatorname{ker}(q^{*}:H^{*}(B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{*}(E;\mathbb{F}_{p}))

for each prime
p
. Examples of strict inequality
\mathrm{Mwgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})>\mathrm{wgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})
can be found in
[
16
,
3
,
4
]
.

The ideas in the previous two paragraphs originated in the work of Fadell and Husseini
[
7
]
on category weight, and were developed in the context of
q
being the based path fibration
P_{0}X\to X
by Rudyak
[
19
]
and Strom
[
23
]
. We have given here the straightforward generalisation to arbitrary fibrations
q:E\to B
.

In this paper we will improve on the module weight using secondary cohomology operations. Recall that whenever stable cohomology operations
\theta
and
\varphi
satisfy a relation
\varphi\circ\theta\equiv 0
, this gives rise to a
secondary cohomology operation

\Phi=\Phi(\varphi,\theta):\operatorname{ker}\,\theta\to\mathrm{coker}\,\varphi

with
indeterminacy

\operatorname{im}\varphi
. Such secondary operations are natural, in the following sense: if
f:X\to Y
is a map of spaces, then the diagram

\textstyle{\operatorname{ker}\,\theta_{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{coker}\,\varphi_{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{ker}\,\theta_{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{\mathrm{coker}\,\varphi_{X}}

commutes. (This makes sense because
f^{*}(\operatorname{ker}\,\theta_{Y})\subseteq\operatorname{ker}\,\theta_{X}
and
f^{*}(\operatorname{im}\varphi_{Y})\subseteq\operatorname{im}\varphi_{X}
.) If we consider only stable cohomology operations with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-coefficients, we can say that
f^{*}:H^{*}(Y;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a map of
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
-modules which commutes with all secondary operations. This leads us to our main definition.

Definition 1.1
.

The
secondary module weight
of the fibration
q:E\to B
at the prime
p
is

\mathrm{Swgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p}):=\min\{k\mid q(k)^{*}:H^{*}(B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{*}(E(k);\mathbb{F}_{p})\mbox{ admits a retraction }\\
\mbox{in the category of $\mathcal{A}_{p}$-modules that commutes with all secondary operations}\}.

We illustrate the utility of this definition by giving examples where
\mathsf{secat}(q)=\mathrm{Swgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})>\mathrm{Mwgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Our first example is the so-called
twistor bundle

\textstyle{S^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}P^{5}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{\mathbb{H}P^{2}}

under which
q
sends a complex line in
\mathbb{C}^{6}\cong\mathbb{H}^{3}
to the quaternionic line in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
containing it. Standard techniques give
1\leq\mathsf{secat}(q)\leq 2
, but are inconclusive in determining the exact value of
\mathsf{secat}(q)
. In fact, it was our attempts to determine whether
q(1)
admits a section using obstruction theory that led us to Definition
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The twistor bundle
q:\mathbb{C}P^{5}\to\mathbb{H}P^{2}
has

2=\mathsf{secat}(q)=\mathrm{Swgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{2})>\mathrm{Mwgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{2})=1>\mathrm{wgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{2})=0.

Theorem
1.2
answers a question on MathOverflow asked by Prateep Chakraborty
[
2
]
. Note that the rational sectional category
\mathsf{secat}_{0}(q)
in this example is
1
, as follows from results of Stanley
[
22
]
.

Our second example concerns Lusternik–Schnirelmann category. As is standard, when
q:P_{0}X\to X
is the based path fibration on a space
X
we denote
\mathrm{wgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
\mathrm{Mwgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\mathrm{Swgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{p})
by
\mathrm{wgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
\mathrm{Mwgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\mathrm{Swgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
, respectively.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\eta\in\pi_{7}(S^{6})
and
\omega\in\pi_{6}(S^{3})
be generators, and let
\alpha\in\pi_{7}(S^{3})
be the homotopy class of the composition
\omega\circ\eta
. If
X=S^{3}\cup_{\alpha}e^{8}
is the homotopy cofibre of
\alpha
, then

2=\mathsf{cat}(X)=\mathrm{Swgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})>\mathrm{Mwgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})=\mathrm{wgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})=1.

In the proofs of both Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, the secondary operation
\Phi
based on the Adem relation
Sq^{3}Sq^{1}+Sq^{2}Sq^{2}\equiv 0
is shown to be nonzero with zero indeterminacy in the total space of the fibred join. In each case, this operation detects the Hopf invariant of the attaching map of the top cell
[
1
,
6
,
14
]
, which turns out to be represented by the class of the composition
\eta\circ\eta\in\pi_{7}(S^{5})
. The main technique to prove non-triviality of
\Phi
is what Harper calls “compatibility with exact sequences” in his book
[
15
]
. This method sometimes allows to compute secondary operations in a space
Y
in terms of primary operations in the other spaces in a cofiber sequence involving
Y
. We review the method in Section 2 below. Thereafter the proofs diverge: Theorem
1.2
uses the Thom isomorphism relating Steenrod squares in the total space of a sphere bundle with Stiefel-Whitney classes, while Theorem
1.3
uses results of Kono–Kozima
[
18
]
on Steenrod squares in the loop space of
\operatorname{Spin}(n)
.

We work in the category of compactly generated spaces with non-degenerate base points. Consider a composition of maps

\mathrm{coker}\,\Omega\varphi_{X}:=[X,\Omega K_{2}]/\operatorname{im}\Omega\varphi_{*},

where
\Omega\varphi_{*}:[X,\Omega K_{1}]\to[X,\Omega K_{2}]

\Omega\varphi_{*}(g)=\Omega\varphi\circ g

Given a composition as in (

H:\varphi\circ\theta\sim\ast

secondary cohomology operation
based on the triple
(\varphi,\theta,H)

\Phi=\Phi(\varphi,\theta,H):\operatorname{ker}\theta_{(-)}\to\mathrm{coker}\,\Omega\varphi_{(-)}.

In most applications the spaces
K_{0}
,
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are products of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces, and
\varphi
and
\theta
represent combinations of Steenrod operations. For example, for each

there are maps
\theta:K(\mathbb{F}_{2},i)\to K(\mathbb{F}_{2},i+1)\times K(\mathbb{F}_{2},i+2)

\begin{pmatrix}Sq^{1}&Sq^{2}\end{pmatrix}:H^{i}(X)\to H^{i+1}(X)\times H^{i+2}(X),\quad x\mapsto\big(Sq^{1}(x),Sq^{2}(x)\big),

\begin{pmatrix}Sq^{3}\\
Sq^{2}\end{pmatrix}:H^{i+1}(X)\times H^{i+2}(X)\to H^{i+4}(X),\quad(x,y)\mapsto Sq^{3}(x)+Sq^{2}(y).

(Here
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-coefficients are understood.) That
\varphi\circ\theta\sim\ast

Sq^{3}Sq^{1}+Sq^{2}Sq^{2}\equiv 0
. There results a homogeneous secondary operation
\Phi:H^{i}(X)\dashrightarrow H^{i+3}(X)

\begin{pmatrix}Sq^{1}&Sq^{2}\end{pmatrix}

\begin{pmatrix}Sq^{3}\\

and

of degree

if for every composable pair of stable operations

and

as above with

\textstyle{\operatorname{ker}\,\theta_{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Our goal is to compute the sectional category of the twistor bundle

\textstyle{S^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}P^{5}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{\mathbb{H}P^{2}.}

The upper bound from the Introduction immediately gives

\mathsf{secat}(q)<\frac{8+1}{2+1}=3,\quad\mbox{or }\mathsf{secat}(q)\leq 2.

For any coefficient ring
R
, the Leray–Serre spectral sequence

H^{p}(\mathbb{H}P^{2};H^{q}(S^{2};R)\implies H^{*}(\mathbb{C}P^{5};R)

is concentrated in even degrees, hence collapses at the
E_{2}
-page. In particular, the map
q^{*}:H^{*}(\mathbb{H}P^{2};R)\to H^{*}(\mathbb{C}P^{5};R)
is injective, which shows that
\mathrm{wgt}(q;R)=0
.

We can see that
q
does not have a section, so that
\mathsf{secat}(q)>0
, by looking at homotopy groups. The long exact homotopy sequences of the fibrations

\textstyle{S^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S^{11}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}P^{5},}

\textstyle{S^{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S^{11}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{H}P^{2},}

imply that
\pi_{4}(\mathbb{C}P^{5})=0
, while
\pi_{4}(\mathbb{H}P^{2})\cong\pi_{3}(S^{3})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Thus there cannot be a section
s
of
q
, since the induced map
\pi_{4}(s)
would be a split injection from
\mathbb{Z}
to
0
.

Thus the question is whether
\mathsf{secat}(q)
is
1
or
2
, or equivalently, whether the
2
-fold fibred join

\textstyle{S^{5}=S^{2}\ast S^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E(1)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q(1)}
\textstyle{\mathbb{H}P^{2}}

admits a section. Note that the Leray–Serre spectral sequence

H^{p}(\mathbb{H}P^{2};H^{q}(S^{5};R))\implies H^{*}(\mathbb{C}P^{5};R)

for
q(1)
again collapses for degree reasons, implying that
q(1)^{*}:H^{*}(\mathbb{H}P^{2};R)\to H^{*}(E(1);R)
is injective.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
q
the twistor bundle,
\mathrm{Mwgt}(q;\mathbb{F}_{2})=1
.

Proof.

Let
H^{*}(-):=H^{*}(-;\mathbb{F}_{2})
. We have

H^{*}(\mathbb{H}P^{2})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[a]/(a^{3}),\qquad|a|=4,

and

is given by

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Examining the Adams–Hilton model for
\Omega X
as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
shows that
H^{*}(X)\to H^{*}(G_{1}(X))
is injective, therefore
\operatorname{wgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})=1
. To see that
\operatorname{Mwgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})\leq 1
we must produce a retraction
H^{*}(G_{1}(X))\to H^{*}(X)
as
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-modules. This is easy enough, since all Steenrod squares in
H^{*}(X)
are trivial and there are no non-trivial Steenrod operations in
H^{*}(G_{1}(X))
taking values in the image of
H^{*}(X)
.

Finally, to see that
\operatorname{Swgt}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})>1
we simply observe that there cannot exist a retraction
H^{*}(G_{1}(X))\to H^{*}(X)
commuting with the secondary operation
\Phi
, since
H^{5}(X)=0
. Since the two-cell complex
X
must have
\mathsf{cat}(X)\leq 2
, this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Similar arguments in

Remark 4.7
.

Note that higher Hopf invariants were not used in the above proof, other than to discover the example. We regard the above example as a “proof-of-concept”, suggesting the secondary module weight may be a useful estimate of category in more complicated examples.

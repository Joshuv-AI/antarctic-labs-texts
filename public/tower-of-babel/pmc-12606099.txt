Observer-based fault tolerant control of shape memory alloy actuator
Authors: Mehdi Mirzaei, Sadra Rafatnia, Mohammad Mohammadi Shahir, Somayeh Jamshidi, Maryam Farbodi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to design and experimentally implement a nonlinear fault tolerant control scheme for shape memory alloy actuator employed for a robotic manipulator. In the proposed scheme, a super twisting sliding mode controller as a baseline controller is firstly developed based on a reduced order model of the overall system. This controller robustly controls the manipulator position in fault-free conditions of the shape memory alloy actuator. Simultaneously, a nonlinear observer is introduced to estimate perturbations and actuator faults. If the estimated perturbation exceeds a predefined threshold for unmodeled system dynamics, a potential actuator fault is detected. When actuator fault is detected, the decision mechanism activates and utilizes observer data to reconfigure the controller, thereby compensating for the fault. The stability of the controller has been demonstrated in the presence of input constraints using the Lyapunov method. Experimental results for healthy actuator demonstrate that the baseline controller maintains robustness against uncertainties and unmodeled dynamics without requiring perturbation estimates. Consequently, the perturbation information enables both fault detection and compensation within a simplified control architecture. Comparative results with a passive fault-tolerant controller indicate the superior performance of the suggested scheme in compensating for faults, uncertainties, and unmodeled dynamics.Keywords: Shape memory alloy actuator, Nonlinear fault tolerant control, Perturbation estimation, Super twisting sliding mode, Robotic manipulatorSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

Shape memory alloys (SMAs) are known as active materials with the ability of returning to their initial shape when heated above a certain temperature1. This kind of material possesses various features such as large deformations, high actuation forces, lightweight, portable size, high displacements, and minimal noise. These characteristics make SMAs significantly useful in different applications, including medical instruments2, robotic systems3, aerodynamics4, and automotive vehicles5. However, the thermal–electrical–mechanical model of SMA is complex and exhibits hysteresis phenomena in their material properties6. Achieving high control accuracy in SMA actuators requires the compensation or rejection of the hysteresis effect7.

Several mathematical models have been developed to describe the behavior of SMAs, such as Preisach model8, Liang model9, Duhem model10, and other empirical models11. However, due to the sensitivity of SMAs to temperature and environmental conditions, these mathematical models cannot precisely provides the hysteresis behavior of SMAs12. An alternative approach to addressing the challenge of obtaining an exact hysteresis model is to use model-free methods. Therefore, various model-free algorithms, such as neural network-based approach13 and neuro-fuzzy network14 have been considered in some researches. However, these methods require a time-consuming off-line training process with different tuning parameters, making them difficult for online implementation. Model-based controllers with the ability of handling the system uncertainties are employed to control SMA behavior. Among them, sliding mode control (SMC) stands out as an efficient algorithm in nonlinear control10. The SMC exhibits high robustness against model uncertainty and unknown environmental disturbances, making it a valuable choice across various fields of study15. However, the chattering phenomenon is identified as the primary drawback of SMC, negatively impacting system performance and leading to high energy consumption5. Several methods have been proposed in the literature to address the chattering issue, such as neural network16, fuzzy logic control17, adaptive methods18, and high-order sliding mode (HOSM) control19. The HOSM control has been widely applied in various practical applications due to its high performance20. One of the most effective methods of HOSM is the super-twisting (ST) algorithm21. This algorithm is highlighted by several features, including the alleviation of chattering, finite-time convergence, and high robustness against uncertainties and disturbances22. Other techniques have also been developed to mitigate the chattering effect. For example, a continuous nonsingular terminal sliding mode was implemented to cancel the effects of the chattering phenomenon7.

In previous researches, due to the lack of an accurate hystersis model for SMA-actuated systems and the complications of availability of several states and parameters of the system, a common strategy was to use reduced-order models for controller design23. However, the unmodelled dynamics causing a mismatch between the actual system and the reduced-order model can lead to large control inputs and a high level of chattering. Therefore, various estimation techniques have been used to compensate for the unmodelled dynamics and other uncertainties of the system. In these methods, sensor measurements are used for application of estimation techniques such as the Kalman filter23,24, time delay estimation25, extended state observer (ESO)26, and other estimation methods27. Beside the modelling uncertainties mentioned above, the actuator fault is another perturbation that may occur in SMA actuated systems, arising due to environmental factors and structural fatigue28. Consequently, the implementation of fault-tolerant control (FTC) considering model uncertainties and environmental disturbances, becomes crucial to maintain system stability and functionality, particularly during hazardous tasks or in hard-to-reach locations29.

There are two primary approaches for fault-tolerant control: passive fault-tolerant control (PFTC) and active fault-tolerant control (AFTC)30. The passive methods involve employing robust control techniques, enabling the closed-loop system to compensate for the impacts of actuator faults, disturbances, and model uncertainties31,32. The design process of passive methods is simple, but they often require high control inputs to handle faults, which can lead to increased energy consumption and excessive wear on actuators. Additionally, these methods are typically designed to handle only a predefined set of faults33. In contrast, active fault-tolerant approaches dynamically reconfigure the nominal controller using real-time fault information provided by a diagnostic observer. These methods offer higher performance compared to PFTC and can cover a wider range of faults in actuators and sensors34. Several advanced fault diagnosis observers were derived, including nonlinear observers utilizing radial basis function (RBF) neural networks35. However, RBF neural networks present several challenges, including high computational complexity, sensitive parameter selection requirements, and intricate training procedures. Traditional proportional–integral observers have been employed as another approach for fault estimation36, but they face limitations in handling nonlinearities, unknown inputs, time delays, and measurement noise. The AFTC strategies have been applied in various applications using different actuators, such as electro-hydraulic37, electromagnetic38, and electrical in wheel motors39. However, while AFTC methods have been successfully implemented in other fields, they have yet to be explored in the context of SMA actuators. This gap presents an opportunity to leverage the unique benefits of AFTC to enhance path tracking in SMA actuators. On the other hand, in most of the AFTC strategies presented in the previous studies, all perturbations including faults, external disturbances and unmodelled dynamics are estimated together as a lumped perturbation. Although this strategy is a simple strategy to compensate for the fault, it is not possible to detect the fault. Upon detecting an actuator fault, the controller can be dynamically reconfigured. This capability enables operators to perform timely maintenance and mitigate the risk of system failure.

Considering the challenges of the SMA-actuated systems reviewed above, the present paper develops a reduced-order algorithm for fault tolerant control of a robot manipulator actuated by a SMA wire in the presence of environmental disturbances. The proposed strategy integrates a super-twisting sliding mode controller (ST-SMC) as a baseline controller with a nonlinear observer to estimate system perturbations, including actuator faults, model uncertainties and external disturbances. The ST-SMC which is designed based on a reduced-order model of the SMA-actuated manipulator, ensures the robustness against un-modelled dynamics and external disturbances in fault-free conditions. Simultaneously with the execution of the baseline controller, the observer uses measurement information to estimate all perturbation of the system. The detection of a fault occurs when the estimated perturbation exceeds the predetermined limits accounting for model uncertainties and external disturbances. Accordingly, the baseline controller is reconfigured to the observer-based controller. In the reconfigured controller for faulty actuator, the estimated perturbation is utilized to mitigate for potential actuator faults. Note that, for a healthy actuator, the baseline controller does not require information about the estimated perturbation due to its intrinsic robustness and ability to achieve desirable tracking. Under faulty conditions, the proposed strategy integrates both fault detection and compensation mechanisms to ensure system reliability. To evaluate the performance of the suggested controller, an experimental setup including an SMA actuator is fabricated for control of the manipulator position across different trajectories. The results indicate that the suggested method exhibits lower control input, higher performance, and greater accuracy compared with a passive fault-tolerant control in following the target trajectory in the presence of faults and uncertainties.

Observer-based fault tolerant control scheme

The earlier section outlined the development of a baseline controller for normal operating conditions with safe actuator. However, in the event of actuator fault, the system demands a fault-tolerant control strategy. This section begins by detailing the construction of an observer capable of estimating system states and perturbations. Subsequently, the methodology for fault detection and diagnosis is introduced. The discussion then shifts to the formulation of a fault-tolerant controller, leveraging data provided by the observer. Once actuator fault is detected, the system transitions from baseline control to the fault-tolerant mode to maintain stability and performance.

Fault-tolerant control (FTC) is designed to ensure that the system can continue to operate safely and effectively even in the presence of actuator fault. Figure 2 presents the overall schematic of the proposed FTC system for the robot manipulator actuated with SMA. The control system is designed to handle external disturbances, modeling mismatching, and fault of actuator. First, a baseline super-twisting sliding mode controller (ST-SMC) is developed for normal operation of actuator with no fault, under the assumption that modelling uncertainties and external disturbances remain within known bounds. However, in the presence of faults, the performance of the baseline controller is reduced. To ensure reliable operation in this situation, a fault detection mechanism is introduced. This mechanism employs an observer to monitor system behavior, triggering fault detection when deviations exceed a predefined threshold accounting for expected uncertainties and disturbances. When a fault is detected, the decision mechanism immediately reconfigures the baseline controller using information obtained by the observer. In this scenario, the system maintains its stability and successfully completes the maneuver despite the detected fault.

Mechanism of fault detection

The observer estimates all system perturbations, encompassing environmental disturbances, unmodeled dynamics, and actuator fault. Here, a specialized fault detection framework for SMA actuator is introduced to detect the occurrence of an actuator fault. Upon fault detection, the system seamlessly transitions to a fault-tolerant control mode, where the perturbation estimated by the observer is actively employed by the control system to compensate for faults and other disturbances. This capability not only ensures system resilience but also enables timely maintenance, preventing unexpected failure of the actuator.

The proposed fault detection mechanism leverages the bounded nature of external disturbances and parametric uncertainties to establish a perturbation threshold for normal operations under safe actuator. In the other words, during fault-free conditions, the estimated perturbation comprising external disturbances and SMA unmodeled dynamics, is below the predetermined threshold (), and the baseline controller maintains the control performance due to its inherent robustness against these perturbations. Exceeding the estimated perturbation over the threshold () indicates the occupance of fault in the actuator. In this condition, the control system automatically switches to fault-tolerant mode, where the controller directly uses the observer’s outcome to compensate for the detected fault and other perturbations.The threshold is determined by different experiments with safe actuator.

Conclusion

Due to the lack of an accurate hysteresis model for the SMA-actuated manipulator and the complications of availability of several states and parameters of the actuator model, a reduced-order model is employed for the design of ST-SMC as a baseline controller. This controller is robust against un-modelled dynamics and other uncertainties in fault-free conditions. The fault in the SMA wire is detected by estimating all perturbations of the system. By using information of the perturbation observer, not only the fault is detected, but also it is completely compensated within a simple structure with few practical limitations. Experimental studies are employed to assess the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. The results show that the proposed algorithm has good features, such as fast transient response and low tracking error in the presence of the uncertainties due to un-modelled dynamics and actuator faults. Importantly, reconfigured control input increases the tracking accuracy and compensates for the actuator fault.

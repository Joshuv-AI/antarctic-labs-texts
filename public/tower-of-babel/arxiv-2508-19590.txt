Global regularity of Leray-Hopf weak solutions to 3D Navier-Stokes equations
Myong-Hwan Ri

Abstract
We show that any Leray-Hopf weak solution to 3D Navier-Stokes equations with initial values u 0 ∈ H 1 / 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) u_{0}\in H^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}) belong to L ∞ ​ ( 0 , ∞ , H 1 / 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) ) L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})) and thus it is regular. For the proof, first, we construct a supercritical space, the norm of which is compared to the homogeneous Sobolev H ˙ 1 / 2 \dot{H}^{1/2} -norm in that it has inverse logarithmic weight very sparsely in the frequency domain. Then we obtain the energy estimates of high frequency parts of the solution which involve the supercritical norm on the right-hand side. Finally, we superpose the energy norm of high frequency parts of the solution to get estimates of the critical norms of the weak solution via the re-scaling argument.



Keywords:
Navier-Stokes equations; Leray-Hopf weak
solution; regularity;
supercritical space.

Introduction and main result

Let us consider the Cauchy problem for the 3D Navier-Stokes
equations:

\begin{array}[]{rl}u_{t}-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\[4.30554pt]
{\rm{div}}\,u=0&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\[4.30554pt]
u(0,x)=u_{0}&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\end{array}

(1.1)

where
u
,
p
are velocity and pressure, respectively, and
\nu
is
the kinematic viscosity of the observed viscous incompressible
fluid.

Since Leray
[
9
]
proved existence of a global weak solution
(Leray-Hopf weak solution) to
(
1.1
)
such that the weak solution

u\in L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))

satisfies
(
1.1
)
in a weak sense and the
energy inequality

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2},\;\forall t\in(0,\infty),\end{array}

(1.2)

the problem of global regularity of the weak solution has been a
consistent key issue.

There is a great number of articles on scaling-invariant regularity
criteria for weak solutions to
(
1.1
)
.
We recall that the Navier-Stokes equations are
invariant by the scaling
u_{\lambda}(t,x)\equiv\lambda u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\lambda>0,
and
a critical space for the equations is the space whose norm is invariant with respect to
the scaling

u(x)\mapsto\lambda u(\lambda x),\;\lambda>0.

The following embedding holds
between critical spaces for the 3D Navier-Stokes equations:

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{H}^{1/2}\hookrightarrow L^{3}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{-1+3/p}_{p,\infty}\hookrightarrow BMO^{-1}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{-1}_{\infty,\infty},\;3\leq p<\infty.\end{array}

Eskauraiza, Seregin and Svérak in
[
3
]
(2003) proved that a Leray-Hopf weak solution

u
to
(
1.1
)
is regular in
(0,T]
if

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})),

employing the backward uniqueness property of parabolic equations.
By developing a profile decomposition technique and using the method of “critical
elements” developed in
[
6
]
-
[
8
]
, Gallagher, Koch and Planchon proved
for a mild solution
u
to
(
1.1
)
with initial values in
L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
that a potential
singularity at
t=T
implies
\lim_{t\rightarrow T-0}\|u(t)\|_{3}=\infty
in
[
4
]
(2013), and extended the result
from
L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to wider critical Besov spaces

\dot{B}^{-1+3/p}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
3<p,q<\infty

in
[
5
]
(2016).
In
[
11
]
(2023), the author proved that the above results can be extended
to the largest critical space
\dot{B}^{-1}_{\infty,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, that is,
a Leray-Hopf weak solution to
(
1.1
)
satisfying

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\dot{B}^{-1}_{\infty,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))

is regular in
(0,T]
by developing a method of energy superposition of high frequency parts
of the weak solution.

Concerning supercritical regularity criteria, we mention recent
results
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
by Barker and Prange that a
Leray-Hopf weak solution
u
satisfying the local energy inequality
is regular in
(0,T]
if

\sup_{0<t<T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{|u(x,t)|^{3}}{\Big(\log\log\log\big(\big(\log(e^{e^{3e^{e}}}+|u(x,t)|)\big)^{1/3}\big)\Big)^{\theta}}dx<\infty,\forall\theta\in(0,1),

based on a Tao’s result
[
13
,
14
]
on
quantitative bound of the critical
L^{3}
-norm near a possible
blow-up epoch for a weak solution to
(
1.1
)
; we also mention Pan

[
10
]
and Seregin
[
12
]
where logarithmically
supercritical regularity criteria for axisymmetric suitable weak
solutions to
(
1.1
)
are obtained.

In this paper, we prove that a Leray-Hopf weak solution to
(
1.1
)

with initial value in the critical space
\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is
regular.

The main result of the paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Let
u
be a Leray-Hopf weak solution to
(
1.1
)

with
u_{0}\in H^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
{\rm{div}}\,u_{0}=0
.
Then,

u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{3/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))

and the estimate

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{1/2}}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{2},\quad\forall t\in(0,\infty),

(1.3)

holds. Thus
u
is globally regular.

We use the following notations. The sets of all natural numbers and
all integers are denoted by
{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}
, respectively. The usual

L^{p}
-norm for
1\leq p\leq\infty
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.
Three-dimensional Fourier transform of a function
u
is given by

{\cal F}u\equiv\hat{u}:=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{u}(t,x)e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,dx
and
{\cal F}^{-1}u\equiv\check{u}
. For

s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
stand for the
homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces with norms

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\equiv\||\xi|^{s}\hat{u}(\xi)\|_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\equiv\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{u}(\xi)\|_{2},

respectively.
We use the notation

\begin{array}[]{l}u^{k}(t,x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int_{|\xi|\geq k}{\mathcal{F}}{u}(t,\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi,\\[4.30554pt]
u_{k}:=u-u^{k},\;u_{h,k}:=u^{h}-u^{k}\quad\text{for }0\leq h<k.\end{array}

(1.4)

We do not distinguish between the spaces of vectorial functions and
scalar functions.

Let us explain the ideas for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. As is
well-known, the cancelation property
((v\cdot\nabla)u,u)=0
for
suitably smooth
u,v
with
{\rm{div}}\,v=0
is the key to obtain global
existence of the Leray-Hopf weak solutions to
(
1.1
)
. However,
this innermost property was not used, so long as the author knows,
in most previous works for regularity. Indeed, as long as the
convection term
(u\cdot\nabla)u
is treated as merely a quadratic
nonlinear term, whatever improvement of its estimate could be made,
regularity of the weak solution is obtained under a smallness
condition of a critical norm of initial values or under additional
scaling-invariant conditions on the weak solution itself.

In order to circumvent such situations, we use essentially the
cancelation property by testing the momentum equation with high frequency parts
u^{k}
,

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In that case, by the cancelation
((u\cdot\nabla)u^{k},u^{k})=0
,
we are led to the kind of estimates

\frac{d}{2dt}\|u^{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla u^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq c(k)\|u\|_{X_{1}}\|\nabla u^{k/2}\|_{2}^{2},\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}},

with a supercritical space
X_{1}
,
where
c(k)
depends on the topology of
X_{1}
.
Though this estimate, at the first glance, still seems of no special
use due to the factor
c(k)
, we pay attention to the fact that

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|u^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\sim\|u^{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2},\quad\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|\nabla u^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\sim\|\nabla u^{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}

and

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}c(k)\|\nabla u^{k/2}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}c(k)\Big)\|\nabla u_{n,n+1}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus, if we can construct a supercritical space
X_{1}
such that the
averaging condition
\sum_{k=1}^{n}c(k)\lesssim n
is satisfied, then
we are led to

\frac{d}{2dt}\|u^{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla u^{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{X_{1}}(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u^{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}),

which can finally yields the estimate of

\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}
provided uniform smallness of

\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{1}}
in
\tau\in[0,t]
is guaranteed by re-scaling.

We successfully construct a supercritical space
X_{1}
with the
above-mentioned averaging condition and the uniform smallness
property by re-scaling, which has a very sparse inverse logarithmic
weight in the frequency domain compared to the critical

\dot{H}^{1/2}
-norm, Section 2.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section 3.

2
A frequency-weighted scaling-variant space

In this section, we introduce a supercritical space, the norm of
which is weaker than that of
\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, being very close
to it.

Let

\Delta_{j}u=(\chi_{j}(\xi)\hat{u}(\xi))^{\vee}\quad\text{for }j\in{\mathbb{Z}},u\in{\cal S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\;\text{with }\hat{u}\in L_{loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}^{3}),

where
\chi_{j}
is the characteristic function of the set

\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|<2^{j}\}
.
For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, by Plancherel’s theorem,

\begin{array}[]{l}2^{2s(j-1)}\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{2}^{2}=2^{2s(j-1)}\int_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|<2^{j}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad\leq\int_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|<2^{j}}|\xi|^{2s}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq 2^{2sj}\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{2}^{2},\forall u\in\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\end{array}

and the homogeneous Sobolev norm
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
is
equivalent to the norm

\big(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|2^{sj}\Delta_{j}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\big)^{1/2}.

(2.1)

Let an infinite sequence

\{a(j)\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be such that

a(j):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\log_{2}j&\text{if
}j=i+2^{2^{k}}\quad\text{for some }k\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in{\mathbb{Z}},-k\leq i\leq k,\\[4.30554pt]
1&\text{else}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Let us define the space
X_{1}
by

\begin{array}[]{l}X_{1}:=\{v\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}):\hat{v}(\xi)\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}^{3}),\;\{2^{j/2}a^{-1}(j)\|\Delta_{j}v\|_{2}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in l_{2}\},\\[4.30554pt]
\|v\|_{X_{1}}:=\Big\|\{2^{j/2}a^{-1}(j)\|\Delta_{j}v\|_{2}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big\|_{l_{2}},\end{array}

(2.3)

where the sequence
\{a(j)\}
is given by
(
2.2
)
.

Obviously, it holds that
a(j)=1
for
j\leq 0
and

\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\hookrightarrow X_{1},\quad\|v\|_{X_{1}}\leq\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},\forall v\in\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

We pursue supercritical properties of the space
X_{1}
via the next
lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(i) Let
v\in\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Then,

\begin{array}[]{l}\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists l_{0}:=\max\{M+1,\log_{2}\big[\frac{\log_{2}M\|v\|_{X_{1}}}{\varepsilon}\big]+1\}>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\|\lambda v(\lambda\cdot)\|_{X_{1}}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\forall\lambda=2^{2^{2^{l}}}(l\geq l_{0}),\end{array}

where
M>0
is such that

\sum_{|j|\geq M}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

(2.4)

(ii) Let
u\in C\big([0,T],\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\big)
with

0<T<\infty
. Then, for any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
there exists

l_{0}>0
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\|\lambda u(t,\lambda\cdot)\|_{X_{1}}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],\;\forall\lambda=2^{2^{2^{l}}}(l\geq l_{0}).\end{array}

Proof:
–
Proof of (i)
:
Let
v\in\dot{H}^{1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and fix
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily. Then, in
view of

\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\sim\Big\|\{2^{j/2}\|\Delta_{j}v\|_{2}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big\|_{l_{2}},

there is
M=M(\varepsilon,v)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\sum_{|j|\geq M}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

(2.5)

In the proof, for the moment, we use a short notation
v_{\lambda}:=\lambda v(\lambda\cdot)
.
By definition of the space
X_{1}
we have

\|v_{\lambda}\|_{X_{1}}=\Big\|\{2^{j/2}a^{-1}(j)\|\Delta_{j}v_{\lambda}\|_{2}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big\|_{l_{2}}.

Observe that
({\mathcal{F}}v_{\lambda})(\xi)=\lambda({\mathcal{F}}v(\lambda\cdot))(\xi)=\lambda^{-2}(\mathcal{F}v)(\frac{\xi}{\lambda})
for all

\lambda>0

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{j}v_{\lambda}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|<2^{j}}{\cal F}v_{\lambda}(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi\\[12.91663pt]

\qquad=\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int_{\lambda^{-1}2^{j-1}\leq|\eta|<\lambda^{-1}2^{j}}{\cal F}v(\eta)e^{i\lambda x\cdot\eta}\,d\eta.\end{array}

Hence, if

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{j}v_{\lambda}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int_{2^{j-2^{2^{l}}-1}\leq|\eta|<2^{j-2^{2^{l}}}}{\cal F}v(\eta)e^{i\lambda x\cdot\eta}\,d\eta\\[12.91663pt]
\qquad=\lambda(\Delta_{j-2^{2^{l}}}v)(\lambda x),\end{array}

(2.6)

yielding

\begin{array}[]{l}2^{j/2}\Delta_{j}v_{\lambda}(x)=2^{j/2+2^{2^{l}}}(\Delta_{j-2^{2^{l}}}v)(\lambda x),\;\forall j\in{\mathbb{Z}}.\end{array}

Note that

\|(\Delta_{j-2^{2^{l}}}v)(\lambda\cdot)\|_{2}=\lambda^{-3/2}\|\Delta_{j-2^{2^{l}}}v\|_{2}.

Therefore, by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle 2^{j/2}\|\Delta_{j}v_{\lambda}\|_{2}&=&2^{j/2+2^{2^{l}}}\|(\Delta_{j-2^{2^{l}}}v)(\lambda\cdot)\|_{2}\\[4.30554pt]

(2.7)

and, in view of
(
2.5
)
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\|v_{\lambda}\|_{X_{1}}^{2}=\displaystyle\Big\|\big\{2^{j/2}a^{-1}(j)\|\Delta_{j}v_{\lambda}\|_{2}\big\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big\|_{l_{2}}^{2}\\[8.61108pt]

Here, for
l\geq M+1
, by definition of
a(j)
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\big\|\{\frac{a(j)}{a(j+2^{2^{l}})}2^{j/2}a^{-1}(j)\|\Delta_{j}v\|_{2}\}_{-M\leq j\leq M}\big\|_{l_{2}}\\[4.30554pt]

Therefore, for all

(2.8)

we have

– Proof of (ii)

Proof of the main theorem

Lemma 3.1

Let

b(j)\equiv 2^{-j-1}\sum_{i=1}^{j}2^{i}a(i),\;j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(3.1)

Then, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}

b(j)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\log_{2}j&\text{if }-k+2^{2^{k}}\leq j\leq 2^{2^{k}}+2k\quad\text{for some }k\in{\mathbb{N}},\\[4.30554pt]
2&\text{else}.\end{array}\right.

Proof:

Note that
a(j)\geq 1
, and

b(j+1)=\frac{1}{2}(b(j)+a(j+1))

First we prove the lemma for

\begin{array}[]{l}b(1)=2^{-2}\cdot 2\cdot a(1)=\frac{1}{2}<a(1)=1,\\[4.30554pt]
b(2)=2^{-3}\cdot(2+2^{2})=\frac{3}{4}<a(2),\\[4.30554pt]
b(3)=\frac{b(2)+a(3)}{2}=\frac{3+4\log_{2}3}{8}\leq\log_{2}3=a(3),\\[4.30554pt]
b(4)=\frac{b(3)+a(4)}{2}\leq 2=a(4).\end{array}

(3.2)

Next, let us fix any

and prove the lemma for

. Let

b(j_{1}+2)=\frac{b(j_{1}+1)}{2}+\frac{\cdot a(j_{1}+2)}{2}\leq\frac{a(j+1)}{2}+\frac{a(j_{1}+2)}{2}\leq a(j_{1}+2)=\log_{2}(j_{1}+2),\\[4.30554pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\\[4.30554pt]
b(j_{1}+l)=\frac{b(j_{1}+l-1)}{2}+\frac{a(j_{1}+l)}{2}\leq a(j_{1}+l)=\log_{2}(j_{1}+l).\end{array}

Moreover, for
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
with

we have

\begin{array}[]{l}b(j_{1}+k+1)=\frac{b(k+2^{2^{k}})}{2}+\frac{a(k+2^{2^{k}}+1)}{2}\leq\frac{a(k+2^{2^{k}})}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\log_{2}(k+2^{2^{k}})}{2}+\frac{1}{2},\\[4.30554pt]
b(j_{1}+k+2)=\frac{b(k+2^{2^{k}}+1)}{2}+\frac{a(k+2^{2^{k}}+2)}{2}\leq\frac{\log_{2}(k+2^{2^{k}})}{4}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2},\\[4.30554pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\\[4.30554pt]
b(j_{1}+k+l)=\frac{b(k+2^{2^{k}}+l-1)}{2}+\frac{a(k+2^{2^{k}}+l)}{2}\leq\frac{\log_{2}(k+2^{2^{k}})}{2^{l}}+\frac{1}{2^{l}}+\cdots\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}

Hence, if

Note that

Consequently,

\begin{array}[]{l}b(j_{1}+k+l)\leq\log_{2}(k+2^{2^{k}}),\quad\text{for }l=1,\ldots,k,\\[4.30554pt]
b(j_{1}+k+l)\leq 2,\quad\text{for }l=k+1,\ldots,2^{2^{k+1}}-(2k+2^{2^{k}}).\end{array}

(3.3)

Finally, we shall prove

b(j)\leq\log_{2}j\quad\text{for }2^{2^{k+1}}-k+1\leq j\leq 2^{2^{k+1}}.

(3.4)

In fact, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}b(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+1)=\frac{b(2^{2^{k+1}}-k)}{2}+\frac{a(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+1)}{2}\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad\qquad\leq 1+\frac{\log_{2}(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+1)}{2}\leq\log_{2}(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+1),\\[4.30554pt]
b(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+2)=\frac{b(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+1)}{2}+\frac{a(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+2)}{2}\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{\log_{2}(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+1)}{2}+\frac{\log_{2}(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+2)}{2}\leq\log_{2}(2^{2^{k+1}}-k+2),\\[4.30554pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\\[4.30554pt]

\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{\log_{2}(2^{2^{k+1}})}{2}+\frac{\log_{2}(2^{2^{k+1}}-1)}{2}\leq 2^{k+1}=\log_{2}{2^{2^{k+1}}}=a(2^{2^{k+1}}).\end{array}

Here, in particular,

3.2
)

\sim

(

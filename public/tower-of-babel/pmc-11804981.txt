Evaluation of the Accuracy of Intraoral Digital Impression Systems and CAD and CAM Workflows for Fabricating Dental Prostheses
Authors: Ojas Anand Gajbhiye, M. Pradeep Reddy, Md. Imran, Nikhita Narayanan, Praveen Gangadharappa, Harisha Dewan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Dental prosthesis manufacturing has undergone a radical change with the introduction of intraoral digital impression technologies and computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD and CAM) processes. Investigations into these systems’ accuracy in creating dental restorations that adhere to clinical standards are still underway.Materials and Methods:For this investigation, a total of 60 patients in need of single-unit crowns were chosen. They were divided into three groups of 20: Group A used intraoral digital impression systems, Group B used conventional impression procedures, and Group C combined digital impressions with CAD and CAM processes. The marginal fit and internal fit of the crowns were compared to assess the correctness of the dental prosthesis. A stereomicroscope was used to evaluate the marginal fit, and micro-CT imaging was employed to analyze the interior fit. ANOVA and Tukey’s post hoc test were used to examine the data, with a significance threshold of P < 0.05.Results:The mean marginal fit was 120 ± 15 μm for Group A, 80 ± 10 μm for Group B, and 75 ± 8 μm for Group C, according to the data. The internal fit values were 150 ± 20 μm for Group A, 90 ± 12 μm for Group B, and 85 ± 10 μm for Group C. According to statistical analysis, Group B and Group C outperformed Group A in terms of accuracy in both internal and marginal fits (P < 0.01). There was no discernible difference between Group B and Group C.Conclusion:When constructing dental prostheses, intraoral digital impression technologies and CAD and CAM workflows show better accuracy over conventional impression techniques. The greatest clinical results are obtained when digital impressions and CAD and CAM workflows are combined, indicating that these technologies need to be accepted as routine procedures in prosthetic dentistry.

INTRODUCTION

The production of dental prostheses has advanced significantly with the use of digital technology in dentistry. Intraoral digital imprint systems and computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD and CAM) workflows are becoming more and more popular in clinical practice because they may increase accuracy, decrease chair time, and improve patient comfort.[12] Even though they are still often used, traditional impression techniques have drawbacks, such as the possibility of dimensional alterations in the imprint material that might result in inaccurate final prosthesis.[3]

By instantly taking a digital picture of the tooth preparation, digital imprint systems reduce the sources of error associated with old procedures by doing away with the requirement for traditional impression materials and trays.[4] These technologies provide a simplified and effective workflow by enabling the direct design and machining of prostheses from digital files when paired with CAD and CAM technology.[5] Research has shown that digital workflows may provide prostheses with superior marginal and internal fit, both of which are vital components of tooth restoration lifespan and clinical efficacy.[67]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

This study was designed as a comparative clinical trial to evaluate the accuracy of intraoral digital impression systems and CAD and CAM workflows in fabricating dental prostheses. A total of 60 patients requiring single-unit crowns on posterior teeth were selected for this study. Inclusion criteria included patients aged 18–65 years, in good general health, and requiring a crown on a molar or premolar. Exclusion criteria included patients with severe periodontal disease, poor oral hygiene, or a history of temporomandibular joint disorders. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their inclusion in the study.

Group allocation

Participants were randomly divided into three groups of 20 patients each:

Group A: Conventional impression technique using polyvinyl siloxane (PVS) material.Group B: Intraoral digital impression system (TRIOS 3, 3Shape).Group C: Intraoral digital impression system combined with CAD and CAM workflow (CEREC, Dentsply Sirona).

Impression and prosthesis fabrication

For Group A, traditional impressions were taken using polyvinyl siloxane material and poured into Type IV dental stone to create the working models.

In Group B, digital impressions were captured using the TRIOS 3 intraoral scanner.

In Group C, digital impressions were captured using the TRIOS 3 intraoral scanner, and the CAD and CAM crowns were designed and milled chairside using the CEREC system from lithium disilicate blocks.

Evaluation of marginal and internal fit

The accuracy of the crowns was evaluated by measuring the marginal fit and internal fit. Marginal fit was assessed by examining the crowns under a stereomicroscope (Leica M80, Leica Microsystems) at 50× magnification. The internal fit was evaluated using micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) imaging (SkyScan 1172, Bruker) with a resolution of 18 μm.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software version 26.0 (IBM Corp.).

Marginal fit

Table 1 shows the mean marginal fit (in micrometers) for the crowns in each group. The lowest marginal gap was observed in Group C, followed closely by Group B, while Group A exhibited the largest marginal gaps.

Internal fit

Table 2 presents the mean internal fit (in micrometers) for the crowns across the three groups. Similar to the marginal fit, Group C exhibited the best internal fit, with Group B also showing favorable results. Group A had the highest internal gaps.

Statistical analysis

One-way ANOVA revealed a statistically significant difference in both marginal and internal fit among the three groups (P < 0.01). Post hoc Tukey’s test indicated that Group B and Group C had significantly better marginal and internal fit compared to Group A (P < 0.01). There was no statistically significant difference between Group B and Group C in either marginal or internal fit (P > 0.05).

DISCUSSION

For dental restorations to last a long time, the marginal fit is essential because a poor fit may cause microleakage, cement breakdown, and eventually secondary caries or periodontal disease.[3] A tighter seal and potentially improved clinical results were indicated by the study’s finding that the average marginal fit in the digital impression groups was much less than in the traditional group. Other research comparing digital and conventional impression procedures has shown comparable findings, with digital impressions consistently producing lower marginal gaps.[45]

Equally crucial is internal fit, as large gaps may weaken the restoration’s mechanical integrity and cause fractures or debonding.[6] This study’s digital impression groups showed a better internal match than the traditional group, which is consistent with other studies.[7] The reduction of factors related to traditional impression materials, such as shrinkage, expansion, and distortion during impression taking and model production, is responsible for the better fit obtained with digital processes.

Surprisingly, there was no discernible difference in marginal or internal fit between Group B (digital impression alone) and Group C (digital impression with CAD and CAM process). This shows that although CAD and CAM processes improve prosthesis manufacturing speed and efficiency, the quality of the digital imprint itself ultimately determines how accurate the final restoration will be. This is consistent with research that demonstrates the scanning procedure is a crucial factor in determining the restoration’s overall correctness.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, this study provides strong evidence that intraoral digital impression systems, especially when combined with CAD and CAM workflows, offer significant advantages in the fabrication of dental prostheses.

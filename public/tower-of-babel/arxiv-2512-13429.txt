Two Families of Linear Codes Containing Non-GRS MDS Codes
Kanat Abdukhalikov Department of Mathematical Sciences, UAE University, PO Box 15551, Al Ain, UAE Email: abdukhalik@uaeu.ac.ae Gyanendra K. Verma Department of Mathematical Sciences, UAE University, PO Box 15551, Al Ain, UAE Email: gkvermaiitdmaths@gmail.com

Abstract
We construct two new families of linear codes by modifying the generator matrices of generalized Reed-Solomon (GRS) codes. For these codes, we explicitly derive parity-check matrices and establish necessary and sufficient conditions ensuring the MDS property. Additionally, we explore subfamilies within these constructions that are non-GRS MDS codes. We also characterize their self-orthogonal and self-dual properties and present some explicit constructions and examples.



Keywords
: MDS codes, GRS codes, non-GRS MDS codes, parity check matrix, self-orthogonal codes, self-dual codes .

Mathematics subject classification
: 94B05, 94B65.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a prime power. A linear code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters
[n,k,d]
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with dimension
k
and minimum Hamming distance
d
. The parameters of a linear code
C
are constrained by the Singleton bound
d\leq n-k+1
. Codes attaining the bound, that is,
d=n-k+1
, are called maximum distance separable (MDS) codes. The study of MDS codes has been driven by their diverse applications, ranging from distributed storage systems and combinatorial designs to finite geometry, random error channels, and secret sharing schemes (see
[
7
,
17
,
25
,
29
]
). Due to these applications, the properties of MDS codes have been extensively investigated, including their existence, classification, and balanced variants
[
12
,
13
,
19
]
.

A linear MDS code is equivalent to an arc in finite projective geometry. Namely, an
[n,k]
linear code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
generated by matrix
G
is an MDS code if and only if the set consisting of columns of
G
as coordinate vectors is an arc of size
n
in projective space
PG(k-1,q)
(see
[
3
]
). Segre
[
30
]
proposed an upper bound on the maximum size of an arc in
PG(k-1,q)
, known as the MDS conjecture, which states that if
3\leq k\leq q-1
, then an arc in
PG(k-1,q)

has size at most
q+1
except for the case when
q
is even and
k=3
or
k=q-1
where arcs of size
q+2
exist. Equivalently, in the language of coding theory, for an
[n,k,d]
MDS code where
3\leq d<n
, the length
n
satisfies
n\leq q+1
, unless
k\in\{3,q-1\}
and
q
is even, in which case
n\leq q+2
. The conjecture has been proved for several particular cases
[
3
]
; for instance, Ball
[
2
]
proved that the conjecture is true over prime fields. The general case of the conjecture remains open. A prominent and well-studied family of MDS codes is the class of generalized Reed-Solomon (GRS) codes
[
25
]
. GRS codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can have length at most
q+1
. The GRS codes of length
q+1
correspond to normal rational curves in projective spaces. All known
(q+1)
-arcs in
PG(k-1,q)
,
k\geq 4
, are normal rational curves (equivalently, GRS codes) except for the cases
k=5
,
q=9
(Glynn
[
15
]
) and
k=4
,
q=2^{m}
(Segre arcs
[
9
]
). This leads to a conjecture proposed in
[
3
]
, for
6\leq k\leq q-5
, all
q+1
size arcs in
PG(k-1,q)
are GRS codes. In view of the above statements, the study of non-GRS MDS codes remains interesting both from the perspective of theoretical classification of arcs in finite projective spaces (equivalently, MDS codes) and for practical applications in cryptography. For example, non-GRS MDS codes have applications in providing resistance against Sidelnikov-Shestakov and Wieschebrink attacks in cryptographic systems, where GRS codes do not offer such cryptographic security
[
4
,
20
]
. Formally, MDS codes that are not (monomial) equivalent to GRS codes are called non-generalized Reed-Solomon (non-GRS) MDS codes.

Roth and Lempel
[
28
]
were the first to study non-GRS MDS codes by augmenting the generator matrices of GRS codes with two additional columns. The study of non-GRS MDS codes later gained renewed interest through the work of Beelen et al.
[
4
,
5
]
, who introduced twisted Reed-Solomon (TRS) codes and demonstrated that certain families of these codes contain non-GRS MDS codes. Recently, in
[
6
,
18
,
21
,
23
,
22
,
32
,
33
]
and references therein, the authors investigated several families of non-GRS MDS codes using a variety of techniques. These studies have led to the introduction of several new classes of codes, including twisted Reed–Solomon (TRS) codes, generalized twisted Reed–Solomon (GTRS) codes, non-GTRS codes, column twisted and row-column twisted Reed-Solomon codes that contain non-GRS MDS codes. In
[
16
,
21
,
33
]
, the authors studied the codes obtained by deleting the penultimate row of the GRS code generator matrices and extending the resulting codes. These constructions produced families of non-GRS MDS or NMDS codes that cannot be derived from GTRS codes. Furthermore, in
[
1
,
18
]
, the authors investigated the codes constructed by removing an arbitrary row from GRS code generator matrices and their extensions, established necessary and sufficient conditions for these codes to be non-GRS MDS codes, and provided explicit constructions.

A linear code is called a self-orthogonal code if
C\subseteq C^{\perp}
and self-dual if
C=C^{\perp}
, where
C^{\perp}
is the dual code of
C
. Self-dual codes have connections with combinatorics and lattice theory, along with applications in cryptography (see
[
10
,
11
,
14
,
25
]
), while self-orthogonal codes are particularly significant in quantum coding theory (for instance, see
[
8
,
31
]
). The study of self-orthogonal and self-dual codes has thus become important both for advancing theoretical understanding and for enabling practical applications in classical and quantum coding. Recently, considerable attention has been given to the construction of self-orthogonal and self-dual MDS and NMDS codes, resulting in new families of codes with desirable properties and enhanced applicability (see
[
16
,
21
]
and references therein). These developments not only enrich the classification of MDS and NMDS codes but also provide valuable tools for secure communication, reliable data storage, and quantum information processing.

Building on these previous developments, in this paper, we propose two new families of linear codes that contain non-GRS MDS codes, namely
C_{k-1,k-2}
and
C_{h,k}
. Our contributions are as follows. We determine the parameters of
C_{k-1,k-2}
and
C_{h,k}
and establish necessary and sufficient conditions for these codes to be MDS. We explicitly determine parity-check matrices for
C_{k-1,k-2}
and
C_{h,k}
. We also investigate their non-GRS properties, using the Schur square method. In addition, we investigate self-orthogonal and self-dual properties of these codes by providing necessary and sufficient conditions. Furthermore, we provide several explicit examples and constructions to illustrate and validate our results.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall basic definitions and introduce the notation used throughout the paper. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to the construction of two families of linear codes, where we study their MDS and non-GRS properties, and explicitly derive their parity-check matrices. The theory of sysmmetric functions
[
24
]
is very useful in our investigations. Furthermore, we investigate the self-orthogonal and self-dual properties of these codes in respective sections. In Section
5
, we conclude the paper and discuss potential directions for future work.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we denote
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a prime power, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
. A
k\times n
matrix
G
is called a generator matrix for a linear code
C
with parameters
[n,k]
if the rows of
G
form a basis of
C
. Two codes
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
are said to be (monomial) equivalent if
C_{1}
can be obtained from
C_{2}
by permutation of coordinates and multiplying coordinates by a nonzero scalar. The dual of
C
is defined as

C^{\perp}=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}:\langle y,c\rangle=0,\forall c\in C\},

where
\langle y,c\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}c_{i}
is the Euclidean inner product. The code
C
is called self-orthogonal if
C\subseteq C^{\perp}
and called self-dual if
C=C^{\perp}
. Moreover, if the code
C
is self-dual, then
n=2k
.
The Singleton bound states that
d\leq n-k+1
, where
d
is the Hamming distance of
C
, defined as

d(C)=\min_{0\neq c\in C}\{wt(c):wt(c)=\text{number of nonzero coordinates in }c\}.

The code
C
is said to be an MDS code if
d=n-k+1
.
A well-known class of MDS codes is generalized Reed-Solomon (GRS) codes defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

For
n\leq q
, let
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
. The generalized Reed-Solomon (GRS) code is defined as

GRS(n,k,\Lambda,v)=\{(v_{1}f(\alpha_{1}),v_{2}f(\alpha_{2}),\dots,v_{n}f(\alpha_{n}))\mid f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x],\deg(f(x))\leq k-1\}.

In particular, when
v=(1,1,\dots,1)
, the code is called a Reed-Solomon (RS) code denoted by
RS(n,k,\Lambda)
. Note that the code
GRS(n,k,\Lambda,v)
and
RS(n,k,\Lambda)
are equivalent. A generator matrix for
GRS(n,k,\Lambda,v)
code is

G=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}&v_{2}&\dots&v_{n}\\
v_{1}\alpha_{1}&v_{2}\alpha_{2}&\dots&v_{n}\alpha_{n}\\
v_{1}\alpha_{1}^{2}&v_{2}\alpha_{2}^{2}&\dots&v_{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots\\
v_{1}\alpha_{1}^{k-1}&v_{2}\alpha_{2}^{k-1}&\dots&v_{n}\alpha_{n}^{k-1}\end{bmatrix}_{k\times n}.

Definition 2.2
.

A non-GRS MDS code is an MDS code that is not equivalent to a GRS code.

The Schur product of two vectors
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is defined as

x\star y=(x_{1}y_{1},x_{2}y_{2},\dots,x_{n}y_{n}).

Let
C_{1},C_{2}
be codes with parameters
[n,k]
. Then the Schur product
C_{1}\star C_{2}
is defined as

C_{1}\star C_{2}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{c_{1}\star c_{2}:c_{1}\in C_{1},c_{2}\in C_{2}\}.

If
C_{1}=C_{2}
, then the Schur product is called the Schur square. The following lemma provides a characterization of GRS codes using the Schur square.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1) If
C
is an
[n,k]
GRS code with
\dim(C)\leq n/2
, then
\dim(C\star C)=2\dim(C)-1
.

(2)
[
27
]
Let
C
be an MDS code of length
n
with
\dim(C)\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then
C
is a GRS code if and only if
\dim(C\star C)=2\dim(C)-1
.

The following lemma is a well known characterization of MDS codes in terms of generator matrices.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
25
, p.319]

Let
C
be an
[n,k]
code with a generator matrix
G
. Then
C
is MDS if and only if all
k\times k
sub-matrices of
G
are non-singular.

Let
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
M_{\Lambda}
be a square Vandermonde matrix of order
n
defined by

M_{\Lambda}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{n-2}&\alpha_{2}^{n-2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n-2}\\
\alpha_{1}^{n-1}&\alpha_{2}^{n-1}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}.

It is well known that the determinant of
M_{\Lambda}
is
\det(M_{\Lambda})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})=D(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
.
Now, we fix some notation that will be used throughout the paper. For an integer
t\geq 0
, the
t^{\rm{th}}
elementary symmetric function in
n
variables is defined as

\sigma_{t}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{n})=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{1}<\dots<j_{t}\leq n}x_{i_{j_{1}}}x_{i_{j_{2}}}\cdots x_{i_{j_{t}}}

with
\sigma_{0}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=1
and
\sigma_{t}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{n})=0
for all
t>n
.

Let
S_{t}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
be the complete symmetric polynomial of degree

S_{t}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{t_{1}+t_{2}+\dots+t_{n}=t,\ t_{i}\geq 0}x_{1}^{t_{1}}x_{2}^{t_{2}}\dots x_{n}^{t_{n}}.

Let
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\dots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By Cramer’s rule, the solution of the system of linear equations

\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}\\
\alpha_{1}^{2}&\alpha_{2}^{2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{n-2}&\alpha_{2}^{n-2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n-2}\\
\alpha_{1}^{n-1}&\alpha_{2}^{n-1}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
x_{3}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n-1}\\
x_{n}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
1\end{bmatrix}

is given by

u_{i}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n,j\neq i}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1},\ 1\leq i\leq n.

(2.1)

We denote
S_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})
by
S_{t}
with
S_{0}=1
and
\sigma_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})
by
\sigma_{t}
. For uniformity, we write
S_{t}=0
for
t<0
. There is a fundamental relation between the elementary symmetric functions and the complete symmetric functions (for instance, see
[
24
, Page 21, Eq.
2.6^{\prime}
]
)

\sum_{t=0}^{N}(-1)^{t}\sigma_{t}S_{N-t}=0,\ \ \ \text{for all }N\geq 1.

(2.2)

Let
n
and
h
be positive integers. Define a generalized Vandermonde matrix
G_{h}
as

G_{h}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{n-2}&\alpha_{2}^{n-2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n-2}\\
\alpha_{1}^{h}&\alpha_{2}^{h}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{h}\end{bmatrix}.

The following lemma determines the determinant of the matrix
G_{h}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
26
, Proposition 3.1]

With notations as above, for
h\geq n-1
, we have

\det(G_{h})=S_{h-n+1}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\cdot\det(M_{\Lambda}).

Lemma 2.4
.

[
1
, Lemma 2.6]

Let
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
u_{i}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n,j\neq i}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1}
for

. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{h}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }0\leq h\leq n-2\\

Through out the paper, we fix
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
S_{t}=S_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})
,
\sigma_{t}=\sigma_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})

’s as in Eq.

unless specified otherwise.

3
First family of linear codes

Let
n,k
be positive integers with
3\leq k\leq n-2\leq q-2
and
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Define

G_{k-1,k-2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{k-3}&\alpha_{2}^{k-3}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{k-3}\\
\alpha_{1}^{k}&\alpha_{2}^{k}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{k}\\
\alpha_{1}^{k+1}&\alpha_{2}^{k+1}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{k+1}\\
\end{bmatrix}_{k\times n}.

We denote the linear code generated by
G_{k-1,k-2}
by
C_{k-1,k-2}
.
In this section, we give parameters of
C_{k-1,k-2}
and determine a parity check matrix for
C_{k-1,k-2}
. We present necessary and sufficient conditions for
C_{k-1,k-2}
to be MDS and investigate the non-GRS property.

Theorem 3.1
.

The linear code
C_{k-1,k-2}
has parameters
[n,k,d]
with
d\in\{n-k-1,n-k,n-k+1\}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
C_{k-1,k-2}
is an
[n,k]
code, since
n>k+1
. Note that
C_{k-1,k-2}
is a subcode of
RS(n,k+2,\Lambda)
. Therefore,
d(C_{k-1,k-2})\geq d(RS(n,k+2,\Lambda))=n-(k+2)+1=n-k-1
. Also, by Singleton bound,
d(C_{k-1,k-2})\leq n-k+1
. Thus
d(C_{k-1,k-2})\in\{n-k-1,n-k,n-k+1\}
.
∎

3.1
Parity check matrix for code
C_{k-1,k-2}

In this subsection, we determine a parity check matrix for code
C_{k-1,k-2}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
C_{k-1,k-2}
be the linear code generated by
G_{k-1,k-2}
. Then

H_{k-1,k-2}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}&u_{2}&\dots&u_{n}\\
u_{1}\alpha_{1}&u_{2}\alpha_{2}&\dots&u_{n}\alpha_{n}\\
u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{2}&u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{2}&\dots&u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-k-3}&u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-k-3}&\dots&u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{n-k-3}\\
u_{1}\Lambda_{1}&u_{2}\Lambda_{2}&\dots&u_{n}\Lambda_{n}\\
u_{1}\Gamma_{1}&u_{2}\Gamma_{2}&\dots&u_{n}\Gamma_{n}\end{bmatrix}_{(n-k)\times n}

is a parity check matrix for
C_{k-1,k-2}
, where

\Lambda_{i}=\alpha_{i}^{n-k+1}-\sigma_{1}\alpha_{i}^{n-k}+\sigma_{2}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-1}-\sigma_{3}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-2}

and

\Gamma_{i}=\alpha_{i}^{n-k}-\sigma_{1}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-1}+\sigma_{2}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-2}.

Proof.

Let
g_{l}
and
h_{m}
be
l^{\mathrm{th}}
and
m^{\mathrm{th}}
rows of
G_{k-1,k-2}
and
H_{k-1,k-2}
, respectively. For
1\leq l\leq k-2
and
1\leq m\leq n-k-2
, we have

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{m}^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+m-1}=0\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
.
For
l=k-1,k
and
1\leq m\leq n-k-2
, we have

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{m}^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+m-1}=0\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
.
For
1\leq l\leq k-2
and
m=n-k-1
, we have

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{m}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1}\Lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+n-k+1}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+n-k}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+n-k-1}-\sigma_{3}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+n-k-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-1}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-2}-\sigma_{3}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
, as
l+n-k\leq n-2
.
For
1\leq l\leq k-2
and
m=n-k
, we have

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{m}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1}\Gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+n-k}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+n-k-1}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1+n-k-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-1}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-2}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
, as
l+n-k-1\leq n-2
. For
l=k-1,k
and
m=n-k-1
, we have

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{m}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1}\Lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+n-k+1}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+n-k}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+n-k-1}-\sigma_{3}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+n-k-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k+2}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k+1}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k}-\sigma_{3}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}S_{2}-\sigma_{1}S_{1}+\sigma_{2}S_{0}-\sigma_{3}\cdot 0=0&\text{ if }l=k-1\\
S_{3}-\sigma_{1}S_{2}+\sigma_{2}S_{1}-\sigma_{3}S_{0}=0&\text{ if }l=k\end{cases}\ \ \

by Eq.
2.2
.
For
l=k-1,k
and
m=n-k

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{m}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1}\Gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+n-k}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+n-k-1}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+1+n-k-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k+1}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+n-k-1})

Next, we show that the rows of
H_{k-1,k-2}
are linearly independent. It is easy to see that the first
n-k-2
rows of
H_{k-1,k-2}
are linearly independent.
Let
v=(\alpha_{1}^{k-2},\alpha_{2}^{k-2},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{k-2})
. Then, for
1\leq m\leq n-k-2
,

vh_{m}^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{m-1+k-2}=0\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
, as
m+k-3<n-2
. But

\displaystyle vh_{n-k-1}^{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-2}\Lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-2+n-k+1}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-2+n-k}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-2+n-k-1}-\sigma_{3}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-2+n-k-2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-2}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-3}-\sigma_{3}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-4})

\displaystyle=1\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
.
Therefore,
h_{n-k-1}\notin\text{span}\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{n-k-2}\}
. Consequently,

\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{n-k-2},h_{n-k-1}\}
is linearly independent. Similarly, take
w=(\alpha_{1}^{k-1},\alpha_{2}^{k-1},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{k-1})
. Then
for
1\leq m\leq n-k-2
,

wh_{m}^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{m-1+k-1}=0\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
, as
m+k-2<n-2
, and

wh_{n-k-1}^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-1}\Lambda_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-2}-\sigma_{3}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-3})=0.

But

\displaystyle wh_{n-k}^{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-1}\Gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-1+n-k}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-1+n-k-1}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-1+n-k-2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-1}-\sigma_{1}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-2}+\sigma_{2}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-3})

\displaystyle=1\ \ \

by Lemma
2.4
. Therefore,
h_{n-k}\notin\text{span}\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{n-k-2},h_{n-k-1}\}
. Consequently, the rows of
H_{k-1,k-2}
are linearly independent. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2
MDS and non-GRS properties of code
C_{k-1,k-2}

In this subsection, we establish the necessary and sufficient conditions under which the code
C_{k-1,k-2}
is MDS and analyze the non-GRS property of this family. We also provide explicit constructions and examples of non-GRS MDS codes.
To derive the necessary and sufficient condition for MDSness, we begin with the following proposition, which serves as a key tool in the proof.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and

V_{n-1,n-2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{n-3}&\alpha_{2}^{n-3}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n-3}\\
\alpha_{1}^{n}&\alpha_{2}^{n}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n}\\
\alpha_{1}^{n+1}&\alpha_{2}^{n+1}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{n+1}\\
\end{bmatrix}_{n\times n}.

Then

\displaystyle\det(V_{n-1,n-2})=D(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\cdot[S_{2}^{2}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})-S_{1}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})S_{3}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})],

where
D(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots\alpha_{n})
is the determinant of Vandermonde matrix
M_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta=(n-1,n-2,\dots,1,0)
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
and
l(\lambda)
is the number of nonzero
\lambda_{i}
’s.
For a given partition
m=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{n})
with
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq\dots\geq m_{n}
, define

A_{m}(\Lambda)=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}^{m_{n}}&\alpha_{2}^{m_{n}}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{m_{n}}\\
\alpha_{1}^{m_{n-1}}&\alpha_{2}^{m_{n-1}}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{m_{2}}&\alpha_{2}^{m_{2}}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{m_{2}}\\
\alpha_{1}^{m_{1}}&\alpha_{2}^{m_{1}}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{m_{1}}\\
\end{bmatrix}_{n\times n}.

Note that
A_{\delta}(\Lambda)
is nothing but a Vandermonde matrix.
For a given partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
, the Schur function is defined as (for instance, see
[
26
, Section 3]
)

s_{\lambda}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})=\frac{\det(A_{\lambda+\delta})}{\det(A_{\delta})},

that is,
\det(A_{\lambda+\delta})=\det(A_{\delta})\cdot s_{\lambda}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})=D(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\cdot s_{\lambda}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
.
Take
\lambda=(2,2,0,0,\dots,0)
. Then
A_{\lambda+\delta}=V_{n-1,n-2}
.
The Jacobi-Trudi identity (see,
[
24
, Eq (3.4), p.41]
states that

s_{\lambda}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})=\det(S_{\lambda_{i}-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n},

where
S_{t}=0
for
t<0
and
S_{0}=1
. For
\lambda=(2,2,0,0,\dots,0)
,

s_{\lambda}=\det\begin{bmatrix}S_{2}&S_{3}&S_{4}&S_{5}&\dots&S_{n+1}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}&S_{3}&S_{4}&\dots&S_{n}\\
0&0&1&S_{1}&\dots&S_{n-3}\\
0&0&0&1&\dots&S_{n-4}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1\end{bmatrix}=S_{2}^{2}-S_{1}S_{3}.

Thus,
\det(V_{n-1,n-2})=D(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\cdot[S_{2}^{2}-S_{1}S_{3}].

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

The linear code
C_{k-1,k-2}
generated by
G_{k-1,k-2}
is MDS if and only if

S_{2}^{2}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})-S_{1}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})S_{3}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})\neq 0

for all
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}\}\subseteq\Lambda
with distinct
\beta_{i}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The linear code
C_{k-1,k-2}
generated by
G_{k-1,k-2}
is MDS if and only if

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{2}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})-\sigma_{1}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})\sigma_{3}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})\neq 0

for all
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}\}\subseteq\Lambda
with distinct
\beta_{i}
.

Proof.

Using Eq.
2.2
, we have
S_{1}=\sigma_{1}
,
S_{2}=\sigma_{1}^{2}-\sigma_{2}
and
S_{3}=\sigma_{1}^{3}-2\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{2}^{2}-S_{1}S_{3}=(\sigma_{1}^{2}-\sigma_{2})^{2}-\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{1}^{3}-2\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3})=\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

For
k=3,4
, let
2k\leq n
,
q\geq 11
and code
C_{k-1,k-2}
be an MDS code. Then the code
C_{k-1,k-2}
is a non-GRS MDS code.

Proof.

(1) Let
k=3
. The Schur square of
C_{k-1,k-2}
is generated by

\displaystyle T=\{(\alpha_{1}^{i},\alpha_{2}^{i},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{i}):i=0,3,4,6,7,8\}.

Also,
8<q-2
, thus
T
is linearly independent. Hence,
\dim(C_{2,1}\star C_{2,1})=2k
.

(2) Let
k=4
. The Schur square of
C_{k-1,k-2}
is generated by

\displaystyle T=\{(\alpha_{1}^{i},\alpha_{2}^{i},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{i}):i=0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,10\}.

Also,
9\leq q-2
, thus the first
8
vectors corresponding to

are linearly independent. Hence,
\dim(C_{3,2}\star C_{3,2})\geq 2k
.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.1

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
C_{k-1,k-2}
be MDS with

Theorem 3.8
.

Let

and

is a non-GRS MDS code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

Let
V_{t}=\{g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]|g(x)\text{ is monic and }\deg g(x)=t\}
. Then the cardinality of
V_{t}
is
p^{t}
. We enumerate the elements of
V_{t}
as
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),\dots,g_{p^{t}}(x)
. Observe that
g_{i}(\gamma)\neq g_{j}(\gamma)
for
i\neq j
. Take
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{i}=g_{i}(\gamma):1\leq i\leq n\}
. Then for any
\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}\subset\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, let
\bm{\beta}_{k}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{k}})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sigma_{2}^{2}(\bm{\beta_{k}})-\sigma_{1}(\bm{\beta_{k}})\sigma_{3}(\bm{\beta_{k}})=\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq k}\alpha_{i_{j_{1}}}\alpha_{i_{j_{2}}}\right)^{2}-\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k}\alpha_{i_{j_{1}}}\right)\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\leq k}\alpha_{i_{j_{1}}}\alpha_{i_{j_{2}}}\alpha_{i_{j_{3}}}\right)\\
&=\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq k}g_{i_{j_{1}}}(\gamma)g_{i_{j_{2}}}(\gamma)\right)^{2}-\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k}g_{i_{j_{1}}}(\gamma)\right)\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\leq k}g_{i_{j_{1}}}(\gamma)g_{i_{j_{2}}}(\gamma)g_{i_{j_{3}}}(\gamma)\right)\\
&\neq 0,\end{split}

as the polynomial

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq k}g_{i_{j_{1}}}(x)g_{i_{j_{2}}}(x)\right)^{2}-\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k}g_{i_{j_{1}}}(x)\right)\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\leq k}g_{i_{j_{1}}}(x)g_{i_{j_{2}}}(x)g_{i_{j_{3}}}(x)\right)

has degree

with leading coefficient
\left(\binom{k}{2}^{2}-k\binom{k}{3}\right)=\frac{1}{12}k^{2}(k^{2}-1)
.
Hence, by Corollary
3.5
, Proposition
3.6
, and Theorem
3.7
, the code
C_{k-1,k-2}
generated by
G_{k-1,k-2}
is an
[n,k]
non-GRS MDS code.
∎

3.3
Self-orthogonality and Self-duality of codes
C_{k-1,k-2}

In this subsection, we investigate the self-orthogonality and self-duality of the codes
C_{k-1,k-2}
. The following lemma is an easy observation.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
C
be an
[n,k]
linear code with a generator matrix
G
. Then
C
is self-orthogonal if and only if
GG^{T}
is a
k\times k
zero matrix. Moreover, if
n=2k
, then a self-orthogonal code is a self-dual code.

Let
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
. Consider a matrix

G_{\bm{v}}=G_{k-1,k-2}\cdot\text{diag}[v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}].

We denote the linear code generated by
G_{\bm{v}}
by
C_{\bm{v}}
. Then the code
C_{\bm{v}}
and
C_{k-1,k-2}
are equivalent. Moreover,
C_{\bm{v}}
is MDS if and only if
C_{k-1,k-2}
is MDS.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
k\geq 5
be an integer,
{\bm{v}}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
, and
u_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
as in Eq.
2.1
. Then the code
C_{\bm{v}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if there exists a polynomial
f(x)=f_{0}+f_{1}x+\dots+f_{n-2k}x^{n-2k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
such that the following hold.

1.

v_{i}^{2}=u_{i}f(\alpha_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq n
.

2.

f_{n-2k-1}+f_{n-2k}S_{1}=0
.

3.

f_{n-2k-2}+f_{n-2k-1}S_{1}+f_{n-2k}S_{2}=0
.

4.

f_{n-2k-3}+f_{n-2k-2}S_{1}+f_{n-2k-1}S_{2}+f_{n-2k}S_{3}=0
.

Where
f_{j}=0
for
j<0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.10
,
C_{\bm{v}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if
G_{\bm{v}}G_{\bm{v}}^{T}=0
, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{l}=0\text{ for }0\leq l\leq 2k-2

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2k}=0

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2k+1}=0

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2k+2}=0.

(3.4)

Let
C_{\bm{v}}
be self-orthogonal, that is, Eqs.
3.1
-
3.4
hold. Then, Eq.
3.1
implies that
(v_{1}^{2},v_{2}^{2},\dots,v_{n}^{2})
is a nonzero solution of the following system of linear equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{l}x_{i}=0\text{ for all}\ l\text{ with}\ 0\leq l\leq 2k-2.

(3.5)

The rank of the coefficient matrix of Eq.
3.5
is
2k-1
. Therefore, the dimension of the solution space of Eq.
3.5
is
n-2k+1
. Also, by Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n}),(u_{1}\alpha_{1},u_{2}\alpha_{2},\dots,u_{n}\alpha_{n}),\dots,(u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-2k},u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-2k},\dots,u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{n-2k})\end{split}

are
n-2k+1
linearly independent solutions of Eq.
3.5
. This implies that there exist scalars
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n-2k}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k}u_{i}f_{j}\alpha_{i}^{j}.\end{split}

Denote

. Then
v_{i}^{2}=u_{i}f(\alpha_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Furthermore,
v_{i}^{2}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, also satisfy Eqs.
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
. Thus, by Lemma
2.4
, we have
2
,
3
and
4
.

Conversely, let there exists a polynomial

satisfying
1
-
4
. It is easy to see that
(v_{1}^{2},v_{2}^{2},\dots,v_{n}^{2})
will satisfy Eqs.
3.1
-
3.4
. Thus,

is self-orthogonal.
∎

4.1
Parity check matrix for code

In this subsection, we determine a parity check matrix for code

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-k-r-1}&u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-k-r-1}&\dots&u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{n-k-r-1}\\
u_{1}\beta_{11}&u_{2}\beta_{21}&\dots&u_{n}\beta_{n1}\\

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.4
, it is easy to see that all rows of

are orthogonal to the first
(n-k-r)
rows of
H_{h,k}
. Let
g_{l}
and
\bar{h}_{m}
denotes

and

and

by

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{a}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1}\beta_{ia}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l-1}\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-r+(a-j)}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-r+(a-j)+(l-1)}

\displaystyle 0

For

\displaystyle g_{l}h_{a}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{h}\beta_{ia}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{(k-1)+r}\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-r+(a-j)}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-r+(a-j)+(k-1+r)}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}S_{a-j}\ \

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

by Lemma
2.4
and Eq.

. Therefore, each row of

is orthogonal to every row of

. Next, we show that the rows of

are linearly independent. For

\displaystyle v_{a}h_{a}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-(a-r+1)}\beta_{ia}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-(a-r+1)}\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-r+(a-j)}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-j-1}

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{j=1}^{a}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-j-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{k-(a-r+1)}\sum_{j=0}^{b}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\alpha_{i}^{n-k-r+(b-j)}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{b}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n-j-1+(b-a)}

4.2
MDS and non-GRS properties of code
C_{h,k}

In this subsection, we determine the necessary and sufficient conditions under which the code
C_{h,k}
attains the MDS property and examine its non-GRS properties. We further present explicit constructions and examples. In the next theorem, we provide the necessary and sufficient condition for
C_{h,k}
to be MDS.

Theorem 4.2
.

The code
C_{h,k}
generated by
G_{h,k}

is an MDS code if and only if

S_{h-k+1}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})\neq 0

for all
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}\}\subseteq\Lambda
with distinct
\beta_{i}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,
C_{h,k}
is MDS if and only if each
k\times k
submatrix of
G_{h,k}
is non-singular. Let
B
be an arbitrary
k\times k
submatrix of
G_{h,k}
. Let

B=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\dots&1\\
\beta_{1}&\beta_{2}&\dots&\beta_{{}_{k}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\beta_{1}^{k-2}&\beta_{2}^{k-2}&\dots&\beta_{k}^{k-2}\\
\beta_{1}^{h}&\beta_{2}^{h}&\dots&\beta_{k}^{h}\end{bmatrix}

for some
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}\}\subseteq\Lambda
. Then by Lemma
2.3
,
\det(B)\neq 0
if and only if
S_{h-k+1}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})\neq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we show that the family of codes
C_{h,k}
constitutes a class of MDS codes that are not generalized Reed–Solomon codes. The proof of their non-GRS nature relies on the Schur product method based on Lemma
2.1
. In
[
16
]
, the authors studied MDS and NMDS code for
h=k
, but not investigated non-GRS properties.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
3\leq k\leq\frac{n-2}{2}
,
h=k
(that is
r=1)
be positive integers, and
C_{h,k}
be an MDS code. Then
C_{h,k}
is a non-GRS MDS code.

Proof.

Denote
\Lambda^{i}=(\alpha_{1}^{i},\alpha_{2}^{i},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{i})
. Then, the Schur square of
C_{h,k}
is generated by

T=\{\Lambda^{i}:0\leq i\leq 2k-2\}\cup\{\Lambda^{2k}\}.

Thus,
\dim(C_{h,k}\star C_{h,k})=2k
. Hence, by Lemma
2.1
,
C_{h,k}
is non-GRS.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
4\leq k\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
,
h=(k-1)+r
with
2\leq r\leq k-2
, and
C_{h,k}
be an MDS code. Then
C_{h,k}
is a non-GRS MDS code.

Proof.

Denote
\Lambda^{i}=(\alpha_{1}^{i},\alpha_{2}^{i},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{i})
.
Since

, the Schur square of
C_{h,k}
is generated by

T=\{\Lambda^{i}:0\leq i\leq 2k-3+r\}\cup\{\Lambda^{2k-2+2r}\}.

Observe that
\{\Lambda^{i}:0\leq i\leq 2k-1\}\subseteq T
is linearly independent as
2k-1\leq q-2
, that is,
\dim(C_{h,k}\star C_{h,k})\geq 2k
. Hence, by Lemma
2.1
,
C_{h,k}
is non-GRS.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

(1) Let
k=3\leq\frac{n}{2}
,
h=(k-1)+r
with
k-1\leq r\leq\frac{q-k-3}{2}
and
C_{h,k}
be an MDS code. Then
C_{h,k}
is a non-GRS MDS code.

(2) Let
4\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}
,
h=(k-1)+r
with
k-1\leq r\leq q-k-3
, and
C_{h,k}
be an MDS code. Then
C_{h,k}
is a non-GRS MDS code.

Proof.

We denote Denote
\Lambda^{i}=(\alpha_{1}^{i},\alpha_{2}^{i},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{i})
.
(1) For
k=3
, the Schur square of
C_{h,k}
is generated by

\displaystyle T=\{\Lambda^{0},\Lambda^{1},\Lambda^{2},\Lambda^{h},\Lambda^{h+1},\Lambda^{2h}\}.

Moreover, since
2h=q-k+1\leq q-2
, the vectors in

are linearly independent. Thus,
\dim(C_{h,k}\star C_{h,k})=6=2k
. Hence, by Lemma
2.1
,
C_{h,k}
is non-GRS.

(2) For

, the Schur square of

is generated by

T=T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup\{\Lambda^{2k-2+2r}\},

where

are linearly independent as

\{\Lambda^{k-1+r},\Lambda^{k+r},\Lambda^{k+r+1}\}\subseteq T_{2}

\dim(C_{h,k}\star C_{h,k})\geq(2k-3)+3=2k
. Hence, by Lemma

,

is non-GRS.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

It is important to note that if two
[n,k]
codes

and
C_{2}
are equivalent, then their Schur squares of

and
C_{2}
are also equivalent. For

with

, let

be the code generated by

, where

with

is at least

. By contrast, in
[

21
]
, the authors constructed non-GRS MDS codes whose Schur squares have dimension

contains non-GRS MDS codes that are not equivalent to codes presented in
[

,

]

Using techniques similar to those in
[

Theorem 4.6

Let

Let
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{k}})

. Then

\displaystyle S_{h-k+1}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})=

\displaystyle S_{2}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})=\sigma_{1}^{2}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})-\sigma_{2}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k}\alpha_{i_{j_{1}}}\right)^{2}-\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq k}\alpha_{i_{j_{1}}}\alpha_{i_{j_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(k^{2}-\binom{k}{2}\right)\gamma^{2t}+\sum_{j=0}^{2t-1}c_{j}\gamma^{j}\ \ \text{ for some }c_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k(k+1)}{2}\gamma^{2t}+\sum_{j=0}^{2t-1}c_{j}\gamma^{j}\neq 0.

Hence, by Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
,
C_{h,k}
is a non-GRS MDS code.
∎

4.3
Self-orthogonal and self-dual properties of code
C_{h,k}

In this subsection, we investigate the self-orthogonality and self-duality of the codes
C_{h,k}
.

Let
{\bm{v}}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
. Consider a matrix

G_{h,{\bm{v}}}=G_{h,k}\cdot\text{diag}[v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}].

We denote the linear code generated by
G_{h,{\bm{v}}}
by
C_{h,{\bm{v}}}
. The codes
C_{h,\bm{v}}
and
C_{h,k}
are equivalent.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
h=(k-1)+r
with
1\leq r\leq k-2
,
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
, and
u_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
be as in Eq.
2.1
. Then
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if there exists a polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k-r+1}f_{j}x^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
such that the following hold:

1.

v_{i}^{2}=u_{i}f(\alpha_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

2.

\sum_{j=s}^{s+r}f_{j}S_{j-s}=0
, where
s=n-2k-2r+1
and
f_{j}=0
if
j<0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.10
,
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if
G_{h,\bm{v}}G_{h,\bm{v}}^{T}=0
, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{l}=0\text{ for all }l\text{ with }0\leq l\leq 2k-3+r,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2k-2+2r}=0.

(4.2)

Let
C_{h,\bm{v}}
be self-orthogonal, that is, Eqs.
4.1
,
4.2
hold. Then, Eq.
4.1
implies that
(v_{1}^{2},v_{2}^{2},\dots,v_{n}^{2})
is a nonzero solution of the following system of linear equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{l}x_{i}=0\text{ for }0\leq l\leq 2k-3+r.

(4.3)

The rank of the coefficient matrix of Eq.
4.3
is
2k-2+r
. Therefore, the dimension of the solution space of Eq.
4.3
is
n-2k-r+2
. Also, by Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n}),(u_{1}\alpha_{1},u_{2}\alpha_{2},\dots,u_{n}\alpha_{n}),\dots,(u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-2k-r+1},u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-2k-r+1},\dots,u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{n-2k-r+1})\end{split}

are
n-2k-r+2
linearly independent solutions of Eq.
4.3
. This implies that there exist scalars
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n-2k-r+1}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k-r+1}u_{i}f_{j}\alpha_{i}^{j}.\end{split}

Denote
f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k-r+1}f_{j}x^{j}
. Then
v_{i}^{2}=u_{i}f(\alpha_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Furthermore,
v_{i}^{2}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, also satisfies Eq.
4.2
, therefore, we have

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}f(\alpha_{i})\alpha_{i}^{2k-2+2r}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k-r+1}f_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{2k-2+2r+j}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k-r+1}f_{j}S_{2k-2+2r+j-n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=s}^{s+r}f_{j}S_{j-s}=0,\text{ where }s=n-2k-2r+1,

using Lemma
2.4
. This completes the necessary part. The proof of the converse part is similar to that of Theorem
3.11
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
h=(k-1)+r
with
k-1\leq r\leq q-k-1
,
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
, and
u_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
be as in Eq.
2.1
. Then
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if there exists a polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k+2}f_{j}x^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
such that the following hold

1.

v_{i}^{2}=u_{i}f(\alpha_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

2.

\sum_{j=b}^{b+2r+1}f_{j}S_{j-b}=0
, where
b=n-2k-2r+1
.

3.

\sum_{j=s}^{s+l-2k+3}f_{j}S_{j-s}=0
for
l
with
(k-1)+r\leq l\leq(2k-3)+r
, where
s=n-l-1
and
f_{j}=0
if
j<0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.10
,
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if
G_{h,\bm{v}}G_{h,\bm{v}}^{T}=0
, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{l}=0\text{ for }0\leq l\leq 2k-4,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{l}=0\text{ for all }l\text{ with }(k-1)+r\leq l\leq(2k-3)+r,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2k-2+2r}=0.

(4.6)

Let
C_{h,\bm{v}}
be self-orthogonal, that is, Eqs.
4.4
,
4.5
,
4.6
hold. Then, Eq.
4.4
implies that
(v_{1}^{2},v_{2}^{2},\dots,v_{n}^{2})
is a nonzero solution of the following system of linear equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{l}x_{i}=0\text{ for all }l\text{ with }0\leq l\leq 2k-4.

(4.7)

The rank of the coefficient matrix of Eq.
4.7
is
2k-3
. Therefore, the dimension of the solution space of Eq.
4.7
is
n-2k+3
. Also, by Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n}),(u_{1}\alpha_{1},u_{2}\alpha_{2},\dots,u_{n}\alpha_{n}),\dots,(u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-2k+2},u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-2k+2},\dots,u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{n-2k+2})\end{split}

are
n-2k+3
linearly independent solutions of Eq.
4.7
. This implies that there exist scalars
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n-2k+2}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k+2}u_{i}f_{j}\alpha_{i}^{j}.\end{split}

Denote
f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k+2}f_{j}x^{j}
. Then
v_{i}^{2}=u_{i}f(\alpha_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Furthermore,
v_{i}^{2}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, also satisfies Eqs.
4.6
, therefore, we have

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k+2}f_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{2k-2+2r+j}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k+2}f_{j}S_{2k-2+2r+j-n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=b}^{b+2r+1}f_{j}S_{j-b}=0,\text{ where }b=n-2k-2r+1,

using Lemma
2.4
. Moreover,
v_{i}^{2}
’s satisfy Eq.
4.5
as well, for
(k-1)+r\leq l\leq(2k-3)+r
,

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k+2}f_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\alpha_{i}^{l+j}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k+2}f_{j}S_{l+j-n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=s}^{s+l-2k+3}f_{j}S_{j-s}=0,\text{ where }s=n-l-1,

using Lemma
2.4
.
The converse follows similar to that of Theorem
3.11
.
∎

Example 9

Let

,

,

,

,

’s such that

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
n=2k\geq 6
,
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
,
u_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
be as in Eq.
2.1
, and
h=(k-1)+r
for some
1\leq r\leq k-2
. Then
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is self-dual if and only if
r=1
and the followings hold

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\text{ all }u_{i}\text{'s },1\leq i\leq n,\text{ are squares or non-squares simultaneously}.

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\ S_{1}=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.7
,
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is self-dual if and only if there exists a polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k-r+1}f_{j}x^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, that is,
n-2k-r+1\geq 0
. Consequently,
r=1
. By Theorem
4.7
, if
r=1
, then
f(x)=f_{0}
and
s=-1
. The first condition of Theorem
4.7
implies that
v_{i}^{2}=f_{0}u_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, that is, all
u_{i}
’s are squares or non-squares simultaneously and
f_{0}\neq 0
.
By the second condition in Theorem
4.7
, we get

f_{-1}S_{0}+f_{0}S_{1}=0\implies f_{0}S_{1}=0\implies S_{1}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

.

.

∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
q\geq 8
be even,
n=q
,
h=\frac{q}{2},k=\frac{q}{2}
, and
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots\alpha_{q}\}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the code
C_{h,\bm{v}}
generated by
G_{h,\bm{v}}
is a self-dual code for some
\bm{v}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
with parameters
[q,\frac{q}{2},\frac{q}{2}]
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
S_{1}=0
. By Corollary
4.10
, the code
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is self-dual. Moreover, as
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is a subcode of
GRS(n,k+1,\Lambda,v)
therefore

. By Theorem

,

is not MDS. Thus,

.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
n=2k\geq 8
and
h=(k-1)+r
with
k-1\leq r\leq q-k-3
. Then code
C_{h,\bm{v}}
is never self-dual for any
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
.

Proof.

On the contrary, let
C_{h,\bm{v}}
be a self-dual code for some
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
. Then by Lemma
3.10
,
G_{h,\bm{v}}G_{h,\bm{v}}^{T}=0
, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{l}=0\text{ for }0\leq l\leq 2k-4,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{l}=0\text{ for all }l\text{ with }(k-1)+r\leq l\leq(2k-3)+r,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2k-2+2r}=0.

This implies that
v_{i}^{2}
’s are a nonzero solution of the following system of linear equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{l}x_{i}=0\text{ for }0\leq l\leq 2k-4,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{l}x_{i}=0\text{ for all }l\text{ with }(k-1)+r\leq l\leq(2k-3)+r,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{2k-2+2r}x_{i}=0.

Consider a submatrix of the augmented matrix of the above system of linear equations

\displaystyle A=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}^{0}&\alpha_{2}^{0}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{0}\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\dots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{2k-4}&\alpha_{2}^{2k-4}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{2k-4}\\
\alpha_{1}^{(k-1)+r}&\alpha_{2}^{(k-1)+r}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{(k-1)+r}\\
\alpha_{1}^{(k-1)+r+1}&\alpha_{2}^{(k-1)+r+1}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{(k-1)+r+1}\\
\alpha_{1}^{(k-1)+r+2}&\alpha_{2}^{(k-1)+r+2}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{(k-1)+r+2}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Since
k+r+1\leq k+q-k-3+1=q-2
, the rank of matrix
A
is
2k
(equal to the number of variables). Hence, the above system of linear equations has only a zero solution, which is a contradiction to
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this work, we constructed two families of linear codes by modifying the generator matrix of GRS codes. For these families, we explicitly determined parity check matrices and established necessary and sufficient conditions on evaluation points under which the codes achieve the MDS property. Using the Schur square method, we further showed that these codes exhibit the non-GRS property. As a result, we concluded that the non-GRS MDS codes constructed in this paper contain codes that are not equivalent to the codes in
[
23
,
21
]
. Further, we investigated self-orthogonality and self-duality of the constructed codes by deriving necessary and sufficient conditions. In addition, we presented several illustrative examples and explicit constructions to demonstrate the results.

Abstract
. We classify the singular limits relative to a free boundary problem arising in plasma physics in dimension d = 2 d=2 , under suitable natural integral bounds. It turns out that one of the asymptotic behaviors allowed corresponds to the Dancer-Yan spikes (J. London Math. Soc. ( 78 ) 2008, 639–662). Interestingly enough, roughly speaking and unlike the higher dimensional case, it is not true that any solution in the limit is a Dancer-Yan spike. Indeed, the spiking structure is more rich and we succeed in a detailed description of the singular behavior by a careful analysis, from local to global, of the tiny difference between the maximum value of the spikes and their “vanishing level” defining the free boundary.



Keywords
: singularly perturbed elliptic problems, free boundary problems, spikes.

We list hereafter some natural open problems about the results discussed so far.

Property (e) in Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

Here we collect some useful facts which will be frequently used later on.

2.1.
Emden solution

Let
B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denote the unit disk and
p\in(1,+\infty)
.
There exists a unique solution (
[
25
]
) to the Emden equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\phi=\phi^{p},&\mbox{ in }B_{1}(0),\\
\phi=0,&\mbox{ on }\partial B_{1}(0),\end{cases}

which is well known (
[
25
]
) to be radial and radially decreasing.
Throughout this paper this unique solution we will be just denoted by
\phi
.
Furthermore, we denote

(2.2)

\displaystyle I_{p-1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi^{p-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x},

(2.3)

\displaystyle I_{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{B_{1}(0)}-\Delta\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=-2\pi\phi^{\prime}(1),

\displaystyle I_{p+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi^{p+1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x},

and call them the
(p+k)
-integrals, for
k=-1,0,+1
respectively.
We list below few useful relations among these quantities:

(a)

By a Pohozaev argument, namely, testing the equation against
x\cdot\nabla\phi
, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p+1}I_{p+1}=\pi(\phi^{\prime}(1))^{2},

which, combined with (
2.3
) gives that,

\displaystyle I_{p+1}=\frac{p+1}{8\pi}I_{p}^{2}.

(b)

The algebraic relation between
I_{p-1}
and
I_{p}
is unclear, but using that
\max\phi=\phi(0)
and
\phi>0
we have that,

\displaystyle\frac{I_{p}}{\phi(0)}<I_{p-1}<\phi(0)^{p-1}\pi.

By using the Hölder inequality, we get another relation:

\displaystyle I_{p-1}\leq I_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\pi^{\frac{1}{p}}=(-2\phi^{\prime}(1))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\pi.

Remark 2.1
.

Observe that

(i)

I_{p+1}
is equivalent to the Dirichlet energy of the solution;

(ii)

I_{p-1}
controls both
I_{p}
and
I_{p+1}
up to multiplication by suitable powers of
\phi(0)
.

We will see later that these hold also for general solutions.

2.2.
A rigidity result for entire solutions

Type I spikes are modeled on the solutions used in
[
20
]
. Concerning this point, we will need the following classification
result about entire solutions of (
2.4
). We could not find a proof of this classification result, which is why we
provide a sketchy proof, based however on well known ideas first pushed forward in
[
12
]
and
[
16
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a distributional solution of

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&-\Delta w=[w]_{+}^{p}\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
&\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[w]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq C_{0}<+\infty.\end{cases}

Then
w\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
[w]_{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Moreover, denoting

\displaystyle\beta_{p}\equiv\beta_{p}(w)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[w]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x},

we have that:

•

either
\beta_{p}=0
, and then
w
is a non-positive constant,

•

or
\beta_{p}>0
, and then
w
is, up to a translation, radial and takes the form,

(2.5)

\displaystyle w(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{R_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\phi\left(\frac{x}{R_{p}}\right),&0\leq|x|\leq R_{p},\\
\frac{1}{R_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\phi^{\prime}(1)\log\left(\frac{|x|}{R_{p}}\right),&|x|>R_{p},\end{cases}

where
R_{p}>0
is uniquely defined in terms of
\beta_{p}
as follows,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\beta_{p}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{I_{p}}{R_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}=\frac{-\phi^{\prime}(1)}{R_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}.

The solution with
R_{p}=1
will be denoted as
w^{*}
, namely,

(2.7)

\displaystyle w^{*}(x)=\begin{cases}\phi(x),&0\leq|x|\leq 1,\\
\phi^{\prime}(1)\log(|x|),&|x|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Step 1: Regularity of weak solutions
. We have the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
w\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a distributional solution of (
2.4
).
Then
w\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
[w]_{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

We argue as in
[
12
]
by a suitable decomposition of
w
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
r>0

and write
w=w_{1}+w_{2}
in
B_{2r}(x_{0})
where,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{1}=[w]_{+}^{p}&\mbox{ in }B_{2r}(x_{0}),\\
w_{1}=0&\mbox{ on }\partial B_{2r}(x_{0}),\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{2}=0&\mbox{ in }B_{2r}(x_{0}),\\
w_{1}=w&\mbox{ on }\partial B_{2r}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

Since
[w]_{+}^{p}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then according to
[
49
]
we have that
w_{1}\in W_{0}^{1,t}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))
for any
t\in[1,2)
, with

\displaystyle\|w_{1}\|_{W^{1,t}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}\leq C(t,C_{0}).

Since we are in dimension
d=2
, by the Sobolev embedding we have
w_{1}\in L^{q}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))
for any
1\leq q<+\infty
with

\displaystyle\|w_{1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}\leq C(q,C_{0}).

Moreover, by the maximum principle
w_{1}\geq 0
in
B_{2r}(x_{0})
. By applying the mean value theorem to the harmonic part

w_{2}=w-w_{1}(\leq w)
, for any
x_{1}\in B_{r}(x_{0})
we have that,

\displaystyle[w_{2}(x_{1})]_{+}\leq\fint\limits_{B_{r}(x_{1})}[w]_{+}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\left(\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{1})}[w]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}|B_{r}(x_{1})|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{C_{0}^{1/p}}{\pi r^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}.

Thus
\|[w_{2}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq C_{0}^{1/p}(\pi r^{1+\frac{1}{p}})^{-1}
.

As a consequence,
[w]_{+}\leq[w_{1}]_{+}+[w_{2}]_{+}\in L^{q}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
for any
q\in[1,+\infty)
, with

\displaystyle\|[w]_{+}\|_{L^{q}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq C(q,C_{0},r).

Now
-\Delta w_{1}=[w]_{+}^{p}
has better integrability properties.
An iteration of the splitting argument shows that
w_{1}\in L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x_{0}))
and so is
[w]_{+}
, with

\displaystyle\|[w]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x_{0}))}\leq C(C_{0},r,p).

Noting that
t\mapsto[t]_{+}^{p}
is a
C^{1}
function (recall
p>1
), by a bootstrap argument we conclude that

w\in C^{2,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
∎

Consequently, all the distributional solutions are actually classical solutions.

Step 2: The case
\beta_{p}=0
.
If
\beta_{p}=0
, then
w
is non-positive and harmonic, which is necessarily constant by Liouville theorem.

Step 3. The case of
\beta_{p}>0
.
We first use the argument, based on a Green representation formula, adopted in
[
16
]
to come up with the following decay estimate:
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle-\beta_{p}\ln(1+|x|)-C\leq w(x)\leq-\beta_{p}\ln(1+|x|)+C,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

In particular, as in
[
16
]
we have that,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{w(x)}{\ln|x|}\rightarrow-\beta_{p},\quad\mbox{ uniformly as }|x|\rightarrow+\infty,

and, in polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle r\frac{\partial w}{\partial w}\rightarrow-\beta_{p},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial\theta}\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\mbox{ as }r\rightarrow+\infty.

At this point, by using a moving plane argument as in
[
16
]
we conclude that
w
is radial with respect to some point and is radially decreasing.
Thus, after a translation if necessary, we may assume that
w
is radial with respect to the origin and that there exists a unique

such that

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid w(x)>0\right\}=B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

By the uniqueness of the positive solutions in a ball of the Emden equation, we conclude that

\displaystyle w(x)=\frac{1}{R^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\phi(\frac{x}{R}),\quad\mbox{ for }|x|\leq R,

where we recall that
\phi
is the unique solution of (
2.1
). In the outer domain
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R}(0)
,

is harmonic with the decay shown in (

), whence, by standard ODE theory, it
necessarily takes the form,

\displaystyle w(x)=\frac{1}{R^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\phi{{}^{\prime}}(1)\ln\frac{|x|}{R},\qquad\forall|x|\geq R,

the coefficients being uniquely defined by the
C^{1}
continuity at the free boundary
\left\{w=0\right\}
.

Since all the argument adopted along the proof work exactly as they stand in
[

]
, we will not provide the details here to avoid repetitions.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

For the non-constant entire solution defined in (
2.5
), we have seen that to each

there correspond a
unique

, so that we may write,

\displaystyle\beta_{p}(R_{p})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[w]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{I_{p}}{R_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}=\frac{-\phi^{\prime}(1)}{R_{p}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}.

Moreover, by an evaluation based on Pohozaev identity we have that,

\displaystyle\beta_{p+1}(R_{p})\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[w]_{+}^{p+1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=\frac{(p+1)}{4}\beta_{p}^{2}.

Another relevant quantity is,

for which we again have no algebraic evaluation but only the direct estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{p}}{\sup w}<\beta_{p-1}<\frac{1}{2\pi}(\sup w)^{p-1}|\Omega|,

\displaystyle\beta_{p-1}\leq\beta_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{|\Omega|}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note also that

),

\displaystyle\beta_{p-1}(R_{p})=\frac{1}{2\pi}I_{p-1},

.

\displaystyle-\Delta w=[w]_{+}^{p}

is invariant under the following transformations:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{t}w(x)\coloneq t^{\frac{2}{p-1}}w(tx)

for any

.
The masses in different scales transform in the following way:

\displaystyle\beta_{p-1}({\mathcal{R}}_{t}w)=\beta_{p-1}(w),

\displaystyle\beta_{p+1}({\mathcal{R}}_{t}w)=t^{\frac{4}{p-1}}\beta_{p+1}(w).

In particular, the decaying rate

(see (

has a positive lower bound,
say
t_{n}\geq\left(\frac{I_{p}}{2H_{p}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}>0
.

Next we consider the case where
s_{n}t_{n}
has a positive lower bound, which means that,
up to subsequence, either
s_{n}t_{n}\rightarrow\frac{1}{r_{0}}>0
or
s_{n}t_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
.
In this case, the rescaled disks
B_{2\delta/s_{n}t_{n}}(0)
are uniformly bounded sets and we don’t have a planar problem to attach to the limiting
function. Instead, we can consider the functions,

\displaystyle\frac{v_{n}(x)-1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}

which satisfy

\displaystyle-\Delta\left(\frac{v_{n}(x)-1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\right)=\left[\frac{v_{n}(x)-1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\right]_{+}^{p}\quad\mbox{ in }\quad B_{2\delta}(x_{n}),

whose maximum values are,

\displaystyle\frac{v_{n}(x_{n})-1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}=\frac{\theta_{n}}{s_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\frac{\tilde{v}_{n}(0)}{\theta_{n}}=\frac{\phi(0)}{(s_{n}t_{n})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}.

Therefore by Lemma
4.5
we have that,

\displaystyle v_{n}(x)-1=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{(s_{n}t_{n})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}(f(x)+o(1)),\quad\forall\,x\in B_{\delta}(x^{*}),

for some bounded function
f
satisfying
f(x)\leq\phi(0)
.
This corresponds to the Fading spike in (B-iii). Note that in this case the
(p-1)
-mass satisfies,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{\delta}(x^{*})}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}[v_{n}-1]_{+}^{p-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq\pi\delta^{2}\frac{\phi^{p-1}(0)}{(s_{n}t_{n})^{2}}(1+o(1)),

which of course need not converge to some integer multiple of
I_{p-1}
. Actually it would converge to zero whenever
s_{n}t_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
.
However, the
(p)
-mass will vanish in the limit as from (
4.2
) we have that,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{\delta}(x^{*})}\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}[v_{n}-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq\pi\delta^{2}\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\frac{\phi^{p}(0)}{(s_{n}t_{n})^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}\leq

\displaystyle\pi\delta^{2}\frac{\phi^{p}(0)}{(s_{n}t_{n})^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}\theta_{n}\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}=\pi\delta^{2}\frac{\phi^{p}(0)}{(s_{n}t_{n})^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}s_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(1+o(1)).

∎

5.
Extraction of a second spike sequence

We prove various partial results which at last will be used to prove Theorem
1.2
. We split the discussion into three
subsections, whose titles are meant to clarify which is the aim therein.

We keep the notations in (
4.4
) and let
x_{n,1}\equiv x_{n}\rightarrow x^{*}\in\Omega
be the interior maxima of
v_{n}
such that

\max\limits_{\Omega_{0}}v_{n}=v_{n}(x_{n,1}).

If
x^{*}
is regular, whence
v_{n}(x_{n})-1\leq 0
, or either if this is a Fading sequence,
i.e.
v_{n}(x_{n})-1\leq\phi(0)\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(A_{n}+o(1))
,
then we see that there are no interior spikes at all in any
\Omega_{0}\Subset\Omega
.

Therefore, in this section we consider the case where

(5.1)

v_{n}(x_{n,1})\mbox{ yields a spike at }x^{*}\in\Omega_{0}\mbox{ either of Type I or of Type II}.

We wonder whether or not there is another sequence of points yielding another spike at the same point
x^{*}
,
which makes
x^{*}
a multiple-spike point and, whenever this were the case, how to describe the second spike.

Let
R_{n,1}=R_{n}
and
t_{n,1}=t_{n}
be the quantities defined in Theorem
1.1
and
let

x_{n,2}\in\Omega\setminus B_{2R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})

be defined as follows,

\displaystyle v_{n}(x_{n,2})=\sup_{\Omega\setminus B_{2R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}v_{n},

where we assume w.l.o.g. that, possibly along a subsequence,

(5.2)

\displaystyle x_{n,2}\rightarrow x^{*}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad v_{n}(x_{n,2})-1>0.

Note that
|x_{n,2}-x_{n,1}|\geq 2R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}
by definition while
0<v_{n}(x_{n,2})-1\rightarrow 0^{+}
by Proposition
4.1
.

Let us define
t_{n,2}\coloneqq\left(\phi(0)/\theta_{n}(v_{n}(x_{n,2}-1))\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\geq t_{n,1}\geq T_{0})
and
rescale
v_{n}
as follows,

(5.3)

\displaystyle u_{n,2}(z)\coloneqq t_{n,2}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\theta_{n}\left(v_{n}(x_{n,2}+s_{n}t_{n,2}z)-1\right),\qquad z\in B_{{2\delta}/{s_{n}t_{n,2}}}(0).

Then
u_{n,2}(0)=\phi(0)
, and
u_{n,2}
satisfies,

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{n,2}=[u_{n,2}]_{+}^{p},\qquad\mbox{ in }B_{{2\delta}/{s_{n}t_{n,2}}}(0),

as well as the integral bounds,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{2\delta/s_{n}t_{n,2}}(0)}[u_{n,2}(z)]_{+}^{p-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{z}=\int\limits_{B_{2\delta}(x_{n,2})}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}[v_{n}-1]_{+}^{p-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq H_{p-1},

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{2\delta/s_{n}t_{n,2}}(0)}[u_{n,2}(z)]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{z}=t_{n,2}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int\limits_{B_{2\delta}(x_{n,2})}\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}[v_{n}(x)-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq\phi(0)H_{p-1}.

Remark 5.1
.

The fact that
s_{n}t_{n,2}\geq s_{n}t_{n,1}
implies that, if
v_{n}(x_{n,1})
yields a spike of Type I, then
v_{n}(x_{n,2})
could be either a
of Type I or of Type II or Fading. On the other side, if
v_{n}(x_{n,1})
is of Type II,
then
v_{n}(x_{n,2})
cannot be of Type I.

5.1.
Two local maximizers cannot be too close each other.

We are forced to compare the relative distance between
x_{n,2}
and
x_{n,1}
with the rescaling rate
s_{n}t_{n,2}
.
The following Lemma says that
|x_{n,1}-x_{n,2}|
is much larger than
s_{n}t_{n,2}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
x_{n,2}
,
x_{n,1}
and
s_{n}t_{n,2}
be defined as above, then we have,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{|x_{n,1}-x_{n,2}|}{s_{n}t_{n,2}}\rightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

By contradiction assume that there exists
C>0
such that,

\displaystyle\frac{R_{n,1}t_{n,1}}{t_{n,2}}\leq\frac{|x_{n,1}-x_{n,2}|}{s_{n}t_{n,2}}\leq C,

implying in particular that,

\displaystyle 2R_{n,1}\leq\frac{|x_{n,1}-x_{n,2}|}{s_{n}}\leq Ct_{n,2},

and
\frac{|x_{n,1}-x_{n,2}|}{s_{n}}\rightarrow+\infty
and
t_{n,2}\rightarrow+\infty
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
.

Recall the functions
u_{n,1}
and
u_{n,2}
defined by (
1.8
) and (
5.3
).
Putting

\displaystyle z_{n,1,2}\coloneqq\frac{x_{n,1}-x_{n,2}}{s_{n}t_{n,2}},

which is uniformly bounded, possibly along a subsequence we have
z_{n,1,2}\rightarrow z_{\infty,1,2}
, and then

\displaystyle u_{n,2}(z_{n,1,2})=t_{n,2}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\theta_{n}\left(v_{n}(x_{n,1})-1\right)=\left(\frac{t_{n,2}}{t_{n,1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u_{n,1}(0)\rightarrow+\infty

since
u_{n,1}(0)=\phi(0)>0
and
\frac{t_{n,2}}{t_{n,1}}\rightarrow+\infty
.
This is a contradiction to the fact that
u_{n,2}
is bounded from above by
\phi(0)
.
∎

5.2.
No Fading spike if there is already a spike of Type I/II

Next we wish to rule out the Fading alternative in presence of a spike of Type I or II. Therefore we consider the case where
s_{n}t_{n,2}
has a positive lower bound,
which means
t_{n,2}
diverges to
+\infty
very fast, so that
v_{n}(x_{n,2})
is a Fading sequence with
x_{n,2}
converging at
x^{*}
.
We will see that this is impossible
as far as (
5.1
) is satisfied.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s_{n}t_{n,2}}\rightarrow\rho\geq 0.

Recall that
v_{n}(x_{n,2})=\sup\left\{v_{n}(x)\mid x\in\Omega\setminus B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})\right\}
and

\displaystyle\theta_{n}(v_{n}(x_{n,2})-1)=\phi(0)t_{n,2}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}=\phi(0)\frac{s_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{(s_{n}t_{n,2})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}=\phi(0)(\rho+o(1))s_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}},

thus

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega\setminus B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}v_{n}\leq v_{n}(x_{n,2})=1+\phi(0)(\rho+o(1))\frac{s_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{\theta_{n}}=1+\phi(0)(\rho+o(1))\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}.

Let
G(x,x^{\prime})
be the Green function for the domain
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary condition, and assume
v_{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
Then for any
x\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle v_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega}G(x,x^{\prime})[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}.

To estimate the value at a point
x\in\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x_{1})
where
r>0
is small,
we split the domain
\Omega
into two parts:
\Omega=B_{R_{n}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})\cup(\Omega\setminus B_{R_{n}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1}))
.
Therefore, near
x_{1}
we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}G(x,x^{\prime})[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\frac{(s_{n}t_{n,1})^{2}}{\theta_{n}^{p}t_{n,1}^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}\int\limits_{B_{R_{n,1}}(0)}G(x,x_{n,1}+s_{n}t_{n,1}z)[u_{n,1}(z)]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{n}t_{n,1}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\int\limits_{B_{R_{n,1}}(0)}G(x,x_{n,1}+s_{n}t_{n,1}z)[u_{n,1}(z)]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{z}.

Since
R_{n,1}\rightarrow+\infty
, the balls
B_{R_{n}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})
exhaust the plane and
u_{n,1}\rightarrow w^{*}

in
C^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, thus
[u_{n,1}]_{+}^{p}\rightarrow[w^{*}]_{+}^{p}
in
C^{0}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and they have uniformly bounded support.
Observing that
s_{n}t_{n,1}\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we deduce that,

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}G(x,x^{\prime})[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{n,1}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\int\limits_{B_{1}(0)}G(x,x_{1})[w^{*}(z)]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{z}+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{I_{p}}{t_{n,1}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}G(x,x_{1})+o(1).

The integral on
\Omega\setminus B_{R_{n}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})
takes the form,

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}G(x,x^{\prime})[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}G(x,x^{\prime})(\phi(0)(\rho+o(1))\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\phi(0)^{p}(\rho+o(1))^{p}\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}G(x,x^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}\right).

Thus for
x\in\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x^{*})
,

\displaystyle\theta_{n}v_{n}(x)=\frac{I_{p}}{t_{n,1}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}G(x,x^{*})+o(1),\qquad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow+\infty.

Since the right hand side is bounded while
\theta_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
and
t_{n,1}
are bounded from below, we conclude that for any
r>0
and any

x\in\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x^{*})
,
v_{n}(x)\rightarrow 0
locally uniformly.
In particular, fix
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\dist(x^{*},\partial\Omega))
, there exists
n_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(5.6)

\displaystyle[v_{n}(x)-1]_{+}=0,\qquad\forall x\in\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x^{*}),\quad\forall n>n_{r}.

Remark that this estimates holds true under the assumption that
x_{n,2}
is a Fading sequence. By no means we can assume it to hold in general.
In particular we cannot claim that there is only one spike in
\Omega
.

Next we apply again the Green representation argument to see that, in view of (
5.5
) and (
5.6
),
then
v_{n}(x_{n,2})\leq 1
which contradicts the assumption (
5.2
).

Indeed, because of (
5.6
), the Green representation formula above reduces to an
integration over
B_{r}(x^{*})
for
n>n_{r}
: for any
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle v_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega}G(x,x^{\prime})[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x^{*})}G(x,x^{\prime})[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}.

Combining this identity with
t_{n,1}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\theta_{n}(v_{n}(x_{n,1})-1)=u_{n,1}(0)=\phi(0)
, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{n}v_{n}(x_{n,1})=\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x^{*})}G(x_{n,1},x^{\prime})[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}=u_{n,1}(0)t_{n,1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}+\theta_{n}.

Therefore, by using the classical decomposition
G(x,x^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x-x^{\prime}|+H(x,x^{\prime})
where
H(x,x^{\prime})
is the regular part (hence locally bounded),
we have

\displaystyle\theta_{n}(v_{n}(x)-1)=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x^{*})}\left(G(x,x^{\prime})-G(x_{n,1},x^{\prime})\right)[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}+u_{n,1}(0)t_{n,1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x^{*})}\left(H(x,x^{\prime})-H(x_{n,1},x^{\prime})\right)[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}+u_{n,1}(0)t_{n,1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x^{*})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|x_{n,1}-x^{\prime}|}{|x-x^{\prime}|}[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}.

Let
t_{n,1}\rightarrow t_{\infty,1}\in[T_{0},+\infty]
, then the term
u_{n,1}(0)t_{n,1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\rightarrow\phi(0)t_{\infty,1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\geq 0
, which is in particular bounded.
The integration involving
H(x,x^{\prime})-H(x_{n,1},x^{\prime})
is also bounded because of (
H2
).
For the remaining part involving the fundamental solutions, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|x_{n,1}-x^{\prime}|}{|x-x^{\prime}|}[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{B_{R_{n,1}t_{n,1}}(0)}\ln\frac{|y|}{|\frac{x-x_{n,1}}{s_{n}}-y|}[\tilde{v}_{n}(y)]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t_{n,1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{B_{R_{n,1}/t_{n,1}}(0)}\ln\frac{|z|}{|\frac{x-x_{n,1}}{s_{n}t_{n,1}}-z|}[u_{n,1}(z)]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t_{n,1}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{B_{2}(0)}\ln\frac{|z|}{|\frac{x-x_{n,1}}{s_{n}t_{n,1}}-z|}[u_{n,1}(z)]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

meanwhile, in view of (
5.5
),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\theta_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{1})\setminus B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|x_{n,1}-x^{\prime}|}{|x-x^{\prime}|}[v_{n}(x^{\prime})-1]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(0)^{p}(\rho+o(1))^{p}}{2\pi}s_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus B_{R_{n,1}s_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})}\left|\ln\frac{|x_{n,1}-x^{\prime}|}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}\leq Cs_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}.

Thus, putting
x=x_{n,1}+s_{n}t_{n,1}z
, and
2R_{n,1}\leq|z|\leq\frac{\dist(x_{1},\partial\Omega)/2}{s_{n}t_{n,1}}
, we have that,

\displaystyle u_{n,1}(z)=t_{n,1}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\theta_{n}(v_{n}(x_{n,1}+s_{n}t_{n,1}z)-1)=O(1)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{B_{2}(0)}\ln\frac{|z^{\prime}|}{|z-z^{\prime}|}[u_{n,1}(z^{\prime})]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}=

\displaystyle O(1)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|z|}\int\limits_{B_{1}(0)}\ln|z^{\prime}|[w^{*}(z^{\prime})]_{+}^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{z^{\prime}}.

This fact immediately implies that,

\displaystyle[v_{n}(x)-1]_{+}^{p}\leq 0,\qquad\mbox{ in }B_{r}(x^{*})\setminus B_{2R_{n}s_{n}t_{n,1}(x_{n,1})},

which contradicts the assumption (
5.2
), that is, for some
x_{n,2}\in B_{r}(x_{1})\setminus B_{2R_{n}s_{n}(x_{n,1})}
,
v_{n}(x_{n,2})-1>0
for any
n
.
Therefore, as far as (
5.1
) is satisfied, there cannot be a Fading spike at
x^{*}
, as claimed.

5.3.
Formation of another spike

Because of (
5.1
) and since we have ruled out the Fading spike at
x^{*}
,
we necessarily have
s_{n}t_{n,2}\rightarrow 0^{+}
together with (
5.4
). Therefore, for any
R\geq 1
and for
n
large,

(5.7)

\displaystyle B_{Rs_{n}t_{n,2}}(x_{n,2})\cap B_{Rs_{n}t_{n,1}}(x_{n,1})=\emptyset.

In particular the rescaled functions
u_{n,2}
defined in (
1.8
) assume their global maximum at the origin:

\sup\limits_{B_{R}(0)}u_{n,2}=u_{n,2}(0)=\phi(0)
for any
R>0
and for
n
large. Moreover we have the following
uniform bound about the
(p)

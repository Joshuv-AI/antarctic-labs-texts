Introduction and preliminaries

A fundamental question in the theory of functional equations is: Under what conditions is a function that satisfies approximately a functional equation necessarily close to an exact solution of that equation? If such conditions are met, the equation is said to be stable.
The initial stability problem related to group homomorphisms was proposed by Ulam in 1940 (see
[
30
]
). In 1941, the investigation into the stability theory of functional equations began with Hyers’ pioneering work. In his paper
[
21
]
, Hyers provided a partial affirmative response to Ulam’s question concerning the additive functional equation, specifically when the groups are Banach spaces. This result was subsequently generalized in 1950 by Aoki
[
5
]
, Bourgin
[
9
]
, and Rassias
[
28
]
, who extended the findings to include additive and linear mappings by addressing the unbounded Cauchy difference. Further advancing this field, Găvruţă
[
19
]
in 1994 introduced a broader generalization of the results by Rassias et al., replacing the bounded condition with a general control positive function
\varphi(x,y)
to establish the existence of a unique linear mapping.

During this period, a special form of stability known as hyperstability was identified. Hyperstability of a functional equation requires that any mapping approximately satisfying the equation (in some defined sense) must indeed be an exact solution. Although the term ”hyperstability” was first introduced by Maksa in 2001
[
24
]
, its earliest occurrence dates back to 1949, as noted by Bourgin in
[
8
]
. Among the most renowned methods for proving the hyperstability of functional equations is the fixed point approach, which has gained prominence over the past two decades thanks to the contributions of J. Brzdȩk and K. Ciepliński
[
10
]
to the fixed point theory, followed by various other authors. For further reference, see
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
Additionally, numerous papers have been published addressing the hyperstability of functional equations (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
18
,
16
,
20
,
22
]
).
The functional equation

f(x+y)+f(x-y)=2f(x)+2f(y)

(4)

is called a quadratic functional equation, and
any solution of this equation is referred to as a quadratic mapping.
The problem of Hyers-Ulam stability for the quadratic functional equation was established by Skof
[
29
]
for mappings
f:X\to Y
, where
X
is a normed space and
Y
is a Banach space. Cholewa
[
15
]
observed that Skof’s theorem remains valid if the domain
X
is replaced by an Abelian group.
Additionally, Czerwik
[
17
]
demonstrated the Hyers-Ulam-Rassias stability of the quadratic functional equation.

Research on modular and modular spaces as extensions of normed spaces was initiated by Nakano
[
26
]
. Since the 1950s, numerous eminent mathematicians
[
6
,
27
,
31
]
have made significant contributions to this field. Orlicz spaces and interpolation theory are two examples of applications of modular spaces in
[
23
,
25
,
27
]
.
We now present the definition, properties and usual terminologies of the theory of modular spaces.

Definition 1
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary vector space. A functional
\rho:Y\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called a modular, if for arbitrary
u,v\in Y
, the following conditions hold:

1.

\rho(u)=0
if and only if
u=0
.

2.

\rho(\alpha u)=\rho(u)
for every scalar
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=1
.

3.

\rho(\alpha u+\beta v)\leq\rho(u)+\rho(v)
if and only if
\alpha+\beta=1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.

If condition (
3
) is replaced by:

4.

\rho(\alpha u+\beta v)\leq\alpha\rho(u)+\beta\rho(v)
if and only if
\alpha+\beta=1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
, then we say that
\rho
is a convex modular.

A modular
\rho
defines a corresponding modular space, denoted by
Y_{\rho}
, which is given by:

Y_{\rho}=\{u\in Y:\rho(\lambda u)\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\lambda\rightarrow 0\}.

A function modular is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{s}
-condition if there exists
\tau_{s}>0
such that

\rho(su)\leq\tau_{s}\rho(u)
for all
u\in Y_{\rho}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
be a sequence in
Y_{\rho}
and let
u\in Y_{\rho}
. Then

1.

The sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is said to be
\rho
-convergent to
u
, denoted as
u_{n}\rightarrow u
, if
\rho\left(u_{n}-u\right)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

2.

The sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is called
\rho
-Cauchy if
\rho\left(u_{n}-u_{m}\right)\rightarrow 0
as
n,m\rightarrow\infty
.

3.

Y_{\rho}
is said to be
\rho
-complete if every
\rho
-Cauchy sequence in
Y_{\rho}
is
\rho
-convergent.

Proposition 1
.

In a modular space,

•

If
u_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}u
and a is a constant vector, then
u_{n}+a\overset{\rho}{\to}u+a
.

•

If
u_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}u
and
v_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}v
then
\alpha u_{n}+\beta v_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}\alpha u+\beta v
, where
\alpha+\beta\leq 1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.

Remark 1
.

Note that
\rho(u)
is an increasing function, for all
u\in X
.
Suppose
0<a<b
. Then, by property (4) of Definition
1
with
v=0
, we have
\rho(ax)=\rho\left(\dfrac{a}{b}bu\right)\leq\rho(bu)
for all
u\in Y
.
Moreover, if
\rho
is a convex modular on
Y
and
|\alpha|\leq 1
, then
\rho(\alpha u)\leq\alpha\rho(u)
.

In general, if
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
, then

\rho(\lambda_{1}u_{1}+\lambda_{2}u_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}u_{n})\leq\lambda_{1}\rho(u_{1})+\lambda_{2}\rho(u_{2})+\dots+\lambda_{n}\rho(u_{n}).

If
\{u_{n}\}
is
\rho
-convergent to
u
, then
\{cu_{n}\}
is
\rho
-convergent to
cu
, where
|c|\leq 1
.
However, the
\rho
-convergence of a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
to
u
does not imply that
\{\alpha u_{n}\}
is
\rho
-convergent to
\alpha u
for scalars
\alpha
with
|\alpha|>1
.

If
\rho
is a convex modular satisfying the
\Delta_{s}
-condition with
0<\tau_{s}<s
, then

\rho(u)\leq\tau_{s}\rho(\dfrac{1}{s}u)\leq\dfrac{\tau_{s}}{s}\rho(u)\text{ for all }u.

Hence,
\rho=0
. Consequently, we must have
\tau_{s}\geq s
if
\rho
is convex modular.

Hyperstability of Equation (
4
)

In this section, we investigate the hyperstability of Eq. (
4
) in modular spaces. Specifically, we will prove that every approximate solution of Eq. (
4
), under some conditions, is an exact solution to it. Throughout this section, we assume that
X
is a vector space over a field
\mathbb{K}
, and
Y_{\rho}
is a convex modular space satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
E
is a non-empty subset of
X
that is symmetric with respect to
0
and satisfies the conditions
x+y,x-y\in E
and
kx\in E
for all
x,y\in E
and all
k\in\mathbb{K}
. Let
\alpha:E^{2}\to[0,\infty)
be a function such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(x,nx)=0,

(5)

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(nx,ny)=0,

for all
x,y\in E
. Let
\varphi:E\to Y_{\rho}
be a mapping satisfying

\rho(\varphi(x+y)+\varphi(x-y)-2\varphi(x)-2\varphi(y)\Big)\leq\alpha(x,y),

for all
x,y\in E
. Then
\varphi
is quadratic on
E
.

Proof.

Letting
y=nx
in (
5
), we obtain

\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x+nx)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x-nx)-\varphi(x)-\varphi(nx)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha(x,nx),

for all
x\in E
.
Hence, for all
x,y\in E
, we have

\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi(y+ny)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi(y-ny)-\varphi(y)-\varphi(ny)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha(y,ny),

\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big(x+y+n(x+y)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big(x+y-n(x+y)\big)-\varphi(x+y)-\varphi\big(n(x+y)\big)\right)\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha(x+y,n(x+y)),

and

\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big(x-y+n(x-y)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big(x-y-n(x-y)\big)-\varphi(x-y)-\varphi\big(n(x-y)\big)\right)\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha(x-y,n(x-y)),

for all
x,y\in E
. Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\varphi(x)=\rho-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((n+1)x\big)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((1-n)x\big)-\varphi(nx)\right),

\varphi(y)=\rho-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((n+1)y\big)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((1-n)y\big)-\varphi(ny)\right),

\varphi(x+y)=\rho-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((n+1)(x+y)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((1-n)(x+y)\big)-\varphi\big(n(x+y)\big)\right),

\varphi(x-y)=\rho-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((n+1)(x-y)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi\big((1-n)(x-y)\big)-\varphi\big(n(x-y)\big)\right),

Then, we have

\displaystyle\rho\left(\frac{1}{7}\varphi(x+y)+\frac{1}{7}\varphi(x-y)-\frac{2}{7}\varphi(x)-\frac{2}{7}\varphi(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x+y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x+y))-\varphi(n(x+y))\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x-y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x-y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x-y))-\varphi(n(x-y))\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)x)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)x)-\varphi(nx)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)y)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)y)-\varphi(ny)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x+y))-\varphi(n(x+y))\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x-y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x-y))-\varphi(n(x-y))\right).

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x+y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x+y))-\varphi(n(x+y))\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x-y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x-y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x-y))-\varphi(n(x-y))\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)x)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)x)-\varphi(nx)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)y)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)y)-\varphi(ny)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau}{14}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x-y))-\varphi((n+1)x)-\varphi((n+1)y)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau}{14}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x-y))-\varphi((1-n)x)-\varphi((1-n)y)\right).

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x+y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x+y))-\varphi(n(x+y))\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x-y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)(x-y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x-y))-\varphi(n(x-y))\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(x)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)x)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)x)-\varphi(nx)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{7}\rho\left(\varphi(y)-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((n+1)y)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)y)-\varphi(ny)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau}{28}\alpha((n+1)x,(n+1)y)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau^{2}}{28}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-n)(x-y))-\varphi((1-n)x)-\varphi((1-n)y)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau^{2}}{28}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi(n(x+y))+\frac{1}{2}\varphi(n(x-y))-\varphi(nx)-\varphi(ny)\right).

for all
x,y\in E
. Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\rho\left(\frac{1}{7}\varphi(x+y)+\frac{1}{7}\varphi(x-y)-\frac{2}{7}\varphi(x)-\frac{2}{7}\varphi(y)\right)\rightarrow 0

which implies that

\varphi(x+y)+\varphi(x-y)=2\varphi(x)+2\varphi(y),

for
x,y\in E
, which means that
\varphi
is quadratic on
E
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\theta\geq 0
and
p
and
q
be two real numbers such that
p+q<0
. Let
\varphi:X\to Y_{\rho}
be a mapping satisfying

\rho\Big(\varphi(x+y)+\varphi(x-y)-2\varphi(x)-2\varphi(y)\Big)\leq\theta\|x\|^{p}\|y\|^{q},

for all
x,y\in E
. Then
\varphi
is quadratic on
E
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha(x,y)=\theta\|x\|^{p}\|y\|^{q}
for all
x,y\in E
. Since
p+q<0
, at least one of
p
or
q
must be negative. Assume that
q<0
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(x,ny)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta n^{q}\|x\|^{p}\|y\|^{q}=0

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(nx,ny)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta n^{p+q}\|x\|^{p}\|y\|^{q}=0.

Therefore, the conditions in Theorem
1
hold which means that
\varphi
is quadratic on
E
.
∎

3
Hyperstability of a general linear functional equation

The following section investigates the hyperstability of a general linear functional equation, providing conditions under which approximate solutions are guaranteed to coincide exactly with the functional equation in a modular space setting.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
E
is a non-empty subset of
X
, symmetric with respect to 0 , and satisfying
x+y,x-y\in E
and
kx\in E
for all
x,y\in E
and all
k\in\mathbb{K}
.
Let
a,b\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha:E^{2}\to[0,\infty)
be a function such that:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha\big(a^{-1}(n+1)x,-b^{-1}nx\big)=0

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha\big(nx,ny\big)=0,

for all
x,y\in E\setminus\{0\}
. Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
,
C\in Y_{\rho}
such that
|A|\leq 1
and
|B|\leq 1
and let
\varphi:X\to Y_{\rho}
satisfying:

\rho\big(\varphi(ax+by)-A\varphi(x)-B\varphi(y)-C\big)\leq\alpha(x,y),

(6)

for all
x,y\in M_{\alpha}=\{z\in E:\|z\|\geq\alpha\}
for some
\alpha>0
. Then
\varphi
satisfies

\varphi(ax+by)=A\varphi(x)+B\varphi(y)+C

(A+B)\varphi(0)=A\varphi(x)+B\varphi\big(-ab^{-1}x\big),

for all
x,y\in E

Substituting
x
by
a^{-1}(n+1)x
and
y
by

6
), we get

\rho\big(\varphi(x)-A\varphi(a^{-1}(n+1)x)-B\varphi(-b^{-1}nx)-C\big)\leq\alpha\big(a^{-1}(n+1)x,-b^{-1}nx\big),

\displaystyle\rho\Bigg(\frac{1}{4}\varphi(ax+by)-\frac{A}{4}\varphi(x)-\frac{B}{4}\varphi(y)-\frac{C}{4}\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\rho\Big(A\varphi(x)-\big(A^{2}\varphi(a^{-1}(n+1)x)+AB\varphi(-b^{-1}nx)+AC\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\rho\Big(B\varphi(y)-\big(AB\varphi(a^{-1}(n+1)y)+B^{2}\varphi(-b^{-1}nx)+BC\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{|A|\tau}{8}\rho\Big(\varphi(a^{-1}(n+1)(ax+by))-A\varphi(a^{-1}(n+1)x)-B\varphi(a^{-1}(n+1)y)-C\Big)

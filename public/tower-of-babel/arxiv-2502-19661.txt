Thermodynamic speed limit for non-adiabatic work and its classical-quantum decomposition
Aoi Yamauchi, Rihito Nagase, Kaixin Li, Takahiro Sagawa, Ken Funo

Abstract
Understanding the fundamental constraint on work far beyond the adiabatic regime is crucial to investigating fast and efficient energy extraction or consumption processes. In this study, we derive thermodynamic speed limits for non-adiabatic work and quantify the fundamental costs of non-adiabatic work extraction or consumption processes in open quantum systems, where the costs are quantified by geometric and thermodynamic quantities. We further decompose the non-adiabatic work into classical and quantum contributions and derive their thermodynamic speed limits, clarifying the classical and quantum nature of the fundamental costs. The obtained results are numerically demonstrated by driven two-level systems.

II
Setup

We assume that the quantum system of interest is weakly coupled to a heat bath at an inverse temperature
\beta
. By assuming the standard Born-Markov-Secular approximations, we arrive at the Markovian master equation of the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad form
[
51
,
52
,
53
]
, given by

\dot{\rho}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho_{t}]=\mathcal{H}_{t}[\rho_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho_{t}],

(1)

where
\dot{X}_{t}:=\partial_{t}X_{t}
is a short-hand notation of the time-derivative of the quantity
X_{t}
, and

\mathcal{H}_{t}[\rho_{t}]:=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{t},\rho_{t}]

(2)

describes the effect of unitary time-evolution generated by the time-dependent Hamiltonian
H_{t}=H[\lambda_{t}]
. Here, we denote the time-dependence of the Hamiltonian by a function
\lambda_{t}
. The second term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
1
) is the dissipator

\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho_{t}]:=\sum_{\omega_{t}}\gamma(\omega_{t})\left(L_{\omega_{t}}\rho_{t}L_{\omega_{t}}^{{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{L_{\omega_{t}}^{{\dagger}}L_{\omega_{t}},\rho_{t}\right\}\right),

(3)

which describes the effect of heat bath, where
\gamma(\omega_{t})
is the rate and
L_{\omega_{t}}
is the jump operator that allows the system to jump from one energy eigenstate to another with their energy difference equal to
\hbar\omega_{t}
, and satisfies
[L_{\omega_{t}},H_{t}]=\hbar\omega_{t}L_{\omega_{t}}
. We further assume the detailed balance condition

\frac{\gamma(\omega_{t})}{\gamma(-\omega_{t})}=\exp(\beta\hbar\omega_{t})

(4)

to make Eq. (
1
) thermodynamically consistent. In particular, the instantaneous steady-state of the system is given by the thermal distribution
\rho^{\mathrm{eq}}_{t}:=\exp(-\beta H_{t})/Z_{t}
with
Z_{t}:=\mathrm{Tr}[\exp(-\beta H_{t})]
, that is

\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho^{\mathrm{eq}}_{t}]=0.

(5)

During time evolution, work is invested in the system from the external control that drives the Hamiltonian, defined as

\dot{W}:=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{t}\dot{H}_{t}].

(6)

In the adiabatically slow driving limit, the density operator of the system can be approximated by its instantaneous thermal state
\rho(t)=\rho_{t}^{\mathrm{eq}}+O(\dot{\lambda}_{t})
. In this regime, the work reduces to the adiabatic work, defined as

\dot{W}_{\rm ad}:=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}\rho_{t}^{\mathrm{eq}}]=\dot{F}_{\rm eq},

(7)

where
F_{\rm eq}:=-\beta^{-1}\ln Z_{t}
is the equilibrium free energy. When driving is quasi-adiabatic, one can use the adiabatic perturbation theory and obtain the next-order correction to Eq. (
7
), expressed by

\dot{W}=\dot{W}_{\rm ad}+\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{+}[\dot{\rho}_{t}^{\mathrm{eq}}]]+O(\dot{\lambda}_{t}^{3}),

(8)

where
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{+}
is the Drazin inverse of the Liouville superoperator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
, defined as
[
54
,
16
,
55
]

\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t}[B]:=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu\,e^{\nu\mathcal{L}_{t}}(\rho^{\mathrm{eq}}_{t}\mathrm{Tr}[B]-B).

(9)

If
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is diagonalizable, let us denote it as
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\sum_{\Gamma}\Gamma P_{\Gamma}
, where
\Gamma
and
P_{\Gamma}
are the eigenvalues and projection superoperators, respectively. Then, the Drazin inverse
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{+}
can be expressed as
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{+}=\sum_{\Gamma\neq 0}\Gamma^{-1}P_{\Gamma}
, where the real part of
-\Gamma^{-1}
quantifies the relaxation time-scales for each eigenmode.

It should be noted that the second term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
8
) can be expressed as a thermodynamic metric in the control parameter space
\lambda_{t}
, and it is possible to optimize the work by finding the optimal driving protocol
\lambda_{t}

[
16
]
.
However, when driving is beyond this near-adiabatic regime, we need a general framework that quantifies the non-adiabatic contribution to the work.
To this end, we employ techniques developed in the study of thermodynamic speed limits and derive a general upper bound on the non-adiabatic work
\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}
.

III
Main result 1: thermodynamic speed limits for non-adiabatic work

In this section, we derive a general upper bound on the non-adiabatic work. To begin with, we use the following property of the Drazin inverse (see Appendix
A
):

\rho_{t}-\rho_{t}^{\mathrm{eq}}=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{+}[\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho_{t}]]=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{+}[\dot{\rho}_{t}],

(10)

and express the non-adiabatic work as

\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{+}[\dot{\rho}_{t}]]=\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]\dot{\rho}_{t}].

(11)

Here,
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
denotes the adjoint of the superoperator
\mathcal{K}
, defined by the relation
\langle X,\mathcal{K}[Y]\rangle=\langle\tilde{\mathcal{K}}[X],Y\rangle
, with
\langle X,Y\rangle:=\mathrm{Tr}[X^{\dagger}Y]
being the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product.
The explicit form of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}
reads

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[B]:=\int^{\infty}_{0}d\nu e^{\nu\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}}\Bigl(\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{t}^{\mathrm{eq}}B]I-B\Bigr),

(12)

where
I
is the identity operator and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}
is the adjoint of the Lindblad superoperator defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(B):=i[H,B]+\sum_{\omega_{t}}\gamma(\omega_{t})\Bigl(L_{\omega_{t}}^{\dagger}BL_{\omega_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}\{L_{\omega_{t}}^{\dagger}L_{\omega_{t}},B\}\Bigr).

(13)

It should be noted that unlike Eq. (
8
), Eq. (
11
) is exact for any driving speed and contains all orders of
\dot{\lambda}_{t}
.

Based on the expression (
11
), we derive a thermodynamic speed limit for non-adiabatic work as

|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq\underbrace{\sqrt{4g_{tt}^{\rm QF}V_{\rho_{t}}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}])}}_{\text{non-diagonal}}+\underbrace{\sqrt{2A(\rho_{t})\dot{\sigma}}}_{\text{diagonal}}=:\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm nd}+\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm d},

(14)

which is the first main result of this paper. The proof of Eq. (
14
) is shown in Appendix
B
, where we utilize the techniques developed in Refs.
[
24
,
30
]
. Here, the bound contains two terms
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm nd}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm d}
, depending on the non-diagonal and diagonal contributions using the basis of the density operator
\rho_{t}
, respectively.

In the following, we explain each term that appears in Eq. (
14
).

•

The quantity
g^{\rm QF}_{tt}
describes a purely quantum contribution to the quantum Fisher information metric and is defined as
[
56
,
22
]

g^{\rm QF}_{tt}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{(p_{n}-p_{m})^{2}}{p_{n}+p_{m}}|\langle n|\partial_{t}m\rangle|^{2},

(15)

where we denote the spectral decomposition of the density operator as
\rho_{t}=\sum_{n}p_{n}(t)|n(t)\rangle\langle n(t)|
, and
p_{n}(t)
and
|n(t)\rangle
are the eigenvalues and eigenstates of
\rho_{t}
, respectively. We also use the notation
\partial_{t}|m(t)\rangle=|\partial_{t}m\rangle
. This quantity characterizes a purely quantum contribution to the quadratic sensitivity of the time-variation of density operators (see Appendix
B.2
for details), and also quantifies the speed of coherent, unitary part of the time-evolution (see Eq. (
65
)).

•

The quantity
V_{\rho_{t}}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}])
quantifies the fluctuation of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]
, where the variance is defined as
V_{\rho_{t}}[X]:=\mathrm{Tr}[X^{2}\rho_{t}]-(\mathrm{Tr}[X\rho_{t}])^{2}
.
When
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is diagonalizable,
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}
rescales
\dot{H}_{t}
by dividing it with effective relaxation rates
\Gamma
for each eigenmode, meaning that
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]
characterizes the relative time-scale between the driving speed
\dot{\lambda}_{t}
of the Hamiltonian and the relaxation rates
\Gamma
. The analytical expression of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]
for a two-level system example is given in Eq. (
92
).

•

The activity-like quantity
A
is defined as

A(\rho_{t}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\langle n|\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]|n\rangle^{2}\langle n|\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\rm sym}[\rho_{t}]|n\rangle,

(16)

where

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\rm sym}[\rho_{t}]:=\sum_{\omega_{t}}\gamma(\omega_{t})\Bigl(L_{\omega_{t}}\rho_{t}L_{\omega_{t}}^{\dagger}+\frac{1}{2}\{L_{\omega_{t}}^{\dagger}L_{\omega_{t}},\rho_{t}\}\Bigr)

(17)

is defined by replacing the minus sign with the plus sign in front of the second term of the dissipator (
3
). In particular,

A_{\rm act}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\langle n|\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\rm sym}[\rho_{t}]|n\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\rm sym}[\rho_{t}]]=\sum_{\omega_{t}}\gamma(\omega_{t})\mathrm{Tr}[L_{\omega_{t}}^{\dagger}L_{\omega_{t}}\rho_{t}]

(18)

quantifies the number of total jumps per unit time and is called (dynamical) activity
[
23
,
24
]
. Therefore, the term
A
is an activity-like quantity that quantifies the instantaneous fluctuation of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]
multiplied by the time-scale of quantum jumps for each eigenbasis
|n(t)\rangle
of
\rho_{t}
. See also Eq. (
42
) for the simplified expression of
A
in the case of two-level systems.

•

The entropy production rate
\dot{\sigma}
is defined as

\dot{\sigma}:=\dot{S}-\beta\dot{Q}\geq 0,

(19)

where
S(\rho):=-\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\ln\rho]
is the von Neumann entropy of the system,
\dot{Q}:=\mathrm{Tr}[H_{t}\dot{\rho}_{t}]
is the heat that quantifies the energy exchange between the system and the heat bath, and the nonnegativity of
\dot{\sigma}
represents the second law of thermodynamics
[
57
,
58
]
. The entropy production quantifies the thermodynamic cost of non-adiabatic processes and vanishes in the adiabatic limit.

The obtained thermodynamic speed limit (
14
) is valid for any driving speed and represents a general upper bound on non-adiabatic work.

In the adiabatic limit, both
g_{tt}^{\rm QF}
and
\dot{\sigma}
vanish, thereby the bound quantifies geometric and thermodynamic costs to produce work in the non-adiabatic regime.

The non-diagonal part of the bound
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm nd}
depends on
g_{tt}^{\rm QF}
, which characterizes the speed of coherent unitary part of the time-evolution. Moreover, it vanishes in the limit where Eq. (
1
) can be approximated by a classical master equation. On the other hand,
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm d}
depends on the entropy production
\dot{\sigma}
and the activity-like quantity
A
, which quantifies the speed of the stochastic jump part of the time-evolution
[
23
,
24
]
. Moreover, it vanishes in the limit where Eq. (
1
) can be approximated by the time-evolution for isolated quantum systems.
Based on the above discussion, the bounds
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm nd}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm d}
can be interpreted as the quantum and classical part of the speed limit for non-adiabatic work, respectively. In the next section, we generalize this classical-quantum interpretation by deriving another bound based on the classical-quantum decomposition of the non-adiabatic work.

IV
Main result 2: Classical-quantum decomposition of the non-adiabatic work and their thermodynamic speed limits

In this section, we decompose the non-adiabatic work into classical and quantum contributions and derive thermodynamic speed limits for individual terms. Specifically, instead of splitting the bound into diagonal and non-diagonal contributions using the basis of
\rho_{t}
as done in Sec.
III
, we now use the basis of
H_{t}
and derive a similar bound.

IV.1
Classical-quantum decomposition of the non-adiabatic work

To begin with, we decompose the time derivative of the Hamiltonian into the classical and quantum contributions, defined by

\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm cl}:=\sum_{n}\dot{\epsilon}_{n}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|,\ \ \dot{H}_{t}^{\rm qm}:=\sum_{n}\epsilon_{n}(|\dot{\epsilon}_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|+|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\dot{\epsilon}_{n}|).

(20)

Based on the decomposition (
20
), the non-adiabatic work can be similarly decomposed into classical and quantum contributions such as
\dot{W}=\dot{W}_{\rm cl}+\dot{W}_{\rm qm}
, where

\dot{W}_{\rm cl}:=\sum_{n}\dot{\epsilon}_{n}\langle\epsilon_{n}|\rho_{t}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm cl}\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]

(21)

is the work originating from the time variation of the energy eigenvalues
\epsilon_{n}(t)
, and depends only on the diagonal component of the density operator using the energy eigenbasis, i.e.,
\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}=\sum_{n}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|\rho_{t}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|
. Therefore, Eq. (
21
) quantifies the classical contribution to the work. On the other hand,

\dot{W}_{\rm qm}:=\sum_{n}\epsilon_{n}\Bigl(\langle\dot{\epsilon}_{n}|\rho_{t}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle+\langle\epsilon_{n}|\rho_{t}|\dot{\epsilon}_{n}\rangle\Bigr)=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm qm}\rho_{t}^{\rm qm}]

(22)

depends solely on the coherence of the density operator
\rho_{t}^{\rm qm}:=\rho_{t}-\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}
and thus quantifies quantum contribution to the work.
We note that in the adiabatic limit, we have
\dot{W}_{\rm cl}=\dot{W}_{\rm ad}+O(\dot{\lambda}_{t}^{2})
and
\dot{W}_{\rm qm}=O(\dot{\lambda}_{t}^{2})
. Therefore, the non-adiabatic contribution to the work is decomposed as

\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}=\underbrace{(\dot{W}_{\rm cl}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad})}_{\text{classical}}+\underbrace{\dot{W}_{\rm qm}}_{\text{quantum}}.

(23)

In the following, we discuss how each term in Eq. (
23
) can be bounded.

IV.2
Thermodynamic speed limits for non-adiabatic classical work

To derive an upper bound on the non-adiabatic classical work, we start from the expression

\dot{W}_{\rm cl}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm cl}(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}-\rho_{t}^{\rm eq})]=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm cl}\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}[\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]]],

(24)

where
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}
is the Drazin inverse of
\mathcal{D}_{t}
, and we have used the relation
\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}-\rho_{t}^{\rm eq}=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}[\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]]
, which follows from Eq. (
45
).
By following a derivation similar to that for Eq. (
14
), which is detailed in Appendix
C
, the non-adiabatic classical work
\dot{W}_{\rm cl}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}
is bounded as

|\dot{W}_{\rm cl}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq\sqrt{2A(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}}=:\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm cl},

(25)

where

\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}:=\dot{S}_{\rm cl}-\beta\dot{Q}\geq 0

(26)

quantifies the incoherent part of the entropy production, with
S_{\rm cl}:=S(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})
being the diagonal entropy. We can further show that
[
59
,
60
,
40
]
(see Eq. (
86
))

\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}\leq\dot{\sigma},

(27)

which means that the generation of coherence increases the entropy production. The explicit expression of
A
in Eq. (
25
) reads

A(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\langle\epsilon_{n}|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm cl}]|\epsilon_{n}\rangle^{2}\langle\epsilon_{n}|\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\rm sym}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]|\epsilon_{n}\rangle.

(28)

This expression follows from the definition of
A(\rho_{t})
in Eq. (
16
), by noting that the diagonal basis of
\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}
is given by
|\epsilon_{n}\rangle
, and that
\langle\epsilon_{n}|\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]|\epsilon_{n}\rangle=\langle\epsilon_{n}|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm cl}]|\epsilon_{n}\rangle
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{t}^{+}
is the adjoint of
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}
.

The obtained bound (
25
) shows that the non-adiabatic classical work is bounded by the incoherent part of the entropy production and the activity-like quantity.
Therefore, the bound (
25
) characterizes the incoherent, classical part of the thermodynamic speed limit on non-adiabatic work and vanishes when the dynamics is purely coherent (see also Sec.
V.2
).

IV.3
Thermodynamic speed limits for non-adiabatic quantum work

Next, we derive an upper bound on the non-adiabatic quantum work by using the expression

\dot{W}_{\rm qm}=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm qm}\rho_{t}^{\rm qm}]=\mathrm{Tr}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm qm}\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}[\mathcal{H}_{t}[\rho_{t}]]],

(29)

where
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}
is the Drazin inverse of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
defined in Eq. (
47
), and we have used the relation
\rho_{t}^{\rm qm}=\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}[\mathcal{H}_{t}[\rho_{t}]]
, which follows from Eq. (
48
). Now,
\dot{W}_{\rm qm}
can be bounded as (see Appendix
C
for the proof):

|\dot{W}_{\rm qm}|\leq\frac{1}{\hbar}\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(H_{t})V_{\rho_{t}}(H_{\rm cd})}=:\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm qm},

(30)

where

\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(H_{t}):=2\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{(p_{n}-p_{m})^{2}}{p_{n}+p_{m}}\left|\langle n|H_{t}|m\rangle\right|^{2}

(31)

is the quantum Fisher information that quantifies the coherence of the density operator
\rho_{t}^{\rm qm}
, and

H_{\rm cd}:=i\hbar\sum_{n}(I-|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|)|\partial_{t}\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|

(32)

is the counter-diabatic Hamiltonian that is used as a control Hamiltonian to guide the system along the instantaneous energy eigenstate
[
61
]
. Note that
V_{\rho_{t}}(H_{\rm cd})
that appears in the bound
\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm qm}
is the energy fluctuation of the control Hamiltonian
H_{\rm cd}
, which quantifies the speed of controlling non-adiabatic time-evolution in isolated
[
62
,
63
]
and quasi-adiabatic open systems
[
24
]
. Moreover,
V_{\rho_{t}}(H_{\rm cd})
sets an upper bound on the work fluctuation in isolated systems controlled by
H_{\rm cd}

[
62
]
.

The obtained bound (
30
) shows that the non-adiabatic quantum work is bounded by the amount of coherence multiplied by the energy variance of
H_{\rm cd}
, where the latter quantifies how the energy eigenstates vary in time via non-adiabatic drives. Therefore, the bound (
30
) characterizes coherent, quantum part of the thermodynamic speed limit on non-adiabatic work and vanishes in the classical limit.

We further note that from Eq. (
51
), we have

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]=\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm qm}]=H_{\rm cd},

(33)

and thus
V_{\rho_{t}}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}])
that appears in Eq. (
14
) reduces to
V_{\rho_{t}}(H_{\rm cd})
. Similarly,
4g_{tt}^{\rm QF}
reduces to
\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(H_{t})
when the generator is given by
\mathcal{H}_{t}
[see Eq. (
65
)]. Therefore, the first term of the bound in Eq. (
14
) can also be interpreted as a quantum contribution to the bound of the non-adiabatic work.

IV.4
Classical-quantum decomposition of the thermodynamic speed limit

We now use the triangle inequality
|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq|\dot{W}_{\rm cl}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|+|\dot{W}_{\rm qm}|
and combine Eqs. (
25
) and (
30
) to derive another version of the non-adiabatic work bound as

|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq\underbrace{\frac{1}{\hbar}\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(H_{t})V_{\rho_{t}}(H_{\rm cd})}}_{\text{quantum}}+\underbrace{\sqrt{2A(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}}}_{\text{classical}}=\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm qm}+\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm cl}.

(34)

These classical-quantum decomposition of the thermodynamic speed limits for non-adiabatic work [Eqs. (
25
), (
30
), and (
34
)] are the second main result of this paper. We again note that the splitting of the bound into
\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm qm}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm cl}
is based on the decomposition with respect to the non-diagonal and diagonal contributions using the basis of the Hamiltonian
H_{t}
, respectively.

By combining (
14
) and (
34
), we obtain

|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq\min\left\{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm nd}+\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm d},\ \mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm qm}+\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\rm cl}\right\},

(35)

where the minimum of the right-hand side of (
35
) depends on the details of the model.

V
Special cases of the speed limit

In this section, we consider two limiting cases of the obtained result.

V.1
Classical drive

First, let us consider a classical driving of the Hamiltonian by only changing its energy eigenvalues
\epsilon_{n}(t)
in time and keeping the eigenstates constant. Then, we have
\dot{H}_{t}=\dot{H}_{\rm cl}
and
\dot{W}=\dot{W}_{\rm cl}
. Therefore, the second main result (
34
) reduces to

|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq\sqrt{2A(\rho_{t})\dot{\sigma}}\hskip 14.22636pt(\text{classical drive}).

(36)

It should also be noted that when the initial density operator satisfies
\rho_{0}=\rho^{\rm cl}_{0}
, we have
\rho_{t}=\rho^{\rm cl}_{t}
at later times
t
. By assuming this initial condition, we have
g_{tt}^{\rm QF}=0
and
\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}=\dot{\sigma}
. Note that this setup is essentially equivalent to considering classical stochastic systems, and the first main result (
14
) also reduces to Eq. (
36
).

V.2
Quantum drive

Next, let us consider the opposite situation compared to Sec.
V.1
, i.e., the energy eigenstates are time-dependent
|\epsilon_{n}(t)\rangle
but the energy eigenvalues
\epsilon_{n}
do not depend on time. For this quantum drive, we have
\dot{H}_{t}=\dot{H}_{t}^{\rm qm}
,
\dot{W}=\dot{W}^{\rm qm}
, and
\dot{W}_{\rm ad}=0
.
Therefore, the second main result (
30
) reduces to

|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq\frac{1}{\hbar}\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(H_{t})V_{\rho_{t}}(H_{\rm cd})}\hskip 14.22636pt(\text{quantum drive}).

(37)

We consider the following type of two-level system Hamiltonian:

H_{t}=\frac{\Delta_{t}}{2}\sigma_{x}+\frac{q_{t}}{2}\sigma_{z},

(38)

where
\Delta_{t}
and
q_{t}
denote the time-dependence of the external driving field (
\lambda_{t}=(\Delta_{t},q_{t})

\sigma_{x}

and

\sigma_{z}|e\rangle=|e\rangle
and
\sigma_{z}|g\rangle=-|g\rangle
.
The time-dependent Hamiltonian (

q_{t}
can be varied by changing the applied magnetic flux, while
\Delta_{t}
can be varied by changing the applied gate voltage
[
64

The time-evolution equation for the two-level system is assumed to take the following form

\dot{\rho}_{t}=-i[H_{t},\rho_{t}]+\gamma_{\downarrow}\left(\sigma_{t}^{-}\rho\sigma_{t}^{+}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{t}^{+}\sigma_{t}^{-},\rho_{t}\right\}\right)+\gamma_{\uparrow}\left(\sigma_{t}^{+}\rho\sigma_{t}^{-}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sigma_{t}^{-}\sigma_{t}^{+},\rho_{t}\right\}\right),

where
\sigma_{t}^{+}=\left.\left|\epsilon_{+}\right\rangle\right.\hskip-4.2679pt\left.\left\langle\epsilon_{-}\right.\hskip-1.42262pt\right|
and
\sigma_{t}^{-}=\left.\left|\epsilon_{-}\right\rangle\right.\hskip-4.2679pt\left.\left\langle\epsilon_{+}\right.\hskip-1.42262pt\right|
are the raising and lowering operators between energy eigenstates, and
\gamma_{\downarrow}:=\gamma_{0}(N(\omega_{t})+1)
and
\gamma_{\uparrow}:=\gamma_{0}N(\omega_{t})
with
N(\omega_{t}):=1/(e^{\beta\omega_{t}}-1)
are the transition rates satisfying the detailed balance condition
\gamma_{\downarrow}/\gamma_{\uparrow}=e^{\beta\omega_{t}}

p_{\pm}=\langle\epsilon_{\pm}|\rho|\epsilon_{\pm}\rangle

\rho_{+-}=\langle\epsilon_{+}|\rho|\epsilon_{-}\rangle

H_{\rm cd}=i\dot{\theta}_{t}(|\epsilon_{+}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{-}|-|\epsilon_{-}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{+}|)=\dot{\theta}_{t}\sigma_{y},

(40)

and its variance reads

V_{\rho_{t}}(H_{\rm cd})=\dot{\theta}^{2}_{t}\left(1-4(\Im[\rho_{+-}])^{2}\right).

(41)

In addition, the activity-like quantity reads

A(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})=2\left(\frac{\dot{\omega}_{t}}{\gamma_{\downarrow}+\gamma_{\uparrow}}\right)^{2}A_{\rm act},

(42)

where

A_{\rm act}=\gamma_{\uparrow}p_{-}+\gamma_{\downarrow}p_{+}

(43)

is the activity. This expression demonstrates that
A(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})
is essentially given by the total number of jump rates
A_{\rm act}
multiplied by the square of the relative timescales between the change of the Hamiltonian
\dot{\omega}_{t}
and the relaxation rates
\gamma_{\uparrow}+\gamma_{\downarrow}
.

(b) Classical drive

V.1

VII
Conclusion

We have derived the thermodynamic speed limit inequalities for non-adiabatic work (
14
), (
34
). The obtained results clarify the fundamental costs of work extraction for arbitrary driving speed. We have further introduced the classical-quantum decomposition of the non-adiabatic work and clarified how the fundamental costs are divided into classical and quantum contributions (
25
), (
30
) [See also Fig.
1
]. The classical part of the cost is quantified by the entropy production rate and the activity-like quantity, representing thermodynamic costs to generate stochastic jump dynamics. The quantum part of the cost is quantified by the quantum Fisher information and the variance of the counter-diabatic Hamiltonian, representing geometrical costs to generate coherent unitary dynamics.

An interesting future direction is to further investigate the classical-quantum decomposition of non-adiabatic work and analyze the role of quantum coherence in thermodynamics. In the quasi-adiabatic regime, it has been reported in Ref.
[
18
]
that generation of quantum coherence violates the fluctuation-dissipation relation. It has also been observed in various models that generation of quantum coherence is typically detrimental to the performance of heat engines
[
64
]
.
However, a general understanding on the effect of having coherence in the non-adiabatic work extraction processes is not complete and deserves further studies.
Understanding the role of coherence in thermodynamics is anticipated to lead to obtaining the design principle of fast and efficient energy conversion and information processing devices.

It is also interesting to investigate further the geometric and thermodynamic properties of the obtained bound for nonequilibrium states. Extending the concepts of geometric quantities defined for adiabatic or equilibrium states and their optimization methods
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
to nonequilibrium setting is another important future direction. We also note that the obtained thermodynamic speed limit bounds the absolute value of the non-adiabatic work, while the sign of the non-adiabatic work is shown to be non-negative in the slow-driving regime
[
18
]
. It would be interesting if we can further generalize our approach and obtain bounds beyond its absolute value. It would also be an interesting direction if we could derive thermodynamic uncertainty relation bounds on non-adiabatic work, for example, utilizing the fluctuation theorem uncertainty relations
[
65
,
66
]
.

Appendix A
Properties of the Drazin pseudo inverse and its adjoint

The Drazin inverse of the Lindblad superoperator
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t}
satisfies the following properties:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{t}[X]=\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t}[X]=X-\rho^{\mathrm{eq}}_{t}\mathrm{Tr}[X],\\
&\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t}[\rho^{\mathrm{eq}}_{t}]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t}[X]\right]=0.\end{split}

(44)

Note that same relations hold for
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}
(
3
):

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}^{+}_{t}\mathcal{D}_{t}[X]=\mathcal{D}_{t}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{t}[X]=X-\rho^{\mathrm{eq}}_{t}\mathrm{Tr}[X],\\
&\mathcal{D}^{+}_{t}[\rho^{\mathrm{eq}}_{t}]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\mathcal{D}^{+}_{t}[X]\right]=0.\end{split}

(45)

Next, let us consider the Drazin inverse of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
(
2
).
We first note that the steady state of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is no longer unique, since any
\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}
satisfies
\mathcal{H}_{t}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]=0
. For given
\rho_{t}
, the steady-state of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
reads

\lim_{\nu\rightarrow\infty}e^{\nu\mathcal{H}_{t}}\rho_{t}=\sum_{n}\langle\epsilon_{n}|\rho_{t}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|=\rho_{t}^{\rm cl},

(46)

where we assume that the Hamiltonian is non-degenerate for simplicity. Therefore, the steady-state of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
depends on the operator acting on it. The explicit form of
\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}
reads

\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}[X]=i\hbar\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{|\epsilon_{m}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{m}|X|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|}{\epsilon_{m}-\epsilon_{n}},

(47)

and satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}\mathcal{H}_{t}[X]=\mathcal{H}_{t}\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}[X]=X-\sum_{n}\langle\epsilon_{n}|X|\epsilon_{n}\rangle|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|,

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}[X]\right]=0.

(49)

We also note that

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{t}^{+}[X]=-\mathcal{H}_{t}^{+}[X].

(50)

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{m}-\epsilon_{n}}\Bigl(\epsilon_{n}|\epsilon_{m}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{m}|\dot{\epsilon}_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|+\epsilon_{m}|\epsilon_{m}\rangle\langle\dot{\epsilon}_{m}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|\Bigr)

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar\sum_{n\neq m}|\epsilon_{m}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{m}|\dot{\epsilon}_{n}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\rm cd}.

Appendix B
Proof of the first main result

In this section, we show the first main result (
14
). To begin with, we use the diagonal-nondiagonal decomposition of the dissipator
\mathcal{D}[\rho_{t}]=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{d}}[\rho_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{nd}}[\rho_{t}]
introduced in Ref.
[
24
]
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{d}}[\rho_{t}]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho_{t}]|n\rangle\langle n|,

(52)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{nd}}[\rho_{t}]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{\mathcal{D}},\rho_{t}],

(53)

with

H_{\mathcal{D}}:=i\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\left\langle m\left|\mathcal{D}[\rho_{t}]\right|n\right\rangle}{p_{n}-p_{m}}\left.\left|m\right\rangle\right.\hskip-4.2679pt\left.\left\langle n\right.\hskip-1.42262pt\right|

(54)

being the “bath Hamiltonian” that describes unitary part of the time-evolution induced by the bath
[
24
]
.

Using Eqs. (
52
) and (
53
), the non-adiabatic work (
11
) reads

\displaystyle|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]\Bigl(-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{t}+H_{\mathcal{D}},\rho_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho_{t}]\Bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr|

(55)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\left|\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left[\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}],H_{t}+H_{\mathcal{D}}\right]\rho_{t}\right]\right|+\left|\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{d}}[\rho_{t}]]\right|,

(56)

where we use the triangle inequality and obtain the second line.

The first term on the right-hand side of (
56
) can be further bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\left|\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left[\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}],H_{t}+H_{\mathcal{D}}\right]\rho_{t}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(H_{t}+H_{\mathcal{D}})V_{\rho_{t}}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}])}

(57)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{4g_{tt}^{\rm QF}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}])}=:\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm nd},

which is shown in Appendix
B.1
. On the other hand, the second term on the right-hand side of (
56
) can be further bounded as:

\left|\mathrm{Tr}[\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}]\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{d}}[\rho_{t}]]\right|\leq\sqrt{2A(\rho_{t})\dot{\sigma}}=:\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\rm d},

(58)

which is shown in Appendix
B.3
. By combining Eqs. (
56
), (
57
), and (
58
), we obtain

|\dot{W}-\dot{W}_{\rm ad}|\leq\sqrt{4g_{tt}^{\rm QF}V_{\rho_{t}}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{+}[\dot{H}_{t}])}+\sqrt{2A(\rho_{t})\dot{\sigma}},

(59)

which proves the first main result (
14
).

B.1
Proof of (
57
)

We now derive inequality (
57
). The first line of (
57
) follows from the inequality
[
26
,
30
]

|\mathrm{Tr}([X,Y]\rho_{t})|\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(X)V_{\rho_{t}}(Y)},

(60)

where the quantum Fisher information is defined as

\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(X):=2\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{(p_{n}-p_{m})^{2}}{p_{n}+p_{m}}\left|\left\langle n\left|X\right|m\right\rangle\right|^{2}.

(61)

To show inequality (
60
), we use the spectral decomposition of the density operator as
\rho_{t}=\sum_{n}p_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|
and obtain

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Tr}([X,Y]\rho_{t})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum_{m\neq n}(p_{n}-p_{m})\langle n|X|m\rangle\langle m|Y|n\rangle\Bigr|

(62)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{(p_{n}-p_{m})^{2}}{p_{n}+p_{m}}|\langle n|X|m\rangle|^{2}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\neq m}(p_{n}+p_{m})|\langle m|Y|n\rangle|^{2},

where we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and obtain the second line. We further note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\neq m}(p_{n}+p_{m})|\langle m|Y|n\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[Y^{2}\rho_{t}]-\sum_{n}p_{n}|\langle n|Y|n\rangle|^{2}

(63)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[Y^{2}\rho_{t}]-\Bigl(\sum_{n}p_{n}|\langle n|Y|n\rangle|\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\rho_{t}}[Y].

By combining (
62
) and (
63
) and using the definition of the quantum Fisher information, we obtain Eq. (
60
).

The second line of Eq. (
57
) follows from the relation

\langle n|\dot{\rho}_{t}|m\rangle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}(p_{m}-p_{n})\langle n|(H_{t}+H_{\mathcal{D}})|m\rangle=(p_{m}-p_{n})\langle n|\partial_{t}m\rangle,

(64)

for
n\neq m
. Using this expression (
64
), we find that

\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{t}}(H_{t}+H_{\mathcal{D}})=4g_{tt}^{\rm QF},

(65)

completing the proof of (
57
).

B.2
Remarks on the quantum Fisher information metric

In this subsection, we give several remarks on the quantum Fisher information metric. Let us consider the Bures angle, which is the geodesic distance related to the quantum Fisher information metric, defined as
[
22
,
67
]

\mathcal{L}(\rho,\sigma):=\arccos{\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}},

(66)

where

F(\rho,\sigma):=\Bigl(\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\Bigr]\Bigr)^{2}

(67)

\rho_{t}
and
\rho_{t}+d\rho_{t}
and denoting

[

\mathcal{L}(\rho_{t},\rho_{t}+d\rho_{t})=\sqrt{(g_{tt}^{\rm CL}+g_{tt}^{\rm QF})dt^{2}},

(68)

where

g_{tt}^{\rm CL}:=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j}\frac{(\partial_{t}p_{j})^{2}}{p_{j}}

(69)

is the classical Fisher information metric and
g_{tt}^{\rm QF}
defined in Eq. (
15
) describes a purely quantum contribution to Eq. (
68
).

B.3

58
)

To show (
58
), we start by introducing

(76)

) and (

\displaystyle 2A(\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl},

where we have introduced

M_{nm}^{\omega}:=\gamma(\omega)|\langle\epsilon_{n}|L_{\omega}|\epsilon_{m}\rangle|^{2}

(81)

and

q_{n}:=\langle\epsilon_{n}|\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}|\epsilon_{n}\rangle.

(82)

Note that Eq. (
80
) is similar to Eq. (
73
). However, the entropy production rate is now given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n,m,\omega}(M_{nm}^{\omega}q_{m}-M_{mn}^{-\omega}q_{n})\ln\frac{M_{nm}^{\omega}q_{m}}{M_{mn}^{-\omega}q_{n}}

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m,\omega}M_{nm}^{\omega}q_{m}\ln\frac{M_{nm}^{\omega}q_{m}}{M_{mn}^{-\omega}q_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m,\omega}M_{nm}^{\omega}q_{m}\ln\frac{\gamma(\omega)q_{m}}{\gamma(-\omega)q_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\sum_{\omega}\omega\gamma(\omega)\mathrm{Tr}[L_{\omega}^{\dagger}L_{\omega}\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]+\sum_{\omega}\gamma(\omega)\mathrm{Tr}[L_{\omega}^{\dagger}L_{\omega}\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}\ln\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}-\ln\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}L_{\omega}\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}L_{\omega}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\dot{Q}-\mathrm{Tr}[(\ln\rho_{t}^{\rm cl})\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}.

This completes the proof of Eq. (
25
).

In the following, we show
\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}\leq\dot{\sigma}
based on Ref.
[
40
]
. We first introduce a completely-positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) map

\mathcal{E}_{t}[\chi]:=\sum_{n}|\epsilon_{n}(t)\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}(t)|\chi|\epsilon_{n}(t)\rangle\langle\epsilon_{n}(t)|,

(84)

which projects the density operator to diagonal states, i.e.,
\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}=\mathcal{E}_{t}[\rho_{t}]
. We next introduce a CPTP map that describes the infinitesimal time-evolution
t\rightarrow t+\Delta t
as

\Lambda_{t,t+\Delta t}:=(1+\mathcal{L}_{t}\Delta t),

(85)

and denote
\rho_{t+\Delta t}=\Lambda_{t,t+\Delta t}[\rho_{t}]
and
\rho_{t+\Delta t}^{\rm cl}=\Lambda_{t,t+\Delta t}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]
. It should be noted that
\Lambda_{t,t+\Delta t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}[\chi]]=\mathcal{E}_{t}[\Lambda_{t,t+\Delta t}[\chi]]
, and therefore
\rho_{t+\Delta t}^{\rm cl}=\mathcal{E}_{t}[\rho_{t+\Delta t}]
.
We now express the difference between
\dot{\sigma}
and
\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}
as

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}-\dot{\sigma}_{\rm cl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\Bigl(S(\rho_{t+\Delta t})-S(\rho_{t})-S(\rho^{\rm cl}_{t+\Delta t})+S(\rho^{\rm cl}_{t})\Bigr)

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\Bigl(\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\rho_{t}\Bigl(\ln\rho_{t}-\ln\mathcal{E}_{t}[\rho_{t}]\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\rho_{t+\Delta t}\Bigl(\ln\rho_{t+\Delta t}-\ln\mathcal{E}_{t}[\rho_{t+\Delta t}]\Bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\Bigl(D(\rho_{t}||\mathcal{E}_{t}[\rho_{t}])-D(\Lambda_{t,t+\Delta t}[\rho_{t}]||\Lambda_{t,t+\Delta t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}[\rho_{t}]])\Bigr)\geq 0,

where we used
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{t}\ln\mathcal{E}_{t}[\rho_{t}]]=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}\ln\rho_{t}^{\rm cl}]
and obtained the second line,
D(\rho||\sigma):=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho(\ln\rho-\ln\sigma)]
is the quantum relative entropy, and the last inequality follows from the monotonicity of the relative entropy
[
67
]
.

Analysis of q rec 2 q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2} -distribution for B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} and B → K ∗ ​ M X B\to K^{*}M_{X} decays in a scalar-mediator dark-matter scenario
Alexander Berezhnoy a , Wolfgang Lucha b and Dmitri Melikhov a,c,d

Abstract
We demonstrate that the scalar-mediator dark-matter scenario is consistent with the experimental data on the decay B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} and provides a good description of the shape of the observed excess. Within this scenario, the interaction with dark-matter particles leads to approximately the same excess in Γ ⁡ ( B → K ∗ ​ M X ) \Gamma(B\to K^{*}M_{X}) and Γ ⁡ ( B → K ​ M X ) \Gamma(B\to KM_{X}) compared to the Standard Model; also the differential distributions of the excess events are similar in shape in the variable q rec 2 q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2} measured by experiment.

II
B\to(K,K^{*})\bar{\chi}\chi
decays via a scalar mediator
\phi

Following
[
30
]
, we focus on a rather simple representative of the really vast class of DM models. To this end, we consider a rather popular model involving an interaction of DM fermions
\chi
with the top quark
t
by exchange of a scalar-mediator field
\phi
, governed by the interaction Lagrangian
[
31
,
32
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L_{\rm int}}=-\frac{ym_{t}}{v}\phi\,\bar{t}t-\kappa\phi\bar{\chi}\chi.

(2)

The emerging effective Lagrangian encoding the flavor-changing neutral-current (FCNC) vertex
b\to s\phi
then reads

[
31
,
32
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{b\rightarrow s\phi}=g_{b\to s\phi}\,\phi\,\bar{s}_{L}b_{R}+{\rm h.c.},\qquad g_{b\to s\phi}=\frac{ym_{b}}{v}\frac{3\sqrt{2}G_{F}m_{t}^{2}V^{*}_{ts}V_{tb}}{16\pi^{2}}.

(3)

This Lagrangian enters in the amplitudes controlling the
\phi
-mediated decay
B\to(K,K^{*})\bar{\chi}\chi
:

\displaystyle A(B(p)\to K^{(*)}(p-q)\bar{\chi}(k)\chi(q-k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\langle K^{(*)}(p-q)\bar{\chi}(k)\chi(q-k)|\mathcal{L}_{b\to s\phi}|B(p)\rangle

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\chi}\chi|\bar{\chi}\chi|0\rangle\kappa\frac{1}{M_{\phi}^{2}-q^{2}-iM_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi}(q^{2})}g_{b\to s\phi}\langle K^{(*)}(p-q)|\bar{s}_{L}b_{R}|B(p)\rangle,

with the abbreviation

\langle\bar{\chi}\chi|\bar{\chi}\chi|0\rangle\equiv\langle\bar{\chi}(k)\chi(q-k)|\bar{\chi}(0)\chi(0)|0\rangle.

(5)

For the present analysis, we take the liberty to assume that
M_{\phi}>2m_{\chi}
and furthermore that the mediator
\phi
decays predominantly into the
\bar{\chi}\chi
pair. As the consequence, we obtain a
q^{2}
-dependent width of the mediator
\phi
which is calculated from the imaginary part of the fermion loop diagram with scalar vertices in the form (cf.
[
33
,
34
]
)

\Gamma_{\phi}(q^{2})=\left(\frac{q^{2}-4m_{\chi}^{2}}{M_{\phi}^{2}-4m_{\chi}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{M_{\phi}}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}\;\Theta(q^{2}-4m_{\chi}^{2})\;\Gamma_{\phi}^{0},\qquad\Gamma_{\phi}^{0}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{8\pi}M_{\phi}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{M_{\phi}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

(6)

Here,
q
is the momentum of the outgoing
\chi\bar{\chi}
pair of unobserved DM particles;
M_{X}^{2}\equiv q^{2}
is the missing mass squared.

Equation (
4
) provides a simplified parametrization of the full propagator of the scalar particle, taking into account the resummation of the
\bar{\chi}\chi
loops and neglecting the real parts of the loop diagrams.

Using the QCD equations of motion, the required amplitudes can be straightforwardly calculated, yielding

\displaystyle\langle K|\bar{s}_{L}b_{R}|B\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle K|\bar{s}(1-\gamma_{5})b|B\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle K|\bar{s}b|B\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{M_{B}^{2}-M_{K}^{2}}{m_{b}-m_{s}}f_{0}^{B\to K}(q^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle K^{*}|\bar{s}_{L}b_{R}|B\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle K^{*}|\bar{s}(1-\gamma_{5})b|B\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle K^{*}|\bar{s}\gamma_{5}b|B\rangle=-i(\epsilon q)\frac{M_{K^{*}}}{m_{b}+m_{s}}A_{0}^{B\to K^{*}}(q^{2}),

(7)

with well-known dimensionless form factors
f_{0}
and
A_{0}
parametrizing the amplitudes
\langle K|\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}b|B\rangle
and
\langle K^{*}|\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b|B\rangle

[
35
]
.

Using these amplitudes and the recursive formula for the phase space

\displaystyle d\Phi_{3}(M_{B}^{2},p_{K},p_{\chi},p_{\bar{\chi}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(M_{B}^{2},p_{K},p_{\chi}+p_{\bar{\chi}})\frac{dq^{2}}{2\pi}d\Phi_{2}(q^{2},p_{\chi},p_{\bar{\chi}})=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})}{8\pi M_{B}^{2}}\frac{dq^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{q^{2}}},

(8)

where
\lambda
is defined by
\lambda(a,b,c)\equiv(a-b-c)^{2}-4bc
, we can factorize the process into the decay of the
B
meson to the
K
meson and a virtual
\phi^{*}
, followed by the decay of
\phi^{*}
to a
\chi\bar{\chi}
pair. The
d\Gamma(B\to(K,K^{*})\bar{\chi}\chi)/dq^{2}
distributions can then be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma}{dq^{2}}(B\to K\bar{\chi}\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\chi\,{\rm polar}}|A|^{2}}{2M_{B}}\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})}{8\pi M_{B}^{2}}\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{q^{2}}}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})}{16\pi M_{B}^{3}}|\langle K|\bar{s}_{L}b_{R}|B\rangle|^{2}\frac{g_{b\to s\phi}^{2}\,\kappa^{2}}{(M_{\phi}^{2}-q^{2})^{2}+M_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma_{\phi}^{2}(q^{2})}\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{q^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\chi\,{\rm polar}}|\langle\bar{\chi}(k)\chi(q-k)|\bar{\chi}(0)\chi(0)|0\rangle|^{2}.

The expression for
B\to K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi
is obtained by the obvious replacements
K\to K^{*}
and
M_{K}\to M_{K^{*}}
in Eq. (
9

Taking into account that

\sum_{\chi\,{\rm polar}}|\langle\bar{\chi}(k)\chi(q-k)|\bar{\chi}(0)\chi(0)|0\rangle|^{2}=q^{2}-4m_{\chi}^{2}

(10)

and

\sum_{K^{*}\,{\rm polar}}|\langle K^{*}|\bar{s}_{L}b_{R}|B\rangle|^{2}=\frac{\lambda(M_{B}^{2},M_{K^{*}}^{2},q^{2})}{4(m_{b}+m_{s})^{2}}|A_{0}^{B\to K^{*}}(q^{2})|^{2},

(11)

we obtain the following expressions for
d\Gamma(B\to K\bar{\chi}\chi)/dq^{2}
and
d\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi)/dq^{2}
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma}{dq^{2}}(B\to K\bar{\chi}\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})}{16\pi M_{B}^{3}}\frac{(M_{B}^{2}-M_{K}^{2})^{2}|f_{0}^{B\to K}(q^{2})|^{2}}{4(m_{b}-m_{s})^{2}}\frac{g_{b\to s\phi}^{2}\,\kappa^{2}}{(M_{\phi}^{2}-q^{2})^{2}+M_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma_{\phi}^{2}(q^{2})}\frac{q^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)^{3/2},

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma}{dq^{2}}(B\to K^{*}\bar{\chi}\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{3/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K^{*}}^{2},q^{2})}{16\pi M_{B}^{3}}\frac{|A_{0}^{B\to K^{*}}(q^{2})|^{2}}{4(m_{b}+m_{s})^{2}}\frac{g_{b\to s\phi}^{2}\,\kappa^{2}}{(M_{\phi}^{2}-q^{2})^{2}+M_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma_{\phi}^{2}(q^{2})}\frac{q^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)^{3/2}.

(12)

A powerful probe of the DM scenario considered is provided by the ratio
[
30
]

\displaystyle R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma(B\to K^{*}\chi\bar{\chi})/dq^{2}}{d\Gamma(B\to K\chi\bar{\chi})/dq^{2}}=\frac{\lambda^{3/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K^{*}}^{2},q^{2})}{\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})}\frac{|A_{0}^{B\to K^{*}}(q^{2})|^{2}}{|f_{0}^{B\to K}(q^{2})|^{2}}\frac{(m_{b}-m_{s})^{2}}{(M_{B}^{2}-M_{K}^{2})^{2}(m_{b}+m_{s})^{2}}.

(13)

Clearly, the dependence on the specific parameters of the DM model cancels out in this ratio. We shall see that this ratio remains largely unaffected by the averaging procedure adopted in the experiment.

(14)

\displaystyle A_{0}(q^{2})

III
Kinematics

The Belle II experiment, as described in
[
1
]
, analyzes partially reconstructed events to increase the statistical sample. For such events, the direction of the
B
meson cannot be determined. Therefore, instead of
q^{2}=(p_{B}-p_{K})^{2}
the variable
q_{\rm rec}^{2}
is used:

q^{2}_{\rm rec}=E_{B}^{2}+M_{K}^{2}-2E_{B}E_{K},

(16)

where
E_{B}
and
E_{K}
are the energies of the
B
and
K
mesons in the center-of-mass frame of the
B\bar{B}
-meson pair produced in
\Upsilon(4S)
decays.

The variable
q^{2}_{\rm rec}
can be expressed through the three-momenta
\mathbf{p}_{B}
and
\mathbf{p}_{K}
of the
B
and
K
mesons, and then through the velocity
\mathrm{v}
of the
B
meson in the
B\bar{B}
center-of-mass frame:

q^{2}_{\rm rec}=E_{B}^{2}+M_{K}^{2}-2E_{B}E_{K}=q^{2}+(E_{B}^{2}-M_{B}^{2})-2(\mathbf{p}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{B})=q^{2}+\frac{M_{B}^{2}\mathrm{v}^{2}}{1-\mathrm{v}^{2}}-2p_{K}^{z}\frac{M_{B}\mathrm{v}}{\sqrt{1-\mathrm{v}^{2}}},

(17)

where
\mathbf{p}_{K}^{z}
is the projection of
\mathbf{p}_{K}
onto the direction of the
B
-meson motion.

It is convenient to express
\mathbf{p}_{K}^{z}
as follows:

\mathbf{p}_{K}^{z}=\frac{\cos\Theta\;\mathbf{p}_{K}^{0}-\mathrm{v}\;E_{K}^{0}}{\sqrt{1-\mathrm{v}^{2}}}.

(18)

Here,
\Theta
is the angle between the direction of the
K
-meson motion in the
B
-meson rest frame and the direction of the
B
-meson motion in the
B\bar{B}
center-of-mass frame, while
\mathbf{p}_{K}^{0}
and
E_{K}^{0}
are, respectively, the projection of the
K
-meson three-momentum onto the
B
-meson direction, and the
K
-meson energy in the
B
-meson rest frame:

\mathbf{p}_{K}^{0}=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})}{2M_{B}},\qquad E_{K}^{0}=\frac{M_{B}^{2}+M_{K}^{2}-q^{2}}{2M_{B}}.

(19)

By combining Eqs. (
17
), (
18
) and (
19
),
q_{\rm rec}^{2}
becomes, in terms of
q^{2}
,
\Theta
and
v
,

q_{\rm rec}^{2}=\frac{q^{2}-M_{K}^{2}\mathrm{v}^{2}-\cos\Theta\;\mathrm{v}\;\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})}{1-\mathrm{v}^{2}}.

(20)

This equation has an exact solution for
q^{2}
:

q^{2}=\frac{1}{1-\cos^{2}\Theta\;\mathrm{v}^{2}}\Bigg\{(1-\mathrm{v}^{2})q_{\rm rec}^{2}+(1-\cos^{2}\Theta)\mathrm{v}^{2}M_{K}^{2}-\cos^{2}\Theta\;\mathrm{v}^{2}M_{B}^{2}+\\
+\cos\Theta\,\mathrm{v}\,\Big[\lambda\Big(M_{B}^{2},(1-\mathrm{v}^{2})M_{K}^{2},(1-\mathrm{v}^{2})q_{\rm rec}^{2}\Big)-4(1-\cos^{2}\Theta)\mathrm{v}^{2}M_{B}^{2}M_{K}^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\Bigg\}.

(21)

Neglecting terms proportional to
\mathrm{v}^{2}
, we obtain

q^{2}\approx q_{\rm rec}^{2}+\cos\Theta\,\mathrm{v}\,\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q_{\rm rec}^{2}).

(22)

Further, upon neglecting
M_{K}
we arrive at an even simpler relation between
q^{2}
and
q_{\rm rec}^{2}
, which matches the previous expression within a few percent:

q^{2}\approx q_{\rm rec}^{2}+(M_{B}^{2}-q_{\rm rec}^{2})\mathrm{v}\cos\Theta.

(23)

Since the
B
meson decays uniformly over
\Theta
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma=\iint\frac{d^{2}\Gamma(q^{2},\cos\Theta)}{dq^{2}d\cos\Theta}dq^{2}d\cos\Theta=\frac{1}{2}\iint\frac{d\Gamma(q^{2}(q_{\rm rec}^{2},\cos\Theta))}{dq^{2}}\frac{dq^{2}(q_{\rm rec}^{2},\cos\Theta)}{dq_{\rm rec}^{2}}dq_{\rm rec}^{2}d\cos\Theta.

(24)

Thus,

\frac{d\Gamma}{dq_{\rm rec}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\Gamma(q^{2}(q_{\rm rec}^{2},\cos\Theta))}{dq^{2}}\frac{dq^{2}(q_{\rm rec}^{2},\cos\Theta)}{dq_{\rm rec}^{2}}d\cos\Theta.

(25)

Therefore, any decay characteristic
{\cal A}(q^{2}_{\rm rec})
at a given
q^{2}_{\rm rec}
is the average of
{\cal A}(q^{2})
over the range

\displaystyle q^{2}\in[q^{2}_{\rm rec}-\mathrm{v}\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2}_{\rm rec});q^{2}+\mathrm{v}\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2}_{\rm rec})].

(26)

In particular, the averaged differential decay rate has the form

\frac{\overline{d\Gamma(q_{\rm rec}^{2})}}{dq_{\rm rec}^{2}}\approx\Bigg\langle\frac{d\Gamma(q^{2})}{dq^{2}}\Bigg\rangle_{q^{2}_{\rm rec}\pm\mathrm{v}\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2}_{\rm rec})}.

(27)

In order to give an idea of the size of the averaging interval, we note that
\mathrm{v}\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},0\mbox{ GeV}^{2})\approx 1.7
GeV
2
and
\mathrm{v}\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},10\mbox{ GeV}^{2})\approx 1
GeV
2
.

It is instructive to consider the real
\phi
-mediator with a negligible decay width, in which case one finds

\frac{d\Gamma}{dq^{2}}(B\to K^{(*)}\phi)=\Gamma(B\to K^{(*)}\phi)\cdot\delta(q^{2}-M_{\phi}^{2}).

(28)

So, in this case, the distributions over
q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}
become rectangular functions with a width of
2\mathrm{v}\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K^{(*)}}^{2},q^{2}_{\rm rec})
:

\frac{d\Gamma}{dq_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}}(B\to K^{(*)}\phi)=\Gamma(B\to K^{(*)}\phi)\;\frac{\Theta(v\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K^{(*)}}^{2},q^{2}_{\rm rec})-|q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}-M_{\phi}^{2}|)}{2\mathrm{v}\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K^{(*)}}^{2},q^{2}_{\rm rec})}.

(29)

IV
Data fitting

The Belle II experiment does not apply efficiency corrections to its data
[
1
]
. Consequently, in order to fit the data, we adopt the
q^{2}
-dependent efficiency
\varepsilon(q^{2})
estimated in
[
24
]
(and provided by Fig. 2 of
[
24
]
),

\frac{\overline{d\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}(q_{\rm rec}^{2})}}{dq_{\rm rec}^{2}}=\Bigg\langle\varepsilon(q^{2})\,\frac{d\Gamma(q^{2})}{dq^{2}}\Bigg\rangle_{q^{2}_{\rm rec}\pm\lambda^{1/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2}_{\rm rec})}.

(30)

To determine the optimal fit parameters, we minimize the
\chi^{2}
value, defined as

\chi^{2}=\sum_{i}\frac{(n^{i}_{\mathrm{exp}}-n^{i}_{\mathrm{theor}})^{2}}{(\Delta^{i}_{\rm exp})^{2}}+\frac{(N_{\mathrm{exp}}-N_{\mathrm{theor}})^{2}}{\Delta_{\mathrm{exp}}^{2}},

(31)

where the sum runs over the experimental bins,
n^{i}_{\mathrm{exp}}
and
n^{i}_{\mathrm{theor}}
are the measured and the predicted numbers of excess events over the SM expectation in the
i
-th bin, and
\Delta^{i}_{\rm exp}
is the experimental error in the
i
-th bin;
N_{\rm exp}
and
N_{\rm theor}
are the full numbers of excess events,

N_{\rm exp}=\sum_{i}n^{i}_{\mathrm{exp}}
and
N_{\rm theor}=\sum_{i}n^{i}_{\mathrm{theor}}
,
and
\Delta_{\rm exp}^{2}=\sum_{i}(\Delta^{i}_{\rm exp})^{2}.

The DM parameters to be determined by our analysis are thus
M_{\phi}
,
\Gamma_{\phi}^{0}
and
m_{\chi}
, which determine the shape of the
q^{2}
-distribution, and the product of the couplings
g_{b\to s\phi}\kappa
which determines the total yield of
the (invisible)
\bar{\chi}\chi
pairs.

Figure 3 shows our prediction for the differential distributions of the excess events in
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays. Assuming the same detection efficiency for
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays, the ratio
\overline{R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})
of excess events in the experimentally measured differential distributions in
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
decays,

\displaystyle\overline{R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})=\frac{\overline{d\Gamma^{\rm eff}}(B\to K^{*}\chi\bar{\chi})/dq_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}}{\overline{d\Gamma^{\rm eff}}(B\to K\chi\bar{\chi})/dq_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}},

(35)

is practically independent of the model parameters, such as
M_{\phi}
,
m_{\chi}
, and
\Gamma^{0}_{\phi}
, since the dependence on these parameters approximately cancels out in the ratio. [Recall that the dependence on these parameters cancels exactly in the theoretical ratio
R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}
of Eq. (
13

Consequently, we have an approximate relation between the measured
\overline{R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})
and
the theoretical

\displaystyle\overline{R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})\simeq R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}).

(36)

27
). Notice, however, that if the reconstruction
efficiencies for the decays
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
differ significantly, then (
36
) must be adjusted accordingly.

q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}
distribution reaches its maximum at
q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}\approx 5\mbox{ GeV}^{2}
, where
R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})\approx 1
. This observation implies that the number of excess events in the decays
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
— assuming the same detection efficiency for both processes — should be approximately equal in magnitude and the corresponding differential distributions in
q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}
should have similar shapes. This behavior is illustrated in Fig.
3
, where predictions for the expected excess events for the
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
decay are represented by dashed curves.

VI
Conclusions

In the current study, our aim was limited to the analysis of the impact of the enhancement in the
B\to KM_{X}
decay, observed by Belle II
[
1
]
, on some DM scenario involving a scalar mediator. For this model, the following conclusions, based solely on Belle II data, hold:

•

The model discussed is consistent with the experimental data and provides a good description of the shape
of the excess observed. One can clearly identify the set of the “best” values of the DM parameters

\displaystyle M_{\phi}=2.4\pm 0.4\penalty\ {\rm GeV},\quad\Gamma^{0}_{\phi}=2.9^{+1.1}_{-0.9}\penalty\ {\rm GeV},\quad m_{\chi}=0.42^{+0.2}_{-0.4}\penalty\ {\rm GeV}.

•

Both the shape and the normalization of the ratio of the excess events which may be measured experimentally,

\overline{R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})
, may be well approximated by the theoretical ratio (
13
) evaluated
at the same
q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}
,
R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})
:

\displaystyle\overline{R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})\simeq R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}).

Consequently, the shape of
\overline{R^{(\phi)}_{K^{*}/K}}(q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2})
is largely independent of the DM model parameters — such as
M_{\phi}
,
\Gamma_{\phi}
and
m_{\chi}
— and provides a clear signature of the DM scenario based on the scalar-mediator field.

•

Within the framework of the model considered, the yields of the excess events over the SM background for the decays
B\to K^{*}M_{X}
and
B\to KM_{X}
should be approximately equal in magnitude and exhibit a qualitatively similar shape in the
q_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}
distribution, assuming the same detection efficiency for both reactions.

These constraints on DM particles may be combined with constraints coming from other phenomena (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
) but such kind of analysis is beyond the scope of our interest in this analysis.

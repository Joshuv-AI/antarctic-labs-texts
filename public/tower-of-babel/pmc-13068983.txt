Intraluminal Hair Identified During Laparoscopic Appendectomy for Perforated Appendicitis in a Child
Authors: Urías Hernandez Lopez, Pastor Thomas Olivares, Jessica Olivera Herrera, Angelica Leal Caicedo, María Duran Pombo, Luisa Morelos Meza
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntraluminal foreign bodies, including hair, have been described in association with appendicitis, sometimes in complicated forms with perforation. It has been suggested that they may contribute to the inflammatory process through luminal obstruction and subsequent progression of inflammation. We present the case of a pediatric patient with intraluminal hair identified in the setting of perforated appendicitis.

Clinical Question

Can a foreign body such as hair cause perforated appendicitis?

Case Presentation

Multiple foreign bodies have been described in association with appendicitis, including seeds, bone fragments, toothpicks, dental material, metallic objects, and hair [1]. We present the case of a 3‐year‐old boy with a diagnosis of appendicitis in whom intraluminal hair was identified during laparoscopic appendectomy (Video 1). Some case reports suggest that intraluminal hair may be associated with a higher frequency of complicated appendicitis possibly through luminal obstruction and subsequent inflammatory progression [2]. However, the available evidence is limited, and additional case reports and larger studies are needed.

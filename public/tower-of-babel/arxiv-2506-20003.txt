In this manuscript, we consider simple graphs: no parallel arcs or edges are permitted, and neither can there be a parallel arc and edge.

Let
G=(V;E\cup A)
be a mixed graph where
V(G)
denotes the set of vertices,
E(G)
the set of (undirected) edges, and
A(G)
the set of arcs. To distinguish clearly between these types, we represent an edge between vertices
u
and
v
as
uv
, and a directed arc from
u
to
v
as
(u,v)
. Two vertices
u
and
v

are said to be edge-adjacent (respectively, arc-adjacent) if there exists an edge (respectively, an arc) between them. We represent walks, paths, and cycles as sequences of the form
(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
, where each consecutive pair
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
is either edge- or arc-adjacent. The
girth
of the graph is defined as the length of its shortest cycle. Given a subset
X\subseteq V
, the graph
G[X]
is obtained by removing all vertices in
V\setminus X

along with any edges or arcs incident to them.

A mixed graph is called

if each vertex has exactly
z

z
outgoing arcs, and
r

g
(i.e., the length of its shortest cycle is

[z,r;g]
-mixed graph
of girth
g
.
A
[z,r;g]
-mixed cage
is a
[z,r;g]
-mixed graph that has the smallest possible order, where the

refers to the number of vertices in the graph. The minimum order of such graphs is denoted by

The concept of mixed cages was first introduced by Araujo-Pardo, Hernández-Cruz, and Montellano-Ballesteros
[
5
]
. In their work, they constructed specific examples of mixed cages for particular parameter values and derived several lower bounds. They established a lower bound on the order of
[1,r;g]
-mixed cages, which is now known as the AHM bound. This bound is derived from a version of Moore’s lower bound (see e.g.
[
13
]
).

The authors in
[
5
]
also showed that the AHM bound can be achieved when
r=2
, regardless of the value of
g
. Additionally, Exoo
[
12
]
demonstrated that this bound is attainable for

and
g=5,6
.

Araujo-Pardo, De la Cruz, and González-Moreno
[
4
]
showed that the order of a
[z,r;g]
-mixed cages is monotone with respect to
g
when

. Furthermore, it was established that
[z,r;g]
-mixed cages are 2-connected, and, for arbitrary values of
z
, the corresponding mixed graphs are strongly connected. In the specific case for
g=5
the study provided additional constructions alongside improved lower bounds. These results are obtained through the use of incidence geometry, with a particular focus on the elliptic semiplane of type
C
–a structure also referred to in the geometric literature as the biaffine plane of type 1 (see, e.g.,
[
14
,
2
,
4
]
for the former terminology and
[
18
,
6
,
8
]
for the latter).

Exoo
[
12
]
concentrated on
z\in\{1,2\}
and showcased a range of mixed cages along with upper bounds for various values of
r\leq 5
and
g\leq 8
, employing computer searches.

Jajcayová and Jajcay
[
17
]
developed
[z,r;g]
-mixed graphs by substituting some edges of certain regular undirected graphs with arcs.

Araujo-Pardo and Mendoza-Cadena
[

]
constructed a family of mixed graphs of girth6 based on the incidence graph of an elliptic semiplane of type
C
(also known as a biaffine plane of type 1). This semiplane arises by deleting a

from a projective plane–that is, a point
p
and an incident line
l
, along with all lines through
p
and all points on
l
.
In a subsequent work
[

, they constructed a family of mixed graphs of girth 5 using the incidence graph of an elliptic semiplane of type

(also known as a biaffine plane of type 2). In this case, the construction involves deleting an

from a projective plane–again, a point
p
and a line

and

In this manuscript, we present a new construction of mixed graphs with a girth of 6, utilizing the incidence graphs of elliptic semiplanes of type L. Our approach is similar to that in
[
6
]
. As in our previous work, we employ a projective plane over a finite field. However, in this case, we utilize the incidence graph of an elliptic semiplane of type

For the Galois Field

\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}

Let
q

G_{q}

\mathbb{Z}_{q}

G_{q}

We will introduce a value

2.1

Consider

q
is a power of
2

.

In the following, all the computations are made modulo

q-1

\mathbb{F}^{*}=\{\xi^{0},\xi^{1},\xi^{2},\dots,\xi^{q-2}\}
.

For a fixed

, we add circulants to the sets
\mathcal{P}_{y}\cup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{y}
and to
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\cup\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\infty}

Let

\frac{p+1}{2}
is an integer number as

is an odd number.
For a jump of length

\big((x,y),({(x\xi^{i-1})^{\prime}},y^{\prime})\big)

\big(L_{x},L_{x\xi^{i-1}}^{\prime}\big)
and
\big(L_{x}^{\prime},L_{x\xi^{i}}\big)

Similarly for lines and the infinity points. For fixed

and for jump

we add arcs
\big([m,b],[(m/\xi^{i})^{\prime},b^{\prime}]\big)
,
\big([m^{\prime},b^{\prime}],[m/\xi^{i-1},b]\big)

\big(P_{m},P^{\prime}_{m/\xi^{i}}\big)

Lemma 2
.

The circulants of the graph
H_{q,p}
has directed girth 6 when
q
is even.

Proof.

Consider the circulant of part
\mathcal{P}_{y}\cup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{y}
for a fixed
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We know there exists a directed cycle of length
6
, e.g. for
x\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
, we obtain the following directed cycle by giving 4 jumps of length
p
, or equivalently for
i=\frac{p+1}{2}
, and then 2 jumps of length
i=1
:
\left((x,y),({x\xi^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}^{\prime},y^{\prime}),(x\xi^{p},y),({x\xi^{\frac{3p-1}{2}}}^{\prime},y^{\prime}),(x\xi^{2p},y),({x\xi^{2p}}^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right)
which is well defined as there is an arc from
({x\xi^{2p}}^{\prime},y^{\prime})
to
(x,y)=(x\xi^{2p+1},y)
–recall that
2(q-1)=4p+2
.

We claim that there is no smaller directed cycle than this type. Let
K
be a directed cycle of smallest length.
First, note that
\mathcal{P}_{y}\cup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{y}
is bipartite (in the directed subgraph), and thus the length of
K
cannot be 5 or 3. Clearly
|K|=2
is not possible by construction.
We show that
|K|\neq 4
.
Suppose we start at position
(x,y)
. For these computations, recall that the circulant is of the form
\overset{\rightarrow}{C}_{q-1}(1,3,5,\ldots,p)
, meaning we only allow jumps of odd lengths. Since
p
represents the length of the longest jump, we can see that by making four jumps of length
p
, we obtain the equation
4p=2(q-1)-2<2(q-1)
. This indicates that we are two positions away from the origin
(x,y)
, even after making four jumps of the maximum length. Consequently, this establishes that
|K|=6
based on the cycle shown above.

Similarly for the other parts.
∎

Lemma 3
.

The graph
H_{q,p}
has girth 6 when
q
is even and
p
is odd.

Proof.

The graph has undirected girth equals to 6 by construction (two copies of graph
G_{q}
), and directed girth equals 6 by Lemma
2
.
We verify that any mixed cycle has a length at least 6.

Let
K
be the smallest mixed cycle of the graph.
Based on the partition and the matching among parts discussed earlier, it is clear that no mixed cycles of lengths 2 and 3 exist. Also,
|K|\neq 5
because the graph is bipartite with parts
\bigcup_{b}\{\mathcal{L}_{b}\}\bigcup_{y}\{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{y}\}\bigcup\{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\}\bigcup\{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\infty}\}
and
\bigcup_{b}\{\mathcal{L^{\prime}}_{b}\}\bigcup_{y}\{\mathcal{P}_{y}\}\bigcup\{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\infty}\}\bigcup\{\mathcal{P}_{\infty}\}
.
We show that
|K|\neq 4
. As we only have arcs between lines (points) that share the first same coordinate (one in vertex is in
G_{q}
and the other in
G^{\prime}
), in order to have a mixed cycle of length 4, we need two edges: one in
B_{q}
and one in
B^{\prime}_{q}
. Thus, we require two arcs, one leaving
B_{q}
(and entering
B^{\prime}_{q}
) and one entering it (and leaving
B^{\prime}_{q}
).
Consider the point
(x,y)
which is edge-adjacent to line
[m,b]
. This line
[m,b]
is arc-adjacent to a line in
G^{\prime}_{q}
of the form
[(m/\xi^{j})^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
for some
j\in\{1,2,\dots,\frac{p-1}{2}\}
. The line
[({m/\xi^{j}})^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
is edge-adjacent to a point
(x^{\prime},({m/\xi^{j}}x+b)^{\prime})
. Even if
y=({m/\xi^{j}}x+b)^{\prime}
, there is no arc from
(x^{\prime},({m\xi^{j}}x+b)^{\prime})
to
(x,y)
by construction. We conclude that there is no cycle of length 4. This concludes the proof.
∎

Construction when

Consider
q

be such that

If

Otherwise, when

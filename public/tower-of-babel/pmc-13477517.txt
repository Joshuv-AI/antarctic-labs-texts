The impact of gestational diabetes mellitus on mid-pregnancy and infant cardiac morphology and function: A prospective cohort study
Authors: Chenyan Guo, Jie Zhou, Bai Jin, Liqun Feng, Zhiping Ge, Yetao Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the effects of gestational diabetes mellitus on cardiac morphology and function in fetuses during mid-pregnancy and in infants during the postnatal period.MethodsThis prospective cohort study included 64 pregnant women diagnosed with gestational diabetes mellitus allocated to the experimental group and 119 healthy pregnant women allocated to the control group. Fetal echocardiography was performed between 24 and 28 weeks of gestation, and the infants underwent echocardiography at approximately 42 days after birth. Maternal baseline characteristics, mid-pregnancy fetal echocardiographic data, and infant cardiac functional assessments were recorded and analyzed.ResultsGestational age at the time of examination did not differ significantly between the control and gestational diabetes mellitus groups (p > 0.05). Compared with controls, in fetuses of mothers with gestational diabetes mellitus, the thickness of the left ventricular wall and interventricular septum was increased, whereas the right ventricular ejection fraction was significantly reduced (p < 0.05). In the gestational diabetes mellitus group, the fetal transvalvular early diastolic velocity to myocardial early diastolic velocity ratio of the tricuspid valve increased (as measured using echocardiography), whereas the tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion was lower than that in the control group (p < 0.05). Compared with the control group, during infancy, the gestational diabetes mellitus group had a higher left ventricular transvalvular early diastolic velocity to myocardial early diastolic velocity ratio and lower tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (p < 0.05). Subgroup analysis of the gestational diabetes mellitus cohort showed no significant differences in fetal and neonatal echocardiographic parameters between the diet-controlled and insulin-treated subgroups.ConclusionsGestational diabetes mellitus is associated with alterations in fetal cardiac structure and function as well as persistent cardiac changes in infancy compared with the control group.

Introduction

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is widely recognized as an increasingly prevalent metabolic disorder during pregnancy, with estimates suggesting that it affects between 12% and 18% of pregnancies globally. 1 As lifestyle and dietary habits change, the incidence of GDM continues to rise, posing significant challenges for healthcare systems worldwide. Diet and physical activity are key components of GDM management. 2 GDM is characterized by glucose intolerance with onset or first recognition during pregnancy. If not adequately managed, it can lead to adverse maternal and fetal complications.3–5

The effects of GDM extend beyond the immediate peripartum period, posing a risk for long-term health issues for both mother and offspring. Maternal health concerns include an elevated risk of developing type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and cardiovascular disease later in life.6,7 For the fetus, exposure to maternal hyperglycemia can result in abnormal development, particularly in terms of cardiac health. Several studies have established that maternal diabetes can induce hemodynamic and metabolic changes in the fetal environment.8–10 The pathophysiological mechanisms through which GDM affects fetal heart development are multifaceted. Maternal hyperglycemia leads to increased glucose availability to the fetus, which may induce hyperinsulinemia as the fetal pancreas responds to the elevated glucose levels. 11 This hyperinsulinemic state can exert teratogenic effects on the developing fetal heart, resulting in changes in cardiac morphology, such as increased left ventricular wall thickness, increased interventricular septum dimensions, and abnormal cardiac output parameters.12,13 In addition to structural changes in the heart, prenatal exposure to GDM has been implicated in functional cardiovascular alterations.14,15 Echocardiographic evaluation of parameters such as ejection fraction, myocardial performance index, and diastolic function have demonstrated that children born to mothers with GDM are more susceptible to cardiac dysfunction during infancy.16,17

The primary aim of this study was to systematically investigate the effects of GDM on fetal cardiac morphology during mid-pregnancy and assess cardiac function in infants postnatally.

Materials and methods

Study population

This prospective observational study was conducted between September 2023 and December 2024 at the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. Pregnant women at 24–28 weeks of gestation and their newborns (approximately 42 days after birth) were recruited as study participants; 64 women diagnosed with GDM were allocated to the experimental group and 119 healthy pregnant women to the control group. We recorded maternal baseline characteristics, including age, body mass index (BMI), and obstetric history. All participants provided written informed consents before undergoing the ultrasound examination. This study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (2023-SR-359).

The study comprised pregnant women with GDM according to Chinese guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of hyperglycemia in pregnancy who were diagnosed via a single 75 g glucose tolerance test. 18 The thresholds for glucose levels in fasting state and at 1 and 2 h after glucose consumption were 5.1, 10.0, and 8.5 mmol/L, respectively. Pregnant women with normal results on the glucose tolerance test were included in the control group.

Exclusion criteria for the study included pregnancies complicated by pre-eclampsia, intrauterine growth restriction, fetal congenital deformities, twin pregnancies, oligo/hydramnios, known genetic and chromosomal disorders, abnormal fetal heart rhythms (either tachycardia or bradycardia), and a failed oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) at the time of enrollment. Fetal echocardiography

Each study participant underwent fetal echocardiographic examinations at 24–28 weeks of gestation. All images were acquired and stored using the Voluson E10 ultrasound machine with a probe frequency of 2–5 MHz (GE Medical Systems, Zipf; Austria). Seventeen fetal echocardiography parameters were measured in this study.

The thickness of the left ventricular wall, right ventricular wall, and interventricular septum were measured on images from an apical 4-chamber view at end-diastole. Left and right myocardial performance indices were obtained using pulsed wave Doppler. Systolic functional assessment included left ventricular ejection fraction, right ventricular ejection fraction (RVEF), mitral annular plane systolic excursion (MAPSE), and tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) using M-mode. Diastolic function was assessed using Doppler blood flow waveforms and tissue Doppler imaging. Measurements included mitral valve early diastolic Doppler velocity (E) and peak mitral annular velocities in early diastole (e’). The ratios of transvalvular early-to-late diastolic velocity (E/A), transvalvular early diastolic velocity to myocardial early diastolic velocity (E/e’), and early diastolic myocardial velocity to late atrial contraction myocardial velocity (e’/a’) were calculated (Figure 2).

Fetal echocardiography measurement in four-chamber views. (a) Right ventricular Tei index (RV TEI = (a-b)/b, where (a-b) is the isovolumetric contraction time plus isovolumetric relaxation time, and b is the ejection time); (b) longitudinal four-chamber view; (c) measurement of fetal mitral inflow peak wave velocities (peak E and peak A) and E/A wave peak velocities; (d) measurement of fetal mitral annular peak wave velocities (e’).

The average of each diameter across three consecutive cardiac cycles was recorded for analysis. The measurements were performed by a single experienced physician, and all images were reviewed by a second physician. All procedures were performed in accordance with the International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology (ISUOG) guideline. 19

Infant cardiovascular assessment

All infants underwent echocardiography at approximately 42 days after birth for evaluation of cardiac morphology. Echocardiography was performed using a PHILIPS Cx50 machine according to the guidelines of the American Society of Echocardiography. 20 The measured parameters included left ventricular end-diastolic diameter, left ventricular end-systolic diameter, right ventricular end-diastolic diameter, and interventricular septum thickness. Left ventricular systolic function was assessed using left ventricular ejection fraction. Right ventricular systolic function was assessed using TAPSE. Additionally, indices of systolic and diastolic left ventricular function, such as E and e’ (a marker for diastolic relaxation) were measured, and ratios of E/A and E/e’ were calculated (Figure 3).

Neonatal echocardiography. (a) Parasternal long-axis view of the left ventricle; (b) measurement of TAPSE in the four-chamber view.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0). Data were assessed for normality. Continuous data were expressed as mean ± SD, whereas categorical data were presented as counts and percentages. For comparison of categorical demographic data and ultrasound parameters, the chi-square test or chi-square test with Yates’ continuity correction was used when the expected frequency in at least one cell was <5. Continuous variables were compared using Student's t test. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

The present findings provide robust evidence regarding the adverse effects of GDM on fetal cardiac morphology and function and demonstrate the persistence of these cardiac alterations into infancy. The prospective cohort design of this study facilitated a rigorous comparison of echocardiographic parameters between fetuses exposed to GDM and those of healthy controls, corroborating the hypothesis that maternal hyperglycemia significantly influences fetal heart development.

A key finding of this study is the increased thickness of the left ventricular wall and interventricular septum in fetuses exposed to maternal GDM. These morphological changes are consistent with previous literature, indicating that hyperglycemia leads to fetal cardiac hypertrophy, likely due to the hyperinsulinemic state driven by excessive maternal glucose levels.12,21–24 The resulting insulin surge acts as a growth factor that promotes cellular hypertrophy and increased myocardial mass, thereby altering the structural integrity of the fetal heart. 25

Additionally, the study demonstrated a significant decrease in RVEF in the GDM group compared with that in the controls, suggesting impaired right ventricular function. This finding raises important concerns as right ventricular function has been linked to overall cardiac efficiency and may precede future cardiovascular complications. 26 The altered hemodynamics may stem from both structural adaptations and the hormonal milieu associated with GDM, underscoring the importance of comprehensive cardiovascular assessments in this high-risk population.

Moreover, echocardiographic assessment demonstrated higher E/e’ ratios and lower TAPSE values in fetuses and infants exposed to GDM, indicating impaired diastolic function and global systolic function, respectively. Elevated E/e’ ratios reflect increased filling pressures and suggest diastolic dysfunction, which may impact long-term cardiac health.16,27 Lower TAPSE values indicate reduced right ventricular systolic function, emphasizing the potential impact of GDM on multidimensional cardiac performance.28,29

The persistence of cardiac alterations from fetal life into infancy raises additional considerations regarding the long-term cardiovascular risks associated with GDM. Infants born to mothers with GDM constitute a high-risk population for developing cardiovascular disorders in childhood and adulthood, highlighting the urgency for early identification and intervention strategies.30,31 Tailored monitoring protocols, including regular echocardiographic evaluations and surveillance of additional Doppler indices (e.g. umbilical cord coiling index and umbilical artery indices), may facilitate the early detection of cardiac dysfunction in these infants, potentially enabling timely therapeutic interventions. 32

It is also crucial to recognize the multifactorial nature of the cardiovascular effects observed in children born to mothers with diabetes. Although maternal GDM plays a significant role, other factors such as maternal obesity, nutritional intake, genetic predispositions, socioeconomic factors, and preterm birth may concurrently affect fetal cardiac development and function.33–37 Future longitudinal studies exploring these interactions are necessary to clarify GDM-specific impacts from other confounding factors in fetal and postnatal cardiovascular health.

Strengths and limitations

Our findings highlight the need for increased clinical awareness regarding routine echocardiographic evaluations in fetuses and infants exposed to GDM. Early identification of potential cardiac dysfunction can enable healthcare providers to implement interventions that can help mitigate the risk of future cardiovascular issues. Such interventions may include lifestyle modifications, dietary counseling, and maternal blood glucose monitoring during pregnancy. Although this study provides compelling insights, certain study limitations warrant consideration. Due to the single-center study design and relatively small sample size of 183, the generalizability of these findings may be limited. Future research should involve larger, multicenter studies to validate these findings.

Conclusions

GDM induces profound, multifactorial alterations in fetal cardiac morphology and function, which persist into infancy. Our study highlights the critical need for managing GDM within the context of holistic maternal and child healthcare. Early identification and interventions for GDM are crucial to mitigate potential long-term health risks.

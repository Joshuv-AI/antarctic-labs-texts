Camrelizumab plus rivoceranib compared sorafenib as first-line therapeutic options for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma in China: a cost-effectiveness analysis
Authors: Hongfu Cai, Jingwen Lin, Huide Zhu, Zhiwei Zheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimThe objective of this research is to assess the cost-effectiveness of combining camrelizumab with rivoceranib in comparison to sorafenib as first-line therapeutic options for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma from the Chinese medical system perspective.MethodsA partitioned survival model was employed to perform a comprehensive cost-effectiveness analysis. This analysis incorporated multiple factors, such as treatment effectiveness, adverse events and costs, all of which were derived from data obtained from the CARES-310 trial. Furthermore, sensitivity analyses were conducted to evaluate the robustness and reliability of the model.ResultsThe comparison between the two groups demonstrated that the cohort receiving camrelizumab combined with rivoceranib exhibited a significant increase of 0.803 quality-adjusted life year (QALY), alongside an additional expenditure of US$7345.051. This computation resulted in an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of US$9147.012 per QALY, which was lower than the willingness-to-pay threshold of US$39 855.785 per QALY in China. Sensitivity analyses conducted in this study further demonstrated the robustness of the results across various assumptions.ConclusionThe adoption of camrelizumab plus rivoceranib as a treatment option is not only associated with improved health outcomes but also represents a cost-effective choice in China.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) represents a prevalent and highly aggressive form of malignancy. This particular type of cancer showcases an exceedingly malignant phenotype, characterised by its rapid progression and substantial mortality rate.1 HCC represents a significant and pressing public health issue in China, taking on the fourth highest incidence rate and the second highest fatality rate among all types of cancer in the country.2 The average overall 5 year survival rate in HCC patients is reported to be 19.6%. However, this figure can significantly plummet to as low as 2.5% for patients with advanced stages of the disease.3 The treatment for HCC is determined by the stage and severity of the disease. Curative treatment options for localised tumours predominantly involve surgical resection, liver transplantation and local ablation therapies.4 Systemic therapeutic modalities, encompassing targeted therapies and immunotherapies, represent essential approaches for patients presenting with advanced HCC.5 6 Despite the notable progress in therapeutic interventions, HCC still demonstrates an unfavourable prognosis, characterised by a substantial recurrence rate coupled with a relatively low overall survival (OS) rate. Therefore, the development of effective therapeutic strategies is of utmost importance in the management of HCC.7

In recent years, there has been a notable advancement in the field of cancer therapy, specifically with the introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) as a revolutionary treatment option. This breakthrough therapy has shown promising results, especially in the case of patients with advanced HCC.8 9 ICIs represent a category of therapeutic agents meticulously tailored to selectively target particular immune checkpoints, with particular emphasis on programmed death 1 and its ligand programmed death ligand 1. These pivotal immune checkpoints effectively govern the immune response by impeding overstimulation and upholding immune homeostasis. Numerous ICIs have demonstrated promising outcomes in clinical trials focusing on the treatment of advanced HCC. These trials have provided compelling evidence of the efficacy and safety of ICIs as a therapeutic approach. The utilisation of ICIs, either as standalone therapy or in conjunction with other treatment modalities, has demonstrated promising outcomes in relation to tumour response rates, overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). These findings have raised optimism regarding the potential for ICIs to become an integral part of the treatment regimen for advanced HCC.10Nivolumab has demonstrated efficacy as a potential alternative for patients suffering from advanced HCC who have priory undergone sorafenib treatment.11 Another one pembrolizumab has shown great potential as a therapeutic option for advanced HCC. This ICI has exhibited long-lasting antitumour effects in patients who had previously experienced disease progression or intolerance to sorafenib, a commonly used therapy for advanced HCC. This development resulted in the expeditious approval of pembrolizumab by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for therapeutic intervention in advanced HCC.12

The recent completion of the CARES-310 phase 3 trial has provided compelling evidence of the benefits of combining camrelizumab and rivoceranib in the treatment of advanced HCC. As a result, this novel combination therapy presents a promising first-line treatment alternative for advanced HCC.13 However, the increasing costs of ICIs and the rapidly escalating healthcare expenses have become significant concerns within the field of medicine, particularly with the rising number of drugs utilised in combination therapy programmes. In response to the dramatic rise in cancer treatment costs, the concept of value-based oncology has gained considerable attention. The pursuit of effective and cost-efficient first-line treatment options has become vital in contemporary healthcare practices. Therefore, the objective of this study was to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of the combination therapy of camrelizumab and rivoceranib when compared with sorafenib as a first-line treatment option for advanced HCC in China.

Methods

Study population and treatment

The CARES-310 clinical trial was undertaken with the primary goal of evaluating the efficacy and safety of the combined administration of camrelizumab and rivoceranib in contrast to sorafenib as the initial therapeutic approach for advanced HCC. The patient sample used in our model was consistent of the population evaluated in the clinical trial. Eligible participants for this study are individuals who are at least 18 years old and have a confirmed diagnosis of HCC through histopathological or cytological examination. These individuals should have clinical stage B or C HCC that either cannot be treated with surgery or localised methods or has shown progression after such treatment. Moreover, participants should not have received any previous systemic therapy.13

During the period from 28 June 2019 to 24 March 2021, a comprehensive evaluation of 842 individuals was conducted to determine their eligibility. Among the participants, a total of 541 individuals were recruited and subsequently assigned at random into two treatment groups: the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib group (n=272) and the sorafenib group (n=269). Patients received camrelizumab intravenously every 2 weeks at a dosage of 200 mg, in conjunction with either rivoceranib administered orally one time per day at a dosage of 250 mg or sorafenib administered orally two times per day at a dosage of 400 mg. The regimen will continue throughout the cycle every 28 days unless terminated due to intolerable side effects, serious adverse reactions or disease progression.

In the phase 3 CARES-310 trial, the findings indicate that the median duration of treatment for camrelizumab was 6.9 months (with IQR: 3.6–13.4), rivoceranib was 6.5 months (IQR: 3.4–11.9) and sorafenib was 3.8 months (IQR: 1.9–7.4). Notably, in the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib group, a significant proportion of patients (88%) experienced grade 3 or higher adverse events (AEs), while a similar occurrence was observed in the sorafenib group, affecting 182 patients (68%). Importantly, our survival model exclusively considered grade 3 or 4 AEs that manifested at a frequency exceeding 10% in any of the treatment cohorts.

In accordance with the progression of the disease, a subsequent systemic therapy was administered to a total of 90 patients (33%) in the group treated with camrelizumab plus rivoceranib, while 130 patients (48%) in the sorafenib group received a similar intervention. In accordance with established protocols and findings from the phase 3 CARES-310 trial, lenvatinib or sintilimab plus bevacizumab was hypothesised as a potential subsequent anticancer therapy for both treatment groups, with the purpose of conducting a cost analysis. However, the optimal selection for the third-line therapy remains unclear. Consequently, our study hypothesised that the administration of the best supportive treatment would be the most efficacious course of action in the event of disease progression.

Model structure

We have developed a partitioned survival model (PSM) in order to simulate the survival regression associated with the disease and to estimate the costs and benefits involved. Our model incorporates diverse direct healthcare expenses into its analysis, such as drug costs, costs of managing AEs, subsequent therapy costs and costs of best supportive treatment. To streamline the modelling procedure, our survival model exclusively emphasises grade 3 or 4 AEs that exhibit a prevalence exceeding 10% in any given treatment cohort.

The PSM consists of three distinct and non-overlapping states: progression-free disease, progressive disease and death (figure 1). The PSM is a statistical technique utilised for estimating survival rates and proportions of individuals at a specific time. Its application involves the use of OS and PFS curves. PSM not only allows for understanding the survival outcomes but also enables extrapolation of survival data beyond the originally studied period. Each state within the PSM framework is associated with a distinct survival curve, representing the time taken for an individual to transition from the initial state to subsequent states. Specifically, PFS curves provide insights into the survival data for non-progressing states, whereas OS curves encompass the OS data. As a result, the survival data for progressing states can be derived by subtracting the survival data obtained from both the OS and PFS curves.

Partition survival model structure.

The simulation period for the study was set at 28 days, which is consistent with the cycle time in the CARES-310 clinical trial. In addition, considering the low 5 year survival rate of advanced liver cancer, the simulation time for our model run was set at 10 years. Our simulation results revealed that 89.90% of the population in the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib group group and 96.60% of the population in the sorafenib group had succumbed to the disease within this 10 year time-frame. Furthermore, our analysis has incorporated a WTP threshold of US$39 855.785 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY), which corresponds to three times the gross domestic product (GDP) of the nation in 2023.14 The software TreeAge Pro 2011was was utilised to conduct the PSM analysis.

Model transition probabilities

The survival data for two groups were meticulously obtained by using the GetData Graph Digitizer software to extract digitised survival curves from the CARES-310 clinical trial. Subsequently, the extracted curves were accurately reconstructed using the R software (V.4.0.5), which facilitated the identification of the suitable statistical distribution for modelling the survival curves.15 Among the array of potential distributions, including the Weibull, log-logistic, log-normal, gamma, Gompertz and exponential distributions, a thorough analysis encompassing visual inspection and the application of the principle of minimum Akaike information criterion and Bayesian information criterion values was conducted.16 Ultimately, the log-logistic distribution was conclusively found to provide the optimal fit for the purpose of simulating survival curves. Detailed results of this discerning evaluation can be found in online supplemental table 1, while online supplemental figure 1 graphically presents the reconstructed curves for each treatment group.

We conducted both internal and external validation to assess the reliability and accuracy of the model in simulating survival curves. Internally, we utilised data from the CARES-310 clinical trial to validate the simulated PFS and OS curves generated by our models. We found that these curves closely resembled the actual survival outcomes observed in the clinical trial. For external validation, we compared our survival curves with those obtained from the RATIONALE-301 trial.17 This comparison aimed to assess the consistency of our results with those obtained from a different study. We specifically focused on the PFS and OS curves and found that the 12 month OS rates were similar between our study and the RATIONALE-301 trial.

In order to enhance the predictive capabilities of our model, we sought to extend its efficacy beyond the confines of the original follow-up period. We conducted a simulation study on survival times by utilising the log-logistic distribution. This approach enabled us to computationally estimate the survival function, denoted as S(t), which can be expressed mathematically as S(t) = 1/(1+λtγ).18 The input parameters λ and γ value data are provided in online supplemental table 2.

Input parameters of cost and utility

This study primarily focused on the assessment of direct healthcare expenditures. These costs encompass expenses related to medication, the management of severe AEs, follow-up care, subsequent therapy expenditures and costs associated with best supportive care. The calculations for drug costs were obtained from the China Data Platform (https://data.yaozh.com/), while other cost data were collected from relevant literature sources. Sensitivity analysis was applied to the variables to evaluate their impact on the study outcomes. All expenses were converted to US dollars using the 2023 average annual exchange rate of US$1 to ¥7.05.19 In this research study, we conducted an assessment of the quality of life associated with each health condition. The quality of life was measured on a scale ranging from 0 to 1, with 0 representing the worst health state and 1 representing the best health state. Due to the unavailability of explicit utility value data from the CARES-310 clinical trial, we derived the utility values utilised in our model from previously published literature. Furthermore, our model takes into account the negative impact of adverse drug events on utility. A comprehensive presentation of the input parameters value data is provided in table 1.

Sensitivity analysis

In conducting the one-way sensitivity analysis, the input parameters were systematically adjusted by ±25% to assess the influence of parameter changes on the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). Furthermore, the discount rate was varied within the range of 0%–8%. The findings derived from these analyses were effectively presented through the use of tornado diagrams. In addition, a comprehensive assessment of uncertainties concerning the ICER was conducted through a probabilistic sensitivity analysis. This investigation involved the execution of 1000 Monte Carlo simulations, enabling the inclusion of a broader array of potential probabilistic scenarios. To accurately represent the cost parameters, a gamma distribution was employed, while a beta distribution was utilised to model the utility value factors. The results of this analysis were visually depicted using scatter plots.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Result

Overview of the base-case results

The total cost incurred by the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib group was determined to be US$16 205.799. The sorafenib group had a lower total cost of $8860.748.

Furthermore, it was observed that the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib demonstrated a substantial gain of 1.715 QALY. In contrast, the sorafenib group achieved a lower gain of 0.912 QALYs. This signifies that the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib treatment option resulted in a significant incremental gain of 0.803 QALY compared with sorafenib. This means that patients receiving the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib treatment experienced a significant improvement in their overall quality of life compared with those undergoing chemotherapy.

However, with this increase in effectiveness comes an incremental cost of US$7345.051 for the camrelizumab plus rivoceranib group when compared with the sorafenib. As a consequence, the ICER was calculated to be US$9147.012 per QALY gained.

Importantly, the ICER value obtained in this analysis is found to be below the WTP threshold of US$39 855.785 per QALY in China. This implies that the use of camrelizumab in combination with rivoceranib is a viable and cost-effective treatment option for patients with advanced HCC. Sensitivity analysis results

The depicted tornado plot in figure 2 portrays the outcomes of a one-way sensitivity analysis. Notably, changes in the utility value for PFS had the largest influence on ICER. Additionally, factors such as the cost of subsequent, the cost of sorafenib, the cost of camrelizumab, the utility value of PFS and the cost of best supportive care also had some impact on ICER. It is important to highlight that altering these parameters within a range of ±25% did not significantly affect the overall findings of the analysis. Furthermore, the ICER values remained consistently below 1 time of GDP, indicating that they were well below the predetermined thresholds of WTP. This observation demonstrates the robustness of the model and affirms that the results are not reversed even with fluctuations in these parameters.

Discussion

HCC ranks among the foremost causes of cancer associated mortality worldwide, while patients with advanced disease face a dearth of therapeutic alternatives at their disposal.20 Recent advancements in immunotherapy have shown promising results in various cancers, including advanced HCC.21 22 Camrelizumabhas demonstrated efficacy in HCC treatment by unleashing antitumour immune responses.23 24 Camrelizumab has recently received approval from the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) in China as a second-line therapeutic option for patients with advanced HCC.25 In addition to these specific immunotherapeutic approaches, ongoing research focuses on combining immunotherapy with other treatment modalities to enhance therapeutic outcomes. Notably, combination regimens that incorporate ICIs with targeted therapies like tyrosine kinase inhibitors or antiangiogenic agents have exhibited encouraging efficacy in clinical trials for advanced HCC.26Although this combination therapy has demonstrated impressive effectiveness, the potential cost burden associated with its implementation remains uncertain. It is essential to determine if the added expenses outweigh the benefits.

The combination therapy of camrelizumab along with rivoceranib in the management of advanced HCC has exhibited a favourable ICER of US$ 9147.012 per QALY gained, in comparison to the conventional treatment alternative of sorafenib. This ICER value is significantly lower than the widely accepted WTP threshold of US$39 855.785 per QALY. Consequently, it suggests that the utilisation of camrelizumab plus rivoceranib as a first-line treatment alternative for advanced HCC in China holds the potential to be considered a cost-effective approach.

Our sensitivity analysis outcomes underline the significant impact of some factors on the ICER. These factors encompass the utility of PFS, subsequent costs, the cost of sorafenib and the cost of camrelizumab. It is important to note that even when these parameters were adjusted by a range of ±25%, the resulting ICER consistently remained below the WTP threshold. Consequently, it becomes evident that the current pricing strategy for the combination therapy of camrelizumab plus rivoceranib presents itself as a cost-effective treatment option for patients afflicted with advanced HCC in China.

The cost-effectiveness analysis provides valuable insights into the value that this combination therapy brings to patients, healthcare providers and the overall healthcare system in terms of improved health outcomes for each dollar spent. The favourable ICER suggests that the use of camrelizumab plus rivoceranib can potentially offer substantial health gains at a reasonable cost. This supports its inclusion in the treatment guidelines and healthcare reimbursement policies in China for advanced HCC.27 By considering the cost-effectiveness of treatment options, policymakers and healthcare professionals can make informed decisions about resource allocation, ensuring that limited healthcare resources are utilised optimally to maximise health benefits for patients. Furthermore, the cost-effectiveness of camrelizumab plus rivoceranib may also have important economic implications. In addition to the potential health benefits, the cost-effectiveness of this therapy can help control healthcare expenditure on HCC, a disease with significant economic burden. By investing in cost-effective therapies, healthcare systems can strive to achieve better patient outcomes while ensuring the sustainability and efficiency of the healthcare system as a whole.28

There have been some studies on the cost-effectiveness economics of other ICIs agents in HCC. For example, Wang et al conducted a research study employing a three-state Markov model to compare the cost-effectiveness of oral multikinase inhibitors and ICIs for advanced HCC. The study’s findings revealed that, in comparison to the standard sorafenib treatment, the sunitinib treatment strategy emerged as a particularly promising option due to its highly favourable cost-effectiveness.29 Gong et al conducted a comprehensive cost-effectiveness analysis to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of various regimens, including sorafenib, lenvatinib, atezolizumab combined with bevacizumab and sintilimab combined with bevacizumab in the Chinese population. Their study aimed to determine the QALY generated by these treatments and assess their cost-effectiveness. The researchers found that lenvatinib, sintilimab plus bevacizumab and atezolizumab plus bevacizumab therapies were associated with significant incremental QALY gains when compared with sorafenib. However, despite their clinical benefits, these treatment regimens were deemed not cost-effective according to a WTP threshold of US$36 600 per QALY.30 Liu et al conducted a comprehensive assessment to determine the cost-effectiveness of immunotherapy or targeted therapy as the initial approach for unresectable HCC. Their study revealed that the combination of sintilimab with a bevacizumab biosimilar and camrelizumab with rivoceranib significantly prolonged OS and PFS, respectively. Furthermore, considering the pharmacoeconomic aspects, the authors identified tislelizumab as the most cost-effective first-line treatment strategy for unresectable HCC in China.31 Although our study also conducted a cost-effectiveness analysis of camrelizumab for advanced HCC, it differs from the study conducted by Liu et al. Unlike Liu et al, who analysed the SHR-1210-III-310 trial, our analysis exclusively relies on data derived from the CARES-310 trial. This disparity in trial selection underscores the dissimilarity between our respective studies. We acknowledge the valuable findings reported by Liu et al but believe that our study, conducted with a different trial data set, offers unique insights and complements the existing evidence.

The demand for effective and cost-effective first-line therapeutic options has become increasingly prominent in the field of the treament of HCC. With escalating healthcare costs and the need to optimise patient outcomes, it has become imperative to identify and utilise treatments that not only demonstrate high efficacy but also offer affordability.32 The combination of camrelizumab and rivoceranib offers a dual-targeted approach, inhibiting both immune evasion and angiogenesis.The synergy between the immune-modulating effects of camrelizumab and the targeted inhibition of angiogenic pathways by rivoceranib may enhance treatment efficacy. Notably, the combination therapy has demonstrated a manageable safety profile, limiting treatment-associated toxicities.33 Our study demonstrates the considerable cost-effectiveness of the combination therapy of camrelizumab and rivoceranib as a viable initial treatment option for advanced HCC in China. These findings provide valuable guidance to policymakers, healthcare providers and other stakeholders involved in the management of advanced HCC, facilitating informed decision-making processes and optimising the allocation of healthcare resources.

Given the limited resources available, healthcare providers must allocate their budgets intelligently, prioritising interventions that provide the most value for money. By opting for cost-effective first-line therapies, healthcare systems can mitigate financial strains, uphold equitable healthcare access, and optimise resource allocation.34

Promoting the availability and affordability of effective first-line therapeutic options can also help address healthcare disparities. In many regions, access to innovative treatments or specialised care is limited, particularly among vulnerable populations. By prioritising cost-effective first-line therapies, healthcare providers can ensure that essential treatments are accessible to a larger population, regardless of socio-economic status or geographical location.35

The study has the following limitations. First, our data come from clinical trials, although we utilised rigorous research methods and comprehensive data sources to perform the cost-effectiveness analysis, it is essential to continuously monitor and update these findings as new evidence emerges and the cost and efficacy landscape evolves. Second, we did not consider any transarterial embolization (TAE)/drug-eluting microsphere chemoembolization (TACE)/ radiofrequency ablation (RFA) in subsequent treatment in this study based on the results of the CARES-310 clinical trial. Third, it is important to acknowledge that this study makes an assumption regarding the cost of second-line treatments after disease progression. However, it is important to consider that in reality, the choice of subsequent treatment options would vary based on the individual circumstances of each patient. Fortunately, the results of the one-way sensitivity analyses provide reassurance as they consistently demonstrate that even when the estimated ranges for subsequent treatments are altered, the ICER values remained below the threshold of WTP. Finally, the limitation of our study is the exclusion of grade 1 or 2 AEs in our analysis, as we assumed that they had minimal impact on clinical outcomes. While this approach allowed for concise modelling, it may not capture the comprehensive impact of treatment-related toxicity on patient prognosis. However, it is reassuring to note that the results of our sensitivity analyses indicate that even a 25% variation in grade 3 or higher AEs would not change our conclusions.

Conclusions

Camrelizumab plus rivoceranib appears to be a potential cost-effectiveness as a preferred initial treatment approach for advanced HCC compared with sorafenib.

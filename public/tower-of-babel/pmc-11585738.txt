Introduction

Osteosarcoma is a bone malignancy with an extreme invasive nature and is also the most common primary bone cancer [1]. Osteosarcoma occurs more frequently in males than in females. It can occur in any age group, including the elderly, but it mainly occurs in adolescents between the ages of 10 and 14, accounting for 2 % of all childhood tumors [2]. Symptoms of osteosarcoma usually begin with mild local bone pain, redness, and warmth at the tumor site. The patient will experience increasing pain, which often affects the patient's movement and joint function. Prompt diagnosis and treatment of early osteosarcoma are very necessary, otherwise, it may lead to metastasis to other parts of the body, such as the lungs, other bones, and soft tissues [3]. Unfortunately, despite the low OS rate, overall mortality rates have been reported to be as high as nearly 70 % in metastatic cases of the disease [4], [5].

Biopsy technology is considered one of the most effective means of detecting whether a certain part of the body is affected by most malignant tumors, including osteosarcoma [6]. A biopsy is the process of removing tissue from a diseased area of a patient's body and examining it under a microscope to examine its cellular characteristics [7]. Although biopsy using a microscope can successfully identify malignant tumors, biopsy still has some limitations in detecting malignant tumors. For example, the increase in the number of cases has made it difficult for pathologists to quickly diagnose cancer by eye [8]. In addition, tissue sections contain a large number of cell types, and pathologists must spend a lot of time manually finding the most severely affected areas and examining cell morphology [9]. Under heavy work pressure, the accuracy of diagnosis is reduced to a certain extent. In addition, it is reported that bone cancer pathology examination is more complicated than breast cancer and prostate cancer [10]. In terms of automated detection, tissue feature detection of osteosarcoma is more challenging. In response to these challenges, there is a high demand for computer-aided diagnosis in the field of medical imaging. The application of artificial intelligence technology has improved the speed of image interpretation and diagnosis, and improved the efficiency and accuracy of examinations. Studies have shown that based on the convolutional neural network (CNN) as a representative, CNN has achieved great success in medical image analysis and can apply the image itself to the automatic learning process without the need for complex feature extraction in advance [11].

Due to the necessity of early diagnosis through medical microscopy imaging and significant breakthroughs in the field of artificial intelligence, this study combines CNN and machine learning (ML) algorithms to classify osteosarcoma cells. That is, tumor (VT and NC) or non-tumor (NT). To optimize the analysis and retrieval process under cell microscopy to ensure efficiency and accuracy, we utilized a publicly available dataset of osteosarcoma pathological images for data augmentation processing. The dataset was divided into training, validation, and testing sets in an 8:1:1 ratio. In addition, To improve the model's ability to extract key features from image information, we also introduced a coordinated attention mechanism (CA) to optimize the original SE attention mechanism of the lightweight model MobileNet V3, namely the CA-MobileNet V3 model. The objectives of this study are: (1) to demonstrate that the CA-MobileNet V3 model based on AI image recognition technology can detect osteosarcoma malignancies with high accuracy; (2) The improved method can improve the recognition accuracy of different types of cells. We trained several state-of-the-art networks (Mobilenet V3, TL-MobileNet V3, ShuffleNet V2, EfficientNet V2) using the same dataset and compared the results to demonstrate the performance and superiority of our improved model. (3) We embedded the trained CA-MobileNet V3 model into a microscope, demonstrating that the improved model and microscope device have strong application prospects.

Related work

In the wave of rapid advancement of digital technology, artificial intelligence (AI) methods have been applied to many research fields such as energy [12], machinery [13], product processing [14] and production [15]. In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) methods have also achieved outstanding results in medical image processing. Machine learning (ML) is a branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the problem of learning from data samples to general reasoning concepts [16]. The core pursuit of machine learning (ML) technology is to build a model capable of various tasks such as classification and prediction. In recent years, the application of computer-assisted technology in pathological biopsy has gradually become feasible, mainly due to the emergence of large-scale data sets and Deep Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) in the field of computer science, especially deep learning (DL) has shown expert-level accuracy in medical image classification. The application scope of DL has extended from chest CT and gastric endoscopy images to cancer cell diagnosis [17], [18], [19]. In digital pathology, the latest DL has shown good results in quantifying and classifying pathological tissue samples [20].

Recent research based on deep learning has been recognized as a major advancement in the field of histological image detection. For example, a study conducted by Erkan on breast cancer histopathology showed excellent performance using AlexNet and VGG16 models, with an average accuracy of over 90 % [21]. Recently, similar techniques have been applied to various image classification scenarios [22], [23], [24]. These advanced deep learning architectures not only cover the prediction of lung cancer pathological invasiveness, but also extend to the classification of histopathological images of cancers such as liver cancer. In particular, in the application of artificial intelligence in osteosarcoma detection, the research results of Arunachalam [25] marked an important milestone. This study first proposed a fully automatic evaluation tool that uses histological images and deep learning technology to distinguish between live and necrotic tumors in osteosarcoma. Arunachalam used machine learning and deep learning models, and the overall accuracy of the integrated learning model was 93.3 %.

Vandana et al. [26] proposed an innovative enhanced framework based on image segmentation technology, the purpose of which is to carry out a quantitative evaluation of the degree of malignancy in the H&E stained tissue image. Accurate extraction of key features.Their method combined mathematical morphological analysis, color clustering, and active contour techniques to extract key image features. These features were then input into a multi-class random forest (RF) classifier to achieve accurate classification of malignant tumors. Nabid et al. [27] developed a classification method based on a sequential region convolutional neural network (R-CNN) specifically for identifying osteosarcoma in histopathological images. The performance of their model was compared with several other models on the same dataset, including AlexNet, support vector machine (SVM) model, ResNet50, LeNet, and VGG16. He et al. [28] used the DenseNet architecture to improve the feature extraction capability and focused on its effectiveness in the diagnosis of spinal osteosarcoma. They conducted an in-depth discussion and analysis on the practical application of gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) technology in improving the accuracy of medical image classification. In the aforementioned studies, most models rely on large-scale datasets. In theory, using larger image patches can provide more accurate classification results because they can capture richer image context and more pixel information [29]. However, due to the current limitations of memory and computing power, processing large-size images faces some challenges [30]. In order to overcome the high complexity, large number of parameters and high requirements for the deployment environment of traditional models, lightweight models came into being. Lightweight models can still maintain efficient performance under limited resources. The MobileNet model is a lightweight network model launched by Google in 2017. As a typical representative of lightweight models, this series of models has relatively few parameters, which means that it can occupy less memory when deploying the model, so it is particularly suitable for running on devices with limited resources. In the same way, MobileNet can reduce the amount of model calculations and improve the reasoning speed without sacrificing performance. In addition, compared with previous networks, the MobileNet series of models have stronger feature extraction capabilities and better performance. In 2023, Vezakis et al. [31] used the MobileNet V2 model to classify osteosarcoma cross-section images using 5-fold cross-validation, achieving an overall accuracy of 91 %.

These findings point to an important trend: by adopting a smaller and more efficient network architecture, the processing results of the osteosarcoma dataset can be optimized without increasing the complexity of the model. This trend encourages researchers to explore simplified models in order to reduce the consumption of computing resources and the difficulty of model deployment while maintaining or improving performance. Therefore, this study uses a lightweight model that introduces a coordinated attention mechanism and improves it, namely CA-MobileNet V3, to identify microscopic images of osteosarcoma cells.

Methodology

Model selection

This study introduces an intelligent recognition model for microscopic images based on improved MobileNet V3, which provides an innovative solution strategy for microscopic data retrieval and classification of osteosarcoma cells. MobileNet V3 is optimized based on the previous generation MobileNet V2. By integrating the Squeeze-and-Excitation mechanism into the Bottleneck Residual Block of MobileNet V2, not only enhances the feature extraction capabilities, but also significantly improves the model's computing speed. This improvement makes the model more efficient in processing osteosarcoma microscopic images while maintaining high classification accuracy. In the same way, the MobileNet V3 model realizes intelligent learning of the relationship between channel features by integrating the SE (Squeeze-and-Excitation) attention module, thereby enhancing the learning ability of the model. In the same way, the model introduces the innovative h-swish(x) activation function, which shows significant advantages in the convolutional layer and fully connected layer of deep neural networks due to its excellent nonlinear mapping capabilities and progressive saturation characteristics., promotes smoother gradient flow inside the model, optimizes the training process, and ultimately achieves a dual improvement in model accuracy and training efficiency. Transfer learning

Considering the diversity and number of osteosarcoma cells, this study selected the MobileNet V3-Small version to adapt to the characteristics of the data set. When the amount of data is sufficient, the deep learning model can deeply explore the multi-level features in the image to identify the subtle differences between different objects. To address this challenge, this study adopted a transfer learning method. Transfer learning is an effective strategy that can help solve the problem of insufficient data. By leveraging the knowledge learned by pre-trained models on large-scale data sets, transfer learning can significantly reduce training costs while improving the model's generalization ability in medical image intelligent recognition tasks. This method not only greatly improves the accuracy of the model, but also improves its ability to adapt and generalize to new data sets, enabling it to achieve good recognition results even when data is scarce.

Coordinate attention mechanism (CA)

In contrast, the CA mechanism embeds position information into channel attention, which not only avoids introducing too much computation but also enables the model to obtain richer information [32]. The implementation of the CA mechanism covers two core links: first, the embedding process of coordination information, and second, the generation stage of coordination information. In the process of coordinating information embedding, the use of the global pooling method causes the global spatial information to be compressed into the channel information, and the position information is difficult to preserve. To enable the attention module to capture feature data with precise position information, the CA mechanism decomposes the global pooling and converts it into a one-to-one-dimensional feature encoding operation.

Given an input X , pooling kernels of size (h,1) and (1,w) is applied to encode each channel in the horizontal and vertical directions, respectively, as shown in equation (1) and equation (2). In this conversion process, feature data is integrated along both horizontal and vertical dimensions, aiming to capture interdependencies and location cues across longer distances. This process enables the attention module to more acutely identify and correlate the intrinsic connections between different targets in the spatial dimension. In the same way, position information is retained in the other direction, thereby improving the model's ability to accurately locate the target and enhancing the feature extraction effect. In the coordination information generation stage, a new intermediate feature is generated by fusing the precisely encoded feature layer with the original feature map, which combines the horizontal and vertical position information. This fusion not only enriches the expression of the feature, but also provides a more comprehensive spatial context for the model. Subsequently, this intermediate feature is sent to the final collaborative attention layer for processing, thereby enhancing the model's ability to accurately capture key information of the image. The entire process effectively improves the model's perception ability and ability to handle complex tasks by carefully processing spatial position information, making the model more accurate and efficient in performing tasks.

The specific implementation process of the CA mechanism in this research model is shown in Fig. 2. The steps are: (1) Input a cell image feature map of size C × H × W (i.e., the number of channels is C, and the height and width are H × W); (2) Pooling is performed along the two directions of the input feature map using different pooling kernels (H, 1) and (1, W), and the two embedded feature maps are concatenated along the spatial dimension; (3) After 1 × 1 convolution (Conv2d) transformation, the activation function is applied to activate it; (4) Split operation is performed along the spatial dimension to divide the feature map into two independent parts; (5) Transform and Sigmoid operations are performed on the separated feature map, and the obtained feature map is multiplied element-by-element with the input feature map through the broadcast mechanism (Re-weight), thereby obtaining the cell image feature weight.

Coordinate Attention block.

The input feature map is finely processed by using methods such as pooling, convolution, activation function, segmentation, and element-by-element multiplication. Such processing enables the model to accurately capture the correlation between image features and assign appropriate weights to different parts, thereby improving the model's ability to accurately describe and understand cell image characteristics [33].

CA-MobileNet V3 osteosarcoma cell intelligent recognition model

To solve the problem of large computational load and low accuracy of the current cell image classification model, this study built a bone cell classification model based on the MobileNet V3 model, adopted a transfer learning strategy, and embedded the CA module, named the CA-MobileNet V3 model. The overall structure of the intelligent recognition model is shown in Fig. 3. The specific process is as follows: (1) The MobileNet V3 model that has been pre-trained on Image Net is used, and after fine-tuning, it is loaded into the bone cell image classification model framework through transfer learning. The general features obtained by large-scale data training on Image Net are used to improve the model's learning ability for bone cell images. (2) The SE module in the MobileNet V3 model is replaced with the CA module, and the CA module is used to capture the correlation between image features and realize the effective fusion of multi-feature information, thereby improving the model's ability to express medical images. (3) The recognition and classification of images are realized through operations such as global average pooling. This structural design aims to optimize the model to more efficiently adapt to the classification task of osteosarcoma medical images, while maximizing the excellent performance of the MobileNet V3 model as a lightweight architecture. In the same way, the CA module is introduced to improve the ability to capture and express image features. Through a series of experimental verifications on osteosarcoma cell image data, the Coordinate Attention Mechanism-MobileNet V3 model constructed in this study has been significantly improved in terms of osteosarcoma cell classification accuracy and model performance and has better performance than the existing mainstream models.

Artificial intelligence microscope (AIM) product design

Diagnosing osteosarcoma from various medical images is a difficult task and a major challenge for medical professionals. Moreover, the limitations of traditional optical microscopes, such as short depth of field, weak stereoscopic perception, and low efficiency, are likely to limit pathologists' observation of cell surface morphology. Therefore, maintaining a consistent level of accuracy and efficiency can be a difficult task [34]. Artificial intelligence methods have gradually been applied to medical images and have made significant progress. Regarding the comfort or usability of the microscope device: Current clinical microscopes are designed for complete practicality and do not take into account the comfort or usability of pathologists when using microscopes. As a result, more than 90 % of senior pathologists suffer from some kind of chronic pain due to frequent use of traditional microscopes. In addition, pathology microscopes have maintained a similar design for decades. However, the development of digitalization in pathology has affected the applicability and efficiency of traditional microscopes.

Based on the above challenges, we have carried out an innovative work: improving the design of microscope products to adapt to the development of digitalization and reduce harm to users. We installed the CA-MobileNet V3 artificial intelligence model into the electron microscope device and carried out an innovative integrated design. We developed an artificial intelligence microscope (AIM) that helps to automatically classify and identify osteosarcoma cells. AIM integrates the computer and CA-MobileNet V3 model and comes with two monitors. As shown in Fig. 4, when the slide is prepared and fixed under the microscope, the pathologist can observe the eye-catching visual marks on the diseased cells using the dual monitors of the AI ​​microscope device, and the system also improves the efficiency of diagnosis. AIM also allows one or more pathologists to jointly perform collaborative diagnosis, which improves the accuracy of osteosarcoma diagnosis. The improved microscope device is easier to operate than traditional microscopes. AIM is also more ergonomic in shape and use, thus reducing the workload of pathologists.

Experiment

Data collection and pre-processing

In this study, our dataset mainly comes from Ma'anshan People's Hospital of Anhui Province and 30 patients from February 2002 to December 2023. In addition, we also included some public datasets from Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) [10]. Since we realize that the sample size may not be sufficient to prevent the risk of overfitting of the model, we implemented a variety of data augmentation strategies on the original dataset. These strategies include rotating the images and transforming them in the vertical direction to increase the diversity of the dataset. Through these methods, we successfully expanded the number of images of each type to about 1,800 [35]. That is not only improves the richness of the dataset, but also provides a more solid foundation for model training, thereby enhancing the model's wide applicability and generalization ability in actual scenarios.

We took a series of measures to ensure that the developed model can learn effectively during the training process and can generalize to a variety of data scenarios. To this end, we carefully divided the image dataset and allocated it to the training set, validation set, and test set in a ratio of 80 %, 10 %, and 10 %, respectively. Considering the limitations of memory and computing resources, we have appropriately adjusted the resolution of the image and set its sampling to 256 × 256 pixels. This resolution was chosen out of consideration for the balance between model accuracy and computational efficiency to ensure that the model can still achieve optimal performance under resource-constrained conditions. In addition, this resolution setting also takes into account the input requirements of different models to ensure that the model can adapt to various input conditions and improve its flexibility and practicality in practical applications. Through these carefully designed operations, we hope to build a model that is both accurate and efficient, which can show good generalization ability in various data situations, while achieving fast learning and prediction under limited computing resources.

Discussion

Osteosarcoma requires a lot of work from pathologists to confirm. While other medical images have been computerized for analysis, osteosarcoma histology images are rarely mentioned when classified using deep-learning models. Based on deep learning to detect biopsy tissue images to identify osteosarcoma cells, our study provides a smaller and more efficient network architecture that can optimize the processing results of osteosarcoma datasets without increasing the complexity of the model. Experiments show that the CA-MobileNet V3 migration model test effect accuracy can reach up to 98.69 %. From Fig. 4, we can know that CA-MobileNet V3 performs best compared with Mobilenet V3, TL-MobileNet V3, ShuffleNet V2, and EfficientNet V2. For this model, future research directions can focus on exploring and integrating more types of attention mechanisms, and broadening the evaluation scope to larger-scale data sets or across different medical imaging fields to verify the generalization ability of the proposed method and robustness. It should be noted that Bansal et al. [36] reported that the overall accuracy of using Efficient was 0.968, which is higher than the accuracy of the EfficientNet V2 model we reported. This may be due to bias in validation on different sample sets, or it may be that the report modified the network after training and filtered out certain features using a binary arithmetic optimization algorithm.

In addition, we mounted this model on a microscope for preliminary usability testing, which proved that the model has strong application prospects. However, although we believe that the improved model has strong application prospects, we have only conducted preliminary usability tests on the AI microscope. We believe that this system can make pathologists work faster and more effectively, and improve the accuracy of cancer diagnosis. Therefore, a lot of testing is still needed in the future, and the infinite possibilities of artificial intelligence will be explored, such as further developing technology that can project the detection results of the CA-MobileNet V3 model directly onto the field of view, expanding the important value of the model in medical diagnosis in the future.

Conclusion

Based on artificial intelligence, this study innovatively integrated the design of traditional microscopes. We replaced the original SE attention mechanism with a coordinated attention mechanism that can fuse multiple features, and used the transfer learning method to train the CA-MobileNetV3 model. The results showed that the model improved the bone cell image feature fusion capability and model convergence speed in the model, proving the effectiveness of the optimized CA-MobileNet V3 model in osteosarcoma recognition. The algorithm showed excellent accuracy and F1 score, which also shows that more efficient lightweight network models can be used to improve the results of osteosarcoma datasets, rather than having to use increasingly complex large models. This study is also the first use of the MobileNet V3 model on an osteosarcoma dataset, providing medical professionals with a convenient solution for diagnosing bone tumors in bone cancer patients.

In addition, we developed an artificial intelligence microscope (AIM) based on CA-MobileNet V3. Although we have only conducted preliminary tests on the newly developed and designed AIM and have not yet been widely used to help diagnose patients, preliminary studies have shown that AIM is very promising and has the prospect of large-scale application. It is very likely to become a very effective auxiliary osteosarcoma diagnostic device for pathologists, and the same framework and device also provides a promising path for automated diagnosis of other types of tumors such as cardiac and lung imaging.

\dim W_{n}^{[i]}=n\binom{n+i}{i+1},\ \dim N_{i}=\binom{n+i-1}{i},\ \dim M_{i}=\frac{(n+i+1)(n+i-1)!}{(i+1)!(n-2)!};

2.

If
S,T
are submodules of the
W_{n}^{[0]}
-module
W_{n},
then
[S,T]
is also a submodule of
W_{n};

3.

[M_{i},M_{j}]=M_{i+j}
for all
i,j\geq 0
;

4.

[N_{i},N_{j}]=N_{i+j}
for all
i,j\geq 0
,
i\neq j
;
[N_{i},N_{i}]=0
, for all
i\geq 0
;

5.

[M_{i},N_{j}]=W_{n}^{[i+j]}
for all
i,j\geq 0
, except the cases:
(a)
i=j=0
, where
[M_{0},N_{0}]=0
,

(b)
i=0
,
j=1,2,\dots
, where
[M_{0},N_{j}]=N_{j}
,

(c)
i\geq 1,j=0,
where
[M_{i},N_{0}]=M_{i}.

6.

[W_{n}^{[-1]},N_{j}]=W_{n}^{[j-1]}
for all
j\geq 0

Proof.

1.

Follows immediately.

2.

Obvious.

3.

To prove this part of the lemma it is enough to show (in view of Theorem 1 and part 2 of this lemma) that
[M_{i},M_{j}]\neq 0
for
i,j\geq 0
. Take the elements
x_{1}^{i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\in M_{i}
,
x_{2}^{j+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\in M_{j}
. Then

[x_{1}^{i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}},x_{2}^{j+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}]=(j+1)x_{1}^{i+1}x_{2}^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-(i+1)x_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{j+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\neq 0.

So,
[M_{i},M_{j}]\neq 0
for
i,j\geq 0
and therefore
[M_{i},M_{j}]=M_{i+j}
.

4.

As in the proof of the part 3 of the lemma it is enough to show that
[N_{i},N_{j}]\neq 0
for
i\neq j
,
i,j\geq 0
(obviously
[N_{i},N_{j}]\subseteq N_{i+j}
). Take the elements
x_{1}^{i}E_{n}\in N_{i}
,
x_{2}^{j}E_{n}\in N_{j}
. Then

[x_{1}^{i}E_{n},x_{2}^{j}E_{n}]=(jx_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{j}-ix_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{j})E_{n}\neq 0

for
i,j\geq 0
,
i\neq j
.
So,
[N_{i},N_{j}]=N_{i+j}
for all
i,j\geq 0
,
i\neq j
.
The equality
[N_{i},N_{i}]=0
for
i\geq 0
can be immediately checked.

5.

If
i=0
, then
[M_{0},N_{j}]=N_{j}
for
j\geq 1
because
N_{j}
is an irreducible
W_{n}^{[0]}
-module. Of course,
[M_{0},N_{0}]=0
since
N_{0}=E_{n}\mathbb{K}
.

Let
i\geq 1
,
j\geq 1
. Take the elements
x_{2}^{i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\in M_{i}
and
x_{1}^{j}E_{n}\in N_{j}
. Let us show that the product
T=[x_{2}^{i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},x_{1}^{j}E_{n}]
lies neither in
M_{i+j}
nor in
N_{i+j}
. Indeed, this product has the form
T=jx_{1}^{j-1}x_{2}^{i+1}E_{n}-ix_{1}^{j}x_{2}^{i+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}
. Since
i\geq 1
,
j\geq 1

T
has nonzero divergence (
\Div(T=j(n+i+j-1)x_{1}^{j-1}x_{2}^{i+1})
) and is
T
not proportional to the Euler derivation
E_{n}
. Thus,
T\notin M_{i+1}
and
T\notin N_{i+1}
. The latter means that the
W_{n}^{[0]}
-submodule
[M_{i},N_{j}]
is nonzero and equals neither
M_{i+j}
nor
N_{i+j}
. By Lemma
3
we have
[M_{i},N_{j}]=W_{n}^{[i+j]}
. Let us look at the remaining case
[M_{i},N_{0}].
Since
N_{0}=E_{n}\mathbb{K},
where
E_{n}
is the Euler derivation which acts scalarly on
M_{i}
(in view of Lemma
2
) we see that
[M_{i},N_{0}]=M_{i}.

6.

It is obvious that
[W_{n}^{[-1]},N_{j}]\subset W_{n}^{[j-1]}.
Therefore it is sufficient to show that the
W_{n}^{[0]}
-module
[W_{n}^{[-1]},N_{j}]
is nonzero and does not coincide with neither
M_{j-1}
nor
N_{j-1}.
Take a nonzero element
fE_{n}\in N_{j}.
Then
[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}},fE_{n}]=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}E_{n}+f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}.
The latter equality implies that
[W_{n}^{[-1]},N_{0}]=W_{n}^{[-1]}.
Let
j>0
. Then
\deg f\geq 1
and at least one partial derivative
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}
is nonzero. As the product
f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}
does not belong to
N_{j-1}
, we see that
[W_{n}^{[-1]},N_{j}]=W_{n}^{[j-1]}
by Lemma
3
.

The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
W^{[i]}_{n}
and
W^{[j]}_{n}
be homogeneous components of the standard grading
W_{n}=W_{n}(\mathbb{K})=\underset{i\geq-1}{\bigoplus}W_{n}^{[i]},n\geq 2.
Then the next equalities are satisfied:

[W^{[i]}_{n},W^{[j]}_{n}]=W^{[i+j]}_{n},i,j\geq 0,

except the case when
i=j=0
, where
[W_{n}^{[0]},W_{n}^{[0]}]=M_{0}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. Obviously
[W_{n}^{[-1]},W_{n}^{[-1]}]=0
since
[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}]=0,i,j=1,\ldots,n.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
3
and Proposition
1
we need only to show that

[W_{n}^{[-1]},W_{n}^{[m]}]\not=0
,
m\geq 0
. But this inequality can be checked immediately.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
W_{n}^{[i]}=M_{i}+N_{i}
be the decomposition of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
-module
W_{n}^{[i]}
into direct sum of irreducible submodules (as in Theorem
1
). The modules
M_{i},M_{j},i\not=j
are non-isomorphic, the same is true for modules
N_{i},N_{j},i\not=j.
The modules
M_{i},N_{j}
are non-isomorphic except the case of submodules
M_{i},N_{i+2}
of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-module
W_{2}
when
M_{i}\simeq N_{i+2}.
These
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-modules are also
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-modules, but they are non-isomorphic as
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-submodules of
W_{2}.

Proof.

The modules
M_{i}
and
M_{j},\ i\not=j
are non-isomorphic because of different dimensions; the same is true for
N_{i}
and
N_{j},\ i\not=j.
Consider the modules
M_{i}
and
N_{j},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.
It follows from the general theory of modules over a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
that irreducible finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules are uniquely defined (up to isomorphism) by their maximal weights (see, for example,
[
4
]
, Ch.VI). Thus, the modules
M_{k}
and
N_{m}
are isomorphic if and only if they have equal weights. We have from the proof of Theorem
1
that the module
N_{m}
has the weight

\lambda^{(1)}(x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}})=m\delta_{i1},i=1,\ldots,n-1

and the module
M_{k}
has the weight

\lambda^{(2)}(x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}})=1+(k+1)\delta_{i1},i=1,\ldots,n-1.

So,
M_{k}\simeq N_{m}
if and only if
m\delta_{i1}=1+(k+1)\delta_{i1},i=1,\ldots,n-1.
If
n>2
, then for
i=2
we have
0=m\delta_{12}=1+(k+1)\delta_{12}=1,
which is impossible. Therefore
n=2,m=k+2.
One can easily check that the irreducible
sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-modules
M_{k}
and
N_{k+2}
have the same dimension (over
\mathbb{K}
), so they are isomorphic.
The proof is complete.
∎

The subalgebra
W_{n}^{[0]}
of the Lie algebra
W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
is contained in the subalgebra
W_{n}^{[-1]}\oplus W_{n}^{[0]}
which is isomorphic to the linear affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{aff}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. This Lie algebra is the semidirect sum of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and the abelian Lie algebra
V
,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}V=n
, with the natural action of
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
on
V
. Note that the Lie algebra
W_{n}^{[-1]}\oplus W_{n}^{[0]}
is not maximal in
W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
because it is contained in the Lie subalgebra of
W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
consisting of all the derivations with constant divergence.

Nevertheless, the subalgebra
W_{n}^{[-1]}\oplus W_{n}^{[0]}
has some properties similar to those of maximal subalgebras of
W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
L=W_{n}^{[-1]}\oplus W_{n}^{[0]}
be the subalgebra of
W_{n},\ n\geq 2
consisting of all the derivations
D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
with
\deg f_{i}\leq 1
,
i=1,\dots,n
. An element
D=D_{-1}+D_{0}+D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k},D_{i}\in W_{n}^{[i]}
,
k\geq 1
with
D_{k}\neq 0
, generates together with
L
the entire Lie algebra
W_{n}
if and only if
\Div D\neq{\rm const}
and either
k\geq 2
or
k=1
and
D_{1}
is not proportional to the Euler derivation
E_{n}
.

Proof.

Let a derivation
D\in W_{n},

D=D_{-1}+D_{0}+D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k},\ k\geq 1,\ D_{k}\neq 0,

satisfy all the conditions of the theorem. Denote by the
U=\langle L,D\rangle
the subalgebra of
W_{n}
generated by
L
and
D
. Let us show that
U=W_{n}
. Since the Euler derivation
E_{n}
belongs to
L
the subalgebra
U
is graded:
U=\underset{i\geq-1}{\bigoplus}U_{i}
, where
U_{i}=U\cap W_{n}^{[i]}
. Indeed, for any element

S\in U,S=S_{-1}+S_{0}+S_{1}+\cdots+S_{m},S_{i}\in W_{n}^{[i]}

it holds (by Lemma
2
) the equality

[E_{n},S]=-S_{-1}+0S_{0}+S_{1}+\cdots+mS_{m}.

By induction on
m
one can prove that
S_{i}\in U
,
i=-1,0,1,\dots,m
, so
U_{i}=W_{n}^{[i]}\cap U
. Note that the
\mathbb{K}
-subspace
U_{i}
,
i\geq-1
, is a submodule of the
W_{n}^{[0]}
-module
W_{n}^{[i]}
because
W_{n}^{[0]}\subset U
. Therefore, for any
i\geq 0,
the submodule
U_{i}
equals either
W_{n}^{[i]}
or
M_{i}
, or
N_{i}
by Lemma
3
. Since
D_{k}\neq 0
in the expression
D=D_{-1}+D_{0}+D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k}
, the submodule
U_{k}
is nonzero.

First, let
k=1
, i.e.
D=D_{-1}+D_{0}+D_{1}
. Since
D_{1}
is not proportional to
E_{n}
we have
U_{1}\neq N_{1}
.
The condition
\Div D_{1}\neq const
implies that
U_{1}\neq M_{1}
. Therefore
U_{1}=W_{n}^{[1]}.
But then
W_{n}^{[2]}=[W_{n}^{[1]},W_{n}^{[1]}]\subseteq U,\ldots,W_{n}^{[i]}\subseteq U,i\geq 2.
The latter means that
U=W_{n}(\mathbb{K}).

Now, let
k\geq 2
. If
U_{k}=W_{n}^{[k]}
then taking into account the inclusion
W_{n}^{[-1]}\subset U,
we see that
W_{n}^{[k-1]}\subset U,\dots,W_{n}^{[1]}\subset U
. But then
W_{n}^{[i]}\subset U
for
i\geq 1
and
U=W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. Further, if
U_{k}=N_{k}
then
[W_{n}^{[-1]},N_{k}]=W_{n}^{[k-1]}
by Proposition
1
. One can show in similar way that
U=W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. Let now
U_{k}=M_{k}
. Since
\Div D\neq const
we have
\Div D_{s}\neq const
for some
1\leq s\leq k
. But then
U_{s}\neq 0
,
U_{s}\neq M_{s}
. If
U_{s}=W_{n}^{[s]}
then we can show as above that
U=W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. Let
U_{s}=N_{s}
. Then, by Proposition
1
,
[U_{k},U_{s}]=[M_{k},N_{s}]=W_{n}^{[k+s]}
. Repeating the previous considerations one can show that
U=W_{n}
.

Now, let
L
and
D
generate the Lie algebra
W_{n}
. By Lemma
1
, all the derivations with constant divergence form a proper subalgebra
W_{c}
of
W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. Since
L\subseteq W_{c}
we see that
\Div D\neq const
. Write the derivation
D
in the form

D=D_{-1}+D_{0}+D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k},\ D_{i}\in W_{n}^{[i]}.

Since
\Div D\neq const
we see that
k\geq 1
. If
k=1
and
D_{1}=fE_{n}
for some
f\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
then
D_{1}\in N_{1}
. But
L+N_{1}
is a proper subalgebra of
W_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. The latter contradicts our assumption on
D
. Therefore
D_{1}
is non-proportional to the Euler derivation
E_{n}
.
The proof is complete.
∎
